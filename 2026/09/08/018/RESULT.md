# Discrete Willmore gap extremal among 8×8–12×12 triangulated tori near the Clifford torus

## Context

The smooth Willmore conjecture, proved by Marques–Neves, states that any torus
immersed in R^3 has Willmore energy W_smooth ≥ 2π² ≈ 19.7392088022 with equality
for the Clifford torus. Bobenko introduced a Möbius-invariant discrete Willmore
functional for simplicial surfaces, with applications to discrete thin-shell
bending energy, and Bobenko–Weidner studied related quadratic conformal
functionals and their minimizers (including nontrivial topologies) with
geometry-processing applications. Knöppel et al. gave a rolling-spheres /
quaternionic interpretation. None of these supplies explicit certified
Bobenko-energy values for coarse triangulated tori: on coarse meshes the
discrete energy lies strictly below 2π² and its dependence on resolution and
aspect ratio must be computed. This record certifies the lowest-energy mesh
among an explicitly enumerated symmetric family and a full per-resolution
calibration table.

## Definitions

- Let R/r denote the torus-of-revolution radii ratio (major/minor), r = 1.
- For integers m, n, the (m,n) diagonal-split torus of revolution has vertices
  V_{i,j} = ((R + r cos θ_i) cos φ_j, (R + r cos θ_i) sin φ_j, r sin θ_i),
  θ_i = 2πi/m, φ_j = 2πj/n, and for each parameter quad (a,b,c,d) the two
  faces (a,b,c), (a,c,d) (consistent diagonal split).
- Bobenko discrete Willmore energy (Möbius normalization, vanishes on
  inscribed spheres):
  W = Σ_e β(e) − π|V|,
  where for each interior edge e shared by two triangles, β(e) ∈ [0,π) is the
  exterior angle between the two circumcircles, computed as
  β(e) = π − arccos(t_1·t_2) with t_1, t_2 the unit circumcircle tangent
  vectors at a shared endpoint. On closed torus meshes every edge is shared by
  exactly two triangles; β is endpoint-independent on these meshes (verified
  to ~0). Per-vertex energy W_v distributes β(e)/2 to each endpoint minus π.
- Clifford baseline: 2π² = 19.739208802178716 (Marques–Neves, cited only as a
  gap-measurement baseline; no smooth claim is made).

## Result (headline claim)

Let T* be the diagonal-split 8×8 torus of revolution with R/r = √2
(enclosed as float 1.4142135623730951).

1. W(T*) = 18.421677060900915 (float64), certified interval
   I* = [18.42167687562556, 18.421840515094946] (width 1.64×10⁻⁴).
2. I* is strictly lowest and disjoint from the certified intervals of all
   19 other surveyed symmetric competitors: the remaining 8–12 square tori at
   R/r = √2, the non-square grids 8×9, 8×10, 8×12, 9×12, 10×12, 11×12, 12×8,
   12×10, 12×11 at R/r = √2, and the 12×12 aspect sweep
   R/r ∈ {1.2, 1.3, 1.35, 1.5, 1.6, 2.0} (20 meshes total).
   Runner-up is the 8×9 torus: 18.518035865259492 in
   [18.5180355909369, 18.518214232689676]. Separation margin
   (runner-up lo − extremal hi) = 0.0961950758, ≈ 588× the extremal width.
3. Gap to Clifford baseline: W(T*) − 2π² ∈ [−1.31753193, −1.31736829].
   The square-torus column is monotone increasing:
   8×8: 18.4216770609, 9×9: 18.6451456722, 10×10: 18.8184466255,
   11×11: 18.9544877747, 12×12: 19.0634917775.
   Supporting float64 refinements converge toward 2π² from below:
   16×16: 19.3348802058, 24×24: 19.5503738977, 32×32: 19.6309907590
   (gap −0.108 at 32×32).
4. The 12×12 aspect sweep attains its minimum near R/r = 1.35
   (19.0156736747) and rises steeply for elongated tori
   (21.8490516718 at R/r = 2.0), certifying the aspect-ratio-driven gap.

Full 20-mesh certified table: see `artifacts/census_intervals.json`
(float, lo, hi, width per mesh); T* vertex/face log and per-vertex energies
(min 0.1297430649, max 0.5581527394, no outlier) in
`artifacts/Tstar_log.json`.

## Proof / evidence

- Finite-computation certificate with independent replay, not a general
  theorem. Every mesh is generated by the closed formula above; integer face
  lists are committed in the artifacts.
- Independent stdlib-only verifier `artifacts/verify.py` replays all 20
  float64 energies from the closed-form formula and checks closedness
  (every edge in exactly 2 faces), Euler characteristic χ = V − E + F = 0,
  interval containment, per-vertex sums, and extremal disjointness:
  ALL CHECKS PASS (auditor re-ran from workspace root with identical output).
- Auditor independently re-derived all 20 values to <1e-9, confirmed T88
  lowest with gap 0.0961950758, confirmed χ = 0 (T*: V=64, E=192, F=128),
  per-vertex sum equality, total angle defect 7.1×10⁻¹⁵, and the monotone
  square column / aspect minimum / refinement values.
- Controls: normalized icosahedron gives W = 0.0 exactly (all 30 β = 2π/5,
  Σβ = 12π = π|V|), confirming the Möbius normalization; torus β is
  endpoint-independent (difference 0 on all tested torus meshes).
- Margin analysis: separation 0.096 ≫ interval widths (1.6–4.4×10⁻⁴) ≫
  plausible float64 error (~10⁻¹²), so the disjointness inequality is robust
  even though the interval-generator code itself was not archived.

## Limitations

- "Extremal" means lowest among the 20 surveyed symmetric diagonal-split
  tori of revolution only — not against all triangulated tori.
- Only the diagonal-split triangulation pattern is certified; other patterns
  (e.g. staggered/criss-cross) are excluded by design.
- R/r = √2 is the float 1.4142135623730951, not symbolic √2; aspect-error
  contribution is negligible versus 10⁻⁴ widths.
- Interval widths (~2–4×10⁻⁴) come from naive interval arithmetic; ample for
  the 0.096 margin but not sharp. The interval generator was not archived;
  only float values, intervals, and the replay verifier are archived.
- 16×16/24×24/32×32 refinement values are float64-only supporting
  observations, not interval-certified.
- 2π² is used strictly as a cited baseline; no claim about the smooth
  Willmore problem.

## Reproducibility

- Run from the record directory: `python3 artifacts/verify.py`
  (stdlib only: json, math, collections). Expected: per-mesh
  `replay W=... in-interval=True match-float=True`, χ = 0,
  `extremal T88 disjoint: True margin: 0.09619...`, `T* replay: True`,
  `ALL CHECKS PASS`.
- Mesh regeneration: `torus(m, n, R, r)` per Definitions with R = √2 (float),
  1.2, 1.3, 1.35, 1.5, 1.6, 2.0 as listed; energy via circumcircle-tangent
  formula above.
