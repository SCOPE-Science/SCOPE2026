## Correctness — PASS

The finite reduction and the small simple census were independently recomputed with a separate implementation.

For the rank-3 lemma, all `2^20-1` nonempty families of triples on a fixed six-element ground set were tested against the basis-exchange axiom. The filter leaves exactly 2053 rank-3 basis families. For each family, all ordered basis pairs were grouped by their element-multiplicity union, exact symmetric single-element-exchange adjacency was constructed, and BFS was run within every compatibility class. All classes were connected. The resulting family-diameter histogram is

`diameter 0: 20; diameter 1: 240; diameter 2: 892; diameter 3: 901`,

with no disconnected case and no diameter above 3. This independently confirms the computer half of the lemma.

The reduction to six elements is valid: two compatible ordered pairs of rank-3 bases have support of size at most six, and restriction to that support preserves the relevant bases and exchange moves. Hence the finite check proves the asserted universal rank-3 upper bound.

A second fresh enumeration of simple rank-3 matroids on `m=3,4,5,6` points found respectively 1, 2, 4 and 9 isomorphism types, from labelled counts 1, 5, 31 and 352. Their exchange diameters are respectively `{0}`, `{1,1}`, four copies of 2, and one copy of 2 plus eight copies of 3. Thus `D3=0,D4=1,D5=2,D6=3`. For `m=7,8`, the universal upper bound gives at most 3, while the displayed disjoint-swap pair in `U(3,m)` needs at least three exchanges because each move changes at most one element of the first basis; the supplied three-step path attains 3. Hence `D7=D8=3`.

## Originality — PASS, with an important antecedent

The rank-3 connectivity part is not new. Kenji Kashiwabara, **The Toric Ideal of a Matroid of Rank 3 is Generated by Quadrics**, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 17 (2010), #R28, DOI `10.37236/300`, proves White's symmetric-exchange generation conjecture for matroids of rank at most 3. That result implies reachability of compatible basis pairs in rank 3, so the present record should not be read as first proving rank-3 connectivity.

The quantitatively stronger statement audited here is the diameter bound `<=3`, i.e. the rank-3 case of Hamidoune's bounded symmetric-exchange conjecture. The checked distance literature continues to state the rank-`r` bound as a conjecture in general, while proving it for special classes such as split matroids. Bérczi–Mátravölgyi–Schwarcz, **Weighted exchange distance of basis pairs**, arXiv `2211.12750`, explicitly presents Hamidoune's rank bound as a strengthening of White's conjecture and proves variants for selected classes. Bérczi–Schwarcz, **Exchange Distance of Basis Pairs in Split Matroids**, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 38 (2024), 132–147, gives shortest-sequence results for split matroids and cites Kashiwabara separately for rank-3 White connectivity. No checked source states the universal rank-3 distance bound 3 or the exact small simple-matroid diameter spectrum.
