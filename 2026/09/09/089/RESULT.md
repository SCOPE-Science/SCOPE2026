# First-power regularity and an extremal induced 8-cycle in C₁₄(1,3)

## Setting and result

Let G be the graph on ℤ/14ℤ with edges between vertices whose difference is ±1 or ±3. It is a connected, 4-regular bipartite graph with 28 edges. Let S=ℚ[x₀,…,x₁₃] and let I=I(G) be its edge ideal. Then

\[
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=3,\qquad \operatorname{reg}(I)=4,\qquad \operatorname{im}(G)=2.
\]

There are exactly 98 induced pairs of edges and no induced matching of size 3 or more. The extremal set W={0,1,2,5,6,7,10,11} induces the cycle 0–1–2–5–6–7–10–11–0 and contributes one to β₅,₈(S/I). In particular the induced-matching lower bound \(\operatorname{reg}(S/I)\ge\operatorname{im}(G)\) is **strict** at this graph. The induced 8-cycle supplies the extra unit of quotient regularity.

## Proof

Write the bipartition as X={x_i=2i} and Y={y_j=2j+1}, now with indices modulo 7 and with x_i denoting a vertex in this paragraph. The nonneighbors in X of any y_j are exactly {x_{j−4},x_{j−3},x_{j−2}}, a consecutive triple. For an induced subgraph on U, put X'=U∩X and Y'=U∩Y. If either part is empty, its independence complex is a simplex. Otherwise, the independence complex is the union of the simplex on X' and the union of the closed stars of all y∈Y'. Every nonempty finite intersection of these stars is the star of that subset of Y', hence is contractible. Their nerve is a simplex, so the union of stars is contractible. Its intersection with the simplex on X' is the complex Γ generated by the sets X'∩(X\N(y)) for y∈Y'. By reduced Mayer–Vietoris, in degrees t≥2 the homology of the independence complex is that of Γ in degree t−1.

Every triangle of Γ consists of three consecutive indices. Its edge joining the first and last index belongs to no other triangle, since the cyclic index set has size 7. Each triangle therefore has a boundary edge unique to it, so the triangle boundary has trivial kernel. There are no larger faces of Γ. Thus every induced independence complex has zero reduced homology in degrees 3 and above. Hochster's formula gives \(\operatorname{reg}(S/I)\le3\).

For the displayed W the cross-part adjacencies give exactly the eight listed cycle edges. The nonneighbor sets in W∩X for odd vertices 1,5,7,11 are, respectively, {6,10},{0,10},{0,2},{2,6}. Hence Γ is a 4-cycle, so \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_2(\operatorname{Ind}(G[W]);\mathbb Q)=1\). This is the required Hochster lower bound. Equivalently, the independence complex has face counts by size 0 through 4 equal to (1,8,20,16,2), with rational boundary ranks \(\operatorname{rank}d_1=7\), \(\operatorname{rank}d_2=13\), \(\operatorname{rank}d_3=2\).

For two distinct vertices of X at cyclic index distance d∈{1,2,3}, their nonneighbor triples in Y have intersection size 3−d. An induced pair using these two X vertices must match each to a nonneighbor of the other that is not its own nonneighbor, giving d² choices. There are seven unordered X pairs per distance, yielding 7(1²+2²+3²)=98. In an induced triple of edges each pair of the three X vertices would lie together in a nonneighbor triple in X of a Y vertex, excluding the third selected X vertex. This forces all three pairwise cyclic distances to be at most 2, so the three X indices are consecutive: their three cyclic gaps sum to 7, so at least one gap is ≥5 and the other two are 1. The first and last index lie together in only one nonneighbor triple, which also contains the middle index, a contradiction.

## Consequences and limits

Over ℚ, Banerjee–Nevo's bipartite upper bound gives \(\operatorname{reg}(I^s)\le2s+2\) for s≥2. A pair of induced disjoint edges gives the lower bound \(\operatorname{reg}(I^s)\ge2s+1\): the restriction to those four vertices is the power of a complete intersection of two disjoint quadratics, with first syzygies of degree 2s+2, and Betti numbers in multidegrees supported on those vertices agree with those of the restriction. Thus each power has one of these two regularities; neither the general exact formula nor the value at s=2 is determined here. No claim about characteristic independence of the full Betti table is made.

The proof above is independent of computations. In the archived artifact directory, `verify_emergent.py` checks the graph, witness, exact rational ranks and matching counts. `step2_hochster.py` gives the modular Hochster scan over both recorded primes, `step3_qq_H.py` checks the exact-rational witness, and `step1b_imcheck.py` gives a second matching enumeration.
