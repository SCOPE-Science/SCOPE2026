# Hexagon-switch depth-two branching ball around the Netto STS(19)

## Statement

Let A4 be the Netto anti-Pasch cyclic STS(19) in Mathon-Phelps-Rosa notation
with its published Z19 base triples, and let G6 be the graph on STS(19)
isomorphism classes with edges for single hexagon (length-6 cycle) switches as
defined in Erskine-Griggs Section 2. Then the ball of radius at most two around
A4 in G6 contains at least 30 distinct isomorphism classes. In fact the
certified count is at least 32, and at least 57 for the 5-sparse (Netto)
starter: the 5-sparse starter yields 2+55=57 distinct classes and the second
anti-Pasch cyclic type yields 2+30=32 distinct classes.

## Context

Cycle switching is a standard transformation of Steiner triple systems: for a
pair (a,b) one forms the cycle graph C_ab and exchanges the roles of a and b
along an even cycle. The isomorphism-class switch graph S_l(19) for a fixed
cycle length l was studied by Erskine-Griggs (arXiv:2405.07750), who proved
S_6(19) has exactly four components of orders 1, 24, 284433, and the remainder,
and by Kaski-Maekinen-Oestergaard for the full-length graph. The total number
of STS(19) isomorphism classes is 11,084,874,829 (Kaski-Oestergaard), of which
2,591 are anti-Pasch and exactly one is 5-sparse (Colbourn et al.). The admitted
target asked whether hexagon (6-cycle) surgery diversifies the order-19
landscape explosively from Pasch-free symmetric ground, benchmarked by at least
30 distinct classes within two hexagon steps of the named Netto system A4.

## Definitions

- Points are Z19. A cyclic STS(19) is generated by 3 base triples whose 18
  ordered differences partition Z19\{0}; blocks are the 57 translates.
- For distinct a,b with third point c on their block, the cycle graph C_ab has
  vertex set V\{a,b,c}; a-edges join the two other points of each block
  containing a but not b, likewise for b. Every even cycle gives a switch.
  A hexagon switch is a switch on a 6-cycle (3 a-blocks + 3 b-blocks).
- Invariants: (i) Pasch count, number of 6-point subsets carrying exactly 4
  blocks in Pasch configuration (over C(19,6)=27132 subsets, with degree check);
  (ii) hexagon-record count, number of (a,b,directed 6-cycle) records;
  (iii) mitre count, number of 7-point subsets carrying exactly 5 blocks
  (over C(19,7)=50388 subsets). Each is exactly computed and invariant under
  relabelling; distinct triples certify non-isomorphism.

## Result

Type B (Netto / 5-sparse) starter {(0,1,8),(0,2,5),(0,4,13)}: starter key
(Pasch,hex-records,mitre)=(0,171,0); first hexagon child key (2,108,16);
171 starter and 108 child hex records; 55 novel depth-2 keys; total 57.
Type A starter {(0,1,4),(0,2,12),(0,5,13)}: starter key (0,57,114); child key
(4,46,94); 30 novel depth-2 keys; total 32. Both are >=30. A single hexagon
step already breaks Pasch-freeness (0 to 2 resp. 4) and the second step reaches
dozens of further isomorphism types.

## Proof / evidence

1. Exhaustive classification of anti-Pasch cyclic starters: canonical backtrack
   over difference partitions of Z19\{0} enumerates all 864 raw cyclic STS(19)
   difference families; exact Pasch counting leaves 216 anti-Pasch families in
   exactly 2 multiplier-equivalence types (sizes 162 and 54), represented by
   the Type A and Type B bases above. Type B is mitre-free with translation
   stabiliser order 171, i.e. the Netto 5-sparse system. Proving the bound for
   both covers A4 under any residual MPR-notation ambiguity.
2. Certified roster: for each starter S, take the first hexagon record, switch
   to depth-1 child N1 (Steiner property revalidated), enumerate all hexagon
   records of N1, switch each to candidates M (each revalidated), and keep S,
   N1, plus one representative per novel invariant triple among M's, discarding
   keys colliding with S or N1. Retained systems lie at distance 0,1,2 from S
   by explicit recorded switches; pairwise-distinct triples imply pairwise
   non-isomorphism. Invariant-distinctness is sufficient, so the true ball is
   at least as large.
3. Independent replay: verify.py reloads every roster system, re-checks the
   Steiner property (57 blocks, 171 pairs), recomputes all three invariants
   from scratch, re-applies each recorded switch, and confirms pairwise
   key-distinctness. Audit reran classify.py (864/216/2 types) and the full
   roster replay (57 and 32 distinct keys, all switches legal): ALL VERIFIED.

## Limitations

Invariant-distinctness is a sufficient but not necessary non-isomorphism
criterion, so counts are lower bounds. MPR-notation matching of A4 with the
5-sparse starter rests on the published uniqueness fact plus both-starters
coverage, not an independently re-verified literal MPR transcription. The
prototype exact backtracking isomorphism tester was too slow for the full
pairwise matrix and is excluded from the proof chain.
