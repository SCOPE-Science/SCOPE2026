# Absence of heterogeneity-induced gamma Hopf at fixed sparseness in a sparse inhibitory QIF network

## Context
Sparse randomly connected inhibitory networks of quadratic integrate-and-fire (QIF) neurons are a canonical model of cortical gamma rhythms. Exact mean-field reductions exist for densely all-to-all coupled QIF populations with Lorentzian heterogeneity (Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin and extensions), and sparse balanced networks admit shot-noise (non-diffusion) mean-field closures. Whether increasing excitability heterogeneity at fixed sparseness alone can destabilize the asynchronous state through a Hopf bifurcation into a 30–120 Hz gamma cycle is an open, admitted target question on the heterogeneity-magnitude-at-fixed-sparseness axis, distinct from delay-dispersion, delay-asymmetry, and cross-population-ratio axes.

## Definitions and admissible instance
- Network: single-population inhibitory QIF, N = 10000 neurons, fixed homogeneous mean in-degree K0 = 150, fixed random regular-ish digraph.
- Neuron: membrane time constant tau_m = 10 ms, threshold V_th = +12, reset V_reset = −12 (voltage units), inhibitory kick g = 0.2 per presynaptic spike with one-Euler-step delay, external DC drive I0 = 4.0.
- Heterogeneity: excitability eta drawn from a Lorentzian with median 0 and half-width Delta in [0, 5] in the same current units; tails clipped at |eta| = 30 in the mean-field quadrature.
- Baseline: at Delta = 0 the network sits on a stable asynchronous fixed point with population rate about 13.6–14.0 Hz and gamma-band (30–120 Hz) power fraction below 1%, with no 30–120 Hz cycle.
- Question: does there exist a finite Delta_ind in [0, 5] at fixed K0 at which the asynchronous fixed point loses stability through a Hopf bifurcation creating a stable 30–120 Hz gamma limit cycle?

## Result
For this admissible instance (g = 0.2, I0 = 4.0, K0 = 150, tau_m = 10 ms, Lorentzian Delta in [0, 5], Delta = 0 stable asynchronous baseline), there is NO finite heterogeneity-induced threshold Delta_ind in [0, 5]. The asynchronous fixed point remains asymptotically stable for every Delta in scope, so no Hopf bifurcation creates a stable 30–120 Hz gamma limit cycle at fixed sparseness. Heterogeneity is pro-rate and anti-synchrony here: the fixed-point rate rises smoothly from about 14 Hz to about 29 Hz while the least-stable oscillatory eigenmode stays damped by at least 69/s and its frequency is pushed upward from about 67 Hz to about 217 Hz. Direct N = 10000 sparse-network simulations confirm asynchronous firing with falling gamma-band power.

## Proof / evidence
1. Delta-parametrized shot-noise closure: per-excitability-class density P_eta(V,t) obeying drift plus shot-noise jump operator r(t)[P(V+g,t) − P(V,t)] with r(t) = K0 nu(t), upwind finite-volume discretization with dv dividing g exactly (NV = 120/240/360), threshold flux to reset cell, exact bin-shift jumps, Lorentzian quantile quadrature (NQ = 11–41). Self-consistency nu = sum_j w_j Ftop_j p^j_last(nu); fixed-point condition H(nu;Delta) = F(nu;Delta) − nu = 0; closed stability condition 1 = K0 chi_bar(lambda;Delta) with chi_bar the quadrature-averaged class resolvent response.
2. Fixed-point branch: bisection roots at NV = 240, NQ = 25 give Delta 0 → 13.999 Hz; 0.25 → 14.294; 0.5 → 14.856; 0.75 → 15.550; 1.0 → 16.340; 1.5 → 18.101; 2.0 → 19.981; 2.5 → 21.903; 3.0 → 23.607; 4.0 → 26.268; 5.0 → 28.812 Hz. Smooth, monotone, no fold. Refinement NV = 120/240/360 and NQ = 15/25/41 agrees to better than 2% at heterogeneous points; Delta = 0 varies 13.87–14.39 due to steep-gain sensitivity, bounded and stable. Picard iteration 2-cycles near Delta = 0, so bisection is used there.
3. Eigenvalue no-crossing: full coupled-matrix spectra (NV = 120, NQ = 11, 1320×1320 dense) at 11 Deltas at bisection nu* give least-stable pairs: Delta = 0: −75.1/s at 66.6 Hz; 0.25: −84.3 at 67.2; 0.5: −84.9 at 71.7; 0.75: −79.5 at 80.1; 1.0: −74.9 at 89.8; 1.5: −70.4 at 108.6; 2.0: −69.0 at 125.9; 2.5: −69.1 at 142.0; 3.0: −69.6 at 157.6; 4.0: −71.4 at 188.3; 5.0: −74.2 at 217.1 Hz. Every 30–120 Hz eigenmode has real part at most −70/s; no eigenvalue comes within 69/s of the imaginary axis anywhere in [0, 5]. Corroborating chi-resolvent scan minima |S| remain of order 10^4–10^6, far from zero.
4. Direct sparse-network test: Euler dt = 20 us, T = 0.6 s, N = 10000, K0 = 150, fixed connectivity, two seeds. Mean rates: Delta = 0: 13.60/13.60 Hz; 1.0: 15.80/15.57; 3.0: 22.67/22.03; 5.0: 28.34/27.51 Hz versus mean-field 14.00/16.34/23.61/28.81 Hz (agreement 2–7%, within the 25% specification). Gamma-band power fraction falls monotonically 0.6% → 0.35% → 0.13–0.14% → 0.07–0.10%; population CV falls 0.61 → 0.42; smoothed-rate traces show only finite-size fluctuations, no sustained gamma cycle at any Delta.

## Limitations
Disproof is scoped to the stated (g, I0, K0, tau_m) operating point with a Delta = 0 stable asynchronous baseline, not to all inhibitory regimes; stronger synapses, different drive, delay dispersion, or E-I architectures are not covered. Computation uses a numpy-only stack with dense eigensolves on one eigenvalue grid (NV = 120, NQ = 11); Lorentzian tails clipped at |eta| = 30; finite-size simulations at T = 0.6 s and two seeds. The stability margin of at least 69/s plus triple cross-check (branch convergence, resolvent scan, simulations) makes the computational disproof robust, but it is not a certified analytic proof or interval-arithmetic exclusion between sampled Deltas.
