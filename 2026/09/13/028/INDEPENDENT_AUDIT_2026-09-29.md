## Correctness

The central claim is correct: in NS(X)=U the primitive class δ=f1-f2 has square -2 and divisibility 1, and it is the only wall class in U that can separate the two isotropic boundary rays. Hence a Kähler/nef chamber can contain at most one of those isotropic rays, so two holomorphic Lagrangian fibrations with those two fiber rays cannot coexist on the same fourfold. The original record overreached only in calling the δ wall crossing a 'Mukai flop'. For K3^[2]-type fourfolds, (-2)-walls are associated with prime-exceptional/divisorial geometry, while the standard flopping walls arise from (-10)-classes of divisibility 2. The repair removes that nonessential birational characterization and keeps the lattice/cone argument.

## Originality

The wall classification, monodromy reflection, Torelli, and nef/Kähler chamber machinery are established results of Markman, Mongardi, Amerik-Verbitsky and related work. The exact U-lattice specialization and its immediate obstruction to simultaneous fibrations are a useful explicit application, but this audit does not treat it as a new general theorem or a priority claim.

## Scientific value

The repaired result cleanly rules out the proposed simultaneous two-fibration phenomenon for the named Picard lattice and explains it by a single explicit wall. Its value is as a sharp obstruction and worked specialization, not as a new theory of hyperkähler wall crossing.

## Limitations

- The repaired record does not identify the (-2) wall crossing as a Mukai flop.
- Existence of the example uses global Torelli/period-map and chamber-realization results rather than explicit projective equations.
- The statement concerns holomorphic fibrations on one model, not rational fibrations after birational modification.
- Originality is assessed conservatively as an explicit specialization of established wall theory.
