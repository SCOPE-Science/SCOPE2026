# Automatic boundedness for deterministic gradient-mapping accumulation

## Context

Wang and Yurtsever (2026) propose an adaptive proximal-gradient method for

\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb R^d} F(x)=f(x)+h(x),
\]

where \(h\) is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex, and the stepsize denominator accumulates squared gradient-mapping norms. Their Algorithm 1 uses

\[
\alpha_k=\frac{\eta}{S_k},\qquad
x_{k+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\alpha_k h}\bigl(x_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(x_k)\bigr),
\]

with \(S_1=\gamma>0\) and, in the deterministic setting,

\[
S_{k+1}^2=S_k^2\left(1+\frac{\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2}{\eta^2}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Their Assumption 1 requires a uniform bound on all increments \(\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|\), and in the convex case also a uniform bound on \(\|x_k-x^*\|\). That assumption is used in their deterministic smooth Theorem 1 and deterministic convex-smooth Corollary 2.

The result below shows that, for the **deterministic full-gradient smooth regime**, this boundedness assumption is automatic under the paper's other standing assumptions. The mechanism is a dichotomy: either the accumulator \(S_k\) stays bounded, which forces square-summable increments, or it grows without bound, which eventually forces the stepsize into the standard descent/Fejer regime.

## Computational model and assumptions

Work in Euclidean \(\mathbb R^d\) with exact arithmetic at the level of the theorem. Assume:

- \(h:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\) is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex;
- \(f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) is differentiable with an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient for some \(L>0\);
- \(F=f+h\) is bounded below, with \(F_*:=\inf F> -\infty\);
- the algorithm uses the exact full gradient and exact proximal operator, with arbitrary \(\gamma,\eta>0\) and \(x_1\in\operatorname{dom}h\).

In the convex part below, additionally assume \(f\) is convex and \(F\) attains a minimizer \(x^*\). These are the standing assumptions used by Wang--Yurtsever in the corresponding deterministic smooth regimes.

## Theorem

Let \(\{x_k,S_k\}\) be generated by deterministic Algorithm 1 of Wang--Yurtsever, and put \(d_k=x_{k+1}-x_k\).

1. **Automatic increment boundedness.** If \(f\) is \(L\)-smooth (possibly nonconvex) and \(F\) is bounded below, then
   \[
   \sup_{k\ge1}\|d_k\|<\infty.
   \tag{2}
   \]

2. **Automatic optimizer-distance boundedness in the convex smooth case.** If in addition \(f\) is convex and \(x^*\in\arg\min F\), then
   \[
   \sup_{k\ge1}\|x_k-x^*\|<\infty.
   \tag{3}
   \]

Consequently, the entirety of Assumption 1 in Wang--Yurtsever is redundant for deterministic Algorithm 1 when \(f\) is smooth. In particular, their deterministic nonconvex-smooth Theorem 1 and deterministic convex-smooth Corollary 2 apply without imposing an independent bounded-iterates/increments hypothesis.

This statement does **not** remove the assumption from their stochastic results, their convex nonsmooth regime, or their accelerated Algorithm 2.

## Proof

### 1. Accumulator identity

Equation (1) gives

\[
S_{k+1}^2-S_k^2=\frac{S_k^2}{\eta^2}\|d_k\|^2,
\qquad S_k\ge \gamma,
\tag{4}
\]

so \(S_k\) is nondecreasing. We split into the exhaustive cases that \(S_k\) is bounded or unbounded.

### 2. Increment boundedness

#### Case A: \(S_k\) is bounded

Let \(B:=\sup_k S_k<\infty\). From (1),

\[
\|d_k\|^2
=\eta^2\left(\frac{S_{k+1}^2}{S_k^2}-1\right)
\le \eta^2\left(\frac{B^2}{\gamma^2}-1\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Hence the increments are uniformly bounded. In fact (4) yields the stronger square-summability estimate

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|d_k\|^2
=\eta^2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{S_{k+1}^2-S_k^2}{S_k^2}
\le \frac{\eta^2}{\gamma^2}(B^2-\gamma^2)<\infty.
\tag{6}
\]

#### Case B: \(S_k\) is unbounded

Since \(S_k\) is nondecreasing, there is a finite index \(K\) with \(S_K\ge L\eta\). For every \(k\ge K\), the stepsize satisfies \(\alpha_k=\eta/S_k\le 1/L\).

The standard proximal-gradient descent inequality (also Lemma 5 in Wang--Yurtsever) gives, in the deterministic full-gradient setting,

\[
F(x_{k+1})
\le F(x_k)-\left(\frac1{\alpha_k}-\frac L2\right)\|d_k\|^2.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(k\ge K\), \(1/\alpha_k=S_k/\eta\ge L\), and therefore

\[
F(x_{k+1})\le F(x_k)-\frac L2\|d_k\|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus \(F(x_k)\) is nonincreasing for \(k\ge K\), and because \(F\ge F_*\),

\[
\|d_k\|^2
\le \frac{2}{L}\bigl(F(x_k)-F(x_{k+1})\bigr)
\le \frac{2}{L}\bigl(F(x_K)-F_*\bigr),
\qquad k\ge K.
\tag{9}
\]

The prefix \(d_1,\ldots,d_{K-1}\) is finite, and every proximal step is a finite vector because the strongly convex proximal subproblem has a unique solution. Hence the whole sequence of increments is bounded. This proves (2).

### 3. A one-step distance inequality in the convex case

Now assume \(f\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth and \(x^*\) minimizes \(F\). Define

\[
e_k=x_k-x^*,\qquad q_k=\nabla f(x_k)-\nabla f(x^*).
\]

The optimality conditions for the proximal step and for \(x^*\), together with monotonicity of \(\partial h\), imply

\[
\langle d_k,e_k+d_k\rangle
\le -\alpha_k\langle q_k,e_k+d_k\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]

Using
\(
\|e_k+d_k\|^2-\|e_k\|^2
=2\langle d_k,e_k+d_k\rangle-\|d_k\|^2
\)
and the Baillon--Haddad inequality
\(
\langle q_k,e_k\rangle\ge \|q_k\|^2/L
\), we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|e_{k+1}\|^2-\|e_k\|^2
&\le -\frac{2\alpha_k}{L}\|q_k\|^2
     -2\alpha_k\langle q_k,d_k\rangle-\|d_k\|^2\\
&\le -\frac{2\alpha_k}{L}\|q_k\|^2
     +2\alpha_k\|q_k\|\,\|d_k\|-\|d_k\|^2\\
&\le \left(\frac{\alpha_k L}{2}-1\right)\|d_k\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The last line is the maximum of the preceding quadratic expression over \(\|q_k\|\ge0\).

### 4. Optimizer-distance boundedness

Again split according to the accumulator.

#### Case A: \(S_k\) is bounded

Let \(B=\sup_k S_k<\infty\). Since \(\alpha_k\le \eta/\gamma\), (11) and (6) imply, for every \(N\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|x_{N+1}-x^*\|^2
&\le \|x_1-x^*\|^2
 +\frac{L\eta}{2\gamma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|d_k\|^2\\
&\le \|x_1-x^*\|^2
 +\frac{L\eta^3}{2\gamma^3}(B^2-\gamma^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus the optimizer distances are uniformly bounded.

#### Case B: \(S_k\) is unbounded

Use the same finite index \(K\) as above, so that \(\alpha_k\le 1/L\) for \(k\ge K\). Equation (11) then gives

\[
\|x_{k+1}-x^*\|^2
\le \|x_k-x^*\|^2-\frac12\|d_k\|^2
\le \|x_k-x^*\|^2,
\qquad k\ge K.
\tag{13}
\]

Hence the distance is nonincreasing after \(K\), and the finite prefix is bounded. This proves (3).

Combining the two parts and taking the maximum of the increment and distance bounds supplies a single finite constant \(D\) satisfying Wang--Yurtsever Assumption 1. ∎

## Consequences and interpretation

The result does not change the first-order oracle complexity claimed by Wang--Yurtsever. Instead it removes a hypothesis from the deterministic smooth guarantees of their **same algorithm**. For smooth nonconvex composite optimization their Theorem 1 therefore retains its \(O(1/t)\) average squared-gradient-mapping rate (equivalently the usual \(O(\varepsilon^{-2})\) iteration scale for an \(\varepsilon\)-stationary point) without a separate increment bound. For convex smooth problems, their deterministic \(O(1/t)\) averaged objective-gap corollary likewise no longer needs an assumed optimizer-distance bound.

The proof also explains why the algorithm self-corrects. If the accumulator remains finite, its multiplicative update itself controls the total squared motion. If the accumulator diverges, the stepsize must eventually become no larger than \(1/L\), after which ordinary proximal-gradient descent and convex Fejer geometry take over.

Other adaptive proximal-gradient algorithms already achieve convergence without bounded-iterate assumptions by different mechanisms. For example, Ye--Ma--Yang--Zhou (2025) use local upper/lower curvature estimates and a different Lyapunov argument. The contribution here is therefore not a new best complexity bound or a claim that boundedness assumptions are unnecessary for all adaptive methods; it is the identification and proof of a self-bounding property specific to the gradient-mapping-accumulation algorithm.

## Limitations

- The argument is deterministic: it uses \(g_k=\nabla f(x_k)\) in the descent inequality. With stochastic gradients, rare large noise realizations can obstruct pathwise uniform bounds, so no stochastic assumption removal is claimed.
- Smoothness is essential to the stated mechanism. No claim is made for the convex nonsmooth regime of Algorithm 1.
- No claim is made for accelerated Algorithm 2, whose state and accumulator are different.
- The theorem proves existence of a finite bound \(D\), as required by Assumption 1. In the unbounded-\(S_k\) branch, the bound contains a finite pre-threshold prefix and need not be available a priori from only \((L,\gamma,\eta,F(x_1)-F_*)\). Thus this is an assumption-removal result, not a new explicit uniform constant independent of the realized early trajectory.
- Originality is to the best of our knowledge. The exact paper currently has only arXiv v1 (7 May 2026); future revisions could incorporate the same observation.
