# Exact coloring-channel capacity via trace monoids

> **Review status: SCOPE Phase II same-model review.** Correctness, originality, and value were assessed by the same-model review, not an independent reviewer. Originality is qualified to the best of our knowledge.

## Claim

Consider a sequence of coloring channels \(\mathcal I=(I_1,\ldots,I_s)\) on an alphabet \(\Sigma\) of size \(q\). For an input word \(w\in\Sigma^n\), channel \(I_j\) outputs the subsequence \(w|_{I_j}\) obtained by deleting all letters outside \(I_j\). Let \(A_{\mathcal I}(n)\) be the set of possible output tuples and let

\[
\operatorname{cap}(\mathcal I)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log_q |A_{\mathcal I}(n)|.
\]

Let \(P\) be the pairs graph on \(\Sigma\): distinct letters \(a,b\) are adjacent iff some channel contains both. Define the signed independence polynomial

\[
\mu_P(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(P)\atop S\text{ independent}}(-1)^{|S|}z^{|S|}.
\]

Let \(r\in(0,1]\) be the unique root of \(\mu_P\) having minimum modulus (equivalently, its smallest positive root). Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cap}(\mathcal I)=-\log_q r.}
\]

Thus the capacity of every coloring-channel family is determined exactly by the signed independence polynomial of its pairs graph.

If every alphabet symbol occurs in at least one channel, there is also the exact finite-length generating function

\[
\boxed{\sum_{n\ge0}|A_{\mathcal I}(n)|z^n=\frac1{\mu_P(z)}.}
\]

If some symbols are completely unused, let \(Q=P[U]\) for \(U=\bigcup_j I_j\). Writing \(a_m=[z^m]\,1/\mu_Q(z)\), one has

\[
|A_{\mathcal I}(n)|=\sum_{m=0}^n a_m,
\]

so the capacity remains the same formula above. (The isolated unused vertices contribute factors \((1-z)\) to \(\mu_P\), whose root is 1 and does not increase the exponential growth rate.)

## Why coloring-channel outputs are traces

Define the output morphism on the visible alphabet \(U\),

\[
\Phi:U^*\to\prod_{j=1}^s I_j^*,\qquad
\Phi(w)=(w|_{I_1},\ldots,w|_{I_s}).
\]

Call two distinct letters **independent** when no channel contains both. This is exactly non-adjacency in the pairs graph.

### Forward direction

If independent letters \(a,b\) occur adjacently, swapping \(ab\) to \(ba\) changes no channel output: each channel contains at most one of \(a,b\). Therefore \(\Phi\) is constant on equivalence classes generated by adjacent swaps of independent letters.

### Converse direction

Suppose \(\Phi(w)=\Phi(v)\). Label the occurrences of each letter by order, e.g. \(a_1,a_2,\ldots\). For every pair of dependent distinct letters \(a,b\), some channel contains both, and its projected output fixes the relative order of every labelled \(a\)-occurrence and \(b\)-occurrence. The order of occurrences carrying the same letter is fixed by their labels.

Hence both \(w\) and \(v\) are linear extensions of the same finite dependence poset on labelled occurrences. Any two linear extensions of a finite poset are connected by adjacent swaps of incomparable elements. Two incomparable labelled occurrences here cannot carry dependent letters, because dependent occurrences were directly ordered. Thus each required swap is between independent letters.

Therefore

\[
\Phi(w)=\Phi(v)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
w\text{ and }v\text{ differ by commuting independent letters}.
\]

The coloring-channel output quotient is consequently the classical **trace monoid** (equivalently, history monoid) whose commuting pairs are the nonedges of \(P\).

## Möbius growth formula

For a trace monoid, Cartier--Foata theory gives

\[
G(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}t_n z^n=\frac1{\mu(z)},
\]

where \(\mu(z)\) is the alternating clique polynomial of the commutation graph. Cliques of the commutation graph are exactly independent sets of the pairs graph \(P\), so this \(\mu\) is precisely \(\mu_P\) above.

Goldwurm and Santini proved that this clique polynomial has a unique root of smallest modulus; that root is real and positive. Hence the trace counts satisfy

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty} t_n^{1/n}=1/r.
\]

When all symbols are visible, \(t_n=|A_{\mathcal I}(n)|\), proving both formulas. With unused symbols, the output of a length-\(n\) word is determined by a visible trace of some length \(m\le n\), and every such \(m\) can be padded to length \(n\); outputs of different visible lengths are distinguishable from their visible-letter counts. Therefore the output count is the partial sum above, which has the same exponential growth rate.

## Cycle corollary: the previously open cycle capacity

For the canonical cycle with pairs graph \(C_t\), the signed independence polynomial is

\[
\mu_{C_t}(z)=\alpha^t+\beta^t,
\qquad
\alpha,\beta\text{ roots of }x^2-x+z=0.
\]

For \(z>1/4\), write \(\alpha=\sqrt z\,e^{i\theta}\), \(\beta=\sqrt z\,e^{-i\theta}\), so that \(2\sqrt z\cos\theta=1\). Then

\[
\mu_{C_t}(z)=2z^{t/2}\cos(t\theta).
\]

There is no positive zero for \(z\le1/4\), and the first positive zero occurs at \(\theta=\pi/(2t)\). Consequently

\[
r_t=\frac1{4\cos^2(\pi/(2t))},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cap}(C_t)=\log_q\!\left(4\cos^2\frac{\pi}{2t}\right).}
\]

In particular, for the four-letter four-cycle,

\[
\operatorname{cap}(C_4)=\log_4(2+\sqrt2)
=0.885776651581\ldots .
\]

Yu and Schwartz (arXiv:2604.08234v1, April 2026) explicitly left cycle capacities without a closed form and, for alphabet size four, left the four-cycle as the sole unresolved sequence up to their equivalence, with only lower and upper bounds. The trace-monoid identification closes that case and gives a formula for every cycle length as a corollary of the general theorem.

## Checks and reproducibility

`artifacts/verify.py` independently:

1. constructs the pairs graph and its signed independence polynomial;
2. derives trace counts from the recurrence implied by \(1/\mu_P(z)\);
3. exhaustively enumerates coloring-channel outputs for several nontrivial small channel families and checks exact equality at finite lengths;
4. checks the unused-symbol partial-sum rule;
5. verifies the cycle formula for \(C_3,\ldots,C_7\), including \(C_4\) growth constant \(2+\sqrt2\).

The checks use exact integer arithmetic except for the final numerical root evaluations.

## Closest prior work

- Jessica Bariffi, Antonia Wachter-Zeh, and Eitan Yaakobi, *Sequence Reconstruction over Coloring Channels for Protein Identification*, ISIT 2025; arXiv:2508.02229. This introduces/studies the coloring-channel model and its capacity questions.
- Wenjun Yu and Moshe Schwartz, *On the Capacity of Sequences of Coloring Channels*, arXiv:2604.08234v1 (2026). This proves that capacity depends only on the pairs graph, gives exact results for several graph families, and explicitly leaves cycles without a closed form (including the four-cycle at alphabet size four).
- M. W. Shields, *Concurrent Machines*, The Computer Journal 28(5), 1985, 449--465. History monoids are classical projection quotients for concurrent systems.
- M. Goldwurm and M. Santini, *Clique polynomials have a unique root of smallest modulus*, Information Processing Letters 75(3), 2000, 127--132. They state that the reciprocal clique polynomial is the length generating function of a trace monoid and prove the minimum-modulus root property used above.
- Samy Abbes, *Introduction to probabilistic concurrent systems*, Fundamenta Informaticae 187 (2022), 71--102; arXiv:2111.00507. This gives a modern account of trace monoids and their Möbius/growth series.

## Originality scope and limitations

The trace/history-monoid facts themselves are classical and are **not** claimed as new. The proposed contribution is the identification of coloring-channel output classes with those classical traces and the resulting exact capacity formula for the 2025 coloring-channel model, including the cycle problem explicitly left open in April 2026.

Searches through 2026-09-17 found no public source applying trace/history monoids, Cartier--Foata theory, or signed independence/clique polynomials to coloring-channel capacity. This is a to-the-best-of-our-knowledge statement, not a guarantee of priority. The full text of Shields's 1985 article was not accessible in this run; it is a generic concurrency source predating coloring channels by decades, but it remains relevant to attribution of the projection-to-history-monoid theorem. A contemporaneous or poorly indexed 2026 note could also contain the same application. No independent review is claimed.
