# Classification of extremal interval endomorphism global dimension

Let \(P\) be a finite connected poset, let \(\operatorname{Int}(P)\) denote its intervals, and let
\[
G_P=\bigoplus_{I\in\operatorname{Int}(P)} k_I,
\qquad
\Lambda_P=\operatorname{End}_{k[P]}(G_P)^{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
For nonnegative integers \(a,b\), write \(D_{a,b}\) for the poset with a distinguished element \(d\), an antichain \(L\) of \(a\) elements below \(d\), and an antichain \(U\) of \(b\) elements above \(d\), with order generated by
\[
\ell<d<u\qquad(\ell\in L,\ u\in U).
\]
Thus its Hasse diagram is a double star; either side may be empty. Write \(K_{r,s}\) for the height-two poset with \(r\) minimal elements and \(s\) maximal elements, with every minimal element below every maximal element.

## Theorem

Let \(P\) be a connected poset with \(n\ge 3\) elements. The following are equivalent:

1. \(\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P=n-1\).
2. \(\operatorname{int\text{-}res\text{-}gldim}_k P=n-3\).
3. \(P\) is isomorphic to one of
   \[
   D_{a,b}\quad(a+b=n-1),\qquad K_{2,n-2},\qquad K_{n-2,2}.
   \]

The statement is independent of the coefficient field. Consequently, the number of isomorphism classes of connected \(n\)-element posets attaining the upper bound is
\[
3\quad(n=3),\qquad 5\quad(n=4),\qquad n+2\quad(n\ge5).
\]

## Proof

Aoki proves that
\[
\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P
 =\Omega(P)
 =\max_{(S,C)\in\operatorname{Sat}(P)}
 \bigl(|\operatorname{Max}(C\setminus S)|+|\operatorname{Min}(S\setminus C)|\bigr),
\]
where a saturated pair \((S,C)\) is a pair of intervals for which the family \(\mathcal W(S,C)\) of simultaneous upper/lower extremal-boundary extensions is a singleton. For a saturated pair, that singleton is \(S\cup C\). Aoki also proves \(\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P\le n-1\), and
\[
\operatorname{int\text{-}res\text{-}gldim}_kP
 =\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P-2
\]
for \(|P|>1\). Thus it suffices to classify the saturated pairs whose weight is \(n-1\).

Assume \(\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P=n-1\), and choose a saturated pair \((S,C)\) with weight \(n-1\). Put
\[
A=C\setminus S,\quad B=S\setminus C,\quad D=S\cap C,
\quad E=P\setminus(S\cup C),
\]
and define
\[
\alpha=|A|-|\operatorname{Max}A|,
\qquad
\beta=|B|-|\operatorname{Min}B|.
\]
All four quantities \(|D|,|E|,\alpha,\beta\) are nonnegative. Since
\[
\begin{aligned}
n-1
&=|\operatorname{Max}A|+|\operatorname{Min}B|\\
&=|A|+|B|-\alpha-\beta\\
&=n-|D|-|E|-\alpha-\beta,
\end{aligned}
\]
we obtain the defect identity
\[
|D|+|E|+\alpha+\beta=1. \tag{1}
\]
Hence exactly one of these four nonnegative integers equals one.

### Case 1: \(|D|=1\)

Then \(E=\varnothing\) and \(\alpha=\beta=0\). Write \(D=\{d\}\). Thus \(A\) is an antichain of maximal elements and \(B\) is an antichain of minimal elements. Since \(S=B\cup\{d\}\) and \(C=\{d\}\cup A\) are connected intervals, every element of \(B\) is comparable with \(d\), and every element of \(A\) is comparable with \(d\).

Here \(S\cup C=P\). Because \(P\in\mathcal W(S,C)\), \(S\) is a relative downset of \(P\) and \(C\) a relative upset. If \(d<b\) for \(b\in B\), the upset property of \(C\) would force \(b\in C\), a contradiction; hence \(b<d\). Dually, \(d<a\) for every \(a\in A\). Therefore \(P\cong D_{|B|,|A|}\).

### Case 2: \(|E|=1\)

Then \(D=\varnothing\) and \(\alpha=\beta=0\). The intervals \(S=B\) and \(C=A\) are connected antichains, so both are singletons. Therefore \(|S\cup C|=2\), whence \(n=3\). Every connected three-element poset is one of a three-element chain, a two-pronged lower star, or its dual, hence is some \(D_{a,b}\) with \(a+b=2\).

### Case 3: \(\alpha=1\)

Now \(D=E=\varnothing\) and \(\beta=0\). Since \(B=S\) is a connected antichain, \(S=\{s\}\). The interval \(C=A\) has exactly one nonmaximal element, say \(a\); all other elements form \(M=\operatorname{Max}C\). Connectedness of \(C\) forces \(a<m\) for every \(m\in M\).

Again \(S\cup C=P\). The upper extremal-boundary condition gives
\[
\partial_P^+S=\operatorname{Max}(P\setminus S)=M.
\]
Thus \(s\lessdot m\) for every \(m\in M\). The elements \(s\) and \(a\) are incomparable: \(a<s\) is forbidden because \(S\) is a relative downset, while \(s<a\) would make \(a\in\partial_P^+S\), contradicting \(\partial_P^+S=M\). Hence \(P\cong K_{2,|M|}=K_{2,n-2}\).

### Case 4: \(\beta=1\)

This is order-dual to Case 3 and gives \(P\cong K_{n-2,2}\).

This proves necessity.

Conversely, let \(P=D_{a,b}\), with lower leaves \(L\), center \(d\), and upper leaves \(U\). Take
\[
S=L\cup\{d\},\qquad C=\{d\}\cup U.
\]
Then \(S\) is a relative downset, \(C\) is a relative upset, and \(S\cup C=P\). Moreover
\[
\partial_P^+S=U=\operatorname{Max}(P\setminus S),
\qquad
\partial_P^-C=L=\operatorname{Min}(P\setminus C).
\]
Thus \(P\in\mathcal W(S,C)\); since \(S\cup C=P\), it is the only possible member, so \((S,C)\) is saturated and has weight \(a+b=n-1\).

For \(P=K_{2,r}\), let its two minimal elements be \(s,a\) and its maximal antichain be \(M\), \(|M|=r\). Set
\[
S=\{s\},\qquad C=\{a\}\cup M.
\]
Then \(S\) is a relative downset, \(C\) is a relative upset, \(S\cup C=P\), and
\[
\partial_P^+S=M=\operatorname{Max}(P\setminus S),
\qquad
\partial_P^-C=\{s\}=\operatorname{Min}(P\setminus C).
\]
Hence \((S,C)\) is saturated with weight \(r+1=n-1\). The case \(K_{r,2}\) follows by duality. Aoki's global-dimension formula therefore gives equality in every listed family.

Finally, the relative Auslander formula gives the equivalence with interval-resolution global dimension. For the enumeration, there are \(n\) pairwise nonisomorphic double stars \(D_{a,n-1-a}\). When \(n=3\), the two complete bipartite cases already occur among them. When \(n=4\), \(K_{2,2}\) contributes one additional self-dual type. For \(n\ge5\), \(K_{2,n-2}\) and \(K_{n-2,2}\) are two distinct additional types. ∎

## Relation to prior literature

Aoki's 2026 preprint proves the sharp uniform bound
\(\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P\le n-1\) and exhibits the one-sided star as an extremizer. It does not classify all equality cases. The same paper gives the exact saturated-pair formula and the relation
\(\operatorname{int\text{-}res\text{-}gldim}_kP=\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_P-2\), from which the theorem above derives a complete equality classification.

Earlier work of Aoki--Escolar--Tada proves monotonicity of interval-resolution global dimension and classifies the opposite extremal regime, namely posets of interval-resolution global dimension zero. Asashiba--Escolar--Nakashima--Yoshiwaki established finiteness and the general relative-homological framework for interval resolutions.

Targeted searches found no prior classification of the posets attaining the new \(n-1\) or \(n-3\) bounds. The originality claim is therefore limited to this equality classification and its enumeration, to the best of our knowledge.

## Limitations

The source global-dimension formula and sharp bound are very recent, so a concurrent revision or independent equality analysis may exist or appear subsequently. No claim is made that the notions of saturated pairs, interval endomorphism algebras, or interval-resolution global dimension are new here. No independent validation is asserted.
