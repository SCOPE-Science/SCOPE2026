# Positive stresses certify infinitesimal jamming of the \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) kissing configurations

## Statement

Let \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) be the two 40-point kissing configurations in \(\mathbb R^5\) described by Szöllősi and by Cohn--Rajagopal, respectively. We use the standard unnormalized coordinates of squared norm \(2\), so a pair is in contact exactly when its inner product is \(1\).

**Theorem.** Both \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) are infinitesimally jammed spherical codes. Consequently, both are jammed.

More precisely, each contact graph has 240 edges and admits a strictly positive integer stress \(w_{ij}\in\{4,5,6\}\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{j\sim i} w_{ij}x_j=30x_i
\qquad\text{for every vertex }i.
\]
The linear system consisting of the 40 tangency equations and the 240 contact-preserving equations has rank \(190\) on the 200 velocity coordinates. Since the configurations span \(\mathbb R^5\), its 10-dimensional kernel is exactly the space of infinitesimal rotations.

This resolves the jamming question that was explicitly left open for \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) in Self's September 2026 analysis of their contact polytopes.

## Coordinates

Let \(D_5\) denote the 40 vectors obtained by permuting
\[
(\pm1,\pm1,0,0,0).
\]
Write \(\sigma(x)=x_1+\cdots+x_5\), and let
\[
\rho(x)=x-\frac{2\sigma(x)}5(1,1,1,1,1)
\]
be reflection across the hyperplane \(\sigma=0\).

The configuration \(Q_5\) is
\[
Q_5=\{x\in D_5:\sigma(x)=0\}
\cup\{x\in D_5:\sigma(x)=-2\}
\cup\rho\{x\in D_5:\sigma(x)=-2\}.
\]

For \(R_5\), first form \(L_5\) by removing the eight vectors of \(D_5\) whose fifth coordinate is \(1\) and adjoining
\[
(\varepsilon_1/2,\varepsilon_2/2,\varepsilon_3/2,\varepsilon_4/2,1),
\]
where \(\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}\) and an odd number of the first four signs are negative. Then
\[
R_5=\{x\in L_5:\sigma(x)=0\}
\cup\{x\in L_5:\sigma(x)=-2\}
\cup\rho\{x\in L_5:\sigma(x)=-2\}.
\]
These descriptions reproduce the coordinates in the published constructions. Every point has squared norm \(2\), and in each configuration the maximum inner product between distinct points is \(1\).

The 13 orbit sizes again sum to 240. Direct exact arithmetic gives, for both configurations,
\[
\boxed{\sum_{j\sim i}w_{ij}x_j=30x_i\quad\text{for every }i.}
\]
Thus these are strictly positive equilibrium stresses for the spherical contact constraints.

## Proof of infinitesimal jamming

Let \(y_i\in\mathbb R^5\) be an infinitesimal deformation of either configuration. Tangency to the sphere gives
\[
\langle x_i,y_i\rangle=0.
\]
For every contact \(\{i,j\}\), feasibility of the spherical code to first order gives
\[
d_{ij}:=\langle x_i,y_j\rangle+\langle x_j,y_i\rangle\le0.
\]

Multiply these inequalities by the positive contact weights and sum over unordered contact pairs. Using the equilibrium identity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\{i,j\}}w_{ij}d_{ij}
&=\sum_i\left\langle\sum_{j\sim i}w_{ij}x_j,y_i\right\rangle\\
&=30\sum_i\langle x_i,y_i\rangle\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every summand on the left is nonpositive and every weight is strictly positive. Therefore
\[
d_{ij}=0
\]
for every contact. Hence every infinitesimal deformation lies in the kernel of the equality rigidity matrix consisting of the 40 tangency rows and 240 contact rows.

The accompanying exact verifier reconstructs both configurations and checks that each spans \(\mathbb R^5\). It then computes the rank of this \(280\times200\) rational matrix modulo the prime \(1000003\), obtaining rank \(190\) for both \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\). A nonzero minor modulo a prime is nonzero over \(\mathbb Q\), so the rational rank is at least 190. On the other hand, the infinitesimal rotations
\[
y_i=Ax_i,\qquad A^T=-A,
\]
form a 10-dimensional kernel because the points span \(\mathbb R^5\). Thus the rank is at most \(200-10=190\), and therefore is exactly 190. Its kernel is exactly the rotation space.

Every feasible infinitesimal deformation is consequently an infinitesimal rotation. Thus \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) are infinitesimally jammed. By the standard tensegrity criterion for spherical codes used by Cohn--Jiao--Kumar--Torquato, infinitesimal jamming implies jamming in this setting. This proves the theorem.

## Relation to the literature

Szöllősi introduced \(Q_5\) in 2023. Cohn and Rajagopal gave a layer description of \(Q_5\), constructed \(R_5\), and identified their symmetry groups and contact data in 2026. Cohn, Jiao, Kumar, and Torquato developed the infinitesimal-jamming framework for spherical codes and verified the then-known best kissing configurations in dimensions 4 through 12.

Most directly, Self's arXiv:2609.12640, submitted on September 11, 2026, studies the unit-edge contact polytopes carried by the known kissing configurations in dimensions 5--7. Its conclusion states that the configurations covered in the 2011 rigidity paper are infinitesimally jammed and explicitly says that for \(Q_5\) and \(R_5\) the question is open. The theorem above answers that question for both configurations.

The positive-stress certificate also supplies a compact structural reason for the result: any first-order motion respecting all contact inequalities must preserve every contact to first order, after which ordinary equality rigidity leaves only rotations.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the two specified 40-point configurations and does not prove that 40 is the five-dimensional kissing number, nor that either configuration is locally unique among all 40-point spherical codes. It also does not classify stresses or establish prestress stability beyond what is needed for infinitesimal jamming.

Originality is asserted to the best of our knowledge. The most directly relevant source declaring the problem open is very recent, so unindexed concurrent work remains a residual risk. No specific inaccessible source was identified as especially likely to contain a prior resolution.
