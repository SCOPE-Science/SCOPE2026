## Disposition

**PASSED.** The record survives independent review on correctness, originality, and scientific value without a substantive research-file change.

## Correctness

The arbitrary-field extension is mathematically sound. At each observed prefix Y, every possible accepted span is generated by a subset of Y, so the state family S_Y is finite over any field. The finite signature consisting of dim L and dim(W cap L) for W in S_Y determines every acceptance-LP inequality, including the post-acceptance terms because W+<e> is also a subset span. Thus only finitely many essential constraints remain. The source paper's finite-signature uncrossing/minimax argument for modular extensions then applies in the vector-space lattice, and its fixed-chain feasibility/1/e probability calculation is field-independent.

### Independent checks

- Independently verified that S_Y={span Z: Z subseteq Y} has at most 2^|Y| states and contains both every predecessor W and W+<e> used by the acceptance constraints.
- Checked algebraically that the signature (dim L, dim(W cap L)) over S_Y determines u_e(L), Delta_e(W,L), and therefore every affine g_p(L) constraint.
- Checked the uncrossing logic: supermodularity plus the rank identity for L,H, L cap H, and L+H lets a finite dual optimum be chosen with chain support by maximizing the secondary squared-rank objective.
- Checked that the final k/(i-1) conditional acceptance and harmonic-sum averaging are independent of the field; finite support and rational probabilities propagate inductively.
- Inspected the source's modular-extension finite-signature section and confirmed that it supplies the exact finite-signature machinery but does not state the arbitrary-field online-representation theorem.

## Originality

PASS, with a deliberately narrow boundary. Bérczi–Dughmi–Livanos–Soto–Verdugo arXiv:2609.20797 explicitly states the online-representation theorem only for finite fields, while allowing arbitrary fields only in the known-matroid model via offline re-representation. The same paper separately develops the finite-signature device for finitary modular extensions. The submitted contribution is the nontrivial information-model observation that this device can be inserted prefix-by-prefix into the online-representation construction. Searches through 2026-09-29 found no source stating that arbitrary-field online extension.

### Literature checked

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20797 — Bérczi–Dughmi–Livanos–Soto–Verdugo, The Strong Secretary Conjecture is True for Linear Matroids; abstract explicitly says online representation over a finite field, and known-matroid finitary modular extension.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19118 — Abdi–Banihashem–Hajiaghayi–Mittal, concurrent strong linear-matroid secretary theorem in the known-matroid setting; no located arbitrary-field online-representation statement.

## Scientific value

Removing the finite-field restriction matters for natural online vector streams over Q, R, and C and clarifies which part of the new strong-secretary proof really used field finiteness. The theorem does not improve 1/e or complexity, but it closes a conspicuous scope restriction in a major very recent result and identifies finite-rank modular ambient geometry as the operative structure.

## Limitations

- The extension reuses the source paper's acceptance LP, supermodular uncrossing, and finite-signature machinery; novelty is the online information-model extension, not a new secretary constant or a new LP architecture.
- No polynomial-time implementation is established, and identifying all realized signatures may be costly over abstract/infinite-field encodings.
- The ambient vector space/modular geometry must have finite rank and expose the required rank/closure operations; unbounded-rank unknown ambient structures are not covered.
- Both motivating preprints are days old, so near-simultaneous revision risk is unusually high.
