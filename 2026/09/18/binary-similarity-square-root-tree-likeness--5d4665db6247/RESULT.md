# Square-root tree-likeness for binary similarities

## Statement

Let \((S,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) satisfy the measurability hypotheses used by Chatterjee--Sloman and Yim for average hyperbolicity and compatible rooted trees: \(\mathcal F\) is countably generated and the diagonal is measurable. Let
\[
s:S\times S\to\{0,b\},\qquad b>0,
\]
be symmetric, measurable, and reflexive, so \(s(x,x)=b\). Write
\[
A(x,y)=s(x,y)/b\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Call a measurable equivalence relation \(E\) on \(S\) **finite-core** if it is generated by finitely many measurable nonsingleton classes, with every remaining point a singleton class. Define its correlation-clustering disagreement by
\[
\operatorname{Clust}(A)
:=\inf_E\mathbb P\bigl(A(X,Y)\ne E(X,Y)\bigr),
\]
where the infimum is over finite-core measurable equivalence relations and \(X,Y\) are independent with law \(\mathbb P\).

Then:

**Theorem.** For every such binary similarity,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tree}(s)=b\,\operatorname{Clust}(A).}
\]
If
\[
\beta(A)
:=\mathbb P\bigl(A(X,Y)=0,\ A(X,Z)=A(Y,Z)=1\bigr),
\]
for independent \(X,Y,Z\), then
\[
\operatorname{Hyp}(s)=b\,\beta(A)
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tree}(s)
\le 2\sqrt{\frac3e}\,\sqrt{b\,\operatorname{Hyp}(s)}
}
\qquad
\left(2\sqrt{3/e}=2.1010838379\ldots\right).
\]

The square-root exponent is optimal on this two-valued subclass. Yim's binary family from Proposition 5.1 satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s_m)=\sqrt{b\,\operatorname{Hyp}(s_m)},
\]
so no estimate of the form
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)\le C b^{1-\alpha}\operatorname{Hyp}(s)^\alpha
\]
can hold uniformly for binary similarities with \(\alpha>1/2\). The best possible constant in the square-root inequality therefore lies between \(1\) and \(2\sqrt{3/e}\); its exact value is not determined here.

For finite graphs this yields an explicit induced-removal inequality. If \(G\) has \(n\) vertices, \(k(G)\) is its minimum cluster-editing distance (edge additions/deletions needed to make a disjoint union of cliques), and \(p_3(G)\) is the number of unordered induced three-vertex paths, then
\[
\boxed{
p_3(G)\ge \frac{e}{6}\,\frac{k(G)^2}{n}.
}
\]
Equivalently, if \(k(G)\ge\varepsilon n^2\), then
\[
p_3(G)\ge \frac e6\varepsilon^2 n^3.
\]
The quadratic dependence on \(\varepsilon\) is of the correct order.

## Proof

### 1. Binary tree fitting is exactly correlation clustering

Let \(T\) be any compatible rooted weighted tree and let
\[
u(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle_r
\]
be its rooted Gromov-product similarity. Increasing a leaf edge changes only the diagonal value \(u(x,x)\). Hence, without increasing the error against a reflexive \(\{0,b\}\)-valued target, every leaf whose depth is below \(b\) may be extended until its depth is \(b\). We may therefore assume every leaf has depth at least \(b\).

Set \(\bar u=\min\{u,b\}\). Pointwise clipping cannot increase the error against \(bA\), so
\[
|bA-\bar u|\le |bA-u|.
\]
For every \(0<t<b\), define
\[
E_t(x,y)=\mathbf 1\{u(x,y)\ge t\}.
\]
Because all root-to-leaf paths have length at least \(b\), \(E_t\) is reflexive. In a rooted tree, two leaves satisfy \(u(x,y)\ge t\) exactly when their initial root paths agree through depth \(t\); therefore \(E_t\) is an equivalence relation. Since the tree has only finitely many internal vertices, \(E_t\) has only finitely many nonsingleton classes, and compatibility makes those classes measurable. Thus \(E_t\) is an admissible finite-core clustering.

For \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(q\in[0,b]\), the layer-cake identity gives
\[
|ba-q|=\int_0^b\left|a-\mathbf 1\{q\ge t\}\right|dt.
\]
Applying this with \(q=\bar u(X,Y)\), then Tonelli's theorem, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|bA(X,Y)-u(X,Y)|
&\ge \mathbb E|bA(X,Y)-\bar u(X,Y)|\\
&=\int_0^b\mathbb P\bigl(A(X,Y)\ne E_t(X,Y)\bigr)dt\\
&\ge b\,\operatorname{Clust}(A).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over compatible trees yields
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)\ge b\,\operatorname{Clust}(A).
\]

Conversely, let \(E\) be any finite-core measurable equivalence relation with nonsingleton classes \(C_1,\dots,C_m\). Build a one-level rooted tree by giving each \(C_i\) an internal node at depth \(b\), attaching its leaves to that node by edges of length \(\varepsilon>0\), and attaching every singleton leaf directly to the root by an edge of length \(b\). Then for distinct points the tree similarity is exactly \(bE(x,y)\); on singleton diagonals it is exactly \(b\), and on the remaining diagonal it is \(b+\varepsilon\). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)
\le b\,\mathbb P(A(X,Y)\ne E(X,Y))+\varepsilon.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and then infimizing over \(E\) proves
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)=b\,\operatorname{Clust}(A).
\]

### 2. Hyperbolicity becomes bad-triangle density

For a binary similarity,
\[
\bigl(\min\{s(X,Z),s(Y,Z)\}-s(X,Y)\bigr)_+
\]
is equal to \(b\) precisely when
\[
A(X,Y)=0,\qquad A(X,Z)=A(Y,Z)=1,
\]
and is zero otherwise. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Hyp}(s)=b\,\beta(A).
\]
This is the one-threshold specialization of the bad-triangle integral appearing in Yim's Lemma 4.1.

### 3. A one-level Poisson pivot bound

Fix \(\lambda>0\). Sample \(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\), and conditional on \(N\), sample pivots \(Z_1,\dots,Z_N\) independently from \(\mathbb P\). Assign a point \(x\) to the first pivot adjacent to it in \(A\):
\[
\ell(x)=\min\{i:A(x,Z_i)=1\},
\]
with \(\ell(x)=\infty\) if there is no such pivot. Points with the same finite value of \(\ell\) form a cluster; every unassigned point is a singleton. This is a finite-core measurable equivalence relation \(E\).

There are three ways for \(A(X,Y)\) and \(E(X,Y)\) to disagree.

**False positive.** If \(A(X,Y)=0\) but \(X,Y\) enter the same nontrivial cluster, one sampled pivot is adjacent to both. Conditioning on \(N=k\) and taking a union bound gives at most \(k\beta(A)\); averaging over the Poisson variable gives
\[
\mathbb P(A=0,E=1)\le\lambda\beta(A).
\]

**Assigned false negative.** Suppose \(A(X,Y)=1\), the points are separated, and at least one is assigned. The earlier of their two relevant assignment events supplies a pivot adjacent to one endpoint and not the other. By exchangeability of \(X,Y,Z\), each orientation has bad-triangle density \(\beta(A)\). A union bound over pivots and the two orientations gives
\[
\mathbb P(A=1,E=0,\text{ at least one assigned})
\le2\lambda\beta(A).
\]
This is exactly the single-threshold form of the separating-pivot estimate in Yim's Proposition 4.2.

**Unassigned false negative.** Put
\[
q(x)=\mathbb P_Z(A(x,Z)=1).
\]
After dropping the extra requirement that \(Y\) is also unassigned, Poisson thinning gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P(A(X,Y)=1,\ X\text{ and }Y\text{ unassigned})\\
&\qquad\le \mathbb E_X\bigl[q(X)e^{-\lambda q(X)}\bigr]
\le\sup_{q\ge0}qe^{-\lambda q}
=\frac1{e\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the expected disagreement of the random clustering is bounded by
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm piv}\mathbb P(A(X,Y)\ne E(X,Y))
\le3\lambda\beta(A)+\frac1{e\lambda}.
\]
Some realization therefore satisfies the same bound. If \(\beta(A)>0\), choosing
\[
\lambda=(3e\beta(A))^{-1/2}
\]
gives
\[
\operatorname{Clust}(A)
\le2\sqrt{\frac3e}\sqrt{\beta(A)}.
\]
If \(\beta(A)=0\), letting \(\lambda\to\infty\) gives \(\operatorname{Clust}(A)=0\). Combining this with the identities from the first two sections proves
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)
\le2\sqrt{\frac3e}\sqrt{b\operatorname{Hyp}(s)}.
\]

### 4. Sharpness of the exponent

Yim's Proposition 5.1 is already binary-valued. On an atomless space partitioned into equal-mass sets
\(U_1,V_1,\dots,U_m,V_m\), it puts similarity \(b\) exactly on \(U_i\times V_i\), \(V_i\times U_i\), and the diagonal, and zero elsewhere. Yim proves
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s_m)=\frac b{2m}
=\sqrt{b\operatorname{Hyp}(s_m)}.
\]
Thus the exponent \(1/2\) achieved above cannot be increased even within the binary subclass. This family also shows that any universal constant in the square-root binary estimate must be at least \(1\).

### 5. Finite graph corollary

Let \(G\) be a simple graph on \(n\) vertices, use the uniform probability measure, and set \(A(x,x)=1\) while for distinct vertices \(A(x,y)=1\) exactly when \(xy\) is an edge. A finite-core equivalence relation is simply a vertex partition. If \(k(G)\) is the minimum number of unordered off-diagonal pair edits needed to make \(G\) a cluster graph, then
\[
\operatorname{Clust}(A)=\frac{2k(G)}{n^2}.
\]
Every bad ordered triple consists of an induced \(P_3\), with \(Z\) its middle vertex and the two endpoints ordered in two ways. Hence
\[
\beta(A)=\frac{2p_3(G)}{n^3}.
\]
Substitution into the clustering bound yields
\[
\frac{2k(G)}{n^2}
\le2\sqrt{\frac3e}\sqrt{\frac{2p_3(G)}{n^3}},
\]
which rearranges to
\[
p_3(G)\ge\frac e6\frac{k(G)^2}{n}.
\]

The exponent is sharp in order. Let \(G\) be the disjoint union of \(m\) copies of \(K_{q,q}\). Then
\[
n=2mq,\qquad
p_3(G)=mq^2(q-1),\qquad
k(G)=mq(q-1).
\]
For one \(K_{q,q}\), if a cluster contains \(a_i\) vertices from one side and \(b_i\) from the other, the disagreement count is
\[
q^2-q+\frac12\sum_i(a_i-b_i)^2\ge q(q-1),
\]
and equality is attained, proving the displayed editing distance. Consequently
\[
\frac{p_3(G)}{k(G)^2/n}=\frac{2q}{q-1}\to2.
\]
Taking \(m,q\to\infty\) shows that a bound of order \(\varepsilon^2n^3\) for graphs at edit distance \(\varepsilon n^2\) cannot in general be improved to a higher power of \(\varepsilon\).

## Relation to prior work

Chatterjee and Sloman introduced average Gromov hyperbolicity and proved a qualitative implication from small average hyperbolicity to small mean tree-approximation error. Yim (arXiv:2609.18023v2, 17 September 2026) gave the first explicit general bound
\[
\operatorname{Tree}(s)\le(63/e)^{1/3}b^{2/3}\operatorname{Hyp}(s)^{1/3},
\]
using a multilevel pivot construction inspired by Ailon--Charikar--Newman's correlation-clustering algorithm. Yim also exhibited the binary family above, proving that exponent greater than \(1/2\) is impossible, and explicitly asked whether the square-root bound holds in the full framework.

The result here settles that exponent question on the natural two-valued subclass. The reason the exponent improves is structural: a binary similarity has only one nontrivial threshold. The common-refinement loss across many threshold clusterings, which is the obstruction identified in Yim's discussion, disappears completely. The exact identity \(\operatorname{Tree}(s)=b\operatorname{Clust}(A)\) makes this one-level reduction explicit, while the single-threshold form of Yim's pivot estimates yields the square-root rate.

Correlation clustering and cluster editing have extensive prior literatures. Bad triangles are standard obstructions, and cluster graphs are exactly the induced-\(P_3\)-free graphs. Targeted searches across correlation clustering, cluster editing, induced-\(P_3\) removal, binary/two-valued average hyperbolicity, and synonymous tree-fitting formulations did not locate the probability-space identity above, the displayed square-root binary theorem, or the explicit consequence \(p_3(G)\ge(e/6)k(G)^2/n\).

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to reflexive two-valued similarities. It does not close Yim's exponent gap for general multilevel similarities, where enforcing nested threshold partitions remains the central obstacle.

The constant \(2\sqrt{3/e}\) is not claimed optimal. Yim's example forces the optimal constant to be at least \(1\), while the present argument gives at most \(2\sqrt{3/e}\). The induced-\(P_3\) constant \(e/6\) is likewise not claimed sharp; the disjoint-\(K_{q,q}\) family only establishes sharpness of the quadratic order.

The motivating quantitative tree-likeness preprint is very recent, and correlation-clustering/cluster-editing literature is broad. Although targeted searches found no matching theorem, unindexed or differently phrased prior work remains a residual originality risk.
