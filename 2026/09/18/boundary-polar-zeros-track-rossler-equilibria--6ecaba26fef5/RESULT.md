# Boundary polar zeros track equilibria in a Rössler zero-Hopf unfolding

## Result

Consider the classical Rössler system
\[
\dot x=-y-z,\qquad
\dot y=x+ay,\qquad
\dot z=b+z(x-c),
\]
with the perturbation used in Theorem 2.3 of Llibre--Szumiński (arXiv:2609.17336v1),
\[
b=a+\varepsilon^2 b_1,\qquad
c=2a+\varepsilon^2 c_1,\qquad
|a|<\sqrt2,\qquad a(c_1-b_1)>0.
\]
Write \(\omega=\sqrt{2-a^2}\).

That paper transforms the zero-Hopf point to the origin, introduces cylindrical coordinates
\[
u=r\cos\theta,\qquad v=r\sin\theta,
\]
then blows up \(r=\varepsilon R,\ w=\varepsilon W\). Its first averaged field has three simple zeros
\[
P_0=\left(\sqrt2\,\omega\sqrt{\frac{c_1-b_1}{a}},0\right),\qquad
P_\pm=\left(0,\ \pm\omega^2\sqrt{\frac{c_1-b_1}{a^3}}\right).
\]
The source applies its first-order averaging theorem to all three and concludes that they generate three periodic-orbit families.

The two boundary zeros \(P_\pm\) cannot be certified this way. More precisely:

**Proposition.** Under the same assumptions, the perturbed Rössler system has two exact equilibria \(E_\pm(\varepsilon)\). In the source's blown-up coordinates they lie exactly on the polar axis \(R=0\), and
\[
(R,W)(E_+(\varepsilon))\longrightarrow P_-,\qquad
(R,W)(E_-(\varepsilon))\longrightarrow P_+
\quad(\varepsilon\to0^+).
\]
At \(R=0\) the angular coordinate is undefined, and the \(\theta\)-reparametrized system does not satisfy the open-neighbourhood smoothness assumptions of the first-order averaging theorem quoted in the source. The polynomial extension of the *averaged field* across \(R=0\) does not extend the underlying polar-coordinate dynamics.

Consequently, the displayed first-order averaging argument rigorously certifies the positive-radius family attached to \(P_0\), but it does not establish the two periodic families attributed to \(P_\pm\). This does **not** prove that additional nearby periodic orbits do not exist; it identifies a failure of the stated proof and of the claimed boundary-zero correspondence.

## Exact identification of the boundary zeros

An equilibrium satisfies
\[
y=-z,\qquad x=az,\qquad az^2-cz+b=0.
\]
Hence
\[
z_\pm(\varepsilon)
=
\frac{2a+\varepsilon^2c_1
\pm \varepsilon\sqrt{4a(c_1-b_1)+\varepsilon^2c_1^2}}{2a},
\]
and the discriminant is positive for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\) because
\(a(c_1-b_1)>0\).

After translating \((a,-1,1)\) to the origin, the equilibrium coordinates are
\[
(X,Y,Z)=\bigl(a(z-1),-(z-1),z-1\bigr).
\]
The linear transformation used in the source has, on its \(w\)-axis,
\[
X=-\frac{a^2}{\omega^2}w,\qquad
Y=\frac{a}{\omega^2}w,\qquad
Z=-\frac{a}{\omega^2}w.
\]
Therefore every perturbed equilibrium above is represented **exactly** by
\[
u=v=0,\qquad
w=-\frac{\omega^2}{a}(z-1).
\]
Thus \(r=R=0\) exactly. With \(W=w/\varepsilon\),
\[
W_\pm^{\rm eq}(\varepsilon)
=
-\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon c_1}{2a^2}
\mp
\frac{\omega^2}{2a^2}
\sqrt{4a(c_1-b_1)+\varepsilon^2c_1^2}.
\]
Since
\[
\sqrt{\frac{c_1-b_1}{a^3}}
=
\frac{\sqrt{a(c_1-b_1)}}{a^2},
\]
the limit is
\[
W_\pm^{\rm eq}(\varepsilon)
\longrightarrow
\mp\omega^2\sqrt{\frac{c_1-b_1}{a^3}}.
\]
These are precisely the \(W\)-coordinates of \(P_\mp\).

So the two radius-zero zeros are not merely close to the equilibrium splitting: they are the leading blown-up locations of the two exact equilibrium branches generated by the same unfolding.

## Why the angular averaging theorem is singular there

The transformed equations have the form
\[
\dot u=-\omega v+F_1(u,v,w,\varepsilon),\qquad
\dot v=\omega u+F_2(u,v,w,\varepsilon),
\]
with \(F_i=O(\|(u,v,w)\|^2+\varepsilon^2)\). For \(r>0\),
\[
\dot\theta
=
\frac{u\dot v-v\dot u}{r^2}
=
\omega+\frac{uF_2-vF_1}{r^2}.
\]
After \(u=\varepsilon R\cos\theta\), \(v=\varepsilon R\sin\theta\), \(w=\varepsilon W\), the correction is generically of size
\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{R}.
\]
It is therefore not \(O(\varepsilon)\) uniformly on any open neighbourhood of a point with \(R=0\).

One can see the singularity directly from the source's transformation. At \(u=v=0,\ w=\varepsilon W\),
\[
F_1(0,0,\varepsilon W,\varepsilon)
=
-\frac{a\varepsilon^2}{\omega^4}
\left(a^3W^2-\omega^4(c_1-b_1)\right)
+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
For \(W\) arbitrarily close to, but different from, either boundary root, the displayed coefficient is nonzero. Taking \(\theta=\pi/2\) and \(R>0\) gives
\[
\dot\theta
=
\omega-\frac{F_1}{\varepsilon R},
\]
so the angular correction contains a nonremovable \(\varepsilon/R\) term as \(R\downarrow0\).

The first-order averaging theorem quoted in arXiv:2609.17336v1 assumes an open domain and \(C^2\) periodic vector fields there. The source obtains its reduced system only after taking \(\theta\) as the independent variable. That operation is valid in a positive-radius chart, but \(R=0\) is exactly where the chart collapses and \(\theta\) ceases to exist.

The fact that the averaged polynomial
\[
g_1(R,W)=\frac{a^4}{2\omega^5}RW,\qquad
g_2(R,W)=
-\frac{R^2}{2\omega^3}
-\frac{a^2W^2}{\omega^5}
+\frac{c_1-b_1}{a\omega}
\]
extends analytically to negative \(R\) does not repair this: the full \(\theta\)-reparametrized differential equation, including its remainder and derivatives, has no corresponding regular extension through the polar axis.

This distinction is also visible in earlier Rössler zero-Hopf averaging work, where the periodic-orbit certificate is attached to an averaged zero with strictly positive radial coordinate \(r_*>0\).

## The numerical section does not validate the two boundary branches

For the parameter example used in arXiv:2609.17336v1,
\[
a=1,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{500},\qquad
b_1=1,\qquad
c_1=2,
\]
we have \(\omega=1\) and
\[
P_+=(0,1),\qquad P_-=(0,-1).
\]
The two exact equilibria are
\[
z_{\rm low}=0.998003996000008\ldots,\qquad
z_{\rm high}=1.002004003999992\ldots,
\]
with \((x,y,z)=(z,-z,z)\). Their blown-up coordinates are
\[
(R,W)=(0,\,0.9980019999959\ldots),
\qquad
(R,W)=(0,\,-1.0020019999958\ldots),
\]
which converge to \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), respectively.

The source's leading \(P_\pm\) points at \(\theta=0\),
\[
(1-\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon),\qquad
(1+\varepsilon,-1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon),
\]
are only \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) from these exact equilibria.

By contrast, the adjusted initial data printed in Eq. (30) of the source,
\[
(1-2\varepsilon,-1+\varepsilon,1-2\varepsilon),\qquad
(1+2\varepsilon,-1-\varepsilon,1+2\varepsilon),
\]
have, under the same inverse linear transformation,
\[
(R,W,\theta)=
(1,1,-\pi/2),\qquad
(1,-1,\pi/2).
\]
Thus they are an \(O(1)\) radial distance from \(P_\pm\) in the very blown-up variables used by the averaging theorem. An \(O(\varepsilon)\) adjustment in the original variables is not a small adjustment after the \(r=\varepsilon R\) blow-up.

The numerical shooting reported in the source may still locate genuine periodic orbits at that parameter value. But those computations, as presented, do not verify periodic families satisfying
\[
(R(0,\varepsilon),W(0,\varepsilon))
=
P_\pm+O(\varepsilon),
\]
which is the asymptotic correspondence asserted in the proof.

## Consequences

1. The positive-radius zero \(P_0\) is unaffected by this objection; it lies in a regular polar chart and remains a valid candidate for the source's first-order averaging theorem.

2. The existence statements for the two families \(\Gamma_{P_+,\varepsilon}\) and \(\Gamma_{P_-,\varepsilon}\) are not established by the displayed first-order averaging proof.

3. The analytic parts of the subsequent \(C^1\)-non-integrability theorem that rely specifically on those two families inherit the same gap. If those periodic orbits are established by another argument, their integrability consequences can be reconsidered separately.

4. No nonexistence theorem for the two additional periodic orbits is claimed here. A regular Cartesian blow-up, a different desingularization, higher-order analysis, or a validated continuation argument could in principle establish additional families.

## Reproducibility

`artifacts/check_boundary_zeros.py` uses only the Python standard library. It evaluates the exact equilibrium roots for the source's parameter example, maps them into the source's blown-up coordinates, checks the \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) match to the boundary-zero leading points, and maps the Eq. (30) numerical seeds. It was executed for the values above; the output gives \(R=0\) for both exact equilibria and \(R=1\) for both Eq. (30) boundary-family seeds.
