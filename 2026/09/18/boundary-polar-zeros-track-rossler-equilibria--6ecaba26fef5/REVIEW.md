## Correctness — PASS

The source studies the classical Rössler system near a zero-Hopf point under
\[
b=a+\varepsilon^2b_1,\qquad c=2a+\varepsilon^2c_1,
\]
with \(|a|<\sqrt2\) and \(a(c_1-b_1)>0\). Its first averaged field in blown-up polar variables has one zero with positive radius, \(P_0\), and two zeros \(P_\pm\) with \(R=0\).

The exact equilibrium equations are
\[
y=-z,\qquad x=az,\qquad az^2-cz+b=0.
\]
For the stated unfolding the quadratic discriminant is
\[
\varepsilon^2\bigl(4a(c_1-b_1)+\varepsilon^2c_1^2\bigr)>0
\]
for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), so there are two exact equilibrium branches. Substitution into the linear coordinates printed in the source shows that both branches satisfy \(u=v=0\) exactly and hence \(r=R=0\). Their scaled \(W\)-coordinates converge to the two values appearing in \(P_\pm\). The identification is algebraic and does not rely on numerical continuation.

The source obtains the averaged system after introducing polar coordinates \(u=r\cos\theta\), \(v=r\sin\theta\) and taking \(\theta\) as the independent variable. At \(r=0\), \(\theta\) is undefined. Moreover, for
\[
\dot u=-\omega v+F_1,\qquad \dot v=\omega u+F_2,
\]
one has for \(r>0\)
\[
\dot\theta=\omega+\frac{uF_2-vF_1}{r^2}.
\]
After \(r=\varepsilon R\), the correction is generically of order \(\varepsilon/R\), not uniformly \(O(\varepsilon)\) near \(R=0\). Direct substitution in the source's transformation gives
\[
F_1(0,0,\varepsilon W,\varepsilon)
=-\frac{a\varepsilon^2}{\omega^4}
\left(a^3W^2-\omega^4(c_1-b_1)\right)+O(\varepsilon^3),
\]
so the singularity is present arbitrarily close to either boundary root. The source's quoted first-order averaging theorem requires an open domain and the corresponding smooth periodic vector field; extending only the averaged polynomial across \(R=0\) does not extend the polar-coordinate differential equation or its remainder. The conclusion is therefore limited to a proof gap at the two boundary zeros, not a nonexistence theorem for periodic orbits.

The numerical example was also checked algebraically. For \(a=1\), \(\varepsilon=1/500\), \(b_1=1\), \(c_1=2\), the exact equilibrium branches have \(R=0\) and \(W\) tending to \(\pm1\). The two initial points printed in Eq. (30) of the source instead map to \(R=1\), \(W=\pm1\). Thus they are not small perturbations of \(P_\pm\) in the blown-up variables used by the averaging theorem. The compact verification script reproduces these coordinate calculations using only the Python standard library.

The sign and branch correspondence were checked explicitly: the upper exact equilibrium branch tends to the negative-\(W\) averaged zero, while the lower branch tends to the positive-\(W\) zero. The positive-radius root \(P_0\) is not affected by the objection.

## Originality — PASS, to the best of our knowledge

The novelty claim is deliberately narrow. Polar-coordinate singularities at zero radius, first-order averaging theory, zero-Hopf bifurcation methods, and the existence of earlier Rössler zero-Hopf periodic-orbit results are prior mathematics and are not claimed as new.

The claim assessed for originality is the source-specific observation that the two radius-zero averaged zeros in arXiv:2609.17336v1 are exactly the blown-up limits of the two equilibrium branches generated by the same unfolding, together with the resulting failure of the stated angular averaging certificate and the scaled-coordinate mismatch in the source's numerical seeds.

Accordingly the originality assessment is only to the best of our knowledge.

## Value — PASS

The distinction affects two of the three periodic-orbit certificates in the source's principal zero-Hopf theorem. It also separates the unaffected positive-radius branch from the two boundary branches that require a different existence argument, and it identifies why the presented numerical seeds do not validate the claimed asymptotic correspondence in the scaled variables. Any later non-integrability conclusions that specifically require those two periodic families likewise require an independent existence justification.

## Limitations

This record does not prove that the two additional nearby periodic orbits are absent. A regular Cartesian blow-up, another desingularization, higher-order analysis, or validated continuation could in principle establish such families. It also does not challenge the positive-radius \(P_0\) branch. The source is a recent preprint and may subsequently be revised.
