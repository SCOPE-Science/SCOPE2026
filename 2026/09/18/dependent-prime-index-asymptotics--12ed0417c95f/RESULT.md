# Finite-prime perturbations and the multiplicatively dependent prime-index case

## Statement

Let \(p_n\) denote the \(n\)-th prime. Fix an integer \(m\ge 2\), and put
\[
\mathcal P_m=\{p_{m^e}:e\ge0\}.
\]
Let
\[
M_m(x)=\#\{n\le x:\text{ every prime divisor of }n\text{ belongs to }\mathcal P_m\},
\]
with the empty product \(1\) included. Thus \(M_m(x)\) differs by at most the harmless convention at \(1\) from the one-generator counting function studied by Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann.

Set
\[
\kappa_m=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6\log m}}.
\]
Let \(D\subset \mathcal P_m\) and \(E\) be finite sets of primes, with \(E\cap\mathcal P_m=\varnothing\), and let \(M_{m;D,E}(x)\) count positive integers \(n\le x\) all of whose prime factors lie in
\[
(\mathcal P_m\setminus D)\cup E.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
M_{m;D,E}(x)
\sim
\kappa_m^{\,|D|-|E|}
\frac{\prod_{p\in D}\log p}{\prod_{q\in E}\log q}
(\log x)^{(|E|-|D|)/2}
M_m(x).
}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, a finite deletion of \(g\) prime generators costs exactly a factor \((\log x)^{-g/2}\), while a finite addition contributes \((\log x)^{1/2}\) per added prime; the locations of the perturbations affect only the explicit multiplicative constant.

This transfer principle completes the multiplicatively dependent case omitted from the recent two-generator theorem of Golafshan. Suppose \(r,s\ge2\) are multiplicatively dependent. Then there are unique data consisting of an integer \(m\ge2\) and coprime positive integers \(u,v\), up to interchanging \(u,v\), such that
\[
r=m^u,\qquad s=m^v,
\]
with \(m\) chosen primitive. Let
\[
H=\langle u,v\rangle=\{ua+vb:a,b\ge0\},\qquad
G=\mathbb N_0\setminus H.
\]
Since \((u,v)=1\), \(G\) is finite and the classical Sylvester genus formula gives
\[
g:=|G|=\frac{(u-1)(v-1)}2.
\]
If \(C_{r,s}(x)\) denotes the number of integers \(n\le x\) whose prime factors all have the form \(p_{r^a s^b}\), then
\[
\boxed{
C_{r,s}(x)
\sim
K_{m;u,v}(\log x)^{-\frac{(u-1)(v-1)}4}M_m(x),
}
\tag{2}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
K_{m;u,v}
=
\left(\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6\log m}}\right)^{\frac{(u-1)(v-1)}2}
\prod_{e\notin\langle u,v\rangle}\log p_{m^e}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The product in (3) is finite. Combining (2) with the explicit strong asymptotic of Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann for \(M_m(x)\) therefore gives an explicit strong asymptotic, including the multiplicative constant, for every multiplicatively dependent pair \((r,s)\).

## Proof of the finite-perturbation theorem

Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann treat the additive partition problem with weights
\[
\lambda_e=\log p_{m^e}\qquad(e\ge0).
\]
Let
\[
N(t)=\#\{e:\lambda_e\le t\}.
\]
Their Lemma 3 proves
\[
\int_0^U\frac{N(t)}t\,dt
=
AU+B(\log U)^2+C\log U+D+o(1),
\tag{4}
\]
with
\[
A=\frac1{\log m},\qquad
B=-\frac1{2\log m},\qquad
C=\frac12,
\]
and an explicit constant \(D=D_m\). Their Corollary 1 is an Ingham-type theorem converting (4) into a strong asymptotic for the cumulative partition count. In the case \(A=1/\log m\), the saddle scale appearing there is
\[
\kappa_m=\pi\sqrt{\frac A6}=\frac\pi{\sqrt{6\log m}}.
\]

Now modify the weight set by deleting the weights \(\log p\) for \(p\in D\) and adding the weights \(\log q\) for \(q\in E\). For all sufficiently large \(U\), the new counting function \(N_{D,E}\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^U\frac{N_{D,E}(t)}t\,dt
&=
\int_0^U\frac{N(t)}t\,dt
-\sum_{p\in D}\int_{\log p}^{U}\frac{dt}{t}
+\sum_{q\in E}\int_{\log q}^{U}\frac{dt}{t}\\
&=AU+B(\log U)^2
+\bigl(C+|E|-|D|\bigr)\log U\\
&\quad+D+\sum_{p\in D}\log\log p-
\sum_{q\in E}\log\log q+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(A\) and \(B\) are unchanged. In Corollary 1 of Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann, changing the coefficient of \(\log U\) from \(C\) to \(C+\Delta\) multiplies the asymptotic by
\[
\kappa_m^{-\Delta}U^{\Delta/2},
\]
while changing the constant term by \(\Delta D\) multiplies it by \(e^{\Delta D}\). Here
\[
\Delta=|E|-|D|,
\qquad
e^{\Delta D}
=
\frac{\prod_{p\in D}\log p}{\prod_{q\in E}\log q}.
\]
Substituting \(U=\log x\) proves (1). Unique prime factorization identifies the additive partition count with the integer count exactly (apart from the immaterial convention for the empty product).

## Proof of the dependent two-generator corollary

If \(r\) and \(s\) are multiplicatively dependent, choose coprime positive integers \(A,B\) with \(r^A=s^B\). Comparing prime-adic valuations gives
\[
A\,v_p(r)=B\,v_p(s)
\]
for every prime \(p\). Since \((A,B)=1\), \(B\mid v_p(r)\) and \(A\mid v_p(s)\). Hence, with
\[
m=\prod_p p^{v_p(r)/B},
\]
we have \(r=m^B\) and \(s=m^A\). After taking the primitive common base this yields coprime exponents \(u,v\).

The distinct indices generated by \(r^a s^b\) are therefore exactly
\[
\{m^{ua+vb}:a,b\ge0\}=\{m^e:e\in\langle u,v\rangle\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\{p_{r^a s^b}:a,b\ge0\}
=
\mathcal P_m\setminus\{p_{m^e}:e\in G\}.
\]
Apply (1) with \(E=\varnothing\) and
\[
D=\{p_{m^e}:e\in G\}.
\]
The Sylvester formula \(|G|=(u-1)(v-1)/2\) then gives (2)--(3).

## Examples and edge cases

- \((r,s)=(2,4)\): \(m=2\), \((u,v)=(1,2)\), so \(G=\varnothing\). The supposedly two-generator family is exactly the one-generator family, agreeing with the repeated-index example singled out by Golafshan.
- \((r,s)=(4,8)\): \(m=2\), \((u,v)=(2,3)\), and \(G=\{1\}\). Hence
  \[
  C_{4,8}(x)\sim
  \frac{\pi\log3}{\sqrt{6\log2}}(\log x)^{-1/2}M_2(x).
  \]
- \((r,s)=(8,32)\): \(m=2\), \((u,v)=(3,5)\), and
  \(G=\{1,2,4,7\}\). Thus the penalty is \((\log x)^{-2}\), with the constant predicted by (3).
- If \(u=1\) or \(v=1\), there are no gaps and (2) reduces identically to the one-generator problem.

The accompanying verification artifact computes numerical-semigroup gap sets for representative coprime pairs, checks the Sylvester genus formula, and evaluates the constants in the displayed examples. It is supporting arithmetic verification; the theorem itself is proved above.

## Literature context and limitations

Golafshan's 2026 two-generator theorem assumes that \(r\) and \(s\) are multiplicatively independent. The paper explicitly explains that this hypothesis is needed so distinct pairs \((a,b)\) give distinct indices and gives \((2,4)\) as a non-allowed repeated-index example. The result above treats the complementary multiplicatively dependent regime by observing that its distinct exponent set is a cofinite two-generator numerical semigroup and then transferring the one-generator strong asymptotic through a finite-prime perturbation.

Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann (2014) provide the one-generator strong asymptotic and the extended Ingham theorem used here. Their theorem is sufficiently sensitive to the \(\log U\) and constant terms in (4) to make the finite-perturbation factor explicit.

Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge. Exact and synonymous searches for finite deletions/additions of the prime-index family \(\{p_{m^e}\}\), numerical-semigroup exponent holes, and the multiplicatively dependent \(p_{r^a s^b}\) case did not locate (1)--(3). A residual risk is a 2017 seminar announcement by Lenny Neyt, cited by Golafshan, concerning strong asymptotics for multiplicatively generated prime-index sets. Golafshan states that no complete proof of the two-generator case subsequently appeared in a published article; a complete source establishing the dependent rank-one formula above was not identified. Because the announcement's full mathematical scope was not available for inspection, it remains the most relevant originality uncertainty.

No claim is made about a uniform asymptotic when \(m,u,v\) vary with \(x\). The result keeps all generators and finite perturbations fixed.
