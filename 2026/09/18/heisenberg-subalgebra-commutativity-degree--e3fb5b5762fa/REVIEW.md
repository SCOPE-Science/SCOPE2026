# Same-model review

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.

## Correctness

PASS. Every subalgebra of H_m(q)=V+F_q z either contains z, in which case it is U+F_q z for an arbitrary subspace U of V, or avoids z, in which case it is the graph of a linear functional on a totally isotropic subspace. For two graph subalgebras, their sum contains z exactly when the two functionals disagree on the intersection; otherwise permutability is equivalent to mutual symplectic orthogonality. Möbius inversion in the subspace lattice gives the stated disjoint-isotropic count D, and quotienting by the intersection gives the exact pair count E_m. The m=1 specialization agrees identically with the published formula in arXiv:2609.19086v1. A direct exhaustive enumeration of H_2(F_2) gives 158 Lie subalgebras and 18,964 permuting ordered pairs, agreeing with the formula. The asymptotic transition follows from the degrees of Gaussian-binomial and isotropic-Grassmannian counts; for m>=4 the z-containing stratum strictly dominates, while the m=3 leading terms are 3q^9 for the subalgebra count and 4q^18 for the nonpermuting-pair count.

## Originality

PASS, to the best of our knowledge. arXiv:2609.19086v1 defines h(m), explicitly asks for sd(h(m)), and proves an exact Heisenberg formula only for m=1. Targeted searches for higher Heisenberg subalgebra commutativity degree, modular/permutable subalgebra pairs, extraspecial p-groups, and equivalent symplectic-subspace formulations found no general m formula or the large-field 0,0,5/9,1 transition. Shen--Voll arXiv:2605.23003 enumerates finite-index subalgebras of higher Heisenberg Lie lattices over compact DVRs, a different invariant. The element commutativity degree of H(m) is known but samples elements rather than subalgebras. The full text of Tărnăuceanu, J. Algebra 321 (2009), 2508--2520, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2009.02.010, was not inspected; because it studies subgroup commutativity degrees broadly, it remains the most plausible inaccessible source for an equivalent odd-prime extraspecial-group special case. Available descriptions and targeted searches did not reveal such coverage. This is a residual originality risk, not evidence of prior coverage.

## Value

PASS. The result answers, for all m and all finite fields, the higher-Heisenberg question posed by the paper that introduced the invariant, while replacing a dimension-specific counting argument by a uniform symplectic mechanism. It yields a compact exact formula, a closed m=2 expression, an extraspecial-group corollary for odd primes through the source paper's Lazard theorem, and a qualitative phase transition that is invisible in the m=1 case: asymptotically almost no pairs permute for ranks 1 and 2, exactly 5/9 do at rank 3, and almost every pair permutes for every rank at least 4.
