# Same-model review

Same-model review: passed. Cross-model review: not yet performed.

## Correctness

PASS. The classification uses only the rank of the F-linear map t on the two-dimensional field E. Rank two forces t(x)=t(1)x by surjectivity and the defining trace axiom. Rank one forces the image line to equal F: if z=t(u) is nonzero, then t(zu)=z^2 lies both in Fz and equals z^2, whence z∈F. Conversely every F-linear functional E→F satisfies t(t(a)b)=t(a)t(b). The standard trace-pairing theorem then separates the cases: for separable E/F the field-trace pairing identifies E with E-dual, while for inseparable E/F the field trace is identically zero. The explicit extension F_2(s)⊂F_2(s^{1/2}) and coefficient functional provide a direct counterexample to the source statement.

## Originality

PASS, to the best of our knowledge. The field-theoretic fact that the trace pairing is nondegenerate exactly for separable extensions is classical prior knowledge and is not claimed. The novelty claim is restricted to applying this obstruction to Lemma 7 and Proposition 11 of arXiv:2609.19797v1, giving the exact replacement family Hom_F(E,F)\{0}, its isomorphism criterion, and an explicit omitted tr-prime algebra. Targeted searches for the paper identifier/title together with inseparable, correction, erratum, and quadratic trace-map terminology found no public correction or equivalent discussion. The preprint is extremely recent and may be revised, so independent or subsequent discovery remains a substantial residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The source explicitly advertises an arbitrary-field classification, and the omitted case occurs for every purely inseparable quadratic extension in characteristic two. The correction is exact rather than merely counterexample-based: it replaces the failed field-trace parametrization by the full dual space, identifies the separability hypothesis under which the published statement is valid, and shows that distinct functionals are pairwise nonisomorphic in the purely inseparable case. At the same time it sharply limits the damage by explaining why the finite-field portion of the paper is not directly contradicted.
