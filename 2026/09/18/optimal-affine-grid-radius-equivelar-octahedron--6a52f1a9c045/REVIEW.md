# Review

Same-model review: passed. Cross-model review: not yet performed.

## Correctness

The source paper was inspected at its theorem, complete integer coordinate table, face walks, supporting planes, geometric verification, symmetry map, and limitations. The published vertex data and face incidences were rechecked exactly.

For the affine-radius theorem, the difference lattice generated by the 23 vectors \(v_i-v_1\) has index 60. The displayed matrix \(B\) also has determinant 60, while \(B^{-1}v_i\) is integral for every published vertex; hence the difference lattice is exactly \(B\mathbb Z^3\). This makes the description of every possible integer-valued coordinate covector as \(q^TB^{-1}\), \(q\in\mathbb Z^3\), complete rather than heuristic.

The lower bound is reduced to a finite short-vector statement. Three explicitly selected transformed vertex differences give a nonsingular matrix \(D\), and the maximum absolute row sum of \((D^T)^{-1}\) is exactly \(157/7980\). Therefore any dual vector of width below 180 has integer coefficient vector in \([-3,3]^3\). Exhaustive exact enumeration of this finite box gives only \(\pm(1,0,0)\) below width 168, proving that three independent coordinate rows force at least one width of 168. The explicit affine map has coordinate radius 84, so the bound is sharp.

For the Euclidean-symmetry refinement, the same finite certificate shows that width below 180 permits only five projective dual directions. There are eight independent unordered triples. Exact rational conjugation of Mizhaev's generator \(T(x,y,z)=(y,-x,-z)\) shows that none of those eight triples yields an orthogonal conjugate. The displayed radius-90 map sends all vertices to integers and commutes with \(T\), proving sharpness. Signed row changes and row permutations preserve the orthogonality question, so the finite enumeration covers all low-radius cases.

The standalone verification artifact reproduces the lattice index, transformed vertices and planes, short-covector lists, and symmetry enumeration using exact standard-library arithmetic. Its output was checked successfully.

## Originality

Mizhaev's arXiv:2609.17700 was inspected directly. It gives the integer realization and states in its limitations that the certificate does not establish coordinate minimality. It does not compute the affine difference lattice, its dual widths, an optimal affine integer radius, or a symmetry-preserving optimum.

Searches used the exact paper title and arXiv identifier together with coordinate minimality, affine, lattice, grid, the candidate radii 84 and 90, the author name, and equivalent descriptions of the genus-three equivelar octahedron. No source was found stating the two sharp affine-normalization theorems. Searches of the current SCOPE archive by the same object and claim family also found no overlap.

The principal unresolved literature risk is Mizhaev's 2020 precursor, DOI 10.31219/osf.io/hvtey. Its abstract and bibliographic metadata were inspected, and the 2026 paper identifies the new realization with its variant V2, but a full text was not available from the sources inspected; one accessible repository page explicitly offered only a request-for-full-text route. The older paper could therefore contain related coordinate normalizations. No accessible metadata gives evidence that it contains the difference-lattice optimality argument or the sharp affine radii, so this remains residual rather than affirmative coverage evidence.

Because the motivating exact integer certificate was submitted in September 2026, unindexed or unpublished parallel work is an additional residual originality risk.

## Value

The result turns a newly published integer certificate into a sharp arithmetic-geometric normalization theorem. The improvement from radius 300 to the provably optimal affine radius 84 is substantial, and the proof identifies the precise obstruction: the short-vector geometry of the dual difference lattice. The separate radius-90 theorem quantifies the exact cost of preserving the published order-four symmetry as a Euclidean isometry rather than merely as an affine symmetry.

The method is reusable for other rational or integer polyhedral certificates: compute the difference lattice, pass to its dual, and reduce coordinate optimization to simultaneous lattice-width constraints. The finite inverse-matrix bound also supplies a compact exact certificate rather than relying on a large opaque search.

## Limitations

The theorem is not a global minimization over all geometric realizations of the abstract equivelar map. A non-affinely-equivalent realization may beat radius 84. The symmetry statement is likewise restricted to affine copies of the published realization and the conjugated published \(C_4\) generator. No claim is made about optimal aspect ratio, edge lengths, or Euclidean distortion.

The full text of the 2020 precursor was not inspected, and the recent date of the 2026 certificate leaves residual originality uncertainty from parallel work.
