# Same-model review

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.

## Correctness

The argument was checked separately at the local, global, and identification stages.

Locally, transitivity gives uniqueness of the member through a prescribed point
with prescribed tangent hyperplane. Hence two distinct members cannot be tangent
at a common point. This makes the two incidence equations independent in the
ambient variable and proves that the ordered pair-incidence projection is a
submersion.

Globally, Martins' classification makes the ambient manifold compact and identifies
the parameter space topologically with real projective space. Properness therefore
makes the image of the pair-incidence projection closed, while submersivity makes
it open. The image is nonempty by choosing, at one point of one member, a different
tangent hyperplane and applying transitivity. The ordered two-point configuration
space is connected in the dimensions under consideration, so every distinct pair
intersects. Ehresmann's theorem then makes the projection a locally trivial bundle.

For the fiber type, a nearby member is a normal graph whose first derivative is the
infinitesimal normal variation used by Martins. Proposition 2.6 makes its zero set
regular; Proposition 2.7 identifies that zero set with an equator in the two-sided
case, while Section 2.2 identifies it with a projective hyperplane in the one-sided
case. Stability of regular zero sets gives the nearby intersection, and the bundle
argument propagates the diffeomorphism type to all pairs. Repeating the proper
submersion argument with one member fixed and using isotopy extension gives the
standard embedding type inside that member.

Adversarial checks included the standard equator and projective-hyperplane models,
the one-sided normal-bundle case, connectivity after deleting the diagonal, and the
possibility of tangencies. No step requires minimality.

## Originality

The full text of Martins' 2026 preprint was inspected around the definition of
transitive families, Propositions 2.6 and 2.7, the one-sided construction, Theorems
A and B, and the special one-parameter intersection argument. It supplies the local
nodal classification and a special path of intersections but does not state that
every distinct pair of members intersects, nor that all pairwise intersections form
a bundle over the ordered configuration space.

The earlier Ambrozio--Marques--Neves and Ambrozio--Guajardo papers were searched for
intersection and pairwise-incidence statements; no matching arbitrary-pair theorem
was found. Targeted literature searches used formulations involving transitive
families, Zoll families, pairwise intersections, minimal equators, incidence, and
standard codimension-two intersections. Current SCOPE records were also searched by
source identifier, object, and synonymous claim families, with no overlap found.

LeBrun--Mason's two-dimensional Zoll projective theory is a relevant lower-dimensional
analogy, so the originality claim is restricted to ambient dimension at least three.
No inaccessible paper was identified as a specific likely source of the same theorem.
Residual originality risk remains from differently phrased or not-yet-indexed work,
especially because the principal motivating preprint is very recent.

## Value

The result upgrades Martins' local infinitesimal projective geometry to a global
two-member incidence theorem. It shows that even transitive sphere families that
cannot be globally straightened to round equators retain the standard intersection
topology and isotopy class for every pair. In dimension three it gives a concrete
geometric consequence: any two distinct Zoll minimal surfaces in the family meet in
one transverse embedded circle.

The configuration-space bundle is also a reusable structural object for studying
higher incidence, monodromy, and arrangement invariants.

## Limitations

The theorem controls only pairwise intersections. It does not assert transversality
or a fixed topology for triple and higher intersections, and it does not globally
conjugate the entire family to the linear projective model. It is restricted to
unoriented transitive families of closed embedded hypersurfaces in ambient dimension
at least three. The search establishes originality only to the best of our knowledge.
