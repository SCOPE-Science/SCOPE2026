# A persistent idempotent in every Dunford–Pettis power ideal on \(L^1\)

Let
\[
X=L^1[0,1],\qquad \mathcal D=\operatorname{DP}(X),\qquad
\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{\ell^1}(X),
\]
over the complex field. Here \(\mathcal G\) is the ideal of representable operators; on \(L^1[0,1]\) it is equivalently the ideal of operators factoring through \(\ell^1\). For \(n\ge 1\), set
\[
\mathcal I_n=
\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{T_1\cdots T_n:T_1,\ldots,T_n\in\mathcal D\}.
\]

## Result

There is an infinite-rank projection \(P\in\mathcal D\setminus\mathcal G\) such that
\[
P\in \mathcal I_n\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1.
\]
Consequently, for every \(n\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal G\subsetneq \mathcal I_n\subseteq\mathcal I_2\subsetneq\mathcal D,}
\]
and every \(\mathcal I_n\) is a proper large closed two-sided ideal of
\(\mathcal B(L^1[0,1])\), where “large” means not contained in the strictly
singular operators.

More is true. If
\[
\mathcal J_P=
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{APB:A,B\in\mathcal B(X)\},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_P\subseteq\bigcap_{n\ge2}\mathcal I_n}
\]
and \(\mathcal J_P\) is itself a proper large closed ideal not contained in
\(\mathcal G\).

Equivalently, let
\[
A=\mathcal B(X)/\mathcal G,\qquad D=\mathcal D/\mathcal G
\]
and
\[
D^{[n]}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{d_1\cdots d_n:d_i\in D\}.
\]
Then \(e=P+\mathcal G\) is a nonzero idempotent and
\[
\boxed{e\in\bigcap_{n\ge1}D^{[n]}.}
\]
In particular,
\[
\bigcap_{n\ge1}D^{[n]}\ne\{0\},
\]
indeed it contains the nonzero closed two-sided ideal of \(A\) generated by
\(e\). Thus \(D\) is not a radical Banach algebra.

This does **not** decide whether
\[
D\supsetneq D^{[2]}\supseteq D^{[3]}\supseteq\cdots
\]
is strictly decreasing. It shows that even if strict decrease holds at every
stage, the power chain has a nonzero idempotent core.

## Proof

Acuaviva proved that there is a Dunford–Pettis projection
\[
P:L^1[0,1]\to L^1[0,1]
\]
whose range \(Y=P(X)\) has the Schur property but fails the Radon–Nikodým
property.

We first show that \(P\notin\mathcal G\). Suppose instead that \(P\) factored
through \(\ell^1\):
\[
P=BA,\qquad A:X\to\ell^1,\quad B:\ell^1\to X.
\]
For \(y\in Y\), \(Py=y\), so
\[
y=BAy.
\]
Hence
\[
\|Ay\|\ge \frac{\|y\|}{\|B\|}.
\]
Thus \(A|_Y\) is an isomorphic embedding of \(Y\) onto a closed subspace of
\(\ell^1\). Since \(\ell^1\) has the Radon–Nikodým property and that property
passes to closed subspaces, \(Y\) would have the Radon–Nikodým property, a
contradiction. Therefore
\[
P\in\mathcal D\setminus\mathcal G.
\]

Because \(P^2=P\), one has \(P=P^n\) for every \(n\ge1\). Since every factor
\(P\) lies in \(\mathcal D\),
\[
P\in\mathcal I_n\qquad(n\ge1).
\]

Next, \(\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal I_n\) for every \(n\ge2\). Indeed, choose
bounded maps
\[
U:\ell^1\to X,\qquad V:X\to\ell^1,\qquad VU=I_{\ell^1},
\]
as in the standard complemented copy of \(\ell^1\) in \(L^1[0,1]\).
If \(G=AB\in\mathcal G\), where
\(B:X\to\ell^1\) and \(A:\ell^1\to X\), then for \(n\ge2\)
\[
G=(AV)(UV)^{\,n-2}(UB).
\]
This is a product of \(n\) operators factoring through \(\ell^1\), hence of
\(n\) members of \(\mathcal D\). Therefore \(G\in\mathcal I_n\).

For \(n\ge2\), every \(n\)-fold product of elements of \(\mathcal D\) is also
a product of two elements of \(\mathcal D\): the product of the final
\(n-1\) factors remains in the two-sided ideal \(\mathcal D\). Hence
\[
\mathcal I_n\subseteq\mathcal I_2.
\]
Nasseri proved \(\mathcal I_2\subsetneq\mathcal D\). Since
\(P\in\mathcal I_n\setminus\mathcal G\), this gives
\[
\mathcal G\subsetneq\mathcal I_n\subseteq\mathcal I_2\subsetneq\mathcal D.
\]

The range \(Y\) is infinite-dimensional because finite-dimensional spaces
have the Radon–Nikodým property. The restriction of \(P\) to \(Y\) is the
identity, so \(P\) is not strictly singular. Thus every \(\mathcal I_n\)
containing \(P\) is large.

For the stronger intersection statement, let \(A_0,B_0\in\mathcal B(X)\).
For every \(n\ge2\),
\[
A_0PB_0=(A_0P)\,P^{\,n-2}\,(PB_0).
\]
Each displayed factor is in \(\mathcal D\), because \(\mathcal D\) is a
two-sided operator ideal. Hence \(A_0PB_0\in\mathcal I_n\) for every
\(n\ge2\). Taking linear spans and norm closures gives
\[
\mathcal J_P\subseteq\bigcap_{n\ge2}\mathcal I_n.
\]
It is proper because it is contained in the proper ideal \(\mathcal I_2\),
large because it contains \(P\), and not contained in \(\mathcal G\) because
\(P\notin\mathcal G\).

Finally, since \(\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal I_n\), quotienting gives
\[
\mathcal I_n/\mathcal G=D^{[n]}.
\]
The class \(e=P+\mathcal G\) is nonzero and satisfies \(e^2=e\), so
\(e=e^n\in D^{[n]}\) for every \(n\). Every \(D^{[n]}\) is a closed
two-sided ideal of \(A\), so their intersection contains the closed ideal
generated by \(e\). A radical algebra has no nonzero idempotents, hence \(D\)
is not radical.

## Relation to the recent literature

Nasseri introduced the power ideals associated with the Dunford–Pettis ideal
modulo representable operators and proved
\[
0\ne D^{[2]}\subsetneq D.
\]
He also observed that \(D^{[n]}\ne0\) for every \(n\), using different
convolution powers of a Rajchman measure, and explicitly left open whether the
power chain is strictly decreasing beyond the first step.

Acuaviva, in a separate preprint submitted one day earlier, constructed the
Dunford–Pettis projection used above. Combining the two results changes the
qualitative picture of the power chain: nonvanishing does not merely occur
separately at each order. A single nonzero idempotent, and in fact a single
proper large closed ideal generated by it, survives through every power.

Nasseri's preprint already cites Acuaviva's construction in connection with
other closed ideals, but its discussion of the power chain uses singular
Rajchman convolution powers and does not state the common-idempotent or
nonzero-intersection conclusion above.

## Limitations and originality status

The mathematical deduction is elementary once the two September 2026
preprints are combined. The originality claim is therefore specifically the
synthesis: the persistent nonrepresentable projection, the nonzero
intersection of all closed power ideals, and the resulting large common core.

Searches for combinations of “Dunford–Pettis projection”, “representable
operator”, “power ideal”, “idempotent”, and “Radon–Nikodým” did not locate this
conclusion. Older work of Saab and others studies Dunford–Pettis,
representability, and the Radon–Nikodým property, and may contain general
lemmas closely related to the short factorization argument above. Those
general ingredients are not claimed new.

The result does not prove that the ideals \(\mathcal I_n\) are pairwise
distinct, nor does it settle Nasseri's strict-decrease question.
