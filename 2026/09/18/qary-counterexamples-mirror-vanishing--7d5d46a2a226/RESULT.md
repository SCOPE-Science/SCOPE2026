# Nonbinary counterexamples to the literal mirror-vanishing extension

## Result

Let \(q>2\) be a prime power and let \(d\ge 2\). Put
\[
 n=2d+1,\qquad m=\left\lceil\frac{d}{q-1}\right\rceil,\qquad t=d-m.
\]
Then \(t\ge 1\), and there exists a \(q\)-ary linear code \(C\) with parameters
\[
 [n,2,d]_q=[2d+1,2,d]_q
\]
such that
\[
 A_{d+1}=A_{d+2}=\cdots=A_{d+t}=0,
\qquad
 A_{2d+1}>0.
\]
Moreover
\[
 2=n-2d+1.
\]
Consequently the literal extension to \(q>2\) of the binary mirror-vanishing implication
\[
 k\ge n-2d+1,\quad A_{d+1}=\cdots=A_{d+t}=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 A_{2d+1}=\cdots=A_{2d+t}=0
\]
is false for every nonbinary finite field. For each fixed \(q>2\), the counterexample can have an arbitrarily long initial gap, since \(t=d-\lceil d/(q-1)\rceil\to\infty\) with \(d\).

## Construction and proof

Choose nonzero elements \(c_1,\ldots,c_d\in\mathbb F_q^*\) as evenly as possible among the \(q-1\) nonzero field elements, so that no value occurs more than
\[
 m=\left\lceil\frac{d}{q-1}\right\rceil
\]
times. Define
\[
 u=(1,1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb F_q^{2d+1}
\]
and
\[
 v=(c_1,\ldots,c_d,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{d+1})\in\mathbb F_q^{2d+1},
\]
and let \(C=\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbb F_q}\). The last \(d+1\) coordinates show that \(u\) and \(v\) are independent, so \(\dim C=2\).

Consider a nonzero codeword \(\alpha u+\beta v\).

If \(\alpha=0\), then \(\beta\ne0\) and the word has weight exactly \(d\). Thus the minimum distance is at most \(d\).

Now suppose \(\alpha\ne0\), and write \(r=\beta/\alpha\). The last \(d+1\) coordinates are all equal to \(\alpha\), hence are nonzero. In the first \(d\) coordinates, a zero occurs exactly when
\[
 1+r c_i=0.
\]
If \(r=0\), there are no such zeros. If \(r\ne0\), the zeros are precisely the coordinates for which \(c_i=-r^{-1}\), and therefore there are at most \(m\) of them. Hence every word with \(\alpha\ne0\) has weight at least
\[
 (2d+1)-m=d+t+1>d.
\]
It follows that \(C\) has minimum distance exactly \(d\), and there are no codewords of weights \(d+1,\ldots,d+t\).

Taking \(\beta=0\) and \(\alpha\ne0\) gives the nonzero scalar multiples of \(u\), all of weight \(2d+1\). Hence \(A_{2d+1}\ge q-1>0\). Finally,
\[
 n-2d+1=(2d+1)-2d+1=2=k,
\]
so the dimension hypothesis of the literal mirror extension holds with equality. This proves the counterexample family.

## Exact weight enumerator

Write
\[
 d=a(q-1)+b,\qquad 0\le b<q-1.
\]
In a balanced choice of the \(c_i\), exactly \(b\) nonzero field elements occur \(a+1\) times and the remaining \(q-1-b\) occur \(a\) times. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_C(z)= {}& 1+(q-1)z^d +(q-1)b\,z^{2d-a}\\
& +(q-1)(q-1-b)\,z^{2d+1-a} +(q-1)z^{2d+1},
\end{aligned}
\]
where coefficients of equal powers are combined when exponents coincide.

The smallest instance is the ternary \([5,2,2]_3\) code generated by
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1\\
1&2&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose weight enumerator is
\[
1+2z^2+4z^4+2z^5.
\]
Thus \(A_3=0\) but \(A_5=2\), while \(k=2=n-2d+1\).

## Relation to the 2026 binary mirror theorem

Xianmang He, in *A Mirror Vanishing Band for Weight Distributions of Binary Linear Codes* (arXiv:2609.20344v1, 17 September 2026), proves the binary implication above. The proof uses the binary-only Ashikhmin--Barg property that every non-minimal binary codeword decomposes as a sum of two nonzero codewords with disjoint supports. Section 3.4 of that paper correctly notes that this decomposition fails for larger alphabets and that a nonbinary analogue needs extra conditions. Its MDS discussion, however, concerns codes having every weight from \(d\) to \(n\), so those codes do not satisfy the local-gap hypothesis of the theorem. The family above supplies a genuine counterexample satisfying the literal hypotheses themselves.

The construction also exposes the mechanism behind the binary boundary. For \(q>2\), the nonzero entries \(c_i\) can be split among at least two distinct field values, so a single scalar ratio can cancel only part of the support of \(v\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\), there is only one nonzero value; the corresponding cancellation removes all \(d\) supported coordinates at once and produces a word of weight \(d+1\), violating the assumed local gap. Thus this construction cannot contradict the binary theorem.

## Originality and limitations

The novelty claim is deliberately narrow. The underlying two-dimensional codes are elementary and may be equivalent to previously catalogued small-dimensional codes. The claimed contribution is the all-\(q>2\), all-\(d\ge2\) counterexample family to the literal mirror-vanishing extension, together with an explicit arbitrarily long local gap and the explanation of why the same construction collapses in the binary case.

Searches by the source title and arXiv identifier, by “mirror vanishing” with ternary or q-ary codes, by the parameter family \([2d+1,2,d]_q\), and by the relevant weight-gap formulation did not locate this counterexample or an equivalent published statement. Because the motivating source appeared only on 17 September 2026, simultaneous or not-yet-indexed responses are a material residual originality risk. Older literature on minimal q-ary codes and two-dimensional few-weight codes could also contain an equivalent construction without the mirror-vanishing formulation.

No claim is made that every possible q-ary reformulation fails. In particular, a theorem with a different dimension threshold or additional intersection/minimality hypotheses may still hold.
