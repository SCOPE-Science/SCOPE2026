# A conditional q-ary mirror band for linear-code weight distributions

## Statement

Let \(C\) be a linear \([n,k,d]_q\) code, let \(A_w\) denote the number of codewords of Hamming weight \(w\), and suppose that for some integer \(t\ge 1\),
\[
A_{d+1}=A_{d+2}=\cdots=A_{d+t}=0.
\]
Write
\[
s=n-k+1,\qquad \rho=\left\lceil \frac{d}{q-1}\right\rceil.
\]

If
\[
t\ge s-d,
\]
then
\[
A_w=0
\]
for every integer
\[
\max\{s+1,\,2d+1\}\le w\le d+t+\rho.
\]

In particular, under the same high-rate hypothesis used in the binary mirror theorem,
\[
k\ge n-2d+1\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad s\le 2d,
\]
one obtains the q-ary upper vanishing band
\[
\boxed{
A_w=0\quad\text{for }2d+1\le w\le d+t+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q-1}\right\rceil
}
\]
whenever \(t\ge n-k+1-d\). The interval is nonempty as soon as
\[
t+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q-1}\right\rceil\ge d+1.
\]

For \(q=2\), the endpoint becomes \(2d+t\), matching the binary mirror-band endpoint of He (2026), although the binary theorem does not need the additional long-gap hypothesis \(t\ge n-k+1-d\).

## Proof

Let \(c\in C\) have weight
\[
w>\max\{s,2d\},
\qquad
w\le d+t+\rho.
\]
Because every minimal codeword has weight at most \(s=n-k+1\), \(c\) is non-minimal.

Choose a nonzero codeword \(u\) of inclusion-minimal support among the codewords whose support is a proper subset of \(\operatorname{supp}(c)\). Then \(u\) is a minimal codeword: a nonzero codeword with strictly smaller support than \(u\) would contradict the choice of \(u\). Hence
\[
d\le \operatorname{wt}(u)\le s.
\]
The hypothesis \(t\ge s-d\) gives \(s\le d+t\). Since weights \(d+1,\ldots,d+t\) do not occur, it follows that
\[
\operatorname{wt}(u)=d.
\]

For each \(i\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\), both \(c_i\) and \(u_i\) are nonzero, so the ratio
\[
c_i/u_i
\]
lies in \(\mathbb F_q^\ast\). Among the \(q-1\) possible ratios, one value \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\ast\) occurs at least
\[
\rho=\left\lceil\frac d{q-1}\right\rceil
\]
times. Therefore the nonzero codeword
\[
y=c-\lambda u
\]
has at least \(\rho\) cancellations and satisfies
\[
d\le \operatorname{wt}(y)\le w-\rho\le d+t.
\]
(The vector \(y\) is nonzero because \(u\) is not proportional to \(c\), having proper support.) The same local gap therefore forces
\[
\operatorname{wt}(y)=d.
\]

Finally,
\[
c=y+\lambda u
\]
implies
\[
\operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq
\operatorname{supp}(y)\cup\operatorname{supp}(u),
\]
hence
\[
w=\operatorname{wt}(c)\le \operatorname{wt}(y)+\operatorname{wt}(u)=2d,
\]
contrary to \(w>2d\). This proves the claimed vanishing interval.

## The unmodified q-ary mirror statement is false

The recent binary mirror-band note explains that the binary proof uses disjoint-support decomposition and that an extension to larger alphabets needs extra conditions. Its MDS discussion shows that the local-gap hypothesis is restrictive, but an MDS code does not satisfy that hypothesis. The following family gives a counterexample under the exact binary-style hypotheses.

For every prime power \(q\ge 3\), let \(C_q\) be generated by
\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0&1\\
0&1&1&1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(C_q\) is a \([5,2,2]_q\) code with weight enumerator
\[
\boxed{
1+(q-1)z^2+2(q-1)z^4+(q-1)(q-2)z^5.
}
\]
Indeed, a codeword is
\[
(a,b,b,b,a+b).
\]
If \(b=0\) and \(a\ne0\), its weight is \(2\). If \(a=0\) and \(b\ne0\), its weight is \(4\). If \(a,b\ne0\), the weight is \(4\) when \(a+b=0\) and \(5\) otherwise.

Thus
\[
d=2,\qquad A_3=0,\qquad A_5=(q-1)(q-2)>0,
\]
while
\[
k=2=n-2d+1.
\]
So the direct q-ary analogue of the binary theorem with \(t=1\) would predict \(A_5=0\), and is false for every \(q\ge3\).

## Interpretation

The binary theorem relies on a special decomposition: a non-minimal binary codeword splits into two nonzero codewords with disjoint supports. Over larger alphabets this fails because coordinate ratios can vary.

The proof above replaces disjointness by a universal ratio-pigeonhole fact. Once a minimal covered word has weight \(d\), one scalar multiple cancels at least
\[
\left\lceil d/(q-1)\right\rceil
\]
of its coordinates. A sufficiently long local gap then forces both summands in
\(c=y+\lambda u\) to be minimum-weight words; their union has size at most \(2d\), excluding any \(c\) above \(2d\).

This gives a quantitative q-ary substitute: the binary cancellation amount \(d\) is replaced by the guaranteed q-ary cancellation amount \(\lceil d/(q-1)\rceil\).

## Originality boundary

The following ingredients are classical and are not claimed as new:

- minimal codewords and the Ashikhmin--Barg bound \(\operatorname{wt}(u)\le n-k+1\);
- the binary disjoint-support decomposition of non-minimal codewords;
- pigeonhole cancellation among coordinate ratios.

The new claim is the combination above: a q-ary long-gap mirror band with endpoint
\[
d+t+\left\lceil d/(q-1)\right\rceil,
\]
together with an explicit \([5,2,2]_q\) family showing that the unmodified binary-style q-ary theorem fails under its exact hypotheses.

Searches for q-ary mirror vanishing bands, local gaps near the minimum distance, minimal-codeword support arguments, and equivalent weight-distribution formulations did not locate this statement. Originality is therefore only to the best of our knowledge.

## Limitations

The additional condition
\[
t\ge n-k+1-d
\]
can be strong, and the resulting interval can be empty unless the local gap is sufficiently long. No optimality is claimed for either this hypothesis or the endpoint. The result does not subsume the stronger binary theorem. It also does not determine the exact q-ary boundary between codes with and without a mirror phenomenon.
