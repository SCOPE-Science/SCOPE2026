# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** For \(T_j\in\mathcal S_4\), every quadratic product appearing in a mixed commutator belongs to \(\mathcal S_2\), and every quartic product in the expansion belongs to \(\mathcal S_1\). The trace manipulations therefore have the required ideal-theoretic justification. Expanding \(\sum_{j,k}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{jk}^*C_{jk})\), then using only cyclicity of trace and pairwise commutativity of the \(T_j\)'s (and hence of their adjoints), gives exactly \(\operatorname{Tr}(D^2)\). Since all summands \(\|C_{jk}\|_2^2\) are nonnegative, \(D=0\) forces every mixed adjoint commutator to vanish. For the Schatten-2 sum-hyponormal corollary, \(D\) is trace class, positive, and has trace zero, so \(D=0\). The finite-tracial-von-Neumann-algebra proof is the same algebraic trace calculation; faithfulness is explicitly used when positivity and zero trace imply zero.

## Originality

**PASS, to the best of our knowledge.** The September 2026 paper of Chavan--Reza--Sequeira formulates the sum-normal normality question and proves several special cases, including finite-dimensional normality and structural results for compact sum-hyponormal tuples. The nearby Misra--Pramanick--Sinha work studies the full mixed-commutator matrix through a symmetrized determinant and trace inequalities. Searches using sum-normal, sum-hyponormal, Schatten-4, Hilbert--Schmidt mixed commutators, commutator-matrix Frobenius norm, and finite tracial von Neumann formulations did not locate the exact quartic identity or its stated rigidity consequences. The older commutator-matrix literature is the principal residual coverage risk, and no claim is made that the exponent \(4\) is optimal.

The most relevant older source available through arXiv metadata/abstract was inspected for its stated determinant and trace-inequality results; not every theorem in all historical multivariable commutator literature was exhaustively checked. Accordingly, the originality claim is deliberately qualified rather than absolute.

## Value

**PASS.** The identity converts a newly posed qualitative cancellation problem into an exact sum-of-squares law on a natural operator ideal. It simultaneously gives an affirmative Schatten-4 case of the sum-normal problem, a normality theorem for sum-hyponormal Schatten-2 tuples, a constant-one stability estimate for all mixed adjoint commutators, and a finite-tracial-von-Neumann-algebra analogue.

## Boundaries checked

No complementability or inheritance assertion is used. Compactness alone is not substituted for Schatten summability. The proof does not infer normality from trace cancellation without first using positivity (in the sum-hyponormal case) or the quartic identity (in the sum-normal case). Commutativity is used essentially in the trace rearrangement and is not omitted from any conclusion.
