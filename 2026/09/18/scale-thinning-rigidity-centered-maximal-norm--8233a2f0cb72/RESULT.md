# Scale-thinning rigidity for the one-dimensional centered Hardy--Littlewood maximal norm

## Statement

Let \(1<p<\infty\), write \(p'=p/(p-1)\), and let \(\mathcal R\subset(0,\infty)\) be nonempty. Define the centered maximal operator with prescribed radii
\[
M_{\mathcal R}f(x)=\sup_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac1{2r}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|f(y)|\,dy.
\]
Assume that the available scales have unbounded logarithmic diameter,
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\log\mathcal R)=\infty,
\]
equivalently, \(\inf\mathcal R=0\) or \(\sup\mathcal R=\infty\). Then
\[
\boxed{\ \|M_{\mathcal R}\|_{L^p(\mathbb R)\to L^p(\mathbb R)}=p'.\ }
\]

More strongly, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there are a finite set
\(\mathcal R_\varepsilon\subset\mathcal R\) and a nonzero
\(f_\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) such that
\[
\frac{\|M_{\mathcal R_\varepsilon}f_\varepsilon\|_p}
     {\|f_\varepsilon\|_p}>p'-\varepsilon.
\]
Thus even an arbitrarily irregular or superlacunary collection of radii retains the full sharp strong-type constant as soon as its logarithmic scale range is unbounded.

## Context

Madrid recently proved that the unrestricted one-dimensional centered Hardy--Littlewood maximal operator has exact \(L^p\) norm \(p'\) for every \(1<p<\infty\) [1]. Earlier, Wei--Nie--Wu--Yan proved that restricting the supremum to all radii \(0<r<\gamma\) does not change the operator norm [2]. A detailed public review of [1] additionally reports an exact-norm result for dyadic radii; that geometric special case is therefore treated here as prior coverage rather than as part of the originality claim [3].

The new point is that no regular spacing, bounded gap ratio, or density of scales is required. For example, the conclusion applies to
\[
\mathcal R=\{e^{-e^k}:k\ge1\},\qquad
\mathcal R=\{k!:k\ge1\},
\]
and to arbitrary irregular perturbations or subsequences with the same scale escape.

## Proof

The upper bound is immediate from Madrid's theorem:
\[
M_{\mathcal R}f\le M_cf,
\qquad
\|M_c\|_{p\to p}=p'.
\]
It remains to prove the matching lower bound using only finitely many radii from \(\mathcal R\).

### 1. A finite near-extremal martingale

Fix an integer \(B\ge2\) and put
\[
q=1-\frac1B.
\]
On the uniform product space
\(\Omega=\{0,1,\ldots,B-1\}^n\), let \(\mathcal F_k\) be the filtration generated by the first \(k\) coordinates. For \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), let \(T(a)\) be the number of initial nonzero coordinates before the first zero, capped at \(n\):
\[
T(a)=\min\{j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}:a_{j+1}=0\},
\]
with \(T(a)=n\) if no coordinate is zero. Define
\[
F(a)=q^{-T(a)/p},\qquad F_k=\mathbb E(F\mid\mathcal F_k),
\qquad F^*=\max_{0\le k\le n}F_k.
\]
If the first \(k\) coordinates are nonzero, then
\[
F_k=q^{-k/p}C_{n-k}(q),
\]
where
\[
C_m(q)=(1-q)\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}q^{s/p'}+q^{m/p'}.
\]
Consequently, on the event \(T=j<n\),
\[
F^*\ge q^{-j/p}C_{n-j}(q).
\]
Directly,
\[
\mathbb E F^p=n(1-q)+1.
\]
For any fixed integer \(L\ge1\), summing only the events \(0\le T\le n-L\) gives
\[
\frac{\mathbb E(F^*)^p}{\mathbb E F^p}
\ge
\frac{(n-L+1)(1-q)C_L(q)^p}{n(1-q)+1}.
\]
First let \(n\to\infty\), then \(L\to\infty\). Since
\[
C_L(q)\longrightarrow
C_\infty(q)=\frac{1-q}{1-q^{1/p'}},
\]
and finally
\[
\lim_{B\to\infty}C_\infty(1-1/B)=p',
\]
finite bounded martingales of this product form have
\[
\frac{\|F^*\|_{L^p(\Omega)}}{\|F\|_{L^p(\Omega)}}
\]
arbitrarily close to \(p'\).

### 2. Realization at arbitrary prescribed separated radii

Fix one such \(B,n,F\) and a small \(0<\delta<1/4\). Because
\(\operatorname{diam}(\log\mathcal R)=\infty\), one can choose
\[
\rho_0>\rho_1>\cdots>\rho_n,\qquad \rho_j\in\mathcal R,
\]
so separated that
\[
\frac{\rho_j}{\rho_{j-1}}\le\frac{\delta^2}{B^2}
\qquad(1\le j\le n).
\]
If \(\inf\mathcal R=0\), choose the chain successively downward; if
\(\sup\mathcal R=\infty\), choose it successively upward and then reverse the indexing.

Choose a large period \(S\). For each \(1\le j\le n\), the separation inequality makes it possible to select an integer \(P_j\) satisfying
\[
1+\log_B\frac{S}{\delta\rho_{j-1}}
\le P_j\le
\log_B\frac{\delta S}{\rho_j}.
\]
Taking \(S\) sufficiently large ensures \(P_1\ge1\); the displayed bounds also force
\(P_1<P_2<\cdots<P_n\). They imply
\[
\frac{\rho_jB^{P_j}}S\le\delta,
\qquad
\frac{SB^{1-P_{j+1}}}{\rho_j}\le\delta.
\]

Define periodic base-\(B\) digit functions
\[
d_j(x)=\left\lfloor\frac{B^{P_j}x}{S}\right\rfloor\pmod B
\]
and the \(S\)-periodic function
\[
g(x)=F(d_1(x),\ldots,d_n(x)).
\]
The digit vector is uniformly distributed on one period, hence
\[
\frac1S\int_0^S|g(x)|^p\,dx=\mathbb EF^p.
\]

For \(1\le k\le n\), remove the points within distance \(\rho_k\) of the grid
\(SB^{-P_k}\mathbb Z\). The removed proportion for level \(k\) is at most \(2\delta\), so the common good set \(G\subset[0,S)\) has
\[
|G|\ge(1-2n\delta)S.
\]
At a good point, the first \(k\) digits stay fixed throughout
\([x-\rho_k,x+\rho_k]\). With this prefix fixed, the remaining digit pattern is periodic with period
\[
h_{k+1}=SB^{1-P_{k+1}},
\]
and its mean over one period is exactly \(F_k\) for that prefix. If \(H\) is a bounded \(h\)-periodic function of mean \(m\), then an interval of length \(2r\) contains full periods plus two boundary fragments, giving the elementary estimate
\[
\left|\frac1{2r}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}H-m\right|
\le 2\|H\|_\infty\frac hr.
\]
Therefore, for \(0\le k<n\), averaging \(g\) at the prescribed radius \(\rho_k\) gives
\[
\frac1{2\rho_k}\int_{x-\rho_k}^{x+\rho_k}g
\ge F_k(d_1(x),\ldots,d_k(x))-2\|F\|_\infty\delta.
\]
For \(k=n\), all digits are constant on the averaging interval and the average is exactly \(F_n=F\). Hence on \(G\),
\[
M_{\{\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_n\}}g(x)
\ge F^*(d_1(x),\ldots,d_n(x))-2\|F\|_\infty\delta.
\]
Because the martingale is finite and bounded, letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) yields, at the level of normalized \(L^p\) integrals over a period, the martingale ratio
\(\|F^*\|_p/\|F\|_p\).

### 3. Compact support and smooth near-extremizers

The periodic function is localized by
\[
g_m=g\,\mathbf1_{[0,mS]}.
\]
On points at distance at least \(\rho_0\) from the two endpoints, all selected averages agree with those of \(g\). Dividing by \(mS\) and letting \(m\to\infty\) removes the boundary loss. Thus the finite-radius operator associated with
\(\{\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_n\}\subset\mathcal R\) has norm at least
\(\|F^*\|_p/\|F\|_p\), up to arbitrarily small error.

Finally, compactly supported step functions may be approximated in \(L^p\) by
\(C_c^\infty\) functions. Indeed,
\[
|M_{\mathcal R}f-M_{\mathcal R}h|
\le M_{\mathcal R}(f-h)\le M_c(f-h),
\]
so Madrid's bound gives
\[
\|M_{\mathcal R}f-M_{\mathcal R}h\|_p
\le p'\|f-h\|_p.
\]
The near-extremal ratio is therefore preserved under sufficiently fine smoothing.

Combining the finite martingales from Step 1 with Steps 2--3 proves that, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\), a finite subfamily of the prescribed radii admits a smooth compactly supported test function with ratio greater than \(p'-\varepsilon\). Together with the upper bound, this proves the theorem.

## Interpretation

The exact constant is insensitive to arbitrarily severe thinning of the scale set. What matters for the sharp strong \(L^p\) norm is not density of radii, but the availability of arbitrarily long finite chains with arbitrarily large multiplicative gaps. Unbounded logarithmic diameter supplies precisely such chains.

This is qualitatively different from many lacunary maximal problems in which restricting the scale set improves the range of boundedness or the norm. For centered interval averages on \(\mathbb R\), the martingale obstruction survives even on superlacunary and irregular scales.

## Limitations

The theorem is one-dimensional and uses Madrid's newly determined unrestricted norm for the final upper bound. It does not determine the norm when \(\mathcal R\) stays inside a compact annulus \(0<a\le r\le b<\infty\), and it does not address the weak \((1,1)\) endpoint or higher-dimensional exact constants. The dyadic-radii special case is not claimed as new. The full text of [1] was not directly inspected here; its abstract was checked, and the reported dyadic special case was conservatively treated as prior coverage based on [3].
