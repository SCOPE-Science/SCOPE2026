# Sharp Assouad-dimension ceiling from uniform disconnectedness on the line

## Statement

For a bounded set \(D\subset \mathbb R\) with at least two points, write
\[
\Gamma_D(u,v)=
\sup\bigl(\{q-p:u\le p<q\le v,\ D\cap(p,q)=\varnothing\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),
\qquad u<v,\quad u,v\in D,
\]
and define its optimal chain-uniform-disconnectedness constant by
\[
c_*(D)=\inf_{u<v,\ u,v\in D}\frac{\Gamma_D(u,v)}{v-u}.
\]
By the chain-bottleneck lemma of İlter--Duru--Kitover--Orhon, this is exactly the largest
\(c\in[0,1]\) for which every finite chain joining \(u\) to \(v\) has a step of length at least
\(c|u-v|\).

Define
\[
\Phi(c)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\log 2}{\log\!\bigl(2/(1-c)\bigr)},&0<c<1,\\[6pt]
0,&c=1.
\end{cases}
\]

**Theorem.** If \(c_*(D)=c>0\), then
\[
\boxed{\dim_A D\le \Phi(c)<1.}
\]
More generally, if \(D\) is \(c\)-uniformly disconnected in the chain sense, then
\(\dim_A D\le\Phi(c)\).

The bound is sharp for every \(c\in(0,1]\). For \(0<c<1\), let \(K_c\subset[0,1]\) be the
two-branch central Cantor set obtained by retaining two subintervals of common contraction ratio
\[
q=\frac{1-c}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
c_*(K_c)=c,\qquad
\dim_A K_c=\frac{\log2}{\log(1/q)}
=\frac{\log2}{\log(2/(1-c))}
=\Phi(c).
\]
For \(c=1\), the two-point set \(\{0,1\}\) gives equality.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sup\{\dim_A D:D\subset\mathbb R\ \text{bounded},\ c_*(D)\ge c\}
=\Phi(c).
}
\]

Since Hausdorff and upper box dimensions are bounded above by Assouad dimension, the same
ceiling holds for those dimensions as well.

## Proof of the upper bound

Fix \(0<c_0<c\) and put \(q_0=1-c_0\). Passing to the closure does not weaken the
\(c\)-chain condition: if a finite chain in \(\overline D\) had all steps strictly smaller than
\(c\) times the endpoint distance, sufficiently close approximations of its vertices by points of
\(D\) would give the same strict inequality in \(D\). Hence every compact subset of
\(\overline D\) is \(c\)-uniformly disconnected.

Take a non-singleton compact \(E\subset\overline D\), and set
\[
u=\min E,\qquad v=\max E,\qquad d=v-u.
\]
The chain-bottleneck/gap identity gives
\[
\Gamma_E(u,v)\ge cd.
\]
Therefore there is an open interval \((p,q)\subset[u,v]\setminus E\) with
\[
q-p>c_0d.
\]
Split \(E\) into the nonempty compact sets
\[
E^- = E\cap[u,p],\qquad E^+=E\cap[q,v].
\]
Their diameters obey the key mass-contraction estimate
\[
\operatorname{diam}E^-+\operatorname{diam}E^+
\le d-(q-p)
<q_0d.
\tag{1}
\]

Now fix \(r\in(0,d)\). Recursively split every node whose diameter is \(>r\), and stop a node
once its diameter is at most \(r\). Let \(I_k\) be the number of active nodes at depth \(k\).
Repeated use of (1) gives
\[
\sum_{\text{active nodes at depth }k}\operatorname{diam}E_j
\le q_0^k d.
\]
Since every active node has diameter \(>r\),
\[
I_k\le \frac d r\,q_0^k.
\]
Binary branching also gives \(I_k\le2^k\). Hence, with \(A=d/r\ge1\),
\[
I_k\le \min\{2^k,Aq_0^k\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
s_0=\frac{\log2}{\log(2/q_0)}.
\]
The crossover \(2^k=Aq_0^k\) occurs at
\[
K=\frac{\log A}{\log(2/q_0)}.
\]
Summing (2) on the two sides of \(K\),
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}I_k
\le
2A^{s_0}+\frac{1}{1-q_0}A^{s_0}
=
\left(2+\frac1{c_0}\right)A^{s_0}.
\tag{3}
\]
The finite binary splitting tree has one more terminal leaf than internal nodes. Each terminal
leaf has diameter at most \(r\), so (3) yields
\[
N(E,r)
\le
\left(3+\frac1{c_0}\right)\left(\frac d r\right)^{s_0},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(N(E,r)\) is the least number of sets of diameter at most \(r\) needed to cover \(E\).

Apply (4) to the closure of \(D\cap B(x,R)\). Its diameter is at most \(2R\), so, after
absorbing the factor \(2^{s_0}\) into the constant,
\[
N(D\cap B(x,R),r)
\le C(c_0)\left(\frac Rr\right)^{s_0}
\qquad(0<r<R).
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim_A D\le s_0.
\]
Letting \(c_0\uparrow c\) gives
\[
\dim_A D\le\Phi(c).
\]
If \(c=1\), the same limiting argument gives \(\dim_A D=0\).

## Sharpness

Fix \(0<c<1\), let \(q=(1-c)/2\), and let \(K_c\) be the self-similar set generated by
\[
x\mapsto qx,\qquad x\mapsto 1-q+qx.
\]
The first removed middle gap has length \(c\), and at every basic interval \(I\) the corresponding
middle gap has length \(c|I|\).

For distinct \(u<v\) in \(K_c\), choose the first basic interval \(I\) for which \(u\) and \(v\)
belong to different children. The middle gap of \(I\) lies between \(u\) and \(v\), so
\[
\Gamma_{K_c}(u,v)\ge c|I|\ge c(v-u).
\]
Thus \(c_*(K_c)\ge c\). Taking \(u=0\), \(v=1\), the largest complementary gap is the
first middle gap of length \(c\), because all later gaps have lengths \(cq^k<c\). Hence
\[
c_*(K_c)=c.
\]

The strong separation condition holds, so the standard self-similar dimension formula gives
\[
\dim_AK_c=\dim_HK_c=\frac{\log2}{-\log q}
=\Phi(c).
\]
This proves sharpness.

## Corollary for Stieltjes clocks

Let \(g:I\to\mathbb R\) be nonconstant, left-continuous and nondecreasing, and let
\[
\kappa_g=
\inf_{\substack{s<t\\g(s)<g(t)}}
\frac{\sup_{\tau\in[s,t)}\Delta^+g(\tau)}{g(t)-g(s)}.
\]
İlter--Duru--Kitover--Orhon prove that \(\kappa_g\) is exactly the optimal
uniform-disconnectedness constant of \(\operatorname{Im}(g)\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\kappa_g>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\dim_A\operatorname{Im}(g)
\le
\Phi(\kappa_g).
}
\tag{5}
\]

This is sharp within the class of Stieltjes clocks. For \(0<c<1\), let \(K_c\) be as above.
For every complementary gap \(G=(a_G,b_G)\) of \(K_c\), let \(\tau_G=(a_G+b_G)/2\), and set
\[
g_c(t)=\sum_{\tau_G<t}|G|,\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(K_c\) has Lebesgue measure zero, for \(t\in K_c\) the total length of complementary
gaps to the left of \(t\) equals \(t\), hence \(g_c(t)=t\). If \(t\) lies in a complementary gap
\((a_G,b_G)\), then \(g_c(t)=a_G\) on the left half (including the midpoint) and \(g_c(t)=b_G\)
on the right half. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Im}(g_c)=K_c.
\]
The function \(g_c\) is left-continuous, nondecreasing and pure jump. By the exact
bottleneck--jump identity,
\[
\kappa_{g_c}=c_*(K_c)=c,
\]
and equality holds in (5).

## Selection-theoretic reformulation

A standard theorem in Assouad geometry states that every metric space of Assouad dimension
strictly smaller than \(1\) is uniformly disconnected. The theorem above supplies the converse
for subsets of the real line. Hence, for every bounded \(D\subset\mathbb R\),
\[
\boxed{
D\ \text{is uniformly disconnected}
\iff
\dim_A D<1.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Combining (7) with Theorem 5.4 of İlter--Duru--Kitover--Orhon gives a dimension-only
characterization of their universal Hölder-selection property. For every
\(0<\alpha<1\) and every nonconstant left-continuous nondecreasing clock \(g\),
the following are equivalent:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{every compact-valued \(g\)-Hölder map into every metric target admits a}\\
&\text{\(g\)-Hölder selection;}\\
&\kappa_g>0;\\
&\dim_A\operatorname{Im}(g)<1.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the threshold for universal Hölder selection can be read as a strict
Assouad-dimension drop of the clock image, while (5) quantifies how the jump-dominance
parameter forces that drop.

## Context and limitations

The qualitative implications connecting Assouad dimension, porosity and uniform disconnectedness
are classical. The new content here is the sharp one-dimensional extremal function
\(\Phi(c)\) for the chain-uniform-disconnectedness constant, together with its sharp transfer
to the Stieltjes jump parameter \(\kappa_g\).

The estimate is specific to subsets of the real line and exploits the fact that one separating
gap produces exactly two ordered children whose total span contracts by \(1-c\). It is not
asserted for higher-dimensional uniformly disconnected sets with the same formula.

The Assouad-dimension criterion (8) is qualitative: the numerical value of
\(\dim_A\operatorname{Im}(g)\) below \(1\) does not by itself recover a positive lower bound for
\(\kappa_g\), because a finite-scale bottleneck can lower the optimal uniform-disconnectedness
constant without changing Assouad dimension.
