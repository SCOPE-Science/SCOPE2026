# Absorbing terms collapse locally finite Schreier ring varieties

## Statement

Let \(\mathcal V\) be a nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety whose language has a constant term \(e\).
Suppose \(t(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), \(n\ge 2\), has two distinct argument positions \(i\ne j\) in which \(e\) is absorbing:
\[
\mathcal V\models
t(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},e,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n)\approx e
\]
and
\[
\mathcal V\models
t(x_1,\ldots,x_{j-1},e,x_{j+1},\ldots,x_n)\approx e.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal V\models t(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\approx e.}
\]

Thus every term that is absorbing in two coordinates collapses to the absorbing constant.

For rings, this gives an exact classification. Here a ring means an algebra
\((R,+,-,0,\cdot)\) whose additive reduct is an abelian group and whose multiplication is distributive; associativity is not required, and a multiplicative identity is not assumed.

For each prime \(p\), let
\[
\mathcal R_p=\{R: xy=0\ \text{for all }x,y,\quad px=0\ \text{for all }x\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\text{The nontrivial locally finite Schreier varieties of rings are exactly the }\mathcal R_p.}
\]
In particular, this is also the classification for associative nonunital rings.

If the language instead requires a multiplicative identity \(1\), then there is no nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety of unital rings.

For associative algebras over a fixed field \(K\), in the usual \(K\)-linear nonunital language, the corresponding statement is:
- if \(K\) is finite, the unique nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety is the variety of \(K\)-vector spaces with zero multiplication;
- if \(K\) is infinite, there is no nontrivial locally finite variety at all.
For unital \(K\)-algebras there is no nontrivial locally finite Schreier variety for any field \(K\).

## Proof of the absorbing-term theorem

Kearnes--Moorhead--Szendrei prove that, for a nontrivial locally finite variety satisfying their constants hypothesis, being Schreier is equivalent to every finite member being \(\langle0,1\rangle\)-minimal. In the presence of a constant symbol or constant term, the constants hypothesis is automatic. A finite \(\langle0,1\rangle\)-minimal algebra is permutational: every unary polynomial operation is either constant or a permutation.

Take a finite algebra \(A\in\mathcal V\). The product \(B=A\times A\) is again finite and belongs to \(\mathcal V\), so \(B\) is permutational. If \(A\) is one-element there is nothing to prove, so assume \(|A|>1\).

Fix arbitrary \(a_k\in A\) for every \(k\ne j\), and in the \(i\)-th position use the parameter
\[
c_i=(a_i,e^A)\in B.
\]
For the other fixed positions use any parameters with first coordinates \(a_k\). Regard the \(j\)-th input as the variable \(z=(x,y)\in B\). This defines a unary polynomial \(P:B\to B\). Because the second coordinate of the \(i\)-th parameter is \(e^A\), absorption in the \(i\)-th position gives
\[
P(B)\subseteq A\times\{e^A\}.
\]
Hence \(P\) cannot be a permutation of \(B\), since \(|A|>1\). Permutationality therefore forces \(P\) to be constant.

Evaluate at \(z=(e^A,e^A)\). Absorption in the \(j\)-th position gives
\[
P(e^A,e^A)=(e^A,e^A).
\]
Thus \(P\) is the constant \((e^A,e^A)\). Its first coordinate shows
\[
t^A(a_1,\ldots,a_{j-1},x,a_{j+1},\ldots,a_n)=e^A
\]
for every \(x\in A\). Since all \(a_k\) were arbitrary, \(t^A\) is constantly \(e^A\).

Therefore the identity \(t\approx e\) holds in every finite member of \(\mathcal V\). For an arbitrary \(A\in\mathcal V\) and any tuple of arguments, the subalgebra generated by that finite tuple is finite by local finiteness. The same identity holds there, hence in \(A\). This proves the theorem.

## Ring classification

In any ring, multiplication has \(0\) absorbing in both coordinates:
\[
0x=x0=0.
\]
The theorem therefore forces
\[
xy=0
\]
throughout every nontrivial locally finite Schreier ring variety \(\mathcal V\).

Let \(F=F_{\mathcal V}(1)\) be the one-generated free ring. Since multiplication is zero, the additive group of \(F\) is cyclic, generated by its free generator. Local finiteness makes \(F\) finite, so
\[
F^+\cong \mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z
\]
for some \(n\ge2\).

The finite algebra \(F\) is \(\langle0,1\rangle\)-minimal. For every integer \(m\), the map
\[
x\mapsto mx
\]
is a unary polynomial. If \(n\) were composite, choose a prime \(q\mid n\) with \(q<n\). Multiplication by \(q\) on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) is neither constant nor bijective: it is nonzero and has nontrivial kernel. This contradicts minimality. Hence \(n=p\) is prime.

It follows that \(\mathcal V\) satisfies both identities
\[
xy\approx0,\qquad px\approx0,
\]
so \(\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal R_p\). Conversely, \(F\) itself is the \(p\)-element zero-multiplication ring, and it belongs to \(\mathcal V\). The variety it generates is exactly \(\mathcal R_p\), equivalently the variety of \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector spaces with zero multiplication. Hence
\[
\mathcal R_p\subseteq\mathcal V,
\]
and equality follows.

Each \(\mathcal R_p\) is locally finite and Schreier: its free algebra on a set \(X\) is the \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector space with basis \(X\) and zero multiplication, while every subring is simply an \(\mathbb F_p\)-subspace and is therefore free.

If a multiplicative identity is required, the absorbing-term theorem would give \(xy=0\); substituting \(x=y=1\) gives \(1=0\). Hence only the trivial unital variety remains.

## Fixed-field algebras

For a fixed field \(K\), the same absorbing-term argument forces multiplication to vanish. A nontrivial \(K\)-linear variety with zero multiplication has no proper nontrivial linear subvariety: a nonzero scalar identity \(\lambda x=0\) would imply \(x=0\). Thus the only candidate is the full variety of \(K\)-vector spaces with zero multiplication.

This candidate is locally finite exactly when \(K\) is finite. When \(K\) is finite it is Schreier because subspaces of free vector spaces are free. When \(K\) is infinite, every nonzero one-generated \(K\)-algebra already contains the infinite set \(Kx\), so no nontrivial locally finite variety exists.

## Relation to prior work

Kearnes, Moorhead and Szendrei, arXiv:2609.19651v1, prove the general equivalence between local-finite Schreier/Nielsen behavior and finite \(\langle0,1\rangle\)-minimality. Their paper discusses classical specializations to groups, semigroups, Lie algebras and \(M\)-sets, but the inspected version does not state the two-coordinate absorbing-term obstruction or the ring classification above.

Burgin's 1974 paper gives a broad classification criterion for Schreier varieties of linear \(\Omega\)-algebras defined by homogeneous identities. In particular, his Theorem 3 forces the effective coefficient ring to be a field, Proposition 4 reduces Schreier \(\Omega\)-ring varieties to a residue field of prime characteristic, and the paper records the zero-multiplication linear-algebra variety as a known Schreier example. These are prior results and are not claimed here. The new claim is the local-finiteness mechanism that kills every two-coordinate absorbing term, and the resulting exact ring classification under arbitrary identities.

The group commutator provides a useful consistency check: it has the identity element absorbing in both arguments, so the theorem forces every locally finite Schreier group variety to be abelian. This agrees with the classical Neumann--Wiegold classification; no group-theoretic novelty is claimed.

## Limitations

The local-finiteness hypothesis is essential to the proof and cannot simply be removed: classical non-locally-finite Schreier varieties can have nontrivial absorbing terms. The theorem also requires a genuine constant term and absorption in two distinct coordinates. It does not classify locally finite varieties lacking such a term, nor does it classify non-Schreier ring varieties.

Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge. The main residual risk is that the two-coordinate argument or its ring corollary appears implicitly in older universal-algebra or Schreier-variety literature under different terminology. Burgin's 1974 paper is especially close background, but the inspected theorem and proposition do not state the local-finite collapse proved here.
