# Review — repaired after independent audit

**Independent audit (2026-09-29): repaired.**

## Correctness

PASS. The classifications are correct. The two-coordinate collapse follows directly from the prior recovery principle for locally finite Schreier varieties: dependence on one variable would allow that variable to be recovered from the term value and the other variables; setting a distinct absorbing coordinate to the absorbing constant makes such recovery impossible in a nontrivial variety. Multiplication therefore vanishes in every ring member. The finite cyclic additive group of the one-generated free ring must have prime order because multiplication by a proper prime divisor would be a unary polynomial that is neither constant nor a permutation. This yields exactly the zero-multiplication exponent-p varieties, and the fixed-field conclusions follow in the same way.

## Originality

REPAIRED. The original review incorrectly treated the absorbing-term collapse as new. A publicly posted PALS presentation dated 19 September 2023 by Kearnes, Kompatscher, Moorhead and Szendrei states the stronger main claim that any term depending on a variable admits a term recovering that variable from the term value and the remaining variables. The absorbing-term statement is an immediate corollary and is now attributed as prior work.

Burgin (1974) remains close prior art: it classifies broad homogeneous linear Ω-algebra Schreier varieties, gives residue-field restrictions, and records trivial multiplication among Schreier examples. The inspected prior sources did not state the exact locally finite ring/fixed-field classification retained here, so that narrower application remains original to the best of the searched evidence.

## Value

PASS after narrowing. The exact specialization is concise but useful: it completely identifies locally finite Schreier varieties in the standard nonunital ring language, rules out every nontrivial unital case, and gives the corresponding fixed-field dichotomy.

## Limitations

The retained novelty claim is only the explicit ring/fixed-field classification, not the absorbing-term mechanism. Equivalent older ring-language coverage may exist under different terminology.
