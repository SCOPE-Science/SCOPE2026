## Correctness

For an anisotropic nondegenerate form, every subspace is nondegenerate, so the kernel/image orthogonal decompositions give a Moore–Penrose inverse for every endomorphism. Conversely, from any nonzero isotropic vector the audited rank-one construction gives elements with zero first triple product and a nonzero transposed product whose image is an isotropic line; such an operator cannot possess a Moore–Penrose inverse because its range would be the range of a self-adjoint idempotent and hence nondegenerate. This proves the zero-triple condition, full transfer condition, anisotropy, and star-regularity equivalence. For transpose over a finite field, the standard quadratic form is anisotropic only in dimension one or in dimension two when the field order is congruent to three modulo four; the dimension-at-least-three case follows from the standard finite-field quadratic-form zero argument.
