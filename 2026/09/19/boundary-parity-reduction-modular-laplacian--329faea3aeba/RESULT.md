# Boundary-controlled parity reduction in modular Laplacian dynamics

## Result

Consider the modular discrete-Laplacian dynamics on \(\mathbb Z^2\) with a finite neighborhood mask \(M\), degree
\[
d=|M|,
\qquad
(L_Mu)(p)=\sum_{v\in M}u(p+v)-d\,u(p).
\]
Write
\[
K_k u=[L_Mu]_k,
\qquad
Bu=[L_Mu]_2,
\]
where residues are represented by \(0,\ldots,k-1\). The recent study arXiv:2609.20416v1 observed a strong even/odd separation under schedules containing binary updates and proved that, for binary input and odd \(k,\ell>d\), the states \(K_ku\) and \(K_\ell u\) have the same parity and therefore coalesce after the next binary update.

There is a sharper exact reduction. It both explains the entire even class and identifies the geometric source of the odd correction.

### Theorem 1: every even insertion is erased by the next binary update

For every integer-valued finitely supported configuration \(v\) and every even modulus \(k\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{\;B(K_kv)=B^2v.\;}
\]

No assumption \(k>d\) and no binary-input hypothesis is needed.

Indeed, reduction modulo an even integer preserves parity:
\[
[x]_k\equiv x\pmod 2
\qquad (k\ {\rm even}).
\]
Hence \(K_kv\equiv L_Mv\pmod2\). Since \(L_M\) is integer-linear,
\[
B(K_kv)
=[L_MK_kv]_2
=[L_M(L_Mv)]_2
=B^2v.
\]

Thus an arbitrary even-modulus insertion is exactly transparent at the first subsequent binary checkpoint.

### Theorem 2: a large odd insertion is a boundary pulse

Now let \(u:\mathbb Z^2\to\{0,1\}\) be binary. Define its inner \(M\)-boundary by
\[
\partial_Mu(p)=
\begin{cases}
1,&u(p)=1\ \text{and}\ u(p+v)=0\ \text{for at least one }v\in M,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For binary \(u\),
\[
\partial_Mu(p)=\mathbf 1_{\{(L_Mu)(p)<0\}}.
\]
If \(k>d\), then \((L_Mu)(p)\in[-d,d]\), and therefore
\[
[x]_k=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge0,\\
k+x,&x<0.
\end{cases}
\]
Writing \(\varepsilon_k=k\bmod2\), pointwise reduction modulo \(2\) gives
\[
\boxed{\;
K_ku\bmod2
=
Bu+\varepsilon_k\,\partial_Mu
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.
\;}
\]
Consequently, after \(r\ge1\) binary updates following the insertion,
\[
\boxed{\;
B^r(K_ku)
=
B^{r+1}u+\varepsilon_k B^r(\partial_Mu)
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.
\;}
\]

For large even \(k\), the boundary term vanishes, consistently with Theorem 1. For large odd \(k\),
\[
\boxed{\;
B^r(K_ku)-B^{r+1}u
=
B^r(\partial_Mu)
\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_2.
\;}
\]
Thus the entire post-insertion difference from the binary reference is generated by the active boundary of the pre-insertion support.

### Corollary 1: exact finite-speed localization of the odd/even difference

Let
\[
\mathcal N_M(S)=S+(\{0\}\cup(-M))
\]
be the closed one-step backward mask neighborhood, and let \(\mathcal N_M^r\) denote \(r\)-fold iteration. Since one binary Laplacian step cannot propagate support beyond one such neighborhood,
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{supp}\!\left(
B^r(K_{k_{\rm odd}}u)-B^r(K_{k_{\rm even}}u)
\right)
\subseteq
\mathcal N_M^r(\operatorname{supp}\partial_Mu)
\;}
\]
whenever \(k_{\rm odd},k_{\rm even}>d\) have opposite parity.

For symmetric masks this is the ordinary graph \(r\)-neighborhood of the active boundary. Hence, before a later insertion intervenes, the parity-dependent correction is confined to a boundary layer with finite propagation speed; points outside that layer evolve identically.

### Corollary 2: exact parity quotient for schedules

Consider any schedule in which each non-binary insertion is followed, before the next insertion, by at least one binary update. If all inserted odd moduli exceed \(d\), then the states at binary checkpoints depend on each such modulus only through its parity.

More precisely:

1. Replacing any even insertion \(k\) by two binary updates leaves every later binary checkpoint unchanged.
2. Replacing one odd insertion \(k>d\) by another odd insertion \(\ell>d\) leaves the first subsequent binary state, and hence all later states up to the next insertion, unchanged.
3. Two insertion schedules with the same insertion locations and the same parity word therefore have identical binary-checkpoint trajectories, provided their odd insertions exceed \(d\).

For the periodic family
\[
[2,k,2^{\times s}]^\infty
\]
of arXiv:2609.20416v1, this yields two exact consequences:

- for **every even \(k\)**, every binary-output phase is exactly the pure-binary state at the same global iteration;
- for **every odd \(k>d\)**, all such moduli have exactly the same complete state at every binary-output phase.

The source paper established the second statement experimentally for \(k=5,7,9\) with degree-four masks and proved the local odd-parity lemma. The first statement is stronger than the reported description of even insertions as merely “binary-like.”

### Corollary 3: support trajectories within the large-modulus parity classes

For binary \(u\) and \(k>d\),
\[
|L_Mu(p)|\le d<k
\]
implies
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{supp}(K_ku)
=
\operatorname{supp}(L_Mu),
\;}
\]
because a nonzero Laplacian value cannot become \(0\pmod k\).

Hence, once two large-modulus schedules agree at the binary input to an insertion, the supports of their insertion outputs agree even if their residue values differ. In particular, schedules with the same large-modulus parity class have identical support trajectories at insertion phases as well as identical complete configurations at binary phases. Any occupation statistic depending only on support therefore coincides exactly within that class.

This strengthens the finite-horizon density coincidences reported in arXiv:2609.20416v1 to an all-time identity for the stated schedules and threshold regime.

## Relation to prior work

The main source, arXiv:2609.20416v1, studies time-dependent modular Laplacian dynamics and reports that even insertions remain binary-like while higher odd moduli can form synchronized classes. Its Lemma 3.1 proves the parity coincidence of odd \(k,\ell>d\) for binary input and explains the observed \(k=5,7,9\) synchronization for degree-four masks. That odd-class statement is prior work and is not claimed as new here.

The earlier arXiv:2509.05815v1 experimentally distinguishes even and odd binary-\(k\) schedules and observes that even insertions preserve much of the binary organization. It does not give the exact erasure identity above or the boundary-forcing formula.

The source-specific refinement here is the combination of:

- exact erasure of **every** even insertion at the next binary update, with no mask-degree threshold and no binary-input assumption;
- the exact boundary formula \(B^rK_ku=B^{r+1}u+B^r\partial_Mu\) for odd \(k>d\);
- finite-speed localization of the parity-dependent correction to a propagated active boundary;
- the resulting exact parity-word quotient of insertion schedules;
- support-level equality at large-modulus insertion phases.

These statements turn the empirical even/odd dichotomy into an exact dynamical decomposition.

## Limitations

The boundary formula for odd insertions uses binary input and the threshold \(k>d\). Odd moduli at or below the mask degree can wrap more than once or hit \(0\pmod k\), so additional configuration-dependent coincidences are not classified here. The finite-speed statement is local between insertions and does not by itself imply a quantitative density bound for arbitrary evolving supports. The result concerns deterministic modular discrete-Laplacian dynamics; it does not establish asymptotic carpet persistence or aperiodicity.
