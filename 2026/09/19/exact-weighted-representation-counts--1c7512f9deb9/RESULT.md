# Gaussian local limits and exact constants for weighted-representation partitions

Let \(k>1\) be fixed. For \(A\subseteq\mathbb N\), write
\[
D_A(n)=R_{1,k}(A,n)-R_{1,k}(\mathbb N\setminus A,n),
\]
where \(R_{1,k}(A,n)\) counts representations \(n=a_1+ka_2\) with \(a_1,a_2\in A\).
Li, Xu and Yan (arXiv:2609.20385v1, 17 September 2026) proved, for every fixed integer \(c\),
\[
\#\{A:D_A(n)=c\ \forall n\ge T\}\asymp_{k,c}\frac{2^T}{T^{k/2}}.
\]
The finite sign-vector reduction in their proof admits a multivariate local-limit analysis that gives the leading constant and, more generally, a Gaussian profile for residue-dependent tail discrepancies.

## Theorem

For an integer vector \(\mathbf c(T)=(c_0(T),\dots,c_{k-1}(T))\), let
\[
F_k(T;\mathbf c)=\#\{A\subseteq\mathbb N:
D_A(n)=c_{n\bmod k}(T)\ \text{for every }n\ge T\}.
\]
Suppose
\[
\frac{\mathbf c(T)}{\sqrt T}\longrightarrow \boldsymbol\lambda\in\mathbb R^k.
\]
Define
\[
G_k=\frac1k I_k+\frac{k+2}{k^2}J_k,
\qquad
G_k^{-1}=kI_k-\frac{k+2}{k+3}J_k,
\]
where \(J_k\) is the all-ones matrix. Then
\[
\boxed{
F_k(T;\mathbf c)
\sim
\frac{(8k/\pi)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{k+3}}
\frac{2^T}{T^{k/2}}
\exp\!\left(-2\boldsymbol\lambda^{\!T}G_k^{-1}\boldsymbol\lambda\right).
}
\]

In particular, for every fixed integer \(c\),
\[
\boxed{
\#\{A:D_A(n)=c\ \forall n\ge T\}
\sim C_k\frac{2^T}{T^{k/2}},
\qquad
C_k=\frac{(8k/\pi)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{k+3}}.
}
\]
The leading constant is independent of the fixed discrepancy \(c\).

For the original Yang--Chen counting function \(f_k(T)\) (the case \(c=0\)),
\[
\boxed{
f_k(T)\sim
\frac{(8k/\pi)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{k+3}}
\frac{2^T}{T^{k/2}}.
}
\]
Consequently, if \(1<k<\ell\), then the order comparison of Li--Xu--Yan sharpens to
\[
\boxed{
\frac{f_k(T)}{f_\ell(T)}
\sim
\frac{C_k}{C_\ell}T^{(\ell-k)/2}.
}
\]

For a scalar discrepancy \(c(T)/\sqrt T\to\lambda\), the Gaussian factor simplifies to
\[
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2k^2}{k+3}\lambda^2\right).
\]
Taking \(c=m+1\) and \(T=mk+t\) also gives, for each fixed \(k>1\) and \(m\ge0\), the exact leading asymptotic for the Yan--Shan function cited in Remark 1.5 of arXiv:2609.20385:
\[
g_{k,m}(t)
\sim
C_k\,2^{mk}\frac{2^t}{t^{k/2}}.
\]

## Proof

For each residue \(0\le s<k\), let \(n_s\) be the unique integer in \([T,T+k-1]\) with \(n_s\equiv s\pmod k\), and put
\[
q_s=\frac{n_s-s}{k}.
\]
The boundary equations in Li--Xu--Yan are
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{q_s}\varepsilon_j+
\sum_{j=0}^{q_s}\varepsilon_{kj+s}=2c_s(T),
\qquad 0\le s<k,
\]
for signs \(\varepsilon_0,\dots,\varepsilon_{T+k-1}\in\{-1,1\}\). Their recurrence
\[
\varepsilon_n=-\varepsilon_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\qquad(n\ge T+k)
\]
then determines the entire tail uniquely. Hence \(F_k(T;\mathbf c)\) is exactly the number of these finite sign vectors satisfying the \(k\) boundary equations.

Let \(N=T+k\). Define the \(k\times N\) integer matrix \(B_T=(b_{s,x})\) by
\[
b_{s,x}=
\mathbf 1_{0\le x\le q_s}
+
\mathbf 1_{x\equiv s\ ({\rm mod}\ k),\ 0\le x\le n_s}.
\]
The boundary system is
\[
B_T\boldsymbol\varepsilon=2\mathbf c(T).
\]
Since the sum of row \(s\) is \(2(q_s+1)\), write
\[
\varepsilon_x=1-2Y_x,
\qquad Y_x\in\{0,1\},
\]
and let \(Y_x\) be independent Bernoulli\((1/2)\) variables. With
\[
\boldsymbol\mu_T=(q_0+1,\dots,q_{k-1}+1)^T,
\qquad
S_T=\sum_{x=0}^{N-1}b_xY_x,
\]
where \(b_x\) is column \(x\) of \(B_T\), one has the exact identity
\[
F_k(T;\mathbf c)=2^{T+k}
\Pr\{S_T=\boldsymbol\mu_T-\mathbf c(T)\}.
\]

The covariance matrix of \(S_T\) is \(\frac14B_TB_T^T\). Put
\[
A_s=\{0,1,\dots,q_s\},
\qquad
B_s=\{s,s+k,\dots,n_s\}.
\]
The row vector \(s\) is the indicator of \(A_s\) plus that of \(B_s\). Since \(q_s=T/k+O_k(1)\), for \(s\ne t\),
\[
|A_s\cap A_t|=\frac Tk+O_k(1),
\]
\[
|A_s\cap B_t|=|B_s\cap A_t|=\frac{T}{k^2}+O_k(1),
\qquad
|B_s\cap B_t|=0,
\]
whereas \(|B_s|=T/k+O_k(1)\). Therefore
\[
\frac1T B_TB_T^T\longrightarrow G_k,
\]
with diagonal entries \(2(k+1)/k^2\) and off-diagonal entries \((k+2)/k^2\). Equivalently,
\[
G_k=\frac1kI_k+\frac{k+2}{k^2}J_k.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(1/k\) with multiplicity \(k-1\) and \((k+3)/k\) in the all-ones direction, so
\[
\det G_k=\frac{k+3}{k^k},
\qquad
G_k^{-1}=kI_k-\frac{k+2}{k+3}J_k.
\]

It remains to justify a lattice local central limit theorem directly for this triangular array. Li--Xu--Yan define disjoint sets
\[
P_s=\{x:Q<x\le n_s,\ x\equiv s\pmod k\},
\qquad
|P_s|=\frac{k-1}{k^2}T+O_k(1),
\]
where \(Q=\max_s q_s\). For every \(x\in P_s\), the column \(b_x\) is exactly the standard basis vector \(e_s\). Thus the lattice generated by the columns is all of \(\mathbb Z^k\), and the characteristic function has no nonzero period inside the fundamental cube.

For the centered sum, Fourier inversion gives an integral over \([ -\pi,\pi]^k\) with characteristic product
\[
\prod_{x=0}^{N-1}\cos\!\left(\frac{\theta\cdot b_x}{2}\right).
\]
Outside a fixed neighborhood of the origin, at least one coordinate \(|\theta_s|\) is bounded below, and the \(|P_s|\asymp T\) copies of the factor \(\cos(\theta_s/2)\) make the product exponentially small in \(T\). Inside a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood, the column norms are bounded (with \(k\) fixed) and
\[
\log\cos(u/2)=-\frac{u^2}{8}+O(u^4).
\]
After the scaling \(\theta=u/\sqrt T\),
\[
\prod_x\cos\!\left(\frac{\theta\cdot b_x}{2}\right)
\longrightarrow
\exp\!\left(-\frac18u^TG_ku\right),
\]
while the phase from \(\mathbf c(T)/\sqrt T\to\boldsymbol\lambda\) converges to the corresponding Fourier phase. The same \(P_s\) factors give an integrable Gaussian majorant in the central region. Dominated convergence therefore yields
\[
\Pr\{S_T=\boldsymbol\mu_T-\mathbf c(T)\}
\sim
\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{k/2}
\frac{T^{-k/2}}{\sqrt{\det G_k}}
\exp\!\left(-2\boldsymbol\lambda^TG_k^{-1}\boldsymbol\lambda\right).
\]
Multiplying by \(2^{T+k}\) and using \(\det G_k=(k+3)/k^k\) gives the theorem.

## Verification

The accompanying exact-integer program reconstructs the finite boundary matrix, counts admissible sign vectors by dynamic programming, and compares the exact counts with the stated asymptotic. For \(c=0\), the actual/predicted ratios include
\[
0.9264421485\ (k=2,T=40),\quad
0.9844297561\ (k=2,T=200),
\]
and
\[
0.6985497974\ (k=3,T=25),\quad
0.8914756257\ (k=3,T=60).
\]
It also checks the natural \(c\asymp\sqrt T\) scale; for \((k,T,c)=(2,200,7)\), the ratio to the Gaussian-profile prediction is \(0.9928569733\). These computations are consistency checks and are not used in the proof.

## Originality and limitations

The claim is to the best of our knowledge. The motivating preprint arXiv:2609.20385v1 was inspected through its theorem statements and proof: it proves only two-sided order bounds for fixed \(c\), using a decomposition into independent sign blocks and central binomial estimates; it does not state a leading constant, a local central limit theorem, or a Gaussian profile for \(c\) on the \(\sqrt T\) scale. Its Remark 1.5 likewise gives only the order of \(g_{k,m}(t)\).

The 2012 Yang--Chen paper that introduced \(f_k\), *Partitions of natural numbers with the same weighted representation functions*, J. Number Theory 132 (2012), 3047--3055, was not inspected in full text. Its role and result are explicitly summarized in the 2026 primary source: it established finiteness and \(\log f_k(t)/t\to\log2\). It remains a residual bibliographic risk, though that summary makes coverage of the present sharp asymptotic unlikely. The 2025 Yan--Shan paper, DOI 10.1007/s11139-025-01113-7, is relevant to the secondary \(g_{k,m}\) corollary; its accessible abstract reports exact small-threshold formulas and existence/uniqueness results, not the large-\(t\) constant derived here. Because arXiv:2609.20385 is very recent, unindexed contemporaneous follow-up work remains a residual originality risk.

No novelty is claimed for general multivariate lattice local central limit theorems themselves. The contribution is the exact covariance/lattice analysis of the weighted-representation boundary system and its resulting constants and Gaussian profile. The theorem keeps \(k\) fixed; no uniform regime with \(k\to\infty\) is claimed.
