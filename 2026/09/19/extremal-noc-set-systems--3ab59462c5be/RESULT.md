# Equality cases for the quadratic NOC bound

## Statement

Let \(X\) be an \(n\)-element set. Two sets overlap if their intersection is nonempty and neither contains the other. A set system \(\mathfrak C\subseteq 2^X\setminus\{\emptyset\}\) has the not-overlap-covered (NOC) property if every \(C\in\mathfrak C\) contains an element that belongs to no member of \(\mathfrak C\) overlapping \(C\).

Lindeberg and Hellmuth proved that every NOC set system satisfies
\[
 |\mathfrak C|\le \frac{n(n+1)}2,
\]
and exhibited a family attaining equality. The equality cases are in fact rigid.

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge2\). A NOC set system \(\mathfrak C\) on \(X\) has \(|\mathfrak C|=n(n+1)/2\) if and only if there is an ordering \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) of \(X\) such that
\[
 \mathfrak C=
 \left\{
 \{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\cup\{x_j\}:
 1\le k\le j\le n
 \right\}.
\]
The ordering is determined by \(\mathfrak C\) up to interchanging \(x_{n-1}\) and \(x_n\).

Consequently, for every fixed labeled \(n\)-element ground set with \(n\ge2\), the number of extremal NOC set systems is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\frac{n!}{2}}.
\]
For \(n=1\), the unique extremal system is \(\{X\}\).

Every extremal NOC set system is automatically a clustering system, since its first level consists of all singletons and its last level is \(\{X\}\). Therefore the same classification gives all clustering systems attaining the sharp maximum number of clusters for tree-child and normal networks.

## Proof

For \(1\le k\le n\), write
\[
 \mathfrak C_k=\{S\in\mathfrak C:|S|=k\}.
\]
The proof of the quadratic bound gives
\[
 |\mathfrak C_k|\le n-k+1.
\]
If the total bound is attained, then equality must hold at every level:
\[
 |\mathfrak C_k|=n-k+1.
\]

Fix \(k\). Choose a NOC witness \(x_S\in S\) for every \(S\in\mathfrak C_k\). The level argument in the sharp-bound proof shows that each \(x_S\) occurs in no other member of \(\mathfrak C_k\), so the chosen witnesses are pairwise distinct. Let
\[
 W_k=\{x_S:S\in\mathfrak C_k\}.
\]
Then \(|W_k|=n-k+1\). For a fixed \(S\in\mathfrak C_k\), the other \(k-1\) elements of \(S\) cannot belong to \(W_k\). Since \(|X\setminus W_k|=k-1\), equality forces
\[
 S=(X\setminus W_k)\cup\{x_S\}.
\]
Thus, with \(Y_k=X\setminus W_k\),
\[
 \mathfrak C_k=\{Y_k\cup\{x\}:x\in X\setminus Y_k\},
 \qquad |Y_k|=k-1.
\]
In particular, every level is a full sunflower with core \(Y_k\).

It remains to show that these cores are nested. Fix \(1\le k\le n-2\) and choose \(x\in X\setminus Y_k\). The set
\[
 S=Y_k\cup\{x\}\in\mathfrak C_k
\]
has \(x\) as its chosen NOC witness. Hence every member of \(\mathfrak C\) containing \(x\) is comparable with \(S\) under inclusion.

If \(x\notin Y_{k+1}\), then
\[
 D=Y_{k+1}\cup\{x\}\in\mathfrak C_{k+1}
\]
contains \(x\). Since \(|D|=|S|+1\), comparability forces \(S\subset D\), and therefore \(Y_k\subset Y_{k+1}\).

If \(x\in Y_{k+1}\), then every member of \(\mathfrak C_{k+1}\) contains \(x\), hence every such member contains \(S\). There are \(n-k\ge2\) members on this level and their intersection is exactly \(Y_{k+1}\). Thus \(S\subseteq Y_{k+1}\), again implying \(Y_k\subset Y_{k+1}\).

Since \(|Y_{k+1}|=|Y_k|+1\), we obtain a strict chain
\[
 \varnothing=Y_1\subset Y_2\subset\cdots\subset Y_{n-1},
 \qquad |Y_k|=k-1.
\]
Choose \(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-2}\) so that
\[
 Y_k=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\quad(1\le k\le n-1),
\]
and let the two remaining elements be \(x_{n-1},x_n\) in either order. Substituting the cores into the level description yields exactly the displayed extremal family.

Conversely, any family of the displayed form is NOC: for
\[
 S=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\cup\{x_j\},
\]
the element \(x_j\) is a NOC witness. This is also the sharpness construction of Lindeberg and Hellmuth after relabeling.

For uniqueness, when \(k\le n-1\) the core \(Y_k\) is the intersection of all members of \(\mathfrak C_k\), so the family determines the chain \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n-1}\). Its successive differences determine \(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-2}\); only the final two elements remain unordered. Hence each extremal family is generated by exactly two permutations, giving \(n!/2\) labeled families.

## Canonical network structure

The current NOC characterization states that every NOC clustering system has a unique regular normal realization given by its Hasse diagram, and that NOC clustering systems are in bijection with strong-phylogenetic normal networks and also with phylogenetic separated normal networks, in both cases up to leaf-label-preserving isomorphism.

For an extremal family write
\[
 S_{k,j}=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\cup\{x_j\},
 \qquad 1\le k\le j\le n.
\]
Its Hasse arcs are exactly the adjacent-level inclusions. For \(1\le k<n\):

- if \(j>k\), then \(S_{k,j}\) has the unique parent \(S_{k+1,j}\);
- the prefix set \(S_{k,k}=\{x_1,\ldots,x_k\}\) has the \(n-k\) parents \(S_{k+1,t}\), \(t=k+1,\ldots,n\).

Hence every extremal regular realization has
\[
 \boxed{\frac{n(n+1)}2\text{ vertices},\qquad n(n-1)\text{ arcs},\qquad n-2\text{ reticulation vertices}}
\]
for \(n\ge2\), where reticulation means indegree at least two. Ignoring leaf labels, all these regular extremal realizations have the same Hasse shape.

By the bijections above, for a fixed labeled leaf set \(X\) there are exactly \(n!/2\) strong-phylogenetic normal networks and exactly \(n!/2\) phylogenetic separated normal networks whose clustering systems contain the maximum \(n(n+1)/2\) distinct clusters. This counts these canonical network classes, not all possible non-regular tree-child or normal realizations of an extremal cluster system.

## Verification

The accompanying verifier checks the NOC definition directly for every set system on ground sets of sizes \(1,2,3,4\). It finds respectively
\[
 1,1,3,12
\]
extremal families, exactly matching \(1\) for \(n=1\) and \(n!/2\) for \(n=2,3,4\), and confirms that every maximizer is of the classified form. It also constructs the Hasse diagram of the canonical family for \(2\le n\le10\) and checks the vertex, arc, and reticulation formulas above. The general theorem is proved independently of these finite checks.

## Relation to prior literature

Lindeberg and Hellmuth introduced the NOC formulation for arbitrary set systems, proved its equivalence with inclusion-visibility, and established the sharp bound \(n(n+1)/2\). Their Lemma 5 gives the same ordered family as a sharpness example but does not classify all equality cases. Their Corollary 9 transfers the bound to distinct clusters of tree-child and normal networks, and Corollary 11 gives the two network bijections used above. Their concluding section explicitly raises enumeration of NOC clustering systems and the refinement by reticulation number as an open direction.

The earlier work of Alcalà, Llabrés, Rosselló, and Rullan introduced strict-compatibility for clustering systems and characterized tree-child cluster networks through it. The accessible full text of that paper contains the strict-compatibility characterization but no quadratic extremal bound or equality classification. Searches for equality cases under the synonymous terms NOC, not-overlap-covered, inclusion-visible, strict-compatible, tree-child clustering systems, private elements, and sunflower formulations did not reveal an equivalent classification.

Originality is therefore asserted only to the best of our knowledge. A residual risk is that the elementary equality analysis of the levelwise private-witness bound may occur in older extremal-set-system literature under different terminology, or that very recent parallel work has not yet been indexed. No source located in the search states the nested-core equality classification, the \(n!/2\) labeled count, or the resulting extremal normal-network enumeration.

## Limitations

The result classifies only exact equality in the quadratic NOC bound; it does not give stability results for nearly extremal systems or enumerate all NOC systems. The \(n!/2\) network counts apply to the two canonical classes covered by the cited bijections, not to all non-regular realizations sharing the same extremal clustering system. Finite enumeration is supporting evidence and does not replace the proof.
