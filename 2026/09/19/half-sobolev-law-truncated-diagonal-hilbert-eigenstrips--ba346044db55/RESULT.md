# Half-Sobolev law for arbitrary cutoffs of diagonal double-Hilbert eigenstrips

## Statement

Let \(H\) be the one-dimensional Hilbert transform with multiplier
\(-i\,\operatorname{sgn}\xi\), let \(\mathcal H=H_1H_2\), and use the unitary Fourier
transform. For \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(g\in L^2(\mathbb R)\), define
\[
F_{\varepsilon,g}(x,y)=\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<\varepsilon\}}\,g(y)
\]
and
\[
J(s)=\int_0^s\left(\frac{\sin u}{u}\right)^2\,du.
\]
Then
\[
\|\mathcal H F_{\varepsilon,g}-F_{\varepsilon,g}\|_2^2
=
\frac{8\varepsilon}{\pi}
\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(\xi)|^2J(\varepsilon|\xi|)\,d\xi.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{\|\mathcal H F_{\varepsilon,g}-F_{\varepsilon,g}\|_2^2}{\varepsilon^2}
=
\frac8\pi\int_{\mathbb R}|\xi|\,|\widehat g(\xi)|^2\,d\xi,
\]
with \(+\infty\) allowed. For nonzero \(g\),
\[
\|\mathcal H F_{\varepsilon,g}-F_{\varepsilon,g}\|_2=O(\varepsilon)
\]
holds exactly when \(g\in\dot H^{1/2}(\mathbb R)\).

If \(g\) is compactly supported, piecewise \(W^{1,1}\), and has jumps
\(\Delta_j\), with \(S(g)=\sum_j|\Delta_j|^2\), then
\[
\|\mathcal H F_{\varepsilon,g}-F_{\varepsilon,g}\|_2^2
=
\frac{8S(g)}{\pi^2}\varepsilon^2\log\frac1\varepsilon\,(1+o(1)).
\]

## Proof

Fourier factorization gives
\[
\widehat{F_{\varepsilon,g}}(\xi_1,\xi_2)
=
\widehat{\mathbf 1_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}}(\xi_1)
\,\widehat g(\xi_1+\xi_2),
\]
with
\[
\left|\widehat{\mathbf 1_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}}(t)\right|^2
=
\frac2\pi\frac{\sin^2(\varepsilon t)}{t^2}.
\]
The multiplier of \(\mathcal H-I\) vanishes on the opposite-sign quadrants and has
modulus two on the same-sign quadrants. Fubini therefore yields
\[
\|\mathcal H F_{\varepsilon,g}-F_{\varepsilon,g}\|_2^2
=
4\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(p)|^2
\int_0^{|p|}
\frac2\pi\frac{\sin^2(\varepsilon t)}{t^2}\,dt\,dp,
\]
which is the displayed identity after \(u=\varepsilon t\).

Since \(0\le J(s)\le s\) and \(J(s)/s\to1\), dominated convergence proves the
finite half-Sobolev limit, while Fatou's lemma proves divergence when the weighted
Fourier energy is infinite.

For the jump law, in distributions
\[
g'=h+\sum_j\Delta_j\delta_{x_j},\qquad h\in L^1.
\]
Hence the high-frequency leading term of \(i\xi\widehat g(\xi)\) is the finite
exponential sum generated by the jumps. Its diagonal terms give
\[
\int_{1<|\xi|<R}|\xi|\,|\widehat g(\xi)|^2\,d\xi
=
\frac{S(g)}{\pi}\log R+o(\log R),
\]
while off-diagonal terms are oscillatory. Substituting this asymptotic into the
exact kernel identity proves the logarithmic formula.

## Prior boundary

A published 18 September 2026 result already determines the exact hard-strip
asymptotic for \(g=\mathbf 1_{(-1,1)}\), including the normalized leading constant
\(2/\pi\), and even gives a convergent expansion. That constant is therefore not a
contribution of the present result.

The contribution retained here is the arbitrary-\(L^2\)-cutoff spectral identity,
the exact \(\dot H^{1/2}\) if-and-only-if threshold, and the general finitely-jumped
cutoff coefficient. The hard-strip formula is only a specialization.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns diagonal-strip cutoff profiles. Except for indicator \(g\), the
profile is not itself the indicator of a set. It does not classify all approximate
invariant sets or settle stability of the invariant-set problem. The motivating 2026
preprint is very recent and its full text was not available through the accessible
text interface during this assessment.
