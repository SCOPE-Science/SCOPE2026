# Exact additive lengths of the norm-one torus in every odd characteristic

## Statement

Let \(q\) be an odd prime power, let \(K=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), write \(\bar x=x^q\), and let
\[
N(x)=x\bar x,\qquad T=\{x\in K^*:N(x)=1\}.
\]
Let \(\chi\) be the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_q\), extended by \(\chi(0)=0\). For \(S\in K\), define
\[
\ell_T(S)=\min\{r\ge 0:S=t_1+\cdots+t_r,\ t_i\in T\},
\]
with the empty sum equal to zero.

Then, for every \(S\in K\),
\[
\ell_T(S)=
\begin{cases}
0,&S=0,\\
1,&N(S)=1,\\
2,&S\ne0,\ N(S)\ne1,\ \chi\!\left(1-\dfrac4{N(S)}\right)\in\{0,-1\},\\
3,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

For every nonzero \(S\), membership in the two-fold sumset has the slightly cleaner form
\[
S\in T+T
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi\!\left(1-\frac4{N(S)}\right)\in\{0,-1\}.
\]
For such a nonzero \(S\), if \(S=t+u\) with \(t,u\in T\), the unordered pair \(\{t,u\}\) is the root multiset of
\[
X^2-SX+\frac{S}{\bar S}.
\]
Thus the two-term decomposition is unique up to order; the two roots coincide exactly when \(1-4/N(S)=0\).

The sumsets satisfy
\[
\boxed{|T+T|=\frac{q^2+2q+3}{2}}
\]
for every odd \(q\), while the three-fold sumset has a characteristic-dependent endpoint:
\[
\boxed{
T+T+T=
\begin{cases}
K,&\chi(-3)\in\{0,-1\},\\
K\setminus\{0\},&\chi(-3)=1.
\end{cases}}
\]
Equivalently, for odd prime powers,
\[
T+T+T=K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\not\equiv1\pmod 3.
\]
When \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), zero is the unique element missing from the exact three-fold sumset, even though its additive length is zero and every nonzero field element still has additive length at most three.

Let \(\varepsilon=1\) when \(\chi(-3)\in\{0,-1\}\), and \(\varepsilon=0\) when \(\chi(-3)=1\). The numbers \(L_j=|\{S:\ell_T(S)=j\}|\) are
\[
L_0=1,\qquad L_1=q+1,
\]
\[
L_2=\frac{(q+1)(q+1-2\varepsilon)}2,
\qquad
L_3=\frac{(q+1)(q-5+2\varepsilon)}2.
\]
In particular, the additive diameter generated by \(T\) is two for \(q=3\) and three for every odd \(q>3\).

## Proof

### Two summands

Assume first that \(S=t+u\ne0\) with \(t,u\in T\). Since \(\bar t=t^{-1}\) and \(\bar u=u^{-1}\),
\[
\bar S=t^{-1}+u^{-1}=\frac{t+u}{tu}=\frac S{tu},
\]
so
\[
tu=\frac S{\bar S}.
\]
Hence \(t,u\) are the roots of
\[
f_S(X)=X^2-SX+\frac S{\bar S}.
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
D=S^2-4\frac S{\bar S}
 =S^2\left(1-\frac4{N(S)}\right).
\]
Put
\[
d=1-\frac4{N(S)}\in\mathbb F_q.
\]
If \(t\ne u\), then
\[
\delta=\frac{t-u}{S}
\]
satisfies \(\delta^2=d\). Conjugating and using \(tu=S/\bar S\) gives \(\bar\delta=-\delta\). Since the characteristic is odd, \(\delta\notin\mathbb F_q\); therefore \(d\) is a nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_q\). If \(t=u\), then \(D=0\), hence \(d=0\). Thus a two-term representation forces \(\chi(d)\in\{0,-1\}\).

Conversely, suppose \(\chi(d)\in\{0,-1\}\). If \(d=0\), then \(N(S)=4\), so
\[
t=u=S/2
\]
has norm one and \(S=t+u\). If \(d\) is a nonsquare, choose \(\delta\in K\setminus\mathbb F_q\) with \(\delta^2=d\). Then \(\bar\delta=-\delta\). Define
\[
t_\pm=\frac S2(1\pm\delta).
\]
They sum to \(S\), and
\[
N(t_\pm)
=\frac{N(S)}4(1\pm\delta)(1\mp\delta)
=\frac{N(S)}4(1-d)=1.
\]
Thus \(t_\pm\in T\). This proves the two-sum criterion and the quadratic formula. Excluding \(S=0\) and \(N(S)=1\) gives the asserted exact-length-two case.

### Size of the two-fold sumset

For nonzero \(S\), whether \(S\in T+T\) depends only on \(a=N(S)\in\mathbb F_q^*\), through
\[
a\longmapsto 1-\frac4a.
\]
This map is a bijection from \(\mathbb F_q^*\) onto \(\mathbb F_q\setminus\{1\}\). The admissible target values are zero together with the \((q-1)/2\) nonsquares, hence there are \((q+1)/2\) admissible norm values. Every nonzero norm fiber in \(K\) has size \(q+1\), and \(0=t+(-t)\) belongs to \(T+T\). Therefore
\[
|T+T|=1+\frac{q+1}{2}(q+1)
=\frac{q^2+2q+3}{2}.
\]

For norm one, the normalized discriminant is \(-3\). Consequently
\[
T\cap(T+T)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi(-3)\in\{0,-1\}.
\]
Because multiplication by an element of \(T\) preserves \(T+T\), this is an all-or-none condition on \(T\): in the first case \(T\subseteq T+T\), and in the second \(T\cap(T+T)=\varnothing\).

### Three summands

Every element of \(T\) lies in \(T+T+T\), since for any \(a\in T\),
\[
S=S+a+(-a).
\]
Now fix \(S\ne0\) with \(S\notin T\). Consider
\[
\phi_S:T\to\mathbb F_q^*,\qquad \phi_S(\beta)=N(S-\beta).
\]
The codomain is nonzero because \(S\notin T\). For fixed \(a\in\mathbb F_q^*\), the equation \(\phi_S(\beta)=a\), after using \(\bar\beta=\beta^{-1}\), becomes a quadratic equation in \(\beta\):
\[
\bar S\,\beta^2-(N(S)+1-a)\beta+S=0.
\]
Hence every fiber has size at most two and
\[
|\operatorname{Im}\phi_S|\ge\frac{q+1}{2}.
\]
Let
\[
G=\left\{a\in\mathbb F_q^*:\chi\!\left(1-\frac4a\right)\in\{0,-1\}\right\}.
\]
The same bijection used above gives \(|G|=(q+1)/2\). Since \(G\) and \(\operatorname{Im}\phi_S\) are subsets of the \((q-1)\)-element set \(\mathbb F_q^*\), they must intersect. Choose \(\beta\in T\) with \(N(S-\beta)\in G\). By the two-sum criterion, \(S-\beta\in T+T\), so \(S\in T+T+T\). Thus every nonzero element of \(K\) is a sum of three norm-one elements.

Finally,
\[
0\in T+T+T
\]
if and only if some element of \(T\) is a sum of two elements of \(T\), because
\[
t_1+t_2+t_3=0 \iff t_1+t_2=-t_3\in T.
\]
The preceding norm-one criterion gives
\[
0\in T+T+T \iff \chi(-3)\in\{0,-1\}.
\]
This proves the exact three-fold sumset statement.

### Length distribution

The only possible lengths are now \(0,1,2,3\). There is one element of length zero and \(q+1\) elements of length one. The admissible norm set for a two-term sum has \((q+1)/2\) elements. If \(\varepsilon=1\), norm one is among them and its entire \((q+1)\)-element fiber already has length one; if \(\varepsilon=0\), it is not among them. Hence
\[
L_2=(q+1)\left(\frac{q+1}{2}-\varepsilon\right),
\]
and subtraction from \(q^2\) gives the formula for \(L_3\).

For the congruence form, if \(q=p^m\) and \(p\ne3\), the standard quadratic-character identity gives \(\chi_q(-3)=-1\) exactly when \(q\equiv2\pmod3\); in characteristic three it is zero. Thus \(\chi(-3)\ne1\) exactly when \(q\not\equiv1\pmod3\).

## Relation to recent work

Shi, Li, Xia, Helleseth and Ozbudak recently proved the characteristic-three case \(q=3^m\) while developing maximum-likelihood decoding for Gashkov--Sidel'nikov codes. Their theorem gives the characteristic-three additive-length criterion, the same two-fold sumset cardinality, \(T+T+T=K\), and the corresponding length distribution. Their proof uses the characteristic-three identities \(4=1\) in the normalized discriminant and \(t=(-t)+(-t)\) to place \(T\) inside \(T+T\). Their conclusion explicitly identifies norm-one tori in other odd characteristics as a natural direction.

The theorem above supplies that odd-characteristic extension. Two changes are essential outside characteristic three: the discriminant becomes \(1-4/N(S)\), including a genuine repeated-root case at \(N(S)=4\), and \(T\subseteq T+T\) can fail. In fact, when \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), \(T\cap(T+T)=\varnothing\) and zero is the unique element omitted by the exact three-fold sumset. Nevertheless every nonzero element still has additive length at most three.

Generalized Zetterberg-code covering-radius results in odd characteristic study related multiplicative subgroups and sums with base-field coefficients. They establish covering-radius-three statements in broad parameter ranges (and ultimately all odd-characteristic cases), but the inspected formulations do not give the unweighted norm-one-torus additive-length classification above.

## Limitations

The result concerns the additive geometry of the full norm-one torus in a quadratic finite-field extension. It does not by itself produce maximum-likelihood decoders for arbitrary odd-characteristic code families: the ternary Gashkov--Sidel'nikov decoding reduction uses a special signed-column bijection, and generalized Zetterberg codes allow a different coefficient structure. No claim is made here about higher-dimensional norm-one tori, higher extension degree, or efficient search procedures optimized beyond the direct decomposition argument.
