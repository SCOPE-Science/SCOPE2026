# Quadratic anisotropy slaving in diffusive simplex covariance dynamics

## Result

Kim, Park, and Shim (arXiv:2609.16978v1) study the diffusive nonlocal flow generated by squared random-simplex volumes. In the subcritical regime 1 <= n < d, the covariance eigenvalues satisfy

    lambda_i' = -4 c_n lambda_i e_{n-1}(lambda with i omitted) + 2 gamma,
    c_n = (n+1)/n!,

and converge to the common equilibrium

    lambda_* = [ gamma n! / (2(n+1) C(d-1,n-1)) ]^(1/n).

The source identifies the two linearized covariance rates

    alpha_perp     = 4 c_n C(d-2,n-1) lambda_*^(n-1),
    alpha_parallel = 4 c_n n C(d-1,n-1) lambda_*^(n-1),

and proves that anisotropic covariance relaxes at alpha_perp, whereas the invariant isotropic family relaxes at alpha_parallel. Its proof bounds the centered nonlinear remainder by a quadratic error. For the genuinely multipoint range 2 <= n < d, that quadratic term has a universal leading coefficient and determines the first nonzero isotropic correction.

Let

    bar_lambda(t) = tr(Sigma_t)/d,
    A_t = Sigma_t - bar_lambda(t) I_d.

If the initial covariance is anisotropic, then there is a nonzero traceless symmetric matrix Q, diagonal in the preserved eigenbasis of Sigma_0, such that

    exp(alpha_perp t) A_t -> Q

in Frobenius norm. Moreover,

    (bar_lambda(t) - lambda_*) / ||A_t||_F^2
      -> C_{d,n}
       = n(n-1) / [2 d lambda_* (d(n-2)+n)] > 0.

Equivalently,

    bar_lambda(t) - lambda_*
      ~ C_{d,n} ||Q||_F^2 exp(-2 alpha_perp t).

Thus every anisotropic covariance trajectory in this range eventually approaches the isotropic equilibrium with mean eigenvalue strictly above lambda_*, regardless of the initial sign of bar_lambda(0)-lambda_*. The slow traceless mode forces a universal scalar recoil at exactly twice its decay rate.

The simplex interaction itself has the analogous limit

    [e_n(Sigma_t) - C(d,n) lambda_*^n] / ||A_t||_F^2
      -> K_{d,n}
       = C(d-2,n-2) lambda_*^(n-2) (d-n) / [d(n-2)+n] > 0.

Since the source interaction functional is V_n[rho_t] = c_n e_n(Sigma_t), its excess above equilibrium also decays at 2 alpha_perp and approaches zero from above.

## Proof

The source proves the exact pairwise identity

    lambda_i(t)-lambda_j(t)
      = (lambda_i(0)-lambda_j(0))
        exp[-4 c_n integral_0^t e_{n-1}(lambda with i,j omitted) ds].

For n < d every eigenvalue converges exponentially to lambda_*. Hence the exponent integrand equals

    C(d-2,n-1) lambda_*^(n-1) + O(exp(-c t))

for some c > 0. The error is integrable, so every scaled pairwise gap exp(alpha_perp t)(lambda_i-lambda_j) has a finite limit; it is nonzero whenever the corresponding initial gap is nonzero. Because

    delta_i := lambda_i-bar_lambda = d^(-1) sum_j (lambda_i-lambda_j),

there are constants q_i with sum_i q_i = 0 and not all q_i zero such that exp(alpha_perp t) delta_i -> q_i. This proves the matrix limit with eigenvalues q_i.

For centered variables delta_i with sum_i delta_i = 0, the elementary symmetric polynomial satisfies the exact combinatorial expansion

    e_n(r+delta_1,...,r+delta_d)
      = sum_{k=0}^n C(d-k,n-k) r^(n-k) e_k(delta).

Since e_1(delta)=0 and e_2(delta)=-1/2 sum_i delta_i^2,

    e_n(r+delta)
      = C(d,n) r^n
        - (1/2) C(d-2,n-2) r^(n-2) ||delta||_2^2
        + O(||delta||_2^3).

Averaging the eigenvalue equations gives the exact scalar equation

    bar_lambda' = 2 gamma - (4 c_n n/d) e_n(lambda_1,...,lambda_d).

Set y = bar_lambda-lambda_*. Expanding about the isotropic equilibrium gives

    y' + alpha_parallel y
      = B_{d,n} ||A_t||_F^2
        + O(y^2 + |y| ||A_t||_F^2 + ||A_t||_F^3),

where

    B_{d,n} = (2 c_n n/d) C(d-2,n-2) lambda_*^(n-2) > 0.

The two rates satisfy the strict gap

    alpha_parallel - 2 alpha_perp
      = 4 c_n C(d-2,n-2) lambda_*^(n-1)
        [d(n-2)+n]/(n-1) > 0

for every 2 <= n < d. The source already provides ||A_t||_F = O(exp(-alpha_perp t)) and a stable scalar variation-of-constants equation with quadratic forcing, so the gap first improves y to O(exp(-2 alpha_perp t)). Substituting the actual anisotropy limit then yields

    y' + alpha_parallel y
      = B_{d,n} ||Q||_F^2 exp(-2 alpha_perp t)
        + o(exp(-2 alpha_perp t)).

Variation of constants gives

    exp(2 alpha_perp t) y(t)
      -> B_{d,n} ||Q||_F^2 / (alpha_parallel-2 alpha_perp).

The quotient simplifies to C_{d,n}, proving the recoil formula and its positive sign.

Finally, applying the centered elementary-symmetric expansion once more and substituting the recoil coefficient gives K_{d,n}. Its factor d-n is positive in the subcritical regime, proving that the interaction energy approaches equilibrium from above.

## Pairwise exception

For n=1 the covariance equation is linear in Sigma. The quadratic coefficient above is absent and alpha_parallel=alpha_perp. The quadratic slaving law is therefore specific to genuinely multipoint simplex interactions.

## Relation to prior work

The covariance reduction, equilibrium classification, and sharp first-order rates are results of arXiv:2609.16978v1 and are not claimed as new here. The source uses only an O(D(t)^2) bound for the centered elementary-symmetric remainder and an O(exp(-2 alpha_perp t)) bound for the scalar forcing. The present result resolves that remainder into its leading coefficient and sign, proves that no quadratic resonance occurs for 2 <= n < d, and extracts the universal mean-covariance and interaction-energy tails.

The authors' nondiffusive predecessor arXiv:2606.21918 concerns rank collapse without diffusion and does not contain this diffusive isotropic-equilibrium correction. General stable-manifold and mode-slaving principles are standard; originality is restricted to the source-specific coefficient formulas, signs, and energy consequence above.

## Verification

`artifacts/verify_asymptotics.py` checks the rate gap, recoil coefficient, and interaction-energy coefficient symbolically; checks the centered elementary-symmetric expansion in several finite dimensions; and integrates one (d,n)=(5,4) covariance system as a numerical sanity check. The numerical ratios converge to the predicted constants and are supporting evidence only.

## Limitations

This result concerns covariance dynamics for anisotropic initial covariance in 2 <= n < d. It does not give a second-order expansion of the full probability measure in Wasserstein distance, where transport memory from the initial distribution can introduce another decay mechanism. Isotropic data stay on the invariant isotropic family, n=1 is linear at covariance level, and the critical case n=d has anisotropic equilibria and requires a separate analysis.
