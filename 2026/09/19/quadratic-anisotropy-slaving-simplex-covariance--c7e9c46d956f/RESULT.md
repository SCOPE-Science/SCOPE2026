# Quadratic anisotropy slaving in diffusive simplex covariance dynamics

## Result

Kim, Park, and Shim, arXiv:2609.16978v1, study the diffusive nonlocal equation generated by squared random-simplex volumes. For the subcritical range \(1\le n<d\), the covariance eigenvalues \(\lambda_i(t)\) satisfy
\[
\lambda_i'=-4c_n\lambda_i e_{n-1}(\widehat\lambda_i)+2\gamma,
\qquad c_n=\frac{n+1}{n!},
\tag{1}
\]
and converge to the common equilibrium value
\[
\lambda_*=\left(\frac{\gamma n!}{2(n+1)\binom{d-1}{n-1}}\right)^{1/n}.
\tag{2}
\]
The source identifies two linearized covariance rates,
\[
\alpha_\perp=4c_n\binom{d-2}{n-1}\lambda_*^{n-1},
\qquad
\alpha_\parallel=4c_n n\binom{d-1}{n-1}\lambda_*^{n-1},
\tag{3}
\]
and proves that anisotropic covariance relaxes at the slow rate \(\alpha_\perp\), while purely isotropic covariance relaxes at \(\alpha_\parallel\). Its proof also observes that the scalar mean-eigenvalue equation is forced by an \(O(e^{-2\alpha_\perp t})\) term, but only uses this to obtain a coarser bound on the isotropic component.

For the genuinely multipoint range
\[
2\le n<d,
\tag{4}
\]
that quadratic forcing has a universal leading coefficient and determines the full first nonzero isotropic correction.

Let
\[
\bar\lambda(t):=\frac1d\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_t,
\qquad
A_t:=\Sigma_t-\bar\lambda(t)I_d.
\tag{5}
\]
Assume that the initial covariance is anisotropic, i.e. it is not a scalar multiple of \(I_d\). Then there exists a nonzero traceless symmetric matrix \(Q\), diagonal in the preserved eigenbasis of \(\Sigma_0\), such that
\[
\boxed{
 e^{\alpha_\perp t}A_t\longrightarrow Q
}
\qquad\text{in Frobenius norm.}
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\bar\lambda(t)-\lambda_*}{\|A_t\|_F^2}
\longrightarrow
C_{d,n}:=
\frac{n(n-1)}{2d\lambda_*\,[d(n-2)+n]}>0.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\bar\lambda(t)-\lambda_*
\sim C_{d,n}\|Q\|_F^2 e^{-2\alpha_\perp t}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every anisotropic covariance trajectory in (4) eventually approaches the isotropic equilibrium with its mean eigenvalue strictly above \(\lambda_*\), independently of the initial sign of \(\bar\lambda(0)-\lambda_*\). The slow anisotropic mode forces a universal scalar recoil at exactly twice its decay rate.

The simplex-volume interaction has an analogous universal quadratic tail. Since
\[
\mathcal V_n[\rho_t]=c_n e_n(\Sigma_t),
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e_n(\Sigma_t)-\binom dn\lambda_*^n}{\|A_t\|_F^2}
\longrightarrow
K_{d,n}:=
\binom{d-2}{n-2}\lambda_*^{n-2}
\frac{d-n}{d(n-2)+n}>0.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathcal V_n[\rho_t]-c_n\binom dn\lambda_*^n
\sim c_nK_{d,n}\|Q\|_F^2e^{-2\alpha_\perp t},
\tag{10}
\]
so the interaction energy approaches its equilibrium value from above at the doubled anisotropy rate.

## Proof

### 1. The anisotropic mode has an actual asymptotic amplitude

The source proves the exact pairwise-difference identity
\[
\lambda_i(t)-\lambda_j(t)
=(\lambda_i^0-\lambda_j^0)
\exp\left[-4c_n\int_0^t
 e_{n-1}(\widehat\lambda_i(s),\widehat\lambda_j(s))\,ds\right].
\tag{11}
\]
For \(n<d\), all eigenvalues converge exponentially to \(\lambda_*\). Therefore
\[
e_{n-1}(\widehat\lambda_i(t),\widehat\lambda_j(t))
=\binom{d-2}{n-1}\lambda_*^{n-1}+O(e^{-ct})
\tag{12}
\]
for some \(c>0\). The error in (12) is integrable, and (3), (11) imply that every scaled difference has a finite limit:
\[
e^{\alpha_\perp t}(\lambda_i(t)-\lambda_j(t))
\longrightarrow q_{ij}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(\lambda_i^0\ne\lambda_j^0\), then the right-hand side of (11) shows that \(q_{ij}\ne0\).

Writing \(\delta_i=\lambda_i-\bar\lambda\), one has the exact identity
\[
\delta_i=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d(\lambda_i-\lambda_j).
\tag{14}
\]
Hence
\[
e^{\alpha_\perp t}\delta_i(t)\longrightarrow q_i
\tag{15}
\]
for constants \(q_i\) satisfying \(\sum_iq_i=0\). Since the initial covariance is anisotropic, at least one pair in (13) has a nonzero limit, and therefore \(q=(q_1,\dots,q_d)\ne0\). This proves (6), with \(Q\) having eigenvalues \(q_i\).

### 2. The centered elementary-symmetric expansion starts at quadratic order

For arbitrary \(r\) and centered variables \(\delta_1,\dots,\delta_d\) with \(\sum_i\delta_i=0\), the elementary symmetric polynomial satisfies
\[
e_n(r+\delta_1,\dots,r+\delta_d)
=
\sum_{k=0}^n
\binom{d-k}{n-k}r^{n-k}e_k(\delta_1,\dots,\delta_d).
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(e_1(\delta)=0\) and
\[
e_2(\delta)=-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^d\delta_i^2,
\tag{17}
\]
we obtain, as \(\delta\to0\),
\[
e_n(r+\delta)
=
\binom dn r^n
-\frac12\binom{d-2}{n-2}r^{n-2}\|\delta\|_2^2
+O(\|\delta\|_2^3).
\tag{18}
\]
For \(n=2\), the displayed expansion is exact because there are no terms of degree three or higher.

### 3. Quadratic forcing of the mean eigenvalue

Averaging (1) gives the exact scalar equation
\[
\bar\lambda'
=2\gamma-\frac{4c_nn}{d}e_n(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d).
\tag{19}
\]
Set
\[
y(t)=\bar\lambda(t)-\lambda_*.
\]
Using (18) with \(r=\bar\lambda\), the equilibrium identity, and Taylor expansion of the isotropic scalar field at \(\lambda_*\), equation (19) becomes
\[
y'+\alpha_\parallel y
=B_{d,n}\|A_t\|_F^2
+O\!\left(y^2+|y|\|A_t\|_F^2+\|A_t\|_F^3\right),
\tag{20}
\]
where
\[
B_{d,n}:=
\frac{2c_nn}{d}\binom{d-2}{n-2}\lambda_*^{n-2}>0.
\tag{21}
\]

The source already proves \(\|A_t\|_F=O(e^{-\alpha_\perp t})\) and writes the mean equation as a stable scalar equation with forcing \(O(e^{-2\alpha_\perp t})\). In the range (4), however, the two linear rates satisfy the stronger strict inequality
\[
\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp
=
4c_n\binom{d-2}{n-2}\lambda_*^{n-1}
\frac{d(n-2)+n}{n-1}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
The source's variation-of-constants factor therefore immediately improves the mean estimate to
\[
y(t)=O(e^{-2\alpha_\perp t}).
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (6), (20), and (23) yields
\[
y'+\alpha_\parallel y
=B_{d,n}\|Q\|_F^2e^{-2\alpha_\perp t}
+o(e^{-2\alpha_\perp t}).
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(\alpha_\parallel>2\alpha_\perp\), variation of constants gives
\[
e^{2\alpha_\perp t}y(t)
\longrightarrow
\frac{B_{d,n}}{\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp}\|Q\|_F^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The coefficient simplifies to
\[
\frac{B_{d,n}}{\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp}
=
\frac{n(n-1)}{2d\lambda_*\,[d(n-2)+n]},
\tag{26}
\]
which proves (7) and (8).

### 4. Interaction-energy coefficient

Applying (18) once more and using (7),
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_n(\Sigma_t)-\binom dn\lambda_*^n
={}&n\binom dn\lambda_*^{n-1}(\bar\lambda-\lambda_*)\\
&-\frac12\binom{d-2}{n-2}\lambda_*^{n-2}\|A_t\|_F^2
+o(\|A_t\|_F^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Substituting (7) and simplifying gives exactly
\[
K_{d,n}
=
\binom{d-2}{n-2}\lambda_*^{n-2}
\frac{d-n}{d(n-2)+n},
\tag{28}
\]
which is positive because \(n<d\). This proves (9) and (10).

## Why the pairwise case is different

For \(n=1\), the interaction is pairwise and the covariance equation is linear in \(\Sigma\). The quadratic coefficient in (21) is absent, while the source rates satisfy \(\alpha_\parallel=\alpha_\perp\). Thus the quadratic slaving mechanism above is specific to the genuinely multipoint interactions \(n\ge2\); it is not a reformulation of the pairwise model.

## Relation to the source result

The source proves the sharp leading covariance rate \(\alpha_\perp\) for anisotropic data and identifies \(\alpha_\parallel\) as the faster isotropic linearized rate. In its proof, the centered expansion of \(e_n\) is used only through the bound
\[
\left|e_n(\lambda)-\binom dn\bar\lambda^n\right|\le C D(t)^2,
\]
and the resulting scalar forcing is estimated only in magnitude. The present result resolves that quadratic remainder, proves that no quadratic resonance occurs for \(2\le n<d\), and extracts its universal coefficient and sign. It therefore refines, rather than changes, the source's sharp leading covariance theorem.

## Limitations

The result concerns the covariance dynamics in the subcritical genuinely multipoint regime \(2\le n<d\) and requires anisotropic initial covariance. It does not provide a second-order asymptotic expansion of the full probability measure in \(W_2\), whose decay can also contain transport memory from the initial distribution. Isotropic initial covariance remains on the invariant isotropic line and relaxes at \(\alpha_\parallel\), while the pairwise case \(n=1\) has no quadratic forcing of the type above. The critical case \(n=d\), where anisotropic equilibria persist, is a different asymptotic regime and is not covered.
