# Norm saturation of radial Toeplitz quantization for a logarithmic Fock weight

## Result

Fix \(n\ge 1\), and on \(\mathbb C^n\) consider the radial weighted Fock space
\[
\mathcal A_h(\mathbb C^n)
=
\mathcal O(\mathbb C^n)\cap L^2(\mathbb C^n,e^{-2h(|z|)}\,dz)
\]
for
\[
h(r)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le r\le 1,\\[1mm]
\frac12(\log r)^{3/2},&r>1.
\end{cases}
\]
For a bounded radial symbol \(a=a(r)\), the Toeplitz operator \(T_a\) acts by a scalar
\(\lambda_a(m)\) on the homogeneous-polynomial space \(P^m(\mathbb C^n)\), where
\[
\lambda_a(m)
=
\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty
a(r)r^{2m+2n-1}e^{-2h(r)}\,dr}
{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty
r^{2m+2n-1}e^{-2h(r)}\,dr}.
\]
Write
\[
\Lambda_n(a)=(\lambda_a(m))_{m\ge0}\in\ell^\infty(\mathbb N_0),
\qquad
\mathfrak B_n=\Lambda_n(L^\infty_{\rm rad}).
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{\overline{\mathfrak B_n}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}
=\ell^\infty(\mathbb N_0).}
\]

More precisely:

1. For every \(c=(c_m)\in\ell^\infty\), there is a bounded radial symbol
   \(a_c\) with \(\|a_c\|_\infty=\|c\|_\infty\) such that
   \[
   \Lambda_n(a_c)-c\in c_0.
   \]

2. Every coordinate vector \(e_N\in c_0\) is a uniform limit of eigenvalue
   sequences of bounded radial Toeplitz operators. Hence
   \[
   c_0\subset \overline{\mathfrak B_n}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}.
   \]

Consequently, under the homogeneous decomposition
\[
\mathcal A_h(\mathbb C^n)=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}P^m(\mathbb C^n),
\]
the norm-closed algebra generated by bounded radial Toeplitz operators is the
entire \(\mathrm U(n)\)-commutant:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{\rm rad}(\mathcal A_h)
=
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm U(n)}(\mathcal A_h)
\cong \ell^\infty(\mathbb N_0).
}
\]
Thus the strong-operator density of invariant Toeplitz operators becomes norm
density for radial symbols for this weight. The compact part corresponds exactly
to \(c_0\):
\[
\mathcal T_{\rm rad}(\mathcal A_h)\cap\mathcal K
\cong c_0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathcal T_{\rm rad}(\mathcal A_h)/
(\mathcal T_{\rm rad}(\mathcal A_h)\cap\mathcal K)
\cong \ell^\infty/c_0.
\]
Equivalently, the Gelfand spectrum of the radial Toeplitz algebra is
\(\beta\mathbb N_0\), and its corona is
\(\beta\mathbb N_0\setminus\mathbb N_0\).

## Context

Bdarneh (2026) studies Toeplitz \(C^*\)-algebras on radially weighted Fock
spaces. For the weight above in one complex dimension, Section 9 constructs a
single bounded radial shell symbol whose eigenvalue sequence satisfies
\[
\lambda_\varphi(m)-(-1)^m\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves failure of the classical square-root-metric description.

The same shell concentration is substantially stronger: it asymptotically
interpolates an arbitrary bounded sequence, not only the alternating sequence.
A second, local moment argument near the origin recovers the missing \(c_0\)
corrections in operator norm. Together they identify the full radial Toeplitz
algebra.

For comparison, in the classical Gaussian Fock space the norm closure is the
proper algebra of bounded sequences uniformly continuous for the square-root
metric (Esmeral--Maximenko). García--Maximenko later gave constructive
approximation of convergent sequences in that classical setting.

## Proof

Set
\[
A_{m,n}
=
\int_0^\infty r^{2m+2n-1}e^{-2h(r)}\,dr.
\]
For \(r>1\), put \(u=\log r\) and
\[
M=2m+2n,\qquad
F_M(u)=Mu-u^{3/2}.
\]
Then
\[
A_{m,n}
=
\frac1M+\int_0^\infty e^{F_M(u)}\,du.
\]
The unique maximizer is
\[
u_M=\frac49M^2,
\qquad
F_M(u_M)=\frac4{27}M^3.
\]

### 1. Pairwise shells capture asymptotically all moment mass

For \(m\ge0\), define
\[
R_{m,n}
=
\exp\!\left(\frac49(2m+2n+1)^2\right),
\qquad
R_{-1,n}=0,
\]
and
\[
E_{m,n}=[R_{m-1,n},R_{m,n}).
\]
The logarithmic endpoints around the saddle are
\[
u_M^-=\frac49(M-1)^2,\qquad
u_M^+=\frac49(M+1)^2.
\]
For large \(M\),
\[
A_{m,n}\ge e^{F_M(u_M)-1}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
F_M(u_M^-)-F_M(u_M)
=
-\frac4{27}(3M-2),
\]
and
\[
F_M(u_M^+)-F_M(u_M)
=
-\frac4{27}(3M+2).
\]

On \([0,u_M^-]\), \(F_M\) is increasing, hence
\[
\int_0^{u_M^-}e^{F_M(u)}\,du
\le
u_M^-e^{F_M(u_M^-)}.
\]
On \([u_M^+,\infty)\),
\(F_M'(u_M^+)=-1\) and \(F_M'\) is decreasing, so
\[
\int_{u_M^+}^\infty e^{F_M(u)}\,du
\le
e^{F_M(u_M^+)}.
\]
Together with the negligible \([0,1]\)-contribution, this gives
\[
\frac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus E_{m,n}}
r^{2m+2n-1}e^{-2h(r)}\,dr}
{A_{m,n}}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]

Now take arbitrary \(c=(c_m)\in\ell^\infty\), and define
\[
a_c(r)=c_m\qquad(r\in E_{m,n}).
\]
Then \(\|a_c\|_\infty=\|c\|_\infty\). Since \(a_c-c_m\) vanishes on
\(E_{m,n}\),
\[
|\lambda_{a_c}(m)-c_m|
\le
2\|c\|_\infty
\frac{\displaystyle\int_{(0,\infty)\setminus E_{m,n}}
r^{2m+2n-1}e^{-2h(r)}\,dr}
{A_{m,n}}.
\]
By (1),
\[
\lambda_{a_c}(m)-c_m\to0.
\]
Hence
\[
\Lambda_n(a_c)-c\in c_0.
\tag{2}
\]

This is an asymptotic interpolation theorem for every bounded target sequence.

### 2. Local Jacobi probes recover \(c_0\) in uniform norm

Fix \(N\ge0\). Let \(Q_{N,n}\) be any degree-\(N\) polynomial orthogonal to
all polynomials of degree \(<N\) in
\[
L^2([0,1],x^{n-1}\,dx).
\]
For instance one may take a nonzero multiple of the shifted Jacobi polynomial
\[
P_N^{(0,n-1)}(2x-1).
\]
Define
\[
I_{N,n}
=
\int_0^1 Q_{N,n}(x)x^{N+n-1}\,dx.
\]
Since \(Q_{N,n}\) has exact degree \(N\), orthogonality implies
\(I_{N,n}\ne0\).

For \(0<\delta<1\), set
\[
a_{N,\delta}(r)
=
\frac{2A_{N,n}}{I_{N,n}}\,
\delta^{-2N-2n}
Q_{N,n}\!\left(\frac{r^2}{\delta^2}\right)
\mathbf 1_{[0,\delta]}(r).
\]
Because \(h=0\) on \([0,1]\), the substitution
\(x=r^2/\delta^2\) gives
\[
\lambda_{a_{N,\delta}}(m)=0
\quad(0\le m<N),
\qquad
\lambda_{a_{N,\delta}}(N)=1.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(m>N\),
\[
\lambda_{a_{N,\delta}}(m)
=
\frac{A_{N,n}}{A_{m,n}}\,
\delta^{2(m-N)}
\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^1
Q_{N,n}(x)x^{m+n-1}\,dx}{I_{N,n}}.
\]
Let \(K_{N,n}=\|Q_{N,n}\|_{L^\infty[0,1]}\). Then
\[
\left|
\int_0^1Q_{N,n}(x)x^{m+n-1}\,dx
\right|
\le
\frac{K_{N,n}}{m+n}.
\]
Also
\[
A_{m,n}
\ge
\int_0^1r^{2m+2n-1}\,dr
=
\frac1{2(m+n)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\lambda_{a_{N,\delta}}(m)|
\le
\frac{2A_{N,n}K_{N,n}}{|I_{N,n}|}
\delta^{2(m-N)}
\le C_{N,n}\delta^2
\qquad(m>N).
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) show
\[
\|\Lambda_n(a_{N,\delta})-e_N\|_\infty
\le C_{N,n}\delta^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence every \(e_N\) lies in
\(\overline{\mathfrak B_n}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}\), and since this closure is a
closed linear subspace,
\[
c_0\subset\overline{\mathfrak B_n}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}.
\tag{5}
\]

### 3. Norm saturation

Given arbitrary \(c\in\ell^\infty\), choose \(a_c\) from Step 1. By (2),
\[
e:=\Lambda_n(a_c)-c\in c_0.
\]
By (5), \(e\in\overline{\mathfrak B_n}\), while
\(\Lambda_n(a_c)\in\mathfrak B_n\). Hence
\[
c=\Lambda_n(a_c)-e\in\overline{\mathfrak B_n}.
\]
Thus
\[
\overline{\mathfrak B_n}^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}
=\ell^\infty.
\]

Finally, the \(\mathrm U(n)\)-representation on
\(\mathcal A_h(\mathbb C^n)\) decomposes multiplicity-freely as
\(\bigoplus_{m\ge0}P^m(\mathbb C^n)\). Therefore its commutant consists
precisely of operators
\[
\bigoplus_{m\ge0}c_m I_{P^m},
\qquad c\in\ell^\infty,
\]
with operator norm \(\|c\|_\infty\). The preceding norm-density gives the claimed
operator-algebra identity. Such a block-diagonal operator is compact exactly
when \(c_m\to0\), yielding the quotient statement.

## Correctness checks

- The shell argument uses disjoint shells and only the normalized radial moment
  measures; no pointwise reproducing-kernel asymptotics are required.
- The arbitrary-sequence lift preserves the exact symbol norm
  \(\|a_c\|_\infty=\|c\|_\infty\).
- The \(c_0\) argument is uniform over all tail indices \(m>N\), not merely
  pointwise in \(m\); inequality (4) is the key norm estimate.
- No claim is made that every bounded diagonal sequence is the eigenvalue
  sequence of a single bounded radial symbol. The conclusion is norm density.
- Compactness is checked at the operator level using the finite-dimensional
  homogeneous blocks.

## Originality and limitations

To the best of our knowledge, the full norm-saturation statement above is not
stated in the cited literature. Bdarneh's 2026 preprint proves, for this
logarithmic weight in one dimension, one alternating asymptotic eigenvalue
sequence and concludes only that the square-root-metric description fails. The
argument above upgrades that phenomenon to arbitrary bounded asymptotic
interpolation, supplies a separate uniform \(c_0\)-recovery mechanism, extends
the conclusion to every \(n\ge1\), and identifies the entire radial Toeplitz
\(C^*\)-algebra with the full \(\mathrm U(n)\)-commutant.

Classical radial Toeplitz theory on Gaussian Fock spaces and standard weighted
Bergman spaces gives proper slowly-oscillating or square-root-uniformly
continuous sequence algebras, not \(\ell^\infty\). The 2025 constructive
approximation result of García--Maximenko concerns convergent sequences in the
classical Gaussian Fock space.

A residual prior-art risk remains from literature on radial Toeplitz algebras
for nonstandard Fock-type or moment spaces that may formulate an equivalent
asymptotic-disjointness criterion in different language. No such result was
found in searches by the exact weight, small-Fock terminology, radial Toeplitz
sequence algebras, full diagonal algebras, or \(\ell^\infty\) interpolation.

The result is specific to the displayed logarithmic weight (and the local flat
part \(h=0\) used for the explicit \(c_0\) probes). It does not classify which
general radial weights have full \(\ell^\infty\) norm closure, nor does it assert
surjectivity of the single-symbol eigenvalue map
\(\Lambda_n:L^\infty_{\rm rad}\to\ell^\infty\).
