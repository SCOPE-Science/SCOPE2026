# Review

**Same-model review: passed. Cross-model review: not yet performed.**

## Correctness

**PASS.** The flat signed chaos has an exact cumulant generating function obtained by multiplying centered chi-square moment generating functions. Its Taylor expansion gives variance one, third cumulant `2 sqrt(2) rho_m/sqrt(m)`, fourth cumulant `12/m`, and a controlled remainder on the Gaussian extreme scale when `m/(log p)^{5/3}->infinity`. Inverting the saddlepoint equation produces the cubic and quartic terms in the rate function, including the `-rho_m^2 x^4/m` contribution generated by the square of the cubic coefficient.

Under exponential tilting, each elementary summand is tilted by `O(sqrt(log p/m))=o(1)`. Uniform tilted moments and Berry--Esseen therefore recover the usual saddlepoint prefactor, which matches the normal Mills prefactor. This yields the stated relative-tail expansion. Substitution at `b_p+y/b_p`, followed by independence across coordinates, gives the translated Gumbel limit. Maximizing the difference of two translated Gumbel cdfs gives the stated closed Kolmogorov limit.

The positive and balanced families are exact specializations. Both have `r_4=m` and fourth cumulant `12/m`; their third cumulants differ. For `m asymp (log p)^{5/2}`, the balanced tail correction vanishes while the positive cubic correction diverges, giving the explicit same-effective-rank separation. The numerical artifact independently evaluates exact chi-square and Bessel-density tails and checks the asymptotic direction and constants.

## Originality

**PASS, to the best of our knowledge, with a narrow claim.** Cai--Hu (2026) prove a general signed-chaos-to-Gaussian maximum comparison and obtain the sufficient condition `r_{4,min}/log^6 p -> infinity`; their phase curve is explicitly described as schematic. They do not state a sharp flat-spectrum critical window, a sign-imbalance correction, or a same-`r_4` counterexample to one-parameter sharpness.

The underlying one-dimensional moderate-deviation exponents are prior art and are not claimed as new. Fang--Koike (2023) explicitly analyze Wiener-chaos moderate deviations and use the positive flat centered-chi-square second chaos to show sharpness of the `m^{1/6}` relative-normal range. Classical Cramer--Petrov expansions give the same cubic mechanism. Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Koike (2023) establish sharp `log^3 d/n` high-dimensional Gaussian-approximation dependence in skewed settings and a `log^2 d/n` improvement under smooth zero-skewness structure. Schulte--Thäle (2016) provide cumulant-based moderate deviations on fixed Wiener chaos.

Searches under quadratic-chaos maxima, signed Gaussian quadratic chaos, effective rank, centered chi-square maxima, zero skewness, Cramer/Edgeworth maxima, and Gumbel critical windows found no prior statement of the combined formula with sign imbalance, the translated-Gumbel constants, or the explicit same-effective-rank separation for flat signed quadratic chaoses. The Fang--Koike and Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Koike full theorem statements relevant to the principal prior mechanisms were inspected. Older classical extreme-value treatments of triangular arrays were not exhaustively inspected and remain the main residual originality risk for the translated-Gumbel formulation. The novelty claim is therefore restricted to the quadratic-chaos/effective-rank synthesis and explicit formulas in this record.

## Value

**PASS.** The result converts a schematic effective-rank transition in a new high-dimensional U-statistic theory into two exact benchmarks. It identifies a concrete missing state variable--spectral sign imbalance/third cumulant--and proves that two targets with the same variance, fourth cumulant and effective rank can lie on opposite sides of Gaussianization. The critical-window constants quantify the failure rather than merely giving upper or lower orders, and they provide test cases for future sharper general chaos-comparison bounds.

## Limitations

The coordinates are independent and the spectrum is flat in magnitude. The result is a benchmark for the chaos target, not a replacement for the finite-sample U-statistic-to-chaos approximation. The tail expansion is stated only when `m/(log p)^{5/3}->infinity`; very sparse ranks are outside the claim. Generic cubic and quartic moderate-deviation exponents are prior art. Older triangular-array extreme-value literature was not exhaustively inspected. Cross-model review has not been performed.
