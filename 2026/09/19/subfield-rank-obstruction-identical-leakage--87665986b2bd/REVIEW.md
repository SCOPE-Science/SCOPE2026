# Same-model review

**Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.**

## Correctness

PASS.

The central reduction is direct. For a nonzero computation-code word \(a=uG_{\rm comp}\), restrict the product code to the one-dimensional family of input codewords \((u_1c,\ldots,u_Kc)\) generated by an arbitrary base codeword \(c\). If the coordinates of \(a\) have \(B\)-span basis \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\), identical \(B\)-linear leakages satisfy
\[
g_j(a_ic_j)=\sum_\ell b_{i,\ell}g_j(\alpha_\ell c_j),
\]
so all product leakages can be synthesized from at most \(r\) leakage values per base share. Recovering the input secret vector then recovers \(c_0\) from any nonzero component of \(u\). This proves the rank-transfer theorem without a probabilistic or genericity assumption.

For the systematic single-output code, rank one is equivalent to the existence of nonzero \((b_i)\in B^K\) with \(\sum b_i\mu_i\in B\), exactly dependence of \(\mu_i+B\) in \(F/B\). If these classes are independent, a generator row has rank two and rank one is excluded, proving that the minimum is exactly two. The dimension consequence \(K\ge m\Rightarrow\rho_B=1\) follows from \(\dim_B(F/B)=m-1\).

The finite verifier independently exhausts representative \(\mathbb F_{2^m}/\mathbb F_2\) cases and agrees with the quotient criterion. The enumeration is supplementary and is not used as a proof of the general theorem.

## Originality

PASS, to the best of our knowledge.

The directly motivating source, Aoutouf--Augot arXiv:2609.19929, was inspected in the sections defining LERSs, general computation codes, the product-code repair problem, the subfield-subcode rank criterion, the numerical examples, and the section on identical leakage. That paper proves the collapse only for simple addition (and indicates a similar argument for array summation), reports simulations for weighted sums with distinct nontrivial coefficients, and explains LFSR accumulation. It does not state a computation-code minimum-subfield-rank obstruction, the projective subfield-word criterion, or the quotient-space classification for one output.

Searches were performed for combinations of: identical leakage functions, Massey secret sharing, linear computations, computation code, linear exact repair, subfield rank, projective subfield codeword/subcode, quotient space, and weighted linear relation. These searches located the motivating paper and the preceding subfield-subcode LERS work, but no equivalent theorem. The SCOPE archive was also searched for Massey secret sharing and linear exact repair/leakage and no overlapping record was found; recent repository changes were checked before publication.

The main residual originality risk is the recency of arXiv:2609.19929. A near-simultaneous note or a later revision could independently include the same elementary restriction argument. The WCC 2026 extended abstract on subfield-subcode LERSs was located and its conference listing inspected; it predates the computation-code setting and is not evidence of the present theorem. No inaccessible source was identified whose title or abstract specifically suggests this rank-transfer statement.

## Value

PASS.

The result turns the source paper's isolated addition impossibility and empirical weighted-sum observations into one invariant of the computation code. It gives a sharp rank-one/no-new-vulnerability criterion, a complete classification for systematic single-output relations, an extension-degree barrier \(K\ge m\), and a structural explanation of why LFSRs can accumulate several independent subsymbols under identical leakage. In the practically relevant two-input weighted-sum example, it shows that distinct nontrivial coefficients are not enough: over quadratic extensions every coefficient pair necessarily collapses to a base one-subsymbol repair.

## Limitations

The obstruction is necessary rather than sufficient when the minimum subfield rank exceeds one. It does not prove that quotient-independent weighted sums admit identical-leakage attacks, and it does not improve the probabilistic existence analysis for independently chosen leakage functions. The theorem is restricted to linear leakages and linear computation codes over finite fields. Priority risk is elevated because the motivating preprint is new.
