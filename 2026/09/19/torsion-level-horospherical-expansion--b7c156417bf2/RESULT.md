# Torsion levels in small horo-convex hyperbolic domains expand faster than horospheres

## Statement

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb H^n\), \(n\ge 2\), be a bounded smooth strictly horo-convex domain with
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\le \frac1{2\sqrt n},
\]
and let \(u\) solve
\[
\Delta u=-2\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
\]
Set
\[
v=-\sqrt u,
\qquad m=\min_{\Omega}v<0.
\]
Zhang and Zhou prove, in the course of their power-convexity theorem, the stronger tensor inequality
\[
\nabla^2v-|\nabla v|g>0\qquad\text{in }\Omega.
\tag{1}
\]
The following geometric consequences hold.

**Theorem.**

1. The function \(v\) has a unique critical point \(x_*\), it is a nondegenerate minimum, and every regular level
   \[
   \Sigma_t=\{v=t\},\qquad m<t<0,
   \]
   is diffeomorphic to \(S^{n-1}\). The levels form a nested smooth foliation of \(\Omega\setminus\{x_*\}\).

2. With outward normal \(\nu=\nabla v/|\nabla v|\), every \(\Sigma_t\) satisfies
   \[
   \mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_t}>g_{\Sigma_t}.
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Hence every nontrivial torsion superlevel set \(\{u>c\}\) has strictly horo-convex boundary. For \(n\ge3\), every \(\Sigma_t\) has strictly positive intrinsic sectional curvature; for \(n=2\), its geodesic curvature is strictly larger than \(1\).

3. Fix \(m<a<b<0\), let \(F_{a,b}:\Sigma_a\to\Sigma_b\) be the level-flow map generated by
   \[
   Y=\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|^2},
   \qquad \frac{d}{dt}v(F_{a,t}(x))=1,
   \]
   and define the length of the corresponding flow arc by
   \[
   \ell_{a,b}(x)=\int_a^b\frac{dt}{|\nabla v|(F_{a,t}(x))}.
   \]
   Then, for every \(x\in\Sigma_a\) and \(W\in T_x\Sigma_a\setminus\{0\}\),
   \[
   |dF_{a,b}(W)|>e^{\ell_{a,b}(x)}|W|,
   \tag{3}
   \]
   \[
   |\nabla v|(F_{a,b}(x))>e^{\ell_{a,b}(x)}|\nabla v|(x),
   \tag{4}
   \]
   and
   \[
   J_{n-1}F_{a,b}(x)>e^{(n-1)\ell_{a,b}(x)}.
   \tag{5}
   \]
   If
   \[
   \delta_{a,b}=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathbb H^n}(\Sigma_a,\Sigma_b),
   \]
   then \(\ell_{a,b}(x)\ge\delta_{a,b}\), so in quadratic-form notation
   \[
   F_{a,b}^*g_{\Sigma_b}>e^{2\delta_{a,b}}g_{\Sigma_a},
   \tag{6}
   \]
   and in particular
   \[
   |\Sigma_b|>e^{(n-1)\delta_{a,b}}|\Sigma_a|.
   \tag{7}
   \]

Thus the torsion-level flow has a sharp hyperbolic comparison interpretation: horospherical normal flow expands tangential lengths exactly at rate \(e^s\), while these torsion levels expand strictly faster when compared using the length of their own outward flow arcs, and at least by the corresponding horospherical factor determined by ambient level separation.

## Proof

Zhang--Zhou establish (1) under the hypotheses above. Since \(v=0\) on \(\partial\Omega\) in the continuous boundary sense and \(v<0\) in \(\Omega\), \(v\) attains an interior minimum. Equation (1) implies \(\nabla^2v>0\), so \(v\) is strictly geodesically convex and has at most one critical point. Hence the minimum \(x_*\) is the unique critical point. At \(x_*\), \(|\nabla v|=0\), and (1) gives \(\nabla^2v(x_*)>0\), so the minimum is nondegenerate.

For \(m<t<0\), \(\Sigma_t\) is regular. If \(X\in T\Sigma_t\), then with \(r=|\nabla v|\) and \(\nu=\nabla v/r\),
\[
\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_t}(X,X)
=\langle\nabla_X\nu,X\rangle
=\frac{\nabla^2v(X,X)}r
>|X|^2,
\]
which proves (2). The Gauss equation in ambient sectional curvature \(-1\) then gives positive intrinsic sectional curvature in every tangent 2-plane when \(n\ge3\). In dimension two, (2) is exactly \(k_g>1\). Since \(u=v^2\) with \(v<0\), a nontrivial superlevel \(\{u>c\}\) is the sublevel \(\{v<-\sqrt c\}\), whose outward normal is \(\nu\); hence its boundary is strictly horo-convex.

The nondegenerate minimum has sufficiently small level spheres diffeomorphic to \(S^{n-1}\) by the Morse lemma. There are no further critical values. On any compact regular slab the vector field \(Y=\nabla v/r^2\) is smooth and its flow identifies all levels, proving the spherical foliation statement.

For the quantitative expansion, let \(W(t)=dF_{a,t}(W)\). It remains tangent to \(\Sigma_t\), and \([Y,W]=0\). Therefore
\[
\frac d{dt}\log|W(t)|
=\frac{\nabla^2v(W(t),W(t))}{r^2|W(t)|^2}
>\frac1r.
\]
Integrating from \(a\) to \(b\) proves (3), because the flow-arc speed is \(|Y|=1/r\). Multiplying the corresponding singular-value inequalities in an orthonormal tangent basis gives (5).

For the gradient size, along a flow arc
\[
\frac d{dt}\log r
=\frac{\nabla^2v(\nu,\nu)}{r^2}
>\frac1r,
\]
which integrates to (4). Finally, every flow arc joining \(\Sigma_a\) to \(\Sigma_b\) has length \(\ell_{a,b}(x)\) at least the ambient distance \(\delta_{a,b}\) between the two compact levels. Substitution into (3) and (5) gives (6) and (7).

## Context and novelty boundary

Zhang--Zhou's theorem states strict convexity of \(-\sqrt u\), and their proof explicitly establishes the stronger tensor inequality (1). Their introduction mentions horo-convex superlevel sets for the **first Dirichlet eigenfunction in \(\mathbb H^2\)**, due to Dai--Ennis--Nguyen--Wei, but does not state the torsion-level conclusions above. Grossi--Provenzano already prove uniqueness and nondegeneracy of the torsion maximum on horo-convex domains in \(\mathbb H^2\), so that two-dimensional fact is not claimed as new here.

The new contribution claimed here, to the best of our knowledge, is the geometric extraction of (1) into: strict horo-convexity of every torsion level in all dimensions covered by Zhang--Zhou; the nested spherical foliation; and especially the pointwise exponential metric, Jacobian, and gradient expansion laws (3)--(7), which compare the torsion-level flow directly with horospherical expansion.

## Limitations

- The result inherits Zhang--Zhou's hypotheses: strict horo-convexity and the small-diameter condition \(\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\le1/(2\sqrt n)\).
- No claim is made that these hypotheses are necessary for torsion-level horo-convexity or for the expansion inequalities.
- The conclusion concerns the geometry of regular torsion levels and their canonical gradient flow; it does not provide an explicit positive lower bound for \(\nabla^2v-|\nabla v|g\) depending only on dimension and diameter.
- The motivating preprint is recent, so a later revision or an unindexed parallel observation could overlap with these consequences.
- The \(\mathbb H^2\) uniqueness/nondegeneracy of the torsion maximum is prior work and is not part of the originality claim.
