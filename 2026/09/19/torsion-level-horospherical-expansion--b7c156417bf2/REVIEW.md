# Same-model review

## Correctness

The proof uses the explicit Zhang--Zhou conclusion
\[
\nabla^2(-\sqrt u)-|\nabla(-\sqrt u)|g>0
\]
under their stated strict horo-convexity and diameter hypotheses. On a regular level of \(v=-\sqrt u\), division of the tangential Hessian by \(|\nabla v|\) gives the second fundamental form, so all principal curvatures are strictly larger than the horospherical value \(1\). The unique nondegenerate minimum follows from strict Hessian positivity, and the spherical foliation follows from the Morse lemma plus the absence of any other critical value.

For the level-flow map generated by \(Y=\nabla v/|\nabla v|^2\), a transported tangent vector \(W\) obeys
\[
\frac d{dt}\log|W|=
\frac{\nabla^2v(W,W)}{|\nabla v|^2|W|^2}
>\frac1{|\nabla v|}.
\]
The same tensor inequality in the normal direction gives
\[
\frac d{dt}\log|\nabla v|>
\frac1{|\nabla v|}.
\]
The right-hand side integrates to the hyperbolic length of the flow arc. Products of tangent singular values give the Jacobian estimate. Since each arc length is at least the ambient distance between the endpoint levels, the uniform metric and area estimates follow. Sign conventions were checked against geodesic spheres, whose outward principal curvature is \(\coth r>1\) and whose induced metric scales by \(\sinh^2r\).

## Originality

The closest source is Zhang--Zhou (arXiv:2609.02516v1). Their Theorem 1.1 states strict convexity of \(-\sqrt u\), while the proof establishes the stronger tensor inequality used here. The accessible full text was checked for torsion superlevel, level-set, principal-curvature, and gradient-flow formulations; the introduction discusses horo-convex superlevel sets only for the first eigenfunction in dimension two, not for the torsion function. No statement of the exponential pullback-metric, Jacobian, gradient-growth, or area expansion laws was located there.

Dai--Ennis--Nguyen--Wei (arXiv:2607.27120) concern the first Dirichlet eigenfunction in \(\mathbb H^2\), not the torsion equation. Grossi--Provenzano (Math. Ann. 389 (2024)) already prove that positive semistable solutions, including torsion, on horo-convex domains in \(\mathbb H^2\) have a unique nondegenerate maximum; that fact is explicitly excluded from the originality claim. Searches using torsion/hyperbolic/horo-convex level-set, principal-curvature, horosphere, and gradient-flow terminology did not locate the stated all-dimensional torsion-level expansion theorem.

The originality claim is therefore limited to the geometric consequences assembled here, especially the quantitative horospherical expansion mechanism, and is **to the best of our knowledge**. The main residual risk is that the Zhang--Zhou preprint is very recent, so a later revision or a not-yet-indexed parallel note could state the same consequences.

## Value

The tensor inequality in the motivating work is analytically stronger than its headline convexity theorem. The present result identifies its direct geometric content: the torsion function canonically foliates the domain by spherical hypersurfaces whose curvature is everywhere beyond the horospherical threshold, and the natural level flow has an explicit exponential expansion law. This supplies a reusable geometric interpretation of the tensor \(\nabla^2v-|\nabla v|g\) and yields quantitative comparisons between separated torsion levels without solving the torsion equation explicitly.

## Limitations

The result inherits strict horo-convexity and the small-diameter hypothesis from Zhang--Zhou. It gives no dimension-only lower gap beyond principal curvature \(1\), no converse, and no removal of the diameter restriction. The two-dimensional unique-maximum statement is prior work. Independent audit has not been performed.
