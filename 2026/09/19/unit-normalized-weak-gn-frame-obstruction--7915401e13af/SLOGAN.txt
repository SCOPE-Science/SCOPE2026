Energy-normalizing weak test functions does not make Euclidean weak Gauss–Newton basis-robust: two unit tests spanning a fixed space can force a correction of size 1/epsilon and amplify the true energy error without bound. The amplification is exactly one half of sqrt(kappa)+1/sqrt(kappa), where kappa is the test Gram condition number; Euclidean measurement loss is energy-consistent precisely for tight frames, while the previously known Gram-dual weighting removes this coordinate artifact.
