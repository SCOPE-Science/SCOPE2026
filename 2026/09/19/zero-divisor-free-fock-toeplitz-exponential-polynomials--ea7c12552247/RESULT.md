# Finite-frequency pluriharmonic Fock Toeplitz operators form a zero-divisor-free algebra

## Result

Let
\[
\mathcal F^2(\mathbb C^n)
=
\left\{h\in\mathcal O(\mathbb C^n):
\pi^{-n}\int_{\mathbb C^n}|h(z)|^2e^{-|z|^2}\,dV(z)<\infty
\right\}
\]
be the standard Fock space. Define the finite-frequency exponential-polynomial class
\[
\mathcal E_n
=
\left\{
\sum_{\alpha\in A}p_\alpha(z)e^{\alpha\cdot z}:
A\subset\mathbb C^n\text{ finite},\ 
p_\alpha\in\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_n]
\right\}.
\]
Every element of \(\mathcal E_n\) is entire of exponential type and belongs to
\(\mathcal F^2(\mathbb C^n)\). The space \(\mathcal E_n\) is dense in Fock space because it contains the holomorphic polynomials.

For a pluriharmonic symbol
\[
f=F+\overline G,\qquad F,G\in\mathcal E_n,
\]
the Toeplitz operator \(T_f=P(f\,\cdot)\) leaves \(\mathcal E_n\) invariant. Let
\(\mathfrak T_n^{\exp}\) denote the algebra of endomorphisms of \(\mathcal E_n\)
generated by all such restrictions \(T_f|_{\mathcal E_n}\).

**Theorem.**
For every \(n\ge1\), the algebra \(\mathfrak T_n^{\exp}\) has no zero divisors.

Consequently, for every finite \(m\ge2\) and nonzero pluriharmonic exponential-polynomial symbols
\[
f_j=F_j+\overline{G_j},\qquad F_j,G_j\in\mathcal E_n,
\]
one has
\[
T_{f_1}\cdots T_{f_m}\ne0
\quad\text{on }\mathcal E_n.
\]
In fact there is a holomorphic polynomial \(p\) for which
\[
T_{f_1}\cdots T_{f_m}p\ne0.
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
T_{f_1}\cdots T_{f_m}p=0
\qquad\text{for every holomorphic polynomial }p,
\]
then at least one \(f_j\) is identically zero.

In particular, Qin's 2026 Question 5.1 has an affirmative answer for the
finite-frequency exponential-polynomial subclass, in every complex dimension.
The statement is stronger on this subclass because it also covers products of arbitrary finite length and detects nonvanishing already on the polynomial test set.

## Context

Qin's recent zero-product paper gives nonzero Toeplitz zero divisors for very broad symbol classes:
bounded nonradial Schwartz symbols when \(n\ge2\), and smooth symbols of controlled
quadratic-exponential growth in one variable whose extended Toeplitz product vanishes on all holomorphic polynomials.
The paper then asks whether the zero-product implication is restored for pluriharmonic symbols
\[
f=f_1+\overline{f_2},\qquad g=g_1+\overline{g_2},
\]
when all entire parts are of exponential type.

The class \(\mathcal E_n\) is a natural finite-frequency subfamily of that question.
Toeplitz operators with exponential-polynomial symbols have appeared previously in the
Segal--Bargmann literature, notably in Cichoń's work on adjoints of unbounded Toeplitz operators.
The point here is not the adjoint formula itself, but that the resulting finite
differential--difference operator algebra admits a zero-divisor-free normal form.

## Toeplitz action on the exponential-polynomial domain

For \(a,b\in\mathbb C^n\), write
\[
(E_a h)(z)=e^{a\cdot z}h(z),
\qquad
(\tau_bh)(z)=h(z+b),
\]
and let \(\partial_j=\partial/\partial z_j\).

If
\[
q(z)=p(z)e^{a\cdot z},
\qquad
p(z)=\sum_\nu c_\nu z^\nu,
\]
define
\[
p^\#(\partial)=\sum_\nu\overline{c_\nu}\,\partial^\nu.
\]
A direct Gaussian reproducing-kernel calculation gives, for every \(h\in\mathcal E_n\),
\[
T_qh=p(z)E_ah
\]
and
\[
T_{\overline q}h
=
p^\#(\partial)\tau_{\overline a}h.
\]
Indeed,
\[
T_{\overline q}h(z)
=
\pi^{-n}\int_{\mathbb C^n}
\overline{p(w)}e^{\overline a\cdot\overline w}
h(w)e^{z\cdot\overline w}e^{-|w|^2}\,dV(w),
\]
and differentiation of the reproducing formula gives the displayed identity.
All integrals are absolutely justified because every function involved has only polynomial times linear-exponential growth against a Gaussian.

Thus every generator of \(\mathfrak T_n^{\exp}\) is a finite sum of operators built from
multiplication by \(z_j\), differentiation \(\partial_j\), exponential multiplication \(E_a\),
and translation \(\tau_b\).

## A zero-divisor-free differential--difference algebra

Let \(A_n\) be the \(n\)-th Weyl algebra generated by \(z_j,\partial_j\) with
\[
[\partial_j,z_k]=\delta_{jk}.
\]
It is a domain: with the standard order filtration its associated graded algebra is the polynomial ring
\[
\operatorname{gr}A_n
\cong
\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_n,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n].
\]

For \(g=(a,b)\in\mathbb C^n\times\mathbb C^n\), set
\[
U_g=E_a\tau_b.
\]
Then
\[
U_gz_jU_g^{-1}=z_j+b_j,
\qquad
U_g\partial_jU_g^{-1}=\partial_j-a_j,
\]
and, for \(g=(a,b)\), \(h=(c,d)\),
\[
U_gU_h=e^{c\cdot b}\,U_{g+h}.
\]
Hence finite sums
\[
\sum_{g}P_gU_g,\qquad P_g\in A_n,
\]
have the multiplication law of a twisted crossed product of \(A_n\) by an additive subgroup
of \(\mathbb C^{2n}\).

For any two such finite sums, all occurring frequencies lie in a finitely generated additive subgroup
\[
G\subset\mathbb C^{2n}.
\]
Because \(G\) is torsion-free and finitely generated, \(G\cong\mathbb Z^r\) and admits a translation-invariant total order.
If
\[
A=\sum_{g\in G}P_gU_g,\qquad
B=\sum_{h\in G}Q_hU_h
\]
are nonzero and \(g_0,h_0\) are the largest support points, then \(g_0+h_0\) is the unique largest support point of \(AB\).
Its coefficient is
\[
P_{g_0}\,\alpha_{g_0}(Q_{h_0})\,\sigma(g_0,h_0),
\]
where \(\alpha_{g_0}\) is the Weyl-algebra automorphism above and
\(\sigma(g_0,h_0)\ne0\) is the scalar cocycle.
Since \(A_n\) is a domain, this coefficient is nonzero. Therefore the formal twisted crossed product is a domain.

It remains to check that the operator representation has no hidden finite relations.

### Normal-form uniqueness

Suppose
\[
\sum_{(a,b)\in S}P_{a,b}(z,\partial)E_a\tau_b=0
\quad\text{on }\mathcal E_n,
\]
with \(S\) finite. Apply this operator to
\[
e_\lambda(z)=e^{\lambda\cdot z}.
\]
Writing
\[
P_{a,b}(z,\partial)=\sum_\nu p_{a,b,\nu}(z)\partial^\nu,
\]
one obtains
\[
0
=
e^{\lambda\cdot z}
\sum_a e^{a\cdot z}
\sum_b e^{\lambda\cdot b}
Q_{a,b}(z,\lambda+a),
\]
where
\[
Q_{a,b}(z,\mu)
=
\sum_\nu p_{a,b,\nu}(z)\mu^\nu
\]
is a polynomial in \((z,\mu)\).

Finite exponential-polynomial functions with distinct linear frequencies are linearly independent over polynomials.
Applying this first in the \(z\)-variable to the distinct \(a\)'s, and then in the \(\lambda\)-variable to the distinct \(b\)'s, gives
\[
Q_{a,b}=0
\]
for every pair \((a,b)\), hence every \(P_{a,b}=0\).
Thus the crossed-product normal form acts faithfully on \(\mathcal E_n\).

The differential--difference operator algebra containing \(\mathfrak T_n^{\exp}\) is therefore a domain.

## Nonzero symbols give nonzero operators

There is one remaining point: a nonzero pluriharmonic symbol must represent a nonzero Toeplitz operator on the common domain.

For the normalized Fock kernel
\[
k_z(w)=e^{w\cdot\overline z-|z|^2/2},
\]
which belongs to \(\mathcal E_n\), one has
\[
\langle T_fk_z,k_z\rangle
=
\pi^{-n}\int_{\mathbb C^n}
f(w)e^{-|w-z|^2}\,dV(w).
\]
A pluriharmonic function is harmonic on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\).
Its spherical mean about \(z\) equals \(f(z)\), and radial integration of those spherical means against the Gaussian gives
\[
\pi^{-n}\int_{\mathbb C^n}
f(w)e^{-|w-z|^2}\,dV(w)
=
f(z).
\]
The growth of the present symbols justifies the integral.
Consequently
\[
T_f|_{\mathcal E_n}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f=0.
\]
Thus every nonzero symbol in the theorem gives a nonzero element of the domain algebra, and their finite product cannot vanish.

## Polynomial witnesses

Let \(A\) be a finite differential--difference operator of the form above.
For each \(z\),
\[
\lambda\longmapsto A(e^{\lambda\cdot z})(z)
\]
is entire. Differentiating at \(\lambda=0\) gives
\[
\partial_\lambda^\nu
\bigl[A(e^{\lambda\cdot z})(z)\bigr]_{\lambda=0}
=
A(z^\nu)(z).
\]
Therefore, if \(A\) annihilates every holomorphic polynomial, then it annihilates every exponential \(e_\lambda\).
Normal-form uniqueness then forces \(A=0\).
Hence every nonzero finite product in the theorem is detected by at least one holomorphic polynomial.

## Significance and boundary

The result isolates a positive finite-frequency region inside a zero-product problem that fails dramatically for general Fock symbols.
The mechanism is algebraic: finite exponential spectra convert Toeplitz quantization into a faithful twisted crossed product of the Weyl algebra by a torsion-free abelian frequency group, and such a finite-support algebra is a domain.

This also identifies a necessary qualitative feature of any counterexample to Qin's pluriharmonic exponential-type question:
it cannot be built entirely from finitely many linear exponential frequencies with polynomial amplitudes.
Any counterexample within the full exponential-type class must leave this finite-frequency algebraic regime.

The theorem does **not** settle Qin's full Question 5.1. General entire functions of exponential type can have genuinely infinite spectral complexity and need not lie in \(\mathcal E_n\).
The result also does not assert boundedness of the individual Toeplitz operators on all of Fock space; it is formulated on the natural common dense invariant domain \(\mathcal E_n\), which is sufficient to rule out a zero product on the full operator domain whenever such a product is defined.
