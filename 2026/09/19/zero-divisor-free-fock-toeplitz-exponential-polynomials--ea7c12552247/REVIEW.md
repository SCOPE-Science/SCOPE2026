## Correctness

**PASS.** The statement is formulated on the common dense invariant domain
\[
\mathcal E_n=
\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in A}p_\alpha(z)e^{\alpha\cdot z}:A\subset\mathbb C^n
\text{ finite},\ p_\alpha\in\mathbb C[z]\right\},
\]
so it does not assume that the generally unbounded Toeplitz generators extend boundedly to all of Fock space.

For \(q(z)=p(z)e^{a\cdot z}\), Gaussian reproduction gives
\[
T_q=p(z)E_a,\qquad T_{\overline q}=p^\#(\partial)\tau_{\overline a}
\]
on \(\mathcal E_n\). The conjugations and translations were checked directly from
\[
P(\overline{w}^{\,\nu} e^{\overline a\cdot\overline w}h(w))
=\partial^\nu h(z+\overline a).
\]
Thus every pluriharmonic finite-frequency exponential-polynomial Toeplitz operator belongs to the finite differential--difference algebra generated by the Weyl algebra and the operators
\[
U_{(a,b)}=E_a\tau_b.
\]

The algebraic multiplication is
\[
U_{(a,b)}U_{(c,d)}=e^{c\cdot b}U_{(a+c,b+d)},
\]
while conjugation by \(U_{(a,b)}\) sends
\[
z_j\mapsto z_j+b_j,\qquad \partial_j\mapsto\partial_j-a_j.
\]
For the finite supports occurring in a product, the generated frequency group is a finitely generated subgroup of the additive vector space \(\mathbb C^{2n}\). It is therefore torsion-free abelian and admits a translation-invariant total order. The largest support point of a product is unique, and its coefficient is the product of two nonzero Weyl-algebra elements, one transported by an automorphism, times a nonzero scalar cocycle. Since the Weyl algebra is a domain, the formal crossed product has no zero divisors.

Faithfulness was checked separately rather than assumed. If
\[
\sum_{(a,b)}P_{a,b}(z,\partial)E_a\tau_b=0
\]
on \(\mathcal E_n\), applying it to \(e^{\lambda\cdot z}\) yields a finite exponential-polynomial identity in \(z\) and \(\lambda\). Distinct linear exponential frequencies are linearly independent over polynomials; separating the \(a\)-frequencies in \(z\) and then the \(b\)-frequencies in \(\lambda\) forces every \(P_{a,b}\) to vanish. Hence the operator representation has no hidden finite relations.

A nonzero pluriharmonic symbol also cannot represent the zero operator on \(\mathcal E_n\). For normalized Fock kernels \(k_z\in\mathcal E_n\),
\[
\langle T_fk_z,k_z\rangle
=\pi^{-n}\int_{\mathbb C^n}f(w)e^{-|w-z|^2}\,dV(w)=f(z),
\]
because a pluriharmonic function is harmonic on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\), and radial Gaussian averaging preserves harmonic functions by the spherical mean property. Polynomial times linear-exponential growth makes the integral absolutely convergent.

Finally, the polynomial-witness strengthening was checked from exponential vectors. For a finite differential--difference operator \(A\), the map
\[
\lambda\mapsto A(e^{\lambda\cdot z})(z)
\]
is entire, and its Taylor coefficients at \(\lambda=0\) are \(A(z^\nu)(z)\). Therefore an \(A\) annihilating all holomorphic polynomials annihilates every exponential vector, which by the faithful normal form implies \(A=0\).

No commutativity, boundedness, compactness, complementability, or basis-inheritance assertion is used.

## Originality

**PASS, to the best of our knowledge.** Jie Qin's arXiv:2609.20555v1 was inspected at the zero-product results and Question 5.1. The paper constructs zero products for broad non-pluriharmonic symbol classes and asks whether, for pluriharmonic symbols whose entire parts are of exponential type, \(T_fT_g=0\) forces one symbol to vanish. It does not state the finite-frequency exponential-polynomial theorem, the arbitrary finite-product extension, or the polynomial-test strengthening proved here.

Bauer--Choe--Koo (J. Funct. Anal. 268 (2015), 3017--3060) was checked as the main neighboring pluriharmonic Toeplitz reference. Its stated focus is commuting and semicommuting Toeplitz operators with pluriharmonic symbols, including several open cases; no matching finite-frequency zero-divisor theorem was found.

Cichoń, *Generalization of the Newman--Shapiro isometry theorem and Toeplitz operators. II* (Studia Math. 150 (2002), 175--188), was inspected in full at its exponential-polynomial Toeplitz section. It develops adjoint formulas for unbounded Toeplitz operators with scalar and certain operator-valued exponential-polynomial symbols. Searches in the full text for zero-product terminology and the relevant product claim found no theorem asserting that the resulting finite-frequency Toeplitz algebra is a domain. Cichoń's adjoint calculus is therefore treated as prior art, not as part of the originality claim.

Cho--Park--Zhu, *Products of Toeplitz operators on the Fock space* (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 142 (2014), 2483--2489), was checked as another adjacent product theorem. It characterizes bounded products of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic Toeplitz operators, which is a different question from algebraic zero divisors on the common exponential-polynomial domain.

Targeted searches used the source title and identifier together with “pluriharmonic”, “exponential polynomial”, “exponential type”, “Fock Toeplitz”, “zero product”, “zero divisor”, “Weyl algebra”, “differential--difference operator”, and synonymous combinations. The current SCOPE archive was also checked by source identifier, object, and claim family immediately before publication. No prior SCOPE result or external source stating the present all-dimensional finite-frequency theorem was found.

The main residual bibliographic risk is that older skew-group, crossed-product, or differential--difference algebra literature may contain a general domain lemma from which the algebraic core follows immediately. The ordered-support crossed-product argument itself is standard in spirit and is not claimed as a new abstract algebra theorem. The originality claim is limited to the Fock Toeplitz consequence, its finite-frequency formulation, the arbitrary finite-product extension, and the polynomial witness. No inaccessible paper was identified whose available metadata gives concrete positive evidence of prior coverage.

## Value

**PASS.** The result gives a clean positive region inside a newly sharpened zero-product boundary. Qin's counterexamples show that Fock Toeplitz zero divisors are abundant once one allows broad smooth or Schwartz symbol classes, while Question 5.1 asks whether pluriharmonic exponential-type structure restores rigidity. The theorem proves that rigidity throughout the natural finite-frequency exponential-polynomial subfamily, in every complex dimension.

The mechanism is reusable: finite exponential spectra turn Toeplitz quantization into a faithful twisted crossed product of the Weyl algebra by a torsion-free abelian frequency group. This also yields a concrete obstruction to future counterexamples to the full question: no counterexample can be assembled solely from finitely many linear exponential frequencies with polynomial amplitudes.

The polynomial-witness statement strengthens the conclusion in a way adapted to unbounded Toeplitz products. A nonzero product is already detected on the holomorphic polynomial core, so vanishing there is enough to force a zero factor within this class.

## Limitations

Only finite sums of polynomial amplitudes times linear exponentials are treated. General entire functions of exponential type can have genuinely infinite spectral complexity and are not covered. The theorem does not settle Qin's full Question 5.1, does not classify bounded pluriharmonic Toeplitz symbols outside this class, and does not address infinite-frequency closures.

The operators are considered on the natural common dense invariant domain \(\mathcal E_n\); no claim is made that every generator is bounded on all of Fock space. No quantitative bound is provided for the degree of a polynomial witness. The crossed-product domain mechanism is not claimed as a new general algebraic theorem.
