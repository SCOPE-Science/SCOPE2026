# Trace-class Mellin semigroup defects and zeta traces for Cesàro powers

## Result

Work on \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\) with indices \(n,k\ge 1\), and write the classical Cesàro matrix as
\[
C_{n,k}=\frac1n\mathbf 1_{\{k\le n\}}.
\]
For a kernel \(\kappa:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb C\), set
\[
W(\kappa)_{n,k}=\frac1n\kappa\!\left(\frac{k}{n}\right).
\]
This is the one-based reindexing of the sampled-ratio matrices introduced by Bellavita and Stylogiannis.

Let
\[
\omega=\mathbf 1_{(0,1]},\qquad \omega_r=\omega^{\star r}\quad (r\ge1),
\]
where \(\star\) is multiplicative convolution. Then
\[
\omega_r(t)=\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}(t)\frac{(-\log t)^{r-1}}{(r-1)!}.
\]
Put \(W_r=W(\omega_r)\). Thus \(W_1=C\).

### Theorem

For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge1\),
\[
\Delta_{p,q}:=W_pW_q-W_{p+q}\in \mathcal S_1.
\]
More precisely, for each \(p,q\) there is a constant \(A_{p,q}<\infty\) such that
\[
\|\Delta_{p,q}e_k\|_2\le A_{p,q}k^{-3/2},\qquad k\ge1.
\]
Hence the integer Mellin-convolution semigroup generated by the Cesàro kernel is multiplicative modulo trace-class operators, rather than merely modulo Hilbert--Schmidt operators.

For every \(s\ge2\) and integers \(r_1,\ldots,r_s\ge1\),
\[
W_{r_1}\cdots W_{r_s}-W_{r_1+\cdots+r_s}\in\mathcal S_1,
\]
and its canonical trace is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
W_{r_1}\cdots W_{r_s}-W_{r_1+\cdots+r_s}
\right)
=
\begin{cases}
\zeta(s),&r_1=\cdots=r_s=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, for every integer \(m\ge2\),
\[
D_m:=C^m-W_m\in\mathcal S_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(D_m)=\zeta(m).
\]
For \(m=2\),
\[
(D_2)_{n,k}
=
\frac1n\left(
H_n-H_{k-1}-\log\frac nk
\right)\mathbf 1_{\{k\le n\}},
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(C^2-W_2)=\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]

## Proof

Bellavita and Stylogiannis show that \(\omega\) belongs to their kernel algebra \(\mathcal A\), and that \(\mathcal A\) is closed under multiplicative convolution. Consequently every \(\omega_r\) belongs to \(\mathcal A\) and every \(W_r\) is bounded.

Fix \(p,q\ge1\). For \(k\le n\),
\[
(W_pW_q)_{n,k}
=
\frac1n\sum_{\ell=k}^n
\frac1\ell
\omega_p\!\left(\frac{\ell}{n}\right)
\omega_q\!\left(\frac{k}{\ell}\right),
\]
whereas the multiplicative-convolution identity gives
\[
(W_{p+q})_{n,k}
=
\frac1n\int_k^n
\frac1x
\omega_p\!\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)
\omega_q\!\left(\frac{k}{x}\right)\,dx.
\]
Thus \((\Delta_{p,q})_{n,k}\) is a one-dimensional lattice-sum error divided by \(n\).

Write
\[
L=\log(n/k),\qquad t=\log(x/k).
\]
On \(k\le x\le n\), the integrand becomes
\[
h_{p,q;n,k}(x)
=
\frac{e^{-t}}{k}
\frac{(L-t)^{p-1}t^{q-1}}{(p-1)!(q-1)!},
\qquad 0\le t\le L.
\]
For a continuous function \(h\) on \([k,n]\),
\[
\left|
\sum_{\ell=k}^n h(\ell)-\int_k^n h(x)\,dx
\right|
\le |h(n)|+\operatorname{Var}_{[k,n]}h.
\]
Differentiating the displayed logarithmic-coordinate expression shows, with a constant depending only on \(p,q\),
\[
|h_{p,q;n,k}(n)|
+
\operatorname{Var}_{[k,n]}h_{p,q;n,k}
\le
\frac{B_{p,q}}{k}
(1+L)^{p+q-2}.
\]
Indeed, after differentiation every term is \(k^{-1}e^{-t}\) times a product of nonnegative powers of \(t\) and \(L-t\); bounding those powers by \((1+L)^{p+q-2}\) and integrating \(e^{-t}\) gives the claim. Terms whose formal coefficient is \(p-1\) or \(q-1\) are absent when the corresponding index is \(1\).

Therefore
\[
|(\Delta_{p,q})_{n,k}|
\le
\frac{B_{p,q}}{nk}
\left(1+\log\frac nk\right)^{p+q-2},
\qquad n\ge k.
\]
A dyadic decomposition in \(n/k\) gives
\[
\sum_{n=k}^\infty
\frac{\left(1+\log(n/k)\right)^{2p+2q-4}}{n^2}
\le
\frac{C_{p,q}}{k}.
\]
Hence
\[
\|\Delta_{p,q}e_k\|_2
\le
A_{p,q}k^{-3/2}.
\]
Since \(\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-3/2}<\infty\), the column decomposition
\[
\Delta_{p,q}
=
\sum_{k\ge1}
(\Delta_{p,q}e_k)\otimes e_k^*
\]
converges in trace norm. Thus \(\Delta_{p,q}\in\mathcal S_1\).

The multi-factor statement follows by induction because \(\mathcal S_1\) is a two-sided ideal:
\[
W_{r_1}\cdots W_{r_s}-W_{R_s}
=
\bigl(W_{r_1}\cdots W_{r_{s-1}}-W_{R_{s-1}}\bigr)W_{r_s}
+
\Delta_{R_{s-1},r_s},
\]
where \(R_j=r_1+\cdots+r_j\).

It remains to compute the trace. Since all \(W_r\) are lower triangular,
\[
(W_r)_{n,n}
=
\frac1n\,\omega_r(1)
=
\begin{cases}
n^{-1},&r=1,\\
0,&r\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Because \(R_s\ge2\), \(W_{R_s}\) has zero diagonal. Therefore the diagonal of the trace-class multi-factor defect is \(n^{-s}\) when every \(r_j=1\), and is identically zero otherwise. Summing the diagonal yields the asserted trace formula.

For \(m=2\),
\[
(C^2)_{n,k}
=
\frac1n\sum_{\ell=k}^n\frac1\ell
=
\frac{H_n-H_{k-1}}n,
\]
while
\[
(W_2)_{n,k}
=
\frac1n\log\frac nk.
\]
This gives the explicit formula above. The elementary monotone-integral comparison also gives
\[
0\le
H_n-H_{k-1}-\log\frac nk
\le \frac1k.
\]

## Relation to prior literature and originality

Bellavita and Stylogiannis, arXiv:2609.09453v1, introduced the sampled-ratio Mellin calculus, proved for their full kernel algebra \(\mathcal A\) that
\[
W(\kappa)W(\eta)-W(\kappa\star\eta)\in\mathcal S_2,
\]
and explicitly listed Schatten refinements of the defect among the natural questions left open by their method. Their paper also identifies \(C=W(\mathbf 1_{(0,1]})\) and proves that the Cesàro kernel belongs to \(\mathcal A\), which is closed under convolution.

To the best of our knowledge, the trace-class upgrade for the entire integer convolution semigroup \(\{\omega_r\}_{r\ge1}\), and the exact zeta trace formula for the resulting Cesàro power defects, have not previously been stated. Searches using the source paper, Cesàro powers, Mellin-convolution defects, trace class, Schatten ideals, harmonic-number formulas, and zeta traces did not locate an equivalent result.

The 2025 work of Bellavita, Dellepiane and Stylogiannis characterizes Schatten membership for Rhaly (terraced) matrices. That theory is adjacent but concerns Schatten membership of the matrices themselves, rather than the discrete-versus-continuous multiplicative defects considered here.

A residual prior-art risk remains in older literature on Cesàro/Hausdorff matrices, Euler--Maclaurin corrections, or discrete Mellin quantization, where an equivalent trace-class lattice-error statement could appear under different terminology.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the specific integer convolution semigroup generated by \(\mathbf 1_{(0,1]}\). It does not assert that the generic Bellavita--Stylogiannis defect is trace class, nor does it identify an optimal Schatten exponent for arbitrary kernels in \(\mathcal A\). It also makes no claim about quasi-Schatten classes \(\mathcal S_p\) for \(p<1\).
