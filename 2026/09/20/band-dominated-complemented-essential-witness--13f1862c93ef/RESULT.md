# Complemented essential-norm witnesses for finite-fiber band-dominated maps

## Statement

Let \((X,d)\) be an infinite locally finite metric space. For every \(x\in X\),
let \(E_x\) and \(F_x\) be nonzero finite-dimensional Banach spaces. Fix either
\(1\le p<\infty\) and put
\[
 \mathcal X=(\bigoplus_{x\in X}E_x)_p,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Y=(\bigoplus_{x\in X}F_x)_p,
\]
or put
\[
 \mathcal X=(\bigoplus_{x\in X}E_x)_{c_0},
 \qquad
 \mathcal Y=(\bigoplus_{x\in X}F_x)_{c_0}.
\]
For \(S\subseteq X\), write \(P_S^E\) and \(P_S^F\) for the coordinate
projections. A bounded operator \(B:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) has propagation at
most \(R\) if
\[
 P_{\{x\}}^F B P_{\{y\}}^E=0\qquad(d(x,y)>R).
\]
Call \(A:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) band-dominated if it is an operator-norm
limit of finite-propagation operators.

Let
\[
 \rho(A)=\operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal K(\mathcal X,\mathcal Y))
\]
be the classical essential norm. Define the complemented disjoint-block lower
modulus
\[
 \Lambda(A)=\sup_E\ \inf_{0\ne z\in E}\frac{\|Az\|}{\|z\|},
\]
where the supremum is over subspaces \(E=[u_j]_{j\ge1}\subseteq\mathcal X\)
generated by normalized finitely supported vectors with pairwise disjoint
supports, and required to be the range of a projection of norm one.

**Theorem.** For every band-dominated \(A:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\),
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(A)=\rho(A).}
\]
More concretely, for every \(c<\rho(A)\) there is a normalized disjoint block
sequence \((u_j)\) such that:

1. \([u_j]\) is isometric to \(\ell_p\) in the \(p\)-sum case and to \(c_0\)
   in the \(c_0\)-sum case;
2. \([u_j]\) is 1-complemented in \(\mathcal X\);
3. \(\|Az\|\ge c\|z\|\) for every \(z\in[u_j]\).

Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal K)
 =\operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal{FSS})
 =\operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal{SS})
 =\rho(A),
 }
\]
where \(\mathcal{FSS}\) and \(\mathcal{SS}\) denote the finitely strictly
singular and strictly singular operators from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\mathcal Y\).
In particular,
\[
 A\text{ compact}\iff A\text{ finitely strictly singular}
 \iff A\text{ strictly singular}
\]
inside this band-dominated class.

If \(a_n(A)\) and \(b_n(A)\) are the approximation and Bernstein numbers, then
\[
 \boxed{b_n(A)\ge \rho(A)\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
 \lim_{n\to\infty}a_n(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n(A)=\rho(A).}
\]
Thus the essential norm is exactly the asymptotic Bernstein lower scale, and is
also exactly the best lower bound carried by a 1-complemented disjoint classical
sequence subspace.

## Proof

### 1. The essential norm forces norm far out in the coordinates

Let \(B:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) be bounded and let \(F\subset X\) be finite.
Because every \(E_x\) is finite-dimensional, \(BP_F^E\) has finite rank. Hence
\[
 \|BQ_F^E\|=\|B-BP_F^E\|\ge \rho(B),
 \qquad Q_F^E=I-P_F^E. \tag{1}
\]
This one-sided estimate is all that will be needed; no assertion that
\(\|BQ_F^E\|\) converges to the essential norm is required.

### 2. Finite propagation gives infinitely many separated almost-essential blocks

First suppose that \(B\) has propagation at most \(R\). Fix \(\eta>0\). We
construct normalized finitely supported vectors \(u_1,u_2,\ldots\) recursively.
After \(u_1,\ldots,u_{j-1}\) have been selected, let
\[
 F=N_{2R}\Big(\bigcup_{i<j}\operatorname{supp}u_i\Big).
\]
Local finiteness makes \(F\) finite. By (1), a unit vector supported outside
\(F\) can be chosen with image norm arbitrarily close to \(\rho(B)\). Since
finitely supported vectors are dense in \(\mathcal X\), it can be approximated,
without introducing coordinates in \(F\), by a normalized finitely supported
vector \(u_j\) satisfying
\[
 \|Bu_j\|>\rho(B)-\eta. \tag{2}
\]
The supports \(S_j=\operatorname{supp}u_j\) are pairwise more than \(2R\) apart.
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(Bu_j)\subseteq N_R(S_j)
\]
and the sets \(N_R(S_j)\) are pairwise disjoint.

For every finitely supported scalar sequence \((\alpha_j)\), disjointness gives
\[
 \left\|B\sum_j\alpha_j u_j\right\|
 =\left(\sum_j|\alpha_j|^p\|Bu_j\|^p\right)^{1/p}
 \ge(\rho(B)-\eta)
   \left\|\sum_j\alpha_j u_j\right\| \tag{3}
\]
in the \(p\)-sum case, with the identical argument using suprema in the \(c_0\)
case. By closure, (3) holds on \(E=[u_j]\).

### 3. The block copy is genuinely 1-complemented

For each \(j\), choose a norm-one functional \(f_j\) on the finite coordinate
block \(P_{S_j}^E\mathcal X\) with \(f_j(u_j)=1\). Define
\[
 Pz=\sum_j f_j(P_{S_j}^E z)u_j. \tag{4}
\]
The supports \(S_j\) are disjoint, so
\[
 \|Pz\|^p=\sum_j|f_j(P_{S_j}^E z)|^p
 \le\sum_j\|P_{S_j}^E z\|^p\le\|z\|^p
\]
for \(p<\infty\). In the \(c_0\) case the same estimate is by suprema, and the
coefficients in (4) tend to zero because a \(c_0\)-sum vanishes along disjoint
finite blocks escaping every finite set. Thus \(\|P\|=1\), \(P^2=P\), and
\(\operatorname{ran}P=E\). This directly verifies complementability.

### 4. Passage from finite propagation to band domination

Let \(A\) be band-dominated and fix \(c<\rho(A)\). Choose a finite-propagation
operator \(B\) so close to \(A\) that, with \(\delta=\|A-B\|\), one has
\[
 c<\rho(A)-2\delta.
\]
The essential norm is 1-Lipschitz, so \(\rho(B)\ge\rho(A)-\delta\). Choose
\(\eta>0\) with
\[
 c<\rho(B)-\eta-\delta.
\]
Steps 2 and 3 give a 1-complemented disjoint block copy \(E\) on which
\(\|Bz\|\ge(\rho(B)-\eta)\|z\|\). Hence
\[
 \|Az\|\ge\|Bz\|-\delta\|z\|>c\|z\|\qquad(z\in E). \tag{5}
\]
This proves \(\Lambda(A)\ge\rho(A)\).

Conversely, suppose \(A\) is bounded below by \(c\) on an infinite-dimensional
subspace \(E\). Every compact operator \(K\) is strictly singular, so for every
\(\varepsilon>0\) there is a unit vector \(z\in E\) with \(\|Kz\|<\varepsilon\).
Then
\[
 \|A-K\|\ge\|(A-K)z\|\ge c-\varepsilon.
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and then the infimum over compact \(K\) yields
\(c\le\rho(A)\). Thus \(\Lambda(A)\le\rho(A)\), proving the theorem.

### 5. Operator-ideal distances

Let \(S\) be strictly singular. For each \(c<\rho(A)\), use the subspace \(E\)
from the theorem. Since \(S|_E\) is not bounded below, for every \(\varepsilon>0\)
there is a unit \(z\in E\) with \(\|Sz\|<\varepsilon\). Therefore
\[
 \|A-S\|\ge c-\varepsilon.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and \(c\uparrow\rho(A)\) gives
\(\operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal{SS})\ge\rho(A)\). Since
\[
 \mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal{FSS}\subseteq\mathcal{SS},
\]
the reverse inequalities from set inclusion force equality of all three
distances with \(\rho(A)\).

### 6. Bernstein and approximation numbers

For every \(c<\rho(A)\), the theorem supplies an infinite-dimensional subspace on
which \(A\) is bounded below by \(c\). Taking arbitrary \(n\)-dimensional
subspaces inside it gives
\[
 b_n(A)\ge c,
\]
and hence \(b_n(A)\ge\rho(A)\) for every \(n\).

The finite-coordinate projections on \(\mathcal Y\) are contractive finite-rank
maps converging strongly to the identity. Consequently every compact
\(K:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) is a norm limit of finite-rank operators
\(P_F^F K\). Therefore the approximation numbers satisfy
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}a_n(A)=\rho(A).
\]
Since \(b_n(A)\le a_n(A)\), the preceding lower bound yields
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}b_n(A)=\rho(A).
\]

## Context and prior literature

Band and band-dominated operators and their Fredholm theory were developed in the
limit-operator literature, including Rabinovich--Roch--Silbermann's treatment of
operator-valued coefficients (2001) and their 2004 monograph. Seidel's 2014 survey
organizes the classical Fredholm framework. Hagger--Lindner--Seidel (2016) gives
sharp formulas for essential norms of band-dominated operators through limit
operators and develops operator-norm localization in that setting. Metric-space
extensions of limit-operator theory were developed by Spakula--Willett (2016).
Roch (2022) studies closed ideals inside the band-dominated algebra on \(\ell_2(\mathbb Z^N)\).

Those works are prior art for band domination, localization at infinity, essential
norms, Fredholm theory, and ideals of the band-dominated algebra. The claim here is
narrower and different: the classical essential norm is identified with an
explicit 1-complemented disjoint-block lower-bound modulus, which in turn gives
exact distances to the strictly singular and finitely strictly singular ideals and
the asymptotic Bernstein-number formula. The proof also allows a locally finite
metric space with nonuniform finite-dimensional fibers and does not use a group
action, richness, Property A, or a uniform bound on the fiber dimensions.

Searches through current literature under band-dominated/finite-propagation
operators, strict and finite strict singularity, complemented copies, Bernstein
numbers, uniform Roe terminology, and essential norms did not locate this combined
statement. Originality is therefore asserted only **to the best of our knowledge**.

## Limitations and sharp scope

- Finite dimensionality of every input fiber is essential to the argument that
  \(BP_F^E\) has finite rank. With an infinite-dimensional fiber, a propagation-zero
  operator supported entirely on that coordinate can already be noncompact, so the
  escaping-block conclusion can fail.
- Local finiteness is used to keep each recursively excluded metric neighborhood
  finite.
- Domain and codomain use the same outer \(p\) (or both use \(c_0\)); cross-exponent
  maps are not covered.
- The \(c_0\) case is included, but \(\ell_\infty\)-sums are not: finite-support
  density, used in Step 2, fails there.
- The supremum \(\Lambda(A)=\rho(A)\) need not be attained; the theorem guarantees
  witnesses at every lower level \(c<\rho(A)\).
- The 2004 Rabinovich--Roch--Silbermann monograph and the full 2022 Roch paper on
  ideals of band-dominated operators were not exhaustively inspected theorem by
  theorem. They are the most plausible sources for an older equivalent
  formulation. Hagger--Lindner--Seidel (2016) was inspected at the primary-source
  theorem/proof level for its essential-norm localization results; no priority
  claim is made for those antecedent ingredients.
