# A 9-by-9 elementary grading with no finite regrading

## Statement

Let \(F\) be an arbitrary field. There exists an elementary group grading on \(M_9(F)\) that cannot be regraded by any finite group. Consequently, such an elementary grading exists on \(M_n(F)\) for every \(n\ge 9\).

In the notation of Gordienko--Schnabel's Problem 1.7, if
\[
\Omega=\{n\in\mathbb N:\text{every elementary grading on }M_n(F)\text{ can be regraded by a finite group}\}
   =\{1,\ldots,n_0\},
\]
then the known lower bound \(n_0\ge3\) combines with the construction below to give
\[
\boxed{3\le n_0\le 8}.
\]

This improves the currently published explicit counterexample range \(n\ge14\) to \(n\ge9\).

## The group-theoretic input

Use the convention \(x^y=y^{-1}xy\) and \([x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\). Consider Baumslag's one-relator group
\[
B=\langle a,b\mid a=[a,a^b]\rangle.
\]
The defining relation is equivalently
\[
a^{a^b}=a^2,
\]
or
\[
b^{-1}a^{-1}ba\,b^{-1}ab\,a^{-2}=1. \tag{1}
\]
Baumslag proved that every finite quotient of \(B\) is cyclic, while \(B\) itself is non-cyclic. In particular \(a\ne1\): if \(a=1\), the presentation would leave a cyclic group generated by \(b\).

## The explicit grading

Set \(c=a^b=b^{-1}ab\) and define the following 9-tuple in \(B\):
\[
(g_1,\ldots,g_9)
=
\bigl(
1,\ a^{-1},\ a^{-2},\ ba^{-2},\ aba^{-2},\ a^{-1}c,\ c,\ ab,\ b
\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]
Give \(M_9(F)\) the elementary \(B\)-grading \(\Gamma\) determined by
\[
\deg(e_{ij})=g_i g_j^{-1}.
\]

The relation \(a^c=a^2\) is equivalent to \(ac=ca^2\), hence
\[
ca^{-2}=a^{-1}c.
\]
Using this once, the degrees around the cyclic chain of matrix units are

| matrix unit | degree in \(B\) |
|---|---|
| \(e_{2,1}\) | \(a^{-1}\) |
| \(e_{3,2}\) | \(a^{-1}\) |
| \(e_{4,3}\) | \(b\) |
| \(e_{5,4}\) | \(a\) |
| \(e_{6,5}\) | \(b^{-1}\) |
| \(e_{7,6}\) | \(a\) |
| \(e_{8,7}\) | \(b\) |
| \(e_{9,8}\) | \(a^{-1}\) |
| \(e_{1,9}\) | \(b^{-1}\) |

Thus multiplying these matrix units in the compatible order gives \(e_{11}\), and their degree product is exactly the relator (1).

## No finite regrading

Suppose, for contradiction, that the same homogeneous decomposition can be regraded by a finite group \(H\). Let \(\alpha\in H\) be the new degree assigned to the old \(a\)-component and let \(\beta\in H\) be the new degree assigned to the old \(b\)-component. Reverse matrix units show that the old \(a^{-1}\)- and \(b^{-1}\)-components receive degrees \(\alpha^{-1}\) and \(\beta^{-1}\), respectively.

The nonzero matrix-unit product around the above cycle therefore forces
\[
\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\beta\alpha\,
\beta^{-1}\alpha\beta\alpha^{-2}=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence the assignment
\[
a\mapsto\alpha,\qquad b\mapsto\beta
\]
defines a homomorphism \(B\to H\). Its image is finite, so Baumslag's theorem says that this image is cyclic. In a cyclic image the relation \(a=[a,a^b]\) forces
\[
\alpha=[\alpha,\alpha^\beta]=1.
\]

But the original \(a\)-component of \(\Gamma\) is nonzero (for example, it contains \(e_{7,6}\)) and is distinct from the identity component because \(a\ne1\). A regrading of a fixed homogeneous decomposition assigns distinct nonzero homogeneous components to distinct support degrees, with the identity component assigned degree \(1_H\). Thus \(\alpha=1_H\) is impossible. This contradiction proves that \(\Gamma\) has no finite-group regrading.

Weak equivalence to a finite-group grading is equivalent, after transporting the grading by the algebra isomorphism, to reindexing the same homogeneous components by that finite group. Therefore the same argument excludes weak equivalence to any finite-group grading.

For \(n>9\), append copies of \(g_9\) to the tuple (2). The upper-left \(M_9(F)\) remains a graded subalgebra, so any finite regrading of the larger grading would restrict to a finite regrading of \(\Gamma\). Hence counterexamples exist for every \(n\ge9\).

## Literature position

Gordienko and Schnabel asked for the exact set \(\Omega\) in 2018 and proved that it is an initial interval, with all \(n\le3\) belonging to \(\Omega\). Their original construction gave large-dimensional counterexamples. Gordienko and Pekarsky later gave a substantially smaller Higman-group construction: the current arXiv version, revised 1 August 2026, produces elementary gradings with no finite regrading for every \(n\ge14\).

The construction above uses instead Baumslag's 1969 two-generator one-relator group, whose finite quotients collapse the distinguished generator \(a\). To the best of our knowledge, targeted searches for finite-regrading counterexamples on \(M_9\), for Baumslag/Baumslag--Gersten group constructions in this grading problem, and for improvements of the \(n\ge14\) bound did not locate this 9-dimensional construction or an equal-or-better published bound.

## Limitations

The result does not determine whether \(n=4,5,6,7,8\) belong to \(\Omega\); it only narrows the remaining threshold to \(3\le n_0\le8\). It also does not claim that the universal group of the grading (2) is isomorphic to \(B\), nor does it claim the additional universal-Hopf/coaction properties established for the separate \(n\ge14\) construction of Gordienko--Pekarsky. The contribution here is specifically the smaller elementary grading obstruction to finite regrading.
