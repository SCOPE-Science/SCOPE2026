# Review: deficient-perfect numbers of the form 2^a p q

**Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.**

## Correctness

The proof was checked directly from
\[
D(n)=2n-\sigma(n)=pq-(2^{a+1}-1)(p+q+1)
\]
for \(n=2^a p q\) with odd primes \(p<q\). Positivity implies \(p>2^{a+1}-1\). Reducing modulo \(q\) then shows that \(q\) cannot divide the deficiency: the residue is \(-(2^{a+1}-1)(p+1)\), and both positive factors are strictly smaller than \(q\). Because the odd part is squarefree, the deficiency therefore has only the two possible shapes \(2^e\) and \(2^e p\), with \(0\le e\le a\).

Both algebraic branches were rederived in reverse. In the first branch, writing \(s=p-M\) with \(M=2^{a+1}-1\) gives exactly
\[
s(q-M)=M(M+1)+2^e.
\]
In the second branch, divisibility by \(p\) forces \(p\mid q+1\); writing \(q+1=kp\) gives exactly
\[
k(p-M)=2^{a+1}+2^e.
\]
Substitution back into the original deficiency formula returns \(2^e\) and \(2^e p\), respectively, so the converses do not rely on hidden congruence assumptions.

The cases \(e=0\), \(a=1\), and the ordering \(p<q\) were checked explicitly. The first branch ordering is equivalent to \(s^2<A_e\); the second has \(k\ge2\). The fixed-\(a\) bounds follow from \(e\le a\), \(s\ge1\), and \(k\ge2\).

A standalone exact-integer program independently compares the parametrization with a direct definition-based search through \(a=7\) and obtains identical sets in every case. It also reproduces the initial known examples and enumerates the parametrized sets through \(a=12\). This finite computation supports but is not needed for the proof.

## Originality

The originality claim is **to the best of our knowledge**.

The following coverage was checked:

- Tang--Ren--Li (2013) defines deficient-perfect numbers by \(\sigma(n)=2n-d\) and classifies those with at most two distinct prime factors. Its abstract was available, and the scope is also explicitly summarized in Tang--Feng (2014).
- Tang--Feng (2014) was inspected at the theorem statement and introduction. It proves that no odd deficient-perfect number has exactly three distinct prime divisors and notes the preceding classification for at most two prime factors. Its theorem does not cover even numbers \(2^a p q\).
- Current OEIS A271816 was checked, including its formulas, references, and 2026 updates. It records the two-prime family and identifies \(884=2^2\cdot13\cdot17\) as the smallest three-prime example, but does not state the two-family parametrization for \(2^a p q\).
- The 2019 paper *Perfect and Deficient Perfect Numbers* was checked at its stated scope; it concerns deficient-perfect divisors of perfect numbers rather than the present three-prime classification.
- Searches for “deficient-perfect” together with “2^a p q”, “2^k p q”, “three distinct prime factors”, “squarefree odd part”, and algebraically equivalent deficiency formulas did not locate a prior statement of the theorem.
- The current SCOPE archive was searched for deficient-perfect terminology and no overlapping record was found.

### Residual originality risk

The main residual risk is a result embedded in older divisor-sum literature under different terminology. The full text of Tang--Ren--Li (2013) was not directly inspected, although its relevant claimed scope is unambiguous in both its abstract and the subsequent 2014 primary paper. No located source gives evidence of an even three-prime classification strong enough to imply the present theorem.

## Value

The theorem treats the first even three-distinct-prime slice beyond the published two-prime classification and the known impossibility of the odd three-prime case. It is exact rather than asymptotic: for each fixed exponent \(a\), every solution is generated by divisors of two explicit integers for each \(e\le a\). The structural exclusion of the larger odd prime from the deficient divisor is what makes this finite reduction possible. The result also supplies explicit bounds on both odd primes and reproduces the known initial sequence values without an open-ended search.

## Limitations

The proof uses that the odd part \(pq\) is squarefree. It does not classify \(2^a p^b q^c\) with larger odd exponents, nor cases with four or more distinct prime factors. The finite verification is not a formal proof, and no proof-assistant formalization or independent audit is asserted.
