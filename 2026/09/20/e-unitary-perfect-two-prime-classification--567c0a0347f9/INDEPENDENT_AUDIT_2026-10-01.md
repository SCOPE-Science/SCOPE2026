# Independent audit — 2026-10-01

Final claim assessed: The only exponential-unitary perfect integer with exactly two distinct prime factors is 36; consequently any exponential-unitary perfect number that is not exponential-perfect has at least three distinct prime factors.

## Correctness — PASS

For an even two-prime candidate, parity and congruence force the normalized exponential-unitary divisor sums to split exactly as complementary pure powers. The ratio product is 2, while a geometric bound leaves only exponents 2 and 4 on the prime 2. Those cases resolve respectively to 36 and a contradiction. The finite verification is only a consistency check; the proof is uniform.

## Originality — PASS

The 2011 primary paper introduces exponential-unitary perfect numbers, gives 36 as an example, proves there are no odd examples, and asks whether any such number fails to be exponential-perfect; it does not classify two-prime support. The classical two-prime exponential-perfect analogue is logically insufficient because equality of the two notions is itself open. Targeted searches found no earlier two-prime exponential-unitary classification.

## Scientific value — PASS

This closes a natural complete support-size case and sharpens the open problem by forcing any counterexample to exponential-unitary-perfect implying exponential-perfect to have at least three distinct primes.
