# Finite-index prime-index lattice asymptotics

Let \(p_n\) be the \(n\)-th prime. Fix multiplicatively independent integers \(r,s\ge2\), write
\[
L=\log r,\qquad M=\log s,
\]
and let \(\Lambda\le \mathbb Z^2\) be a fixed full-rank sublattice of finite index
\[
h=[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda].
\]
Set
\[
\Lambda_+=\Lambda\cap\mathbb N^2,
\qquad
\mathcal P_\Lambda=\{p_{r^A s^B}:(A,B)\in\Lambda_+\},
\]
let \(S_\Lambda\) be the multiplicative semigroup generated by \(\mathcal P_\Lambda\), including \(1\), and define
\[
C_\Lambda(x)=\#\{n\le x:n\in S_\Lambda\}.
\]

The unrestricted case \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2\) is the recent two-generator problem. The theorem below shows that every fixed finite-index exponent sublattice admits the same level of strong asymptotic precision, while its leading logarithmic growth depends only on the lattice index \(h\).

## Theorem

Choose any positive integer \(q\) such that \(q\mathbb Z^2\subseteq\Lambda\), and let
\[
R=\Lambda\cap\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}^2.
\]
Thus \(|R|=q^2/h\). Put
\[
S_1=\sum_{(i,j)\in R}(iL+jM),
\qquad
S_2=\sum_{(i,j)\in R}(iL+jM)^2.
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{c_{2,\Lambda}=\frac{|R|}{q^2LM}=\frac1{hLM},}
\]
\[
\boxed{
c_{1,\Lambda}
=\frac{|R|(L+M)}{2qLM}-\frac{S_1}{q^2LM},}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
c_{0,\Lambda}={}&|R|\left(\frac14+\frac{M}{12L}+\frac{L}{12M}\right)
-\frac{S_1(L+M)}{2qLM}\\
&+\frac{S_2}{2q^2LM}-1.
\end{aligned}}
\]
These coefficients are intrinsic to \(\Lambda\); the formulas above give them from any admissible \(q\).

For \(\Re z>2\), define the lattice zeta function
\[
Z_\Lambda(z)=
\sum_{(A,B)\in\Lambda_+\setminus\{(0,0)\}}
(AL+BM)^{-z}.
\]
Then \(Z_\Lambda\) extends meromorphically to \(\Re z>-1\), with simple poles only at \(2\) and \(1\),
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=2}Z_\Lambda(z)=c_{2,\Lambda},
\qquad
\operatorname{Res}_{z=1}Z_\Lambda(z)=c_{1,\Lambda},
\]
and
\[
Z_\Lambda(0)=c_{0,\Lambda}.
\]

Put
\[
Z_{\Lambda,1}(z)=Z_\Lambda(1+z)-\frac{c_{1,\Lambda}}z,
\qquad
Z_{\Lambda,2}(z)=Z_\Lambda(2+z)-\frac{c_{2,\Lambda}}z.
\]
For \((A,B)\in\Lambda_+\setminus\{(0,0)\}\), set
\[
u_{A,B}=AL+BM,
\qquad
\lambda_{A,B}=\log p_{r^A s^B},
\]
and define
\[
\mathscr R_\Lambda=
\sum_{\Lambda_+\setminus\{(0,0)\}}
\left[
\log\frac{\lambda_{A,B}}{u_{A,B}}
-\frac{\log u_{A,B}}{u_{A,B}}
+\frac{(\log u_{A,B})^2/2-\log u_{A,B}+1}{u_{A,B}^2}
\right].
\]
This series converges absolutely. Let
\[
\kappa_0=\frac{\pi^2}{12}-\gamma_1-\frac{\gamma^2}{2},
\qquad
\kappa_1=\frac{\gamma_2}{2}+\gamma\gamma_1+
\frac{\gamma^3-\zeta(3)}{3},
\]
and
\[
A_{1,\Lambda}=c_{2,\Lambda}\zeta(3),
\qquad
A_{2,\Lambda}=c_{2,\Lambda}\zeta(2),
\]
\[
A_{3,\Lambda}
=(c_{1,\Lambda}-c_{2,\Lambda})\zeta(2)
-c_{2,\Lambda}\bigl(\zeta'(2)-\gamma\zeta(2)\bigr).
\]
Further define
\[
\nu_\Lambda
=c_{0,\Lambda}+c_{2,\Lambda}(1+\kappa_0)
+\frac{A_{2,\Lambda}(A_{3,\Lambda}-A_{2,\Lambda})}{6A_{1,\Lambda}},
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak C_\Lambda={}&
\frac1{(\log2)\sqrt{12\pi A_{1,\Lambda}}}
\exp\Bigg(
Z_\Lambda'(0)+Z_{\Lambda,1}'(0)+Z_{\Lambda,2}(0)
+Z_{\Lambda,2}'(0)\\
&\quad+\frac12Z_{\Lambda,2}''(0)-\mathscr R_\Lambda
-c_{1,\Lambda}\kappa_0+c_{2,\Lambda}\kappa_1
-\frac{(A_{3,\Lambda}-A_{2,\Lambda})^2}{12A_{1,\Lambda}}
\Bigg).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(\mathfrak C_\Lambda>0\). If
\[
W_\Lambda=
\left(\frac{\log x}{2A_{1,\Lambda}}\right)^{1/3},
\]
then, as \(x\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_\Lambda(x)\sim{}&
\mathfrak C_\Lambda W_\Lambda^{\nu_\Lambda}
\exp\Bigg(
3A_{1,\Lambda}W_\Lambda^2
-A_{2,\Lambda}W_\Lambda\log W_\Lambda
+A_{3,\Lambda}W_\Lambda\\
&\quad+\frac{c_{2,\Lambda}}6(\log W_\Lambda)^3
-\left(\frac{c_{1,\Lambda}}2
+\frac{A_{2,\Lambda}^2}{12A_{1,\Lambda}}\right)
(\log W_\Lambda)^2
\Bigg).
\end{aligned}}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\log C_\Lambda(x)\sim
3\left(\frac{\zeta(3)}{4h\log r\log s}\right)^{1/3}
(\log x)^{2/3}.}
\]
Thus the leading logarithmic term sees a finite-index exponent restriction only through its index \(h\): relative to the unrestricted lattice, its coefficient is multiplied by exactly \(h^{-1/3}\). Boundary arithmetic of the sublattice first enters at lower orders through \(c_{1,\Lambda}\), \(c_{0,\Lambda}\), and the regular zeta data.

## Collision corollary

Fix \(q\ge2\) and consider the dependent three-generator index monoid
\[
\mathcal I_{r,s;q}
=\{(r^q)^a(s^q)^b(rs)^c:a,b,c\ge0\}.
\]
Because
\[
(r^q)(s^q)=(rs)^q,
\]
exponent triples collide. Nevertheless,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_{r,s;q}
=\{r^As^B:A,B\ge0,\ A\equiv B\pmod q\}.}
\]
This is the positive cone of the index-\(q\) lattice
\[
\Lambda_q=\{(A,B)\in\mathbb Z^2:A\equiv B\pmod q\}.
\]
For this family,
\[
c_{2,q}=\frac1{qLM},\qquad
c_{1,q}=\frac{L+M}{2qLM},
\]
\[
\boxed{
c_{0,q}=\frac{L}{12qM}+\frac{M}{12qL}
+\frac{q^2-12q+2}{12q},}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\log C_{r,s;q}(x)\sim
3\left(\frac{\zeta(3)}{4q\log r\log s}\right)^{1/3}
(\log x)^{2/3}.}
\]
For \((r,s,q)=(2,3,2)\), this becomes the prime-index system generated by \(4,9,6\):
\[
\{4^a9^b6^c:a,b,c\ge0\}
=\{2^A3^B:A\equiv B\pmod2\},
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
\log C_{2,3;2}(x)\sim
3\left(\frac{\zeta(3)}{8\log2\log3}\right)^{1/3}
(\log x)^{2/3}.}
\]
The apparent three-generator description therefore does not produce a rank-three \((\log x)^{3/4}\) law: after distinct-index deduplication the true exponent geometry remains two-dimensional.

## Proof

### 1. Exact heat kernel for a finite-index sublattice

Since \(q\mathbb Z^2\subseteq\Lambda\), every point of \(\Lambda_+\) has a unique representation
\[
(A,B)=(qa+i,qb+j),
\qquad a,b\ge0,\quad(i,j)\in R.
\]
Hence the heat kernel is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_\Lambda(y)
&=\sum_{\Lambda_+\setminus\{(0,0)\}}
 e^{-y(AL+BM)}\\
&=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in R}e^{-y(iL+jM)}}
{(1-e^{-qLy})(1-e^{-qMy})}-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The numerator has expansion
\[
|R|-S_1y+\frac{S_2}{2}y^2+O(y^3),
\]
while
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{(1-e^{-qLy})(1-e^{-qMy})}
={}&\frac1{q^2LMy^2}
+\frac{L+M}{2qLM}\frac1y\\
&+\left(\frac14+\frac{M}{12L}+\frac{L}{12M}\right)+O(y).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplication gives
\[
K_\Lambda(y)=
\frac{c_{2,\Lambda}}{y^2}
+\frac{c_{1,\Lambda}}y+c_{0,\Lambda}+O(y).
\]
Since \(|R|=q^2/h\), the leading coefficient simplifies to \(1/(hLM)\). The heat kernel decays exponentially at infinity. Therefore
\[
\Gamma(z)Z_\Lambda(z)=\int_0^\infty y^{z-1}K_\Lambda(y)\,dy
\]
continues \(Z_\Lambda\) to \(\Re z>-1\) and gives the stated poles and \(Z_\Lambda(0)=c_{0,\Lambda}\). The uniqueness of the heat expansion also shows that these coefficients do not depend on the auxiliary choice of \(q\).

### 2. Vertical-strip control

The residue decomposition also gives
\[
Z_\Lambda(z)=q^{-z}Z_{\mathrm{full}}(z)
+\sum_{(i,j)\in R\setminus\{(0,0)\}}
\sum_{a,b\ge0}
(qLa+qMb+iL+jM)^{-z},
\]
where \(Z_{\mathrm{full}}\) is the unrestricted two-dimensional lattice zeta. For a nonzero residue \((i,j)\), put
\[
c=iL+jM>0,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{c}{qL}>0.
\]
Euler--Maclaurin summation in \(b\) expresses the shifted double sum as
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{(qL)^{1-z}}{qM(z-1)}\zeta(z-1,\alpha)
+\frac12(qL)^{-z}\zeta(z,\alpha)\\
&\qquad+\frac{qMz}{12}(qL)^{-z-1}\zeta(z+1,\alpha)+E_{i,j}(z),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(E_{i,j}\) is holomorphic for \(\Re z>-1\) and has polynomial growth on closed vertical strips there. Euler--Maclaurin bounds for the Hurwitz zeta give the same vertical control for the displayed terms. Since \(R\) is finite, \(Z_\Lambda\) and the derivatives required below have the same kind of polynomial strip bounds as in the unrestricted case.

### 3. Prime-weight Dirichlet series

For \(u_{A,B}=AL+BM\), the classical expansion of the \(n\)-th prime gives
\[
\lambda_{A,B}
=u_{A,B}+\log u_{A,B}
+\frac{\log u_{A,B}-1}{u_{A,B}}
+O\!\left(\frac{(\log u_{A,B})^2}{u_{A,B}^2}\right).
\]
Expanding \(\lambda_{A,B}^{-z}\) through second order gives the same prime perturbation terms as in the unrestricted lattice. Each shell \(A+B=m\) contains no more points of \(\Lambda_+\) than the full lattice, so the remainder still sums absolutely and locally uniformly for \(\Re z>-1\). Consequently the prime-weight Dirichlet series has the same Laurent pattern at \(2,1,0\), with \(c_2,c_1,c_0,Z\) replaced by \(c_{2,\Lambda},c_{1,\Lambda},c_{0,\Lambda},Z_\Lambda\). The correction \(\mathscr R_\Lambda\) is absolutely convergent, and truncating it at \(u_{A,B}\le T\) has error
\[
O_{r,s,\Lambda}\!\left(\frac{(\log T)^3}{T}\right).
\]

### 4. Mellin expansion and saddle point

The Euler product
\[
\mathcal F_\Lambda(\tau)
=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_\Lambda}(1-p^{-\tau})^{-1}
\]
has Mellin kernel \(\Gamma(z)\zeta(z+1)D_\Lambda(z)\), with \(D_\Lambda\) the prime-weight Dirichlet series. The continuation and strip bounds above give the same contour shift as in the unrestricted two-generator proof. The residues at \(2,1,0\) yield
\[
\log\mathcal F_\Lambda(\tau)
=\frac{A_{1,\Lambda}}{\tau^2}
+\frac{A_{3,\Lambda}-A_{2,\Lambda}\log(1/\tau)}{\tau}
+Q_\Lambda(\log(1/\tau))+o(1),
\]
where
\[
Q_\Lambda(X)
=\frac{c_{2,\Lambda}}6X^3
-\frac{c_{1,\Lambda}}2X^2
+(d_{0,\Lambda}+c_{2,\Lambda}\kappa_0)X
+d_{1,\Lambda}-c_{1,\Lambda}\kappa_0+c_{2,\Lambda}\kappa_1,
\]
\[
d_{0,\Lambda}=1+c_{0,\Lambda}+c_{2,\Lambda},
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{1,\Lambda}={}&-\log\log2+Z_\Lambda'(0)+Z_{\Lambda,1}'(0)
+Z_{\Lambda,2}(0)+Z_{\Lambda,2}'(0)\\
&+\frac12Z_{\Lambda,2}''(0)-\mathscr R_\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
\]
The positive leading coefficient \(A_{1,\Lambda}\) supplies the same sectorial control required by Ingham's theorem. Expanding the saddle equation gives the displayed strong equivalent and \(\mathfrak C_\Lambda\). Finally,
\[
A_{1,\Lambda}=\frac{\zeta(3)}{hLM}
\]
gives the universal leading logarithmic law.

### 5. Collision family

For the dependent generators \(r^q,s^q,rs\), every exponent triple maps to
\[
(A,B)=(qa+c,qb+c),
\]
so \(A\equiv B\pmod q\). Conversely, any nonnegative pair with that congruence has a unique common residue \(c\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) and therefore such a representation. Taking
\[
R=\{(j,j):0\le j<q\}
\]
in the general coefficient formulas gives the three displayed \(c_{k,q}\), proving the corollary.

## Context and originality boundary

Golafshan (2026) proves the strong asymptotic for the unrestricted free exponent lattice \(\mathbb N^2\), with prime factors \(p_{r^as^b}\), and explicitly requires multiplicative independence so that exponent pairs give distinct indices. The paper notes that repeated-index choices fall outside that theorem. The present theorem inserts an arbitrary fixed finite-index exponent sublattice and recomputes the complete strong asymptotic; its collision corollary then treats a natural dependent generating system after exact deduplication.

Neyt's 2017 slides formulate a broad problem for multiplicatively generated sets from distinct integer generators. Their displayed Laplace transform for the logarithmic index-counting measure is
\[
\prod_i(1-e^{-s\log\lambda_i})^{-1}.
\]
If the generators satisfy multiplicative relations, this is a representation-counting transform rather than the transform of the underlying set of distinct indices. Thus it does not establish the finite-index distinct-lattice theorem or the dependent collision corollary above.

Searches for finite-index and congruence sublattices of the \((a,b)\)-exponent lattice, the condition \(A\equiv B\pmod q\), the concrete family \(p_{4^a9^b6^c}\), and collision-constrained variants of the recent two-generator theorem did not locate a prior statement of these results. Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge. The principal residual risks are unindexed concurrent work around the very recent two-generator preprint and unpublished material related to the broad 2017 slide announcement.

## Limitations

The lattice \(\Lambda\), its index, and \(r,s\) are fixed as \(x\to\infty\). The base integers \(r,s\) are still assumed multiplicatively independent, so additional collisions between them are not included. No uniformity over growing lattice index is claimed. The theorem concerns full-rank sublattices through the nonnegative quadrant, not arbitrary affine cosets or lower-rank exponent sets. The verification artifact checks the exact residue-moment formulas and representative finite-index examples but is not a substitute for the analytic proof.
