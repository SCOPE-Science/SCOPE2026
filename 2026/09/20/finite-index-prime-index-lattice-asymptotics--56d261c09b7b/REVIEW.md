## Correctness

**PASS.**

For a full-rank finite-index lattice \(\Lambda\le\mathbb Z^2\), choose \(q\) with \(q\mathbb Z^2\subseteq\Lambda\) and let \(R\) be its residue subgroup in \([0,q-1]^2\). Every nonnegative lattice point decomposes uniquely as \((qa+i,qb+j)\) with \((i,j)\in R\), so the heat kernel is the exact finite numerator
\[
K_\Lambda(y)=
\frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in R}e^{-y(iL+jM)}}
{(1-e^{-qLy})(1-e^{-qMy})}-1.
\]
Its Laurent expansion gives the stated \(c_{2,\Lambda},c_{1,\Lambda},c_{0,\Lambda}\), and \(|R|=q^2/[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda]\) yields the intrinsic leading residue
\[
c_{2,\Lambda}=\frac1{[\mathbb Z^2:\Lambda]LM}.
\]
The uniqueness of the heat expansion makes the coefficient formulas independent of the auxiliary period \(q\).

The main analytic issue is vertical-strip control after the lattice restriction. Splitting by the finitely many residues in \(R\) expresses \(Z_\Lambda\) as one scaled unrestricted lattice zeta and finitely many shifted double sums. Euler--Maclaurin summation in one coordinate reduces each shifted sum to Hurwitz-zeta terms plus a remainder holomorphic for \(\Re z>-1\) with polynomial vertical growth. The required continuation and derivative bounds therefore persist.

The prime-weight expansion also persists without loss: each shell \(A+B=m\) contains no more restricted lattice points than the full lattice, so the second-order prime perturbation remainder is still summable for \(\Re z>-1\). This gives the same Laurent pattern for the prime-weight Dirichlet series after substituting the new lattice zeta data. Mellin inversion, the sectorial estimate, Ingham's theorem and the saddle expansion then reproduce the stated strong equivalent. The \(q=1\) specialization agrees exactly with the recent unrestricted two-generator formula.

The dependent collision corollary is exact: the monoid generated by exponent vectors \((q,0),(0,q),(1,1)\) is precisely
\[
\{(A,B)\in\mathbb N^2:A\equiv B\pmod q\}.
\]
Multiplicative independence of \(r,s\) makes distinct exponent pairs distinct integer indices even though the original three-generator representations collide.

Adversarial checks focused on representation multiplicities, dependence on the auxiliary period \(q\), the heat-kernel constant term, the shifted-zeta continuation, and whether an apparent three-generator system could introduce a third-dimensional singularity. The exact residue decomposition resolves each point. Symbolic and finite exact verification independently reproduces the general residue-moment coefficients, the invariant leading residue, several finite-index examples, and the collision-family exponent identity.

## Originality

**PASS, to the best of our knowledge.**

Golafshan's September 2026 preprint proves a complete strong asymptotic for the unrestricted prime-index set \(\{p_{r^as^b}\}\) under multiplicative independence of \(r,s\). It explicitly says that the independence condition is needed to prevent repeated indices. The present theorem replaces the full exponent lattice by an arbitrary fixed finite-index sublattice and derives its full strong asymptotic; the leading effect is the new universal index factor \(h^{-1/3}\), while lower terms retain boundary arithmetic through \(c_{1,\Lambda}\), \(c_{0,\Lambda}\) and the regular zeta data.

Neyt's 2017 slides state a broad problem for multiplicatively generated sets from distinct integer generators. The key displayed transform for the logarithmic index-counting measure is a product of geometric factors. When the generators have a multiplicative relation, that product counts exponent representations rather than distinct generated integers. The collision family \((r^q,s^q,rs)\) has the explicit relation \((r^q)(s^q)=(rs)^q\), so the slide transform does not establish the distinct-index theorem here. The finite-index lattice formulation also goes beyond the free-product transform used in those slides.

The 2014 Vernaeve--Vindas--Weiermann paper treats the one-generator case. Searches for finite-index exponent sublattices, congruence restrictions on \((a,b)\), the diagonal condition \(A\equiv B\pmod q\), the special prime-index family \(p_{4^a9^b6^c}\), and collision-constrained variants of the recent two-generator theorem did not locate prior coverage. The most relevant residual originality risks are concurrent or unindexed work prompted by the very recent September 2026 preprint and unpublished material underlying the 2017 slide announcement.

## Value

**PASS.**

The theorem upgrades a single free two-generator exponent lattice to every fixed full-rank finite-index sublattice while preserving a complete relative-error-\(o(1)\) asymptotic including the multiplicative constant. It isolates a simple invariant law: the leading logarithmic constant depends on the restriction only through the lattice index \(h\), via \(h^{-1/3}\). At the same time, the lower-order terms record finer boundary and residue arithmetic. The dependent family generated by \(r^q,s^q,rs\) then gives a concrete collision application and shows why counting formal generators can predict the wrong growth dimension.

## Limitations

The theorem fixes the lattice, \(r\), and \(s\), and assumes \(r,s\) multiplicatively independent. It does not treat growing lattice index, affine cosets, lower-rank exponent sets, or additional base relations between \(r\) and \(s\). The public verification material is corroborative rather than a substitute for the analytic proof. No independent validation, independent audit, or formal proof-assistant verification is asserted.
