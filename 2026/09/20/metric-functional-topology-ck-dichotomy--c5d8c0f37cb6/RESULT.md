# Metric-functional weak topology on normed spaces and a sharp C(K) dichotomy

## Setting

Let \(X\) be a real normed space, with basepoint \(0\), and write
\[
h_w(x)=\|x-w\|-\|w\| \qquad (w,x\in X).
\]
Let \(X^\diamond\) be the pointwise closure of the internal metric functionals \(\{h_w:w\in X\}\). Following Gutiérrez--Nevanlinna, let
\[
\tau_\diamond:=\sigma(X,X^\diamond)
\]
be the topology generated by the basic sets
\[
B(x,F,c)=\bigcap_{h\in F}\{y:h(y)>h(x)-c\},
\]
where \(F\subset X^\diamond\) is finite and \(c>0\). Let \(\tau_w=\sigma(X,X^*)\) denote the classical weak topology.

The recent preprint *A Weak Topology on Metric Spaces* proves that \(\tau_\diamond\) is coarser than the norm topology and that convergence in \(\tau_\diamond\) is exactly d-weak convergence. It also constructs an unbounded \(\tau_\diamond\)-null sequence in \(C[0,1]\). The results below compare \(\tau_\diamond\) directly with the classical weak topology and extend the \(C[0,1]\) phenomenon to a complete compact-Hausdorff dichotomy.

## Theorem 1: a topology sandwich and finite-codimensional neighborhoods

Let \(E=\operatorname{ext} B_{X^*}\) be the extreme points of the closed dual unit ball. Then
\[
\boxed{
\sigma(X,E)\subseteq \tau_\diamond\subseteq \tau_w.
}
\]
Moreover, for every basic neighborhood \(B(0,F,c)\) there is a closed linear subspace \(L_F\subset X\) with
\[
\operatorname{codim}L_F\le |F|,
\qquad
L_F\subseteq B(0,F,c).
\]
Consequently, if \(X\) is infinite-dimensional, every nonempty \(\tau_\diamond\)-open set contains an affine translate of an infinite-dimensional finite-codimensional subspace and is therefore norm-unbounded.

### Proof

Each internal \(h_w\) is convex and 1-Lipschitz. Pointwise limits preserve both convexity and the 1-Lipschitz inequality, hence every \(h\in X^\diamond\) is norm-continuous and convex. A norm-continuous convex function on a normed space is weakly lower semicontinuous. Therefore every set \(\{x:h(x)>a\}\) is weakly open, so every basic \(\tau_\diamond\)-open set is weakly open. This gives
\[
\tau_\diamond\subseteq\tau_w.
\]

Walsh proved that every extreme point of \(B_{X^*}\), regarded as a linear functional on \(X\), is a metric functional. Since \(B_{X^*}\) is centrally symmetric, \(-e\in E\) whenever \(e\in E\). Thus both \(e\) and \(-e\) are lower semicontinuous for \(\tau_\diamond\), so \(e\) is \(\tau_\diamond\)-continuous. Hence
\[
\sigma(X,E)\subseteq\tau_\diamond.
\]

For the neighborhood statement, fix \(h\in F\). Since \(h\) is continuous, convex, and \(h(0)=0\), the subgradient theorem gives \(\varphi_h\in X^*\) such that
\[
\varphi_h(x)\le h(x)\qquad(x\in X).
\]
Set
\[
L_F=\bigcap_{h\in F}\ker\varphi_h.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{codim}L_F\le |F|\), and for \(x\in L_F\),
\[
h(x)\ge \varphi_h(x)=0>-c
\]
for every \(h\in F\). Hence \(L_F\subseteq B(0,F,c)\). Surjective isometries are \(\tau_\diamond\)-homeomorphisms, so translating this statement gives the corresponding affine subspace inside every nonempty open set. ∎

## Corollary 2: when the metric-functional topology is exactly weak

If
\[
\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{ext}B_{X^*})=X^*,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{\tau_\diamond=\tau_w.}
\]
In particular:

1. if \(X\) is finite-dimensional, then
   \[
   \boxed{\tau_\diamond=\tau_w=\text{norm topology};}
   \]
2. if \(X^*\) is strictly convex, then
   \[
   \boxed{\tau_\diamond=\tau_w.}
   \]

The second statement strengthens, at the level of nets and topologies, the previously known sequential fact that d-weakly convergent sequences are bounded when the dual is strictly convex.

### Proof

If the extreme points linearly span \(X^*\), then the topology generated by them is the full weak topology, so Theorem 1 squeezes \(\tau_\diamond\) between two equal topologies. In finite dimension, the compact convex dual unit ball is the convex hull of its extreme points, so those points span \(X^*\). If \(X^*\) is strictly convex, every norm-one functional is extreme, and their span is plainly all of \(X^*\). ∎

## Theorem 3: exact C(K) dichotomy and arbitrary norm profiles

Let \(K\) be a compact Hausdorff space and let \(C(K)\) be the real Banach space of continuous functions with the supremum norm. The following are equivalent:

1. \(K\) is infinite;
2. there exists a norm-unbounded sequence converging to \(0\) in \(\sigma(C(K),C(K)^\diamond)\);
3. for every prescribed sequence \((a_n)_{n\ge1}\) of positive real numbers, there are nonnegative functions \(f_n\in C(K)\) with pairwise disjoint supports such that
   \[
   \|f_n\|_\infty=a_n
   \]
   and \(f_n\to0\) in \(\sigma(C(K),C(K)^\diamond)\).

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\sigma(C(K),C(K)^\diamond)=\sigma(C(K),C(K)^*)
\iff K\text{ is finite}.
}
\]
For every infinite compact Hausdorff \(K\), the metric-functional topology is therefore strictly coarser than the classical weak topology.

### Proof

Assume first that \(K\) is infinite. There is a countable family of pairwise disjoint nonempty open sets \(U_n\subset K\). One proof is as follows. If there are infinitely many isolated points, take singleton open sets. Otherwise choose a non-isolated point \(x\). Starting with an open neighborhood of \(x\), repeatedly choose a second point in the current neighborhood and use Hausdorff separation to split off a nonempty open set \(U_n\) while retaining a smaller open neighborhood of \(x\); the retained neighborhoods remain infinite because \(x\) is non-isolated.

Compact Hausdorff spaces are normal. For each \(n\), choose \(g_n\in C(K,[0,1])\) with
\[
\|g_n\|_\infty=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}g_n\subset U_n,
\]
and put \(f_n=a_ng_n\).

Fix an internal metric functional
\[
h_w(f)=\|f-w\|_\infty-\|w\|_\infty.
\]
Because \(K\) is compact, there is \(s\in K\) with \(|w(s)|=\|w\|_\infty\). Since the supports of the \(f_n\) are pairwise disjoint, \(f_n(s)=0\) for all but at most one \(n\). For each such \(n\),
\[
\|f_n-w\|_\infty\ge |w(s)|=\|w\|_\infty,
\]
so \(h_w(f_n)\ge0\). Thus an internal metric functional can be negative on at most one member of the sequence.

Now let \(h\in C(K)^\diamond\). If \(h(f_n)<0\) and \(h(f_m)<0\) for two distinct indices, then a net of internal metric functionals converging pointwise to \(h\) would eventually be negative at both \(f_n\) and \(f_m\), contradicting the preceding paragraph. Therefore \(h(f_n)<0\) for at most one \(n\), and hence
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}h(f_n)\ge0=h(0).
\]
By the Gutiérrez--Nevanlinna characterization, \(f_n\to0\) in the metric-functional topology. Since the positive numbers \(a_n\) were arbitrary, (3) follows, and choosing \(a_n\to\infty\) gives (2).

If \(K\) is finite, then \(C(K)\) is finite-dimensional, so Corollary 2 gives \(\tau_\diamond=\) norm topology. Hence no norm-unbounded sequence can converge to zero, and one cannot realize, for example, the constant profile \(a_n=1\) by a \(\tau_\diamond\)-null sequence. This proves the equivalence.

Finally, classical weakly convergent sequences in a normed space are norm-bounded. Thus, for infinite \(K\), the unbounded \(\tau_\diamond\)-null sequence proves that \(\tau_\diamond\ne\tau_w\). The finite case was already covered by Corollary 2. ∎

## Further consequence: compact sets can be norm-unbounded

If \(K\) is infinite and \(a_n\to\infty\), the set
\[
\{0,f_1,f_2,\ldots\}
\]
is compact in the metric-functional topology because it is a convergent sequence together with its limit, yet it is unbounded in the Banach norm. Thus metric-functional compactness can differ sharply from both norm compactness and weak compactness on \(C(K)\).

## Relation to prior work

Gutiérrez--Nevanlinna (2026) introduced d-weak convergence and proved that, for bounded sequences in arbitrary normed spaces, d-weak convergence is equivalent to ordinary weak convergence. Their September 2026 preprint constructs a specific unbounded d-weakly null sequence in \(C[0,1]\), correcting their earlier assertion that this could not happen. The present \(C(K)\) construction uses the same disjoint-support mechanism but shows that no metrizability, connectedness, interval structure, or prescribed growth rate is needed: every infinite compact Hausdorff \(K\) admits every positive norm profile.

Walsh's 2018 description of normed-space horofunctions supplies the fact that extreme points of the dual unit ball are metric functionals. Combining that fact with convex weak lower semicontinuity yields the topology sandwich above. The recent topology paper states only that \(\sigma(X,X^\diamond)\) is coarser than the metric topology; it does not state this comparison with the classical weak topology or the resulting finite-dimensional/strict-dual identifications.

## Limitations

The results do not characterize all infinite-dimensional normed spaces for which \(\tau_\diamond=\tau_w\). In particular, the algebraic-span condition on extreme dual functionals is sufficient, not claimed necessary. The strict inequality for infinite \(C(K)\) concerns exact topology and exact convergence, not quantitative rates. The \(C(K)\) extension is conceptually close to the new \(C[0,1]\) counterexample, so an equivalent generalization could appear in a subsequent version or in unindexed work; the originality claim is therefore only to the best of our knowledge.
