# Scientific review

**Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.**

## Correctness

PASS.

The necessity argument uses only pairwise independence and the rare-event hypotheses. Pairwise independence gives the exact row variance
\(\sum_i p_i(1-p_i)\), while the maximal marginal assumption makes \(\sum_i p_i^2\to0\). Bounded second moments give uniform integrability of the counts, preserving the limiting mean; lower semicontinuity of the second moment gives the limiting variance upper bound.

The converse was checked at the level of exact factorial moments. For an exchangeable row generated by conditioning on its total count \(K_n\), pairwise independence with marginal \(\lambda/n\) is equivalent to
\[
E K_n=\lambda,\qquad E[K_n(K_n-1)]=(1-1/n)\lambda^2.
\]
For a finite-support target of variance below \(\lambda\), transferring a vanishing amount of mass from any positive atom to \(0\) and \(n\) preserves the mean and raises the second factorial moment by exactly the required amount. The transfer probability is positive and smaller than the source atom for all sufficiently large \(n\). For variance exactly \(\lambda\), a vanishing mixture with the minimum-variance adjacent-integer law lowers the second factorial moment by exactly \(\lambda^2/n\). Arbitrary targets are approximated by finite-support mean-preserving tail compression, which cannot increase the second moment.

Adversarial checks included the integer-mean deterministic endpoint, the variance-equals-mean boundary, noninteger means, positivity of all modified masses, support staying inside \([0,n]\), and the possibility of strict loss of second moment in weak convergence. The explicit \(\lambda=1\) construction was also checked directly.

## Originality

PASS, to the best of our knowledge, with a meaningful residual risk.

The independent Poisson law of small numbers is classical and is not claimed as new. Le Cam (1960) is a primary reference for the independent Bernoulli approximation.

Boros–Prékopa (1989) studied the discrete first-two-binomial-moment problem and sharp event-count probability bounds. Ramachandra–Natarajan (2023) was read in accessible full text. Its introduction explicitly identifies Boros–Prékopa's first-two-binomial-moment formulation, and its Section 4 treats tight finite-n bounds for identical marginals under pairwise independence. This strongly overlaps the finite-dimensional moment machinery but does not state the weak-limit classification found here.

Gupta–Hu–Kehne–Levin's open-access pairwise-independent contention-resolution paper was checked at its explicit symmetric construction. It gives a marginal-\(1/n\) pairwise-independent count law whose limit is \(\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_2\). Therefore the mere existence of a non-Poisson rare-event limit is prior art and is not claimed here. Its purpose and statement do not give the full set of possible weak limits.

Searches were also made for pairwise-independent Poisson limits, laws of small numbers under pairwise independence, exchangeable pairwise-independent Bernoulli sums, and possible limiting distributions. No source located states that the possible limits are exactly all nonnegative integer laws with mean \(\lambda\) and variance at most \(\lambda\), nor the converse realization inside exchangeable equal-marginal rows.

Residual risk remains because older Bonferroni/discrete-moment, finite-exchangeability, orthogonal-array, and limited-independence literature may contain an equivalent asymptotic statement under different terminology. The full 1989 Boros–Prékopa article was not independently checked theorem-by-theorem; its role was verified through its publisher abstract and the detailed accessible discussion in Ramachandra–Natarajan. This is the main scientifically relevant access limitation.

## Value

PASS.

The classification is structurally sharp rather than a single counterexample. It identifies exactly what pairwise independence retains from the classical rare-event limit: the mean and an upper bound on limiting variance, and nothing further at the scalar-count level. The equal-marginal exchangeable converse is especially useful because it rules out marginal heterogeneity as the cause of the phenomenon. The explicit \(\lambda=1\) construction makes the failure of Poisson convergence unusually strong: the count can converge to a constant despite every pair of indicators being independent and every marginal being \(1/n\).

## Scientific limitations

The result concerns scalar sums, not point-process convergence; higher-wise independence is not classified; and the constructions are not optimized for support size or entropy. Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge, with the older discrete-moment literature noted above as the principal residual risk.
