# Schatten-small radical shift with maximal power obstruction

## Result

Let \((\eta_j)_{j\ge 0}\) be any positive decreasing sequence with
\(\eta_j\to0\). There is a sequence of positive weights \(w_j\le \eta_j\) such
that the unilateral weighted shift
\[
Te_j=w_je_{j+1}\qquad (j\ge0)
\]
on \(\ell_2(\mathbb N_0)\) is injective, compact, and quasinilpotent, while its
norm-closed nonunital algebra
\[
\mathcal A_T=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{T,T^2,T^3,\ldots\}
\]
has the following maximal failure of uniform topological nilpotence:
\[
\boxed{\quad
\sup_{\substack{X\in\mathcal A_T\\ \|X\|\le1}}
\|X^m\|^{1/m}=1
\quad\text{for every }m\ge2.
\quad}
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal A_T\) is a commutative radical operator algebra that is
not uniformly topologically nil, and hence is not topologically nilpotent.
Every element of \(\mathcal A_T\) is compact. Because all weights are nonzero,
\(\mathcal A_T\) is also a nonunital integral domain.

In particular, choosing for example
\[
\eta_j=2^{-(j+1)^2}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{T\in S_p\quad\text{for every }p>0}
\]
while the displayed power obstruction remains maximal. Thus membership of a
quasinilpotent generator in every Schatten class does not force uniform
topological nilpotence of the radical algebra it generates.

## Construction

Set \(P_1=0\) and \(h_1=1\). For \(m=2,3,\ldots\), after \(P_{m-1}\) has been
defined, put
\[
n_m=P_{m-1}+1,\qquad
P_m=P_{m-1}+1+mn_m.
\]
Choose \(h_m>0\) so small that
\[
h_m<
\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{h_{m-1}}2,
\min_{P_{m-1}\le j<P_m}\eta_j\right\}.
\]
At index \(P_{m-1}\) place one separator
\[
w_{P_{m-1}}=h_m^{\,n_m},
\]
and follow it by a plateau of exactly \(mn_m\) weights equal to \(h_m\):
\[
w_{P_{m-1}+1}=\cdots=w_{P_m-1}=h_m.
\]
All weights are positive. They satisfy \(w_j\le\eta_j\), and the plateau
heights strictly decrease to zero.

## Exact power computation

For a unilateral weighted shift,
\[
\|T^n\|=\sup_{j\ge0}\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}w_{j+r}.
\]
Fix \(m\ge2\). We claim
\[
\|T^{n_m}\|=h_m^{n_m}.
\]

A length-\(n_m\) block starting before index \(P_{m-1}\) must contain the
separator at \(P_{m-1}\), because \(n_m=P_{m-1}+1\). Every weight is at most
one, so the product is at most
\(h_m^{n_m}\). A length-\(n_m\) block starting at or after \(P_{m-1}\) consists
only of weights at most \(h_m\), because all later plateau heights and
separators are smaller. Hence its product is again at most \(h_m^{n_m}\).
A block lying completely inside the \(m\)-th plateau attains this bound.

The same plateau has length \(mn_m\), so
\[
\|T^{mn_m}\|\ge h_m^{mn_m}.
\]
Submultiplicativity and the preceding equality give the reverse inequality:
\[
\|T^{mn_m}\|
\le \|T^{n_m}\|^m
=h_m^{mn_m}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\|T^{mn_m}\|=\|T^{n_m}\|^m.}
\]
With
\[
X_m=\frac{T^{n_m}}{\|T^{n_m}\|},
\]
we obtain \(\|X_m\|=1\) and \(\|X_m^m\|=1\). The opposite inequality
\(\|X^m\|^{1/m}\le1\) is automatic on the unit ball, proving the claimed
equality for every \(m\ge2\).

## Compactness, quasinilpotence, and the radical algebra

Since \(w_j\le\eta_j\to0\), the weighted shift \(T\) is compact. It is
injective because no weight vanishes.

For completeness, quasinilpotence follows directly from \(w_j\to0\). Given
\(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(N\) so that \(w_j\le\varepsilon\) for \(j\ge N\).
A sufficiently long consecutive product contains at most \(N\) factors from
the initial segment and all remaining factors are at most \(\varepsilon\).
Taking \(n\)-th roots and then \(n\to\infty\) gives
\(r(T)\le\varepsilon\); hence \(r(T)=0\).

The norm-closed algebra generated by a single quasinilpotent operator without
constant term is commutative and radical. Since \(T\) is compact and compact
operators form a norm-closed ideal, every element of \(\mathcal A_T\) is
compact. For a weighted shift with all weights nonzero, the weighted-shift
algebra is an integral domain.

## Schatten consequence

For the shift above,
\[
T^*T=\operatorname{diag}(w_0^2,w_1^2,\ldots).
\]
Hence, for every \(p>0\),
\[
T\in S_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j=0}^\infty w_j^p<\infty.
\]
Thus any envelope \(\eta\) belonging to every \(\ell_p\), such as
\(2^{-(j+1)^2}\), forces the constructed generator to belong to every
Schatten class while leaving the algebraic power obstruction unchanged.

## Relation to prior literature

Peters and Wogen (1999) introduced the relevant weighted-shift mechanism in
their study of commutative radical operator algebras. Their Theorem 2.4 proves
topological nilpotence for positive decreasing weights tending to zero, while
Example 2.5 constructs a nonmonotone quasinilpotent weighted shift whose
generated algebra is not uniformly topologically nil. The present statement
does not claim that qualitative phenomenon as new.

The point here is the strengthened construction: the plateau-separator
mechanism can be made subordinate to an arbitrary prescribed null envelope,
and at the same time can force a norm-one \(m\)-th power witness for every
\(m\ge2\). This yields the all-Schatten counterexample above. Peters (2024)
revisited singly generated radical operator algebras and proved, among other
things, that weighted-shift algebras with nonzero weights are nonunital
integral domains. The 2024 paper also treats square-summable weight sequences,
but no statement giving the arbitrary-envelope/all-Schatten strengthening of
the topological-nilpotence obstruction was located.

For commutative Banach algebras, the equivalence between topological
nilpotence and uniform topological nilpotence is classical; it is used here
only to translate the explicit unit-ball obstruction.

## Limitations

Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge. The qualitative
existence of quasinilpotent weighted shifts generating radical algebras that
are not topologically nilpotent is prior art. The claim of novelty is limited
to the arbitrary-decay-envelope strengthening, including the consequence that
the generator may lie in every Schatten class while the power obstruction is
maximal for every finite exponent.

The older literature on radical Banach algebras and weighted shifts is large.
P. G. Dixon's 1991 work on topologically nilpotent Banach algebras was checked
through bibliographic/abstract-level material and through the way its results
are used in Peters--Wogen (1999), but was not exhaustively inspected in full.
A. Shields's 1974 chapter on weighted shifts was likewise not exhaustively
inspected; it is a plausible source for equivalent weighted-shift arguments,
although it predates the topological-nilpotence formulation. No specific
inaccessible source was found with concrete evidence of containing the
arbitrary-envelope/Schatten conclusion.

## Reproducibility

Given a chosen positive decreasing null envelope \(\eta\), the recursive
construction above is explicit. The two identities to check at stage \(m\) are
\[
\|T^{n_m}\|=h_m^{n_m},
\qquad
\|T^{mn_m}\|=h_m^{mn_m},
\]
and the proof shows these directly from consecutive products of weights. No
numerical experiment is required for the theorem.
