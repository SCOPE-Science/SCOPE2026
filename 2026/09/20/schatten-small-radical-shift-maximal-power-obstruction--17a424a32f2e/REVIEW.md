# Same-model review

**Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.**

## Correctness — PASS

The construction and all boundary assertions were checked separately.

- At stage \(m\), the chosen length is \(n_m=P_{m-1}+1\). Any
  length-\(n_m\) consecutive product beginning before the new separator must
  cross that separator. Since all weights are at most one, such a product is
  at most \(h_m^{n_m}\).
- Any length-\(n_m\) product beginning at or after the separator uses only
  weights at most \(h_m\). A product wholly inside the plateau attains
  \(h_m^{n_m}\). Hence \(\|T^{n_m}\|=h_m^{n_m}\) exactly.
- The plateau contains exactly \(mn_m\) copies of \(h_m\), so it gives the
  lower bound \(h_m^{mn_m}\) for \(\|T^{mn_m}\|\). Submultiplicativity gives
  the matching upper bound \(\|T^{n_m}\|^m\).
- Therefore \(X_m=T^{n_m}/\|T^{n_m}\|\) belongs to the unit ball of
  \(\mathcal A_T\) and satisfies \(\|X_m^m\|=1\). No unit-ball element can
  exceed one after taking the \(m\)-th root, so the claimed supremum equals
  one for every \(m\ge2\).
- The recursion has no circular dependence: \(P_m\) is determined from
  \(P_{m-1}\) and \(m\) before \(h_m\) is chosen. Thus \(h_m\) can always be
  chosen below the finitely many envelope values used at that stage.
- Since the weights tend to zero, \(T\) is compact. The direct consecutive
  product estimate gives spectral radius zero. Positivity of every weight
  gives injectivity.
- Radicality follows from the standard fact that the norm-closed commutative
  algebra generated by commuting quasinilpotent elements is radical.
  Compactness of every algebra element follows from norm closedness of the
  compact operators.
- For a unilateral weighted shift, \(T^*T\) is diagonal with entries
  \(w_j^2\), so \(T\in S_p\) exactly when \((w_j)\in\ell_p\). This remains
  valid for \(0<p<1\), where \(S_p\) is a quasi-Banach ideal.
- Integrality is not inferred from injectivity alone; it uses the established
  weighted-shift algebra result for nonzero weights.

No complementability, duality, or inheritance assertion is used.

## Originality — PASS

Peters and Wogen (1999) are direct prior art. Their Theorem 2.4 proves
topological nilpotence for decreasing positive weights, and their Example 2.5
constructs a nonmonotone quasinilpotent weighted shift for which normalized
powers obstruct uniform topological nilpotence. That qualitative
counterexample is not claimed as new.

The present strengthening decouples plateau length from the smallness of its
height: a separator of size \(h_m^{n_m}\) fixes the selected power norm while
a plateau of length \(mn_m\) forces exact multiplicativity. Because the
height can be chosen after the block endpoints are fixed, the entire weight
sequence can lie below an arbitrary prescribed positive decreasing null
envelope.

Peters (2024) revisits weighted-shift radical algebras, including
square-summable weights, normalized powers, ideal structure, and the
nonunital integral-domain property. Targeted searches using "trace class",
"Schatten", "uniformly topologically nil", "topologically nilpotent",
"quasinilpotent weighted shift", and synonymous combinations did not locate
the arbitrary-envelope statement or the consequence that one generator can
belong to every \(S_p\) while
\[
\sup_{\|X\|\le1}\|X^m\|^{1/m}=1
\]
for every finite \(m\ge2\).

Residual literature risk remains. P. G. Dixon's 1991 paper on topologically
nilpotent Banach algebras was checked through bibliographic/abstract-level
material and through the explicit use made of it by Peters--Wogen, but was
not exhaustively inspected in full. A. Shields's 1974 chapter on weighted
shifts was also not exhaustively inspected; it may contain equivalent
weighted-shift arguments, though it predates the topological-nilpotence
formulation. No inaccessible source was found with concrete evidence of
already containing the arbitrary-envelope/Schatten strengthening.

Originality is therefore asserted only to the best of our knowledge.

## Value — PASS

The theorem separates two kinds of smallness that might otherwise be
conflated. The generator can have arbitrarily fast prescribed weight decay,
and in particular can lie in every Schatten class, yet its radical algebra
retains the largest possible unit-ball power modulus at every finite
exponent. This is stronger than merely exhibiting another non-topologically
nil radical algebra, and the construction provides a reusable mechanism for
combining stringent operator-ideal decay with exact normalized-power
obstructions.

## Limitations checked

The result is an existence theorem for unilateral weighted shifts; it is not
a classification of compact or Schatten-class quasinilpotent generators.
It does not claim that every rapidly decaying weighted shift has this
behavior. The all-Schatten statement concerns the generator \(T\); individual
elements of the generated algebra are compact, but no common Schatten class
for all elements is asserted.
