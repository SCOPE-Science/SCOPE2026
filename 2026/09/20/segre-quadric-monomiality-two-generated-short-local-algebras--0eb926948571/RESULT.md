# Segre-quadric monomiality criterion for two-generated short local algebras

## Result

Let \(k\) be any field, let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional split local \(k\)-algebra, and write \(J=J(A)\). Assume
\[
J^3=0,\qquad A/J\cong k,\qquad \dim_k J/J^2=2.
\]
Put
\[
V=J/J^2,\qquad W=J^2.
\]
Multiplication induces a canonical surjective bilinear map
\[
\mu:V\otimes_k V\longrightarrow W,\qquad
(\bar a,\bar b)\longmapsto ab,
\]
and let
\[
K=\ker\mu\subseteq V\otimes V.
\]

Then whether \(A\) is isomorphic to a monomial algebra is determined completely by the position of \(K\) under the diagonal action of \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\). More explicitly, put \(d=\dim_k W\), so \(\dim K=4-d\).

1. If \(d=0\) or \(d=4\), then \(A\) is automatically isomorphic to a monomial algebra.

2. If \(d=1\), choose \(0\ne\omega\in W\) and write
   \[
   \mu(u,v)=\beta(u,v)\omega
   \]
   for a bilinear form \(\beta\) on \(V\). Then
   \[
   A\text{ is monomializable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \operatorname{rank}\beta=1.
   \]

3. If \(d=3\), then \(K\) is a line. For \(0\ne z\in K\), regard \(z\) as a \(2\times2\) matrix after choosing any basis of \(V\). Then
   \[
   A\text{ is monomializable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \operatorname{rank}z=1,
   \]
   equivalently \(K\) is generated by a decomposable tensor.

4. Suppose \(d=2\). Let
   \[
   Q=\{[u\otimes v]:0\ne u,v\in V\}
   \subset \mathbf P(V\otimes V)\cong\mathbf P^3
   \]
   be the Segre quadric of decomposable tensors, and let \(L=\mathbf P(K)\).

   Then \(A\) is monomializable if and only if one of the following holds:

   - **ruling case:** \(L\subset Q\);
   - **matched-secant case:** \(L\not\subset Q\), the scheme \(L\cap Q\) consists of two distinct \(k\)-rational points
     \[
     q_i=[u_i\otimes v_i]\quad(i=1,2),
     \]
     and the unordered sets of factor lines agree:
     \[
     \{[u_1],[u_2]\}=\{[v_1],[v_2]\}
     \quad\text{in }\mathbf P(V).
     \]

In particular, this gives a basis-free decision procedure over every field, including characteristic \(2\). It also yields exactly ten isomorphism classes of monomial algebras inside this entire two-generated short-local class: respectively
\[
1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 2,\ 1
\]
classes for \(d=0,1,2,3,4\).

## Reduction to a tensor kernel

Because \(J^3=0\), replacing a lift of an element of \(V=J/J^2\) by another lift changes it by an element of \(J^2\), whose product with \(J\) is zero. Thus \(\mu\) is well defined. It is surjective because \(W=J^2\) is spanned by products of elements of \(J\).

Choose a vector-space splitting \(J=U\oplus W\), so \(U\cong V\). Since
\[
JW=WJ=0,
\]
the multiplication of \(A=k1\oplus U\oplus W\) is completely determined by \(\mu\). Consequently two such algebras \(A,A'\) are isomorphic if and only if there are linear isomorphisms
\[
g:V\to V',\qquad h:W\to W'
\]
such that
\[
h\mu=\mu'(g\otimes g).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
K'=(g\otimes g)K.
\]
The same change of basis \(g\) acts on both tensor factors; this is the key point.

If \(A\) is monomial, then its Gabriel quiver has one vertex and two loops. Since \(J^3=0\), all paths of length three vanish, while its quadratic zero-relations are a subset of the four paths
\[
x^2,\ xy,\ yx,\ y^2.
\]
Hence, in the basis \(x,y\) of \(V\), \(K\) is spanned by a subset of
\[
x\otimes x,\quad x\otimes y,\quad y\otimes x,\quad y\otimes y.
\]
Conversely, if \(K\) has this form in some basis, then \(A\) is the quotient of \(k\langle x,y\rangle\) by those quadratic monomials together with all cubic monomials. Thus
\[
A\text{ is monomializable}
\]
if and only if \(K\) lies in the diagonal \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\)-orbit of a coordinate subspace of \(V\otimes V\).

## Proof of the four cases

The cases \(d=0\) and \(d=4\) correspond to \(K=V\otimes V\) and \(K=0\), so they are immediate.

### The case \(d=3\)

Here \(K=kz\). A coordinate line is generated by a decomposable tensor, so decomposability is necessary. Conversely, write \(z=u\otimes v\). If \([u]=[v]\), one basis change sends \(z\) to a scalar multiple of \(x\otimes x\). If \([u]\ne[v]\), then \(u,v\) are a basis of \(V\), and one basis change sends \(z\) to a scalar multiple of \(x\otimes y\). Thus every decomposable line is coordinate for the diagonal action.

For a two-dimensional \(V\), decomposability of \(z\) is equivalent to the representing \(2\times2\) matrix having rank one.

### The case \(d=1\)

After choosing \(\omega\), the map \(\mu\) is a nonzero bilinear form \(\beta\), and \(K=\ker\beta\). A coordinate hyperplane in \(V\otimes V\) is the kernel of a decomposable functional in
\[
(V\otimes V)^*\cong V^*\otimes V^*.
\]
Therefore \(K\) is coordinate exactly when \(\beta\) has tensor rank one, which for a \(2\times2\) bilinear form is ordinary matrix rank one.

For completeness, write \(\beta=f\otimes g\). If \(f,g\) are proportional, a basis change sends \(\beta\), up to a nonzero scalar absorbed into \(\omega\), to the coefficient functional of \(x\otimes x\). If they are independent, a basis change sends it to the coefficient functional of \(x\otimes y\). Hence rank one is also sufficient.

### The case \(d=2\)

Now \(L=\mathbf P(K)\) is a projective line in \(\mathbf P(V\otimes V)\). The quadric \(Q\) is exactly the set of projective rank-one tensors.

If \(K\) is a coordinate two-plane, there are two possibilities.

First, two coordinate tensors can share a left factor or share a right factor. Then
\[
K=u\otimes V\qquad\text{or}\qquad K=V\otimes v,
\]
so \(L\) is a line on \(Q\). Conversely every line on the Segre quadric belongs to one of its two rulings, hence has one of these forms and becomes
\[
x\otimes V
\quad\text{or}\quad
V\otimes x
\]
after a single basis change. This proves the ruling case.

Second, a coordinate two-plane not lying on \(Q\) is one of the two secant types
\[
\operatorname{span}\{x\otimes x,y\otimes y\},
\qquad
\operatorname{span}\{x\otimes y,y\otimes x\}.
\]
In both cases \(L\cap Q\) consists of two distinct rational points, and the unordered set of their left factor lines equals the unordered set of their right factor lines.

Conversely, suppose \(L\not\subset Q\) and
\[
L\cap Q=\{[u_1\otimes v_1],[u_2\otimes v_2]\}
\]
consists of two distinct \(k\)-rational points with matching unordered factor sets. Since \(L\not\subset Q\), both \(u_1,u_2\) and \(v_1,v_2\) are linearly independent; otherwise their span would be a ruling line on \(Q\). Choose a basis change sending the two common factor lines to \(kx,ky\). The two points then become, up to nonzero scalars, either
\[
x\otimes x,\ y\otimes y
\]
or
\[
x\otimes y,\ y\otimes x.
\]
Thus \(K\) is coordinate. This proves the criterion.

The argument uses only the Segre description of decomposable tensors and remains valid in characteristic \(2\): tangent lines or secants whose intersection points are not both \(k\)-rational are excluded exactly as stated.

## The ten monomial isomorphism types

The diagonal \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\)-orbits of coordinate subspaces are:

- one orbit when \(\dim K=4\);
- two when \(\dim K=3\), according as the unique surviving quadratic monomial is a square or a mixed product;
- four when \(\dim K=2\): common-left ruling, common-right ruling, diagonal secant, and crossed secant;
- two when \(\dim K=1\), according as its decomposable generator has equal or distinct factor lines;
- one when \(\dim K=0\).

These distinctions are invariant under the diagonal action: left and right rulings cannot be interchanged, and equality versus inequality of the two factor lines is preserved. Hence the ten coordinate types are pairwise non-isomorphic in the indicated Hilbert types.

## Context and comparison with the literature

Bardzell and Green gave a general invariant characterization of monomial algebras using finite abelian group gradings and first Hochschild cohomology of associated covering algebras. Mojiri later revisited the isomorphism-to-monomial problem via uniserial modules, giving a new characterization and a necessary condition, and identifying a class in which being isomorphic to a monomial algebra forces a given presentation to be monomial.

A later set of lecture slides by Green still lists, as an open question, the problem of finding a criterion deciding whether a given bound quiver algebra \(KQ/I\) is isomorphic to a monomial algebra. The theorem above supplies a complete, elementary, basis-free criterion for the full short-local class with one vertex and two loops. Ringel's terminology calls these short local algebras of Hilbert type \((2,d)\).

Targeted searches through later monomial-algebra and short-local-algebra literature did not locate this Segre-quadric criterion, the complete treatment of all \(d=0,\dots,4\), or the resulting ten monomial isomorphism types.

## Limitations

- The theorem treats split local algebras with \(\dim J/J^2=2\); it does not provide a criterion for three or more generators or for several simple modules.
- Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge.
- The full texts of Bardzell--Green (1999) and Mojiri (2006) were not inspected. Their accessible abstracts establish that they contain broad recognition results and make them the most plausible sources of an equivalent low-dimensional specialization not visible from indexed statements.
- The lecture slides give evidence that the broad recognition problem remained open in the cited presentation, but they are not a substitute for an exhaustive literature search.
