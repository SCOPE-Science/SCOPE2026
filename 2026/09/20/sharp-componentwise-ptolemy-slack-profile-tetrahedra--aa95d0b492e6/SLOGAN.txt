For a Euclidean tetrahedron, the three normalized strict-Ptolemy slacks form an arbitrary positive probability vector, already realizable by disphenoids, and their product is exactly \(576R^2V^2/L^4\). This yields a complete sharp componentwise slack interval at fixed \(R,V,L\), plus an exact sum-of-squares/centroid-circumcenter decomposition of the classical \(d_3\ge72V^2\) defect.
