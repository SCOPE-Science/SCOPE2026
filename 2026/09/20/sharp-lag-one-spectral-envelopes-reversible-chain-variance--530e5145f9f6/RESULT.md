# Sharp lag-one and spectral-ceiling envelopes for reversible Markov-chain variance

## Statement

Let \((X_t)_{t\ge 0}\) be stationary and reversible with invariant law \(\pi\). Let \(f\in L^2(\pi)\) be nonconstant and centered, and write

\[
\gamma_k=\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(f(X_0),f(X_k)),
\qquad
\rho_k=\frac{\gamma_k}{\gamma_0}.
\]

By reversibility, the normalized autocorrelation sequence has a spectral-moment representation

\[
\rho_k=\int \lambda^k\,\nu_f(d\lambda),
\]

where \(\nu_f\) is a probability measure on the spectrum seen by \(f\).

Assume that the observable-specific spectral measure is supported in

\[
\operatorname{supp}\nu_f\subset[0,\beta],
\qquad 0\le \beta<1,
\]

and denote the lag-one correlation by

\[
\rho:=\rho_1=\int\lambda\,\nu_f(d\lambda).
\]

Necessarily \(0\le \rho\le\beta\).

### Theorem 1 — sharp autocorrelation envelope

For every integer \(k\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{\rho^k\le \rho_k\le \rho\,\beta^{k-1}.}
\]

Both bounds are simultaneously sharp. The lower endpoint is generated by the one-point spectral measure \(\delta_\rho\). For \(\beta>0\), the upper endpoint is generated by

\[
\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\beta}\right)\delta_0
+\frac{\rho}{\beta}\delta_\beta.
\]

Thus, among positive reversible dynamics having fixed lag-one correlation \(\rho\), the geometric sequence \(\rho^k\) is the smallest possible autocorrelation sequence. If a spectral ceiling \(\beta\) is also known, the largest possible sequence is \(\rho\beta^{k-1}\).

### Theorem 2 — exact finite-sample variance interval

For \(n\ge1\), let

\[
\bar f_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}f(X_t)
\]

and define

\[
\Psi_n(t)
=
1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)t^k.
\]

For \(t\ne1\),

\[
\Psi_n(t)
=
\frac{n(1-t^2)-2t(1-t^n)}{n(1-t)^2},
\]

with the continuous extension \(\Psi_n(1)=n\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_n(\rho)
\le
\frac{n\,\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_n)}{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(f)}
\le
1+\frac{\rho}{\beta}\bigl(\Psi_n(\beta)-1\bigr)
}
\]

when \(\beta>0\). If \(\beta=0\), then \(\rho=0\) and the normalized factor equals \(1\).

The interval is exact: both endpoints, and every value between them, are attained by finite-state positive reversible chains.

### Theorem 3 — exact asymptotic variance / IAT interval

The spectral ceiling \(\beta<1\) guarantees a finite asymptotic variance for \(f\). Its normalized factor, equivalently the integrated autocorrelation time in the convention

\[
\tau_f
=
\frac{\sigma^2_{f,P}}{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(f)}
=
1+2\sum_{k\ge1}\rho_k,
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
\le
\tau_f
\le
1+\frac{2\rho}{1-\beta}.
}
\]

Again the entire interval is attainable by finite-state positive reversible chains.

Equivalently, the asymptotic relative effective sample size satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1-\beta}{1-\beta+2\rho}
\le
\frac{\mathrm{ESS}}{n}
\le
\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}.
}
\]

## No spectral ceiling: a sharp impossibility result

If only positivity and the lag-one correlation \(\rho\in(0,1)\) are known, there is no finite upper bound on \(\tau_f\).

More precisely, over finite-state irreducible aperiodic positive reversible chains with lag-one correlation exactly \(\rho\),

\[
\inf \tau_f=\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho},
\qquad
\sup \tau_f=+\infty.
\]

For finite \(n\), however, there is a sharp identification region

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_n(\rho)
\le
\frac{n\,\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_n)}{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(f)}
<
1+\rho(n-1),
}
\]

where the upper endpoint is the supremum over geometrically ergodic finite-state chains and can be approached arbitrarily closely.

Consequently, the AR(1) plug-in formula

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{AR}(1)}=\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
\]

has an extremal interpretation for positive reversible chains: at fixed lag-one correlation it is the smallest possible integrated autocorrelation time, not a generic reconstruction of the full autocorrelation tail. Without additional spectral information, chains with the same \(\rho_1\) can have arbitrarily larger asymptotic variance.

## Proof

### Spectral representation

For a reversible Markov operator \(P\), self-adjoint spectral calculus gives a finite positive spectral measure \(E_{f,P}\) such that

\[
\gamma_k=\langle f,P^k f\rangle_\pi
=\int\lambda^k\,E_{f,P}(d\lambda).
\]

Dividing by \(\gamma_0=\|f\|_\pi^2\) produces the probability measure

\[
\nu_f=\frac{E_{f,P}}{\gamma_0}
\]

and therefore

\[
\rho_k=\int\lambda^k\,\nu_f(d\lambda).
\]

The support assumption places \(\lambda\) in \([0,\beta]\).

### Proof of Theorem 1

For \(k\ge1\), \(x\mapsto x^k\) is convex on \([0,\beta]\), so Jensen's inequality yields

\[
\rho_k
=
\int\lambda^k\,d\nu_f
\ge
\left(\int\lambda\,d\nu_f\right)^k
=
\rho^k.
\]

Also, pointwise on \([0,\beta]\),

\[
\lambda^k\le \beta^{k-1}\lambda,
\]

hence

\[
\rho_k\le\beta^{k-1}\int\lambda\,d\nu_f
=\rho\beta^{k-1}.
\]

The stated one- and two-point spectral measures attain equality for every \(k\) simultaneously.

### Proof of Theorem 2

Stationarity gives the exact identity

\[
\frac{n\,\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_n)}{\gamma_0}
=
1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\rho_k.
\]

All coefficients in this sum are nonnegative. Substituting the two sharp simultaneous bounds from Theorem 1 therefore gives the claimed endpoints.

Equivalently,

\[
\frac{n\,\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_n)}{\gamma_0}
=
\int \Psi_n(\lambda)\,\nu_f(d\lambda).
\]

On \([0,\beta]\), \(\Psi_n\) is convex because all coefficients of \(t^k\) for \(k\ge2\) are nonnegative. Jensen gives the lower endpoint, while the chord inequality for a convex function on \([0,\beta]\),

\[
\Psi_n(\lambda)
\le
1+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\bigl(\Psi_n(\beta)-1\bigr),
\]

gives the upper endpoint after integration.

### Proof of Theorem 3

The reversible-chain spectral formula gives

\[
\tau_f
=
\int\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\,\nu_f(d\lambda).
\]

The function

\[
g(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}
\]

is convex on \([0,\beta]\). Hence

\[
\tau_f\ge g(\rho)=\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}.
\]

For the upper bound, the chord from \(0\) to \(\beta\) gives

\[
g(\lambda)
\le
1+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\left(\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}-1\right)
=
1+\frac{2\lambda}{1-\beta}.
\]

Integrating yields

\[
\tau_f\le 1+\frac{2\rho}{1-\beta}.
\]

The endpoint spectral measures are the same as in Theorem 1, so all three theorems have common extremizers.

## Explicit finite-state extremizers

For \(r\in[0,1)\), define the two-state refresh kernel on \(\{-1,1\}\),

\[
K_r(u,v)=\frac{1+r\,uv}{2}.
\]

It is irreducible, aperiodic, reversible with respect to the uniform law, and has eigenvalues \(1,r\).

### Lower endpoint

Take \(K_\rho\) and \(f(u)=u\). Then

\[
\rho_k=\rho^k.
\]

This attains the lower endpoints in Theorems 1–3.

### Upper endpoint

For \(0<\rho<\beta<1\), take the product kernel

\[
K_\beta\otimes K_0
\]

on \(\{-1,1\}^2\), with observable

\[
f(u,v)
=
\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\beta}}\,u
+
\sqrt{1-\frac{\rho}{\beta}}\,v.
\]

The stationary law is uniform, \(\operatorname{Var}(f)=1\), and for every \(k\ge1\),

\[
\rho_k
=
\frac{\rho}{\beta}\beta^k
=
\rho\beta^{k-1}.
\]

Thus this four-state chain attains every upper endpoint. The cases \(\rho=0\) and \(\rho=\beta\) reduce to \(K_0\) and \(K_\beta\).

Mixing the lower and upper spectral measures preserves the first moment \(\rho\). A product of the three refresh coordinates with eigenvalues \(\rho,\beta,0\), together with a suitable linear observable, realizes every convex mixture and therefore every point in both variance intervals.

## Sharpness without a spectral ceiling

For a positive reversible chain, \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), so

\[
\rho^k\le\rho_k\le\rho
\]

for every \(k\ge1\). Therefore

\[
\Psi_n(\rho)
\le
\frac{n\,\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_n)}{\gamma_0}
\le
1+\rho(n-1).
\]

Take the four-state upper construction with \(\beta\uparrow1\). Every member of this family remains finite-state, irreducible, aperiodic, reversible, positive and geometrically ergodic, while

\[
\rho_1=\rho
\]

for every member. Its finite-\(n\) variance factor converges to \(1+\rho(n-1)\), while

\[
\tau_f=1+\frac{2\rho}{1-\beta}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

This proves both sharp statements.

## Relation to prior literature

The spectral ingredients are classical.

Kipnis--Varadhan theory gives the reversible-chain spectral formula for asymptotic variance. Łatuszyński and Roberts (2013) state it explicitly in the form

\[
\sigma^2_{f,P}
=
\int_{[-1,1]}\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\,E_{f,P}(d\lambda)
\]

and discuss positive reversible kernels.

Geyer (1992) exploited the spectral representation to prove shape restrictions on paired autocovariances and to construct initial-sequence variance estimators.

Roberts and Rosenthal (2008) characterized variance bounding for reversible chains by an upper spectral gap. That theory supplies chain-wide control from the spectrum but does not condition the sharp variance range on a specified observable's lag-one correlation.

Berg and Song (2023) made explicit use of the fact that a reversible-chain autocovariance sequence is a moment sequence, developing shape-constrained estimation of the sequence and asymptotic variance. Subsequent work extends these estimators to weighted and multivariate settings.

The result here uses that moment representation as an extremal problem: fixing the first moment \(\rho_1\), and optionally an upper support point \(\beta\), determines an exact identification interval for every higher autocorrelation, every finite-sample mean variance, and the asymptotic variance. The finite-state constructions show that the bounds are not merely consequences of loose spectral inequalities.

## Limitations

- The lower-envelope conclusions require the observable spectral measure to be nonnegative, as occurs for positive reversible kernels. Reversibility alone permits negative spectrum and does not imply these bounds.
- The ceiling \(\beta\) is observable-specific in the theorem. A chain-level nonconstant spectral ceiling is a sufficient stronger assumption.
- The result is deterministic conditional on \(\rho\) and \(\beta\); it does not address statistical error in estimating either quantity from a finite trajectory.
- The extremizers establish worst-case sharpness and are not claimed to describe typical MCMC algorithms.
- Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge. The proof reduces to classical one-moment extremal inequalities once the reversible spectral measure is exposed, so an equivalent formulation may exist in moment-problem, operator-theoretic, or MCMC literature under different terminology.
