## Correctness

PASS.

The argument separates into two independently checkable parts.

First, for complementary semiperimeter variables
\[
x=s-a,\quad y=s-b,\quad z=s-c,
\]
fixed perimeter and fixed side deficit
\[
D=(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2
\]
fix the first two elementary symmetric functions of \(x,y,z\). Writing
\[
m=p/6,\qquad t=\sqrt{2D/p^2},
\]
the fixed-moment circle has its maximal third elementary symmetric function at
\[
m(1+2t,1-t,1-t),
\]
and, while \(t<1/2\), its minimal third elementary symmetric function at
\[
m(1-2t,1+t,1+t).
\]
At \(t=1/2\), equivalently \(D/p^2=1/8\), the constraint circle first reaches a coordinate plane. For larger deficit the product infimum is therefore zero.

Second, the non-Euclidean Heron-L'Huilier formulas reduce the area problem to the transformed products
\[
\prod\tanh(x_i/2)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\prod\tan(x_i/2).
\]
For
\[
f_H(u)=\log\tanh(u/2),\qquad
f_S(u)=\log\tan(u/2),
\]
one has
\[
f_H'''(u)=\frac{1+\cosh^2u}{\sinh^3u}>0,\qquad
f_S'''(u)=\frac{1+\cos^2u}{\sin^3u}>0.
\]
If \(x_i\) are roots of \(u^3-e_1u^2+e_2u-e_3\), differentiation at fixed \(e_1,e_2\) gives
\[
\frac d{de_3}\sum_i f(x_i)
=
\sum_i\frac{f'(x_i)}{\prod_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)}
=
f'[x_1,x_2,x_3]>0.
\]
Thus each transformed product is strictly increasing in \(xyz\). This proves both sharp boundary families and their uniqueness up to permutation. Continuity on the fixed-moment set gives the complete interval of intermediate areas.

The spherical domain restriction \(p<2\pi\) ensures \(s<\pi\), all \(x,y,z\in(0,\pi)\), and positivity of the tangent factors. The extremal side triples remain valid spherical side triples because all sides are \(<s<\pi\) and satisfy the strict triangle inequalities.

As a consistency check, the Euclidean choice \(f(u)=\log u\) has \(f'''(u)=2/u^3>0\), and the resulting profiles reduce exactly to the boundary functions in Bogosel's 2025 optimal Euclidean Finsler-Hadwiger result.

## Originality

PASS, to the best of our knowledge.

The closest older source located is Svrtan--Veljan, which proves spherical and hyperbolic versions of the Finsler-Hadwiger and isoperimetric triangle inequalities. Its non-Euclidean inequalities are not complete fixed-perimeter/fixed-deficit area profiles; the paper also records that the appropriate non-Euclidean analogue needed for sharper isoperimetric refinements was not known there.

The closest current source is Bogosel (2025), which explicitly solves the Euclidean complete-system problem for area, perimeter squared, and the same deficit \(D\). The Euclidean part of the present derivation is therefore treated as known and is not claimed as original.

Searches through current literature under exact and synonymous formulations -- non-Euclidean Finsler-Hadwiger, hyperbolic/spherical triangle area with fixed perimeter and side deficit, side variance, sum of squared side differences, and optimal/complete Finsler-Hadwiger inequalities -- did not locate a spherical or hyperbolic theorem giving the sharp two isosceles boundary families and the \(D/p^2=1/8\) lower-bound phase transition.

Residual originality risk remains because classical non-Euclidean triangle inequalities are often written in transformed variables such as \(\sin(a/2)\), \(\sinh(a/2)\), \(\tan((s-a)/2)\), or \(\tanh((s-a)/2)\). An equivalent fixed-moment statement could therefore exist under terminology not captured by the searches.

## Value

PASS.

This closes a natural gap between two established lines of work: non-Euclidean analogues of Finsler-Hadwiger inequalities and the modern complete sharp Euclidean Finsler-Hadwiger diagram. Instead of a single one-sided inequality, the result gives the exact admissible area interval for every fixed perimeter and side deficit in both constant-curvature signs, identifies all nondegenerate equality shapes, and exposes a sharp geometric phase transition in the lower envelope.

The proof also supplies a reusable mechanism: for three variables with fixed first two moments, any product generated by a positive function whose logarithm has positive third derivative is ordered by the ordinary third elementary symmetric function.

## Scientific limitations

- Spherical triangles are restricted to the standard convex minor-arc regime \(p<2\pi\).
- Curvature is normalized to \(\pm1\); other radii require the stated rescaling.
- Only the Finsler-Hadwiger side deficit is controlled.
- The Euclidean profile is prior work and is not an originality claim.
- Originality is to the best of our knowledge; transformed-variable formulations in older literature remain the principal residual risk.
