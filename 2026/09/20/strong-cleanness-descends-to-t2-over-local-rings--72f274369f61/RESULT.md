# Strong cleanness descends from \(M_2\) to \(T_2\) over local rings

## Result

Let \(R\) be a local ring, not assumed commutative, and let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}\in T_2(R).
\]
If \(A\) is strongly clean as an element of \(M_2(R)\), then \(A\) is strongly clean as an element of \(T_2(R)\).

Consequently, if \(M_2(R)\) is strongly clean, then \(T_2(R)\) is strongly clean.

This gives an affirmative answer in dimension two to the elementwise triangular-descent problem posed as Problem 49 by Borooah--Diesl--Dorsey, and hence also to their Problem 48 in dimension two.

## Proof

Write \(J=J(R)\) and \(D=R/J\). If \(A\) is a unit, use the strongly clean decomposition \(A=0+A\). If \(I-A\) is a unit, use \(A=I+(A-I)\). We may therefore suppose that neither \(A\) nor \(I-A\) is a unit.

For an upper triangular matrix over a local ring, invertibility is equivalent to invertibility of both diagonal entries. Since the nonunits of \(R\) are exactly \(J\), and since \(a\) and \(1-a\) cannot both lie in \(J\), the diagonal entries must have opposite residue types. Thus exactly one of the following holds:
\[
\text{(I)}\quad a\in J,\qquad c\in 1+J,
\]
or
\[
\text{(II)}\quad a\in 1+J,\qquad c\in J.
\]

Because \(A\) is strongly clean in \(M_2(R)\), there is an idempotent \(E\in M_2(R)\) such that
\[
AE=EA,\qquad A-E\in U(M_2(R)).
\]
Reducing modulo \(J\), we obtain commuting idempotents \(\bar A,\bar E\in M_2(D)\), with \(\bar A-\bar E\) invertible. For commuting idempotents \(p,e\),
\[
(p-e)(p+e-1)=0.
\]
Hence, if \(p-e\) is invertible, then \(p+e-1=0\), so \(e=1-p\). Therefore
\[
\bar E=I-\bar A.
\]

### Case I: \(a\in J\), \(c\in1+J\)

Here
\[
\bar A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bar b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\bar E=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\bar b\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(Q=\ker E\). Since \(E\) is idempotent, \(Q\) is a finitely generated projective right \(R\)-module. Over a local ring every finitely generated projective module is free. Moreover,
\[
Q/QJ\cong \ker \bar E
\]
is one-dimensional over \(D\), so \(Q\) is free of rank one. The line \(\ker\bar E\) is generated by \((\bar b,1)^T\). We may therefore choose a basis vector
\[
q=(t,1)^T
\]
for \(Q\): the second coordinate of any lift is a unit and can be normalized to \(1\).

Because \(AE=EA\), the module \(Q\) is \(A\)-invariant. Hence \(Aq=qr\) for some \(r\in R\). Comparing second coordinates gives \(r=c\), and therefore
\[
at+b=tc.
\]
Put \(x=-t\) and
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\in T_2(R).
\]
Then \(F^2=F\), and \(AF=FA\) is equivalent to
\[
ax-xc=b,
\]
which is exactly the preceding identity. Finally,
\[
A-F=\begin{pmatrix}a-1&b-x\\0&c\end{pmatrix}
\]
has unit diagonal entries, so it is a unit in \(T_2(R)\). Thus \(A\) is strongly clean in \(T_2(R)\).

### Case II: \(a\in1+J\), \(c\in J\)

Now
\[
\bar A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar b\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\bar E=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\bar b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(P=\operatorname{im}E\). Again \(P\) is a rank-one free right \(R\)-module, and \(P/PJ\) is generated by \((-\bar b,1)^T\). Choose a basis vector
\[
p=(t,1)^T.
\]
Since \(P\) is \(A\)-invariant, \(Ap=pr\) for some \(r\), and comparison of second coordinates yields \(r=c\). Hence again
\[
at+b=tc.
\]
Set
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(F^2=F\), \(AF=FA\), and
\[
A-F=\begin{pmatrix}a&b-t\\0&c-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
has unit diagonal entries. Hence \(A-F\) is a unit of \(T_2(R)\), proving the claim.

## Context and comparison with the literature

Borooah--Diesl--Dorsey explicitly asked whether a triangular matrix that is strongly clean in \(M_n(R)\) must already be strongly clean in \(T_n(R)\), primarily for local rings, and separately whether strong cleanness of \(M_n(R)\) forces strong cleanness of \(T_n(R)\). The theorem above settles both questions for \(n=2\).

Their earlier work gives criteria for strong cleanness of triangular matrix rings over local rings, and Yang--Zhou characterize when \(M_2(R)\) is strongly clean for a general local ring. Tang--Zhou later proved triangular-matrix embedding and similarity results under a bleaching hypothesis. Targeted searches did not locate the elementwise descent statement above for arbitrary local rings.

The proof here uses the mod-\(J\) rigidity of the commuting idempotent together with the rank-one invariant summand supplied by a strong clean decomposition. No commutativity, bleaching, completeness, or finiteness hypothesis is used.

## Limitations

- The argument is specific to \(2\times2\) matrices and does not resolve the corresponding questions for \(n\ge 3\).
- Originality is asserted only to the best of our knowledge.
- The full texts of the Yang--Zhou 2008 paper and the Tang--Zhou 2017 paper were not inspected; their accessible abstracts and bibliographic metadata were checked. An equivalent formulation hidden in those or other older local-ring literature remains a residual originality risk.
