# Fixed prime support in square-perimeter primitive Pythagorean triangles

## Statement

Let \(S=\{\ell_1,\dots,\ell_r\}\) be a nonempty finite set of distinct odd primes. Let \(N_S(X)\) denote the number of primitive integer right triangles, counted up to interchange of the two legs, whose perimeter \(P\) is a perfect square, satisfies \(P\le X\), and has exact prime support
\[
\{p:p\mid P\}=\{2\}\cup S.
\]

For a nonempty subset \(A\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}\) and an exponent vector \(\mathbf b=(b_1,\dots,b_r)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}^r\), put
\[
L_A(\mathbf b)=
\sum_{i\in A}b_i\log_2\ell_i-
\sum_{i\notin A}b_i\log_2\ell_i.
\]
Then the required triangles are in bijection with the pairs \((A,\mathbf b)\) satisfying
\[
L_A(\mathbf b)>\frac12,
\qquad
\{L_A(\mathbf b)\}>\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
For such a pair, set
\[
a=\lfloor L_A(\mathbf b)\rfloor,
\qquad
U=2^a\prod_{i\notin A}\ell_i^{b_i},
\qquad
V=\prod_{i\in A}\ell_i^{b_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
The associated primitive Pythagorean triangle is generated by
\[
m=2U^2,
\qquad
n=V^2-2U^2,
\tag{3}
\]
so its sides are
\[
m^2-n^2,
\qquad
2mn,
\qquad
m^2+n^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Its perimeter is
\[
P=(2UV)^2.
\tag{5}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
N_S(X)\sim
\frac{\binom{2r-1}{r}}
{2\,4^r\,r!\,\prod_{i=1}^r\log \ell_i}
(\log X)^r
}
\qquad(X\to\infty),
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\log\) denotes the natural logarithm. In particular, every prescribed finite odd-prime set \(S\) occurs in infinitely many primitive Pythagorean triangles with square perimeter and exact perimeter support \(\{2\}\cup S\).

For \(r=1\), with \(S=\{\ell\}\), the classification becomes especially simple:
\[
\boxed{\{b\log_2\ell\}>\frac12.}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the admissible exponents \(b\) have natural density \(1/2\), and
\[
N_{\{\ell\}}(X)\sim\frac{\log X}{8\log\ell}.
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

Every primitive Pythagorean triangle has the unique Euclidean form, up to interchanging the legs,
\[
(x,y,z)=(m^2-n^2,2mn,m^2+n^2),
\]
where \(m>n>0\), \((m,n)=1\), and \(m,n\) have opposite parity. Its perimeter is
\[
P=2m(m+n).
\tag{9}
\]
Since \((m,m+n)=1\), if \(P\) is a square then \(m\) cannot be odd: otherwise both \(m\) and \(m+n\) are odd and \(v_2(P)=1\). Hence \(m\) is even and \(m+n\) is odd. Coprimality in (9) then forces
\[
m=2U^2,
\qquad
m+n=V^2
\tag{10}
\]
for integers \(U,V>0\), with \(V\) odd and \((U,V)=1\). Conversely, (10) gives a primitive Euclidean pair exactly when
\[
0<V^2-2U^2<2U^2,
\]
that is,
\[
\sqrt2\,U<V<2U.
\tag{11}
\]
The perimeter is then \((2UV)^2\). This is the standard square-perimeter normal form recorded by Yiu.

Now impose exact prime support \(\{2\}\cup S\). Because \((U,V)=1\) and \(V\) is odd, every odd prime \(\ell_i\in S\) occurs, with its entire positive exponent \(b_i\), in exactly one of \(U\) and \(V\). Let \(A\) be the set of indices occurring in \(V\). It is nonempty. The only remaining factor allowed in \(U\) is a power of \(2\), so (2) follows for some \(a\ge0\).

Writing
\[
U_0=\prod_{i\notin A}\ell_i^{b_i},
\qquad
V=\prod_{i\in A}\ell_i^{b_i},
\]
we have
\[
L_A(\mathbf b)=\log_2(V/U_0).
\]
Condition (11) is exactly
\[
a+\frac12<L_A(\mathbf b)<a+1.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(a=\lfloor L_A(\mathbf b)\rfloor\), and (12) is equivalent to (1). The endpoints cannot occur: a rational relation between \(\log_2(V/U_0)\) and \(1\) would imply an equality between a nontrivial ratio of odd-prime powers and a power of \(2\), contrary to unique factorization. This proves the bijection.

It remains to count. Fix a nonempty \(A\), write \(k=|A|\), and put
\[
s=\sum_{i\in A}b_i\log\ell_i,
\qquad
t=\sum_{i\notin A}b_i\log\ell_i,
\qquad f=\{L_A(\mathbf b)\}.
\]
For an admissible vector, \(f\in(1/2,1)\), and from (2) and (5),
\[
P=2^{2(1-f)}V^4.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore
\[
V^4<P<2V^4.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently replacing \(P\le X\) by \(s\le T=(\log X)/4\), and replacing the fixed-width inequality \(s-t>(\log2)/2\) by \(s>t\), changes only a boundary layer of lower order.

On lattice points in the resulting expanding polytope, the values
\[
L_A(\mathbf b)\pmod1
\]
are uniformly distributed. One may apply the multidimensional Weyl criterion: for every nonzero integer frequency, summation in any one exponent coordinate is a bounded geometric sum because each \(\log_2\ell_i\) is irrational, while the remaining coordinates contribute only a boundary-order number of slices. Hence asymptotically one half of the lattice points satisfy \(f\in(1/2,1)\).

After the change of variables \(y_i=b_i\log\ell_i/T\), the relevant normalized region is
\[
y_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in A}y_i\le1,
\qquad
\sum_{i\notin A}y_i<\sum_{i\in A}y_i.
\tag{15}
\]
Its volume is
\[
\int_0^1
\frac{s^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}
\frac{s^{r-k}}{(r-k)!}\,ds
=
\frac1{r(k-1)!(r-k)!}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the contribution of this fixed subset \(A\) is asymptotic to
\[
\frac12\,
\frac{T^r}{\prod_i\log\ell_i}\,
\frac1{r(k-1)!(r-k)!}.
\tag{17}
\]
Summing over all nonempty \(A\) and using Vandermonde's identity gives
\[
\sum_{k=1}^r
\binom rk\frac1{r(k-1)!(r-k)!}
=
\frac1{r!}\binom{2r-1}{r}.
\tag{18}
\]
Substituting \(T=(\log X)/4\) proves (6).

## Examples

For \(S=\{3\}\), \(b=1\) has \(\{\log_2 3\}>1/2\). Equations (2)--(4) give
\[
(16,63,65),
\qquad P=144=12^2.
\]
The next admissible exponent is \(b=3\), giving another triangle with perimeter \(864^2\).

For \(S=\{5\}\), the first admissible exponent is \(b=2\). It gives
\[
(115712,249375,274913),
\qquad P=640000=800^2.
\]

For \(S=\{3,5\}\), formula (6) gives
\[
N_{\{3,5\}}(X)
\sim
\frac{3}{64\log3\log5}(\log X)^2.
\]

## Computational verification

The accompanying exact-integer script independently checks the square-perimeter normal form and support decomposition over all 1,266,847 primitive Euclidean parameter pairs with \(m\le2500\). It finds 150 square-perimeter cases and zero normal-form/support mismatches. It also constructs 140 bounded examples for the fixed supports \(\{3\}\), \(\{3,5\}\), and \(\{3,5,7\}\), with zero failures. Additional counts for these supports approach the leading constants in (6). These finite checks support but do not replace the proof.

Run:

```bash
python artifacts/verify.py
```

The expected deterministic output is stored in `artifacts/verification.txt`.

## Literature context and originality boundary

Yiu's *Recreational Mathematics*, §6.2, gives the square-perimeter normal form \(m=2U^2\), \(m+n=V^2\), \((U,V)=1\), \(\sqrt2 U<V<2U\), and \(P=(2UV)^2\), together with initial examples. OEIS A120089 and A120090 record the corresponding square perimeters and their square roots and point back to Yiu. Those facts are prior work and are not claimed here.

The contribution here is the exact fixed-prime-support bijection (1)--(5), the consequence that every finite odd-prime support occurs infinitely often, and the explicit asymptotic (6). Searches for square-perimeter primitive Pythagorean triangles together with prime support, smooth/S-unit terminology, exact numbers of prime factors, and asymptotic counting did not locate an equivalent or stronger result in accessible indexed literature or the current sequence references. Originality is therefore asserted only to the best of our knowledge. Because the square-perimeter parametrization is elementary and has circulated in recreational-number-theory sources for many years, an equivalent refinement could exist under different terminology in older problem literature.

The counting theorem concerns **triangles**, not distinct perimeter values: for \(|S|>1\), different coprime allocations of the same prime-power data can in principle give different primitive triangles with the same perimeter.
