# Stationary height defect and a sharp joint excursion barrier in the Shimizu–Morioka system

Consider the classical Shimizu–Morioka system
\[
\dot x=y,\qquad
\dot y=x(1-z)-a y,\qquad
\dot z=x^2-bz,
\tag{1}
\]
with \(a,b>0\). Its three equilibria are
\[
O=(0,0,0),\qquad
E_\pm=(\pm\sqrt b,0,1).
\]

Let \(\mu\) be a compactly supported invariant Borel probability measure for (1), and let \(\nu=z_\#\mu\) be its \(z\)-marginal.

## Theorem

Every such \(\mu\) satisfies the following statements.

### 1. Exact heightwise balance

The conditional second moment of \(x\) is determined exactly by height:
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\mu[x^2\mid z]=bz\qquad \nu\text{-a.e.}}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq\{z\ge0\},
\tag{3}
\]
and in particular
\[
\langle x^2\rangle=b\langle z\rangle,\qquad
\langle x^2z\rangle=b\langle z^2\rangle,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\langle\cdot\rangle=\int(\cdot)\,d\mu\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(x^2,z)=b\,\operatorname{Var}_\mu(z).
\tag{5}
\]

### 2. Exact stationary height defect

Define
\[
W(x,y,z)=xy-z+\frac{z^2}{2}+\frac a2x^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Along every classical solution,
\[
\boxed{\dot W=y^2-bz(z-1).}
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore every compactly supported invariant measure obeys
\[
\boxed{\langle y^2\rangle=b\langle z(z-1)\rangle}
\tag{8}
\]
and equivalently
\[
\boxed{
\left\langle\left(z-\frac12\right)^2\right\rangle
=\frac14+\frac1b\langle y^2\rangle.
}
\tag{9}
\]
In particular,
\[
\left\langle\left(z-\frac12\right)^2\right\rangle\ge\frac14.
\tag{10}
\]

### 3. Sharp equality rigidity

The following are equivalent:
\[
\left\langle\left(z-\frac12\right)^2\right\rangle=\frac14,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
\mu(\{z>1\})=0,
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mu=p_0\delta_O+p_+\delta_{E_+}+p_-\delta_{E_-},
\qquad
p_0,p_+,p_-\ge0,\quad p_0+p_++p_-=1.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus the lower bound in (10) is sharp, and its complete equality set is the simplex generated by the three equilibrium measures.

### 4. Joint excursion barrier

If \(\mu\) is not an equilibrium mixture of the form (13), then
\[
\boxed{
\mu\!\left(\left\{z>1,\ |x|>\sqrt b\right\}\right)>0.
}
\tag{14}
\]
Hence every nonconstant periodic orbit of (1) has a time \(t\) at which the two strict inequalities hold simultaneously:
\[
\boxed{z(t)>1,\qquad |x(t)|>\sqrt b.}
\tag{15}
\]
The thresholds are exact: the two nonzero equilibria sit precisely at \(z=1\) and \(|x|=\sqrt b\).

## Proof

For any \(C^1\) test function \(F\), invariance and compact support give
\[
\int L F\,d\mu=0,
\tag{16}
\]
where \(L\) is the generator of (1).

Let \(h\) be any continuous function on the compact \(z\)-projection of \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and choose \(H\) with \(H'=h\). Since
\[
L(H(z))=h(z)(x^2-bz),
\]
(16) gives
\[
\int h(z)(x^2-bz)\,d\mu=0
\]
for every such \(h\). This is exactly the conditional-expectation statement (2). Because a conditional expectation of the nonnegative quantity \(x^2\) is nonnegative, (2) and \(b>0\) imply \(z\ge0\) \(\nu\)-almost surely, which gives (3). Choosing \(h=1\) and \(h=z\) gives (4), and (5) follows by subtraction of the product of means.

A direct differentiation of (6), using (1), yields
\[
LW=y^2-bz(z-1),
\]
which proves (7). Averaging gives (8), and completing the square gives (9).

If equality holds in (10), then (9) implies \(y=0\) \(\mu\)-almost surely. Since \(y\) is continuous, the support of \(\mu\) lies in \(\{y=0\}\). The support of a compactly supported invariant measure is invariant. A complete bounded orbit that remains in \(\{y=0\}\) must satisfy \(\dot x=0\) and \(x(1-z)=0\). If \(x\ne0\), then \(z=1\), and \(\dot z=0\) forces \(x^2=b\), giving \(E_\pm\). If \(x=0\), then \(\dot z=-bz\); the only complete bounded orbit on this axis is \(z=0\), giving \(O\). Hence equality forces (13), and every measure in (13) plainly gives equality.

Conversely, suppose \(\mu(\{z>1\})=0\). From (3), \(0\le z\le1\) almost surely, so \(z(z-1)\le0\). Equation (8) has a nonnegative left side and a nonpositive right side; both must vanish. Thus equality holds in (9), and the preceding rigidity argument gives (13). This proves the equivalence of (11)--(13).

Now assume that \(\mu\) is not an equilibrium mixture. The rigidity just proved and (8) imply
\[
\langle z(z-1)\rangle>0.
\]
Since \(z\ge0\), this forces \(\nu((1,\infty))>0\). By (2),
\[
\int_{\{z>1\}}x^2\,d\mu
=b\int_{\{z>1\}}z\,d\mu
>b\,\mu(\{z>1\}).
\tag{17}
\]
If \(x^2\le b\) almost surely on \(\{z>1\}\), the left side of (17) would be at most the rightmost quantity, a contradiction. This proves (14). Applying (14) to the normalized occupation measure of a nonconstant periodic orbit proves (15).

## Bounded-trajectory balance

The same identity (7) also has a trajectory-level consequence that does not assume ergodicity. For every bounded forward solution,
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T\left[y(t)^2-bz(t)(z(t)-1)\right]dt
=\frac{W(T)-W(0)}T\longrightarrow0.
\tag{18}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T\left(z(t)-\frac12\right)^2dt
-\frac14
-\frac1{bT}\int_0^T y(t)^2dt
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{19}
\]
Also,
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T\bigl(x(t)^2-bz(t)\bigr)\,dt
=\frac{z(T)-z(0)}T\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
These are asymptotic balance laws only; they do not assert convergence of the trajectory or uniqueness of its limiting invariant measure.

## Relation to prior work

Shimizu and Morioka introduced (1) in 1980 in their study of the symmetry-breaking bifurcation of a limit cycle. Messias, Gouveia and Pessoa (2012) gave a global Poincaré-compactification analysis and, in particular, a theorem-level description of the dynamics at infinity and of the degenerate \(\alpha=0\) geometry. Llibre and Pessoa (2015) analyzed the codimension-one and codimension-two Hopf bifurcations at the two nonzero equilibria. Capiński, Turaev and Zgliczyński (2018) rigorously established a Lorenz attractor for an open parameter set. Huang, Shi and Li (2020) related the system by scaling to a Rucklidge system and studied integrability and non-integrability. More recent work continues to study Lorenz-attractor boundaries and validated connecting orbits.

The novelty claimed here is narrower: the exact invariant-measure conditional law (2), the square height defect (9), its complete equality rigidity (13), and the simultaneous excursion barrier (14), for all \(a,b>0\). No novelty is claimed for the model, its equilibria, its Hopf or homoclinic bifurcations, existence of Lorenz attractors, the Shimizu–Morioka/Rucklidge equivalence, or the general generator identity \(\int LF\,d\mu=0\).

To the best of our knowledge, targeted searches of Shimizu–Morioka and equivalent Rucklidge formulations did not locate these stationary-measure identities or the joint threshold (14). The full 2012 global-dynamics paper and the accessible 2015 Hopf preprint were checked for the relevant theorem statements and for measure/average formulations. The 1980 original article and some older Russian bifurcation literature were not fully inspected at theorem level; the 2020 integrability paper was inspected through its bibliographic/abstract-level description rather than complete text. These are the main residual priority risks.

## Scientific limitations

The theorem assumes \(a,b>0\) and compact support of the invariant probability measure. It does not prove that a bounded attractor or a particular invariant measure exists for a given parameter pair, does not establish ergodicity or uniqueness, and does not give pointwise bounds for arbitrary nonperiodic trajectories. The joint excursion conclusion is a positive-measure statistical statement; the pointwise periodic-orbit corollary follows because periodic occupation measure is compactly supported. The case \(b=0\) is excluded and is structurally different, with a line of equilibria appearing in the classical analysis.
