# Same-model review

**Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.**

## Correctness — PASS

The central identity was checked algebraically with all operator domains and
codomains explicit. For \(R=ST\), the complemented copy of the self-similar
factor space gives \(U:X\to Y\) and \(V:Y\to X\) with \(UV=I_Y\), so
\[
[SU,VT]=R-V(TS)U.
\]
On the countable self-sum, the left/right shifts satisfy
\(LR_+=I\) and \(R_+L=I-\iota_0\pi_0\). For
\(\widetilde C=\operatorname{diag}(C,C,\ldots)\),
\[
[L,R_+\widetilde C]=\iota_0C\pi_0.
\]
Conjugating this corner identity through the complemented embedding gives the
second commutator exactly. Each of the four factors visibly factors through
\(Y\) (or through its isomorphic self-sum), so the ideal-membership claim does
not rely on an inheritance assumption.

The James-space applications use the factorization and complementedness
statements proved in the cited sources rather than an unconditional-basis
shortcut.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit priority risk

Laustsen (2002) proved that every weakly compact operator on \(J_p\) is a sum
of three weakly compact commutators and explicitly asked in Question 4.9 whether
three is optimal. Dosev (2009) contains the relevant self-similar
corner-commutator mechanism, so that mechanism itself is prior art and is not
claimed as new. Kania--Kochanek (2014) and Kania--Koszmider--Laustsen (2014)
still stated three-commutator bounds in the analogous factorization settings.

No explicit theorem located in the checked literature states the present
two-commutator transfer or resolves Laustsen's Question 4.9 by this route.
The principal residual risk is nevertheless substantial: the proof is a short
synthesis of known ingredients, so an equivalent observation may appear in
older commutator literature under different language. The originality claim is
therefore restricted to **to the best of our knowledge** and excludes the
shift-corner identity itself.

No specifically identified inaccessible source was found whose available
bibliographic information indicated explicit coverage of the two-commutator
James-space conclusion. The broader commutator literature was not exhaustively
checked theorem-by-theorem.

## Value — PASS

The result settles a named optimality question from Laustsen's 2002 paper at
the level of the upper bound, replacing three by two. The same structural lemma
also sharpens two later published three-commutator bounds without requiring new
space-specific calculations. The decomposition keeps all commutator factors
inside the same operator ideal, which is stronger than merely representing the
operator as a sum of two commutators in the ambient algebra.

## Boundary of the claim

No assertion is made that two is optimal. Whether every element of the
relevant ideals is itself a single commutator is left open here.
