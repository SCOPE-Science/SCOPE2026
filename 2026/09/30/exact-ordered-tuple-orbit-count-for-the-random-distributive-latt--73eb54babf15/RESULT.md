# Exact ordered-tuple orbit count for the random distributive lattice
## Finding
Let \(\mathbb D\) be the Fraïssé limit of the class of all finite distributive lattices in the language with the two binary operations \((\wedge,\vee)\), and write \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb D)\). For every integer \(n\ge 1\), the \(G\)-orbits on ordered \(n\)-tuples are canonically classified by congruences of the free \(n\)-generated distributive lattice \(F_n\). More explicitly, they are parametrized by arbitrary subsets of
\[
\{S\subset[n]:\varnothing\ne S\ne[n]\}.
\]
Consequently the exact number \(o_n\) of ordered \(n\)-tuple orbits is
\[
o_n=2^{2^n-2}.
\]
The first values are \(o_1=1\), \(o_2=4\), \(o_3=64\), and \(o_4=16384\).

## Assumptions and scope
The language is exactly the binary lattice language \((\wedge,\vee)\); the least and greatest elements of a finite lattice are not named constants. This is the language used for the random distributive lattice in Kechris--Sokić. The statement concerns ordered tuples, with repetitions allowed, and integers \(n\ge 1\).

Write \(F_n\) for the free distributive lattice on generators \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) in this same language. It is finite. For a tuple \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb D^n\), let
\[
e_a:F_n\longrightarrow \langle a_1,\ldots,a_n\rangle
\]
be the evaluation homomorphism onto the finite sublattice generated by the tuple.

## Proof
First classify tuple orbits by kernels. If \(a,b\in\mathbb D^n\) lie in the same \(G\)-orbit, every lattice-term equality holding at \(a\) also holds at \(b\), so
\[
\ker(e_a)=\ker(e_b).
\]
Conversely, if these kernels agree, the rule \(e_a(t)\mapsto e_b(t)\) is a well-defined isomorphism between the two finite generated sublattices, carrying \(a_i\) to \(b_i\) for every \(i\). Ultrahomogeneity of the Fraïssé limit extends that finite isomorphism to an automorphism of \(\mathbb D\). Hence \(a\) and \(b\) are in the same orbit. Finally, every congruence \(\theta\in\operatorname{Con}(F_n)\) occurs: the quotient \(F_n/\theta\) is a finite distributive lattice, so it embeds into \(\mathbb D\); the images of the free generators then give a tuple whose evaluation kernel is \(\theta\). Therefore
\[
G\backslash\mathbb D^n\;\cong\;\operatorname{Con}(F_n).
\]

It remains to count the congruences. For any finite distributive lattice \(L\), let \(J(L)\) be its poset of nonzero join-irreducible elements. By finite Birkhoff duality,
\[
L\cong \mathcal O(J(L)),
\]
where \(\mathcal O(P)\) denotes the lattice of down-sets of a finite poset \(P\). For each subset \(Q\subseteq J(L)\), restriction
\[
\pi_Q:\mathcal O(J(L))\longrightarrow\mathcal O(Q),\qquad A\longmapsto A\cap Q
\]
is a surjective lattice homomorphism. Finite Birkhoff duality sends every quotient of \(L\) to an order embedding of the dual poset of the quotient into \(J(L)\), so every congruence kernel is \(\ker(\pi_Q)\) for a unique subset \(Q\subseteq J(L)\). Thus
\[
|\operatorname{Con}(L)|=2^{|J(L)|}.
\]

For \(F_n\), use disjunctive normal form. Its join-irreducibles are precisely the meet monomials
\[
m_S=\bigwedge_{i\in S}x_i
\]
with \(\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq[n]\). The case \(S=[n]\) is the least element of \(F_n\), hence is not join-irreducible; \(S=\varnothing\) would be an empty meet and is not a term in the binary language. Therefore
\[
|J(F_n)|=2^n-2.
\]
Combining the two steps gives
\[
|G\backslash\mathbb D^n|=|\operatorname{Con}(F_n)|=2^{2^n-2}.
\]

As a low-rank sanity check, \(n=2\) gives four orbits, corresponding exactly to \(a=b\), \(a<b\), \(a>b\), and \(a\) incomparable with \(b\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently constructs the free distributive lattices in the binary language for \(1\le n\le4\), checks the element counts \(1,4,18,166\), and exhaustively verifies that the numbers of join-irreducibles are \(0,2,6,14\). It also enumerates all lattice congruences by congruence closure for \(n\le3\), obtaining \(1,4,64\), exactly as predicted.

Same-model review reconstructed the kernel-orbit argument, the finite Birkhoff-duality step, the free-lattice join-irreducible count, and the edge cases \(n=1,2\). Independent audit, formal proof-assistant verification, and expert attestation have not been performed.

## Relationship to prior work
Kechris and Sokić establish that the class of finite distributive lattices in the binary lattice language is a Fraïssé class, denote its limit by \(\mathbb D\), and study dynamical properties of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb D)\). Their paper does not formulate the congruence-kernel classification or the ordered-tuple orbit count above. The lattice-theoretic ingredient is the standard finite Birkhoff representation theorem together with the elementary description of free distributive lattices.

Malicki later studied amenability and the universal minimal flow for closely related random-lattice automorphism groups. These dynamical results do not subsume the explicit tuple-orbit classification given here.

## Limitations
The originality assessment is best-of-knowledge rather than an exhaustive theorem-index proof of nonpublication. The result concerns ordered tuples in the pure binary lattice language; naming bounds or adding operations changes the relevant free algebra and can change the orbit count. No claim is made here about orbits on unordered finite subsets, stabilizer isomorphism types, or orbital growth for other Fraïssé limits.
