# Same-model review of Exact six-vertex clique extremum for \(K_5^{(3)}\)-free triple systems

## Correctness assessment
PASS. The proof reduces \(K_5^{(3)}\)-freeness to empty intersection of missing triples, then by complementation to a covering family \(\mathcal G\) of triples. The identity \(k(H)=57-(|\mathcal G|+|\partial_2\mathcal G|)\) was reconstructed directly. The case analysis proving \(|\mathcal G|+|\partial_2\mathcal G|\ge8\) handles every possible family size and has a unique equality case. Two separately written exhaustive programs agree on the value \(49\), ten labeled equality cases, and one isomorphism type.

## Originality assessment
PASS, best-of-knowledge. The closest current primary source, arXiv:2606.02210v1, proves unique balanced-bipartite extremality only for sufficiently large \(n\). Searches for the exact six-vertex statement, the equivalent value \(49\), the formulation \(|H|+N(K_4^{(3)},H)\), and the shadow reduction returned no matching theorem. published-finding corpus's closest semantic hit concerns the different forbidden tight cycle \(C_5^{(3)}\) on six vertices, not \(K_5^{(3)}\), and does not imply this claim.

## Value assessment
PASS. The result closes the first order beyond the five-vertex forbidden clique itself at which the large-order extremal construction can be tested nontrivially. It supplies an exact benchmark and equality classification for the clique-counting conjecture's \((r,\ell)=(3,4)\) case and a short shadow argument that is independently reusable for checking finite computations.

## Closest literature
Chen, Deng, Hou, Liu, and Zhang, arXiv:2606.02210v1, establish the same balanced-bipartite extremal picture for all sufficiently large orders and list primary MSC2020 05C35. Frankl, Gryaznov, and Talebanfard formulate the general clique-counting conjecture. Neither source, in the material checked, supplies the six-vertex theorem above.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem is confined to six vertices and does not determine the onset of the general eventual theorem. Novelty is a best-of-knowledge conclusion based on current primary-literature and published-finding corpus searches rather than a proof of nonpublication.
