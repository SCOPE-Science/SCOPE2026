# Exact tuple-orbit profiles of the five reduct groups of the Rado graph
## Finding
Let \(R=(V,E)\) be the countable Rado graph. Bodirsky and Pinsker recall the five closed permutation groups containing \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\): the automorphism group itself, its extensions by a complement isomorphism, by Seidel switching, by both operations, and the full symmetric group.

For one of these groups \(G\), write \(f_G(k)\) for the number of orbits on ordered \(k\)-tuples of pairwise distinct vertices and \(o_G(n)\) for the number of orbits on all ordered \(n\)-tuples. Then

\[
f_{G_E}(k)=2^{\binom{k}{2}},
\]

\[
f_{G_C}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&k\le 1,\\2^{\binom{k}{2}-1},&k\ge2,\end{cases}
\qquad
f_{G_S}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&k=0,\\2^{\binom{k-1}{2}},&k\ge1,\end{cases}
\]

\[
f_{G_{CS}}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&k\le2,\\2^{\binom{k-1}{2}-1},&k\ge3,\end{cases}
\qquad
f_{G_\infty}(k)=1.
\]

For all \(n\ge0\),

\[
o_G(n)=\sum_{k=0}^n {n\brace k} f_G(k),
\]

where \({n\brace k}\) is a Stirling number of the second kind. Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}o_G(n)\frac{z^n}{n!}
 =\sum_{k\ge0}f_G(k)\frac{(e^z-1)^k}{k!}.
\]

The resulting full tuple-orbit sequences through arity \(8\) are:

- \(G_E\): \(1,1,3,15,127,1895,53071,2953575,337064047\);
- \(G_C\): \(1,1,2,8,64,948,26536,1476788,168532024\);
- \(G_S\): \(1,1,2,6,28,210,2716,66698,3369908\);
- \(G_{CS}\): \(1,1,2,5,18,113,1374,33381,1685018\);
- \(G_\infty\): \(1,1,2,5,15,52,203,877,4140\).

Thus the five reduct groups are already pairwise distinguished by their full ordered-tuple orbit counts at arity at most \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns the five closed supergroups of the automorphism group of the countable Rado graph, acting diagonally on finite ordered tuples. The complement and switch operations are those in the cited classification. The tuple coordinates are labeled by their positions; no quotient by permutations of coordinates is taken.

The primary literature anchor is Bodirsky--Pinsker, whose author PDF lists primary Mathematics Subject Classification \(03C40\) and whose first arXiv version is dated 2009-03-14. Simon Thomas's earlier classification is used as historical background. The counting result here is not asserted to be independently audited or formally verified.

## Proof
Fix \(k\) pairwise distinct tuple entries. By homogeneity of the Rado graph, two such ordered tuples are in the same \(G_E\)-orbit exactly when they induce the same labeled graph on the coordinate set. Encode a labeled graph by its edge vector in

\[
W_k=\mathbb F_2^{E(K_k)},
\qquad \dim W_k=\binom{k}{2}.
\]

Hence \(f_{G_E}(k)=|W_k|=2^{\binom{k}{2}}\).

A complement isomorphism adds the all-ones vector \(\mathbf 1\in W_k\). For \(k\ge2\) this nonzero translation acts freely, giving \(2^{\binom{k}{2}-1}\) orbits; for \(k=0,1\) there is only one labeled graph.

Switching a set of tuple vertices adds a cut vector. More explicitly, for \(s\in\mathbb F_2^k\), define

\[
(\delta s)_{ij}=s_i+s_j.
\]

The kernel of \(\delta\) consists exactly of the two constant vectors when \(k\ge1\), so the cut space \(C_k=\operatorname{im}\delta\) has dimension \(k-1\). Translation by \(C_k\) is free on \(W_k\), which gives \(f_{G_S}(k)=2^{\binom{k}{2}-(k-1)}=2^{\binom{k-1}{2}}\) for \(k\ge1\).

For the group generated by switching and complementation, quotient by \(C_k+\langle\mathbf1\rangle\). If \(k\ge3\), the all-ones edge vector is not a cut vector: every cut meets a triangle in either zero or two edges, whereas \(\mathbf1\) meets it in three. Therefore this translation space has dimension \(k\), and the quotient has size \(2^{\binom{k}{2}-k}=2^{\binom{k-1}{2}-1}\). For \(k=2\), complementation is already the unique nonzero cut translation, so there is one orbit. The cases \(k=0,1\) are also singletons. The full symmetric group is highly transitive, hence has one orbit on pairwise distinct ordered \(k\)-tuples.

Now allow repetitions. Equality of coordinates is preserved by every permutation of \(V\). For a fixed equality partition of the \(n\) coordinate positions into \(k\) blocks, choosing the distinct values of those blocks produces exactly \(f_G(k)\) orbits. There are \({n\brace k}\) such equality partitions, so summing over \(k\) gives the stated Stirling transform. The exponential generating function is the standard labeled-set-partition composition identity.

## Verification
The accompanying script `artifacts/verify.py` independently represents all labeled graphs on up to five vertices by bit masks, generates complement and cut translations directly, exhaustively computes their translation orbits, and compares the resulting orbit counts with every closed formula above. It then computes Stirling numbers and verifies all five displayed full tuple-orbit sequences through arity \(8\). It terminates with `VERIFY_OK`.

The exhaustive checks include the small exceptional cases where complement lies in the cut space. They do not constitute an independent audit of the literature search or of the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Thomas classified the five reducts/closed supergroups of the random graph. Bodirsky and Pinsker give another proof and explicitly describe the complement and switch generators. Classical two-graph theory identifies Seidel-switching classes of labeled graphs with two-graphs, which is consistent with the quotient by the cut space used here. Applegate and Cameron study general lower bounds for orbit counts on ordered tuples of distinct points, but the checked text does not give these five random-graph reduct profiles.

Targeted searches for the exact formulas, the first terms of the two nontrivial switching-derived sequences, and the five-group profile found no publication or published published-finding corpus finding stating this unified tuple-orbit classification. The originality claim is therefore best-of-knowledge, not a bibliographic completeness theorem.

## Limitations
The result is an elementary consequence of the known five-group classification plus finite-dimensional binary linear algebra; its value is the exact unified quantitative profile rather than a new classification of reducts. The literature search cannot exclude an equivalent formula under different terminology, especially in older two-graph or oligomorphic-group literature. No independent audit, Lean verification, or expert attestation has been performed.
