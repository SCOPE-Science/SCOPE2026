# Review of Finite-collapse saturation of the equality-modal theory of infinite sets

## Correctness
PASS. Wołoszyn's all-functions elimination theorem makes every equality-modal formula on infinite sets depend only on the equality partition of its finite tuple. The comparison induction is exact: a restricted arrow is automatically an all-functions arrow, while any all-functions modal witness induces only a coarsening of the current tuple partition; the finite-collapse hypothesis realizes that same coarsening by an allowed endomap. Partition invariance and the induction hypothesis then transport the subformula truth. Quantifiers are unchanged because both semantics range over the same current set. The finite-to-one corollary follows because a map that is the identity off a finite set has finite fibers.

## Originality
PASS, best-of-knowledge. The closest recent source lists functions, surjections, injections, inclusions, bijections, and identities, but not finite-to-one maps, and it does not formulate a general finite-collapse-rich invariance theorem. Targeted searches for finite-to-one, finite-fiber, finite-support, and finite-collapse variants found no matching modal-set classification. The prior local result on modal non-elementarity is scientifically distinct.

## Value
PASS. The result identifies a structural sufficiency principle rather than adding only one omitted morphism class: cross-world functions can be discarded entirely for pure equality-modal semantics as long as all finite tuple collapses remain available. This explains why finite-to-one maps retain the all-functions logic and marks the boundary with arrow classes such as injections or fixed bounded-to-one maps that cannot realize arbitrary finite coarsenings.

## Closest literature
The closest source is Wojciech Aleksander Wołoszyn, *The modal theory of the category of sets*, arXiv:2603.25550. Its modality-and-quantifier elimination theorem supplies partition invariance for the all-functions category, and its infinite-world classification supplies the transferred \(\mathrm{Triv}\) and \(\mathrm{Grz.2}\) consequences.

## Scientific limitations
The argument uses only equality and infinitude. It should not be extrapolated to richer signatures or finite worlds without new work. The literature comparison is targeted rather than exhaustive, and no independent audit or formal proof has been performed.
