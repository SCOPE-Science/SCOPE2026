# Honzik's random integer is not hereditarily countable
## Finding
Assume \(\mathsf{CH}\). Work in the forcing setup of Honzik: \(V\) is a transitive ground model of set theory, \(M\in V\) is an \(\omega_1\)-saturated nonstandard model of true arithmetic of size \(\omega_1\), \(\Omega\in M\) is nonstandard, and \(G\) is \(V\)-generic for the probability algebra \(\mathcal B_{M,\Omega}\).

Honzik proves
\[
\mathcal B_{M,\Omega}\cong\mathcal B_{\omega_1},
\]
where \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\) is the product random algebra on \(2^{\omega_1}\), and also proves
\[
V[\mathrm{id}_G]=V[G].
\]

In this situation,
\[
\mathrm{id}_G\notin H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}.
\]
More strongly, no hereditarily countable parameter \(x\in H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}\) satisfies
\[
V[x]=V[G].
\]
The same holds for every countable family of hereditarily countable parameters. Moreover, if
\[
\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{HC}}=\min\{|X|:X\subseteq H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}\text{ and }V[X]=V[G]\},
\]
then
\[
\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{HC}}=\omega_1.
\]
The lower bound is the countable-support obstruction. For the upper bound, partition the \(\omega_1\) forcing coordinates into \(\omega_1\) countable blocks; the corresponding block-random reals jointly recover the full generic.

Thus the single object called the “random integer” in the arithmetic extension is not externally a real-sized or hereditarily countable object. Its ability to generate the entire \(\omega_1\)-random forcing extension necessarily uses genuinely uncountable external set-theoretic information.

## Assumptions and scope
The cardinal \(\omega_1\) is the ground-model \(\omega_1^V\). Random forcing is ccc, so this cardinal remains \(\omega_1\) in the generic extension.

For \(S\subseteq\omega_1\), let \(\mathcal B_S\) denote the product random algebra using the coordinates in \(S\), and let \(G_S\) be the generic induced by \(G\) on that complete subalgebra.

The argument uses two ingredients.

First, Honzik's Theorem 5.13 identifies \(\mathcal B_{M,\Omega}\) with \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\) under the displayed hypotheses. Second, his Theorem 5.21 proves that the random integer generated by the identity random variable satisfies \(V[\mathrm{id}_G]=V[G]\).

The additional support argument below is a standard fact about product random forcing: every measurable set, and hence every Boolean value used in a name for a real, depends on only countably many coordinates.

## Proof
Consider the product probability space \(2^{\omega_1}\) with its product \(\sigma\)-algebra. A measurable set has countable coordinate support. Indeed, let \(\mathscr C\) be the class of sets that depend on a countable set of coordinates. The finite-coordinate cylinder sets belong to \(\mathscr C\); complements preserve the same support; and the union of countably many members of \(\mathscr C\) is supported by the countable union of their supports. Hence \(\mathscr C\) is a \(\sigma\)-algebra containing the cylinders and therefore contains every product-measurable set. Passing modulo null sets gives the corresponding statement for elements of \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\).

Let \(r\in 2^\omega\cap V[G]\). Choose a Boolean name for \(r\). For each \(n\in\omega\), the Boolean value deciding \(r(n)\) has countable support. The union of these supports is a countable set \(S\subseteq\omega_1\), and the name can be interpreted already by \(G_S\). Therefore
\[
r\in V[G_S]
\]
for some countable \(S\subseteq\omega_1\).

Such a countable-coordinate subextension is proper. Choose \(\alpha\in\omega_1\setminus S\). Product random forcing factors over disjoint coordinate sets, so the \(\alpha\)-coordinate random real is random-generic over \(V[G_S]\). In particular it is not in \(V[G_S]\), whereas it belongs to \(V[G]\). Hence
\[
V[G_S]\subsetneq V[G].
\]
It follows immediately that no real \(r\in V[G]\) can generate the full extension over \(V\): since \(r\in V[G_S]\),
\[
V[r]\subseteq V[G_S]\subsetneq V[G].
\]

Now let \(x\in H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}\). In \(V[G]\), the transitive closure of \(x\) is countable, so \(x\) is coded by a real \(r\): one may code a countable well-founded extensional relation together with a distinguished element and recover \(x\) by Mostowski collapse. Thus
\[
x\in V[r].
\]
By the real-support argument, \(r\in V[G_S]\) for some countable \(S\), and therefore
\[
V[x]\subseteq V[r]\subseteq V[G_S]\subsetneq V[G].
\]
So no hereditarily countable \(x\) generates the full extension.

The same proof handles a countable family \(\langle x_n:n\in\omega\rangle\) of hereditarily countable parameters. Choose a real code \(r_n\) for each \(x_n\), take countable supports \(S_n\), and set
\[
S=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}S_n.
\]
Then \(S\) is countable and every \(x_n\) belongs to \(V[G_S]\), so
\[
V[\langle x_n:n\in\omega\rangle]\subseteq V[G_S]\subsetneq V[G].
\]

This also gives the lower bound
\[
\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{HC}}\geq\omega_1,
\]
because random forcing is ccc, so every family of cardinality below \(\omega_1\) in \(V[G]\) is countable. For the reverse inequality, fix in \(V\) a partition
\[
\omega_1=\bigcup_{\xi<\omega_1}S_\xi
\]
into countably infinite sets. For each \(\xi\), the generic restricted to \(S_\xi\) is coded by a real \(r_\xi\). The family \(\langle r_\xi:\xi<\omega_1\rangle\) determines the generic on every coordinate and hence generates \(V[G]\). Therefore
\[
\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{HC}}=\omega_1.
\]

Finally, Honzik's Theorem 5.21 gives
\[
V[\mathrm{id}_G]=V[G].
\]
The preceding conclusion therefore forces
\[
\mathrm{id}_G\notin H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}.
\]

## Verification
The argument was checked at each of the two places where a hidden size assumption could enter.

The countable-support step uses only the definition of the product \(\sigma\)-algebra: countable unions of countable supports remain countable. It is not an assertion that the full algebra is countably generated. In fact, Honzik's identification has Maharam type \(\omega_1\), which is precisely the nonseparable regime.

The properness step uses a fresh coordinate \(\alpha\notin S\) and the standard factorization of random forcing on disjoint coordinate sets. Thus the argument does not merely say that a countable-coordinate algebra is smaller as an abstract Boolean algebra; it produces a real present in the full extension and absent from the subextension.

For a hereditarily countable \(x\), the proof does not assume that \(x\) itself is a real. It first codes \(x\) by a real and then reconstructs \(x\) by Mostowski collapse. Therefore the conclusion applies to all of \(H_{\omega_1}\), not only to subsets of \(\omega\).

The exact value \(\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{HC}}=\omega_1\) was checked in both directions: the lower bound uses ccc preservation of \(\omega_1\) together with the countable-family obstruction, while the upper bound explicitly partitions the forcing coordinates into \(\omega_1\) countable blocks whose random reals recover the full generic.

Finally, applying the conclusion to \(\mathrm{id}_G\) uses the exact equality \(V[\mathrm{id}_G]=V[G]\) from Honzik's Theorem 5.21. No independent or formal-machine verification is claimed.

## Relationship to prior work
Honzik proves two facts that are crucial here but states them for different purposes: under the indicated hypotheses, Krajíček's probability algebra is isomorphic to the product random algebra \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\), and the random integer \(\mathrm{id}_G\) generates the entire set-theoretic generic extension.

Classical work on product random forcing records the complementary support fact that every individual real in an \(\omega_1\)-coordinate random extension depends on only countably many coordinates, and that disjoint coordinate sets factor as iterated random forcing.

Combining these facts yields the external-size obstruction above. Targeted literature searches for the conjunction “random integer generates the whole extension” with “no real generator,” “hereditarily countable,” or countable-coordinate support did not locate this consequence stated in the ownership source or in adjacent literature.

The point is not merely terminological. A single internal element of a nonstandard arithmetic structure can generate a forcing extension that no real, and indeed no hereditarily countable parameter, can generate. Among families of hereditarily countable parameters, the exact generating cardinal is nevertheless \(\omega_1\).

## Limitations
The conclusion is conditional on the hypotheses under which Honzik identifies \(\mathcal B_{M,\Omega}\) with \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\), including \(\mathsf{CH}\) and the stated saturation and cardinality assumptions.

The phrase “random integer” is internal to the nonstandard arithmetic construction. The theorem does not say that \(\mathrm{id}_G\) is an ordinary natural number of the ambient set-theoretic universe; the conclusion is exactly that its external set-theoretic size is not hereditarily countable.

The result does not classify the least possible hereditary cardinality of a generator beyond excluding \(H_{\omega_1}\), and it does not claim that every element of the arithmetic extension has the same external size.
