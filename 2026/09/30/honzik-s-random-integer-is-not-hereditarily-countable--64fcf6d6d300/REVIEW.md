# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the forcing representation rather than inferred from nonseparability alone. Every product-measurable subset of \(2^{\omega_1}\) depends on countably many coordinates because the countable-support sets form a \(\sigma\)-algebra containing all finite cylinders. A name for a real therefore has countable total support. A fresh coordinate outside that support supplies a random real absent from the corresponding subextension, proving that the subextension is proper. Every hereditarily countable set is real-coded via a countable well-founded extensional relation, so the same proper-subextension argument applies. Combining this with the published identity \(V[\mathrm{id}_G]=V[G]\) forces \(\mathrm{id}_G\notin H_{\omega_1}^{V[G]}\).

The proof also checks the countable-family extension by taking the union of countably many countable supports. Since the forcing is ccc, this gives the lower bound \(\omega_1\) for any generating family of hereditarily countable parameters; a ground-model partition of the forcing coordinates into \(\omega_1\) countable blocks supplies the matching upper bound by block-random reals. No step confuses internal nonstandard-arithmetic size with external hereditary cardinality.

## Originality
PASS. The ownership source proves both the \(\mathcal B_{\omega_1}\) representation and the whole-extension generation theorem for \(\mathrm{id}_G\), but does not state that the random integer lies outside \(H_{\omega_1}\), nor the stronger prohibition on all hereditarily countable generators. Targeted database and literature searches using the source title and combinations of “no real generator,” “hereditarily countable,” “countable coordinate support,” and “whole generic extension” found the source theorem and standard support facts, but no equivalent or stronger statement of this consequence.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves an apparent compression paradox in the source: one arithmetic object generates an extension equivalent to adding \(\omega_1\) random coordinates, even though every real sees only countably many coordinates. The theorem identifies the necessary reason precisely—the generator is not hereditarily countable—and computes the exact generating cardinal within \(H_{\omega_1}\): it is \(\omega_1\).

## Closest literature
Honzik's 2026 paper is the ownership source and supplies the two forcing facts being combined. Standard product-random forcing literature, including Kellner--Shelah, supplies the countable-support and coordinate-factorization facts. The searches found no prior statement of the hereditary-countability obstruction for Honzik's random integer.

## Scientific limitations
This is a structural consequence of a recent forcing analysis, not a new forcing construction. It is conditional on the hypotheses of the ownership theorem, and it gives only a lower bound on the external hereditary size of a generator.
