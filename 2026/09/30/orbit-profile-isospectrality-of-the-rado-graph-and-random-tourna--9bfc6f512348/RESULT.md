# Orbit-profile isospectrality of the Rado-graph and random-tournament reduct families
## Finding
Let \(R=(V,E)\) be the countable Rado graph and let \(T=(W,\to)\) be the countable random tournament. Match the five closed reduct groups by
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(R)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(T),\qquad
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(R),-\rangle\leftrightarrow\langle\operatorname{Aut}(T),\mathrm{rev}\rangle,
\]
\[
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(R),\mathrm{sw}\rangle\leftrightarrow\langle\operatorname{Aut}(T),\mathrm{tsw}\rangle,
\]
with the analogous correspondence for the group generated by both extra operations and for the full symmetric group.

If \(f_G(k)\) denotes the number of \(G\)-orbits on ordered injective \(k\)-tuples, then the two groups in every corresponding pair have the same value for every \(k\ge0\). Explicitly,
\[
f_{\mathrm{base}}(k)=2^{\binom{k}{2}},
\]
\[
f_{\mathrm{global}}(k)=
\begin{cases}
1,&k\le1,\\
2^{\binom{k}{2}-1},&k\ge2,
\end{cases}
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{switch}}(k)=
\begin{cases}
1,&k=0,\\
2^{\binom{k-1}{2}},&k\ge1,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
f_{\mathrm{both}}(k)=
\begin{cases}
1,&k\le2,\\
2^{\binom{k-1}{2}-1},&k\ge3,
\end{cases}
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{sym}}(k)=1.
\]
For arbitrary ordered tuples, including repetitions,
\[
o_G(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}f_G(k),
\]
so the complete ordered-tuple orbit profiles also coincide under the correspondence. Their first values for \(n=0,1,2,3,4\) are respectively
\[
(1,1,3,15,127),\ (1,1,2,8,64),\ (1,1,2,6,28),\ (1,1,2,5,18),\ (1,1,2,5,15).
\]
Despite this profile equality, \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\) are not conjugate as permutation groups: transposing the two coordinates fixes each of the two injective binary orbitals of \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\), while it exchanges the two injective binary orbitals of \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\). Thus the full numerical oligomorphic profile does not determine binary orbital pairing.

## Assumptions and scope
The random graph and random tournament are taken in their standard countable homogeneous forms. The complement operation on the graph toggles every unordered pair. Tournament reversal reverses every arc. A graph switch at a vertex toggles adjacency on all pairs incident with that vertex; a tournament switch reverses all arcs incident with that vertex. The cited classification source records the five reduct groups in both settings and these operations.

The statement concerns numerical orbit counts on ordered tuples. It does not claim that corresponding reduct structures are interdefinable, that their automorphism groups are abstractly isomorphic, or that numerical profiles determine higher orbital composition data.

## Proof
Fix \(k\) pairwise distinct tuple positions and put \(m=\binom{k}{2}\). Encode a finite induced graph by a vector in \(\mathbb F_2^m\), one bit per unordered pair, recording edge versus non-edge. Encode a finite tournament by the same vector space, fixing the lower tuple index on each unordered pair and recording whether its arc points toward the higher index. Homogeneity implies that, for either base automorphism group, two injective ordered tuples lie in the same orbit exactly when their bit vectors agree. Hence the base count is \(2^m\).

Complement and tournament reversal both translate every bit by the all-one vector \(\mathbf 1\). When \(m>0\) this is a free involution, giving \(2^{m-1}\) orbits; for \(k\le1\) there is one orbit.

For switching, let \(s_i\in\mathbb F_2^m\) be the star vector consisting of the pairs incident with tuple position \(i\). Conjugating a fixed switch by base automorphisms realizes switching at every tuple coordinate, so the finite pattern action is translation by
\[
C_k=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_k\rangle.
\]
For \(k\ge1\), \(C_k\) is the cut space of the complete graph and has dimension \(k-1\): the sum of all star vectors is zero, and any \(k-1\) of them are independent. Therefore the switching quotient has
\[
2^{m-(k-1)}=2^{\binom{k-1}{2}}
\]
orbits. The case \(k=0\) again has one orbit.

For the group generated by both operations, quotient by \(C_k+\langle\mathbf1\rangle\). If \(k\ge3\), the all-one vector is not a cut: every bipartition of at least three vertices has two vertices on the same side, whose pair is absent from the cut. Thus this space has dimension \(k\), giving
\[
2^{m-k}=2^{\binom{k-1}{2}-1}.
\]
For \(k\le2\), the global toggle lies already in the cut space, and there is one orbit. The full symmetric group is injectively \(k\)-transitive, so its injective count is one.

Passing from injective tuples to arbitrary ordered tuples uses equality patterns. Every permutation preserves the set partition of coordinate positions into equal-value blocks. For each partition into \(k\) blocks, the remaining orbit data is exactly an injective \(k\)-tuple orbit. Since there are \({n\brace k}\) such partitions, the Stirling transform follows.

Finally, conjugacy of permutation groups preserves the involution on binary orbitals induced by swapping the two coordinates. In the undirected random graph, the edge and non-edge orbitals are each self-paired. In the tournament, the two orientation orbitals are exchanged. Hence the two base automorphism groups are not conjugate even though all their numerical tuple-orbit counts agree.

## Relationship to prior work
Bodirsky, Pinsker, and Pongrácz record both the random-graph reduct operations and the random-tournament reversal/switching operations as subclassifications inside their reduct catalog. Their paper classifies the reduct groups; it does not state the orbit-profile formulas above or the cross-family profile-isospectrality conclusion. The calculation here isolates the common affine \(\mathbb F_2\) action on finite pair patterns and records the resulting numerical non-reconstruction phenomenon.

## Limitations
The argument compares numerical orbit profiles, not full orbit algebras or canonical relational structures. The non-conjugacy certificate is asserted for the two base automorphism groups from their binary orbital pairing; no corresponding non-conjugacy theorem is claimed here for every matched higher reduct group. Originality is best-of-knowledge from targeted comparison with the cited classification and current literature; independent audit, proof-assistant verification, and expert attestation have not been performed.
