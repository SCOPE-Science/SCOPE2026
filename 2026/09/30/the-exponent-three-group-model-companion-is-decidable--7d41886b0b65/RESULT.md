# The exponent-three group model companion is decidable
## Finding
Let \(T_3\) be the first-order theory of groups satisfying
\[
\forall x\,(x^3=1),
\]
and let \(T_3^*\) be the model companion whose existence is proved by Ishida, Mizuno, and Takeuchi.

Then \(T_3^*\) is a complete decidable first-order theory. In fact, the proof of existence yields a computably enumerable axiom scheme for \(T_3^*\).

For a finite exponent-three group \(B\), write \(m=d(B)\) for its minimum number of generators and set
\[
t(r)=r+\binom{r}{2}+\binom{r}{3},
\qquad
n(m)=15\bigl((3m+4)t(m)+1\bigr)^2.
\]
For every finite inclusion \(A\leq B\), the finite obstruction set \(K(A,B)\) occurring in the model-companion axioms can be computed effectively. Consequently theoremhood in \(T_3^*\) is decidable.

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are in the usual finite group language. “Exponent three” means exponent dividing \(3\), exactly as in the source.

The source proves that \(T_3\) has a model companion by establishing a uniform finite bound on witnesses to non-amalgamation. Its Theorem 3.3 gives
\[
f(m)=f_0\bigl((3m+4)t(m)+1\bigr),
\]
and Proposition 4.13 permits the explicit choice
\[
f_0(q)=15q^2.
\]
Thus \(n(m)=f(m)\) is a valid computable bound.

The source also recalls the classical formula
\[
|B(r,3)|=3^{t(r)}
\]
for the free Burnside group of rank \(r\) and exponent \(3\). Hence every \(r\)-generated exponent-three group is finite of size at most \(3^{t(r)}\).

The conclusion is about the complete first-order theory \(T_3^*\). No claim is made that its decision procedure is computationally efficient.

## Proof
We first make the source axiom scheme effective.

Fact 2.6 of the source axiomatizes the existentially closed models as follows. For every finite inclusion \(A\leq B\), choose a bound \(n_{A,B}\) such that whenever an existentially closed model \(M\supseteq A\) fails to amalgamate with \(B\) over \(A\), there is a finite \(C\) with
\[
A\leq C\leq M,\qquad d(C)\leq n_{A,B},
\]
such that \(C\) also fails to amalgamate with \(B\) over \(A\). Let \(K(A,B)\) consist of the finitely many isomorphism types over \(A\) of such \(C\). The associated first-order axiom says, informally, that a copy of \(A\) extends to a copy of \(B\) unless it already extends to one of the finite obstructions in \(K(A,B)\).

For \(T_3\), if \(m=d(B)\), Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 4.13 allow
\[
n_{A,B}=n(m)=15\bigl((3m+4)t(m)+1\bigr)^2.
\]
This bound depends effectively only on \(B\).

Now fix finite \(A\leq B\). The finite groups \(C\supseteq A\) with \(d(C)\leq n(m)\) can be exhaustively enumerated up to isomorphism over \(A\). Indeed, every such \(C\) is a quotient of \(B(n(m),3)\), so
\[
|C|\leq 3^{t(n(m))}.
\]
One may therefore enumerate all multiplication tables up to that finite size bound, retain exactly the exponent-three groups, test which contain the prescribed copy of \(A\), and test finite generation by at most \(n(m)\) elements.

It remains to decide whether such a finite \(C\) amalgamates with \(B\) over \(A\). If any exponent-three amalgam exists, take the subgroup generated by the images of \(B\) and \(C\). This is still an amalgam because the two original maps remain injective. It is generated by at most
\[
m+n(m)
\]
elements, and hence has size at most
\[
3^{t(m+n(m))}.
\]
Thus amalgamation is decidable by a finite exhaustive search through exponent-three group tables up to this bound and all pairs of injective homomorphisms from \(B\) and \(C\) that agree on \(A\).

Therefore the exact finite set \(K(A,B)\) is computable. Finite exponent-three groups and finite inclusions between them are themselves effectively enumerable, so the source's Fact 2.6 scheme gives a computably enumerable axiomatization of \(T_3^*\).

Next, \(T_3^*\) is complete. The base theory \(T_3\) has the joint embedding property: for any \(G,H\models T_3\), the direct product \(G\times H\) is again a model of \(T_3\) and contains copies of both factors.

Let \(M,N\models T_3^*\). Embed both into \(M\times N\models T_3\). Because \(T_3^*\) is a companion of \(T_3\), this common \(T_3\)-extension embeds into a model \(U\models T_3^*\). Since \(T_3^*\) is model complete, the embeddings
\[
M\longrightarrow U
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N\longrightarrow U
\]
are elementary. Hence \(M\) and \(N\) satisfy the same sentences. Thus \(T_3^*\) is complete.

Finally, a consistent complete computably enumerable theory is decidable. Given any group-language sentence \(\varphi\), enumerate formal proofs from the effective axiomatization of \(T_3^*\) in parallel until either a proof of \(\varphi\) or a proof of \(\neg\varphi\) appears. Completeness guarantees that exactly one of these outcomes occurs. This is a terminating decision procedure.

## Verification
The proof was checked at three potentially delicate points.

First, the numerical witness bound is genuinely effective: combining Theorem 3.3 with Proposition 4.13 gives the displayed \(n(m)\), rather than merely asserting the existence of an unspecified finite bound.

Second, finite amalgamability is decidable here because local finiteness comes with an explicit free-object size bound. An arbitrary amalgam can be replaced by the subgroup generated by the two finite images, so searching only up to \(3^{t(m+n(m))}\) is exhaustive, not merely a search for small examples.

Third, completeness does not follow from model companionship alone. The required extra hypothesis is joint embedding of \(T_3\), supplied here by direct products. Once a common \(T_3^*\)-extension is obtained, model completeness makes the two embeddings elementary.

Edge cases cause no difficulty. If \(B\) is trivial, the same effective bounds remain finite, and the direct-product joint embedding argument also includes the trivial model.

## Relationship to prior work
Ishida, Mizuno, and Takeuchi prove the existence of the model companion \(T_3^*\) and provide the bounded non-amalgamation machinery and explicit generator bounds used above. Their paper does not state a decidability theorem, and its text contains no discussion of decidability.

Lipparini's 1982 work gives a general characterization of locally finite theories admitting model companions in terms of amalgamability conditions. Burris's 1984 theorem gives primitive-recursive model companions for the more restrictive setting of finitely generated universal Horn classes in a finite operational language. Burris's 1989 decidable-model-companion criterion gives another general effectiveness route under hypotheses controlling primitive formulas and the universal-existential theory. These are important general precedents, but none states the exponent-three conclusion derived here from the new explicit obstruction bounds.

Earlier group-specific work cited by Ishida, Mizuno, and Takeuchi gives stronger classification in some lower-nilpotency settings; for example, Maier obtained an \(\aleph_0\)-categorical model companion for prime exponent \(p\) and nilpotency class \(c<p\). Those results do not cover the full exponent-three variety because exponent-three groups can have nilpotency class \(3\).

Targeted searches for decidability, recursive axiomatization, effective model companionship, locally finite model companions, joint embedding, and existentially closed exponent-three groups located the 2026 existence theorem and the older general results above, but no equivalent or stronger statement of the specific conclusion proved here.

The additional content is therefore an algorithmic extraction from the source proof: the explicit finite witness bounds make its obstruction scheme effective for the full exponent-three variety, while joint embedding upgrades model completeness to completeness.

## Limitations
The decision procedure is only an existence result in computability terms. The explicit Burnside bounds and exhaustive finite-table searches are enormous, and no practical complexity upper bound is claimed.

The proof uses the special local finiteness and explicit free Burnside size formula for exponent \(3\). It does not automatically extend to exponents for which finitely generated Burnside groups are infinite, or even to other locally finite varieties without effective bounds and decidable finite amalgamability.

No quantifier elimination, model classification, stability property, finite axiomatization, or \(\aleph_0\)-categoricity claim is made for \(T_3^*\).
