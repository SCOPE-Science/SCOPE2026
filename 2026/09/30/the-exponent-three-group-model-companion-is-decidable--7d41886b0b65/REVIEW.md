# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source's quantitative statements were combined without changing their hypotheses. Theorem 3.3 reduces non-amalgamation to a boundedly generated finite witness, and Proposition 4.13 gives the explicit quadratic strict-envelope bound, yielding
\[
n(m)=15\bigl((3m+4)t(m)+1\bigr)^2.
\]
The classical free Burnside formula quoted in the source bounds every \(r\)-generated exponent-three group by \(3^{t(r)}\). This makes both the obstruction list and finite amalgamability decidable by exhaustive finite search. The completeness argument was checked independently: \(T_3\) has joint embedding via direct products, and a common extension to a model of the model companion makes the embeddings elementary by model completeness. Completeness plus computable enumerability gives a terminating proof-search decision procedure.

The polarity of amalgamation was checked carefully: \(K(A,B)\) contains precisely bounded finite \(C\) that fail to amalgamate with \(B\) over \(A\). The finite search is exhaustive because any amalgam contains the subgroup generated by the two finite images, with the stated generator and cardinality bounds.

## Originality
PASS. The ownership source proves existence of the model companion and supplies the explicit bounded-witness machinery, but does not state decidability or an effective axiomatization theorem. Targeted searches using the exact source title together with “decidable,” “recursive axiomatization,” “complete theory,” “existentially closed groups of exponent three,” and general locally finite model-companion terminology found no equivalent or stronger published statement of this exponent-three conclusion. The search did locate Lipparini's general 1982 characterization of locally finite theories with model companions, Burris's 1984 primitive-recursive result for finitely generated universal Horn classes, and Burris's 1989 decidable-model-companion criterion under recursive primitive-formula and universal-existential hypotheses. Those are the closest general precedents. None was found to state the exponent-three application, and the 2026 source supplies the explicit bounded non-amalgamation data needed for the direct finite-search proof above. Searches of nearby recent mathematical-logic findings likewise found no overlap.

## Value
PASS. Decidability is a substantive algorithmic strengthening of bare model-companion existence. The result identifies why the new existence proof is effective: its finite non-amalgamation witnesses come with an explicit computable bound, and exponent-three local finiteness converts that bound into a finite search. The conclusion gives a uniform decision procedure for every first-order sentence about existentially closed exponent-three groups.

## Closest literature
Ishida, Mizuno, and Takeuchi's 2026 paper is the direct ownership source. It proves the model companion and the explicit finite witness bounds. Lipparini (1982) provides a broad amalgamability characterization for locally finite theories with model companions, while Burris (1984) proves primitive-recursive model-companion results under the narrower hypothesis of a finitely generated universal Horn class. The earlier prime-exponent, lower-nilpotency group results summarized by the 2026 source include \(\aleph_0\)-categorical model companions, but they exclude the class-\(3\) exponent-\(3\) situation handled by the new paper.

## Scientific limitations
The procedure is not claimed to be feasible: the free Burnside bounds and finite-table searches are extremely large. The argument does not supply quantifier elimination, a finite axiom set, stability, or \(\aleph_0\)-categoricity, and it should not be generalized to other exponents without new effective local-finiteness and amalgamation input.
