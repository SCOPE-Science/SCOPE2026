# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof separates a published global premise from new local calculations. The rank-at-most-two scheme is obtained by specializing the published decomposition; each of the three quadratic pieces acquires one double root because its discriminant is exactly \(9\rho^2-14\rho+9\). The checker confirms the support points and exact local jets. The rank-three exhaustion uses the adjugate formula and symmetry of the third derivative tensor, reducing any further Hessian-quartic singularity to a singular point of the cubic. The three resulting incidence systems each have a unique projective solution. At the three collision points the transverse quadratic form is nondegenerate and the reduced kernel-direction germ starts in degree four with coefficient \(8\rho\ne0\), hence type \(A_3\). The other seven local quadratic forms are nondegenerate, hence type \(A_1\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:1909.12538, explicitly identifies the same exceptional parameter condition and says the ten Hessian rank-drop points are no longer distinct, but does not compute the special-fiber scheme or the Hessian quartic's analytic singularities. The inspected cubic-Hessian and non-Sylvester K3 literature does not provide the \(7A_1+3A_3\) specialization. Targeted published-record comparisons found no implication-equivalent record. The remaining risk is that an older classical treatment may encode the same basket in different coordinates.

## Value
PASS. The parameter condition is not an arbitrary slice: it is exactly the distinguished collision locus already singled out by the Hessian-discriminant geometry. Determining that the length-ten rank-drop scheme becomes four reduced points plus three curvilinear double points, and that the quartic acquires exactly three \(A_3\) points plus seven \(A_1\) points, gives a compact geometric description of that degeneration and supplies natural input for resolution and discriminant studies.
