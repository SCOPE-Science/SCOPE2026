# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The projection coordinates are obtained by orthogonal projection onto the two equal sides and the base. From those coordinates, the pedal side lengths and area give the displayed formulas for \(R_p(y)\) and \(r_p(y)\). At \(y=1/100\), the reversal reduces to the exact radical inequality
\[
398\sqrt2\,(\sqrt{50005}-199)>13745.
\]
The rational bounds \(\sqrt2>140/99\) and \(\sqrt{50005}>1118/5\) make the left side strictly larger than \(6853560/495\), which exceeds \(13745\). No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof. Continuity of the explicit formulas proves the additional boundary-layer statement.

## Originality
PASS relative to the inspected sources and indexed searches. The 2011/2013 Liu source states the opposite universal linear inequality as a conjecture. Huang's 2018 full-text paper says it proves Liu's conjectures corresponding to \(PO\ge|R-2R_p|\) and the product inequality involving \(R_1R_2R_3/(r_1r_2r_3)\); those statements neither imply nor resolve the linear \(R_p+\sqrt2\,r_p<R\) conjecture. Exact-formula, alias, and published-finding searches did not locate a covering disproof. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased prior work.

## Value
PASS. A published universal geometric conjecture is directly refuted by an exact, strictly interior counterexample. The proof also identifies a geometric mechanism rather than an isolated numerical failure: a fixed isosceles triangle contains an open interval of counterexamples approaching a side midpoint. This gives a reusable boundary test for any attempted replacement inequality involving the same two pedal radii.

The present result does not optimize a replacement coefficient and does not classify the complete set of counterexamples.
