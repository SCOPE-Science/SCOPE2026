# A complete census of the shortest LCD embeddings in Example 7.5
## Finding

Let
\[
R=\mathbb F_2+u\mathbb F_2,\qquad u^2=0,
\]
and let \(\mathcal C\subset R^8\) be the free rank-\(3\) code from Example 7.5 of An–Kim with generator
\[
G=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0&1&u&u&0&1\\
0&1&0&1+u&1&1+u&1+u&1\\
0&0&1&1+u&1+u&0&u&u
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Using the matrix
\[
U=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}
\]
printed in that example, Theorem 6.3 parametrizes every shortest LCD embedding by
\[
\widetilde G=[G\mid B],\qquad
B=U^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}D\\E\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \(D\in \operatorname{GL}_2(R)\) and \(E\in R^{1\times2}\).

There are exactly
\[
|\operatorname{GL}_2(R)|\,|R|^2=96\cdot16=1536
\]
such embeddings. Exhausting all of them gives the exact census
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d_R&d_{\rm Gray}&\text{number}\\
\hline
3&6&768\\
4&7&720\\
4&8&48.
\end{array}
\]

Every Gray image is a binary LCD \([20,6]\) code. The \(48\) distance-\(8\) Gray images split into two weight-enumerator types:
\[
W_A(z)=1+11z^8+18z^9+11z^{10}+12z^{11}+3z^{12}+2z^{13}+5z^{14}+z^{16},
\]
occurring \(32\) times, and
\[
W_B(z)=1+12z^8+18z^9+9z^{10}+12z^{11}+3z^{12}+2z^{13}+7z^{14},
\]
occurring \(16\) times.

The embedding displayed in Example 7.5 has weight enumerator \(W_B\). Since weight enumerators are invariant under coordinate permutations, the optimal Gray images arising from the shortest-embedding family include at least two permutation-inequivalent binary codes.

## Assumptions and scope

The census fixes the source generator \(G\), the coordinate order, and the matrix \(U\) displayed in Example 7.5. It treats two embeddings as distinct when the resulting labeled submodules of \(R^{10}\) are distinct.

Because the first three columns of \(G\) form \(I_3\), each codeword is uniquely determined by its first three coordinates. Consequently two matrices \(B\neq B'\) cannot generate the same labeled embedded code while the first eight coordinates remain fixed.

The Gray map is the source map
\[
\phi(a+ub)=(b,a+b),
\]
applied coordinatewise from \(R^{10}\) to \(\mathbb F_2^{20}\).

## Proof

Reduction modulo \(u\) maps \(\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\) onto \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\). Every invertible binary \(2\times2\) matrix has \(2^4\) lifts obtained by choosing the four \(u\)-coefficients arbitrarily. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{GL}_2(R)|=
|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)|\,2^4
=(4-1)(4-2)\cdot16
=96.
\]
There are \(4^2=16\) choices for \(E\), giving exactly \(1536\) matrices \(B\).

For each such \(B\), the verifier enumerates all \(4^3=64\) codewords of the free rank-\(3\) code generated by \([G\mid B]\). This determines its exact ring Hamming distance. Applying \(\phi\) to the same \(64\) words gives the exact binary Gray weight distribution and hence the Gray-image minimum distance.

The binary Gray image is generated over \(\mathbb F_2\) by the Gray images of the three ring rows and their \(u\)-multiples. For every one of the \(1536\) embeddings, this binary generator has rank \(6\), and its \(6\times6\) Gram matrix has rank \(6\). Therefore every Gray image is LCD.

The complete enumeration yields \(768\), \(720\), and \(48\) embeddings in the three distance cells displayed above. Among the \(48\) distance-\(8\) images, exactly two weight distributions occur, with multiplicities \(32\) and \(16\). Direct evaluation of the source matrix
\[
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
u&1+u\\
1+u&u
\end{bmatrix}
\]
gives \(W_B(z)\).

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs \(R\), \(G\), \(U\), all \(96\) matrices in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(R)\), and all \(16\) possible rows \(E\). It forms every shortest embedding from Theorem 6.3, checks that all \(1536\) matrices \(B\) are distinct, enumerates all \(64\) ring codewords for each embedding, computes the Gray images, and verifies the binary rank and LCD condition.

It independently checks the source-displayed \(B\), the \(768/720/48\) distance census, and the two optimal weight enumerators with multiplicities \(32\) and \(16\). Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

An and Kim prove that the shortest LCD embedding length for this code is \(10\), characterize all shortest embeddings by Theorem 6.3, and state that they enumerated all shortest LCD embeddings in their examples and displayed one having the largest ring minimum distance. For Example 7.5 they report ring minimum distance \(4\) for the displayed embedding and an optimal binary LCD Gray image with parameters \([20,6,8]\).

The present result resolves the full finite landscape behind that existential optimum statement: it counts every shortest embedding by both ring and Gray distance, proves that all Gray images are binary LCD, and identifies two distinct optimal Gray-image weight spectra. Grassl's table independently confirms that the maximum possible binary minimum distance for parameters \([20,6]\) is \(8\), but it does not classify the ring embeddings or these two spectra.

The classical classification of optimal binary self-orthogonal \([20,6,8]\) codes does not cover the present optimal images: its listed weight enumerators contain only even weights, whereas both \(W_A\) and \(W_B\) contain weight-\(9\) and weight-\(11\) words and are LCD rather than self-orthogonal.

## Limitations

The count \(1536\) is for the labeled shortest embeddings arising from the fixed source \(G\) and the displayed \(U\). The result does not quotient the \(1536\) embeddings by all coordinate permutations or ring-code equivalences.

The two distinct weight enumerators prove at least two permutation-inequivalent optimal binary Gray images, but no claim is made that each weight-enumerator class is a single permutation-equivalence class.

The census is finite and exhaustive for this example only. It does not assert analogous multiplicity formulas for shortest LCD embeddings of arbitrary codes over \(\mathbb F_q+u\mathbb F_q\).
