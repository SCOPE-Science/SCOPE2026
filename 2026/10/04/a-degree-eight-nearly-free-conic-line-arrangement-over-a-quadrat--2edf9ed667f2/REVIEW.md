# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The six-line incidence structure is explicit, the conic determinant is \(-32\), the three coordinate restrictions are exact squares giving three tangencies, and the remaining conic intersections are the three pairwise intersections of the noncoordinate lines. This exhausts the component intersections by Bézout and gives \((n_2,t,n_3)=(3,3,6)\) and \(\tau=36\). The exact degree-four Jacobian map has rank \(42\) on \(45\) columns, while the published lower bound gives \(\operatorname{mdr}\ge4\); hence \(\operatorname{mdr}=4\). Gałecka's stated criterion then gives near freeness.

## Originality
PASS. The lead paper explicitly leaves degrees \(8\) and \(9\) undecided. Searches using the exact degree, singularity types, weak combinatorics, and quadratic parameter did not locate a prior degree-eight construction of this type. The closest later construction paper concerns free curves obtained by adding special lines and has different singularity types; recent plus-one generated work concerns conic arrangements and a different classification problem. Residual risk remains that an unindexed or differently phrased source contains an equivalent example.

## Value
PASS. The result closes one of the two explicit low-degree existence gaps stated by the source, with a compact exact construction and a reproducible syzygy certificate. It is therefore a direct answer to a motivated classification question rather than an arbitrary parameter computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Gałecka's 2022 paper is the direct predecessor and supplies both the open degree-eight question and the criteria used in the proof. Dimca–Ilardi–Pokora–Sticlaru give broader line-addition constructions but not this claim. The result does not settle degree nine or uniqueness in degree eight.
