# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Johnson–Kollár’s general quasismoothness criterion reduces sharply because every subset containing a weight-one coordinate is automatically handled. At the \(a\)-vertex, the distinguished coordinate can have weight \(a,b,1\), giving exactly divisibility by \(r+d,r,r+d-1\); the \(b\)-vertex gives \(r-d,r,r-d-1\). The two-heavy subset introduces no additional condition once the singleton conditions hold; in the distinguished weight-one case the hypothesis \(r\ge2\) supplies distinct unit coordinates for the injection. The Reid–Tai chart calculation gives the canonical triangle \(0\le d\le r\), \(a\le r+d\). Counting only the three possible \(a\)-divisibility branches gives \((r+1)\tau(r)+\sum_{r}^{2r}\tau(n)+\sum_{r-1}^{2r-1}\tau(n)\); \(\tau(n)\le2\sqrt n\) yields the claimed bound and vanishing density. Fresh finite checks for \(2\le r\le11\) matched the quasismooth divisibility criterion against the injection condition, and for \(2\le r\le14\) matched the canonical-strip formula against direct Reid–Tai inequalities; these finite tests are supporting evidence only.

## originality

PASS

The Johnson–Kollár full text gives the general monomial/injection quasismoothness criterion, not the two-heavy arithmetic collapse or the asymptotic density theorem. Published SCOPE already contains the canonical/terminal triangular classification, so that strip characterization is treated here as prior input rather than novelty. Published SCOPE also covers the repeated-heavy-weight slice and the canonical non-terminal boundary. Those slices do not imply the simultaneous all-\((a,b)\) three-way divisibility criterion or the \(O(r^{3/2})\) global count over the full canonical triangle. The surviving novelty is therefore the complete two-heavy quasismooth classifier plus sparsity, not the canonical triangle itself.

## value

PASS

Quasismoothness is the central existence condition for weighted anticanonical sections, and this family has a natural two-parameter canonical region of quadratic size. Collapsing the general injection criterion to two explicit divisibility tests gives a usable complete classification, while the density-zero theorem shows a genuine asymptotic sparsity phenomenon. The result is structurally motivated and not a recomputation of a known finite list.
