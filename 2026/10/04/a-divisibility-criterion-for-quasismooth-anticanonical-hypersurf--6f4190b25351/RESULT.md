# A divisibility criterion for quasismooth anticanonical hypersurfaces in two-heavy-weight projective spaces
## Finding
For every integer \(r\ge 2\) and weights \(1\le a\le b\), put \(d=b-a\) and \(X=\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\). A general anticanonical hypersurface of weighted degree \(D=r+a+b\) is quasismooth if and only if \(a\) divides at least one of \(r\), \(r+d\), \(r+d-1\), and simultaneously \(b\) divides at least one of \(r\), \(r-d\), \(r-d-1\). If one restricts to the canonical ambient spaces, equivalently \(0\le d\le r\) and \(1\le a\le r+d\), and writes \(Q(r)\) for the number admitting a quasismooth anticanonical hypersurface, then \[Q(r)\le (r+1)\tau(r)+\sum_{n=r}^{2r}\tau(n)+\sum_{n=r-1}^{2r-1}\tau(n)\le 2(1+2\sqrt2)(r+1)\sqrt r.\] Since the canonical strip contains exactly \(3r(r+1)/2\) weight pairs, its quasismooth proportion is at most \(4(1+2\sqrt2)/(3\sqrt r)\), and in particular tends to zero as \(r\to\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For integers \(r\ge 2\) and \(1\le a\le b\), set \(d=b-a\), \(X=\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\), and \(D=r+a+b\). The hypersurface is a general member of \(|\mathcal O_X(D)|\). Quasismooth means that the affine cone is smooth away from its vertex. The asymptotic statement concerns only those ambient weighted projective spaces having at worst canonical singularities.

## Proof
Johnson--Kollár record the general quasismoothness criterion in terms of every coordinate subset: for each subset \(I\), one needs an injection of the indices in \(I\) to coordinate indices giving degree-\(D\) monomials, equivalently the Fletcher formulation allowing a degree-\(D\) monomial supported on \(I\).

In \(\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\), every subset containing a weight-one coordinate automatically satisfies the Fletcher condition, because \(x^D\) is a degree-\(D\) monomial supported on that subset. Thus only \(I=\{a\}\), \(I=\{b\}\), and \(I=\{a,b\}\) remain.

For \(I=\{a\}\), a degree-\(D\) monomial of the form required by the criterion can use, as its distinguished coordinate, a weight \(a\), a weight \(b\), or a weight \(1\). These three possibilities are respectively
\[a\mid D,\qquad a\mid D-b,\qquad a\mid D-1.\]
Since \(D=r+2a+d\) and \(b=a+d\), they reduce to
\[a\mid r+d,\qquad a\mid r,\qquad a\mid r+d-1.\]
The identical argument at the \(b\)-vertex gives
\[b\mid D,\qquad b\mid D-a,\qquad b\mid D-1,\]
which, after subtracting suitable multiples of \(b\), is exactly
\[b\mid r-d,\qquad b\mid r,\qquad b\mid r-d-1.\]
Finally, the two-element subset \(\{a,b\}\) adds no condition: whenever the \(b\)-vertex condition is met, either \(D\) itself is in the semigroup generated by \(a,b\), or \(D-1\) is; in the latter case the two distinct weight-one coordinates available because \(r\ge2\) provide the required injection. This proves the divisibility criterion.

For the canonical strip, a direct Reid--Tai check on the two heavy charts gives the stated inequalities. At the \(b\)-chart the quotient is \(\frac1b(1^r,a)\) with \(a=b-d\); its age at the \(k\)-th element is \((rk+(ka\bmod b))/b\). If \(d\le r\), writing \(a\equiv-d\pmod b\) shows every age is at least one, while \(k=1\) fails when \(d>r\). At the \(a\)-chart, after imposing \(d\le r\), the same argument with \(d\bmod a\) gives canonicity exactly when \(a\le r+d\). Hence the canonical pairs are precisely \(0\le d\le r\), \(1\le a\le r+d\), totaling
\[\sum_{d=0}^{r}(r+d)=\frac{3r(r+1)}2.\]

For each fixed \(d\), the first divisibility condition leaves at most \(\tau(r)+\tau(r+d)+\tau(r+d-1)\) choices for \(a\). Summing over \(0\le d\le r\) gives the first displayed upper bound for \(Q(r)\). The elementary estimate \(\tau(n)\le2\sqrt n\) gives the second bound. Dividing by \(3r(r+1)/2\) yields the stated \(O(r^{-1/2})\) density bound.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_qs_divisibility.py` checks the closed divisibility criterion against an exhaustive implementation of the Johnson--Kollár/Fletcher injection criterion for all \(2\le r\le30\) and \(1\le a\le b\le3r\), including noncanonical weights. It separately checks the canonical-strip formula by direct local Reid--Tai ages for all \(2\le r\le60\) and \(1\le a\le b\le4r\), and verifies the counting bound for every \(2\le r\le200\). The replay ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Johnson--Kollár give the general coordinate-subset criterion for quasismooth weighted hypersurfaces and emphasize its use in classification searches. Kasprzyk gives general canonical/terminal criteria for weighted projective spaces. The present statement specializes those tools to the two-heavy-weight family and collapses the full quasismoothness test to two elementary three-way divisibility conditions, then derives a uniform sparsity theorem inside the canonical region. Searches of the cited primary literature, web literature, published-finding corpus, and the available local ledger did not locate this closed criterion or its density consequence. Two earlier local-ledger results treat only strict subfamilies: the canonical non-terminal boundary and one terminal layer; neither implies the all-weight criterion or the global canonical-strip density estimate.

## Limitations
The density estimate is an upper bound, not an asymptotic formula for \(Q(r)\). The finite checker is verification of the symbolic proof, not a replacement for it. Literature search cannot exclude an equivalent specialization hidden under different notation, so residual originality risk remains.
