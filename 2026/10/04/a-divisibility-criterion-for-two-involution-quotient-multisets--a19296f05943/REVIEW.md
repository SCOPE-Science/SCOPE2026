# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the ambient statement to a base theorem in \(H=\langle s,t\rangle\) using the published subgroup-support theorem. The base theorem is proved from the cycle-tiling criterion: simple product-one words over two involutions are only \((s,s)\), \((t,t)\), or an alternating word of length \(2n\); two-point tiles form a perfect matching of the alternating Cayley cycle \(C_{2n}\), whose two perfect matchings are monochromatic in the generator coloring. The converse constructions are explicit. The finite checker independently exhausts all supported labelings for \(3\le n\le10\) and agrees with the theorem. Risk: the proof relies on the cited cycle-tiling and subgroup-support theorems; their statements were inspected in the primary full text.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source proves only the \(S_3\) multiplicity set \(\{0,3,6\}\) and asks for explicit dihedral tests. The closest later paper proves the \(D_8\) multiplicity set \(\{0,4,8\}\) but explicitly says it treats no group of order larger than \(8\) and makes no claim on Problem 7.7. Searches for the all-\(n\) two-involution criterion and its ambient divisibility form found no covering statement. Risk: negative literature searches are not a proof of novelty, and unindexed or unpublished work may exist.

## Value
PASS. The theorem converts a general cycle-tiling criterion into a closed divisibility test for a natural infinite family singled out by the source's Problem 7.7. It simultaneously generalizes the known \(S_3\) and \(D_8\) two-letter lemmas and subsumes the multiplicity obstruction used in Aliabadi's infinite-family theorem. The result is structurally motivated by the Cayley-cycle geometry rather than an arbitrary finite slice. Limitation: it handles only two-reflection support, not all dihedral multisets.
