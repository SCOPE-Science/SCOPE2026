# A finite-cyclic three-term majorant reversal exactly from order \(5\) onward

## Finding
For every integer \(L\ge 1\), put \(\omega_L=e^{2\pi i/L}\) and
\[
\Delta_L=\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\left(\left|1+\omega_L^j-\omega_L^{3j}\right|^3-\left|1+\omega_L^j+\omega_L^{3j}\right|^3\right).
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_L<0\quad(1\le L\le4),\qquad \Delta_L>0\quad(L\ge5).
\]
Moreover,
\[
\Delta_L>0.0237\qquad(L\ge5).
\]
Thus the classical signed trinomial \(1+z-z^3\) first beats its coefficientwise idempotent majorant \(1+z+z^3\), in normalized third moment on the complete set of \(L\)-th roots of unity, exactly at order \(L=5\), and it continues to do so for every larger order.

Equivalently, if \(G\) is a finite cyclic group and \(\chi\) is a character of order \(L\), then normalized counting measure gives
\[
\|1+\chi-\chi^3\|_3^3-\|1+\chi+\chi^3\|_3^3=\Delta_L.
\]
Hence the finite-cyclic Hardy--Littlewood majorant inequality is reversed by this three-character pair for every character order \(L\ge5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The group is finite cyclic and the measure is normalized counting measure. The statement depends only on the order \(L\) of the character, because quotienting by the kernel of \(\chi\) replaces the group average by the uniform average over all \(L\)-th roots of unity. No assertion is made here for noncyclic finite groups beyond cyclic subquotients generated by one character.

The low orders have exact values
\[
\Delta_1=-26,\qquad
\Delta_2=-13,\qquad
\Delta_3=-8-2\sqrt3,
\]
and
\[
\Delta_4=\frac{5\sqrt5-14}2<0.
\]

## Proof
For \(t\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
P_\pm(t)=1+e^{it}\pm e^{3it},\qquad
H_\pm(t)=|P_\pm(t)|^3,
\]
and
\[
D(t)=H_-(t)-H_+(t),\qquad
I=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}D(t)\,dt.
\]
For either choice of sign,
\[
|P_\pm(t)|\le3,\qquad |P_\pm'(t)|\le4,\qquad |P_\pm''(t)|\le10.
\]
Write \(r=|P_\pm|\). Away from zeros of \(P_\pm\), differentiation gives
\[
H_\pm''=3r(r')^2+3r|P_\pm'|^2+3r\operatorname{Re}(P_\pm''\overline{P_\pm}).
\]
Since \(|r'|\le|P_\pm'|\),
\[
|H_\pm''|\le6r|P_\pm'|^2+3r^2|P_\pm''|\le558.
\]
The map \((x,y)\mapsto(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}\) is \(C^2\), so the same bound extends through any zero. Consequently
\[
\|D''\|_1\le2232\pi.
\]

With Fourier coefficients normalized by
\[
\widehat D(n)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}D(t)e^{-int}\,dt,
\]
two integrations by parts yield, for \(n\ne0\),
\[
|\widehat D(n)|\le\frac{\|D''\|_1}{2\pi n^2}.
\]
The Fourier series is therefore absolutely summable. Averaging it over the \(L\)-th roots of unity gives the exact aliasing identity
\[
\Delta_L-I=\sum_{m\ne0}\widehat D(mL),
\]
and hence
\[
|\Delta_L-I|\le\frac{\pi\|D''\|_1}{6L^2}
\le\frac{372\pi^2}{L^2}.
\]

A directed interval computation at order \(10000\) gives
\[
0.115540979579483608345382687211526474116269209806
<\Delta_{10000}
<0.115540979579483608345382687211526474116269209820.
\]
Applying the preceding aliasing bound once more, now between \(\Delta_{10000}\) and \(I\), gives
\[
I>0.1155042646511115559313206229050069.
\]
Therefore, for every \(L\ge200\),
\[
\Delta_L>I-\frac{372\pi^2}{L^2}
>0.0237169437209805207761598566.
\]
For the remaining finite range \(5\le L\le199\), outward-rounded interval evaluation of the defining root-of-unity sum gives a smallest certified lower endpoint at \(L=10\):
\[
\Delta_{10}>0.1055728090000841214363305325074895.
\]
Thus \(\Delta_L>0.0237\) for every \(L\ge5\). The exact values displayed above settle \(1\le L\le4\), completing the sign classification.

## Verification
The accompanying checker evaluates the finite sums with outward-rounded interval arithmetic, verifies the \(10000\)-point enclosure, checks every order \(5\le L\le199\), and combines those values with the analytic aliasing constant \(372\pi^2\) to certify the tail \(L\ge200\). It also verifies the exact low-order formulas numerically at high precision. The infinite assertion does not come from enumeration: the passage from finitely many checked orders to all larger orders is the proved \(L^-2\) aliasing estimate.

## Relationship to prior work
Hardy and Littlewood's classical \(p=3\) counterexample on the circle uses the continuous pair \(1+e_1\pm e_3\). Krenedits proved the continuous three-term inequality throughout \(2<p<4\), and in discussing Mockenhaupt's 1996 thesis explicitly reports a related "discrete and uniform" inequality for the same pair whose lower bound was supported by numerical calculations rather than a proof. The result here addresses the finite-cyclic sampling question directly: it gives a rigorous all-order statement, the sharp order threshold \(L=5\), and a uniform positive gap for every \(L\ge5\).

A 2026 proof of Mockenhaupt's three-term conjecture settles the continuous-circle problem for all relevant exponents and all three-term frequency gaps, but its stated theorem concerns continuous \(L^p(\mathbb T)\) norms rather than uniform averages on every finite root-of-unity grid. Continuous positivity by itself does not imply positivity on each finite grid, which is why the aliasing estimate is essential here.

## Limitations
The full text of Mockenhaupt's 1996 habilitation thesis was not available for direct statement-by-statement inspection. Krenedits' detailed published description says that Example 3.4 gives a discrete and uniform version whose lower bound was established numerically and did not provide a proof. The strongest residual literature risk is therefore that the thesis contains additional rigorous material not reflected in that description. The present claim is limited to the rigorous all-order sign theorem, its sharp threshold, and the explicit uniform lower bound proved above; it does not claim that the underlying discrete inequality was never previously conjectured or numerically observed.

The numerical constants in the verification are certificates for the stated bounds, not closed forms for \(\Delta_L\) when \(L\ge5\).
