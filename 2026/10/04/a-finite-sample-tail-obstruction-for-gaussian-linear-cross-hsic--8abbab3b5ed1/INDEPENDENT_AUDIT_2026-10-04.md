# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The published linear-kernel definitions reduce exactly to \(T_n=\operatorname{sgn}(f_2)(n-2)C/\sqrt{n(n-1)Q}\). Gaussian centering makes the residual directions independent Haar points on the sphere of the sum-zero subspace, so the statistic is a constant times \(|u\cdot v|/\|P(u\circ v)\|\). For \(n=3\), the denominator is constant and the numerator is a cosine of a uniform angle difference, giving the arcsine law. For \(n\ge4\), the displayed reciprocal-coordinate zeros have nonzero numerator; the rank-three certificate at \(n=4\) and the five-distinct-coordinate quartic argument for \(n\ge5\) make the denominator map a submersion. A tube around its zero manifold therefore has probability of order at least \(t^{-(n-1)}\), and the tail integral gives the moment divergence. The actual verifier was inspected and independently confirms the algebraic reduction and rank certificates.

## originality

PASS

The exact primary cross-HSIC paper was read through its linear-kernel and studentization sections. It proves asymptotic Gaussian null behavior and power guarantees but does not state an exact finite-sample Gaussian law, a denominator-zero geometry, polynomial lower tails, or infinite moments. Searches in cross-U-statistic and Studentized-U-statistic literature did not locate a theorem implying this specific phase transition.

## value

PASS

The theorem exposes a sharp finite-sample pathology of a statistic introduced specifically to enable Gaussian calibration: at the smallest admissible sizes the law changes from bounded arcsine to polynomially heavy with divergent high moments. This is a natural and practically relevant boundary fact, not an arbitrary finite calculation.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
