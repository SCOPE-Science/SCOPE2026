# Review of A full weak-limit ball for the diagonal WMP witness on \(X_c\)

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the problem to the asymptotic scalar function \(L_c(a)=\max\{1,\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\}\). Property \((M)\) transfers the relevant limsup to the explicit two-coordinate calculation, and a subsequence argument upgrades it to a limit. Repeating this for \(D_cx\) yields the exact limiting norm ratio. The source's strict inequality \(\|D_cx\|<\|x\|\) for nonzero \(x\) makes the threshold sharp outside \(\sqrt{1-c^2}\,B_{X_c}\). The proof covers the boundary and the possible vanishing of one denominator is harmless because at most one index is affected.

## Originality
PASS. Searches were made for the focal source identifier, arbitrary weak limits of maximizing sequences, the radius \(\sqrt{1-c^2}\), property-\((M)\) formulations, and implications from general WMP theory. The focal paper was inspected at the definition and proof level and provides only the special boundary-center sequence. The closest broader WMP sources do not imply the arbitrary-center threshold. The main residual risk is recency and the brevity of the extension.

## Value
PASS. A single nonzero weak limit proves failure of WMP, but it does not describe how large the failure is. Showing that one fixed non-norm-attaining operator supports maximizing sequences with every weak limit in a full closed ball gives a substantially richer and quantitatively sharp picture of the new counterexample. The radius is intrinsic to the asymptotic flattening built into the source construction.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is García--Maestre--Pinasco--Zalduendo, arXiv:2609.23198v1, especially Lemma 3.5 and Theorem 4.2. Han--Kim and Dantas--Jung--Martínez-Cervantes provide the nearest general WMP context. The finding classifies only the normalized one-spike family and proves only containment for the complete weak-limit set; it does not classify all maximizing sequences.
