# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The final claim was reconstructed directly from the displayed operations. The intrinsic subspaces are \(R=P_q^2=Fu\) and \(V=[P_q,P_q]=Fu\oplus Fv\). For an arbitrary adapted basis, the product and bracket normalization force the change-of-basis coefficients \(d=1\) and \(c=a\), after which \([x_0,v_0]=v_0+q u_0\). The explicit Lie isomorphism for \(q,q'\ne0\) was checked on both defining brackets, and \(q=0\) is separated by the scalar-versus-nonscalar action on the characteristic derived ideal. The dimension-two obstruction and the exact Poisson parameter-separation theorem were verified in the primary source.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest source is Petrov--Pypka's classification itself: it defines \(P_{3,22}(q)\) and proves that Poisson isomorphism forces equality of \(q\). The final claim is not that theorem restated. It identifies \(q\) as an adapted-basis compatibility scalar, proves that all nonzero parameters have one Lie-isomorphism type while the associative type is constant, and combines this with the dimension-two theorem to obtain sharp dimension minimality. Searches of the exact family, defining products, component-isomorphism formulation, published-finding corpus records, the characteristic-zero deformation literature, and the complex three-dimensional classification did not locate an equivalent statement.

Residual risk remains that older low-dimensional classification work may encode the same nonzero Jordan-parameter collapse under another normal form. The source's own isomorphism proof also contains coefficient calculations close to the intrinsic-scalar argument, so originality is assigned to the combined compatibility-fiber and minimality statement rather than to the elementary coefficient manipulation alone.

## Value
**PASS.** The question is intrinsic to Poisson structure: it asks whether knowing the two operations separately, up to isomorphism, determines their compatibility. The answer fails first in dimension three, and the failure is measured by a genuine field-valued scalar invisible to both separate component types. Over \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\) with \(m\ge2\), one fixed component pair supports \(2^m-1\) pairwise nonisomorphic members of the nonzero-parameter family. This is a structural boundary, not a routine numerical recomputation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary comparison is A. V. Petrov and O. O. Pypka, arXiv:2609.13784v1. E. Remm, arXiv:1105.2670, provides deformation-theoretic context for preserving an associative or Lie component, but in characteristic zero. H. Abdelwahab, A. Fernández Ouaridi, and C. Martín González, arXiv:2209.09150, treats three-dimensional Poisson algebras over the complex field. The final result does not classify the entire global fiber over the component pair; it certifies the stated subfamily and its dimension-minimality phenomenon.
