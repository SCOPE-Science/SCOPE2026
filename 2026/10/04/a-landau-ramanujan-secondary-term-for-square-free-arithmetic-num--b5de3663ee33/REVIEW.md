# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The square-free criterion reduces the exceptions exactly to \(2m\) with \(m\) square-free and supported on primes congruent to \(1\pmod4\). The associated Euler product is \(\zeta(s)^{1/2}G(s)\) with \(G\) regular and nonzero at \(1\). Landau–Selberg–Delange gives the required \(x/\sqrt{\log x}\) scale, and the constant simplification to \(2K_{\mathrm{LR}}/\pi^2\) is exact. Direct computation through \(10^6\) checks the structural criterion independently.

## Originality
**PASS.** Oller-Marcén states the classical square-free criterion but not this asymptotic. Bateman–Erdős–Pomerance–Straus count the ambient set of all non-arithmetic integers on a different scale and do not isolate the square-free exceptions. Granville–Koukoulopoulos supplies the analytic method but not this application. Targeted searches over the criterion, the equivalent prime-support count, the Landau–Ramanujan constant, and arithmetic-number databases found no covering statement. Residual risk remains that a non-indexed note records the same standard analytic corollary.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem gives the first explicit lower-order term for the natural square-free stratum of arithmetic numbers, identifies the exact exceptional proportion, and connects a classical divisor-integrality problem to the Landau–Ramanujan constant. The statement is structurally motivated by the classical square-free characterization and is not a routine parameter increment.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest arithmetic-number source is Antonio M. Oller-Marcén, *On arithmetic numbers*, arXiv:1206.1823v1, especially Section 3. The closest broad distribution source is Bateman, Erdős, Pomerance, and Straus (1981), whose Theorem 2.1 concerns all non-arithmetic integers. The analytic input is the classical Landau–Selberg–Delange theorem; a modern reference is Granville and Koukoulopoulos, arXiv:1710.01389. The claim is restricted to square-free integers and does not assert a sharper error term.
