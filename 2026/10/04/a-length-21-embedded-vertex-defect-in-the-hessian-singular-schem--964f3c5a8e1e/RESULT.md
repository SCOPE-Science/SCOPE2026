# A length-\(21\) embedded vertex defect in the Hessian singular scheme of a general cyclic cubic threefold
## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Consider a general cyclic cubic threefold
\[
X=V(f)\subset \mathbb P^4,\qquad f=s^3+g(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4),
\]
where \(g\) defines a general smooth cubic surface. Write \(h\) for the Hessian quartic of \(g\), and let \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_{10}\) be its ten ordinary nodes. If \(P_0=[1:0:0:0:0]\), then the Hessian hypersurface of \(f\) is \(V(s h)\).

For a nonempty Zariski-open subset of this cyclic family, the Jacobian singular scheme \(\Sigma\) of \(V(s h)\) has reduced support
\[
\Sigma_{\mathrm{red}}=V(s,h)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{10}\langle P_0,Q_i\rangle,
\]
but \(\Sigma\) is not reduced at \(P_0\). On the chart \(s=1\), put
\[
R=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad K=(h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4),\qquad h_i=\partial h/\partial x_i,
\]
and let \(I=K^{\mathrm{sat}}\), the ideal of the ten node directions. Then the finite-length saturation defect \(I/K\) has Hilbert function
\[
\dim_k(I/K)_d=
\begin{cases}
6,&d=3,\\
9,&d=4,\\
6,&d=5,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
so \(\operatorname{length}(I/K)=21\). Thus the reduced rank-locus picture hides a canonical embedded contribution at the cyclic vertex.

## Assumptions and scope
“General” means a member of a nonempty Zariski-open subset on which the cubic surface has a Sylvester pentahedral form with ten ordinary Hessian nodes and the finite Macaulay ranks used below take their maximal values. No assertion is made for special cubic surfaces with extra degeneracies, including Eckardt or non-Sylvester specializations.

The statement concerns the Jacobian singular scheme of the Hessian hypersurface, not merely its set-theoretic singular locus. The lead source already gives the set-theoretic Hessian-locus stratification for a general cyclic cubic threefold.

## Proof
The Hessian matrix of \(f=s^3+g\) is block diagonal, so its determinant is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(s h\). Therefore, after dropping a scalar, the Jacobian ideal of the Hessian hypersurface is
\[
J=(h,s h_1,s h_2,s h_3,s h_4).
\]
On the chart \(s=1\), Euler’s identity for the quartic \(h\) gives \(h\in K=(h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4)\), hence the local singular scheme at \(P_0\) is defined by \(K\).

For a general cubic surface, the Hessian quartic has exactly ten ordinary nodes and no other singularities. Its saturated gradient ideal is therefore the reduced ideal \(I\) of those ten points. In Sylvester coordinates the ten nodes are the vertices of a complete pentahedron. They lie on no quadric, so
\[
\dim_k(R/I)_d=1,4,10,10,10,\ldots
\]
in degrees \(d=0,1,2,3,4,\ldots\). In particular, \(I\) is \(3\)-regular.

The four cubic partial derivatives of \(h\) are linearly independent for a general Hessian quartic, giving \(\dim_k(R/K)_3=20-4=16\). In degrees \(4\) and \(5\), the spaces generated by linear and quadratic multiples of the four partials have dimensions at most \(16\) and \(40\), so
\[
\dim_k(R/K)_4\ge 19,\qquad \dim_k(R/K)_5\ge16.
\]
Also \(K\subset I\), hence \(\dim_k(R/K)_6\ge10\).

The exact witness in the accompanying verifier realizes equality in all three bounds:
\[
\dim_k(R/K)_d=(1,4,10,16,19,16,10,10,\ldots).
\]
These equalities are maximal-rank conditions on finite coefficient matrices, so they persist on a nonempty Zariski-open subset. Equality \(K_6=I_6\), together with the \(3\)-regularity of \(I\), gives \(K_d=I_d\) for all \(d\ge6\). Subtracting the two Hilbert functions gives defect \((6,9,6)\) in degrees \(3,4,5\), of total length \(21\). Since \(I/K\neq0\) is supported at the homogeneous origin, the vertex is an embedded associated point of the local Jacobian scheme.

Away from \(P_0\), the scheme is reduced. At a smooth point of \(h=0\) on \(s=0\), a local coordinate can be chosen with \(h=u\), so the hypersurface equation is \(su=0\) and the Jacobian singular scheme is \((s,u)\). At a node \(Q_i\), the formal Morse lemma gives \(h=u^2+v^2+w^2\), and
\[
J=(u^2+v^2+w^2,su,sv,sw)=(s,u^2+v^2+w^2)\cap(u,v,w),
\]
which is radical. Along a radial line away from \(P_0\), the node of \(h\) gives transverse type \(A_1\) with the line parameter smooth.

## Verification
The verifier uses the Sylvester witness with \(z_0+z_1+z_2+z_3+z_4=0\), coefficients \((1,2,3,5,7)\), and
\[
g=\sum_{i=0}^4 c_i^{-1}z_i^3,\qquad h=\sum_{i=0}^4 c_i\prod_{j\ne i}z_j.
\]
It checks exactly that \(\det(\operatorname{Hess}(g))=(216/35)h\), that the ten pentahedral vertices are ordinary nodes, and that the cubic surface is smooth. A Gröbner computation gives
\[
H_{R/K}=(1,4,10,16,19,16,10,10,10),
\]
while exact evaluation on the ten nodes gives
\[
H_{R/I}=(1,4,10,10,10,10,10,10,10).
\]
Their difference is \((0,0,0,6,9,6,0,0,0)\), summing to \(21\). A dehomogenizing form avoiding all ten nodes independently gives a length-ten projective gradient scheme.

## Relationship to prior work
Bricalli–Favale–Pirola, Example 2.7 in the arXiv version (Example 3.8 in the journal presentation), proves that for a general cyclic cubic threefold the Hessian is a hyperplane union a cone and gives the Hessian-locus stratification. In particular, its set-theoretic singular locus is the Hessian quartic section together with the ten joins \(\langle P_0,Q_i\rangle\). The inspected example does not determine the Jacobian-scheme saturation at \(P_0\), its embedded length, or the local scheme decomposition at the nodes.

Dardanelli–van Geemen supplies the classical geometry of the ten-nodal Hessian quartic of a general cubic surface. Bricalli’s later work on linear spaces in Hessian loci again treats the cyclic singular locus set-theoretically; targeted full-text checks for scheme-level or Jacobian-saturation statements did not locate the length-\(21\) defect.

## Limitations
The length \(21\) is asserted on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the cyclic family, not on every cyclic cubic threefold. The result does not classify specializations where the Hessian quartic acquires extra singularities, nor does it compute a primary decomposition of the embedded component beyond the canonical saturation module \(I/K\). The verifier establishes one exact witness and the proof uses openness of finite matrix ranks to pass to the stated general open subset.
