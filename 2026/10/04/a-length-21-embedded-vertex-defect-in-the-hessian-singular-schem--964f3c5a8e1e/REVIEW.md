# Review
## Correctness
PASS. Differentiating the cyclic Hessian equation \(F=s h\) gives the Jacobian ideal directly. Euler’s identity identifies the vertex chart with the quartic gradient ideal. The ten ordinary nodes determine the saturation, and the exact witness verifies the required Hilbert functions and ordinary-node hypotheses. The maximal-rank equalities are open conditions, giving the stated nonempty Zariski-open family. Local radicality away from the vertex is checked from explicit normal forms.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Bricalli–Favale–Pirola, gives the reduced Hessian-locus stratification for the same general cyclic cubic threefold but not the Jacobian-scheme saturation. A later paper revisiting cyclic Hessian singular loci remains set-theoretic in the inspected statements. published-finding corpus searches for the exact object, aliases, Hilbert profile, saturation defect, and embedded-vertex formulation found no covering result. The residual risk is older literature using different Milnor-algebra terminology.

## Value
PASS. The distinction between reduced support and the actual Jacobian singular scheme is mathematically substantive here: the source’s quartic-plus-ten-lines picture misses a finite embedded contribution of exact length \(21\). The graded profile \((6,9,6)\) is a natural complete local saturation invariant and can affect scheme-theoretic desingularization and deformation calculations.

The result is only for a nonempty Zariski-open cyclic family; special degenerations are excluded, and no finer primary decomposition of the embedded component is claimed.
