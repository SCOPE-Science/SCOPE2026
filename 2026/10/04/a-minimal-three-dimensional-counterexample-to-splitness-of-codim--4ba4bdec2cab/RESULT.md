# A minimal three-dimensional counterexample to splitness of codimension-base ideals
## Finding
Over every field \(k\), the codimension-only splitness question for standard graded, locally finite, semiconnected algebras has a negative answer in the smallest possible total dimension.

Set
\[
A=k\times k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
\]
and write \(e=(1,0)\), \(f=(0,1)\), and \(x=(0,\varepsilon)\). Give \(A\) the grading
\[
A_0=ke\oplus kf,\qquad A_1=kx,\qquad A_i=0\quad(i\ge2).
\]
Let \(J=ke\). Then
\[
\dim_k(A/J)=2=\dim_k(A_0),
\]
but \(J\) is not split with respect to this grading. Thus the question asking whether every ideal of quotient dimension \(\dim_k(A_0)\) must be split has a negative answer. The example has total dimension \(3\), and no counterexample exists in smaller total dimension.

There is also a sharp tangent-space diagnostic:
\[
J^2=J,\qquad J/J^2=0,\qquad \dim_k(A_1)=1.
\]
Hence the example isolates precisely the information discarded by retaining codimension while dropping tangent dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(k\) is arbitrary. No algebraic-closure, characteristic, or finiteness assumption on \(k\) is used.

The terminology is that of Dramburg: a grading is semiconnected when it is nonnegative and \(A_0\) is semisimple; standard graded when \(A\) is generated by \(A_0\cup A_1\); locally finite when every homogeneous component is finite-dimensional; and an ideal \(J\) is split when the composite \(A_0\to A\to A/J\) is an isomorphism.

The minimality assertion concerns total vector-space dimension among finite-dimensional examples satisfying these hypotheses.

## Proof
The multiplication on the basis \(e,f,x\) is
\[
e^2=e,\quad f^2=f,\quad fx=xf=x,
\]
with every other product of basis elements equal to \(0\). In particular, products respect degrees. The degree-zero algebra \(A_0=ke\oplus kf\cong k\times k\) is semisimple. The algebra is generated by \(A_0\cup A_1\), and only degrees \(0\) and \(1\) occur, so the grading is standard, locally finite and semiconnected.

Because \(e\) is a central idempotent, \(J=ke\) is a two-sided ideal. The quotient \(A/J\) has basis given by the images of \(f\) and \(x\), and is isomorphic to \(k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\). Thus \(\dim_k(A/J)=2=\dim_k(A_0)\). However, the composite
\[
A_0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow A/J
\]
has kernel \(ke\neq0\), so it is not an isomorphism. Therefore \(J\) is not split.

Furthermore, \(e^2=e\) gives \(J^2=J\), hence \(J/J^2=0\). By contrast, \(A_1\) is one-dimensional. This makes the failure compatible with the source paper's proof strategy, which tracks both prescribed codimension and tangent dimension rather than codimension alone.

For minimality, suppose \(A\) has total dimension at most \(2\). If \(\dim_k A_0=1\) and \(\dim_k(A/J)=1\), then \(J\) is proper and the unit survives in the quotient. The unital linear map \(A_0\cong k\to A/J\) is therefore nonzero between one-dimensional spaces and hence is an isomorphism, so \(J\) is split. If \(\dim_k A_0=2\), total dimension at most \(2\) forces \(A=A_0\); the equality \(\dim_k(A/J)=\dim_k A_0\) then forces \(J=0\), which is split. The zero-dimensional base case is trivial. Thus total dimension \(3\) is minimal.

## Verification
The proof above is symbolic and works over every field. The included script `artifacts/verify.py` independently checks the displayed multiplication table over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\): associativity, grading compatibility, the ideal property, quotient dimension, failure of splitness, and \(J^2=J\). Its replay output is stored in `artifacts/verification_output.txt` and ends with `CHECK_OK`.

The finite checks are regression tests only. They are not used to infer the arbitrary-field statement or the dimension-minimality claim; those follow from the preceding exact proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Dramburg's 2026 paper defines semiconnected, standard graded and locally finite algebras, defines split ideals, and explicitly asks whether every ideal \(J\) with \(\dim_k(A/J)=\dim_k(A_0)\) is necessarily split. The present example answers that question negatively.

The same paper explains that its proof follows the earlier Bell--Zhang and Gaddis strategy of controlling ideals by both codimension and tangent dimension. Bell--Zhang's connected case studies codimension-one ideals together with \(\dim_k(J/J^2)\). The present ideal has the required quotient dimension but has tangent dimension \(0\) while the positive-degree ideal has tangent dimension \(1\), so it does not contradict those stronger arguments; it shows that their tangent condition cannot simply be discarded in the semiconnected setting.

Direct semantic searches for the question, the algebra \(k\times k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), a three-dimensional counterexample, and equivalent codimension/split formulations found no prior answer or stronger result. The closest indexed results concerned unrelated short local algebras, homological counterexamples, or finite regradings and do not imply this statement.

## Limitations
The claim answers only the codimension-only splitness question. It does not classify all non-split ideals of the prescribed codimension, and it does not address the paper's other open questions. The source is recent, so unindexed or unpublished parallel work remains a residual originality risk.
