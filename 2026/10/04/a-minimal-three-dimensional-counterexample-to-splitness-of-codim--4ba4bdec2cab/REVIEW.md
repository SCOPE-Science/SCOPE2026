# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The example is an explicit direct product. Its grading, semisimple degree-zero part, standard generation, ideal property, quotient dimension and failure of splitness are all checked directly on the three basis elements. The minimality proof exhausts the possible values of \(\dim_k A_0\) when total dimension is at most \(2\). The arbitrary-field proof is symbolic; the finite-field script is only a regression test.

## Originality
PASS. The 2026 motivating paper states the exact codimension-only splitness problem as an open question. Its full text and Bell--Zhang's connected antecedent were inspected at the relevant definitions and tangent-dimension arguments. Searches for the named question, the direct-product/dual-number model, equivalent split formulations and a dimension-three counterexample found no equivalent or stronger answer. The prior isomorphism results retain tangent-dimension information and therefore do not imply the present statement.

## Value
PASS. This is not merely a small example: it settles an explicit open structural question, proves the smallest possible dimension of failure, and identifies the exact missing invariant through \(J/J^2=0\) versus \(\dim_k A_1=1\). It therefore provides a sharp boundary for attempts to simplify the codimension-and-tangent method used in graded-isomorphism proofs.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Dramburg, arXiv:2609.03288v1, which defines split ideals and asks the question answered here. Bell--Zhang, arXiv:1509.08812v1, treats the connected case and explicitly couples codimension with tangent dimension; Gaddis, arXiv:1712.01650v1, treats graded path algebras. None covers the arbitrary codimension-only semiconnected ideal in this example. The source is recent, so unindexed contemporaneous work remains a residual risk. The result does not classify all non-split ideals and does not address the source's other open questions.
