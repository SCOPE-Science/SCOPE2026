# A multiplicity-\(256\) local model for the eight sub-hyperdeterminants at the diagonal rank-two tensor
## Finding
Let \(H\subset\mathbb P^{15}_{\mathbb C}\) be the scheme cut out by the eight coordinate \(2\times2\times2\) Cayley hyperdeterminants of a \(2\times2\times2\times2\) tensor. At
\[
T=[e_0^{\otimes4}+e_1^{\otimes4}],
\]
use the affine chart \(x_{0000}=1\), write \(x_{1111}=1+y_{1111}\), and write \(x_\alpha=y_\alpha\) for every other index \(\alpha\ne0000\). The eight defining quartics have order exactly \(2\), and their quadratic initial forms are
\[
y_{0001}^2,\ y_{0010}^2,\ y_{0100}^2,\ y_{1000}^2,
\ y_{0111}^2,\ y_{1011}^2,\ y_{1101}^2,\ y_{1110}^2.
\]
Thus the eight normal directions indexed by Hamming weight \(1\) or \(3\) are independently doubled in the scheme-theoretic tangent cone. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}\mathcal O_{H,T}
\simeq
\frac{\mathbb C[y_\alpha:\alpha\ne0000]}
{(y_{0001}^2,y_{0010}^2,y_{0100}^2,y_{1000}^2,y_{0111}^2,y_{1011}^2,y_{1101}^2,y_{1110}^2)}.
\]
Consequently \(H\) is a local complete intersection of codimension \(8\) and local dimension \(7\) at \(T\), its Zariski tangent space is the whole \(15\)-dimensional affine chart, the tangent-cone Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{(1+t)^8}{(1-t)^7},
\]
and the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is
\[
e(H,T)=2^8=256.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All local-algebra statements are over \(\mathbb C\). The scheme \(H\) means exactly the scheme generated by the eight coordinate sub-hyperdeterminants; its underlying zero set is the locus called \(\mathrm{Hyp}\) in Seigal--Sturmfels for the \(2\times2\times2\times2\) format. The point \(T\) is the diagonal real rank-two tensor used in their Proposition 4.6 and Remark 4.7. The claim is scheme-theoretic for these eight quartics and does not identify the multiplicity of a reduced structure on the underlying set.

## Proof
Cayley's \(2\times2\times2\) hyperdeterminant in coordinates \(a_{ijk}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta={}&a_{000}^2a_{111}^2+a_{001}^2a_{110}^2+a_{010}^2a_{101}^2+a_{011}^2a_{100}^2\\
&+4a_{000}a_{011}a_{101}a_{110}+4a_{001}a_{010}a_{100}a_{111}\\
&-2a_{000}a_{001}a_{110}a_{111}-2a_{000}a_{010}a_{101}a_{111}-2a_{000}a_{011}a_{100}a_{111}\\
&-2a_{001}a_{010}a_{101}a_{110}-2a_{001}a_{011}a_{100}a_{110}-2a_{010}a_{011}a_{100}a_{101}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Fixing one of the four tensor modes and fixing its bit to \(0\) or \(1\) gives the eight slice quartics. Direct substitution at \(T\) yields, respectively,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{fixed mode}&\text{bit }0&\text{bit }1\\\hline
0&y_{0111}^2&y_{1000}^2\\
1&y_{1011}^2&y_{0100}^2\\
2&y_{1101}^2&y_{0010}^2\\
3&y_{1110}^2&y_{0001}^2
\end{array}
\]
as the complete degree-\(2\) parts, and there are no constant or linear terms. These eight initial forms involve pairwise distinct variables, so they form a regular sequence in the polynomial associated graded ring of the regular local chart.

For completeness, use the standard filtered regular-sequence argument. If \(f\) has initial form \(f^*\) that is a non-zero-divisor in the associated graded ring, then \(\operatorname{ord}(fg)=\operatorname{ord}(f)+\operatorname{ord}(g)\) for nonzero \(g\); hence the initial ideal of \((f)\) is generated by \(f^*\). Applying this inductively after quotienting by the preceding generators gives the displayed associated graded ring. It follows that the local equations themselves form a regular sequence. There are \(15\) affine coordinates and \(8\) equations, giving local dimension \(7\). Since every equation starts in degree \(2\), the Jacobian at \(T\) is zero and the Zariski tangent space has dimension \(15\). Finally, the tangent-cone complete intersection has Hilbert series \((1+t)^8/(1-t)^7\), so its multiplicity is the product of the eight quadratic degrees, namely \(2^8=256\).

## Verification
The standalone script `artifacts/verify.py` implements Cayley's quartic with exact integer sparse-polynomial arithmetic using only the Python standard library. It reconstructs all eight slices after the affine substitution, checks that each has order exactly \(2\), checks the eight displayed quadratic initial forms term-for-term, verifies that the eight variables are distinct and precisely the Hamming-weight \(1\) or \(3\) indices, and checks the derived codimension, dimension, and multiplicity. Running `python3 artifacts/verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Seigal--Sturmfels prove that their hyperdeterminantal zero set contains the diagonal tensor \(T\) even though \(T\) lies in the relative interior of the real rank-two locus: all eight \(2\times2\times2\) sub-tensors have rank one, so all eight hyperdeterminants vanish. Their statement is set-theoretic and does not compute the local scheme, tangent cone, or multiplicity at \(T\). Oeding studies the much larger representation-theoretic hyperdeterminantal module and gives set-theoretic equations for tangential Segre varieties and principal-minor varieties; those results do not imply the local eight-coordinate-quartic calculation above. The present local computation therefore explains, at the canonical strict-containment example, why these eight equations have no first-order boundary sensitivity and quantifies their common second-order degeneracy.

## Limitations
The result concerns only the eight coordinate sub-hyperdeterminants in format \(2\times2\times2\times2\) and the single diagonal point \(T\). It does not classify the other components or singularities of their global zero set, does not identify a reduced local structure, and does not replace the additional equations needed to describe the tangential variety. The literature comparison cannot exclude an unpublished or differently phrased local computation.
