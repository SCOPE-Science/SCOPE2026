# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The facet-area formula gives the incenter, direct equidistance gives the circumcenter, and the center-collinearity determinant factors exactly as \((p-h)Q\). On the nonsymmetric factor, the substitution \(x=p+h\), \(y=ph\) reduces the problem to a single radical equation. Squaring is accompanied by an explicit positivity check, so no extraneous branch is accepted. The quadratic discriminant gives exactly the threshold \(ph\ge2\). The explicit \((\sqrt7,3/\sqrt7)\) example satisfies the branch, the center relation, a nonorthogonal opposite-edge test, and the failure of all biregular partitions.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary literature gives a characterization only under orthocentricity, while two 2013 full texts explicitly identify the general incenter-on-Euler-line characterization as open. published-finding corpus, exact-coordinate, exact-form, alias, and broader-coverage searches found no statement of this two-parameter classification or the displayed nonorthocentric witness. The prior orthocentric theorem cannot imply the example because a pair of opposite edges has nonzero dot product. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased later work.

## Value
PASS. The family is geometrically motivated by one reflection symmetry and an orthogonal-axis normalization, not by an arbitrary parameter slice. The complete classification exposes a continuous nonorthocentric solution branch and therefore supplies concrete structural information directly relevant to the published characterization problem. It rules out any approach that implicitly assumes the orthocentric/biregular regime exhausts the tetrahedral solutions.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest theorem is Edmonds–Hajja–Martini (2008), which characterizes the orthocentric case. Hajja–Martini (2013) and Bezdek–Deza–Ye (2013) explicitly discuss the extension to general simplices as open. The present result classifies only \(T_{p,h}\); it does not characterize all reflection-symmetric tetrahedra, all tetrahedra, or higher-dimensional simplices. The 2008 paper was compared through its abstract and later full-text statements of its theorem.
