# A one-dimensional formula for the universal Kagome spectral probability
## Finding
For the preferred-orientation Kagome quantum graph studied by Marzieh Baradaran and Pavel Exner, assume the two independent edge phases are generated by positive lengths \(b=d-c\) and \(c\) with \(b/c\notin\mathbb Q\). Their high-energy band criterion is the sign condition
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(2\cos\frac{y-2x}{2}+\cos\frac y2\right)
\left(\cos\frac{y-x}{2}+2\cos\frac{y+x}{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\times
\left(\cos\frac{2y-x}{2}+2\cos\frac x2\right)\ge 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(x=kd\pmod{2\pi}\) and \(y=kc\pmod{2\pi}\). Because \((kb,kc)\mapsto(x,y)=(kb+kc,kc)\) is a torus automorphism and \(b/c\notin\mathbb Q\), the phase pair is equidistributed on \([0,2\pi)^2\).

Set
\[
q_0=\sqrt{\frac35},\qquad
a(q)=2q-\sqrt{4q^2+3},\qquad
b_+(q)=2q+\sqrt{4q^2+3},
\]
\[
g(q)=\frac3q,\qquad
e(q)=-\frac{3+q^2}{2q},\qquad q>0,
\]
and define
\[
h_<(q)=1+\frac{\arctan e(q)+\arctan b_+(q)-\arctan a(q)-\arctan g(q)}{\pi},
\]
\[
h_>(q)=1+\frac{\arctan e(q)+\arctan g(q)-\arctan a(q)-\arctan b_+(q)}{\pi}.
\]
Then the universal high-energy spectral probability appearing in the source has the exact one-dimensional representation
\[
P_\mathrm{Kag}
=\frac2\pi\left(
\int_0^{q_0}\frac{h_<(q)}{1+q^2}\,dq+
\int_{q_0}^\infty\frac{h_>(q)}{1+q^2}\,dq
\right)
=0.639080422792129545\ldots.
\]
This replaces the source's two-dimensional numerical area calculation by a one-dimensional elementary quadrature with a single explicit breakpoint.

## Assumptions and scope
The claim concerns the incommensurate Kagome case and the asymptotic criterion (3.13) in arXiv:2106.16019v1. The lengths satisfy \(b>0\), \(c>0\), \(d=b+c\), and \(b/c\notin\mathbb Q\). The coupling scale \(\ell>0\) drops out of the leading high-energy criterion. Measure-zero phase values at which tangent coordinates have poles or one factor vanishes do not affect the probability.

The result is an exact representation of the universal limiting constant selected by the source's high-energy condition. It does not claim a new dispersion relation, a new universality theorem for arbitrary quantum graphs, or an elementary closed form in named constants.

## Proof
Put \(p=\tan(x/2)\) and \(q=\tan(y/2)\). Away from the measure-zero pole sets, direct half-angle algebra gives
\[
2\cos\frac{y-2x}{2}+\cos\frac y2
=
\cos\frac y2\,
\frac{3-p^2+4pq}{1+p^2},
\]
\[
\cos\frac{y-x}{2}+2\cos\frac{y+x}{2}
=
\cos\frac y2\cos\frac x2\,(3-pq),
\]
and
\[
\cos\frac{2y-x}{2}+2\cos\frac x2
=
\cos\frac x2\,
\frac{3+q^2+2pq}{1+q^2}.
\]
The omitted multiplier is nonnegative, so the sign of the trigonometric product is exactly the sign of
\[
F(p,q)=(3-p^2+4pq)(3-pq)(3+q^2+2pq).
\]

Uniform phase on the circle sends \(\tan(\cdot/2)\) to a standard Cauchy variable. Thus \(p\) and \(q\) are independent with density \(\pi^{-1}(1+t^2)^{-1}\). By \(F(-p,-q)=F(p,q)\), it is enough to condition on \(q>0\).

For fixed \(q>0\), the four roots in \(p\) are
\[
e(q)=-\frac{3+q^2}{2q},\qquad
a(q)=2q-\sqrt{4q^2+3},\qquad
b_+(q)=2q+\sqrt{4q^2+3},\qquad
g(q)=\frac3q.
\]
One has \(e(q)<a(q)<0<b_+(q)\). Indeed,
\[
a(q)-e(q)=
\frac{5q^2+3-2q\sqrt{4q^2+3}}{2q}>0,
\]
because
\[
(5q^2+3)^2-4q^2(4q^2+3)=9(q^2+1)^2>0.
\]
The positive roots cross only when \(b_+(q)=g(q)\), which is equivalent to \(q^2=3/5\). Hence, with \(q_0=\sqrt{3/5}\),
\[
\{p:F(p,q)\ge0}=
(-\infty,e]\cup[a,b_+]\cup[g,\infty)
\quad(0<q<q_0),
\]
while
\[
\{p:F(p,q)\ge0}=
(-\infty,e]\cup[a,g]\cup[b_+,\infty)
\quad(q>q_0).
\]
Integrating the standard Cauchy density over these intervals gives \(h_<(q)\) and \(h_>(q)\). A second Cauchy integral in \(q\), doubled by the symmetry \(q\leftrightarrow-q\), yields the stated one-dimensional formula.

The source defines its spectral probability on the energy scale. If \(I(k)\) denotes the asymptotic momentum-band indicator and \(\int_0^T I(k)\,dk=P T+o(T)\), then integration by parts gives
\[
\int_0^T 2kI(k)\,dk=P T^2+o(T^2),
\]
so the same limiting torus fraction appears after the change from momentum \(k\) to energy \(k^2\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently evaluates the one-dimensional integral by adaptive Simpson quadrature after the substitution \(q=\tan u\), so the domain becomes \(0\le u\le\pi/2\). It checks the root ordering and crossover, checks the sign factorization on a deterministic two-dimensional set of nonsingular phase points, and separately estimates the original two-phase area by a \(900\times900\) midpoint grid.

The replay returns `VERIFY_OK`, the one-dimensional value \(0.639080422792130\), and an independent two-dimensional midpoint estimate \(0.639086420\). The one-dimensional value differs from the source's displayed approximation \(0.639081\) by less than \(10^{-6}\).

## Relationship to prior work
Baradaran and Exner derive the high-energy inequality above, state that the resulting expression is too complicated to solve directly, and evaluate the corresponding two-dimensional torus area numerically as approximately \(0.639081\). Their paper therefore supplies the physical model, the asymptotic criterion, and the universality target, but not the tangent-half-angle factorization, the root-order classification, or the one-dimensional formula proved here.

Band and Berkolaiko prove the general torus-volume/equidistribution mechanism behind generic momentum-band-density universality for periodic quantum graphs. Their framework explains why rationally independent edge phases reduce spectral probability to a torus-volume calculation, but it does not evaluate the preferred-orientation Kagome region or imply the explicit functions \(h_<\), \(h_>\), or the breakpoint \(\sqrt{3/5}\).

Targeted searches for the numerical constant, the preferred-orientation Kagome probability, tangent-half-angle reductions, and the factor \(3-pq\) did not locate an equivalent published formula. This is evidence against coverage, not a proof of global novelty.

## Limitations
The proof takes the source's asymptotic band criterion (3.13) as its starting point rather than re-deriving it from the full Floquet determinant. The integral is exact but is not reduced here to a finite combination of standard named constants. Search-based originality checking cannot exclude an equivalent calculation hidden under different terminology in quantum-graph, trigonometric-area, or Cauchy-integral literature.

The two-dimensional grid in `verify.py` is only an independent numerical check; it is not part of the proof. The proof itself is the algebraic factorization, root ordering, Cauchy conditioning, and equidistribution reduction above.
