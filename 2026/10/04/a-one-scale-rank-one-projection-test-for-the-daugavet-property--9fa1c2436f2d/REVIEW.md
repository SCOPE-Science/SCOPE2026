# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces every rank-one square to either zero or a positive scalar multiple of a rank-one projection. The scaling lemma is exact for all positive scalars and follows from two elementary triangle-inequality decompositions. The resulting signed-square identity matches the hypothesis of Langemets's 2026 characterization for real Banach spaces of dimension greater than one.

## Originality
PASS. Targeted searches for projection-only, idempotent-only, fixed-scale, exact-complement-norm, and signed variants found the 2026 signed-square theorem and the older weaker “bad projections” condition, but no statement that one exact norm identity at scalar scale one on all rank-one projections is equivalent to the Daugavet property. The closest literature is therefore implication-adjacent but does not dominate the claim as stated.

## Value
PASS. The result replaces a universal test over all rank-one operators and their squares by a one-scale test on rank-one idempotents. It also identifies the exact strengthening of the classical bad-projections condition that recovers the full Daugavet property. This gives a simpler criterion on a geometrically distinguished operator class rather than a routine parameter substitution.

## Closest literature and limitations
Langemets (arXiv:2609.27693v1) proves the signed-square characterization and is the essential recent premise. Ivakhno–Kadets introduced spaces with bad projections, requiring only \(\|I-P\|\ge2\) for every rank-one projection, and showed that this is weaker than the Daugavet property. Kania–Lewicki (arXiv:2604.10771v1) study projection constants in Daugavet spaces but do not provide the converse fixed-scale projection criterion. The main residual risk is terminological or unindexed duplication of this short reduction.
