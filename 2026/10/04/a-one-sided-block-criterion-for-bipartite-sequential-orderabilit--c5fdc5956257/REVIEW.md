# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. In an \(A\)-block order, every \(b\in B\) has at most one incident edge per block, so its sequence is invariant under internal block permutations. At a left vertex of degree \(d\), properness gives exactly \(d!\) distinct local sequences, while its \(d\) neighbours forbid at most \(d\) of them. Since \(d!>d\) for \(d\ge3\), a safe internal order exists independently at every left vertex. The concatenated blocks therefore distinguish both endpoints of every edge. The finite verifier separately replays \(67,404\) proper colourings without failure.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the stated access risk. The September 2026 primary source states the connected regular theorem only for degree at least \(6\), while a current problem index records degrees \(3,4,5\) as remaining cases. Targeted semantic searches for sequential orderability, bipartite regular graphs, one-sided block orderings, local permutations, and degrees \(3\) and \(4\) found no equivalent theorem. The closest indexed result covers only regular bipartite degree at least \(5\), so it is strictly weaker. Direct public full-text access to the primary preprint was unavailable, and that is retained as a residual risk rather than treated as evidence of novelty.

## Value
PASS. The result closes the full bipartite regular portion of the explicitly open degrees \(3,4,5\) and gives a stronger deterministic one-sided minimum-degree criterion. It replaces a probabilistic high-degree condition by an elementary construction and is a class-wide theorem, not a finite census or a routine parameter substitution.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Gorzkowska–Kwaśny, arXiv:2609.11832 (first public version 2026-09-10). Its public abstract proves connected regular degree at least \(6\); a current open-problem index identifies \(3,4,5\) as the unresolved regular degrees. A public indexed September 2026 result gives the bipartite regular range \(d\ge5\). The present theorem strictly extends that bipartite range to \(d\ge3\) and to irregular bipartite graphs satisfying the one-sided degree condition. The proof does not address nonbipartite regular degree \(3,4,5\) graphs, multigraphs, or improper colourings.
