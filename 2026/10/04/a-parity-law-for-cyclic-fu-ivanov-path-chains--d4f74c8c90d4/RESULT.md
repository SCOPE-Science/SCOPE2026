# A parity law for cyclic Fu–Ivanov path chains
## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge 3\), define a digraph \(G_m\) with vertices
\[
\{s,t\}\cup\{a_i,b_i,c_i:i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}
\]
and arrows
\[
s\to a_i,\qquad c_i\to t,\qquad a_i\to b_i,b_{i+1},\qquad b_i\to c_i,c_{i+1},\qquad a_i\to c_i,c_{i+2}.
\]
For every commutative ring \(R\), the degree-four GLMY path-chain module is
\[
\Omega_4(G_m;R)\cong
\begin{cases}
R,&m\text{ even},\\
R[2]=\{r\in R:2r=0\},&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus for a field \(F\),
\[
\dim_F\Omega_4(G_m;F)=
\begin{cases}
1,&m\text{ even or }\operatorname{char}F=2,\\
0,&m\text{ odd and }\operatorname{char}F\ne2.
\end{cases}
\]
The member \(G_3\) is the coefficient-sensitive digraph of Fu and Ivanov, after the relabeling \(a_i=x^i_1\), \(b_i=x^i_2\), \(c_i=x^i_3\), \(s=x_0\), and \(t=x_4\).

## Assumptions and scope
Indices are read modulo \(m\). The coefficient ring \(R\) is commutative with unit, although the calculation uses only its additive abelian-group structure. The path-chain convention is the standard one: \(A_n(G;R)\) is freely generated by allowed directed \(n\)-paths and
\[
\Omega_n(G;R)=A_n(G;R)\cap\partial^{-1}(A_{n-1}(G;R)).
\]
The claim concerns the chain module \(\Omega_4\), not the homology group \(PH_4\).

## Proof
Every directed path of length four must pass successively through \(s\), an \(a\)-vertex, a \(b\)-vertex, a \(c\)-vertex, and \(t\). Hence there are exactly four such paths for each \(i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_i&=(s,a_i,b_i,c_i,t),&
B_i&=(s,a_i,b_i,c_{i+1},t),\\
C_i&=(s,a_i,b_{i+1},c_{i+1},t),&
D_i&=(s,a_i,b_{i+1},c_{i+2},t).
\end{aligned}
\]
Write a general allowed four-chain as
\[
z=\sum_i(\alpha_iA_i+\beta_iB_i+\gamma_iC_i+\delta_iD_i).
\]
Deleting an endpoint always leaves an allowed path. Deleting an internal vertex leaves a nonallowed three-path in exactly five indexed families. Vanishing of their coefficients in \(\partial z\) is therefore equivalent to
\[
\alpha_i+\gamma_{i-1}=0,\qquad
\beta_i+\delta_{i-1}=0,\qquad
\beta_i+\gamma_i=0,\qquad
\alpha_i+\beta_i=0,\qquad
\gamma_i+\delta_i=0
\]
for every \(i\). The last three equations give
\[
\beta_i=-\alpha_i,\qquad \gamma_i=\alpha_i,\qquad \delta_i=-\alpha_i.
\]
The first two then both reduce to
\[
\alpha_i=-\alpha_{i-1}.
\]
Thus all coefficients are determined by a single element \(r=\alpha_0\). Going once around the cyclic index gives \(r=(-1)^m r\). If \(m\) is even there is no further restriction and the solution module is naturally \(R\). If \(m\) is odd the condition is \(2r=0\), so the solution module is naturally \(R[2]\). This proves the claimed ring-level formula and its field corollary.

## Verification
The accompanying verifier independently constructs \(G_m\), enumerates all allowed four-paths, constructs the matrix of forbidden boundary terms directly from the path boundary operator, and checks for every \(3\le m\le24\) that there are \(4m\) allowed four-paths and exactly the five families of forbidden boundary terms used in the proof. It then computes matrix nullities over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\), obtaining dimension one over \(\mathbb F_2\) for every tested \(m\), dimension zero over odd characteristics for odd \(m\), and dimension one over odd characteristics for even \(m\). The finite replay is a consistency check; the proof above establishes the theorem for every \(m\ge3\) and every commutative ring.

## Relationship to prior work
Asao relates magnitude homology and path homology and records path homology as a central homological invariant of digraphs; the first arXiv version appeared on 2022-01-20, and the published article lists \(55N35\) among its primary MSC 2020 classifications. Fu and Ivanov construct the case \(m=3\), proving coefficient dependence in degree four and using it to separate path homology from ordinary homology-of-spaces behavior. Their paper does not state the cyclic-width family above or its even/odd classification. Burfitt and Cutler later construct a different family \(E_t\) whose degree-four path chains can detect arbitrary prime divisors of \(t\). That stronger existence theorem does not imply the explicit all-ring formula for the present cyclic extension, whose point is to classify the most direct width-\(m\) continuation of the Fu–Ivanov example and to isolate the parity mechanism in its boundary equations.

## Limitations
No claim is made that \(G_m\) is minimal for its coefficient behavior, nor that the full path homology groups of \(G_m\) have been classified. The novelty assessment is relative to the inspected sources and indexed searches; an unindexed prior computation of this exact cyclic family remains possible.
