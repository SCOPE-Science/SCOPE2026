# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Under \(A\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})\), Fourier covariance gives \(\mathcal{N}(AP)=A^{-T}\mathcal{N}(P)\). Theorem 1.7 of arXiv:2609.10517v1 identifies the twelve side-midpoint directions as exactly the minimising directions of \(P_{12}\), so twelve explicit radius-\(\kappa(P_{12})\) zeros are available as witnesses after the affine map. Among their six unoriented lines, one lies within \(\pi/12\) of the minor singular axis. The resulting norm bound is exact, and its comparison with one reduces to the factorisation \(\sin^2(\pi/12)(q-1)(q-\cot^2(\pi/12))\) with \(q=s^2\). The cutoff and equality of the finite-witness bound at the threshold were checked symbolically.

## Originality
PASS with residual bibliographic risk. The full primary source was inspected. It poses the global dodecagon maximisation problem and proves the complete nearest-zero direction theorem, but contains no affine, linear-transformation, or \(SL(2)\) treatment. Searches by affine-image, singular-value, condition-number, and exact-constant formulations found no covering result. A nearby recent Fourier-zero result concerns the higher-dimensional bipyramid construction, not planar affine images. The main residual risk is an older equivalent observation indexed under different affine-Fourier terminology.

## Value
PASS. This gives a quantitative partial resolution inside the source's explicit Open Problem 1.6: every non-rotational determinant-one image whose spectral condition number is below \(7+4\sqrt3\) is strictly worse than the regular dodecagon. The same calculation identifies the exact point at which the twelve nearest source zeros alone stop forcing strict decrease. That is a motivated structural cutoff tied directly to the open problem, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Gómez-Serrano, Levitin, Platt and Polterovich, arXiv:2609.10517v1, especially Open Problem 1.6 and Theorem 1.7. The theorem here uses their nearest-zero classification but adds the determinant-one affine analysis. It does not settle affine maps with condition number at least \(7+4\sqrt3\), because farther source zeros could become decisive after strong anisotropy; nor does it address non-affine competitors.
