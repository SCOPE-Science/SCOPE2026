# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The source indexing is reconstructed, the lattice correction is derived from Stirling's formula and midpoint summation, and the consecutive-difference sign reduces to ten exact residue classes. Their smallest leading coefficient is \(3/4\), so the uniform \(O(k^{-1})\) remainder cannot reverse the sign for all sufficiently large \(k\). The bundled exact enumeration checks finite indexing but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The lead source defines the same sequence and explicitly says it believes \(\sqrt2\) is the only \(\xi>1\) with asymptotic monotonicity. The \(\sqrt5/2\) proof therefore contradicts a concrete prior expectation. Searches of exact-sequence aliases, the parameter, broader normalized Rademacher-tail work, and later anti-concentration literature found no statement implying the ten-residue result. The 2015 Pinelis journal text was not fully re-read and remains a residual risk, although its accessible abstract concerns a different supremal-tail problem.

## Value
PASS. Resolving a published uniqueness belief by an explicit counterexample is mathematically motivated. The parity-ceiling phase calculation also exposes a reusable mechanism for the irregular regime \(\xi>1\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Hendriks and van Zuijlen (2017), DOI 10.1007/s00026-017-0351-3. Pinelis (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.spl.2015.01.010, and Hollom--Portier (2025), DOI 10.1002/rsa.70024, concern broader but different tail extremal problems. The result proves only eventual strict increase and does not classify all parameters above one.
