# A sign error breaks the printed fractional Adams–Bashforth derivation in a COVID delay model

## Finding

Consider the Caputo initial-value equation in the normalization used by the source,
\[
{}^C D_t^h y(t)=F(t),
\qquad
0<h\le1.
\]
Its Volterra form is
\[
y(t)-y(0)
=
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}}{\Gamma(h)}
\int_0^t (t-s)^{h-1}F(s)\,ds.
\]

The numerical section of the motivating article writes this representation for each epidemiological compartment. At the mesh points
\[
t_v=v\Delta,
\]
it gives the two identities corresponding to \(t_{v+1}\) and \(t_v\). The next line says that the latter is subtracted from the former, but the displayed equation (4.5) adds both Volterra integrals:
\[
y(t_{v+1})
=
y(t_v)
+
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}}{\Gamma(h)}I_{v+1}
+
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}}{\Gamma(h)}I_v,
\]
where
\[
I_j=
\int_0^{t_j}(t_j-s)^{h-1}F(s)\,ds.
\]
The second sign must be negative.

The error is decisive already on the constant test problem
\[
{}^C D_t^h y=1.
\]
The exact solution is
\[
y(t)=y(0)+\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}t^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}.
\]
Therefore the true increment from \(t_v\) to \(t_{v+1}\) is
\[
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}\Delta^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}
\left((v+1)^h-v^h\right).
\]
The printed equation (4.5) instead yields
\[
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}\Delta^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}
\left((v+1)^h+v^h\right).
\]

At
\[
h=1,
\qquad
v=1,
\qquad
\Delta=1,
\]
the exact increment is \(1\), whereas the printed relation gives \(3\).

The failure does not disappear under mesh refinement. If the printed parent recurrence is iterated to a fixed terminal time
\[
T=N\Delta,
\]
then for constant forcing its accumulated change is
\[
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}\Delta^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}
\left(
N^h+2\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}v^h
\right).
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}v^h
\sim
\frac{N^{h+1}}{h+1},
\]
this behaves as
\[
\frac{2\Lambda^{1-h}T^{h+1}}
{(h+1)\Gamma(h+1)}\frac1\Delta,
\]
and therefore diverges as \(\Delta\to0\). The exact change at time \(T\) is finite:
\[
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}T^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}.
\]
Thus the displayed equation (4.5), which is the stated parent relation for equations (4.7)–(4.11), is inconsistent for every
\[
0<h\le1.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The statement concerns the numerical derivation printed in Section 4 of the source. It uses only the source's Caputo integral representation and the algebraic operation it explicitly describes between equations (4.3) and (4.4).

The constant test problem is not an epidemiological model; it is a consistency certificate for the claimed numerical derivation. A valid numerical method for a general right-hand side must reproduce, at minimum, the sign structure of the exact Volterra increment.

The finding does not assert that every plotted curve in the article was generated by an implementation containing the same sign. No executable code was supplied in the inspected source, so the implementation cannot be identified with the printed recurrence without further evidence.

## Proof

Let
\[
C_h=\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}}{\Gamma(h)}
\]
and write the exact Volterra relation as
\[
y(t)-y(0)=C_h I(t),
\qquad
I(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)^{h-1}F(s)\,ds.
\]
At consecutive mesh points,
\[
y(t_{v+1})-y(0)=C_hI(t_{v+1})
\]
and
\[
y(t_v)-y(0)=C_hI(t_v).
\]
Subtracting the second equality from the first gives
\[
y(t_{v+1})-y(t_v)
=
C_h\bigl(I(t_{v+1})-I(t_v)\bigr).
\]
No approximation has been made here. Hence a plus sign before \(I(t_v)\) cannot result from subtraction.

Now take
\[
F(s)=1.
\]
Then
\[
I(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)^{h-1}\,ds
=
\frac{t^h}{h},
\]
so
\[
C_h I(t)
=
\frac{\Lambda^{1-h}t^h}{\Gamma(h+1)}.
\]
This proves the exact and printed one-step formulas stated above.

For the mesh-refinement statement, sum the printed increments from \(v=0\) through \(N-1\). The two power sums combine to
\[
N^h+2\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}v^h.
\]
The standard power-sum asymptotic
\[
\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}v^h
=
\frac{N^{h+1}}{h+1}+O(N^h)
\]
for \(h>0\) gives
\[
\Delta^h
\left(
N^h+2\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}v^h
\right)
=
\frac{2T^{h+1}}{h+1}\frac1\Delta+O(1).
\]
Multiplication by \(\Lambda^{1-h}/\Gamma(h+1)\) gives the claimed divergence.

## Verification

The bundled `verify.py` evaluates the constant-forcing formulas independently. It checks the integer-order witness exactly and, for several fractional orders, compares the correct and printed increments. It also verifies numerically that the cumulative printed relation grows proportionally to \(1/\Delta\) at fixed terminal time while the exact terminal value remains fixed.

The inconsistency itself is analytic and does not depend on finite experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhang, Rahman, Ahmad, Riaz, and Jarad derive equations (4.3) and (4.4) from the Caputo Volterra representation and then print equation (4.5) with the wrong sign before the earlier-time integral. Equations (4.7)–(4.11) are introduced as consequences of that displayed parent relation, and the subsequent section uses the stated numerical method for the figures.

Diethelm, Ford, and Freed developed a standard Adams-type predictor–corrector method for Caputo fractional differential equations. Their derivation emphasizes that the fractional problem is nonlocal: the Volterra integral starts at the initial time, and the fractional Adams weights retain the history over all previous nodes. In the integer-order case the corresponding Adams increment is the ordinary signed integral over the newest time interval. This is methodological prior art, not a source-specific correction of the 2021 COVID article.

Searches by the exact DOI and title together with terms for equation (4.5), Adams–Bashforth, sign, correction, and erratum did not locate a published correction of this source-specific algebraic step.

## Limitations

The result invalidates the printed derivation, not necessarily an unavailable implementation. If the authors' simulation code used a corrected minus sign or a different fractional integrator, the numerical curves would need to be assessed from that code rather than from equations (4.5)–(4.11).

The finding does not reassess the source's existence, uniqueness, Ulam–Hyers stability, equilibrium, or epidemiological interpretations except where those interpretations rely specifically on the numerical scheme.

The final formulas (4.7)–(4.11) contain additional algebraic structure from interpolation. No separate claim is made here about every coefficient in those formulas; the accepted claim is the prior exact sign error and the resulting inconsistency of the displayed parent recurrence.
