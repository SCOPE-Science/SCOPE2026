# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The accepted statement is proved by an exact specialization of the published complete-sweep determinant/Cauchy--Binet formula to three active eigenvalues and history length \(p=2\). At each extreme eigenvalue only one two-index term survives, so the quotient closes on the extreme-coordinate ratio. The published fixed-weight conjugacy gives the weight factor exactly. After normalizing by the unique positive fixed ratio, the logarithm obeys \(z_{k+2}=z_{k+1}-2z_k\); its characteristic roots have modulus \(\sqrt2\). The proof distinguishes the fixed state from every nonzero log-state and does not use finite experimentation to establish the infinite asymptotics.

## Originality

PASS. Full-text inspection of Yang--Yuan (arXiv:2609.12114v1) found the determinant identity, support/termination structure, three-dimensional rank-changing example, fixed-weight conjugacy, and R-linear theorem, but not the derived endpoint-ratio recurrence or projective exponent. Ferrandi--Hochstenbach's open LMSD review/variant paper was checked for equivalent Ritz/rank/conditioning statements, and the Curtis--Guo convergence lineage was checked at abstract/citation level. Targeted searches using three-mode, ratio-recurrence, projective, rank-loss, and weighted-LMSD aliases returned no implication-equivalent statement. Search failure is not treated as proof of priority; an older equivalent result under different notation remains the principal residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The case of three active modes with \(p=2\) is not an arbitrary low-dimensional slice: it is the smallest support that can remain nonterminating under the source's finite-termination theorem. The exact recurrence explains how R-linear norm convergence can coexist with exponentially unstable spectral composition, and it forces subsequences toward the finite-termination boundary. The fixed-weight form simultaneously covers standard, harmonic-Ritz, and power-weighted LMSD.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Yang--Yuan, arXiv:2609.12114v1, because its Proposition 11 supplies the algebraic complete-sweep formula used here. Ferrandi--Hochstenbach, DOI 10.1007/s11075-024-01895-9, gives close method and conditioning context. Curtis--Guo, DOI 10.1093/imanum/drx016, gives the established R-linear convergence context. The present claim is limited to exact arithmetic, strictly convex quadratics, fixed positive spectral weights, exactly three active eigenvalues, and \(p=2\).
