# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof identifies \(\mathbb F_p^\times\subset H\), descends \(\chi\) to the nontrivial quotient character on \(H/\mathbb F_p^\times\), and partitions every \(H\)-orbit into projective lines. The trace kernel in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) is exactly one projective line. Additive-character summation over a projective line is \(p-1\) on that trace-zero line and \(-1\) elsewhere. Quotient-character orthogonality then gives either \(0\) or \(p\chi(t_0)\) for every matrix entry. The product condition on orbit classes yields one nonzero entry per row and column, so the determinant magnitude and zero-entry count follow.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked literature and database. Garcia--Karaali--Katz prove a different subfield obstruction requiring \(H\) to lie inside a proper subfield; here \(H\) strictly contains \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) and is too large to lie in \(\mathbb F_p\). Their non-prime-field classifications stop at indices \(2\) and \(3\). The 2023 thesis contains finite index-\(4\) examples but explicitly leaves larger-index characterizations open. Exact-form, alias, implication, and semantic-database searches found no theorem giving the monomial collapse for characters trivial on \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).

## Value
PASS. The result gives a uniform structural obstruction in the first explicitly open larger-index regime, not a single finite census. It identifies a natural character-theoretic condition under which the entire compressed transform collapses to a scaled monomial matrix, simultaneously explaining invertibility, the exact number of zero entries, and failure of the nonvanishing-minors property. For each admissible index it supplies a reusable infinite-family mechanism.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Garcia--Karaali--Katz, arXiv:1807.07648, and Díaz Padilla's 2023 thesis. The theorem does not classify characters that are nontrivial on the prime-field subgroup, and it does not claim exhaustiveness among larger-index failures.
