# A switching-class refinement of the \(p^2\) exceptional-basis obstruction
## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime and let
\[
\mathcal E=(e_0,\ldots,e_{p-1})
\]
be a numerical exceptional basis of \(K_0(\mathbf P^{p-1})\) in the standard orbit generated by numerical mutations, sign changes of basis vectors, and isometries of the Euler form. Write
\[
B_{\mathcal E}=(\chi(e_i,e_j))_{0\le i,j<p}.
\]
Eliseev proves that every such basis satisfies
\[
B_{\mathcal E}\equiv I\pmod p.
\]
Therefore the normalized skew residue
\[
A_{\mathcal E}
=
\frac{B_{\mathcal E}-B_{\mathcal E}^{\mathsf T}}{p}\pmod p
\in M_p(\mathbf F_p)
\]
is well-defined.

The signed-permutation congruence class of \(A_{\mathcal E}\) is constant on the whole standard orbit. More precisely:

\[
\text{mutation:}\quad A\longmapsto P^{\mathsf T}AP,
\]
for an adjacent-transposition permutation matrix \(P\);

\[
\text{sign changes:}\quad A\longmapsto DAD,
\]
for a diagonal matrix \(D\) with entries in \(\{\pm1\}\);

\[
\text{Euler-form isometries:}\quad A\longmapsto A.
\]

Consequently the multiset
\[
\Theta(\mathcal E)
=
\left\{
\left(A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}\right)^2:
\{i,j,k\}\subset\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
\right\}
\]
is an invariant of the standard orbit.

For the Beilinson basis
\[
([\mathcal O],[\mathcal O(1)],\ldots,[\mathcal O(p-1)]),
\]
Eliseev's congruence
\[
\frac1p\chi(\mathcal O(a),\mathcal O(a+d))
\equiv d^{-1}\pmod p
\]
gives, for \(i<j<k\),
\[
A_{ij}=(j-i)^{-1},\qquad
A_{jk}=(k-j)^{-1},\qquad
A_{ki}=-(k-i)^{-1}.
\]
Hence
\[
\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}
=
\left\{
[(j-i)(k-j)(k-i)]^{-2}:
0\le i<j<k\le p-1
\right\}.
\]

The first two cases relevant to Eliseev's new nontransitivity examples are
\[
p=5:\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}=\{1^{\times5},4^{\times5}\},
\]
and
\[
p=7:\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}=\{1^{\times14},2^{\times21}\}.
\]

Thus the \(p^2\)-level obstruction contains a finer switching-class invariant than the nonvanishing of the individual off-diagonal Euler pairings. A candidate numerical exceptional basis can fail the standard-orbit test even when every normalized skew entry is nonzero, simply by having the wrong signed-permutation class or the wrong triangle-square fingerprint.

## Assumptions and scope
The base is \(\mathbf P^{p-1}_{\mathbf C}\), \(p\) is an odd prime, and the Euler lattice is \(K_0(\mathbf P^{p-1})\). The standard orbit is exactly the orbit considered by Eliseev: numerical mutations, independent sign changes of basis vectors, and integral isometries of the Euler form.

The theorem is a numerical statement about the Euler lattice. It does not assert that a numerical exceptional basis lifts to a full exceptional collection, and it does not classify all signed-permutation classes of normalized skew residues.

## Proof
Let
\[
B=B_{\mathcal E}.
\]
Eliseev proves that
\[
B\equiv I\pmod p
\]
throughout the standard orbit. Hence every entry of \(B-B^{\mathsf T}\) is divisible by \(p\), so
\[
A=\frac{B-B^{\mathsf T}}p\pmod p
\]
is well-defined.

Consider a right mutation of the adjacent pair \((e_i,e_{i+1})\). If
\[
m=\chi(e_i,e_{i+1}),
\]
then the new pair is
\[
(e_{i+1},\,e_i-me_{i+1}).
\]
Let \(T\in\operatorname{GL}_p(\mathbf Z)\) be the corresponding basis-change matrix. Since \(m\equiv0\pmod p\),
\[
T\equiv P_i\pmod p,
\]
where \(P_i\) is the permutation matrix of the adjacent transposition \((i,i+1)\). The Gram matrix transforms as
\[
B'=T^{\mathsf T}BT,
\]
so
\[
B'-B'^{\mathsf T}
=
T^{\mathsf T}(B-B^{\mathsf T})T.
\]
After division by \(p\) and reduction modulo \(p\),
\[
A'
=
P_i^{\mathsf T}AP_i.
\]
The same argument applies to a left mutation.

A sign change \(e_i\mapsto-e_i\) is represented by a diagonal matrix \(D\) with entries \(\pm1\). Therefore
\[
B'=D^{\mathsf T}BD=DBD,
\]
and hence
\[
A'=DAD.
\]

If an isometry \(g\) of the Euler form is applied simultaneously to all basis vectors, then
\[
\chi(ge_i,ge_j)=\chi(e_i,e_j)
\]
for every \(i,j\). Thus the Gram matrix, and therefore \(A\), is unchanged.

This proves invariance of the signed-permutation congruence class.

Now consider
\[
\tau_{ijk}=A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki}.
\]
Under \(A\mapsto DAD\), each vertex sign occurs twice in \(\tau_{ijk}\), so \(\tau_{ijk}\) is unchanged. Under a permutation of the indices, an odd permutation of a triple reverses its orientation and changes the sign of \(\tau_{ijk}\); therefore
\[
\tau_{ijk}^2
\]
is independent of the ordering and is merely permuted among the unordered triples. This proves that \(\Theta(\mathcal E)\) is an orbit invariant.

For the standard basis, Eliseev's Lemma 4 gives
\[
\frac1p\binom{p-1+d}{p-1}\equiv d^{-1}\pmod p,
\qquad 1\le d\le p-1.
\]
Because the standard Gram matrix is upper triangular, for \(i<j\)
\[
A_{ij}=(j-i)^{-1},
\qquad
A_{ji}=-(j-i)^{-1}.
\]
Thus for \(i<j<k\),
\[
(A_{ij}A_{jk}A_{ki})^2
=
[(j-i)(k-j)(k-i)]^{-2}.
\]
Direct enumeration of the \(\binom53=10\) and \(\binom73=35\) triples gives the displayed fingerprints for \(p=5\) and \(p=7\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs exact integer and finite-field checks.

It constructs the Beilinson Gram matrix
\[
B_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
\binom{p-1+j-i}{p-1},&i\le j,\\
0,&i>j,
\end{cases}
\]
for \(p=5\) and \(p=7\), verifies \(B\equiv I\pmod p\), constructs the normalized skew matrix \(A\), and checks the closed formula for every entry.

It then enumerates all unordered triples and verifies
\[
\Theta_5=\{1^{\times5},4^{\times5}\},
\qquad
\Theta_7=\{1^{\times14},2^{\times21}\}.
\]

Finally, it executes several exact numerical right mutations of the standard Gram matrix and verifies after each step that the normalized skew residue is obtained by the predicted adjacent permutation modulo \(p\). It separately checks the diagonal-sign conjugation law. The saved output ends in `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite verifier is a replay of the algebra in small prime cases. The general invariance statement is proved symbolically above.

## Relationship to prior work
Eliseev introduces two arithmetic obstructions for the standard orbit of numerical exceptional bases on \(\mathbf P^{p-1}\): squared ranks modulo \(p\), and the fact that every upper-triangular Gram entry has exact \(p\)-adic valuation one. His proof of the latter already records that mutation correction terms disappear modulo \(p^2\), and his categorical argument uses the normalized skew form
\[
\frac{\chi(x,y)-\chi(y,x)}p\pmod p
\]
to rule out mutually orthogonal exceptional objects.

The invariant here retains the whole normalized skew matrix up to its natural signed-permutation action. The inspected source does not formulate this switching class, does not record triangle products or triangle-square fingerprints, and does not give the \(p=5\) or \(p=7\) fingerprint distributions above.

Targeted searches for normalized-skew mutation invariants, triangle products, switching classes, and equivalent Gram-matrix formulations found no covering statement.

## Limitations
The theorem does not determine whether the signed-permutation switching class is a complete invariant of the standard orbit. It also does not construct a new numerical exceptional basis that passes Eliseev's rank and nonvanishing tests but fails only the triangle-square test.

Thus the result should be viewed as a structural refinement of the available \(p^2\)-level data and a new efficiently computable obstruction, not as a classification of numerical exceptional bases.
