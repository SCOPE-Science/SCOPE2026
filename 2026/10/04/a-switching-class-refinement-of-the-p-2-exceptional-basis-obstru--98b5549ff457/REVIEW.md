# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Eliseev's \(B\equiv I\pmod p\) theorem makes the normalized skew residue integral modulo \(p\). Under a numerical mutation the change-of-basis matrix reduces to an adjacent transposition because the mutation coefficient is divisible by \(p\); therefore the normalized skew form is conjugated by that transposition. Sign changes give diagonal \(\{\pm1\}\)-congruence, while Euler-form isometries do not change the Gram matrix. The squared cyclic product on a triple is invariant under both diagonal switching and relabeling. The standard formula follows from Eliseev's Lemma 4, and exact replay gives the stated \(p=5\) and \(p=7\) fingerprints.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating full text was inspected. It proves exact \(p\)-adic order one for the off-diagonal Gram entries and uses the normalized skew pairing in a pairwise categorical obstruction, but it does not formulate the signed-permutation switching class of the whole normalized skew matrix or any triangle-square invariant. Targeted searches for these formulations found no covering statement. The result is therefore a new statement-level refinement of the source's \(p^2\) mechanism, with residual risk from unindexed or differently phrased work explicitly retained.

## Value
**PASS.** The central problem is orbit separation for numerical exceptional bases. This invariant preserves the entire normalized skew matrix up to the natural operation induced by mutations and signs, rather than only its zero pattern. The explicit triangle-square fingerprint is a fast necessary condition for membership in the standard orbit and gives concrete fingerprints in the first two nontransitive dimensions.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Eliseev, arXiv:2609.31413v1, especially Section 2 and Theorem 3. Polishchuk's mod-\(p\) theorem concerns actual exceptional objects and does not supply this matrix invariant. The result does not classify switching classes and does not yet exhibit a numerical exceptional basis separated only by the new fingerprint.
