# A two-adic semigroup decomposition proves the parity conjecture for stable Waring exceptions

## Finding
For each integer \(k\ge2\), let \(\mathbf B^k\) be the stabilized Benfield--Lippard set, and write
\[
a_k=\max \mathbf B^k,
\qquad
b_k=\lvert\mathbf B^k\rvert.
\]
Define
\[
S_k=\left\langle n^k-1:n\ge2\right\rangle,
\qquad
A_k=2^k-1,
\]
and
\[
T_k=
\left\langle
A_k,\frac{u^k-1}{2}:u\ge3\text{ odd}
\right\rangle.
\]

Then
\[
S_k=2T_k\ \sqcup\ \bigl(A_k+2T_k\bigr),
\]
where the union is disjoint because its two parts have opposite parity.

If \(F(T_k)\) and \(g(T_k)\) denote the Frobenius number and genus of \(T_k\), respectively, then
\[
a_k=A_k+2F(T_k)
\]
and
\[
b_k=2g(T_k)+\frac{A_k-1}{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
a_k\equiv b_k\equiv1\pmod2
\]
for every \(k\ge2\). This proves Benfield--Lippard Conjecture 10.4.

## Assumptions and scope
Benfield and Lippard define \(\mathbf B^k\) as the eventual stabilized translate of the sets of positive integers that cannot be written as sums of exactly \(j\) positive \(k\)-th powers. Their Conjecture 10.4 states that \(a_k\) and \(b_k\) are odd for all \(k\).

The argument here is unconditional for every integer \(k\ge2\). It uses only the source definition of \(\mathbf B^k\), elementary additive-semigroup manipulations, and standard Frobenius-number terminology.

## Proof
First identify the stabilized exceptional set with a numerical-semigroup gap set.

For a positive integer \(c\) and any \(j\),
\[
j+c=\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_i^k
\]
with positive integers \(x_i\) if and only if
\[
c=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(x_i^k-1).
\]
Terms with \(x_i=1\) contribute zero. Therefore, once \(j\) is large enough to pad any fixed representation with copies of \(1^k\),
\[
c\notin\mathbf B^k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c\in S_k.
\]
Thus \(\mathbf B^k\) is precisely the positive gap set of \(S_k\).

Next reduce the generators of \(S_k\). For every \(m\ge1\),
\[
(2m)^k-1=(m^k-1)+m^k(2^k-1).
\]
Induction on the power of \(2\) dividing the base shows that every even-base generator belongs to the semigroup generated by \(A_k\) and the odd-base generators \(u^k-1\), with \(u\ge3\) odd. The reverse inclusion is immediate, so
\[
S_k=
\left\langle
A_k,\ u^k-1:u\ge3\text{ odd}
\right\rangle.
\]
Every generator other than \(A_k\) in this presentation is even.

Write an arbitrary element of \(S_k\) as
\[
rA_k+2e,
\]
where \(r\ge0\) and \(e\) belongs to the semigroup generated by the numbers \((u^k-1)/2\) for odd \(u\ge3\). If \(r=2s\), then
\[
rA_k+2e=2(sA_k+e)\in2T_k.
\]
If \(r=2s+1\), then
\[
rA_k+2e=A_k+2(sA_k+e)\in A_k+2T_k.
\]
Conversely, both \(2T_k\) and \(A_k+2T_k\) are visibly contained in \(S_k\). Hence
\[
S_k=2T_k\sqcup(A_k+2T_k).
\]

The semigroup \(T_k\) is numerical. Indeed, \(A_k\ge3\) is odd, and the allowed odd base \(u=A_k\) gives the generator
\[
\frac{A_k^k-1}{2}.
\]
Any common divisor of this generator and \(A_k\) divides both \(A_k\) and \(A_k^k-1\), hence divides \(1\). Thus \(T_k\) contains two coprime positive integers and has finite complement.

Let \(F=F(T_k)\) and \(g=g(T_k)\). The even positive gaps of \(S_k\) are exactly
\[
2t
\]
with \(t\) a positive gap of \(T_k\). They contribute \(g\) gaps and have largest element \(2F\).

For odd numbers, every positive odd integer below \(A_k\) is a gap of \(S_k\). There are
\[
\frac{A_k-1}{2}
\]
such integers. Every odd integer at least \(A_k\) has a unique form
\[
A_k+2t
\]
with \(t\ge0\), and it is a gap of \(S_k\) exactly when \(t\) is a positive gap of \(T_k\). These contribute another \(g\) gaps, with largest element
\[
A_k+2F.
\]

Since \(A_k>0\), the largest gap of \(S_k\) is therefore
\[
a_k=A_k+2F(T_k),
\]
and the total number of positive gaps is
\[
b_k=2g(T_k)+\frac{A_k-1}{2}.
\]
Now \(A_k=2^k-1\) is odd, while
\[
\frac{A_k-1}{2}=2^{k-1}-1
\]
is odd for every \(k\ge2\). Hence both \(a_k\) and \(b_k\) are odd.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently reconstructs the gap sets for \(2\le k\le9\), the range tabulated by Benfield and Lippard. It verifies their published values of \(a_k\) and \(b_k\), constructs the corresponding \(T_k\), and checks
\[
a_k=A_k+2F(T_k),
\qquad
b_k=2g(T_k)+\frac{A_k-1}{2}.
\]
It also verifies a full conductor block after each reported Frobenius number.

These finite computations are regression checks only. The theorem for all \(k\ge2\) is supplied by the symbolic proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Benfield and Lippard's current arXiv version explicitly states Conjecture 10.4 and tabulates odd values of \(a_k\) and \(b_k\) for \(2\le k\le9\).

A later published research record identifies \(\mathbf B^k\) with the gaps of
\[
\langle n^k-1:n\ge2\rangle
\]
and uses that model to correct a different modular lower-bound theorem. That equivalence is consistent with, and supplies an alternative route to, the first step above, but its indexed claim does not contain the parity decomposition
\[
S_k=2T_k\sqcup(A_k+2T_k),
\]
the formulas for \(F(S_k)\) and \(g(S_k)\), or a proof of Conjecture 10.4.

Another published computation determines the tenth-power obstruction semigroup and obtains the special identity
\[
a_{10}=2b_{10}-1.
\]
That finite \(k=10\) result is compatible with the theorem here but does not imply parity for arbitrary \(k\).

Exact searches for Conjecture 10.4, the phrases “\(a_k\) and \(b_k\) are odd” and “numerical semigroup generated by \(n^k-1\),” and the stronger Frobenius/genus formulas did not locate a prior general proof.

## Limitations
The result proves only the parity assertion of Conjecture 10.4. It does not prove Benfield--Lippard Conjectures 10.1, 10.2, 10.3, 10.5, or 10.6.

The formulas reduce \(a_k\) and \(b_k\) to invariants of \(T_k\), but they do not provide closed forms or asymptotics for those invariants.

Search non-detection is not a proof of novelty. A residual risk remains that an unindexed numerical-semigroup source contains the same parity decomposition.
