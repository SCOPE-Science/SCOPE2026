# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The norm is a maximum of three absolute linear functionals whose first two normals are independent, so it is a genuine norm. The maximization of the published upper-bound profile at \(p=4\) reduces to the sign of \(1-8v-v^2\), giving the unique maximizer \(v=\sqrt{17}-4\). The explicit unit pair is isosceles, and the chosen \(\lambda_0\) makes the ratio equal to the universal upper bound. The third strip is shown to be nonredundant while not disturbing the extremizing configuration. A compactness/supporting-line argument proves that every non-uniformly-non-square Banach plane has a parallelogram unit ball, so the resulting hexagonal plane is uniformly non-square.

## Value
PASS. The finding answers the source paper’s explicit conjecture negatively at the first algebraically convenient superquadratic exponent. The construction also isolates the structural reason the proposed characterization fails: an extremizing \(p\)-isosceles configuration can survive a corner cut that restores uniform non-squareness. This is a motivated boundary result about the exact equality case of a newly introduced Banach-space invariant, not a routine recomputation.

The closest literature is the 2024 Baronti--Bertella paper itself, whose Theorem 5.1 supplies the sharp universal upper bound and whose Conjecture 6 predicts the opposite classification. Later search hits concern different isosceles-orthogonality constants and do not dominate the stated claim. Residual risk is that an unindexed or very recent source may contain the same counterexample.
