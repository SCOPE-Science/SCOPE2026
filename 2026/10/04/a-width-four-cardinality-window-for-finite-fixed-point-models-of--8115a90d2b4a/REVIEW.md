# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The lower bound is a direct contradiction argument. Every fixed-point poset with at most \(9\) elements is connectedly collapsible by the Rutkowski small-set result as explicitly restated by Schröder. Baclawski's collapse implications make its order complex contractible, whereas any finite \(T_0\)-space weakly equivalent to \(S^1\) has first integral homology \(\mathbb Z\) by McCord's theorem. Barmak's explicit \(14\)-point example supplies the upper bound.

## Originality
PASS, with a stated residual risk. Searches were made for the invariant itself, the finite-poset/order-complex formulation, the numerical interval, and aliases involving weak circle type and the fixed point property. The closest literature supplies the two sides separately: Rutkowski/Baclawski give the small-cardinality obstruction, while Barmak gives the \(14\)-point witness. No inspected source states the combined interval. Because failed search cannot establish novelty, the claim is presented as a literature synthesis rather than as a priority claim.

## Value
PASS. The invariant is a natural quantitative refinement of the existence theorem for fixed-point representatives of weak homotopy types. For the circle, the bound confines the exact minimum to the five cardinalities \(10,11,12,13,14\), turning an open-ended minimization question into a finite classification/search problem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary sources are Rutkowski's *The fixed point property for small sets* (1989), Baclawski's *A combinatorial proof of a fixed point property* (2012), and Barmak's *The fixed point property in every weak homotopy type* (arXiv:1307.1722, 2013). McCord's finite-space theorem supplies the standard order-complex weak equivalence. The direct Rutkowski full text was unavailable; the exact at-most-nine connected-collapse statement was therefore checked through Schröder's explicit restatement. The exact minimum remains unresolved.
