# A2Grad-inc has a sharp \(\sqrt{3}\) alternating-gradient scaling advantage
## Finding

A2Grad was designed to combine Nesterov acceleration with adaptive diagonal scaling, and its defining paper proposes three adaptive-scale variants: uniform, quadratically weighted incremental, and exponential. The paper describes the quadratic weighting as a more aggressive nonuniform moving average. On the canonical period-two gradient signal, that aggressiveness has an exact asymptotic constant.

Consider one coordinate of the practical empirical-variance heuristic in the defining paper. Let
\[
G_k=(-1)^kG,
\qquad
G\ne0,
\]
and estimate the mean gradient by
\[
\widetilde g_k
=
\frac1{k+1}
\sum_{\tau=0}^k G_\tau.
\]
Define
\[
\delta_k
=
G_k-\widetilde g_k.
\]
Then
\[
\delta_{2n+1}=-G,
\]
while
\[
\delta_{2n}
=
\frac{2n}{2n+1}G.
\]
Thus
\[
\delta_k^2\longrightarrow G^2.
\]

Use the source parameter schedule
\[
\gamma_k=\frac{2L}{k+1},
\qquad
\beta_k=\beta>0,
\]
and the practical unprojected coordinate update
\[
\Delta x_k
=
-\frac{G_k}{\gamma_k+\beta h_k}.
\]

For A2Grad-uni,
\[
v_k
=
\sum_{\tau=0}^k\delta_\tau^2,
\qquad
h_k=\sqrt{v_k}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{v_k}{k}\longrightarrow G^2,
\qquad
\frac{h_k}{\sqrt{k}}\longrightarrow |G|.
\]

For A2Grad-inc,
\[
v_k
=
\frac{k^2}{(k+1)^2}v_{k-1}
+
\delta_k^2,
\qquad
h_k=\sqrt{v_k}.
\]
This recurrence has the exact unrolling
\[
v_k
=
\frac1{(k+1)^2}
\sum_{\tau=0}^k
(\tau+1)^2\delta_\tau^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{v_k}{k}
\longrightarrow
\frac{G^2}{3},
\qquad
\frac{h_k}{\sqrt{k}}
\longrightarrow
\frac{|G|}{\sqrt{3}}.
\]

For A2Grad-exp, let
\[
\widetilde v_0=\delta_0^2,
\]
\[
\widetilde v_k
=
\rho\widetilde v_{k-1}
+
(1-\rho)\delta_k^2,
\qquad
0<\rho<1,
\]
and
\[
v_k=\max\{\widetilde v_k,v_{k-1}\},
\qquad
h_k=\sqrt{(k+1)v_k}.
\]
Since
\[
\delta_k^2\longrightarrow G^2,
\]
the stable exponential recursion satisfies
\[
\widetilde v_k\longrightarrow G^2.
\]
The monotone envelope is bounded above by \(G^2\) and dominates \(\widetilde v_k\), so
\[
v_k\longrightarrow G^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{h_k}{\sqrt{k}}\longrightarrow |G|.
\]

Because
\[
\gamma_k
=
O(k^{-1}),
\]
whereas
\[
h_k
=
\Theta(\sqrt{k}),
\]
the adaptive term dominates the denominator asymptotically. Therefore
\[
\sqrt{k}\,|\Delta x_k|
\longrightarrow
\frac1\beta
\]
for A2Grad-uni and A2Grad-exp, while
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{k}\,|\Delta x_k|
\longrightarrow
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\beta}
}
\]
for A2Grad-inc.

Thus the quadratic-weight incremental scheme has an exact asymptotic inner-step magnitude larger by
\[
\sqrt{3}
\]
than both the uniform and exponential variants on a persistent alternating gradient. The factor is independent of the signal amplitude \(G\), the Lipschitz parameter \(L\), and, for A2Grad-exp, the exponential smoothing parameter \(\rho\).

## Assumptions and scope

The result uses the practical empirical-mean heuristic stated in the defining A2Grad paper:
\[
\widetilde g_k
=
\frac1{k+1}
\sum_{\tau=0}^kG_\tau,
\qquad
\delta_k=G_k-\widetilde g_k.
\]

The scale recurrences are exactly those of A2Grad-uni, A2Grad-inc, and A2Grad-exp in Algorithms 4--6. The coordinate update is the \(x\)-update in the paper's practical rewritten Algorithm 7.

The source schedule
\[
\gamma_k=\frac{2L}{k+1}
\]
and constant
\[
\beta_k=\beta>0
\]
are retained.

The gradient sequence is an externally prescribed period-two input-response diagnostic. It need not be generated by a single fixed objective.

The theorem compares the adaptive \(x\)-step. It does not claim that the complete accelerated state, objective values, or stochastic training trajectories differ by the same factor.

## Proof

For the alternating input,
\[
\sum_{\tau=0}^kG_\tau
=
\begin{cases}
G,&k\text{ even},\\
0,&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\[
\widetilde g_{2n}
=
\frac{G}{2n+1},
\qquad
\widetilde g_{2n+1}=0,
\]
which gives the displayed formulas for \(\delta_k\).

For A2Grad-uni,
\[
v_k=\sum_{\tau=0}^k\delta_\tau^2.
\]
Since \(\delta_\tau^2\to G^2\), ordinary Cesàro convergence gives
\[
v_k/k\to G^2.
\]

For A2Grad-inc, repeatedly substitute
\[
v_j
=
\frac{j^2}{(j+1)^2}v_{j-1}
+
\delta_j^2.
\]
The coefficient multiplying \(\delta_\tau^2\) in \(v_k\) is
\[
\prod_{j=\tau+1}^{k}
\frac{j^2}{(j+1)^2}
=
\frac{(\tau+1)^2}{(k+1)^2},
\]
which proves the exact unrolling. Since
\[
\frac1{k^3}
\sum_{\tau=0}^k(\tau+1)^2
\longrightarrow
\frac13
\]
and
\[
\delta_\tau^2/G^2\longrightarrow1,
\]
weighted Cesàro convergence yields
\[
v_k/k\to G^2/3.
\]

For A2Grad-exp, put
\[
e_k=\widetilde v_k-G^2.
\]
Then
\[
e_k
=
\rho e_{k-1}
+
(1-\rho)(\delta_k^2-G^2).
\]
The forcing term tends to zero, so the stable scalar linear recursion gives
\[
e_k\to0.
\]
Moreover
\[
0\le\delta_k^2\le G^2
\]
for every \(k\), and the exponential average starts at zero, hence
\[
0\le\widetilde v_k\le G^2.
\]
The envelope \(v_k\) is nondecreasing, remains at most \(G^2\), and is at least \(\widetilde v_k\), so its limit is also \(G^2\).

Finally,
\[
\frac{\gamma_k}{\sqrt{k}}
=
\frac{2L}{(k+1)\sqrt{k}}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Divide the update denominator by \(\sqrt{k}\) and substitute the three limits for \(h_k/\sqrt{k}\). The signal magnitude \(|G|\) cancels, producing the constants
\[
1/\beta,
\qquad
\sqrt{3}/\beta,
\qquad
1/\beta.
\]

## Relationship to prior work

Deng, Cheng, and Lan introduced A2Grad as an adaptive accelerated stochastic-gradient framework and derived uniform, quadratically weighted incremental, and exponential moving-average scale rules. Their paper explicitly emphasizes that momentum permits nonuniform and aggressive moving averages, and notes that the incremental scheme gives earlier scales relative weights decaying quadratically.

For practical implementation, the paper proposes estimating the stochastic-gradient error by subtracting the running empirical mean of past stochastic gradients. It also rewrites the adaptive coordinate update as
\[
x_{k+1}
=
x_k
-
\frac{G_k}{\gamma_k+\beta_kh_k}.
\]

The inspected full text gives convergence-rate guarantees and experimental comparisons among A2Grad-uni, A2Grad-inc, and A2Grad-exp, but it does not state their response to a period-two gradient, the constants
\[
1,
\qquad
1/\sqrt{3},
\qquad
1
\]
for their asymptotic scale normalization, or the resulting exact \(\sqrt{3}\) inner-step separation.

Focused published-record and web searches for A2Grad alternating gradients, periodic gradients, high-frequency response, and the quadratic-weight scheme did not identify an implication-equivalent result.

Nearby optimizer analyses of alternating gradients concern different moment recurrences, so their gain formulas do not imply the A2Grad weighted-innovation calculation here.

## Limitations

The period-two signal is a canonical high-frequency diagnostic, not a model of every stochastic-gradient process.

The theorem concerns the adaptive \(x\)-update, not the full accelerated output sequence.

The practical empirical mean is a heuristic substitute for the unavailable full-gradient error used in the paper's theoretical development.

The result does not assert that the larger A2Grad-inc step is uniformly better or worse for optimization.
