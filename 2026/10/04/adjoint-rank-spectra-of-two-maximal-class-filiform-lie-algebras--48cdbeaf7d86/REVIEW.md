# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(L_n(q)\), the coefficient of \(e_1\) gives a rank-\(n-2\) stratum, its zero hyperplane splits into one rank-one stratum and the one-dimensional center, and the counts exhaust the algebra. For \(M_n(q)\), triangular leading terms give ranks \(n-2\) and \(n-3\) when the first or second coefficient is the first nonzero leading coefficient; after both vanish, a minimal-index argument gives rank two unless only the penultimate basis coefficient survives, yielding rank one, with the center giving rank zero. Rank-nullity then gives the two commutativity formulas. The packaged checker reconstructs the brackets over three finite fields, including \(\mathbf F_4\), and verifies Jacobi and all rank multiplicities.

## Originality
PASS. The nearest primary paper gives the general averaging identity and computes the five-dimensional standard-filiform boundary \(L_{5,7}\), but not an all-dimensional adjoint-rank spectrum. Its later follow-up treats class-three algebras with derived dimension two, which cannot cover the present \(n\ge6\) maximal-class families. Searches using both \(\mathfrak m_0\)/\(\mathfrak m_2\) notation and commutativity-degree, commuting-probability, centralizer and adjoint-rank terminology found no equivalent result. The already-covered \(n=5\) specialization is explicitly excluded from the originality domain.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives complete centralizer statistics for two canonical maximal-class families, not merely a single numerical value. It shows that the element commutativity degree separates the families in every dimension in the stated range and that their fixed-field asymptotic levels differ by two powers of \(q\). This supplies a natural benchmark for a recently introduced invariant on standard filiform objects.

Residual bibliographic risk remains that an equivalent centralizer calculation may appear under different filiform notation. The result does not claim novelty for the general existence of asymptotic values \(q^{-2}\) or \(q^{-4}\).
