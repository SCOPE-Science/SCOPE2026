# Affine first integral forces a two-neutral Lyapunov spectrum in a memristive Sprott-A flow
## Finding
Consider the smooth flow
\[
\dot x=a y,\qquad
\dot y=-x+y[bF(z)+cG(w)],\qquad
\dot z=\varepsilon-y^2,\qquad
\dot w=y,
\]
where \(a\ne0\), \(\varepsilon>0\), \(F(z)=z+\alpha\sin z\), and \(G(w)=\beta+3\xi w^2\). The quantity
\[
I=w-\frac{x}{a}
\]
is an exact first integral. Hence every trajectory is confined to one affine three-dimensional leaf \(I=K\).

For every compactly supported ergodic invariant probability measure \(\mu\),
\[
\int y^2\,d\mu=\varepsilon.
\]
Moreover, the four-dimensional Lyapunov spectrum contains zero with multiplicity at least two. One zero exponent is generated by time translation along the nonvanishing vector field inside the invariant leaf. A second zero exponent comes from the identity action on the one-dimensional quotient by the tangent bundle of that leaf. Therefore, whenever the Lyapunov sum is negative, at most one exponent can be positive; such a measure cannot be dissipative hyperchaos in the standard sense of two positive exponents.

At the published parameter \(a=0.05\), the first integral is \(I=w-20x\). The two initial conditions highlighted in the source, \((0.1,0,0,0)\) and \((0.1,0.2,0.3,0)\), both have \(I=-2\). They therefore evolve on the same exact three-dimensional leaf \(w=20x-2\), despite being used to illustrate different chaotic classifications.

## Assumptions and scope
The result assumes the printed smooth vector field of Adelakun and Odusote, with \(a\ne0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). The Lyapunov statement concerns compactly supported ergodic invariant probability measures at Oseledec-regular points. The conclusion excluding two positive exponents is conditional on negative Lyapunov sum; no claim is made that every invariant measure of the model has negative sum.

The source article uses \(a=0.05\), \(b=0.1\), \(c=0.01\), \(\alpha=25\), \(\varepsilon=1\), \(\beta=-6\times10^{-4}\), and \(\xi=5\times10^{-4}\) for a principal example. The affine reduction does not depend on \(b,c,\alpha,\beta,\xi\).

## Proof
Differentiate \(I=w-x/a\) along the flow:
\[
\dot I=\dot w-\frac{1}{a}\dot x=y-y=0.
\]
Thus every orbit remains on exactly one leaf \(I=K\). In the coordinates
\[
(u,v,r,K)=\left(x,y,z,w-\frac{x}{a}\right),
\]
the system becomes
\[
\dot u=av,\qquad
\dot v=-u+v\left[bF(r)+cG\left(\frac{u}{a}+K\right)\right],\qquad
\dot r=\varepsilon-v^2,\qquad
\dot K=0.
\]
The tangent bundle \(E=\{\delta K=0\}\) is therefore invariant under the derivative cocycle, while the induced cocycle on the one-dimensional quotient is exactly the identity. Hence zero occurs as a quotient Lyapunov exponent.

Because \(\varepsilon>0\), the vector field has no equilibrium: stationarity would force \(y=0\) from \(\dot x=0\) and then \(\dot z=\varepsilon>0\), a contradiction. On the compact support of an invariant measure the vector field is therefore bounded above and bounded away from zero. The identity \(D\phi_t(X(p))=X(\phi_t(p))\) then gives a zero Lyapunov exponent in the invariant leaf tangent bundle \(E\). For a block triangular cocycle, the Lyapunov spectrum is the multiset union of the spectrum on \(E\) and the spectrum of the quotient, so the full spectrum has at least two zero exponents.

For a compactly supported invariant probability measure, invariance applied to the bounded coordinate observable \(z\) gives
\[
0=\int \dot z\,d\mu=\varepsilon-\int y^2\,d\mu,
\]
which proves \(\int y^2\,d\mu=\varepsilon\).

Finally, a four-dimensional spectrum with at least two zeros has at most two nonzero entries. If its total sum is negative, those two entries cannot both be positive, so there is at most one positive exponent. At \(a=0.05=1/20\), \(I=w-20x\), and direct substitution of each displayed source initial condition gives \(I=-2\).

## Verification
The bundled script `verify.py` symbolically differentiates the claimed first integral, performs the affine coordinate substitution, verifies that the transformed fourth equation is identically zero, checks the no-equilibrium contradiction for \(\varepsilon>0\), and verifies that both displayed source initial conditions have \(I=-2\) at \(a=0.05\). Running `python3 verify.py` returns `VERIFY_OK`.

The two-neutral Lyapunov conclusion is analytic rather than numerical: it uses the invariant tangent subbundle, identity quotient cocycle, and the nonvanishing flow direction on compact support. No finite-time numerical exponent is used as proof of an asymptotic Lyapunov statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Adelakun and Odusote define the four-dimensional system and describe it as a memristive Sprott-A hyperchaotic generator with no equilibrium, including finite-time Lyapunov classifications, dissipativity language, and the two highlighted initial conditions. Their displayed equations contain the exact proportionality \(\dot x=a\dot w\), but the inspected article does not state the resulting conserved affine quantity or the induced leaf reduction.

A 2020 comment by Szumiński, Przybylska, and Maciejewski explains the general principle that autonomous symmetries and conservation laws force neutral Lyapunov exponents and can invalidate numerical hyperchaos classifications. That work treats constrained Hamiltonian systems, not the Adelakun–Odusote memristive Sprott-A flow, and therefore does not imply the present first integral, the exact leaf \(w=x/a+K\), the stationary law \(\int y^2\,d\mu=\varepsilon\), or the fact that the source's two highlighted initial conditions lie on the same leaf.

Semantic and web searches for the source title, DOI, the equation pair \(\dot x=ay\), \(\dot w=y\), and aliases such as conserved quantity, first integral, invariant leaf, and Lyapunov neutral exponent did not locate a published correction of this exact system. This search evidence reduces but does not eliminate the possibility of unindexed prior coverage.

## Limitations
The theorem does not rule out two positive exponents for an arbitrary non-dissipative invariant measure: the final exclusion uses negative Lyapunov sum. It also does not determine whether the reduced three-dimensional leaf dynamics is chaotic, periodic, quasiperiodic, or multistable for any particular parameter set. The stationary identity is proved only for invariant probability measures for which the stated coordinate observables are integrable; compact support is a sufficient condition used here.

The literature search was not exhaustive across every language, database, thesis, or unpublished manuscript. The source's numerical Lyapunov values were not recomputed; the finding instead identifies exact structural constraints that any asymptotic spectrum must satisfy.
