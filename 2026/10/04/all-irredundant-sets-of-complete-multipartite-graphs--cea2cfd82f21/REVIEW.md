# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the private-neighborhood definition directly. Any one-part set is independent and therefore self-private. If an irredundant set meets more than one part, a selected vertex must use an external private vertex; the complete-multipartite adjacency pattern forces every other selected vertex into the private vertex's part. Applying the same argument from the opposite part collapses the set to a two-vertex transversal, and the existence of the two external private vertices forces both parts to be non-singleton. Maximality and the polynomial counts then follow from the classification. Literal exhaustive verification through order \(9\) agrees on every tested subset and on both extremal parameters.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational irredundance sources define the parameter and general inequalities but do not expose the complete-multipartite all-set classification in the inspected material. The closest full-text theorem found is Alon–Defant's direct-product result: for a product of \(n\) complete multipartite factors, an irredundant set is an independent set plus at most \(2^n\) exceptional vertices. For a single factor that is strictly weaker than the exact classification here and does not imply the maximal-set or polynomial formulas. Exact semantic searches in the published finding index returned no statement-equivalent complete-multipartite irredundance result. Generic irredundance-polynomial references define the counting objects but do not give this family formula.

## Value
PASS. Irredundance is a foundational domination-chain invariant, and maximal irredundant-set enumeration remains a distinct structural problem. The theorem gives a natural complete classification on a basic graph family, not merely a scalar value: it determines every irredundant set, every maximal irredundant set, two standard counting polynomials, and both lower and upper irredundance numbers. It also sharpens the one-factor case suggested by the later direct-product theorem.

The main residual risk is bibliographic: the full 1979 article was not available in the inspected lawful sources, so an unindexed older family-specific statement cannot be ruled out solely by search.
