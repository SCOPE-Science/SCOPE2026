# All logical symmetries of a binary bipartite box world are operational relabelings

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal L_{N,M}\) be the concrete quantum logic of a bipartite non-signalling box world with \(N\ge2\) binary-output inputs for Alice and \(M\ge2\) binary-output inputs for Bob. Its atoms are the elementary propositions
\[
[i,a;j,b],
\qquad
1\le i\le N,\quad
1\le j\le M,\quad
a,b\in\{0,1\}.
\]
An automorphism means an automorphism of the orthomodular poset: it preserves the order, orthocomplementation, and hence orthogonality.

Then every automorphism is operational. More precisely,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{N,M})
\cong
(C_2\wr S_N)\times(C_2\wr S_M)
\]
when \(N\ne M\). When \(N=M\), there is one further symmetry interchanging Alice and Bob, so
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{N,N})
\cong
\bigl((C_2\wr S_N)\times(C_2\wr S_N)\bigr)\rtimes C_2,
\]
where the final \(C_2\) exchanges the two factors.

Equivalently, the complete list of logical symmetries consists of:

1. a permutation of Alice's \(N\) inputs;
2. an independent swap of the two outputs for each Alice input;
3. a permutation of Bob's \(M\) inputs;
4. an independent swap of the two outputs for each Bob input;
5. if \(N=M\), an optional interchange of the two parties.

Consequently,
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{N,M})|
=
2^{N+M}N!M!
\]
for \(N\ne M\), while
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{N,N})|
=
2^{2N+1}(N!)^2.
\]

For the familiar two-input, two-output case, this gives
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{2,2})|=128.
\]
Thus the abstract propositional logic has no hidden symmetries beyond the relabelings that are already operationally available in the Bell scenario.

## Assumptions and scope

The result concerns exactly two parties, at least two inputs on each side, and exactly two outcomes for every input. It uses the concrete box-world logic generated by the elementary events as defined by Tylec, Kuś, and Krajczok.

The theorem is about automorphisms of the full orthomodular poset, not merely affine symmetries of the non-signalling state polytope, symmetries of a selected Bell inequality, or automorphisms of a smaller exclusivity graph containing only some events.

The restrictions \(N,M\ge2\) are essential to the stated group formula. If one party has only one input, the atom orthogonality graph becomes degenerate and has additional graph symmetries; those boundary cases are not included.

## Proof

Write
\[
X=\{(i,a):1\le i\le N,\ a\in\{0,1\}\},
\qquad
Y=\{(j,b):1\le j\le M,\ b\in\{0,1\}\}.
\]
Each set carries a fixed-point-free involution that flips the outcome while keeping the input:
\[
\overline{(i,a)}=(i,1-a),
\qquad
\overline{(j,b)}=(j,1-b).
\]

The atoms of \(\mathcal L_{N,M}\) are naturally \(X\times Y\). The concrete construction gives
\[
(x,y)\perp(x',y')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x'=\bar x\ \text{or}\ y'=\bar y.
\]
Because atoms are exactly the elements covering \(0\), every logic automorphism permutes the atoms. Since orthogonality is definable by
\[
p\perp q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\le q^\perp,
\]
the induced atom permutation is an automorphism of this orthogonality graph.

For an atom \(v=(x,y)\), define its closed non-orthogonality set
\[
C(v)=\{v\}\cup\{w:w\not\perp v\}.
\]
The displayed orthogonality rule gives the exact rectangle
\[
C(x,y)=
\bigl(X\setminus\{\bar x\}\bigr)
\times
\bigl(Y\setminus\{\bar y\}\bigr).
\]
For distinct \(v=(x,y)\) and \(w=(x',y')\), the intersection size is therefore
\[
|C(v)\cap C(w)|
=
\begin{cases}
(2N-1)(2M-2),&x=x',\ y\ne y',\\
(2N-2)(2M-1),&x\ne x',\ y=y',\\
(2N-2)(2M-2),&x\ne x',\ y\ne y'.
\end{cases}
\]
Since \(N,M\ge2\), the last value is strictly smaller than the first two. Hence the atom graph itself recovers the relation
\[
v\sim_R w
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x=x'\ \text{or}\ y=y'.
\]
The graph of \(\sim_R\) is the rook graph on the \(2N\) by \(2M\) array \(X\times Y\). Its maximal cliques are exactly the \(2N\) rows
\[
\{x\}\times Y
\]
and the \(2M\) columns
\[
X\times\{y\}.
\]

If \(N\ne M\), row and column clique sizes are different, so every automorphism preserves the two families separately. If \(N=M\), an automorphism may preserve the two families or interchange them. In either case, after an optional global interchange when \(N=M\), every atom-graph automorphism has the form
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(\pi_X(x),\pi_Y(y))
\]
for permutations \(\pi_X\) of \(X\) and \(\pi_Y\) of \(Y\).

It remains to recover the binary-output pairings. Inside a fixed row \(\{x\}\times Y\),
\[
(x,y)\perp(x,y')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y'=\bar y.
\]
Therefore \(\pi_Y\) must preserve the \(M\) disjoint pairs
\[
\{y,\bar y\}.
\]
The full permutation group preserving those pairs is
\[
C_2\wr S_M:
\]
one may permute the \(M\) input pairs and independently flip each pair. The same argument in a fixed column shows
\[
\pi_X\in C_2\wr S_N.
\]

Thus every logic automorphism restricts on atoms to one of the operational relabelings listed in the finding, with an optional party interchange only when \(N=M\).

Conversely, every such relabeling preserves elementary-event orthogonality and sends the generating family of concrete events to itself. It therefore preserves complements and disjoint unions and extends to an automorphism of the generated logic. Since the logic is atomistic, an automorphism is determined by its action on atoms. This proves both the group isomorphism and the order formula.

## Verification

`verify_box_logic_symmetry.py` reconstructs the atom orthogonality graph directly from the binary event rule. For \(2\le N,M\le5\), it verifies the three possible closed-nonorthogonality intersection numbers, reconstructs the rook rows and columns from graph data alone, verifies the binary matching inside every recovered row and column, and checks every generator of the proposed relabeling group.

For the square cases it also verifies the party-swap automorphism. The script checks the group-order formula, including
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal L_{2,2})|=128.
\]
The finite computation is supplementary; the theorem for arbitrary \(N,M\ge2\) is proved above.

## Relationship to prior work

Tylec and Kuś reconstructed the propositional logic of the binary two-input box world and emphasized that it is a finite orthomodular poset rather than the projection lattice of a two-qubit system. Their concrete model has \(82\) propositions in the \(N=M=2\) case.

Tylec, Kuś, and Krajczok subsequently gave the concrete construction for arbitrary finite input and output sets. Their Observation 10 states that every proposition is an empty set or a finite union of mutually disjoint elementary events, and they give the elementary-event disjointness criterion used in this proof. A later paper by Tylec and Kuś identifies the two-box construction as a tensor product of the one-box logics.

Bell-scenario literature routinely quotients inequalities and behaviors by operational relabelings of parties, inputs, and outputs. Rosset's work on symmetries of Bell correlation scenarios formalizes these relabeling groups. The present statement is different: it identifies the full automorphism group of the *propositional logic itself* and proves that no extra abstract logical automorphisms exist.

Targeted searches for box-world logic automorphisms, the \(82\)-element logic symmetry group, and automorphisms of the full binary Bell-event orthogonality structure did not locate the displayed classification. General discussions of Bell relabelings establish a natural subgroup but do not, in the inspected material, prove that it exhausts all automorphisms of the Tylec--Kuś logic.

## Limitations

The proof relies strongly on binary outcomes. With three or more outcomes, the local contradiction relation is no longer a perfect matching, and the reconstruction argument changes.

The theorem also does not assert that every affine automorphism of the non-signalling state polytope is induced by a logic automorphism; that is a different object. Likewise, it does not classify symmetries of individual Bell inequalities, which may have proper stabilizer subgroups.

A residual literature risk remains because Bell-scenario relabeling groups are standard and the same no-hidden-symmetry statement may appear under exclusivity-graph or event-hypergraph terminology. No such statement for the full box-world propositional logic was located in the inspected sources.
