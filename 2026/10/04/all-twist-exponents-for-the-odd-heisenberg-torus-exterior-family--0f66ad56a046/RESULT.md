# All twist exponents for the odd Heisenberg torus-exterior family
## Finding
Let \(k>1\) be odd. For the doubly-null Kanenobu--Kazama torus exterior used in the torus-replacement construction, its complement group has the form
\[
G_k\cong H_k\rtimes_\phi\mathbb Z,
\qquad
H_k=\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb Z_k),
\]
where
\[
H_k=
\langle A,B,C\mid A^k=B^k=C^k=1,\ C=[A,B],\ [A,C]=[B,C]=1\rangle.
\]

Let \(M_n\) denote the \(n\)-twisted meridional quotient for \(n\ge1\). Then
\[
\pi_1(M_n)\cong
\begin{cases}
H_k\rtimes_\phi C_n, & 6\mid n,\\[4pt]
C_3\rtimes_{-1}C_n, &
n\equiv2,4\pmod6\ \text{and}\ 3\mid k,\\[4pt]
C_n, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In the middle case, a generator of \(C_n\) acts on \(C_3\) by inversion.

Thus the finite nonabelian groups arising from this odd-\(k\) family have a sharp congruence trichotomy. Multiples of six retain the full Heisenberg kernel of order \(k^3\); residues \(2\) and \(4\) retain exactly a \(C_3\) kernel when \(3\mid k\); every other positive twist exponent kills the kernel completely.

## Assumptions and scope
The construction and notation are those of the recent torus-exterior replacement paper. Its general quotient lemma states that if
\[
G=N\rtimes_\phi\mathbb Z
\]
and the meridian generates the displayed \(\mathbb Z\)-factor, then imposing the relation \(\mu^n=1\) gives
\[
N_n\rtimes_{\bar\phi}C_n,
\qquad
N_n=
N/
\left\langle\!\left\langle
\phi^n(g)g^{-1}:g\in N
\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.
\]

The odd-\(k\) case is the one in which the normal subgroup is the finite Heisenberg group \(H_k\) above. The claim concerns \(n\ge1\). The untwisted value \(n=0\) is not included.

The primary subject is higher-dimensional knot theory: the objects are knotted tori in \(S^4\) and their exterior groups.

## Proof
The classical Kanenobu--Kazama presentation records the meridional conjugation on generators of the Heisenberg subgroup:
\[
\phi(A)=B,
\qquad
\phi(B)=A^{-1}B.
\]
Because \(C=[A,B]\) is central,
\[
\phi(C)
=
[B,A^{-1}B]
=
C.
\]
A direct iteration gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
r&\phi^r(A)&\phi^r(B)\\
\hline
0&A&B\\
1&B&A^{-1}B\\
2&A^{-1}B&A^{-1}C^{-1}\\
3&A^{-1}C^{-1}&B^{-1}C^{-1}\\
4&B^{-1}C^{-1}&AB^{-1}\\
5&AB^{-1}&A\\
6&A&B.
\end{array}
\]
Hence \(\phi^6=1\), agreeing with the period used in the recent source.

Write
\[
(H_k)_n
=
H_k/
\left\langle\!\left\langle
\phi^n(g)g^{-1}:g\in H_k
\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.
\]
Only \(r=n\bmod6\) matters.

If \(r=0\), there are no additional relations in \(H_k\), so
\[
(H_k)_n=H_k.
\]

If \(r=1\), the relation \(\phi(A)=A\) gives \(B=A\), while \(\phi(B)=B\) then gives
\[
A^{-1}B=B,
\]
so \(A=B=1\), hence also \(C=1\). Thus \((H_k)_n=1\). The case \(r=5\) is equivalent because the coinvariants of an automorphism and its inverse are the same.

If \(r=2\), the relation \(\phi^2(A)=A\) gives
\[
B=A^2.
\]
The relation \(\phi^2(B)=B\) gives
\[
A^{-1}C^{-1}=B.
\]
Since \(B=A^2\), the commutator relation forces
\[
C=[A,A^2]=1,
\]
and then \(A^3=1\). Together with \(A^k=1\), this leaves a cyclic group of order
\[
\gcd(k,3).
\]
Thus
\[
(H_k)_n\cong C_{\gcd(k,3)}.
\]
The case \(r=4\) is the same because \(\phi^4=\phi^{-2}\).

If \(r=3\), the relations are
\[
A^{-1}C^{-1}=A,
\qquad
B^{-1}C^{-1}=B.
\]
Hence
\[
A^2=B^2=C^{-1}.
\]
Since \(A^2\) is central, the class-two commutator identity gives
\[
[A^2,B]=C^2=1.
\]
But \(C\) has odd order dividing the odd integer \(k\), so \(C=1\). Then \(A^2=B^2=1\), and odd order again forces \(A=B=1\). Therefore \((H_k)_n=1\).

It remains to identify the descended action when \(r=2\) or \(4\) and \(3\mid k\). The quotient is generated by the image of \(A\) with \(A^3=1\), and \(B=A^2=A^{-1}\). Therefore
\[
\bar\phi(A)=B=A^{-1}.
\]
Since \(n\) is even in residues \(2\) and \(4\), inversion defines an action of \(C_n\) on \(C_3\). Applying the general quotient lemma gives exactly the three cases in the statement.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and uses only the source quotient lemma, the source meridional action, and class-two Heisenberg identities.

The bundled checker independently implements
\[
H_k=\operatorname{UT}_3(\mathbb Z_k)
\]
in normal coordinates, computes normal closures of the coinvariant relations for every residue modulo six, and verifies the quotient orders for
\[
k\in\{3,5,7,9,11,15\}.
\]
It also checks \(\phi^6=1\) on generators and the descended inversion action in the \(C_3\) cases. Its output is:

`VERIFY_OK k=3,5,7,9,11,15 residues=0..5 quotient_sizes=[k^3,1,gcd(k,3),1,gcd(k,3),1] inversion_action=true`

The finite computations are regression checks only. The proof above establishes the statement for every odd \(k>1\) and every \(n\ge1\).

## Relationship to prior work
The recent torus-replacement paper proves the general semidirect-product quotient lemma and identifies
\[
G_k\cong H_k\rtimes_{\phi_k}\mathbb Z
\]
for odd \(k\). It then uses only the fact that \(\phi_k^6=1\) to obtain the clean family for exponents divisible by six, and explicitly notes that for general exponents the extra relations may shrink the normal subgroup.

The earlier Kanenobu--Kazama paper supplies the missing meridional action
\[
A\mapsto B,
\qquad
B\mapsto A^{-1}B
\]
in the Heisenberg presentation. Combining that action with the recent quotient lemma gives the complete residue-by-residue collapse above.

Targeted searches for the all-exponent classification, the \(C_3\rtimes C_n\) intermediate case, and the modulo-six Heisenberg coinvariants found no covering statement.

## Limitations
The theorem is for the odd-\(k\) doubly-null Kanenobu--Kazama family used in the source construction. It does not classify the even-\(k\) cases or other torus-exterior families.

The result identifies fundamental groups of the twisted constructions. It does not by itself classify the resulting four-manifolds or embedded tori up to diffeomorphism or isotopy.

No assertion is made for \(n=0\).
