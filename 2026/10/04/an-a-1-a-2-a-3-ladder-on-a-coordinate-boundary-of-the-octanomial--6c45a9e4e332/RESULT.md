# An \(A_1\)-\(A_2\)-\(A_3\) ladder on a coordinate boundary of the octanomial cubic
## Finding
Over \(\mathbb C\), consider the octanomial cubic surface \(S=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^3\), where \[F=a xyz+b xyw+c xzw+d yzw+e x^2y+fxy^2+gz^2w+hzw^2.\] Assume \(abcgh\ne0\) and put \(P=[1:0:0:0]\). Then \(P\) is singular exactly when \(e=0\). On that coordinate boundary set \(D=ab-cf\) and \(T=ah+bg-cd\). The analytic singularity of \(S\) at \(P\) is \(A_1\) if \(D\ne0\), is \(A_2\) if \(D=0\) and \(T\ne0\), and is \(A_3\) if \(D=T=0\). For \(D=0\), the Hessian has kernel generated by \((-c,b,a)\); the cubic term on that kernel is \(abT\,t^3\). After the adapted change \(w=t\), \(y=u-ct/a\), \(z=v+bt/a\), the transverse \((u,v)\)-Hessian is nondegenerate. If \(T=0\), the one-variable function obtained by the analytic splitting lemma has quartic coefficient \(-b^2gh/(a^2c)\ne0\), so no worse singularity occurs under the stated nonvanishing assumptions. This gives an explicit ADE refinement of the coordinate discriminant boundary \(e=0\), whose square factor is present in the published octanomial discriminant.

\[

\]
Its gradient at the origin is \((e,0,0)\), so \(P\) is singular exactly for \(e=0\). From now on set \(e=0\). The Hessian at the origin is

\[

## Relationship to prior work
Panizzut--Sertöz--Sturmfels introduce this eight-term cubic and compute its discriminant. In particular, their Proposition 2.4 displays a square factor \(e^2\) (and the analogous factors \(f^2g^2h^2\)) in the discriminant, but the inspected statement does not classify the local analytic type on the boundary \(e=0\). Kaneko later notes, in the discussion of the same octanomial normal form, that vanishing of one of the four boundary coefficients produces a singular cubic in the relevant normalization; the inspected text likewise does not give the nested \(A_1\), \(A_2\), \(A_3\) conditions above. The present result refines one coordinate boundary by an explicit local ADE stratification rather than merely detecting singularity.

## Limitations
The result is local at \(P\). It does not classify the full singular locus of a cubic with \(e=0\), does not treat intersections with other coordinate boundaries such as \(g=0\) or \(h=0\), and does not claim that every point of the coefficient strata described above occurs in the moduli parametrization by six blown-up points. A residual literature risk is that the same local calculation may be implicit in general sparse-discriminant or cubic-surface singularity theory under different coordinates; targeted searches of the exact normal form and defining coefficient combinations did not locate such a statement.
