# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The negative half begins from the focal theorem's exact negation: failure of Banach-isomorphic Radon--Nikodym structure yields a Lipschitz curve with no differentiability point. Affine endpoint correction preserves nondifferentiability. Dyadic copies have the same Lipschitz constant after simultaneous horizontal and vertical scaling. The quasi-triangle inequality controls pairs in different supports, while each support yields the lower norm bound. The two-sided estimate proves a closed \(\ell_\infty\) range, and every nonzero coefficient leaves an interval on which differentiability would contradict differentiability of the seed.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of arXiv:2609.26992v1 found the single-curve differentiability dichotomy and closedness of the almost-everywhere differentiable class, but no lineability or spaceability conclusion. Searches for the exact complement, disjoint-support formulation, Radon--Nikodym reformulation, and the requirement that every nonzero vector fail on an interval found no covering statement. The closest recent primary sources concern scalar Lipschitz norm attainment or scalar subdifferential pathologies and do not imply this vector-valued result.

## Value
PASS. The statement upgrades existence of one bad curve to a closed nonseparable classical subspace with a uniform local failure property. It therefore gives a sharp large-complement alternative for the newly characterized differentiability class rather than a routine numerical or notational extension.

## Closest literature and limitations
The focal source is arXiv:2609.26992v1. Related large-linear-structure results include doi:10.1016/j.na.2026.114063 and doi:10.1112/plms.70007, but their ambient objects and bad properties differ. The construction does not prove complementedness or maximal algebraic dimension. A residual literature risk is an older equivalent generic lineability theorem under terminology not found in the focused searches.
