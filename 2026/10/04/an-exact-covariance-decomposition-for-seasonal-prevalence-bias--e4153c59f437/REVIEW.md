# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is obtained by integrating the exact logarithmic derivatives of \(S\) and \(I\) over a full period and subtracting the corresponding averaged-equilibrium balance equations. Strict positivity supplies every required division. The proof does not use a perturbation, a finite-time simulation, or an unproved asymptotic step.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Liu–Li–Zhang–He (2026), numerically compares mean periodic prevalence with equilibria of the averaged system and explicitly notes that the approximation is not guaranteed in general. It does not give the exact prevalence-bias or companion covariance identities. General periodic-epidemic threshold theory addresses disease-free next-infection operators and therefore does not imply the nonlinear endemic mean balances. Targeted title, DOI, covariance, averaging, and reciprocal-susceptible searches found no equivalent statement. A residual literature risk remains because equivalent identities can be phrased under different notation.

## Value
PASS. The result turns a source-motivated numerical comparison into an exact diagnostic: equality of mean infection with the averaged endemic level occurs precisely when a reciprocal-susceptible level effect balances transmission–infection covariance. The companion identity separately records how transmission and reinfection covariances constrain mean susceptible/recovered shifts. This is useful for interpreting when averaging is reliable without claiming a universal error sign.

## Closest literature and limitations
The motivating paper is DOI 10.3390/math14060953. Wang and Zhao, DOI 10.1007/s10884-008-9111-8, supply broad periodic threshold theory but not this mean-prevalence identity. The result assumes a strictly positive periodic solution and a positive averaged equilibrium; it neither proves their existence nor uniqueness, and it does not by itself determine the sign of the prevalence error.
