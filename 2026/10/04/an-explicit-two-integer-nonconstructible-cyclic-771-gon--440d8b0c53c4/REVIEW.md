## Correctness
PASS. The side data determine a convex cyclic polygon, the half-angle identity gives the stated polynomial equation, and the coefficient valuations force a single \(769\)-adic Newton-polygon segment of reduced slope \(-1/384\). The local ramification argument therefore forces algebraic degree divisible by \(384\). The bundled exact checker reconstructs the degree-\(768\) polynomial and verifies all valuation inequalities.

## Originality
PASS, narrowly scoped. The 2013 primary version explicitly left \(n=771\) unresolved computationally. The 2015 paper later proved a broader existential theorem for all odd \(n\ge8\), so existence at \(771\) is already known. The new content is the concrete side pair \(768,769\), its multiplicities \(769+2\), and the elementary \(769\)-adic certificate. Full-text searches of the 2015 paper and targeted literature/database searches did not locate that explicit example. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased prior examples.

## Value
PASS. The witness directly fills the concrete instance highlighted by the 2013 table, but without claiming to improve the later 2015 general theorem. Its consecutive integer lengths and short local-field proof make the formerly computationally inaccessible case a reproducible exact benchmark.

## Closest literature and limitations
Czédli–Kunos (2015) is stronger at the level of existence: every odd order at least eight has some two-integer nonconstructible cyclic polygon. The present result is only an explicit certificate for one order and does not establish minimality, uniqueness, or a classification of two-length \(771\)-gons.
