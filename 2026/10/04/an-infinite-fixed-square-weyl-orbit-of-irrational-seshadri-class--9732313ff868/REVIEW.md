# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The seed theorem gives an ample class \(L\) with \(\varepsilon(L;y)=2\sqrt5\) on a very general nine-point blow-up. For \(\delta=3H-\sum E_i\) and \(\alpha=E_1-E_2\), the checked Kac formula gives
\[
T_\alpha^m(L)=L+4m\alpha+4m^2\delta.
\]
This is the displayed family \(L_m\), and the translation is an integral isometry, so all \(L_m\) are primitive with square \(20\). Very general nine-tuples are in Cremona general position, so each finite Weyl word is geometrically realized by an isomorphism between the corresponding blown-up surfaces. A countable-intersection argument chooses one configuration for which every transformed configuration lies in the seed theorem's very-general locus, and then one common very general point on the fixed source surface. Pullback preserves ampleness and Seshadri constants. The packaged checker independently verifies the lattice formulas and inverse.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating full text proves one polarization and contains no Weyl-orbit or infinite-family formulation. The primary affine-Weyl source supplies only the lattice/Cremona machinery. A highly relevant later preprint with an “infinite series” title was inspected: its new plane examples vary the odd parameter and start at a ten-point blow-up, rather than producing infinitely many square-\(20\) polarizations on one \(X_9\). Exact-formula, fixed-square, common-surface, and equivalent generic-degree searches located no covering statement. The main residual risk is an unindexed very recent observation of the same corollary.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem converts a single newly constructed irrational Seshadri class into an infinite, primitive, fixed-volume family on one surface and at one common point. The fixed square \(20\) rules out the trivial tensor-power mechanism, while the unbounded plane degree shows that the classes genuinely move through the Picard lattice. This gives a structural picture of the new irrationality phenomenon rather than a routine numerical variant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature consists of Laface--Ugaglia for the seed Seshadri equality, Alonso--Suris and Totaro for the affine-Weyl/Cremona transport, and the later Malara--Merta--Szpond--Zieliński series. None states the fixed-\(X_9\), fixed-square orbit. The result does not classify all square-\(20\) polarizations and applies only at a very general common point.
