# Review

## Correctness
PASS. At \(C_{\max}=1/2\), the Cartan concurrence formula has exactly two non-perfect branches. The identity-like branch forces \(\theta_1+\theta_2=\pi/12\), which yields \(g_{\mathrm t}\le(5-2\sqrt3)/12\). The SWAP-like branch forces \(\theta_2+\theta_3=5\pi/12\), which yields \(g_{\mathrm t}\ge3/4\). Applying the source's exact gate-typicality upper and lower envelopes, together with the verified monotonicity of the common envelope branch, gives the stated forbidden interval. Explicit controlled and iSWAP–SWAP gates lie on opposite sides, proving disconnection.

PASS. The primary preprint gives only the sharp outer envelope at fixed \(C_{\max}\), explicitly warns that intervening values are not asserted attainable, treats the disconnected endpoint \(C_{\max}=0\), proves interval filling at \(C_{\max}=1\), and lists topology/connected-component analysis inside the bands as future work. Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches for fixed-concurrence QPD topology, the specific \(C_{\max}=1/2\) slice, and the numerical radical gap found no equivalent or stronger interior-disconnection statement.

## Value
PASS. The result answers a concrete interior instance of an open structural question posed by the source and shows that its sharp one-descriptor band can substantially overstate the physically attainable cost range even away from endpoints. The forbidden interval has width greater than three in QPD extent, so the effect is not a tiny numerical artifact.
