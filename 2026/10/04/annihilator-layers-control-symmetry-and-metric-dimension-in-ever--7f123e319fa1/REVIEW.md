# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Every nonzero element in a finite commutative chain ring has a unique unit-times-power valuation. This gives the exact layer sizes, the adjacency threshold \(i+j\ge\ell\), and the annihilator identity \(\operatorname{ann}(u\pi^i)=\mathfrak m^{\ell-i}\). Distinct layers have strictly different degrees, so automorphisms cannot mix them; within a layer all permutations preserve adjacency. The determining-number formula follows from the direct-product symmetric action. The metric lower bound is forced by twin classes, and the all-but-one-per-layer set is explicitly proved resolving by a layer \(V_{\ell-j}\) witness for each omitted pair.

Risk: the \(\ell=2,q=2\) one-vertex convention is stated explicitly; it uses the standard metric dimension \(0\) for \(K_1\).

## Originality

PASS. Annihilator compression is classical, and recent papers already cover important special cases: determining/metric dimension for \(\mathbb Z_n\), valuation layers for truncated polynomial rings, and an automorphism product for \(\mathbb Z_{p^k}\). The accepted claim is deliberately broader: it proves the full package for every finite commutative chain ring and identifies the compression classes, automorphism group, determining number, metric dimension, and graph-isomorphism invariant in one parameter theorem. Targeted searches found no source with this general chain-ring statement.

Risk: a 2021 paper on zero-divisor spectra of finite chain rings could not be read in full. Its abstract describes rank, determinant, and spectral calculations through reduction graphs rather than metric or automorphism invariants, so it is not a decisive unresolved overlap, but full-text inaccessibility remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The theorem converts a ring-theoretic filtration into exact graph symmetry and resolving parameters, extending several isolated special-case results at once. It also shows precisely what the zero-divisor graph forgets: nonisomorphic chain rings with the same residue-field size and length are graph-isomorphic. This is a structural classification rather than a routine numerical recomputation.

Risk: outside the chain-ring class, the linearly ordered valuation argument breaks and no analogous formula is claimed.
