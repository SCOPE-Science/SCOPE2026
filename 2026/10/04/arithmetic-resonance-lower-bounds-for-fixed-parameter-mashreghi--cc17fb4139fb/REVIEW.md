# Review of Arithmetic resonance lower bounds for fixed-parameter Mashreghi–Ransford constants

## Correctness

PASS. For the real sequence
\[
a_n
=
\frac{(i\alpha)^n-(-i\alpha)^n}{2i},
\]
the normalized input limsup is exactly \(1\). The two binomial transforms are computed exactly as
\[
b_n=\beta^n\sin(n\phi),
\qquad
c_n=(-1)^{n+1}\beta^n\sin(n\phi),
\]
with
\[
\cos\phi=1/\beta.
\]
Hence the fixed-parameter ratio is the reciprocal of
\[
\limsup|\sin(n\phi)|.
\]
Density modulo \(\pi\) gives value \(1\) at irrational angles; a complete residue-class calculation gives value \(1\) at even rational denominators and
\[
\cos(\pi/(2q))
\]
at odd denominator \(q\). The universal upper theorem then certifies equality at \(\beta=2\).

## Originality

PASS. The full 2026 primary text was inspected. It proves the universal constant \(2/\sqrt3\) and cites a single sharpness example at \(\beta=2\). It does not define or study the optimal constant at fixed \(\beta\), and searches within the full text found no fixed-parameter or rational-resonance discussion.

The 2005 predecessor establishes the original universal inequality and is the source of the \(\beta=2\) lower example. Targeted literature and database searches for fixed-\(\beta\) constants, rational-angle resonance, and parameterized two-pole witnesses found no implication-equivalent statement.

The principal residual risk is that the short parameterized calculation may have appeared informally or in unindexed commentary.

## Value

PASS. After the universal constant has been solved, the natural finer question is how much of the universal loss is already forced at each fixed growth parameter. The calculation shows that the standard sharpness mechanism is not parameter-flat: it has a precise arithmetic resonance law and singles out \(\beta=2\) as the shortest odd resonance.

This supplies a concrete lower landscape for the fixed-parameter problem and identifies where future extremal constructions must improve on the canonical two-pole family.
