# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The cubic is split exactly into its \((2,1)\) and \((1,2)\) pieces. For each projection, the branch discriminant is derived as the adjugate quadratic-form discriminant after imposing the moving line. Exact Gröbner-basis checks establish that \(D_1\) is smooth and that \(D_2\) has the unique singular point \([0:1:0]\); its quadratic tangent cone is nondegenerate. Substitution over that point gives the entire exceptional fiber explicitly, while the discriminant argument excludes any further positive-dimensional fiber. Smoothness of the K3 is stated and computed in the source and was also checked chartwise from the displayed equations.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source gives the cubic, K3 base locus, bidegrees, degree, and multidegree, but not either branch sextic or the contracted curve. Hassett gives the abstract complete-intersection K3 and its rank-two lattice, so those facts are explicitly treated as prior art rather than claimed. Auel's discriminant-14 treatment provides broader moduli context but does not state the coordinate branch models. Searches using the source title, the explicit degree-\(14\) K3 description, the \((2,1)\)-\((1,2)\) presentation, branch-sextic language, and the two-disjoint-plane cubic description found no statement implying the exact smooth/nodal branch asymmetry.

## Value
PASS. The source's symmetric multidegree \((2,5,2)\) hides a substantive geometric asymmetry: one square-two polarization is a base-point-free double-plane morphism with smooth branch, whereas the other lies on a contraction wall and collapses a unique \((-2)\)-curve to an \(A_1\) point. This directly clarifies the birational geometry of a named computational K3 example and supplies exact equations for its two natural genus-two models.
