# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The source's exhaustive dyadic scalar calculus gives a unique positive dyadic \(q\) for every internal one-one degree and gives both
\[
S_q\le_1S_r\iff q\le r
\]
and
\[
S_q\oplus S_r\equiv_1S_{q+r}.
\]
Hence the direct-sum structure is exactly \((\mathbb Z[1/2]_{>0},+,\le)\).

The group completion is all of \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\) because every dyadic is a difference of two positive dyadics. An additive automorphism is determined by \(c=F(1)\), satisfies \(F(q)=cq\) on every positive dyadic, and is surjective exactly when \(c\) is a positive unit of \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\). Those units are precisely \(2^z\), \(z\in\mathbb Z\). Fixing \(1\) forces \(z=0\).

## Value

PASS. Direct sum is the canonical algebraic operation on reducibility degrees. Retaining it reveals that Cintioli's dense order carries much more structure than a generic copy of \((\mathbb Q,\le)\): it is the positive cone of the dyadic ordered group. The resulting automorphism classification shows a strong rigidity contrast—countably many operation-preserving automorphisms versus continuum many pure order automorphisms—and the distinguished base degree eliminates even those residual symmetries.
