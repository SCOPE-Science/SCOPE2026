# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For a general matrix \(P(e_1)=ae_1+ce_2\), \(P(e_2)=be_1+de_2\), the averaging identities reduce to \(b^2=0\), \(ab=0\), \(b(a-d)=0\), and \(a(a-d)=0\). Over \(\mathbb C\) this is exactly \(b=0\) with either \(a=0\) or \(d=a\). The automorphism group follows from \(e_2=e_1^2\), and direct matrix conjugation gives the two displayed parameter actions. These actions exhaust the operator set and prove the three normal-form strata and stabilizers.

Risk: the result uses ordinary algebra-pair isomorphism, namely conjugation by \(\operatorname{Aut}(A)\). It does not address weaker equivalence relations that might be used in other operator categories.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 2025 operator paper gives raw averaging matrices but no automorphism or conjugacy analysis, and its displayed nonzero restrictions do not themselves supply the boundary-inclusive orbit classification. The 2024 SIGMA paper gives \(\operatorname{Aut}(C_{03})\), but its orbit calculations act on compatible bilinear products rather than averaging endomorphisms. Targeted database and web searches found no source stating the three normal forms or stabilizers for averaging operators on this algebra.

Risk: the computation is short once the two prior ingredients are placed together, and an older thesis or differently named classification may contain the same quotient calculation without being well indexed.

## Value
**PASS.** The source literature presents parameter families, while isomorphism classification requires removing automorphism redundancy and retaining valid boundary operators. The result turns a two-parameter raw description into a complete, unique list of pair-isomorphism types and records stabilizers and a nonclosed-orbit phenomenon. This is a natural moduli refinement of a recent low-dimensional operator classification rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

Risk: only one of the two-dimensional pre-Lie isomorphism types is treated, so no claim is made about the global moduli problem.
