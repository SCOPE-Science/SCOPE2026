# Bell census of forced equivalence modalities on finite quantum frames
## Finding

Let
\[
(W,R_Q)
\]
be a finite orthoframe in the relational quantum-modal semantics introduced by Tokuo. Thus \(R_Q\) is reflexive and symmetric. Let
\[
\mathcal C=\{C_1,\ldots,C_c\}
\]
be the connected components of the undirected compatibility graph determined by \(R_Q\).

Consider accessibility relations \(R_M\) satisfying both:

\[
R_M\text{ is an equivalence relation},
\]

and Tokuo's forcing condition
\[
R_M(u,w)\wedge R_Q(u,v)
\Longrightarrow
R_M(v,w).
\]

Then the \(R_M\)-equivalence classes are exactly unions of compatibility components. Therefore forced equivalence modalities are in bijection with set partitions of \(\mathcal C\), and their exact number is
\[
\boxed{B_c},
\]
where \(B_c\) is the \(c\)-th Bell number.

Equivalently, if
\[
\pi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal C/{\equiv}
\]
is any partition of the component set, define
\[
uR_Mv
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi([u]_Q)=\pi([v]_Q).
\]
Every forced equivalence accessibility relation arises uniquely in this way.

The count depends only on the number of compatibility components, not on their cardinalities. In particular, when the compatibility graph is connected,
\[
c=1,
\]
there is exactly one forced equivalence accessibility relation:
\[
R_M=W\times W.
\]

Thus the equivalence-frame class singled out in *Component Modalities of Quantum Logic* has a finite semantic search space of Bell size on a fixed orthoframe, rather than the unrestricted \(2^{|W|^2}\) relation space.

## Assumptions and scope

The compatibility relation \(R_Q\) is reflexive and symmetric, as in the primary quantum-modal semantics. A compatibility component means a connected component of the undirected graph generated by \(R_Q\); equivalently, two worlds lie in the same component when they are joined by a finite \(R_Q\)-path.

The theorem counts only those modal relations \(R_M\) that are equivalence relations and satisfy the forcing condition. This is the equivalence-frame class used in the 2026 component-modality completeness result.

The orthoframe \((W,R_Q)\) is fixed and finite. The valuation of atomic formulas is irrelevant to the count. The theorem counts labelled accessibility relations on that fixed carrier. It does not count orthoframes themselves, valuations, or isomorphism classes under automorphisms of \((W,R_Q)\).

No claim is made that distinct counted relations yield distinct one-conclusion logics. Indeed, the 2026 source proves a logic coincidence among several frame classes; the present theorem concerns exact finite frame multiplicity inside one of those classes.

## Proof

For \(u\in W\), write its modal successor row as
\[
R_M[u]=\{w\in W:uR_Mw\}.
\]

Suppose \(R_Q(u,v)\). Tokuo's forcing condition gives
\[
R_M[u]\subseteq R_M[v].
\]
Because \(R_Q\) is symmetric, we also have \(R_Q(v,u)\), so applying the same condition in the opposite direction gives
\[
R_M[v]\subseteq R_M[u].
\]
Hence
\[
R_M[u]=R_M[v].
\]

Equality propagates along finite \(R_Q\)-paths. Therefore
\[
[u]_Q=[v]_Q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_M[u]=R_M[v].
\]
This is the component invariance emphasized in the 2026 follow-up paper.

Now assume that \(R_M\) is an equivalence relation. Let \(u\) and \(v\) lie in the same compatibility component. Their modal rows are equal. Reflexivity of \(R_M\) gives
\[
u\in R_M[u],
\]
so row equality gives
\[
u\in R_M[v],
\]
i.e.
\[
vR_Mu.
\]
Likewise, because
\[
v\in R_M[v]=R_M[u],
\]
we have
\[
uR_Mv.
\]
Thus every compatibility component lies inside a single \(R_M\)-equivalence class.

Consequently each \(R_M\)-class is a union of compatibility components. The equivalence relation \(R_M\) therefore induces a set partition of
\[
\mathcal C.
\]

Conversely, start with an arbitrary partition \(\Pi\) of \(\mathcal C\). Define
\[
uR_M^{\Pi}v
\]
exactly when the compatibility components \([u]_Q\) and \([v]_Q\) belong to the same block of \(\Pi\).

This is plainly an equivalence relation. If
\[
R_Q(u,v),
\]
then \(u\) and \(v\) lie in the same compatibility component. Hence they belong to the same \(R_M^{\Pi}\)-class and have identical \(R_M^{\Pi}\)-rows. Therefore
\[
R_M^{\Pi}(u,w)
\Longrightarrow
R_M^{\Pi}(v,w),
\]
so the forcing condition holds.

The two constructions are inverse. Hence forced equivalence accessibility relations are in bijection with set partitions of a \(c\)-element set. By the definition of the Bell numbers, there are exactly
\[
B_c
\]
such relations.

For \(c=1\), the unique partition has one block. Its induced equivalence relation has one equivalence class, so it is the universal relation
\[
W\times W.
\]

## Verification

The bundled checker performs an exhaustive finite replay independent of the Bell-number argument.

For every carrier size
\[
1\le n\le5,
\]
it enumerates every reflexive symmetric compatibility relation \(R_Q\), equivalently every simple undirected graph with loops added. For each such \(R_Q\), it computes the number \(c\) of compatibility components.

It then enumerates every equivalence relation \(R_M\) on the same labelled carrier, filters them directly by the forcing condition, and checks that:

\[
\#\{R_M:R_M\text{ equivalence and forced}\}=B_c;
\]

every accepted \(R_M\)-class is a union of \(R_Q\)-components; and the accepted relations are exactly those reconstructed from partitions of the component set.

As a separate check of the structural premise, for carriers through size three the verifier enumerates every binary relation \(R_M\) and confirms directly that the forcing condition is equivalent to constancy of modal successor rows on compatibility components.

The computation is corroborative. The arbitrary finite theorem follows from the row-invariance and partition proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Tokuo's *Quantum modal logic* introduces the forcing condition in the exact form used here. The full author-accepted manuscript defines a quantum modal structure \((W,R_Q,R_M,\rho)\), requires \(R_Q\) to be reflexive and symmetric, and requires
\[
R_M(i,l)\Longrightarrow
\forall j\bigl(R_Q(i,j)\Longrightarrow R_M(j,l)\bigr).
\]
It motivates this by saying that the situations seen through \(R_M\) are invariant among similar situations.

The 2026 follow-up *Component Modalities of Quantum Logic* makes the structural consequence explicit: modal successor sets are constant on compatibility components. Its public abstract also singles out equivalence frames satisfying the forcing condition and proves that their one-conclusion logic coincides with the component-frame and connected-universal-frame logics.

The checked accessible material does not state a finite enumeration of the equivalence-frame class. The present result adds that enumeration and identifies the exact quotient object: once \((W,R_Q)\) is fixed, an equivalence accessibility relation satisfying forcing is nothing more and nothing less than a partition of the compatibility components.

The classical fact that set partitions of a \(c\)-element set are counted by \(B_c\) is standard. The mathematical content here is the reduction of the quantum-modal equivalence-frame class to that partition lattice.

## Limitations

The result does not enumerate arbitrary forced modal relations. Without the equivalence requirement, component invariance leaves a much larger family of successor-set choices.

The Bell count is for a fixed labelled orthoframe. Quotienting by automorphisms of \((W,R_Q)\) gives a different orbit-count problem.

The theorem is finite only in its enumeration statement. The structural correspondence itself extends to arbitrary component sets, where equivalence modalities correspond to arbitrary set partitions of the component set.

The full body of the 2026 follow-up preprint was not available in the accessible primary route during this review. Its primary abstract states component invariance and explicitly identifies the equivalence-frame class, while the predecessor's author-accepted manuscript was inspected in full at the forcing definition and semantics. An unobserved finite-enumeration remark in the follow-up therefore remains a literature risk.
