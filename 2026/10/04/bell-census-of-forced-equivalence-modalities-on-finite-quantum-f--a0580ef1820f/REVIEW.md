# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Symmetry of \(R_Q\) turns the one-way forcing condition into equality of modal successor rows across every compatibility edge, hence across each compatibility component. If \(R_M\) is an equivalence relation, equal rows plus reflexivity force all worlds in one compatibility component to lie in the same \(R_M\)-class. Conversely, any partition of the compatibility components induces an equivalence relation with constant rows on each component and therefore satisfies forcing. This is a bijection with set partitions, giving exactly \(B_c\).

The bundled verifier exhaustively checks every reflexive symmetric compatibility graph through five worlds against every equivalence relation on the same carrier, and independently checks the row-constancy equivalence for arbitrary modal relations through three worlds.

## Originality

PASS, with a stated source-access residual risk. The 2026 primary abstract proves component invariance and explicitly studies equivalence frames satisfying forcing, but it does not state a Bell-number census. The fully accessible predecessor manuscript defines the forcing condition but does not analyze compatibility components or finite counts. Candidate-specific searches of the published-finding index and public literature found no quantum-modal Bell enumeration or partition-lattice classification of the equivalence-frame class.

The Bell numbers themselves are classical. Originality is claimed only for the exact semantic reduction of this recent quantum-modal frame class to partitions of compatibility components and its finite count.

## Value

PASS. The 2026 paper uses equivalence frames satisfying forcing as one of three complete semantic classes for its component-modality logic. On a finite orthoframe, the theorem replaces a search over arbitrary \(|W|^2\)-bit relations by the partition lattice of only the \(c\) compatibility components. The connected case collapses to a single relation, while multi-component cases have an exact Bell-sized search space independent of component sizes. This is a natural finite structural invariant of the source's semantic class.
