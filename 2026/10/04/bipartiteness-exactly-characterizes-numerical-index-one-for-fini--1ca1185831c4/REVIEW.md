# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The primal extreme points are exactly oriented graph-edge molecules: edges have no third point in their metric segment, while every nonedge has an interior vertex on a shortest path. The dual unit ball is defined by edge inequalities, and the proof establishes the exact vertex criterion \(f\in\operatorname{ext}B_{X^*}\) iff the tight-edge subgraph \(H_f\) is connected. Connectedness forces uniqueness in every midpoint decomposition; disconnectedness permits a componentwise perturbation controlled by the positive minimum slack of finitely many crossing edges. In a bipartite graph, every path between the endpoints of an edge has odd length, so summing \(\pm1\) tight increments forces every dual extreme potential to saturate every graph edge. In a nonbipartite graph, a spanning-tree parity potential is dual-extreme and is constant on some graph edge. McGregor's finite-dimensional characterization then gives exactly the claimed equivalence. The standalone checker exhaustively corroborates the graph criterion through five vertices.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the stated residual bibliographic risk. The 2023 primary source computes the numerical index of all two-dimensional Lipschitz-free spaces and therefore only three-point metrics; it does not state an arbitrary finite-graph characterization. The 2017/2018 extremal-structure work supplies the extreme-molecule criterion but does not study numerical index. McGregor supplies an abstract finite-dimensional criterion but no graph-metric translation. Reisner's graph-associated CL-spaces use a different graph-to-space construction with a 1-unconditional basis and do not identify general Lipschitz-free graph spaces. Targeted literature and database searches using Lipschitz-free, Arens--Eells, transportation-cost, graph, bipartite, numerical-index-one, and CL-space formulations did not locate the present equivalence. A previously checked exact cycle result computes cycles; its even-cycle case is a strict special case of the new classification, while it gives exact values for odd cycles that the present theorem does not claim.

## Value
PASS. Bipartiteness is a central structural invariant of graphs, and numerical index \(1\) is the maximal operator-geometric value. The theorem gives a complete iff classification for every finite unweighted graph metric, not an isolated example or arbitrary parameter slice. The tight-edge connectivity lemma is independently reusable for the dual polytope, and the theorem simultaneously covers all finite trees, complete bipartite graphs, grids, hypercubes, and every other finite bipartite graph while proving that every odd-cycle-containing graph fails maximal numerical index.
