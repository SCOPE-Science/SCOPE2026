# Boundary-one pentagon friezes over division rings are forced commutative
## Finding
Let \(D\) be a division ring and let \(C=(c_{r,s})\) be a non-commutative frieze in the sense of the directed polygon exchange and triangle relations, with every boundary entry equal to \(1\).

If \(C\) has height at most \(2\), then all its values commute pairwise. For height \(2\), index the pentagon vertices by \(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\) and set
\[
q_i=c_{i,i+2}=c_{i+2,i}.
\]
Then for every \(i\),
\[
q_iq_{i+1}=q_{i+1}q_i=1+q_{i+3},
\]
and \(q_0,\ldots,q_4\) commute pairwise. Hence every entry of the pentagon frieze lies in the commutative subring generated by these five units.

For height \(1\), the two internal values commute as well. Consequently height \(3\) is the smallest possible height at which a boundary-one non-commutative frieze can have genuinely noncommuting entries. The known quaternionic height-\(3\) example attains this bound.

## Assumptions and scope
The ring \(D\) is a division ring. A frieze assigns a unit \(c_{r,s}\) to every ordered pair of distinct polygon vertices and satisfies the local triangle and local non-commutative exchange relations. Boundary-one means \(c_{i,i+1}=c_{i+1,i}=1\) for every boundary edge. Height \(n\) corresponds to an \(n+3\)-gon.

No positivity, norm, finite-dimensionality, characteristic, or quaternion assumption is used in the rigidity argument. The claim concerns only heights \(1\) and \(2\); it does not classify all commuting pentagon friezes.

## Proof
For boundary-one friezes, Proposition 3.6 of Cuntz--Holm--Jørgensen gives
\[
c_{r,r+2}=c_{r+2,r}.
\]
Thus on a pentagon every internal directed diagonal is represented by exactly one of the units \(q_i=c_{i,i+2}\).

Fix \(i\) modulo \(5\) and apply the two oppositely oriented local exchange relations to the quadrangle with cyclic vertices \(i,i+1,i+2,i+3\). Substituting boundary values \(1\) and using the preceding symmetry gives
\[
q_i=q_{i+1}^{-1}+q_{i+1}^{-1}q_{i+3},
\]
and
\[
q_i=q_{i+1}^{-1}+q_{i+3}q_{i+1}^{-1}.
\]
Multiplying the first identity on the left and the second on the right by \(q_{i+1}\) yields
\[
q_{i+1}q_i=1+q_{i+3}=q_iq_{i+1}.
\]
Hence cyclically adjacent quiddity entries commute, and also
\[
q_{i+3}=q_iq_{i+1}-1.
\]

It remains to compare entries at cyclic distance \(2\). Since \(q_i\) commutes with \(q_{i+4}\), and the preceding recurrence with index \(i+1\) gives
\[
q_{i+4}=q_{i+1}q_{i+2}-1,
\]
we obtain
\[
q_iq_{i+1}q_{i+2}=q_{i+1}q_{i+2}q_i.
\]
Because \(q_i\) commutes with \(q_{i+1}\) and \(q_{i+1}\) is a unit, cancellation gives
\[
q_iq_{i+2}=q_{i+2}q_i.
\]
Every pair of the five quiddity indices has cyclic distance \(1\) or \(2\), so all five \(q_i\) commute pairwise.

For height \(1\), Example 3.7 of the same paper gives internal units \(a,b\) with \(ab=2\). Since \(2=1+1\) is central, \(b=2a^{-1}\) and therefore \(ba=2=ab\). Thus height \(1\) also cannot contain genuinely noncommuting entries.

The paper's displayed quaternionic height-\(3\) example contains both \(i\) and \(j\) among its entries. In the Hamilton quaternions, \(ij=k\) while \(ji=-k\), so those two frieze values do not commute. Hence the height threshold is sharp.

## Verification
The proof was reconstructed directly from the published local exchange relation and the boundary-one length-\(2\) symmetry. Both oriented exchange equations were substituted separately; no commutativity was assumed before deriving it. The only cancellation is by a frieze value, which is a unit by definition.

The index argument is exhaustive on a pentagon: the five internal undirected diagonals are the pairs \((i,i+2)\), and every unordered pair of the five quiddity entries has cyclic distance \(1\) or \(2\). The height-\(1\) step uses only the published equation \(ab=2\) and centrality of the integer scalar \(2\).

No finite experiment is used as evidence for the infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Cuntz--Holm--Jørgensen's 2026 paper introduces the normed-division-ring setting, records a genuinely noncommutative quaternionic frieze of height \(3\), proves that boundary-one diagonals of length \(2\) agree in the two orientations, and explicitly classifies height \(1\) by \(ab=2\). The inspected paper does not state the height-\(2\) pairwise-commutativity theorem or the sharp minimal-height consequence. Exact full-text search found no occurrence of the phrase “height 2” in that paper.

The 2024 foundational paper develops the local exchange and triangle calculus and already displays the height-\(3\) quaternionic example, but the inspected full text contains no “height 2” or “pentagon” statement. The 2025 determinant/Laurent/gluing paper gives broader structural machinery but does not supply this pentagon rigidity statement in the inspected relevant material.

## Limitations
The result is a rigidity theorem, not an enumeration or classification of all height-\(2\) friezes. It assumes all boundary entries are \(1\); arbitrary coefficients are not covered. It also does not assert that every height-\(3\) frieze is genuinely noncommutative, only that height \(3\) is the first height where such behavior can occur and that an explicit example exists.

A residual originality risk is that the same pentagon consequence may have appeared under non-commutative polygon or type-\(A_2\) terminology outside the searched frieze literature; targeted semantic and exact searches did not locate such a statement.
