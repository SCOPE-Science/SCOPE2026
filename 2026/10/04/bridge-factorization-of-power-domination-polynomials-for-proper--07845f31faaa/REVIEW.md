# Review of Bridge factorization of power domination polynomials for proper double stars

## Correctness
PASS. If an unselected center has two unselected private leaves, neither leaf can ever be observed because only that center can observe them and it can never force while two such neighbors remain. Conversely, selecting the center or all but at most one private leaf on each side makes the centers observed immediately and leaves at most one vertex per side to force. The two necessary-and-sufficient side conditions are independent, so their ordinary generating functions multiply. Exhaustive direct simulation for \(2\le a,b\le7\) agrees with every coefficient and corollary.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational source gives the individual star factor and general decomposition results, but its conclusion still asks for all-set counts on nontrivial trees and for splitting formulas based on cut vertices or separating sets. The connected double-star bridge is not covered by disjoint-union multiplicativity, and the hypotheses of the foundational attachment formula do not supply this two-star bridge factorization. A later same-invariant article treats other named families; targeted full-text and literature searches found no double-star or bistar power-domination polynomial. A prior spider-tree formula has one branching center and does not imply the two-center characterization here.

## Value
PASS. The result answers, on a natural two-branching-center tree family, two explicit structural directions highlighted in the foundational paper: complete counting of power dominating sets in trees and a separator-based splitting phenomenon. The factorization determines every cardinality count, the total number of power dominating sets, the power domination number, and the rigidity of minimum solutions when both sides have at least three leaves.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
