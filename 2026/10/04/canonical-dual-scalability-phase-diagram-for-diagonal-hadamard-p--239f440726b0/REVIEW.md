# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The frame operator is \(S=I+D^2\). The necessity argument uses only the published canonical-dual scaling criterion, nonnegativity of squared scaling coefficients, and the Hadamard identity \(|(x_i)_j|^2=1/n\). This forces an affine function to interpolate \(n(1+t)^2\) on the distinct squared diagonal values, giving at most two values. In the two-level case the exact coefficients are \(A/n=1-uv\) and \(B/n=2+u+v\). The converse is verified by direct substitution, and the strict-scaling boundary is handled separately.

## Originality
PASS with a stated residual risk. The closest primary source proves the general feasibility criterion and gives one three-level Hadamard obstruction, but it does not state the exact all-parameter diagonal-Hadamard classification or the two-level product threshold. Scientific searches under canonical-dual, Hadamard, diagonal-anisotropy, spectral-level, and product-threshold formulations found no statement implying the theorem. A 2024 paper on dual scalable frames is the principal residual risk because its accessible abstract was inspected but its full theorem body was not materially available; its advertised complete frame classification is restricted to \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\mathbb R^3\), whereas the present theorem applies in every real Hadamard order.

## Value
PASS. The motivating literature supplies a single scalable frame whose canonical dual fails to be scalable. The theorem identifies the full phase diagram of the natural family containing that example, including a sharp positive two-level region and the exact boundary \(uv=1\). This is structural information rather than a routine recomputation of the published example.

## Closest literature and limitations
The principal source is Casazza--De Carli--Tran, *Remarks on scalable frames*, Theorem 5.7 and Proposition 5.10. The later Heydarpour--Arefijamaal--Ghaani Farashahi paper on dual scalable frames was checked at abstract/bibliographic level; incomplete full-text access is retained as an originality risk. The theorem is limited to real Hadamard bases and positive diagonal operators.
