# Review

## Correctness

PASS. For \(S_r=T+rB_2^d\), rotation invariance of the Euclidean ball gives
\[
\rho S_r+w=\rho T+w+rB_2^d.
\]
By definition of the inner parallel body,
\[
\rho T+w+rB_2^d\subset K
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\rho T+w\subset K\ominus rB_2^d.
\]
Thus the entire placement space of \(S_r\) in \(K\), with its rotation and translation parameters, is identical to the placement space of \(T\) in the eroded host. Convexity and compactness of the eroded host follow from its representation as an intersection of translates of \(K\). Hence every path theorem for \(T\) transfers without loss to \(T+rB_2^d\). Applying Janzer's Theorem 1.2 to a segment, with a similarity for arbitrary segment length, proves the capsule result.

The packaged checker independently confirms the halfspace form of this equivalence and the non-ball/non-segment width test. The continuum theorem rests on the exact set identity, not on finite experiments.

## Originality

PASS with a stated residual risk. Janzer's full open-access article was inspected at the formal definitions, Theorem 1.2, the general counterexample, Question 5.2, and its MSC classification. Question 5.2 explicitly asks for non-ball, non-segment higher-dimensional shapes with this universal path property; the article itself gives only segments and balls as positive examples.

The earlier reverse-Kakeya result of Bae, Cabello, Cheong, Choi, Stehn, and Yoon is planar and therefore does not imply the requested \(d\ge3\) capsule statement.

Targeted semantic and literature searches for capsules, spherocylinders, stadiums, Euclidean parallel bodies, Minkowski sums, and \(S\)-Kakeya rotation did not locate the inheritance lemma or the capsule consequence.

## Value

PASS. The result answers an explicit open question from the primary source with an infinite, geometrically natural family rather than a single ad hoc example. The mechanism is structural: Euclidean erosion identifies the full placement space of a ball-thickened shape with that of the original shape. This also yields a reusable inheritance principle, explaining why the segment theorem automatically extends to positive-thickness capsules.
