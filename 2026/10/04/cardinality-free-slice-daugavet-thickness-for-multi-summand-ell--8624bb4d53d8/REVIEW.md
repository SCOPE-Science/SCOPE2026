# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the definition of \(\mathcal T^s\), the dual of an arbitrary \(\ell_p\)-sum, the finite-support norming reduction, the Daugavet slice choice in each active coordinate, and the two inequalities needed for the lower estimate. The upper estimate is uniform because a slice concentrated in one coordinate forces the complementary \(\ell_p\)-mass to zero. No compactness, separability, or countability of \(\Gamma\) is assumed.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source is arXiv:2005.02045. Its Theorem 2.6 gives the exact value \(2^{1/p}\) for two summands, and its Remark 2.5 extends only the preceding upper-bound proposition to finite absolute sums. Focused web and published-finding corpus searches for finite multi-summand, arbitrary-index, cardinality-free, and equivalent direct-sum formulations did not locate the matching exact slice-index theorem. The residual risk is an equivalent statement under different notation.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a natural permanence law for a quantitative Daugavet invariant in a regime where \(\ell_p\)-sums with \(1<p<\infty\) lose the Daugavet property itself. The exact constant is independent of the number and density of summands, so the statement separates cardinality from the geometric obstruction measured by \(\mathcal T^s\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The 2020 source proves the two-summand equality for all three thickness indices and explicitly notes finite-sum upper-bound generalization. The new claim closes only the slice-index equality for every larger finite or infinite family. It does not claim corresponding formulas for the weak-open or convex-combination indices, and it excludes \(p=1,\infty\).
