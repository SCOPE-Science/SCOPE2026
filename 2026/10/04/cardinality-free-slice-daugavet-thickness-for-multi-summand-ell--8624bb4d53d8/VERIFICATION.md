The proof was checked directly from the definition of the slice Daugavet index of thickness. The critical lower-bound construction uses a finite support approximation to the norming vector of the dual \(\ell_q\)-profile and the standard slice characterization of the Daugavet property in each active coordinate. The key deterministic inequalities are \(\|re-sv\|\ge(1-\eta)(r+s)\) under the near-antipodal hypothesis and \((r+s)^p\ge r^p+s^p\).

The upper-bound slice is supported on one coordinate while the center is supported on a distinct coordinate; membership in the slice forces the remaining \(\ell_p\)-mass to tend to zero, producing the limit \(2^{1/p}\).

No numerical experiment, finite enumeration, or external certificate is used to justify the infinite-dimensional theorem. The verification does not cover \(p=1\), \(p=\infty\), or the weak-open and convex-combination thickness indices.
