# Review of Cartesian-product rigidity for monotone invariant conic valuations

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is reduced to exact coefficient identities. The one-dimensional case is handled separately. For \(d\ge2\), the verified fixed-dimensional classification gives \(\mu_d=\sum a_{d,k}v_k\). Since \(v_j(L_k)=\delta_{jk}\), the coefficient \(a_{d,k}\) equals the value on a \(k\)-plane. Orthogonal invariance and Cartesian-product additivity on \(\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}^{d-k}\) then give \(a_{d,k}=(d-k)\alpha+k\beta\), and the intrinsic-volume normalization gives the claimed formula. The converse uses the verified direct-product convolution. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2609.09335v2, classifies each dimension independently and does not state a Cartesian-product compatibility theorem. The classical canonicality theorem in arXiv:1303.6672v2 assumes the value on every linear subspace (plus localizability); the present normalized statement instead assumes only two one-dimensional values and product additivity, and derives all higher-dimensional subspace values. Oymak--Tropp record both canonicality language and direct-product additivity for statistical dimension, but do not classify arbitrary product-additive valuation families. Targeted semantic and exact-phrase searches found no covering statement. Residual risk remains that older valuation literature could use different monoidal terminology.

## Value
PASS. Product compatibility is intrinsic to conic geometry: intrinsic-volume distributions convolve under Cartesian products, and statistical dimension is their first moment. The theorem shows that this compatibility reduces the independent fixed-dimensional coefficient freedoms to a single affine first-moment law. The normalized corollary gives a concise structural characterization of statistical dimension without continuity or an all-subspace calibration hypothesis.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main recent input is Martin Lotz, arXiv:2609.09335v2, whose Theorem 1.1 classifies monotone rotation-invariant conic valuations. The main older comparator is Amelunxen--Lotz--McCoy--Tropp, arXiv:1303.6672v2, Sections 5.2 and 5.6, for the direct-product law and the localizable canonicality theorem. The claim is restricted to real-valued valuations on all closed convex cones with exact orthogonal invariance and exact Cartesian-product additivity; it does not address pointed-cone-only domains or approximate product laws.
