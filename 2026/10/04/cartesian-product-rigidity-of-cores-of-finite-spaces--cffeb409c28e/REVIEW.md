# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof identifies the strict upper set of a product point and shows that a minimum can exist only if at least one coordinate is maximal. When one coordinate is maximal, the strict upper set reduces exactly to the strict upper set of the other coordinate, giving the stated up-beat equivalence; the down-beat statement follows dually. Minimality of the product is then equivalent to minimality of both factors. Products of strong deformation retractions give a strong deformation retraction onto the product of cores, and the local theorem shows that this retract is minimal. The checker independently confirms the local equivalences on 2,281 finite-poset product pairs.

## Originality
PASS relative to the recorded searches and inspections. The closest primary full text inspected, Barmak–Minian, supplies the beat-point/core framework but contains no occurrence of “product” and does not state the product classification or core-cardinality formula. Searches using product-core, product-minimality, beat-point, Stong-core, and multiplicativity language did not locate an implication-equivalent statement. Older literature not available in full text remains an explicit residual risk.

## Value
PASS. Core cardinality is the exact size of the minimal representative in the strong homotopy type of a finite space. The theorem makes this invariant multiplicative under Cartesian products and proves that no hidden beat-point compression appears after multiplying two minimal finite spaces. It therefore replaces product-by-product reduction with a closed structural rule and gives a reusable local criterion for every point of a product.

## Closest literature and limitations
Barmak–Minian (arXiv:math/0611158; Advances in Mathematics 218 (2008), 87–104) is the closest inspected source: §2.2 states the beat-point definitions, beat-point deletion as a strong deformation retract, and core uniqueness. The theorem here is restricted to finite nonempty \(T_0\)-spaces and to Stong/beat-point cores; it does not extend the same conclusion to weak-point or simple-homotopy reductions.
