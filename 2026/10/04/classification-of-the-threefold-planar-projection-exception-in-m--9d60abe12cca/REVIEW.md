# Review of Classification of the threefold planar-projection exception in \(\mathbb R^3\)

## Correctness
PASS. The proof converts the per-projection translation freedom into the exact circle-frequency set \(\{0,\pm1,\pm3,\pm6,\ldots}\). The cited 2026 harmonic-projection lemma prevents cancellation between different spherical-harmonic degrees, while its great-circle frequency lemma forces a forbidden frequency \(2\) in every nonzero even degree \(\ell\ge2\) and a forbidden frequency \(5\) in every nonzero odd degree \(\ell\ge5\). This leaves exactly degrees \(0\), \(1\), and \(3\), and a cubic restricts to circle frequencies \(1\) and \(3\), proving the converse. The support-function curvature criterion then gives the exact positive-semidefinite condition. Oddness of degree three makes the antipodal curvature contribution change sign, yielding the stated operator-norm condition and constant width.

Risk: the proof relies on the published source lemmas being applied with their stated quantifiers to the closed rotation-invariant allowed-frequency space. The relevant theorem, reduction, and lemmas were read in the full preprint rather than inferred from snippets.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2609.27192v1, proves ball rigidity for orders \(q\ge4\) and gives a concrete degree-three example when \(q=3\), but does not state a complete characterization of all order-three bodies. Targeted searches for threefold projection symmetry, cubic spherical-harmonic support functions, the \(\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_3\) formulation, and constant-width cubic bodies did not locate an equivalent classification. The older arXiv:1611.09443v1 treats a stronger centered complete-symmetry condition and therefore does not imply the present classification with independently translated projections.

Residual risk: equivalent older work may exist under different terminology in spherical harmonic or geometric tomography literature. No decisive covering source was found in the targeted searches.

## Value
PASS. The 2026 paper identifies order three as the unique exceptional rotational order and supplies isolated non-spherical examples. Determining the entire exceptional class is a natural structural gap. The result converts that exception into an exact finite-dimensional moduli statement, shows every example is constant width after one global translation, and identifies the precise convexity body in the seven-dimensional degree-three harmonic space. This is materially stronger than checking another perturbative example or another numerical parameter value.

## Closest literature and limitations
The nearest source is arXiv:2609.27192v1, especially its order-three example and the harmonic lemmas used in the proof. arXiv:1611.09443v1 supplies a stronger centered-symmetry rigidity comparison but does not cover plane-by-plane translations. The claim is limited to \(\mathbb R^3\), does not quotient the moduli body by rotations, and does not assert strict convexity on its boundary.
