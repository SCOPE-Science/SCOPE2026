# Review of Coefficient-robust maximal valence for logharmonic polynomials

## Correctness

PASS. The recent extremal construction supplies exactly \(3n-1\) nonsingular zeros of an anti-rational fixed-point equation. Translating that equation to
\[
F_0(z)=p_0(z)\overline{q_0(z)}-1
\]
gives the exact differential identity
\[
dF_0=p_0\,d\overline H
\]
at every root. Hence all logharmonic roots are nonsingular, and conjugation reverses the source's orientation split, giving \(2n-1\) positive and \(n\) negative Jacobians.

The real-analytic implicit-function theorem then produces \(3n-1\) distinct root branches under simultaneous perturbation of every coefficient of \(p\) and \(q\). The independent global upper theorem supplies the missing global step: nearby maps cannot have any additional roots, so the local lower count is the exact total count.

## Originality

PASS. The full recent primary source was inspected through its sharpness theorem, fixed-point reduction, proof that the extremal zeros are nonsingular, orientation count, and its final openness remark. The only openness stated there varies the single construction parameter \(c\).

The earlier upper-bound paper was inspected through its main \(3n-1\) theorem and its stability lemma. That lemma holds the map fixed and varies the target value, rather than holding the target fixed while varying every polynomial coefficient.

Searches for coefficient robustness, full-parameter perturbation, open maximal-valence loci, and nonsingular \(3n-1\) logharmonic extremizers did not locate a covering statement. The residual risk is that the finite-dimensional implicit-function consequence may have been recorded informally or in a general perturbation reference without logharmonic terminology.

## Value

PASS. The sharpness theorem is existential, so a natural structural question is whether the extremizers are fine-tuned. The finding shows they are not: maximal valence persists on a full-dimensional open set in coefficient space at one fixed target. This is stronger than one-parameter stability and stronger than target-value stability of a fixed map.

The persistent orientation signature also gives a robust dynamical/topological profile of the extremal roots, useful for perturbative constructions and for distinguishing boundary degenerations from generic maximal-valence behavior.
