# Common rotational symmetry forces anisotropic Cheeger edge regularity

## Finding

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a planar convex body with \(k\)-fold rotational symmetry about a point \(o\), and let \(W\) be the centrally symmetric Wulff body of a norm, centered at the origin. Denote by \(C_W(\Omega)\) the unique planar \(W\)-anisotropic Cheeger set.

If an orthogonal map \(Q\) satisfies
\[
Q\Omega=\Omega
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
QW=W,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{QC_W(\Omega)=C_W(\Omega)}.
\]

Consequently, if \(W\) is \(\widetilde k\)-rotationally symmetric and
\[
g=\gcd(k,\widetilde k),
\]
then \(C_W(\Omega)\) is at least \(g\)-rotationally symmetric. In particular,
\[
\boxed{k\mid\widetilde k}
\]
is a sufficient divisibility condition for the anisotropic Cheeger set to inherit the full \(k\)-fold symmetry of \(\Omega\).

Under this sufficient condition, \(\partial C_W(\Omega)\) touches every one of Cañete's \(k\) edges of \(\Omega\). Thus the anisotropic analogues of both the symmetry-inheritance conclusion and the edge-contact conclusion hold whenever the anisotropy contains the domain's rotational symmetry. The Wulff body need not be polygonal, and no assumption concerning parallel sides is needed for these two conclusions.

## Assumptions and scope

The anisotropic perimeter is generated by a norm, so its Wulff body \(W\) is a centrally symmetric compact convex body with nonempty interior. The Cheeger problem is planar and the ambient domain is convex.

For compact convex sets \(A,B\), write
\[
A\oplus B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}
\]
for Minkowski addition and
\[
A\ominus B=\{x:x+B\subseteq A\}
\]
for Minkowski erosion.

Kawohl and Novaga's planar anisotropic Cheeger theorem states that for a bounded open convex planar domain there is a unique anisotropic Cheeger set and, with \(\rho=1/h_W(\Omega)\),
\[
C_W(\Omega)
=
(\Omega\ominus\rho W)\oplus\rho W,
\]
where \(\rho\) is the unique positive number satisfying
\[
|\Omega\ominus\rho W|=\rho^2|W|.
\]
We use the compact-body version obtained by taking closures; this does not alter the boundary-contact or symmetry statements.

Cañete's \(k\) edges are the \(k\) boundary arcs cyclically permuted by the basic rotation through angle \(2\pi/k\). The only fact needed below is that the rotation carries each edge to the next and that the edges cover \(\partial\Omega\).

## Proof

Put
\[
A=\Omega\ominus\rho W,
\qquad
C=A\oplus\rho W,
\]
where \(\rho=1/h_W(\Omega)\). By the planar anisotropic structure theorem, \(C=C_W(\Omega)\).

First suppose that \(Q\) is orthogonal with
\[
Q\Omega=\Omega,
\qquad
QW=W.
\]
For every \(x\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
x\in A
\iff
x+\rho W\subseteq\Omega.
\]
Applying \(Q\) and using \(QW=W\) and \(Q\Omega=\Omega\),
\[
x+\rho W\subseteq\Omega
\iff
Qx+\rho W\subseteq\Omega.
\]
Hence
\[
QA=A.
\]
Minkowski addition also commutes with \(Q\), so
\[
QC
=Q(A\oplus\rho W)
=QA\oplus\rho QW
=A\oplus\rho W
=C.
\]
This proves invariance under every orthogonal symmetry common to the domain and the Wulff body.

Now assume \(\Omega\) is \(k\)-rotationally symmetric and \(W\) is \(\widetilde k\)-rotationally symmetric. The rotations generated by angles \(2\pi/k\) and \(2\pi/\widetilde k\) contain a common cyclic subgroup of order
\[
g=\gcd(k,\widetilde k).
\]
Therefore \(C_W(\Omega)\) is invariant under that \(g\)-fold rotation. If \(k\mid\widetilde k\), then rotation by \(2\pi/k\) is itself a symmetry of \(W\), so the full \(k\)-fold symmetry of \(\Omega\) is inherited.

It remains to prove edge contact under full common \(k\)-fold symmetry. The area identity
\[
|A|=\rho^2|W|>0
\]
shows that \(A\) has nonempty interior. Choose \(z\in\partial A\). By the definition of erosion,
\[
z+\rho W\subseteq\Omega.
\]
This translate must touch \(\partial\Omega\). Indeed, if it were contained in the interior of \(\Omega\), compactness would give a positive Euclidean clearance from \(z+\rho W\) to \(\partial\Omega\). Every sufficiently small translate of \(z\) would then remain in \(A\), contradicting \(z\in\partial A\).

Hence there is
\[
q\in(z+\rho W)\cap\partial\Omega.
\]
Since
\[
z+\rho W\subseteq C\subseteq\Omega,
\]
we have \(q\in C\cap\partial\Omega\), and therefore
\[
q\in\partial C\cap\partial\Omega.
\]
Thus at least one boundary contact exists.

Let \(R\) be rotation through \(2\pi/k\). We have already proved \(RC=C\), and by assumption \(R\Omega=\Omega\). Therefore every point in the orbit
\[
q,Rq,\ldots,R^{k-1}q
\]
is again a point of \(\partial C\cap\partial\Omega\). If \(q\) lies in the interior of one Cañete edge, its orbit supplies one contact in every edge. If \(q\) is one of the dots separating adjacent edges, its orbit consists of all corresponding dots, so every edge again has a boundary-contact endpoint. Hence \(\partial C_W(\Omega)\) touches all \(k\) edges.

## Verification

The proof has three independently checkable steps.

First, the anisotropic Cheeger representation and uniqueness are exactly the planar convex theorem of Kawohl and Novaga. Second, the symmetry step is a direct set-theoretic equivalence for Minkowski erosion followed by equivariance of Minkowski addition; it does not use approximation, smoothness, or a numerical computation. Third, boundary contact follows from compactness: a translate \(z+\rho W\) attached to a boundary point of the erosion cannot have positive clearance from \(\partial\Omega\).

The group-theoretic statement is elementary: the cyclic groups generated by rotations through \(2\pi/k\) and \(2\pi/\widetilde k\) have a common cyclic subgroup of order \(\gcd(k,\widetilde k)\), and \(k\mid\widetilde k\) makes the domain's generator a Wulff symmetry.

No finite experiment is used as evidence for the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Cañete proves in the Euclidean case that every \(k\)-rotationally symmetric planar convex body is \(k\)-Cheeger-regular and that its Cheeger set inherits the same rotational symmetry. In Remark 10 he explicitly identifies the anisotropic extension as future work, predicts strong dependence on the Wulff shape, and suggests that for a \(k\)-rotational polygon the Wulff shape should have a rotational order satisfying a divisibility relation with \(k\).

Kawohl and Novaga supply the planar anisotropic structure theorem needed to make that suggestion precise: the unique Cheeger set is the Minkowski sum of a Wulff erosion and a scaled Wulff body.

The present result gives a clean sufficient relation:
\[
k\mid\widetilde k.
\]
More generally it identifies the common rotational subgroup through \(\gcd(k,\widetilde k)\), and it proves edge contact when full \(k\)-symmetry is common. Targeted searches for anisotropic Cheeger symmetry, Wulff rotational symmetry, and divisibility did not locate this statement in later literature. A 2022--2023 paper on optimizing the anisotropic Cheeger constant with respect to the anisotropy studies a different optimization problem and does not state the common-symmetry theorem.

## Limitations

The divisibility condition is proved sufficient, not necessary. Additional accidental symmetry of a particular Cheeger set may occur when the displayed group-theoretic condition fails.

The theorem concerns convex planar domains, where the anisotropic Cheeger set is unique and has the Minkowski representation used above. It does not treat nonconvex domains, where uniqueness can fail.

The result establishes rotational symmetry inheritance and Cañete-edge contact. It does not classify which individual polygon sides are touched, nor does it analyze the finer parallel-side phenomena anticipated in Cañete's remark.

Because common-symmetry inheritance also follows abstractly from uniqueness and invariance of the anisotropic functional, that part alone is structurally simple. The substantive refinement here is the explicit divisibility criterion together with the edge-contact conclusion from the anisotropic Minkowski representation. An equivalent observation could exist in unindexed literature, although no such statement was found in the inspected sources and targeted searches.
