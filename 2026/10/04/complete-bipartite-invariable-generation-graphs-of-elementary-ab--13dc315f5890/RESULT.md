# Complete bipartite invariable-generation graphs of elementary-abelian cyclic Frobenius groups

## Finding

Let \(p\) be a prime, let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbf F_p\), and let
\[
H=\langle h\rangle\cong C_m,
\qquad m>1,
\]
act fixed-point-freely on \(V\setminus\{0\}\). Put
\[
G=V\rtimes H.
\]

Since the action is fixed-point-free, \(p\nmid m\), so \(V\) is semisimple as an \(\mathbf F_pH\)-module. Write
\[
V\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}U_i^{e_i},
\]
where the \(U_i\) are pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible \(\mathbf F_pH\)-modules, and put
\[
d_i=\dim_{\mathbf F_p}U_i.
\]

Then \(G\) is invariably \(2\)-generated if and only if
\[
e_i=1
\qquad
\text{for every }i.
\]

Assume this multiplicity-free condition. Define
\[
A=\frac1m\prod_{i=1}^{s}(p^{d_i}-1).
\]
Then the graph obtained from the invariably generating graph by deleting isolated conjugacy classes is
\[
\boxed{\Lambda^+(G)\cong K_{A,\varphi(m)}}.
\]

One part consists of the \(H\)-orbits of cyclic vectors of the \(\mathbf F_pH\)-module \(V\). The other consists of the conjugacy classes
\[
Vh^j,
\qquad
\gcd(j,m)=1.
\]

Every other nontrivial conjugacy class is isolated. Hence the number of isolated nontrivial classes is
\[
\boxed{\frac{|V|-1}{m}-A+m-1-\varphi(m)}.
\]

In particular, \(\Lambda^+(G)\) is connected, with diameter at most \(2\), whenever \(G\) is invariably \(2\)-generated.

For
\[
G=\operatorname{AGL}(1,q)=\mathbf F_q\rtimes\mathbf F_q^\times,
\]
the kernel is irreducible and \(m=q-1\), so \(A=1\). Therefore
\[
\Lambda^+(G)\cong K_{1,\varphi(q-1)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The invariably generating graph \(\Lambda(X)\) of a finite group \(X\) has the nontrivial conjugacy classes of \(X\) as vertices. Two distinct classes \(C,D\) are adjacent when every pair of representatives
\[
c\in C,
\qquad
d\in D
\]
generates \(X\). The graph \(\Lambda^+(X)\) is obtained by deleting isolated vertices.

The action of \(H\) on \(V\) is faithful and fixed-point-free away from \(0\). Thus \(G\) is a Frobenius group with elementary-abelian kernel and cyclic complement.

No irreducibility assumption is made: reducible multiplicity-free modules are included. Repeated irreducible constituents are allowed in the statement and are exactly the obstruction to invariable \(2\)-generation.

## Proof

First, \(p\nmid m\). Otherwise \(H\) would contain an element of order \(p\), and its action on the nonzero \(\mathbf F_p\)-space \(V\) would have a nonzero fixed vector. Therefore Maschke's theorem applies.

For \(v\ne0\), conjugation by \(V\) fixes \(v\), while conjugation by \(H\) sends it through its \(H\)-orbit. Since the action is fixed-point-free,
\[
|v^H|=m.
\]
Thus every nontrivial kernel conjugacy class is an \(H\)-orbit of size \(m\).

Now let
\[
1\le j<m.
\]
The linear map
\[
1-h^j
\]
is invertible, because \(h^j\ne1\) has no nonzero fixed vector. Conjugating \(h^j\) by elements of \(V\) therefore produces every element of the coset
\[
Vh^j.
\]
Hence \(Vh^j\) is one conjugacy class. Distinct values of \(j\) give distinct classes because their images in the abelian quotient
\[
G/V\cong C_m
\]
are distinct.

No two kernel classes are adjacent, because their representatives generate a subgroup of \(V\). No two classes outside the kernel are adjacent: from each such class choose its representative \(h^j\) in the fixed complement \(H\); the chosen pair then generates a subgroup of \(H\), which is proper in \(G\).

It remains to decide when a kernel class represented by \(v\ne0\) is adjacent to the class \(Vh^j\).

Necessity of
\[
\gcd(j,m)=1
\]
is immediate from the quotient: otherwise the images of the pair cannot generate \(G/V\).

Assume now that \(\gcd(j,m)=1\). Every representative of \(Vh^j\) is conjugate by an element of \(V\) to \(h^j\). Conjugation by \(V\) fixes every element of the abelian kernel \(V\). Therefore the adjacency condition is equivalent to asking whether
\[
\langle v',h^j\rangle=G
\]
for every \(v'\in v^H\).

Since \(h^j\) generates \(H\), the kernel generated by conjugates of \(v'\) is precisely its \(\mathbf F_pH\)-span. Hence adjacency holds exactly when \(v\) is a cyclic vector for the \(\mathbf F_pH\)-module \(V\).

We next characterize cyclic vectors. Since \(\mathbf F_pH\) is a commutative semisimple algebra, each irreducible \(U_i\) has a field of endomorphisms
\[
E_i=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf F_pH}(U_i),
\]
and the isotypic component \(U_i^{e_i}\) is an \(E_i\)-space of dimension \(e_i\). The cyclic submodule generated by a single vector has \(E_i\)-dimension at most \(1\) in this component. Therefore, if some \(e_i\ge2\), no cyclic vector exists.

Conversely, if every \(e_i=1\), the semisimple algebra has central idempotents projecting to the distinct constituents. A vector
\[
v=(v_1,\ldots,v_s)
\]
is cyclic exactly when
\[
v_i\ne0
\qquad
\text{for every }i.
\]
Thus the number of cyclic vectors is
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{s}(p^{d_i}-1).
\]

The action of \(H\) on nonzero vectors is free, so the number of kernel conjugacy classes represented by cyclic vectors is
\[
A=\frac1m\prod_{i=1}^{s}(p^{d_i}-1).
\]

There are exactly \(\varphi(m)\) outside classes \(Vh^j\) with \(\gcd(j,m)=1\). Every cyclic-vector class is adjacent to every one of these classes, and the preceding necessity arguments show there are no other edges. Therefore
\[
\Lambda^+(G)\cong K_{A,\varphi(m)}.
\]

The total number of nontrivial kernel classes is
\[
\frac{|V|-1}{m},
\]
and there are \(m-1\) nontrivial outside classes. Deleting the \(A\) active kernel classes and the \(\varphi(m)\) active outside classes gives
\[
\frac{|V|-1}{m}-A+m-1-\varphi(m)
\]
isolated classes.

Finally, an invariable generating pair exists exactly when the graph has an edge, which occurs exactly when a cyclic vector exists, equivalently exactly when every \(e_i=1\).

## Relationship to prior work

Garzoni introduced the connectivity problem for the graph \(\Lambda^+(G)\) and asked whether it is connected whenever \(G\) is a finite soluble, invariably \(2\)-generated group. The same paper notes that this is known for nilpotent groups but leaves the soluble case open.

Detomi and Lucchini developed general criteria for invariable generation in groups with elementary-abelian normal subgroups. Their lifting criterion for semidirect products gives strong existence information for invariable generating sets, but it does not state the conjugacy-class graph decomposition above, the exact complete-bipartite structure, the isolated-class formula, or the multiplicity-free classification for this cyclic Frobenius family.

The theorem here gives a complete graph-level answer for all elementary-abelian-kernel Frobenius groups with cyclic complement. It includes reducible multiplicity-free modules and identifies repeated irreducible constituents as the exact obstruction to invariable \(2\)-generation.

Targeted searches using invariable-generation graph, affine group, Frobenius group, elementary-abelian kernel, cyclic complement, multiplicity-free module, and complete-bipartite terminology did not locate an equivalent graph theorem.

## Limitations

The complement is required to be cyclic and fixed-point-free on the nonzero kernel.

The kernel is elementary abelian. More general nilpotent Frobenius kernels can have substantially different conjugacy-class and generation behavior.

The theorem resolves the published connectivity question only for this family, not for all finite soluble groups.

The existence component is compatible with broader semidirect-product generation criteria in the literature. The originality claim concerns the exact graph classification, the multiplicity-free iff criterion for this family, and the closed counts of active and isolated conjugacy classes.

An equivalent formulation may exist under affine-primitive or Frobenius-generation terminology not indexed by the searches used here.
