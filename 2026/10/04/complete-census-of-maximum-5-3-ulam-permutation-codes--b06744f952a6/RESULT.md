# Complete census of maximum \((5,3)\) Ulam permutation codes
## Finding
Let \(S_5\) be the permutations of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) in one-line notation, and let
\[
d_U(\pi,\sigma)=5-L(\pi,\sigma),
\]
where \(L(\pi,\sigma)\) is the length of a longest common subsequence. The previously known optimum for minimum Ulam distance at least \(3\) is \(A(5,3)=4\). There are exactly \(4020\) labeled maximum codes \(C\subseteq S_5\) of size \(4\). Every one of these \(4020\) codes is equidistant: for all distinct \(\pi,\sigma\in C\), one has \(d_U(\pi,\sigma)=3\).

Let \(G\) be the \(240\)-element isometry subgroup generated by arbitrary common relabelings of the five symbols and simultaneous reversal of one-line positions. The \(4020\) maximum codes split into exactly \(24\) \(G\)-orbits: five orbits have size \(60\), seven have size \(120\), and twelve have size \(240\). Equivalently, the stabilizer orders inside \(G\) are respectively \(4\), \(2\), and \(1\).
## Assumptions and scope
The code alphabet is exactly \(S_5\), not multipermutations. Distance is the standard Ulam/translocation distance. A labeled code is a literal subset of \(S_5\); no quotient is taken when counting the \(4020\) maxima. The orbit statement concerns only the explicitly defined subgroup \(G\); it does not assert that \(G\) is the full isometry group.

The optimum \(A(5,3)=4\) is prior work. The finding here is the complete extremizer census, universal equidistance, and the stated orbit decomposition.
## Proof
Construct a graph \(H\) with vertex set \(S_5\), joining two permutations exactly when their Ulam distance is at least \(3\). Then Ulam codes of minimum distance at least \(3\) are precisely cliques of \(H\).

For each pair of permutations, compute \(L(\pi,\sigma)\) by mapping the symbols of one permutation to their positions in the other and taking the longest increasing subsequence. Exhaustive construction gives \(120\) vertices, \(2520\) edges, and degree \(42\) at every vertex.

An exact Bron--Kerbosch enumeration with pivoting visits the complete maximal-clique search tree. Its maximal-clique histogram is
\[
60\text{ of size }2,\qquad 120\text{ of size }3,\qquad 4020\text{ of size }4,
\]
with no larger maximal clique. Hence the maximum size is \(4\) and there are exactly \(4020\) labeled maximum codes.

For every maximum code, all six internal distances are recomputed directly. In every case the six distances are all equal to \(3\); thus no maximum code contains a pair at Ulam distance \(4\).

Finally, for every \(\alpha\in S_5\), common relabeling \(\pi\mapsto\alpha\circ\pi\) preserves common subsequences, and reversing both one-line strings preserves common-subsequence length. The resulting \(2\cdot 5!=240\) transformations are distinct and preserve every pairwise Ulam distance. Applying all of them to all \(4020\) maxima and partitioning into orbits yields orbit-size multiset
\[
60^5,\quad 120^7,\quad 240^{12},
\]
which sums to \(4020\). Orbit--stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(4,2,1\).
## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the same directory. The verifier reconstructs all \(120\) permutations, all Ulam distances and graph edges, exhaustively enumerates maximal cliques, checks all six distances in every maximum code, constructs all \(240\) declared isometries, verifies their distance preservation on every unordered vertex pair, and recomputes all maximum-code orbits. It also regenerates `orbit_representatives.json`.

The expected terminal line begins with `VERIFY_OK` and reports `maximum=4`, `labeled_maxima=4020`, `equidistant_maxima=4020`, `orbits=24`, and `orbit_sizes=60x5,120x7,240x12`.
## Relationship to prior work
Farnoud, Skachek, and Milenkovic introduced the translocation model and proved that the translocation distance equals the Ulam distance \(n-L\); their treatment also proves left-invariance under common relabeling. Their arXiv version first appeared on 2012-02-05.

Göloğlu, Lember, Riet, and Skachek later studied finite Ulam codes by integer programming and computer search. For the exact parameter cell \(n=5,d=3\), their Example III.1 explicitly states that the actual optimum is \(A(5,3)=4\), after an integer-programming bound of \(5\). Their published finite table records the optimum size but not a count or classification of all extremizers.

Targeted searches for the exact parameter, the number \(4020\), equidistant maximum codes, maximum-clique censuses, and relabeling/reversal classifications did not locate a source implying the census, equidistance statement, or \(24\)-orbit decomposition. Later Ulam-sphere and diametric/anticode work addresses different quantities and does not imply this extremizer classification.
## Limitations
The result is a finite exact classification for one parameter cell. It does not give a formula for larger \(n\) or other distances. The \(24\)-orbit statement is with respect to the specified \(240\)-element subgroup, not a claim about the full isometry group. Originality searches cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed enumeration that was not retrieved.
