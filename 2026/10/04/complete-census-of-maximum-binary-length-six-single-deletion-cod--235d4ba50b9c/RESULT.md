# Complete census of maximum binary length-six single-deletion codes
## Finding
For binary single-deletion-correcting codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\), the known optimum is \(A(6,1)=10\). The complete extremizer census consists of exactly nine labeled maximum codes. Every one of the nine contains the four-word core
\[
\{000000,001100,110011,111111\}.
\]
Exactly one maximum is perfect: \(\mathrm{VT}_0(6)\). Among the eight nonperfect maxima, four have \(30\) distinct length-five descendants, three have \(28\), and one has \(26\). Under the group generated by reversal and global bit complement, the nine maxima form six orbits of sizes \(2,2,2,1,1,1\).

## Assumptions and scope
For a word \(x=x_1\cdots x_6\), let \(D_1(x)\) be the set of distinct length-five words obtained by deleting one coordinate. A code corrects one deletion exactly when \(D_1(x)\cap D_1(y)=\varnothing\) for all distinct \(x,y\in C\). A maximum code means a code of largest possible cardinality in the full space \(\{0,1\}^6\). A code is called perfect here when the descendant sets of its codewords partition all \(32\) binary words of length five. The symmetry statement is only for the explicit four-element group generated by reversal and bit complement; no claim about the full automorphism group of the confusability graph is made.

## Proof
Build the compatibility graph whose \(64\) vertices are all binary words of length six, joining two words precisely when their one-deletion descendant sets are disjoint. Single-deletion-correcting codes are exactly cliques in this graph.

The bundled verifier performs a complete Bron--Kerbosch enumeration of maximal cliques using exact integer bitsets. It finds \(62,707\) maximal cliques in total, maximum clique size \(10\), and exactly nine cliques of that size. The file `maxima.json` stores all nine; the verifier regenerates them from the graph and requires byte-level equality of the sorted codeword lists, so the census is not inferred from a sample or a timeout.

Intersecting the nine regenerated maxima gives exactly the four-word core above. For each maximum the verifier explicitly forms all ten descendant sets and checks pairwise disjointness. Their union sizes are
\[
26,28,28,28,30,30,30,30,32.
\]
Hence exactly one maximum partitions all \(32\) possible received words. Direct evaluation of the Varshamov--Tenengolts checksum \(\sum_{i=1}^6 i x_i\equiv0\pmod 7\) shows that this unique perfect maximum is \(\mathrm{VT}_0(6)\).

Finally, reversal \(x_1\cdots x_6\mapsto x_6\cdots x_1\) and global complement \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\) preserve deletion-confusability. Applying their four generated transformations to all nine maxima partitions them into orbit sizes \(2,2,2,1,1,1\), which the verifier recomputes directly.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `maxima.json`. The expected terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK vertices=64 maximal_cliques=62707 maximum=10 labeled_maxima=9 core=4 perfect=1 coverage=26x1,28x3,30x4,32x1 orbits=1x3,2x3 group_size=4`

A separate NetworkX maximal-clique enumeration during the review also returned \(62,707\) maximal cliques and the same nine size-ten codes. This second implementation is a cross-check, not an independent audit.

## Relationship to prior work
Sloane's survey gives the exact binary single-deletion optimum sequence through length eight, including \(A(6,1)=10\), and presents \(\mathrm{VT}_0(6)\) as an optimal construction. It also explicitly observes that two words of \(\mathrm{VT}_0(6)\) can be replaced by two others to obtain a nonperfect optimum. The present finding completes that length-six observation: there are exactly nine maxima in total, only one is perfect, and the remaining eight have the stated coverage and symmetry structure.

No's later full-text treatment states that VT codes are optimal for block lengths at most ten and formulates the same problem as a maximum-independent-set problem in the deletion-confusability graph. The inspected discussion concerns bounds and optimization formulations, not an enumeration or classification of all length-six optimizers.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact classification for binary length six and one deletion. It does not give a new proof of general VT optimality, does not classify lengths larger than six, and does not identify the full automorphism group of the length-six graph. An obscure or unindexed earlier finite census could still exist; targeted literature and published-finding corpus searches did not locate one.
