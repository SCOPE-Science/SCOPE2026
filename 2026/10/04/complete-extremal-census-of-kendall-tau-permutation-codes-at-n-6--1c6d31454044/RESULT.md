# Complete extremal census of Kendall-tau permutation codes at \(n=6,d=10\)
## Finding
For Kendall-tau permutation codes in \(S_6\) with minimum distance at least \(10\), the known maximum size is \(4\). Among these maximum four-word codes there are exactly \(3{,}960\) labeled codes; every maximum is equidistant, with all six pairwise Kendall distances equal to \(10\); and under the natural \(1{,}440\)-element isometry group generated by arbitrary relabeling of the six ranked items and reversal of every ranking, the maxima form exactly four orbits of sizes \(360,720,1{,}440,1{,}440\).

One representative of each orbit, written in one-line notation, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\{123456,165432,364251,524631\},\\
&\{123456,165432,264531,354621\},\\
&\{123456,165432,256431,346521\},\\
&\{123456,165432,356241,426531\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The corresponding orbit sizes are respectively \(360\), \(720\), \(1{,}440\), and \(1{,}440\).

## Assumptions and scope
A permutation is treated as a ranking of six distinct items. For \(\pi,\sigma\in S_6\), the Kendall distance \(d_K(\pi,\sigma)\) is the number of unordered item-pairs whose relative order differs between \(\pi\) and \(\sigma\), equivalently the minimum number of adjacent swaps needed to transform one ranking into the other. A code is a subset \(C\subseteq S_6\) with \(d_K(\pi,\sigma)\ge 10\) for every two distinct codewords. The value \(P(6,10)=4\) is prior work; the finding here is the complete census, equidistance rigidity, and orbit decomposition of the maximum four-word codes.

The symmetry group used for the orbit statement has \(6!\cdot 2=1{,}440\) elements. Its generators are simultaneous relabelings of the six items in every ranking and reversal of every one-line ranking. Both operations preserve pairwise-order disagreements and hence preserve Kendall distance.

## Proof
Build the graph \(G\) on the \(720\) permutations in \(S_6\), joining two vertices exactly when their Kendall distance is at least \(10\). A code of minimum distance at least \(10\) is exactly a clique of \(G\).

Direct generation gives \(60{,}840\) edges; every vertex has degree \(169\). Exact increasing-order clique enumeration then finds exactly \(3{,}960\) four-cliques and no five-clique. Thus the independently recomputed maximum clique size is \(4\), agreeing with the published value \(P(6,10)=4\). For each of the \(3{,}960\) four-cliques, recomputing its six pairwise distances gives the same multiset
\[
\{10,10,10,10,10,10\},
\]
which proves the equidistance assertion.

For the orbit census, act on every four-clique by all item relabelings and by reversal. The generated set has \(1{,}440\) maps. Starting from an unassigned maximum code, compute its full image set and remove that orbit from the remaining maxima. Repetition exhausts all \(3{,}960\) maxima in four disjoint orbits of sizes \(360\), \(720\), \(1{,}440\), and \(1{,}440\); their sizes sum to \(3{,}960\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs \(S_6\), Kendall distance, the threshold graph, all four- and five-cliques, all pairwise distance profiles of the maxima, and the \(1{,}440\)-element symmetry action from definitions. It uses only the Python standard library and no stored list of maxima. A successful replay prints:

`VERIFY_OK maximum=4 labeled_maxima=3960 equidistant=3960 natural_orbits=4 orbit_sizes=360,720,1440,1440 edges=60840 degree=169`

The four representative codes are also listed in `orbit_representatives.txt` and are checked by the verifier.

## Relationship to prior work
Barg and Mazumdar introduced the rank-modulation coding problem in this Kendall space and defined \(A(n,d)\) for permutation codes; their 2009 preprint supplies the archive-era source and the coding-theoretic motivation. Vijayakumaran later determined the values of \(P(6,d)\) for \(d\ge 4\) by integer programming and supplied attaining examples. Parvaresh et al. prove the broader theorem \(P(n,d)=4\) for \(n\ge 6\) and \(\frac{3}{5}\binom{n}{2}<d\le\frac{2}{3}\binom{n}{2}\), which includes \(P(6,10)=4\). These results therefore cover the optimum value but do not imply a count of all maximum codes, equidistance of every maximum, or the four-orbit decomposition recorded here. Targeted searches for the exact parameters, the count \(3{,}960\), equidistant formulations, and symmetry classifications did not locate such a census.

## Limitations
The classification is only for \(S_6\) at minimum distance \(10\). The orbit statement is with respect to the explicitly defined natural \(1{,}440\)-element isometry group; no claim is made here that this is the full abstract isometry group of the Kendall metric space. The full text of the 2016 small-order exact-value paper was not accessible in this run; its indexed abstract describes exact values and attaining instances rather than an all-extremizer classification, but an unadvertised census there or in another unindexed source remains a residual originality risk.
