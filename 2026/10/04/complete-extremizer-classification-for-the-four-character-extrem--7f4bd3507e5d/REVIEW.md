# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof first invokes the standard equality criterion for extremal measures, reducing the problem to unit-modulus coefficients with flat Fourier modulus. For the embedded length-five sequence, flatness is exactly equivalent to two cyclic autocorrelation equations and their conjugates. The first equation is a sum of three unit vectors, so the equilateral-triple lemma is exhaustive. Substitution into the second equation yields \(t^5=\omega^q\); the representation redundancy then normalizes this to \(t^5=1\) and leaves precisely two residue choices. The converse is checked directly. No finite numerical experiment is used to infer an infinite or continuous statement.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Graham-Ramsey (1981), proves that the support \(\{0,1,2,3\}\subset\mathbb Z_5\) is extremal and classifies four-element extremal supports, not all extremizing coefficient vectors. Graham (arXiv:1910.00924v1) explicitly classifies all extremal measures for the analogous three-point support in \(\mathbb Z_4\), but for this four-point \(\mathbb Z_5\) support gives a witness in Proposition 7.5 and separately records two different witnesses. The inspected text does not state the ten-solution classification or its two modulation orbits. Searches under Sidon, extremal-measure, finite-cyclic, roots-of-unity, and complex-weighing formulations found no stronger covering result. Residual risk remains for older literature indexed under sequence or weighing-matrix terminology.

## Value
PASS. The finding closes a natural equality-case question on one of the smallest exceptional finite-cyclic extreme supports already singled out in the classical four-element classification. It replaces observed nonuniqueness by a complete structural description: exactly ten normalized solutions, two character-modulation orbits, and conjugation as the bridge between them. The proof is short, reusable, and reveals why cube-root and fifteenth-root witnesses coexist.

## Closest literature and limitations
The known Sidon constant \(2\) and support extremality are prior work and are not claimed. The result is confined to this one support. The older 1981 article has only month/issue-level public dating in the inspected bibliographic records; no day-level publication date was invented.
