# Review

## Correctness
PASS. A line-contained exponential basis forces the scalar projection to be essentially injective. The projected measure is then spectrally equivalent to the original one. Uniformity of absolutely continuous spectral measures forces the two projected densities to agree; scaling the direction gives \(|\langle u,v_j\rangle|=c_j\) and reduces the problem to Lebesgue measure on two disjoint intervals of total length one. Łaba's exhaustive two-interval theorem translates exactly into the integer-gap lattice branch and the equal-mass two-coset branch. The converse follows from the same projection equivalence. The explicit exceptional example is independently replayed by the packaged script.

## Originality
PASS, with a stated residual risk. The 2026 weighted two-segment paper proves existence of one line spectrum for non-coplanar segments and constructs it by making the projected intervals adjacent. The earlier projection theorem gives an abstract equivalence with a one-dimensional projection, and Łaba classifies the one-dimensional two-interval problem. None of the inspected sources states the resulting complete weighted non-coplanar line-spectrum list or isolates the equal-mass two-coset exception. Targeted published-finding corpus searches returned no matching claim. The result is therefore a new explicit synthesis/classification rather than a new foundational theorem.

## Value
PASS. The recent source leaves the set of possible line spectra in the intrinsically three-dimensional non-coplanar case undescribed. A complete classification is a natural next question. The theorem shows that unequal segment masses rigidly force every line spectrum to be a lattice, while equal masses alone permit a non-lattice two-coset branch, and it produces a concrete non-lattice line spectrum for the paper's basic skew example. This is structural information not supplied by a routine numerical recomputation.

The closest literature is the 2026 weighted existence theorem, the 2025/2026 projection principle, and Łaba's one-dimensional classification. The main limitation is that nonlinear spectra, which do exist for skew pairs, are outside the claim.
