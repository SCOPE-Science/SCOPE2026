# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The source's exact criterion
\[
\frac{\mu}{n}+\sum_{p\mid n}\frac1p\in\mathbb Z
\]
reduces the problem immediately to the three multiplicative shapes allowed by \(\Omega(n)\le2\). Under \(0<\mu<n\), the resulting positive rational integer is strictly less than \(2\) in every case, hence equals \(1\). This yields the three claimed formulas, and direct substitution proves their converses. The \(673\) corollary is an exhaustive discriminant and factor-pair check.

## Originality
PASS. The primary paper states the general criterion and the large-witness conjecture but does not specialize it to a complete low-\(\Omega\) classification. It reports \(673\) as a parameter with no witness found through \(10^{10}\), without an unbounded prime-factor obstruction. Searches using semiprime terminology, the equivalent formula \(\mu+1=(p-1)(q-1)\), the three-family classification, and the exact \(673\) consequence found no covering theorem. The closest later \(\mu\)-Sondow problem located concerns an additional strongly pseudoperfect condition.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves the entire \(\Omega(n)\le2\) layer of the source's open large-witness problem and gives a structural explanation for the computationally difficult case \(\mu=673\): every possible future witness must have at least three prime factors counted with multiplicity. This is a natural multiplicative-complexity boundary rather than a bounded census.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Grau–Oller-Marcén–Sadornil, “On \(\mu\)-Sondow Numbers,” which supplies the defining equivalences and Conjecture 1(ii). The present theorem does not address witnesses with \(\Omega(n)\ge3\) and does not settle the conjecture for \(673\) or general \(\mu\).
