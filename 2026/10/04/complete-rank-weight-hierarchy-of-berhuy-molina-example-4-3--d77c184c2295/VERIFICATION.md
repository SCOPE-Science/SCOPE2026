`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library.

It implements \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3(i)\) with \(i^2=-1\), the coefficient subfield containing every non-base-field coefficient used by the example. It verifies \((x-i)(x+i)=x^2+1\), reconstructs the first active component generated by \(x-i\), and checks its three generator shifts.

The verifier checks that \((1,0,1,0)\) and \((0,1,0,1)\) are independent base-field codewords in that component, giving rank-support weights \(1\) and \(2\). It expands the full component generator over the basis \(1,i\) and verifies that the resulting base-field support rows have rank \(4\). It also checks that the second active component is generated by \((1,2,1)\), hence has rank weight \(1\).

Finally it evaluates the exact two-component generalized-weight convolution and checks that the output is \((1,2,3,5)\). The general primary-decomposition theorem is a cited mathematical input rather than a machine-verified theorem; its hypotheses for this example are checked explicitly.
