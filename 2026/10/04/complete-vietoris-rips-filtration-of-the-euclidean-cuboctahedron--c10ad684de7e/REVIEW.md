# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof identifies all four possible nonzero pairwise distances from the coordinates. At the three nontrivial thresholds it gives explicit structural models: the cube nerve of the eight triangular faces, the common face nerve with the geometric cuboctahedron boundary, and the join of six antipodal \(S^0\) factors. The standalone verifier exhaustively reconstructs every simplex and maximal simplex, checks mod-two homology independently, and verifies acyclic discrete-Morse matchings at the first three thresholds.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary full text determines the five Platonic solids and explicitly names Archimedean solids as a possible generalization; the cuboctahedron is not covered by that theorem. Exact alias searches using cuboctahedron, cuboctahedral graph, line graph of the cube, rectified cube, and rectified octahedron found no checked source with the same statement. The related thesis abstract does not state the result, but its full text was not machine-readable, so this is recorded as a residual risk rather than treated as negative evidence.

## Value
PASS. The cuboctahedron is the most symmetric rectification connecting the cube and octahedron and is a natural first Archimedean test case for the explicitly proposed extension beyond Platonic solids. Its filtration exhibits three different nontrivial homotopy types, \(\bigvee^5S^1\), \(S^2\), and \(S^5\), so the computation reveals a genuine topology-changing pattern rather than a single isolated Betti number.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main comparison is Saleh--Titz Mite--Witzel, *Vietoris–Rips complexes of Platonic solids* (arXiv:2302.14388), whose theorem is restricted to Platonic solids and whose introduction explicitly points to Archimedean solids as a future direction. Saleh's related thesis was inspectable only through repository metadata and abstract. The result is limited to the finite vertex metric and does not cover the whole cuboctahedral surface or intrinsic distances.
