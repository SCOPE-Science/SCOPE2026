# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the exact prime-power valuation formula in the source. For \(p=3\), the only nontrivial order is \(2\); for \(p=5\), the only nontrivial orders are \(2\) and \(4\). In each case the relevant \(p\)-power divisor is bounded by half of an even cyclotomic factor, and the remaining \(p\)-power from \(a+1\) is bounded by an elementary power of \(q\). This gives a strict floor bound for every \(q>p\). The case \(q=p\) has valuation zero.

## Originality
PASS, with an explicit access-limited residual risk. The primary source states Conjecture 1.6 and does not supply these two special-case proofs. Targeted searches did not locate a prior \(p=3\) or \(p=5\) verification of condition (2). Zhao and Chen later prove the global valuation inequality without conditions, but their accessible abstract does not state the stronger local condition. Their full text was not accessible through the lawful open sources inspected, so possible overlap there remains a named risk.

## Value
PASS. The result verifies the first two odd-prime cases of a stated structural conjecture and explains the role of the small multiplicative-order sets modulo \(3\) and \(5\). It is not merely a recomputation of the later unconditional global bound.
