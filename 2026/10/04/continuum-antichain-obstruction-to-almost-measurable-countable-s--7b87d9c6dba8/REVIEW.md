# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The language explicitly supplies at least countably many distinct propositional variables and permits countable conjunctions. For each \(x\in2^\omega\), the conjunction selecting \(p_n\) or \(\neg p_n\) according to \(x(n)\) is satisfiable, hence nonzero in the Lindenbaum--Tarski algebra. Two different sequences disagree at some coordinate, so the corresponding classes have zero meet. This is a disjoint family of cardinality \(2^{\aleph_0}\), directly contradicting the c.c.c. requirement.

The downstream implication is purely definitional: the recent framework defines “almost measurable” by requiring c.c.c., and its classical and modal models require such an algebra as a component. Therefore those model classes have no instances under the stated language assumptions.

## Originality

PASS. The primary 2026 paper states all premises but does not identify the continuum antichain or the resulting emptiness of the model class. The earlier thesis contains a countably generated atomicity/cardinality observation that is closely related, so this was treated as the strongest prior comparison rather than ignored. That observation does not state the uniform argument for every allowed \(\kappa\ge\omega\), and neither source draws the model-class emptiness conclusion.

Targeted searches for infinitary propositional c.c.c. obstructions, almost-measurable Lindenbaum--Tarski algebras, and critiques of the new modal-sentential framework found no equivalent published statement. Classical free-\(\sigma\)-complete-Boolean-algebra literature supplies background but not the application to this new model definition.

## Value

PASS. This is a decisive existence boundary for a newly proposed semantic framework. It identifies a concrete incompatibility among the framework's language strength, its Lindenbaum--Tarski algebra, and its c.c.c. requirement; without addressing that incompatibility, the advertised model class is vacuous. The obstruction is explicit, uniform in every permitted \(\kappa\ge\omega\), and indicates exactly which structural ingredients a repair must alter.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest sources are Cosyns's 2026 paper and the 2025 Durham thesis from which it was abridged. The thesis's countable-generator atomicity remark is materially relevant but does not cover the all-\(\kappa\) antichain theorem or the emptiness consequence. Rieger's classical work is background on free infinitary Boolean algebras.

The result diagnoses the stated setup only; it does not prove that no modified probability-inspired modal semantics can work.
