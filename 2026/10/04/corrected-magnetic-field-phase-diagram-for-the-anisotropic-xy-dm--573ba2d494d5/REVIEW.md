# Same-model review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The Gibbs \(X\)-state was reconstructed from the two energy blocks used by the source. Its two concurrence candidates reduce exactly to the disjoint scalar conditions \(q_B>2\) and \(q_B<0\). The derivative of \(q_B\) is strictly positive when \(\Delta_B\le K\); when \(\Delta_B>K\), a monotone hyperbolic-sine ratio proves that \(q_B\) has exactly one maximum. Pointwise monotonicity in \(B\), together with the endpoint limits of that maximum, proves existence and uniqueness of \(B_*\). The high-field asymptotic follows directly from the unique \(q_B=0\) root. A standalone checker replays the source parameter example and sign/crossing structure.

## Originality

**PASS, narrowly scoped.** Earlier anisotropic XY work without DM already proves that a magnetic field can raise the critical temperature and even permit entanglement at arbitrarily high finite temperature. The 2008 XY--DM paper nevertheless states field independence for its anisotropic example. The inspected literature did not state the exact scalar \(q_B\) criterion, the unique maximum/tangency classification, or the source-specific \(B_*\) correction for this XY--DM model.

The claim therefore does not take ownership of generic field-dependent anisotropic-XY entanglement. It is restricted to the exact reduction and full phase topology of the later source formula.

## Value

**PASS.** The corrected result changes the physical interpretation of a published parameter sweep: the critical temperature is not constant, and for a nonempty field interval the model has a temperature-induced entanglement island separated from its low-temperature branch. The theorem also gives the unique field where that island disappears and the high-field growth law for the surviving threshold. These are direct control features of the source model rather than a cosmetic algebraic correction.
