# Cozero-divisor graphs determine finite reduced nonfield rings

## Finding

Let \(R\) and \(S\) be finite reduced commutative rings with identity that are not fields. Then their cozero-divisor graphs are isomorphic if and only if the rings are isomorphic: \[\Gamma'(R)\cong\Gamma'(S)\iff R\cong S.\] More explicitly, if \[R\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb F_{q_i},\qquad r\ge2,\] then \(\Gamma'(R)\) intrinsically recovers \(r\) and the unordered multiset \(\{q_1,\ldots,q_r\}\). The field case is necessarily excluded because every field has an empty cozero-divisor graph.

The reconstruction uses only graph-theoretic data. Vertices with the same coordinate support are exactly the false-twin classes of the graph. The quotient by false twins is the incomparability graph of the proper nonempty subsets of an \(r\)-element set. Its minimum-degree classes identify the singleton and co-singleton supports, while their class sizes recover the numbers \(q_i-1\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(R\) be a finite reduced commutative ring with identity and assume that \(R\) is not a field. Since a finite reduced ring is Artinian and reduced,
\[
R\cong \prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb F_{q_i}
\]
for uniquely determined finite-field orders \(q_i\), up to permutation, with \(r\ge2\).

The cozero-divisor graph \(\Gamma'(R)\) has as vertices the nonzero nonunits of \(R\). Distinct vertices \(x,y\) are adjacent exactly when
\[
x\notin Ry
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y\notin Rx.
\]

For \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\), define
\[
\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{i:x_i\ne0\}.
\]
Every vertex has nonempty proper support.

## Proof

Put
\[
a_i=q_i-1.
\]
For a nonempty proper subset \(A\subset[r]\), let
\[
V_A=\{x:\operatorname{supp}(x)=A\}.
\]
Then
\[
|V_A|=\prod_{i\in A}a_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Because each coordinate ring is a field, the principal ideal generated by \(x\) is exactly the coordinate ideal supported on \(\operatorname{supp}(x)\). Consequently
\[
x\in Ry
\iff
\operatorname{supp}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(y),
\]
and hence
\[
x\sim y
\iff
\operatorname{supp}(x)\not\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(y)
\ \text{and}\
\operatorname{supp}(y)\not\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x).
\tag{2}
\]

Thus adjacency depends only on support, and each \(V_A\) is an independent false-twin class.

We next show that these are all false-twin classes. Suppose \(A\ne B\).

If \(A\) and \(B\) are incomparable, choose \(i\in A\setminus B\). The singleton support \(\{i\}\) is comparable with \(A\) but incomparable with \(B\), so the corresponding vertices distinguish the open neighborhoods of \(V_A\) and \(V_B\).

If, say, \(A\subsetneq B\), choose \(i\in B\setminus A\). Then \(\{i\}\) is incomparable with \(A\) but comparable with \(B\), again distinguishing the neighborhoods.

Therefore the false-twin classes are exactly the sets \(V_A\).

Collapse each false-twin class to one vertex. The resulting quotient \(Q_r\) is the incomparability graph of
\[
\mathcal B_r^\circ
=
2^{[r]}\setminus\{\varnothing,[r]\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
|V(Q_r)|=2^r-2,
\]
so the graph determines \(r\).

For a support \(A\) of cardinality \(k\), the degree in \(Q_r\) is
\[
d_r(k)
=
2^r-2^k-2^{r-k}+1.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(r\ge3\), this degree is minimal exactly when
\[
k=1
\quad\text{or}\quad
k=r-1.
\]
Hence the quotient graph intrinsically identifies the \(2r\) singleton and co-singleton classes.

The subgraph induced by those \(2r\) classes consists of two copies of \(K_r\): one on the singleton supports and one on the co-singleton supports. Between them there is exactly a perfect matching, joining
\[
\{i\}
\quad\text{to}\quad
[r]\setminus\{i\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(r\ge3\), these are the two \(r\)-cliques in that induced subgraph. Thus the graph recovers the two sides up to interchanging them and permuting coordinates.

Choose one side as the singleton side. By (1), its false-twin class sizes are
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_r.
\]
If the chosen side is the genuine singleton side, this directly recovers
\[
q_i=a_i+1.
\]

It remains to show that the possible interchange of the two sides creates no ambiguity. Let another reduced ring have parameters
\[
b_i=s_i-1,
\qquad
B=\prod_i b_i,
\]
while
\[
A=\prod_i a_i.
\]
If a graph isomorphism swaps singleton and co-singleton sides, then, after a permutation \(\sigma\), preservation of matched class sizes gives
\[
a_i=\frac{B}{b_{\sigma(i)}},
\qquad
\frac{A}{a_i}=b_{\sigma(i)}.
\tag{5}
\]
The two equalities in (5) imply \(A=B\). Multiplying either family of equalities then yields
\[
A=A^{r-1}.
\]
Since \(r\ge3\) and \(A\) is a positive integer,
\[
A=1.
\]
Hence every \(a_i=1\), so every factor is \(\mathbb F_2\). In that case the side swap gives the same field-order multiset. Thus no ambiguity remains.

When \(r=2\), the quotient has two support classes and
\[
\Gamma'(R)\cong K_{q_1-1,q_2-1},
\]
so the two part sizes recover the unordered pair \(\{q_1,q_2\}\).

Therefore \(\Gamma'(R)\) recovers the multiset of finite-field orders. Finite fields are determined up to isomorphism by their orders, and the direct-product decomposition of a finite reduced ring is unique up to permutation. This proves
\[
\Gamma'(R)\cong\Gamma'(S)
\iff
R\cong S.
\]

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` constructs the graph solely from the support model in (2), computes false-twin classes from identical open neighborhoods, forms the quotient without using support labels, identifies the two minimum-degree cliques, and reconstructs all field orders from class sizes.

It exhaustively tests every nondecreasing field-order profile of length \(2\), \(3\), or \(4\) with each field order in
\[
\{2,3,4,5\},
\]
plus four five-factor profiles. The largest tested graph has \(368\) vertices.

## Relationship to prior work

The cozero-divisor graph was introduced to encode incomparability among principal ideals. Earlier work then introduced the reduced cozero-divisor graph, whose vertices identify elements that generate the same principal ideal, and explicitly studied how that reduced graph reflects decompositions into fields and local rings.

That reduced graph cannot by itself retain the finite-field orders in a product of fields: for
\[
\prod_i\mathbb F_{q_i},
\]
its vertices are only the nontrivial coordinate supports. The present result uses the multiplicities discarded by that reduction: the false-twin class above a support \(A\) has size
\[
\prod_{i\in A}(q_i-1).
\]

Afkhami and Khashyarmanesh proved explicitly that the cozero-divisor graph of a product of two fields is complete bipartite. Their result supplies the \(r=2\) structure. The theorem here extends the reconstruction problem to arbitrary numbers of finite-field factors and proves that the full graph recovers every field order.

A later spectral paper studies \(\Gamma'(\mathbb Z_n)\) through Laplacian eigenvalues and the Wiener index rather than ring reconstruction. Targeted searches under cozero-divisor, reduced cozero-divisor, product-of-fields, graph-isomorphism, and ring-reconstruction formulations did not locate the all-factor reconstruction theorem proved here.

## Limitations

The reduced hypothesis is essential to the proof: it identifies the ring with a product of finite fields and makes principal ideals depend only on coordinate support. No assertion is made for finite rings with nonzero nilpotents.

The nonfield hypothesis is also essential. If \(R\) is a field, there are no nonzero nonunits, so every field has the empty cozero-divisor graph.

The theorem reconstructs the ring only inside the finite reduced nonfield class. It does not claim that a nonreduced ring cannot share the same cozero-divisor graph with a reduced ring.

One highly relevant 2010 paper on principal ideals and reduced cozero-divisor graphs was readable through its abstract, introduction, and section roadmap, but its complete PDF was unavailable through the inspected access path. Because that paper centers the reduced graph, whose quotient discards field-size multiplicities, it does not supply evidence contradicting the present full-graph reconstruction; nevertheless it remains the main residual literature-access risk.
