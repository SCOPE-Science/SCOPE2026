# Review

## Correctness

PASS. For a product of finite fields, the principal ideal generated by an element is exactly the coordinate ideal on its nonzero support, so cozero adjacency is support incomparability. Distinct supports have distinct open neighborhoods, making support fibers precisely the false-twin classes. Their quotient is the proper Boolean-lattice incomparability graph. Its degree formula identifies singleton and co-singleton supports, and the false-twin class sizes then recover all values \(q_i-1\). The only possible side interchange forces all factors to be \(\mathbb F_2\), so it creates no ambiguity.

Risk: the finite verifier is corroborative only; the theorem rests on the symbolic support and quotient reconstruction.

## Originality

PASS. The closest literature develops the reduced cozero-divisor graph and studies its relation to direct-product decompositions, while the full cozero graph literature gives direct-product structure and graph parameters. The reduced graph collapses precisely the multiplicities used here, so it cannot recover the field orders from products of fields. The inspected full-text theorem for two fields gives only the complete-bipartite structure, which covers the two-factor structural special case but not the arbitrary-factor reconstruction theorem. Searches under graph-isomorphism, ring-isomorphism, finite-reduced, product-of-fields, and reduced/full cozero-divisor aliases located no statement implying the final claim.

Risk: a complete copy of one highly relevant 2010 paper was unavailable through the inspected access path; its readable abstract, introduction, and roadmap emphasize the reduced graph and decomposition rather than full-graph field-order reconstruction.

## Value

PASS. This is a natural reconstruction theorem with a sharp boundary. The reduced cozero-divisor graph forgets field sizes, but the full graph restores them through canonical false-twin multiplicities. The result therefore turns an element-level graph into a complete isomorphism invariant for a broad and canonical ring class, while explaining exactly why fields are excluded.

Risk: no analogous conclusion is asserted for nonreduced finite rings.
