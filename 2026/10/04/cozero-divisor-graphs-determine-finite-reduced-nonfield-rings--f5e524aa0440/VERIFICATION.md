The proof can be checked in four stages.

1. **Support model.** For a product of finite fields, the principal ideal generated by an element is the coordinate ideal on its nonzero support. Thus two vertices are adjacent exactly when their supports are incomparable.

2. **False-twin quotient.** Vertices with the same support have identical open neighborhoods. Distinct supports are separated by a singleton-support test, so the false-twin classes are exactly the support fibers. The quotient therefore has exactly
\[
2^r-2
\]
vertices and recovers \(r\).

3. **Extremal support recovery.** A quotient vertex representing a \(k\)-element support has degree
\[
2^r-2^k-2^{r-k}+1.
\]
For \(r\ge3\), the minimum-degree vertices are precisely singleton and co-singleton supports. Their induced graph consists of two \(r\)-cliques joined by a perfect matching.

4. **Field-order recovery.** The singleton fiber sizes are \(q_i-1\), and the matched co-singleton fiber sizes are
\[
\frac{\prod_j(q_j-1)}{q_i-1}.
\]
If an isomorphism swaps the two extremal \(r\)-cliques, the resulting size equations force
\[
\prod_i(q_i-1)=1
\]
for \(r\ge3\), hence every factor is \(\mathbb F_2\). Therefore side swapping creates no new profile.

The standalone verifier builds graphs from the support model, forgets all support labels, recomputes false-twin classes from open neighborhoods, reconstructs the quotient, and recovers field orders. It checks every nondecreasing profile of lengths \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\) with field orders from \(2\) through \(5\), together with four five-factor profiles.
