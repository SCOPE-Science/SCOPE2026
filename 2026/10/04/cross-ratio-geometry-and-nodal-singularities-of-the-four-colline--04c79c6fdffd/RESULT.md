# Cross-ratio geometry and nodal singularities of the four-collinear line-multiview correction

## Finding

For four translational cameras
\[
C_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&v_i\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad i=1,2,3,4,
\]
with pairwise distinct \(v_i\in\mathbb C\), consider the component of the determinantal rank locus in which every back-projected plane contains the common camera baseline. In image-line coordinates \(\ell_i=[x_i:y_i:z_i]\), that component is
\[
E=\{x_1=x_2=x_3=x_4=0\}\cong(\mathbb P^1)^4,
\]
with factor coordinates \([y_i:z_i]\).

The line multiview variety meets \(E\) scheme-theoretically in the single four-collinear correction divisor
\[
D_v=V_E(F),
\]
where the correction determinant is
\[
F=
\det\!
\begin{pmatrix}
v_1z_1&v_2z_2&v_3z_3&v_4z_4\\
v_1y_1&v_2y_2&v_3y_3&v_4y_4\\
z_1&z_2&z_3&z_4\\
y_1&y_2&y_3&y_4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This divisor is the closure of the diagonal \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-orbit of the ordered tuple \(([v_i:1])_{i=1}^4\); equivalently, it is the fixed-cross-ratio hypersurface. Its singular locus is exactly the small diagonal
\[
\Delta=\{[y_1:z_1]=[y_2:z_2]=[y_3:z_3]=[y_4:z_4]\}\cong\mathbb P^1.
\]
Moreover, \(D_v\) is irreducible and normal, and every point of \(\Delta\) is transversely an \(A_1\) singularity.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is \(\mathbb C\). The camera parameters \(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\) are pairwise distinct. The claim concerns the correction divisor \(D_v=L_C\cap E\) inside the extraneous determinantal baseline component \(E\), not the singular locus of the full line multiview variety \(L_C\).

The standard translational form is the one used in the published four-collinear correction formula. Projective changes of the common baseline parameter and simultaneous projective changes of the four \(\mathbb P^1\) target factors transport the description equivariantly.

## Proof

For these cameras,
\[
C_i^T\ell_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i,v_ix_i)^T.
\]
Hence when \(x_i=0\) for every \(i\), all four columns lie in the fixed two-dimensional span generated by the second and third coordinate vectors. Every \(3\times3\) minor of the back-projection matrix therefore restricts identically to zero on \(E\). The all-collinear ideal theorem of Breiding--Duff--Gustafsson--Rydell--Shehu identifies the vanishing ideal with the minors together with the four-camera correction determinants. For four cameras there is one such determinant, so restriction to \(E\) gives the scheme-theoretic intersection \(D_v=V_E(F)\).

Introduce the bracket
\[
[ij]=z_i y_j-z_j y_i.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
F=(v_1-v_4)(v_2-v_3)[13][24]
 -(v_1-v_3)(v_2-v_4)[14][23].
\]
On the dense affine chart \(y_1y_2y_3y_4\ne0\), put \(t_i=z_i/y_i\). Then
\[
F=(y_1y_2y_3y_4)D,
\]
where
\[
D=(v_1-v_4)(v_2-v_3)(t_1-t_3)(t_2-t_4)
 -(v_1-v_3)(v_2-v_4)(t_1-t_4)(t_2-t_3).
\]
Thus, on the locus where the displayed denominators are nonzero,
\[
\frac{(t_1-t_3)(t_2-t_4)}{(t_1-t_4)(t_2-t_3)}
=
\frac{(v_1-v_3)(v_2-v_4)}{(v_1-v_4)(v_2-v_3)}.
\]
This is precisely equality of the corresponding ordered four-point cross ratios. Conversely, two ordered quadruples of pairwise distinct points of \(\mathbb P^1\) are related by one projective transformation exactly when their cross ratios agree. Substitution
\[
[z_i:y_i]=[av_i+b:cv_i+d]
\]
annihilates \(F\) identically. Hence the diagonal \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-orbit lies in \(D_v\).

The polynomial \(D\) is a homogeneous quadratic form in \((t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)\). Let \(H\) be its Hessian. Exact differentiation gives
\[
H(1,1,1,1)^T=0
\]
and, for every principal \(3\times3\) minor,
\[
\det H_{\widehat k,\widehat k}
=
2\prod_{1\le i<j\le4}(v_j-v_i)\ne0.
\]
Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}H=3\) and
\[
\ker H=\mathbb C(1,1,1,1)^T.
\]
Since \(D=\tfrac12 t^THt\), one has \(\nabla D=Ht\). Consequently the singular points in this affine chart are exactly
\[
t_1=t_2=t_3=t_4,
\]
and all of them lie on \(D=0\).

The simultaneous diagonal action of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) on \((\mathbb P^1)^4\) preserves \(F\) up to a nonzero scalar: the upper and lower pairs of rows in the determinant each transform by the same \(2\times2\) matrix. Any four target points can be moved simultaneously into a finite affine chart. Singularity is invariant under this ambient automorphism, so the affine computation proves globally that
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(D_v)=\Delta.
\]

The rank-three quadratic form \(D\) is irreducible. No component of the global divisor can be contained in the boundary of the affine chart because \(F\) is not divisible by any \(y_i\). Hence \(D_v\) is irreducible. The orbit of the distinct ordered quadruple has dimension three, the same dimension as \(D_v\), and is contained in it; its closure therefore equals \(D_v\).

Finally, near a point of \(\Delta\), use one common-shift coordinate and three difference coordinates. The equation is independent of the common-shift coordinate and is a nondegenerate quadratic form in the three transverse differences. Over \(\mathbb C\), a linear change of the transverse coordinates puts it in the form
\[
u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2=0.
\]
Thus the singularity is locally analytically
\[
\mathbb A^1\times\{u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2=0\},
\]
so it is transversely \(A_1\). Since \(D_v\) is an irreducible hypersurface in the smooth fourfold \((\mathbb P^1)^4\), it is Cohen--Macaulay and satisfies Serre's \(S_2\) condition. Its singular locus has dimension one inside the threefold \(D_v\), hence codimension two. Therefore it is regular in codimension one and is normal by Serre's criterion.

## Relationship to prior work

Breiding--Rydell--Shehu--Torres prove that four or more collinear cameras create an additional component of the determinantal rank locus and require extra equations beyond the rank minors. Their set-theoretic theorem describes the exceptional condition for arbitrary camera arrangements.

Breiding--Duff--Gustafsson--Rydell--Shehu give the explicit four-collinear determinant used above and prove, for distinct collinear translational cameras, that the extended ideal is radical and is the vanishing ideal of the line multiview variety. The present result uses those equations as input and identifies the geometry of their restriction to the baseline component: fixed cross ratio, exact singular locus, normality, and transverse \(A_1\) type.

Classical work of Aluffi--Faber studies closures of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)-orbits of point configurations on \(\mathbb P^1\). It is relevant general background for the orbit-closure interpretation. The comparison made here does not claim that general orbit-closure theory is new; the specific identification with the four-collinear line-multiview correction and the stated singularity calculation are the asserted contribution.

## Limitations

The proof assumes four distinct camera parameters and characteristic zero. If camera centers collide, the Hessian certificate degenerates and the statement need not persist. The result classifies the correction divisor inside the baseline component, not all singularities of the ambient multiview variety. The literature search found no source stating this exact camera-correction identification and singularity classification, but a logically equivalent formulation could exist in broader classical orbit-closure literature.
