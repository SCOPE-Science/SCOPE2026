# Cycle-parity gauge law for phase-shifted EPR-like Hamiltonians
## Finding
Consider the phase-shifted EPR-like Hamiltonian introduced by Wong, Bravyi, Gosset, and Liu,
\[
H_s(\theta)=-\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E} h_e\!\left(se^{i\theta_e}\right),
\qquad 0\le s<1,
\]
where
\[
h(z)=|00\rangle\langle00|+z|11\rangle\langle00|+\overline z|00\rangle\langle11|+|z|^2|11\rangle\langle11|.
\]
On a simple cycle \(C_m\), the effect of the edge phases is governed exactly by the parity of \(m\).

If \(m\) is odd, every phase assignment is removable by a tensor product of single-qubit \(Z\)-rotations. Consequently the full spectrum of \(H_s(\theta)\) is independent of \(\theta\).

If \(m\) is even, define the alternating flux
\[
\Phi=\theta_1-\theta_2+\theta_3-\cdots-\theta_m\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
It is invariant under all local \(Z\)-rotations and is the only phase parameter left after gauge fixing. Writing
\[
A_s(\theta)=-H_s(\theta),
\]
there is a phase-independent polynomial \(P_m(s)\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(A_s(\theta)^r\right)
\quad\text{is phase-independent for }0\le r<m,
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(A_s(\theta)^m\right)
=P_m(s)+2m!s^m\cos\Phi.
\]
Thus, for every even \(m\ge4\) and every \(s>0\), the spectrum is a nonconstant function of the gauge flux. Among simple cycles, odd cycles are therefore exactly phase-blind under this pairing gauge, whereas even cycles carry a spectrally visible alternating flux.

For the smallest cycle \(C_3=K_3\), one can additionally diagonalize the two parity blocks exactly. For arbitrary edge phases,
\[
\operatorname{spec} H_s(\theta)
=\bigl\{-(3+s^2)},\;-(1+3s^2),\;-1,-1,\;-s^2,-s^2,\;0,0\bigr\}.
\]
Hence, for \(0\le s<1\),
\[
\operatorname{gap} H_s(\theta)=2(1-s^2),
\]
and the first excited energy belongs to odd Hamming-weight parity. This gives an analytic phase-shifted verification of the source's spectral-gap and odd-parity conjectures on the smallest cyclic interaction graph.

For \(C_4\), the first flux-sensitive moment is already explicit:
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(A_s(\theta)^4\right)
=4\left(81+124s^2+122s^4+124s^6+81s^8\right)+48s^4\cos\Phi.
\]
In particular the fourth moments at \(\Phi=0\) and \(\Phi=\pi\) differ by \(96s^4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is a simple cycle, all edge amplitudes have the same modulus \(s\), and the Hamiltonian uses exactly the local rank-one operator \(h(z)\) displayed above. Edge phases are arbitrary elements of \(\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\). The spectral-moment statement holds for every integer \(m\ge3\); the flux-sensitive case requires even \(m\) and \(s>0\).

The result concerns phase dependence and finite-cycle spectra. It does not prove the source's conjectured spectral-gap lower bound for all graphs. For odd cycles it reduces every phase-shifted spectral question to the corresponding unshifted one. For even cycles it proves genuine spectral flux dependence but does not give a closed form for every eigenvalue or a uniform gap formula.

## Proof
A local rotation \(R_z(\alpha)=e^{-i\alpha Z/2}\) satisfies
\[
\bigl(R_z(\alpha)\otimes R_z(\beta)\bigr)
 h\!\left(se^{i\theta}\right)
\bigl(R_z(\alpha)\otimes R_z(\beta)\bigr)^\dagger
=h\!\left(se^{i(\theta+\alpha+\beta)}\right).
\]
Thus a vertex-angle vector \((\alpha_v)\) changes each edge phase by
\[
\theta_{uv}\longmapsto\theta_{uv}+\alpha_u+\alpha_v.
\]
On \(C_m\), removing all phases means solving
\[
\alpha_j+\alpha_{j+1}=-\theta_j\pmod{2\pi},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,
\]
with cyclic indices. Recursively determining \(\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_m\) from \(\alpha_1\), the closing equation always has a solution when \(m\) is odd, because it reduces to an equation of the form \(2\alpha_1=\gamma\) on the circle. When \(m\) is even, closure is possible exactly when
\[
\theta_1-\theta_2+\cdots-\theta_m=0\pmod{2\pi}.
\]
The same alternating sum is unchanged by every vertex rotation, proving the gauge statement. When \(m\) is even, one may therefore gauge all phases to zero except a single edge carrying the residual flux \(\Phi\).

It remains to identify when this flux can enter spectral invariants. Write the off-diagonal part of an edge term as
\[
s e^{i\theta_e}T_e^+ + s e^{-i\theta_e}T_e^-,
\qquad
T_e^+=|11\rangle\langle00|,
\qquad
T_e^-=|00\rangle\langle11|.
\]
Expand \(\operatorname{Tr}(A_s(\theta)^r)\) into products of diagonal pieces and the operators \(T_e^\pm\). Associate to each monomial an integer vector \(k=(k_e)\), where \(k_e\) is the number of \(T_e^+\) factors minus the number of \(T_e^-\) factors. A nonzero trace requires, at every vertex, equally many local raising and lowering operations. Hence
\[
Bk=0,
\]
where \(B\) is the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of the cycle.

For an even cycle, the integer kernel of \(B\) is generated by
\[
a=(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots,1,-1).
\]
If \(r<m\), then \(\|k\|_1\le r<m=\|a\|_1\), so necessarily \(k=0\). Therefore every moment below order \(m\) is phase-independent.

At order \(r=m\), a phase-dependent monomial must have \(k=a\) or \(k=-a\). Equality in the \(\ell^1\) bound forces exactly one off-diagonal factor from every edge and no diagonal factors. For either alternating choice of signs, choose any ordering of the \(m\) edges. At each vertex the ordered product contains exactly one single-qubit raising operator and one lowering operator. Operators on distinct vertices commute, so the product factors into one rank-one diagonal projector at every vertex and has trace one. There are \(m!\) edge orderings for each of the two alternating sign choices. Their total contribution is therefore
\[
m!s^m e^{i\Phi}+m!s^m e^{-i\Phi}
=2m!s^m\cos\Phi.
\]
All remaining terms have \(k=0\) and are phase-independent; collecting them gives \(P_m(s)\). This proves the even-cycle moment formula and the nonconstant spectral dependence for \(s>0\).

For \(C_3\), phase removal reduces the problem to three real edges. In the even-parity basis \(|000\rangle,|110\rangle,|101\rangle,|011\rangle\), the matrix \(A_s=-H_s\) has one central state coupled with amplitude norm \(\sqrt3s\) to three leaves of diagonal weight \(s^2\). Two leaf directions have eigenvalue \(s^2\), while the remaining two-dimensional block is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}3&\sqrt3s\\ \sqrt3s&s^2\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose eigenvalues are \(3+s^2\) and \(0\). In odd parity, two directions have eigenvalue \(1\), while the remaining block
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt3s\\ \sqrt3s&3s^2\end{pmatrix}
\]
has eigenvalues \(1+3s^2\) and \(0\). Negating these eigenvalues gives the displayed spectrum of \(H_s(\theta)\). Since \(3+s^2-(1+3s^2)=2(1-s^2)>0\) for \(s<1\), the stated gap and odd-parity first excitation follow.

## Verification
The accompanying checker reconstructs the Hamiltonian directly from the definition. It tests the \(C_3\) characteristic polynomial against the phase-independent closed spectrum for several nontrivial phase choices. It also performs exact rational arithmetic for \(C_4\) at fluxes \(0\) and \(\pi\), verifying the fourth-moment formula and the exact difference \(96s^4\) for several rational \(s\). These finite checks corroborate the algebraic proof; they are not used as a substitute for the universal argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Wong, Bravyi, Gosset, and Liu introduce the phase-shifted Hamiltonian, prove that phases can be removed on trees, observe that phases on graphs with cycles cannot generally be removed, and leave open whether their Lee-Yang, parity, and spectral-gap conjectures continue to hold with arbitrary phases. Their numerical study samples random phase shifts on connected graphs with at most ten vertices. They do not state the odd/even cycle gauge dichotomy, the first flux-sensitive moment, or the exact \(C_3\) spectrum above.

The later work of Bravyi, Gosset, Liu, and Wong proves spectral-gap bounds for a real-coupling Suzuki-Fisher/Heisenberg family. It does not supply the complex edge-phase cycle classification used here and does not imply the alternating-flux moment formula.

The gauge language is analogous to magnetic-flux ideas in quantum mechanics, but the transformation law here is a pairing law involving sums of endpoint phases rather than the usual oriented hopping difference. Consequently the relevant cycle invariant is the alternating flux on even cycles.

## Limitations
The result is exact only for the cycle family and uniform edge modulus. It does not classify all gauge invariants on arbitrary multicyclic graphs, although the incidence-matrix proof indicates the appropriate general framework. The exact moment formula proves that even-cycle spectra vary with flux but does not determine which flux minimizes the spectral gap. No claim is made about Lee-Yang radii beyond what follows from the cited source.

A residual literature risk is that the signless-incidence gauge criterion may have appeared under pairing-Hamiltonian, Bogoliubov-de Gennes, or superconducting-network terminology. Searches using those aliases did not reveal a statement that covers the present EPR-like Hamiltonian together with the exact first flux-sensitive moment.
