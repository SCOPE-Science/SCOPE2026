# Cyclic-subgroup edge minimality for generalized dicyclic groups

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of even order \(m\), let \(y\in A\) have order \(2\), and define
\[
G=\operatorname{Dic}(A,y)
=
\langle A,\gamma\mid
\gamma^2=y,\ 
\gamma^{-1}a\gamma=a^{-1}\text{ for every }a\in A
\rangle.
\]
Write
\[
\varepsilon(H)=|E(C(H)^*)|
\]
for the number of edges in the cyclic subgroup graph of a finite group \(H\).

Then
\[
\varepsilon(G)=\varepsilon(A)+\frac m2
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon(G)\ge \varepsilon(C_{2m}).
\]
Equality holds exactly when
\[
A\cong C_2
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
A\cong C_6.
\]

The first equality case gives the degenerate cyclic group \(G\cong C_4\). Hence among nonabelian generalized dicyclic groups, the unique equality case is
\[
\operatorname{Dic}(C_6,y),
\]
of order \(12\).

If
\[
m=2^a s,
\qquad
a\ge1,
\qquad
s\text{ odd},
\]
then the comparison splits sharply as
\[
\varepsilon(G)-\varepsilon(C_{2m})
=
\bigl(\varepsilon(A)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr)
+
\bigl(2^{a-1}s-\tau(s)-\varepsilon(C_s)\bigr).
\]
Both terms are nonnegative.

## Assumptions and scope

The cyclic subgroup graph \(C(H)^*\) is the Hasse diagram of the poset of cyclic subgroups of \(H\), ordered by inclusion.

The generalized dicyclic presentation used here is the standard one for an abelian index-two subgroup \(A\), with a distinguished involution \(y\in A\). The group has order \(2m\).

The comparison theorem used below is Tărnăuceanu's result: for a finite nilpotent group \(K\) of order \(r\),
\[
\varepsilon(K)\ge\varepsilon(C_r),
\]
with equality if and only if \(K\cong C_r\).

## Proof

Every element of \(G\setminus A\) has the form \(a\gamma\) with \(a\in A\). Since \(\gamma\) acts on \(A\) by inversion,
\[
(a\gamma)^2
=
a\gamma a\gamma
=
aa^{-1}\gamma^2
=
y.
\]
Thus every element outside \(A\) has order \(4\).

The cyclic subgroup generated by \(a\gamma\) is
\[
\langle a\gamma\rangle
=
\{1,y,a\gamma,ya\gamma\}.
\]
Two elements \(a\gamma\) and \(b\gamma\) generate the same cyclic subgroup exactly when
\[
b\gamma\in\{a\gamma,ya\gamma\}.
\]
Hence the \(m\) elements in \(G\setminus A\) determine exactly
\[
\frac m2
\]
cyclic subgroups of order \(4\).

Each such subgroup is maximal among cyclic subgroups of \(G\). Indeed, a cyclic subgroup generated by an element of \(A\) lies inside \(A\), while every generator outside \(A\) has order \(4\). Its only nontrivial proper cyclic subgroup is
\[
\langle y\rangle.
\]
Therefore \(C(G)^*\) is obtained from \(C(A)^*\) by adjoining exactly \(m/2\) pendant vertices at the vertex \(\langle y\rangle\). Consequently
\[
\varepsilon(G)=\varepsilon(A)+\frac m2.
\]

Because \(A\) is finite abelian, it is nilpotent, so Tărnăuceanu's theorem gives
\[
\varepsilon(A)\ge\varepsilon(C_m),
\]
with equality exactly when \(A\cong C_m\).

It remains to prove
\[
\varepsilon(C_m)+\frac m2\ge\varepsilon(C_{2m}).
\]
Write
\[
m=2^a s,
\qquad a\ge1,
\qquad s\text{ odd}.
\]
Let
\[
e(r)=\varepsilon(C_r).
\]
For coprime positive integers \(u,v\), the cyclic edge formula gives
\[
e(uv)=e(u)\tau(v)+e(v)\tau(u).
\]
Since
\[
e(2^a)=a,
\qquad
\tau(2^a)=a+1,
\]
we have
\[
e(m)=a\tau(s)+(a+1)e(s)
\]
and
\[
e(2m)=(a+1)\tau(s)+(a+2)e(s).
\]
Subtracting,
\[
e(m)+\frac m2-e(2m)
=
2^{a-1}s-\tau(s)-e(s).
\]

Define
\[
f(s)=\tau(s)+e(s)
\]
for odd \(s\).

If
\[
s=p^b
\]
is an odd prime power, then
\[
f(s)=2b+1\le p^b,
\]
with equality only for
\[
p=3,\qquad b=1.
\]
Indeed, \(2b+1\le3^b\), with equality only at \(b=1\), and \(p^b\ge3^b\).

If \(u,v>1\) are coprime, then the product formula gives
\[
f(uv)
=
f(u)f(v)-e(u)e(v)
<
f(u)f(v).
\]
Induction over the distinct prime-power factors of \(s\) therefore yields
\[
f(s)\le s,
\]
with equality only for
\[
s=3.
\]
The case \(s=1\) has
\[
f(1)=1.
\]

It follows that
\[
2^{a-1}s-f(s)\ge0.
\]
Equality occurs only in the following two cases:
\[
(a,s)=(1,1)
\]
or
\[
(a,s)=(1,3).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
m=2
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
m=6.
\]

Finally,
\[
\varepsilon(G)-\varepsilon(C_{2m})
=
\bigl(\varepsilon(A)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr)
+
\bigl(2^{a-1}s-\tau(s)-e(s)\bigr).
\]
Both summands are nonnegative. Equality requires \(A\cong C_m\) and \(m\in\{2,6\}\). Thus
\[
A\cong C_2
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
A\cong C_6,
\]
as claimed.

## Verification

The included replay constructs \(\operatorname{Dic}(A,y)\) directly for several finite abelian kernels, including cyclic and noncyclic examples and multiple choices of the distinguished involution.

For each example it enumerates all cyclic subgroups, constructs the Hasse graph by inclusion, and verifies
\[
\varepsilon(\operatorname{Dic}(A,y))
=
\varepsilon(A)+\frac{|A|}{2}.
\]
It also constructs the cyclic group of the same order and checks the edge inequality and the two equality cases.

The replay separately verifies
\[
\tau(s)+\varepsilon(C_s)\le s
\]
for every odd \(s\le401\), with equality only at \(s=1\) and \(s=3\) in the precise forms used in the proof. It returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite computation is not used to establish the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Lazorec and Tărnăuceanu introduced and studied generalized dicyclic groups in the same abelian-kernel framework in 2016. Their paper focuses on subgroup and cyclic-subgroup commutativity degrees; for arbitrary abelian kernels it explicitly leaves further counting problems open.

Tărnăuceanu proved in 2023 that cyclic groups minimize cyclic-subgroup-graph edge count among nilpotent groups and among groups of odd order, and conjectured the same inequality for arbitrary even-order groups.

Sharma and Reddy's 2024 preprint gives explicit vertex and edge counts for ordinary dicyclic groups, where the abelian kernel is cyclic. Its first version contains no treatment of generalized dicyclic groups with arbitrary abelian kernel.

The theorem above extends the ordinary dicyclic edge structure to every finite abelian kernel and verifies the 2023 even-order conjecture on this entire generalized-dicyclic family, with a complete equality classification.

## Limitations

The argument relies on the generalized dicyclic relation that every element outside \(A\) squares to the same involution \(y\). It does not apply unchanged to arbitrary index-two extensions of abelian groups.

The result verifies the cyclic edge-minimality conjecture only for generalized dicyclic groups, not for all finite groups of even order.

The originality search covered the principal generalized-dicyclic source, the conjecture source, the first public version of the cyclic-subgroup-graph paper, targeted alias searches, and semantic database searches. An equivalent theorem could still exist in unindexed literature.
