# Cyclic-subgroup edge minimality for generalized dihedral groups

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order \(m\), and let
\[
G=\operatorname{Dih}(A)=A\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
\qquad
tat=a^{-1}
\]
for every \(a\in A\). For a finite group \(H\), write
\[
\varepsilon(H)=|E(C(H)^*)|
\]
for the number of edges in its cyclic subgroup graph.

Then
\[
\varepsilon(G)=\varepsilon(A)+m
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon(G)\ge \varepsilon(C_{2m}).
\]
Equality holds if and only if
\[
A\cong C_3,
\]
so the unique equality case in this family is
\[
\operatorname{Dih}(C_3)\cong S_3.
\]

More explicitly,
\[
\varepsilon(G)-\varepsilon(C_{2m})
=
\bigl(\varepsilon(A)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr)
+
\bigl(m-\tau(m)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr),
\]
and both summands are nonnegative. The second summand is zero exactly when \(m=3\).

Thus the conjecture that the cyclic group minimizes the number of edges in the cyclic subgroup graph, among groups of a fixed order, holds for every generalized dihedral group with nontrivial odd abelian kernel.

## Assumptions and scope

The cyclic subgroup graph \(C(H)^*\) is the Hasse diagram of the poset of cyclic subgroups of \(H\), ordered by inclusion. Its vertices are cyclic subgroups, and two distinct cyclic subgroups are adjacent exactly when one is maximal in the other.

The kernel \(A\) is assumed finite, abelian, nontrivial, and of odd order. These hypotheses ensure that inversion is an automorphism and that every element in the nontrivial coset \(At\) is an involution.

The comparison theorem used below is the 2023 result of Tărnăuceanu: if a finite group \(H\) of order \(r\) is nilpotent, or if \(r\) is odd, then
\[
\varepsilon(H)\ge\varepsilon(C_r),
\]
with equality exactly for \(H\cong C_r\).

## Proof

Every element of \(G\) has a unique form \(a\) or \(at\), with \(a\in A\). Since \(A\) has odd order,
\[
(at)^2=atat=aa^{-1}=1.
\]
Therefore every element of \(At\) is an involution. Distinct elements of \(At\) generate distinct subgroups of order \(2\).

No cyclic subgroup properly containing one of these order-\(2\) subgroups exists. Indeed, a cyclic subgroup generated by an element of \(A\) lies in \(A\), whereas a cyclic subgroup generated by an element of \(At\) has order \(2\). Thus each subgroup
\[
\langle at\rangle
\]
is a pendant vertex of \(C(G)^*\), adjacent only to the trivial subgroup.

The cyclic subgroups contained in \(A\) have exactly the same cover relations inside \(C(G)^*\) as they have in \(C(A)^*\). Consequently \(C(G)^*\) is obtained from \(C(A)^*\) by attaching \(m\) new pendant vertices to the trivial subgroup, and hence
\[
\varepsilon(G)=\varepsilon(A)+m.
\]

Because \(A\) is abelian, it is nilpotent. Tărnăuceanu's theorem therefore gives
\[
\varepsilon(A)\ge\varepsilon(C_m),
\]
with equality exactly when
\[
A\cong C_m.
\]

It remains to compare the cyclic groups of orders \(m\) and \(2m\). Put
\[
c(r)=|C(C_r)|=\tau(r)
\]
and
\[
f(r)=c(r)+\varepsilon(C_r).
\]
For coprime positive integers \(u,v\), the product formula for cyclic subgroup graphs gives
\[
\varepsilon(C_{uv})
=
\varepsilon(C_u)c(v)+\varepsilon(C_v)c(u).
\]
Since \(m\) is odd,
\[
\varepsilon(C_{2m})
=
2\varepsilon(C_m)+\tau(m).
\]

We now prove
\[
f(m)\le m,
\]
with equality exactly when \(m=3\).

For an odd prime power \(p^a\), the cyclic subgroup graph of \(C_{p^a}\) is a path with \(a+1\) vertices and \(a\) edges, so
\[
f(p^a)=2a+1\le p^a.
\]
Because \(p\ge3\), equality occurs only for
\[
p=3,\qquad a=1.
\]

If \(u,v>1\) are coprime, then
\[
c(uv)=c(u)c(v)
\]
and the edge product formula gives
\[
f(uv)
=
f(u)f(v)-\varepsilon(C_u)\varepsilon(C_v).
\]
Both edge counts on the right are positive, so
\[
f(uv)<f(u)f(v).
\]
Induction over the distinct prime-power factors of \(m\) now gives
\[
f(m)\le m,
\]
and equality can occur only when \(m\) consists of the single prime-power factor \(3\). Therefore
\[
m-\tau(m)-\varepsilon(C_m)\ge0,
\]
with equality exactly for \(m=3\).

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon(G)-\varepsilon(C_{2m})
&=\varepsilon(A)+m-2\varepsilon(C_m)-\tau(m)\\
&=\bigl(\varepsilon(A)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr)
+\bigl(m-\tau(m)-\varepsilon(C_m)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both summands are nonnegative. Equality requires \(A\cong C_m\) and \(m=3\), hence exactly
\[
A\cong C_3.
\]
This proves the claim.

## Verification

The included replay constructs generalized dihedral groups directly as semidirect products for several cyclic and noncyclic odd abelian kernels. It enumerates every cyclic subgroup, constructs the Hasse graph by inclusion, and verifies
\[
\varepsilon(\operatorname{Dih}(A))=\varepsilon(A)+|A|.
\]
It independently constructs the cyclic group of the same even order and checks the claimed inequality and equality case.

The replay also checks the auxiliary inequality
\[
\tau(m)+\varepsilon(C_m)\le m
\]
for every odd \(m\) in a bounded range, with equality only at \(m=3\). It returns `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite replay is not used to prove the universal result.

## Relationship to prior work

Tărnăuceanu proved in 2023 that the cyclic group minimizes the edge count of the cyclic subgroup graph among nilpotent groups of a fixed order and among all groups of a fixed odd order. The same paper explicitly conjectured the inequality for arbitrary even-order groups and noted that equality need not force cyclicity in even order.

A later study of cyclic subgroup graphs gives the exact edge formula for the ordinary dihedral group
\[
D_{2m}=\operatorname{Dih}(C_m),
\]
namely
\[
\varepsilon(D_{2m})=\varepsilon(C_m)+m.
\]
Its inspected text does not treat generalized dihedral groups with noncyclic kernel. The present theorem extends the structural formula to every odd abelian kernel and, more importantly, proves the conjectured comparison with the cyclic group of the same even order, including the exact equality classification.

A published database result on cyclic-subgroup-graph intersection distances was also compared. Its statement concerns graph distances and induced geodesics, not edge counts or extremal comparison among groups of a fixed order, and does not imply the theorem above.

## Limitations

The proof uses that the generalized dihedral kernel has odd order. When the kernel has even order, elements outside the kernel need not form \(m\) distinct pendant order-\(2\) subgroups in the same way, so the argument does not transfer unchanged.

The theorem proves Tărnăuceanu's conjecture only for this generalized-dihedral family, not for all finite groups of even order.

Targeted searches did not locate the same generalized-dihedral edge-minimality theorem, but an equivalent result could exist under different terminology or in unindexed literature.
