## Correctness
PASS. In explicit semidirect-product coordinates, every element outside the prime kernel lies in exactly one conjugate cyclic complement because the scalar \(1-u^b\) is nonzero modulo \(r\) whenever the complement coordinate is nontrivial. Thus distinct complements intersect only at the identity, every non-kernel cyclic subgroup belongs to one complement, and the kernel itself is a leaf. This proves the one-point-union graph description. The exact vertex and edge counts then follow immediately, and the cyclic comparison is a direct divisor-lattice calculation. Strictness follows from connectedness of the cyclic-subgroup graph of \(C_m\) and \(r\ge5\). The packaged replay independently reconstructs every cyclic subgroup and cover relation for six examples.

PASS. The theorem resolves a published edge-minimality conjecture on a natural infinite class of even-order nonnilpotent groups and does so through an exact graph decomposition, not only a numerical bound. It identifies the mechanism behind the strict inequality: \(r\) disjoint complement divisor lattices share only the identity, while the prime kernel contributes one leaf.
