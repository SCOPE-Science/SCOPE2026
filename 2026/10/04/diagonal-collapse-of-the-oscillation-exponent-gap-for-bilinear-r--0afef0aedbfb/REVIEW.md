# Review of Diagonal collapse of the oscillation-exponent gap for bilinear rough commutators

## Correctness

PASS. The recent source defines
\[
S_{r,t}(b_1,b_2)
\]
as a product of separate oscillation moments and
\[
T_u(b_1,b_2)
\]
as one moment of the product of centered symbols. Setting \(b_1=b_2=b\) gives the exact identities
\[
S_{r,r}(b,b)=\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_r}^2,
\qquad
T_u(b,b)=\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{2u}}^2.
\]
The source lower theorem therefore makes each natural diagonal second-order commutator norm dominate \(\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_2}^2\). Its upper theorems, with one fixed exponent bump such as \(\varepsilon=1\), bound the same norms by finite higher \(\mathrm{BMO}_r\) moments. John--Nirenberg makes all these moments quantitatively equivalent to \(\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}\). No passage to a vanishing bump and no unproved endpoint limiting argument is used.

The repeated-position lower theorem requires \(b^2\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}\), which is exactly covered by the stated \(b\in L^4_{\mathrm{loc}}\) hypothesis. In the sufficient direction, \(\mathrm{BMO}\) supplies every finite local moment.

## Originality

PASS. The full motivating source was inspected through its joint-oscillation definitions, geometric non-degeneracy assumption, natural-triple lower bounds, mixed and repeated-position upper bounds, and its statement that removing the exponent bump is open for general distinct symbols. It does not state the diagonal collapse or the resulting exact complex-symbol BMO characterization.

The full 2025 Zeng preprint was inspected through its main upper and lower theorems. Its lower theorem assumes a real-valued single symbol and a non-degenerate bilinear Calderón--Zygmund operator. That does not cover the bounded rough angular kernels or complex-valued symbols in the present claim.

The earlier linear joint-oscillation theorem concerns linear Calderón--Zygmund operators, while Chaffee's bilinear characterization concerns first-order commutators. Candidate-specific searches using same-symbol, diagonal-joint-oscillation, rough-kernel, exponent-gap, and BMO-equivalence formulations found no covering result.

Residual risk remains that the diagonal observation may have been recorded informally or in later notes not indexed by the searches.

## Value

PASS. The motivating paper leaves a small exponent gap between necessity and sufficiency at its natural triples. The finding identifies a natural boundary where that gap vanishes exactly: it is absent for a single repeated symbol and persists only as a genuinely joint two-symbol issue. This cleanly separates the difficulty caused by interacting symbol oscillations from the roughness of the bilinear kernel.

It also upgrades the single-symbol necessity picture to complex-valued symbols for this rough homogeneous class, whereas the closest earlier bilinear lower theorem cited by the source uses real-valued symbols and smoother Calderón--Zygmund kernels.
