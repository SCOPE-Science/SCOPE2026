# Direct recovery contaminates the cumulative-confirmation observable in an SIHR COVID-19 fit

## Finding

Jiao and Huang's Hubei fitting specialization sets
\[
A=\mu=\mu_1=\mu_2=0
\]
in the SIHR system. The two compartments relevant to the reported cumulative-confirmed data then satisfy
\[
\dot H=\delta I-mH,
\qquad
\dot R=\gamma I+mH.
\]
The paper defines \(I\) as non-confirmed infectious individuals, \(H\) as confirmed/hospitalized individuals, \(\delta\) as the confirmation rate from \(I\) to \(H\), and \(\gamma\) as natural recovery from \(I\). Nevertheless, its data-fitting figure labels
\[
X(t):=H(t)+R(t)
\]
as the model simulation of cumulative confirmed cases.

Introduce instead the accounting variable \(C(t)\), the cumulative number ever confirmed after the fitting start \(t_0\), initialized by
\[
C(t_0)=H(t_0)+R(t_0).
\]
Because the only confirmation transition in the printed fitting model is \(I\to H\) at rate \(\delta I\),
\[
\dot C=\delta I.
\]
But the plotted quantity satisfies
\[
\dot X=\dot H+\dot R=(\delta+\gamma)I.
\]
Therefore, for every trajectory with \(\delta>0\),
\[
X(t)-X(t_0)
=
\frac{\delta+\gamma}{\delta}
\bigl(C(t)-C(t_0)\bigr).
\]
Thus \(H+R\) agrees with cumulative confirmations only in the degenerate cases \(\gamma=0\) or when there is no post-start infectious mass contributing to either counter.

For the fitted parameters
\[
\gamma=0.0185,
\qquad
\delta=0.1836,
\]
the exact increment ratio is
\[
\frac{\delta+\gamma}{\delta}
\approx1.1007625272.
\]
So, along any fixed trajectory generated by those printed parameters, each post-start increment of \(H+R\) exceeds the model-consistent cumulative-confirmation increment by about \(10.0763\%\).

The source initializes
\[
H(t_0)=20736,
\qquad
R(t_0)=633,
\]
so
\[
X(t_0)=21369,
\]
exactly its first revised cumulative-confirmed datum. If the published trajectory reaches \(X\approx68000\), then the confirmation counter on that same trajectory is
\[
C
\approx
21369+
\frac{0.1836}{0.1836+0.0185}
(68000-21369)
\approx63731.45.
\]
This conversion is an internal consistency calculation, not a replacement empirical forecast, because a corrected observation model would require refitting the parameters.

## Assumptions and scope

The exact identity concerns the fitting specialization printed in Section 3 of the source, where recruitment, natural mortality, and disease-related mortality are set to zero. It assumes \(\delta>0\), as in the fitted parameter set.

The accounting interpretation is fixed by the source's own compartment definitions: individuals in \(I\) are explicitly non-confirmed, and confirmation is the transition \(I\to H\) at rate \(\delta I\). Direct natural recovery \(I\to R\) at rate \(\gamma I\) therefore does not represent a confirmation event.

The result does not infer corrected best-fit values for \(\beta\), \(\gamma\), \(\delta\), \(m\), or \(I(t_0)\). Those require a new data fit using the corrected observation map.

## Proof

Under the source's fitting assumptions,
\[
\dot H=\delta I-mH
\]
and
\[
\dot R=\gamma I+mH.
\]
Adding gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(H+R)=(\delta+\gamma)I.
\]

Now define \(C\) by the model's event semantics. A new confirmation occurs exactly when an individual leaves the non-confirmed infectious compartment \(I\) for the confirmed compartment \(H\). The transition intensity is \(\delta I\), hence
\[
\dot C=\delta I.
\]
Integrating both identities from \(t_0\) to \(t\) yields
\[
X(t)-X(t_0)
=(\delta+\gamma)\int_{t_0}^t I(s)\,ds
\]
and
\[
C(t)-C(t_0)
=\delta\int_{t_0}^t I(s)\,ds.
\]
Eliminating the common integral proves
\[
X(t)-X(t_0)
=
\frac{\delta+\gamma}{\delta}
\bigl(C(t)-C(t_0)\bigr).
\]

Because \(\gamma>0\) in the fitted parameter set, every interval on which
\[
\int_{t_0}^t I(s)\,ds>0
\]
has strict inequality
\[
X(t)-X(t_0)>C(t)-C(t_0).
\]
The discrepancy is therefore structural, not a numerical-integration artifact.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` recomputes the published initial cumulative value,
\[
20736+633=21369,
\]
the exact increment factor,
\[
\frac{0.1836+0.0185}{0.1836}
\approx1.1007625272,
\]
and the same-trajectory conversion of the reported \(68000\) endpoint,
\[
C\approx63731.4523.
\]

The general result is analytic. The numerical script is used only to replay arithmetic attached to the published fitted values.

## Relationship to prior work

Jiao and Huang (2020), DOI 10.3934/math.2020431, is the primary source. Its printed model sends non-confirmed infectious individuals directly to the recovered compartment at rate \(\gamma I\), while its fitting section compares \(H+R\) with cumulative-confirmed data. The source does not introduce a separate cumulative-confirmation counter.

Hou, Lan, Yuan, and Zhang (2022), DOI 10.3934/mbe.2022195, reuse the same deterministic SIHR compartment structure as the basis of a stochastic model. Their full text again identifies \(I\) as non-confirmed, \(H\) as confirmed, \(\gamma\) as natural recovery, and \(\delta\) as confirmation. Their study concerns stochastic extinction and persistence rather than correcting the 2020 observation map.

He, Tang, and Rong (2020), DOI 10.3934/mbe.2020153, use a different discrete stochastic COVID-19 model and calibrate explicitly reported flows such as newly confirmed and newly recovered cases. That work illustrates a different observation architecture and does not imply the exact correction above for the Jiao–Huang SIHR equations.

## Limitations

The result proves an incompatibility between the printed compartment transitions and the observation map used in the published fit. It does not determine how a corrected least-squares or likelihood fit would change the parameter estimates, forecast peak time, or final confirmed count.

The value \(63731.45\) is only the model-consistent confirmation counter evaluated on the already-published trajectory corresponding to \(H+R\approx68000\). It must not be interpreted as a re-estimated forecast from the Hubei data.
