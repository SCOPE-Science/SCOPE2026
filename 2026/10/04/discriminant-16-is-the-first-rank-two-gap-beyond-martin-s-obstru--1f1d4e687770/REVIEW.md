# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For an even hyperbolic rank-two lattice, the reconstructed binary-form reduction gives \(m^2\le\Delta\), where \(m\) is the least positive half-self-intersection. Thus \(\Delta<16\) forces a primitive self-intersection \(2\), \(4\), or \(6\), and the 2026 classification gives degree of irrationality \(3\). The boundary lattice \(U(4)\) has determinant \(-16\), norms \(8xy\), pairing image \(4\mathbf Z\), and a verified primitive embedding into \(U^3\). Period surjectivity and generic avoidance of extra Hodge classes give abelian surfaces with exact Néron--Severi lattice \(U(4)\); the complete classification then gives degree of irrationality \(4\).

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating paper contains the classification and the reduction inequality but does not state \(U(4)\), discriminant \(16\), or a smallest-discriminant threshold. Martin's older theorem is inapplicable because \(12\in4\mathbf Z\). Exact-lattice, discriminant, obstruction, and equivalent threshold searches found no covering statement. A residual risk remains that older Humbert-surface literature may mention \(U(4)\) under different notation without connecting it to degree of irrationality.

## Value
**PASS.** The result is a sharp intrinsic cutoff, not an arbitrary example: it identifies the first Picard-rank-two discriminant where the new complete criterion can certify degree of irrationality \(4\), and simultaneously the first boundary where Martin's \(12\)-intersection test can be silent. The canonical lattice \(U(4)\) realizes equality.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Huang--Jiang--Yang, arXiv:2609.02169v1, which proves the complete four-polarization classification and contains the binary-form reduction used here. Martin supplies the older sufficient \(12\)-obstruction, while Harder records Shioda's period-surjectivity framework for lattice-polarized abelian surfaces. The threshold is asserted only for Picard number \(2\), and the existence proof is period-theoretic rather than an explicit projective model.
