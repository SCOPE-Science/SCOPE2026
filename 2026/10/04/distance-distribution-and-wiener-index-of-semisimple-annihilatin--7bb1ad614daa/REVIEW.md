# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Ideals in a finite product of fields are exactly the coordinate-support ideals. Their product is zero exactly for disjoint supports, so the graph is the strong Boolean graph on nonempty proper subsets. The proof then partitions every unordered distinct pair into exactly three distance cases. The distance-one and distance-three counts are independent inclusion-exclusion counts, the distance-two count is the complement, and substitution verifies the Wiener formula.

## Originality
PASS. The closest exact Hosoya/Wiener paper located treats annihilating-ideal graphs of \(\mathbb Z_n\), while the 2017 product-of-fields paper supplies only the Boolean-graph structural identification and contains no Wiener/Hosoya discussion. The 2022 survey explicitly identifies annihilating-ideal graphs as a remaining Wiener-index direction. Searches in published-finding corpus, ordinary web literature, and the strong-Boolean-graph alias found no equivalent formula.

## Value
PASS. Products of fields are the canonical finite semisimple commutative rings, and the theorem gives their entire distance distribution in one closed form. The result therefore supplies more information than the requested Wiener invariant and settles a natural infinite family singled out by the survey's open direction.

The principal residual risk is bibliographic rather than mathematical: the same subset-disjointness enumeration could exist in an unindexed source under different graph terminology.
