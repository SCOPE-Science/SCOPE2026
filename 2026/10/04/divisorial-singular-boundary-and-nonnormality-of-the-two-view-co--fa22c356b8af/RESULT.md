# Divisorial singular boundary and nonnormality of the two-view conic multiview variety

## Finding

Work over \(\mathbb C\). For the canonical two-camera arrangement
\[
C_1=[0\ I],\qquad C_2=[I\ 0],
\]
let \(X\subset \mathbb P^5_\gamma\times\mathbb P^5_\delta\) be the two-view conic multiview variety. Define
\[
q_\gamma=
\bigl(
\gamma_4^2-4\gamma_3\gamma_5,
\gamma_2\gamma_4-2\gamma_1\gamma_5,
\gamma_2^2-4\gamma_0\gamma_5
\bigr)
\]
and
\[
q_\delta=
\bigl(
\delta_2^2-4\delta_0\delta_5,
\delta_1\delta_2-2\delta_0\delta_4,
\delta_1^2-4\delta_0\delta_3
\bigr).
\]
The published defining equations of \(X\) are the \(2\times2\) minors of the matrix with rows \(q_\gamma\) and \(q_\delta\).

Let
\[
P_\gamma=V(\gamma_2,\gamma_4,\gamma_5)\cong\mathbb P^2,
\qquad
P_\delta=V(\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2)\cong\mathbb P^2.
\]
Then
\[
D_\gamma=P_\gamma\times\mathbb P^5_\delta,
\qquad
D_\delta=\mathbb P^5_\gamma\times P_\delta
\]
are codimension-one subvarieties of \(X\), and every point of both is singular on \(X\). Consequently \(X\) is not normal.

Geometrically, \(P_\gamma\) and \(P_\delta\) are exactly the plane families of reducible image conics that are unions of two lines through the corresponding epipole. This divisorial singular behavior is specific to those plane components of the zero-row locus: a general point on the double-line Veronese component is smooth on \(X\).

The same conclusion holds for every pair of cameras with distinct centers, because the published two-view equations for a general pair are obtained from the canonical equations by invertible projective coordinate changes in the two image-conic factors.

## Assumptions and scope

The source paper proves that the canonical two-view conic multiview variety is irreducible of dimension \(8\) and that its prime defining ideal is generated by the rank-one condition above. It also identifies the zero-row locus geometrically: either the image conic is a double line, or it is a union of two lines through the relevant epipole.

The result here concerns the singularity and normality consequences of that published ideal. It does not claim a complete description of the singular locus, a normalization map, or an Euclidean-distance-degree computation.

The first public version of the source is arXiv:2404.03063v1 from 2024-04-03, in algebraic geometry.

## Proof

Write the three \(2\times2\) minors as \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). Since \(q_\gamma=0\) on \(P_\gamma\), all three minors vanish there for every \(\delta\), so \(D_\gamma\subset X\). Likewise \(D_\delta\subset X\).

On \(P_\gamma\), direct differentiation of the three minors shows that every Jacobian entry vanishes except possibly those in the single column corresponding to \(\gamma_5\). Thus the Jacobian rank of \((F_1,F_2,F_3)\) is at most \(1\) at every point of \(D_\gamma\). The same calculation on \(P_\delta\) leaves only the column corresponding to \(\delta_0\), so the Jacobian rank is at most \(1\) everywhere on \(D_\delta\).

The ambient product has dimension \(10\), while \(X\) has dimension \(8\), hence \(X\) has codimension \(2\). Because the defining ideal is prime, the Jacobian criterion applies to this reduced irreducible variety in characteristic zero: Jacobian rank less than \(2\) implies singularity. Therefore both \(D_\gamma\) and \(D_\delta\) lie in \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\).

Each of \(P_\gamma\) and \(P_\delta\) has dimension \(2\), so each product \(D_\gamma,D_\delta\) has dimension \(7\). Since \(\dim X=8\), these are codimension-one singular subvarieties. A normal variety is regular in codimension one, so \(X\) cannot be normal.

For the contrast with the other zero-row component, consider a double line
\[
\gamma=(u^2:2uv:2uw:v^2:2vw:w^2).
\]
On the dense open set \(w\neq0\), the Jacobian of \(q_\gamma\) has rank \(3\). If \(q_\delta\neq0\), the linearization of the rank-one minors is the composition of this surjective differential with the rank-\(2\) map \(z\mapsto z\wedge q_\delta\). Hence the defining Jacobian has rank \(2\), and such a point is smooth on \(X\). Thus the codimension-one singularity is caused by the epipolar reducible-conic plane, not by the generic double-line part of the zero-row locus.

Finally, the source paper shows that a general pair of distinct cameras is obtained from the canonical arrangement by invertible changes of image coordinates, inducing invertible linear changes on conic coefficients. These preserve codimension, singularity, and normality, so the conclusion is coordinate-independent.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_conic_multiview_nonnormal.py` reconstructs the three published minors symbolically and checks:

- all three minors vanish identically on \(P_\gamma\times\mathbb P^5\) and on \(\mathbb P^5\times P_\delta\);
- after restricting to \(P_\gamma\), only the \(\gamma_5\)-column of the Jacobian can be nonzero;
- after restricting to \(P_\delta\), only the \(\delta_0\)-column can be nonzero;
- the explicit point
  \[
  \gamma=(1:0:0:1:0:0),\qquad
  \delta=(1:0:0:0:0:1)
  \]
  lies on \(X\) and has defining-Jacobian rank \(1\);
- the explicit double-line point
  \[
  \gamma=(1:2:2:1:2:1),\qquad
  \delta=(1:0:0:0:0:1)
  \]
  lies on \(X\) and has defining-Jacobian rank \(2\), while the differential of \(q_\gamma\) there has rank \(3\).

Running the script prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Rydell and Sundelius give the canonical two-view rank-one equations, prove that they generate the prime defining ideal, and explain geometrically that a zero row means either a double line or a union of two lines through the epipole. They also state that the two-view conic multiview variety has dimension \(8\). Their discussion does not identify the epipolar plane component as a singular divisor and does not state the resulting failure of normality.

Targeted searches for the exact object together with `singular locus`, `singular divisor`, `normal`, `nonnormal`, and equivalent rank-one descriptions did not locate a published statement of this codimension-one singularity or the normality consequence. A later overview of algebraic multiview geometry was also checked at the level available through public indexing; no matching statement was found.

## Limitations

This is not a computation of the full singular locus. Additional higher-codimension singular strata may occur where both quadratic rows degenerate or where their differentials fail transversality. No normalization ring is constructed here. The originality assessment is literature-based and cannot exclude an equivalent statement hidden under different terminology or in an unindexed source.
