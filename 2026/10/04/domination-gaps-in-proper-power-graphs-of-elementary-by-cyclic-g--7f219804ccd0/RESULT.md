# Domination gaps in proper power graphs of elementary-by-cyclic groups

## Finding

Let \(p\) and \(q\) be distinct primes, let \(d\ge1\), and let \(G\cong C_p^d\times C_q\). Write \(\mathcal P^*(G)\) for the proper power graph and \[t=\frac{p^d-1}{p-1},\] the number of order-\(p\) subgroups of \(C_p^d\). Then \[\gamma(\mathcal P^*(G))=t,\qquad \gamma_t(\mathcal P^*(G))=t+1,\qquad \gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\mathcal P^*(G))=2t.\] In particular, this family has \(\gamma_t-\gamma=1\) while \(\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}-\gamma=t\), so the paired-domination gap is unbounded as \(d\) grows.

The formulas come from an intrinsic block decomposition of the proper power graph. Each one-dimensional subgroup of the elementary abelian factor contributes a clique block that must be represented in every dominating set, while the nonidentity elements of the cyclic \\(q\\)-factor form a transversal clique that can support total domination but cannot reduce the number of matching edges required for paired domination.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \\(p\\) and \\(q\\) be distinct primes and let
\\[
G=P\\times Q,
\\qquad
P\\cong C_p^d,
\\qquad
Q\\cong C_q,
\\qquad d\\ge1.
\\]
The proper power graph \\(\\mathcal P^*(G)\\) has vertex set \\(G\\setminus\\{1\\}\\), with two distinct vertices adjacent when one is a positive power of the other.

Let \\(\\mathscr L\\) be the set of subgroups of \\(P\\) of order \\(p\\). Since \\(P\\) is a \\(d\\)-dimensional vector space over \\(\\mathbb F_p\\),
\\[
|\\mathscr L|=t=\\frac{p^d-1}{p-1}.
\\]
For \\(L\\in\\mathscr L\\), define
\\[
B_L=(L\\setminus\\{1\\})\\times Q,
\\]
and put
\\[
Q^*=\\{1\\}\\times(Q\\setminus\\{1\\}).
\\]
Then
\\[
G\\setminus\\{1\\}=Q^*\\;\\dot\\cup\\;\\bigsqcup_{L\\in\\mathscr L} B_L.
\\]

## Proof

Fix \\(L\\in\\mathscr L\\). The subgroup \\(L\\times Q\\) is cyclic of order \\(pq\\). Every element of \\(B_L\\) either has order \\(p\\) or is a generator of \\(L\\times Q\\). Therefore every two vertices of \\(B_L\\) are adjacent, so \\(B_L\\) is a clique.

If \\(L\\ne K\\), then there are no edges between \\(B_L\\) and \\(B_K\\). Indeed, adjacency would force the cyclic subgroups generated by the two vertices to have comparable order-\\(p\\) parts, hence the same order-\\(p\\) subgroup of \\(P\\), contrary to \\(L\\ne K\\).

The set \\(Q^*\\) is also a clique. A vertex of \\(Q^*\\) is adjacent to every mixed-order vertex of every \\(B_L\\), because each mixed-order vertex generates \\(L\\times Q\\) and hence contains \\(Q\\). It is not adjacent to the order-\\(p\\) vertices of \\(B_L\\).

We now compute the three parameters.

For ordinary domination, each block \\(B_L\\) contains order-\\(p\\) vertices having no neighbors outside \\(B_L\\). Hence every dominating set meets every \\(B_L\\), giving
\\[
\\gamma(\\mathcal P^*(G))\\ge t.
\\]
Choose one mixed-order generator from each \\(B_L\\). These \\(t\\) vertices dominate their respective clique blocks and also dominate \\(Q^*\\). Thus
\\[
\\gamma(\\mathcal P^*(G))=t.
\\]

For total domination, a set of size \\(t\\) meeting every block has exactly one selected vertex in each \\(B_L\\) and none in \\(Q^*\\). Since distinct blocks have no edges between them, every selected vertex then has no selected neighbor. Therefore
\\[
\\gamma_t(\\mathcal P^*(G))\\ge t+1.
\\]
Taking one mixed-order generator from each \\(B_L\\) together with any vertex of \\(Q^*\\) gives a total dominating set of size \\(t+1\\): the transversal \\(q\\)-vertex is adjacent to every selected mixed-order generator, and those generators dominate all blocks. Hence
\\[
\\gamma_t(\\mathcal P^*(G))=t+1.
\\]

For paired domination, let \\(D\\) be paired dominating and fix a perfect matching \\(M\\) of the induced graph on \\(D\\). As above, \\(D\\cap B_L\\ne\\varnothing\\) for every \\(L\\). Any selected vertex in \\(B_L\\) can be matched only to another selected vertex in \\(B_L\\) or to a vertex of \\(Q^*\\); it cannot be matched to a vertex in a different block. Consequently each matching edge can account for selected vertices from at most one block \\(B_L\\). Since all \\(t\\) blocks must be represented, \\(M\\) has at least \\(t\\) edges and
\\[
|D|\\ge2t.
\\]
For each \\(L\\), choose two mixed-order generators of the cyclic group \\(L\\times Q\\). Such a pair exists because
\\[
\\varphi(pq)=(p-1)(q-1)\\ge2.
\\]
The chosen pair is adjacent; pairing the two choices inside every block gives a perfect matching on \\(2t\\) selected vertices. These vertices dominate every \\(B_L\\) and also \\(Q^*\\). Therefore
\\[
\\gamma_{\\mathrm{pr}}(\\mathcal P^*(G))=2t.
\\]

## Relationship to prior work

Doostabadi and Farrokhi studied connectivity of proper power graphs and, for finite \\(p\\)-groups, identified connected components with minimal cyclic subgroups. Their nilpotent results also show how strongly Sylow structure controls proper-power connectivity, but they do not give the block decomposition above for \\(C_p^d\\times C_q\\) or any total/paired domination formula.

Bera, Dey, Patra, and Sahoo later determined ordinary domination of proper power graphs in broad finite-group and nilpotent settings. In particular, their results connect ordinary domination to prime-order subgroups. The present theorem is consistent with the ordinary value \\(t\\), but the total and paired parameters require additional adjacency and matching information. The paired lower bound is not a consequence of the ordinary domination number: it uses the fact that no matching edge can serve two distinct order-\\(p\\) blocks.

Paired domination was introduced by Haynes and Slater for general graphs. Targeted searches combining proper power graphs, elementary abelian factors, direct products with a cyclic prime factor, total domination, and paired domination did not locate the displayed triple or the unbounded paired-versus-ordinary gap.

## Limitations

The elementary abelian hypothesis on the \\(p\\)-factor is essential to the proof because it partitions nonidentity \\(p\\)-components uniquely by order-\\(p\\) lines and makes each \\(B_L\\) a clique. The same formulas are not asserted for arbitrary \\(p\\)-groups.

The \\(q\\)-Sylow subgroup is assumed cyclic of prime order. Higher \\(q\\)-power or noncyclic \\(q\\)-factors can introduce additional block types and are not covered.

No domination polynomial, locating variant, or independent domination claim is made.
