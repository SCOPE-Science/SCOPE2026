# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The source necessary condition gives \(k\le\sigma(L_k)/L_k\). Grönwall's maximal-order theorem gives, for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the eventual bound
\[
\frac{\sigma(n)}n\le(e^\gamma+\varepsilon)\log\log n.
\]
The minima \(L_k\) tend to infinity along any unbounded set of existing \(k\)-layered levels, so the two inequalities combine for all sufficiently large such \(k\). Taking a lower limit and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the stated constant \(e^{-\gamma}\). No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. Jokar's 2022 full text states the exponential-growth conjecture and the necessary abundancy inequality separately, but does not derive a maximal-order lower bound. The earlier systematic paper and the current sequence database also do not state the theorem. Exact and semantic searches for the conjecture, Grönwall, double-exponential growth, and abundancy maximal order found no covering result. The main residual risk is an unindexed short observation.

## Value
PASS. The theorem rigorously advances a named growth conjecture and changes the scale of the known lower-growth picture from an informal exponential suggestion to a double-exponential necessary size. This constrains any construction aimed at the still-open existence problem for large \(k\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary source is Farid Jokar, “On \(k\)-layered numbers,” arXiv:2207.09053v1. The closest earlier systematic source is Mahanta--Saikia--Yaqubi, arXiv:2008.11096v1. The result gives no existence theorem and no matching upper bound, and therefore does not determine the true asymptotic growth of the smallest \(k\)-layered number.
