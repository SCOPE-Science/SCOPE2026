# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Jahn--Winter's sufficient face criterion implies that any seven-dimensional obstruction has a face \\(F\\) for which both \\(F\\) and \\(F^\\Delta\\) have strict vertex excess. Dimension duality forces both to be three-dimensional. Euler's formula plus minimum vertex degree shows that such a three-polytope has at least six vertices. Four additional vertices are needed to raise a three-face to ambient dimension seven. If the total were ten, equality in that affine-dimension count forces a free join with a tetrahedron, which makes exactly four facets contain \\(F\\), contradicting the six vertices required in \\(F^\\Delta\\). Duality gives the facet bound. The prism/dual-prism join has the claimed counts and violates the exact published face criterion.

The standalone checker replays every finite arithmetic component. No finite experiment is used to establish the structural implication.

## Originality

PASS with residual search risk. The full open-access primary article was inspected at Theorems 6 and 7, Corollary 8, Construction 10, Theorem 11, Example 12, and the concluding open questions. It proves existence of seven-dimensional free-join obstructions but does not state a smallest vertex/facet threshold. Its displayed generic dimension-seven example uses the cube and cross-polytope, yielding fourteen vertices and fourteen facets.

Exact-phrase and alias searches for an eleven-vertex obstruction, triangular-prism/bipyramid joins, minimum or smallest seven-dimensional assignment counterexamples, and Hall-deficiency formulations found no covering source. Indexed semantic searches likewise returned only unrelated polytope or matching results.

The main residual risk is that this short extremal consequence of the published criterion may have appeared in unindexed notes, talks, or under different terminology.

## Value

PASS. Dimension seven is the first dimension in which the assignment property can fail. Determining the exact smallest vertex and facet counts therefore gives a natural extremal benchmark for the first nontrivial dimension, improves the explicit fourteen-by-fourteen template to eleven-by-eleven, and proves that no unrelated construction can beat that size. The self-dual sharp witness is simple enough to serve as a canonical test example for future classification work.
