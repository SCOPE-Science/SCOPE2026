# Endomorphism ranks and symmetries of the two-generator free orthomodular lattice
## Finding

Let \(F_2\) be the free orthomodular lattice on two generators. The recent finite calculus of Emori, Iriki, Khrennikov, and Kondo gives
\[
F_2\cong \mathrm{MO}_2\times 2^4,
\]
where \(\mathrm{MO}_2\) is the six-element orthomodular lattice with four middle elements arranged in two orthocomplementary pairs.

Every ordered pair
\[
(u,v)\in F_2^2
\]
determines a unique endomorphism of \(F_2\), by freeness. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{End}(F_2)|=96^2=9216.
\]

The exact image-cardinality distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
|\operatorname{im} f|&2&4&8&12&16&24&48&96\\
\hline
\#\{f:|\operatorname{im} f|=\cdot\}&4&564&3720&32&2880&672&1152&192.
\end{array}
\]
No other endomorphism image size occurs.

The rank-\(96\) endomorphisms are exactly the automorphisms. Their group is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{OML}}(F_2)
\cong
(C_2\wr S_2)\times S_4
\cong
D_8\times S_4,
\]
so
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{OML}}(F_2)|=8\cdot24=192.
\]

The automorphism action on the \(96\) elements has exactly \(15\) orbits. Under
\[
F_2\cong \mathrm{MO}_2\times 2^4,
\]
an element is classified up to automorphism by:

1. whether its \(\mathrm{MO}_2\)-coordinate is \(0\), is \(1\), or is one of the four middle elements; and
2. the Hamming weight
\[
j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
\]
of its Boolean coordinate.

The corresponding orbit sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
&j=0&j=1&j=2&j=3&j=4\\
\hline
c=0&1&4&6&4&1\\
c=1&1&4&6&4&1\\
c\text{ middle}&4&16&24&16&4.
\end{array}
\]

Finally, an ordered pair \((u,v)\) generates \(F_2\) exactly when the associated endomorphism is surjective. Therefore there are exactly
\[
192
\]
ordered generating pairs. No generating pair has equal coordinates, so the coordinate swap acts freely; hence there are exactly
\[
96
\]
unordered generating pairs.

## Assumptions and scope

Automorphisms and endomorphisms here are orthomodular-lattice homomorphisms: they preserve \(0,1,\wedge,\vee\), and orthocomplementation.

The notation
\[
2^4
\]
denotes the sixteen-element Boolean algebra, represented by four-bit vectors with coordinatewise Boolean operations. The lattice \(\mathrm{MO}_2\) has elements
\[
0,1,a,a^\perp,b,b^\perp,
\]
with the four middle elements pairwise meeting in \(0\) and joining to \(1\), and with orthocomplementary pairs
\[
a\leftrightarrow a^\perp,
\qquad
b\leftrightarrow b^\perp.
\]

The source paper discusses orthocomplementation as an order-reversing involution and explicitly notes that it is not the full automorphism group. The group computed here consists of order-preserving orthomodular-lattice automorphisms.

The endomorphism-rank profile is finite and exhaustive. It is not inferred from sampling: all \(9216\) ordered generator images are enumerated by the bundled verifier.

## Proof

Because \(F_2\) is free on two generators, every map from the two free generators to an arbitrary pair
\[
(u,v)\in F_2^2
\]
extends uniquely to an endomorphism. Thus there are exactly
\[
96^2=9216
\]
endomorphisms. The image of the endomorphism associated with \((u,v)\) is exactly the orthomodular sublattice generated by \(u\) and \(v\).

The displayed rank distribution is obtained by an exhaustive finite classification. Using the published product representation
\[
F_2\cong\mathrm{MO}_2\times2^4,
\]
the verifier constructs all \(96\) elements and all operation tables. For each of the \(9216\) ordered pairs, it computes the least set containing the pair and closed under orthocomplement, meet, and join. The process terminates only at a closure fixed point. Since every orthomodular subalgebra containing the pair must contain every element produced by these closure operations, this fixed point is exactly the generated subalgebra. Counting its cardinality gives
\[
4,564,3720,32,2880,672,1152,192
\]
at image sizes
\[
2,4,8,12,16,24,48,96,
\]
respectively. The counts sum to \(9216\).

The automorphism group can be determined independently, giving a structural cross-check of the rank-\(96\) count.

In the product
\[
F_2=\mathrm{MO}_2\times2^4,
\]
the center is
\[
Z(F_2)=\{0,1\}\times2^4.
\]
It is a \(32\)-element Boolean algebra with five atoms. One central atom,
\[
e_*=(1,0000),
\]
has interval
\[
[0,e_*]\cong\mathrm{MO}_2
\]
of size \(6\). The other four central atoms,
\[
e_i=(0,\varepsilon_i)
\qquad(1\le i\le4),
\]
have two-element intervals. Hence every automorphism fixes \(e_*\) and may permute the four Boolean atoms \(e_i\).

It follows that every automorphism preserves the distinguished \(\mathrm{MO}_2\)-factor and permutes the four two-element Boolean factors. Conversely, every automorphism of \(\mathrm{MO}_2\), together with every permutation of those four Boolean factors, acts componentwise as an automorphism of \(F_2\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{OML}}(F_2)
\cong
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{MO}_2)\times S_4.
\]

An automorphism of \(\mathrm{MO}_2\) fixes \(0\) and \(1\) and permutes the four middle elements while commuting with the orthocomplement pairing
\[
(a\ a^\perp)(b\ b^\perp).
\]
The centralizer of this pairing in \(S_4\) is
\[
C_2\wr S_2\cong D_8
\]
of order \(8\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{OML}}(F_2)\cong D_8\times S_4
\]
and has order \(192\), exactly matching the exhaustive count of surjective endomorphisms.

The orbit classification is immediate from this product action. The \(D_8\)-factor has three orbits on \(\mathrm{MO}_2\):
\[
\{0\},\qquad\{1\},\qquad\{a,a^\perp,b,b^\perp\},
\]
while \(S_4\) has five orbits on four-bit vectors, indexed by Hamming weight. Their product gives \(3\cdot5=15\) orbits and the displayed sizes.

An ordered pair generates \(F_2\) iff its induced endomorphism has image size \(96\), giving \(192\) ordered generating pairs. If \(u=v\), the generated algebra is one-generated and cannot be all of the two-generated free algebra; equivalently, the exhaustive profile contains no diagonal rank-\(96\) pair. Thus the coordinate swap has no fixed point on the generating pairs, yielding \(96\) unordered pairs.

## Verification

The bundled `verify.py` reconstructs the finite product model from scratch.

It checks that the standard published generator representatives generate all \(96\) elements. It enumerates all \(8\) complement-preserving permutations of the four middle \(\mathrm{MO}_2\) elements and all \(24\) permutations of the Boolean coordinates, verifies that the resulting \(192\) maps preserve meet, join, orthocomplementation, \(0\), and \(1\), and confirms the \(15\)-orbit profile.

Independently, it closes every one of the
\[
96^2=9216
\]
ordered pairs under the orthomodular operations and obtains exactly
\[
\{2:4,\ 4:564,\ 8:3720,\ 12:32,\ 16:2880,\ 24:672,\ 48:1152,\ 96:192\}.
\]
It confirms that exactly \(192\) pairs generate the whole lattice and that none of them is diagonal.

The checker prints `VERIFY_OK`. This is an exhaustive finite proof of the rank table, not a partial enumeration or a timeout-based inference.

## Relationship to prior work

Emori, Iriki, Khrennikov, and Kondo give the explicit \(96\)-element decomposition
\[
F_2\cong\mathrm{MO}_2\times2^4
\]
and a complete coordinate calculus. They study the orthocomplement involution and explicitly remark that it is not the full automorphism group, which is larger and includes the generator swap. The checked text does not compute that full group.

Their paper also provides complete normal forms for all \(96\) elements, but it does not give the endomorphism image-rank distribution, an automorphism-orbit census, or the exact number of generating pairs.

Takagi, Do, and Ogata use the same classical \(96\)-element decomposition in a reachability analysis for equality of terms in free orthomodular lattices. The checked proceedings text does not state an automorphism group or endomorphism-rank profile.

The automorphism factorization itself is accessible from standard direct-product and central-decomposition arguments once the published product representation is known. The additional rank table is not a formal consequence of the factorization: it requires classifying the subalgebras generated by all ordered pairs, which the exhaustive calculation supplies.

## Limitations

The result concerns the two-generator free orthomodular lattice. Free orthomodular lattices on three or more generators are not finite, so the same exhaustive method does not extend directly.

The group computed is the orthomodular-lattice automorphism group, not the automorphism group of the underlying unlabeled lattice after forgetting orthocomplementation.

The rank profile records only image cardinality. Distinct endomorphisms with the same image size can have non-isomorphic kernels or different image subalgebras; those finer classifications are not claimed here.

An equivalent rank census may exist in older orthomodular-lattice literature under subalgebra-generation terminology not surfaced by the checked searches.
