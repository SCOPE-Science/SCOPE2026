# Endpoint-localized first off-diagonal magnitude homology of finite projective planes
## Finding
For every finite projective plane \(\Pi\) of order \(q\ge2\), let \(G_\Pi\) be the bipartite point-line incidence graph, equipped with its shortest-path metric. For every field \(F\) and every ordered pair of distinct points \(P,Q\),
\[
\dim_F \operatorname{MH}^{P,Q}_{3,4}(G_\Pi;F)=q^2.
\]
By point-line duality the same formula holds for every ordered pair of distinct lines. Here \(\operatorname{MH}^{P,Q}_{3,4}\) denotes the summand generated by magnitude chains that start at \(P\) and end at \(Q\). The value \(q^2\) equals the number of points outside the unique line \(L=PQ\).

## Assumptions and scope
A finite projective plane of order \(q\) has \(N=q^2+q+1\) points and the same number of lines; every point and line is incident with \(q+1\) objects of the opposite type; two distinct points determine a unique line, and dually. The incidence graph has distance \(2\) between distinct points and between distinct lines. Coefficients may be taken in an arbitrary field \(F\). The statement is endpoint-localized and does not assert a complete description of all bidegrees of magnitude homology.

## Proof
Fix distinct points \(P,Q\) and put \(L=PQ\). In bidegree \(3,4\), only endpoint distance \(2\) is relevant here. The localized chain groups in homological degrees \(2,3,4\) have the following dimensions.

For \(C_2\), a generator \((P,x,Q)\) of total length \(4\) has either a point \(x\notin\{P,Q\}\), or a line through exactly one of \(P,Q\). Hence
\[
\dim C_2=(N-2)+2q=q^2+3q-1.
\]

For \(C_3\), the three successive distances are a permutation of \((2,1,1)\). Each of the three positions for the distance \(2\) contributes \(q(q+2)\) generators. For example, in pattern \((2,1,1)\), if the second vertex is a point different from both \(P,Q\), its following line is forced; if it is \(Q\), there are \(q+1\) possible following lines. Thus
\[
\dim C_3=3q(q+2).
\]

For \(C_4\), every step has length \(1\). Write a generator as \((P,a,R,b,Q)\), where \(a\) is a line through \(P\) and \(b\) a line through \(Q\). If \(a\ne b\), then \(R=a\cap b\) is forced, giving \((q+1)^2-1\) generators. If \(a=b=L\), then \(R\) may be any of the \(q+1\) points on \(L\). Therefore
\[
\dim C_4=q^2+3q+1.
\]

The boundary \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\) is surjective. Indeed, let \(r_R=(P,R,Q)\) for a point \(R\notin\{P,Q\}\), let \(p_a=(P,a,Q)\) for a line \(a\ni P\), \(a\ne L\), and let \(q_b=(P,b,Q)\) for a line \(b\ni Q\), \(b\ne L\). If \(R\in L\setminus\{P,Q\}\), the chain \((P,R,L,Q)\) has boundary \(\pm r_R\). If \(R\notin L\), the chain \((P,R,RQ,Q)\) has boundary \(\pm r_R\pm q_{RQ}\), while \((P,Q,b,Q)\) has boundary \(\pm q_b\) for every \(b\ni Q\), \(b\ne L\). Thus all \(r_R\) and \(q_b\) lie in the image. The dual argument using chains of pattern \((1,1,2)\) supplies every \(p_a\).

The boundary \(\partial_4:C_4\to C_3\) is injective. If \(a\ne b\), the boundary of \((P,a,a\cap b,b,Q)\) contains the basis element \((P,a,b,Q)\), and no other \(C_4\)-generator has that same basis element in its boundary. Hence any linear relation among \(\partial_4\)-images has zero coefficient on every generator with \(a\ne b\). The remaining generators are \((P,L,R,L,Q)\) with \(R\in L\). For \(R=P\) and \(R=Q\) their boundaries have the unique terms \((P,L,P,Q)\) and \((P,Q,L,Q)\), respectively; for \(R\in L\setminus\{P,Q\}\), the boundary is the sum, up to signs, of the two distinct basis elements \((P,R,L,Q)\) and \((P,L,R,Q)\). These \(q+1\) remaining images are linearly independent over every field.

Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3=q^2+3q-1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_4=q^2+3q+1\). Since \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\),
\[
\dim_F\operatorname{MH}^{P,Q}_{3,4}
=3q(q+2)-(q^2+3q-1)-(q^2+3q+1)=q^2.
\]
Point-line duality proves the line-line case.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_projective_plane_mh.py` independently constructs the Desarguesian planes \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\) for prime \(q=2,3,5\), enumerates the localized magnitude chain groups, builds the mod-\(2\) boundary matrices, and verifies for every ordered distance-\(2\) endpoint pair the dimensions
\[
(q^2+3q-1,\;3q(q+2),\;q^2+3q+1)
\]
and ranks \(q^2+3q-1\) and \(q^2+3q+1\). The finite replay is a check of the formulas, not a substitute for the projective-plane-axiom proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Asao, Hiraoka and Kanazawa prove that sufficiently large girth forces successive off-diagonal magnitude-homology groups to vanish; in particular their girth bound places \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}\) exactly at the first unresolved boundary when the girth is \(6\). Their paper does not discuss projective planes, incidence graphs, or an endpoint-localized rank formula. Gu develops algebraic-Morse methods and computes several classical graph families, but does not state this projective-plane formula. The result here therefore gives an exact value at the sharp girth boundary for a canonical infinite family.

## Limitations
The proof determines only the endpoint-localized bidegree \((3,4)\) for distinct same-side endpoints. It does not compute equal-endpoint summands, mixed point-line endpoint summands in other lengths, higher off-diagonals, or integral torsion beyond what is forced by the fieldwise rank statement. The computational replay covers prime-order Desarguesian examples \(q=2,3,5\); the general proof uses only the axioms of a finite projective plane and therefore does not require Desarguesianity.
