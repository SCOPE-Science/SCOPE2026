# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof explicitly classifies the localized chain generators in degrees \(2,3,4\), obtains dimensions \(q^2+3q-1\), \(3q(q+2)\), and \(q^2+3q+1\), proves \(\partial_3\) surjective by explicit preimages of every \(C_2\)-basis type, and proves \(\partial_4\) injective using unique \((P,a,b,Q)\) pivots plus an independent residual family on the joining line. The argument uses only coefficients \(\pm1\), so it works over every field. The replay verifies all ordered distance-\(2\) endpoint blocks for \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\), \(q=2,3,5\).

## Originality
PASS. Database searches for the exact rank formula, projective-plane incidence aliases, girth-six formulations, and \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}\) returned no covering finding. The closest projective-geometry hit concerns a different graph invariant, and the closest Heawood hit concerns metric \(L^1\)-distortion. The full text of Asao--Hiraoka--Kanazawa was inspected: their Theorem 1.3/Corollary 1.4 gives vanishing beyond the girth-six boundary but contains no occurrences of “projective,” “incidence,” or “Heawood,” and no exact localized rank at girth six. Gu's stated computations cover trees, pawful graphs, the icosahedral graph, and cycles, not finite projective-plane incidence graphs. The prior local ledger contains a Heawood total-rank computation at \(q=2\); that special computation neither states nor implies the arbitrary-plane, arbitrary-field, endpoint-localized formula.

## Value
PASS. Endpoint localization is intrinsic to magnitude homology, and projective-plane incidence graphs are canonical highly symmetric girth-six graphs lying exactly at the sharp boundary where the published girth theorem stops forcing \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}\) to vanish. The formula identifies a geometric quantity—points off the joining line—as the exact local homological dimension, uniformly for every finite projective plane and every coefficient field.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest theorem is Asao--Hiraoka--Kanazawa's girth-controlled vanishing theorem. The result here treats the excluded girth-six boundary rather than a corollary of their vanishing range. The claim does not give the complete magnitude homology of the incidence graph and does not assert a total-rank formula.
