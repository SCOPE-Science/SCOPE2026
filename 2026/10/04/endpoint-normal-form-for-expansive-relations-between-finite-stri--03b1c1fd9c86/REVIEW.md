# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Forward confluence on a strict chain is equivalent to strict increase of row maxima; backward confluence is equivalent to strict increase of row minima. The converse uses the later-row maximum and earlier-row minimum as witnesses. The existence threshold, size bounds, unique maximum, functional minimum-size classification, and weighted census then follow by elementary but complete endpoint arguments. A standalone exhaustive checker verifies the quantified definitions against the theorem for every relation between chains of sizes at most four.

## Originality

PASS. The 2026 primary source defines and motivates expansive relations, proves their closure properties, and uses them in simulations, but does not classify finite-chain instances or give endpoint, extremal-size, or counting formulas. Candidate-specific published-finding and web searches using the source terminology and linear-order aliases did not locate an equivalent theorem.

The closest separate literature found studies surjective bounded morphisms between finite linear temporal processes. Those morphisms are functional and surjective, whereas the present relation may be multivalued and is required to be total only on the source. Its accessible abstract therefore does not dominate the claim. Lack of an open full-text copy in the bounded search is recorded as a residual bibliographic risk.

## Value

PASS. Expansive relations are explicitly presented in the motivating work as the bisimulation-invariant analogue of homomorphisms and are used to define simulations. Finite strict chains are the canonical linear transitive test frames. The theorem replaces quantified back-and-forth conditions by two monotone endpoint sequences and supplies an exact existence criterion, sharp uniquely attained maximum size, complete functional-minimum classification, and exact total census.

## Closest literature and limitations

The primary comparison is Fernández-Duque’s 2026 Fine-selection preprint. The closest separate comparison found is Gabelaia–Kuznetsov–Mihailescu–Razmadze–Uridia on surjective bounded morphisms between finite linear processes. Neither inspected source states the multivalued endpoint normal form.

The theorem does not extend verbatim to branching frames or clustered preorders, where row extrema cannot encode all confluence witnesses.
