# Equal-share covariance of root degree and leaves in a random recursive tree

## Finding

Let \(T_n\) be the random recursive tree on vertices
\[
1,\ldots,n,
\qquad
n\ge2.
\]
For each arrival \(j\ge2\), choose its parent \(P_j\) independently and uniformly from
\[
\{1,\ldots,j-1\}.
\]

Let
\[
D_n=\deg^+_{T_n}(1)
\]
be the outdegree of the root, and let \(L_n\) be the number of leaves. Define
\[
A_j=\mathbf 1\{P_j=1\},
\qquad
2\le j\le n,
\]
and, for \(i\ge2\),
\[
Y_i=\mathbf 1\{i\text{ is a leaf in }T_n\}.
\]
Then
\[
D_n=\sum_{j=2}^n A_j,
\qquad
L_n=\sum_{i=2}^n Y_i.
\]

The covariance of root degree with each labeled leaf indicator is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(D_n,Y_i)
=
\frac{n-i}{(n-1)^2},
\qquad
2\le i\le n.
}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus older vertices carry more of the root-degree/leaf dependence, with an exactly linear label profile.

More strikingly, every nondeterministic root-attachment decision contributes exactly the same covariance to the final leaf count:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A_j,L_n)
=
\frac1{2(n-1)},
\qquad
3\le j\le n.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(A_2=1\) deterministically,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(D_n,L_n)
=
\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

For \(n\ge3\),
\[
\mathbb EL_n=\frac n2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(L_n)=\frac n{12},
\tag{4}
\]
while
\[
\mathbb ED_n=H_{n-1},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(D_n)
=
H_{n-1}-H_{n-1}^{(2)},
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
H_m=\sum_{k=1}^m\frac1k,
\qquad
H_m^{(2)}=\sum_{k=1}^m\frac1{k^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(D_n,L_n)
=
\frac{\sqrt3\,(n-2)}
{(n-1)\sqrt{n\left(H_{n-1}-H_{n-1}^{(2)}\right)}}.
}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(D_n,L_n)\longrightarrow\frac12,
\qquad
\operatorname{Corr}(D_n,L_n)
\sim
\sqrt{\frac3{n\log n}}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus root degree and total leaf count remain positively coupled at order one, even though their ordinary correlation vanishes. The equal-share identity (2) shows that this covariance is accumulated uniformly over all post-initial arrival times.

## Assumptions and scope

The tree is the standard random recursive tree generated by independent uniform parent choices. Degree means outdegree in the rooted orientation; for the root this equals ordinary graph degree.

Leaves are vertices of outdegree zero. For \(n\ge2\), the root is not a leaf, so \(L_n\) is exactly the sum of the indicators \(Y_i\) over \(i\ge2\).

The covariance statements are finite-\(n\) identities. They do not assert positive association of the full degree/leaf process.

## Proof

The parent variables
\[
P_2,\ldots,P_n
\]
are independent, with
\[
\Pr(P_j=r)=\frac1{j-1},
\qquad
1\le r<j.
\]

Fix \(i\ge2\). Vertex \(i\) is a leaf at time \(n\) precisely when no later arrival chooses it as parent. Therefore
\[
\Pr(Y_i=1)
=
\prod_{t=i+1}^n
\left(1-\frac1{t-1}\right)
=
\frac{i-1}{n-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

If \(j\le i\), then \(A_j\) is independent of \(Y_i\), because \(Y_i\) depends only on the parent choices made after time \(i\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_j,Y_i)=0
\qquad
(j\le i).
\tag{9}
\]

Now let \(j>i\). On the event \(A_j=1\), arrival \(j\) attaches to the root and therefore cannot destroy the leaf status of vertex \(i\). Using independence of the parent choices at all other times,
\[
\Pr(A_j=1,Y_i=1)
=
\frac1{j-1}
\prod_{\substack{t=i+1\\t\ne j}}^n
\left(1-\frac1{t-1}\right).
\]
Removing the \(t=j\) factor from (8) gives
\[
\Pr(A_j=1,Y_i=1)
=
\frac{i-1}{(n-1)(j-2)}.
\]
Since
\[
\Pr(A_j=1)\Pr(Y_i=1)
=
\frac{i-1}{(n-1)(j-1)},
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A_j,Y_i)
=
\frac{i-1}
{(n-1)(j-1)(j-2)},
\qquad
j>i\ge2.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Summing (10) over \(j=i+1,\ldots,n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(D_n,Y_i)
&=
\frac{i-1}{n-1}
\sum_{j=i+1}^n
\frac1{(j-1)(j-2)}\\
&=
\frac{i-1}{n-1}
\left(
\frac1{i-1}-\frac1{n-1}
\right)\\
&=
\frac{n-i}{(n-1)^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (1).

Alternatively, fix \(j\ge3\) and sum (10) over all \(i<j\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(A_j,L_n)
&=
\frac1{(n-1)(j-1)(j-2)}
\sum_{i=2}^{j-1}(i-1)\\
&=
\frac1{2(n-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (2). Summing over \(j=3,\ldots,n\) proves (3).

It remains to record the marginal variances needed for (6). Since
\[
A_2=1
\]
and the variables \(A_j\), \(j\ge3\), are independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities
\[
\frac1{j-1},
\]
we have
\[
\mathbb ED_n
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac1k
=
H_{n-1}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(D_n)
=
\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}
\left(
\frac1k-\frac1{k^2}
\right)
=
H_{n-1}-H_{n-1}^{(2)}.
\]

For leaves, condition on \(L_{m-1}\) when vertex \(m\) arrives. The new vertex is always a leaf. If it attaches to an existing leaf, that parent ceases to be a leaf; otherwise the total leaf count increases by one. Thus
\[
L_m
=
L_{m-1}+1-B_m,
\]
where, conditionally on \(L_{m-1}\),
\[
B_m\sim
\operatorname{Bernoulli}
\left(
\frac{L_{m-1}}{m-1}
\right).
\tag{11}
\]
This immediately gives
\[
\mathbb EL_m
=
\left(1-\frac1{m-1}\right)\mathbb EL_{m-1}+1,
\]
and with \(L_2=1\),
\[
\mathbb EL_m=\frac m2.
\]
A second-moment calculation from (11) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}(L_m)=\frac m{12},
\qquad
m\ge3,
\]
with the base value
\[
\operatorname{Var}(L_3)=\frac14.
\]
This proves (4).

Substitution of (3)--(5) gives (6). Finally,
\[
H_{n-1}-H_{n-1}^{(2)}\sim\log n,
\]
so (7) follows.

It verifies (1), (2), and (3) entry by entry using exact rational arithmetic. It also verifies the exact means and variances in (4)--(5).

Separately, it checks the leaf-count first- and second-moment recursion and the closed correlation formula for a large range of \(n\).

Finite replay is supplementary. The universal theorem is proved by the independent-parent calculation and telescoping sums above.

## Relationship to prior work

Bryc, Minda, and Sethuraman study the number of leaves in random recursive trees as a Markov chain. Their full treatment identifies the random-recursive-tree leaf process with the linear case of their general chain and develops laws of large numbers, central limit theorems, and large deviations. This supplies the classical leaf-count framework but does not introduce the distinguished root degree or its covariance with leaves.

Janson studies the degree-count vector of a random recursive tree and gives asymptotic variances and covariances among the numbers of vertices of specified outdegrees. The statistic here is different: \(D_n\) is the degree of the distinguished root, not the number of vertices of a given degree, and the exact covariance with total leaves is finite-\(n\).

Recent work on leaf degrees in growing random trees studies, for each vertex, the number of neighboring leaves and the associated empirical distribution. That local leaf-degree statistic is again different from the covariance between root degree and the global leaf count considered here.

Targeted searches for root-degree/leaf-count covariance, joint laws, and equivalent recursive-tree formulations did not locate (1)--(3), in particular the equal-share identity (2).

## Limitations

The result is specific to the standard random recursive tree with independent uniform parent choices. Preferential-attachment and nonuniform recursive-tree models change both the root attachment probabilities and the leaf-survival probabilities.

The theorem gives covariance structure, not a complete joint distribution of \((D_n,L_n)\).

The proof is elementary once the arrival-time leaf indicators are paired with root-attachment indicators. Older recursive-tree literature may contain an equivalent mixed moment under a different encoding; this is the principal originality risk.
