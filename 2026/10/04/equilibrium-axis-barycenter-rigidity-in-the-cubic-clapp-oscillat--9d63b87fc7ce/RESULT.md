# Equilibrium-axis barycenter rigidity in the cubic Clapp oscillator
## Finding
Consider the cubic polynomial realization of the Clapp oscillator
\[
C_1\dot v_1=-g v_1-h v_2+i,\qquad
C_2\dot v_2=\kappa v_1(v_1^2-\rho^2)-i,
\]
\[
C_3\dot v_3=i,\qquad
L\dot i=v_2-v_1-v_3,
\]
with \(C_1,C_2,C_3,L,g,h,\kappa,\rho>0\). This is the polynomial circuit equation printed in the source after the identifications \(g=1/R_1\), \(h=K V_a/R_2\), \(\kappa=K/R_z\), and \(\rho^2=V_b\).

For every compactly supported invariant Borel probability measure \(\mu\), set \(m_j=\int v_j\,d\mu\) and \(m_i=\int i\,d\mu\). Then
\[
m_i=0,\qquad m_2=-\frac{g}{h}m_1,\qquad m_3=-\left(1+\frac{g}{h}\right)m_1,
\]
and
\[
\int v_1^3\,d\mu=\rho^2m_1.
\]
Therefore the barycenter is exactly
\[
\left(m_1,-\frac{g}{h}m_1,-\left(1+\frac{g}{h}\right)m_1,0\right),
\]
which lies on the same line through the three equilibria \(0\) and
\[
\pm\rho\left(1,-\frac{g}{h},-1-\frac{g}{h},0\right).
\]
The statement applies to invariant measures generated by periodic, chaotic, or hyperchaotic recurrent dynamics whenever their support is compact.

There is also a sharp one-sided geometric consequence. If \(\mu\) is ergodic, non-equilibrium, and supported in \(v_1\ge0\), then
\[
0<m_1<\rho,
\]
and both sets \(0<v_1<\rho\) and \(v_1>\rho\) have positive \(\mu\)-measure. By inversion symmetry, if \(\mu\) is supported in \(v_1\le0\), then \(-\rho<m_1<0\), with positive measure both in \(-\rho<v_1<0\) and in \(v_1<-\rho\). Thus every one-sided non-equilibrium recurrent state crosses the voltage of the corresponding outer equilibrium.

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns the polynomial Clapp model, not the source's piecewise-linear alternative. The parameters above are strictly positive; in the source's notation this is the regime with a real pair of outer equilibria. Compact support is assumed so that coordinate polynomials are integrable and trajectories in the invariant support are complete. The threshold-crossing conclusion additionally assumes ergodicity and one-sided support. No assertion is made that every attractor displayed in the source is one-sided.

## Proof
For any continuously differentiable observable \(\phi\) on a neighborhood of a compact invariant support, invariance gives \(\int \mathcal L\phi\,d\mu=0\), where \(\mathcal L\) is differentiation along the vector field. Apply this to the four coordinate functions.

From \(\mathcal L v_3=i/C_3\), one gets \(m_i=0\). Averaging the first equation then gives \(-g m_1-hm_2=0\), hence \(m_2=-(g/h)m_1\). Averaging the fourth gives \(m_2-m_1-m_3=0\), hence \(m_3=-(1+g/h)m_1\). Averaging the second and using \(m_i=0\) gives
\[
\kappa\left(\int v_1^3\,d\mu-\rho^2m_1\right)=0,
\]
which proves the cubic moment identity and the barycenter formula.

Assume now that \(\mu\) is ergodic, non-equilibrium, and supported in \(v_1\ge0\). If \(m_1=0\), then \(v_1=0\) almost surely. Since the support is invariant and \(v_1\) is continuous, \(v_1\) is identically zero on every orbit in the support. The first two equations then give \(i=h v_2\) and \(\dot v_2=-(h/C_2)v_2\). A complete bounded orbit forces \(v_2=i=0\), and the fourth equation forces \(v_3=0\). Thus \(\mu\) would be the origin point mass, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence \(m_1>0\).

Because \(x\mapsto x^3\) is strictly convex on \([0,\infty)\), Jensen's inequality and the moment identity give
\[
m_1^3\le\int v_1^3\,d\mu=\rho^2m_1.
\]
Thus \(m_1\le\rho\). Equality in Jensen would make \(v_1=\rho\) almost surely. Invariance then forces the complete bounded orbit to be the positive outer equilibrium, again contradicting non-equilibrium. Therefore \(m_1<\rho\).

Finally,
\[
\int v_1(v_1^2-\rho^2)\,d\mu=0.
\]
If either \(0<v_1<\rho\) or \(v_1>\rho\) had zero measure, the integrand would have one sign almost surely; equality would force \(v_1\) to take only the values \(0\) and \(\rho\) almost surely. The support is then contained in those two threshold levels. A continuous orbit in that support cannot move between the two levels, so \(v_1\) is constant on each support orbit. The same bounded-complete-orbit argument reduces such an orbit to one of the equilibria, contradicting the non-equilibrium ergodic hypothesis. Both intervals therefore have positive measure. The negative-side statement follows by inversion symmetry.

## Verification
The derivation uses only the four printed differential equations, invariance of a compactly supported probability measure, strict convexity on a half-line, and bounded completeness of trajectories inside a compact invariant support. Substituting the two outer equilibria directly into the vector field gives zero in all four components. The proof does not infer an infinite-time claim from finite numerical simulation.

The source's circuit equation has \(\rho^2=V_b\), so the exact threshold in physical circuit notation is \(|v_1|=\sqrt{V_b}\). The source independently prints the same three equilibrium locations, which is a consistency check on the normalization.

## Relationship to prior work
Petržela's 2022 article introduces and numerically studies this polynomial Clapp oscillator, gives its three equilibria, reports coexisting chaotic and hyperchaotic regimes, and identifies multistability and coexistence as directions for further investigation. It does not state the invariant-measure barycenter identity, the cubic stationary moment law, or the one-sided threshold-crossing theorem.

A related 2019 single-transistor chaotic snap-circuit paper discusses a different Clapp-based oscillator and damping/attractor-dimension relations. That literature does not imply the stationary cubic-moment identity above. General facts such as \(\int\mathcal L\phi\,d\mu=0\) are standard and are not claimed as new; the claim is the exact consequence for this Clapp vector field and its threshold interpretation.

## Limitations
The theorem is conditional on compact invariant support. The strict crossing conclusion is conditional on a one-sided ergodic state; centrally symmetric invariant states may instead have zero barycenter. The theorem does not establish existence, uniqueness, or hyperbolicity of any particular attractor, and it does not identify the mechanism of a chaos-to-hyperchaos transition.
