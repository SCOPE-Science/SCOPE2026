# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For even \(k\), any prime block is odd. Above the proposed threshold the starting prime cannot equal the least missing prime \(\rho(k)\), so the exact progression lemma forces a composite by index \(\rho(k)-1\). The largest prime divisor of that odd composite is at most one third of it, and the resulting prime is strictly smaller. Such strict descent cannot close into a cycle. Disjoint cycles then inject into the primes at most the threshold. The packaged checker independently replays the local inequalities and finite trajectory regressions.

## Originality
PASS. Das states a generic \(k\sqrt{k}/2\) prime-in-loop bound. Dubickas introduces the same least-missing-prime parameter and proves the sharp prime-run ceiling, but the inspected full text gives broad trajectory bounds rather than this parity-specific cycle locator. Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches found no statement of \(B(k)=\max\{\rho(k),k(\rho(k)-1)/2\}\) or its even \(3\nmid k\) corollary \(L(k)\le\pi(k)\). A prior finite odd-parameter computation does not imply the even theorem.

## Value
PASS. On the natural family of even parameters not divisible by \(3\), the bound is linear: every cycle contains a prime at most \(k\). For \(k\ge8\) this strictly improves the published \(k\sqrt{k}/2\) locator and materially shrinks the finite search space for cycles. The mechanism also yields explicit bounds for other least-missing-prime strata.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Das's loop-count theorem and Remark 2.8, and Dubickas's later least-missing-prime progression lemma and global trajectory bounds. The result does not determine exact cycle counts, does not settle unboundedness in \(k\), and is not uniformly the numerically smallest bound for every small parameter.
