# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), ground-field diagonalizability is equivalent to idempotence. A square-zero \(3\times3\) matrix has rank at most one, so the determinant-lemma calculation reduces all nontrivial decompositions to four possible characteristic-polynomial shapes. This excludes the two length-three Jordan classes and the two irreducible-cubic classes. Explicit decompositions cover every other rational-canonical type. Exact enumeration independently confirms 380 decomposable matrices and the complementary four conjugacy classes of sizes 42, 42, 24, and 24.

## Originality
PASS, with recorded residual risk. The closest 2026 characteristic-two source proves the universal diagonalizable-plus-square-zero theorem only for finite fields with more than three elements and gives, for \(\mathbb F_2\), the weaker conclusion “potent of index at most four plus square-zero.” The 2025 odd-characteristic source gives an analogous cubic obstruction over \(\mathbb F_3\), not the binary classification. The older general theorem covers all \(2\times2\) matrices and finite \(n\times n\) matrices only when the field has at least \(n+1\) elements. Searches for the exact four-class boundary, the counts 380/132, and the class-size tuple 42/42/24/24 found no covering source. The full text of the older 2022 article was not inspected beyond the accessible abstract/theorem scope, so a buried equivalent small-case observation cannot be completely excluded.

PASS. The binary \(3\times3\) case is the first dimension/field-size pair immediately outside the universal \(2\times2\) theorem and outside the finite-field size hypothesis. The result replaces the recent weaker potent decomposition over \(\mathbb F_2\) with an exact yes/no boundary, identifies the complete obstruction mechanisms, and supplies exact finite counts.
