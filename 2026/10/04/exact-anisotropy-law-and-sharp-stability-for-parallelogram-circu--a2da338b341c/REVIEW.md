PASS. The proof reduces every circumellipse of a parallelogram to a one-parameter positive-definite matrix \(K_c=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(|c|<1\), in the half-diagonal basis. Direct determinant and generalized-eigenvalue calculations yield the exact \(\cosh\) law. The sharp quadratic coefficient follows from \(\sinh t\ge t\) and its equality-order expansion at zero. The checker replays the identities after nonsingular affine transformations.

The accepted claim is the exact generalized-eigenvalue anisotropy profile, its inverse, and the best global quadratic stability constant. Targeted scientific/database searches found no equivalent statement. The full text of Horwitz's 2024 monograph Section 9.5 was unavailable; publisher material shows that it concerns an area inequality for parallelogram circumellipses, so overlap there remains a concrete residual risk.
