# Review of Exact aperture blow-up above the Hausdorff–Choquet rough-maximal threshold

## Correctness

PASS. The primary source proves a uniform weak-\((1,1)\) bound for each normalized geodesic-cap kernel, independent of cap radius and center. Its Proposition 4.3 gives
\[
\|u_{\theta,r}\|_{\mathcal{HC}_\alpha}
\asymp_{n,\alpha}
r^{2\alpha-(n-1)}.
\]
The source's own supercritical test with \(f=\mathbf 1_{B(0,2)}\) gives a radius-independent lower bound on the ordinary weak operator norm, while the single-cap theorem gives the matching upper bound. Dividing by the cap quasi-norm proves the two-sided aperture law exactly.

## Originality

PASS. The primary theorem states only the threshold dichotomy: a uniform \(\mathcal{HC}_\alpha\)-normalized estimate holds at and below \((n-1)/2\) and fails above it. The full text does not state the best normalized constant for a cap of radius \(r\), nor the equivalent minimum-aperture regularization law. Claim-level searches across the publication database and the broader literature using the source identifier, normalized cap kernels, aperture dependence, and supercritical Hausdorff–Choquet weak type found no implication-equivalent statement.

The closest indexed results concern unrelated scale-degeneration and maximal-operator phenomena. The main residual risk is that this short quantitative extraction may have been noted informally because its ingredients are adjacent in the source.

## Value

PASS. The source identifies a sharp exponent but leaves the supercritical side qualitative. The finding measures exactly how the theorem deteriorates when angular resolution is pushed below a scale \(r\): every excess \(2\alpha-(n-1)\) above criticality becomes the power of the weak-type constant. This converts a threshold statement into a quantitative stability/instability law useful for truncated or regularized angular families and distinguishes critical order-one behavior from supercritical power blow-up.
