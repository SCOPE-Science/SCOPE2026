# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Properness gives the \(\Delta\) lower bound, a C4 forces four colors, and an odd cycle forces three. For the matching upper bound, root the cactus block-cut tree. At an attachment vertex x, a new cycle has two incident edges and at most \(\deg(x)-2\) previously used incident colors, so at least two palette colors remain. A C4 uses those two plus two further distinct colors; any other even cycle alternates the two; an attached odd cycle has K>=3 and can be completed with a third color. Bridges take any unused incident color. Because every cycle of a cactus is wholly contained in one cycle block, no cross-block C4 exists, so the local construction is global. The frozen verifier exhaustively checks all 6074 connected labeled cacti through six vertices using the independent conflict-graph chromatic formulation; that finite census is corroborative, not the infinite proof.

## originality

PASS

The inspected B-coloring literature gives broad degeneracy/codegree bounds and high-degree outerplanar exactness, but no exact cactus classification. Ordinary cactus edge-coloring and lollipop/cactus results for other invariants are inapplicable. A directly relevant 2026 subcubic preprint was available only at abstract level; its abstract gives a universal bound and equality-at-six characterization, not the audited cactus formula, so fuller low-color content remains a residual literature risk rather than grounds for rejection.

## value

PASS

Cacti are a standard sparse graph class and B-coloring is a natural refinement of edge coloring. The theorem gives a complete exact formula, isolates the only obstructions beyond maximum degree, and sharpens the known outerplanar degree-optimal range specifically because cactus block structure prevents interactions between C4s. This is a natural family classification rather than an arbitrary finite computation.
