# Exact binary one-deletion one-substitution codes at lengths seven and eight
## Finding
Let \(DS_{1,2}(n)\) denote the maximum cardinality of a binary length-\(n\) code correcting one deletion and up to one substitution, with the error ball defined by deleting one symbol and then changing at most one surviving symbol. Then
\[
DS_{1,2}(7)=3,\qquad DS_{1,2}(8)=5.
\]
Moreover, there are exactly \(12\) optimal length-eight codes. Every optimal code contains both constant words \(0^8\) and \(1^8\). Under the symmetry group generated by reversal and bit complementation, the \(12\) optima split into exactly four orbits, of sizes \(2,2,4,4\). Canonical representatives are listed in `classification.json`. In particular, blocklength \(8\) is the minimum binary blocklength that can carry at least four messages while correcting this error pattern.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\). For a word \(x\) of length \(n\), its error ball \(B^{DS}_{1,1}(x)\) is the set of length-\(n-1\) words obtainable after one deletion and at most one substitution. A code is valid when these balls are pairwise disjoint. This is the convention used in the primary source. The claim is finite and exact only for lengths seven and eight; it does not assert a formula for general \(n\).

## Proof
Form the compatibility graph \(G_n\) whose \(2^n\) vertices are the binary words of length \(n\), joining two vertices precisely when their \(B^{DS}_{1,1}\) balls are disjoint. A valid code is exactly a clique of \(G_n\).

For \(n=7\), the three words `0010110`, `1110000`, and `1111111` have pairwise disjoint error balls, so \(DS_{1,2}(7)\ge 3\). Direct exhaustive generation of all four-cliques of \(G_7\) returns none, hence \(DS_{1,2}(7)\le 3\).

For \(n=8\), exhaustive generation of all five-cliques of \(G_8\) returns exactly \(12\). Thus a size-five code exists. For each of those \(12\) five-cliques, the intersection of the five common-neighbor sets is empty, so none extends to a six-clique. Any six-clique would contain a five-clique, hence no six-clique exists and \(DS_{1,2}(8)=5\). The same exhaustive list shows that all \(12\) optima contain `00000000` and `11111111`.

Reversal and bit complementation preserve deletion/substitution balls and therefore act on the set of optimal codes. Canonicalizing the \(12\) codes under the four transformations identity, complement, reversal, and reverse-complement yields four classes, with cardinalities \(2,2,4,4\).

## Verification
`verify.py` is a standard-library-only bitset implementation. It constructs every error ball directly from the definition, builds \(G_7\) and \(G_8\), excludes four-cliques at length seven, enumerates all five-cliques at length eight, checks that none extends to six, and checks the symmetry-orbit classification. It terminates with `VERIFY_OK`.

`independent_check.py` repeats the computation with string-valued balls, Python sets, and a separate recursive clique enumerator. It terminates with `INDEPENDENT_OK 3 5 12`. Thus the finite upper bounds are exhaustive computations, not extrapolations from samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Smagloy, Welter, Wachter-Zeh, and Yaakobi introduced the notation and channel model and derived non-asymptotic general upper bounds plus explicit constructions. Their 2020 paper defines \(B^{DS}_{t,s}(x)\) using \(t\) deletions and at most \(s\) substitutions, and denotes the largest \(q\)-ary length-\(n\) single-deletion \(s\)-substitution code by \(DS_{s,q}(n)\). Their stated general bound is far from sharp at these small binary lengths and no exact \(n=7,8\) table or classification appears in the inspected paper. Sun and Ge later improved the asymptotic redundancy for binary one-deletion up-to-one-substitution codes, again without an exact small-length classification in the inspected material.

Database and web searches for the exact expressions \(DS_{1,2}(7)\), \(DS_{1,2}(8)\), binary length-eight single-deletion single-substitution optima, and equivalent small-parameter descriptions did not return a covering result. Exact small-parameter deletion-code records found in the literature/database concerned different channels or alphabets and do not imply this claim.

## Limitations
The result is a finite exact classification. It does not supply a general formula for \(DS_{1,2}(n)\), asymptotic improvements, or nonbinary analogues. Originality is supported by targeted searches and inspection of the closest primary sources, but an unindexed small-parameter computation could still exist. The exhaustive proof depends on the bundled programs; both implementations are included and replayable.
