# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** In real \(\ell_4^2\), smoothness makes Birkhoff orthogonality equivalent to the exact support-functional equation \(a^3y_1+b^3y_2=0\). The unit orthogonal vector is therefore parametrized explicitly. Fourth-power expansion gives a scalar objective \(F(u)^2\) on the complete interval \([0,1]\), and its derivative changes sign exactly once because \(9u^4+18u^3+3u^2-2\) is strictly increasing on \((0,1)\). The critical equation simplifies the exact maximum to \(1/6-u_0^2/2\). The bundled checker verifies the stationary polynomial identity and reconstructs a maximizing witness.

Risk: the printed decimal is numerical presentation only; the proof and claim are exact and do not rely on sampling.

## Originality
**PASS.** The complete defining article was inspected. It introduces \(C_B\), proves global inequalities and endpoint examples, but does not give the exact value for \(\ell_4^2\). The closest discrepancy constant \(D(X)\) is known on two-dimensional \(\ell_p\) spaces, but only inequalities connect it to \(C_B\), so it does not imply the present value. A related 2024 skew-constant paper optimizes a different first-power norm-difference expression. Targeted searches for the invariant, space, exact decimal, aliases, and stationary quartic did not locate an equivalent formula.

Residual risk: unindexed or differently named prior literature may exist, and the original Ji–Wu article was not obtained in full text; however, the exact implication comparison is resolved because the defining Du–Li article states only inequalities between the two invariants.

## Value

An exact value and extremizer are therefore a natural benchmark for future inequalities and comparisons, not a routine renaming or arbitrary finite computation. The reduction to a one-variable algebraic extremum explains the geometry rather than merely tabulating a decimal.
