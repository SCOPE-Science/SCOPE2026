# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is a fixed finite statement. The proof gives an if-and-only-if rank criterion for exact time support \(A\) and exact Fourier zero set \(B\), reduces all support/zero-set pairs by valid independent translation and modulation symmetries, and evaluates every required Fourier rank with exact cyclotomic arithmetic. The exhaustive counts close all cases: full-rank cases fail immediately, rank-three cases fail a support-coordinate condition, and all \(136\) surviving rank-four cases fail an additional-row condition. No floating-point tolerance or finite-to-infinite extrapolation is used.

## Originality
PASS. Krahmer--Pfander--Rashkov explicitly describe this same order-\(16\) nonexistence point as numerical evidence arising from singular-value calculations on \(49{,}969{,}920\) matrices, rather than as a proof. Delvaux--Van Barel give broader rank-deficiency and monotone uncertainty results for prime-power Fourier matrices, but those do not imply nonattainability of this exact support pair. Yang et al. prove a no-hole theorem only on and above \(n_{\mathcal A}+n_{\mathcal B}=d+1\); the point \((5,9)\) at \(d=16\) lies strictly below that region. Targeted exact-pair, rank-deficiency, uncertainty-diagram, and numerical-count searches did not locate a later exact proof. Residual risk is an unindexed or differently worded finite computation.

## Value
PASS. This is not an arbitrary table entry: the originating paper singled out this exact case as computationally demanding and reported only numerical nonexistence after almost fifty million singular-value calculations. Replacing that status with an exact, tolerance-free proof and a symmetry-reduced reproducible certificate closes a concrete published gap in a classical finite Fourier uncertainty diagram.
