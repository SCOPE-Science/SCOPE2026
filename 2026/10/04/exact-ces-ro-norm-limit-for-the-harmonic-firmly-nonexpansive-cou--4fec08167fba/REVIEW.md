# Same-model review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof starts from the published exact inner-product formula and the published harmonic-mesh identities. Replacing \(\rho_i\rho_j\) by its limiting value is justified by an explicit Cesàro estimate. On the bulk square \(i,j\ge\varepsilon n\), the harmonic-number difference converges uniformly to \(\log(i/j)\); the discarded boundary strips have normalized mass at most \(2\varepsilon\). This gives the two-dimensional Riemann integral, which reduces exactly to a one-dimensional Gaussian-logarithmic integral and then to the complementary error function. No finite experiment is used to establish an infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS
The focal source proves that the harmonic Cesàro means are bounded away from zero and displays a positive lower bound, but the inspected full text does not state an exact norm limit or complementary-error-function formula. Targeted searches using the source identifier, harmonic-mesh terminology, Gaussian-kernel formulation, nonlinear mean-ergodic aliases, and exact-limit language found no covering result. Earlier nonlinear nonexpansive counterexamples concern failure of strong convergence in broader classes rather than the exact asymptotic of this harmonic Gaussian orbit.

Residual originality risk remains that the short Riemann-sum refinement may have appeared in an unindexed note or under different terminology.

## Value — PASS
The harmonic mesh is the canonical non-oscillatory Cesàro counterexample singled out by the focal paper. Determining its exact asymptotic norm replaces a qualitative positive lower envelope by a complete scalar asymptotic and separates this example sharply from the source's block construction whose Cesàro norms genuinely oscillate. The resulting constant is intrinsic to the Gaussian harmonic kernel and is available in a closed form.

## Closest literature and limitations
Bauschke–Tung supplies the orbit, exact Gaussian inner products, and the harmonic lower bound. Krengel–Lin supplies broader nonlinear mean-ergodic counterexamples but does not cover this firmly nonexpansive Hilbert-space construction or its exact norm asymptotic. The theorem is restricted to \(0<\delta\le1/8\), the parameter range in which the cited harmonic construction supplies the firmly nonexpansive mapping.
