# Exact classification of binary length-five MDS symbol-pair codes
## Finding
For binary length-five symbol-pair codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^5\) with minimum pair-distance at least \(4\), the maximum size is \(8\). There are exactly \(20\) labeled maximum codes. Every maximum is an affine \(3\)-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^5\). More precisely, the \(20\) maxima are exactly the four affine cosets of each of five linear \(3\)-subspaces, and those five direction subspaces are the five cyclic coordinate shifts of
\[
L=\{00000,00101,01011,01110,10010,10111,11001,11100\}.
\]
Under the \(320\)-element pair-metric isometry subgroup generated by dihedral symmetries of the five coordinate positions and independent coordinatewise bit complements, all \(20\) maxima form a single orbit, with stabilizer order \(16\).

## Assumptions and scope
For \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^5\), indices are read modulo \(5\), and the symbol-pair distance is
\[
d_{\mathrm p}(x,y)=\left|\{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z:(x_i,x_{i+1})\ne(y_i,y_{i+1})\}\right|.
\]
A code is admissible when every two distinct codewords have pair-distance at least \(4\). “Labeled” means that the five coordinate positions and the binary symbols are not factored out. The orbit assertion is only for the explicitly stated \(320\)-element subgroup; no assertion about the full abstract isometry group is needed.

## Proof
Construct the graph whose \(32\) vertices are the binary length-five words, joining two vertices exactly when their pair-distance is at least \(4\). Admissible codes are precisely cliques of this graph. Exhaustive Bron–Kerbosch enumeration with exact set operations produces \(118\) maximal cliques. Their largest size is \(8\), and exactly \(20\) of the maximal cliques have size \(8\). Thus the maximum code size is \(8\) and the labeled maximum-code count is \(20\).

For each maximum code \(C\), choose one word \(a\in C\) and translate by bitwise addition in \(\mathbb F_2^5\). Direct closure testing shows that \(C+a\) contains zero and is closed under addition, hence is a linear \(3\)-subspace. Therefore every maximum is an affine \(3\)-flat. Exactly five distinct direction subspaces occur, each with exactly four affine cosets among the maxima. Direct cyclic shifting shows that these five directions are precisely the five coordinate shifts of \(L\) displayed above.

Finally act on words by a dihedral symmetry of the five cyclic coordinate positions and then independently complement any subset of the five coordinates. These transformations preserve symbol-pair distance and form a subgroup of order \(10\cdot2^5=320\). Exhausting all \(320\) transformations on one maximum produces all \(20\) maxima. Its stabilizer therefore has order \(320/20=16\), which is also counted directly by the verifier.

## Verification
The standalone verifier `verify.py` reconstructs the \(32\) words, computes pair-distance from the definition, builds the compatibility graph, performs the complete maximal-clique search, checks affine closure of every maximum, checks the five cyclic directions, and exhausts all \(320\) stated isometries. Its successful replay is:

`VERIFY_OK maximum=8 labeled_maxima=20 maximal_cliques=118 affine_maxima=20 direction_subspaces=5 cosets_per_direction=4 orbit_size=20 stabilizer=16 group_size=320`

The data file `maxima.json` records all \(20\) labeled maxima. The computation is finite and exhaustive; no timeout, sampling, numerical approximation, or unproved extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Chee, Ji, Kiah, Wang, and Yin define the same symbol-pair metric, prove the symbol-pair Singleton bound, and give a linear construction that settles existence of MDS \((n,4)_q\) symbol-pair codes for all relevant \(n\) and \(q\). In particular their results imply existence at the present binary length-five parameters, but the inspected paper does not enumerate or classify all maximum codes. Ding, Ge, Zhang, Zhang, and Zhang list pair-distance \(4\) among the already-known general MDS existence families and focus their new constructions on other distances; the inspected text likewise does not give this finite extremizer census. Targeted exact-parameter and equivalent-formulation searches found no source implying the count \(20\), the universal affine-flat structure, the five-direction decomposition, or the one-orbit statement.

## Limitations
The claim concerns only binary length \(5\) and minimum pair-distance \(4\). It classifies all labeled maxima for this finite instance but does not classify larger alphabets, other lengths, or other pair-distances. The originality assessment retains the possibility of an obscure or unindexed finite census not found by the searches and source inspections performed here.
