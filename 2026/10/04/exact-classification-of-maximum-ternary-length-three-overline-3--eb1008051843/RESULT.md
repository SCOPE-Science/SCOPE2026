# Exact classification of maximum ternary length-three \(\overline{3}\)-separable codes
## Finding
For \(Q=\{0,1,2\}\), the maximum size of a length-three \(\overline{3}\)-separable code \(C\subseteq Q^3\) is \(6\). Exactly \(135\) labeled six-word codes attain the maximum. Under coordinate permutations together with independent alphabet permutations in each coordinate, these maxima form exactly two equivalence classes, of orbit sizes \(27\) and \(108\), represented by \(\{000,001,012,022,102,202\}\) and \(\{000,011,101,122,212,220\}\), with stabilizer orders \(48\) and \(12\), respectively.

The two classes have visibly different internal geometry. In the orbit of the first representative, each coordinate has sorted symbol multiplicities \((4,1,1)\), and the \(15\) unordered codeword pairs have Hamming-distance counts \((3,12,0)\) at distances \((1,2,3)\). In the second orbit, every coordinate is balanced with multiplicities \((2,2,2)\), and the corresponding distance counts are \((0,9,6)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(Q=\{0,1,2\}\). For a nonempty coalition \(A\subseteq Q^3\), define its descendant by
\[
\operatorname{desc}(A)=A(1)\times A(2)\times A(3),
\]
where \(A(j)\) is the set of symbols appearing in coordinate \(j\) among words of \(A\). A code \(C\subseteq Q^3\) is \(\overline{3}\)-separable when \(\operatorname{desc}(A)\neq\operatorname{desc}(B)\) for every two distinct nonempty subsets \(A,B\subseteq C\) satisfying \(1\le |A|,|B|\le3\).

Equivalence means the action generated by all permutations of the three coordinates and, independently in each resulting coordinate, all permutations of \(Q\). The acting group therefore has order \(6\cdot 6^3=1296\).

## Proof
Encode a descendant by the three coordinate symbol sets. For each code candidate, it is therefore enough to compare the descendant signatures of every one-, two-, and three-word coalition.

The accompanying verifier performs a complete enumeration of the \(\binom{27}{6}=296010\) six-word subsets of \(Q^3\). Exactly \(135\) satisfy the required distinct-signature condition. It independently enumerates all \(\binom{27}{7}=888030\) seven-word subsets and finds none. Because \(\overline{3}\)-separability is hereditary under taking subcodes, the absence of a seven-word code rules out every larger code. Either displayed six-word representative supplies the matching lower bound, hence the maximum is exactly \(6\).

For classification, the verifier applies all \(1296\) coordinate/symbol transformations to the complete list of \(135\) maxima. The list splits into orbits of sizes \(27\) and \(108\). Orbit-stabilizer then gives stabilizer orders \(1296/27=48\) and \(1296/108=12\). The verifier also recomputes the coordinate multiplicity and pairwise Hamming-distance profiles stated above; these profiles distinguish the two orbits without relying on their chosen representatives.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify.py classification.json` using the two accompanying files. The expected terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK maximum=6 labeled_maxima=135 orbits=2 orbit_sizes=27,108 stabilizers=48,12 checked6=296010 checked7=888030`

The verifier uses only the Python standard library. It constructs all \(27\) ternary words, compares all coalition descendants of sizes at most \(3\), exhausts every six- and seven-word subset, and traverses the full equivalence group. Thus the certificate is a finite exhaustive proof, not a sampling experiment or a timeout-based exclusion.

## Relationship to prior work
Cheng, Jiang, Li, Miao, and Tang define \(\overline{3}\)-separability for length-three codes and prove the general upper bound
\[
M(\overline{3},3,q)\le \left\lfloor\frac{3q^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
At \(q=3\) this gives the numerical ceiling \(6\). Their paper explicitly reports the exact binary case by exhaustive search, while for \(q>2\) it proceeds with the general upper bound and construction families; no exact ternary enumeration or classification of its extremizers is stated in the inspected material. The present result supplies an attaining ternary code and, more substantially, the complete labeled and symmetry classification of every attaining code.

Zhang, Jiang, and Cheng later prove that length-three \(\overline{3}\)-separable and strongly \(\overline{3}\)-separable codes are equivalent, so the classification also describes the ternary maximum objects for that equivalent length-three notion. Their inspected discussion again gives general bounds and construction methods rather than this finite classification. Earlier work on separable codes concentrates its explicit short-code classifications on \(\overline{2}\)-separability, while Blackburn's probabilistic results concern asymptotic existence.

## Limitations
The result is only for alphabet size \(3\), length \(3\), and coalition bound \(3\); it makes no claim for larger alphabets, longer lengths, or other separability parameters. The originality comparison cannot exclude an obscure unindexed finite enumeration, although targeted searches by exact parameter, descendant formulation, and symmetry terminology did not locate one. The proof does not infer an infinite pattern from the finite census.
