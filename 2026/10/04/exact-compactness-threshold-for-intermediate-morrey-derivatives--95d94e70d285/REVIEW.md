# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof was reconstructed with the exact source graph space, the finite-interval Morrey norm, the Hilbert interpolation identity, and the compact-resolvent spectral theorem. Subcritical compactness follows by choosing an intermediate exponent in the strict compactness range of arXiv:2610.00292v1 and composing with the continuous fractional-domain inclusion. Critical boundedness is the source Hilbert-scale theorem. For every fixed compact-resolvent \(B\), the explicit eigenmodes \(\beta_n^{-1}e^{i\beta_n^{1/m}t}e_n\) have constant source graph norm, a fixed positive pairwise critical distance, and diverging supercritical norms. No finite experiment is used as proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The focal full text states strict-loss same-Morrey compactness, the critical Hilbert bound, and a particular spectral sharpness model, but not the exact compact/bounded-noncompact/unbounded trichotomy for every fixed compact-resolvent operator. The companion 2026 compactness paper treats first-order Morrey evolution and a time-continuous Hilbert-triple threshold; the 2018/2019 Morrey embedding paper gives compatible strict-subcritical compactness under a different framework. Targeted searches for compact-resolvent, endpoint-noncompactness, eigenmode, and exact-trichotomy formulations found no covering result. The residual risk is an equivalent theorem hidden in older Sobolev--Lions terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The critical exponent \(1-j/m\) is the natural endpoint singled out by the focal Hilbert theorem. Showing that the same exponent is universally noncompact for every infinite-dimensional compact-resolvent Hilbert scale, while every strict subcritical target is compact and every supercritical target is unbounded, gives a complete and reusable structural boundary.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is arXiv:2610.00292v1 itself, arXiv:2609.29152v1, and DOI:10.1007/s13373-018-0129-x. The result is limited to positive self-adjoint compact-resolvent operators on complex Hilbert space, finite intervals, and fractional-domain exponents in \([0,1]\). It does not cover non-self-adjoint scales or pointwise-in-time compactness.
