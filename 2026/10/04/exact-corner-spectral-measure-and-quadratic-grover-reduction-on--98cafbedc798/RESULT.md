# Exact corner spectral measure and quadratic Grover reduction on weighted \(P_4\) Cartesian powers
## Finding
Consider the four-vertex reversible probabilistic Laplacian used by Mograby, Balu, Okoudjou and Teplyaev,
\[
\Delta_p=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&0&0\\
p-1&1&-p&0\\
0&-p&1&p-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<p<1,
\]
with vertex measure
\[
\mu_p=\left(1,\frac{1}{1-p},\frac{1}{1-p},1\right).
\]
For the normalized \(d\)-fold Cartesian sum
\[
\Delta_{p,d}=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d I^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\Delta_p\otimes I^{\otimes(d-j)}
\]
and the corner \(w=(0,\ldots,0)\), the spectral measure of the normalized corner state \(e_w\) is exactly the law of
\[
\frac{X_1+\cdots+X_d}{d},
\]
where the \(X_j\) are independent and
\[
\Pr(X=0)=\Pr(X=2)=a_p:=\frac{1-p}{2(2-p)},\qquad
\Pr(X=p)=\Pr(X=2-p)=b_p:=\frac{1}{2(2-p)}.
\]
Equivalently, writing
\[
\mathcal S_d=\{-d,-d+2,\ldots,d\},
\]
the corner-supported eigenvalues belong to
\[
\lambda_{S,T}=1+\frac{pS+(2-p)T}{2d},\qquad S,T\in\mathcal S_d,
\]
with weights obtained by collecting equal eigenvalues from
\[
W_{S,T}=[x^S y^T]\left(a_p(xy+x^{-1}y^{-1})+b_p(xy^{-1}+x^{-1}y)\right)^d.
\]
Therefore the \(\Delta_{p,d}\)-cyclic subspace generated by \(e_w\) has dimension at most \((d+1)^2\). If \(p\) is irrational, all \((d+1)^2\) displayed eigenvalues are distinct, so equality holds. In the homogeneous case \(p=1/2\), the dimension is exactly \(4d+1\) for every \(d\ge2\); in particular, the source's \(d=5\) corner-target problem has an exact search-active dimension \(21\), rather than the ambient dimension \(4^5=1024\).

Let
\[
H_\gamma=\gamma\Delta_{p,d}-|e_w\rangle\langle e_w|.
\]
The same cyclic subspace is invariant under \(H_\gamma\) and contains the normalized constant ground state \(s\). Thus the complete corner-target search amplitude
\[
\langle e_w,e^{-itH_\gamma}s\rangle
\]
is reproduced exactly by a matrix of dimension at most \((d+1)^2\). If \(\Lambda\) is the set of distinct corner-supported eigenvalues and \(W_\lambda\) denotes the corresponding aggregated spectral weight, an orthonormal cyclic basis gives the explicit reduced Hamiltonian
\[
H_\gamma^{\mathrm{red}}=\gamma\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}-qq^*,
\qquad q_\lambda=\sqrt{W_\lambda},
\]
with initial state the basis vector corresponding to \(\lambda=0\). The diagonal Green function is consequently
\[
G_\gamma(z,w,w)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{W_\lambda}{\gamma\lambda-z}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The parameter satisfies \(0<p<1\), and \(d\ge1\) is an integer. The Hilbert space uses the reversible product measure induced by \(\mu_p\). The target is a corner vertex, so \(\mu_p(0)^d=1\) and \(e_w=\delta_w\). The Cartesian-product Laplacian is normalized by \(1/d\), as in the motivating construction. The oracle is the rank-one projector onto \(e_w\).

The result is an exact structural reduction. It does not assert an optimal value of \(\gamma\), an optimal hitting time, or asymptotic Grover optimality. For rational \(p\), arithmetic collisions can reduce the active dimension below \((d+1)^2\); no general closed formula for every rational collision pattern is claimed.

## Proof
The source matrix has four simple eigenvalues
\[
0,\qquad p,\qquad 2-p,\qquad 2.
\]
Direct substitution gives corresponding eigenfunctions
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=(1,1,1,1),\\
f_p&=(-1,p-1,1-p,1),\\
f_{2-p}&=(1,p-1,p-1,1),\\
f_2&=(-1,1,-1,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Using the weighted inner product determined by \(\mu_p\), their squared norms are
\[
\|f_0\|^2=\|f_2\|^2=\frac{2(2-p)}{1-p},\qquad
\|f_p\|^2=\|f_{2-p}\|^2=2(2-p).
\]
Because the corner state is \(e_0=\delta_0\), its squared overlap with a normalized eigenfunction \(f\) is \(|f(0)|^2/\|f\|^2\). Hence the four corner spectral weights are exactly \(a_p,b_p,b_p,a_p\). This proves the one-coordinate four-atom measure.

The operators acting in different tensor coordinates commute and have tensor-product eigenvectors. Therefore the spectrum seen by \(e_w=e_0^{\otimes d}\) is the normalized additive convolution of the one-coordinate corner measure, proving the independent-sum representation. Each one-coordinate eigenvalue can be written
\[
X=1+\frac{p\sigma+(2-p)\tau}{2},\qquad (\sigma,\tau)\in\{-1,1\}^2,
\]
where equal signs have weight \(a_p\) and opposite signs have weight \(b_p\). Summing the signs over \(d\) coordinates gives the displayed \(\lambda_{S,T}\) formula and generating function. Since \(S\) and \(T\) each have \(d+1\) possible values, there are at most \((d+1)^2\) distinct corner-supported eigenvalues.

If two pairs \((S,T)\) and \((S',T')\) yield the same eigenvalue, then
\[
p\bigl((S-S')-(T-T')\bigr)+2(T-T')=0.
\]
For irrational \(p\), this forces \(T=T'\) and then \(S=S'\), proving the exact \((d+1)^2\) count. When \(p=1/2\), write \(S=-d+2i\) and \(T=-d+2j\) with \(0\le i,j\le d\). Then
\[
\lambda_{S,T}=\frac{i+3j}{2d}.
\]
For \(d\ge2\), the integer intervals \([3j,3j+d]\), \(0\le j\le d\), join without gaps, so \(i+3j\) runs through every integer from \(0\) to \(4d\). The homogeneous support therefore has exactly \(4d+1\) points.

For a finite-dimensional self-adjoint operator, the cyclic subspace generated by \(e_w\) is the orthogonal span of the nonzero spectral projections \(P_\lambda e_w\). Its dimension is therefore exactly the number of distinct eigenvalues in the corner spectral support. Define
\[
v_\lambda=\frac{P_\lambda e_w}{\sqrt{W_\lambda}}.
\]
In this orthonormal basis, \(\Delta_{p,d}\) is diagonal and
\[
e_w=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sqrt{W_\lambda}\,v_\lambda.
\]
Consequently the rank-one oracle also preserves the cyclic subspace and has matrix \(qq^*\), which proves the formula for \(H_\gamma^{\mathrm{red}}\).

Finally, the zero atom has weight
\[
W_0=a_p^d=\left(2+\frac{2}{1-p}\right)^{-d}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(G_d)}.
\]
The normalized constant ground state satisfies \(|\langle s,e_w\rangle|^2=1/\operatorname{vol}(G_d)=W_0\), so the normalized zero spectral projection of \(e_w\) is exactly \(s\). Thus \(s\) lies in the same invariant cyclic subspace, proving exact equality of the full and reduced search amplitudes. The Green-function expression follows by spectral resolution.

## Relationship to prior work
Mograby, Balu, Okoudjou and Teplyaev define the four-state probabilistic Laplacian, its reversible measure, the normalized Cartesian-product Laplacian, the rank-one oracle Hamiltonian, and the Green-function representation. They then study the \(d=5\), \(1024\)-vertex corner-target problem numerically for several values of \(p\). Their paper does not state the four-atom corner spectral measure, the \((d+1)^2\) cyclic-dimension bound, the exact \(4d+1\) homogeneous count, or the reduced rank-one Hamiltonian above.

The authors' earlier spectral-decimation paper studies substitution graphs and piecewise centrosymmetric Jacobi operators; its full-text construction is based on edge substitution and Schur complements rather than Cartesian powers with a rank-one search oracle. Chan, Godsil, Tamon and Xie analyze spatial search on undirected distance-regular families, including Hamming graphs and the binary cube, using normalized adjacency spectra and spectral-gap criteria. That work covers the standard Hamming setting but not this reversible one-parameter four-state path product, its corner weights, or the exact cyclic reduction stated here.

A generic Kronecker-sum spectral theorem supplies the tensor-product eigenvectors used in the proof, and generic cyclic-subspace theory supplies the invariant-space language. The new content is their exact specialization to the motivating weighted \(P_4\) family, including the explicit corner measure, arithmetic support count, and exact search-active reduction.

## Limitations
The originality search cannot exclude an equivalent result stated in generic tensor-product Krylov, association-scheme, or orthogonal-polynomial terminology. The strongest nearby search literature inspected treats ordinary Hamming graphs or general rank-one spatial-search criteria, not the weighted family here. No assertion is made about computational complexity once an optimal \(\gamma\) or time search is included; the proved reduction concerns the exact linear-algebraic state space and Green function.
