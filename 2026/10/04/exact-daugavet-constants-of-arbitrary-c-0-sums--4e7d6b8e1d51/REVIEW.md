# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The theorem is reconstructed from the slice-radius form of the Daugavet constant. For the lower bound, an arbitrary dual functional in the \(\ell_1\)-sum is approximated on a finite coordinate set; this avoids the invalid step of trying to norm infinitely many coordinates by unit vectors, which would generally leave the \(c_0\)-sum. The selected coordinate is forced to realize its component slice radius, and the case of a zero selected dual coordinate is handled by an opposite unit vector. For the upper bound, a finite convex combination of component slice functionals forces all selected coordinates simultaneously, and the \(c_0\)-tail is controlled only after establishing the necessary floor \(M\ge1\) in the infinite-index case.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source is Choi--Jung, arXiv:2307.10647. It defines the quantitative invariant, computes scalar \(\ell_\infty^N\) and scalar \(c_0\) cases, and proves one-sided stability estimates for absolute sums. The inspected full text does not state or imply the exact componentwise formula for arbitrary Banach summands in a finite \(\ell_\infty\)-sum or arbitrary \(c_0\)-sum. Targeted semantic searches for aliases, componentwise maxima, direct-sum stability, and slice-radius formulations likewise produced no covering record. The remaining risk is an equivalent statement under terminology not reached by the searches.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives a natural exact permanence law for a recently introduced quantitative diametral invariant. It is not a scalar recomputation: arbitrary summands are allowed, and an arbitrary index family is handled in one formula. It upgrades a lower-stability phenomenon to equality and explains, as special cases, two previously separate scalar computations. The finite simultaneous-slice mechanism and the \(c_0\)-tail mechanism are reusable for quantitative Banach-space geometry.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is G. Choi and M. Jung, *The Daugavet and Delta-constants of points in Banach spaces*, arXiv:2307.10647v1 (first public 2023-07-20), especially Definition 2.1, the scalar \(\ell_\infty^N\) and \(c_0\) computations in Section 3, and the absolute-sum stability results in Section 4. The result here is restricted to real spaces and the Daugavet constant; no \(\Delta\)-constant or infinite \(\ell_\infty\)-product analogue is asserted.
