# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The upper bound follows from two adjacent grid constraints whose second-harmonic coefficients have opposite signs; a positive weighted sum cancels the free coefficient. The proposed extremizer factors into two adjacent cosine roots, so its sign on the complete cyclic grid is immediate. The boundary case where the critical root itself is sampled is treated separately. The equality criterion against the continuous value follows from an exact factorization.

The accompanying finite checker is corroborative only and agrees with the symbolic theorem.

## Originality
PASS. Kolountzakis–Révész define the same fixed-support discretized problem. Ivanov–Ivanov (2010) formulate the equivalent second discrete Fejér problem and state that, at that time, it had been solved only for the highest coefficient; \((
u,p)=(1,3)\) is not of that form. Ivanov (2018) later proves selected new cases and, after normalization, covers exactly the present moduli \(N=6,7,9\) among the inspected theorems. Those special cases agree with the formula here, but the inspected 2018 results do not give \(\lambda(1,3,N)\) for arbitrary \(N\). Targeted searches for the fixed support, standard notation, and closed form found no all-modulus covering statement.

Residual risk: an older or poorly indexed finite-dimensional extremal-polynomial source may contain the same all-\(N\) adjacent-root formula in different notation.

## Value
PASS. Degree two is the first finite-grid Carathéodory–Fejér problem in which a second harmonic can trade against the optimized first coefficient. The result completely resolves that natural family, quantifies the exact sampling penalty relative to the classical continuous value, and identifies the sharp divisibility condition for no penalty.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kolountzakis–Révész formulate the discretized problem; Ivanov–Ivanov identify the equivalent second discrete Fejér problem; Ivanov (2018) computes selected parameter regimes including N=6,7,9; Krenedits–Révész give the general LCA formulation. The result here is restricted to real even degree-two polynomials and does not address arbitrary sparse supports or higher degrees.
