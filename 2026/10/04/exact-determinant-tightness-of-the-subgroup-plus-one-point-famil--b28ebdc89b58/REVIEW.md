# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof first shows that invertibility forces exactly one repeated restriction class and no missing class. A column subtraction then reduces the determinant to a single root-of-unity difference times the full \(\mathbb Z_m\) Fourier determinant, whose absolute value is \(m^{m/2}\). Maximizing the chord length among \(m\)-th roots gives the parity-dependent closed form. The normalized asymptotic follows from an explicit logarithmic expression.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked material. The primary paper studies the same subgroup-plus-one-point family but uses it only to force \(\rho(E_m)\to\infty\); it does not state the determinant spectrum, the exact value of \(D(E_m)\), or the asymptotic separation \(\widetilde D(E_m)\to1\) while \(\rho(E_m)\to\infty\). Exact-form, alias, parameter, and implication searches did not find a covering result.

## Value
PASS. The paper introduces several normalized tightness quantities as quantitative proxies for spectrality. This family shows a sharp asymptotic mismatch between two of them: the normalized determinant becomes spectrally optimal while the Riesz ratio becomes arbitrarily bad. That is a structural boundary of the framework rather than an isolated numerical computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ferguson--Mayeli--Sothanaphan, arXiv:1904.04487. The result is restricted to their subgroup-plus-one-point family and leaves the exact Riesz ratio open.
