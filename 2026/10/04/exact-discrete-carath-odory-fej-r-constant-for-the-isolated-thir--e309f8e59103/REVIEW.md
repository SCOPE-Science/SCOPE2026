# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof gives an explicit feasible factorization and a matching two-point dual certificate. The separate contact case \(12\mid N\) is handled directly, and the inequalities ensuring the third factor has fixed sign are checked for \(N=6\), \(N=7\), and \(N\ge8\). The finite verifier is corroborative rather than a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The 2003 source defines the discretized support-restricted extremal problem but does not supply the all-mesh \(H=\{3\}\) formula in the inspected text. The 2015 source gives general structural equivalences and convergence results, while the 2018 discrete Fejér problem allows a broader set of free harmonics. published-finding corpus searches using the exact support, third-harmonic, finite-grid, and Chebyshev formulations did not return a statement implying this formula. The closest own prior result supplied only the exact no-loss divisibility criterion, not the off-contact optimum.

## Value
PASS. The formula turns a canonical discretized Carathéodory–Fejér optimization from a mesh-by-mesh linear program into an explicit two-contact law, with an extremizer and a dual certificate. It quantifies every finite-grid instance rather than giving a numerical table or a single exceptional modulus.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inspected literature is Kolountzakis–Révész for the exact problem formulation, Krenedits–Révész for the general group-theoretic reduction, and Ivanov for a related but broader discrete Fejér class. Residual risk remains that an equivalent singleton-harmonic formula occurs in older literature under different indexing or quadrature terminology.
