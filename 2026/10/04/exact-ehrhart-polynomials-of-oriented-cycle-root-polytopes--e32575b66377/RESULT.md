# Exact Ehrhart polynomials of oriented cycle root polytopes
## Finding
Let \(C_m\) be a cycle on \(m\ge 3\) vertices with an arbitrary orientation. Fix either cyclic traversal, let \(p\) be the number of edges directed with the traversal and \(q=m-p\) the number directed against it, and put
\[
a=\min(p,q),\qquad b=\max(p,q).
\]
For an oriented edge \(\overrightarrow{th}\), write \(x_e=\mathbf e_h-\mathbf e_t\), and define
\[
Q_C=\operatorname{conv}\{x_e:e\in E(C)\},\qquad
\widetilde Q_C=\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup Q_C).
\]
Then
\[
h^*_{\widetilde Q_C}(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^{b-1}.
\]
For the non-extended root polytope,
\[
h^*_{Q_C}(t)=
\begin{cases}
1+t+\cdots+t^{a-1},&p=q=a,\\
1+t^{a+1}+t^{a+2}+\cdots+t^{b-1},&p\ne q,
\end{cases}
\]
where the second displayed sum is empty when \(b-a=1\). In particular, for \(p\ne q\), \(Q_C\) is an \((m-1)\)-dimensional lattice simplex of normalized volume
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb Z}(Q_C)=|p-q|,
\]
and therefore it is unimodular exactly when \(|p-q|=1\). The formulas depend only on the orientation imbalance, not on the cyclic placement of the forward and backward edges.

Equivalently, the Ehrhart series are obtained by dividing the displayed \(h^*\)-polynomials by \((1-t)^{d+1}\), where \(d=m-1\) except that \(d=m-2\) for \(Q_C\) in the semi-balanced case.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycle is simple and has at least three vertices. The ambient lattice for a full-dimensional root polytope is the type-\(A\) root lattice
\[
A_{m-1}=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^m:\sum_i z_i=0\right\}.
\]
In the semi-balanced case, normalized volume and unimodularity are taken in the affine lattice induced on the \((m-2)\)-dimensional affine span, as in the standard root-polytope convention. Reversing the chosen cyclic traversal interchanges \(p\) and \(q\), so \(a\) and \(b\) are intrinsic.

## Proof
Choose cyclic edge-difference vectors \(d_i\) so that \(\sum_i d_i=0\). Write \(s_i\in\{1,-1\}\) for whether the actual edge orientation agrees with the chosen traversal. Then \(v_i=s_i d_i\) are the vertices of \(Q_C\) and
\[
\sum_i s_i v_i=0.
\]
The cycle incidence matrix has one primitive integral dependence, so this is the only linear dependence among the \(v_i\), up to scale.

Assume first that \(p\ne q\), reverse the traversal if necessary so that \(p=b>q=a\), and put \(r=b-a>0\). Since the coefficients in the displayed dependence have sum \(r\ne0\), the \(m\) vertices \(v_i\) are affinely independent; hence \(Q_C\) is an \((m-1)\)-simplex. Homogenize with \(w_i=(v_i,1)\) and \(z=(0,1)\) in \(A_{m-1}\times\mathbb Z\). The vectors \(v_i\) span \(A_{m-1}\) integrally because deleting any cycle edge leaves a tree incidence matrix, hence a lattice basis. Therefore \(A_{m-1}\times\mathbb Z\) is generated by the \(w_i\) together with \(z\). Moreover,
\[
\sum_{s_i=1}w_i-\sum_{s_i=-1}w_i=r z.
\]
Thus the quotient by the lattice generated by the \(w_i\) is cyclic, generated by the class of \(z\), with order dividing \(r\). If \(kz=\sum_i c_iw_i\), then projection to \(A_{m-1}\) gives \(\sum_i c_iv_i=0\), so primitivity of the cycle relation forces \(c_i=\ell s_i\) for some integer \(\ell\). Comparing last coordinates gives \(k=\ell r\). Hence the quotient has order exactly \(r\), proving \(\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb Z}(Q_C)=r\).

The half-open fundamental parallelepiped of the cone generated by the \(w_i\) has one lattice representative for each class \(kz\), \(0\le k<r\). For \(1\le k<r\), the representative with coefficients in \([0,1)\) is
\[
\sum_{s_i=1}\frac{k}{r}w_i+
\sum_{s_i=-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{r}\right)w_i.
\]
Its height is
\[
b\frac{k}{r}+a\left(1-\frac{k}{r}\right)=a+k.
\]
The zero class has height \(0\). The fundamental-parallelepiped interpretation of the \(h^*\)-polynomial of a lattice simplex therefore gives
\[
h^*_{Q_C}(t)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}t^{a+k}
=1+t^{a+1}+\cdots+t^{b-1}.
\]

For \(\widetilde Q_C\), the affine circuit on the points \(0,v_1,\ldots,v_m\) has positive side consisting of the \(b\) edges pointing with the majority direction. The standard circuit triangulation has one maximal simplex for each such edge, obtained by omitting that edge. Every maximal simplex is \(\operatorname{conv}(0,Q_T)\) for a spanning tree \(T\) of the cycle, and is unimodular. Its abstract simplicial complex is the join of the boundary of a \((b-1)\)-simplex with a simplex. Its \(h\)-polynomial is therefore
\[
1+t+\cdots+t^{b-1},
\]
and unimodularity identifies this with \(h^*_{\widetilde Q_C}(t)\).

Finally suppose \(p=q=a\). The relation \(\sum_{s_i=1}v_i=\sum_{s_i=-1}v_i\) is affine, so \(Q_C\) has dimension \(m-2\). The same circuit triangulation, now omitting one edge from either fixed side of size \(a\), consists of unimodular spanning-tree simplices. Its complex is the join of a simplex with the boundary of an \((a-1)\)-simplex, so
\[
h^*_{Q_C}(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^{a-1}.
\]
A compatible layering places \(Q_C\) in an affine hyperplane not containing the origin, so \(\widetilde Q_C\) is a lattice pyramid over \(Q_C\) and has the same \(h^*\)-polynomial. This is the claimed formula because \(a=b\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. For every orientation of every cycle with \(3\le m\le8\), it checks the primitive cycle relation, exact normalized determinants for all spanning-tree simplices, the determinant \(|p-q|\) for each un-semibalanced root simplex, the fundamental-parallelepiped heights, and the \(h\)-polynomial of the circuit triangulation from its face set. These finite checks are cross-checks only; the proof above establishes the formulas for every \(m\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Kálmán and Tóthmérész introduced the directed root-polytope framework and proved the dimension dichotomy between semi-balanced and other orientations. Their 2021 preprint, later published in *Mathematika*, develops \(h^*\)-theory for semi-balanced root polytopes and notes that an un-semibalanced cycle gives a simplex. The same paper states without a qualification that such a simplex fails to be unimodular. The exact determinant computation above refines that boundary: the normalized volume is \(|p-q|\), so imbalance-one orientations are unimodular.

Their later work on graph-polytope \(h^*\)-vectors treats \(Q_G\) for semi-balanced digraphs. Their 2023 preprint on interior polynomials treats the extended root polytope \(\widetilde Q_G\) for arbitrary digraphs and determines its degree and leading coefficient from dijoins. For a cycle, that degree theorem is consistent with the degree \(b-1\) above, but it does not state the complete coefficient sequence, nor the non-extended un-semibalanced simplex formula. Searches for the exact cycle formula, its orientation-imbalance form, and the un-semibalanced cycle volume found no statement that implies these formulas among the directly relevant sources inspected.

## Limitations
This result is only for a single cycle. It does not give an \(h^*\)-formula for arbitrary unicyclic digraphs or general non-semi-balanced digraphs. The finite verifier does not substitute for the all-dimensions proof. A residual literature risk remains that the same formulas may be derivable as an unstated special case of general lattice-circuit theory outside the graph-root-polytope literature; the targeted searches and the directly relevant full texts inspected did not locate such a statement.
