# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The equilibrium equations were reconstructed separately in the two sign half-spaces. The origin argument does not apply a smooth theorem to a nonsmooth point: it uses the smooth left extension, whose characteristic polynomial has a positive real root for every \(c>0\), and the corresponding negative-side local unstable-manifold branch is also a trajectory of the original absolute-value flow. The nonzero-equilibrium polynomial and the Routh-Hurwitz boundary were independently simplified and checked by exact arithmetic.

## Originality
PASS. The primary accepted manuscript was inspected at the equation and bifurcation-calculation level. It provides the same vector field but not the two-sided origin analysis or the exact negative-branch threshold, and its smooth-Hopf interpretation at the origin is incompatible with the left-extension instability. Searches by title, DOI, full equation, aliases, threshold forms, broader jerk bifurcation terminology, and semantically close published findings located no stronger statement covering this claim. The closest later smooth-jerk literature treats different systems.

## Value
PASS. The correction changes the qualitative bifurcation geometry: the origin cannot undergo the reported classical smooth Hopf transition, positive nonzero equilibria disappear already at \(c=1/100\), and the actual smooth nonzero spectral crossing lies on the negative branch at an exact algebraic parameter. This is a structural boundary result rather than a cosmetic numerical adjustment.

## Closest literature and limitations
The introducing article is the closest source and is compared directly. A later general paper on double-zero bifurcations of jerk systems provides background but does not cover this nonsmooth model. The result is restricted to \(a=3/10\), \(b=1/5\), \(d=1/10\); it proves a Hopf-type spectral crossing at the negative equilibrium but does not claim a nondegenerate Hopf periodic-orbit bifurcation. Literature search cannot exclude an unindexed or inaccessible prior correction.
