PASS. Every Euclidean isomorphism is represented by a positive-definite quadratic pullback. Averaging over the two coordinate-sign symmetries keeps the same two-sided comparison with the norm and removes the cross term, so the global Banach--Mazur problem reduces without loss to \(q_t(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2+t x_2^2\). The exact directional ratio has three exhaustive parameter regimes; their minimum is \(2-\lambda^{-2}\) at \(t=\lambda^{-2}\). The explicit unit-vector pair \((\lambda^{-1},\pm\sqrt{1-\lambda^{-2}})\) attains the same von Neumann--Jordan ratio, and Euclidean parallelogram comparison gives the matching upper bound.
