# Exact Euclidean Banach--Mazur profile of the quartic polynomial-index-zero plane family
## Finding
For every \(\beta\in[0,3]\), let \(X_\beta=(\mathbb R^2,N_\beta)\) with \(N_\beta(x,y)=(x^4+2\beta x^2y^2+y^4)^{1/4}\). Then the exact Euclidean Banach--Mazur distance is \[d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_\beta,\ell_2^2)=\begin{cases}(2/(1+\beta))^{1/4},&0\le\beta\le1,\\((1+\beta)/2)^{1/4},&1\le\beta\le3.\end{cases}\] Equivalently, \(d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_\beta,\ell_2^2)^4=\max\{2/(1+\beta),(1+\beta)/2\}\), and the standard Euclidean norm is an optimal Euclidean pullback; any positive rescaling has the same distortion. The defining norm is invariant under the symmetries of \(N_\beta\).

The family is the complete family identified by García, Grecu, Maestre, Martín and Merí among normalized absolute symmetric real two-dimensional norms with cubic polynomial numerical index zero. Thus the formula gives the Euclidean distortion throughout that classified family, not only at isolated examples.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix \(\beta\in[0,3]\) and define
\[
N_\beta(x,y)=\left(x^4+2\beta x^2y^2+y^4\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The cited source proves that these and only these parameters define the normalized absolute symmetric norms occurring in its classification theorem. The claim is over the real field and concerns Banach--Mazur distance to the real Euclidean plane.

For a positive-definite quadratic form \(q\), write \(Q(z)=q(z)^{1/2}\). Its distortion relative to \(N_\beta\) is
\[
D(q)=\left(\frac{\sup_{z\ne0}q(z)/N_\beta(z)^2}{\inf_{z\ne0}q(z)/N_\beta(z)^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The Banach--Mazur distance is the infimum of \(D(q)\) over positive-definite quadratic forms.

## Proof
The norm \(N_\beta\) is invariant under the eight transformations generated by independent coordinate sign changes and interchange of the two coordinates. Let \(G\) denote this finite isometry group. Given any positive-definite quadratic form \(q\), put
\[
m=\inf_{z\ne0}\frac{q(z)}{N_\beta(z)^2},\qquad
M=\sup_{z\ne0}\frac{q(z)}{N_\beta(z)^2}.
\]
Then \(mN_\beta(z)^2\le q(z)\le MN_\beta(z)^2\) for every \(z\). Averaging over \(G\) gives
\[
\bar q(z)=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}q(gz),
\]
and the same two inequalities hold for \(\bar q\), because every \(g\) preserves \(N_\beta\). Invariance under the coordinate sign changes kills the mixed term of \(\bar q\), and invariance under coordinate interchange makes the two diagonal coefficients equal. Hence \(\bar q(z)=c(x^2+y^2)\) for some \(c>0\). Therefore the Euclidean quadratic form has distortion no larger than that of any \(q\), so it is globally optimal.

It remains to compute its distortion. On the Euclidean unit circle \(x^2+y^2=1\), set \(s=x^2y^2\). Then \(0\le s\le1/4\) and
\[
N_\beta(x,y)^4=x^4+y^4+2\beta x^2y^2
=1+2(\beta-1)s.
\]
If \(0\le\beta\le1\), this expression decreases with \(s\). Its maximum is \(1\) at the coordinate axes and its minimum is \((1+\beta)/2\) at the diagonals. Consequently
\[
D(x^2+y^2)=\frac{1}{((1+\beta)/2)^{1/4}
=\left(\frac2{1+\beta}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
If \(1\le\beta\le3\), the monotonicity reverses: the minimum of \(N_\beta\) on the Euclidean circle is \(1\) at the axes and the maximum is \(((1+\beta)/2)^{1/4}\) at the diagonals. Thus
\[
D(x^2+y^2)=\left(\frac{1+\beta}{2}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
The two formulas agree at \(\beta=1\), completing the proof.

## Verification
The group-averaging argument is exact and does not depend on a numerical search. A supplementary exact-arithmetic script checks the branch values at representative rational parameters and verifies the source's parameter involution
\[
\gamma=\frac{3-\beta}{1+\beta}
\]
for \(1\le\beta\le3\): the two branches satisfy \(2/(1+\gamma)=(1+\beta)/2\). The replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The endpoint checks are also exact: \(\beta=0\) is \(\ell_4^2\) and gives \(d_{\mathrm{BM}}=2^{1/4}\); \(\beta=1\) is Euclidean and gives distance \(1\); \(\beta=3\) is linearly equivalent, via the source's coordinate change, to the \(\beta=0\) endpoint and again gives \(2^{1/4}\).

## Relationship to prior work
García, Grecu, Maestre, Martín and Merí prove that every normalized absolute symmetric real two-dimensional norm with cubic polynomial numerical index zero has the displayed quartic form for a unique parameter in the stated classification range, and Proposition 3.1 establishes exactly the norm range \(0\le\beta\le3\). Their full text also records the linear change of variables sending the branch \(1\le\beta\le3\) to the branch \(0\le\gamma\le1\), where \(\gamma=(3-\beta)/(1+\beta)\). The inspected paper contains no Banach--Mazur discussion and no Euclidean-distortion formula.

Searches for the quartic formula together with Banach--Mazur distance, Euclidean distortion, and polynomial numerical index did not locate an equivalent exact profile. The parameter involution is not competing coverage; it is consistent with the formula because the two branch values transform identically.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to this real two-dimensional quartic family. It does not classify all optimal linear maps, and it does not address nonsymmetric examples with cubic polynomial numerical index zero. Search failure is not a proof of novelty; an older equivalent computation could exist under planar convex-body or extremal-ellipse terminology, although no specific unresolved source was identified.
