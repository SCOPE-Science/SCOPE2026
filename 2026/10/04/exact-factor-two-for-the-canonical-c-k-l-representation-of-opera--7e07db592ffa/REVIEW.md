# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The functional norm formula reduces the upper estimate to \(\sum_i r_i\le2\), where \(r_i=\max(|x_i^*(m)|,|x_i^*(M)|)\). The separation lemma gives \(q(m)=0\), \(q(M)=1\), and \(0\le q\le1\); the translated lattice homomorphism \(p=q-1\) satisfies \(|q(x)|+|p(x)|=1\) identically, so \((q,p)\) is admissible and forces the canonical strong unit to have norm at least \(2\). The preceding upper bound forces equality. A separate generator witness gives \(\|\Phi_L\|=1\).

## Originality
PASS, with disclosed residual risk. The 2022 theorem proves the canonical \(C(K_L)\) representation with distortion at most \(2\), deriving it from \(\|u\|\le2\); the inspected proof does not establish equality. The 2026 paper recalls that representation, gives the free norm and a suitable separation lemma, but its inspected strong-unit section does not state the exact norm of \(u\) or sharpness of the canonical factor. Semantic searches for exact canonical distortion, exact strong-unit norm, and sharpness returned no matching published-finding corpus record. Because the result is a short sharpening of a known bound, an unindexed equivalent observation remains a substantive residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The constant \(2\) is the quantitative content of the standard canonical representation theorem. Showing that it is universally attained for every nontrivial bounded distributive lattice converts a uniform estimate into an exact invariant of the canonical map and supplies a simple, reusable two-functional extremal certificate. This is a natural optimality question rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
