# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof classifies every possible idempotent factor by its image line and every nilpotent completion by a two-equation trace/determinant test. Boundary cases \(A=0\), \(E=0\), \(E=I\), nonzero nilpotent targets, nonnilpotent rank-one targets, and invertible targets are all handled separately. The total fiber mass agrees with the independently counted number of idempotent–nilpotent pairs. Exact exhaustive replay over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_4\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_7\) agrees with the formulas.

## Originality
PASS. The closest recent paper, arXiv:2608.20934v1, determines the image set of the IN/NI maps and its total cardinality over finite local principal rings, but its inspected statements do not count ordered factorizations above a fixed matrix. The 2024 Călugăreanu–Pop paper gives existence criteria over Prüfer and related domains, again without fiber enumeration. published-finding corpus and web searches for the exact fiber problem and formulas returned no implication-equivalent statement. Residual risk remains for unindexed literature.

## Value
PASS. Fiber cardinality is the natural next invariant after the recent paper's image-cardinality theorem. The answer is structurally nonuniform—zero, nonzero nilpotent, and nonnilpotent rank-one targets have different multiplicities—and it explains exactly how the total count decomposes. This is a complete classification for a natural finite-field specialization, not a parameter spot check.

## Closest literature and limitations
The result is limited to \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\). The dimension-two proof uses the projective-line geometry of rank-one idempotents and nilpotents, so no higher-dimensional formula is asserted. The searches cannot establish absolute novelty outside the inspected and indexed literature.
