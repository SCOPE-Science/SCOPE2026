# Exact finite-sample size inflation for a studentized Fisher randomization test

## Finding

Consider a completely randomized treatment-control experiment with \(N=6\) units and exactly \(N_1=N_0=3\) units assigned to treatment and control. Take
\[
Y(0)=(-0.996,-1.004,0.019,-0.997,-0.016,-0.989)
\]
and
\[
Y(1)=(1.044,1.026,2.000,0.967,-0.033,-8.987).
\]
The unit-level effects are
\[
(2.040,2.030,1.981,1.964,-0.017,-7.998),
\]
whose sum is exactly \(0\). Thus the weak null of zero finite-population average treatment effect holds, while the sharp null does not.

For each realized assignment, use the Fisher randomization test obtained by imputing the sharp null and comparing the studentized difference in means
\[
T=\frac{|\widehat\tau|}{\sqrt{S_1^2/3+S_0^2/3}}.
\]
With the inclusive randomization tail, the exact p-value distribution over the \(\binom{6}{3}=20\) possible realized assignments is
\[
\Pr(P=1/10)=7/20,\quad
\Pr(P=1/5)=3/20,\quad
\Pr(P=9/10)=3/20,\quad
\Pr(P=1)=7/20.
\]
Consequently, at nominal level \(\alpha=0.1\), the exact type-I error under this weak null is
\[
\Pr(P\le 0.1)=\frac{7}{20}=0.35.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The claim concerns a finite population of six fixed units under complete randomization with three treated units. Sample variances use denominator \(N_j-1=2\). For a realized assignment \(z\), the observed response vector is fixed and the Fisher reference distribution is generated by the sharp-null imputation, so every one of the twenty treatment allocations is applied to that same observed vector. The p-value is the fraction of reference allocations whose statistic is at least the observed statistic. The result is an exact finite-population calculation; it makes no superpopulation or asymptotic claim.

## Proof

The average treatment effect is zero because the six displayed unit effects sum to zero. Let \(\Omega\) be the twenty subsets of three treated units. For each \(z\in\Omega\), write \(y(z)\) for the observed outcome vector. Under the sharp-null imputation used by the Fisher randomization test, \(y(z)\) is held fixed while the treatment label ranges over \(w\in\Omega\).

Because \(T\ge0\), all comparisons can be made with
\[
T^2(y(z),w)=\frac{\widehat\tau(y(z),w)^2}{S_1(y(z),w)^2/3+S_0(y(z),w)^2/3}.
\]
Every displayed outcome is rational with denominator \(1000\), so each of these \(400\) quantities is an exact rational number. Direct enumeration gives exactly seven realized assignments for which two of the twenty reference statistics are at least the observed statistic, hence \(P=2/20=1/10\). Three realized assignments have \(P=4/20=1/5\), three have \(P=18/20=9/10\), and seven have \(P=20/20=1\). Therefore exactly seven of the twenty actual assignments reject at \(\alpha=0.1\), which proves the size \(7/20\).

The smallest studentizing denominator over all realized-assignment/reference-assignment pairs is
\[
\frac{246719}{2250000}>0,
\]
so no comparison uses a zero or undefined standard error.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. It checks the zero average effect, enumerates all twenty actual assignments and all twenty sharp-null rerandomizations for each actual assignment, verifies all standard-error denominators are positive, reproduces the four p-value multiplicities, and asserts the exact rejection probability \(7/20\). No floating-point comparison is needed for the certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Wu and Ding define the treatment-control studentized statistic as the squared difference in means divided by \(S_1^2/N_1+S_0^2/N_0\), and prove that its Fisher randomization test is finite-sample exact under the sharp null and asymptotically conservative under the weak null. Their finite-sample simulations focus on three- and four-arm settings rather than an exact two-arm enumeration. The present result isolates a finite-sample boundary that their asymptotic statement does not settle: even in a balanced two-arm experiment, the advocated studentized test can have substantial finite-sample weak-null inflation.

Cohen and Fogarty likewise distinguish exact sharp-null validity from asymptotic weak-null validity and explain that randomization reference distributions built under a sharp null can be anti-conservative under a weak null. Their general framework does not imply this six-unit p-value distribution or the exact size \(7/20\).

## Limitations

This is an explicit counterexample, not a worst-case envelope and not a claim that six units are minimal. It does not contradict asymptotic conservativeness. The example uses continuous-valued fixed potential outcomes, a balanced complete-randomization design, and the inclusive finite randomization p-value. Other definitions of randomized tie-breaking or other designs can have different finite-sample behavior.
