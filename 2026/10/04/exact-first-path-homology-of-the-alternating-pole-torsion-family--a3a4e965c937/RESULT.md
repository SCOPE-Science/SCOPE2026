# Exact first path homology of the alternating-pole torsion family
## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 2\), define a loopless digraph \(D_n\) with central vertices \(c_i\), where \(i\in\mathbb Z/(2n)\mathbb Z\), two poles \(p_0,p_1\), central arcs \(c_i\to c_{i+1}\), and bidirected spokes \(c_i\leftrightarrow p_{i\bmod 2}\). In the non-regular integral path-homology convention used by Chowdhury--Huntsman--Yutin,
\[
\widetilde H_1(D_n;\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z\oplus \mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
In particular, the torsion subgroup is exactly \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) for every \(n\ge2\). This proves the published all-parameter torsion conjecture for the alternating-pole family and also determines its free summand.

## Assumptions and scope
Path homology is taken over \(\mathbb Z\) with the non-regular convention of the source paper: allowed elementary paths follow directed edges, and an allowed \(2\)-chain lies in \(\Omega_2\) exactly when its ordinary alternating boundary contains no non-allowed \(1\)-path. The graph has no loops. Indices on central vertices are taken modulo \(2n\). Since \(D_n\) is connected, reduced and unreduced first path homology agree.

Write \(e_i=(c_i,c_{i+1})\), \(a_i=(c_i,p_{i\bmod2})\), \(b_i=(p_{i\bmod2},c_i)\), and \(T_i=a_i+b_i\). Define the \(1\)-cycles
\[
R_i=a_i+b_{i+2}-e_i-e_{i+1}.
\]

## Proof
First determine \(\Omega_2\). There are no allowed elementary \(2\)-paths whose middle-vertex deletion is itself an allowed edge. Among paths with a missing shortcut, cancellation of the illegal middle-deletion term can occur only inside endpoint classes with more than one allowed two-step route.

For endpoints \(c_i,c_{i+2}\), the only two routes are \( (c_i,p_{i\bmod2},c_{i+2})\) and \((c_i,c_{i+1},c_{i+2})\). Their difference
\[
A_i=(c_i,p_{i\bmod2},c_{i+2})-(c_i,c_{i+1},c_{i+2})
\]
lies in \(\Omega_2\) and satisfies \(\partial A_i=R_i\).

For a fixed pole \(p_\epsilon\), the \(n\) paths \((p_\epsilon,c_j,p_\epsilon)\) with \(j\equiv\epsilon\pmod2\) have the same missing loop shortcut. Differences between any one reference path and the remaining \(n-1\) paths form an integral basis for the cancellation subspace, and their boundaries are \(T_j-T_r\). Every other missing-shortcut endpoint class contains a single path, so its coefficient must vanish. Consequently \(\Omega_2\) has rank \(2n+2(n-1)=4n-2\), and its boundary subgroup is generated by all \(R_i\) together with the same-parity differences \(T_j-T_r\).

It remains to describe the integral \(1\)-cycle lattice. The connected graph has \(2n+2\) vertices and \(6n\) edges, so \(\ker\partial_1\) has rank \(4n-1\). The \(4n\) cycles \(R_i,T_i\) generate it integrally. Indeed, for an arbitrary cycle
\[
z=\sum_i E_i e_i+\sum_i A_i a_i+\sum_i B_i b_i,
\]
the vertex equations give \(A_i-B_i=E_{i-1}-E_i\), while the two pole equations imply \(\sum_i(-1)^iE_i=0\). Hence the cyclic integer system \(x_i+x_{i-1}=-E_i\) has a solution. Putting \(y_i=A_i-x_i\) then yields
\[
z=\sum_i x_iR_i+\sum_i y_iT_i.
\]
A relation \(\sum_i x_iR_i+\sum_i y_iT_i=0\) forces, from the central-edge coefficients, \(x_i=(-1)^iq\) for some \(q\in\mathbb Z\), and then the spoke coefficients force \(y_i=-x_i\). Thus the unique primitive relation is
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^i(R_i-T_i)=0.
\]

Passing to \(H_1=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) sets every \(R_i\) to zero and identifies all even \(T_i\) with a class \(U\) and all odd \(T_i\) with a class \(V\). The primitive cycle relation becomes \(n(U-V)=0\). Therefore
\[
H_1(D_n;\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z\langle U,V\rangle/\langle n(U-V)\rangle
\cong \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs \(D_n\) from the definition, enumerates every allowed elementary \(2\)-path, forms the non-regular \(\Omega_2\) cancellation basis by endpoint class, and checks that every displayed basis chain has no illegal boundary term. For \(2\le n\le12\) it independently verifies \(\operatorname{rank}\Omega_2=4n-2\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\ker\partial_1=4n-1\). It terminates with `ALTERNATING_POLE_PATH_H1_VERIFY_OK`.

The finite replay is a regression check only. The all-parameter conclusion follows from the integral argument above, not from extrapolating the tested values.

## Relationship to prior work
Chowdhury, Huntsman, and Yutin introduced this alternating-pole family in arXiv:2008.11885v1 and the later journal article. They computed the first examples with torsion \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\), reported computational verification through \(n=8\), and conjectured that a central directed cycle of length \(2n\) has torsion subgroup \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The theorem above proves that conjecture for all \(n\ge2\) and additionally identifies the free rank as one.

Di, Ivanov, Mukoseev, and Zhang developed general covering methods for path homology, but their covering spectral sequence does not state this alternating-pole computation. A later paper by Theriault, Wu, Yau, and Zhang still describes the alternating-pole \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) pattern as conjectural beyond the displayed low cases. Liu and Grigor'yan prove a different conjecture from the temporal-network work, concerning vanishing of higher path homology for temporal digraph representations, rather than this torsion family.

## Limitations
The theorem is specifically for the non-regular integral path-homology convention used in the motivating source. Other regularization conventions can change the treatment of directed two-cycles and should not be conflated with this statement. The result determines \(H_1\) only; it does not assert a homotopy classification of the digraph or determine higher path homology groups. Literature searches cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed independent proof, although the original source, later papers that cite it, and exact claim/alias searches were checked.
