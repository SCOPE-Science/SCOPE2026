# Exact fixed-profile range of variance relative to squared range

## Finding

Let \(X\) be a nondegenerate finite discrete random variable with distinct ordered atoms
\[
x_1<\cdots<x_m
\]
and fixed positive masses
\[
p_i=\Pr(X=x_i),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m p_i=1.
\]
Write
\[
P_j=\sum_{i=1}^j p_i,
\qquad
R=x_m-x_1,
\qquad
\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(X).
\]
Define
\[
L(\mathbf p)=\frac{p_1p_m}{p_1+p_m}
\]
and
\[
U(\mathbf p)=\max_{1\le j<m}P_j(1-P_j).
\]

Then the complete range of the scale-free variance is as follows.

For \(m=2\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma^2}{R^2}=L(\mathbf p)=U(\mathbf p)=p_1p_2.
}
\]

For \(m=3\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma^2}{R^2}\in[L(\mathbf p),U(\mathbf p)).
}
\]
The lower endpoint is attained exactly when
\[
\boxed{
x_2=
\frac{p_1x_1+p_mx_m}{p_1+p_m}.
}
\]

For every \(m\ge4\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sigma^2}{R^2}\in(L(\mathbf p),U(\mathbf p)).
}
\]

The lower boundary is approached by moving every interior atom toward the conditional mean of the two endpoint atoms while preserving strict order. The upper boundary is approached by collapsing the atoms on each side of a cut to the two endpoints, where the cut maximizes
\[
P_j(1-P_j).
\]

For equal masses
\[
p_i=\frac1m,
\]
the lower constant is
\[
L=\frac1{2m}.
\]
The upper constant is
\[
U=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac14,&m\text{ even},\\[2mm]
\dfrac{m^2-1}{4m^2},&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the lower endpoint recovers the classical equal-weight von Szokefalvi-Nagy constant, while odd support size forces a strict profile-specific improvement over the universal Popoviciu upper constant.

## Assumptions and scope

The masses and their order are fixed. Only the numerical support locations vary.

All masses are positive and all displayed atoms are distinct. Boundary configurations with coalescing atoms are used only to identify sharp infima and suprema.

The normalization by \(R^2\) removes location and positive-scale freedom.

The theorem concerns the population variance of the finite law, not an unbiased sample-variance estimator.

## Proof

Positive affine transformations do not change
\[
\frac{\sigma^2}{R^2}.
\]
Normalize
\[
x_1=0,
\qquad
x_m=1.
\]

For the lower bound, let \(E\) denote the event that \(X\) is one of the two endpoint atoms. Its probability is
\[
w=p_1+p_m.
\]
Conditional on \(E\), the two endpoint values have probabilities
\[
\frac{p_1}{w}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac{p_m}{w},
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X\mid E)
=
\frac{p_1p_m}{w^2}.
\]
Apply the law of total variance to the partition into \(E\) and its complement:
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid\mathcal G)]
+
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]),
\]
where \(\mathcal G\) is generated by \(E\). The endpoint group alone contributes
\[
w\operatorname{Var}(X\mid E)
=
\frac{p_1p_m}{p_1+p_m}.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)\ge L(\mathbf p).
\tag{1}
\]

Equality in (1) requires both the conditional variance of the interior group and the between-group variance to vanish. Thus all interior atoms must coincide at
\[
c=
\mathbb E[X\mid E]
=
\frac{p_m}{p_1+p_m}.
\tag{2}
\]
With distinct support, this is possible exactly when there is at most one interior atom. For \(m=3\), (2) gives the stated lower-endpoint support. For \(m\ge4\), equality is impossible, but choosing distinct interior atoms converging to \(c\) proves sharpness of the infimum.

For the upper bound, write the normalized adjacent gaps as
\[
d_j=x_{j+1}-x_j>0,
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_j=1.
\]
For each cut define the centered upper-block indicator
\[
H_j
=
\mathbf 1_{\{X>x_j\}}-(1-P_j).
\]
Then
\[
X-\mathbb EX
=
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}d_jH_j.
\tag{3}
\]
Also
\[
\|H_j\|_2^2
=
P_j(1-P_j)
\le
U(\mathbf p).
\tag{4}
\]
The triangle inequality in \(L^2\), followed by (4), gives
\[
\sigma
=
\left\|
\sum_jd_jH_j
\right\|_2
\le
\sum_jd_j\|H_j\|_2
\le
\sqrt{U(\mathbf p)}
\sum_jd_j
=
\sqrt{U(\mathbf p)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)\le U(\mathbf p).
\tag{5}
\]

When \(m\ge3\), at least two gaps are positive. Distinct centered cut indicators are not positive scalar multiples of one another, so the triangle inequality is strict. Hence the upper endpoint is unattained by every strict support with at least three atoms.

If \(j_*\) maximizes
\[
P_j(1-P_j),
\]
make the first \(j_*\) atoms converge to zero and the remaining atoms converge to one, retaining strict order. Then the centered normalized variable converges to \(H_{j_*}\), and its variance converges to
\[
P_{j_*}(1-P_{j_*})=U(\mathbf p).
\]
This proves sharpness of the supremum.

Finally, after fixing the endpoints, the strict support region
\[
0<x_2<\cdots<x_{m-1}<1
\]
is connected, and variance is continuous. Its image is therefore an interval. Combining the endpoint analysis above gives the three exact ranges.

For equal masses,
\[
L=\frac1{2m}.
\]
The maximum of
\[
\frac{j}{m}\left(1-\frac{j}{m}\right)
\]
occurs at the integer \(j\) nearest \(m/2\), which gives the stated even--odd formula for \(U\).

## Verification

The accompanying exact-rational checker generates random positive rational mass profiles and strict rational supports normalized to range one.

It verifies the lower bound, the strict upper bound for at least three atoms, the exact two-point identity, and the exact three-point lower extremizer.

It separately checks the equal-mass formulas and explicit rational support families approaching both sharp boundary configurations.

The finite replay is supplementary. The universal result follows from the law of total variance, the cumulative-cut decomposition, the \(L^2\) triangle inequality, and connectedness.

## Relationship to prior work

Classical range--variance theory provides two complementary equal-weight statements: Popoviciu's universal upper bound and the von Szokefalvi-Nagy lower bound for a finite list whose minimum and maximum are both represented. Sharma, Gupta, and Kapoor review these inequalities and several refinements for finite equal-weight universes, including refinements using the mean, harmonic mean, and third central moment.

Afendras, Balakrishnan, and Papadatos develop a broad theory of upper and lower variance bounds for functions of discrete distributions in the cumulative Ord family. Their bounds are driven by distribution-specific difference identities and orthogonal polynomials rather than by fixing an arbitrary atom-probability profile and varying only the support geometry.

The present statement freezes the complete ordered mass profile. The lower constant then depends only on the two endpoint masses, while the upper constant depends only on the cumulative cut closest to one half. This simultaneously gives the exact weighted analogue of the lower range constraint and a profile-specific sharpening of the upper range constraint.

Targeted searches using weighted variance, fixed probabilities, ordered support, Popoviciu, von Szokefalvi-Nagy, and range normalization did not locate the two endpoint formulas together with the exact attainable-set classification.

## Limitations

The law is finite and every prescribed atom mass is positive.

The result fixes the atom-probability profile. If probabilities are allowed to vary, the classical unrestricted range bounds are recovered instead.

Except for the two-point case and the three-point lower endpoint, sharp boundary configurations require atom coalescence and are therefore not attained by a strict support.

The originality search was targeted. Older weighted finite-population or inequality literature may contain an equivalent fixed-frequency formulation under different terminology.
