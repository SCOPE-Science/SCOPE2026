# Exact four-key birthday envelope under three-wise-independent hashing

## Finding

Let \(q\ge4\), and let \(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3,Y_4\) be three-wise-independent random variables, each uniform on the same \(q\)-point set. Then the collision-free probability has the exact attainable interval
\[
\boxed{\max\left\{0,\frac{(q-1)(q-5)}{q^2}\right\}\le \Pr(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3,Y_4\text{ are all distinct})\le \frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{q^2}.}
\]
Both endpoints and every intermediate value are attainable.

The upper endpoint is exactly
\[
\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{q^2}=\Pr(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3\text{ are all distinct}),
\]
so three-wise independence can make the fourth value avoid creating any new collision whenever the first three are distinct. At \(q=4\) and \(q=5\), the collision-free probability can be zero.

## Assumptions and scope

The variables are exactly uniform and three-wise independent: every three-coordinate marginal is the product-uniform law. No mutual-independence assumption is made. The theorem optimizes over all such joint laws, not only laws generated by a particular hash architecture. Four keys are the first number for which three-wise independence leaves any freedom beyond the fully random law.

## Proof

Symmetrize any admissible law over permutations of the four coordinates and of the \(q\) symbols. This preserves the objective and all three-coordinate marginals. The symmetrized law is determined by the occupancy partition of four balls. Let \(a,b,c,d,e\) denote the probabilities of the five types
\[
1+1+1+1,\qquad 2+1+1,\qquad 2+2,\qquad 3+1,\qquad 4.
\]
Their numbers of equal pairs and equal triples are respectively
\[
(0,0),\ (1,0),\ (2,0),\ (3,1),\ (6,4).
\]
Three-wise independence implies
\[
\Pr(Y_i=Y_j)=\frac1q,\qquad \Pr(Y_i=Y_j=Y_k)=\frac1{q^2}.
\]
Summing over the six pairs and four triples gives
\[
b+2c+3d+6e=\frac6q,\tag{1}
\]
\[
d+4e=\frac4{q^2}.\tag{2}
\]
Together with \(a+b+c+d+e=1\), these are the only constraints required after symmetrization.

Indeed, suppose a symbol-symmetric occupancy law satisfies (1)-(2). For any three coordinates put \(u=\Pr(Y_1=Y_2)=1/q\) and \(t=\Pr(Y_1=Y_2=Y_3)=1/q^2\). Each concrete all-equal triple has probability \(t/q=1/q^3\). Each concrete pattern with one specified equal pair has probability
\[
\frac{u-t}{q(q-1)}=\frac1{q^3}.
\]
The all-distinct mass is \(1-3u+2t=(q-1)(q-2)/q^2\), which symbol symmetry splits among \(q(q-1)(q-2)\) ordered distinct triples, again giving \(1/q^3\). Hence the constructed symmetric law is genuinely three-wise independent.

Eliminate \(d\) from (2):
\[
d=\frac4{q^2}-4e,\qquad 0\le e\le\frac1{q^2}.\tag{3}
\]
Then (1) becomes
\[
b+2c=\frac{6(q-2)}{q^2}+6e=:S.\tag{4}
\]
Finally,
\[
a=1-\frac4{q^2}+3e-(b+c).\tag{5}
\]
For nonnegative \(b,c\) with \(b+2c=S\),
\[
\frac S2\le b+c\le S.
\]
Using \(b+c\ge S/2\) in (5) gives
\[
a\le \frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{q^2}.\tag{6}
\]
Equality is attained by
\[
e=b=0,\qquad d=\frac4{q^2},\qquad c=\frac{3(q-2)}{q^2},\qquad a=\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{q^2}.\tag{7}
\]

Using \(b+c\le S\) in (5), followed by \(e\le1/q^2\), yields
\[
a\ge \frac{(q-1)(q-5)}{q^2}.\tag{8}
\]
Together with \(a\ge0\), this gives the lower bound. For \(q\ge5\), equality is attained by
\[
e=\frac1{q^2},\qquad d=c=0,\qquad b=\frac{6(q-1)}{q^2},\qquad a=\frac{(q-1)(q-5)}{q^2}.\tag{9}
\]
For \(q=4\), the value zero is attained by
\[
b=\frac34,\qquad d=\frac14,\qquad a=c=e=0.\tag{10}
\]
Uniformizing within each occupancy type turns (7), (9), and (10) into explicit joint laws. Convex mixtures of the lower and upper extremizers remain three-wise independent and vary the all-distinct probability linearly, so every intermediate value is attained.

## Verification

The accompanying exact-rational checker verifies the symbolic endpoint laws for thousands of alphabet sizes. For \(4\le q\le10\) it explicitly enumerates all \(q^4\) assignments, uniformizes each endpoint law within occupancy types, and checks every concrete three-coordinate marginal. It also checks convex mixtures of the endpoint laws. Finite replay is supplementary; equations (1)-(5) prove the universal interval.

## Relationship to prior work

Limited-independence hashing was developed to reduce randomness while retaining probabilistic guarantees. Schmidt, Siegel, and Srinivasan gave Chernoff-Hoeffding-type bounds under limited independence. Pătraşcu and Thorup emphasize that simple tabulation is three-wise independent but not four-wise independent and nevertheless has strong hashing guarantees. The inspected full text motivates the first nontrivial four-key question but does not state the exact all-distinct interval above.

The closest indexed published result found in the repository search gives the complete all-distinct range under only pairwise-independent uniform allocations. It fixes one collision moment. The present theorem is not a special case: three-wise independence adds the equal-triple constraint and strictly changes the feasible interval. In particular, the pairwise upper endpoint \(1-1/q\) exceeds the present \((q-1)(q-2)/q^2\) for every \(q\ge4\).

Targeted searches using three-wise-independent hashing, four-key birthday probabilities, all-distinct occupancy probabilities, and strength-three orthogonal-array language did not locate the displayed interval or its endpoint occupancy laws.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to four variables. For five or more keys, symmetrization still gives an occupancy moment problem, but the feasible polytope is higher-dimensional. The theorem optimizes over abstract three-wise-independent uniform laws; a fixed implementable hash family may have a smaller attainable interval.

The closest repository result on pairwise-independent occupancy was available by indexed title and exact statement summary, but its repository full text could not be fetched in this run. Its stated pairwise scope is distinct, but that access gap is retained as a residual originality risk.
