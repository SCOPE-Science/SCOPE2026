# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The four-point defect of a cyclically ordered quadruple is reduced exactly to two nonnegative trigonometric expressions. The diagonal pairing is proved to be the largest pair-sum by explicit factorizations. For fixed opposite-gap totals, each relevant cosine and sine product is maximized by the most balanced integer split. The resulting bound \(U_n(p)\) is proved strictly increasing up to \(p=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) using two elementary positive difference identities. Balanced terminal gap patterns attain the bound in every residue class modulo \(4\). The standalone checker exhaustively verifies all four-subsets for \(4\le n\le60\), but the infinite theorem rests on the analytic argument rather than on enumeration.

## Originality

PASS with residual historical risk. Semantic-index searches were run for regular polygon chord metrics, four-point hyperbolicity, cyclic Euclidean point sets, exact constants, and balanced quadruples. No indexed result with the same statement or an implication covering it was located. The directly relevant finite-metric paper of Bauer and Roll was inspected in full at the hyperbolicity section and searched for regular polygons and circles; neither object appears. Chatterjee and Sloman supply the motivating worst-case metric invariant but no regular-polygon computation.

A potentially confusing broader-looking result is the exact hyperbolicity of the cycle graph. It is scientifically inapplicable because its metric is shortest-path distance along unit edges, whereas the present theorem uses ambient Euclidean chord distance. The two invariants even have different scaling: cycle-graph hyperbolicity grows with \(n\), while the present value stays bounded by a multiple of \(R\).

## Value

PASS. Exact Gromov hyperbolicity values are natural benchmarks for tree-likeness of finite metrics, and Bauer–Roll explicitly motivate finite non-geodesic spaces through Vietoris–Rips filtrations. Regular cyclic samples are canonical finite Euclidean data sets. The theorem gives a complete exact profile, identifies the extremal four-point configurations, exposes a genuine modulo-\(4\) discretization effect, and recovers the continuous-circle limiting constant directly.
