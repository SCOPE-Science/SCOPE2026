# Exact free-algebra growth for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra over finite fields
## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field and let \(L=ke\oplus kf\) be the Lie algebra with \([e,f]=f\). For every integer \(n\ge 1\), if \(F_n\) denotes the relatively free Lie algebra of rank \(n\) in \(\operatorname{var}_k L\), then
\[
\dim_k F_n=n+(n-1)(q^n-1).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
|F_n|=q^{\,n+(n-1)(q^n-1)}.
\]
There is also an intrinsic description of the derived ideal. In the term-function model on \(L^n\), write each input as \(x_i=a_i e+b_i f\), and identify an \(f\)-valued function linear in \(b=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)\) with its coefficient vector \(F(a)\in k^n\). Then
\[
[F_n,F_n]=\{F:k^n\to k^n: F(0)=0,\ a\cdot F(a)=0\text{ for every }a\in k^n\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\dim_k [F_n,F_n]=(n-1)(q^n-1).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The field is an arbitrary finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), including characteristic \(2\). The Lie algebra \(L\) is the unique two-dimensional nonabelian Lie algebra over \(k\), normalized by \([e,f]=f\). The free object is taken in the variety of \(k\)-Lie algebras generated by \(L\); no statement is made here about free metabelian Lie algebras over an infinite field or about the higher-dimensional irreducible-module examples from the motivating source.

## Proof
A relatively free algebra in a variety generated by a finite algebra can be realized as the algebra of term functions on the corresponding finite Cartesian power, generated by the coordinate projections. Thus realize \(F_n\) inside the Lie algebra of functions \(L^n\to L\), with pointwise bracket, generated by the coordinate functions \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\).

For one input \(x_i=a_i e+b_i f\), the bracket formula in \(L\) is
\[
[a e+b f,c e+d f]=(ad-cb)f.
\]
Hence every commutator term is \(f\)-valued, and its coefficient is linear in \(b\). The basic commutator has the form
\[
[x_i,x_j]=(a_i b_j-a_j b_i)f.
\]
Associate to it the vector-valued coefficient
\[
s_{ij}(a)=a_i\mathbf e_j-a_j\mathbf e_i\in k^n,
\]
where \(\mathbf e_i\) are the standard coordinate vectors. If \(h(a,b)f\) is already \(f\)-valued, then bracketing with \(x_r\) multiplies its coefficient by \(-a_r\). Therefore the derived ideal contains every polynomial-function multiple of every \(s_{ij}\).

Because \(k\) is finite, every function \(k^n\to k\) is represented by a polynomial: the function algebra is
\[
R=k[a_1,\ldots,a_n]/(a_1^q-a_1,\ldots,a_n^q-a_n).
\]
Thus the coefficient vectors occurring in the derived ideal contain the full \(R\)-submodule generated by the \(s_{ij}\). Pointwise, for \(a\ne0\), the vectors \(s_{ij}(a)\) span the hyperplane
\[
a^\perp=\{v\in k^n:a\cdot v=0\},
\]
while every \(s_{ij}(0)\) is zero. Since \(R\) contains the characteristic function of every point of \(k^n\), arbitrary choices in these pointwise spans can be interpolated independently. Hence the generated module is exactly
\[
\{F:k^n\to k^n:F(0)=0,\ a\cdot F(a)=0\text{ for all }a\}.
\]
Conversely, every bracket term starts from a linear combination of the \(s_{ij}\) and subsequent bracketing only multiplies by coordinate functions, so no larger coefficient space occurs. This proves the asserted description of \([F_n,F_n]\).

For each nonzero \(a\), the hyperplane \(a^\perp\) has dimension \(n-1\), while the value at \(a=0\) is forced to be zero. Therefore
\[
\dim_k[F_n,F_n]=(q^n-1)(n-1).
\]
The span \(U=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle_k\) has dimension \(n\), and \(U\cap[F_n,F_n]=0\): a linear combination \(\sum c_i x_i\) lying in the derived ideal has zero \(e\)-coordinate, so \(\sum c_i a_i\) vanishes for every \(a\in k^n\), forcing every \(c_i=0\). Every Lie term is a sum of a linear term and commutator terms, so \(F_n=U\oplus[F_n,F_n]\). The dimension and cardinality formulas follow.

Finally,
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\dim_k F_n\bigr)^{1/n}=q,
\]
because the dominant term is \((n-1)q^n\).

## Verification
The accompanying script `verify.py` independently constructs the coordinate term functions and closes their span under pointwise Lie brackets over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). It verifies the exact dimensions for \((q,n)=(2,1),(2,2),(2,3),(3,1),(3,2)\), namely \(1,5,17,1,10\), and ends with `CHECK_OK`. These finite computations are consistency checks only; the proof above establishes the formula for every prime power \(q\) and every \(n\ge1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Bahturin and Olshanskii study locally finite varieties of algebras over finite fields. Their Example 7 considers a semidirect product of an abelian irreducible ideal \(M\) by a one-dimensional Lie algebra and proves that the exponential growth rate of the dimensions of the relatively free algebras is the field order \(q\), even when \(M\) has arbitrarily large dimension. The present result takes the smallest member of that family, \(\dim_k M=1\), and replaces the asymptotic exponent with an exact formula for every free rank \(n\), together with an explicit description of the derived ideal as a bundle of orthogonal hyperplanes over \(k^n\setminus\{0\}\).

Olshanskii's subsequent work gives general potential bounds for finite algebras and again emphasizes the rank-growth function of relatively free algebras. The exact formula above is not stated in the inspected material. Older work of Bahturin on identities of free polynilpotent Lie algebras is highly relevant to identities of metabelian Lie algebras; only bibliographic metadata and indexed snippets were available for that paper, so equivalent older coverage under polynomial-identity language remains a residual literature risk.

## Limitations
The statement is specific to the two-dimensional nonabelian Lie algebra over a finite field. The pointwise interpolation step uses finiteness of \(k\) essentially. The result does not determine exact dimensions for the higher-dimensional irreducible-module semidirect products in the motivating family. An older polynomial-identity source could conceivably encode an equivalent basis or dimension formula under different terminology; that source was not available in full text during the literature comparison.
