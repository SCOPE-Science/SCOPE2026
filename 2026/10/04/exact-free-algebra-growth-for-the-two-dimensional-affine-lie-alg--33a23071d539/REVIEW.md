# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof realizes the relatively free algebra as coordinate term functions on \(L^n\), computes every commutator as an \(f\)-valued function linear in the \(b\)-coordinates, and identifies the derived ideal with the module generated by \(a_i\mathbf e_j-a_j\mathbf e_i\). Finite-field polynomial interpolation separates points, giving exactly one \((n-1)\)-dimensional orthogonal fiber over each nonzero \(a\in\mathbb F_q^n\). The linear span of the free generators intersects this derived ideal trivially, which yields the formula. The small-field exhaustive checker agrees with the formula in all tested cases.

## Originality
PASS with a stated residual risk. The closest recent source proves only the exponential rate \(q\) for a broader semidirect-product family; its inspected Example 7 and surrounding argument do not provide the exact rank-\(n\) dimension in the one-dimensional-module case. Exact-form and semantic searches did not locate the formula \(n+(n-1)(q^n-1)\), the equivalent orthogonal-section description, or a stronger statement implying it. A 1979 paper on identical relations in free polynilpotent Lie algebras is plausibly relevant, but its full text was unavailable; this prevents claiming exhaustive historical uniqueness and is recorded as the principal residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The motivating work studies dimension growth of relatively free algebras and singles out this semidirect-product mechanism to show that the growth exponent can stay at \(q\) despite a large monolith. The exact free-rank sequence for its smallest nonabelian Lie member is therefore a natural invariant, not an arbitrary slice. It upgrades an asymptotic exponent to a complete finite-rank formula and exposes the geometric reason for the growth: one orthogonal hyperplane over every nonzero point of \(\mathbb F_q^n\).

## Closest literature and limitations
Bahturin--Olshanskii, arXiv:2603.09655v1, Example 7, gives the exponent \(q\) for the semidirect-product family but not the exact formula established here. Olshanskii, arXiv:2606.04577v1, provides broader growth/potential context. Bahturin, DOI 10.1112/jlms/s2-20.1.39, is the main unresolved historical comparison because only metadata and indexed snippets were available. The proof here is confined to finite fields and the two-dimensional nonabelian member.
