# Exact full-spark criterion for the four-row Fourier pattern \(\{0,1,3,4\}\)
## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge 5\), let \(F_N=(\omega^{rs})_{r,s\in\mathbb Z_N}\), where \(\omega=e^{-2\pi i/N}\), and select the four rows
\[
\mathcal M=\{0,1,3,4\}.
\]
The resulting \(4\times N\) harmonic frame is full spark if and only if
\[
\gcd(N,6)=1.
\]
The same row set is uniformly distributed over every divisor of \(N\) if and only if
\[
3\nmid N\quad\text{and}\quad4\nmid N.
\]
Consequently every \(N\equiv2\) or \(10\pmod{12}\) gives a uniformly distributed row set whose harmonic frame is nevertheless not full spark. This is an infinite extension of the classical order-\(10\) counterexample for these same four rows.

## Assumptions and scope
The order satisfies \(N\ge5\), so the four row indices are distinct modulo \(N\). Full spark means that every choice of four columns gives a nonsingular \(4\times4\) submatrix. Uniform distribution means that for every divisor \(d\mid N\), the counts of row indices in the residue classes modulo \(d\) differ by at most one.

## Proof
Choose four distinct columns and write their corresponding \(N\)-th roots of unity as \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\). The generalized Vandermonde determinant for exponents \(0,1,3,4\) factors as
\[
\det\bigl(x_j^{m_i}\bigr)_{m_i\in\{0,1,3,4\},\,1\le j\le4}
=\pm\!\prod_{1\le i<j\le4}(x_j-x_i)\,e_2(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4),
\]
where
\[
e_2(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}x_ix_j.
\]
The Vandermonde factor is nonzero because the columns are distinct. Hence a four-column minor is singular exactly when \(e_2=0\).

Suppose first that \(\gcd(N,6)=1\). Then every prime divisor of \(N\) is at least \(5\). If \(e_2=0\), the six products \(x_ix_j\) with \(i<j\) form a vanishing sum of six \(N\)-th roots of unity, counted with multiplicity. Lam and Leung's weight theorem for vanishing sums of roots of unity says that the weight of such a sum must lie in the additive semigroup generated by the prime divisors of \(N\). No nonnegative combination of primes at least \(5\) equals \(6\). Thus \(e_2\ne0\), so every four-column minor is nonsingular.

Conversely, assume \(2\mid N\). For \(N\ge6\), let \(t\) be a primitive \(N\)-th root and choose
\[
\{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}=\{1,t,t^2,-t\}.
\]
These roots are distinct, and their pair products satisfy
\[
t+t^2-t+t^3-t^2-t^3=0.
\]
Hence \(e_2=0\), so the frame is not full spark.

Now assume \(3\mid N\). For \(N\ge6\), let \(u\) be a primitive cube root among the \(N\)-th roots and choose an \(N\)-th root \(t\notin\{1,u,u^2\}\). For
\[
\{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}=\{1,u,u^2,t\},
\]
one has
\[
e_2=(u+u^2+1)+t(1+u+u^2)=0.
\]
Thus divisibility by \(2\) or \(3\) produces a singular four-column minor. This proves the full-spark criterion.

For uniform distribution, reduce \(\{0,1,3,4\}\) modulo a divisor \(d\). Modulo \(2\) the counts are \((2,2)\), so distribution is uniform. Modulo \(3\) the counts are \((2,2,0)\), and modulo \(4\) they are \((2,1,0,1)\), so uniformity fails when \(3\mid N\) or \(4\mid N\). For every \(d\ge5\), the four residues are distinct and hence automatically uniformly distributed. Therefore uniformity over every divisor of \(N\) is equivalent to \(3\nmid N\) and \(4\nmid N\). Combining this with the full-spark criterion gives the congruence family \(N\equiv2\) or \(10\pmod{12}\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_pattern_0134.py` uses exact integer and finite-field arithmetic. It symbolically expands the determinant and verifies the generalized Vandermonde factorization. It checks the uniform-distribution criterion for every \(5\le N\le5000\). As an independent finite check of the nonvanishing direction, it tests every normalized four-column choice for every \(N\le120\) with \(\gcd(N,6)=1\) using primitive \(N\)-th roots in several finite fields; a candidate is accepted only after a reduction in which its determinant is nonzero. This covers \(2{,}693{,}520\) normalized quadruples. It also verifies the explicit singular witnesses through \(N=500\). The finite checks support, but do not replace, the all-order proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Lam and Leung classify the possible weights of vanishing sums of \(N\)-th roots of unity; their theorem supplies the key obstruction at weight six. Alexeev, Cahill, and Mixon prove that divisor-uniformity is necessary for full spark at arbitrary Fourier order and exhibit the order-\(10\) counterexample with the same row set \(\{0,1,3,4\}\), but they do not give an all-order criterion for this row pattern. Matter, Fischer, Pesavento, and Pfetsch encounter the same elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_2\) for the continuous ambiguity problem of the four-sensor array \((0,1,3,4)\); their classification concerns continuous array ambiguities rather than the discrete order-\(N\) full-spark criterion proved here.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns exactly the fixed four-row pattern \(\{0,1,3,4\}\); it is not a characterization of all four-row Fourier selections. The originality comparison used the named primary sources and targeted searches for equivalent formulations, but differently phrased or unindexed prior work remains a residual risk. The computational replay verifies large finite ranges only; the infinite statement rests on the determinant factorization, the published weight theorem for vanishing sums, and the explicit obstruction constructions.
