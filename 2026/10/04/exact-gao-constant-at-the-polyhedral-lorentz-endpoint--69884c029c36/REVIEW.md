# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The domain is the real plane with \(0<\omega<1\), and the norm is exactly the Lorentz endpoint \(l^{(2)}(\omega,1)\). The published comparison theorem gives \(c'_{\mathrm{NJ}}\ge(M_1M_2)^{-2}\). The proof computes \(M_1=\sqrt{1+\omega^2}\) by Cauchy--Schwarz and \(M_2=\max\{1,\sqrt2/(1+\omega)\}\) by a one-variable derivative with no omitted interior maxima. The two displayed unit-vector pairs attain the two lower-bound branches exactly, including agreement at \(\omega=\sqrt2-1\). No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual literature-search risk. The closest same-object primary source computes the Lorentz family only for \(2\le r<\infty\) and explicitly names \(1\le r<2\) as unresolved. The 2007 Lorentz paper's abstract states its exact two-dimensional calculation for \(2\le q<\infty\). The 2023 closely related paper treats a different generalized supremal invariant and again uses \(q\ge2\). Exact-formula and norm-alias searches did not locate this two-branch endpoint formula. The 2022 general lower-bound theorem does not by itself imply equality here; equality requires the new branch-specific extremizers.

## Value
PASS. The case \(r=1\) is the polyhedral endpoint of a Lorentz interval explicitly posed as unresolved, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. The exact answer exhibits a genuine structural change at \(\omega=\sqrt2-1\): the extremizing pair changes from coordinate-swapped to sign-reflected. This supplies a tractable boundary case and a benchmark for the remaining \(1<r<2\) range.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main comparator is Zuo--Huang--Huang--Wang (2022), DOI 10.3390/math10234591, especially its comparison theorem, Lorentz Example 6, and conclusion. Cui--Wang (2007) covers the two-dimensional Lorentz problem only for exponents at least \(2\) according to its abstract. Zuo--Huang--Wang (2023), DOI 10.7153/mia-2023-26-09, is close in objects but studies a different generalized supremal constant and again restricts the displayed Lorentz example to \(q\ge2\). The remaining scientific limitation is that \(1<r<2\) and Gao-type exponents other than \(2\) are not determined here.
