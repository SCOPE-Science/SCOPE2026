# Same-model review

## Correctness

**PASS.**  The proof starts from the published GC2 condition for \(b=c\), substitutes \(a=2b\), and reduces it exactly to a one-variable inequality on each neighborhood of a zero of \(\cos x\).  The sign of the coupling is forced by the inequality and selects one flank.  The remaining lower and upper bounds are continuous strictly decreasing functions with a common endpoint limit, which gives both the existence threshold and uniqueness of each component.  The integer count follows from the strict affine threshold in \(m\).  Supplementary numerical replay checks the raw GC2 inequality against the reduced form and the component counts for multiple couplings of both signs.

## Originality

**PASS, narrowly scoped.**  The 2014 Exner--Turek paper gives condition (19) and proves in Corollary 4.5(i) only that for rational \(a/b\) it generates at most finitely many gaps.  The same paper proves separately that GC1 generates infinitely many gaps at rational ratios.  The inspected later Bethe--Sommerfeld paper obtains exact prescribed total gap counts for rectangular lattices with suitable irrational ratios, not for the honeycomb ratio \(2:1:1\).  Targeted database and web searches did not locate the displayed threshold constants or count formula.  The accepted originality claim is therefore only the exact GC2 threshold/count law for this natural commensurate specialization.

Residual risk: the simplification is elementary enough that an equivalent formula may exist in lecture notes, unpublished calculations, or literature not indexed by the searches.  No priority beyond the explicit statement and proof is asserted.

## Value

**PASS.**  The ratio \(2:1:1\) is the simplest nonequilateral commensurate one-direction dilation inside the source's detailed \(b=c\) family.  The result upgrades a qualitative finiteness theorem to a complete one-parameter staircase law: it identifies which side of each resonance can satisfy GC2, the exact coupling at which each interval is born, and the exact number present at any coupling.  This is a reusable benchmark for spectral calculations and for testing more general rational-ratio analyses.
