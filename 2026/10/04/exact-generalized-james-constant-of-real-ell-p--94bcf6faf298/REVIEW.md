# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the sharp Hanner sum-difference inequalities with their correct direction on the two exponent ranges. For \(1\le p\le2\), the inequality prevents both weighted sum and difference norms from exceeding \((\lambda^p+(1-\lambda)^p)^{1/p}\), and disjoint coordinate vectors attain equality. For \(2\le p<\infty\), applying the reversed Hanner form to the two weighted sum/difference vectors bounds their \(p\)-power average, and the two-coordinate Hanner pair attains equality. The endpoint \(p=\infty\) is handled directly. No finite sampling is used to prove an infinite statement.

## Originality
PASS. The 2021 source that introduced \(J(\lambda,X)\) was inspected in full-text HTML through its Section 5. It gives general bounds, comparison with the classical James constant, the non-uniformly-non-square value, and the Hilbert characterization, but no exact \(\ell_p\) formula. Targeted searches for the exact weighted \(\ell_p\) formula and its Hanner formulation did not identify a published statement implying this theorem. The classical Hanner theorem is a proof tool rather than a statement about \(J(\lambda,\ell_p)\).

## Value
PASS. The result gives the complete exact parameter profile on the canonical sequence spaces, simultaneously recovering the ordinary James constant at \(\lambda=\tfrac12\), identifying the phase transition at \(p=2\), and exhibiting the extremizing geometries on both sides. This is a natural benchmark for a geometric constant introduced specifically to quantify Banach-space non-squareness.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Liu–Sarfraz–Li (2021), which introduces the exact invariant and establishes only general structural bounds plus the Hilbert and non-uniformly-non-square cases. Hanner (1956) supplies the sharp inequalities used in the proof. A distinct 2026 weighted Birkhoff-orthogonal James-type invariant restricts the optimizing pairs by Birkhoff–James orthogonality and therefore does not cover the unrestricted constant treated here. The theorem does not claim an extension beyond \(\ell_p\) spaces.
