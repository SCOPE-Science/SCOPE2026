# Review

## Correctness
PASS. In the stated \(\epsilon(K)=1\) and high-framing range, the recent satellite formula makes \(\tau(P(K,n))\) affine in \(n\) with slope \(\binom{p}{2}\) for a \(p\)-strand braided pattern. Since one crossing change alters \(\tau\) by at most one, this gives the lower bound. A unit framing shift is one full twist, and the standard pure-braid factorization has exactly one conjugate square for each unordered pair of strands. One crossing change kills each conjugate square, giving the matching upper bound.

## Originality
PASS. The primary recent paper gives the affine \(\tau\)-formula but no pairwise Gordian-distance theorem was found in its full text. The closest later paper studies unknotting number, not the exact distance between two framing values. Searches for equivalent Gordian, full-twist, cable, and satellite formulations found no theorem covering the accepted equality. The equality needs both the recent lower-bound formula and an independent sharp braid-geometric upper bound.

## Value
PASS. The result identifies an exact geodesic family in the Gordian graph for every braided L-space satellite operator covered by the hypotheses. It shows that the natural full-twist sequence is not merely an explicit route but an optimal one, converting a Floer-theoretic formula into a sharp metric statement on an infinite family.

The main limitation is the high-framing threshold \(m,n\ge2\tau(K)\) with \(\epsilon(K)=1\); nothing is asserted outside that region. A close 2026 unknotting-number preprint was only inspectable at metadata/abstract depth in this check, so an unindexed pairwise remark remains a residual literature risk.
