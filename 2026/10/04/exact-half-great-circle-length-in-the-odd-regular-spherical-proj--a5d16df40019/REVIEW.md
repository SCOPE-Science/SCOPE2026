# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The specialization \(j=(n-1)/2\) gives \(\lambda=v(2v+1)\) and the positive form \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,v^2(2v+1))\). For side midpoints the normalized \(Q\)-inner product of consecutive lifts is exactly \(v=\cos(\pi/n)\), so each spherical segment is \(\pi/n\) and the witness length is \(\pi\). Local constancy then propagates the value on the connected open primitive component.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2609.28817v1, proves the arbitrary-period family and the weaker odd-spherical residue \(L\equiv\pi\pmod{{2\pi}}\); Remark 5.34 explicitly explains why that residue alone does not give \(L=\pi\). Exact \(\pi\) is proved there for a different period-five family. Targeted semantic and literature searches found no statement covering the arbitrary odd regular midpoint family.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves the source's explicit branch-selection issue for the canonical spherical member of every odd regular period. It supplies an exact geometric invariant for the open family with no numerical approximation and is materially stronger than another isolated periodic-orbit computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is González, arXiv:2609.28817v1, together with Fierobe's 2024 projective-billiard constructions. The claim is local to the connected primitive component containing the midpoint witness; other components and indefinite members are not classified. Unindexed folklore remains a residual originality risk.
