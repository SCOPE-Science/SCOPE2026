# Exact heteroscedastic size curve for the four-observation permutation mean test

## Finding
Let \(X_1,X_2\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(\mu,\sigma_X^2)\) and \(Y_1,Y_2\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(\mu,\sigma_Y^2)\) be independent, where \(\sigma_X,\sigma_Y>0\). Consider the ordinary two-sided exact permutation test based on \(T=|\bar X-\bar Y|\), with all six allocations of two observations to each group and the inclusive permutation tail. If \(k=\sigma_X^2/\sigma_Y^2\), then at the smallest attainable rejection level \(\alpha=1/3\),
\[
\Pr\{p_{\rm perm}\le 1/3\}=
S(k)=\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan\!\left(
\frac{(\sqrt{1+2k}-\sqrt{k})(\sqrt{k+2}-1)}{1+k}
\right).
\]
Moreover \(S(k)=S(1/k)\), \(S(1)=1/3\), \(1/3<S(k)<1/2\) for \(k\ne1\), and \(S(k)\to1/2\) as \(k\to0\) or \(k\to\infty\). Hence variance heterogeneity alone can inflate the exact weak-null size from \(1/3\) to arbitrarily close to \(1/2\), a sharp relative factor approaching \(3/2\), despite perfectly balanced sample sizes and exactly equal Gaussian means.

## Assumptions and scope
The four observations are mutually independent; each pair is iid Gaussian with the same mean \(\mu\) but possibly different positive variances. The statistic is the unstudentized absolute difference of sample means. The permutation p-value is computed from all six label allocations and counts ties inclusively. Gaussian continuity makes permutation-statistic ties a probability-zero event. The claim concerns the exact rejection probability at \(\alpha=1/3\); it does not assert a formula for larger sample sizes, non-Gaussian populations, or a studentized permutation statistic.

## Proof
Because location and common positive scaling do not affect the permutation ordering, set \(\mu=0\) and \(\sigma_Y=1\). Among the six allocations there are three complementary unordered partitions, each appearing twice. For four distinct pooled values \(z_1<z_2<z_3<z_4\), the largest absolute difference of two-pair means is attained uniquely by the partition \(\{z_1,z_2\}\mid\{z_3,z_4\}\). Therefore \(p_{\rm perm}=1/3\) exactly when the original two \(X\)-observations are the two smallest or the two largest pooled observations.

Equivalently, write the three pair-partition contrasts
\[
C_1=\frac{X_1+X_2-Y_1-Y_2}{2},\quad
C_2=\frac{X_1-X_2+Y_1-Y_2}{2},\quad
C_3=\frac{X_1-X_2-Y_1+Y_2}{2}.
\]
They have the same variance \((\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2)/2\). After standardization, \(Z_1=C_1/s\) is independent of \((Z_2,Z_3)=(C_2/s,C_3/s)\), while
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(Z_2,Z_3)=\rho=\frac{\sigma_X^2-\sigma_Y^2}{\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2}.
\]
Let
\[
a=\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho}{2}}=\frac{\sigma_X}{\sqrt{\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2}},\qquad
b=\sqrt{\frac{1-\rho}{2}}=\frac{\sigma_Y}{\sqrt{\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2}}.
\]
For independent standard normals \(U,V,Z_1\), one may represent \(Z_2=aU+bV\) and \(Z_3=aU-bV\). Hence
\[
\max(|Z_2|,|Z_3|)=a|U|+b|V|,
\]
so rejection is the isotropic Gaussian cone event
\[
|Z_1|>a|U|+b|V|.
\]
In the first octant the cone is generated by rays \((1,0,0)\), \((a,1,0)\), and \((b,0,1)\). Its solid angle is
\[
\Omega=2\arctan q,\qquad
q=(\sqrt{1+a^2}-a)(\sqrt{1+b^2}-b).
\]
There are eight sign-symmetric octants, so the Gaussian probability is \(8\Omega/(4\pi)=4\arctan(q)/\pi\). Substituting \(a=\sqrt{k/(1+k)}\) and \(b=1/\sqrt{1+k}\) gives the displayed formula for \(S(k)\).

For the sharp bounds, note
\[
\log q=-\operatorname{arsinh}(a)-\operatorname{arsinh}(b),\qquad a^2+b^2=1.
\]
The function \(g(x)=\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt{x})\) is strictly concave on \((0,1)\). Thus \(g(x)+g(1-x)\) is uniquely maximized at \(x=1/2\), which makes \(q\), and therefore \(S(k)\), uniquely minimized at \(k=1\). There \(q=2-\sqrt3=\tan(\pi/12)\), so \(S(1)=1/3\). At either variance-ratio endpoint, \((a,b)\to(1,0)\) or \((0,1)\), giving \(q\to\sqrt2-1=\tan(\pi/8)\) and \(S(k)\to1/2\). Positive finite variances keep both \(a,b\) strictly positive, so the upper endpoint is not attained.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` checks the closed form, the symmetry \(S(k)=S(1/k)\), the equal-variance value, the endpoint approach, and the combinatorial equivalence between \(p_{\rm perm}=1/3\) and complete sample separation on deterministic quadruples. A separate numerical stress test with seeded Gaussian simulation agreed with the formula at variance ratios \(1\), \(4\), and \(100\) within ordinary Monte Carlo error. The proof, not the simulation, establishes the infinite-model statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Ditzhaus, Fried and Pauly develop studentized permutation procedures for heterogeneous and heteroscedastic factorial designs and emphasize that finite exactness comes from exchangeability, while studentization supplies asymptotic validity beyond exchangeability. Bertanha and Chung likewise state that classical two-sample permutation tests can fail to control size for equality of summary parameters, including mean-type problems, and motivate studentization. Those sources establish the general phenomenon and remedy, but the inspected text does not give the exact four-observation Gaussian size curve above, its solid-angle representation, or the sharp \([1/3,1/2)\) envelope. Murphy's 1967 paper reports a simulation study of unequal-variance two-sample permutation tests; only its abstract was accessible here, so overlap with uninspected tables remains a residual literature risk, although targeted searches did not surface this closed form.

## Limitations
This is a smallest-\(n\) exact boundary calculation, not a claim about practical nominal levels such as \(0.05\), which are unattainable with only six allocations. It concerns the unstudentized absolute difference of means; robust studentized permutation methods are outside the claim. The older Murphy paper could not be fully inspected, so a residual possibility remains that it tabulates some matching small-sample values, though no exact formula or equivalent implication was found in accessible material.
