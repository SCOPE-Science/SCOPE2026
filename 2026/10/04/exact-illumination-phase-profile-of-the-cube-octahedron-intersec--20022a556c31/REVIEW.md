# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The vertex sets of \(K_c\) have exactly two open combinatorial types: signed permutations of \((1,c-1,0)\) for \(1<c<2\), and signed permutations of \((1,1,c-2)\) for \(2<c<3\), with \((1,1,0)\) at \(c=2\). For each type, the proof explicitly lists the active facet normals and chooses one of four tetrahedral directions whose dot product with every active normal is \(-1\) or \(-3\). The outer-normal criterion therefore proves the four-direction upper bound for every parameter in the open interval. The standard convex-hull separation argument proves the universal lower bound four in dimension three. Endpoint arguments show that a direction can illuminate at most one octahedron vertex and at most one cube vertex, while explicit axial and sign directions attain six and eight respectively.

## Originality

PASS with residual risk. The motivating 2024 full text gives an all-dimensional illumination-conjecture result for 1-symmetric bodies and a six-direction dimension-three consequence, not the exact four-direction profile of this family. The close-to-cube theorem gives at most seven directions near the cube, not four. The 2012 Archimedean-solid paper studies the weighted illumination parameter rather than the ordinary illumination number. Targeted searches for the cube–octahedron intersection, cuboctahedron, truncated cube, truncated octahedron, and tetrahedral four-direction illumination found no statement covering the continuous family. One indexed result already gives value four for a canonical truncated-octahedron representative; this is a single parameter and does not imply the family theorem. A prior independent-corner-truncation result does not apply because all eight adjacent cube corners are truncated for \(c>2\).

## Value

PASS. The claim gives a complete exact phase diagram for a canonical one-parameter interpolation between two Platonic solids and includes three classical centrally symmetric Archimedean solids. The result exhibits a sharp discontinuity: the illumination number is dimension-minimal throughout every strict mixture, even arbitrarily close to the cube, while both endpoints require strictly more directions. The fixed tetrahedral illuminating set explains this phenomenon structurally rather than by separate finite computations for named solids.
