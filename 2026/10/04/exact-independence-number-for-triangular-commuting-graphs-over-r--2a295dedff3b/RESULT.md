# Exact independence number for triangular commuting graphs over residue rings

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\), put
\[
R_n=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z
\]
and
\[
T_n=\operatorname{Tr}_2(R_n)
=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c\in R_n
\right\}.
\]
Let \(\Gamma(T_n)\) be the commuting graph on the noncentral elements of \(T_n\).

Then
\[
\boxed{
\alpha(\Gamma(T_n))
=
\psi(n)
=
n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac1p\right)
=
\left|\mathbb P^1(R_n)\right|,
}
\]
where \(\psi\) is Dedekind's psi function and \(\mathbb P^1(R_n)\) is the projective line of unimodular rank-one submodules.

Thus the independence-number question posed for
\[
\Gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_2(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z))
\]
has a uniform answer for every \(n\ge2\). The expression proposed with that question,
\[
n\varphi(n)(2n-\varphi(n)),
\]
is not correct in general. In particular,
\[
\alpha(\Gamma(T_4))=6,
\]
whereas the proposed expression gives \(48\).

## Assumptions and scope

The commuting graph has vertex set
\[
T_n\setminus Z(T_n)
\]
and joins two distinct vertices precisely when they commute.

A vector
\[
u=(x,y)\in R_n^2
\]
is unimodular when
\[
xR_n+yR_n=R_n,
\]
equivalently \(\gcd(x,y,n)=1\). A point of
\[
\mathbb P^1(R_n)
\]
is a submodule \(R_nu\) generated by a unimodular vector. Its unimodular generators are exactly the unit multiples of \(u\).

The theorem determines the independence number. It does not classify all maximum independent sets up to automorphism.

## Proof

For
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
d&e\\
0&f
\end{pmatrix},
\]
direct multiplication gives
\[
AB=BA
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(a-c)e-(d-f)b=0
\quad\text{in }R_n.
\]

Define
\[
\rho(A)=(a-c,b)\in R_n^2.
\]
The center consists exactly of the scalar matrices, so
\[
A\notin Z(T_n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho(A)\ne(0,0).
\]
Also
\[
AB=BA
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det(\rho(A),\rho(B))=0.
\]

For a fixed nonzero vector \(v\), the fiber \(\rho^{-1}(v)\) has \(n\) elements: choose \(c\) freely and set \(a=c+v_1\). A fiber is a clique. Hence an independent set uses at most one element from every fiber. Conversely, one matrix above each vector in a set
\[
S\subseteq R_n^2\setminus\{0\}
\]
with
\[
\det(v,w)\ne0
\qquad(v\ne w)
\]
is an independent set. Therefore
\[
\alpha(\Gamma(T_n))
=
\max\left\{
|S|:
S\subseteq R_n^2\setminus\{0\},
\ \det(v,w)\ne0\text{ for }v\ne w
\right\}.
\]

Every nonzero \(v\in R_n^2\) lies on a projective line. Write
\[
n=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{a_p}
\]
and use the Chinese remainder decomposition. In a local component
\[
(\mathbb Z/p^{a_p}\mathbb Z)^2,
\]
if \(v_p\ne0\), factor the largest common power \(p^{s_p}\) from its coordinates:
\[
v_p=p^{s_p}u_p
\]
with \(u_p\) unimodular. If \(v_p=0\), use scalar \(0\) and any unimodular \(u_p\). The Chinese remainder theorem assembles these local choices into
\[
v=\lambda u
\]
with \(u\) unimodular.

Now let \(S\) have all pairwise determinants nonzero. For each \(v\in S\), choose a projective line \(L(v)\) containing it. If two distinct vectors had the same chosen line, both would be scalar multiples of one vector, so their determinant would vanish. Thus
\[
v\longmapsto L(v)
\]
is injective and
\[
|S|\le|\mathbb P^1(R_n)|.
\]

Conversely, choose one unimodular generator from each projective line. Suppose two unimodular vectors \(u,w\) satisfy
\[
\det(u,w)=0.
\]
Because \(u\) is unimodular, it can be completed to a basis of \(R_n^2\). An invertible change of basis sends \(u\) to \((1,0)\), after which determinant zero forces the transformed \(w\) to have second coordinate \(0\). Since \(w\) remains unimodular, its first coordinate is a unit. Hence \(w\) generates the same projective line as \(u\). Therefore generators of distinct projective points have nonzero determinant. Choosing one from every point gives a determinant-independent set, and hence
\[
\alpha(\Gamma(T_n))=|\mathbb P^1(R_n)|.
\]

Finally, the number of unimodular vectors in \(R_n^2\) is
\[
n^2\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac1{p^2}\right).
\]
Each projective line has exactly
\[
\varphi(n)
\]
unimodular generators. Hence
\[
|\mathbb P^1(R_n)|
=
\frac{
n^2\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-2})
}{
\varphi(n)
}
=
n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac1p\right)
=
\psi(n).
\]
This proves the formula.

## Verification

The included replay checks the projective-line argument for every
\[
2\le n\le40.
\]
For each modulus it constructs all projective lines from unimodular vectors and verifies their exact number, verifies that every nonzero vector lies on at least one such line, verifies that vectors in one line have determinant zero, and verifies that chosen unimodular generators of different lines have nonzero determinant.

For
\[
2\le n\le10
\]
it also exhaustively checks the matrix multiplication identity against the determinant criterion on reduced representatives and two scalar lifts.

For
\[
2\le n\le6
\]
an independent branch-and-bound calculation computes the exact maximum size of a pairwise nonzero-determinant set. It returns
\[
3,4,6,6,12,
\]
matching
\[
\psi(2),\psi(3),\psi(4),\psi(5),\psi(6).
\]

The replay also checks directly that the modulus-four proposed value is \(48\), while the exact value is \(6\). It returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite computation is not used to prove the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Cheraghpour, Ghosseiri, Jafari, and Seyfpour study commuting graphs of \(2\times2\) upper triangular rings. Their 2024 paper determines the finite-field case, proves diameter \(3\) for \(\mathbb Z_n\) when it is not a field, and explicitly asks for the independence number of
\[
\Gamma(\operatorname{Tr}_2(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)).
\]
They propose
\[
n\varphi(n)(2n-\varphi(n))
\]
as a possible answer.

For a finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), their theorem gives independence number
\[
q+1.
\]
The formula proved here specializes to
\[
\psi(p)=p+1
\]
for a prime field, so it agrees with that boundary.

For composite moduli, zero divisors change the geometry. The determinant reduction shows that the relevant extremal object is the projective line
\[
\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z),
\]
and its cardinality is Dedekind's psi function. Targeted searches using the graph invariant, the proposed expression, the modular determinant formulation, projective-line terminology, Dedekind psi, and the source identifier did not locate an equivalent theorem or a later solution to the stated question.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to \(2\times2\) upper triangular matrices over \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Higher triangular size gives several coupled commutator equations instead of one determinant.

The projective-line cardinality is standard. The new mathematical content is its exact role as this commuting-graph independence number, including the upper bound for independent sets containing nonprimitive vectors.

The result does not classify all maximum independent sets.

A later or differently indexed solution to the 2024 question may exist despite the searches performed.
