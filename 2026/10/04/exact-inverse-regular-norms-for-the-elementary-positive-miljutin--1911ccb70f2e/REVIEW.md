# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The focal paper's inverse formulas give the displayed regular-norm upper bounds. For \((\Phi_E^a)^{-1}\), finitely supported sign choices make one output coordinate equal to the full finite geometric coefficient sum; its supremum is exactly \(\max\{1,1/(a-1)\}\). For \((\Psi_E^{b,c})^{-1}\), two independent finite alternating-tail inputs force respectively \(1/(b-1)\) in a sequence coordinate and \(b/[c(b-1)]\) in the \(E\)-coordinate. Since ordinary norm is bounded above by regular norm, both bounds are exact. The distortion optimizations are piecewise monotone and have the stated minima/infimum.

## Originality
PASS, with a residual folklore risk. The focal Proposition 3.1 was inspected as the primary source: it states the inverse formulas and upper bounds, but the inspected text does not state equality or the parameter-optimal local distortions. Targeted claim-level searches using the map names, exact constants and positive Banach--Mazur terminology found the focal paper and related positive-isomorphism work but no source giving these equalities. The proof is short, so an equivalent observation under different notation remains plausible.

## Value
PASS. These exact constants identify which quantitative losses in the elementary engine of the positive Cantor--Bernstein construction are intrinsic to those maps. In particular, no sharper estimate of the same inverse formulas can improve the local factor \(3\) for the \(\Phi\)-family, while the contrasting near-isometric behavior of the \(\Psi\)-family is quantified exactly. This separates local operator loss from any possible global improvement of the Miljutin constant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Laguna-Ricart--Martínez-Cervantes--Rondoš--Salguero-Alarcón, arXiv:2609.14148v1, Proposition 3.1, which supplies the maps, inverse formulas and matching upper estimates. Cúth--Havelka--Rondoš--Sarı, arXiv:2601.11463v1, and Korpalski--Plebanek, arXiv:2604.23637v1, are nearby quantitative positive-isomorphism literature but study different operators. The present claim does not assert global sharpness of \(9+6\sqrt3\).
