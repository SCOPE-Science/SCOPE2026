# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Uniqueness of the maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid forces invariance under the full transverse orthogonal symmetry of the half-cone. This reduces every possible optimizer to three scalar parameters. The slab and cone constraints are exact; the support-function maximum of the conic linear forms is computed in closed form. After saturating the transverse radius, differentiation shows that the volume is strictly increasing in the axial semiaxis until the slab constraint is tight, then strictly increasing in the center parameter up to \(s=1/2\) and strictly decreasing afterward. The unique optimizer is exactly the source's displayed \(E_n\).

Risk: the argument relies on the standard uniqueness of the John ellipsoid and on the source's independently proved normalization \(J(K_n)=B_2^n\). Both are stated and proved in the inspected source.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the bibliographic risk below. The fresh source defines the same cone and ellipsoid, proves only \(E_n\subset K_n^-\), and explicitly says that no maximality assertion about \(E_n\) in the half-cone is needed. Its finite-dimensional section gives general upper estimates but does not identify the best prescribed-dimension constant. Targeted published-finding corpus and literature searches found no exact John-ellipsoid formula for this retained half-cone.

The closest older source is Güler--Gürtuna, which develops the symmetry machinery for extremal ellipsoids and treats truncated second-order cones on the covering side. Its introduction explicitly states that the corresponding inscribed problem for that second class is not solved there. The historical 1988/1990 sources credited with the cone limit were not independently read line by line, so a residual bibliographic risk remains.

## Value
PASS. The canonical family is the sharpness mechanism for the newly proved dimension-free Khachiyan constant. Showing that its exhibited ellipsoid is not merely feasible but actually John gives the exact finite-dimensional ratio of that witness in every \(n\ge2\). This creates a concrete benchmark for the source's still-open prescribed-dimension problem without claiming global fixed-dimensional optimality.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is Zhou--Zou--Liu, arXiv:2609.28447v1, especially Sections 4, 5.1, and 7.4, and Güler--Gürtuna, arXiv:0709.0707v1. The result is restricted to the stated circular half-cone. It does not determine the fixed-dimensional optimum over arbitrary convex bodies.
