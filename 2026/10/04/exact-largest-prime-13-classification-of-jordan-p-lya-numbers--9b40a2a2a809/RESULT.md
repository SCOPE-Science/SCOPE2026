# Exact largest-prime-\(13\) classification of Jordan–Pólya numbers
## Finding
For a positive integer \(n\) whose largest prime divisor is \(13\), write \(e_p=\nu_p(n)\) for \(p\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}\) and define the prime-factorial coordinates \((c_1,\ldots,c_6)\) by \[c_1=e_2-e_3-2e_5-e_{13},\quad c_2=e_3-e_5-e_7-e_{11}-e_{13},\quad c_3=e_5-e_7-e_{11},\quad c_4=e_7-e_{11},\quad c_5=e_{11}-e_{13},\quad c_6=e_{13}.\] Put \(r_0=\max(0,-c_3)\) and \(m=\min(c_4,c_6)\). Then \(n\) is a Jordan–Pólya number if and only if \[c_5\ge0\quad\text{and}\quad m\ge\max\!\left(0,-c_2,r_0,\left\lceil\frac{3r_0-c_1}{2}\right\rceil\right).\] Whenever this holds, a canonical representation is \(n=(2!)^{a_1}(3!)^{a_2}(5!)^{a_3}(7!)^{a_4}(11!)^{a_5}(13!)^{a_6}(14!)^{r_0}(15!)^{m-r_0}\), where \(a_1=c_1+2m-3r_0\), \(a_2=c_2+m\), \(a_3=c_3+r_0\), \(a_4=c_4-m\), \(a_5=c_5\), and \(a_6=c_6-m\). Equivalently, this entire largest-prime-\(13\) stratum is generated by \(2!,3!,5!,7!,11!,13!,14!,15!\).

This gives an exact membership test and a deterministic factorization for the first prime-support stratum in which prime factorials alone do not generate every Jordan–Pólya number.

## Assumptions and scope
A Jordan–Pólya number is a positive integer that is a finite product of factorials. Throughout, \(n\) has largest prime divisor exactly \(13\), so \(e_{13}\ge1\). Any factorial factor \(k!\) occurring in a representation of such an \(n\) must satisfy \(k\le16\), because \(17!\) and every larger factorial contain the prime \(17\).

Let \(A\) be the \(6\times6\) matrix whose columns are the valuation vectors of \(2!,3!,5!,7!,11!,13!\) at the primes \(2,3,5,7,11,13\). It is upper triangular with diagonal entries \(1\), hence unimodular. The displayed \(c_i\) are exactly the integral coordinates \(A^{-1}e\).

## Proof
In the prime-factorial coordinate basis, every \(k!\) with \(2\le k\le13\) has nonnegative coordinates and is generated by the six prime factorials. The two new coordinate vectors are
\[
u=(1,-1,-1,1,0,1)\quad\text{for }14!,\qquad v=(-2,-1,0,1,0,1)\quad\text{for }15!.
\]
Moreover \(16!\) has coordinate vector \(v+(4,0,0,0,0,0)\), equivalently \(16!=15!(2!)^4\). Thus the largest-prime-\(13\) Jordan–Pólya exponent semigroup is exactly
\[
\mathbb N^6+\mathbb N u+\mathbb N v.
\]

Write \(c=a+ru+sv\), where \(a\in\mathbb N^6\) and \(r,s\in\mathbb N\), and set \(t=r+s\). Coordinatewise nonnegativity of \(a\) is equivalent to
\[
 c_1+2t-3r\ge0,\quad c_2+t\ge0,\quad c_3+r\ge0,\quad c_4-t\ge0,\quad c_5\ge0,\quad c_6-t\ge0.
\]
Hence \(r\ge r_0=\max(0,-c_3)\), while \(t\le m=\min(c_4,c_6)\), and also
\[
 t\ge0,\qquad t\ge-c_2,\qquad t\ge r,\qquad t\ge\left\lceil\frac{3r-c_1}{2}\right\rceil.
\]
For fixed \(c\), the lower bounds involving \(r\) are nondecreasing in \(r\), so the least feasible choice is \(r=r_0\). Therefore a feasible pair exists exactly when the criterion in the Finding holds. If it holds, taking \(t=m\), \(r=r_0\), and \(s=m-r_0\) gives the six nonnegative exponents \(a_i\) stated there, proving necessity, sufficiency, and the canonical representation.

## Relationship to prior work
De Koninck, Doyon, Razafindrasoanaivolala, and Verreault define Jordan–Pólya numbers, introduce the submonoid generated by prime factorials, prove uniqueness of prime-factorial representations inside that submonoid, and explicitly note that \(14!\) lies outside it. Their paper gives global counting bounds, not a fixed-largest-prime-\(13\) membership criterion. Luca studies factorials that themselves factor into smaller factorials; that Diophantine problem supplies closely related identities but does not state this semigroup classification. Erdős and Graham study products of factorials and the number of distinct products, including the distinct-factorial problem, rather than the unrestricted fixed-support semigroup characterized here. OEIS A001013 supplies the standard sequence definition but no such criterion.

A previous fixed-support result establishes that prime factorials give a complete normal form only through largest prime \(11\) and identifies \(14!\) as the first obstruction at support \(13\). The present result closes the entire next stratum by showing exactly how the two new generators \(14!\) and \(15!\) repair the semigroup and by eliminating their coefficients to a necessary-and-sufficient valuation inequality.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to integers whose largest prime divisor is exactly \(13\). It does not classify the next support, where additional generators appear. The literature search did not locate an equivalent fixed-support criterion, but the 1976 factorial-product literature is broad and only portions relevant to its stated main results were materially inspectable; an unindexed equivalent formulation remains a residual originality risk.
