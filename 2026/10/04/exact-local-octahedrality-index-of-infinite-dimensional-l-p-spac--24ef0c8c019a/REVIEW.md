# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the definition of \(s(X)\). Infinite-dimensional sigma-finite \(L_p\)-spaces admit disjoint positive finite-measure supports, which gives the universal lower bound \(2^{1/p}\). Clarkson's inequalities give the sharp upper bounds on the two sides of \(p=2\). Atomlessness supplies an exact half-\(|x|^p\)-mass sign split for every unit vector, while a single atom supplies a unit vector reducing all competitors to a monotone one-variable expression. The cases \(p=1\) and \(p=2\) were checked separately at the boundaries.

## Originality
PASS. The principal public source introducing \(s(X)\) was inspected at the definition, Proposition 3.4, and the subsequent benchmark examples. It lists exact values for \(\mathbb R\), \(c_0\), \(\ell_\infty\), finite-dimensional sup-norm spaces, and Hilbert spaces, but not this \(L_p\) classification. The later Köthe-Bochner source was inspected at Theorem 3.1; it proves qualitative LOH/LASQ stability and does not compute \(s(X)\). Focused web and semantic-database searches for equivalent formulations, Clarkson exponents, and atomic/atomless variants found no stronger covering statement. Residual literature risk remains because differently named local moduli may exist.

## Value
PASS. The result computes a natural quantitative invariant on the fundamental \(L_p\) family and exposes an exact atomicity phase transition for \(p>2\). The distinction is structural: atomless spaces attain the larger Clarkson value \(2^{1-1/p}\), whereas one atom forces \(2^{1/p}\). This sharpens the qualitative LOH/non-LOH dichotomy and extends the previously recorded Hilbert benchmark in a nontrivial direction.
