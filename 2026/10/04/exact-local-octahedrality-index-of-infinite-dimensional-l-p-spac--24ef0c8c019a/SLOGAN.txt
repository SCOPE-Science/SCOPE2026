For every infinite-dimensional real \(L_p(\mu)\), Hardtke's local-octahedrality index is exactly \(2^{1/p}\) for \(1\le p\le2\); for \(p>2\) it equals \(2^{1-1/p}\) precisely in the atomless case and drops to \(2^{1/p}\) as soon as one atom is present. Thus the quantitative local-octahedrality index detects atomicity above the Hilbert exponent.
