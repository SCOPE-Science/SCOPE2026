# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For tensor data the two-parameter truncated operator factors, so the principal-value limit is \(h_1(x)h_2(y)\). Compact support and the \(C^1\) homogeneous kernel give the uniform far-field expansion \(h_i(x)=M_iK_i(x)+O(|x|^{-d_i-1})\). After scaling, dominated convergence gives \(t\,|\{|h_i|>t\}|\to A_i=|M_i|d_i^{-1}\int|\Omega_i|\). The product distribution is then an exact layer-cake calculation: two index-one tails give \(A_1A_2\lambda^{-1}\log(1/\lambda)\), while the high-amplitude pieces are only \(O(\lambda^{-1})\) because one-parameter Calderón-Zygmund \(L^p\) boundedness gives the required truncated first moments. The proof explicitly excludes zero masses and does not infer the limit from finite experiments.

## originality

PASS

The recent rough-product source's accessible primary abstract states \(L^p\) boundedness for \(1<p<\infty\) under product \(H^1\) angular hypotheses, not a \(p=1\) distribution asymptotic. The 2025 Cowling-Lee-Li-Pipher article was read in open full text: it emphasizes failure of weak type \((1,1)\) and proves global hyperweak \(L\log L\) bounds for broad product operators, but does not determine the exact small-level coefficient for factorized tensor data. Targeted Resultary searches returned no distinct exact coefficient theorem. The full 2026 rough-product manuscript was not retrievable in this run after the open route failed and the authorized institutional route returned an operational error; that access limitation is retained as a residual risk rather than treated as negative evidence.

## value

PASS

Product singular integrals are a canonical setting where the logarithmic endpoint loss is known to be unavoidable. Determining the exact leading coefficient for a natural infinite smooth factorized class explains the endpoint scale quantitatively and supplies a sharp benchmark beyond comparability or upper bounds.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
