## correctness

PASS

The orthogonal diagonal coordinates have Jacobian \(\sqrt3\) when parameterized by the sample mean t and two orthogonal contrasts, and \(S^2=(\xi_1^2+\xi_2^2)/2\), so the regular tube cross-section has area \(2\pi y\). If both endpoint exponents exceed \(2/3\), \(f^3\) is integrable and dominated localization yields the stated \(2\sqrt3\pi\int_0^1f^3\) coefficient. At a critical endpoint, \(f(t)^3\sim B^{-3}t^{-1}\), and integration from the \(\sqrt y\) corner scale contributes exactly \(\sqrt3\pi B^{-3}y\log(1/y)\) per critical endpoint. Below threshold, scaling \(X_i=\sqrt y\,u_i\) gives the power \(y^{3m/2}\) and the corner integral. In simplex/radial coordinates the only tail divergence occurs near the simplex center, where the integrand behaves as \(r^{1-3m}\), giving finiteness exactly for \(m<2/3\); faces are integrable for m>0. The inspected checker verifies the Royen critical normalization for \(\operatorname{Beta}(2/3,1)\) and representative exponent boundaries, while the universal claim rests on the analytic localization proof.

## originality

PASS

Royen's Theorem 3 already contains the one-sided pure-power threshold \(np=n-1\), the subcritical exponent, and the critical logarithm, so those mechanisms are not credited as new. The full 2026 Osan--Chu--Yu paper treats the same n=3 geometry and explicitly derives the arcsine exponent \(3/4\), but quotes its lower-tail constant only numerically and does not state the general two-endpoint Beta competition law. The audited claim survives narrowly as the exact transfer to arbitrary \((\alpha,\beta)\): regular \(f^3\) coefficient, exact critical endpoint-count coefficient, exact corner-integral constant below threshold, and the rule selecting or adding endpoint contributions.
