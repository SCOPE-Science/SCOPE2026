# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the exact diagonal coordinates for three observations. The Jacobian and the normalization \(S^2=(\xi_1^2+\xi_2^2)/2\) give the regular tube constant. Critical logarithmic growth follows from an endpoint diagonal density proportional to \(t^{-1}\), and subcritical scaling follows from \(X_i=\sqrt y\,u_i\). The simplex form of \(J_m\) shows finiteness exactly for \(m<2/3\). The \(\operatorname{Beta}(2/3,1)\) coefficient agrees exactly with Royen's pure-power theorem after converting \(Q=2S^2\).

## Originality
PASS, with narrow scope. Royen (2008) already covers the one-sided pure-power phase transition and critical logarithm, so those are explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Osan, Chu, and Yu (2026) give the motivating three-observation geometry and singular arcsine example. The assessed surviving statement is the exact two-sided \(\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)\) lower-tail phase diagram for \(n=3\), including the regular coefficient, critical endpoint count, subcritical integral constant, and endpoint competition/addition rule. Claim-level database searches and direct primary-source comparisons did not locate that full statement.

## Value
PASS. The theorem turns an isolated singular-parent phenomenon into a sharp parameter-space classification. It specifies when the ordinary linear small-variance law survives and gives exact constants in every regime, information that is directly relevant to tail approximation and numerically stable evaluation for singular Beta parents.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Royen (2008), Theorem 3, which is strong prior coverage and exactly matches the critical \(\operatorname{Beta}(2/3,1)\) specialization after normalization. The 2026 motivating preprint treats the same statistic and singular parents but not the full two-parameter theorem. A broader regular-variation theorem could exist under different terminology; this remains the principal originality risk. No second-order expansion, remainder rate, or general closed form for \(J_m\) is claimed.
