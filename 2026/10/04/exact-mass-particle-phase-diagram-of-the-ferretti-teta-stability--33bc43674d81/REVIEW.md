# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof starts from the exact threshold in Ferretti–Teta and makes the substitution \(r=(\varsigma+1)^{-1}\). Strict monotonicity follows from an explicit positive derivative. Endpoint limits and strictness handle all equality cases in the particle-count and all-\(N\) criteria. The critical scalings are obtained only after proving the relevant roots tend to \(r=0\), so the Taylor expansions are used within their justified asymptotic regime. The numerical checker is corroborative only.

## Originality
**PASS.** The primary preprint and version of record give \(\gamma_c\), its mass-ratio infimum/supremum, and the sufficient condition \(\gamma>\gamma_c\). The inspected text does not state the exact fixed-\(\gamma\) particle-number ceiling, the criterion for one finite mass to work for every finite \(N\), or the two square-root critical mass laws. Formula/alias/thermodynamic searches and a comparison with a later different contact-interaction model did not reveal a stronger implication. A related doctoral thesis was located but not available in full and remains a residual risk. The nearest previously indexed finding concerns a different three-boson model and does not imply this claim.

## Value
**PASS.** The result turns the paper's pointwise threshold formula into a quantifier-sensitive phase diagram. The value \(2/\pi\) separates a regime where the theorem can certify only finitely many particle numbers at fixed \(\gamma\) from a regime where one finite mass ratio can certify every finite particle number. The exact ceiling and the critical mass divergences quantify a natural many-body stability question rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ferretti–Teta itself, which is necessarily a premise because it supplies the threshold. Its stated mass-ratio extrema do not by themselves contain the all-\(N\) fixed-mass classification or critical scaling. The later Ferretti–Teta all-boson contact-interaction paper has a different interaction structure. The related doctoral thesis was not fully accessible, so no whole-document non-coverage claim is made for it. Most importantly, this finding classifies the source theorem's sufficient condition and does not prove necessity for physical stability.
