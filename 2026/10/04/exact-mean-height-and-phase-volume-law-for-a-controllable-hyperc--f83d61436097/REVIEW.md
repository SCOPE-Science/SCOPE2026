# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(R=m y^2+(z-c)^2\), direct substitution gives \(\dot R=-2b(z-c)\) with complete cancellation of the \(mxy(z-c)\) terms. Integration gives the finite-time mean formula exactly. Boundedness makes the endpoint term sublinear. Invariance of a compactly supported probability measure gives zero mean generator on \(R\), hence \(\int z\,d\mu=c\). The vector-field divergence is exactly \(-z\), so Liouville's determinant formula and the standard ergodic Lyapunov-sum identity give \(-c\). No finite simulation is used as proof.

## Originality
PASS. The introducing article gives the same ODE, reports the divergence \(-z\), and describes \(c\) only as being almost positively correlated with the offset of \(z\); it does not state or prove the exact cohomological balance, exact invariant-measure mean, finite-time determinant identity, or exact Lyapunov-sum law. Searches for the DOI, the equation, “time average,” “mean z,” “offset z,” and “Lyapunov sum” found no source covering this statement. Closest published mathematical findings concern analogous balance laws in different flows and do not imply this system-specific identity. The 2014 three-dimensional precursor is a plausible source for a fixed-parameter special case, but its accessible abstract does not state such a law and its full text was unavailable in the inspected lawful sources; this is retained as a residual risk rather than treated as negative evidence.

## Value
PASS. The result converts a parameter-control observation that the source presents numerically as an approximate correlation into an exact theorem valid across all bounded regimes and compact stationary states, including coexisting attractors. It simultaneously fixes an exact phase-volume rate and supplies a nontrivial consistency constraint on Lyapunov computations. The fact is structurally tied to the source's offset-control parameter and is not an arbitrary slice or recomputation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest object-level source is the introducing paper itself, DOI 10.3390/sym12040550. The earlier Cang–Wang–Chen–Jia three-dimensional precursor, DOI 10.1007/s11071-013-1101-7, is structurally related but does not, in the accessible material, cover the four-dimensional parametric law. The theorem does not establish boundedness, existence of chaos or hyperchaos, or the individual Lyapunov exponents.
