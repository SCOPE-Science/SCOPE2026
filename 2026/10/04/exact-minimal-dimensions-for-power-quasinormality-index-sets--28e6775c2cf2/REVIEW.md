# Review of Exact minimal dimensions for power-quasinormality index sets

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof reconstructs the finite-dimensional block decomposition at the first exponent \(m=dq\). Proposition 2.4 gives \(T=\begin{bmatrix}A&X\\0&B\end{bmatrix}\) with \(A\) injective, \(A^*X=0\), and \(B^m=0\). Finite dimensionality makes \(A\) invertible, hence \(X=0\). The injective sector has an index set \(e\mathbb N\), and agreement with the target above \(m\) forces \(e=d\). Excluding \((q-1)d\) then forces \(B^{(q-1)d}\ne0\), producing the exact nilpotent-dimension lower bound. All four boundary regimes are checked separately. The upper constructions use the source's explicit \(S_d\), the Jordan-block calculation, and the direct-sum intersection identity.

## Originality
**PASS.** The focal full text was inspected through Proposition 2.4, Theorem 2.10, Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.3, and Example 3.9. It gives existence in dimension \(dq+2\) but does not state minimality; a full-text search found no occurrence of "minimal". Targeted semantic searches for minimal matrix dimension, dimension-optimal realizations, arithmetic-tail index sets, and Jordan-block realizations returned no covering statement. The closest older paper by Ko and Lee concerns structural and local spectral properties and operator matrices, not the exact minimum dimension for a complete index set. Residual risk remains for an older equivalent consequence phrased in the language of \(n\)-power normal matrices.

## Value
**PASS.** The source completely classifies which arithmetic tails occur but leaves the size of a smallest finite-dimensional witness open. The exact minimum is therefore a natural invariant of that classification. The proof also identifies why the optimum has two independent costs: periodicity requires a nontrivial invertible sector of dimension \(2\) when \(d>1\), while the cutoff requires a nilpotent chain of length \((q-1)d+1\). This is a complete natural finite classification rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Zhou–Yang, arXiv:2609.09775v1, especially its complete index-set theorem and Example 3.9. Ko–Lee, DOI 10.2298/FIL2311371K, is earlier background on the same operator class. The result is only for complex matrices and does not classify every minimizer.
