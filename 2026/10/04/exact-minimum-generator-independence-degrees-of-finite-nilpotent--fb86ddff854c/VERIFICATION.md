The universal argument is symbolic and does not rely on finite enumeration.

Its critical steps are:
- \(G=\prod_pG_p\) is generated by a tuple exactly when every Sylow projection is generated;
- Burnside's basis theorem turns that condition into spanning each \(G_p/\Phi(G_p)\);
- with \(d=d(G)\), a fixed pair can be completed by \(d-2\) further vectors exactly in the three rank regimes stated in `RESULT.md`;
- quotient partner counts multiply across primes and each quotient point has \(|\Phi_p|\) lifts;
- if \(g_p\notin\Phi_p\), then \(|\langle g_p\rangle\cap\Phi_p|=|g_p|/p\), which supplies the exact order bound needed for divisibility.

The packaged checker `artifacts/verify.py` performs direct group-theoretic replay for
\[
C_2^2\times C_3,\quad
D_8\times C_3,\quad
D_8\times C_3^2,\quad
C_4\times C_2\times C_3.
\]
For every element it computes the degree in \(\Gamma_2\) by explicit subgroup generation and compares it with the theorem.

A separate exhaustive quotient check treats
\[
\mathbf F_2^3\times\mathbf F_3^2
\]
at generator rank \(3\): it searches every possible third vector to determine pair extendability, independently recovering the predicted degree counts.

The replay returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite checks do not prove the universal theorem and no claim is made outside the finite nilpotent class.
