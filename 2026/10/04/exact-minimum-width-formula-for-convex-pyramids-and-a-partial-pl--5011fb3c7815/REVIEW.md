# Same-model review
## Correctness
PASS. In coordinates with the base in height zero and the apex at height \(h\), the width in a tilted direction is exactly \(rb+\max\{ra,h\sqrt{1-r^2}\}\). Its branch point, concavity before the branch point, and monotonicity afterwards give the displayed global formula. Strict separation handles an apex projection outside the base. The symmetric altitude criterion follows by applying the oriented inequality to both \(e\) and \(-e\). The partial-plank consequence uses the published Minkowski-subtraction bound and exact homothety of a base truncation.

## Originality
PASS. Statement-level searches covered support-function, thickness, altitude, pyramid, and partial-plank formulations. The 2026 catalyst manuscript was read through its main reduction and Section 5 discussion; it assumes the relevant simplex altitude equals minimum width rather than characterizing that condition. The directly relevant 2008 paper *On pyramids and reducedness* was also inspected; it proves a non-reducedness theorem via antipodality and projections and does not state the formula here. Residual risk remains that an equivalent elementary formula exists in older unindexed width literature.

## Value
PASS. The result turns a geometric hypothesis singled out by a current partial-plank problem into an explicit necessary-and-sufficient support-function test, valid for arbitrary convex bases. It simultaneously computes the pyramid's minimum width in the natural apex-over-base regime and identifies a higher-dimensional class for which the exact partial-plank optimum is immediate and sharp.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Bakaev--Polyanskii, arXiv:2607.27483v1: its Section 5 treats simplices subject to \(h=w(S)\), and its Corollary 7/Lemma 8 provide the plank bound used here. Averkov--Martini (2008) is the closest older pyramid/minimum-width paper inspected. No closed formula is asserted here when the apex projection is outside the base; only \(w(K)<h\) is proved in that case. The covered-volume consequence uses prior plank machinery and is not claimed as a separate new mechanism.
