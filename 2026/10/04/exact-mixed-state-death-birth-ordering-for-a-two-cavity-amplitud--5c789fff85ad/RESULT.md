# Exact mixed-state death–birth ordering for a two-cavity amplitude-damping pair

## Finding

Consider the two-cavity extended Werner-like state
\[
\rho_{r,\theta}
=
r|\Phi_\theta\rangle\langle\Phi_\theta|
+
\frac{1-r}{4}I,
\qquad
|\Phi_\theta\rangle
=
\cos\theta|00\rangle+\sin\theta|11\rangle,
\]
with
\[
0<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2},
\qquad
0<r\le1.
\]
Each cavity interacts only with its own initially empty reservoir through the zero-temperature amplitude-damping dynamics used in the source. Let
\[
\eta=e^{-\kappa t}
\]
be the excitation-survival probability, and set
\[
A=\frac{1-r}{4},
\qquad
b=\sin^2\theta,
\qquad
c=\sin\theta\cos\theta.
\]

The exact cavity and two-reservoir concurrences generated by the physical local channel are
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm c}(\eta)
=
2\eta
\left[
rc-A(2-\eta)-rb(1-\eta)
\right]_+
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm r}(\eta)
=
C_{\mathrm c}(1-\eta).
}
\]

The state is initially entangled exactly when
\[
r>
r_{\mathrm c}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{1+4c}.
\]
For every such state define
\[
\alpha(r,\theta)
=
\frac{2A+r(b-c)}{A+rb}.
\]
Initial entanglement implies \(\alpha<1\).

If
\[
0<\alpha<1,
\]
there is one finite cavity death time and one finite reservoir birth time:
\[
\boxed{
t_{\mathrm D}
=
-\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\alpha,
\qquad
t_{\mathrm B}
=
-\frac{1}{\kappa}\log(1-\alpha).
}
\]
They satisfy
\[
t_{\mathrm B}<t_{\mathrm D}
\iff
\alpha<\frac12,
\]
\[
t_{\mathrm B}=t_{\mathrm D}
\iff
\alpha=\frac12,
\]
and
\[
t_{\mathrm B}>t_{\mathrm D}
\iff
\alpha>\frac12.
\]

Solving the equality gives the exact mixed-state simultaneous boundary
\[
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm s}(\theta)
=
\frac{3}
{3+8\sin\theta\cos\theta-4\sin^2\theta}.
}
\]
Using \(u=\tan\theta\), this is
\[
r_{\mathrm s}
=
\frac{3(1+u^2)}{3+8u-u^2},
\]
so
\[
0<r_{\mathrm s}\le1
\]
in the relevant entangled region exactly when
\[
0<u\le2.
\]
For
\[
0<u<2,
\]
the simultaneous boundary lies strictly above \(r_{\mathrm c}\). Within the finite-threshold regime,
\[
r<r_{\mathrm s}
\]
means reservoir birth after cavity death, and
\[
r>r_{\mathrm s}
\]
means reservoir birth before cavity death. At
\[
u=2,\qquad r=1,
\]
the two events are simultaneous, recovering the pure-state boundary of López, Romero, Lastra, Solano, and Retamal.

There is also a second, distinct boundary. For
\[
0<\theta\le\frac{\pi}{4},
\]
define
\[
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm E}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}
{1+2(\sin\theta\cos\theta-\sin^2\theta)}.
}
\]
At
\[
r=r_{\mathrm E},
\]
one has \(\alpha=0\). For
\[
r\ge r_{\mathrm E},
\]
the cavity concurrence is positive at every finite time and tends to zero only asymptotically, while the reservoir concurrence is positive for every
\[
t>0.
\]
Thus the mixed problem has not only an ordering transition but also a sharp boundary beyond which finite sudden-death and finite sudden-birth thresholds disappear.

For the source's illustrative angle
\[
\theta=\frac{\pi}{3},
\]
the simultaneous boundary is
\[
r_{\mathrm s}
=
\frac{\sqrt3}{2}
=
0.8660254037\ldots.
\]
Hence the frequently illustrated values
\[
r=0.6
\quad\text{and}\quad
r=1
\]
lie on opposite sides of the exact ordering boundary.

The mixed matrices printed in the source lead to a different simultaneous curve. If those printed formulas are treated literally, their cavity and reservoir concurrence thresholds are complementary roots of the same quadratic, and simultaneity occurs at
\[
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm s}^{\mathrm{print}}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}
{1+2\sin\theta\cos\theta-\sin^2\theta}.
}
\]
For
\[
u=\tan\theta,
\qquad
0<u<2,
\]
the difference is
\[
\boxed{
r_{\mathrm s}^{\mathrm{print}}-r_{\mathrm s}
=
\frac{u(2-u)(1+u^2)}
{(1+2u)(3+8u-u^2)}
>0.
}
\]
Thus there is an open parameter wedge
\[
r_{\mathrm s}<r<r_{\mathrm s}^{\mathrm{print}}
\]
where the physical channel predicts reservoir birth before cavity death while the printed frozen-noise formulas predict birth after death.

At
\[
\theta=\frac{\pi}{3},
\]
the corrected and printed simultaneous values are, respectively,
\[
0.8660254037\ldots
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
0.8960369510\ldots.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The result uses the \(|\Phi_\theta\rangle\) family only. The two reservoirs start in vacuum, the two cavity-reservoir interactions are independent, and the single-cavity reduced dynamics is the zero-temperature amplitude-damping channel associated with
\[
|1\rangle|0\rangle_{\mathrm r}
\longmapsto
\sqrt\eta\,|1\rangle|0\rangle_{\mathrm r}
+
\sqrt{1-\eta}\,|0\rangle|1\rangle_{\mathrm r}.
\]

The conclusions concern concurrence between the two cavities and concurrence between the two effective one-excitation reservoir qubits.

The parameter \(r\) is the mixing weight in the extended Werner-like state. It is sometimes called a purity parameter in the source literature, but it is not numerically equal to the density-matrix purity except at special points.

## Proof

At time zero the only nonzero off-diagonal entry is
\[
\rho_{00,11}=rc,
\]
and the diagonal entries are
\[
\rho_{00,00}=A+r(1-b),
\qquad
\rho_{01,01}=\rho_{10,10}=A,
\qquad
\rho_{11,11}=A+rb.
\]

Apply the local amplitude-damping channel to both cavities. The evolved cavity state remains an X state with
\[
\rho_{11,11}(t)
=
\eta^2(A+rb),
\]
\[
\rho_{01,01}(t)
=
\rho_{10,10}(t)
=
\eta\left[A(2-\eta)+rb(1-\eta)\right],
\]
and
\[
\rho_{00,11}(t)
=
rc\,\eta.
\]
The remaining diagonal element follows from the trace.

For an X state with only the \(00\)-\(11\) coherence nonzero, concurrence is
\[
C
=
2\left[
|\rho_{00,11}|
-
\sqrt{\rho_{01,01}\rho_{10,10}}
\right]_+.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
C_{\mathrm c}(\eta)
=
2\eta
\left[
rc-A(2-\eta)-rb(1-\eta)
\right]_+.
\]

The Stinespring isometry above exchanges the cavity survival weight \(\eta\) with the reservoir excitation weight \(1-\eta\). Tracing out the cavities therefore gives exactly
\[
C_{\mathrm r}(\eta)
=
C_{\mathrm c}(1-\eta).
\]

The bracket in \(C_{\mathrm c}\) is affine in \(\eta\):
\[
rc-2A-rb+\eta(A+rb)
=
(A+rb)(\eta-\alpha),
\]
where
\[
\alpha
=
\frac{2A+r(b-c)}{A+rb}.
\]
Thus, when
\[
0<\alpha<1,
\]
cavity entanglement is present exactly for
\[
\eta>\alpha.
\]
Since \(\eta=e^{-\kappa t}\), this yields
\[
t_{\mathrm D}
=
-\kappa^{-1}\log\alpha.
\]
The reservoir concurrence is positive exactly when
\[
1-\eta>\alpha,
\]
so
\[
t_{\mathrm B}
=
-\kappa^{-1}\log(1-\alpha).
\]
The ordering follows immediately from monotonicity of the logarithm.

The simultaneous condition is
\[
2\alpha=1.
\]
After inserting \(A=(1-r)/4\), it becomes
\[
3-r(3+8c-4b)=0,
\]
which gives \(r_{\mathrm s}(\theta)\).

To locate this boundary, put \(u=\tan\theta\). Then
\[
r_{\mathrm s}
=
\frac{3(1+u^2)}{3+8u-u^2}.
\]
The inequality
\[
r_{\mathrm s}\le1
\]
is equivalent to
\[
u\le2.
\]
Moreover,
\[
r_{\mathrm s}>r_{\mathrm c}
\]
for every \(u>0\) for which \(r_{\mathrm s}\) is positive, because the cross-multiplied difference is
\[
4u(1+u)>0.
\]

Finally,
\[
\alpha\le0
\]
is equivalent, for
\[
0<\theta\le\frac{\pi}{4},
\]
to
\[
r\ge
\frac{1}
{1+2(c-b)}.
\]
At equality the concurrence vanishes only in the infinite-time limit; above equality the affine bracket is already positive at \(\eta=0\), so the same asymptotic conclusion remains true.

The mixed-state formulas printed by Zhang and Xia add
\[
A=\frac{1-r}{4}
\]
unchanged to every diagonal entry throughout the evolution. That would keep the maximally mixed component invariant. The amplitude-damping channel is not unital: for one qubit,
\[
\frac{I}{2}
\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}
1-\eta/2&0\\
0&\eta/2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
which differs from \(I/2\) unless \(\eta=1\). Hence the printed mixed-state evolution cannot be the reduced dynamics generated by the stated cavity-reservoir Hamiltonian when \(r<1\). Applying the channel to the complete density matrix gives the formulas proved here.

For comparison, the printed cavity threshold satisfies
\[
rb\eta^2+r(c-b)\eta-A=0.
\]
The printed reservoir threshold is obtained by replacing \(\eta\) with \(1-\eta\). Simultaneity therefore places the common positive root at \(1/2\), giving
\[
r_{\mathrm s}^{\mathrm{print}}
=
\frac{1}{1+2c-b}.
\]
With \(u=\tan\theta\),
\[
r_{\mathrm s}^{\mathrm{print}}
=
\frac{1+u^2}{1+2u},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm s}
=
\frac{3(1+u^2)}{3+8u-u^2}.
\]
Subtracting gives the strictly positive difference displayed in the finding for every \(0<u<2\). Therefore the discrepancy changes the event ordering on an open region; it is not merely a different time normalization.

## Verification

`verify_mixed_death_birth.py` constructs the two local Stinespring isometries directly, evolves the full cavity-plus-reservoir density matrix, and takes both partial traces.

On deterministic grids of \(r\), \(\theta\), and \(\eta\), it compares those partial traces to the analytic cavity matrix and its complementary reservoir matrix. It independently recomputes X-state concurrence, checks the unique thresholds on both sides of each transition, checks the exact simultaneous boundary, and verifies the non-unital action on the maximally mixed state.

The finite computation is supplementary. The classification itself follows analytically from the affine concurrence bracket.

## Relationship to prior work

López, Romero, Lastra, Solano, and Retamal introduced the cavity-reservoir death-versus-birth problem for initially pure Bell-like states. They proved that reservoir entanglement can be born before, simultaneously with, or after cavity entanglement dies. The pure-state simultaneous point recovered here is therefore prior work.

Zhang and Xia extended that question to mixed extended Werner-like initial cavity states and emphasized the role of the mixing parameter. Their printed mixed-state reduced matrices, however, leave the white-noise component invariant even though their stated cavity-reservoir interaction generates a non-unital amplitude-damping channel. The present result is a correction and exact completion for the physical channel generated by that Hamiltonian, not a claim to the qualitative death-versus-birth phenomenon.

Shan and collaborators independently treated mixed extended Werner-like states under a completely positive trace-preserving amplitude-damping Kraus map. After exchanging the sine and cosine convention in their Bell-like state, their cavity-side evolution agrees with the cavity reduction used here. Their paper analyzes the two-qubit system-side loss of entanglement, not the exact mixed cavity-versus-reservoir ordering boundary or the complementary two-reservoir classification proved here.

Targeted literature searches for mixed cavity-reservoir sudden-birth ordering, exact simultaneous-purity boundaries, complementary amplitude-damping Werner-like phase diagrams, and corrections to the Zhang-Xia mixed formulas did not locate the theorem above.

## Limitations

The theorem does not address the second extended Werner-like family based on single-excitation Bell states.

Finite-temperature reservoirs, correlated reservoirs, non-Markovian reservoirs, direct cavity-cavity coupling, and local driving require different channels and are outside the claim.

The correction is about consistency with the Hamiltonian and channel written in the source. It does not assert that the source's printed mixed-state formulas cannot define some separate phenomenological interpolation.

A residual literature risk remains that an equivalent mixed cavity-reservoir ordering boundary appears in a later paper under a different parameterization or Bell-state convention.
