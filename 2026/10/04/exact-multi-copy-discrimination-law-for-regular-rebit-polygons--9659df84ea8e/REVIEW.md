# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof decomposes the weighted circulant Gram matrix into mutually orthogonal cyclic characters. The character multiplicity in residue class \(r\) is exactly the fair-binomial mass \(p_r\), and pure geometrically uniform state discrimination makes the pretty-good measurement optimal. The fixed-\(N\) expansion follows from the dominant conjugate Fourier modes and a controlled Taylor expansion. The asymptotic extremal statement then follows from the multiple quantum Chernoff theorem and the exact packing radius of \(N\) points on the real projective circle. The finite checker is corroborative only.

## Originality
PASS. The closest 2026 sources state the non-aliasing value for \(N>k\) or \(N\ge k+1\) and leave the complementary regime outside their analytic treatment. Broader GU papers supply PGM optimality and representation machinery, but the inspected statements do not give the binomial-residue spectrum, the arbitrary-\(N,k\) formula, or the sharp fixed-\(N\) prefactor. Ke Li's theorem supplies the generic multiple-Chernoff exponent; the regular-polygon extremal specialization is separated from that prior theorem. Targeted semantic searches found no covering result. Residual risk remains from older cyclic-frame or phase-shift-keying terminology.

## Value
PASS. The missing \(N\le k\) regime is explicitly motivated by the recent literature, and the new formula gives a complete exact benchmark rather than a small numerical extension. Its sharp error asymptotic and the unique fixed-ensemble Chernoff exponent clarify why the regular polygon is asymptotically distinguished, while leaving the stronger finite-copy global conjecture intact.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are arXiv:2604.26647v1 and arXiv:2604.26927v1 for the regular rebit polygon, arXiv:2501.12376 and arXiv:1507.04737 for broader GU discrimination theory, and arXiv:1508.06624 for the multiple Chernoff theorem. The result does not prove finite-copy global optimality among all rebit ensembles, and it does not cover unequal priors, mixed states, local measurements, or complex-qubit ensembles.
