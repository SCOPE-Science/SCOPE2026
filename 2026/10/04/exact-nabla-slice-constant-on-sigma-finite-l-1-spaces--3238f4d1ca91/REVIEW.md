# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is proved directly from the slice representation of real \(L_1(\mu)\) for \(\sigma\)-finite \(\mu\). The upper bound uses an atom attaining the largest \(|f|\)-mass and gives both an exact slice estimate and an explicit equality witness. The lower bound exhausts the high-level set of an arbitrary avoiding slice into atomic and atomless cases. The endpoint cases \(m(f)=0\) and \(m(f)=1\) are checked separately, so the discontinuity at a normalized atom is not hidden by the interior formula. Exact-rational finite-dimensional replay agrees with all asserted bounds.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the residual search risk stated below. The 2023 \(\nabla\)-point paper gives the qualitative scalar \(L_1\) classification but no quantitative avoiding-slice constant. The 2023 quantitative paper defines Daugavet and \(\Delta\)-constants using all slices and slices containing the point, not slices avoiding the point. Its atomic \(L_1\) formula therefore does not cover the normalized-atom endpoint, where the Daugavet constant is \(0\) but the new avoiding-slice constant is \(2\). The 2024 vector-valued function-space paper was also inspected; its scalar \(L_1\) discussion is qualitative.

## Value
PASS. The invariant is the exact quantitative counterpart of the defining slice condition for \(\nabla\)-points, complements the existing Daugavet and \(\Delta\)-constants, and compresses the complete scalar \(L_1\) behavior into one natural atomic statistic. The endpoint jump distinguishes normalized atomic \(\nabla\)-points from Daugavet points quantitatively, so the result is not merely a relabeling of the known atomic Daugavet formula.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are arXiv:2303.07037v1, arXiv:2307.10647v1, and arXiv:2410.04706v1. The theorem is restricted to real scalar \(\sigma\)-finite \(L_1\) spaces. Targeted searches did not reveal a quantitative \(\nabla\)-constant under the same or nearby terminology, but this does not prove bibliographic uniqueness.
