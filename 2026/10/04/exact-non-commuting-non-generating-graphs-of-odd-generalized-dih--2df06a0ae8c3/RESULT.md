# Exact non-commuting non-generating graphs of odd generalized dihedral groups

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order
\[
m=|A|,
\]
and let
\[
G=\operatorname{Dih}(A)=A\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
\qquad
 tat=a^{-1}
\]
for every \(a\in A\). Define
\[
g(A)=|\{a\in A:\langle a\rangle=A\}|,
\qquad
b(A)=m-1-g(A).
\]
Thus \(g(A)=\varphi(m)\) when \(A\) is cyclic and \(g(A)=0\) otherwise.

Then \(Z(G)=1\), and the non-commuting, non-generating graph \(\Xi(G)\) has the following exact form.

- A rotation \(x\in A\setminus\{1\}\) is isolated precisely when \(\langle x\rangle=A\). Every other nonidentity rotation has degree \(m\).
- Every reflection has degree \(2b(A)\).
- Every non-generator rotation is adjacent to every reflection.
- The induced graph on the \(m\) reflections is
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(A,S),
\qquad
S=\{a\in A\setminus\{1\}:\langle a\rangle\ne A\}.
\]

Consequently
\[
\boxed{|E(\Xi(G))|=\frac{3mb(A)}2}.
\]

If \(A\) is noncyclic, then \(b(A)=m-1\) and therefore
\[
\boxed{\Xi(G)\cong \overline{K}_{m-1}\vee K_m}.
\]
In particular, for noncyclic odd abelian kernels the graph depends only on \(|A|\), not on the isomorphism type of \(A\).

If \(A=C_m\), identify the reflections with \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). Two reflections are adjacent exactly when their difference is not a unit modulo \(m\). Hence their induced graph is the complement of the unitary Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\).

Finally, \(\Xi(G)\) is edgeless exactly when \(A\) has prime order. In every other case the non-isolated subgraph \(\Xi^+(G)\) is connected with diameter \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope

For a finite nonabelian group \(X\), the graph \(\Xi(X)\) has vertex set
\[
X\setminus Z(X),
\]
and two vertices are adjacent when they do not commute and do not generate \(X\).

The kernel \(A\) is assumed abelian of odd order. Oddness is essential for the particularly clean reflection calculation: squaring is an automorphism of \(A\), distinct reflections never commute, and the generalized dihedral center is trivial.

No cyclicity assumption is imposed on \(A\).

## Proof

Write the reflections as
\[
r_a=at,
\qquad a\in A.
\]
Because \(|A|\) is odd, the only element of \(A\) equal to its inverse is the identity. A central element of \(A\) would therefore have to be fixed by inversion, and no reflection can centralize all of \(A\). Thus
\[
Z(G)=1.
\]

Two rotations commute, so there are no edges inside \(A\setminus\{1\}\).

Let \(x\in A\setminus\{1\}\). Since
\[
r_a x r_a^{-1}=x^{-1}\ne x,
\]
\(x\) does not commute with any reflection. Moreover
\[
\langle x,r_a\rangle
\]
is a generalized dihedral group on the cyclic subgroup \(\langle x\rangle\); in particular it has order
\[
2|\langle x\rangle|.
\]
Therefore
\[
\langle x,r_a\rangle=G
\]
if and only if
\[
\langle x\rangle=A.
\]
It follows that a generator rotation is isolated, while every non-generator rotation is adjacent to all \(m\) reflections.

Now consider two distinct reflections. Their products in the two orders are
\[
r_a r_b=ab^{-1},
\qquad
r_b r_a=ba^{-1}=(ab^{-1})^{-1}.
\]
Since \(|A|\) is odd and \(a\ne b\), the element \(ab^{-1}\) is nontrivial and is not its own inverse. Hence distinct reflections never commute.

The subgroup generated by them is
\[
\langle r_a,r_b\rangle
=
\langle ab^{-1},r_b\rangle,
\]
which is the dihedral group on the cyclic subgroup \(\langle ab^{-1}\rangle\). Hence
\[
\langle r_a,r_b\rangle=G
\]
if and only if
\[
\langle ab^{-1}\rangle=A.
\]
Thus the reflection vertices form the Cayley graph
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(A,S),
\qquad
S=\{c\in A\setminus\{1\}:\langle c\rangle\ne A\}.
\]
Its degree is
\[
|S|=b(A).
\]

A reflection has, in addition, one neighbor for every non-generator nonidentity rotation, of which there are \(b(A)\). Hence every reflection has total degree
\[
2b(A).
\]

There are \(b(A)\) active rotations, each incident with \(m\) mixed edges. The reflection graph contributes
\[
\frac{mb(A)}2
\]
edges. Therefore
\[
|E(\Xi(G))|
=
mb(A)+\frac{mb(A)}2
=
\frac{3mb(A)}2.
\]

If \(A\) is noncyclic, no element generates \(A\), so \(g(A)=0\) and \(b(A)=m-1\). The reflection graph is then \(K_m\), while the \(m-1\) nonidentity rotations form an independent set joined to every reflection. Thus
\[
\Xi(G)\cong\overline{K}_{m-1}\vee K_m.
\]

If \(A=C_m\), a difference generates \(A\) exactly when it is a unit modulo \(m\). Hence the reflection graph is the complement of the unitary Cayley graph.

Finally, \(b(A)=0\) exactly when every nonidentity element generates \(A\), which occurs exactly for cyclic groups of prime order. This is precisely the edgeless case. If \(b(A)>0\), choose an active rotation \(x\). Every reflection is adjacent to \(x\); any two active rotations have every reflection as a common neighbor; and any nonadjacent pair of reflections has \(x\) as a common neighbor. Therefore \(\Xi^+(G)\) has diameter \(2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Freedman's 2022 preprint, later published in *Algebraic Combinatorics*, studies the same graph \(\Xi(G)\) for finite non-simple groups and proves general component and diameter alternatives. The full text discusses Frobenius groups and includes computational examples for some dihedral groups, but does not state the generalized-dihedral graph decomposition above.

Earlier work of Cameron, Freedman, and Roney-Dougal establishes strong connectivity and diameter results for nilpotent groups. Generalized dihedral groups with nontrivial odd kernel are nonnilpotent, so that theorem does not imply the present classification.

The exact formula here is stronger than a connectivity statement: it identifies every edge, every isolated vertex, both vertex degrees, the total number of edges, a complete join form for all noncyclic kernels, and a unitary-Cayley complement for cyclic kernels.

Targeted searches using non-commuting non-generating graph, generalized dihedral, dihedral, Frobenius, reflection, rotation, and Cayley-graph terminology did not locate this exact classification.

## Limitations

The kernel must have odd order. For even-order kernels, central involutions and commuting reflection pairs alter both the vertex set and the adjacency criterion.

The theorem concerns generalized dihedral groups, not arbitrary Frobenius groups with noncyclic complements.

The literature search did not establish an exhaustive theorem-level absence result across every possible terminology. In particular, an equivalent statement could exist under specialized Cayley-graph or generalized-dihedral language not captured by the searches.
