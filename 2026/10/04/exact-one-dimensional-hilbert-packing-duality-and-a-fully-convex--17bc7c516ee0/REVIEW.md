# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The Hilbert cross-ratio on an interval is explicitly straightened by an increasing logarithmic coordinate. Under this coordinate, each new convexified-separated point must enlarge the span of the previous coordinates by at least the separation parameter, which proves the upper bound; equally spaced monotone coordinates attain it. The same argument proves the ordinary packing formula. The reversed-polar diameter identity follows by direct cancellation of the four endpoint differences. Substitution into Arya–Mount's Lemma 4.3 proves the diagonal bound. The proof is quantified for every admissible interval pair and every positive separation parameter; finite computations are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS. The lead source defines the diagonal quantity, proves only a mixed product estimate, and states the fully convexified form as Problem 5.1. It does not state the one-dimensional exact packing formula or the same-scale reversed-polar identity. Claim-focused published-finding corpus and web searches found no statement implying the combined claim. The classical cross-ratio coordinate is acknowledged as prior background rather than claimed as new.

Closest literature: Arya–Mount, arXiv:2608.05533v2; Artstein–Milman–Szarek–Tomczak-Jaegermann, arXiv:math/0407238 / DOI 10.1007/s00039-004-0486-3.

Residual risk: an older, unindexed Hilbert-geometry note could contain the interval packing equality, although no checked source states its diagonal-product consequence.

## Value

PASS. Dimension one is a natural base case for the source's dimension-free fully convexified diagonal-product question. The exact formula identifies the mechanism eliminating the ordinary-packing factor and supplies a sharp benchmark rather than a routine numerical instance. The result does not claim progress in dimensions at least two.
