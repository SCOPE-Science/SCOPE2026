# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For square \(D=s^2\), the Kronecker class \((D/p)=-1\) is empty by definition. The onset classification follows from the exact first terms \(U_2=P\), \(U_3=P^2-Q\), \(U_4=P^3-2PQ\), regularity \(\gcd(P,Q)=1\), and the observation that a prime dividing both \(s\) and \(U_3\) must be \(3\). The only remaining pure-\(3\)-power case at \(U_3\) is \((P,Q)=(\pm1,-2)\), where \(U_4=\pm5\). The finite verifier independently matches the theorem throughout its stated test box.

## Originality
PASS with residual bibliographic risk. The primary paper states Conjecture 3.1 for all regular Lucas sequences with \(D>0\) and does not separate square discriminants in the inspected text. Semantic searches for square-discriminant Lucas entry-point bias, exact onset \(2/3/4\), and the \(U(1,-2)\) exception returned no statement implying this theorem. General Lucas references cover Binet formulas and ranks of apparition but not this bias-boundary classification.

## Value
PASS. This is a motivated boundary theorem for a newly formulated conjecture: it shows that an infinite parameter subfamily included in the conjecture is not a genuine two-class Chebyshev race at all, and it gives the sharp universal onset classification rather than merely observing eventual triviality. It therefore cleanly identifies the nonsquare-discriminant condition needed for the conjecture's nontrivial content.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ross--Shen--Cai, arXiv:2512.03468, Section 3. Standard Lucas-sequence literature supplies the recurrence, Binet form, and rank-of-apparition framework. No claim is made about nonsquare positive discriminants, and absolute novelty cannot be certified by search alone.
