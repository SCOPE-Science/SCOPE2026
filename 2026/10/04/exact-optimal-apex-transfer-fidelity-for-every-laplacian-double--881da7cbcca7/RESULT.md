## Assumptions and scope

The graph is finite, simple, and unweighted.  The Hamiltonian is the combinatorial Laplacian, not the adjacency, normalized-Laplacian, or signless-Laplacian Hamiltonian.  The claim concerns transfer between the two conical vertices of \(\overline{K_2}ee H\).  No regularity, connectivity, or spectral assumption is imposed on \(H\).

## Relationship to prior work

Alvir, Dever, Lovitz, Myer, Tamon, Xu, and Zhan proved that the Laplacian double cone \(\overline{K_2}ee H\) has perfect state transfer between its conical vertices exactly when \(|V(H)|\equiv2\pmod4\).  Their paper also gives the general join-amplitude identity from which the displayed three-frequency amplitude follows.  The finding here does not reclaim either fact.  It optimizes that amplitude globally for every remaining congruence class and obtains a closed sharp fidelity deficit.

Later work on state transfer in graph joins and on transfer between graph hubs develops broader structural and perfect-transfer criteria.  The targeted searches and inspected sources did not state the above all-time maximum or the two exact non-perfect deficits.  The result should therefore be viewed as a quantitative completion of the double-cone perfect-transfer dichotomy, not as a new theory of Laplacian state transfer.

## Limitations

The conclusion is special to the two-apex double cone and the combinatorial Laplacian.  Weighted cones, adjacent apexes, other graph joins, and other Hamiltonians can have different critical sets.  The originality claim is intentionally restricted to the closed optimum and deficit formula; the amplitude identity and the perfect-transfer congruence are prior work.  A residual literature risk remains because the optimization is elementary once the prior amplitude formula is written down.
