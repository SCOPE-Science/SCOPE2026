# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof starts from exact geometric expansions of \(1/\Phi_m\) in the upper and lower half-planes and obtains the Fourier transform as \((2i)^{-m}(T_-+(-1)^mT_+)\). For odd \(m\), the boundary values subtract and the Sokhotski--Plemelj jump leaves only pole-supported terms; for even \(m\), they add and give the nonzero regular function \(2(2i)^{-m}/\Phi_m\) off the pole set. The quadratic-square-root generators supply the required \(1/|k|\) Diophantine lower bounds, so all residue coefficients have polynomial growth. The weighted-simplex count proves the density asymptotic. The \(m=3\) normalization exactly reproduces the published coefficients, and the packaged checker replays the sign and coefficient calculations.

## Originality

PASS with a stated access risk. The recent source arXiv:2608.09354v1 proves one three-generator example. Targeted searches for arbitrary factor count, higher delta derivatives, principal-value formulations, and odd/even support behavior found no covering theorem. The closest broad classifications concern strict Fourier quasicrystals or crystalline measures, not a family with origin derivatives in odd cases and full Fourier support in even cases. A detailed public reading of the source speculates that larger odd factor counts should produce higher derivatives, but explicitly labels this as an editorial extension and does not state the exact parity theorem, even-factor obstruction, or coefficients. Direct retrieval of the primary PDF failed, so a hidden equivalent remark remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The mathematical gap is structural: why does the three-sine construction localize its Fourier transform on reciprocal arithmetic progressions, and what happens if the construction is enlarged? The answer is a sharp parity law. Odd factor count gives a boundary jump and therefore an arbitrarily high odd-density hierarchy; even factor count gives a boundary sum and necessarily full Fourier support. This identifies both a mechanism for extension and a hard boundary of that mechanism.

## Closest literature and limitations

Tselishchev's 2026 preprint is the direct three-factor source. Olevskii--Ulanovskii and Gonçalves provide the closest classification context, but their hypotheses and Fourier-output classes do not contain this parity statement. The theorem is restricted to the explicit square-root generator family; no claim is made for arbitrary irrational generators.
