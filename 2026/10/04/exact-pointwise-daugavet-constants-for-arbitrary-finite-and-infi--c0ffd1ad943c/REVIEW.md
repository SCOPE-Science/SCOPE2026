# Review of Exact pointwise Daugavet constants for arbitrary finite and infinite \(\ell_p\)-sums

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim was reconstructed from the slice definition of the Daugavet constant. For an arbitrary dual slice, the normalized dual-coordinate profile \(b\in S_{{\ell_q}}\) produces \(c_\gamma=b_\gamma^{{q-1}}\in S_{{\ell_p}}\). Daugavet witnesses in every active summand give a point in that same slice whose distance is at least \(\|a+c\|_p\) in the limit. The scalar optimization is exact because \(F(t)=(1+t)^p-t^p\) is increasing, and a coordinate-supported slice gives the matching upper bound. The argument covers zero coordinates, unattained infima, singleton families, and arbitrary index cardinality.

Risk retained: the result depends essentially on the real-scalar Daugavet slice characterization and on \(1<p<\infty\); no endpoint extension is inferred.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest inspected primary source is Choi--Jung, arXiv:2307.10647. Its Section 4 gives lower stability estimates for absolute sums and an upper bound for axis points in \(\ell_p\)-sums, but the inspected statements do not give the arbitrary-point formula. Haller--Langemets--Lima--Nadel--Rueda Zoca, arXiv:2005.02045, gives the global thickness identity \(\mathcal T^s=2^{{1/p}}\) for two Daugavet summands; that global infimum does not imply a pointwise profile. Focused searches for coordinate-profile, direct-sum, and exact-constant aliases found no stronger covering statement. Previously established nearby results were compared at statement level; the closest one records a global multi-summand thickness value, not this localized formula.

Residual risk: a differently phrased pointwise direct-sum theorem outside the inspected sources may exist. The review therefore supports non-coverage by the checked literature rather than claiming exhaustive world uniqueness.

## Value
**PASS.** The Daugavet constant was introduced specifically as a localized quantitative refinement of global thickness indices. The formula resolves that local invariant on an entire natural class, identifies the smallest coordinate as the complete finite-sum parameter, and reveals a structural infinite-cardinality collapse: every sphere point of an infinite \(\ell_p\)-sum has exactly the same nonmaximal Daugavet constant. It also explains the known global \(2^{{1/p}}\) thickness value as the infimum of a strictly richer pointwise profile.
