# Review of Exact power-weight critical exponent for real degenerate parabolic Riesz theory

## Correctness

PASS. The primary theorem states, for real coefficients,
\[
p_-(\mathcal H)<q
\iff
\omega\in RH_{q'/2}.
\]
For the negative power \(|x|^\beta\), the exact reverse-Hölder condition is
\[
|x|^\beta\in RH_s
\iff
\beta s>-n.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
q>\frac{2n}{2n+\beta}.
\]
The same number is independently forced by local finiteness of \(\mu_{q'}\), so the infimum is exactly the claimed value. For nonnegative powers the source's \(RH_\infty\) example gives \(p_-=1\). The dual exponent and Sobolev-transition formulas are elementary algebra and are replayed by the packaged checker.

## Originality

PASS. The primary paper contains all structural ingredients but leaves the power-weight information in parameterized reverse-Hölder form. It does not state the closed critical-exponent formula, the coincidence with the dual-measure local-integrability barrier, or the exact boundary example
\[
\beta=-\frac{n^2}{n+2}
\]
where \(p_-=2_*\) although \(RH_{1+2/n}\) fails.

Exact-source and implication-level searches for the power law, the critical \(\beta\)-surface, and the equivalent reverse-Hölder formulation found no covering published result. The closest database hits concerned unrelated weighted endpoint phase transitions. Earlier unweighted parabolic theory covers only \(\beta=0\), while weighted elliptic theory does not define the same non-autonomous parabolic resolvent invariant.

The residual risk is that an author note or later exposition could spell out this immediate but structurally useful specialization without being indexed by the searches.

## Value

PASS. Power degeneracies are the canonical concrete examples in the source itself. The finding turns an implicit family of reverse-Hölder tests into a complete phase diagram for the source's central lower exponent, identifies its exact local-integrability mechanism, and pinpoints the parabolic Sobolev transition.

The boundary case is not merely a numerical substitution: it shows that in the real-coefficient power family the sufficient endpoint hypothesis \(RH_{1+2/n}\) is not necessary for the conclusion \(p_-\le2_*\). It also gives immediately usable explicit strong Riesz and dual reverse-inequality ranges for every power in the full \(A_2\) interval.
