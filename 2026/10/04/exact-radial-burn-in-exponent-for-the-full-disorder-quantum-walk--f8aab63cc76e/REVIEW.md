# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Rescaling the published reduced Riccati map gives exactly \(w_{n+1}=e^{-i\chi_n}(w_n+s)/(1+s w_n)\). The disk-automorphism identity gives the logarithmic defect recursion. Full disorder makes each new angle Haar-uniform conditional on the previous state, and Jensen's circle integral makes the logarithmic denominator a bounded martingale difference. The martingale strong law yields the almost-sure exponent \(\log(1-s^2)=2\log(\cos\theta)\). The standalone replay checks the algebraic identity, the zero Haar average, telescoping, and representative finite-horizon slopes.

## Originality

PASS, with residual risk. The 2018 source identifies the invariant circle, says disorder makes it uniformly populated, and supports stationarity by long-run histograms after a discarded burn-in. It does not state that angular Haar uniformity is exact after at most two updates or give a radial burn-in exponent. Dorsch–Schulz-Baldes treat random Möbius dynamics and radial invariant measures in perturbative regimes, but the inspected full text does not state this exact full-disorder quantum-walk law. Dedicated published-finding corpus and web searches returned no matching statement.

## Value

PASS. The source's exact localization-length argument invokes a stationary limiting circle and empirically discards a substantial burn-in before measuring it. The finding replaces that qualitative/numerical burn-in picture with an exact finite-step angular statement and a sharp almost-sure radial exponent. This clarifies how rapidly the specific Riccati dynamics reaches the manifold on which the source evaluates the Lyapunov exponent.
