# Correctness
The support classification follows from neighborhood nesting: a maximal independent set can contain only a prefix of one bipartition class and the complementary suffix of the other, and an interior boundary is maximal exactly at a strict degree jump. For a fixed maximal independent set, greedy outputs it exactly when every omitted vertex has an earlier selected neighbor. In a chain graph these requirements split across two disjoint vertex blocks, so their probabilities are independent. Each block is evaluated by conditioning on the earliest active vertex, which yields the stated finite recurrence for \(\Phi\). Exact-rational brute force matched the complete support and every probability on all 127 connected canonical chain degree sequences of total order at most eight, covering 2,754,350 vertex permutations.

# Originality
The 2015 full text arXiv:1504.03666 gives the nested-neighborhood definition and degree structure of bipartite chain graphs but contains no occurrence of “greedy”, “maximal”, or “random permutation” in targeted full-text searches. The later quasi-chain work arXiv:2104.04471 / DOI:10.1007/s00453-022-01019-6 explicitly observes that chain graphs have only linearly many inclusion-wise maximal independent sets and uses that support structure algorithmically, but the inspected chain/independent-set sections do not provide stochastic weights. DOI:10.1002/rsa.21200 studies the same random-order greedy process broadly; full-text searches found no occurrence of “chain” or “Ferrers”. published-finding corpus searches under chain, Ferrers, difference-graph, nested-neighborhood, random-greedy, and random-sequential-adsorption formulations returned no theorem covering the factorized chain-graph law. The closest stochastic hit is a complete-multipartite result, which covers complete bipartite graphs only as a strict special case. Residual risk remains from older difference/Ferrers literature that may be incompletely indexed.

# Value
The result upgrades the known small support of maximal independent sets in chain graphs to the entire exact probability measure induced by random greedy. It gives the probability of every possible greedy output and therefore every moment and the full size distribution, while retaining a direct recurrence on the Ferrers degree data. This supplies an exact benchmark on a canonical nested-neighborhood class and exposes how random greedy weights different maximal independent sets rather than merely enumerating them.
