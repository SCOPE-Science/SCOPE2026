# Exact recurrence balance and forcing-selected RMS in the sine-modified Sprott-B flow
## Finding
Consider the four-dimensional flow
\[
\dot x_1=a(x_2-x_1),\qquad
\dot x_2=kx_1\sin x_3+x_4,\qquad
\dot x_3=b-x_1x_2,\qquad
\dot x_4=-cx_2.
\]
For \(a>0\), \(c>0\), \(k>0\), and \(b\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
H=x_3+\frac{x_1^2}{2a}.
\]
Then
\[
\dot H=b-x_1^2.
\]
Therefore every bounded forward trajectory satisfies
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T x_1(t)^2\,dt=b.
\]
In particular, no bounded forward trajectory exists for \(b<0\). At \(b=0\), every recurrent point belongs to the equilibrium line \(\{(0,0,\xi,0):\xi\in\mathbb R\}\).

For \(b>0\), every compactly supported invariant probability measure \(\mu\) obeys
\[
\int x_1\,d\mu=\int x_2\,d\mu=0,\qquad
\int x_1^2\,d\mu=\int x_1x_2\,d\mu=b.
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{Var}_\mu(x_1)=b\). The support of \(\mu\) meets both half-spaces \(x_1>0\) and \(x_1<0\). Every periodic orbit therefore crosses \(x_1=0\), and its maximum satisfies \(\max |x_1|\ge\sqrt b\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vector field is the sine-modified Sprott-B system introduced by Lai, Kuate, Pei, and Fotsin. The proof allows any real \(b\) to expose the forcing-sign trichotomy, while retaining \(a>0\), \(c>0\), and \(k>0\). A bounded forward trajectory means its full state remains in a bounded subset for all positive time. A recurrent point means that some sequence of positive times tending to infinity returns its orbit arbitrarily close to the initial point. A compactly supported invariant probability measure is invariant under the complete flow and has compact support.

The conclusions for \(b>0\) are necessary conditions on compact stationary dynamics; they do not assert that such dynamics exist. The result does not rely on numerical integration, a finite parameter scan, or the periodic translation symmetry used in the source to replicate attractors.

## Proof
Differentiate \(H\) along the vector field:
\[
\dot H=\dot x_3+\frac{x_1}{a}\dot x_1
       =b-x_1x_2+x_1(x_2-x_1)
       =b-x_1^2.
\]
If a forward trajectory is bounded, then \(H(t)\) is bounded. Integrating the identity from \(0\) to \(T\) gives
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T x_1(t)^2\,dt
=b-\frac{H(T)-H(0)}{T},
\]
and the last term tends to zero. Since the left side is nonnegative, \(b<0\) is impossible for a bounded forward trajectory.

Now let \(b=0\) and let the initial point be recurrent. Then
\[
H(t)-H(0)=-\int_0^t x_1(s)^2\,ds.
\]
For recurrence times \(t_n\to\infty\), continuity gives \(H(t_n)\to H(0)\). The nondecreasing quantities \(\int_0^{t_n}x_1(s)^2\,ds\) therefore converge to zero, so \(x_1(t)=0\) for all \(t\ge0\). The first equation then gives \(x_2=0\); the second gives \(x_4=0\); and the third gives \(\dot x_3=0\). Hence every recurrent point is \((0,0,\xi,0)\), and each such point is an equilibrium when \(b=0\).

Finally let \(b>0\) and let \(\mu\) be a compactly supported invariant probability measure. For every continuously differentiable observable used below, compact support makes the Lie derivative integrable and invariance gives zero mean Lie derivative. Applying this to \(x_4\), \(x_1\), \(H\), and \(x_3\), respectively, yields
\[
0=-c\int x_2\,d\mu,
\]
\[
0=a\int(x_2-x_1)\,d\mu,
\]
\[
0=b-\int x_1^2\,d\mu,
\]
and
\[
0=b-\int x_1x_2\,d\mu.
\]
Thus the four stated moment identities follow. Since \(\int x_1\,d\mu=0\) and \(\int x_1^2\,d\mu=b>0\), \(x_1\) cannot have one sign on the support: otherwise zero mean would force \(x_1=0\) almost everywhere, contradicting the positive second moment. A periodic orbit carries its normalized time measure, so continuity forces a zero crossing. Its root-mean-square amplitude is \(\sqrt b\), hence its maximum absolute amplitude is at least \(\sqrt b\).

## Verification
The bundled script `verify.py` symbolically differentiates \(H\) and checks the algebraic identities used in the proof. It uses exact symbolic arithmetic. The analytic steps after the symbolic identity are integration, recurrence continuity, and the standard zero-mean Lie-derivative identity for compactly supported invariant measures; no numerical trajectory is used as a proof certificate.

For the source's common choice \(b=1\), every compact stationary state would therefore have exact root-mean-square \(x_1\)-amplitude \(1\), independent of \(k\). This is a necessary consistency check, not an existence assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Lai, Kuate, Pei, and Fotsin introduced this sine-modified Sprott-B vector field and numerically exhibited periodic, quasiperiodic, and chaotic behavior, including infinitely many translated copies generated by the periodic nonlinearity. Their article explicitly notes that analytic boundedness is difficult and uses simulation to support boundedness for the displayed parameter regimes. The periodic-copy mechanism is therefore prior work and is not part of the present novelty claim.

The closest inspected predecessor, Lai and Chen's 2016 modification of Sprott B, studies multiple attractors generated by a polynomial modification. The accessible inspected material for that paper was limited to its abstract and publisher-preview metadata; no assertion is made here about uninspected full-text contents. Searches for the present source together with the balance \(\dot H=b-x_1^2\), stationary mean-square laws, and forcing-selected root-mean-square amplitude did not locate prior coverage of this claim. Related exact-balance results for other named chaotic flows concern different vector fields and do not imply this identity.

## Limitations
The theorem gives necessary restrictions on bounded or compact recurrent dynamics, not a proof that the numerically displayed attractors exist. At \(b=0\) it classifies recurrent points but does not classify every bounded connecting trajectory. It does not validate numerical Lyapunov exponents, basin sizes, or the source's hidden-attractor classifications. Originality is supported by the searches and source inspections described in the review material, but unindexed or inaccessible literature remains a residual risk.
