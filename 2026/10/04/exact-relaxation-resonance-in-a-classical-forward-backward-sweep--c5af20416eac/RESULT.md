# Exact relaxation resonance in a classical forward–backward sweep counterexample
## Finding
Consider the continuous forward–backward sweep method for
\[
\min_u \frac12\int_0^1\bigl(3x(t)^2+u(t)^2\bigr)\,dt,
\qquad x'(t)=x(t)+u(t),\qquad x(0)=1.
\]
Given a control \(u^k\), let \(x^{k+1}\) solve the state equation forward, let \(p^{k+1}\) solve
\[
(p^{k+1})'(t)=-3x^{k+1}(t)-p^{k+1}(t),\qquad p^{k+1}(1)=0,
\]
and set \(\widetilde u^{k+1}=-p^{k+1}\). For the constant-relaxation update
\[
u^{k+1}=(1-\omega)u^k+\omega\widetilde u^{k+1},
\]
the exact \(L^2(0,1)\) error norm is
\[
\rho(\omega)=\max\bigl\{{|1-\omega|},{|1-4\omega|}\bigr\}.
\]
Consequently, the relaxed sweep converges for every initial control error if and only if \(0<\omega<1/2\). Its unique norm-optimal parameter is
\[
\omega_*=\frac25,
\qquad
\rho(\omega_*)=\frac35.
\]
The formal unrelaxed sweep \(\omega=1\) has an exact unstable eigenmode \(1-t\) with multiplier \(-3\). At the boundary \(\omega=1/2\), the same mode has multiplier \(-1\), while its orthogonal complement contracts with operator norm \(1/2\). Thus a nonzero component in that mode produces an asymptotic two-cycle, but averaging each pair of consecutive controls cancels the neutral mode and converges to the optimum.

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the exact continuous-time sweep on the Hilbert space \(L^2(0,1)\), with exact state and adjoint solves and with the relaxation rule displayed above. It is a statement about the modified scalar linear–quadratic example described in Remark 4.1 of McAsey, Mou, and Han (2012). It does not claim that a particular unpublished or undocumented implementation used \(\omega=1/2\), and it does not identify the spectrum of a finite-step Runge–Kutta discretization.

The problem is strictly convex in the control, so it has a unique optimal control. All convergence claims below are convergence to that unique control in \(L^2(0,1)\).

## Proof
Define the Volterra state operator
\[
(Kv)(t)=\int_0^t e^{t-s}v(s)\,ds.
\]
Its Hilbert adjoint is
\[
(K^*y)(s)=\int_s^1 e^{t-s}y(t)\,dt.
\]
Subtracting two sweeps removes the affine terms generated by the fixed initial state. If \(e^k=u^k-u^*\), then the state error is \(Ke^k\), the adjoint error is \(3K^*Ke^k\), and therefore
\[
\widetilde e^{k+1}=-3K^*Ke^k.
\]
Writing \(A=K^*K\), the relaxed error map is the self-adjoint operator
\[
R_\omega=I-\omega(I+3A).
\]

The spectrum of \(A\) is explicit. If \(Af=\mu f\) with \(\mu>0\), differentiating the integral equations gives
\[
f''+\left(\frac1\mu-1\right)f=0,
\qquad f'(0)+f(0)=0,
\qquad f(1)=0.
\]
For \(\mu=1\), this has the eigenfunction \(f_0(t)=1-t\). Directly,
\[
K(1-t)=t,
\qquad K^*t=1-t,
\]
so \(Af_0=f_0\). There is no eigenvalue \(\mu>1\): writing \(\beta^2=1-1/\mu\in(0,1)\), the boundary conditions would require \(\tanh\beta=\beta\), impossible for \(\beta>0\).

For \(0<\mu<1\), put \(k^2=1/\mu-1\). The boundary conditions reduce to
\[
\tan k=k.
\]
Thus the remaining eigenvalues are \(\mu_j=(1+k_j^2)^{-1}\), where \(k_j\) are the positive roots of \(\tan k=k\). They decrease to zero. The first positive root lies in \((\pi,3\pi/2)\), hence every nonleading eigenvalue satisfies
\[
0<\mu_j<\frac1{1+\pi^2}<\frac13.
\]
Since \(A\) is compact, positive, and self-adjoint, its spectrum is \(\{1,\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,0\}\). Therefore
\[
\|R_\omega\|
=
\max_{\mu\in\sigma(A)}|1-\omega(1+3\mu)|
=
\max\bigl\{|1-\omega|,|1-4\omega|\bigr\},
\]
because the absolute value of an affine function is convex on \([0,1]\), and both endpoints are in the spectrum (with \(0\) as a spectral limit point).

Both endpoint magnitudes are strictly below one exactly when \(0<\omega<1/2\). The minimax choice equates the two active endpoint magnitudes with opposite signs:
\[
1-\omega=-(1-4\omega),
\]
which yields \(\omega=2/5\) and factor \(3/5\); uniqueness follows from the strict V-shape of the maximum around the intersection.

At \(\omega=1\), the leading multiplier is \(1-4=-3\). At \(\omega=1/2\), it is \(-1\), while for every \(j\ge1\)
\[
0<\frac12-\frac32\mu_j<\frac12.
\]
Because \(\mu_j\to0\), the operator norm on the orthogonal complement of \(f_0\) is exactly \(1/2\). Decompose \(e^0=c f_0+g\) with \(g\perp f_0\). Then
\[
e^k=(-1)^k c f_0+R_{1/2}^k g.
\]
Hence raw iterates fail to converge whenever \(c\ne0\), but their consecutive average satisfies
\[
\left\|\frac{e^k+e^{k+1}}2\right\|_2
\le \frac34\,2^{-k}\|g\|_2,
\]
because the neutral component cancels and \(\|(I+R_{1/2})/2\|\le3/4\) on \(f_0^\perp\).

## Verification
The proof is analytic and uses only the exact integral operators above. The accompanying `verify.py` checks the identities \(K(1-t)=t\) and \(K^*t=1-t\), isolates the first positive root of \(\tan k=k\), confirms the resulting spectral separation below \(1/3\), and checks the endpoint minimax calculation \(\omega_*=2/5\), \(\rho_*=3/5\). The verifier is supplementary; the infinite-dimensional claim follows from the compact self-adjoint spectral argument, not from finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
McAsey, Mou, and Han prove sufficient convergence conditions for the forward–backward sweep method and explicitly note that those conditions can be sharpened. In their Remark 4.1 they modify their linear–quadratic example to the coefficients used here, report failure to terminate, describe alternating iterates, and state that averaging two sufficiently late iterates recovers the solution. Their paper does not give the exact error spectrum, the unstable mode \(1-t\), the sharp relaxation interval, or the optimizer \(\omega=2/5\).

Mitter's earlier successive-approximation work treats a different second-variation/Newton-like update and does not imply this source-specific spectrum. Sharp, Burrage, and Simpson later formulate FBSM as a fixed-point iteration and study generic acceleration methods; Liu and Frank analyze a regularized forward–backward sweep with sufficient regularization for convergence; and Armengou-Riera and coauthors study discretized CG/GMRES updates. These broader works motivate controlling an unstable fixed-point sweep but do not state the exact \(-3\) resonance, the \(\omega=1/2\) neutral boundary, or the sharp \(2/5\) relaxation for this continuous example.

The calculation also distinguishes two facts that can otherwise be conflated: the formal unrelaxed continuous map has multiplier \(-3\) on the top mode, whereas the explicitly defined half-relaxed map has multiplier \(-1\) and therefore exhibits the exact two-cycle/averaging mechanism. No claim is made about which relaxation was used in any implementation not documented in the cited text.

## Limitations
This is an exact result for one scalar linear–quadratic control problem and one constant-relaxation family. It does not establish an optimal relaxation rule for nonlinear FBSM, for state/control constraints, or for a particular time discretization. It also does not identify unpublished implementation details behind the numerical observation in the 2012 paper. The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed thesis, lecture note, or implementation note containing the same spectral calculation.
