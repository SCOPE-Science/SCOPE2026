# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For \(t\ge0\), every slice of the unit ball contains points of norm arbitrarily close to one, giving the universal Daugavet lower bound \(1-t\); coordinate slices with scalar coordinate tending to one give the matching upper bound. For the Delta constant, an arbitrary slice containing \((t,0)\), represented by a norm-one functional \((a,y^*)\), also contains \((t,R_tu)\) with \(R_t=(1-t^p)^{1/p}\) and \(y^*(u)\ge0\), giving the lower bound. Conversely the slices \(s>t-\eta\) reduce the distance estimate to \(F_t(s)=|s-t|^p+1-|s|^p\); on \(s\ge t\) this is decreasing and on the short left interval it converges uniformly to \(1-t^p\). Endpoints \(t=0,1\) and sign symmetry are handled separately. An independent replay for noninteger as well as integer exponents checked the scalar monotonicity and strict gap; the arbitrary-space theorem rests on the slice argument, not on those samples.

## originality

PASS

Choi and Jung introduce the quantitative pointwise constants, compute the Hilbert-space Delta formula, and prove stability inequalities for absolute sums. Their full paper does not state the exact interior scalar-axis formula for an arbitrary nonzero transverse Banach space. Haller, Pirk and Veeorg treat qualitative Daugavet/Delta points in absolute sums at the unit sphere, not these interior quantitative constants. Current Resultary search found the assigned formula and neighboring exact constants for other spaces/sums, but no stronger theorem that specializes to \(\delta c_{\mathbb R\oplus_pY}(t,0)=(1-|t|^p)^{1/p}\) for arbitrary \(Y\).

## value

PASS

These pointwise constants are newly established quantitative invariants, and \(\ell_p\)-sums are canonical Banach constructions. The exact scalar-axis formula isolates a genuinely geometry-independent transverse effect and gives a strict quantitative separation between the two constants at every interior nonzero axis point. It is a natural exact benchmark rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.
