# Exact scale phase diagram for a four-dimensional zonotope log-submodularity defect
## Finding
Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^4\), set \(d_{ij}=e_i-e_j\), and define
\[
\widetilde V=(e_1,e_2,e_3,d_{24},d_{34}),\qquad
h=d_{12}-e_3,\qquad
w=d_{14},\qquad
w'=d_{23}.
\]
For a finite list of vectors \(G=(g_1,\ldots,g_m)\), write
\[
Z(G)=\sum_{i=1}^m[0,g_i]
\]
for its zonotope. For every real \(s\ge0\), put
\[
A_s=Z(\widetilde V\mid s h),\qquad B=[0,w],\qquad C=[0,w'].
\]
Then the four relevant volumes are
\[
|A_s|=4+10s,\qquad |A_s+B|=12+18s,
\]
\[
|A_s+C|=8+18s,\qquad |A_s+B+C|=24+32s.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
\Delta(s)=|A_s|\,|A_s+B+C|-|A_s+B|\,|A_s+C|,
\]
then
\[
\Delta(s)=4s(2-s).
\]
Thus the log-submodularity inequality
\[
|A_s|\,|A_s+B+C|\le |A_s+B|\,|A_s+C|
\]
is an equality exactly for \(s=0\) and \(s=2\), fails strictly for every \(0<s<2\), and holds strictly for every \(s>2\). At \(s=1\), the volumes are \((14,30,26,56)\), the published Skorupinski counterexample.

For every integer \(d\ge4\), adjoining the standard generators in the last \(d-4\) coordinates makes each of the four zonotopes a Cartesian product with \([0,1]^{d-4}\). Its volume is therefore unchanged, so the same exact phase diagram holds in every dimension \(d\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope
All volumes are Euclidean Lebesgue volumes. The parameter is restricted to \(s\ge0\); negative scaling gives a translate of the segment generated by \(|s|h\), so the same volume statements would depend only on \(|s|\). The result concerns the displayed one-parameter deformation of a specific zonotope. Except at special parameter values, the scaled generating matrix is not asserted to be integral, unimodular, or \(2\)-modular.

The parameter is geometrically natural: deleting the special generator \(h\) gives the unimodular equality configuration highlighted in the source, while \(s=1\) gives its explicit \(2\)-modular counterexample. The theorem determines the complete sign behavior along the straight generator-scaling path between and beyond those configurations.

## Proof
For vectors \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\in\mathbb R^4\), the standard zonotope-volume formula is
\[
|Z(g_1,\ldots,g_m)|
=
\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |I|=4}
\left|\det(g_i:i\in I)\right|.
\]
Fix any one of the four generator lists obtained from \(\widetilde V\) by optionally adjoining \(w\) and \(w'\), and then adjoin \(s h\). For \(s\ge0\), every determinant not using \(s h\) is independent of \(s\), whereas every determinant using it is \(s\) times the corresponding determinant with \(h\). Therefore each volume is affine in \(s\).

Exact enumeration of the maximal minors gives the intercept and slope pairs
\[
(4,10),\qquad (12,18),\qquad (8,18),\qquad (24,32),
\]
respectively for \(A_s\), \(A_s+B\), \(A_s+C\), and \(A_s+B+C\). This proves the four displayed volume formulas. In particular, at \(s=1\) they give \(14,30,26,56\), agreeing with the source's determinant table.

Substitution now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(s)
&=(4+10s)(24+32s)-(12+18s)(8+18s)\\
&=8s-4s^2\\
&=4s(2-s).
\end{aligned}
\]
The sign classification follows immediately: the defect is positive exactly for \(0<s<2\), zero exactly for \(s\in\{0,2\}\), and negative for \(s>2\).

For \(d>4\), append the coordinate segments \([0,e_5],\ldots,[0,e_d]\) to \(A_s\) and extend \(w,w'\) by zeros. Every relevant zonotope is the Cartesian product of its four-dimensional counterpart with \([0,1]^{d-4}\), so each volume and hence \(\Delta(s)\) is unchanged.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs exact integer determinant enumeration, with no numerical optimization and no floating-point arithmetic. It independently recovers the four affine coefficient pairs and checks the identity \(\Delta(s)=4s(2-s)\) coefficient by coefficient. The script also checks the source point \(s=1\).

The computation is finite only because the proof has already reduced the all-parameter claim to affine determinant coefficients. The sign conclusion for every real \(s\ge0\) is analytic and is not inferred from sampled parameter values.

## Relationship to prior work
Skorupinski's 2026 preprint introduces exactly the vectors \(\widetilde V,h,w,w'\), observes that removing \(h\) returns to a unimodular equality case, and proves failure after appending \(h\) at unit scale by the volumes \(14,30,26,56\). It does not state the scaled family, the four affine volume formulas, the second equality at \(s=2\), or the strict restoration of log-submodularity for \(s>2\).

Fradelizi--Hubard--Manui--Ndiaye--Wang--Zvavitch independently recast zonoid log-submodularity as a Rayleigh property of zonotope volume polynomials and establish the sharp generator-count regime. Averkov--von Dichter--Soprunov likewise discuss the failure of the original conjecture in dimension at least four and prove a different log-submodularity-type inequality. These broader theories explain why a six-generator four-dimensional failure is possible, but the inspected statements do not determine the sign of the Skorupinski defect along this one-generator scaling ray.

Sra later records that discrete counterexamples are not isolated and explicitly mentions the volume vector \((14,30,26,56)\). That qualitative robustness does not give the exact maximal interval \(0<s<2\), the equality at \(s=2\), or the re-entry into the valid regime for \(s>2\).

## Limitations
This is a complete classification only along one natural deformation ray. It does not classify the full parameter region obtained by independently rescaling all generators, nor does it assert that \(s=2\) has a special matroidal or modular-matrix interpretation. The result also does not restore the original conjecture globally: it identifies a sharp sign change inside one published counterexample family.

Literature searches cannot prove absolute novelty. The strongest residual risk is that an unindexed note or computation has already written down the same scaled family. The primary and closely related recent preprints inspected below do not contain the stated phase diagram.
