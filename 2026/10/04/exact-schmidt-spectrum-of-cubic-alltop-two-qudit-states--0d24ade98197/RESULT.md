# Exact Schmidt spectrum of cubic Alltop two-qudit states
## Finding
For every prime \(d\ge5\), choose a nonsquare \(\nu\in\mathbf F_d\), let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}\), and define
\[
|\Psi_{d,\nu}\rangle=\frac1d\sum_{x,y\in\mathbf F_d}\omega^{2x^3+6\nu xy^2}|x\rangle|y\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{spec}(\rho_A)=
\left\{
\frac2d\text{ with multiplicity }\frac{d-1}{2},\quad
\frac1d\text{ once},\quad
0\text{ with multiplicity }\frac{d-1}{2}
\right\}.
\]
In particular the Schmidt rank is \((d+1)/2\), and the von Neumann entanglement entropy is
\[
S(\rho_A)=\log d-\frac{d-1}{d}\log2.
\]
The same states have stabilizer purity and order-two stabilizer Rényi entropy
\[
\Pi_2(\Psi_{d,\nu})=\frac{2d^2-1}{d^4},\qquad
M_2(\Psi_{d,\nu})=\log\!\frac{d^4}{2d^2-1}.
\]
Thus the cubic Alltop construction gives, for every prime local dimension at least five, an explicit two-qudit state on a fixed Schmidt-spectrum pattern that realizes the magic value conjectured in arXiv:2607.07197. The theorem is an achievability-and-structure result, not an upper bound on arbitrary two-qudit states.

## Assumptions and scope
The two local systems are each \(d\)-dimensional with prime \(d\ge5\). The tensor-product computational basis is indexed by \((x,y)\in\mathbf F_d^2\). A nonsquare \(\nu\) makes \(\mathbf F_{d^2}=\mathbf F_d[\theta]/(\theta^2-\nu)\). Writing \(z=x+y\theta\), the field trace satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_{d^2}/\mathbf F_d}(z^3)=2x^3+6\nu xy^2,
\]
so the displayed state is the cubic-trace Alltop vector on \(\mathbf F_{d^2}\), expressed in a bipartite \(\mathbf F_d\)-basis.

Generalized Pauli operators are the local tensor products generated by shifts and clocks. Equivalently, after identifying \(\mathbf F_d^2\) with \(\mathbf F_{d^2}\), they are indexed by a shift \(u\in\mathbf F_{d^2}\) and an additive character parameter \(v\in\mathbf F_{d^2}\). The trace pairing is nondegenerate, so this parametrization contains exactly the \(d^4\) local tensor-product Pauli operators up to irrelevant global phases.

## Proof
Let \(q=d^2\), let \(\chi(t)=\omega^{\operatorname{Tr}(t)}\), and write
\[
|\Psi\rangle=q^{-1/2}\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_q}\chi(z^3)|z\rangle.
\]
For a Pauli operator with shift \(u\) and character parameter \(v\), its expectation value differs by at most an irrelevant unit phase from
\[
q^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathbf F_q}
\chi\!\left((z+u)^3-z^3+vz\right).
\]
If \(u=0\), additive-character orthogonality gives value one for \(v=0\) and zero for every \(v\ne0\).

Suppose \(u\ne0\). The exponent is a quadratic polynomial in the two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_d\)-vector space \(\mathbf F_q\). Its quadratic part is \(\operatorname{Tr}(3uz^2)\), whose polar form is
\[
B_u(z,w)=\operatorname{Tr}(6uzw).
\]
This form is nondegenerate: if \(B_u(z,w)=0\) for every \(w\), then nondegeneracy of the field-trace pairing gives \(6uz=0\), and since \(d\ge5\) and \(u\ne0\), one has \(z=0\).

For completeness, a nondegenerate quadratic exponential sum on a two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_d\)-space has magnitude \(d\), even after adding any linear term. Indeed, if
\[
S=\sum_{z}\chi(Q(z)+L(z)),
\]
then after writing the squared modulus as a double sum and setting \(h=z-w\), the inner sum over \(w\) is an additive-character sum of \(B(w,h)\). It vanishes for every \(h\ne0\) by nondegeneracy and contributes \(d^2\) for \(h=0\). Thus \(|S|^2=d^2\). Therefore every Pauli expectation with \(u\ne0\) has magnitude \(d/q=1/d\).

There are \(d^2-1\) nonidentity operators with \(u=0\), and \(d^2(d^2-1)=d^4-d^2\) operators with \(u\ne0\). Hence
\[
\sum_P|\langle\Psi|P|\Psi\rangle|^4
=1+(d^4-d^2)d^{-4}=2-d^{-2}.
\]
Using the two-qudit normalization
\[
\Pi_2(\Psi)=d^{-2}\sum_P|\langle\Psi|P|\Psi\rangle|^4
\]
gives the claimed formulas for \(\Pi_2\) and \(M_2\).

It remains to diagonalize a reduced density matrix. Multiplying rows of the coefficient matrix by phases does not change Schmidt coefficients, so remove the factor \(\omega^{2x^3}\). The remaining coefficient matrix is
\[
B_{x,y}=d^{-1}\omega^{cxy^2},\qquad c=6\nu\ne0.
\]
Thus
\[
( BB^\dagger)_{x,x'}=d^{-2}\sum_{y\in\mathbf F_d}\omega^{c(x-x')y^2}.
\]
This is a circulant matrix in \(x-x'\). Its additive Fourier eigenvalue at frequency \(k\in\mathbf F_d\) is
\[
\lambda_k=d^{-2}\sum_{t,y\in\mathbf F_d}\omega^{t(cy^2-k)}
=\frac1d\#\{y\in\mathbf F_d:cy^2=k\}.
\]
For \(k=0\) the count is one. Among nonzero \(k\), exactly \((d-1)/2\) values make \(k/c\) a square, and each then has two square roots; the other \((d-1)/2\) values have no root. This is exactly the displayed spectrum. Summing \(-\lambda_k\log\lambda_k\) gives the entropy formula.

## Verification
The analytic proof establishes the universal statement for every prime \(d\ge5\); no finite experiment substitutes for the quadratic-sum or Fourier arguments. The accompanying standard-library checker directly evaluates every generalized Pauli expectation and the circulant Schmidt spectrum for \(d=5\) and \(d=7\). It reproduces, respectively,
\[
(2/5,2/5,1/5,0,0)
\]
and
\[
(2/7,2/7,2/7,1/7,0,0,0),
\]
and verifies the purity formula in both cases. These finite checks are corroborative only.

## Relationship to prior work
Knipfer, Roman, Matcheva, and Matchev derive the stabilizer purity of a Weyl--Heisenberg MUB fiducial and conjecture that the resulting value \(\log(d^4/(2d^2-1))\) is the global two-qudit maximum for every prime \(d\). Their two-ququint table already contains the \(d=5\) spectrum \((2/5,2/5,1/5,0,0)\) as one of six numerically found maximizing spectra, but they do not give a general formula for its continuation in \(d\).

The cubic Alltop construction itself is older. In particular, the literature records that \(z\mapsto z^3\) is an Alltop function over every finite field of characteristic at least five and that Alltop functions yield mutually unbiased bases. Therefore neither the existence of the cubic Alltop MUB nor the MUB-to-magic purity count is claimed here as new. The new structural statement is the exact bipartite Schmidt spectrum after the prime-square field is identified with two local prime-dimensional systems. It supplies an explicit all-prime continuation of the first two-ququint maximizing spectrum and places the known MUB magic value on a dimension-uniform entanglement stratum.

Targeted searches for “Alltop Schmidt spectrum”, “cubic phase Schmidt rank”, and the exact multiplicity pattern did not locate a prior statement of this bipartite spectrum. The closest directly relevant sources discuss MUB orbits, Alltop-function existence, or the recent maximal-magic conjecture separately.

## Limitations
The result proves an explicit candidate family and its exact structure. It does not prove the conjectured global upper bound on \(M_2\), does not classify all states attaining the same magic value, and does not address local dimensions two or three, where the cubic-trace nondegeneracy argument changes. The Schmidt spectrum depends on the chosen tensor factorization of the prime-square Hilbert space; the theorem fixes the natural factorization induced by a quadratic \(\mathbf F_d\)-basis. Although no covering source was found, older finite-geometry literature may contain an equivalent singular-value calculation under sequence-correlation terminology.
