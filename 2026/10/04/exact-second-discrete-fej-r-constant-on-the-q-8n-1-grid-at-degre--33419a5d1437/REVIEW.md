# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The candidate polynomial is nonnegative because its two roots are adjacent points in the strictly monotone half-grid of the cosine. The upper bound follows from an explicit positive two-node quadrature identity whose three coefficient equations are checked directly. Equality holds because the extremizer vanishes at both quadrature nodes. The trigonometric simplification to the displayed closed form is exact.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked literature. Ivanov's 2018 paper supplies the problem, the exactness criterion, several solved families, and explicitly calls for the family \(q=2(p+1)n+1\). Its solved \(q=2p+1\) family covers only the \(n=1\) member when \(p=3\). Targeted searches for \(\lambda(1,3,8n+1)\), the \(p=3\) family, the zeros \(3n,3n+1\), and the closed formula found no covering statement. The 2015 Krenedits–Révész paper supplies the general framework rather than this exact family. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased literature.

## Value
PASS. This is an infinite exact family in a direction singled out in the primary source, rather than an isolated numerical case. It gives both the extremal value and the extremizing polynomial together with the positive quadrature certificate that proves optimality.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Ivanov (2018), DOI 10.21538/0134-4889-2018-24-4-156-175. The broader framework is Krenedits–Révész (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.jat.2015.02.001. The result is limited to \(p=3\), \(q=8n+1\), \(n\ge2\), and does not assert uniqueness.
