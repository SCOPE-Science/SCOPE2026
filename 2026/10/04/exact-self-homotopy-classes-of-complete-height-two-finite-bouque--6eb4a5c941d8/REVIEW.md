# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim reduces to a complete case split on the images of the two minimal points. Every non-isolated case is explicitly comparable to a constant map; every claimed isolated map is proved to have no distinct comparable neighbor in either direction. The finite-space fence criterion then gives the complete homotopy-component decomposition. The counting formula follows from four disjoint image-type cases. The homology criterion is proved using an explicit four-cycle in the one-dimensional order complex \(K_{2,q}\), so no appeal to finite experiments is used for the all-\(q\) theorem. The bundled exhaustive verifier independently checks all maps for \(q=2,3,4\). Risk: the theorem uses the standard finite-space identification of homotopies with function-space fence components; this is sourced to the finite-space literature rather than reproved from first principles.

## Originality
PASS. The strongest same-object primary source, Barmak--Minian's *Minimal Finite Models*, classifies minimal graph models but not self-map homotopy components. Barmak--Minian's *Simple homotopy types and finite spaces* classifies maps inducing simple homotopy equivalences, a different implication. Earlier exact records for \(P_3\) and \(P_4\) classify induced \(H_1\) matrices, not ordinary self-homotopy classes. Targeted searches over complete-height-two, \(K_{2,q}\), mapping-space, homotopy-class, and homological-isolation aliases found no statement implying the all-\(q\) formula. Residual risk remains from obscure or unindexed historical literature and from the original Stong full text not being required for direct inspection.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a closed structural description of the entire self-homotopy set of an infinite family of finite spaces, not a finite census or parameter slice. It exposes a sharp intrinsic phenomenon invisible from the weak homotopy type: one large null component coexists with \(2(q^q-q)\) homologically nontrivial isolated maps. The theorem also explains and organizes previously observed small-model self-map counts while separating direct finite-space homotopy from ordinary graph homotopy.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature establishes the finite-space/poset correspondence, minimal graph models, beat-point/core homotopy theory, fence homotopies, and simple homotopy equivalences. None of those statements supplies the component formula proved here. The claim is limited to the complete height-two two-minimum family and does not assert that homology distinguishes different isolated classes.
