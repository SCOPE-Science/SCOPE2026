# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof separates surjective from nonsurjective order maps. Surjective self-maps of the finite poset are automorphisms, and the level structure gives exactly \(2^{n+1}\) of them. For a proper image, assuming every occupied target level were full forces a bijective source level over every occupied target level; the endpoint and no-gap arguments then force all \(n+1\) target levels to be full, contradicting properness. Hence a proper image has a singleton level. Removing levels toward that singleton by valid up- and down-beat deletions contracts the image, so every nonhomeomorphism is null-homotopic as a finite-space map. Minimal-space rigidity then isolates every homeomorphism. The packaged verifier exhaustively confirms all ingredients for \(n=1,2,3\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest foundational paper proves minimality/uniqueness of the sphere models and states the Stong rigidity fact, but full-text inspection found no self-homotopy classification. The function-space paper gives the compact-open/path-homotopy framework but no sphere-model census. The prior sphere-map result only concerns maps after order-complex realization, which does not imply finite-space null-homotopy. The prior complete height-two theorem covers the \(n=1\) instance, so the accepted claim explicitly starts at \(n=2\). Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches found no equivalent or stronger all-dimensional statement.

## Value
PASS. The result determines the entire ordinary self-homotopy set of the canonical minimal finite sphere models in every dimension \(n\ge2\), not merely an induced homology or degree invariant. It exposes a concrete separation between weak sphere realization and finite-space homotopy: the finite model has exponentially many isolated homeomorphism classes in addition to one null class. This is a structural mapping-space fact, rather than a routine finite census.

## Closest literature and limitations
Barmak--Minian is the closest source for the models and minimal-space rigidity; Kukieła is the closest source for the mapping-space formalism. A 2002 four-point-circle pairing paper is related to the \(n=1\) boundary but its accessible abstract does not state this result and its full text was unavailable. The theorem does not address nonminimal sphere models or higher homotopy of the mapping space.
