# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Pairing opposite cyclic samples reduces the three-term norm to a two-term root-of-unity covering problem. The angular covering radius is exactly \(2\pi/N\), the quadratic lower bound reduces to \((1-q)(a-c)^2\ge0\), and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives the sharp constant. The displayed coefficients attain equality because the even and odd parts are pointwise orthogonal and the shifted cosine grid has maximum \(\cos(\pi/N)\).

## Originality
PASS. Neuwirth's exact three-frequency theorem is for the continuous torus norm and yields \(\sqrt2\) for \(\{0,1,2\}\); it does not determine the norm sampled only at \(N\)-th roots. Graham's finite-group result concerns the entire group, while the extremal-Sidon literature concerns the boundary value \(\sqrt{|E|}\). Searches using finite-cyclic, three-character, arithmetic-progression, unconditionality, discrete-sampling, and the exact proposed formula found no equivalent statement. The \(N=4\) endpoint can also be viewed through extremal-set theory, but the all-even formula and its non-extremal \(N\ge6\) cases are not implied by that endpoint.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a closed sharp interpolation constant for an infinite family, identifies exact extremizers, and measures quantitatively how finite cyclic sampling changes the classical three-frequency Sidon problem. It is not an enumeration or numerical estimate: the formula exposes the geometric source of the sampling penalty and recovers the continuous constant in the large-order limit.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Stefan Neuwirth, arXiv:math/0102145, whose Theorem 4.3 solves the continuous-circle three-frequency problem. C. C. Graham's 1978 finite-group theorem and later extremal-set work provide broader finite-group context but do not supply this prescribed-subset constant. Odd orders and nonconsecutive triples remain outside the claim. A residual risk is an equivalent formula in older finite-group interpolation literature using different terminology.
