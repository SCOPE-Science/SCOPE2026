PASS. The local equations are reconstructed from \(g^{-1}Ng\) on the exact source chart. The restricted Jacobian is computed symbolically. Every maximal minor is divisible by \(\Delta=z_{12}-z_{23}-z_{45}+z_{56}\), one maximal minor equals \(\pm\Delta\), and a \(5\times5\) minor is identically \(1\). With the source's radical codimension-six local ideal, the Jacobian criterion proves the claimed rank dichotomy and exact singular hyperplane.

## Originality
PASS. Abe–Insko's Example 4.10 proves smoothness on \(\Delta\neq0\) and only concludes generic nonsingularity. The converse \(\Delta=0\Rightarrow\) singular is not proved there. Exact published-index searches, exact-coordinate literature searches, and inspection of the later Horiguchi–Shirato singular-locus paper did not find the hyperplane equality or a broader theorem that implies it. The main residual risk is an uncatalogued or differently coordinated equivalent computation.

## Value
PASS. Example 4.10 is introduced precisely to exhibit failure of a cellwise singularity propagation principle. Completing its singularity classification is mathematically motivated: it reveals an entire affine \(5\)-space of non-permutation singular points and gives an exact rank criterion, rather than another sampled or arbitrary invariant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Abe–Insko itself. The phrase immediately before Example 4.10 says the “rest” of the cell is nonsingular, but the displayed calculation only proves that \(\Delta\neq0\) is smooth and the example concludes only that generic points are smooth. The exact maximal-minor computation shows that the complement is singular. Horiguchi–Shirato classify singular loci in an open opposite Schubert cell for \(h_m=(m,n,\ldots,n)\), which is a different family and does not cover this cell.
