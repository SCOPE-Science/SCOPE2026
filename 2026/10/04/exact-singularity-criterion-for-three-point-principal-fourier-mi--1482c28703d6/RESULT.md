# Exact singularity criterion for three-point principal Fourier minors
## Finding
Let \(N\ge 3\), let \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/N)\), and let
\[
F_N=(\omega^{jk})_{j,k\in\mathbb Z_N}.
\]
For every three-element set \(K=\{x,y,z\}\subset\mathbb Z_N\), the principal submatrix \(F_N[K,K]\) is singular if and only if there is a pair of distinct points \(u,v\in K\), with \(w\) the third point, such that
\[
N\mid (u-v)^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N\mid (u-v)(u-w).
\]
If \(q=N/\gcd(N,u-v)\) is the additive order of \(u-v\), these two divisibilities are equivalently
\[
q^2\mid N
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
u\equiv w\pmod q.
\]
Thus every singular three-point principal minor is generated by a square-divisor scale \(q^2\mid N\): two points differ by an element of exact order \(q\), and the third lies in the same residue class modulo \(q\).

Equivalently, for each prime power \(p^e\Vert N\), one and the same pair \(u,v\) satisfies
\[
2v_p(u-v)\ge e,
\qquad
v_p(u-v)+v_p(u-w)\ge e.
\]
In particular, if \(N\) is square-free, every \(3\times 3\) principal minor is nonzero.
## Assumptions and scope
The Fourier matrix is unnormalized; multiplying it by a nonzero scalar does not affect singularity. Indices are residue classes in \(\mathbb Z_N\), and differences in the divisibility statements are represented by arbitrary integer lifts, which does not change the conditions. The result concerns principal \(3\times 3\) submatrices only; it does not classify nonprincipal minors or principal minors of larger sizes.

For the local valuation formulation, \(v_p\) is the ordinary \(p\)-adic valuation of an integer lift. Because \(u\ne v\) in \(\mathbb Z_N\), the difference is nonzero modulo \(N\); the displayed inequalities are invariant under changing lifts by multiples of \(N\).
## Proof
Translation of the index set preserves singularity. Indeed, replacing every index \(j\) by \(j+t\) multiplies the entry \(\omega^{jk}\) by nonzero row and column factors together with one common nonzero scalar. It therefore suffices to write
\[
K=\{0,a,b\},\qquad 0<a<b<N.
\]
A direct determinant expansion gives
\[
D:=\det F_N[\{0,a,b\}]
=(\omega^{a^2}-1)(\omega^{b^2}-1)-(\omega^{ab}-1)^2.
\]

First suppose \(\omega^{ab}=1\), equivalently \(N\mid ab\). Then
\[
D=(\omega^{a^2}-1)(\omega^{b^2}-1),
\]
so \(D=0\) exactly when \(N\mid a^2\) or \(N\mid b^2\). These are precisely the first two alternatives in the criterion, with the pair \(\{0,a\}\) or \(\{0,b\}\).

Now suppose \(\omega^{ab}\ne1\). Using
\[
e^{i\theta}-1=2i e^{i\theta/2}\sin(\theta/2),
\]
the equation \(D=0\) becomes
\[
\sin(\pi a^2/N)\sin(\pi b^2/N)
 e^{\pi i(b-a)^2/N}
=\sin^2(\pi ab/N).
\]
The right side is strictly positive. Hence the real sine prefactor on the left is nonzero, and its phase must be real. Therefore
\[
e^{\pi i(b-a)^2/N}=\pm1,
\]
which is equivalent to \(N\mid(b-a)^2\).

Put \(c=b-a\), \(X=\omega^{a^2}\), and \(R=\omega^{ac}\). Since \(N\mid c^2\), one has \(\omega^{c^2}=1\), hence \(\omega^{b^2}=XR^2\) and \(\omega^{ab}=XR\). Substitution into the determinant gives the exact factorization
\[
D=(X-1)(XR^2-1)-(XR-1)^2=-X(R-1)^2.
\]
Thus \(D=0\) exactly when \(R=1\), namely when \(N\mid a(b-a)\). This is the third alternative and corresponds to the pair \(\{a,b\}\). The three alternatives are therefore necessary and sufficient.

For the order-coset form, let \(d=u-v\), \(g=\gcd(N,d)\), and \(q=N/g\). Write \(d=gr\) with \(\gcd(r,q)=1\). Then
\[
N\mid d^2
\iff gq\mid g^2r^2
\iff q\mid g
\iff q^2\mid N,
\]
and
\[
N\mid d(u-w)
\iff q\mid u-w.
\]
This proves the structural formulation. The valuation formulation is simply the prime-by-prime expansion of the two divisibilities.
## Verification
The standalone script `verify_three_point_principal.py` performs exact integer and cyclotomic arithmetic. For every \(3\le N\le120\) and every normalized triple \(\{0,a,b\}\), it computes the determinant as an element of \(\mathbb Z[x]/(\Phi_N(x))\), tests the three divisibility alternatives, tests the symmetric pair criterion, and tests the additive-order/coset formulation. It also checks that no square-free modulus in the range has a singular normalized triple.

A replay reports:

`MAX_N 120`

`NORMALIZED_TRIPLES 280840`

`SINGULAR_NORMALIZED_TRIPLES 2602`

`SQUAREFREE_MODULI 73`

`VERIFY_OK`

The finite computation is corroborative only. The theorem for arbitrary \(N\) follows from the determinant identity and the complete case split in the proof.
## Relationship to prior work
Tao's prime-order uncertainty theorem explains the prime case through the nonvanishing of all Fourier minors. Frenkel gives a short proof of the classical prime-order Chebotarev theorem.

Caragea and Lee prove that when \(N\ge4\) is square-free, every \(2\times2\) and \(3\times3\) principal Fourier minor is nonzero, and that when \(N\) is not square-free there exist zero principal minors of every admissible size. Their proof of the square-free \(3\times3\) case derives the determinant identity used above and observes that singularity forces one of \(a^2\), \(b^2\), or \((b-a)^2\) to be divisible by \(N\). The inspected statement and proof do not give a necessary-and-sufficient condition for an individual triple at arbitrary composite \(N\), nor the equivalent additive-order/coset characterization. The present result supplies that local classification and recovers their square-free \(3\times3\) theorem as a corollary.
## Limitations
The result is specific to three-point principal minors. It does not decide singularity for nonprincipal \(3\times3\) Fourier submatrices or for principal minors of order at least four. The originality comparison included the closest full-text treatment of principal Fourier minors and targeted searches for equivalent divisibility, valuation, and additive-order formulations; a differently phrased or poorly indexed prior local classification remains a residual literature risk.
