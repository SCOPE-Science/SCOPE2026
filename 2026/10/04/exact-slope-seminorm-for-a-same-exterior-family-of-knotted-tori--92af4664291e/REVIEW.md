# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The tangent-coordinate inclusion sends \(a[\lambda_r]+b[s_r]\) to \(\lambda^{a-Nb}\). Nifa proves \(\lambda\) is a commutator and \(\operatorname{scl}(\lambda^N)=(N-1)/2\). Homogeneity therefore gives \(\operatorname{scl}(\lambda^{a-Nb})=(N-1)|a-Nb|/(2N)\). Liu--Ni--Sun--Wang identify this stable commutator length with their induced slope seminorm and extend it uniquely to real homology. Nifa's exact commutator-length theorem and singular-genus lemma give the stated unstable formula for primitive integral slopes when \(a-Nb\in N\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\).

## Originality
PASS. The recent source states exact singular genus for one specified surgery direction, but does not state the induced seminorm, its kernel, its unit ball, or the all-member comparison; its full text contains neither “seminorm” nor “unit ball”. The earlier foundational paper provides the general stable-commutator-length interpretation but predates this family. Targeted literature and semantic searches found no equivalent all-slope formula.

## Value
PASS. The result completely computes the stable slope invariant on a newly introduced family with arbitrarily large finite sets of inequivalent tori sharing one exterior. The calculation shows exactly why the whole stable seminorm fails to distinguish those embeddings and simultaneously identifies an index-\(N\) lattice on which exact singular genera remain available.

The exact unstable singular genus outside primitive integral slopes satisfying \(a-Nb\in N\mathbb Z\) remains unproved and is not claimed. No embedded-genus conclusion is made.
