# Exact small-market stochastic-dominance census for probabilistic serial versus random serial dictatorship
## Finding
Consider strict one-sided assignment with the same number \(n\) of agents and objects. Random serial dictatorship (RSD) chooses one of the \(n!\) priority orders uniformly and lets agents choose their best remaining object in that order. Probabilistic serial (PS) lets all agents simultaneously consume their most-preferred available object at unit speed until each has consumed one unit.

The exact head-to-head stochastic-dominance census through the first nontrivial sizes is:

- For \(n=1\), the unique profile gives the same assignment under PS and RSD.
- For \(n=2\), all \(4\) labeled profiles give the same assignment.
- For \(n=3\), among all \(6^3=216\) labeled profiles, exactly \(144\) give the same assignment and exactly \(72\) give incomparable assignments. There is no three-agent profile at which either mechanism strictly stochastically dominates the other.
- For \(n=4\), among all \(24^4=331{,}776\) labeled profiles, exactly
\[
72{,}288
\]
give the same assignment,
\[
11{,}664
\]
are profiles at which PS strictly stochastically dominates RSD, and
\[
247{,}824
\]
are incomparable. Thus the exact uniform-profile probability of PS strictly stochastically dominating RSD at \(n=4\) is
\[
\frac{11{,}664}{331{,}776}=\frac{9}{256}.
\]
The equality and incomparability probabilities are, respectively,
\[
\frac{251}{1152}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1721}{2304}.
\]
RSD never strictly stochastically dominates PS in this census, consistent with the general stochastic-dominance efficiency of PS.

Quotienting the four-agent profiles by independent relabeling of agents and objects produces exactly \(762\) classes. Their comparison types split as
\[
209\text{ equal},\qquad 36\text{ PS-dominance},\qquad 517\text{ incomparable}.
\]
The \(36\) PS-dominance classes have orbit-size distribution
\[
4\cdot72+3\cdot144+20\cdot288+9\cdot576=11{,}664.
\]
Their preference-multiplicity patterns are \(4\) classes of type \(2+2\), \(9\) of type \(2+1+1\), and \(23\) using four distinct orders.

The minimum number of distinct preference orders in a PS-dominance profile is therefore two. Exactly four symmetry classes attain the minimum, all with multiplicities \(2+2\). Writing the objects as \(a,b,c,d\), one representative order may be fixed as
\[
a\succ b\succ c\succ d.
\]
The second repeated order can, up to relabeling, be taken as one of
\[
a\succ b\succ d\succ c,\quad
a\succ c\succ b\succ d,\quad
b\succ a\succ c\succ d,\quad
b\succ a\succ d\succ c.
\]
The last of these is the standard four-agent PS-versus-RSD dominance example already present in the literature.

## Assumptions and scope
Preferences are strict, each of the \(n\) objects has unit capacity, and each agent receives one unit in total. RSD randomizes uniformly over all agent priority orders. PS uses equal unit eating speeds.

For a fixed strict preference order, lottery \(A_i\) weakly stochastically dominates lottery \(B_i\) when, for every rank cutoff, the probability under \(A_i\) of receiving an object at least as good as that cutoff is at least the corresponding probability under \(B_i\). An assignment strictly stochastically dominates another when this holds for every agent and at least one cutoff is strict.

The symmetry quotient identifies profiles under arbitrary relabeling of agents and arbitrary relabeling of objects. It does not quotient by reversing preference orders or by any transformation not generated by those relabelings.

## Proof
For each finite \(n\le4\), the claim is proved by exhaustive exact enumeration from the mechanism definitions.

For RSD, every priority order is enumerated. Each deterministic serial-dictatorship assignment contributes one count to the selected agent-object pairs; division by \(n!\) gives the exact RSD matrix.

For PS, the eating process is simulated with exact rational arithmetic. At each stage, every agent eats her top currently non-exhausted object. If object \(o\) has remaining capacity \(r_o\) and is being eaten by \(k_o\) agents, the next event occurs after
\[
\min_o \frac{r_o}{k_o}.
\]
Exact fractions are added to the corresponding assignment entries until total time reaches one.

Two assignments are then compared by every cumulative rank cutoff for every agent. For \(n=1,2,3\), every labeled profile is directly enumerated. At \(n=3\), the resulting labeled split is \(144\) equal and \(72\) incomparable; no dominance occurs.

For \(n=4\), the first exhaustive route runs over all
\[
\binom{24+4-1}{4}=17{,}550
\]
anonymous multisets of four strict rankings. Each multiset is classified exactly, weighted by the number of agent labelings, and canonicalized under all \(24\) object relabelings. This gives the labeled counts \(72{,}288\), \(11{,}664\), and \(247{,}824\), together with the \(209+36+517=762\) symmetry-class decomposition.

As a separate orbit check, Burnside's lemma is applied to the action of the \(24\) object permutations on the complete anonymous multiset list, stratified by comparison type. It reproduces exactly the same \(209\), \(36\), and \(517\) class counts. Independently walking all \(24^4\) labeled ranking-index tuples and looking up their exhaustive anonymous classification reproduces the same labeled totals.

The four minimum two-order classes are read off from the complete PS-dominance stratum. Replaying the published profile with two copies of \(a\succ b\succ c\succ d\) and two copies of \(b\succ a\succ d\succ c\) gives the published PS and RSD matrices exactly and places it in one of those four classes.

## Relationship to prior work
Bogomolnaia and Moulin introduced PS and established its stochastic-dominance efficiency while showing that random priority/RSD can fail that efficiency property. The standard four-agent dominance example is therefore prior work and is used here only as a replay anchor.

Hosseini, Larson, and Cohen directly compare RSD and PS. Their 4 March 2015 preprint gives the same standard four-agent profile where PS stochastically dominates RSD, and a three-agent profile where the two mechanisms are incomparable. Crucially for the present finite census, their experimental section states that the full profile space is too large for their study and that each agent-object data point is estimated from \(1{,}000\) uniformly sampled profiles. They formulate a conjecture about the fraction of profiles at which PS stochastically dominates RSD as the market grows.

The result here does not claim the existence of the four-agent dominance example, the three-agent incomparability example, or PS's general efficiency as new. It gives the exact exhaustive small-market calibration: the complete \(n=3\) split, the exact four-agent probability \(9/256\), the full \(209/36/517\) symmetry decomposition, and the four minimum two-order dominance classes. Targeted searches by these exact fractions, counts, symmetry strata, and mechanism names did not locate an equivalent published statement.

## Limitations
The theorem is complete only for square strict-assignment instances through \(n=4\). It does not establish the asymptotic conjecture of Hosseini, Larson, and Cohen, and it does not address unequal numbers of agents and objects, weak preferences, quotas, or alternative tie-breaking conventions.

The exact counts are computer-assisted finite mathematics. The two counting routes and exact rational arithmetic sharply constrain implementation error, but no external independent audit has been performed. Search failure is not a proof of novelty; an unindexed thesis, course note, software table, or supplementary data set may contain the same census.
