For square strict assignment, PS and RSD never dominate one another at \(n=3\): exactly \(144\) of \(216\) profiles coincide and \(72\) are incomparable. At \(n=4\), PS first strictly stochastically dominates RSD on exactly \(11{,}664\) of \(331{,}776\) profiles, probability \(9/256\), corresponding to \(36\) of \(762\) relabeling classes; exactly four minimum dominance classes use only two preference orders.
