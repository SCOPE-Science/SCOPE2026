On a one-dimensional convex Lipschitz objective with a perfectly constant startup subgradient, dual-averaging D-Adaptation cannot raise its initial distance estimate before update \(8\), while dual-averaging Prodigy first raises it at update \(5\). The exact \(8\)-versus-\(5\) cutoff quantifies a finite-time adaptation advantage of Prodigy's reweighting without making a global-rate claim.
