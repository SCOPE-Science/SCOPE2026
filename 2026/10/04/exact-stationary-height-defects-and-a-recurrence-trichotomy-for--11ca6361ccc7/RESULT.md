# Exact stationary height defects and a recurrence trichotomy for the Newton–Leipnik flow
## Finding
Consider the canonical Newton–Leipnik flow
\[
\dot x=-\frac{2}{5}x+y+10yz,\qquad
\dot y=-x-\frac{2}{5}y+5xz,\qquad
\dot z=\alpha z-5xy,
\]
with real parameter \(\alpha\).

The compact recurrent dynamics obey the following exact trichotomy.

For \(\alpha<0\), the origin is the only bounded complete trajectory; therefore \(\delta_0\) is the only compactly supported invariant probability measure. For \(\alpha=0\), the compactly supported invariant probability measures are exactly the probability measures supported on the equilibrium line \(\{(0,0,z):z\in\mathbb R}\}\).

For \(\alpha>0\), every compactly supported invariant probability measure \(\mu\) obeys the conditional product law
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[xy\mid z]=\frac{\alpha z}{5}
\quad \mu\text{-a.e.},
\]
and the two sharp height-defect identities
\[
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[\left(z+\frac1{20}\right)^2\right]
=\frac1{400}+\frac{\mathbb E_\mu[x^2]}{5\alpha},
\]
\[
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[\left(z-\frac1{10}\right)^2\right]
=\frac1{100}+\frac{2\mathbb E_\mu[y^2]}{5\alpha}.
\]
Equality in either lower bound occurs only for \(\mu=\delta_0\). Thus every other compact stationary state assigns positive mass outside each of the two slabs
\[
-\frac1{10}\le z\le0,
\qquad
0\le z\le\frac15.
\]
In particular, a nonconstant periodic orbit contained in \(z\ge0\) reaches \(z>1/5\), and one contained in \(z\le0\) reaches \(z<-1/10\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns compactly supported invariant Borel probability measures and bounded complete trajectories of the autonomous flow above. No ergodicity, physicality, mixing, or existence of a strange attractor is assumed. The parameter \(\alpha\) is allowed to be any real number, extending the usual numerical interval \(0\le\alpha\le1\) used in the early Newton–Leipnik literature.

The conditional expectation is with respect to the sigma-algebra generated by the coordinate \(z\). Compact support ensures that all polynomial observables used below are integrable and that a smooth antiderivative can be chosen for every continuous test function on the projected \(z\)-support.

## Proof
Set
\[
V=\frac12\left(x^2+y^2+3z^2\right).
\]
A direct Lie-derivative calculation gives
\[
\dot V=-\frac25x^2-\frac25y^2+3\alpha z^2.
\]
If \(\alpha<0\), then with \(\kappa=\min\{4/5,-2\alpha\}>0\),
\[
\dot V\le-\kappa V.
\]
Every nonzero complete trajectory would therefore grow at least exponentially when followed backward in time, so it cannot remain bounded. Hence the origin is the only bounded complete trajectory.

If \(\alpha=0\), invariance of a compactly supported probability measure gives \(\int\dot V\,d\mu=0\), so
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[x^2+y^2]=0.
\]
Thus \(x=y=0\) almost surely. When \(\alpha=0\), every point of the \(z\)-axis is an equilibrium, proving the stated classification.

Now assume \(\alpha>0\). For any continuously differentiable \(\Phi\), invariance gives
\[
0=\int \Phi'(z)(\alpha z-5xy)\,d\mu.
\]
Because the projected support in \(z\) is compact, this identity can be tested against an arbitrary continuous function of \(z\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[\alpha z-5xy\mid z]=0,
\]
which is the conditional product law.

Next define
\[
F_1=\frac{x^2}{2}+z^2+\frac z5,
\qquad
F_2=\frac{y^2}{2}+\frac{z^2}{2}-\frac z5.
\]
Exact differentiation yields the coboundary identities
\[
\dot F_1
=2\alpha\left[\left(z+\frac1{20}\right)^2-\frac1{400}\right]-\frac25x^2,
\]
\[
\dot F_2
=\alpha\left[\left(z-\frac1{10}\right)^2-\frac1{100}\right]-\frac25y^2.
\]
Integrating against an invariant measure proves the two height-defect formulas.

It remains to identify equality. Equality in the first defect implies \(x=0\) on the support. Invariance of the support then forces a trajectory starting there to remain in the plane \(x=0\). On that plane the equations give \(y'=-2y/5\), \(z'=\alpha z\), and the constraint \(x'=y(1+10z)=0\) must persist for all time. If \(y\ne0\), this persistence is incompatible with \(z'=\alpha z\); if \(y=0\) but \(z\ne0\), the forward trajectory \(z(t)=z(0)e^{\alpha t}\) leaves every compact set. Hence only the origin can lie in a compact invariant support. The second equality case is analogous after setting \(y=0\): persistence of \(y'=x(-1+5z)=0\), together with \(x'=-2x/5\) and \(z'=\alpha z\), again leaves only the origin.

For a non-origin invariant measure both defect inequalities are strict. If its support were contained in \(-1/10\le z\le0\), then \((z+1/20)^2\le1/400\) everywhere, contradicting the first strict defect. The second slab is excluded similarly. Applying the invariant measure of a periodic orbit gives the final periodic-orbit consequences.

## Relationship to prior work
Leipnik and Newton introduced the rigid-body system in 1981 and reported the double-attractor phenomenon. A later detailed account by Marlin reproduces the canonical equations and states that the original investigation used a coarse numerical parameter search, with \(\alpha=0.175\) singled out for the oscillatory double-attractor dynamics. Marlin also summarizes subsequent work as numerical bifurcation, return-map, symbolic-dynamics, and inverse-limit analysis rather than stationary-moment identities.

Richter's 2002 study uses the Newton–Leipnik flow as the numerical example for intra-attractor and inter-attractor chaos control, and lists the five equilibria at the standard parameter values. Later rigid-body studies continue to emphasize attractors, bifurcations, hidden dynamics, and control.

Searches for Newton–Leipnik invariant measures, stationary moments, the two centered-square identities, conditional \(xy\)-laws, and the sign trichotomy did not locate a statement implying the theorem above. The closest located results with exact invariant-measure balances concern different vector fields and do not specialize or reduce to the Newton–Leipnik equations.

## Limitations
The result constrains compact stationary states; it does not prove that a strange attractor exists for any parameter, nor does it determine an SRB measure, entropy, dimension, Lyapunov spectrum, or basin geometry. The strict slab conclusions are measure-theoretic: they assert positive stationary mass outside each slab, not a quantitative mass fraction.

The 1981 primary paper was identified through bibliographic records and later equation-level reproductions, but its full text could not be inspected during this verification because publisher access required a human verification step. This leaves a residual bibliographic risk that an equivalent balance identity appears in the inaccessible primary text, although the inspected later literature characterizes the original work as numerical/oscillatory and does not report such a theorem.
