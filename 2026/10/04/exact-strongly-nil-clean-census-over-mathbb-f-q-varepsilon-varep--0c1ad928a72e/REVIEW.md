# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Strong nil-cleanness of a dual matrix reduces to that of its residue matrix because the generalized \(1\)- and \(0\)-primary blocks have coprime minimal polynomials, making the required Sylvester maps invertible over every field. Strongly nil-clean residue matrices are exactly those with only the primary factors \(t\) and \(t-1\). Counting the ordered primary decomposition, the two independent nilpotent blocks, and then the arbitrary dual coefficient gives the stated sum. Small finite fields were exhaustively checked independently of the symbolic count.

## Originality
PASS. The closest 2026 dual-number paper gives the structural strong nil-clean lifting criterion over the real dual numbers but contains no counting result and no finite-field census. Targeted searches for strongly nil-clean matrix counts, Gaussian-binomial formulas, and dual-number finite-field enumerations found no equivalent statement. Older work on nil-clean matrix rings is ring-level, while known finite-local-ring counts concern idempotent or von Neumann regular matrices. The main residual risk is an equivalent elementwise count implicit in older finite-matrix cycle-index literature.

## Value
PASS. The source isolates strong nil-cleanness as a structural property of dual matrices and separately notes that the binary dual matrix ring is nil-clean. The exact census measures the size of the strong locus for every finite field and every matrix size, and at \(M_2(\mathbb F_2[arepsilon]/(arepsilon^2))\) it quantifies the nil-clean/strongly-nil-clean gap as \(256-224=32\) elements. This is a natural finite invariant of the source's central object rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Şentürk--Özbay, arXiv:2609.29468v1. Breaz--Călugăreanu--Danchev--Micu gives the classical matrix-ring nil-clean context, and Fine--Herstein supplies the nilpotent-block count. Finite-local-ring idempotent and regular-matrix enumerations are nearby but concern distinct element classes. The result is limited to square-zero dual numbers; higher thickenings and the full nil-clean element count for \(q>2\) are not addressed.
