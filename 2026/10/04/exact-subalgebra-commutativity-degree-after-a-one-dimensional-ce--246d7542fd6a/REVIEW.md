# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof classifies every subalgebra through the quotient \(L_q/Z(L_q)\), with separate counts for projection dimensions \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\). The only nonpermutable case is a pair of distinct projection lines whose central kernels fail to span \(z\); the weighted count gives \(q^4(q+1)(3q+1)\). Exhaustive enumeration for \(q=2\) and \(q=3\) independently matches both formulas. The finite checks are corroborative and are not used to infer the arbitrary-\(q\) theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source computes the invariant for the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra, and the closest published database results extend that to the one-dimensional-center Heisenberg family. The present four-dimensional algebra has a two-dimensional center and is not in that family. Direct-sum, four-dimensional, central-extension, and group-side Lazard aliases were searched; no inspected source states or implies the exact formula. Residual risk remains that older subgroup-lattice literature could encode the odd-prime case for the corresponding Heisenberg group with an extra central cyclic factor.

## Value
PASS. A one-dimensional central stabilization is the smallest natural degeneration of the source paper’s basic Heisenberg example. It changes the large-field subalgebra-commutativity limit from \(0\) to \(1/4\), giving a concrete boundary showing that the invariant is highly sensitive to center enlargement even when the derived algebra remains one-dimensional.
