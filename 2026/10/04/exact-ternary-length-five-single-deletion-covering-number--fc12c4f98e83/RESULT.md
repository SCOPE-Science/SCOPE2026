# Exact ternary length-five single-deletion covering number
## Finding
For ternary single-deletion covering codes of length \(5\), in the notation of Lenz–Rashtchian–Siegel–Yaakobi, \(\mathsf K_{\mathsf D}^{3}(5,1)=21\). Equivalently, 21 length-five words over \(\{0,1,2\}\) suffice so that every ternary length-four word is obtained from at least one codeword by deleting one symbol, and no set of 20 length-five words has this property.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(\Sigma_3=\{0,1,2\}\). For a word \(c\in\Sigma_3^5\), let \(D_1(c)\subseteq\Sigma_3^4\) be the set of distinct words obtained by deleting exactly one coordinate of \(c\). A single-deletion covering code is a set \(C\subseteq\Sigma_3^5\) satisfying
\[
\bigcup_{c\in C}D_1(c)=\Sigma_3^4.
\]
The parameter \(\mathsf K_{\mathsf D}^{3}(5,1)\) is the minimum possible cardinality of such a code. Deletion descendants are sets: repeated deletions producing the same descendant are counted only once.

## Proof
For the upper bound, start with the seven representatives

`00121`, `00200`, `01010`, `01022`, `01112`, `02120`, `02221`.

For each representative, add its two nontrivial cyclic alphabet translates under \(0\mapsto1\mapsto2\mapsto0\). The resulting set \(C\) has 21 distinct length-five words. Exact enumeration of all five deletion positions of each codeword shows that the union of the distinct descendants is all 81 words in \(\Sigma_3^4\). Hence \(\mathsf K_{\mathsf D}^{3}(5,1)\le 21\).

For the lower bound, let the group generated by all permutations of the three alphabet symbols and reversal act on \(\Sigma_3^4\). Each orbit has the lexicographically least representative in the first column below. Give every word in an orbit weight equal to the listed numerator divided by 23.

| Representative | Orbit size | Weight numerator |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `0000` | 3 | 10 |
| `0001` | 12 | 8 |
| `0010` | 12 | 5 |
| `0011` | 6 | 7 |
| `0012` | 12 | 6 |
| `0101` | 6 | 3 |
| `0102` | 12 | 4 |
| `0110` | 6 | 7 |
| `0112` | 6 | 6 |
| `0120` | 6 | 4 |

The total weight of all 81 target words is exactly
\[
\frac{468}{23}>20.
\]
The accompanying verifier exhausts all 243 words \(c\in\Sigma_3^5\) and checks, using exact integers, that
\[
\sum_{y\in D_1(c)} w(y)\le 1
\]
for every such \(c\). Therefore every deletion-covering code \(C\) satisfies
\[
|C|=\sum_{c\in C}1
\ge \sum_{c\in C}\sum_{y\in D_1(c)}w(y)
\ge \sum_{y\in\Sigma_3^4}w(y)
=\frac{468}{23},
\]
where the second inequality uses the fact that every target is covered at least once and all weights are nonnegative. Since \(|C|\) is an integer, \(|C|\ge21\). Together with the explicit construction, this proves the claim.

## Verification
`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the ten target-word symmetry orbits, checks that their sizes sum to 81, verifies that the dual-weight numerator totals 468, exhausts all 243 length-five words to prove every deletion ball has weight at most 1, generates the 21-word construction, and verifies that its distinct deletion descendants cover all 81 targets. A successful run ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

`artifacts/certificate.json` records the seven construction generators and the ten dual-weight orbit representatives. The proof depends only on these finite data and exact integer comparisons; no floating-point optimization output is used in the certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Lenz, Rashtchian, Siegel, and Yaakobi define \(R\)-deletion-covering codes and the minimum-cardinality parameter \(\mathsf K_{\mathsf D}^{q}(n,R)\), and give general upper and lower bounds. Their single-deletion bounds are not exact at \(q=3,n=5\); the weighted certificate above specializes the same covering-duality principle to this finite instance and closes the gap exactly.

Xie, Sun, and Ge later develop improved bounds and constructions for insertion/deletion covering codes, emphasizing the large-alphabet regime and asymptotically optimal nonbinary single-deletion constructions. The accessible abstract and preview inspected here do not state the exact value at \(q=3,n=5\). Vorobyev studies a different fixed-length Levenshtein covering model in which both insertions and deletions occur. The later notion of a subsequence cover of a single word is also distinct from covering the whole space \(\Sigma_3^4\) by deletion descendants of a codebook.

## Limitations
The result is only for the exact parameter \(q=3,n=5,R=1\); it does not assert a formula for other lengths, alphabets, or deletion radii. The proof is a finite exhaustive certificate, not an asymptotic argument. Only the abstract and available preview of the 2026 Xie–Sun–Ge preprint were inspectable; an unobserved small-parameter table in its full text remains a residual literature risk. No claim is made about uniqueness or classification of optimal 21-word codes.
