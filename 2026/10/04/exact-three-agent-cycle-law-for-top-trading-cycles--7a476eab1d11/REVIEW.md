# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The eight chronology counts are proved by partitioning all \(27\) first-choice functional graphs and then resolving the only ambiguous one-loop/no-two-cycle family using the two lower-ranked choices. Every first-choice map has exactly \(8\) full-ranking refinements, so the case analysis is exhaustive. A separate exact replay enumerates all \(216\) preference profiles with two independent TTC implementations and reproduces the same chronology and trade-volume distributions.

Risk: the round statistic depends on the explicitly stated convention of executing all disjoint current cycles simultaneously. The final-allocation trade-volume claims do not depend on that convention.

## Originality
**PASS.** The inspected literature establishes the Shapley--Scarf housing model, the canonical role of TTC, and the relevance of cycle dynamics. Che--Tercieux derive random round-size dynamics for a different two-sided TTC model, while Ekici gives a full modern TTC cycle definition and axiomatic characterization context. Targeted searches by three-agent housing-market terminology, impartial culture, cycle lengths, exact round probabilities, and exact trade-volume probabilities found no equivalent complete eight-cell law.

Risk: search failure is not a novelty proof. An unindexed exercise, lecture note, software table, or supplementary computation may contain the same finite distribution. One potentially relevant strict-preference characterization paper had inaccessible full text at the checked endpoint and is not used for a whole-document absence claim.

## Value
**PASS.** TTC is the canonical mechanism for strict Shapley--Scarf housing markets, and random TTC research explicitly treats the number assigned in successive rounds as a structural object. The three-agent market is the first size supporting a genuine three-cycle and nontrivial multi-round branching. Its complete exact chronology therefore provides a natural finite calibration for cycle dynamics, while the trade-volume law gives convention-independent economic content about how often endowments actually move.
