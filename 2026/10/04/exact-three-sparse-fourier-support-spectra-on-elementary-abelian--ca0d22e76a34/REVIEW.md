# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Translation and restriction to the difference span reduce the problem to a local transform on \(\mathbb F_p\) or \(\mathbb F_p^2\), with each local character repeated on a coset of the annihilator. In the collinear case, the prime cyclic Fourier-minor theorem limits a three-term transform to two zeros; explicit nonzero coefficient triples realize zero, one, and two zeros on every support. In the noncollinear case, every zero forces \(|a+b\zeta^x|=|c|\), a line-circle intersection condition with at most two possible \(x\), and then \(y\) is unique. Explicit triples realize zero, one, and two zeros. The stated scaling by \(p^{d-r}\) follows exactly.

## Originality
PASS with residual indexing risk. Bonami--Ghobber determines the minimum uncertainty function for \(\mathbb Z_p^2\) and classifies equality cases; at support size three, their minimizers are precisely the line-supported examples, but their inspected theorem does not give all exact Fourier-support sizes for each three-point support or the noncollinear \(p^2-j\) classification. Biró--Lev gives later structure-sensitive inequalities in \(\mathbb F_p^2\) but the inspected statements do not provide this complete exact spectrum or every-support realizability. Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches for three-point, three-sparse, affine-span, and \(p^2-1,p^2-2\) formulations found no equivalent statement.

## Value
PASS. Three points are the smallest support at which affine geometry in \(\mathbb F_p^d\) splits into two genuinely different types. The theorem turns a coarse minimum uncertainty bound into a complete exact spectrum, including the non-minimizing noncollinear stratum and every-support realizability, while extending uniformly to all dimensions by a transparent annihilator-coset mechanism.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest archival source is Bonami--Ghobber's exact equality-case analysis for \(\mathbb Z_p^2\). Tao supplies the prime cyclic zero bound used on affine lines, and Biró--Lev supplies later structure-sensitive finite-plane comparisons. The main residual originality risk is an equivalent three-sparse statement hidden under sparse polynomial or finite-plane terminology. The theorem does not address larger support sizes or composite exponent groups.
