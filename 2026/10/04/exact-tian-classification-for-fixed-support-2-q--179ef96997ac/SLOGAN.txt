For a fixed odd prime \(q\), every positive-exponent solution of \(\binom{n+1}{2}=2^{\alpha}q^{\beta}\) is forced into a Mersenne-prime branch, a Fermat-prime branch, or the single exceptional square \(36=2^2\cdot3^2\). Thus Tian’s two-prime bound holds for every support \(\{2,q\}\), with two solutions only when \(q=3\).
