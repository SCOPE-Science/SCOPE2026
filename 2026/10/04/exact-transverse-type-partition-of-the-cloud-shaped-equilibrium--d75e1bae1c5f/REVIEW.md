# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. On each differentiable quadrant, the Jacobian calculation gives \(\det(\lambda I-J)=\lambda(\lambda^2-T)\). Eliminating \(x\) from the equilibrium conic and \(T=0\) gives the two sextics in the finding. Exact rational Sturm sequences isolate all real roots, and sign checks select exactly one valid transition in the first quadrant and one in the fourth. Representative exact signs classify the remaining connected arc components. The axis points are excluded because the vector field is not differentiable there.

## Originality
PASS. The introducing full text was inspected at equation level and by full-document searches: it defines the cloud-shaped equilibrium curve but does not compute eigenvalues or give a local-stability partition of that curve. published-finding corpus and web searches by DOI, equations, aliases, spectrum form, and transition terminology found only different vector fields or the source itself. Related later infinite-equilibrium papers do not imply the two sextic boundaries by specialization. Residual risk remains that a poorly indexed source could contain the same calculation.

## Value
PASS. A curve of equilibria cannot be summarized by one fixed-point label. The exact partition distinguishes normally hyperbolic saddle segments from spectrally elliptic segments and identifies the only two smooth transition points at the parameter values used for the paper's chaotic example. This supplies a reusable local geometric map for continuation and basin analysis rather than another finite trajectory or parameter-table computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is the introducing article itself, DOI 10.1155/2017/7138971, which supplies the model and equilibrium curve but not this classification. Later infinite-equilibria papers concern different systems. The four axis equilibria require nonsmooth methods, and the center-normal label is only a linear spectral conclusion; no nonlinear stability or hidden-attractor claim is made.
