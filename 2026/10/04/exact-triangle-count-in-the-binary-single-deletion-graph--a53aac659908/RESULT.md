# Exact triangle count in the binary single-deletion graph

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 1\), let \(\Gamma_{n,1}\) be the graph on \(\{0,1\}^n\) in which two distinct words are adjacent exactly when they have a common subsequence of length \(n-1\). If \(T_n\) denotes the number of unordered triangles of \(\Gamma_{n,1}\), then
\[
T_n=\frac{2^n(n^3+n^2-6n-28)+16(n^2+n+2)}8.
\]
In particular, \(T_n=2^{n-3}n^3+O(2^n n^2)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is binary, the block length satisfies \(n\ge1\), and the graph uses one-deletion confusability: two distinct length-\(n\) words are adjacent when their deletion distance is at most one. The statement counts ordinary unordered graph triangles. It does not claim a formula for larger deletion radius, nonbinary alphabets, or higher cliques.

## Proof
First count ordered triples while temporarily allowing equal coordinates. A six-state nondeterministic automaton recognizes a pair \((x,y)\) of equal-length binary words having a common subsequence after deleting at most one symbol from each. The states are: an aligned state before a deletion; an aligned state after one deletion from each word; and four one-symbol-offset states recording whether the unmatched symbol lies on the first or second word and whether that symbol is \(0\) or \(1\). Determinization has exactly \(11\) reachable states.

Take the product of three copies of this deterministic pair automaton, for the pairs \((x,y)\), \((y,z)\), and \((z,x)\). Exactly \(287\) product states are reachable. Let \(M\) be its integer transition matrix over the eight possible input triples, let \(u\) select the start state, and let \(f\) select product states in which all three pair automata accept. Then
\[
A_n=uM^n f
\]
counts ordered triples \((x,y,z)\) for which every pair has deletion distance at most one, with equal words allowed.

The exact transfer certificate in `verify.py` proves that the row Krylov space generated by \(uM,uM^2,\ldots\) has dimension \(14\), is invariant under right multiplication by \(M\), and every vector in a basis annihilates
\[
Q(M)f,
\qquad
Q(t)=(t-2)^4(t-1)^3.
\]
Hence \(A_n\), for \(n\ge1\), satisfies the recurrence induced by \(Q\). Direct exact evaluation of the initial terms gives
\[
A_n=\frac{2^n(3n^3+9n^2-24n-56)+48n^2+48n+72}4.
\]
This is an infinite proof: the finite computation certifies an invariant finite-dimensional linear representation and its annihilating polynomial, rather than extrapolating from a finite table.

The same pair automaton gives the exact number \(P_n\) of ordered pairs \((x,y)\) with deletion distance at most one, including equality:
\[
P_n=2^{n-1}(n^2-n+6)-2.
\]
Its transfer certificate has a five-dimensional invariant row Krylov space and annihilator \((t-2)^3(t-1)\).

Among the \(A_n\) ordered triples, the number having at least one equality among the three coordinates is, by inclusion-exclusion,
\[
3P_n-2^{n+1}.
\]
Every triangle with three distinct vertices contributes its six orderings. Therefore
\[
T_n=\frac{A_n-3P_n+2^{n+1}}6,
\]
and substitution simplifies to the displayed formula.

## Verification
Running `python verify.py` rebuilds both automata from their transition semantics. It directly enumerates the graph for small block lengths, independently checks the resulting triangle counts, and then verifies the exact invariant-subspace/annihilator certificates for the pair and triple transfer matrices. The finite graph enumeration is corroborative; the transfer certificate is what establishes the formulas for every \(n\ge1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Alon, Bourla, Graham, He, and Kravitz define the deletion graph \(\Gamma_{n,k}\) and make triangle sparsity the central graph-theoretic input in their improvement of deletion-code lower bounds. Their argument bounds the total number of triangles by \(O_k(2^n n^{3k}(\log n)^k)\); it does not state an exact enumeration for \(k=1\). Their later longest-common-subsequence and shortest-common-supersequence estimates concern multiplicities for a fixed pair and likewise do not imply the global triangle count. The formula here specializes the same graph invariant to the first deletion radius and determines all lower-order terms.

The topic lies in synchronization error-correcting codes, MSC 94B50. The primary literature anchor is the first public version of Alon et al., dated 2022-09-23.

## Limitations
The exact transfer argument is binary and radius one. No assertion is made for \(k\ge2\), for alphabets larger than two, or for code-size improvements beyond the structural triangle count. Although targeted literature and database searches found no statement implying this exact formula, an unindexed or differently phrased prior enumeration remains a residual originality risk.
