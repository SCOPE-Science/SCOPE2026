# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The degree is reconstructed from the minimal spinor polarization, the strict-partition Schubert basis, and the one-box Pieri rule. This identifies the degree with the number of shifted standard tableaux of staircase shape. The shifted hook-length formula gives the factorial quotient, and Legendre's exact factorial valuation reduces it to \(n-s_2(n(n+1)/2)\). The modulo-four classification is then proved uniformly after checking the first six values and bounding the binary digit sum for \(n\ge7\).

The bundled checker independently compares the factorial quotient with a recursive saturated-chain count in small rank and verifies the valuation identity through a much larger range. The finite computation is not used as an infinite proof.

PASS. The inspected algebraic-geometric sources provide the minimal embedding and Schubert/Pieri calculus, while the combinatorial source provides the shifted hook formula. None states the exact two-adic digit-sum identity or the resulting complete oddness and modulo-four classifications. Searches using equivalent geometric, tableau, divisibility, and binary formulations did not locate a covering statement.

The nearest prior item in the checked local ledger concerns log-concavity of ordinary Grassmannian Pluecker degrees, not arithmetic of spinor degrees. Residual risk remains because the valuation derivation is short once the classical formulas are combined, and an unindexed older source could contain the same observation.

## Value
PASS. Spinor-variety degree is a canonical enumerative invariant, and the theorem determines its full two-adic content in every dimension by one binary statistic. Equivalently, it gives the exact power of two dividing every transverse complementary linear-section count in this homogeneous family, with sharp small-dimensional residue exceptions.
