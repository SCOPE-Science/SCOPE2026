# Exact two-frequency \(L^1\) concentration on every cyclic group
## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) and target \(a\in\mathbb Z_N\), define the two-frequency \(L^1\) point-concentration constant by \[\Gamma^{(2)}_1(N,a)=\max_{r\not\equiv s\pmod N}\frac{2\lvert e_N(ra)+e_N(sa)\rvert}{\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_N}\lvert e_N(rx)+e_N(sx)\rvert},\qquad e_N(t)=\exp(2\pi i t/N).\] For \(L\ge2\), put \(S_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\lvert\cos(\pi j/L)\rvert\), so \(S_L=\csc(\pi/(2L))\) for odd \(L\) and \(S_L=\cot(\pi/(2L))\) for even \(L\). Then \[\Gamma^{(2)}_1(N,a)=\max_{\substack{L\mid N\\L\ge2}} \frac{2L}{N S_L}\left\lvert\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi\gcd(a,L)}{L}\right)\right\rvert.\] In particular, for every odd prime \(p\) and nonzero \(a\in\mathbb Z_p\), \[\Gamma^{(2)}_1(p,a)=2\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2p}\right)\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{p}\right)=\frac{\pi}{p}+O(p^{-3}).\]

This is a complete formula for the smallest nontrivial spectral-cardinality slice of finite cyclic idempotent \(L^1\) point concentration. The composite-modulus answer is governed by the divisor \(L\) generated by the frequency difference together with \(\gcd(a,L)\); for prime modulus the entire two-frequency slice decays at the exact scale \(\pi/p\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient group is \(\mathbb Z_N\) with \(N\ge2\). A two-frequency idempotent has the form
\[f_{r,s}(x)=e_N(rx)+e_N(sx),\qquad r\not\equiv s\pmod N.\]
The quantity above uses the finite-group \(L^1\) normalization customary in the discrete concentration problem: the numerator is twice the mass at the selected target \(a\), while the denominator is the full counting-measure \(L^1\) norm. No assertion is made here about idempotents with three or more frequencies or about the unrestricted finite concentration constant.

## Proof
Multiplication by the character \(e_N(rx)\) does not change absolute values, so only the frequency difference \(d=s-r\) matters. Put \(g=\gcd(d,N)\), \(L=N/g\), and \(u=d/g\). Then \(u\) is a unit modulo \(L\), and
\[\lvert1+e_N(dx)\rvert=2\left\lvert\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi u x}L\right)\right\rvert.\]
As \(x\) runs through \(\mathbb Z_N\), every residue modulo \(L\) occurs exactly \(g\) times after multiplication by \(u\). Hence
\[\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_N}\lvert1+e_N(dx)\rvert=2gS_L.\]
At the target \(a\), the concentration ratio for this difference is therefore
\[\frac{2L}{NS_L}\left\lvert\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi u a}L\right)\right\rvert.\]

Let \(h=\gcd(a,L)\), write \(a=hb\), and put \(m=L/h\). Reduction of units modulo \(L\) onto units modulo \(m\) is surjective. Since \(b\) is a unit modulo \(m\), the residues \(ub\) run through all units modulo \(m\). For \(m\ge3\), the largest value of \(\lvert\cos(\pi v/m)\rvert\) over units \(v\) is attained at \(v\equiv\pm1\pmod m\), and equals \(\cos(\pi/m)\). The cases \(m=1,2\) give respectively \(1\) and \(0\), agreeing with
\[\left\lvert\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi h}L\right)\right\rvert.\]
Thus the optimum among all differences with the same \(L\) is exactly the corresponding term in the displayed maximum. Every divisor \(L\mid N\) with \(L\ge2\) occurs from a difference having \(\gcd(d,N)=N/L\), so maximizing over divisors proves the formula.

It remains only to evaluate \(S_L\). Pairing terms around the half-circle and using the elementary finite cosine sum gives
\[S_L=\begin{cases}\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}\right),&L\text{ odd},\\[1mm]\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}\right),&L\text{ even}.\end{cases}\]
For an odd prime \(p\) and \(a\ne0\), the only admissible divisor is \(L=p\) and \(\gcd(a,p)=1\), yielding the prime formula. Its asymptotic expansion follows from the Taylor expansions of sine and cosine at the origin.

## Relationship to prior work
Anderson, Ash, Jones, Rider, and Saffari introduced the idempotent concentration framework and highlighted the \(L^1\) endpoint problem. Bonami and Révész then formulated the finite cyclic analogue, defining the unrestricted point-concentration constant on \(\mathbb Z_q\), noting the target-change symmetry for coprime targets, proving failure of uniform \(L^1\) concentration over prime moduli, and leaving the asymptotic unrestricted constant as an open problem. The formula here does not answer that unrestricted problem: it gives an exact classification after imposing the natural spectral-cardinality condition \(\lvert H\rvert=2\), and it does so for every modulus and every target, including non-coprime targets where divisor structure matters.

Searches for two-frequency, two-term, cardinality-two, and sum-of-two-characters formulations did not locate a statement giving this divisor-and-gcd formula. The closest broad finite-group result is the unrestricted Bonami--Révész problem, which neither fixes spectral cardinality two nor determines the composite-target dependence.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to exactly two distinct frequencies and to \(L^1\) point concentration. It does not determine the unrestricted quantity obtained by allowing arbitrary frequency sets, nor does it claim a new estimate for the Bonami--Révész open exponent. The originality comparison cannot rule out an unindexed folklore calculation of the two-term case.
