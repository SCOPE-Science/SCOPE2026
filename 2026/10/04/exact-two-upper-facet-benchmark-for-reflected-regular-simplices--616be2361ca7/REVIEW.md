# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof starts from Horváth's published exact \(k=2\) upper-side volume formula and its two endpoint inequalities. Convexity in the second scalar parameter reduces the chamber maximum to two endpoint branches. The upper branch is bounded by \(1-1/n^2\). The lower branch is exactly a two-by-two Rayleigh quotient, giving the claimed radical eigenvalue. A separate construction of the supporting normal verifies that the maximizing eigenvector lies strictly inside the two-upper-facet chamber and that every other facet is non-upper. The comparison with \(2n\) and the asymptotic limit are exact algebraic consequences.

The packaged checker independently reconstructs the eigenvector, endpoint equalities, visibility inequalities, published volume bracket, \(n=5\) specialization, threshold, and asymptotic behavior over a large finite dimension range. The finite checks are not used as the proof of the all-dimensional statement.

## Originality

PASS with explicit residual risk. The 2019 primary full text was inspected through its statement of the higher-dimensional open problem, general upper-side volume formula, parameter inequalities, \(n=4\) two-facet calculation, and complete \(n=5\) two-facet/global calculation. It contains the \(n=5\) quadratic form but does not state the all-dimensional two-upper-facet maximum.

The earlier 2013/2014 paper and 2015 survey were also inspected at their reflection problem statements. They state the now-corrected all-dimensional value \(2n\), so they do not cover the present formula. Targeted semantic and public searches using the exact object, two-upper-facet condition, radical expression, and higher-dimensional follow-up language did not locate an equivalent result.

## Value

PASS. Horváth explicitly leaves the global problem open beyond dimension five. The two-upper-facet chamber is the simplest nontrivial chamber after the height-orthogonal one-facet case and is exactly the chamber producing the known five-dimensional optimizer. Solving it in every dimension supplies a natural rigorous benchmark for the open problem, proves that this single chamber already defeats \(2n\) in every dimension \(n\ge5\), and identifies the asymptotic scale \((1+\sqrt2)n\). This is a structural partial resolution rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
