# Exact unary orbit decomposition of the universal homogeneous Heyting algebra

## Finding
Let \(L\) be the Fraïssé limit of the finite nontrivial Heyting algebras and let \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(L)\). Then the natural action of \(G\) on \(L\) has exactly two singleton orbits, \(\{0\}\) and \(\{1\}\), and for every integer \(n\ge 3\) exactly one further orbit \(O_n=\{a\in L:|\langle a\rangle|=n\}\). Each \(O_n\) is countably infinite. Equivalently, two nonconstant elements are automorphic exactly when their one-generated Heyting subalgebras have the same cardinality. In particular, the natural action is already non-oligomorphic on \(1\)-tuples.

## Assumptions and scope
A Heyting algebra is taken in the language \(\{0,1,\wedge,\vee,\to}\). Let \(L\) be the countable Fraïssé limit of all finite nontrivial Heyting algebras. For \(a\in L\), write \(\langle a\rangle\) for the subalgebra generated by \(a\), including the named constants \(0\) and \(1\). Local finiteness makes \(\langle a\rangle\) finite.

## Proof
Yamamoto records that every isomorphism between finitely generated subalgebras of \(L\) extends to an automorphism of \(L\). Hence two elements \(a,b\in L\) are in the same \(G\)-orbit exactly when the pointed algebras \((\langle a\rangle,a)\) and \((\langle b\rangle,b)\) are isomorphic. The forward implication is restriction of an automorphism; the reverse implication is ultrahomogeneity.

The classical Nishimura classification gives, for each integer \(n\ge2\), one finite one-generated Heyting algebra \(Z_n\) of cardinality \(n\), up to isomorphism. Citkin's generator analysis sharpens the pointed classification: \(Z_2,Z_4,Z_5\) have two generators; in \(Z_4\) and \(Z_5\) the two generators are exchanged by an automorphism; in \(Z_2\) they are not; and every other finite one-generated Heyting algebra has a unique generator. Therefore there is one pointed isomorphism type of generator for every \(Z_n\) with \(n\ge3\), while \(Z_2\) contributes two pointed types. In the language with named \(0,1\), those two generators of \(Z_2\) are precisely \(0\) and \(1\), and every automorphism fixes both constants. This proves that \(\{0}\), \(\{1}\), and the sets \(O_n\) for \(n\ge3\) are exactly the unary orbits.

It remains to determine their cardinalities. The two constant orbits are singletons. Fix \(n\ge3\). The age of \(L\) has strong amalgamation over the two-element Heyting algebra. Starting with one copy of \(Z_n\), repeatedly strongly amalgamate another copy over the common constants \(\{0,1}\). At each stage the new copy meets the previous amalgam only in \(\{0,1}\), so its generator is new. Every resulting finite amalgam embeds into \(L\); ultrahomogeneity puts all of these generators in the same orbit \(O_n\). Thus \(O_n\) is infinite. Since \(L\) is countable, \(O_n\) is countably infinite.

## Verification
The proof reduces the orbit question to three independently checkable ingredients: (i) ultrahomogeneity of \(L\), (ii) the pointed generator classification of the finite one-generated Heyting algebras, and (iii) strong amalgamation of finite Heyting algebras. The first and third are stated in Yamamoto's full text; the second is stated in Citkin's one-generated analysis and is consistent with the later algebraic proof of Nishimura's theorem. The exceptional \(Z_2\) case is also forced directly by the named constants: its two elements are fixed separately by every automorphism.

## Relationship to prior work
Yamamoto proves substantially deeper topological properties of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\) and, in his non-Roelcke-precompactness argument, explicitly notes that realized quantifier-free types correspond to finitely generated subalgebras with distinguished generators. His witness there uses infinitely many two-generated pointed algebras whose individual generators have the same one-type. The present result instead determines the complete unary action and shows that non-oligomorphicity is visible already at arity \(1\). Citkin's work supplies the finite pointed one-generator classification but does not discuss the Fraïssé limit or its automorphism orbits. Exact-phrase, implication-shaped, and semantic-index searches located no source stating this unary decomposition.

## Limitations
The result concerns only the natural action on single elements. Higher tuple orbits require the isomorphism types of multi-generated finite Heyting algebras and are much richer. The originality search cannot exclude unpublished folklore or an unindexed observation obtained by combining the same standard ingredients. The 2018 paper's relevant generator passage was available through indexed text, while a direct PDF fetch failed during checking; the bibliographic record and quoted mathematical content were cross-checked against the open proceedings listing and the later Nishimura article.
