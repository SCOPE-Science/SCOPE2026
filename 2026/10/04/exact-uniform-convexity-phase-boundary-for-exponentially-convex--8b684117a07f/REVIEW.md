# Review of Exact uniform-convexity phase boundary for exponentially convex functions

## Correctness

PASS. Uniform convexity is equivalent to the curvature quantity lying in the parabolic domain
\[
\mathcal P
=
\{w:\operatorname{Re}w>|w-1|\}.
\]
Because the class is defined by subordination to
\[
e^{\lambda z},
\]
the whole-class question is exactly the geometric inclusion
\[
e^{\lambda\mathbb D}\subset\mathcal P.
\]
The principal logarithm converts this to the centered-disk inradius of \(\Log\mathcal P\).

The parabolic boundary has the exact parameterization
\[
w_y=\frac{1+y^2}{2}+iy.
\]
The squared logarithmic distance has derivative proportional to the explicit function \(N(y)\). The proof shows \(N''>0\) on \((0,1)\), obtains the resulting one-turn behavior of \(N\), and separately proves \(N>0\) for \(y\ge1\). Hence there is exactly one positive minimizer. The source extremal realizes
\[
1+\frac{zf_\lambda''}{f_\lambda'}=e^{\lambda z},
\]
so every parameter above the threshold is genuinely excluded at an interior point.

## Originality

PASS. The full 2026 source defines the parameterized exponentially convex class and studies growth, distortion and Schwarzian-type extremals, not the uniform-convexity phase boundary. The 2020 coefficient paper treats a different question.

The most relevant older paper states a larger parabolic-starlikeness radius \(\log2\) for the exponential class. Its proof uses a real-ray displacement bound that does not control all complex points of the exponential image. The exact boundary minimization produces a smaller off-axis contact at
\[
0.6905440336407464278\ldots,
\]
so that earlier statement does not cover the accepted claim.

Searches over the exact 2026 source, exponential Ma–Minda uniform convexity, parabolic-starlike formulations, the older \(\log2\) claim, and the numerical threshold found no published equivalent.

## Value

PASS. Uniform convexity is a canonical geometric strengthening of convexity, and the 2026 paper explicitly emphasizes the geometry of the exponential curvature image. The result supplies an exact phase boundary for the entire parameterized family, not an arbitrary special value.

It also resolves a concrete discrepancy in the classical exponential-radius literature: the obstruction is not on the negative real axis but at a unique off-axis contact with the parabolic boundary. This identifies the true geometric mechanism and produces a sharp threshold with an explicit extremal.
