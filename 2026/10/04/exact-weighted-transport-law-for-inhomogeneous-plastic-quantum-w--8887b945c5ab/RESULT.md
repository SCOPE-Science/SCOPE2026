# Exact weighted transport law for inhomogeneous plastic quantum walks
## Finding
Consider the massless \(1+1\)-dimensional plastic quantum walk on the integer lattice,
\[
W=\Lambda^{-1}SCSC\Lambda,
\]
where \(S|x,0\rangle=|x+1,0\rangle\), \(S|x,1\rangle=|x-1,1\rangle\), the local coin is
\[
C(x)=\begin{pmatrix}-b_x&\sqrt{1-b_x^2}\\ \sqrt{1-b_x^2}&b_x\end{pmatrix},\qquad b_x=\cos\theta(x)\in(0,1),
\]
and \(\Lambda(x)\) is the local unitary appearing in the plastic-walk construction. For a real diagonal spatial observable
\[
A(a)=\sum_x a_x|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes I_2,
\]
the commutator seminorm is exactly
\[
\boxed{\ \|[W,A(a)]\|=\sup_x b_{x+1}|a_{x+2}-a_x|\ }.
\]
Therefore the constraint \(\|[W,A(a)]\|\le 2\epsilon\) is precisely the unit-Lipschitz constraint on the parity graph with edge lengths
\[
\ell_x=\frac{2\epsilon}{b_{x+1}}
\]
for the edge \(x\leftrightarrow x+2\).

If the initial position is localized at \(0\) and a finite-time walk state has spatial law \(P_N\) on the even parity component, then
\[
\boxed{\ d_W(\omega_N,\omega_0)=\sum_x P_N(x)d_\theta(0,x)\ },
\]
where \(d_\theta\) is the shortest-path metric generated by the \(\ell_x\). On the infinite line there is a unique path. On an even periodic lattice with at least three active sites, \(d_\theta\) is the shorter weighted-cycle distance. Thus the finite-lattice convex problem used for the inhomogeneous case has an exact weighted-shortest-path solution.

When \(b_x=\kappa c(x)\),
\[
\ell_x=\frac{2\epsilon}{\kappa c(x+1)}.
\]
For \(\kappa=1\), this is exactly the midpoint lattice discretization of the continuum spatial line element \(dl=|dx|/c(x)\). If \(b_x=b\) is constant, then \(d_\theta(0,x)=\epsilon|x|/b\) on the infinite line and the formula reduces to the homogeneous expression
\[
d_W(\omega_N,\omega_0)=\frac{\epsilon}{b}\sum_x |x|P_N(x).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The result is for the massless plastic walk, so the phase/mass parameter is \(\zeta=0\), and for the diagonal commutative position algebra used in the source's \(1+1\)-dimensional calculation. We assume \(0<b_x<1\) at every relevant lattice site. The finite-time distribution has finite support automatically on the infinite walk. For the periodic statement, the active parity component is a cycle with at least three vertices; the two-vertex degeneracy is excluded.

The result does not address the source's \(2+1\)-dimensional magnetic walk, the restricted Weyl-operator family, or a nonzero mass parameter. If some \(b_x\) vanishes, the corresponding edge acquires infinite metric cost and must be treated as a disconnected limit rather than by the displayed finite-weight formula.

## Proof
Write \(P_+=|0\rangle\langle0|\), \(P_-=|1\rangle\langle1|\), and \(U_x=C(x)\Lambda(x)\). A right-moving maximal path from \(x\) to \(x+2\) contributes
\[
T_+(x)=\Lambda(x+2)^{-1}P_+C(x+1)P_+C(x)\Lambda(x).
\]
Since \(P_+C(x+1)P_+=-b_{x+1}P_+\),
\[
\|T_+(x)v\|^2=b_{x+1}^2\|P_+U_xv\|^2.
\]
Similarly the left-moving maximal path satisfies
\[
\|T_-(x)v\|^2=b_{x-1}^2\|P_-U_xv\|^2.
\]
The zero-displacement paths disappear from the commutator because they end at the same site at which they started.

For \(\psi=\sum_x|x\rangle v_x\), the right-moving contribution arriving at a site \(y\) lies in \(\Lambda(y)^{-1}P_+\mathbb C^2\), while the left-moving contribution arriving at the same site lies in \(\Lambda(y)^{-1}P_-\mathbb C^2\). These subspaces are orthogonal. With \(\Delta_x=a_{x+2}-a_x\), orthogonality therefore gives
\[
\|[W,A(a)]\psi\|^2
=\sum_x b_{x+1}^2|\Delta_x|^2\|P_+U_xv_x\|^2
+\sum_x b_{x-1}^2|\Delta_{x-2}|^2\|P_-U_xv_x\|^2.
\]
If
\[
L=\sup_x b_{x+1}|\Delta_x|,
\]
then both coefficients at each \(x\) are at most \(L^2\), and the unitarity of \(U_x\) yields \(\|[W,A(a)]\psi\|\le L\|\psi\|\). Conversely, for any edge attaining the maximum, choose a state localized at its left endpoint with coin vector \(U_x^*|0\rangle\). The left branch vanishes and the right branch has norm \(b_{x+1}|\Delta_x|\). On the infinite lattice, a maximizing sequence gives the same conclusion when the supremum is not attained. This proves the boxed seminorm identity.

The Connes constraint is now equivalent, edge by edge, to
\[
|a_{x+2}-a_x|\le\ell_x=\frac{2\epsilon}{b_{x+1}}.
\]
Hence every admissible \(a\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz for the weighted shortest-path metric \(d_\theta\), so
\[
a_x-a_0\le d_\theta(0,x).
\]
For a probability law \(P_N\), this gives
\[
\sum_xP_N(x)(a_x-a_0)\le\sum_xP_N(x)d_\theta(0,x).
\]
Equality is achieved by the distance function \(a_x=d_\theta(0,x)\) on the active parity component, with an arbitrary constant on the inactive component. A distance-to-a-fixed-vertex function is \(1\)-Lipschitz on every weighted graph, including a weighted cycle. This proves the distance formula.

## Verification
The proof is analytic and does not depend on finite sampling. The accompanying checker independently constructs position-dependent periodic plastic-walk matrices, evaluates their commutators by singular values, and compares them with the predicted maximum weighted edge gradient. It also verifies the homogeneous reduction and the weighted-cycle distance formula on deterministic examples.

The decisive identities are the local maximal-path coefficients \(b_{x+1}\) and \(b_{x-1}\), orthogonality of the two arrival coin subspaces after applying the same final unitary \(\Lambda(y)^{-1}\), and the saturation state \(U_x^*|0\rangle\). No numerical experiment is used to infer the universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Trabelsi, Reyes-Lega, and Arrighi derive the homogeneous seminorm \(\|[W,A]\|=|\cos\theta|\max_x|a_{x+2}-a_x|\) and the homogeneous distance \(\epsilon|\cos\theta|^{-1}\sum_x|x|P_N(x)\). For the spatially varying case they state that the homogeneous reduction no longer applies and solve the defining spectral-norm convex problem numerically at every step. The present result keeps the correct conclusion that ordinary coordinate distance \(|x|\) is no longer the cost, but identifies its exact replacement: the weighted shortest-path metric with local edge length \(2\epsilon/\cos\theta(x+1)\).

General work on Connes distance and optimal transport explains why a genuine Lipschitz seminorm can lead to a Wasserstein-type duality, but it does not supply the walk-specific commutator identity above. Besnard's analysis of standard finite-graph spectral triples in fact emphasizes that a generic graph Dirac operator need not reproduce the graph geodesic distance. Here the equality arises from the special two-shift unitary structure of the plastic walk and the orthogonal coin branches.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to one-dimensional massless plastic walks and diagonal spatial observables. It does not claim that arbitrary discrete spectral triples have geodesic spectral distance. It does not analyze nonzero \(\zeta\), higher-dimensional inhomogeneous walks, magnetic Weyl observables, or continuum convergence rates. On a periodic lattice the exact cost is the weighted cycle metric, not the infinite-line metric; this distinction remains important even when the wavepacket itself has not reached the wrap-around region.
