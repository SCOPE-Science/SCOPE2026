# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The final claim was reconstructed from the position-dependent walk rather than inferred by substituting a variable angle into the homogeneous formula. The proof tracks both maximal-displacement paths, including the shifted coin values \(b_{x+1}\) and \(b_{x-1}\), and uses the common final unitary at each arrival site to establish orthogonality. The upper operator-norm bound is saturated by an explicit localized coin state. The resulting edge constraints are exactly the Lipschitz constraints of a weighted parity graph, and the distance-to-origin function attains the state-distance supremum.

A deterministic matrix checker independently confirms the commutator identity on several position-dependent periodic instances. These computations are corroborative only; the universal proof is analytic.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest source is Trabelsi–Reyes-Lega–Arrighi, arXiv:2609.31488v1. Its homogeneous calculation gives the constant-weight formula, while its inhomogeneous section says the coordinate-moment reduction no longer holds and solves the defining convex problem numerically. The new claim does not restore the false coordinate-cost formula; it replaces coordinate distance by the exact weighted shortest-path metric.

Besnard's arXiv:2105.09056 treats a different weighted-graph Dirac operator and does not imply this equality. Martinetti's arXiv:1803.07538 and D'Andrea–Martinetti's arXiv:1807.06935 explain general spectral/transport dualities but do not determine the local seminorm generated by this quantum-walk unitary. Targeted searches for weighted-path, Wasserstein, inhomogeneous-plastic-walk, and commutator formulations found no stronger covering result. The residual originality risk is an older equivalent theorem phrased in edge-spectral-triple or coined-walk terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The result turns the source's inhomogeneous per-time-step convex optimization into an exact weighted shortest-path evaluation. It identifies the lattice line element \(2\epsilon/\cos\theta(x+1)\), gives the correct periodic-cycle boundary version, and recovers the source's homogeneous formula as a strict special case. This directly sharpens one of the source's central numerical case studies and provides a reusable analytic benchmark for inhomogeneous simulations.

## Limitations and closest literature
The statement is limited to the massless \(1+1\)-dimensional plastic walk and the diagonal position algebra. Nonzero mass/phase, magnetic Weyl observables, and higher-dimensional walks are not covered. Generic discrete spectral triples need not have geodesic spectral distance, so the proof is not exported beyond this walk factorization.
