# Exact worker-count mean-square stability frontier for scalar EF21
## Finding

Consider distributed EF21 on \(n\ge1\) identical scalar quadratics
\[
f_i(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2}x^2,
\qquad
\lambda>0.
\]
Each worker uses an independent half-dropout contractive compressor
\[
\mathcal C_i(z)=\delta_i z,
\qquad
\delta_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),
\]
independently across workers and iterations. Since
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
|\mathcal C_i(z)-z|^2
\right]
=
\frac12 z^2,
\]
this is a valid EF21 biased compressor with contraction parameter
\[
\alpha=\frac12.
\]

Let
\[
s=\gamma\lambda
\]
be the normalized learning rate. Define normalized local gradient memories
\[
h_{i,t}=\frac{g_{i,t}}{\lambda},
\qquad
m_t=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n h_{i,t},
\qquad
q_t=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n h_{i,t}^2.
\]
Then the EF21 state is mean-square stable for every square-integrable exchangeable initialization exactly when
\[
0<s<s_n,
\qquad
s_n=\frac{6n}{n+8}.
\]
Here mean-square stability means
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
x_t^2+q_t
\right]\longrightarrow0.
\]

The frontier is strictly increasing:
\[
s_1=\frac23,
\qquad
s_4=2,
\qquad
s_8=3,
\qquad
s_n\uparrow6.
\]
Its large-worker expansion is
\[
s_n
=
6-\frac{48}{n}
+O(n^{-2}).
\]

Thus independent worker averaging has a qualitatively stronger effect than simply recovering the uncompressed gradient-descent ceiling. With at least four workers, the exact EF21 mean-square stability interval reaches or exceeds
\[
s<2,
\]
and in the many-worker limit the admissible normalized step approaches
\[
s<6.
\]

The limiting value has a deterministic interpretation. As \(n\to\infty\), the independent compression average self-averages, and the normalized EF21 recursion becomes
\[
x_{t+1}=x_t-sm_t,
\]
\[
m_{t+1}
=
\frac12m_t+\frac12x_{t+1}.
\]
Its state matrix is
\[
A_\infty(s)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&-s\\
1/2&(1-s)/2
\end{bmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(1/2\) and whose trace is \(3/2-s/2\). The second-order Jury conditions give the exact Schur interval
\[
0<s<6.
\]
Hence the finite-\(n\) mean-square frontier converges to the exact deterministic lagged-gradient frontier.

## Assumptions and scope

The theorem uses the distributed EF21 update from the defining paper, identical scalar local quadratics, independent compressors, and a constant learning rate.

The compressor is deliberately simple but is a genuine biased contractive compressor in EF21's class. Half-dropout means that on each worker, each gradient-memory correction is either transmitted exactly or replaced by zero with equal probability.

The state-space claim is for exchangeable square-integrable initial conditions. This includes symmetric deterministic initial memories as well as the exchangeable random initialization generated by applying identical independent compressors to the same scalar gradient.

The theorem classifies mean-square stability of the joint model-and-memory state. It does not cover heterogeneous local curvatures, correlated compression, stochastic gradients, momentum extensions, or nonlinear objectives.

## Proof

Normalize the local memories by
\[
h_{i,t}=\frac{g_{i,t}}{\lambda}.
\]
The distributed EF21 model step is
\[
x_{t+1}=x_t-sm_t.
\]
The local EF21 memory update is
\[
h_{i,t+1}
=
h_{i,t}
+
\delta_{i,t}
\left(
x_{t+1}-h_{i,t}
\right).
\]
Write
\[
x'=x-sm
\]
for one generic step and suppress the time index.

For exchangeable states, the complete second-order information relevant to mean-square stability is
\[
X=\mathbb E[x^2],
\qquad
C=\mathbb E[xm],
\qquad
M=\mathbb E[m^2],
\qquad
Q=\mathbb E[q].
\]
The new model moment is
\[
X'
=
X-2sC+s^2M.
\]

Conditioning on the current state,
\[
\mathbb E[m'\mid x,h_1,\ldots,h_n]
=
\frac{x'+m}{2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
C'
=
\frac12X
+
\left(\frac12-s\right)C
+
\left(\frac{s^2}{2}-\frac{s}{2}\right)M.
\]

Since the compression coins are independent,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(m'\mid x,h_1,\ldots,h_n)
=
\frac1{4n^2}
\sum_{i=1}^n
(x'-h_i)^2.
\]
Using
\[
\frac1n
\sum_{i=1}^n
(x'-h_i)^2
=
x'^2-2x'm+q,
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
M'
={}&
\left(
\frac14+\frac1{4n}
\right)X\\
&+
\left[
\frac{1-s}{2}
-
\frac{1+s}{2n}
\right]C\\
&+
\left[
\frac{(1-s)^2}{4}
+
\frac{s(s+2)}{4n}
\right]M
+
\frac1{4n}Q.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally,
\[
Q'
=
\frac12Q+\frac12X-sC+\frac{s^2}{2}M.
\]

Thus
\[
u' = M_n(s)u,
\qquad
u=
\begin{bmatrix}
X\\C\\M\\Q
\end{bmatrix},
\]
with
\[
M_n(s)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&-2s&s^2&0\\
1/2&1/2-s&(s^2-s)/2&0\\
\frac14+\frac1{4n}&
\frac{1-s}{2}-\frac{1+s}{2n}&
\frac{(1-s)^2}{4}+\frac{s(s+2)}{4n}&
\frac1{4n}\\
1/2&-s&s^2/2&1/2
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Direct determinant expansion gives the exact unit-eigenvalue condition
\[
\det(I-M_n(s))
=
\frac{s\left[6n-(n+8)s\right]}{16n}.
\]

The second-moment transformation is induced by expectation of random congruence maps on the covariance matrix of
\[
(x,h_1,\ldots,h_n).
\]
Hence it is a positive linear operator on the cone of exchangeable positive semidefinite covariance matrices. Its spectral radius is therefore a real nonnegative eigenvalue and depends continuously on \(s\). Whenever the spectral radius reaches one, \(+1\) is an eigenvalue, so the boundary must satisfy
\[
\det(I-M_n(s))=0.
\]

EF21's general linear-convergence theorem supplies a nonempty stable interval for sufficiently small positive \(s\) on this strongly convex quadratic. On the other hand, the trace of \(M_n(s)\) grows quadratically with \(s\), so its spectral radius is unbounded for large \(s\). The displayed determinant has only two nonnegative roots,
\[
s=0
\]
and
\[
s=\frac{6n}{n+8}.
\]
Continuity and positivity therefore imply that the latter is the unique positive mean-square stability boundary.

Monotonicity is immediate:
\[
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dn}
\frac{6n}{n+8}
=
\frac{48}{(n+8)^2}>0
\]
when \(n\) is viewed continuously, and hence the integer sequence is strictly increasing. Expanding
\[
\frac{6n}{n+8}
=
\frac6{1+8/n}
\]
gives
\[
s_n
=
6-\frac{48}{n}
+\frac{384}{n^2}
+O(n^{-3}).
\]

For the infinite-worker limit, independence makes the empirical compression average deterministic:
\[
m_{t+1}
=
m_t+\frac12(x_{t+1}-m_t).
\]
The stated \(2\times2\) matrix follows. Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
z^2-\left(\frac32-\frac{s}{2}\right)z+\frac12.
\]
The real second-order Jury inequalities reduce to
\[
s>0
\]
and
\[
6-s>0,
\]
proving the exact limiting ceiling.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs the four-moment operator from the stochastic EF21 update, checks its exact determinant identity for rational client counts and steps, enumerates all compression outcomes for small \(n\), and compares the spectral radius on both sides of the claimed boundary.

The finite checks are algebra and transcription guards. The exact boundary follows from the positive covariance-operator argument and the closed determinant formula above.

## Relationship to prior work

Richtárik, Sokolov, and Fatkhullin introduced EF21 as a Markov-compressor error-feedback mechanism. Their distributed algorithm updates the model using the average stored gradient memory and then compresses the difference between each new local gradient and its stored memory. Their theory gives broad sufficient convergence conditions for contractive compressors and proves linear convergence under a Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition. The inspected full text does not derive an exact scalar mean-square stability boundary or a worker-count law.

The same paper explicitly reports that EF21 tolerates substantially larger empirical learning rates than classical error feedback. The exact calculation here supplies a minimal mechanism for such tolerance: independent worker memories create a lagged averaged estimator whose deterministic many-worker limit is stable up to the normalized step \(6\), three times the ordinary gradient-descent ceiling.

Richtárik et al. later placed EF21 inside the three-point-compressor framework and linked it to lazy aggregation. The inspected full text recovers EF21 algebraically and sharpens its general communication theory, but does not state the half-dropout scalar moment matrix or the exact threshold
\[
\frac{6n}{n+8}.
\]

A previous exact calculation for classical Bernoulli error-feedback sparsification gives the one-memory threshold \(2/3\). The present result agrees at \(n=1\) but is not a routine restatement: it classifies the distributed EF21 memory system and shows a strict worker-count expansion all the way to \(6\).

## Limitations

The worker-count law depends on identical scalar Hessians and independent half-dropout correction events. Heterogeneous curvatures or correlated compression produce additional moment couplings.

The proof classifies second moments, not almost-sure Lyapunov exponents or transient tail probabilities.

The source EF21 theorem is used only to establish that the positive stability component adjacent to zero is nonempty. The exact boundary itself is derived independently from the moment operator.

The large-worker limit is a deterministic mean-field limit for this scalar model. It should not be interpreted as a universal large-scale learning-rate rule.

Equivalent random-delay calculations may exist under lazy-aggregation or switched-systems terminology; that remains the main originality risk.
