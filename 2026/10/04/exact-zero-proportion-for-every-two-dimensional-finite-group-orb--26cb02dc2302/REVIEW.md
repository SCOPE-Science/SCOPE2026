# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. In complex dimension two, the orthogonal complement of a nonzero test vector is a single projective ray. Therefore a matrix-coefficient zero set is either empty or exactly one fiber of the projective orbit map. Orbit--stabilizer gives every such fiber cardinality \(|G|/m(\eta)\), and every projective ray can occur as \([f^\perp]\). The minimization over windows is valid because a finite faithful subgroup of \(PU(2)\) has only finitely many rays fixed by nonidentity elements, so a trivial-stabilizer ray exists.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Führ--Oussa, introduces \(p_0\), records projective-kernel invariance, gives the sampling interpretation, and proves general estimates used in induction. The inspected full text does not state the exact formula \(p_0(\pi,\eta)=1/|H\cdot[\eta]|\). Full-spark and maximal-spanning literature addresses different or stronger properties for special representation classes. A 2025 paper on phase retrievability of finite-group irreducibles was identified but its full text was not materially available; its indexed scope does not expose the claimed zero-proportion formula, so it remains a residual priority risk.

## Value
PASS. The source describes \(p_0\) as potentially independently useful and relates it to sampling. The present theorem computes it exactly for every two-dimensional finite-group representation and every window, and gives a sharp worst-case sample-count guarantee. The invariant and its exact threshold are therefore mathematically motivated rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary comparison is Führ--Oussa, arXiv:2201.08654 / Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications 29 (2023), which supplies the definition and general estimates. Malikiosis--Oussa and Cheng--Han concern full-spark or maximal-spanning orbit vectors. Cheng's 2025 finite-group phase-retrieval paper is a residual access risk because only metadata and indexed keywords were materially available. The theorem is restricted to complex dimension two and does not itself assert magnitude phase retrieval.
