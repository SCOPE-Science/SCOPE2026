# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is an exact decomposition of \(\overline{\sigma_{-2}^*(\mathbb N)}\). The proof reconstructs the prime-tail criterion at \(r=2\), verifies the four exceptional fixed tail inequalities by exact rational bounds, uses the published prime-ratio monotonicity for the remaining tail, and then exhaustively glues the possible exponent states for the primes \(3\) and \(2\). The final gap inequality is strict and has a positive margin.

## Originality
PASS. The closest same-object source proves only the connectedness threshold and explicitly keeps unitary component counts as a future direction. At \(t=-2\), prior work supplies disconnectedness but not the exact number of components. Later component-count and gap papers inspected concern the ordinary divisor function, whose prime-power factors are different. Searches using the exact constants, the component-count notation, and unitary-divisor aliases did not locate a stronger or equivalent statement.

## Value
PASS. The result computes a natural invariant asked for in the foundational paper and gives the entire closure at the first simple disconnected parameter rather than a one-sided gap. The decomposition isolates the structural source of the gap: exponent one at both \(2\) and \(3\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is C. Defant, *Ranges of Unitary Divisor Functions* (arXiv:1507.02654; *Integers* 18 (2018), A15). The paper proves connectedness iff \(r\leq\eta^*\) for the negative parameter \(-r\), and its published Future Directions section defines the component-count sets \(\mathcal E_m^*\). Zubrilina (arXiv:1711.02871) and Achenjang--Berger (arXiv:1808.07550) address the ordinary divisor function instead. The present result is only for \(t=-2\); it does not establish an interval contained in \(\mathcal E_2^*\) or any monotonicity theorem.
