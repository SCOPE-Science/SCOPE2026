# Example 4.1 generates a ternary \([21,3,5]\) code, not \([21,3,6]\)
## Finding

In Example 4.1 of Yang–Gao–Mi, let \(C\) be the ternary \(2\)-GQT code in
\[
\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^{11}-2)\times\mathbb F_3[x]/(x^{10}-2)
\]
generated by
\[
(f_2f_3,f_5f_6),
\]
with the six factors printed in that example. Then the specified code has exact parameters
\[
[21,3,5]_3,
\]
not \([21,3,6]_3\). Its Hamming weight enumerator is
\[
W_C(z)=1+4z^5+4z^{10}+2z^{11}+8z^{16}+8z^{21},
\]
and its generalized Hamming weight hierarchy is
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(5,10,21).
\]

Thus the specified generator in Example 4.1 does not certify sharpness of the lower bound \(6\) claimed there. This finite correction does not, by itself, assert that the general minimum-distance theorem is false.

## Assumptions and scope

Arithmetic is over \(\mathbb F_3\), and both blocks use constacyclic multiplier \(\lambda=2\). The source factors are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=x+1,\\
f_2&=x^5+2x^3+2x^2+2x+1,\\
f_3&=x^5+2x^4+2x^3+2x^2+1,\\
f_4&=x^2+1,\\
f_5&=x^4+x^3+2x+1,\\
f_6&=x^4+2x^3+x+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
They satisfy
\[
x^{11}-2=f_1f_2f_3,\qquad x^{10}-2=f_4f_5f_6.
\]

The code is exactly the \(\mathbb F_3[x]\)-submodule generated by \((f_2f_3,f_5f_6)\), with multiplication in the two quotient rings above.

## Proof

Direct multiplication gives
\[
f_2f_3=1+2x+x^2+2x^3+x^4+2x^5+x^6+2x^7+x^8+2x^9+x^{10}
\]
and
\[
f_5f_6=1+2x^2+x^4+2x^6+x^8.
\]

Take
\[
h(x)=1+2x^2=(x+1)(1+2x).
\]
Because \(f_1f_2f_3=x^{11}-2\), the first component of
\[
h(f_2f_3,f_5f_6)
\]
vanishes modulo \(x^{11}-2\).

For the second component,
\[
h\,f_5f_6
=1+x^2+2x^4+x^6+2x^8+2x^{10}.
\]
Modulo \(x^{10}-2\), the relation \(x^{10}=2\) gives
\[
h\,f_5f_6\equiv 2+x^2+2x^4+x^6+2x^8,
\]
which has Hamming weight \(5\). Therefore
\[
d(C)\le5.
\]

The three module generators corresponding to multiplication by \(1,x,x^2\) have rank \(3\). Exhausting all \(3^3=27\) row combinations gives exactly
\[
A_0=1,\quad A_5=4,\quad A_{10}=4,\quad A_{11}=2,\quad A_{16}=8,\quad A_{21}=8.
\]
There are no nonzero words of weight below \(5\), so
\[
d(C)=5.
\]

For the generalized weights, write the columns of the resulting \(3\times21\) generator matrix as points in \(\mathbb F_3^3\). After projective normalization, there are exactly three projective column points, with multiplicities
\[
11,\quad5,\quad5.
\]
For a dimension-\(3\) code,
\[
d_2=21-\max_P m(P)=21-11=10,
\]
while all columns are nonzero, so \(d_3=21\). Together with \(d_1=5\), this gives
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(5,10,21).
\]

## Relationship to prior work

Yang, Gao, and Mi introduce an improved minimum-distance bound for generalized quasi-twisted codes. Their Example 4.1 specifies the factors and generator used above, states that the older bound gives \(2\), states that their new bound gives \(6\), and reports a Magma computation with parameters \([21,3,6]\), calling the bound sharp for the example.

The explicit multiplier \(h(x)=1+2x^2\) shows directly that the specified code contains a weight-\(5\) word. Complete enumeration confirms that \(5\) is the exact distance. Searches for the article title, Example 4.1, the stated \([21,3,6]\) parameters, the corrected \([21,3,5]\) parameters, and the printed factor products did not locate a published correction or an earlier statement of this discrepancy.

Chauhan and Sharma previously developed Hamming weight enumerators for classes of multi-twisted codes with at most two nonzero one-dimensional constituents. That broader theory is relevant background, but the searched literature did not identify this 2026 example or flag its published distance claim.

## Limitations

The result is a correction of the concrete generator in Example 4.1. It does not establish that the general bound theorem in the paper is false, because a discrepancy in the example can arise from an incorrect auxiliary distance, an incorrect application of the theorem, or an incorrect computational parameter report. No claim is made about Examples 4.2 or 4.3.

The weight enumerator and generalized hierarchy are finite consequences of the specified generator. An older general enumerator formula may imply the same finite distribution after specialization; the scientifically material new point is the explicit weight-\(5\) obstruction to the published \([21,3,6]\) claim.
