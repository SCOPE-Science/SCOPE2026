# Exterior-power packing makes exponent-three strict envelopes near-linear
## Finding

Let \(T_3\) be the theory of groups of exponent \(3\), let \(M\models T_3\) be existentially closed, and let \(C\le M\) be a nontrivial finite subgroup generated by at most \(n\ge1\) elements.

Define
\[
q_2(n)=\min\left\{q:\binom{q}{2}\ge n\right\},
\qquad
N=n+q_2(n),
\]
and
\[
q_3(N)=\min\left\{q:\binom{q}{3}\ge\binom{N}{2}\right\}.
\]

Then there exists a finite subgroup \(D\le M\) with \(C\le D\) such that the lower central series of \(D\) coincides with its upper central series in reverse order and
\[
\boxed{
d(D)\le B(n):=n+q_2(n)+q_3(N)+2.
}
\]

In particular,
\[
B(n)=n+O(n^{2/3}),
\]
so the strict-envelope bound is asymptotically linear rather than quadratic.

Ishida--Mizuno--Takeuchi prove the same structural conclusion with
\[
d(D)\le15n^2.
\]
Their Remark 4.11 explicitly notes that the generator bounds in the preceding repair lemmas are not optimal because several independent commutators can be packed into one free factor. Applying that observation at both lower-central layers gives the bound above.

The same sharpening propagates to their bounded-amalgamation obstruction theorem. Set
\[
t(m)=m+\binom m2+\binom m3
\]
and
\[
s(m)=(3m+4)t(m)+1.
\]
Their proof of Theorem 3.3 first constructs a subgroup generated by at most \(s(m)\) elements and then invokes the strict-envelope bound. Replacing their envelope function \(15r^2\) by \(B(r)\) therefore gives the explicit witness bound
\[
\boxed{
d(D)\le B(s(m))=O(m^4),
}
\]
instead of the bound
\[
15s(m)^2=O(m^8)
\]
coming from Proposition 4.13 as printed.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are in the variety \(\mathcal V_3\) of groups of exponent dividing \(3\). The notation \(\gamma_i(G)\) is the lower central series and \(d(G)\) is the minimum number of generators.

The argument uses the source's graded-Lie-algebra identifications for a relatively free exponent-three group \(F_q\):
\[
\operatorname{gr}_2(F_q)\cong\Lambda^2(\mathbb F_3^q),
\qquad
\operatorname{gr}_3(F_q)\cong\Lambda^3(\mathbb F_3^q).
\]
Hence the degree-two and degree-three graded pieces contain respectively
\[
\binom q2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\binom q3
\]
independent basic commutators.

The result sharpens only the quantitative generator bounds in the source's strict-envelope and finite-obstruction constructions. It does not change the model-companion theorem, prove optimality of \(B(n)\), or improve the source's lower-bound examples.

For \(C=1\), one may take \(D=1\); the displayed \(B(n)\) statement is formulated for \(n\ge1\).

## Proof

The source repairs lower-central strictness in two stages.

### First-stage packing

Let
\[
V=(C\cap\gamma_2(G))/\gamma_2(C).
\]
In Lemma 4.8, the source chooses a basis represented by elements
\[
g_1,\ldots,g_r,
\qquad
r\le \dim\operatorname{gr}_1(C)\le n,
\]
and realizes each \(g_i\) as a commutator in a separate pair of fresh generators. This uses at most \(2n\) new generators.

Instead, choose
\[
q=q_2(n),
\]
so that
\[
\binom q2\ge n\ge r.
\]
Let \(F_q\) be the relatively free exponent-three group on fresh generators
\[
x_1,\ldots,x_q.
\]
Because
\[
\operatorname{gr}_2(F_q)\cong\Lambda^2(\mathbb F_3^q),
\]
we may choose \(r\) distinct basic commutators
\[
w_i=[x_{a_i},x_{b_i}]
\]
whose images in \(\operatorname{gr}_2(F_q)\) are linearly independent.

Form
\[
H_0=G*_{\mathcal V_3}F_q
\]
and quotient by the normal closure of
\[
g_i^{-1}w_i,
\qquad
1\le i\le r.
\]

The injectivity proof of Lemma 4.7 uses only the linear independence of the degree-two classes assigned to the \(g_i\). In the source's Claim B, the degree-two component separates the coefficients of the relations. In Claim C, the direct-sum term
\[
\sum_i s_i\otimes \operatorname{gr}_2(w_i)
\]
vanishes only when every \(s_i=0\), again by linear independence. Pairwise disjoint fresh generators are not required.

Thus the proof of Lemma 4.8 goes through with \(q_2(n)\) new generators rather than \(2n\). We obtain
\[
G\le H_1,
\qquad
C\le D_1\le H_1,
\]
such that
\[
D_1\cap\gamma_2(H_1)=\gamma_2(D_1)
\]
and
\[
d(D_1)\le N:=n+q_2(n).
\]

### Second-stage packing

Lemma 4.10 next repairs the degree-three defect. For a group generated by at most \(N\) elements, the source chooses representatives
\[
h_1,\ldots,h_r
\]
for
\[
(D_1\cap\gamma_3(H_1))/\gamma_3(D_1)
\]
with
\[
r\le\dim\operatorname{gr}_2(D_1)\le\binom N2.
\]

The printed proof realizes the \(h_i\) by separate triple commutators and therefore spends \(3r\) new generators.

Choose instead
\[
q=q_3(N),
\]
so that
\[
\binom q3\ge\binom N2\ge r.
\]
Because
\[
\operatorname{gr}_3(F_q)\cong\Lambda^3(\mathbb F_3^q),
\]
choose \(r\) linearly independent basic triple commutators
\[
u_i=[x_{a_i},x_{b_i},x_{c_i}]
\]
in one copy of \(F_q\).

The source's Lemma 4.9 remains valid with these \(u_i\): the relevant subgroup of \(\gamma_3(F_q)\) is elementary abelian, and linear independence implies that
\[
\prod_i u_i^{e_i}=1
\]
forces every \(e_i=0\). The two intersection calculations in Lemma 4.10 use only that each defect representative is identified with a degree-three element spanning an independent coordinate. Therefore the same proof gives an extension
\[
H_1\le H_2
\]
and
\[
D_1\le D_2\le_{\mathrm{LCS}}H_2
\]
with
\[
d(D_2)\le N+q_3(N).
\]

This is precisely the packing mechanism highlighted by the source's Remark 4.11, now applied with the full exterior-power bases.

### Final two generators

As in Proposition 4.13, take a relatively free rank-two factor \(F_2\) and form
\[
D_3=D_2*_{\mathcal V_3}F_2.
\]
The source's Lemma 4.6 makes the lower central series of \(D_3\) coincide with its upper central series in reverse order. Thus
\[
d(D_3)\le N+q_3(N)+2=B(n).
\]

Because \(M\) is existentially closed and \(D_3\) is finite, the finite diagram of \(D_3\) over \(C\) is realized back inside \(M\), exactly as in the last step of Proposition 4.13. Its image is the required \(D\).

### Asymptotic bound

By definition,
\[
q_2(n)=O(n^{1/2}).
\]
Since
\[
N=n+O(n^{1/2}),
\]
the condition
\[
\binom{q_3(N)}3\ge\binom N2
\]
gives
\[
q_3(N)=O(N^{2/3})=O(n^{2/3}).
\]
Hence
\[
B(n)=n+O(n^{2/3}).
\]

Finally, the source's Theorem 3.3 sets
\[
s(m)=(3m+4)t(m)+1,
\qquad
t(m)=m+\binom m2+\binom m3.
\]
Since
\[
s(m)=O(m^4),
\]
substitution into the improved envelope gives
\[
B(s(m))=O(m^4).
\]

## Verification

The proof was checked against the actual statements and proofs of Lemmas 4.7--4.10, Remark 4.11, Proposition 4.13, and Theorem 3.3 in the primary source.

The critical source facts are:

- the coproduct graded pieces split so that the fresh degree-two and degree-three components can be separated;
- \(\operatorname{gr}_i(F_q)\cong\Lambda^i(\mathbb F_3^q)\) for \(i=1,2,3\);
- Lemma 4.7 uses linear independence of the chosen degree-two commutators in both its degree-two and degree-three separation steps;
- Lemma 4.9 uses independence of the chosen central triple commutators to preserve injectivity;
- Lemma 4.10 bounds the number of degree-three defects by \(\dim\operatorname{gr}_2(D_1)\);
- Remark 4.11 explicitly observes that one free factor can supply several linearly independent triple commutators and states that the same trick works for Lemma 4.8;
- Proposition 4.13 adds exactly two final free generators before using existential closedness;
- Theorem 3.3 applies the strict-envelope function to \(s(m)=(3m+4)t(m)+1\).

The bundled checker independently evaluates \(q_2\), \(q_3\), \(B(n)\), compares the improved bound with \(15n^2\), and checks the polynomial growth of the substituted obstruction bound over a range of inputs. These computations corroborate the arithmetic only; the arbitrary-\(n\) theorem is the symbolic exterior-power argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Ishida--Mizuno--Takeuchi prove that \(T_3\) has a model companion by establishing a bounded finite obstruction theorem. A central ingredient is Proposition 4.13, which embeds every finite \(n\)-generated subgroup of an existentially closed model into a strict finite subgroup generated by at most \(15n^2\) elements.

Their Remark 4.11 already identifies the local inefficiency: four independent triple commutators in a rank-four free factor can repair four defects using four new generators rather than twelve, and the authors state that the same trick applies to Lemma 4.8. The paper does not turn this observation into a general binomial packing formula, a near-linear bound for Proposition 4.13, or the resulting degree-four bound in Theorem 3.3.

The present refinement systematically uses the full dimensions
\[
\dim\Lambda^2(\mathbb F_3^q)=\binom q2,
\qquad
\dim\Lambda^3(\mathbb F_3^q)=\binom q3.
\]
This changes the two repair costs from linear-in-defect and three-times-linear-in-defect to inverse-binomial costs.

Targeted searches for improved strict-envelope bounds, packed commutator repairs, and quantitative refinements of Proposition 4.13 did not locate a statement equivalent to \(B(n)\). A separately indexed result deriving decidability of the new model companion uses the existence of explicit finite bounds but does not sharpen the strict-envelope function and does not imply the result here.

## Limitations

The bound \(B(n)\) is an upper bound, not an optimality theorem. The source's lower-bound examples show that no uniform bound exists without existential closedness, but they do not determine the optimal growth rate inside existentially closed models.

The packing argument uses the special exterior-power description of the free exponent-three graded Lie algebra. It does not automatically extend to groups of other exponents or nilpotency classes.

The \(O(m^4)\) finite-obstruction bound is the bound produced by substituting \(B\) into the source's existing proof of Theorem 3.3. No lower bound of order \(m^4\) is claimed.

The source explicitly hints at the packing idea in Remark 4.11. The contribution here is the full quantitative execution and its propagation through the main bounded-obstruction proof, not the discovery of the local packing phenomenon itself.
