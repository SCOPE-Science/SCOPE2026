# Same-model review
## Correctness
PASS. The fibre of the pushforward map over an extreme invariant measure is a compact convex face. An extreme point of that fibre is extreme in the full simplex of shift-invariant trajectory-space measures, hence ergodic. Birkhoff and disintegration then give the almost-everywhere trajectory realization. The clockwise/counterclockwise circle construction has a completely classified invariant-measure simplex and directly verifies the nonergodic-lift obstruction.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source is Suda's 2026 preprint itself. Under its displayed-lift definition, Theorem 3.7 uses an invalid inference from extremality of \(\pi_*\nu\) to extremality of \(\nu\). The explicit circle example refutes that chosen-lift reading. The positive repair is the exact existential statement supplied by compact-convex geometry. Ordinary factor-map literature contains the general existence mechanism for ergodic lifts, but no checked source states this sharp failure/repair in Suda's generalized evaluation-map framework or the resulting partial answer to its time-average question.

## Value
PASS. The theorem being repaired is central to connecting base-space ergodicity with trajectory-space Birkhoff theory. Distinguishing “there exists an ergodic lift” from “the chosen lift is ergodic” prevents an incorrect transfer of ergodicity and preserves the strongest valid conclusion. The time-average corollary identifies a guaranteed almost-everywhere value for every extreme invariant measure.

## Closest literature and limitations
Suda 2026 is the direct source. Downarowicz--Weiss 2024 is the closest broader comparison: ordinary factor maps admit ergodic lifts of ergodic measures, while nonunique lifts create subtler generic-point questions. The present result is limited to extreme invariant measures in the affine image \(\mathcal I\); it does not prove that all Suda-ergodic measures are extreme or classify all realizable time-average values. The counterexample does not impose switching, which is also not an assumption of the source theorem being corrected.
