# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** A crossing tile has four connection points, so any boundary side adjacent to an empty cube face excludes crossing tiles from every tile position touching that side. The cube has only the support symmetry types listed in the proof. Counting the remaining rows and columns yields the five formulas. The all-six-face term is the published theorem of Nagasawa-Hinck and Wood. An exact enumerator independently checks every nonempty proper face support for each \(2\le n\le100\).

## Originality
**PASS, with a stated access risk.** The fully inspected recent spherical-mosaic source defines \(sf_n(K)\) and proves the global \(6n^2-3n+1\) crossing bound, but does not give the five support-sensitive capacities in the inspected sections. Targeted semantic searches over published-finding corpus and the accessible literature found no equivalent claim. Sharp rectangular-mosaic crossing bounds are broader in a different direction and are explicitly accounted for by limiting the new formulas to positional capacities. The full text of *Cubic knot mosaics* (2025) was not available for complete comparison, so an equivalent statement there remains a residual originality risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The claim connects a newly introduced face-count invariant to the classical crossing number through a complete cube-support classification. It gives necessary lower bounds on \(sf_n(K)\) without requiring a knot census, and at \(n=2\) yields the explicit threshold sequence \((0,0,3,6,12,19)\). This is a motivated structural lemma rather than a routine parameter substitution.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Nagasawa-Hinck--Wood, arXiv:2510.26469v2, which defines the spherical face invariants and supplies the six-face crossing maximum. Howards--Li--Liu, arXiv:2410.19570, supplies sharp planar rectangular bounds and prevents interpreting the one-face positional count as knot-sharp. The inaccessible full text of Pezzimenti--Meintel--Shabon, *Cubic knot mosaics* (2025), is the main remaining literature risk. No claim is made that \(B_1,\ldots,B_5\) are attained by one-component knots for every parameter pair.
