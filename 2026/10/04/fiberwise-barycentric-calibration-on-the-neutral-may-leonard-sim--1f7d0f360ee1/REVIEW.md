# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. On \(\alpha+\beta=2\), the total abundance obeys
\[
\dot N=N(1-N).
\]
Stationarity of \(\log N\) and \(N\) forces \(N=1\) almost surely for every compact positive invariant measure. Logarithmic antiderivative tests in each species then give the weighted competitor-load conditional law; combining it with \(N=1\) and \(\alpha\ne1\) gives the exact barycentric fiber law. The per-capita growth identities are pointwise on the simplex, and the variance formula follows from the elementary pairwise-difference identity.

Risk: positivity bounded away from the coordinate planes is essential for the logarithmic test functions.

## Originality
PASS. The invariant simplex, neutral periodic regime, integrability literature, and global mean-value literature are all treated as prior. The surviving claim is the stronger conditional statement on every own-species abundance fiber and the resulting exact fluctuation identity. Direct semantic searches and same-object source comparisons did not locate an implication of those statements.

Risk: the complete foundational and mean-values articles were not both available for exhaustive full-text comparison, and the short conditional proof could have appeared incidentally under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The theorem resolves a natural stationary question that global averages do not answer: at fixed abundance of one species, how are the two cyclic competitors distributed on average? The exact answer is barycentric, and it converts immediately into an exact per-capita growth-energy law. This is a meaningful structural fact for a canonical neutral competition model rather than a routine recomputation of the invariant plane or mean \(1/3\).
