# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Restricting Fink's positive convolution expansion to the divisor-closed monoid generated by a finite prime set \(Q\) gives
\[
A_Q(s)=\frac{Z_Q(s)}{2-Z_Q(s)}
\]
whenever \(Z_Q(s)<2\). Under \(Z_Q(1)>2\), its pole at the unique \(\alpha_Q>1\) with \(Z_Q(\alpha_Q)=2\) contradicts the finite upper bound \(A_Q(s)\le Z_Q(s-1)\) that would follow if every supported integer satisfied \(a(n)\le n\). Fink's supermultiplicativity yields infinitely many powers after one witness, and the classical divergence of the prime Euler product supplies a qualifying support beyond any fixed first \(k\) primes.

## Originality
PASS. The 2020 primary source states the all-\(k\) assertion as Conjecture 1. The 2022 follow-up conference abstract describes its approach as ongoing. The 2023 primary paper proves the global Dirichlet-series and convolution identities used here but does not localize them to finite prime supports or state an all-\(k\) resolution. A current problem database still listed the conjecture as open, and targeted literature and database searches did not find a covering theorem.

## Value
PASS. This closes a published named conjecture in full and replaces enormous isolated witnesses by a general structural criterion. The criterion connects the ordinary Euler product of a finite prime support to recursive abundance and explains why deleting any fixed finite initial block of primes cannot prevent ample numbers forever.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inputs are Fink's 2020 conjecture and supermultiplicativity theorem, Fink's 2023 Dirichlet-series formula, and the 2022 Liyanage–Ranasinghe partial approach. The proof is existential and does not bound the least witness or characterize all supports that contain ample numbers.
