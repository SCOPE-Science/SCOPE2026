# Review

## Correctness

PASS. For each externally fixed finite \(k\), computation relative to \(A^{(k)}\) is arithmetically definable using the set parameter \(A\). If every set in an omega-model were reducible to \(A^{(k)}\), one arithmetical formula \(\varphi_k(e,y,A)\) would enumerate every member of the model by an index \(e\). Pakhomov's Theorem 6.1 shows that the relevant \(\Sigma^1_1\)-reflection instances refute every such arithmetical uniform enumeration. Finite tuples reduce to one parameter by taking their join inside \(\mathsf{RCA}_0\).

The proof explicitly avoids the invalid stronger inference that one set must escape all finite jumps simultaneously.

## Originality

PASS. The primary source formulates its sharpness result as failure of uniform enumeration. It does not state the resulting Turing-degree geometry of omega-models. Candidate-specific published-finding and literature searches for Lévy–Montague reflection combined with finite jumps, Turing bounds, principal ideals, and omega-model degree structure did not locate an equivalent statement.

The closest retrieved degree-theoretic results concern unrelated spectra or uniform introreducibility and do not imply the reflection-theoretic bound.

## Value

PASS. The source's uniform-enumeration obstruction is definability-theoretic. The accepted theorem turns it into a concrete and reusable structural invariant of omega-models: no fixed finite jump of finitely many internal parameters can dominate the model. This immediately rules out principal Turing ideals and all fixed-finite-jump-bounded ideals as candidate omega-models of the reflection principle.

That is a natural model-theoretic consequence in reverse mathematics, where omega-models are routinely analyzed through their Turing ideals.

## Closest literature and limitations

Pakhomov (2026), especially Theorem 6.1 and its discussion of \(\Sigma^1_1\)-reflection, is the direct source. Standard reverse-mathematics literature supplies the Turing-ideal viewpoint on omega-models.

The result is a structural corollary of the source's non-enumerability theorem. It does not characterize reflected omega-models and does not establish jump closure.
