# Review

## Correctness

PASS. In a finite sigma-field the null ideal is principal, generated by the union \(Z\) of its members. Modal closure of the ideal forbids arrows from non-null atoms to null atoms. The defining countable-intersection condition then forbids two distinct non-null successors of the same non-null atom: two disjoint successor atoms, followed by copies of the whole space, give an immediate contradiction. This yields a partial function on the surviving atoms.

The quotient isomorphism sends a measurable set to the set of its non-null atoms. Under that map the quotient modal operator is exactly inverse image under the partial function. The converse is direct because inverse images commute with arbitrary intersections whenever the partial function is defined. The algebraic meet-preservation criterion follows, and the four-element branching algebra violates it while every two-element algebra satisfies it.

## Originality

PASS. The closest source introduces marked modal measurable spaces, proves the general modal Loomis-Sikorski representation theorem, and gives a functional dynamical-system example. It does not classify finite marked spaces, prove that functional behavior is forced after quotienting null atoms, identify meet preservation as the exact finite algebraic criterion, or isolate the four-element minimal obstruction.

Targeted searches using the terms “finite marked modal measurable,” “partial function,” “functional relation,” “meet preserving diamond,” “branching obstruction,” and “finite modal Loomis-Sikorski representation” did not locate an equivalent theorem.

## Value

PASS. The theorem identifies a sharp finite-representation boundary inside a new general representation theory. It shows that the measurable quotient semantics has unexpectedly rigid finite behavior: arbitrary relational branching disappears after null atoms are removed. The minimal four-element example also demonstrates that the general representation theorem genuinely needs infinite representing spaces even for a very small finite measurable modal algebra.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest literature is the 2026 paper of Bezhanishvili, de Groot, and Moss. Its marked-space definition and modal Loomis-Sikorski theorem are the premises specialized here. Its measure-preserving map example exhibits partial-function behavior in one important class, whereas the new finite theorem proves that this behavior is universal after quotienting.

The proof is finite and atom-based; it does not classify infinite marked modal measurable spaces. An equivalent finite observation could exist under different terminology despite the targeted searches.
