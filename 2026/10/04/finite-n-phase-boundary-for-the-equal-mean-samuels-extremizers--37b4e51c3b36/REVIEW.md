# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is reduced to the sign of the explicit logarithmic endpoint ratio \(F_n\). Its derivative has the exact factorization with sign controlled by \(n+\delta-\delta^2\), yielding a single-hump shape. The small-\(\delta\), interior, and large-\(\delta\) signs imply exactly one positive crossing. The large-\(n\) correction follows from a uniform logarithmic expansion and a nonzero derivative at \(1/(e-1)\). The numerical roots are not used in the proof.

## Originality
PASS, with residual alias risk. Ling's theorem covers the sharp product formula, and Paulin already covers the endpoint examples and the classical \(1/(e-1)\) breakpoint in a related bound; endpoint reduction itself is not claimed new. The final claim is restricted to the unique finite-\(n\) switch and the explicit \(1/n\) correction. Focused searches and the inspected primary sources did not reveal an implication-equivalent statement. The residual risk is an older formulation of the same crossing under different Samuels/Feige notation.

## Value
PASS. The equal-mean specialization is the canonical Feige/Samuels regime, and the two endpoint constructions encode qualitatively different extremal mechanisms: concentration in one active coordinate versus a diffuse all-coordinate construction. The theorem identifies exactly where the mechanism changes at finite dimension and quantifies how that transition approaches the classical asymptotic breakpoint. This is a structural refinement of a sharp theorem rather than a routine restatement.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary sources are Ling's sharp theorem, Paulin's earlier Feige/Samuels analysis, and Han's proof reformulation. The result does not extend the sharp theorem to new distributions or unequal means, and it does not claim the endpoint reduction itself. No monotonicity in \(n\), second-order asymptotic, or stability theorem is asserted.
