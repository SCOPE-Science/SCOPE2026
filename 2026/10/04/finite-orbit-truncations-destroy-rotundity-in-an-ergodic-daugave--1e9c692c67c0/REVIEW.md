# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For every nonempty finite \(F\), the proof constructs \(q\notin F-F\) and a short interval whose required irrational-rotation translates are pairwise disjoint. This gives an exact isometric \(\ell_1^2\) subspace, not a limiting or experimental approximation. Failure of strict convexity and Gâteaux smoothness follows directly, and failure of wMLUR follows because wMLUR implies strict convexity. The uniform tail estimate is a direct analytic inequality and uses only measure preservation and \(\sum_k\sqrt{a_k}<\infty\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2609.12562v1, treats the full orbit norm with every weight positive and proves its regularity under ergodicity. Its inspected text does not state a finite-support or finite-window theorem. Full-text comparison with arXiv:2508.16182v1 and targeted searches for finite windows, finite-support weights, orbit truncations, and isometric \(\ell_1^2\) witnesses found no statement implying the claim. The main residual risk is the recency of the focal preprint and the possibility of an uncatalogued equivalent observation.

## Value
PASS. The claim isolates an exact implementation boundary for the new construction: no finite orbit horizon can exhibit its rotundity or smoothness, despite uniform norm convergence at a quantitative tail rate. The isometric \(\ell_1^2\) witness gives a structural reason for the failure and shows that the infinite orbit is mathematically essential to these geometric features rather than a dispensable presentation choice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is the Dantas--Kaasik--Perreau infinite-coordinate Daugavet renorming and Doucha's broader theory of invariant strictly convex renormings. The theorem here is limited to irrational rotations and makes no assertion about whether finite truncations retain the Daugavet property or about arbitrary ergodic transformations.
