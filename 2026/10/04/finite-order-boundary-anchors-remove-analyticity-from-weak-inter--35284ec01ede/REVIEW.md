# Review of Finite-order boundary anchors remove analyticity from weak-interconnection transitivity

## Correctness
PASS. The claim keeps all geometric hypotheses of the motivating theorem and changes only central analyticity to smoothness plus explicit finite-order anchors. The source proof was reconstructed at each analyticity-dependent step. The reparameterization is already \(C^1\); bounded distortion follows for each selected finite-order smooth germ from the same logarithmic-derivative calculation and its finite Taylor jet; the one-dimensional intersection estimate then uses only those distortion bounds and the standard hyperbolic/parabolic iterate scales. Minimal finite order forces all lower periodic jets to vanish, so the finite-jet Livšic normalization remains valid even when other periodic germs are infinitely flat. Positive iterates preserve both weak boundary interconnection and finite order.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2609.24437v1, states the theorem only for analytic central dynamics and explicitly identifies central analyticity as a hypothesis. Searches for smooth weak-boundary-interconnection variants, finite-order anchor formulations, infinitely flat periodic fibers, and removal of analyticity found no implication-equivalent result. The 2025 Li--Shi--Xia robust-transitivity theorem assumes the stronger boundary-interconnection condition; Kan-type smooth mixing/transitivity results impose stronger north--south structure. These do not cover the claimed weak-interconnection extension.

## Value
PASS. The result removes the principal regularity hypothesis from a new total-transitivity mechanism on a natural and substantially larger smooth class. In particular it separates the genuinely needed finite-order information from irrelevant periodic germs, which may now be infinitely flat. This is a structural clarification of the mechanism rather than a parameter substitution or isolated computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest paper is Li--Xia, arXiv:2609.24437v1. Li--Shi--Xia, arXiv:2509.21954, gives smooth robust transitivity under a stronger interconnection hypothesis. Xia (2024) and later Kan-type mixing results concern more restrictive fiber geometry. A residual risk is that an equivalent smooth weak-interconnection extension appears in literature not surfaced by the targeted searches. The all-infinitely-flat anchor case remains open here, and no mixing statement is made.
