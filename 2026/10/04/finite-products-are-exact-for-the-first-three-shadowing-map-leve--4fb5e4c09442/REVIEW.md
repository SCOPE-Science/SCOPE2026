# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was checked separately in both directions for all three hierarchy levels. Coordinatewise selectors give a product pseudo-orbit map. Proposition 3.2 converts coordinatewise induced shadowing into the paper's shadowing-map definition. L-shadowing passes through a finite max. For self-tuning, Proposition 5.3 turns the defining condition into a local pointwise estimate, and factor discrepancies are bounded by the product discrepancy.

For reflection, a factor pseudo-orbit is embedded by filling every passive coordinate with a genuine orbit. Projection of the product selector is a continuous pseudo-orbit map on the active factor. The passive coordinates have zero jump error, and in the self-tuning comparison their sequence discrepancies vanish identically, so the product discrepancy equals the active-factor discrepancy.

## Originality
PASS. The full motivating preprint was searched for Cartesian/direct-product language and contains no product theorem for the new selector hierarchy. Exact web searches and several published-finding corpus semantic searches found no equivalent statement. Older product results for ordinary shadowing are not covering because Artigue's source explicitly exhibits a system with the shadowing property but no shadowing map.

Residual risk: selector-level product closure could appear in unindexed literature under different terminology. The claim is limited to the three levels actually proved.

## Value
PASS. The source introduces a hierarchy in which continuity and compatibility of the chosen shadowing point carry information not present in the ordinary shadowing property. The theorem gives an exact finite-product reduction at the first three levels and shows that selector regularity cannot be generated by product coupling. This is useful for constructing examples and for reducing product systems to irreducible factors.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Artigue's 2025 paper defining the hierarchy. Thakkar's 2014 theorem covers ordinary product shadowing, while Antunes--Carvalho--Cordeiro treat L-shadowing for hyperspace dynamics. Neither addresses continuous pseudo-orbit selectors on finite Cartesian products. The present theorem does not extend to the shift-invariant or dynamically-invariant selector levels without extra hypotheses.
