# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Simon's construction identifies the relevant finite cover as a homogeneous separation relation with equivalence classes of fixed cardinality \(d\), quotiented by that equivalence relation. For an injective labeled tuple, the full preimage is therefore a fixed-content cyclic arrangement, with reversal identified by separation rather than circular orientation. Burnside counting gives the displayed rotation and reflection terms. The reflection parity cases agree with the standard fixed-content bracelet formula. Exhaustive enumeration for every tested \((d,k)\) with \(dk\le10\) matches the formula exactly.

## Originality
**PASS, with a bounded residual risk.** Simon supplies the model-theoretic family but does not state a bracelet interpretation or an exact ordered-tuple orbit profile. Fixed-content bracelet enumeration is prior work and is explicitly credited. published-finding corpus searches for the Simon quotient, dihedral fixed-content formulation, and equal-multiplicity bracelet orbit profile returned no covering result. Web searches likewise located Simon's family and classical bracelet enumeration separately, but no source joining them. The own ledger contains a degree-two stabilizer type-count result for the same broad family, but not this full injective orbit profile, general degree formula, or growth-exponent recovery.

## Value
**PASS.** Simon's family is an infinite family of primitive oligomorphic structures with no proper nontrivial reducts. Ordered-tuple orbit profiles are a basic invariant of oligomorphic permutation groups, and the formula supplies a closed profile for every cover degree while showing that the degree is asymptotically recoverable from orbit growth. The bridge also imports a mature fixed-content bracelet toolkit into this model-theoretic family.

## Closest literature and limitations
Simon motivates orbit counting and constructs the quotient family. Karim–Sawada–Alamgir–Husnine and OEIS A214609 cover the bracelet side. The novelty is not the Burnside formula as a standalone combinatorial identity. A residual possibility remains that the bridge appears in unindexed notes or folklore.
