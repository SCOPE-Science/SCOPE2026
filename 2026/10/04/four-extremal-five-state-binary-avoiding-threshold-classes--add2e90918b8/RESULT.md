# Four extremal five-state binary avoiding-threshold classes
## Finding
For a complete deterministic binary automaton \(\mathcal A=(Q,\{a,b\},\delta)\) with \(|Q|=5\), a word \(w\) avoids a state \(q\) when \(q\notin \delta(Q,w)\). For a synchronizing automaton define its 1-avoiding threshold as the maximum, over \(q\in Q\), of the shortest length of a word avoiding \(q\).

Among all ordered labeled binary transition pairs on five states, the largest 1-avoiding threshold among synchronizing automata is \(8=2\cdot5-2\). Exactly \(960\) ordered labeled transition pairs attain threshold \(8\). Under simultaneous relabeling of the five states and interchange of the two letters, the \(960\) extremals form exactly four isomorphism classes, each of orbit size \(240=5!\cdot2\).

Using states \(0,1,2,3,4\), canonical representatives \((a,b)\) and their state-wise shortest avoidance lengths are:

1. \(a=(3,1,1,0,0)\), \(b=(0,2,3,4,2)\), avoidance vector \((8,1,1,2,1)\); a shortest word avoiding state \(0\) is `abbbabba`.
2. \(a=(3,2,1,0,0)\), \(b=(0,3,1,4,1)\), avoidance vector \((8,2,1,3,1)\); a shortest word avoiding state \(0\) is `baababba`.
3. \(a=(3,2,1,0,0)\), \(b=(0,2,3,4,1)\), avoidance vector \((8,2,3,4,1)\); a shortest word avoiding state \(0\) is `abaabbba`.
4. \(a=(3,2,1,0,0)\), \(b=(0,4,3,1,3)\), avoidance vector \((8,2,1,4,1)\); a shortest word avoiding state \(0\) is `baabbaba`.

The published five-state exceptional automaton \(\mathcal A_5\) lies in the third class. Thus the exact maximum value \(8\) is prior, while the complete count and four-class symmetry classification are the finding here.

## Assumptions and scope
The enumeration ranges over every pair of functions \(a,b:\{0,1,2,3,4\}\to\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with the two letters ordered and states labeled. Hence the finite domain has \((5^5)^2=9,765,625\) automata. Only synchronizing automata enter the avoiding-threshold maximum. Isomorphism means the action generated by all \(5!\) simultaneous state relabelings together with optional interchange of letters \(a,b\).

The claim is restricted to complete deterministic binary automata on exactly five states. It does not assert a general upper bound for arbitrary state counts.

## Proof
For each transition pair, perform breadth-first search in the power automaton on the \(2^5=32\) subsets of states, starting at the full set \(Q\). The transition of a subset \(S\) under a letter is its image. A transition pair is synchronizing exactly when a singleton is reachable. For every state \(q\), the first reached subset not containing \(q\) gives the exact shortest avoidance length for \(q\); the maximum of these five distances is the automaton's exact 1-avoiding threshold.

The exhaustive census gives \(8,063,385\) synchronizing transition pairs. Their threshold histogram is
\[
1:321685,\quad 2:3448500,\quad 3:2890280,\quad 4:1133640,
\]
\[
5:231600,\quad 6:30720,\quad 7:6000,\quad 8:960.
\]
Thus the global maximum is \(8\), attained by exactly \(960\) labeled ordered pairs.

For each of the \(960\) extremals, canonicalize its pair of transition tables over the group generated by every state permutation and optional letter swap. Exactly four canonical forms occur, the four listed above. Independently generating the full symmetry orbit of each representative gives \(240\) distinct labeled ordered pairs; the four orbits are disjoint and their union has size \(4\cdot240=960\). This proves completeness of the four-class classification.

Finally, the five-state automaton from the Ferens–Szykuła–Vorel construction has, in zero-based notation, \(a=(1,0,0,4,3)\) and \(b=(0,2,3,4,1)\). The state permutation \((0,3,4,1,2)\) maps it to the third orbit representative without swapping letters.

## Verification
`census.c` enumerates all \(9,765,625\) transition pairs, computes the exact power-automaton distances, records the histogram, and canonicalizes all extremals. `CENSUS.txt` is its complete deterministic output.

`verify.py` recompiles and reruns `census.c`, requires byte-for-byte agreement with `CENSUS.txt`, then independently checks the four representatives in Python: synchronization, all five exact shortest avoidance distances, strong connectivity, the stated hard-state witness words, four disjoint symmetry orbits of size \(240\), and membership of the published \(\mathcal A_5\) representative in the third orbit. A successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Szykuła's work on avoiding words supplies a general quadratic upper bound for a state-avoiding word. Ferens, Szykuła, and Vorel subsequently studied large avoiding thresholds, constructed an infinite binary strongly connected synchronizing family attaining \(2n-3\), and identified isolated examples attaining \(2n-2\). Their paper explicitly states that \(\mathcal A_5\) has 1-avoiding threshold \(8\) and reports small-state experimental verification of the conjectured exceptional pattern.

Accordingly, this result does not claim discovery of threshold \(8\) or of the existence of \(\mathcal A_5\). The added statement is the complete five-state binary classification: exactly \(960\) labeled ordered extremals, forming exactly four classes under state relabeling and letter interchange, with three classes outside the published \(\mathcal A_5\) orbit.

Targeted searches for five-state binary avoiding-threshold classifications, exact extremal counts, and equivalent symmetry descriptions found no source stating the \(960\)-element count or the four-class result. This is evidence of non-coverage, not a proof that no unindexed or unpublished computation exists.

## Limitations
The result is a finite exhaustive classification for exactly five states and a binary alphabet. It gives no new infinite-family upper bound and no leading theory for larger state counts. The classification depends on the stated equivalence relation; a different convention that fixes letter names would split the symmetry classes differently. There remains a residual originality risk that an older experimental dataset or unpublished computation contains the same class count without being indexed in the sources checked here.
