# Review

## Correctness
The proof reduces the normalized fourth Fourier moment exactly to the sum of squared coefficient autocorrelations. For four support points, the six unordered differences determine a small multiplicity profile. The argument proves that, for prime \(p\ge11\), only the four profiles \((3,2,1)\), \((2,2,1,1)\), \((2,1,1,1,1)\), and \((1,1,1,1,1,1)\) can occur. Singleton difference classes have fixed contribution, doubled classes admit the stated simultaneous cancellations, and the only nontrivial minimization is the four-term progression. There it reduces to minimizing \((r-1)^2+r^2\) over \(0\le r\le2\), giving the sharp value \(19\).

The finite verifier checks support profiles for all four-subsets of several prime cyclic groups and replays explicit minimizing phases. It is corroborative only and is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
Schmidt's 2013 unimodular-polynomial paper is the closest analytic source. It supplies the classical fourth-norm/autocorrelation identity and studies asymptotic families of dense consecutive-support polynomials; it does not classify sparse four-point supports by additive geometry. Jedwab--Katz--Schmidt likewise concerns binary Littlewood polynomials on long consecutive supports and asymptotic merit factors, not exact finite prime-cyclic support-by-support minima.

Targeted searches covered four-point support, sparse unimodular polynomial, fourth Fourier moment, merit factor, additive energy, repeated differences, and phase-only autocorrelation formulations. No inspected source gave the four-valued classification or the characterization of affine four-term progressions as the unique minimizers. The earlier Newman--Byrnes article is a plausible small-degree source, but only bibliographic metadata and its publisher abstract were available; this is retained as an explicit residual risk rather than treated as negative evidence.

## Value
The theorem turns a phase optimization problem into an exact additive-geometric classification. It identifies the complete stable-range list of possible optimal fourth moments for four-point supports and shows that arithmetic progressions, rather than generic Sidon-type supports, uniquely maximize the phase-only merit factor. This supplies a finite structural counterpart to the classical asymptotic merit-factor problem and is not a numerical table or a routine parameter increment.
