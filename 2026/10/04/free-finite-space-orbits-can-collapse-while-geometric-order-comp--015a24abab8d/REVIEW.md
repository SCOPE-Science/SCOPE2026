# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The quotient finite space has one orbit for each level and inherits exactly the total level order, hence its order complex is a simplex. The two-level retraction is order-preserving, fixes the two-level subspace, is pointwise above the identity, and is equivariant. On that two-level graph, left translation has two vertex orbits and exactly \(r+1\) edge orbits, so the geometric orbit graph has first Betti number \(r\). The local neighborhood at level \(1\) contains two distinct minima that the quotient identifies, proving that the finite orbit map is not a covering. The standalone checker independently verifies representative finite groups but is not used to infer the general theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The closest construction paper gives the poset, automorphism action, deformation retract, and the homotopy type of the unreduced space, but not the finite-orbit versus geometric-orbit comparison. The closest covering paper gives the general covering criterion and order-complex correspondence for genuine coverings, but does not apply it to this group-realization family. Searches for quotient-poset/order-complex commutation and group-action aliases did not locate this parameterized comparison. A residual risk remains that a general equivariant-poset theorem elsewhere may subsume the example.

## Value
PASS. The claim isolates a sharp structural boundary: freeness on points of a finite \(T_0\)-space is insufficient for the finite orbit map to behave like a covering, and the resulting quotient can erase all circle homology even when the geometric order-complex orbit retains rank \(r\). The family works for every finite nontrivial group and every admissible generating set, rather than being an isolated finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Barmak--Minian (2009) supplies the universal finite-poset group realization and its two-level retract. Barmak--Minian (2016) supplies the finite-poset covering criterion and the covering/order-complex correspondence. Cianci--Ottina (2014) supplies the finite-space homology/order-complex framework and an explicitly dated topology classification anchor. The result does not classify all group actions or all quotient constructions, and broader equivariant-poset literature remains a residual originality risk.
