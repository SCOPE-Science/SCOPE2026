# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The source's forcing proof of existence starts from an arbitrary positive-measure closed condition. Every basic Cantor cylinder is such a condition, so the same construction yields a \(\Pi^0_n\)-generic point in every cylinder. The weakly generic class contains the generic class and is therefore also dense.

At dyadic scale \(2^{-k}\), the \(2^k\) length-\(k\) cylinders give a cover. Density forces every cylinder to contain a point of each class, while the ultrametric prevents a set of diameter at most \(2^{-k}\) from meeting two different length-\(k\) cylinders. Hence the covering number is exactly \(2^k\), which gives both box dimensions equal to \(1\). The source proves Hausdorff dimension zero for the weakly generic class, and inclusion gives the same for the generic class.

## Originality

PASS. The primary source explicitly asks in Question 3.28 whether the higher \(\Pi^0_n\)-genericity classes are large in a meaningful sense other than cardinality. It proves continuum cardinality and Hausdorff dimension zero but does not discuss box-counting or Minkowski dimension. Targeted published-finding and literature searches for the source terminology together with box dimension, Minkowski dimension, and Question 3.28 did not locate the full-box-dimension conclusion.

The general fact that dense subsets of Cantor space have full box dimension is standard fractal geometry. The new claim is its application after proving, from the source's forcing construction, that these newly introduced higher effective-genericity classes are dense, yielding an explicit answer to the source's open largeness question.

## Value

PASS. The source presents the higher genericity classes as simultaneously meager, null, and Hausdorff-dimension zero, and asks for another meaningful largeness notion. Full box-counting dimension is a canonical quantitative notion of geometric largeness and gives the strongest possible ambient value. The exact covering formula \(N_k=2^k\) makes the contrast with Hausdorff dimension transparent and supplies a precise answer to the paper's stated question.

## Closest literature and limitations

Veltri (2026), especially Lemma 3.18, Corollary 3.19, Theorem 3.20, Corollary 3.24, and Question 3.28, is the direct source. Standard fractal-geometry references supply the box-dimension framework.

The result is closure-sensitive and specific to the standard Cantor geometry. It makes no claim about packing, Assouad, or effective dimension.
