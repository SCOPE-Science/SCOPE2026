# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Projective normalization gives exactly sixteen flat sign lines. Exact integer determinant evaluation rules out every seven-line full-spark subset and verifies an explicit six-line full-spark witness, proving the sharp capacity. The exact census and orbit computation give \(1088\) maximizers in five signed-coordinate-permutation orbits of sizes \(16,160,192,240,480\). The weak-phase-retrieval consequence separately uses the published minimal-cardinality full-spark theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source, arXiv:1612.08018, proves the full-spark necessity and exhibits flat sign examples in \(\mathbb R^3\) and \(\mathbb R^4\), but it does not state a dimension-five flat-sign obstruction. arXiv:2110.06868 explicitly poses the existence problem for minimal weak-phase-retrieval frames from dimension five onward. arXiv:2301.03520 gives a different obstruction for frames containing a canonical basis vector, which does not apply to flat sign vectors. Searches under weak-phase-retrieval, full-spark, hypercube, sign-vector, exact-count and orbit formulations found no covering statement.

## Value
PASS. The result addresses the first open dimension of an explicit published weak-phase-retrieval problem by proving that the natural all-sign pattern of the known low-dimensional constructions cannot extend there. The exact capacity and symmetry classification make the obstruction structural rather than an isolated failed example.

## Closest literature and limitations
The analytic implication uses arXiv:1612.08018, Theorem 3.8. The motivating open problem is arXiv:2110.06868, Problem 9.2. The closest later negative result is arXiv:2301.03520, whose canonical-basis-vector hypothesis is disjoint from the flat sign configuration. General full-spark theory in arXiv:1110.3548 does not determine this constrained capacity. The theorem does not resolve whether non-flat eight-vector weak-phase-retrieval frames exist in \(\mathbb R^5\). An unindexed equivalent finite classification under different combinatorial terminology remains a literature risk.
