# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the canonical map and inverse from the stated barycentric identities. The forward witness \((0,tu)\) gives \(\omega_\varphi(t)\ge\omega(t)\). The inverse witness \(t\delta_\omega(u)\) has input norm \(t\), barycenter \(tu\), and image under the inverse whose \(\ell_1\)-sum norm is at least \(\omega(t)\) by the reverse triangle inequality. Together with the published upper estimates this proves the claimed two-sided bounds. The logarithmic comparison then shows that every exponent below \(\alpha\) fails.

## Originality
PASS. The focal 2026 paper and Kalton's 2004 Proposition 5.1 were inspected at the relevant statements. They give the canonical homeomorphism and upper estimates but do not state the matching lower estimates or the exact minimal logarithmic exponent for that map. Targeted searches for exact modulus, sharpness, barycentric-splitting aliases, and broader gauge formulations did not locate a covering theorem. The main residual risk is that the short lower-bound observation may be unindexed folklore.

## Value
PASS. The result calibrates the quantitative construction used to show strictness of the newly introduced almost-Lipschitz category: its logarithmic loss is unavoidable in both directions for the canonical map. The inverse lower witness exposes a nontrivial cancellation defect in the free-space splitting. The claim is intentionally limited to this canonical homeomorphism and does not assert optimality among all homeomorphisms.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kalton's Proposition 5.1 is the closest older result: it gives the same map and the upper constants \(2\) and \(3\). Cheng--He--Xiang specialize it to the logarithmic gauge and obtain an \((\alpha,\alpha)\)-almost-Lipschitz homeomorphism. The present claim supplies matching gauge-scale lower bounds. Numerical constants are not optimized, and alternative homeomorphisms may behave better.
