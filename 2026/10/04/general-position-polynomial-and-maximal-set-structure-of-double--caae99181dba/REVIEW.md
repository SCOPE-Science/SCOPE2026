# Review of General position polynomial and maximal-set structure of double stars

## Correctness
PASS. In a tree the geodesic between two vertices is unique. When neither center is selected, every leaf subset is valid. With exactly one center selected, the only obstructions are two leaves on that center's side or one leaf from each side; with both centers selected, every added leaf creates a selected internal geodesic vertex. These four cases are disjoint and exhaustive, so both the polynomial and the maximal-set list follow directly. Exhaustive distance-based testing for all \(2\le a,b\le7\) matches the classification, every coefficient, and every maximal set.

## Originality
PASS. The 2024 defining paper treats standard families, graph operations, brooms, and combs, but targeted full-text searches found no double-star or bistar formula. The 2020 complementary-prism paper does contain a double-star example, but only for an extremal general-position-number argument, not an all-set enumerator. A 2026 follow-up on explicit formulas treats complete multipartite graphs and coronas and contains no double-star or bistar treatment in targeted full-text searches. Semantic-index and exact-phrase searches likewise located related general-position results but no equivalent double-star polynomial or maximal-set classification.

## Value
PASS. Double stars are the canonical diameter-three trees with two branching centers. The theorem determines their complete general-position-set size distribution and every inclusion-maximal general position set, strengthening the scalar parameter to a full counting and facet description. This is a natural missing tree family alongside the broom and comb families emphasized in the defining polynomial literature.
