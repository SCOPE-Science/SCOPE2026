# Generation profiles of cyclic binary Hamming codes collapse by pair radius six
## Finding
Let \(m\ge 3\), let \(n=2^m-1\), and let \(g\in\mathbb F_2[x]\) be a primitive monic polynomial of degree \(m\). Consider the cyclic binary Hamming code
\[
C=\langle g\rangle\subset R:=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^n-1),
\]
with the natural cyclic coordinate order. Let \(\beta\) denote the residue class of \(x\) in \(\mathbb F_2[x]/(g)\). There is a unique \(\ell\pmod n\) satisfying
\[
\beta^\ell=1+\beta.
\]
Put \(c(x)=1+x+x^\ell\) and \(d(x)=\gcd(c(x),x^n-1)\). For the pair-generation profile
\[
\gamma_p^C(a)=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\left\langle u\in C:0<w_p(u)\le a\right\rangle,
\]
one has the exact formula
\[
\gamma_p^C(a)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le a\le4,\\
n-\deg d,&a=5,\\
n-m,&a\ge6.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the \(a\)-pair-distance graph on \(C\) has respectively \(2^{n-m}\), \(2^{\deg d-m}\), and \(1\) connected components in those three ranges. Its disconnection threshold is \(4\) if \(d=g\), and \(5\) otherwise.

Both possibilities already occur for degree \(6\). For \(g=x^6+x+1\), one has \(\ell=6\) and \(d=g\), so the threshold is \(4\). For \(g=x^6+x^4+x^3+x+1\), one has \(\ell=56\) and
\[
\gcd(1+x+x^{56},x^{63}-1)=x^8+x^7+1,
\]
so \(\gamma_p^C(5)=55\) while \(\dim C=57\); the radius-five graph has four components and the threshold is \(5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The polynomial \(g\) is assumed primitive over \(\mathbb F_2\); this fixes a primitive cyclic Hamming code of length \(2^m-1\). Coordinates are ordered by the cyclic polynomial positions, because symbol-pair weight depends on coordinate order. The symbol-pair weight of \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1})\) is the number of indices \(i\pmod n\) for which \((u_i,u_{i+1})\ne(0,0)\). The disconnection threshold is the largest integer \(a\) for which the graph joining codewords at pair distance at most \(a\) is disconnected.

The statement is exact for every primitive degree-\(m\) polynomial, not only for primitive trinomials. It does not claim that either threshold value occurs for every degree, and it does not address noncyclic coordinate permutations of a Hamming code.

## Proof
Because \(g\) is primitive of degree \(m\), its root \(\beta\) has order \(n=2^m-1\). The cyclic code \(C=\langle g\rangle\) has dimension \(n-m\). Since \(\beta\) and \(\beta^2\) are conjugate roots of \(g\), the binary BCH bound gives Hamming distance at least \(3\); the usual parity-check description with columns \(1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{n-1}\) shows that this is the binary Hamming code and its minimum Hamming distance is exactly \(3\).

For a nonzero binary vector that is not the all-one vector, if its support has \(r\) cyclic runs of ones, then
\[
w_p(u)=w_H(u)+r.
\]
Hence a codeword of pair weight at most \(4\) would have Hamming weight \(3\) and one cyclic run, so after a cyclic shift its support would be three consecutive positions. Such a word would give
\[
1+\beta+\beta^2=0,
\]
forcing \(\beta\) to have order \(3\), impossible because \(n\ge7\). Thus no nonzero codeword has pair weight at most \(4\), proving \(\gamma_p^C(a)=0\) for \(a\le4\).

A pair-weight-five codeword can only have Hamming weight \(3\) with exactly two cyclic runs, or Hamming weight \(4\) with one cyclic run. The latter would be four consecutive positions and would imply
\[
1+\beta+\beta^2+\beta^3=0.
\]
Since \((\beta+1)(1+\beta+\beta^2+\beta^3)=\beta^4+1\), this would force \(\beta^4=1\), again impossible for order \(n\ge7\). Therefore every pair-weight-five word has Hamming weight \(3\) and exactly one adjacent pair of support positions.

Shift that adjacent pair to positions \(0\) and \(1\). The parity-check relation determines the third position uniquely by
\[
1+\beta+\beta^\ell=0,
\]
that is, \(\beta^\ell=1+\beta\). Thus the complete set of pair-weight-five codewords consists precisely of the \(n\) cyclic shifts of \(c(x)=1+x+x^\ell\). The relation \(c(\beta)=0\) shows that \(g\mid c\), so these words lie in \(C\). Their span is the cyclic ideal generated by \(c\) in \(R\), whose generator polynomial is \(d=\gcd(c,x^n-1)\). Its dimension is \(n-\deg d\), proving the middle value of the profile.

It remains to show that radius \(6\) generates all of \(C\). Every Hamming-weight-three word has pair weight at most \(6\). The weight-three words span the binary Hamming code: in the parity-check model, a codeword support is a set of nonzero field elements with zero sum. Given two support elements \(a,b\), the triple \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) is a weight-three codeword. If \(a+b\) already lies in the support, subtracting this triple reduces the support size by three; otherwise it replaces \(a,b\) by \(a+b\), reducing the support size by one. Repeating terminates because no nonzero Hamming codeword has weight one or two. Hence every codeword is a sum of weight-three codewords, all of pair weight at most \(6\), and \(\gamma_p^C(a)=n-m\) for \(a\ge6\).

Finally, for a linear code the \(a\)-pair-distance graph is the Cayley graph of the additive group \(C\) with connection set consisting of nonzero words of pair weight at most \(a\). Its connected components are the cosets of the span of that connection set. Therefore the component count is \(2^{(n-m)-\gamma_p^C(a)}\), giving the stated threshold criterion.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses only exact integer arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2[x]\). It verifies primitiveness of the two degree-six example polynomials, computes the discrete logarithm defining \(\ell\), computes the stated polynomial gcds, and checks the resulting dimensions and component counts. It also exhaustively enumerates the degree-three and degree-four Hamming codes to confirm that no nonzero word has pair weight below \(5\) and that the computed radius-five span agrees with the gcd formula.

The executable checks are corroborative rather than substitutes for the general proof. In particular, no finite enumeration is used to infer the theorem for unbounded \(m\).

## Relationship to prior work
Singh and Singh introduced the symbol-pair generation profile and disconnection threshold in 2026 as invariants governing data protection for function-correcting symbol-pair codes. Their public description gives general structural bounds and characterisations, but the literature searches performed for this result did not locate an exact generation profile for cyclic binary Hamming codes.

Earlier work of Liu and Pan computes generalized \(b\)-symbol weights for Hamming codes. That invariant minimizes the pair-support size of an \(r\)-dimensional subcode; it is not the dimension of the span of all individual codewords below a pair-weight cutoff. Their Hamming-code tables therefore do not imply the profile above. Likewise, work on perfect \(b\)-symbol Hamming codes concerns packing/perfection rather than the low-pair-weight span and its Cayley-graph connectivity.

The present formula also sharpens what minimum pair distance alone says. Every code here has minimum pair distance \(5\), but the radius-five graph can already be connected or can still have multiple components; the gcd \(\gcd(1+x+x^\ell,x^n-1)\) decides which behavior occurs.

## Limitations
The result is coordinate-order sensitive and applies to the standard cyclic order of primitive binary Hamming codes. It does not classify generation profiles after arbitrary coordinate permutations, nonbinary Hamming codes, shortened Hamming codes, or larger pair radii for other code families. Literature search cannot prove absolute novelty; a differently phrased minimum-word-generation result could exist outside the sources inspected. The exact statement here is limited to the generation profile and connectivity consequences proved above.
