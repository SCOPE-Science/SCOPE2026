# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The code-theoretic proof reconstructs completely. For a non-all-one binary word, pair weight equals Hamming weight plus the number of cyclic runs. Since a primitive binary Hamming code has Hamming distance three, pair weight below five would force three consecutive support positions and the relation \(1+\beta+\beta^2=0\), impossible for primitive order \(2^m-1\ge7\). Pair weight five cannot come from four consecutive ones and therefore consists exactly of weight-three words with one adjacent pair; after cyclic shift these are the shifts of \(c(x)=1+x+x^\ell\), where \(\beta^\ell=1+\beta\). Their span is the cyclic ideal generated by \(d=\gcd(c,x^n-1)\), of dimension \(n-\deg d\). Every weight-three Hamming word has pair weight at most six, and the standard zero-sum-column reduction expresses every Hamming codeword as a sum of weight-three words, so radius six generates all \(n-m\) dimensions. Fresh independent exact checks reproduced the complete radius profiles for degrees three and four and the degree-six examples \((\ell,\deg d)=(6,6)\) and \((56,8)\).

## value

PASS

Generation profile and disconnection threshold were introduced specifically to refine what minimum symbol-pair distance says about protection. Cyclic binary Hamming codes are a canonical family, and the theorem shows that codes with the same minimum pair distance can differ at the radius-five connectivity layer, with an explicit gcd deciding the behavior. This is a natural exact evaluation of a new invariant, not a repackaged distance computation.
