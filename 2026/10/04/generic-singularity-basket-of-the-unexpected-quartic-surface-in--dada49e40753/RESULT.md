# Generic singularity basket of the unexpected quartic surface in \(\mathbb P^3\)

## Finding
Let \(Q_R\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}\) be the quartic surface introduced by Bauer--Malara--Szemberg--Szpond, with parameter \(R=(a:b:c:d)\), defined by
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_R={}&b^2(c^3-d^3)x^3y+a^2(d^3-c^3)xy^3+c^2(d^3-b^3)x^3z+c^2(a^3-d^3)y^3z\\
&+a^2(b^3-d^3)xz^3+b^2(d^3-a^3)yz^3+d^2(b^3-c^3)x^3w+d^2(c^3-a^3)y^3w\\
&+d^2(a^3-b^3)z^3w+a^2(c^3-b^3)xw^3+b^2(a^3-c^3)yw^3+c^2(b^3-a^3)zw^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
There is a nonempty Zariski-open set of parameters \(R\) for which the singular locus of \(Q_R\) consists exactly of the five points
\[
R,
\quad R_1=(-2a:b:c:d),\quad R_2=(a:-2b:c:d),\quad
R_3=(a:b:-2c:d),\quad R_4=(a:b:c:-2d).
\]
The point \(R\) is an ordinary triple point: its projectivized tangent cone is a smooth plane cubic. Equivalently, one blow-up resolves the germ at \(R\), with exceptional curve a smooth elliptic curve of self-intersection \(-3\). Each \(R_i\) is an ordinary double point of type \(A_1\). Thus the generic singularity basket is one ordinary triple point together with four \(A_1\) nodes, and there are no other singular points.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The claim is generic in the parameter \(R\): it asserts the existence of a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the parameter space on which the stated description holds. It does not claim that every special parameter outside that open set has the same singularity basket. The defining family and the five prescribed singular points are those of Bauer--Malara--Szemberg--Szpond. Their theorem states that \(R\) is a triple point and that the four points \(R_i\) are additional double points; the new content here is that generically the tangent cone at \(R\) is smooth, the four double points are \(A_1\), and these five points exhaust the singular locus.

## Proof
Consider the family of quartics given by the displayed equation. The assertions that the cubic tangent cone at the moving point \(R\) is smooth, that the Hessian at each moving point \(R_i\) is nondegenerate, and that no further singular point occurs are Zariski-open conditions once the five sections are distinct. It is therefore enough to produce one exact parameter value at which all three properties hold.

Take
\[
R_0=(1:2:3:4).
\]
In the affine chart \(w=1\), the corresponding quartic is
\[
\begin{aligned}
q={}&19xw^3-104yw^3+63zw^3-304x^3w+416y^3w-112z^3w\\
&-148x^3y+504x^3z+37xy^3-56xz^3-567y^3z+252yz^3,
\end{aligned}
\]
with \(w=1\) after differentiation. Let \(J\) be the ideal generated by the four projective partial derivatives of \(Q_{R_0}\), restricted to \(w=1\). An exact lexicographic Gröbner basis for \(J\) has final elimination polynomial
\[
\frac{(2z+3)(4z-3)^4(8z+3)}{4096}.
\]
Solving the remaining basis equations at these three possible \(z\)-values gives exactly
\[
\left(\frac14,\frac12,\frac34\right),
\left(-\frac12,\frac12,\frac34\right),
\left(\frac14,-1,\frac34\right),
\left(\frac14,\frac12,-\frac32\right),
\left(-\frac18,-\frac14,-\frac38\right),
\]
which are precisely \(R_0,R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4\) after scaling to \(w=1\). On \(w=0\), the three standard affine charts of \(\mathbb P^2_{x:y:z}\) give the unit ideal, so there are no singular points at infinity. Hence the singular support of this fiber is exactly the five prescribed points.

Center affine coordinates \((u,v,s)\) at \(R_0=(1/4,1/2,3/4)\) in the chart \(w=1\). The terms of degrees zero, one, and two vanish, while the degree-three tangent form is
\[
T=-\frac32\bigl(84s^2u-378s^2v-252su^2+567sv^2+74u^2v-37uv^2\bigr).
\]
The three partial derivatives of \(T\) generate the unit ideal on each of the charts \(u=1\), \(v=1\), and \(s=1\) of \(\mathbb P^2\). Thus \(V(T)\subset\mathbb P^2\) is smooth, proving that \(R_0\) is an ordinary triple point. Blowing up such a surface germ once gives the smooth cubic \(V(T)\) as exceptional divisor; its normal bundle has degree \(-3\), so its self-intersection is \(-3\).

At the four other singular points, the determinants of the affine \(3\times3\) Hessian matrix are respectively
\[
\frac{10762227}{2},\qquad -530181288,
\qquad \frac{4921675101}{2},\qquad -121002336,
\]
all nonzero. The holomorphic Morse lemma therefore identifies each germ with an \(A_1\) ordinary double point.

Finally, smoothness of the projectivized cubic tangent cone and nonvanishing of the four Hessian determinants persist on a Zariski-open neighborhood of \(R_0\). To control possible singularities that might otherwise collide with the moving triple point, blow up the ambient family along the section \(R\). The strict transform meets the exceptional \(\mathbb P^2\)-bundle in the family of cubic tangent cones. At \(R_0\) this cubic is smooth, so the strict transform is smooth along the exceptional cubic there; smoothness is open, hence after shrinking the parameter space no residual singular point can approach the triple-point section. The nondegenerate Hessians give the analogous isolation of the four node sections. The relative singular locus away from suitable open neighborhoods of these five sections is then closed in a projective family, so its image in parameter space is closed. Because the fiber over \(R_0\) has no such residual point, a nonempty Zariski-open neighborhood of \(R_0\) has none. This proves the generic statement.

## Verification
The bundled script `artifacts/verify.py` reconstructs \(Q_{(1:2:3:4)}\) over \(\mathbb Q\), checks all five singular points, computes the exact Gröbner elimination polynomial, verifies emptiness of the singular locus on \(w=0\), checks smoothness of the cubic tangent cone on all three projective charts, and verifies the four nonzero Hessian determinants. Running it prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The computational witness is used only to prove that the relevant open conditions are nonempty. The passage from that witness to a generic statement uses standard algebraic openness and properness, not numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Bauer, Malara, Szemberg, and Szpond introduced this quartic surface family and proved that it has a triple point at \(R\) and four additional double points \(R_1,\ldots,R_4\). Their paper also gives a rational model by projection from \(R\), but the inspected theorem and geometry section do not identify the four double points as \(A_1\), do not state that the tangent cone at \(R\) is smooth, and do not state that these five points exhaust the generic singular locus. A later exposition on Fermat-type arrangements records that the generic unexpected quartic is unique and irreducible, again without this singularity-basket refinement.

Classical work on quartic monoids studies quartic surfaces with a triple point and many special configurations of nodes. Those general classifications do not, in the inspected material, identify the singular scheme of this specific unexpected family or imply that its four named double points are generically the only additional singularities. Targeted searches for the family name together with “ordinary double point”, “ordinary triple point”, “singular locus”, and the named points found no published statement matching the result above.

## Limitations
The open subset of parameter space is proved nonempty but is not given by a fully factored global discriminant equation. The calculation does not classify the special parameters where some of the five sections collide, the tangent cubic becomes singular, a node degenerates, or extra singularities appear. It also makes no claim in positive characteristic. The originality comparison is limited to the inspected literature and targeted database searches; an equivalent statement could conceivably be hidden in classical quartic-monoid literature in different coordinates.
