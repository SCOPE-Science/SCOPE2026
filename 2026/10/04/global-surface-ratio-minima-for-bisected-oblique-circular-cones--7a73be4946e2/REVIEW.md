# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The global step is the strict monotonicity in \(b\): pointwise \(B>A>0\) gives both \(I_->I_+\) and \(J_+>J_-\), so every differentiated surface-area ratio has a strictly positive numerator. The overlap-inclusive convention adds another positive term because \(\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) increases with \(b\). The boundary problem is then solved exactly and globally by the substitution \(x=\arcsin(1/a)\) and the displayed derivative factorizations. The proof covers all \(a\ge0,b>0\) and distinguishes an unattained infimum from an attained minimum.

## Originality
PASS. Finch's primary full text supplies the boundary formulas and numerical critical parameters but explicitly states that rigorous minimality proofs are absent and that the \(a>1,b\to0^+\) regime is only conjectured to be relevant. The new statement proves that reduction for the full positive-height family. A later paper citing Finch addresses different convex-body constraints; an earlier lateral-area minimization paper addresses total area rather than a split ratio. Exact-form, alias, and literature-index searches, together with comparison against previously published findings, found no covering theorem. Residual risk remains for unindexed literature and for the inaccessible full text of the earlier Ding–Fay paper.

## Value
PASS. This closes the surface-area portion of the optimization question posed in the source for the canonical split: all three surface-area conventions treated there now have rigorous global infima, unique horizontal parameters, and exact root formulas. The result is structural because strict height monotonicity explains why the degenerate boundary controls the entire two-parameter problem.
