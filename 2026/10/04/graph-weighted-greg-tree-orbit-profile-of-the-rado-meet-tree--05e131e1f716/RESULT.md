# Graph-weighted Greg-tree orbit profile of the Rado meet-tree

## Finding
Let \(\mathbb T^{\mathrm R}\) be the Rado meet-tree, the generic meet-tree expansion of the Rado graph. Let \(a_n\) denote the number of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb T^{\mathrm R})\)-orbits on injective ordered \(n\)-tuples. For \(0\le k\le n-1\), let \(g_{n,k}\) be the number of rooted Greg trees with \(n\) black labeled vertices and \(k\) white unlabeled vertices, where every white vertex has at least two children. Then
\[
a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} g_{n,k}\,2^{\binom{n+k}{2}}.
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
G(x,u)=\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{n,k}\frac{x^n}{n!}u^k,
\]
then \(G\) is the unique formal solution with zero constant term of
\[
G=x e^G+u\bigl(e^G-1-G\bigr),
\]
and the orbit profile is obtained by weighting the coefficient with \(2^{\binom{n+k}{2}}\). The first values are
\[
1,\ 12,\ 3784,\ 33{,}870{,}848,\ 7{,}387{,}750{,}908{,}928,\ 34{,}292{,}468{,}404{,}362{,}149{,}888,\ldots.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\log_2 a_n=\binom{2n-1}{2}+O(n\log n),
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 a_n}{n^2}=2.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Kaplan, Riahi, and Rodríguez Fanlo define the Rado meet-tree as the Fraïssé limit of finite structures carrying both a finite graph and a finite meet-tree structure. Their generic-mix construction is over disjoint languages, and for a finite parameter set \(X\) the meet-generated substructure is \(\{x\wedge y:x,y\in X\}\), of size at most \(2|X|-1\). The count above uses the usual simple undirected Rado-graph convention and concerns injective ordered tuples. The first public version of the source appeared on 2023-10-11 and lists MSC 03C98, 03C15, and 20B27.

## Proof
Fix an injective ordered tuple \(\bar a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) and let
\[
C=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_n\rangle_{\wedge}.
\]
By the meet-closure lemma, every element of \(C\) is a meet \(a_i\wedge a_j\), and \(|C|=n+k\) for some \(0\le k\le n-1\). Draw the finite rooted tree representing the meet-order on \(C\), color the tuple entries black and the other meet points white, and label the black vertices by their tuple positions. Every white vertex is the meet of two black descendants from different child cones, so it has at least two children. Conversely, every rooted tree with \(n\) black labeled vertices and \(k\) white unlabeled vertices satisfying that condition is generated by its black vertices under meet. Thus the possible meet-closure shapes are exactly the \((n,k)\) rooted Greg trees counted by \(g_{n,k}\).

For a fixed closure shape with \(n+k\) vertices, the generic mix permits an arbitrary finite simple graph on the same carrier. Hence there are
\[
2^{\binom{n+k}{2}}
\]
possible graph relations. No further quotient occurs: an isomorphism sending the ordered tuple to itself fixes each black vertex and therefore fixes every closure point, because every closure point is a meet term in the black vertices. Thus distinct graph edge sets on the same closure shape give distinct ordered-tuple orbits. Summing over \(k\) proves the formula.

The bivariate Greg-tree equation follows from the root decomposition. A black labeled root contributes \(x\) and may have an arbitrary unordered set of child Greg trees, giving \(x e^G\). A white root contributes \(u\) and must have an unordered set of at least two children, giving \(u(e^G-1-G)\). This proves
\[
G=x e^G+u(e^G-1-G).
\]

For the growth estimate, the term \(k=n-1\) is present and has graph factor \(2^{\binom{2n-1}{2}}\). For the upper bound, each \(g_{n,k}\) is at most the number of rooted fully labeled trees on \(n+k\) vertices, namely \((n+k)^{n+k-1}\), after assigning distinct auxiliary labels to the white vertices. Therefore
\[
a_n\le n(2n-1)^{2n-2}\,2^{\binom{2n-1}{2}}.
\]
Together with the single positive \(k=n-1\) term, this yields the stated logarithmic asymptotic and the limit \(2\).

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` computes the refinement \(g_{n,k}\) in two independent exact ways. One route uses the classical Greg-tree triangle recurrence recorded as OEIS A048160. The second route computes the bivariate species equation coefficient-by-coefficient using exact rational arithmetic. The rows agree through \(n=10\), including the published A048160 rows through \(n=7\). The script then evaluates the graph-weighted sum, checks the displayed first six orbit counts, verifies \(g_{n,n-1}=(2n-3)!!\), and exits with `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Kaplan, Riahi, and Rodríguez Fanlo supply the generic meet-tree construction, the meet-closure lemma, and the Rado meet-tree itself, but the inspected source does not enumerate finite tuple orbits; targeted full-text searches found no occurrence of “orbit” or “Greg.” OEIS A048160 and the older Greg-tree literature already provide the refined numbers \(g_{n,k}\); that enumeration is not claimed as new. The contribution isolated here is the exact graph-weighted bridge
\[
g_{n,k}\longmapsto g_{n,k}2^{\binom{n+k}{2}}
\]
for the Rado meet-tree, together with the resulting orbit profile and quadratic logarithmic growth constant. Searches using the structure name, orbit terminology, Greg-tree terminology, the initial values, and the weighted formula did not locate a prior statement of this bridge.

## Limitations
The originality check cannot exclude an unindexed observation or informal folklore. The Greg-tree triangle itself is classical, and the source-supplied Fraïssé construction is prior work; novelty is asserted only for the explicit orbit-profile identification and consequences. The formula counts automorphism orbits of injective ordered tuples, not isomorphism types of arbitrary finite induced substructures selected without order.
