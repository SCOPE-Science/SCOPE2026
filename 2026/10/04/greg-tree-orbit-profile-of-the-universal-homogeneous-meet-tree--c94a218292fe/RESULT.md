# Greg-tree orbit profile of the universal homogeneous meet-tree
## Finding
Let \(\mathbf T\) be the universal homogeneous countable meet-tree in the language \(\{<,\sqcap\}\). For \(n\ge 1\), let \(a_n\) denote the number of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf T)\)-orbits on injective ordered \(n\)-tuples. Then \(a_n\) is exactly the number of labeled rooted Greg trees on \(n\) black vertices. Thus
\[
(a_n)_{n\ge1}=1,3,22,262,4336,91984,2381408,\ldots .
\]
If
\[
G(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\frac{x^n}{n!},
\]
then
\[
G(x)=x e^{G(x)}+\bigl(e^{G(x)}-1-G(x)\bigr),
\]
equivalently
\[
(1+x)e^{G(x)}=1+2G(x).
\]
The branch with \(G(0)=0\) is
\[
G(x)=-\frac12-W_0\!\left(-\frac{1+x}{2\sqrt e}\right).
\]

For all ordered tuples, with repetitions allowed, let \(b_n\) be the orbit count. Then
\[
b_n=\sum_{k=1}^n S(n,k)a_k,
\]
where \(S(n,k)\) is a Stirling number of the second kind. Consequently the exponential generating function for \((b_n)\) is
\[
G(e^x-1).
\]
The first values are \(b_1=1\), \(b_2=4\), \(b_3=32\), and \(b_4=416\).

## Assumptions and scope
A meet-tree is a lower semilinear order in which every pair has a greatest common lower bound, written \(\sqcap\). The structure \(\mathbf T\) is the Fraïssé limit of the finite meet-trees. The claim concerns the natural action of its full automorphism group on finite ordered tuples.

A rooted Greg tree here means a finite rooted tree whose vertices are colored black or white, in which the black vertices carry distinct labels and every white vertex has at least two children. Only black vertices are counted by the size parameter. This is the convention used for OEIS A005264.

## Proof
Fix an injective ordered tuple \(\bar a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) in \(\mathbf T\), and let \(C(\bar a)\) be the finite substructure generated by its entries. Since the only function symbol is meet, \(C(\bar a)\) is exactly the closure of \(\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) under finite meets. Finite meet-trees are uniformly locally finite, and the Fraïssé limit is ultrahomogeneous. Therefore two injective ordered tuples lie in the same automorphism orbit exactly when their generated meet-substructures are isomorphic by an isomorphism sending the first tuple coordinate to the first, the second to the second, and so on.

Every finite meet-tree has a least element and its cover graph, oriented away from that least element, is a rooted tree. Color the tuple entries black and all other points of \(C(\bar a)\) white; give black point \(a_i\) label \(i\). A white point \(w\) belongs to the meet-closure, so \(w\) is the meet of a nonempty finite set of black points. It cannot be the meet of only one black point, because then it would itself be that black point. Hence there are black descendants of \(w\) in at least two distinct child subtrees. Thus every white point has at least two children in the finite rooted tree. The resulting labeled colored rooted tree is a Greg tree.

Conversely, take a rooted Greg tree with black labels \(1,\ldots,n\). Order vertices by ancestry, with the root least. The meet of two vertices is their lowest common ancestor. This makes the vertex set a finite meet-tree. Every white vertex has at least two children, and every child subtree contains a black leaf because a white leaf is forbidden. Choosing black descendants in two different child subtrees shows that the white vertex is their meet. Hence the whole meet-tree is generated under meets by its black vertices. The two constructions are inverse up to label-preserving isomorphism. This proves the bijection between injective tuple orbits and labeled rooted Greg trees.

The generating-function equation follows directly from the root decomposition. If the root is black, it contributes one labeled atom and an unordered set of Greg subtrees, giving the labeled-species term \(X\operatorname{SET}(\mathcal G)\). If the root is white, it has an unordered set of at least two Greg subtrees, giving \(\operatorname{SET}_{\ge2}(\mathcal G)\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal G=X\operatorname{SET}(\mathcal G)+\operatorname{SET}_{\ge2}(\mathcal G),
\]
which translates to
\[
G=x e^G+e^G-1-G.
\]
Rearranging gives \((1+x)e^G=1+2G\). Solving for the branch satisfying \(G(0)=0\) gives the stated Lambert-\(W\) expression.

Finally, an arbitrary ordered \(n\)-tuple determines a partition of its coordinate positions according to equality. If the partition has \(k\) blocks, collapsing each block to one representative produces an injective ordered \(k\)-tuple, and the order of the blocks is canonically determined by their least coordinate positions. Conversely, an equality partition together with an injective orbit reconstructs the tuple orbit. There are \(S(n,k)\) equality partitions, so
\[
b_n=\sum_{k=1}^n S(n,k)a_k.
\]
The standard labeled-composition identity for Stirling transforms gives the exponential generating function \(G(e^x-1)\).

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library script performs two bounded checks. First, it constructs Greg trees canonically from set partitions for \(n\le5\) and obtains \(1,3,22,262,4336\). Second, it computes the exact rational coefficients forced by \((1+x)e^G=1+2G\) through \(n=10\), multiplies by \(n!\), and matches the published A005264 values through the checked range. It also computes the Stirling transform and verifies \(b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4=1,4,32,416\).

These finite computations are checks of the enumeration and algebra; the all-\(n\) statement rests on the structural bijection and species decomposition above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kaplan, Rzepecki, and Siniora establish the relevant model-theoretic setting: finite meet-trees form a Fraïssé class whose limit is the universal countable ultrahomogeneous meet-tree, and they study the automorphism group of that structure. Mennuni records that in dense meet-trees definable closure of a set coincides with its closure under meets. Those facts support the reduction of a finite tuple orbit to its finite labeled meet-closure.

The combinatorial sequence itself is classical. OEIS A005264 records labeled rooted Greg trees and the same exponential generating-function equation and Lambert-\(W\) form, with earlier tree-counting literature including Felsenstein. The contribution here is not a new enumeration of Greg trees; it is the identification of the injective tuple-orbit profile of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf T)\) with that classical family, together with the resulting Stirling-transform formula for all tuple orbits. Searches of the inspected meet-tree and orbit-profile literature did not locate this identification.

## Limitations
No claim is made that A005264, its generating function, or the Lambert-\(W\) expression is new. The originality claim is restricted to the model-theoretic identification and its tuple-orbit consequence. The literature search cannot prove priority, and an unindexed or differently phrased source could already contain the same identification. No asymptotic analysis is asserted here. The computational verification is finite and is not a substitute for the proof.
