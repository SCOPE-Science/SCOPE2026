# Hamming-isospectral but permutation-inequivalent sides of an optimal quasi-cyclic LCP
## Finding

Example 6.1 of Abdukhalikov--Ho--Ling--Verma gives an optimal-security quinary quasi-cyclic linear complementary pair
\[
(C,D)
\]
of length \(16\) and dimension split \(8+8\). The security parameter is
\[
\min\{d(C),d(D^\perp)\}=7.
\]

For the displayed generators, both security-side codes are in fact
\[
[16,8,7]_5
\]
and have the same complete ordinary Hamming weight enumerator
\[
\begin{aligned}
W(z)={}&1+704z^7+1992z^8+8000z^9+21280z^{10}\\
&+45696z^{11}+79856z^{12}+96768z^{13}\\
&+80160z^{14}+45504z^{15}+10664z^{16}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Nevertheless, \(C\) and \(D^\perp\) are not permutation equivalent. Their complete composition distributions differ already at minimum weight. If the symbols of \(\mathbb F_5\) are ordered as
\[
0,1,2,3,4,
\]
then \(C\) contains exactly \(8\) words having composition
\[
(9,0,0,1,6),
\]
whereas \(D^\perp\) contains no word with that composition.

Equivalently, both codes have \(704\) minimum-weight words, but the minimum layer of \(C\) occupies \(48\) distinct symbol-composition types while that of \(D^\perp\) occupies only \(20\). Thus equality of the entire Hamming weight distribution does not detect the inequivalence of the two security sides in this optimal quasi-cyclic example.

## Assumptions and scope

The code \(C\) is the \(R\)-submodule of
\[
R^2,\qquad R=\mathbb F_5[x]/(x^8-1),
\]
generated by
\[
(g_{11},g_{12}),\qquad (0,g_{22}),
\]
where
\[
g_{11}=x+2,
\]
\[
g_{12}=(x+2)(x^3+x^2+1),
\]
and
\[
g_{22}=(x+1)(x+2)(x+4)(x^2+2)(x^2+3).
\]

The code \(D\) is generated by
\[
(f_{11},f_{12}),\qquad (0,f_{22}),
\]
where
\[
f_{11}=x+3,
\]
\[
f_{12}=(x+3)(4x^3+x^2+1),
\]
and
\[
f_{22}=(x+1)(x+3)(x+4)(x^2+2)(x^2+3).
\]

The verification uses block coefficient order
\[
(a_0,\ldots,a_7\mid b_0,\ldots,b_7)
\]
rather than the source's interleaved order. These orders differ by one fixed coordinate permutation applied to both codes, so dimensions, Hamming weights, symbol compositions, and the question of permutation equivalence are unchanged.

## Proof

Taking all eight cyclic shifts of each displayed polynomial generator and row-reducing over \(\mathbb F_5\) gives
\[
\dim C=\dim D=8.
\]
The stacked generator matrix has rank \(16\), independently confirming
\[
C\oplus D=\mathbb F_5^{16}.
\]

A basis of \(D^\perp\) is obtained by exact modular nullspace computation. Exhaustive enumeration of all
\[
5^8=390625
\]
words of \(C\) and all \(5^8\) words of \(D^\perp\) gives the same Hamming enumerator displayed above. In particular,
\[
d(C)=d(D^\perp)=7.
\]

Permutation equivalence is more restrictive than equality of Hamming enumerators. For a word \(u\in\mathbb F_5^{16}\), define its composition
\[
\operatorname{comp}(u)=(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4),
\]
where \(n_a\) is the number of coordinates equal to \(a\). Every coordinate permutation preserves this ordered composition.

The exhaustive census gives
\[
\#\{u\in C:\operatorname{comp}(u)=(9,0,0,1,6)\}=8
\]
and
\[
\#\{u\in D^\perp:\operatorname{comp}(u)=(9,0,0,1,6)\}=0.
\]
For example, in block coefficient order,
\[
(0,4,0,0,0,0,0,4,0,3,4,4,4,0,0,4)
\]
is a codeword of \(C\) with that composition.

Therefore the complete weight enumerators of \(C\) and \(D^\perp\) differ. Since complete weight enumerators are invariant under coordinate permutations, \(C\) and \(D^\perp\) cannot be permutation equivalent.

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the six source polynomials over \(\mathbb F_5\), forms the two quasi-cyclic row spaces from cyclic shifts, checks both dimensions and the direct-sum rank, and computes \(D^\perp\) as the exact modular nullspace of \(D\).

The verifier then exhausts all \(390625\) words of each security-side code. It checks the full Hamming histogram, the common minimum distance \(7\), the exact count \(704\) of minimum-weight words on each side, and the complete-composition obstruction
\[
8\ne0
\]
for composition \((9,0,0,1,6)\). It also checks that the displayed witness belongs to \(C\).

Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

The primary article emphasizes that, unlike constacyclic and multidimensional cyclic LCPs, a general quasi-cyclic LCP need not have \(C\) permutation equivalent to \(D^\perp\). It cites an earlier quasi-cyclic counterexample for that general phenomenon. In Example 6.1, however, the article records only the optimal security parameter and the displayed polynomial generators; it does not give Hamming or complete weight enumerators and does not analyze permutation equivalence for this particular pair.

A previously studied quaternary row from the same article can have visibly different Hamming and higher-support data on its two security sides. The present example is qualitatively different: the two sides agree at every ordinary Hamming weight, so ordinary spectral information cannot reveal their inequivalence.

Focused searches by source identifier, exact Example 6.1 polynomials, parameters \([16,8,7]_5\), permutation-equivalence language, and complete-weight-enumerator language found no prior statement of this isospectral-but-inequivalent property.

## Limitations

The result concerns Example 6.1 as printed and proves non-equivalence only under coordinate permutations. It does not rule out equivalence under the larger monomial group, where independent nonzero coordinate scalings are also allowed.

The common Hamming weight enumerator is an exact finite invariant of this pair, not a classification of all optimal \([16,8,7]_5\) codes.

The result does not claim a new general existence theorem for permutation-inequivalent quasi-cyclic LCPs; the contribution is the stronger Hamming-isospectral inequivalence of this particular optimal-security example.
