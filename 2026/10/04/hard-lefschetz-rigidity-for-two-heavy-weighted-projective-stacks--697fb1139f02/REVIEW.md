# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The argument uses the quotient presentation of the weighted projective stack to compute the age of a sector represented by \(\lambda=e^{2\pi i\theta}\) as \(r\theta+\{a\theta\}+\{b\theta\}\), together with the standard identity that the ages of inverse sectors sum to the complex codimension of the fixed sector. In the equal-weight case, the primitive heavy root forces equality only at weight two. In the unequal case, the primitive \(b\)-th root forces \(2(r+a)=b(r+1)\); if \(a\ge2\), then \(0<b-a<1\), impossible, and \(a=1\) gives \(b=2\). The three surviving cases are checked directly.

## Originality
PASS with a residual folklore risk. Jiang gives the general weighted-projective twisted-sector and age machinery, and Fernandez gives the age-symmetry characterization of orbifold Hard Lefschetz, but neither inspected full text states the closed classification for \(\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\). Fernandez explicitly notes that understanding how common the condition is would be interesting. Coates--Iritani--Tseng record the isolated examples \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\) and \(\mathbb P(1,1,1,3)\), again without the infinite classification. Semantic searches for the exact family and for age-involution classifications found no source stating this result.

## Value
PASS. The Hard Lefschetz condition is a structural hypothesis in orbifold Hodge theory and crepant-resolution comparisons. A complete classification in a basic infinite weighted-projective family directly answers, on that family, the prevalence question highlighted in the foundational literature. The result is not a finite table: the primitive-sector proof gives a uniform rigidity theorem in every complex dimension at least three.

The closest literature provides the general age criterion and isolated examples; the principal limitation is that the classification does not extend to weight patterns with three or more heavy coordinates.
