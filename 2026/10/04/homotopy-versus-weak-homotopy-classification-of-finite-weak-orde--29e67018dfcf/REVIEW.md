# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The singleton-level contraction is an explicit two-comparability homotopy. In the complementary case every level has at least two elements, which excludes both up- and down-beat points. The order complex is exactly an iterated join of discrete level complexes. The standard cone quotient identity gives the wedge formula, and Stong core rigidity plus the intrinsic level decomposition gives the ordinary homotopy classification. McCord's theorem and top reduced homology give the weak classification. The bundled checker independently stress-tests representative level vectors and the singleton contraction without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest prior finite-space sources provide the general tools, not the combined classification. Barmak--Minian establish core rigidity and weak equivalence through order complexes; Mosquera-Lois gives modern Whitehead-failure examples and uses ordinal sums of two-point antichains for canonical sphere models. Searches under `weak order`, `ordinal sum of antichains`, `complete multipartite order`, `weak homotopy`, and product-of-level-size formulations did not locate a theorem reducing weak type to \(h\) and \(\prod_i(r_i-1)\) while simultaneously classifying ordinary homotopy by the full ordered level vector. The inspected comparisons cover general tools, self-map phenomena, and current Whitehead examples, but not equivalence classification between arbitrary weak orders.

## Value
PASS. The result supplies a complete and reusable structural family showing exactly how much finite-space information is lost under weak homotopy. It converts a qualitative Whitehead-failure phenomenon into an explicit invariant comparison, producing systematic examples rather than an isolated counterexample. The contractible singleton boundary is part of the same classification rather than an ad hoc exception.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is Barmak--Minian's finite-space homotopy machinery and Mosquera-Lois's 2026 study of Whitehead's theorem for minimal finite models. Pouzet--Zaguia supplies the weak-order/lexicographic-sum terminology but studies perpendicularity rather than topology. A residual risk remains that the join calculation and core rigidity may have been assembled into an equivalent classification in older literature under different terminology; no such statement was found in the inspected sources or database searches.
