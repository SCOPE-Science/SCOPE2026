# Ideal-based zero-divisor graphs recover finite chain-quotient parameters

## Finding

Let \(R,R'\) be finite commutative rings with identity and let \(I\subset R\), \(I'\subset R'\) be proper ideals. Suppose \(R/I\) and \(R'/I'\) are finite commutative chain rings whose maximal ideals have nilpotency lengths \(\ell,\ell'\ge3\), residue-field orders \(q,q'\), and put \(s=|I|\), \(s'=|I'|\). Then \[\Gamma_I(R)\cong\Gamma_{I'}(R')\iff(\ell,q,s)=(\ell',q',s').\] Indeed, \(\Gamma_I(R)\) has exactly \(\ell-1\) degree classes, and the class of quotient valuation \(i\) has size \(s(q-1)q^{\ell-i-1}\). Thus the graph intrinsically recovers \(\ell\), then \(q\) from consecutive class-size ratios, and then \(s\). The threshold \(\ell\ge3\) is sharp: for \(\ell=2\), \(\Gamma_I(R)\cong K_{s(q-1)}\), so \(q\) and \(s\) need not be separately recoverable; for example \(\Gamma_0(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)\) and \(\Gamma_{(4)}(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)\) are both \(K_2\).

This gives a complete graph-isomorphism classification inside the finite chain-quotient family once the quotient has nilpotency length at least three, together with the exact low-length obstruction.

## Assumptions and scope

Let
\[
S=R/I
\]
be a finite commutative chain ring. Write its maximal ideal as
\[
\mathfrak m=(\pi),
\]
let
\[
\mathfrak m^\ell=0,\qquad \mathfrak m^{\ell-1}\ne0,
\]
and let
\[
q=|S/\mathfrak m|,\qquad s=|I|.
\]
Assume first that
\[
\ell\ge3.
\]

The ideal-based zero-divisor graph \(\Gamma_I(R)\) has vertex set
\[
\{x\in R\setminus I:xy\in I\text{ for some }y\in R\setminus I\},
\]
with distinct vertices adjacent exactly when their product belongs to \(I\).

For a nonzero zero-divisor \(\bar x=x+I\in S\), define its valuation \(v(\bar x)=i\) by
\[
\bar x\in\mathfrak m^i\setminus\mathfrak m^{i+1},
\qquad
1\le i\le\ell-1.
\]

## Proof

For \(1\le i\le\ell-1\), let
\[
V_i=\{x\in R\setminus I:v(x+I)=i\}.
\]
Every nonzero zero-divisor of the finite chain ring \(S\) has a unique valuation in this range, and every quotient element has exactly \(s\) lifts to \(R\). Since
\[
|\mathfrak m^i|=q^{\ell-i},
\]
we have
\[
|V_i|=s(q-1)q^{\ell-i-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

Every element of valuation \(i\) is a unit multiple of \(\pi^i\). Hence for \(x\in V_i\) and \(y\in V_j\),
\[
xy\in I
\iff
(x+I)(y+I)=0
\iff
i+j\ge\ell.
\tag{2}
\]

Put \(n_i=|V_i|\). A vertex of \(V_i\) has degree
\[
d_i=
\sum_{j=\ell-i}^{\ell-1}n_j-\varepsilon_i,
\qquad
\varepsilon_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&2i\ge\ell,\\
0,&2i<\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
For \(1\le i\le\ell-2\),
\[
d_{i+1}-d_i
=
n_{\ell-i-1}-(\varepsilon_{i+1}-\varepsilon_i).
\tag{4}
\]
The second term is either \(0\) or \(1\). At its unique possible jump, the newly added layer has size at least \(2\), because it contains a positive power of \(q\). Thus
\[
d_1<d_2<\cdots<d_{\ell-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

Therefore the degree classes of the abstract graph are exactly the valuation layers. The graph determines
\[
\ell=1+\#\{\text{distinct vertex degrees}\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Ordering those degree classes increasingly, equation (1) gives
\[
\frac{|V_i|}{|V_{i+1}|}=q
\qquad(1\le i\le\ell-2),
\tag{7}
\]
so the graph determines \(q\), and then
\[
s=\frac{|V_{\ell-1}|}{q-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

Conversely, the triple \((\ell,q,s)\) determines every layer size by (1), every edge between layers by (2), and whether a layer is a clique or an independent set according as \(2i\ge\ell\) or \(2i<\ell\). Hence two graphs in this family are isomorphic exactly when their triples agree.

The restriction \(\ell\ge3\) is sharp. If \(\ell=2\), there is only one nonzero valuation layer, it has size \(s(q-1)\), and every pair of distinct vertices is adjacent. Hence
\[
\Gamma_I(R)\cong K_{s(q-1)}.
\]
For example,
\[
\Gamma_0(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)\cong K_2
\]
has \((q,s)=(3,1)\), whereas
\[
\Gamma_{(4)}(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)\cong K_2
\]
has quotient \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) and \((q,s)=(2,2)\).

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` checks the proof at two levels. First, it constructs the layer-size and degree formulas for residue-field orders
\[
2,3,4,5,7,8,9,11,13,16,
\]
nilpotency lengths from \(3\) through \(8\), and ideal sizes from \(1\) through \(6\), then reconstructs the parameters from the unlabeled degree classes.

Second, it constructs ideal-based graphs directly in several rings \(\mathbb Z/p^N\mathbb Z\), using the ideal generated by \(p^\ell\), and reconstructs the parameters from actual adjacency lists. It separately verifies the length-two collision.

## Relationship to prior work

Redmond introduced the ideal-based zero-divisor graph. Anderson and Shirinkam subsequently studied its relation to the ordinary zero-divisor graph, complete and complete-bipartite cases, and when an ideal-based graph can itself occur as an ordinary zero-divisor graph.

Anderson, Ebrahimi Atani, Shajari Kohan, and Ebrahimi Sarvandi studied precisely the case in which \(R/I\) is a chained ring. Their full paper proves structural facts such as the completeness of the square-zero part, total disconnectedness of its complement, and exact diameter and girth alternatives. It does not state an isomorphism classification by residue-field order, nilpotency length, and ideal cardinality.

A 2014 dissertation on ideal-based zero-divisor graphs explicitly studies the relationship between isomorphisms of \(\Gamma_I(R)\) and those of \(\Gamma(R/I)\). Its public abstract and repository metadata were readable, but its complete theorem text was not available through the inspected access path. This remains the main residual literature-access risk.

The present theorem isolates a finite chain-quotient regime in which the graph itself recovers three arithmetic parameters and identifies the exact length-two failure boundary. The special case \(I=0\) reduces to the ordinary zero-divisor graph of a finite chain ring, but the ideal cardinality and the sharp collision at length two are specific to the ideal-based setting.

## Limitations

The theorem reconstructs \((\ell,q,|I|)\), but it does not claim to reconstruct \(R\), \(I\) as an \(R\)-module, or the chain quotient \(R/I\) up to ring isomorphism: nonisomorphic finite chain rings can share the same residue-field order and nilpotency length.

The chain-ring hypothesis is essential because it gives a single valuation and the threshold rule (2). No analogous classification is asserted for general finite local quotients.

At nilpotency length two, only the product
\[
|I|(q-1)
\]
is recoverable from the graph.
