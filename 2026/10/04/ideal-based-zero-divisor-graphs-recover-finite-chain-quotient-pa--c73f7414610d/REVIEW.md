# Review

## Correctness

PASS. In a finite chain quotient, every nonzero zero-divisor has a unique valuation, each quotient element has exactly \(|I|\) lifts, and multiplication lands in \(I\) exactly when the quotient valuations sum to at least the nilpotency length. The resulting degree formula is strictly increasing with valuation, so the abstract graph recovers the valuation layers. Their count gives \(\ell\), consecutive class-size ratios give \(q\), and the last class size gives \(|I|\). The converse follows because the same triple fixes all layer sizes and threshold adjacencies. The length-two counterexample is checked directly.

Risk: the finite verifier is corroborative only; the full parameter family is justified by the symbolic valuation proof.

## Originality

PASS. The closest full primary paper on chained quotients was inspected through its propositions and theorems on the square-zero subgraph, diameter, and girth; it does not state the three-parameter graph-isomorphism classification. The foundational paper studies relationships with ordinary zero-divisor graphs and complete/complete-bipartite cases. Targeted searches under isomorphism, residue-field, nilpotency-length, valuation-layer, and ideal-cardinality formulations did not locate the final theorem.

Risk: a 2014 dissertation explicitly discusses graph-isomorphism relationships for ideal-based graphs, but its full theorem text was unavailable in the inspected access path. Its readable abstract does not state the chain-quotient reconstruction theorem, so this remains a genuine literature-access risk rather than evidence of coverage.

## Value

PASS. The result gives a natural complete isomorphism classification for a canonical algebraic graph family and identifies the exact low-length obstruction. It shows that the ideal-based graph contains information absent from the quotient graph alone—namely the size of the defining ideal—while separating the rigid regime from the complete-graph collision regime.

Risk: the theorem reconstructs only three arithmetic parameters, not the full chain-ring isomorphism type.
