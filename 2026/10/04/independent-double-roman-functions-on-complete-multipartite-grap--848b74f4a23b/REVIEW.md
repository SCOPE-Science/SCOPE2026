# Review of Independent double Roman functions on complete multipartite graphs

## Correctness
PASS. Independence forces all positive labels into one part. A zero inside that part is impossible because it would see no positive neighbor, and a label \(1\) there is impossible because it would have no adjacent label \(2\) or \(3\). Thus the supporting part is entirely labeled \(2\) or \(3\), while every outside vertex is zero. The outside double Roman condition is then equivalent to the presence of a \(3\) or of at least two \(2\)-labels. This yields exactly all \(2/3\)-assignments for a part of size at least two and only label \(3\) for a singleton part. The generating polynomial follows by direct counting. A definition-level exhaustive checker agrees through order nine.

## Originality
PASS. The 2019 initiating preprint and the 2020 full article study minimum independent double Roman domination through general inequalities, complexity-related structure, and comparisons with independent domination variants. The 2020 article's general bounds already imply the minimum value on much of the complete-multipartite family, so that minimum is not treated as novel. No inspected source gives an arbitrary complete-multipartite classification of every feasible function or its weight enumerator. Exact and semantic searches for complete multipartite, complete bipartite, polynomial, enumerator, and all-function formulations returned no equivalent statement.

## Value
PASS. Independent double Roman domination is a studied domination variant, but minimum-weight information discards almost all feasible-function structure. On the canonical complete multipartite class, the theorem gives the entire function space, every weight coefficient, the total number of functions, and the number of minimum functions in one compact formula. This is a natural exact enumerative refinement of the prior optimization theory rather than a relabeling of the known minimum bounds.
