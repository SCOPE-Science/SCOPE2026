# Infinite excess is forced for normalized normal orbit frames

## Finding

Let \(T\) be a bounded normal operator on a separable Hilbert space \(H\), and let \(g\in H\) satisfy
\[
T^n g\neq0\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Assume that
\[
x_n=\frac{T^n g}{\|T^n g\|},\qquad n\ge0,
\]
is a frame for \(H\). Let
\[
\Phi:\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\longrightarrow H,\qquad
\Phi(a)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nx_n
\]
be its synthesis operator. Then
\[
\dim\ker\Phi=\infty.
\]
In fact, for every \(N\ge0\), \(\ker\Phi\) contains a nonzero vector supported in
\[
\{N,N+1,\ldots\}.
\]
Thus every normalized orbit frame generated by a bounded normal operator has infinite frame excess, where excess is understood in the standard synthesis-kernel sense.

## Assumptions and scope

The normalized orbit is assumed to be defined at every nonnegative integer, so \(T^ng\neq0\) for every \(n\). The statement concerns one-generator normalized orbits of bounded normal operators.

The result does not assert that deleting an arbitrary infinite subsequence leaves a frame, and it does not identify a particular removable subsequence. Infinite-dimensional synthesis kernel is weaker than the strong subsampling properties known for special unnormalized Carleson frames.

## Proof

Because the normalized orbit is a frame, it is complete, so \(g\) is cyclic for \(T\). Use the scalar spectral model and rescale the outer spectral radius to one. The model has a finite positive measure \(\mu\) supported in \(\overline{\mathbb D}\), moments
\[
m_n=\int_{\overline{\mathbb D}}|z|^{2n}\,d\mu(z),
\]
and normalized monomials
\[
u_n(z)=\frac{z^n}{\sqrt{m_n}}.
\]
The frame \((x_n)\) is unitarily equivalent to \((u_n)\) in \(L^2(\mu)\).

Let \(\mathcal H_0\) be the analytic Hilbert space in Tian's measure model:
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n,
\qquad
\|f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2=\sum_{n\ge0}|c_n|^2m_n.
\]
Then \((u_n)\) is an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal H_0\), and the coefficient map
\[
U_0:\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\longrightarrow\mathcal H_0,
\qquad
(a_n)\longmapsto\sum_{n\ge0}a_nu_n
\]
is unitary. The frame synthesis operator in the spectral model is
\[
J^*:\mathcal H_0\longrightarrow L^2(\mu).
\]
Consequently
\[
\dim\ker\Phi=\dim\ker J^*.
\]

Tian proves first that
\[
\ker J^*\neq\{0\}.
\]
The same theorem shows that \(\mu\) is purely atomic in \(\mathbb D\),
\[
\mu=\sum_jw_j\delta_{\lambda_j},
\]
with no interior accumulation of the atoms, and the following corollary identifies the synthesis kernel:
\[
\ker J^*
=
\{f\in\mathcal H_0:f(\lambda_j)=0\text{ for every }j\}.
\]

Now use the outer-radius normalization. Since \(|z|\le1\) on the support of \(\mu\),
\[
m_{n+1}\le m_n\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Hence multiplication by \(z\) is a contraction on \(\mathcal H_0\). Indeed, if
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n,
\]
then
\[
\|zf\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2
=
\sum_{n\ge0}|c_n|^2m_{n+1}
\le
\sum_{n\ge0}|c_n|^2m_n
=
\|f\|_{\mathcal H_0}^2.
\]
The zero-set description of \(\ker J^*\) therefore gives
\[
f\in\ker J^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z^kf\in\ker J^*
\qquad(k\ge0).
\]

Choose \(0\neq f\in\ker J^*\). The functions
\[
f,zf,z^2f,\ldots
\]
are linearly independent. Indeed, a finite relation would have the form
\[
P(z)f(z)=0
\]
for a polynomial \(P\). Elements of \(\mathcal H_0\) are analytic in \(\mathbb D\), and a nonzero analytic function is nonzero on some open set; hence \(P\) vanishes on an open set and therefore \(P=0\). Thus
\[
\dim\ker J^*=\infty.
\]

Finally, multiplication by \(z\) shifts the \((u_n)\)-coefficient support one place to the right:
\[
zu_n
=
\sqrt{\frac{m_{n+1}}{m_n}}\,u_{n+1}.
\]
All weights are positive because the normalized orbit is defined for all \(n\). Therefore the coefficient vector of \(z^Nf\) is nonzero and supported in \(\{N,N+1,\ldots\}\). Pulling it back through \(U_0\) gives the claimed arbitrarily late synthesis relations.

## Verification

No finite experiment is used in the proof. The critical inputs from the recent source were checked in the full primary text:

1. the unitary coefficient identification \(\ell^2\simeq\mathcal H_0\);
2. the proof that \(\ker J^*\neq\{0\}\);
3. the atomic spectral conclusion;
4. the exact zero-set description of \(\ker J^*\).

The additional argument is internal: \(m_{n+1}\le m_n\) makes multiplication by \(z\) bounded on \(\mathcal H_0\), the zero-set description makes the kernel shift-invariant, and analyticity makes the powers of any nonzero kernel element linearly independent.

The conclusion is an infinite-dimensional statement proved analytically, not inferred from truncations or numerical rank tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Tian's 2026 paper introduces the measure model used here and proves that the synthesis kernel is nonzero as part of the spectral-structure theorem for normalized normal orbit frames. It does not state that the kernel is infinite-dimensional; the term “excess” does not appear in the paper. The present observation uses Tian's later zero-set characterization together with the contractive multiplier \(f\mapsto zf\) to upgrade one nonzero dependence to infinitely many independent dependencies, including dependencies supported arbitrarily far out in the orbit.

There is an active redundancy theory for unnormalized dynamical frames. Christensen, Hasannasab, Philipp, and Stoeva prove strong subsampling and finite-removal properties for a special class of Carleson frames. Krishtal and Miller substantially strengthen the subsequence theorem for positive Carleson spectra. Wu proves that finitely generated unnormalized dynamical frames of positive operators have infinite excess via power stability. Those results do not imply the present statement: normalized iterates generally are not an orbit of a fixed operator because the normalization factor changes with \(n\), and Wu explicitly requires positivity for the unnormalized theorem.

The result therefore isolates a different mechanism for redundancy in the normalized normal setting: shift-invariance of the synthesis kernel in the measure-dependent analytic space.

## Limitations

The theorem proves infinite synthesis-kernel dimension and arbitrarily late nonzero coefficient relations. It does not show that every finite deletion, every arithmetic subsequence, or any prescribed infinite deletion remains a frame.

The argument depends on normality through the scalar spectral model and Tian's kernel characterization. No corresponding claim is made here for general nonnormal operators or for finitely many normalized orbit generators.
