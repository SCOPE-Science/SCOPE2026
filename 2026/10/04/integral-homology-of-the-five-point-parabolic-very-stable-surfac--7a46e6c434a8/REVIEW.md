# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The sixteen exceptional classes on a degree-\(4\) del Pezzo surface give a \(5\)-regular triangle-free incidence graph, hence \(40\) distinct double intersections. This yields \(\chi(W)=-8\) and \(\chi(Y)=16\). The very-stable locus is a smooth affine surface, so it has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex of dimension at most \(2\). Combining this with the published \(H_1(Y,\mathbf Z)\cong\mathbf Z^{10}\) gives free \(H_2(Y,\mathbf Z)\cong\mathbf Z^{25}\) and vanishing above degree \(2\). The published group homology has the same free rank, so the Hopf surjection is an isomorphism.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating full text distinguishes ordinary \(H_1(Y,\mathbf Z)\) from \(H_2(\pi_1Y,\mathbf Z)\); it does not state ordinary \(H_2(Y,\mathbf Z)\), the Euler characteristic, the complete homology profile, or the Hopf-map isomorphism. Exact and alias searches found no covering statement. A prior same-source result concerning normalization of the wobbly divisor for \(n\ge3\) does not imply this surface-complement homology calculation.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem determines all ordinary integral homology of the moduli-theoretic very-stable surface and resolves the space-versus-group-homology gap left by the closest source. The Hopf-map isomorphism is a structural consequence useful for comparing geometric and group-theoretic topology, while the stated limitation avoids any unsupported asphericity claim.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Bhattacharya--Matsubara, arXiv:2609.31421v1, especially Theorems 2.6 and 5.5. Donagi--Pantev supplies the identification of the surface wobbly divisor with the sixteen exceptional curves. The result is specific to \(n=2\) and does not determine \(\pi_2(Y)\) or prove that \(Y\) is a \(K(\pi,1)\).
