# Juvenile consumption creates a unique predator-invasion cutoff
## Finding
For the age-structured role-reversal model of Suarez, Cameron, Fagan, and Levy, the predator-free state has an exact Euler--Lotka invasion threshold. With \(\bar x=r/a\), define
\[
\bar B(\tau)=B(\bar x,\tau),\qquad m_0(\tau)=\mu_B(\tau)+\mu_Me^{-\rho\bar x},\qquad A(\tau)=\int_0^\tau\varphi_{\{s<\tau^*\}}(s)\,ds,
\]
and
\[
\mathcal R_P(g)=\int_0^\infty \bar B(\tau)\exp\!\left[-\int_0^\tau m_0(s)\,ds-g\bar xA(\tau)\right]d\tau.
\]
Whenever the integral is finite, \(\mathcal R_P(g)\) is the net production of newborn predators generated by one newborn predator in the prey environment \(\bar x\). The linearized predator can invade exactly when \(\mathcal R_P(g)>1\). For the smooth maturation function printed in the source, \(\mathcal R_P(g)\) is strictly decreasing in \(g\) and tends to zero. Hence, if \(\mathcal R_P(0)>1\), there is one and only one positive critical juvenile-consumption rate \(g_c\) with \(\mathcal R_P(g_c)=1\); if \(\mathcal R_P(0)\le1\), no increase of \(g\) can restore invasion.

## Assumptions and scope
The source model is
\[
x'=x\left(r-ax+s\int_0^{\tau^*}u(t,\tau)\,d\tau-b\int_{\tau^*}^\infty u(t,\tau)\,d\tau\right),
\]
\[
u_t+u_\tau=-\mu(x,\tau)u,\qquad u(t,0)=\int_0^\infty B(x,\tau)u(t,\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
with
\[
B(x,\tau)=kx\varphi_{\{\tau\ge\tau^*\}}(\tau)+\widetilde B(\tau)(1-e^{-\zeta x}),
\]
\[
\mu(x,\tau)=gx\varphi_{\{\tau<\tau^*\}}(\tau)+\mu_B(\tau)+\mu_Me^{-\rho x}.
\]
Here \(r,a>0\), \(g\ge0\), and \(\tau^*>0\). The result fixes \(\tau^*\) and all parameters other than \(g\). For the untruncated renewal model, assume \(\mathcal R_P(0)<\infty\); where derivatives of the characteristic root are used, also assume the corresponding first moment is finite. The paper's numerical phase-diagram specialization imposes the maximal lifespan \(L=30\), so all displayed renewal integrals may be restricted to \([0,L]\) and these conditions are automatic.

The conclusion concerns linear predator invasion at the predator-free state. It does not claim global nonlinear stability, identify the entire basin boundary, or exclude distant coexistence equilibria or periodic attractors.

## Proof
At \(u=0\), the prey equation reduces to \(x'=x(r-ax)\), so the positive predator-free prey equilibrium is \(\bar x=r/a\). If \(x=\bar x+\xi\) and \(u=v\) are infinitesimal perturbations, the prey component has
\[
\xi'=-r\xi+\bar x\left(s\int_0^{\tau^*}v(t,\tau)\,d\tau-b\int_{\tau^*}^\infty v(t,\tau)\,d\tau\right).
\]
In the predator equation and renewal boundary, every derivative with respect to \(x\) is multiplied by the equilibrium density \(u=0\). Therefore the predator perturbation does not receive \(\xi\) at first order. The linearization is block upper triangular and the predator block is
\[
v_t+v_\tau=-\bar\mu_g(\tau)v,
\qquad
v(t,0)=\int_0^\infty \bar B(\tau)v(t,\tau)\,d\tau,
\]
where
\[
\bar\mu_g(\tau)=m_0(\tau)+g\bar x\varphi_{\{\tau<\tau^*\}}(\tau).
\]
The prey-only diagonal eigenvalue is \(-r<0\).

Seek a predator exponential mode \(v(t,\tau)=e^{\lambda t}q(\tau)\). Solving the age equation gives
\[
q(\tau)=q(0)\exp\!\left[-\lambda\tau-\int_0^\tau\bar\mu_g(s)\,ds\right].
\]
Substitution into the renewal boundary yields
\[
1=F(\lambda,g):=\int_0^\infty \bar B(\tau)\exp\!\left[-\int_0^\tau m_0(s)\,ds-g\bar xA(\tau)-\lambda\tau\right]d\tau.
\]
Thus \(F(0,g)=\mathcal R_P(g)\). For real \(\lambda\), \(F\) is strictly decreasing wherever births occur at positive ages. Hence \(\mathcal R_P(g)>1\) gives a unique real \(\lambda(g)>0\), and equality gives \(\lambda=0\). If \(\mathcal R_P(g)<1\) and a complex characteristic root had \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge0\), then
\[
1=|F(\lambda,g)|\le F(\operatorname{Re}\lambda,g)\le F(0,g)=\mathcal R_P(g)<1,
\]
a contradiction. Therefore no exponential predator mode has nonnegative real part when \(\mathcal R_P(g)<1\).

The source uses
\[
\varphi_{\{\tau<\tau^*\}}(\tau)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\nu(\tau^*-\tau)}},\qquad \nu>0,
\]
so \(A(\tau)>0\) for every \(\tau>0\). Let
\[
K(\tau)=\bar B(\tau)\exp\!\left[-\int_0^\tau m_0(s)\,ds\right].
\]
Differentiating under the integral gives, for every integer \(n\ge0\),
\[
(-1)^n\mathcal R_P^{(n)}(g)=\bar x^n\int_0^\infty A(\tau)^nK(\tau)e^{-g\bar xA(\tau)}d\tau\ge0.
\]
For \(n=1\), positivity of \(A\) and positive births on a set of positive measure make the inequality strict. Hence \(\mathcal R_P\) is strictly decreasing. Also, for every \(\tau>0\), the factor \(e^{-g\bar xA(\tau)}\) tends to zero as \(g\to\infty\), and it is dominated by the integrable kernel \(K\). Dominated convergence gives \(\mathcal R_P(g)\to0\). The intermediate value theorem then gives a unique \(g_c>0\) whenever \(\mathcal R_P(0)>1\).

At a simple real characteristic root, implicit differentiation of \(F(\lambda(g),g)=1\) gives the sharper monotonicity
\[
\lambda'(g)=-\bar x\,
\frac{\int_0^\infty A(\tau)K(\tau)e^{-g\bar xA(\tau)-\lambda(g)\tau}d\tau}
{\int_0^\infty \tau K(\tau)e^{-g\bar xA(\tau)-\lambda(g)\tau}d\tau}<0.
\]
Thus increasing juvenile consumption can cross the local predator-invasion boundary at most once.

## Verification
The source's open full text was checked for the prey equation, renewal boundary, birth and death functions, smooth maturation functions, finite-lifespan numerical specialization, and the numerical method used to classify the predator-free region. The paper reports that \(g\) and \(\tau^*\) are the most influential parameters and that predators become extinct at large \(g\) for sufficiently large \(\tau^*\); it does not state a net reproductive number or an Euler--Lotka invasion threshold.

The accompanying `verify.py` replays three independent algebraic/numerical checks on an admissible finite-lifespan instance of the source functional forms: the prey diagonal derivative is \(-r\); \(\mathcal R_P(g)\) decreases strictly on a test grid; and the characteristic root changes sign at the unique numerical solution of \(\mathcal R_P(g)=1\). This finite check is a stress test only; the proof above, not the test instance, establishes the quantified statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Suarez et al. investigate the 15-parameter role-reversal model primarily through existence/positivity theory, finite-difference equilibrium stability, Latin-hypercube sampling, and phase diagrams. Their full text identifies \(g\) and \(\tau^*\) as the most influential parameters and observes predator extinction at large \(g\), but no net reproductive threshold or Euler--Lotka invasion formula is given.

Lu and Liu (2018) prove threshold dynamics using a predator net reproductive number in a different system: the prey, rather than the predator, is age structured, and the predator is an unstructured ODE population. This establishes that reproductive-threshold methods are standard in structured ecology, but it does not imply the present source-specific functional \(\mathcal R_P(g)\), its complete monotonicity in juvenile consumption, or the unique \(g_c\) for the role-reversal model.

Ripoll and Font (2023) study numerical stability and Hopf bifurcation in a different age-structured Lotka--Volterra system. Their results do not contain the role-reversal mortality term \(g\bar x\varphi_{\{\tau<\tau^*\}}\) or its monotone invasion cutoff.

## Limitations
The result is local to predator invasion of the predator-free equilibrium. It does not prove global predator extinction for every initial condition when \(\mathcal R_P(g)<1\), nor global persistence when \(\mathcal R_P(g)>1\). In an infinite-lifespan formulation, semigroup exponential-stability statements require standard tail or exponential-moment hypotheses beyond the characteristic-root exclusion proved here; the paper's finite-lifespan numerical specialization avoids that issue. The maturation age \(\tau^*\) changes both the birth and mortality profiles, so no monotonicity in \(\tau^*\) is asserted.
