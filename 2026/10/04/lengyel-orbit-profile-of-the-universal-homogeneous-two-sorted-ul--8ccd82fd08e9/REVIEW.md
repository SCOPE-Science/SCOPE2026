# Review

Correctness: PASS. An injective finite point tuple in the full dc-structure is classified by the equality/order pattern of its pairwise distances. In an ultrametric, thresholding at the distinct realized distance levels produces a strict chain from the discrete to the indiscrete partition; conversely every such chain gives an ultrametric by assigning each pair its first merge level. The primary 2026 source proves that finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces form a Fraïssé class and that its limit is dc-homogeneous, so equality of these finite dc-types is exactly equality of automorphism orbit. Repetitions are separated by the coordinate equality partition, giving the Stirling transform. The OEIS/Lengyel recurrence then proves the exact identity \(b_n=2a_n\) for \(n\ge2\).

Originality: PASS, with a priority caveat. The classical enumeration A005121 and its recurrence/asymptotic are explicitly prior art and are not claimed as new. Exact web searches for the 2026 object together with “tuple orbit”, “partition chain”, “Lengyel”, and A005121 did not locate the orbit-profile statement. Full-text searches of both 2026 dc-ultrametric papers found no occurrences of “Lengyel” or “partition lattice” and no matching finite-tuple enumeration. Three semantic-index queries returned orbit-profile records for random posets, the Rado graph, random bipartite structures, and the random distributive lattice, but no two-sorted ultrametric result. The shortness of the deduction means folklore duplication cannot be excluded.

Value: PASS. The result turns a newly introduced canonical Fraïssé automorphism action into a completely explicit all-arity profile, identifies it with a classical partition-lattice sequence, transfers a sharp asymptotic, and gives the particularly clean full-tuple law \(b_n=2a_n\). It also sharply illustrates the difference between dc-automorphisms, which retain only the order of finite distance levels, and isometries, which retain the actual rational distances.
