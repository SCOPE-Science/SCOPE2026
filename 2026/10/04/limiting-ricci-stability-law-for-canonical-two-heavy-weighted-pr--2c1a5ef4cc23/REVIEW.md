# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof has four independent components: the Reid--Tai reduction to the exact canonical lattice region; derivation of the weighted-projective simplex barycentric coordinates from the fan relation; application of the published toric log-Fano convex-body formula; and an elementary Riemann-sum/area calculation. The singular applicability is covered by the klt toric log-Fano theorem, since canonical singularities are klt and the boundary divisor is zero. The finite checker is corroborative rather than a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The known general toric results already imply the single-space expression \(R=(r+2)/(r+a+b)\), so that specialization is explicitly treated as prior-covered. The assessed claim is instead the complete empirical limiting law, its piecewise survival function, exact mean \(\log(3)/3\), and sharp finite endpoints over the canonical two-heavy family. Searches for the invariant together with weighted projective spaces, canonical singularities, and the two-heavy parameterization did not locate this distribution. The closest inspected sources provide the general analytic invariant or the fan relation, not the family-level census.

## Value
PASS. The greatest lower Ricci bound is a standard quantitative measure of the failure of a Fano variety to be Kähler--Einstein and is the endpoint of a natural continuity method. The theorem converts the complete canonical two-heavy family into an explicit probability law, giving both a global asymptotic benchmark and sharp finite extremizers. The limiting mean \(\log(3)/3\) and the piecewise law are structural consequences of the canonical parameter cone rather than an arbitrary finite statistic.

The principal limitation is that the empirical measure is uniform on weight pairs; other natural weighting schemes are not addressed. The literature search also leaves residual risk that an equivalent family-level computation appears under terminology not captured by the searches.
