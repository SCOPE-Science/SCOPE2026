# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The two source asymptotics are on the same mirror-distinguishing 2-bridge-knot ensemble. For knots, \(2g\) is the minimum first Betti number among orientable spanning surfaces and \(\Gamma\) is the minimum among all spanning surfaces, so \(D=2g-\Gamma\ge0\). Subtracting the two verified averages gives \(\overline D(c)=c/6+1/18+o(1)\). The standard bound \(2g\le c-1\) makes \(D/c<1\), and the elementary bounded-variable estimate yields the stated positive-density inequality and the \(1/11\) specialization.

## Originality
PASS. The recent primary paper explicitly recalls prior average-genus work and proves the average-unoriented-genus theorem, but does not state the mean orientability penalty, the \(2/3\) normalized comparison, or the knot-level density consequence. The earlier average-genus papers predate the new marginal theorem. Full-text and targeted literature searches using the constants, invariant names, and positive-proportion formulation found no equivalent statement. Residual risk remains for an unindexed short observation.

## Value
PASS. Orientability is a natural constraint on exactly the same class of spanning surfaces, so \(2g-\Gamma\) measures a geometrically motivated complexity penalty rather than an arbitrary invariant difference. The positive-density theorem upgrades a mean calculation to a statement about a nonvanishing proportion of individual knots with linearly large penalty, which is useful structural information about typical 2-bridge spanning surfaces.
