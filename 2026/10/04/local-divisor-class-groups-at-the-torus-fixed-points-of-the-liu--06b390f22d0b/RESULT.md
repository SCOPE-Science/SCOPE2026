# Local divisor class groups at the torus fixed points of the Liu--Zhu small-alpha examples
## Finding
Let \(X_n\) be the \(n\)-dimensional K-polystable toric \(\mathbf Q\)-Fano variety constructed by Liu and Zhu from the face fan of
\[
Q_n=\left\{d\in\mathbf R^{n+1}:\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i=0,\quad -(2n-1)\le d_i\le2\right\}.
\]
For every \(n\ge2\), the \(2(n+1)\) torus-fixed affine maximal-cone charts split into two cyclic orbits, corresponding to the facets
\[
d_i=-(2n-1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_i=2.
\]
If \(U_-\) is any lower-facet chart, then
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_-)\cong\mathbf Z/(2n-1).
\]
If \(U_+\) is any upper-facet chart, then
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_+)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbf Z/8,&n=2,\\[3pt]
\mathbf Z^{\,n(n-2)}\oplus\mathbf Z/2,&n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the fixed-point singularities have two sharply different local divisor-class profiles: the lower orbit is always simplicial with cyclic torsion of order \(2n-1\), whereas for \(n\ge3\) the upper orbit has a free class-group defect of rank \(n(n-2)\) in addition to \(2\)-torsion.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\), as in the complex toric setting of the source construction. The notation \(U_\sigma\) denotes the affine toric chart attached to a full-dimensional cone \(\sigma\) of the face fan. The group \(\operatorname{Cl}(U_\sigma)\) is the Weil divisor class group of that affine chart.

The statement is a classification of the maximal-cone affine charts at torus fixed points. It is not a classification of all local analytic singularity types, all orbit-stratum local class groups, deformation types, or completed local rings.

## Proof
Set
\[
\ell=2n-1.
\]
Liu and Zhu identify the cocharacter lattice with
\[
N\cong\left\{d\in\mathbf Z^{n+1}:\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i=0\right\},
\]
and the vertices of \(Q_n\) are the vectors \(v_{p,q}\), indexed by ordered pairs \(p\ne q\), whose coordinates are
\[
d_p=-\ell,\qquad d_q=1,\qquad d_i=2\quad(i\notin\{p,q\}).
\]

First note that every coordinate inequality cuts out a facet. For \(d_i=-\ell\), assigning all remaining coordinates the common value \(\ell/n=2-1/n\) gives a relative-interior point of that face. For \(d_i=2\), assigning all remaining coordinates the common value \(-2/n\) does the same. Hence the face fan has exactly \(2(n+1)\) maximal cones, one for each coordinate upper or lower facet.

For a full-dimensional affine toric cone \(\sigma\) with primitive ray generators \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\), the standard divisor sequence gives
\[
M\longrightarrow\mathbf Z^r\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(U_\sigma)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently the free rank is \(r-n\), and the torsion subgroup is canonically the quotient
\[
N/N_\sigma,
\]
where \(N_\sigma\) is the sublattice generated by the primitive rays.

By cyclic symmetry it suffices to study the facets with last coordinate fixed.

For the lower facet \(d_n=-\ell\), the rays are \(v_{n,q}\) for \(0\le q<n\), so there are exactly \(n\) rays. Identify \(N\) with \(\mathbf Z^n\) by retaining the first \(n\) coordinates. The ray vectors become
\[
w_q=2\mathbf 1-e_q,\qquad 0\le q<n.
\]
Their differences satisfy
\[
w_q-w_r=e_r-e_q,
\]
so the generated lattice contains the root lattice
\[
A_{n-1}=\left\{z\in\mathbf Z^n:\sum z_i=0\right\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_i(w_q)_i=2n-1=\ell
\]
for every \(q\). Therefore the ray lattice is exactly
\[
\left\{z\in\mathbf Z^n:\sum z_i\equiv0\pmod{\ell}\right\}.
\]
Its quotient in \(\mathbf Z^n\) is cyclic of order \(\ell\). Since the cone has \(r=n\) rays, there is no free part, and
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_-)\cong\mathbf Z/\ell=\mathbf Z/(2n-1).
\]

For the upper facet \(d_n=2\), the rays are \(v_{p,q}\) with
\[
p,q\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},\qquad p\ne q.
\]
Hence there are
\[
r=n(n-1)
\]
rays. In the same \(\mathbf Z^n\) coordinates, every such ray has coordinate sum \(-2\).

Assume first \(n\ge3\). Given distinct \(i,j\), choose \(p\) distinct from both. Then
\[
v_{p,i}-v_{p,j}=e_j-e_i.
\]
Thus the upper ray lattice contains \(A_{n-1}\). Since every upper ray has coordinate sum \(-2\), and at least one such ray maps to \(-2\) under the sum homomorphism, the ray lattice is exactly
\[
\left\{z\in\mathbf Z^n:\sum z_i\equiv0\pmod2\right\}.
\]
Hence
\[
N/N_\sigma\cong\mathbf Z/2.
\]
The free rank of the class group is
\[
r-n=n(n-1)-n=n(n-2),
\]
so the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups gives
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_+)\cong\mathbf Z^{\,n(n-2)}\oplus\mathbf Z/2.
\]

It remains to treat \(n=2\). The upper facet has two rays. In \(\mathbf Z^2\) coordinates they are
\[
(-3,1),\qquad(1,-3).
\]
The corresponding \(2\times2\) matrix has determinant \(8\), and its entries have greatest common divisor \(1\). Its Smith normal form is therefore \(\operatorname{diag}(1,8)\), yielding
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_+)\cong\mathbf Z/8.
\]
This proves the stated classification.

## Verification
The accompanying script `artifacts/verify_lz_fixed_classgroups.py` enumerates the Liu--Zhu vertices for \(2\le n\le8\), checks all facet incidence counts, verifies the lower-facet determinant \(\lvert\det(2J-I)\rvert=2n-1\), checks the special \(n=2\) upper determinant \(8\), and explicitly verifies the root-lattice difference identities used for all upper facets with \(n\ge3\). Its recorded output ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite computation is only a replay of the incidence arithmetic and lattice identities. The proof above establishes the result for every \(n\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Liu and Zhu give the complete polar box-slice description of \(Q_n\), the explicit primitive ray generators \(v_{p,q}\), and use them to prove that \(X_n\) is a K-polystable toric \(\mathbf Q\)-Fano variety with
\[
\alpha(X_n)=\frac{2}{2n+1}.
\]
Their paper states that these varieties are singular but does not compute the affine divisor class groups of the two torus-fixed-point orbits.

Wu later generalizes the construction to realize the full rational toric alpha spectrum and discusses coarse singularity properties such as Gorenstein, \(\mathbf Q\)-factorial, and canonical behavior. The public searches and indexed summaries inspected did not state the local class-group decomposition above. In particular, the free-rank formula \(n(n-2)\) and the torsion split \(\mathbf Z/(2n-1)\) versus \(\mathbf Z/2\) (with the exceptional \(\mathbf Z/8\) surface case) are strictly finer data than a yes/no \(\mathbf Q\)-factoriality statement.

## Limitations
The result concerns the affine maximal-cone charts determined by the face fan and therefore the torus fixed points. It does not classify class groups at every nonclosed torus orbit or determine completed local analytic types.

The originality search did not find the stated class-group decomposition, but absence from semantic and web search is not a proof that no equivalent calculation exists. The full text of Wu's later preprint could not be retrieved through the available primary-source path during this run; its comparison was therefore limited to the arXiv metadata and detailed indexed summaries, which is recorded as a residual risk.
