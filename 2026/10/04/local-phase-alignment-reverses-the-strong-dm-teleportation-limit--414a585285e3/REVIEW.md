# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The thermal spectrum and phase are reconstructed from the primary source. The local gate \(U_D\) maps the phase-twisted odd-sector eigenvectors to standard Bell states without changing thermal weights. The Bell-overlap calculation gives the aligned product-Pauli channel, and averaging over the exact logical Bloch sphere used by the source yields the stated \(F_{{\rm align}}\). Replacing the coherence by its fixed-basis real component \(1/\sqrt{{1+D^2}}\) reproduces Zhang's Eq. (11) exactly, so the positive difference and asymptotic limits follow analytically.

## Originality
PASS. The nearest own-ledger item on the same 2007 source concerns a factor-two correction to output concurrence in the unaligned protocol and its finite-\(D\) strong-coupling tail; it neither changes the resource basis nor determines the average-fidelity improvement here. The general Horodecki teleportation theorem covers optimization through singlet fraction conceptually but does not state this source-specific two-copy input-ensemble formula. The directly related later DM-teleportation literature inspected did not expose the exact phase-aligned correction; one especially close 2010 paper was only partially accessible and is retained as a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The result changes the operational interpretation of a standard thermal-spin teleportation calculation: strong \(z\)-axis DM coupling does not intrinsically drive this protocol to the classical fidelity boundary when the known local phase is compensated. The asymptotic change from \(2/3\) to \(1\), together with the exact positive gain for every \(JD\neq0\), is a substantive benchmark for basis-adapted implementations rather than a routine reparameterization.

## Closest literature and limitations
Zhang supplies the fixed-basis protocol and formula; Gürkan--Pashaev supply the detailed complex DM eigenstructure; Horodecki--Horodecki--Horodecki provide the general optimal-fidelity/singlet-fraction framework. No global LOCC optimality claim is made for \(F_{{\rm align}}\).
