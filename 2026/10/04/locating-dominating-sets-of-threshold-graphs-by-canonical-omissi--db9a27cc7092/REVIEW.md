# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the two possible trace forms in the canonical threshold decomposition and derives domination, same-side separation, and cross-side separation exactly. Same-block twin arguments justify the one-omission-per-block reduction. The packaged exhaustive checker agrees with the theorem for every canonical profile through order 10 and does not substitute for the general proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest full-text split-graph source proves only a twin-free half-order bound, while the closest threshold-specific source concerns the strictly stronger solid-location-domination number. Searches under threshold, creation-sequence, split, locating-dominating, and polynomial/enumeration terminology found no statement implying the five-condition all-set theorem. A 2025 paper gives minimum formulas only for complete split and split-corona graphs according to its accessible abstract. Residual risks are older weakly indexed terminology and one inaccessible 2015 full text.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves a natural exact-enumeration question on a standard structured graph class. It explains how singleton twin blocks are the only source of nontrivial compatibility constraints and gives a product formula, exact minimum, and exact minimum-set count in the natural twin-thick regime. This is structurally stronger than recomputing a known minimum parameter.

## Closest literature and limitations
Foucaud et al. (2016) treat locating-domination on twin-free split graphs by upper bounds rather than exact threshold structure. Junnila et al. (2019) characterize threshold graphs through the maximum solid-location-domination number, which is a stronger predicate and does not imply the ordinary code family. Foucaud (2015) treats restricted-class complexity; direct full-text retrieval failed in this run, so that source remains an access risk rather than negative novelty evidence. The theorem is limited to connected threshold graphs, and outside the no-singleton-block case the enumerator is left as an explicit admissible-pair sum.
