# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the explicit level structure of the canonical minimal finite sphere model. For two noncollapsed source levels, order preservation forces strict growth of the minimum target rank. This gives the pigeonhole collapse for \(n>m\). A collapsed level makes the entire simplicial image a cone. In equal dimensions, the only way to avoid collapse is to use every target rank once; descending from the top level then forces a levelwise bijection. The remaining \(n<m\) case is the standard vanishing \(\pi_n(S^m)=0\). The degree conclusion follows from the cross-polytope join model.

## Originality
PASS, with a documented residual source-access risk. Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches using finite-space, poset, non-Hausdorff-suspension, sphere-map, Hopf, Whitehead and degree formulations did not locate the all-dimensions rigidity statement. Barmak–Minian provides the minimal sphere models, not the map classification. The accessible older map-construction literature uses larger or subdivided models for nontrivial classes. Mosquera-Lois (2026) partially overlaps the equal-dimensional weak-equivalence endpoint but does not cover the cross-dimensional nullity theorem or the complete directly-realized class set. Two older construction papers were inaccessible in full after lawful access attempts, so possible partial overlap remains a stated risk.

## Value
PASS. Finite models are used not only to represent spaces but also to build explicit models of maps. The result isolates a sharp structural limitation of the smallest sphere models: they preserve the weak homotopy type of each sphere but erase every nonzero unstable map between different sphere dimensions, and even exclude self-degrees of absolute value at least two. This explains, uniformly, why classical finite-poset constructions of Hopf/Whitehead-type maps enlarge or subdivide their sphere models, and it identifies a natural map-complexity boundary for future minimality questions.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest current theorem located is Mosquera-Lois's facet-essential Whitehead rigidity result, which concerns weak homotopy equivalences and therefore only overlaps part of the equal-dimensional case. The older Whitehead-square and quaternion-multiplication papers are especially relevant examples of enlarging/subdividing sphere models; their accessible abstracts do not state the general obstruction proved here. The result does not quantify the least enlargement needed for a particular nonzero homotopy class.
