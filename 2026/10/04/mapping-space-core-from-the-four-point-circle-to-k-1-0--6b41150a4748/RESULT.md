# Mapping-space core from the four-point circle to \(K_{1,0}\)
## Finding
Let \(C\) be the four-point minimal finite circle and let \(K_{1,0}\) be the sixteen-point Cianci--Ottina minimal finite model of the Klein bottle. The pointwise finite mapping space \(\operatorname{Map}(C,K_{1,0})\) contains exactly \(1{,}288\) order-preserving maps and has exactly \(11\) homotopy components, with sizes \(1{,}024,64,64\), and eight components of size \(17\). Its Stong core is homeomorphic to \(K_{1,0}\sqcup \mathfrak C_4\sqcup \mathfrak C_4\sqcup D_8\), where \(\mathfrak C_4\) is the eight-point crown and \(D_8\) is an eight-point discrete space. More precisely, the \(1{,}024\)-point component retracts by beat-point deletions to the sixteen constant maps, each \(64\)-point component retracts to an eight-point crown, and each \(17\)-point component is contractible.

Equivalently, after deleting beat points componentwise, the mapping space has a \(40\)-point core consisting of one copy of the target Klein-bottle model, two circle cores, and eight isolated points.

## Assumptions and scope
The source \(C\) is the height-two poset with two minimal points \(u_0,u_1\), two maximal points \(v_0,v_1\), and all four relations \(u_i<v_j\). The target \(K_{1,0}\) is the sixteen-point height-three poset drawn in Figure 4(b) of Cianci--Ottina, with minimal points \(c_1,\ldots,c_4\), middle points \(b_1,\ldots,b_8\), and maximal points \(a_1,\ldots,a_4\). The cover relations used by the verifier are transcribed explicitly in `artifacts/verify.py`.

The mapping space is the set of all order-preserving functions \(C\to K_{1,0}\), ordered pointwise. For finite \(T_0\)-spaces this is the specialization order of the compact-open function space. Homotopy components are therefore the connected components of this finite mapping space, and deleting a beat point is a strong deformation retract.

The claim is finite and exact. It does not assert a formula for other finite models of the Klein bottle, for subdivisions of \(C\), or for arbitrary representatives of classical homotopy classes.

## Proof
Write a map as \(f=(f(u_0),f(u_1),f(v_0),f(v_1))\). It is order preserving exactly when
\[
f(u_i)\leq f(v_j)\qquad (i,j\in\{0,1\}).
\]
The verifier exhausts all \(16^4\) functions and retains exactly those satisfying these four inequalities. It obtains \(1{,}288\) maps. As an independent count, after choosing \(x=f(u_0)\) and \(y=f(u_1)\), each upper image may be chosen independently from the common upper set \(U(x)\cap U(y)\); hence the exact count is also
\[
\sum_{x,y\in K_{1,0}} |U(x)\cap U(y)|^2=1{,}288.
\]

Two maps are adjacent in the comparability graph when one is pointwise below the other. Finite-space homotopy is generated by such comparable-map steps, so the connected components of this graph are exactly the homotopy classes. Exhaustive component computation gives sizes
\[
1{,}024,\ 64,\ 64,\ \underbrace{17,\ldots,17}_{8\ \mathrm{times}}.
\]

For every component the verifier repeatedly applies the exact beat-point criterion: an up-beat point has a least strict upper element, and a down-beat point has a greatest strict lower element. In the \(1{,}024\)-point component, \(1{,}008\) certified deletions leave exactly the sixteen constant maps \(x\mapsto p\), and their inherited pointwise order is exactly the order of \(K_{1,0}\). Each \(64\)-point component undergoes \(56\) certified deletions and leaves an eight-point height-two poset with four minima, four maxima, eight cover relations, connected cover graph, and degree two at every vertex; this is \(\mathfrak C_4\). Each \(17\)-point component undergoes \(16\) certified deletions and leaves one point. The final \(40\)-point subspace has no beat points, so it is a core.

Core uniqueness for finite \(T_0\)-spaces then identifies the Stong core of the mapping space as
\[
K_{1,0}\sqcup \mathfrak C_4\sqcup \mathfrak C_4\sqcup D_8.
\]

The expected terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK maps=1288 components=11 sizes=1024,64,64,17x8 deletions=1248 up=368 down=880 core=40`

The computation is exhaustive over a finite set; it is not an extrapolation from samples. The mathematical use of the computation is limited to this explicitly specified source and target.

## Relationship to prior work
Cianci and Ottina classify the minimal finite models of the Klein bottle and give the Hasse diagram of \(K_{1,0}\), but their result does not compute the finite mapping space from the four-point circle or its component cores. Barmak gives the general pointwise function-space order, finite-space homotopy criterion, beat-point reductions, and core theory used here, but no instance-specific decomposition of \(\operatorname{Map}(C,K_{1,0})\).

Searches for the exact source-target pair, aliases such as “four-point finite circle” and “pseudocircle,” and the numerical signatures \(1{,}288\), \(1{,}024\), \(64\), and \(17\) did not locate a published statement of this decomposition. Search failure is not itself a novelty proof; the originality assessment also compared the full statements and scopes of the two directly relevant sources.

## Limitations
The result concerns direct continuous maps from the four-point model, with no barycentric subdivision of the source. It therefore should not be read as a classification of all classical maps \(S^1\to\) Klein bottle. The exact core depends on the chosen finite model \(K_{1,0}\); the other sixteen-point minimal Klein-bottle model is not analyzed here. The literature search cannot exclude every unpublished or unindexed computation.
