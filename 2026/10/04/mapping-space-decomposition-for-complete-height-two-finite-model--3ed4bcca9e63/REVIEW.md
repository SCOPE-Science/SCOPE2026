# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The map classification is exhaustive: an off-level lower image forces the entire upper source level to one target maximum, and dually for an off-level upper image. This yields the exact map count. A level-preserving map is isolated exactly when both level restrictions are nonconstant. Every remaining map is comparable to a constant map, giving one nonisolated component. The explicit retraction \(\rho\) is order preserving, fixes constants, and the chain-by-chain comparison proves relative contiguity of \(\operatorname{id}\) with \(i\rho\), which establishes the claimed order-complex strong deformation retraction. The finite verifier checks the formulas and the contiguity mechanism on representative cases; it is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest same-object prior result in the checked collection covers a self-map specialization at the level of homotopy classes; the present claim treats independent source and target sizes and adds the topology of the unique nonisolated mapping-space component. Speed's Hom-poset paper studies the componentwise map order but its inspected text concerns Möbius functions. May's account of Stong supplies the general map-space/homotopy machinery but not this complete-height-two classification. Targeted published-finding corpus searches returned no statement implying the formula or the target retraction. Residual risk remains that an unindexed or differently phrased poset-Hom computation may exist.

## Value
PASS. Complete height-two posets are canonical minimal finite models of graph-type spaces, and their pointwise map posets are natural finite mapping spaces. The result gives a full structural decomposition rather than a small numerical census: one component retains exactly the target's graph homotopy type, while every other component is a rigid singleton with a closed count. This distinguishes source-dependent rigidity from target-dependent component topology and supplies a reusable template for studying finite mapping spaces.

## Closest literature and limitations
The archive-qualified anchor is Barmak--Minian, arXiv:math/0611156v1. Speed, DOI:10.1017/S0004972700021250, studies \(\operatorname{Hom}(P,Q)\) with componentwise order but not the mapping-space homotopy classification found here. The result is limited to complete height-two posets with all four level sizes at least two, and the deformation-retraction statement is about order complexes.
