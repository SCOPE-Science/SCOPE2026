# Review of Matrix amplification collapses \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)-fineness to fineness

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the 2026 source's two explicit premises that a \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)-fine ring is simple and that \(M_n(R)\) remains \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)-fine. Simplicity gives \(J(R)=0\). Leroy--Matczuk's identity \(\Delta(M_n(R))=J(M_n(R))=M_n(J(R))\) for \(n\ge2\) then gives \(\Delta(M_n(R))=0\), so its radical square-root is precisely the nilpotent set. The claimed ordinary-fine decomposition follows directly. The full-corner calculation for \(E_{11}\) is explicit and standard.

## Originality
PASS with stated residual risk. Exact and semantic searches for the source, matrix-fineness formulation, full-corner formulation, and Morita consequence found no matching published statement. The closest primary literature splits the ingredients: the 2026 preprint gives \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)-matrix closure and an open size-one question; Leroy--Matczuk give the matrix \(\Delta\) identity; Călugăreanu--Lam formulate Morita/full-corner descent as an open question; Zhou studies Morita contexts under hypotheses that do not imply general descent. The inaccessible full body of the 2026 preprint remains the main residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The theorem sharply localizes the new open problem: every proper full matrix amplification is already in the classical fine class. It also links a counterexample to the new problem to a negative solution of the longstanding Morita-invariance problem for fine rings. This is a structural reduction between two motivated questions rather than a routine parameter specialization.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Danchev--Hasanzadeh--Moussavi--Javan (arXiv:2608.30734v1), Leroy--Matczuk (DOI: 10.1090/conm/727/14640), Călugăreanu--Lam (DOI: 10.1142/S0219498816501735), and Zhou (DOI: 10.1142/S021949882250205X). The result does not decide whether a non-fine \(\sqrt{\Delta}\)-fine ring exists, and it does not establish Morita invariance in either direction generally.
