# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The rank-two Schur-cover commutator relation gives the determinant adjacency criterion modulo \(p^a\). A clique spans an isotropic \(R\)-submodule of \(R^2\), where \(R=\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z\). Smith normal form reduces every submodule to \(p^iRe_1\oplus p^jRe_2\) up to an invertible change of basis. Isotropy is exactly the condition \(i+j\ge a\), yielding the sharp order bound \(p^a\); equality is exactly \(i+j=a\), so every maximum clique is an order-\(p^a\) subgroup and conversely. The stabilizer-index calculation gives the stated count by invariant-factor type. The embedded finite replay independently enumerates all maximum cliques in five small cases.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational 2020 source gives no maximum-clique theorem. The 2025 full-text treatment supplies the general finite-abelian commutator relation, the \(a=1\) clique decomposition, and one complete subgroup for \(a=2\), but does not state the general clique number, classify every maximum clique, or count them. Exact-claim and equivalent-formulation searches in the published-result database found no covering result. A 2026 direct-product article remains a bounded bibliographic risk because only its abstract was readable in the inspected interface.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural infinite-family extremal theorem in an explicitly developing graph-on-groups program. It upgrades isolated low-exponent examples to a sharp all-\(p\), all-\(a\) theorem, identifies the extremizers as a canonical class of subgroups, and gives their exact type distribution. The result is structural rather than a parameter substitution or finite computation.

The main residual risk is that an equivalent isotropic-submodule theorem may exist under finite-chain-ring terminology, or in the partially accessible 2026 direct-product paper.
