# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof reconstructs the maximum law as \(G_M=\Delta\circ F\), uses the published \(d\)-Lipschitz characterization of every copula diagonal to obtain an almost-everywhere derivative \(q\in[0,d]\) with mean one, and then differentiates the composition to obtain \(g_M/f=q\circ F\). Convexity of \(\Delta\) is equivalent to existence of a nondecreasing derivative representative, which is exactly likelihood-ratio order after composition with the increasing \(F\). The converse is immediate in the uniform-margin realization. The \(f\)-divergence formula follows by the probability-integral transform; Jensen and the endpoint chord inequality give the two sharp bounds. The upper and lower Fréchet–Hoeffding diagonal checks and the independence calculation agree with the formulas; upper-bound uniqueness is claimed only after imposing diagonal convexity.

## Originality
**PASS with stated residual risk.** Focused searches were made for the density-ratio identity, copula-diagonal convexity as likelihood-ratio order, and maximum-versus-margin \(f\)-divergence bounds. The motivating Cattaneo–Masini–Underwood full text was inspected in the sections defining copula diagonals, diagonal convexity, and examples; searches within the paper found no likelihood-ratio or stochastic-order formulation. The Jaworski–Rychlik open full text was inspected through its single-order-statistic characterization and likewise contains no likelihood-ratio formulation. Published findings returned by semantic search concern different copula information quantities, Gaussian magnitudes, order-law identification, and anti-concentration, and do not imply this statement. Belzunce–Gurler–Ruiz (2011) is a genuine residual risk because its abstract explicitly concerns likelihood-ratio ordering of order statistics under dependence, but accessible material describes comparisons among order-statistic vectors and conditional order statistics rather than this maximum-versus-own-margin characterization; full text was unavailable in the inspected lawful sources.

## Value
**PASS.** The motivating paper introduces convexity of the copula diagonal as a useful nonparametric dependence restriction for maxima. The finding turns that analytic restriction into an exact stochastic-order property, so the condition acquires a directly interpretable meaning and imports the standard consequences of likelihood-ratio dominance. The companion information identity is invariant to the common margin and yields a sharp dimension-only envelope with exact extremal diagonals. These are structural facts about the same object that drives the new anti-concentration theory, not a routine parameter substitution or a numerical refinement.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest fully inspected sources are Cattaneo–Masini–Underwood (arXiv:2502.07699), which supplies the diagonal framework and convexity condition but not the likelihood-ratio or information statements, and Jaworski–Rychlik (2008), which characterizes feasible order-statistic marginals but does not state the equivalence. Durante–Fernández-Sánchez (2011) is relevant for constructions with convex diagonals. Belzunce–Gurler–Ruiz (2011) is the principal unresolved literature risk because only abstract-level material was lawfully accessible. The theorem is restricted to regular continuous common margins for its likelihood-ratio formulation and does not characterize the entire copula.
