# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Every ideal of a finite product of fields is uniquely determined by a nonempty proper coordinate support, and ideal intersection corresponds exactly to support intersection. For two selected supports, total domination is equivalent to being adjacent and having union all coordinates. The same two vertices then form a matched edge, so minimum total and paired sets coincide. The inclusion-exclusion count is the number of surjections onto the three nonempty regions \(A\setminus B\), \(B\setminus A\), and \(A\cap B\), divided by the free swap of the two supports.

Risk: the proof depends on the product-of-fields hypothesis and on excluding the zero and unit ideals from the graph.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary source proves only ordinary domination number two for Artinian products of fields. A later full-text treatment of finite principal ideal rings likewise states ordinary domination and several metric/convexity invariants, but no total or paired domination result. Exact searches under the ideal-intersection and equivalent set-intersection formulations did not locate the existence threshold, the coincidence of minimum total and paired sets, or the exact enumerator.

Risk: because products of fields reduce to a classical subset-intersection graph, an unindexed combinatorics source could contain an equivalent minimum-pair classification under different terminology.

## Value

PASS. The result resolves the natural strengthened-domination question left open by the known ordinary value: ordinary domination remains two already for two field factors, whereas total and paired domination fail there and become two exactly from three factors onward. It further classifies every minimum strengthened dominating pair and gives a closed exact count, so the conclusion is structural rather than a routine value substitution.

Risk: the invariant detects the number of factors but not their field sizes.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
