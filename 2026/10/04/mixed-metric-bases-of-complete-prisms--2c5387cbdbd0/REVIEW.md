# Review of Mixed metric bases of complete prisms

## Correctness
PASS. For each matching index \(j\), comparing \(a_i\) with \(a_ia_j\) for any \(i\ne j\) proves that every mixed generator must contain \(a_j\) or \(b_j\), giving the lower bound \(n\). Equality therefore forces a matching transversal. A transversal with fewer than two selected vertices in either clique fails through an explicit edge-edge or vertex-edge collision. When both layers contribute at least two selected vertices, every vertex and edge has distances only in \(\{0,1,2\}\), and the exact \((Z_0,Z_2)\) table recovers its type and indices. The sole cross-layer edge ambiguity requires two selected vertices in each layer and hence \(n=4\). Thus for \(n\ge5\) the classification is exact. Exhaustive direct mixed-distance checks agree for every \(5\le n\le9\).

## Originality
PASS. The foundational mixed-metric paper studies trees and grids and explicitly asks for product-graph relationships, but targeted full-text inspection found no prism treatment. The 2021 prism-related paper studies a different \(4n\)-vertex planar graph built by augmenting the ordinary cycle prism with extra and pendant vertices; it is not \(K_n\square K_2\). Targeted exact-phrase, alias, and semantic searches for complete prisms, clique prisms, and \(K_n\square K_2\) found no equivalent mixed metric dimension formula or basis enumeration.

## Value
PASS. \(K_n\square K_2\) is a basic Cartesian-product family, directly aligned with the foundational paper’s open product-graph direction. The result gives not only the exact mixed metric dimension but a complete structural description and count of every optimum. The \(n=4\) obstruction also explains precisely why the stable matching-transversal phase starts at \(n=5\).
