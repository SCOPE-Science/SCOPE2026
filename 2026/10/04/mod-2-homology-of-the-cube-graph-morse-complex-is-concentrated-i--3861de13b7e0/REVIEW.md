# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The definition of the graph Morse complex is reconstructed as acyclic partial orientations with each tail used at most once. The finite state space is exhausted twice by independent enumeration routes. The nonempty face vector is \((24,240,1296,4080,7488,7424,3072)\), and it is independently matched by the cube Laplacian generating polynomial. Exact mod-\(2\) boundary ranks \((1,23,217,1079,3001,4487,2933)\) are recomputed with three elimination schemes, every \(\partial^2\) identity is checked, and the Euler characteristic agrees. The result is only a mod-\(2\) statement.

## Originality
PASS. The closest checked hypercube result gives connectivity bounds for \(\mathcal M(Q_n)\), not these Betti ranks. A Laplacian result controls the f-vector but not the boundary maps. Exact Morse-complex papers inspected treat other graph families. Equivalent-formulation and exact-parameter searches did not locate the cube-graph homology values. The principal residual risk is a 2020 thesis on directed forest complexes of Cayley graphs: its abstract is relevant, but its full text was inaccessible during the comparison, so exact overlap cannot be excluded.

## Value
PASS. The cube \(Q_3\) is the first hypercube beyond the cycle \(Q_2\), and hypercubes are explicitly used as a test family in the general connectivity literature. Finding nonzero reduced homology in both dimensions \(5\) and \(6\) is structural: it rules out a homotopy type that is a wedge of spheres all in one dimension and gives a precise benchmark for the first non-cycle hypercube.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest accessible literature consists of general Morse-complex connectivity bounds, exact computations for other graph families, and a Laplacian identity that determines face counts. None of those statements implies the mod-\(2\) boundary ranks. No integral-homology, torsion, ring, or full-homotopy-type conclusion is made. The inaccessible directed-forest thesis remains an explicit bibliographic risk.
