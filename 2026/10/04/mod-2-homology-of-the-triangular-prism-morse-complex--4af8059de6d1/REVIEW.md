# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The graph-Morse definition reduces exactly to compatible oriented incidences with no directed cycle. Exhaustive enumeration gives nonempty face counts \(18,126,428,705,450\), independently matched by a forest/root count. Augmented mod-\(2\) boundary ranks are \(1,17,109,319,386\); column and transposed-row elimination agree and every adjacent boundary composition is zero. Hence the reduced Betti ranks are \((0,0,0,0,64)\).

## Originality
PASS with a recorded residual risk. published-finding corpus and public-literature searches used the exact object, prism aliases, directed-forest terminology, and the numerical invariant. The closest inspected primary source, Scoville--Zaremsky, gives graph connectivity bounds but not this calculation. Courtney's Cayley-graph directed-forest thesis is materially relevant; its abstract was inspected and exact searches did not surface a prism statement, but its institutional full text was inaccessible, so possible coverage remains the principal residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The triangular prism is a canonical small cubic graph and natural Cayley/product graph. The primary literature explicitly presents precise Morse-complex topology as difficult and supplies general connectivity bounds. An exact first nonzero mod-\(2\) homology group is a reproducible benchmark for that program rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
Scoville--Zaremsky implies simple connectivity from \(|E(P)|=9\) and \(d(P)=3\), but not the degree-four rank. Courtney's closely related thesis remains access-limited. The result is restricted to \(\mathbb F_2\) homology of one graph.
