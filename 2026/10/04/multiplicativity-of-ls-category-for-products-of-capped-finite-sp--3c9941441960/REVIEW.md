# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof is reconstructed with explicit conventions, hypotheses, and quantifiers. The upper bound is the contractible principal-open cover indexed by global maxima. The lower bound is not computational: any categorical open containing two distinct global maxima contains a coordinate slice \(P_j\oplus D_2\); projection gives its inclusion into the factor, an explicit order retraction splits that inclusion, and a Stong-core argument proves the slice noncontractible. Hence each categorical open contains at most one global maximum. The argument applies to every finite number of factors.

The verifier is supplementary. It checks representative capped antichain bases, all maximal pairs in those examples, the slice containment, beat-point obstruction, and explicit retractions, but it is not used to infer the universal claim.

## Originality
PASS. The closest archive-qualified finite-space source gives maximal-point category bounds, while the inspected simplicial product source gives only a multiplicative upper bound. Later finite-space literature gives the one-factor cap formula but not the heterogeneous product lower bound. Targeted searches also found no exact database or table subsuming this family.

A prior result on topological complexity of one capped finite space already forces the equal-factor twofold category value as an intermediate consequence. That diagonal case is therefore not counted as new here. The accepted claim is the strictly broader arbitrary finite heterogeneous-product formula, together with the exact reduced two-factor defect.

## Value
PASS. Exact category of products is a natural structural question, and this family exhibits genuinely non-Hausdorff behavior: reduced product subadditivity fails by the explicit unbounded amount \((m-1)(n-1)\). The proof isolates a reusable coordinate-slice/retraction obstruction rather than relying on a finite census.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inspected sources are arXiv:1501.07540v1, arXiv:1606.01205v1, DOI:10.33044/revuma.3601, and arXiv:2609.39615v1. The theorem assumes nonempty noncontractible bases and cap sizes at least two. No claim is made for arbitrary finite spaces or contractible bases. Literature search cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent formulation.
