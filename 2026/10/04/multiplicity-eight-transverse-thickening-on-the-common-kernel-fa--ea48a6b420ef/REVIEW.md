# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is reduced to an explicit affine Grassmann chart. The coefficient ideal of \(\det M(x,y,z)\) is derived symbolically, three variables are eliminated by equations with unit coefficients, and a polynomial coordinate change separates two reduced-component coordinates from four transverse coordinates. The resulting transverse ideal is verified exactly. Its Gröbner basis has eight standard monomials, so the transverse length is \(8\); all degree-three monomials vanish, and the radical is the unique maximal ideal. The primary source's tangent-excess formula independently predicts four transverse tangent directions, agreeing with the chart. The \(\operatorname{GL}_3\)-orbit statement in the source transports the local type along the whole component.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the stated residual literature-access risk. The primary source proves generic non-reducedness by tangent-space dimension and, in its complete \(n=r=3\) example, identifies the reduced component with \(\mathbb P^2\); it does not state a local ring, multiplicity, Hilbert function, or nilpotence order. Direct full-text searches found no occurrence of “multiplicity”, “local ring”, “thickening”, or “length 8” covering the claim. published-index searches for the exact object plus “multiplicity 8”, “common kernel”, “local scheme structure”, and “Hilbert function 1,4,3” returned no matching published finding. The closest older general determinantal-Fano calculation treats the full nonsymmetric determinant hypersurface and has a different multi-component formal neighborhood, so it does not imply the symmetric local algebra here.

## Value
PASS. Generic non-reducedness alone does not quantify how thick the component is. The exact length \(8\), Hilbert function \((1,4,3)\), and nilpotence index \(3\) identify the infinitesimal structure of a natural moduli component and explain the source's four-dimensional tangent excess. This is a structural refinement of a named low-dimensional example rather than a routine recomputation of a tabulated invariant.
