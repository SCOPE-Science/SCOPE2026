# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** In an ultrahomogeneous relational structure, orbits on injective labeled tuples are exactly labeled age structures. Kwiatkowska–Malicki define the relevant age as finite posets together with a linear extension. Choosing the linear extension first gives \(n!\) total orders and, relative to each, exactly A006455\((n)\) suborders. Choosing the poset first gives the equivalent sum of its linear-extension counts. Equality partitions give the unrestricted-tuple Stirling transform. The small-arity brute-force replay independently confirms every finite counting bridge used in the argument.

## Originality
**PASS, with substantial folklore risk.** A006455 and its connection with total linear-extension counts are prior, and the defining Fraïssé class is prior. What targeted full-text, exact-sequence, equivalent-phrase, broader oligomorphic-profile, and semantic-index searches did not locate is the statement that these counts are precisely the tuple-orbit profile of the universal ordered poset, or its repeated-coordinate Stirling transform. Because the proof is short once the two literatures are juxtaposed, the novelty is best regarded as a model-theoretic identification rather than a new enumeration theorem.

## Value
**PASS.** The result assigns a classical, well-studied sequence to a canonical Polish permutation group and translates total linear-extension enumeration directly into oligomorphic orbit growth. It also places the universal ordered poset next to the random-poset reduct profile: the latter counts labeled posets, whereas the ordered structure counts each poset with a chosen linear extension.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kwiatkowska–Malicki study conjugacy phenomena for the universal ordered poset but do not state its finite tuple-orbit profile. OEIS A006455, Dean–Keller’s natural partial orders, Bevan–Cheon–Kitaev, and Brightwell–Prömel–Steger cover the underlying combinatorics. A semantic-index record on the random-poset reducts is the closest model-theoretic profile result found, but it concerns a different automorphism group and a different count. The asymptotic entropy corollary uses the classical enumeration of labeled posets and is not presented as new.
