# Necklace classification of maps from finite crowns to complete height-two spaces
## Finding
For integers \(m,r,s\ge 2\), let \(\mathfrak C_m\) be the \(2m\)-point crown with minimal points \(x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1}\), maximal points \(y_0,\ldots,y_{m-1}\), and relations \(x_i<y_{i-1},y_i\) modulo \(m\). Let \(P_{r,s}=A_r\oplus B_s\) be the complete height-two finite \(T_0\)-space with \(|A_r|=r\) and \(|B_s|=s\). Define
\[
c_\ell(q)=(q-1)^\ell+(-1)^\ell(q-1)
\]
and, for \(\ell\ge 2\),
\[
N_\ell(r,s)=\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid \ell}\varphi(\ell/d)c_d(r)c_d(s).
\]
Then the unbased finite-space homotopy classes of maps \(\mathfrak C_m\to P_{r,s}\) consist of one null class; for every \(2\le \ell<m\), one class for each simultaneous cyclic-shift orbit of a pair of proper colorings of the cycle \(C_\ell\) by \(r\) and \(s\) colors; and \(c_m(r)c_m(s)\) singleton classes of maximal reduced length. Consequently
\[
\bigl|[\mathfrak C_m,P_{r,s}]\bigr|
=
1+\sum_{\ell=2}^{m-1}N_\ell(r,s)+c_m(r)c_m(s).
\]
The reduced length and the cyclic orbit are obtained by viewing a level-preserving map as the alternating closed walk \(a_0,b_0,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_{m-1},b_{m-1}\) in \(K_{r,s}\) and cyclically deleting spurs \(u,v,u\).

Equivalently, a homotopy class has a well-defined cyclically reduced alternating core. Nonmaximal cores are remembered only up to simultaneous rotation of their lower- and upper-level colorings, whereas a maximal core has no slack and its representative is isolated.

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces are finite \(T_0\)-spaces, equivalently finite posets with continuous maps interpreted as order-preserving maps. Indices on the crown are taken modulo \(m\). Homotopy is ordinary unbased topological homotopy of maps between finite spaces; for finite posets it is generated by finite fences of pointwise-comparable maps.

The target \(P_{r,s}\) has two antichain levels and every lower point lies below every upper point. The restrictions \(m,r,s\ge 2\) are essential to the statement as written. The symbol \(\varphi\) denotes Euler's totient function. For \(\ell\ge 2\), \(c_\ell(q)\) is the number of proper \(q\)-colorings of the cycle \(C_\ell\).

## Proof
Write a map on crown minima and maxima as \(f(x_i)\) and \(f(y_i)\). First reduce arbitrary maps to level-preserving ones. If \(f(x_i)\in B_s\), order preservation along \(x_i<y_{i-1},y_i\) forces
\[
f(y_{i-1})=f(x_i)=f(y_i).
\]
Replacing \(f(x_i)\) by any point of \(A_r\) gives a pointwise smaller order-preserving map. Dually, if \(f(y_i)\in A_r\), then
\[
f(x_i)=f(y_i)=f(x_{i+1}),
\]
and replacing \(f(y_i)\) by any point of \(B_s\) gives a pointwise larger order-preserving map. Repeating these elementary comparable-map moves makes every crown minimum land in \(A_r\) and every crown maximum land in \(B_s\).

A level-preserving map is therefore encoded by cyclic words
\[
a_i=f(x_i)\in A_r,\qquad b_i=f(y_i)\in B_s.
\]
The only elementary changes between normalized representatives are the following local moves:
\[
b_{i-1}=b_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_i\text{ may be replaced by any point of }A_r,
\]
and
\[
a_i=a_{i+1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_i\text{ may be replaced by any point of }B_s.
\]
Indeed, the displayed equalities allow the changed source vertex to pass through the common neighboring target point. Conversely, if two arbitrary maps are pointwise comparable, they admit a common level-preserving normalization: at crown minima use the lower value when a comparison crosses target levels, and at crown maxima use the upper value. Thus no additional equivalence between normalized maps is created by leaving the level-preserving locus.

Now regard
\[
a_0,b_0,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_{m-1},b_{m-1},a_0
\]
as a closed alternating walk in the complete bipartite graph \(K_{r,s}\). The two local moves above are exactly replacements of the middle vertex in a spur \(u,v,u\). Cyclic deletion of such spurs gives a cyclically reduced core of even length \(2\ell\); the core is unique up to cyclic rotation, with the empty core corresponding to \(\ell=0\). A core of length two is impossible because every two-edge closed walk is itself a spur.

For completeness, the fixed-length spur calculus gives the converse. If a walk contains a spur, a redundant pair can be slid one position along the walk by the two allowed moves
\[
u,v,u,w,z\ \longmapsto\ u,w,u,w,z\ \longmapsto\ u,w,z,w,z,
\]
where the displayed vertices alternate between the two parts of \(K_{r,s}\). Repeating this slide gathers redundant pairs at any chosen cut of the reduced core; their middle vertices can then be changed arbitrarily. Hence two length-\(2m\) walks with the same reduced core are connected whenever \(\ell<m\). Because at least one redundant pair is then available, sliding it once around the core also changes the chosen cut, so cyclic rotations of the same core lie in the same component. This proves, by induction on \(m-\ell\), that nonmaximal components are exactly cyclic-rotation classes of reduced cores. For the empty core the same moves reduce to a constant map, yielding one null component.

If \(\ell=m\), there is no spur. Equivalently,
\[
a_i\ne a_{i+1}\quad\text{and}\quad b_i\ne b_{i+1}
\]
for every \(i\). Neither local move is then available, so every maximal reduced map is isolated.

It remains to count reduced cores. For \(\ell\ge2\), the \(a\)-word is a proper \(r\)-coloring of \(C_\ell\), and the \(b\)-word is a proper \(s\)-coloring. Their numbers are
\[
c_\ell(r)=(r-1)^\ell+(-1)^\ell(r-1),\qquad
c_\ell(s)=(s-1)^\ell+(-1)^\ell(s-1).
\]
For \(\ell<m\), simultaneous cyclic rotation acts on pairs of these colorings. A rotation whose shift has greatest common divisor \(d\) with \(\ell\) fixes exactly \(c_d(r)c_d(s)\) pairs. Burnside's lemma therefore gives
\[
N_\ell(r,s)=\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\varphi(\ell/d)c_d(r)c_d(s).
\]
At maximal reduced length there is no rotation quotient because each map is isolated. Summing the null component, all nonmaximal necklace classes, and all maximal isolated maps proves the formula.

## Verification
A standalone exhaustive checker enumerates every order-preserving map for the parameter tuples
\[
(2,2,2),\ (2,2,3),\ (3,2,2),\ (3,2,3),\ (3,3,3),\ (4,2,2),
\]
builds the pointwise-comparability graph of the map space, and compares its number of connected components with the closed formula. The observed component counts are respectively
\[
5,\ 13,\ 3,\ 7,\ 55,\ 7,
\]
and all agree with the theorem. The checker also verifies the two boundary identities corresponding to \(m=2\) and to \(r=s=2\) for a range of parameters. These finite checks support but do not replace the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak and Minian's finite-space framework identifies finite \(T_0\)-spaces with posets and develops minimal finite models of graphs, placing crowns and complete height-two graph models in the same topological setting. Dochtermann studies a different graph-theoretic notion of homotopy through graph Hom complexes. That theory does not imply the result here: graph \(\times\)-homotopy allows multihomomorphism moves that are strictly more permissive than pointwise comparability in the finite-space mapping poset, whereas maximal reduced maps in the present theorem are isolated.

Currie--Visentin and Farley give exact enumerations of order-preserving maps involving fences and crowns. Their results count maps rather than finite-space homotopy classes; Farley's crown-to-crown formulas also have crown targets, not the complete height-two targets \(P_{r,s}\) except at the four-point boundary.

Two previously known boundary cases are recovered without being re-counted as separate findings. When \(m=2\), the source itself is the complete height-two space \(P_{2,2}\), and the formula becomes
\[
1+(r^2-r)(s^2-s).
\]
When \(r=s=2\), the target is the four-point crown and the necklace sum collapses to the earlier crown-to-crown classification. The present statement supplies the cross-family classification for arbitrary \(m,r,s\ge2\).

## Limitations
The theorem concerns unbased homotopy classes for crowns mapping to complete height-two targets only. It does not describe the homotopy type of each nontrivial mapping-space component, nor does it extend the necklace formula to arbitrary height-two target graphs. The exhaustive checker covers only the finite parameter tuples listed above; it is not an exhaustive proof for unbounded parameters. No independent audit has been performed.
