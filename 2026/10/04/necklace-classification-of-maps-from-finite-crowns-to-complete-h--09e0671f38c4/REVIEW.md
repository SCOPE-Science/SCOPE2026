# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof first shows that every order-preserving map from the crown can be normalized to a level-preserving map by pointwise-comparable moves. It then proves that all homotopy information among normalized maps is generated by two explicit local moves, because any two comparable maps admit a common normalization. These moves are exactly fixed-length spur replacements in an alternating closed walk in \(K_{r,s}\). The spur-sliding identity supplies the converse classification for nonmaximal reduced length, while the absence of any spur makes maximal reduced maps isolated. Burnside's lemma then gives the stated orbit count.

The standalone verifier independently enumerates the full pointwise map poset for six small parameter tuples and reproduces the predicted component counts. The computation is evidence for boundary behavior, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of the closest literature separates three nearby statements. Barmak--Minian supply the finite-space and finite-graph-model framework but no crown-to-\(P_{r,s}\) map classification. Dochtermann classifies a different graph \(\times\)-homotopy through Hom complexes; its allowed moves are not the finite-space pointwise-comparability moves and therefore do not imply the isolated maximal classes here. Currie--Visentin and Farley enumerate order-preserving maps involving fences and crowns but do not quotient them by finite-space homotopy, and their crown targets do not cover general complete height-two targets.

The theorem has two already-covered boundary specializations: \(m=2\) specializes the complete-height-two mapping result, and \(r=s=2\) specializes the four-point crown target case. Those boundary statements are treated as prior coverage. The new claim is the joint arbitrary-\(m,r,s\) cross-family classification and its necklace formula.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a natural complete classification of homotopy classes between two standard families of finite graph models. It identifies a sharp structural transition: nonmaximal reduced loops are classified by cyclic necklaces because a spare spur can move the base cut, whereas maximal reduced loops are rigid and isolated. The formula unifies two previously separate boundary classifications and explains the change by a reusable local-move mechanism rather than by case enumeration.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest sources inspected were Barmak--Minian's finite graph models, Dochtermann's graph Hom-complex homotopy, Currie--Visentin's crown endomorphism enumeration, and Farley's maps between fences and crowns. No inspected source states or implies the finite-space homotopy formula for crown sources and arbitrary complete height-two targets. Search cannot prove global absence from the literature, so residual novelty risk remains. The theorem does not claim component homotopy types or arbitrary height-two target graphs.
