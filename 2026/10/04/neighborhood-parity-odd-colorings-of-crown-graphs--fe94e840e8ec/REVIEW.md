# Review of Neighborhood-parity odd colorings of crown graphs

## Correctness
PASS. For a proper coloring, the colors occurring oddly in \(N(a_i)\) are exactly \(P_B\triangle\{\varphi(b_i)\}\). This gives an exact criterion: when \(n\) is even every proper coloring is odd, while when \(n\) is odd each bipartition class must have at least three odd-sized color classes. Properness also forces any color used on both sides to be confined to one deleted matched pair. These two facts imply the complete three-color and four-color classifications and the counting formulas. Independent enumeration for \(3\le n\le9\) found no smaller palette and matched every claimed minimum coloring.

## Originality
PASS. The 2021 foundational paper and a 2024 same-parameter follow-up were inspected in full-text form and contain no crown-graph or complete-bipartite-minus-perfect-matching theorem. Targeted web and semantic searches under crown, deleted matching, and neighborhood-parity aliases found no equivalent statement. A recent proper conflict-free crown-graph theorem is a close comparison because that coloring notion is stronger; it does not imply the odd lower bound or the parity transition and gives a different answer on every even crown.

## Value
PASS. Crown graphs are a classical dense bipartite family and expose a clean parity phenomenon that is invisible to ordinary chromatic number: the odd chromatic number alternates between \(2\) and \(4\) with the parity of \(n\), apart from the single \(n=3\) boundary. The complete optimum classification explains this transition structurally and quantifies all minimum colorings. It also separates neighborhood-parity odd coloring from the stronger proper conflict-free variant on an infinite natural family.
