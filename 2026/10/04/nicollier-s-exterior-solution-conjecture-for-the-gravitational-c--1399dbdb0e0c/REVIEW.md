# Review of Nicollier's exterior-solution conjecture for the gravitational-center equation

## Correctness
PASS. For an exterior point, the side integral identity gives \(J_a=\int_{BC}|P-Q|^{-1}ds\) and cyclic analogues. Applying the divergence theorem to \(Q\mapsto |P-Q|^{-1}\) gives \(E(P)=n_aJ_a+n_bJ_b+n_cJ_c\). Equality in Nicollier's equation implies \(J_a/a=J_b/b=J_c/c\), while the side-normal balance \(a n_a+b n_b+c n_c=0\) then forces \(E(P)=0\). Strict separation of \(P\) from the compact convex triangle gives a direction with strictly positive field component, a contradiction. Every denominator is positive for an exterior point.

## Originality
PASS with residual literature risk. Nicollier's primary note explicitly ends by conjecturing that its equation has no solution outside the triangle. Abraham and Kovač prove that an exterior field cannot vanish and derive the distance equation from an interior stationary point, but the inspected full text does not establish the exterior converse from the distance equation to field vanishing. The new boundary-integral identity supplies precisely that missing implication. Exact-phrase, alias, \(X(5626)\), database, and exact-phrase searches found no covering theorem.

## Value
PASS. This closes an explicit published conjecture about whether the defining distance equation has spurious solutions outside the reference triangle. The proof also gives a structural interpretation of the three logarithmic terms as side contributions to the inverse-distance field, explaining why an exterior solution is impossible rather than excluding exterior points by case analysis or computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Abraham and Kovač's foundational treatment of the same potential and triangle center. It contains both ingredients adjacent to the result—nonvanishing of the exterior field and the interior distance relation—but only Nicollier later asks whether the distance relation can have exterior solutions. General gravitational/radial-center uniqueness theorems concern extrema of the potential and do not by themselves exclude algebraic solutions of Nicollier's equation. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased literature.
