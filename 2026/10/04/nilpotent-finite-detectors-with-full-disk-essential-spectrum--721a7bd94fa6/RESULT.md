# Nilpotent finite detectors with full disk essential spectrum
## Finding
Let \(M_n(\mathbb C)\) carry the operator norm induced by \(\ell_2^n\). For \(n\ge1\), define the nilpotent shift \(J_n\) by
\[
J_ne_j=e_{j+1}\quad(1\le j<n),\qquad J_ne_n=0.
\]
Set
\[
A=\left(\bigoplus_{n\ge1}M_n(\mathbb C)\right)_{\ell_\infty},\qquad a=(J_n)_{n\ge1}.
\]
Liu and Shen prove that this \(\ell_\infty\)-product is isometrically a Calkin algebra of a separable Banach space. Hence there are a separable complex Banach space \(X\) and an isometric unital Banach-algebra isomorphism
\[
\Phi:A\longrightarrow \operatorname{Cal}(X)=\mathcal B(X)/\mathcal K(X).
\]
Choose \(T\in\mathcal B(X)\) with \([T]=\Phi(a)\). Then
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm e}(T)=\overline{\mathbb D},
\]
and for every complex polynomial \(p\),
\[
\|p(T)\|_{\mathrm e}=\max_{|z|\le1}|p(z)|.
\]
Therefore the closed unital algebra generated by \([T]\) is isometrically isomorphic to the classical disk algebra \(A(\mathbb D)\), with the coordinate function sent to \([T]\).

If \(\rho_r\) denotes the finite detector obtained by transporting coordinate restriction to the first \(r\) matrix factors, then
\[
\rho_r([T])=(J_1,\ldots,J_r),\qquad \rho_r([T])^r=0.
\]
Thus every finite detector sees a nilpotent element. Nevertheless, for every polynomial \(p\),
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm e}(p(T))=p(\overline{\mathbb D}),
\qquad
\sigma\bigl(p(\rho_r([T]))\bigr)=\{p(0)\}.
\]
In particular, no fixed finite detector sees any of the nontrivial spectral geometry present globally.

## Assumptions and scope
The scalar field is \(\mathbb C\). The essential spectrum is the spectrum of the Calkin class. The finite detector maps are the coordinate restrictions supplied by the product realization; they are unital contractive homomorphisms, and the family is jointly faithful and norming because
\[
\|(b_n)\|=\sup_r\max_{n\le r}\|b_n\|.
\]
The result concerns this explicit element in the Calkin algebra furnished by the cited realization theorem. It does not claim that arbitrary finite-dimensional detector systems have the same spectral behavior.

## Proof
An element \((b_n)\) of \(A\) is invertible exactly when every \(b_n\) is invertible and \(\sup_n\|b_n^{-1}\|<\infty\). Fix \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\).

For \(|\lambda|>1\),
\[
(\lambda I-J_n)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{-k-1}J_n^k,
\]
so
\[
\|(\lambda I-J_n)^{-1}\|\le \sum_{k\ge0}|\lambda|^{-k-1}=\frac1{|\lambda|-1},
\]
uniformly in \(n\). Hence \(\lambda\notin\sigma_A(a)\).

For \(\lambda=0\), every \(J_n\) is singular. If \(0<|\lambda|\le1\), then the same inverse formula gives
\[
(\lambda I-J_n)^{-1}e_1
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{-k-1}e_{k+1}.
\]
When \(|\lambda|<1\), its norm is at least \(|\lambda|^{-n}\); when \(|\lambda|=1\), its norm is \(\sqrt n\). Thus the inverse norms are unbounded, and \(\lambda I-a\) is not invertible in \(A\). Therefore
\[
\sigma_A(a)=\overline{\mathbb D}.
\]
Because \(\Phi\) is a unital algebra isomorphism, spectra are preserved, proving the essential-spectrum identity.

For the polynomial norm, let \(S\) be the unilateral shift on \(\ell_2(\mathbb N)\). Every \(J_n\) is a finite compression of \(S\), so von Neumann's inequality gives
\[
\|p(J_n)\|\le \max_{|z|\le1}|p(z)|.
\]
Conversely, if \(x\) has finite support and \(n\) exceeds the support bound by at least \(\deg p\), then \(p(J_n)x=p(S)x\). Finite-support unit vectors are dense, hence
\[
\sup_n\|p(J_n)\|\ge\|p(S)\|.
\]
For the unilateral shift,
\[
\|p(S)\|=\max_{|z|\le1}|p(z)|,
\]
so
\[
\|p(a)\|=\sup_n\|p(J_n)\|=\max_{|z|\le1}|p(z)|.
\]
Transporting this identity through \(\Phi\) gives the essential-norm formula. Completing the polynomials in this norm yields an isometric copy of \(A(\mathbb D)\), and its image is exactly the closed unital algebra generated by \([T]\).

Finally, \(J_n^n=0\), so \((J_1,\ldots,J_r)^r=0\). Polynomial spectral mapping gives
\[
\sigma\bigl(p(\rho_r([T]))\bigr)=\{p(0)\},
\]
while Banach-algebra spectral mapping and \(\sigma_{\mathrm e}(T)=\overline{\mathbb D}\) give
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm e}(p(T))=p(\overline{\mathbb D}).
\]

## Verification
The proof is symbolic. The only non-elementary input used to obtain the ambient Calkin algebra is Liu--Shen's isometric realization of countable \(\ell_\infty\)-products of finite-dimensional unital Banach algebras. The spectral calculation is checked directly by the coordinatewise inverse criterion, and the polynomial norm identity is checked by finite compressions of the unilateral shift. No finite experiment is used as evidence for an infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu--Shen's Corollary 10.7 realizes arbitrary countable \(\ell_\infty\)-products of finite-dimensional unital Banach algebras as Calkin algebras, but their paper does not state the present spectrum or Jordan-block consequence; full-text searches for “spectrum” and “Jordan” return no occurrence. Blazhko--Homza--Wojtylak study nilpotent Jordan blocks in finite-dimensional Banach-algebra spectral-set estimates, but do not assemble all sizes into a Calkin algebra or obtain the finite-detector/global-spectrum separation above. Standard finite-section literature studies stability and spectral approximation under additional hypotheses; the present example exhibits the opposite boundary inside the new Calkin realization.

## Limitations
The disk algebra and the nilpotent finite sections are classical objects. The new point is their exact coexistence inside the finite-detector Calkin realization supplied by the 2026 theorem. The checked literature did not reveal the same Calkin-algebra statement, but an equivalent formulation under different finite-section terminology remains a residual originality risk. The result does not assert that spectra are never recoverable from finite detectors; it shows that joint faithfulness and norm detection alone do not force such recovery at any fixed finite stage.
