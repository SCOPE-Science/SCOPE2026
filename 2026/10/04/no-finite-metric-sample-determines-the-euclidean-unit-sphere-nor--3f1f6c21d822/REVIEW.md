# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the finite metric constraints to preservation of the Euclidean norm on the finite set \(W=F\cup(F-F\setminus\{{0\}})\). A generic unit vector has strict angular separation from every direction in \(W\), and the explicit max norm preserves exactly those finitely many values. The global distortion bound and the non-strict convexity certificate are immediate from the same formula.

## Originality
PASS. The closest recent source, arXiv:2609.31096v1, proves global unit-sphere rigidity and does not state a finite determining-set theorem. The closest inspected local-to-global source, arXiv:1810.02461v3, still assumes a surjective isometry of the entire sphere. Searches over finite samples, exact finite distances, strip truncations, and renorming aliases found no statement implying this exact all-finite-samples obstruction.

## Value
PASS. The finding supplies a clean boundary for the new sphere-rigidity theorem: exact finite data are fundamentally insufficient, even under arbitrarily small Banach--Mazur perturbations. This is stronger than merely exhibiting one pair of nonisometric norms with a common finite configuration.

## Closest literature and limitations
Nakamura--Tanaka establish full-sphere object-level rigidity in finite dimensions; Cabello Sánchez establishes a different local-to-global principle under a full-sphere surjective isometry plus local structure. The present finding does not address infinite or dense samples, approximate data, or marked extension.
