# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The construction is correct for every real dimension \(n\ge2\), every nonempty finite \(F\subset S_{\ell_2^n}\), and every \(\eta>0\). With \(W=F\cup\{x-y:x,y\in F,\ x\ne y\}\), a unit vector \(u\) can be chosen outside the finitely many lines generated by \(W\), so \(c=\max_{w\in W}|\langle u,w\rangle|/\|w\|_2<1\). Choosing \(\max\{c,(1+\eta)^{-1}\}<a<1\) and \(p(z)=\max\{\|z\|_2,|\langle u,z\rangle|/a\}\) gives \(\|z\|_2\le p(z)<(1+\eta)\|z\|_2\) and exact equality with the Euclidean norm on all vectors in \(W\). Hence all sampled unit norms and pairwise distances are preserved. The unit ball is the Euclidean ball cut by the strip \(|\langle u,z\rangle|\le a\), producing nontrivial exposed faces, so \(p\) is not strictly convex and cannot be linearly isometric to the Euclidean norm.

## originality

PASS

Nakamura and Tanaka prove the global object-level rigidity theorem for isometric entire unit spheres, with no finite-sample substitute in the inspected theorem and surrounding comparison. Cabello Sánchez likewise studies surjective whole-sphere isometries under local-linearity or low-dimensional hypotheses. The audited strip-truncation construction gives the complementary exact boundary: every finite Euclidean sphere sample survives in a non-Euclidean norm arbitrarily close to Euclidean. Current Resultary and web searches did not locate a theorem implying this exact finite-sample renorming.

## value

PASS

The result gives the exact finite-data boundary of a new global sphere-rigidity theorem: no finite metric sample, however rich in pairwise distances, can determine the ambient Euclidean norm, even under arbitrarily small Banach--Mazur perturbation. That is a motivated rigidity counterexample with a reusable explicit construction.
