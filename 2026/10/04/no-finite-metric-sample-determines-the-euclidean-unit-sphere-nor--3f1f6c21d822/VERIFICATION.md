# Verification

The construction is explicit. For a finite nonempty \(F\subset S_{\ell_2^n}\), form \(W=F\cup\{x-y:x,y\in F,\ x\ne y\}\). Choose a Euclidean unit vector \(u\) outside the finite union of lines generated by \(W\), and define \(c=\max_{w\in W}|\langle u,w\rangle|/\|w\|_2\). Then \(c<1\). For any \(\eta>0\), pick \(a\) with \(\max\{c,(1+\eta)^{-1}\}<a<1\) and set \(p(z)=\max\{\|z\|_2,|\langle u,z\rangle|/a\}\).

For every \(w\in W\), the second term is strictly smaller than \(\|w\|_2\), so \(p(w)=\|w\|_2\). This verifies membership of \(F\) in the new unit sphere and every required pairwise distance. Cauchy--Schwarz yields \(\|z\|_2\le p(z)<(1+\eta)\|z\|_2\) for nonzero \(z\). The new unit ball is \(B_2^n\cap\{|\langle u,z\rangle|\le a\}\); because \(n\ge2\) and \(a<1\), each truncating hyperplane exposes a nontrivial face. Hence the new norm is not strictly convex, while the Euclidean norm is strictly convex.

No computational enumeration, asymptotic extrapolation, or external certificate is required. The unproved limits are exactly those stated in the result: no claim is made for infinite samples, dense samples, approximate sphere metrics, or marked extension of a prescribed full-sphere isometry.
