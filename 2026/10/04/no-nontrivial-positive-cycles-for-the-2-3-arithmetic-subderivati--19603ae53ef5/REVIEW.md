# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Writing every orbit term as \(c2^a3^b\) gives an exact transition controlled by \(3a+2b\). Periodicity forces the coprime multiplier introduced at every step to be \(1\), and return of both prime exponents gives the exact product identity \(\prod(3a_j+2b_j)=6^L\). All possible positive values below \(6\) are exhausted: \(2\) and \(3\) terminate, \(4\) immediately creates \(5\), and \(5\) has a forbidden coprime factor. Hence every factor is at least \(6\), so every factor equals \(6\), leaving only the two fixed exponent pairs.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational paper defines arithmetic subderivatives, and Emmons--Xiao explicitly identify finite-set subderivative dynamics as a natural next step. A 2026 dynamics paper already determines fixed points, so the families \(4m\) and \(27m\) are prior-covered and are not claimed as new. Its inspected full text addresses termination and fixed points but not periodic cycles. The inspected 2025 generalization and 2026 eigenpoint paper also do not imply the no-cycle result. Targeted published-finding corpus, full-text, alias, and implication searches found no statement excluding all nontrivial positive cycles for \(S=\{2,3\}\).

## Value
PASS. The theorem closes the periodic-point problem in the smallest genuinely multi-prime subderivative system. It answers a natural dynamical question left beyond the one-prime theory and beyond the recent fixed-point classification, with a short structural mechanism that tracks exactly how new coprime factors obstruct recurrence.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest prior coverage consists of the 2019 definition paper, the 2022 one-prime dynamical study, and the 2026 generalized-subderivative paper on termination and fixed points. The result does not classify cycles for arbitrary finite prime sets, and an equivalent elementary argument could exist in an unindexed source.
