# Review of No real tangent-plane ramification in the five Appendix A.1 double-line spectrahedral witnesses

## Correctness
PASS. The five matrices were transcribed from Appendix A.1 and independently replayed by exact determinant algebra. For each matrix the rank-two double line is defined by the repeated diagonal linear form together with the off-diagonal \(x_{12}\) form. Expanding normal to that line gives a binary quadratic tangent cone. The included verifier recomputes \(B^2-4AC\), matches the five stated factorizations, checks every quadratic factor is positive definite over \(\mathbb R\), and checks the paired factors are coprime. This proves the asserted absence of real tangent-plane coalescence and the existence of four distinct nonreal coalescence points.

## Originality
PASS. The lead paper's Proposition 2.7 and Appendix A.1 classify isolated real-node counts and give the five witness matrices, but do not record their transverse branch divisors. The earlier complex classification of rational quartic symmetroids covers the double-line family and off-line rank-two points, not this five-example real tangent-cone census. Searches by the equivalent terms *pinch point*, *uniplanar point*, *branch point*, *tangent-plane coalescence*, and *tangent-cone discriminant* found no covering statement. The closest private-ledger item studies a different Helsø example with two double lines and therefore does not imply these five factorizations.

## Value
PASS. The source presents these five matrices as the complete existence list for all realized \((a,b)\) pairs in the double-line case. Determining the real branch type along the shared nonisolated singularity adds a natural local-geometric coordinate to that benchmark list: changing the isolated-node counts does not change the fact that the real double line is everywhere split and unramified in all five printed witnesses. The result is exact, finite, and directly reusable when comparing future examples, including any candidate for the missing \((0,0)\) case.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Helsø--Ranestad, arXiv:1810.11235, which supplies the matrices and node-count classification, and Helsø, arXiv:1708.04101, which supplies the broader complex double-line classification. Neither inspected source gives the five discriminant factorizations. The claim does not settle the missing \((0,0)\) case and does not assert universality beyond the five printed matrices. A residual literature risk remains that an obscure source may have computed the same branch divisors under different terminology.
