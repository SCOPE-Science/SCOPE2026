# No torsion in tournament neighborhood homology through seven vertices
## Finding
For every tournament \(T\) with at most seven vertices, the integral homology groups of its out-neighborhood complex \(\overrightarrow{\mathcal N}(T)\) are torsion-free. Consequently, any tournament whose out-neighborhood complex has torsion in integral homology has at least eight vertices.

The statement concerns ordinary integral simplicial homology. A tournament is a finite loopless directed graph in which, for each pair of distinct vertices, exactly one of the two possible directed edges is present. For a vertex \(v\), write \(N^+(v)\) for its out-neighborhood. The out-neighborhood complex \(\overrightarrow{\mathcal N}(T)\) has a simplex \(\sigma\) precisely when \(\sigma\subseteq N^+(v)\) for some vertex \(v\).

## Assumptions and scope
The quantifier is over all tournament isomorphism classes on \(1\) through \(7\) vertices. Empty simplices are treated in the usual way and homology is computed over \(\mathbb Z\). No assertion is made for tournaments on \(8\) or more vertices, and no assertion is made for directed flag complexes, which are a different construction.

The literature makes the cutoff mathematically natural rather than an arbitrary small-order slice. Dochtermann and Singh show that a directed neighborhood complex on at most \(2d+2\) vertices is \(d\)-Leray and construct tournaments on \(2d+3\) vertices whose neighborhood complexes are homotopy equivalent to \(S^d\). Thus order \(7\) is the first order at which their tournament construction can realize \(S^2\), while the same work explicitly records torsion in tournament neighborhood homology as an open phenomenon.

## Proof
A complete isomorphism census is generated recursively. Starting from the unique one-vertex tournament, every representative on \(n\) vertices is extended in all \(2^n\) ways by a new vertex. Each extension is canonically relabeled by minimizing its upper-triangular adjacency word over all vertex permutations that preserve the out-degree partition. This is exhaustive because deleting the new vertex from any \((n+1)\)-vertex tournament gives an \(n\)-vertex tournament, and every tournament isomorphism preserves out-degree. The resulting numbers of isomorphism classes are
\[
1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 12,\ 56,\ 456
\]
for orders \(1\) through \(7\), agreeing with Brendan McKay's independent tournament catalogue.

For every one of the resulting \(532\) isomorphism classes, the verifier constructs every nonempty face of \(\overrightarrow{\mathcal N}(T)\) by downward closure of the out-neighborhoods. It then builds every integral simplicial boundary map \(\partial_k\) with the standard increasing-vertex orientation and checks the chain relation \(\partial_k\partial_{k+1}=0\).

For each \(k\), Smith-normal-form changes of basis for \(\partial_k\) identify an integral basis of \(\ker \partial_k\). The next boundary \(\partial_{k+1}\) is expressed in that kernel basis, and a second Smith normal form computes
\[
H_k\!\left(\overrightarrow{\mathcal N}(T);\mathbb Zight)
=\ker \partial_k/\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}.
\]
Across all \(532\) complexes, every nonzero Smith invariant factor in every homology degree equals \(1\). Hence no homology group contains torsion. The same faces are independently reduced modulo \(2\); whenever the integral calculation reports no torsion, the mod-\(2\) Betti numbers agree with the free integral ranks, providing a separate rank check.

## Verification
Run `python verify_tournament_torsion.py`. The verifier reconstructs the tournament census rather than reading a precomputed list, asserts the class counts above, checks all chain complexes, computes exact Smith normal forms over \(\mathbb Z\), and performs an independent mod-\(2\) rank calculation. A successful replay ends with `VERIFY_OK` and reports an empty torsion-witness list.

The exhaustive range contains \(532\) tournament isomorphism classes in total, including all \(456\) classes on seven vertices. The catalogue count is used only as an external completeness cross-check; the representatives themselves are generated from first principles.

## Relationship to prior work
Dochtermann and Singh introduced the directed neighborhood complex used here and proved its relation to directed Hom complexes. Their paper and its 2022 extended abstract explicitly state that they had not found a tournament whose neighborhood complex has torsion in homology. The same paper gives a five-vertex tournament table and constructs sphere examples, but it does not state the torsion-free cutoff through seven vertices.

A separate computation of directed flag complexes of tournaments by Govc concerns a different simplicial complex: directed flag complexes are generated by transitively ordered subtournaments, whereas \(\overrightarrow{\mathcal N}(T)\) is generated by common out-neighborhoods. Results about Moore spaces or torsion for the directed flag construction therefore do not imply this finding.

McKay's tournament catalogue supplies an independent count of \(456\) non-isomorphic seven-vertex tournaments. It contains combinatorial representatives, not neighborhood-complex homology, so it checks the enumeration size without covering the claimed homology statement.

## Limitations
This is a finite cutoff, not a proof that tournament neighborhood complexes are torsion-free in general. It leaves the first possible torsion order at \(8\) or larger and does not classify the complete homotopy types of all seven-vertex complexes. Exact Smith normal form is decisive for the finite complexes constructed here, but the computation depends on exact integer linear algebra as implemented by the packaged verifier. An obscure or unindexed later computation of the same cutoff under directed-Hom terminology remains a residual literature risk.
