# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The package recursively generates tournament isomorphism representatives and canonicalizes exactly within out-degree blocks; the resulting counts \(1,1,2,4,12,56,456\) match the known independent catalogue. For each of the 532 classes it builds the full downward-closed out-neighborhood complex and exact integer boundary maps, checks the chain identities and computes homology invariant factors via Smith normal form in an integral kernel basis. I independently reran that exact census/SNF core: all 532 classes were processed and the torsion-witness list was empty. The finite quantifier is therefore exhaustive, not sampled.

## originality

PASS

The defining source explicitly says that no tournament whose directed neighborhood complex has homology torsion was known; it gives sphere examples but no finite torsion-free cutoff. Resultary returns the assigned theorem, a six-vertex homotopy census and unrelated torsion computations, none covering all 456 seven-vertex tournaments. The exact enumeration therefore remains new best-of-knowledge evidence on a stated open phenomenon.

## value

PASS

The defining paper explicitly presents tournament-neighborhood torsion as an open phenomenon. Seven vertices is a natural next topological cutoff—large enough for the source's sphere construction to reach \(S^2\)—and the exact negative census raises the first possible torsion order to eight.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
