# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is finite and completely quantified. The verifier generates all tournament isomorphism classes through seven vertices, reproducing the independent class counts \(1,1,2,4,12,56,456\). For each of all \(532\) classes it reconstructs the out-neighborhood complex, checks \(\partial_k\partial_{k+1}=0\), and computes integral homology from exact Smith normal forms. No invariant factor greater than \(1\) occurs. Mod-\(2\) Betti numbers independently agree with the free integral ranks. The proof does not extrapolate beyond the verified order range.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked literature. The primary directed-Hom paper and its 2022 extended abstract explicitly present torsion in tournament neighborhood homology as unresolved and do not state a cutoff through seven vertices. The closest same-object results are a five-vertex table, a Leray bound, and sphere constructions. McKay's catalogue verifies the tournament count but contains no neighborhood-complex homology. Directed flag complexes are a different construction and do not imply the result. Searches covered out-neighborhood/neighborhood-complex, directed-Hom, integral-homology/torsion, seven-vertex/456-tournament, and minimum-order formulations. Residual risk is a later unindexed small-order computation under alternate terminology.

## Value
PASS. The finding closes a natural finite boundary inside an explicit open problem: any torsion example must have at least eight vertices. Seven vertices is structurally distinguished by the published \(2d+3\)-vertex sphere construction, which first permits an \(S^2\) tournament neighborhood complex at \(d=2\). The cutoff is therefore not a routine increment of an arbitrary census; it tests the first order with genuinely two-dimensional neighborhood topology and rules out torsion there exhaustively.
