# Non-power-of-two dicyclic groups are determined by their noncyclic graphs

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\) be an integer that is not a power of \(2\), and let \[\operatorname{Dic}_n=\langle a,x\mid a^{2n}=1,\ x^2=a^n,\ xax^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle.\] If \(H\) is any finite group whose noncyclic graph is isomorphic to that of \(\operatorname{Dic}_n\), then \[H\cong\operatorname{Dic}_n.\] Thus every non-power-of-two dicyclic group is uniquely determined, among finite groups, by its noncyclic graph.

The result extends the known noncyclic-graph uniqueness of generalized quaternion \(2\)-groups to the complementary dicyclic parameters.

## Assumptions and scope

For a finite group \(G\), write
\[
\operatorname{Cyc}(G)=\{g\in G:\langle g,h\rangle\text{ is cyclic for every }h\in G\}.
\]
The noncyclic graph \(\Gamma_G\) has vertex set
\[
G\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}(G),
\]
with distinct vertices adjacent exactly when they generate a noncyclic subgroup.

Fix an integer \(n\ge3\) that is not a power of \(2\), and put
\[
D=\operatorname{Dic}_n
=\langle a,x\mid a^{2n}=1,\ x^2=a^n,\ xax^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle.
\]
The group \(D\) has order \(4n\).

Published descriptions of the cyclic and noncyclic graphs give
\[
\operatorname{Cyc}(D)=\{1,a^n\},
\]
and show that \(\Gamma_D\) is the complete multipartite graph
\[
K_{\,2n-2,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{n\text{ times}}}.
\tag{1}
\]
The large part consists of the noncentral elements of \(\langle a\rangle\); each two-element part consists of the two generators outside \(\langle a\rangle\) of one cyclic subgroup of order \(4\).

## Proof

Let \(H\) be a finite group with
\[
\Gamma_H\cong\Gamma_D.
\]
Put
\[
C=\operatorname{Cyc}(H),\qquad c=|C|.
\]
By (1), the vertex set of \(\Gamma_H\) has one part \(A\) of size \(2n-2\) and \(n\) parts \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\), each of size \(2\).

For a vertex \(y\in P_i\), its only nonneighbors among graph vertices are the two vertices of \(P_i\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cyc}_H(y)=C\cup P_i
\]
and therefore
\[
|\operatorname{Cyc}_H(y)|=c+2.
\]
A standard cyclicizer lemma states that \(C\) divides every finite cyclicizer size, so
\[
c\mid(c+2),
\]
and therefore
\[
c\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first exclude \(c=1\). For any \(u\in A\),
\[
\operatorname{Cyc}_H(u)=C\cup A.
\]
Every two elements of this set generate a cyclic subgroup: this is automatic if one lies in \(C\), and otherwise it follows because vertices in the same multipartite part are nonadjacent. Thus \(C\cup A\) is a finite locally cyclic group, hence a cyclic subgroup, of order
\[
1+(2n-2)=2n-1.
\]
But then Lagrange's theorem would force
\[
2n-1\mid |H|=(4n-2)+1=4n-1.
\]
This is impossible for \(n\ge2\), because
\[
4n-1=2(2n-1)+1.
\]
Therefore \(c=2\).

Now
\[
|H|=(4n-2)+2=4n.
\]
Again, for \(u\in A\), the set
\[
K=C\cup A
\]
is a finite locally cyclic subgroup, so it is cyclic. Its order is
\[
|K|=2+(2n-2)=2n.
\]
Hence \(K\) has index \(2\) in \(H\) and is normal.

For every small part \(P_i\), the set
\[
C\cup P_i
\]
is likewise finite locally cyclic, hence cyclic of order \(4\). Therefore every element of \(H\setminus K\) has order \(4\), and its square is the unique nonidentity element \(z\) of \(C\). If \(a\) is a generator of \(K\), then \(z\) is the unique involution in \(K\), so
\[
z=a^n.
\]

Choose \(x\in H\setminus K\). Since \(K\) is normal of index \(2\), every element \(xk\) with \(k\in K\) also lies outside \(K\), so
\[
(xk)^2=z=x^2.
\]
Let
\[
\alpha(k)=xkx^{-1}.
\]
Because \(K\) is cyclic,
\[
(xk)^2=\alpha(k)x^2k=\alpha(k)zk=z\alpha(k)k.
\]
Comparison with \((xk)^2=z\) gives
\[
\alpha(k)=k^{-1}
\]
for every \(k\in K\). In particular,
\[
xax^{-1}=a^{-1},
\qquad
x^2=a^n,
\qquad
a^{2n}=1.
\]
Thus \(H\) is generated by \(a,x\) and satisfies the dicyclic presentation. Both groups have order \(4n\), so
\[
H\cong\operatorname{Dic}_n.
\]

## Relationship to prior work

Abdollahi and Mohammadi Hassanabadi introduced the noncyclic graph and explicitly studied when it uniquely determines a group. Their Proposition 7.13 proves uniqueness for generalized quaternion \(2\)-groups inside the finite \(p\)-group setting. That result does not cover \(\operatorname{Dic}_n\) when \(n\) is not a power of \(2\).

Ma, Wei, and Zhong later described the cyclic graph of the full family \(Q_{4n}\), including the cyclicizer \(\{1,a^n\}\) and the order-four cyclic subgroups outside \(\langle a\rangle\). This supplies the multipartite structure used here but does not prove recognition from the noncyclic graph.

Ma, Li, and Wang subsequently computed the full automorphism group of the noncyclic graph for \(Q_{4n}\) for arbitrary \(n\). Their result again confirms the same neighborhood classes, but an automorphism-group computation does not imply that no different finite group realizes the same graph.

Targeted searches under the aliases “dicyclic,” “generalized quaternion,” \(Q_{4n}\), and \(\operatorname{Dic}_n\), together with “noncyclic graph,” “isomorphic,” “unique,” and “recognition,” did not locate a theorem covering the non-power-of-two dicyclic case.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns finite comparison groups \(H\).

The novelty claim concerns parameters \(n\) that are not powers of \(2\); the \(2\)-power generalized quaternion case was already established in the foundational paper.

A 2019 paper on algebraic properties of noncyclic graphs was available only through its abstract in the inspected sources. Its abstract states characterizations of certain small graph forms, not the dicyclic recognition theorem, but inaccessible full text remains a residual literature risk.
