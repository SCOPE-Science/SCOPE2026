# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The complete multipartite structure gives exact cyclicizer sets for representatives of the large and small parts. Divisibility of finite cyclicizer sizes forces the global cyclicizer size to be \(1\) or \(2\); Lagrange's theorem excludes \(1\). The large part together with the cyclicizer is then a cyclic index-two subgroup, each small part together with the cyclicizer is cyclic of order \(4\), and squaring every element in the nontrivial coset forces conjugation by inversion. These relations recover the dicyclic presentation.

Risk: the argument assumes the comparison group is finite, as stated.

## Originality

PASS. The foundational uniqueness theorem covers generalized quaternion \(2\)-groups through a finite \(p\)-group proposition, not dicyclic groups with non-power-of-two parameter. Later sources describe the full \(Q_{4n}\) cyclic/noncyclic graph structure and its graph automorphism group but do not state recognition of the underlying finite group from the noncyclic graph. Exact alias and implication searches did not locate a covering theorem.

Risk: a 2019 noncyclic-graph paper was accessible only through its abstract, so an unobserved theorem in its full text remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. This closes the natural complementary branch of a foundational graph-recognition result: the same noncyclic-graph uniqueness phenomenon is shown for dicyclic groups outside the \(2\)-group regime. The proof is structural rather than a parameter computation: it recovers the cyclic index-two subgroup and the inversion action directly from graph nonadjacency classes.

Risk: the result is specific to the dicyclic family and does not classify arbitrary generalized dicyclic groups.
