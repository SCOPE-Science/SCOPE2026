# Review of Nonattainment and parameter rigidity at the sharp pseudo-hyperbolic Bloch constant

## Correctness

PASS. The primary source reduces every normalized ordered point pair to parameters
\[
a\ge1,\qquad x>0
\]
and proves that the quotient is bounded by its explicit continuous function \(P(a,x)\). The source also proves that \(P\) has no critical point for \(a>1,\ x>0\), while at \(a=1\) its derivative in the inward \(a\)-direction is positive. Since its global supremum is \(C_0\), these two facts exclude equality at every finite point of the parameter domain.

For an extremizing sequence, the source's uniform large-\(a\) decay prevents escape in \(a\), and its large-\(x\) estimate prevents escape in \(x\). Pointwise nonattainment then forces \(x\to0\). The source proves compact-uniform convergence \(P(a,x)\to B(a)\) as \(x\downarrow0\), and the one-variable function \(B\) has a unique maximizer \(a_0=(3+\sqrt5)/2\). Hence \(a\to a_0\). The exact source identities
\[
\rho=\frac{x}{x+2},
\qquad
\alpha=ae^{1-a}
\]
give the claimed limits. The argument covers arbitrary extremizing sequences, not only the source's explicit sharpness example.

## Originality

PASS. The full 2026 primary source was inspected through its main theorem, auxiliary-function lemma, global-bound proof, pair reduction, and sharpness construction. It states sharpness of \(C_0\) but does not state nonattainment at distinct pairs or rigidity of arbitrary extremizing sequences.

The directly preceding Huang--Rasila--Zhu theorem has a larger nonsharp harmonic constant and poses the optimal-constant question, so it cannot imply behavior at the newly identified sharp boundary. The Chen--Hamada--Zhu analytic result concerns a different sharp constant and a different class.

Targeted published-finding searches used the source identifier, the exact constant, nonattainment language, extremizing-sequence language, pseudo-hyperbolic aliases, and Bloch near-saturation terminology. No returned result stated the same pair of conclusions.

A residual risk remains that an equivalent observation may occur in classical Bloch extremal literature under different terminology. No such implication was found in the inspected sources.

## Value

PASS. A sharp Lipschitz constant does not determine whether equality is realized or how near-equality can occur. The source itself proves sharpness through a coalescing-point limit, making attainment and concentration the natural next extremal questions. The new result shows that coalescence is not merely a feature of one example: it is forced for every extremizing sequence, and it pins down the unique normalized Bloch level at which concentration occurs.

This distinguishes a genuinely unattained supremum from an ordinary extremal inequality and provides a precise compactness obstruction for any future stability or classification theorem.
