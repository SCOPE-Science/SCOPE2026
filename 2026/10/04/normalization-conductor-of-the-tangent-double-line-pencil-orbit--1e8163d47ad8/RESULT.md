# Normalization conductor of the tangent-double-line pencil orbit closure
## Finding
Let \(U\) be a three-dimensional complex vector space. Hauenstein--Manivel--Szendrői define \(Y_5\subset\operatorname{Gr}(2,S^2U)\) as the orbit closure of pencils generated by a double line and a conic tangent to that line, and construct the incidence model
\[
\nu:\widetilde Y_5\longrightarrow Y_5,
\qquad
(P,\ell)\longmapsto P,
\qquad \ell^2\in P.
\]
Their paper proves that this morphism is birational and becomes a double cover over \(Y_4\), where \(Y_4\simeq\operatorname{Hilb}^2\mathbb P(U)\).

The incidence model is in fact the normalization of \(Y_5\). Moreover, if
\[
D=(\nu^{-1}Y_4)_{\mathrm{red}},
\]
then \(D\) is canonically the universal length-two family over \(\operatorname{Hilb}^2\mathbb P(U)\). Equivalently,
\[
D\simeq \operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(\mathbb P(U)\times\mathbb P(U)).
\]
The induced finite map \(D\to Y_4\) is the standard degree-two ordered-to-unordered cover, branched exactly along \(Y_3\simeq\mathbb P(T_{\mathbb P(U)})\).

There is a second structural consequence. Writing \(E=S^2U/\mathcal O(-2)\), \(Q=U/\mathcal O(-1)\), and \(K=\ker(E\to S^2Q)\), one has
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde Y_5)=\mathbb P(K).
\]
This \(\mathbb P^1\)-bundle over \(\mathbb P(U)\) lies in \(D\), is exactly the ramification locus of \(D\to Y_4\), and maps isomorphically to \(Y_3\).

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\). The notation \(Y_3\subset Y_4\subset Y_5\) and the incidence model \(\widetilde Y_5\) are those of Hauenstein--Manivel--Szendrői. The statement concerns the orbit closure \(Y_5\) and its incidence normalization; it does not alter the defining equations or the punctual-Hilbert-scheme results of the source.

## Proof
The source realizes \(\widetilde Y_5\) as a relative quadric in \(\mathbb P(E)\to\mathbb P(U)\). The natural map \(E\to S^2Q\) is surjective with rank-two kernel \(K\), while the determinant of a binary quadratic form is a nondegenerate quadratic form on the rank-three bundle \(S^2Q\). Hence, after a local trivialization and a linear change of fiber coordinates, \(\widetilde Y_5\) has relative equation
\[
d^2-4ce=0
\]
in a projective \(4\)-space with two additional kernel coordinates \(a,b\). The singular locus in each fiber is exactly \(c=d=e=0\), namely \(\mathbb P(K)\). Since this hypersurface is Cohen--Macaulay and its singular locus has codimension \(2\), Serre's criterion gives normality of \(\widetilde Y_5\).

The morphism \(\nu\) is proper and birational by construction. A pencil contains only finitely many double lines because the Veronese surface of double lines contains no projective line. Thus \(\nu\) is quasi-finite, hence finite. A finite birational morphism from a normal variety is the normalization, proving the first assertion.

For the conductor geometry, first work over the dense reduced-pair locus of \(Y_4\). A point of \(Y_4\) corresponding to the unordered pair \(\{\ell,m\}\) is the pencil \(\langle\ell^2,m^2\rangle\). Its two points in \(D\) are obtained by marking \(\ell\) or \(m\). Therefore \(D\) agrees there with the universal family over \(\operatorname{Hilb}^2\mathbb P(U)\).

The collision is resolved exactly by the blow-up of the diagonal. To see this explicitly, fix \(\ell=x\) and write a second line near it as \(m=x+\beta y+\gamma z\). Modulo \(x^2\),
\[
m^2\equiv 2\beta xy+2\gamma xz+\beta^2y^2+2\beta\gamma yz+\gamma^2z^2.
\]
Thus, in fiber coordinates \([a:b:c:d:e]\), the marked-pair map is
\[
(\beta,\gamma)\longmapsto
[2\beta:2\gamma:\beta^2:2\beta\gamma:\gamma^2].
\]
It is undefined only at the collision \(\beta=\gamma=0\). On the blow-up chart \(\beta=s,\ \gamma=st\), division by the exceptional factor gives the regular extension
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto[2:2t:s:2st:st^2],
\]
and on the chart \(\gamma=s,\ \beta=st\) it gives
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto[2t:2:st^2:2st:s].
\]
These charts agree on their overlap and the exceptional divisor maps to \(c=d=e=0\), with parameter \([a:b]\). Consequently the closure of the marked reduced-pair locus is \(\operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(\mathbb P(U)\times\mathbb P(U))\), which is the universal length-two family. The exceptional divisor is the ramification divisor of the involution exchanging the two ordered points, and its quotient is the nonreduced-subscheme divisor \(Y_3\simeq\mathbb P(T_{\mathbb P(U)})\). The same local coordinates identify this exceptional divisor with \(\mathbb P(K)=\operatorname{Sing}(\widetilde Y_5)\), and the map to \(Y_3\) is an isomorphism.

## Verification
The accompanying verifier checks, over exact integer polynomial arithmetic, the relative quadric equation, the singular-kernel condition, the two blow-up chart formulas, and their overlap relation. It also checks that the exceptional charts land exactly in \(c=d=e=0\). The normalization step uses the geometric facts proved above: normality by Serre's criterion, and finiteness from properness plus quasi-finiteness.

## Relationship to prior work
Hauenstein--Manivel--Szendrői prove that \(Y_5\) parametrizes pencils generated by a double line and a tangent conic, construct \(\widetilde Y_5\) as the corresponding rank-three quadric bundle, show that \(\widetilde Y_5\to Y_5\) is birational and a double cover over \(Y_4\), and identify \(Y_4\) with \(\operatorname{Hilb}^2\mathbb P(U)\) and \(Y_3\) with its nonreduced-subscheme divisor. The source does not state the normalization, universal-family, branch-locus, or singular-locus identifications proved here. The standard fact that the universal family over \(\operatorname{Hilb}^2(S)\) for a smooth surface \(S\) is \(\operatorname{Bl}_\Delta(S\times S)\) is classical and is recalled explicitly in the literature on universal Hilbert families.

## Limitations
The statement is specific to the orbit closure \(Y_5\) of pencils of plane conics and to characteristic zero. It identifies the normalization and conductor geometry but does not compute the conductor ideal inside the homogeneous coordinate ring of \(Y_5\), nor does it give a global presentation of the normalization ring by generators and relations.
