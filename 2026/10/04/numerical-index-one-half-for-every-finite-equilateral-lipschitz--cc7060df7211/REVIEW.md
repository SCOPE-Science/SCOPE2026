# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the equilateral transportation norm exactly, proves the lower bound for an arbitrary norm-one operator through molecular norm attainment and an exhaustive sign-support argument, and proves sharpness with a single explicit three-coordinate cyclic operator. The extension from normed molecules to arbitrary norming pairs is justified by a convex decomposition whose positive-weight molecules must all be normed by the same functional. The two-point boundary is handled separately.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary Lipschitz-free source, arXiv:2304.13183, computes the two-dimensional case and contains the equilateral triangle value \(1/2\), but its statement does not extend to arbitrary finite cardinality. The general polyhedral method in arXiv:1809.04778 treats a different exact three-dimensional family. The extreme-point result DOI:10.4171/RMI/1191 supports the molecular geometry but does not determine the numerical index. Searches across equilateral, complete-graph, Arens--Eells, transportation-cost, root-polytope, and zero-sum \(\ell_1\)-hyperplane terminology found no stronger covering statement. Residual risk remains that an older isometric-hyperplane computation exists under unrelated terminology.

## Value
PASS. Equilateral finite metrics are a canonical homogeneous family, and the result gives a sharp, cardinality-independent exact invariant throughout all finite dimensions above one. The explicit operator also supplies a reusable extremal witness, while the two-point exception shows a genuine threshold rather than a routine restatement.
