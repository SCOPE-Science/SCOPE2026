The claim is proved analytically; no finite numerical experiment is used as evidence.

Checks performed:

1. Constant width gives \(K-K=wB_2\), so every exposed face of \(K\) is a singleton.
2. The support relation gives \(\mu+\tau_\pi\mu=w\,d\theta\), hence a curvature density \(0\le\rho\le w\) and a Lipschitz support-point parametrization.
3. The unoriented affine-diameter family is a Möbius strip whose boundary traces \(\partial K\) once, so its mod-\(2\) degree at every interior point is one.
4. The Lipschitz area formula makes the nondifferentiable and zero-Jacobian images null; at finite fibers outside that set every local mod-\(2\) degree is one.
5. Published planar mean-affine-diameter theory applies because \(K\) and \(-K\) have singleton support faces, giving finite multiplicity almost everywhere.

The proof is almost-everywhere only. Exceptional points may have infinite or otherwise uncontrolled multiplicity.
