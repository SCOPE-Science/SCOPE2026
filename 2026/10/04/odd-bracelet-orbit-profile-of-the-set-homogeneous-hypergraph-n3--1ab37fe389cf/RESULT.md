# Odd-bracelet orbit profile of the set-homogeneous hypergraph \(N_3\)

## Finding

Let \(N_3=(Z,E)\) be the countable set-homogeneous 3-hypergraph constructed by Assari, Hosseinzadeh and Macpherson from the dense local order \(T=(Z,\to)\), and let
\[
H=\operatorname{Aut}(N_3).
\]
Their Proposition 3.3.2 identifies \(H\) with the group generated by the automorphisms and anti-automorphisms of \(T\).

For \(k\ge 1\), let \(f_{N_3}(k)\) be the number of \(H\)-orbits on \(k\)-element subsets of \(Z\). Then \(f_{N_3}(k)\) is exactly the number of binary bracelets of length \(k\) having odd Hamming weight. Equivalently,
\[
f_{N_3}(1)=f_{N_3}(2)=1,
\]
and for \(k\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{
 f_{N_3}(k)=
 \frac{1}{4k}
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\ d\text{ odd}}}
 \varphi(d)2^{k/d}
 +
 \begin{cases}
 2^{(k-3)/2},&k\text{ odd},\\[2mm]
 2^{k/2-2},&k\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}}
\]
Thus
\[
1,1,2,2,4,5,9,12,23,34,63,102,190,325,612,1088,\ldots
\]
is the exact set-orbit profile of \(N_3\), and
\[
\boxed{f_{N_3}(k)=\frac{2^{k-2}}{k}+O(2^{k/2}).}
\]

There is also a finite-tournament corollary. Let \(L(k)\) be the number of local orders on \(k\) vertices up to isomorphism. Babai and Cameron proved
\[
L(k)=\frac{1}{2k}
\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\d\text{ odd}}}
\varphi(d)2^{k/d}.
\]
If \(S(k)\) denotes the number of self-converse local orders on \(k\) vertices, then
\[
\boxed{S(k)=2^{\lfloor (k-1)/2\rfloor}.}
\]
Indeed, \(H\)-orbits are local-order isomorphism classes modulo converse, so \(f_{N_3}(k)=(L(k)+S(k))/2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The primary object is the specific hypergraph \(N_3\) of Assari--Hosseinzadeh--Macpherson. In their circle model, a triple is an \(E\)-edge exactly when its three points lie in an open semicircle. For a finite \(U\subset Z\), they define
\[
a\sim_U b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a=b\ \text{ or every }x\in U\setminus\{a,b\}\text{ makes }\{a,b,x\}\text{ an edge}.
\]
They prove that the \(\sim_U\)-classes are convex in the circular order, that a set with all classes singletons is balanced, and that every finite balanced set has odd size and dihedral automorphism group. They also prove that \(\operatorname{Aut}(N_3)\) is exactly the automorphism/anti-automorphism group of the dense local order.

The bracelet enumeration itself is elementary Burnside counting. The mathematical contribution claimed here is the exact all-arity identification of the finite \(N_3\)-types with odd-weight bracelets, the resulting closed orbit formula and asymptotic, and the self-converse local-order corollary. No claim is made that binary-bracelet enumeration as a stand-alone combinatorial problem is new.

## Proof

Fix a finite \(U\subset Z\). List its \(\sim_U\)-classes in clockwise cyclic order as
\[
U_1,U_2,\ldots,U_m
\]
and put \(t_i=|U_i|\). Choosing one representative from each class gives a balanced set. By Claim 1 in the proof of Proposition 3.3.2, \(m\) is odd and the induced automorphism group on those representatives is the dihedral group \(D_m\).

The cyclic size sequence
\[
(t_1,\ldots,t_m)
\]
therefore determines the isomorphism type of \(U\) only up to cyclic rotation and reversal. Conversely, suppose two finite subsets have the same class-size sequence up to one of those dihedral operations. Choose the corresponding dihedral map between representative sets. Claim 1 makes it an isomorphism of the induced \(R\)-structures on the representatives. Ordering the elements inside each convex class compatibly, or in reverse order when the dihedral map reverses orientation, gives exactly the map used in Claim 2 of the paper. The Claim 2 argument shows that this map is an \(R\)-isomorphism on the whole finite sets, hence an \(E\)-isomorphism. Thus finite induced \(N_3\)-types are precisely dihedral classes of cyclic compositions with an odd number of parts.

Every cyclic composition with an odd number of positive parts is realized. For \(m=2r+1\), begin with the vertices of a regular \(m\)-gon. This set is balanced: for any pair of vertices, choose a third vertex in the longer complementary arc so that the three cyclic gaps are all at most \(r\) polygon steps; then the triple is not contained in an open semicircle. The relevant inequalities are strict, so the same remains true after sufficiently small perturbations. Replace the \(i\)-th polygon vertex by \(t_i\) points in a sufficiently small arc around it. Pairs within one small arc form an edge with every third selected point, while pairs from distinct arcs retain a non-edge witness coming from the balanced skeleton. Since the circle model uses a dense set \(Z\), the required points can be chosen in \(Z\). Hence the \(\sim\)-classes have exactly the prescribed sizes.

Now encode a cyclic composition \((t_1,\ldots,t_m)\) of \(k\) by a binary word on a \(k\)-cycle: place a \(1\) at the first position of each block and \(0\)'s at the remaining positions of the block. The word has Hamming weight \(m\), which is odd. Changing the first block rotates the word, and reversing the cyclic composition reflects it. This gives a bijection between finite \(N_3\)-types of size \(k\) and binary bracelets of length \(k\) with odd weight.

It remains to count those bracelets. Apply Burnside's lemma to the dihedral group of order \(2k\). A rotation whose cycles have length \(d\) fixes an odd-weight binary word only when \(d\) is odd. For such a rotation there are \(k/d\) cycles, and exactly half of the \(2^{k/d}\) assignments to those cycles have odd total weight. There are \(\varphi(d)\) rotations with cycle length \(d\). Hence the total fixed-point contribution from rotations is
\[
\frac12
\sum_{\substack{d\mid k\\d\text{ odd}}}
\varphi(d)2^{k/d}.
\]

If \(k\) is odd, every reflection fixes one position and swaps the other \((k-1)/2\) pairs. Odd weight forces the fixed position to be \(1\), so each of the \(k\) reflections fixes \(2^{(k-1)/2}\) odd-weight words. If \(k\) is even, half the reflections have no fixed positions and therefore fix no odd-weight word; the other \(k/2\) reflections have two fixed positions, which must receive different bits, and they fix \(2^{k/2}\) odd-weight words each. Dividing the total by \(2k\) gives the displayed formula.

The identity rotation contributes \(2^{k-1}/(2k)=2^{k-2}/k\). Every nonidentity odd-cycle rotation contributes \(O(2^{k/3})\), while the reflection term is \(O(2^{k/2})\), proving the asymptotic.

For the final corollary, homogeneity of the dense local order identifies \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\)-orbits on \(k\)-subsets with isomorphism classes of \(k\)-vertex local orders. An anti-automorphism sends an induced local order to its converse, so adjoining anti-automorphisms quotients these classes by the converse involution. Thus
\[
f_{N_3}(k)=\frac{L(k)+S(k)}2.
\]
Substituting the Babai--Cameron formula for \(L(k)\) and the bracelet formula above yields
\[
S(k)=
\begin{cases}
2^{(k-1)/2},&k\text{ odd},\\
2^{k/2-1},&k\text{ even},
\end{cases}
=2^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}.
\]

## Verification

A separate checker performs complementary finite replays. Through length \(14\), it enumerates all odd-weight binary strings and canonically quotients them by every rotation and reflection. Through length \(20\), it evaluates the Burnside formula directly from the divisors of \(k\), computes the Babai--Cameron local-order count, and checks that \(2f_{N_3}(k)-L(k)=2^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\). The brute-force and formula calculations agree on their common range, and the checker ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite replay validates the enumeration once the bracelet classification is granted. The structural classification itself is checked against the finite-class decomposition and the two claims in the proof of Proposition 3.3.2; the realizability argument is supplied explicitly above.

## Relationship to prior work

Assari, Hosseinzadeh and Macpherson introduce \(N_3\), prove \(\operatorname{Aut}(N_3)=H\), define the convex equivalence relation \(\sim_U\), prove that balanced quotients have odd size and dihedral automorphism group, and use the class-size decomposition to prove set-homogeneity. Their paper explicitly places these examples in the study of oligomorphic growth, but does not state an exact formula for the \(k\)-set orbit profile.

Babai and Cameron give the exact number of local orders on \(k\) vertices up to isomorphism. That is the orbit profile of \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\), the index-two subgroup, not the profile after adjoining anti-automorphisms. Their formula supplies an important cross-check: the new profile is exactly the quotient by converse, with the reflection term measuring self-converse classes.

Braunfeld highlights the local order in the sharp \(2^n\)-scale growth threshold and records Simon's conjecture that the two primitive groups at this near-\(2^n\) scale should be \(\operatorname{Aut}(S(2))\) and its automorphism/anti-automorphism extension. The exact formula above therefore resolves the full finite-set orbit profile for the second named group, rather than only its exponential scale.

Targeted searches were run using the group-theoretic, hypergraph, local-order, converse, binary-bracelet and exact-sequence formulations. The closest exact prior enumeration found is the Babai--Cameron local-order formula; the closest public data table lists the same local-order counts, not the quotient-by-converse profile. Searches specifically for self-converse locally transitive/local-order tournaments and for the sequence displayed above did not locate a covering theorem or table.

## Limitations

The formula counts orbits on unordered \(k\)-element subsets, not ordered tuples. It relies on the specific set-homogeneous hypergraph \(N_3\) and its identification with the automorphism/anti-automorphism group of the dense local order.

The originality check cannot exclude unpublished folklore, terminology under which the same quotient-by-converse enumeration is indexed differently, or material in older sources whose full text was not accessible in the sources checked. In particular, publisher metadata for Cameron's 1981 paper on orbits of permutation groups was available, but its full text was not available through the checked access path. The 2000 Babai--Cameron paper and the 2023 Assari--Hosseinzadeh--Macpherson paper were available in full and were inspected directly.
