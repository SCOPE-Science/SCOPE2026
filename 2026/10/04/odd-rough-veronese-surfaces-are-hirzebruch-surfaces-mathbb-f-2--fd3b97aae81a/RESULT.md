# Odd rough Veronese surfaces are Hirzebruch surfaces \(\mathbb F_2\)
## Finding
For every odd integer \(k=2r+1\ge 3\), the rough Veronese surface \(\mathcal R_{2,k,2}\) is smooth and isomorphic to the Hirzebruch surface \(\mathbb F_2\). The rational parametrization from \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\) by all weighted-degree \(k\) monomials has exactly one base point, \([0:0:1]\). Resolving that point torically gives the minimal resolution \(\pi:\mathbb F_2\to\mathbb P(1,1,2)\), and the lifted map is an embedding whose lattice polygon is
\[
P_k=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(k,0),(1,r),(0,r)\}.
\]
Thus the abstract surface is independent of odd \(k\), even though its polarization changes and its degree is
\[
\deg \mathcal R_{2,k,2}=\frac{k^2-1}{2}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(k=2r+1\) with \(r\ge 1\). For \(d=m=2\), the Lyndon variables can be written \(x,y,a\) with weights \(1,1,2\). The cited rough-Veronese description identifies \(\mathcal R_{2,k,2}\), up to a linear change of projective coordinates, with the closure of the image of all monomials
\[
x^p y^q a^j,\qquad p,q,j\ge 0,\qquad p+q+2j=k.
\]
The claim concerns the projective image variety and the resolution of this specific weighted-projective rational map. It does not assert an analogous identification for higher \(d\) or \(m\).

## Proof
Project the exponent vector \((p,q,j)\) to \((q,j)\); then \(p=k-q-2j\) is determined. Hence the exponent set is
\[
A_k=\{(q,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:q+2j\le k\}.
\]
Because \(k=2r+1\), its convex hull is the trapezoid
\[
P_k=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(k,0),(1,r),(0,r)\}.
\]
Indeed every point of \(A_k\) satisfies \(q\ge0\), \(j\ge0\), \(j\le r\), and \(q+2j\le k\), while all four displayed vertices occur in \(A_k\).

The four primitive inward facet normals of \(P_k\), in cyclic order, are
\[
(1,0),\quad (0,1),\quad (-1,-2),\quad (0,-1).
\]
Every adjacent determinant has absolute value \(1\). Equivalently, the primitive edge directions at the four vertices can be taken as
\[
(1,0),(0,1);\quad (-1,0),(-2,1);\quad (2,-1),(-1,0);\quad (1,0),(0,-1),
\]
and each pair is a lattice basis. Therefore the normal fan of \(P_k\) is smooth.

The toric fan of \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\) has rays
\[
(1,0),\quad(0,1),\quad(-1,-2).
\]
Its unique nonsmooth cone is generated by \((-1,-2)\) and \((1,0)\), with determinant \(2\). The additional ray \((0,-1)\) in the fan of \(P_k\) subdivides precisely that cone, and
\[
(-1,-2)+(1,0)=2(0,-1).
\]
Thus the corresponding exceptional divisor has self-intersection \(-2\). This is the minimal toric resolution of the unique quotient singularity of \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\), hence the resulting smooth toric surface is \(\mathbb F_2\).

For odd \(k\), every weighted-degree \(k\) monomial contains \(x\) or \(y\), so all monomials vanish at \([0:0:1]\). Conversely, if \(x\ne0\) then \(x^k\ne0\), and if \(y\ne0\) then \(y^k\ne0\). Hence \([0:0:1]\) is the unique base point.

After the fan subdivision, the complete set \(A_k=P_k\cap\mathbb Z^2\) defines the toric line bundle whose normal fan is exactly the fan of \(\mathbb F_2\). Since this divisor is ample on the smooth complete toric surface, its complete toric linear system is very ample; equivalently, the associated map embeds \(\mathbb F_2\) as the normal toric variety of \(P_k\). Away from the exceptional divisor it agrees with the original weighted monomial map, so its image closure is \(\mathcal R_{2,k,2}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal R_{2,k,2}\cong\mathbb F_2
\]
for every odd \(k\ge3\).

Finally, the normalized area of \(P_k\) is
\[
2\,\operatorname{Area}(P_k)=(k+1)r=\frac{k^2-1}{2},
\]
which recovers the known degree formula and independently checks the polygon normalization.

## Verification
The bundled script `verify_odd_rough_veronese.py` enumerates the weighted-degree monomials for odd \(k\) in a broad finite test range, checks the four extreme exponent vectors, all facet inequalities, the smooth-fan determinants, the unique-base-point combinatorics, the \(-2\) neighbor relation, and the normalized-area formula. It prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The infinite statement does not rest on this finite replay: the proof above derives the exponent set and fan symbolically for arbitrary \(r\ge1\). The replay is a consistency check on the nonstandard combinatorial steps and normalization.

## Relationship to prior work
Galuppi proves that rough Veronese varieties are toric and that, for \(d=m=2\), the map comes from the weights \(1,1,2\). He explicitly exhibits the base point \([0:0:1]\) for \(k=3\) and proves the general base-point criterion. Colmenarejo--Galuppi--Michałek later write down the two-dimensional polytope for \(\mathcal R_{2,k,2}\), including the odd-\(k\) trapezoid and its degree formula.

The inspected sources do not identify the odd-\(k\) image with \(\mathbb F_2\), do not state that every odd-\(k\) image is smooth, and do not identify the weighted-projective base-point resolution with the minimal toric resolution. The present claim is the conjunction of those three geometric consequences of the published monomial and polygon descriptions.

## Limitations
The originality comparison was targeted at the rough-Veronese literature, exact phrase searches for the \(\mathbb F_2\) identification, and semantically close database records. An equivalent observation could still occur in a source using only classical toric-surface or rational-normal-scroll terminology without mentioning rough Veronese varieties. The claim is restricted to \(d=m=2\) and odd \(k\ge3\).
