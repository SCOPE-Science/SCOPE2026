# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The weighted-degree exponent set for \(d=m=2\) is reconstructed directly from the published monomial parametrization. For odd \(k=2r+1\), its convex hull is the stated trapezoid. The primitive normal fan is smooth, and the single added ray subdivides the determinant-two cone of \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\); the neighbor relation gives the exceptional self-intersection \(-2\). The base-point argument is exhaustive. The normalized-area calculation agrees with the published degree formula. The bundled replay checks the nonstandard lattice computations independently of the prose proof.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational paper gives the weighted monomial model, the \(k=3\) base point, and the general base-point criterion. The later paper gives the odd-\(k\) trapezoid and degree. Statement-level comparison found neither the uniform \(\mathbb F_2\) identification nor the smoothness theorem nor the identification of the lifted map with the minimal toric resolution. Exact and alias searches for rough Veronese, Hirzebruch surface, minimal resolution, smooth odd degree, and rational normal scroll produced no covering statement.

## Value
PASS. The result converts an indeterminate weighted-projective parametrization into a concrete smooth surface model for the entire odd family. This gives a useful parity description and a fixed surface on which the changing polarizations can be studied by standard intersection theory; it is more than a numerical recomputation of the published degrees.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are F. Galuppi, *The Rough Veronese variety* (arXiv:1809.02522), and L. Colmenarejo, F. Galuppi, M. Michałek, *Toric geometry of path signature varieties* (arXiv:1903.03779; Adv. Appl. Math. 121 (2020), 102102). The result is limited to \(d=m=2\) and odd \(k\ge3\). A residual literature risk is an equivalent statement phrased only in classical toric-surface or scroll terminology.
