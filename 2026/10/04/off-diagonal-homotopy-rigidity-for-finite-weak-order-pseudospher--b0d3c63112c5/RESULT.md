# Off-diagonal homotopy rigidity for finite weak-order pseudospheres
## Finding
Let \(h\ge 3\). Write
\[
P=A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_h,\qquad Q=B_1\oplus\cdots\oplus B_h,
\]
where every \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) is an antichain, \(p_i=|A_i|\ge2\), \(q_i=|B_i|\ge2\), and the level-size vectors are different. Then every order-preserving map \(f:P\to Q\) is null-homotopic unless
\[
f(A_i)\subseteq B_i\quad\text{and}\quad |f(A_i)|\ge2
\]
for every \(i\). Each map satisfying these conditions is isolated in the pointwise-ordered mapping poset. Therefore
\[
|[P,Q]|=1+\prod_{i=1}^h\bigl(q_i^{p_i}-q_i\bigr).
\]
For an isolated map, the induced homomorphism on top integral homology has rank
\[
\operatorname{rank} H_{h-1}(f)=\prod_{i=1}^h\bigl(|f(A_i)|-1\bigr).
\]
Every map in the remaining homotopy class induces zero in degree \(h-1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The specialization order convention is used, so continuous maps between finite \(T_0\)-spaces are exactly order-preserving maps. The ordinal sum \(A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_h\) has every element of \(A_i\) below every element of \(A_j\) when \(i<j\), and no nontrivial comparabilities within one level. The hypotheses \(h\ge3\), \(p_i,q_i\ge2\), and unequal level-size vectors isolate the off-diagonal arbitrary-height case; the proof itself also works when the size vectors agree.

The order complex of such a weak order is the join of its discrete levels. Hence its top reduced integral homology is naturally
\[
\widetilde H_{h-1}(P;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^h\widetilde H_0(A_i;\mathbb Z),
\]
and analogously for \(Q\).

## Proof
For an order-preserving \(f:P\to Q\), let \(\ell_i\) and \(u_i\) be the least and greatest target-level indices met by \(f(A_i)\). If \(i<h\), order preservation gives \(u_i\le \ell_{i+1}\). If equality holds, say \(u_i=\ell_{i+1}=j\), then every source point of \(A_i\) mapped to \(B_j\) and every source point of \(A_{i+1}\) mapped to \(B_j\) must have the same image: points in one target antichain are comparable only when equal. Thus the whole image in the shared boundary level \(B_j\) is a singleton.

Suppose now that every occupied target level contains at least two image points. Then all inequalities are strict:
\[
u_i<\ell_{i+1}\qquad(1\le i<h).
\]
There are \(h\) nonempty source-level image intervals inside only \(h\) target levels. Strict separation therefore forces
\[
\ell_i=u_i=i
\]
for every \(i\). The assumption on occupied levels then gives \(|f(A_i)|\ge2\). Contrapositively, every map that is not level-preserving and nonconstant on every level has some occupied \(B_j\) whose intersection with \(f(P)\) is a singleton \(\{b\}\).

Such an image is contractible. On the image poset define \(g(z)=b\) for \(z\le b\), and \(g(z)=z\) for \(z>b\). Then the identity is pointwise below \(g\), and the constant map with value \(b\) is also pointwise below \(g\). Pointwise comparable finite-space maps are homotopic, so the image contracts to \(b\). Since \(f\) factors through this image, \(f\) is null-homotopic. All constant maps \(P\to Q\) are homotopic because \(Q\) is connected.

It remains to prove rigidity of a level-preserving map \(f\) with \(|f(A_i)|\ge2\) for every \(i\). If \(f\le g\) pointwise, then on \(A_h\) maximality forces \(g=f\). Induct downward. Assuming equality on \(A_{i+1}\), for \(x\in A_i\) the point \(g(x)\) must lie above \(f(x)\in B_i\) and below every point in the at-least-two-point set \(f(A_{i+1})\subseteq B_{i+1}\). It cannot lie in level \(i+1\) or higher, so it lies in \(B_i\), where comparability with \(f(x)\) forces equality. Thus \(g=f\). The dual upward induction proves that \(g\le f\) also implies \(g=f\). Hence \(f\) is isolated.

There are \(q_i^{p_i}-q_i\) nonconstant functions \(A_i\to B_i\), independently for each level, yielding the stated class count.

For the homology statement, a set map \(\alpha:A_i\to B_i\) induces on reduced degree-zero homology an image of rank \(|\alpha(A_i)|-1\). For a level-preserving map the order-complex map is the join of the level maps, so in top degree the induced homomorphism is the tensor product of these reduced degree-zero maps. Ranks therefore multiply, giving the formula. A null-homotopic map induces zero on positive-degree reduced homology.

## Verification
A standalone exhaustive checker enumerates every set map for four unequal source-target triples of level sizes, retains exactly the order-preserving maps, forms the graph generated by pointwise comparability, and checks both the component classification and the homology-rank formula for every isolated map. The tested cases are \((2,2,2)\to(2,2,3)\), \((2,2,3)\to(2,2,2)\), \((2,3,2)\to(3,2,2)\), and \((2,2,3)\to(2,3,2)\). The respective totals of order-preserving maps are \(755\), \(1146\), \(1647\), and \(1865\), with \(24\), \(24\), \(72\), and \(72\) isolated maps. The checker reports `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite enumerations test boundary behavior but are not used as a proof of the general theorem; the general claim is established by the symbolic argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak and Minian give the finite-space/poset homotopy framework and the correspondence between continuous maps and order-preserving maps. Pseudosphere literature identifies the relevant order complexes as joins of discrete sets and, equivalently, the underlying posets as ordinal sums of trivial posets. Earlier work on \(\operatorname{Hom}(P,Q)\) studies the poset of order-preserving maps through its Möbius function, while more recent work on ordinal sums of antichains studies endomorphisms and polymorphisms rather than this off-diagonal homotopy classification.

The present statement is specifically the unequal-source/unequal-target, equal-height case with at least three levels. It is not the height-two mapping-space classification and not the diagonal self-map classification: both source and target level sizes may vary independently, and the theorem determines the complete homotopy set in that off-diagonal family.

## Limitations
No claim is made for unequal heights, singleton levels, or a description of the full non-isolated mapping-space component beyond its homotopy-class collapse. The exhaustive computation covers only the four displayed small instances. Literature search cannot prove absolute novelty; the residual risk is an equivalent statement under different finite-space, weak-order, or pseudosphere terminology.
