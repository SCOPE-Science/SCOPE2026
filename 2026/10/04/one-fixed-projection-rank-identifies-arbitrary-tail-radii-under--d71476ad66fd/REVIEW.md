# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the needed rank-local implication from the cited source, proves the deterministic-to-Haar transfer, uses the exact Haar beta law to recover \(R_p-\rho_p\xrightarrow{P}0\) without moments, and then invokes the source's one-rank characterization only after its missing radius premise has been established. The assumptions, quantifiers, normalization, and real-vector scope are explicit.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source results divide the needed content: Theorem 2.3 has one fixed rank but assumes \(R_p\Rightarrow\nu\); Theorem 9.3 recovers \(R_p\Rightarrow\nu\) for arbitrary tails under ACI but assumes all proportional ranks; Theorem 2.4 has one-rank radius recovery but a finite-second-moment requirement. The accepted claim fills the logical gap by a Haar projection argument. Yaskov's earlier isotropic characterization does not imply the arbitrary-radial statement from one fixed rank.

## Value
PASS. Reducing an all-ranks spectral hypothesis to one fixed rank while retaining arbitrary tails is a structural simplification for a natural model class and addresses an explicit radius-identification limitation in the motivating paper. The result does not overclaim beyond ACI.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest is Xiaohui Xie, arXiv:2609.08328v1, especially Theorems 2.3, 2.4, and 9.3 and equation (9.8). Pavel Yaskov, arXiv:1511.02711, is the classical isotropic comparison. The theorem still requires ACI and all orientations at the chosen rank, and it gives no quantitative finite-dimensional rate. A concise folklore formulation remains a residual originality risk.
