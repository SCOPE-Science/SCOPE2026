# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The disjoint-support construction gives the exact identity \(\|T_\lambda x\|_p^p=\|\lambda\|_p^p\|x\|_p^p\), so nonzero \(T_\lambda\) is injective with closed range. The block decomposition \(M_i\) supplies infinitely many linearly independent quotient classes. Quotienting an alleged finite-dimensional supercyclic subspace kills every positive iterate, leaving only a finite-dimensional image, which cannot be dense in the infinite-dimensional Hausdorff quotient. The proof remains valid for \(0<p<1\) without using local convexity.

## Originality
PASS. The focal paper's Theorem 4.1 gives the isometric copy with injectivity, non-cyclicity, and non-dense range, while Theorem 5.2 gives a separate spaceable family for each fixed \(N\). The new statement identifies infinite codimension of every nonzero range in the Theorem 4.1 family and consequently obtains one fixed isometric copy avoiding every finite \(N\) simultaneously. The 2004 Bourdon--Feldman--Shapiro adjoint-eigenvalue theorem is a broader obstruction but neither states nor implies the isometric-family construction without the additional block analysis. Targeted semantic-database and literature searches did not locate a covering statement.

## Value
PASS. The focal paper explicitly treats both isometric lineability of non-cyclic operators and fixed-level \(N\)-supercyclicity. Showing that its canonical isometric copy already lies in the intersection of all finite-level complements connects those two themes and strengthens the geometric conclusion from merely non-dense range to closed infinite-codimensional range. This is a structural simultaneous result, not a parameter tweak.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2609.27211v1 itself; the closest general prior implication is Bourdon--Feldman--Shapiro's bound on adjoint eigenvalues for \(N\)-supercyclic operators. Feldman's 2002 paper introduced \(n\)-supercyclicity and a distinct notion of infinite-supercyclicity. The present statement does not address that latter notion or claim a maximal subspace.
