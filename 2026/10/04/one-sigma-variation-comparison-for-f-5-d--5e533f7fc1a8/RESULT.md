# One-sigma variation comparison for \(F(5,d)\)
## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge 5\), let \(X_{5,d}\sim F(5,d)\) and define
\[
p_d=\Pr\!\left\{|X_{5,d}-\mathbb E X_{5,d}|\le \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X_{5,d})}\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
p_{d+2}<p_d\qquad(d\ge5).
\]
Consequently, along either parity of \(d\), the central one-standard-deviation probability decreases to the same chi-square limit, and
\[
\inf_{d\ge5}p_d
=\Pr\!\left\{|\chi^2_5-5|\le\sqrt{10}\right\}
>\Pr\{|Z|\le1\},
\]
where \(Z\) is standard normal. Hence the variation-comparison conjecture holds for numerator degree \(5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The denominator degree \(d\) is an integer with \(d\ge5\), so the variance of \(F(5,d)\) is finite. The result concerns the central probability within one standard deviation of the mean. It does not assert monotonicity when the numerator degree is larger than \(5\).

Following arXiv:2305.13615, put
\[
s_+(d)=\sqrt{\frac{2(d+5)}{5(d-2)}},\qquad
s_-(d)=\sqrt{\frac{2(d+3)}{5(d-4)}},
\]
and
\[
A=\frac{5}{5+d/(1+s_+)},\qquad
B=\frac{5}{5+(d-2)/(1+s_-)}.
\]
For the lower one-standard-deviation endpoint, put \(C=0\) when \(s_+\ge1\), otherwise \(C=5/(5+d/(1-s_+))\); define \(D\) analogously from \(s_-\) and \(d-2\).

## Proof
The beta-integral reduction in arXiv:2305.13615 shows that \(p_{d+2}<p_d\) follows from its inequalities (4.1) and (4.2). In its odd-numerator analysis, inequality (5.2) is a sufficient condition for (4.1). For numerator degree \(5\), write
\[
h=\frac d2,\qquad q=\frac{1-B}{1-A},\qquad v=1-q,\qquad
u=\frac{1-A}{A},\qquad r=q^h,\qquad z=hv.
\]
Because \(\lfloor 5/2-1\rfloor=1\), the finite sum in (5.2) reduces exactly to the positivity of
\[
M_d=2(1-r)+\frac{3\nu}{h+1}\bigl[1-r(1+z)\bigr]-2A.
\]
Exact rational interval arithmetic certifies \(M_d>0\) for every integer \(5\le d\le26\).

For \(d\ge27\), use \((1-v)^h\le e^{-z}\),
\[
1-e^{-z}\ge z-\frac{z^2}{2},
\qquad
1-(1+z)e^{-z}=\int_0^z t e^{-t}\,dt\ge \frac{e^{-z}z^2}{2},
\]
and, once \(0<z<1\), \(e^{-z}\ge1-z\). These give
\[
M_d\ge L_d:=2z-z^2+
\frac{3\nu}{2(h+1)}(1-z)z^2-2A.
\]
The quantities in \(L_d\) lie in the biquadratic extension generated by
\[
a^2=\frac{2(d+5)}{5(d-2)},\qquad b^2=\frac{2(d+3)}{5(d-4)}.
\]
Exact resultant elimination followed by Sturm root counting shows that \(z-1\) has no zero on \([27,\infty)\) and is negative at \(27\), while \(L_d\) has no zero on \([27,\infty)\) and is positive at \(27\). Hence \(0<z<1\) and \(M_d>0\) for all real \(d\ge27\), in particular for every integer \(d\ge27\). Thus (4.1) holds for every integer \(d\ge5\).

It remains to control (4.2). The endpoint-order condition from the same beta reduction becomes, at numerator degree \(5\),
\[
D>C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T(d):=3d^4-37d^3-108d^2+640d-800>0.
\]
Direct evaluation gives \(T(d)<0\) for \(5\le d\le13\), while
\[
T(14+r)=3r^4+131r^3+1866r^2+8788r+712>0\qquad(r\ge0).
\]
Therefore the lower endpoints cross exactly at \(d=14\). When \(d\le13\), the lower-end contribution is absent or has the favorable orientation, so (4.2) is automatic once (4.1) is known.

For \(d\ge14\), set
\[
w=\frac{D-C}{1-C},\qquad u=\frac{1-C}{C}.
\]
The finite sum in (5.3) is the integral of the quadratic Taylor majorant
\[
(1+x)^{3/2}\le1+\frac32x+\frac38x^2\qquad(x\ge0).
\]
Also \((1-s)^{h-1}\le1\) on \(0\le s\le w\). Hence the right side of (5.3) is at most
\[
d\left(w+\frac34u w^2+\frac18u^2w^3\right).
\]
Define
\[
N_d=2C-d\left(w+\frac34u w^2+\frac18u^2w^3\right).
\]
A second exact resultant-and-Sturm certificate shows that \(N_d\) has no zero on \([14,\infty)\) and \(N_{14}>0\). Thus (5.3), hence (4.2), holds for every integer \(d\ge14\). This completes the proof that \(p_{d+2}<p_d\) for all \(d\ge5\).

Finally, for fixed numerator degree \(5\), \(F(5,d)\) converges to \(\chi^2_5/5\) as \(d\to\infty\). The Gamma-distribution one-standard-deviation theorem gives
\[
\Pr\!\left\{|\chi^2_5-5|\le\sqrt{10}\right\}>\Pr\{|Z|\le1\}.
\]
Taking the common parity limit proves the stated infimum and the variation comparison.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs four exact checks. First, it uses rational interval arithmetic with certified square-root enclosures to verify \(M_d>0\) for every integer \(5\le d\le26\). Second, it constructs the exact resultants for \(z-1\) and \(L_d\) and uses Sturm root counting to certify no zero on \([27,\infty)\). Third, it constructs the exact resultant for \(N_d\) and certifies no zero on \([14,\infty)\). Fourth, it checks the exact endpoint-crossing polynomial and its positive expansion about \(d=14\). Running the verifier prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite interval stage is only the bounded prefix of the proof; the infinite tail is supplied by exact algebraic elimination and Sturm theory, not by extrapolation from numerical samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Sun, Hu, and Sun, arXiv:2305.13615, formulate the variation-comparison conjecture for all numerator degrees, prove it for numerator degrees \(1,2,3,4\), and reduce the odd cases \(d_1\ge5\) to the sufficient inequalities (5.2) and (5.3). Their Section 5 explicitly leaves those two-variable inequalities unresolved. The present result closes the first unresolved numerator degree, \(d_1=5\), for every admissible denominator degree.

The Gamma-distribution theorem used for the limiting comparison is Theorem 1.2 of Sun, Hu, and Sun, *Journal of Inequalities and Applications* 2024:5, which proves that every Gamma law has strictly more than Gaussian one-standard-deviation central mass.

## Limitations
No assertion is made for numerator degree \(6\) or larger. The proof is partly computer-assisted, but the certificates use exact rational arithmetic, symbolic resultants, and exact Sturm root counts; floating-point experiments are not used as proof. Originality searches found no statement covering the \(d_1=5\) case, but an unindexed or differently phrased later proof remains a residual literature risk.
