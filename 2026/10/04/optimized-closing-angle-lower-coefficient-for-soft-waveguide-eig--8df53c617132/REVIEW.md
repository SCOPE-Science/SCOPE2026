# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The primary full text was inspected at Proposition 2.1, Theorem 3.1, and equations (3.1)--(3.6). Keeping the exact right-hand side of (3.6) and choosing \(L=x/\tan(\beta/2)\) gives a finite nonzero limiting longitudinal threshold. Counting the Dirichlet interval modes and applying min--max yields the stated liminf. The optimizer is the unique positive root of the displayed derivative polynomial. The delta specialization follows from the standard two-delta jump equation and a direct norm integral.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source is Exner--Spitzkopf itself: Theorem 3.1 states only existence of some \(C_\nu>0\), and its proof deliberately replaces the exact quotient by unspecified \(a_\nu,b_\nu\). Searches for an explicit closing-angle liminf coefficient, optimized bookcover counting constant, the exact optimizer, and the double-delta specialization found no equivalent statement. The earlier bookcover paper gives existence, and the later broad bent-waveguide paper gives qualitative existence rather than a closing-angle counting coefficient.

## Value
PASS. The result turns an unspecified order constant into a directly computable profile-dependent lower coefficient and identifies the best coefficient obtainable from the published trial family. This makes the closing-angle accumulation theorem quantitatively testable and gives a closed formula for the canonical delta-profile channel.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is arXiv:2307.01536v1, Theorem 3.1 and equations (3.1)--(3.6). The result here is one-sided and ansatz-optimal only; it does not claim an exact spectral asymptotic or a globally optimal counting coefficient.
