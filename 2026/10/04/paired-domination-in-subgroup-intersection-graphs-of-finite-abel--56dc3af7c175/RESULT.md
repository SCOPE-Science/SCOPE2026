# Paired domination in subgroup intersection graphs of finite abelian groups

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group having at least one proper nontrivial subgroup, and let \(\Gamma(G)\) be its subgroup intersection graph. A paired dominating set exists exactly when \(G\) is not isomorphic to \(C_{p^2}\), \(C_p^2\), or \(C_{pq}\) for primes \(p\ne q\). Whenever it exists, \[\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))=\begin{cases}4,&G\cong C_2^d\text{ with }d\ge3,\\p+1,&G\cong C_p^d\text{ with }d\ge3\text{ and }p\text{ odd},\\2,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\]

Thus paired domination gives a complete three-way classification: the invariant is undefined because of isolated vertices in three elementary families, it has a parity-forced value \(4\) on elementary abelian \(2\)-groups of rank at least \(3\), and otherwise it is either \(2\) or the odd-prime value \(p+1\).

## Assumptions and scope

For a finite group \(G\), the subgroup intersection graph \(\Gamma(G)\) has the proper nontrivial subgroups of \(G\) as vertices; distinct vertices \(H,K\) are adjacent exactly when
\[
H\cap K\ne 1.
\]
A paired dominating set is a dominating set \(D\) such that the induced graph \(\Gamma(G)[D]\) has a perfect matching. Its minimum size is denoted by
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G)).
\]
If the graph has an isolated vertex, no paired dominating set exists.

The theorem concerns finite abelian groups with at least one proper nontrivial subgroup. The groups \(C_p\), whose intersection graph is empty, are outside this domain.

## Proof

A theorem of Kayacan gives two facts used throughout. First, a set of subgroup-vertices dominates \(\Gamma(G)\) exactly when their union contains every subgroup of prime order. Second, for a finite abelian group with proper nontrivial subgroups, the ordinary domination number is \(1\) when the square-free part of the group order is smaller than the exponent, is \(2\) when the exponent is square-free composite, and is \(p+1\) for an elementary abelian \(p\)-group.

We now impose the perfect-matching condition.

### Nonsquare-free exponent

Suppose the exponent of \(G\) is not square-free. Let \(S\) be the subgroup generated by all minimal subgroups of \(G\). Equivalently, \(S\) is the socle of \(G\), the product of the subgroups of elements killed by the relevant primes. In this case \(S\) is a proper subgroup. Every nontrivial subgroup contains an element of prime order, so every vertex of \(\Gamma(G)\) intersects \(S\) nontrivially. Hence \(S\) is a universal vertex.

If \(G\cong C_{p^2}\), then \(S\cong C_p\) is the only vertex, so no paired dominating set exists. Conversely, a finite abelian group with exactly one proper nontrivial subgroup is \(C_{p^2}\). Therefore, if \(G\not\cong C_{p^2}\), choose any other vertex \(K\). Then
\[
\{S,K\}
\]
is a dominating set, its two vertices are adjacent, and hence it is paired dominating. Thus
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))=2.
\]

### Square-free composite exponent

Now suppose the exponent is square-free and divisible by at least two primes. Write
\[
G=P_1\times\cdots\times P_r,
\]
where each \(P_i\) is a nonzero elementary abelian Sylow subgroup.

If \(r\ge3\), choose distinct indices \(i,j\) and set
\[
H=\prod_{k\ne i}P_k,
\qquad
K=\prod_{k\ne j}P_k.
\]
Every subgroup of prime order lies in \(H\) or \(K\), so \(\{H,K\}\) dominates. Moreover,
\[
H\cap K=\prod_{k\ne i,j}P_k\ne1,
\]
so the pair is adjacent and is paired dominating.

It remains to treat \(r=2\), say
\[
G=P\times Q.
\]
If both Sylow factors have rank one, then \(G\cong C_{pq}\). Its only proper nontrivial subgroups are \(P\) and \(Q\), and they intersect trivially, so no paired dominating set exists.

Otherwise, suppose for example that \(P\) has rank at least \(2\). Choose a nontrivial proper subgroup \(A<P\), and set
\[
H=P\times1,
\qquad
K=A\times Q.
\]
Every subgroup of prime order lies in \(H\) or \(K\), while
\[
H\cap K=A\times1\ne1.
\]
Hence \(\{H,K\}\) is paired dominating and
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))=2.
\]

### Elementary abelian groups

Finally suppose
\[
G\cong C_p^d.
\]
For \(d=2\), every proper nontrivial subgroup is a one-dimensional subspace, and two distinct such subspaces meet only in \(0\). Thus every vertex is isolated and no paired dominating set exists.

Assume \(d\ge3\). Kayacan's domination theorem gives
\[
\gamma(\Gamma(G))=p+1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))\ge p+1.
\]

Choose a codimension-two subspace \(W<G\). The quotient \(G/W\) is two-dimensional over \(\mathbb F_p\), so it has exactly \(p+1\) one-dimensional subspaces. Their inverse images are \(p+1\) distinct hyperplanes
\[
H_1,\ldots,H_{p+1}
\]
containing \(W\). These hyperplanes cover \(G\), so they dominate every subgroup of prime order. Since each pair contains \(W\ne0\), they induce a clique.

If \(p\) is odd, then \(p+1\) is even. The clique on
\[
H_1,\ldots,H_{p+1}
\]
has a perfect matching, so
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))=p+1.
\]

If \(p=2\), then the lower bound is \(3\), but every paired dominating set has even cardinality. Hence
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))\ge4.
\]
There are three hyperplanes \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) through \(W\). Adding the vertex \(W\) gives four vertices inducing a clique, and the three hyperplanes already dominate. Therefore
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(\Gamma(G))=4.
\]

This exhausts the finite abelian groups in the stated domain.

## Verification

The proof is structural and does not depend on finite enumeration. The accompanying `verify.py` independently constructs subgroup lattices of several small finite abelian groups, builds their intersection graphs from the subgroup intersections, and exhaustively searches for minimum paired dominating sets through the relevant sizes.

Its exact output is:

```text
VERIFY_OK

C8: vertices=2 paired_domination=2
C2xC2: vertices=3 paired_domination=none

C3^2: vertices=4 paired_domination=none
C3^3: vertices=26 paired_domination=4
C6: vertices=2 paired_domination=none
C2^2xC3: vertices=8 paired_domination=2
C30: vertices=6 paired_domination=2
```

The tests include every exceptional pattern, both elementary-abelian parity cases, nonsquare-free examples, and square-free mixed-prime examples. The computations corroborate the theorem but are not used to infer the general classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Kayacan's work gives the ordinary domination criterion and classifies finite abelian groups by ordinary domination number. It does not impose total or paired conditions. The paired-domination invariant was introduced earlier by Haynes and Slater for general graphs, with the basic inequality
\[
\gamma(G)\le\gamma_t(G)\le\gamma_{\mathrm{pr}}(G).
\]

The present theorem combines the subgroup-covering structure with the matching requirement and completely classifies paired domination for finite abelian subgroup intersection graphs. The only genuinely larger value than the ordinary lower bound occurs for elementary abelian \(2\)-groups of rank at least \(3\), where parity raises the value from \(3\) to \(4\).

Targeted searches using subgroup-intersection, total-domination, paired-domination, finite-abelian, and elementary-abelian formulations did not locate a prior paired-domination classification for this graph family.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to finite abelian groups. For nonabelian groups the subgroup intersection graph may have substantially more complicated minimal-subgroup incidence, and no analogous paired-domination classification is asserted.

The result concerns the ordinary subgroup intersection graph on all proper nontrivial subgroups. It does not address intersection graphs restricted to cyclic subgroups or other subgroup classes.

The elementary-abelian total-domination values are not new ingredients here; the new claim is the paired-domination existence and exact paired-domination classification.
