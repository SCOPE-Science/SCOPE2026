# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Each local Artinian factor has a nonzero socle element annihilated by its maximal ideal (with a field factor handled by the unit element). Placing one such element in each coordinate gives a clique of size \(k\) that totally dominates the standard zero-divisor graph. The published Artinian domination theorem gives the lower bound \(\gamma_t\ge\gamma=k\) throughout the stated nonreduced nonlocal domain. Paired domination is therefore at least the least even integer at least \(k\); the socle clique attains this for even \(k\), and one explicit two-coordinate vertex supplies the parity correction for odd \(k\).

Risk: the computational replay checks only finite examples; the general result rests on the symbolic Artinian decomposition and socle argument.

## Originality

PASS. The closest full primary papers were inspected at theorem level. The 2011 paper computes ordinary Artinian domination and some total-domination values; the 2014 paper gives general Noetherian total-domination formulas. Neither full text contains “paired,” and exact phrase searches for paired domination of the zero-divisor graph did not locate a covering result. The claim is restricted to the nonreduced Artinian regime, where nilpotent local factors require socle witnesses rather than support-only field arguments.

Risk: the final parity step is short once the canonical socle clique and the published total-domination lower bound are combined, so an unindexed note could contain the same formula.

## Value

PASS. Paired domination is a standard strengthening of domination, and the theorem gives its exact value on the entire nonreduced nonlocal Artinian class. The formula shows that residue-field sizes, nilpotency indices, and internal ideal lattices disappear from the invariant: only the number and parity of local factors remain. This gives a structural boundary between local nilpotent behavior and the rigid product regime.

Risk: the numerical gap above total domination is at most one; the mathematical value is the uniformity over all nilpotent Artinian factors and the explicit matching mechanism.
