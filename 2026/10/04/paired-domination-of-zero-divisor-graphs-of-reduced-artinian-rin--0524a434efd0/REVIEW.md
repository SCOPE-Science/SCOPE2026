# Review

## Correctness

PASS. In a finite product of fields, nonzero zero-divisors are exactly the elements with nonempty proper coordinate support, and two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their supports are disjoint. For each coordinate, a dominating set must hit either the singleton-support fiber or its complementary-support fiber; for at least three factors these requirements are disjoint and give the lower bound. Paired dominating sets have even size. The singleton-support vertices form a dominating clique, and the odd-factor construction adds one two-coordinate vertex with an explicit perfect matching. The two-factor boundary case is handled separately.

Risk: the finite computation is corroborative only; the proof of the arbitrary-factor statement is symbolic.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary zero-divisor-graph source studies ordinary, total, and connected domination and computes ordinary domination for commutative Artinian rings, but it does not impose the perfect-matching condition defining paired domination. The foundational paired-domination paper is general graph theory and does not specialize to zero-divisor graphs. Searches under paired-domination, zero-divisor graph, reduced Artinian ring, product-of-fields, and minimal-prime formulations found no covering statement.

Risk: an unindexed algebraic-graph paper may contain the same short support argument under different terminology.

## Value

PASS. The result is a complete natural classification for a canonical algebraic graph family. It identifies exactly how the matching constraint refines the known domination behavior: the answer depends only on the number of minimal primes and has a sharp parity correction. This gives an exact strengthened invariant rather than a routine recomputation of the ordinary domination number.

Risk: the reduced hypothesis is essential to the support-disjointness proof and leaves the nonreduced Artinian case open.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
