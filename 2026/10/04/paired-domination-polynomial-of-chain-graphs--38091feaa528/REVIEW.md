# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The endpoint requirements \(b_1\ge1\) and \(a_p\ge1\) are forced by domination together with matchability of the endpoint twin classes. The prefix inequalities are necessary for any perfect matching and sufficient by Hall's theorem because nested neighborhoods reduce every Hall test to the largest \(A\)-class index met by the tested set. Equal side sizes then give a perfect matching. Exhaustive replay through order \(10\) matched the theorem on \(348500\) subsets and matched the dynamic-program coefficients on every tested profile.

## Originality
PASS with recorded residual risk. The foundational 1998 source studies the minimum paired-domination parameter. The 2016 paired-domination-polynomial source was inspected in its complete accessible one-page PDF form; it defines the polynomial and mentions unspecified standard families, so it is retained as a bibliographic risk rather than used to assert noncoverage. The closest chain-class optimization papers found concern a minimum solution in strongly orderable graphs or the upper parameter over inclusion-minimal paired-dominating sets. published-finding corpus and web searches under chain graph, Ferrers graph, difference graph, paired-domination polynomial, Hall condition, minimum, and upper variants did not return an equivalent all-cardinality theorem.

## Value
PASS. The claim determines every paired-dominating set on an infinite structured graph class, not merely an optimum. It converts the graph constraint into a ballot-type prefix condition and hence into an exact size-generating recurrence. This supplies both structural understanding and reproducible enumeration, with \(\gamma_{pr}(G)=2\) and the minimum-set count as immediate corollaries.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest same-class result located is Henning–Pradhan on upper paired domination in chain graphs; the accessible abstract has a different target and does not state an all-set enumerator. The 2016 polynomial abstract is the main access risk because its family list is not exposed in the available one-page record. Older Ferrers/difference-graph indexing remains a residual search risk. The finite verifier corroborates but does not prove the infinite theorem.
