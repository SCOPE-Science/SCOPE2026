# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The finite-local hypothesis gives \(Z(R)=M\) and the exact Anderson--Badawi component decomposition. Paired domination is componentwise on a disjoint union. In \(K_s\), precisely the positive even subsets qualify; in \(K_{s,s}\), precisely subsets choosing the same positive number from both parts qualify. Multiplication of the component polynomials yields the stated formula. The least exponent gives the paired-domination number. Reconstruction uses only the lowest exponent, degree, and leading coefficient, with the parity of \(s=|M|\) justified by the finite-local composition series.

Risk: finite computations do not prove the general theorem; they are used only as checks.

## Originality

PASS. The closest ring sources give the total-graph component structure and ordinary/total domination parameters, but not paired domination or a paired-domination polynomial. The general paired-domination-polynomial source concerns graph families and does not state the finite-local-ring reconstruction result. The final theorem adds a ring-specific all-set classification and proves that the polynomial is complete for total-graph isomorphism within the finite local nonfield class.

Risk: formulas for the individual standard components \(K_s\) and \(K_{s,s}\) may already appear in the general paired-domination-polynomial literature; originality is not claimed for those component formulas in isolation.

## Value

PASS. The result upgrades a minimum-parameter statement to an exact enumerator of every paired dominating set and shows that this graph polynomial recovers concrete algebraic data \(q=|R/M|\) and \(s=|M|\). The leading-coefficient dichotomy cleanly detects residue-characteristic parity and makes the polynomial a complete invariant for total graphs in a natural ring class.

Risk: no ring-isomorphism reconstruction is claimed.
