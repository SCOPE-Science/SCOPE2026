# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The period-average identity follows by telescoping the logarithm of the Ricker recurrence, and the predator multiplier follows from the scalar transverse variational equation. Strict AM-GM proves that every nonconstant positive cycle has an invasion threshold strictly above \(1\). The period-two numerical witness is independently reproduced by `verify.py`.

## Originality
PASS. Database and literature searches covered the exact source DOI/title, periodic-orbit/transverse-multiplier aliases, geometric-mean formulations, the 2021 Ricker cooperative-hunting paper, the 2023 open refuge/cooperation predecessor, and the 2025 conference precursor. The closest database item, “Spectral Floquet reduction for synchronous cycles in row-regular Ricker competition,” concerns row-regular Ricker competition, not predator invasion in this model. The 2026 source abstract mentions a predator-extinct 2-cycle but not the arbitrary-period law. The article body was not exposed in the accessible publisher text view, so possible hidden special-case overlap remains a stated residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The theorem identifies a mathematically sharp distinction between the fixed-point reproduction threshold and invasion of oscillatory prey-only attractors. This matters precisely when Ricker prey growth destabilizes the carrying-capacity equilibrium, and the period-two witness shows that the distinction can reverse the local extinction/coexistence expectation for \(R_0>1\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is De Silva–Jang (2026), DOI 10.3934/math.2026868, which publicly states moderate-growth persistence for reproduction number above one and a condition for a predator-extinct 2-cycle. Chou–Chow–Hu–Jang (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.matcom.2021.06.003, treats the Ricker/cooperation setting without the same refuge-period claim; Jang–Yousef (2023), DOI 10.1080/17513758.2023.2242372, uses Beverton-Holt prey growth. The present result is limited to local transverse invasion of positive Ricker boundary cycles and does not prove global persistence or extinction.
