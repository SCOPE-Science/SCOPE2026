# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the exact Gaussian Mercer spectrum and the exact positive finite-rank correction. The trace-norm size of the correction follows from the published trace formula, and the noncommutative telescoping identity gives the required \(O_{d,r}(\beta^{-dr})\) remainder. The \(r=2\) specialization independently matches the published variance asymptotic. The cumulant constants and the Berry--Esseen order follow from standard formulas for independent centered \(\chi_1^2\) summands.

## Originality
PASS. The closest spectral paper proves the \(r=2\) asymptotic and a qualitative Gaussian limit, but it does not state the fixed-order power-sum law for all \(r\). Henze--Wagner supplies the first three cumulants. A contemporaneous later preprint supplies an arbitrary-dimension fourth cumulant and the general cumulant/power-sum identity, but the inspected full text does not state the all-fixed-order vanishing-bandwidth power-sum law or Berry--Esseen rate. The claim does not assert novelty for the known low-order formulas themselves.

## Value
PASS. The theorem quantifies how the full spectrum Gaussianizes in the zero-bandwidth regime. It yields the complete decay hierarchy of standardized cumulants, the simple dimension-independent leading excess-kurtosis coefficient \(12\), and a concrete Kolmogorov convergence-rate order, all of which are useful for assessing moment approximations to the limiting null law.

The main limitations are that \(d\) and \(r\) are fixed, the Berry--Esseen rate is only an upper bound, and no joint finite-sample \(n,\beta\) limit is proved. A general trace-ideal perturbation theorem might package part of the operator estimate abstractly.
