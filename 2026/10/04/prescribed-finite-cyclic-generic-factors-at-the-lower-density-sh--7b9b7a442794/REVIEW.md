# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is proved for every integer \(m\ge2\) and every \(r\ge1\). The quotient is compact metrizable, \(f_m\) is well defined, every point is proximal to the common fixed point, and the averaged sheet measure is invariant with full support. The transitivity equivalence is an exact congruence-plus-mixing argument. For maximality, each sheet return is conjugate to the mixing E-system \((Y,g^m)\); therefore any equicontinuous generic factor is constant on each sheet and factors through the finite cycle. The fixed point excludes an ordinary cyclic factor. No finite computation is used as evidence for an infinite statement.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source is Wu--Wei--Zhang, arXiv:2609.31724v1, Example 4.1, which constructs only the two-sheet endpoint counterexample and does not state an arbitrary finite cyclic family, a maximal-equicontinuous-generic-factor identification, an exact classification of transitive powers, or the generic-versus-ordinary factor separation. Kwietniak--Łącka--Oprocha supply the base subshift; Huang--Ye and Keller supply generic-factor theory. Targeted searches for cyclic endpoint shadowing, prescribed finite generic factors, and power transitivity found no statement implying the theorem. Residual risk remains that the finite-cycle extension may be folklore not captured by indexed sources.

## Value
PASS. The result turns a single sharpness example into a complete family realizing every finite cyclic generic obstruction, identifies the maximal generic equicontinuous structure exactly, and gives the precise arithmetic law for all powers. This directly clarifies why the positive-parameter weak-mixing theorem fails at \(\alpha=0\), while showing that the obstruction is invisible to ordinary topological factor maps.

## Closest literature and limitations
The direct comparison source is arXiv:2609.31724v1. The base construction is supported by DOI 10.1007/s00605-016-1002-1. Generic-factor maximality is compared with Huang--Ye (2012) and DOI 10.3934/dcds.2020132. The theorem is limited to finite cyclic wedges over this proximal mixing base and does not claim a classification of all endpoint systems or all finite equicontinuous generic factors.
