# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

If the largest prime divisor is p_r, no factorial factor can reach the next prime. For r≤5, every composite index in the allowed range is reduced by the six exact identities for 4!,6!,8!,9!,10!,12!, so every such Jordan–Pólya number lies in the prime-factorial monoid. The prime-valuation matrix is triangular with diagonal one, hence unimodular, giving unique integer—and here nonnegative—coordinates. Fixed-dimensional weighted-simplex lattice counting gives the stated asymptotic after shifting the positive top exponent. At support 13, direct valuation inversion gives 14! coordinates (1,-1,-1,1,0,1), so nonnegative prime-factorial generation fails. Independent exact arithmetic rechecked all six identities, determinant one, and the 14! coordinates. The prior literature already knows 14!∉J_wp; correctness does not depend on novelty of that boundary fact.

## originality

PASS

De Koninck–Doyon–Razafindrasoanaivolala–Verreault already introduce the prime-factorial submonoid J_wp, prove uniqueness inside it, give global counting bounds, and explicitly note that 14! lies outside J_wp. Thus the 14! nonmembership itself is prior. What is not stated in their full text is the exact fixed-largest-prime theorem that every Jordan–Pólya number through support 11 lies in J_wp, with unique nonnegative prime-factorial coordinates and the stratum-by-stratum asymptotic. A later published SCOPE support-13 classification explicitly cites the audited fixed-support result as the preceding theorem and extends it with the 14!,15! generators, so it is later corroborating coverage rather than prior domination.
