# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The source propositions legitimately reduce a hypothetical two-prime host to \(N=q^{2a}r^{2b}\) with \(r\ge q+2\). The strict Euler-factor bound forces \(r=q+2\) except for the single extra pair \((q,r)=(3,7)\). In the twin-prime branch, exact prime-support separation of the two divisor sums produces incompatible congruences modulo \(r\). The remaining pair is excluded by \(\operatorname{ord}_7(3)=6\).

## Originality
PASS, with a named access limitation. The 2020 primary source proves only \(\omega(N)\ge2\) and explicitly leaves the prime quasi-friendly-divisor existence problem open. Searches for the exact ratio, the source terminology, and the two-prime-support formulation found no covering result. Ryan's 2002 paper is highly relevant but could not be inspected in full; this is retained as a residual risk rather than treated as novelty evidence.

## Value
PASS. The result removes the complete two-prime-support case of a stated open problem and sharpens the source's structural lower bound from two distinct prime divisors to three. It is a uniform theorem with a concise congruence mechanism, not a routine finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inspected source is C. A. Holdener and J. A. Holdener, “Characterizing Quasi-Friendly Divisors,” *Journal of Integer Sequences* 23 (2020), Article 20.8.4. The main unresolved comparison is R. F. Ryan's 2002 paper on two-prime abundancy ratios, whose full text was not accessible. The theorem does not exclude hosts with three or more distinct prime factors.
