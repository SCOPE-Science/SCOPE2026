# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is reduced to a one-step implication derived directly from the two inequalities of Theorem 3.9. If \(q\mid n\), then the theorem gives \(\varepsilon\le U_k(q,v_q(n))\le U_k(q,1)\); if \(p\nmid n\), it gives \(L_k(p,0)\le\varepsilon\). Thus \(L_k(p,0)>U_k(q,1)\) forces \(p\mid n\). The source supplies the even seed. Exact rational interval arithmetic proves every sign in the order-\(8\) chain through \(89\) and proves the opposite sign at \(97\). The computation is finite evidence only for those explicit inequalities; the divisibility statement for all qualifying contacts follows from the symbolic induction.

## Value
PASS. The source explicitly develops effective divisibility as an arithmetic restriction on higher-order contacts. Reusing a newly forced prime rather than discarding its sharp upper bound changes the quantitative conclusion substantially: at order \(8\), the one-shot criterion directly reaches only through prime \(5\), while the bootstrap reaches all twenty-four primes through \(89\). The exact endpoint is a natural finite cutoff of the recursive method and supplies a reusable forcing lemma for other orders and prime-power refinements.
