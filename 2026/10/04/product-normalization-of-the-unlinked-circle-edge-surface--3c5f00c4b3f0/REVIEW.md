# Review of Product normalization of the unlinked-circle edge surface

## Correctness
PASS. The key identity on the edge curve gives a principal divisor \(2B_\infty-2A_\infty\), hence \(\pi_1^*\mathcal O(2)\simeq\pi_2^*\mathcal O(2)\). The incidence surface is therefore a trivial projective bundle. Its hyperplane self-intersection is eight, matching the published degree of the edge surface and forcing generic degree one. The explicit ruling-intersection witnesses exclude a positive-dimensional fiber, so the proper birational map is finite and is the normalization. The branch-point computation of \(j(E)\) is exact and replayed independently by the bundled checker.

## Originality
PASS. The primary paper gives the edge equation, genus-one ruling curve, degree-eight surface, generic double-curve behavior, and the general branch-point \(j\)-formula. Full-text inspection found no normalization, bundle, product, or exact-value statement for this example. Searches using the exact edge equation, the named example, stationary-bisecant normalization terminology, and the exact \(j\)-value did not locate a source implying the product-normalization theorem. The residual risk is ordinary indexing incompleteness rather than a known competing statement.

## Value
PASS. Determining the normalization of an explicit irrational ruled boundary component is structural: it identifies the ruled surface as the simplest possible elliptic ruled surface, explains the two double conics via the endpoint sections, and makes divisor and cohomological calculations on the normalization immediate. The exact modulus anchors the example in the moduli of elliptic curves. This is substantially more informative than recomputing the source's degree or genus.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Nash–Pir–Sottile–Ying, arXiv:1612.09382, which supplies the exact Example 3.4 equation and the global edge-surface facts used as premises. The claim does not extend the product conclusion to general pairs of conics and does not determine every local analytic singularity of the image.
