# Same-model review
## Correctness
PASS. For a closed-range operator \(K\), the restriction of \(K^*\) to \(R(K)\) is bounded below. Therefore any subfamily that remains a \(K\)-frame projects to an ordinary frame on \(R(K)\), proving \(E_K(\Phi)\le E(P_{R(K)}\Phi)\). The converse deletion implication is proved under the stated support condition, and the near \(K\)-Riesz equality follows because deleting the finite exceptional set leaves a projected Riesz basis. The orthonormal-basis construction has \(E_K=0\) and projected excess \(N-1\) by direct tests valid for every \(N\ge2\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest source is Agheshteh Moghaddam--Arefijamaal, Theorem 5.9, which states the stronger unrestricted equality in equation (5.1), not the one-sided inequality or the arbitrary-gap phenomenon. Exact-title, theorem-number, correction/erratum, projected-excess, near-\(K\)-Riesz, and arbitrary-gap searches found no indexed correction or prior statement of this distinction. Holub's ordinary-frame excess theorem identifies ordinary excess with synthesis-kernel dimension and therefore applies to the projected frame, but it does not compare that number with deletion excess of the original \(K\)-frame.

## Value
PASS. Excess is the paper's principal redundancy invariant for \(K\)-frames. The result identifies the precise direction in which projection is always safe, preserves the important near \(K\)-Riesz case, gives a second natural equality class, and shows by an all-dimensions construction that the unrestricted equality can fail by an arbitrarily large amount rather than by an isolated edge case.

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary source is DOI 10.15672/hujms.1008448, especially Definition 5.1, Proposition 5.2, and Theorem 5.9. The ordinary near-Riesz comparison is J. R. Holub, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 122(3) (1994), DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-1994-1204376-4. A residual literature risk is an unindexed correction or informal note observing the same issue. The theorem here does not characterize all equality cases and does not treat non-closed-range \(K\).
