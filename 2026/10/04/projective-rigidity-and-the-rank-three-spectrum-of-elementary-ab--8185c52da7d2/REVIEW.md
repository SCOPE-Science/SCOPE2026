# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. For proper subspaces \(U,W\), the equivalence
\[
U\le W\iff N(U)\subseteq N(W)
\]
has a direct proof: if containment fails, a hyperplane can be chosen to contain \(W\) but exclude a vector of \(U\), producing a graph neighbor of \(U\) that is not a neighbor of \(W\). Thus every graph automorphism preserves the entire subspace order. The fundamental theorem of projective geometry gives \(\operatorname{PGL}(d,p)\) for \(d\ge3\), because the prime field has no nontrivial field automorphism. In rank three, the exact point-plane block matrix and the identities \(MM^{\mathsf T}=pI+J\) and \(BB^{\mathsf T}=pI+p(p-1)J\) yield the stated two quadratic spectral factors. The packaged checker verifies the structural identities for \(p=2,3,5\).

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary source reconstructs the maximal-intersection lattice from the join graph and studies groups sharing nilpotent join graphs, but does not state the graph automorphism group or the rank-three spectrum. Foundational and later join-graph papers inspect planarity, regularity, domination, independence, distance indices, and adjacency matrices for other families. Targeted searches found no exact \(\operatorname{PGL}(d,p)\) automorphism theorem or the displayed characteristic polynomial. One highly relevant 2019 full text was inaccessible; that is retained as a bibliographic residual risk rather than treated as evidence of noncoverage.

## Value

PASS. The result directly advances the structural reconstruction question motivating the primary source: elementary-abelian join graphs of rank at least three have no accidental automorphisms and determine their prime and rank within the family. Rank three is the first genuinely projective, noncomplete case, and its exact spectrum and energy provide a natural calibrated base case with a sharp rank-two exception.
