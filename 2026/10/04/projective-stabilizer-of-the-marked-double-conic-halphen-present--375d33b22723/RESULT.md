# Projective stabilizer of the marked double-conic Halphen presentation
## Finding
Over \(\mathbb C\), set
\[
Q=x^2+yz,\qquad Q'=x^2+yz+y^2,\qquad C=y^3+z(x^2+yz),
\]
and let the marked sextic divisor be \(B=Q+2Q'\), with defining polynomial \(Q(Q')^2\). The projective transformations preserving \(B\) and \(C\) individually form exactly
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_3}(B,C)=\{1,\iota\},
\qquad \iota[x:y:z]=[-x:y:z].
\]
In particular the marked plane presentation has projective stabilizer isomorphic to \(C_2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The statement concerns projective automorphisms of the displayed plane model that preserve the distinguished sextic divisor \(B\) and the cubic divisor \(C\) individually. It does not claim to determine the full abstract automorphism group of the resolved genus-one surface, nor automorphisms that are allowed to move the distinguished fibers.

The equations are those of the explicit double-conic example in Zanardini, Example 7.46: \(Q:x^2+yz=0\), \(Q':x^2+yz+y^2=0\), and \(C:y^3+z(x^2+yz)=0\), with Halphen pencil generated by \(B=2Q'+Q\) and \(2C\).

## Proof
Because the two irreducible conic components of \(B\) occur with different multiplicities, every projective automorphism preserving \(B\) must preserve \(Q\) and \(Q'\) separately. Their intersection is supported at the single point \(P=[0:0:1]\): indeed, on \(Q\) the equation of \(Q'\) differs by \(y^2\), so \(y=0\) and then \(x=0\). Bézout's theorem gives total intersection multiplicity four, hence \(P\) is the unique common point. Therefore every such automorphism fixes \(P\).

Parametrize \(Q\) by
\[
\nu[s:t]=[st:-s^2:t^2],
\]
so that \(P=\nu[0:1]\). The stabilizer of a smooth conic is \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\), and an automorphism fixing \([0:1]\) has a representative
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\ c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad ad\ne0.
\]
Its induced projective transformation is
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto
[ad\,x-ac\,y:a^2y:2cd\,x-c^2y+d^2z].
\]
For this transformation,
\[
Q\longmapsto a^2d^2Q,
\qquad
Q'\longmapsto a^2\bigl(d^2Q+a^2y^2\bigr).
\]
Thus preservation of \(Q'\) is equivalent to \(d^2=a^2\). After projective rescaling, write \(r=c/a\) and \(\varepsilon=d/a\in\{1,-1\}\). Every projective transformation preserving both conics therefore has the form
\[
A_{r,\varepsilon}[x:y:z]
=[\varepsilon x-r y:y:z+2\varepsilon r x-r^2y].
\]
These transformations preserve \(Q\), and a direct substitution into \(C=y^3+zQ\) gives
\[
C\circ A_{r,\varepsilon}-C
=r(2\varepsilon x-r y)Q.
\]
If \(A_{r,\varepsilon}\) preserves the cubic divisor, then \(C\circ A_{r,\varepsilon}=\lambda C\) for some nonzero scalar \(\lambda\). The coefficient of \(y^3\) is unchanged, so \(\lambda=1\). The displayed difference vanishes identically only for \(r=0\). Hence only \(A_{0,1}=1\) and \(A_{0,-1}=\iota\) remain. Both visibly preserve all three displayed equations, proving the claim.

## Verification
The standalone script `artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It represents multivariate polynomials as exact integer coefficient dictionaries and verifies the identities
\[
Q\circ A_{r,\varepsilon}=Q,
\qquad
C\circ A_{r,\varepsilon}-C=r(2\varepsilon x-r y)Q
\]
subject to \(\varepsilon^2=1\), together with the two surviving substitutions \(r=0\), \(\varepsilon=\pm1\). It prints `VERIFY_OK` on success.

## Relationship to prior work
Zanardini constructs the marked pencil and proves that it is an index-two Halphen pencil whose resolved surface has a fiber of type \(III^*\). The same paper gives the explicit equations used above. Its searchable full text does not state a projective stabilizer calculation for this example. A later paper on GIT stability of plane-sextic pencils studies stability of Halphen pencils but likewise does not state this marked stabilizer.

The new statement is therefore not a rephrasing of the construction: the pair of conics alone has a positive-dimensional stabilizer \(A_{r,\varepsilon}\), while the cubic cuts that stabilizer down exactly to the involution \(x\mapsto -x\).

## Limitations
The result is for the marked plane model and the subgroup preserving the distinguished sextic and cubic individually. It does not classify birational self-maps, automorphisms of the blown-up surface that fail to descend to \(\mathbb P^2\), or projective transformations that might preserve the pencil while permuting special fibers. No claim of independent verification is made.
