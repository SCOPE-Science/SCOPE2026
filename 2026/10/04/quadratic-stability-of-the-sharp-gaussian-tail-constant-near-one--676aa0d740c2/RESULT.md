# Quadratic stability of the sharp Gaussian tail constant near one-sparse uniform sums
## Finding
Let \(U_1,U_2,\ldots\) be independent uniform random variables on \([-1,1]\), let \(G\) be standard Gaussian, and set
\[
Q(t)=\Pr\{|G|>\sqrt3\,t\}.
\]
For a finite unit vector \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), define the best Gaussian tail-comparison ratio
\[
K(a)=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\Pr\{|\sum_{j=1}^n a_jU_j|>t\}}{Q(t)}.
\]
After signs and permutation, suppose \(a_1>0\), and write
\[
r(a)=\sum_{j=2}^n|a_j|,
\qquad
\varepsilon(a)^2=\sum_{j=2}^n a_j^2=1-a_1^2.
\]
There is a dimension-free \(\eta>0\) such that whenever \(r(a)<\eta\), the supremum defining \(K(a)\) has a unique maximizing threshold \(t_a\), this threshold satisfies
\[
t_a<a_1-r(a),
\]
and the entire local optimum depends on the residual weights only through \(a_1\). More precisely, with \(\phi\) and \(\overline\Phi\) denoting the standard-normal density and upper tail,
\[
h(t)=\frac{\overline\Phi(\sqrt3\,t)}{\sqrt3\,\phi(\sqrt3\,t)},
\]
we have
\[
a_1=t_a+h(t_a),
\qquad
K(a)=\frac{a_1-t_a}{a_1Q(t_a)}.
\]
Let \(t_0=0.642908335031999\ldots\) be the unique maximizer from the sharp one-uniform comparison and let
\[
C_*=\frac{1-t_0}{Q(t_0)}=1.345118212049187\ldots.
\]
Along every sequence with \(r(a)\to0\),
\[
t_a=t_0-\frac{\varepsilon(a)^2}{6t_0(1-t_0)}+O(\varepsilon(a)^4)
\]
and
\[
K(a)=C_*-\kappa\,\varepsilon(a)^2+O(\varepsilon(a)^4),
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{t_0}{2Q(t_0)}=1.210876358885690\ldots.
\]
Thus the sharp comparison constant has an isotropic quadratic deficit in every tangent direction away from a one-sparse extremizer; to second order, only the total off-axis squared weight matters.

## Assumptions and scope
The vector \(a\) has finite support and Euclidean norm one. Signs of its coordinates are immaterial because the \(U_j\) are symmetric, and coordinates may be permuted. The local statement uses the residual \(\ell_1\) size \(r(a)\) to ensure that the optimizing threshold lies in the flat-density region generated by the dominant uniform summand. The asymptotic parameter is \(\varepsilon(a)^2=1-a_1^2\); the claim does not assert a global deficit formula for arbitrary weight vectors away from the coordinate axes.

The lead prior theorem of He, Tkocz, and Wyczesany proves the global bound \(K(a)\le C_*\) for every unit vector and identifies \(C_*\) and its one-uniform maximizing threshold \(t_0\). That theorem, including the value \(C_*\), is prior work. The finding here is the local weight-space stability law, the exact local reduction to \(a_1\), and the sharp quadratic coefficient \(\kappa\).

## Proof
Let
\[
R=\sum_{j=2}^n a_jU_j.
\]
Then \(|R|\le r(a)\) almost surely. If \(0<t<a_1-r(a)\), conditioning on \(R\) gives an exact identity. For every realized \(R=s\), the interval
\[
[-t-s,t-s]
\]
is contained in \([-a_1,a_1]\). Since \(a_1U_1\) is uniform on that latter interval,
\[
\Pr\{|a_1U_1+s|\le t\}=\frac{t}{a_1}.
\]
Averaging over \(R\),
\[
\Pr\{|\sum_{j=1}^n a_jU_j|>t\}=1-\frac{t}{a_1}
\tag{1}
\]
throughout this dominant-summand plateau. This already shows why the residual direction disappears locally.

It remains to justify that the global maximizing threshold lies in this plateau when \(r(a)\) is small. Let \(e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\). Under the natural coupling,
\[
\left|\sum_j a_jU_j-U_1\right|
\le |1-a_1|+r(a)=:\delta_a.
\]
Consequently the two-sided tail functions are uniformly within \(\delta_a\) of the tail of \(U_1\). For sufficiently small \(r(a)\), the support of the weighted sum lies in a fixed compact interval, say \([-3/2,3/2]\); on \([0,3/2]\), \(Q(t)\) is bounded away from zero. Hence the ratios defining \(K(a)\) converge uniformly to
\[
K_0(t)=\frac{(1-t)_+}{Q(t)}.
\]
The prior sharp theorem states that \(K_0\) has the unique global maximizer \(t_0\). Uniform convergence and uniqueness therefore force every maximizer for sufficiently small \(r(a)\) into a fixed neighborhood of \(t_0\). Shrinking that neighborhood and then \(\eta\), if necessary, ensures
\[
t<a_1-r(a)
\]
throughout it. Formula (1) then applies at every possible global maximizer.

Inside the plateau define
\[
F_a(t)=\frac{a_1-t}{a_1Q(t)}.
\]
Since
\[
Q'(t)=-2\sqrt3\,\phi(\sqrt3\,t),
\]
the sign of \(F_a'(t)\) is the sign of
\[
a_1-t-h(t).
\]
Writing \(x=\sqrt3\,t\) and using the standard Mills-ratio derivative gives
\[
h'(t)=3t\,h(t)-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\{t+h(t)\}=3t\,h(t)>0
\]
for \(t>0\). Thus there is exactly one critical point near \(t_0\), it is the unique local maximum, and because all global maximizers have already been localized there, it is the unique global maximizer. This proves
\[
a_1=t_a+h(t_a)
\]
and the displayed exact formula for \(K(a)\).

For the expansion, set \(s=\varepsilon(a)^2\). Then
\[
a_1=\sqrt{1-s}=1-\frac{s}{2}+O(s^2).
\]
At \(s=0\), the critical equation is \(1=t_0+h(t_0)\), and
\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\{t+h(t)\}\right|_{t=t_0}=3t_0(1-t_0).
\]
The implicit-function theorem therefore yields
\[
\frac{dt_a}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}=-\frac{1}{6t_0(1-t_0)},
\]
which is the threshold expansion.

Finally, at the maximizing threshold the \(t\)-derivative of \(F_a\) vanishes. Differentiating the optimized value with respect to \(a_1\) therefore gives the envelope derivative
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial a_1}\frac{a_1-t}{a_1Q(t)}\bigg|_{(a_1,t)=(1,t_0)}
=\frac{t_0}{Q(t_0)}.
\]
Since \(da_1/ds=-1/2\) at \(s=0\),
\[
\frac{dK}{ds}\bigg|_{s=0}=-\frac{t_0}{2Q(t_0)}=-\kappa.
\]
Smoothness of the implicit solution gives the stated \(O(s^2)=O(\varepsilon(a)^4)\) remainder.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` solves the defining Mills-ratio equation by bisection and reproduces
\[
t_0=0.6429083350319993,
\quad
C_*=1.3451182120491867,
\quad
\kappa=1.2108763588856895.
\]
It also checks the derivative identity for \(h\), the threshold-shift coefficient, and several three- and four-summand cases using the exact inclusion-exclusion CDF for weighted sums of uniforms. In those stress cases, direct global maximization agrees with the local formula to numerical tolerance. The computation is a consistency check; the proof of localization and the asymptotic expansion is analytic.

## Relationship to prior work
He, Tkocz, and Wyczesany prove the sharp global Gaussian tail comparison for weighted sums of uniforms, with primary MSC 60E15, and identify \(C_*\) and the unique one-uniform threshold \(t_0\). Their inspected full text contains no deficit statement in coefficient space and no expansion of the optimized constant away from a coordinate vector. Their small-threshold proof invokes Barthe--Koldobsky's extremal-slab theorem, which identifies face-parallel extremizers over a range of slab widths but does not state the optimized Gaussian-ratio Hessian above.

The stability literature inspected is related but does not dominate the claim. Chasapis, Nayar, and Tkocz study stability of central hyperplane sections of \(\ell_p\)-balls and negative-moment formulations, not noncentral slab tails optimized against a Gaussian denominator. Eskenazis, Nayar, and Tkocz study distributional stability of Szarek and Ball inequalities; their Ball-type theorem assumes the coefficient cap \(\|a\|_\infty\le1/\sqrt2\), excluding the one-sparse regime \(a_1\to1\) considered here. Glover, Tkocz, and Wyczesany study complex polydisc slicing and its central-section deficit, a different object.

Targeted database and web searches for the numerical coefficient \(1.210876\ldots\), the threshold \(0.642908\ldots\) together with stability terminology, one-sparse Gaussian-tail stability, and equivalent weighted-uniform formulations did not locate this local law. Search failure is not a proof of historical novelty; older cube-slab or weighted-uniform literature could contain an equivalent local calculation under different terminology.

## Limitations
The result is local near one-sparse weight vectors in residual \(\ell_1\) distance. It does not give a global quantitative deficit in terms of \(\|a\|_\infty\), does not locate all secondary stationary configurations of the Gaussian tail ratio, and does not settle the intermediate-dimensional extremizer questions discussed in the lead source. The existence of the neighborhood \(\eta\) is proved qualitatively by uniform localization rather than optimized numerically. The originality assessment remains vulnerable to an older, differently phrased local analysis of cube slabs or weighted-uniform convolutions that was not found in the inspected literature.
