# Review

## Correctness

PASS. In a bounded morphism onto a universal S5 relation, the back condition forces every source equivalence class to map surjectively onto the entire \(m\)-world target, hence every class has at least \(m\) points. In a proper two-agent S5 frame, an \(S_1\)-class and an \(S_2\)-class intersect in at most one point. Therefore one \(S_1\)-class, already of size at least \(m\), meets at least \(m\) distinct \(S_2\)-classes, each of size at least \(m\), forcing at least \(m^2\) states.

The primary source's construction gives exactly \(m^2\) states, is proper, projects by a surjective bounded morphism, and preserves equivalence relations. Thus the lower bound is attained.

## Originality

PASS. The primary paper gives the \(X\times X\) construction but does not discuss its minimum size or prove a lower bound. The later simplicial-belief paper reuses the construction and explicitly displays a three-world model expanded to nine worlds, again without an optimality theorem.

Candidate-specific searches for minimum-size proper covers, p-morphic properization bounds, quadratic lower bounds, and universal-S5 proper covers did not locate an equivalent statement. The closest indexed findings concern unrelated finite modal collapse or finite-template problems.

## Value

PASS. Properization is used as a technical bridge from standard epistemic models to simplicial semantics. The source construction squares finite model size, and the later belief application visibly incurs this proliferation. The theorem shows that this is not merely construction overhead: for the basic two-agent universal S5 family, no proper S5 bounded-morphic cover can do better. This supplies an exact worst-case model-size barrier for a semantic transformation used in completeness arguments.

## Closest literature and limitations

Bjorndahl--Sink (2025), Propositions 2.2 and 2.3 together with their preservation observation, provide the matching \(m^2\)-state construction. Sink--Bjorndahl (2025) shows why properization matters operationally in simplicial belief semantics and gives a concrete three-to-nine-world example.

The theorem is not a general minimum-cover classification. It uses exactly two S5 equivalence relations and a universal target frame.
