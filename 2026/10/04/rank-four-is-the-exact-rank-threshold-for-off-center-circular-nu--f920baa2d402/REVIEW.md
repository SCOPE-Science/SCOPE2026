# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the definitions of partial isometry and numerical range. The rank and dimension formulas were checked separately in the even and odd cases. The only nontrivial imported ingredients are the published rank-four seed, the published rank-at-most-three obstruction, and the classical exact numerical range of a Jordan block. The containment \(D(0,\sqrt{2}/2)\subset D(a,3/4)\) follows from \(a<3/4-\sqrt{2}/2\), and the direct-sum convex-hull identity then gives the claimed disc exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source constructs rank-four counterexamples and propagates them across dimensions by adding zero blocks, which cannot change rank. The low-rank source proves the opposite statement through rank three. Searches using the all-rank, direct-sum, Jordan-block, rank-five/rank-six, and exact focal-source formulations found no statement that every rank at least four occurs. The new claim is therefore not implied by the inspected prior statements.

## Value
PASS. The claim completes the rank axis: ranks at most three are impossible, while every rank at least four is possible. This is a natural structural boundary and shows that the newly discovered rank-four phenomenon is not exceptional. The explicit dimension bound makes the propagation quantitative.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main comparison is O’Loughlin--Virtanen, arXiv:2608.12579v1, whose Corollary 1.3 keeps rank four while enlarging dimension. Popov--Shen--Spitkovsky gives the sharp lower-rank obstruction. The dimension threshold in the present construction is not claimed to be minimal, and there remains a residual possibility that an equivalent direct-sum observation appears in older literature under different terminology.
