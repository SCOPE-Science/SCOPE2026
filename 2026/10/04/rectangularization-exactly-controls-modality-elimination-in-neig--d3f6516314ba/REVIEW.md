# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The published semantics makes the product box true exactly on sets containing a rectangle generated by the two local filters. A direct section calculation makes \(\Box_1\Box_2\) true exactly on sets whose \(\mathcal G\)-large vertical sections form an \(\mathcal F\)-large set. Equality of these two filters is equivalent, in both directions, to the stated rectangularization property.

Every filter on a finite carrier is principal because the intersection of its finitely many members remains in the filter. This makes rectangularization automatic and proves finite modality elimination. The infinite \(\mathsf T\)-example is checked directly: every section contains a cofinite neighborhood, but no single cofinite vertical neighborhood works uniformly across all horizontal points.

## Originality

PASS. The 2026 primary paper proves only the forward interaction for general neighborhood full products and contrasts it with full Kripke products, where the third modality is eliminable. It notes that genuinely non-Kripke behavior requires points with no smallest neighborhood but does not state an exact reverse-interaction criterion, a finite full-product collapse theorem, or the explicit reflexive reverse-mix counterexample.

Kudinov's 2014 neighborhood-product paper studies the bimodal product without the third product modality and similarly uses nonprincipal filters to escape Kripke behavior. Published-finding and literature searches for finite full-product collapse, reverse mix, section uniformization, and Fubini/rectangle filter equality did not locate the same modal theorem.

## Value

PASS. The new full-product semantics introduces a third modality precisely because rectangle neighborhoods are weaker than iterated section behavior in general. The theorem gives the exact local boundary at which that extra modality becomes redundant. It also shows that every finite normal neighborhood full product lies on the Kripke-like side of this boundary, while a concrete reflexive infinite example lies on the genuinely neighborhood side. This identifies a structural compactness issue behind the infinite machinery used in the completeness construction.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Aghamov, Kudinov, Nguyen, and Piribauer (2026), whose definitions and forward interaction are used verbatim as semantic input. Kudinov (2014) is the closest predecessor for products of normal neighborhood frames and explicitly discusses the need for filters without minimal neighborhoods.

The theorem is local and normal-filter specific. Abstract filter theory may contain equivalent terminology for equality of rectangle and Fubini products; no claim of novelty is made for such terminology independently of the modal application.
