# Resource-quantization law for repulsive mean-field contractions
## Finding
Let \(I=[0,1]\). Assume that \(T\in C^1(I,I)\) is strictly increasing and satisfies \(0<\tau\le T'(x)\le L<1\) for every \(x\in I\). Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). Let \(\rho\) be a probability law on \(I\) whose quantile \(R:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) is continuous, strictly increasing and bi-Lipschitz.

For an atomless probability measure \(\mu\) on \(I\), define
\[
g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu}(x)=(1-\varepsilon)x+\varepsilon R(F^-_\mu(x)),
\qquad
\mathcal L_\rho\mu=(T\circ g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu})_*\mu.
\]
Then
\[
Q_{\mathcal L_\rho\mu}(u)=T((1-\varepsilon)Q_\mu(u)+\varepsilon R(u)),
\]
and, for atomless \(\mu,\nu\),
\[
W_1(\mathcal L_\rho\mu,\mathcal L_\rho\nu)\le L(1-\varepsilon)W_1(\mu,\nu).
\]
In fact, before applying \(T\), the resource term cancels exactly in quantile coordinates:
\[
W_1((g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu})_*\mu,(g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\nu})_*\nu)=(1-\varepsilon)W_1(\mu,\nu).
\]
Thus there is a unique attracting nonatomic equilibrium \(\mu_*\), it is absolutely continuous, and its quantile is the unique solution of
\[
Q_*(u)=T((1-\varepsilon)Q_*(u)+\varepsilon R(u)).
\]

For \(N\ge1\), the ordered particle system
\[
x_i^{n+1}=T((1-\varepsilon)x_i^n+\varepsilon R(i/N)),
\qquad 0\le i\le N-1,
\]
has the unique ordered equilibrium \(x_i^*=Q_*(i/N)\). Define
\[
\rho_N=\frac1N\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\delta_{R(i/N)},
\qquad
e_N(\rho)=W_1(\rho_N,\rho).
\]
Then the equilibrium empirical law \(\mu_N^*=N^-1\sum_i\delta_{Q_*(i/N)}\) satisfies the two-sided resource-quantization law
\[
\frac{\tau\varepsilon}{1-\tau(1-\varepsilon)}e_N(\rho)
\le W_1(\mu_N^*,\mu_*)
\le
\frac{L\varepsilon}{1-L(1-\varepsilon)}e_N(\rho).
\]
If \(\eta_{N,n}\) is an ordered particle empirical law and \(\mu_n=\mathcal L_\rho^n\mu_0\), with \(\sigma=L(1-\varepsilon)\), then
\[
W_1(\eta_{N,n},\mu_n)
\le \sigma^nW_1(\eta_{N,0},\mu_0)
+\frac{L\varepsilon(1-\sigma^n)}{1-\sigma}e_N(\rho).
\]
For uniform resources, \(R(u)=u\) and \(e_N(\rho)=1/(2N)\). The resulting lower equilibrium bound is
\[
W_1(\mu_N^*,\mu_*)\ge
\frac{\tau\varepsilon}{2N[1-\tau(1-\varepsilon)]},
\]
which is strictly stronger than the previously stated lower bound \(\tau\varepsilon/(2N)\) whenever \(\tau(1-\varepsilon)>0\).

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns the one-dimensional monotone contracting regime. The resource law is fixed in time and its quantile is assumed continuous, strictly increasing and bi-Lipschitz. Atomless states are used for the exact quantile formula. The particle statement uses the lower-rank convention, so the \(i\)-th ordered particle sees resource quantile \(R(i/N)\).

No uniqueness claim is made among arbitrary atomic invariant measures of the self-consistent operator. The uniqueness and attraction statement is for the nonatomic class generated by the quantile contraction. No assertion is made for atomic resource laws, quantiles with flat portions, nonmonotone microscopic maps, or dimensions above one.

## Proof
Because \(R\circ F^-_\mu\) is nondecreasing, \(g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu}\) is strictly increasing: for \(x<y\), its increment is at least \((1-\varepsilon)(y-x)>0\). If \(\mu\) is atomless, then \(F_\mu(Q_\mu(u))=u\). Monotone pushforward therefore gives
\[
Q_{(g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu})_*\mu}(u)
=(1-\varepsilon)Q_\mu(u)+\varepsilon R(u),
\]
and applying the increasing map \(T\) yields the displayed quantile update.

For two atomless measures, the one-dimensional Wasserstein formula and cancellation of the common resource term give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&W_1((g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\mu})_*\mu,(g^{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\nu})_*\nu)\\
&=\int_0^1 |(1-\varepsilon)(Q_\mu(u)-Q_\nu(u))|\,du\\
&=(1-\varepsilon)W_1(\mu,\nu).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(T\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, applying it gives the contraction factor \(\sigma=L(1-\varepsilon)<1\).

The map on quantiles
\[
\Phi(Q)(u)=T((1-\varepsilon)Q(u)+\varepsilon R(u))
\]
is a contraction in the uniform norm with factor \(\sigma\), and it preserves continuous increasing maps into \(I\). Hence it has a unique fixed point \(Q_*\). For \(u<v\), write \(\Delta Q=Q_*(v)-Q_*(u)\) and \(\Delta R=R(v)-R(u)\). The derivative bounds on \(T\) imply
\[
\tau[(1-\varepsilon)\Delta Q+\varepsilon\Delta R]
\le \Delta Q
\le L[(1-\varepsilon)\Delta Q+\varepsilon\Delta R].
\]
Rearrangement gives
\[
\frac{\tau\varepsilon}{1-\tau(1-\varepsilon)}\Delta R
\le \Delta Q
\le
\frac{L\varepsilon}{1-L(1-\varepsilon)}\Delta R.
\]
Since \(R\) is bi-Lipschitz, \(Q_*\) is bi-Lipschitz. Its pushforward of Lebesgue measure is therefore absolutely continuous, which proves the stated regularity of \(\mu_*\).

For an ordered \(N\)-tuple, the empirical lower distribution function at \(x_i\) equals \(i/N\), so the particle update is exactly the displayed recurrence. Strict order is preserved. The fixed-point equation gives \(x_i^*=Q_*(i/N)\), and coordinatewise contraction gives uniqueness in the ordered sector.

The quantile of \(\mu_N^*\) equals \(Q_*(i/N)\) on the \(i\)-th interval \((i/N,(i+1)/N]\). Hence
\[
W_1(\mu_N^*,\mu_*)
=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{i/N}^{(i+1)/N}
[Q_*(u)-Q_*(i/N)]\,du.
\]
The two-sided increment estimate for \(Q_*\) converts this expression term by term into the corresponding quantile error for \(R\), namely
\[
e_N(\rho)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{i/N}^{(i+1)/N}
[R(u)-R(i/N)]\,du,
\]
which proves the two-sided equilibrium bound.

For the transient estimate, on the \(i\)-th quantile cell the particle quantile after one step is \(T((1-\varepsilon)x_i+\varepsilon R(i/N))\), whereas the continuum quantile is \(T((1-\varepsilon)Q_\mu(u)+\varepsilon R(u))\). The \(L\)-Lipschitz property gives, after integration,
\[
W_1(\eta_{N,n+1},\mu_{n+1})
\le \sigma W_1(\eta_{N,n},\mu_n)+L\varepsilon e_N(\rho).
\]
Iterating this affine recurrence proves the claimed finite-time and uniform-in-time bound.

## Verification
The argument is analytic and uses no finite computation as a substitute for proof. The critical identities were checked directly from the lower-rank particle convention, the one-dimensional quantile representation of \(W_1\), monotonicity of the coupling, and the derivative bounds on \(T\). The fixed-point increment inequality was solved on both sides before integrating, which is essential for the strengthened lower constant.

Boundary cases are explicit. The proof uses \(0<\varepsilon<1\), \(0<\tau\le L<1\), and strict monotonicity of the resource quantile. It does not infer an infinite-dimensional or higher-dimensional statement from finite experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Castorrini, Galatolo and Tanzi introduce the repulsive rank coupling with homogeneous resources, corresponding to \(R(u)=u\). Their quantile update is \(Q\mapsto T((1-\varepsilon)Q+\varepsilon u)\), and they prove exponential contraction, a unique nonatomic invariant measure in the contracting regime, ordered finite-particle convergence, a thermodynamic-limit estimate, and the equilibrium bound
\[
\frac{\tau\varepsilon}{2N}
\le W_1(\mu_N^*,\mu_*)
\le
\frac{L\varepsilon}{2N[1-L(1-\varepsilon)]}.
\]
The present result replaces homogeneous resources by a regular arbitrary resource law and shows that the correct finite-size scale is the resource quantization error \(e_N(\rho)\). It also resolves the self-dependence in the lower fixed-point increment inequality, yielding the stronger factor \([1-\tau(1-\varepsilon)]^-1\) even in the homogeneous case.

A review by Tanzi develops the broader self-consistent-transfer-operator and thermodynamic-limit framework, but the resource-targeted quantile theorem and the two-sided \(e_N(\rho)\) equilibrium law were not found there. Standard one-dimensional optimal-transport facts explain why replacing uniform ranks by a target quantile is natural, but they do not by themselves give the dissipative fixed-point bounds or the finite-particle error law above.

## Limitations
The resource quantile regularity is substantive. If \(R\) has atoms or flat intervals, the exact contraction identity still suggests variants, but the absolute-continuity conclusion and the two-sided comparison through a positive lower quantile slope require separate treatment. The theorem is one-dimensional and monotone; it does not address nonmonotone resource allocation or multidimensional rank couplings.

The literature search did not locate an equivalent published theorem, but an elementary target-quantile extension may be regarded as implicit by specialists. The strongest originality lies in the complete resource-quantization formulation, the exact two-sided finite-size scale, and the sharpened lower constant in the original homogeneous model.
