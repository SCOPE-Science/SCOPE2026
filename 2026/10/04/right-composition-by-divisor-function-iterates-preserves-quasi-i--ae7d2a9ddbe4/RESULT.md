# Right composition by divisor-function iterates preserves quasi-injectivity

## Finding
Call a function \(F:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\) **quasi-injective** if
\[
F(an)=F(bn)\quad\text{for every }n\in\mathbb N
\]
implies \(a=b\).

Let \(\tau(n)\) be the number of positive divisors of \(n\), and write \(\tau^{(m)}\) for the \(m\)-fold functional iterate, with \(\tau^{(0)}\) the identity. If \(F\) is quasi-injective, then
\[
F\circ\tau^{(m)}
\]
is quasi-injective for every integer \(m\ge0\).

Pongsriiam proved that every iterate \(J_s^{(k)}\) of the Jordan totient function is quasi-injective. Therefore, for every \(k,m,s\ge1\),
\[
J_s^{(k)}\circ\tau^{(m)}
\]
is quasi-injective.

This settles one of the six mixed families in Question 18 of Pongsriiam for all parameters, including the explicitly difficult regime \(k,m>2\). It also supplies a closure principle for Question 19: right composition by any iterate of \(\tau\) preserves quasi-injectivity.

## Assumptions and scope
All functions have domain and codomain \(\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}\). Functional iterates are used throughout. The result assumes only that the outer function \(F\) is quasi-injective.

The theorem does not assert that the composition of two arbitrary quasi-injective functions is quasi-injective. It isolates a specific right factor, \(\tau^{(m)}\), whose prime-power amplification identity is strong enough to force the conclusion.

## Proof
We prove the theorem by induction on \(m\).

For \(m=0\), the assertion is exactly the hypothesis that \(F\) is quasi-injective.

Assume \(m\ge1\) and that
\[
G=F\circ\tau^{(m-1)}
\]
is quasi-injective. Suppose that positive integers \(a,b\) satisfy
\[
F\!\left(\tau^{(m)}(an)\right)=F\!\left(\tau^{(m)}(bn)\right)
\]
for every \(n\in\mathbb N\). We must prove \(a=b\).

Fix an arbitrary positive integer \(k\). Choose a prime \(p\) with
\[
p\nmid abk.
\]
For every positive integer \(r\), substitute
\[
n=p^{r-1}k.
\]
Because \(p\) is coprime to both \(ak\) and \(bk\), multiplicativity of \(\tau\) gives
\[
\tau(an)=\tau(ak)\tau(p^{r-1})=r\tau(ak)
\]
and
\[
\tau(bn)=r\tau(bk).
\]
Hence the assumed equality becomes
\[
G\!\left(r\tau(ak)\right)=G\!\left(r\tau(bk)\right)
\]
for every \(r\in\mathbb N\). Since \(G\) is quasi-injective, this forces
\[
\tau(ak)=\tau(bk).
\]
The integer \(k\) was arbitrary, so
\[
\tau(ak)=\tau(bk)\quad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb N.
\]
Pongsriiam's Corollary 6 proves that \(\tau\) itself is quasi-injective; therefore \(a=b\). This completes the induction.

For the stated Jordan-totient consequence, Pongsriiam's Theorem 15 says that \(J_s^{(k)}\) is quasi-injective for every \(k,s\ge1\). Apply the theorem with
\[
F=J_s^{(k)}.
\]

## Verification
The proof has two nontrivial dependencies, both explicitly present in the cited primary source:

1. Pongsriiam's Corollary 6 proves that \(\tau\) is quasi-injective.
2. Pongsriiam's Theorem 15 proves that \(J_s^{(k)}\) is quasi-injective for every \(k,s\ge1\).

The new induction uses only the standard identity
\[
\tau(xp^{r-1})=r\tau(x)
\]
when \(p\nmid x\). For each fixed \(k\), Euclid's theorem supplies a prime \(p\nmid abk\), and then the same prime works simultaneously for every multiplier \(r\ge1\). Thus the definition of quasi-injectivity applies to \(G\) with the two fixed arguments \(\tau(ak)\) and \(\tau(bk)\).

No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof, and no unproved arithmetic conjecture enters the argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Pongsriiam introduced this quasi-injectivity framework for arithmetic functions, proved that \(\tau\) and all iterates \(J_s^{(k)}\) are quasi-injective, and proved only the first two iterate orders for generalized divisor sums. In Question 18, the paper asks whether six mixed compositions are quasi-injective, explicitly including
\[
J_s^{(k)}\circ\tau^{(m)},
\]
and asks in particular what can be said when both iterate counts exceed two. Question 19 separately asks for conditions under which a composition of quasi-injective functions remains quasi-injective; the paper records surjectivity plus complete multiplicativity of the inner function as an obvious sufficient condition.

The theorem here supplies a different closure mechanism. The inner function \(\tau\) is surjective, since \(\tau(p^{r-1})=r\) for every \(r\ge1\), but it is not completely multiplicative. Thus it does not satisfy the source's stated conjunction of surjectivity and complete multiplicativity. The proof instead exploits precisely the freely variable factor \(r=\tau(p^{r-1})\) generated by adjoining a fresh prime power.

Targeted searches for the exact mixed composition, for Question 18, and for a general right-composition closure theorem with \(\tau\) did not locate a published statement covering this result. Searches of the available published-finding corpus corpus likewise returned no implication-equivalent finding.

## Limitations
The result is directional: it proves closure under **right** composition by \(\tau^{(m)}\). It does not show that \(\tau^{(m)}\circ F\) is quasi-injective for an arbitrary quasi-injective \(F\), and it does not settle the other five mixed families in Question 18.

The originality search cannot exclude an equivalent observation in an unindexed source. The argument is elementary once the quasi-injectivity definition is in place, so that residual risk should be kept in view.
