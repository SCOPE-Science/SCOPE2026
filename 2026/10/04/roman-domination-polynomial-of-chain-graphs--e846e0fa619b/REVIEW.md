# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof partitions all Roman dominating functions by which bipartition sides contain label \(2\). In each nontrivial case the relevant extremal class index or pair of indices is unique. Nested neighborhoods then make the availability of label \(0\) a threshold condition, while the definition of the extremal indices makes the availability of label \(2\) the opposite threshold. The factors \(F_n^{z,t}\) enumerate the resulting independent class assignments, and boundary subtraction enforces the required presence of label \(2\). Exhaustive direct verification over all positive canonical profiles of order at most \(9\) agrees coefficient-by-coefficient with the theorem.

Risk: the computation is finite and therefore corroborative only. The arbitrary-order statement rests on the structural proof.

## Originality
PASS, with explicit residual bibliographic risk. The 2021 Roman-domination-polynomial paper was inspected in full and gives the complete-bipartite formula; that special case is excluded from the originality claim. The 2023 Roman Census paper was inspected in full and concerns minimal Roman dominating functions and different graph classes, so it does not imply the all-function weight enumerator here. A 2021 full-text source for the broader class of convex bipartite graphs computes only the minimum Roman domination objective and likewise does not imply the all-weight polynomial. Targeted semantic searches for chain/Ferrers/nested-bipartite aliases and all-weight Roman enumeration found no stronger published-database result. The closest directly relevant chain-graph paper located from 2020 is framed in accessible metadata as a minimum-weight Roman domination algorithm; its full text was not lawfully accessible during this review, so possible incidental stronger coverage remains a recorded risk rather than being asserted absent.

## Value
PASS. The Roman domination polynomial is an established all-weight invariant, while chain graphs form a natural nested-neighborhood graph class with existing minimum-optimization results. The theorem upgrades minimum-value information to an exact distribution of every Roman weight and provides a finite \(O(p^2)\)-term formula directly from canonical twin-class sizes. It also explains structurally why the known complete-bipartite formula is the one-pair boundary case. This is a natural class-level enumerative result, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest exact polynomial coverage is the 2021 complete-bipartite theorem, which is subsumed only as the known \(p=1\) specialization. The closest same-class source is the 2020 minimum Roman domination paper on chain graphs, whose inaccessible full text is the main residual originality risk. The 2023 Roman Census paper treats minimal functions rather than all functions.
