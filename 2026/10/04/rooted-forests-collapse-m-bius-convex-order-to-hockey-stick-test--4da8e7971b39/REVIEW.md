# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof reduces Möbius differentiation on a rooted forest to the unique-parent difference formula. This gives nonnegative nonroot second Möbius derivatives for every Möbius-convex test function, while equal first moment annihilates the only unconstrained root coefficients. The total-variation bound follows because every indicator has second Möbius derivative bounded by \(2\). Direct matrix replay confirms the local formula and the three-point sharpness example.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary source develops the same finite-poset calculus, proves the chain equivalence, constructs examples on rooted trees, and gives a general size-dependent total-variation estimate. Its inspected sections do not state the all-rooted-forest equivalence or the dimension-free sharp constant \(2\). Focused searches for rooted-tree/forest formulations, Möbius convex order, hockey-stick domination, and Zolotarev/TV comparisons found no stronger covering statement.

## Value

PASS. Rooted trees are an explicit motivating class in the source, but branching is left outside its convex-order equivalence. The unique-parent theorem identifies the exact structural reason the chain argument survives branching and simultaneously turns a coarse general metric estimate into a sharp dimension-free one. The sharp three-point example shows the constant is intrinsic rather than a proof artifact.

## Closest literature and limitations

The primary comparison is Jaramillo--Sigarreta, arXiv:2606.12017v1. Boutsikas--Vaggelatou gives a real-line convex-order metric comparison but does not cover finite posets. The argument does not extend automatically past unique-parent posets because merge points can create negative second Möbius derivatives despite increasing first Möbius derivative. A residual risk remains that equivalent rooted-tree language occurs in older incidence-algebra or stochastic-order literature.
