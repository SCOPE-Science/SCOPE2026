# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The minimum and maximum moments are obtained exactly from three elementary events for the symmetric three-point parent. The derivative at the uniform mass vector reduces to a closed positive expression \(D_k\). The two initial cases are exact, and for every \(k\ge4\) the dominant difference \(4(8/3)^k-9\,2^k\) is already greater than \(27\) and then grows by more than a factor of two, proving strict negativity of the correlation derivative for every \(n\ge3\). The verifier checks the finite algebra independently but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating paper explicitly states the probability-vector extremality expectation as unresolved. The closest published database result proves the opposite behavior for exactly two draws on the same three-point lattice; it does not imply any \(n\ge3\) failure. A prior exact three-draw counterexample is a strict special case of the present theorem, while the new all-sample-size derivative formula establishes a structural threshold at sample size two. Targeted searches for the conjecture, the three-point symmetric path, the derivative obstruction, and aliases of minimum--maximum correlation found no covering statement. A 2021 paper on maximal correlation for fixed discrete parents is a residual literature risk but addresses a different optimization variable in the material inspected.

## Value
PASS. The result changes a one-off counterexample into a complete sample-size phase statement on the smallest nontrivial lattice: two draws are the unique uniform-optimal regime, whereas every larger sample size has an explicit local direction that improves on uniformity. This directly sharpens a published conjectural direction and explains why the two-draw theorem does not extend to higher sample sizes.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is the 2022 discrete Terrell paper, which poses the probability-vector direction, the published sharp two-draw three-point result, and the 2021 discrete-order-statistic maximal-correlation paper. The theorem is local along a symmetric mass path and does not solve the global optimization problem for larger samples.
