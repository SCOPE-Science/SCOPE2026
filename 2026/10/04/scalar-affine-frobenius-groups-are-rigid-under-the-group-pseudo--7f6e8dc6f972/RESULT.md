# Scalar affine Frobenius groups are rigid under the group pseudo-algebra

## Finding

Let
\[
q=p^f
\]
be a prime power, let
\[
m>1,\qquad m\mid(q-1),
\]
and let \(H\) be the unique subgroup of order \(m\) in the cyclic group
\[
\mathbf F_q^\times.
\]
Define the scalar affine Frobenius group
\[
G(q,m)=(\mathbf F_q,+)\rtimes H,
\]
where \(H\) acts by multiplication. Put
\[
e=\operatorname{ord}_m(p),
\]
the multiplicative order of \(p\) modulo \(m\).

For an irreducible character \(\chi\) of a finite group, write
\[
\operatorname{cod}(\chi)=\frac{|G:\ker\chi|}{\chi(1)}.
\]
If
\[
m_G(d)=
\left|
\{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G):\operatorname{cod}(\chi)=d\}
\right|,
\]
then the group pseudo-algebra is
\[
C(G)=\{(d,m_G(d)):d\in\operatorname{cod}(G)\}.
\]

For \(G(q,m)\), the complete group pseudo-algebra is
\[
\boxed{
C(G(q,m))
=
\{(d,\varphi(d)):d\mid m\}
\cup
\left\{
\left(p^e,\frac{q-1}{m}\right)
\right\}.
}
\]
The two displayed parts are disjoint because
\[
m\mid(p^e-1),
\]
so
\[
p^e>m.
\]

Hence the pseudo-algebra recovers the defining parameters:
\[
\boxed{
p^e=\max\operatorname{cod}(G(q,m)),
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
m=\max\bigl(\operatorname{cod}(G(q,m))\setminus\{p^e\}\bigr),
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
q=1+m\,m_{G(q,m)}(p^e).
}
\]
Therefore, inside this scalar affine Frobenius family,
\[
\boxed{
C(G(q,m))=C(G(r,n))
\iff
G(q,m)\cong G(r,n).
}
\]

The nonlinear codegree has an additional structural meaning:
\[
\boxed{
p^e
=
\left|\mathbf F_p(H)\right|,
}
\]
where \(\mathbf F_p(H)\) denotes the smallest subfield of \(\mathbf F_q\) containing \(H\). Thus the value of the nonlinear codegree detects the field generated by the complement, while its multiplicity detects how many complement-orbits occur on the nontrivial additive characters.

## Assumptions and scope

The group \(G(q,m)\) is the semidirect product of the additive group of a finite field by a scalar subgroup of its multiplicative group.

The restriction
\[
m>1
\]
excludes the abelian case. Since
\[
m\mid(q-1),
\]
the characteristic prime \(p\) is coprime to \(m\), so
\[
\operatorname{ord}_m(p)
\]
is defined.

The rigidity conclusion is internal to the scalar affine family. It does not claim that an arbitrary finite group having the same pseudo-algebra as \(G(q,m)\) must be isomorphic to \(G(q,m)\).

## Proof

Write
\[
K=(\mathbf F_q,+).
\]
The action of every nonidentity element \(h\in H\) on \(K\) is fixed-point-free away from zero, because
\[
hx=x
\]
with \(x\ne0\) implies
\[
h=1.
\]
Thus \(G(q,m)\) is a Frobenius group with abelian kernel \(K\) and cyclic complement \(H\).

First consider the linear characters. Since \(H\ne1\), choose
\[
h\in H,\qquad h\ne1.
\]
The map
\[
x\longmapsto hx-x=(h-1)x
\]
is a bijection of \(K\), so
\[
[K,H]=K.
\]
Hence
\[
G(q,m)'=K
\]
and
\[
G(q,m)/G(q,m)'\cong H\cong C_m.
\]
The linear irreducible characters are therefore precisely the characters inflated from \(C_m\).

A linear character of exact order \(d\mid m\) has kernel of index \(d\), and hence codegree \(d\). There are exactly
\[
\varphi(d)
\]
characters of exact order \(d\). This gives the part
\[
\{(d,\varphi(d)):d\mid m\}.
\]

Now consider the nonlinear characters. Fix a nontrivial additive character
\[
\theta_a(x)
=
\exp\left(
\frac{2\pi i}{p}
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_q/\mathbf F_p}(ax)
\right),
\qquad
a\in\mathbf F_q^\times.
\]
The complement acts on these characters by
\[
\theta_a^h=\theta_{ah}
\]
up to the harmless choice of left-versus-right action convention. Since \(a\ne0\), this action is semiregular: if
\[
\theta_{ah}=\theta_a,
\]
then the nondegeneracy of the trace pairing gives
\[
ah=a,
\]
hence \(h=1\).

Thus every nontrivial additive-character orbit has size \(m\), and there are exactly
\[
\frac{q-1}{m}
\]
such orbits. Standard Frobenius character theory gives one nonlinear irreducible character
\[
\chi_a=\theta_a^{\,G(q,m)}
\]
from each orbit, and
\[
\chi_a(1)=m.
\]

The kernel of \(\chi_a\) is the normal core of \(\ker\theta_a\). Since the kernel lies in \(K\), it is
\[
\ker\chi_a
=
\bigcap_{h\in H}\ker\theta_{ah}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
x\in\ker\chi_a
\]
exactly when
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_q/\mathbf F_p}(ahx)=0
\]
for every
\[
h\in H.
\]

Let
\[
W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_p}(H)\subseteq\mathbf F_q.
\]
Because multiplication distributes over addition and \(H\) is a multiplicative group, \(W\) is the \(\mathbf F_p\)-subalgebra generated by \(H\), hence a finite field:
\[
W=\mathbf F_p(H).
\]
If \(\zeta\) generates \(H\), then \(\zeta\) has order \(m\). The least positive integer \(e\) such that
\[
m\mid(p^e-1)
\]
is precisely
\[
e=\operatorname{ord}_m(p).
\]
Therefore
\[
W=\mathbf F_{p^e}
\]
and
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}W=e.
\]

Multiplication by \(a\ne0\) preserves dimension, so
\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_p}(aH)=aW
\]
also has dimension \(e\). The trace pairing
\[
(u,x)\longmapsto
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf F_q/\mathbf F_p}(ux)
\]
is nondegenerate. Hence the simultaneous orthogonal complement of \(aH\) has dimension
\[
f-e.
\]
Thus
\[
|\ker\chi_a|=p^{f-e}.
\]

The nonlinear codegree is therefore
\[
\operatorname{cod}(\chi_a)
=
\frac{|G(q,m):\ker\chi_a|}{m}
=
\frac{qm/p^{f-e}}{m}
=
p^e.
\]
All
\[
\frac{q-1}{m}
\]
nonlinear irreducible characters have this same codegree. This proves the formula for \(C(G(q,m))\).

Since
\[
m\mid(p^e-1),
\]
we have
\[
p^e>m.
\]
Every linear codegree divides \(m\), so \(p^e\) is the unique largest codegree. The largest remaining codegree is \(m\). Finally, the multiplicity of \(p^e\) equals
\[
\frac{q-1}{m},
\]
so
\[
q=1+m\,m_{G(q,m)}(p^e).
\]

The pair \((q,m)\) determines \(G(q,m)\) up to isomorphism: finite fields of order \(q\) are isomorphic, and their cyclic multiplicative groups have a unique subgroup of order \(m\). Hence equality of pseudo-algebras implies equality of the parameters and therefore group isomorphism within the family.

## Relationship to prior work

Moretó introduced the group pseudo-algebra
\[
C(G)=\{(d,m_G(d))\}
\]
as the multiset of irreducible-character codegrees with multiplicity and asked which structural information it determines. The paper proves rigidity results when the comparison group is abelian in several \(p\)-group cases, but its full text contains no Frobenius, affine, or semidirect-product treatment.

Lewis and Yan later showed that the group pseudo-algebra does not determine group order in general by constructing \(p\)-groups of different orders with the same pseudo-algebra. Their paper then develops positive rigidity results for several \(p\)-group families. Its inspected full text contains no Frobenius or affine family.

The literature on groups with few codegrees includes a corrected theorem that a Frobenius group with exactly three distinct codegrees has elementary abelian derived subgroup and prime-order complement. That result is about the set of codegree values under a three-codegree hypothesis. The theorem here instead gives every multiplicity and every codegree for scalar affine Frobenius groups with an arbitrary cyclic complement order
\[
m\mid(q-1),
\]
and it uses those multiplicities to reconstruct the group within the family.

Targeted searches under “group pseudo-algebra,” “character codegree multiplicities,” “affine Frobenius group,” “cyclic complement,” and semidirect-product formulations did not locate the displayed formula or the resulting rigidity statement.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns scalar complements
\[
H\le\mathbf F_q^\times.
\]
More general fixed-point-free cyclic actions on elementary abelian groups need not be scalar over the originally chosen field, and their induced-character kernels can involve several irreducible module constituents.

The rigidity statement is relative to this family; it does not exclude a group outside the family from sharing the same pseudo-algebra.

The character-theoretic parametrization of irreducibles in a Frobenius group with abelian kernel is standard. The new claim is the exact nonlinear codegree
\[
p^{\operatorname{ord}_m(p)},
\]
the complete multiplicity formula, and the reconstruction of \((q,m)\) from the pseudo-algebra.

Failed searches do not prove novelty.
