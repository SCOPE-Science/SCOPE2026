# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The scalar complement acts freely on nonzero additive characters, so nonlinear irreducibles are induced from complement orbits and all have degree \(m\). Their kernels are the intersections of the trace hyperplanes indexed by \(aH\). The \(\mathbf F_p\)-span of \(H\) is exactly the smallest subfield \(\mathbf F_{p^e}\) containing the complement, where \(e=\operatorname{ord}_m(p)\). Nondegeneracy of the field trace therefore gives kernel size \(p^{f-e}\) and nonlinear codegree \(p^e\). Linear characters are inflated from \(C_m\), giving codegree \(d\) with multiplicity \(\varphi(d)\). Since \(m\mid p^e-1\), the recovery formulas are unambiguous. The packaged checker independently verifies the finite-field span and trace-kernel calculations.

## Originality

PASS. The primary 2022 paper introduces the group pseudo-algebra but contains no Frobenius, affine, or semidirect-product treatment. The 2024 follow-up gives global counterexamples and positive \(p\)-group results, again without this family. The closest Frobenius codegree theorem assumes exactly three distinct codegrees and proves a structural property of the kernel; it does not supply multiplicities, arbitrary complement order, the value \(p^{\operatorname{ord}_m(p)}\), or parameter recovery. Targeted searches found no equivalent formula. Older Frobenius-character literature under different terminology remains a residual bibliographic risk.

## Value

PASS. Global pseudo-algebra rigidity is false, so an exact positive rigidity theorem for a standard infinite solvable family is mathematically meaningful. The theorem also explains what the nonlinear codegree measures: the minimal subfield containing the complement, with multiplicity recovering the remaining extension size. This is a complete structural formula rather than an isolated computation.
