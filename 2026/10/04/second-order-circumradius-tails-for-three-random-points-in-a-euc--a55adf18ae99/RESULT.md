# Second-order circumradius tails for three random points in a Euclidean ball

## Finding

For each fixed integer \(d\ge 2\), let \(\rho_d\) denote the circumradius of three independent points chosen uniformly with respect to volume in the Euclidean unit ball \(B^d\). Then, as \(R\to\infty\),
\[
\mathbb P(\rho_d>R)=\frac{C_d}{R^{d-1}}\left(1+\frac{a_d}{R^2}+O_d(R^{-4})\right),
\]
with
\[
C_d=\frac{6d}{3d-1}\frac{\kappa_{d-1}^2}{\kappa_d^2}B\!\left(d,\frac12\right)B\!\left(\frac{3d}{2},\frac{d+1}{2}\right)
\]
and
\[
a_d=\frac{3d(d^2-1)(3d-1)}{2(2d+1)(3d+1)(4d+1)}.
\]
Here \(\kappa_j\) is the volume of the \(j\)-dimensional Euclidean unit ball and \(B\) is the beta function.

For \(d=3\), this specializes to
\[
\mathbb P(\rho_3>R)=\frac{3}{55R^2}+\frac{432}{25025R^4}+O(R^{-6}).
\]

There is also a second-order extreme-value consequence. If \(M_k\) is the maximum of \(k\) independent copies of \(\rho_d\) and \(b_k=(C_dk)^{1/(d-1)}\), then for every fixed \(x>0\) and \(d\ge4\),
\[
\mathbb P(M_k\le b_kx)=e^{-x^{-(d-1)}}
\left[
1-a_dC_d^{-2/(d-1)}x^{-(d+1)}k^{-2/(d-1)}
+o\!\left(k^{-2/(d-1)}\right)
\right].
\]
For \(d=3\), where the geometric second-order term and the ordinary binomial-to-exponential correction occur at the same order,
\[
\mathbb P\!\left(M_k\le \sqrt{\frac{3k}{55}}\,x\right)
=e^{-x^{-2}}
\left[
1-\frac{1147}{182}\frac{x^{-4}}{k}+o(k^{-1})
\right].
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The dimension \(d\) is fixed before \(R\to\infty\). The three sample points are independent and volume-uniform in \(B^d\). Their circumradius is almost surely finite because the points are almost surely non-collinear in their affine span. The extreme-value statements concern independent copies of this circumradius, with \(x>0\) fixed as \(k\to\infty\).

The displayed coefficient \(a_2=1/7\) is not asserted as new: it agrees with the explicit planar expansion in the lead source. The contribution is the dimension-uniform formula for the second coefficient, especially \(d\ge3\), together with the resulting first finite-\(k\) correction to the Fréchet limit.

## Proof

Write \(L\in[0,1]\) for half the distance between two independent uniform points of \(B^d\). The lead source proves the exact identity
\[
\mathbb P(\rho_d>R)
=\frac{3}{\kappa_d}\,
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{Vol}_d\!\left(\mathrm{Sg}_R(L)\right),
\qquad R\ge1,
\]
where \(\mathrm{Sg}_R(\ell)\) is the \(R\)-ball-segment generated by two points at half-distance \(\ell\).

A cross-section perpendicular to the segment axis gives, for \(0\le\ell\le1\),
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_d\!\left(\mathrm{Sg}_R(\ell)\right)
=
\kappa_{d-1}
\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}
\left(
\sqrt{R^2-s^2}-\sqrt{R^2-\ell^2}
\right)^{d-1}\,ds.
\]
Use the exact factorization
\[
\sqrt{R^2-s^2}-\sqrt{R^2-\ell^2}
=
\frac{\ell^2-s^2}
{\sqrt{R^2-s^2}+\sqrt{R^2-\ell^2}}.
\]
Because \(0\le |s|\le\ell\le1\), expansion of the denominator is uniform:
\[
\frac{1}
{\sqrt{R^2-s^2}+\sqrt{R^2-\ell^2}}
=
\frac1{2R}
\left[
1+\frac{s^2+\ell^2}{4R^2}+O(R^{-4})
\right].
\]
For fixed \(d\), raising to the \((d-1)\)-st power and integrating therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_d\!\left(\mathrm{Sg}_R(\ell)\right)
=
\frac{\kappa_{d-1}}{(2R)^{d-1}}
\left[
I_0(\ell)+\frac{d-1}{4R^2}I_1(\ell)
+O_d\!\left(R^{-4}\ell^{2d+3}\right)
\right],
\]
where
\[
I_0(\ell)=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}(\ell^2-s^2)^{d-1}\,ds
=B\!\left(d,\frac12\right)\ell^{2d-1}
\]
and
\[
I_1(\ell)=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}
(\ell^2-s^2)^{d-1}(\ell^2+s^2)\,ds.
\]
The beta-integral ratio is
\[
\frac{I_1(\ell)}{I_0(\ell)}
=
\ell^2\left(1+\frac1{2d+1}\right)
=
\frac{2(d+1)}{2d+1}\ell^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_d\!\left(\mathrm{Sg}_R(\ell)\right)
=
\frac{\kappa_{d-1}B(d,\frac12)}{(2R)^{d-1}}
\ell^{2d-1}
\left[
1+\frac{d^2-1}{2(2d+1)}\frac{\ell^2}{R^2}
+O_d(R^{-4}\ell^4)
\right].
\]

The lead source also records the exact half-distance moments
\[
\mathbb E L^q
=
\frac{d\,2^d\kappa_{d-1}}{(d+q)\kappa_d}
B\!\left(\frac{d+q+1}{2},\frac{d+1}{2}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\mathbb E L^{2d+1}}{\mathbb E L^{2d-1}}
=
\frac{3d-1}{3d+1}\,
\frac{3d}{4d+1}.
\]
Taking expectation in the uniform ball-segment expansion gives the asserted relative coefficient
\[
a_d
=
\frac{d^2-1}{2(2d+1)}
\frac{3d-1}{3d+1}
\frac{3d}{4d+1}
=
\frac{3d(d^2-1)(3d-1)}{2(2d+1)(3d+1)(4d+1)}.
\]
Substitution of the moment with exponent \(2d-1\) reproduces exactly the lead source's constant \(C_d\), so the normalization is consistent. At \(d=3\), \(C_3=3/55\) and \(a_3=144/455\), giving \(C_3a_3=432/25025\).

For the extreme-value consequence put \(\alpha=d-1\) and \(b_k=(C_dk)^{1/\alpha}\). For fixed \(x>0\),
\[
\mathbb P(\rho_d>b_kx)
=
\frac{x^{-\alpha}}{k}
\left[
1+a_dC_d^{-2/\alpha}x^{-2}k^{-2/\alpha}
+O\!\left(k^{-4/\alpha}\right)
\right].
\]
Now
\[
\log\mathbb P(M_k\le b_kx)
=
k\log\!\left(1-\mathbb P(\rho_d>b_kx)\right).
\]
If \(d\ge4\), the geometric correction of order \(k^{-2/\alpha}\) dominates the binomial correction of order \(k^{-1}\), which gives the stated expansion. If \(d=3\), both are of order \(k^{-1}\). Using \(C_3=3/55\) and \(a_3=144/455\),
\[
\frac{a_3}{C_3}+\frac12
=
\frac{1147}{182},
\]
which proves the three-dimensional formula.

## Verification

The accompanying `artifacts/verify.py` checks the rational coefficient formula, the specializations \(a_2=1/7\), \(a_3=144/455\), \(C_3a_3=432/25025\), and the three-dimensional extreme-value coefficient \(1147/182\). It also numerically evaluates the exact ball-segment integral for \(d=2,3,4\) and confirms fourth-order residual scaling after subtracting the displayed second-order approximation.

These computations are consistency checks only. The universal statement is proved by the uniform analytic expansion above.

## Relationship to prior work

Bakó-Szabó, Besau and Fodor prove the exact ball-segment identity for the circumradius tail and, for every \(d\ge2\), the leading asymptotic
\[
\mathbb P(\rho_d>R)
=
\frac{C_d}{R^{d-1}}\left(1+O(R^{-2})\right).
\]
Their planar exact formulas yield the refinement with coefficient \(1/7\) when \(d=2\), so that case is used here only to check the general expression. Their general-dimensional statement does not identify the coefficient of \(R^{-(d+1)}\), nor a finite-\(k\) correction to their Fréchet limit.

Le Caër's earlier work studies circumradii of random simplices. For unrestricted circumspheres, the tail section of the available preprint reports Monte Carlo evidence for a power law and fits Fréchet behavior; it does not give the exact second asymptotic coefficient. Affentranger's earlier random-circle result concerns the probability that the entire random circumference is contained in the unit ball, a different event from the large unrestricted circumradius tail.

## Limitations

The asymptotic expansion is for fixed dimension. No claim is made about uniformity as \(d\to\infty\), about more than three defining points, or about non-Euclidean or non-ball sampling domains. The originality search cannot exclude unindexed literature; the most relevant only partially inspected older source is Affentranger's short 1989 article, whose published abstract concerns containment rather than the tail treated here.
