For the Gaussian local alternative, the admissible Fisher symmetry e-factor and its quadratic surrogate both have first-order Pitman efficiency one, but they separate at the next order: the admissible factor gains \(\theta^4/4+O(\theta^6)\) e-power per observation. For a fixed target e-power \(\beta\), this becomes a \(\beta+o(1)\) saving in the continuous sample-size budget as \(\theta\to0\).
