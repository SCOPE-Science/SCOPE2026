# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In a connected complete multipartite graph, total domination is equivalent to meeting at least two parts. Therefore support at least three is automatically secure under any legal one-vertex exchange. With support exactly \(\{i,j\}\), an omitted vertex in part \(i\) can only be defended from \(j\); after the exchange two parts remain represented exactly when \(s_i=n_i\) or \(s_j\ge2\), and symmetrically for \(j\). Subtracting the two one-defender obstruction families from all subsets meeting at least two parts, with their one-one intersection restored, gives the displayed polynomial. A fresh direct brute-force replay on representative multipartite profiles matched the criterion for every subset.

PASS

A later complete-multipartite secure-total cover-pebbling paper studies a different invariant. Because the complete 2007 foundational body was not accessible in this run, that source is explicitly retained as a residual bibliographic risk rather than treated as a reason to fail originality.

## value

PASS

Secure total domination is a standard robustness variant, and complete multipartite graphs are a canonical family. The theorem gives the exact feasible-set structure and all-cardinality polynomial, from which the minimum parameter follows immediately. This is a natural enumerative refinement rather than a routine recomputation of a known minimum.
