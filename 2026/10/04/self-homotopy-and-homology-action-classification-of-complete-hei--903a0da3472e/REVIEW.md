# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof partitions every order-preserving self-map into three disjoint cases and counts each case exactly. The homotopy classification follows from explicit pointwise inequalities to constant maps and from a two-sided isolation argument when both shore maps are nonconstant. The first-homology computation uses an explicit cycle basis of \(K_{p,q}\), identifies the induced map as \(\widetilde\alpha_*\otimes\widetilde\beta_*\), and derives the rank from image cardinalities. The packaged verifier independently reconstructs maps and edge-chain homology ranks for \(P_{3,3}\) and \(P_{3,4}\); for \(P_{3,3}\) it also reconstructs every map-poset component.

## Originality
PASS, with a stated archival-access risk. The closest inspected primary source is Barmak--Minian's 2006 paper on minimal finite models. It characterizes minimal graph models and supplies the finite-poset framework, but the inspected full text does not state the self-map count, map-poset isolation classification, tensor homology action, or rank-profile formulas proved here. Semantic searches using complete-bipartite, finite-space, self-map, homotopy, and homology-action aliases did not locate an implication-equivalent published result. The closest published-finding hits concern interval-endomorphism algebras and graph/poset questions rather than continuous self-maps of these finite spaces. Stong's 1966 article is a residual risk because its full text was not directly accessible.

## Value
PASS. The family is canonical: its order complex is \(K_{p,q}\), hence a wedge-type graph with first Betti number \((p-1)(q-1)\). The result gives a uniform structural description of an entire finite mapping space, not just a numerical census: homological nontriviality is exactly map-poset isolation, all homologically trivial self-maps collapse into one homotopy class, and the complete rank distribution is expressed by standard surjection numbers. This provides reusable exact data for comparing finite models of the same graph weak type.

## Closest literature and limitations
Barmak--Minian, arXiv:math/0611156v1, is the closest inspected full text because it classifies minimal finite graph models and states the order-theoretic continuity framework. The theorem here is restricted to \(p,q\ge3\), does not claim a classification of individual induced matrices, and uses finite computation only as a regression check. The inaccessible full text of Stong's foundational 1966 paper remains an explicit residual literature risk.
