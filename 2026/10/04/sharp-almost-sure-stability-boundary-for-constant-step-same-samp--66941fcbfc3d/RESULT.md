# Sharp almost-sure stability boundary for constant-step same-sample extragradient under multiplicative rotation noise

## Finding

Let \(J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), let \(\xi_k\in\{+1,-1\}\) be iid with equal probabilities, and define
\[
F(x;\xi)=(\xi I+J)x,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^2.
\]
The mean operator is \(F(x)=Jx\), which is monotone and has the unique zero \(x_\star=0\). Each sample map is \(\sqrt{2}\)-Lipschitz. Apply unconstrained same-sample stochastic extragradient with the same constant step \(\gamma>0\) in its extrapolation and update stages.

For every \(x_0\neq0\),
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac1k\log\frac{\|x_k\|_2}{\|x_0\|_2}
=
\frac14\log\!\left(16\gamma^8-4\gamma^4+1\right)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Hence \(\gamma=1/\sqrt{2}\) is the exact almost-sure last-iterate stability boundary. If \(0<\gamma<1/\sqrt{2}\), then \(x_k\to0\) exponentially almost surely. If \(\gamma>1/\sqrt{2}\), then \(\|x_k\|_2\to\infty\) exponentially almost surely. At \(\gamma=1/\sqrt{2}\),
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|x_k\|_2=0,\qquad
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|x_k\|_2=\infty
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]

Nevertheless, for every \(\gamma>0\),
\[
\mathbb{E}\|x_k\|_2^2
=
\left(1+2\gamma^2+4\gamma^4\right)^k\|x_0\|_2^2,
\]
so the second moment grows exponentially even throughout the almost-surely convergent range.

## Assumptions and scope

The claim concerns only the stated two-dimensional iid two-sample linear family, equal constant extrapolation and update steps, and the unconstrained Euclidean S-SEG update. It is a specialization of the linear stochastic-VI family in Lemma 3.11 of Yoon and Loizou with \(\rho=1\) and \(\nu=0\). The sample operator with sign \(-1\) is not monotone; only the mean operator is monotone. No claim is made for arbitrary stochastic monotone variational inequalities, for projected iterations, or for unequal steps.

## Proof

Identify \(\mathbb{R}^2\) with \(\mathbb{C}\) so that \(J\) acts as multiplication by \(i\). For a sampled sign \(s\in\{+1,-1\}\), the sample operator acts as multiplication by \(s+i\). One S-SEG iteration is
\[
\widehat z_k=\bigl(1-\gamma(s+i)\bigr)z_k,
\qquad
z_{k+1}=z_k-\gamma(s+i)\widehat z_k.
\]
Since \((s+i)^2=2si\),
\[
z_{k+1}=m_s(\gamma)z_k,\qquad
m_s(\gamma)=1-s\gamma+i(2s\gamma^2-\gamma).
\]
Thus
\[
|m_+|^2=1-2\gamma+2\gamma^2-4\gamma^3+4\gamma^4,
\]
\[
|m_-|^2=1+2\gamma+2\gamma^2+4\gamma^3+4\gamma^4,
\]
and direct multiplication gives
\[
|m_+|^2|m_-|^2=16\gamma^8-4\gamma^4+1.
\]
Because the signs are iid and the two logarithms are finite, the strong law of large numbers yields
\[
\frac1k\log\frac{|z_k|}{|z_0|}
\longrightarrow
\frac12\log|m_+|+\frac12\log|m_-|
=
\frac14\log\!\left(16\gamma^8-4\gamma^4+1\right)
\]
almost surely.

Now
\[
16\gamma^8-4\gamma^4+1<1
\]
holds exactly when \(0<\gamma<1/\sqrt{2}\), while it is greater than \(1\) exactly when \(\gamma>1/\sqrt{2}\). This proves the subcritical and supercritical statements.

At \(\gamma=1/\sqrt{2}\),
\[
|m_+|=\sqrt{2}-1,\qquad |m_-|=\sqrt{2}+1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\frac{|z_k|}{|z_0|}
\]
is a simple symmetric random walk on the lattice generated by \(\log(1+\sqrt{2})\). A one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk has almost surely unbounded positive and negative excursions, giving the stated \(\liminf\) and \(\limsup\).

Finally,
\[
\mathbb{E}|m_{\xi}|^2
=
\frac{|m_+|^2+|m_-|^2}{2}
=
1+2\gamma^2+4\gamma^4.
\]
Independence of the signs across iterations gives the exact second-moment identity.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library script `verify_seg_threshold.py` checks the polynomial identities for \(|m_\pm|^2\), their product, the second-moment factor, the critical moduli, and the signs of the Lyapunov exponent on both sides of the threshold. The proof itself is analytic; the script is a finite replay of the algebra rather than evidence for the almost-sure limit theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Yoon and Loizou, arXiv:2608.06182, distinguish same-sample from independent-sample stochastic extragradient and give in Lemma 3.11 the general two-dimensional complex multiplier family from which the present recurrence is specialized. Their paper develops diminishing-step counterexamples, including almost-sure divergence, and explicitly stresses that moment growth alone need not determine almost-sure behavior. The inspected text does not state the constant equal-step specialization above, its exact threshold \(\gamma=1/\sqrt{2}\), its critical random-walk law, or the simultaneous almost-sure convergence and exponential second-moment growth.

Gorbunov, Berard, Gidel, and Loizou, arXiv:2111.08611, provide a broad S-SEG framework; their standard same-sample guarantees rely on stronger per-sample regularity such as monotonicity of stochastic realizations. The present family falls outside that route because \(F(\cdot;-1)\) has symmetric part \(-I\).

Li et al., arXiv:2107.00464, analyze constant-step same-sample SEG for stochastic bilinear games and obtain linear behavior in an interpolation regime. Their bilinear saddle operators are structurally skew in each sample, whereas the present stochastic samples have symmetric parts \(\pm I\). Their inspected results therefore neither imply nor contain this signed-radial-noise threshold.

## Limitations

The exact threshold depends on the highly structured two-point linear sample family and equal constant steps. The result does not classify general constant-step S-SEG, nor does it imply that second moments are generally misleading. The critical simple-random-walk conclusion uses equiprobable sampling; biased sign probabilities change the log-radius drift. No independent audit has been performed.
