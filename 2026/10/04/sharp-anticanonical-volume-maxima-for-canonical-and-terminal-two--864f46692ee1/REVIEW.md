# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The singularity region is reconstructed from Reid--Tai ages on both heavy charts. The anticanonical-degree formula is standard for weighted projective space. The optimization is reduced, for each fixed \(d=b-a\), to endpoint comparison by an explicit quadratic derivative sign, and the remaining one-variable endpoint functions are handled by exact derivatives and inequalities. The low-dimensional exceptional terminal case \(r=2\) is checked separately. An exact-rational script independently confirms all formulas and equality sets through \(r=100\).

## Originality
PASS. Targeted published-finding corpus searches for anticanonical volume, two-heavy weighted projective spaces, canonical/terminal bounds, and the explicit formula did not locate an equivalent statement. The closest published-finding corpus item concerns a sharp Ehrhart threshold for reduced reflexive weighted-projective four-simplices, not anticanonical-degree extrema. Full-text inspection of Kasprzyk's archive source found general weight bounds and degree context but not this family-specific sharp optimization. Bäuerle's later sharp degree theorem is parameterized by dimension and Gorenstein index and does not give the stated two-heavy canonical/terminal formulas or equality cases.

## Value
PASS. Anticanonical volume is a central numerical invariant in Fano boundedness. This gives an exact all-dimensional extremal theorem, with complete equality classification, on a natural infinite family where the singularity conditions cut out a nontrivial two-parameter region. It separates canonical from terminal behavior and exhibits a low-dimensional equality exception, rather than reporting an arbitrary finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The strongest nearby sources checked were Kasprzyk's general canonical/terminal weight bounds and Bäuerle's Gorenstein-index degree bounds. The theorem is deliberately limited to \(\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\), and search-based originality cannot rule out an equivalent older formulation under different notation.
