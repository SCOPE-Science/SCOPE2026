# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The claim is reduced by affine invariance to an equilateral triangle. The support-function equations for a centered ellipse determine its positive-definite matrix and reproduce the prescribed-center area formula. After the substitution \(x=1-2t\), \(y=1-2u\), \(z=1-2v\), fixing \(D\) fixes both the sum and pairwise sum of \(x,y,z\); a constrained-product extremization forces two equal coordinates. Both branches, their positivity ranges, the boundary infimum, equality cases, and the sharp coefficient in \(1-\eta\ge2D\) were reconstructed independently. The deterministic verifier checks the algebra numerically but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS with historical-access risk. Horwitz's 2003 primary text gives the prescribed-center area formula (Lemma 6) and attributes the interior case to Chakerian. Minda and Phelps give the classical global Steiner maximum. Neither inspected source states a complete area interval at fixed barycentric center spread, the \(r=1/2\) transition, or the sharp global coefficient \(2\). Multiple semantic searches for aliases and equivalent parameterizations returned no equivalent statement. The inaccessible Chakerian chapter remains a residual historical risk.

## Value

PASS. The fixed-spread profile turns a classical qualitative maximization theorem into a complete affine-invariant quantitative law. It identifies all extremizers, describes the loss of a positive lower area bound beyond a precise center-spread threshold, and gives a sharp inverse-stability estimate for how close an almost-maximal inellipse center must be to the centroid. These are natural geometric data for the full family of inellipses, not an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest literature and limitations

Horwitz, arXiv:math/0312403, supplies the center-dependent area formula but optimizes a quadrilateral problem rather than the fixed-spread triangle profile. Minda–Phelps, DOI 10.1080/00029890.2008.11920581, proves only the global Steiner maximum. Chakerian's 1979 chapter is cited as antecedent for the area formula but was not directly inspected. Historical exhaustiveness is therefore not asserted.
