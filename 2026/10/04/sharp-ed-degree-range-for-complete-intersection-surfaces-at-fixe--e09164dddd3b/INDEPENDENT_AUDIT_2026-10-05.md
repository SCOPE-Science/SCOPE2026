# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Draisma et al. Corollary 2.10 gives equality for a general complete intersection and, in projective dimension two, specializes to product(d_i) times 1+T+(T^2+Q)/2. For a balancing move x<y-1 with delta=y-x-1, the pair product rises from B to B+delta while the symmetric factor falls from A to A-delta, so the change has sign delta(A-B-delta). Since B+delta=(x+2)y and A-(x+2)y is at least 1+x^2+x+y^2-y>0, every genuine balancing move strictly increases ED degree. Iteration yields the unique balanced maximum; reversing moves yields the unique one-heavy minimum. An independently written exhaustive replay over 425 multidegrees for 2<=c<=6 and 2c<=S<=4c reproduced both extrema and the examples.

## originality

PASS

The foundational theorem supplies the closed ED-degree formula but not the fixed-sum extremal classification. The balancing step is not a mere parameter substitution: two factors move in opposite directions and the strict inequality above is the substantive comparison. Claim-specific Resultary and web searches found no prior theorem with the same fixed-adjunction maximum/minimum statement.

## value

PASS

ED degree is the algebraic complexity of generic nearest-point optimization. Fixing c and S fixes the canonical polarization exponent for these surfaces, so the comparison is geometrically natural. The theorem gives both sharp extrema and a strict balancing mechanism over an infinite family, not an arbitrary numerical slice.
