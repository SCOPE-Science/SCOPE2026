# Review of Sharp extreme lower tails of the two- and three-dimensional torus matching limits

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the exact series representation
\[
L_d=\sum_{[k]}\frac{1}{2\pi^2|k|^2}(\zeta_{[k]}-1)
\]
and computes the cumulant generating function of \(-L_d\). In dimension three, a lattice Riemann-sum calculation yields \(K_3(s)\sim s^{3/2}/(3\sqrt2\,\pi)\), and exponential tilting matches the optimized Chernoff exponent \((8\pi^2/3)x^3\). In dimension two, Abel summation gives \(K_2'(s)\sim(4\pi)^{-1}\log s\); Chernoff and a concentrated tilted law then give the double-logarithmic constant \(4\pi\). The proof explicitly distinguishes the limiting-law theorem from any finite-sample extreme-tail claim.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating paper supplies the limiting series but not its extreme lower-tail constants or lower-quantile asymptotics. A broader 2026 second-chaos partition-function theorem supplies determinant formulas and only order-level Laplace growth in the relevant logarithmic/Riesz regimes. The inspected small-deviation literature concerns positive quadratic norms and does not directly cover this centered non-trace-class series. Targeted searches for the exact constants and equivalent tail formulations produced no same-statement source. The remaining risk is that a general sharp Tauberian theorem for Hilbert-Schmidt quadratic chaos may imply the result after spectral specialization.

## Value
PASS. The exact constants expose a qualitative dimension split hidden by the weak limit statement alone: double-exponential lower tails in dimension two versus cubic stretched-exponential lower tails in dimension three. The quantile asymptotics are natural invariants of the newly identified limiting laws and are relevant to understanding unusually small null matching fluctuations, while the stated limitation prevents overextension to finite-sample calibration.
