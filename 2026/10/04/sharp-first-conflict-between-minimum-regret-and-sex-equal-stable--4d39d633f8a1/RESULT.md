# Sharp first conflict between minimum-regret and sex-equal stable marriage
## Finding
Consider the classical stable-marriage problem with complete strict preferences. For a stable matching \(M\), define
\[
r(M)=\max_x \operatorname{rank}_x(M(x))
\]
and
\[
s(M)=\left|\sum_m \operatorname{rank}_m(M(m))-\sum_w \operatorname{rank}_w(M(w))\right|.
\]
Let \(R(I)\) be the stable matchings of an instance \(I\) minimizing \(r\), and let \(S(I)\) be those minimizing \(s\).

For every \(2\times2\) instance,
\[
R(I)=S(I).
\]

For \(3\times3\), the complete classification over all
\[
6^6=46656
\]
labeled strict-preference profiles is
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|c}
\text{relation} & \text{profiles} & \text{probability}\\ \hline
R(I)=S(I) & 38880 & 5/6\\
S(I)\subsetneq R(I) & 7488 & 13/81\\
R(I)\subsetneq S(I) & 144 & 1/324\\
R(I)\cap S(I)=\varnothing & 144 & 1/324.
\end{array}
\]
There are no profiles with a nonempty nonnested overlap.

Hence three pairs are the smallest balanced strict stable-marriage market in which the minimum-regret and sex-equal criteria can force incompatible stable choices.

The \(144\) disjoint profiles have additional structure. Every one has exactly two stable matchings. One has
\[
(r,s)=(2,3),
\]
and the other has
\[
(r,s)=(3,2).
\]
Under independent relabeling of men and women together with exchange of the two sides, the \(144\) profiles form exactly two orbits, each of size \(72\).

A representative is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_1&:A\succ B\succ C,\\
m_2&:A\succ C\succ B,\\
m_3&:B\succ A\succ C,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
A&:m_3\succ m_1\succ m_2,\\
B&:m_1\succ m_2\succ m_3,\\
C&:m_2\succ m_1\succ m_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
It has exactly two stable matchings. The matching
\[
M_S=(A,C,B)
\]
has participant-rank vector
\[
(1,2,1,2,3,1),
\]
men's and women's aggregate costs \(4\) and \(6\), regret \(3\), and sex-equality cost \(2\). The matching
\[
M_R=(B,C,A)
\]
has participant-rank vector
\[
(2,2,2,1,1,1),
\]
aggregate costs \(6\) and \(3\), regret \(2\), and sex-equality cost \(3\). Thus \(S(I)=\{M_S\}\) and \(R(I)=\{M_R\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
Preferences are complete and strict and every matching is perfect. Partner ranks are one-based. Stability means absence of a man-woman pair who both strictly prefer each other to their assigned partners.

The minimum-regret objective minimizes the largest participant rank. The sex-equal objective minimizes the absolute difference between the two sides' aggregate rank costs. All tied optima are retained; no external tie-breaking rule is imposed.

The exact theorem covers markets with two and three pairs. No frequency or structure claim is made for larger markets, ties, incomplete lists, roommates, or many-to-one matching.

## Proof
For two pairs, complete enumeration of all \(2^4=16\) labeled profiles gives equality of the two argmin sets. The only profiles with two stable matchings have both stable matchings tied at regret \(2\) and sex-equality cost \(2\), so both belong to both optimum sets.

For three pairs, each of six participants has one of \(3!=6\) strict orders. The verifier therefore enumerates all \(6^6=46656\) labeled profiles. For every profile it tests all six perfect matchings for blocking pairs, computes the full stable-matching set, then computes the exact integer pair \((r,s)\) for every stable matching and retains the complete argmin sets.

Two independently organized stability tests agree on every profile: one uses precomputed rank maps and one compares positions directly in the preference lists. The resulting relation counts are exactly
\[
38880,\quad7488,\quad144,\quad144
\]
for equality, strict sex-equal-set inclusion in the regret set, strict regret-set inclusion in the sex-equal set, and disjointness. No nonnested overlap occurs.

As an independent marginal check, the same enumeration reproduces the known count distribution
\[
34080,\quad11484,\quad1092
\]
for profiles with one, two, and three stable matchings respectively.

For the disjoint class, every profile has exactly two stable matchings. Their objective pairs are \((2,3)\) and \((3,2)\). Canonicalization under the symmetry group generated by independent relabelings of the two sides and side exchange gives two orbits, both of full size \(72\). The displayed instance is checked separately and has precisely the two stable matchings stated above.

## Relationship to prior work
Minimum-regret and sex-equal stable marriage are established fairness objectives. De Clercq, Schockaert, De Cock, and Nowé define sex-equality cost as the absolute difference between the two sides' aggregate partner costs and regret as the maximum individual partner cost, and give exact optimization encodings for both criteria. Iwama, Miyazaki, and Yanagisawa study the sex-equal objective and its interaction with other stable-marriage objectives.

Small-instance enumeration is also prior. Borodin and coauthors enumerate classical stable-marriage statistics and, for three pairs, record the \(34080/11484/1092\) distribution of the number of stable matchings together with egalitarian-cost data. Their inspected paper does not tabulate minimum-regret versus sex-equal argmin sets.

The result here is the exact interaction boundary for these two distinct fairness principles: equality for every two-pair instance, the complete four-way argmin-set relation census at three pairs, and the two symmetry classes of the first disjoint cases. Targeted searches for the exact counts, the \(1/324\) conflict incidence, and the three-pair disjointness boundary did not locate an equivalent published statement.

## Limitations
The theorem is a finite exhaustive classification, not a general-size structural theorem. The exhaustive computation proves the stated finite domain because every profile and every perfect matching in that domain is examined; it does not support extrapolation to larger markets.

The originality comparison covered the canonical objective literature, an exact implementation paper, and the closest small-instance enumeration paper. An unindexed thesis, exercise, note, or software table could contain the same three-pair relation census.
