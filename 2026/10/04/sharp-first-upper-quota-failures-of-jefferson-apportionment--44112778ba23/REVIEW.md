## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim has an analytic proof. Two entities satisfy upper quota because the last selected Jefferson quotient forces \(a<hp+p<hp+1\). One seat is trivial, and the three-entity/two-seat cell is excluded by comparing the dominant second quotient with the average minor share. The two boundary cells then reduce to exact highest-averages inequalities, which give the stated chambers. The simplex probabilities are exact integrals, and integer minimality follows from the same strict inequalities. An exact-rational verifier cross-checks both Jefferson implementations and the chamber formulas on finite grids.

Risk: the executable grid is a consistency check rather than a proof of the real-share continuum; the continuum result rests on the displayed inequalities and integrals.

Risk: absence from the searched literature is not a uniqueness proof. An unindexed textbook exercise, historical note, thesis, or supplementary calculation may contain the same boundary classification.

The result identifies exactly where that bias can first cross the upper-quota boundary, gives the complete geometry in each minimal cell, and quantifies each chamber under the neutral uniform-simplex model.
