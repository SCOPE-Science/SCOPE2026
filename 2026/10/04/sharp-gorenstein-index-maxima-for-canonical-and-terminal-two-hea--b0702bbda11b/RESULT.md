# Sharp Gorenstein-index maxima for canonical and terminal two-heavy weighted projective spaces
## Finding
Let \(r\ge2\), let \(1\le a\le b\), and set
\[
X_{r,a,b}=\mathbb P(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r},a,b).
\]
Write \(\iota(X)\) for the Gorenstein index, meaning the least positive integer \(m\) such that \(-mK_X\) is Cartier.

Among all members with at worst canonical singularities, and also among all members with at worst terminal singularities, the same sharp maximum occurs. When \(r=2\),
\[
\iota(X)\le6,
\]
with equality uniquely at \((a,b)=(2,3)\). For every \(r\ge3\),
\[
\iota(X)\le(2r-3)(3r-4),
\]
with equality uniquely at
\[
(a,b)=(2r-3,3r-4).
\]
The unique canonical maximizer is terminal, so the terminal subfamily has exactly the same sharp bound and equality case.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\), and \(r\ge2\), so the dimension is \(r+1\ge3\). The spaces are ordinary well-formed weighted projective spaces. The invariant \(\iota(X)\) is the Cartier index of the anticanonical divisor, not the Fano index.

## Proof
Put
\[
S=r+a+b,
\qquad
L=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b).
\]
Because the weight vector contains unit weights, the divisor class group is generated by \(\mathcal O_X(1)\), the Picard group is generated by \(\mathcal O_X(L)\), and
\[
-K_X\cong\mathcal O_X(S).
\]
Therefore
\[
\iota(X)=\frac{L}{\gcd(L,S)}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\iota(X)\le L\le ab.
\]

Let \(d=b-a\). The two heavy affine charts are cyclic quotients of types
\[
\frac1a(1^r,b),\qquad \frac1b(1^r,a).
\]
The Reid--Tai age test gives
\[
X_{r,a,b}\text{ canonical}\iff 0\le d\le r\text{ and }1\le a\le r+d,
\]
and
\[
X_{r,a,b}\text{ terminal}\iff 0\le d<r\text{ and }1\le a<r+d.
\]
Thus it is enough to optimize over the canonical triangle; if its unique maximizer is terminal, the same result automatically holds for the terminal triangle.

Assume first \(r\ge3\). Reparameterize the canonical triangle by
\[
u=r-d\ge0,
\qquad
v=r+d-a\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
a=2r-u-v,
\qquad
b=3r-2u-v.
\]
Set
\[
C_r=(2r-3)(3r-4).
\]
The product \(ab\) strictly decreases when either \(u\) or \(v\) increases. Hence, outside the following eight pairs,
\[
(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),
\]
we have \(ab<C_r\). Indeed:

- if \(u\ge3\), then \(ab\le(2r-3)(3r-6)<C_r\);
- if \(u=2\) and \(v\ge1\), then \(ab\le(2r-3)(3r-5)<C_r\);
- if \(u=1\) and \(v\ge3\), then \(ab\le(2r-4)(3r-5)<C_r\);
- if \(u=0\) and \(v\ge4\), then \(ab\le(2r-4)(3r-4)<C_r\).

For the exceptional pairs, all but \((u,v)=(1,2)\) have a large forced cancellation in the Cartier-index formula. If \(u=0\), then \(d=r\) and \(S=2b\), so \(b\mid S\) and
\[
\iota(X)\le \frac{L}{b}\le a\le2r<C_r.
\]
If \(v=0\), then \(a=r+d\) and \(S=3a\), so \(a\mid S\) and
\[
\iota(X)\le \frac{L}{a}\le b\le3r<C_r.
\]
At \((u,v)=(1,1)\), the weights are \(a=2(r-1)\) and \(b=3(r-1)\), hence
\[
L=6(r-1)<C_r.
\]

The remaining pair \((u,v)=(1,2)\) gives
\[
a=2r-3,
\qquad
b=3r-4,
\qquad
S=6r-7.
\]
Now
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(2r-3,r-1)=1.
\]
Also \(a\) is odd and
\[
\gcd(S,a)\mid \gcd(6r-7,3(2r-3))=\gcd(6r-7,2)=1,
\]
while
\[
\gcd(S,b)\mid \gcd(6r-7,2(3r-4))=\gcd(6r-7,1)=1.
\]
Thus \(L=ab\), \(\gcd(L,S)=1\), and
\[
\iota(X)=ab=(2r-3)(3r-4)=C_r.
\]
Every other canonical pair has strictly smaller index. Moreover its difference is
\[
d=(3r-4)-(2r-3)=r-1<r,
\]
and
\[
2r-3<2r-1=r+d,
\]
so this maximizer is terminal.

For \(r=2\), the nine canonical pairs are obtained from \(0\le d\le2\) and \(1\le a\le2+d\). Direct evaluation of \(L/\gcd(L,S)\) gives indices
\[
1,1,2,6,4,1,1,3,1
\]
for the pairs \((1,1),(2,2),(1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(1,3),(2,4),(3,5),(4,6)\), respectively. Hence the unique maximum is \(6\) at \((2,3)\), which is terminal.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic. The accompanying script `verify_gorenstein_index_two_heavy.py` independently enumerates the canonical and terminal parameter regions for every \(2\le r\le200\), computes
\[
\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)}{\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b),r+a+b)},
\]
and checks the sharp maxima, uniqueness, and terminality of the maximizer. It returns `VERIFY_OK`. The finite sweep is a regression check and is not used to infer the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Kasprzyk's 2013 work gives general fractional-part criteria for canonical and terminal weighted projective spaces and separately studies the Gorenstein subclass. It does not state a sharp all-dimensional maximum for the Gorenstein index on the two-heavy family.

Ghirlanda's later general formula for the Picard group and Gorenstein index of fake weighted projective spaces supplies a broad structural framework for computing the index, but it does not optimize that invariant under the two-heavy canonical or terminal constraints.

Bäuerle's work on Fano simplices studies sharp volume and multiplicity bounds at fixed Gorenstein index and gives large computational classifications by index. That direction treats the Gorenstein index as an input parameter, whereas the present result maximizes the index itself over a singularity-constrained infinite family.

## Limitations
The bound is specific to ordinary weighted projective spaces of the form \(\mathbb P(1^r,a,b)\). It does not claim a bound for arbitrary weighted or fake weighted projective spaces of the same dimension. The originality assessment is search-based, so an equivalent elementary formula under different notation could exist in older literature.
