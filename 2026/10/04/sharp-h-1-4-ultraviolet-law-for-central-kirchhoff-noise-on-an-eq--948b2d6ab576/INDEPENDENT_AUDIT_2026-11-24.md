With edge coordinates increasing from the center, Green's identity against a deterministic Kirchhoff eigenmode pairs the noisy sum of outward derivatives with its common central value up to sign. The symmetric sector has Neumann–Neumann modes with values (Nℓ)^−1/2 at zero frequency and (2/(Nℓ))^1/2 at frequency nπ/ℓ; the sum-zero sector has Dirichlet–Neumann modes with central value zero. The cutoff contains exactly the symmetric modes 0 through M; any sum-zero modes it also contains are unforced. Integrating squared Ornstein–Uhlenbeck kernels gives the term t/(Nℓ) at zero and (1−exp(−2λ_nt))/(Nℓλ_n) for n≥1; correlations from the same Brownian driver do not enter the trace in an orthonormal basis. At the endpoint subtracting 1/(Nπn) from each weighted variance leaves O(n^−3) plus an exponentially small term at every fixed t>0. Hence the finite constant is the zero-mode variance plus Euler's constant divided by Nπ plus that convergent series. Elsewhere the summand is asymptotic to π^(4α−2)ℓ^(1−4α)n^(4α−2)/N, yielding the stated power law and L² summability precisely below 1/4. A Hilbert-valued Gaussian version has finite second moment and contractive spectral projections, so divergent projected moments exclude it.
