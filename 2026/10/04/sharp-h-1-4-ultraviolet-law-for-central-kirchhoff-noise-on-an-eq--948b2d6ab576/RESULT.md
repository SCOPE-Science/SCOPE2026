# Sharp \(H_{1/4}\) ultraviolet law for central Kirchhoff noise on an equilateral star

## Finding
Consider the linear heat equation on an equilateral star with \(N\ge 2\) edges of common length \(\ell>0\), unit diffusion, zero potential, continuity at the central vertex, Kirchhoff condition at the center, and Neumann conditions at the leaves. Perturb only the central Kirchhoff condition by a scalar Brownian motion of unit variance. Let \(A\) be the resulting deterministic Kirchhoff Laplacian, use the fractional-domain norm
\[
\|u\|_{\mathcal H_\alpha}=\|(1-A)^\alpha u\|_{\mathcal H},
\]
and let \(Z(t)\) denote the stochastic convolution from zero initial data. If \(P_M\) is the spectral projector of \(-A\) onto eigenvalues at most \((M\pi/\ell)^2\), then for every fixed \(t>0\),
\[
\mathbb E\|P_M Z(t)\|_{\mathcal H_\alpha}^2
=\frac{t}{N\ell}+\frac{1}{N\ell}\sum_{n=1}^M
\frac{(1+\lambda_n)^{2\alpha}}{\lambda_n}
\bigl(1-e^{-2\lambda_n t}\bigr),
\qquad
\lambda_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{\ell}\right)^2.
\]
Consequently the regularity threshold \(\alpha<1/4\) is sharp for this canonical star. At the critical index,
\[
\mathbb E\|P_M Z(t)\|_{\mathcal H_{1/4}}^2
=\frac{1}{N\pi}\log M+C_{N,\ell,t}+o(1),
\]
for a finite constant \(C_{N,\ell,t}\). For \(1/4<\alpha<1\),
\[
\mathbb E\|P_M Z(t)\|_{\mathcal H_\alpha}^2
\sim
\frac{\pi^{4\alpha-2}\ell^{1-4\alpha}}{N(4\alpha-1)}M^{4\alpha-1}.
\]
Thus for every \(t>0\) the stochastic convolution has no \(\mathcal H_\alpha\)-valued Gaussian version when \(\alpha\ge 1/4\). The critical logarithmic coefficient is exactly \(1/(N\pi)\): increasing the star degree attenuates the ultraviolet energy from one noisy central flux by the factor \(1/N\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has exactly one central vertex and \(N\) leaves, all edges have length \(\ell\), and the edge operator is the scalar Laplacian. Coordinates are chosen from the center \(x=0\) to the leaf \(x=\ell\). Noise acts only in the central Kirchhoff flux equation and has covariance one. The statement concerns the linear stochastic convolution at a fixed positive time. It does not claim endpoint path continuity, nonlinear endpoint regularity, or the same coefficient for unequal edge lengths, nonzero potentials, multiple noisy vertices, or correlated vertex noise.

The norm is the specific fractional-domain norm generated by \((1-A)^\alpha\). Other fixed positive resolvent shifts give the same leading critical coefficient, although arbitrary equivalent renormings need not preserve a numerical leading constant.

## Proof
The Kirchhoff Laplacian splits into the permutation-symmetric sector and an orthogonal antisymmetric sector. In the symmetric sector an orthonormal eigenbasis is
\[
f_0|_e=(N\ell)^{-1/2},\qquad
f_n|_e=\sqrt{\frac{2}{N\ell}}\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{\ell}\right),\quad n\ge1,
\]
with \(Af_0=0\) and \(Af_n=-\lambda_n f_n\), where \(\lambda_n=(n\pi/\ell)^2\). Every antisymmetric eigenfunction vanishes at the center: continuity forces the common center value to be zero, and the remaining edge amplitudes have zero sum to satisfy Kirchhoff flux balance.

For Kirchhoff noise, the control/noise operator satisfies \(B^*=-L\), where \(L\) is the vector of vertex values. With covariance supported only at the center, the noise coefficient of an eigenfunction is therefore its central value. Hence all antisymmetric modes are unforced, while
\[
|f_0(v_c)|^2=\frac{1}{N\ell},\qquad
|f_n(v_c)|^2=\frac{2}{N\ell}\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Itô isometry gives
\[
\mathbb E|\langle Z(t),f_0\rangle|^2=\frac{t}{N\ell}
\]
and, for \(n\ge1\),
\[
\mathbb E|\langle Z(t),f_n\rangle|^2
=\frac{1}{N\ell\lambda_n}\bigl(1-e^{-2\lambda_n t}\bigr).
\]
Multiplying each modal variance by \((1+\lambda_n)^{2\alpha}\) yields the displayed exact cutoff formula.

For fixed \(t>0\), the exponential factor differs from one by a summable sequence. Also
\[
\frac{(1+\lambda_n)^{2\alpha}}{\lambda_n}
\sim \lambda_n^{2\alpha-1}
=\left(\frac{n\pi}{\ell}\right)^{4\alpha-2}.
\]
Thus the tail is comparable to \(n^{4\alpha-2}\), which is summable exactly when \(\alpha<1/4\). At \(\alpha=1/4\),
\[
\frac{1}{N\ell}\frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda_n}}{\lambda_n}
=\frac{1}{N\pi n}+O(n^{-3}),
\]
so subtraction of \((N\pi)^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^M n^{-1}\) leaves a convergent series. Since the harmonic sum is \(\log M+\gamma+o(1)\), the critical logarithmic law follows. For \(\alpha>1/4\), the standard power-sum asymptotic yields the stated coefficient of \(M^{4\alpha-1}\).

Finally, an \(\mathcal H_\alpha\)-valued centered Gaussian random variable has finite second moment. The diverging projected second moments for \(\alpha\ge1/4\) therefore rule out an \(\mathcal H_\alpha\)-valued Gaussian version of \(Z(t)\).

## Verification
The supplied `verify.py` evaluates the exact modal formula for several choices of \(N\), \(\ell\), and \(t\). It checks that the critical finite-difference slope converges to \(1/(N\pi)\), that subtracting \((N\pi)^{-1}\log M\) stabilizes, that a supercritical case has the predicted power-law coefficient, and that a subcritical case has a convergent tail. Running it with the Python standard library prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The analytical verification is independent of those finite calculations: the mode normalization, center values, Itô variances, and asymptotic comparison are all explicit above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kovács and Sikolya prove that Kirchhoff-noise solutions on quantum graphs have continuous versions in \(\mathcal H_\alpha\) for \(\alpha<1/4\) under a bounded-vertex-eigenfunction hypothesis, and their unit-diffusion/unit-length example satisfies that hypothesis. Their proof supplies the boundary-value spectral identity used to control the stochastic convolution, but it does not state endpoint failure or a critical spectral-cutoff asymptotic. The earliest public version is arXiv:2207.01944, first posted 2022-07-05; the journal version is Electronic Journal of Probability 28 (2023), article 74, DOI 10.1214/23-EJP962.

Fkirine, Kovács, and Sikolya later remove the uniform eigenfunction-bound hypothesis for the subcritical statement and analyze equilateral Neumann stars for strong Feller behavior. Their Proposition A.1 again states \(\alpha<1/4\) regularity, while their star example concerns controllability/strong Feller modes rather than the endpoint covariance divergence. The first public preprint is arXiv:2401.11866, posted 2024-01-22.

Da Prato and Zabczyk's 1993 boundary-noise theory is the classical one-interval precursor cited by both papers. Its accessible abstract states necessary and sufficient linear existence criteria, but the inspected material did not provide the star-specific critical cutoff law or the degree coefficient \(1/(N\pi)\).

## Limitations
The result is an exact benchmark for a highly symmetric graph and a single noisy central flux. It does not establish a universal endpoint law for arbitrary quantum graphs. The non-membership conclusion concerns the stated Gaussian stochastic convolution at each fixed \(t>0\); no assertion is made about distributions valued in weaker endpoint spaces such as logarithmically weighted scales.

An equivalent critical coefficient could exist in boundary-noise literature under a different spectral normalization or in work not retrieved by the searches. The checked quantum-graph papers do not state it, and the closest inspected general results give only the subcritical regularity theorem or general covariance representations.
