# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the complete explicit star spectrum, the published identity \(B^*=-L\), Itô isometry, and elementary asymptotics. The symmetric sector normalization and all noise coefficients are explicit. The numerical replay checks representative finite cutoffs but is not used as a substitute for the proof.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source proves only the positive statement for \(\alpha<1/4\). The later equilateral-star paper removes a hypothesis from that positive statement and uses star modes for strong Feller analysis, but the inspected text does not state endpoint non-membership, the logarithmic cutoff law, or the coefficient \(1/(N\pi)\). published-finding corpus searches for the source, the endpoint, and equivalent spectral formulations returned no covering record. Residual risk remains from classical boundary-noise literature and from the fact that the later general covariance machinery makes the present computation accessible once the symmetric star sector is isolated.

## Value
PASS. The endpoint \(\alpha=1/4\) is mathematically forced by the published regularity theorem, and the exact logarithmic defect is the natural sharpness question. The coefficient gives a concrete degree-dependent network law and a benchmark for spectral discretizations.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Kovács–Sikolya (2023), Theorem 3.8 and Example 3.10, and Fkirine–Kovács–Sikolya (arXiv:2401.11866), Proposition 4.3, Example 4.8, and Proposition A.1. Da Prato–Zabczyk (1993) is the classical interval precursor; only its accessible abstract was inspected here. The theorem is restricted to an equilateral star with one unit-variance noisy central flux and zero potential.
