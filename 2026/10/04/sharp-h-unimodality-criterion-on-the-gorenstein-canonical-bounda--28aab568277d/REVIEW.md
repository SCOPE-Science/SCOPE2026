# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The argument has three independently checkable layers. First, the Gorenstein boundary follows from the exact identities \(r-d=Q(y-2)/y\) and \(r+d-a=Q(x-3)/x\) after the two Reid--Tai inequalities are established. Second, the Braun--Davis--Solus floor formula collapses on the divisor family to \(w(m)=\lceil m/2\rceil-\lfloor m/(t+2)\rfloor\); its even and odd periods give the stated factorizations exactly. Third, the unimodality criterion is read from explicit local coefficient patterns: repeated central 4-peaks for odd \(t\ge3\), and the pattern \(3,3,2,3\) once three even blocks occur for \(t\ge6\). The checker exhaustively agrees with the formulas for every \(r\le400\) and independently reconstructs the Gorenstein canonical/non-terminal boundary through \(r=60\).

## Originality
PASS. The inspected archive source (Kasprzyk) supplies the weighted-projective/reflexive-simplex geometry but no Ehrhart classification of this boundary. Braun--Davis--Solus provide the general floor formula and study different counterexample families. Braun--Liu provide a universal geometric-series factor for reflexive \(\Delta_{(1,\mathbf q)}\) and focus their main classifications on other support patterns; their three-support discussion does not state the present boundary formulas or sharp unimodality dichotomy. The 2021 weighted-projective-simplex paper classifies IDP in low-support settings and studies stabilization, not this canonical-boundary \(h^*\) criterion. Exact-form published-finding corpus searches returned only different weighted-projective/reflexive-simplex results.

Residual risk: broad Ehrhart literature contains many specializations of weighted-projective simplex formulas, so an equivalent closed form may exist under different parameters despite the targeted searches. The sharp divisor-arithmetic unimodality criterion appears not to be stated in the inspected sources.

## Value
PASS. The theorem links a singularity-theoretic boundary singled out in algebraic geometry to an exact Ehrhart phase transition. It provides the full \(h^*\)-polynomial, not just a yes/no test, and produces infinite explicit unimodal and non-unimodal reflexive families separated by elementary arithmetic. This gives a concrete bridge between canonical/non-terminal weighted projective spaces and a central combinatorial invariant of reflexive polytopes.
