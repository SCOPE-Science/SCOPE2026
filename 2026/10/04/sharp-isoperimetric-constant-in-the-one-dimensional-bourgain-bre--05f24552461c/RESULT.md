# Sharp isoperimetric constant in the one-dimensional Bourgain--Brezis model

## Finding

Let
\[
\Pi_+=1_{\{D>0\}},\qquad \Pi_-=1_{\{D<0\}}
\]
be the Hardy frequency projections on \(\mathbb R\), with Fourier transform
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx.
\]
Suppose \(f\in L^1_0(\mathbb R)\), so \(\int_{\mathbb R}f=0\), and suppose at least one of \(\Pi_+f\) and \(\Pi_-f\) belongs to \(\dot H^{-1/2}(\mathbb R)\). Then both projections belong to \(\dot H^{-1/2}\), and
\[
\left|
\|\Pi_+f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
-
\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
\right|
\le
\frac1{2\pi}\|f\|_{L^1}^2.
\]
The coefficient \(1/(2\pi)\) is optimal.

There is an exact geometric identity behind the inequality. Define the primitive
\[
F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x f(t)\,dt.
\]
Because \(f\in L^1\) and has mean zero, \(F\) is an absolutely continuous closed curve: \(F(-\infty)=F(+\infty)=0\). Its algebraic area is
\[
\mathcal A(F)
=
\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}
\left(
\operatorname{Re}F\,(\operatorname{Im}F)'
-
\operatorname{Im}F\,(\operatorname{Re}F)'
\right)dx.
\]
Then
\[
\|\Pi_+f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
-
\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
=
2\mathcal A(F).
\]
Thus the sharp analytic estimate is exactly the signed isoperimetric inequality for the primitive curve.

Equality in the positive-sign inequality occurs exactly when, after removing intervals on which it is stationary, \(F\) goes once counterclockwise around a circle through the origin, with arbitrary orientation-preserving absolutely continuous reparameterization. The negative-sign equality cases are the clockwise circles.

In particular,
\[
\|f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
\le
2\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
+
\frac1{2\pi}\|f\|_{L^1}^2,
\]
and the coefficient \(1/(2\pi)\) is sharp.

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces and operators use the Fourier normalization of the cited Bourgain--Brezis source. The homogeneous Sobolev norm is the one generated by \(|D|^{-1/2}\), and the \(L^2\) pairing is Hermitian.

The hypothesis \(f\in L^1_0\) is essential for the closed-curve interpretation: it gives the same endpoint value of the primitive at \(-\infty\) and \(+\infty\). The result is one-dimensional and concerns the Hilbertian half-Sobolev model. It does not claim a sharp constant for the higher-dimensional Hodge estimates or for the Besov and Triebel--Lizorkin extensions in the source.

The equality classification is for the sharp imbalance inequality. It allows pauses and nonconstant positive speed around the circle, because both algebraic area and length are invariant under orientation-preserving reparameterization.

## Proof

The recent source proves the exact identity, initially under annular Fourier regularization,
\[
\|\Pi_+f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
-
\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
=
\left\langle
f,
|D|^{-1}\operatorname{sign}(D)f
\right\rangle.
\]
We identify the pairing exactly.

With the source's Fourier convention, the inverse Fourier transform of the principal-value multiplier \(1/\xi\) is
\[
\frac i2\operatorname{sign}(x).
\]
Therefore, for \(f\in L^1_0\),
\[
|D|^{-1}\operatorname{sign}(D)f(x)
=
\frac i2\int_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{sign}(x-y)f(y)\,dy
=
iF(x).
\]
The Hermitian pairing becomes
\[
\left\langle f,iF\right\rangle
=
\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\overline{iF(x)}\,dx.
\]
Since \(f=F'\) and \(F\) has the same endpoint value at both ends,
\[
\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb R}F'\overline F\,dx
=
\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}(|F|^2)'\,dx
=0.
\]
Also,
\[
\operatorname{Im}(F'\overline F)
=
-\left(
\operatorname{Re}F\,(\operatorname{Im}F)'
-
\operatorname{Im}F\,(\operatorname{Re}F)'
\right).
\]
Consequently
\[
\left\langle f,iF\right\rangle=2\mathcal A(F),
\]
which proves the area identity for annularly regularized data. Applying the source's symmetric annular regularization and passing to the limit gives the identity in the general case. Equivalently, one may truncate the multiplier to \(\varepsilon<|\xi|<R\); the corresponding kernels are uniformly bounded, and the pairings converge to the displayed \(L^1\)-integral. If one frequency energy is finite, the sharp finite truncated difference then forces the other to be finite as well.

It remains to bound the area. The length of the closed primitive curve is
\[
L=\int_{\mathbb R}|F'(x)|\,dx=\|f\|_{L^1}.
\]
Parameterize the nonstationary part by arclength on \([0,L]\), obtaining a periodic absolutely continuous curve \(G\) with \(|G'|=1\) almost everywhere. Translation does not change algebraic area, so subtract the mean of \(G\). Periodic Wirtinger gives
\[
\int_0^L|G|^2\,ds
\le
\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^2
\int_0^L|G'|^2\,ds
=
\frac{L^3}{4\pi^2}.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
2|\mathcal A(F)|
\le
\left(\int_0^L|G|^2\,ds\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^L|G'|^2\,ds\right)^{1/2}
\le
\frac{L^2}{2\pi}.
\]
Hence
\[
|\mathcal A(F)|\le\frac{L^2}{4\pi},
\]
and the desired projection estimate follows after multiplying by two.

Equality in Wirtinger requires \(G\) to contain only the first Fourier harmonic. Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz, together with \(|G'|=1\), forces its two coordinate harmonics to have equal amplitudes and quarter-period phase shift. Thus \(G\) is a once-traversed circle at constant arclength speed; undoing the arclength parameterization permits exactly orientation-preserving reparameterizations and pauses. This proves the equality classification.

Sharpness is also visible from an explicit example. Fix \(r>0\) and let
\[
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x\le0,\\
r(e^{ix}-1),&0<x<2\pi,\\
0,&x\ge2\pi.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
f(x)=ir e^{ix}1_{(0,2\pi)}(x),
\qquad
\|f\|_{L^1}=2\pi r,
\qquad
\mathcal A(F)=\pi r^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|\Pi_+f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
-
\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2
=
2\pi r^2
=
\frac1{2\pi}\|f\|_{L^1}^2.
\]

## Verification

The critical normalization was checked directly against the primary source. It defines
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx,
\]
sets \(H=-i\operatorname{sign}(D)\), uses Hermitian \(L^2\) pairings, and states the exact difference-of-energies identity in the proof of its one-dimensional Hilbertian proposition.

The kernel computation is exact under this convention:
\[
\mathcal F^{-1}(1/\xi)=\frac i2\operatorname{sign}(x),
\]
so mean zero converts the singular integral into the primitive without an unspecified constant.

The isoperimetric constant was reconstructed from periodic Wirtinger and Cauchy--Schwarz rather than inferred from numerical tests. The equality conditions of those two inequalities give the circle classification. The explicit circle primitive then reproduces the coefficient \(1/(2\pi)\) exactly.

The proof does not rely on a finite computation, numerical optimization, or a truncation as evidence for the infinite statement; truncations are used only to justify passage from the source's regularized identity to general \(L^1_0\) data.

## Relationship to prior work

Arsénio's 2026 paper introduces the trace-free Beurling--Ahlfors approach to the Bourgain--Brezis problem and records a one-dimensional Hilbertian model in Appendix A. Its Proposition A.1 proves that \(f\in L^1_0\) and \(\Pi_-f\in\dot H^{-1/2}\) imply \(f\in\dot H^{-1/2}\), but the displayed bound uses only an unspecified comparison constant. The proof contains the exact frequency-energy difference that is the starting point here.

The new observation is that the source's endpoint multiplier is not merely bounded on mean-zero \(L^1\): in its self-pairing it is exactly twice the algebraic area of the primitive curve. The classical signed isoperimetric inequality then supplies the sharp coefficient and all equality cases.

Fourier proofs of the planar isoperimetric inequality are classical; in particular, the Hurwitz method expresses signed area through Fourier coefficients, and later treatments emphasize that no smoothness or convexity is required beyond rectifiability. Those geometric results provide the sharp area ingredient, but they do not state the half-Sobolev Hardy-projection estimate in the Bourgain--Brezis formulation above.

A later paper by the same author develops broader Besov and Triebel--Lizorkin selection maps for Hodge systems. Its full text contains no one-dimensional, Hilbert-transform, or isoperimetric refinement of the Appendix A estimate.

## Limitations

The result sharpens only the one-dimensional Hilbertian model. It does not determine optimal constants in the higher-dimensional trace-free Beurling--Ahlfors estimates or in nonlinear Bourgain--Brezis selection maps.

The circle equality classification concerns the imbalance
\[
\|\Pi_+f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2-\|\Pi_-f\|_{\dot H^{-1/2}}^2.
\]
It does not classify extremizers for the separate selection-map norm problem.

Although targeted searches located the classical geometric ingredient and the directly related recent operator papers, an equivalent Hardy-projection reformulation could conceivably exist under older Fourier-geometry terminology.
