# Review of Sharp Lorentz endpoint for weakly degenerate Grushin harmonic gradients

## Correctness

PASS. The full primary proof of the weakly degenerate reverse-Hölder theorem gives a pointwise intrinsic-gradient bound of the form
\[
|\nabla_Lu|\le C A|x|^{-\alpha}
\]
after normalization of an anchored ball, where \(A\) is controlled by an outer local average of \(|u|\). The one-dimensional distribution of \(|x|^{-\alpha}\) is exactly of order
\[
\lambda^{-1/\alpha}.
\]
This gives a normalized weak
\[
L^{1/\alpha}
\]
estimate, and Grushin dilation restores the factor \(r(B)^{-1}\).

The explicit profile
\[
u_*(x,y)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)|x|^{1-2\alpha}
\]
has finite Friedrichs energy, matching conormal flux, and
\[
|\nabla_Lu_*|=(1-2\alpha)|x|^{-\alpha}.
\]
Its distribution function proves weak endpoint membership, logarithmic failure of strong endpoint membership, and failure of every larger weak exponent.

## Originality

PASS. The motivating paper states strong reverse-Hölder estimates below \(1/\alpha\) and proves failure at and above \(1/\alpha\), but it does not state the intermediate fact that the endpoint survives exactly in weak \(L^{1/\alpha}\). The full proof was inspected rather than inferred from the theorem title.

Searches covering the exact source identifier, Lorentz endpoint language, weak reverse-Hölder formulations, the explicit singular profile, and broader Grushin endpoint terminology found no implication-equivalent published result. Related Lorentz endpoint work by the same author concerns global Riesz transforms on metric cones and other geometries, not this local harmonic-gradient statement.

The residual risk is that the endpoint extraction is short enough to have appeared informally or under different local-PDE terminology.

## Value

PASS. The source emphasizes that \(1/\alpha\) is the sharp strong threshold and that the obstruction is the odd Friedrichs branch. The finding identifies the precise endpoint space generated by that same branch rather than recording only strong failure. This gives a complete local integrability picture:
\[
L^p\ \text{for }p<1/\alpha,\qquad
L^{1/\alpha,\infty}\ \text{at the endpoint},\qquad
\text{no weak }L^q\ \text{for }q>1/\alpha.
\]
That is a natural endpoint refinement of the paper's main mechanism and a reusable boundary statement for future endpoint Riesz-transform questions.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
