# Sharp normal-operator stability for the approximate Fong–Tsui inequality
## Finding
Let \(H\) be a complex Hilbert space and let \(T\in\mathcal B(H)\) be normal. Write \(M=\|T\|\). If
\[
|T|\le |\operatorname{Re}T|+\varepsilon I,
\]
with \(\varepsilon\ge0\), then
\[
\|\operatorname{Im}T\|\le \Phi(M,\varepsilon),
\]
where
\[
\Phi(M,\varepsilon)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{2M\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2},&0\le\varepsilon\le M,\\
M,&\varepsilon\ge M.
\end{cases}
\]
This bound is optimal for every \(M>0\) and every \(\varepsilon\ge0\), already among one-dimensional scalar operators. In particular, as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the sharp normal-operator scale is
\[
\|\operatorname{Im}T\|\sim \sqrt{2M\varepsilon},
\]
so the optimal Hölder exponent in the normal class is \(1/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The Hilbert space is complex and \(T\) is bounded and normal. No separability, compactness, or finite-dimensional assumption is used. The statement concerns the operator-order perturbation \(|T|\le |\operatorname{Re}T|+\varepsilon I\). It does not assert the same sharp profile for nonnormal operators.

## Proof
Because \(T\) is normal, \(A=\operatorname{Re}T\) and \(B=\operatorname{Im}T\) are commuting self-adjoint operators. The unital C*-algebra generated by \(T\) is therefore commutative, and continuous functional calculus identifies the positive-operator inequality with the pointwise inequality
\[
|z|\le |\operatorname{Re}z|+\varepsilon
\]
for every \(z\in\sigma(T)\).

Fix \(z=a+ib\in\sigma(T)\) and put \(r=|z|\). Then \(r\le M\) and \(|a|\ge r-\varepsilon\) whenever \(r\ge\varepsilon\). If \(0\le\varepsilon\le M\) and \(r\ge\varepsilon\),
\[
|b|^2=r^2-a^2\le r^2-(r-\varepsilon)^2
=2r\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2
\le2M\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2.
\]
If instead \(r\le\varepsilon\), then
\[
|b|^2\le r^2\le\varepsilon^2\le2M\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2,
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \(\varepsilon\le M\). Taking the supremum over the spectrum and using the normal functional calculus gives
\[
\|\operatorname{Im}T\|\le\sqrt{2M\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2}.
\]
For \(\varepsilon\ge M\), the universal estimate \(\|\operatorname{Im}T\|\le\|T\|=M\) gives the second branch.

Sharpness is scalar. For \(0\le\varepsilon\le M\), set
\[
z=(M-\varepsilon)+i\sqrt{2M\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Then \(|z|=M\), \(|\operatorname{Re}z|=M-\varepsilon\), and equality holds in both the hypothesis and the claimed bound for \(T=zI\). For \(\varepsilon\ge M\), take \(T=iMI\), for which \(|T|=MI\le\varepsilon I\) and \(\|\operatorname{Im}T\|=M\). This proves optimality on every nonzero complex Hilbert space.

## Verification
The proof uses only the spectral theorem for bounded normal operators and scalar maximization. The two regimes \(r\ge\varepsilon\) and \(r\le\varepsilon\) cover the spectrum. The boundary values agree: at \(\varepsilon=0\) the estimate gives \(\operatorname{Im}T=0\), and at \(\varepsilon=M\) it gives \(\|\operatorname{Im}T\|\le M\). The scalar equality examples verify sharpness for every parameter value.

## Relationship to prior work
Aouichaoui, Kittaneh, and Ma prove the zero-defect Fong–Tsui theorem \(|T|\le|\operatorname{Re}T|\Rightarrow T=T^*\) for arbitrary bounded operators. An earlier superseded preprint of Ma records a dimension-free approximate estimate of order \(M^{7/8}\varepsilon^{1/8}\) and identifies optimal quantitative stability as open; indexed discussion of that preprint notes only a general obstruction to exponents larger than \(1/2\). Earlier normal and binormal results address the exact zero-defect implication, not the sharp approximate profile above. The present statement computes the full optimal profile in the natural normal subclass.

## Limitations
No bound sharper than the published general estimate is claimed for nonnormal operators. The full text of the superseded preprint arXiv:2609.10797v1 was not recoverable during comparison; its abstract and indexed open-problem description were inspected, and targeted searches did not expose this exact normal-class formula. This leaves a residual risk that an equivalent scalar-functional-calculus observation appears there or in older literature under different wording.
