# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof converts the parity objective to the exact product
\[
\beta=\prod_i(1-2p_i),
\]
partitions the feasible parameter space by the number of negative factors,
and solves each sign class by two AM--GM inequalities plus the exact feasible
range of the negative-block mass. The small-mean boundary and the
\(\lambda>n/2\) region are handled separately by a product inequality and
complement symmetry. The endpoint constructions meet the mean constraint and
attain the stated values. Exact-rational replay independently checks the
formulas on a broad family of rational grids.

## Originality

PASS with an explicit historical-literature risk. The Fourier product identity
is standard and is not claimed as new. Searches for fixed-mean parity
extrema, even/odd Poisson-binomial probabilities, and extremization of
\(\prod_i(1-2p_i)\) did not locate the endpoint theorem. Full public result
texts for the closest Poisson-binomial stop-loss and cumulant results were
inspected; neither statement implies the parity envelope. The closest
classical sources concern Poisson approximation, Fourier representation, or
convex functionals rather than this oscillatory objective.

## Value

PASS. Parity is the highest-frequency two-point Fourier character of a
Bernoulli sum and is not controlled by the usual convex-order
homogenization principle. The theorem gives a complete fixed-mean range for
all \(n\) and \(\lambda\), identifies the phase competition between
deterministic shifts and sparse homogeneous active sets, and supplies exact
extremizers rather than only a bound.
