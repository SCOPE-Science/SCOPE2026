# Sharp phase flatness of the four-character Walsh affine basis
## Finding
Let \(G=\mathbb F_2^3\), with coordinate characters \(\chi_j(x)=(-1)^{x_j}\) for \(j=1,2,3\). For coefficients \(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3\in\mathbb C\) of unit modulus, define
\[
f_c(x)=c_0+c_1\chi_1(x)+c_2\chi_2(x)+c_3\chi_3(x).
\]
Then
\[
\min_{|c_0|=|c_1|=|c_2|=|c_3|=1}\|f_c\|_\infty^2=4+2\sqrt2.
\]
Equality holds exactly when the four coefficient directions, viewed modulo sign, are equally spaced by \(\pi/4\). Equivalently, there is \(\rho\in\mathbb T\) such that
\[
\{c_0^2,c_1^2,c_2^2,c_3^2\}=\rho\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]
One extremizer is
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(1,e^{i\pi/4},i,e^{3i\pi/4}).
\]
For this extremizer the eight squared sample magnitudes are exactly \(4+2\sqrt2\) and \(4-2\sqrt2\), each occurring four times.

## Assumptions and scope
The group is exactly \(G=\mathbb F_2^3\), and the spectrum is the affine character basis \(\{1,\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_3\}\). Only phase choices are varied: every Fourier coefficient has modulus one. The norm is the maximum over the eight points of \(G\). No claim is made here about arbitrary coefficient moduli, larger Walsh spectra, or continuous trigonometric spectra.

## Proof
Identify \(\mathbb C\) with \(\mathbb R^2\), and write \(u_j\) for the unit vector represented by \(c_j\). The eight values of \(f_c\) have norms
\[
\left|u_0+\varepsilon_1u_1+\varepsilon_2u_2+\varepsilon_3u_3\right|,
\qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Allowing the sign of \(u_0\) as well does not change the maximum, because simultaneous reversal of all four signs only negates the sum. Hence \(\|f_c\|_\infty\) is the largest norm among all sixteen signed sums.

Consider the centrally symmetric zonogon
\[
Z=[-u_0,u_0]+[-u_1,u_1]+[-u_2,u_2]+[-u_3,u_3].
\]
Its extreme points are among the signed sums, so its circumradius about the origin is exactly
\[
R=\|f_c\|_\infty.
\]
A planar zonogon generated by four segments has at most eight sides. Its perimeter is the sum of the perimeter contributions of the four centered unit segments; equivalently, from its support function \(h_Z(\theta)=\sum_j|\cos(\theta-\arg u_j)|\),
\[
P(Z)=\int_0^{2\pi}h_Z(\theta)\,d\theta
     =4\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos\theta|\,d\theta
     =16.
\]

Any convex polygon with \(m\le 8\) vertices contained in a disk of radius \(R\) has perimeter at most
\[
2mR\sin(\pi/m)\le 16R\sin(\pi/8).
\]
Indeed, if the successive central angle gaps are \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m\), each side is at most \(2R\sin(\alpha_j/2)\); concavity of sine and \(\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi\) give the first inequality, and \(m\sin(\pi/m)\) is increasing for \(m\ge2\). Therefore
\[
16=P(Z)\le16R\sin(\pi/8),
\]
so
\[
R^2\ge\csc^2(\pi/8)=4+2\sqrt2.
\]

Equality throughout forces \(Z\) to have eight vertices on its circumcircle with all central gaps equal to \(\pi/4\); thus \(Z\) is a regular octagon. Conversely, a regular octagon generated by four centered unit segments attains equality. In a planar zonogon with four distinct generator directions, the eight edges occur in four opposite pairs parallel to the generators. Thus \(Z\) is a regular octagon exactly when the four directions \(\arg c_j\pmod\pi\) are equally spaced by \(\pi/4\). Doubling angles yields the equivalent square condition
\[
\{c_0^2,c_1^2,c_2^2,c_3^2\}=\rho\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]
For the displayed extremizer, direct exact expansion gives four samples of squared modulus \(4+2\sqrt2\) and four of squared modulus \(4-2\sqrt2\).

## Verification
The standalone verifier `verify.py` checks the displayed extremizer in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\): all eight squared magnitudes reduce to the two claimed values with multiplicity four, and the larger value equals \(\csc^2(\pi/8)\). It also performs a deterministic numerical stress test over phase quadruples; this is corroborative only and is not used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Neuwirth's work on maximum-modulus trigonometric trinomials gives exact phase dependence, unimodular multiplier norms, and Sidon constants for three-frequency continuous spectra. It provides the archive-era harmonic-analysis setting for phase-sensitive maximum-modulus problems but does not contain this four-character Walsh calculation.

Seigner proves that the Sidon constant of the infinite Rademacher system is \(\pi/2\) and uses finite equal-modulus root-of-unity phase choices to establish asymptotic sharpness. That argument addresses the unrestricted Sidon constant and an asymptotic family; it does not identify the exact minimum maximum modulus for four equal-modulus coefficients, nor the regular-octagon equality class above.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns exactly four affine Walsh characters with equal coefficient magnitudes. It does not determine the unrestricted four-character Sidon constant, because allowing unequal magnitudes is a different optimization problem. The originality comparison found no covering statement in the inspected harmonic-analysis sources or semantic database searches, but an equivalent planar signed-sum lemma could exist in older convex-geometry literature under zonogon or vector-balancing terminology.
