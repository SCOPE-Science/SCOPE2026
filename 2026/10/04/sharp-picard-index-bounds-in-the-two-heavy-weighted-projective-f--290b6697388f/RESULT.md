# Sharp Picard-index bounds in the two-heavy weighted-projective family
## Finding
For every integer \(r\ge2\), let
\[
X_{r,a,b}=\mathbb P(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r},a,b),\qquad 1\le a\le b,
\]
and write
\[
\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)=[\mathrm{Cl}(X):\mathrm{Pic}(X)].
\]
Then
\[
\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b).
\]
Among the canonical members,
\[
\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)\le (2r-1)(3r-1),
\]
with equality uniquely at
\[
(a,b)=(2r-1,3r-1).
\]
Among the terminal members, when \(r=2\) the maximum is \(6\), uniquely at \((a,b)=(2,3)\). For every \(r\ge3\),
\[
\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)\le (2r-3)(3r-4),
\]
with equality uniquely at
\[
(a,b)=(2r-3,3r-4).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\). The integer \(r\) is at least \(2\), so the space has dimension \(r+1\ge3\). Because at least two weights equal \(1\), the weight vector is well formed. The invariant \(\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}\) is the index of the Picard group inside the divisor class group; equivalently here it is the least positive weighted degree whose hyperplane Weil class becomes Cartier. The theorem is restricted to ordinary weighted projective spaces with exactly two possibly non-unit weights.

## Proof
Put \(d=b-a\). First determine the Picard index. For a well-formed weighted projective space \(\mathbb P(q_0,\ldots,q_n)\), the divisor class group is generated by the weighted hyperplane Weil class \(H\). At the torus fixed point corresponding to the coordinate of weight \(q_i\), the local class group has order \(q_i\), and the image of \(H\) generates that local class group. Hence \(mH\) is Cartier exactly when every \(q_i\) divides \(m\). Therefore
\[
\mathrm{Pic}(X)=\operatorname{lcm}(q_0,\ldots,q_n)\,\mathbb Z H
\]
inside \(\mathrm{Cl}(X)=\mathbb Z H\). In our family this gives
\[
\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)
=\frac{a(a+d)}{\gcd(a,d)}.
\]

For completeness, the singularity region is recalled directly. The unit-weight charts are smooth. The two heavy charts are cyclic quotients
\[
\frac1a(1^r,b),\qquad \frac1b(1^r,a).
\]
For a quotient \(\frac1m(1^r,c)\), the \(k\)-th nontrivial element has age
\[
\frac{rk+[kc]_m}{m},
\]
where \([u]_m\) denotes the least nonnegative residue. Applying the Reid--Tai inequalities to the two heavy charts gives exactly
\[
0\le d\le r,\qquad 1\le a\le r+d
\]
for canonicity, and
\[
0\le d\le r-1,\qquad 1\le a\le r+d-1
\]
for terminality. Indeed, on the \(b\)-chart the \(k=1\) age forces \(d\le r\), strictly \(d<r\) in the terminal case; once this holds, the residue \([-kd]_b\) proves all remaining ages. On the \(a\)-chart the residue \([kd]_a\) similarly reduces the condition to \(a\le r+d\), strictly \(a<r+d\) in the terminal case.

Now optimize the Picard index.

For canonical spaces, suppose first that \(d=r\). Then \(a\le2r\). If \(a=2r\),
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(2r,3r)=6r<(2r-1)(3r-1)
\]
for every \(r\ge2\). If instead \(a\le2r-1\), then
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(a,a+r)\le a(a+r)\le(2r-1)(3r-1).
\]
At \(a=2r-1\), one has \(\gcd(2r-1,r)=1\), so equality is attained. If \(d\le r-1\), then \(a\le r+d\le2r-1\) and \(b=a+d\le r+2d\le3r-2\), whence
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\le ab\le(2r-1)(3r-2)<(2r-1)(3r-1).
\]
Thus the canonical maximizer is unique.

For terminal spaces with \(r\ge3\), suppose first that \(d=r-1\). Then \(a\le2r-2\). If \(a=2r-2\),
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(2r-2,3r-3)=6(r-1)<(2r-3)(3r-4).
\]
If \(a\le2r-3\), then
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(a,a+r-1)\le a(a+r-1)\le(2r-3)(3r-4).
\]
At \(a=2r-3\), \(\gcd(2r-3,r-1)=1\), so equality holds. If \(d\le r-2\), then \(a\le r+d-1\le2r-3\) and \(b=a+d\le r+2d-1\le3r-5\), so
\[
\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\le ab\le(2r-3)(3r-5)<(2r-3)(3r-4).
\]
This proves uniqueness for \(r\ge3\). When \(r=2\), the terminal triangle consists of \((1,1),(1,2),(2,3)\), with Picard indices \(1,2,6\), respectively.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic. The bundled script `verify_picard_index_bounds.py` independently evaluates the full Reid--Tai age conditions, compares them with the two triangular descriptions, computes \(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\), and checks every maximizing equality case for all \(2\le r\le60\). It prints `VERIFY_OK`. The finite computation is a regression test only; it is not used to justify the infinite quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work
Kasprzyk gives general fractional-part criteria for canonical and terminal weighted projective spaces and emphasizes the combinatorial nature of their singularities. Springer studies Picard indices of torus-action surfaces; in the weighted-projective discussion the Picard group is computed as the intersection of the local Cartier subgroups, giving the least-common-multiple rule, and a higher-dimensional weighted-projective example illustrates the same local-class-group mechanism.

The present theorem combines these two established ingredients in the all-dimensional two-heavy family and performs the resulting arithmetic optimization. Targeted searches using the exact family, the terms “Picard index”, “Cartier index”, “minimal Veronese degree”, and the candidate equality patterns did not locate the stated canonical or terminal sharp bounds.

## Limitations
The result does not optimize Picard index over weighted projective spaces with three or more non-unit weights, nor over fake weighted projective spaces with class-group torsion. The originality assessment is search-based rather than exhaustive; an equivalent specialization could exist under different terminology. The later Picard-index literature supplies the general local-class-group mechanism, not the sharp all-dimensional optimization proved here.
