# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The local class groups of a well-formed weighted projective space show that the hyperplane Weil class becomes Cartier precisely in degrees divisible by all weights, giving \(\iota_{\mathrm{Pic}}=\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\). The Reid--Tai specialization is reconstructed in the proof, not imported as a black box. The canonical and terminal lcm maxima then follow from a complete split into the outer difference boundary and its complement, with the exceptional terminal case \(r=2\) enumerated explicitly. The regression script independently checks the local ages, triangle conditions, lcm values, and equality cases through \(r=60\).

## Originality
PASS. The inspected singularity literature gives general canonical/terminal criteria; the inspected Picard-index literature gives the lcm/local-class-group mechanism. Searches using the exact family, “Picard index”, “Cartier index”, “minimal Veronese degree”, and the candidate extremal formulas did not locate the combined sharp theorem. The closest indexed weighted-projective result found concerns reflexive four-simplices and \(h^*\)-vectors rather than Picard index.

## Value
PASS. The Picard index controls the failure of Weil divisor classes to be Cartier and identifies the first weighted Veronese degree carrying the natural hyperplane line bundle. The theorem gives exact worst-case growth and unique equality cases in every dimension under two central singularity conditions, so it is a natural structural invariant rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kasprzyk, arXiv:1304.3029, supplies general canonical and terminal weighted-projective criteria. Springer, DOI 10.1007/s13348-024-00443-x, supplies the local-class-group description of weighted-projective Picard index. No equivalent all-dimensional two-heavy extremal theorem was located. The result does not cover arbitrary weighted or fake weighted projective spaces, and literature-search incompleteness remains a residual originality risk.
