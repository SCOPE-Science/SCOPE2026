# Review: Sharp radial product profile for vertex distances in a regular tetrahedron

## Correctness
PASS. The proof converts directions to the tight-frame coordinates \(t_i\), classifies every stationary point of the squared distance product by a single quadratic Lagrange equation, enumerates the only three multiplicity types, and compares their values by exact factorizations. The exceptional zero at \(\rho=R\) and the center case \(\rho=0\) are handled separately. The connectedness argument establishes the complete interval, not merely the two extrema.

## Originality
PASS with residual historical-access risk. Hajja–Hayajneh–Nguyen–Shaqaqha (arXiv:1609.06552v1) was inspected in full at the relevant sections: it concerns the global quartic relation among regular-simplex vertex distances and its algebraic uniqueness, not a radial product profile. Murphy–Tran (arXiv:2605.12985) concerns planar triangle product optimization and does not imply the tetrahedral theorem. Direct full text of Bentin's 1995 one-page note was unavailable; this is retained as a risk rather than converted into a novelty claim.

## Value
PASS. The multiplicative vertex-distance invariant is geometrically natural, and the fixed-\(OP\) constraint is canonical under the regular tetrahedron's symmetry. The theorem gives a complete exact range at every radius and identifies all endpoint directions, providing substantially more information than the known polynomial relation among the four distances.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest literature: Hajja et al., arXiv:1609.06552v1; Bentin, The Mathematical Gazette 79 (1995), 106; Murphy–Tran, arXiv:2605.12985. The theorem is restricted to Euclidean regular tetrahedra; no higher-dimensional extension is asserted. The Bentin note and potentially unindexed older terminology remain residual originality risks.
