# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The final claim was reconstructed from the standard orthogonal-coordinate model of a disphenoid. The identities for \(R\), \(r\), the edge-pair lengths, and \(q\) reduce the problem to the product of three positive normalized coordinates with fixed first two elementary symmetric functions. The cosine parameterization gives the exact \(\cos(3\theta)\) law, and positivity gives the \(q=1/4\) transition. The bundled verifier replays all critical formulas but is not used as a substitute for the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with recorded access risk.** The closest inspected full text, Hajja–Walker, gives the equifacial characterization, coincidence of centers, and the circumradius identity \(R^2=(\ell^2+m^2+n^2)/8\), but not the fixed-anisotropy radius-ratio profile. Hajja's 2007 optimization paper addresses the solid-angle sum. Published-results searches found no equivalent profile; the nearest tetrahedral result uses normalized Ptolemy slacks at different fixed invariants. Leech 1950 and Scott 2006 could not be read in full and remain explicit residual risks.

## Value
**PASS.** The claim turns two standard shape descriptors—edge-square anisotropy and the inradius/circumradius ratio—into a complete sharp phase diagram for disphenoids. It identifies all extremizing similarity classes and a natural degeneracy threshold, supplying more structural information than the classical regular-case inequality alone.

## Closest literature and limitations
The strongest directly relevant inspected source is Hajja–Walker on equifacial tetrahedra. Its structural formulas are used as prior context, not claimed as new. The theorem is restricted to nondegenerate disphenoids, and zero is only an infimum when \(q\ge1/4\). The inaccessible historical items are not treated as non-covering merely because searches failed.
