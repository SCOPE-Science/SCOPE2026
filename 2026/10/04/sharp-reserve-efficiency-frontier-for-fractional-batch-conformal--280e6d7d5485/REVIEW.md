# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof reconstructs the candidate-set inequality from the ratio e-value and checks all boundary regimes. The map \(e(v)=(n+1)v/(B+v)\) is strictly increasing on positive scores, with supremum \(n+1\). This yields the all-space branch exactly at \(h\ge1+n\lambda\) and the finite endpoint when \(h<1+n\lambda\). The endpoint derivative is strictly negative because \(h>1\). The reserve frontier follows from the exact infimum \(\inf_{v>0}W_\lambda(v)=W(1-\lambda)\). A deterministic checker reproduces the finite-grid implications but is not used as a substitute for proof.

## Originality

PASS. The closest source, Gauthier–Bach–Jordan (arXiv:2503.13050), supplies both the ratio e-value and the predictable fractional martingale family, but it displays the batch prediction set only for the all-in process and explicitly leaves alternative processes for exploration. The inspected fuzzy-prediction-set, p-to-e calibration, and generic betting/sequential-testing papers do not state or imply the combined exact phase transition and worst-case reserve frontier. Residual risk remains that equivalent algebra exists in unindexed or unpublished material, so no historical-priority claim is made.

## Value

PASS. The theorem converts a qualitative alternative-process suggestion into an exact design frontier: it tells when a finite current upper score is possible, how sharply it changes with the fraction, and how much fraction can be spent while retaining enough wealth to make the next batch informative. This is directly motivated by the sequential conformal construction and isolates a nontrivial current-versus-future tradeoff. It does not claim a multi-step stochastic-control optimum.

## Closest literature and limitations

The direct source is arXiv:2503.13050, especially Corollary 2.6 and Remark 2.7. arXiv:2509.13130v3 gives a broader e-value/prediction-set optimality perspective; DOI 10.1093/jrsssb/qkad009 supplies the betting framework; arXiv:2606.03600 studies a different set-preserving p-to-e efficiency problem. The present theorem is conditional and one-step, and the reserve guarantee is worst-case over the current score.
