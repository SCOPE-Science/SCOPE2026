# Review of Sharp starlikeness order for the half-tanh Ma–Minda class

## Correctness

PASS. The exact identity
\[
\operatorname{Re}\tanh(x+iy)
=
\frac{\sinh(2x)}
{\cosh(2x)+\cos(2y)}
\]
reduces the disk maximum to the first-quadrant boundary arc. On that arc the derivative has the sign of the explicit function \(G\). The packaged verifier establishes one global sign change, and the resulting critical bracket gives the stated numerical order.

Subordination then places every logarithmic derivative \(zf'/f\) in the same supporting half-plane. The canonical Ma–Minda extremal satisfies \(zf_*'/f_*=\Phi\), so the boundary infimum is attained asymptotically and the order cannot be increased.

## Originality

PASS. The 2026 defining paper was checked for the class definition and result scope: it develops coefficient functionals, inverse/logarithmic coefficients, and majorization, not the exact starlikeness order. The accessible full 2023 paper concerns the full-tanh generator and focuses on convolution, determinant, coefficient, and Bernardi-operator results. The 2021 full-tanh radius paper studies radii and disk containment, not the exact supporting half-plane.

Candidate-specific published-finding searches using the DOI, half-tanh terminology, order-of-starlikeness terminology, and minimum-real-part formulation returned no implication-equivalent result. The closest indexed geometric-function result concerns an arcsine-generated class with a different image and invariant.

The main residual risk is that the exact real-part minimum for \(\tanh(\mathbb D)\) may have been recorded under a geometric image-domain formulation not surfaced by the searches.

## Value

PASS. The defining object is a new special-function Ma–Minda class, and the classical order of starlikeness is one of its basic global geometric invariants. The result gives the exact sharp half-plane inclusion rather than a coarse disk or radius estimate, and it is accompanied by a sharp extremal. This places the new class quantitatively inside the classical hierarchy \(\mathcal S^*(\alpha)\) and supplies a reusable geometric constant for subsequent radius and inclusion problems.
