# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The final claim is a pointwise theorem for the exact published s-wave Mellin multiplier. The reduction to \(F(t)\), the positive-coefficient series, the affine-in-\(\gamma\) extension, and the Taylor obstruction below \(52/27\) jointly prove the global threshold and the sharp upper constant. The only computation used in the proof is exact algebra; finite checker samples are not used as proof.

## Originality
PASS. The primary article gives the multiplier and uses it for a lower-bound stability argument, not for a sharp upper norm. The follow-up uses the same multiplier at zero to prove subcritical instability and explicitly notes loss of accuracy in its position-space bounds. Exact-constant, Mellin-multiplier, zero-frequency-maximizer, and \(52/27\) searches found no equivalent statement. The closest literature therefore supplies the object but does not imply the claimed upper-bound transition.

## Value
PASS. The sharp continuity constant is a natural norm invariant of the homogeneous s-wave sector, and \(52/27\) is not an arbitrary slice: it is exactly where the curvature of the multiplier at zero changes sign and, by the global inequality, where the maximizing Mellin frequency locks to zero. This quantifies the strong-repulsion side of the same multiplier that controls ultraviolet stability.

## Closest literature and limitations
Basti--Cacciapuoti--Finco--Teta prove stability for \(\gamma>\gamma_c\) with a non-sharp full-form continuity estimate. Ferretti--Teta prove optimality of \(\gamma_c\) from below and give a deliberately less accurate position-space estimate. The present theorem is restricted to the homogeneous s-wave upper constant and does not solve the off-zero maximization problem below \(52/27\). Residual originality risk is an unindexed equivalent calculation under different Mellin notation.
