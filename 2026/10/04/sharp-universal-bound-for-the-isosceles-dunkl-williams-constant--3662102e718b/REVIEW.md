# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The equivalent unit-sphere formula for \(DW_I(X)\) was checked in the full defining paper and independently reconstructed via the transformations \(r=(x+y)/2\), \(s=(x-y)/2\) and the converse normalization. The new estimate then uses only the triangle inequality and \(\|x+y\|\le2\). The scalar remainder is exactly \(9/4-(2+t-t^2)=(t-1/2)^2\). The explicit \(\ell_\infty^2\) pair attains equality, so the constant cannot be lowered.

Risk: no equality-case classification is established, but none is needed for the stated sharp universal bound.

## Originality
**PASS.** Fu–Xie–Li 2023 is the exact-source comparison. Its Proposition 3.4 supplies the equivalent formulation, its Example 3.5 proves \(DW_I(\ell_\infty^2)=9/4\), and the following discussion explicitly conjectures that \(9/4\) is the best universal upper bound while saying the authors cannot prove it. Their closing open-problem paragraph again asks for the best upper bound. Targeted searches covered the invariant name, \(9/4\), the conjecture, aliases, and the defining authors. The later Birkhoff-orthogonality paper studies different constants and does not imply this statement on its displayed abstract.

Risk: bibliographic searches cannot rule out every unindexed or unpublished duplication, and the later Birkhoff paper was not available as full text in the inspected sources. This does not create a decisive unresolved comparison because its abstract defines a different invariant.

## Value
**PASS.** The finding resolves an explicit sharp-constant conjecture in the defining paper and replaces the earlier universal upper bound \(\sqrt2+1\) by the exact optimum \(9/4\). Sharpness is already witnessed by the classical plane \(\ell_\infty^2\), so the result identifies the global extremal constant rather than an arbitrary slice or a small numerical refinement.
