# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The unit ball, norming functionals, smoothness diameter, Birkhoff--James orthogonality witnesses, side-condition equality, and exact minimum approximate-orthogonality parameter are all derived algebraically. The key endpoint values are \(f_+(w)=s\varepsilon\), \(f_-(w)=\varepsilon\), and \(\|w\|_\delta=2s\), so the exact threshold is \(\varepsilon/2\). The proof explicitly distinguishes the two ranges split at \(\varepsilon=2/3\).

## Originality
**PASS.** The source theorem proves the \(\varepsilon/2\) upper bound but does not state its sharpness. Its preceding hexagonal example uses the same geometry for a different obstruction: with equal scaling, the sum is collinear with the distinguished point and the later theorem's side condition is not satisfied. The new rescaling makes that side condition an equality and simultaneously attains the functional bound. Searches for the theorem name, approximate smoothness, right-additivity, the coefficient, and the source hexagon found no statement implying this sharpness construction. A later paper on approximate orthogonality preservers studies additive mappings, a different implication.

## Value
**PASS.** Optimality of a quantitative constant in a published structural theorem is a natural boundary question. The construction is two-dimensional, exact, parameterized for a continuum of \(\varepsilon\), and identifies why the theorem's proof loses no constant under its stated side condition. It is not a renaming, a numerical recomputation, or an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Chmieliński--Khurana--Sain, *Approximate smoothness in normed linear spaces*, especially characterization (1.8), Example 3.1, and Theorem 3.6. Chmieliński--Stypka (2025) concerns approximate preservation by additive operators and does not cover right-additivity sharpness. The construction is proved only for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), which already establishes universal optimality of the coefficient \(1/2\). No exhaustive classification of extremizers is asserted.
