# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the published harmonic-triple face-poset theorem only as a premise. The codimension formula shows that an incident edge can arise only by one adjacent merge or by adding one second product maximum to the marked set. The two merge exceptions and the \(s-1\) set-enlargement choices yield the stated degree. The right-to-left-maxima enumeration is then bijective. The packaged verifier independently enumerates the combinatorics through \(n=8\).

## Originality
PASS. The primary harmonic-polytope paper gives the full face-poset model, global vertex/facet counts, and a qualitative non-simplicity observation, but no vertex-degree formula or classification/enumeration of simple vertices. The later bipermutahedron paper studies a related simple refinement and does not supply the claimed statistic for the harmonic polytope. Targeted searches of the published-finding index, exact-phrase web searches, and the SageMath exact polytope entry revealed no equivalent statement. The main residual risk is that the result is a nontrivial derived statistic of an already complete face-poset encoding, or that an uncatalogued source uses different terminology.

## Value
PASS. Simplicity is a canonical local invariant of a polytope and is specifically relevant here because non-simplicity is a known obstacle in using the harmonic polytope's toric geometry. The exact classification identifies the surviving regular local cones, while the closed count shows that their density tends to zero. This is structural information rather than a routine small-case census.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ardila--Escobar, arXiv:2006.03078, whose Proposition 2.4 supplies the harmonic-triple face poset from which the new local theorem is proved. Ardila, arXiv:2008.02295, concerns the related bipermutahedron. The result does not give a closed full degree distribution, a complete one-skeleton, or a singularity resolution. The finite checker through \(n=8\) is corroboration only.
