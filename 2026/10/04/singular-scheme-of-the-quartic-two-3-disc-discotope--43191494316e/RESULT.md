# Singular scheme of the quartic two-3-disc discotope
## Finding
Let \(S\subset\mathbb A^4_{\mathbb C}\) be the quartic purely nonlinear part from Gesmundo--Meroni Example 8.1. With coordinates \((x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\), its equation is
\[
F=(x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_4^2)^2-4(x_1^2x_4^2+x_2^2+x_3^2)=0.
\]
Set \(p=x_2+i x_3\) and \(q=x_2-i x_3\). Then the reduced singular locus consists of exactly ten affine lines:
\[
L_+=V(p,q,x_4-x_1),\qquad L_-=V(p,q,x_4+x_1),
\]
and, for each \(\varepsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}\),
\[
M^p_{\varepsilon,\delta}=V(x_1-\varepsilon,x_4-\delta,p),\qquad
M^q_{\varepsilon,\delta}=V(x_1-\varepsilon,x_4-\delta,q).
\]
The scheme-theoretic singular locus is reduced everywhere except at the origin. At the origin,
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,0}\cong\mathbb C[[u,v,P,Q]]/(PQ-u^2v^2),
\]
and its completed singular ideal is
\[
(P,Q,u^2v,uv^2).
\]
Its radical is \((P,Q,uv)\). Thus the reduced support is the crossing of the two main lines, while the nilradical quotient has \(\mathbb C\)-length one: it is generated by the class of \(uv\), which is annihilated by \(u\) and \(v\) and has square zero.

At each of the four points
\[
v_{\varepsilon,\delta}=(\varepsilon,0,0,\delta),
\]
the unique main line through that point meets the two isotropic lines \(M^p_{\varepsilon,\delta}\) and \(M^q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\). The completed local equation is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,v_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\cong\mathbb C[[U,V,p,q]]/(U^2+Vpq),
\]
whose singular ideal \((U,pq,Vp,Vq)\) is radical with three line components. At every other singular point the Hessian has rank three, so the germ is analytically a line times an ordinary double point. Hence the generic transverse singularity along every one of the ten reduced irreducible singular lines is \(A_1\).

Over \(\mathbb R\), the equations \(p=0\) or \(q=0\), together with real \(x_2,x_3\), force \(x_2=x_3=0\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(S)(\mathbb R)=L_+(\mathbb R)\cup L_-(\mathbb R).
\]
The four points that the source identifies as the only boundary points exposed by more than one vector are exactly the four triple-branch points of the complex reduced singular locus.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\), and the real statement uses the standard real form. The object is the affine hypersurface \(S\subset\mathbb A^4\) of Example 8.1, not its projective closure. No claim is made for generic pairs of three-dimensional discs, for discotopes with more summands, or for the general irreducibility and birationality conjectures in the source. The local analytic identifications use characteristic zero through formal coordinate changes and the splitting lemma.

## Proof
Write \(a=x_1\), \(b=x_4\), \(p=x_2+i x_3\), and \(q=x_2-i x_3\). Since \(pq=x_2^2+x_3^2\), the quartic becomes
\[
F=(a^2+b^2+pq)^2-4(a^2b^2+pq).
\]
Its four partial derivatives are
\[
F_a=4a(a^2+pq-b^2),\qquad F_b=4b(-a^2+pq+b^2),
\]
\[
F_p=2q(a^2+b^2+pq-2),\qquad F_q=2p(a^2+b^2+pq-2).
\]
If \(p=q=0\), then \(F=(a^2-b^2)^2\), so a singular point satisfies \(b=\pm a\), giving \(L_+\cup L_-\).

Now suppose at least one of \(p,q\) is nonzero. The last two derivative equations imply
\[
a^2+b^2+pq=2.
\]
Neither \(a\) nor \(b\) can vanish: if \(a=0\), then the derivative in \(b\) forces \(b=0\), while the displayed equality gives \(pq=2\), contradicting \(F=0\); the argument for \(b\) is symmetric. Hence the first two derivative equations give
\[
a^2+pq-b^2=0,\qquad -a^2+pq+b^2=0.
\]
Adding yields \(pq=0\), and then \(a^2=b^2\). Together with \(a^2+b^2=2\), this gives \(a^2=b^2=1\). Therefore the remaining singular points lie on the eight isotropic lines \(M^p_{\varepsilon,\delta}\) and \(M^q_{\varepsilon,\delta}\). This proves the set-theoretic decomposition.

At the origin, use the exact identity
\[
F=(a^2-b^2)^2-pq\bigl(4-2(a^2+b^2)-pq\bigr).
\]
The factor in parentheses is a unit there. Put \(u=a-b\), \(v=a+b\), replace \(p\) by the formal coordinate \(P=p(4-2(a^2+b^2)-pq)\), and put \(Q=q\). The completed local equation becomes
\[
G=PQ-u^2v^2=0.
\]
The Jacobian singular ideal is therefore
\[
(P,Q,u v^2,u^2v).
\]
Its radical is \((P,Q,uv)\). Modulo the singular ideal the class \(uv\) is nonzero, while \(u(uv)=v(uv)=(uv)^2=0\). Hence the nilradical quotient over its reduction is one-dimensional over \(\mathbb C\), supported only at the origin. This proves the asserted unique nonreduced point of the singular scheme.

By changing signs of \(a\) and \(b\), every point \(v_{\varepsilon,\delta}\) is equivalent to \((a,b,p,q)=(1,1,0,0)\). Write
\[
a=1+\frac{v+u}{2},\qquad b=1+\frac{v-u}{2}.
\]
A direct expansion gives
\[
F=u^2\bigl((v+2)^2+pq\bigr)+pq\bigl(v(v+4)+pq\bigr).
\]
The first coefficient is a unit, so absorb it into \(U^2\). The function \(V=v(v+4)+pq\) is a formal coordinate because its derivative with respect to \(v\) is \(4\) at the origin. Thus the completed local equation is \(U^2+Vpq=0\). Its singular ideal is \((U,pq,Vp,Vq)\), a squarefree monomial ideal, so the three line branches meet with reduced singular scheme.

Away from the origin and the four points \(v_{\varepsilon,\delta}\), the Hessian has rank three. On \(L_+\), parametrized by \((a,b,p,q)=(t,t,0,0)\), one \(3\times3\) Hessian minor is
\[
-128t^2(t-1)^2(t+1)^2,
\]
and the same conclusion holds by symmetry on \(L_-\). On an isotropic line, for instance \((a,b,p,q)=(1,1,0,t)\), one \(3\times3\) Hessian minor is
\[
-512t^2.
\]
Thus the formal splitting lemma gives an ordinary double point transverse to the one-dimensional singular stratum. Such a transverse \(A_1\) has reduced Jacobian singular scheme, completing the scheme-theoretic classification away from the origin.

## Verification
The embedded exact script `verify_discotope.py` uses exact SymPy polynomial arithmetic. It reconstructs the compact quartic and its four derivatives; verifies that all ten listed lines lie in the Jacobian zero set; checks the two Hessian-minor certificates; verifies the exact origin and vertex identities; and checks directly that, for the origin normal form, the class \(uv\) survives modulo \((u^2v,uv^2)\) but is annihilated by \(u\), \(v\), and itself. Running the script prints `VERIFY_OK`; the bundled `verification_output.txt` records the replay output.

The symbolic replay supports the displayed polynomial identities and local algebra. Exhaustiveness of the singular-locus list is the algebraic case split above, not an inference from finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Gesmundo and Meroni introduce this exact pair of three-dimensional discs in Example 8.1 as an illustration of the difficulties that arise beyond sums of two-dimensional discs. They give the irreducible quartic equation above, its degree, and the four real boundary points exposed by more than one vector. They do not state the singular locus, its ten reduced components, the embedded nilpotent structure at the origin, or the local analytic types. Their surrounding discussion presents the example as motivation for higher-dimensional analogues of their irreducibility and critical-locus results.

Mathis and Meroni's later work on fiber convex bodies treats discotopes in \(\mathbb R^3\), including the three-disc “dice,” but does not cover this four-dimensional quartic or its complex singular scheme. Targeted searches for the exact example, discotope singular loci, the ten-line decomposition, and the origin's nonreduced Jacobian scheme did not locate a published statement implying the result.

## Limitations
This is a classification of the affine singular scheme of one named quartic example. It does not claim that the same ten-line pattern persists under perturbing the two three-discs, and it does not determine the singularities of the projective closure at infinity. The literature search cannot exclude uncrawled or unpublished equivalent descriptions under unrelated terminology; the principal residual originality risk is an unnoticed classical classification of this specific quartic hypersurface after a non-obvious coordinate change.
