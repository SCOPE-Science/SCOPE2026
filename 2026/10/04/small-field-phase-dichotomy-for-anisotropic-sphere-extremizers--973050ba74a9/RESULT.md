# Small-field phase dichotomy for anisotropic sphere extremizers
## Finding

Let
\[
Q(x)=x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2
\]
over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\), and define
\[
S_3=\{x\in\mathbb F_3^3:Q(x)=1\},\qquad
S_5=\{x\in\mathbb F_5^3:Q(x)=2\}.
\]
For either surface, use the standard additive character and the usual normalized finite-field Fourier extension operator. The normalization changes the numerical sharp constant but not the extremizers.

Pedro Ronda's recent sharp theorem applies to both surfaces because \(-1\) is nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(-2\) is nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_5\); it gives, in particular, that every maximizer has constant modulus.

The phases of all nonzero maximizers can then be classified exactly.

For \(S_3=\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}\), write a constant-modulus maximizer as \(f=c\,u\) with \(|u|=1\). Then
\[
u(e_1)u(-e_1)=u(e_2)u(-e_2)=u(e_3)u(-e_3),
\]
and this condition is also sufficient. Consequently the phase family is a four-dimensional torus before quotienting by overall scale.

For \(S_5\), every nonzero maximizer is exactly
\[
f(x)=C\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{5}\,a\cdot x\right),
\qquad C\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\},\quad a\in\mathbb F_5^3.
\]
The parameter \(a\) is unique once the scalar \(C\) is fixed. Thus the smallest two anisotropic spheres show a genuine phase-rigidity transition: \(q=3\) has continuous non-character phase freedom, whereas \(q=5\) has only scalar multiples of restricted additive characters.

## Assumptions and scope

The result concerns the two explicit prime-field spheres above. It does not assert an all-\(q\) classification. It uses only the constant-modulus necessity from the recent sharp sphere theorem; the remaining phase classification is proved independently below.

The \(L^2\) and \(L^4\) normalizations may be taken to be those of the cited finite-field extension literature. Since the argument compares functions of the same constant modulus and uses only the convolution identity for the fourth moment, changing between normalized and unnormalized counting measures multiplies every ratio by the same fixed scalar and leaves the equality set unchanged.

## Proof

Let \(S\) be either surface and let
\[
A_f(s)=\sum_{x,y\in S\,:\,x+y=s} f(x)f(y).
\]
Finite-group Plancherel gives
\[
\|Ef\|_4^4=K\sum_s |A_f(s)|^2
\]
for a positive constant \(K\) depending only on the chosen normalization.

By the recent sharp theorem, every maximizer has constant modulus. Write such a function as \(f=c\,u\), where \(c>0\) and \(|u(x)|=1\). If \(N(s)\) is the number of ordered pairs \((x,y)\in S^2\) with \(x+y=s\), then
\[
|A_f(s)|\le c^2N(s).
\]
The constant phase attains equality simultaneously for every \(s\). A maximizer exists because the problem is finite dimensional; since every maximizer has constant modulus, the constant phase is therefore also a maximizer. Hence a constant-modulus function is extremal exactly when equality holds in every triangle inequality above. Equivalently,
\[
u(x)u(y)=u(z)u(w)
\]
whenever \(x+y=z+w\) with \(x,y,z,w\in S\).

For \(q=3\), direct inspection gives
\[
S_3=\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}.
\]
Among unordered pairs, the only sum fiber containing more than one pair is the zero fiber, consisting of
\[
\{e_1,-e_1\},\quad \{e_2,-e_2\},\quad \{e_3,-e_3\}.
\]
Thus the displayed common-opposite-product condition is necessary and sufficient. Six phases subject to two independent circle equations give a four-dimensional torus.

For \(q=5\), order the twenty points of \(S_5\) lexicographically and let \(L\subset\mathbb Z^{S_5}\) be the lattice generated by all additive-relation vectors
\[
e_x+e_y-e_z-e_w
\]
with \(x+y=z+w\). Put
\[
H=\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^{S_5}:\sum_x v_x=0\right\}
\]
and define the moment map
\[
\Theta:H\longrightarrow\mathbb F_5^3,\qquad
\Theta(v)=\sum_x v_xx.
\]
Every additive-relation vector lies in \(\ker\Theta\), so \(L\subseteq\ker\Theta\).

The attached exact certificate exhibits nineteen genuine additive-relation vectors whose determinant, in the basis \(e_i-e_{20}\) of \(H\), has absolute value
\[
125=5^3.
\]
Thus \([H:L]\) divides \(125\). On the other hand, the three differences between the lexicographic base point and points numbered \(2,3,5\) have determinant \(1\) modulo \(5\), so \(\Theta\) is onto. Therefore
\[
[H:\ker\Theta]=125.
\]
Since \(L\subseteq\ker\Theta\), the two index bounds force
\[
L=\ker\Theta.
\]

It follows that
\[
\mathbb Z^{S_5}/L\cong \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb F_5^3.
\]
A unimodular phase satisfying every additive relation is exactly a circle-valued character of this quotient. Consequently it has the unique form
\[
u(x)=\zeta\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{5}\,a\cdot x\right),
\qquad \zeta\in\mathbb T,\quad a\in\mathbb F_5^3.
\]
Absorbing \(\zeta\) and the common modulus into one nonzero complex scalar \(C\) gives the stated classification.

## Verification

The standalone checker `verify_phase_dichotomy.py` uses only the Python standard library. It independently enumerates both spheres and their pair-sum fibers. For \(q=3\) it verifies that the sole nontrivial unordered pair-sum collision is the three-pair zero fiber. For \(q=5\) it verifies all nineteen displayed certificate relations, computes the exact integer determinant \(125\) by fraction-free Bareiss elimination, and verifies an explicit determinant \(1\) modulo \(5\) for three point differences. Its recorded output ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite certificate proves the group-theoretic phase classification; it is not being used as a numerical proxy for an infinite assertion.

## Relationship to prior work

Ronda, arXiv:2609.13023v1, proves the sharp \(L^2\to L^4\) extension inequality for the relevant anisotropic spheres and states that maximizers must have constant modulus. The present result starts precisely at that remaining phase freedom and closes it for the two smallest prime-field examples.

González-Riquelme and Oliveira e Silva, arXiv:2405.16647, initiated sharp finite-field extension theory and fully characterized maximizers for selected paraboloid and hyperbolic-paraboloid regimes. González-Riquelme and Ismoilov, arXiv:2509.05600v2 and DOI 10.1016/j.jfa.2026.111478, classify extremizers for finite-field cones. Those results concern different quadratic surfaces and do not imply the sphere-specific relation lattice computed here.

Targeted searches for the explicit \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\) sphere phase relations, additive-character rigidity on \(S_5\), and Freiman-phase classifications found no statement covering this small-field dichotomy.

## Limitations

The claim is deliberately restricted to \(q=3\) and \(q=5\). The determinant argument does not by itself predict the relation lattice for larger fields.

The recent Ronda source was available through indexed title, date, abstract, and metadata, but its full PDF was not retrievable through the available access route during this check. The source is used only for the stated theorem-level input that maximizers on these anisotropic spheres have constant modulus; all phase-rigidity assertions are proved and exactly certified here. Because a full-text comparison with that very recent preprint was unavailable, there remains a residual literature risk that an equivalent small-field phase classification appears inside the paper under different notation.

No independent external audit has been performed.
