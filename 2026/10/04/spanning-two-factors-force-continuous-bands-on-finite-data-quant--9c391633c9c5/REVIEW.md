# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The pullback of a spanning two-factor is a covering-invariant, vertex-covering line ensemble because every lifted vertex has selected degree \(2\) and the universal cover is acyclic. The proof of Proposition 4.1 in the primary source was checked at the point where the finite Hamiltonian cycle enters; all subsequent steps use only the full invariant line ensemble. The source's independent inequality \(a_0<\mathcal E_D\) then excludes the spectral bottom from the eigenvalue set, and the band-plus-discrete decomposition forces a nonempty continuous band.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source states the arbitrary-data result for a finite Hamiltonian base, not for a base with an arbitrary spanning two-factor. Bordenave--Sen--Virag provide the more general line-ensemble mechanism for a discrete adjacency operator, not the metric Schrödinger conclusion. Exact-source and semantic searches found no statement implying the two-factor weakening. The main residual risk is an unindexed equivalent observation, since the covering lemma is short.

## Value
PASS. The criterion is a natural graph-structural weakening of a hypothesis used specifically to ensure nonempty continuous spectrum. It yields arbitrary admissible edge-dependent data on the Petersen graph and other non-Hamiltonian two-factor bases, a setting not reached by merely representing the same homogeneous regular tree as the cover of another Hamiltonian graph.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary literature is Anantharaman--Ingremeau--Sabri--Winn, arXiv:2003.12765v1, especially Remark 1.3, Definition/Remark 4.1, Proposition 4.1 and Section 4.3. The closest methodological predecessor is Bordenave--Sen--Virag, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/750, Theorem 1.5. A spanning two-factor is only sufficient; no classification of all bases admitting full invariant line ensembles is claimed, and isolated Dirichlet eigenvalues remain possible.
