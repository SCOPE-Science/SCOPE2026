# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source formula was checked in the full text of Kleiman’s Chapter IV.D and in Tevelev’s §7.1. For a type-\((d,e)\) complete intersection, the conormal twist splits as \(\mathcal O_X(1-d)\oplus\mathcal O_X(1-e)\); extracting the \(H^3\)-coefficient and using \(\int_XH^3=de\) gives the stated polynomial exactly. The fixed-\(q\) reduction is an identity, and completing the square converts the global optimization to a one-dimensional nearest-square problem on the correct parity lattice. The tie equation and asymptotic follow without computational assumptions. The packaged exact checker reproduces the algebra over one million admissible pairs as regression evidence.

## Originality
PASS, with a bounded literature-search residual risk. Kleiman and Tevelev give the dual-degree machinery and complete-intersection hypersurface property, but the inspected passages do not compare bidegrees at fixed degree sum. Semantic searches for complete-intersection dual degree/discriminant degree, fixed degree sum, imbalance, extremizers, and the exact tie law returned no statement implying the theorem. The closest indexed result concerns the tangential variety of one intersection of four quadrics, a different variety and invariant.

## Value
PASS. The projective dual degree is the degree of the discriminant of tangent hyperplanes and is a standard intrinsic-to-the-embedding projective invariant. Fixing \(d+e\) fixes the adjunction coefficient \(K_X\cong\mathcal O_X(d+e-6)\), so comparing the dual complexity across bidegrees is a natural family-level question. The exact square-root imbalance, the complete maximizer classification, and the infinite exceptional tie sequence are structural rather than a numerical table or arbitrary slice.

The closest literature establishes the general dual-degree formula; it does not, in the inspected material, contain the fixed-sum extremal classification. The result is limited to smooth complex codimension-two threefolds, and the novelty conclusion remains subject to literature under unsearched terminology.
