# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(n=2pq\), the exact omitted-divisor sum is \(D=-pq+3p+3q+3\). Positivity immediately excludes \(p\ge7\). The \(p=3\) branch has \(D=12\): all \(q\ge13\) fail uniformly because the only divisors below \(12\) are \(1,2,3,6\), while \(q=5,7,11\) are checked exactly. The \(p=5\) branch forces \(q=7\) and fails. The two surviving identities for \(30\) and \(66\) are direct.

## Originality
PASS. The 2023 primary source and the 2025 follow-up both prove classifications with only two distinct prime factors. The later companion repository contains three-prime computational data and records \(30\) and \(66\), but presents this material as search output rather than a proof of completeness for squarefree \(2pq\). OEIS A341475 likewise lists both examples without the classification. Exact, structural, database, and semantic searches found no covering theorem. Residual risk remains from unindexed or unpublished work.

## Value
PASS. This is the first natural squarefree three-prime-support slice beyond the published two-prime classifications. The theorem converts computational examples into a complete, unbounded structural classification and gives explicit omitted-divisor witnesses.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Aryan–Madhavani–Parikh–Slattery–Zelinsky, “On \(2\)-Near Perfect Numbers,” and the later Fearon–Foushee–Porosoff–Skula–Zelinsky–Zhang two-prime-factor classification. The theorem here does not classify nonsquarefree three-prime-support cases.
