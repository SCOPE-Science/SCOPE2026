# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Stationarity of arbitrary one-variable antiderivatives in \(y\) and \(z\) gives the two conditional expectations. Nonnegativity of \(\mathbb E[x^2\mid y]\) yields the support ceiling. Stationarity of \(z^2/2\) gives \(\mathbb E[xz]=-c\mathbb E[z^2]\), while stationarity of \((x^2+z^2)/2\) gives \(c\mathbb E[z^2]=\mathbb E[x^2(y-a)]\). Eliminating \(z^2\) produces \(\mathbb E[\dot z^2]=c\mathbb E[x^2(a+1/c-y)]\), and conditioning on \(y\) gives the factorized defect. Zero defect forces \(\dot z=0\) on invariant support and then exactly the equilibrium set. Weighting by \(x^2\) yields the stated mean interval, with the lower inequality strict because noncentral invariant support has positive \(z^2\). If \(a+1/c<1/b\) and a non-equilibrium measure never went below \(a+1/c\), the factorized defect would be nonpositive, contradicting its strict positivity.

PASS

Szuminski's full primary article was inspected for the same three-dimensional finance system. Targeted Resultary searches found no same-model stationary formula. Full text of Gao--Ma 2009 was not securely available, so it is retained as a residual risk rather than used as negative evidence.

PASS

The finance system is a widely studied nonlinear model, but its literature is dominated by bifurcation, chaos and integrability. The audited theorem gives exact support and mean-square restrictions on every compact recurrent statistical state, identifies precisely when adjustment energy vanishes, and forces non-equilibrium recurrence below a natural equilibrium investment level. These are substantive constraints on the model's recurrent economics rather than a transient normalization check.
