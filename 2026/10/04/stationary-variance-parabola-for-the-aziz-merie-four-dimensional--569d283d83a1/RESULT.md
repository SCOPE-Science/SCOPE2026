# Stationary variance parabola for the Aziz–Merie four-dimensional flow
## Finding
Consider the smooth flow
\[
\dot x=\rho(y-x),\qquad
\dot y=ax-\delta xz+w,\qquad
\dot z=\phi xy-z,\qquad
\dot w=-kx.
\]
Assume \(\rho,\delta,\phi,k>0\) and define \(B=(a+k/\rho)/\delta>0\). For every compactly supported invariant Borel probability measure \(\mu\), write \(\bar z=\int z\,d\mu\). Then
\[
\bar z=\phi\int x^2\,d\mu,\qquad
\int z^2\,d\mu=B\bar z,
\]
and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(z)=\bar z(B-\bar z).
\]
The endpoint \(\bar z=0\) occurs exactly for the Dirac measure at the origin. The other endpoint \(\bar z=B\) is impossible for a compact invariant probability measure. Hence every nontrivial compact stationary state satisfies
\[
0<\bar z<B,\qquad 0<\int x^2\,d\mu<\frac{B}{\phi}.
\]
At the parameters used in the introducing paper, \((\rho,a,\delta,\phi,k)=(10,296.5,40,10,8)\), one has \(B=2973/400\), so every nontrivial compact stationary state obeys
\[
0<\int x^2\,d\mu<\frac{2973}{4000}=0.74325.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns compactly supported invariant Borel probability measures of the autonomous flow generated by the displayed polynomial vector field. Compact support is used to justify the stationary generator identities for the polynomial observables below and to make every orbit in the support bounded and complete. The parameter assumptions are exactly \(\rho,\delta,\phi,k>0\) and \(B>0\). No ergodicity assumption is needed.

## Proof
For a polynomial observable \(g\), invariance and compact support give \(\int Lg\,d\mu=0\), where \(L\) is the Lie derivative along the vector field. Successively applying this identity gives
\[
0=\int L(x^2/2)\,d\mu
 =\rho\int x(y-x)\,d\mu,
\]
so \(\int xy\,d\mu=\int x^2\,d\mu\). Applying stationarity to \(z\) yields
\[
\bar z=\phi\int xy\,d\mu=\phi\int x^2\,d\mu.
\]
Next,
\[
0=\int L(w^2/2)\,d\mu=-k\int xw\,d\mu,
\]
so \(\int xw\,d\mu=0\). Stationarity of \(xw\) gives
\[
0=\rho\int yw\,d\mu-\rho\int xw\,d\mu-k\int x^2\,d\mu,
\]
hence \(\int yw\,d\mu=(k/\rho)\int x^2\,d\mu\). Stationarity of \(y^2/2\) then gives
\[
\int xyz\,d\mu=\frac{a+k/\rho}{\delta}\int x^2\,d\mu
=B\int x^2\,d\mu.
\]
Finally, stationarity of \(z^2/2\) gives
\[
\int z^2\,d\mu=\phi\int xyz\,d\mu
=B\phi\int x^2\,d\mu=B\bar z.
\]
Subtracting \(\bar z^2\) gives the variance identity and therefore \(0\le\bar z\le B\).

If \(\bar z=0\), then \(\int x^2\,d\mu=0\), so the support lies in \(x=0\). Invariance forces \(y=0\) and then \(w=0\) on the support. The remaining equation is \(\dot z=-z\); a compact complete orbit has \(z=0\). Thus \(\mu\) is the origin measure.

If \(\bar z=B\), the variance is zero, so the support lies in \(z=B\). Invariance then forces \(xy=B/\phi>0\) on the support. Along any orbit in that compact support, \(x\) never vanishes and has fixed sign. Compactness bounds \(|y|\) above, so \(|x|\) is bounded below by a positive constant. Since \(\dot w=-kx\), \(|w|\) must grow at least linearly in one time direction, contradicting compactness. Hence the upper endpoint cannot occur.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` symbolically recomputes all six Lie derivatives used above, checks the algebraic elimination leading to \(\int z^2\,d\mu=B\bar z\), and evaluates the source-parameter constants exactly. Running it with Python and SymPy returns `VERIFY_OK`. The endpoint arguments are deductive compactness arguments and do not rely on numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Aziz and Merie introduce this four-dimensional vector field and analyze its equilibrium, local stability, divergence, numerical Lyapunov exponents, control, and synchronization. Their later circuit paper reuses the same system and parameters for an electronic realization. The stationary moment chain and the exact variance parabola above are not among those analyses. Closest retrieved comparison results for other classical flows derive stationary moment or variance identities by analogous generator methods, but those theorems concern different vector fields and do not imply the coefficients or endpoint rigidity here.

## Limitations
The result is a necessary structural constraint on compact stationary states. It does not prove existence, uniqueness, ergodicity, mixing, or chaos of a nontrivial invariant measure, nor does it identify the full attractor. It does not extend automatically to unbounded invariant measures without additional integrability assumptions. Search coverage cannot exclude every poorly indexed later derivation of an equivalent identity.
