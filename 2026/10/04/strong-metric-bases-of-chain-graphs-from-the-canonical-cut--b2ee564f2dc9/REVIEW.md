# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The MMD classification is proved case-by-case from the canonical nested neighborhoods. The strong resolving graph is therefore explicit. Its maximum independent sets are then classified by the largest chosen \(B\)-index and smallest chosen \(A\)-index, forcing one unique cut at equality. The standard vertex-cover correspondence yields \(\operatorname{sdim}(G)=N-p-1\) and the exact basis count. The exceptional graph \(K_2\) is separated. A literal verifier checks all canonical profiles through order \(9\).

## Originality
PASS. Dedicated searches covered strong dimension/strong metric dimension, strong resolving graphs, chain/Ferrers/difference graphs, half graphs, and bipartite permutation terminology. The closest same-object full paper treats ordinary metric dimension. The broad distance-hereditary result gives an efficient algorithm, and the foundational/survey results give the general vertex-cover reduction, but none of the inspected statements provides the chain-specific MMD structure, closed formula, or enumeration of all strong metric bases. The complete-bipartite scalar specialization is prior-covered and is not claimed as new.

## Value
PASS. The theorem turns a generally NP-hard shortest-path resolving invariant into a transparent canonical-cut structure on a natural graph class. It gives both the exact invariant and all minimum bases, revealing that the scalar value depends only on \(N\) and \(p\) while the number of bases retains the twin-class sizes. This is a substantive structural classification rather than a routine substitution into a known formula.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest broad sources are Oellermann–Peters-Fransen on the strong resolving graph/vertex-cover reduction and May–Oellermann on distance-hereditary graphs. The closest same-object source is Bhat–Hanif–Sudhakara on ordinary metric dimension of chain graphs. The full 2014 survey was searched for chain/Ferrers terminology and did not contain it. Residual risk remains from older material indexed only under Ferrers/difference-graph language and from the inaccessible foundational full text; these risks do not supply a contrary implication in the sources actually inspected.
