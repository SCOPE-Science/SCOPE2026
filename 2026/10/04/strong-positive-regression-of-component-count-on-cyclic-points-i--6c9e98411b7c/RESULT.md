# Strong positive regression of component count on cyclic points in a uniform random mapping

## Finding

Let \(F_n\) be uniformly distributed over the \(n^n\) mappings from \([n]\) to itself, with \(n\ge2\). Its functional digraph is a disjoint union of connected components, each containing one directed cycle.

Let
\[
L_n=\#\{\text{cyclic vertices}\},
\qquad
C_n=\#\{\text{connected components}\}.
\]
For
\[
1\le m\le n,
\]
put
\[
q_{n,m}=\frac{(n)_m}{n^m},
\qquad
(n)_m=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\Pr(L_n\ge m)=q_{n,m}.}
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Pr(L_n=m)=q_{n,m}-q_{n,m+1}
=\frac{m(n)_m}{n^{m+1}},
\tag{2}
\]
where \(q_{n,n+1}=0\).

The joint law of cyclic points and components has the exact mixture representation
\[
\boxed{
(L_n,C_n)
\overset d=
\left(L_n,\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}B_j\right),
}
\tag{3}
\]
where \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\) are independent of \(L_n\) and mutually independent, with
\[
B_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/j).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
C_n\mid(L_n=m)
\overset d=
\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_j,
}
\tag{4}
\]
the classical cycle-count law of a uniform permutation of \(m\) objects.

Equation (3) yields a strict stochastic-regression law:
\[
\boxed{
C_n\mid(L_n=m+1)
>_{\mathrm{st}}
C_n\mid(L_n=m),
\qquad 1\le m<n.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, for every pair of nondecreasing real functions \(f,h\) on the respective supports,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(f(L_n),h(C_n))\ge0.
}
\tag{6}
\]
If both functions are strictly increasing, the inequality is strict.

Write
\[
H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1j,
\qquad H_0=0.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E[C_n\mid L_n]=H_{L_n}.
\tag{7}
\]
The exact finite-\(n\) covariance is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,C_n)
=
\sum_{m=1}^n q_{n,m}(1+H_{m-1})
-
\left(\sum_{m=1}^nq_{n,m}\right)
\left(\sum_{m=1}^n\frac{q_{n,m}}m\right)
>0.
}
\tag{8}
\]

As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,C_n)}{\sqrt n}
\longrightarrow
\sqrt{\frac\pi2}(1-\log2).
}
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(L_n,C_n)
\sim
(1-\log2)
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{4-\pi}}
\frac1{\sqrt{\log n}}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the covariance grows on the natural \(\sqrt n\) cyclic-point scale, but correlation still vanishes because the component count has logarithmically growing variance.

## Assumptions and scope

The mapping is uniform over all functions \([n]\to[n]\). The variables \(L_n\) and \(C_n\) count cyclic vertices and weakly connected functional-digraph components respectively.

The representation (3) is an equality in joint distribution. It does not assert that the actual component cycles of one realized mapping are generated by an auxiliary Bernoulli sequence independent of its cyclic-vertex set.

The positive-regression statement concerns increasing functions of these two scalar statistics. No claim is made that all detailed component statistics are positively associated with the number of cyclic vertices.

## Proof

Fix \(m\). Choose the set of cyclic vertices in
\[
\binom nm
\]
ways. On those vertices choose a permutation in
\[
m!
\]
ways. Finally attach the remaining vertices as a rooted forest whose prescribed roots are exactly the cyclic vertices. The classical rooted-forest count is
\[
m n^{n-m-1}.
\]
Hence the number of mappings with exactly \(m\) cyclic vertices is
\[
\binom nm m!\,m n^{n-m-1}
=(n)_m m n^{n-m-1}.
\]
Dividing by \(n^n\) proves (2).

Now define
\[
q_{n,m}=\frac{(n)_m}{n^m}.
\]
Since
\[
q_{n,m}-q_{n,m+1}
=q_{n,m}\frac mn
=\frac{m(n)_m}{n^{m+1}},
\]
(1) follows by telescoping.

Conditional on \(L_n=m\), every permutation of the cyclic set occurs equally often: for each cyclic permutation the number of compatible rooted forests is the same. Therefore \(C_n\mid L_n=m\) is the number of cycles in a uniform permutation of \(m\) elements.

The standard insertion construction of uniform permutations gives
\[
K_m\overset d=\sum_{j=1}^m B_j,
\qquad
B_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/j)
\]
independently. Taking this Bernoulli sequence independent of \(L_n\) proves the joint representation (3).

Under the same coupling,
\[
K_{m+1}=K_m+B_{m+1},
\]
where \(B_{m+1}\) has positive success probability. This proves (5). If \(h\) is nondecreasing, then
\[
g_h(m)=\mathbb E[h(C_n)\mid L_n=m]
\]
is nondecreasing in \(m\), and is strictly increasing when \(h\) is strictly increasing. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(f(L_n),h(C_n))
=
\operatorname{Cov}(f(L_n),g_h(L_n)).
\]
For an independent copy \(L_n'\),
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(f(L_n),g_h(L_n))
=
\mathbb E[(f(L_n)-f(L_n'))(g_h(L_n)-g_h(L_n'))]\ge0,
\]
proving (6) and its strict version.

Since the cycle count of a uniform permutation has mean \(H_m\), (7) follows. For any positive integer-valued random variable \(L\),
\[
\mathbb EL=\sum_{m\ge1}\Pr(L\ge m).
\]
Also the increment of \(mH_m\) is
\[
mH_m-(m-1)H_{m-1}=1+H_{m-1}.
\]
Using (1),
\[
\mathbb EL_n=\sum_{m=1}^nq_{n,m},
\]
\[
\mathbb EH_{L_n}=\sum_{m=1}^n\frac{q_{n,m}}m,
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E[L_nH_{L_n}]
=
\sum_{m=1}^nq_{n,m}(1+H_{m-1}).
\]
This proves (8).

For the asymptotics, if \(m=x\sqrt n+o(\sqrt n)\), then
\[
\log q_{n,m}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\log\left(1-\frac jn\right)
\longrightarrow-\frac{x^2}{2}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
q_{n,m}\le
\exp\left(-\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}\right),
\]
which gives moment convergence. Hence
\[
\frac{L_n}{\sqrt n}\Longrightarrow R,
\qquad
\Pr(R\ge x)=e^{-x^2/2},
\]
with convergence of the first two moments and of the logarithmic moments needed below.

The harmonic estimate
\[
H_m=\log m+\gamma+O(m^{-1})
\]
and the birthday tail bound imply
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,H_{L_n})
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cov}(R,\log R).
\]
For Rayleigh \(R\),
\[
\mathbb ER=\sqrt{\frac\pi2},
\qquad
\mathbb E\log R=\frac{\log2-\gamma}{2},
\]
and differentiating
\[
\mathbb ER^s=2^{s/2}\Gamma(1+s/2)
\]
at \(s=1\) gives
\[
\mathbb E[R\log R]
=
\sqrt{\frac\pi2}
\left(1-\frac{\gamma+\log2}{2}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R,\log R)
=
\sqrt{\frac\pi2}(1-\log2),
\]
proving (9).

Finally,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(L_n)
\sim
\frac{4-\pi}{2}n.
\]
Conditionally on \(L_n=m\), the Bernoulli-sum representation gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C_n\mid L_n=m)
=H_m-H_m^{(2)},
\]
where \(H_m^{(2)}=\sum_{j\le m}j^{-2}\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C_n)
=
\mathbb E[H_{L_n}-H_{L_n}^{(2)}]
+
\operatorname{Var}(H_{L_n})
=
\frac12\log n+O(1).
\]
Combining this with (9) proves (10).

## Verification

The accompanying exact checker exhaustively enumerates every mapping through \(n=7\). It reconstructs cyclic vertices and connected components directly from each function, verifies the cyclic-point mass function, and verifies the complete conditional component-count distribution against the cycle-count distribution of a uniform permutation.

It separately checks the tail formulas, exact mean/covariance identities, strict stochastic ordering of the conditional component laws, and the variance decomposition using rational arithmetic.

The checker also evaluates the normalized covariance and correlation on large \(n\) grids as a numerical sanity check for the analytic asymptotics. These numerical values are not used as proof of the limits.

## Relationship to prior work

Hansen and Jaworski's 2006 preprint, later published in *Random Structures & Algorithms*, develops a broad random-mapping model and explicitly treats the distributions of cyclic points and components. The arXiv record lists primary MSC \(60C05\). Their work supplies the classical random-mapping context and confirms that both statistics are central objects.

A later Hansen--Jaworski paper gives a construction in which one first chooses the number of cyclic vertices, then a rooted forest, then a permutation of the cyclic vertices. Its full text explicitly notes that conditional on \(m\) cyclic vertices, the number of components has the same distribution as the number of cycles in a random permutation of \(m\) elements. That conditional fact is therefore treated as prior art here.

Kupka gives formulas for the marginal distribution and moments of the number of components of a random function. The inspected abstract and bibliographic material concern the component count marginal, not its regression on the number of cyclic vertices.

The contribution claimed here is the consequence package not located in those sources: an exact independent-Bernoulli joint representation, strict stochastic regression, positive covariance for every pair of increasing transforms, and the explicit \(\sqrt n\)-scale covariance and \(1/\sqrt{\log n}\) correlation constants.

## Limitations

The model is the uniform random mapping. For nonuniform or constrained mapping models, the conditional permutation law on cyclic vertices can change and the regression theorem need not persist in this form.

The conditional cycle-count law itself is classical and is not claimed as new. The originality claim concerns the regression consequences and asymptotic covariance/correlation constants derived from combining that law with the exact cyclic-point distribution.

Older random-mapping literature, especially work using bivariate generating functions for cyclic elements and components, may encode an equivalent mixed-moment asymptotic under different notation. This is the principal residual originality risk.
