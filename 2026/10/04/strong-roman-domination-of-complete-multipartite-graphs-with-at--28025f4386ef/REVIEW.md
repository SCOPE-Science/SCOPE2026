# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the defining defender threshold. In a zero-free defender part, the positive-vertex baseline and the defender's required excess give \(N-\lfloor z/2\rfloor\), minimized at \(z=N-m\). If every defender part contains a zero, two distinct defender parts are forced; because \(r\ge3\), a third part of size at least \(m\) remains, yielding the stronger lower bound \((N+m+2)/2\). The smallest-part construction attains \(\lceil(N+m)/2\rceil\). Exhaustive bounded replay through order \(10\) agrees with the theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational paper supplies the invariant and the general maximum-degree upper bound, but its inspected full text does not state a complete-multipartite exact theorem. Searches for the formula, its maximum-degree-equality formulation, complete-multipartite aliases, and nearby strong-Roman variants found no statement implying the \(r\ge3\) result. The closest indexed result concerns total strong Roman domination, whose extra positive-support condition makes it a different parameter. A later \(k\)-strong Roman paper treats complete bipartite graphs under a different generalization and does not cover this theorem.

## Value
PASS. Exactness of a general domination bound on an arbitrary-size complete multipartite class is a natural structural result rather than a one-off numerical computation. The formula depends only on the order and smallest part, gives an explicit optimal defense, and isolates a genuine structural boundary between two and at least three parts.

The principal residual risk is an older or poorly indexed exact formula under alternate strong-Roman terminology that was not surfaced by the inspected literature searches. The theorem's proof and verification are independent of that bibliographic risk.
