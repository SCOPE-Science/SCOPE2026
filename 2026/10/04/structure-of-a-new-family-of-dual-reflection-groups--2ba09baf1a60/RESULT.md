# Structure of a new family of dual reflection groups
## Finding
For every positive integer \(n\), let \(\Delta_n=\langle a,b,c\mid ab=c^2,\ bc=a^2,\ ca=b^2,\ (acb)^n=e\rangle\), the dual reflection group introduced for the Sklyanin algebra \(S_{q,0,1}\). Then \(Z(\Delta_n)\cong C_{3n}\), \([\Delta_n,\Delta_n]\cong C_{3n}\times C_n\), and \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_{3n}\times C_3\). Moreover \(\Delta_n\) has exponent \(9n\), is metabelian, and is nilpotent if and only if \(n\) is a power of \(3\) (including \(n=1\)).

These invariants give a compact intrinsic profile of the new family: the center has order \(3n\), the derived subgroup has order \(3n^2\), and the abelianization has order \(9n\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix a positive integer \(n\), put \(m=3n\), and write \(C_3=\langle\sigma\rangle\), where \(\sigma\) cyclically permutes the three coordinates of \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^3\). Buzaglo and Rogalski prove that the group
\[
\Delta_n=\langle a,b,c\mid ab=c^2,\ bc=a^2,\ ca=b^2,\ (acb)^n=e\rangle
\]
has order \(27n^3\). Their Lemma 6.4 embeds the parent group into \(\mathbb Z^3\rtimes C_3\), sends \(a,b,c\) to \((e_1,\sigma),(e_2,\sigma),(e_3,\sigma)\), and identifies the subgroup generated by \(acb,bac,cba\) with \(3\mathbb Z^3\). Quotienting by the subgroup generated by their \(n\)-th powers therefore identifies \(\Delta_n\) with
\[
D_n=\{(v,\sigma^i)\in (\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^3\rtimes C_3:\ s(v)\equiv i\pmod 3\},
\]
where \(s(v)\) is the sum of the three coordinates.

## Proof
Let \(T=\{(v,1)\in D_n:s(v)\equiv0\pmod3\}\). Then \(T\) is abelian and \(D_n/T\cong C_3\).

For the center, suppose \((v,\sigma^i)\) is central. If \(i\ne0\), it fails to commute with the translation \((e_1-e_2,1)\in T\), because a nontrivial cyclic permutation does not fix \(e_1-e_2\) modulo \(m\ge3\). Hence \(i=0\). A translation \((v,1)\) commutes with \((e_1,\sigma)\) exactly when \(\sigma(v)=v\), so \(v=(t,t,t)\). Every such diagonal translation lies in \(D_n\), and therefore
\[
Z(\Delta_n)=\{((t,t,t),1):t\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}\cong C_m=C_{3n}.
\]

Using the semidirect-product multiplication, the pairwise commutators of the three standard generators are translations generated by
\[
u=(1,-2,1),\qquad v=(1,1,-2),
\]
and their cyclic translate. Let \(L=\langle u,v\rangle\le(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^3\). Modulo \(L\), the generators commute, so \([\Delta_n,\Delta_n]=L\). In the sum-zero plane with basis \((1,0,-1),(0,1,-1)\), the two generators have coordinate matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-2&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose Smith normal form is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,3)\). Since \(m=3n\), this gives
\[
[\Delta_n,\Delta_n]\cong C_m\times C_{m/3}=C_{3n}\times C_n.
\]
In particular the group is metabelian. Since \(|\Delta_n|=27n^3\), its abelianization has order \(9n\). Directly abelianizing the defining presentation gives relation matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&-2\\
-2&1&1\\
1&-2&1\\
n&n&n
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with Smith normal form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,3,3n)\), so
\[
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_3\times C_{3n}.
\]

For the exponent, every translation has order dividing \(m\). If \(g=(v,\sigma^i)\) has \(i\ne0\), then
\[
g^3=(s(v),s(v),s(v),1),
\]
so \(g^{3m}=e\). Thus the exponent divides \(3m=9n\). The generator \(a=(e_1,\sigma)\) satisfies \(a^3=((1,1,1),1)\), whose order is exactly \(m\), so \(a\) has order \(3m=9n\). Hence the exponent is exactly \(9n\).

Finally, if \(n\) is a power of \(3\), then \(|\Delta_n|=27n^3\) is a power of \(3\), so \(\Delta_n\) is a finite \(3\)-group and is nilpotent. Conversely, suppose a prime \(p\ne3\) divides \(n\). The \(p\)-primary subgroup \(P\) of the translation group is a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup and is isomorphic to a product of three nontrivial cyclic \(p\)-groups. Any Sylow \(3\)-subgroup maps onto \(D_n/T\cong C_3\), so it contains an element whose conjugation on \(P\) is the nontrivial cyclic permutation of coordinates. Thus the Sylow \(3\)-subgroup does not centralize \(P\), impossible in a finite nilpotent group. Therefore \(\Delta_n\) is nilpotent exactly when \(n\) is a power of \(3\).

## Verification
The proof is symbolic for arbitrary \(n\). The only finite-abelian-group calculations used are the two displayed Smith normal forms. The first follows from determinant \(3\) of the displayed \(2\times2\) matrix; the second has invariant factors \(1,3,3n\), which can also be checked from the gcds of its minors. No finite enumeration is used to infer the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Buzaglo and Rogalski introduce \(\Delta_n\) as a new infinite family of dual reflection groups. Their Lemma 6.4 proves the wreath-product embedding, identifies the rank-three abelian subgroup generated by \(acb,bac,cba\), and proves \(|\Delta_n|=27n^3\). The inspected paper does not state the center, derived subgroup, abelianization, exponent, metabelian property, or nilpotency criterion above. Targeted searches for the exact presentation, the order \(27n^3\), and these invariants returned the source paper but no publication containing this structural profile.

## Limitations
The result concerns only the groups \(\Delta_n\), not the other dual reflection group families in the source paper. The originality check cannot exclude an isomorphic presentation under an unrelated name in literature not surfaced by the targeted searches; this is the main residual risk. The nilpotency statement does not determine the nilpotency class when \(n\) is a power of \(3\).
