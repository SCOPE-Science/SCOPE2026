# Subnormality of commuting-normal degree-one Laurent Toeplitz operators
## Finding
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a separable complex Hilbert space and let \(A,B\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) be commuting normal operators. Define
\[
\Phi(\zeta)=\zeta A+\overline{\zeta}B,\qquad \zeta\in\mathbb T,
\]
and let \(T_\Phi\) act on \(H^2\otimes\mathcal H\). Put \(D=A^*A-B^*B\). Then
\[
T_\Phi	ext{ is hyponormal}\iff D\ge0,
\]
\[
T_\Phi	ext{ is normal}\iff D=0,
\]
and
\[
T_\Phi	ext{ is subnormal}\iff D\ge0	ext{ and }DB=0.
\]
In the subnormal case, \(\mathcal H_0=\ker D\) and \(\mathcal H_1=\mathcal H_0^\perp\) reduce both coefficients, \(B|_{\mathcal H_1}=0\), and
\[
T_\Phi\cong T_{\Phi|_{\mathcal H_0}}\oplusigl(S\otimes A|_{\mathcal H_1}igr).
\]
The first summand is normal and the second is analytic and subnormal. Hence both nonzero sectors may coexist, yielding a subnormal operator that is neither globally normal nor globally analytic.

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient space is any separable complex Hilbert space, finite- or infinite-dimensional. The hypotheses are exactly that \(A\) and \(B\) are bounded, normal, and commute. No invertibility, coprimeness, finite-rank, rational-matrix, or finite-dimensional coefficient hypothesis is imposed. The claim concerns only the degree-one Laurent symbol displayed above.

## Proof
Let \(S\) be the unilateral shift and let \(P_0=I-SS^*\) be the projection onto the constants. Under the canonical identification,
\[
T_\Phi=S\otimes A+S^*\otimes B.
\]
Because commuting normal operators doubly commute, the mixed products in the self-commutator cancel. Using \(S^*S=I\) and \(SS^*=I-P_0\) gives
\[
[T_\Phi^*,T_\Phi]=P_0\otimes D.
\]
This proves the hyponormal and normal assertions.

For the subnormal necessity, use the following elementary consequence of a normal extension. If \(T=N|_M\) is subnormal, where \(M\) is invariant for a normal operator \(N\) and \(P_M\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(M\), then for \(x\in M\),
\[
\langle[T^*,T]x,xangle=\|(I-P_M)N^*x\|^2.
\]
Consequently \(\ker[T^*,T]=\{x\in M:N^*x\in M\}\), and this kernel is \(T\)-invariant because normality gives \(N^*Nx=NN^*x\).

Assume now that \(T_\Phi\) is subnormal. Then \(D\ge0\). Since
\[
\ker(P_0\otimes D)=igl(zH^2\otimes\mathcal Higr)\oplusigl(1\otimes\ker Digr),
\]
every \(z\otimes h\) lies in the self-commutator kernel. Its invariance under \(T_\Phi\) gives
\[
T_\Phi(z\otimes h)=z^2\otimes Ah+1\otimes Bh\in\ker(P_0\otimes D).
\]
Thus \(Bh\in\ker D\) for every \(h\), which is exactly \(DB=0\).

Conversely, suppose \(D\ge0\) and \(DB=0\). Since \(D\) belongs to the abelian operator algebra generated by \(A\) and \(B\), \(\mathcal H_0=\ker D\) and \(\mathcal H_1=\mathcal H_0^\perp\) reduce both coefficients. The condition \(DB=0\) sends the range of \(B\) into \(\mathcal H_0\); reduction then forces \(B|_{\mathcal H_1}=0\). On \(\mathcal H_0\), the self-commutator is zero, so the corresponding Toeplitz summand is normal. On \(\mathcal H_1\), the operator is \(S\otimes A|_{\mathcal H_1}\), which has the normal extension \(U\otimes A|_{\mathcal H_1}\) on \(L^2(\mathbb T)\otimes\mathcal H_1\), with \(U\) the bilateral shift. The direct sum is therefore subnormal.

## Verification
The proof was checked symbolically at the endpoints \(D=0\) and \(B=0\), for singular \(D\), and for both finite- and infinite-dimensional \(\mathcal H\). No finite experiment is used. The only external operator-theoretic inputs are Fuglede double commutation for commuting normal coefficients and the definition of subnormality via a normal extension; the self-commutator and kernel-invariance calculations are written explicitly above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bala and Pandey, arXiv:2609.30801v1, characterize hyponormal Toeplitz operators with operator-valued normal symbols and motivate the normal-versus-analytic question. The present degree-one result adds an exact subnormality obstruction and a reducing-sector description.

Curto, Hwang and Lee, arXiv:1201.5974v2, prove normal-or-analytic conclusions for matrix-valued bounded-type symbols under coprime hypotheses; their rational corollary requires an invertibility condition at zeros of the relevant scalar inner factor. Those hypotheses exclude precisely the singular coanalytic situation in which the two reducing sectors above can coexist. Abhinand, Curto, Hwang, Lee and Prasad, DOI:10.1002/mana.70235, treat analytic operator-valued symbols and give sufficient conditions for normal-or-analytic behavior in a matrix-valued nonanalytic setting. The inspected statements do not provide this degree-one commuting-normal classification.

## Limitations
The theorem does not treat noncommuting coefficients, coefficients that are not normal, or higher Laurent degree. Published-record searches can miss equivalent older formulations, so an older degree-one block-Toeplitz statement under different notation remains a residual originality risk. The scalar specialization is classical in spirit and is not claimed as the novel part; the structural contribution is the exact operator-valued obstruction and mixed reducing decomposition.
