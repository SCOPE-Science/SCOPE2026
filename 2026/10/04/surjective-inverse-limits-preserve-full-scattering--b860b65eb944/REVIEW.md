# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces an arbitrary nontrivial finite open cover of the inverse limit to a nontrivial finite open cover at one finite stage. The stage cover is built from closed sets with nonempty interior placed inside cylinder holes of the original cover. Surjectivity prevents those closed sets from having a common point, hence their complements cover the stage. Pullback by a surjective factor map preserves the least subcover cardinality exactly, and refinement gives the required lower bound. All quantifiers in the definition of full-scattering are retained.

The countable-product corollary is valid because countable products of compact metric spaces are compact metrizable, the product is the inverse limit of its finite coordinate products, the coordinate projections are surjective, and Xu's theorem supplies full-scattering at every finite stage.

## Originality
PASS. The closest directly relevant source is Hui Xu, arXiv:2609.27790v1. Its advertised second result is closure under every finite product, and the inspected full text treats finite products. Searches combining “full-scattering” with “inverse limit,” “countable product,” and “infinite product” did not locate a theorem implying the present inverse-limit permanence statement. The new theorem is not merely a restatement of finite-product closure: it handles arbitrary finite open covers of a limit space, which need not themselves be cylinder covers, by producing a common-stage nontrivial coarsening.

Residual risk remains that an older source using different terminology may contain an equivalent inverse-limit permanence lemma. No such implication was found in the checked sources.

## Value
PASS. The finding supplies a natural structural closure operation for a mixing/complexity property whose newly established product closure was only finite. It converts the 2026 finite-product theorem into a countable-product theorem and, more broadly, allows full-scattering systems to be assembled as surjective inverse limits of finite-stage models. The common-stage cover reduction is reusable in questions where a dynamical property is formulated by divergence of finite-open-cover complexity.

## Closest literature and limitations
Xu's 2026 paper is the decisive comparison because it is devoted specifically to products of full-scattering systems. Huang and Ye's 2004 paper supplies the open-cover complexity framework and background equivalences. The present proof requires surjective bonding maps and gives no rate of divergence beyond the finite-stage lower bound.
