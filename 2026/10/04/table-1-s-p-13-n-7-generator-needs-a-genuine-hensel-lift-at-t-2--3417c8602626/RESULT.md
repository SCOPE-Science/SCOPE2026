# Table 1's \(p=13,n=7\) generator needs a genuine Hensel lift at \(t=2\)
## Finding

Consider the \(p=13\), length-\(7\) row of Table 1 in Karbaski–Abualrub–Siap, advertised with parameters
\[
(7,13^{4t},6)
\]
over \(\mathbb Z_{13^t}[i]\). In the paper's coefficient-pair notation the displayed generator has real and imaginary components
\[
f_0(x)=12+6x+4x^2+9x^3+7x^4+x^5
\]
and
\[
g_0(x)=1+3x+8x^2+8x^3+3x^4+x^5.
\]

At \(t=1\), these are valid generators over \(\mathbb F_{13}\): \(f_0\mid x^7-1\), \(g_0\mid x^7+1\), and both components are LCD \([7,2,6]_{13}\) codes.

At \(t=2\), however, reusing the same integer coefficients in \(\mathbb Z_{169}\) fails. Polynomial division gives
\[
x^7-1\equiv 26+13x+39x^2+117x^4\pmod{f_0,169}
\]
and
\[
x^7+1\equiv 13x^3+13x^4\pmod{g_0,169}.
\]
Thus neither displayed component is a divisor of the required modulus polynomial in \(\mathbb Z_{169}[x]\).

More strongly, in
\[
\mathbb Z_{169}[x]/(x^7-1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb Z_{169}[x]/(x^7+1),
\]
the ideals generated by the displayed \(f_0\) and \(g_0\) have cardinalities \(13^9\) and \(13^8\), respectively. Their direct-sum additive conjucyclic code therefore has \(13^{17}\) words, not the advertised \(13^8\).

A genuine lift that restores the advertised \(t=2\) code is
\[
\widehat f(x)=168+6x+134x^2+35x^3+163x^4+x^5
\]
and
\[
\widehat g(x)=1+29x+164x^2+164x^3+29x^4+x^5.
\]
These satisfy
\[
x^7-1=\widehat f(x)(x^2+6x+1)\pmod{169}
\]
and
\[
x^7+1=\widehat g(x)(x^2+140x+1)\pmod{169},
\]
and reduce coefficientwise to \(f_0\) and \(g_0\) modulo \(13\).

Each corrected component is a free rank-two LCD code of length \(7\) and minimum Hamming distance \(6\) over \(\mathbb Z_{169}\). Hence
\[
\widehat C=\langle\widehat f\rangle\oplus i\langle\widehat g\rangle
\]
is an additive ACD MDS conjucyclic code with exactly \(13^8\) words and minimum distance \(6\).

## Assumptions and scope

The coefficient pairs in Table 1 are interpreted according to the paper's Notation 6: a sequence \((a_0,b_0),(a_1,b_1),\ldots\) represents the coefficients of \(f(x)+ig(x)\) from degree \(0\) upward. The specialization considered here is exactly \(p=13\), \(n=7\), and \(t=2\).

The cyclic component is the ideal generated by \(f\) in \(\mathbb Z_{169}[x]/(x^7-1)\); the negacyclic component is the ideal generated by \(g\) in \(\mathbb Z_{169}[x]/(x^7+1)\). The additive conjucyclic code is their real-imaginary direct sum.

The correction concerns the explicit generator string as a \(t=2\) generator over \(\mathbb Z_{169}[i]\). It does not assert that the paper's abstract existence theorem is false.

## Proof

For the printed cyclic polynomial \(f_0\), monic polynomial division in \(\mathbb Z_{169}[x]\) gives the nonzero remainder
\[
26+13x+39x^2+117x^4.
\]
For the printed negacyclic polynomial \(g_0\), division gives
\[
13x^3+13x^4.
\]
This already shows that the displayed residue-field polynomials are not themselves valid divisors after the lift to \(t=2\).

To determine the sizes of the generated quotient ideals without assuming freeness, form the \(7\times7\) multiplication matrices of \(f_0\) and \(g_0\) in the cyclic and negacyclic quotient rings. Over \(\mathbb Z_{13^2}\), elementary invertible row and column operations reduce such a matrix to unit invariant factors, \(13\)-divisible invariant factors, and zeros. If there are \(r\) unit factors and \(s\) factors associated to \(13\), the image has cardinality
\[
13^{2r+s}.
\]
For \(f_0\) the invariants have \((r,s)=(2,5)\), yielding \(13^9\). For \(g_0\) they have \((r,s)=(2,4)\), yielding \(13^8\). The direct sum therefore has \(13^{17}\) words.

For the corrected cyclic polynomial,
\[
\widehat f(x)(x^2+6x+1)=x^7-1\pmod{169},
\]
and for the corrected negacyclic polynomial,
\[
\widehat g(x)(x^2+140x+1)=x^7+1\pmod{169}.
\]
Both multiplication matrices have \((r,s)=(2,0)\), hence each ideal has \(13^4=169^2\) words and is generated freely by the two shifts corresponding to multiplication by \(1\) and \(x\).

Exhausting all \(169^2\) coefficient pairs in each corrected component gives the same weight distribution
\[
1+1176z^6+27384z^7.
\]
Thus both components have exact minimum distance \(6\).

For the natural two-row generator matrices, the Gram matrices modulo \(169\) are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}158&33\\33&158\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}44&131\\131&44\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their determinants are \(46\) and \(154\), both units modulo \(169\). Hence if a component codeword \(aG\) is orthogonal to the whole component, then
\[
aGG^{T}=0
\]
forces \(a=0\); both components are LCD.

The paper's decomposition of additive conjucyclic codes identifies the additive dual componentwise, so the direct sum of these two LCD components is ACD. Its size is
\[
13^4\cdot13^4=13^8.
\]
For \(r+is\), the Hamming support is the union of the supports of \(r\) and \(s\), so the direct-sum distance is the minimum of the two component distances, namely \(6\). Since the alphabet \(\mathbb Z_{169}[i]\) has \(13^4\) elements, \(13^8=(13^4)^2\), and
\[
6=7-2+1,
\]
so the corrected additive code is MDS.

## Relationship to prior work

Karbaski–Abualrub–Siap state that Table 1 gives generator polynomials of ACD MDS conjucyclic codes over \(\mathbb Z_{p^t}[i]\), and the \(p=13,n=7\) row is labeled \((7,13^{4t},6)\). Their earlier \(t=1\) work already lists the corresponding \((7,13^4,6)\) parameter set over the order-\(13^2\) Gaussian quotient, but does not provide the \(t=2\) lifted coefficient string at issue here.

The current paper invokes Hensel lifting in its construction of codes over prime-power rings. The finite calculation above isolates the missing lift in this concrete table row: the displayed coefficients are correct modulo \(13\), but keeping them unchanged modulo \(169\) does not produce the advertised code. Focused searches by article title, parameter set, the printed coefficient string, the corrected coefficient string, and the resulting \(13^{17}\) cardinality did not locate a published correction of this row.

## Limitations

Only the \(p=13,n=7,t=2\) specialization of the first \(p=13\) row is asserted here. No claim is made about the correctness of other Table 1 rows or about the abstract existence theorem.

There is a presentation ambiguity: a reader might interpret the printed Table 1 coefficients merely as residue-field representatives that are to be Hensel-lifted afresh for each \(t\), rather than as literal coefficients over \(\mathbb Z_{p^t}\). The table caption, however, describes them as generator polynomials over \(\mathbb Z_{p^t}[i]\), and no \(t\)-dependent lift is supplied in the row. Under the literal explicit-generator reading, the \(t=2\) failure and corrected lift above are exact.
