# Same-model review

## Correctness

**PASS.** A relative local \(z\)-rotation gauges the \(D_z\) phase into the transverse exchange magnitude \(R=\sqrt{J^2+D_z^2}\), leaving a Bell-diagonal Gibbs state. Exact Bell weights give two equal transverse correlations and one longitudinal correlation. Their magnitude difference factorizes with sign exactly equal to \(\operatorname{sign}(J_z-R)\), proving the optimizer boundary for every positive temperature. The high-temperature coefficient follows from the Bell-diagonal entropy expansion. Direct matrix replay agrees.

## Originality

**PASS, narrowly scoped.** The Chen--Yin source numerically studies the same thermal discord and emphasizes its asymptotic high-temperature persistence, but does not state a temperature-independent measurement-optimizer boundary or a leading \(T^{-2}\) coefficient. Luo supplies the general Bell-diagonal discord theorem, and Kundu--Subrahmanyam discuss measurement-basis switching in many-spin ground states; neither inspected source gives this finite-temperature DM specialization.

Targeted semantic and literature searches for the exact boundary and asymptotic coefficient did not locate a covering result.

## Value

**PASS.** Discord calculations are defined through an optimization over local measurements. Replacing that temperature-by-temperature optimization by one exact coupling inequality is a structural simplification of the source model. The \(T^{-2}\) coefficient also quantifies precisely the “asymptotically near zero but nonzero” regime highlighted in the paper and shows how the DM and XXZ anisotropies enter on opposite sides of the optimizer boundary.
