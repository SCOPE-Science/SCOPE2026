# The 750 minimizers of Example 4.6 split into four natural orbits
## Finding

Carvalho--López--San-José Example 4.6 considers
\[
q=3,\qquad m=5,\qquad d=2,\qquad r=3,\qquad
\mathbb X=(\mathbb F_3^*)^5
\]
and reports, using a generalized-Hamming-weight package, that
\[
d_3\!\left(C_2^{\mathbb X,S}\right)=16.
\]
The code has length
\[
32
\]
and dimension
\[
\binom52=10.
\]

For this exact code there are precisely
\[
750
\]
three-dimensional subcodes whose support has size \(16\).

There is also a natural symmetry classification. The subgroup generated by permutations of the five variables and independent sign changes of the variables has effective order
\[
2^4\cdot5!=1920,
\]
because simultaneous sign change of all five variables acts trivially on degree-\(2\) monomials. Under this group, the \(750\) minimum-support subcodes split into exactly four orbits, of sizes
\[
10,\qquad20,\qquad240,\qquad480.
\]

Thus the package-only value \(d_3=16\) in Example 4.6 has a complete finite minimizer census and a nontrivial four-orbit geometry.

## Assumptions and scope

Order the ten square-free degree-\(2\) monomials as
\[
x_ix_j,\qquad 1\le i<j\le5.
\]
For \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_5)\in(\mathbb F_3^*)^5\), let
\[
v(x)=\bigl(x_ix_j\bigr)_{1\le i<j\le5}\in\mathbb F_3^{10}.
\]
These are the columns of a generator matrix for the code.

Since all generating monomials have even degree,
\[
v(-x)=v(x).
\]
Hence the \(32\) torus points give \(16\) distinct generator columns, each with multiplicity two. We use representatives with
\[
x_1=1.
\]

A three-dimensional subcode is identified, through the full-rank evaluation map, with a three-dimensional coefficient subspace
\[
U\le\mathbb F_3^{10}.
\]
A projective column type \(v(x)\) is zero on the entire subcode precisely when
\[
U\subseteq v(x)^\perp.
\]

The orbit statement is only for the explicit natural signed-permutation subgroup. No claim is made that the four classes remain distinct under the full monomial automorphism group of the code.

## Proof

Let \(Z(U)\) be the set of the \(16\) projective column types on which every word of the three-dimensional subcode \(U\) vanishes. Then
\[
\operatorname{span} Z(U)\subseteq U^\perp.
\]
Since
\[
\dim U^\perp=7,
\]
we must have
\[
\operatorname{rank} Z(U)\le7.
\]

Exact row reduction over \(\mathbb F_3\) on the \(16\) projective columns gives two decisive facts:

\[
\text{no \(9\)-subset has rank at most \(7\),}
\]
and
\[
\text{exactly \(750\) \(8\)-subsets have rank \(7\).}
\]

The first statement implies
\[
|Z(U)|\le8
\]
for every three-dimensional subcode. Since every projective column occurs twice in the length-\(32\) generator matrix,
\[
|\operatorname{Supp}(U)|
=
32-2|Z(U)|
\ge16.
\]

Conversely, let \(Z\) be one of the \(750\) rank-\(7\) eight-subsets. Its annihilator
\[
U_Z=(\operatorname{span}Z)^\perp
\]
has dimension \(3\). It cannot vanish on a ninth projective type, because that would produce a \(9\)-subset of rank at most \(7\). Therefore
\[
Z(U_Z)=Z
\]
and
\[
|\operatorname{Supp}(U_Z)|=32-2\cdot8=16.
\]
The annihilator is unique because \(\operatorname{rank}Z=7\). Different eight-subsets therefore produce different three-dimensional subcodes. This proves both
\[
d_3(C)=16
\]
and the exact minimizer count \(750\).

For the symmetry statement, coordinate permutations act on the variables, while a sign vector
\[
(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_5)\in\{\pm1\}^5
\]
rescales each degree-\(2\) monomial by \(\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j\) and permutes torus evaluation points. The global sign vector acts trivially, so the induced group on projective column types has order
\[
2^4\cdot5!=1920.
\]
Applying all \(1920\) induced transformations to the \(750\) extremal eight-subsets partitions them into four orbits. Their sizes are
\[
10,\quad20,\quad240,\quad480,
\]
whose sum is \(750\).

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library.

It constructs all \(32\) points of \((\mathbb F_3^*)^5\), evaluates the ten degree-\(2\) square-free monomials, verifies generator rank \(10\), and confirms that the \(32\) columns consist of \(16\) distinct types of multiplicity two.

It then checks every
\[
\binom{16}{9}=11440
\]
nine-subset and every
\[
\binom{16}{8}=12870
\]
eight-subset by exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_3\). It verifies that no nine-subset has rank at most \(7\), and that exactly \(750\) eight-subsets have rank \(7\).

Finally, the verifier constructs the \(1920\) distinct signed-permutation actions on the \(16\) projective column types and checks that the \(750\) extremal subsets form four orbits of sizes
\[
10,\quad20,\quad240,\quad480.
\]
Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

The primary article introduces Cartesian square-free codes and, in Example 4.6, isolates this ternary torus case as a failure of sharpness for its footprint method: the footprint gives \(14\), while direct computation gives \(d_3=16\). The article does not count or classify the subcodes attaining \(d_3\).

Earlier work by Patanker and Singh determines generalized Hamming weights for hypersimplex toric codes in specified parameter ranges. For the third generalized Hamming weight, their exact regimes require
\[
m>2d+1
\quad\text{or}\quad
m<2d-1.
\]
The present boundary case has
\[
m=5=2d+1,
\]
so it lies outside those exact regimes. Earlier work of Jaramillo--Pinto--Villarreal determines the first and second generalized Hamming weights of the relevant square-free evaluation codes, not this third-weight minimizer census.

Focused searches by the article identifier, Example 4.6 parameters, the exact value \(d_3=16\), the number \(750\), and orbit/minimizer terminology found no prior same-object classification. General formulas for generalized Hamming weights do not by themselves count all minimizing subcodes or determine their symmetry orbits.

## Limitations

The result concerns exactly the \(q=3\), \(m=5\), \(d=2\), \(r=3\) torus code of Example 4.6. It is not a formula for arbitrary Cartesian square-free codes.

The four-orbit statement uses the natural signed-coordinate-permutation subgroup of order \(1920\). A larger automorphism group could fuse some of these four natural orbits.

The computation gives a complete finite classification of the minimizers but does not yet provide a closed conceptual description of each orbit family.
