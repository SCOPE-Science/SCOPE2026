# The axis-signature surface of shape \(1,2,1\) is a degree-six weak del Pezzo with \(A_1\sqcup A_2\) singularities
## Finding
For the axis-parallel path of shape \((1,2,1)\), let \([a:b:c]\) denote the three projectivized step lengths. After diagonal rescaling of the seven nonzero third-signature coordinates, the map is
\[
\phi([a:b:c])=[(a+c)^3:a^2b:abc:ab^2:bc^2:b^2c:b^3]\in\mathbb P^6.
\]
The closure \(X=\mathcal A_{(1,2,1),3}\) has exactly two singular points,
\[
P=[1:0:0:0:0:0:0],\qquad Q=[0:1:-1:0:1:0:0].
\]
The point \(P\) is a Du Val singularity of type \(A_1\), and \(Q\) is of type \(A_2\). More structurally, the graph resolution of \(\phi\) is the blow-up of \(\mathbb P^2\) at a chain of three successive infinitely-near points, and \(X\) is its anticanonical model.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. The coordinates above differ from the raw signature coordinates only by nonzero scalar factors and by omission of the identically zero coordinate \(\sigma_{212}\). The statement concerns the specific source example \(\mathcal A_{(1,2,1),3}\); it does not assert the same singularity pattern for other shapes or signature levels.

## Proof
For a path with consecutive steps \(a e_1\), \(b e_2\), \(c e_1\), Chen multiplication gives
\[
\sigma_{111}=\frac{(a+c)^3}{6},\quad
\sigma_{112}=\frac{a^2b}{2},\quad
\sigma_{121}=abc,\quad
\sigma_{122}=\frac{ab^2}{2},
\]
\[
\sigma_{211}=\frac{bc^2}{2},\quad
\sigma_{221}=\frac{b^2c}{2},\quad
\sigma_{222}=\frac{b^3}{6},\qquad \sigma_{212}=0,
\]
which yields the displayed parametrization after diagonal rescaling.

The common base locus is supported at \(p=[1:0:-1]\). On the affine chart \(a=1\), put \(v=a+c\). Since \(a^2b=b\) is one of the sections, the local base ideal is exactly \((b,v^3)\). Principalizing this ideal requires three successive point blow-ups along the strict transform of \(b=0\). If \(H\) is the pullback of a line and \(E_1,E_2,E_3\) are the total-transform exceptional classes, each point occurs with multiplicity one, so the resolved divisor is
\[
D=3H-E_1-E_2-E_3=-K_{\widetilde X}.
\]
The seven displayed cubics are linearly independent, and cubics through the three-point cluster impose three independent conditions, so they form the complete anticanonical system. On the dense set where \(ab\neq0\), the ratios \(x_3/x_1=b/a\) and \(x_5/x_3=c/a\) recover \([a:b:c]\); hence the anticanonical morphism is birational.

The three curves
\[
C_0=H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\qquad C_1=E_1-E_2,\qquad C_2=E_2-E_3
\]
all have self-intersection \(-2\) and satisfy \((-K_{\widetilde X})\cdot C_i=0\). Here \(C_1\cdot C_2=1\), while \(C_0\) is disjoint from both. The blow-up can be chosen toric: the initial point is a torus fixed point and the next two are the torus fixed points determined by the successive transform of \(b=0\). Checking the six torus-invariant boundary curves shows that \(-K_{\widetilde X}\) is nef and that these are exactly the invariant curves of anticanonical degree zero. Therefore the anticanonical map contracts precisely the isolated \(C_0\) and the chain \(C_1\cup C_2\), producing one \(A_1\) and one \(A_2\) rational double point.

For an independent local check, elimination of \(a,b,c\) gives the nine quadrics reproduced by `verify_axis_surface.py`. On \(x_0=1\), three coordinates eliminate polynomially and every remaining relation is generated by
\[
x_1x_4-x_2^2,
\]
so the only singular point in that chart is \(P\), with type \(A_1\). On \(x_1=1\), the relations give
\[
x_4=x_2^2,\qquad x_5=x_2x_3,\qquad x_6=x_3^2,
\]
and the sole remaining equation is
\[
x_0x_3-(1+x_2)^3=0.
\]
Its unique singular point is \(Q\), analytically \(uv-w^3=0\), hence type \(A_2\). The chart \(x_4=1\) is the symmetric copy of the \(x_1=1\) chart, and on \(x_6=1\) one has the smooth parametrization \((x_3,x_5)\mapsto ((x_3+x_5)^3,x_3^2,x_3x_5,x_3,x_5^2,x_5,1)\). These four charts cover \(X\), proving that there are no further singularities.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_axis_surface.py` performs exact symbolic calculations over \(\mathbb Q\). It reconstructs the elimination ideal from the graph of \(\phi\), checks the nine quadrics, verifies the two local normal forms, verifies the Jacobian-rank drop at \(P\) and \(Q\), checks the chart cover argument, and checks the intersection matrix and anticanonical degrees of the three contracted curves. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Colmenarejo, Galuppi, and Michałek prove that axis-path varieties are toric in the relevant low-level cases and, in Example 3.7, identify \(\mathcal A_{(1,2,1),3}\) as a degree-six toric surface in \(\mathbb P^6\) and determine its lattice polytope. Their article does not state the base-scheme resolution of the path-length parametrization, the anticanonical weak-del-Pezzo model, or the exact \(A_1\sqcup A_2\) singularity classification. Earlier work of Améndola, Friz, and Sturmfels introduces axis-path signature varieties and explicitly calls for study of their prime ideals, geometric invariants, and parametrization fibers, but does not contain this example-level singularity resolution.

## Limitations
The singularity classification is for the single shape \((1,2,1)\) at signature level \(3\). The proof uses characteristic zero in the local \(A_2\) Jacobian argument and in the standard Du Val interpretation. No claim is made about deformation families of axis-path varieties, higher signatures, or statistical consequences of the singular points.
