# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The path-signature parametrization follows directly from Chen multiplication for the three segments. The base ideal is locally \((b,(a+c)^3)\), so three successive simple blow-ups resolve it and give the complete divisor \(3H-E_1-E_2-E_3=-K\). Exact elimination independently produces the stated projective ideal. The four affine charts \(x_0\neq0\), \(x_1\neq0\), \(x_4\neq0\), and \(x_6\neq0\) cover the image; their equations show that the only singular points are \(P\) and \(Q\), with local equations \(uv-w^2\) and \(uv-w^3\), respectively. The resolution intersection calculation gives the same \(A_1\sqcup A_2\) configuration.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Colmenarejo--Galuppi--Michałek Example 3.7, states toricness, degree six, ambient \(\mathbb P^6\), and determines the polytope, but it does not state the path-parameter base scheme, the three-step infinitely-near resolution, the anticanonical model, the singular points, or their \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) analytic types. Targeted published-finding corpus and web searches for the exact example together with “singular”, “del Pezzo”, “anticanonical”, and “A1 A2” found no covering statement. The earlier Améndola--Friz--Sturmfels paper motivates geometric-invariant and fiber questions for axis-path varieties without supplying this classification.

## Value
PASS. The result turns a computationally identified toric surface into an explicit birational model tied to the original path-length parameters. It determines exactly where and how the parametrization degenerates, identifies the two singularity types, and relates them to a curvilinear length-three base scheme. This directly addresses geometric-invariant and fiber structure for a foundational low-dimensional axis-signature example rather than merely recomputing its degree or ideal.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest published description is Example 3.7 of *Toric geometry of path signature varieties* (arXiv:1903.03779), which gives the degree-six toric surface and its polytope. The present claim is limited to characteristic zero and to \(\mathcal A_{(1,2,1),3}\); it does not extrapolate to other shapes or levels. A residual originality risk remains that an equivalent singularity description may occur in toric-surface tables under the abstract lattice polygon rather than axis-signature terminology, but no such source was located and none of the searched sources tied that abstract model back to the path-length base scheme.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
