# The Cartwright--Steger critical values form a non-torsion Eisenstein orbit
## Finding
For the explicit Albanese map of the Cartwright--Steger surface computed by Szabó, write
\[
E:\ y^2=x^3-3888
\]
for the Albanese elliptic curve, let \(\theta^3=1116\), and set
\[
q=\frac{6267859}{1459^2},\qquad
y_*=\frac{487135434066}{1459^3},\qquad
P=(q\theta,y_*).
\]
Szabó's three critical values are
\[
P,\quad \rho P,\quad \rho^2P,
\qquad
\rho(x,y)=(\zeta_3^2x,y).
\]

The point \(P\) has infinite order. Consequently, over
\[
L=\mathbf Q(\theta,\zeta_3),
\]
the subgroup generated by the three critical values is a free rank-one \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]\)-module. Equivalently, the complete lattice of integral relations among the three critical values is generated by
\[
P+\rho P+\rho^2P=O.
\]
Thus every critical value and every difference of two critical values is non-torsion.

This sharply contrasts with the three values of the smooth fibers fixed by the same order-three base automorphism: those three values are exactly
\[
\ker(\rho-1),
\]
which is an order-three subgroup of \(E\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbf C\) for the geometric Albanese fibration, while the arithmetic argument is carried out over the number fields generated by the exact coordinates in Szabó's formula. The real cube root \(\theta\) is fixed by \(\theta^3=1116\), and \(P\) denotes the corresponding real critical value.

The claim determines the subgroup generated by the three critical values. It does not determine the full Mordell--Weil group \(E(L)\), its rank, or the index of the critical-value subgroup inside any larger arithmetic subgroup.

## Proof
Szabó gives the critical values in the form
\[
[q\theta:y_*:1],\qquad \theta^3=1116,
\]
with the constants displayed above. Direct exact arithmetic gives
\[
y_*^2=q^3\cdot1116-3888,
\]
so \(P\in E(K)\) for \(K=\mathbf Q(\theta)\).

We prove that \(P\) is non-torsion using two degree-one good reductions.

First take \(p=17\). Since
\[
12^3\equiv1116\pmod{17},
\]
the specialization \(\theta\mapsto12\) gives a degree-one prime of \(K\) above \(17\). The denominator \(1459\) is a unit there, and \(E\) has good reduction because \(17\nmid6\cdot3888\). The point reduces to
\[
\overline P_17=(2,8)\in E(\mathbf F_{17}).
\]
Exact group-law calculation gives
\[
9\overline P_17=O,
\]
while no smaller positive multiple vanishes, so \(\overline P_17\) has order \(9\).

Next take \(p=23\). Since
\[
13^3\equiv1116\pmod{23},
\]
there is likewise a degree-one prime above \(23\), and the reduced point is
\[
\overline P_23=(17,17)\in E(\mathbf F_{23}).
\]
It has exact order \(4\).

If \(P\) had finite order \(N\), injectivity of prime-to-\(p\) torsion under good reduction would force
\[
N=9\cdot17^a
\]
for some \(a\ge0\), from the first reduction, and simultaneously
\[
N=4\cdot23^b
\]
for some \(b\ge0\), from the second. No positive integer has both forms. Hence \(P\) has infinite order.

Over \(L=K(\zeta_3)\), the automorphism
\[
\rho(x,y)=(\zeta_3^2x,y)
\]
has order three and fixes the origin. Therefore
\[
1+\rho+\rho^2=0
\]
in \(\operatorname{End}_L(E)\), so
\[
P+\rho P+\rho^2P=O.
\]
Suppose
\[
aP+b\rho P=O
\]
for integers \(a,b\). Then the endomorphism \(a+b\rho\) kills \(P\). If \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), this is a nonzero endomorphism of an elliptic curve and therefore has finite kernel, contradicting the infinite order of \(P\). Thus \(a=b=0\). The \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]\)-module generated by \(P\) is therefore free of rank one, and the displayed three-term relation generates all integral relations among the orbit points.

Likewise, each pairwise difference is the image of \(P\) under a nonzero endomorphism such as \(1-\rho\) or \(1-\rho^2\). A nonzero endomorphism cannot send a non-torsion point to torsion: if its image were torsion, a nonzero integer multiple of the endomorphism would kill \(P\), again impossible. Hence every pairwise difference is non-torsion.

Finally, Szabó records that the order-three symmetry has exactly three fixed values on the Albanese base and that the corresponding fibers are smooth. The fixed subgroup is
\[
\ker(\rho-1).
\]
Since the Eisenstein norm of \(\rho-1\) is \(3\), the isogeny \(\rho-1\) has degree \(3\), so this kernel consists of exactly three points. It is therefore the order-three subgroup formed by the smooth invariant-fiber values.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact rational and modular arithmetic. It checks the defining equation for \(P\), the two cube-root specializations, the reduced points, and the exact group-law orders
\[
\operatorname{ord}(\overline P_{17})=9,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(\overline P_{23})=4.
\]
It also checks the good-reduction arithmetic prerequisites and the elementary incompatibility of the two possible torsion-order forms. The saved replay output ends in `VERIFY_OK`.

The infinite-order conclusion is a theorem-level consequence of good reduction, not an extrapolation from a finite search. The \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]\)-module statement is then deduced from the finite-kernel property of nonzero elliptic-curve endomorphisms.

## Relationship to prior work
Szabó determines the Albanese curve, an exact polynomial formula for the Albanese map, all critical points and critical values, and the order-three orbit structure of the three singular fibers. The inspected article gives the exact coordinates used here, but does not discuss torsion, Mordell--Weil order, or integral relations among the critical values.

Akhmedov--Yeung subsequently use Szabó's real critical value to choose vanishing paths for a monodromy relation. Their inspected treatment concerns vanishing cycles and monodromy and does not determine the arithmetic order of the critical values.

Targeted searches for the exact critical coordinates together with torsion, Mordell--Weil, non-torsion, and pairwise-difference formulations did not locate a covering statement. Searches of the available published-result index likewise returned only unrelated elliptic-curve torsion or infinite-order computations.

## Limitations
The argument does not compute the full Mordell--Weil group over \(K\) or \(L\), and it does not claim that the critical-value subgroup is saturated. The later monodromy paper is used only as a close same-fibration comparison, not as an initiating source.

An ultra-recent or unindexed note could contain the same arithmetic observation. The originality conclusion rests on the inspected primary and follow-up texts plus targeted statement-level searches, not on search failure alone.
