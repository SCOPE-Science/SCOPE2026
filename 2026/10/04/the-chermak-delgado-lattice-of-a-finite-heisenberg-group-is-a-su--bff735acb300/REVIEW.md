# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The commutator induces the standard nondegenerate \(\mathbb F_q\)-symplectic form on \(V=H_n(q)/Z\). For any subgroup \(K\ge Z\), its image \(U\) is an \(\mathbb F_p\)-subspace and its centralizer depends only on the \(\mathbb F_q\)-span \(S=\langle U\rangle_q\), giving \(|C_G(K)|=q^{1+2n-\dim_q S}\). Writing \(q=p^f\), \(s=\dim_p U\), \(d=\dim_qS\), one gets \(m_G(K)=q^{2n+2}p^{s-fd}\le q^{2n+2}\), with equality exactly when \(U\) is already \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear. Adjoining \(Z\) only increases the measure, so these are all maximizers. Every \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace occurs as a centralizer via symplectic orthogonal complement, proving both lattice identifications. For \(q=p\), every subgroup above \(Z\) has field-linear image, giving \(\mathcal{CD}(G)=[G/Z]\) and hence full-lattice monotonicity by the known criterion; for \(f>1\), the subgroup with image \(\mathbb F_p v\) gives a strict measure drop and disproves monotonicity. The inspected verifier exhaustively checks additive subspaces for \((n,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(1,4)\).

## originality

PASS

Cocke--McCulloch's complete open article gives the general monotonicity criteria and discusses the three-dimensional unitriangular group over \(GF(p^a)\), whose centralizer/Chermak--Delgado lattice is only the rank-one quasi-antichain of width \(p^a+1\). The 2025 pseudocentralizer paper treats a different mod-\(p^n\) Heisenberg family and advertises the maximal measure, not the full field-Heisenberg lattice. Targeted searches for higher finite-field Heisenberg centralizer/Chermak--Delgado lattices found no published subspace-lattice theorem. A current faculty research page explicitly lists the Chermak--Delgado lattice of finite Heisenberg groups as an active project, so possible unpublished overlap is retained as a residual risk.

The finite Heisenberg groups are canonical class-two groups, and the Chermak--Delgado lattice is a standard structural invariant. Identifying it uniformly with the full finite-field subspace lattice, plus the exact prime-versus-prime-power monotonicity transition, gives a natural complete family classification extending the known rank-one quasi-antichain picture.
