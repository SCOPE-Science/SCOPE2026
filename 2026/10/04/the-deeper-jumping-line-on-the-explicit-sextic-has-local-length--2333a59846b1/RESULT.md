# The deeper jumping line on the explicit sextic has local length three
## Finding
Let
\[
F_0=\prod_{i=0}^{5}(x_0-i x_1)+x_2^2\bigl(x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_0x_1x_2(x_0+x_1+x_2)\bigr)
\]
and let \(B_0=\{F_0=0\}\subset\mathbf P^2\). Faro--Gounelas show that \(B_0\) is smooth, that its jumping-line scheme for \(T_{\mathbf P^2}(-\log B_0)\) is zero-dimensional of degree \(171\), and that the transverse line
\[
\ell_0=\{x_2=0\}
\]
lies in the deeper determinantal stratum: the relevant restriction matrix has rank \(4\), so the jumping scheme is nonreduced at \(\ell_0\).

The local structure is exact. In the affine dual chart
\[
\ell_{u,v}=\{x_2-u x_0-v x_1=0\},
\]
with \(\ell_0\) represented by \((u,v)=(0,0)\), one has
\[
\mathcal O_{J(B_0),\ell_0}\cong
\mathbf C[u,v]_{(u,v)}/(u,v)^2.
\]
Hence the local length is exactly
\[
3,
\]
and the Zariski tangent space has dimension \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The jumping scheme \(J(B_0)\) is the maximal-minor degeneracy scheme used in Proposition 4.6, Remark 4.7, and Appendix A of Faro--Gounelas. In an affine dual chart, restricting the three partial derivatives of \(F_0\) to a line and multiplying by the two binary coordinates produces a \(7\times6\) coefficient matrix \(M(u,v)\). The jumping ideal is generated by the \(6\times6\) minors of this matrix.

The statement concerns only the local scheme at the explicit line \(\ell_0\). It does not claim that \(\ell_0\) is the only nonreduced point of the jumping scheme, nor does it classify the remaining local multiplicities.

## Proof
Use binary coordinates \(x,y\) on the line
\[
x_2=u x+v y,\qquad x_0=x,\qquad x_1=y.
\]
For each partial derivative \(\partial_iF_0\), form the two degree-six binary forms
\[
x\,\partial_iF_0|_{\ell_{u,v}},
\qquad
y\,\partial_iF_0|_{\ell_{u,v}}.
\]
Their coefficients in the ordered monomial basis
\[
x^6,x^5y,\ldots,y^6
\]
give a \(7\times6\) matrix \(M(u,v)\).

At the origin, exact arithmetic gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}M(0,0)=4.
\]
In fact, the upper-left \(4\times4\) minor has determinant
\[
-23520,
\]
so it is a unit in the local ring
\[
R=\mathbf C[u,v]_{(u,v)}.
\]
Unimodular row and column operations over \(R\) therefore reduce \(M\) locally to a block matrix with an identity \(4\times4\) block and a residual \(3\times2\) matrix \(B(u,v)\) satisfying
\[
B(0,0)=0.
\]
Consequently the ideal \(J\subset R\) generated by the \(6\times6\) minors of \(M\) is exactly the ideal of the \(2\times2\) minors of \(B\).

It remains to determine the quadratic initial forms. Let \(K=\ker M(0,0)\), of dimension \(2\), and \(C=\operatorname{coker}M(0,0)\), of dimension \(3\). The linear part of \(B\), up to changes of bases in \(K\) and \(C\), is the induced normal map
\[
A(u,v)=uA_u+vA_v:K\longrightarrow C.
\]
Using exact rational bases, the computation gives
\[
A_u=
\begin{pmatrix}
1900&405\\
-48360/7&-9623/7\\
39600/7&7530/7
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_v=
\begin{pmatrix}
405&412/5\\
-9623/7&-1734/7\\
7530/7&182
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The three \(2\times2\) minors of \(A(u,v)\), written in the basis
\[
u^2,\ uv,\ v^2,
\]
have coefficient matrix of determinant
\[
\frac{2655279064360000}{2401}\neq0.
\]
Therefore their quadratic initial forms span all of
\[
\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^3,
\qquad
\mathfrak m=(u,v).
\]

Because \(B(0,0)=0\), every \(2\times2\) minor belongs to \(\mathfrak m^2\), so
\[
J\subseteq\mathfrak m^2.
\]
The spanning calculation gives
\[
\mathfrak m^2=J+\mathfrak m^3.
\]
Applying Nakayama's lemma to the finitely generated \(R\)-module \(\mathfrak m^2/J\) yields
\[
J=\mathfrak m^2.
\]
Thus
\[
R/J\cong R/\mathfrak m^2,
\]
which has basis \(1,u,v\) and length \(3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Faro--Gounelas introduce this explicit sextic as a contrasting example to their reduced \(171\)-point jumping scheme. They prove that the jumping scheme of \(B_0\) is zero-dimensional of degree \(171\), identify \(\ell_0\) as a transverse jumping line in the deeper rank-\(4\) stratum, and conclude that the scheme is nonreduced there. They do not state its local multiplicity or local algebra.

General literature on jumping lines of logarithmic bundles describes jumping loci through splitting types, Jacobian modules, and determinantal constructions. The checked sources do not give this local calculation for the Faro--Gounelas sextic. Targeted searches for the source identifier, the explicit line, local multiplicity, local length, and first-infinitesimal-neighbourhood formulations found no covering statement.

## Limitations
The exact local algebra is proved only at \(\ell_0\). The calculation does not determine the number, support, or multiplicities of any other nonreduced points of the \(171\)-length scheme.

The interpretation under deformation is not asserted: although local length \(3\) is the natural scheme-theoretic mass at \(\ell_0\), no claim is made here about a particular three-branch splitting in a chosen deformation.
