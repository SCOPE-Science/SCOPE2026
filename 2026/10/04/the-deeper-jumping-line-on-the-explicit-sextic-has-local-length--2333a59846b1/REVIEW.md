# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The published affine jumping scheme is a maximal-minor degeneracy locus of a \(7\times6\) restriction matrix. Reconstructing that matrix from the exact sextic gives rank \(4\) at \(\ell_0\) and an invertible \(4\times4\) minor \(-23520\). Local block reduction therefore leaves a \(3\times2\) matrix vanishing at the origin. Its induced linear kernel-to-cokernel map has three quadratic maximal minors whose coefficient determinant in \(u^2,uv,v^2\) is
\[
\frac{2655279064360000}{2401}\neq0.
\]
Thus the local determinantal ideal has quadratic initial space \(\mathfrak m^2/\mathfrak m^3\). Since the ideal lies in \(\mathfrak m^2\), Nakayama gives equality with \(\mathfrak m^2\), and the local length is \(3\).

## Originality
**PASS.** Faro--Gounelas state only that the named line is in the deeper rank-\(4\) stratum and that the finite jumping scheme is therefore nonreduced there. The checked primary text does not state a multiplicity or local algebra. Searches by source identifier, explicit line, local-length language, first-infinitesimal-neighbourhood language, and general logarithmic jumping-line terminology found no covering statement. Older general jumping-line literature does not determine this source-specific local algebra.

## Value
**PASS.** The source uses this sextic precisely as the contrasting nonreduced example. Determining that its named deeper point is exactly the first infinitesimal neighbourhood gives a canonical, deformation-relevant measure of the failure of reducedness and accounts for exactly three units of the global length \(171\). This is a structural local invariant, not a restatement of the published global count.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Faro--Gounelas, arXiv:2609.29501v1, especially Remark 4.7 and Appendix A. Dimca--Sticlaru provide broader background on jumping lines of logarithmic bundles. The result determines only the local algebra at \(\ell_0\); it does not classify the other points or assert how the length-three point splits in any chosen deformation.
