# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The published sufficient criterion handles every \(m\ge3,d>m\) pair after a closed-form boundary inequality and every \(m=2,d\ge4\) pair after the \(d=4\) inequality. The published boundary, low-dimensional, and quadratic-diagonal results handle all other previously settled cases. For the remaining \((m,d)=(2,3)\) case in \(n\ge4\), the restriction of a general cone quadric to \(H\cap H'\) has rank at least \(4\), leaving only gcd degrees \(0\) and \(2\). The published two-cone Hilbert functions then give codimensions that strictly dominate the corresponding second-cone strata. The same-support case is excluded directly. This proves generic uniqueness of the cone and hence birationality.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating paper explicitly says that the exact birational range in \(\mathbf P^{2n-1}\) for \(n\ge4\) is unknown. Targeted searches for the exact range, the higher-dimensional cubic-quadric pair, uniqueness of the cone, and generic incidence degree located no covering result, and the published-finding index returned no overlap. The older thesis cited by the paper was checked only at metadata/abstract level and remains a stated residual risk rather than being treated as proved noncoverage.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem closes an explicit open problem and replaces a sufficient criterion by a complete parameter classification. It also has direct enumerative significance because birationality identifies the incidence integral with the degree of the image without an additional generic-degree factor.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Guedes, arXiv:2609.19427v1. It supplies the incidence framework, the exact low-dimensional range, the quadratic-diagonal generic degree, the \(d=m\) boundary, the higher-dimensional sufficient criterion, and the two-cone Hilbert-function calculation, but explicitly leaves the exact \(n\ge4\) range open. The present theorem does not describe exceptional multiple-cone strata or compute degrees and singularities of the incidence images.
