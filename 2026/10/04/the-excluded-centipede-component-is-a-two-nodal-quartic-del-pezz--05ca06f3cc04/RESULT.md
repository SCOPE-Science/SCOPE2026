# The excluded centipede component is a two-nodal quartic del Pezzo surface
## Finding
For the centipede game in Portakal--Sturmfels, Example 4, let \(S\subset\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb C}\) be the second prime component of the displayed Spohn ideal. Then \(S\) lies in the hyperplane \(p_{11}+p_{12}=0\) and, inside that \(\mathbb P^4\), it is a complete intersection of two quadrics. Its singular locus consists of exactly two points,
\[
P=[-1:1:0:0:0:0],\qquad Q=[0:0:1:0:-1:0].
\]
Both are ordinary double points, equivalently rational double points of type \(A_1\). Hence \(S\) is a normal quartic del Pezzo surface with singularity basket \(2A_1\).

Writing \(x=p_{12}\), \(y=p_{21}\), \(z=p_{22}\), \(u=p_{31}\), \(v=p_{32}\), so that \(p_{11}=-x\), the two quadrics are
\[
q=3zu-2yv+zv,
\]
\[
f=6xy+3xz+3yz+6xu+12xv-4yv-zv-6uv.
\]
For symmetric matrices \(A,B\) satisfying \(q=\mathbf t^{\!T}A\mathbf t\) and \(f=\mathbf t^{\!T}B\mathbf t\), where \(\mathbf t=(x,y,z,u,v)^T\), the discriminant of the pencil is
\[
\det(\lambda A+\mu B)=54\mu^2(\lambda-\mu)^2(\lambda+4\mu).
\]
The rank-four quadrics at the two double roots have vertices \([1:0:0:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0:-1:0]\), exactly the two singular points of \(S\) in these coordinates. The line joining them,
\[
[s:t]\longmapsto[-s:s:t:0:-t:0],
\]
is contained in \(S\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\). The component \(S\) is the second prime component printed in Example 4 of arXiv:2201.05506v1. The claim concerns that specific non-generic component; it does not assert that arbitrary Spohn surfaces have the same singularity type. The source already states that this component is a singular quartic surface in a hyperplane and is disjoint from the open probability simplex. The new content is the exact singular locus, the \(A_1\sqcup A_1\) local classification, the quartic-del-Pezzo identification, and the explicit pencil discriminant.

## Proof
The source gives the prime ideal
\[
\langle p_{11}+p_{12},\;3p_{22}p_{31}-2p_{21}p_{32}+p_{22}p_{32},\;c\rangle,
\]
where
\[
c=6p_{12}p_{21}+3p_{12}p_{22}+3p_{21}p_{22}+6p_{12}p_{31}+12p_{12}p_{32}-4p_{21}p_{32}-p_{22}p_{32}-6p_{31}p_{32}.
\]
Eliminating \(p_{11}\) gives the two quadrics \(q,f\) above in \(\mathbb P^4\).

A point of the complete intersection \(V(q,f)\) is singular exactly when the \(2\times5\) Jacobian matrix of \((q,f)\) has rank at most one. Thus the singular locus is defined by \(q\), \(f\), and the ten \(2\times2\) Jacobian minors. Exact Gröbner reduction on the five standard affine charts gives:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 x=1 & (y,z,u,v)\\
 y=1 & (x,z,u+1,v)\\
 z=1 & (1)\\
 u=1 & (x,y+1,z,v)\\
 v=1 & (1).
\end{array}
\]
Hence the projective singular locus consists exactly of \([1:0:0:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0:-1:0]\), which are \(P\) and \(Q\) in the original coordinates.

At \(P\), work on \(x=1\). Since \(\partial f/\partial u=6\) at the origin of the local coordinates \((y,z,u,v)\), the equation \(f=0\) eliminates \(u\) analytically. Substitution into \(q\), followed by multiplication by the local unit \(2(v-1)\), gives a local hypersurface equation whose Hessian at the origin is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&6&4\\
6&6&10\\
4&10&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=384\ne0.
\]
Thus the complex Morse lemma gives an ordinary double point.

At \(Q\), work on \(y=1\), write \(u=U-1\), and set \(g=f+q\). The linear terms of \(g\) vanish, while \(q=0\) eliminates \(v\) analytically because \(\partial q/\partial v=-2\) at the point. After substitution and multiplication by the local unit \(z-2\), the resulting hypersurface equation has Hessian
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-12&30\\
-12&0&-24\\
30&-24&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=17280\ne0.
\]
So \(Q\) is also an ordinary double point.

Because the source identifies the component as prime and the equations make it a complete intersection of type \((2,2)\) in \(\mathbb P^4\), adjunction gives \(K_S=-H\), where \(H\) is the hyperplane class, and \(H^2=4\). The two \(A_1\) points are normal singularities, so \(S\) is a normal quartic del Pezzo surface. Direct substitution verifies that the displayed line through \(P\) and \(Q\) lies on both quadrics.

For the pencil, writing the two quadrics by symmetric \(5\times5\) matrices and taking the exact determinant gives \(54\mu^2(\lambda-\mu)^2(\lambda+4\mu)\). The matrices at \(\mu=0\) and \(\lambda=\mu\) have rank four and kernels generated by the two node vectors, respectively.

Its successful terminal output is `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Portakal--Sturmfels explicitly print the prime decomposition for the centipede game and describe the second component only as a singular quartic surface in a hyperplane, disjoint from the open simplex; they do not identify its singular points, ADE types, del Pezzo type, or pencil discriminant. The later Portakal--Windisch study revisits boundary issues for dependency equilibria and gives a detailed geometric classification for \((2\times2)\)-games, but its text does not treat the centipede example. General literature on singular quartic del Pezzo surfaces includes the abstract \(2A_1\) type, so the novelty claimed here is not the existence or general classification of \(2A_1\) quartic del Pezzo surfaces, but the exact identification of this named Spohn component, together with its coordinates and discriminant pencil.

## Limitations
This is a classification of one explicit non-generic component. It does not classify all non-generic \(3\times2\) Spohn surfaces, all quartic components arising from extensive-form games, or the real topology of this surface. The literature search cannot exclude an uncatalogued treatment of the same explicit component; the closest general quartic-del-Pezzo classifications do not identify this centipede component by its game-theoretic equations.
