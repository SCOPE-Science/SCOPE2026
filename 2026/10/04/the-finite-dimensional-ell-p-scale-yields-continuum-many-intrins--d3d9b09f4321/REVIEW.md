# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The final claim is: Let \(n\ge 2\). For each \(r\in[1,\infty]\), let \(p_r=\|\cdot\|_r\) on \(\mathbb R^n\). Then \(p_r\) and \(p_s\) lie in the same intrinsic part of \(\mathcal N(\mathbb R^n)\) if and only if \(r=s\). For \(1<r<\infty\), the separating local triangular-defect asymptotic along \(e_1\) and \(e_1+t e_2\) is \(\Delta_{p_r}(e_1,e_1+t e_2)=((1-2^{1-r})/r)t^r+O(t^{2r})\). Consequently, for every \(n\ge 2\), the cone of equivalent norms on \(\mathbb R^n\) has continuum many intrinsic parts.

For \(1<r<\infty\), direct expansion gives the positive leading coefficient \(c_r=(1-2^{1-r})/r\). If \(1<p<q<\infty\), the ratio of the two defects on the common path is asymptotic to \((c_q/c_p)t^{q-p}\), so a uniform positive lower comparison is impossible. The endpoint \(r=1\) is separated by the pair \(e_1,e_2\), and \(r=\infty\) is separated from every finite \(r>1\) by \(e_1,e_1+e_2\). These are exactly the two mechanisms allowed by the source criterion: failure of quantitative defect comparability or failure of equality of defect zero sets.

## Originality

PASS with residual literature risk. The closest source, arXiv:2609.28922v1, proves the general triangular-defect criterion and explicitly separates only the pair \(\ell^2\) versus \(\ell^4\) in Example 4.8. Focused searches for a full \(\ell^r\) classification, continuum many intrinsic parts from this scale, and a local triangular-defect exponent did not locate a statement implying the final claim. The new statement is not inferred from titles alone: the relevant theorem, corollary, example, and neighboring finite-dimensional proposition were inspected in the primary full text.

## Value

PASS. The focal paper introduces intrinsic parts as a new global decomposition of the cone of equivalent norms and gives one finite-dimensional pair showing that distinct parts really occur. The final claim completely classifies the most canonical one-parameter family of norms under that decomposition and shows that the phenomenon is not sparse: every real dimension at least \(2\) already has continuum many parts. The proof also identifies a simple local asymptotic invariant, the power of triangular-defect decay, that can be reused to separate other norm families.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest literature is Juan Rafael Acosta-Portilla, *Intrinsic Hilbert metrics on cones of equivalent norms*, arXiv:2609.28922v1. Its Theorem 4.6 gives the uniform-comparability characterization; Corollary 4.7 gives the zero-set obstruction; Example 4.8 establishes only the \(\ell^2\)-versus-\(\ell^4\) case. The present argument is limited to the canonical \(\ell^r\) scale and does not classify arbitrary equivalent norms. Because the source is recent, incomplete indexing remains a genuine originality risk.
