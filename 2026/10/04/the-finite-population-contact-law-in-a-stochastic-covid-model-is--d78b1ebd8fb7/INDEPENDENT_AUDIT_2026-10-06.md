# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The publisher PDF explicitly contains both laws. Under distinct sampling, conditioning on symptomatic and asymptomatic counts gives the displayed multivariate-hypergeometric no-infection average. With one infective, inclusion probability is exactly N/M and subsequent transmission probability is q, hence beta=Nq/M. The source power formula is strictly smaller for N>1 by Bernoulli's inequality, and at M=N=2,q=1 it equals 3/4 while without replacement every contact set contains the infective and gives probability 1. Conversely, independent with-replacement contact events imply a binomial susceptible-contact count, not hypergeometric.

## originality

PASS

The sampling distinction itself is classical and is explicitly recognized by the predecessor SIR paper, which invokes the binomial law only after assuming the population is much larger than the contact count. The 2020 primary paper, however, prints a multinomial/power contagion calculation and later a hypergeometric susceptible-contact count as exact statements within the same finite-population model. Searches found no correction or erratum identifying this source-specific incompatibility. The accepted originality is the diagnosis and exact two-class correction, not the hypergeometric distribution itself.

## value

PASS

This is a substantive correction to the exact transition law of a finite-population epidemic model, not a cosmetic normalization check. It identifies which of two incompatible mechanisms must be changed, supplies the correct two-infectious-class formula, and explains why the paper's large-population first-order reproduction approximation can nevertheless survive.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
