# Review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(\Omega\le4\), exact maxima over the five exponent partitions are
all below abundancy \(3\). At \(\Omega=5\), oddness is impossible because an
odd integer with odd divisor sum is a square and therefore has even
\(\Omega\). Splitting by the exact exponent of \(2\) then exhausts all cases.
The \(a=3\) squarefree branch reduces to
\[
(3q-5)(3r-5)=40,
\]
whose unique odd-prime solution is \(q=3,r=5\). The \(a=2\) branches are
eliminated by exact inequalities and
\[
(2r-7)(2s-7)=63,
\]
while the \(a=1\) odd-part maxima are all below \(2\). The converse is the
direct calculation \(\sigma(120)=360\).

## Originality
PASS. Cohen's 1980 theorem singles out \(120\) only after assuming the
divisibility pattern \(2^2\cdot3\cdot5\mid n\), and treats odd triperfect
numbers separately by a distinct-prime lower bound. Kishore strengthens only
the odd branch. Cohen--Sorli's 2012 result classifies a bounded-exponent
squarefree-odd-part family, not all numbers at fixed \(\Omega\). Exact database
and semantic searches found no statement implying the unrestricted
\(\Omega=5\) classification.

Residual risk remains from older non-searchable computational literature and
from the possibility that this short low-abundancy argument appeared under
different terminology.

## Value
PASS. Triperfect numbers form a classical family whose complete set is still
unknown. Total prime-factor multiplicity is an intrinsic structural measure.
The theorem identifies the exact first possible layer and proves that the first
known example \(120\) is the only triperfect number in that layer, rather than
merely being the first entry in a finite table.
