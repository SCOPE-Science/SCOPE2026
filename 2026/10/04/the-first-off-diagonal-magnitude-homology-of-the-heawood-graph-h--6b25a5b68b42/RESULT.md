# The first off-diagonal magnitude homology of the Heawood graph has mod-2 rank 336
## Finding
For the Heawood graph \(H\), presented as the incidence graph of the Fano plane with its shortest-path metric, \(\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(H;\mathbb F_2)=336\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(H\) be the bipartite incidence graph of the Fano plane. Label its point vertices by \(0,\ldots,6\) and its line vertices by \(7,\ldots,13\), with line \(7+j\) incident to points \(j\), \(j+1\), and \(j+3\) modulo \(7\). Distances are ordinary shortest-path distances. Magnitude chains are taken over \(\mathbb F_2\): \(\operatorname{MC}_{k,\ell}\) is generated by tuples \((x_0,\ldots,x_k)\) with consecutive entries distinct and total successive distance \(\ell\); the boundary deletes an internal vertex exactly when the triangle equality is preserved. The finding concerns only bidegree \((k,\ell)=(3,4)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Proof
For length \(4\), exhaustive generation gives
\[
\dim \operatorname{MC}_{2,4}=840,\qquad
\dim \operatorname{MC}_{3,4}=2268,\qquad
\dim \operatorname{MC}_{4,4}=1134.
\]
The boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) have ranks
\[
\operatorname{rank}(\partial_3)=840,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(\partial_4)=1092.
\]
Every column of \(\partial_4\) is checked to map to zero under \(\partial_3\). Hence
\[
\dim \operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(H;\mathbb F_2)
=2268-840-1092=336.
\]
The chain counts are obtained independently both by tuple enumeration and by a weighted dynamic program. Each boundary rank is independently recomputed by column elimination and by elimination after transposition.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_magnitude_heawood.py`. The script constructs the graph from the stated incidence rule, recomputes all-pairs distances, enumerates the relevant magnitude chains, builds the two boundary maps directly from the triangle-equality deletion rule, checks \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\), and performs two independent rank computations. Its successful terminal line is `VERIFY_OK`. It also verifies that point \(0\) and line \(8\) are at distance \(3\) with exactly three shortest paths, so this metric space is not geodetic.

## Relationship to prior work
Asao, Hiraoka, and Kanazawa relate graph girth to near-diagonal magnitude homology. Their Corollary 1.4 forces the first \(i\) off-diagonals to vanish under the hypothesis \(\operatorname{gir}(G)\ge 2i+5\). For the first off-diagonal \(i=1\), this requires girth at least \(7\); the Heawood graph has girth \(6\), so that theorem does not determine the present group. Gu's algebraic-Morse computations cover several graph families and named examples but do not state the Heawood value. The later exact description for geodetic metric spaces by Asao and Wakatsuki is inapplicable because the Heawood graph has pairs joined by multiple shortest paths. Targeted searches for the Heawood graph, its Fano-incidence description, and the bidegree \((3,4)\) did not locate a published exact value; this is evidence from the checked literature, not a proof that no unindexed computation exists.

## Limitations
The result is an exact finite computation over \(\mathbb F_2\) in one bidegree. It makes no claim about the integral group, torsion, other bidegrees, a closed formula for incidence graphs, or diagonality. The originality assessment is relative to the sources and exact-table searches listed below; obscure or unindexed computations remain a residual risk.
