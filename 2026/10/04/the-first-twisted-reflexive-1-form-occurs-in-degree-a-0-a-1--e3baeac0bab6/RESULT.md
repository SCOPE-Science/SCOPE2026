# The first twisted reflexive \(1\)-form occurs in degree \(a_0+a_1\)
## Finding
Let
\[
a_0\le a_1\le a_2\le a_3
\]
be positive integers, let
\[
\mathbf P=\mathbf P(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)
\]
be well formed, and let
\[
S=S_d\subset\mathbf P
\]
be a well-formed quasi-smooth hypersurface over \(\mathbf C\). Assume
\[
d>a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3.
\]
Then the first twist carrying a nonzero reflexive \(1\)-form is exactly
\[
a_0+a_1.
\]
More precisely,
\[
H^0\!\left(S,\left(\Omega_S^1\otimes\mathcal O_S(t)\right)^{\vee\vee}\right)=0
\qquad\text{for every integer }t<a_0+a_1,
\]
while
\[
H^0\!\left(S,\left(\Omega_S^1\otimes\mathcal O_S(a_0+a_1)\right)^{\vee\vee}\right)\ne0.
\]

On the smooth hypersurface stack, a nonzero section at the threshold is represented by the weighted Koszul form
\[
a_1x_1\,dx_0-a_0x_0\,dx_1.
\]
If the coarse hypersurface is smooth and contained in the regular locus of the ambient weighted projective space, the same threshold holds for the ordinary cotangent bundle.

For example, on the Fermat hypersurface
\[
x_0^{105}+x_1^{70}+x_2^{42}+x_3^{30}=0
\subset\mathbf P(2,3,5,7)
\]
of weighted degree \(210\), the first nonzero twisted reflexive \(1\)-forms occur in twist \(5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement uses the weighted polarization \(\mathcal O_S(1)\). For a quasi-smooth coarse surface, the relevant sheaf is the reflexive hull
\[
\left(\Omega_S^1\otimes\mathcal O_S(t)\right)^{\vee\vee},
\]
because quotient singularities may create torsion in the naive Kähler differential sheaf.

The inequality
\[
d>a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3
\]
is the general-type range used in the initiating weighted-hypersurface vanishing theorem. In particular it implies
\[
a_0+a_1<d.
\]
All varieties are over \(\mathbf C\).

## Proof
Let \(\mathcal S\) denote the smooth hypersurface stack associated with \(S\), and put
\[
A=\Omega_{\mathbf P}^1|_{\mathcal S},
\qquad
E=\bigoplus_{i=0}^3\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}(-a_i).
\]
The restricted weighted Euler sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the conormal sequence is
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}(-d)
\longrightarrow A
\longrightarrow\Omega_{\mathcal S}^1
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The line-bundle cohomology calculation for a quasi-smooth weighted hypersurface gives
\[
H^0(\mathcal S,\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}(k))=0\quad(k<0),
\qquad
H^1(\mathcal S,\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}(k))=0\quad(k\in\mathbf Z).
\]
After twisting the conormal sequence by an integer \(t<d\), these two vanishings show that
\[
H^0(\mathcal S,A(t))
\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
H^0(\mathcal S,\Omega_{\mathcal S}^1(t)).
\]

Write
\[
R=\mathbf C[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]/(F),
\qquad
\deg x_i=a_i.
\]
Taking global sections of the twisted Euler sequence identifies \(H^0(\mathcal S,A(t))\) with the weighted homogeneous relations
\[
\sum_{i=0}^3 a_ix_if_i=0\quad\text{in }R,
\qquad
\deg f_i=t-a_i.
\]
When \(t<d\), such a relation already holds in the polynomial ring
\[
P=\mathbf C[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3].
\]
Indeed, if the left side equals \(hF\) in \(P\), then \(h\) would have weighted degree \(t-d<0\), so \(h=0\).

The variables \(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3\) form a regular sequence in \(P\). Hence their first syzygy module is generated by the Koszul relations. A Koszul relation between \(x_i\) and \(x_j\) has weighted degree
\[
a_i+a_j.
\]
Because the weights are ordered, the least possible degree of a nonzero first syzygy is therefore
\[
\min_{i<j}(a_i+a_j)=a_0+a_1.
\]
Thus
\[
H^0(\mathcal S,A(t))=0
\qquad(t<a_0+a_1),
\]
and hence
\[
H^0(\mathcal S,\Omega_{\mathcal S}^1(t))=0
\qquad(t<a_0+a_1).
\]

At \(t=a_0+a_1\), the tuple
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)
=
(a_1x_1,-a_0x_0,0,0)
\]
lies in the kernel of the Euler map because
\[
a_0x_0(a_1x_1)+a_1x_1(-a_0x_0)=0.
\]
It is nonzero. Since \(a_0+a_1<d\), the conormal comparison above is an isomorphism on global sections at this twist, so it gives a nonzero section of
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal S}^1(a_0+a_1).
\]

For a well-formed quasi-smooth hypersurface, the stack-to-coarse comparison identifies the pushforward of \(\Omega_{\mathcal S}^1(t)\) with
\[
\left(\Omega_S^1\otimes\mathcal O_S(t)\right)^{\vee\vee}.
\]
Therefore the same exact threshold holds on the coarse surface. If \(S\) is smooth and lies in the regular locus of \(\mathbf P\), the stack and coarse hypersurface agree, giving the ordinary-cotangent statement.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs exact weighted-degree linear algebra for the Euler map
\[
\bigoplus_i P_{t-a_i}\longrightarrow P_t,
\qquad
(f_i)\longmapsto\sum_i a_ix_if_i.
\]
For every nondecreasing weight quadruple with entries between \(1\) and \(6\), it checks that the kernel is zero in every nonnegative twist below \(a_0+a_1\) and nonzero at \(a_0+a_1\). This is a finite consistency check of the Koszul-degree argument, not a replacement for the regular-sequence proof.

The script separately checks the concrete weights \((2,3,5,7)\): it finds kernel dimensions \(0\) for twists \(0,1,2,3,4\) and a positive kernel in twist \(5\). It also checks that the displayed Fermat equation has weighted degree \(210\) and that
\[
210>2+3+5+7.
\]
The stored replay output ends in `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Xu proves, for the same class of weighted hypersurface surfaces and under the same inequality on \(d\), the vanishing
\[
H^0\!\left(S,
\left(\operatorname{Sym}^m\Omega_S^1\otimes\mathcal O_S(t)\right)^{\vee\vee}
\right)=0
\qquad(t<m).
\]
For \(m=1\), this only gives vanishing below twist \(1\). The proof uses the weighted Euler and conormal sequences but estimates every weight merely by \(a_i\ge1\). The present statement instead determines the exact first twist for \(1\)-forms, namely \(a_0+a_1\), and proves sharpness by an explicit weighted Koszul form.

Earlier work of Araujo, Corrêa and Massarenti describes twisted \(1\)-forms on weighted projective spaces via the weighted Euler relation and studies restrictions to smooth weighted complete intersections of dimension at least \(3\). In particular, their restriction theorem assumes dimension at least \(3\), while the surfaces here have dimension \(2\). Their paper also treats twist \(2a_0\) when several smallest ambient weights coincide. It does not state the surface threshold \(a_0+a_1\) proved here.

Targeted searches for the threshold \(a_0+a_1\), its Koszul formulation, and equivalent first-twist statements located no result covering this surface theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns first symmetric order \(m=1\). It does not claim the exact first twist for higher symmetric powers.

The proof depends on the general-type inequality only through the fact that the threshold is below the hypersurface degree and through the initiating setting. It does not classify what happens when the hypersurface degree is at or below \(a_0+a_1\), where the defining equation can participate in low-degree relations.

The originality comparison is strongest against the initiating full text and the older weighted-complete-intersection restriction theorem. An unindexed source phrasing the same degree threshold through graded syzygies remains a residual risk.
