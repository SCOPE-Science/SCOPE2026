# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For twists below the hypersurface degree, the conormal sequence and the all-twist vanishing \(H^1(\mathcal O_{\mathcal S}(k))=0\) identify global \(1\)-forms on the hypersurface stack with global sections of the restricted ambient cotangent bundle. The weighted Euler sequence then turns the problem into first syzygies of the four coordinate variables. Below degree \(d\), every relation modulo the hypersurface equation lifts to the polynomial ring. The coordinate variables are a regular sequence, so the first syzygies are generated by the Koszul relations and have minimum weighted degree \(a_0+a_1\). The explicit boundary relation \((a_1x_1,-a_0x_0,0,0)\) is nonzero. Since \(d>\sum_i a_i\), the whole claimed range lies below \(d\). The stack-to-coarse comparison gives the reflexive statement.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating paper proves only \(t<m\), which for first-order forms is \(t<1\), and its proof explicitly discards weight information by using only \(a_i\ge1\). The closest older weighted-projective restriction result was inspected in full at the relevant statements: its standing assumptions require dimension at least \(3\), while the present hypersurfaces are surfaces, and its specialized theorem concerns repeated minimum weight and twists \(1\) and \(2\). Targeted exact and equivalent-formulation searches did not locate the \(a_0+a_1\) surface threshold. The elementary nature of the weighted Koszul step remains a stated residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The exact first twist is a natural polarization-sensitive invariant in the same weighted-differential problem as the initiating theorem. The statement is sharp on both sides and the gain over the published first-order bound can be arbitrarily large with the weights. It therefore gives a meaningful structural boundary rather than an arbitrary finite computation or a tiny numerical increment.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Xu, arXiv:2609.27625v1, especially Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.3, and Corollary 3.5. Araujo--Corrêa--Massarenti, arXiv:1702.04751v1, provides the closest older ambient weighted-Euler and higher-dimensional restriction framework. The result here treats only first-order differentials and does not determine sharp thresholds for higher symmetric powers or for hypersurface degrees at or below the first Koszul degree.
