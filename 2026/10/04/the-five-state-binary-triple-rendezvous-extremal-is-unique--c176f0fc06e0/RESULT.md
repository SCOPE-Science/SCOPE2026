# The five-state binary triple-rendezvous extremal is unique

## Finding
For a complete deterministic binary automaton \(\mathcal A=(Q,\{a,b\})\) with \(|Q|=5\), define \(m_3(\mathcal A)\) as the minimum length of a word that maps some three-element subset of \(Q\) to a singleton. Restricting to synchronizing automata, the exact maximum is
\[
\max m_3(\mathcal A)=7.
\]
Among the \(5^5\cdot5^5=9{,}765{,}625\) ordered labeled binary automata, exactly \(8{,}063{,}385\) are synchronizing and exactly \(240\) attain \(m_3=7\). Under state relabeling and interchange of the two letters, all \(240\) extremals lie in a single isomorphism class.

A canonical representative on \(Q=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) is
\[
a=(0,4,1,2,3),\qquad b=(4,1,2,1,0),
\]
where the tuples list the images of states \(0,1,2,3,4\). Here \(a\) is a permutation and \(b\) has rank \(4\). The triple \(\{1,3,4\}\) has shortest merging word `baaabab`, of length \(7\).

## Assumptions and scope
Automata are complete and deterministic, the alphabet has exactly two named letters during labeled enumeration, and synchronization means that some word maps all five states to a singleton. The extremal classification subsequently quotients by all state permutations and by swapping the two letters. This is a finite five-state result; no claim is made for arbitrary alphabet size or arbitrary \(n\).

## Proof
For each of the \(5^5=3125\) state maps, the program precomputes its action on all nonempty subsets of \(Q\). It then visits every ordered pair of maps. For each pair, the directed power automaton on nonempty subsets is reversed and breadth-first search starts simultaneously from the five singletons. The resulting distance of a subset is exactly the shortest length of a word mapping that subset to one state.

An ordered pair is synchronizing exactly when the full five-state subset receives a finite distance. For each synchronizing pair the value \(m_3\) is the minimum distance among the ten three-element subsets. Exhausting all ordered pairs gives the exact histogram
\[
1:4{,}183{,}905,\quad
2:3{,}369{,}480,\quad
3:387{,}600,\quad
4:93{,}600,\quad
5:23{,}760,\quad
6:4{,}800,\quad
7:240.
\]
These counts sum to \(8{,}063{,}385\), and no synchronizing binary five-state automaton has \(m_3>7\).

For every one of the \(240\) extremals, the program computes the lexicographically least encoding over all \(120\) state permutations and both letter orders. All \(240\) extremals have the same canonical encoding. Conversely, the orbit of the displayed representative under those transformations contains \(240\) distinct labeled ordered pairs. Hence the extremal orbit is unique and has trivial stabilizer in this action.

## Verification
`census.c` is the exhaustive proof program. Compiling it with a conforming C compiler and running it reproduces `census_output.txt`, including the full histogram, the maximum \(7\), the count \(240\), and one canonical orbit. `verify.py` independently checks shortest merging words for all ten triples of the canonical representative, its reset word, and the size \(240\) of its relabeling/letter-swap orbit; it also recompiles and replays the exhaustive census and requires byte-for-byte agreement with `census_output.txt`. A successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Gonze and Jungers introduced and bounded the triple-rendezvous viewpoint for synchronizing automata. Behague and Johnson formulate \(m(k,\Omega)\) and the extremal function \(rdv(k,n)\), and improve general upper bounds for merging some \(k\)-set; their 2020 paper is the literature anchor for this finite binary refinement. Their inspected material does not provide an exact exhaustive five-state binary census.

A 2026 preprint by Zhang gives a two-letter construction for every \(n\ge9\), with one permutation letter and one rank-\((n-1)\) letter, and its public artifact description says that additional finite enumerations and upper bounds for restricted classes accompany the article. The accessible description does not state a global five-state binary census or the unique-orbit claim. The supplement itself was not accessible in this run, so possible overlap with its restricted-class finite data is retained as an originality risk. The present statement is global over every ordered pair of five-state binary transition maps, not only the permutation/rank-four subclass.

## Limitations
The result is computational and specific to five states and two letters. It does not determine unrestricted \(rdv(3,5)\) when larger alphabets are allowed, nor does it imply a general formula in \(n\). The uniqueness assertion is only for the natural equivalence generated by state relabeling and exchange of the two input letters. A highly relevant 2026 technical supplement could not be inspected, so no claim of first discovery is made beyond the documented searches and accessible-source comparison.
