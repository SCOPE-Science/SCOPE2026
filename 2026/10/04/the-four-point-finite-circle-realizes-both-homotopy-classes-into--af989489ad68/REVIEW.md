# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The incidence presentation of \(P_2^2\) is independently reconstructed from Cianci--Ottina. The packaged verifier exhaustively checks all \(13^4\) set maps, the pointwise mapping-poset components, the mod-two homology computation, the exact identification of the \(132\) essential maps, and every beat-point deletion in the \(721\)-map component. The quantified claim is finite and exhaustively decided; no finite sample is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. Cianci--Ottina classify the smallest projective-plane models but do not compute this mapping poset. May's mapping-space theorem explains how components encode homotopy classes, and his subdivision discussion gives a general existence mechanism after subdivision, but neither result implies that the four-point source already realizes both classical loop classes. Exact searches for the map count \(853\), the component sizes \(721\) and \(132\), and equivalent finite-circle/projective-plane formulations found no covering statement. The older finite-pairing paper was checked at metadata level only, leaving a stated access risk.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves a natural finite-model question at the smallest source and target scales: whether barycentric subdivision is actually needed to realize the two classical loop classes of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). The answer is no for \(P_2^2\), and the proof gives an exact algebraic detector and internal homotopy structure of the mapping poset rather than only existence.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Cianci--Ottina for the target model, May/Stong for finite mapping spaces and homotopy fences, and Hardie--Vermeulen--Witbooi for older finite-space pairing/subdivision constructions. The claim is restricted to \(P_2^2\) and the four-point circle. The full Hardie--Vermeulen--Witbooi text was not available in the inspected source, so that source remains a residual originality risk.
