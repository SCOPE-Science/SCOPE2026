# The four-wheel second degeneracy fourfold has a six-component singular curve
## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In coordinates \((z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4,y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)\), consider
\[
Q_4=\begin{pmatrix}
z_1&y_1&0&y_4\\
y_1&z_2&y_2&0\\
0&y_2&z_3&y_3\\
y_4&0&y_3&z_4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(\Delta_4\subset\mathbb P^7\) be the projective variety cut out by the \(3\times3\) minors of \(Q_4\). Its reduced singular locus is exactly the union of six irreducible rational curves. Four are Veronese conics
\[
C_{12},C_{23},C_{34},C_{41},
\]
where, for example, \(C_{12}\) is parametrized by
\[
[s:t]\longmapsto[s^2:t^2:0:0:st:0:0:0],
\]
and the other three are obtained by cyclically shifting the indices. The remaining two components are the lines
\[
\ell_{13}=\{z_2=z_4=y_1=y_2=y_3=y_4=0\},\qquad
\ell_{24}=\{z_1=z_3=y_1=y_2=y_3=y_4=0\}.
\]
Writing \(P_i=[E_{ii}]\), the six components connect every pair of the four points \(P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4\) exactly once: the four conics connect adjacent indices and the two lines connect the opposite pairs. Thus the component-incidence picture is \(K_4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement is over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. It concerns the singularities of the second degeneracy scheme itself, not merely the singular locus of the wheel graph hypersurface. Denham--Schulze--Walther identify the wheel/whirl second degeneracy scheme with the submaximal-minor locus of the displayed cyclic symmetric matrix and prove that it is integral of codimension \(3\); these facts provide the ambient model and expected dimension used below.

## Proof
Embed the matrix space into \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{Sym}^2 k^4)\cong\mathbb P^9\), and let \(Z_2\) be the symmetric determinantal variety of matrices of rank at most \(2\). The variety \(\Delta_4\) is the linear section of \(Z_2\) by the two equations saying that the \((1,3)\) and \((2,4)\) entries vanish.

First suppose that a point \([A]\in\Delta_4\) has rank at most \(1\). Every differential of a \(3\times3\) minor is a linear combination of \(2\times2\) minors. All such minors vanish at rank at most \(1\), so the Jacobian of the defining minors vanishes there. Hence every rank-at-most-one point of \(\Delta_4\) is singular. Writing \(A=vv^t\) with \(v=(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)\), the two linear-section equations become
\[
u_1u_3=0,\qquad u_2u_4=0.
\]
Their reduced zero set in \(\mathbb P^3\) is the union of the four coordinate lines spanned by the adjacent basis pairs \((e_1,e_2),(e_2,e_3),(e_3,e_4),(e_4,e_1)\). The quadratic Veronese map sends these four lines to \(C_{12},C_{23},C_{34},C_{41}\).

Now let \([A]\) have rank exactly \(2\), and put \(K=\ker A\), so \(\dim K=2\). The symmetric rank-at-most-two variety \(Z_2\) is smooth at \([A]\), and its conormal space is canonically \(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\). The codimension-two linear space defining \(\Delta_4\) has conormal span generated by the symmetric tensors \(E_{13}\) and \(E_{24}\). Thus \([A]\) is singular on the linear section precisely when
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2K\cap\langle E_{13},E_{24}\rangle\ne0.
\]
A nonzero tensor \(aE_{13}+bE_{24}\) has rank \(4\) when \(ab\ne0\), whereas every element of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2K\) has rank at most \(2\). Therefore one of \(a,b\) is zero. If the tensor is a nonzero multiple of \(E_{13}\), its rank is \(2\), forcing \(K=\langle e_1,e_3\rangle\). Then symmetry and \(K=\ker A\) force all rows and columns \(1,3\) of \(A\) to vanish; the remaining section equation makes the \((2,4)\) entry zero. Hence \([A]\in\ell_{24}\). The converse is immediate at every rank-two point of that line. Interchanging the two pairs gives exactly \(\ell_{13}\). Hence there are no other rank-two singular points.

The four conics meet only at the coordinate rank-one points \(P_i\), according to the intersections of their source coordinate lines. The two extra lines join \(P_1\) to \(P_3\) and \(P_2\) to \(P_4\), respectively, and have no other intersections with the conics or with each other. This proves the claimed six-component reduced singular locus and its \(K_4\) incidence.

## Verification
The accompanying exact symbolic verifier constructs all distinct \(3\times3\) minors of \(Q_4\), checks that all six parametrized components lie in their common zero set, verifies Jacobian rank \(0\) at a generic rank-one point, rank less than \(3\) at generic points of both extra lines, and rank \(3\) at an explicit rank-two point off the predicted singular locus. It also verifies
\[
\det(aE_{13}+bE_{24})=a^2b^2,
\]
which is the key rank dichotomy in the transversality argument. Running `python3 verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Denham, Schulze, and Walther prove that the second degeneracy scheme of a connected configuration is reduced Cohen--Macaulay of codimension \(3\), and integral for a \(3\)-connected matroid. For wheels and whirls they give the cyclic symmetric matrix \(Q_n\) and identify the second degeneracy ideal with the submaximal minors; for \(n=4\) their proof realizes the variety as the section of the symmetric rank-at-most-two locus by the two missing opposite entries. Their paper does not determine the singular locus of this second degeneracy variety. Patterson's earlier result identifies the singular locus of a graph/configuration hypersurface set-theoretically with the corresponding rank locus; it does not classify the singularities of that rank locus itself. The present statement computes that next singular stratum for the smallest non-generic wheel case.

## Limitations
No claim is made about the scheme structure of the Jacobian singular scheme before reduction, local analytic types along the six curves, characteristic \(2\), or the analogous singular strata for \(n>4\). The proof uses the standard conormal description of the smooth rank-two stratum of a symmetric determinantal variety.
