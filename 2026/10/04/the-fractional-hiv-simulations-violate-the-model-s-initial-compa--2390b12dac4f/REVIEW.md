# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. Under the source's own Caputo–Fabrizio definition, the derivative of an absolutely continuous trajectory tends to zero at the initial time, forcing the autonomous vector field to vanish there. The exact source data give
\[
F(100,0,1)=(0.109,0.027,-2),
\]
so the displayed fractional initial-value problem has no absolutely continuous solution with that initial condition for \(0<\gamma<1\). Independently, the exact difference of consecutive exponential-memory integrals contains an old-history term omitted by the paper's derivation; a constant forcing gives an elementary exact counterexample to the replacement.

## Originality

PASS. The general Caputo–Fabrizio initial-compatibility defect is prior art and is explicitly treated as such. The source-specific contribution is the exact published-data incompatibility plus the independent comparison of Definition 2.2, equation (3.4), equation (4.3), and the equation-(4.6) history subtraction. Exact-title, DOI, correction, initial-condition, and numerical-equation searches found no published repair of these points.

## Value

PASS. The paper's main fractional conclusions are drawn from trajectories generated by the disputed recurrence. Since the published initial data are incompatible with the stated fractional equation and the numerical update is not derived from a consistent version of that equation, the plots cannot certify the claimed dynamics of the displayed model. A corrected study must specify a compatible formulation and retain the correct memory history.

## Closest literature and limitations

Diethelm et al. (2020), DOI 10.1515/fca-2020-0032, provide the closest prior general compatibility result. Shankar and Bora (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.fraope.2023.100043, provide related later theory. Neither source inspects the HIV paper's exact data or numerical-history algebra.

The result does not rule out oscillation or chaos in a different compatible fractional HIV model, and it does not challenge the order-one ordinary differential equation.
