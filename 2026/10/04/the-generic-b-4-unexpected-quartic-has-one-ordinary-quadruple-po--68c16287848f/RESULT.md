# The generic \(B_4\) unexpected quartic has one ordinary quadruple point
## Finding
Over \(\mathbb C\), let \(Z_{B_4}\subset\mathbb P^3\) be the 16-point projectivized root configuration of type \(B_4\). There is a nonempty Zariski-open set \(U\subset\mathbb P^3\) such that for every \(P\in U\), the unique unexpected quartic \(S_P\) through \(Z_{B_4}\) with multiplicity four at \(P\) is a normal cone whose singular locus is exactly \(\{P\}\). The point \(P\) is an ordinary quadruple point: its projectivized tangent cone is a smooth plane quartic of genus \(3\). Blowing up \(P\) resolves \(S_P\) in one step; the exceptional curve \(E\) has \(E^2=-4\) and discrepancy \(a(E,S_P)=-2\). Consequently \(S_P\) is not log canonical at \(P\).

The literature establishes the existence and uniqueness of the degree-four cone for a general vertex, but it does not classify the singularity of the cone. The additional statement here is that the generic base curve is smooth, which makes the vertex the only singular point and determines the resolution and discrepancy.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(Z_{B_4}\subset\mathbb P^3\) be the 16 projective points represented by the four coordinate vectors and the twelve vectors \(e_i\pm e_j\) with \(0\le i<j\le3\). The parameter \(P\) is restricted to a nonempty Zariski-open subset of \(\mathbb P^3\). No assertion is made about the special parameter locus outside that open set.

Harbourne--Migliore--Nagel--Teitler show that for a general \(P\) there is a unique unexpected quartic through \(Z_{B_4}\) with multiplicity four at \(P\), and hence that it is a cone. Di Gennaro--Ilardi--Miró-Roig--Szemberg--Szpond later write an explicit bihomogeneous equation for this \(B_4\) cone family.

## Proof
Use homogeneous coordinates \([x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]\). Specialize the explicit cone family at
\[
P=[1:2:4:8].
\]
After dividing by a nonzero scalar and making the linear change
\[
x_0=y_0,\qquad x_1=2y_0+y_1,\qquad x_2=4y_0+y_2,\qquad x_3=8y_0+y_3,
\]
the equation becomes independent of \(y_0\) and equals
\[
\begin{aligned}
f={}&84y_1^3y_2-10y_1^3y_3-48y_1^2y_2y_3-84y_1y_2^3+120y_1y_2^2y_3\\
&-48y_1y_2y_3^2+10y_1y_3^3+y_2^3y_3-y_2y_3^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the specialized surface is the cone over the plane quartic \(C=V(f)\subset\mathbb P^2_{[y_1:y_2:y_3]}\).

For each of the three standard affine patches \(y_i=1\), the ideal generated by the three partial derivatives of \(f\) has reduced Gröbner basis \([1]\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Therefore the three partial derivatives have no common projective zero, so \(C\) is smooth. The exact checker also verifies directly that the specialized quartic vanishes at all 16 points of \(Z_{B_4}\), and that the linear system of quartics through those 16 points with multiplicity four at \([1:2:4:8]\) has dimension one.

Smoothness of the plane-quartic base is an open condition in the parameter wherever the one-dimensional cone family is defined. Since the explicit witness above is smooth and the source establishes uniqueness on a dense open set, their intersection gives a nonempty Zariski-open set \(U\) on which the base curve remains smooth.

For \(P\in U\), the cone is smooth away from its vertex: after sending \(P\) to \([1:0:0:0]\), its equation is a homogeneous quartic in the three transverse variables, and the base gradient never vanishes. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(S_P)=\{P\}.
\]
A hypersurface surface is Cohen--Macaulay, and the singular locus here has codimension two, so Serre's \(R_1+S_2\) criterion makes \(S_P\) normal. The multiplicity at \(P\) is four and its projectivized tangent cone is the smooth plane quartic \(C\); therefore \(P\) is an ordinary quadruple point. By the plane genus formula, \(g(C)=3\).

Blowing up the vertex of a cone over a smooth curve resolves it. The exceptional curve is \(E\cong C\) with normal bundle \(\mathcal O_C(-1)\), so
\[
E^2=-\deg\mathcal O_C(1)=-4.
\]
Because \(S_P\subset\mathbb P^3\) is a quartic hypersurface, adjunction gives \(K_{S_P}\sim0\). Writing
\[
K_Y=\pi^*K_{S_P}+aE
\]
for the blow-up resolution and applying adjunction on \(E\),
\[
(K_Y+E)\cdot E=2g(E)-2=4.
\]
Since \(E^2=-4\), this gives \((a+1)(-4)=4\), hence \(a=-2\). Thus the singularity is not log canonical.

## Relationship to prior work
Harbourne--Migliore--Nagel--Teitler identify the \(B_4\) configuration, prove that the generic unexpected hypersurface has degree four and multiplicity four at the general point, and therefore note that it is a cone. They also determine the 24-point common base locus as the vertex varies. Di Gennaro--Ilardi--Miró-Roig--Szemberg--Szpond give an explicit equation for the \(B_4\) unexpected cone and show that the same cones arise from \(F_4\). The inspected statements do not assert that the generic plane-quartic base is smooth, do not identify the singular locus as the single vertex, and do not compute the exceptional curve or discrepancy.

Targeted semantic searches for the \(B_4\) cone together with the phrases “smooth plane quartic,” “ordinary quadruple point,” “genus 3,” and “discrepancy” found no statement covering this classification. The closest published database hits concern unrelated quartic Cremona maps, apolar quartics, and local Poisson singularities and do not imply the present claim.

## Limitations
The claim is generic in the vertex \(P\). It does not classify the discriminant locus where the plane-quartic base becomes singular, nor the resulting special cone singularities. The proof is over characteristic zero; no positive-characteristic analogue is asserted. The originality comparison is limited to the inspected primary sources, the closest downstream treatment, and the recorded targeted database searches.
