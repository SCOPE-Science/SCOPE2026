# The inner symmetry group of the prism quandle is not its orbit group
## Finding
Let
\[
P_7=\operatorname{GAlex}(Q_8	imes C_7,	heta)
\]
be the order-
\(56\) prism quandle used by Jabłonowski, where \(	heta\) acts by the order-three automorphism \(lpha\) on \(Q_8\) and by inversion on \(C_7\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)
\cong
(Q_8times_lpha C_3)	imes(C_7times_{-1}C_2)
\cong
T^*	imes D_{14},
\]
so \(|\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)|=336\).

The same realization paper constructs a geometric orbit group
\[
\Lambda\cong T^*	imes_{\{\pm1\}}Q_{28}
\]
of order \(336\). These two groups are not isomorphic. The paper's distinguished element \(f\in\Lambda\) satisfies
\[
f^6=-1,
\]
so \(f\) has order \(12\), whereas \(T^*	imes D_{14}\) has no element of order \(12\).

Thus the intrinsic inner symmetry group of the finite quandle and the geometric orbit symmetry group in its fibred realization have the same order but different group structure.

## Assumptions and scope
For a quandle \(Q\), \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q)\) is the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Sym}(Q)\) generated by its right translations. For a generalized Alexander quandle \(\operatorname{GAlex}(\Gamma,	heta)\), the operation is
\[
x*y=	heta(xy^{-1})y.
\]

Here \(\Gamma=Q_8	imes C_7\), and Jabłonowski defines \(	heta=(lpha,\iota)\), where \(lpha\) cyclically permutes \(i,j,k\in Q_8\) and \(\iota\) is inversion on \(C_7\). Hence \(	heta\) has order \(6\). The paper proves that the displacement subgroup generated by the generalized-Alexander translations is all of \(\Gamma\).

The notation \(T^*\) denotes the binary tetrahedral group \(Q_8times_lpha C_3\), and \(D_{14}\) denotes the dihedral group \(C_7times_{-1}C_2\) of order \(14\).

## Proof
For \(y\in\Gamma\), let \(S_y\) be the right translation of \(P_7\), and let \(R_g\) denote right multiplication by \(g\in\Gamma\). From the generalized Alexander operation,
\[
S_y(x)=	heta(xy^{-1})y
      =	heta(x)	heta(y)^{-1}y.
\]
Writing
\[
\delta_y=	heta(y)^{-1}y,
\]
we obtain
\[
S_y=R_{\delta_y}	heta.
\]
In particular, \(S_e=	heta\), and therefore
\[
S_yS_e^{-1}=R_{\delta_y}.
\]
The source proves that the displacement elements \(\delta_y\) generate all of \(\Gamma\). Hence the full regular right-multiplication subgroup \(R_\Gamma\) lies in \(\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)\). Conversely every \(S_y\) belongs to the subgroup generated by \(R_\Gamma\) and \(	heta\), so
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)=R_\Gammatimes\langle	hetaangle.
\]
The intersection is trivial: every power of \(	heta\) fixes the identity element of \(\Gamma\), whereas a nonidentity right multiplication does not. Since \(|\Gamma|=56\) and \(|	heta|=6\),
\[
|\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)|=56\cdot6=336.
\]

Because the order-three and order-two parts of \(	heta\) act on separate direct factors,
\[
(Q_8	imes C_7)times_	heta C_6
\cong
(Q_8times_lpha C_3)	imes(C_7times_{-1}C_2),
\]
which is \(T^*	imes D_{14}\).

To distinguish this group from \(\Lambda\), first note that the element orders in \(T^*\) are among \(1,2,3,4,6\), while those in \(D_{14}\) are among \(1,2,7\). An element of a direct product has order the least common multiple of the component orders, so no element of \(T^*	imes D_{14}\) has order \(12\).

Jabłonowski's orbit-group extension has a distinguished lift \(f\) satisfying \(f^6=-1\), where \(-1\) is the nontrivial central involution. Therefore \(f\) has order \(12\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)
ot\cong\Lambda.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
Jabłonowski identifies the associated marked group of \(P_7\), proves that the quandle has exactly two fibred realizations, and separately computes the order-
\(336\) orbit group \(\Lambda\). The preprint does not identify \(\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)\) or compare it with \(\Lambda\).

General work on generalized Alexander quandles and associated groups explains how inner automorphism groups and central extensions arise, but the explicit split identification
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)\cong T^*	imes D_{14}
\]
and its nonisomorphism with the geometric group \(\Lambda\) were not found in the inspected literature.

The distinction is substantive because the two natural groups have the same order \(336\). The order-
\(12\) witness shows that the geometric circle-action symmetry contains information not captured by merely taking the intrinsic inner automorphism group of the finite quandle.

## Limitations
The result distinguishes \(\operatorname{Inn}(P_7)\) from the particular geometric orbit group \(\Lambda\) in the cited realization. It does not classify all automorphisms \(\operatorname{Aut}(P_7)\), nor does it assert that the full automorphism group fails to contain a subgroup isomorphic to \(\Lambda\).

The argument also does not alter the source's classification of the two fibred realizations or its associated-group calculation. It isolates a separate intrinsic-versus-geometric symmetry comparison.
