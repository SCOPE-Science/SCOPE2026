# The invariably generating graph of a two-generated finite \(p\)-group

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite noncyclic two-generated \(p\)-group, let
\[
\Phi=\Phi(G),
\]
and let \(\Lambda(G)\) be the invariably generating graph whose vertices are the conjugacy classes of \(G\) other than \(\{1\}\), with two vertices adjacent exactly when the two classes invariably generate \(G\).

Then the isolated vertices of \(\Lambda(G)\) are exactly the conjugacy classes contained in \(\Phi\).

By Burnside's basis theorem,
\[
G/\Phi\cong C_p^2.
\]
For each projective point
\[
\ell\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p),
\]
let \(m_\ell\) be the number of conjugacy classes \(C\) of \(G\) whose image in \(G/\Phi\) is a nonzero vector belonging to \(\ell\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda^+(G)\cong
K_{(m_\ell)_{\ell\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)}}.
}
\]
Thus the non-isolated invariably generating graph is a complete \((p+1)\)-partite graph.

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\omega(\Lambda^+(G))
=
\chi(\Lambda^+(G))
=
p+1,
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{diam}(\Lambda^+(G))=2.
}
\]
Each part has size at least
\[
\boxed{p-1.}
\]

Equivalently, two non-isolated conjugacy classes are nonadjacent precisely when their images in the Frattini quotient lie on the same projective line.

## Assumptions and scope

The group is finite, noncyclic, and generated by two elements. Since it is a \(p\)-group,
\[
G/\Phi(G)
\]
is an elementary abelian \(p\)-group, and two-generation together with noncyclicity forces
\[
G/\Phi(G)\cong \mathbf F_p^2.
\]

The graph uses conjugacy classes as vertices, following the standard definition of the invariably generating graph. The reduced graph
\[
\Lambda^+(G)
\]
is obtained by deleting isolated vertices.

The theorem does not claim that the multipartite part sizes \(m_\ell\) are equal. They can depend on the distribution of conjugacy classes among Frattini cosets.

## Proof

Let
\[
\pi:G\longrightarrow G/\Phi
\]
be the quotient map.

For every finite \(p\)-group,
\[
[G,G]\le\Phi.
\]
Hence conjugate elements have the same image modulo \(\Phi\): if
\[
x^g=g^{-1}xg,
\]
then
\[
x^{-1}x^g\in[G,G]\le\Phi,
\]
so
\[
\pi(x^g)=\pi(x).
\]
Therefore every conjugacy class is contained in a single coset of \(\Phi\).

Burnside's basis theorem says that, for \(x,y\in G\),
\[
\langle x,y\rangle=G
\]
if and only if
\[
\pi(x),\pi(y)
\]
span \(G/\Phi\).

Because conjugation does not change either image in \(G/\Phi\), the same criterion holds uniformly over arbitrary choices of conjugates. Thus for conjugacy classes \(C,D\),
\[
\langle C,D\rangle_I=G
\]
if and only if, for representatives \(x\in C\) and \(y\in D\),
\[
\pi(x),\pi(y)
\]
are linearly independent in \(\mathbf F_p^2\).

If \(C\subseteq\Phi\), then \(\pi(C)=0\), so \(C\) is isolated.

Conversely, suppose
\[
C\nsubseteq\Phi.
\]
Then \(\pi(C)\ne0\). Choose a vector \(v\in G/\Phi\) linearly independent from \(\pi(C)\), and choose \(y\in G\) with \(\pi(y)=v\). The conjugacy class of \(y\) is adjacent to \(C\). Hence no class outside \(\Phi\) is isolated.

Now partition the non-isolated conjugacy classes according to the one-dimensional subspace
\[
\ell=\langle\pi(C)\rangle
\]
of \(G/\Phi\). There are
\[
|\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_p)|=p+1
\]
such parts.

If two classes lie in the same part, their nonzero images are linearly dependent, so they are not adjacent. If they lie in different parts, their images are linearly independent and they are adjacent. This is exactly the adjacency relation of a complete \((p+1)\)-partite graph.

Every complete multipartite graph with at least two nonempty parts has clique number and chromatic number equal to its number of parts. Here all \(p+1\) parts are nonempty, so
\[
\omega(\Lambda^+(G))
=
\chi(\Lambda^+(G))
=
p+1.
\]
Since \(p+1\ge3\), two vertices in the same part have a common neighbor in any other part, while vertices in different parts are adjacent. Hence the diameter is \(2\).

Finally, a projective line contains \(p-1\) nonzero vectors, equivalently \(p-1\) nonzero Frattini cosets. Each such coset contains at least one conjugacy class. Therefore
\[
m_\ell\ge p-1
\]
for every projective line \(\ell\).

## Verification

The included replay constructs four finite two-generated \(p\)-groups directly from multiplication laws:
\[
D_8,\qquad C_4\times C_4,\qquad C_9\times C_3,
\qquad H_3,
\]
where \(H_3\) is the Heisenberg group of order \(27\).

For each example it independently:

- enumerates the entire subgroup lattice;
- finds the maximal subgroups and their intersection \(\Phi(G)\);
- enumerates all conjugacy classes;
- checks invariable generation by testing every pair of representatives from each pair of conjugacy classes;
- verifies that the isolated vertices are exactly the classes contained in \(\Phi(G)\);
- assigns each non-isolated class to the unique maximal subgroup corresponding to its projective line in \(G/\Phi(G)\);
- verifies edge-for-edge that classes are adjacent exactly when their projective-line labels differ;
- confirms that the number of parts is \(p+1\) and every part has size at least \(p-1\).

For example, \(D_8\) has one isolated nonidentity conjugacy class and
\[
\Lambda^+(D_8)\cong K_3.
\]
For the Heisenberg group of order \(27\),
\[
\Lambda^+(H_3)\cong K_{2,2,2,2}.
\]

The replay returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite computation is not used to prove the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Garzoni introduced the invariably generating graph in work on alternating and symmetric groups. A later paper on connected components recalls the definition, observes that conjugacy classes contained in the Frattini subgroup are isolated, and explicitly notes that generation and invariable generation coincide for finite nilpotent groups.

Harper and Lucchini study the ordinary generating graph of finite nilpotent groups, proving strong connectivity and Hamiltonicity properties for its non-isolated part.

The result here passes from those general observations to an exact conjugacy-class graph description for every noncyclic two-generated finite \(p\)-group. The decisive additional step is the projective geometry of the rank-two Frattini quotient: conjugacy classes outside \(\Phi(G)\) are partitioned by the \(p+1\) projective lines of
\[
G/\Phi(G)\cong\mathbf F_p^2,
\]
and adjacency is exactly inequality of those line labels.

Targeted searches using the invariably generating graph, Frattini quotient, two-generated \(p\)-groups, complete multipartite graphs, clique number, and chromatic number did not locate this exact statement.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns groups with generator rank exactly two. For \(d(G)>2\), no pair generates \(G\), so the two-vertex invariably generating graph has no edges.

The complete multipartite decomposition determines several graph invariants but does not determine the part sizes from \(p\) alone. Those sizes retain conjugacy-class information specific to \(G\).

The proof is an elementary consequence of Burnside's basis theorem once the conjugacy-class version of the graph is considered. An equivalent statement could therefore exist in generating-graph literature under different terminology.
