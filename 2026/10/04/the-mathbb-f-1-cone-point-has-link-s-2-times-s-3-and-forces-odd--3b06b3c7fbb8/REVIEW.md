# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Wuebben's theorem identifies the unique singularity as the anticanonical cone over \(\mathbb F_1\) and gives a smooth crepant resolution with Hodge numbers \((4,108)\). The singularity link is the unit circle bundle of \(K_{\mathbb F_1}\). Exact intersection arithmetic, the integral Gysin sequence, and the parity of \(w_2\) show that this is a simply connected spin \(5\)-manifold with \(H_2\cong\mathbf Z\), so Smale--Barden gives \(S^2\times S^3\). Euler additivity then gives \(-208-4+1=-211\). The odd Euler number is incompatible with rational Poincaré duality in formal dimension \(6\).

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating full text states the cone type, nonsmoothability, and the resolution Hodge data but not the link diffeomorphism type, the singular-space Euler number, or the Poincaré-duality consequence. Gross's classical treatment of the exceptional \(\mathbb F_1\) contraction was also inspected; the relevant sections do not state these fixed-object invariants. Exact-number, link-topology, and equivalent-formulation searches did not locate a covering statement. A residual risk remains that the local link computation appears under different terminology in older singularity literature.

## Value
**PASS.** The result gives a complete local topological identification and a global ordinary topological invariant for Wuebben's minimal one-singularity nonsmoothable Calabi--Yau example. The odd Euler characteristic shows that the underlying singular space fails rational Poincaré duality, a structural distinction not contained in the source's deformation-theoretic obstruction.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Wuebben, arXiv:2609.28499v1, Theorem 5.6, and Gross, arXiv:alg-geom/9506022, on primitive contractions with exceptional divisor \(\mathbb F_1\). The calculation does not determine the full ordinary cohomology ring, intersection cohomology, or mixed Hodge structure of \(X_C\).
