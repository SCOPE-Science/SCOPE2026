# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof handles the boundary case \(n=6\) by symmetry and every \(n>6\) analytically. Simmons' inequality gives the first sign, and the displayed exact factorization gives the last sign. Since mean three produces exactly three coefficients, either possible sign of the middle coefficient yields condition \(L_1\). The algebraic certificate is replayable, but the theorem does not depend on finite enumeration.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source is the paper that defines left-loadedness and states Conjecture 4.1. Its full text explicitly records binomial proofs for \(m\in\{1,2\}\) and for \(4\le m\le n/3\), not for \(m=3\). Simmons' theorem gives only the first coefficient. Exact semantic, bibliographic, and statement searches did not find a later theorem covering the complete mean-three slice. The remaining risk is a differently phrased or poorly indexed later proof.

## Value
PASS. The mean-three slice is the isolated gap between the source's small-mean cases and its higher-mean range, and the theorem covers every admissible \(n\ge6\). It therefore resolves a natural complete slice of an explicit probability conjecture rather than an arbitrary parameter check.

## Closest literature and limitations
The motivating 2022 paper is the closest source and explicitly identifies the unresolved general conjecture. Perrin--Redside and Simmons cover the near-mean tail imbalance used for \(\alpha_1\), but not the far-tail sign needed for left-loadedness. The result does not settle arbitrary mean.
