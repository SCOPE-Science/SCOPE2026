# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The graph-to-rooted-forest model is the graph specialization of acyclic discrete vector fields. All 3^9 edge states are exhaustively classified. The resulting face vector is independently reproduced by det(lambda I+L)=lambda(lambda+6)(lambda+3)^4. A complete 855-pair matching on all 1791 nonempty faces is checked against all 6894 Hasse covers, and the reversed Hasse orientation is acyclic. Its critical profile is exactly one dimension-0 face and eighty dimension-4 faces. Forman's theorem then gives a CW model with exactly those cells, hence a wedge of eighty 4-spheres. Independent mod-2 boundary elimination gives reduced Betti numbers (0,0,0,0,80).

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source explicitly says the complete-bipartite Morse-complex homotopy type was unknown and provides only connectivity bounds. Later checked families do not include \(K_{3,3}\). Exact-claim and alias searches in the published semantic index and on the web found no statement covering or implying the 80-sphere decomposition. The closest own result is the separate family \(K_{2,n}\), which does not imply the \(K_{3,3}\) case.

Closest literature: arXiv:2004.10481, especially Example 4.6, gives complete-bipartite connectivity bounds while explicitly presenting the exact homotopy type as unknown there. arXiv:1909.11440 and arXiv:2207.13780 compute other graph families and do not cover \(K_{3,3}\). The closest own published claim is the \(K_{2,n}\) family, which does not imply the present case.

Residual limitation: a very recent, non-indexed, or differently phrased duplicate cannot be ruled out by search alone.

## Value
PASS. \(K_{3,3}\) is the smallest balanced complete bipartite graph beyond the star and \(K_{2,n}\) regimes and sits directly inside an explicit literature gap about complete-bipartite Morse complexes. An exact homotopy type is substantially stronger than the known connectivity bound. The result is a natural benchmark for any future formula for \(\mathcal M(\(K_{p,q}\))\), and the 80-sphere decomposition supplies a reusable exact test case rather than an arbitrary finite slice.
