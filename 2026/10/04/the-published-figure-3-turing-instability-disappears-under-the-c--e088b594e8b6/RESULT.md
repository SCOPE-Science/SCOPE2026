# The published Figure 3 Turing instability disappears under the correct Jacobian

## Finding

Consider the reaction terms
\[
f(u,v)=u\left(\frac{bu}{c+u}-e-hu\right)-\frac{luv}{1+mu+pv},
\]
\[
g(u,v)=\frac{qluv}{1+mu+pv}\frac{v}{r+v}-sv.
\]
The 2024 source uses these terms in a diagonal-diffusion system and states that its Figure 3 parameter set is unstable for \(d_1=0.01\) and \(d_2=10\).

For the printed Figure 3 parameters
\[
s=0.2,\ b=3,\ c=0.5,\ e=0.2,\ h=0.4,\ l=0.5,\ m=1,\ p=0,\ q=0.56,\ r=1,
\]
the displayed coexistence state is the positive root
\[
(u_0,v_0)\approx(3.880786577827113,8.836967740350949).
\]
Using the reaction terms exactly as printed, the correct Jacobian is
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}
f_u&f_v\\
g_u&g_v
\end{pmatrix}
\approx
\begin{pmatrix}
-0.529188015870781&-0.397557495697221\\
0.093309032858106&0.020331468525571
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{tr}J\approx-0.508856547345211<0,
\qquad
\det J\approx0.0263365359402125>0.
\]

For a Laplacian mode with \(\mu=k^2\ge0\), the characteristic polynomial has trace and determinant
\[
\tau(\mu)=\operatorname{tr}J-(d_1+d_2)\mu,
\]
\[
\delta(\mu)=\det J-(d_2f_u+d_1g_v)\mu+d_1d_2\mu^2.
\]
At \(d_1=0.01\), \(d_2=10\),
\[
d_2f_u+d_1g_v\approx-5.29167684402256<0,
\]
so
\[
\tau(\mu)\approx-0.508856547345211-10.01\mu<0
\]
and
\[
\delta(\mu)\approx0.0263365359402125+5.29167684402256\mu+0.1\mu^2>0
\]
for every \(\mu\ge0\). Therefore no Fourier or Neumann Laplacian mode is linearly unstable.

The discrepancy is numerically large. Figure 3(a) marks a negative value near \(\delta=-0.06913\) at \(k^2=0.83\), whereas the printed model gives
\[
\delta(0.83)\approx4.48731831648.
\]
The source also defines \(a_{22}=g_u\) and \(a_{21}=g_v\) in the displayed Jacobian setup. For the matrix ordering used immediately above and in the Taylor system immediately below, the correct assignment is \(a_{21}=g_u\), \(a_{22}=g_v\).

Hence the source's Figure 3(a) is not the linear dispersion relation of the printed reaction-diffusion model at the printed equilibrium and parameters. Figure 3(b) may still show a finite-amplitude spatial state, but it cannot be justified as a Turing pattern from linear instability of that equilibrium without changing some ingredient of the published calculation.

## Assumptions and scope

The calculation uses the equations and parameter values printed in the source, with positive coexistence equilibrium determined by \(f(u_0,v_0)=g(u_0,v_0)=0\). The conclusion is about linear Turing instability of that equilibrium. It does not exclude nonlinear spatial structure generated by a large perturbation, multistability, numerical boundary effects, or an undocumented alternative model.

For \(p=0\), the positive-equilibrium equations reduce to
\[
v=\frac{5(1+u)}{2u-5},
\]
while the prey equation becomes
\[
16u^3-144u^2+314u+15=0.
\]
The root near \(3.8808\) reproduces the equilibrium printed in the Figure 3 initial condition.

## Proof

Let \(D=1+mu+pv\). At a positive equilibrium, differentiation gives
\[
f_u=u\left(\frac{bc}{(c+u)^2}-h+\frac{lmv}{D^2}\right),
\qquad
f_v=-\frac{lu(1+mu)}{D^2},
\]
\[
g_u=\frac{qlv^2(1+pv)}{D^2(r+v)},
\qquad
g_v=s\left(\frac{1+mu}{D}-\frac{v}{r+v}\right).
\]
The formula for \(g_v\) uses the positive-equilibrium identity \(qluv/[D(r+v)]=s\).

Substitution of the Figure 3 equilibrium yields the matrix and signs in the Finding section. For diagonal diffusion \(D_0=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2)\), a Laplacian mode with eigenvalue \(-\mu\) is governed by \(J-\mu D_0\). Its characteristic polynomial has
\[
\tau(\mu)=f_u+g_v-(d_1+d_2)\mu
\]
and
\[
\delta(\mu)=f_ug_v-f_vg_u-(d_2f_u+d_1g_v)\mu+d_1d_2\mu^2.
\]
For the Figure 3 values, the constant, linear, and quadratic coefficients of \(\delta\) are all positive, and \(\tau(\mu)\) is strictly negative. Every modal eigenvalue therefore has negative real part.

This conclusion is robust to the printed rounding of the equilibrium: the decisive coefficient \(d_2f_u+d_1g_v\) is about \(-5.29\), far from zero, and the zero-mode determinant is about \(2.63\times10^{-2}\), also strictly positive.

## Relationship to prior work

Li and Li (2024), DOI 10.3934/math.20241309, is the primary source. Its Section 2 prints the reaction terms, its Jacobian setup interchanges the labels \(g_u\) and \(g_v\), and its Figure 3 text states that the \(d_1=0.01\), \(d_2=10\) case is unstable. The numerical panel marks a negative dispersion determinant near \(k^2=0.83\).

Earlier and contemporary reaction-diffusion predator-prey studies with Allee effects analyze Turing instability in different systems. Those results establish that diffusion-driven instability is biologically and mathematically plausible in related models, but they do not imply instability for this parameter point. Searches by the exact title, DOI, Figure 3 equilibrium, marked dispersion value, and Jacobian terms did not locate an erratum or a published correction of this source-specific calculation.

The correction is therefore deliberately narrow: it does not challenge generic Turing criteria or the possibility of Allee-driven pattern formation in other parameter regimes. It establishes that the particular published Figure 3 instability claim is inconsistent with the model and numbers printed in the same article.

## Limitations

The source code and raw numerical data for the published simulations were not available in the inspected material. Consequently, the calculation identifies the incompatibility but does not determine whether it arose from a transcription error, a different internal parameter set, a Jacobian-indexing mistake, or another implementation issue. The finite-amplitude pattern panel is not reproduced here and is not claimed to be impossible.
