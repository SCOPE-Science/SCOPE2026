# Same-model review

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is obtained by direct comparison and exact cancellation of the printed state equations. In the unit-control limit, the controlled incidence remains \(\lambda SI/(1+A)\), not \(\lambda SI/(1+cA)\); for positive \(ASI\) these agree only when \(c=1\). Summing the six human compartments gives the residual source \((1-u_1)A(r_1I+r_2Q+r_3H)\), because the paper multiplies the three recovery losses by \(u_1\) but leaves the recovered gain unscaled. Both statements were replayed with exact rational witnesses using the source's parameter values.

The repair is also checked symbolically: putting the same \(u_1\) factor on the recovered gain cancels recovery internally, and differentiating the repaired Hamiltonian yields the required costate differences \(\xi_3-\xi_6\), \(\xi_4-\xi_6\), and \(\xi_5-\xi_6\). The only normalization-dependent feature is the scalar denominator attached to the quadratic control cost.

## Originality — PASS
The primary article was inspected across its uncontrolled state system, controlled state system, Hamiltonian/control characterization, parameter table, and numerical-control discussion. It presents the controlled equations as the intervention model but does not state the two consistency failures or the corrected costate structure. Searches using the title, DOI, unit-control reduction, transfer conservation, and alternative optimality wording found no published correction covering this result.

The closest directly relevant earlier paper by Al Basir et al. (2023) uses a balanced treatment transfer, with the same control factor on the infected loss and recovered gain. That construction is compatible with the repair but does not imply the source-specific incidence mismatch, total-population residual, or corrected seven-state stationarity formula established here.

## Value — PASS
These are not cosmetic sign checks. The control section supplies the trajectories and Pontryagin system used to compare interventions. Failure of the unit-control limit means the “controlled” and “uncontrolled” simulations are generated by different incidence laws even when the controls are nominally inactive. Failure of recovery transfer balance changes the human population equation whenever treatment control is below one, and repairing that transfer changes the first-order condition for \(u_1\). The finding therefore identifies a structural defect in the mathematical object optimized by the paper and gives a precise minimal correction.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inspected awareness-treatment control model is Al Basir et al., Axioms 12 (2023), 608, DOI 10.3390/axioms12060608; its treatment transfer is balanced. General optimal-control references provide the Pontryagin differentiation rule but do not compare the two state systems in the 2026 article.

The result is limited to the equations as printed. It does not derive a complete repaired adjoint system, recompute numerical policies, or claim what the authors intended beyond those equations. An unstated rescaling of awareness could only explain the incidence denominator after corresponding transformations of all other awareness-dependent coefficients; no such full transformation is part of the printed control model.
