# The sharp line–conic Hadamard quartic has a twisted-cubic singular locus
## Finding
Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, consider the line \(X\subset\mathbb P^3\) and conic \(Y\subset\mathbb P^3\) from Example 4.3 of Calussi–Carlini–Fatabbi–Lorenzini. Their Hadamard product \(S=X\star Y\) is the singular quartic surface used there to show sharpness of the lower bound on the singular-locus dimension. Its reduced singular locus is exactly a twisted cubic.

In coordinates \([z_0:z_1:z_2:z_3]\), the reduced singular curve is cut out by
\[
q_1=294z_0z_1-494z_1^2-2655z_1z_2-147z_1z_3-1080z_2^2,
\]
\[
q_2=1110z_0z_1+1800z_0z_2+230z_1^2+957z_1z_2+1209z_1z_3-9720z_2z_3,
\]
and
\[
q_3=32400z_0^2+152346z_0z_1+88200z_0z_2-191160z_0z_3+10334z_1^2+23079z_1z_2+491835z_1z_3+87480z_3^2.
\]

A homogeneous parametrization of this curve is
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0&=6(U+4W)(225U^2+1070UW+1269W^2),\\
p_1&=36(5U+11W)W^2,\\
p_2&=(5U+11W)(49U+115W)W,\\
p_3&=(2U+5W)(125U^2+850UW+1409W^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficient matrix of these four binary cubics has rank four, so this map is a projective linear re-embedding of the standard degree-three Veronese map \(\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^3\). Thus its image is a twisted cubic.

## Assumptions and scope
The calculation is over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The source line is
\[
x_0+x_1+x_2+2x_3=0,\qquad x_0-x_1+4x_2-x_3=0,
\]
and the source conic is the intersection of
\[
x_0+2x_1+3x_2+x_3=0
\]
with
\[
x_0^2+2x_0x_2+2x_0x_3+x_1^2+2x_1x_2-2x_1x_3+x_2^2+2x_2x_3+x_3^2=0.
\]
The result identifies the reduced singular locus. It does not classify the full nonreduced Jacobian scheme or the local analytic type at every point of the double curve.

## Proof
Parametrize the line by
\[
[-(5p+q):3(p-q):2p:2q]
\]
and the conic by
\[
[-9u^2-16uv-4v^2:2u^2:2uv:5u^2+10uv+4v^2].
\]
Coordinatewise multiplication gives a bihomogeneous parametrization of \(S\). Eliminating the parameters yields a quartic equation \(F\); the verifier records the complete polynomial and checks directly that it vanishes on the parametrization.

On the affine chart \(u\ne0\), put \(t=v/u\). The corresponding ruling line is spanned by
\[
P(t)=(5A(t),6,4t,0),\qquad Q(t)=(A(t),-6,0,2B(t)),
\]
where \(A(t)=4t^2+16t+9\) and \(B(t)=4t^2+10t+5\). For distinct parameters \(s,t\), the determinant controlling intersection of the two ruling lines factors as
\[
192(s-t)^2\bigl(20s^2t+20st^2+44st-49s-49t-115\bigr).
\]
Writing \(U=s+t\) and \(V=st\), the residual factor gives
\[
V=\frac{49U+115}{20U+44}.
\]
Substitution into the line-intersection coordinates and homogenization give the four binary cubics displayed in the finding. Their coefficient matrix has rank four, so the resulting curve is a twisted cubic.

To prove that this curve is the whole reduced singular locus, let \(J\) be the Jacobian ideal generated by the four partial derivatives of \(F\), and let \(I=(q_1,q_2,q_3)\). Exact polynomial identities express every generator of \(J\) as a linear combination of \(q_1,q_2,q_3\), so \(J\subset I\). Conversely, exact Gröbner reduction gives \(q_i^3\in J\) for \(i=1,2,3\), hence \(I\subset\sqrt{J}\). Since the four cubics above form a basis of the binary cubics, their image is the standard twisted cubic up to projective coordinates; its three-dimensional space of quadratic equations is spanned by \(q_1,q_2,q_3\), so \(I\) is prime. Therefore
\[
\sqrt{J}=I,
\]
which proves that the reduced singular locus is exactly this twisted cubic.

## Relationship to prior work
Calussi–Carlini–Fatabbi–Lorenzini use this explicit line–conic product to show that their lower bound is sharp: the Hadamard product is a singular quartic surface and its singular locus has dimension one. They do not identify that singular curve in Example 4.3.

Classical classifications of ruled quartic surfaces include models whose double curve is a twisted cubic, but they also include several other one-dimensional singular-locus types. Such a classification therefore does not by itself identify which type occurs for these particular Hadamard-product coefficients. The exact radical computation above supplies that missing identification. A later general study of surfaces that are Hadamard products of curves likewise proves one-dimensional singularity phenomena for line–curve products without identifying this example's reduced singular curve.

## Limitations
Only the reduced singular locus is classified. The scheme structure of the Jacobian ideal along the twisted cubic, possible special points on that curve, and a normalization map for the quartic are not claimed here. The originality comparison is statement-level for this explicit example; an unlocated classical source could conceivably recognize a projectively equivalent ruled quartic from different coordinates.
