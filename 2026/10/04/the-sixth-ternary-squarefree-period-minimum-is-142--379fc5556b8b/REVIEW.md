# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. A squarefree word cannot have a proper border of length at least half its length, and the longest border inherits every shorter border. Those two facts give a complete recursion on the number of borders. Exhaustive enumeration under first-letter alphabet normalization reproduces the published minima \(1,3,7,23,59\) and yields \(142\) as the first length with five proper borders. The displayed length-\(142\) witness has exactly the borders \(1,3,11,30,67\).

## Originality
PASS. The direct paper's exact squarefree table ends at \(g(4)=59\); its next theorem is only a non-optimal general upper bound. The current OEIS A332866 entry still contains only \(1,3,7,23,59\) and requests more terms. Five published-finding corpus searches and targeted literature searches under both the five-nontrivial-period and six-total-period conventions found no statement of \(142\).

Residual risk remains from an unindexed thesis, note, or computation, and from a later unrestricted-period-set paper whose full text was not available for comparison.

## Value
PASS. The result gives the first missing exact term of a named published extremal sequence and materially sharpens the direct source's broad upper bound at the next parameter. The parameter is forced by the end of the published table rather than chosen arbitrarily.
