# The size-three independence graph of \(A_5\)

## Finding

Let \(\Gamma_3(A_5)\) be the graph on the elements of \(A_5\) in which two distinct elements are adjacent when they occur together in a minimal generating set of size three, and let \(\Delta_3(A_5)\) be the induced subgraph on the non-isolated vertices.

Then the non-isolated vertices are exactly the 15 involutions and the 20 elements of order \(3\). All 24 elements of order \(5\) are isolated in \(\Gamma_3(A_5)\).

There are exactly
\[
1240
\]
minimal generating 3-subsets of \(A_5\). The reduced graph has
\[
\boxed{|V(\Delta_3(A_5))|=35,\qquad |E(\Delta_3(A_5))|=405,\qquad \operatorname{diam}\Delta_3(A_5)=2.}
\]
Its degree distribution is
\[
\boxed{\deg(x)=26\text{ for }o(x)=2,\qquad \deg(x)=21\text{ for }o(x)=3.}
\]

The adjacency has a concrete description.

- The 15 involutions form a clique \(K_{15}\).
- If \(x\) and \(y\) are 3-cycles, then they are adjacent exactly when the two-point fixed sets of \(x\) and \(y\) intersect in exactly one point. Hence the graph induced on the 20 elements of order \(3\) is the two-fold independent blow-up
  \[
  T(5)[\overline{K_2}],
  \]
  where \(T(5)\) is the triangular graph on the ten 2-subsets of a five-point set.
- If \(x\) is an involution and \(y\) is a 3-cycle, then \(x\) and \(y\) are adjacent exactly when
  \[
  \langle x,y\rangle<A_5.
  \]
  The proper possibilities are \(S_3\) and \(A_4\).

Since \(d(A_5)=2\) and the known maximal size of a minimal generating set of \(A_5\) is \(3\), these are the only fixed sizes that occur. The generating graph \(\Delta_2(A_5)\) is connected, while the theorem above proves the same for \(\Delta_3(A_5)\). Thus the fixed-size connectivity question posed for arbitrary finite groups has an affirmative answer for \(A_5\), the smallest unsoluble finite group.

## Assumptions and scope

A generating set is *minimal* if none of its proper subsets generates the group. The graph notation is the fixed-size refinement introduced in the literature on the independence graph of a finite group.

The group is the alternating group on five points. Its nonidentity elements have orders \(2\), \(3\), or \(5\), with class sizes \(15\), \(20\), and \(24\), respectively.

The result is a complete finite classification for the size-three layer of \(A_5\). It does not assert the fixed-size connectivity question for all nonsoluble groups.

## Proof

First exclude elements of order \(5\). Let \(x\) have order \(5\). Its cyclic subgroup \(\langle x\rangle\) is contained in a unique maximal subgroup of \(A_5\), namely its normalizer, which is dihedral of order \(10\). If \(\{x,y,z\}\) were a minimal generating triple, then both \(\langle x,y\rangle\) and \(\langle x,z\rangle\) would be proper. Hence both \(y\) and \(z\) would lie in that unique dihedral maximal subgroup, forcing \(\langle x,y,z\rangle<A_5\), a contradiction.

Thus every vertex of \(\Delta_3(A_5)\) has order \(2\) or \(3\). Conversely, the triple
\[
\{(3\ 4\ 5),(2\ 3)(4\ 5),(1\ 2)(4\ 5)\}
\]
is a minimal generating set for \(A_5\). Since \(A_5\) is transitive by conjugation on its involutions and on its 3-cycles, every element of order \(2\) or \(3\) occurs in a minimal generating triple. This proves the vertex classification.

For adjacency, a pair contained in a minimal generating triple cannot itself generate \(A_5\). The remaining proper pair types are determined by the subgroup structure of \(A_5\):
\[
A_4,\qquad S_3,\qquad D_{10},\qquad V_4,\qquad C_3.
\]
A pair of inverse 3-cycles generates the same \(C_3\), and cannot be extended minimally: for every third element \(z\), the two subgroups \(\langle x,z\rangle\) and \(\langle x^{-1},z\rangle\) coincide. Every other proper pair type is extendable. Representatives are witnessed by the following minimal triples:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\{(2\ 3)(4\ 5),(2\ 4)(3\ 5),(1\ 2)(4\ 5)\},\\
&\{(2\ 3)(4\ 5),(1\ 2)(4\ 5),(3\ 4\ 5)\},\\
&\{(2\ 3)(4\ 5),(1\ 2)(3\ 4),(3\ 4\ 5)\},\\
&\{(3\ 4\ 5),(2\ 3)(4\ 5),(1\ 2)(4\ 5)\},\\
&\{(3\ 4\ 5),(1\ 2)(4\ 5),(2\ 3)(4\ 5)\},\\
&\{(3\ 4\ 5),(1\ 4\ 5),(2\ 3)(4\ 5)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These cover, up to conjugacy and inversion of a 3-cycle, the pair types
\[
(2,2;4),\ (2,2;6),\ (2,2;10),\ (2,3;12),\ (2,3;6),\ (3,3;12),
\]
where the last entry is the order of the subgroup generated by the pair. Hence every pair of distinct involutions is adjacent; an involution and a 3-cycle are adjacent exactly when their generated subgroup is proper; and two distinct noninverse 3-cycles are adjacent exactly when they generate an \(A_4\).

A 3-cycle fixes two points. Two noninverse 3-cycles generate an \(A_4\) exactly when their two-point fixed sets have one common point. The ten possible fixed-point pairs are the vertices of \(T(5)\), and each supports two inverse 3-cycles. Therefore the induced 3-cycle graph is
\[
T(5)[\overline{K_2}].
\]
Each 3-cycle consequently has \(12\) 3-cycle neighbors.

For cross edges, a fixed 3-cycle lies in exactly two point-stabilizers \(A_4\), contributing six involutions in total, and its normalizer \(S_3\) contributes three further involutions. These nine involutions are distinct. Thus every 3-cycle has nine involution neighbors and total degree
\[
12+9=21.
\]
There are \(20\cdot9=180\) cross edges. By conjugacy each involution has \(180/15=12\) 3-cycle neighbors, and therefore degree
\[
14+12=26.
\]
The edge count is
\[
\binom{15}{2}+120+180=105+120+180=405.
\]

Every 3-cycle is adjacent to nine involutions, while the involutions form a clique. Any two 3-cycles have intersecting sets of involution neighbors because each such set has size nine inside a 15-element set. Hence every two vertices are at distance at most two. Since inverse 3-cycles are nonadjacent, the diameter is exactly \(2\).

Finally, the included exhaustive replay enumerates all \(\binom{59}{3}\) nonidentity triples directly from the permutation multiplication law and confirms that exactly \(1240\) are minimal generating triples. This finite enumeration is exhaustive, not a sample.

## Relationship to prior work

Lucchini introduced the fixed-size graphs \(\Gamma_u(G)\) and \(\Delta_u(G)\), proved that \(\Delta_u(G)\) is connected for every finite soluble group, and asked whether the same is true for every finite group and every fixed size \(u\). The paper does not work out \(\Delta_3(A_5)\).

Earlier work on irredundant generating sets of projective special linear groups uses the fact that minimax sets of \(A_5\) have size three and consist of elements of orders \(2\) and \(3\). That information identifies the possible vertex orders, but it does not determine which pairs coexist in minimal triples, the edge count, the two degree classes, or fixed-size connectivity.

The theorem above fills the smallest nonsoluble test case of the fixed-size connectivity question and gives the complete graph structure of the nontrivial size-three layer.

## Limitations

This is a finite exact result for \(A_5\), not a proof of fixed-size connectivity for all nonsoluble groups.

The argument exploits the small and rigid maximal-subgroup structure of \(A_5\). Larger simple groups can have several nonconjugate maximal subgroup families containing the same element type, so the same orbit argument does not automatically extend.

The exact graph description is new relative to the inspected sources, but an equivalent computation could exist in unpublished software tables or literature phrased solely in terms of irredundant generating triples rather than independence graphs.
