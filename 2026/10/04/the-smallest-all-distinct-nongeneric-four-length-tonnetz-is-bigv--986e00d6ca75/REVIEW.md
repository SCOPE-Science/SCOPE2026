# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The final claim is about the exact finite complex \(\mathrm{Tonn}^{10,4}(1,2,3,4)\). Reconstruction from the published definition gives \(60\) tetrahedral facets and face vector \((10,45,100,60)\). A complete discrete-Morse matching has \(103\) pairs and exactly one critical \(0\)-cell, six critical \(2\)-cells, and two critical \(3\)-cells; acyclicity is checked on every one of the \(630\) Hasse covers. Exact rational boundary ranks \((9,36,58)\) give Betti vector \((1,0,6,2)\). These data imply that the two Morse \(3\)-cell boundaries vanish, and the Hurewicz isomorphism for the wedge-of-six-\(2\)-spheres skeleton makes both attaching maps null-homotopic.

## Originality
PASS relative to the checked literature. The defining paper proves the torus theorem only for generic reduced length vectors and explicitly separates non-generic behavior; \((1,2,3,4)\) fails genericity by \(1+2=3\). Full-text inspection did not locate this \(k=4\) example. Catanzaro's arbitrary non-generic classification is restricted to triads/\(k=3\). Yust's inspected three-dimensional construction uses networks of multiple tetrachord types in Fourier phase spaces and does not state the present Jevtić--Živaljević complex or homotopy type. Exact-object, alias, parameter, and stronger-coverage searches found no statement implying the claim.

## Value
PASS. The vector \((1,2,3,4)\) is the unique smallest multiset of four pairwise-distinct positive lengths, so this is a natural boundary test for whether simple entry distinctness can replace the stronger subset-sum genericity condition. The answer is structurally sharp: the generic conclusion would be a \(3\)-torus, whereas the exact nongeneric complex is simply connected and has mixed-dimensional sphere-wedge homotopy \(\bigvee^6S^2\vee\bigvee^2S^3\). This isolates a concrete effect of subset-sum resonance rather than reporting an arbitrary small computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Jevtić--Živaljević, arXiv:2002.09184, which defines the object and proves the generic torus theorem but does not cover this nongeneric parameter. Catanzaro, arXiv:1612.03519, covers the non-generic \(k=3\) setting only. Yust's DOI 10.1142/9789813235311_0013 treats a different three-dimensional Tonnetz construction. The result does not classify nongeneric \(k=4\) vectors, and a residual risk remains that a differently indexed special-case computation exists.
