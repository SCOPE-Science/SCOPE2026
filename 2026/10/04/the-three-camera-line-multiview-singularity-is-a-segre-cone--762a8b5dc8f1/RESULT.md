# The three-camera line multiview singularity is a Segre cone
## Finding
Let \(A,B,C\) be three projective pinhole cameras over \(\mathbb C\) whose centers are linearly independent. Let \(X^{LLL}_{A,B,C}\subset((\mathbb P^2)^\vee)^3\) be the line multiview fourfold. Its known singular point is the triple of image lines whose three back-projected planes equal the plane spanned by the camera centers. At this point, an affine neighborhood is algebraically isomorphic to a neighborhood of the vertex of
\[
\{N\in\mathbb A^6: \operatorname{{rank}}N\le 1\},\qquad N\text{{ a }}3\times2\text{{ matrix}}.
\]
Equivalently, the singularity is the affine cone over the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1\subset\mathbb P^5\). Hence it is an isolated four-dimensional singularity with embedding dimension and Zariski tangent-space dimension \(6\). Its standard graded Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{{1+2t}}{{(1-t)^4}},
\]
and its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity at the vertex is \(3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The three camera centers are distinct and linearly independent, so in particular they are not collinear and span a unique plane. The statement concerns the LLL variety of Kileel, equivalently the three-camera line multiview variety. It is a local classification at the already-known singular point; it does not claim that the existence or uniqueness of that singular point is new.

## Proof
Kileel proves that \(X^{LLL}_{A,B,C}\) has dimension \(4\) and that, for linearly independent centers, its prime ideal is generated by the maximal minors of the \(4\times3\) matrix of back-projected plane equations. Projective changes in world coordinates and independent image-coordinate changes preserve the variety up to isomorphism. Thus take the centers to be the first three coordinate points of \(\mathbb P^3\). Writing the three back-projected planes as columns gives
\[
M=\begin{{pmatrix}}
0&d&g\\
a&0&h\\
b&e&0\\
c&f&i
\end{{pmatrix}}.
\]
The common plane spanned by the centers is \(X_3=0\), so the singular point is
\[
([a:b:c],[d:e:f],[g:h:i])=([0:0:1],[0:0:1],[0:0:1]).
\]
On the affine chart \(c=f=i=1\), subtract the third column of \(M\) from each of the first two. Since the last row is then \((0,0,1)\), the condition \(\operatorname{{rank}}M\le2\) is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{{rank}}N\le1,
\qquad
N=\begin{{pmatrix}}
-g&d-g\\
a-h&-h\\
b&e
\end{{pmatrix}}.
\]
The six entries of \(N\) are independent linear coordinates: the inverse linear change recovers \(g,d,h,a,b,e\) successively. Thus the affine chart of the multiview variety is exactly the generic \(3\times2\) rank-one determinantal cone, not merely tangent to it.

For completeness, the four maximal minors of the original chart matrix are
\[
F_1=aeg+bdh,
\quad F_2=-ad+ag+dh,
\quad F_3=-bd+bg-eg,
\quad F_4=ae+bh-eh.
\]
The three \(2\times2\) minors of \(N\) are exactly \(F_2,F_3,F_4\), and \(hF_3-gF_4=-F_1\), so the two descriptions define the same affine scheme.

The projectivization of the rank-one cone is the Segre variety \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1\). Its degree is
\[
(H_1+H_2)^3=3H_1^2H_2=3,
\]
so the vertex multiplicity is \(3\). Moreover
\[
\dim H^0(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(n,n))
=\binom{{n+2}}{{2}}(n+1),
\]
whose generating series is \((1+2t)/(1-t)^4\). Because all three defining minors are quadratic, the Zariski tangent space at the vertex is all of \(\mathbb A^6\), hence has dimension \(6\). The rank-one determinantal cone is smooth away from the zero matrix, so the singularity is isolated.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs the four \(3\times3\) minors of \(M\), performs the column operation, reconstructs the three \(2\times2\) minors of \(N\), checks their exact equality with \(F_2,F_3,F_4\), verifies \(hF_3-gF_4=-F_1\), and checks the inverse linear coordinate change. It also checks the first several Hilbert-function coefficients against the closed form \(\binom{{n+2}}{{2}}(n+1)\). Replay under Python with SymPy returns `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Kileel's Theorem 8 gives the \(4\)-dimensional LLL variety and its maximal-minor ideal for three cameras with linearly independent centers (arXiv:1611.05947). Breiding--Rydell--Shehu--Torres later prove that, for cameras with no three collinear centers, the singular locus is the rank-one locus of the back-projected-plane matrix; for three cameras this gives exactly one singular point, associated with the plane through the three centers (arXiv:2203.01694, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2). Their smoothness section identifies the singular point and a two-dimensional exceptional fiber in an incidence resolution, but does not state the local analytic/algebraic type, Hilbert series, or multiplicity. Breiding--Duff--Gustafsson--Rydell--Shehu study the line multiview ideal and Gröbner bases (arXiv:2303.02066); full-text searches for “singular”, “multiplicity”, and “Segre” return no such discussion. The present finding is restricted to the explicit local Segre-cone classification and its immediate local invariants.

## Limitations
The finding is local and applies to exactly three cameras with linearly independent centers. It does not classify degenerations with collinear centers or the extra equations needed when four or more centers are collinear. The uniqueness of the singular point is prior work. The originality assessment is literature-search based and cannot exclude an unindexed source that makes the same local identification.
