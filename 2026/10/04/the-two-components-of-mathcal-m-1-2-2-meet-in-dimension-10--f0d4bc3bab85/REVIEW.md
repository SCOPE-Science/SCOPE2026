# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The Hilbert-component boundary has dimension \(7\): distinct incident line pairs with their spatial embedded point give a \(7\)-dimensional locus, while the planar-double-line locus has dimension at most \(6\). The published \(11\)-dimensional stable-pair component maps properly and dominantly to the \(8\)-dimensional skew-line Hilbert component. Its inverse image of the \(7\)-dimensional boundary is proper, hence has dimension at most \(10\), while the fibre-dimension theorem gives dimension at least \(7+(11-8)=10\). The forgetful-map fibre is a projective space, so the pair-component intersection maps exactly onto the sheaf-component intersection. On the generic incident-line boundary the scheme is nonplanar, and the published criterion gives \(h^0(E(1))=1\); therefore no dimension is lost.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating paper explicitly asks to compute this intersection dimension after Proposition 4.5. Its Hilbert-scheme input source describes the incident-line and double-line degenerations but does not transfer that geometry to the rank-\(2\) sheaf-moduli intersection. Targeted searches for the exact component names, the dimension \(10\), stable-pair boundary language, and equivalent codimension formulations located no covering statement. An older source using different boundary-family notation remains a residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The result answers the dimension half of an explicit open question in the initiating paper. It shows that the two classical components meet along a divisor in the \(11\)-dimensional reflexive component and in codimension \(5\) inside the \(15\)-dimensional elementary-transformation component. This is a structural statement about the full moduli boundary rather than a routine example.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Almeida--Jardim--Oliveira, arXiv:2609.32668v1, which proves the two-component decomposition and asks for the dimension of their intersection. Soulimani--Gulbrandsen supplies the needed geometric description of the two components of \(\operatorname{Hilb}^{2t+2}(\mathbf P^3)\) and their intersection. The present result does not prove irreducibility or determine the scheme structure of the sheaf-component intersection.
