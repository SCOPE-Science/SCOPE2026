# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For \(p=3\), the source codifferential consists of one multiplication map. The source's low-\(m\) degree lemma gives \((1,1)\) at \(m=2\) and \((1,2)\), up to order, at \(m=3\). On an elliptic curve, positive-degree line bundles have \(h^0=\deg\). The first multiplication map therefore has rank \(1\); the second has rank \(2\) because multiplication by a nonzero degree-one section is injective. Since the source space has dimension \(m\), each generic fiber is one-dimensional. The order-\(3\) Hodge signatures give invariant symmetric-square tangent dimensions \(1\) and \(2\), exactly matching those ranks.

## Originality
**PASS.** The initiating paper states generic finiteness only for \(p\ge5,m\ge2\) and \(p=3,m\ge4\). It supplies the ingredients for the omitted cases but does not state their exact ranks, generic fiber dimensions, or the fact that the differential saturates the corresponding invariant tangent space. Searches by source ID, low-\(m\) parameters, rank/fiber wording, cyclic-triple-cover aliases, and Eisenstein/PEL terminology found no covering statement. A residual risk remains because the rank calculation is short and could have appeared informally.

## Value
**PASS.** These are precisely the two exceptional cases at the boundary of the paper's main generic-finiteness theorem. Showing that both fail by exactly one dimension and that this is exactly the rank constraint forced by the order-\(3\) automorphism gives a natural geometric completion of the theorem's parameter range.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Valdebenito Sepúlveda, arXiv:2608.04298v1, especially Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2, Corollary 5.3, and Proposition 5.4. General unitary and Picard-modular literature explains the ambient order-\(3\) automorphism loci. The result is local in moduli and does not assert a global identification, degree, or birationality of the Prym–Tyurin image with a Shimura variety.
