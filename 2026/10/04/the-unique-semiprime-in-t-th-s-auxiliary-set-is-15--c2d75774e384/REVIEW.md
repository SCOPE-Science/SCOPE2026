# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The defining reciprocal equation becomes an exact divisibility relation. For distinct odd primes, a sharp elementary inequality forces \(x\in\{2,3,4\}\), and each case is reduced to a complete factorization equation; only \(x=4\) yields \((p,q)=(3,5)\). Prime squares are excluded by a strict interval for the same ratio. No finite search is used for exhaustiveness.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source defines and computationally enumerates the auxiliary set while explicitly noting the lack of a theoretical framework that avoids brute force. OEIS A222263 records \(15\) and its witness but does not state semiprime uniqueness. Searches using the sequence identifier, the \(pq\) form, the exact reciprocal equation, and the unique-\(15\) formulation found no covering result. Residual risk remains from unindexed or unpublished work.

## Value
PASS. This is the natural first composite factor-count slice of the set central to the source. It proves that every composite member after \(15\) has at least three prime factors, replacing bounded observation with a complete structural sieve.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is László Tóth, “On the Density of Spoof Odd Perfect Numbers,” together with OEIS A222263. The theorem does not address members with three or more prime factors and does not settle the density conjectures for the full set.
