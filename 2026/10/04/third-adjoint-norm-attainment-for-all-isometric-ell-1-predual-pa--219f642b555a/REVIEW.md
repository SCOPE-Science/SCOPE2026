# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the claim to the published universal second-adjoint norm-attainment theorem for endomorphisms of \(\ell_1(\Lambda)\). For \(R:\ell_1(\Delta)\to\ell_1(\Gamma)\), the block map \(A(d,g)=(0,Rd)\) on \(\ell_1(\Delta)\oplus_1\ell_1(\Gamma)\) satisfies \(\|A\|=\|R\|\), and
\[
A^{**}(d^{**},g^{**})=(0,R^{**}d^{**}).
\]
A norm-attaining unit vector for \(A^{**}\) therefore has \(\|d^{**}\|=1\) when \(R\ne0\), proving that \(R^{**}\) attains its norm. The finite-index and zero-operator cases are immediate. Conjugating \(T^*:Y^*\to X^*\) by surjective linear isometries onto the corresponding \(\ell_1\)-spaces and then bidualizing transfers norm attainment to \(T^{***}:Y^{***}\to X^{***}\).

## Originality
PASS. The inspected primary paper states universal second-adjoint norm attainment for endomorphisms of \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\), and separately proves the special third-adjoint separation for \(c\). Its systematic \(\ell_1\)-predual analysis concerns second adjoints within the \(W_\alpha\) family. The inspected text does not state the cross-cardinality \(\ell_1(\Delta)\to\ell_1(\Gamma)\) consequence or the resulting universal third-adjoint theorem for arbitrary pairs of isometric \(\ell_1\)-preduals. Targeted searches for those formulations and their natural aliases found no covering result. Residual risk remains because the block-embedding argument is short.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a clean class-wide stabilization theorem: every operator between arbitrary isometric \(\ell_1\)-preduals reaches norm attainment by the third adjoint, with no separability or equal-cardinality restriction. The \(c\) example proves that the uniform depth \(3\) cannot be lowered to \(2\). This organizes a broad class by a sharp adjoint-depth invariant rather than adding a routine special case.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Dantas--Jung--Martín, arXiv:2609.00794v1. Theorem 3.17 and Remark 3.20 supply universal second-adjoint norm attainment on \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\) endomorphisms, while Corollary 3.19 supplies the sharp \(c\) example. The new statement depends essentially on isometric \(\ell_1\)-dual identifications and does not extend the second-adjoint classification itself.
