# Three-sparse Fourier support is controlled by affine span on \(\mathbb Z_4^d\)
## Finding
Let \(G=\mathbb Z_4^d\), and let \(f:G\to\mathbb C\) have exactly three nonzero values. Write \(S=\operatorname{supp}f\) and
\[
H=\langle S-S\rangle.
\]
For a fixed three-point support \(S\), the possible cardinalities of \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f\), as the three nonzero coefficients of \(f\) vary, depend only on the abstract group \(H\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
H & \text{attainable }|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|\\
\hline
\mathbb Z_4 & \{2,3,4\}\,4^{d-1}\\
\mathbb Z_2^2 & \{3,4\}\,4^{d-1}\\
\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2 & \{6,7,8\}\,2^{2d-3}\\
\mathbb Z_4^2 & \{14,15,16\}\,4^{d-2}.
\end{array}
\]
The third and fourth rows can occur only for \(d\ge2\). In particular, for every \(d\ge2\),
\[
\bigl\{|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|:|\operatorname{supp}f|=3\bigr\}
=\{8,12,14,15,16\}\,4^{d-2}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The Fourier transform is
\[
\widehat f(\chi)=\sum_{x\in G} f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}
\qquad(\chi\in\widehat G).
\]
Only support cardinalities are asserted; no statement is made about coefficient uniqueness or phase retrieval. The theorem allows arbitrary complex nonzero coefficients on the prescribed three-point support.

## Proof
Translate \(S\) so that \(S=\{0,a,b\}\). Translation multiplies Fourier coefficients by unimodular factors and therefore does not change Fourier-support cardinality. Put \(H=\langle a,b\rangle\). Every character of \(H\) extends to \(G\), and the restriction map \(\widehat G\to\widehat H\) has fibers of size \(|G|/|H|\). Hence, if \(f_H\) denotes the same three coefficients regarded as a function on \(H\), then
\[
|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|
=\frac{4^d}{|H|}\,|\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f_H}|.
\]
Because \(H\) is generated by two elements, has exponent dividing \(4\), and contains at least three elements, its only possible isomorphism types are \(\mathbb Z_4\), \(\mathbb Z_2^2\), \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2\), and \(\mathbb Z_4^2\).

For \(H\cong\mathbb Z_4\), every three-point support is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,1,2\}\), so the Fourier values are \(A+Bz+Cz^2\) at \(z\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}\), with \(ABC\ne0\). A nonzero quadratic has at most two roots. Zero, one, and two roots all occur, for example for
\[
1+z+z^2,\qquad 1+z-2z^2,\qquad (z-1)(z-i),
\]
respectively. Thus the local Fourier-support sizes are exactly \(4,3,2\).

For \(H\cong\mathbb Z_2^2\), every three-point support is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,e_1,e_2\}\). Its four Fourier values are \(A\pm B\pm C\). Two distinct zeros would force one of \(A,B,C\) to vanish: if the corresponding sign pairs differ in one coordinate, subtraction kills one coefficient, while opposite sign pairs force \(A=0\). Hence there is at most one zero. Both zero and one zero occur, so the local support sizes are exactly \(4,3\).

For \(H\cong\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2\), a generating three-point support is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,e_1,e_2\}\), where \(e_1\) has order \(4\) and \(e_2\) has order \(2\). The Fourier values can therefore be written
\[
A+Bu+Cv,
\qquad u\in\{1,i,-1,-i\},\quad v\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
For fixed \(v\), at most one \(u\) can give zero, so there are at most two zeros. Zero, one, and two zeros occur, for example with \((A,B,C)=(3,1,1)\), \((1,1,-2)\), and \((-(1+i),2,-1+i)\). The local support sizes are exactly \(8,7,6\).

For \(H\cong\mathbb Z_4^2\), the two translated support differences form a basis, so the support is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,e_1,e_2\}\). The Fourier values are
\[
A+Bu+Cv,
\qquad u,v\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}.
\]
If this vanishes, then \(|A+Bu|=|C|\). As \(u\) varies, the points \(A+Bu\) lie on the circle centered at \(A\) with radius \(|B|\); a zero requires the point also to lie on the circle centered at \(0\) with radius \(|C|\). Since \(A,B,C\ne0\), these are distinct nondegenerate circles and have at most two intersections. Thus there are at most two Fourier zeros. The same three coefficient triples used above realize zero, one, and two zeros, so the local support sizes are exactly \(16,15,14\).

Multiplying each local list by the restriction-fiber size \(4^d/|H|\) gives the four rows of the table. For \(d\ge2\), all four subgroup types occur, and their union is exactly \(\{8,12,14,15,16\}4^{d-2}\).

## Verification
A standalone exact verifier is included as `artifacts/verify.py`. It exhausts all \(\binom{16}{3}=560\) supports in \(\mathbb Z_4^2\), classifies their difference-span type, and checks attainable Fourier-support sizes using Gaussian-integer character rows with no floating-point arithmetic. It returns `VERIFY_OK`, with span counts \(96,16,192,256\) for \(\mathbb Z_4\), \(\mathbb Z_2^2\), \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2\), and \(\mathbb Z_4^2\), respectively. The infinite-dimensional statement is not inferred from enumeration: it follows from the character-restriction fiber formula and the four local analytic arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Krahmer, Pfander, and Rashkov give general support-pair rank criteria for finite Abelian groups and numerical uncertainty diagrams for groups of order at most \(16\), including \(\mathbb Z_4^2\). Their diagram records global attainable or unattainable support-size pairs, but not the fixed-support classification by \(\langle S-S\rangle\), nor the dimension-free lifting to every \(\mathbb Z_4^d\). Bonami and Ghobber treat equality cases for the families \(\mathbb Z_p^2\), \(\mathbb Z_{p^2}\), and \(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_q\) with distinct primes in the latter family; these do not include \(\mathbb Z_4^d\) for \(d\ge2\). Meshulam's divisor interpolation gives general lower bounds but not these exact coefficient-attainable lists. Delvaux and Van Barel study rank-deficient submatrices of Kronecker products of Fourier matrices; the accessible abstract gives a broader rank-deficiency framework, not the present three-point affine-span theorem.

## Limitations
The claim concerns only three-point supports in powers of \(\mathbb Z_4\). It does not classify four-point supports, general exponent-four groups with additional \(\mathbb Z_2\) factors, or the geometry of the coefficient loci beyond the attainable support cardinalities. A residual literature risk remains that an equivalent three-term classification is hidden in older work under polynomial or Kronecker-Fourier terminology; the closest identified sources do not state it in the inspected material.
