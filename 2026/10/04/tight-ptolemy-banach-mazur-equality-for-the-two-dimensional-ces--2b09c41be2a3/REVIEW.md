# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof is exact. The one-variable distortion quotient has derivative numerator \(2(5-t^2)\), so its global maximum on \([0,\infty)\) is attained at \(t=\sqrt5\) and the axis values are the minima. The resulting Euclidean distortion is exactly \(1+1/\sqrt5\) after squaring. Zuo's Lemma 2.5 gives the matching universal lower bound from the exact Ptolemy constant.

## Originality
PASS. ## Value
PASS. The claim identifies an exact natural invariant of a classical Cesàro plane, proves sharpness of the Ptolemy lower bound, and certifies a globally optimal Euclidean pullback rather than merely giving a coordinate-wise estimate.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is Zuo's 2012 paper, which supplies the exact Ptolemy value and the relevant equivalent-norm comparison. The main residual risk is an obscure unindexed prior observation. The claim is restricted to the real two-dimensional space \(\mathrm{{ces}}_2^{(2)}\).
