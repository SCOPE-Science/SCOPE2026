# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Every order-two reflection subgroup must be contained in some selected subgroup, and a proper subgroup of a dihedral group contains at most \(n/p\) reflections, where \(p\) is the smallest prime divisor of \(n\). This gives the sharp \(p\)-subgroup reflection-cover lower bound. If \(p^2\mid n\), the \(p\) maximal dihedral subgroups \(\langle a^p,a^r b\rangle\) also contain every prime-order rotation subgroup and form a clique. If \(p^2\nmid n\), those \(p\) subgroups miss the order-\(p\) rotation subgroup, forcing one extra vertex; adding \(\langle a\rangle\) yields a clique. Paired domination is bounded below by total domination and parity, and explicit clique extensions attain the least even majorant.

Risk: the subgroup-intersection convention must exclude the trivial subgroup; this is stated explicitly and matches the archive ownership source.

## Originality

PASS. The closest archive source proves the ordinary domination formula for the same graph and same dihedral family, but its full text contains no total- or paired-domination treatment. A second full-text dihedral intersection-graph paper computes degrees and clique number rather than domination variants. Exact searches under the total-domination, paired-domination, perfect-matching, and dihedral subgroup-intersection formulations did not locate the present classification.

Risk: the total formula is a sharp strengthening of an existing ordinary-domination construction, so the new step is structurally short once the clique intersections are recognized. No stronger published statement implying the paired parity formula was located.

## Value

PASS. The result gives a complete strengthened-domination classification for an infinite classical group family, including the precise prime-order obstruction and an exact relation among ordinary, total, and paired domination. It shows that total domination costs nothing beyond ordinary domination for every composite dihedral group, while paired domination measures exactly the parity defect of the ordinary minimum.

Risk: minimum-set enumeration is not included and can be nontrivial in the \(p^2\nmid n\) branch.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
