# Review

## Correctness

PASS. In a local factor, two proper ideals cannot sum to the whole ring, so co-maximal adjacency in an Artinian product is exactly union of whole-coordinate supports. The \(r\) maximal ideals have co-singleton supports, form a clique, and totally dominate every vertex. For \(r\ge3\), the published ordinary domination value \(r\) supplies the matching lower bound for total domination; for \(r=2\), total domination is at least two directly. Paired domination is at least the least even integer at least \(r\), and the maximal-ideal clique attains this for even \(r\); for odd \(r\), one support-singleton vertex pairs with the corresponding maximal ideal and the remaining maximal ideals pair inside the clique.

Risk: finite computation is not used as an infinite proof; the general argument depends on the Artinian local-product decomposition.

## Originality

PASS. The foundational co-maximal ideal graph paper studies connectivity, diameter, clique number, and chromatic number. The closest domination paper computes ordinary domination for finite commutative Artinian rings and gives the two-factor complete-bipartite structure, but does not state total or paired domination formulas. Exact searches combining the graph name with “total domination” and “paired domination” returned no covering theorem.

Risk: the paired formula is short once the ordinary domination theorem and the maximal-ideal clique are placed together, so an unindexed note could contain the parity refinement.

## Value

PASS. The result gives a complete strengthened domination profile for a canonical algebraic graph family. It shows that total domination costs nothing beyond ordinary domination once there are at least three factors, while paired domination detects only the parity of the number of local factors. The formulas are independent of residue-field sizes and internal ideal lattices, exposing a clean structural invariant of the Artinian decomposition.

Risk: the paired gap is only one, so the value lies in the structural uniformity and exact classification rather than a large numerical separation.
