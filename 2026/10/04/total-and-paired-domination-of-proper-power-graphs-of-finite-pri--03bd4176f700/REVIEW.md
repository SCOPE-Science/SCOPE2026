# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Adjacency in a proper power graph of a finite \(p\)-group preserves the unique order-\(p\) subgroup of the cyclic subgroup generated by a vertex. Conversely, vertices with the same order-\(p\) subgroup share a prime-order neighbor, so these fibers are exactly the connected components. Each component has a universal prime-order vertex. For odd \(p\), two nonidentity elements of that order-\(p\) subgroup give an adjacent total-dominating pair. For \(p=2\), a component has more than one vertex exactly when its involution is the square of an order-four element. Two vertices per nontrivial component are both necessary and sufficient, and the selected pairs form a perfect matching.

Risk: finite computations cannot establish the theorem by themselves; they are used only to replay representative group structures. The general proof is entirely component-theoretic.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary paper on domination of proper power graphs gives the ordinary domination number of a finite \(p\)-group as the number of its order-\(p\) subgroups and records the corresponding component count. It does not state the total/paired existence criterion or the value \(2t_p(G)\). Exact and semantic searches under total domination, paired domination, proper power graph, prime-power groups, involutions, and perfect matchings did not locate a covering theorem. A paper on total domination of full power graphs of cyclic groups is inapplicable because retaining the identity changes the graph decisively.

Risk: once the component structure is known, the strengthened formulas have short proofs, so an unindexed note could contain the same deduction.

## Value

PASS. The result completes two standard strengthened domination invariants over the entire finite \(p\)-group family and exposes a sharp structural obstruction unique to \(2\)-groups. It also shows that every minimum total dominating set is automatically paired and gives the concrete abelian criterion excluding direct \(C_2\)-factors. This is a natural classification adjacent to the published ordinary-domination theorem, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

Risk: for odd \(p\) the numerical formula is a uniform doubling of the component count; the substantive added content is the exact strengthened-domination mechanism and the \(2\)-group existence boundary.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
