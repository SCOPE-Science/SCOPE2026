# Review of Total weak Roman domination on complete multipartite graphs

## Correctness
PASS. Total domination on a complete multipartite graph is equivalent to positive support on at least two parts. With at least three support parts, a one-unit transfer from any positive neighbor cannot reduce support below two parts. With exactly two support parts, zeros on one side are protected exactly when that side is already full or the opposite side has total label weight at least two. This proves the structural criterion. The enumerator subtracts exactly the two bad two-support events and restores their unique intersection. Definition-level exhaustive verification matches the criterion, every polynomial coefficient, and every minimum case through order nine.

## Originality
PASS. The 2019 foundational article introduces total weak Roman domination, gives general bounds, rooted-product results, and NP-hardness, but no complete-multipartite all-function theorem was located in its full text. The 2022 lexicographic-product paper provides genuine broader coverage for minimum values on a product class, including balanced complete multipartite specializations, but its stated results concern minimum total weak Roman and secure total domination numbers rather than enumeration of all feasible ternary functions. Targeted semantic searches found no arbitrary complete-multipartite total weak Roman weight enumerator. The claim is therefore restricted to the stronger all-function classification and polynomial.

## Value
PASS. Total weak Roman domination was introduced as a graph-protection optimization problem and remains computationally difficult in general. On a canonical dense class, the finding resolves the entire feasible configuration space rather than only the optimum, yielding every weight coefficient and exact counts of optimal functions. The result also cleanly quantifies how label-two donors enlarge the secure-total feasible family.
