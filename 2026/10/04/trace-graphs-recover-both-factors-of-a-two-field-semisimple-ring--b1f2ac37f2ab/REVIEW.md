# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Under
\[
M_n(\mathbf F_q\times\mathbf F_r)\cong M_n(\mathbf F_q)\times M_n(\mathbf F_r),
\]
trace vanishing is the conjunction of two field-valued trace equations. The pairing
\[
(A,C)\longmapsto\operatorname{tr}(AC)
\]
is nondegenerate over every field, because a nonzero entry of \(A\) is detected by a suitable matrix unit. Therefore the orthogonal kernel has size \(u^{n^2-1}\) for every nonzero matrix over \(\mathbf F_u\). The three support types give the three kernel-size multipliers \(1,q,r\), and deletion of the zero vertex and possible self-loop gives the two degrees per multiplier. Matrix units \(E_{12}\) and \(E_{11}\) realize both self-orthogonality cases. The graph-intrinsic reconstruction formulas then follow algebraically. The packaged checker independently enumerates the relevant trace kernels and degrees in several prime and non-prime field cases.

## Originality
PASS. The full 2024 survey explicitly poses the direct-product trace graph as Problem 1. It also reproduces the prior general degree formula and the prior isomorphism theorem recovering only ring cardinality and matrix size. The accepted claim does not present that general degree formula as new. Its new content is the exact two-field support fingerprint and the observation that a divisibility filter on the complete graph degree set reconstructs the two field orders separately, giving an if-and-only-if classification inside the two-field semisimple family. Targeted searches found no equivalent statement. The main residual risk is an equivalent formulation under bilinear orthogonality terminology or in inaccessible portions of older paywalled articles.

## Value
PASS. The theorem closes a mathematically natural first semisimple instance of an explicit open direction and strengthens an existing trace-graph rigidity invariant from total ring size to recovery of the individual simple factors. The reconstruction is uniform for all finite field orders and all matrix sizes \(n\ge2\), rather than a finite census.
