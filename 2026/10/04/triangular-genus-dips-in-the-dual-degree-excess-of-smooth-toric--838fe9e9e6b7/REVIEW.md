# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the dual-degree excess from the smooth toric discriminant formula, Pick's theorem, and toric adjunction. The only nonstandard classification step is the smooth-triangle lemma, and it is proved directly from primitive edge determinants. The nontriangular case is closed by the universal vertex bound and an explicit smooth rectangle of every required genus. The defective unimodular simplex is excluded explicitly.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of the closest toric discriminant sources found the general degree machinery but not the fixed-sectional-genus lower envelope, the triangular-genus one-unit dips, or the unique Veronese minimizers. Exact and semantic searches using dual-degree excess, smooth lattice polygons, fixed genus, triangular genera, and Veronese language found no covering statement. A previously checked Segre--Veronese result concerns only one special surface family and does not imply the all-toric classification.

Residual risk: because the proof combines classical ingredients economically, an unindexed older source could contain the same corollary under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The result classifies the least possible dual-degree excess for every sectional genus in a canonical class of algebraic surfaces. Its equality pattern is arithmetic and geometrically rigid: triangular genera are exactly the genera where a smooth triangular polygon exists, and the unique minimizer is Veronese. This is a natural structural boundary theorem rather than an isolated calculation.
