# Truth-preservation accessibility collapses the forcing Kripke frames to S5
## Finding

Chopoghloo and Golshani define, for each admissible forcing interpretation, an accessibility relation between generic-extension worlds by preservation of **all** translated modal formulas.

Writing the relation abstractly as
\[
w\,R\,v
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall\varphi\,\forall\bar a\,
\bigl(
w\models\varphi(\bar a)
\Rightarrow
v\models\varphi(\bar a)
\bigr),
\]
where the same tuple of names is evaluated at the two worlds, the relation is not merely reflexive. It is an equivalence relation.

Reflexivity is immediate.

Transitivity is immediate: if every truth at \(w\) persists to \(v\), and every truth at \(v\) persists to \(u\), then every truth at \(w\) persists to \(u\).

Symmetry follows from classical negation. Suppose
\[
wRv
\]
and that
\[
v\models\varphi(\bar a)
\]
while
\[
w\not\models\varphi(\bar a).
\]
Then
\[
w\models\neg\varphi(\bar a).
\]
Preservation along \(wRv\) gives
\[
v\models\neg\varphi(\bar a),
\]
contradicting \(v\models\varphi(\bar a)\). Therefore truth agrees in both directions for every formula and tuple, so
\[
vRw.
\]

Consequently every Kripke frame produced by Definition 5.1 is an equivalence frame. In particular it validates the propositional modal principles of
\[
\mathsf{S5},
\]
including
\[
\Box p\to\Box\Box p.
\]

This creates a direct obstruction to the claimed general Kripke completeness theorem. The paper works with the ambient first-order modal logic \(\mathsf{FOT}\), whose modal frame condition is reflexivity, and allows a consistent recursively axiomatizable normal modal extension \(T_m\) of the base first-order theory. Such a theory need not prove axiom \(4\).

For a concrete allowed instance, enlarge the language of Robinson arithmetic by a fresh unary predicate \(P\) and a fresh constant \(c\), and let \(T\) be Robinson arithmetic with no additional axioms about \(P\) or \(c\). Let \(T_m\) be the minimal \(\mathsf{FOT}\)-closed modal extension of \(T\).

Consider the reflexive three-world frame
\[
w_0Rw_0,\quad
w_1Rw_1,\quad
w_2Rw_2,\quad
w_0Rw_1,\quad
w_1Rw_2,
\]
with no edge
\[
w_0Rw_2.
\]
Use one fixed model of \(T\) as the constant domain structure at all worlds, keep the arithmetic symbols fixed, and interpret
\[
P(c)
\]
as true at \(w_0,w_1\) and false at \(w_2\). Then
\[
w_0\models\Box P(c)
\]
but
\[
w_0\not\models\Box\Box P(c),
\]
because \(w_1Rw_2\) and \(w_2\not\models P(c)\). Thus
\[
\Box P(c)\to\Box\Box P(c)
\]
is not valid over the intended reflexive semantics of the minimal \(\mathsf{FOT}\)-based theory, hence is not derivable there.

By contrast, that formula is valid on every frame generated by Definition 5.1, since all such frames are transitive.

Therefore Theorem 5.4, as stated for the general \(T_m\) setting of the paper, cannot hold without strengthening the modal theory to include at least the modal consequences forced by equivalence frames, or changing the accessibility definition.

## Assumptions and scope

The argument uses the Definition 5.1 relation exactly as printed: accessibility is quantified over every modal formula and every tuple of names, with ordinary classical negation available in the language.

The counterexample does not challenge the Boolean-valued translation itself or the soundness calculations of Section 4. It targets the later passage to Kripke models in Section 5 and, specifically, the completeness claim that equates derivability in the stated general \(T_m\) with validity in every associated Kripke frame.

The argument is independent of the particular forcing ideal. It uses only the logical form of the accessibility definition.

The conclusion is conditional in the precise way required: if a chosen \(T_m\) already contains the full \(\mathsf{S5}\) propositional modal principles, the specific axiom-\(4\) contradiction disappears. The obstruction is to the theorem's advertised generality over normal extensions based on the ambient \(\mathsf{FOT}\) setting.

## Proof

Let
\[
\operatorname{Th}(w)
=
\{
(\varphi,\bar a):
w\models\varphi(\bar a)
\}.
\]
Definition 5.1 says
\[
wRv
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Th}(w)\subseteq\operatorname{Th}(v).
\]

Because the language contains negation, these complete truth sets cannot be properly included.

Indeed, assume
\[
\operatorname{Th}(w)\subseteq\operatorname{Th}(v).
\]
Take any formula \(\varphi\) and tuple \(\bar a\). If
\[
v\models\varphi(\bar a)
\]
but
\[
w\not\models\varphi(\bar a),
\]
classical semantics gives
\[
w\models\neg\varphi(\bar a).
\]
The assumed inclusion then gives
\[
v\models\neg\varphi(\bar a),
\]
contradiction. Hence
\[
v\models\varphi(\bar a)
\Rightarrow
w\models\varphi(\bar a)
\]
for all \(\varphi,\bar a\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Th}(v)\subseteq\operatorname{Th}(w),
\]
and the truth sets are equal.

So
\[
wRv
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Th}(w)=\operatorname{Th}(v).
\]
Equality is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Therefore the relation of Definition 5.1 is an equivalence relation.

Every equivalence Kripke frame validates \(\mathsf{S5}\). In particular, transitivity validates
\[
\Box\psi\to\Box\Box\psi
\]
for every formula \(\psi\).

Now choose an allowed base theory as follows. Start with Robinson arithmetic in a language expanded by an unconstrained unary predicate \(P\) and a constant \(c\). No new nonlogical axiom is imposed on these symbols. Let \(T_m\) be the minimal modal theory obtained by closing \(T\) under the ambient \(\mathsf{FOT}\) consequence relation.

The reflexive three-world relation
\[
R=
\{
(w_0,w_0),(w_1,w_1),(w_2,w_2),
(w_0,w_1),(w_1,w_2)
\}
\]
is not transitive.

Put the same arithmetic model at every world and vary only the interpretation of \(P\) so that
\[
P(c)
\]
is true at \(w_0,w_1\) and false at \(w_2\). Every world still satisfies the base theory \(T\).

At \(w_0\), the only accessible worlds are \(w_0,w_1\), where \(P(c)\) is true, so
\[
w_0\models\Box P(c).
\]
But \(w_1\) accesses \(w_2\), where \(P(c)\) is false, so
\[
w_1\not\models\Box P(c).
\]
Therefore
\[
w_0\not\models\Box\Box P(c).
\]
Thus the axiom-\(4\) instance fails on a reflexive \(T_m\)-model.

Hence it is not derivable in the minimal \(\mathsf{FOT}\)-based \(T_m\), while it is valid in every frame produced by Definition 5.1. This contradicts the equivalence claimed by Theorem 5.4 for that allowed choice of \(T_m\).

## Verification

The proof is purely semantic.

The bundled checker verifies two finite facts independently.

First, it enumerates complete truth signatures for a finite collection of formulas closed under negation and confirms that the preservation relation
\[
sRt
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every truth at }s\text{ is a truth at }t
\]
is always an equivalence relation.

Second, it checks the explicit three-world reflexive nontransitive frame and confirms
\[
w_0\models\Box p
\]
while
\[
w_0\not\models\Box\Box p
\]
for the valuation \(p(w_0)=p(w_1)=\mathsf{true}\), \(p(w_2)=\mathsf{false}\).

These computations only corroborate the direct proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2026 paper itself states that its ambient first-order modal logic is \(\mathsf{FOT}\), defines the Section 5 accessibility relation by preservation of all modal formulas, proves a truth lemma for the resulting Kripke models, and then states Theorem 5.4 equating \(T_m\)-provability with validity in every associated Kripke model.

The source discusses \(\mathsf{S4}\) in its modal-logic preliminaries as the logic of reflexive transitive frames, but does not note that its later Definition 5.1 relation is automatically transitive. More strongly, because the relation preserves a language closed under negation, it is automatically symmetric as well.

Boos's earlier “Virtual Modality” is cited by the source as the predecessor of the forcing translation and describes a closely related Kripke accessibility semantics in its abstract. The accessible material did not suffice to determine whether Boos uses the same all-formulas truth-preservation definition, so no claim is made that the present obstruction applies to that paper.

Targeted searches for the 2026 paper together with “axiom 4,” “S4,” “S5,” “transitive,” “preorder,” and “completeness” did not locate a published correction or the equivalence-collapse observation.

## Limitations

The finding isolates a defect in the Section 5 Kripke completeness theorem as stated. It does not by itself determine the strongest correct replacement theorem.

One possible repair is to weaken the accessibility test so that it preserves only a suitable positive or boxed fragment rather than all formulas; another is to strengthen the target modal theory to match the frame class actually produced. No repair theorem is claimed here.

The Boolean-valued completeness theorem of Section 4 is logically distinct from the Section 5 Kripke-frame claim and is not assessed by this result.

The exact relation between the source's construction and Boos's 2003 accessibility semantics remains a literature risk because only abstract-level material for the latter was inspected.
