# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Definition 5.1 quantifies over every modal formula. If \(wRv\) and a formula is true at \(v\) but false at \(w\), then its negation is true at \(w\); preservation forces that negation at \(v\), contradiction. Hence accessibility is symmetric. Reflexivity and transitivity are immediate, so every associated frame is an equivalence frame and validates \(\mathsf{S5}\).

The counterexample to general completeness uses an allowed language extending Robinson arithmetic by an unconstrained predicate. A three-world reflexive nontransitive constant-domain model satisfies the base theory at every world while falsifying an axiom-\(4\) instance. Thus the minimal ambient \(\mathsf{FOT}\)-based modal extension does not prove that instance, whereas every Definition 5.1 frame validates it.

## Originality

PASS. The primary paper does not state that Definition 5.1 collapses accessibility to equality of complete truth theories, nor does it mention an \(\mathsf{S5}\) restriction on Theorem 5.4. Candidate-specific published-finding and web searches found no correction or equivalent observation.

The earlier Boos paper is a plausible predecessor, but only abstract-level material was accessible in the comparison performed here. That source is therefore recorded as a residual risk rather than treated as non-covering.

## Value

PASS. Theorem 5.4 is the paper's final Kripke completeness theorem. The obstruction is structural, not cosmetic: the accessibility definition forces a strictly stronger modal frame class than the ambient \(\mathsf{FOT}\) semantics. The argument also identifies the precise cause—quantifying truth preservation over a language closed under negation—so it sharply constrains any repair.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Chopoghloo--Golshani (2026), especially the ambient-\(\mathsf{FOT}\) convention, Definition 5.1, Lemma 5.3, and Theorem 5.4. Standard modal semantics supplies the correspondence between equivalence frames and \(\mathsf{S5}\).

The finding does not determine whether the source's Section 4 Boolean-valued completeness can be retained unchanged, and it does not propose a complete repaired Section 5 construction.
