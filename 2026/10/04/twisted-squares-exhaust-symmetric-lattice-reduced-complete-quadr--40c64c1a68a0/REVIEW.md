PASS

After width normalization, reducedness supplies primitive width directions uniquely selecting adjacent vertices. If those directions generated a proper sublattice, a nonzero lattice point (or its complement in the fundamental parallelogram) would lie in the triangle spanned with the origin and hence in the interior of the polar, contradicting normalized lattice width; therefore they form a lattice basis. The resulting normal form is \(\operatorname{conv}\{\pm(1,r),\pm(-s,1)\}\), \(0\le r,s<1\). The exact gauge \((|p+sq|+|q-rp|)/(1+rs)\) shows that \(r<s\) leaves only \(\pm e_1\) shortest and \(s<r\) only \(\pm e_2\); the source completeness criterion requires diameter segments meeting every edge, so completeness forces \(r=s\). The zero endpoint is the known incomplete diamond and every positive equal-parameter case is the published twisted-square example. The area, diameter and polar involution then follow algebraically.
