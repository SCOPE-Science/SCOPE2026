# Two-generated class-two commuting graphs are determinant blow-ups

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite nonabelian group generated by two elements and of nilpotency class \(2\). Put
\[
c=|G'|,
\qquad
s=|Z(G)|.
\]
Define \(\Delta_c\) to be the graph on
\[
(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z)^2
\]
in which distinct vectors
\[
(i,j),\qquad(k,\ell)
\]
are adjacent exactly when
\[
i\ell-jk\equiv0\pmod c.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{G/Z(G)\cong C_c\times C_c}
\]
and the full commuting graph is
\[
\boxed{\Gamma(G)\cong\Delta_c[K_s].}
\]
Thus the entire commuting graph depends only on the two integers
\[
|G'|
\quad\text{and}\quad
|Z(G)|.
\]

The degree distribution is explicit. For every divisor \(d\mid c\), the degree
\[
\boxed{scd-1}
\]
occurs with multiplicity
\[
\boxed{sJ_2(c/d),}
\]
where
\[
J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2})
\]
is Jordan's second totient, with \(J_2(1)=1\).

Consequently, for finite nonabelian two-generated class-two groups \(G,H\),
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(G)\cong\Gamma(H)
\iff
\bigl(|Z(G)|,|G'|\bigr)
=
\bigl(|Z(H)|,|H'|\bigr).
}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(G)\cong\Gamma(H)
\Longrightarrow
G\text{ and }H\text{ are isoclinic}.
}
\]

The endpoint is genuinely isoclinism rather than isomorphism. There are nonisomorphic groups of order \(32\) with the same center order \(8\), derived-group order \(2\), and commuting graph.

## Assumptions and scope

The commuting graph \(\Gamma(G)\) is the full simple graph with vertex set \(G\); two distinct elements are adjacent exactly when they commute.

A group has nilpotency class \(2\) when its commutator subgroup is central and nontrivial.

Two groups are isoclinic when their central quotients and derived subgroups are isomorphic in a way that intertwines the commutator maps.

No prime-power assumption is imposed. The theorem applies to every finite nonabelian two-generated class-two group.

## Proof

Choose generators
\[
G=\langle a,b\rangle
\]
and put
\[
z=[a,b].
\]
Because \(G\) has class \(2\), the standard commutator identities give
\[
[a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell]
=
z^{\,i\ell-jk}.
\]
Every commutator of words in \(a,b\) is therefore a power of \(z\), so
\[
G'=\langle z\rangle
\]
is cyclic. Let
\[
c=|z|=|G'|.
\]

Since
\[
[a^c,b]=z^c=1
\]
and
\[
[b^c,a]=z^{-c}=1,
\]
both \(a^c\) and \(b^c\) are central.

Conversely, if
\[
a^ib^j\in Z(G),
\]
then commuting it with \(b\) gives
\[
z^i=1,
\]
and commuting it with \(a\) gives
\[
z^{-j}=1.
\]
Hence
\[
c\mid i
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
c\mid j.
\]
Thus \(aZ(G)\) and \(bZ(G)\) both have order exactly \(c\), and no nontrivial product of their powers is central. Therefore
\[
G/Z(G)\cong C_c\times C_c.
\]

Under the coordinate identification
\[
a^ib^jZ(G)\longleftrightarrow(i,j),
\]
two central cosets commute elementwise exactly when
\[
i\ell-jk\equiv0\pmod c.
\]
Each central coset contains \(s\) elements, forms a clique, and between two distinct cosets either all edges occur or none occur. Hence
\[
\Gamma(G)\cong\Delta_c[K_s].
\]

For the degree formula, fix
\[
v=(i,j)\in(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z)^2
\]
and put
\[
d=\gcd(i,j,c).
\]
Consider
\[
L_v:(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z)^2\to\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z,
\qquad
L_v(k,\ell)=i\ell-jk.
\]
Its image is the subgroup generated by \(i\) and \(j\), which has order
\[
c/d.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\ker L_v|=cd.
\]
An element lying above \(v\) consequently has centralizer of order
\[
scd,
\]
so its graph degree is
\[
scd-1.
\]

The number of vectors modulo \(c\) satisfying
\[
\gcd(i,j,c)=d
\]
is
\[
J_2(c/d).
\]
Each such quotient vector represents \(s\) group elements. This proves the degree multiplicity formula.

Now suppose
\[
\Gamma(G)\cong\Gamma(H).
\]
The universal vertices of the full commuting graph are precisely the elements of the center, so the graph recovers
\[
s=|Z(G)|.
\]
It also recovers \(|G|\). Since
\[
|G|=|Z(G)|\,|G/Z(G)|=sc^2,
\]
the graph recovers
\[
c=\sqrt{|G|/s}.
\]
Thus isomorphic graphs force equality of the pairs \((s,c)\). Conversely, equal pairs produce the same graph \(\Delta_c[K_s]\).

If two groups have the same \(c\), choose generators \(a,b\) and \(A,B\) whose basic commutators generate their derived groups. The maps
\[
a^ib^jZ(G)\longmapsto A^iB^jZ(H)
\]
and
\[
[a,b]^r\longmapsto[A,B]^r
\]
are isomorphisms and preserve
\[
[a^ib^j,a^kb^\ell]
=
[a,b]^{\,i\ell-jk}.
\]
They therefore define an isoclinism.

For an explicit nonisomorphic collision, let
\[
G(A,B,c)
=
\mathbb Z/A\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/B\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z,
\]
where \(c\mid A\) and \(c\mid B\), with multiplication
\[
(u,v,w)(u',v',w')
=
\bigl(
u+u',
v+v',
w+w'-vu'
\bigr).
\]
Coordinates are reduced modulo \(A,B,c\), respectively. This is a two-generated class-two group satisfying
\[
|G'|=c,
\qquad
|Z(G)|=\frac{AB}{c},
\qquad
G/G'\cong C_A\times C_B.
\]
Hence
\[
G(8,2,2)
\quad\text{and}\quad
G(4,4,2)
\]
both have order \(32\), center order \(8\), and derived-group order \(2\), so their commuting graphs are isomorphic. Their abelianizations
\[
C_8\times C_2
\quad\text{and}\quad
C_4\times C_4
\]
are not isomorphic, so the groups are not isomorphic.

## Relationship to prior work

Broche and del Río classify finite two-generated class-two \(p\)-groups by a finite tuple of group-theoretic parameters in their solution of the modular isomorphism problem for this family. Their classification makes explicit that the family contains many nonisomorphic groups with cyclic derived subgroup, but it does not state a commuting-graph collapse to two parameters.

Arvind and Cameron ask whether nilpotent groups of class \(2\) with isomorphic commuting graphs must be isoclinic. Their full preprint proves and uses this property for extraspecial groups and explicitly leaves the general class-two statement as a conjecture.

The theorem here proves that conjecture on the complete two-generated class-two family. The mechanism is stronger than the isoclinism conclusion: the whole graph is the determinant-zero graph over \(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\), blown up by the center size, and its degree distribution is explicit.

A later paper on commuting graphs of cyclic-by-abelian groups studies a different structural class and notes that cyclicity of the derived subgroup alone is not enough for its results. It does not state the determinant blow-up classification above.

Targeted searches using two-generator, class-two, cyclic-commutator, determinant-pairing, commuting-graph, and isoclinism terminology did not locate this theorem.

## Limitations

The theorem requires two generators. With three or more generators the commutator subgroup need not be cyclic and the commutator map becomes a higher-rank alternating bilinear map.

The graph is the full commuting graph including central elements. Deleting the universal vertices gives the corresponding reduced graph immediately.

Cyclicity of the derived subgroup in a two-generated class-two group and the class-two commutator identities are standard ingredients. The claimed new content is the exact graph model, its degree distribution, the graph-isomorphism classification, and the isoclinism consequence.

Failed searches do not prove novelty.
