The classical prime-power least-common-multiple formula gives \(\lambda(12)=2\) while \(\varphi(12)=4\), and \(\lambda(24)=2\) while \(\varphi(24)/2=4\). Exact finite checking establishes that these are the least failures in their respective branches. The later proof uses only \(p-1\mid\lambda(m)\) for \(p\mid m\), which follows immediately from the correct formula.
