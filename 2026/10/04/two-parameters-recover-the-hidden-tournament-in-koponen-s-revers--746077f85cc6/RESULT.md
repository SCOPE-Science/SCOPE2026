# Two parameters recover the hidden tournament in Koponen’s reversal reduct, with an exact finite-type spectrum

## Finding

Let \\(T=(D,E)\\) be the countable random tournament, and let \\(M\\) be the four-ternary-relation structure built by Koponen in Definition 7.5 and Lemma 7.7 of *On constraints and dividing in ternary homogeneous structures*. For tuples of the same equality pattern, Koponen's relation \\(\\approx_n\\) identifies two tournament diagrams precisely when all directed edges are either preserved simultaneously or reversed simultaneously. Lemma 7.6 shows that for \\(n\\ge3\\) this global preserve/reverse condition is already determined on triples; the four \\(\\approx_3\\)-classes become the four basic ternary relations of \\(M\\).

The resulting reduct has a sharp parameter threshold that is not stated in the source:
\[
\boxed{\text{the hidden tournament relation }E\text{ is definable over }A\subseteq M\text{ iff }|A|\ge2,}
\]
where the forward statement means that every set of at least two distinct parameters defines one of the two converse orientations, and in the fixed expansion \\(T=(D,E)\\) the original \\(E\\) is definable over those parameters. Equivalently, zero or one parameter leaves the global reversal symmetry alive, while fixing two distinct points kills it.

This symmetry break controls the entire finite-parameter type spectrum. Let \\(A\\subseteq M\\) be finite, put \\(m=|A|\\), and let \\(S_n^M(A)\\) be the complete \\(n\\)-type space over \\(A\\). For \\(0\\le k\\le n\\), define
\[
B_{m,n,k}=\frac1{k!}\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i\binom{k}{i}(m+k-i)^n.
\]
This is the number of equality patterns in which the \\(n\\) variables use exactly \\(k\\) genuinely new equality classes, while any of the \\(m\\) named parameters may also occur. Put
\[
d_{m,k}=mk+\binom{k}{2}
\]
and
\[
w_{m,k}=\begin{cases}
2^{d_{m,k}},&m\ge2,\\
1,&m\le1\text{ and }d_{m,k}=0,\\
2^{d_{m,k}-1},&m\le1\text{ and }d_{m,k}>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{|S_n^M(A)|=\sum_{k=0}^n B_{m,n,k}w_{m,k}.}
\]
Thus the type count depends only on \\(m\\), not on the isomorphism type of the finite parameter set.

Several consequences are immediate. For \\(m\ge2\\),
\[
\boxed{|S_1^M(A)|=m+2^m,}
\]
with \\(m\\) algebraic types and \\(2^m\\) nonalgebraic types. By contrast, over one parameter there are only two 1-types: equality and inequality. Over the empty set there is one 1-type. For injective ordered \\(r\\)-tuples over the empty set, the orbit count is
\[
\boxed{a_1=1,\qquad a_r=2^{\binom r2-1}\quad(r\ge2),}
\]
because a labeled tournament and its converse form a free two-element orbit once at least one edge is present. For arbitrary ordered tuples,
\[
\boxed{|S_n^M(\varnothing)|={n\brace1}+\sum_{k=2}^n {n\brace k}2^{\binom k2-1}.}
\]
The first values are
\[
1,2,8,64,948,26536,1476788\qquad(n=1,\ldots,7).
\]
For \\(m\ge2\\), the exponential generating function of the full type counts is also explicit:
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}|S_n^M(A)|\frac{z^n}{n!}
=e^{mz}\sum_{k\ge0}2^{mk+\binom k2}\frac{(e^z-1)^k}{k!}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The structure \\(M\\) is exactly Koponen's Section 7 construction from the generic tournament: its four ternary predicates are the four equivalence classes of ordered triples under simultaneous preservation or reversal of every tournament edge. The result concerns complete types of real tuples over finite real parameter sets. It does not count imaginary types, types over infinite parameter sets, or types in the other random-tournament reducts generated by vertex switching.

The statement that \\(E\\) is definable over two parameters uses the standard Ryll--Nardzewski correspondence for the countable omega-categorical structure \\(M\\): a finite-arity relation invariant under the pointwise stabilizer of a finite parameter tuple is definable over that tuple.

## Proof

First identify the automorphism group. Every tournament automorphism preserves each \\(\\approx_3\\)-class, and every anti-automorphism reverses all tournament edges, hence also preserves each class. Thus both kinds of permutations lie in \\(\\operatorname{Aut}(M)\\).

Conversely, let \\(g\\in\\operatorname{Aut}(M)\\). Choose three distinct points. Since \\(g\\) preserves their \\(M\\)-type, Koponen's definition says that on this triple \\(g\\) either preserves all tournament edges or reverses all of them. Fix one directed edge \\(u\to v\\) in that triple. For any fourth point \\(w\\), the triple \\(u,v,w\\) shares the edge \\(u,v\\), so it must use the same preserve/reverse mode: preservation and reversal cannot both agree on a directed edge. Varying \\(w\\), and then using triples \\(u,w,w'\\), propagates the same mode to every edge. Hence \\(g\\) is globally either a tournament automorphism or a tournament anti-automorphism. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(M)=\operatorname{Aut}(T)\rtimes C_2,
\]
where the nontrivial coset consists of anti-automorphisms. This agrees with Bennett's classification of the random-tournament reduct generated by global reversal; the group identification itself is prior context, not the retained novelty.

Now fix parameters. A global anti-automorphism exists because \\(T\\) is isomorphic to its converse. Given a single point \\(a\\), compose any anti-isomorphism with a tournament automorphism so that the composite fixes \\(a\\). Thus the pointwise stabilizer of \\(a\\) in \\(\\operatorname{Aut}(M)\\) still contains a reversal, so \\(E\\) cannot be definable over \\(a\\). The same argument with no parameters shows nondefinability over the empty set.

If \\(a\\ne b\\) are fixed pointwise, however, no anti-automorphism can fix both: if \\(E(a,b)\\), an anti-automorphism fixing \\(a,b\\) would force \\(E(b,a)\\), impossible in a tournament. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(M)_{a,b}=\operatorname{Aut}(T)_{a,b}.
\]
The relation \\(E\\) is invariant under the right-hand side, so omega-categoricity makes it definable in \\(M\\) over \\(a,b\\). This proves that the exact threshold is two parameters.

For the type formula, first fix an equality pattern of an \\(n\\)-tuple over \\(A\\) with exactly \\(k\\) new equality classes. Temporarily label those new classes by \\(1,\ldots,k\\). There are \\((m+k)^n\\) assignments of the variables to the \\(m\\) named parameters and these \\(k\\) new labels. Inclusion--exclusion forces every new label to occur, and dividing by \\(k!\\) forgets their temporary names. This gives exactly \\(B_{m,n,k}\\).

Once the equality pattern is fixed, a realization is determined at tournament level by the orientations between the \\(k\\) new classes and the \\(m\\) parameters, together with orientations among the new classes. There are
\[
d_{m,k}=mk+\binom{k}{2}
\]
independent bits. The random tournament extension property realizes every such choice.

If \\(m\ge2\\), fixing the parameters kills the reversal coset, by the threshold argument. Hence all \\(2^{d_{m,k}}\\) orientation vectors give distinct \\(M\\)-types. If \\(m\le1\\), a reversal fixing the parameter set still exists and complements all \\(d_{m,k}\\) orientation bits. When \\(d_{m,k}>0\\), this involution has no fixed bit vector, so the quotient has \\(2^{d_{m,k}-1}\\) orbits; when \\(d_{m,k}=0\\), there is one orbit. Multiplying by the equality-pattern count and summing over \\(k\\) proves the formula.

The injective and arbitrary empty-parameter formulas follow by taking one equality block count at a time. The exponential generating function for \\(m\ge2\\) follows from the standard identity that equality patterns with \\(k\\) unlabeled new blocks contribute \\(e^{mz}(e^z-1)^k/k!\\).

## Relationship to prior work

Koponen defines \\(\\approx_n\\) by preserving equality patterns and then either preserving every tournament edge or reversing every tournament edge. Lemma 7.6 proves that, for \\(n\ge3\\), the relation is determined by triples; she notes that \\(\\approx_3\\) has exactly four classes and uses them as the four ternary predicates of \\(M\\). Lemma 7.7 proves that \\(M\\) is homogeneous, supersimple of SU-rank one, with degenerate algebraic closure. These are the essential prior ingredients.

The broader reduct theory is also prior. Bodirsky, Pinsker, and Pongrácz summarize Bennett's classification of the five reducts of the random tournament and identify the nontrivial reduct generated by a global orientation reversal. Thus this result does **not** claim novelty for the reversal reduct or its automorphism group. The retained contribution is the exact two-parameter recovery threshold for the hidden tournament orientation and the resulting closed finite-parameter complete-type spectrum.

Targeted published-finding corpus searches using “generic tournament reduct global reversal tuple orbit profile,” “ordered tuple orbits random tournament modulo reversal,” “finite parameter type counts random tournament reversal reduct,” and direct searches for Koponen's Lemma 7.7 returned nearby orbit-profile results for random-poset and random-bipartite reducts but no matching random-tournament formula. Web searches using the same structural terminology, exact initial sequence, and \\(\\operatorname{Betw}_T\\) terminology likewise did not locate this threshold or spectrum.

## Limitations

The formulas concern Koponen's global-converse reduct only. The vertex-switching and combined switching-plus-reversal reducts have different stabilizer actions and are not covered. The definability statement recovers one of the two converse orientations only after two named points select the orientation; without parameters there is intentionally no canonical choice.

The novelty claim has residual folklore risk: once the reversal-reduct viewpoint is recognized, the stabilizer argument and Burnside quotient are short. The exact all-parameter formula nevertheless does not appear in the inspected primary construction, Bennett-summary literature, published-finding corpus index, or targeted web searches.
