# Two unary functions give the generic type space a Cantor perfect kernel
## Finding
Let \(T=\mathit{EC}_{\{F,G\}}\) be the model completion of the empty theory in the language consisting of two unary function symbols. For \(p\in S_1(T)\), let \(A_p\) be the pointed unary algebra generated by a realization \(a\models p\):
\[
A_p=\langle a\rangle=\{w(a):w\in\{F,G\}^{<\omega}\}.
\]
Then:
\[
p\text{ is isolated }\Longleftrightarrow |A_p|<\infty.
\]
The isolated types are countable and dense in \(S_1(T)\). Moreover,
\[
S_1(T)'=\{p:|A_p|=\infty\},
\]
this derivative has no isolated points, and therefore
\[
S_1(T)'\cong 2^\omega,\qquad S_1(T)''=S_1(T)'.
\]
Thus the Stone space has a dense countable set of isolated finite-orbit types and a Cantor perfect kernel.

## Assumptions and scope
The language is exactly \(\{F,G\}\), with both symbols unary and with no constants or relation symbols. The theory \(T\) is the complete generic theory of such structures. Kruckman–Ramsey prove that the model companion of the empty theory exists in arbitrary languages, has quantifier elimination, and is complete when there are no constants. Jeřábek independently gives the model-completion and quantifier-elimination result and proves that with two unary functions there are \(2^{\aleph_0}\) complete \(1\)-types.

The claim concerns the topology of \(S_1(T)\), not merely its cardinality. The topology is the ordinary Stone topology on complete \(1\)-types over the empty set.

## Proof
By quantifier elimination, a complete \(1\)-type is determined by the atomic equalities and inequalities among unary terms \(w(x)\), where \(w\) ranges over finite words in \(F\) and \(G\). Equivalently, \(p\) is determined by the congruence on the free one-generated \(\{F,G\}\)-algebra induced by
\[
u\equiv_p v\Longleftrightarrow p\vdash u(x)=v(x).
\]
The quotient is precisely the pointed generated algebra \(A_p\).

Suppose first that \(A_p\) is finite. Choose terms \(t_1(x)=x,t_2(x),\dots,t_m(x)\) representing all elements of \(A_p\). For each \(i\) and each \(H\in\{F,G\}\), record the unique transition equation
\[
H(t_i(x))=t_{j(i,H)}(x),
\]
and also record \(t_i(x)\neq t_j(x)\) for \(i\neq j\). This is one finite quantifier-free formula. Every term reduces inductively to one of the \(t_i\), so the formula decides every atomic equality between terms. Quantifier elimination therefore makes it a complete formula isolating \(p\).

Conversely, suppose \(A_p\) is infinite. Let \(U\) be any basic neighborhood of \(p\). Strengthen its defining formula inside \(p\) so that it gives the complete equality/inequality diagram on a finite subterm-closed set \(\Theta\) of terms. This finite diagram has a finite realization: take the finitely many \(p\)-equivalence classes represented in \(\Theta\), preserve every transition whose image term still lies in \(\Theta\), and send all otherwise undefined transitions to finitely many new states arbitrarily. Because the empty theory imposes no universal restrictions, this finite unary algebra embeds in a model of \(T\). Its distinguished generator realizes some \(q\in U\) with finite generated orbit. Hence an infinite-orbit type cannot be isolated. This also proves that finite-orbit types are dense.

It remains to show that the set
\[
D=\{p:|A_p|=\infty\}
\]
has no isolated points. Again fix \(p\in D\) and a finite complete diagram on a subterm-closed set \(\Theta\). Let \(V\) be the finite set of represented congruence classes. If every \(F\)- and \(G\)-transition from every class in \(V\) were already represented inside \(\Theta\), then every term would reduce into \(V\), contradicting \(|A_p|=\infty\). Thus there is a represented class \(v\) and one function \(H\in\{F,G\}\) whose outgoing transition is not fixed by the finite diagram.

Send this transition to a fresh state \(y_0\), attach a fresh infinite \(G\)-ray
\[
y_0,\ y_1,\ y_2,\dots,\qquad G(y_i)=y_{i+1},
\]
and preserve all transitions already fixed by the finite diagram. There are at least two distinct infinite completions of the same finite diagram: for example, in one put \(F(y_0)=y_0\), and in another put \(F(y_0)=y_1\), while leaving the \(G\)-ray infinite. Both pointed unary algebras realize types in \(D\cap U\), and they give different complete types. So \(D\) has no isolated points.

Since \(S_1(T)\) is compact, metrizable, and zero-dimensional, its closed subspace \(D=S_1(T)'\) is also compact, metrizable, and zero-dimensional. It is nonempty and perfect. By the classical characterization of Cantor space,
\[
D\cong 2^\omega.
\]
Because \(D\) is perfect, its derivative is itself, giving \(S_1(T)''=S_1(T)'\).

## Verification
The accompanying checker performs two finite sanity tests of the proof mechanism. First, it exhaustively enumerates all reachable finite pointed algebras with two unary functions on at most four states, quotients by rooted isomorphism, and obtains \(1,12,216,5248\) isolated finite-orbit types for orbit sizes \(1,2,3,4\). It also checks that a rooted automorphism of a reachable pointed algebra is always trivial, as expected because the root generates every state.

Second, for free cylinders through term depth at most five, it constructs explicit finite completions agreeing with all equalities and inequalities visible at that depth. For depths through seven it also checks two symbolic infinite completions that agree on the whole visible cylinder but differ strictly beyond it. These computations are finite tests; the infinite statement is proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kruckman–Ramsey prove that the generic \(L\)-structure is the model completion of the empty theory, that it has quantifier elimination, and that with no constants the theory is complete. They also identify generated substructures with definable and algebraic closure. Jeřábek proves the same model-completion framework in a form tailored to arbitrary function languages and, in Appendix B, proves that a countable language with two unary functions has \(2^{\aleph_0}\) complete \(1\)-types.

Those results give the cardinality and the structural setting but do not state the Cantor–Bendixson decomposition above. The new refinement identifies exactly which \(1\)-types are isolated, proves that they are dense, and identifies the whole perfect kernel as Cantor space.

## Limitations
The argument is specific to the empty-theory generic in the language of exactly two unary functions. With one unary function the type space is scattered rather than having this perfect kernel, while adding infinitely many relation symbols or changing the universal theory can alter isolation behavior. No claim is made here about higher-arity type spaces \(S_n(T)\) for \(n>1\).

The literature searches located the known continuum-cardinality theorem but not this exact topological refinement. Because the proof is short and uses standard Stone-space ideas, residual folklore risk remains.
