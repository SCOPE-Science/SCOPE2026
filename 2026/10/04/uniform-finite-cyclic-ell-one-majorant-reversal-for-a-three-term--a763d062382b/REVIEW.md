# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Quotienting by the character kernel is exact. The positive sum is bounded by a quartic majorant whose root-of-unity moments are exact for every quotient order at least five; the signed sum is bounded below by the endpoint chord of a concave square-root function. The quotient orders three and four are evaluated directly. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The continuous-circle three-term result of Krenedits covers \(1+e_1\pm e_2\) at exponent one, but it does not compare every finite cyclic sampling. Bonami--Révész treat finite cyclic \(L^1\) idempotent concentration, not this signed majorant comparison. Krenedits' description of Mockenhaupt's discrete uniform inequality places it in the different \(2<p<4\), \(1+e_1\pm e_3\) regime. Targeted searches in the published finding database returned no statement implying the all-order finite-cyclic theorem. Residual risk remains that an unindexed exercise or inaccessible older source states the elementary finite-grid inequality under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. Three terms are the smallest possible idempotent size at which Hardy--Littlewood majorization can fail, and finite cyclic groups are an established setting for \(L^1\) idempotent questions. Showing that the reversal persists on every nondegenerate cyclic quotient, with a uniform all-modulus gap from a short moment certificate, gives a natural discrete structural counterpart to the classical continuous phenomenon.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary sources are arXiv:1006.0409v1 for continuous three-term majorant counterexamples and arXiv:0811.4576v1 for finite-cyclic \(L^1\) idempotent concentration. The theorem is restricted to exponent one, equal coefficient magnitudes, and consecutive three-term arithmetic progressions. The full Mockenhaupt 1996 thesis was not directly inspected; its relevant example was compared through Krenedits' specific published description.
