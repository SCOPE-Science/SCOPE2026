# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the focal paper's exact quotient representation for the normalized ratio, its exponential numerator concentration, its uniform Gaussian-supremum mean and concentration estimates, its deterministic coefficient-ratio bound, and its uniform comparison between \(N_{m,n}\) and \(H_{m,n}\). These imply a polynomial envelope times exponentially small bad-event probability, which is enough for every fixed finite \(L^s\) norm. The Gaussian-to-spherical transfer is exact because the ratio is homogeneous of degree zero.

## Originality
PASS. The focal source proves uniform convergence in spherical measure and a large-deviation statement, but does not state finite-moment convergence, uniform integrability, or an expected-ratio asymptotic. Targeted searches for those formulations and broader classical Bohnenblust–Hille results found no implication covering the claim. The closest classical paper controls extremal constants rather than the distribution over coefficient directions.

## Value
PASS. Expectations and higher moments are natural quantitative summaries of a random coefficient direction, and they are not determined by convergence in measure. The result shows that rare large ratios do not alter any fixed finite moment, uniformly over dimension, so the typical scale is also the average-case scale.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Pellegrino–Teixeira, arXiv:2609.31779v1, especially Theorem C, Proposition 6.5 and equation (6.19). The result is restricted to full complex polynomial spaces and fixed finite moment order. It does not cover moment order growing with degree, exponential moments, or the real case. A short equivalent uniform-integrability observation could remain hidden under different terminology.
