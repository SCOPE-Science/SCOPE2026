# Review of Uniform Klein-four quotient description for all magic octic--dodecic K3 maps

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is quantified over every magic octic model and every partition of its six coordinates into three pairs. The source supplies the degree-four generic-orbit description. Invariance gives \(\mathbf C(S'_P)\subseteq \mathbf C(S)^{G_P}\), and the equal degree four forces equality. Symplecticity is checked from the residue form: every pair-sign class changes an even number of coordinates, hence has ambient determinant \(+1\), while the diagonal defining quadrics are fixed. Rational-double-point resolution is crepant, so the two-form extends. The standard finite symplectic quotient theorem then gives a K3 quotient resolution, and birational smooth projective K3 surfaces are isomorphic.

Risk: the proof relies on the motivating paper's stated generic-fiber theorem for all magic octic models. That premise was inspected in the primary full text; no attempt is made to rederive its elimination computation here.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating paper explicitly proves the quotient description only for Bremner's smooth octic, whereas its general construction establishes degree-four maps with pair-sign generic fibers for all magic octics. Searches for the same quotient formulation, its aliases, and broader symplectic-quotient coverage found no source that identifies every associated magic dodecic with the quotient resolution of every magic octic. General K3 quotient theorems supply only the abstract quotient geometry, not this identification.

Risk: the extension is a short structural consequence of the source's general degree-four orbit statement plus classical symplectic-quotient theory, so an unindexed note could regard it as implicit. The claim is therefore presented as a uniform structural corollary, not as a new method or classification theorem.

## Value
PASS. The result removes the smooth-Bremner restriction from a quotient interpretation that is geometrically stronger than knowing only the degree of a rational map. It organizes all 15 associated dodecics for every magic octic through standard symplectic-K3 quotient geometry, making fixed-point and lattice methods available uniformly. This directly addresses a natural structural gap in the motivating paper rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
