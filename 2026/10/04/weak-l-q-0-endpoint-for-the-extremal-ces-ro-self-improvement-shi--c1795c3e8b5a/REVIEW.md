# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the exact iterates of the weighted shift and reduces the endpoint to a discrete weak fractional-tail estimate. That estimate is proved directly by a head/tail kernel split, Tonelli's theorem, and Markov's inequality. The exponent identities \(r=1/\beta\), \(q_0=pr\), and \(\alpha q_0=1\) were checked explicitly. Arnold's harmonic-divergence calculation supplies the strong endpoint failure, and the basis-vector test supplies failure of every larger weak exponent.

## Originality
PASS. Arnold's full paper gives strong \(q\)-absolute Cesàro boundedness for \(q<q_0\) and failure at the strong endpoint for the same explicit shift, but does not state a weak-Lorentz endpoint. Full-text searches found no “Lorentz” or “weak type” statement. Semantic searches over endpoint, distribution-function, weighted-shift, and exact-parameter formulations found no covering result. The closest earlier weighted-shift paper concerns strong \(q\)-absolute Cesàro boundedness.

## Value
PASS. The result closes the natural endpoint left by an explicitly optimal self-improvement theorem: the extremal family has a proper weak endpoint at exactly the exponent where strong boundedness fails, and no weak estimate survives above it. This gives a sharp phase classification rather than a routine restatement of the published strong-range theorem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is L. Arnold, arXiv:2610.00271v1, especially Theorem 3.1 and Example 3.2. A. Abbar, L. Arnold and C. Coine, arXiv:2609.24601v1, supplies the weighted-shift strong characterization cited there. G. Cohen, C. Cuny, T. Eisner and M. Lin, DOI:10.1016/j.jmaa.2020.124035, provides foundational \(p\)-absolute Cesàro theory. The present claim is restricted to the explicit extremal shift and does not establish a universal weak endpoint for all \(p\)-absolutely Cesàro bounded operators.
