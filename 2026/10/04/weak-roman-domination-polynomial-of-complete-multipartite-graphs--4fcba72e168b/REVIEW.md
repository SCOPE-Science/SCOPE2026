# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The support-domination lemma for complete multipartite graphs is exact, and the proof checks all three possible numbers of supported parts. The only delicate case is exactly two supported parts: a zero in one supported part becomes unsafe precisely when the opposite part contains a single unit and at least two zeros remain in the attacked part. The polynomial is a direct inclusion-exclusion translation of this criterion. The independent verifier exhausts every ordered part profile through total order \(8\) and every \(\{0,1,2\}\)-labeling.

## Originality
PASS. The classical literature covers weak Roman domination numbers for complete multipartite graphs and generalized finite-order scalar values for complete bipartite graphs. Those are explicitly treated as prior coverage. Targeted searches over weak Roman, smart weak Roman, guard-function, complete multipartite, complete bipartite, generating-polynomial, and all-function aliases found no inspected source classifying every feasible weak Roman labeling or giving the weight enumerator in RESULT.md. The inaccessible 2003 full text and nonstandard indexing remain stated risks.

## Value
PASS. A full feasible-function classification is a materially stronger object than the already known minimum number: it determines every weight count, isolates the exact support obstruction, and gives a reusable enumerator for a canonical graph family. The theorem is structural and infinite-family, not a finite table or arbitrary parameter slice.

The closest literature is the foundational weak Roman paper and the 2004 finite-order protection paper. The latter explicitly says the scalar classical parameters were known on complete multipartite graphs and gives complete-bipartite generalized scalar formulas; those facts bound the originality claim rather than undermine the all-function enumerator.
