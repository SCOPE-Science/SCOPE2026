# Weight enumerator of all minus dominating functions on complete multipartite graphs
## Finding
Let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) be a connected complete multipartite graph with \(r\ge2\), partite classes \(V_i\), and \(N=\sum_i n_i\). A minus dominating function is a map \(f:V(G)\to\{-1,0,1\}\) satisfying \(\sum_{u\in N[v]}f(u)\ge1\) for every vertex \(v\).

For each part define \(s_i=\sum_{v\in V_i}f(v)\), let \(w=\sum_i s_i\), and let \(\mu_i=\min_{v\in V_i}f(v)\). Then
\[
f\text{ is minus dominating}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w-s_i+\mu_i\ge1\text{ for every }i.
\]

For a Laurent polynomial \(A(y)=\sum_s a_s y^s\), write \(\tau_qA(y)=\sum_{s\le q}a_s y^s\). For \(1\le w\le N\), define
\[
P_{n,w}(y)=\tau_{w-2}\!\left((y^{-1}+1+y)^n-(1+y)^n\right)+\tau_{w-1}\!\left((1+y)^n-y^n\right)+\mathbf 1_{n\le w}y^n.
\]
The exact weight enumerator is
\[
M_G(x)=\sum_{w=1}^N\left([y^w]\prod_{i=1}^r P_{n_i,w}(y)\right)x^w.
\]
Thus every coefficient counts all minus dominating functions of the corresponding weight, not only optimum functions.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and connected complete multipartite graphs with at least two nonempty parts. The result concerns the standard minus domination convention \(f:V(G)\to\{-1,0,1\}\) and closed-neighborhood threshold \(1\). The scalar minimum \(\gamma^-(G)\) is not claimed as new; it is used as a consistency check against published complete-multipartite results.

## Proof
Fix \(v\in V_i\). In a complete multipartite graph, \(v\) is adjacent to every vertex outside \(V_i\) and to no other vertex in \(V_i\). Hence
\[
\sum_{u\in N[v]}f(u)=f(v)+\sum_{j\ne i}s_j=w-s_i+f(v).
\]
The closed-neighborhood inequalities for every vertex of \(V_i\) therefore hold exactly when their smallest left side does, namely when \(w-s_i+\mu_i\ge1\). Doing this independently for every part proves the structural criterion.

Now fix the total weight \(w\). A labeling of a part of size \(n\) falls into exactly one of three disjoint types. If the part contains at least one \(-1\), then \(\mu=-1\) and its part sum \(s\) must satisfy \(s\le w-2\); all such labelings are generated by \((y^{-1}+1+y)^n-(1+y)^n\), truncated to exponents at most \(w-2\). If the part contains no \(-1\) but at least one \(0\), then \(\mu=0\) and \(s\le w-1\); these labelings are generated by \((1+y)^n-y^n\), truncated at \(w-1\). Finally, if every label is \(+1\), then \(s=n\) and the criterion is \(n\le w\), giving the last term. Therefore \(P_{n,w}(y)\) counts exactly the admissible labelings of one part by part sum.

Multiplying \(P_{n_i,w}(y)\) over the parts makes the exponent of \(y\) equal the global weight. Extracting \([y^w]\) therefore counts exactly the minus dominating functions of global weight \(w\), proving the enumerator formula.

Finally, every minus dominating function has positive weight. Indeed, if \(w\le0\), no part can be all \(+1\), because for such a part the criterion would force \(w\ge n_i>0\). Thus every part has \(\mu_i\le0\), so \(w-s_i\ge1\) and hence \(s_i\le w-1\) for every \(i\). Summing gives \(w\le r(w-1)\), impossible for \(r\ge2\) and \(w\le0\). This justifies summing only over \(1\le w\le N\).

## Relationship to prior work
Liang's 2012 preprint, later published in *Ars Combinatoria*, determines the exact signed, signed-total, and minus domination numbers of complete multipartite graphs. Its Theorem 3 gives \(\gamma^-(G)=1\) when a singleton part exists and \(\gamma^-(G)=2\) otherwise. The paper's proof uses the same basic identity \(f(N[v])=f(v)+f(V\setminus V_i)\), but it optimizes weight and does not state an all-function classification or a weight enumerator. The earlier complete-bipartite paper of Zelinka likewise concerns scalar signed and minus domination numbers. The present claim retains only the stronger all-function criterion and exact coefficient enumerator; the scalar minimum is explicitly treated as prior-covered.

## Limitations
No claim is made that the term “minus domination polynomial” has a standardized literature definition. The displayed \(M_G(x)\) is defined here transparently as the ordinary weight enumerator of all standard minus dominating functions. Search cannot prove absence from all literature, and older or poorly indexed work could contain an equivalent census under different terminology. The exact theorem itself depends only on the standard definition and the complete-multipartite neighborhood identity.
