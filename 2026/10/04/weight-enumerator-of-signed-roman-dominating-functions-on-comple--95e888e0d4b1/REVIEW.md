# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For a vertex in \(V_i\), the closed-neighborhood sum is exactly \(W-\sigma_i+f(v)\); hence the minimum label \(\mu_i\) gives a necessary-and-sufficient partwise inequality. The label \(-1\) witness condition is exactly the existence of a label \(2\) outside the same part. The multinomial summation then counts every valid labeling exactly once. Exhaustive replay through order \(9\) agrees with both the literal definition and all enumerator coefficients.

## Originality
PASS, with a named residual access risk. The founding paper gives general and bipartite bounds, Zhao--Miao gives exact complete-bipartite scalar values, and Yin--Chen (2017) is confirmed to cover the complete-multipartite signed Roman domination number. That scalar minimum is excluded from novelty. Focused searches did not find an arbitrary complete-multipartite all-function weight enumerator. The Yin--Chen full text was not available, so an equivalent internal structural lemma remains a specific residual risk rather than being treated as negative evidence.

## Value
PASS. A domination number retains only the least feasible weight; the enumerator retains every feasible weight together with its multiplicity. The result therefore gives a complete census of signed Roman assignments for a standard and widely used graph class and can recover counts of minimum as well as nonminimum solutions.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest exact published graph-class results are the complete-bipartite scalar computation of Zhao--Miao and the complete-multipartite scalar paper of Yin--Chen. The present claim is restricted to complete multipartite graphs, and the packaged finite checker stops at order \(9\). The arbitrary-order statement is proved symbolically. The exact-day public-date record used here is \(2014\text{-}04\text{-}08\); the founding article also carries \(2012\) copyright/date-only evidence, whose exact day was not established.
