# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. If \(d\) is a consecutive gap modulo \(P_k\), the \(p_{k+1}\) shifts of a realizing interval preserve every old-prime divisibility relation. At most two shifts make a new-prime divisor hit an endpoint, so at least one shift survives because \(p_{k+1}\ge3\). Thus every old gap value persists. The row-\(44\) database data give \(308\) distinct positive even gaps with maximum \(616\); there are exactly \(308\) positive even integers through \(616\). Hence row \(44\) is complete through \(616\), and persistence proves \(N_{\min}(45)\ge616=h(44)\).

## Originality
PASS. The general persistence mechanism is not claimed as new; related wheel recursions already exist. The reviewed claim is the explicit \(k=45\) consequence for Ziller's conjecture. The primary source reports computation only through \(k=44\), and targeted exact, equivalent-form, database, and broader-framework searches did not locate a publication giving \(N_{\min}(45)\ge616\). Brown's later full-text treatment of primorial unit-group gaps was inspected and does not state this threshold result.

## Value
PASS. This resolves the first case beyond the published computational frontier of a stated conjecture. The argument is not an arbitrary one-row recomputation: it exposes why the next case follows from the preceding complete row and shows that a direct construction of the enormous \(45\)-th primorial residue system is unnecessary. It also isolates the genuine new obstruction for the next case, namely the interval from \(618\) to \(642\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Mario Ziller, “On differences between consecutive numbers coprime to primorials,” arXiv:2007.01808v1. Fred Holt's cycle-of-gaps recursion supplies related persistence machinery, and Steven Brown's 2024 paper gives general fixed-gap counting formulas for the same primorial unit groups. The result proves only \(k=45\); it does not establish the conjecture at \(k=46\) or beyond. An unindexed prior observation remains possible.
