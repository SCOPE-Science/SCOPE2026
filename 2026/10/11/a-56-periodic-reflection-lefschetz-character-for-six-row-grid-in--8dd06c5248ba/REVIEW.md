# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The augmented Hopf trace assigns \((-1)^{s-1}\) to a fixed independent set with \(s\) vertex orbits, proving the signed independence-polynomial quotient formula, including the empty simplex. Direct inspection of the four orbit quotients gives the explicit row-transfer boundaries. The finite matrix assertions \(T^{28}\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), \(T^{28}e=e\), and \(T^{28}o=o\) were reconstructed independently from a 21-state integral matrix and checked by the embedded verifier, which also checks the separate two-row four-period relation. This proves all-parameter periodicity without extrapolating finite sample values. Direct quotient-graph recursion supplies 32 independent small-parameter checks. The 56-table and proper-period-divisor witnesses establish least period exactly \(56\). Limits: only alternating rational homology representations are classified, not each individual degree.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Matsushita–Wakatsuki [1, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2] supply the nonequivariant homotopy decomposition and period-28 ordinary Euler characteristic. Section 3 of the same paper employs vertical reflection to identify auxiliary graph subcomplexes, but never calculates the induced character of either reflection or the half-turn; the reflection *being used* in their proof is not the reflection *representation being determined* here. Bousquet-Mélou–Linusson–Nevo [2, Sections 6–7] provide general hard-square transfer matrices and related spectra, but not the boundary-sensitive half-turn value or period-56 full reflection character. Published-result and semantic-record searches for these equivalent formulations, stronger equivariant claims, half-turn orbits, and the period-56 sequence returned no covering statement. Unindexed equivalent work or an unnoticed indirect theorem remains possible; no claim of guaranteed uniqueness is made.

**Value — PASS.** The natural horizontal/vertical reflections act on a topology-rich infinite graph family, so their rational virtual homology character is a meaningful invariant that nonequivariant sphere multiplicities do not determine. The reflected character doubles the minimum ordinary Euler period, is uniformly bounded despite growing grid size, and gives a concrete decomposition into one-dimensional symmetry types at \(n=8\). The result is a full all-parameter symmetry classification, not an arbitrary special-case recomputation.

[2] arXiv:math/0701890v2, Sections 6–7. Other independence-grid papers cited in [1] address nonequivariant or cylindrical variants. Residual risk is a separate unindexed equivariant calculation or a general transfer theorem making the boundary identity an implicit corollary.
