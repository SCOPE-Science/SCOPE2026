# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The 2009 full text gives the complete facet list and dimension for the admissible parameters. Projecting the standard coordinate and row/column normals into the fixed-total tangent hyperplane gives squared norms \(1-1/N^2\) and \(N-1\). Summing their clearance inequalities gives two sharp upper bounds, both attained at the uniform matrix. Equality in the coordinate bound forces a unique center; equality in the row/column bound forces constant margins and leaves exactly a translated scaled Birkhoff polytope. The vertex barycenter identity certifies the unique outer sphere. The packaged LP and small-vertex computations independently corroborate these statements.

## Originality
PASS with explicit residual indexing risk. Both primary full texts were inspected rather than inferred from titles: Torres develops normality and bipartite H-representations, while Gill--Linusson develops facets, graph diameter, face lattice and ear decompositions. Neither gives the intrinsic Euclidean inradius, its square-root transition, or the full Birkhoff-polytope incenter family. Candidate-specific database and literature searches covered k-assignment, partial permutation matrix, nonattacking rooks, partial Birkhoff and fixed-sum doubly substochastic aliases and Euclidean-center formulations. The older same-object facet source was fully inspected, and the closest related transportation and all-matching results do not imply this theorem under the required Frobenius metric.

## Value
PASS. The complete classification of all largest-ball centers for an important fixed-size assignment family reveals a jump from one point to a Birkhoff polytope of dimension \((N-1)^2\). It includes exact radii, the integer critical ranks, tangency strata, and the contrast with the unique all-contact outer sphere. These are natural metric characteristics of the assignment constraints rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or a known table recomputation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Gill--Linusson's 2009 *The k-assignment polytope* and Torres's 2023 *Normality of k-Matching Polytopes of Bipartite Graphs* supply the exact source data. Their graph-diameter and normality theorems do not cover Frobenius inspheres. Rectangular boards, the cardinality endpoints, arbitrary bipartite graphs, and affine-invariant ellipsoids remain outside the claim. A classical formulation in matrix geometry may still be unindexed.
