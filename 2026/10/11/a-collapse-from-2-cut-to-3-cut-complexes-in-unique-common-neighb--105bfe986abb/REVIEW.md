# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. Every nonedge uv has a unique common neighbor w. The complementary path face V ∖ {u,v,w} is a free codimension-one face of the facet V ∖ {u,v} in the ordinary two-cut complex. These free faces are distinct, and deleting all pairs leaves exactly the ordinary three-cut complex: a surviving complement containing a nonedge either contains a disconnected triple or would have been one of the deleted pairs. Bayer et al.'s connected triangle-free theorem then supplies the wedge type. Independent finite enumeration confirms 17 eligible labeled five-vertex graphs, six eligible labeled six-vertex graphs, and the Petersen nonempty face vectors and 30 collapse pairs. This finite check does not substitute for the all-graph proof.

## Originality
PASS for the explicit simplicial collapse, not for the derived sphere count. Bayer et al. compute the two-cut homotopy type, without the three-cut inclusion collapse. A separate published result, *Three-cut complexes of graphs without short cycles: sphere wedges and symmetry characters*, already determines the ordinary three-cut homotopy type for every triangle- and square-free graph with n≥7, including the six-sphere Petersen result. Its proof collapses the Alexander dual, not the actual two-cut complex onto the three-cut complex. Equality of abstract homotopy types does not imply that the inclusion is a simplicial collapse. Results for squared cycles and powers of cycles cover different graph families. The direct structural bridge survives these comparisons as the contribution assessed here.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The graph-theoretic unique-common-neighbor condition yields a canonical, explicit simple-homotopy bridge between two consecutive natural cut-complex constructions. This relative collapse is finer than either already-known wedge decomposition and identifies the precise local obstruction (multiple common neighbors) when the hypothesis is dropped. The graph class contains stars and standard Moore-graph examples; it is not an arbitrary parameter substitution or small computation.

## Scope and residual priority risk
The theorem applies only to finite connected simple graphs with n≥5, no triangles or four-cycles and diameter at most two. It does not assert shellability or a universal theorem for arbitrary graphs. The wider three-cut wedge formula is already published in a same-date record, so the numerical Petersen corollary is not offered as separately original. A differently phrased earlier theorem establishing the inclusion collapse could remain unindexed. The finite verifier checks selected examples only.
