# Scientific review: cycle-anchor invariant and exact flex geodesics

**Correctness — PASS.** The signed orientation cochain difference is twice an integral gradient precisely when the signatures coincide. Directed cycles saturate their signature, hence cannot differ between orientations with that signature. Their vertices can never be flexed; unequal potential levels on different directed-cycle components obstruct any path. If those levels are equal, normalization to zero on all cyclic vertices makes positive maximum level sets and negative minimum level sets acyclic, allowing legitimate source/sink flips decreasing the potential's absolute sum. Net per-vertex flip counts give the exact matching distance lower bound. The proof handles all connected finite simple graphs with at least one edge; eleven finite checks involving 62,804 ordered orientation pairs independently reproduced the predicted distances.

**Originality — PASS.** Kozlov, *Flex complexes of graphs* (2026), Proposition 7.2 proves signature invariance and Question 2 explicitly asks for a complete algebraic augmentation; his Fig. 18 supplies a counterexample to signature-only classification. The present relative cyclic-anchor invariant answers that question. Aichholzer et al, *Flip Distances Between Graph Orientations* (2021), Theorems 6 and 10 address acyclic c-orientations with a distinguished root after directed-cycle contraction, whereas contracting a cyclic region changes the original legal-vertex-flex relation. The older acyclic branch is prior art; novel coverage is arbitrary cyclic strata together with their exact original-graph geodesic formula. Search comparisons also considered Propp's fixed-circulation lattice and toric-poset acyclic click equivalence, and published indexed results with related terminology.

**Value — PASS.** The result supplies a finite integer-potential criterion for every orientation pair of every eligible graph and constructs a shortest path. It resolves a specifically stated published structural reconstruction question instead of adding isolated finite tables. Distinct frozen cycle anchors explain signature-blind disconnected components and recover reachability and distance with one simple extra obstruction.

**Closest-source and limitation notes.** The fixed-root acyclic c-orientation lattice machinery of Aichholzer et al and Propp may suggest different derivations; the latter's original full text was inaccessible, though its abstract and downstream theorem were examined. An unindexed earlier equivalent remains possible. The result does not describe higher-dimensional cubical attaching maps or new homotopy types, and its graph scope excludes disconnected inputs (which may be handled componentwise).
