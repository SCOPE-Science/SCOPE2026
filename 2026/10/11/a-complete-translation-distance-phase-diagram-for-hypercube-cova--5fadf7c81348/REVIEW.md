# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exact cube-overlap product is transformed into a constrained sum of logarithms. On a support face, stationary magnitudes are either equal or complementary; an unequal complementary pair violates the second-order condition for a maximum. Equal-coordinate supports exhaust all global maxima, and the one-dimensional continuation and adjacent-pair crossing analysis prove every threshold and all equality cases. The numerical script reproduces the cube product and searches independently across sampled directions; it is diagnostic, not the basis of the proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical uncertainty.** Bianchi (arXiv:1312.7816) solves covariogram determination problems, Bianchi–Gardner–Kiderlen (arXiv:1003.4486) develop data-based reconstruction algorithms and cube brightness estimates, and Schymura (arXiv:1010.2446) gives general symmetric-difference bounds for translated shapes. These primary mathematical claims were compared as implications, not by title alone. They do not give the fixed-norm directional maximizers, full equal-support classification, or the strictly ordered support-switch thresholds. A classical constrained-product inequality not indexed by these terms remains a residual historical risk.

## Value

**PASS.** A prescribed Euclidean misalignment in cube matching has an exact, dimension-uniform optimal overlap profile. The full classification has an observable geometric transition: first displacement along one axis and then successive activation of more axes, ending with the body diagonal. All \(d-1\) transitions and their asymptotic law are determined, yielding a reusable geometric benchmark rather than an isolated computation.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem is not claimed for general convex bodies, anisotropic boxes, rotations, or radial minima. Previous exact covariogram definitions and bounds are credited, and publication priority remains subject to independent literature evaluation.
