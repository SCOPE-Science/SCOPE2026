# Scientific review: concentration criterion for product measures

## Correctness — PASS
For every compatible metric, uniform coordinate boxes and two-sided Bowen balls contain one another at appropriately chosen radii, by product-topology compactness and uniform continuity. Independence turns box mass into the infinite product of closed-interval masses. Maximizing independently identifies the exact worst-case product. Uniform positive lower bounds on each maximizing mass prove equivalence of logarithmic-product vanishing and divergence of the deficits. The fixed-radius upper semicontinuity lemma follows from Portmanteau with moving closed intervals; its lower-semicontinuous complement yields the displayed dense \(G_\delta\). The summably contaminated shrinking-interval family has full-support nonatomic marginals and produces a positive box for every radius. None of these deductions requires finite numerical inference.

## Originality — PASS (residual source-access risk)
The full primary-source introduction, principal theorem statements and section 6 proof of Pedrosa–Rego–Trilles concern continuous flows, not the two-sided nonstationary product criterion or its weak-product-space Baire dichotomy. Lee–Morales–Shin establish general ambient \(G_{\delta\sigma}\) complexity but no product concentration classification. The closely related later paper of Lacerda establishes the Hilbert-cube product Lebesgue example and genericity for an ambient space, based on available abstract and opening theorem statements. It does not visibly provide the exact series criterion or dense non-expansive full-support nonatomic product class. Equivalent prior treatment in product probability or an inaccessible part of Lacerda remains a concrete risk; absent an implication or identical theorem in accessible material, the independently proved stronger classification is not treated as covered.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A sharp necessary-and-sufficient criterion decides an entire infinite-dimensional, nonstationary class from the one-coordinate concentration functions. The density-meagreness dichotomy and the explicit full-support nonatomic counterexamples show a structurally relevant distinction: product-law expansivity is generic yet cannot be read off support, non-atomicity, or isolated finite marginal data. This addresses a natural mathematical question about the topology of expansive measures, rather than a single ad hoc example.

## Boundaries and source risks
The theorem concerns the weak product topology of independent marginals, not all laws on the Hilbert cube. Generalizations to dependent processes and one-sided time are unproved. The Lacerda full text was not completely inspected; its closely relevant statements were compared using only material actually seen. More obscure equivalent small-ball criteria remain possible.
