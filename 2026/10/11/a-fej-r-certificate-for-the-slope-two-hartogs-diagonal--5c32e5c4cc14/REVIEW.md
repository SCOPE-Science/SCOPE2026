# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Substitution of \(m=2n+1\) into the five-term primary kernel formula gives the parity identities for \(L\) and \(\kappa\), the explicit palindromic coefficients, and the Fejér decomposition. For every integer \(n\ge1\), completing a square yields the uniform lower bound \(2n+1-1/n>0\). Reciprocal symmetry and the nonzero constant coefficient then force exactly \(n+1\) roots inside and outside the circle, counted with multiplicity. The supplied exact-integer checker confirmed agreement between the independently generated coefficients and the closed formula for all \(1\le n\le500\); the proof itself covers all positive integers. Denominator nonvanishing was checked on the punctured diagonal.

## Originality
PASS, with residual risks. Edholm--Mathew's Theorem 1.7 covers differences \(1\) and \(2\); their Section 4.3 reports additional fixed differences \(3,4,6\) and work in preparation, not the unbounded difference \(m-n=n+1\). Edholm--McNeal's Theorem 3.8 covers existence of arbitrary kernel zeros when the exponent is at least \(2\), but their proof uses the coordinate slice \(s=0\) and does not imply diagonal root counts or the circle lower bound. Searches for the equivalent representations \(Q_{2n+1,n}\), rational slope \(2+1/n\), reciprocal trigonometric positivity, and Fejér certificates yielded no inspected stronger result. Earlier specialized finite-gap results do not imply the all-integer parameter statement. Unindexed, unpublished, or forthcoming work remains a risk; search absence is not a proof of novelty.

## Value
PASS. The natural arithmetic ray \(m=2n+1\) tends to the geometric exponent \(2\), while its diagonal polynomials have unbounded degree \(2n+2\) and escape any fixed-gap result. The statement resolves an infinite, unbounded-degree subfamily of an explicitly open diagonal root-count conjecture, supplies a uniform quantitative nonvanishing margin, and yields the asymptotically tight positive minimum interval \([2n+1-1/n,2n+1]\). It is not merely another example of already-known existence of some kernel zero.

## Scope and limitations
The full rational-exponent conjecture is not resolved. Root simplicity, off-diagonal zero locations, and uniqueness of zero configurations are not asserted. The proof's main literature comparison is to the 2025/2026 source and the 2017 rational-Hartogs theorem, and originality against unindexed work is not guaranteed.
