# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The stated object is the canonical Fibonacci finite-type cover admitting length-\(n+1\) factors. The proof checks the exact offset: the only excluded composable pair at a Rauzy-graph level comes from a bispecial middle factor, so nonbispecial levels yield a directed line graph and preserve spectral radius. The explicit Fibonacci bispecial graph geometry is supplied by Bruin–Leplaideur (2013); it gives the two-loop Perron equation at each right endpoint. Continuity, Fibonacci asymptotics, and integer interpolation give *all* rescaled accumulation points. An independent exact finite graph checker passed lengths \(1\) through \(200\). Those diagnostics do not prove the infinite combinatorics.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The 2026 work *Stable covers of subshifts* defines and analyzes finite-type cover stabilization but does not state this sharp Fibonacci entropy staircase. Bruin–Leplaideur (2013), the mathematically closest source, already determines the bispecial lengths and return-loop geometry: that ingredient is expressly prior work. The inspected Fibonacci Rauzy and central-word papers likewise give structural facts rather than the exact all-index entropy sequence or its accumulation set. Several research-record and publication searches under Markov approximation, Rauzy graph and entropy aliases returned no direct duplicate. An unstated but immediate corollary in older work is a nontrivial residual priority risk; no universal novelty certificate is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result solves a natural quantitative finite-memory approximation question for a canonical zero-entropy subshift. The full interval of first-order scaled entropy constants is a structural obstruction to replacing the convergence rate by a single limit, relevant when comparing SFT approximations at different word lengths. Its scope is deliberately confined to the natural Fibonacci language, and no unsupported downstream theorem is claimed.
