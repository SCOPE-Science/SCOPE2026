# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The sharp minimizer classification is used only in the ranges in which the source proves it. On the acute branch, the width-realizing altitude of the regular spherical triangle gives the exact side relation by spherical Pythagoras; the equilateral cosine law and spherical excess then give the area branch. On the obtuse branch, the source's polar area--perimeter identity reduces the minimizer area to the perimeter of a spherical Reuleaux triangle; its three congruent small-circle arcs give the second branch. Exact differentiation yields the one-sided slopes \(3/\sqrt2\) and \(3\). The symbolic verification artifact reproduces these limits.

## Originality

PASS with a stated residual literature risk. The source paper gives the two minimizing families and notes agreement at \(w=\pi/2\), but the checked theorem/proof text does not state an exact minimum-value profile, compare its one-sided derivatives, or identify a cusp. Searches for the spherical Pál value function, its transition derivative, equivalent Reuleaux-polar formulations, and the exact slope pair found no checked result implying the claim. The closest 2026 stability work concerns quantitative stability rather than differentiability of the optimal value.

## Value

PASS. The regime switch in the sharp theorem could a priori be smooth at the level of the optimal value. The exact slope jump shows that it is instead a first-order phase transition and quantifies the loss of sensitivity at the critical width. This is a structural fact about the sharp variational profile and provides a natural benchmark for any stability analysis that crosses \(w=\pi/2\).

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary source is Freyer--Sagmeister, arXiv:2411.11462v1, whose sharp classification supplies the two branches. Fodor--Sagmeister, arXiv:2606.02882v1, is close by topic through stability. The latter was available only at abstract level in the checked public material. No claim is made about higher-order shape regularity, higher dimensions, or uniqueness beyond the source theorem.
