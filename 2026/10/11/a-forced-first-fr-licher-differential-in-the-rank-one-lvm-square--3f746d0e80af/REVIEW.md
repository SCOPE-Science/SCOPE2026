# Review of A forced first Frölicher differential in the rank-one LVM square

## Correctness

PASS. The source gives
\[
h^{0,1}=2,
\qquad
h^{1,0}=1,
\qquad
h^{2,0}=0,
\qquad
b_1=2.
\]
The unique \((1,0)\) class survives to \(E_\infty\): the incoming differential from constants is zero, the first outgoing target \(E_1^{2,0}\) vanishes, and later outgoing targets have negative antiholomorphic degree.

Therefore total-degree-one convergence forces
\[
\dim E_\infty^{0,1}=1.
\]
No differential enters \((0,1)\). After \(d_1\), its only possible higher outgoing differential would be
\[
d_2:E_2^{0,1}\to E_2^{2,0},
\]
whose target is zero. Hence
\[
E_\infty^{0,1}=\ker d_1^{0,1}
\]
has dimension one, proving rank one. The punctured-fibre rank is zero because the source proves \(E_1\)-degeneration there.

## Originality

PASS. The fresh source proves only that deficient curvature prevents \(E_1\)-degeneration and explicitly lists computation of deficient-rank Frölicher differentials as a remaining problem. It does not state the rank of
\[
d_1^{0,1}
\]
for the rank-one square.

The closest older moment-angle paper identifies a differential with the same bidegree in a rational moment-angle setting and proves \(E_2\)-degeneration there. A later general moment-angle DGA model also supplies related background. The fresh source nevertheless treats deficient curvature separately and states that its differentials still require a model retaining both Dolbeault operators.

Claim-specific published-result and literature searches did not locate the exact rank-one statement or its rank-jump formulation.

## Value

PASS. The source singles out deficient-rank Frölicher differentials as an unresolved structural problem. The rank-one square is its canonical explicit deficient example and lies in a proper holomorphic family whose topology is fixed while the Hodge diamond jumps.

Determining an actual nonzero differential, even without a full double-complex model, identifies the mechanism behind the first-degree obstruction and gives an explicit rank jump under holomorphic deformation. This is a structural spectral-sequence fact, not a restatement of the Hodge-number excess.
