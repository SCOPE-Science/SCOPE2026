# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The source radius-of-curvature density is \(1-3\varepsilon\cos(2t)-8\varepsilon\cos(3t)\), so \(|\varepsilon|<1/11\) gives a strict oval. Its Steiner centroid is exactly zero because the support has no first harmonic. The orthoptic parametrization is the published isoptic formula at \(\alpha=\pi/2\). For \(z'=Be_t+Cn_t\), direct differentiation gives the curvature measure \(1+(BC'-CB')/(B^2+C^2)\). Its exact second-order centroid expansion has zero constant and linear coefficients and quadratic coefficient \((2,0)\). Positive curvature of the orthoptic for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) follows by \(C^2\)-continuity from the circular case. The packaged symbolic replay verifies the critical trigonometric coefficients.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 2014 primary source explicitly calls general Steiner-centroid coincidence for an oval and its isoptics an open problem and proves only the circular-isoptic case. The directly relevant later follow-up classifies circular isoptics and invokes only that special theorem. It does not cover arbitrary noncircular isoptics. Searches by the open-problem wording, orthoptic terminology, curvature-centroid formulation, and the exact support perturbation located no covering counterexample or theorem. Residual risk remains for an unindexed later note.

## Value

The counterexample uses only two Fourier modes, and the exact quadratic displacement isolates a simple mechanism: interaction of the even and odd support harmonics generates the first-harmonic drift of the orthoptic curvature centroid.
