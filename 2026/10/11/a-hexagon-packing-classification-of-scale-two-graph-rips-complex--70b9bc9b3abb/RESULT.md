# A hexagon-packing classification of scale-two graph Rips complexes

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph with girth at least six. Assume it contains at least one simple six-cycle and that any two distinct simple six-cycles have disjoint edge sets (they may share vertices). Set
\[
 b(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1,
 \qquad h(G)=\#\{\text{unoriented simple six-cycles in }G\}.
\]
Give \(V(G)\) the shortest-path metric with unit edge lengths, and use the **inclusive** Vietoris–Rips complex \(K=\operatorname{VR}_{\leq 2}(V(G))\): a nonempty vertex subset is a simplex precisely when all pairwise graph distances are at most two.

**Theorem.** Under these assumptions, \(h(G)\leq b(G)\) and
\[
\boxed{K\simeq\Bigl(\bigvee^{b(G)-h(G)}S^1\Bigr)\vee
                 \Bigl(\bigvee^{h(G)}S^2\Bigr).}
\]
Here a wedge with zero summands means a point. More precisely, for the canonical inclusion \(G=\operatorname{VR}_{\leq 1}(V(G))\hookrightarrow K\), the map on integral first homology is onto, with kernel freely and primitively generated by the oriented six-cycle classes. The integral second homology has one free generator per six-cycle; all higher reduced integral homology vanishes. Thus precisely \(h(G)\) independent integral degree-one classes die and \(h(G)\) independent degree-two classes appear between inclusive scales one and two.

The theorem allows branching, vertices of arbitrary degree, and overlapping six-cycles **at vertices**. For example, for three internally disjoint paths of lengths three, three and four sharing their two endpoints, the conclusion is \(S^1\vee S^2\); this graph is not a cactus or a one-point wedge of circles.

## Assumptions and scope

A simple six-cycle is an undirected cycle visiting six distinct vertices; reverse traversal and changes of starting vertex do not create another cycle. The edge-disjointness condition applies to **all** such cycles, not just a selected cycle basis. The girth restriction excludes cycles of lengths three, four and five. No assumption is made about induced subgraphs, regularity, planarity, or the length of longer cycles. Distances always refer to the whole graph rather than to a subgraph or a continuously interpolated metric graph. The claim concerns the single inclusive threshold two and the induced map from threshold one; it makes no assertion about higher thresholds.

## Proof

**1. Uniqueness of length-two geodesics.** Because \(G\) has no four-cycle, any two vertices at distance two have exactly one common neighbor. Because \(G\) has no triangle, the neighbors of each vertex are pairwise nonadjacent.

**2. The centered subcomplex.** Put
\[
B=\bigcup_{c\in V(G)}\Delta[N_G[c]]\subseteq K,
\]
where \(N_G[c]\) is the closed graph neighborhood and \(\Delta[S]\) is the full simplex on \(S\). The original graph is a subcomplex of \(B\). Every face of \(B\setminus G\) contains a distance-two pair, hence belongs to a unique closed-neighborhood simplex: the midpoint of any such pair determines its center uniquely. For every center \(c\), pair each face \(\sigma\subseteq N_G(c)\) with \(|\sigma|\geq2\) to \(\sigma\cup\{c\}\). Process the former in decreasing order of cardinality. Faces in different centered simplices cannot interfere because of the unique-center property; within one simplex, every larger coface has already been removed. Thus each pair is an elementary free-face collapse, and all faces outside \(G\) disappear. In particular,
\[
B\searrow G.
\]
Each distance-two edge \(uv\) is replaced up to homotopy (relative to the original graph) by the two-edge path \(u-c-v\) through its unique midpoint \(c\).

**3. The exceptional triangles.** Take a triangle \(\{a,c,e\}\) of \(K\) that is not contained in \(B\). A distance pattern \((1,2,2)\) would give a simple cycle of length at most five by combining the adjacent edge and the two unique shortest length-two paths. Patterns with at least two adjacent edges are either impossible (three graph edges) or contained in a single closed neighborhood. Therefore all three distances are exactly two. Let their unique midpoints be \(b,d,f\) in the paths \(a-b-c\), \(c-d-e\), and \(e-f-a\). If any two midpoints agreed, the triple would be centered. Otherwise these six vertices are all distinct and form a simple six-cycle
\[
a-b-c-d-e-f-a.
\]
Conversely, the two alternating vertex triples \(\{a,c,e\}\) and \(\{b,d,f\}\) of any simple six-cycle are triangles of \(K\) not contained in \(B\): if one had a common graph neighbor, its corresponding pair of hexagon vertices would have two distinct midpoints, giving a four-cycle.

**4. The exceptional triangles are maximal.** Suppose the exceptional triangle \(\{a,c,e\}\) is contained in a larger simplex, and let \(x\notin\{a,c,e\}\) be another vertex in that simplex. It cannot be adjacent in \(G\) to any of \(a,c,e\). Indeed, if \(x\) were adjacent to \(a\), the requirement \(d_G(x,c)\leq2\) forces \(x\) to be the unique midpoint \(b\) of \(a-b-c\); otherwise the union of an edge \(x-a\), a path \(a-b-c\) and a path of length at most two from \(c\) to \(x\) would contain a cycle of length at most five. But \(d_G(b,e)\geq3\), since a length-two path from \(b\) to \(e\) together with either of the two hexagon three-edge routes would again give a cycle of length at most five. The same argument applies to \(c,e\). Hence \(d_G(x,a)=d_G(x,c)=d_G(x,e)=2\).

At most one of \(\{a,c,x\}\), \(\{c,e,x\}\), and \(\{e,a,x\}\) can be centered: if the first two were centered then \(x\) would be adjacent to both \(b\) and \(d\), creating the four-cycle \(b-c-d-x-b\), and similarly for any two. Thus at least one such triangle, say \(\{a,c,x\}\), is exceptional and produces a **different** simple six-cycle containing the two graph edges \(a-b\), \(b-c\) of the original hexagon. This violates the hypothesis that distinct six-cycles are edge-disjoint. No simplex strictly contains an exceptional triangle.

It follows that \(K\) is obtained from \(B\) by adjoining **exactly two two-simplices per six-cycle**, with no higher noncentered simplices.

**5. Attaching loops.** The three boundary edges of either alternating triangle have graph length two. Under the collapse \(B\searrow G\) in Step 2, their attaching loop becomes, up to free homotopy, the full six-edge boundary of the corresponding graph hexagon. The two alternating triangles therefore attach two 2-cells along the same graph loop, up to orientation and homotopy. Homotopy invariance of attaching maps gives a CW model consisting of \(G\) and two such 2-cells per six-cycle.

**6. Canceling the first disk in each pair.** Attach one disk along every hexagon. Choose a graph edge \(e_i\) on hexagon \(i\). Because the hexagons are pairwise edge-disjoint, this edge appears once in its chosen attaching map and in none of the other chosen maps. Consequently this disk and its private graph edge form a free CW-cell pair and can be collapsed, for each \(i\), leaving the graph obtained from \(G\) by deleting one edge from each of its six-cycles. Those edge deletions do not disconnect \(G\), since the remainder of each hexagon supplies an alternative path that contains none of the other selected edges. The remaining connected graph has first Betti number \(b(G)-h(G)\), proving in particular \(h(G)\leq b(G)\). The second disk associated with each hexagon attaches along a loop already nullhomotopic after the first disk is attached; by the homotopy-invariance of cell attachments it contributes a wedged \(S^2\). These sphere classes are independent. This proves the asserted homotopy equivalence.

**7. Map on first homology.** In the cellular chain complex of the model before the cancellations, the boundary of each of the two hexagon 2-cells is the same primitive integral oriented six-cycle class up to sign. Their supports on graph edges are disjoint. Thus these classes are integrally independent and primitive, and the map \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) has exactly the asserted kernel and cokernel. The differences of each pair of 2-cells give a free basis of \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). The graph-plus-two-cell model has no higher homology. This also proves the stated scale-one-to-scale-two persistence transition. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying dependency-free `verify_hexagon.py` reconstructs graph-distance Rips complexes from edge lists, enumerates every simple six-cycle, checks girth and pairwise hexagon-edge disjointness, and independently checks all noncentered simplices against the two alternating triangles per hexagon. It also checks an actual elementary-collapse sequence for \(B\searrow G\), and computes the complete reduced mod-two homology by exact binary elimination.

Run from the package directory:

```text
python3 verify_hexagon.py
```

The cases include one hexagon, two vertex-touching hexagons, two bridged hexagons, one hexagon together with a seven- or eight-cycle, and theta graphs with path lengths \((3,3,4)\), \((3,3,5)\), and \((3,4,5)\). The last is a zero-hexagon boundary sanity check; the theorem itself assumes at least one hexagon. A separate three-hexagon theta graph with overlapping six-cycle edges is checked as an out-of-scope boundary, where noncentered tetrahedra indeed appear. All checks produce `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks support the proof but do not replace its all-graph arguments, especially the structural exclusion of larger noncentered cliques and the CW cancellations.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers*, Proposition 2.2, proves that a graph of girth at least seven has its scale-two clique complex collapsing onto the original graph; its example of a single six-cycle gives a sphere. Neither statement determines the mixture of spheres and loops for a graph of girth six with several edge-disjoint hexagons. Its Theorem 3.2 also assumes the absence of six-cycles and is not applicable.

Adamaszek and collaborators, *On Homotopy Types of Vietoris–Rips Complexes of Metric Gluings*, Theorem 3.15 and Proposition 4.1, classify gluing constructions along sufficiently short paths, including some one-point wedges of circles. Those results imply special cases of the formula here but do not cover arbitrary graphs with edge-disjoint hexagons. In particular, a theta graph with path lengths \((3,3,4)\) meets the present assumptions but is not generated by the cited one-point/one-edge circle attachment criterion, and its relevant shared path is too long for the metric gluing threshold.

Saleh, Titz Mite and Witzel, *Vietoris–Rips complexes of Platonic solids*, analyze the same **graph-shortest-path distance** Rips construction on selected highly symmetric finite graphs, motivating the need for structural predictions outside special examples. Their classification does not state the present all-graph hexagon-packing result. The homotopy classification of individual six-cycles and the high-girth collapse are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The hypothesis must concern **every** six-cycle: if distinct six-cycles share graph edges, extra cliques can occur and the formula is not asserted. Without the girth assumption, length-two midpoint uniqueness fails. The paper does not classify higher scales, metric graphs including edge-interior points, or the equivariant homotopy type. This proof is mathematical rather than computer-assisted for arbitrary graph order; the finite verifier is a finite-case diagnostic, not an exhaustive proof over all graphs.
