A noncentered clique triangle must be one alternating triple of a simple six-cycle; if it had a fourth compatible vertex, another six-cycle would share at least one graph edge, contradicting the all-six-cycle edge-disjointness hypothesis. Therefore exactly two noncentered maximal triangles occur per hexagon and no higher noncentered simplices. They attach twice along the same primitive six-edge cycle of G; attach/collapse the first disk against a different private edge per hexagon, then attach the second disk to create an S2. Finite examples corroborate, not prove, the all-graph argument.
