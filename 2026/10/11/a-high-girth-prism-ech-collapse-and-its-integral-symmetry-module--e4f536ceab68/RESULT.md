# A high-girth prism Čech collapse and its integral symmetry modules

## Finding

Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite connected simple graph with \(n=|V|\ge2\), \(m=|E|\ge1\), no triangles and no four-cycles. Let \(P=H\square K_2\), with \(v_i=(v,i)\) for \(i\in\{0,1\}\). For the intrinsic unit-edge metric and the nerve of closed radius-one balls centered at vertices, set \(K=\mathcal N(P,2)\). Then **an explicit elementary simplicial collapse** gives

\[
K\searrow K'=\bigcup_{\{x,y\}\in E}\partial\Delta(\{x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1\}),\qquad K\simeq H\vee\bigvee^{m}S^2.
\]

The collapse has precisely \(4\sum_{v\in V}(2^{\deg_H(v)}-\deg_H(v)-1)\) elementary pairs. Hence the nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{m-n+1}\) (when this rank is positive) and \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^m\). The canonical sphere cycles make \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) the edge-permutation module \(\mathbb Z[E]\) under \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\); the layer-switching involution acts trivially. On \(H_1\), the action is the ordinary \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\)-action on \(H_1(H;\mathbb Z)\), and the layer switch is trivial.

For \(j:\mathcal N(P,1)=P\hookrightarrow\mathcal N(P,2)\), the induced map \(j_*:H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) is surjective. Its kernel is freely generated by the \(m\) oriented vertical square cycles, and, as an integral \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\times C_2\)-module, is \(C_1(H;\mathbb Z)\) with the natural signed oriented-edge action and a negative layer-switch action. Thus precisely \(m\) independent integral first-homology classes die and \(m\) independent second-homology classes are born at scale two.

## Assumptions and scope

A graph with no three- or four-cycles includes every connected tree and every graph of girth at least five. No degree bound, regularity or planarity is assumed. The statement concerns the **finite-vertex Čech nerve in the geometric realization of a unit metric graph**, not a Vietoris–Rips complex or the Lovász open-neighborhood complex. The scale-one complex is exactly \(P\); the scale-two nerve is generated by closed graph neighborhoods \(N_P[z]\). Define \(d(v)=\deg_H(v)\). The empty-face convention does not affect the elementary collapses or reduced homology.

## Proof

**1. Identify the scale-two maximal generators.** The even-scale graph Čech description (Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2) gives

\[
K=\bigcup_{v\in V,\,i\in\{0,1\}}\Delta(B_{v,i}),\qquad B_{v,i}=\{v_i,v_{1-i}\}\cup W_{v,i},\qquad W_{v,i}=\{w_i:\{v,w\}\in E\}.
\]

For distinct \(a_i,b_i\in W_{v,i}\), the only generating simplex containing both is \(\Delta(B_{v,i})\). Indeed, an opposite-layer center has at most one vertex in layer \(i\) in its closed neighborhood. A different same-layer center would be a second common neighbor of \(a\) and \(b\), and would form a triangle with \(v\) or a four-cycle through \(v\). Both are excluded. This proves uniqueness **for every face containing at least two vertices of \(W_{v,i}\)**.

**2. Exhibit all elementary free-face pairs.** For each \((v,i)\), each subset \(A\subseteq W_{v,i}\) with \(|A|\ge2\), and each \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), form

\[
(\sigma,\tau)=\bigl(A\cup (\{v_{1-i}\}\text{ if }\epsilon=1\text{ else }\varnothing),\quad \sigma\cup\{v_i\}\bigr).
\]

The uniqueness just proved makes these pairs disjoint across all centers, and every larger coface of \(\sigma\) lies in \(\Delta(B_{v,i})\). Order all pairs by decreasing cardinality of \(\sigma\), with arbitrary choices within each level. At the time a pair is removed, every strictly larger coface of \(\sigma\) besides \(\tau\) has already been removed: every such coface is paired in the same generator (possibly as a lower or upper face of an earlier pair). Thus \(\sigma\) has the unique remaining immediate coface \(\tau\), so the pair is an elementary simplicial collapse. The number of pairs per center is \(2(2^{d(v)}-d(v)-1)\), and both layers give the announced total. This argument is for all finite graphs satisfying the hypotheses, not an extrapolation from finite enumeration.

**3. Identify the residue.** After these collapses, a surviving face in a generator \(B_{v,i}\) contains at most one horizontal neighbor \(w_i\in W_{v,i}\). For each edge \(\{v,w\}\), these faces are exactly the triangles, edges and vertices of \(\partial\Delta(\{v_0,v_1,w_0,w_1\})\). Conversely, all those faces survive because they have at most one horizontal neighbor relative to either of the two corresponding centers. Hence the residue is precisely \(K'\). Its number of nonempty faces is \(8m+3n\): each tetrahedral boundary has fourteen faces, and the only intersections between distinct boundaries are vertical edges at shared base vertices.

**4. Determine the integral homotopy type.** In \(K'\) let \(F\) be the copy \(H_0\) in layer zero together with all vertical edges \([v_0,v_1]\). Then \(F\simeq H\). For an edge \(e=\{v,w\}\), its tetrahedral boundary meets \(F\) in the contractible three-edge path \(v_1-v_0-w_0-w_1\). Intersections of different tetrahedral boundaries lie in \(F\) (they are shared vertical edges). Successively attaching each two-sphere along such a contractible path gives \(K'\simeq F\vee\bigvee_{e\in E}S^2\). In particular all integral groups are torsion-free and the statement is about homotopy, not merely mod-two homology.

**5. Identify natural group representations.** Orient the top-dimensional cycle of the tetrahedral boundary for \(e=\{v,w\}\) using \(\partial[v_0,v_1,w_0,w_1]\). Reversing the arbitrary ordering of \(v,w\) interchanges two two-vertex blocks, an even permutation, so the cycle is independent of this ordering. Any graph automorphism sends the resulting basis cycle to the cycle of its image edge without sign; simultaneously switching both layers is also even on the four vertices, hence acts as \(+1\). The inclusion \(K'\hookrightarrow K\) is equivariant and a homotopy equivalence even though the chosen collapse ordering need not be equivariant. Projection \(K'\to H\), \(v_i\mapsto v\), is simplicial and induces the described natural isomorphism on \(H_1\). Layer switching does not change this projection and therefore acts trivially on \(H_1\).

**6. Prove the persistent kernel, including signs.** The graph \(P\) together with a two-cell on each vertical quadrilateral \(v_0-w_0-w_1-v_1-v_0\) is the product of the one-dimensional CW graph \(|H|\) with an interval; it retracts onto \(|H|\). The attaching square boundaries \(q_e\), oriented by an arbitrary edge orientation in \(H\), form an integral basis of the kernel of \(H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(H;\mathbb Z)\). Each square is already null-homologous in its tetrahedral boundary in \(K'\), and projection \(K'\to H\) is an isomorphism on \(H_1\); hence this is exactly \(\ker j_*\). Reorienting \(e\) negates \(q_e\), a graph automorphism acts as on oriented edges, and switching layers reverses the square orientation. This proves the claimed integral module and exact rank \(m\).

## Verification

The standalone pure-Python program `verify_prism.py` builds the full closed-neighborhood nerve, checks **every** proposed free-face removal with the unique-remaining-coface test, verifies that the residue is precisely the asserted union of tetrahedral boundaries, and independently computes mod-two boundary ranks. It reports `VERIFY_OK` for 41 finite connected triangle- and square-free graphs: paths, cycles, stars, a theta graph, the Petersen graph, the Heawood graph, and twenty reproducibly constructed sparse graphs. Examples include the Petersen base \((n,m)=(10,15)\) with \((\beta_1,\beta_2)=(6,15)\) and the Heawood base \((14,21)\) with \((8,21)\). The program also measures failure outside the hypotheses for the triangle and four-cycle bases. These finite tests are cross-checks of, not substitutes for, the unrestricted combinatorial proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Shukla and Singh define the intrinsic graph Čech nerve and identify the even-scale neighborhood generators (Lemma 2.2); their scale-two homotopy theorem classifies **hypercubes**, not all prisms over graphs with no short cycles. Matsushita studies closed-neighborhood complexes and includes a classification for Cartesian products of two **complete** graphs, overlapping the present family only in the single-edge base case; it supplies no general high-girth prism collapse or these integral symmetry modules. Previte's earlier \(D\)-neighborhood theory treats trees, cycles and one-point unions for other graph families, not this Cartesian-prism assertion. Shukla's bounded-degree theorem uses **open** Lovász neighborhoods and hence a different complex. The previously established circular-ladder case is an acknowledged proper special case; the new statement permits arbitrary degrees, branching and higher cycle rank, and computes the natural modules rather than only Betti numbers and one kernel rank. The graph Čech and Vietoris–Rips constructions must not be interchanged.

## Limitations

The theorem requires the absence of both triangles and four-cycles in the **base** graph. For triangular and square bases, the conclusion need not hold: exact calculations give \(\widetilde\beta_2=4\) and \(7\), respectively, instead of the edge counts \(3\) and \(4\). No statement is made about barcode death times after scale two or an equivariant *sequence* of collapses. The novelty comparison may miss an unindexed equivalent result. Independent audit has not been performed.
