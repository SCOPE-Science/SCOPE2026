**Correctness — PASS.** The coordinate family has property \(\beta\) with \(\rho=0\); the norming-state triangle-equality chain forces nonnegative masses adding to one. Every full numerical value has real part at least \(r\), while an explicit two-coordinate state equals \(r\). Coordinate states all give modulus \(1\). Elementary infimum stability establishes the stated perturbation bound. All dimensions, cardinalities, strict boundaries, and scalar-field assumptions are handled analytically. Finite tests are not substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS (remaining literature risk).** Choi--Lee explicitly ask for the restricted-infimum equality in their 2022 Question 3.4. Their finite-dimensional attainment and density theorems do not imply it, and the author-uploaded later full-text version retains the question. The classical description of a diagonal numerical range gives part of the calculation, but does not state or settle the property-\(\beta\) restriction, sharp fixed-Crawford deficit, or positive-Crawford perturbation family. Searches of published research records and relevant literature located no direct covering result. An unindexed elementary counterexample remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A published mechanism proposed for the density of Crawford-attaining operators fails not only at zero Crawford number but at every strictly positive value below one, already for invertible isometries on natural sequence spaces. The obstruction reaches the maximal possible normalized deficit at each value, works for arbitrary cardinalities, and holds on an open operator-norm neighborhood. It invalidates that reduction, not the separate density question.

The source-level comparison is to G. Choi and H. J. Lee, arXiv:2201.10031, Questions 3.4–3.5 (2022), and to the author-uploaded journal-version full text.
