# Scientific review: punctured nonsplit conic sharp extension

**Correctness — PASS.** The argument first proves, using anisotropy, that every nonzero pair sum on the norm-one conic has a unique unordered representation; this includes diagonal tangent pairs. The only collision is the zero fiber of antipodal pairs. Character orthogonality then gives the exact self-convolution identity (1). The subsequent optimization keeps the unmatched retained antipode explicitly and has a sharp change of maximizer at \(q=3\). Equality in the complex triangle inequality, arithmetic–geometric mean and Cauchy–Schwarz is tracked in both regimes, proving every equality case. Replayed standard-library checks cover seven field orders, including two nonprime fields, but do not substitute for the unrestricted argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Iosevich–Koh (arXiv:0804.4505, Theorem 2) controls the \(L^2\to L^4\) norm uniformly in the field size on nondegenerate quadratic curves, without the claimed sharp constant or the deleted-point equality conditions. González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva (arXiv:2405.16647v1) give sharp results for different quadratic hypersurfaces and higher-dimensional cones; their surface list does not include the present punctured planar norm-one conic. The closest indexed finite-field sphere classification concerns an unpunctured three-variable quadratic surface at two small orders and does not imply our all-order result. Exact-norm theorems expressed through additive-energy language could escape indexed searches, so global uniqueness is not certified.

**Value — PASS.** A single lost sample is a natural perturbation of a quadratic Fourier extension surface. The answer does more than give a bound: it shows that for every odd order at least five an optimal coefficient vector has to discard a second, surviving, precisely identified sample; in the smallest field it instead uses every available sample equally. The fully quantified constants and equality geometry provide an explicit sharp benchmark for erasure sensitivity of a structured Fourier operator. This is not a bare renormalization of the uniform extension exponent.

The exact proof is restricted to nonsplit conics with one deletion and to fourth moments; neither wider deletions nor other exponents are asserted. The published exact finite-field restriction literature, older uniform-exponent results, and adjacent indexed work were compared by mathematical implication.
