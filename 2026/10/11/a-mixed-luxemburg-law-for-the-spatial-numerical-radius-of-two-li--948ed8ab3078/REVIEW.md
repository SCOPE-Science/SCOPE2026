# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each real or complex \(1<p<\infty\), diagonal phase isometries reduce the spatial radius to a positive three-variable homogeneous optimization. Its maximum is interior for two positive weights; two stationarity equations give a strictly decreasing one-variable root equation, and the Luxemburg expression is algebraically identical to that equation. Zero-weight endpoints follow by continuity. The unitary rotation symmetry and a connectedness argument give the complete complex numerical-range disk, and the real interval follows similarly. The quartic orientation inequality is proved from two cubics and a positive explicitly factored polynomial. All normalization, zero-coordinate conventions, ordering of weights, and open exponent endpoints have been checked. The finite checker reproduces the stationary witness and tests representative parameters, but the proof—not these samples—supports the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty

An and Heo (2022, Example 2.4) obtain the one-edge \(2\times2\) radius; it is strictly weaker than the mixed two-edge formula. Gau–Wu (2014) and Undrakh et al. (2015) use Hilbert numerical radius, so they contain at most the \(p=2\) specialization. Chang–Wang (2012) handles Hilbert-weight permutations. The earlier local Hilbert-exponent sensitivity of weighted nilpotent shifts implies neither the global \(p\)-dependent radius nor the exact coefficient-unit-ball identification. Searches for Banach numerical radii, mixed Luxembourg/Nakano formulae, conjugate-exponent reversals, and three-state weighted shifts did not locate the stated complete identity. Since absence from searches cannot prove novelty, historical Banach numerical-range literature is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

This determines a natural norm on the entire two-dimensional coefficient family at the smallest nilpotent shift length where adjacent weights interact. The norm's unit ball is an explicitly identified mixed-exponent Luxemburg ball rather than the Euclidean disk of the Hilbert case; duality exchanges the two edge positions, and the strict quartic reversed-weight inequality demonstrates a non-Hilbert orientation effect absent from standard Hilbert shift theory. Exact values, extremal vectors, and full numerical ranges are accessible for every positive exponent in scope. The result is a complete classification of this natural operator family, not a numerical estimate for one arbitrary matrix.

## Closest antecedents and scope

The published one-edge constant \(k_p\) is prior mathematics and is not claimed to be new. The exact mixed two-weight law and its quartic orientation corollary are the reviewed contribution. This does not establish formulas for longer shifts, nonsmooth endpoints, or a general strict orientation theorem at all exponents. Review is limited to currently located evidence and should not be confused with an independent audit.
