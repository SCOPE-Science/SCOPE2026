# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The classical degree-two polynomial factors as
\[
1+\sqrt2\cos\theta+\frac12\cos(2\theta)
=\left(\cos\theta+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)^2.
\]
For \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), evaluation at the contact point \(3\pi/4\) annihilates both free higher harmonics and forces the upper bound \(a\le\sqrt2\). For every other residue class, the perturbation
\[
P_n(\theta)=\cos\theta+2\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\cos(3\pi n/4)\right)\cos(n\theta)
\]
is strictly positive at both zeros of the square. Continuity near the contacts and a positive compact minimum of the square away from them produce a sufficiently small globally nonnegative perturbation with first coefficient greater than \(\sqrt2\). No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS, with residual literature risk. The inspected general sources formulate support-restricted Carathéodory–Fejér problems but do not give this all-\(n\) mod-four law. Searches for aliases using positive-definite sequences, sparse nonnegative trigonometric polynomials, the support \(\{0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm n\}\), and degree-two one-harmonic extensions did not locate a statement implying the theorem. Closest indexed results concern finite-grid analogues or isolated supports such as \(\{1,2,4\}\), which do not cover the family. A short equivalent observation could still be hidden in older literature under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The theorem resolves a natural structural boundary question for a classical extremal problem: it gives an exact infinite arithmetic classification of which single extra harmonics are completely ineffective and which necessarily improve the degree-two optimum. The contact-set obstruction and perturbative mechanism explain the dichotomy rather than merely tabulating values.
