# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is on an explicitly realizable fixed-coefficient exchange-symmetric slice of equation (1.7) in Cao–Wang–Liu. The predator equilibrium equation enforces \(u+v=\sigma\). The per-capita prey difference forces either symmetry or equality between within-prey crowding and cross-prey competition; the latter identifies the unique critical predator level and its unique parameter threshold. Both positive and exclusion branches follow from strictly increasing scalar equations. The Jacobian splits into a prey-composition exchange eigenvalue and a strictly stable quadratic factor at the symmetric branch. At the boundary branches the missing-species invasion eigenvalue supplies the opposite stability sign. On the entire critical segment the exact Jacobian has one simple tangent zero and a strictly Hurwitz normal quadratic whose coefficients are bounded away from zero. The embedded symbolic checker recomputes all three characteristic-polynomial identities and a rational example. No claim of global attraction is used.

## Originality — PASS

The motivating article [1] states a nondegenerate interior equilibrium existence condition in Theorem 3.1, a local Routh–Hurwitz condition in Theorem 4.1, a sufficient global-stability theorem in Theorem 4.2, and a Hopf calculation. These cannot classify an entire family of equilibria with a simple zero eigenvalue; the present complete threshold/line/transverse-spectrum theorem is not supplied there. Ahmed et al. [2] use different Holling-II predator intake, while Mukherjee [3] use saturating interference and antipredator response in one prey. Their inspected equations and equilibrium theorems cannot be specialized to the precise fear-dependent self-versus-cross competition equality here. Relevant published database results address different models; no exact equivalence or stronger covering result was identified. Known generic neutral-competition phenomena are not claimed as new; residual risk is possible unindexed model-specific or generic literature with stronger coverage.

## Value — PASS

The critical equality marks when a predator-dependent within-prey competition coefficient drops through a fixed cross-prey competition coefficient. A biologically meaningful qualitative distinction follows: stable two-prey coexistence gives way to stable single-prey exclusion, with a continuum of neutral compositions exactly at transition. The formula locates a precise regime boundary and distinguishes an entire normally attracting equilibrium manifold from an ordinary isolated pitchfork or a Hopf onset. The classification is exact for all positive parameters satisfying the stated symmetry and inequality, rather than an arbitrary numerical instance. No extrapolation to arbitrary asymmetry or global basins is made.
