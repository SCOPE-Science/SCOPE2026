# Review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(G=\mathbb Z\times S_3\), one has \(Z(G)=\mathbb Z\times\{e\}\), so the projection \(\pi:G\to\mathbb Z\) satisfies the exact hypothesis \(\pi(Z(G))=Z(\mathbb Z)\) of Proposition 5.15. Pulling back any strong doubly minimal \(\mathbb Z\)-system therefore gives a strong doubly minimal \(G\)-system. Yet \(g=(0,(12))\) is noncentral with finite conjugacy class. The contradiction with Remark 5.3 is literal. The proof-gap diagnosis is also exact: \(g\in\ker\pi\) implies \(S^g=\operatorname{{id}}\), so its graph is the diagonal, which Definition 5.1 does not require to have dense orbit.

## Value
PASS. Remark 5.3 presents a structural obstruction on the acting group, whereas Proposition 5.15 explicitly permits nonfaithful pullbacks. The counterexample shows that these two parts of the same recent paper are incompatible without an effectiveness condition and isolates the precise failure of the graph argument. This matters whenever the proposed sTMSJ notion is used for nonfaithful group actions, while leaving the paper's main horofunction results and a possible faithful version untouched.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Bitar--Donoso--Petite, arXiv:2609.26479v1: it contains both the disputed Remark 5.3 and the pullback Proposition 5.15. King (1990) and Weiss (1998) provide the classical existence background for strong doubly minimal \(\mathbb Z\)-systems. No complete faithful-action repair is proved here.
