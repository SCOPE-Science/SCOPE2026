# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The example consists of two Parseval continuous \(g\)-frames on \([0,1]\). Their analysis ranges coincide in the coefficient \(L^2\)-space, so weak disjointness fails. A change at the single point \(0\) makes the literal pointwise combined kernel trivial without changing either analysis range.

The repaired condition uses almost-everywhere vanishing. It is exactly equivalent to injectivity of the combined analysis operator and therefore to trivial intersection of the two individual analysis ranges.

## Originality
PASS. Exact-source, theorem-number, weak-disjointness, pointwise-kernel, almost-everywhere-kernel, null-set, erratum, and correction formulations were compared with the relevant literature and indexed scientific records.

The closest prior literature treats weak disjointness through analysis-operator ranges. No checked source states the singleton counterexample or explicitly corrects Theorem 2.2(v).

## Value
PASS. The published criterion is supposed to characterize a property defined in an \(L^2\)-type Hilbert space. A criterion that changes when a representative is altered at one null point is not well-defined at that structural level. The corrected almost-everywhere condition is exact and makes the theorem invariant under the equivalence relation intrinsic to continuous frame analysis.

## Closest literature and limitations
Earlier discrete \(g\)-frame disjointness theory has no null-set ambiguity. Later continuous \(g\)-frame work continues to use measure-indexed analysis and synthesis spaces, so the almost-everywhere distinction is structural rather than specific to the chosen example.

If the source intended “for every \(\omega\)” as informal shorthand for almost-everywhere equality, the repaired theorem is the intended statement and the defect is in the printed quantifier.
