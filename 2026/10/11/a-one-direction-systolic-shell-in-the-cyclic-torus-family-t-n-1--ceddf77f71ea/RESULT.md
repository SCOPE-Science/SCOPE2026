# A one-direction systolic shell in the cyclic torus family \(T_{n,1,3}\)
## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge17\), let
\[
K_n=T_{n,1,3}
\]
be the cyclic torus triangulation with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and faces
\[
\{i,i+3,i+4\},\qquad \{i,i+1,i+4\}.
\]
Then
\[
H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Its simple integral homology \(1\)-systole has edge length exactly \(4\).

There are exactly \(n\) systolic carriers. They are
\[
C_i=(i,i+1,i+2,i+3),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
\]
with the last vertex joined back to \(i\). All \(C_i\) represent the same primitive homology direction up to sign.

More strongly, if a simple edge cycle represents a class outside the cyclic subgroup generated by that systolic class, then its length is at least
\[
\left\lceil\frac n4\right\rceil,
\]
and this bound is attained. Hence the two successive independent integral homology lengths are
\[
4\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lceil\frac n4\right\rceil.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The triangulations \(T_{n,1,k}\) are those defined by Datta and Upadhyay. The statement uses their member with \(k=3\), restricted to \(n\ge17\). Length means the number of edges in a simple cycle of the one-skeleton.

A homology direction means a rank-one subgroup generated by a primitive class. The second successive independent homology length is the minimum length of a simple cycle whose class is not contained in the subgroup generated by a shortest nonzero class.

## Proof
Use the standard triangular tessellation of the plane with vertex set \(\mathbb Z^2\), edges in the six directions
\[
\pm e_1,\qquad \pm e_2,\qquad \pm(e_1+e_2),
\]
and triangular faces
\[
\{x,x+e_2,x+e_1+e_2\},\qquad
\{x,x+e_1,x+e_1+e_2\}.
\]
The map
\[
\phi_n(a,b)=a+3b\pmod n
\]
sends these faces to exactly the two defining face families of \(T_{n,1,3}\). Its kernel
\[
L_n=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a+3b\equiv0\pmod n\}
\]
acts by deck translations, and the quotient is \(K_n\). Since the triangular tessellation is simply connected,
\[
H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong L_n.
\]
For example
\[
u=(-3,1),\qquad v=(n,0)
\]
form a basis of \(L_n\), because their determinant has absolute value \(n\), the index of \(L_n\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\).

For \((a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2\), the edge-word distance in the triangular lattice is
\[
N(a,b)=\max\bigl(|a|,|b|,|a-b|\bigr).
\]
Any edge cycle of length \(\ell\) lifts to a path ending at some \((a,b)\in L_n\), and \(N(a,b)\le\ell\).

A path of at most three edges changes the integer \(a+3b\) by a sum of at most three terms from
\[
\{\pm1,\pm3,\pm4\},
\]
so its absolute value is at most \(12\). For \(n\ge17\), membership in \(L_n\) therefore forces \(a+3b=0\). The shortest nonzero integral solution of that equation is \((-3,1)\), whose triangular-lattice norm is
\[
N(-3,1)=4.
\]
Thus no nonzero homology class has a representative of length at most three.

At length four, the same bound gives \(|a+3b|\le16<n\), so again \(a+3b=0\). The only nonzero endpoint vectors reachable in four steps are
\[
(-3,1)\quad\text{and}\quad(3,-1).
\]
To reach \((-3,1)\) in four steps, every step must decrease \(a-b\) by one, so the multiset of steps is three copies of \(-e_1\) and one copy of \(e_2\). Projecting to \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) gives exactly a consecutive four-cycle. Varying the starting label yields precisely the \(n\) cycles \(C_i\), and cyclic translation is transitive on them. Since \(u=(-3,1)\) belongs to the displayed basis of \(L_n\), this systolic class is primitive.

Now consider a class outside \(\mathbb Zu\). For its lift endpoint \((a,b)\), the integer \(a+3b\) is a nonzero multiple of \(n\). Hence
\[
n\le |a+3b|\le |a|+3|b|\le4N(a,b),
\]
so every such cycle has length at least \(\lceil n/4\rceil\).

This lower bound is sharp. Write \(n=4r+s\), where \(s\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). Vectors with \(a+3b=n\) and norm \(\lceil n/4\rceil\) are respectively
\[
(r,r),\qquad(r+1,r),\qquad(r-1,r+1),\qquad(r,r+1).
\]
They are represented by simple projected paths using increments \(4\), with one final increment \(1\) in the second case, two final increments \(3\) in the third case, and one final increment \(3\) in the fourth case. Their partial sums are distinct modulo \(n\), so each closes to a simple cycle of the required length.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` enumerates every triangular-lattice word of length at most four and confirms that the first nonzero kernel vectors are exactly \(\pm(-3,1)\). It also reconstructs the actual cyclic one-skeletons for every \(17\le n\le40\), exhausts all simple three- and four-cycles, checks that the only homologically nonzero four-cycles are the \(n\) consecutive ones, and verifies the explicit second-minimum witnesses in all four residue classes modulo four.

Exact successful output:

`VERIFY_OK short_relation_vectors=none_through3;length4=plusminus(-3,1) | family=T_{n,1,3} range=n>=17 H1=Z^2 | simple_integral_1_systole=4 minimizers=n consecutive_4_cycles one_primitive_direction=yes | second_independent_homology_minimum=ceil(n/4) | finite_replay=n17..40 passed`

## Relationship to prior work
Datta and Upadhyay define \(T_{n,1,k}\), prove that it triangulates the torus, and exhibit its cyclic vertex-transitive structure. Their paper classifies degree-regular torus and Klein-bottle triangulations, but the inspected text does not state homology systoles, successive homology minima, or the one-direction shortest shell above.

Targeted searches used the exact \(T_{n,1,3}\) notation, cyclic-torus aliases, shortest-cycle and homology-systole language, the four-consecutive-vertex description, the lattice equation \(a+3b\equiv0\pmod n\), and the formula \(\lceil n/4\rceil\). No covering statement was located.

## Limitations
The result concerns combinatorial edge length in the specific cyclic triangulations \(T_{n,1,3}\) for \(n\ge17\). It does not assert metric optimality among all triangulations of a torus or among geometric flat metrics. The first shell is classified completely; only the minimum length, not the complete carrier census, is asserted for the first homology direction independent of it.
