# Scientific review: sparse two-frame observation parity law

## Correctness — PASS
The conjugacy reads each binary coordinate from the first component and the threshold metric observes precisely two coded frames. A single-site discrepancy proves the necessity of the integer-cover condition; arbitrary differing sequences prove sufficiency. Uniform four-point incidence gives the general density floor, while the interval of \(d\) target coordinates admits at most two incidences per chosen time and establishes the odd-distance lower bound. The two explicit modular schedules cover all integer coordinates with the stated densities. The standalone exact-arithmetic checks confirm 500 finite instances, but the proof is valid for every integer separation, independently of the checks.

## Originality — PASS with residual literature risk
The displayed introduction of Ferrari (DOI 10.3934/jdg.2026045) provides general witness-cover bounds, the contiguous-window full-shift value, the golden-mean example, and finite symbolic methods. It does not display a parity law for the disconnected patterns \(\{0,1,d,d+1\}\); that full-body comparison remains limited by access. Bollobás–Janson–Riordan (arXiv:0910.3815) concerns general translate coverings, not an explicitly verified uniform formula for this parameter family. Exact phrase, equivalent covering-language, and broader-system searches did not identify a covering theorem implying the parity formula. Absence from search results is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Sparse observation cost is determined here for a natural family of two-sensor, two-frame sampling architectures. The parity penalty distinguishes non-tiling four-point sensing patterns from exact tilers and is certified by a sharp local incidence argument. The result is an exact infinite-family structural law, not a table recomputation or a claim of conjugacy invariance. Possible overlap with unavailable full texts is the primary remaining risk.
