# Scientific review: Clebsch scale-two Čech nerve

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph and the sixteen maximal closed neighborhoods are defined independently of any graphical representation. Exact enumeration gives 808 nonempty simplices and a complete 375-pair ordinary acyclic discrete-Morse matching (plus a separately accounted augmented empty-face pair). The entire 2,752-incidence Hasse graph is checked acyclic. The reduced one-cell/two-cell integral Morse differential has forty-four zero and twelve nonzero coefficients, the latter all of absolute value two. Two independent signed algorithms agree. Thus the one-dimensional skeleton and attaching-loop degrees prove the full homotopy equivalence to \(\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\bigvee^{55}S^2\). Independent mod-two and mod-three ranks corroborate the chain calculation, not substitute for the proof. Residual risk: implementation error despite independently formulated checks; the finite code is provided for further review.

**Originality — PASS.** The owning literature classifies ordinary hypercube Čech nerves but not the folded five-cube. Closed-neighborhood structural dualities and the previously studied rook-graph example neither state nor imply this unsuspended Clebsch homotopy type. Previte's distance-neighborhood formulation includes the object in principle but does not calculate this example in the inspected work. The combinatorial Alexander-dual, open-neighborhood, and independence-complex formulations are distinct or require extra information; no checked source provides the asserted homotopy classification. Searches also examined the synonyms Clebsch, folded cube, Greenwood–Gleason, closed neighborhood, Čech and projective plane. Residual risk: a differently indexed equivalent or a stronger specialized result may exist.

**Value — PASS.** The folded five-cube is the canonical first five-regular folded hypercube, with strongly regular parameters \((16,5,0,2)\). Its complete Čech homotopy type at the first nontrivial closed-neighborhood scale exhibits \(\mathbb Z/2\) first-homology torsion, whereas scale-two Čech nerves of ordinary hypercubes are sphere wedges. Thus the exact natural calculation answers how antipodal folding changes the topology of the best-studied graph Čech family and gives a non-shellability obstruction. Neither an arbitrary parameter slice nor a routine finite-field Betti count is substituted for this structure. The result is limited to the stated finite graph; it does not prove a folded-hypercube family theorem.

Relevant works: Adams–Shukla–Singh, arXiv:2212.05871 (Lemma 2.2, Theorem 4.1); Matsushita, arXiv:2511.12608 (Theorems 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, Example 3.5); Previte (2014), DOI 10.25675/3.018305 (§3); and the Clebsch graph identification in MathWorld.
