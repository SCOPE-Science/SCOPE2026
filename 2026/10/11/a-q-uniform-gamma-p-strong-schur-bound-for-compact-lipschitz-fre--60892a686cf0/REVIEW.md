# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The generalized lacunary assembly is proved by the same three-region decomposition as the focal source but retains the relative locations of the two geometric tails. Under \(b_j\le a_j/s\) and \(a_{j+1}\le b_j/s\), the tails decay by \(s^{-2(q-p)}\) and \(s^{-2p}\). If a middle index is present, both neighboring tails gain a factor at least \(s^{-1}\); if no middle index is present, the two boundary ratios have product at most \(1/s\), so one gains a square-root factor. The resulting loss \(G_s\) tends to \(1\). Lemma 4.4 of the source explicitly allows every sufficiently small detector scale \(b\), so the stronger recursion is admissible. Substituting \(G_s^{1/p}\) into the published quantitative selection proof gives every lower constant strictly below \(\delta/(2^{1/p}\Gamma_p)\), and the compact finite-net argument gives the stated strong Schur constant.

## Originality
PASS. The focal paper states the compact-base constant \(\Gamma_pH_{p,q}^{1/p}\), whose first term diverges as \(q\downarrow p\), and its proof fixes the lacunarity ratio \(4\). The predecessor introduces the strong Schur \(p\)-property and proves constant-preserving results for \(\ell_p\)-sums, but not this compact Lipschitz-free result. Searches for the exact source identifier, \(\Gamma_p\)-strong compact free spaces, \(K_{p,q}\), superlacunary assembly, and equivalent lower-estimate formulations did not locate a covering statement. The closest indexed quantitative-Schur result concerns ordinary Banach Lipschitz-free spaces over bounded uniformly discrete metrics and does not imply a strong Schur \(p\)-constant for quasi-Banach free \(p\)-spaces.

## Value
PASS. The improvement is structural rather than a small numerical refinement: it removes the full dependence on \(q\) from the compact-base quantitative theorem and eliminates a factor that becomes unbounded as \(q\) approaches \(p\). The resulting estimate is uniform across the entire natural range \(p<q\le1\) and isolates the canonical-embedding constant \(\Gamma_p\) as the only remaining loss. This is directly motivated by the focal theorem and its proof architecture.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2609.10747v1, especially Theorem 2.1, Lemma 3.2, Lemma 4.4, Theorems 4.6 and 4.7. The predecessor DOI:10.1007/s12220-025-02260-5 supplies the definition and general strong-Schur framework. The present result excludes \(q=p\), does not compute or claim optimality of \(\Gamma_p\), and retains a residual risk from very recent indexing or an older equivalent lacunary-sum optimization under different terminology.
