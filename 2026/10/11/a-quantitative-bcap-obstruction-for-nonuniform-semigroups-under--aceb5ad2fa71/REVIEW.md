# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The resolvent of an unbounded generator has dense proper range and is therefore non-Fredholm. The focal Calkin hypothesis makes the \(m\)-th powers of the resolvents, and hence of the Yosida approximants, compact. Their powers converge strongly to the identity. Keeping the asymptotic resolvent estimate and optimizing the semigroup growth prefactor down to \(L=\limsup_{t\downarrow0}\|T(t)\|\) gives \(\beta_{\mathcal K}(X)\le L^m\). The local-to-global exponential bound and all limiting steps were checked explicitly.

## Originality
PASS. The focal source proves the qualitative criterion “failure of BCAP plus uniform Calkin nilpotence implies the Lotz property” and remarks that an unbounded generator would imply BCAP. It does not state the best-constant inequality or a lower bound for the short-time operator-norm jump. Five implication-level searches of the published findings database and targeted web searches found no covering theorem. A quantitative BCAP paper based on semi-Fredholm operational quantities was inspected and does not cover semigroup/Yosida behavior.

## Value
PASS. The theorem replaces a binary approximation-property obstruction by a quantitative one: finite BCAP coefficients are distinguished, and any coefficient above one forces a nonzero lower bound on the operator-norm discontinuity of every nonuniform \(C_0\)-semigroup satisfying the same Calkin hypothesis. This is an intrinsic relation between two natural invariants, not merely a tighter disposable constant inside the focal proof.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2609.38396v1, especially Theorem 4.1, the Yosida estimate, and Remark 6.5. The closest quantitative approximation-property comparison located is DOI:10.1007/s10114-023-1007-3, which studies BCAP through semi-Fredholm quantities. The result does not prove equality or sharpness of the exponent, and an older equivalent formulation under different approximation-constant terminology remains a literature risk.
