# A real quadratic-form test sees only the symmetric part of a \(K\)-dual operator

## Finding
Remark 1.2 of Li--Li gives a scalar characterization of \(K\)-dual frame pairs for complex Hilbert spaces and also states a real-Hilbert version when \(K\) is self-adjoint.

The real-Hilbert implication is false as written.

Let \(H\) be a real Hilbert space, let
\[
K=K^*\in B(H),
\]
and let \(F=\{f_j\}\) and \(G=\{g_j\}\) be Bessel sequences. Put
\[
A=T_FT_G^*.
\]
The scalar condition in the source is
\[
\langle Kf,f\rangle
=
\sum_j\langle f,g_j\rangle\langle f_j,f\rangle
=
\langle Af,f\rangle
\]
on a dense subset.

By continuity, the equality extends to every \(f\in H\). In a real Hilbert space it is equivalent to
\[
K=\frac{A+A^*}{2},
\]
not to
\[
A=K.
\]
Equivalently, the scalar test permits exactly the defect
\[
A=K+J,
\qquad
J^*=-J.
\]

A two-dimensional counterexample uses two Riesz bases. Let
\[
H=\mathbb R^2,
\qquad
K=I,
\qquad
f_1=e_1,\quad f_2=e_2,
\]
and
\[
g_1=(1,-1),
\qquad
g_2=(1,1).
\]
For
\[
f=(x,y),
\]
the source's scalar condition holds identically:
\[
\sum_{j=1}^2\langle f,g_j\rangle\langle f_j,f\rangle
=
(x-y)x+(x+y)y
=
x^2+y^2
=
\langle f,f\rangle.
\]
However,
\[
T_FT_G^*
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\ne I.
\]
Thus \(F\) and \(G\) are not a dual pair even though the scalar test holds for every vector.

The exact real-Hilbert replacement is the symmetric-part identity. Actual \(K\)-duality follows if one additionally knows that \(T_FT_G^*\) is self-adjoint, or if one tests the full bilinear reconstruction identity instead of only diagonal quadratic values.

## Assumptions and scope
The source explicitly includes a real separable Hilbert-space branch in Remark 1.2 and assumes \(K=K^*\) there. The example uses the permitted special case
\[
K=I.
\]

Both sequences in the counterexample are Riesz bases: \(F\) is orthonormal, while \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) are orthogonal vectors of norm \(\sqrt2\). Hence the failure is not caused by incompleteness, redundancy, convergence, or weak Bessel behavior.

The complex-Hilbert branch is not challenged. Complex polarization determines an arbitrary bounded sesquilinear form from its diagonal values, whereas real quadratic values determine only the symmetric part.

## Proof
Let
\[
D=K-A.
\]
If the scalar identity holds on a dense subset, continuity of
\[
f\longmapsto\langle Df,f\rangle
\]
extends it to all \(f\in H\).

Write the symmetric part of \(D\) as
\[
S=\frac{D+D^*}{2}.
\]
Because the field is real,
\[
\langle Sf,f\rangle
=
\langle Df,f\rangle
=
0
\]
for every \(f\). The real polarization identity for the symmetric bilinear form generated by \(S\) gives
\[
\langle Sx,y\rangle
=
\frac14\left(
\langle S(x+y),x+y\rangle
-
\langle S(x-y),x-y\rangle
\right)
=
0.
\]
Therefore
\[
S=0.
\]
Since \(K=K^*\),
\[
D+D^*=0
\]
is equivalent to
\[
2K-A-A^*=0,
\]
and hence to
\[
K=\frac{A+A^*}{2}.
\]

Conversely, if the symmetric-part identity holds, then
\[
A-K
\]
is skew-adjoint and
\[
\langle (A-K)f,f\rangle=0
\]
for every real \(f\), so the scalar condition follows.

For the explicit example,
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}
=
I+J,
\qquad
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
J^*=-J.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{A+A^*}{2}=I,
\]
while \(A\ne I\).

The obstruction is fully realizable. Let \(J\) be any bounded skew-adjoint operator on a real separable Hilbert space and let \(F=\{e_j\}\) be an orthonormal basis. Define
\[
g_j=(I-J)e_j.
\]
Then
\[
T_FT_G^*=I+J.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\|(I-J)x\|^2
=
\|x\|^2+\|Jx\|^2
\ge
\|x\|^2,
\]
and the same estimate holds for \(I+J\). Hence \(I-J\) is bounded below and has closed range, while its adjoint \(I+J\) is injective; therefore the range is dense as well as closed. Thus \(I-J\) is boundedly invertible and \(G\) is a Riesz basis.

## Verification
The complete source article was inspected, including its definitions, Remark 1.2, the operator characterization of \(K\)-dual pairs, all construction theorems, examples, common-\(K\)-dual results, and references.

Remark 1.2 explicitly treats the real case and identifies the diagonal scalar condition with the operator identity
\[
K=T_FT_G^*.
\]
Its proof passes from
\[
\langle (K-T_FT_G^*)f,f\rangle=0
\]
to the vanishing of the whole operator. That implication is valid over a complex Hilbert space by complex polarization, but over a real Hilbert space it eliminates only the symmetric part.

The \(2\times2\) matrices above were multiplied directly. The scalar equality, the nonidentity reconstruction operator, the skew-adjoint defect, and the Riesz-basis bounds are exact.

## Relationship to prior work
The operator-theoretic fact that a real quadratic form detects the symmetric part of an operator is classical. It is used here as prior input rather than claimed as a new theorem.

The source-specific conclusion is exact: the real branch of Remark 1.2 characterizes
\[
\frac{T_FT_G^*+T_GT_F^*}{2}=K,
\]
and every bounded skew-adjoint defect can actually occur in frame data, even with two Riesz bases. Thus the gap is not merely formal.

Targeted searches covered the exact article and DOI, the real-Hilbert branch, quadratic-form and polarization aliases, skew-adjoint defects, dual-frame formulations, and correction language. No indexed correction or published frame-theoretic statement containing this source-specific repair and counterexample was located.

The closest indexed frame findings found in the search concern a different dual-\(g\)-frame eigenspace obstruction, canonical-dual scalability, \(K\)-frame range compression, and perturbation stability. None implies the symmetric-part characterization here.

## Limitations
The finding corrects only the real-Hilbert scalar characterization in Remark 1.2. The complex-Hilbert version remains valid.

The repair does not claim novelty for real polarization itself. Its content is the exact diagnosis of the published \(K\)-dual criterion and the realization of every skew-adjoint false-positive defect by frame data.

A nonindexed erratum, thesis remark, or informal author correction may contain the same observation.
