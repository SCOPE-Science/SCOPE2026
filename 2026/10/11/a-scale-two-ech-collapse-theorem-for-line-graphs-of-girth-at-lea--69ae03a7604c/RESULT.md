# A scale-two Čech collapse theorem for line graphs of girth at least five

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite, connected, simple graph containing at least one edge and containing no cycles of length three or four. Give each edge of its line graph \(L(H)\) unit length, and let \(K=\mathcal N(L(H),2)\) be the intrinsic graph Čech nerve of closed radius-one balls centered at vertices of \(L(H)\). Write \(Y=\operatorname{Cl}(L(H))\) for the flag (clique) complex of \(L(H)\). Then there is a sequence of **elementary simplicial collapses**

\[
K\searrow Y.
\]

In particular,

\[
K\simeq H\simeq\bigvee^{\lvert E\rvert-\lvert V\rvert+1}S^1,
\qquad
\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\lvert E\rvert-\lvert V\rvert+1},&j=1,\\
0,&j\ne1.
\end{cases}
\]

**Persistence refinement.** The map on integral first homology associated with the inclusion \(\mathcal N(L(H),1)\hookrightarrow \mathcal N(L(H),2)\) is onto. Its kernel is the internal direct sum of the triangle-cycle subgroups supported within each clique on the edges incident to a fixed \(v\in V\); its free rank is

\[
\sum_{v\in V}\binom{\deg_H(v)-1}{2}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

A graph here is undirected, finite and simple. An edge of \(H\) is a vertex of \(L(H)\), and two vertices of \(L(H)\) are adjacent precisely when the corresponding edges of \(H\) share an endpoint. For each \(v\in V\), define \(S_v=\{e\in E:v\in e\}\). Let \(\Delta(S)\) denote the full simplex on \(S\), and interpret a complex generated by simplices as including all their faces.

The intrinsic graph Čech convention is that a scale \(r\) uses closed balls of radius \(r/2\) in the geometric realization of the unit-edge graph. Its scale-one complex equals the underlying graph, and its scale-two complex is generated by the closed neighborhoods of vertices (Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2). Thus for \(e=uv\in E\), its closed neighborhood in \(L(H)\) is \(S_u\cup S_v\), and

\[
K=\bigcup_{uv\in E}\Delta(S_u\cup S_v).
\]

We allow bridges, leaves and vertices of degree exceeding three. The theorem assumes absence of triangles and four-cycles, whether or not the cycles are induced.

## Proof

**1. Identify the target subcomplex.** In a triangle-free graph, every pairwise intersecting collection of edges has a common vertex: if two distinct edges meet at \(v\), any third edge meeting both but not incident to \(v\) completes a triangle. Consequently the simplices of \(\operatorname{Cl}(L(H))\) are exactly the collections of edges lying in one star \(S_v\). Hence

\[
Y=\bigcup_{v\in V}\Delta(S_v)\subseteq K.
\]

**2. Every face outside \(Y\) has one and only one generating center.** Let \(\sigma\in K\setminus Y\). Its edges of \(H\) do not have a common endpoint. By the preceding step, there are disjoint edges \(a,b\in\sigma\). A generating simplex \(\Delta(S_u\cup S_v)\) can contain both disjoint edges only when the edge \(uv\) joins one endpoint of \(a\) to one endpoint of \(b\). Between disjoint edges \(a\) and \(b\), there is at most one such connecting edge: two connections with a common endpoint form a triangle together with \(a\) or \(b\); two connections with distinct endpoints form a four-cycle using \(a\) and \(b\). Both are excluded. Existence follows from \(\sigma\in K\). Thus there is a **unique** edge \(e(\sigma)=uv\in E\) with

\[
\sigma\subseteq S_u\cup S_v.
\]

The edge \(e(\sigma)\) is distinct from \(a,b\), since \(a,b\) are disjoint. The same pair \(a,b\) witnesses that \(\sigma\cup\{e(\sigma)\}\notin Y\). The unique generating edge is unchanged when \(e(\sigma)\) is added or removed.

**3. Give an explicit sequence of elementary collapses.** Pair each \(\sigma\in K\setminus Y\) not containing its unique generator \(e(\sigma)\) with \(\sigma\cup\{e(\sigma)\}\). Every face outside \(Y\) occurs exactly once in this pairing: if it contains its generator, remove the generator to obtain the lower face, which remains outside \(Y\). Remove these pairs in **descending order of the number of vertices of each lower face** (ties arbitrary).

At the moment a lower face \(\sigma\) is processed, suppose \(\tau\) is a remaining coface of \(\sigma\) other than the paired upper face. Because \(Y\) is a subcomplex and \(\sigma\notin Y\), we have \(\tau\notin Y\). Its unique generator equals \(e(\sigma)\), since \(\tau\) contains the same disjoint witnesses \(a,b\). If \(\tau\) omits the generator, it is another lower face strictly larger than \(\sigma\) and was already removed. If \(\tau\) contains the generator, then unless \(\tau=\sigma\cup\{e(\sigma)\}\), its partner \(\tau\setminus\{e(\sigma)\}\) is a lower face strictly larger than \(\sigma\), also removed already. Therefore the only remaining proper coface of \(\sigma\) is its paired upper face. This is a free-face/cofacet pair, so its removal is an elementary simplicial collapse. Iterating leaves exactly \(Y\) and proves \(K\searrow Y\).

**4. Compute the target homotopy type.** Cover \(Y\) by the full simplices \(\Delta(S_v)\). Two different such simplices intersect in the single vertex \(\{uv\}\) exactly when \(uv\in E\), and otherwise have empty intersection. Intersections of three distinct simplices are empty. All nonempty intersections are therefore contractible, and the nerve of this cover is precisely the original graph \(H\). The simplicial nerve lemma yields \(Y\simeq H\). A connected graph has first Betti number \(\lvert E\rvert-\lvert V\rvert+1\), and is homotopy equivalent to that many circles wedged together. This proves the first assertions over \(\mathbb Z\).

**5. Identify the persistence kernel integrally.** At scale one the Čech complex is exactly the one-dimensional graph \(L(H)\). At scale two, its first-homology map factors through \(Y\), and the collapse \(K\searrow Y\) makes \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) an isomorphism. Since \(Y\) has precisely the same one-skeleton as \(L(H)\), this map is surjective and its kernel is generated by boundaries of the two-simplices of \(Y\), namely triangles lying within \(\Delta(S_v)\). The corresponding local subgroup is the entire cycle lattice of the complete graph on the \(\deg_H(v)\) incident edges; it has rank \(\binom{\deg_H(v)-1}{2}\). Distinct local cliques have disjoint *one-dimensional edge supports*: two edges of \(H\) have at most one common endpoint. Thus the local cycle sublattices have direct sum, giving exactly the asserted kernel and rank. No torsion occurs in source, target or kernel.

**6. Sharpness of the forbidden short cycles.** For \(H=C_3\), the line graph is again \(C_3\), whose scale-two Čech complex is a simplex, not a circle. For \(H=C_4\), the line graph is \(C_4\), and its four closed neighborhoods give the four triangular faces of the boundary of a tetrahedron; hence its scale-two Čech complex is \(S^2\), not \(S^1\). Neither forbidden cycle length can be admitted without additional conditions.

## Verification

The companion standalone script `verify.py` enumerates every face of each tested Čech complex, verifies the unique-generator statement for every face outside its flag subcomplex, checks that the proposed pair partition is complete, and directly executes the elementary-collapse sequence while checking freeness of every lower face. It also compares Euler characteristics and the rank identity for the persistence kernel. Examples cover paths, stars, two pentagons joined at one vertex, a five-cycle, the Petersen graph, the Heawood graph and the dodecahedral graph. For \(C_4\), it confirms the tetrahedral-boundary counterexample. Running `python verify.py` exits successfully with `VERIFY_OK`.

The program is a **finite corroboration**. The proof above, not sample enumeration or a search result, proves the asserted theorem for **all** finite connected graphs satisfying the hypotheses.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Shukla and Singh [1, Lemma 2.2] supply the exact even-scale neighborhood description of the intrinsic graph Čech construction; [1, Lemma 2.3] supplies a girth-based general recovery criterion. That general criterion may not apply to \(L(H)\) even for large-girth \(H\), because vertices of degree at least three in \(H\) make triangles in \(L(H)\). The present theorem supplies an explicit collapse in that situation.

Goyal, Shukla and Singh [2, Theorem 3.7] prove the known statement \(\operatorname{Cl}(L(H))\simeq H\) for triangle-free \(H\). That known equivalence is **not claimed as a discovery here**. It does not imply that the larger scale-two Čech complex collapses to the flag complex, or determine which first-homology cycles the scale-one inclusion kills. The new step is the unique-connector collapse, supported by excluding both triangles and quadrilaterals.

Matsushita [3] treats general closed-neighborhood complexes, with suspension and fundamental-group relationships. The inspected results do not contain this line-graph collapse, its explicit simple-homotopy sequence or the full integral degree-one inclusion kernel. Equivalence under broader unpublished classifications cannot be ruled out.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes the absence of both three- and four-cycles and concerns the scale-two Čech nerve, not the full persistence barcode at scales above two. The local kernel statement is an integral first-homology result; it is not a claim of a canonical decomposition of fundamental groups. Only representative finite graphs were computationally checked; they are not an exhaustive search through all admissible graphs. Related results for graphs having short cycles may hold with extra hypotheses, but are not asserted here. No claim is made about graph Čech filtrations at higher scales or about results under weaker girth restrictions.
