# Scientific review: A sharp Airy-edge law for four-periodic geometric weighted shifts

## Correctness — PASS

The top numerical-radius value is exactly the largest eigenvalue of a compact self-adjoint Jacobi matrix. Its positive period-four Bloch mode gives a ground-state transform with **three exact nonnegative terms**: discrete stiffness, endpoint penalty and geometric damping potential. The boundary penalty imposes Dirichlet behavior on the \(\varepsilon^{1/3}\) scale. Period-cell Cauchy--Schwarz supplies the *sharp* effective resistance, not an arithmetic averaging guess. Uniform local bounds, a tail estimate from the damping potential and compactness give the lower variational limit; an equality-case cell interpolation gives recovery and the matching upper limit. The half-line linear-potential eigenvalue is the first Airy zero, and its normalization reproduces the asserted coefficient. The packaged exact-rational checks replay the key algebra; they are not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

Ghosh--Birbonshi--Ojha, arXiv:2608.17486v1 (18 August 2026), treats exactly this four-periodic geometric shift. Inspection of its complete original HTML, including its numerical-radius bounds in Theorem 2.2, its extremum polynomial in Theorems 2.5 and 2.12, and determinant recurrences in Section 3, found **no Airy edge law, no \(q\uparrow1\) limit and no effective coefficient**. The full original papers of Chien--Nakazato (2009), Chakraborty--Ojha--Birbonshi (2022) and Undrakh and collaborators (2015) address, respectively, the unmodulated determinant/bounds, periodic tails and finite-prefix perturbations; their stated theorems do not entail the two-parameter Airy constant. General discrete WKB/Airy theory anticipates the method but does not directly supply this numerical-radius limit with its endpoint normalization. The generalized geometric two-periodic specialization was also published by Chakraborty--Ojha (2024/2025); its complete text was unavailable, so independent literature assessment remains necessary.

## Value — PASS

The pre-existing fixed-parameter determinant recurrence does not quantify the singular limit as geometric damping vanishes. The result identifies the limit edge, a nonanalytic exponent \(2/3\), and the exact two-modulation coefficient for *every* positive parameter pair. The constant is a nontrivial interaction of periodic mass and harmonic resistance, and the derivation provides a robust analytical method for compact-to-periodic transitions. This is a sharp invariant calculation for a naturally published operator family rather than an arbitrary finite coefficient.
