# Review of the theta-graph scale-two theorem

## Correctness — PASS
For \(\ell=3\), explicit graph distances give the face-set identity \(R_2=S^0*K_m\) with \(K_m=\Delta(A)\cup\Delta(B)\cup\bigcup_i[a_i,b_i]\). Contracting the two disjoint contractible simplices gives the \(m\)-edge two-vertex graph, proving \(\bigvee^{m-1}S^2\) and integral freeness. The chain \(c_{ij}\) reverses sign while both suspension vertices exchange, so the second homology reversal sign is positive. For \(\ell\ge4\), the girth-eight bound and explicit free-face pairings recover the original theta graph; for \(\ell=2\), the graph is diameter two. Finite GF(2) enumeration and integral chain tests corroborate, but are not substitutes for, these symbolic arguments.

## Originality — PASS
The closest published comparison is Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers*, Proposition 2.2 and the scale-two \(C_6\) example: it supplies the girth-seven collapse and the **two-arm** six-cycle sphere, but does not state the multiple-arm exceptional homotopy type, its augmentation lattice, or the endpoint-reversal sign cancellation. Parks–Marchette give general girth/persistence guarantees; their accessible full text does not determine this exceptional exact complex. General metric-gluing results, circular Rips results, and other published geometric graph-power calculations likewise lack this implication. Searches used graph-squares, theta-graphs, subdivisions of parallel paths, suspensions, representation signs, and the sharp girth boundary. The remaining risk is an unindexed equivalent formulation, especially under two-terminal networks or graph-power topology.

## Value — PASS
Equal-arm theta graphs are a canonical multi-cycle, two-terminal family. The result pinpoints a sharp, structurally explained change at girth six, distinguishing a second-homology suspension from the girth-eight persistence regime, and determines the natural integral symmetry representation rather than only a Betti count. The dimension shift and endpoint-reversal sign cancellation give a usable constraint on general graph-square and graph-metric persistence results.

## Independent validation and scope
All claims are mathematical statements about finite simple graphs and their vertex shortest-path metrics; the only complete later-scale persistence computation is the length-three case. The included finite experiments test bounded ranges. A broader literature equivalence might remain unidentified.
