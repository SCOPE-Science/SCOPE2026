# Scientific review of the triangle-chorded-cycle stability theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The only additional edge joins vertices \(0\) and \(2\); the proof identifies all four families of added simplices for every \(n\geq7\). The explicitly paired faces are free in their stated order, giving a literal simplicial collapse to the cycle complex. The separate six-vertex case has a fully enumerated, repeatably validated sequence of 27 elementary collapses. The published cycle sphere classification is invoked only after the stronger relative collapse is proved. Finite checks of twelve cases corroborate but do not establish the uniform claim.

**Originality — PASS, with limitations.** The closest primary reference establishes the homotopy type for unchorded cycles, not this triangle-chord insertion or the resulting relative elementary collapse. The robust-clique square-sequence theorem applies to bipartite graphs, whereas the chorded cycles are nonbipartite. Comparisons of mathematical statements with indexed results and earlier claims identified no theorem implying this chorded-cycle stability. These searches do not prove uniqueness; equivalent unindexed material remains a possible risk.

**Value — PASS.** The result gives an explicit, infinite simple-homotopy stability mechanism for a graph perturbation that creates an odd triangle and raises the simplicial dimension. It pinpoints a six-vertex failure and explains all higher-face cancellations uniformly. This is an exact structural boundary rather than a recomputation of cycle homology or an arbitrary isolated finite example.

**Limitations and literature.** It applies only to the distance-two chord and to \(n\geq6\). Existing sphere results for cycles are credited to Carnero Bravo and earlier work; Filakovský supplies a different bipartite robustness setting. No assertion about other chords, extra edges, or equivariant collapses is made.
