# Mathematical assessment of the triangle-chorded-cycle stability theorem

**Correctness: PASS.** Every added face for n >= 7 belongs to one of four explicit families. Matching each added tetrahedron B_j with its unique five-vertex coface U_j, then each added triangle T_j with A_j, gives exactly 2n-9 elementary free-face collapses. A complete independent enumeration of the n=6 complex yields 56 faces including the empty face, and 27 elementary collapses to one vertex. The known sphere type for unchorded cycles is used as a published premise. Finite checks for n=7,...,18 corroborate, not replace, the symbolic argument.

**Originality: PASS, best of knowledge.** Cycle and cycle-power results in Carnero Bravo and Bayer et al. do not establish this specified one-chord inclusion collapse. The grid-like graph results of Filakovský require bipartiteness; the present graph contains a triangle. Kim and Lew give general collapsibility bounds rather than this relative elementary collapse. Related ordinary three-cut theorems use disconnected triples, while the complex here forbids independent triples. No identified independent source implies the full claim. Equivalent unindexed work remains a residual risk.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result supplies an explicit uniform stability mechanism for a natural graph perturbation and pinpoints the exceptional six-vertex topology. It proves a simple-homotopy relation, not merely agreement of Betti numbers.

**Scope.** The graph is the cycle with precisely one length-two chord; no result for arbitrary chords, additional edges or equivariant collapses follows.
