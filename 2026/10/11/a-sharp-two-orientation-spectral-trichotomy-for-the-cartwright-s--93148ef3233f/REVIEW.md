# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source's Proposition 5.1 gives the exact reciprocal quadratic factors for the two cyclic orders. The classification uses the exact criterion \(|a|\le2\) for a reciprocal quadratic \(z^2-az+1\) to have unit-circle roots, followed by elementary inequalities valid on the full integer tails. The spectral lower bound is then the exact larger-root formula at \(|a|=18\). The finite script is only a regression check.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source leaves \(m\) undetermined and does not state the two-orientation integer trichotomy or the sharp constant \(9+4\sqrt5\). Searches covered the source title, characteristic polynomial, vanishing-cycle intersection aliases, products of three Dehn twists, spectral-radius language, and the gap constant. The closest indexed monodromy findings concern different objects. A separate result using the same Cartwright–Steger source computes the spectrum of the order-three symmetry \(S_*\), not either nodal-twist product, and does not imply this claim.

## Value
PASS. The parameter \(m\) is the source's unresolved geometric input to the nodal monodromy. The result shows that its spectral consequences are not gradual: after a finite seven-value window, both cyclic orientations have a uniform and sharp homological spectral gap. This is a natural structural constraint on the marked-monodromy problem rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is D. Akhmedov and S.-K. Yeung, arXiv:2610.01467v1, especially Propositions 5.1 and 5.2. It supplies the formulas but not the integer classification. The actual value of \(m\) remains unknown, and the result does not determine Jordan blocks at repeated roots or Nielsen–Thurston type.
