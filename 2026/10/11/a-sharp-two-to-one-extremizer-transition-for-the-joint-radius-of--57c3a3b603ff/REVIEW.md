# Review of the exact rank-one projection joint-radius classification

## Correctness — PASS
Projection expectation values are nonnegative real squares of overlaps, hence the balanced joint radius is a fourth-moment maximization. The proof first eliminates vectors outside the two-dimensional span (strict radial scaling), then forces alignment of their relative phase and reduces the optimization to \(F_c(y)\) on the exact interval \([c,1]\). A strict interior vertex exists exactly for \(0<c<1/2\); at the transition and beyond, the unique maximum is at the bisector. The degenerate orthogonal and identical-projection cases are addressed separately. The factorized excess and its expansion imply the stated square-root branch separation and quadratic gain. Exact rational checks pass. No experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with unresolved bibliographic access
The ordinary Euclidean operator radius and several broad bounds were already established by Dragomir (2006), Feki (2024), and Altwaijry et al. (2025). The weighted variant was introduced by Aljawi et al. (2026). Their inspected statements do not entail the complete projection-pair value/equality-geometry theorem, and semantic searches under projection, frame-overlap and fourth-moment formulations did not locate the claimed bifurcation. The 2006 repository PDF and 2024 journal PDF were materially inspected. The full 2026 manuscript and the whole 2025 HTML article were inaccessible; unindexed or hidden equivalent examples remain plausible and require independent checking. Originality is not claimed from a negative search alone.

## Value — PASS
The overlap \(c\) is the complete unitary invariant of an ordered pair of rank-one orthogonal projections. The result solves their entire natural balanced joint-radius optimization and gives the exact point where two maximizing rays coalesce. The square-root singularity describes a genuine structural phase transition in optimal vectors, while the exact quadratic loss measures how much the naive bisector misses the optimum. This is more than a bound or a sample computation.

## Scope and limits
No classification is claimed for unequal weights, higher-rank projections, or arbitrary semidefinite metrics. The defining joint radius is prior art. This is same-model analytical review and exact-arithmetic checking, not independent validation.
