# Review of the weighted two-charge collision cusp

**Correctness — PASS.** The individually normalized one-pole integral is exactly \(4\); the translated, rescaled disk gives an exact formula for its triangle deficit. The deficit is nonnegative, bounded at the two poles, and decays at least cubically at infinity, so dominated convergence rigorously yields the half-plane coefficient. Confocal coordinates have Jacobian equal to the product of focal distances and give a manifestly positive integral. The equal-weight binomial expansion has nonnegative terms, so Tonelli applies; the two beta integrals then give the stated convergent coefficient series. A portable checker tests the identities and numerical scale, but the analytic proof does not depend on it.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The principal weighted Coulomb paper provides global weight-dependent norm bounds, not an asymptotic coefficient for two merging boundary charges. The related weighted Chui paper bounds a signed *difference* at nearby poles, not the positive-charge triangle deficit. Bezrukov proves equispaced global minimization, and Nazarov's full MathOverflow answer proves two-charge monotonicity for positive separation, without a sharp limiting derivative or coefficient. Hilbert Bergman analogues concern a different norm. Targeted mathematical-index searches and claim-only deduplication did not locate this result. A hidden equivalent derivation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** Two-charge collision is a canonical degeneracy in the contemporary weighted Coulomb/Chui problem. The answer determines the first nonzero term and its sharp, weight-sensitive coefficient, exposes a genuinely nondifferentiable linear rather than quadratic stability scale, and supplies a positive exact series for equal charges; it is not an arbitrary finite numerical slice. The result does not by itself resolve arbitrary multipole optimization.
