# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The explicit coisometry sends a closed graph subspace onto the dense nonclosed domain of a closed unbounded diagonal operator. The original two-component family is an \(I\)-\(g\)-fusion frame with exact bounds \(1\) and \(2\), so every hypothesis of Theorem 3.5(1) is met. The quotient argument proves the exact boundary
\[
UW\text{ closed}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W+N(U)\text{ closed}.
\]

## Originality
PASS for the source-specific claim. The general fact that bounded images of closed subspaces can be nonclosed is standard and is not claimed as new. Searches covered the exact DOI and theorem, image-subspace closedness, the quotient-kernel formulation, and projection/closure aliases. No inspected source or indexed record stated the same correction to Theorem 3.5.

The target full text was inspected at its definitions, Lemma 2.8, Theorem 3.5, and Corollary 3.6. A related relay-fusion paper was inspected at its operator-image projection lemma; it also uses closure explicitly but does not correct the target theorem.

## Value
PASS. The theorem's output may fail to belong to the class it purports to construct, and the proof can invoke an orthogonal projection onto a nonclosed image. The obstruction survives for coisometries and has an exact kernel-sum characterization, making it a reusable boundary for operator constructions of fusion subspaces.

## Limitations
The finding concerns closed-subspace and projection admissibility. Replacing \(UW_j\) by \(\overline{UW_j}\) repairs the projection domain, but no claim is made that this alone resolves every separate codomain convention for transformed analysis operators. An unindexed correction remains a literature risk.
