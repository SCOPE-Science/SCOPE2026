## correctness

PASS

For the interval negative-doubling map T(x)=1−2x on the left and 2−2x on the right, a survivor is confined to L=[0,a]∪R=[1−a,1]. If a<2/5, a repeated L step ends at y≥1−2a>a/2; any subsequent L→R requires y≤a/2. Hence LL is absorbing, and by reflection so is RR. An eventually constant L tail must equal the repelling fixed point 1/3 since T^k(x)−1/3=(−2)^k(x−1/3); the R fixed point is 2/3. Endless alternating itineraries force x=0 or 1 from T²=4x on L. At a=2/5, an LL→R transition forces y=1/5 and preceding x=2/5, hence the 4-cycle (2/5,1/5,3/5,4/5). The reflected RR→L case is the same orbit. All other surviving itineraries become constant or alternating. These exhaust all periodic orbits; their endpoint inclusion requires a≥1/3 for the fixed points and equality a=2/5 for the 4-cycle. Therefore for all n≥1 Fix count is 2·1_{2|n}+2·1_{a≥1/3}+4·1_{a=2/5}·1_{4|n}, with zeta (1−z²)^{−1}(1−z)^{−2·1_{a≥1/3}}(1−z⁴)^{−1_{a=2/5}}. Finite preimages of the finite periodic set imply countability. S_a is compact (closed survivor set of the continuous restricted map on L∪R), all invariant measures are supported on periodic points by recurrence, and the variational principle gives topological entropy zero. Fresh independent exact enumeration of 90 parameter/period pairs (n≤15, six a including both endpoints) and direct 4-cycle checks agree; finite checks are not a proof of universal exclusions.
