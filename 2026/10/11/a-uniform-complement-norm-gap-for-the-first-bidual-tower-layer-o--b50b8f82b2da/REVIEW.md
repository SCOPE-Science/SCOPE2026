# Scientific assessment: complement-norm gap for a canonical dual-tower layer

## Correctness — PASS
The assertion is proved for all infinite index sets over complex scalars. The norm-one fiber lemma is derived from the probability measure representing a unital contractive functional and the exact total variation between it and a point mass. The \(C(\beta\Gamma)^{**}\) central decomposition follows from the \(\ell_1(\Gamma)\oplus_1M(\beta\Gamma\setminus\Gamma)\) measure-band splitting, with canonical embedding given as a graph. Nonreflexivity of the remainder function algebra forces a fiber of size at least two, and a disjoint added base point makes three. Restriction transfers the obstruction to the tower. Explicit adjoint retractions establish existence of a norm-one projection. No computational sample substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent bibliographic validation
The motivating full preprint (Liu–Uglešić, arXiv:2610.03179v1, §4 after Proposition 4.1) proves the absence of an \(M\)-projection but explicitly leaves the weaker bicontractive question open. The new inequality \(\|I-P\|\ge4/3\) rules out bicontractivity and is not implied by absence of an \(M\)-projection alone. Gwizdek's older tower construction supplies retractions, not this obstruction. Blecher–Neal addresses bicontractive operator-algebra maps broadly, but its inspected analysis does not give this particular three-point spectrum-fiber comparison. Targeted mathematical record searches found no same-claim item. A possibly unindexed corollary of broader positive-projection structure remains the principal residual literature risk.

## Value — PASS
This resolves an explicit unresolved quantitative complementability distinction in a natural and canonical example, giving a uniform positive gap even while norm-one projections exist. The distinction between contractive complementation, bicontractivity and the stronger \(M\)-summand property is structurally meaningful in Banach geometry and tower systems. The extension to arbitrary infinite index sets shows the obstruction is not a countability artifact.

## Closest literature and scientific limitations
Primary comparison: arXiv:2610.03179v1, §4 and Proposition 4.1. Background: arXiv:2205.14385v7 and arXiv:1905.05836v4. Structural theorems about contractive projections could yield alternative proofs; they are not claimed to be original here.
