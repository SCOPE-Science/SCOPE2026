# Mathematical review: inverse-power two-cycle dynamics

## Correctness — PASS

For the positive discrete map \(F(x)=ax+b/x^p\) with real \(p>1\), \(0<a<1\), \(b>0\), the two-cycle equations imply, and are implied by, the ratio identity \(a=(r^p-r)/(r^{p+1}-1)\) for \(r>1\). On writing \(r=e^{2t}\), the ratio is \(\sinh((p-1)t)/\sinh((p+1)t)\). Monotonicity follows from the strictly increasing function \(u\coth u\). The range \((0,(p-1)/(p+1))\) proves exact existence and uniqueness and rules out a primitive pair at the upper endpoint.

For \(p=2\), exact symmetric-sum identities give \(S=(b(1+a)/a^2)^{1/3}\), \(x_\pm=S(1\pm\sqrt{(1-3a)/(1+a)})/2\), and two-step derivative \(9a^2+6a-2\). Its unique crossing of \(-1\) is at \(a=(\sqrt2-1)/3\); strict modulus inequalities justify the claimed local attracting and repelling regimes. The neutral cases are correctly excluded from nonlinear classifications. Exact-rational and symbolic spot checks supplement, but do not replace, these arguments.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Le Van, Nguyen-Trong, Pham and Pham, *From Balanced Growth to Innovation Cycles* (arXiv:2610.01610v1), Proposition 5 and Appendix A.2, study precisely this reduced map and provide the sufficient two-cycle existence threshold. Their stated result does not supply necessity, uniqueness, the explicit two-cycle pair, or the quadratic-inverse multiplier and stability partition. This existing contribution must be credited.

Abdelrahman, Chatzarakis, Li and Moaaz, *On the difference equation x_(n+1)=a x_(n-l)+b x_(n-k)+f(x_(n-l),x_(n-k))* (2018, DOI 10.1186/s13662-018-1880-8), require a nonlinear term homogeneous of degree zero in two lagged arguments; those theorems do not contain \(b/x^p\). Classical iterate-derivative stability principles are standard. The complete exact classification and stability boundary were not found in the bounded mathematical-literature comparison, but historical priority is not certified; older unindexed equivalent recurrences remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem turns a known sufficient existence statement for a natural scalar innovation model into a complete uniqueness classification for every real inverse exponent \(p>1\), and it separates repelling from attracting period-two oscillations at an exact, scale-independent threshold when \(p=2\). This answers a motivated local-dynamics question; it does not claim global attraction, higher-period classification, or behavior of the unreduced economic system.
