# Scientific review: the four-mode fourth-moment constant

## Correctness — PASS
The claim covers arbitrary complex four-tuples and compact abelian groups whose selected character has order at least seven or infinite order. Formula (1) follows from orthogonality of the seven **distinct** correlations indexed by \(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\). The three estimates (2) use the exact outer- and inner-pair square masses and impose no unproved phase restriction. Their common upper envelope is the rational function \(H(t)\); the derivative factorization and the unique positive cubic root show a global maximum, including both zero-mass boundaries. Equality in the outer/inner arithmetic-geometric inequalities fixes all moduli, and phase alignment in the first correlation fixes all consecutive phase differences. Every displayed extremizer attains the bound. For character orders four, five, and six, positive aliases of the seven autocorrelation frequencies show strict failure of the constant. The checker executed the direct Fourier and correlation formulas for 4,060 parameterized/randomized cases. Numerical tests do not stand in for the proof. No uncovered analytic lemma or computation is needed.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty
The closest same-support record, *Four-point support geometry quantizes the phase-only fourth Fourier moment*, computes the **minimum** fourth moment at **fixed unit coefficient magnitudes** as a function of four-point support geometry, so it neither calculates nor upper-bounds the unrestricted **maximum** at free magnitudes. Its full `RESULT.md` was inspected, not only its title. Schmidt's arXiv:1302.2766 proves asymptotic **small-norm** results for specific long unimodular sequences and records the standard correlation identity; it does not optimize all four free complex coefficients. Katz--van der Linden's arXiv:2108.07318 bounds **peak** correlation for binary recursive sequences; it does not state the exact four-mode fourth-moment embedding constant or equality set. Similarity is in the correlation apparatus, not implication of the theorem. The older small-degree polynomial literature may contain the same algebra under a different name, but no such covering statement appeared in the performed published-finding corpus and targeted external searches.

## Value — PASS
The fixed-four-mode coefficient problem is a natural, unconstrained sharp \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) Fourier embedding problem, and four modes exhibit the first three-lag coupled autocorrelation problem with a nontrivial algebraic maximizing outer/inner amplitude ratio. The theorem gives one exact constant across both finite compact groups and the circle, complete complex equality cases and a sharp aliasing threshold at character order seven. The result is a complete classification for a canonical spectrum rather than a numerical sample, arbitrary parameter slice, or reformulation of the standard correlation identity. It does not claim broader applications or a general higher-mode solution.
