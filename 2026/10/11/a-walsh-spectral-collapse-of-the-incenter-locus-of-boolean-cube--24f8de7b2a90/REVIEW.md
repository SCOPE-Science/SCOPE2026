# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim uses the intrinsic zero-sum Euclidean metric with a fully specified segment normalization. The cut-coarea support-function identity proves the full center halfspace description, while the inductive edge-isoperimetric inequality, its power-of-two equality classification, and the unimodal endpoint comparison isolate all active cuts. Their normal ranks and strict inequalities prove the center dimensions and Walsh span. The finite verifier exhausts cut cases only through \(d=4\); the universal theorem is proved analytically, not by extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS
Padrol–Pilaud–Poullot's graphical-fan and deformation-cone theory and Rashtchian–Raynaud's hypercube isoperimetry supply essential background. Pešović–Stojadinović study different, combinatorial invariants of related generalized permutohedra. Neither the inspected graphical-zonotope sources nor the hypercube isoperimetry paper establishes the all-dimensional Euclidean incenter locus, the Walsh interaction span, or the switch to uniqueness at \(d=5\). A generic cut formula gives a radius optimization but does not by itself identify all strict equality cuts and independent active normal directions; the claim makes and proves those additional structural statements. Residual risk: an unlocated prior treatment of Chebyshev-center loci could imply the result, so this assessment does not claim exhaustive historical coverage.

## Value — PASS
The abrupt collapse of an \(11\)-dimensional incenter locus at \(d=4\) to a singleton at \(d=5\), while the ambient dimensions increase from \(15\) to \(31\), is a natural geometric uniqueness phenomenon rather than an arbitrary small numerical instance. The Walsh Fourier interpretation gives an explicit structural description of the allowable motions of maximum balls. Such uniqueness and contact classifications are geometric data not determined by the inradius value alone.

## Scope and unresolved questions
The theorem does not classify the face lattice or volume of the positive-dimensional incenter polytope, nor weighted or deformed variants. The computations are exhaustive for four discrete cube dimensions and sampled for two larger dimensions, not exhaustive for infinitely many cubes. Independent external confirmation has not been performed.
