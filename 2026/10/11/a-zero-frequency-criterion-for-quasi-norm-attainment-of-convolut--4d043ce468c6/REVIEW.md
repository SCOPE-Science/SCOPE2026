# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** On the stated domain and both scalar fields, each convolution row has an explicitly computable coefficient norm; taking its supremum establishes the exact operator norm. Strictly positive summable functional weights force every norming sequence to converge coordinatewise to one constant phase, and the range requirement \(c_0\) forces the coefficient sum to vanish. Conversely, finite-support ramps have an explicit coordinate-uniform error bounded by a vanishing tail of partial sums and an \(\ell_1\)-dominated modulus of continuity. Coordinatewise strict row inequalities rule out quasi norm attainment by infinitely supported convolution components. A phase-aligned coefficient correction attains the asserted \(\ell_1\) distance without exiting the admissible open ball. Quantifiers, the strict norm gap, endpoint \(c=0\), and complex phases have been checked; numerical samples are not used as proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Choi–Choi–Jung–Martín's complete accessible 2020/2022 work provides general quasi norm attainment theory, existence examples, adjoint characterizations and range-sum *density* questions, but no symbol-at-one condition or exact within-family metric formula. In particular, Proposition 4.3 gives non-quasi-norm-attaining maps into \(c_0\), not a criterion for a graph of a strictly positive scalar functional and an absolutely summable shift series. The nearest independently indexed operator records concern shift calculus, s-number profiles, or unrelated diagonal examples; their available statements do not imply the present theorem. The related prior fixed-norm rank-one-minus-diagonal family has a different codomain and parameter geometry and does not imply this cancellation classification. The main residual risk is an equivalent criterion in unindexed operator-attainment or summability literature. This is a literature comparison, not a proof of priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result completely classifies a natural infinite-dimensional Banach-algebra family by one intrinsic zero-frequency evaluation, computes the exact cost of enforcing the property, and exhibits both directions of failure of naive coordinatewise tests. It sharpens the structural understanding of direct-sum quasi norm attainment without pretending to solve the distinct density question.
