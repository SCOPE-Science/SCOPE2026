# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** For a forward-shift convolution with absolutely summable coefficients, the \(n\)-th output row has norm \(\sum_{j<n}|c_j|\), giving the full norm \(\|c\|_1\). The strictly positive summable scalar functional has norm \(A\), but no norming vector in \(B_{c_0}\). The strict inequality \(\|c\|_1<A\) forces scalar norming in any near-maximal graph image. Equality in the positive weighted series makes its inputs tend coordinatewise to a fixed unimodular phase, which forces the convolution image limit to be the phase times the coefficient partial sums. Such a limit belongs to \(c_0\) only when the full coefficient sum vanishes. Conversely, the finite-support ramp estimate is uniform in output coordinates and converges by an \(\ell_1\)-dominated argument. The proof also establishes the independent finite-support criterion for convolution norm attainment, and constructs a phase-aligned, admissible perturbation attaining the exact relative distance \(|\sum_jc_j|\). The zero kernel, both scalar fields, quantifiers and strict gap have been checked.

**Originality — PASS, with qualifications.** The general theory of Choi, Choi, Jung and Martín supplies the QNA framework, examples of non-QNA maps into \(c_0\), abstract adjoint characterizations and density questions. It does not directly establish the full symbol-at-one criterion, norm-limit circle, or exact within-family distance here. Closely related examples involving rank-one perturbations of the canonical inclusion or weak-star attainment use different target spaces and different topology. Unindexed equivalent classifications remain possible; no exhaustive historical priority assertion is made.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The family uses a natural Wiener-convolution calculus coupled to a positive \(\ell_1\) functional. Its quasi norm attainment locus is exactly a symbol-evaluation hyperplane, with an attained operator-norm distance and contrasting behavior of the two coordinate maps. This is a complete natural infinite-dimensional classification, not a finite test or an assertion about global operator-space density.

The mathematical scope requires every functional weight to be strictly positive and the convolution norm to be strictly smaller than \(A\). The paper's range-sum density question remains distinct.

**Sources.** G. Choi, Y. S. Choi, M. Jung, M. Martín, *On quasi norm attaining operators between Banach spaces*, arXiv:2004.11025; DOI 10.1007/s13398-022-01281-x. G. Choi, M. Jung, S. K. Kim, M. Martín, *Weak-star quasi norm attaining operators*, arXiv:2209.07763.
