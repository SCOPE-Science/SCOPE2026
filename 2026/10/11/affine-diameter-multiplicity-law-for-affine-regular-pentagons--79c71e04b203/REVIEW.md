# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Outside a finite union of exceptional segments, every affine diameter of a pentagon in general relative position is uniquely represented by an edge and its opposite support vertex. For an affine-regular pentagon these five families sweep five explicit triangles. Their internal boundaries are exactly the five pentagram diagonals, each shared by two triangle families, so crossing a generic boundary preserves the parity of the multiplicity. A boundary-near point has multiplicity \(1\); hence all generic multiplicities are \(1\), \(3\), or \(5\). Exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) geometry identifies the fivefold region with the central pentagon and gives its area. The other two level areas follow from total area and the exact first moment. The packaged checker replays all algebraic identities without floating-point inference.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 2014 primary source gives an exact mean-multiplicity theory and, for planar polytopes, the edge--opposite-face decomposition. It does not state or imply the three level-set measures without an additional overlap analysis of the five swept triangles. The 2020 regular-pentagon affine-diameter example concerns Fermat--Ceder concurrency and depicts relevant lines but does not give pointwise multiplicity areas. Searches by the object name, multiplicity formulation, pentagram formulation, exact radical fractions, and moment formulation found no covering statement. Residual risk remains because the regular-pentagon line arrangement is classical enough that an unindexed exercise may contain the same area decomposition.

## Value
**PASS.** Mean affine-diameter multiplicity is a published invariant with sharp general bounds, while pointwise multiplicity is central to classical characterization results such as Eggleston's triangle theorem. The affine-regular pentagon is the first canonical odd polygon beyond the triangle where the multiplicity is genuinely nonconstant. The result gives its complete almost-everywhere distribution, not merely another mean value, and identifies the geometric fivefold core and variance. This is a natural finite classification tied directly to the motivating invariant.
