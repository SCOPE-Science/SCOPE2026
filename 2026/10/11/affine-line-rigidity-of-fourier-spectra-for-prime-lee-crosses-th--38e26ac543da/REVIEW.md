# Review of Fourier spectra of prime Lee crosses

## Correctness — PASS
The cyclotomic minimal polynomial gives the *exact* Fourier zero set, rather than an approximation. Signed-coordinate transitivity normalizes one nonzero spectrum difference. Each further vector lies in the explicitly enumerable graph, and pairwise orthogonality is exactly a graph clique condition. Exhaustive Bron--Kerbosch maximal-clique enumeration gives the four complete profiles in the proof and certifies a unique clique of the necessary size. Direct independent arithmetic tests verify the local noncollinear witness and all count formulas. Thus the four-prime claim follows without extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest checked papers give general finite spectral and tiling principles, Fourier-zero ray structure, and Gabor basis characterizations. Those statements alone do not force every spectrum of a particular prime Lee cross to be a line: the thirteen-field example explicitly exhibits noncollinear compatible partial sets. A targeted semantic search of published mathematical records found no claim that subsumes the full four-prime classification or exact line counts. Non-detection is not a mathematical proof of novelty. An equivalent result in older perfect-Lee-code, complex Hadamard, or specialized spectral-set literature remains a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
A complete classification of Fourier bases for a canonical radius-one Lee ball is a natural, non-arbitrary invariant of this finite Fourier geometry. The classification reaches the first four nontrivial odd primes, including a structural local-to-global phenomenon at thirteen: noncollinear orthogonal partial families exist but no noncollinear full bases do. The exact counts and global obstruction are not merely the elementary existence of a spectrum, and they can serve as finite spectral/tiling benchmarks.

## Closest literature and scope
Kiss--Matolcsi--Matolcsi--Somlai (arXiv:2212.05513v1, Sections 1--3) supplies the general setting and zero-ray lemma; Iosevich--Kolountzakis--Lyubarskii--Mayeli--Pakianathan (Sections 1 and 3 of the accessible manuscript) supplies the spectral-pair/Gabor connection. Neither examined result contains the four complete Lee-cross spectra classifications. Larger primes and other Lee radii are not addressed, and independent validation has not been performed.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
