# Aligned rank-one transfers contract regular-splitting reproduction numbers toward one
## Finding
Let \\(J=F-V\\) be a regular splitting of a real \\(n\\times n\\) matrix, meaning that \\(F\\ge0\\) and \\(V^{-1}\\ge0\\). Assume the nonnegative part has rank one,
\\[
F=u f^{\\mathsf T},\qquad u,f\\in\\mathbb R_{\\ge0}^n,\quad u\\ne0,\quad f\\ne0.
\\]
For any \\(w\\in\\mathbb R_{\\ge0}^n\\) and \\(t\\ge0\\), move the aligned rank-one term \\(tuw^{\\mathsf T}\\) from the \\(-V\\) side to the \\(F\\) side while leaving the Jacobian fixed:
\\[
F_t=u(f+t w)^{\\mathsf T},\qquad V_t=V+tuw^{\\mathsf T},\qquad F_t-V_t=J.
\\]
Put
\\[
R_0:=\\rho(FV^{-1}),\qquad s:=w^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u.
\\]
Then \\(s\\ge0\\), \\(V_t\\) is invertible for every \\(t\\ge0\\), and the transfer is a regular splitting exactly when
\\[
V^{-1}-\\frac{t}{1+ts}(V^{-1}u)(w^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1})\\ge0
\\]
entrywise. On every admissible value of \\(t\\), the reproduction number is
\\[
R_t:=\\rho(F_tV_t^{-1})=\\frac{R_0+ts}{1+ts},
\\qquad
R_t-1=\\frac{R_0-1}{1+ts}.
\\]
Consequently, if \\(s>0\\) and \\(t>0\\), then \\(R_t\\) is strictly between \\(R_0\\) and the threshold \\(1\\), except at \\(R_0=1\\), where it remains exactly \\(1\\). If \\(s=0\\), the reproduction number is unchanged.

The recent ALT-graph construction of Johnston and Cupons recommends a common-target preference precisely to keep \\(F\\) low rank. The theorem quantifies one natural matrix-level regime generated by such common-row transfers: among regular rank-one splittings, an aligned transfer cannot move the reproduction number arbitrarily; it performs an exact fractional-linear contraction toward the stability threshold.

Their Example 3.12 also contains a concrete, independent contraction identity. Write
\\[
A=\\partial_2R_4,\quad B=\\partial_2R_3,\quad C=\\partial_3R_5,\quad D=\\partial_3R_6,
\\]
with \\(A,B,C,D>0\\). Their autocatalytic and catalytic reproduction numbers are
\\[
R_a=\\frac{D(A+B)}{B(C+2D)},\qquad
R_c=\\frac{AD}{B(C+D)}.
\\]
They satisfy
\\[
R_a-1=\\frac{C+D}{C+2D}(R_c-1).
\\]
Because \\(1/2<(C+D)/(C+2D)<1\\), \\(R_a\\) is strictly closer to \\(1\\) than \\(R_c\\) whenever the system is off threshold.

## Assumptions and scope
The matrix theorem is finite-dimensional and purely algebraic. A regular splitting means \\(F\\ge0\\), \\(V\\) invertible, and \\(V^{-1}\\ge0\\). The aligned transfer uses the same left factor \\(u\\) as the original rank-one matrix \\(F=u f^{\\mathsf T}\\). Regularity after transfer is not automatic; the displayed inverse-positivity inequality is the exact condition for it. No claim is made for unaligned rank-one updates, higher-rank \\(F\\), or transfers for which \\(V_t^{-1}\\) has a negative entry.

For the Johnston--Cupons example, positivity of \\(A,B,C,D\\) is supplied by their kinetic assumptions at the boundary state. The scalar identity compares only their displayed reproduction numbers; it does not assert that their two particular ALT-graph decompositions are related by the aligned matrix transfer above.

## Proof
Since \\(FV^{-1}=u(f^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1})\\) is nonnegative and has rank at most one, its only possible nonzero eigenvalue is
\\[
f^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u.
\\]
Hence
\\[
R_0=f^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u.
\\]
Set \\(s=w^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u\\). Nonnegativity of \\(w\\), \\(V^{-1}\\), and \\(u\\) gives \\(s\\ge0\\). The Sherman--Morrison identity gives
\\[
V_t^{-1}
=
V^{-1}-\\frac{t}{1+ts}(V^{-1}u)(w^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}),
\\]
because \\(1+ts>0\\). This proves invertibility for every \\(t\\ge0\\), and because \\(F_t\\ge0\\) automatically, the transfer is regular exactly when the displayed inverse is entrywise nonnegative.

Multiplying the inverse identity by \\(u\\) gives
\\[
V_t^{-1}u=\\frac{V^{-1}u}{1+ts}.
\\]
Whenever the transfer is regular, \\(F_tV_t^{-1}\\) is again nonnegative of rank at most one. Therefore its spectral radius is its nonnegative rank-one eigenvalue:
\\[
R_t=(f+t w)^{\\mathsf T}V_t^{-1}u
=\\frac{f^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u+t w^{\\mathsf T}V^{-1}u}{1+ts}
=\\frac{R_0+ts}{1+ts}.
\\]
Subtracting \\(1\\) yields
\\[
R_t-1=\\frac{R_0-1}{1+ts}.
\\]
If \\(s>0\\) and \\(t>0\\), the positive factor \\(1/(1+ts)\\) is strictly below \\(1\\), proving strict contraction of threshold distance without a sign change.

For the concrete formulas, direct subtraction gives
\\[
R_a-1
=\\frac{AD-B(C+D)}{B(C+2D)},
\\qquad
R_c-1
=\\frac{AD-B(C+D)}{B(C+D)}.
\\]
Division of the two identities proves the stated factor \\((C+D)/(C+2D)\\).

## Verification
The proof uses exact rank-one spectral algebra and the exact Sherman--Morrison inverse formula. The denominator \\(1+ts\\) is strictly positive for every \\(t\\ge0\\), so no singular case is silently excluded. The theorem does not infer inverse positivity from the update; instead it states the exact entrywise condition under which the transferred pair remains regular. The concrete Johnston--Cupons identity was reduced to the same numerator \\(AD-B(C+D)\\) on both sides, so the strict-between conclusion uses only \\(C,D>0\\).

No finite experiment, numerical fit, or truncated computation is used as proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Avram, Adenane, and Halanay prove that regular splittings exist for the Metzler transversal blocks of positive systems and emphasize that different meaningful splittings can yield different reproduction numbers with the same threshold. Their SEIR example exhibits two such values, one being the square root of the other. That work does not give an exact transformation law between rank-one splittings.

Avram, Adenane, Basnarkov, and Johnston make the nonuniqueness of \\((F,V)\\) central, exhibit several competing decompositions, and compare their threshold domains. Their examples include square relations and more complicated alternatives, but the inspected rank-one and nonuniqueness sections do not state the aligned-transfer formula, its exact inverse-positivity criterion, or the threshold-distance contraction law.

Johnston and Cupons give an algorithm that repeatedly moves ALT-graph instances from the \\(-V\\) component into \\(F\\), with a common-target preference to keep terms in a common row and lower the rank. They state that different splittings may produce different reproduction numbers while preserving the bifurcation set. The present theorem supplies an exact quantitative law for the aligned rank-one case. Their equations (16) and (17) further imply the displayed strict contraction identity, which is not stated in the source.

Classical matrix-splitting comparison theorems give qualitative inequalities between spectral radii under entrywise ordering hypotheses. They do not supply the exact fractional-linear dependence on an aligned rank-one transfer exhibited here.

## Limitations
The aligned hypothesis is essential to the proof: for a general rank-one update \\(ab^{\\mathsf T}\\) not sharing the left factor of \\(F\\), \\(F_t\\) need not remain rank one and the scalar law can fail. Regularity after transfer may cease before any chosen \\(t\\); the theorem characterizes but does not simplify the resulting entrywise admissibility region. The theorem concerns the reproduction number attached to a regular splitting, not a unique biological interpretation of that number.

The originality search cannot exclude an equivalent identity hidden in older matrix-iteration literature under different terminology. The closest located comparison theorems give ordering, not this exact law; this remains a bibliographic residual risk.
