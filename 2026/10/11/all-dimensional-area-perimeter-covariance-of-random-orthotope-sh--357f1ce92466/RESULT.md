# All-dimensional area–perimeter covariance of random orthotope shadows

## Finding
Let \(d\ge3\) be an integer, let \(a_1,\ldots,a_d>0\), and let \(A\) and \(P\) be the Euclidean area and perimeter of the orthogonal projection of the axis-aligned \(d\)-orthotope with side lengths \(a_i\) onto a Haar-uniform unoriented two-plane. Write \(e_j\) for the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(j\) in the side lengths. Define
\[
\alpha_d=\frac{2\Gamma((d-1)/2)}{d\sqrt\pi\Gamma(d/2)},\qquad
\beta_d=\frac{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(d/2)}{2(d-1)\Gamma((d+1)/2)},\qquad
\eta=1-\frac\pi4.
\]
Then the following are exact:
\[
\mathbb E[A]=\frac{e_2}{d-1},\qquad
\mathbb E[P]=2(d-1)\beta_d e_1,
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[AP]=2\alpha_d(e_1e_2-3\eta e_3)},
\]
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)=2\big[(\alpha_d-\beta_d)e_1e_2-3\eta\alpha_d e_3\big]}.
\]
In particular, with the explicit positive constant
\[
c_d=6\left[\frac{d}{d-2}(\alpha_d-\beta_d)-\eta\alpha_d\right]>0,
\]
one has the **sharp, all-shapes inequality**
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)\ge c_d e_3>0},
\]
with equality in the first inequality if and only if all \(d\) side lengths agree. In high dimension,
\[
\boxed{c_d\sim\frac32\sqrt{\frac2\pi}(16-5\pi)d^{-5/2}}.
\]
The equal-side case gives the exact smallest covariance after division by \(e_3\); no scale normalization or aspect-ratio restriction is imposed.

## Assumptions and scope
All projections are orthogonal with respect to the usual Euclidean metric. The two-plane is uniform on the Grassmannian \(G(d,2)\), so both the projected area and perimeter are measured in the **same randomly sampled plane**. The orthotope is the Cartesian product \(\prod_{i=1}^{d}[-a_i/2,a_i/2]\). The six displayed identities and inequalities apply to every integer \(d\ge3\) and every vector of strictly positive side lengths. The covariance is an ordinary ensemble covariance, not a pointwise relation between area and perimeter.

## Proof
**1. Zonotope reduction and one-row moments.** Select an orthonormal Haar two-frame, with row coordinates \((x_i,y_i)\), and define \(r_i=\sqrt{x_i^2+y_i^2}\), \(D_{ij}=x_iy_j-x_jy_i\). The projected orthotope is the planar zonotope generated by \(a_i(x_i,y_i)\). Its area and perimeter are, without overlap multiplicities,
\[
A=\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j|D_{ij}|,\qquad
P=2\sum_i a_i r_i.
\tag{1}
\]
For every fixed coordinate \(i\), the squared projection length \(r_i^2\) has the beta law \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,(d-2)/2)\); thus
\[
\mathbb E[r_i^2]=\frac2d,\qquad
\mathbb E[r_i]=\frac{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(d/2)}{2\Gamma((d+1)/2)}=(d-1)\beta_d.
\tag{2}
\]
Conditionally on \(r_i\), the vector \((D_{ij})_{j\ne i}\) has norm \(r_i\), and its direction is uniform on the sphere in the \((d-1)\)-dimensional coordinate complement. The mean absolute first coordinate of a uniformly distributed point on the sphere \(S^{d-2}\) equals
\[
\mu_d=\frac{\Gamma((d-1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(d/2)}.
\]
Consequently, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\mathbb E[|D_{ij}|r_i]=\frac2d\mu_d=\alpha_d,
\qquad
\mathbb E|D_{ij}|=\mathbb E[r_i]\mu_d=\frac1{d-1}.
\tag{3}
\]

**2. The disjoint-incidence moment.** For mutually distinct \(i,j,k\), condition on \(t=r_k\). An orthonormal frame may be selected so that its first direction has perpendicular-to-\(e_k\) component of length \(\sqrt{1-t^2}\), while the second direction lies completely in \(e_k^\perp\). Rotational invariance in \(e_k^\perp\cong\mathbb R^{d-1}\) yields
\[
|D_{ij}|=\sqrt{1-t^2}\,|\widetilde D_{ij}|,
\]
where the tilde determinant is from an independent Haar two-frame in \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\). The mean of that determinant is \(1/(d-2)\) by (3) with dimension reduced by one. The beta density of \(t\) gives
\[
\mathbb E[t\sqrt{1-t^2}]
=\frac{d-2}{2}B\big(\tfrac32,\tfrac{d-1}{2}\big)
=\frac{(d-2)\sqrt\pi\Gamma((d-1)/2)}{4\Gamma((d+2)/2)}.
\]
Thus the exact incidence ratio is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[|D_{ij}|r_k]
=\frac{\sqrt\pi\Gamma((d-1)/2)}{4\Gamma((d+2)/2)}
=\frac\pi4\alpha_d},\qquad k\notin\{i,j\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This includes \(d=3\), because \(d-1=2\) remains a valid Haar two-frame dimension.

**3. Mixed moment and covariance.** Expand (1) and collect repeated and distinct indices using (3)–(4):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[AP]
&=2\alpha_d\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j(a_i+a_j)
+\frac\pi2\alpha_d\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j\sum_{k\ne i,j}a_k\\
&=2\alpha_d[(e_1e_2-3e_3)+(3\pi/4)e_3].
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the claimed exact mixed moment. Equations (2)–(3) give both means, whose product is \(2\beta_d e_1e_2\); subtraction proves the covariance formula.

**4. Sharp positivity.** Maclaurin's inequalities imply
\[
\boxed{e_1e_2\ge\frac{3d}{d-2}e_3},
\tag{5}
\]
with equality precisely when all positive side lengths coincide. We next prove strictly that \(\alpha_d>\beta_d\) and \(c_d>0\), including all dimensions rather than relying on experiments.

Put \(R_d=\Gamma(d/2)/\Gamma((d-1)/2)\). Log-convexity of \(\Gamma\) yields \(R_d^2<(d-1)/2\). Direct simplification gives
\[
\frac{\beta_d}{\alpha_d}=\frac{\pi dR_d^2}{2(d-1)^2}
<\frac{\pi d}{4(d-1)}<1\qquad (d\ge5).
\]
For \(d=3\), \((\alpha_3,\beta_3)=(4/(3\pi),\pi/8)\), and for \(d=4\), \((\alpha_4,\beta_4)=(1/4,2/9)\); both pairs have positive difference.

To prove the stronger condition \(c_d>0\), define
\[
H_d=\frac{(d-1)^2}{2d^2}\left(d-2+\frac8\pi\right),
\qquad D_d=H_d-R_d^2.
\]
Substitution and cancellation show exactly that
\[
\boxed{c_d=\frac{3\pi d^2\alpha_d}{(d-2)(d-1)^2}D_d}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(d=3\), positivity of \(D_3\) is equivalent to \(64+8\pi-9\pi^2>0\). The polynomial is decreasing for \(\pi>1\) and remains \(12/49>0\) at \(\pi=22/7\), proving the strict inequality since \(\pi<22/7\).

For every integer \(d\ge4\), the gamma recurrence and an exact algebraic subtraction give
\[
R_{d+2}=\frac d{d-1}R_d,
\qquad
D_{d+2}=\left(\frac d{d-1}\right)^2D_d
+\frac{d(5\pi-16)+8\pi-24}{2\pi(d+2)^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The final term is strictly negative, because \(5\pi<16\) and \(8\pi-24<8(22/7)-24=8/7\), while for \(d\ge4\), \(4(5\pi-16)+(8\pi-24)=28\pi-88<0\). Stirling's ratio asymptotic gives
\[
R_d^2=\frac d2-\frac34+O(d^{-1}),\qquad
H_d=\frac d2-2+\frac4\pi+O(d^{-1}),
\]
so \(D_d\to4/\pi-5/4>0\). Were some \(D_d\) with \(d\ge4\) nonpositive, (7) would force all its same-parity successors to be negative, contradicting their positive limit. Hence \(D_d>0\) in every dimension. By (6), \(c_d>0\). Combining this with (5) and \(\alpha_d>\beta_d\) proves sharp positivity and characterizes equality.

Finally, the gamma-ratio expansions \(\alpha_d\sim2\sqrt{2/\pi}\,d^{-3/2}\) and \(D_d\to4/\pi-5/4\), inserted into (6), yield \(c_d\sim\frac32\sqrt{2/\pi}(16-5\pi)d^{-5/2}\) as claimed.

## Verification
The accompanying pure-Python `verify.py` reconstructs the exact formulas, checks the dimension-three and dimension-four anchors, checks the sign and equality constant numerically in dimensions \(3\) through \(999\), and independently samples \(120{,}000\) Haar two-planes for selected nonuniform and uniform orthotopes in dimensions \(3,4,5,8\). It reports `VERIFY_OK 45 Haar planes 120000`. Its finite computations test the formulas but do not prove the all-dimensional theorem; the proof is (1)–(7).

For a three-dimensional unit cube the formulas specialize to \(\mathbb E[AP]=2+16/\pi\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)=2+16/\pi-9\pi/4\). For a four-dimensional unit cube they give \(\mathbb E[AP]=6+3\pi/2\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)=3\pi/2-14/3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Finch's 2012 study [1] evaluates the three-dimensional unit-cube mixed shadow moment exactly and reports numerical data for the four-dimensional cube; it also supplies marginal projection formulas. Neither the inspected main results nor their appendices give the all-dimensional arbitrary-orthotope formula or the sharp normalized covariance inequality. Paouris, Pivovarov and Zinn [2] develop a central limit theorem for **projection volume alone** when ambient dimension grows, and use the zonotope determinant formula, but their theorem does not imply finite-dimensional joint area–perimeter covariances. The statement here extends those special cases by an exact same-plane disjoint-incidence moment and a sharp uniform inequality across dimensions. Earlier equivalent integral-geometric formulas cannot be ruled out definitively by a finite literature search.

## Limitations
Only axis-aligned orthotopes in Euclidean space are considered; arbitrary parallelotopes are not covered. No joint distribution or higher moments of the area and perimeter are obtained. The expectation formulas concern a common two-plane, not independently projected shapes. The positive covariance inequality does not assert pointwise monotonicity. The analytic proof establishes the infinite-dimensional quantifiers without relying on the finite numerical verifier. Independent external validation and exhaustive historical-priority verification have not been performed.
