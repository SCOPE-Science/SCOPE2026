PASS. The source tetrahedra reconstruct \(B\) with face vector \((8,25,30,12)\). Exact recursion verifies all eight non-evasive first roots. Pure vertex-decomposability has exactly one first shedding vertex, \(5\); the other seven deletions are non-pure, and the two branches at \(5\) pass the recursive definition.

## Originality
PASS. The closest exact-object source proves that \(B\) is vertex-decomposable and non-evasive and gives one sequence, \(5,2,4,0,1\). That existence statement does not imply the all-eight non-evasive root set or the singleton vertex-decomposable root set. General theorems that vertex-decomposable balls are non-evasive are also existence results. Exact-object, alias, root, shedding-vertex, and sequence searches found no statement covering the dichotomy.

## Value
PASS. Because \(B\) is the vertex-minimal non-extendably collapsible ball, the contrast between complete first-step flexibility for non-evasiveness and a unique first step for vertex-decomposability gives a natural boundary between two standard recursive simplification notions on an extremal example.
