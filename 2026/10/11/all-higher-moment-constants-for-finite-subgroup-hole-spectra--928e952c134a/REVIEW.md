# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The two-coordinate zero-sum case is immediate. In dimensions at least three, the proof independently reconstructs the needed fourth-moment bound on the complex zero-sum hyperplane, then uses the unique quadratic Hermite interpolant with contacts at \(1/(m(m-1))\) and \((m-1)/m\). Strict increase of the second derivative for \(p/2>2\) gives a pointwise majorant, while the positive quadratic coefficient allows the fourth-moment bound to close the estimate. Equality forces one large squared modulus and \(m-1\) equal small squared moduli; zero-sum closure makes their phases collinear. Coset decomposition and strict convexity of the coset energies then give the Fourier constant and the complete equality class. The bundled checker corroborates the majorant, specializations, and explicit extremizers but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual terminology risk. Exact and alias searches for subgroup-complement \(\Lambda(p)\) constants, zero-sum hyperplane \(\ell^2\to\ell^p\) norms, conditional-mean coset formulations, and the closed formula found no implication-equivalent indexed result. Michels supplies the general \(\Lambda(p)\) framework for infinite character sets; Gilbert--Rzeszotnik compute unrestricted finite Fourier-transform norms. Neither inspected theorem restricts to the complement of a dual subgroup or yields this all-real-\(p\) constant. The strongest residual risk is an older centered-sequence or matrix-norm formulation not retrieved by the searches.

## Value
PASS. Complements of dual subgroups are a canonical structured family of finite spectral sets, and passing from isolated even moments to every real \(p>4\) changes the problem from polynomial moment expansion to a continuum norm theorem. The result gives an exact constant independent of the ambient group structure, a complete equality class, and a reusable sharp zero-sum-hyperplane inequality. It is not a routine parameter increment or a finite table.

## Closest literature and limitations
Michels is the closest source for the \(\Lambda(p)\) invariant and all-\(p\) perspective. Gilbert--Rzeszotnik is the closest full finite-abelian Fourier norm source and explicitly treats subgroup wave packets for the unrestricted transform. The present theorem instead concerns a codimension-\(k\) spectral deletion subspace. The range \(2<p<4\) is not treated, and an unindexed equivalent centered-sequence inequality remains possible.
