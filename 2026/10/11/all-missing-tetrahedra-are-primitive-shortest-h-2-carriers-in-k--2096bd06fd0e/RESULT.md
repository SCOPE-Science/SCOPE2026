# All missing tetrahedra are primitive shortest \(H_2\)-carriers in Kühnel's nine-vertex \(\mathbb{CP}^2\)
## Finding
Let \(K\) be Kühnel's nine-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), in the standard labelled presentation with face vector
\[
(9,36,84,90,36).
\]
For an integral simplicial \(2\)-cycle \(z\), write \(|V(z)|\) for the number of vertices appearing in its support. Among cycles representing nonzero classes of
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
the minimum possible value of \(|V(z)|\) is \(4\).

Exactly \(36\) four-vertex supports attain the minimum. They are precisely the four-subsets that are not tetrahedra of \(K\). Because \(K\) is three-neighborly, each such missing four-set contains all four of its triangular faces, and their alternating sum is an embedded tetrahedral two-sphere. Every one of these \(36\) spheres represents a primitive generator \(\pm g\) of integral middle homology.

The full simplicial automorphism group has order \(54\). On the \(36\) primitive shortest carriers it has two support orbits, of sizes \(9\) and \(27\). Its action on integral middle homology is nontrivial:
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(K)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(H_2(K;\mathbb Z))\cong\{\pm1\}
\]
is surjective, with \(27\) automorphisms acting by \(+1\), \(27\) acting by \(-1\), and kernel of order \(27\).

## Assumptions and scope
The complex is the standard Kühnel--Banchoff nine-vertex triangulation. A missing tetrahedron means a four-element vertex set whose four triangular faces are present but whose three-simplex is absent. A shortest carrier is measured by vertex-support size, not by number of triangles or by a geometric area.

The claim is about integral simplicial homology. The signs of individual carrier classes depend on orientation choices; primitivity, the unordered support orbits, and the induced \(\pm1\)-action are orientation-independent.

## Proof
The exact facet presentation reconstructs
\[
f(K)=(9,36,84,90,36).
\]
In particular, all \(\binom 93=84\) triangles occur, so every four-set has the complete tetrahedral boundary as a two-dimensional subcomplex. There are
\[
\binom94-90=36
\]
missing tetrahedra.

No nonzero integral two-cycle can be supported on at most three vertices: on three vertices there is at most one oriented triangle and its boundary is nonzero. Thus any nonzero middle-homology class needs at least four vertices.

For a four-set \(Q\), the integral cycle group supported on its four triangular faces is generated by the oriented tetrahedral boundary \(\partial Q\). If \(Q\) is a tetrahedron of \(K\), then \(\partial Q\) is an integral boundary in \(K\), so that four-support carries no nonzero middle-homology class.

For the \(36\) missing tetrahedra, use the integral two-cocycle \(w\) supplied and checked by `verify.py`. It has coefficient \(+1\) on
\[
126,145,146,148,178,235,245,268,278,358,378,457,458,459,
\]
coefficient \(-1\) on
\[
134,167,256,257,347,
\]
and coefficient zero on every other triangle, with all triangles increasingly oriented. The verifier checks that \(w\) evaluates to zero on the oriented boundary of every one of the \(90\) tetrahedra, hence \(w\) is a cocycle. On the \(36\) missing tetrahedral boundaries its evaluations are exactly \(+1\) eighteen times and \(-1\) eighteen times.

Therefore every missing tetrahedral sphere is nonzero in integral homology. Since a cohomology class pairs with it as \(\pm1\), its homology class is primitive. The published topology gives \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), so each one equals \(\pm g\) for a generator \(g\). This proves both the minimum support and the complete set of minimizers.

For symmetry, `verify.py` exhausts all \(9!\) vertex permutations and finds exactly \(54\) preserving the facet set. Their action partitions the \(36\) missing four-sets into orbits of sizes \(9\) and \(27\). Fix an oriented missing tetrahedral boundary on which \(w\) evaluates \(+1\). For each simplicial automorphism, push this oriented cycle forward and evaluate \(w\) again. Because the source cycle is a primitive generator, the resulting value is exactly the scalar \(\pm1\) by which the automorphism acts on \(H_2\). Exhaustion gives \(27\) of each sign, proving surjectivity and the order-\(27\) kernel.

As an independent mod-two check, the verifier computes boundary ranks \(28\) and \(55\) in degrees two and three, hence
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)=84-28-55=1.
\]
All \(90\) tetrahedral boundaries reduce to zero and all \(36\) missing tetrahedral boundaries reduce to the unique nonzero class.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` contains the complete \(36\)-facet presentation and uses only Python's standard library. It reconstructs every face, checks three-neighborliness, computes exact mod-two boundary ranks, exhausts all \(126\) four-sets, checks the integral cocycle on all \(90\) tetrahedra and all \(36\) missing tetrahedra, exhausts all \(9!\) relabelings, and computes both carrier orbits and the integral \(H_2\) sign action.

Its exact successful output is:

`VERIFY_OK f=9,36,84,90,36 H2_F2_dim=1 missing4=36 present4=90 missing4_mod2_nonzero=36 cocycle_nonzero=19 missing4_integral_evals=+1:18,-1:18 aut=54 carrier_orbits=9,27 H2_action=+1:27,-1:27 kernel=27`

The finite computation proves the carrier classification and symmetry action from the supplied combinatorial data. Identification of the rank-one integral homology group uses the published topology of the Kühnel triangulation.

## Relationship to prior work
Kühnel and Banchoff introduced the nine-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). Bagchi and Datta later gave computer-free uniqueness proofs and emphasized complementarity, the embedded affine-plane structure, and the full automorphism group. Köhler and Lutz place the complex in the vertex-transitive census and record it as a vertex-minimal triangulation.

The closest indexed prior finding proves a different but related statement: every proper induced nonface in this complex collapses to a tetrahedral sphere. That result concerns the intrinsic simple-homotopy type of induced subcomplexes. It does not determine whether the resulting tetrahedral sphere maps trivially, primitively, or as a multiple in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\), nor does it determine the sign action of the full automorphism group on integral middle homology. The present result supplies those inclusion-sensitive integral statements and the complete shortest-carrier classification.

## Limitations
The result concerns one fixed minimal triangulation and vertex-support length. It does not classify area-minimizing geometric representatives, arbitrary two-cycles with more than four vertices, or analogous spectra for other triangulations of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). The originality assessment is based on indexed searches and inspected source material; the inaccessible portions of older literature could contain some of the same finite orbit data.
