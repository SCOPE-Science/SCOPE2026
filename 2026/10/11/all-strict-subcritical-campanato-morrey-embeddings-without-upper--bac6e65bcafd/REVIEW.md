# Scientific review: all-subcritical Campanato–Morrey embedding counterexample

## Correctness — PASS
The radius-normalized definitions agree with the cited source. The exact primitive \(F(t)=2t-t\log t\) gives \(\mu(X)=2\) and the explicit two-sided ball bounds. For nested balls with radius ratio \(t\ge1\), the lower volume-growth factor \(t/[4(1+\log t)]\) follows directly from endpoint monotonicity. Telescoping their averages reduces the inclusion to the convergent series \(\sum_{j\ge0}(1+j\log2)^{1/p}2^{-j(1-\lambda)/p}\), valid precisely on the claimed strict range. The finite \(L^p\) term controls the largest scale; \(f\equiv1\) witnesses endpoint failure. All endpoints \(p=1\) and \(\lambda=0\) are included. Finite numerical tests are supplementary rather than proof.

## Originality — PASS, with literature risk
The direct predecessor, Górka–Stępień, arXiv:2610.04622v1, Theorem 3.2, assumes Ahlfors regularity in its sufficient direction and proves only individually necessary upper \(\lambda\)-regularity. Its Question 2 explicitly asks for the converse uniform-in-exponent inference that this example refutes. Stempak–Tao (2014) considers generalized local Morrey/Campanato spaces and the classical global Lebesgue cases; the material reviewed does not state this log-density counterexample or the all-strict-subcritical/no-endpoint contrast. The closest available published Morrey-related records concern derivative compactness or weighted multipliers, not spatial Campanato-to-Morrey inclusion. Earlier relevant results on Hölder targets and Morrey time derivatives do not imply this counterexample. An implicit consequence of a more general weighted-Campanato result has not been ruled out exhaustively.

## Value — PASS
The example gives a negative answer to a specifically posed 2026 structural question, in a compact connected interval carrying a nonatomic, Borel regular, doubling, lower \(1\)-regular measure. It isolates a genuine endpoint phenomenon: all strict exponents work simultaneously, but at the limiting exponent the constant function fails the target space. The logarithmic mass-growth estimate supplies a reusable analytic mechanism, beyond a mere parameter substitution or finite example.

## Scope and residual limitations
The result does not characterize all measures for which all subcritical inclusions hold. The embedding constants depend on \(p\) and \(\lambda\). A broader generalized weighted-space theorem may implicitly contain the example; the review of the available 2014 online full text was limited by incomplete mathematical formula rendering. Only the published scientific claim has been compared, not any unpublished work.
