# An exact divisibility law for embeddings of modular gap shifts

## Finding
For each integer \(m\ge2\), consider the binary *modular gap shift* \(Y_m\), whose runs of zeros between consecutive ones have lengths in \(m\mathbb N_0\). For all integers \(m,n\ge2\), a continuous shift-commuting injection exists from \(Y_m\) to \(Y_n\) **if and only if** \(n\mid m\). Thus embeddability within this family is precisely reverse divisibility of the gap modulus, even though entropy imposes only an inequality.

More explicitly, if \(m>n\) and \(n\nmid m\), then \(h(Y_m)<h(Y_n)\) but no embedding exists. The exact entropy is \(h(Y_m)=\log\lambda_m\), where \(\lambda_m>1\) uniquely solves \(\lambda_m^m=\lambda_m^{m-1}+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\) and all sequences are indexed by \(\mathbb Z\), with left shift \(\sigma\). The rule constrains every pair of consecutive occurrences of symbol \(1\), where *consecutive* means that no further \(1\) lies between them. In particular, the all-zero and all-one sequences, sequences with a unique \(1\), and configurations with finitely many \(1\)'s belong to \(Y_m\) whenever the intervening finite zero-runs obey the rule. An embedding is an injective continuous map \(\phi:Y_m\to Y_n\) satisfying \(\phi\sigma=\sigma\phi\); no assumption that it is symbolwise or a subshift inclusion is imposed.

## Proof
**Sufficiency.** If \(n\mid m\), then every nonnegative multiple of \(m\) is a multiple of \(n\). Hence \(Y_m\subseteq Y_n\), and inclusion is an embedding.

**The two fixed points.** For every \(j\ge2\), the shift \(Y_j\) has exactly two shift-fixed configurations, \(\mathbf0=0^{\mathbb Z}\) and \(\mathbf1=1^{\mathbb Z}\). Any embedding \(\phi:Y_m\to Y_n\) is a finite-radius sliding block code: there exists \(R\ge0\) such that the output at any site depends only on the input on the corresponding radius-\(R\) window. Injectivity forces the images of the fixed configurations to be distinct. We first rule out interchanging them.

**The fixed points cannot be swapped.** Assume for contradiction that \(\phi(\mathbf1)=\mathbf0\) and \(\phi(\mathbf0)=\mathbf1\). Let \(v\in Y_m\) be \(\mathbf1\) except for a single block of exactly \(m\) zeros. Since \(v\) differs from \(\mathbf1\) at finitely many sites, locality makes \(\phi(v)\) differ from \(\mathbf0\) at finitely many sites. Injectivity shows \(\phi(v)\ne\mathbf0\), so the output has a nonempty finite set \(S\subset\mathbb Z\) of occurrences of \(1\). Let \(a=\min S\) and \(b=\max S\).

For each sufficiently large integer \(\ell\), form \(v^{(\ell)}\in Y_m\) with two blocks of \(m\) zeros, the second beginning \(m+\ell\) positions after the first, and with \(1\)'s everywhere else. Each zero-block has allowed length \(m\), so \(v^{(\ell)}\in Y_m\) for every \(\ell\ge1\). For \(\ell\) larger than the finite block-code interaction radius and the span \(b-a\), locality implies that the \(1\)'s of \(\phi(v^{(\ell)})\) form exactly \(S\cup(S+m+\ell)\). The output therefore has a pair of consecutive \(1\)'s with \(m+\ell+a-b-1\) intervening zeros, which must be divisible by \(n\). This must hold for all sufficiently large successive values of \(\ell\), a contradiction because \(n\ge2\). Therefore no embedding swaps the fixed points.

**Modulus obstruction when the fixed points are preserved.** We necessarily have \(\phi(\mathbf0)=\mathbf0\) and \(\phi(\mathbf1)=\mathbf1\). Let \(u\in Y_m\) be the configuration whose sole \(1\) is at the origin. Since \(u\) differs from \(\mathbf0\) at one site, \(\phi(u)\) contains finitely many \(1\)'s, and it contains at least one by injectivity. Write \(S\) for their finite nonempty set of positions and again set \(a=\min S\), \(b=\max S\).

For each positive integer \(k\), let \(u^{(k)}\) have exactly two \(1\)'s, at positions \(0\) and \(km+1\), and zeros elsewhere. Their gap has length \(km\), so \(u^{(k)}\in Y_m\). For all sufficiently large \(k\), the radius-\(R\) windows around these isolated input \(1\)'s do not interact, and the nonzero output sites are exactly \(S\cup(S+km+1)\). The last \(1\) in the first cluster (at \(b\)) and the first \(1\) in the second cluster (at \(a+km+1\)) are consecutive occurrences, separated by \(km+a-b\) zeros. Hence \(n\mid (km+a-b)\) for every sufficiently large \(k\). Subtracting the two divisibilities for successive \(k\)'s gives \(n\mid m\), as required.

**Entropy separation.** The shift \(Y_j\) has a right-resolving irreducible \(j\)-state labeled graph: at state \(0\), a \(1\)-loop and a \(0\)-edge to state \(1\); at each subsequent state a sole \(0\)-edge to the next state modulo \(j\). The labeling is finite-to-one (only the all-zero configuration can have multiple phase lifts), and therefore preserves topological entropy. The Perron eigenvalue \(\lambda_j>1\) of the graph satisfies \(1=\lambda_j^{-1}+\lambda_j^{-j}\), equivalently \(\lambda_j^j=\lambda_j^{j-1}+1\); this equation has a unique root greater than one. For fixed \(\lambda>1\), its right-hand expression \(\lambda^{-1}+\lambda^{-j}\) strictly decreases with \(j\), while for fixed \(j\) it strictly decreases with \(\lambda\). Thus \(m>n\) implies \(\lambda_m<\lambda_n\) and hence \(h(Y_m)<h(Y_n)\). Together with the obstruction, this proves the stated entropy counterexamples.

## Verification
Every embedding is a sliding block code by compactness and the Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem. The two finite-island arguments use only exact locality and the existence of two allowed far-separated perturbations; the congruence conclusions hold for every sufficiently large separation, not merely finitely many test cases. The unique Perron root and strict entropy ordering follow from monotonicity of the displayed scalar equation. In the smallest case \(m=n=2\), the inclusion is the identity; when \(n\mid m\), all allowed source gaps are allowed target gaps. No numerical experiment is needed for the general theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Marcus, Meyerovitch and Wu (2026) characterize embeddings into near-Markov sofic shifts through periodic-point and blowup conditions, and discuss the even shift and more general congruence-constrained sofic shifts. Their general criteria do not state the explicit pairwise formula \(Y_m\hookrightarrow Y_n\iff n\mid m\). The present result evaluates the embeddability question in this natural modulus-parameterized family using a fixed-point and finite-island obstruction; it does not purport to replace the general theorem. Marcus, Meyerovitch, Thomsen and Wu (2025) study embeddings that factor through SFT covers, which is a distinct restriction and does not supply the claimed arbitrary-embedding divisibility classification.

## Limitations
The claim concerns this specific family of two-symbol gap shifts and embeddings, not factors, conjugacies between arbitrary sofic shifts, or arbitrary generalized gap sets. Although extensive source searches were made, an equivalent formulation in older or unindexed work on renewal, \(S\)-gap, or almost-finite-type shifts remains possible. The general near-Markov embedding theorem can in principle decide the individual cases, but the explicit modulus criterion is the contribution asserted here.
