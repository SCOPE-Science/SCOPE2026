# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The two tests have their exact one-dimensional normalizations: a two-point \(|x-y|^{-2}\) BSVY measure with a level for the **difference itself**, and an \(r^{-2}\,dr\,da\) mean-oscillation measure. The base log cusp is absolutely continuous and its BSVY membership is independently present in Chen--Yang--Yuan--Zhang, Lemma 2.5. For mean oscillation, a near-tip section of length proportional to the radius yields a logarithmic weak-level integral, while off-tip intervals admit an \(L^1\) derivative majorant; large radii are bounded by the compact support and \(L^1\) norm. The derivative hypothesis makes a bounded envelope ratio give a globally Lipschitz outer map. Both lower tests use disjoint positive and negative subintervals; the weighted integrals grow like \(\Phi(s)/s\), proving both contrapositives. The \(W^{1,1}\) statement follows from monotone absolute continuity on the two arcs, including endpoint matching.

## Originality

PASS with qualified literature risk. The focal BSVY source proves one base cusp and identifies a weak-\(L^1\) term under logarithmic substitution; Frank proves divergence for a specific subcritical power family in a different mean-oscillation test. The 2024 foundational BSVY source describes generic endpoint pathology rather than this joint infinite-dimensional profile equivalence. Statement-level comparisons and actual searches did not locate the present shared necessary-and-sufficient envelope criterion, including its two-scale borderline. An unindexed treatment of monotone endpoint oscillation profiles could still imply it and deserves independent expert literature review.

## Value

PASS. The precise structural question is where two *different* derivative-free weak tests, both exceptional at Sobolev exponent one, recover simultaneous finiteness on the canonical one-sided cusp family. The theorem answers this for an infinite-dimensional regular profile cone by the same simple but nontrivial logarithmic envelope and determines the next loglog cutoff at equality. These sharp boundary conditions distinguish passing and failing continuous \(W^{1,1}\) functions and motivate further comparison of endpoint nonlocal and mean-oscillation criteria. The result makes no global equivalence or sharp-constant claim.
