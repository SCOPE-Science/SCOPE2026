# An explicit scale-two Rips collapse onto the rhombicuboctahedral sphere
## Finding
Let \(P\) be the rhombicuboctahedron whose vertices are the \(24\) permutations of \((\pm(1+\sqrt2),\pm1,\pm1)\), with independent signs; let \(G\) be its edge graph. Equip the vertices with **shortest-path graph distance**, not Euclidean chordal distance, and use the inclusive Vietoris–Rips convention. A canonical triangulation \(K\) of the boundary \(\partial P\), using the lexicographically least diagonal on each square, is a subcomplex of the scale-two Rips complex, and there is an explicit sequence of exactly \(252\) elementary simplicial collapses
\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(G),d_G)\searrow K\cong S^2.
\]
At scale one, \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le1}(V(G),d_G)\simeq\bigvee^{17}S^1\). Consequently all seventeen independent integral first-homology classes present at scale one die at scale two, while the scale-two complex has one free integral second-homology generator. The statement does not determine the death threshold of that second-dimensional class.

## Assumptions and scope
Set \(a=1+\sqrt2\). Index vertices by triples of signs \(s\in\{-1,1\}^3\) and a long-coordinate axis \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\): the point \(v(i,s)\) has coordinate \(a s_i\) in axis \(i\) and coordinate \(s_j\) otherwise. In the certificate, vertex \(8i+k\) uses the lexicographically \(k\)-th sign triple, starting at \((-1,-1,-1)\).

Two such vertices are adjacent in \(G\) exactly when either (i) their long-coordinate axes differ and their sign triples agree, or (ii) their axes agree, their signs at that axis agree, and their other two signs differ in precisely one position. This follows by comparing squared Euclidean distances with the edge length squared \(4\). The graph metric counts the minimum number of these edges along a path. A finite vertex set spans a simplex at threshold \(r\) exactly if every pair has graph distance at most \(r\). The finding concerns thresholds \(1\) and \(2\), and not Euclidean Vietoris–Rips complexes on these coordinates.

Define \(K\) combinatorially: include every \(3\)-clique of \(G\) as a triangle; for each induced \(4\)-cycle with vertex set \(Q\), insert two triangles incident to the lexicographically smaller of its two missing diagonals. Include all faces of these triangles. This yields the stated fixed triangulation.

## Proof
**Polyhedral boundary.** The eight triangles are the facets of \(P\) with supporting planes \(\sum_{j=0}^{2} s_j x_j=a+2\), one for each sign triple. The eighteen square facets consist of the six coordinate planes \(x_i=\pm a\), plus twelve planes \(\epsilon x_i+\delta x_j=a+1\) for unordered coordinate pairs and signs \(\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}\). These facets account for every edge of the convex hull, which has \(24\) vertices, \(48\) edges, and \(26\) facets. The eight triangular faces are precisely all graph triangles, and the eighteen square faces are precisely all induced \(4\)-cycles, as verified by complete enumeration from the explicit adjacency rule. Therefore \(K\) triangulates \(\partial P\). Independently, the finite certificate verifier establishes that \(K\) is connected and has face vector \((24,66,44)\), every edge lies in exactly two triangles, every vertex link is a single cycle, and Euler characteristic \(2\); the classification of closed connected surfaces then gives \(K\cong S^2\).

**Scale two.** Breadth-first search from each of the \(24\) vertices computes every graph distance. A strictly increasing clique enumeration in the graph of pairs at distance at most two produces exactly \(638\) nonempty simplices, with face vector \((24,132,248,186,48)\) in dimensions zero through four, and verifies \(K\) is a subcomplex. The accompanying `collapse_certificate.json` lists \(252\) ordered pairs of simplices. For each successive pair \((\sigma,\tau)\), the standalone verifier checks \(\sigma\subset\tau\), \(\dim\tau=\dim\sigma+1\), both faces currently exist outside \(K\), and \(\tau\) is the **only** codimension-one coface of \(\sigma\) in the current complex. Thus \(\sigma\) is a free face and deletion of both is an elementary simplicial collapse. After all \(252\) deletions the remaining face set is **exactly** \(K\), since \(638-2(252)=134=|K|\) and equality is checked face by face. This exhaustive finite certificate and its standalone checker constitute a proof of the collapse, rather than a sample-based inference.

**Scale one.** The \(1\)-skeleton has \(24\) vertices and \(48\) edges. Its only higher-dimensional simplices are eight vertex-disjoint triangular faces. Remove a free edge with its triangle for each of the eight triangles, leaving a connected graph with \(24\) vertices and \(40\) edges. This graph has cycle rank \(40-24+1=17\); hence its homotopy type is a wedge of seventeen circles. The degree-one classes all map to zero at threshold two because the target there is a sphere.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_rhombicuboctahedron.py` uses only Python's standard library. Execute it next to `collapse_certificate.json` with `python3 verify_rhombicuboctahedron.py`. It reconstructs all \(24\) vertices and \(48\) edges, checks all graph-distance values by breadth-first search, enumerates the \(638\) scale-two simplices, all eight triangles and eighteen induced squares, confirms the closed connected surface conditions for \(K\), and individually validates the \(252\) listed free-face collapses. It terminates with `VERIFY_OK`; the companion certificate records every deletion pair in order. The graph-metric, finite-domain and topology statements are fully inspectable without external mathematical packages.

## Relationship to prior work
Saleh, Titz Mite and Witzel determine graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complexes of the five Platonic solids and identify Archimedean solids as a possible generalization. The rhombicuboctahedron is Archimedean, not Platonic, and its explicit scale-two collapse is not among their results. Adamaszek’s *Clique complexes and graph powers* (arXiv:1104.0433) establishes general stability criteria, a sufficient large-girth condition, and a universality realization theorem. The target graph contains triangles and induced squares, violates the high-girth hypothesis, and changes its homotopy type from a wedge of circles to a sphere; therefore the stability results cannot imply its collapse. The universality theorem constructs some graph for an arbitrary target homotopy type and does not identify the fixed rhombicuboctahedral graph or the stronger embedded-boundary simple collapse. Published computations of the **twelve-vertex Euclidean cuboctahedron** use a different vertex set and metric, and a published graph-metric result for the **truncated tetrahedron** uses a different graph; neither implies this statement. Exact searches for `rhombicuboctahedron`, `rhombicuboctahedral graph`, `graph square clique complex`, `scale-two graph Rips` and related formulations in the literature and published mathematical record did not identify an equivalent or stronger covering theorem. Absence from indexed searches alone cannot prove worldwide novelty.

## Limitations
The theorem is for the explicitly specified graph and thresholds \(1\) and \(2\). The collapse count is for this particular witness sequence, not a proved minimum over all collapse sequences. No claim is made about thresholds at least \(3\), Euclidean chordal-distance complexes, complete persistent homology after threshold two, robustness under perturbations, or all Archimedean solids. The literature check may miss unindexed or differently formulated coverage. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
