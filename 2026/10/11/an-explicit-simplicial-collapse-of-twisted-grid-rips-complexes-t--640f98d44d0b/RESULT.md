# An explicit simplicial collapse of twisted grid Rips complexes to the Klein bottle

## Finding

For all integers \(m,n\ge 7\), define the **twisted square-grid graph** \(G_{m,n}\) on vertices \(v_{i,j}\), with \(0\le i<m\) and \(0\le j<n\). Put edges \(v_{i,j}v_{i,j+1\bmod n}\), ordinary horizontal edges \(v_{i,j}v_{i+1,j}\) for \(i<m-1\), and twisted wrap edges \(v_{m-1,j}v_{0,n-1-j}\). Equip its vertex set with the shortest-path graph metric and form the **inclusive** Vietoris–Rips complex \(R_{m,n}=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(G_{m,n}))\): a finite vertex set spans a face precisely when every two vertices are at distance at most two.

**Theorem.** There is an explicit sequence of exactly \(9mn\) elementary simplicial free-face collapses
\[
R_{m,n}\searrow T_{m,n},
\]
where \(T_{m,n}\) is the square-cell triangulation of the Klein bottle obtained by placing the same chosen diagonal in every grid square. In particular \(|R_{m,n}|\simeq\mathrm{Klein}\) and
\[
H_k(R_{m,n};\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Here the triangulation is of the twisted quotient, not a triangulation of an untwisted torus. The initial face counts in dimensions zero through four are \((mn,6mn,10mn,6mn,mn)\); the final face counts in dimensions zero through two are \((mn,3mn,2mn)\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(V=\mathbb Z^2/\Gamma\), with \(\Gamma\) generated by the vertical shift \(h(i,j)=(i,j+n)\) and the glide reflection \(g(i,j)=(i+m,n-1-j)\). Edges are induced by unit horizontal/vertical steps in the square lattice. Representatives \((i,j)\) for \(0\le i<m\), \(0\le j<n\) give precisely the above graph. Its quotient-square cellular surface is a Klein bottle, since \(ghg^{-1}=h^{-1}\), and the glide reflection reverses orientation.

Write \(E,W,N,S\) for the four lattice neighbors of a vertex \(v\). Its closed star \(A_v=\{v,E,W,N,S\}\) is a five-vertex simplex at scale two. For each oriented lattice unit square, write its corners in cyclic order \(a,b,c,d\), where \(a\) is a chosen lower-left lift, \(b\) is right, \(c\) is up-and-right and \(d\) is up; its simplex is \(B_Q=\{a,b,c,d\}\). All these definitions descend to the twisted quotient. The specified triangulation \(T_{m,n}\) uses triangles \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\) for each square, and all their faces. The parameter lower bound ensures no local identifications interfere with the face classification or collapse.

## Proof

**Step 1: complete facet classification.** Every nonidentity deck transformation moves every square-lattice point by at least \(\min(m,n)\ge7\) in Manhattan distance. The cyclic vertical translations are separated by at least \(n\); transformations involving an odd glide have horizontal displacement at least \(m\). Fix a clique of graph distances at most two and lift one vertex to the lattice. Every other clique vertex has a unique lift within distance two of it. For any two such lifts, an alternative deck translate differs by distance at least \(7-4=3>2\), so their quotient distance at most two forces their lifted distance to be at most two as well. Thus every clique has a mutually distance-at-most-two lift in \(\mathbb Z^2\).

In \(\mathbb Z^2\), a pairwise Manhattan-distance-at-most-two set is contained in either a five-point closed unit star or the four corners of a unit square. Indeed, if one coordinate span equals two, the two extreme-coordinate points must share the other coordinate; all points are then at lattice distance at most one from their midpoint. Otherwise each coordinate span is at most one and the set lies in a unit square. Therefore the maximal simplices are **exactly** the \(mn\) five-point stars \(A_v\) and the \(mn\) four-point unit squares \(B_Q\), which are distinct and maximal for \(m,n\ge7\).

**Step 2: five phases of elementary collapses.** In each pair below, the first listed simplex has codimension one in the second and is contained in exactly one remaining larger simplex. Quotient uniqueness follows from the established lattice face classification and minimum deck displacement.

1. For every vertex \(v\), pair the noncentral neighbor tetrahedron \(\{E,W,N,S\}\) with its five-point simplex \(A_v\). This removes \(mn\) pairs. A neighbor tetrahedron is contained in no square and no other closed star.
2. For each \(v\) and each three-element subset \(U\subseteq\{E,W,N,S\}\), pair \(U\) with \(U\cup\{v\}\). This removes \(4mn\) pairs. Every such triple contains an opposite-neighbor pair. After phase one, only the indicated centered star tetrahedron contains it; distinct centers cannot contain the same opposite-neighbor triple.
3. For every square \(Q=(a,b,c,d)\), pair \(\{a,b,d\}\) with \(B_Q\), removing \(mn\) pairs. Following phases one and two, no star tetrahedra remain, and this corner triangle lies in only its own square tetrahedron.
4. For every \(Q=(a,b,c,d)\), pair the unchosen diagonal edge \(\{b,d\}\) with the remaining triangle \(\{b,c,d\}\), removing \(mn\) pairs. The edge is shared only by the two corner triangles of its own square; the other, \(\{a,b,d\}\), was removed in phase three.
5. For every \(v\), pair the straight horizontal distance-two edge \(\{W,E\}\) with \(\{W,v,E\}\), and pair the straight vertical distance-two edge \(\{S,N\}\) with \(\{S,v,N\}\). This removes \(2mn\) pairs. Such an opposite-neighbor edge belongs to just one remaining collinear triangle, after the neighbor triples and square tetrahedra have disappeared.

There are no other faces left: the only triangle types in the complete facet classification are triples of centered neighbors, right-angle corner triangles, and straight collinear triples. Phases two through five remove respectively the superfluous neighbor triples, the triangles involving the discarded diagonals, and the straight triples; the surviving triangles are exactly \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\). The surviving one-skeleton consists precisely of the original graph edges together with the chosen square diagonals. Counting gives \((1+4+1+1+2)mn=9mn\) genuine codimension-one elementary collapses.

**Step 3: identify the target and its integral homology.** The two triangles of each square subdivide the cellular quotient \(\mathbb R^2/\Gamma\) without changing its topology. The glide reflection presents the resulting closed connected surface as the Klein bottle. The standard Klein-bottle cellular chain complex has one vertex, two one-cells, and one two-cell whose attaching boundary represents twice one of the one-cell generators; hence its homology is \(H_0=\mathbb Z\), \(H_1=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), and zero in higher degrees. Elementary simplicial collapses preserve simple-homotopy type and integral homology. This proves the theorem for every \(m,n\ge7\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_klein_grid.py` with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. The standalone program independently constructs graph distances, lists **all** maximal scale-two cliques via Bron–Kerbosch search, expands the full simplicial complex, and tests that every specified pair is a codimension-one free-face collapse. It asserts equality with the target triangulation and all face counts, then computes exact finite-field boundary ranks. On twisted grids \((m,n)=(7,7),(7,8),(8,9),(9,10)\), it checks respectively \(441,504,648,810\) elementary pairs; in every case it finds mod-two reduced positive Betti ranks \((\beta_1,\beta_2)=(2,1)\) and mod-three ranks \((1,0)\). The corresponding untwisted control grids retain mod-three ranks \((2,1)\). The program finishes with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests check implementation and boundary cases but do **not** replace the all-parameter proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Saleh–Titz Mite–Witzel, *Vietoris–Rips complexes of Platonic solids* (arXiv:2302.14388), classify graph-metric Rips complexes of specific Platonic graphs and motivate explicit combinatorial collapses; they do not study twisted rectangular grid quotients. Adams–Adetowubo–Barriga-Acosta–Feng–Sterling, *Vietoris–Rips complexes of torus grids* (arXiv:2502.07134), establish the scale-two homotopy type for **untwisted torus** square grids in an appropriate size regime and classify Manhattan-lattice clique shapes. The local star/square description overlaps their lattice methods, and is not asserted to be new. Their results do not imply the present **nonorientable** quotient, its integral two-torsion, or the displayed simple-homotopy collapse certificate. Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers* (arXiv:1104.0433), studies graph-power clique complexes, with high-girth collapse theorems; the present graph has four-cycles and is outside that high-girth case.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only finite twisted grids with \(m,n\ge7\), the **vertex** shortest-path metric, and inclusive scale exactly two. No statement is made about the continuous Klein-bottle metric, smaller grid parameters, other Rips thresholds, or complete barcode multiplicities beyond the specified homology group. Finite computational checks are supporting evidence and not infinite certification. Although the closest covering literature was investigated, equivalent unindexed results and more general unpublished theorems cannot be ruled out. Independent audit has not been performed.
