# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof establishes an exhaustive star/square classification by lifting every scale-two clique to the Manhattan plane; the deck displacement bound is uniform for both grid parameters at least seven. It gives five explicit collapse phases with codimension-one, unique surviving coface arguments, followed by a topological Klein-bottle quotient identification. The program `verify_klein_grid.py` independently enumerates all maximal scale-two cliques and replays every collapse for four twisted parameter pairs and four untwisted controls; it also verifies two coefficient-field homology profiles. Only the mathematical local classification, not finite experiments, proves the infinite family.

**Originality — PASS.** The strongest same-type work inspected in full, Adams et al. (arXiv:2502.07134, Sections 4–5), already treats the Manhattan lattice and finite **torus** grids. The local star/square classification is therefore not independently claimed as new. Their orientable torus statement does not classify the glide-reflection quotient or imply nonorientable integral torsion and the exact elementary-collapse sequence. Saleh et al. (arXiv:2302.14388) address specific Platonic graph vertex sets; Adamaszek (arXiv:1104.0433) proves graph-power collapse facts under substantially different graph hypotheses. Targeted searches found no equivalent Klein-grid theorem, but cannot rule out an unindexed or more general result that implies it.

**Value — PASS.** A twisted finite square grid is a canonical combinatorial model of a nonorientable flat surface. It has a different integral homology torsion structure from its orientation-preserving torus companion; the explicit \(9mn\)-pair collapse proves this at a sharp, fixed discrete threshold and all rectangular sizes at least seven. This supplies a natural structural family classification and a reproducible simple-homotopy witness, not an arbitrary numerical instance.

**Scope and residual risks.** Only inclusive scale two, graph vertices, and \(m,n\ge7\) are covered. The proof relies on minimum deck displacement and exact free-face identification, which the reproducibility program checks for eight finite grids. More general unindexed literature may already cover the result. No independent scientific audit was performed.
