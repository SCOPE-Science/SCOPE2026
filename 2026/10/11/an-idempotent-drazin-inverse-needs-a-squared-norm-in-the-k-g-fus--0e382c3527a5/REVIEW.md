# Review

## Correctness
The displayed matrices satisfy the Drazin identities exactly: \(S^2=S\), \(SK=KS=S\), \(K^2=S\) and \(KSK^2=K^2\). The one-component family has coefficient energy \(E(f)=\|K^*f\|^2\). At \(f=(1,0,-1)\), the printed lower coefficient \(1/\|S\|\) would require \(\sqrt2\le1\), which is false. The corrected estimate follows from \(S^*=S^*K^*\) and is attained when \(z=-x\) in this family.

## Originality
The target open-access source was checked at Definition 2.9, Theorem 3.9, and Corollary 3.10. Its general theorem contains a norm-square inequality which specializes to the valid corrected estimate under idempotence, but it does not provide the explicit sharp counterexample to the printed corollary. Comparisons with relevant frame results did not establish the same full counterexample-plus-sharpness statement. A nonindexed correction remains possible.

## Mathematical value
The published corollary is intended to sharpen the Drazin-inverse frame construction under idempotence. Its displayed lower constant fails, whereas the corrected constant \(A/\|S\|^2\) is independent of the power \(r\) and sharp as a universal norm-only guarantee. This resolves the quantitative mistake without disputing the qualitative frame-preservation conclusion.

## Limitations
Particular frames may have better lower bounds. The sharpness is about what can be asserted from the given operator-norm data; no general optimality claim for every frame is made.
