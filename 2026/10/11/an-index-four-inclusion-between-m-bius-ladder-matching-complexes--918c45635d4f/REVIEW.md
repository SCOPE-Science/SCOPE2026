# Review: an index-four inclusion between Möbius-ladder matching complexes

## Correctness — PASS
The exact ten-vertex graph, both complexes, all orientation conventions and the inclusion are explicit. Enumeration yields 344 and 284 faces. The relative chain complex has a single signed 30-by-30 boundary, decomposing into ten unit blocks and two 10-by-10 signed cycles of determinant -2 with 9-by-9 unit minors. Therefore its homology is precisely two order-two classes in degree two. Two complete discrete-Morse checks give the homotopy types, with a four-by-six integral Morse boundary containing a unit four-by-four minor. The long exact sequence then forces the claimed Smith form (2,2).

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Agarwal–Basak arXiv:2608.30601, Theorem 21 determines the perfect subcomplex's homotopy type and Problem 26 calls for studying ordinary matching complexes. It neither computes the ordinary ten-vertex complex nor the natural integral homology map. The 2022 and 2023 polygonal line-tiling papers cover open strings of polygons, not a Möbius ladder formed by a cyclic twisted closure. Indexed mathematical research records concerning different graphs (including the Möbius–Kantor, Heawood, and Desargues graphs) do not imply this map computation. Searches did not identify an exact prior calculation, but they cannot prove global novelty. Closest unpublished or poorly indexed calculations remain a risk.

## Value — PASS
The pair arises naturally from a recent explicit research problem and exhibits a genuine topological distinction that absolute homotopy types miss: two spaces with the same two-sphere wedge type have a canonical inclusion of index four. The signed-cycle decomposition explains the otherwise invisible two-primary obstruction. It is a motivated exact finite boundary example, not a claim to solve the full Möbius-ladder family.

## Limitations
The finding is restricted to this pair of finite simplicial complexes. It makes no all-parameter, equivariant, or higher-dimensional inclusion claim. The complete finite certificates establish correctness of the stated instance, not originality against inaccessible scholarship.
