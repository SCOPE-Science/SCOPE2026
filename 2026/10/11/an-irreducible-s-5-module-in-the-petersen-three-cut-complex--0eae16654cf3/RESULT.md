# An irreducible \(S_5\)-module in the Petersen three-cut complex
## Finding
Let \(P=KG(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph whose vertices are the two-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), adjacent exactly when disjoint. Let \(\Delta_3(P)\) be the ordinary cut complex generated by complements of triples whose induced subgraphs are disconnected. Under the action of \(S_5\) permuting these five labels, the **single nonzero reduced rational homology representation** is
\[
\widetilde H_6(\Delta_3(P);\mathbb Q)\cong \bigwedge^2 V\cong S^{(3,1,1)},
\]
where \(V=\{(t_0,\ldots,t_4)\in\mathbb Q^5:\sum t_i=0\}\) is the standard four-dimensional permutation-augmentation representation and \(S^{(3,1,1)}\) denotes the irreducible Specht module. All other reduced rational homology groups vanish. In particular this is a single irreducible six-dimensional module, not a sum of one-dimensional modules.

The character is given for every \(g\in S_5\) by
\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(g\mid\widetilde H_6\right)=\tfrac12\left((f(g)-1)^2-f(g^2)+1\right),
\]
where \(f(h)\) counts fixed letters of \(h\). For the seven cycle types, in the order identity, transposition, double transposition, three-cycle, disjoint three- and two-cycle, four-cycle, five-cycle, the character values are respectively \(6,0,-2,0,0,0,1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph and the simplicial complex are finite. Each graph vertex is an unordered *pair of letters*, distinct from a particle or a graph edge. A triple \(T\) is disconnected if the **induced** graph \(P[T]\) is not connected. Its complement is a facet, with seven graph vertices and simplicial dimension six. This is the **ordinary** cut complex, not the *total* cut complex, which instead tests independent triples. The group is the natural \(S_5\) action by letter permutations; the claim is over \(\mathbb Q\). No integral isomorphism to an exterior-square lattice or equivariant homotopy classification is claimed.

## Proof
**Complete face poset and homotopy support.** Lexicographically order the ten graph vertices by their underlying two-element letter sets. A triple induces a connected subgraph if and only if it has at least two edges, because the Petersen graph is triangle-free. Of the \(\binom{10}{3}=120\) triples, exactly \(30\) are connected, so the complement of every one of the other \(90\) triples is a facet. Enumerating all subsets of these facets gives the face vector (in dimensions zero through six)
\[
(10,45,120,210,252,210,90),
\]
for \(937\) nonempty faces in total.

Here is a completely specified finite discrete-Morse matching. Begin with all nonempty face masks unmatched. Visit the ten vertices in lexicographic order. At vertex index \(i\), pair each still-unmatched face \(F\) lacking that vertex with \(F\cup\{i\}\), **provided both are still unmatched faces**, then remove the two faces from consideration. Within a stage, visit masks in increasing integer order. The accompanying `verify_petersen.py` reconstructs this matching from first principles and gives \(465\) disjoint pairs, leaving exactly one critical zero-face and six critical six-faces. It generates every one of the \(4440\) covering relations, reverses precisely the selected pair edges in the otherwise downward-oriented Hasse graph, and topologically sorts the resulting graph. The sort visits all \(937\) faces, proving that the directed graph is acyclic. Forman's discrete-Morse theorem therefore gives a CW complex with one zero-cell and six six-cells. All attaching maps are to a point (there are no cells in intermediate dimensions), so \(\Delta_3(P)\simeq\bigvee^6 S^6\). This nonequivariant wedge type is used here only as a homology-concentration input; it is not the new equivariant result.

**Signed equivariant Lefschetz trace.** A letter permutation \(g\in S_5\) permutes the ten pair-vertices and preserves disconnected induced triples and therefore the whole cut complex. For a nonempty face \(F\) fixed as an *unordered set* by \(g\), let \(\operatorname{sgn}(g|_F)\) be the parity of the permutation induced on the ordered vertices of \(F\). The integral simplicial chain map on the oriented basis has signed fixed-face trace, so the (unreduced) Lefschetz number is
\[
L(g)=\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne F\in\Delta_3(P)\\gF=F}}(-1)^{|F|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_F).
\]
The exact checker enumerates each face and all \(120\) permutations, compares transformed bitmasks, computes the inversion parity, and sums these integers. By the homology concentration and the trivial one-dimensional action on \(H_0\), and because degree six is even, \(L(g)=1+\operatorname{tr}(g|\widetilde H_6)\). Its computed seven conjugacy-class values for the latter trace are:

| Cycle type | Class size | Trace |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((1,1,1,1,1)\) | 1 | 6 |
| \((2,1,1,1)\) | 10 | 0 |
| \((2,2,1)\) | 15 | -2 |
| \((3,1,1)\) | 20 | 0 |
| \((3,2)\) | 20 | 0 |
| \((4,1)\) | 30 | 0 |
| \((5)\) | 24 | 1 |

The standard module \(V\) has character \(\chi_V(g)=f(g)-1\), since it is the permutation representation on the five letters minus the invariant line. The elementary identity \(\chi_{\wedge^2 V}(g)=(\chi_V(g)^2-\chi_V(g^2))/2\) gives exactly the same seven character values. Rational representations of a finite group are determined by their complex characters, and here both rational modules have matching complexification, so semisimplicity and the rational form of this Specht representation identify them over \(\mathbb Q\). Finally, the class-size-weighted self-inner product is \((1\cdot6^2+15\cdot(-2)^2+24\cdot1^2)/120=1\), proving irreducibility; this also agrees with the hook-length dimension-six Specht module \(S^{(3,1,1)}\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_petersen.py` with Python 3.8+; it has only standard-library dependencies and exits on a failed assertion. The verified output reports ten graph vertices, fifteen graph edges, ninety facets, 937 nonempty faces, 4440 Hasse covers, 465 matching pairs, critical cells \(\{0:1,6:6\}\), and an acyclic oriented Hasse graph. It tests all 120 distinct letter permutations, every signed fixed face, the character formula from fixed letters, and the class-size inner product. It ends `VERIFY_OK`. The face enumeration is exhaustive since it enumerates all \(1023\) nonempty subsets of the ten vertices; this is a finite computation proving exactly the stated finite instance, not an induction over other graphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue define ordinary cut complexes and prove in Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6 that the related **two-cut** complex of the Petersen graph has a six-sphere wedge type. That result supplies a nonequivariant homology dimension, not this \(S_5\) character on the ordinary three-cut complex. The subsequent *Topology of Cut Complexes II* computes equivariant homology for certain other graph families, rather than for this Petersen construction. Existing nonequivariant descriptions of the ordinary Petersen three-cut complex do not determine whether its dimension-six homology is irreducible: the present signed fixed-face calculation supplies that missing information. The different *total* three-cut complex and configuration-space homology of the same graph must not be conflated with this object.

## Limitations
The result concerns one canonical finite graph and a rational representation, not a general formula for all Kneser graphs. Neither an explicit integral stable lattice decomposition nor a canonical equivariant homotopy equivalence is established. The literature comparison was conducted against the most relevant full-text primary papers and searched public mathematical records, but could miss an equivalent character computation expressed in a different language; absence of a search result is not itself a proof of novelty. Independent external validation has not been performed.
