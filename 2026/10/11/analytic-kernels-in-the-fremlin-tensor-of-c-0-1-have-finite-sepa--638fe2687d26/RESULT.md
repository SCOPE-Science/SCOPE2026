# Analytic kernels in the Fremlin tensor of \(C([0,1])\) have finite separation rank
## Finding
Let \(I=[0,1]\), and let
\[
H=C(I)\mathbin{\bar{\otimes}}C(I)\subset C(I^2)
\]
be the Fremlin tensor product in its canonical realization as the Riesz subspace generated by \(C(I)\otimes C(I)\). If \(h\in H\) is real-analytic on \((0,1)^2\), then \(h\) already lies in the algebraic tensor product \(C(I)\otimes C(I)\). Equivalently, for some integer \(r\ge 0\) there are \(f_1,\ldots,f_r,g_1,\ldots,g_r\in C(I)\) such that
\[
h(s,t)=\sum_{j=1}^r f_j(s)g_j(t)
\qquad ((s,t)\in I^2).
\]
Hence the interior-real-analytic slice of the Fremlin tensor contains no additional kernels beyond the finite-separation-rank algebraic tensor product.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector lattices are real. The interval is \(I=[0,1]\). The Fremlin tensor product is the Archimedean Riesz tensor product, represented inside \(C(I^2)\) as the vector sublattice generated by \(C(I)\otimes C(I)\), exactly as in the focal source. Real-analyticity is required only on the connected open square \((0,1)^2\); continuity on the closed square is automatic from \(h\in C(I^2)\).

The conclusion concerns algebraic separation rank, not norm approximation, projective-tensor membership, nuclearity, or quantitative approximation rates. No claim is made for merely smooth kernels.

## Proof
Put \(V=C(I)\otimes C(I)\). A standard description of the vector sublattice generated by a vector subspace gives every \(h\in H=\operatorname{lat}(V)\) in the form
\[
h=\bigvee_{i=1}^m u_i-\bigvee_{j=1}^n v_j,
\qquad u_i,v_j\in V.
\]
For each point \((s,t)\in I^2\), choose indices attaining the two finite maxima. Then at that point
\[
h(s,t)=w_{ij}(s,t),\qquad w_{ij}:=u_i-v_j\in V.
\]
Thus finitely many closed coincidence sets
\[
C_{ij}=\{(s,t)\in I^2:h(s,t)=w_{ij}(s,t)\}
\]
cover \(I^2\). By the Baire category theorem one of them has nonempty relative interior. Shrinking inside the ordinary interior of the square gives nonempty open intervals \(U,V_0\subset(0,1)\) and a single \(w\in V\) such that
\[
h=w\quad\text{on }U\times V_0.
\]
Write
\[
w(s,t)=\sum_{q=1}^N a_q(s)b_q(t).
\]
Therefore the family of sections
\[
\{h(\cdot,t)|_U:t\in V_0\}
\]
spans a finite-dimensional subspace of \(C(U)\). Let its dimension be \(r\). If \(r=0\), then \(h\) vanishes on \(U\times V_0\), and the real-analytic identity theorem immediately gives \(h=0\) on \((0,1)^2\), hence on \(I^2\) by continuity.

Assume \(r>0\). Choose \(t_1,\ldots,t_r\in V_0\) such that
\[
\phi_j(s):=h(s,t_j)|_U,\qquad 1\le j\le r,
\]
form a basis of the section span. Because \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r\) are linearly independent, there are points \(s_1,\ldots,s_r\in U\) for which the evaluation matrix
\[
M=(h(s_i,t_j))_{1\le i,j\le r}
\]
is invertible. Indeed, if the evaluation vectors \((\phi_1(s),\ldots,\phi_r(s))\) failed to span \(\mathbb R^r\), a nonzero vector orthogonal to their span would give a nontrivial linear relation among the \(\phi_j\).

For \(t\in(0,1)\), define
\[
b(t)=(h(s_1,t),\ldots,h(s_r,t))^T,
\qquad c(t)=M^-1b(t).
\]
Each coordinate \(c_j(t)\) is real-analytic on \((0,1)\) and continuous on \(I\), because it is a fixed linear combination of the continuous, interior-real-analytic functions \(t\mapsto h(s_i,t)\). For \(t\in V_0\), the section \(h(\cdot,t)|_U\) belongs to the span of the \(\phi_j\), and evaluation at the \(s_i\) shows that its unique coefficient vector is precisely \(c(t)\). Hence
\[
R(s,t):=h(s,t)-\sum_{j=1}^r c_j(t)h(s,t_j)
\]
vanishes on \(U\times V_0\).

The function \(R\) is real-analytic on the connected domain \((0,1)^2\): the first term is real-analytic by hypothesis, each \(s\mapsto h(s,t_j)\) is real-analytic, and each \(c_j\) is real-analytic. The real-analytic identity theorem therefore gives \(R\equiv0\) on \((0,1)^2\). Continuity extends the identity to \(I^2\). Taking
\[
f_j(s)=h(s,t_j),\qquad g_j(t)=c_j(t)
\]
produces the required finite separated representation, so \(h\in C(I)\otimes C(I)\). The reverse implication is tautological, completing the classification.

## Verification
The proof uses only four ingredients, each checked directly in the displayed argument: the finite lattice representation of an element of the generated Riesz subspace; Baire category for a finite closed cover of the compact square; the elementary evaluation-matrix lemma for finitely many linearly independent continuous functions; and the identity theorem for real-analytic functions on a connected domain.

The critical boundary issue is explicit: the analytic continuation is performed only on \((0,1)^2\), while the resulting finite-rank formula is extended to \(I^2\) solely by continuity. No numerical experiment, finite enumeration, or approximation argument is used as an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Ben Amor's 2026 counterexample identifies the same realization of \(H\) and proves that the particular analytic kernel \((s,t)\mapsto e^{st}\) does not belong to \(H\). Its Lemma 2.2 supplies the finite-branch/Baire mechanism above and then uses the Wronskian of exponentials to contradict local finite separation rank. The finding here replaces that kernel-specific final step by an analytic-continuation argument and obtains an if-and-only-if classification of the entire interior-real-analytic slice of \(H\).

Fremlin's foundational construction and the Fremlin--Talagrand decomposition theorem provide the broader tensor-product setting. Grobler--Labuschagne study approximation in the Riesz tensor product, while Azouzi--Ben Amor--Jaber and Buskes--Wickstead study algebra structures on tensor products of \(f\)-algebras. Searches under analytic-kernel, finite-separation-rank, local-finite-rank, and algebraic-tensor formulations did not locate a statement implying the classification above. The older decomposition and approximation literature remains the principal residual originality risk because complete text was not available for every comparator inspected.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to the canonical Fremlin tensor of \(C([0,1])\) with itself and to kernels real-analytic on the full connected interior. It does not classify smooth nonanalytic elements, does not estimate minimal separation rank, and does not address completed Banach-lattice tensor norms. The literature comparison cannot exclude an older equivalent formulation under different terminology, especially in decomposition or approximation work on Riesz tensor products.
