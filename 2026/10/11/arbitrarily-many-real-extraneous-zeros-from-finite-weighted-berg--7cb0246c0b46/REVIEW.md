# Review of Arbitrarily many real extraneous zeros from finite weighted-Bergman zero sets

## Correctness
PASS. The focal source gives \(K_\tau(x)=e^{-s_0x}P(x)+O(e^{-bx})\) with \(P\) a nonzero real finite trigonometric sum supported on nonzero frequencies. Its sign lemma applied to \(P\) and \(-P\) gives both signs arbitrarily far out. The smaller remainder transfers those signs to \(K_\tau\). For any finite \(N\), compact-uniform convergence to \(K_h\) and pointwise finite-truncation convergence preserve a finite alternating sign pattern simultaneously. Continuity yields \(N\) distinct zeros, the conformal identity sends them to \((0,1)\), and a polynomial in \(I_A\) that is nonzero on the real axis proves they are extraneous.

## Originality
PASS. The focal source proves one finite extraneous real zero, not arbitrary finite multiplicity. Database and literature searches covered kernel, canonical-divisor, extremal-function, multiple-zero, and wandering-subspace aliases. Gu--Park's theorem is complementary and local, while Hedenmalm--Perdomo is broader background on extraneous zeros. A hidden older equivalent formulation remains a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The number of extraneous zeros is a natural measure of failure of the prescribed-zero extremal problem. Unbounded finite multiplicity shows that the failure beyond \(\alpha=1\) is not a one-zero accident, and it uses the focal construction at its natural oscillatory strength rather than introducing an arbitrary slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2609.36723v1 itself. It contains the analytic ingredients but transfers only one sign change. Gu--Park (2022) gives nearby positive results for zeros in a sufficiently small disk. Hedenmalm--Perdomo (2004) is the principal older extraneous-zero comparison. No effective bound for the required finite truncation is claimed.
