# Scientific review

## Correctness
The right-affine orbit admits an exact trigonometric expression for its first coordinate before a switching hit. Its sign gives the first return to the zero-second-coordinate section, including boundary hits. Rational rotation makes every section point return within the denominator, while irrational rotation has one nonreturning section point and gives a one-sided shrinking-circle-arc test for every other point. Continued-fraction approximation yields an inverse-distance return-time comparison exactly in bounded type; near the tangency this entails an order-1/n Lebesgue tail and divergent mean.

## Relationship to existing mathematics
Sushko and Gardini (2008), Section 3, establish the invariant neutral ellipse and qualitative rational or quasiperiodic center dynamics. Simpson (2025), Section 6.2, examines the same center-bifurcation parameter line, but not the stated one-sided section-return or arithmetic return-time law. Simpson (2026), Theorems 2.3 and 2.6, analyzes finite return partitions for a repelling focus with right determinant strictly greater than one. Classical continued-fraction and irrational-rotation estimates supply the arithmetic ingredients. The distinguishing claim is the exact off-ellipse section-return reduction and its parameter-arithmetic consequences, not novelty of the ellipse or of the arithmetic tools.

## Mathematical significance
A natural neutral parameter boundary supports a unique infinitely nonreturning section point amid finite returns everywhere else. The exact one-sided circle code gives a sharp criterion for uniform inverse-distance return-time growth and a heavy-tailed first-return distribution in the natural finite observation section. This quantifies a dynamically significant arithmetic singularity without claiming a full global attractor classification.

## Scope and residual risks
Only the zero-left-determinant, unit-right-determinant, unit-offset regime with elliptic right trace is treated. The stated order-1/n tail requires bounded-type irrational rotation; no corresponding claim is made for arbitrary unbounded-type angles. An unindexed application of classical shrinking-target estimates to this section or a revised primary source could affect historical priority.
