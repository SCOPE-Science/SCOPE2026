# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The section-return time is reduced exactly to the sign of the right-branch orbit coordinate. Its closed expression gives the rational-versus-irrational dichotomy and the one-sided circular hitting condition, with equality endpoints controlled. Classical convergent-denominator bounds give the inverse-distance comparison exactly for bounded-type angles; a small-divisor sequence gives its converse. The bounded-type tail follows by integrating near the unique tangency. The included finite checker reports 309 diagnostic passes but does not substitute for the infinite proofs.

## Originality
PASS with a residual literature-priority risk. The invariant neutral ellipse itself is **not original**: Sushko and Gardini (2008), Section 3, explicitly derives it and the rational/irrational rotation alternatives. The assessed claim instead concerns the exact section first-return encoding, unique nonreturning section coordinate, sharp badly-approximable characterization, and resulting infinite-mean tail. Simpson (2026) is the most direct return-map precedent but its theorems require a repelling focus with right determinant strictly greater than one; the neutral boundary is outside that scope. The accessible full 2008 paper does not give the claimed arithmetic hitting-time equivalence or tail. The broader Simpson (2025) work could not be read in full; an equivalent older result is possible, and a database search alone is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The focus boundary is dynamically singular: just off the tangent nonreturning point, all trajectories return but the return-time law is controlled by Diophantine quality. The theorem identifies exactly when there are two-sided inverse-distance bounds, and proves a nonintegrable return-time tail for bounded type. This supplies a quantitative boundary counterpart to the finite Farey-return regime, not merely an algebraic reformulation of an invariant ellipse.

## Limits and close sources
Only the neutral right determinant and zero left determinant are treated. No classification of whole-system attractors or unbounded-type tail law is asserted. Closest primary sources: Sushko and Gardini (2008), DOI:10.1142/S0218127408020823 (full author-hosted PDF inspected, neutral ellipse and qualitative quasiperiodicity already known); Simpson (2026), arXiv:2606.08901 (full open-access text inspected, repelling focus); Simpson (2025), DOI:10.1137/24M1683548 (abstract accessible, full text unavailable).
