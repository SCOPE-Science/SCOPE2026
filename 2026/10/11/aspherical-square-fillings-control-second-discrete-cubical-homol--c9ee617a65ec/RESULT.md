# Aspherical square fillings control second discrete cubical homology

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite simple undirected triangle-free graph. Let \(X=CW^\Box(G)\) be the canonical cubical CW completion, with one cell for each rigid cube subgraph, as in Jamil–Behrens. Suppose \(X\) has dimension at most two and \(\pi_2(X)=0\). Then every embedded square of \(G\) is **2-monophobic**, and the second normalized discrete cubical homology satisfies
\[
H_2^{\mathrm{cub}}(G;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
In particular, for every pair of integers \(m,n\ge5\),
\[
H_2^{\mathrm{cub}}(C_m\mathbin\square C_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The canonical generator on the cellular side is the sum of the \(mn\) elementary squares with consistent torus orientation. The monophobicity hypothesis becomes genuinely delicate at length four; in \(C_4\mathbin\square C_5\), an explicit singular three-cube has precisely one face supported by a designated elementary square.

## Assumptions and scope
A graph map may send adjacent vertices to equal or adjacent vertices. A *singular \(j\)-cube* is a graph map \(I^j\to G\). A *rigid square* is an induced subgraph isomorphic to \(C_4\). A square \(Q\) is *2-monophobic* if, for **every** singular \(3\)-cube \(\sigma\) with at least one square face mapped bijectively onto \(Q\), another distinct two-dimensional face of \(I^3\) also maps bijectively onto \(Q\). This is stronger than the 2-quasimonophobic requirement on noninjective three-cubes in Jamil–Behrens. The CW completion is the regular cubical complex constructed from the rigid induced cubes as in the source. Its two-dimensional cells correspond to embedded graph quadrilaterals. If there are no squares, the criterion is vacuous and its conclusion gives zero second homology. The homology groups use integer coefficients throughout.

## Proof
**Step 1: Extend the boundary of a singular three-cube.** Since \(G\) is triangle-free, every closed edge walk of length four, with stationary steps allowed, either traverses an embedded quadrilateral with four distinct vertices or reduces by backtracking to the trivial walk. Indeed, any reduced nontrivial closed walk of length at most four must have length four (a triangle is forbidden), and then has distinct vertices. Given a singular three-cube \(\sigma:I^3\to G\), extend its restriction to the boundary graph of each of the six square faces to a cellular disk in \(X\): map a face spanning a quadrilateral homeomorphically to its square two-cell with the appropriate boundary orientation; if its boundary reduces by backtracking, map that face into the one-skeleton, so its cellular two-chain coefficient is zero. Such fillings exist because a backtracking loop contracts within a graph. The six extensions agree on edges and vertices, yielding a continuous cellular map \(F:\partial[0,1]^3\cong S^2\to X\).

**Step 2: Prove monophobicity without enumerating cubes.** By \(\pi_2(X)=0\), the sphere map \(F\) is nullhomotopic, so the pushforward of the oriented fundamental class of \(S^2\) is zero in \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\). The complex \(X\) has no three-cells, hence \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\subseteq C_2^{\mathrm{cell}}(X;\mathbb Z)\), with no quotient by any cellular three-boundaries. Therefore the cellular two-chain \(F_\#[S^2]\) itself is zero. Its coefficient at any fixed rigid square \(Q\) is the signed sum of contributions from the six cube faces mapped bijectively onto \(Q\); all other faces contribute zero at \(Q\). If one face is bijectively supported on \(Q\), it contributes \(+1\) or \(-1\), so some **other** face must also be bijectively supported on \(Q\) in order to cancel. Thus every rigid square is 2-monophobic. Since the graph is triangle-free, every graph-theoretic 4-cycle is induced, as required for a rigid square.

**Step 3: Transfer to discrete cubical homology.** In the notation of Jamil–Behrens, any triangle-free graph is 1-quasimonophobic (their Definition 1, accompanying remarks). Step 2 proves 2-monophobicity, hence 2-quasimonophobicity. Their Theorem 7, proved via the degree spectral sequence and oriented cube relations, gives the integral isomorphism \(H_2^{\mathrm{cub}}(G;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(CW^\Box(G);\mathbb Z)\). This application depends on the published general comparison theorem; it is not an independent proof of that theorem.

**Step 4: Toroidal grids.** For \(m,n\ge5\), every embedded quadrilateral in the Cartesian graph \(C_m\mathbin\square C_n\) is an elementary product square: an orthogonal pair of coordinate moves closes in four steps, whereas a nontrivial winding in either factor needs at least five steps. There is no embedded three-cube, since at each vertex the local four-edge coordinate arrangement has no three mutually independent commuting directions; equivalently all squares are coordinate faces in a two-dimensional product graph. Therefore \(X\) is precisely the standard \(m\)-by-\(n\) square tiling of the topological torus, with \(\pi_2(X)=0\). Its cellular two-boundary kernel consists of the integral multiples of the coherently oriented sum of all squares: each edge belongs to exactly two squares with opposite oriented incidences, and the dual adjacency graph of squares is connected. This proves the stated \(\mathbb Z\) in degree two. A length-four factor introduces winding four-cycles and embedded three-cubes; the torus-completion identification and the monophobicity argument do not extend to that boundary.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and its standard library only. It enumerates every singular three-cube extending a fixed elementary face for five toroidal grids with dimensions between five and seven; all 245 enumerated maps have at least two faces supported by that face. It independently enumerates all four-cycles and verifies they are precisely elementary squares, and checks the cellular orientation/incidence relations and connectivity of square dual graphs. It also finds local counterexamples for \(C_4\mathbin\square C_5\) and \(C_4\mathbin\square C_6\). These finite checks corroborate the general topological proof; they cannot establish statements for all graph sizes.

## Relationship to prior work
Jamil and Behrens (arXiv:2605.03894, first publicly posted 2026-05-05) introduce quasimonophobicity and prove that 1- and 2-quasimonophobic graphs have second discrete cubical homology equal to the second homology of their cubical CW completion (Theorem 7). The new step here is the *asphericity-to-monophobicity implication*, which replaces the combinatorial face-by-face verification by the vanishing of \(\pi_2\) of a two-dimensional completion. The uniform toroidal-grid calculation is a direct but meaningful application of that criterion. Barcelo–Greene–Jarrah–Welker (2019) develop the original cubical/path comparison and general graph examples; their accessible results do not directly state this asphericity criterion. Their 2021 vanishing theorem instead covers graphs without triangles or squares, so it cannot subsume the grid calculation with many squares.

## Limitations
No assertion is made about cubical homology in degrees greater than two, non-triangle-free graphs, complexes containing three-dimensional cubes, or graphs with nonzero \(\pi_2\) in the square completion. Asphericity is a sufficient criterion, not a necessary one; the Greene sphere examples already illustrate non-aspherical graphs with monophobic square behavior. The boundary examples with four-cycle factors demonstrate a failure of the **monophobicity criterion**, not a computation of their second cubical homology. The proof invokes Jamil–Behrens for the final integral homology comparison. Its originality is subject to the possibility of equivalent unindexed literature, including unpublished foundational notes on monophobic cube neighborhoods.
