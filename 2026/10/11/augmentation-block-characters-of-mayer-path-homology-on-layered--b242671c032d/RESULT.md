# Augmentation-block characters of Mayer path homology on layered digraphs

## Finding

Consider a complete, transitively oriented, multipartite directed graph whose nonempty levels are \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\), with every arrow directed from the smaller to the larger level index and no arrows within a level. For every \(N\ge2\), every primitive \(N\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\in\mathbb C\), and every \(1\le q<N\), its Mayer path homology admits a complete, level-wise equivariant decomposition into standard symmetric-group representations. In particular, the homology characters are **symmetric in the layer data even though the digraph carries a specified total order of its levels**.

Define \(n_i=|A_i|\ge1\), \(G=\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{n_i}\), and the standard augmentation representation
\[
W_i=\ker\left(\varepsilon_i:\mathbb C[A_i]\longrightarrow\mathbb C\right),\qquad \varepsilon_i\left(\sum_{v\in A_i}c_vv\right)=\sum_{v\in A_i}c_v.
\]
Let \(\mathsf B(s,t)\), for \(0\le s\le r\), \(t=r-s\), be the finite graded \(N\)-complex with a basis \(e_J\) for each \(J\subseteq[t]\), placed in degree \(|J|+s-1\), except that \(e_\varnothing\) is omitted when \(s=0\). Its boundary, on nonempty \(J=\{j_0<\cdots<j_{\ell-1}\}\), is
\[
d(e_J)=\sum_{a=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta^a e_{J\setminus\{j_a\}},
\]
with terms omitted if the target basis element does not exist. The empty-basis boundary is zero. Thus \(d^N=0\). Define the **explicit universal multiplicities**
\[
h_{s,k}^{N,q}(r)=\dim\ker\left(d^q:\mathsf B(s,r-s)_k\to\mathsf B(s,r-s)_{k-q}\right)
-\operatorname{rank}\left(d^{N-q}:\mathsf B(s,r-s)_{k+N-q}\to\mathsf B(s,r-s)_k\right).
\]
Each is computable exactly from zero-one-indexed matrices with at most \(\binom{r-s}{j}\) columns in each degree and is independent of the layer sizes.

**Complete representation and character formulas:** for every \(k\ge0\),
\[
H_k^{N,q}(D;\mathbb C)\cong
\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[r]}
\left(\bigotimes_{i\in S}W_i\right)^{\oplus h_{|S|,k}^{N,q}(r)}
\]
as \(G\)-representations. For \(g=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\in G\), write \(f_i=|\mathrm{Fix}_{A_i}(g_i)|\), and let \(e_s\) denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(s\), including \(e_0=1\). Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(g\mid H_k^{N,q}(D;\mathbb C)\right)
=\sum_{s=0}^{r}h_{s,k}^{N,q}(r)\,e_s(f_1-1,\ldots,f_r-1).
\]
In particular, both dimensions and the complete character polynomial are invariant under reordering the layer data (after identifying the corresponding product-group factors). This does not assert an isomorphism of the differently oriented digraphs.

**Closed three-layer computation.** For \(r=N=3\) define \(V=n_1+n_2+n_3\), \(E=n_1n_2+n_1n_3+n_2n_3\), \(F=n_1n_2n_3\), \(T=\prod_{i=1}^{3}(n_i-1)\). Then the complete nonzero degree-by-degree dimensions are
\[
\big(\dim H_0^{3,1},\dim H_1^{3,1},\dim H_2^{3,1}\big)=(2,E-V,T),
\qquad
\big(\dim H_0^{3,2},\dim H_1^{3,2},\dim H_2^{3,2}\big)=(0,V-1,F-V+2).
\]
These dimensions are nonnegative for every \(n_i\ge1\); degrees beyond \(2\) vanish. More precisely, writing \(\mathbf1\) for the trivial representation, the four potentially nonzero higher group modules, along with the degree-zero groups, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_0^{3,1}&\cong\mathbf1^{\oplus2},\\
H_1^{3,1}&\cong\bigoplus_iW_i\ \oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}W_i\otimes W_j,\\
H_2^{3,1}&\cong W_1\otimes W_2\otimes W_3,\\
H_0^{3,2}&=0,\\
H_1^{3,2}&\cong\mathbf1^{\oplus2}\oplus\bigoplus_iW_i,\\
H_2^{3,2}&\cong\bigoplus_{i<j}W_i\otimes W_j\oplus W_1\otimes W_2\otimes W_3.
\end{aligned}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All levels are finite and nonempty; \(r\ge2\). The coefficient field is \(\mathbb C\), the root \(\zeta\) is primitive of order \(N\), and the Mayer index lies in \(1\le q\le N-1\). This is **unreduced Mayer path homology**, with nonregular path boundary (so singleton paths have zero boundary). Vertices of the directed graph are disjointly labeled by their layers. Chains in degree \(k\) are generated by all directed paths of \(k\) edges. Completeness between ordered layers means that every deletion of a vertex from an allowed directed path leaves an allowed directed path, so the invariant path subcomplex equals the entire allowed path chain space.

## Proof

**1. Identify the chain complex and its equivariance.** Every directed \(k\)-path has an increasing sequence of \(k+1\) distinct layer indices. Hence
\[
C_k(D;\mathbb C)=\bigoplus_{I\subseteq[r],\ |I|=k+1}\ \bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathbb C[A_i],\quad 0\le k\le r-1.
\]
For a tensor \(v_{i_0}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i_k}\) in increasing layer order,
\[
d(v_{i_0}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i_k})
=\sum_{a=0}^{k}\zeta^a\varepsilon_{i_a}(v_{i_a})
(v_{i_0}\otimes\cdots\widehat{v_{i_a}}\cdots\otimes v_{i_k})
\]
for \(k\ge1\); define \(d(C_0)=0\). This is precisely the nonregular Mayer path boundary on this transitive digraph. The standard root-of-unity deletion identity gives \(d^N=0\); alternatively, the coefficient of any ordered deletion of \(N\) distinct entries is a scalar multiple of the vanishing \(N\)-factorial \([N]_{\zeta}!=\prod_{a=1}^{N}(1+\zeta+\cdots+\zeta^{a-1})=0\). The boundary commutes with \(G\).

**2. Split the individual permutation modules.** Let
\[
u_i=\frac1{n_i}\sum_{v\in A_i}v,
\qquad\mathbb C[A_i]=\mathbb C u_i\oplus W_i,
\qquad\varepsilon_i(u_i)=1,
\qquad\varepsilon_i(W_i)=0.
\]
Both summands are \(S_{n_i}\)-stable. In every tensor chain, choose the \(W_i\) summand at exactly the fixed subset \(S\subseteq[r]\), and the \(\mathbb Cu_i\) summand at the other included layers. Deleting a \(W_i\)-slot contributes zero, so these fixed-subset blocks never mix under \(d\). Therefore the entire \(N\)-chain complex splits \(G\)-equivariantly as
\[
C_\bullet(D)\cong\bigoplus_{S\subseteq[r]}
\left(\bigotimes_{i\in S}W_i\right)\otimes T_\bullet(S),
\]
where \(T_k(S)\) has basis \(e_I\) for \(S\subseteq I\subseteq[r]\), \(|I|=k+1\), and its differential deletes only indices in \(I\setminus S\), with the original powers of \(\zeta\) determined by the deleted index's position in the full increasing list. The tensor representations have zero differential and the \(G\)-action on \(T(S)\) is trivial.

**3. Prove that the numerical block depends only on the cardinality of its fixed set.** Write \(s=|S|\), \(J=I\setminus S\). For any optional index \(j\in[r]\setminus S\), put \(a_j=\zeta^{|\{i\in S:i<j\}|}\). When deleting \(j\) from \(I=S\cup J\), its old position equals its position among optional indices plus \(|\{i\in S:i<j\}|\). Rescale the vector \(e_{S\cup J}\) by \(\prod_{j\in J}a_j^{-1}\). After rescaling, deleting the \(p\)-th optional vertex has exactly coefficient \(\zeta^p\), with no dependence on the locations of \(S\). Thus \(T(S)\cong\mathsf B(s,r-s)\) as \(N\)-complexes, uniformly for all \(S\) of size \(s\). When \(s=0\), the empty optional subset is not an original path and must be omitted; when \(s\ge1\), its basis vector corresponds to the surviving fixed-set path in degree \(s-1\). These edge cases are precisely incorporated into the definition of \(\mathsf B\).

**4. Pass to Mayer homology and trace.** Direct sums and tensor products with degree-zero vector spaces commute with kernels and images of each power of \(d\). Since \(d^N=0\), the image of \(d^{N-q}\) lies in the kernel of \(d^q\). The resulting quotient gives the stated exact \(G\)-equivariant decomposition. Each standard module \(W_i\) has character \(f_i-1\): it is the permutation module of character \(f_i\) minus one trivial summand. Multiplying tensor characters and summing over \(|S|=s\) yields the elementary-symmetric character formula. The dimension formula follows by replacing \(\dim W_i=n_i-1\), and the symmetric dependence on layer data is immediate.

**5. Compute all three-layer cubic-root blocks exactly.** With \(r=N=3\), the ranks of the small universal matrices give the following complete list of multiplicity vectors in degrees \(0,1,2\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
s& (h_{s,k}^{3,1})_{k=0}^{2}&(h_{s,k}^{3,2})_{k=0}^{2}\\\hline
0&(2,0,0)&(0,2,0)\\
1&(0,1,0)&(0,1,0)\\
2&(0,1,0)&(0,0,1)\\
3&(0,0,1)&(0,0,1)
\end{array}
\]
For completeness, the nontrivial \(s=0\) block has three vertices, three edges, and one face. Its degree-one matrix has columns \(\zeta e_1+e_2\), \(\zeta e_1+e_3\), and \(\zeta e_2+e_3\) and determinant \(-\zeta(1+\zeta)=1\); the face map and its composite with the edge map both have rank one. At \(s=1\), the block has dimensions \((1,2,1)\), and the edge map, face map, and their composite each have rank one (the composite's coefficient is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(1+\zeta\)). At \(s=2\), the block has dimensions \((0,1,1)\), with a rank-one face map; at \(s=3\), only one degree-two generator survives. Substitution into the general formula gives exactly the six stated module decompositions. Their dimensions simplify using \(\dim W_i=n_i-1\) to the displayed closed formulas. No enumeration bound enters this proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Karaguler and Wei (first public May 15, 2026) introduced Mayer path homology, established the nonregular \(N\)-boundary and path-invariance definitions, and classified low-dimensional invariant path generators and first-cycle conditions on general digraphs. Their inspected paper does not state the complete layered-graph representation, subset-size universality, or three-level formulas. Shen, Liu, and Wei (2023) developed Mayer homology and Laplacians for simplicial complexes, supplying the broader \(N\)-complex setting; the inspected work does not present the equivariant graph-family calculation. Ordinary homology of weak-order/complete-level posets, including indexed existing results, concerns a different differential and does not determine the nonclassical Mayer groups.

The present result specializes to standard unreduced homology when \(N=2\), but for \(N=3\) exhibits genuinely different degree-zero and middle-degree homology characters. Targeted indexed and published searches did not reveal the same family theorem; this absence alone is not a proof of worldwide novelty. Equivalence to an unindexed general-purpose \(q\)-Künneth computation remains a residual literature risk.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only complete **transitively directed** multipartite graphs, finite nonempty levels, and complex coefficients at a primitive root of unity. It does not assert a homotopy invariant of the underlying undirected multipartite graph, an integral decomposition, or a classification of arbitrary nontransitive directed graphs. The universal matrices give an exact finite algorithm for all \(r,N,q\) rather than a simple closed numerical formula in every degree for arbitrary \(r\). The specific \(r=N=3\) formulas are complete. The finding has undergone same-model mathematical review only; no independent validation is asserted.
