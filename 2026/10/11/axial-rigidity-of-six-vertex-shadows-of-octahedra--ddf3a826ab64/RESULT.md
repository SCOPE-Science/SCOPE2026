# Axial rigidity of six-vertex shadows of octahedra

## Finding
For each \(\lambda>0\), put \(K_\lambda=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm\lambda e_3\}\subset\mathbb R^3\). A uniformly random two-dimensional orthogonal projection has, almost surely, either four or six vertices. Set \(h=\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\) and \(a=\sqrt2-1\). Its exact four-vertex probability is
\[
 p_4(\lambda)=\frac4\pi\left[2\arctan\!\left(\frac{a\lambda}{1+h}\right)+\arctan\!\left((h-\lambda)^2\right)\right],\qquad p_6(\lambda)=1-p_4(\lambda).
\]
The function \(p_4\) strictly decreases on \((0,1)\), strictly increases on \((1,\infty)\), and tends to one at both ends. Equivalently, \(p_6\) has its unique global maximum within this entire axially scaled family at the regular octahedron \(\lambda=1\):
\[
 p_6(\lambda)\le p_6(1)=\frac2\pi\left(3\operatorname{arcsec}3-\pi\right)=0.3509593121\ldots,
\]
with equality exactly for \(\lambda=1\). The regular-case value is previously known; the claim here is the complete deformation law and its strict uniqueness.

For completeness, this follows from an exact finite solid-angle expression for every affine octahedron \(K_A=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm a_1,\pm a_2,\pm a_3\}\), where \(A=[a_1\ a_2\ a_3]\in\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb R)\):
\[
 \mathbb P_A(N=4)=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{s,t\in\{-1,1\}}\Omega\bigl(a_i,\,a_i+s a_j,\,a_i+t a_k\bigr),
\]
where for each index \(i\), the other two indices \((j,k)\) may be taken in increasing order, and \(\Omega\) is the spherical solid angle of the positive cone generated by the displayed three vectors. This general expression is a proof device, not a claim of a globally extremal regular octahedron among all affine images.

## Assumptions and scope
The random projection plane is \(U^\perp\), with \(U\) uniform on the unit sphere \(S^2\) under normalized area measure. The six vertices \(\pm a_i\) are assumed noncoplanar through \(\det A\ne0\). Events where projected vertices coincide or lie on a projected hull edge have spherical measure zero. All probabilities refer to genuinely **orthogonal** projections in the fixed Euclidean metric, not arbitrary affine projection directions with an affine-adjusted sampling law.

## Proof
**1. A finite cone partition for every affine octahedron.** Let \(P_U=I-UU^{\mathsf T}\), and set \(c=A^{-1}U\). Then \(\sum_{i=1}^3c_iP_Ua_i=0\). For generic \(U\), the four points \(\{\pm P_Ua_j,\pm P_Ua_k\}\) form a parallelogram, while \(P_Ua_i=-\frac{c_j}{c_i}P_Ua_j-\frac{c_k}{c_i}P_Ua_k\). Therefore the pair \(\pm P_Ua_i\) lies strictly inside that parallelogram exactly when \(\lvert c_i\rvert>\lvert c_j\rvert+\lvert c_k\rvert\). Such strict dominance can hold for at most one index. The six projected input points thus yield four vertices exactly on the three disjoint, antipodally symmetric dominance events, and six otherwise.

For a fixed positive-dominance event \(c_i>\lvert c_j\rvert+\lvert c_k\rvert\), subdivide by \(s=\operatorname{sgn}c_j\) and \(t=\operatorname{sgn}c_k\). The new coordinates \(z_1=c_i-s c_j-t c_k>0\), \(z_2=\lvert c_j\rvert>0\), \(z_3=\lvert c_k\rvert>0\) give \(U=z_1a_i+z_2(a_i+s a_j)+z_3(a_i+t a_k)\). This is a disjoint union, except for null boundaries, of four trihedral cones. Pairing each with its negative, dividing their combined spherical area by \(4\pi\), and summing gives the stated \(1/(2\pi)\) solid-angle formula.

For linearly independent \(p,q,r\), the (unoriented) solid angle used above can be evaluated without spherical integration by
\[
 \Omega(p,q,r)=2\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(\lvert\det(p,q,r)\rvert,\,\lVert p\rVert\lVert q\rVert\lVert r\rVert +(p\cdot q)\lVert r\rVert+(p\cdot r)\lVert q\rVert+(q\cdot r)\lVert p\rVert\right),
\]
with \(\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)\in(0,\pi)\) for \(y>0\). This is the standard trihedral solid-angle identity; its use is not asserted as new.

**2. Axial evaluation.** For \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\lambda)\), all four positive-dominance cones for either of the first two indices have the same solid angle
\[
 \Omega_x=2\arctan\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{(1+\sqrt2)(1+h)}\right)=2\arctan\!\left(\frac{a\lambda}{1+h}\right).
\]
Their generating-vector lengths are \(1,\sqrt2,h\), their absolute determinant is \(\lambda\), and all off-diagonal dot products are one. For the third index, each of its four cones has angle
\[
 \Omega_z=2\arctan\!\left(\frac1{(h+\lambda)^2}\right)=2\arctan\!\left((h-\lambda)^2\right),
\]
using \((h-\lambda)(h+\lambda)=1\). Consequently \(p_4=(4\Omega_x+2\Omega_z)/\pi\), which reduces exactly to the displayed formula.

**3. Strict monotonicity.** Put \(t=\operatorname{arsinh}\lambda\), \(u=\tanh(t/2)=\lambda/(1+h)\in(0,1)\), and retain \(a=\sqrt2-1\). Since \(h-\lambda=e^{-t}\), differentiation yields
\[
 \frac\pi4\frac{d p_4}{dt}=\frac{a(1-u^2)}{1+a^2u^2}-\frac1{\cosh(2t)}
 =\frac{(1-u^2)(2-\sqrt2)(u^2-a^2)(u^2+3+2\sqrt2)}{(1+a^2u^2)(1+6u^2+u^4)}.
\]
Every factor other than \(u^2-a^2\) is positive. Because \(u\) strictly increases through \(a\) exactly at \(\lambda=1\), the derivative is negative before \(1\) and positive after \(1\). As \(\lambda\downarrow0\), the two \(\Omega_x\) terms vanish and \(\arctan((h-\lambda)^2)\to\pi/4\), giving \(p_4\to1\). As \(\lambda\to\infty\), \(u\to1\), so \(2\arctan(a)\to\pi/4\) and the other arctangent vanishes, again giving \(p_4\to1\). Finally \(p_4(1)=12\arctan(3-2\sqrt2)/\pi=3(\pi-2\operatorname{arcsec}3)/\pi\), the previously published regular-octahedron probability.

## Verification
The included `verify_octahedron.py` independently evaluates the twelve cones and their sum, checks the symbolic derivative factorization, compares the axial closed formula at eleven different positive axial scales, and compares the cone probability to seeded direction samples for eight invertible matrices. It also computes projected six-point convex hulls for 960 independently selected orientations, checking the dominance classification without reusing the cone test. The recorded execution in `verification_output.txt` ends in `VERIFY_OK` and the largest absolute closed-form disagreement was \(3.33\times10^{-16}\). These finite numerical tests are not proofs of strict monotonicity or the formula for all matrices; those follow from the analytic arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Finch's *Planar Projections and Second Intrinsic Volume* (2012), Section 2.3, explicitly gives the four- versus six-vertex shadow probabilities for the **regular** octahedron. Böröczky Jr. and Henk's *Random Projections of Regular Polytopes* (1997), especially Sections 1, 3, and 4, studies face-number expectations and the full-vertex probability for regular crosspolytopes; its dimension-three formula also covers the regular octahedron. The present claim is **not** this regular-case number or the existence of a spherical cone formula. It is the full one-parameter deformation law with its unique strict optimizer. Generic random-polytope silhouette results concern a different probability space, with random input vertices rather than a fixed octahedron and a random viewing direction. No novelty claim is made for general angle-sum principles that may imply the all-affine auxiliary formula after additional evaluation.

## Limitations
No claim is made that the regular octahedron maximizes hexagonal-shadow probability among *all* affine octahedra: the uniqueness statement applies only to \(\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm\lambda e_3\}\) for \(\lambda>0\). No exact conditional shadow-area moments are claimed. The result has not undergone independent validation. Historical unindexed treatments of stretched-octahedron shadows or a dominating general shadow theorem remain possible and are not ruled out by unsuccessful searches.
