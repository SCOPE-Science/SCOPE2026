# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. Barycentric coordinates turn the two endpoint constraints in the Minkowski subtraction into lower bounds on every barycentric coordinate, so the eroded set is exactly a translate of \(S\) with ratio \(1-(W/2)\sum_i|\langle g_i,u\rangle|\). The remaining identity is proved by showing that the polar of \(S-S\) is exactly the zonotope generated by the barycentric gradients, equivalently the convex hull of their subset sums. Its Euclidean circumradius is \(1/w(S)\), which gives the sharp coefficient \(2/w(S)\). Equality in the subset-sum and Cauchy--Schwarz steps yields the complete minimizing-direction classification. No finite enumeration is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS. The focal preprint proves the general plank-family reduction, the homothetic inclusion, and single-plank optimality only for a special simplex class having a facet altitude equal to minimum width. Its full simplex discussion does not state that the homothetic inclusion is directionally volume-sharp for every simplex, does not give the barycentric-gradient erosion formula, and does not classify the minimizing directions by complementary faces. Targeted statement-level searches under Minkowski subtraction, erosion, barycentric gradients, complementary faces, and width found no equivalent or stronger statement. The closest published finding computes minimum width for convex pyramids and an altitude criterion; it does not imply the new erosion identity.

## Value

PASS. The source's main reduction leaves a directional Minkowski-subtraction minimum as the geometric quantity controlling every finite plank family. The theorem evaluates that quantity exactly for the entire simplex class and identifies every extremal direction. In particular it shows that regular simplices in dimensions \(d\ge3\), although excluded from the source's facet-altitude attainment class, already make the Bang homothetic lower bound sharp at the erosion stage. This isolates precisely where additional geometry is needed to settle the remaining single-plank problem.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary source is Bakaev--Polyanskii, arXiv:2607.27483v1. Corollary 7 reduces plank families to a directional erosion minimum; Lemma 8 places a homothetic core in every such erosion; Section 5 obtains a single-plank optimum when a facet altitude equals minimum width. The present theorem proves exactness of that core after directional optimization for every simplex, without asserting single-plank attainment.

The closest published overlap record is the exact minimum-width formula for convex pyramids. Its invariant is minimum width itself, not centered-segment Minkowski subtraction. An unindexed elementary equivalent in older convex-geometry literature remains a residual originality risk.
