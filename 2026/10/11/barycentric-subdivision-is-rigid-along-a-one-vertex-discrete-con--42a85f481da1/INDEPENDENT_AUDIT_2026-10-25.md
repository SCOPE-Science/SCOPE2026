# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For any t>−log 4, set z=e^t>1/4 and embed the scaled isosceles triangle with base endpoints (±1/2,0) and height h=sqrt(z−1/4). Its original side lengths are (e^(t/2), e^(t/2), 1), so it lies on the stated one-vertex discrete-conformal ray. Equal ratios on both halves of each original side force U_1=U_2=U_3=c and midpoint factors t−c,t−c,−c. For edges from the centroid to m12 and m23, the squared old distances agree at z=1; the new squared distances are 1/16+h²/36 and h²/9. Thus a necessary scaling equation is z=(z+2)/(4z−1), or (2z+1)(z−1)=0. In z>1/4 only z=1 remains, i.e. t=0; at t=0 all subdivided lengths coincide and zero new vertex factors suffice. Exact symbolic reconstruction confirmed this factorization and admissible boundary. No finite test is used as an infinite proof; no assertion is made about alternative subdivision metrics.

## value

PASS

Bauer 2015 explicitly singles out barycentric subdivision as a natural metric-subdivision example while asking if any scheme can preserve vertex-scaling equivalence. The construction shows that the canonical Euclidean midpoint/centroid realization fails already on the smallest two-dimensional simplicial domain and gives a complete iff classification along a naturally motivated one-vertex ray. It is a substantive structural obstruction to that candidate, not an arbitrary slice; its utility is to rule out using that elementary construction as a universally preserving scheme. It is not claimed to solve the broader existence question or other subdivision metric assignments.
