# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For each of the 84 reduced-residue pairs \(7\le k\le17\), the reindexed coefficient identity is derived from the published equation (3.21), and the dependence on the unbounded quotient parameter \(q\) has degree at most three by summing quadratic index polynomials over affine block endpoints. Consequently four exact coefficient evaluations suffice to establish a polynomial identity in \(q\), not just an experiment at four inputs. Direct exact-rational checks establish the cubic and quadratic Fejér coefficients, and strict Bernstein lower bounds for the remaining two polynomials on every dyadic cell. This proves the strict angular inequality for all \(q\ge0\), then root counts follow from reciprocity and kernel zeros from the published geometric realization. The checker was executed successfully for all 84 cases.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The full open-access Edholm–Mathew article (DOI:10.1007/s00209-026-04059-8, especially Sections 3.3 and 4.3 and Conjecture 4.20) establishes the general numerator and handles the differences one and two, mentioning separately treated differences three, four and six and an in-preparation infinite-subfamily result. None covers all coprime pairs simultaneously for each difference from seven through seventeen, nor the exact 84-family Bernstein verification. Searches also targeted equivalent phase-normalized trigonometric positivity, reciprocal-polynomial zero location, and the specific Hartogs zero problem. Exact comparison with an unindexed future work of the same authors is still unavailable, and older rational-kernel papers have not all been read in full. Neither absence of matching search hits nor partitioning establishes global novelty.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural 11-gap consecutive finite theorem in the published open Lu Qi-Keng classification program. The cutoff reaches the first reduced-residue multiplicity \(\varphi(k)=16\) at \(k=17\), well beyond isolated adjacent cases, while exhibiting a common cubic-in-parameter decomposition with a nonnegative Fejér factor. The theorem uniformly settles infinitely many coprime parameters in each covered gap, not merely a numerical sample. It does not prove the conjecture for every gap, and the proof is partly computational rather than fully uniform in \(k\).
