# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. On every ordered smooth sector the unique norming functional gives an exact Birkhoff--James orthogonality test. For \(x_t=(1,t)\), the constructed direction \(y_t=(\omega t^{q-1},-1)\) is Birkhoff orthogonal, and the reverse relation reduces exactly to \(\omega^q t^{q(q-2)}=1\). The three exponent regimes are exhaustive. Every non-Euclidean parameter therefore has an explicit one-way orthogonal pair, while \((\omega,q)=(1,2)\) is Euclidean and hence Radon.

## Originality
PASS with explicit bibliographic risks. Searches covered the Lorentz notation \(d^{(2)}(\omega,q)\), the max/min norm formula, Radon-plane terminology, symmetric Birkhoff--James orthogonality, and normality aliases. The classical same-object sources found compute James and Jordan--von Neumann constants, while the closest modern Radon theorem is a general generalized-Day--James characterization. No inspected statement gives the Lorentz reciprocity equation or the iff classification. The 2001 same-object full text and the 2020 general theorem were not both available for complete inspection, so possible hidden overlap is retained as a residual risk rather than suppressed.

## Value
PASS. The result is a complete structural classification, not a single numerical invariant: among the full smooth two-dimensional Lorentz family, it isolates the Euclidean norm as the unique Radon member and describes exactly where reciprocal Birkhoff pairs occur in the generic sector. This directly links a classical sequence-space family with a central symmetry notion in Minkowski geometry.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kato--Maligranda (2001) and Suzuki--Yamano--Kato (2006) are the closest Lorentz-family sources. Komuro--Saito--Tanaka (2020) is the closest broader Radon-plane source. The theorem excludes \(q=1\) and \(q=\infty\), and it does not enumerate reciprocal pairs located on nonsmooth seams.
