# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the equivariant problem to a signed fixed-simplex count. For a fixed simplex, the orientation sign and chain-degree sign collapse to a sign determined by the number of edge orbits. The invariant-matching sum factors over blade cycles, with a separate term for the unique possible center incident edge. The recurrence \(a_r=a_{r-1}-a_{r-2}\) yields the required period-six values. Substitution into the reduced Lefschetz formula produces the stated characters, and the published homotopy theorem supplies the single homology degree. Exhaustive checks for \(n=2,3\) and \(m=4,5,7,8\) agree for every blade permutation.

## Originality
PASS. Donovan--Scoville Theorem 5.8 gives only the nonequivariant homotopy types. Its statement and surrounding proof were inspected in full at the Dutch-windmill theorem. Exact searches using both Dutch-windmill and friendship-graph terminology found no blade-permutation homology character. General symmetric-group results for complete-graph or complete-hypergraph matching complexes concern different objects and do not imply this representation.

## Value
PASS. The action by permuting identical blades is canonical, and the result upgrades an infinite-family wedge calculation to an explicit equivariant invariant. The parity dependence on \(k\) and the decomposition into a sign twist of \(\mathbf1\oplus2\,\mathrm{Std}_n\) give structural information that is invisible to the Betti number \(2n-1\) alone and can be reused in equivariant constructions or quotient calculations.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Donovan--Scoville, arXiv:2207.13780 / New York J. Math. 29 (2023), Theorem 5.8. Dong--Wachs and Bate--Everitt--Ford--Ramos provide representation-theoretic context for other matching-complex families. The present result does not determine the full automorphism-group action or an integral lattice decomposition; obscure unindexed treatment under alternate cactus terminology remains a residual originality risk.
