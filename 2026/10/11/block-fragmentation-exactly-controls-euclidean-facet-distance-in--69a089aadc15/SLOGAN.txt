Every genuine Chapoton–Pilaud \((m,n)\)-multiplihedron has the same Euclidean inradius as the ordinary permutahedron on \(m+n\) coordinates, namely \(\frac12\sqrt{(m+n)(m+n-1)}\), with a unique uniform incenter. More precisely, splitting the associahedral coordinate set into several blocks adds an exact positive fragmentation penalty to the corresponding center-to-facet distance.
