# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The complete facet system is reconstructed from the primary independent-block inequality theorem: a block path permits precisely the alternating \(+1,-1,\ldots,+1\) sign vectors in addition to nonnegative coordinate facets. Two explicit upper facets have normals adding to the two endpoint coordinate vectors. Combining their ball-clearance inequalities with endpoint lower-facet clearances yields a sharp global radius bound in both parities. Explicit feasible center vectors attain the bound. Every optimal center satisfies three independent linear equations; strict-feasibility perturbations within that affine space establish the exact \(n-3\) dimension of the center locus. An independent convex-hull and LP replay checked small dimensions, but the proof is arbitrary-dimensional.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the stated residual literature risk. The principal connected-block paper's full open-access text was inspected at its facet theorem, independent-block definition, block-path specialization, and Narayana-polynomial result; it does not state Euclidean inradii or classify centers. The earlier connected-subgraph paper supplies tree facet characterizations but no Euclidean ball theorem. A third full-text paper studies the closely related nonzero-only interval-vector hull, not the zero-inclusive body, and has no radius theorem. Published-result searches under connected-block, connected-subgraph, interval-indicator, interval-vector, consecutive-one, root-polytope, Chebyshev-center, and inradius terminology located no implication-equivalent result. A unimodular root-polytope equivalence is not metric preserving.

## Value
PASS. The block-path polytope is a canonical connected-subgraph lattice polytope with Narayana \(h^*\)-data. The theorem resolves the complete Euclidean inradius in every dimension, exposes a parity-dependent radical expression, and shows a structurally nonunique incenter locus of codimension three rather than isolated exceptional centers. These conclusions complement the existing facet, optimization, and Ehrhart classifications and give an intrinsic metric invariant not implied by them.

## Closest literature and limitations
Bruckamp–Chimani–Juhnke, Theorem 5.11, is the closest exact facet result, and their Theorem 7.6 gives the Narayana specialization. Didi Biha–Kerivin–Ng's tree-connected-subgraph facets are also close. Beck–De Silva–Dorfsman-Hopkins–Pruitt–Ruiz treat nonzero interval vectors. The theorem does not cover arbitrary connected-block graphs, weighted coordinates, or general Euclidean embeddings. It describes the incenter locus by inequalities and dimension, not by its vertices; equivalent metric descriptions outside the searched terminology remain possible.
