For the zero-inclusive polytope of consecutive-one interval vectors, the exact Euclidean inradius splits by parity: \(1/(1+\sqrt{n-1})\) in even dimension and \(2/(2+\sqrt n+\sqrt{n-2})\) in odd dimension. For every \(n\ge3\), its maximum inscribed balls have a whole \((n-3)\)-dimensional polytope of possible centers rather than generally sharing a unique incenter.
