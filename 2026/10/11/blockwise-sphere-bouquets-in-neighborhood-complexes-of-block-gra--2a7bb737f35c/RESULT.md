# Blockwise sphere bouquets in neighborhood complexes of block graphs

## Finding
Let \(B\) be a finite connected simple **block graph** with at least two vertices: every block (maximal subgraph with no articulation vertex, with bridge edges counted as two-vertex blocks) is a complete graph. Let \(\mathcal C(B)\) be the set of its blocks having at least three vertices, and put \(b=|\mathcal C(B)|\). Define the Lovász **open-neighborhood complex** \(\mathcal N(B)\) to consist of all vertex subsets with a common adjacent vertex (a vertex is not adjacent to itself).

If \(b=0\), then \(B\) is a tree and \(\mathcal N(B)\simeq S^0\). If \(b\ge1\), there is a homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal N(B)\simeq\left(\bigvee^{b-1}S^1\right)\vee\bigvee_{C\in\mathcal C(B)}S^{|C|-2}.
\]
In particular, if \(c_r\) is the number of clique blocks on \(r\) vertices, the fundamental group is free of rank \(b-1+c_3\), and for every \(k\ge2\) the integral homology is canonically generated by oriented boundaries of clique blocks:
\[
\bigoplus_{\substack{C\in\mathcal C(B)\\|C|=k+2}}
\widetilde H_k(\partial\Delta_C;\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow[\cong]{\ \mathrm{inclusion}\ }
\widetilde H_k(\mathcal N(B);\mathbb Z).
\]
Here \(\Delta_C\) denotes the full simplex on \(C\), and the embedded \(\partial\Delta_C\) is the boundary of that simplex. This isomorphism is natural under graph automorphisms: after fixing vertex orders, \(\operatorname{Aut}(B)\) permutes the clique-boundary generators, multiplying each by the sign of the induced permutation of its ordered clique vertices. Thus all integral homology is torsion-free, and the higher-degree automorphism modules are signed block-permutation modules.

As a consequence, if \(T\) is any finite tree with at least two edges and \(L(T)\) its line graph, let \(R=\{v\in V(T):\deg_T(v)\ge3\}\). When \(R\ne\varnothing\),
\[
\mathcal N(L(T))\simeq\left(\bigvee^{|R|-1}S^1\right)\vee\bigvee_{v\in R}S^{\deg_T(v)-2};
\]
when \(R=\varnothing\), it has two contractible connected components. For example, a tree with precisely two adjacent branch vertices of degrees four and five yields \(\mathcal N(L(T))\simeq S^1\vee S^2\vee S^3\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, and loopless. The sole domain restriction on \(B\) besides connectivity and at least one edge is that all of its blocks are complete. A bridge is a block of order two and does not contribute a sphere. A wedge of zero spheres is a point. The formula concerns ordinary homotopy type, not a claimed specified simplicial-collapse target. The signed generator statement is asserted only in degrees \(k\ge2\), since there is no canonical equivariant splitting of the extra degree-one cycles in the formulation above.

## Proof
**1. Complete blocks.** For \(r\ge3\), every proper subset of the vertices of \(K_r\) has a common neighbor within \(K_r\), while the full vertex set does not. Hence \(\mathcal N(K_r)=\partial\Delta^{r-1}\cong S^{r-2}\). In a block graph, any clique block \(C\) of order at least three is maximal as a clique. Consequently its full vertex set still has no common neighbor outside \(C\), while every proper subset has one inside \(C\). Therefore \(\partial\Delta_C\) is an embedded subcomplex of \(\mathcal N(B)\), with its standard integral orientation cycle.

**2. One-vertex gluing.** Suppose connected graphs \(G\) and \(H\), each with an odd cycle, meet only in a vertex \(v\), and have no edges between their remaining vertices. Put \(A=N_G(v)\), \(D=N_H(v)\); both are nonempty and disjoint. Every open neighborhood other than that of \(v\) lies entirely in its original graph, while \(N_{G\cup H}(v)=A\cup D\). Thus an exact simplicial identity holds:
\[
\mathcal N(G\cup_v H)=\mathcal N(G)\cup\mathcal N(H)\cup\Delta_{A\cup D}.
\]
The first two complexes meet in the single vertex \(v\), and the new simplex meets their union in precisely \(\Delta_A\cup\Delta_D\), whose realizations are two disjoint contractible subspaces. Each \(\mathcal N(G)\) is path-connected: vertices are joined by paths in its one-skeleton exactly when there are even-length walks joining them in \(G\), and a connected graph with an odd cycle has even walks joining any two vertices. The homotopy pushout obtained by attaching a contractible simplex along two disjoint contractible faces is therefore equivalent to attaching one interval between points of an already path-connected complex. Hence
\[
\mathcal N(G\cup_v H)\simeq \mathcal N(G)\vee\mathcal N(H)\vee S^1.
\]
Moreover the inclusions of the two old complexes induce an integral homology isomorphism in every degree at least two: the attached interval has relative cellular chains only in degree one. This is independent of the location of \(v\) and of the sizes of its two neighborhoods.

**3. Bridges and trees.** If a new pendant vertex \(w\) is adjoined to a non-isolated vertex \(v\) of a connected graph \(G\), the only new faces of \(\mathcal N(G+w)\) lie in \(\Delta_{N_G(v)\cup\{w\}}\), attached along \(\Delta_{N_G(v)}\). This whole simplex collapses onto that nonempty base face without touching any old faces, so \(\mathcal N(G+w)\searrow\mathcal N(G)\). Repeating leaf removal in a tree of at least two vertices reaches \(K_2\), whose neighborhood complex consists of two points. Thus trees have neighborhood complex homotopy equivalent to \(S^0\).

**4. Block-tree induction.** The block–cut incidence graph of a connected block graph is a tree. Start at any block \(K_r\) of order \(r\ge3\) and attach the other blocks in a rooted order, so that each new block intersects the constructed graph in exactly one vertex. A two-vertex block contributes nothing by step 3. Each further block of order at least three has an odd cycle, so step 2 adds its \(S^{r-2}\) together with one extra circle. Exactly \(b-1\) such additional circles appear. This proves the displayed wedge type, including mixed dimensions and the case of arbitrary intervening bridge blocks.

**5. Canonical higher homology and symmetry.** In step 2, attaching the new simplex changes integral homology only in degree one; a pendant edge changes no homology. Induction therefore shows that the embedded fundamental cycles of \(\partial\Delta_C\) for the blocks of order \(k+2\) form a basis of \(\widetilde H_k\) for \(k\ge2\). Every graph automorphism permutes the maximal clique blocks, and its restriction between two ordered blocks sends the oriented simplex boundary to that of the image block times the parity of the vertex permutation. This proves the stated equivariant signed-permutation description; no orientation choices can create torsion.

**6. Tree line graphs.** The non-bridge clique blocks of \(L(T)\) are exactly the cliques on all edges incident with branch vertices of \(T\) having degree at least three. Vertices of \(T\) of degree two contribute only two-vertex blocks, and leaves contribute no blocks. Applying step 4 gives the line-graph formula.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library and constructs neighborhoods directly from adjacency sets. Running `python3 verify.py` checks the integral-domain assumptions of the construction with finite combinatorial tests: it exhaustively enumerates 4,792 connected labeled block graphs on two through six vertices, computes all mod-two boundary ranks, and compares them with the theorem. It also tests 150 generated larger block graphs, 99 exact one-vertex-gluing face-set identities, 100 independent line-graph-of-tree branch-degree predictions, and all 72 automorphisms of two four-cliques meeting at a vertex for the signed boundary-cycle action. The run returned `VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations are corroboration only; the all-order proof is steps 1–6.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov established a simple-homotopy equivalence between neighborhood complexes and \(\operatorname{Hom}(K_2,-)\), but did not give this blockwise homotopy classification or the signed clique-boundary modules. Matsushita developed general fundamental-group descriptions, not the higher-dimensional blockwise decomposition. Braun and Vega studied circle summands under Hajós operations and distant vertex identifications; the present one-vertex block-gluing identity concerns a different operation and determines all sphere dimensions and multiplicities. Shukla's low-degree and circulant-graph classifications have distinct restricted graph domains and do not imply the arbitrary-block-size formula. An already-known elementary clique-bouquet special case would not establish the all-block-graph and equivariant statement.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify arbitrary chordal graphs whose maximal cliques overlap in edges or larger faces, nor disconnected graphs with isolated vertices. The degree-one homology representation is not canonically split in the assertion. The verifier computes mod-two homology in finite cases and the orientations in a finite symmetry example; torsion-freeness and the global homotopy type follow from the proof, not from finite enumeration. The literature comparison cannot rule out every unindexed equivalent presentation.
