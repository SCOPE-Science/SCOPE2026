# Review

## Correctness
PASS. All original weaves are explicitly enumerated through four \(2\times2\) frame operators. Their spectra give the positive universal lower bound
\[
\frac{7-\sqrt{41}}2
\]
and upper bound
\[
4+\sqrt{13}.
\]
The two canonical duals are computed exactly. The partition taking indices \(1,2\) from the first canonical dual and index \(3\) from the second consists only of multiples of \(e_1\), so it is not a frame.

The common-frame-operator repair is a direct change-of-variable argument with one shared invertible operator.

## Originality
PASS. The exact source, theorem label, canonical-dual formulation, redundant two-dimensional counterexamples, inverse-frame-operator aliases, and correction terms were compared with later duality literature and the nearest indexed frame results.

Later work gives additional hypotheses under which canonical duals are woven and records a distinct failure for canonical Parseval frames. No checked source contains this source-specific three-vector counterexample or an implication that subsumes it.

## Value
PASS. The disputed corollary is a structural preservation claim for a fundamental reconstruction operation. Its failure can collapse a mixed dual family from a frame to a one-dimensional span. The example cleanly isolates the problem: different constituent inverse frame operators cannot be applied independently as though one common weaving-preserving operator were being used.
