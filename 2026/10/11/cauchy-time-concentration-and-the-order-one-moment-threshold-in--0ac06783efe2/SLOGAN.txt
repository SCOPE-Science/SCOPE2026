In the cubic global center \(\dot x=(1+\kappa x^2)y\), \(\dot y=-x\), sampling a large-amplitude orbit uniformly in *original time* makes its \(x\)-coordinate converge to a Cauchy law of scale \(1/\sqrt\kappa\). The absolute time-moments have a sharp threshold: orders below one approach finite Cauchy moments, order one grows logarithmically in the squared amplitude, and higher orders diverge with an explicit power-law coefficient.
