# Clique-fiber Čech nerves over graphs of girth at least five

## Finding
Let \(H\) be any finite, connected, simple graph on \(n\ge2\) vertices with \(e\ge1\) edges, containing no triangles or four-cycles. For every integer \(m\ge3\), let \(G=H\square K_m\) be the Cartesian product of \(H\) with the complete graph on \(m\) vertices, equipped with unit-length geometric edges. Let \(\mathcal C(G,r)\) be the nerve of the closed intrinsic balls of radius \(r/2\) centered at its graph vertices. Then the scale-two nerve admits an explicit sequence of exactly
\[
m\,2^{m-1}\sum_{v\in V(H)}\bigl(2^{\deg_H(v)}-\deg_H(v)-1\bigr)
\]
elementary simplicial collapses to a complex assembled from edge blocks. Its integral homotopy type is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C(G,2)\simeq H\vee\bigvee^{e(m-1)^2}S^2.}
\]
The inclusion of the scale-one graph \(G\) into its scale-two nerve induces a surjection on integral first homology, with a free abelian kernel of rank
\[
(m-1)e+\frac{n(m-1)(m-2)}2.
\]
For the natural cyclic-strip family \(H=C_n\), with \(n\ge5\), this specializes to
\[
\mathcal C(C_n\square K_m,2)\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n(m-1)^2}S^2.
\]
This describes the full homotopy type, not only mod-two Betti numbers. The earlier width-two prism theorem is recovered by the same collapse but is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
The product \(H\square K_m\) has vertex set \(\{(v,a):v\in V(H),\ a\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\}\). Two vertices are adjacent when their base vertices agree and their color indices differ, or their base vertices are adjacent and their color indices agree. Each base vertex supports a complete fiber \(F_v=\{(v,a):1\le a\le m\}\). All cross-fiber graph edges retain the same color. The graph \(H\) has girth at least five, where forests are allowed. The theorem assumes connectedness and at least one base edge, and requires \(m\ge3\).

The Čech parameter uses closed metric balls centered only at graph vertices, inside the unit-edge geometric realization. The scale-two complex coincides with the abstract closed-neighborhood complex: a set of centers is a simplex exactly when it belongs to the closed neighborhood of some graph vertex. This is the even-scale Čech lemma from Adams, Shukla and Singh; it is not a Vietoris–Rips statement.

## Proof
**1. Neighborhood facets and unique bridging pairs.** For every base vertex \(v\) and color \(a\), the corresponding closed neighborhood in \(G\) is
\[
S_{v,a}=F_v\cup\{(u,a):u\in N_H(v)\}.
\]
The scale-two nerve is generated by the simplices \(\Delta(S_{v,a})\). For distinct neighbors \(u,w\) of \(v\), the vertices \((u,a)\) and \((w,a)\) cannot both lie in any other generating simplex: if \(u,w\) were adjacent, they would complete a triangle with \(v\), and if they had another common neighbor they would complete a four-cycle. Therefore every face containing at least two distinct off-fiber vertices \((u,a),(w,a)\) from \(S_{v,a}\) belongs to this one generator, and to no generator centered elsewhere or in another color.

**2. Explicit elementary collapses.** For every \(v,a\), put \(p=(v,a)\). Match each simplex
\[
\sigma=B\cup T,\qquad B\subseteq F_v\setminus\{p\},\quad
T\subseteq\{(u,a):u\in N_H(v)\},\quad |T|\ge2,
\]
with its one-vertex extension \(\sigma\cup\{p\}\). The uniqueness just proved shows that matching pairs do not overlap between distinct generators. Process all lower faces in decreasing cardinality. At each step, all larger cofaces of the lower face have already been removed, so its unique remaining coface is its matched extension; that is an elementary simplicial collapse. Every such face is removed, and no other face is removed. The number of matched pairs for a fixed \(v,a\) is
\[
2^{m-1}\bigl(2^{\deg_H(v)}-\deg_H(v)-1\bigr).
\]
Summing over \(v,a\) gives the displayed exact collapse count. The residual complex consists of the simplices supported on one fiber together with vertices of at most one neighboring fiber.

**3. An edge-block decomposition.** For each base edge \(uv\in E(H)\), let \(D_v\) denote the zero-dimensional complex on the \(m\) vertices of \(F_v\), and set
\[
P_{uv}=\bigl(\Delta(F_u)*D_v\bigr)\cup
       \bigl(D_u*\Delta(F_v)\bigr).
\]
The residual complex is exactly \(L=\bigcup_{uv\in E(H)}P_{uv}\). Both pieces of \(P_{uv}\) are contractible because each contains a full nonempty fiber simplex as a join factor. Their intersection is \(D_u*D_v\), the one-dimensional complete bipartite graph \(K_{m,m}\). Homotopy-pushout theory therefore gives
\[
P_{uv}\simeq\Sigma K_{m,m}\simeq\bigvee^{(m-1)^2}S^2.
\]
Here the graph \(K_{m,m}\) is connected and has first Betti number \((m-1)^2\), so its suspension is the indicated wedge of two-spheres.

**4. Assemble the edge blocks without losing fundamental-group information.** Distinct blocks sharing the base vertex \(v\) intersect exactly in the full fiber simplex \(\Delta(F_v)\); blocks over disjoint base edges have empty intersection. More generally all simultaneous intersections of blocks incident at \(v\) are that same contractible simplex. One may assemble the blocks as a graph of spaces with one contractible vertex space \(\Delta(F_v)\) for every \(v\), one edge space \(P_{uv}\) for every \(uv\), and the two endpoint simplex inclusions as its attaching maps. Its homotopy colimit is the same as \(L\), since these are simplicial-cofibration gluings and there are no unintended intersections. Contract each fiber simplex, choose a spanning tree of \(H\), and apply the two-sided homotopy-pushout construction one base edge at a time. Tree edges contribute the wedge of their \(P_{uv}\) sphere summands, and every remaining base edge contributes its sphere summands plus one circle. Thus
\[
L\simeq\bigvee^{e-n+1}S^1\vee\bigvee^{e(m-1)^2}S^2
\simeq H\vee\bigvee^{e(m-1)^2}S^2.
\]
Combined with Step 2, this proves the theorem integrally.

**5. Integral persistence kernel.** The graph \(G\) has \(nm\) vertices and \(em+n\binom m2\) edges. Its first integral homology is therefore free of rank \(em+n\binom m2-nm+1\). The residual complex admits a simplicial map to \(H\) sending all vertices of \(F_v\) to \(v\), and every edge block to its base edge. Fixing one color \(a\) gives a subgraph of \(G\) isomorphic to \(H\) whose composition with this map is the identity. Hence \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(\mathcal C(G,2);\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(H;\mathbb Z)\) is surjective. Its kernel is a subgroup of a free abelian group, hence free, and subtracting the rank \(e-n+1\) gives exactly
\[
(m-1)e+\frac{n(m-1)(m-2)}2.
\]
This conclusion describes the rank, not a preferred basis of the kernel.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_clique_fibers.py` using the Python standard library. The script independently generates the scale-two closed-neighborhood nerve, explicitly tests every proposed elementary free-face pair in descending size, checks that the residual faces equal the asserted union of edge blocks, and performs independent mod-two simplicial boundary-rank calculations. It checks paths, stars, cycles, and the Petersen graph at clique widths two, three and four, including examples with branching base vertices and multiple base cycles; it ends with `VERIFY_OK`. Width two is included solely as a pre-existing boundary comparison. The exhaustive finite checks corroborate but do not establish the universal theorem; the preceding argument supplies the all-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh introduce the vertex-centered graph Čech model and its even-scale closed-neighborhood description, and classify hypercube examples. Matsushita treats closed-neighborhood complexes and, in one example, Cartesian products of two complete graphs. That example already covers the special base \(H=K_2\) here; it does not cover noncomplete connected high-girth bases with at least three vertices. Existing calculations of intrinsic Vietoris–Rips complexes for cyclic clique strips use a different simplicial construction. Previously established scale-two Čech classifications for width-two prisms, including circular ladders, are the \(m=2\) special case and are explicitly excluded from the asserted new range \(m\ge3\). The present theorem supplies the larger family, exact elementary collapse count, homotopy classification and integral first-homology kernel.

## Limitations
The girth hypothesis is essential to the unique-generating-simplex argument, and the result is not asserted for base graphs containing triangles or four-cycles. The fiber must be a uniform complete graph, and edge lengths must be one. The homotopy equivalence need not be canonical or equivariant under arbitrary graph automorphisms. The verification script proves only the enumerated finite cases. The literature review cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent classification. No independent audit has been performed.
