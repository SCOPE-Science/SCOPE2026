In the clock-quasi-metric realization of the cyclohedron, all \(n(n-1)\) Lipschitz facets and Kantorovich--Rubinstein vertices split into \(n-1\) strict Euclidean shells indexed by clockwise distance. The zero-mean Lipschitz cyclohedron has unique inradius \((n-1)^2/(n\sqrt2)\), while its polar has unique minimum-enclosing radius \(n\sqrt2/(n-1)^2\), with exactly \(n\) contacts on each side.
