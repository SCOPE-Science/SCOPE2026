# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For the displayed regular dodecagonal norm, signed-facet inequalities on the first six relative edge classes give a lower envelope for the sum chord at fixed difference chord. The opposite classes follow by central symmetry because the envelope is a decreasing involution. Three explicit affine edge families attain the three branches. The boundaries at 0, 1, sqrt(3), and 2 were checked, and the certificate inequalities and witness norms were independently reconstructed with exact algebraic sign checks. This verifies the stated all-separation profile, not a complete classification of every minimizing pair.

The published dodecagonal angle-convexity result gives the opposite **maximum** sum-chord curve; the P-angle result provides weighted upper constraints on chord pairs, not the sharp lower curve. An all-even-regular-polygon **Day** modulus theorem concerns a different scaled, unconstrained supremum. The published octagonal angle-smoothness result evaluates a different norm at one separation. The absence of a previously indexed exact table is not a proof of absolute novelty; alternative notation or older polygonal-geometry sources remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem computes a complete natural modulus of the canonical twelve-sided normed plane. Its three affine regimes, sharp transitions at separations 1 and sqrt(3), and involutive chord boundary describe geometry not fixed by existing upper-envelope or one-point results. It complements the regular-dodecagonal convexity profile while remaining specific to one norm. No general regular-polygon theorem, minimizer uniqueness, or nonlinear stability is claimed.
