# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The published Reinhardt optimizer correspondence and cyclotomic test reduce the nine-sided classification to two cases. Direct exact division by the ninth-vertex composition polynomials modulo \(\Phi_{18}(z)=z^6-z^3+1\) checked all 128 odd-part compositions and retained only \([(3)^3]\) and \([(1)^9]\). For the Reuleaux-triangle subdivision, the three polygonal caps give \(A_{\mathrm{tri}}=\frac92\sin(\pi/9)-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\). The diameter-one regular nonagon gives \(A_{\mathrm{reg}}=9\sin^2(\pi/18)\cot(\pi/9)\). Their difference is \((\sqrt3-9\tan(\pi/18))/2>0\) by the triple-angle argument. Independent coordinate/area calculations agreed to high precision. The universal optimizer correspondence is a cited theorem, not a conclusion from finite testing.

## Originality — PASS (best of knowledge)
Hare–Mossinghoff's arXiv:1203.4107 and arXiv:1405.5233 determine the relevant optimizer classes and composition counts. Bingane–Mossinghoff's arXiv:2204.04547 recalls the global fixed-diameter area theorem, which gives regular-area superiority but not the nonregular exact area. The additional contribution is the explicit nonregular value and the complete two-value secondary area spectrum. The 2011 Mossinghoff enumeration paper could be checked only at abstract level; it remains a priority risk. No complete-text noncoverage claim is made about that source.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Area is the natural second classical fixed-diameter extremal quantity, and nine sides give the first nonunique odd-sided maximum-perimeter class. Computing its entire secondary area spectrum and exact gap is a motivated narrow complete invariant; no general side-count classification or new global area bound is claimed.
