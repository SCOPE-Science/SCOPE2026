# Scientific assessment: binary 4×4 Fourier optimizer classification

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed finite domain comprises all 65,536 fully populated real sign arrays. Orthogonality gives the aperiodic autocorrelation identity for the normalized fourth moment, while Parseval for the polynomial square gives a separate integer formula. The three independent sign modulations reduce the domain by a free factor of eight. A fresh enumeration of all 65,536 arrays independently confirms minimum 304, precisely 32 minimizers, second moment 336, and two disjoint symmetry orbits of size 16. Every normalized state also satisfies both exact moment formulas. The two moment-equal extremizer geometries have dominant-shift dot-product magnitudes zero and eight; their common half-plane correlation histogram is 16 zeros, six ones, two threes. The proof is complete only for the specified finite binary domain.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic qualifications
The sharp scalar merit factor 16/3 is prior mathematics, reported by Schmidt (2011) with attribution to Eggers (1986). The additional claim is the full 32-array equality census and its two geometrically distinguished symmetry orbits. Schmidt's relevant theorems concern asymptotic families of quadratic-character arrays; Svalbe–Tirkel (2017) concerns periodic low-correlation families; Pai–Chen (2021) concerns complementary sets, not the single-array equality fiber. Searches for alternate aperiodic-array and bivariate Littlewood-polynomial descriptions did not identify a distinct covering theorem. The original full Eggers dissertation and Bömer–Antweiler (1993) article were unavailable; these remain genuine priority risks. This is a best-of-knowledge assessment, not absolute priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete equality locus and symmetry geometry of a canonical planar Fourier aperture distinguish arrays with the same total sidelobe energy but different directions of largest autocorrelation lobes. These exact finite invariants go beyond a pre-existing scalar table and are natural benchmarks for directional sidelobe geometry. No unproved performance advantage or extension to other sizes is claimed.
