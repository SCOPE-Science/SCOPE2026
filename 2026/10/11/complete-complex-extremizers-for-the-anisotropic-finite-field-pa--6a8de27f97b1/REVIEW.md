# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The sharp numerical constant and constant modulus of nonzero extremizers are independently established in González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva, Theorem 1.1 and §4.1. Reading the equality case of each noncritical Cauchy–Schwarz fiber yields the orthogonal parallelogram identity. The proof here derives from this identity a globally constant symmetric bicharacter of all additive increments; its kernel on every orthogonal line forces a single scalar multiple of the field dot product. Subtracting the corresponding quadratic phase leaves a linear additive character. Conversely each claimed chirp translates the extension output and preserves the sharp fourth moment. The finite computation gives rank \(p^2-4\) of phase constraints for \(p=3,7,11\), and verifies sharpness for explicit chirps. The finite checks are not being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
The closest source, González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva, arXiv:2405.16647, proves the sharp constant, general constant-modulus necessity and the phase classification for \(q\equiv1\pmod4\), but its §4.1 for \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) ends without classifying complex phases. The later González-Riquelme–Ismoilov arXiv:2509.05600, §1.2, independently reports that only the opposite congruence class has a known complex phase classification; its own classification pertains to cones. Iosevich–Koh gives extension estimates, not sharp equality classes. Searches for chirps, anisotropic quadratic surfaces, orthogonally additive equations and extremizer equality did not reveal a dominating classification. An older general orthogonally additive phase theorem might imply a subsidiary algebraic lemma, and unindexed overlapping literature is possible; the complete sharp extension equality classification is not established in the inspected sources.

## Value — PASS
The phase of an extremizer is not determined by constant modulus alone. Determining that every equality case arises from only three field parameters closes the anisotropic branch of a natural, explicitly posed sharp extension classification and gives an exact mod-scaling census of \(q^3\). It rules out other phase oscillations even over proper field extensions, and characterizes the equality geometry rather than merely recomputing a known norm.

## Limits
The claim is restricted to odd-field two-dimensional paraboloids with \(q\equiv3\pmod4\). No classification of other finite-field quadratic surfaces is claimed. The finite tests support, but cannot establish, the all-prime-power assertion. Originality searches do not prove absence of unindexed equivalents.
