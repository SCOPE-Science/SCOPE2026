# Complete coordinate-symmetry homology of Hamming-graph total two-cut complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge2\), choose finite sets \(A_i\) with \(n_i=|A_i|\ge2\) and put \(V=A_1\times\cdots\times A_k\), \(N=\prod_i n_i\). Let \(G=K_{n_1}\square\cdots\square K_{n_k}\): two words are adjacent precisely when they differ in one coordinate. Write \(T=\Delta^t_2(G)\) for its *total two-cut complex*, comprising all subsets of \(V\) whose complements contain two nonadjacent vertices.

Let \(\Gamma\) be generated by arbitrary relabelings in the individual alphabets and swaps of equal-sized coordinate axes. For each \(i\), define the rational augmentation module
\[
U_i=\ker\bigl(\mathbb Q[A_i]\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q\bigr).
\]
For a set of coordinate indices \(S\subseteq[k]\), set
\[
E_S=\ker\bigl(\mathbb Q[S]\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q\bigr).
\]
The only nonzero reduced rational homology of \(T\) is in degree \(N-4\), with the complete \(\Gamma\)-representation
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{N-4}(T;\mathbb Q)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\otimes
\bigoplus_{\substack{S\subseteq[k]\\ |S|\ge2}}
E_S\otimes\bigotimes_{i\in S}U_i.}
\]
The group sends the summand indexed by \(S\) to that indexed by its image on coordinate indices, acting by natural transport on the corresponding tensor factors and the basis of \(E_S\). Thus the formula covers the full natural coordinate group, including swaps of equal-size axes. The sign \(\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\) is taken on all \(N\) vertices, not just on coordinate indices.

In particular, after restriction to the independent alphabet-permutation subgroup \(\prod_iS_{n_i}\), the summands are inequivalent irreducibles labeled by \(S\), each of multiplicity \(|S|-1\), with a common sign twist. This is an irreducible-representation decomposition for that subgroup; the displayed \(\Gamma\)-decomposition is an explicit module description without asserting that each subset orbit is irreducible for \(\Gamma\).

For any \(g\in\Gamma\), write \(v(g)\) for its fixed vertices of \(G\), \(d(g)\) for its fixed coordinate directions, and \(\ell(g)\) for its setwise fixed maximal coordinate-line cliques. The character is completely specified by
\[
\boxed{\chi_{N-4}(g)=\operatorname{sgn}_{V}(g)
\bigl(1-\ell(g)+(d(g)-1)v(g)\bigr).}
\]
At the identity this recovers the known nonequivariant dimension
\[
\dim\widetilde H_{N-4}(T;\mathbb Q)
=(k-1)N+1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{N}{n_i}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All finite sets are nonempty and \(n_i\ge2\); thus \(N\ge4\). Coefficients are \(\mathbb Q\). The complex is the *ordinary* total two-cut complex, not an ordered or directed simplicial-set variant. The equivariance is for the displayed natural coordinate-relabeling group. Any equal-cardinality alphabets are identified when their axes are swapped; different choices of identification conjugate the action. Homology is reduced, and \(\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\) means the determinant of the permutation matrix on \(\mathbb Q[V]\). All tensor products and direct sums are over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**1. Every nontrivial clique has a unique direction.** A clique with two distinct vertices \(u,v\) differing in coordinate \(i\) cannot have a third vertex differing from \(u\) only in another coordinate \(j\ne i\), since that third vertex would differ from \(v\) in two coordinates. Hence every positive-dimensional simplex of \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(G)\) is contained in precisely one *coordinate line*, obtained by fixing all coordinates except one. Each line spans a full simplex; distinct line simplices share at most one vertex. There are \(\sum_iN/n_i\) lines, each assigned a direction \(i\).

**2. A natural incidence-kernel calculation.** For each line \(L\), take its *unaugmented* simplicial chain complex \(C_*(\Delta_L;\mathbb Q)\). Their direct sum \(D_*\) maps onto the unaugmented complex \(C_*(X;\mathbb Q)\). The map is an isomorphism in every positive degree by the uniqueness just proved. In degree zero it is the surjection
\[
\mathbb Q[V\times[k]]\longrightarrow\mathbb Q[V],
\qquad (v,i)\longmapsto v.
\]
Its kernel \(K_0\), considered in chain degree zero, yields a short exact sequence of \(\Gamma\)-chain complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow K_0[0]\longrightarrow D_*\longrightarrow C_*(X;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since each line simplex is contractible and \(X\) is connected, its long exact homology sequence identifies
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Q)=
\ker\Bigl[\mathbb Q[V\times[k]]\xrightarrow{(\partial_V,\partial_L)}
\mathbb Q[V]\oplus\mathbb Q[\mathcal L]\Bigr],
\]
where \(\mathcal L\) is the set of coordinate lines, \(\partial_V(v,i)=v\), and \(\partial_L(v,i)\) is the line of direction \(i\) through \(v\). Simultaneously, \(H_j(X;\mathbb Q)=0\) for every \(j>1\). The kernel has no orientation convention: changing the sign of one target block leaves it unchanged. This is the cycle-space representation of the bipartite incidence graph of coordinate lines and vertices.

**3. Decompose the incidence kernel.** Split every permutation module using the normalized constant vector and its sum-zero augmentation module,
\[
\mathbb Q[A_i]=\mathbb Q\mathbf1_i\oplus U_i,
\qquad \mathbf1_i=\frac1{n_i}\sum_{a\in A_i}a.
\]
Then \(\mathbb Q[V]\) decomposes into \(W_S=\bigotimes_{i\in S}U_i\), with constant factors in coordinates not in \(S\), as \(S\) ranges over every subset of \([k]\). The incidence-edge module contains one copy of \(W_S\) for each direction \(i\). The line module contains a copy in direction \(i\) precisely when \(i\notin S\): summing over the varying coordinate annihilates \(U_i\) and sends \(\mathbf1_i\) to \(1\). On the coefficient vector \(c\in\mathbb Q^{[k]}\), the line equation forces \(c_i=0\) for \(i\notin S\), while the vertex equation forces \(\sum_{i\in S}c_i=0\). This gives exactly
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
\bigoplus_{\substack{S\subseteq[k]\\ |S|\ge2}}
E_S\otimes W_S.
\]
The construction commutes with every allowed relabeling and coordinate swap, since constants, augmentation kernels, lines, and incidence maps do. For the direct-product subgroup, each nonzero \(U_i\) is the irreducible standard \(S_{n_i}\)-module (also for \(n_i=2\), when it is the sign line). Tensor products indexed by different \(S\) are inequivalent; \(\dim E_S=|S|-1\).

**4. Equivariant Alexander duality and signs.** Because the complement of a subset of \(V\) contains a nonedge exactly when it is *not* a clique, \(T\) is the combinatorial Alexander dual \(X^*\). Equivariant simplicial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(X^*;\mathbb Q)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_V\otimes
\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(X;\mathbb Q).
\]
The sign is the orientation character of the ambient \(N-1\)-simplex, and in particular is essential when a group element reverses a vertex ordering. Since \(X\) is connected and has homology only in degree one, the dual is concentrated in degree \(N-4\). The invariant standard inner products give \(\Gamma\)-equivariant identifications of the relevant rational representations with their duals, proving the announced \(\Gamma\)-module formula.

**5. Character and dimension.** In the representation ring, the exact incidence sequence gives
\[
[H_1(X)]=[\mathbb Q[V\times[k]]]-[\mathbb Q[V]]-[\mathbb Q[\mathcal L]]+[\mathbf1].
\]
If \(g\) fixes \(d(g)\) directions and \(v(g)\) vertices, it fixes exactly \(d(g)v(g)\) incidence pairs; it fixes \(\ell(g)\) coordinate lines. Taking traces, then the sign twist from duality, yields the stated formula. At the identity, the dimension is \(kN-N-\sum_iN/n_i+1\), which equals the sum of \((|S|-1)\prod_{i\in S}(n_i-1)\) over all \(|S|\ge2\). This also checks the precise multiplicities.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_hamming.py` reconstructs each graph from the Hamming-distance adjacency rule and enumerates faces of the *actual total two-cut complex* directly from nonedges, without using the line-decomposition argument. It independently computes signed fixed-face Lefschetz numbers and compares them with the closed character expression. For all elements of the natural automorphism groups of the four shapes \((2,2)\), \((2,3)\), \((3,3)\), and \((2,2,2)\), all \(140\) character evaluations agree. Further generator and cyclic-direction checks for shapes \((2,2,3)\), \((2,2,2,2)\) and the four smaller shapes yield \(36\) matching traces. Full independent mod-\(2\) boundary-rank computations for the five shapes with at most \(12\) vertices give homology only in the claimed degree, with ranks agreeing with the formula. The complete face counts for the six enumerated shapes are \(7,46,478,235,4055,65487\), respectively.

To replay from the provided code, execute `python verify_hamming.py` with Python 3 and standard library only. Successful output ends in `EXHAUSTIVE_GROUP_OK elements=140`. Finite checks do not prove the infinite-family theorem; the exact incidence-kernel and duality arguments above do.

## Relationship to prior work
Carnero Bravo, *Total cut complexes and their duals*, arXiv:2602.21427v1 (first publicly available 2026-02-24), Theorem 41 in the updated text, determines the nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres homotopy type for all Cartesian products of complete graphs and exactly the dimension recovered here. It does not supply the induced coordinate-group representation. Bayer–Denker–Jelić Milutinović–Rowlands–Sundaram–Xue, arXiv:2209.13503v2, Theorem 4.8 gives the earlier nonequivariant two-factor prism special case. Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak, arXiv:2512.04486v1, analyze total two-cut homotopy for Cartesian products with paths and cycles, without the full Hamming coordinate representation. Bayer et al., arXiv:2407.08158v1, consider different ordinary cut-complex families and provide general context. An independent public exploration of Hamming-graph clique complexes derives the known first Betti number by counting coordinate lines, but it does not calculate the coordinate-group action or the augmentation-tensor constituents.

The strengthened content here is the complete rational \(\Gamma\)-module, its restricted irreducible constituents and multiplicities, and the character on *every* allowed symmetry, not the previously published rank or wedge count. The scope of comparison and residual novelty risks are recorded separately in `AUDIT.json`.

## Limitations
The theorem is a rational homology representation classification. It does not claim an integral-module decomposition, an equivariant homotopy splitting, a classification of actions beyond the natural coordinate group, or analogous formulas for larger cut parameters. The finite code checks are corroborative only. Publication and database coverage are not exhaustive; independently obtained equivalent representations may exist outside inspected sources.
