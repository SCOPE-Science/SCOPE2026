# Complete dihedral and layer-swap homology representations of circular ladders

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\) and let \(L_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the prism (circular ladder) graph. The natural symmetries generate \(G_n=D_n\times C_2\). The reduced rational homology of \(X_n=\operatorname{Ind}(L_n)\) is supported in one degree and, in that degree, has the following complete \(G_n\)-module structure.

Put \(r(j,a)=(j+1,a)\), \(s(j,a)=(-j,a)\), and \(t(j,a)=(j,1-a)\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\), \(a\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(R=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), \(S=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{psmallmatrix}\). Then, writing \(H_n=\widetilde H_{d_n}(X_n;\mathbb Q)\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{case}&\dim H_n&r|_{H_n}&s|_{H_n}&t|_{H_n}\\\hline
n\text{ odd}&1&1&(-1)^{\lfloor(n+1)/4\rfloor}&1\\
n=4k+2&1&-1&(-1)^k&-1\\
n=4k&3&1\oplus R&(-1)^{k+1}\oplus S&(-1)\oplus I_2
\end{array}
\]

The homology degree is
\[
d_n=\begin{cases}2k-1,&n=4k,\\ 2k-1,&n=4k+1,\\2k,&n=4k+2,\\2k+1,&n=4k+3.\end{cases}
\]
The two-dimensional summand occurs precisely at multiples of four. Its rotation has order four and its layer swap is trivial, whereas the extra line has nontrivial layer swap.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. Their independence complexes contain the empty face, with its usual degree \(-1\) contribution in reduced simplicial chains. The cyclic graph \(C_n\) is the ordinary \(n\)-cycle, and the product is Cartesian. The graph action is simplicial, and the homology coefficients are \(\mathbb Q\). The subgroup generated by the three displayed automorphisms is considered even when the full graph automorphism group is larger (for example, at \(n=4\)). The theorem applies to every integer \(n\ge3\), including both parities and all four residue classes.

The starting nonequivariant result is Agarwal--Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2, Main Theorem 2(a) and Theorem 15: \(X_n\) is homotopy equivalent to one sphere of dimension \(d_n\), except when \(4\mid n\), in which case it is a wedge of three such spheres. This supplies the degree concentration and dimension, but gives no canonical action of automorphisms on homology.

## Proof
**Signed simplicial fixed-face formula.** Let \(g\) be any simplicial automorphism of \(X_n\). If \(F\) is a \(g\)-invariant independent set and \(c_g(F)\) is the number of permutation cycles of \(g|_F\), then the contribution to the alternating trace on its oriented simplex is \((-1)^{c_g(F)-1}\): for \(|F|=q\), the simplex dimension is \(q-1\) and the sign of the induced permutation is \((-1)^{q-c_g(F)}\). Including the empty face in degree \(-1\) therefore yields the reduced Lefschetz number
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(g;X_n)=-\sum_{\substack{F\subseteq V(L_n)\text{ independent}\\gF=F}}(-1)^{c_g(F)}.
\tag{1}
\]
The same formula applies when \(F=\varnothing\), taking \(c_g(F)=0\).

**Circular and open transfer evaluations.** An independent set can choose in each column either the empty state \(0\), an upper vertex \(u\), or a lower vertex \(\ell\). Neighboring columns may not choose the same nonempty state. Give each occupied column weight \(-1\). In the state order \(0,u,\ell\), an admissibility-weighted transfer is
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-1\\1&0&-1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The total signed weight of cyclic admissible words of length \(m\) is \(Q_m=\operatorname{tr}(T^m)=1+2\cos(m\pi/2)\), for every positive integer \(m\), since the characteristic polynomial of \(T\) is \((z-1)(z^2+1)\). The open-chain sum of length \(m\ge1\) is
\[
P_m=(1,-1,-1)T^{m-1}(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}=(-1)^{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(P_1=P_2=-1\), and \(T^2(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}=-(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\) gives \(P_{m+2}=-P_m\).

**Rotations.** If \(0\le j<n\), a face fixed by \(r^j\) consists of whole column orbits, with \(q=\gcd(n,j)\) such orbits. An occupied orbit contributes exactly one vertex-permutation cycle, so its signed contribution is \(-1\), irrespective of its length. The orbit adjacency conditions are exactly cyclic admissibility of the \(q\)-column word (including \(q=1\) and \(q=2\)). Thus (1) gives
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(r^j;X_n)=-Q_{\gcd(n,j)}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, every odd \(q\) gives \(-1\); a \(q\) divisible by four gives \(-3\); and a \(q\) congruent to two modulo four gives \(+1\).

**Layer swap.** Each orbit of \(t\) is an edge of a rung of \(L_n\), and therefore cannot belong to an independent face. Hence the empty face is its only fixed face and
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(t;X_n)=-1.
\tag{4}
\]

**Reflections.** Take the column reflection \(s(j,a)=(-j,a)\). If \(n=2m+1\), the reflection fixes one column and pairs the others; the pair at the opposite axis contains a horizontal edge and must be vacant. The remaining \(m\) column orbits form a path with the same three local states and forbidden adjacent equal occupied states. If \(n=2m\), there are two fixed columns and \(m-1\) paired columns, giving an open path with \(m+1\) column orbits. An occupied fixed column and an occupied paired orbit each contribute one permutation cycle in the fixed-face signed sum. Consequently
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(s;X_n)=
\begin{cases}-P_m,&n=2m+1,\\-P_{m+1},&n=2m.\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
This identifies all rotation characters and the reflection-generator character without assigning a basis to homology.

**Recovery of the full representation.** By the cited sphere-wedge theorem all nonzero reduced homology is in degree \(d_n\), so the ordinary homology trace is \(\chi_n(g)=(-1)^{d_n}\widetilde\Lambda(g;X_n)\). For odd \(n\), (3) makes \(r\) trivial on the one-dimensional homology, and (4)--(5) give \(t=1\) and \(s=(-1)^{\lfloor(n+1)/4\rfloor}\). For \(n=4k+2\), (3) yields \(r=-1\) and (4)--(5) yield \(t=-1\), \(s=(-1)^k\).

For \(n=4k\), formula (3) gives
\[
\chi_n(r^j)=1+2\cos(j\pi/2).
\tag{6}
\]
The rational rotation representation is therefore a trivial line \(L\) plus a two-dimensional \(W\) on which \(r\) acts as \(R\); this follows by comparing the eigenvalue multiplicities of the finite-order operator \(r\) from the Fourier characters in (6). Formula (5) gives \(\chi_n(s)=(-1)^{k+1}\). Since \(srs=r^{-1}\), reflection exchanges the \(i\)- and \(-i\)-eigenspaces of \(r\) in \(W\), so it has trace zero there; therefore \(s\) acts on \(L\) as \((-1)^{k+1}\). The involution \(t\) commutes with \(r\); on the irreducible rational rotation module \(W\cong\mathbb Q(i)\), it must act as either \(I_2\) or \(-I_2\). Since \(\chi_n(t)=1\) by (4), it must act as \(I_2\) on \(W\) and as \(-1\) on \(L\). Finally, choose a rational \(+1\)-eigenvector \(v\) for the reflection on \(W\); the basis \((v,Rv)\) simultaneously gives \(r=R\) and \(s=S\). This proves the stated three-dimensional matrices and, because \(r,s,t\) generate \(G_n\), the complete representation in every case. \(\square\)

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only Python 3's standard library. Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing the script. It evaluates finite signed chains in two independently implemented ways: direct enumeration of all invariant independence faces for each natural group element at widths \(3\le n\le10\), and fixed-vertex-orbit quotient enumeration at widths \(3\le n\le24\). It also computes exact mod-two simplicial boundary ranks at widths \(3\le n\le11\), and checks the transfer matrix at 80 consecutive positive lengths. The observed completion marker is `VERIFY_OK: direct_signed_traces=208, orbit_signed_traces=1188, mod2_homology_widths=9, transfer_periods=80`. These tests are finite corroboration, **not** a proof for arbitrary widths; the uniform argument is the signed fixed-face and transfer derivation above together with the stated prior degree-concentration theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2, determine the ordinary homotopy type of the circular-ladder independence complex, but do not determine an action of its symmetries. Their original arXiv v1 (August 31, 2026) discusses only Möbius ladders; the circular-ladder material first appears in the September 14, 2026 v2, the first verified public source of the particular motivating circular-ladder theorem. The homotopy classification gives the ranks \(1\) and \(3\) but does not imply the character values or the distinction between the quarter-turn plane and the layer-antisymmetric line. Previously published calculations of analogous Möbius-ladder symmetry actions concern a different graph and do not imply this result.

## Limitations
The decomposition is over \(\mathbb Q\) for the specified natural \(D_n\times C_2\) subgroup. It does not assert an integral \(G_n\)-lattice classification, an equivariant homotopy splitting, or a description under additional automorphisms of exceptional small prisms. The homology degree-concentration premise is taken from the cited full preprint and is independently tested only for finite widths. An equivalent unindexed calculation is not excluded by database and literature searches.
