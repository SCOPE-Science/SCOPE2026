# Complete graph Čech persistence of the Petersen graph

## Finding

Let \(P\) be the Petersen graph, represented as the Kneser graph \(KG(5,2)\). Its vertices are the two-element subsets of a five-element set, and adjacency means disjointness. Equip each graph edge with length one, take the induced path metric on the geometric realization \(|P|\), and form the intrinsic graph Čech complex \(\mathcal N(P,r)\): a nonempty collection of **vertices** is a simplex precisely when the corresponding closed radius-\(r/2\) balls in \(|P|\) have nonempty intersection. The nerve has exactly ten potential vertices, not all points of the geometric graph.

For every real scale \(r\ge0\),

\[
\mathcal N(P,r)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{10}\{*\}, &0\le r<1,\\
\bigvee^{6}S^1, &1\le r<3,\\
\bigvee^{36}S^3, &3\le r<4,\\
\{*\}, &r\ge4.
\end{cases}
\]

Moreover, the full reduced **integral** persistent-homology barcode is nine degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), six degree-one intervals \([1,3)\), and thirty-six degree-three intervals \([3,4)\), with no other bars. At scale three the nerve has exactly 475 nonempty faces, with dimension-ordered face vector \((10,45,120,195,90,15)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes contain their nonempty simplices; homology uses integer coefficients unless specified. The graph \(P\) is simple, connected, cubic, has ten vertices, fifteen edges, girth five, and diameter two. Scales are real and nonnegative, with **closed** metric balls and inclusive threshold conventions. This is the graph Čech nerve, not the Vietoris–Rips complex, the open Lovász neighborhood complex, or a Čech construction on the discrete vertex metric alone. The concrete vertex labels used in the certificate are defined in the embedded `verify.py`.

## Proof

**Facet reduction.** Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] shows that for every finite connected simple graph, \(\mathcal N(G,r)=\mathcal N(G,\lfloor r\rfloor)\) and that the maximal simplices at even integer scale \(2k\) are generated by the radius-\(k\) closed vertex neighborhoods, whereas at odd integer scale \(2k+1\) they are generated by unions of radius-\(k\) closed neighborhoods of two adjacent vertices. Thus \(\mathcal N(P,1)=P\); \(\mathcal N(P,2)\) is generated by the ten four-element sets \(\{v\}\cup N(v)\); and \(\mathcal N(P,3)\) by the fifteen six-element sets \((\{u\}\cup N(u))\cup(\{v\}\cup N(v))\) for edges \(uv\). Because the diameter is two, \(\mathcal N(P,4)\) and all later complexes are full simplices.

**The scale-two inclusion.** Because the Petersen graph has no triangles or squares, any pair of distinct neighbors of a vertex \(v\) has \(v\) as its unique common neighbor. In the closed-neighborhood tetrahedron centered at \(v\), first delete the free triangular face comprising its three neighbors together with its unique tetrahedral coface. Then delete the three neighbor-pair edges, each with its now unique triangular coface containing \(v\). These are four genuine elementary free-face collapses for each of the ten centers: no face on at least two neighbors can be shared with another center. The forty pairs remove all faces of dimension at least two and all edges joining distance-two vertices, leaving exactly the embedded graph \(P\). The relative collapse proves that the inclusion \(\mathcal N(P,1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal N(P,2)\) is a homotopy equivalence, not merely that the two endpoints have equal Betti numbers. Since \(|E(P)|-|V(P)|+1=6\), both have homotopy type \(\bigvee^6 S^1\).

**The odd-scale calculation.** The face set of the fifteen six-element facets above is exhaustively enumerated by `verify.py`. Its face vector is \((10,45,120,195,90,15)\). On the full 475-face poset, process the ten vertices successively in the order \(0,1,\ldots,9\). At stage \(v\), pair every as-yet-unpaired nonempty face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), provided both faces remain unpaired. Delete both from the unmatched pool, and proceed to the next stage. This fully specified finite algorithm produces 219 disjoint covering pairs and leaves precisely one zero-dimensional face and 36 three-dimensional faces unmatched, with no other critical faces. To prove that the matching is acyclic rather than merely count its critical faces, orient all 1-step incidence arrows from a simplex to its facet, reverse precisely the 219 paired incidences, and topologically sort the resulting directed graph. The certificate constructs all 1,770 incidences and checks that the entire 475-node oriented Hasse diagram has a topological order. Hence the matching is acyclic. Forman's discrete-Morse theorem gives a CW model with one zero-cell and thirty-six three-cells and no other cells. Since every attaching map of a three-cell to the zero-skeleton is constant, the model is \(\bigvee^{36}S^3\), as claimed.

**Persistence.** The complex at scale zero is ten isolated points. The scale-one graph is connected, and its inclusion into scale two is a homotopy equivalence by the explicit relative collapse. At scale three, reduced homology is concentrated in degree three, with rank 36, so all six first-homology classes die at this threshold. At scale four a full simplex appears, killing the 36 third-homology classes. Since these are the only thresholds, the barcode statement follows over \(\mathbb Z\) (all groups and maps involved are free in the relevant degrees).

## Verification

The embedded standard-library Python script `verify.py` is a standalone, executable finite certificate: run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing the decoded file. It checks the exact graph, generates every nonempty simplex at scales one through three from the source definitions, replays all forty **elementary** scale-two free-face deletions, constructs all 219 scale-three matching pairs, tests acyclicity on all 1,770 face-poset cover incidences by directed topological sorting, and independently performs mod-two Gaussian elimination. The scale-three mod-two boundary ranks are \((9,36,84,75,15)\), giving ordinary Betti numbers \((1,0,0,36,0,0)\). An independent mod-two rank computation corroborates but is **not** substituted for the integral homotopy proof supplied by the acyclic matching. In the present computation the script returned `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] establish the general graph-ball Čech conventions, the odd/even neighborhood description, and the small-scale nerve-lemma range; their explicit larger-scale classifications concern **hypercube graphs**, not the Petersen graph. This work uses their facet description and credits the known graph-recovery range. Previte [2] computes the Petersen graph's vertex-centered \(D=\{0,1\}\) neighborhood complex with first Betti number six and notes contractibility when all graph distances are included. Those results correspond to even-scale vertex-neighborhood nerves and do not compute the **odd-scale edge-centered** nerve here, nor its complete integral barcode. Petersen-graph matching and cut complexes are different simplicial constructions; their theorems do not imply the Čech statement.

## Limitations

The claim is confined to the ten-vertex Petersen graph with the stated intrinsic graph-geometric Čech convention; it does not classify arbitrary generalized Petersen graphs, Rips complexes, or higher-dimensional Kneser graphs. The scale-three homotopy classification relies on an **exhaustive, checkable finite combinatorial certificate**, not a hand-enumerated uniform infinite-family argument. Independent external validation has not been performed. An unindexed prior equivalent computation could exist; lack of a search hit alone is not a proof of originality.
