# Scientific assessment of the Desargues graph Rips filtration

## Correctness — PASS

The assertion is for the incidence graph on the two- and three-subsets of a five-element set with inclusive vertex shortest-path distance, at every real nonnegative scale. A direct symmetric-difference metric verification and exhaustive clique generation fix the domain and normalization. The new scale-three calculation constructs all faces, checks 1,040 actual elementary free-face reductions, computes 409 exact integral unit pivots with one residual zero column, and proves simple connectivity by reducing every distance-three edge to the already simply connected distance-two complex. These establish an integral homology sphere with a three-dimensional CW model, hence its claimed homotopy type by Hurewicz and Whitehead. At scale four the ten antipodal pairs directly yield the cross-polytope sphere. The scale-two calculation reproduces the established theorem. Computational exhaustion is appropriate here because the object has precisely twenty vertices; executable source checks every step rather than relying on trial examples.

## Originality — PASS, with scoped qualification

Feng–Nukala's Theorem 13 already implies the nineteen-two-sphere result at scale two. The older dodecahedral filtration of Saleh–Titz Mite–Witzel is about a different graph despite matching order and degree; its corresponding three-scale term is a wedge of nine three-spheres. Adamaszek–Adams gives a circle-filtration classification, and Carnero Bravo's graph-product results concern product classes different from the Cartesian Petersen prism. The accepted mathematical addition is the exact scale-three sphere and the complete Desargues persistence barcode, not a new proof of the covered scale-two case or a new claim that antipodal joins are spheres. Search and full-text inspections did not identify a theorem implying the exact prism independence-complex calculation. Residual risk: an unindexed equivalent computation, or a less obviously applicable product-reduction theorem, may exist.

## Value — PASS

The Desargues \((10_3)\) incidence graph is a canonical symmetric cubic graph. Its complete filtration displays nonzero homology in four widely separated degrees, distinguishing it from the dodecahedron with identical vertex and edge counts. The scale-three identification with the Petersen prism independence complex connects distance topology to graph-complex topology and supplies a reproducible exact benchmark. The result is finite but is a complete natural object-specific invariant rather than an arbitrary slice or a restatement of a known table.
