# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The main source's Theorem 9.1 gives the complete irreducible root-lattice local-minimizer list. Equation (9.1) gives two orthogonal Frobenius coefficients for \(D_d\), and equation (9.2) gives a scalar coefficient for \(E_6,E_7,E_8\); the \(A_2\) scalar coefficient is stated in the same proof. The ratio, factorization, discrete difference, and limit in the finding are exact algebraic consequences. The claim is explicitly limited to the source's optimized local second variation.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of the lead paper covered the introduction, the \(D_4\) stability section, Theorem 9.1, and equations (9.1)--(9.2). Targeted literature-index searches were made for the \(D_d\) isoperimetric Hessian, diagonal/off-diagonal stiffnesses, Hessian condition number, unique \(D_4\) isotropy, and the \(3/2\) limiting ratio. The checked lead source states the raw coefficients and local-minimizer classification but not the complete Hessian-isotropy classification or the monotone condition-number law. The closest checked predecessor concerns the three-dimensional BCC/truncated-octahedron problem and does not imply the all-rank statement. Residual risk: because the new statement is an exact synthesis of displayed coefficients, an unindexed note could have recorded the same algebraic consequence.

## Value
PASS. The result converts an all-rank second-variation formula into a structural classification: it pinpoints exactly which root-lattice local minima have direction-independent quadratic stiffness and quantifies every anisotropic \(D_d\) case by a closed, monotone condition number. This identifies \(D_4\) as the sharp transition inside the infinite \(D_d\) family and supplies a dimension-explicit benchmark for local stability estimates.

## Closest literature and limitations
The lead source is Novaga--Song, arXiv:2610.01653v1, especially Theorem 9.1 and equations (9.1)--(9.2). Cesaroni--Novaga's 2026 three-dimensional paper is the closest checked predecessor but studies BCC in a different local parameter space. The present result is local and quadratic only; it does not extend the four-dimensional global minimization theorem to higher dimensions.
