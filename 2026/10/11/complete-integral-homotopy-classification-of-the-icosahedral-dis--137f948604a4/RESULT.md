# Complete integral homotopy classification of the icosahedral distance-neighborhood complexes

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the twelve-vertex icosahedral graph and let \(D\) be any **nonempty** subset of its distance set \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). The distance-neighborhood complex \(\operatorname{DN}(I,D)\), whose simplices are the nonempty subsets of at least one distance-shell union \(N_D(v)=\{w:d_I(v,w)\in D\}\), has one of the following eight integral homotopy types. The table exhausts all fifteen possible \(D\):

| Distance sets \(D\) | Homotopy type of \(\operatorname{DN}(I,D)\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(\{0\},\{3\}\) | Twelve discrete points |
| \(\{0,3\}\) | Six discrete points |
| \(\{1\},\{2\}\) | \(\bigvee^{23}S^2\) |
| \(\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\) | \(S^2\) |
| \(\{1,2\}\) | \(S^4\) |
| \(\{0,1,2\},\{1,2,3\}\) | \(S^{10}\) |
| \(\{0,1,3\},\{0,2,3\}\) | \(\bigvee^{6}S^2\vee\bigvee^{21}S^3\) |
| \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) | A point |

In particular, the omitted-middle-shell cases not covered by the inspected prior tables \(D=\{0,1,3\}\) and \(D=\{0,2,3\}\) have nonzero **integral** reduced homology of ranks six in degree two and twenty-one in degree three, with no torsion or other reduced homology, and their full homotopy type is the displayed mixed-dimensional wedge.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(I\) is the simple connected graph on \(\{N,S,u_0,\ldots,u_4,v_0,\ldots,v_4\}\), with subscripts modulo five. Its edges are exactly \(\{N,u_i\}\), \(\{S,v_i\}\), \(\{u_i,u_{i+1}\}\), \(\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}\), \(\{u_i,v_i\}\) and \(\{u_i,v_{i-1}\}\), for every \(i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). The vertex numbering in the verification programs is \(N=0\), \(u_i=1+i\), \(v_i=6+i\), \(S=11\). The graph has 30 edges, all vertices have degree five, and all distance shells have sizes \((1,5,5,1)\).

The complex is the simplicial union \(\bigcup_{v\in V(I)}\Delta(N_D(v))\); it is not the Vietoris--Rips complex, the flag complex of a distance graph, or the simplicial complex generated by balls unless \(D\) specifies those balls. We classify ordinary non-equivariant homotopy types, over the integers, for every nonempty \(D\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}\). The empty distance set is excluded because it supplies no nonempty simplices.

## Proof
For every vertex \(x\), there is a unique antipodal vertex \(a(x)\) at distance three, and direct calculation from the displayed edges gives \(d_I(a(x),y)=3-d_I(x,y)\) for all vertices \(x,y\). Thus \(N_D(a(x))=N_{3-D}(x)\), where \(3-D=\{3-j:j\in D\}\). Consequently \(\operatorname{DN}(I,D)=\operatorname{DN}(I,3-D)\) as **simplicial complexes**, not merely up to homotopy.

Here is a finite discrete-Morse certificate that proves the remaining cases. Encode a nonempty simplex by a nonzero twelve-bit integer. Enumerate exactly the submasks of every \(N_D(v)\). For vertices in the fixed order \(0,1,\ldots,11\), pair every still-unmatched simplex \(\sigma\) not containing the current vertex with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), provided both are still unmatched. Independently of an abstract acyclicity assertion, `verify_icosa_morse.py` orients every original Hasse cover downwards, reverses each paired cover and checks by a complete topological sort that the resulting finite digraph is acyclic. Thus Forman's discrete-Morse theorem applies. Its exact critical-cell counts, along with the numbers of nonempty faces, are:

| \(D\) | Faces | Critical cells by dimension |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| \(\{0\}\), \(\{3\}\) | 12 | Twelve dimension-zero cells |
| \(\{1\}\), \(\{2\}\) | 264 | One dimension-zero cell, 23 dimension-two cells |
| \(\{0,1\}\), \(\{0,2\}\), \(\{1,3\}\), \(\{2,3\}\) | 446 | One dimension-zero cell, one dimension-two cell |
| \(\{1,2\}\) | 3,366 | One dimension-zero cell, one dimension-four cell |
| \(\{0,1,2\}\), \(\{1,2,3\}\) | 4,094 | One dimension-zero cell, one dimension-ten cell |
| \(\{0,3\}\) | 18 | Six dimension-zero cells |
| \(\{0,1,3\}\), \(\{0,2,3\}\) | 1,006 | One dimension-zero cell, six dimension-two cells, 21 dimension-three cells |
| \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) | 4,095 | One dimension-zero cell |

These critical-cell lists give the asserted homotopy types immediately except for the two mixed-dimensional cases. In those cases the critical two-skeleton is \(\bigvee^6 S^2\). The script computes each signed integral gradient-path coefficient for the attachment of every critical three-cell to each of the six critical two-cells. The resulting integral Morse boundary matrix has **six rows and twenty-one columns, all zero**. These are integral signed sums, not reductions modulo two. Since \(\pi_2(\bigvee^6S^2)\cong H_2(\bigvee^6S^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\) by the Hurewicz theorem, the vanishing of all six degree coordinates makes every three-cell attaching map null-homotopic. This gives \(\bigvee^6 S^2\vee\bigvee^{21} S^3\), with no possible hidden integral torsion.

For the disconnected cases, \(D=\{0\}\) or \(D=\{3\}\) gives twelve isolated vertices, whereas \(D=\{0,3\}\) gives precisely six disjoint edges, each contractible. The full distance set produces the entire eleven-simplex and is contractible. This exhausts all fifteen nonempty choices.

## Verification
The package contains two independently written, standard-library-only Python programs: `verify_icosa_morse.py` and `verify_icosa_homology.py`. Execute `python3 verify_icosa_morse.py` and `python3 verify_icosa_homology.py` in a directory containing both files. They respectively print `ICOSAHEDRAL_DISTANCE_MORSE_PASS` and `ICOSAHEDRAL_DISTANCE_HOMOLOGY_PASS` on success.

The first program reconstructs the complete 30-edge graph, all fifteen complexes, each staged matching, every oriented Hasse incidence and the acyclic topological order; for the two mixed cases it recursively computes the **signed integral** Morse attaching matrix. The largest instance contains 4,095 faces and 24,564 Hasse incidences. Its 1,006-face mixed case has 489 matching pairs, six critical two-cells and 21 critical three-cells; all 4,080 Hasse incidences are checked. The second program independently reconstructs the faces from graph distances using combinations, performs Gaussian elimination on every mod-two simplicial boundary, and obtains reduced Betti ranks \((6,21)\) in degrees \((2,3)\) for the mixed cases. Mod-two ranks alone are not the proof of the integral homotopy assertion; the acyclic matching and signed attaching calculation are.

## Relationship to prior work
Previte's 2014 dissertation introduces the \(D\)-neighborhood construction and explicitly computes icosahedral **field homology** for the two initial ball distance sets \(D=\{0,1\}\) and \(D=\{0,1,2\}\) (Table 3.1, Chapter 3): one degree-two class and one degree-ten class, respectively. Those special-case homology computations are prior results, not claimed here as new. An earlier exact computation of the **ordinary open** icosahedral neighborhood complex already establishes \(\operatorname{DN}(I,\{1\})\simeq\bigvee^{23}S^2\); this is also acknowledged as covered. The present complete classification adds the complementary and mixed distance sets, proves the full integral homotopy types, and in particular computes the mixed-dimensional six-plus-twenty-one wedge. Barmak's 2010 work supplies a foundational topological treatment of graph-associated neighborhood and independence complexes, but does not classify the distance-indexed complexes here.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification for the single icosahedral graph and every nonempty distance subset, not a theorem for all distance-regular or antipodal graphs. It does not compute maps induced by inclusions of distance sets, equivariant homotopy types or a persistence module. The computation supplies explicit reproducible finite certificates; it does not establish that no unindexed source contains an equivalent table. All conclusions are ordinary mathematical assertions and have not received an independent scientific audit.
