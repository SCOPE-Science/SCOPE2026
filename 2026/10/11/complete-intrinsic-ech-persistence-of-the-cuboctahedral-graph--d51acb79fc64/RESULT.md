# Complete intrinsic Čech persistence of the cuboctahedral graph

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the three-dimensional cube graph, let \(C=L(Q_3)\) be its 12-vertex line graph (the cuboctahedral graph), and assign unit length to every edge of \(C\). For real \(r\geq0\), let \(\mathcal N(C,r)\) be the nerve of the closed radius-\(r/2\) balls about the 12 vertices in the geometric realization of \(C\) with its intrinsic shortest-path metric. Then
\[
\mathcal N(C,r)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{12}\{\mathrm{pt}\},&0\leq r<1,\\
\bigvee^{13}S^1,&1\leq r<2,\\
\bigvee^{7}S^2,&2\leq r<3,\\
\bigvee^{5}S^3,&3\leq r<4,\\
S^{10},&4\leq r<5,\\
\{\mathrm{pt}\},&r\geq5.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently, with integral coefficients, the complete reduced persistence module is the direct sum of eleven degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), thirteen degree-one intervals \([1,2)\), seven degree-two intervals \([2,3)\), five degree-three intervals \([3,4)\), and one degree-ten interval \([4,5)\). The same intervals form the barcode over every field. The complexes at scales \(2\) and \(3\) are non-shellable.

## Assumptions and scope
The 12 vertices of \(C\) correspond to the cube edges \((a,b)\), for \(a,b\in\{0,\ldots,7\}\), written in binary, where the endpoints differ in one bit. Two vertices of \(C\) are adjacent if and only if the cube edges share an endpoint. The distance used in the Čech nerve is the shortest-path distance on the full unit-edge *metric graph* \(C\), not the Euclidean chordal metric on a geometric cuboctahedron, the polyhedral surface metric, or the metric on the 12-point vertex set alone. The balls are closed. Homotopy is ordinary, nonequivariant homotopy of geometric realizations.

## Proof
**1. Exact finite-scale description.** For any finite graph with unit edges, the graph Čech nerve is constant for \(r\) between consecutive integers. Lemma 2.2 of Adams--Shukla--Singh (Reference 1) says that at even scale \(r=2k\) the nerve is generated by the sets
\[
F_v=\{w:d_C(w,v)\leq k\},\qquad v\in V(C),
\]
and at odd scale \(r=2k+1\) it is generated by
\[
F_{uv}=\{w:\min(d_C(w,u),d_C(w,v))\leq k\},\qquad uv\in E(C).
\]
The graph \(C\) has 12 vertices, 24 edges, diameter three, and every vertex has exactly one other vertex at distance three. The verifier reconstructs all distances from the cube-edge definition rather than assuming a named-graph library representation.

**2. Scale zero and scale one.** The complex at \(r=0\) is 12 isolated vertices. At \(r=1\) it is the underlying connected graph, whose cycle rank is \(24-12+1=13\).

**3. Scales two and three.** At \(r=2\), the generating facets are the 12 distinct five-vertex closed neighborhoods. Exhaustively expanding their nonempty subsets gives the face vector (in increasing dimensions)
\[
(12,60,104,60,12).
\]
At \(r=3\), the facets are the 24 distinct seven-vertex unions of the closed neighborhoods of the two endpoints of an edge, yielding
\[
(12,66,212,354,300,132,24).
\]
We give an exact acyclic discrete-Morse certificate at each scale. Label the 12 vertices by the lexicographically sorted cube edges
\[
(0,1),(0,2),(0,4),(1,3),(1,5),(2,3),(2,6),(3,7),(4,5),(4,6),(5,7),(6,7).
\]
Encode a face as a 12-bit mask in this order. For an ordered sequence of vertices, perform one stage per vertex \(v\): among currently unmatched faces in increasing integer-mask order, match each face not containing \(v\) with its one-vertex coface obtained by adjoining \(v\), provided both faces remain unmatched. Remove both faces from later stages. Use these stage orders:
\[
\begin{aligned}
r=2 &: (7,6,1,11,5,4,0,10,9,8,3,2),\\
r=3 &: (1,8,2,6,4,11,0,10,5,3,7,9).
\end{aligned}
\]
The resulting scale-two matching contains exactly 120 pairs and leaves one critical zero-cell and seven critical two-cells. The scale-three matching contains exactly 547 pairs and leaves one critical zero-cell and five critical three-cells. For each scale, orient every cover in the nonempty-face Hasse diagram downward, except that matched covers are oriented upward. Complete directed acyclicity checks visit all 248 faces and 732 incidences at scale two and all 1,100 faces and 4,644 incidences at scale three; neither directed graph contains a cycle. The script in Reference 6 reconstructs and checks every pair and directed incidence.

By discrete Morse theory, a complex with one critical zero-cell and only \(m\) critical cells of dimension \(d\geq2\) is homotopy equivalent to the CW complex with one zero-cell and \(m\) \(d\)-cells. Every attaching map is constant because its target \((d-1)\)-skeleton is a point. Thus the homotopy types are, integrally, \(\bigvee^7 S^2\) and \(\bigvee^5 S^3\), respectively.

**4. Scale four and higher.** For each vertex \(v\), there is exactly one antipode \(a(v)\) at distance three; symmetry and uniqueness of the distance-three partner imply that \(a\) is an involutive bijection. Hence the radius-two vertex neighborhood equals \(V(C)\setminus\{a(v)\}\). At scale four all 12 complements of single vertices are generating facets, so the nerve is the boundary of the 11-simplex, namely \(S^{10}\). For any adjacent vertices \(u\ne v\), their two radius-two neighborhoods miss distinct antipodes, and their union is all of \(V(C)\). Thus at scale five the nerve is the full simplex and stays contractible thereafter.

**5. Persistence and shellability.** The nonzero reduced homology degrees at successive integer scales are disjoint: degree one at scale one, degree two at scale two, degree three at scale three, and degree ten at scale four. Therefore every positive-dimensional class dies at the next integer threshold. Reduced degree zero consists of eleven classes until connectivity at scale one. This proves the complete stated integral interval decomposition. At scales two and three the complexes are pure of dimensions four and six but have reduced homology in dimensions two and three respectively; since a pure shellable simplicial complex has reduced homology only possibly in its top dimension, neither is shellable.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_cuboctahedron_cech.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. Its only dependencies are Python's standard library. The program independently constructs the cube-edge graph, obtains all shortest-path distances by breadth-first search, expands every generating facet at scales zero through five, checks the expected face vectors, reconstructs both matching sequences, proves their acyclicity by topological sorting of complete oriented Hasse diagrams, and calculates independent mod-two boundary ranks at scales two and three.

At scale two, the mod-two boundary ranks in degrees one through four are \((11,49,48,12)\). At scale three, those in degrees one through six are \((11,55,157,192,108,24)\). These independently give the stated Betti numbers and agree with the integral homotopy certificates. The verifier asserts that scale four has every proper nonempty face on 12 vertices and that scale five has every nonempty face. Successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams--Shukla--Singh (Reference 1) introduce the intrinsic graph Čech construction and prove the even/odd facet description; their higher-scale classifications concern hypercube graphs, not this *line graph* of a cube. Previte's thesis (Reference 2) defines the equivalent distance-neighborhood construction at even scales but computes examples for other graphs; its Platonic-solid table does not contain the cuboctahedron. Matsushita (Reference 3) investigates closed-neighborhood complexes and structural relations, with explicit product-of-complete-graphs examples, but does not classify this graph or its odd-scale Čech nerve. Goyal--Shukla--Singh (Reference 4) determine clique complexes of line graphs, a different construction.

The previously published Euclidean cuboctahedron Vietoris--Rips filtration (Reference 5) concerns the same 12 geometric vertices but uses **Euclidean chordal distances** and a **flag complex**. It is not equivalent to this intrinsic graph Čech filtration, and its sphere dimensions and scale thresholds differ. Comparisons included the cuboctahedron, line graph of \(Q_3\), rectified cube/octahedron, Čech, closed-neighborhood, and distance-neighborhood aliases. No inspected source gives a theorem implying the exact six-stage homotopy sequence here.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-graph classification, not a formula for every line graph or every Archimedean solid. The matching certificates prove ordinary homotopy types; they do not determine natural symmetry actions or claim a universal classification of filtrations. The literature checks cannot eliminate the possibility of an unindexed or differently formulated equivalent result.
