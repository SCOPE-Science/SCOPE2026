# Complete intrinsic Čech persistence of the truncated tetrahedral graph

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the twelve-vertex truncated tetrahedral graph equipped with its unit-edge intrinsic metric, and let \(\mathcal N(T,r)\) be the nerve of the closed metric balls of radius \(r/2\), one centered at each vertex in the geometric realization of \(T\). For **every real** \(r\geq0\), the homotopy type is
\[
\mathcal N(T,r)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{12}\{\mathrm{pt}\},&0\leq r<1,\\
\bigvee^{7}S^1,&1\leq r<2,\\
\bigvee^{3}S^1,&2\leq r<3,\\
\bigvee^{9}S^2,&3\leq r<4,\\
\left(\bigvee^{11}S^3\right)\vee\left(\bigvee^{4}S^4\right),&4\leq r<5,\\
\bigvee^{5}S^7,&5\leq r<6,\\
\{\mathrm{pt}\},&r\geq6.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, with the usual half-open interval convention, the **complete reduced integral persistence barcode** comprises eleven degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\); four degree-one intervals \([1,2)\) and three degree-one intervals \([1,3)\); nine degree-two intervals \([3,4)\); eleven degree-three and four degree-four intervals \([4,5)\); and five degree-seven intervals \([5,6)\). There are no other reduced bars. All nonzero integral homology groups are free abelian.

The complex at scale four is a mixed-dimensional wedge of spheres, although its maximal simplices have dimension seven. In contrast with the graph-metric Vietoris–Rips filtration of the same graph, which becomes contractible at scale three, the intrinsic Čech nerve retains nontrivial homotopy until scale six.

## Assumptions and scope
Define \(V(T)=\{(a,b):a,b\in\{0,1,2,3\},\ a\neq b\}\). Distinct vertices \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\) are adjacent precisely when \(a=c\) or \((c,d)=(b,a)\). There are twelve vertices, eighteen edges, four triangles, and graph diameter three. Graph edges are unit intervals; balls are taken in the **geometric realization**, not solely in the discrete vertex set and not in a Euclidean embedding of an Archimedean solid. The nerve is indexed by real \(r\ge0\), and its constituent balls are closed.

For a vertex \(v\), write \(B_k(v)=\{w\in V(T):d_T(v,w)\leq k\}\). At a nonnegative even integer \(r=2k\), the nerve is generated by the simplices \(B_k(v)\) as \(v\) varies; at a positive odd integer \(r=2k+1\), it is generated by \(B_k(u)\cup B_k(v)\) as \(uv\) varies over graph edges. These are the graph Čech facet rules of Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2. The nerve remains constant on each half-open interval between successive integers.

## Proof
Construct the adjacency matrix from the ordered-pair definition and run breadth-first search from each vertex. It gives all pairwise graph distances and diameter three. Applying the even/odd facet rules and closing under subsets produces the **complete** face sets, not sampled subcomplexes. In the table, the face vector \((f_0,f_1,\ldots)\) counts nonempty simplices by dimension; \(m\) is the number of matched face–coface pairs; and the critical-cell vector lists \((\text{dimension}:\text{count})\) for a discrete-Morse matching:

| \(r\) | Number of maximal facets | Face vector | \(m\) | Critical cells |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| 0 | 12 | (12) | 0 | (0:12) |
| 1 | 18 | (12,18) | 11 | (0:1,1:7) |
| 2 | 12 | (12,42,40,12) | 51 | (0:1,1:3) |
| 3 | 18 | (12,66,148,126,48,6) | 198 | (0:1,2:9) |
| 4 | 12 | (12,66,220,486,552,322,96,12) | 871 | (0:1,3:15,4:8) |
| 5 | 18 | (12,66,220,495,792,924,792,465,148,18) | 1963 | (0:1,7:5) |
| 6 | 1 | (12,66,220,495,792,924,792,495,220,66,12,1) | 2047 | (0:1) |

For each integer scale, enumerate all nonempty faces as twelve-bit masks. Scan vertices in ascending index order, and for each vertex pair every still-unmatched face not containing it to its still-unmatched one-vertex extension, when present. The attached `verify_cech.py` reconstructs **every** pair from this deterministic prescription. It checks that every pair has codimension one, that no face occurs twice, and that the full directed Hasse graph—matched incidences reversed, unmatched incidences downward—is acyclic. The respective exhaustive Hasse-incidence totals for scales zero through six are \(0,36,252,1356,8196,23052,24564\). The entire test, including all matching and Morse-differential checks, is recomputed from the graph; it is not based on logs of a previous calculation.

Forman's theorem gives a CW model with exactly those critical cells. Scales one and two therefore yield wedges of seven and three circles. At scale three, only one zero-cell and nine two-cells remain, and at scale five only one zero-cell and five seven-cells remain; these are the stated sphere wedges. At scale six, the complex is the full simplex on twelve vertices and contractible.

At scale four the Morse CW model has one zero-cell, fifteen three-cells and eight four-cells. Its three-skeleton is \(\bigvee^{15}S^3\). Signed gradient-path reduction of the **oriented integral simplicial boundary** gives a \(15\times8\) attaching-degree matrix \(A\). It has rank four over \(\mathbb Q\), thirty nonzero entries, and the \(4\times4\) submatrix with zero-based rows \((0,1,2,4)\) and columns \((2,3,4,5)\) has determinant \(+1\). A rank-four integral matrix with a unit rank-four minor has exactly four nonzero Smith invariants, all equal to one. Since \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{15}S^3)\cong H_3(\bigvee^{15}S^3;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{15}\), the degree matrix determines the attaching maps. Integral changes of cellular bases and cancellation of the four unit attaching maps reduce the model to \(\bigvee^{11}S^3\vee\bigvee^{4}S^4\). This also rules out integral torsion; a mere rational-rank computation would not.

For the barcode, the graph at scale one has cycle rank \(18-12+1=7\). The inclusion \(\mathcal N(T,1)\hookrightarrow\mathcal N(T,2)\) induces a **surjection** on integral first homology: each edge newly appearing at scale two joins vertices at graph distance two, so a common neighbor supplies a Čech two-simplex relating that edge to an original two-edge path. The first-homology kernel consequently has rank four, giving exactly four bars ending at two and three surviving to three. At every later adjacent pair of scales the positive-dimensional reduced homology occurs in disjoint degrees, so all the remaining nonzero groups die at the next integer. The eleven extra components die when the graph becomes connected at scale one. The facet rule establishes that no changes occur between integer thresholds.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_cech.py` using a standard Python 3 interpreter; no third-party libraries or stored data files are needed. It produces one diagnostic line for each integer scale and ends in `VERIFY_OK`, or raises an exception if a count, acyclicity check, signed boundary, integral rank/unit minor, filtration inclusion, or independent mod-two Betti number check fails. The checker independently eliminates the **original simplicial boundary matrices** over \(\mathbb F_2\), rather than relying solely on critical cells. It recomputes the signed integral Morse boundary using recursively enumerated gradient paths and verifies the full-scale mixed-dimensional ranks and unit minor.

The matched face–coface pairs are generated algorithmically, not merely asserted. The critical Morse-boundary argument is finite and complete, and its geometric interpretation invokes standard Forman discrete-Morse and cellular-attachment theorems. No assertion about spheres at other graphs, metrics, or scales is implied.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh supply the intrinsic graph Čech definition and even/odd facet rules; their higher-scale homotopy classifications are for hypercube graphs and do **not** specialize to the truncated tetrahedral graph. Previte's \(D\)-neighborhood construction includes the even scales of the present nerve as a special case; her explicit tables concern Petersen and Platonic graphs, not this twelve-vertex Archimedean graph. Saleh, Titz Mite and Witzel study Vietoris–Rips filtrations for the five Platonic solids and motivate extension to Archimedean solids, but neither their simplicial construction nor their graph family supplies this Čech filtration.

The published graph-metric **Vietoris–Rips** filtration of this same truncated tetrahedral graph is especially close. Its scale-two homotopy type is \(\bigvee^5 S^2\), whereas the scale-two intrinsic Čech nerve here is \(\bigvee^3 S^1\). The two filtrations have different simplices and non-isomorphic integral homology in the same scale, so neither cited Rips classification nor its numbers implies the Čech results. Search comparisons also considered alternative names “closed-ball nerve,” “closed neighborhood complex,” “\(D\)-neighborhood complex,” and “ordered-pair tetrahedral truncation graph.” No searched source supplied the displayed Čech statement; that negative result is evidence of a gap, **not a proof of absolute novelty**.

## Limitations
This theorem is limited to the intrinsic unit-edge metric on the twelve-vertex truncated tetrahedral graph and to vertex-centered **closed** balls. It is not about Euclidean chordal distance, vertex-only balls, a different truncation, or general Archimedean solids. The discrete-Morse matchings are constructive but not claimed canonical or equivariant. The reported homotopy types are intrinsic integral statements; the persistence description is the corresponding reduced integral homology filtration, not a claim about a canonical persistent homotopy invariant. As with any literature search, an unindexed equivalent computation remains possible. Independent external assessment has not been performed.
