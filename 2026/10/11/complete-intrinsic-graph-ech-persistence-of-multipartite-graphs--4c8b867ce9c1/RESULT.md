# Complete intrinsic graph Čech persistence of multipartite graphs, including their symmetry modules

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge2\) and every tuple \(n_1,\dots,n_k\ge2\), let \(G=K_{n_1,\dots,n_k}\) be the complete multipartite graph with ordered parts \(P_i\) of cardinality \(n_i\). Give every edge length one. Set \(v=\sum_i n_i\), \(e=\sum_{i<j}n_i n_j\), and \(b=\prod_i(n_i-1)\). The intrinsic graph Čech complex \(\mathcal N(G,r)\) has the following complete homotopy classification for all real \(r\ge0\):

\[
\mathcal N(G,r)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{v}\{*\},&0\le r<1,\\
\bigvee^{e-v+1}S^1,&1\le r<2,\\
\bigvee^{b}S^{2k-2},&2\le r<3,\\
\{*\},&r\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

All integral reduced homology groups are free. The nonempty reduced persistence bars are precisely \(v-1\) copies of \([0,1)\) in degree zero, \(e-v+1\) copies of \([1,2)\) in degree one, and \(b\) copies of \([2,3)\) in degree \(2k-2\). There are no other reduced bars. For the natural action of the part-preserving group \(H=\prod_i S_{n_i}\) on the unique nonzero reduced homology group at scale two, there is an isomorphism of integral \(H\)-modules

\[
\widetilde H_{2k-2}(\mathcal N(G,2);\mathbb Z)
\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k} U_i,
\qquad U_i=\ker\!\left(\mathbb Z^{n_i}\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z\right).
\]

Here each symmetric group factor permutes the standard basis of its own \(\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), and acts trivially on the other factors. The assertion does not describe the extra action of permutations among equal-sized parts.

## Assumptions and scope
The unit-edge metric is on the **geometric realization of the graph**, not merely on its discrete vertex set. For each vertex \(x\), use the **closed** ball of radius \(r/2\) around \(x\); \(\mathcal N(G,r)\) is the abstract nerve of this finite family. Filtration intervals are left-closed, right-open and all coefficients are integral unless explicitly stated. We require \(k\ge2\), and \(n_i\ge2\) for every part, in order to obtain the asserted nonzero top homology. Part labels are fixed for the indicated symmetry statement.

## Proof
**1. Critical scales.** The graph Čech-neighborhood characterization of Adams, Shukla and Singh (Lemma 2.2 in the cited preprint) states that for integer \(r=2s\), the complex is generated by the vertex sets of closed graph-distance \(s\) balls, and for \(r=2s+1\), by unions of graph-distance \(s\) balls around adjacent vertices. It also proves that \(\mathcal N(G,r)=\mathcal N(G,\lfloor r\rfloor)\) for all \(r\ge0\). At \(r=0\), the nerve is the set of \(v\) isolated vertices. At \(r=1\), its simplices are vertices and edges of \(G\). Because \(G\) is connected, its first Betti number is \(e-v+1\), and its graph realization is a wedge of that many circles.

For any edge \(xy\) with endpoints in different parts, the union of the two closed graph neighborhoods \(N_1[x]\cup N_1[y]\) equals **every vertex**: \(N_1[x]=V(G)\setminus(P_i\setminus\{x\})\), and analogously for \(y\in P_j\), \(i\ne j\). Consequently, the odd-scale description makes \(\mathcal N(G,3)\) the full simplex on \(V(G)\), hence it and all later complexes are contractible.

**2. Exact scale-two faces.** Let \(D_i\) be the zero-dimensional complex of all singleton vertices of \(P_i\) (together with the empty face), and let \(\Delta_i\) be the full simplex on \(P_i\). Since \(N_1[x]=(V(G)\setminus P_i)\cup\{x\}\) for \(x\in P_i\), the even-scale description shows that a vertex subset \(\sigma\) is a face of \(\mathcal N(G,2)\) if and only if \(|\sigma\cap P_i|\le1\) for at least one index \(i\). Therefore

\[
\mathcal N(G,2)=\bigcup_{i=1}^k
\left(\Delta_1*\cdots*\Delta_{i-1}*D_i*\Delta_{i+1}*\cdots*\Delta_k\right).
\tag{1}
\]

No unproved nerve-theorem condition is used in this identification: it follows from the explicit distance-ball description, including the equality cases at integer thresholds.

**3. Suspensions and homotopy type.** Write \(T_j\) for the complex (1) restricted to the first \(j\) parts. Then \(T_1=D_1\), and for \(j\ge2\), decompose

\[
T_j=(T_{j-1}*\Delta_j)\ \cup\
((\Delta_1*\cdots*\Delta_{j-1})*D_j).
\]

The intersection is exactly \(T_{j-1}*D_j\). Both terms are contractible, because they each have a nonempty simplex as one join factor. The inclusions are simplicial cofibrations; the ordinary union computes the homotopy pushout. Since both targets are contractible, the union is homotopy equivalent to the unreduced suspension of its intersection. Thus

\[
T_j\simeq\Sigma(T_{j-1}*D_j),\qquad
T_k\simeq\Sigma^{k-1}(D_1*\cdots*D_k).
\tag{2}
\]

Each \(D_i\) is a based wedge of \(n_i-1\) copies of \(S^0\). Repeated join/smash-product equivalence (or a cellular induction) gives \(D_1*\cdots*D_k\simeq\bigvee^b S^{k-1}\). Applying the \(k-1\) suspensions in (2) proves \(T_k\simeq\bigvee^b S^{2k-2}\) for every parameter tuple, including \(k=2\) and \(n_i=2\).

**4. Integral symmetry module.** The decomposition (1), the two contractible subcomplexes and their intersection in (2) are natural under permutations **within** each part. The Mayer–Vietoris connecting homomorphism is therefore \(H\)-equivariant. Its inductive suspension shift identifies the top integral homology with that of the join of the \(D_i\). The integral join Künneth isomorphism, without Tor terms because these groups are free, identifies the latter with \(\bigotimes_i\widetilde H_0(D_i;\mathbb Z)\). Each \(\widetilde H_0(D_i;\mathbb Z)\) is precisely the augmentation kernel \(U_i\) with coordinate-permutation action. No signs are introduced by automorphisms fixing every part setwise, because the order of join factors and of the recursive Mayer–Vietoris decomposition is fixed. This yields the stated integral representation.

**5. Persistence maps.** The only nonzero reduced groups at the four integer steps are respectively in degrees \(0\), \(1\), \(2k-2\), and none; because \(2k-2\ge2\), positive-degree groups on adjacent steps occur in distinct degrees. Consequently every nonzero reduced class born at one step dies at the next. All integer step multiplicities follow from the homotopy formulas, completing the barcode.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_cech_multipartite.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages. The program independently constructs nerve complexes from the exact graph-neighborhood generators at scales zero through three, calculates simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) using complete row reduction, and exhaustively checks the scale-two face criterion for eight tuples: \((2,2)\), \((3,2)\), \((3,3)\), \((2,2,2)\), \((3,2,2)\), \((3,3,2)\), \((2,2,2,2)\), and \((3,2,2,2)\). It returns `VERIFY_OK`. The size and degree of the computed nonzero groups agree with the theorem in every tested case; for instance, \(K_{2,2,2}\) has one degree-four class at scale two, whereas \(K_{3,3,2}\) has four. These computations are finite cross-checks, not a substitute for the unrestricted proof in (1)–(2). The integral and equivariant assertions follow from the proved homotopy decomposition and naturality, not from the mod-two tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh's *Čech complexes of hypercube graphs* (first public 12 December 2022, arXiv:2212.05871) supplies the graph Čech definition, general integer-threshold facet description (Lemma 2.2), and classification results specifically for hypercube graphs. None of its stated theorems gives the complete-multipartite wedge formula, all-scale barcode or integral within-part symmetry module. The definition alone does not compute the homotopy type.

Previte's 2014 thesis *The D-neighborhood complex of a graph* identifies even-scale graph Čech complexes with a \(D\)-neighborhood construction for \(D=\{0,1\}\) and mentions multipartite graph homology as a conjectural direction (Chapter 8, open questions). Its main proved families are other graph classes (not the complete multipartite filtration theorem here). This earlier motivation is credited. Published records on Morse and matching complexes of complete bipartite graphs concern different face sets, so their sphere counts do not imply this Čech classification. The statement is a specific consequence of the new cover/suspension argument, not a relabeling of a known hypercube result.

## Limitations
The theorem requires \(n_i\ge2\) and makes no claim about disconnected one-part graphs. The equivariant statement addresses only the subgroup preserving every part individually; equal-size-part interchange characters are not computed. It is a graph-realization Čech filtration; Euclidean point-cloud Čech or discrete-metric Čech constructions are different and are not covered. Search-based originality comparison cannot exclude all unindexed equivalent results. No independent audit has been performed.
