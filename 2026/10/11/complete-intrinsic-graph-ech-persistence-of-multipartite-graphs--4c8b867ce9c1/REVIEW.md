## Correctness — PASS
The finite-simple-graph Čech convention is unambiguous (closed balls in unit-edge geometric realization), and the integer-threshold graph-neighborhood description is Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2212.05871). At scale two, the face criterion is proved in both directions and produces the exact union of \(k\) polyhedral joins. For each \(j\ge2\), two contractible subcomplexes intersect in the indicated join; subcomplex inclusions are cofibrations, so the homotopy-pushout suspension is justified. Integral join Künneth yields torsion-free top homology with the claimed dimension and part-preserving representation. The scale-one graph rank and scale-three full simplex establish the persistence endpoints. All quantifiers \(k\ge2\), \(n_i\ge2\), \(r\ge0\) and equality at the thresholds were checked. The extracted finite verifier returned `VERIFY_OK` on eight tuples, with exact mod-two ranks. This verification does not purport to prove the infinite theorem on its own.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The inspected full accessible arXiv HTML primary text (arXiv:2212.05871v2, introduction, Lemma 2.2, main theorem statements, persistence, references and open questions) treats **hypercube graphs** and provides general construction tools but not this complete-multipartite homotopy/persistence/symmetry classification. The inspected 2014 Previte dissertation (table of contents, Chapter 3 material, Chapter 8 open questions, relevant term searches) explicitly points to multipartite \(D\)-neighborhood homology as a conjectural direction without establishing the family claimed here. The closest published published-finding corpus hits have Morse, matching, cut or Rips complexes rather than the ball nerve. Multiple exact, alias and stronger-coverage searches identified no claim implying this theorem. Search incompleteness and an equivalent theorem under another name are residual risks; the novelty assessment is not an assertion of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
A complete, parameter-unbounded and integrally equivariant classification is given for a natural graph family exactly at the first Čech threshold where the one-dimensional graph shape fails to persist. Its homology jumps to a sphere dimension \(2k-2\) governed by the **number of parts**, not by the potentially much larger total vertex count; the precise multiplicity and symmetric-group augmentation lattice can distinguish balanced and unbalanced graphs. This answers a motivated complete-multipartite special family in a topic Previte flagged as conjectural and supplies its full barcode, rather than only face counts or a routine finite example.
