# Complete intrinsic graph Čech persistence of the Heawood graph

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the fourteen-vertex Heawood graph with unit-length geometric edges, and let \(\mathcal N(H,r)\) be the nerve of its closed metric balls of radius \(r/2\), centered at graph vertices. For every real \(r\ge0\), the homotopy types are
\[
\mathcal N(H,r)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{14}\{*\},&0\le r<1,\\
\bigvee^{8}S^1,&1\le r<3,\\
\bigvee^{48}S^2,&3\le r<4,\\
\bigvee^{64}S^4,&4\le r<5,\\
\{*\},&r\ge5.
\end{cases}
\]
The complete reduced **integral** persistence barcode has thirteen degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), eight degree-one intervals \([1,3)\), forty-eight degree-two intervals \([3,4)\), and sixty-four degree-four intervals \([4,5)\), and no others. In particular the first scale at which girth-based graph recovery ceases gives a wedge of forty-eight two-spheres; the next scale gives sixty-four four-spheres.

## Assumptions and scope
Identify the points of the Fano plane with \(P=\{p_0,\ldots,p_6\}\) and its lines with \(L=\{\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_6\}\). There is an edge between \(p_i\) and \(\ell_j\) precisely when \((i-j)\bmod7\in\{0,1,3\}\). The two vertex types are disjoint, all graph edges have length one, and metric balls are taken in the **geometric realization** of this graph, not merely in its vertex metric space. An endpoint belongs to a closed ball. The symbol \(\mathcal N(H,r)\) refers to this intrinsic graph Čech construction and not to the Lovász open-neighborhood complex or to a Vietoris–Rips complex.

Each vertex has three neighbors. Every pair of distinct points determines exactly one line, and dually every pair of lines intersects at one point. Consequently the graph has twenty-one edges, girth six and diameter three. Integer-threshold constancy follows from the graph Čech facet description: at even threshold \(2t\) the complex is generated by closed graph-distance-\(t\) neighborhoods; at odd threshold \(2t+1\) it is generated by unions of these neighborhoods across graph edges [1, Lemma 2.2]. Thus no unlisted real scale changes the complex.

## Proof
**Scales zero through two.** At scale zero there are fourteen disjoint vertices. At scale one the complex is the incidence graph, a connected graph of cycle rank \(21-14+1=8\). At scale two the generating facets are the fourteen tetrahedra \(\{x\}\cup N_H(x)\). For every vertex \(x\), its three neighbors form a unique triangular face of this tetrahedron; collapse that triangle with its tetrahedron. Then every pair of different vertices in the same bipartition class has one common neighbor \(x\). Collapse its newly free distance-two edge together with the triangle through \(x\). The fourteen first pairs and forty-two second pairs are all elementary free-face collapses, relative to the original incidence graph. The result is exactly \(H\). In particular, the inclusion from scale one to scale two induces an isomorphism on integral first homology.

**Scale three.** By the odd-threshold formula the facets are
\[
F_{p\ell}=\bigl(\{p\}\cup N_H(p)\bigr)\cup\bigl(\{\ell\}\cup N_H(\ell)\bigr),\qquad p\sim\ell.
\]
There are twenty-one distinct facets of six vertices each. Exhaustive subface generation gives the face vector \((14,91,308,287,126,21)\) in dimensions zero through five, or 847 nonempty simplices. An explicit acyclic discrete-Morse matching is specified completely by the vertex order
\[
(9,11,10,0,4,2,6,1,3,13,12,8,5,7),
\]
with vertices \(0,\ldots,6\) representing \(p_0,\ldots,p_6\), and \(7,\ldots,13\) representing \(\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_6\). At each successive vertex \(v\), match every still-unmatched nonempty simplex \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) if both are present. This gives exactly 399 face-coface pairs; the unmatched simplices comprise one vertex and forty-eight triangles. The self-contained verifier in `verify.py` enumerates every one of the 3,010 codimension-one incidences, orients matched incidences upward and all others downward, and proves by topological sorting that the full directed Hasse graph is acyclic. Forman's discrete-Morse theorem therefore gives a CW complex with one zero-cell, forty-eight two-cells and no other cells. All two-cell attaching maps have a point as target, so the complex is \(\bigvee^{48}S^2\). This is an exhaustive proof for this fixed finite graph, not an extrapolation from sampled cases.

**Scale four.** Let \(B_P\) be the simplicial complex on the seven points generated by the seven Fano-line triples; let \(B_L\) be its point-line dual. Let \(\Delta(P)\) and \(\Delta(L)\) be full simplices. Since the distance-two neighborhood of a point consists of all seven points and its three incident lines, and dually for a line, the scale-four complex decomposes exactly as
\[
\mathcal N(H,4)=\bigl(\Delta(P)*B_L\bigr)\cup\bigl(B_P*\Delta(L)\bigr),
\qquad
\bigl(\Delta(P)*B_L\bigr)\cap\bigl(B_P*\Delta(L)\bigr)=B_P*B_L.
\]
Both union summands are contractible. The cofibration homotopy pushout gives \(\mathcal N(H,4)\simeq\Sigma(B_P*B_L)\). Each of \(B_P,B_L\) has seven vertices, twenty-one edges and seven triangular faces, with every edge belonging to exactly one triangular face. Collapsing one edge with each triangle leaves a connected graph of cycle rank \(14-7+1=8\). Hence \(B_P\simeq B_L\simeq\bigvee^8 S^1\), so their join is \(\bigvee^{64}S^3\) and its suspension is \(\bigvee^{64}S^4\). Independently, the embedded verifier constructs all 7,919 nonempty scale-four faces, checks a 3,927-pair acyclic matching with one critical vertex and sixty-four critical four-simplices, and checks exact mod-two homology.

**Scale five onward.** For every incident point-line edge, the union of the closed graph-distance-two neighborhoods of its endpoints contains all seven points (within distance two of the point endpoint) and all seven lines (within distance two of the line endpoint). Therefore the scale-five complex is the full thirteen-simplex and remains so thereafter.

The reduced integral barcodes follow: the first-homology inclusion from scales one to two is an isomorphism by the relative collapse, and every other nonzero reduced homology group occurs at one consecutive integer threshold only. There are no coefficient-dependent extensions because every nonzero group is free by the proved homotopy types.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using only the Python standard library. The script independently reconstructs all five subcritical complexes from the Fano difference set; checks the line incidence axioms and face vectors; replays all 56 relative free-face collapse pairs at scale two; reconstructs every pair at scales three and four; checks each complete oriented Hasse graph for cycles; and independently performs exact binary Gaussian elimination on the simplicial boundary matrices at scales three and four. It verifies respectively 399 and 3,927 matching pairs with 3,010 and 45,122 directed Hasse incidences. Successful execution ends with the literal line `VERIFY_OK`. These are exact finite certificates, and the source code rather than a log is the reproducibility artifact.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] define the intrinsic graph Čech filtration and prove the even- and odd-threshold facet formulas used here; their detailed homotopy results concern hypercube graphs rather than the Heawood incidence graph. Their girth-based recovery theorem proves the graph homotopy type for scales strictly below three but does not cover the scale-three boundary computed here. The published incidence-graph Vietoris–Rips result [2] computes a different complex, namely the scale-two *clique* complex, on projective-plane incidence graphs; its suspension formula does not imply the Čech results at scales three or four. Hughes [3] studies distance-set neighborhood complexes of Cayley graphs, usually with distance set \(\{0,1\}\), and does not compute the present metric Čech filtration or the Fano incidence graph's indicated homotopy types. The proof above does not use any earlier topological assertion about this particular Čech filtration.

## Limitations
The conclusion concerns exactly the fourteen-vertex Fano-plane incidence graph and its intrinsic vertex-centered Čech complexes; it makes no assertion for projective planes of greater order, for arbitrary cubic girth-six graphs, or for Vietoris–Rips complexes. Finite Hasse-graph acyclicity checks establish the scale-three homotopy type for this fixed finite object; they do not constitute an infinite-family theorem. Bibliographic search cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent computation or a differently named distance-neighborhood theorem, so independent literature review remains worthwhile.
