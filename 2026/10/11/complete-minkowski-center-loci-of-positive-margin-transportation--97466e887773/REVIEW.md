**Correctness — PASS.** For every fixed matrix coordinate, the largest feasible value is exactly the minimum of its row and column margin, attained by a complete-support residual outer-product filling. Minkowski inclusion becomes the coordinatewise bound on the candidate center; feasibility of that bound is equivalent to the nonnegativity of all residual row and column sums. These facts establish the exact scalar threshold and the entire translated residual transportation-polytope locus. The dimension proof deletes zero residual rows and columns, then uses a strictly positive residual rank-one table to certify relative interior and the rank of the row/column equations. The minimum-margin reduction and square invariance follow from monotonicity. Exact-rational checks and independent finite-dimensional LP comparisons are corroborative, not universal proof.

**Originality — PASS (literature-qualified).** The strongest close general source is den Hertog–Pauphilet–Soali, which already gives a robust-optimization linear program for the Minkowski center of *any* polyhedron. Belloni–Freund likewise established generic symmetry-function and LP methods. The general LP claim is excluded from any priority claim. The present exact elimination of that LP for arbitrary positive transportation margins, complete classification of all centers by residual transport polytopes and their affine dimensions, and the square-margin invariance are not found as statements in these inspected sources. The transportation-polytope-specific papers inspected study combinatorial vertices, faces and monotone paths rather than these invariants. Remaining risk: the exact formula may be derivable as an unpublished, undocumented or differently named known specialization; research-search nonappearance is not a proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete natural-class solution rather than a generic algorithm or a single computed instance. It replaces polyhedral optimization by the two smallest margins and finite sums, describes all optimal centers and their uniqueness for every positive margin vector, and identifies the unexpected rigidity that asymmetry of every square transportation polytope equals its size minus one even when center-space dimension changes. The precise class restrictions are necessary: structural zeros invalidate the cell-max and residual-fill arguments.
