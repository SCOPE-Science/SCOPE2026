# Complete mod-two one-cosystolic spectrum of Dyck's regular genus-three map
## Finding
Let \(K\) be Dyck's regular twelve-vertex triangulation of the orientable genus-three surface, in the Köhler--Lutz labeling \({}^{2}12^{113}_{1}\). For a nonzero class \(\alpha\in H^1(K;\mathbf F_2)\), define its edge-support weight by
\[
w(\alpha)=\min\{\lvert\operatorname{supp}(z)\rvert:z\in Z^1(K;\mathbf F_2),\ [z]=\alpha\}.
\]
Then \(H^1(K;\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^6\), and the full simplicial automorphism group of order \(192\) has exactly five orbits on the \(63\) nonzero cohomology classes. The orbit data, written as
\[
(\text{minimum weight},\text{class-orbit size},\text{number of minimizing cocycles per class}),
\]
are exactly
\[
(6,16,1),\quad(10,24,4),\quad(12,6,8),\quad(14,16,18),\quad(16,1,12).
\]
In particular, the mod-two one-cosystole is \(6\). Across each class orbit, the union of all minimizing supports decomposes into automorphism orbits as follows: at weights \(6,10,12,14,16\), respectively, the support-orbit sizes are
\[
16;\qquad 48+48;\qquad24+24;\qquad96+192;\qquad12.
\]
Thus the unique automorphism-invariant nonzero cohomology class is the class whose minimum support has size \(16\), while every one of the sixteen cosystolic classes of weight \(6\) has a unique minimizing cocycle.

## Assumptions and scope
The simplicial complex is the exact twelve-vertex labeled model `manifold_2_12_113_1`, with the thirty-two triangles listed in `artifacts/spectrum.json` and `artifacts/verify.py`. The edge set is induced by those triangles and has \(48\) edges. Cochains, cocycles, coboundaries, and cohomology are over \(\mathbf F_2\). Weight means the number of edges on which a cocycle is nonzero; it is a combinatorial support metric, not a metric-geometric systole.

The automorphism group is generated by the four published permutations for the transitive group `t12n113(192)`. The verifier reconstructs the generated group rather than assuming its order, checks that every generated permutation preserves the facet set, and then computes its induced action on cohomology and on minimizing supports.

## Proof
Index the \(48\) edges and encode a one-cochain as a binary vector of length \(48\). The thirty-two triangle cocycle equations have rank \(31\), so
\[
\dim Z^1(K;\mathbf F_2)=48-31=17.
\]
The vertex coboundaries have rank \(11\), hence
\[
\dim H^1(K;\mathbf F_2)=17-11=6.
\]
This independently agrees with the genus-three topology of the surface.

Choose an exact basis of \(Z^1\) whose first eleven vectors span \(B^1\). There are therefore exactly \(2^{17}=131072\) cocycles and exactly \(64\) cohomology classes, indexed by the six quotient coordinates. Exhaustive enumeration of all cocycles gives, for the \(63\) nonzero classes, the complete pair distribution
\[
16\times(6,1),\quad24\times(10,4),\quad6\times(12,8),\quad16\times(14,18),\quad1\times(16,12),
\]
where each pair records minimum support size and number of minimizers in that class.

Next generate the group from the four published permutations. Its order is exactly \(192\), and every element preserves all thirty-two triangles. Transporting cocycles by edge permutation and reducing modulo coboundaries yields five nonzero cohomology orbits of sizes \(16,24,6,16,1\). The minimum weight and minimizer count are constant on each orbit, giving the five triples stated above. Finally, exhaustively transport every minimizing support. The resulting support-orbit partitions are exactly \(16\), \(48+48\), \(24+24\), \(96+192\), and \(12\), respectively.

## Verification
Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py`. It reconstructs the edge set from the published facets; performs binary row reduction; exhausts all \(131072\) cocycles; regenerates the \(192\)-element automorphism group; checks facet preservation; and recomputes both the class and minimizing-support orbit decompositions. The recorded output is `artifacts/verification_output.txt`. The exact finite certificate data are also summarized in `artifacts/spectrum.json`.

## Relationship to prior work
Dyck's regular genus-three map is classical. Köhler and Lutz identify the exact twelve-vertex vertex-transitive triangulation \({}^{2}12^{113}_{1}\) as Dyck's regular map, give its one-orbit triangle presentation, and list the transitive group `t12n113(192)` together with generators. Their full text contains no occurrence of “cocycle,” “cohomology,” or “systole.” Earlier work by Schulte--Wills, Brehm, and Bokowski studies geometric realizations and symmetry of Dyck's map, rather than minimum supports of simplicial cohomology classes.

Targeted searches under the aliases “Dyck regular map,” the Köhler--Lutz census label, cocycle support, cosystole, cohomology-code distance, and the numerical fingerprint returned no statement implying the five-orbit support spectrum above. The closest indexed topology records concern different complexes or different invariants, so they do not imply this claim.

## Limitations
The theorem is for this fixed twelve-vertex triangulation and for coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). It does not claim an invariant of all triangulations of the genus-three surface, nor a geometric length statement for any Euclidean realization. The originality comparison is strong against the exact census source and targeted indexed literature, but an unindexed historical computation of the same finite support spectrum remains a residual risk. The body of Brehm's 1987 realization paper was not available in the inspected source; its abstract is geometric and does not suggest the present cohomological calculation.
