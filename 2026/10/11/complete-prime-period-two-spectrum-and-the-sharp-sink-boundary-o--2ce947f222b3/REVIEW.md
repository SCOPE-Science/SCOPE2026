# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The two-step equations are exactly factored into a complete dichotomy: either the orbit is antipodal, or its coordinates satisfy \(uv=1+b\) and \(u^2+v^2=a\). Every real solution is accounted for, including the collision with fixed points at \(a=2(1+b)\). The return determinant \(b^2\) and traces are obtained directly from the Jacobians. Applying the strict quadratic Schur inequalities proves the sink window, the exact flip multipliers, and the saddle regimes. The standalone checker reproduces sample computations; the proof does not rely on sampling.

## Originality

PASS with bounded residual risk. The matched full-text 2026 Coomes–Koçak–Palmer treatment discusses the same map and the onset at \(2(1+b)\), but its Section 3.2 gives no exact full prime-period-two classification or upper stability surface; it instead treats homoclinic tangencies. The accessible 2004 Kraut–Grebogi abstract focuses on noise robustness, rather than exact orbit enumeration. The accessible 2017 Chen–Wang–Li abstract focuses on fixed-point bifurcation and Marotto chaos. Direct search for the exact trace and stability formula did not locate a stronger covering result. The 2004 and 2017 complete texts were not successfully retrieved; an unindexed equivalent formula remains a risk, not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural complete classification for the first nontrivial period of a well-studied, invertible, dissipative two-dimensional cubic map. The exact two-parameter terminal sink surface distinguishes the loss of stability of a particular period-two branch from the emergence and coexistence of other attractors in the classic numerical bifurcation diagrams. It also identifies an always-present antiphase saddle, making precise the count of stable and unstable period-two orbits through both branch boundaries. No downstream theorem or entire-period classification is claimed.

## Limits and literature

The upper endpoint is nonhyperbolic; nonlinear behavior at equality is unproved. Original historical studies were not all fully accessible and possible equivalent parameterizations remain. References and distinctions are supplied in RESULT.md.
