# Same-model mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The defined 18-vertex LCF graph has 27 edges, is bipartite, and has diameter 4. Exact BFS proves that distance-four pairs partition into six disjoint 3-vertex cliques. Therefore the induced scale-two graph in each color class is \(K_{3,3,3}\). At radius two a genuine two-subcomplex cover has simply connected pieces each homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^8 S^2\), with intersection the original connected 10-cycle-rank graph; its homotopy pushout is \(\bigvee^{26} S^2\). The radius-three complex is exactly the join of six discrete 3-point complexes, yielding \(\bigvee^{64} S^5\). Radius one and radius four follow directly. The actual standalone script exhaustively checks distance profiles, the cover, simplex dimensions, mod-two ranks, and triangle-relator simple connectivity; no infinite extrapolation is used.

**Originality — PASS (with residual risk).** The most relevant full-text comparison is Kawamura, *A variant of the bipartite relation theorem and its application to clique graphs* (2011), particularly Theorems 2.5 and 3.3, which develop bipartite-square comparison methods without the Pappus homotopy calculation. Larrión–Pizaña–Villarroel-Flores (2009) controls fundamental groups only. Adamaszek (2011) addresses graph-power collapses but its girth \(\ge7\) hypothesis for radius two does not hold. The published projective-plane incidence theorem assumes a complete pairwise incidence geometry, unlike Pappus, and the Pappus matching-complex calculation concerns a different complex. Adamaszek–Adams (2015) concerns circle Rips complexes. Searches for the exact graph and its aliases, all 4 threshold scales, the numbers 10, 26 and 64, and structurally dominating theorems did not expose a covering computation. The main risk is an unindexed calculation or specialized configuration treatment.

**Value — PASS.** The Pappus graph is a canonical 18-vertex cubic distance-regular Levi graph. The complete integral homotopy evolution and field-independent barcode distinguish it from the extensively studied projective-plane examples. The jump from a graph, to a 2-sphere wedge, to a 5-sphere wedge is structurally explained, not merely enumerated. This exact benchmark can test techniques for graph powers of finite incidence configurations. Value attaches to the complete filtration and its explanatory decompositions, not to a small numerical improvement.

**Scientific scope and limits.** The theorem covers only the unweighted standard Pappus metric; it does not determine equivariant homotopy representations or nearby graph families. Boundary-rank computations support but do not substitute for the structural homotopy proof. No independent audit is claimed.
