# Scientific review: exact simplex-volume spectrum in simplex–parallelogram products

## Correctness

**PASS.** The homogeneous determinant reduction is an exact unimodular column change followed by row subtraction. Nonzero determinant forces at least one selected point from every simplex-vertex layer, leaving precisely two excess rows. The two possible multiplicity patterns yield determinants from differences of three square corners or from a pair of square segments, and the enumerated \(36\) ordered segment-type pairs partition exactly as \(10+24+2=36\). The resulting formulas cover every vertex subset with no missing multiplicity pattern. Separate affinity of the determinant proves the unrestricted maximum, and product affine equivalence covers arbitrary base simplices and parallelograms. Exact finite diagnostics agree in dimensions \(3,4,5,6\) (parameter \(n=1,2,3,4\)). All-dimensional correctness is analytic and combinatorial, not empirical.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical priority uncertainty.** The closest directly overlapping primary result is Seacrest–Su, arXiv:0910.1134, Section 7, which already identifies all \(24\) maximal-class vertex simplices for the triangle-times-square case \(n=2\); their Section 3 also supplies general simplex volume-class terminology. The familiar cube case \(n=1\) is also prior. Neither the inspected portions of that primary text nor Ardila–Billey's two-factor triangulation paper supply the displayed exact three-class census for every \(n\) or its affine-invariant maximal contained-simplex formula for the entire series. General determinant and triangulation literature may contain an equivalent reformulation; absence of a search match is not proof of priority.

## Value

**PASS.** The whole series \(\Delta_n\times Q\) is a natural, geometrically motivated subclass of simplotopes; maximal volume simplex classes are used in volume-covering and triangulation arguments. The result gives the complete distribution of their full-dimensional vertex-simplex volumes, a constructive classification of all vertex-supported sharp cases, and the sharp unrestricted volume ratio in every dimension. It is more than a single low-dimensional determinant tabulation, while not claiming a minimum triangulation theorem. The recurrence-free proof makes the exact data independently reusable.

## Scope, overlap and limitations

Known special cases include the \(n=1\) cube and the \(n=2\) triangle–square product, with \(24\) largest-class simplices already enumerated in Seacrest–Su. The all-dimensional formula and the two precise multiplicity formulas beyond those special cases are the proposed contribution. Maximizers with nonvertex points are not classified. Published statements under alternate terminology could still cover the result, so independent literature verification remains appropriate.
