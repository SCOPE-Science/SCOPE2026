# Scientific evaluation: complete sixth-moment extremizers on the prime-field parabola

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized Fourier sixth moment is exactly a weighted square sum of triple-convolution fibers. The main sharp-norm and constant-modulus necessity premises are Theorem 1.3 of González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva, arXiv:2405.16647, inspected in the complete online paper, including §6. For a nonzero constant-modulus extremizer, sharpness relative to the constant function forces exact alignment of all triple products within each fiber. The translated integral identity \((0,3,3)\) versus \((1,1,4)\) therefore forces a multiplicative fourth-order relation. Its third multiplicative finite difference alternates between reciprocal values; odd cyclic length and telescoping force it to be \(1\). Two lower differences then show that every phase is a quadratic additive character. Converse extremizers are target-frequency translations of the constant function. Distinct parameter pairs yield distinct projective classes by a degree-two polynomial root argument.

Exact computations from the standalone `checker.py` corroborate all triple-fiber counts, collision relations and character constructions for the seven stated primes; finite computations do not prove the all-prime theorem. Characteristic \(2\), characteristic \(3\), and extension fields of non-prime order are excluded. The sharp inequality itself is prior published mathematics, not claimed as a new theorem.

## Originality — PASS (residual bibliographic risk)
The motivating source [1], Theorem 1.3 and §6, states a sharp parabola sixth moment and constant modulus for every maximizer but does **not** state a complete phase equality set there; its explicit phase classification Theorem 1.2 instead concerns a two-dimensional paraboloid fourth moment. The entire online text of [1] and the highly relevant 2025 paper [2] were inspected. Paper [2] treats a \(2d\)-moment on the \(d\)-dimensional moment curve, so \(d=2\) means a fourth moment, and \(d=3\) a different curve. Its published equality classification does not imply the sixth moment on the planar parabola. The 2026 follow-up [3] concerns the same \(2d\) endpoint. Searches by exact claim, phase-rigidity, quadratic-chirp, and moment-curve terminology, together with published related-result descriptions, revealed no dominating equality classification; the closest separate finite-field sphere result uses different supports and fourth moments. This is evidence of a new equality-set statement, not a proof of global novelty. An older equivalent statement under Freiman-polynomial or finite-field Strichartz terminology cannot be excluded.

## Value — PASS
The gap is the entire equality set of an existing natural sharp Fourier restriction/extension inequality, not an artificial statistic. The classification identifies the exact full symmetry orbit and its \(p^2\) projective cardinality uniformly over infinitely many primes. The third-difference rigidity mechanism is structurally tied to equality of three-term sums and sums of squares; it explains why unrestricted unimodular phases do not generally extremize. No paper-sized or downstream theorem is claimed, and no value is inferred merely from reproducible computation.
