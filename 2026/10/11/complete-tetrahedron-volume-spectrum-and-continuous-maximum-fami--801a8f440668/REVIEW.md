# Scientific assessment: icosahedral tetrahedron-volume spectrum

## Correctness — PASS

The proof normalizes the edge-two icosahedron with 12 explicit golden-ratio vertices and works exactly in \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\). Two separate determinant implementations agree for every one of the 495 unordered four-vertex selections, obtaining the five-level spectrum and 30 maximizers. The simultaneous structural equality checks confirm 20 base-to-opposite-facet classes and exclude any two-by-two maximum-set grid. Separate multiaffine reasoning proves the upper bound and the claimed complete equality locus for **all** contained tetrahedra, including nonvertex apex points. The icosahedron volume is independently reconstructed from its 20 equal center-to-face pyramids. The computed certificate does not replace the stated continuous equality proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Horváth–Lángi (arXiv:1402.6496, complete HTML inspected) study volume maximization under a circumsphere condition; their regular-simplex extremizer is unavailable within this fixed icosahedron, and their work does not give the vertex determinant histogram or continuous equality locus. Conway–Torquato (PNAS 2006, complete open text and Appendix inspected) optimize packing twenty regular tetrahedra with a common central apex in an icosahedron rather than one arbitrarily positioned tetrahedron. Nearby geometric results concerning bounded simplex diameters or different highly symmetric polytopes also do not imply the current sharp restricted enumeration. An older regular-polytope table may contain equivalent facts, especially sources not inspected in full; novelty is provisional pending comprehensive independent bibliographic checking.

## Value — PASS

The complete shape-restricted simplex-volume spectrum of the classical regular icosahedron is a natural discrete-geometric invariant, and the sharp volume fraction is affine-scale invariant. Classifying the twenty continuous maximal-face families, rather than merely naming thirty vertex supports, resolves the full equality phenomenon and exposes non-rigidity of maximizers in a familiar 3-polytope. The finite certificate is transparent and self-contained.

## Limits

No analogous claim is made for other regular polyhedra or for non-volume objectives; the historical-priority search was bounded. Proof checking and scientific evaluation here do not constitute independent external validation.
