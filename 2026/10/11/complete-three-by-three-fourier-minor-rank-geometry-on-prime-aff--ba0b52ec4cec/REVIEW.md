# Review of the three-by-three prime-affine-plane Fourier-minor classification

**Correctness — PASS.** Every three-by-three matrix of unit-modulus entries is singular only when a pair of rows or columns is proportional: the normalized determinant equation, its conjugate, and the resulting equal sum/product argument prove this without genericity assumptions. In the Fourier character matrix, proportionality becomes a pairwise affine-direction orthogonality condition. The proof checks both affine support types, all three ranks, each fixed-row multiplicity, and the global double count for every prime. An independent integer-cyclotomic computation reproduces every minor at primes three and five. The finite replay is a diagnostic, not an all-prime proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The inspected 2024 cyclic-principal Fourier-minor theorem does not imply an arbitrary-pair, noncyclic, all-prime-plane incidence count. The published cyclic-prime-square three-minor census concerns a nonisomorphic group with different counts. The 2006/07 Kronecker Fourier work develops maximal rank-deficient patterns and Hamming numbers, not this individual-minor trichotomy. The 2018 finite-plane uncertainty inequalities similarly do not supply the complete rank incidence. An older specialized classification could coincide with the result and remains a genuine risk.

This theorem completely resolves their geometry and frequency for the smallest genuinely two-dimensional minor size, uniformly for every prime field. Its separation of all-affine and noncollinear support types and exact counts is an invariant of the entire Fourier character table, not a routine recomputation of one order or a mere reformulation of the cyclic case.
