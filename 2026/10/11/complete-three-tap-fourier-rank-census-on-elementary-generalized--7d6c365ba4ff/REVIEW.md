# Review of the three-tap convolution nullity census

## Correctness — PASS
For each additive character of the elementary-abelian subgroup, the two-coset Fourier transform reduces the convolution operator to a concrete \(2\times2\) block whose determinant equals the difference of squares of two character sums. The singularity conditions reduce to an equilateral three-root identity. Counting linear character constraints gives the four mutually exclusive cases and the complete three-row census. The formulas agree with independent finite modular adjacency-rank calculations for \(1,653\) tested supports, including \(1,572\) exhaustively enumerated supports across five parameter cases and \(81\) sampled cases. The unrestricted result rests on the proof, not the script.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The 2013 full-text decomposition of Arezoomand and Taeri contains an algorithmic block method for any finite Cayley digraph and is credited, not claimed as new. The 2016 full-text spectral formulas of Huang, Huang, and Lu cover undirected cubic Cayley graphs on cyclic dihedral groups; those overlapping cases are not claimed separately as new. Neither inspected source states the exact rank/nullity classification **and complete support census** for arbitrary three-element convolution supports over elementary-abelian generalized dihedral groups. Searches of relevant public mathematical sources and published records did not identify a stronger explicit classification. Since a general-purpose decomposition can be specialized and older work may contain unindexed equivalents, independent priority verification remains necessary.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Convolution invertibility and rank support are intrinsic finite noncommutative harmonic invariants with direct consequences for the information lost by a three-tap group filter. The result gives a natural complete support classification uniformly in \(p\) and \(d\), exact kernel dimensions, and the complete support census, including a sharp prime-three transition. This is more than a single numerical example or reformulation of an abelian zero criterion.

## Scope and limitations
Unit coefficients and elementary-abelian kernels are essential to the stated theorem. The modular tests are not a proof of complex-rank identities for unbounded families. Representation-theoretic decomposition is standard and the noncommutative rank-census novelty remains subject to independent examination.
