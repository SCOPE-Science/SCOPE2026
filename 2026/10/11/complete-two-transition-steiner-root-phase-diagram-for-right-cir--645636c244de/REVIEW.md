# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a unit-base right circular cone of positive aspect ratio t, the known intrinsic quermassintegrals give m=π+t−arctan(t), s=1+√(1+t²), q=√(m²−4s)>0. The two general Steiner-root transition functionals specialize to Ψ±=m³−6ms+8t±q³. Direct differentiation, with x=t/√(1+t²), proves the global identities Ψ±′=2+(3/2)[x(m±q)−2]²>0. Fresh exact-rational interval calculations independently certify Ψ+(1)<0<Ψ+(√3) and Ψ−(tan(2π/5))<0<Ψ−(tan(5π/12)); therefore each transition occurs uniquely. The primary 2007 Type 1/Type 2 sign criterion yields the five claimed regimes. High-precision approximate roots are corroborative, not the existence or uniqueness proof. ## Originality — PASS (best-of-knowledge)

Hernández Cifre and Saorín's 2007 full text establishes the general three-dimensional Type 1/Type 2 sign classification. Its explicit-family treatment classifies cylinders, not circular cones. Saorín's complete 2009 cone example gives the same V/S/M coefficients, confirms all three root regimes occur, and quotes the Type 1 transition near 3.37108. Those ingredients are credited. The final complete cone phase theorem supplies a uniquely characterized second Type 2 boundary near 1.152393952, exact monotonicity of both functionals, and the precise intermediate three-real-roots interval. Equivalent wording, broad sign criteria, numerical tables and prior implications were compared; the exact matching indexed entry is this same research record, not independent priority evidence. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The right circular cone is a canonical family explicitly used in the motivating Steiner-root/Blaschke-diagram literature. Its complete aspect-ratio root-type classification is a natural exact result. The square-completed derivative identities prove global uniqueness of both transitions and recover the formerly unstated lower boundary and entire real-root interval, going beyond isolated examples or a table lookup. No wider theorem about oblique or truncated cones is claimed.
