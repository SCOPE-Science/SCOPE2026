# Complete Vietoris–Rips homotopy of the full transposition graph on 24 permutations

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the Cayley graph of \(S_4\) generated by all six transpositions. Give its 24 vertices shortest-path distance \(d\), and write \(K_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le}(T;r)\) for the **inclusive** Vietoris–Rips complex: a nonempty subset is a simplex precisely when every two of its vertices have distance at most \(r\). Then, for every real \(r\geq 0\),

\[
K_r\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{24}\mathrm{pt},&0\leq r<1,\\
\bigvee^{49}S^1,&1\leq r<2,\\
\bigvee^{33}S^3,&2\leq r<3,\\
\mathrm{pt},&r\geq 3.
\end{cases}
\]

For any field, its positive-dimensional persistence barcode therefore consists of 49 degree-one intervals \([1,2)\) and 33 degree-three intervals \([2,3)\), and no other positive-dimensional intervals. In degree zero it consists of 23 intervals \([0,1)\) and one interval \([0,\infty)\). The homotopy equivalences also determine integral homology without torsion.

The nontrivial assertion is \(K_2\simeq\bigvee^{33}S^3\). It is established by a finite explicit acyclic discrete-Morse matching, reconstructed and exhaustively checked by the accompanying self-contained program `verify.py`.

## Assumptions and scope
A permutation is represented by a tuple \((\sigma(0),\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\sigma(3))\); the vertices are listed in lexicographic tuple order. An edge in \(T\) exchanges any two tuple positions. For \(\sigma,\tau\in S_4\), if \(c(\sigma^{-1}\tau)\) is the number of cycles of the relative permutation, including fixed points, then
\[
d(\sigma,\tau)=4-c(\sigma^{-1}\tau).
\]
In particular the diameter is 3, and two distinct vertices are distance 3 exactly when the relative permutation is a four-cycle. Thus \(K_2\) also equals the independence complex of the graph on \(S_4\) generated by all six four-cycles. That reformulation does not use the adjacent-swap or Kendall metric: those give a different, three-regular Cayley graph.

The statement concerns precisely this 24-point metric and all real inclusive filtration parameters. No claim is made about all symmetric groups or about metric perturbations.

## Proof
**Endpoint thresholds.** By the cycle-distance formula, distinct vertices have distances 1, 2, or 3. Thus below 1 the complex is discrete and at or above 3 it is the 23-simplex. There are 24 vertices and \(24\cdot6/2=72\) edges in \(T\). The graph is connected because transpositions generate \(S_4\), and it is bipartite because transpositions reverse permutation parity; consequently it contains no triangles. Therefore \(K_1\) is a connected graph, hence a wedge of \(72-24+1=49\) circles.

**Exact finite model at threshold 2.** For the remaining threshold, encode each nonempty vertex subset by its 24-bit mask. Enumerate faces in increasing vertex order by a depth-first clique-extension recursion in the graph with edges corresponding to distances at most 2. The exhaustive counts by dimension \(j=0,1,\ldots,11\) are
\[
(f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_{11})=(24,204,800,1632,2040,2008,1608,990,440,132,24,2),
\]
so there are exactly 9,904 nonempty simplices and no simplices of higher dimension.

**Deterministic matching.** Initialize \(R\) as the set of all nonempty faces, and initialize an empty matching \(M\). Process the 24 vertex labels \(v=0,1,\ldots,23\) in order. At each stage list all remaining faces \(F\in R\) which do not contain \(v\) and for which \(F\cup\{v\}\in R\); sort those faces by their integer bitmask. For each such \(F\), pair \(F\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\) and remove both from \(R\). The choice is deterministic, each pair is a codimension-one incidence, and removal ensures that a simplex belongs to at most one pair. Running the exact procedure gives 4,935 matched pairs. Exactly 34 faces remain unmatched: one vertex and 33 three-simplices.

**Acyclicity, without heuristic inference.** On the full face Hasse diagram, orient each matched codimension-one incidence from its lower-dimensional face to its upper-dimensional face and every other incidence downward. The program constructs every one of the 56,328 directed incidences from the enumerated face set and matching, and performs a full Kahn topological sorting. All 9,904 vertices of this directed graph are removed, so the orientation has no directed cycles. This is an exhaustive finite test, not an extrapolation or a count of sampled paths. The computed matching is therefore an acyclic matching in Forman's discrete Morse theory.

The discrete Morse theorem gives a CW model with exactly one zero-cell and 33 three-cells and no other cells. Each three-cell attaching map lands in the unique point of the two-skeleton, so all attaching maps are constant. That CW model is a wedge of 33 three-spheres. Therefore \(K_2\simeq\bigvee^{33}S^3\), completing the classification.

**Persistence.** At each positive degree, homology appears at only one integer interval: degree one occurs only between thresholds 1 and 2, and degree three only between 2 and 3. Hence no nontrivial positive-degree class can persist across a second threshold. The preceding homotopy types directly yield the stated barcodes over any coefficient field.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and only its standard library. The program independently constructs all 24 vertices and the six generating transpositions, performs graph breadth-first searches from all vertices, and verifies all 576 ordered vertex-pair distances against the cycle formula. It reconstructs all cliques and the staged matching from the definitions, checks the complete oriented Hasse graph acyclic, and independently reduces every simplicial boundary matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\).

Expected final line:

`VERIFY_OK faces=9904 pairs=4935 critical=1x0+33x3 incidences=56328 mod2_beta3=33 graph_edges=72`

The machine calculation here certifies a **finite** exhaustive combinatorial fact. The passage from the checked matching to a homotopy type is a mathematical application of discrete Morse theory; mod-two homology alone would not imply the claimed wedge decomposition.

## Relationship to prior work
The graph is the full-transposition graph (also called the Reye graph), not the adjacent-transposition permutahedral graph. MathWorld records the Reye-graph identification, whereas the latter uses Kendall distance and has a different one-skeleton. Adamaszek's work on clique complexes of graph powers develops general results for graph squares and explicit families. In particular, the large-girth collapse criterion (Proposition 2.2) does not apply because commuting transpositions give four-cycles here; the square-stability condition (Theorem 3.2) excludes four-cycles and likewise does not apply. Its studied cycle- and tree-power classes are different objects. Kozlov studies simple homotopy through elementary collapses and related graph complexes, without determining this flag complex of the square of the Reye graph. Adamaszek–Adams study circle-metric Vietoris–Rips complexes, which are not the present group metric.

A separate finite calculation for the adjacent-swap graph cannot imply this result: the six-generator graph has 72 edges, in contrast to the three-generator permutahedron's 36. Searches of the equivalent four-cycle Cayley-graph independence complex, the Reye-graph alias, and the exact sphere multiplicity did not find a covering statement. That absence is evidence of a possible gap, not a proof of priority.

## Limitations
The classification is confined to \(S_4\) with all transpositions as generators and to the inclusive radius convention. There is no assertion of the same formula for larger symmetric groups, other metrics, or Vietoris–Rips complexes on continuous permutation spaces. The combinatorial certificate is reproducible from source code rather than an independent external validation. Related literature that is not indexed or uses a different presentation could contain an equivalent calculation; independent mathematical review remains to be performed.
