# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof exhausts all active-functional products in the max norm. Same-functional products are bounded by \(1\). Every distinct-functional branch reduces to an exact symmetric \(2\times2\) quadratic form whose spectral radius is at most \(5/4\). Equality tracing forces a side midpoint, a vertex, and the \(2:1\) norm ratio. The explicit representative gives the original quotient \(5/4\).

## Originality
PASS. The known scalar \(C_Z=5/4\) is explicitly treated as prior work. The complete 2012 source states that scalar value for the same counterexample but does not classify all maximizing pairs. The complete 2011 general paper gives value criteria but no pairwise classification for this nonabsolute hexagon. Searches under named-space, Day--James, value, equality, and maximizing-pair formulations found no equivalent theorem. The inaccessible 2006 original and full 2010 article are retained as residual risks.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural equality-case problem for the canonical counterexample to the conjectured identity \(C_Z=C_{\mathrm{NJ}}\). The result identifies the unique geometric mechanism of sharpness rather than merely recomputing the scalar value: a vertex direction, a side-midpoint direction, and a forced \(2:1\) scale ratio.

## Closest literature and limitations
Alonso--Martín introduced the counterexample. Llorens-Fuster--Mazcuñán-Navarro--Reich study the hexagon within the Ptolemy--Zbăganu framework. Mizuguchi--Saito's complete 2012 exposition records the exact scalar counterexample, while their complete 2011 paper develops general Zbăganu value criteria. The theorem is only two-dimensional and does not give near-extremizer stability.
