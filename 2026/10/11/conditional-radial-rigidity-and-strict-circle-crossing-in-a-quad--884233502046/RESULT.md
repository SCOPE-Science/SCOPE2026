# Conditional radial rigidity and strict circle crossing in a quadratic equilibrium-circle flow

## Finding
Consider the Gotthans–Petržela autonomous system
\[
\dot x=az,\qquad \dot y=z(bx+cz^{2}),\qquad
\dot z=x^{2}+y^{2}-r^{2}+dxz,
\]
where \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb R\), \(a\ne0\), and \(r>0\). Write \(R^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}\), \(E=\{(x,y,0):R^{2}=r^{2}\}\), and the corrected height \(\Phi=z-\frac{d}{2a}x^{2}\). For **every** compactly supported invariant Borel probability measure \(\mu\) of this flow,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mu}[R^{2}\mid\Phi]=r^{2}\quad\mu\text{-almost surely}.}
\]
Moreover, with the standard covariance under \(\mu\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu}(x,R^{2})=-a\int z^{2}\,d\mu.}
\]
Equality of this covariance to zero holds **if and only if** \(\mu(E)=1\). Consequently, every invariant measure with \(\mu(E)<1\) has a strict covariance of sign opposite to \(a\) and assigns positive mass both to the open disk \(\{R<r\}\) and to its exterior \(\{R>r\}\). Every nonconstant periodic orbit intersects both regions. Separately, every forward-bounded solution has the exact finite-time balance
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T R(t)^2\,dt-r^{2}=\frac{\Phi(T)-\Phi(0)}{T}
\]
and hence its radial mean square converges to \(r^{2}\) as \(T\to\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope
Time is continuous, with the real polynomial vector field displayed above. The equilibria form precisely the circle \(E\): because \(a\ne0\), an equilibrium must have \(z=0\), after which the third equation requires \(R^{2}=r^{2}\). A compactly supported invariant probability measure means a Borel probability invariant under the two-sided flow and with compact support; all trajectories on its support are complete. The conditional expectation is with respect to the sigma-algebra generated by the scalar random variable \(\Phi\), and its equality holds for almost every corrected-height value, not for an arbitrarily chosen null level. A forward-bounded trajectory is a solution defined for all positive time with bounded phase coordinates; boundedness guarantees continuation for the polynomial ODE.

## Proof
**1. Corrected-height coboundary.** Direct differentiation along the vector field gives the exact algebraic identity
\[
\dot\Phi=\dot z-\frac{d}{a}x\dot x
 =(R^{2}-r^{2}+dxz)-dxz=R^{2}-r^{2}.
\]
Let \(\mu\) be compactly supported and invariant. For every smooth scalar function \(G\), invariance and compact support imply
\[
0=\int \frac{d}{dt}G(\Phi)\,d\mu
=\int G'(\Phi)(R^{2}-r^{2})\,d\mu.
\]
The range of \(\Phi\) on the compact support is compact, and the derivative \(G'\) can be chosen to equal any prescribed continuous function on that range. It follows, by the defining property of conditional expectation, that \(\mathbb E_{\mu}[R^{2}-r^{2}\mid\Phi]=0\). In particular, \(\int R^{2}\,d\mu=r^{2}\).

**2. Transverse-activity coboundary.** With
\[
\Psi=xz-\frac{d}{3a}x^{3},
\]
one obtains, without restrictions on \(b,c,d\),
\[
\dot\Psi=(az)z+x(R^{2}-r^{2}+dxz)-dx^{2}z
=a z^{2}+x(R^{2}-r^{2}).
\]
Integrating against \(\mu\) yields \(\int x(R^{2}-r^{2})\,d\mu=-a\int z^{2}\,d\mu\). Since the radial mean square is \(r^{2}\), the left side equals \(\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu}(x,R^{2})\).

**3. Equality and crossing.** Because \(a\ne0\), the covariance vanishes precisely when \(\int z^{2}\,d\mu=0\). In that case the compact invariant support lies in \(\{z=0\}\). At every point of this support, its full trajectory stays in the plane \(z=0\); differentiating that constraint gives \(\dot z=R^{2}-r^{2}=0\). Thus the support lies in \(E\). Conversely every point of \(E\) is an equilibrium, and measures supported on \(E\) have zero covariance. For any other invariant measure \(\int z^{2}\,d\mu>0\), and therefore \(\int (R^{2}-r^{2})\,d\mu=0\) with a nonzero covariance. The continuous function \(R^{2}-r^{2}\) must take both positive and negative values on sets of positive measure; otherwise the zero integral would force it to vanish almost surely and hence make the covariance zero. A nonconstant periodic orbit carries its uniform invariant probability measure, which cannot be supported in the equilibrium set, so it crosses the circle in both directions of radial side.

**4. Forward boundedness.** For every forward-bounded solution integrate the exact identity in Step 1 over \([0,T]\) to obtain the stated finite-time formula. The bounded corrected height makes its right side tend to zero. This assertion uses neither an ergodic limit nor the existence of an invariant measure.

**5. Nonvacuity.** The family contains nonconstant periodic orbits. For \(a=-1\), \(b=c=d=0\), \(r=1\), restrict to the invariant plane \(y=0\). There \(\dot x=-z\), \(\dot z=x^{2}-1\), with first integral
\[
H(x,z)=\frac12z^{2}+\frac13x^{3}-x.
\]
The potential has a strict minimum at \(x=1\), of value \(-2/3\), separated from the saddle at \(x=-1\), of value \(2/3\). For example, the compact oval component of the regular energy level \(H=-1/2\) surrounding \((1,0)\) is a nonconstant periodic orbit. Thus the strict crossing and covariance conclusions have genuine, nonstationary instances.

## Relationship to prior work
Gotthans and Petržela introduced this exact vector field, specified the chaotic example and its equilibrium circle, analyzed the local linearization, and presented numerical and circuit evidence. Their complete open-access article does not give the corrected-height conditional radial law, the transverse-activity covariance identity, or the strict two-sided radial crossing theorem. Gotthans, Sprott and Petržela later studied a distinct circle/square-equilibrium flow whose first coordinate has unit \(z\) feedback and whose third coordinate has \(\dot z=R^{2}-1\); that system is related by the shared equilibrium geometry but does not supply the parameter-uniform \(d\)-corrected identities for the original flow. A 2016 Kingni–Pham–Jafari–Kol–Woafo paper also studies a circular-equilibrium flow; its full text could not be compared, so possible equivalent earlier identities remain a priority risk. Research-record searches identified analogous moment balances in different differential equations, not a result logically dominating the stated theorem for this exact vector field.

## Limitations
The theorem does not prove that chaotic invariant probability measures exist at the numerical parameter set; it constrains any compactly supported invariant measure that does exist. Conditional expectations are asserted almost surely in corrected height, not pointwise at every height. The strict inequalities apply to measures not entirely supported on the equilibrium circle and to nonconstant periodic orbits, not to every forward-bounded trajectory (which may approach an equilibrium from one side). The theorem does not classify all invariant measures or all escaping trajectories. The priority comparison for the 2016 Kingni et al. source is incomplete. No independent validation is claimed.
