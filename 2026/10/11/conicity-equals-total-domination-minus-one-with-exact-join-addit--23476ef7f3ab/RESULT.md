# Conicity equals total domination minus one, with exact join additivity
## Finding
Let \(K\) be a finite nonempty simplicial complex. A vertex set \(S\subseteq V(K)\) is *totally dominating* when, for every vertex \(v\in V(K)\), there is a simplex \(\sigma\subseteq S\) with \(\sigma\in K\) but \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\notin K\). Let \(\widetilde\gamma(K)\) be the minimum cardinality of such a set, with \(\widetilde\gamma(K)=+\infty\) if none exists. Let
\[
\kappa(K)=\sup\{r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge -1}:K\text{ is }r\text{-conic}\},
\]
where \(K\) is \(r\)-conic when every subcomplex on at most \(r\) vertices lies in the closed star of a vertex. Then
\[
\kappa(K)=\widetilde\gamma(K)-1,
\]
with the convention \(+\infty-1=+\infty\).

Moreover, for finite nonempty simplicial complexes \(K\) and \(L\),
\[
\widetilde\gamma(K*L)=\widetilde\gamma(K)+\widetilde\gamma(L)
\]
and therefore
\[
\kappa(K*L)=\kappa(K)+\kappa(L)+1,
\]
with the usual \(+\infty\) conventions. Thus the standard lower bound for conicity under joins is exact at the level of the maximal conicity parameter.

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite and nonempty, with their declared vertex sets equal to the union of their simplices. The closed star is
\[
\operatorname{st}_K(v)=\{\sigma\in K:\sigma\cup\{v\}\in K\}.
\]
The join \(K*L\) is taken on disjoint vertex sets. The definition of \(\widetilde\gamma(K)\) is the total-domination parameter for a general simplicial complex used in the Aharoni--Berger line of work. The definition of \(r\)-conicity is Barmak's.

The result identifies these two finite obstruction parameters exactly. It does not assert a new sharp connectivity theorem beyond consequences obtained by inserting this identity into existing conicity theorems.

## Proof
Fix an integer \(r\ge -1\). We first prove
\[
K\text{ is }r\text{-conic}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \widetilde\gamma(K)>r.
\]
Suppose \(K\) is not \(r\)-conic. Then there is a subcomplex \(A\subseteq K\) whose vertex set \(S=V(A)\) has cardinality at most \(r\), and \(A\) is contained in no closed star. Hence for every \(v\in V(K)\) there is a simplex \(\sigma_v\in A\) with \(\sigma_v\notin\operatorname{st}_K(v)\). Equivalently, \(\sigma_v\cup\{v\}\notin K\). Since every \(\sigma_v\subseteq S\), the set \(S\) is totally dominating, so \(\widetilde\gamma(K)\le r\).

Conversely, suppose \(S\subseteq V(K)\) is totally dominating and \(|S|\le r\). For each \(v\in V(K)\), choose a witness simplex \(\sigma_v\subseteq S\) satisfying \(\sigma_v\in K\) and \(\sigma_v\cup\{v\}\notin K\). Let \(A\) be the subcomplex generated by the simplices \(\sigma_v\). Its vertex set is contained in \(S\), so it has at most \(r\) vertices. For every \(v\), the simplex \(\sigma_v\in A\) is not in \(\operatorname{st}_K(v)\), so no closed star contains \(A\). Thus \(K\) is not \(r\)-conic.

This proves the equivalence for every finite \(r\). If no totally dominating set exists, take \(S=V(K)\): failure of total domination yields a vertex \(v\) such that every simplex \(\sigma\in K\) satisfies \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\in K\), so \(K=\operatorname{st}_K(v)\) is a cone. Conversely a cone apex prevents every vertex set from being totally dominating. Hence \(\widetilde\gamma(K)=+\infty\) exactly when \(K\) is a cone, matching the case \(\kappa(K)=+\infty\). Therefore \(\kappa(K)=\widetilde\gamma(K)-1\) in all cases.

For the join, write any set \(S\subseteq V(K*L)\) uniquely as \(S_K\sqcup S_L\). Assume first that \(S\) totally dominates \(K*L\). If \(v\in V(K)\), choose a witness join-simplex \(\alpha\cup\beta\subseteq S\), with \(\alpha\in K\), \(\beta\in L\), such that \((\alpha\cup\{v\})\cup\beta\notin K*L\). Since \(\beta\in L\), join membership fails precisely because \(\alpha\cup\{v\}\notin K\). Thus \(S_K\) totally dominates \(K\). The symmetric argument shows that \(S_L\) totally dominates \(L\).

Conversely, if \(S_K\) and \(S_L\) totally dominate their factors, a witness \(\alpha\subseteq S_K\) for a vertex \(v\in V(K)\), together with the empty simplex of \(L\), is a witness in \(K*L\); likewise for vertices of \(L\). Hence \(S_K\sqcup S_L\) totally dominates the join. Therefore total dominating sets of the join are exactly the disjoint unions of total dominating sets of the factors, and minimizing cardinality gives
\[
\widetilde\gamma(K*L)=\widetilde\gamma(K)+\widetilde\gamma(L).
\]
The identity for \(\kappa\) follows from the first part.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library verifier is a finite sanity check, not a substitute for the general proof. It independently enumerates every labeled simplicial complex on one through four declared vertices: \(1,2,9,114\) complexes respectively. For each complex it computes the total-domination number directly and separately tests \(r\)-conicity by enumerating all supported subcomplexes. Every case satisfies
\[
K\text{ is }r\text{-conic}\Longleftrightarrow \widetilde\gamma(K)>r.
\]
It also checks \(144\) ordered pairs of complexes on at most three vertices and verifies exact additivity of \(\widetilde\gamma\) under joins, including cone cases. Finally it checks the joins of one through four copies of \(S^0\), obtaining total-domination numbers \(2,4,6,8\) and maximal conicities \(1,3,5,7\), agreeing with the sharp sphere examples in the conicity literature.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak introduced \(r\)-conic complexes and proved that if \(K_1\) is \(r_1\)-conic and \(K_2\) is \(r_2\)-conic, then \(K_1*K_2\) is \((r_1+r_2+1)\)-conic. The same paper uses joins of copies of \(S^0\) as sharp examples. Its full text contains no discussion of total domination. A later survey on large simplicial complexes restates the conicity theory and the join examples without connecting them to total domination.

The total-domination number of an arbitrary simplicial complex predates conicity. Lew's thesis reproduces the Aharoni--Berger definition: a set \(S\) is totally dominating if every vertex has a simplex \(\sigma\subseteq S\) for which adjoining that vertex leaves the complex; it also records the earlier homological-connectivity bound in terms of this parameter. The checked sources therefore contain the two ingredients separately, but the exact identity \(\kappa(K)=\widetilde\gamma(K)-1\) and the resulting equality for joins were not found in the inspected literature.

The new statement is stronger than merely observing that the two parameters give related bounds: it identifies their obstruction witnesses in both directions and turns the published one-sided join estimate for maximal conicity into an equality.

## Limitations
The originality assessment is relative to the searched literature. The original 2006 Aharoni--Berger article could not be retrieved in full text through the available publisher and institutional routes; its definition and attribution were checked through a later thesis that states them explicitly. Because \(r\)-conicity was introduced much later, that older article cannot state the conicity identity under that terminology, but an unindexed post-2021 source could contain the same observation or an equivalent reformulation.

The proof is elementary once the two definitions are placed side by side. Its value is structural: it exactly identifies two independently used parameters and proves exact join additivity for maximal conicity. No claim is made that every connectivity consequence obtained by substitution is itself new or sharper than all existing domination-based connectivity bounds.
