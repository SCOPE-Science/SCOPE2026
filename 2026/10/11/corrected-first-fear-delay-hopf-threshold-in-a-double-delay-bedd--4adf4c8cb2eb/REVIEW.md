# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Direct differentiation of the printed equations on the \(\tau_2=0\) slice gives a scalar quasipolynomial \(\lambda^2+P\lambda+Q+R e^{-\lambda\tau_1}\). Eliminating the phase gives a quadratic in \(\omega^2\) with exactly one positive root. The associated first phase-compatible delay is \(4.87168883017987792\ldots\); the zero-delay polynomial is Hurwitz, \(\lambda=0\) is impossible for all delays, and the crossing speed has positive real part. Recomputing from the source's rounded equilibrium gives \(4.874589989058015\ldots\), excluding ordinary rounding as the explanation.

## Originality
PASS. The introducing article reports \(4.5923\), so it does not imply the corrected number. Exact-title, DOI, author, numerical-value, characteristic-form, and model-alias searches found no correction or equivalent statement. Semantic database searches returned general delay-bifurcation results for other systems but no same-source coverage. The closest inspected related 2023 paper concerns a different three-species food-chain model with a different delay structure. Residual risk is an unindexed correction or alternative derivation not located by the searches.

## Value
PASS. The corrected number is the first linear-stability boundary in a published numerical bifurcation case and differs from the reported value by about \(0.2794\), roughly six percent. It is not a cosmetic last-digit correction: the recomputation identifies the actual characteristic-root crossing and explains why the paper's coarser simulations at \(3.5\) and \(5\) still bracket the transition. The result is reproducible and directly relevant to interpreting the paper's Case II stability diagram.

## Closest literature and limitations
The introducing Axioms article is the decisive comparison because it states the same model, parameter set, and incompatible Case II threshold. The 2023 AIMS Mathematics fear/gestation-delay article is structurally related but has three state variables and does not cover this two-dimensional characteristic equation. The 2019 Beddington–DeAngelis fear paper supplies background rather than the corrected double-delay threshold. The accepted claim is deliberately restricted to local linear stability for the stated one-delay slice.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
