# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the exact separated-variable identity and uses the elementary two-sided exponential inequality to obtain a global potential comparison. That comparison determines the three power-time-scale limits. The first-order potential correction follows by dominated convergence after the substitution \(s=e^{-x}\), and the first state correction follows by a uniform mean-value argument on critical-time intervals bounded away from zero. The \(p>1\) boundary at \(\tau=0\) is explicitly excluded from uniformity.

## Originality
PASS. The 2024 same-object predecessor already distinguishes \(y'=k(1-y^\beta)/\beta\) from \(y'=k(1-y^\beta)\), including the logarithmic-integral versus constant limiting behavior; that qualitative distinction is therefore not claimed as new. The accepted claim is the exact potential comparison, the complete \(p<1\), \(p=1\), \(p>1\) time-scale classification, the critical inverse-function bracket, and the uniform first correction away from \(\tau=0\). Full-text inspections, five semantic published-finding searches, targeted web searches, and exact searches of the current and prior own ledgers did not locate an implication-equivalent statement. Residual risk remains for unindexed literature under a different normalization.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves the natural normalization ambiguity in the small-exponent regime of the motivating model and turns it into a complete scaling phase diagram with a quantitative error law. The critical exponent \(p=1\) is intrinsic: it is the only power-law observation scale producing nontrivial logarithmic-integral dynamics. The result is exact and reusable for interpreting parameter limits and benchmark trajectories.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is arXiv:2609.31748v1 and DOI:10.1007/s11071-024-10370-y. The first supplies the exact primitive but states the small-exponent limit using a divided expression; the second correctly separates the divided and undivided normalizations but does not state the quantitative scaling theorem accepted here. The result is limited to the scalar deterministic model with fixed \(C>0\), \(\alpha>0\), and the stated rescalings.
