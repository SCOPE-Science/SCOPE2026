# Review of critical delayed predator extinction

## Correctness — PASS

The model is specified with fourteen strictly positive parameters, finite nonnegative delays, and nonnegative continuous histories with strictly positive present populations. Positivity and independent scalar bounds give global existence. At \(\beta<d\), a scalar delayed comparison gives exponential intermediate-predator decay, followed by exponential top-predator decay. At \(\beta=d\), differentiating \(P=y+d\int_{t-\tau_1}^t y\) yields two explicitly nonpositive dissipation terms, including a strictly coercive delayed quadratic term on bounded trajectories. Square integrability and bounded derivative prove \(y\to0\), and a delayed comparison gives exponential \(z\to0\). Under \(r>b+\alpha/m\), logistic lower and upper comparisons force \(x\to(r-b)/a>0\). The normalized compactness argument and the bounded-entire-solution spectral lemma justify the full, rather than formal, limit \(t y(t)\to (r-b)(1+d\tau_1)/(a d m)\). The counterexample above threshold has an exact stable Jacobian. `CHECK.py` symbolically checks the central nonlinear identity, formal coefficient, and rational sharpness example. No finite experiment substitutes for the infinite-time proof.

## Originality — PASS

The directly relevant 2023 Wang–Shao–Chai full text presents the local predator-free criterion \(\beta<d\) in Theorem 5.2 and the characteristic equation in Appendix (A.1), but neither the critical global extinction mechanism nor the exact \(t^{-1}\) coefficient. Its positive-equilibrium existence conditions above threshold are not claimed as new. Tian–Xu (2011), on a different nondelayed Holling-II stage-structured model, treats global extinction under a strict feasibility condition; it has neither equation (1) nor its critical lag coefficient. Shao–Kong (2022) proves global coexistence stability for a distinct two-species delayed Beddington–DeAngelis system. Generic nonlinear-delay center arguments explain why algebraic decay is plausible but do not supply the present global basin argument and exact source-specific constant. Searches for aliases, stronger exact conclusions, and a recorded critical-rate table did not identify matching coverage. This is a supported, scoped novelty assessment, not a proof that no inaccessible treatment exists.

## Value — PASS

The critical equality is mathematically motivated: the predator-free linearization has a simple zero characteristic root, so strict spectral-stability arguments do not decide nonlinear critical relaxation. The precise positive coefficient in the long-time law distinguishes inevitable extinction from exponential recovery and quantifies a non-obvious, linear dependence on gestation delay. Its independence from the separate fear and top-predator parameters is structural, while the explicit stable positive-predator equilibrium for \(\beta>d\) shows the extinction threshold is sharp as a uniform condition. This supplies a useful baseline and a falsifiable rate prediction, not merely a numerical benchmark or minor strengthening of an inequality.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest full-text comparison is Wang, Shao and Chai, *AIMS Mathematics* (2023), DOI 10.3934/math.2023378, especially model (2.1), Theorem 5.2 and Appendix (A.1). Other inspected or compared literature: Tian and Xu (2011), DOI 10.15388/NA.16.2.14109, and Shao and Kong (2022), DOI 10.3390/math10183378; the related Shao (2022), DOI 10.1016/j.matcom.2022.04.013, was available in abstract but not full text, and concerns a different two-species Holling-II model. Exact algebraic relaxation requires \(r>b+\alpha/m\); predator extinction alone does not. Arbitrary time-dependent delays, stochastic perturbations, and a complete classification above threshold remain open here. A further specialist literature check and independent proof audit would be appropriate before external reliance.
