In a three-level food chain with two gestation delays, the critical predator conversion–mortality threshold \(\beta=d\) still forces both predator populations to extinction for every finite delay; when \(r>b+\alpha/m\), the intermediate predator obeys the sharp relaxation law \(t x_2(t)\to (r-b)(1+d\tau_1)/(a d m)\). The leading critical amplitude grows linearly with its gestation delay, while the top predator disappears exponentially, and the universal extinction threshold cannot be extended above \(\beta=d\).
