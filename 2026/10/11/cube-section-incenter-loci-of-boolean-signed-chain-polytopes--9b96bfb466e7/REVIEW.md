# Scientific review: Boolean signed-chain incenter locus

## Correctness — PASS

For every nonempty Boolean chain, the Euclidean norm of its signed-sum normal equals the square root of its length. The longest-chain slab bound and the origin witness give the exact inradius. At the optimum, every longest-chain sum vanishes; adjacent swaps and the connected Johnson graph force coordinates to be constant at each rank. Omitting one rank gives the sharp coordinate cap. Conversely, the convexity inequality for the selected and complementary rank sums verifies all shorter-chain inequalities. An active-bound count establishes the complete central cube section, affine dimension and parity-dependent vertices. Independent rational matrix-rank and finite slack checks support, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (best of knowledge)

Hlavacek's 2023 dissertation, Chapter 5.6, and Beck–Hlavacek's *Signed Poset Polytopes* (2023), Section 6, define the two-sided signed-chain model and investigate integer points and reflexivity, without stating this complete Euclidean incenter locus. Related enriched-chain results yield the same numeric radius, but impose every sign pattern inside a chain constraint and hence have a unique Boolean incenter; the present two-sided signed-chain body has a whole positive-dimensional center set. Ordinary nonnegative chain polytopes and signed order polytopes likewise have different defining halfspaces. A differently phrased earlier metric theorem remains a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Boolean inclusion lattice is canonical, and the theorem determines all maximum-inscribed Euclidean-ball centers in an established signed-chain construction: a d-dimensional rank-constant cube section in 2^d coordinates with an exact parity-sensitive vertex census. The new content is the complete identification of the optimal-center set, not the textbook facet-clearance, Johnson-graph, or cube-section counting ingredients separately.

## Scope and verification limitations

The result uses the standard Euclidean coordinates and includes the empty and full subsets as distinct rank coordinates. It does not extend to all signed posets or to arbitrary metrics. Finite computations cannot establish the arbitrary-dimensional theorem; the analytic proof provides that extension. No formal proof-assistant certificate or external expert attestation is asserted.
