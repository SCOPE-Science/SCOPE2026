# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the classical Cesàro matrix, the self-commutator has an explicit positive Gram expansion with coefficients \(a_j=((j+1)^2(j+2))^{-1}\), exact trace one, and trivial kernel. A consecutive-block min--max comparison proves \(\lambda_n\geq1/(48n^3)\). Dyadic zero-sum blocks with fewer than \(7n\) constraints and a controlled tail give \(\lambda_{7n+1}\leq4/n^3\). Consequently the eigenvalue order is cubic, the Schatten threshold is exactly \(p>1/3\), and the weak endpoint holds. Independent rational checks confirm finite arithmetic only; the general proof is analytic.

**Originality — PASS, best of knowledge.** The 2023 essential-normality result of Uğur Gül proves compactness of this self-commutator. Sang's 2026 work establishes Toeplitz C*-algebra membership of the classical Cesàro operator, while Ma's 2026 generalized-Cesàro theorem proves hyponormality and a weighted lower bound. Two relevant published generalized-Cesàro analyses concern the sharp relative Gram coefficient and exact trace; the latter covers the trace-one subclaim. None of the compared mathematical statements supplies both cubic eigenvalue bounds or the precise Schatten cutoff. Complete accessible HTML versions of Sang and Ma and the available full Gül text were checked; possible hidden overlap with broader weighted Hardy spectral theory remains a priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The quantitative spectral decay and exact weak-Schatten threshold refine generic positivity and trace-class results for a canonical nonnormal averaging operator. The finding is naturally motivated, not an isolated numerical approximation.

**Limits.** No leading spectral constant, all-order generalized Cesàro theorem, formal proof, expert attestation, or exhaustive historical novelty guarantee is asserted.
