# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Periodicity annihilates both the averaged \(p\)-Laplacian derivative and all averaged velocity-coupling terms, giving the exact identity \(\overline{g_j(u_{\varepsilon,j})}=g_j(\alpha_j)\). The source proves the uniform leading profile with zero-mean \(w\). The exact cubic Taylor identity then determines the mean at scale \(\varepsilon^{2/(p-1)}\); simple-root nondegeneracy supplies the nonzero denominator. No experiment is used as an infinite-time or asymptotic proof.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating full text gives only the leading nonconstant profile and says restoring and velocity terms enter at higher order; searches for mean shift, rectification, curvature/variance corrections, the exact exponent, and the defining arXiv source found no statement implying this coefficient. The 2021 Fredholm predecessor treats a semilinear setting and different applications, while the companion 2026 preprint explicitly excludes the nondifferentiable \(p\)-Laplacian case from its smooth implicit-function framework. Residual risk remains that an equivalent coefficient exists in older or unindexed higher-order averaging literature under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The source singles out the nonclassical response scale and leaves the higher-order contribution qualitative. The result turns that gap into an explicit branchwise law for the mean, including a sign criterion and the structural fact that velocity coupling does not enter the coefficient. This distinguishes oscillatory amplitude from rectified displacement and is directly usable when comparing multiple simple averaged roots.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest are Mawhin--Novoa, arXiv:2610.02155v1; Cid--Mawhin--Zima, DOI 10.1016/j.jde.2020.11.051; and Mawhin--Novoa, arXiv:2610.02156v1. The result does not compute the full next-order waveform and is silent on stability. If the restoring curvature or the relevant forcing profile vanishes in a component, only a smaller-order mean is concluded.
