# Curve-extension rigidity and Picard detectability in compact M-manifolds

## Finding

Let \(X\) be a complex manifold and let
\[
C\subset X
\]
be a smooth connected compact complex curve. Suppose that global meromorphic functions on \(X\) separate every pair of distinct points of \(C\) by functions holomorphic near those two points.

Let
\[
K_C\subset\mathcal M(C)
\]
be the subfield generated by meromorphic restrictions to \(C\) of global meromorphic functions on \(X\) that are generically defined along \(C\). Then
\[
K_C=\mathcal M(C).
\]
Thus every meromorphic function on \(C\) is the meromorphic restriction of a global meromorphic function on \(X\).

In particular, if \(X\) is a compact \(M\)-manifold, then every meromorphic function on every smooth connected compact curve
\[
C\subset X
\]
extends meromorphically to \(X\).

There is also a Picard-theoretic consequence. Every smooth compact curve in a compact \(M\)-manifold is positively detected by the ambient Picard group: there exists
\[
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
\]
such that
\[
\deg(L|_C)>0.
\]
Therefore, if a compact complex manifold contains a smooth connected compact curve \(C\) satisfying
\[
\deg(L|_C)=0
\]
for every
\[
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X),
\]
then the manifold is not an \(M\)-manifold.

For smooth curves, this recovers the fresh source's torsion-curve obstruction and replaces the torsion hypothesis by the weaker Picard-numerical vanishing condition.

## Assumptions and scope

A meromorphic restriction
\[
F|_C
\]
is meant in the generic sense: \(F\) induces an element of the one-variable function field
\[
\mathcal M(C)
\]
on a nonempty open subset of \(C\), hence on the whole curve as a meromorphic function.

The \(M\)-manifold terminology is that of the recent meromorphic-convexity literature. Only its point-separation axiom is needed for the curve-extension theorem itself.

The Picard obstruction is asserted for smooth compact curves. The fresh source's torsion obstruction applies to irreducible compact curves more generally; no singular-curve extension theorem is claimed here.

The positive Picard degree condition is necessary for the \(M\)-property, not claimed sufficient.

## Proof

Let
\[
K_C
\]
be the subfield of
\[
\mathcal M(C)
\]
generated by ambient meromorphic restrictions.

By hypothesis, for every pair of distinct points of \(C\), there is an ambient meromorphic function that is holomorphic near those points and takes different values there. Its restriction lies in \(K_C\). Hence \(K_C\) separates points of \(C\).

In particular, \(K_C\) contains a nonconstant function; choose
\[
u\in K_C.
\]
On the compact Riemann surface \(C\), the map
\[
u:C\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1
\]
is finite. Therefore
\[
[\mathcal M(C):\mathbb C(u)]<\infty.
\]
Since
\[
\mathbb C(u)\subset K_C\subset\mathcal M(C),
\]
the extension
\[
\mathcal M(C)/K_C
\]
is finite.

The one-variable function field \(K_C\) has a smooth compact Riemann surface model \(Y\). The inclusion
\[
K_C\hookrightarrow\mathcal M(C)
\]
is induced by a finite holomorphic map
\[
\pi:C\longrightarrow Y,
\]
and
\[
\deg\pi=[\mathcal M(C):K_C].
\]

If
\[
\deg\pi>1,
\]
then outside the finite branch locus a fiber contains at least two distinct points
\[
p\ne q.
\]
Every element of
\[
K_C=\mathcal M(Y)
\]
takes the same value at \(p\) and \(q\), because it factors through \(\pi\). This contradicts point separation. Hence
\[
\deg\pi=1,
\]
so
\[
K_C=\mathcal M(C).
\]

Now let
\[
h\in\mathcal M(C).
\]
Since
\[
h\in K_C,
\]
there are finitely many
\[
F_1,\ldots,F_m\in\mathcal M(X)
\]
with generically defined restrictions to \(C\), and a rational expression \(R\), such that
\[
h=R(F_1|_C,\ldots,F_m|_C).
\]
The same expression
\[
F=R(F_1,\ldots,F_m)
\]
is a global meromorphic function on \(X\), and its meromorphic restriction to \(C\) is \(h\). This proves extension.

If \(X\) is an \(M\)-manifold, its defining point-separation axiom applies to every pair of points of \(C\), so the extension conclusion follows.

For the Picard consequence, choose an ambient meromorphic function \(F\) with nonconstant restriction to \(C\). Choose a value
\[
\lambda\in\mathbb P^1
\]
attained by the restricted map away from its poles and critical values. The effective zero-fiber divisor
\[
D_\lambda
=
\overline{\{F=\lambda\}}
\]
does not contain \(C\), while its restriction to \(C\) is a nonempty effective divisor. Consequently
\[
\mathcal O_X(D_\lambda)|_C
\]
has positive degree:
\[
\deg\!\left(
\mathcal O_X(D_\lambda)|_C
\right)>0.
\]

Thus if every ambient line bundle has degree zero on \(C\), the ambient manifold cannot satisfy the \(M\)-manifold point-separation property.

## Verification

The fresh primary source was checked at its definition of \(M\)-manifold, its construction of nonprojective compact \(M\)-manifolds, and its curve obstruction. Its Proposition 4 proves non-\(M\) behavior when an irreducible compact curve has torsion homology class, using positivity of the intersection with a meromorphic fiber divisor.

The earlier primary source introducing meromorphic convexity was checked at the \(M\)-manifold definition, the proof that compact \(M\)-manifolds are Moishezon, the role of point separation, and its discussion that a relation with the Picard group is a natural direction.

The new argument independently reconstructs the restriction-field step from compact Riemann-surface function theory. The only classical input is the standard correspondence between finite extensions of one-variable function fields and finite holomorphic maps of compact Riemann surfaces.

No numerical computation or finite experiment is used.

## Relationship to prior work

The recent paper proves that compact \(M\)-manifolds form a proper class between projective and Moishezon manifolds. It also gives a curve obstruction: a compact complex manifold containing an irreducible compact curve with torsion homology class is not an \(M\)-manifold.

The present result extracts a stronger curve-wise consequence of the point-separation axiom. On every smooth compact curve, ambient meromorphic functions do not merely separate points; they generate the entire intrinsic meromorphic function field, so every intrinsic meromorphic function extends to the ambient manifold.

The Picard obstruction follows from this field rigidity and the same fiber-divisor mechanism as the source. For a smooth curve, torsion of its homology class forces degree zero against every ambient line bundle, but the latter numerical condition does not require the homology class itself to be torsion.

The earlier meromorphic-convexity paper establishes ambient point separation and the Moishezon consequence but does not state this restriction-field surjectivity or the positive-degree condition for every smooth curve.

Targeted searches under meromorphic extension from compact curves, restriction fields, point separation, Picard degree, and numerically trivial curves did not locate a statement covering this consequence for \(M\)-manifolds.

## Limitations

The theorem is proved for smooth compact curves. Singular irreducible curves require a normalization-compatible formulation and are not claimed here.

The Picard positivity condition is only necessary for the \(M\)-property.

A classical theorem under different meromorphic-separability terminology may already encode the restriction-field lemma in a more general setting. No such covering result was located in the bounded literature search; the claimed contribution is the curve-extension and Picard consequence in the context of the recent \(M\)-manifold theory.
