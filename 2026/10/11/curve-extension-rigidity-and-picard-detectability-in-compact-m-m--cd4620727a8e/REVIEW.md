# Review of Curve-extension rigidity and Picard detectability in compact M-manifolds

## Correctness

PASS. Ambient point separation makes the restriction field
\[
K_C
\]
a point-separating intermediate field of the compact curve function field. A nonconstant element gives
\[
\mathbb C(u)\subset K_C\subset\mathcal M(C)
\]
with finite upper extension. The corresponding finite map of compact Riemann surfaces must have degree one, because a generic fiber of degree greater than one would contain distinct points indistinguishable by every function in \(K_C\). Thus
\[
K_C=\mathcal M(C).
\]

Every element of the generated field is a rational expression in finitely many ambient restrictions, so the same rational expression gives an actual global meromorphic extension. The Picard consequence follows from restricting an effective fiber divisor of a nonconstant ambient meromorphic function to the curve.

## Originality

PASS. The fresh primary source proves a torsion-homology curve obstruction but does not state that ambient meromorphic functions generate the complete function field of every smooth compact curve in an \(M\)-manifold.

The earlier primary source proves point separation and the Moishezon consequence, and points toward a Picard-group connection, but it does not state this curve-extension theorem or the positive-degree condition for every smooth curve.

Searches under restriction-field, meromorphic-extension, point-separation, Picard-degree, and numerically-trivial-curve formulations did not locate a covering theorem in the recent literature. The classical compact-curve function-field correspondence is used only as a supporting lemma.

## Value

PASS. The defining point-separation property of \(M\)-manifolds is upgraded to full meromorphic extension on every smooth compact curve. This supplies a projective-like rigidity statement that applies to the genuinely nonprojective compact \(M\)-manifolds of the fresh source.

The Picard corollary sharpens the fresh source's obstruction for smooth curves: instead of requiring torsion in ambient homology, it is enough that every ambient line bundle have degree zero on the curve. This gives a concrete structural link between meromorphic convexity and Picard geometry.
