# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Let C be any smooth connected compact complex curve and K_C the intermediate subfield generated by ambient meromorphic restrictions generically defined on C. The explicit point-separation hypothesis means each distinct p,q in C is separated by an ambient F holomorphic at both, hence by an element of K_C. Choose a nonconstant u in K_C; compact Riemann surface theory gives [M(C):C(u)]<infinity and thus [M(C):K_C]<infinity. The finite map pi:C->Y for this extension has degree [M(C):K_C]; if >1, a generic unramified fiber has p != q with identical values under EVERY element of K_C (including possible poles as P1 values), contradicting separation. Thus the degree is 1 and K_C=M(C). By definition an arbitrary h in K_C is a finite rational expression in generically defined ambient F_i, and the identical ambient rational expression is meromorphic on X and restricts generically to h. This proves meromorphic extension and works even if X is noncompact, because only C compact is used. Every compact M-manifold satisfies point separation by Definition 1(b) of arXiv:2609.36378 / Definition 10(b) of arXiv:2510.27100. For Picard positivity, choose distinct p,q in C and an ambient F holomorphic near them with F(p)!=F(q), then choose a regular finite value lambda attained at a point of C at which F is holomorphic in an ambient neighborhood; the effective divisor D_lambda=Z(F-lambda) does not contain C and meets C properly, yielding positive deg O_X(D_lambda)|_C. This final Picard step reproduces the source paper divisor argument. Genus zero, finite branched map boundaries, potentially indeterminate ambient functions, and the meaning of generic meromorphic restriction are addressed. No finite experiment is part of an infinite proof.

## originality

PASS

Qualified PASS for the specific curve-wise surjectivity of the generic meromorphic restriction field: no inspected independent source states the theorem for the recently introduced M-manifold class. The map/field lemma is classical, not a new lemma. Crucially, the positive-Picard-degree corollary is ALREADY a direct consequence of the published divisor proof of Proposition 4 of arXiv:2609.36378, once point separation supplies a nonconstant restriction; it is NOT independent new priority evidence or a second novel theorem. The remaining non-covered claim is the full restriction-field equality and extension of arbitrary curve meromorphic functions. Prior-coverage risk is nonzero because a general classical separability theorem may imply this exact formulation.

## value

PASS

The worthwhile surviving point is the universal restriction-field extension property for smooth compact curves in a newly studied class of compact meromorphically separable manifolds, including nonprojective examples in the fresh source. It is a natural structural bridge from pairwise ambient point separation to full intrinsic one-variable meromorphic function-field access, potentially usable for curve-wise maps/divisors and comparisons of projective and Moishezon behaviors. The argument uses a classical finite-map lemma and is short; mathematical value attaches to the curve-function extension consequence for the class, NOT to a new proof technique or to Picard positivity (which is directly available in the prior source proof). This is more than a parameter renaming or arbitrary numerical slice, but it is narrower than an ambient algebraicity characterization.
