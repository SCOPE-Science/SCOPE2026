# Cycle Riesz transforms as parity blocks of doubled cyclic Hilbert transforms
## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge3\), write \(C_N=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), let \(T f(x)=f(x+1)\), and put
\[
\mathcal L_N=2I-T-T^{-1}.
\]
Let \(P_0\) be averaging onto constants, and define \(\mathcal L_N^{-1/2}\) to vanish on constants. The forward cycle Riesz transform
\[
\mathcal R_N=(T-I)\mathcal L_N^{-1/2}
\]
has Fourier multiplier
\[
m_N(k)=i e^{\pi i k/N},\qquad 1\le k\le N-1,
\]
and \(m_N(0)=0\). With normalized convolution
\[
(\mathcal R_N f)(x)=\frac1N\sum_{y\in C_N}K_N(x-y)f(y),
\]
its kernel is exactly
\[
K_N(r)=-\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi(r+1/2)}N\right),\qquad r\in C_N.
\]
It also satisfies the exact algebraic identity
\[
\mathcal R_N^2=-T(I-P_0).
\]

Let \(H_{2N}\) denote the canonical cyclic Hilbert transform on \(C_{2N}\): its multiplier is \(-i\) on frequencies \(1,\ldots,N-1\), \(+i\) on \(N+1,\ldots,2N-1\), and zero at \(0,N\). If \(E_0,E_1\) embed a function on \(C_N\) onto respectively the even and odd sites of \(C_{2N}\), while \(J_0,J_1\) restrict to those two parity classes, then
\[
J_1H_{2N}E_0=-\mathcal R_N,
\qquad
J_0H_{2N}E_1=-S_-\mathcal R_N,
\]
where \(S_-g(r)=g(r-1)\). Thus, under even/odd parity decomposition,
\[
H_{2N}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-S_-\mathcal R_N\\
-\mathcal R_N&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Consequently the two strong endpoint norms are sharp and equal:
\[
\|\mathcal R_N\|_{\ell^1\to\ell^1}
=
\|\mathcal R_N\|_{\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty}
=
\|H_{2N}\|_{\ell^1\to\ell^1}
=
\|H_{2N}\|_{\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty}
=C_N,
\]
where
\[
C_N=\frac1N\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}
\left|\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi(r+1/2)}N\right)\right|
=\frac2N\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1}
\cot\!\left(\frac{(2r+1)\pi}{2N}\right).
\]
The finite-size correction remembers the parity of \(N\):
\[
C_N=
\frac2\pi\log N+
\frac2\pi\left(\gamma+\log\frac4\pi\right)
+\frac{a_N}{N^2}+O(N^{-4}),
\]
with
\[
a_N=
\begin{cases}
\pi/18,&N\text{ even},\\
-7\pi/36,&N\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All sequence spaces use normalized counting measure. The orientation is fixed by \(T f(x)=f(x+1)\); reversing the edge orientation conjugates the multiplier and only changes the corresponding shift convention. The inverse square root is the spectral inverse on the orthogonal complement of constants and is zero on constants. The result concerns the one-dimensional cycle and the strong endpoints \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^\infty\); it does not assert sharp \(\ell^p\) norms for \(1<p<\infty\).

## Proof
For the character \(\chi_k(x)=e^{2\pi i kx/N}\),
\[
\mathcal L_N\chi_k=4\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k}N\right)\chi_k,
\]
while
\[
(T-I)\chi_k=
2i e^{\pi i k/N}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi k}N\right)\chi_k.
\]
Because \(0<\pi k/N<\pi\) for \(1\le k\le N-1\), the positive square root of the Laplacian eigenvalue is \(2\sin(\pi k/N)\). Division gives \(m_N(k)=i e^{\pi i k/N}\).

Fourier inversion therefore gives
\[
K_N(r)=i\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}
 e^{2\pi i k(r+1/2)/N}.
\]
Put \(q=e^{2\pi i(r+1/2)/N}\). Since \(q^N=-1\),
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}q^k=\frac2{1-q}
=1+i\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi(r+1/2)}N\right).
\]
Subtracting the \(k=0\) term and multiplying by \(i\) yields the displayed cotangent kernel. Squaring the multiplier gives
\[
m_N(k)^2=-e^{2\pi i k/N}
\]
for every nonzero frequency, while both sides vanish on constants. This proves \(\mathcal R_N^2=-T(I-P_0)\).

For \(H_{2N}\), pairing frequency \(k\) with \(2N-k\) gives the kernel
\[
J_{2N}(s)=2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi ks}N\right).
\]
The finite sine-sum identity reduces this to
\[
J_{2N}(s)=
\begin{cases}
2\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi s}{2N}\right),&s\text{ odd},\\
0,&s\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(H_{2N}\) switches parity. Evaluating the odd output generated by even input gives exactly \(-\mathcal R_N\); evaluating the even output generated by odd input gives the shifted copy \(-S_-\mathcal R_N\). This proves the block factorization.

A convolution operator on a finite group has \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^\infty\) norms equal to the normalized absolute kernel sum. A point mass attains the \(\ell^1\) bound, and a phase choice matching one row of the kernel attains the \(\ell^\infty\) bound. Applying this to \(K_N\), then using the parity block identity, gives the exact common endpoint norm \(C_N\).

For the asymptotic expansion, separate the singular term by writing
\[
\cot(\pi x)=\frac1{\pi x}+g(x),
\qquad
0\le x\le\frac12,
\]
where \(g\) is smooth on the closed interval. The singular half-grid sum reduces to harmonic numbers, while midpoint Euler--Maclaurin applies to \(g\). Using
\[
2\int_0^{1/2}g(x)\,dx=\frac2\pi\log\frac2\pi,
\]
with the standard harmonic-number expansion, and carrying the midpoint expansion through fourth order, gives the stated constant term. For even \(N\) the quadratic coefficient is \(\pi/18\); for odd \(N\), the missing central half-step changes it to \(-7\pi/36\). The odd powers through order three cancel, leaving remainder \(O(N^{-4})\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verifier.py` independently reconstructs the Fourier multiplier and cotangent kernel for many cycle sizes, checks the square identity frequency by frequency, checks both parity blocks against the doubled cyclic Hilbert kernel, compares all endpoint norm formulas, and tests the two parity-dependent quadratic asymptotic coefficients at large sizes. It uses floating-point arithmetic only as a regression check; the universal statements are proved analytically above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kobayashi, Nilsson and Sato study multiplier characterizations of Riesz transforms on \(\mathbb T^n\) and \(\mathbb Z^n\) through maximal semigroup symmetry. Their setting supplies an archive-era primary \(42B15\) source for discrete Riesz multipliers, but it does not formulate the finite-cycle transform above or the half-grid cotangent, square-shift, parity-block, or sharp endpoint identities.

Lust-Piquard introduced difference-Laplacian Riesz transforms on products of abelian groups and explicitly included cyclic-group cases, obtaining dimension-free \(L^p\) estimates. Wróbel later treated single Riesz transforms and a cyclic-group extension in the same broad framework. These are materially closer to the present operator, but the comparison located no exact finite-cycle endpoint formula or doubled-Hilbert parity factorization in the inspected material. A recent Bellman-function treatment also covers locally compact abelian groups, including products of cyclic groups, while pursuing dimension-free \(L^p\) bounds rather than exact finite-cycle endpoint structure.

A previously published exact cyclic-Hilbert endpoint formula determines the norm and finite-size correction of \(H_M\). It does not identify the graph Riesz transform \(\mathcal R_N\), prove \(\mathcal R_N^2=-T(I-P_0)\), or exhibit the parity block decomposition of \(H_{2N}\). Once the new parity identification is established, the endpoint formula and asymptotic expansion can also be read as corollaries of that prior Hilbert calculation.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to a one-generator cycle and to the forward difference paired with its standard graph Laplacian. It does not classify alternate discrete Riesz conventions, higher-dimensional product cycles, vector Riesz transforms, or sharp interior \(\ell^p\) norms. The 2004 Lust-Piquard paper is a particularly plausible older source for related cyclic-group identities; its abstract and later full-text descriptions were checked, but a reliable full-text inspection of that paper itself was not available here. Older digital-signal-processing literature could also contain an equivalent half-grid cotangent or parity-decimation identity under different terminology.
