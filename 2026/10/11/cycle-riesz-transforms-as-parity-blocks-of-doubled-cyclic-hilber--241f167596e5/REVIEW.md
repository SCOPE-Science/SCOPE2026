# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Fourier diagonalization gives the exact multiplier \(i e^{\pi i k/N}\); a geometric sum gives the half-grid cotangent kernel; squaring the multiplier gives \(-T(I-P_0)\). The doubled Hilbert kernel is zero on even offsets and twice the corresponding cotangent value on odd offsets, which proves both parity blocks. Endpoint sharpness is exact for finite-group convolution. The asymptotic follows from harmonic-number expansion plus midpoint Euler--Maclaurin after removing the cotangent singularity.

## Originality
PASS. Targeted searches covered cycle-graph Riesz transforms, the difference--Laplacian form \(D(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\), half-grid cotangent kernels, doubled cyclic Hilbert transforms, parity factorization, and exact endpoint norms. The closest prior theory gives dimension-free \(L^p\) bounds for difference Riesz transforms on cyclic or abelian groups, while the closest prior exact endpoint result concerns the cyclic Hilbert transform itself. Neither inspected source family states or implies the structural finite-cycle identities \(\mathcal R_N^2=-T(I-P_0)\) and the displayed parity block decomposition without the new Fourier calculation.

## Value
PASS. The graph Riesz transform and cyclic Hilbert transform are canonical singular multipliers. The theorem identifies them exactly after a natural doubling/parity decomposition, rather than merely comparing their norms. It simultaneously exposes a square-root algebra, a closed half-grid kernel, exact strong endpoints, and the parity of the finite-size correction, giving a reusable structural model for finite cyclic harmonic analysis.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kobayashi--Nilsson--Sato treat discrete Riesz multipliers on \(\mathbb Z^n\) through semigroup symmetry. Lust-Piquard and Wróbel treat difference--Laplacian Riesz transforms on abelian or cyclic groups with dimension-free \(L^p\) estimates. The closest exact cyclic-Hilbert record supplies the norm of \(H_M\), but not the graph-Riesz identification. The main residual risk is an older equivalent parity-decimation identity under different finite-filter terminology; the 2004 Lust-Piquard full text was not reliably available for direct inspection.
