# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The published facet-label theorem gives all supporting hyperplanes with support value \(1\). Subtracting a label mean gives the intrinsic zero-sum normal, so origin-to-facet distance is its reciprocal norm. Central symmetry reduces the global inball problem to the largest origin-centered ball. The exact walk-variance argument bounds every facet normal and has forced equality only at a maximum-range mountain walk: one rise and one fall for even cycles, and the same pattern with a single extremal plateau for odd cycles. The resulting maximum norms give the displayed radii and contact counts. The tangent-normal orbit spans all of \(H_0\) for odd cycles and exactly the anti-periodic subspace for even cycles, yielding the incenter uniqueness and dimension statements.

## Originality
PASS. The full same-object literature was inspected at three particularly relevant sources: the symmetric-edge facet/cycle paper, the later facet-subgraph paper, and the weighted-cycle Kantorovich--Rubinstein paper whose unit-weight case is exactly the same polytope. None contains a Euclidean inradius or center theorem. Candidate-specific database and literature searches covered symmetric-edge, adjacency, KR, Lipschitz-free, cycle, inradius, insphere, tangent-facet, and Chebyshev-center aliases. Existing formulas for complete multipartite and wheel symmetric edge polytopes do not imply the cycle family; the one overlap \(C_4=K_{2,2}\) is explicitly treated as a consistency check rather than new content.

## Value
PASS. Cycles are a canonical graph family in the symmetric-edge and finite-metric-space literature. The theorem determines their Euclidean inradius in closed form, classifies the maximal-insphere contacts, and exposes a strong parity phenomenon in the center set. In particular, exponentially or combinatorially many facets collapse to only \(n\) or \(2n\) active contacts, while even cycles have a positive-dimensional incenter family and odd cycles have a unique center.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest papers determine facets, face subgraphs, triangulations, lattice invariants, and weighted-cycle KR boundary complexes. They do not optimize Euclidean facet distances. The theorem is restricted to unweighted cycles in the standard root-coordinate metric; weighted cycles and other graph families need separate analysis.
