# Cyclic four-blocks characterize induced second-homology support in the ten-vertex \(S^2\times S^1\)
## Finding
Let \(K=M^3_2(10)\) be the cyclic ten-vertex triangulation of \(S^2\times S^1\) with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\), generated by the three difference cycles
\[
(1:1:2:6),\qquad (1:1:6:2),\qquad (1:2:1:6).
\]
For a vertex subset \(S\subseteq V(K)\), write \(K[S]\) for the induced subcomplex and define
\[
r(S)=\dim_{\mathbf F_2}\operatorname{im}\!\left(H_2(K[S];\mathbf F_2)\longrightarrow H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\right).
\]
Then
\[
r(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{i,i+1,i+2,i+3\}\subseteq S
\]
for some \(i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\), with indices modulo \(10\). Otherwise \(r(S)=0\).

The ten cyclic four-blocks are exactly the missing tetrahedra of \(K\): all four triangular faces occur, but the tetrahedron itself does not. Each therefore induces \(\partial\Delta^3\), and each maps to the unique nonzero class in \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2\).

For \(k=0,\ldots,10\), the numbers of \(k\)-vertex subsets with \(r(S)=1\) are
\[
0,0,0,0,10,50,100,100,45,10,1.
\]
Thus the minimum vertex support of global second mod-two homology is four, with exactly ten minimal supports. Moreover, eight is the least \(k\) for which every \(k\)-vertex induced subcomplex surjects onto \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns exactly the labeled cyclic complex above and coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). A "cyclic four-block" means \(\{i,i+1,i+2,i+3\}\) modulo \(10\). No assertion is made for other ten-vertex triangulations of \(S^2\times S^1\), for other coefficient rings, or for non-induced subcomplexes.

The published construction identifies this complex as a ten-vertex triangulation of \(S^2\times S^1\). The proof below independently reconstructs its simplicial face set from the three displayed difference cycles.

## Proof
Expanding each displayed difference cycle under translation by \(\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\) gives \(30\) tetrahedra. Closing under faces yields
\[
f(K)=(10,40,60,30).
\]
Over \(\mathbf F_2\), the boundary matrices satisfy
\[
\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=30,\qquad \operatorname{rank}\partial_3=29,
\]
so \(\dim H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)=1\).

For each of the \(2^{10}=1024\) vertex subsets \(S\), form the induced chain complex of \(K[S]\). A basis for \(Z_2(K[S];\mathbf F_2)\) is embedded in the global two-chain group. If \(B_2(K)=\operatorname{im}\partial_3\), then
\[
r(S)=\dim\left((B_2(K)+Z_2(K[S]))/B_2(K)\right),
\]
which is computed by one additional binary rank calculation. Exhaustion of all \(1024\) subsets gives exactly the layer counts stated above.

Independently, exhaustion of all \(\binom{10}{4}=210\) four-subsets shows that the only four-sets whose four triangular faces all occur while the tetrahedron does not are the ten cyclic four-blocks. Each is therefore an induced tetrahedral sphere. The global rank test sends every one of those ten spheres to the nonzero element of \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\), and no other four-set has nonzero image.

Finally, the exhaustive subset calculation verifies that a subset has nonzero image exactly when it contains one of these four-blocks. The threshold statement can also be seen directly: every eight-subset has complement of size two, so its retained vertices form at most two cyclic runs totaling eight vertices; at least one run has length at least four. By contrast, the seven-set \(\{0,1,2,4,5,6,8\}\) contains no cyclic run of length four.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs the complex from the three difference cycles, computes the binary boundary ranks, checks every four-subset for the missing-tetrahedron condition, and exhausts all \(1024\) induced vertex subsets. Its successful terminal line is

`VERIFY_OK f=10,40,60,30 H2_F2=1 missing4=10 support_generators=10 support_layers=4:10,5:50,6:100,7:100,8:45,9:10,10:1 threshold=8 criterion=cyclic4block`

The computation is finite and exhaustive for this ten-vertex complex. It is not being used to infer an infinite family statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Walkup established the sharp ten-vertex range for orientable triangulated \(S^2\times S^1\) and explicitly constructed a neighborly ten-vertex example. Kühnel and Lassmann later placed the ten-vertex \([112]\) complex in their cyclic sphere-bundle family, and Spreer's cyclic classification records the exact three difference cycles used here together with the global homology type. Altshuler's complete ten-vertex neighborly-manifold classification and Kühnel--Lassmann's earlier cyclic/dihedral classification provide broader exact-object context.

Those sources establish existence, topology, symmetry, global homology, and classification data. The inspected statements do not classify which induced vertex subsets carry the global second mod-two class, do not identify all ten minimal four-vertex supports, and do not give the sharp universal surjectivity threshold eight. In particular, existence of a missing tetrahedral boundary in handle arguments does not imply the quantified criterion over all \(1024\) induced subsets.

## Limitations
The claim is object-specific and mod two. The computation does not classify integral representatives, other coefficient fields, or analogous support spectra for the full cyclic sphere-bundle family. Originality was checked against the principal exact-object and small-vertex classification sources inspected here and against a published-result index; an unindexed historical finite computation remains a residual risk.
