# Cyclic symmetry on Möbius–Kantor independence homology
## Finding
Let \(r\) be the order-eight automorphism \(u_i\mapsto u_{i+1}\), \(v_i\mapsto v_{i+1}\) of the Möbius–Kantor graph \(M=G(8,3)\). As \(\mathbb Q[C_8]\)-modules, \(H_3(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbf 1\oplus\varepsilon\oplus V_4\) and \(H_4(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Q)\cong\varepsilon\), where \(\varepsilon(r)=-1\) and \(V_4\) is the two-dimensional rational irreducible on which \(r\) has minimal polynomial \(x^2+1\). Equivalently, for \(k=0,\ldots,7\), the two characters on \(r^k\) are \((4,0,0,0,4,0,0,0)\) and \((1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1)\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
Write the Möbius–Kantor graph as \(M=G(8,3)\) with vertices \(u_0,\ldots,u_7,v_0,\ldots,v_7\), indices modulo \(8\), and edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+3}\). Let \(C_8=\langle rangle\) act by the simultaneous cyclic shift \(r(u_i)=u_{i+1}\), \(r(v_i)=v_{i+1}\). The action on vertices induces a simplicial action on \(\operatorname{Ind}(M)\) and hence rational homology representations.

## Proof
The exact base certificate reconstructs all \(1090\) nonempty independent faces and gives an acyclic discrete-Morse matching with one critical \(0\)-cell, four critical \(3\)-cells, and one critical \(4\)-cell. Its integral Morse boundary from degree \(4\) to degree \(3\) is zero. Thus
\[
H_3(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4,
\qquad
H_4(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and the other positive-dimensional homology groups vanish.

For every \(k\in\{0,\ldots,7}\), the verifier computes the signed trace of \(r^k\) on each oriented simplicial chain group by summing orientation signs over setwise fixed simplices. The resulting Lefschetz numbers are
\[
L(r^k)=(-2,0,2,0,-2,0,2,0).
\]
Because \(H_4\) is infinite cyclic, each \(r^k\) acts there by \(\pm1\). Exact quotient calculations modulo \(3\) and modulo \(5\) both show that the generator \(r\) acts by \(-1\), so
\[
\chi_4(r^k)=(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1).
\]
Since \(H_0\) is the trivial one-dimensional representation and no other positive homology is present, the Lefschetz identity
\[
L(r^k)=1-\chi_3(r^k)+\chi_4(r^k)
\]
gives
\[
\chi_3(r^k)=(4,0,0,0,4,0,0,0).
\]
The rational characters of the trivial module \(\mathbf1\), the sign module \(arepsilon\), and the two-dimensional module \(V_4\) with minimal polynomial \(x^2+1\) add to this \(H_3\) character. Maschke semisimplicity over \(\mathbb Q\) then yields the stated module decomposition.

## Relationship to prior work
Bıyıkoğlu and Civan explicitly use the Möbius–Kantor graph as an independence-complex example and determine its nonequivariant homotopy type in their study of prime graphs and regularity. That result fixes the underlying homology dimensions but does not, from the checked material and indexed descriptions, determine the action of the canonical order-eight rotation on those homology groups. Matsushita and Wakatsuki provide a primary-topology independence-complex framework under MSC 55P10. MathWorld identifies the graph with \(G(8,3)\) and records its high symmetry. Knill studies symmetries and Lefschetz numbers of the Möbius–Kantor graph itself, rather than the induced action on the independence complex. Searches combining the graph aliases with equivariant homology, cyclic rotation, and representation terminology found no statement of the two characters above.

## Limitations
The claim concerns only the canonical cyclic subgroup generated by simultaneous rotation, not the full automorphism group of order \(96\). The 2019 prime-graph paper is the closest same-object source; a searchable excerpt confirms that it computes the base independence-complex homotopy type, while the full publisher PDF was not fully inspectable through the available text interface. This access limitation is a residual originality risk, but the nonequivariant result alone does not imply the stated \(C_8\)-module structure. No claim is made about integral module decomposition beyond the explicitly verified rank-one sign action in degree \(4\).
