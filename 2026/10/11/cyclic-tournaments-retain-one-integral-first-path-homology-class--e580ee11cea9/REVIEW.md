# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof works in the standard regular path chain complex. For every long tournament arc, the displayed allowed \(2\)-path has an entirely allowed boundary and supplies the stated reduction. After all reductions, the cycle condition on the directed Hamiltonian cycle forces a single integer coefficient. The displacement functional extends to all regular ordered pairs and annihilates the full boundary of every allowed \(2\)-path, hence every \(\Omega_2\)-boundary, while taking value \(2m+1\) on the generator. This simultaneously proves nontriviality and absence of torsion in the generated group.

## Originality
PASS. Searches compared the claim against cyclic/carousel/circulant/regular-tournament aliases and against both broader path-homology results and same-object results for other topological constructions. The closest database records either compute a directed Hom complex for \(R_9\) or compute first path homology for a different alternating-pole family. Full-text inspection of arXiv:2102.10518 found the general path-homology/discrete-Morse framework but no tournament, cyclic, or circulant specialization. Full-text inspection of arXiv:2008.11885 found general definitions and small-digraph computations but no cyclic-tournament result. No searched source implies the all-parameter integral statement.

## Value
PASS. Cyclic tournaments are canonical maximally dense strongly connected orientations, so persistence of one integral path-homology class across the entire family is a structural phenomenon rather than an isolated small computation. The chord-reduction normal form and winding obstruction explain exactly why density does not kill first path homology here, complementing probabilistic work where first path homology can disappear in dense regimes.

Closest literature: arXiv:2102.10518 supplies the topology-owned path-homology framework; arXiv:2111.13493 studies first Betti numbers in random directed graphs. Neither gives this deterministic cyclic-tournament calculation. Limitation: an equivalent result could exist in unindexed literature under another tournament alias, and no higher path-homology groups are claimed.
