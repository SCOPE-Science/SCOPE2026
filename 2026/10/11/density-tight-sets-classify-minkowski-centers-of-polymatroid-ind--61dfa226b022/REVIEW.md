**Correctness — PASS.** The exact containment criterion is reconstructed for the same normalized polymatroid independence polytope as in the claim. The reflected inclusion holds if and only if the scaled center dominates every axis intercept and lies inside the scaled independence polytope; setting the scaled center to the singleton-weight vector realizes the sharp density value. The rank-difference function is nonnegative submodular, making the tight sets a finite lattice. Their maximal union pins the fixed coordinates. Submodularity contracts every residual constraint to the maximal tight union; its strictly positive capacities leave a full-dimensional residual orthant box. This proves *all* centers, dimension, and the unique-center test, not just a lower bound. Matroid density equals fractional arboricity by established literature. Exact-rational finite tests check nontrivial cases, but the proof is universal and independent of enumeration.

**Originality — PASS with explicitly bounded residual uncertainty.** Ferroni [1] supplies polymatroid and independence-polytope definitions, not the reflection-center geometry. Van den Heuvel and Thomassé [2] and Catlin et al. [3] already give matroid density and fractional arboricity; those are **not** new claims. Truong–Poggi-Corradini [4] optimize the base-modulus density, distinct from this independence-polytope minimization. Jochemko–Ravichandran [6] use Minkowski-linear-functionals in a different meaning. Graph stable-set and order-polytope results do not imply this general matroid theorem: the uniform matroid \(U_{2,4}\) is not a graph stable-set system. Searches covered exact and alias terms, but failed searches alone are not treated as evidence of universal novelty. The indexed Catlin et al. article is highly relevant; full text could not be inspected after bounded lawful attempts, and equivalent convex-corner formulations remain a risk.

**Value — PASS.** The result completely determines a canonical affine-geometric invariant and *every* minimizing center over all finite polymatroid independence polytopes. Its maximal-density tight core isolates rigid coordinates and gives an exact residual packing polytope; uniform density becomes a sharp uniqueness criterion. The bridge-attached triangle has a nontrivial one-dimensional center locus whereas the uniform matroid has a singleton. This is a motivated structural classification, not a random numerical cutout or a renaming of the preexisting arboricity identity.

The theorem is restricted to positive singleton capacities and the independence-polytope coordinate realization. Its residual function is not assumed monotone; the result does not cover base-polytopes by silent substitution. Literature priority and mathematical validity have not been independently assessed.
