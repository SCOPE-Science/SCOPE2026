# Dihedral Euler characters of full strong Morse complexes of cycles

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\), let \(C_n\) be the cycle on the vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and put \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}(C_n)\), the **full** strong discrete Morse matching complex in the sense of Knudson--Owens-White. The dihedral action is generated by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\). For a group element \(g\), write
\[
\mathcal L_n(g)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j\operatorname{tr}\!\left(g_*\mid\widetilde H_j(X_n;\mathbb Q)\right).
\]
Define \(L(d)=-1+6\mathbf 1_{3\mid d}-4\mathbf 1_{4\mid d}\), and define \(Z_0=1\), \(Z_1=-1\), \(Z_{j+2}=-Z_j\) for \(j\ge0\). The complete dihedral **virtual homology character** is
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a)=L(\gcd(n,a)),
\]
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a s)=\begin{cases}
-Z_{(n-3)/2}, & n\text{ odd},\
-Z_{n/2-1}, & n\text{ even and }a\text{ even},\
-Z_{n/2-2}, & n\text{ even and }a\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, with \(C_n=\langle r\rangle\) and \(B_n=-Z_{(n-3)/2}\) for odd \(n\), or \(B_n=-(Z_{n/2-1}+Z_{n/2-2})/2\) for even \(n\),
\[
\widetilde\chi(X_n/C_n)=-1+2\mathbf1_{3\mid n}-\mathbf1_{4\mid n},\qquad
\widetilde\chi(X_n/D_n)=\frac{-1+2\mathbf1_{3\mid n}-\mathbf1_{4\mid n}+B_n}{2}.
\]
Both quotient values depend only on \(n\bmod24\); the full formula gives the alternating trace of **every** dihedral element, not only the quotient averages.

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite, and homology is reduced rational homology. A vertex of \(X_n\) is a possible matching of a vertex \(i\) of \(C_n\) with its immediately clockwise or counterclockwise incident edge. Such matching vertices form faces if they can simultaneously occur in a strong Morse matching from some strict vertex filtration; this is the full complex, not the pure maximal-facet subcomplex or the ordinary Forman Morse complex. A fixed simplex contributes to an alternating chain trace with the orientation sign of the induced permutation of its matching vertices. No acyclicity of the quotient and no individual Betti-number formula are asserted.

## Proof
**1. Three-symbol face criterion.** Represent a candidate simplex by a cyclic word \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1})\) over \(\{0,+,-\}\), where \(0\) is an unmatched vertex and the other two symbols encode its clockwise and counterclockwise choices. A strict vertex filtration induces an acyclic orientation of the cycle, i.e. a cyclic sequence of edge signs not all equal; a matching at vertex \(i\) forces the two adjacent edge signs to be equal to the indicated sign. Conversely any nonconstant cyclic sequence of edge signs is induced by a total vertex ordering (because an orientation of a cycle is acyclic exactly when it is not all clockwise or all counterclockwise).

Therefore \(u\) is a face if and only if (i) adjacent nonzero symbols never differ, and (ii) if the nonzero symbols have just one sign, the word has two consecutive zeros somewhere, permitting one edge sign opposite to the forced sign. The all-zero word is admissible. To prove sufficiency, assign the compelled sign to every edge incident to a marked vertex; (i) makes these assignments consistent. Either both signs occur, or a pair of consecutive zeros leaves an unconstrained edge that can receive the opposite sign. Necessity is immediate. This criterion also independently identifies the correct full strong complex.

**2. Signed fixed-face formula.** Let \(g\) act on the \(2n\) possible matching vertices. A simplex fixed setwise under \(g\) contributes \((-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|\sigma)=-(-1)^{c_g(\sigma)}\) to the reduced Lefschetz trace, where \(c_g(\sigma)\) is the number of cycles of the permutation of its selected matching vertices. The empty simplex is included and contributes \(-1\). Thus
\[
\mathcal L_n(g)=-\sum_{\sigma\text{ fixed by }g}(-1)^{c_g(\sigma)}.
\]

**3. Rotations.** A word fixed by \(r^a\) is obtained by repeating a cyclic word of length \(d=\gcd(n,a)\), and each nonzero position of the fundamental word contributes exactly one selected matching-vertex orbit. The face criterion is periodic and gives the same signed count as the length-\(d\) cyclic model; \(d=1,2\) also give exactly \(-1\) by direct one-/two-position inspection. For length \(d\ge3\), introduce the transfer matrix
\[
T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&t\1&t&0\1&0&t\end{pmatrix}
\]
for the adjacency restriction; subtract the two all-one-sign patterns whose zeros are pairwise nonadjacent. The latter patterns are counted by \(U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\1&t\end{pmatrix}\). The signed sum over faces (including the empty face) is \(\operatorname{tr}T(-1)^d-2\operatorname{tr}U(-1)^d\). Its negative simplifies, by the eigenvalues of the two matrices or by their recurrences, to
\[
L(d)=-1+6\mathbf1_{3\mid d}-4\mathbf1_{4\mid d}.
\]
This elementary transfer evaluation agrees with the \(d=1,2\) direct cases.

**4. Reflections.** A reflection reverses the signs \(+\) and \(-\). Any fixed vertex of the reflection on the underlying cycle must therefore carry \(0\). Any two adjacent vertices exchanged by the reflection must also both carry \(0\), since otherwise opposite nonzero symbols would be adjacent. Each remaining exchanged pair has choices \(0,+,-\); the two entries of a nonzero pair have opposite signs, so the global single-sign exclusion in step 1 never occurs. The free pairs form a linear path, whose adjacent nonzero choices cannot differ. Write \(Z_j\) for the weighted sum over words of length \(j\) on that path, assigning weight \(-1\) for each nonzero pair. A two-state recurrence on the aggregate weights of words ending in \(0\) versus either nonzero sign gives \(Z_0=1\), \(Z_1=-1\), \(Z_{j+2}=-Z_j\).

For odd \(n\), the axis fixes one vertex and swaps an opposite adjacent pair, leaving \((n-3)/2\) free pairs. For even \(n\), a vertex-axis reflection fixes two sites and leaves \(n/2-1\) free pairs; an edge-axis reflection forces two exchanged boundary pairs to zero and leaves \(n/2-2\) free pairs. Step 2 now yields precisely the three displayed reflection values.

**5. Quotients.** In characteristic zero, averaging the finite group action on rational chains computes the quotient Euler characteristic (equivalently, pass to the barycentric subdivision and transfer). Since \(\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(n/d)=n\) and, whenever \(h\mid n\), \(\sum_{d\mid n,\,h\mid d}\varphi(n/d)=n/h\), averaging the rotation formula yields \(-1+2\mathbf1_{3\mid n}-\mathbf1_{4\mid n}\). The reflection average is exactly \(B_n\), giving the dihedral result. The divisibility indicators have periods \(3,4\) and the sequence \(Z_j\) has period \(4\), hence both quotient formulas have periods dividing \(24\).

## Verification
The standalone stdlib program `verify_full_strong_dihedral.py` independently reconstructs the full simplex set from **all vertex orders** for \(3\le n\le8\), compares it with the new cyclic word criterion, and computes fixed-face signed Lefschetz traces using explicit permutations on the \(2n\) matching vertices. It verifies 66 dihedral group actions and separately checks all cyclic/dihedral quotient arithmetic identities for \(3\le n\le300\). The observed terminal line is `VERIFY_OK independent_filtration_faces=1734 signed_action_checks=66 arithmetic_sizes=298`. These bounded checks corroborate but do not substitute for the all-\(n\) proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson--Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (arXiv:2609.06844v1), introduce the full complex, compute the pure cycle homotopy type, and tabulate homology of the full cycle complexes through \(n=21\). They do not give the dihedral virtual homology character or these all-length quotient Euler formulas in the inspected preprint. Chari--Joswig's earlier work treats the **ordinary** discrete Morse matching complex rather than the full **strong** complex. The new contribution is the equivariant signed-face analysis; the existence and nonequivariant topology of the source complexes are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The theorem determines alternating character traces and reduced rational **Euler characteristics**, not the individual homology representations, quotient Betti numbers, integral homology, or quotient homotopy types. It does not classify the full strong cycle homology beyond previously computed ranges. No originality claim excludes every unpublished or unindexed equivalent argument.
