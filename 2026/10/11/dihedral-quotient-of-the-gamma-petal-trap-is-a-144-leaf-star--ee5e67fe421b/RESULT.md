# Dihedral quotient of the gamma petal trap is a 144-leaf star
## Finding
For Vernitski's hard nine-petal unknot \(\gamma=(0,2,4,7,3,8,6,1,5)\), let \(C_{\le 11}(\gamma)\) be the move-state set reachable using \(\mathcal M=\{\mathsf T^{\pm},\mathsf X,\mathsf R,\mathsf S\}\) without exceeding eleven petals, with cyclic rotations of positions identified. Quotient the states at each petal number \(N\) by the natural dihedral action \(\langle\mathsf R,\mathsf S\rangle\cong D_N\), and join two quotient orbits when some representatives differ by one move in \(\mathcal M\). Then \(C_{\le 11}(\gamma)\) has one nine-petal orbit of size \(18\) and exactly \(144\) eleven-petal orbits, each of size \(22\) and hence with trivial dihedral stabilizer; the quotient simple graph is exactly the star \(K_{1,144}\), centered at the nine-petal orbit. Every inter-orbit edge is realized by \(\mathsf T^+\) or \(\mathsf T^-\); the moves \(\mathsf X,\mathsf R,\mathsf S\) create no edge between distinct quotient orbits.

## Assumptions and scope
A petal word is taken up to cyclic rotation of its positions, as in the source enumeration. The move set is exactly \(\mathcal M=\{\mathsf T^{\pm},\mathsf X,\mathsf R,\mathsf S\}\) with the definitions in Sections 3.1--3.4 of Vernitski's preprint. The set \(C_{\le 11}(\gamma)\) contains only words reachable from the displayed hard word while every intermediate word has at most eleven petals. The quotient uses the natural order-\(2N\) dihedral action generated by \(\mathsf R\) and \(\mathsf S\) separately on each \(N\)-petal layer. The graph is a simple undirected quotient graph: loops and repeated edges are suppressed.

## Proof
The source proves that \(C_{\le 11}(\gamma)\) is closed, has exactly \(3186\) words, and splits into \(18\) words with nine petals and \(3168\) words with eleven petals. It also states that the nine-petal words form the dihedral orbit of \(\gamma\).

The accompanying verifier implements the published definitions directly. For \(\mathsf T^+\), it inserts either order of a consecutive height pair after applying the published height-raising map; \(\mathsf T^-\) deletes cyclically adjacent consecutive heights; \(\mathsf X\) checks the published paired-position nesting condition for every basepoint; and \(\mathsf R,\mathsf S\) are the stated height rotation and reverse-complement operations. Breadth-first search with the eleven-petal cap reconstructs exactly \(3186\) states, with layer sizes \(18\) and \(3168\). This agreement with the independently published totals is an internal check that the implemented state space and moves match the source conventions.

Next, each state is replaced by the lexicographically least member of its \(\langle\mathsf R,\mathsf S\rangle\)-orbit. The nine-petal layer has one orbit of size \(18\). The eleven-petal layer has \(144\) orbits, every one of size \(22\). Since the acting dihedral group has order \(22\), orbit-stabilizer gives a trivial stabilizer for every eleven-petal state.

Finally, the verifier projects every enumerated move edge to the orbit quotient. After loops and duplicate edges are removed there are exactly \(144\) quotient edges. The degree multiset is one vertex of degree \(144\) and \(144\) vertices of degree \(1\), with the unique high-degree vertex being the nine-petal orbit of \(\gamma\). Hence the quotient graph is \(K_{1,144}\). The labels of all edges joining distinct quotient vertices are only \(\mathsf T^+\) and \(\mathsf T^-\), proving the final assertion.

## Verification
Run `python3 petal_gamma_quotient_verify.py`. A successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`, the state total \(3186\), layer counts \(18\) and \(3168\), orbit profile consisting of one \(18\)-element orbit and \(144\) \(22\)-element orbits, \(144\) quotient edges, and degree profile \(1^{144},144^1\). The verifier is standalone and uses only the Python standard library.

## Relationship to prior work
Vernitski proves the hard-word classification and the excess statement for \(\gamma\), including the exact closed-set count \(3186=18+3168\), and notes that every available trivial deletion from the eleven-petal states returns to the orbit of \(\gamma\). The source does not give the dihedral orbit decomposition of the \(3168\) eleven-petal states, does not establish freeness of those dihedral actions, and does not identify the quotient adjacency graph. The count \(3168\) alone does not imply \(144\) free orbits or exclude crossing-exchange edges between them. Targeted searches for the exact orbit count, star quotient, crossing-exchange quotient structure, and equivalent formulations found no covering statement. A prior calculation on the same source determined shortest move distances for \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\); it neither concerns the trapped component of \(\gamma\) nor implies this quotient-graph structure.

## Limitations
The claim is only about the source's eleven-petal-capped reachable set. It does not describe the full unrestricted move graph, paths using thirteen or more petals, or the corresponding quotient for other hard petal words. The originality search can miss unindexed notes or unpublished computations of the same ancillary state graph.
