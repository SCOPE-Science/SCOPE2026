# Dihedral rational homology representations of Möbius-ladder independence complexes

## Finding
Let \(n\ge 2\), and let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder on vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\) with edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and \(\{i,i+n\}\), with indices modulo \(2n\). Write \(X_n=\operatorname{Ind}(M_{2n})\). The canonical dihedral action is generated by \(\rho(i)=i+1\) and \(\sigma(i)=-i\); its group has order \(4n\).

**Theorem.** The only nonzero reduced rational homology of \(X_n\) is in degree
\[
d(n)=\begin{cases}2k-1&n=4k,\\2k&n=4k+1,4k+2,4k+3.\end{cases}
\]
For \(n\not\equiv2\pmod4\), the homology is one-dimensional, with \(\rho\) acting by \((-1)^n\) and \(\sigma\) by \((-1)^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\). For \(n=4k+2\), the three-dimensional representation is \(W_{\square}\oplus\eta_k\), where
\[
W_{\square}(\rho)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad W_{\square}(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad \eta_k(\rho)=-1,\quad\eta_k(\sigma)=(-1)^k.
\]
Equivalently, in the exceptional case,
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^r\mid\widetilde H_{d(n)})=\begin{cases}3&4\mid r,\\-1&4\nmid r,\end{cases}\quad
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^r\sigma\mid\widetilde H_{d(n)})=(-1)^{r+k}.
\]
All vector spaces and representations in this statement are over \(\mathbb Q\). The matrix representation is an inflation of the ordinary symmetry action on a square.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple, with the two kinds of edges merged in the small boundary case \(n=2\) (then \(M_4=K_4\)). The independence complex is the simplicial complex of vertex subsets spanning no edge. The group is the explicit dihedral subgroup above (not necessarily the full automorphism group of the graph in exceptional small cases). Reduced homology is taken with rational coefficients; all traces use the natural map on oriented simplicial chains. In particular, no statement about an integral equivariant wedge splitting is intended.

## Proof
**1. An exact fixed-simplex formula.** For a graph automorphism \(g\) of finite order, form its orbits on vertices. Let \(Z(g)\) be the signed sum \(\sum(-1)^j\) over all choices of \(j\) orbits whose union is independent. Orbits with an internal graph edge are prohibited, and two different orbits conflict when an edge connects them. A fixed independent simplex with \(s\) vertices and \(j\) orbits contributes the orientation sign \((-1)^{s-j}\) to the trace on \(C_{s-1}\). Including the empty face in the augmented chain complex gives precisely
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(g;X_n)=-Z(g).
\]
This is the signed simplicial Lefschetz formula, not an assumption that a geometric fixed-point set has the ordinary quotient homotopy type.

**2. Rotations.** For \(g=\rho^r\), put \(h=\gcd(2n,r)\), taking \(h=2n\) when \(r=0\), and \(L=2n/h\). If \(L\) is even, the orbit of every vertex contains its antipode, so no nonempty orbit can be selected and \(Z(g)=1\). If \(L\) is odd, then \(h\) is even and the quotient conflict graph is exactly the Möbius ladder \(M_h\), so \(Z(g)=I(M_h;-1)\), where \(I\) is the independence polynomial.

Compute this polynomial by representing \(M_{2m}\) as a ladder with a swap at the end. In a column, the permitted independent states are empty, top, and bottom, weighted \(1,-1,-1\). With transfer and end-swap matrices
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-1\\1&0&-1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
one checks by enumerating consecutive allowed column states that \(I(M_{2m};-1)=\operatorname{tr}(T^mJ)\). The symmetric subspace has eigenvalues \(i,-i\) and \(J=1\); the antisymmetric subspace has \(T=1\) and \(J=-1\). Thus
\[
I(M_{2m};-1)=i^m+(-i)^m-1.
\]
For \(m\) modulo four, the sequence is \((-3,-1,1,-1)\) in residue order \(2,1,0,3\), respectively. This includes \(m=1\), since \(M_2=K_2\) by the quotient convention.

**3. Reflections.** A reflection has form \(g=\rho^c\sigma\), and the orbit-conflict graph depends only on the parity of \(c\). Listing orbits \(\{i,c-i\}\), omitting those with internal cycle or antipodal edges, and identifying conflicts of consecutive and antipodal orbits gives the ordinary two-row ladder \(P_{\lceil n/2\rceil}\square K_2\) for even \(c\), and \(P_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}\square K_2\) for odd \(c\); the second graph is empty when \(n=2\). To check the endpoint accounting explicitly, in the even-axis case the two fixed singleton orbits form the first column, while the other admissible two-vertex orbits pair into the remaining columns; in the odd-axis case the orbits containing an edge of the axis are forbidden, and the admissible pairs form the shorter ladder. In each case, cycle edges connect consecutive ladder columns and antipodal edges connect the two vertices within each column; this exhausts all possible conflicts.

Put \(A_j=I(P_j\square K_2;-1)\). Column decomposition gives \(A_0=1\), \(A_1=-1\), and \(A_j=-A_{j-2}\) for \(j\ge2\), so \((A_j)_{j\ge0}=(1,-1,-1,1,1,-1,\ldots)\). Consequently \(Z(\rho^{2r}\sigma)=A_{\lceil n/2\rceil}\) and \(Z(\rho^{2r+1}\sigma)=A_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}\).

**4. Pass from Lefschetz numbers to characters.** Agarwal and Basak's nonequivariant theorem, independently tested for the small cases described below, gives concentration in exactly degree \(d(n)\) with rank one except in residue class two, where the rank is three. Thus
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_{d(n)}(X_n;\mathbb Q))=(-1)^{d(n)}\widetilde\Lambda(g;X_n).
\]
Inserting the signed rotation and reflection counts yields \(\chi_n(\rho^r\sigma^\varepsilon)=(-1)^{nr+\varepsilon\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\) outside residue class two. For \(n=4k+2\), it yields rotational traces \(3\) for \(4\mid r\) and \(-1\) otherwise, and reflection traces \((-1)^{r+k}\). The displayed two- and one-dimensional modules have exactly these traces. Since rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and equal characters give isomorphic representations, the stated rational decompositions follow. This exhausts every group element and all \(n\ge2\).

## Verification
The included `verify_dihedral.py` constructs the graph independently from the modular adjacency rule. Its first algorithm exhaustively enumerates independent faces and directly computes signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz numbers; its second constructs all group-orbits and independently computes a signed independence polynomial of the quotient conflict graph. The two calculations agree with the theorem on all 308 group elements for \(2\le n\le12\), including identity and reflections; exact mod-two simplicial boundary-rank elimination separately checks the concentrated homology for \(2\le n\le9\). With `--extended`, a further 1,548 dihedral elements for \(13\le n\le30\) pass the orbit-quotient check. Actual checks reported `VERIFY_OK` and `EXTENDED_ORBIT_OK`. The finite tests corroborate but do not substitute for the transfer-matrix proof and the cited nonequivariant theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Anshu Agarwal and Biplab Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs*, arXiv:2608.30601 (first public version dated 2026-08-31; version 2 dated 2026-09-14), Theorem 12, determine the nonequivariant homotopy types of \(\operatorname{Ind}(M_{2n})\), including the periodic sphere dimensions and multiplicities. Their theorem does not identify the action of the dihedral symmetries on homology. The present theorem computes the entire rational dihedral character and identifies a two-dimensional square representation in the rank-three cases. General graph-independence-complex results and the classifications for other graph families neither give nor imply these action matrices. A representation-theoretic equivalent could be unindexed in the literature.

## Limitations
The calculation is a rational representation theorem, not an integral module classification, an integral lattice splitting, a geometric equivariant homotopy decomposition, or a determination of action of extra graph automorphisms in small cases. The proof invokes the published nonequivariant homology concentration; its independent exact homology checks cover finite values only. Unindexed equivalent equivariant calculations remain a possible priority risk; independent audit has not been performed.
