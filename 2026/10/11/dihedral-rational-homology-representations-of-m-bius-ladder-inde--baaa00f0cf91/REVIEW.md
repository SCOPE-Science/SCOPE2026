# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The theorem uses a fixed-simplex signed Lefschetz identity that keeps permutation orientations, an exact orbit-conflict reduction of rotations and reflections, a three-state transfer matrix with explicitly checked eigenvalues, and the published nonequivariant concentration theorem. Both independent finite approaches check all elements of the dihedral group for \(2\le n\le12\), with separate exact mod-two homology checks for \(2\le n\le9\), and an extended orbit comparison through \(n=30\). The proof treats \(n=2\), the empty reflection quotient, parity boundaries, and the rational semisimplicity step. Computations are not substituted for the uniform argument.

**Originality: PASS.** The August 2026 Agarwal–Basak paper gives nonequivariant homotopy groups and sphere multiplicities but no induced dihedral homology representations. Its 19-page text, including Theorem 12 and future directions, was inspected. Searches compared reflections, rotations, homology characters, quotient independence polynomials, and related graph-complex calculations; the closest available computations concern different graphs or different complexes. Neither the nonequivariant wedge type nor a different graph's homology determines the matrices here. Residual risk: an equivalent group-character calculation may be unindexed.

**Value: PASS.** A complete all-width rational dihedral module refines the known sphere-wedge classification and exhibits a genuine two-dimensional square representation in every \(n\equiv2\pmod4\) case. This resolves a natural symmetry question for an infinite graph family and distinguishes rank-equivalent but inequivalent symmetry actions. It does not claim an integral lattice or equivariant geometric decomposition.

**Closest source and limits.** Agarwal–Basak, arXiv:2608.30601, Theorem 12, concerns precisely the same complexes but only their nonequivariant homotopy type. Exact verification is finite; the proof and source theorem establish the general claim. Independent assessment remains outstanding.
