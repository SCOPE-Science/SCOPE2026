# Review
## Correctness — PASS
The source equations for \(n=2\) and \(n\ge3\) become a single positive-integral level equation \(H_{n-2}(r_n)=M_1/M_2\). The spatial monotonicity of \(H_p\) is immediate from its numerator and denominator intervals, while \(\partial_p\log H_p\) is the difference between the upper-interval and lower-interval weighted averages of \(\log s\), which is strictly positive because the intervals are separated by \(R>0\). The high-dimensional coefficient follows from an exact rescaled equation and the rigorously established estimate \((n-2)(r_n-R_1)\to0\). Quantifiers, the strict geometric gap, and the distinction between the limiting free-boundary problem and the finite-competition system are explicit.

## Originality — PASS
Claim-level searches used dimension monotonicity, high-dimensional asymptotics, annular-interface, radial-harmonic, and interaction-distance formulations. No published-finding corpus record or inspected primary source states the cross-dimensional ordering or the sharp exponential pinning law. The motivating paper gives the fixed-dimension equations from which the result is derived but does not compare dimensions. The closest older paper with “radius” in its title studies uniform spatial Lipschitz estimates for a different optimal-partition problem, not this interface coordinate.

## Value — PASS
Ambient dimension is a structural parameter of the source's arbitrary-dimensional theorem, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The finding gives a complete direction-of-motion theorem across all dimensions and identifies the exact boundary-layer scale and prefactor as dimension grows. It explains how radial harmonic geometry redistributes the fixed interaction gap and supplies a quantitative high-dimensional limit that is not available from the source's separate fixed-dimension sensitivity formulas.

## Closest literature and limitations
The decisive comparison is the full motivating paper, arXiv:2610.02141v1. The general long-range segregation paper DOI:10.4171/JEMS/747 and the higher-dimensional regularity paper arXiv:2605.07043v1 address existence/regularity rather than this annular dimension sequence. The full text of arXiv:2106.03661v1 was also checked because its title mentions dependence on a radius; its object is a variational eigenvalue partition and its main result is a uniform spatial Lipschitz bound. A residual bibliographic risk remains that an equivalent integral-ratio monotonicity appears in older radial potential-theory language, but no implication-equivalent model statement was located.
