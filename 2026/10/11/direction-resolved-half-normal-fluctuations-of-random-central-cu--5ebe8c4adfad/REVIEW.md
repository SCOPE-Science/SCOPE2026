# Review of the directional cube-section half-normal limit

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has an exact domain (Haar-uniform central hyperplanes, fixed coordinate directions, dimensions tending to infinity) and a self-contained proof. Vertex–edge identification and the crossing indicator are exact off a probability-zero exceptional set. The nontrivial dependence estimate uses the even Hermite expansion and the exact mean of squared pair correlations \(1/(n-1)\); it does not assume independent edge tests. Conditional variance decomposition, the sixth Gaussian moment, and a Cauchy ratio prove the quantitative \(L^2\) estimate. Convergence of second moments proves the positive asymptotic variance. The independent finite enumerations and Gaussian sanity checks support the indexing but are not proof of the infinite-dimensional assertion.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the stated residual risk
Brandenburg–Meroni (2025), arXiv:2510.09265v1, gives finite-dimensional combinatorial classifications and mentions expected vertex counts; Lonke (1998/2000), arXiv:math/9812123v1, derives the expected count. Calkin et al. (2008), *Cuts of the Hypercube*, works with the same Haar-normal model and computes probabilities of crossing one or two designated edges, not a joint high-dimensional half-normal law for directionwise aggregated counts. The named statement and its proof are not implications of those inspected conclusions without a new many-edge dependence argument. Search evidence for equivalent formulations, stronger coverage, tables, and original implications is recorded in `AUDIT.json`. A previously unnoticed publication could contain an equivalent asymptotic limit; absence from searches alone would not prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The geometry is canonical: cube sections, coordinate edge families, and Haar normals. The result supplies a complete asymptotic distribution for a natural vector of direction-resolved section-vertex counts, beyond the known total expectation. The strictly positive limiting variance establishes a nonconcentration phenomenon despite exponentially many candidate edges, and asymptotic independence of finitely many directions clarifies the geometric source of fluctuations. It is not a routine perturbation of a previously tabulated finite-dimensional numerical value.

## Scope and scientific limitations
The result says nothing about arbitrary faces, noncentral slices, arbitrary normal laws, joint limits in a growing number of directions, or the full total-count distribution. No claim of a sharp finite-dimensional variance bound or independent certification is made. The sources are 2025 Brandenburg–Meroni, 1998/2000 Lonke, and 2008 Calkin et al.; an equivalent result in unindexed literature remains possible.
