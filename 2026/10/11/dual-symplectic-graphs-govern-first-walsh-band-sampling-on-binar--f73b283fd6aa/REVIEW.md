# Scientific review: first-Walsh-band sampling on binary quadrics

## Correctness — PASS
The calculation of the quadratic Gauss transform explicitly uses an invertible alternating polar form, yielding the exact signed character transform. Factoring the dual quadratic by its polar form shows the compressed matrix is diagonally equivalent to the claimed real Seidel-type matrix. The graph-realization argument uses the parity form of the Pfaffian identity and the fact that linear refinements realize both Gauss signs. The two-point intersection count for the quadratic-zero difference set is strictly positive for all nonzero shifts when the ambient dimension is at least four; this proves injectivity by coordinate differencing. The two-dimensional exceptional rank follows from the one-point elliptic quadric. For dimension four, all four relevant graph isomorphism types and their exact characteristic polynomials are enumerated, with a second exact integer checker. The matched-pair eigenvalues follow from a two-dimensional invariant sector plus pairwise symmetric and antisymmetric sectors. These arguments cover the stated quantifiers, boundaries and normalization.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The closest inspected source on quadratic bent Cayley graphs is Leopardi's 2015 work, especially its bent difference-set and strongly-regular-graph material; it concerns full Cayley spectra, not a fixed first-Walsh-band compression. His 2017 classification treats global extended Cayley equivalences and therefore does not imply that the coordinate-specific spectral matrices have three types in dimension four. Kaşıkcı's publicly available thesis studies quadratic Walsh spectra and plateaued functions but does not identify the inverse-polar-graph compression and its sampling-rank criterion. Published research-record searches under terms including dual quadrics, Reed–Muller puncturing, Walsh concentration, and Seidel compressions did not expose a directly covering statement. A historical equivalent implicit in punctured Reed–Muller codes, binary quadratic difference sets, or principal submatrices of Hadamard designs remains possible. Failure of a search is not treated as a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Fourier sampling set is the natural zero locus of a nonsingular bent quadratic, and the constant-plus-coordinate band is a canonical low-frequency Walsh space. The result simultaneously identifies the exact sampling spectrum through an inverse-polar graph, proves universal uniqueness of sampling from dimension four onward, realizes precisely the odd-perfect-matching graph class, and gives a complete first nontrivial dimension-four spectral classification. Its distinction between globally affine-equivalent forms and coordinate-sensitive sampling spectra explains why the exact invariant is not a restatement of the classical bent property. The resulting eigenvalues determine sharp reconstruction bounds for any chosen coordinate system, and the matched-pair family gives a closed-form example in all even dimensions.

General proofs are independent of those finite checks. The theorem does not cover higher Walsh degrees or degenerate quadrics, and potential equivalents in older algebraic coding literature have not been exhaustively excluded.
