# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. Orthogonal contact with a face makes the surface normal equal, up to sign, to the planar unit normal of the boundary curve, so plane-curve total absolute curvature equals Gauss-image arclength. The established degree-one Gauss image is a spherical square whose adjacent vertices have inner product \(1/2\), hence each side has length \(\pi/3\). The exact Gauss-map derivative is nonzero on the open boundary side, so the signed curvature does not vanish there and each arc is strictly convex.

## Originality

PASS. The recent exact representation gives the Gauss square, total Gaussian curvature, boundary length, and area, but not the total Euclidean curvature of one planar free-boundary arc. The original 1984 paper proves qualitative convexity and bounds each tangential indicatrix by less than \(\pi\), without an exact regular-tetrahedron value. Targeted statement-level searches found no equivalent \(\pi/3\) turning theorem.

## Value

PASS. The theorem supplies a natural scale-free boundary invariant for the canonical regular-tetrahedron free-boundary minimal disk and sharpens a four-decade-old qualitative turning bound. It also converts the newly explicit cuboctahedral Gauss image into directly testable extrinsic geometry of the physical contact curves.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest sources are the exact 2026 representation and Smyth's original 1984 existence-and-boundary analysis. The theorem concerns only the four smooth face arcs of the regular-tetrahedron disk. It does not include corner turning, a pointwise curvature formula, arbitrary tetrahedra, or global area-minimizing properties.
