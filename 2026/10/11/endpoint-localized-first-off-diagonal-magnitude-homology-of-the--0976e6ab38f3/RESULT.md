# Endpoint-localized first off-diagonal magnitude homology of the Pappus graph
## Finding
Let \(P\) be the Pappus graph represented by the LCF word \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\), with shortest-path metric \(d\). For every ordered pair \((x,y)\in V(P)^2\),
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{MH}^{x,y}_{3,4}(P;\mathbb F_2)=
\begin{cases}
2,&d(x,y)=2,\\
1,&d(x,y)=4,\\
0,&d(x,y)\in\{0,1,3\}.
\end{cases}
\]
The distance shells from each vertex have sizes \((1,3,6,6,2)\), hence there are \(108\) ordered pairs at distance \(2\) and \(36\) at distance \(4\). Therefore
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(P;\mathbb F_2)=108\cdot2+36\cdot1=252.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Magnitude chains use the shortest-path metric on the finite simple graph \(P\). For fixed length \(\ell=4\), \(\operatorname{MC}_{k,4}\) is generated by tuples \((x_0,\ldots,x_k)\) with consecutive entries distinct and total metric length
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k d(x_{i-1},x_i)=4.
\]
The differential deletes an interior entry exactly when the deletion preserves total length. The computation is over \(\mathbb F_2\), so signs do not affect matrix ranks. The endpoint subcomplex fixes \((x_0,x_k)=(x,y)\). No claim is made here about integral torsion or coefficients other than \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Proof
The supplied verifier constructs the graph directly from the LCF word and independently checks that it has \(18\) vertices, \(27\) edges, degree \(3\), girth \(6\), diameter \(4\), and distance-shell vector \((1,3,6,6,2)\) at every vertex.

For every one of the \(18^2\) ordered endpoint pairs, it exhaustively enumerates the endpoint-localized chain groups in degrees \(2,3,4\) at length \(4\), forms the two boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\), verifies the chain condition, and computes exact bit-elimination ranks. The complete local data depend only on endpoint distance:

| \(d(x,y)\) | \(\dim C_2\) | \(\dim C_3\) | \(\dim C_4\) | \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3\) | \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_4\) | \(\dim H_3\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 6 | 18 | 15 | 6 | 12 | 0 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 7 | 18 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 2 |
| 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 12 | 18 | 6 | 11 | 6 | 1 |

Thus \(\dim H_3=\dim C_3-\operatorname{rank}\partial_3-\operatorname{rank}\partial_4\) gives the stated endpoint formula. Summing the endpoint blocks yields global chain dimensions \((1296,2916,1458)\) in degrees \((2,3,4)\), boundary ranks \((1260,1404)\), and therefore \(2916-1260-1404=252\).

## Relationship to prior work
Asao, Hiraoka, and Kanazawa established girth-controlled vanishing near the diagonal in magnitude homology. For the first off-diagonal group \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}\), their general vanishing theorem applies at girth at least \(7\), whereas the Pappus graph has girth \(6\). Thus the present computation lies exactly at the first girth boundary not determined by that theorem. Their paper supplies the magnitude-chain conventions and endpoint localization used here.

Gu's algebraic-Morse computations cover trees, a diagonal graph class, the icosahedral graph, and cycles; the inspected text contains no Pappus computation. A separate published result for incidence graphs of finite projective planes gives endpoint-localized \(\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}\) values for that family. The Pappus graph is instead the Levi graph of the Pappus \(9_3\) configuration, so that projective-plane statement does not imply this calculation.

## Limitations
The result is an exact finite computation for one graph and one bidegree over \(\mathbb F_2\). It does not identify integral torsion, establish a general theorem for all symmetric configurations, or determine other bidegrees. The literature comparison found no statement implying this endpoint profile, but absence from the searched sources is not an absolute guarantee that no unindexed computation exists.
