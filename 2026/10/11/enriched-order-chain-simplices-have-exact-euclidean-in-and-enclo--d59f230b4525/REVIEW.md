# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The chain specialization of the published signed-filter definition reduces to an explicit \(n\)-simplex. Direct vertex substitution proves its \(n+1\) facet inequalities and their normal norms. A positive dependence
\[
b+\sum_{k=1}^{n}2^{k-1}a_k=0
\]
turns the common facet-distance equations into the exact inradius formula. The enclosing center follows from exact perpendicular-bisector recurrences. Because that center lies in the ordinary order polytope, which is contained in the enriched order polytope, the common vertex sphere has a convex contact certificate and is the unique minimum enclosing sphere.

## Originality
PASS. The introducing enriched-order paper was inspected at its construction and facet section, and the later geometry-classified paper was inspected at its signed-filter definition, transfer theorem, and defining inequalities. Neither states an inradius, circumradius, or minimum-enclosing-sphere theorem. Targeted published-result searches covered enriched-order, enriched-Hibi, chain-poset, inradius, circumcenter, and smallest-enclosing formulations. The only same-family internal overlap is an enriched-chain-polytope radius theorem, which does not transfer through the non-isometric enriched transfer map.

## Value
PASS. The chain case is a canonical reflexive simplex family, not an arbitrary slice. The result gives a complete exact Euclidean ball profile and shows that the two Ehrhart-equivalent enriched poset polytopes of a chain have sharply different metric scaling. The aspect ratio of the enriched-order simplex grows like \(n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt3)\), compared with \(\sqrt n\) for its enriched-chain crosspolytope partner.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature provides reflexivity, general facets, Ehrhart equivalence, transfer maps, and unimodular triangulations. The result is realization-specific and covers only chain posets; it does not provide radii for arbitrary enriched order polytopes. A generic simplex-radius treatment applied independently to the same chain vertices remains the principal residual originality risk.
