# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For a finite connected simple bipartite graph \(G\), the complex \(I_3(G)\) has dimension at most three. The exact equivariant triangle and tetrahedron matching maps have, respectively, an adjacent-edge-pair kernel and an unsigned four-cycle kernel. Their elimination, together with the graph cycle-space exact sequence and the virtual symmetric-square identity, proves
\[
\sum_j(-1)^j[\widetilde H_j(I_3(G);\mathbb Q)]
=[\mathbb Q[\mathcal C_4(G)]]-[\operatorname{Sym}^2 H_1(G;\mathbb Q)].
\]
The symmetric-square trace formula gives every graph-automorphism Lefschetz number, and averaging over finite symmetry groups yields rational quotient Euler characteristics. Independent direct-face signed character calculations on 230 small connected bipartite graphs (786 automorphisms) and all 48 cube automorphisms agreed; these finite cases corroborate rather than prove the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, best-of-knowledge
The published **all-connected-bipartite three-sphere wedge theorem** already gives the same nonequivariant Euler characteristic \(c_4(G)-\binom{b+1}{2}\) and degree-three homology concentration. That part of the present conclusion is covered prior mathematics and is not claimed as a novel sphere or Euler count. The prior abstract homotopy type does not determine the graph-automorphism representation, nonidentity Lefschetz traces, or quotient Euler numbers. The original grid-like robust-complex theorem, the bounded-independence and higher-secant algebra literature, and a published ordinary three-cut square-grid character theorem were compared at their actual statements; none of their inspected results implies the universal equivariant virtual-module formula. A related exact formulation in unindexed literature cannot be excluded. Relative chronological priority among records sharing the same public collection date is not established.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The representation-valued identity is a nontrivial structural refinement for a natural graph-complex family. It computes a full set of symmetry-sensitive invariants from four-cycle permutations and first graph homology. Its finite-group quotient and grid/cube character consequences are not determined by the previously published nonequivariant sphere ranks alone.

## Scope and limitations
The statement assumes a finite connected simple bipartite graph and uses rational representations. It determines alternating homology, not integral torsion or a general separate degreewise representation. The one-vertex case is included. Finite examples are consistency checks, not a proof of an infinite theorem.
