# Equivariant homology and symmetry quotients of complete-graph flex complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(F_n=\mathrm{Flex}(K_n)\), the cubical complex of source/sink flips of orientations of the complete graph on \([n]\). Relabeling induces an action of \(S_n\). The following formulas determine both reduced rational homology characters in all degrees and the actual homeomorphism types of the symmetric- and alternating-group quotients.

For a permutation \(g\in S_n\), let \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_c)\) be its multiset of cycle lengths, and let \(f\) be the multiplicity of \(1\) in \(\lambda\). For a partition \(\mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_s)\), define
\[
B(\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^s\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_i}{2}\right\rfloor+\sum_{1\le i<j\le s}\gcd(\mu_i,\mu_j).
\]
Define \(N(\mu)=2^{B(\mu)}\) if all parts of \(\mu\) are odd, and \(N(\mu)=0\) otherwise. With \(h\) denoting the multiplicity of \(1\) in \(\mu\), set
\[
\Phi(\mu)=N(\mu)-2hN(\mu\setminus1)+h(h-1)N(\mu\setminus1^2),
\]
where a term multiplied by zero is omitted, so removal is required only if sufficient singleton parts exist. For \(0\le t\le f\), write \((f)_t=f!/(f-t)!\) and \(\lambda\setminus1^t\) for deletion of \(t\) singleton parts. Put
\[
P_n(\lambda)=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(f,n-3)}(f)_t\,\Phi(\lambda\setminus1^t).
\]
Finally define
\[
C_n(\lambda)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(d)d^k k!}{n}, & \lambda=(d^k),\quad dk=n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient and \((d^k)\) means \(k\) equal cycles of length \(d\). The complete reduced rational homology character is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_1(F_n;\mathbb Q))=C_n(\lambda),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_0(F_n;\mathbb Q))=C_n(\lambda)+P_n(\lambda)-1.}
\]
All other reduced rational homology groups vanish. In particular, \(H_1(F_n;\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{S_n}\mathbf1\), where \(C_n\) is generated by an \(n\)-cycle.

For \(m\ge3\), let \(a_m\) be the number of isomorphism classes of tournaments on \(m\) vertices having neither a source nor a sink. It is determined without a tournament-isomorphism algorithm by
\[
a_m=\sum_{\mu\vdash m}\frac{\Phi(\mu)}{z_\mu},\qquad
z_\mu=\prod_{j\ge1}j^{m_j(\mu)}m_j(\mu)!.
\]
Let \(I_t\) denote the closed edge-path of length \(t\), with \(I_0\) a point. Then, as topological spaces,
\[
\boxed{F_n/S_n\cong S^1\ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{m=3}^n\bigsqcup^{a_m} I_{n-m},}
\]
\[
\boxed{F_n/A_n\cong\left(\bigsqcup^{1+\mathbf1_{n\text{ odd}}}S^1\right)
\ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{m=3}^n\bigsqcup^{2a_m}I_{n-m}.}
\]
Here the \(S^1\) factors are ordinary circles, not necessarily CW quotients with an injective simplicial attaching map. In particular, the first rational Betti numbers of the two quotients are respectively \(1\) and \(1+\mathbf1_{n\text{ odd}}\). The initial values are \(a_3=1\), \(a_4=1\), \(a_5=6\), \(a_6=36\), \(a_7=356\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, loopless and simple; every orientation of \(K_n\) is a tournament. A vertex is a source when all incident edges point away from it and a sink when all point toward it. A source/sink flip reverses every incident edge. The one-cells of \(F_n\) encode these flips, and independent simultaneous flips define higher cubes. For a complete graph with \(n\ge3\), no two distinct vertices can be flipped independently, so \(F_n\) is one-dimensional. Group actions are by relabeling every vertex and every flex.

## Proof
**1. The component parameterization.** Every orientation of a complete graph has at most one source and at most one sink. Its degree in the flex graph is therefore \(0\), \(1\) or \(2\). Kozlov's complete-graph decomposition, Theorem 3.4 in the reference below, identifies the degree-two cyclic components with circular orderings of the \(n\) labels; each such component is an \(n\)-cycle. Every remaining component is a path of length \(t\), where \(0\le t\le n-3\), uniquely described by an ordered \(t\)-tuple of distinct labels that are flexed successively, together with an orientation of the induced complete graph on the other \(n-t\) vertices with neither source nor sink. The path is canonically directed from its sink-only endpoint to its source-only endpoint. This parameterization is natural under vertex relabeling: labels are transported termwise and the residual tournament is transported as an oriented graph. The case \(t=0\) is an isolated source/sink-free tournament. These are all the connected components; there are no hidden higher cells in this complete-graph case.

**2. Fixed tournaments and the formula for \(\Phi\).** Let \(g\) have cycle type \(\mu\). If it has an even cycle, some power of \(g\) exchanges the ends of one edge, so it cannot preserve any tournament orientation. Suppose every cycle length is odd. An invariant orientation can be chosen independently on each \(g\)-orbit of unordered pairs. Pairs within an odd cycle of length \(r\) give \((r-1)/2\) orbits; pairs joining cycles of lengths \(r,s\) give \(\gcd(r,s)\) orbits. No orbit exchanges the two ends of an edge. The number of invariant tournaments is therefore \(N(\mu)\). An invariant tournament has at most one source and at most one sink; any such vertex must be fixed by \(g\). For a specified fixed vertex to be a source or sink, all its incident directions are forced, leaving \(N(\mu\setminus1)\) options. For an ordered distinct pair of fixed source and sink vertices the remaining options are \(N(\mu\setminus1^2)\). Inclusion-exclusion gives exactly \(\Phi(\mu)\), the number of \(g\)-fixed tournaments with neither source nor sink.

**3. The two characters.** A path component is fixed under \(g\) precisely when every entry of its ordered flip tuple is fixed individually and its residual tournament is invariant. Thus, with \(f\) available fixed labels, the number of fixed path components is \(P_n(\lambda)\). A circular ordering is fixed by \(g\) precisely when \(g\) acts as a rotation of that order. This is possible exactly when its cycle lengths are all equal. For cycle type \((d^k)\), an \(n\)-cycle \(c\) has exactly \(\varphi(d)\) powers of that cycle type, and the centralizer of \(g\) has order \(d^k k!\). Counting the conjugating permutations and dividing by the \(n\) rotations gives \(C_n(\lambda)\) fixed circular orders. Orient each cycle component by successive sink flips. Any label permutation preserving that component preserves this cyclic direction, not its reverse, because it carries sinks to sinks. Hence the action on its one-dimensional first homology is \(+1\), and \(H_1\) is exactly the permutation module on circular orderings, namely \(\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{S_n}\mathbf1\). The action on \(H_0\) is the permutation module on all components. Subtracting its trivial summand gives the displayed reduced degree-zero character.

**4. Quotient topologies.** The stabilizer in \(S_n\) of one cycle component is precisely the rotation subgroup \(C_n\), which acts by rotations, so its quotient is a circle. All circular orders form a single \(S_n\)-orbit. For a path associated with an ordered \(t\)-tuple and a residual tournament \(T\), the stabilizer fixes the ordered tuple and acts as \(\operatorname{Aut}(T)\) on the remaining vertices. It also fixes the path pointwise, since it preserves the distinct sink-only and source-only endpoints. Its \(S_n\)-orbit is determined exactly by \(t\) and the isomorphism type of \(T\), and its quotient is an interval with \(t\) edges. Burnside's lemma applied to the fixed-tournament formula proves the expression for \(a_m\), giving the \(S_n\) quotient.

Every tournament automorphism has odd order: an even-order automorphism would have an involution power exchanging two vertices and hence reverse their directed edge. Thus every path stabilizer lies in \(A_n\), so its \(S_n\)-orbit splits into two \(A_n\)-orbits. On a circle, the stabilizer is \(C_n\); it lies in \(A_n\) exactly when \(n\) is odd. Therefore there are two \(A_n\)-orbits of circles when \(n\) is odd and one when \(n\) is even. The quotient of each remains a circle because all stabilizer actions are rotations. This proves the exact \(A_n\) quotient decomposition and the first-Betti parity law.

## Verification
A self-contained Python program in `verify.py` constructs every tournament orientation, every admissible source/sink flip and every connected component, independently of the closed formulas. For \(3\le n\le6\), it tests each conjugacy-class representative of \(S_n\): fixed path components, fixed circular components, and the orientation sign on any fixed circular component. It also computes actual component orbits under adjacent-transposition generators of \(S_n\) and three-cycle generators of \(A_n\), checking the predicted length-resolved quotient decompositions. The program completed \(26\) conjugacy-type comparisons, and its final output was `VERIFY_OK`. The full enumeration encompasses respectively \(4\), \(38\), \(728\), and \(26,744\) connected components. Its finite checks support, but do not replace, the general argument.

Reproduce by extracting `verify.py` in the same directory and invoking `python3 verify.py`; it imports only the Python standard library.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov, *Flex complexes of graphs* (2026), Theorem 3.4, proves the nonequivariant path/cycle component decomposition for complete graphs and enumerates its components. That classification is an indispensable published input, not a claim of novelty here. Royle–Praeger–Glasby–Freedman–Devillers, *Tournaments and even graphs are equinumerous* (2023), §3, establishes the tournament automorphism odd-order property and fixed-orientation enumeration for permutations of odd order. The present combination determines, for the flex topology, complete graded symmetric-group homology characters (including the previously untracked fixed-path contributions) and the exact \(S_n\)- and \(A_n\)-quotient graphs. No character or quotient formula is attributed to either source without evidence.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns complete graphs on \(n\ge3\) vertices, their natural relabeling groups, and rational homology characters. It does not assert an equivariant extension to arbitrary graphs, an equivariant homotopy type for a general flex complex, or a new formula for the total number of tournaments. The quotient descriptions are exact as spaces, but the displayed CW decompositions are not claimed to provide canonical quotient cell structures. Published full-text comparisons do not exclude an independently obtained unindexed equivalent result.
