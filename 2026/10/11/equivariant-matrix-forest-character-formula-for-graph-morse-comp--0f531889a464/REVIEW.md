# Review of the equivariant matrix–forest character formula

**Correctness — PASS.** A setwise fixed acyclic Hasse matching is equivalent to an equivariant partial successor function. Its outgoing orbit must target an orbit of divisor length; the resulting orbit functional graph is acyclic exactly when the original selected graph is. Each arrow orbit contributes a computable simplex-orientation sign, and the weighted directed matrix–forest theorem gives the full graded character determinant. The complete-graph factorization is a block-eigenvalue computation. Independent finite calculations in `verify.py` reproduced all polynomial and Lefschetz specializations described in `VERIFICATION.md`. Critical conventions (empty simplex, outdegree and directed row Laplacian, chain orientation) were checked in the proof.

**Originality — PASS with residual risks.** Chari–Joswig's graph-rooted-forest correspondence and Laplacian face polynomial are exactly the identity-automorphism specialization, not the character for nonidentity automorphisms. Contreras–Tawfeek revisit that nonequivariant formula; Chebotarev–Shamis supply a general determinant enumeration, not the new fixed-face orbit-divisibility interpretation. Knill supplies a generic fixed-simplex sign formula. Kozlov's complete-graph orbit-quotient study computes invariant homology, not the present individual graded class character. Searches for alias formulations and prior stronger implications identified no complete covering theorem. The accessible 2026 lead's abstract does not address this computation, but its full text was not read and may contain related observations; unindexed equivalents remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** The result replaces enumeration of exponentially many forest faces by a determinant indexed only by vertex orbits and supplies the entire graded automorphism character for arbitrary finite graphs. It strengthens the classical nonequivariant Laplacian enumeration in a natural representation-theoretic direction and gives an explicit complete-graph cycle-type product for every symmetric-group conjugacy class. This is a structural all-order formula, not a small numerical increment or a special-case recomputation. It does not claim to resolve full homology representations.
