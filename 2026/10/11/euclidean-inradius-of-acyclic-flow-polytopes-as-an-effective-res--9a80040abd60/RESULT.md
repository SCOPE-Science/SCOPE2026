# Euclidean inradius of acyclic flow polytopes as an effective-resistance cut ratio
## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite directed acyclic graph whose underlying undirected graph is connected. Orient its incidence matrix \(B\) so that the column for an arc \(e:u\to v\) is
\[
b_e=e_u-e_v.
\]
For an integral netflow \(a\in\mathbb Z^V\) with \(\sum_{v\in V}a_v=0\), assume that the flow polytope
\[
\mathcal F_G(a)=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^E:Bx=a\}
\]
is nonempty and has positive dimension. Let \(F\subseteq E\) be the set of edges for which \(x_e=0\) cuts out a facet of \(\mathcal F_G(a)\).

Give every underlying edge unit electrical resistance. For \(e\in E\), let
\[
R_e=b_e^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^+b_e
\]
be the effective resistance between the endpoints of \(e\), and put
\[
c_e=\sqrt{1-R_e}.
\]
For a lower ideal \(S\subset V\), meaning that no arc enters \(S\), write
\[
C(S)=\sum_{e\in\delta^+(S)\cap F}c_e.
\]
Then the intrinsic Euclidean inradius is
\[
\boxed{
r(\mathcal F_G(a))=
\min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq V\\S\text{ lower ideal}\\C(S)>0}}
\frac{a(S)}{C(S)},
\qquad
a(S)=\sum_{v\in S}a_v.
}
\]
Moreover, if this minimum is \(r\), the complete set of Chebyshev centers is
\[
\boxed{
\{x\in\mathcal F_G(a):x_e\ge r c_e\text{ for every }e\in F\}.
}
\]
Thus the Euclidean inradius of an acyclic flow polytope is a directed cut ratio whose edge weights are square roots of cycle-space leverage scores.

## Assumptions and scope
The metric is the Euclidean metric inherited from \(\mathbb R^E\), restricted to the affine hull of the flow polytope. The graph is acyclic only to make the nonnegative-flow feasibility criterion reduce to lower-ideal cut inequalities; the effective-resistance distance calculation itself does not require acyclicity.

Edges outside \(F\) are coordinate inequalities that do not define facets for the chosen netflow. They must not be inserted into the denominator. This is essential: a non-bridge edge can have positive effective cycle leverage but still be bounded away from zero throughout the flow polytope.

## Proof
Fix a point \(x^0\in\mathcal F_G(a)\). Its affine hull is
\[
x^0+\ker B.
\]
For an edge \(e\in F\), the coordinate functional \(x\mapsto x_e\), restricted to this affine hull, has Euclidean gradient equal to the orthogonal projection of the standard edge vector \(\varepsilon_e\in\mathbb R^E\) onto \(\ker B\). Therefore the intrinsic distance from \(x\in\operatorname{relint}\mathcal F_G(a)\) to the facet \(x_e=0\) is
\[
\frac{x_e}{\|P_{\ker B}\varepsilon_e\|_2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Because the underlying graph is connected, the orthogonal projection onto the cut space \(\operatorname{im}B^{\mathsf T}\) is
\[
P_{\operatorname{im}B^{\mathsf T}}=B^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^+B.
\]
Hence
\[
\|P_{\ker B}\varepsilon_e\|_2^2
=1-\varepsilon_e^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^+B\varepsilon_e
=1-b_e^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^+b_e
=1-R_e.
\tag{2}
\]
For a facet edge this quantity is positive. Combining (1) and (2), a Euclidean ball of radius \(t\) centered at a feasible flow \(x\) is contained in the polytope exactly when
\[
x_e\ge t c_e\qquad(e\in F).
\tag{3}
\]
All boundary points lie in coordinate facets, so nonfacet coordinate inequalities add no further distance condition.

Define a lower-bound vector \(\ell(t)\in\mathbb R^E\) by
\[
\ell_e(t)=
\begin{cases}
t c_e,&e\in F,\\
0,&e\notin F.
\end{cases}
\]
Condition (3) is feasible precisely when there is \(y\ge0\) with
\[
B y=a-B\ell(t).
\tag{4}
\]
We now use the elementary feasibility criterion for a nonnegative flow on a DAG. A zero-sum vector \(d\in\mathbb R^V\) lies in the cone generated by the incidence columns \(b_e=e_u-e_v\) if and only if
\[
d(S)\ge0
\tag{5}
\]
for every lower ideal \(S\). Necessity follows by summing a nonnegative flow over a lower ideal: no arc enters \(S\), so \(d(S)\) is the total flow leaving \(S\). For sufficiency, Farkas duality says it is enough to test potentials \(p\) satisfying \(p_u\ge p_v\) on every arc \(u\to v\). After subtracting a constant, every such isotone potential is a nonnegative layer-cake combination of indicators of lower ideals, so (5) implies \(p\cdot d\ge0\) for every dual feasible \(p\).

Apply (5) to \(d=a-B\ell(t)\). If \(S\) is a lower ideal, no arc enters it, and therefore
\[
(B\ell(t))(S)
=t\sum_{e\in\delta^+(S)\cap F}c_e
=t C(S).
\]
Thus (4) is feasible exactly when
\[
a(S)\ge t C(S)
\]
for every lower ideal \(S\). The largest feasible \(t\) is consequently the displayed minimum ratio. Finally, equality of the radius condition with (3) gives the stated complete center set.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs exact-rational checks on three small acyclic flow polytopes. It computes effective resistances from reduced graph Laplacians, enumerates lower ideals, applies the cut-ratio formula, and compares with the direct Euclidean radius of the resulting one-dimensional flow polytopes. One example is a transitive triangle in which an edge is non-bridging but does not define a facet; omitting that edge from \(F\) is necessary to obtain the correct radius.

The finite replay is corroborative only. The arbitrary-dimensional result follows from the projection identity and the DAG flow-feasibility proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Mészáros, Simpson, and Wellner define flow polytopes as nonnegative solutions of an incidence system \(Bx=a\), develop their lattice and face structure, and study the family attached to partitions. Their paper gives the exact ambient model used here but contains no Euclidean inradius, Chebyshev-center, effective-resistance, or metric formula in the inspected full text.

A previously established transportation-polytope inradius formula is recovered as a special case when the directed graph is complete bipartite with all row-to-column arcs: the effective-resistance weights are constant, and the lower-ideal cut ratios reduce to the row and column margin bounds. The present theorem is strictly broader because the weights \(c_e\) vary with the graph and because arbitrary lower ideals, rather than only single row or column cuts, can control the radius.

Database and literature searches for flow-polytope inradii, Chebyshev centers, effective resistance, cycle-space projections, and cut formulas found the standard flow-polytope literature and general effective-resistance material, but no source stating this combined theorem.

## Limitations
The formula is stated for bounded acyclic flow polytopes. For a general directed graph with cycles, nonnegative flow feasibility is governed by a different cone and the lower-ideal reduction is unavailable in this form. The theorem also requires the facet-edge set \(F\); determining \(F\) is a separate combinatorial problem for a given netflow.

No claim is made that the literature search is a formal global novelty certificate. Differently phrased or unindexed work could exist.
