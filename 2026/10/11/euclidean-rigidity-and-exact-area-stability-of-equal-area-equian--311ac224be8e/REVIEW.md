# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Equal vertex-triangle areas reduce immediately, under equiangularity, to equality of the products of adjacent side lengths. Positivity then forces the exact recurrence \(\ell_{i+2}=\ell_i\), giving regularity for odd \(n\) and a two-length alternating family for even \(n\). Closure of every alternating even family follows from the two regular root-of-unity sums. The area formula is independently derived from a two-mode complex Fourier representation of the edge sequence; orthogonality removes the mixed terms and gives the exact cotangent/tangent identity. The central-symmetry parity follows from the opposite-edge condition. The bundled checker independently reconstructs the polygons and shoelace areas.

## Originality
**PASS, with an elementary-observation residual risk.** The author-uploaded full text of Harel--Rabin was materially searched and inspected through its definition, parametrization, and section on symmetric equal-area polygons; it contains no occurrence of “equiangular” and its symmetric class is a different recurrence condition. The open full text of Craizer--Teixeira--da Silva was inspected through its definition, moduli discussion, and affine isoperimetric theorem; it also contains no equiangular specialization. Published-database and public-literature searches using equal-area/equiangular, alternating-side, semiregular, vertex-triangle, and exact area-deficit formulations did not locate an implication-equivalent theorem. The main residual risk is that the classification is elementary enough to occur in unindexed recreational or expository literature.

## Value
**PASS.** Equal-area polygons are a named affine-geometric class with nontrivial moduli, while equiangular polygons are a basic Euclidean class. Their exact intersection is therefore a natural structural question, not an arbitrary slice. The result gives a complete all-\(n\) classification, exposes a parity transition in central symmetry, and strengthens the classification by an exact fixed-perimeter area stability identity.
