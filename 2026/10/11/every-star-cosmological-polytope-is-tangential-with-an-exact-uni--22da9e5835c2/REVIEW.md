# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The published connected-subgraph theorem gives exactly \(m\) leaf facets and \(2^m\) center-containing facets for \(K_{1,m}\). Projecting their \(0/1\) normals into the fixed-total affine hull gives exactly two normal lengths, \(\beta_m\) and \(\alpha_m\). Summing the leaf clearance inequalities and averaging the subset-facet clearances gives a sharp upper bound. The proposed center attains equality in every facet. Equality in the two aggregate bounds then forces every individual facet to be tight and forces \(x_i=y_i\), proving uniqueness.

## Originality
PASS. The primary full text was inspected at the standard realization and the complete facet theorem. A later same-object triangulation paper was checked as a plausible broader source. Candidate-specific database and literature searches covered inradius, insphere, Chebyshev-center, largest-inscribed-ball, star, and tangential formulations. No covering theorem was located. The closest same-object metric result is a minimum-enclosing-sphere theorem, which is vertex-controlled and does not imply facet distances or tangentiality.

## Value
PASS. Star cosmological polytopes form an all-dimensional natural graph family with exponentially many facets. The result proves an unexpectedly strong property: every facet is tangent to one sphere, and the incenter is unique with a closed radical formula. This is a substantive Euclidean complement to the established face, triangulation, volume, and vertex-shell theory.

## Closest literature and limitations
The source supplies the exact facets but does not normalize their Euclidean normals or optimize an inball. The theorem is restricted to stars in the standard realization; general graph facets have more varied coefficient patterns. A broader tangentiality theorem for special cosmological-polytopes families remains the principal residual originality risk.
