# Mathematical review of the all-order raised-simplex profile

## Correctness — PASS
For any integer n>=2 and k>=1, and 0<epsilon<1/(n-1)^2, the Euclidean simplex barycentric identity gives the required separation n*a/(n-1) from one coordinate discrepancy a. Rounding k times the barycentric weights makes every height j*epsilon/k with 0<=j<=sum floor(k*lambda_i) attainable above the same base point. Their final accessible height gives the matching global upper bound. For k<n the top centroid is exactly epsilon from the averaged set; for k>=n the explicit barycentric witness has precisely k-n+1 feasible lifted copies and forces horizontal distance above the claimed gap for every larger lifted count. Its last accessible level attains the gap exactly. The top-face covering of A+s conv(A) gives Schneider index n-1. Exact rational independent finite checks of 14 instances corroborate, but do not prove, the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, best of available knowledge
The raised-simplex family and equality for k<n were proved by Peter van Hintum, *The sharp threshold for Hausdorff convexification under Minkowski addition* (arXiv:2606.10815v1, Theorem 1.3). Its Theorem 1.4 gives a general n-fold upper coefficient but not the exact full profile of this family. Fradelizi, Madiman, Marsiglietti and Zvavitch, *The convexification effect of Minkowski summation*, EMS Surveys 5 (2018), Theorem 7.10, already proves the general Schneider-index upper bound. Neither inspected primary source supplies the all-order matching lower witnesses or simultaneous equality. Published exact simplex-skeleton and pure-vertex grid theorems apply to different nonconvex sets and invariants. A historically earlier or unindexed equivalent calculation remains possible; the conclusion does not certify absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact full trajectory of the canonical raised-simplex convexification example explains the first post-threshold loss and exhibits sharpness of a natural general Schneider-index inequality at every averaging order. It yields a necessary lower bound (n-1)/n for any proposed universal contraction ratio at order n, without claiming the converse universal inequality. The result is motivated as a complete exact geometric invariant of the very construction used in the threshold problem.

## Boundaries and verification limits
The bound on epsilon is sufficient, not shown optimal. The Euclidean metric and regularity of the simplex are essential to the stated proof. The finite checks are not an all-dimensional proof and no formal proof or third-party expert attestation is asserted. The mathematical reasoning and specific primary literature comparisons above constitute the review.
