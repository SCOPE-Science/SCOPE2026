# Mathematical assessment — four-periodic geometric weighted shift

A separate mathematical assessment of the published claim found its compact-Jacobi argument correct (C: PASS), its fixed-budget allocation classification original on the inspected best-of-knowledge record (O: PASS, with specific priority limitations), and its exact natural invariant mathematically valuable (V: PASS). ## Correctness

The circle-unitary reduction gives numerical radius equal to one-half the top eigenvalue of the compact symmetric Jacobi operator. At zero first modulation the operator splits into an initial unit edge and scaled four-vertex paths. Their characteristic equation establishes the sharp threshold \(c_*(q)=q^{-3}\sqrt{(1-q^4)(1-q^8)}\). A three-vertex principal submatrix excludes attaining the value 1/2 for any positive first modulation. Above threshold, endpoint perturbations, the strictly positive Hessian and convex optimization give a unique positive minimizer, strict convexity and analyticity in the budget. A two-dimensional spectral compression with a uniform positive complementary gap yields the fixed-\(q\) large-budget optimizer displacement and the constant term in the optimal numerical radius. Independent symbolic and finite-truncation checks corroborate the arguments but do not constitute the infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality

Ghosh, Birbonshi and Ojha (arXiv:2608.17486, August 2026) previously introduced the identical modulated weight list and fixed-parameter numerical-radius determinant. The accessible original abstract and detailed secondary synopsis do not directly state the full constrained minimum, though the full original paper was inaccessible and may overlap; this is the major residual priority risk. Chakraborty, Ojha and Birbonshi (Operators and Matrices 16, 2022) give determinant methods for eventually alternating nonzero tails, unlike these compact geometric weights. A separate published Airy-edge theorem for the same modulated shift fixes the modulation parameters and studies \(q\to1\), not optimization over \(s+t=c\) at fixed \(q\). A geometric finite cyclic shift bound-optimization theorem concerns a different operator and objective. The exact Resultary hit matching this claim is the record itself and cannot establish prior novelty or priority.

## Value and limits

A fixed total modulation budget is a natural question once two independently variable geometric coefficients are introduced. The exact threshold between endpoint-only and unique interior regimes, analytic strict convexity, and the nonzero constant-order optimizer displacement provide a complete infinite-family allocation result rather than a numerical lookup. The statement holds only for fixed \(0<q<1\), does not yield an elementary formula for every finite budget above threshold, and does not assert a uniform remainder as \(q\) approaches either endpoint. Historical priority remains best-of-knowledge, not exhaustive certification.
