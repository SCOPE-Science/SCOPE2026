# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the exact fixed-separation definition of \(\rho_X^{a}\). Two explicit linear isometries reduce all unit-sphere edge pairs to four cyclic separations. Each class is solved exactly: same-edge pairs give sum norm \(2\); adjacent edges give the sharp minimum \(2-\epsilon/2\); edges separated by one edge give \(4-2\epsilon\) for \(1\le\epsilon\le2\); opposite edges occur only at \(\epsilon=2\). The two relevant branches cross exactly at \(\epsilon=4/3\), and explicit unit pairs attain both branches throughout their ranges. The bundled checker replays the finite identities with exact arithmetic.

## Originality
PASS. Searches covered the named invariant, \(\rho_X^{a}\) notation, equivalent smoothness terminology, the affine-regular hexagon, the two branch expressions, the transition \(4/3\), and the diagnostic value \(-1/8\). The defining 2022 full text was inspected through the definition, its concrete examples, the relation to the associated smoothness modulus, and the conclusion. No affine-regular hexagonal profile was found there. The closest same-invariant indexed result concerns only unit separation in the regular octagonal plane; same-hexagon records concern different invariants and do not imply this statement.

## Value
PASS. The angle modulus of smoothness is tied by the source to uniform non-squareness and to a classical smoothness modulus. The affine-regular hexagon is a canonical non-Hilbert plane, and its complete profile displays a nontrivial geometric phase transition at \(\epsilon=4/3\), rather than merely producing one isolated number.
