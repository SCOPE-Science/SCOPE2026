# Review of exact angular return and Floquet rigidity

## Correctness — PASS

The polynomial homogeneity makes the angular radial-power equation linear: \(dv/ds=2p(\lambda+Fv)/G\). Variation of constants supplies a positive-intercept affine section map with slope strictly between zero and one. Its fixed point yields one attracting cycle, and a scalar comparison guarantees the angular return is defined for every positive initial radius. Integrating the logarithmic radial derivative around the cycle yields the **original physical-time** period, while the derivative of the section map gives the transverse Floquet multiplier. The identity \(\rho=e^{-2p\lambda T}\) and exponent \(-2p\lambda\) follow algebraically; the cubic anisotropic family satisfies the assumptions with exact \(f\) and \(g\). A standalone fourth-order numerical check of several noncircular examples passed. Finite checks are illustrative, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

Alarcón–Castro–Labouriau (2024), sections 2 and 4, prove an attracting invariant circle and classify when it is a limit cycle. The broader homogeneous-star-node work (arXiv:2106.07516, Theorem 4.1) states an integral **sign** existence criterion and hyperbolicity; it does not state the numerical original-time period, a global affine radial-power return or a universal Floquet exponent. The 2013 Bendjeddou–Llibre–Salhi study establishes integrability and existence/nonexistence, but its complete text could not be examined. This creates real residual priority uncertainty, as does incompleteness of broader mathematical indexing. The claimed novelty is strictly the quantitative time-and-return rigidity, not the known existence and uniqueness of the cycle.

## Value — PASS

A topological classification does not quantify the physical-time period or the transverse exponential relaxation rate. The exact global section law is stronger than an infinitesimal linearized multiplier, and the exponent's complete independence of the nonlinear coefficients is a structural invariant. The noncircular anisotropic family makes that invariant observable and supplies a natural application: exact isoperiodicity and isostability under continuous geometric deformation. This is neither an arbitrary parameter slice nor an unsupported numerical gain.
