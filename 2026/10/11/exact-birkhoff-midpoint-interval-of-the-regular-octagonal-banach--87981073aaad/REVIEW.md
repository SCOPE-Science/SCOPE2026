# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The support-functional characterization of Birkhoff orthogonality exhausts the regular-octagon cases after dihedral symmetry. On a smooth edge the unique support fixes the normalized kernel vector, giving two affine sum-norm branches whose union is the claimed interval. At a vertex the support cone sweeps exactly two boundary edges in the kernel-direction sector; the four signed arc formulas remain inside the same endpoints. Both endpoints are attained by explicit unit Birkhoff-orthogonal pairs. The exact checker certifies every segment norm formula in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) and the final conversion to \(\delta_B,\rho_B\).

## Originality
PASS. Searches used the original \(\delta_B,\rho_B\) notation, the equivalent \(\mu'(X)\) formulation, oriented sum-length language, and the older \(J_\perp,g_\perp\) notation. Du--Li's defining full text has no regular-octagon computation. Baronti--Papini Example 5.3 does compute the same plane, but only \(J_\perp=g_\perp=\sqrt2\), which concerns min/max of both sum and difference lengths and does not determine the oriented endpoints. The closest public research records use different admissibility conditions or objectives.

## Value
PASS. The two moduli were introduced to measure midpoint geometry under Birkhoff orthogonality. The regular octagon is a canonical benchmark where the older orthogonal James and Schäffer constants collapse to the Hilbert value, so the asymmetric oriented interval supplies genuinely finer information. The exact identity \(\rho_B=3\delta_B\) and the support-cone decomposition provide a natural benchmark for further polygonal calculations.

## Closest literature and limitations
D. Du and Y. Li, Australian Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 20 (2023), No. 1, Art. 2, introduced the two moduli and was published on 31 January 2023 with primary MSC 46B20. M. Baronti and P. L. Papini, Constructive Mathematical Analysis 5 (2022), 37–45, compute \(J_\perp,g_\perp\) for the regular octagon but not the present oriented interval. The result is specific to the regular octagon and does not classify all regular polygonal norms.
