# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

The theorem covers every regular Euclidean \(n\)-gon with \(n\ge3\), every boundary center, and all positive radii. Squared distance is strictly convex along each edge. A trigonometric vertex criterion bounds far-side strict maxima by two for even \(n\) and three for odd \(n\). For even \(n\), an opposite-side interior projection gives four intersections at every nonvertex center; distance along the two boundary arcs from any vertex is strictly monotone, giving two there. For odd \(n\), the two opposite edges' interior projections yield the four-point baseline, while the weak-peak versus strong-valley squared distance gap factors exactly into \(c^2(t-t_-)(t-t_+)\). Its smaller root is \(2s(1-\cos(\alpha/2))^2/c\) and its larger root lies beyond the edge. The strict six-point window, its excluded endpoints, and the perimeter density follow. Independent symbolic reduction and radius-chamber experiments for multiple odd and even orders supplement the proof; finite computations are not a proof for all orders.

## Originality and relationship to earlier mathematics

Bárány and Roldán-Pensado, *A Question from a Famous Paper of Erdős*, Discrete & Computational Geometry 50 (2013), 253–261, DOI 10.1007/s00454-013-9507-z, introduce \(N(K)\) and the level sets \(J(K,m)\), but the inspected theorem and examples do not give the exact six-center locus of every regular \(n\)-gon. The corresponding 2013 thesis, *Problems in Convex Geometry*, Chapter 3, likewise gives the general formulation, supporting examples, and sufficient crossing lemmas without a full regular-polygon interval classification. A published polygon-complexity bound and the closed farthest-distance profile of regular polygons optimize different quantities. The latter controls maximal radius, not counts at intermediate radii. The present precise interval and perimeter fraction were not derived by these sources. This is a best-of-knowledge assessment: unindexed equivalent calculations remain possible.

## Mathematical value

The level set \(J(P_n,6)\) is natural in the original geometric question. Its exact interval description and the \(n^{-4}\) decay of its perimeter fraction provide quantitative information that the best-center invariant cannot encode. The result does not imply a universal intersection bound for arbitrary convex bodies.

## Limitations

All geometric statements concern Euclidean regular polygons. Tangencies and coincident vertices count once. Symbolic algebra and bounded numerical diagnostics do not establish historical novelty or serve as a substitute for the proof.
