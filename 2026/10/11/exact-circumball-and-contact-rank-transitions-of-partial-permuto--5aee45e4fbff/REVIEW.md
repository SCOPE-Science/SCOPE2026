# Scientific assessment: partial-permutohedron circumballs

## Correctness — PASS
Heuer--Striker's rank-indexed vertex theorem is an independently checked premise. The maximum squared distance from a scalar center is a finite maximum of rank quadratics, whose consecutive differences factor exactly. Their maximizing intervals cover the relevant nonnegative line. Projection to each interval gives the exact rational minimizer. A convex mixture of touching vertex orbits has barycenter at the proposed center, giving a global squared-distance certificate against arbitrary nonsymmetric competing balls. Boundary cases \(m=1\), \(n=1\), \(m=n(n+1)\) and the one-/two-orbit contact cases are included. Finite exact-arithmetic experiments corroborate but do not prove the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty
The primary full texts inspected were Heuer--Striker (arXiv:2012.09901), Behrend et al. (arXiv:2207.14253), Hanada--Lentfer--Vindas-Meléndez (arXiv:2212.06885), and Bayer et al. (arXiv:2403.07387). They provide vertex/facet descriptions, face structures, and enumerative results; the exact Euclidean circumball, contact-rank transition and rational formula were not identified in their inspected portions or across full-text terminology searches. Generic minimum-sphere algorithms do not by themselves state the parameterized result. Earlier results about a Euclidean diameter or a minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid of this family do not imply the present metric optimum. The exact possibility of equivalent, earlier treatments under other terminology remains a residual priority risk; independent literature audit is not performed.

## Value — PASS
The family has two unbounded parameters and changes combinatorial behavior between sparse and dense label regimes. A full classification of its metric outer radius includes an exact finite rational procedure with no vertex enumeration, all farthest-vertex orbit ranks and multiplicities, a sharp quadratic dimension threshold \(m=n(n+1)\) and contrasting simplex-like and cubical limits. This is a complete natural geometric invariant, rather than a single small-instance value, renaming or recomputation of a known table.

## Limitations
Only the standard ambient Euclidean realization and consecutive labels are covered. A generic prior algorithm for minimal enclosing spheres exists; the claimed contribution is the family-specific exact result, not a new general algorithm. Remaining bibliographic priority risk does not constitute independent novelty certification.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
