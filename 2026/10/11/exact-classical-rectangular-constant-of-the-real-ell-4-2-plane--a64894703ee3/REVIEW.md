# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The smooth Birkhoff--James condition in real \(\ell_4^2\) gives a one-dimensional orthogonal direction. Expanding the fourth power of the norm reduces every normalized pair to two invariants \(A\) and \(B\) satisfying \(B^2=A(1-A^2)\). Strict concavity gives a unique \(A\) for each positive scalar parameter, and the remaining stationarity equation is an intersection of one strictly increasing and one strictly decreasing function. This proves global uniqueness before the polynomial is formed, so the degree-eight equation is not being trusted as a numerical certificate.

## Originality
PASS. The 1970 primary paper reduces the \(L_p\) problem to \(\ell_p^2\) and reports only numerical values; its \(p=4\) table entry is approximately \(1.888\). The complete 2021 primary paper explicitly describes its \(\ell_p\) table as numerical and gives the refined \(p=4\) value \(1.9337\). Complete inspection of the 1977 two-dimensional paper and the 2014 attainment paper found no exact \(\ell_4\) evaluation. Targeted searches found an exact generalized \(\mu_4(\ell_4^2)\) result, but that is a different functional and does not imply the classical value.

## Value
PASS. This resolves a canonical published numerical benchmark for one of the oldest Birkhoff--James geometric constants. The exact value comes with a unique maximizing scalar and a complete invariant description of normalized extremizers. The quartic algebraic closure is structurally distinct and makes the result useful as a sharp benchmark, rather than a routine numerical increment.

## Closest literature and limitations
Gastinel--Joly give the two-dimensional reduction and an early numerical table. Baronti--Casini--Papini give the modern \(1.9337\) benchmark and general bounds. Paul--Ghosh--Sain give attainment and endpoint structure. Del Río--Benítez give the two-dimensional Hilbert endpoint theorem. The result is restricted to the real \(\ell_4^2\) plane and does not solve the full \(\ell_p\) problem.
