# Scientific review: trigonometric composition-center locus

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain is the \(4n\)-real-dimensional space of zero-mean trigonometric coefficient pairs, \(n\geq1\). For any nonzero composition pair, the highest harmonic of \(P(h)h'\) is exactly \(d(\deg P+1)\), which bounds the polynomial degrees. Two affine normalizations of the common factor \(h\) give a semialgebraic image bound \(2d+2\lfloor n/d\rfloor-1\). Intermediate harmonic degrees contribute at most \(2n-1\), whereas both the proportional and common-reflection branches attain dimension \(2n+1\). Explicit Chebyshev identities show the reflection family exactly matches degree-one factors, and degree-\(n\) factors imply proportionality. Their intersection has dimension \(n+2\). For \(n=2\) no intermediate degrees exist, proving the complete union and its polynomial Fourier test. The supplementary rational checker passed; the unbounded-degree conclusion rests on the analytic proof, not finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

Bravo–Trinidad-Forte, arXiv:2503.03247v1, Theorem 2, establishes an algebraic composition characterization under a two-simple-zero hypothesis, but not the exact semialgebraic codimension, two maximal strata, first nonproportional harmonic threshold, or degree-two coefficient test. Its original preprint and open-access journal full text were checked. Bravo–Fernández–Ojeda (2024), Theorem 2.3, handles degree-one coefficients and proves proportionality, matching the lower-cutoff boundary but not the degree-two classification. The earlier Giné–Grau–Santallusia trigonometric Abel article concerns a different equation and was accessible only at abstract level. Pakovich (2017, full author-hosted text, Theorem 1.1) classifies alternative composition decompositions of trigonometric polynomials rather than dimensions of pairs with one shared factor. Briskin–Pakovich–Yomdin (2016, full author-hosted text, Theorem 1.1) does compute dimensions of polynomial Abel composition sets with one fixed coefficient; its domain and quantifiers differ. The 2013 Giné–Grau–Santallusia text was accessible only at abstract level. An equivalent pair-stratum statement in other Abel literature remains possible. Searches of the published mathematical findings database for proportional/reflection/coefficient-stratum aliases did not identify a matching result. Search absence is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The composition criterion is an important dynamical source of all-periodic piecewise-linear cylinder flows, but it does not quantify how exceptional these coefficient pairs are or what genuinely different mechanisms account for most of them. The exact codimension quantifies their rarity at every Fourier cutoff; the two maximal families expose a sharp change at cutoff two; and the complete two-harmonic coefficient test makes the first nontrivial regime decidable without solving a differential equation. This is a natural structural classification and exact invariant, not an arbitrary numerical restriction or an incremental constant improvement.

## Limitations and comparison risks

The theorem is confined to composition centers, not every possible global center when the source's Hypothesis 1 fails. Lower-dimensional intermediate-degree factor strata are not completely resolved. Literature aliases involving the algebraic geometry of the Abel center problem could contain related descriptions in other sources; this review is not independent validation.
