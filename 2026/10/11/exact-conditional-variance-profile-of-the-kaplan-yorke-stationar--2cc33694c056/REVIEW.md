# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Disintegration over the Lebesgue first marginal gives equal weights to the two inverse branches of the doubling map. The conditional first moment solves \(m=\cos(2\pi\cdot)+\alpha Pm\), whose unique square-integrable solution is \(m(x)=\cos(2\pi x)\). The second-moment equation then closes on three Fourier modes and yields the stated formula. Subtracting \(m^2\) gives the conditional variance. The packaged checker independently verifies the coefficient identities.

Risk: the theorem does not extend unchanged to invariant measures whose first marginal is not Lebesgue.

## Originality
PASS. A complete 2016 same-object article explicitly writes the Frobenius–Perron operator and studies information flow, but targeted searches found no variance, moment, or conditional-moment theorem. The 1979 source was only partially text-extractable, which is recorded as residual bibliographic risk rather than treated as evidence of absence.

Risk: because the calculation is a short Fourier consequence of the transfer operator, an equivalent observation could exist in unindexed notes or in inaccessible portions of older literature.

## Value
PASS. The result determines how stationary vertical fluctuations vary with the expanding coordinate, not merely a global second moment. It provides an exact benchmark for invariant-density computations and distinguishes the deterministic conditional mean from the unresolved fiberwise randomness. The profile is structurally tied to the dyadic inverse branches and the forcing harmonic, rather than being an arbitrary parameter slice.
