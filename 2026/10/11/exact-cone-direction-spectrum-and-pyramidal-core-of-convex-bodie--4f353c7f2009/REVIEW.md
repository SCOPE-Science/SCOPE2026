# Exact cone-direction spectrum: mathematical assessment

## Correctness

For each cone direction all extreme points except its apex belong to its base supporting hyperplane. The base functionals distinguish the apexes, proving affine independence. When at most n apices occur, a residual extreme point belongs to every base; this shows linear independence of the normals and yields a residual base of dimension n-k. Residual dimensions zero or one make the whole body a simplex, excluding n and n−1. Simplices realize n+1, while iterated pyramids over cross-polytopes of dimension at least two realize all 0,…,n−2. This is a proof for every dimension and full-dimensional compact convex body; illustrative exact-rank computations establish only their tested instances.

## Prior-work comparison

Feldman, *Arranging convex bodies for maximum intersection volume: the sharp efficiency of centroid alignment*, arXiv:2608.04516v1, §3.1 Lemma 8, establishes only finiteness of cone directions and explicitly conjectures the n+1 simplex-only maximum. Pineda-Villavicencio, Ugon, and Yost, *Lower bound theorems for general polytopes* (2019), discuss multifold pyramids in the context of face counts. Werner and Wotzlaw, *Linkages in Polytope Graphs* (2007), §3.3 Lemma 3.7, treat polytope facet-complement graphs under the additional condition that each facet omits at most two vertices. The latter statements do not establish the full universal cone-direction count and residual-base claim. The exact-match mathematical record is the present result, not an independent predecessor.

## Mathematical value and limits

The theorem answers a recently stated sharp question, determines all possible counts and supplies a canonical geometric explanation beyond finite examples. It makes no claim about approximate cones, improved centroid-overlap bounds, or comprehensive historical priority; a classical equivalent formulation in older affine-join literature remains possible.
