# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The published hypersurface identity and the geodesic-sphere shape operator give
\[
d\eta(X,Y)=2g((\cot r\,\phi-G_N)X,Y).
\]
On \(\mathcal D\),
\[
\phi^2=G_N^2=-I,
\qquad
G_N\phi=-\phi G_N.
\]
Hence
\[
(\cot r\,\phi-G_N)^2=-\csc^2r\,I.
\]
The vertical part vanishes because \(\phi\xi=0\) and \(G(\xi,N)=0\). Therefore \(d\eta\) has maximal horizontal rank, two equal singular values \(2\csc r\), and exact contact-volume norm \(8\csc^2r\). The bundled checker independently replays the skew-matrix square and Pfaffian computation.

## Originality

PASS. The closest source explicitly says its norm estimate does not determine rank and does not imply contactness. Its geodesic-hypersphere calculation stops at
\[
\|d\eta|_{\mathcal D}\|^2=8\csc^2r.
\]
The full Anarella--D'haene preprint classifies compatible almost-contact tensors and the totally umbilical family but does not state the canonical \(d\eta\) spectrum or the radius-dependent scaled fundamental-form identity. Targeted exact and equivalent-formulation searches found no covering statement.

An older paper is accessible only through an indexed abstract stating a Sasakian result for the equatorial \(S^5\). This is a genuine special-case precedent and remains an access risk, but it does not settle the non-equatorial all-radius quantitative theorem from the material available.

## Value

PASS. The primary source itself singles out rank/contactness as the information missing from its sharp norm theorem. Resolving that question completely on its principal explicit family is mathematically natural. The equal-singular-value formula and exact contact-volume density provide a stronger structural benchmark than a yes/no contact statement.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Lee--Lee, arXiv:2610.00078v1. Anarella--D'haene, arXiv:2507.22526v1, supplies the relevant family of compatible almost-contact tensors and the classification context.

The theorem is restricted to geodesic hyperspheres in the standard nearly Kähler \(S^6\). It does not classify contactness for general real hypersurfaces.
