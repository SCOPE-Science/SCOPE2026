# Scientific review

## Correctness
The complete real-tree flow identity is reconstructed from all leaf and inner-edge cut flows. Its boundary coefficients are included, and the normalized molecule reduction holds because finite free-space unit balls are generated by molecules. For a molecule, signed flips contribute exactly their incident heights and selected-coordinate runs contribute exactly their initial heights. Monotonicity proves the optimal alternating-run selection, and finite-section compatibility gives the infinite formulas. The uniform-tail versus block-geometric equivalence follows from a direct comparison of consecutive blocks. Fifteen exact-rational enumerations and sixty independent leaf-Lipschitz dual linear-program checks were performed, but they do not establish the infinite theorem without the analytic argument.

## Originality
Cúth–Doucha (arXiv:1411.2434, Section 1) prove monotone nearest-point retractional bases and a separate \(\ell_1\)-isomorphism theorem. Their stated results do not quantify arbitrary sign or subset selectors. Godard (arXiv:0904.3178, Section 3) computes tree free-space norms in general rather than sharp constants for this coefficient basis. Novotný (arXiv:1905.13188, Section 4) supplies unconditionality obstructions on other metric families and a broad sufficient obstruction, not this exact positive/negative classification. Albiac–Ansorena–Cúth–Doucha (arXiv:1905.07201, Section 5) analyze different free-space bases. Searches for comb ultrametrics, retractional unconditionality and coordinate-suppression constants found no exact statement with the same hypotheses. Historical equivalent formulations in weighted summing bases remain a material bibliographic risk.

## Value
The theorem answers a structural question left unquantified by the nearest-point basis construction: which prescribed ultrametric scales permit unconditional coordinate decompositions? Both exact operator constants and an equivalence with uniform block-geometric decay hold for all strictly decreasing positive sequences, supplying sharp positive and negative regimes and arbitrary finite-dimensional values. This is an exact classification of a natural basis phenomenon, not a purported obstruction to other bases on an \(\ell_1\)-isomorphic space.

## Limitations and closest literature
Only real scalars, this explicit point ordering and its nearest-predecessor basis are covered. Unconditionality of a different basis is not classified. Generalized tree-norm and retractional-basis theory are acknowledged as prior foundations. An equivalent older theorem about weighted summing bases could reduce originality and would require a further bibliographic check.
