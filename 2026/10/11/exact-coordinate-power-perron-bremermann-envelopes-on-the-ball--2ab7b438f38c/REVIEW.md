# Review of Exact coordinate-power Perron–Bremermann envelopes on the ball

## Correctness

PASS. For
\[
0<q\le1,
\]
the candidate
\[
u_q(z)=-(1-\|z'\|^2)^q
\]
has a positive-semidefinite Levi matrix. Independence of the remaining complex coordinate forces the top Monge–Ampère measure to vanish. The candidate agrees exactly with the prescribed data on the sphere and lies strictly below the obstacle inside the ball. Maximal comparison identifies it with the obstacle envelope, and the minimum set follows directly from the formula.

For
\[
q>1,
\]
the radial Levi eigenvalue
\[
q(1-s)^{q-2}(1-qs)
\]
is negative for
\[
s>1/q,
\]
so the claimed sharp boundary for this formula is rigorous.

## Originality

PASS. The fresh 2026 source gives the general obstacle-envelope theory but does not state the coordinate-power formula. Its explicit unit-ball examples concern logarithmic singularities and pluripolar-hull phenomena of a different form.

Exact-source and implication-equivalent literature searches found no published result giving the same closed formula, minimum-disk classification, or the sharp
\[
q=1
\]
threshold for the orthogonal-shadow ansatz. Classical general existence theorems do not compute this solution, and the located Legendre-transform envelope framework does not supply an identified specialization implying the claim.

A residual risk remains that an older pluripotential-theory text or symmetry calculation contains this elementary explicit solution without terminology captured by the searches.

## Value

PASS. The new paper develops a broad abstract theory whose explicit tests are mainly singular. The accepted family supplies a finite, continuous, exactly solvable benchmark in every complex dimension, with a nontrivial interior minimum disk and an explicit rank-deficient homogeneous Monge–Ampère solution.

The formula also exposes a sharp structural power transition: the simplest orthogonal-shadow maximal solution works exactly through
\[
q=1
\]
and fails by a signed Levi eigenvalue immediately above it. This gives a reusable test family for envelope regularity, hull contact, and numerical or analytic schemes for the complex Dirichlet problem.
