# Same-model scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For every planar convex body, the weak support-function area formula gives a nonnegative, summable Fourier decomposition of the isoperimetric deficit. Reflection symmetrization is a modewise orthogonal projection; the squared mode attenuation is a product of independent angular factors. The exact second and crossed moments \(1/2\), \(3/8\), and \(1/4\) prove the mean, variance, and two-time covariance formulas. Nonnegative Fourier summability justifies all infinite sums. Equality in the sharp variance bound requires and is realized by one higher Fourier harmonic. For the almost-sure result, the mean-scaled deficits form a nonnegative martingale; each fixed mode decays by the negative logarithmic mean of its multiplier, and Fatou's lemma controls the infinite tail. The embedded `verify.py` returned `VERIFY_OK` for the smooth finite-frequency check, including direct support-integral area calculations. Finite tests do not establish infinite-dimensional or almost-sure claims; those are supported by the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual historical uncertainty.** Coupier–Davydov, arXiv:1211.1785, Proposition 5 and Section 4.2, prove a planar factor-\(1/2\) contraction for the expected squared centered-support \(L^2\) norm. That is an acknowledged first-order antecedent, not a formula for the variance of area deficits or two-time covariance, and does not imply the latter without crossed fourth-moment information. Bianchi–Gardner–Gronchi, arXiv:1603.00643, Sections 3 and 7–9, characterize deterministic Minkowski symmetrization rather than its random fourth moments. Bourgain–Lindenstrauss–Milman treat high-dimensional Euclidean-ball approximation; Kwong–Lee treat deterministic planar isoperimetric Fourier inequalities. Search comparisons found no mathematically dominating theorem. An older equivalent stochastic-geometric calculation cannot be ruled out solely by indexed searches, and broader independent historical review remains desirable.

**Value — PASS.** Unlike a routine recalculation of mean contraction, the complete two-time covariance identity gives exact uncertainty of an intrinsic geometric functional for every planar convex body, with a sharp and geometrically classified extremizer. It also reveals an important distinction between expectation and typical behavior: normalized deficits converge almost surely to zero while their coefficients of variation diverge exponentially. Neither finite-mode restriction nor a numerical census is needed.

**Scope and integrity.** All convex bodies with nonempty interior, including polygons, are covered; the angle law must be independent uniform axes. No claim is made in higher dimensions or for Steiner symmetrization. The numerical verifier is a consistency check, and no independent audit has been performed.
