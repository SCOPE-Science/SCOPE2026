# Scientific review

## Correctness
The unit-sphere formula of Fu and Li (Proposition 5.4) gives the exact three-coordinate range quotient. Normalizing one range-one triple by simultaneous permutation and using the exchange symmetry restricts the scale to \(0<q\le1\). All six relative permutations are covered: identity and reversal have quotient at most \(1\); both transpositions and both cycles have upper bound \(\sqrt q(2-q)\). The exact identity \(32/27-q(2-q)^2=(8-3q)(3q-2)^2/27\) proves the universal maximum, attained at \(q=2/3\) by the specified pair. Direct independent optimization of all six cases corroborates the bound but is not the proof of infinite quantifiers.

## Originality and coverage
Fu and Li (2024) introduce the constant, prove its unit-sphere reduction and general comparisons, and compute the square and diamond endpoints; their complete 23-page article does not calculate this hexagonal value. Wang, Liu, Xia and Sarfraz (2025), arXiv:2504.01267, generalize the constant to a parameterized angular/skew-angular family; their fully inspected 13-page preprint computes the \(\ell_1\) case, not this hexagon. Related exact calculations on the same hexagonal plane concern different invariant functionals, not a stronger theorem determining this quotient. The unrestricted claimed value was not located in the checked sources; this does not prove universal bibliographic absence. In particular, the later Birkhoff-restricted variant was accessible only through its abstract and remains an explicit residual coverage risk.

## Mathematical value and boundaries
The affine-regular hexagonal plane is a canonical non-Hilbert polygonal Banach space. An exact attained value \(\sqrt{32/27}\), strictly between the Hilbert value \(1\) and the square/diamond endpoint \(2\), provides a natural benchmark for the recently studied distance comparison. The range/permutation reduction supplies a reusable method. The theorem neither determines other polygonal norms nor the full maximizing set.
