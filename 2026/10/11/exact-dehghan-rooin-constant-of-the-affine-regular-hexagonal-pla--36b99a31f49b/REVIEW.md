# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Fu–Li's equivalent unit-sphere formula converts the invariant to a three-variable range quotient. The range representation \(N(s,t)=\operatorname{rng}(0,s,t)\) is exact. Simultaneous coordinate permutations and exchange of the two unit vectors reduce all six relative permutations to four cases. Each case has an explicit global upper bound; the only nontrivial envelope is \(\sqrt q(2-q)\), whose exact maximum on \(0<q\le1\) is \(4\sqrt6/9\). The displayed unit vectors and scale attain that value exactly. The checker verifies the finite symmetry bookkeeping and the critical algebraic identities; no finite computation is used as a substitute for an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2024 article was inspected at the definition, equivalent-formulation proposition, model examples, and comparison results; it does not state this hexagonal evaluation. The later Maligranda–Rooin generalization was inspected at its definition and example section; its parameter-zero specialization is the same invariant, but its worked model is \(\ell_1\), not the affine-regular hexagon. Direct semantic searches for Dehghan–Rooin/angular-versus-skew-angular constants together with the Day–James or affine-regular hexagonal plane returned neighboring geometric-constant results rather than a statement implying this value. The closest retrieved hexagonal results concern different invariants and therefore do not imply the claim.

## Value
PASS. This is an exact, attained value of a recently introduced geometric constant on a canonical non-Hilbert polygonal Banach plane. It supplies a nontrivial benchmark strictly between the Hilbert value \(1\) and the endpoint value \(2\) occurring in the square and diamond examples, and the proof exposes a reusable range/permutation reduction rather than merely recomputing a numerical table.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Fu–Li (2024), DOI 10.1007/s43034-024-00341-0, which defines the invariant and its equivalent sphere formula. Wang–Liu–Xia–Sarfraz, arXiv:2504.01267, embeds the invariant as a special parameter of a broader family. Neither inspected source contains the stated affine-regular hexagonal computation. The claim is limited to this real two-dimensional norm, and bibliographic search cannot prove absolute absence from all non-indexed literature.
