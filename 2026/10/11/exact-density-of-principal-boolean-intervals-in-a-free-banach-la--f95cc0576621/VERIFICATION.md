# Verification

The mathematical check was performed from the stated definitions and the cited source statements.

For the lower bound, Proposition 4.3 of arXiv:2604.27841v1 applies because \([\emptyset,A]_L=\mathcal{P}(A)\), whose cardinality is \(2^{|A|}\). The nondegenerate interval also forces the \(\aleph_0\) term.

For the upper bound, the restriction map from Example 4.4 is used with upper endpoint \(A\). For arbitrary \(B\subseteq\Gamma\), write \(B_0=B\cap A\) and \(B_1=B\setminus A\). On \(U\), meet preservation gives \(\min\{x^*(B_1),x^*(A)\}=x^*(\emptyset)\). Since \(x^*(A)>x^*(\emptyset)\) and \(x^*(B_1)\geq x^*(\emptyset)\), this forces \(x^*(B_1)=x^*(\emptyset)\). Join preservation then yields \(x^*(B)=x^*(B_0)\). Thus every restricted generator is represented by a subset of \(A\).

The resulting closed vector sublattice is generated by at most \(2^{|A|}\) elements. Approximating scalar coefficients by rationals shows its density is at most \(\max\{\aleph_0,2^{|A|}\}\). The restriction map is isometric on the interval, so the same upper bound holds there.

No numerical experiment or finite enumeration is used to infer an infinite-cardinal conclusion. The proof does not address \(A=\emptyset\), arbitrary lower endpoints, or general distributive lattices.
