The exact setting is C=[-1,1]^2 and z_c=(c,c), 0<=c<1, with positively oriented nondegenerate boundary triangle and cyclic gauge lengths. Translation gives K_c=[-b,a]^2, a=1-c>0,b=1+c and p_c(x,y)=max(x/a,-x/b,y/a,-y/b). For common side lambda>0, q_i=(P_{i+1}-P_i)/lambda lie on the gauge boundary and sum to zero. Choose one active supporting coordinate on each q_i; with 3 choices and only 2 coordinate axes, at least two share an axis. Active values (a,-b) force lambda b<=2 by the square coordinate diameter; (a,a) imply third coordinate -2a in [-b,a], feasible only b>=2a (c>=1/3), and force 2a lambda<=2; (-b,-b) demand 2b<=a, impossible since b>=a>0. Thus lambda<=2/b if c<1/3 and lambda<=max(2/b,1/a) otherwise. For c<=1/3, t=-b+2a/b, the three vertices (a,t),(-b,t),(t,-b) lie on the boundary, have strictly positive determinant and cyclic gauge length 2/b. For c>=1/3, (-b,-c),(-c,-b),(a,-b) have consecutive side vectors (1,-1),(1,0),(-2,1), positive signed area, and all gauge lengths 1/a because b>=2a. At c=1/3 branches meet at 3/2. First branch strictly decreases, second strictly increases; reflection interchanges the orientations and diagonal square isometries generate all equivalent minimizing origins. End c=1 excluded because the origin is on the boundary and the gauge degenerates. Independent exact rational checks at eight c samples confirm the witnesses; the continuum conclusion follows from the case proof rather than the samples.
