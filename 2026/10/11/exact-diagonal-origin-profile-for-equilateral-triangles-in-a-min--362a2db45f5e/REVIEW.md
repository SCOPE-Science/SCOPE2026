# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Translating the square gives the asymmetric gauge \(p_c(x,y)=\max\{x/a,-x/b,y/a,-y/b\}\) with \(a=1-c\) and \(b=1+c\). Normalizing the three cyclic side vectors to the gauge boundary and applying a two-coordinate pigeonhole argument yields all possible upper-bound mechanisms. The two explicit boundary triples attain the two branches, and the transition condition is exactly \(b=2a\), i.e. \(c=1/3\). Reflection in \(x=y\) transfers the result to the opposite orientation.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source is Fabińska–Lassak (2004), which treats the single diagonal ratio giving \(c=1/3\) and proves only the resulting \(3/2\) upper bound for that square. Fabińska–Lassak (2009) proves the universal sharp \(3/2\) theorem but does not imply the pointwise diagonal-origin profile. The 2025 review concerns centrally symmetric normed planes and cites these papers as the non-centrally-symmetric branch of the literature. Exact-form, parameter, source-name, and implication searches found no statement of the displayed two-branch formula. The principal residual risk is an inaccessible 2003 paper by the same authors.

## Value
PASS. The one-parameter family is the canonical deformation of the published square extremizer: the square is fixed and only the origin moves along the same diagonal used in the sharpness example. The theorem identifies the exact phase transition, proves uniqueness of the worst origin modulo square symmetries, and structurally explains why the ratio \(1:2\) produces the universal sharp constant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The 2004 paper states the square extremizer at one origin and the 2009 paper establishes global sharpness. The new statement is strictly stronger only within the diagonal-origin square family and makes no claim about arbitrary off-diagonal origins or arbitrary asymmetric convex bodies. The unavailable 2003 source remains a specific residual access risk.
