# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For a generic central hyperplane of the unit cross-polytope, all section vertices are crossed original edges. Central symmetry reduces Euclidean diameter to twice the largest of their norms. The pair-extreme inequality selects the smallest and largest absolute normal coordinates. Conditioning on the largest coordinate of a Haar Gaussian normal yields the full finite-dimensional half-normal CDF. Classical Gaussian extreme values give the exponential upper-endpoint limit with its exact rate. Uniform moment bounds and a superpolynomially small low-maximum event justify every fixed positive integer moment, not merely weak convergence. Independently constructed finite edge-geometry and CDF comparisons corroborate, but do not replace, the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS (best-of-knowledge)

The exact three-dimensional regular-octahedron diameter CDF is an existing special case, not a new contribution. The all-dimensional formula and its sharp endpoint-moment limit are not mathematically implied by this single-dimensional antecedent. The complete main theorems and relevant proofs of Guédon–Litvak–Tatarko–Vritsiou's 2026 random-diameters manuscript were inspected; they concern general probability bounds, not this exact codimension-one distribution. Hmadi–Liakopoulos's cube-projection inradius work was inspected through its full arXiv HTML; the duality does not promote its quantitative bounds to the present exact law. Earlier section-volume and regular-polytope projection-length sources study different observables. The priority finding is necessarily limited to identified and inspected sources, with equivalent alternative formulations still a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural distributional classification for an unbounded family of canonical convex bodies. It gives the complete finite-dimensional CDF, the n sqrt(log n) non-Gaussian endpoint scale, the exponential rate 1/sqrt(pi), and convergence of all fixed positive integer moments, providing exact benchmarks for random-section theory and its polar cube-projection formulation.

## Scope

Central Haar hyperplanes in the standard ℓ1 unit ball, n≥2, Euclidean diameter, and n→∞ at fixed codimension one. All finite computational checks are supplemental; no formal verification or separate expert attestation is asserted.
