# Mathematical assessment of exact diameter of geodesic Zare monotiles

## Correctness

PASS. The source defines a compact simple nonconvex geodesic polygon with lower vertices V_k=(k/q^2,1) (0<=k<=q+1), upper vertices W_j=((q+1)j/q^3,(q+1)/q) (0<=j<=q), and curvature -1. A geodesic triangulation of the polygon proves diameter is attained at its original vertices. Exact optimization of lower/lower, upper/upper and mixed vertex pairs yields the stated closed two-branch formula. The sign of q^3-q^2-2q-1 changes between integer q=2 and q=3, with strict positivity thereafter. Elementary asymptotics yield q diam(T_q)->sqrt(2) and diam(T_q)/sqrt(Area(T_q))->sqrt(2). Numerical enumeration is supplementary, not an all-q proof.

## Originality

PASS (best of knowledge). Liao and Wang, arXiv:2610.05438v1, Theorem 4 and Section 7.2, prove only diam(T_q)<3/q for the geodesic realization. Section 6.1 establishes a convex-polygon vertex principle but gives no nonconvex T_q diameter. Douglas Zare's 2018 and 2010 descriptions provide the underlying small-tile idea, not the exact geodesic metric values. Targeted published-result searches found only the submitted exact claim itself, not independent covering mathematics. Full text of a reported October 6 revision could not be inspected; this is a priority risk, not evidence of noncoverage.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is the canonical explicit nonconvex family illustrating the necessity of side-count constraints in hyperbolic diameter gaps. Exact farthest-pair classification and the sharp sqrt(2) scale improve a coarse 3/q bound and provide a useful quantitative reference for comparisons of small monotiles. No universal optimum among all monotiles is claimed.

## References and limitations

- Y. Liao and E. Wang, *Area and diameter gaps for hyperbolic monotiles*, arXiv:2610.05438v1, Sections 6.1 and 7.2.
- D. Zare, MathOverflow answers 291454 (2018) and 18417 (2010).

The finding is restricted to the explicit geodesic family and ambient hyperbolic distance. Unindexed prior computations and uninspected later source versions remain originality risks. Formal proof assistance and external expert attestation are not claimed.
