# Exact Dixmier residue and trace-class boundary for polydisc quotient self-commutators
## Finding
For compressed coordinate multiplication on a finite-transverse-multiplicity diagonal quotient of the polydisc Hardy space, the absolute self-commutator has an exact weak trace-class spectral coefficient governed by the derivative at the origin of the selected inner function. Fix an integer \(d\ge2\) and a coordinate \(i\). With the notation below,
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mu_n(C_i)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_k(C_i)}{\log(n+1)}
=2D_i(d-1)\sqrt{1-|\eta_i'(0)|^2}.
\]
Every normalized Dixmier trace of the positive operator \(|C_i|\) has this value. Moreover,
\[
C_i\in\mathcal S_1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\eta_i'(0)|=1,
\qquad\text{and then}\qquad \|C_i\|_1=D_i.
\]
For a strictly smaller derivative modulus the absolute commutator has nonzero Dixmier trace and does not belong to \(\mathcal S_1\). The numerical coefficient is exact, not just an upper bound.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(\mathbb D\) for the unit disc and let \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_d\) be nonconstant inner functions satisfying \(\eta_j(0)=0\). In \(H^2(\mathbb D^d)\) let \(M_\eta\) be the closed submodule generated by \(\eta_j(z_j)-\eta_{j+1}(z_{j+1})\) for \(1\le j<d\). Set \(N_\eta=H^2(\mathbb D^d)\ominus M_\eta\) and \(S_i=P_{N_\eta}M_{z_i}|_{N_\eta}\). Define the self-adjoint commutator \(C_i=S_i^*S_i-S_iS_i^*\). Its singular values \(\mu_1(C_i)\ge\mu_2(C_i)\ge\cdots\) include multiplicities and are arranged decreasingly.

Let \(K_j=H^2(\mathbb D)\ominus\eta_jH^2(\mathbb D)\) and assume only that the transverse multiplicity \(D_i=\prod_{j\ne i}\dim K_j\) is a finite positive integer. The chosen \(K_i\) may be infinite-dimensional. Put \(\alpha=|\eta_i'(0)|\in[0,1]\) and \(\beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\). Weak trace class here means \(\sup_{n\ge1}n\mu_n(C_i)<\infty\); the logarithmic-mean ideal also contains such operators. A normalized Dixmier trace is evaluated on \(|C_i|\), not on the signed commutator.

## Proof
**1. Orthogonal model and its defect operators.** The standard inner-function Wold decomposition gives \(H^2(\mathbb D)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}\eta_j^kK_j\) for each \(j\). In the tensor-product basis, orthogonality to every generator \(\eta_j(z_j)-\eta_{j+1}(z_{j+1})\) forces constant coefficients over the connected set of nonnegative multi-indices of a given total degree. The normalized degree-\(l\) vector has \(\binom{l+d-1}{d-1}\) equal summands. Consequently, for each choice of basis vectors in the \(d-1\) transverse model spaces, the quotient has a reducing layer unitarily equivalent to \(K_i\otimes\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\), and there are exactly \(D_i\) such layers.

On each layer the compressed coordinate multiplier takes the form
\[
S_i\simeq A\otimes I+T\otimes B_d,
\qquad B_de_l=\sqrt{\frac{l+1}{l+d}}e_{l+1},
\]
where \(A=P_{K_i}M_z|_{K_i}\), \(e=1\in K_i\), \(u(z)=\eta_i(z)/z\in K_i\) are unit vectors and \(T=|e\rangle\langle u|\). To see the shift coefficient directly, multiplying a normalized equal-sum degree-\(l\) vector by \(\eta_i\) and projecting to degree \(l+1\) gives \(\sqrt{\binom{l+d-1}{d-1}/\binom{l+d}{d-1}}\). Multiplication by \(z\) on \(K_i\) splits as \(A\) plus \(\eta_i\langle\cdot,u\rangle\), establishing the claimed layer model.

The backward-shift identities on \(K_i\) imply \(A^*T=TA^*=0\), \(I-A^*A=T^*T\), and \(I-AA^*=TT^*\). Expanding \(S_i^*S_i-S_iS_i^*\) therefore leaves
\[
C_i\simeq \bigoplus_{r=1}^{D_i}\bigoplus_{l\ge0}R_l,
\qquad R_l=a_lP-b_lQ,
\]
with \(P=|e\rangle\langle e|\), \(Q=|u\rangle\langle u|\), and
\[
a_l=\frac{d-1}{l+d-1},\qquad b_l=\frac{d-1}{l+d}.
\]
Indeed, these coefficients are the eigenvalues of \(I-B_dB_d^*\) and \(I-B_d^*B_d\) on \(e_l\), respectively. The overlap is \(|\langle e,u\rangle|=|u(0)|=|\eta_i'(0)|=\alpha\).

**2. Exact rank-two spectrum.** If \(\alpha<1\), the span of \(e,u\) is two-dimensional, and the trace and determinant of \(R_l\) restricted to this span equal \(a_l-b_l\) and \(-a_lb_l(1-\alpha^2)\). The two nonzero eigenvalues are therefore
\[
r_{l,\pm}=\frac{a_l-b_l\pm\sqrt{(a_l-b_l)^2+4a_lb_l\beta^2}}{2},
\qquad r_{l,+}>0>r_{l,-}.
\]
Since \(l(a_l-b_l)\to0\) while \(la_l\to d-1\) and \(lb_l\to d-1\), both singular-value branches satisfy
\[
\lim_{l\to\infty}l\,r_{l,+}=\lim_{l\to\infty}l\,|r_{l,-}|=(d-1)\beta.
\]
The remaining eigenvalues of \(R_l\) are zero, even if \(K_i\) has infinite dimension.

**3. Rearrangement and logarithmic means.** For any positive sequence \(v_l\) with \(lv_l\to c>0\), elementary two-sided comparison with \((c\pm\varepsilon)/l\) gives
\[
\lim_{t\downarrow0}t\#\{l:v_l>t\}=c.
\]
There are two positive singular-value sequences per layer and exactly \(D_i\) layers. Hence, with \(N(t)=\#\{k:\mu_k(C_i)>t\}\),
\[
\lim_{t\downarrow0}tN(t)=2D_i(d-1)\beta.
\]
Monotone inversion of this counting limit yields \(n\mu_n(C_i)\to2D_i(d-1)\beta\). Comparison of the decreasing singular values with constant multiples of \(1/n\) and the harmonic sum then gives the same limit for \(\sum_{k\le n}\mu_k(C_i)/\log(n+1)\). The weak trace-class bound follows, and the convergent ordinary logarithmic mean forces every normalized Dixmier trace of \(|C_i|\) to equal the displayed coefficient. Because the coefficient is strictly positive when \(\beta>0\), the trace norm diverges.

**4. The trace-class boundary.** If \(\alpha=1\), equality in Schwarz's lemma yields \(\eta_i(z)=\lambda z\) for some \(|\lambda|=1\), hence \(u\) and \(e\) are collinear and \(P=Q\). Thus every block is the positive rank-one operator
\[
R_l=(a_l-b_l)P
=\frac{d-1}{(l+d-1)(l+d)}P.
\]
The complete trace norm telescopes:
\[
\|C_i\|_1=D_i\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(a_l-b_l)
=D_i(d-1)\sum_{l\ge0}\left(\frac1{l+d-1}-\frac1{l+d}\right)=D_i.
\]
Here \(\mu_n(C_i)=O(n^{-2})\), so both limits in the finding vanish, consistently with \(\beta=0\). These two cases exhaust the hypotheses.

## Verification
The source-level layer identities were reconstructed using the inner-function orthogonal decomposition, the weighted shift coefficients, and the two rank-one model-space defect projections. The standalone `verify_endpoint.py` checks the exact rational trace, determinant, squared discriminant, Hilbert–Schmidt energy, and finite telescoping identities. It also compares reordered finite spectra with the predicted singular-value tail for multiple dimensions, multiplicities and overlaps. Run it with `python3 verify_endpoint.py`; successful execution prints `QUOTIENT_ENDPOINT_BLOCK_CHECK_OK`. Numerical finite cutoffs are diagnostics only: the limit theorem is proved by comparison and counting, not extrapolation.

As a concrete continuous parameter family, for \(|a|<1\) choose \(\eta_i(z)=z(z-a)/(1-\overline a z)\) and \(\eta_j(z)=z\) for \(j\ne i\). Then \(D_i=1\), \(\alpha=|a|\) and the exact coefficient is \(2(d-1)\sqrt{1-|a|^2}\). When \(\eta_i(z)=z\), the singular trace vanishes and the ordinary trace norm equals \(1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Xu and Xiang's arXiv:2610.02692v1, Sections 3 and 4, supplies the foundational quotient-space layer model and Hilbert–Schmidt computation, and establishes \(\mathcal S_p\)-membership for \(p>1\) under finite transverse multiplicity. In particular, Proposition 3.3 gives the unsorted rank-two blocks. This finding adds their exact signed spectra, the reordered weak trace-class coefficient, the corresponding noncommutative residue, and the precise exceptional trace-class condition. The exceptional case is relevant because a generic harmonic lower bound does not apply when the two rank-one defect projections coincide. The general Dixmier trace criterion is standard and is not claimed as new.

The 2022 work of Zou, Yu and Yang computes Hilbert–Schmidt norms in a related bidisk setting; work by Engliš, Guo and Zhang and by Upmeier and Wang concerns Dixmier traces of Toeplitz commutators on other domains. Their abstract results do not state this derivative-dependent coefficient for the specified quotient module. Broader equivalences from the existing rank-two model are a residual literature risk.

## Limitations
The theorem requires finite transverse multiplicity \(D_i\); when this product is infinite, the direct-sum compactness and singular-value counting argument does not apply. The statement concerns absolute self-commutators, not their signed Dixmier traces and not cross-commutators. The uniqueness of this exact coefficient in the wider literature is not independently certified; the 2022 closely related full text was inaccessible. The spectral limits are mathematical consequences of the stated model, and finite numerical checks alone do not prove them. No claim is made about journal publication, independent validation or external certification.
