# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For a unit pair \(x\perp_B y\), the least Dragomir reverse parameter is determined exactly by \(\operatorname{dist}(y,\mathbb Rx)\). Dihedral symmetry reduces \(x\) to one octagon edge. Edge-interior points force one vertical \(y\)-direction through the unique support functional; vertices contribute one complete normal-cone family. The determinant quotient formula gives the distance exactly on both cases, and each is bounded below by \((2+\sqrt2)/4\). The explicit pair \(x=(1,\sqrt2-1)\), \(y=(0,-1)\) attains equality, so the stated \(\sigma_D\) follows algebraically.

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2020 arXiv full text was inspected at the modified Dragomir definition, the global D-symmetry definition, the finite-dimensional existence theorem, and the regular-polygon C-symmetry theorem. It does not compute an optimal regular-octagonal D-parameter. Exact-symbol, radical-value, reverse-distance, and octagon searches found no covering result. The closest indexed exact D-symmetry computation is for \(\ell_3^2\) and \(\ell_{3/2}^2\), which does not imply the polyhedral octagon value.

## Value
PASS. The result quantitatively sharpens a qualitative finite-dimensional existence theorem on a canonical regular polygonal Banach plane. Its equivalent reverse-line separation identifies the precise worst asymmetry and a sharp configuration rather than only proving that some parameter below \(1\) exists.

## Closest literature and limitations
Chmieliński, Khurana and Sain prove that finite-dimensional Banach spaces are globally D-approximately symmetric and that regular polygonal planes are C-approximately symmetric. The two approximate relations are distinct, and their paper supplies no optimal D-parameter for the octagon. A later exact result treats the Hölder-dual planes \(\ell_3^2\) and \(\ell_{3/2}^2\). The present calculation is specific to the regular octagon and does not establish an all-regular-polygon formula.
