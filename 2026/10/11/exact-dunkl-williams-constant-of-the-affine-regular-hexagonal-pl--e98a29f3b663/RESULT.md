# Exact Dunkl--Williams constant of the affine-regular hexagonal plane
## Finding
Let \(X=(\mathbb R^2,N)\), where
\[
N(s,t)=\max\{|s|,|t|,|s-t|\}.
\]
Then the ordinary Dunkl--Williams constant is
\[
DW(X)=\frac94.
\]
In the two-dimensional frame formula
\[
DW(X)=2\max_{v\in\operatorname{ext}(B_X)}M(v),
\]
every extreme point has
\[
M(v)=\frac98.
\]
At \(v=(1,1)\), the maximizing Birkhoff--James directions are, up to nonzero scaling and coordinate interchange, exactly the directions with coordinate-magnitude ratio \(2:1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The scalar field is real. The Dunkl--Williams constant is
\[
DW(X)=
\sup_{\substack{x,y\in X\setminus\{0\}\\x\ne y}}
\frac{N(x)+N(y)}{N(x-y)}
N\!\left(\frac{x}{N(x)}-\frac{y}{N(y)}\right).
\]

The unit ball of \(N\) is the affine-regular centrally symmetric hexagon with vertices
\[
(1,1),\ (1,0),\ (0,-1),\ (-1,-1),\ (-1,0),\ (0,1).
\]
The linear maps generated by
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto(t,s)
\]
and
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto(s,s-t)
\]
are isometries and act transitively on these six vertices.

Mizuguchi, Saito, and Tanaka proved that for every real two-dimensional normed space,
\[
DW(X)=2\sup_{v\in\operatorname{ext}(B_X)}M(v),
\]
where \(M(v)\) is defined from Birkhoff--James directions through their frame construction. Their definitions and this theorem were checked in the complete article text before using them here.

## Proof
By vertex transitivity it is enough to compute \(M(v)\) at
\[
v=(1,1).
\]

At this vertex the active norming functionals are the two coordinate functionals. Hence
\[
v\perp_B w
\]
if and only if the two coordinates of \(w\) have opposite signs or one is zero. The frame quantity is unchanged by nonzero rescaling of \(w\). Coordinate interchange fixes \(v\). Thus every non-axis direction may be represented as
\[
w=(q,-1),\qquad q\ge1.
\]
Axis directions arise as the limiting case \(q\to\infty\).

Put
\[
\phi(r)=N(v+r w).
\]
For \(r=-u\le0\), direct evaluation of the three affine pieces of the norm gives
\[
\phi(-u)=
\begin{cases}
1+u,&0\le u\le q^{-1},\\
(q+1)u,&u\ge q^{-1}.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(r=t\ge0\),
\[
\phi(t)=
\begin{cases}
1+qt,&0\le t\le1,\\
(q+1)t,&t\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]

For each level \(R\ge1\), let \(-u_R\le0\le t_R\) be the unique parameters satisfying
\[
\phi(-u_R)=\phi(t_R)=R.
\]
By the definition of the frame set \(\Gamma(v,w)\), its relevant midpoint parameter is
\[
\gamma_R=\frac{t_R-u_R}{2},
\]
and
\[
m(v,w)=\sup_{R\ge1}N(v+\gamma_R w).
\]

There are three level ranges.

If
\[
1\le R\le1+\frac1q,
\]
then
\[
u_R=R-1,\qquad
t_R=\frac{R-1}{q},
\]
and therefore
\[
-\gamma_R=
\frac{(R-1)(q-1)}{2q}.
\]
This parameter stays in the first negative branch, so
\[
N(v+\gamma_R w)
=
1+\frac{(R-1)(q-1)}{2q}.
\]
It increases to
\[
1+\frac{q-1}{2q^2}
\]
at \(R=1+q^{-1}\).

If
\[
1+\frac1q\le R\le q+1,
\]
then
\[
u_R=\frac{R}{q+1},
\qquad
t_R=\frac{R-1}{q},
\]
and
\[
-\gamma_R=
\frac{q+1-R}{2q(q+1)}.
\]
Again this lies in the first negative branch, giving
\[
N(v+\gamma_R w)
=
1+\frac{q+1-R}{2q(q+1)}.
\]
This decreases from the same transition value to \(1\).

Finally, when
\[
R\ge q+1,
\]
both sides lie on the homogeneous outer branch:
\[
u_R=t_R=\frac{R}{q+1},
\]
so
\[
\gamma_R=0
\]
and the midpoint norm is \(1\).

Consequently
\[
m(v,w)
=
1+\frac{q-1}{2q^2}.
\]
The axis limiting value is \(1\), so no direction was lost. Differentiating the nonconstant term gives
\[
\frac{d}{dq}\frac{q-1}{2q^2}
=
\frac{2-q}{2q^3}.
\]
Thus its unique maximum for \(q\ge1\) occurs at
\[
q=2,
\]
where
\[
M(v)=1+\frac18=\frac98.
\]
All six vertices are isometric, hence
\[
DW(X)=2M(v)=\frac94.
\]

For direct attainment in the original definition take
\[
x=(-1,-2),\qquad y=(-1,0).
\]
Then
\[
N(x)=2,\qquad N(y)=1,\qquad N(x-y)=2,
\]
and
\[
N\!\left(\frac{x}{N(x)}-\frac{y}{N(y)}\right)
=
N\!\left(\frac12,-1\right)
=
\frac32.
\]
Therefore the Dunkl--Williams quotient equals
\[
\frac{2+1}{2}\cdot\frac32=\frac94.
\]

## Verification
The proof is an exhaustive one-variable calculation after applying only genuine linear isometries of the hexagonal norm.

The Birkhoff--James direction description is exact: at \(v=(1,1)\), the norming-functional segment is
\[
\{(a,1-a):0\le a\le1\},
\]
so a direction \(w=(w_1,w_2)\) is Birkhoff--James orthogonal to \(v\) exactly when zero lies between \(w_1\) and \(w_2\).

For \(w=(q,-1)\), the two displayed formulas for \(N(v+r w)\) were checked by comparing all three defining affine pieces. Their transition parameters are exactly \(-1/q\) and \(1\). Solving each level equation gives all possible equal-norm pairs on the line, so the calculation of \(m(v,w)\) is exhaustive rather than sampled.

The critical external theorem
\[
DW(X)=2\sup_{v\in\operatorname{ext}(B_X)}M(v)
\]
was checked in the complete 2013 article text, together with the definitions of \(V(v)\), \(\Gamma(v,w)\), \(m(v,w)\), and \(M(v)\).

## Relationship to prior work
Jiménez-Melado, Llorens-Fuster, and Mazcuñán-Navarro developed the Dunkl--Williams constant as a geometric parameter connected to convexity, smoothness, uniform nonsquareness, and normal structure.

Mizuguchi, Saito, and Tanaka subsequently introduced the frame method used here. Their two-dimensional theorem reduces the ordinary Dunkl--Williams constant to extreme points of the unit ball. In the same article they apply the method to the Day--James plane \(\ell_2-\ell_\infty\), obtaining \(2\sqrt2\); the affine-regular hexagonal plane above is not that space.

The same authors also studied symmetric octagonal norms. An affine-regular hexagonal unit ball has six extreme points and is not a member of the nondegenerate symmetric-octagonal class.

Later work introduced restricted Dunkl--Williams constants associated with Singer and isosceles orthogonality and explicitly discusses Radon planes with affine-regular hexagonal unit sphere. Those restricted constants are different functionals and do not state the ordinary value proved here.

Targeted searches using the ordinary invariant name, the affine-regular-hexagon description, the explicit norm, and the candidate value \(9/4\) located no statement equivalent to the present ordinary Dunkl--Williams calculation.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the single real affine-regular hexagonal norm above. It does not give a formula for a parameterized family of general hexagonal norms.

The full text of the 2012 symmetric-octagonal paper was not obtained through the checked route. Its stated object class is different from the six-vertex space here, but this incomplete inspection is retained as a bibliographic residual.

No complete classification of all ordered pairs attaining the original Dunkl--Williams quotient is claimed. The proof does classify the maximizing frame direction at every extreme point and supplies an explicit attaining original pair.
