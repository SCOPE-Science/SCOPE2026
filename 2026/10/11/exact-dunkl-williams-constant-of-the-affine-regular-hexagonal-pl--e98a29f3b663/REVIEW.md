# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The verified two-dimensional frame theorem reduces the ordinary Dunkl--Williams constant to \(2M(v)\) on extreme points. The affine-regular hexagon is vertex-transitive. At \(v=(1,1)\), the complete Birkhoff--James direction cone reduces to \(w=(q,-1)\), \(q\ge1\), and the exact equal-level calculation yields
\[
m(v,w)=1+\frac{q-1}{2q^2}.
\]
Its unique maximum is \(9/8\) at \(q=2\), so \(DW=9/4\). The explicit pair \(x=(-1,-2)\), \(y=(-1,0)\) independently attains \(9/4\) in the original definition.

## Originality
PASS. The closest ordinary-Dunkl--Williams sources provide general bounds, the frame method, the \(\ell_2-\ell_\infty\) computation, and symmetric-octagonal calculations. None of their inspected statements covers the six-vertex affine-regular hexagon. Later work mentioning affine-regular hexagons uses restricted Singer/isosceles Dunkl--Williams constants. Current and prior archive ledgers contain related hexagonal invariants and other spaces' Dunkl--Williams constants but not this claim. Exact-name, norm-formula, alias, and \(9/4\) searches found no covering ordinary result.

## Value
PASS. The affine-regular hexagon is a standard benchmark in two-dimensional Banach geometry, while exact ordinary Dunkl--Williams calculations are comparatively scarce. The finding gives a sharp natural invariant and isolates the unique frame-direction ratio that causes the extremum, making it structurally useful for comparisons with later orthogonality-restricted variants.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Mizuguchi--Saito--Tanaka's 2013 frame calculation paper. Their 2012 symmetric-octagonal paper is a nearby polygonal comparison but was not inspected in full; its stated object class differs by having an octagonal rather than hexagonal unit ball. The 2023 affine-regular-hexagon source concerns restricted \(DW_S\) and \(DW_I\) functionals. No complete classification of all ordered maximizers of the original quotient is claimed.
