# Same-model scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The centered point-incidence Gram matrix is an explicitly scaled rank-\(v-1\) orthogonal projection. Because \([k]_q^2/[n]_q<1\) whenever \(n\ge2k\), its negative entries occur precisely for disjoint \(k\)-subspaces. Double-counting disjoint graphs gives the sharp row mass, and an exact Gaussian-binomial product yields the balanced Euler-product limit. Counting-field tests for 500 parameter triples and literal subspace enumeration in 15 cases independently agree. Boundary cases \(k=1\) and \(n=2k\) are included, and the argument applies to all prime-power fields without relying on finite tests as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Scarabotti–Tolli (arXiv:math/0701533v1) describe finite-homogeneous Fourier decompositions, Srinivasan (arXiv:1304.6244v1) constructs Grassmann common eigenbases, and Filmus–Ihringer (arXiv:1801.06034v1) identify degree-one incidence harmonics while classifying Boolean functions. Their inspected material supplies important premises, but no statement evaluating this operator's absolute row sum, complementary endpoint norm, or fixed-excess product coefficient. Queries also checked q-Hahn aliases, primitive-idempotent terminology, published Grassmann harmonic literature, and adjacent Johnson/Walsh endpoint-norm claims. A hidden equivalent in older association-scheme literature remains a stated risk; unsuccessful discovery alone cannot guarantee novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The first Grassmann primitive idempotent is canonical in finite homogeneous Fourier analysis. The exact all-parameter endpoint norm and the sharp codimension-indexed Euler product answer a natural quantitative question about stability of finite-field harmonic projections, substantially beyond an arbitrary finite computation or a restatement of a spectral decomposition. The claim does not extend to higher primitive idempotents or intermediate \(L^p\) norms.
