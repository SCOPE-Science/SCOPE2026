# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The induced-character formula gives \(\chi_\lambda(a)=\lambda(a)+\lambda(a)^{-1}\) on the abelian kernel and zero on the reflection coset. Finite-group Fourier inversion therefore makes the single-isotype projector convolution by \(2\chi_\lambda\). Both endpoint convolution norms equal the normalized absolute kernel mass. Since an order-\(L\) character maps onto all \(L\)-th roots with equal fibers, the norm collapses exactly to a complete cyclic absolute-cosine average. Explicit Dirichlet-kernel summations give the three residue-class formulas, and elementary monotonicity gives the unique norm-one case \(L=4\). The existence criterion follows from equality of the exponents of a finite abelian group and its dual.

## Originality
PASS. The general compact-group multiplier source supplies the framework, not the exact generalized-dihedral norm. Classical abelian minimum-projection work does not imply the nonabelian isotype statement. Finite-field dihedral group-algebra work and the recent complex dihedral idempotent paper are close at the kernel/idempotent level, but the inspected material does not state the exact endpoint norms or the reduction solely to character order. Greenleaf’s norm-one idempotent-measure theorem is genuinely broader for the qualitative contractive case: once the kernel is identified, it can recover the character-weighted Haar structure of a norm-one projector. Accordingly, no novelty is claimed for a general classification of norm-one idempotent measures; the surviving originality claim is the exact all-order quantitative formula, cyclic quotient principle, and resulting generalized-dihedral classification. Direct, alias, broader, and exact-formula searches found no statement implying that full quantitative theorem. The recent idempotent paper remains an explicit residual risk because its full article body was not exposed through the accessed interface.

## Value
PASS. Nonlinear irreducible blocks are the atomic spectral pieces of generalized-dihedral Fourier analysis. The theorem classifies every such block across all finite abelian kernels by a single intrinsic invariant, character order, and identifies exactly when the corresponding projection is contractive. The quotient principle explains why a noncommutative spectral projection has the same endpoint constant as a two-frequency cyclic projection, and the exponent criterion converts the analytic norm-one question into a structural property of the kernel group.
