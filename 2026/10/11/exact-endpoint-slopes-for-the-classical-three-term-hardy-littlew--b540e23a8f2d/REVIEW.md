# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim is an exact evaluation of two derivatives. Differentiation under the integral is justified because neither trinomial vanishes on the unit circle. Reflecting outside roots gives an absolutely convergent logarithmic Fourier series; the relevant power sums reduce by the two cubic equations. Orthogonality against \(G_\pm\) and \(G_\pm^2\) gives the two closed forms. The bundled checker independently reconstructs the convolution coefficients and power-sum reductions and proves the two signs by exact rational root isolation plus a rigorous positive-series tail bound for the logarithm.

## Originality
**PASS, with a named residual source risk.** Krenedits' primary paper proves the interior sign and explicitly says that its numerical \(d'(1)\approx0.0948\) is used only to guide the proof and that the proof does not determine the value. Targeted searches for the exact lower slope, the upper slope, endpoint derivatives, algebraic-logarithmic forms, and the numerical fingerprints returned no covering statement. The current full text of the 2026 all-gap theorem records equality at even exponents and proves the interior inequality by resonant modes but does not state the two closed forms inspected here. Its version history says that version one contained an endpoint-exponent section; direct retrieval of that older full text failed after bounded attempts, so that version remains a specific residual originality risk. The own prior finite-cyclic third-moment result for the same trinomial pair does not imply continuous derivatives with respect to the exponent.

## Value
**PASS.** The two even endpoints are the natural boundaries of the classical minimal three-term majorant reversal, and the motivating source explicitly singled out the lower endpoint derivative while leaving its precise value unevaluated. Exact slopes quantify how rapidly the defect opens and closes, providing sharp first-order constants for perturbations in the exponent and a symbolic replacement for the numerical lower-end estimate. The result is therefore a motivated structural refinement of a classical example rather than an arbitrary numerical invariant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Krenedits, arXiv:1006.0409, especially Proposition 6, Lemma 7, and Remark 8. Pan--You--Wang--Zhou--Zhang--Chen, arXiv:2609.09740v2, is the strongest broader current theorem on the same family. The earlier version of that recent paper could not be inspected and remains the main residual overlap risk. No claim is made beyond the classical \(k=1\) pair or beyond first-order endpoint transversality.
