# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof establishes the only nonstandard reduction needed. Steiner symmetrization about a reflection axis of \(K\) preserves containment of \(K\), area, convexity, and central symmetry; the new center is the orthogonal projection of the old center onto the axis. Alternating two nonparallel axes drives the centers to their intersection while all bodies remain in one fixed disk. Blaschke selection and continuity of area give a centered limit with exactly the same area. Inclusion then forces the centered minimum to be \(\operatorname{conv}(K\cup(2o-K))\). For regular odd polygons, the original and antipodal vertex sets interlace into a regular \(2n\)-gon, and the area ratio simplifies exactly to \(\cos(\pi/n)\). The supplied checker independently confirms the polygon/hull bookkeeping through \(n=101\). Risk: the proof is planar and uses standard compactness and Steiner-symmetrization facts.

The closest exact formulas found for regular polygons concern different symmetry measures and do not imply the outer-area optimization. The comprehensive 2015 monograph was inspected in the symmetry-measure portions and through relevant term searches; no equivalent regular-polygon Estermann formula was located. The principal residual risk is historical: Estermann's 1928 note and Grünbaum's 1963 survey are not fully searchable through the available lawful sources, and a short equivalent observation could occur there or in an unindexed note.

**PASS.** The structural result reduces a center-optimization problem to a fixed, symmetry-forced center for every planar convex body with two reflection axes. The regular-polygon corollary gives a complete closed formula for a classical measure across the entire family and identifies the actual minimizing supersets. It also provides a natural outer-symmetry counterpart to exact inner-symmetry benchmarks and a quantitative \(n^{-2}\) approach to central symmetry along odd regular polygons. This is a family-level structural fact rather than a one-off numerical computation.
