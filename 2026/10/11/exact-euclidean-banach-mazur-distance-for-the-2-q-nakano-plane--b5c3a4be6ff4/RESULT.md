# Exact Euclidean Banach--Mazur distance for the \((2,q)\) Nakano plane
## Finding
For every real \(q>2\), let \(X_q\) be \(\mathbb R^2\) with the Luxemburg norm generated by the modular \(I_q(x,y)=|x|^2+|y|^q\), equivalently with closed unit ball \(B_q=\{(x,y):x^2+|y|^q\le 1\}\). Put \(m=q/2\). Then the exact Euclidean Banach--Mazur distance is
\[
d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_q,\ell_2^2)^2=1+(m-1)m^{-m/(m-1)}=1+\left(\frac q2-1\right)\left(\frac2q\right)^{q/(q-2)}.
\]
The Euclidean norm itself is optimal up to an overall scalar: among all Euclidean pullbacks \(H_Q(z)=\sqrt{z^TQz}\), an optimal quadratic form is \(Q=I\). The maximum Euclidean squared norm on the \(X_q\)-unit sphere occurs at the four sign choices satisfying \(y^2=m^{-1/(m-1)}\) and \(x^2=1-m^{-m/(m-1)}\); the minimum occurs at the coordinate-axis points.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix a real exponent \(q>2\). The space is the real two-coordinate Musielak--Orlicz (Nakano) space with coordinate Young functions \(f_1(t)=t^2\) and \(f_2(t)=t^q\), equipped with the Luxemburg norm. Thus its unit ball is exactly
\[
B_q=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x^2+|y|^q\le1\}.
\]
The claim concerns the multiplicative Banach--Mazur distance to the Euclidean plane and allows arbitrary real linear isomorphisms.

## Proof
Write \(N_q\) for the Luxemburg norm. For a positive-definite quadratic form \(Q\), let \(H_Q(z)^2=z^TQz\). Its squared distortion relative to \(N_q\) is
\[
\frac{\sup_{N_q(z)=1}H_Q(z)^2}{\inf_{N_q(z)=1}H_Q(z)^2}.
\]
The norm \(N_q\) is invariant under the two coordinate sign changes. If
\[
aN_q(z)^2\le z^TQz\le bN_q(z)^2
\]
for all \(z\), then the same inequalities hold for every sign-conjugate of \(Q\). Averaging those four quadratic forms deletes the off-diagonal entries and preserves the same constants \(a,b\). Hence an optimal Euclidean pullback may be taken diagonal.

After an irrelevant positive rescaling, write the diagonal form as \(H_r(x,y)^2=x^2+r y^2\) with \(r>0\). On the unit sphere set \(t=y^2\in[0,1]\) and \(m=q/2>1\). Since \(x^2=1-t^m\),
\[
H_r(x,y)^2=F_r(t):=1-t^m+rt.
\]
The function \(F_r\) is concave. Therefore
\[
\min_{0\le t\le1}F_r(t)=\min\{1,r\}.
\]
If \(0<r<m\), its unique maximum occurs at \(t=(r/m)^{1/(m-1)}\), giving
\[
\max F_r=1+(m-1)(r/m)^{m/(m-1)}.
\]
If \(r\ge m\), then \(F_r\) is increasing on \([0,1]\) and \(\max F_r=r\).

For \(0<r\le1\), the squared distortion is
\[
R(r)=\frac{1+(m-1)(r/m)^{m/(m-1)}}{r}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
r^2R'(r)=-1+(r/m)^{m/(m-1)}<0,
\]
so \(R\) decreases up to \(r=1\). For \(1\le r<m\), the denominator is \(1\) and the numerator is strictly increasing, while for \(r\ge m\) the distortion is \(r\). Thus the global minimizer is \(r=1\). Substitution yields
\[
d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_q,\ell_2^2)^2=1+(m-1)m^{-m/(m-1)}.
\]
At \(r=1\), the maximum is attained at \(t=m^{-1/(m-1)}\), and the two endpoint values are both \(1\), proving the stated extremal directions and completing the proof.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and does not depend on finite enumeration. The only optimization is the one-variable concave function \(F_r(t)=1-t^m+rt\), whose endpoint minima, unique interior critical point for \(r<m\), and monotonicity in the three ranges \(0<r\le1\), \(1\le r<m\), and \(r\ge m\) are explicit. The sign-averaging step shows that no rotated ellipse can improve the diagonal optimum.

As a consistency check, \(q=4\) gives \(m=2\) and
\[
d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_4,\ell_2^2)^2=\frac54,
\qquad d_{\mathrm{BM}}(X_4,\ell_2^2)=\frac{\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
This check is not used as evidence for the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Kobos, arXiv:1305.6288 (first public version 2013-05-27), treats finite-dimensional Musielak--Orlicz spaces with Luxemburg norms and explicitly places Banach--Mazur distance in the surrounding finite-dimensional geometry, but its main results concern equilateral dimension rather than the exact Euclidean distance of this mixed-power plane. Castillo-Santos and Japón, DOI 10.1186/s13663-015-0357-7, give the Luxemburg definition for Musielak--Orlicz sequence spaces and identify Nakano spaces by coordinate powers.

A published research record for the special mixed-power body with exponent \(q=4\) computes a different invariant for the same body through a linear optimization and obtains the reciprocal constant \(2/\sqrt5\). That special case is therefore not claimed as independently new evidence; the present result is the all-\(q>2\) Banach--Mazur formula and the proof that the Euclidean quadratic form is globally optimal for every exponent in this family.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to the two-coordinate exponent pair \((2,q)\) with \(q>2\). It does not determine the Euclidean Banach--Mazur distance for a general exponent pair \((p,q)\), for higher-dimensional Nakano spaces, or for arbitrary Musielak--Orlicz coordinate functions. The literature search did not locate an earlier statement of this exact all-\(q\) formula, but terminology in convex geometry may differ; the most relevant residual risk is an equivalent ellipse-approximation result stated for mixed-power convex bodies rather than Nakano spaces.
