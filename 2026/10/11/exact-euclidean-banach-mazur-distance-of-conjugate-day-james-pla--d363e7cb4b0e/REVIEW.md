# Review: Exact Euclidean Banach–Mazur distance of conjugate Day–James planes

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the Euclidean comparison to the symmetric quadratic family \(H_\rho\), proves exact antinorm reciprocity from the Radon structure, establishes the unique scalar balance point, and checks the two monotone branches on both sides of the balance. The quantified domain, conjugate exponent relation, and boundary \(p=2\) are stated explicitly. Independent sanity checks at several exponents agree with the formula, but they are not used as proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest inspected primary source, Martini–Swanepoel, supplies antinorm duality and explicitly lists the conjugate mixed \(\ell_p\)-\(\ell_q\) family as Radon; Alonso supplies the Day–James framework. Neither inspected source states the exact Euclidean Banach–Mazur formula or the unique balance equation. A prior exact \(p=3\) record is a genuine special case and is therefore treated as covered; the accepted claim is the all-\(p\) theorem, which strictly subsumes that one point. Broad semantic searches for Day–James/Radon/Euclidean Banach–Mazur aliases did not reveal equivalent or stronger coverage. The main residual risk is an unindexed historical distance-ellipsoid formulation.

## Value
PASS. The conjugate Day–James family is a classical, naturally motivated family of non-Euclidean Radon planes. An exact Euclidean distortion formula for the entire continuum \(p>2\), together with its optimal ellipse and a closed radical value at \(p=4\), is a structural invariant rather than a parameter-table recomputation. The proof also isolates a reusable self-duality/equioscillation mechanism for Euclidean approximation of Radon norms.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest inspected literature is H. Martini and K. J. Swanepoel, “Antinorms and Radon curves,” arXiv:math/0409200, and J. Alonso, “Any two-dimensional Normed space is a generalized Day-James space,” DOI 10.1186/1029-242X-2011-2. Defant–Michels gives an exact identity for spaces with enough orthogonal symmetries, but its symmetric-sequence hypothesis does not apply to the mixed Day–James norm when \(p\ne2\). No claim is made beyond real conjugate Day–James planes.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
