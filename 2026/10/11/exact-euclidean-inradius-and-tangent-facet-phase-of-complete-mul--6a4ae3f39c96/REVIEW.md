# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the inradius of a centrally symmetric polytope to the minimum centered facet distance, computes the intrinsic normal by orthogonal projection to the sum-zero hyperplane, and optimizes the two published complete-multipartite facet types exactly. The type-I parity term and the type-II balanced-cut term are derived directly. The only possible admissibility gap for balanced type-II labels is handled separately and shown to occur only when the type-I obstruction already dominates. A direct exact-rational facet-label enumeration through \(n=8\) corroborates every case tested without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source supplies the facet classification but does not state the Euclidean inradius, Chebyshev-center reduction, or the local-versus-global tangent-facet phase. Two broader full-text sources on the same polytope family were also inspected and did not supply an implication covering this metric theorem. Searches included aliases involving symmetric edge polytopes, root polytopes, Lipschitz/Kantorovich--Rubinstein terminology, complete multipartite graphs, inradii, and inscribed balls. Residual risk remains for unindexed older metric terminology.

## Value
PASS. The inradius is a natural affine-span Euclidean invariant of a standard graph-polytope family. The result is not a finite census or a renamed facet count: it gives an exact all-dimension formula and exposes a structural phase transition between two geometrically different facet mechanisms, including parity-sensitive equality cases and a complete tangent-facet description.

## Closest literature and limitations
The facet classification of Higashitani--Jochemko--Michałek is essential prior work and is explicitly credited. Later work develops face/adjoining structure and regular-matroid generalizations, but the inspected texts do not state this metric consequence. The theorem is limited to the standard Euclidean realization of complete multipartite symmetric edge polytopes, and the computational replay is only finite corroboration.
