# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the exact facet offsets from the standard Minkowski sum and uses the published exhaustive facet indexing. All singleton facets have the same intrinsic normal norm, and summing their radius constraints gives a global upper bound that simultaneously forces the unique uniform center at equality. Every non-singleton center-containing tube is then checked by an arbitrary-dimensional inequality. The required monotonicity reduces to
\[
(j-3)2^{j-1}+2\ge0,
\]
and the equality analysis correctly isolates the two-dimensional exceptional pentagon. Finite computation is only a corroborative replay.

PASS. The closest stellohedron source gives the exact facet family but no metric theorem; the closest realization source writes the standard star-graph-associahedron Minkowski sum and notes only combinatorial equivalence with partial permutohedra. Full-text searches in those sources found no inradius or Chebyshev-center statement. Targeted published-result and literature searches under direct and alias formulations located no covering theorem. A prior own partial-permutohedron inradius result concerns a different Euclidean realization, and the current cyclohedron result concerns a different building set, so neither implies this claim.

## Value
PASS. The standard stellohedron is a canonical graph associahedron with \(2^n+n-1\) facets. The theorem gives its exact Euclidean inradius and unique incenter in every dimension and identifies the entire contact-facet set. For \(n\ge3\), only \(n+1\) singleton facets control the largest ball, despite exponentially many available facets. The uniformity of the unique incenter is additionally nontrivial because the star center is distinguished by the graph.

The theorem is realization-specific and does not address circumradii or other norms.
