# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the full symmetric group on \(K_n\) to reduce the inball problem to a one-parameter center without assuming the answer. The matching-polytope facet theorem then gives exact coordinate-, degree-, and odd-set-facet distances. The odd-set balance threshold is monotone in the odd-set size, and exact comparison with the degree threshold yields the finite exceptional phase \(n=3,5,7\), with the tie at \(n=4\). Uniqueness of the incenter follows from the active shrunken facet together with the coordinate lower bounds. The circumradius calculation uses every matching vertex through its cardinality and proves the unique center by strong convexity. No finite experiment is used as the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The decisive source is Eisley–Matsushita–Vindas-Meléndez, *Matching Polytopes, Gorensteinness, and the Integer Decomposition Property* (arXiv:2407.08820; DOI 10.1007/s00373-025-02923-8). Its full matching-polytope section and Proposition 3 were inspected. The source gives the irredundant facets but no Euclidean inradius, circumradius, Chebyshev center, or radius statement. Direct and equivalent-formulation searches under complete-graph, blossom-facet, minimum-enclosing-ball, and Chebyshev-center terminology found no prior formula or contact-facet phase. Residual risk remains for differently phrased older work.

## Value
PASS. This gives a complete Euclidean metric profile for a classical symmetric family. The inradius has a genuine low-dimensional phase transition between blossom and degree facets, while the circumradius is controlled exactly by maximum matchings. Both optimal centers are explicit and unique, and both inner and outer contact sets are identified.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest full-text source supplies the exact facet theorem used as a premise and studies Gorensteinness and integer decomposition rather than metric balls. A 2019 same-object article studies the one-skeleton rather than Euclidean radii. The theorem is specific to complete graphs in canonical incidence coordinates; it does not claim a closed formula for arbitrary graphs or affine images.
