# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the defining Minkowski-sum convention through the metric conclusion. Opposite generators give the exact support function \(h_Z(u)=\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}|\langle u,\alpha\rangle|\). Central symmetry makes the Euclidean inradius the minimum of this support function on the unit sphere. Reflection-group invariance reduces that minimum to one chamber, where the support function is \(\langle s,u\rangle\), and a simplicial-cone argument reduces the minimum exactly to the extreme chamber rays. The semistar sum \(s\) is a vertex; vertex-transitivity and the antipodal vertex \(-s\) then give the exact circumradius. No finite experiment substitutes for the arbitrary-dimensional proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source is Martin Winter, *Classification of Vertex-Transitive Zonotopes* (arXiv:1912.10469; DOI 10.1007/s00454-021-00303-6). Its full text was inspected for the canonical generator convention, semistar-to-vertex correspondence, root-system equivalence, and classification theorem. It contains no occurrence of “inradius,” “Weyl vector,” or “support function”; its use of “circumradius” is confined to a two-dimensional normalization argument. published-finding corpus and web searches used vertex-transitive zonotope, Coxeter permutahedron, root zonotope, Weyl-vector, fundamental-ray, and metric-radius formulations. No covering statement was found. Residual risk remains for an equivalent formula hidden under another Coxeter normalization or in unindexed older literature.

## Value
PASS. Winter's theorem classifies the object class structurally; the present theorem adds a uniform Euclidean metric classification. Both radii are exact in every dimension, and the inradius computation collapses a continuous spherical minimization to finitely many chamber rays. The same calculation classifies exactly which facet orbits touch the centered maximal ball, including reducible and unequal-root-length cases.

## Closest literature and limitations
Winter's paper is the decisive closest source and supplies the premises rather than the conclusion. Broader Coxeter-permutahedron literature was searched for stronger coverage but no metric formula implying this result was located. The theorem does not describe all incenters when they are nonunique and does not extend beyond vertex-transitive zonotopes. The literature comparison is evidence-based but cannot exclude every differently phrased or unindexed source.
