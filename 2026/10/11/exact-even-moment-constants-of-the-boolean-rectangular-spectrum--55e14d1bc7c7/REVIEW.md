# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof reduces the problem to an exact Hilbert-valued one-coordinate moment identity. The constant term of \(\|x+zy\|^{2h}\) is computed with all coefficients nonnegative; Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\|x\|\|y\|\le(\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2)/2\) then give the sharp factor \(B_h=2^{-h}\binom{2h}{h}\). The scalar case \(x=y=1\) identifies the combinatorial sum without approximation. Induction through Hilbert direct sums proves the \(d\)-dimensional inequality. Since the \(k=1\) coefficient is strictly positive for \(h\ge2\), equality forces both Cauchy--Schwarz and equal-norm equality at each coordinate, yielding exactly the product-phase extremizers. The finite checker is corroborative only.

## Originality

**PASS.** The classical archive source arXiv:math/9904176v1 defines and studies \(\Lambda(p)\)-sets but the inspected theorem is a qualitative operator-ideal characterization, not this exact Boolean rectangular constant. The inspected multiple-Steinhaus inequality in arXiv:1912.10313v1 uses independent Steinhaus coordinates, a different random system. published-finding corpus searches across exact-value, multiaffine, Hilbert-valued, Boolean-spectrum, tensor-product and \(\Lambda(2h)\) aliases found no statement implying the theorem. The closest own recorded result is the \(h=3\) specialization, which is subsumed by the present theorem rather than implying it. Residual risk remains that an older equivalent tensor-product formulation is indexed under terminology not retrieved.

## Value

**PASS.** The Boolean rectangular spectrum is a canonical tensor-product model in harmonic analysis. Determining its complete exact even-moment profile turns an isolated sixth-moment value into a reusable all-even theorem and simultaneously classifies all extremizers. The result is structural, parameter-uniform, and directly interpretable as an exact family of \(\Lambda(2h)\) constants rather than an arbitrary finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Michels (arXiv:math/9904176v1) supplies the \(\Lambda(p)\) setting but not these constants. Cavalcante--Núñez-Alarcón--Pellegrino--Rueda (arXiv:1912.10313v1) gives a multiple-Steinhaus Khinchine theorem for a different independence structure. The proof here is restricted to even exponents and Boolean Cartesian blocks, and independent audit has not been performed.
