# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Dividing the positive fish equation by \(F\) and integrating over a period gives \(\bar q=g_2/\gamma_2\); integrating the undivided equation gives \(\overline{Fq}=(g_2/\gamma_2)\bar F\). These imply the exact mean identity. The reciprocal expansion is an exact pointwise identity, and its averaged remainder is a positive weighted square. Equality forces \(Z\), then \(F\), then \(P\), to be constant, so strictness is valid precisely for non-equilibrium periodic solutions.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of the motivating article found local stability, Hopf, and numerical periodic-orbit analysis but no exact mean/variance identity of this form. Full-text inspection of the closest accessible periodic Crowley–Martin paper showed a structurally different stage-structured nonautonomous model and period integrations serving existence estimates, not this result. Broader exact-phrase and implication-oriented searches did not locate an equivalent theorem. Two related articles remained available only at abstract/bibliographic level and are recorded as residual risk rather than treated as evidence of noncoverage.

## Value
PASS. The identity is a finite-amplitude diagnostic for the very cycles produced by the source's Hopf analysis. It gives an exact, delay-independent fish-mean ceiling and a quantitative lower bound for mean zooplankton in terms of mean fish biomass; these are directly checkable constraints on simulations and separate equilibrium balance from fluctuation-induced bias.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest full-text comparison is Lu–Xia (2022), DOI 10.3934/math.2022454; its stage structure, time-periodic coefficients, and density-dependent predator mortality prevent its period balances from implying this constant-turnover identity. Tripathi (2020), DOI 10.1111/nrm.12287, studies a nonautonomous Crowley–Martin model. Maiti–Dubey–Chakraborty (2019), DOI 10.1016/j.matcom.2019.01.009, and Upadhyay–Naji (2009), DOI 10.1016/j.chaos.2009.03.020, were not fully accessible in the material inspected, leaving a stated literature-access risk. The result itself is restricted to positive spatially homogeneous periodic solutions.
