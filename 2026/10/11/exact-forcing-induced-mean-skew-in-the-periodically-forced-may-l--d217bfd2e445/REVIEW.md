# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The printed periodically forced May–Leonard vector field has three exact logarithmic derivatives. For any invariant probability measure compactly supported in the open biological cube, their integrals vanish. Subtracting these three linear balances gives the mean gaps \(Y-X=cA/(c^2+ce+e^2)\) and \(X-Z=eA/(c^2+ce+e^2)\), together with the total mean \((3+A)/(3+c-e)\). Uniform phase rotation has Haar marginal. The nonnegative forcing is strictly positive on a set of full phase measure when \(x<1\), so \(A>0\). The same proof applies over any full period of a strictly positive phase-locked periodic orbit. Exact symbolic verification supports the algebra but does not establish dynamical existence.

## Originality
The model is that of Alexandre A. P. Rodrigues, *Abundance of Strange Attractors Near an Attracting Periodically Perturbed Network* (arXiv:2010.05776; doi:10.1137/20M1335510). That source develops return-map bifurcations and strange-attractor/SRB existence, without an explicit species-mean skew theorem. Afraimovich, Hsu and Lin, *Chaotic Behavior of Three Competing Species of May–Leonard Model under Small Periodic Perturbations* (doi:10.1142/S021812740100216X), use a different additive forcing architecture and establish periodic, quasiperiodic, and chaotic regimes, not this one-coordinate mean-gap law. The logarithmic-averaging method is standard. Available comparisons do not establish a previously published formula for the exact forced system; an unindexed equivalent remains possible.

## Mathematical value
The exact finite-amplitude ratio \((Y-X)/(X-Z)=c/e\) connects observable population means to the source's natural network-attraction parameter \(\delta=c/e\), independently of the forcing amplitude, frequency, or type of stationary recurrence. The strict ordering \(Y>X>Z\) is a model-specific structural prediction. Both statements are conditional on compact interior recurrence rather than being claims about all attractors of the flow.

## Limitations
No claim is made about existence, uniqueness, or stability of compact-interior invariant sets; no direct inference is made for boundary-accumulating SRB measures without additional integrability. General invariant-measure coboundary identities explain the method; bibliographic searches do not prove absolute originality.
