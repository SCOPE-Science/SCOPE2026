# Scientific assessment

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact disjoint-fiber identity reduces the operator to normalized, disjoint input columns. Each of the four upper bounds follows from discarding fewer than \(j\) large columns. Dimension counting proves the lower bounds for approximation, Bernstein and Gelfand numbers. The Kolmogorov lower bound instead uses a fully quantified lemma: \(j\) normalized pairwise disjoint columns cannot simultaneously lie at distance less than one from a subspace of dimension below \(j\) in \(\ell_p\) for \(0<p\le1\). The lemma follows from \(p\)-subadditivity, strict column dominance and an elementary linear-dependence contradiction. The proof handles nonattained suprema and vanishing fibers. Computational tests are nonexhaustive and not used to infer an infinite theorem.

## Originality
**PASS, conditional on the accessible comparison.** The motivating 2016 preprint concerns analytic Hardy \(H^2\), where Hilbert singular-value dualities are available; the 2023 weighted sequence-space paper's accessible abstract states compactness, closed range and essential norms, not finite-index approximation or quotient widths. The full published four-s-number result for \(C(K)\) uses sup-norm geometry, not nonlocally convex \(\ell_p\) with \(0<p<1\); the finite-Euclidean-fiber multiplication theorem explicitly assumes \(1<p<\infty\) and uses reflexive duality for Kolmogorov numbers. Neither covers the new strict block-\(p\)-dominance lemma. This assessment does not claim literature exhaustiveness. The entire 2023 article was not materially available through the attempted open full-text routes, and an unindexed stronger theorem remains possible.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The complete four-invariant spectral profile addresses natural approximation and quotient-width questions for a family whose boundedness/compactness theory was developed specifically to avoid Banach duality. The Kolmogorov component depends on a nonstandard strict block-dominance lemma, not merely a diagonal renaming, because disjoint quasi-Banach image subspaces can fail to admit contractive retractions. The characterization holds for arbitrary symbols, fibers and positive weights at every index, and yields the compact/strict-singularity boundary without finite-dimensional restrictions.
