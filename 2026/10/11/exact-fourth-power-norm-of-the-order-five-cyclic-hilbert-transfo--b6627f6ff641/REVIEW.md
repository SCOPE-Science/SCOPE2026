# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the normalized Fourier convention, real-valued conjugacy, the exact fourth-moment Parseval expansion, and the action of the sign multiplier on the two positive-frequency phases. Because the two phase combinations are independent, the quotient is globally maximized at the two endpoint phase conditions. The remaining one-variable quotient has an exact derivative factorization, and the critical polynomial has exactly one positive root. The equality class follows from solving the two phase equations and from the unique magnitude ratio.

The standalone verifier independently replays the exact derivative identity, the elimination identity for the algebraic norm, the root bracket, the moment formula, and the direct five-point transform at an extremizer. Sampling is explicitly non-exhaustive and is not used to establish the global bound.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary sources concern the discrete Hilbert transform on the infinite lattice. A public finite-cyclic problem uses the same sign multiplier at order \(31\), but records only a non-sharp bracket and no order-five implication. Searches using the order-five formulation, finite-cyclic aliases, the critical polynomial, the algebraic norm polynomial, and the numerical fingerprint found no implication-equivalent result. The closest indexed order-five four-character item concerns an extremal-measure problem, not the operator norm of \(H\).

Residual risk remains that an older finite-signal calculation exists under substantially different terminology; no source with matching operator, group order, exponent, and unrestricted real zero-mean domain was found.

## Value
PASS. This is not a routine parameter increment: \(C_3\) is automatically \(L^4\)-isometric on the real zero-mean subspace, so \(C_5\) is the first odd cyclic order at which the fourth-power norm problem becomes nontrivial. The result gives an exact algebraic sharp constant and all extremizers for a finite analogue of a classical sharp Hilbert-transform norm problem, and supplies a compact two-phase reduction relevant to larger-order investigations.
