# Scientific review: fractional illumination of convex polygons

## Correctness — PASS

The boundary-to-vertices reduction is exact for a convex polygon with genuine edges: each inward direction at a vertex is inward at either incident edge. Vertex direction sets are precisely open arcs determined by adjacent normals. An interval packing bound and a cyclic-block weighted construction match on every equiangular polygon; in the odd perturbed case the expanded reference arcs have length less than \(q\Delta\), while reference block-midpoints retain strict angular margin. The endpoint inequalities are strict. The bundled checker independently replays exact rational incidence tests. All universal assertions rest on the written argument rather than finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Naszódi (2009) established the definition and the special regular-pentagon lower bound \(5/2\), which this result explicitly acknowledges; the source does not give this all-side-count formula or an open perturbation plateau. Gorodzha (arXiv:2609.23913, 2026) proves a universal upper bound and characterizes equality but does not give the proposed exact values. Two close-titled results about illumination bodies concern convex-hull area level sets, not weighted direction covers, as confirmed by reading their theorem statements and proofs. Targeted searches of public mathematical records for fractional polygonal illumination and cyclic-arc equivalences did not find a dominating result. The possibility of a weakly indexed older derivation remains; search absence alone is not a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new value is an exact geometric optimization invariant for all equiangular polygons, including nonregular shapes, rather than an isolated symmetric numerical example. The quantitative odd-sided plateau identifies an open set of outward normal fans on which the infinite-dimensional fractional covering problem has the same optimum. Its proof gives an explicit optimal finite-support covering and matching counting obstruction. This complements the recent general upper bound with sharp constructive two-dimensional information.

## Limitations and prior-work comparison

The angular tolerance is sufficient, not proved best possible; the result does not classify arbitrary polygonal normal fans. The known pentagon lower bound is prior art, not counted as the original component. An independent review may uncover antecedents via the general circular-arc fractional-transversal literature.
