# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof first establishes the exact reciprocal identity \(J[t,X]=tJ[1/t,X]\), reducing the problem to \(0<t\le1\). The hexagon's dihedral isometries reduce every unit pair to one fixed edge for \(x\) and four canonical edge classes for \(y\). Two classes have an immediate transformed-norm bound \(1\). The other two have explicit max-affine formulas; assuming both skew chord norms exceed \(L=(t^2+t+1)/(t+1)\) forces contradictory inequalities on the edge parameter. The pair \(x=(1,0)\), \(y=(t/(1+t),1)\) gives equality. The proof is valid on all boundaries, including \(t=1\).

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2023 full text was inspected at the same affine-regular hexagon example, its skew von Neumann--Jordan calculation, and all of Section 5 on \(J[t,X]\). The paper computes other skew invariants on this plane but gives no skew James profile. A later generalized-mean paper was inspected because it explicitly contains Fu's \(J[t,X]\) as the mean-exponent \(-\infty\) endpoint and treats the same hexagon. Its hexagonal extreme-point formula and proof are explicitly restricted to mean exponent at least \(1\), so they do not determine this endpoint. published-finding corpus and the current own ledger were searched under exact notation, aliases, formula forms, and the source authors without finding a covering statement.

## Value
PASS. This is the missing minimum-type member of a canonical family of skew constants on the exact plane already used by the defining paper. The formula also proves that the source's general comparison \(J[t,X]\le\min\{1,t\}J(X)+|1-t|\) is strictly nonsharp on every unbalanced parameter of this example, with an explicit positive gap. That makes the result a structural benchmark rather than a routine isolated evaluation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Fu, Yang, Liu and Li introduced \(J[t,X]\) in Kyungpook Mathematical Journal 63 (2023), 199--223 and used the same hexagonal plane for \(E[t,X]\) and \(C_{\mathrm{NJ}}[X]\). Rao, Liu, Wu, Yin and Ni later embedded \(J[t,X]\) into a generalized-mean family, but their hexagon examples are proved for mean exponent at least \(1\). The present result does not classify all maximizing pairs or extend the profile to arbitrary polygonal spaces.
