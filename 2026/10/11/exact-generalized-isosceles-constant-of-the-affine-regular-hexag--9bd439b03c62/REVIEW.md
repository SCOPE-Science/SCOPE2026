# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source identity relating \(\Omega'\) to \(\gamma_X(1/3)\) was reconstructed algebraically rather than accepted only by citation. For the hexagonal norm, the profile objective is convex on the product unit ball, so a maximum occurs at a vertex pair. Exact rational evaluation of all \(36\) pairs gives the sharp value \(25/18\), hence \(\Omega'=5/4\). The direct pair \(u=(1,1/2)\), \(v=(0,1/2)\) independently attains \(5/4\). Risk: the argument is tied to the stated norm and does not extend automatically to other hexagonal models.

## Originality
PASS. The inspected defining source computes \(\Omega=49/40\) on the same plane, then separately introduces \(\Omega'\) and proves its profile identity, but does not compute \(\Omega'\) for that example. Searches using the exact value, the profile \(\gamma_X(1/3)\), the affine-regular hexagon, and the original notation did not locate the \(5/4\) evaluation. Closest located literature treats the same plane with different geometric constants or treats generalized isosceles constants on other spaces. Risk: finite indexing cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent computation under different notation.

## Value
PASS. The defining source explicitly motivates \(\Omega'\) by removing the unit-vector restriction from \(\Omega\). Computing the generalized constant on the source's own canonical affine-regular hexagonal example answers the most direct comparison question and proves that the relaxation is genuinely strict, with exact gap \(1/40\). This is a natural structural benchmark rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is arXiv:2111.08392: it supplies the definitions, the exact value \(\Omega(X_H)=49/40\), and the identity \(\Omega'(X)=\frac9{10}\gamma_X(1/3)\), but not the claimed hexagonal evaluation of \(\Omega'\). A 2025 paper on isosceles orthogonal constants for Morrey spaces was checked as later related literature; its scope is different and does not cover this finite-dimensional hexagonal value. The result here is not a classification beyond \(X_H\).
