# Exact \(H_2\) constant of the affine-regular hexagonal Banach plane
## Finding
Let
\[
X_H=(\mathbb R^2,N),
\qquad
N(s,t)=\max\{|s|,|t|,|s-t|\}.
\]
For the Baronti--Bertella quadratic isosceles constant
\[
H_2(X)=
\sup\left\{
\frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}{\|x+\lambda y\|}:
 x,y\in S_X,\ x\perp_I y,\ \lambda\ge0
\right\},
\]
where \(x\perp_I y\) means \(\|x+y\|=\|x-y\|\), one has
\[
H_2(X_H)=\frac{3\sqrt{10}}8.
\]
A maximizing configuration is
\[
x=\left(1,\frac13\right),
\qquad
y=\left(-\frac13,-1\right),
\qquad
\lambda=3.
\]
Indeed,
\[
N(x)=N(y)=1,
\qquad
N(x+y)=N(x-y)=\frac43,
\]
and
\[
N(x+3y)=\frac83,
\]
so the displayed quotient is \(3\sqrt{10}/8\).

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are real. The unit ball of \(X_H\) is the affine-regular hexagon with vertices
\[
(1,0),\ (1,1),\ (0,1),\ (-1,0),\ (-1,-1),\ (0,-1).
\]
The computation concerns the exponent \(p=2\) in the Baronti--Bertella family \(H_p\). It does not claim a formula for arbitrary \(p\).

The dihedral linear isometry group of this hexagon is generated by
\[
(s,t)\mapsto(t,s),
\qquad
(s,t)\mapsto(s,s-t),
\qquad
(s,t)\mapsto(-s,-t).
\]
Because this group is transitive on edges, an isosceles-orthogonal unit pair may be analyzed after putting
\[
x=(1,u),\qquad 0\le u\le1.
\]

## Proof
Baronti and Bertella prove that for their pointwise quantity \(\mu_2(x,y)\),
\[
\mu_2(x,y)=\mu_2(y,x).
\]
Consequently it is enough to bound the defining quotient for \(\lambda\ge1\): if \(0<\lambda<1\), then
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}{N(x+\lambda y)}
=
\frac{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{-2}}}{N(y+\lambda^{-1}x)}.
\]

We first classify the unit isosceles pairs. Fix \(x=(1,u)\) on one edge and write \(y\) on one of the six edges. On each edge pair, both
\[
N(x+y)
\quad\text{and}\quad
N(x-y)
\]
are maxima of six affine functions. Choosing their active signed facets and imposing equality gives a finite exact decomposition. After deleting empty pieces, there are eight line segments. The exact facet certificate in `verify.py` lists all eight and checks that, after a hexagonal isometry and possibly interchanging \(x,y\), every segment is one of the two families
\[
x=(1,u),
\qquad
y_+(u)=\left(\frac{1-u}{2},1\right),
\qquad 0\le u\le\frac13,
\tag{1}
\]
or
\[
x=(1,u),
\qquad
y_-(u)=-\left(\frac{1-u}{2},1\right),
\qquad 0\le u\le\frac13.
\tag{2}
\]
This is an exhaustive continuum classification, not a sampling argument.

For the plus family (1), let \(\lambda\ge1\). Two coordinates of \(x+\lambda y_+(u)\) are
\[
S=1+\frac{1-u}{2}\lambda,
\qquad
T=u+\lambda.
\]
Therefore
\[
N(x+\lambda y_+(u))\ge\max\{S,T\}.
\]
They cross at
\[
\lambda_+(u)=\frac{2(1-u)}{1+u},
\]
which lies in \([1,2]\). On \([1,\lambda_+(u)]\), the ratio \(S/\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\) is decreasing. On \([\lambda_+(u),\infty)\), the ratio \(T/\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\) has no interior minimum: its derivative changes, if at all, from positive to negative. Hence the minimum is attained at the crossing or at infinity; the crossing value is at most \(1\), so it is the relevant one. Thus
\[
\frac{N(x+\lambda y_+(u))^2}{1+\lambda^2}
\ge
\frac{(u^2-u+2)^2}{5u^2-6u+5}.
\]
Moreover
\[
45(u^2-u+2)^2-32(5u^2-6u+5)
=
45u^4-90u^3+65u^2+12u+20>0
\]
for \(0\le u\le1/3\), because
\[
65u^2-90u^3=u^2(65-90u)\ge35u^2.
\]
Therefore the plus family is strictly above the target \(32/45\).

For the minus family (2), put
\[
A=\lambda-u,
\qquad
B=1-u+\frac{1+u}{2}\lambda.
\]
For \(\lambda\ge1\), both are nonnegative and
\[
N(x+\lambda y_-(u))\ge\max\{A,B\}.
\]
They cross at
\[
\lambda_-(u)=\frac{2}{1-u}.
\]
On \([1,\lambda_-(u)]\), the ratio \(B/\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\) is decreasing, since
\[
\frac{1+u}{2}-(1-u)\lambda
\le
\frac{-1+3u}{2}\le0.
\]
On \([\lambda_-(u),\infty)\), the ratio \(A/\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\) is increasing. Hence the minimum occurs at the crossing, giving
\[
\frac{N(x+\lambda y_-(u))^2}{1+\lambda^2}
\ge
R(u):=
\frac{(u^2-u+2)^2}{u^2-2u+5}.
\]
The target inequality \(R(u)\ge32/45\) is equivalent to
\[
(3u-1)(15u^3-25u^2+56u-20)\ge0.
\]
On \([0,1/3]\), the first factor is nonpositive. The cubic is increasing because
\[
45u^2-50u+56>0
\]
(the discriminant is negative), and at \(u=1/3\) it equals \(-32/9\). Thus the cubic is also negative throughout the interval, proving
\[
N(x+\lambda y)^2
\ge
\frac{32}{45}(1+\lambda^2)
\]
for every unit isosceles pair and every \(\lambda\ge0\).

Therefore
\[
H_2(X_H)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{45}{32}}
=
\frac{3\sqrt{10}}8.
\]
The explicit pair in the Finding section attains equality, completing the proof.

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to reconstruct the six vertices, six signed facets and all active-facet cases for
\[
N(x+y)=N(x-y).
\]
After fixing one edge for \(x\), it certifies exactly eight nonempty isosceles segments and verifies, by explicit matrices from the twelve-element linear isometry group and optional interchange of the two vectors, that all eight reduce to the two canonical families (1)--(2).

The same checker uses symbolic algebra to verify the crossing parameters, the two rational lower envelopes, the factorization establishing the sharp \(32/45\) bound, and the equality configuration. No floating-point enumeration is used as proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Baronti and Bertella introduced \(H_p\), proved the symmetry \(\mu_p(x,y)=\mu_p(y,x)\), established general bounds, and characterized Hilbert spaces by the extremal value. Their inspected full text contains no occurrence of “hexagon” or “regular” and gives no affine-regular-hexagonal computation.

The closest indexed work on the same hexagonal norm computes different invariants, including an isosceles von Neumann--Jordan profile and Euclidean Banach--Mazur data. Those statements do not imply the present ratio optimization for \(H_2\). A separate exact formula for \(H_2\) on symmetric Minkowski planes does not cover this affine-regular hexagon: the latter does not admit the required unconditional quarter-turn symmetry.

The present value is genuinely interior to the universal range for \(H_2\): it is larger than the Hilbert value \(1\) but below the square endpoint from the general theory.

## Limitations
The result is specific to \(p=2\) and the affine-regular hexagonal plane. It does not determine \(H_p(X_H)\) for arbitrary \(p\), nor does it classify every maximizing triple \((x,y,\lambda)\).

Bibliographic and semantic searches cannot establish absolute novelty. An equivalent calculation under a different affine normalization or under pointwise \(\mu_2\) notation remains a residual literature risk, but no covering statement was found in the inspected defining paper, the checked public research index, direct exact-value searches, or the current prior ledger.
