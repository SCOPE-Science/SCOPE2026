# Exact Haar hexagon law and cubic rigidity for central sections of rectangular boxes

## Finding

Let \(a,b,c>0\) and \(K_{a,b,c}=[-a,a]\times[-b,b]\times[-c,c]\subset\mathbb R^3\). Draw a unit vector \(U\) uniformly from \(S^2\), and intersect the box with the central plane \(U^\perp\). Write \(p_6(a,b,c)\) for the probability that this plane section is a hexagon. Define
\[
\alpha=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{a^2+c^2},\qquad
\gamma=\sqrt{b^2+c^2},
\]
and
\[
Q(a,b,c)=\alpha\beta\gamma+bc\gamma+ac\beta+ab\alpha.
\]
**Theorem (exact law, rigidity, and range).** For every \(a,b,c>0\),
\[
\boxed{p_6(a,b,c)=\frac4\pi\arctan\!\left(\frac{2abc}{Q(a,b,c)}\right).}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{0<p_6(a,b,c)\le \frac4\pi\arctan\!\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{5}\right)
=1-\frac6\pi\arcsin\!\left(\frac13\right),}
\]
and equality holds **if and only if** \(a=b=c\). The set of possible probabilities as positive box dimensions vary is exactly
\[
\left(0,\frac4\pi\arctan\!\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{5}\right)\right].
\]
Almost every central section of a three-dimensional box is a parallelogram or a hexagon; consequently its parallelogram probability equals \(1-p_6(a,b,c)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The three coordinate half-lengths are arbitrary positive real numbers; no aspect-ratio, volume, or ordering restriction is made. The law on planes is the unique rotation-invariant probability on the Grassmannian of planes through the origin, induced by Haar-uniform \(U\in S^2\). It is **not** the affine image of a Haar-distributed plane, nor a distribution on translated cutting planes. The section is counted by vertices/edges, not by Euclidean regularity. Null sets of planes through box vertices or parallel to an edge do not affect the probability. The classification is specific to three-dimensional **orthogonal** boxes; it is not asserted for arbitrary parallelotopes or higher dimensions.

## Proof

**1. Six faces give six edges precisely in the triangle chamber.** Let \(u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\in S^2\) have nonzero coordinates and avoid equality cases. The intersection of \(u^\perp\) with the relative interior of the face \(x_1=a\) is nonempty exactly when there exist \(|x_2|<b\), \(|x_3|<c\) with \(a u_1+u_2x_2+u_3x_3=0\). As the possible values of \(u_2x_2+u_3x_3\) fill the open interval of radius \(b|u_2|+c|u_3|\), this holds exactly when
\[
a|u_1|<b|u_2|+c|u_3|.
\]
The same criterion applies to the opposite face. Repeating for all three coordinate pairs shows that **all six** faces contribute edges exactly when the positive numbers
\[
X=a|u_1|,\qquad Y=b|u_2|,\qquad Z=c|u_3|
\]
satisfy the three strict triangle inequalities. A generic centrally symmetric planar polygon cut from a box has either four or six edges; no three-edge or five-edge option occurs. The equalities define finitely many great-circle arcs and have spherical probability zero.

**2. The chamber is a simplicial spherical triangle.** Coordinate-sign symmetry allows us to work in the positive octant \(x,y,z\ge0\), whose spherical area is \(\pi/2\). Under the positive diagonal change of variables \((x,y,z)\mapsto(ax,by,cz)\), the closed triangle-inequality cone is generated by
\[
(1,1,0),\qquad (1,0,1),\qquad(0,1,1).
\]
Its pullback is the cone generated by
\[
r_1=(b,a,0),\qquad r_2=(c,0,a),\qquad r_3=(0,c,b).
\]
All three rays are in the positive octant, the determinant is nonzero, and their spherical triangle has positive area \(\Omega\). Thus
\[
p_6(a,b,c)=\frac{\Omega}{\pi/2}=\frac{2\Omega}{\pi}.
\]

**3. Exact spherical area.** For any noncoplanar rays \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) with positive pairwise scalar products, the standard solid-angle identity [3] states
\[
\Omega=2\arctan\!\frac{|\det(r_1,r_2,r_3)|}
{\|r_1\|\|r_2\|\|r_3\|+(r_1\cdot r_2)\|r_3\|+(r_2\cdot r_3)\|r_1\|+(r_3\cdot r_1)\|r_2\|}.
\]
Its denominator is strictly positive here, hence the principal arctangent is unambiguous. Direct substitution gives
\[
|\det(r_1,r_2,r_3)|=2abc,\qquad
\|r_1\|=\alpha,\quad\|r_2\|=\beta,\quad\|r_3\|=\gamma,
\]
\[
r_1\cdot r_2=bc,\qquad r_2\cdot r_3=ab,\qquad r_3\cdot r_1=ac.
\]
The denominator is precisely \(Q(a,b,c)\), yielding the claimed probability formula. Independently, the solid angle can be calculated from Girard's spherical excess: for \(p_i=r_i/\|r_i\|\), let \(\theta_i\) be the angle between projections of the two other \(p_j\) into \(p_i^\perp\); then \(\Omega=\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3-\pi\). This supplies the analytically equivalent, numerically independently implemented check in the companion verifier.

**4. Universal upper bound with a unique maximizer.** Positive dimensions imply
\[
\alpha\ge\sqrt{2ab},\quad\beta\ge\sqrt{2ac},\quad\gamma\ge\sqrt{2bc},
\]
by arithmetic–geometric mean. Consequently
\[
\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma}{abc}\ge2\sqrt2.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Q}{abc}
&=\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma}{abc}+\frac\gamma a+\frac\beta b+\frac\alpha c\\
&\ge2\sqrt2+\sqrt2\left(\frac{\sqrt{bc}}a+\frac{\sqrt{ac}}b+\frac{\sqrt{ab}}c\right)\\
&\ge2\sqrt2+3\sqrt2=5\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last bound is arithmetic–geometric mean applied to three positive expressions whose product is \(1\). Since arctangent is strictly increasing,
\[
p_6(a,b,c)\le\frac4\pi\arctan\!\left(\frac2{5\sqrt2}\right).
\]
Equality in the first three inequalities requires \(a=b\), \(a=c\), and \(b=c\), and hence \(a=b=c\). Conversely, the equal cube attains equality. The trigonometric equality with \(1-(6/\pi)\arcsin(1/3)\) follows, for example, by the triple-angle identities in the first quadrant, or by comparing the standard exact cube probability in [2].

**5. Sharp full range.** The formula is continuous and positive on the positive octant, invariant under common rescaling. Fix \(b=c=1\) and set \(a=t\ge1\). At \(t=1\) it equals the sharp maximum; as \(t\to\infty\), the factor \(\alpha\beta\gamma/(abc)\) grows without bound, so \(p_6(t,1,1)\to0\). The intermediate value theorem supplies every value strictly between zero and the maximum. Positivity excludes zero itself. For generic sections the only other face count is four, so \(p_4=1-p_6\). \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 program `verify_probability.py` re-evaluates the solid angle from spherical excess, independently of the determinant/denominator formula. It compares 400 random positive boxes using two independent area formulas, 720,000 reproducible Gaussian sampled directions across four boxes, checks the known cube value and its trigonometric identity, and tests scale invariance and the analytic sharp denominator inequality at 200 positive triples. Run `python3 verify_probability.py` from a directory containing that file. The observed output ends with `VERIFY_OK`. Symbolic direct determinant and dot-product checks were separately performed using exact algebra. These finite tests are **not** proofs of the result for all positive dimensions: the preceding argument supplies the universal proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Abas [1] studies spherical random *width* laws and spectral reconstruction for orthotopes; it motivates studying shape-sensitive geometric measurements under the same Haar model. Its width law \(2(a|U_1|+b|U_2|+c|U_3|)\) and recovery theorems do not state a probability law for the **number of edges in a random central planar section** or its optimizing aspect ratio. Lonke [2] derives expected face counts for Haar-random sections of the **cube**, including the three-dimensional cube value. In dimension three the mean vertex count determines the cube's hexagon probability, and [2] therefore already gives the **equal-edge specialization** of our law. The new assertion concerns all unequal rectangular boxes, gives their explicit aspect-ratio formula, and proves sharp cubic optimality over the entire family. The cube-only identity is also indexed as OEIS A349605 [4]. An arbitrary diagonal affine transformation of a uniform plane does not preserve the Haar law, so the cube result cannot be extended to unequal boxes merely by affine equivalence. The standard spherical-triangle formula of van Oosterom and Strackee [3] is explicitly used, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

No uniqueness or priority assertion can rule out an unindexed equivalent stereological formula. The probability uses centered planes and uniformly sampled **normal directions**; random offsets and different plane measures lead to distinct models. The theorem does not cover oblique parallelotopes, distributions of other section metrics, or any higher-dimensional polytope. It does not identify a new formula for the cube alone; that case was already known. The computer checks have limited finite scope and do not constitute an independent mathematical audit.
