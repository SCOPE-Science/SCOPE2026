# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from the exact matrix-masa formula
\[
\cos\alpha\!\left(\Delta^{{(k)}},Z\Delta^{{(k)}}Z^*\right)=\frac{{k}}{{k-1}}\left\||Z|^{{\circ2}}\circ Z-\frac1kZ\right\|_{{\mathrm{{op}}}},
\]
and the verified characterization of maximal angle by a Hadamard relative unitary. For a Hadamard \(H\in\mathrm U(m)\), the defect for \(H\otimes W\) factors exactly as \(m^{{-1}}H\otimes Q_n(W)\). Operator-norm multiplicativity then gives the claimed coefficient \((n-1)/(mn-1)\). Coincident and maximally angled second-factor boundary cases were checked explicitly.

## Originality
PASS. The focal full text supplies the two premises but does not state the two-pair tensor law or the exact attenuation factor. The closest directly relevant tensor result, Gupta--Sharma Proposition 5.5, preserves angle only when the same unital algebra is tensored onto both subalgebras; it does not imply the present formula for tensoring two distinct pairs. Targeted searches under equivalent tensor, Kronecker, Hadamard, and orthogonal-masa formulations found no covering statement. Residual risk remains that an older projection-angle or subfactor formulation encodes the same identity.

## Value
PASS. Tensorial behavior is a natural structural question for the newly explicit matrix-masa angle. The theorem gives a complete exact law in the first nontrivial regime where one factor is orthogonal, shows that one maximally angled factor does not force maximality of the product, and provides an explicit tensor-orthogonalization mechanism with dimension-dependent rate. This complements rather than restates the known common-factor stability theorem.

Closest literature: Ghosh--Kumar, arXiv:2609.38206v1; Gupta--Sharma, arXiv:2602.03694v1. Limitation: no scalar law is claimed when both factor angles are nonmaximal.
