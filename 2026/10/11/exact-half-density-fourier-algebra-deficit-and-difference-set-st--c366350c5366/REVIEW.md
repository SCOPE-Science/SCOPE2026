# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Parseval fixes the nonprincipal squared Fourier mass. Expanding the squared distance from the constant radius \(c_v\) gives the claimed deficit identity exactly, without approximation. Fourier inversion of the autocorrelation proves that zero deficit is equivalent to constant nonzero difference multiplicity. A second Parseval identity gives \(V(A)=v^3\sum_{\gamma\ne1}(|\widehat{1_A}(\gamma)|^2-c_v^2)^2\), and the stated two-sided bounds follow from the explicit range of \(|\widehat{1_A}(\gamma)|+c_v\). The Paley prime case follows from the standard quadratic Gauss-sum evaluation. Finite verification is corroborative only.

## Originality
**PASS.** Green--Konyagin use exactly the half-density Fourier-algebra norm but optimize in the opposite direction: their Question 1.1 defines the minimum and Theorem 1.2 gives a lower bound for it. Their inspected full text does not state a maximum, the exact quadratic deficit, or autocorrelation stability. Matolcsi--Ruzsa treat difference-avoidance and positive exponential-sum constants, not this norm deficit. Targeted searches under the equivalent formulations “half-set Fourier-algebra maximum,” “difference-set Fourier \(L^1\) deficit,” “autocorrelation variance stability,” and “Paley projection norm stability” found no statement implying the theorem. The classical equivalence between difference sets and flat nonprincipal character magnitudes is not claimed as new; the surviving claim is the exact deficit/stability theorem and its sharp prime-cyclic extremal consequence.

Residual risk: an equivalent deficit identity could be implicit in older design, perfect-sequence, or frame literature under different normalization.
