# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The unit ball is the explicit cut-square octagon. The arithmetic endpoint objective \((N_\lambda(x+y)+N_\lambda(x-y))/2\) is convex on the product unit ball, hence reaches its maximum at a vertex pair. Symmetry reduces the exact check to four pair classes, giving \(1\), \(2\lambda\), \(\max\{\lambda^{-1},2\lambda\}\), and \(\lambda^{-1}\). Therefore \(A_2=\max\{\lambda^{-1},2\lambda\}\). The scalar Heinz inequality bounds every \(H_\nu\) by \(A_2\), and the explicit pair \((1,a),(-a,1)\), where \(a=\lambda^{-1}-1\), has equal sum and difference norms at exactly that value. This proves equality for every \(0\le\nu\le1\), including the degenerate endpoint polygons.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary paper defines the same invariant and the same max-of-two-\(\ell_p\) family, but Example 4.10 displays formulas only for \(1\le q<p\le2\) and \(2\le q<p\le\infty\). The mixed regime \(q<2<p\), including \((p,q)=(\infty,1)\), is absent from both displayed cases. Targeted searches by exact norm, invariant, mixed-exponent aliases, and candidate formula found no equivalent or dominating result. Same-object records located in the literature index concern different invariants and do not imply the Heinz formula.

## Value
PASS. This supplies an exact mixed-endpoint branch in a family introduced specifically for exact Heinz-constant calculations. The result is uniform in the Heinz parameter and yields a natural V-shaped phase diagram \(\max\{\lambda^{-1},2\lambda\}\), with a unique minimum \(\sqrt2\) at the regular-octagon threshold \(\lambda=1/\sqrt2\). The transition has a geometric interpretation as a switch between the square-facet and diamond-facet constraints.

## Closest literature and limitations
Zuo--Huang (2022), Example 4.10, is the closest source. Its proof treats the two same-side exponent regimes and its Remark 4.13 calls the example general, but no formula for \(q<2<p\) is displayed or proved in the inspected text. The present result settles only the endpoint \((p,q)=(\infty,1)\), not the entire mixed regime. Search noncoverage is not an absolute novelty guarantee.
