# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Kegel--Teragaito Theorem 1.1 supplies the current-family premise: for every \(n\ge1\), \(K_n\) is a hyperbolic L-space knot with a unique strong inversion. Fiberedness follows for L-space knots, and hyperbolicity makes the fiber monodromy pseudo-Anosov. Baker--Kegel's exact factorization of \(\Delta_{K_n}\), together with the standard identification of the Alexander polynomial with the characteristic polynomial of homological monodromy for a fibered knot, reduces the order computation to roots of unity. The two quotient factors are squarefree and coprime, and each retains a primitive root of order \(8n+10\) or \(8n+4\), so diagonalizability gives the exact least common multiple rather than an upper bound. The gcd calculation gives the stated mod-\(3\) split. The Torelli conclusion is exactly the kernel condition for the homological action.

## Originality
PASS. The 2026 source establishes unique strong inversion and the hyperbolic L-space family but contains no monodromy or Alexander-polynomial discussion. The Baker--Kegel source gives the Alexander factorization and observes unit-circle roots, but targeted full-text checks found no monodromy or Torelli statement. Searches for the family name, census knot \(o9\_30634\), periodic homological monodromy, exact homological order, and first Torelli power did not locate an equivalent or stronger statement. The exact period requires an additional squarefreeness and primitive-root argument; roots of unity alone do not imply finite order in the presence of possible Jordan blocks.

## Value
PASS. The result gives an exact mapping-class invariant for every member of a newly symmetry-rigid hyperbolic L-space family: a pseudo-Anosov monodromy of infinite geometric order has explicitly periodic homological action, and the first Torelli power is known in closed form with a nontrivial arithmetic split. This addresses a recognized structural contrast between pseudo-Anosov dynamics and periodic homological action and is more informative than merely restating that the Alexander roots lie on the unit circle.

The result is intentionally limited to first homology and does not claim novelty for periodic homological pseudo-Anosov monodromies in general.
