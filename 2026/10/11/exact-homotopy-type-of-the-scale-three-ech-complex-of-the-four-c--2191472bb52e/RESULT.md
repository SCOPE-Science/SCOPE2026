# Exact homotopy type of the scale-three Čech complex of the four-cube
## Finding
Let \(Q_4\) be the graph on \(\{0,1\}^4\) in which two vertices are adjacent when they differ in exactly one coordinate. Let \(\mathcal N(Q_4,3)\) be the graph Čech complex whose balls are taken in the geometric realization of \(Q_4\) with the shortest-path metric. Then
\[
\mathcal N(Q_4,3)\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^{24}S^4.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
For an odd integral scale \(r\), the graph Čech complex is generated by the vertex sets \(N_{(r-1)/2}[v]\cup N_{(r-1)/2}[w]\) over graph edges \(vw\). Thus at \(r=3\), the complex in this result is generated by \(N_1[v]\cup N_1[w]\) over the 32 edges of \(Q_4\). Each generating simplex has eight vertices. The conclusion concerns this intrinsic graph Čech convention, not the Euclidean Čech complex of an embedding of the 16 cube vertices.

## Proof
The exact verifier reconstructs all 32 generating simplices and all their nonempty faces. It obtains 3,944 nonempty simplices with face vector
\[
(16,112,448,1016,1264,800,256,32).
\]

On this face poset, apply staged vertex-toggle matchings in the vertex order
\[
15,8,13,4,14,6,7,0,11,3,1,2,12,9,10,5.
\]
At a stage indexed by a vertex \(v\), every still-unmatched simplex \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) is paired with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both simplices are present and still unmatched. The verifier checks all 18,800 Hasse covers, verifies that the 1,959 selected pairs are genuine disjoint Hasse pairs, reverses precisely those matched edges, and topologically sorts the entire directed Hasse graph. All 3,944 faces are processed, proving that the matching is acyclic.

Exactly 26 critical simplices remain: one critical 0-simplex, one critical 3-simplex, and 24 critical 4-simplices. Hence Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW complex with one 0-cell, one 3-cell, 24 four-cells, and no other cells.

It remains to determine the attaching maps of the 4-cells. The verifier computes the signed Morse differential by exact integer gradient-path elimination, using the standard sorted-vertex orientation on every simplex. Each of the 24 coefficients from a critical 4-cell to the unique critical 3-cell is exactly zero. The 3-skeleton is therefore \(S^3\). An attaching map of a 4-cell is a map \(S^3\to S^3\); its homotopy class is determined by its degree, which is exactly its cellular boundary coefficient. Every degree is zero, so all 24 attaching maps are null-homotopic. Therefore the Morse CW complex, and hence the original Čech complex, is \(S^3\vee\bigvee^{24}S^4\).

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_q4_cech3.py` rebuilds the complex directly from Hamming distance, checks the complete Hasse diagram and matching acyclicity, identifies all critical cells, and recomputes the signed Morse boundary. Its `certificate.json` records the critical cells and the 24 zero boundary coefficients. As a separate chain-complex cross-check, the program computes simplicial homology over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\); in every case the Betti vector is
\[
(1,0,0,1,24,0,0,0).
\]
This agrees with the integral homology ranks reported in the prior literature.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla, and Singh describe the graph Čech convention, prove the generator formula used above, and give primary classification 55N31. Their table reports only the integral reduced homology of \(\mathcal N(Q_4,3)\): one copy of \(\mathbb Z\) in degree 3 and 24 copies in degree 4, with zero reduced homology elsewhere. The same discussion states that the remaining table entries, including this one, were not known there up to homotopy type. Thus the present statement determines the exact homotopy type rather than recomputing the published Betti numbers.

Nukala's 2024 dissertation studies related finite-metric Čech complexes and, in its future-work discussion, proposes wedge-of-\(S^3\)-and-\(S^4\) behavior for scale-three complexes built from consecutive Hamming layers. That discussion motivates the sphere dimensions but does not supply the exact homotopy type of \(\mathcal N(Q_4,3)\).

## Limitations
The proof is for the single natural pair \((Q_4,3)\). It does not establish the corresponding homotopy type for \(Q_n\) with \(n>4\), nor does it prove the broader layer-decomposition conjectures in the dissertation. The finite verification is exhaustive for this 16-vertex complex, but it is not an argument about an infinite family.
