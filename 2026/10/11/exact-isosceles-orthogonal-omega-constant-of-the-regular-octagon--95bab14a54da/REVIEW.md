# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The defining full text gives an exact reduction of \(\Omega(X)\) on every symmetric Minkowski plane to a one-variable function \(\Phi(t)\). The regular-octagonal norm satisfies the required axis symmetries. Exact facet comparisons give four pieces of \(\Phi\) on \(0\le t\le1\), with derivative signs \(+,+,-,-\). Hence the unique maximum on that interval is \(t=\sqrt2-1\). The identity \(\Phi(t)=\Phi(1/t)\) gives the only second maximizing ratio \(\sqrt2+1\). The explicit octagonal vertex pair attains \((3+2\sqrt2)/5\).

## Originality
PASS. The defining source was inspected through its definition, concrete examples, James comparison, and full symmetric-Minkowski reduction. It contains no occurrence of “octagon”, “octagonal”, or “regular”. Searches covered the fixed \(\Omega(X)\) notation, its equivalent one-variable formulation, the regular octagon, the radical value, and same-space neighboring invariants. A 2022 paper with similar title and notation was checked separately and its public description concerns a distinct parameterized \(\Omega_X(\alpha)\) invariant. No covering statement was found in published-finding corpus or the current own ledger.

## Value
PASS. The source specifically develops a symmetric-Minkowski reduction and computes square and hexagonal benchmarks. The regular octagon is the next canonical fourfold-symmetric polygonal test space, and the exact calculation reveals a nontrivial facet transition and sharp vertex ratios while giving a reusable benchmark for later regular-polygon work.

## Closest literature and limitations
Liu, Yang, Fu and Li introduced \(\Omega(X)\) in arXiv:2111.08392, first posted 16 November 2021 with primary MSC 46B20. Their paper computes the square value \(8/5\), a classical hexagonal value \(49/40\), and proves the symmetric-plane reduction used here, but not the octagonal evaluation. The present result does not extend to all regular polygons and does not claim absolute bibliographic uniqueness.
