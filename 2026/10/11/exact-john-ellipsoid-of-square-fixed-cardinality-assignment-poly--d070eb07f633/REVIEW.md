# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is checked for all integers \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le k<n\) with the intrinsic Frobenius normalization and correct affine-hull dimension \(n^2-1\). The partial-permutation polytope equals the stated H-representation by Gill--Linusson, Theorem 3, and Torres, Section 3. The orthogonal \(U\) and \(W\) projection norms give the complete necessary and sufficient ellipsoid support inequalities. Strict concavity of the log-determinant establishes the explicit two-regime optimizer. A separate John first-/second-moment contact certificate, with nonnegative row/column weights and strictly positive coordinate weights, proves unrestricted global optimality and uniqueness, even against nonsymmetric or off-center ellipsoids. Exact rational projection/weight computations reproduce all algebraic boundary and KKT identities for thousands of parameters. Finite testing is not used in place of the proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual literature risk

The closest primary papers are Gill--Linusson (2009, exact H-representation/faces/diameter) and Torres (2023, H-representation and normality). Neither gives maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid semiaxes or their determinant-optimized anisotropy. Generic John theory gives a certificate criterion, not these numerical formulas. The known intrinsic Euclidean inradius and its square-root facet transition are explicitly excluded from the claim of novelty; they do not imply the non-round John ellipsoid or its exact volume. Published geometric-search records concerning other ellipsoids and matching complexes have different objects/invariants. Bibliographic priority is not independently established; an equivalent computation under matrix-section or optimal-design terminology is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

A fixed-cardinality assignment polytope interpolates geometrically between a simplex and the Birkhoff-polytope endpoint. Its exact all-parameter John ellipsoid is a natural Euclidean metric invariant; the result identifies the full spectrum and contact geometry in every admissible dimension. The anisotropic branch yields an explicit strict, dimension-dependent determinant/volume gain that is inaccessible from the inradius alone. This is an infinite-family classification with a motivated sharp transition, not a numerical benchmark or an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope of the conclusion

The result excludes perfect assignments \(k=n\) and rectangular dimensions. Its proof depends on the cited exact H-representation. The independently reproduced finite checks support, but do not establish, its universal quantifiers. Literature comparison does not certify first discovery.
