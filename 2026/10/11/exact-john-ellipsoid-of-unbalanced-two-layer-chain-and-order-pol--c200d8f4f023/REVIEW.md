# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim defines all parameters and confines the conclusion to unequal positive integers. The two governing algebraic relations imply the exact diagonal and cross covariance entries. Positivity of the Schur complement proves the ellipsoid exists; exact support equalities show containment and contact against all \(a+b+ab\) facets. Strictly positive facet weights yield zero first moment and inverse covariance as second moment, so John's characterization proves global volume optimality and uniqueness, with no tacit symmetry assumption on competitors. The four distinct spectral subspaces (allowing zero multiplicities) and determinant give the displayed volume. Numerical and rational checks provide additional, non-exhaustive corroboration.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Ahmad–Fourier–Joswig, arXiv:2309.01626, treat maximal-ranked-poset chain/order polytopes and compare their face vectors, whereas Mori, arXiv:2404.00263, treats triangular face counts. Their inspected full texts do not assert the metric ellipsoid formula or John weights. General John-ellipsoid theory establishes the optimality criterion but not its evaluated radical parameters for this family. The balanced subfamily was already treated; it is excluded, and the unbalanced two-parameter case is not a mere specialization of balanced block symmetry. Search comparisons included related convex-geometric names and recorded claims, but the possibility of an equivalent result under a free-sum or optimal-design terminology remains.

**Value — PASS.** Rank sizes in a two-layer poset are independent natural structural parameters; in the unbalanced setting the shape has four spectral types rather than the symmetric balanced simplification. A complete center, spectrum, determinant, and all-facet contact classification for this entire nontrivial infinite family provides a useful exact model for ellipsoid computations on chain polytopes, rather than a small isolated numerical example. There is no claim of a new general John's theorem or a result for arbitrary posets.

**Closest sources and limits.** Ahmad–Fourier–Joswig (arXiv:2309.01626) and Mori (arXiv:2404.00263) are primary object-level sources; Gruber–Schuster (arXiv:1207.7246) describes the general contact criterion. The proof works uniformly for \(a,b\ge1\) with \(a\ne b\), including one-element ranks, but says nothing about omitted comparabilities or higher-ranked posets. The verifier tests finitely many instances and is not an infinite proof. Literature searches cannot establish absolute priority, and no independent audit has been carried out.
