# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The exact type-\(D_n\) orbit model was reconstructed from the published fundamental weights and Weyl group. The standard parity-polytope halfspace system gives two facet orbits. Their distances from the origin are \(1/2\) and \((n-2)/(2\sqrt n)\), producing the exact \(n=4\) tangency transition. Opposite coordinate facets give a global inradius upper bound and force the incenter uniquely. Every vertex has norm \(\sqrt n/2\), and symmetry forces the unique circumcenter to the origin. Finally, uniqueness of John and Löwner ellipsoids makes them invariant under the type-\(D_n\) Weyl group; the invariant quadratic forms are scalar, so the extremal ellipsoids are exactly the inball and circumball.

## Value
PASS. The two spin weight polytopes are canonical fundamental-weight objects and also classical parity/demihypercube polytopes. The theorem gives their complete extremal Euclidean ellipsoid geometry:
\[
E_J=\frac12B_2^n,
\qquad
E_L=\frac{\sqrt n}{2}B_2^n,
\]
with unique centers and explicit contact sets. The inball changes from touching every facet in dimension four to touching only the \(2n\) coordinate facets in all higher dimensions, despite exponentially many parity-simplex facets. For even dimension the family attains the sharp \(\sqrt n\) symmetric John containment factor.

A residual risk remains for a classical uniform-polytope or coding-theory table under different normalization. The theorem is specific to the standard spin-weight normalization and does not treat other type-\(D\) fundamental weights.
