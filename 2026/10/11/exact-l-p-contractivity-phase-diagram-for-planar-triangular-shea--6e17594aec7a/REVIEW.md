# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The \(\ell_p^2\) norming functional reduces the numerical abscissa exactly to a scalar supremum. Its sign is controlled by the exact minimum of \(a/r+cr^{p-1}\), and differentiating \(\log\beta_p\) gives the unique exponent maximum. The \(p=1\) endpoint agrees with the standard column-sum formula. Constant functions identify the multiplication-operator norm with the matrix norm. The logarithmic-norm derivative at zero proves optimality of the prefactor-one exponent. No finite experiment is used as proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The focal full text treats one fixed triangular matrix only at \(p=1\) and \(p=2\). The present theorem gives the complete finite-\(p\) crossover for that example and the full three-parameter threshold, including a two-crossing reentrant regime. Searches covered numerical-abscissa, logarithmic-norm, matrix-measure, dissipativity, triangular-shear, and reentrant-contractivity formulations. The closest indexed shear result concerns a different compact-perturbation property, while the closest transient result concerns Gauss--Seidel amplification. Classical logarithmic-norm sources provide the framework but not this phase classification. The main residual risk is an older equivalent two-dimensional calculation under different terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The recent source uses the triangular \(2\times2\) example to show that Banach-space geometry can reverse the semigroup conclusion between \(p=1\) and \(p=2\). Locating the exact crossover, identifying the coupling threshold for every \(p\), and revealing a reentrant contractivity window answer the natural structural question left by that comparison. This is a complete classification of the minimal nonnormal stable model rather than an isolated numerical slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is arXiv:2608.19913v1 itself and the classical logarithmic-norm survey DOI:10.1007/s10543-006-0069-9. The result is limited to constant planar triangular shears, induced \(\ell_p\) norms, and prefactor-one semigroup bounds.
