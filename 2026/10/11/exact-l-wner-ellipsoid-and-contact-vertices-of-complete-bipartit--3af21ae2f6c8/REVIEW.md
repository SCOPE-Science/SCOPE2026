# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The edge-space projection identity proves the stated enclosing ellipsoid and the equality criterion without approximations. For sign matrices, the vanishing alternating two-by-two sums are equivalent to incidence-row-space membership and force the row-monochromatic/column-monochromatic dichotomy, giving the exact contact set and cardinality. Positive rational weights supported only at these contacts reproduce the proposed shape matrix divided by the ambient dimension; averaging any competitor's ellipsoid inequality and applying matrix AM--GM proves global optimality and uniqueness. All parities and boundary dimensions \(p,q\ge2\) are covered. The independent standalone exact-rational checker verifies selected finite instances; no infinite claim relies on the numerical tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The full-text comparison identified graphical-zonotope combinatorics and deformation theory in Padrol--Pilaud--Poullot, the explicit complete-bipartite shuffle in Chapoton--Pilaud, general zonotope enclosing-ellipsoid conversion algorithms in Gaßmann--Althoff, and general group-orbit ellipsoid principles in Barvinok--Blekherman. The matched objects and the strongest relevant implications were compared, not just the titles: none of the inspected proofs gives the concrete complete-bipartite optimality certificate, its all-parameter shape, and all boundary contacts. Searches for alternative projected-hypercube and covariance-ellipsoid descriptions returned no decisive equivalent. This absence does not prove worldwide novelty: an equivalent specialized treatment may exist under unfamiliar terminology or in literature not inspected.

## Value — PASS

The complete-bipartite graphical zonotope is a standard nontrivial family of generalized permutohedra. A generic generator-covariance ellipsoid always encloses a zonotope, but need not be minimum-volume; identifying a full infinite family where it is *exactly optimal* resolves a motivated metric-affine question. The explicit positive contact distribution explains *why* optimization is exact, while the precise exponential contact count and semiaxis/volume formulas describe geometry beyond mere correctness of a bound. The conclusion applies simultaneously to all two-part sizes, including even/odd parity combinations.
