# Review of the sharp metric-projection Lipschitz profile

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete proof starts from the unique scalar minimizer, establishes monotonicity and the global \(n^{1/p}\) bound, then treats the two-dimensional and Hilbert cases separately. In the subquadratic regime, a vanishing-residual perturbation saturates the universal bound. In the superquadratic regime, the implicit derivative has weights \(w_i\le1/2\), yielding the global \((n/2)^{1/p}\) bound, which a two-nonzero-residual configuration attains infinitesimally. Segment integration covers inputs beyond the differentiability locus, and each boundary case is stated.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 1997 primary paper proves uniform Lipschitz existence for finite-dimensional \(\ell_p\) best approximation but not this sharp operator norm. In the complete 2017 paper, Theorem 2 explicitly treats the line of constants yet gives only \(K(I_n)\le 2^{1/q}(n-1)^{1/p}+1\) and an asymptotic comparison. The 1983 exact \(\ell_p^2\) maximum metric-projection bound is a different problem. The 1998 polyhedral-set follow-up was accessible at abstract level only, a documented remaining bibliographic risk. Public semantic and specialized record searches included the p-mean and diagonal-projection aliases; absence of a database hit is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** This is a natural complete sharp constant for the canonical finite-dimensional metric best approximation problem; it improves nonsharp classical estimates in every non-Hilbert range and identifies a genuine jump at exponent \(p=2\) for all \(n\ge3\). The profile is uniform in both dimensions and exponents, with explicitly justified extremal limiting configurations.

## Scientific limitations and bibliographic residuals
The normed spaces and scalars are real, finite-dimensional, and strictly convex \(1<p<\infty\). The map projects onto the all-ones line, not arbitrary targets. Supremum sharpness is obtained from infinitesimal perturbations, not asserted from separated-pair attainment. Full text of the Finzel--Li 1998 paper was inaccessible in checked routes. A distinct terminology in unindexed literature remains a residual originality possibility.
