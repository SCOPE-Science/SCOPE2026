# Review of the characteristic-three parabolic local sixth-moment result

## Correctness — PASS

The proof fixes the normalization \(\mathcal J_q=M/(qS^3)\) and establishes the fiber count from the characteristic-three identity \(w=s^2+sv-u^2\). It checks both off-diagonal pair-correlation multiplicities and the weighted second-order pair term by explicit finite-field bijections. The resulting real and imaginary Taylor coefficients and their two-dimensional scalar kernel follow algebraically for every \(q=3^n\), \(n\ge1\). The standalone checker independently enumerates the fibers in field orders 3, 9, 27, and 81, but this finite enumeration is not treated as an infinite proof. The local stability conclusion is strictly local and follows from positive transverse Hessian on a finite-dimensional quotient.

## Originality — PASS, with qualification

The closest primary source, González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva, arXiv:2405.16647, Theorem 1.3, assumes characteristic strictly greater than three and establishes global maximality, not the characteristic-three second variation. The finite-field moment-curve papers arXiv:2508.08377 and arXiv:2609.29882 study a degree-\(d\) endpoint \(L^{2d}\): their degree-two instance is not the sixth moment considered here. Related sharp Hessians for two-dimensional quadratic surfaces concern fourth moments, rather than the one-dimensional characteristic-three triple sum. Mathematical comparisons include aliases as parabolic Strichartz Hessian, sixth-moment curvature, and threefold Fourier convolution; no equivalent statement was identified in the indexed records inspected. Remaining risks are equivalent unpublished or unindexed calculations and possible literature phrased as a general finite-group polynomial-extension stability theorem.

## Value — PASS

The primary sharp parabola theorem explicitly excludes characteristic three. Here the characteristic-three collision fibers have exceptional \(s=0\) strata, and a full correlation count is necessary to identify the two distinct curvature bands. The result supplies a sharp local quantitative stability invariant, exhibits phase perturbations as the least-stable directions, and proves strict local maximality without pretending to settle the omitted global extension problem. This is a structurally motivated boundary analysis, not a generic numerical Taylor coefficient.

## Limits of verification

The checker covers four finite fields, while the proof covers all powers of three. No global sixth-moment inequality, global equality classification, or uniform-in-field remainder is claimed. An independently verified originality assessment has not been performed.
