# Review of Exact logarithmic scaling profile for sharp Schwarz–Bohr radii

## Correctness

PASS. Substituting
\[
R=e^{-s/k}
\]
into the source's exact sharp-root equation gives
\[
p\tanh\!\left(\frac{(m/k)s}{2}\right)
=
\frac{2}{e^{2s}-1}
\]
with no approximation. The two sides have opposite strict monotonicity, proving the profile is well defined.

The large-ratio limit follows by monotone passage to \(\tanh=1\). In the small-ratio limit, the defining equation first forces \(s\to\infty\), then forces \(\lambda s\to0\); this justifies both asymptotic replacements before Lambert-\(W\) inversion. The balanced-ray quadratic and the simultaneous \(k(1-R)\) limit were independently rederived and sanity-checked numerically.

## Originality

PASS. The source states the radius as a unique sharp root and supplies numerical examples, but does not reduce the family to a ratio profile or state any asymptotic analysis of that profile.

The closest recent Bohr--Rogosinski paper treats a different radius equation and gives one-parameter limits. It does not imply the exact logarithmic collapse, simultaneous \(m/k\) scaling, Lambert-\(W\) regime, or the balanced-ray formula for the source radius.

Searches under the exact source identifier and root, ratio scaling, logarithmic coordinates, and Lambert-\(W\) terminology did not locate an equivalent statement.

## Value

PASS. The source's radius is a principal sharp constant in both its Banach-space theorem and its JB-star-triple extension. The accepted result removes a redundant integer parameter after the natural logarithmic normalization and converts a table of roots into a complete one-variable phase profile.

The two endpoint laws describe how the sharp radius changes when the two Schwarz-map orders become strongly unbalanced, while the balanced ray gives an infinite exact closed-form family. This is structural information about the sharp constant rather than a routine numerical refinement.
