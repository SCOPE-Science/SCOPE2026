# Verification

The theorem is analytic. For an invariant probability measure supported in a compact subset of the positive quadrant, the generator identity may be applied to \(\log v\) and \(v\). These yield respectively
\[
m\bar v=q\int\frac{u}{1+cu}\,d\mu
\]
and
\[
m\int v^2\,d\mu=\int v\frac{qu}{1+cu}\,d\mu.
\]
The second equation combined with the first is exactly the covariance identity in the finding.

The only nontrivial algebraic transformation is generated by
\[
u-b=(1+cb)\frac{u-b}{1+cu}+c\frac{(u-b)^2}{1+cu},
\]
with \(b=\bar u\). The packaged `verify.py` checks this identity symbolically and checks the rearrangement producing the defect coefficient. Running the packaged file must print `VERIFY_OK`.

The equality case is not inferred from numerics. The defect vanishes exactly when \(u\) is constant almost surely. For fixed positive \(u\), the prey per-capita drift is strictly decreasing in \(v\), so an invariant measure concentrated on one vertical line can be supported at only one positive equilibrium point.

The variance inequality follows from the exact covariance identity, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the global derivative bound
\[
0<\frac{d}{du}\left(\frac{qu}{1+cu}\right)=\frac{q}{(1+cu)^2}\le q.
\]
No numerical orbit integration, finite enumeration, or asymptotic approximation is used as proof.

Limits: the verification does not prove existence or uniqueness of a limit cycle, does not determine its period or amplitude, and does not cover the stochastic extension.
