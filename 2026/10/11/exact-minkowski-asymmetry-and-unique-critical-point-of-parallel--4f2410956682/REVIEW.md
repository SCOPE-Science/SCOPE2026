# Same-model mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

All strictly convex six-edge polygons with parallel opposite sides admit the proved affine normal form, with positive parameters \(a,b,c,a+t,b-t,c+t\). The inclusion \(-(P-o)\subseteq\lambda(P-o)\) is equivalent to exactly six facet-support inequalities; summing their coordinate intervals supplies the two necessary and sufficient bounds \(\lambda S\ge S+t\) and \(\lambda(S+t)\ge S\). Their active triples force the unique optimizing center, and parameter positivity proves the sharp range. Both 3,424 rational admissible cases and 375 independent exact-rational LP optima agree. Infinite correctness rests on the displayed interval proof, not finite testing. Critical boundary cases \(t=0\) and \(t\to b^-\) were handled.

## Originality — PASS, residual literature risk

The most directly overlapping objects are the polygons studied in Craizer--Teixeira--da Silva (2013) and Konicer et al. (arXiv:2606.22612v1). The latter's accessible full text establishes affine-equidistant reconstruction and area inequalities (Theorems 4.4 and 4.7), not the homothetic-containment minimizer of this result. The former treats the area evolute and center-symmetry set, not the present one-ratio Minkowski factor and explicit unique critical point. Grünbaum (1964) optimizes a different three-line area-partition measure. Nearby published results on regular polygons address p-/log-Minkowski measures, and their symmetric examples cannot determine asymmetric members of this family. The 2026 affine-reflection-transform paper's distinct area-energy asymmetry was accessible only at abstract level; more literature could contain an equivalent formula. Failed retrieval alone is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first six-edge class of noncentrally symmetric parallel-opposite convex polygons admits a complete and affine-invariant classification of its Minkowski asymmetry, critical center and exact attainable range. The center's uniqueness is a structural rigidity statement, and the four positive shape degrees collapse to one positive edge-balance ratio. This addresses a natural distance-from-central-symmetry invariant in the very polygon class used in current discrete affine geometry; it is not a chosen numeric subcase, computer enumeration or a repetition of the isoperimetric inequalities.
