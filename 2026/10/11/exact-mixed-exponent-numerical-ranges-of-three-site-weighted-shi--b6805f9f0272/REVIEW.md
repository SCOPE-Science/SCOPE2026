# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized \(\ell_p\)-duality functional gives a two-term scalar expression. Phase alignment is exact on a three-vertex path. For two nonzero weights, every maximizer lies in the strictly positive octant: first- or third-coordinate perturbations improve any positive-valued boundary point at orders \(\varepsilon^{p-1}\) or \(\varepsilon\), both dominating the order-\(\varepsilon^p\) normalization loss. The three Lagrange equations give one strictly decreasing mixed-exponent root equation, proving uniqueness of both the value and maximizing moduli. Compactness, circular symmetry, and connectedness establish the full closed disk. The zero-weight and Hilbert cases are checked separately. The fixed-exponent asymmetry certificate is an exact rational sign test at \(65/4\), not a rounded comparison.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Müller–Tomilov's first public version (2026-06-21, arXiv:2606.22693v1) supplies arbitrary \(2\times2\) spatial-range parametrization (Proposition 2.1) and truncated-shift polynomial estimates (Proposition 4.16), but not the two-weight three-site mixed-exponent radius, its maximizing-vector classification, or its non-Hilbert orientation witness. Chien–Nakazato (2010) has a superficially similar title but the full-text definition is a *Hilbert-space* \(q\)-numerical range with a prescribed inner product between two unit vectors, not the present Banach \(\ell_p\) spatial range. Wang–Wu's Hilbert-space weighted-shift results cover the \(p=2\) special case but not general \(p\). Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches found no covering statement; unknown unindexed earlier computations remain a genuine risk. Neither absent search hits nor a domain label alone is treated as proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Three coordinates constitute the first shift length with two independently orderable weights, immediately beyond the 2026 paper's \(2\times2\) analysis. The result gives a complete exact two-parameter invariant for all \(1<p<\infty\), exhibits a non-Hilbert weight-order effect absent from the classical Hilbert formula, and shows the correct conjugate-exponent duality. The mixed-exponent modular equation and extremal-vector rigidity provide structural information for investigating longer shifts, without asserting a longer-shift theorem.

The independent audit has not been performed; the checked certificate supplements a self-contained analytic proof. The highest remaining scientific risk is a pre-existing equivalent formula in older or unindexed non-Hilbert numerical-range literature. The result makes no claim about all matrices, infinite shifts, or endpoints \(p=1,\infty\).

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
