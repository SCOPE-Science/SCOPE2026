# Exact mixed shadow-area–perimeter law and sharp positive-covariance bound for four-dimensional orthotopes

## Finding
Let \(Q=\prod_{i=1}^{4}[-a_i/2,a_i/2]\subset\mathbb R^4\), where all \(a_i>0\). Choose a two-dimensional linear subspace \(E\) according to normalized rotation-invariant measure on \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)\), and let \(A=A_Q(E)\), \(P=P_Q(E)\) be the ordinary Euclidean area and perimeter of the same orthogonal planar shadow. Put
\[
e_1=\sum_i a_i,\qquad e_2=\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j,\qquad e_3=\sum_{i<j<k}a_i a_j a_k.
\]
Then the exact mixed moment and covariance are
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[AP]=\frac12e_1e_2+\frac38(\pi-4)e_3,}
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)=\frac{e_1e_2}{18}-\frac38(4-\pi)e_3.}
\]
In particular, every such orthotope satisfies the sharp scale-invariant bound
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)\ge\frac{9\pi-28}{24}e_3>0,}
\]
with equality in the non-strict inequality exactly for cubes (all side lengths equal). For the unit four-cube,
\[
\mathbb EA=2,\quad \mathbb EP=\frac{16}{3},\quad
\boxed{\mathbb E[AP]=6+\frac{3\pi}{2}=10.71238898038469\ldots,}\quad
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)=\frac{3\pi}{2}-\frac{14}{3}=0.04572231371802\ldots.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Only finite positive Euclidean side lengths and a Haar-uniform *two-dimensional* projection plane in ambient dimension four are assumed. Both statistics are measured on the *same* shadow, not independently rotated shadows. Degenerate lower-dimensional boxes, biased directional sampling, projections into other dimensions, and nonorthogonal parallelotopes are outside the assertion. There are no unproved asymptotic hypotheses.

## Proof
**Step 1: zonotope formula.** Take any orthonormal ordered basis \(x,y\in\mathbb R^4\) for \(E\). Set \(p_{ij}=x_i y_j-x_j y_i\) for \(i<j\) and \(r_k=(x_k^2+y_k^2)^{1/2}\). The projected orthotope is the planar zonotope generated by \(a_i(x_i,y_i)\). The elementary planar zonotope formulas are
\[
A=\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j|p_{ij}|,\qquad P=2\sum_k a_k r_k.
\]
The formulas remain invariant under a change of orthonormal basis in the projection plane.

**Step 2: incidence with a shared index.** Fix \(k\). By the Cauchy–Binet identity, \(\sum_{j\ne k}p_{kj}^2=r_k^2\), with \(p_{kj}=-p_{jk}\) when \(j<k\). Under Haar measure, the first row of an orthogonal frame has the distribution of the first two coordinates of a uniform point of \(S^3\). Therefore \(r_k^2\) has the beta law \(\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)\), that is, is uniform on \([0,1]\). The three-dimensional vector \((p_{kj})_{j\ne k}\) is rotationally isotropic under transformations fixing the \(k\)-th ambient coordinate; conditional on its norm \(r_k\), its direction is uniform on \(S^2\). The absolute value of a coordinate of a uniform point of \(S^2\) has mean \(1/2\). Hence, whenever \(k\in\{i,j\}\),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[|p_{ij}|r_k]=\mathbb E[r_k^2]\cdot\frac12=\frac14.}
\]
The same calculation gives \(\mathbb E[r_k]=2/3\) and \(\mathbb E|p_{ij}|=1/3\).

**Step 3: incidence with a disjoint index.** By symmetry it suffices to compute \(\mathbb E[|p_{34}|r_1]\). Give the plane a uniformly random orientation and use the Hodge-star decomposition of the unit simple bivector \(x\wedge y\). Explicitly, there exist two **independent uniform** random vectors \(U,V\in S^2\) for which
\[
(p_{12},p_{13},p_{14})=\frac{U+V}{2},\qquad
(p_{34},-p_{24},p_{23})=\frac{U-V}{2}.
\]
For completeness, the self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of every unit simple bivector have equal norm \(1/\sqrt2\). Their normalized coordinate vectors give a bijection from the oriented Grassmannian \(\operatorname{Gr}^{+}(2,4)\) to \(S^2\times S^2\): the two unit-vector conditions are exactly the unit-norm and Plücker decomposability equations. The product of normalized spherical measures is invariant under rotations of \(\mathbb R^4\) (rotations preserve the two Hodge eigenspaces), and therefore equals the invariant probability measure on the homogeneous oriented Grassmannian. This proves the stated independence, not merely equality of separate marginals.

Write \(t=U\cdot V\). Since \(U,V\) are independent and uniform, \(t\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\). Except for a null set, the directions of \(U+V\) and \(U-V\) are orthogonal; conditional on \(t\), they form a uniformly rotated orthogonal pair. Their respective lengths divided by two are \(\sqrt{(1+t)/2}\) and \(\sqrt{(1-t)/2}\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[|p_{34}|r_1]
&=\mathbb E\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-t^2}}2\right]\,\mathbb E|Z_1|\\
&=\left(\frac14\int_{-1}^1\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt\right)\frac12
=\boxed{\frac\pi{16}},
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(Z\in S^2\) is uniform. Rotational symmetry establishes the same number for every \(k\notin\{i,j\}\).

**Step 4: mixed moment and covariance.** Multiply the formulas in Step 1, and partition their terms according to whether \(k\) belongs to \(\{i,j\}\). Steps 2–3 yield
\[
\mathbb E[AP]
=\frac12\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j(a_i+a_j)
+\frac\pi8\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}a_k.
\]
Every distinct triple appears three times in the second sum. Also
\(\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j(a_i+a_j)=e_1e_2-3e_3\). Thus
\[\mathbb E[AP]=\frac12e_1e_2+\frac38(\pi-4)e_3.\]
The first moments from Step 2 are \(\mathbb EA=e_2/3\) and \(\mathbb EP=4e_1/3\). Their product subtraction gives the claimed covariance identity.

**Step 5: optimal positive bound.** The following exact sum-of-squares identity is valid for all four real side lengths:
\[
e_1e_2-6e_3
=\frac12\sum_{i<j<k}\left[
 a_i(a_j-a_k)^2+a_j(a_i-a_k)^2+a_k(a_i-a_j)^2\right].
\]
For positive sides its right side is nonnegative, and vanishes only when every side length agrees. Substitution into the covariance gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A,P)
=\frac{9\pi-28}{24}e_3+\frac{e_1e_2-6e_3}{18}
\ge\frac{9\pi-28}{24}e_3.
\]
Since \(\pi>28/9\), the last expression is positive. Every scaled cube attains the bound, proving its optimality. The unit-cube specialization follows upon inserting \(e_1=4\), \(e_2=6\), \(e_3=4\).

## Verification
The standalone reproducibility script `verify.py` samples random Haar two-planes using Gram–Schmidt on two independent four-dimensional standard Gaussian vectors, not the Hodge parameterization used in the critical disjoint-index proof. It compares the incidence values \(1/4\) and \(\pi/16\), and the derived mixed moment for three orthotopes. All 180,000 finite numerical trials completed successfully (`VERIFY_OK`). The script is supplementary: its approximate checks do not establish the exact integrations or the global inequality. Each analytic identity is proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Finch's 2012 study *Planar Projections and Second Intrinsic Volume* reports the four-cube area–perimeter mixed moment approximately as \(10.7\ldots\) (§4.2), not as a closed form. The present claim evaluates that particular mixed statistic and, crucially, gives a single exact identity and sharp positive-covariance inequality valid for every four-dimensional orthotope. Paouris, Pivovarov and Zinn's 2012 work *A central limit theorem for projections of the cube* proves high-dimensional projected-volume asymptotics and records the general zonotope formula; it does not determine this fixed-four-dimensional area–perimeter mixed moment or its sign classification. Formulae for the separate area and perimeter moments cannot alone determine their mixed moment. A literature search cannot rule out all alternative equivalent earlier derivations.

## Limitations
This is a finite-four-dimensional Euclidean result for axis-aligned orthotopes. It does not classify other convex bodies, arbitrary parallelotopes, or projections to a different dimensional subspace. It requires identical sampled planes for area and perimeter. The formula and inequality have analytic proofs, but no independent external mathematical audit has been performed; hidden historical equivalent statements remain possible.
