# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof completely classifies the Birkhoff support geometry up to the regular octagon's dihedral symmetry. Facet-endpoint supports give the exact candidate denominator envelope and the fixed pair \(x=(-(\sqrt2-1),1)\), \(y=(1,0)\) attains it for every \(\lambda\). Interior support functionals expose one vertex; three independent inequalities give exactly the same envelope. The resulting three scale intervals are then optimized analytically, and the logarithmic derivatives exclude every interior maximum. The bundled checker independently reconstructs these cases and the two explicit maximizing scales.

## Originality
PASS. The full 2021 defining paper was inspected at the invariant definition, its regular-hexagon calculation, and its final \(\ell_p\) estimates; targeted full-text searches found no octagonal calculation. A 2024 paper with the potentially confusable title “Generalized rectangular constant in Banach spaces” was also obtained and read; it recalls \(\mu_p\) only as background and introduces different parameter-\(a\) constants. published-finding corpus searches covered exact invariant names, object aliases, formula fragments, and the stronger denominator formulation. The own ledger contains only the ordinary \(p=1\) regular-octagon endpoint, which does not imply the all-exponent result.

## Value
PASS. The defining paper singles out a regular hexagon as the canonical polygonal example for \(\mu_p\). The regular octagon is the next natural highly symmetric benchmark, and its answer is qualitatively richer: the full profile is the maximum of two branches arising from two distinct Birkhoff scales. The stronger exact denominator law is reusable and explains both the known \(p=1\) endpoint and the limiting value as \(p\to\infty\).

## Closest literature and limitations
Baronti, Casini and Papini give the definition, structural theory, the regular-hexagon law \(2^{1/p}\), and \(\ell_p\)-space estimates. Xie, Fu and Li later study different constants \(\mu(X,a)\) and \(\mu'(X,a)\). The closest public exact generalized-rectangular computation found is \(\mu_4\) on \(\ell_4^2\), a different space. The theorem here is restricted to the Euclidean regular octagonal norm and does not classify arbitrary polygonal norms or give a closed elementary form for the branch-crossing exponent.
