# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For each point evaluation, positivity and unitality make \(P^*\delta_t\) a probability measure. Splitting off its atom at \(t\) gives the exact total-variation norm of \((P-M_f)^*\delta_t\). The focal proof supplies both \(d(t)\ge\alpha\) everywhere and one point with \(d(t)=\alpha\), which makes the distance and sharpness arguments exact. The best-approximant inequalities and scalar profile follow algebraically from the same formula. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. Statement-level searches were made for the full multiplication-operator distance formula, scalar perturbation profile, best central/multiplication approximation, and complementary projection norm. The focal full text was inspected around Theorem 2.1 and Lemmas 2.9–2.11; it proves the reciprocal-integrality theorem and the atomic representation used here, but does not state the metric projection formula. Wickstead's earlier paper was inspected around Question 7.3 and its norm discussion; it motivates the diagonal problem but does not cover this norm profile. The closest indexed results concern different operator ideals, weighted composition operators, or unrelated projection constants. Residual risk remains that an older general Markov-kernel or positive-projection lemma gives an equivalent formula under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The focal theorem identifies an order-theoretic diagonal parameter. The new statement shows that the same parameter is exactly recoverable as a metric invariant: \(1-\alpha\) is the operator-norm distance to all multiplication operators. It also determines the whole set of best multiplication approximants and the complete scalar-axis norm profile, rather than extracting only one routine norm value. This supplies a structural bridge between the order diagonal and the metric geometry of the projection.

## Closest literature and limitations
The result uses the recent constant-diagonal structure theorem and does not extend its scope to \(\alpha=0\). The 2017 Wickstead paper and the 2026 focal paper are the closest inspected primary sources. Indexed comparison results on multiplier distances and projection constants concern different operator classes and do not imply the claimed \(C(K)\) formula. The literature search cannot exclude an unindexed folklore formulation.
