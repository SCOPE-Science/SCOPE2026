# Review of the exact two-link spatial numerical radius

## Correctness
PASS. The norming-functional formula holds for real and complex \(\ell_p^3\). Positivity and compactness provide a maximum, all boundary faces are strictly suboptimal, and Lagrange stationarity leads to one strictly increasing scalar equation. Its unique root establishes both the sharp radius and all magnitude extremizers. Equality in the two-term triangle inequality gives the stated real/complex phase classification. The independent diagnostic program verifies representative cases and cross-exponent adjoint consistency but is not the proof.

## Originality
PASS subject to bounded bibliographic risk. The openly available Martín–Merí–Popov primary preprint gives the numerical radius of a **single-link** rank-one shift and positive-operator lower bounds, neither of which fixes a three-coordinate two-link value. The Undrakh–Nakazato–Vandanjav–Chien paper analyzes weighted shifts via Hermitian parts on \(\ell_2\); its value at \(p=2\) is already recognized here. Chang–Wang's accessible introduction and abstract also explicitly use complex Hilbert-space numerical range. Available investigations of spatial numerical ranges in Hardy and semi-inner-product spaces do not give the same operator/parameter claim. The full Chang–Wang text was inaccessible, and unindexed Banach-space formulas remain a residual possibility; no claim of exhaustive bibliographic uniqueness is made.

## Value
PASS. The two-link nilpotent matrix is the first natural weighted shift in which the two adjacent numerical-radius terms interact. The theorem gives an exact nonlinear radius, a monotone one-variable evaluation procedure, and rigidity of every extremal vector for all weights and exponents, rather than recomputing the known Hilbert or rank-one special cases. These are useful finite-dimensional test objects for spatial numerical-range estimates and positive-operator norm comparisons.

## Prior-work and verification limits
Primary comparisons: arXiv:1011.4785, DOI 10.13001/1081-3810.2821, DOI 10.1016/j.jmaa.2012.04.039, DOI 10.1155/2011/812680, DOI 10.1155/2012/846396. Finite arithmetic checks support but do not replace the proof. The claim makes no assertion about higher-dimensional chains, other matrices, or comprehensive priority.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
