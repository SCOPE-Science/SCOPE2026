# Exact \(\Omega\)-profile of the regular dodecagonal Banach plane
## Finding
Let \(X_{12}\) be the real Banach plane whose closed unit ball is the Euclidean regular dodecagon with vertices
\[
v_k=
\left(
\cos\frac{k\pi}{6},
\sin\frac{k\pi}{6}
\right),
\qquad
0\le k<12.
\]
Let
\[
R(s,t)=(-t,s).
\]
For unit vectors \(x,y\), write
\[
x\perp_I y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\|x+y\|=\|x-y\|.
\]
For the Liu--Yang--Fu--Li constant
\[
\Omega(X)
=
\sup_{\substack{x,y\in S_X\\x\perp_I y}}
\frac{\|x+2y\|^2+\|2x+y\|^2}{5\|x+y\|^2},
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\Omega(X_{12})=\frac{14}{5}-\sqrt3
}.
\tag{1}
\]

More precisely, every admissible unit pair has
\[
y=Rx
\quad\text{or}\quad
y=-Rx.
\tag{2}
\]
After dihedral reduction to
\[
x(a)=(1-a)v_0+av_1,
\qquad
0\le a\le1,
\tag{3}
\]
the reduced objective is symmetric under
\[
a\longmapsto1-a,
\]
has one active-facet transition on
\[
0\le a\le\frac12,
\]
and is strictly increasing there. Hence the maximizers are exactly the edge midpoints, up to the natural symmetries.

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are real. The norm can be written as
\[
\|z\|
=
\max_{0\le j<6}
\frac{
\left|
\left\langle
z,
n_j
\right\rangle
\right|
}{
\cos(\pi/12)
},
\qquad
n_j=
\left(
\cos\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{12},
\sin\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{12}
\right).
\tag{4}
\]

The regular dodecagon is invariant under coordinate sign changes, coordinate interchange, and quarter-turn rotation. Thus it is a symmetric Minkowski plane with coordinate axes. The symmetric-plane isosceles-orthogonality lemma of Liu, Yang, Fu and Li therefore applies and gives (2).

The value of the objective is unchanged when \(Rx\) is replaced by \(-Rx\). Indeed,
\[
R(2x+Rx)=-(x-2Rx),
\]
\[
R(x+2Rx)=-(2x-Rx),
\]
and
\[
R(x+Rx)=-(x-Rx).
\]
Since \(R\) is an isometry, the two numerator terms are merely exchanged and the denominator is unchanged.

## Proof
By dihedral symmetry, it is enough to use (3), where
\[
v_0=(1,0),
\qquad
v_1=
\left(
\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\frac12
\right).
\]
Put
\[
\Phi(a)
=
\frac{
\|x(a)+2Rx(a)\|^2+
\|2x(a)+Rx(a)\|^2
}{
5\|x(a)+Rx(a)\|^2
}.
\tag{5}
\]

Reflection in the perpendicular bisector of the reference edge sends
\[
x(a)\longmapsto x(1-a)
\]
and conjugates \(R\) to \(-R\). By the sign observation above,
\[
\Phi(1-a)=\Phi(a).
\tag{6}
\]
It is therefore enough to consider
\[
0\le a\le\frac12.
\]

Set
\[
q=\frac{5-2\sqrt3}{13}.
\tag{7}
\]
A direct comparison of the six facet functionals in (4) gives
\[
A(a):=\|x(a)+2Rx(a)\|
=
4-\sqrt3+(4\sqrt3-7)a,
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
C(a):=\|x(a)+Rx(a)\|
=
2\sqrt3-2+(5-3\sqrt3)a
\tag{9}
\]
throughout
\[
0\le a\le\frac12.
\]
The remaining numerator factor has exactly one transition:
\[
B(a):=\|2x(a)+Rx(a)\|
=
\begin{cases}
4-\sqrt3+(2\sqrt3-4)a,
&
0\le a\le q,
\\[4pt]
3\sqrt3-3+(7-4\sqrt3)a,
&
q\le a\le\frac12.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
The two expressions in (10) agree exactly at \(a=q\).

Consequently
\[
\Phi(a)
=
\frac{A(a)^2+B(a)^2}{5C(a)^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
On the first interval, direct differentiation and simplification give
\[
\Phi'(a)
=
\frac{
3a(7+4\sqrt3)
}{
5(4+2\sqrt3-a)^3
}
\ge0,
\qquad
0\le a\le q.
\tag{12}
\]
On the second interval,
\[
\Phi'(a)
=
\frac{
(2+\sqrt3)(a+69+40\sqrt3)
}{
5(4+2\sqrt3-a)^3
}
>0,
\qquad
q\le a\le\frac12.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing on
\[
0<a\le\frac12.
\]
Together with (6), this proves that the unique reduced maximizer is
\[
a=\frac12.
\tag{14}
\]

At this point
\[
x=
\left(
\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4},
\frac14
\right),
\qquad
y=Rx=
\left(
-\frac14,
\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4}
\right).
\]
Equations (8)--(10) give
\[
\|x+y\|
=
\frac{1+\sqrt3}{2},
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
\|x+2y\|
=
\|2x+y\|
=
\sqrt3+\frac12.
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore
\[
\Phi\left(\frac12\right)
=
\frac{
2\left(\sqrt3+\frac12\right)^2
}{
5\left(\frac{1+\sqrt3}{2}\right)^2
}
=
\frac{14}{5}-\sqrt3.
\]
This proves (1).

Strictness in (12)--(13), together with the dihedral and sign reductions, also proves the equality classification: modulo dihedral symmetry, signs, and interchange of the two vectors, the maximizing vector is the midpoint of an edge and its partner is its quarter-turn.

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` works symbolically over expressions generated by \(\sqrt3\). It reconstructs the twelve vertices and the six facet normals, checks that the reference edge consists of unit vectors, verifies the quarter-turn and reflection symmetries, and confirms the facet formulas (8)--(10) on their full intervals by exact affine dominance tests.

It also verifies
\[
q=\frac{5-2\sqrt3}{13},
\]
the derivative identities (12)--(13), the midpoint chord values (15)--(16), and the final radical in (1).

No finite sample is used as a proof of the continuum maximum. The decisive checks are exact affine comparisons and symbolic derivative identities.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu, Yang, Fu and Li introduced \(\Omega(X)\), proved general bounds, studied its relation to uniform non-squareness and other classical constants, and supplied a symmetric-Minkowski-plane reduction. Their full text computes the square and a classical hexagonal example, but contains no regular-dodecagon evaluation.

The symmetric-plane reduction does not by itself identify which facets are active for the three chord vectors in (5). For the regular dodecagon, that missing computation produces the transition (7), the two exact profile branches, and the equality classification above.

A previously available exact \(\Omega\) computation for the regular octagonal plane concerns a different norm and has a different value and maximizing orbit. Other exact regular-dodecagon records concern James, P-angle, angle-modulus, equilateral-triangle, and rectangular constants. None determines the three-norm ratio in (5), and none implies the active-facet transition or the value in (1).

## Limitations
The result concerns the regular dodecagonal plane. It does not assert a closed formula for \(\Omega\) on all regular polygons.

The symmetric-Minkowski-plane lemma is used as a published structural reduction. The new work is the exact dodecagonal facet profile, its monotonicity, and the equality classification.

Bibliographic and semantic searches cannot prove absolute novelty. An equivalent dodecagonal computation could exist under a different affine normalization or be stated only as a specialized symmetric-plane profile.
