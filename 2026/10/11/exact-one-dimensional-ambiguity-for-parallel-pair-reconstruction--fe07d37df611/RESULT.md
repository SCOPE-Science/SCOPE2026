# Exact one-dimensional ambiguity for parallel-pair reconstruction of quadrangle nets

## Finding

Let \(Q_1,Q_2\) be quadrangles over a field \(\mathbb{k}\) of characteristic different from \(2\). Assume that they have the same locus in the sense of Olberding--Walker and share a bisector pair \(\mathsf A\) whose two component lines are parallel; the two lines may coincide.

Let \(F\) be a line-pair polynomial for \(\mathsf A\), and put
\[
\mathcal W_i=\mathcal V(Q_i)\oplus\mathbb{k}\cdot1.
\]
Choose \(G_i\) so that
\[
\mathcal W_i=\operatorname{span}\{1,F,G_i\}.
\]
Since the two quadrangles have the same locus conic, \(G_2\) can be rescaled so that
\[
J(F,G_2)=J(F,G_1).
\]

After an affine change of coordinates sending the shared parallel pair to
\[
Y=\alpha,\qquad Y=\beta,
\]
with \(\alpha=\beta\) allowed, take
\[
F=(Y-\alpha)(Y-\beta).
\]
Then there is a unique scalar \(\lambda\in\mathbb{k}\), modulo the harmless replacements of either \(G_i\) by an element differing by \(\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}\), such that
\[
\boxed{
G_2-G_1\in\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}+\lambda Y.
}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\boxed{
Q_1\text{ and }Q_2\text{ are entangled}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda=0.
}
\]

Equivalently, the linear reconstruction map
\[
T_F:H\longmapsto J(F,H)
\]
on quadratic polynomials has
\[
\boxed{
\ker T_F=\operatorname{span}\{1,F,Y\}
}
\]
for a parallel line pair. The source proves that for a nonparallel pair the corresponding kernel is only \(\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}\). Hence parallelity creates exactly one additional algebraic degree of freedom, and no more.

For the source's Example 7.6,
\[
F=Y^2-1,\qquad G_{\rm p}=X^2-1,
\]
and
\[
G_{\rm t}=4X^2-Y^2+6Y-9.
\]
The Jacobians are \(J(F,G_{\rm p})=-4XY\) and \(J(F,G_{\rm t})=-16XY\). After the required normalization,
\[
\frac14G_{\rm t}-G_{\rm p}
=-\frac32-\frac14F+\frac32Y,
\]
so the example has the nonzero defect
\[
\lambda=\frac32.
\]
Thus the counterexample in the source is not an isolated accident: it realizes the unique extra kernel direction that exists for every parallel shared pair.

## Assumptions and scope

A quadrangle, its pencil space \(\mathcal V(Q)\), net, locus conic, bisector pairs, and entanglement are used exactly as defined in the source. In particular, degenerate quadrangles are allowed subject to the source's noncollapse condition.

The field may be arbitrary of characteristic different from \(2\). No ordering, metric, or algebraic closedness is used.

The assertion is conditional on two already existing quadrangles having the same locus and sharing a parallel bisector pair. It does not assert that every scalar value in the ambient one-dimensional kernel quotient is realized by a quadrangle.

The scalar \(\lambda\) depends on the normalization of the transverse affine coordinate \(Y\). Its vanishing, and the statement that the ambiguity quotient has dimension one, are coordinate-independent.

## Proof

Write
\[
\mathcal W_i=\mathcal V(Q_i)\oplus\mathbb{k}\cdot1.
\]
The proof of Theorem 7.5 in the source shows that, for a shared bisector pair represented by \(F\), one may choose \(G_i\) with
\[
\mathcal W_i=\operatorname{span}\{1,F,G_i\},
\]
and with suitable constants subtracted from \(F\) and \(G_i\), the resulting two polynomials form a basis of \(\mathcal V(Q_i)\). Constants do not affect Jacobians. Therefore the locus conic of \(Q_i\) is represented by
\[
J(F,G_i).
\]

Because the quadrangles have the same locus, Theorem 7.4 of the source says that they have the same locus conic. Hence \(J(F,G_1)\) and \(J(F,G_2)\) are nonzero scalar multiples. Rescale \(G_2\) so that
\[
J(F,G_2)=J(F,G_1).
\]
It follows that
\[
J(F,G_2-G_1)=0.
\]

Now make an affine coordinate change sending the two parallel component lines of \(\mathsf A\) to \(Y=\alpha\) and \(Y=\beta\). Multiplying \(F\) by a nonzero scalar does not affect the kernel, so take
\[
F=(Y-\alpha)(Y-\beta).
\]
For a general quadratic polynomial
\[
H=aX^2+bXY+cY^2+rX+sY+t,
\]
direct differentiation gives
\[
J(F,H)
=-(2Y-\alpha-\beta)(2aX+bY+r).
\]
The first factor is a nonzero polynomial because the characteristic is not \(2\). Since \(\mathbb{k}[X,Y]\) is an integral domain,
\[
J(F,H)=0
\]
if and only if
\[
a=b=r=0.
\]
Thus
\[
H=cY^2+sY+t.
\]
Since
\[
F=Y^2-(\alpha+\beta)Y+\alpha\beta,
\]
every such \(H\) belongs to \(\operatorname{span}\{1,F,Y\}\), and conversely every element of that span has zero Jacobian with \(F\). Hence
\[
\ker T_F=\operatorname{span}\{1,F,Y\}.
\]
Modulo \(\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}\), this kernel is one-dimensional, generated by the class of \(Y\). This proves the existence and uniqueness of the defect coefficient \(\lambda\) after the normalization above.

If \(\lambda=0\), then \(G_2-G_1\in\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}\), so
\[
\mathcal W_1=\mathcal W_2.
\]
Therefore the two quadrangles have the same net and are entangled.

Conversely, suppose the quadrangles are entangled. Then \(\mathcal W_1=\mathcal W_2\), so
\[
G_2=A+BF+CG_1
\]
for some scalars \(A,B,C\). Applying \(J(F,\cdot)\) and using the normalized equality of Jacobians gives
\[
J(F,G_1)=C\,J(F,G_1).
\]
The Jacobian is nonzero by Proposition 7.1 of the source, so \(C=1\). Hence
\[
G_2-G_1\in\operatorname{span}\{1,F\},
\]
and therefore \(\lambda=0\).

The calculation for Example 7.6 is the substitution displayed in the Finding.

## Verification

The bundled symbolic checker differentiates a general quadratic polynomial against
\[
F=(Y-\alpha)(Y-\beta)
\]
and confirms the factorization
\[
J(F,H)=-(2Y-\alpha-\beta)(2aX+bY+r).
\]
It then verifies the kernel parametrization by \(1,F,Y\) and checks the exact decomposition of Example 7.6 after matching its two Jacobians.

The checker is supplementary. The proof over an arbitrary field of characteristic different from \(2\) follows from coefficient comparison in a polynomial ring and does not rely on numerical experiments.

## Relationship to prior work

Olberding and Walker prove in Theorem 7.5 that a common locus together with one shared **nonparallel** bisector pair determines the entire net. Their proof normalizes such a pair to \(F=XY\) and obtains
\[
\ker(H\mapsto J(F,H))=\operatorname{span}\{1,F\}.
\]
They immediately show in Example 7.6 that the nonparallel hypothesis cannot simply be deleted: a parallelogram and a nonparallelogram trapezoid can have the same locus and a shared parallel pair without being entangled.

The present result supplies the missing companion calculation for the parallel case. It shows that the obstruction in Example 7.6 is universal and exactly one-dimensional, rather than an unspecified failure of reconstruction.

Earlier work of Čerin and Gianella studies real families of quadrangles sharing a nine-point conic and produces an explicit conic parameter locus for one construction. That result concerns a particular geometric family and does not identify the Jacobian kernel or the exact one-dimensional defect created by fixing a parallel bisector pair.

The earlier bisector-field papers of Olberding and Walker explain how parallel line pairs arise from degenerate parabolas in pencils and develop the general bisector-field framework. They do not state the parallel-pair reconstruction defect above.

## Limitations

The theorem classifies the algebraic ambiguity after fixing a common locus and one shared parallel bisector pair. It does not classify which values of the one-dimensional defect are geometrically realized by quadrangles for a fixed pair of reconstruction data.

It also does not replace the source's full entanglement criteria. In particular, it gives a sharp description of one reconstruction failure mode rather than a moduli classification of all quadrangles with a prescribed locus.
