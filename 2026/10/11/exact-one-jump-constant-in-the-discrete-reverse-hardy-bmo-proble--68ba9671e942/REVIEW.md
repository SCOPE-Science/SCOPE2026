# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Every indicator jump has discrete BMO mean absolute deviation seminorm 1/2. For the two-point jump, starts at index four or later contribute at most 1/4, and blocks starting at indices one through three with endpoint at least 128 fall below 4/21 by a harmonic tail bound. All remaining blocks are checked in exact rational arithmetic. They give the global value 3371/13440 at [1,8]. The jump m=1 has larger ratio 14/27. For 3<=m<=199 an explicit harmonic witness L_m exceeds 3371/6720, with minimum 167723/332640 at m=3; m>=200 is covered by explicit integral and rational logarithm bounds. The unique optimizing length is m=2. Finite verification is tied to rigorous analytical tail bounds.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Caldera's *The Sharp Reverse Hardy Inequality in BMO for Nonincreasing Functions* (arXiv:2608.08093v1), §5.1, defines this same one-jump alpha_d parameter without evaluating it, and conjectures a stronger universal discrete inequality. Its Proposition 5.1 supplies only the weaker bound alpha_0/8; the sharp continuous theorem concerns a different domain. Ben Said–Boza–Soria's Hardy-type oscillation papers concern C-I/Copson-I norms in l^p/L^p, not the BMOd seminorm of Ce^(m). No inspected independent record supplies the exact rational value and unique optimizer. This is a best-of-knowledge judgment, subject to possible unindexed results and the inaccessible full text of one adjacent published paper.

**Value — PASS.** This exact, attained scalar benchmark is part of a stated discrete Hardy–BMO research problem, not an arbitrary small example. Its unique finite jump-length optimizer is a discrete phenomenon, and its value is a necessary sharp upper ceiling for any conjectured universal reverse inequality. It does not establish that universal conjecture.
