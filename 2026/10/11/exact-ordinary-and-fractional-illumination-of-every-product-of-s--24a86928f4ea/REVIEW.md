# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** After affine normalization, the interior tangent cones of the \(d+1\) vertices of a simplex have explicit strict sign descriptions and are pairwise disjoint. Product tangent cones factor coordinatewise, so one direction can illuminate at most one product vertex. This gives both the ordinary and fractional lower bounds. One interior tangent direction per vertex illuminates every face because the active-facet set only decreases from a face vertex to the face interior. The fixed-dimension \(3/4\) gap is the sharp product inequality \(d+1\le3\cdot2^{d-2}\) for one nonscalar factor and \(d+1\le2^d\) for the others. The bundled checker independently replays the cone and partition calculations.

## Originality
**PASS, with an implicit-literature residual risk.** The primary fractional-illumination paper was inspected in full. It defines \(i^*\), proves general difference-body bounds, records the parallelotope equality case, and has no simplex result in its searchable text. A later full-text paper on the cube's local illumination extremality was also inspected; it proves a neighborhood theorem around the cube and does not give a product-of-simplices formula. Exact searches under product-of-simplices, polysimplex, prism, Cartesian-product, ordinary illumination, fractional illumination, and vertex-count formulations found no implication-equivalent published theorem. The closest searchable illumination results concern special three-dimensional polytopes or near-cube perturbations rather than all products of simplices.

## Value
**PASS.** Products of simplices are a canonical simple-polytope class interpolating between a simplex and the cube. The theorem determines both classical and fractional illumination exactly in every dimension and turns the qualitative cube-uniqueness expectation into a sharp quantitative \(25\%\) gap throughout the nonparallelotope part of this class. The equality family \(\Delta_2\times[0,1]^{n-2}\) gives a natural sharp benchmark rather than an arbitrary isolated example.
