# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Cauchy's facet formula reduces normalized projected area of an equal-faced polyhedron to the average absolute inner product with its facet normals. A direct polar-Gaussian integration proves the spherical two-normal kernel \(2(\sqrt{1-t^2}+t\arcsin t)/(3\pi)\) for absolute normal correlation \(t\). The full ordered pair distributions of all five Platonic facet-normal arrays are enumerated exactly in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\); substitution gives the asserted closed forms. A standard-library rational-interval verifier proves the strict ordering and exact integer sample cutoffs without relying on rounded decimals. The independent sampling regression is explicitly non-probative for universal correctness.

## Originality
**PASS, with archival access risks.** The open full Cauchy proof supplies the first spherical moment, not its square. Horváth's complete open brightness section gives the exact facet projection formula and, crucially, already identifies the octahedron as the tetrahedron's Blaschke body; equality of their normalized brightness is credited as **prior work**, and only the full variance spectrum is asserted as new. Exact-object, projection-body, brightness, random-shadow variance, cube/octahedron and trigonometric-fingerprint searches did not uncover the four distinct closed variance expressions, their strict rank, or sharp uniform-view sample counts. Moran's 1944 original sources and the plausible 1983 Betke--McMullen estimator paper were accessible only through abstracts/metadata despite bounded lawful retrieval attempts; no full-text noncoverage judgment is drawn for them.

## Value
**PASS.** Surface-area estimation from random views is a classical, concrete geometric-statistics problem: Cauchy's first moment gives an unbiased estimator but does not determine its precision. The complete five-solid variance spectrum yields exact root-mean-square sample complexity, a nontrivial strict comparison across all Platonic types, and particularly economical exact sampling for the icosahedron. This is an intrinsically motivated complete finite classification, not an arbitrary isolated trigonometric computation.
