# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(a>b>0\) and all integers \(n\ge3\), the complete area-maximizing inscribed family follows from the circle's vertex-moving area argument and affine area scaling. The exact Euclidean chord expression reduces perimeter to a repeated cyclic cosine sum. Chebyshev product factorization, logarithmic differentiation, and the positive Stieltjes integral for the square root give negative first and second derivatives with respect to \(c=(-1)^q\cos(2q\phi)\). The conditions \(T_q(z)>1\), \(T_q'(z)>0\) for \(z>1\) justify signs; near-zero and infinity bounds justify derivatives under the integral. The exact deficit factorization proves both globally optimal constants. The included standalone script passes 6,732 deterministic finite diagnostics; these are supplemental, not exhaustive proofs.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical residual risks

Full text of Craizer–Teixeira–da Silva, arXiv:1103.2722, was checked in the introduction and all substantive sections, including Section 6. Their affine perimeter and affine isoperimetric inequality do not order the *Euclidean* perimeters of rotated affine-regular polygons on one fixed ellipse. Gronchi–Longinetti treat affine-invariant area-drop functionals; their abstract does not assert this orientation ordering, but full text was unavailable. Fisher–Jamison cover general affine-regular polygon properties, with inaccessible full text. The maximal-area ellipse construction records the family but not a Euclidean perimeter selection. Searches of published related records for ellipse perimeter phases, Chebyshev aliases, and stronger optimization did not find an equivalent theorem. Historical literature cannot be ruled out conclusively; the inaccessible sources remain documented.

## Value — PASS

A fixed ellipse admits continuously many *globally maximum-area* inscribed polygons, so an independent natural measurement is needed to choose an orientation. Ordinary Euclidean perimeter resolves that degeneracy for every polygon order and aspect ratio, with explicit parity-dependent orientations and optimal global angle-deficit constants. This is a structural classification with meaningful exact stability, not just recomputation of known area or an arbitrary finite instance. The theorem deliberately does not claim global maximum perimeter over all inscribed polygons.
