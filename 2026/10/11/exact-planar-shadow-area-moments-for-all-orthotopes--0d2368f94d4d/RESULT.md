# Exact planar shadow-area moments for all orthotopes

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge 3\), every orthotope \(Q=\prod_{i=1}^d[-a_i/2,a_i/2]\subset\mathbb R^d\) with \(a_i>0\), and a Haar-uniform unoriented two-plane \(E\), define \(A_Q(E)=\operatorname{area}_2(\operatorname{proj}_E Q)\). Then
\[
\mathbb E A_Q(E)=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{1\le i<j\le d}a_i a_j
\]
and the exact raw second moment is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E A_Q(E)^2=\frac{2}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i<j}a_i^2a_j^2+\frac{8}{\pi d(d-1)}\sum_{i<j<k}a_i a_j a_k(a_i+a_j+a_k)+\frac{6}{d(d-1)}\sum_{i<j<k<\ell}a_i a_j a_k a_\ell.}
\]
All indices in these sums lie in \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\); an empty sum is zero. Subtracting the square of the displayed mean gives the exact variance for every side-length vector. In particular, for the unit \(d\)-cube (side lengths all \(1\)),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(A_Q(E))=(d-2)\left(\frac4\pi-\frac54\right).}
\]
This cube formula is stated for \(d\ge3\) as a consequence of the general tensor identity; the three-dimensional cube case and the cube's leading large-dimensional variance coefficient are known from earlier work, and no novelty is claimed for them.

A more informative form describes every pair of absolute Plücker coordinates. Let \((u,v)\) be an orthonormal basis of \(E\), and put \(D_{ij}=|u_i v_j-u_j v_i|\), for \(i<j\). Then
\[
\mathbb E D_{ij}=\frac1{d-1},\quad \mathbb E D_{ij}^2=\frac2{d(d-1)},
\]
\[
\mathbb E(D_{ij}D_{ik})=\frac4{\pi d(d-1)}\quad (i,j,k\ \text{pairwise distinct}),
\]
\[
\mathbb E(D_{ij}D_{k\ell})=\frac1{d(d-1)}\quad (i,j,k,\ell\ \text{pairwise distinct; }d\ge4).
\]
The notation \(D_{ij}=D_{ji}\) is understood. Consequently the covariance of different minors with one shared index has sign determined by \(4(d-1)/\pi-d\): it is negative in dimensions \(3,4\), positive in all integer dimensions \(d\ge5\). The covariance of disjoint minors is strictly negative in every possible dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
We use Euclidean orthogonal projections with ordinary two-dimensional Lebesgue area. Haar probability means the unique rotation-invariant probability measure on the Grassmannian of unoriented two-dimensional linear subspaces of \(\mathbb R^d\). Side lengths are finite and strictly positive, and \(d\) is an integer at least three. Both first and second moments are taken over the random plane only; the input orthotope is deterministic. The formulas use no high-dimensional approximation. They do not assert Gaussian fluctuation limits, higher moments, or analogous formulas for arbitrary parallelepipeds.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to absolute minors.** The projection is the planar zonotope generated by the planar segments \(a_i\operatorname{proj}_E(e_i)\). The planar zonotope area formula, obtained by adding parallelogram mixed areas, is
\[
A_Q(E)=\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}u_i&u_j\\v_i&v_j\end{pmatrix}\right|=\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j D_{ij}.
\]
Changing the orthonormal basis of \(E\) multiplies all signed minors by the same sign, so the absolute minors depend only on the plane.

**2. Diagonal and first absolute moments.** Write \(p_{ij}=u_i v_j-u_j v_i\). The bivector \(u\wedge v\) has squared norm one. Thus the Cauchy–Binet identity and coordinate-permutation invariance give
\[
\sum_{i<j}p_{ij}^2=1,\qquad \mathbb E p_{ij}^2=\binom d2^{-1}=\frac2{d(d-1)}.
\]
To find the first absolute moment, select \(u\) uniformly on \(S^{d-1}\) and then \(v\) uniformly on the unit sphere in \(u^\perp\). Conditional on \(u\), the scalar \(p_{12}\) is a spherical coordinate times \(\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2}\). In the \((d-1)\)-dimensional space \(u^\perp\), the absolute spherical-coordinate mean is
\[
c_d=\frac{\Gamma((d-1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(d/2)}.
\]
Meanwhile \(T=u_1^2+u_2^2\) has beta distribution \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,(d-2)/2)\), and hence
\[
\mathbb E\sqrt T=\frac{\Gamma(3/2)\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}.
\]
Multiplying, using \(\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2\) and the gamma recurrence, gives \(\mathbb E|p_{12}|=1/(d-1)\), also valid at the boundary dimension \(d=3\).

**3. Minors sharing a coordinate.** Fix indices \(1,2,3\). Rotate the \((e_2,e_3)\)-plane while fixing \(e_1\). The joint law of \((p_{12},p_{13})\) is invariant under all planar rotations. Conditional on its Euclidean norm, its angle is therefore uniform. For any fixed radius \(r\ge0\), the circular mean of \(|r^2\cos\theta\sin\theta|\) is \(r^2/\pi\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E|p_{12}p_{13}|=\frac1\pi\mathbb E(p_{12}^2+p_{13}^2)
=\frac4{\pi d(d-1)}.
\]

**4. Disjoint minors and the four-dimensional Grassmannian.** Assume \(d\ge4\). Restrict \(u\) and \(v\) to their first four coordinates and form the simple bivector \(\xi=u'\wedge v'\in\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4\). Let \(r=\|\xi\|\). By invariance under rotations of those four coordinates, conditional on any \(r>0\), the direction \(\xi/r\) is uniformly distributed over oriented unit simple bivectors in four dimensions. This orbit is the oriented Grassmannian \(\operatorname{Gr}^+_2(\mathbb R^4)\). Under its normalized self-dual and anti-self-dual splitting, it has the representation
\[
\xi/r\ \longleftrightarrow\ (X,Y),\qquad X,Y\ \text{independent and uniform on }S^2,
\]
and a suitable choice of coordinates gives
\[
\frac{p_{12}}r=\frac{X_1+Y_1}{2},\qquad
\frac{p_{34}}r=\frac{X_1-Y_1}{2}.
\]
To justify independence rather than assume it, note that the \(SO(4)\) action becomes the independent \(SO(3)\times SO(3)\) actions on the two unit two-spheres, up to a finite kernel. Haar invariance and uniqueness of invariant probability on this product imply product-uniform measure. The coordinates \(X_1,Y_1\) are independent and each uniform on \([-1,1]\). Consequently
\[
\mathbb E\left(\frac{|p_{12}p_{34}|}{r^2}\ \middle|\ r\right)
=\frac14\int_0^1\!\int_0^1 |x^2-y^2|\,dx\,dy
=\frac1{12}.
\]
When \(r=0\), both minors vanish, so the same unconditional relation holds. As \(r^2=\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}p_{ij}^2\), step 2 yields \(\mathbb E r^2=12/[d(d-1)]\). Thus \(\mathbb E(D_{12}D_{34})=1/[d(d-1)]\). Every disjoint index-pair configuration has the same moment by symmetry.

**5. Expansion, covariance, and cube specialization.** Expanding the square of the step-1 zonotope sum yields diagonal terms, cross terms indexed by triples (pairs of two-edge subsets sharing one index), and cross terms indexed by quadruples (three disjoint edge-pairings). Step 2 contributes the coefficient \(2/[d(d-1)]\), step 3 the cross-term coefficient \(2\cdot4/[\pi d(d-1)]\), and step 4 the three-disjoint-pair cross-term coefficient \(2\cdot3/[d(d-1)]\), giving precisely the displayed second-moment formula.

Subtracting the square of the first moment gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(D_{12})=\frac{d-2}{d(d-1)^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{12},D_{13})=\frac{4(d-1)/\pi-d}{d(d-1)^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(D_{12},D_{34})=-\frac1{d(d-1)^2}.
\]
The last identity is for \(d\ge4\). For \(a_i=1\), the three classes contain respectively \(\binom d2\), \(3\binom d3\), and \(3\binom d4\) unordered pairings. Direct substitution simplifies to
\[
\mathbb E A_Q(E)^2=1+\frac{4(d-2)}\pi+\frac{(d-2)(d-3)}4,
\qquad \mathbb E A_Q(E)=\frac d2.
\]
Subtracting \(d^2/4\) yields the cube variance formula. Since \(4(d-1)/\pi-d\) changes sign between integers \(4\) and \(5\), the covariance parity threshold follows.

## Verification
The standalone accompanying `verify.py` recomputes the analytic moment polynomial from independent combinatorial sums, samples Haar planes using Gaussian Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization, and separately computes planar areas from the projected full vertex set using planar convex hulls. On its recorded run, the hull-versus-zonotope maximum absolute discrepancy was \(2.665\times10^{-15}\) over 120 polygon projections in dimensions \(3\) through \(7\). For 120,000 independent sampled planes at each listed dimension \(3,4,5,7,12\), the largest cube-variance discrepancy was \(8.36\times10^{-4}\); for unequal side lengths in dimensions \(3,4,6,9\), the largest raw-second-moment discrepancy was \(7.063\times10^{-3}\). The verifier printed `VERIFY_OK`. These are diagnostic numerical results, not an infinite-dimensional certificate; the preceding argument establishes the exact identities for all stated dimensions and side lengths.

## Relationship to prior work
Paouris–Pivovarov–Zinn [1] establish a central limit theorem for random projections of high-dimensional cubes using zonotope determinants. Their theorem concerns fixed target dimension as ambient dimension tends to infinity and does not furnish the full finite-dimensional second-order absolute-minor tensor for arbitrary orthotopes. Kabluchko–Prochno–Thäle [2] treat laws and deviations of scaled random projected cubes and product measures; their conclusions do not give this finite-dimensional quartic side-length formula. Prochno–Thäle–Tuchel [3], especially their discussion of cube projections, **do provide the asymptotic variance coefficient** for the two-dimensional cube shadow: after accounting for the \([-1,1]^d\) side-length convention, it equals \(16(4/\pi-5/4)\) per ambient dimension. That known coefficient is expressly excluded from originality claims here; the finite correction \(d-2\) and arbitrary side-length tensor are not implied by an asymptotic theorem. The three-dimensional cube area-variance case is also known and not claimed independently. Averkov–von Dichter–Richard–Soprunov [4] analyze the absolute Grassmannian and mixed-volume inequalities, including four-index Plücker products, but do not give Haar expectations or the area-moment identity above.

## Limitations
This is a same-model mathematical review, not independent validation. The analysis does not determine higher moments or all laws, nor the variance of nonorthogonal parallelotopes. Finite sampling does not certify the analytic theorem. The evaluated literature does not rule out an earlier unindexed integral-geometric treatment giving equivalent absolute-minor correlations; the d=3 cube case and leading cube asymptotic constant have known coverage. The dimension-three overlap and asymptotic overlap are acknowledged, and the remaining claim specifically concerns the universal finite-dimensional second-order tensor, unequal side lengths, and covariance sign transition.
