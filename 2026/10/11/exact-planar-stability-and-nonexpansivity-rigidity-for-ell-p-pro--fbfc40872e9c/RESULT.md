# Exact planar stability and nonexpansivity rigidity for \(\ell_p\) projections onto scaled Lorentz cones

## Finding
Let \(1<p<\infty\), \(q=p/(p-1)\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(n\ge1\) be an integer. In real \(\ell_p^n\), define the circular cone
\[
K_{\kappa,n}=\{(t,u)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{n-1}:t\ge\kappa\|u\|_2\}
\]
and let \(P_{p,\kappa,n}\) be its unique nearest-point map in the ambient \(\ell_p\) norm.

**Theorem.** In the plane its exact global Lipschitz constant is
\[
\operatorname{Lip}(P_{p,\kappa,2})
=\frac{(1+\kappa^p)^{1/p}(1+\kappa^{q(q-1)})^{1/q}}{1+\kappa^q}.
\]
This value is one exactly when \(p=2\) or \(\kappa=1\). In dimension one, \(\operatorname{Lip}(P_{p,\kappa,1})=1\). In every dimension \(n\ge3\), the map is nonexpansive exactly when \(p=2\); for every \(p\ne2\) and every \(\kappa>0\), explicit points \(x,z\) satisfy
\[
\|P_{p,\kappa,n}(x)-P_{p,\kappa,n}(z)\|_p>\|x-z\|_p.
\]
Thus the complete nonexpansivity classification is: all Hilbert exponents \(p=2\); the one-dimensional halfline for every \(p\); and the symmetric planar cone \(\kappa=1\) for every \(p\). No claim is made that the expansive higher-dimensional maps have any particular global Lipschitz constant.

## Assumptions and scope
The Euclidean norm \(\|u\|_2\) defines the **target cone** and \(\|\cdot\|_p\) defines the **ambient approximation norm**. They are different unless \(p=2\). The cone is closed, convex and nonempty. Strict convexity and coercivity of \(\ell_p^n\), for \(1<p<\infty\), give existence and uniqueness of the minimizer. For \(n=1\), \(\mathbb R^0=\{0\}\), so the cone is the positive halfline. The Lipschitz constant means the supremum of \(\|P(x)-P(z)\|_p/\|x-z\|_p\) over distinct ambient points, not an estimate for a fixed ray or region. The statement is real-valued and makes no assertion for \(p=1,\infty\), when uniqueness can fail.

## Proof
**1. The planar metric projection.** For \(n=2\), \(K_{\kappa,2}=\{(t,u):t\ge\kappa |u|\}\), the wedge generated by \(a_+=(\kappa,1)\) and \(a_-=(\kappa,-1)\). For \(u>0\), every \(s\ge0\) satisfies \(|u-s|\le|u+s|\). The positive generating ray therefore offers a no-worse competitor than the negative one at the same first coordinate; a minimizer outside the wedge is either on the positive ray or at the origin. The corresponding mirrored statement holds for \(u<0\).

Minimizing \(|t-\kappa s|^p+|u-s|^p\) over \(s\ge0\) and differentiating at an interior minimum yields
\[
\kappa\,|t-\kappa s|^{p-2}(t-\kappa s)
+|u-s|^{p-2}(u-s)=0.
\]
Strict monotonicity of the signed power shows that, when \(t<\kappa u\) and the minimizing coefficient is positive, the two residuals have opposite signs and \(u-s=-\kappa^{q-1}(t-\kappa s)\), hence
\[
s=\frac{\kappa^{q-1}t+u}{1+\kappa^q}.
\]
(The equality also follows by direct substitution into the signed-power equation.) Including the nonnegativity constraint and reflection gives the complete continuous formula
\[
P_{p,\kappa,2}(t,u)=
\begin{cases}
(t,u),&t\ge\kappa|u|,\\
(0,0),&t<\kappa|u|,\ \kappa^{q-1}t+|u|\le0,\\
(\kappa s,\operatorname{sgn}(u)s),&t<\kappa|u|,\ \kappa^{q-1}t+|u|>0,
\end{cases}
\quad s=\frac{\kappa^{q-1}t+|u|}{1+\kappa^q}.
\]
In each of the two open ray-selection regions the linear part is the rank-one map
\[
L_\pm(t,u)=\frac{\kappa^{q-1}t\pm u}{1+\kappa^q}(\kappa,\pm1).
\]
Its induced \(\ell_p\) operator norm is exactly
\[
\|L_\pm\|_{p\to p}=\frac{\|(\kappa,1)\|_p\|(\kappa^{q-1},1)\|_q}{1+\kappa^q}.
\]
The remaining linear pieces are the identity and zero, of norms one and zero. Since the continuous projection is piecewise linear on a finite polyhedral partition, subdividing each line segment at the region boundaries gives a global Lipschitz bound by the largest piece norm. Every ray-selection region has nonempty interior, so infinitesimal perturbations in a maximizing direction for its rank-one linear part attain that same norm as a Lipschitz supremum. Hölder's inequality applied to the inner product \(\langle(\kappa,1),(\kappa^{q-1},1)\rangle=1+\kappa^q\) makes this norm at least one. Equality in Hölder for positive vectors requires \(\kappa^{q-1}=\kappa^{p-1}\), equivalent to \(\kappa=1\) or \(p=q=2\). This proves the planar formula and rigidity.

**2. Higher-dimensional expansion.** Fix \(n\ge3\), \(p\ne2\), and \(\kappa>0\). Choose \(0<c<1\) with \(\kappa c\ne1\), and write \(s=\sqrt{1-c^2}\). Embed the following three-coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb R^n\) by adding zero coordinates:
\[
y=(\kappa,c,s),\qquad f=(-1,\kappa c,\kappa s).
\]
The point \(y\) belongs to the smooth boundary of the cone. The vector \(f\) is its outward Euclidean supporting normal, since for every \(w=(t,u)\in K_{\kappa,n}\),
\[
\langle f,w-y\rangle=-t+\kappa(cu_1+su_2)
\le-t+\kappa\|u\|_2\le0.
\]
For real \(r>1\) define the coordinatewise duality map \(J_r(a)_j=\operatorname{sgn}(a_j)|a_j|^{r-1}\). Set \(v=J_q(f)\). The identity \(J_p(v)=f\) and convex first-order optimality show that, for **every** \(\delta>0\),
\[
P_{p,\kappa,n}(y+\delta v)=y.
\]
Indeed, the derivative of the objective \(w\mapsto\|y+\delta v-w\|_p^p\) at \(w=y\), in every feasible direction \(w-y\), equals \(-p\delta^{p-1}\langle f,w-y\rangle\ge0\); convexity and strict convexity make \(y\) its unique minimizer.

Consider nearby points on the same cone boundary,
\[
z(\eta)=\bigl(\kappa\sqrt{(c+\eta)^2+s^2},\ c+\eta,\ s\bigr),
\qquad z(0)=y.
\]
Their tangent is \(h=z'(0)=(\kappa c,1,0)\). A direct calculation gives
\[
\langle J_p(h),v\rangle
=(\kappa c)^{q-1}-(\kappa c)^{p-1}\ne0.
\]
The inequality follows from \(\kappa c\ne1\) and \(p\ne2\). Choose a sufficiently small nonzero \(\eta\) of the same sign as that difference. Since \(z(\eta)-y=\eta h+O(\eta^2)\) and the duality map is continuous and homogeneous of degree \(p-1\),
\[
\langle J_p(z(\eta)-y),v\rangle>0.
\]
Consequently the derivative, at \(\delta=0\), of the scalar function \(\delta\mapsto\|y+\delta v-z(\eta)\|_p^p\) is strictly negative. For a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\),
\[
\|y+\delta v-z(\eta)\|_p
<\|y-z(\eta)\|_p.
\]
Because \(P(y+\delta v)=y\) and \(P(z(\eta))=z(\eta)\), this is a strict violation of nonexpansivity. All extra coordinates remain zero, so the argument works for every \(n\ge3\).

**3. Remaining cases.** For \(p=2\), nearest-point projection onto a closed convex subset of a real Hilbert space is firmly nonexpansive and hence one-Lipschitz. For \(n=1\), the projection is \(t\mapsto\max(t,0)\), also one-Lipschitz. This exhausts the stated cases. \(\square\)

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library program `verify_projection.py` checks 3,750 sampled input pairs against the **exact planar constant** for 25 combinations of exponent and aperture; it also checks 12 direct higher-dimensional strict-expansion ratios and the equality regimes. Run `python3 verify_projection.py` in the extracted folder. These are numerical regression tests with deterministic sampling, not an exhaustive proof. Exact global bounds and all-parameter classification come from the preceding analytic argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Németh and Zhang's *Extended Lorentz Cones and Mixed Complementarity Problems* (first posted 2014-05-30) studies isotonicity **with respect to a cone-induced partial order**, using Euclidean projections onto prescribed closed convex sets, including cylinders. Their *Extended Lorentz Cones and Variational Inequalities on Cylinders* (first posted 2015-03-20) continues that Euclidean isotone-projection program. Neither theorem implies a nonexpansivity classification in a **non-Euclidean ambient \(\ell_p\) norm** for metric projection **onto** the scaled circular cone itself. The general Hilbert nonexpansivity theorem is standard; the new content is the sharp planar constant and the all-aperture higher-dimensional obstruction.

## Limitations
The theorem is finite-dimensional and real and requires \(1<p<\infty\), \(\kappa>0\). It establishes strict expansion in non-Hilbert dimensions at least three but does not determine their exact global Lipschitz constants, nor the quantitative maximum expansion. No conclusion is asserted for endpoint exponents, arbitrary cones, or arbitrary norms. Source comparison cannot exclude an obscure prior equivalent special-case formula.
