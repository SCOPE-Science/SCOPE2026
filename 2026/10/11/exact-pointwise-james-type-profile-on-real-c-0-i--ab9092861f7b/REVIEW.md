# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(x\in S_{c_0(I)}\), choose \(k\) with \(|x_k|=1\) and let \(s=s_2(x)\). For any \(y\in S_{c_0(I)}\), one sign makes the \(k\)-th coordinate of \(x\pm y\) at most \(1\); every other coordinate for that sign is at most \(1+s\). The other chord is at most \(2\). Every generalized mean is coordinatewise increasing, so the objective is at most \(M_t(2,1+s)\). A two-coordinate finitely supported \(y\) attains chord lengths exactly \(2\) and \(1+s\). The proof covers all \(-\infty\le t<\infty\). The range and equality cases follow from the exact dependence on \(s_2(x)\).

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2023 article was inspected through its definition, examples, transitivity results, and the finite-dimensional extreme-point proposition. No \(c_0\) calculation appears. The source's extreme-point reduction explicitly assumes finite dimension and is in the \(t\ge1\) section, so it does not imply the accepted infinite-dimensional all-parameter formula. Targeted searches for \(c_0\), second-largest-coordinate formulations, the explicit power-mean expression, and infinite-dimensional aliases returned no covering result. The closest pointwise-James record concerns \(\ell_p\) constancy thresholds, while the closest exact \(c_0\) record concerns the different pointwise \(\Delta\)-constant.

## Value
PASS. The result is a complete pointwise classification on the canonical nonreflexive space \(c_0(I)\), not a routine value at one selected vector. One coordinate statistic determines the whole generalized-mean scale, including negative parameters, and the theorem gives the full image, minimizers, and maximizers. It also supplies a direct infinite-dimensional mechanism where the defining paper's finite-dimensional compactness argument is unavailable.

## Closest literature and limitations
Rincón--Villamizar, *Analysis Mathematica* 49 (2023), 651--659, introduced the pointwise James type constant and proved a finite-dimensional extreme-point formula in the parameter range \(t\ge1\). The present theorem is specific to real \(c_0(I)\); it does not classify arbitrary \(C(K)\) spaces. Absolute novelty cannot be certified by database search alone, and an older pointwise-nonsquareness source might contain a special endpoint case under different notation.
