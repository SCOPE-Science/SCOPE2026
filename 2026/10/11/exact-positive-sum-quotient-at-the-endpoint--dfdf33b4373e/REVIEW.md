# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The endpoint identity follows by substituting \(f(0)=1-\|f'\|_\infty\) and normalizing each derivative by its supremum. The converse indistinguishability direction is not inferred from search: it is proved by a pointwise reconstruction formula using continuous bump functions. The fiber interval and the fiberwise-surjection construction are then exact algebraic consequences. No numerical approximation or finite extrapolation is involved.

## Originality
PASS. The focal source establishes the affine \(\delta=0\) behavior and uses a bijection of that affine family, but does not give the all-fiber quotient formula or the if-and-only-if twin classification. Searches targeted star sets, peak-set formulations, derivative deficits, constant-shift equivalence, source ID, and arbitrary fiber permutations. The closest same-space comparator concerns surjective isometries; the closest norm-additive function-space comparators concern different domains or sequence spaces. A full-text extraction of the same-space isometry paper was unavailable, so that paper remains an explicitly recorded residual risk rather than being declared non-covering in full.

## Value
PASS. The finding identifies the exact quotient seen by all positive-sum norms at the exceptional endpoint. It replaces a single affine counterexample with a complete decomposition into invisible fibers and shows that arbitrary surjective motion inside every fiber is norm-additive. This is a structural explanation of endpoint non-rigidity and a natural starting point for a later classification of quotient-kernel automorphisms.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ezumi--Miura, arXiv:2610.02862v1, especially Counterexample 6.1. Their affine family is exactly the \(\delta=0\) fiber of the present quotient. Enami--Hirota--Koshimizu--Lin, arXiv:2606.30952v1, treats isometries on the same sphere rather than norm-additive maps; only its public abstract was readable in this review. The result here does not classify nontrivial transformations of the quotient variable \(\delta\).
