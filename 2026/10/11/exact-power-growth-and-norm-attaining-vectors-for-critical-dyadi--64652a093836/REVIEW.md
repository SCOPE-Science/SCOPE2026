## Correctness — PASS
The published operator definition and telescoping identity were checked directly against Li and Li, arXiv:2608.09694v2, Theorem 1.4 and Section 5. For each output dyadic block, the shifted interval intersects one or two input blocks; independent \(\ell_p\)-budgets show that its exact squared-style budget (the \(p\)-th power norm) is the sum of the maximal coefficients in those source blocks. Strict bounds exclude all output blocks with initial index at least four. The initial-index-two output block crosses a dyadic boundary exactly at the specified exceptional powers. Explicit finitely supported inputs attain each optimum, and strict winning-block inequalities force the stated complete unit-vector equality cases. All integer powers and all real \(1\le p<\infty\) are covered analytically. The included finite checker supplies independent arithmetic corroboration but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS
Li and Li's exact source family and its lower and upper estimates are credited, including the already published two-block overlap observation. The inspected full primary text (Theorem 1.4 and Section 5, especially the power norm proof) supplies only \((n+1)^{1/p}\le\|T_p^n\|\le2^{1/p}(n+1)^{1/p}\), not the exact sequence or equality vectors. Semantic mathematical-record searches under source-name, dyadic-source, shift-profile, exceptional-power, and equivalent weighted-shift terminology found no covering result. The complete published proofs of the two closest published-finding corpus records were inspected: the Wallen-type theorem constructs a different forward weighted shift on Hilbert space, and the other studies weak-orbit endpoints for a subcritical backward shift on ordinary sequence space. Neither implies the fixed critical dyadic model's full exact power sequence. Owned earlier claims on weighted shifts and operator growth concern other spaces/weights and do not imply this arithmetic classification. Residual risk: an equivalent exact computation could be implicit in older block-operator theory or unindexed literature; lack of discovery does not establish absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
The motivating family is the canonical sharp-order example for positive mean-L-stable operators on \(p\)-convex lattices. Its exact constants were left between a factor-two window on \(p\)-th powers. The result closes the window for *every* positive power and exponent, proves the optimal uniform factor \(17/12\) on \(p\)-th powers, and explains why two small powers are exceptional; complete norm-attainer classification reveals the geometric source-block mechanism. This answers the natural sharpness problem for that concrete family, without claiming any wider growth theorem.
