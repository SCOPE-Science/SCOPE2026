# Exact powers-of-two torus orders for all-to-all coupled cat maps

## Finding

Let \(n\ge2\) and \(s\ge1\) be integers. Write \(I_n\) for the identity matrix and \(J_n\) for the all-ones matrix. Consider the finite torus \(X_{n,s}=(\mathbb Z/2^s\mathbb Z)^{2n}\) and the invertible symplectic cat-map update

\[
M(C)=\begin{pmatrix}I_n&C\\ C&I_n+C^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad x\longmapsto M(C)x\pmod{2^s}.
\]

For the looped all-to-all coupling \(C=J_n\), and the loopless complete-graph coupling \(C=J_n-I_n\), its exact global permutation order \(T_{n,s}(C)=\min\{t\ge1:M(C)^t\equiv I_{2n}\pmod{2^s}\}\) is

\[
\boxed{T_{n,s}(J_n)=\begin{cases}2^s,&n\equiv0\pmod2,\\3\cdot2^{\max(0,s-2)},&n\equiv1\pmod2,\end{cases}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{T_{n,s}(J_n-I_n)=\begin{cases}3\cdot2^{s-1},&n\equiv0,3\pmod4,\\3\cdot2^{\max(0,s-2)},&n\equiv1,2\pmod4.\end{cases}}
\]

In particular, for the loopless complete graph the entire powers-of-two period spectrum depends on the number of sites only modulo four. The two residue families have different lifting behavior already at modulus four; for example \(T_{6,2}=3\) whereas \(T_{4,2}=6\). Self-loops instead produce a pure power-of-two period at every even site count.

## Assumptions and scope

The coupling and updates are exactly the displayed integer matrices reduced modulo \(2^s\). The graph with loops has \(C=J_n\), not the adjacency matrix of a simple graph. Both families are symmetric circulants. Global permutation order is the least positive exponent fixing **all** states; it need not equal the period of each individual state. No conclusion is asserted for odd moduli, other circulant graphs, or irrational torus points.

Invertibility follows for every integer symmetric \(C\) from

\[
M(C)=\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\\ C&I_n\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I_n&C\\0&I_n\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose factors have integral inverses. Symmetry of \(C\) also gives symplecticity for the standard form.

## Proof

**Step 1 (a faithful two-coefficient algebra).** Put \(e=J_n\). Because \(e^2=ne\), the subring generated by \(I_n,J_n\) is the image of

\[
R_n=\mathbb Z[e]/(e^2-ne).
\]

The map \(a+be\mapsto aI_n+bJ_n\) is injective for \(n\ge2\): an off-diagonal entry is \(b\), and then a diagonal entry fixes \(a\). This remains true over every \(\mathbb Z/2^k\mathbb Z\). Thus the entrywise \(2\)-adic order of a block matrix is determined without loss by the coefficients in the free basis \(1,e\) of \(R_n\).

Put \(c=e\) (with loops) or \(c=e-1\) (without loops), and let

\[
A(c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1+c^2\end{pmatrix}\in M_2(R_n).
\]

All powers of \(M(C)\) are the images of powers of \(A(c)\). Two exact polynomial identities are

\[
A(c)^2-I_2=\begin{pmatrix}c^2&c(c^2+2)\\c(c^2+2)&c^2(c^2+3)\end{pmatrix}
\]

and

\[
A(c)^3-I_2=\begin{pmatrix}c^2(c^2+3)&c(c^2+1)(c^2+3)\\ c(c^2+1)(c^2+3)&c^2(c^2+2)(c^2+3)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**Step 2 (exhaustive initial \(2\)-adic valuations).** Define \(v_2(B)\) as the minimum \(2\)-adic valuation of the entries of the nonzero integer matrix \(B\). The two polynomial identities, \(e^2=ne\), and one additional squaring suffice to compute the following table. Here \(m\) is the exact order modulo two, and the valuations are exact (not just divisibility bounds):

| Coupling | Number of sites | \(m\) | \(v_2(M^m-I)\) | \(v_2(M^{2m}-I)\) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(J_n\) | even | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| \(J_n\) | odd | 3 | 2 | at least 3 |
| \(J_n-I_n\) | \(n\equiv0,3\pmod4\) | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| \(J_n-I_n\) | \(n\equiv1,2\pmod4\) | 3 | 2 | at least 3 |

For transparency, the table is a finite **symbolic** calculation rather than a finite-site extrapolation. Work in \((\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)[e]/(e^2-\bar n e)\), evaluate \(A(e)\) or \(A(e-1)\) for **each of the eight** residues \(\bar n=0,1,\ldots,7\), and inspect the coefficients of \(A^2-I\), \(A^3-I\), \(A^4-I\), and \(A^6-I\). Because all coefficient expressions are integer polynomials in \(n\), evaluation of these eight residues is an exhaustive proof for every integer \(n\). The attached standalone `verify.py` reproduces all eight evaluations with exact modular arithmetic. In particular the looped-odd upper-left coefficient of \(A^3-I\) is \(n(n^2+3)e\), which has valuation exactly two for every odd \(n\). Modulo two, the claimed minimal orders are likewise checked in the same quotient algebra, not inferred from eigenvalues over a field.

**Step 3 (lifting an exact matrix order).** If \(B=I+2^rE\) with \(r\ge2\) and at least one entry of \(E\) odd, then

\[
B^2-I=2^{r+1}(E+2^{r-1}E^2),
\]

and the parenthesized matrix retains an odd entry. Hence \(v_2(B^{2^j}-I)=r+j\) for every \(j\ge0\). In rows with initial valuation one, the preceding table first proves that squaring raises the exact valuation to two; the same argument then applies. Because the order modulo two is \(m=2\) or \(m=3\), and successive kernels of reduction from modulus \(2^{k+1}\) to \(2^k\) are \(2\)-groups, any higher order is \(m\) multiplied by a power of two. The exact valuations therefore force precisely the stated orders for all \(s\ge1\).

## Verification

The self-contained Python 3 program `verify.py` executes exact coefficient-arithmetic tests in the abstract quotient ring for all eight residue classes and both couplings, then independently constructs full integer matrices for all \(2\le n\le14\) and all \(1\le s\le8\). It checks predicted orders and the relevant proper-divisor exponents and prints `VERIFY_OK; exact assertions: 576` when successful. The eight-residue coefficient calculation supports the universal polynomial argument; dense finite-node computations are independent regression tests, not a proof by sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Manolas and Floratos, *Coupled Arnol'd cat maps on circulant graphs* (first public May 1, 2026), introduce the general symmetric-circulant matrix family, analyze the entropy of fully connected models with and without self-loops in their Section 4, and numerically study finite-torus matrix periods in Section 4.1. They derive the general matrix-power recurrence but do not present this exact uniform all-site, all-powers-of-two arithmetic classification. Their earlier 2023 theory with Axenides and Nicolis supplies general cat-map lattice dynamics and period methods. Standard single-cat-map congruence arithmetic is part of the background, not claimed as new. The present theorem is a closed complete-graph specialization of the existing dynamical model; it does not assert a new universal period theory for arbitrary circulant couplings.

## Limitations

The only two adjacency families proved here are \(J_n\) and \(J_n-I_n\); the modulus is exactly a power of two. Global order is the least exponent fixing all configurations, not a uniform primitive orbit period. Finite tests cannot replace the polynomial-residue and valuation-lifting proof. Earlier unindexed work might contain an equivalent specialization; literature searches do not certify absolute publication priority. Independent audit has not been performed.
