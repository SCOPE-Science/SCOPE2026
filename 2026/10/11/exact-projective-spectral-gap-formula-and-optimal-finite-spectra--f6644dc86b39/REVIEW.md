# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The continuous functional calculus identifies the operator norm of the rotated real part of a unitary with the cosine of the distance to the antipodal spectrum. On each complementary projective spectral arc of length \(\ell\), the exact squared-norm deficit integrates to \((\ell-\sin\ell)/2\). The sum is justified without a finite-spectrum assumption because the total gap length is at most \(\pi\). For finite projective spectra, strict Jensen concavity and strict monotonicity of \(q\sin(\pi/q)\) establish the optimal constants and exact equality cases. Edge cases (one projective class, full projective circle, multiplicity collapse, and infinite compact spectra) agree with the proof. Numerical tests are supplementary.

## Originality — PASS, subject to documented residual risk

The directly motivating paper [1] defines the invariant and establishes universal normal-operator inequalities. Its inspected Theorem 17 does not state an exact unitary gap formula or optimize finite spectral configurations. Comparisons with available prior claims on rank-one matrices and a distance-to-self-adjoint bound for the same invariant show no statement-level overlap. Focused mathematical-record and public searches found no covering exact result. However, a classical result on integrated support functions of spectral sets could imply the identity under different terminology; search cannot exclude that possibility. Closest numerical-range and normal-stability records address different quantities.

## Value — PASS

The theorem computes a newly introduced operator invariant exactly in a canonical spectral class, including continuous and infinite spectra. It completely classifies the maximum across all unitary matrices of each dimension, with a nontrivial sharp dimension dependence and all extremizing eigenvalue configurations. The result is spectral-geometric rather than a renamed general inequality.

## Literature and limitations

[1] W. Audeh, H. R. Moradi, and S. Sahoo, *The \(L^2\)-Cartesian Numerical Radius of Hilbert Space Operators*, arXiv:2610.04844v1 (2026-10-04), especially Theorem 17. Normal-contraction extensions and general nonnormal operators are not proved. Possible historical reformulations involving mean-square convex support functions remain a genuine originality risk.
