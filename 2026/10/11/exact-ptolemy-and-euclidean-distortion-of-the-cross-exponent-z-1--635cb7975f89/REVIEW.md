# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Signed-permutation symmetry reduces the norm-to-Euclidean ratio to
\[
Q_\lambda(t)=\frac{\lambda+(1+t)^2}{1+t^2},
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Its unique interior maximum is at \(t_\lambda=(\sqrt{\lambda^2+4}-\lambda)/2\), while its minimum is axial for \(\lambda\le2\) and diagonal for \(\lambda\ge2\). This gives the global Hilbert comparison. Symmetry averaging rules out a better nonsymmetric ellipse. Exact phase-dependent triples attain the resulting Ptolemy upper bound.

## Originality
PASS. Yang (2015) gives the same two-branch formula only for the Jordan--von Neumann constant. Zuo (2018) treats weighted means of \(\ell_p\) and \(\ell_q\) norms and explicitly records the entire cross regime \(1\le p<2<q\le\infty\) as having undiscovered exact Ptolemy values. Targeted searches and the checked database found no later \(p=1,q=\infty\) Ptolemy or Euclidean-distance formula. The closest mixed-plane Euclidean-distance record covers only same-side exponent regimes.

## Value
PASS. The theorem settles the canonical endpoint of a published open cross-exponent Ptolemy direction for every mixing parameter, identifies a nontrivial phase transition at \(\lambda=2\), and proves a structural coincidence among Ptolemy, Euclidean distortion, and the known Jordan--von Neumann constant.
