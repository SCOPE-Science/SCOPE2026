# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The dual norm is reconstructed from its defining support-function maximization. For \(p=q/(q-1)\), the ordered dual norm has exactly two branches according to whether \(m\le\omega M\). Its Euclidean comparison profile has one critical point
\[
\rho=\omega^{1/(2-q)},
\]
and no other interior extrema. The resulting Hilbert comparison gives the global Ptolemy upper bound. Two explicit triples attain that bound on the two sides of
\[
\omega=2^{q/2}-1.
\]
All branch conditions and quantifiers are checked symbolically.

## Originality
PASS. The directly relevant 2018 primary paper computes the primal value below exponent two, computes the dual only at \(q=1\) and \(q\ge2\), and explicitly identifies the dual range \(1<q<2\) as unknown. Searches using Lorentz notation, dual-space aliases, the invariant name, and primal-dual equality language located the known \(q=2\) result and related Lorentz constants, but no later exact solution of the missing range. A later unindexed solution remains a residual bibliographic risk.

## Value
PASS. The result closes the explicit missing range in a natural classical two-dimensional Lorentz problem, gives the exact dual constant with its weight phase transition, and completes
\[
C_{\mathrm{Pt}}\!\left(d^{(2)}(\omega,q)\right)
=
C_{\mathrm{Pt}}\!\left(d^{(2)}(\omega,q)^*\right)
\]
for every \(0<\omega<1\) and \(1\le q<\infty\). This is a structural completion of the primal-dual question rather than a routine isolated value.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Zuo (2018), which states the missing dual range explicitly. Zuo (2012) supplies the earlier planar Ptolemy framework. The new proof is restricted to real dimension two and \(1<q<2\); the all-\(q\) corollary combines it with the already known remaining ranges. No claim is made in higher-dimensional Lorentz spaces.
