# Exact Ptolemy constant of the canonical nonabsolute Day--James octagon
## Finding
Let \(\psi:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\) be
\[
\psi(t)=
\begin{cases}
1-t,&0\le t\le\frac18,\\
\frac{11-4t}{12},&\frac18\le t\le\frac12,\\
\frac{1+t}{2},&\frac12\le t\le1,
\end{cases}
\]
and let \(X=\ell_\psi-\ell_\infty\) be the real generalized Day--James plane whose norm is the absolute normalized \(\psi\)-norm when the coordinate product is nonnegative and the \(\ell_\infty\)-norm when it is nonpositive. Then
\[
C_{\mathrm{Pt}}(X)=\frac{13}{9}.
\]
The value is attained by
\[
x=\left(-\frac23,-\frac23\right),\qquad
y=\left(\frac32,-\frac32\right),\qquad
z=\left(\frac56,-\frac{13}{6}\right).
\]

This is the canonical nonabsolute generalized Day--James octagon introduced by Nilsrakoo and Saejung. The exact value comes from a sharp Hilbert comparison whose two extremal contact levels are simultaneously realized by one Ptolemy triple.

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are real and the dimension is exactly two. For an absolute normalized norm associated with \(\psi\),
\[
\|(a,b)\|_\psi=
\begin{cases}
(|a|+|b|)\psi\!\left(\dfrac{|b|}{|a|+|b|}\right),&(a,b)\ne(0,0),\\
0,&(a,b)=(0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
The generalized Day--James norm is
\[
N(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
\|(a,b)\|_\psi,&ab\ge0,\\
\max\{|a|,|b|\},&ab\le0.
\end{cases}
\]
At points on the coordinate axes the two branches agree.

The Ptolemy constant is
\[
C_{\mathrm{Pt}}(X)=
\sup_{\substack{x,y,z\in X\setminus\{0\}\\x,y,z\ {\rm pairwise\ distinct}}}
\frac{N(x-y)N(z)}
{N(x-z)N(y)+N(z-y)N(x)}.
\]
The theorem concerns this single canonical nonabsolute octagon.

## Proof
For \(a,b\ge0\), put \(t=b/(a+b)\). Then \(N(a,b)=(a+b)\psi(t)\). The three pieces of \(\psi\) give the first-quadrant unit-boundary segments
\[
a=1,\qquad 11a+7b=12,\qquad a+2b=2.
\]
Together with the opposite-sign \(\ell_\infty\) branch, the unit sphere is the centrally symmetric octagon with cyclic vertices
\[
(0,-1),\quad(1,-1),\quad\left(1,\frac17\right),\quad
\left(\frac23,\frac23\right)
\]
and their negatives.

Define
\[
q(a,b)=a^2+\frac{10}{13}ab+b^2,\qquad H(a,b)=\sqrt{q(a,b)}.
\]
On the four independent consecutive edges, exact one-variable minimization gives
\[
\frac{144}{169},\qquad
\frac{144}{169},\qquad
\frac{72}{65},\qquad
\frac{64}{65},
\]
at affine parameters \(5/13,7/13,3/10,4/5\), respectively. The corresponding vertex values on this half of the boundary are
\[
1,\qquad\frac{16}{13},\qquad\frac{720}{637},\qquad\frac{16}{13},\qquad1.
\]
Central symmetry supplies the remaining edges. Since a convex quadratic on a segment has its maximum at an endpoint,
\[
m:=\min_{N(w)=1}q(w)=\frac{144}{169},
\qquad
M:=\max_{N(w)=1}q(w)=\frac{16}{13}.
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt m\,N(w)\le H(w)\le\sqrt M\,N(w)
\qquad(w\in\mathbb R^2).
\]

The Hilbert norm \(H\) satisfies Ptolemy. Therefore every admissible triple obeys
\[
N(x-y)N(z)\le \frac{H(x-y)H(z)}m
\]
and
\[
N(x-z)N(y)+N(z-y)N(x)
\ge
\frac{H(x-z)H(y)+H(z-y)H(x)}M.
\]
Consequently
\[
C_{\mathrm{Pt}}(X)\le\frac Mm
=\frac{16/13}{144/169}
=\frac{13}{9}.
\]

For
\[
x=\left(-\frac23,-\frac23\right),\qquad
y=\left(\frac32,-\frac32\right),\qquad
z=\left(\frac56,-\frac{13}{6}\right),
\]
direct substitution gives
\[
N(x)=1,\quad N(y)=\frac32,\quad N(z)=\frac{13}{6},
\]
\[
N(x-y)=\frac{13}{6},\quad N(x-z)=\frac32,\quad N(z-y)=1.
\]
Hence the Ptolemy ratio is
\[
\frac{(13/6)^2}{(3/2)^2+1}=\frac{13}{9}.
\]
The upper and lower bounds coincide.

## Verification
The proof uses the actual eight-sided sphere generated by the source definition. Each edge restriction of \(q\) is a convex quadratic and is minimized exactly at its stationary point when that point lies on the edge. The four calculations plus central symmetry exhaust the boundary. The accompanying checker reconstructs all vertices with rational arithmetic, verifies every edge minimum and vertex maximum, and evaluates the extremal triple exactly. No grid or floating-point optimization is used as evidence.

## Relationship to prior work
Nilsrakoo and Saejung introduced the generalized Day--James class and used exactly the displayed \(\psi\) to produce a normalized norm that is not linearly isometric to any absolute normalized plane. Their Proposition 2.4 states that the sphere of this \(\ell_\psi-\ell_\infty\) space is an octagon. Their paper studies James constants and uniform nonsquareness, not this Ptolemy constant.

Alonso later proved that every two-dimensional normed space is isometrically isomorphic to a generalized Day--James space.

Zuo's exact Ptolemy theory for planar normalized norms is formulated for absolute normalized norms and their associated one-variable convex functions. The present octagon is the canonical example introduced specifically to lie outside the absolute class, so those exactness theorems do not cover this claim.

A separate exact Euclidean-distortion calculation for the same octagon gives a closely related sharp Hilbert comparison, but a distortion statement supplies only an upper mechanism for Ptolemy. Exact Ptolemy equality additionally requires an attaining three-point configuration; the displayed triple supplies that lower bound. The proof here reconstructs the Hilbert comparison directly from the source-defined octagon.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the canonical piecewise-linear nonabsolute generalized Day--James octagon. It does not classify Ptolemy constants for arbitrary generalized Day--James norms. An unindexed equivalent computation under different affine terminology remains a residual bibliographic risk.
