# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. With \(H(x,y)^2=x^2+3^{-4/3}y^2\), the complete norm comparison reduces to
\[
F(t)=
\frac{\left((8+(1+t)^3)/8\right)^{2/3}}
{1+3^{-4/3}t^2}.
\]
The endpoint values agree, and
\[
R(t)=\frac{(1+t)^2}{t((1+t)^2+8)}
\]
is strictly decreasing because
\[
R'(t)=
-\frac{(t+1)(t^3+3t^2-5t+9)}
{t^2(t^2+2t+9)^2}<0.
\]
This proves a unique global comparison maximum. Hilbert Ptolemy gives the upper bound, and \((-1,0),(0,-\tau),(-1,-\tau)\) attains it exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The closest full-text source defines \(\mathrm{ces}_p^{(2)}\) for every \(1<p<\infty\) but computes a Cesàro Ptolemy constant only for \(p=2\). Searches covering \(\mathrm{ces}_3^{(2)}\), the exact decimal, Euclidean comparison, and Ptolemy terminology found no cubic formula. Same-object Euclidean distortion is a different invariant and does not imply the required Ptolemy lower bound.

## Value
PASS. The cubic section is the first odd nonlinear exponent after the classical quadratic case. Its extremal direction is off the midpoint and produces a new exact four-point invariant together with a unique algebraic contact equation and an explicit extremizing triple.

## Closest literature and limitations
Zuo (2012) is the decisive same-invariant source and was inspected in full; its Cesàro example is quadratic. Saejung (2010) treats other constants only for \(1<p\le2\), and Nikolova--Varošanec (2013) treats other geometric constants. The theorem is restricted to the real two-dimensional exponent-\(3\) Cesàro norm, and an obscure older equivalent formulation remains a residual bibliographic risk.
