# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Writing \(N_\lambda=\max\{E,F\}\) yields four active-branch cases. Same-branch numerator products obey Euclidean or one-dimensional Ptolemy directly. In mixed cases the Hilbert norm
\[
H(v)^2=
\frac{\lambda^2}{2}v_1^2+
\frac{\lambda^2}{2(\lambda^2-1)}v_2^2
\]
satisfies \(H\le N_\lambda\) globally and gives the sharp mixed comparison factor
\[
2\sqrt{1-\lambda^{-2}}.
\]
The branch-boundary triple
\[
(1,\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}),\quad
(1,-\sqrt{\lambda^2-1}),\quad
(2,0)
\]
attains the bound exactly.

## Originality
PASS, with one explicit access residual. Direct object/formula searches returned the known first regime and the same object's Euclidean-distance result, but not the second-regime Ptolemy formula. The 2012 and 2018 Ptolemy comparison theorems were checked by implication and do not cover the transformed norm's off-midpoint nonsymmetric associated function. The 2016 same-family paper computes different constants. A 2015 reconsideration remains access-limited and is retained as a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The result completes the natural phase split at \(\lambda=\sqrt2\) for a classical named Banach plane. The second branch tends to the square-plane value \(2\) and is supported by a new mixed-branch Hilbert certificate plus an exact extremal configuration.

## Closest literature and limitations
The original 1993 article defines the exact plane. Zuo (2012, 2018) supplies the closest general Ptolemy frameworks, and Yang--Yang (2016) treats the same generalized family for other constants. The new claim is strictly for \(\lambda>\sqrt2\); it does not reassert the already covered first regime or extend to general \(p\).
