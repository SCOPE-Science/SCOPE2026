# Scientific review: quantitative illumination of pair-sum polytopes

## Correctness — PASS
The result is proved for every integer \(d\ge3\). The vertex hull identity follows by splitting according to the largest coordinate; the complete vertex list consists of \(2d\) signed axis points and \(2^d\) sign corners. The Minkowski gauge is half the sum of the two largest coordinate magnitudes. Direct inspection of supporting facets gives necessary and sufficient **strict** illumination criteria. Their combinatorics restrict any one source to at most one axis point and two corners. The four possibilities for the number and types of illuminated vertices give strict gauge lower bounds \(1,2,2,3\), with the three-vertex case accounting for coincident/different special coordinates. Thus the total illumination cost exceeds the number of vertices, while radial near-vertex sources approach exactly that cost. The boundary-to-vertex reduction is proved for polytopes. The supplementary verifier tests \(8452\) discrete exact-arithmetic sources; it is not used to infer the universal claim. A remaining interpretive risk is confusion between the non-attained per-source inequality and the attained infimum equality; the proof explicitly separates them.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority risk
Kiss and de Wet's accessible complete 2012 paper introduces exactly the quantitative illumination functional and computes certain Platonic and Archimedean solids but not their Catalan dual, the rhombic dodecahedron. Its Conjecture 3.5 predicts a universal value \(3\cdot 2^{d-1}\). Bezdek and Khan's complete 2016 survey explicitly records the three-dimensional upper-bound conjecture \(12\) for this **same quantitative invariant** (Conjecture 4.2), which the exact rhombic-dodecahedron value \(14\) contradicts. Bezdek and Litvak's complete 2011 preprint studies the distinct vertex index and only gives inequalities connecting it with quantitative illumination. A separate publicly available full work on the ordinary illumination number of a rhombic-dodecahedron representative reports \(6\) directions, not the minimum sum of gauges. Thus neither ordinary directional illumination, the vertex index, nor the cuboctahedral polar calculation implies the statement. An earlier equivalent formulation in the illumination/dual-root-polytope literature remains a documented originality risk, not an established exclusion.

## Value — PASS
The formula is a complete all-dimensional classification for a canonical polar-root family, and it yields an explicit three-dimensional counterexample to the previously recorded quantitative upper-bound conjecture. Its sharp source-efficiency lemma is structurally motivated and distinguishes gauge cost from ordinary illumination number. This is not a numerical one-off or a routine table computation; the proof gives a uniform geometric mechanism and a sharp matching construction. The result does not determine the general supremum or settle the ordinary Hadwiger illumination problem.

## Scope and limits
The historical source evidence covers the full texts of the most relevant 2012, 2011 and 2016 sources and a publicly accessible full ordinary-illumination comparison. Historical priority cannot be certified from search alone; a 2006 paper on successive illumination variants was only accessible by metadata/abstract during these checks. No independent audit was performed.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
