# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The polynomial/symmetric-matrix dictionary is exact: the off-diagonal monomial coefficient is twice a matrix entry. Takagi and real spectral diagonalization reduce the convex coefficient norm maximum to symmetric unitary/orthogonal matrices. The ensuing exact invariant is the minimum squared diagonal of such matrices. Real odd dimensions are settled by the trace obstruction with an explicit constant-diagonal Fourier projection; real even dimensions have transposition-block extremizers. The complex three-dimensional cofactor inequality proves the nonzero minimum \(1/3\), attained by a Householder matrix. A symmetric zero-diagonal Fourier unitary in dimension five and direct sums give all remaining odd complex cases. The finite computations are supplementary; the proof holds for all dimensions.
