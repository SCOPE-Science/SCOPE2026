For real \(a,b\), the normalized exterior Laurent map \(z+a/z+b/z^2\) is exterior convex exactly on the sharp coefficient region \(-1\le a\le1\), \(|b|\le B(a)\), where \(B(a)=(1+a)/4\) for \(a\le0\) and \(B(a)=(1-a)\sqrt{1+a}/4\) for \(a\ge0\). This region is convex, so quadratic real-Laurent exterior convexity survives every coefficient interpolation despite the recent counterexample for the unrestricted class.
