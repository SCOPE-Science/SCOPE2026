# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The support-functional reduction is complete for a polygonal norm: exposed edges and the normal cones at vertices exhaust Birkhoff--James orthogonality. The edge case is bounded by the last unit-length point on a supporting edge plus the asymptotic bound \(\sqrt2\). At the representative vertex, the unit tangent cone splits into exactly two algebraic branches; the quotient rises to the intersection of the controlling norm pieces and then falls. The second branch reaches \(2\sqrt2-1\), and the displayed tangent-edge triple attains it.

## Originality
PASS. Searches for rectangular-constant aliases, regular-octagon evaluations, Birkhoff--James polyhedral formulas and the exact radical returned no covering record. The 2021 modern source defines and develops \(\mu_p\) but does not compute this octagon. The 2014/2017 polyhedral paper proves attainment and an extreme-point reduction without the final octagonal optimization. The full 1977 two-dimensional paper proves the Hilbert characterization rather than an exact polygon table. The closest current results concern different invariants or different normed planes.

## Value
PASS. The result gives an exact value of a classical orthogonality-sensitive invariant on the canonical regular-octagonal plane. It supplies a reproducible benchmark for comparing geometric constants on a standard uniformly nonsquare polygonal space and identifies a nontrivial equality value distinct in definition from nearby constants.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest general literature is Baronti--Casini--Papini on \(\mu_p\), Paul--Ghosh--Sain on finite-dimensional/polyhedral rectangular constants, and del Río--Benítez on the two-dimensional Hilbert endpoint. The calculation does not determine \(\mu_p(X_8)\) for \(p\ne1\) or classify all extremizers. An unindexed earlier special-case computation remains a residual bibliographic risk.
