# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The tangent line admits a one-dimensional coordinate in which both section endpoints are simple roots of one explicit implicit equation. Differentiating those roots gives the exact two-shift first variation. Fourier diagonalization then reduces kernel classification to the elementary equation \(2ka=b(2m+1)\), yielding the even-denominator criterion. A second implicit expansion gives the exact quadratic coefficient, and substitution of the fundamental resonant harmonic produces the stated negative \(\sin^2\) term. Strict convexity and containment of the inner disk follow by continuity from the concentric circle. The packaged symbolic checker independently reconstructs the endpoint coefficients and resonance examples.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-indexing risk.** The closest archive-era work formulates and studies the Barker–Larman determination problem but does not state this fixed-gap disk linearization. The materially inspected 2016 paper treats a different asymptotic regime and explicitly says that the planar ball problem leads, after differentiation, to coupled values and first and second derivatives at two points without an immediate solution. A later survey still records the tangent-chord uniqueness problem as open near the unit ball, while a 2026 polygonal theorem identifies stability and nonpolyhedral extensions as open directions. Exact-form, Fourier, resonance, circle-perturbation, and implication searches found no covering statement.

## Value
**PASS.** The result gives a complete local spectral diagnostic for a long-standing geometric-tomography problem. It identifies an infinite arithmetic family of singular radii, distinguishes injective and resonant linearizations, and shows that the simplest resonant directions are recovered at the next nonlinear order. This directly informs the stability and smooth-extension questions raised in the current literature without claiming a solution of the global reconstruction problem.
