# Exact stationary threshold-crossing laws for the SL8II and SL8III controlled flows
## Finding
Consider the two higher-degree controlled oscillators printed in Sheng–Li–Gao–Li–Chai. They can be written uniformly as
\[
\dot x=\kappa y^{2r+1}-y^2,\qquad
\dot y=a z^2+xy,\qquad
\dot z=b x^2+cxy,
\]
where \(a,b,c,\kappa>0\). SL8II is \(r=1\) with \(\kappa=p\), while SL8III is \(r=2\) with \(\kappa=q\). Define the positive control level
\[
h=\kappa^{-1/(2r-1)}.
\]
For every compactly supported invariant Borel probability measure \(\mu\),
\[
\int y^2(\kappa y^{2r-1}-1)\,d\mu=0,
\qquad
b\int x^2\,d\mu=ac\int z^2\,d\mu.
\]
If \(\mu\) is ergodic and is not an equilibrium measure, then
\[
\mu(\{y<h\})>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mu(\{y>h\})>0.
\]
Thus every nonconstant periodic orbit crosses \(y=h\). If, in addition, \(\mu\) is supported in \(y\ge0\), then
\[
\operatorname{RMS}_\mu(y)\le h,
\]
and equality forces an equilibrium measure. Therefore every non-equilibrium ergodic state supported in \(y\ge0\) has \(\operatorname{RMS}_\mu(y)<h\) even though its support must contain points with \(y>h\).

At the coefficients printed for SL8II, \(a=1/2\), \(b=11/10\), \(c=6/5\), so every compact stationary state obeys
\[
\operatorname{RMS}(x)=\sqrt{6/11}\,\operatorname{RMS}(z).
\]
At the coefficients printed for SL8III, \(a=1/2\), \(b=3/5\), \(c=6/5\), hence
\[
\operatorname{RMS}(x)=\operatorname{RMS}(z)
\]
for every compact stationary state, independently of the amplitude-control parameter \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope
The theorem concerns the autonomous differential equations exactly as printed in the 2023 source and compactly supported invariant Borel probability measures. The support-crossing conclusion is unconditional for non-equilibrium ergodic measures. The RMS ceiling for \(y\) additionally assumes support in the closed half-space \(y\ge0\); no claim is made here that every numerically displayed attractor in the source has that sign restriction.

The result does not assert existence or uniqueness of a chaotic attractor, does not reconstruct numerical Lyapunov exponents, and does not infer recurrence from a finite simulation. It supplies exact constraints that any compact recurrent statistical state must satisfy if it exists.

## Proof
For any invariant probability measure with compact support, the integral of the derivative of a smooth observable vanishes. Applying this to the coordinate \(x\) gives
\[
0=\int \dot x\,d\mu
 =\int y^2(\kappa y^{2r-1}-1)\,d\mu.
\]
The second identity is generated by the exact coboundary
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(cy-z)=ac z^2-bx^2.
\]
Integrating against \(\mu\) therefore gives
\[
b\int x^2\,d\mu=ac\int z^2\,d\mu.
\]

Now \(g(y)=\kappa y^{2r-1}-1\) is strictly increasing and has the unique real zero \(h>0\). Hence \(y^2g(y)\) is negative for \(y<h\) except at \(y=0\), positive for \(y>h\), and zero only at \(y=0\) or \(y=h\). If an ergodic invariant measure were supported in \(y\ge h\), the first stationary identity would force \(y=h\) almost everywhere. If it were supported in \(y\le h\), that identity would force \(y\in\{0,h\}\) almost everywhere. Since an orbit carried by a compact ergodic measure is invariant and \(y(t)\) is continuous, it must then stay on one of the constant levels \(y=0\) or \(y=h\).

On \(y=0\), invariance requires \(\dot y=a z^2=0\), so \(z=0\); then invariance of \(z=0\) gives \(\dot z=b x^2=0\), hence \(x=0\). Thus only the origin remains. On \(y=h\), invariance requires \(a z^2+xh=0\). Along such an invariant trajectory, differentiating this constraint and using \(\dot y=0\) yields \(2az\dot z+h\dot x=0\). Because \(h\) is a zero of \(\dot x=h^2(\kappa h^{2r-1}-1)\), this reduces to \(z\dot z=0\). If \(z=0\), then \(x=0\) and the point is an equilibrium. If \(\dot z=0\), together with \(\dot y=0\) and \(\dot x=0\), the point is again an equilibrium. Therefore a non-equilibrium ergodic measure cannot be supported entirely on one side of \(h\), proving the strict two-sided support statement.

Assume now that \(y\ge0\) on the support. The first stationary identity becomes
\[
\kappa\int y^{2r+1}\,d\mu=\int y^2\,d\mu.
\]
Monotonicity of \(L^p\) norms on a probability space gives
\[
\int y^{2r+1}\,d\mu
\ge
\left(\int y^2\,d\mu\right)^{(2r+1)/2}.
\]
If \(M=\int y^2\,d\mu>0\), then
\[
M^{(2r-1)/2}\le\kappa^{-1},
\qquad
\sqrt M\le\kappa^{-1/(2r-1)}=h.
\]
Equality in the norm comparison forces \(y\) to be constant almost everywhere; the invariant-level argument above then forces an equilibrium measure. The stated strict inequality follows for every non-equilibrium ergodic measure supported in \(y\ge0\).

## Verification
The two identities are algebraic consequences of the printed vector field, not numerical conjectures. The included `verify.py` checks the exact polynomial cancellations with rational arithmetic at independent rational states and verifies the two source coefficient ratios \(ac/b=6/11\) and \(ac/b=1\). Its output is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks validate the implementation of the formulas; the general theorem is established by the symbolic argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
The 2023 source introduces the switchable SL8I/SL8II/SL8III family and emphasizes amplitude and frequency control. For SL8II it reports inverse amplitude scaling with \(p\); for SL8III it reports inverse-cube-root amplitude scaling with \(q\). The theorem above converts those control levels into exact stationary recurrence thresholds and moment identities that hold for every compact invariant measure, rather than for selected simulated trajectories.

The closest published database result found concerns the same paper's lower-degree SL8I system and proves a second-moment envelope there. That result does not imply the present theorem: SL8II and SL8III have different nonlinear \(x\)-equations, different threshold polynomials, and the present support-crossing law is tied to \(y^2(\kappa y^{2r-1}-1)\). The 2014 Li–Sprott precursor supplies the nonquadratic SL8 construction and discusses scaling transformations, but the inspected material does not state these invariant-measure identities or the mandatory crossing of \(y=h\).

## Limitations
The source-specific scientific risk is that an equivalent invariant-measure formulation could exist under different notation outside the inspected literature. The originality search therefore supports, but cannot logically prove, global uniqueness of the result. The half-space RMS ceiling is conditional on support in \(y\ge0\); the unconditional conclusions are the exact two stationary identities and the two-sided support crossing for non-equilibrium ergodic measures.
