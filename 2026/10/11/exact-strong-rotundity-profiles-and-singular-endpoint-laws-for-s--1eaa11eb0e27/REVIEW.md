# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. By direct norm comparison, every point \(z=(t,y)\) of the unit ball has distance \(1-t\) from \(f^{-1}(\{1\})\), and the minimum is attained by replacing its scalar coordinate by one. For finite \(p\), the maximum displacement in a slice is exactly the one-dimensional envelope \(G_p(d)^{1/p}\), attained with a unit transverse vector. The scalar envelope is nondecreasing at \(p=1\) and strictly increasing at \(1<p<\infty\); its inverse gives the claimed index. For \(p=\infty\), the slice radius is exactly \(\max\{d,1\}\). Boundary values, the one-half pivot and the small-scale exponent follow algebraically. The proof works for arbitrary nonzero real Banach \(Y\).

## Originality
PASS for the non-Hilbert exact phase law, subject to the residual literature risk below. The defining 2023 primary full text was inspected at its introduction, Definition 2.1, Theorems 2.3, 2.4 and 2.6 and concluding sections. It states the \(p=2\) special case, an \(\ell_\infty\) coordinate-vector value at \(\epsilon=2\), and qualitative stereographic projection for \(p>1\), but not the sharp strong-rotundity profile for \(p\ne2\). The 2024 full-text follow-up was inspected at its index/core characterization results, which do not supply the exponent law. Exact-formula and alias searches found no covering record. Related published computations of other pointwise geometric constants do not state the new sharp family; a general support-functional reduction by itself also does not evaluate the present family. Absolute novelty cannot be certified by searches alone.

## Value
PASS. This computes a naturally defined, recently introduced geometric invariant for all scalar \(\ell_p\)-summand vectors, uniformly over arbitrary nonzero transverse Banach spaces. In addition to determining the entire radius function, it detects the precise strong-exposure power \(p\), a universal pivot at unit separation, a discontinuity of the endpoint value at \(p=1\), and a different singular phenomenon at \(p=\infty\). Those boundary and scaling features provide benchmarks not contained in the previously known Hilbert case.

## Closest literature and limitations
García-Pacheco (2023), Theorem 2.3, already gives \(\epsilon^2/2\) for \(p=2\); Proposition 2.2 covers the \(\epsilon=2\) endpoint at one \(\ell_\infty\) coordinate vector; Theorem 2.6 treats stereographic projection of general \(L^p\)-summand vectors instead of the index. Campos-Jiménez and García-Pacheco (2024), Theorems 3.4--3.5, address the core and endpoint criteria without deriving the full profile. This finding is local to a canonical scalar-summand support pair and makes no assertion about the global index or nonsummand vectors. Unindexed equivalent formulas remain a residual originality risk.
