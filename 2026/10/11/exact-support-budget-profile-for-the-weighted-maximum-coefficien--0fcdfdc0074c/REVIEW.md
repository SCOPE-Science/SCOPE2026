# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The focal paper proves, for each multi-index \(\alpha\), both the exact coefficient-functional norm and the exact \(\ell_t\)-monomial maximum. Multiplying those two sharp formulas reduces the support-capped problem to minimizing \(\alpha^\alpha\) among positive integer compositions of \(m\) using at most \(k=\min\{s,m\}\) parts. Strict splitting forces exactly \(k\) parts and strict convex smoothing forces the balanced composition. The corresponding monomial and norming diagonal vector attain equality, including the endpoint \(t=\infty\). The branch \(t\le p\) is exactly \(1\), attained by a pure power.

## Originality
PASS with a stated residual risk. The focal Proposition 4.4 gives the unrestricted sharp constant but does not determine the support-budget profile of the diagonal vector. The closest support-sensitive 2026 Bohnenblust--Hille paper restricts variables occurring inside active monomials and studies a coefficient-sum norm, not sparse diagonal weights in the maximum functional. Implication-level searches for sparse weights, support cardinality, balanced exponent patterns, and equivalent monomial formulations found no covering statement.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a complete exact finite-sparsity profile for a natural parameter that is already central to the focal paper's sparse/weighted framework. It identifies the extremal exponent geometry, proves a strict staircase through all support budgets up to the degree, and recovers the published unrestricted constant as the terminal value. This is a structural finite-cutoff classification rather than a numerical recomputation or an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2610.01500v1, whose exact unrestricted weighted maximum is a premise. DOI:10.1007/s00574-026-00527-1 is close in its use of bounded monomial support but imposes the support restriction on the polynomial rather than on the diagonal weight. The final theorem does not address finite coefficient exponents or coefficient-support sparsity. An older equivalent statement under monomial-convergence language remains possible.
