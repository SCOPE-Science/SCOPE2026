# Exact two-ball covering of regular simplices and a parity rigidity transition

## Finding

For a Euclidean regular \(d\)-simplex \(S\) of common edge length \(s>0\), let \(\rho_2(S)\) be the minimum \(r\) for which there exist two centers \(c_1,c_2\in\mathbb R^d\) such that
\[
S\subseteq \overline B(c_1,r)\cup\overline B(c_2,r).
\]
For every integer \(d\ge3\), with \(k=\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil\),
\[
\boxed{\rho_2(S)=s\sqrt{\frac{k-1}{2k}}.}
\]
This value is also the optimal two-center radius of the finite vertex set. The correspondence with the whole convex body is substantive: a vertex cover by balls does not in general cover the polytope spanned by those vertices.

Every regular simplex admits an optimal cover whose centers are the centroids of complementary vertex subsets of cardinalities \(k\) and \(d+1-k\). Moreover the optimal-center set has a parity transition:

- If \(d\ge3\) is odd, then *every* unordered pair of optimal centers consists of centroids of complementary subsets of \(k=(d+1)/2\) vertices. All such pairs are distinct, and their number is precisely \(\frac12\binom{d+1}{(d+1)/2}\).
- If \(d\ge4\) is even, then at least one center of every optimal covering is the centroid of a \(k\)-vertex face. There are uncountably many optimal pairs: after fixing the centroid of any \(k\)-vertex subset, the other center can vary through the closed ball of radius
\[
\varepsilon_{d}=s\left(\sqrt{\frac{k-1}{2k}}-\sqrt{\frac{k-2}{2(k-1)}}\right)>0
\]
centered at the centroid of the complementary \((k-1)\)-vertex subset. No complete classification of *all* optimal pairs for even \(d\) is asserted.

For illustration, the sharp radius is \(s/2\) in dimension \(3\), \(s/\sqrt3\) in dimensions \(4\) and \(5\), and \(s\sqrt{3/8}\) in dimensions \(6\) and \(7\). The radius divided by \(s\) tends to \(1/\sqrt2\) as \(d\to\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope

The dimension is an integer \(d\ge3\). A regular simplex has \(N=d+1\) affinely independent vertices \(v_1,\dots,v_N\), with \(\|v_i-v_j\|=s\) for distinct indices. The object being covered is the entire closed convex hull \(S=\operatorname{conv}(v_1,\dots,v_N)\), including its interior, not merely the vertices or boundary. Each covering ball is closed and has the same radius, and its center is unconstrained in Euclidean space. An unordered pair of centers is counted once; interchanging the two balls has no effect.

For a nonempty vertex subset \(I\) of size \(q\), set \(a_I=q^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}v_i\) and
\[
r_q=s\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{2q}}.
\]
The quantity \(r_q\) is the smallest-enclosing-ball radius of the \(q\) selected vertices. In the even-dimensional continuum statement \(q=k-1\ge2\).

## Proof

**Step 1: a sharp radius and unique center for a face's vertices.** For any \(q\)-vertex subset \(I\), the variance identity gives, for every Euclidean center \(c\),
\[
\frac1q\sum_{i\in I}\|c-v_i\|^2
 =\|c-a_I\|^2+\frac1q\sum_{i\in I}\|v_i-a_I\|^2
 =\|c-a_I\|^2+\frac{s^2(q-1)}{2q}.
\]
The final equality follows by averaging the \(q(q-1)\) ordered pairwise squared distances. Hence a ball containing those \(q\) vertices has radius at least \(r_q\), with equality possible only at \(c=a_I\). Conversely all \(q\) selected vertices are at distance exactly \(r_q\) from \(a_I\), so this lower bound is achieved. The sequence \(r_q\) is strictly increasing for positive integers \(q\).

**Step 2: vertex counting forces the proposed global lower bound.** In any two-ball cover of the entire simplex, at least one of the two balls contains at least \(k=\lceil N/2\rceil\) of its \(N\) vertices. Step 1 forces its radius to be at least \(r_k\), so \(\rho_2(S)\ge r_k\). This remains true even when ball centers are outside the affine hull.

**Step 3: a complete two-ball cover, not just a vertex cover.** Partition the vertex indices into \(A\) and \(B\), with \(|A|=k\) and \(|B|=\ell=N-k\le k\). Let \(a=a_A\), \(b=a_B\), and write any \(x\in S\) uniquely as \(x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_i v_i\), where \(\lambda_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\). Cut \(S\) into two convex polytopes at \(\sum_{i\in A}\lambda_i=1/2\). The upper cell \(S_A\) is the convex hull of the vertices \(v_i\) for \(i\in A\), together with the cross-edge midpoints \(m_{ij}=(v_i+v_j)/2\) for \(i\in A,j\in B\). The lower cell \(S_B\) is analogously generated by the vertices of \(B\) and the same cross-edge midpoints. Indeed, the cutting hyperplane changes sign only on edges joining \(A\) to \(B\), meeting each at its midpoint.

The vertex distances satisfy \(\|v_i-a\|=r_k\) for \(i\in A\) and \(\|v_j-b\|=r_\ell\le r_k\) for \(j\in B\). For every cross-edge midpoint,
\[
\boxed{\|m_{ij}-a\|^2=\|m_{ij}-b\|^2=\frac{s^2}{4}.}
\]
To verify this directly, expand \(m_{ij}-a=\frac12(v_j-a)+\frac12(v_i-a)\), or use the barycentric identity
\[
\left\|\sum_i (\lambda_i-\mu_i)v_i\right\|^2
=\frac{s^2}{2}\sum_i(\lambda_i-\mu_i)^2
\qquad\text{whenever }\sum_i\lambda_i=\sum_i\mu_i=1.
\]
The squared deviations of the cross-edge midpoint's barycentric weights from the uniform weights on \(A\) sum to \(1/2\), independently of \(k\), and likewise on \(B\). Since \(k\ge2\), \(s/2\le r_k\); and since \(\ell\ge2\) for \(d\ge3\), also \(s/2\le r_\ell\). By convexity of Euclidean balls, all of \(S_A\) belongs to \(\overline B(a,r_k)\) and all of \(S_B\) belongs to \(\overline B(b,r_\ell)\subseteq\overline B(b,r_k)\). Together with Step 2 this proves \(\rho_2(S)=r_k\).

**Step 4: exact rigidity in odd dimensions.** Suppose \(d\) is odd, so \(N=2k\). At optimal radius \(r_k\), Step 1 and strict monotonicity show that each covering ball contains at most \(k\) vertices. Since their union must contain all \(2k\) vertices, each contains exactly \(k\) and their vertex sets are disjoint. Step 1 then forces their centers to be the two complementary \(k\)-vertex centroids. Conversely Step 3 constructs such a cover for every balanced partition. Distinct balanced subsets give distinct centroids because the simplex vertices are affinely independent. Quotienting by swapping the two subsets gives exactly \(\frac12\binom{2k}{k}\) optimal unordered pairs.

**Step 5: positive-dimensional optimality in even dimensions.** Now suppose \(d\ge4\) is even, so \(N=2k-1\) and \(\ell=k-1\ge2\). The lower bound in Step 2 still forces the center of at least one ball to be the centroid of a \(k\)-vertex set. For every balanced partition, the same proof in Step 3 gives the *stronger* inclusion \(S_B\subseteq\overline B(b,r_{k-1})\). Therefore, if \(b'\) satisfies \(\|b'-b\|\le r_k-r_{k-1}\), the triangle inequality gives \(S_B\subseteq\overline B(b',r_k)\). The other cell remains in \(\overline B(a,r_k)\), so \((a,b')\) is optimal for every such \(b'\). The permitted radius is strictly positive because \(r_{k-1}<r_k\). This proves the asserted continuum and concludes the theorem.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` independently evaluates the unit-edge regular-simplex distance form using exact rational barycentric coordinates. It reconstructs the cut-cell vertex lists and verifies both enclosing-ball inequalities for representative partitions through dimension \(40\), as well as all balanced partitions with up to \(10\) vertices. It checks the positivity of the even-dimensional center perturbation by explicit rational displacements, counts complementary subset pairs for small odd-dimensional examples, and performs deterministic interior-point diagnostics. Execute with `python3 verify.py`; the expected final line begins `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite checks are diagnostics; Steps 1–5 prove the statements for every integer dimension \(d\ge3\). No infinite assertion is inferred from enumeration or sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Agarwal, Ben Avraham and Sharir study the two-center problem for finite sets of points in three-dimensional Euclidean space, with general-purpose randomized algorithms. They do not analyze the *entire* regular simplex or derive this dimension-uniform closed formula and optimizer structure. Their preprint was first submitted on 2010-12-13 [1]. Böröczky Jr., Fábián and Wintsche determine two-ball coverings of the *crosspolytope* [2]. In their dimension-three example, vertex-only and full-body covering differ, illustrating why the transition from vertex covering to convex-body covering in the present theorem cannot be assumed without the cut-cell proof.

The familiar circumradius formula for a regular simplex face is used as a classical input and is not claimed as original. The asserted contribution is its sharp coupling with the whole-simplex two-ball partition and with the parity-dependent optimizer classification; no broader characterization of arbitrary polytopes is claimed.

## Limitations

The equal-radius, two-ball theorem concerns Euclidean *regular simplices*, not arbitrary simplices, other norms, more than two balls, or all finite vertex sets. For even \(d\), the construction gives an explicit continuum of optimal centers and a forced center, but it does not classify every optimal pair. A published mathematical treatment with different terminology, or a result hidden in earlier covering literature, could overlap the theorem; the source comparisons are limited to the works identified below and related accessible records. Independent external validation has not been performed.
