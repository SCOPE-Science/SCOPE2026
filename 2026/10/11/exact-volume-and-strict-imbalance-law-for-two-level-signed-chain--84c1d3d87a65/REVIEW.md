# Scientific review: two-level signed-chain volume and imbalance

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns the explicit full-dimensional halfspace polytope \(\mathcal C_{p,q}\), for all integers \(p,q\ge1\), in the inherited coordinate Euclidean measure. The proof partitions feasible coordinate tuples according to their total range. The range-at-most-one region contributes \(p+q+1\); beyond that range, the extreme coordinate pair must be entirely in one of the two parts, producing the two displayed integrals. The cases are exhaustive up to zero-measure ties, and their Jacobian and boundary factors are given explicitly. An independent conditional-extrema integration matches the result in \(36\) parameter cases using exact rationals. The strict imbalance proof is a positive-integrand hyperbolic identity with all signs checked; the asymptotic follows from an explicit gamma-integral limit and dominated convergence. Seven low-dimensional convex-hull reconstructions passed. Computations supplement rather than replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Beck and Hlavacek, *Signed Poset Polytopes*, arXiv:2311.04409v1, first posted 2023-11-08, introduce the signed order/chain constructions and analyze antichains, reflexivity, and Ehrhart information. Sections 1–2 and 6 were inspected, including their two-element chain inequalities. They do not give the present two-parameter volume formula, strict fixed-dimension imbalance theorem, or fixed-part asymptotic. Gordon and Petrov, arXiv:1608.06848v1, consider the broader Lipschitz-polytope face theory, not this volume evaluation; D’Alì, Delucchi, and Michałek (2022) compare Lipschitz polytopes with symmetric edge polytopes and study other volume and lattice invariants. Tsuchiya, arXiv:1512.08332v2, proves a volume theorem for convex hulls of two ordinary chain polytopes (a mathematically different construction). Searches also considered the rooted Lipschitz model on a three-part complete multipartite graph, the cube-with-cross-difference formulation, broader coverage, and source-specific volume tables. No checked theorem was found whose statement implies all three present conclusions. A differently named older formula for these bounded Lipschitz bodies is a genuine remaining novelty risk; lack of a search hit is not treated as proof of novelty. The originating signed-chain definition has a textual ambiguity in its multistep chain condition; the final claim is self-contained because it explicitly defines its body by inequalities.

## Value — PASS
The object is a natural two-parameter family coming from complete height-two posets and, independently, rooted graph Lipschitz geometry. Euclidean volume is an intrinsic quantitative invariant of the specified coordinate realization, not determined by the common Gorenstein/reflexive context. The theorem gives exact all-dimensional values, strict extremizers under redistribution between the two levels, and an informative sharply normalized high-imbalance asymptotic. For example, \(\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal C_{2,2})=6\) differs from the signed order-polytope volume \(8/3\), emphasizing the need for a separate signed-chain volume calculation. This is a structural family statement, not an isolated small-case recomputation. It does not claim a general theorem for arbitrary signed posets, nor an Ehrhart formula.
