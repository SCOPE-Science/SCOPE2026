# Exact von Neumann–Jordan constant of the three-coordinate James section
## Finding
Let \(J_3\) be the real three-dimensional coordinate section generated by three successive vectors of the classical James basis. Equivalently, by the branch identification in Argyros--González--Motakis, the same space occurs on any three successive nodes of a branch of \(JT\). For \(z=(a,b,c)\),
\[
\|z\|_{J_3}^2=\max\left\{a^2+b^2+c^2,(a+b)^2+c^2,a^2+(b+c)^2,(a+b+c)^2\right\}.
\]
Its von Neumann--Jordan constant is
\[
C_{\mathrm{NJ}}(J_3)=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The value is attained by \(x=(0,\sqrt2,0)\) and \(y=(-1,0,1)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are real. The von Neumann--Jordan constant is
\[
C_{\mathrm{NJ}}(X)=\sup_{(x,y)\ne(0,0)}
\frac{\|x+y\|^2+\|x-y\|^2}{2(\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2)}.
\]
The claim concerns only the canonical three-coordinate James section \(J_3\), not the full infinite-dimensional James space and not arbitrary three-dimensional subspaces of \(J\) or \(JT\).

## Proof
Write \(q_0(a,b,c)=a^2+b^2+c^2\), and let
\[
h_0=0,\qquad h_1=ab,\qquad h_2=bc,\qquad h_3=ab+ac+bc.
\]
Then
\[
\|z\|_{J_3}^2=q_0(z)+2m(z),\qquad m(z)=\max\{h_0(z),h_1(z),h_2(z),h_3(z)\}.
\]
For \(x,y\in J_3\), put
\[
E=\|x\|_2^2+\|y\|_2^2,\qquad M=m(x)+m(y),\qquad u=x+y,\quad v=x-y.
\]
The parallelogram quotient becomes
\[
\frac{\|u\|_{J_3}^2+\|v\|_{J_3}^2}{2(\|x\|_{J_3}^2+\|y\|_{J_3}^2)}
=\frac{E+m(u)+m(v)}{E+2M}.
\]
Represent \(h_i(z)=z^TH_i z\) with real symmetric matrices. Direct diagonalization gives
\[
\|H_i-H_j\|_{2\to2}\le\frac1{\sqrt2}
\]
for every pair except \(\{i,j\}=\{0,3\}\). Therefore, if \(h_i(u)=m(u)\) and \(h_j(v)=m(v)\) and the pair is nonexceptional,
\[
\begin{aligned}
m(u)+m(v)
&=h_i(x)+h_j(x)+h_i(y)+h_j(y)+2x^T(H_i-H_j)y\\
&\le 2M+\frac{E}{\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the exceptional pair, \(H_3\) has eigenvalues \(1,-1/2,-1/2\). We use the elementary estimate
\[
2|x^TH_3y|\le\frac{E}{\sqrt2}+\sqrt2\bigl(h_3(x)_++h_3(y)_+\bigr).
\]
To verify it, let \(e=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\), write \(x=ae+u\) and \(y=be+v\) with \(u,v\perp e\), and set \(A=|a|\), \(B=|b|\), \(U=\|u\|_2\), \(V=\|v\|_2\). Then
\[
2|x^TH_3y|\le2AB+UV.
\]
For \(A,U\ge0\), define
\[
g(A,U)=\frac{A^2+U^2}{\sqrt2}+\sqrt2\left(A^2-\frac{U^2}{2}\right)_+.
\]
If \(A^2\le U^2/2\), direct comparison at the endpoint \(A^2=U^2/2\) gives \(g(A,U)\ge A^2+U^2/2\); if \(A^2\ge U^2/2\), then \(g(A,U)=3A^2/\sqrt2\ge A^2+U^2/2\). Hence
\[
g(A,U)+g(B,V)\ge A^2+B^2+\frac{U^2+V^2}{2}\ge2AB+UV,
\]
which proves the exceptional estimate. Since \(h_3(z)_+\le m(z)\), every maximizing pair satisfies
\[
m(u)+m(v)\le\frac{E}{\sqrt2}+(2+\sqrt2)M.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{E+m(u)+m(v)}{E+2M}
\le1+\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]
For sharpness, take \(x=(0,\sqrt2,0)\) and \(y=(-1,0,1)\). Then \(\|x\|_{J_3}^2=\|y\|_{J_3}^2=2\) while
\[
\|x+y\|_{J_3}^2=\|x-y\|_{J_3}^2=4+2\sqrt2.
\]
The resulting quotient is exactly \(1+1/\sqrt2\).

## Verification
The four quadratic forms are exactly the four contiguous interval partitions of three successive James coordinates: three singletons, the first adjacent pair plus the last singleton, the first singleton plus the last adjacent pair, and the full interval. The matrix differences used above have operator norms \(0\), \(1/2\), or \(1/\sqrt2\), except \(H_3-H_0\), whose spectrum is \(\{1,-1/2,-1/2\}\) and is handled separately. Substitution of the displayed extremizing pair gives the stated equality exactly. No numerical search, finite sampling, or unproved computational certificate is used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Argyros--González--Motakis identify every branch basis of \(JT\) isometrically with the classical James basis and study complemented copies of \(J\) and \(\ell_2\) in subspaces of \(JT\). Their paper does not state or imply the exact von Neumann--Jordan constant of the canonical three-coordinate James section. General literature on von Neumann--Jordan constants supplies the invariant and computations for other classical families, but the targeted comparisons inspected did not contain this \(J_3\) formula. Nearby indexed results on Day--James planes and on James-type constants of \(\ell_4\) concern different spaces or different invariants.

## Limitations
The theorem does not determine \(C_{\mathrm{NJ}}(J)\), does not classify extremizing pairs beyond the exhibited one, and does not address longer finite James sections. A residual literature risk remains that an older calculation of this exact three-coordinate section is indexed under different James-space or finite-section terminology.
