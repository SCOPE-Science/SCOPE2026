# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The weighted invariant is reduced exactly to a radius profile. Dihedral symmetry reduces the decagonal boundary to one half-edge for the first vector and five edge classes for the second. The substitution \(w=t\beta\) makes every support value affine, and the two candidate envelopes are affine on their respective \(t\)-intervals. The exact bundled certificate exhausts all support-pair maxima over the continuum in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), while two explicit unit pairs attain the two branches. The proof therefore establishes both upper bounds and sharpness without relying on a finite grid.

## Originality
PASS. The 2021 defining full text was inspected at the generalized-James definition, midpoint relation, general estimates and Hilbert comparison. It contains no affine-regular decagonal computation. Searches covered the defining notation, the equivalent radius formulation, polygon aliases and the midpoint value. The closest same-invariant comparisons are an exact \(\ell_p\) profile and a prior regular-octagon profile; neither implies the decagonal two-branch law. The ordinary decagonal James endpoint is treated as known background rather than as the novelty claim.

## Value
PASS. The finding determines a full continuous weighted profile and exposes a sharp change of extremizer at the intrinsic golden-ratio threshold \(s=(\sqrt5-1)/2\). This gives substantially more information than the midpoint James constant and supplies an exact non-Hilbert polygonal benchmark for the generalized coefficient.

## Closest literature and limitations
Liu, Sarfraz and Li introduced the coefficient in *Demonstratio Mathematica* 54 (2021), 299--310. Exact computations for real \(\ell_p\) and for the regular octagonal plane concern different space families. The present result is specific to the affine-regular decagon; it does not classify every maximizing pair or all regular polygons.
