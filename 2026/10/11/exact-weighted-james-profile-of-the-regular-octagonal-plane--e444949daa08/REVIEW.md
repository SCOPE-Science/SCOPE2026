# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The defining 2021 article gives the exact universal upper estimate \(J(\lambda,X)\le\lambda J(X)+|2\lambda-1|\). On \(0<\lambda\le1/2\), the published regular-octagon value \(J(X_8)=\sqrt2\) converts it to \(1-(2-\sqrt2)\lambda\). The proposed adjacent-vertex pair attains exactly that value: every active norm facet is checked algebraically, including the only absolute-value split at \(\lambda=1-1/\sqrt2\). Parameter symmetry covers the other half. The bundled exact symbolic replay passes, but the infinite statement is proved analytically rather than by sampling.

## Originality
PASS. The defining paper was inspected through Section 5, including Definition 5.1, Theorem 5.2 and the surrounding examples/properties; it supplies bounds but no exact regular-octagon weighted profile. The closest later full-text octagon paper computes different Euler--Lagrange sum-of-squares constants. Database searches using the invariant name, exact affine expression, octagon aliases, parameter symmetry and broader James-constant terminology found no covering statement. A published exact \(\ell_p\) profile is statement-wise distinct and does not contain the regular-octagonal norm as a special case.

## Value
PASS. The regular octagon is a canonical two-dimensional non-Hilbert space with the same classical James constant \(\sqrt2\) as the Euclidean plane. The complete weighted profile is therefore a natural stress test for how much information the one-number James constant loses. It shows sharpness of the source paper's universal \(J(X)\)-based estimate for every parameter and produces a strict profile separation from Hilbert geometry away from the symmetric parameter. This is a complete benchmark result, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

Closest literature and limitations: Liu--Sarfraz--Li (2021), DOI 10.1515/dema-2021-0033, defines the invariant and proves the upper inequality used in the proof. Komuro--Saito--Tanaka establishes the regular octagon as a \(J=\sqrt2\) model. Yang--Li--Yang (2024), DOI 10.2298/FIL2405583Y, computes distinct regular-octagon Euler--Lagrange constants. Residual risk remains for unindexed or differently phrased literature; the theorem is only for the stated real octagonal plane.
