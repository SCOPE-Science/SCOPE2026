The two-ball dilation factor \(\rho_2(K)\) reaches its universal ceiling \(3\) precisely when two points at \(K\)-distance at least \(1\) share a boundary point through exposed faces. This gives a sharp transition at \(a=\sqrt3/2\) for strip-clipped disks, including a region where the known modulus-of-convexity bound detects no improvement, and a twelve-sided cutoff for regular even polygons.
