# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. In the inradius-one normalization, the regular \(4n\)-gon lies between the Euclidean unit disk and the disk of radius \(\sec(\pi/(4n))\). Hence the smaller polygonal chord is bounded by the smaller Euclidean chord, the parallelogram identity bounds that by the Euclidean root-sum-square of the two vectors, and the outer Euclidean comparison gives \(\sqrt2\sec(\pi/(4n))\). For equality, two orthogonal polygon vertices are scaled in the intrinsic ratio \(\tan(\pi/(4n))\); their sum and difference land exactly on adjacent facet-normal rays, where the polygonal and Euclidean norms coincide. The normalization and both chord values check exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2022 article was inspected through the full \(J_4\) section, including its equivalent parameterization, general upper bounds, two exact example families, and conclusion. It contains no regular-polygon family formula. published-finding corpus searches using the exact candidate formula, \(J_4\) notation, regular-\(4n\)-gon aliases, and the Takahashi--Kato comparison found no covering record. The own ledger contains only the \(n=2\) octagon specialization and different regular-polygon invariants. A 2014 source on the comparison constant \(A(X)\) predates \(J_4\); only its public abstract and metadata were available, so a matching upper formula there remains a literature risk but cannot itself cover the later-defined equality theorem.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural complete infinite-family computation, not a routine parameter substitution. It reveals the exact geometric saturation mechanism, identifies the optimizing norm ratio \(\tan(\pi/(4n))\), and gives the precise quadratic approach to the Hilbert endpoint. It also replaces the prior octagon-specific smoothness calculation by a uniform argument valid for every regular polygon with side count divisible by four.

## Closest literature and limitations
Chen, Yang, Liu and Li introduced \(J_4\) in *Symmetry* 14 (2022), article 405 and computed \(L_p\) spaces and the Day--James hexagon. The closest own result is the regular-octagon value, recovered here at \(n=2\). Public regular-polygon search hits concern other invariants. The theorem does not cover regular \((4n+2)\)-gons and does not classify every maximizing pair.
