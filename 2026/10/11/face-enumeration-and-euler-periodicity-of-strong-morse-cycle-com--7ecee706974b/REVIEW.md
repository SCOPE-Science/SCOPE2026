# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the one-dimensional cycle with at least three vertices, every injective vertex filtration is encoded by a nonconstant cyclic sign assignment, and every such assignment admits a topological ordering. The local descending link is a single point precisely where consecutive signs agree. A chosen subset of incidence pairs therefore corresponds exactly to a three-symbol cyclic word subject to adjacent-color compatibility and exclusion of monochromatic marked vertex covers. A weighted cyclic transfer count gives the claimed full face polynomial. The eigenvalue calculation at \(t=-1\) gives the exact reduced Euler characteristic. Direct enumeration from all vertex permutations for \(3\le n\le8\) agrees with local-rule and transfer counts; symbolic counts were also checked to \(n=100\). The proof, rather than finite tests, supports the unbounded quantifier.

## Originality — PASS

The closest article is Knudson and Owens–White [1], whose Section 8 computes the pure-subcomplex homotopy type and a small-range homology table for the full cycle complex. Its top-face count does not determine the full nonpure face vector. Chari and Joswig [2] give an all-length face formula for the larger **ordinary** Morse complex, not this filtration-restricted strong complex. Fernández [3] establishes strong filtration constructions, but no such enumerator. Searches for complete face polynomials, periodic Euler characteristics, equivalent colored-word constructions, and transfer-matrix enumerations did not identify a dominating result. The possibility of an unindexed implicit or equivalent formula remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The full nonpure strong matching complex has intricate homology even at moderate cycle lengths, and the recent literature could compute its homology only through \(n=21\). The theorem determines a natural complete combinatorial invariant, the entire face polynomial, for every length and identifies an unexpected \(12\)-periodic Euler constraint on future homology calculations. The count at \(n=22\) is a first value beyond the published homology-computation range, not an arbitrary slice. It does not purport to compute the homology itself.
