# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Write the non-torsion fiber class as \(F=mA\) in the free intersection lattice with \(A\) primitive. Since \(F^2=0\), integrality gives \(A^2=0\). The adapted almost-complex structure has \(\langle c_1,F\rangle=2-2g\), while \(c_1\) is characteristic because \(c_1\bmod2=w_2\). Thus \(\langle c_1,A\rangle\) is even, and division by \(2\) gives \(m\mid(g-1)\). Corollary 8 of the source then gives exactly the stated multisection consequences. No step relies on finite sampling.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source defines \(m(f)\), proves that its multiples are exactly the multisection degrees, and explicitly asks whether \(m(f)\mid2\); it does not state \(m(f)\mid(g-1)\). Its full text was inspected at the definition, Corollary 8, and Question 20, and searched for the genus-minus-one and characteristic-parity formulations. The earlier Baykur--Hamada divisibility proposition and older genus-two multisection examples were also inspected at the relevant statements. Targeted semantic and web searches found no implication covering the theorem. Residual risk remains for unindexed folklore using different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The result is a structural bound on the exact obstruction introduced by a current paper, not an arbitrary example. It gives a uniform natural-degree multisection in every genus \(g\ge3\) and completely answers the paper's explicit \(2\)-section question for genera \(2\) and \(3\). It also isolates precisely what remains unresolved in higher genus.

## Closest literature and limitations
The nearest source is arXiv:2610.01776v1, especially Corollary 8 and Question 20. *Lefschetz fibrations with arbitrary signature* supplies a monodromy computation of fiber divisibility, while Baykur--Hayano and Akhmedov--Monden provide specific low-genus multisection examples. None of the inspected statements implies the universal characteristic-parity bound. The theorem does not settle \(2\)-sections for \(g\ge4\), does not prove general primitivity, and does not cover achiral fibrations.
