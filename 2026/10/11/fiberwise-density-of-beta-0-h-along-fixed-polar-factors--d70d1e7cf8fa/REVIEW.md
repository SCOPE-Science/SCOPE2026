# Review of Fiberwise density of \(\beta_0(H)\) along fixed polar factors

## Correctness
**PASS.** On \(H_0=N(T)^\perp\), the modulus is positive and injective, so its spectral projection at \(0\) vanishes. A finite Borel partition of the positive spectrum can therefore be assigned strictly positive representatives while keeping the approximation error below \(\varepsilon\). Fixing the representative in the top cell at \(\|T\|\) preserves the norm. The resulting finite-spectrum modulus has the same support projection as \(|T|\); hence multiplying by the original polar partial isometry gives an operator whose actual polar decomposition uses the same partial isometry. The finite-spectrum lemma in arXiv:2609.37621 then gives membership in \(\beta_0(H)\).

## Originality
**PASS.** The focal source states density of \(\beta(H)\), not the fixed-polar-factor theorem. Its proof already makes the approximant's modulus finite-spectrum, so plain density of \(\beta_0(H)\) is not treated as new. The additional theorem requires a positive support-preserving spectral quantization with exact top value, yielding simultaneous preservation of the polar partial isometry, kernel, and norm. Focused semantic searches and inspection of the focal full text did not locate this stronger statement. The main residual risk is an older equivalent approximation lemma under general polar-decomposition terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The focal paper emphasizes both density and the compact-perturbation fragility of \(\beta_0(H)\). The result identifies a natural stronger largeness property: approximation can be carried out within each polar fiber while fixing three basic invariants. This is structural information about the class, not a numerical restatement of the source density theorem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2609.37621v2, Section 8. Ramesh--Osaka (2021, 2022) provide the underlying reducing-subspace norm-attainment class and structural theory. The older papers were available here only through public abstracts/metadata, so an equivalent older formulation remains a residual originality risk. The theorem does not assert compactness or finite rank of the perturbation.
