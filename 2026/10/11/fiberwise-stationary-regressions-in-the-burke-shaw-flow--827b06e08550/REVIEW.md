# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Arbitrary one-coordinate antiderivative tests give the three exact conditional expectations
\[
\mathbb E[y\mid x]=-x,
\qquad
\mathbb E[xz\mid y]=-\frac{y}{k},
\qquad
\mathbb E[xy\mid z]=-\frac{d}{g}.
\]
Their residuals are exactly the coordinate speeds divided by \(a\), \(k\), and \(g\), so conditional orthogonality yields the three variance defects and covariance identities. Zero first defect forces constant \(x\), then \(y=-x\), \(z=1/k\), and \(x^2=d/g\), so compact invariant support is confined to the two equilibria.

Risk: the theorem is conditional on compact invariant support and does not apply automatically to the original noncompact \(d=13\) attracting set.

## Originality
PASS. The closest same-object published result already contains global forcing balances, so those scalar identities were removed from the originality claim. They do not imply the stronger coordinate-fiber conditional laws. The complete recent paper contains no conditional, variance, moment, or invariant-measure theorem matching the accepted result.

Risk: the generator argument is short, and a differently phrased equivalent observation could remain in unindexed literature.

## Value
PASS. The theorem upgrades known global forcing balances to three exact stationary regressions and converts each regression residual into a measurable mean-square speed defect. It yields universal covariance structure and a sharp equilibrium rigidity statement for the \(x\)-\(y\) variance gap, giving exact diagnostics for bounded periodic or chaotic stationary states.
