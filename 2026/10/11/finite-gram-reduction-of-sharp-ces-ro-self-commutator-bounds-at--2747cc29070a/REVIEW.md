# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The definitions, rising factorials, index ranges, canonical diagonal weight, and quantified sharp coefficient are fixed in the result. The analytic input is the published generalized Cesàro finite-rank defect identity and right inverse. The new step is to prove that the positive rank-one component has an unbounded dual functional for the weighted completion, so its finite-support annihilator is dense. This establishes the exact finite-Gram infimum; the negative-parameter witness proves no finite coefficient exists. Matrix entries and the right inverse were independently replayed with rational arithmetic on actual finite columns, while the all-order assertions are proved analytically.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The closely relevant full text of Ma (arXiv:2610.00193v1) provides the exact defect decomposition and a nonsharp uniform coefficient but not its sharp Gram eigenvalue. Its weighted Cauchy–Schwarz estimates are lower bounds; they do not establish equality. The full Rhaly order-two article (DOI:10.7153/oam-2018-12-08) contains interrupter identities and qualitative hyponormality, not the full sharp coefficient. The present finite-Gram spectrum also strictly refines a known equality-threshold statement, rather than repeating its values. Matching and broader-coverage searches found no complete covering formula. One recent Mellin source was inspected only in relevant extracts, and an implicit older equivalent remains a genuine originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact coercivity coefficient is a natural invariant of the operator family's canonical weighted self-commutator. Replacing an infinite-dimensional optimization by a finite Hermitian Gram spectrum works for every integer order and all real shifts, and the order-two digamma computation gives an explicit nonattained optimum. This supplies an exact quantitative structural tool, not just an isolated inequality or a small numerical improvement.

## Limitations and sources

The conclusion concerns only the defined generalized Cesàro family and canonical diagonal weight. Its Gram sums converge but need not admit elementary forms at higher orders. Key sources: Yicen Ma, arXiv:2610.00193v1, especially Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 3.2; H. C. Rhaly Jr., DOI:10.7153/oam-2018-12-08, Sections 2–3. The remaining literature-equivalence risk and lack of independent validation are explicitly recorded in the structured originality report.
