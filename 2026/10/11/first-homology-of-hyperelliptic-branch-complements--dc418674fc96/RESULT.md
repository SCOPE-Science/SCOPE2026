# First homology of hyperelliptic branch complements
## Finding
Let \(K\subset S^3\) be a genus-\(g\ge1\) fibered knot whose capped monodromy is hyperelliptic. In the associated twofold branched cover
\[
M_K\times S^1\longrightarrow T^2\times S^2,
\]
write the branch surface as \(B_K=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^c B_j\), where the induced permutation of the \(2g+2\) Weierstrass points has orbit lengths \(r_1,\ldots,r_c\). Then every branch component has class
\[
[B_j]=r_j[T^2\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}].
\]
If \(U=(T^2\times S^2)\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu B_K\), then
\[
H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\left(\mathbb Z^c/\langle(r_1,\ldots,r_c)\rangle\right)
\cong\mathbb Z^{c+1}\oplus\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z,
\qquad d=\gcd(r_1,\ldots,r_c).
\]
Equivalently, if \(\mu_j\) is the positive meridian of \(B_j\), then the meridional subgroup has the single relation
\[
r_1\mu_1+\cdots+r_c\mu_c=0.
\]
In particular, a transitive Weierstrass orbit gives \(H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/(2g+2)\), while any fixed Weierstrass point makes the meridional quotient torsion-free.

## Assumptions and scope
The knot is assumed fibered of genus \(g\ge1\), and the capped monodromy is assumed hyperelliptic so that the branched-cover construction applies. The branch components are indexed by the orbits of the induced permutation of the Weierstrass points. The statement concerns the ordinary integral first homology of the branch-surface exterior in \(T^2\times S^2\); it does not determine the nonabelian complement group.

## Proof
Before identifying the quotient with a product, the construction gives the branch surface in
\[
(S^2\mathbin{\times_{\bar f}}S^1)\times S^1,
\]
where \(\bar f\) is the sphere monodromy obtained after quotienting by the hyperelliptic involution. A permutation orbit of length \(r_j\) traces a connected closed curve \(C_j\) in the mapping torus \(S^2\mathbin{\times_{\bar f}}S^1\), and the projection \(C_j\to S^1\) has degree \(r_j\). Because \(\bar f\) is isotopic to the identity after the marked points are forgotten, the mapping torus has first homology generated by the base circle. Hence
\[
[C_j]=r_j[S^1]
\]
in its first homology. Crossing with the second circle gives
\[
[B_j]=r_jA,
\qquad A=[T^2\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}].
\]

Let \(Y=T^2\times S^2\). Excision and the Thom isomorphism for the oriented normal disk bundles of the components give
\[
H_2(Y,U;\mathbb Z)\cong H_0(B_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^c,
\qquad H_1(Y,U;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
The long exact sequence of the pair therefore contains
\[
H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^c\longrightarrow H_1(U;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose \(A=[T^2\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}]\) and \(F=[\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times S^2]\). Their intersections satisfy \(A^2=F^2=0\) and \(A\cdot F=1\). Since \([B_j]=r_jA\), the first map sends \(aA+bF\) to
\[
b(r_1,\ldots,r_c).
\]
Its image is therefore the cyclic subgroup generated by \((r_1,\ldots,r_c)\). Because \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) is free, the resulting short exact sequence splits, giving
\[
H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z^c/\langle(r_1,\ldots,r_c)\rangle.
\]
Smith normal form of the one-row relation gives
\[
\mathbb Z^c/\langle(r_1,\ldots,r_c)\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^{c-1}\oplus\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z,
\qquad d=\gcd(r_1,\ldots,r_c),
\]
which proves the formula and the meridian relation.

## Verification
The proof is exact and does not infer an infinite statement from finite enumeration. A bundled arithmetic checker independently verifies the Smith-normal-form consequences for every partition of \(2g+2\) with \(1\le g\le8\), together with the basic section, parallel-section, transitive-orbit, and published \((1,3)\) sanity cases. These finite checks test the bookkeeping; the universal theorem follows from the mapping-torus degree argument and the long exact sequence above.

## Relationship to prior work
Akhmedov's construction identifies branch components with permutation orbits of the Weierstrass points and gives the total class \([B_K]=(2g+2)A\). Its proof also exhibits each orbit as a connected trace over the base circle. The source does not state the componentwise formula \([B_j]=r_jA\), nor the resulting full integral first homology of the branch complement. Targeted searches for branch-complement homology, meridian relations, orbit-length formulas, and equivalent gcd formulations found no covering statement. The source's explicit genus-one trefoil and figure-eight example has orbit pattern \((1,3)\), and the formula above gives \(H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3\).

## Limitations
Only first homology is determined. The result does not compute the nonabelian fundamental group, linking form, Alexander modules, or the topology of the branched cover itself. Different orbit partitions can have the same number of components and the same gcd, so this invariant alone need not distinguish branch surfaces. The product identification of the quotient is not canonical, but the component-class calculation uses the mapping-torus projection degree and is invariant under the isotopy used to obtain the product.
