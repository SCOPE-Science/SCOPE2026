# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The component-class step follows from the actual mapping-torus model: an orbit of length \(r_j\) traces a connected curve of projection degree \(r_j\), so after the product identification its branch torus represents \(r_j[T^2\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}]\). Excision, the Thom isomorphism, the pair long exact sequence, and Smith normal form then give the claimed group. The argument fixes orientations through positive meridians and states the full domain of validity.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source gives the orbit-component correspondence and total branch class, but not the individual component classes or the integral first homology formula. Searches for the source together with branch-complement homology, meridian relations, orbit lengths, gcd torsion, multisections, and equivalent formulations did not locate a statement implying the claim. A previously recorded result from the same source concerns genus-two orbit patterns and an Alexander-polynomial test, not branch-complement homology.

## Value
PASS. The branch complement is one of the natural topological objects in the construction, and the theorem converts monodromy orbit data into an exact, computable abelian invariant. The torsion criterion sharply separates transitive monodromy, fixed-point monodromy, and intermediate orbit patterns, which is directly relevant when comparing branch surfaces and their marked complements.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Akhmedov's arXiv:2610.00409v1, especially Theorem 2.1 and its proof. The result is only an abelian calculation: it does not determine the complement fundamental group or prove that distinct orbit data produce inequivalent branch surfaces. An unindexed observation could conceivably contain the same homology calculation, but no covering statement appeared in the inspected primary text or targeted searches.
