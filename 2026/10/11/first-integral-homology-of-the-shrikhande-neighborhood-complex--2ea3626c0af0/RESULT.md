# First integral homology of the Shrikhande neighborhood complex
## Finding
Let \(\mathrm{Sh}=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_4^2,\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\})\) be the Shrikhande graph, and let \(\mathcal N(\mathrm{Sh})\) denote the Lovász neighborhood complex: a nonempty vertex set is a simplex exactly when its vertices have a common neighbor in \(\mathrm{Sh}\). Then
\[
H_1(\mathcal N(\mathrm{Sh});\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
The same exact enumeration gives nonempty face vector
\[
(16,120,320,240,96,16),
\]
and rational Betti vector
\[
(1,0,55,0,0,0).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the standard Cayley model on \(\mathbb Z_4^2\). Its six connection steps are \(\pm(1,0)\), \(\pm(0,1)\), and \(\pm(1,1)\), with arithmetic modulo \(4\). The neighborhood complex uses open neighborhoods. The integral claim is only about first homology. The rational Betti vector is included as context and does not determine higher integral torsion.

## Proof
Every two distinct vertices of \(\mathrm{Sh}\) have exactly two common neighbors, so every two-vertex subset is a simplex of \(\mathcal N(\mathrm{Sh})\). Hence the one-skeleton of the neighborhood complex is the complete graph \(K_{16}\). Root its cycle space at vertex \(0\). The cycles
\[
c_{ij}=[i,j]-[0,j]+[0,i],\qquad 1\le i<j\le15,
\]
form an integral basis of \(\ker \partial_1\), of rank \(\binom{15}{2}=105\).

The neighborhood complex is generated by the sixteen six-vertex open neighborhoods. Exhaustively taking all nonempty subsets of those neighborhoods yields exactly 808 simplices and the face vector shown above. There are 320 two-simplices. Express each oriented triangle boundary in the basis \(\{c_{ij}\}\). This gives an integer matrix \(M\) of size \(105\times320\). Exact Smith reduction gives diagonal entries
\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{104\text{ times}},2.
\]
Because \(H_1=\ker\partial_1/\operatorname{im}\partial_2\), this Smith form proves
\[
H_1(\mathcal N(\mathrm{Sh});\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

For context, exact rational ranks of \(\partial_1,\ldots,\partial_5\) are
\[
15,105,160,80,16,
\]
which give rational Betti vector \((1,0,55,0,0,0)\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ren, Wang, Wu, and Wu develop associated simplicial complexes and homological/discrete-Morse machinery for hypergraphs; their first public version is arXiv:1804.07132 (2018-04-19), whose primary classifications include 55U10. A graph's open neighborhoods form a natural hypergraph and its associated simplicial complex is precisely the neighborhood complex used here. Their paper supplies a relevant topological framework but contains no Shrikhande-specific computation.

Shukla's arXiv:1802.04526 and published paper DOI 10.37236/7549 directly study Lovász neighborhood complexes. Its stated scope is graphs of maximum degree at most three and four-regular circulant graphs. The Shrikhande graph is six-regular and is a Cayley graph of \(\mathbb Z_4^2\), not a four-regular circulant, so those classification theorems do not imply the present calculation. Full-text checks of that paper found no Shrikhande or strongly-regular-graph case.

A 2026 paper by Cioabă and Mim, arXiv:2606.27328, studies clique-complex homology of strongly regular graphs and explicitly includes the Shrikhande graph among cases of interest. Its invariant is the clique complex \(\mathrm{Cl}(G)\), not the Lovász neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(G)\), so its stated results do not imply this first-homology computation.

## Limitations
This result does not identify the homotopy type or fundamental group of \(\mathcal N(\mathrm{Sh})\). It also does not assert that higher integral homology is torsion-free. The exact calculation establishes the first integral homology and the rational Betti vector only.
