# Five consecutive squared-cycle total-cut complexes are seven-spheres

## Finding

For every integer \(k\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}\), let \(n=3k+3\), let \(W_n=C_n^2\) be the squared cycle, and let \(X_k=\Delta_k^t(W_n)\) be the simplicial complex with facets the complements of independent vertex sets of cardinality \(k\). Then

\[\boxed{X_k\simeq S^7\qquad(4\le k\le8).}\]

In particular, the reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z\) in degree seven and zero in every other degree. These are homotopy equivalences, not just Betti-number calculations.

## Assumptions and scope

Vertices are numbered \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\) cyclically. Vertices at circular distance one or two are adjacent in \(W_n\). A subset \(I\) is independent exactly when each pair of distinct positions is separated by circular distance at least three. A face \(F\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) lies in \(X_k\) precisely when its complement contains an independent \(k\)-set. The assertion covers exactly five finite parameter values, \((k,n)=(4,15),(5,18),(6,21),(7,24),(8,27)\), and makes no assertion for larger \(k\).

The elementary cyclic-gap count shows that the number of independent \(k\)-sets, and thus the number of facets, is

\[\frac nk\binom{k+2}{3}=\frac{n(k+1)(k+2)}6.\]

Indeed, the gaps between consecutive independent vertices are at least three and the total excess above \(3k\) is exactly three.

## Proof

**Step 1: exact representation of the complete face poset.** Encode a subset \(F\) by the nonnegative integer \(\operatorname{mask}(F)=\sum_{v\in F}2^v\). The accompanying complete finite verification program iterates over **every** cardinality-\(k\) subset \(I\) by binary combination generation; it tests each pair of its vertices for cyclic distance at most two, retaining precisely independent \(I\). For every retained \(I\), it visits every submask of the complementary facet \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus I\) and marks that submask as a face. Since a simplicial complex generated by these facets consists exactly of their subsets, this marks every face and no nonface.

**Step 2: an acyclic element matching.** Beginning with all marked faces (including the empty face), process vertices \(v=0,1,\ldots,n-1\) in this order. At vertex \(v\), for each currently unmatched face \(F\) not containing \(v\), pair \(F\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\) and remove both whenever both are still unmatched. Every such pair is a codimension-one incidence, and neither member is reused. The standard sequential-element-matching theorem (Shen et al., Theorem 2.4; also Bayer et al., Appendix, Theorem 7.5) proves that the union of these matchings is acyclic, even though the survivors after each step need not form a simplicial subcomplex. In particular, it is not necessary to infer acyclicity from a heuristic search.

**Step 3: fully finite exhaustive counts.** The accompanying standalone source `verify_squared_cycle.cpp` evaluates the complete face set and all matching stages for all five \(k\). The resulting exact integer counts are:

| \(k\) | \(n\) | facets | faces, including empty | matched pairs | critical faces |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 4 | 15 | 75 | 22,547 | 11,273 | one 7-face |
| 5 | 18 | 126 | 142,309 | 71,154 | one 7-face |
| 6 | 21 | 196 | 854,721 | 427,360 | one 7-face |
| 7 | 24 | 288 | 4,920,705 | 2,460,352 | one 7-face |
| 8 | 27 | 405 | 27,332,161 | 13,666,080 | one 7-face |

For every row, the program exhaustively confirms that the unique surviving face is the mask `21846`, with vertex set \(\{1,2,4,6,8,10,12,14\}\), of size eight and hence dimension seven. It separately checks that every face is either in one of the disjoint matching pairs or is this survivor. The empty face is not the survivor, and is therefore matched. All these statements are finite exact tests on the entire explicitly defined face poset; there is no sampled or asymptotic inference.

**Step 4: homotopy conclusion.** By the acyclic matching theorem and discrete Morse theory (Shen et al., Theorems 2.4–2.5; Bayer et al., Theorems 7.2 and 7.5), matching the empty face and leaving one critical seven-dimensional simplex produces a CW model with one 0-cell and one 7-cell, hence a space homotopy equivalent to \(S^7\). This proves the stated claim for all five parameter values. \(\square\)

## Verification

Compile and execute `verify_squared_cycle.cpp` in the extracted package:

```sh
g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic verify_squared_cycle.cpp -o verify_squared_cycle
./verify_squared_cycle 4 8
```

The program prints all five face/facet/matching counts, the unique surviving mask and dimension, and terminates with `VERIFY_OK`. It independently checks the gap-count facet formula, the total face-pair partition, and reduced Euler characteristic \(-1\) in every case. The largest calculation scans every bitmask of a 27-element vertex set, and therefore covers all potential faces; it is not a random or bounded-depth sample. The algorithm and its expected results are inspectable in the package, rather than relying on an unattached execution log.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer et al., *Total Cut Complexes of Graphs*, Conjecture 5.1, predicts that \(\Delta_k^t(W_{3k+i})\simeq S^{2i+1}\) for \(0\le i\le k-1\). Its supporting computations were limited to \(k\le7\) and \(n\le21\). The present five assertions are precisely the \(i=3\) cases, including \((k,n)=(7,24),(8,27)\), beyond that stated supporting range.

Shen et al., *Homotopy Type of Total Cut Complexes of Squared Cycle Graphs*, prove the conjecture for \(k=3\) and, for arbitrary \(k\ge3\), for \(n=3k+1\) or \(3k+2\). The case \(n=3k+3\) with \(k\ge4\) is outside those results. Their Table 3 already reports \(\widetilde H_7\cong\mathbb Z\) for \((k,n)=(4,15)\). **That numerical homology rank is not claimed as new**; for this row the present conclusion is the stronger homotopy classification. The other four rows also establish the stronger homotopy statement. Related ordinary cut-complex results are not interchangeable with total cut complexes for \(k\ge3\).

## Limitations

The theorem is a complete but finite classification of five consecutive values. It does not settle the corresponding conjecture for \(k\ge9\) or for other \(n\), and no structural all-parameter recurrence is claimed. Although a finite exhaustive program constitutes an exact certificate here, users should inspect its logic and reproduce its execution; a partial enumeration would not prove this theorem. General-purpose mathematical indexing cannot eliminate the possibility of equivalent unindexed work. No independent external audit is asserted.
