# Scientific review of the three-character fourth-moment classification

## Correctness
**PASS.** The fourth moment is exactly the squared norm of coefficient self-convolution. Every collision among three-character two-term sums is a midpoint relation or an equality of doubles; their possible combinations are exhausted by six cases. For each, a sharp scalar inequality and its equality conditions are explicitly established. The order-three endpoint follows from concentration on a three-element quotient. The supplied checker independently evaluates 5,790 frequency triples, 23,160 exact integer-Gaussian coefficient inequalities, and 167 direct Fourier identities. This numerical range supports but does not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** Shkredov (arXiv:2103.14670v1) addresses higher additive energies and Sidon-type subsets, not the exact weighted three-character fourth-moment operator norm. Schmidt (arXiv:1302.2766v1) treats long unimodular polynomial families and small fourth moments. Neuwirth (arXiv:math/0703236v1) treats maximum-modulus norms. The published three-frequency phase-moment calculation restricts all coefficient magnitudes to one; the four-point phase-only fourth-moment calculation concerns a minimum for a different support size. None of the inspected statements implies the six collision classes and the five constants with all extremizers. The older \(\Lambda(4)\) and sequence-design literature may contain an equivalent elementary calculation under other terminology; a literature search cannot establish priority conclusively.

## Value
**PASS.** The problem asks for the exact optimal \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) Fourier embedding constant on every three-character subspace, the smallest support size exhibiting interactions between midpoint collisions and order-two torsion. The result is an all-group classification: equality geometry distinguishes lone involutions, arithmetic progressions, Klein four configurations, order-four triples and order-three cosets. It also identifies the subtle case in which one of three available frequencies must have zero coefficient at the optimum. This is a natural exact small-support benchmark for higher-order Fourier restriction constants rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope and risks
The claim is limited to exactly three available distinct characters, with coefficients permitted to vanish. Originality risk persists for unindexed older small-support \(\Lambda(4)\) computations. All verification beyond the proof is finite. The scientific source comparisons do not establish the absence of unpublished equivalents.
