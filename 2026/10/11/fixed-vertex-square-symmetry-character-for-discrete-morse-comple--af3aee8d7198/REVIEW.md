# Review of the fixed-vertex-square character theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reduces Morse simplices to acyclic outgoing-arrow configurations, invokes the established complete-source shellability theorem only for homology concentration, derives the actual equivariant trace (including orientation signs) from an orbit determinant, and evaluates its fixed and moving blocks for both vertex-fixing automorphisms and central-edge inversions. Matrix-tree cofactor signs, orbit periods, parity, and the identity case are explicitly accounted for. The included standalone code enumerates all 24 unlabeled tree types on two through seven vertices and all 967 automorphisms; exact augmented simplicial homology checks cover all four tree types on two through four vertices. Finite checks support but do not replace the proof. Integral module equivalence is not claimed.

**Originality — PASS (residual risk disclosed).** Jojić (arXiv:1109.4475) explicitly gives complete-source shellability and generating facets, which cover the input homological concentration, not the claimed equivariant action formula. Donovan–Scoville (arXiv:2207.13780) analyze Morse complexes of trees/extended stars; Engström (arXiv:math/0508148) analyzes directed tree complexes. The character is a new additional calculation obtained here by signed orbit enumeration and a rooted-orbit determinant. Published-result searches and own prior-claim comparisons found no covering formula. An earlier triangular-book character calculation explicitly covers the star-tree subfamily under page permutations; that subfamily is credited, and the new contribution is the uniform nonstar-tree result. Kozlov's older primary full text could not be inspected, and equivariant consequences of generating-facet descriptions or an unindexed result remain genuine risks. A failed literature search is not a uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** The result determines a complete, degree-specific rational representation for the automorphism group of **every** finite tree, distinguishing automorphisms by their fixed vertices and permutation parity, beyond the identity trace and Betti number. This natural equivariant refinement is potentially useful for symmetry-aware Morse-complex computations and constitutes an infinite-family structural answer; neither a special numerical slice nor a routine restatement of shellability.

**Scope/limitations.** Finite simple trees on at least two vertices; a strictly one-dimensional cone graph; automorphisms fixing its hub; rational module isomorphism only. No independent validation of the scientific conclusions has occurred.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
