# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Guo's proof gives an actual homological eigenvalue \(\rho=\pm\lambda\) with \(\lambda>1\) and proves \(\Delta_K(\rho)=0\). For any L-space-knot staircase polynomial, the paired-sum argument in RESULT.md proves \(\Delta_K(t)>0\) for every real \(t>1\). Thus \(+\lambda\) cannot be the source eigenvalue and \(\rho=-\lambda\). Reciprocity then gives \(-\lambda^{-1}\). The mirror reduction used for negative surgery preserves the Alexander polynomial and pseudo-Anosov dilatation.

Searches for the exact negative-root statement, the equivalent negative-dilatation eigenvalue statement, and broader combinations of lens surgery, L-space Alexander polynomials, and pseudo-Anosov monodromy found no equivalent or stronger statement. The principal residual risk is that this short consequence may occur in an unindexed note or future revision.

## Value
PASS. The sign is not cosmetic: it turns the new two-sided spectral obstruction into a one-sided root-location test. A candidate at the \(\pm(4g-2)\) threshold must have an Alexander root in \(( -\infty,-1)\), together with its reciprocal in \((-1,0)\); a positive real root outside the unit circle cannot satisfy the obstruction. This directly sharpens how the recent theorem can be used to screen candidate lens-surgery knots and connects the Floer staircase sign pattern to the pseudo-Anosov homological eigenvalue.
