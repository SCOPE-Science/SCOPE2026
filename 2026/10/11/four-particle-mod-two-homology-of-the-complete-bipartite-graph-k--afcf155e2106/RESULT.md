# Four-particle mod-two homology of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{5,4}\)

## Finding
Let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_4(K_{5,4})\) be the space of unordered four-element subsets of the geometric realization of the finite simple complete bipartite graph with vertex parts of cardinalities five and four, with the usual configuration-space topology. Its complete mod-two Poincaré polynomial is
\[
P_X(t)=\sum_{i\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_i(X;\mathbb F_2)t^i
=1+13t+281t^2+353t^3.
\]
In particular, \(H_i(X;\mathbb F_2)=0\) for \(i\ge4\) and \(\chi(X)=-84\).

## Assumptions and scope
All particles are *unlabeled and distinct*. The graph is an undirected simple one-dimensional CW complex with five vertices in one part, four in the other, and all twenty cross edges. Homology coefficients are \(\mathbb F_2\); no integral or rational Betti-number assertion is made. The graph has no isolated vertices. The configuration space is the whole unordered configuration space, not a fixed discretization. Interchanging the two bipartition parts gives the same graph.

## Proof
Use the reduced Świątkowski chain model, which computes unordered graph-configuration homology in each particle weight [1, §2]. Work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Give each edge variable weight one and degree zero. At every vertex \(v\), choose one incident reference edge \(e_v^0\). Each nonreference incident halfedge supplies a degree-one, weight-one generator \(h_{v,e}-h_{v,e_v^0}\), with differential \(e+e_v^0\) in characteristic two. In weight four a degree-\(d\) chain is a product of \(d\) such generators at *distinct* vertices together with an unordered degree-\((4-d)\) edge monomial. The differential removes one halfedge factor and inserts either its incident edge or its reference edge, with coefficients reduced modulo two. Terms occurring twice cancel. In particular, \(\partial^2=0\).

Label the four vertices of degree five first and the five vertices of degree four second. The graph has twenty edges. For a chosen \(d\)-subset \(S\) of vertices there are \(\prod_{v\in S}(\deg v-1)\) choices of nonreference halfedge factors, and \(\binom{20+4-d-1}{4-d}\) edge multisets. Therefore the exact number of basis cells in chain degree \(d\) is
\[
c_d=\binom{20+3-d}{4-d}\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V\\|S|=d}}\prod_{v\in S}(\deg v-1),\qquad 0\le d\le4.
\]
Explicit evaluation gives \((c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(8855,47740,89460,68120,17461)\). The executable, standalone `verify.py` constructs **every** basis element and every boundary column, then performs exact bit-vector Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\). With \(\partial_d:C_d\to C_{d-1}\), the four computed ranks are
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}\partial_3,\operatorname{rank}\partial_4)
=(8854,38873,50306,17461).
\]
These integers are obtained from a finite exhaustive algorithm using only integer bit operations, not sampling or a floating-point estimate. Each column is generated by an explicitly specified deterministic combinatorial rule. The verifier reconstructs the calculation for two different reference-halfedge choices, independently enumerates the alternating basis-size formula, checks \(\partial^2=0\) on every degree-two through degree-four basis cell for one reference choice, and compares the homological Euler characteristic with an independent formula.

Rank-nullity now yields \(\beta_i=c_i-\operatorname{rank}\partial_i-\operatorname{rank}\partial_{i+1}\), where out-of-range maps have rank zero. This gives \((\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_4)=(1,13,281,353,0)\). No higher-degree chain groups exist. As an independent arithmetic check, Gal's generating function, quoted in [1, §2], specializes here to
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\chi(\operatorname{UConf}_n(K_{5,4}))t^n
=\frac{(1-4t)^4(1-3t)^5}{(1-t)^{20}},
\]
whose coefficient of \(t^4\) equals \(-84\), matching both the chain dimensions and the Betti numbers.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with a Python 3 standard library. The expected terminator is `VERIFY_OK`. The program checks the exact dimensions, differential ranks, two choices of local reference halfedges, the complete boundary-square identity, Euler characteristic and final Betti vector. There is no probabilistic component, numerical tolerance, external dataset or network dependency. The source provides a reproducible finite certificate procedure rather than an independently conducted formal verification.

## Relationship to prior work
An [1, Theorem 3.10 and Question 8.1] studies \(\operatorname{UConf}_m(K_{m+1,m+r-1})\), giving the top homology and torsion one degree below it, and explicitly identifies the remaining Betti numbers for \(m\ge4\) as open. The present graph is exactly the \(m=4,r=1\) member \(K_{5,4}\). The known zero top homology does not determine its field-valued degree-two and degree-three dimensions. This result gives a complete coefficient-two numerical answer for that specific four-particle instance, not the integral free-rank answer for the family. Maciążek–Sawicki [2, Table 2] computed the four-particle second integral homology of the smaller graph \(K_{4,4}\), which does not determine the homology for \(K_{5,4}\). Their structural formulas for \(K_{3,3}\) and \(K_{2,p}\) likewise do not cover this graph. The case is motivated particularly by [1], which identifies \(K_{4,5}\) as the minimal graph exhibiting odd torsion in degree two with three particles; the fourth-particle, mod-two topology is a different, complementary invariant.

## Limitations
The result concerns only one graph, exactly four unordered particles and coefficients \(\mathbb F_2\). It does not determine rational or integral Betti numbers, integral torsion, the 3-primary component or homology ring operations. In particular it does not claim that an odd torsion class persists when the particle count increases. The finite calculation establishes the stated homology dimensions using the established chain-model equivalence; it does not give an explicit basis of homology classes or a homotopy-type classification. No originality search proves absolute uniqueness.
