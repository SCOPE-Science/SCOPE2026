# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Poisson Laplace transform and fractional moments give an exponential almost-sure tail for the grouped segment lengths. Hyperplane arrangements then provide polynomial-in-the-number-of-atoms covers; because the covering balls are closed and finite in number, passing from exposed points to the full extreme set is justified. The support-point map is injective on all good normals when the atomic directions are dense, and \(d\) independent positive generators give a full-dimensional body. The argument uses no finite experiment as an infinite proof. Remaining scientific limitation: the mass-tail condition is sufficient, not shown necessary.

## Originality — PASS with residual literature risk

Davydov–Paulauskas (2019), Theorem 5 and Remark 6, give a planar upper bound and describe zero dimension for **finite** directional support, whereas the present law has **dense infinite** support and continuum exposed points. Kukushkin (2026), Theorem 2.1 and Section 7, treats **uniform** directions and belt-regular anisotropic upper bounds: the positive-mass atoms here violate the uniform belt condition. Neither inspected theorem implies the all-dimensional zero upper-box law and polylogarithmic covering estimate. A matching example in older unindexed sources cannot be categorically excluded. No global novelty or independent certification is claimed.

Kukushkin (2025) proves a general Hausdorff upper bound, not the zero upper-box law; its publicly available theorem statement was checked, with full-document comparison incomplete. This is retained as a residual originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The geometric–probabilistic question is whether full topological support of a spectral law forces substantial extreme-point complexity. This provides a sharp qualitative negative boundary: even a full-dimensional body with continuum exposed points may have an extreme-point set of dimension zero, simultaneously for three metric notions. The robust ordered-tail criterion and quantitative covering bound distinguish the result from the elementary finite-atomic degeneracy and delimit the reach of recent smooth-direction dimension theorems.
