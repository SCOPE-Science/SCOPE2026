# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the published L-space coefficient restrictions, the published high-elliptic-surgery root theorem, the published surgery-slope bounds, and an exact finite Sturm computation. The verifier was executed from the packaged artifact and returned the expected unique support \(\{6,5,3,2,1\}\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, Schmalian--Sivek, states equality of dilatation in the genus-six \(22/23\)-surgery situation, not equality of the entire Alexander polynomial. Ni--Zhang already cover the O-type \(22\) and I-type \(23\) subcases through identical knot Floer homology; those subcases are not claimed as new. The checked literature did not state the surgery-type-independent Alexander-polynomial consequence. Residual risk remains that a finite Berge-knot table could imply the cyclic subcase in an unstated form.

## Value
PASS. Genus \(6\) is the first boundary immediately beyond the source's genus-at-most-five theorem. The result replaces a single shared dilatation with a complete, explicit Alexander polynomial and therefore gives a sharper invariant constraint on every genus-six high elliptic surgery.

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary comparison is M. Schmalian and S. Sivek, arXiv:2609.39260v1, especially Remark 1.7, Corollary 3.7, Example 3.8, and Lemma 3.9. The strongest older coverage is Y. Ni and X. Zhang, doi:10.2140/agt.2018.18.441, whose finite-surgery tables directly cover the O-type and I-type subcases and eliminate the two half-integral slopes. The claim does not classify the knot itself and is limited to genus \(6\).
