# Global isoperimetric minimizers among root-lattice Voronoi cells

## Finding

Let \(\Lambda\) be a rank-\(d\) simply-laced root lattice, normalized so that every root has squared length \(2\), and let \(V_\Lambda\) be its Euclidean Voronoi cell. Write
\[
\mathcal I_d(P)
=
\frac{\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial P)}
{\operatorname{vol}(P)^{(d-1)/d}}.
\]
Put
\[
r\equiv d\pmod 8
\]
and
\[
(q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_7)
=
(1,2,3,4,4,4,3,2).
\]

Then for every integer \(d\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_d(V_\Lambda)
\ge
d\sqrt2\,q_r^{1/(2d)}.
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\min_{\operatorname{rank}\Lambda=d}\det(\Lambda)=q_r,
}
\]
where \(\det(\Lambda)\) is the Gram determinant.

The equality types are completely explicit.

If \(d=8k\), the unique type is
\[
E_8^{\perp k}.
\]

If \(d=8k+1\), the unique type is
\[
E_8^{\perp k}\perp A_1.
\]

If \(d=8k+2\), the unique type is
\[
E_8^{\perp k}\perp A_2.
\]

If \(d=8k+6\), the unique type is
\[
E_8^{\perp k}\perp E_6.
\]

If \(d=8k+7\), the unique type is
\[
E_8^{\perp k}\perp E_7.
\]

If \(d=8k+3\), the equality types are
\[
E_8^{\perp k}\perp A_3
\]
and
\[
E_8^{\perp(k-j)}\perp D_{8j+3},
\qquad
1\le j\le k.
\]

If \(d=8k+4\), the equality types are
\[
E_8^{\perp(k-j)}\perp D_{8j+4},
\qquad
0\le j\le k.
\]

If \(d=8k+5\), the equality types are
\[
E_8^{\perp(k-j)}\perp D_{8j+5},
\qquad
0\le j\le k.
\]

Thus the fixed-rank global optimum inside the discrete class of root lattices is periodic modulo \(8\). This is compatible with, but logically different from, the recent local theorem that every reducible root lattice has a decreasing variation when one is allowed to leave the root-lattice locus.

## Assumptions and scope

A root lattice is understood in the normalization used by Novaga--Song: it is an even lattice generated by roots of squared length \(2\). Every such lattice is an orthogonal sum of irreducibles of types
\[
A_m,\quad D_m,\quad E_6,\quad E_7,\quad E_8.
\]

The theorem minimizes \(\mathcal I_d\) only among Voronoi cells of root lattices of fixed rank. It does not claim global minimality among all lattices, all parallelohedra, or all convex bodies.

Orthogonal sums are considered up to lattice isometry and reordering of factors.

## Proof

Novaga--Song record that the Voronoi facet vectors of a root lattice are precisely its roots. Since every root has squared length \(2\), each facet-supporting bisector has equation
\[
\langle x,v\rangle=1
\]
with
\[
\|v\|=\sqrt2.
\]
Hence every facet of \(V_\Lambda\) lies at distance
\[
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\]
from the origin. The Voronoi cell is therefore tangential to the ball of radius \(1/\sqrt2\).

Let
\[
\delta(\Lambda)
=
\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda).
\]
Because a Voronoi cell is a fundamental domain,
\[
\operatorname{vol}(V_\Lambda)=\delta(\Lambda).
\]
Decomposing \(V_\Lambda\) into pyramids from the origin to its facets gives
\[
\delta(\Lambda)
=
\frac1d\frac1{\sqrt2}
\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial V_\Lambda).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal H^{d-1}(\partial V_\Lambda)
=
d\sqrt2\,\delta(\Lambda),
\]
and so
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_d(V_\Lambda)
=
d\sqrt2\,\delta(\Lambda)^{1/d}.
}
\]

If \(G\) is a Gram matrix of \(\Lambda\), then
\[
\delta(\Lambda)=\sqrt{\det G}.
\]
Thus fixed-rank isoperimetric minimization is exactly determinant minimization.

For the irreducible simply-laced root lattices, the Cartan determinants are
\[
\det(A_m)=m+1,
\qquad
\det(D_m)=4,
\]
and
\[
\det(E_6)=3,
\qquad
\det(E_7)=2,
\qquad
\det(E_8)=1.
\]
Orthogonal sums multiply determinants.

Define the periodic function
\[
h(0),h(1),\ldots,h(7)
=
1,2,3,4,4,4,3,2.
\]
Every irreducible root lattice \(L\) satisfies
\[
\det(L)\ge h(\operatorname{rank}L\bmod8).
\]
The equality cases are exactly:
\[
E_8\quad\text{in residue }0,
\]
\[
A_1\quad\text{in residue }1,
\qquad
A_2\quad\text{in residue }2,
\]
\[
A_3\ \text{or}\ D_m\text{ with }m\equiv3\pmod8
\quad\text{in residue }3,
\]
\[
D_m\text{ with }m\equiv4\pmod8
\quad\text{in residue }4,
\]
\[
D_m\text{ with }m\equiv5\pmod8
\quad\text{in residue }5,
\]
and
\[
E_6,\qquad E_7
\]
in residues \(6,7\), respectively.

A direct check of the eight residues gives the key multiplicative inequality
\[
h(a)h(b)\ge h(a+b\bmod8)
\]
for all
\[
a,b\in\{0,\ldots,7\}.
\]
Moreover equality holds only when
\[
a=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
b=0.
\]

Now decompose a rank-\(d\) root lattice orthogonally:
\[
\Lambda=L_1\perp\cdots\perp L_t,
\]
where every \(L_i\) is irreducible. Put
\[
r_i=\operatorname{rank}(L_i)\bmod8.
\]
Then
\[
\det(\Lambda)
=
\prod_i\det(L_i)
\ge
\prod_i h(r_i)
\ge
h\!\left(\sum_i r_i\bmod8\right)
=
h(d\bmod8).
\]
This proves the determinant lower bound.

The displayed constructions using \(E_8\) and one residual factor attain the bound, so it is sharp.

For equality, every irreducible factor must itself attain its residue bound, and every multiplication step must attain equality in the periodic inequality. Since equality there requires one residue to be \(0\), at most one irreducible factor can have nonzero residue. The residue-zero equality factor is necessarily \(E_8\). Combining this observation with the irreducible equality list gives exactly the stated classification.

Finally,
\[
\delta(\Lambda)_{\min}
=
\sqrt{q_r},
\]
and the isoperimetric identity yields
\[
\mathcal I_d(V_\Lambda)_{\min}
=
d\sqrt2\,q_r^{1/(2d)}.
\]

## Verification

The bundled exact checker verifies the complete residue multiplication table
\[
h(a)h(b)\ge h(a+b\bmod8)
\]
and its equality condition. It also performs dynamic programming over all irreducible \(A_m,D_m,E_6,E_7,E_8\) blocks through rank \(256\), using exact integer determinants, and checks both the periodic minimum
\[
q_{d\bmod8}
\]
and the complete equality-type list.

These finite checks are supplementary. The infinite theorem follows from the Cartan determinant formulas and the eight-entry multiplicative inequality.

The source's full root-lattice section was inspected to verify the normalization, the orthogonal ADE decomposition, the fact that roots are exactly the Voronoi facet vectors, and the distinction between its local classification and the global discrete optimization proved here.

## Relationship to prior work

Novaga--Song classify which irreducible root-lattice Voronoi cells are strict local minima under nearby metric and affine perturbations. Their positive local list is
\[
A_2,\quad D_d\ (d\ge4),\quad E_6,\quad E_7,\quad E_8,
\]
while every \(A_d\) with \(d\ge3\) and every reducible root lattice admits a decreasing variation.

That local result does not compare distinct root lattices of the same rank globally. In particular, reducible lattices can be globally optimal inside the discrete root-lattice class even though they are unstable in the larger continuous class.

The determinant table for ADE root systems is classical. What is new here is the exact translation of the root-lattice Voronoi isoperimetric quotient to covolume, followed by the fixed-rank global optimization and equality classification. Targeted searches for root-lattice Voronoi isoperimetric quotients, fixed-rank global minimizers, \(E_8\)-sum periodicity, and the exact formula
\[
d\sqrt2\,q_{d\bmod8}^{1/(2d)}
\]
found no prior statement of this classification.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to root lattices. Leaving the root-lattice locus can decrease the quotient even at some equality cases here; this is explicit in the local variation theorem for reducible lattices and for \(A_d\) with \(d\ge3\).

The result does not classify the global isoperimetric minimizer among arbitrary rank-\(d\) lattices. It also does not assert that the periodic root-lattice minimizers are locally stable under unrestricted lattice deformations.

The main residual bibliographic risk is that the fixed-rank determinant optimization may have appeared implicitly in older lattice literature without being phrased as an isoperimetric theorem for Voronoi cells.
