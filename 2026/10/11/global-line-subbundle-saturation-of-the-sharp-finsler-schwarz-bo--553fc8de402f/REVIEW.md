# Review of Global line-subbundle saturation of the sharp Finsler Schwarz bound

## Correctness

PASS. The source formula gives
\[
F_{t,k}^2(z;v)
=
\frac{
\sum_i a_i^2
+
t(\sum_i a_i^{2k})^{1/k}
}{1+t},
\qquad
a_i=\frac{|v_i|}{1-|z_i|^2}.
\]
For
\[
f_0(z)=(z_1,\ldots,z_1),
\]
direct substitution gives
\[
F_{t,k}^2(f_0(z);df_0v)
=
K_{n,t,k}^2a_1^2.
\]
Equality in the sharp Schwarz inequality is therefore equivalent to
\[
F_{t,k}^2(z;v)=a_1^2.
\]
Both terms in the defining weighted average are at least \(a_1^2\), and equality occurs exactly when every \(a_i\) with \(i\ge2\) vanishes. This is exactly
\[
v\in\mathbb C e_1.
\]
The argument separately covers \(t=0\) and \(t>0\).

## Originality

PASS. The 2026 source displays the same map but verifies sharp equality only at
\[
(0,e_1).
\]
The full 2023 unit-polydisk paper gives the sharp constant for \(F_{t,k}\) but does not identify this map or a global equality locus.

Searches covering the exact source, the explicit map, equality directions, line fields, and polydisk Lu constants did not locate the global equality classification. The closest primary sources provide the sharp inequality and its single-pair extremizer, not the all-base-point line-subbundle statement.

## Value

PASS. Equality cases control the rigidity boundary of a sharp Schwarz estimate. The fresh source deliberately contrasts sharp equality at one tangent pair with its separate all-direction unit-factor rigidity theorem.

The accepted result shows that the source's nonrigid sharp extremizer has much stronger equality persistence than stated: sharp equality occurs at every point along an entire holomorphic line subbundle, and nowhere else for that map. This is an exact geometric description of the extremal set, not a numerical refinement.
