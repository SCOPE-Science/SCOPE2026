Integration produces t+(2/b)log(t)+C+o(1) with a genuinely convergent remainder. Global bounded-orbit convergence follows from Q'=-(x-b/2)^2; a standard strong-stable foliation transfers the asymptotic to all other bounded orbits. Explicit assumptions distinguish the exceptional exponentially convergent set and do not assert all trajectories are bounded. The existing symbolic checker verifies the necessary jet, not the global analytic argument.

**Originality — PASS for the corrected narrow claim, not the original.** An explicit affine conjugacy identifies the entire Sprott M family with a parameter subfamily of the modified Sprott-N system. The published *Sharp recurrence threshold and critical relaxation in a quadratic chaotic flow* consequently already proves the global discriminant threshold, compact-set rigidity, bounded-orbit convergence and leading 1/t law for this model. Eichhorn–Linz–Hänggi (1998), Table II, also identifies the JD2 jerk class and a sign-based no-chaos criterion. These results must not be presented as new. Their inspected statements do not contain the cubic-order center coefficient, the logarithmic coefficient 2/b or convergence of the corrected remainder. Sprott (1994), Zhao–Lin–Dai (2014), and the earlier generalized Sprott M stationary-measure study do not give the two-term relaxation. Unindexed equivalent calculations remain a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS, modest.** The precise logarithmic subleading correction in original time is a natural asymptotic refinement of a known sharp saddle-node rate for a standard quadratic chaotic-flow family. Its coefficient and finite renormalized remainder distinguish a coordinate-specific nonlinear delay not supplied by the earlier global or leading-order theorem. This is a narrow exact dynamical statement, not a new general fold theory or a discovery of the recurrence boundary.

**Scope.** The corrected result is restricted to b>0, a=-b^2/4, bounded forward-complete trajectories off the global strong-stable set. Earlier threshold and leading-rate conclusions are context. No external expert or formal verification is claimed.
