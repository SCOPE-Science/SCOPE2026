# Scientific review: Gumbel transition for random boundary approximation of regular polygons

## Correctness
PASS. The distance-to-hull maximum reduces to polygon vertices; local arclength-rescaled nearest samples converge to independent exponential endpoint gaps. The obtuse-angle chord-altitude identity is exact. Uniform deterministic comparison reduces all corner defects to the maximum of exponential harmonic means, whose exact Bessel tail has a square-root prefactor. Extreme-value convergence and uniform integrability establish the Gumbel and sharp expectation constants. The finite verifier checks independent numerical representations, geometry, and selected normalized distribution values; it is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual historical uncertainty. The 2014 random-polytope approximation paper of Fodor--Hug--Ziebarth (arXiv:1409.8105) addresses volume/mean-width statistics and does not provide this polygon-corner Gumbel law. Prochno--Schütt--Sonnleitner--Werner (arXiv:2404.02870, full published article inspected) already prove the fixed-polygon limiting distribution and expectation and obtain only a two-sided order \(c_m\asymp(\log m)/m\) for regular polygons. The present large-\(m\) theorem, the \(\frac12\log\log m\) correction, and exact centering constant require an additional Bessel-tail asymptotic and extreme-value analysis. Sonnleitner (arXiv:2508.16442) gives a different Gumbel limit for smooth positive-curvature convex bodies as their sample count grows. Semantic published-database and literature searches found related geometry rather than this double-limit claim. Search absence is not proof of priority, and hidden/equivalent earlier asymptotics are a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The 2024/2025 source explicitly isolates how the Hausdorff approximation constant depends on the number of vertices but establishes only its order. The new limit identifies the precise coefficient and a nontrivial logarithmic-logarithmic correction and shows a universal extreme-value law. These are structural geometric probability statements about a canonical family, not a routine recomputation of a single table entry.

## Verification and limits
The proof applies for every fixed regular polygon with at least four vertices, followed by the large-number-of-vertices limit. Simultaneous growth of polygon complexity and sample count, irregular polygon arrays, and independent external audit are not addressed. Historical coverage cannot be ruled out absolutely.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
