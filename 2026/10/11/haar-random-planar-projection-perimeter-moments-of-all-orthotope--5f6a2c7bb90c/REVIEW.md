# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The projected orthotope is a planar zonotope, so the projection perimeter is twice the sum of projected edge lengths. The squared length of one projected coordinate axis has the exact \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,(d-2)/2)\) distribution. Conditioning on that length and choosing a Haar frame in its orthogonal complement reduces the mixed edge-length moment to an explicitly derived angular Gauss hypergeometric average; a beta-series integration gives the displayed \({}_3F_2\) value. This establishes every coefficient of the orthotope second-moment quadratic form. The gamma-ratio and controlled beta-series expansions then prove the variance limit. The standalone verifier directly checks the independent integral and convex-hull interpretation, and reproduces the four-cube number. The analytic proof, not the finite computation, covers all dimensions.

## Originality — PASS
The most relevant geometry-classified primary source, Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets, proves higher moments for **line** widths, not two-plane perimeters. Finch's full paper reports an exact three-cube hypergeometric moment but only a numerical four-cube second moment and no general formula. The accepted statement keeps both the three-cube calculation and all first moments as prior work, while adding the universal distinct-edge correlation, the arbitrary-orthotope formula, and the high-dimensional variance constant. Paouris–Pivovarov–Zinn's projected-volume limit theorem concerns the planar projected **area** rather than perimeter. Related general Gaussian integral identities may imply an unevaluated representation, but they do not themselves supply the evaluated cross-edge coefficient. Novelty searches covered equivalent box, zonotope, periwidth, and random-projection terminology. Residual risk: older unindexed zonotope or integral-geometry work could already contain the same correlation evaluation.

## Value — PASS
The exact variance measures the orientation sensitivity of the perimeter of random shadows, a natural geometric quantity previously tabulated numerically for four-dimensional cubes. The theorem is not a single parameter substitution: it supplies one formula for every dimension and every rectangular box, together with a new nonzero limiting variance. The pairwise projected-edge correlation is a reusable integral-geometric kernel for zonotopes generated by orthogonal vectors.
