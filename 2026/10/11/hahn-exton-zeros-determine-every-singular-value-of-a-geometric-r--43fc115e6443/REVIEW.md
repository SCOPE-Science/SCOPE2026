The operator's coefficient normalization was reconstructed directly from its weighted-composition formula. Its positive Gram matrix is \(t^{n+m}(\min\{n,m\}+1)\), and the determinant of each selected principal submatrix factors into geometric factors and strictly positive integer gaps. The independent gap sums produce all coefficients of the Fredholm determinant for every order. Positivity and trivial kernel identify the zeros with positive eigenvalue reciprocals; a three-term difference recurrence proves simple eigenspaces. The third Jackson/Hahn–Exton series was checked term by term with the normalization explicitly defined in the claim. Exact rational finite checks support but do not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risks

The closest full accessible papers discuss the same Rhaly objects but prove different conclusions. Galanopoulos–Girela establish mapping properties, Gallardo-Gutiérrez–Partington compute ordinary spectra and invariant subspaces for the very same point-mass weighted composition map, and Bellavita–Dellepiane–Stylogiannis give Schatten ideal membership conditions; none of those conclusions determines the nonnormal operator's individual singular values. The independent summation of all principal Gram minors gives a concrete Hahn–Exton spectral determinant not established by these statements. Classical Hahn–Exton zero theory may supply properties of the resulting zero set, but not the asserted operator identification without a new linkage. The 2026 weighted-Hardy/factorable-matrix paper was available mainly in abstract and summary form, and unindexed earlier equivalent spectral reductions remain possible.

## Value — PASS

The exact sequence \(s_j(W_t)\) gives sharp best rank-constrained approximation errors and the operator norm of a canonical compact Rhaly/weighted-composition family. It is qualitatively stronger than knowing compactness, Schatten membership, or the ordinary spectrum; the explicit identification with a classical entire special function opens direct use of its zero-location and asymptotic theory. The geometric point-mass family is a canonical building block for moment-measure Cesàro/Rhaly operators rather than an arbitrary finite truncation. No assertion about generic weights or a novel special-function zero theorem is made.
