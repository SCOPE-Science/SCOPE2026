# Harmonic Amini–Harandi–Rahimi profile of the regular octagonal plane
## Finding
Let
\[
h(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2ab}{a+b},&a+b>0,\\
0,&a=b=0,
\end{cases}
\]
be the harmonic mean. It is continuous, homogeneous of degree one, and satisfies \(h(1,1)=1\), so it belongs to the class \(\Lambda\) used by Amini–Harandi and Rahimi.

For
\[
X_8=(\mathbb R^2,N_8),
\qquad
N_8(s,t)=\max\left\{|s|,|t|,\frac{|s|+|t|}{\sqrt2}\right\},
\]
and every \(1\le p\le\infty\), let \(q\) be Hölder conjugate to \(p\), including the endpoint conventions \(q=\infty\) when \(p=1\) and \(q=1\) when \(p=\infty\). Then
\[
\boxed{
C_{h,p}(X_8)
=
\left(1+(\sqrt2-1)^q\right)^{1/q}
}.
\tag{1}
\]
At the endpoints this means
\[
C_{h,1}(X_8)=1,
\qquad
C_{h,\infty}(X_8)=\sqrt2.
\tag{2}
\]

For \(1<p<\infty\), equality is attained by the fixed unit pair
\[
u=(1,\sqrt2-1),
\qquad
v=(1-\sqrt2,1),
\tag{3}
\]
at the intrinsic scale
\[
t_p=(\sqrt2-1)^{1/(p-1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
The same pair is sharp with \(t=0\) for \(p=1\) and \(t=1\) for \(p=\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope
For \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), Amini–Harandi and Rahimi define
\[
C_{\lambda,p}(X)
=
\sup_{u,v\in S_X,\ 0\le t\le1}
\frac{\lambda(\|u+t v\|,\|u-t v\|)}{\|(1,t)\|_p}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equation (5) is their one-parameter reduction of the original two-scale definition.

This finding uses the canonical harmonic mean member \(\lambda=h\). The domain is the real regular octagonal plane \(X_8\), and the entire exponent interval \(1\le p\le\infty\) is covered.

Put
\[
r=\sqrt2-1.
\tag{6}
\]
The unit ball of \(X_8\) has vertices
\[
(1,r),\ (r,1),\ (-r,1),\ (-1,r)
\]
and their negatives.

## Proof
Write the norm as a maximum of eight norm-one support functionals:
\[
N_8(z)=\max_{f\in\mathcal F} f(z),
\]
where
\[
\mathcal F=
\left\{
\pm(1,0),\ \pm(0,1),\
\pm\frac{(1,1)}{\sqrt2},\
\pm\frac{(1,-1)}{\sqrt2}
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(x,y\in S_{X_8}\) and \(0\le t\le1\). Choose \(f,g\in\mathcal F\) with
\[
N_8(x+t y)=f(x+t y),
\qquad
N_8(x-t y)=g(x-t y).
\]
Then
\[
N_8(x+t y)+N_8(x-t y)
=(f+g)(x)+t(f-g)(y)
\]
and therefore
\[
N_8(x+t y)+N_8(x-t y)
\le
N_8^*(f+g)+tN_8^*(f-g).
\tag{8}
\]

A direct finite evaluation on the eight vertices of the primal octagon shows that, as \(f,g\) range over \(\mathcal F\), the only possible pairs
\[
\bigl(N_8^*(f+g),N_8^*(f-g)\bigr)
\]
are
\[
(2,0),\quad(0,2),\quad(2,2r),\quad(2r,2),\quad(\sqrt2,\sqrt2).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), every affine expression generated by (9) is bounded by
\[
2+2rt.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the only non-immediate comparisons are
\[
2r+2t\le2+2rt,
\]
because
\[
(2+2rt)-(2r+2t)=2(1-r)(1-t)\ge0,
\]
and
\[
\sqrt2(1+t)\le2+2rt,
\]
because
\[
(2+2rt)-\sqrt2(1+t)=(2-\sqrt2)(1-t)\ge0.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{N_8(x+t y)+N_8(x-t y)}2
\le1+rt.
\tag{11}
\]

Since the harmonic mean never exceeds the arithmetic mean,
\[
h(a,b)\le\frac{a+b}{2},
\tag{12}
\]
(11) gives
\[
h\bigl(N_8(x+t y),N_8(x-t y)\bigr)
\le1+rt.
\tag{13}
\]

The bound is sharp for every \(t\in[0,1]\), not merely at the final optimizing scale. Take the pair (3). Direct substitution gives
\[
N_8(u+t v)=N_8(u-t v)=1+rt.
\tag{14}
\]
Hence equality holds in both (12) and (13). Combining (5), (13), and (14),
\[
C_{h,p}(X_8)
=
\sup_{0\le t\le1}
\frac{1+rt}{\|(1,t)\|_p}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(1<p<\infty\), Hölder's inequality gives
\[
1+rt
\le
\left(1+r^q\right)^{1/q}
\left(1+t^p\right)^{1/p}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equality in (16) occurs exactly when
\[
t^{p-1}=r,
\]
which yields (4). Thus (1) follows for finite interior \(p\). For \(p=1\),
\[
\frac{1+rt}{1+t}\le1,
\]
with equality at \(t=0\). For \(p=\infty\), the denominator is one on \([0,1]\), so the maximum is \(1+r=\sqrt2\) at \(t=1\). This proves (1)--(2).

## Verification
The proof is analytic and finite at its only polyhedral step. The support-functional reduction (8) converts the continuum maximization into the five dual-norm pairs in (9), and the scalar optimization is exactly Hölder duality.

The bundled `verify.py` independently enumerates all \(64\) ordered support-functional pairs, reconstructs the five numerical dual-norm pairs from the eight primal vertices, checks inequality (10) on a dense parameter grid, verifies the equal-chord pair (3) for every sampled scale, and checks formula (1) against direct scalar maximization for representative exponents.

The finite grid in the checker is not an infinite proof. The exhaustive support-pair list (9), inequalities (10)--(13), and Hölder equality condition provide the continuum proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Amini–Harandi and Rahimi introduced the family \(C_{\lambda,p}(X)\) in 2019. Their paper treats arbitrary \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), identifies standard specializations using the minimum, power means, and geometric mean, and proves the one-parameter reduction (5). Its explicit planar calculation is the geometric-mean member \(C_{\pi,p}\) on the \(\ell_\infty-\ell_1\) hexagonal plane. Inspection of the complete article finds neither the harmonic mean \(h(a,b)=2ab/(a+b)\) nor a regular-octagonal evaluation.

Dinarvand's later work uses the same \(C_{\lambda,p}\) family to derive normal-structure estimates and explicitly attributes the family to Amini–Harandi and Rahimi. It likewise does not supply the harmonic regular-octagon profile.

The closest indexed regular-octagon results concern different objectives: parameterized von Neumann–Jordan power means, an equilateral geometric-mean constant, and an angle modulus of smoothness. None implies the harmonic profile (1). In particular, replacing the harmonic mean by a quadratic or geometric mean changes the objective before the scale optimization.

A 2026 preprint introduces a skew-parameterized extension by replacing the symmetric pair \(ru+sv,ru-sv\) with \(ru+sv,su-rv\). That altered admissible expression does not contain the symmetric harmonic profile as a stated special case, and its abstract-level indexed material gives no regular-octagonal harmonic computation.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the regular octagonal norm. The support-pair list (9) is special to its eight-facet dual geometry.

The result computes the harmonic member of the Amini–Harandi–Rahimi family for every exponent \(p\); it does not claim a formula for arbitrary \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), for other regular polygons, or for the skew-parameterized family.

The literature comparison is necessarily finite. An equivalent calculation could exist under a different notation for the harmonic mean or an affine-equivalent normalization of the regular octagon.
