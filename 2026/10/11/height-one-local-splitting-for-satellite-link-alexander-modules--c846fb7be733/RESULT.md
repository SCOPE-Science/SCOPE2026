# Height-one local splitting for satellite-link Alexander modules
## Finding
Let \(L=C(P_1,O,\ldots,O)\) be a satellite link in which only the first component of the companion is replaced. Assume the pattern winding vector \(\mathbf w_1\neq 0\) and the companion linking vector \(\boldsymbol\ell_1\neq 0\). Write \(R\) for the Laurent polynomial ring of the satellite, \(M_L=H_1(\widehat X_L;\mathbb Z)\) for its Alexander module, and let \(M_C^R\) and \(M_P^R\) be the companion and augmented-pattern Alexander modules after extension of scalars to \(R\) along the inclusion-induced coefficient maps.

For every height-one prime \(\mathfrak p\subset R\),
\[
(M_L)_{\mathfrak p}\cong (M_C^R)_{\mathfrak p}\oplus(M_P^R)_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Equivalently, the failure of the natural companion-plus-pattern map to be an isomorphism is supported in codimension at least two.

## Assumptions and scope
In the notation of arXiv:2609.36040v1, let \(C=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_\mu\), let \(P_1=P_{11}\cup\cdots\cup P_{1m}\), and set
\[
T_1=\prod_{j=1}^m t_{1j}^{w_{1j}},\qquad
A_1=\prod_{i=2}^\mu t_i^{\ell_{1i}}.
\]
The hypotheses are \(\mathbf w_1\neq0\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell_1\neq0\). The ring is
\[
R=\mathbb Z[t_{11}^{\pm1},\ldots,t_{1m}^{\pm1},t_2^{\pm1},\ldots,t_\mu^{\pm1}].
\]
Let \(P_1'\) be the augmented pattern. Proposition 4.10 of the source identifies
\[
M_C^R\cong H_1(\widehat X_C)\otimes_{R_C}R,
\qquad
M_P^R\cong H_1(\widehat X_{P_1'})\otimes_{R_P}R.
\]
No assertion is made here when either vector vanishes, and no global direct-sum decomposition over \(R\) is asserted.

## Proof
The source's absolute Mayer--Vietoris sequence reduces, under the two nonvanishing hypotheses, to the short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow M_C^R\oplus M_P^R\longrightarrow M_L\longrightarrow\mathfrak K\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here \(\mathfrak K\) is the kernel of the map on zeroth homology associated with the lifted gluing torus. By construction \(\mathfrak K\) is a submodule of
\[
R/\langle T_1-1,A_1-1\rangle.
\]
Therefore both \(T_1-1\) and \(A_1-1\) annihilate \(\mathfrak K\).

Because \(T_1\) uses only the pattern variables and \(A_1\) uses only the unchanged-companion variables, while neither monomial is \(1\), the source proves
\[
\gcd(T_1-1,A_1-1)=1
\]
in the UFD \(R\). Now fix a height-one prime \(\mathfrak p\subset R\). Since a height-one prime of this UFD is generated by an irreducible element, it cannot contain both relatively prime elements \(T_1-1\) and \(A_1-1\). Thus at least one of these annihilators becomes a unit in \(R_{\mathfrak p}\), forcing
\[
\mathfrak K_{\mathfrak p}=0.
\]
Localization is exact, so the localized short exact sequence becomes the claimed isomorphism
\[
(M_L)_{\mathfrak p}\cong(M_C^R)_{\mathfrak p}\oplus(M_P^R)_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
Since \(R_{\mathfrak p}\) is a discrete valuation ring, the isomorphism also identifies the free rank and the complete multiset of local elementary-divisor exponents. This gives the stated codimension-one refinement.

## Verification
The proof was reconstructed directly from Proposition 4.10, the short exact sequence (4.13), and Proposition 4.13 of arXiv:2609.36040v1. The only additional commutative-algebra step is localization at a height-one prime of the Laurent-polynomial UFD \(R\): relative primality of the two annihilators makes one invertible, so the defect module vanishes there. No finite experiment is used as evidence for the infinite statement.

As a cross-check, Theorem 1.11 of the source states that every higher Alexander polynomial is the gcd-convolution of the companion and augmented-pattern higher Alexander polynomials. The local direct-sum theorem implies the same equality after taking every height-one valuation, so it is consistent with, and conceptually explains, that formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Azarpendar and McCulloch prove the exact higher-Alexander-polynomial formula in the nonzero-winding/nonzero-linking case and exhibit the short exact sequence above, but their text does not formulate localization at height-one primes or a codimension-one module splitting. Targeted searches of the full source for “localiz”, “height-one”, “codimension”, and “pseudo” returned no such formulation.

For classical satellite knots, a stronger global Alexander-module direct-sum formula is already standard; for example, Burde--Zieschang--Heusener, *Knots*, Proposition 8.34, states the global module decomposition in the one-component setting. The present statement is therefore not a new claim about ordinary satellite knots. Its content is the multicomponent link setting of arXiv:2609.36040v1, where the source retains a possibly nonzero defect module \(\mathfrak K\): that defect disappears at every height-one localization even though a global split need not follow.

## Limitations
The result does not show that \(M_L\) globally splits over \(R\), does not control codimension-two extension data carried by \(\mathfrak K\), and does not assert compatibility of Blanchfield pairings. It also does not extend to the source's cases where \(\mathbf w_1=0\) or \(\boldsymbol\ell_1=0\). The classical knot-satellite module decomposition is prior work and is excluded from the originality claim.
