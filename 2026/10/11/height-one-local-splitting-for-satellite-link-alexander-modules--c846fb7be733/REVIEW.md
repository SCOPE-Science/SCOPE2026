# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The source gives the exact sequence
\[
0\to M_C^R\oplus M_P^R\to M_L\to\mathfrak K\to0
\]
and places \(\mathfrak K\) inside \(R/\langle T_1-1,A_1-1\rangle\). It also proves \(\gcd(T_1-1,A_1-1)=1\) under the two nonvanishing hypotheses. A height-one prime in the UFD \(R\) cannot contain both annihilators, so one becomes a unit after localization and kills the localized defect. Exactness then yields the direct-sum isomorphism. The proof keeps the global and localized claims distinct.

## Originality
**PASS.** The triggering source proves equality of higher Alexander polynomials and develops the defect module, but targeted full-text searches found no formulation in terms of height-one localization, codimension-one support, or local module splitting. Classical satellite knots have a stronger global module decomposition, so they are explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The new statement concerns the multicomponent companion/pattern setting introduced in the 2026 source and identifies precisely where its nontrivial defect can live.

## Value
**PASS.** This is a structural refinement rather than a restatement of one polynomial identity: every codimension-one localization of the whole Alexander module splits, hence all local invariant factors and valuations split simultaneously. It explains why all higher Alexander-polynomial orders behave as if there were a direct sum while preserving the possibility of genuinely multivariable codimension-two extension data. That separation is useful when deciding which Alexander-module invariants can or cannot detect the satellite gluing defect.

The closest prior literature is the classical knot-satellite Alexander-module decomposition and the 2026 higher-polynomial formula. The former is globally stronger but applies to the one-component knot setting; the latter covers the present link setting but stops at polynomial orders and the unsplit defect exact sequence. The remaining risk is that an older multivariable-link source may have stated this localization consequence in different terminology; targeted searches did not locate one, and the 2026 paper itself presents the companion-and-pattern link setting as its extension of the classical module-theoretic approach.
