# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The statement is an exact classification with quantifiers over all odd integers \(N\ge5\) and every unit step. The fixed-word proof forces a unit index shift; the crossing-exchange obstruction uses the necessary two height pairs from the published definition, not the stronger nesting condition; the unit steps \(\pm1\) are handled separately; and \(\mathsf S\) is checked explicitly. The first change in petal number must therefore be a two-petal addition. The independent executable checks are finite regression tests; the theorem is proved by modular arithmetic, not extrapolation from them.

## Originality (PASS)
The nearest source, Vernitski (arXiv:2610.01596v1, Sections 3, 5–7), explicitly provides the move definitions and numerical computations for unknot words through nine petals, but does not classify the fixed locus of \(\mathsf R\) at arbitrary odd sizes. Colton et al. (arXiv:1812.08930v1, Sections 1–2) provide completeness of the move calculus, not a rigidity classification. The case \((N,s)=(7,2)\) is a particular instance of the all-odd-size theorem; no knot-identification assertion is made. Actual targeted indexed searches were compared at statement level; no all-length theorem was identified. Residual risk: an unindexed earlier result or implicit generalization in other petal-symmetry literature could cover the claim.

## Value (PASS)
The height rotation is an intrinsic allowed symmetry of the move graph, not an arbitrarily chosen parameter slice. Classifying its complete fixed locus and proving a universal move obstruction yields a structural family of \(\varphi(N)-2\) locally trapped diagrams at every odd size, clarifying the mechanism behind scarcity of permitted exchanges beyond the published nine-petal unknot census. The theorem is precisely limited to the move calculus, without unsupported claims about knot types or global hardness.
