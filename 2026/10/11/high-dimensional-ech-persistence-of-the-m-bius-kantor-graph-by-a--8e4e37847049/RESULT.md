# High-dimensional Čech persistence of the Möbius–Kantor graph by Alexander duality

## Finding
Let \(M=G(8,3)\) be the Möbius–Kantor graph, with its edges realized as unit intervals. For real \(r\geq 0\), let \(\mathcal C_r(M)\) be the nerve of the closed balls of radius \(r/2\) in the geometric graph, centered at its sixteen vertices. The **complete reduced integral persistence barcode in degrees \(j\geq 9\)** is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 j & \text{intervals, with multiplicities}\\\hline
 9 & [5,6)\text{ once}\\
 10 & [5,6)\text{ four times}\\
 14 & [6,7)\text{ once}\\
 \text{all other }j\geq 9 & \text{none}.
\end{array}
\]
In particular,
\[
\widetilde H_j(\mathcal C_5(M);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&j=9,\\\mathbb Z^4,&j=10,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad \mathcal C_6(M)\cong S^{14},\qquad \mathcal C_r(M)\text{ is a simplex for }r\geq 7.
\]
The degree-\(9\) and degree-\(10\) classes are born simultaneously at the penultimate nontrivial scale, then disappear at the same scale at which a different degree-\(14\) class is born.

## Assumptions and scope
The generalized Petersen graph \(M=G(8,3)\) has vertex set \(V=\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\}\) and edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), and \(v_iv_{i+3}\), with indices modulo eight. The metric is the intrinsic shortest-path metric on the continuous unit-edge realization. The nerve is defined using **closed** metric balls, so the critical scales belong to the complexes at their left endpoints. We use \(N_t[x]\) for the graph-distance-\(t\) closed neighborhood and \(\operatorname{Ind}(M)\) for the simplicial complex of independent vertex sets. No claim is made about persistence in degrees below nine.

## Proof
**1. Exact metric geometry.** Breadth-first distance enumeration, or direct examination of the three rotational edge orbits, gives, for each vertex \(x\), the distance distribution \((1,3,6,5,1)\) at graph distances zero through four. The unique distance-four vertex is \(\tau(x)\), where \(\tau(u_i)=u_{i+4}\) and \(\tau(v_i)=v_{i+4}\). The map \(\tau\) preserves the edge set. For **every** edge \(xy\),
\[
N_2[x]\cup N_2[y]=V\setminus\{\tau(x),\tau(y)\}.
\]
The supplied verifier checks the identity on all twenty-four edges, not merely on representatives. The intrinsic graph Čech nerve formula says that at even scale \(2t\) its generating simplices are \(N_t[x]\), and at odd scale \(2t+1\) they are \(N_t[x]\cup N_t[y]\) for edges \(xy\). For a real scale between consecutive integers no nerve change occurs. The formula follows by locating a common center for intersecting vertex-centered balls either at a vertex or on an edge.

**2. An exact Alexander dual at scale five.** For a simplicial complex \(K\) on \(V\), define its combinatorial Alexander dual by \(K^*=\{\sigma\subseteq V:V\setminus\sigma\notin K\}\). The maximal faces of \(\mathcal C_5(M)\) are precisely \(V\setminus\{\tau(x),\tau(y)\}\), as \(xy\) ranges over edges. Since \(\tau\) permutes edges, these are exactly the complements of edges. A set belongs to \(\operatorname{Ind}(M)^*\) precisely when its complement contains an edge. Thus there is an **equality of complexes**
\[
\mathcal C_5(M)=\operatorname{Ind}(M)^*.
\]

**3. Integral homology of the independence complex.** Enumerate the 1,090 nonempty independent faces. In increasing stages of the vertex-toggle order
\[
(3,7,13,11,6,5,0,10,14,8,4,15,1,12,2,9),
\]
pair each still-available face not containing the current vertex with its still-available extension by that vertex, when present. This gives 542 disjoint matched incidences and exactly one critical vertex, four critical three-faces, and one critical four-face. Orient every unmatched codimension-one Hasse incidence downward and every matched incidence upward. A topological sort of all 4,272 incidences visits every one of the 1,090 faces, establishing **acyclicity**. Exact integer elementary cancellation along all 542 unit-incidence pivots yields a reduced integral chain complex with critical-cell counts \((1,0,0,4,1)\) in dimensions zero through four and **zero differentials**. Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_3(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4,\qquad
\widetilde H_4(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
with all other reduced homology zero and no torsion. The reduced integral cohomology has the same free ranks. By combinatorial Alexander duality on sixteen vertices,
\[
\widetilde H_j(\mathcal C_5(M);\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{13-j}(\operatorname{Ind}(M);\mathbb Z).
\]
The asserted degree-nine and degree-ten groups follow. The discrete-Morse construction, acyclicity, and exact integer cancellation are independently regenerated by the included standalone verifier; they are not inferred from rational or finite-field homology alone.

**4. Adjacent scales and persistence.** For scale four, each of the sixteen maximal generating faces \(N_2[x]\) has ten vertices. Any two intersect in at most seven vertices. Every nine-simplex therefore has an eight-dimensional boundary face not belonging to any other nine-simplex; comparison of these boundary coefficients proves that \(\partial_9\) is injective, hence \(\widetilde H_9(\mathcal C_4(M);\mathbb Z)=0\). There are no faces of dimension ten or above. For all smaller real scales, the generating simplices have at most six vertices, so there is no reduced homology in degrees nine or above. At scale six, \(N_3[x]=V\setminus\{\tau(x)\}\); all sixteen facets of the boundary of the fifteen-simplex appear, giving \(\mathcal C_6(M)=\partial\Delta^{15}\cong S^{14}\). At scale seven, every generating union \(N_3[x]\cup N_3[y]\) for an edge \(xy\) equals \(V\), giving the full simplex for every subsequent scale. Together with the exact descriptions of scale five, these facts identify every high-degree birth and death and prove the claimed intervals.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla, and Singh establish the intrinsic graph Čech construction and even/odd neighborhood formulas, and investigate hypercube graphs; their results do not state the Möbius–Kantor Alexander-dual identification or this high-degree integral barcode. Existing computations of the ordinary matching complex or of Vietoris–Rips complexes of the same graph use different defining simplices. A previously established homotopy classification of the Möbius–Kantor independence complex already determines its integral homology, which is **not claimed as new here**. The present construction independently verifies that premise and adds the metric antipodal identity, the **equality of the scale-five Čech nerve with the Alexander dual**, and all subsequent and preceding high-dimensional persistence thresholds. The new claim is this specific Čech-duality and persistence theorem, not the independence-complex homotopy type or the general Alexander-duality theorem.

## Limitations
This determines integral persistent homology **only in degrees at least nine**, not the full low-degree barcode. The acyclic matching and integer reductions are finite exact computations on a named sixteen-vertex graph, supported by an executable complete checker; they do not establish corresponding statements for arbitrary generalized Petersen graphs. Some potentially equivalent results may be unindexed, and no independent audit has been carried out.
