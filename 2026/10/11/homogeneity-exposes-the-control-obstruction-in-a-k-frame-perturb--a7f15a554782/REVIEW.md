# Review of Homogeneity exposes the control obstruction in a \(K\)-frame perturbation theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The target theorem and its proof were reconstructed from the full article. The universal coefficient quantifier permits scaling \(c\mapsto tc\); after division by \(t\) and passage to \(t\downarrow0\), the degree-two \(\alpha\)-term vanishes exactly. The resulting synthesis estimate proves ordinary Besselness on finitely supported sequences and hence on \(\ell^2\). The two-dimensional counterexample satisfies every stated source hypothesis with \(K=I\), \(\alpha=\beta=0\), and \(\gamma=\varepsilon<1\), while \(CS_G\) is non-self-adjoint. The sharpness example attains the repaired ordinary bound exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The closest earlier perturbation results inspected use homogeneous coefficient inequalities. The same authors' 2016 compact-perturbation preprint contains a homogeneous \(K\)-frame perturbation theorem, not the 2018 nonhomogeneous Theorem 4.3. The 2020 correction by Mahmoudieh--Hosseinnezhad--Abbaspour Tabadkan identifies commutation as necessary in a different controlled \(K\)-frame proposition; it does not address this perturbation theorem, the scaling collapse of \(\alpha\), the sharp ordinary Bessel constant, or the explicit counterexample. Source-specific searches using the theorem number, coefficient structure, homogeneity formulation, commutation obstruction, and sharp-bound formulation did not locate a checked source covering the final combined claim.

## Value
PASS. The finding is not a cosmetic bound improvement. The published coefficient-dependent expression cannot serve as a uniform Bessel bound, and the stated controlled conclusion fails even for an arbitrarily small two-dimensional perturbation of an orthonormal basis. The repair separates exactly what the perturbation hypothesis does guarantee from the extra geometric compatibility needed for a controlled Bessel sequence, while providing a sharp universal ordinary Bessel constant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The source's neighboring Theorem 4.2 and the classical Casazza--Christensen perturbation theorem are homogeneous and therefore do not contain the degree-two coefficient term. The 2020 controlled \(K\)-frame correction supplies prior literature for the general commutation mechanism, so originality is claimed only for its application to Theorem 4.3 together with the homogeneity collapse, counterexample, and sharp repaired bound. The result does not claim that the source's controlled upper bound equals the ordinary Bessel bound \(B_F\), and it makes no lower-frame conclusion for \(G\).
