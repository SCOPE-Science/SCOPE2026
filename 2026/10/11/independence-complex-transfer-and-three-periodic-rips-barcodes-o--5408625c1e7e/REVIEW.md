# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns every finite bipartite forbidden graph \(H\) satisfying the strict two-sided degree bounds, not only the small examples. For every pair in the graph complement \(G\), the proof reconstructs the exact shortest-path distance (same side two; retained cross-edge one; missing cross-edge three). These are enough to identify the entire scale-two flag complex with \(\operatorname{Ind}(H)\) *simplicially*. The oriented simplicial two-chain for each even graph cycle proves that the first homology inclusion from scale one to scale two is zero over \(\mathbb Z\). The path and cycle cofibre arguments reconstruct the previously known three-periodic independence-complex types, and the join of disjoint even cycles gives the stated sphere degree and multiplicity. Finally, the graph cycle rank, connectivity, and terminal scale-three simplex determine all bar births/deaths over any field. Integer and degree boundaries are checked: \(n\ge5\) is essential to the displayed sufficient inequalities for the two-factor family; every cycle has length at least four, and the sphere dimension \(D\ge2\). The included executable tests enumerate all graph distances, all independent faces and boundary ranks in seventeen natural examples and two irregular examples, and independently check a cycle basis of mod-two fillings. Such finite checks are corroboration; the all-parameter proof is in `RESULT.md`. Residual risk: an incorrect interpretation of scale conventions would alter the barcode; the result explicitly uses inclusive scales, the unit-edge graph metric, and births at integer thresholds.

## Originality — PASS
The standard relation \(\operatorname{Cl}(\overline H)=\operatorname{Ind}(H)\) is explicitly not claimed as new. Adamaszek's 2013 graph-power paper identifies graph-square clique complexes with graph-metric Rips complexes and proves a broad homotopy-realization theorem, but does not establish the exact bipartite forbidden-edge distance criterion, the integral vanishing first-persistence map, or the complete deleted-two-factor barcode. Adamaszek's 2012 independence-complex paper reproduces Kozlov's cycle calculations and the join identity; these supply prior inputs, not the new metric realization and scale-one-to-scale-two map computation. Adamaszek–Adams (2017) classifies Rips complexes of circles, not shortest-path metrics on complements of arbitrary bipartite forbidden graphs. Public semantic comparisons also located projective-plane incidence-Rips calculations and a hypercube independence-complex reduction; those use different graph metrics and give no implication to the general complement transfer theorem. Searches for bipartite graph-square complements, exact \(2\)-factor cycle parameters, and equivalent barcode descriptions did not produce a stronger covering statement. The absence of a result in an index is not proof of historical priority. Residual originality risk: an equivalent observation in unindexed graph-square or independence-complex literature; the claimed novelty is the combined exact transfer and full persistence theorem, not the cycle formula itself.

## Value — PASS
This is an explicit *all-forbidden-graph* transfer law with an exact simplicial identification, not just an abstract existence or qualitative homotopy realization: it converts a large, very dense graph's metric complex into a sparse forbidden graph's independent-set complex. The integral zero-map theorem adds persistence information not recoverable from endpoint Betti numbers alone. Applied to all spanning bipartite \(2\)-factors, it classifies a natural family of \((n-2)\)-regular graphs for every \(n\ge5\), with a striking mod-three change in sphere multiplicities and degrees and a complete field-independent barcode. These are meaningful structural constraints and benchmark examples for metric graph filtrations. Limitation: the answer uses classical three-periodic cycle independence-complex calculations, so the new aspect is their exact graph-metric transfer and filtration, not the discrete cycle topology in isolation.
