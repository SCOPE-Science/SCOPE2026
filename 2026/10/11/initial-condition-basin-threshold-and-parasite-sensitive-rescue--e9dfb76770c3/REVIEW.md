# Scientific comparison and limitations

**Correctness — PASS.** Positivity, boundedness, and asymptotic extinction of the host reduce the commensal equation to a scalar asymptotically autonomous problem with three isolated equilibria. Order preservation, open hyperbolic sink basins, comparison at the lower equilibrium, and exponential separation of hypothetical twin saddle-limit solutions rigorously yield exactly one threshold and the claimed three-way classification. A stable-manifold pullback and the bounded first-variation equation yield the integral derivative, while logistic monotonicity gives its strict parasite dependence. Boundary cases were excluded explicitly; numerical examples were not treated as proof.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** The 2021 Puspitasari analysis already contains two positive root branches, so their existence is acknowledged as prior work. The 2019 Chen and the 2022 Chen–Zhou–Lin and Liu–Yue articles do not give the exact host/parasite-initial-state-dependent basin threshold or the displayed sensitivity integral. An unrelated bistability theorem in a two-gene system and a threshold theorem in an age-structured population model do not imply the present three-species result. Search limitations leave an explicit residual risk of unindexed exact basin results.

**Value — PASS.** Bistability makes equilibrium existence and linear eigenvalues insufficient to predict the outcome from an arbitrary physical initial state. The threshold theorem resolves the complete extinction-versus-survival decision on a natural open harvesting regime, including the single saddle-attracting trajectory. Its quantitative response to initial host and parasite densities separates transient rescue from long-run coexistence and supplies an interpretable first-order ecological sensitivity; it is not a re-count of known equilibria.

**Limitations and closest literature.** The literature already gives the possible two-equilibrium configuration. This result applies only when the parasite ultimately excludes the host and the harvest law has distinct lower and upper positive branches. A complete closed form for the threshold at general host density, extension to fold parameters, and the entire host-persistent regime remain open. Similar scalar-order methods exist elsewhere but were not found to contain this full statement for the cited model.
