# Integral and particle-swap homology of two-point sink configurations on cactus graphs

## Finding
For any finite connected simple cactus graph \(G\), write \(E=|E(G)|\) and \(b=E-|V(G)|+1\). Let \(Y=\operatorname{Conf}^{\mathrm{sink}}_2(G,V(G))\) be the ordered two-particle configuration space with coincidences permitted precisely at graph vertices. Its integral homology is free, with
\[
H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^{E+b},&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z^{b(b-1)},&i=2,\\
0,&i\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
The involution \(\tau\) interchanging the two labels has rational eigenspace dimensions
\[
\dim H_1(Y;\mathbb Q)^+=b,\quad \dim H_1(Y;\mathbb Q)^-=E,
\qquad
\dim H_2(Y;\mathbb Q)^+=\dim H_2(Y;\mathbb Q)^-=\binom b2.
\]
The statement includes trees (\(b=0\)) and unicyclic graphs (\(b=1\)).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, connected and simple, with its stated vertices as the *only* collision sites; subdivisions therefore change the problem. A cactus means each edge belongs to at most one simple cycle; different cycles may meet at vertices but never share an edge. The resulting \(b\) simple cycles have pairwise disjoint edge supports. All homology is singular homology; the cell-level proof uses the natural product cell structure. The involution exchanges labels, not graph vertices.

## Proof
Let \(X=G\times G\). The discrete sink configuration model \(D\subset X\), consisting of all product cells except the \(E\) diagonal squares \(e\times e\), is a homotopy-equivalent subcomplex of \(Y\): this is the ordered two-coordinate case of Ramos's model and inclusion theorem. In particular, it is connected because it retains the entire connected one-skeleton of \(X\), and is two-dimensional.

There are no relative cellular generators in degrees other than two, and the missing \(E\) oriented diagonal squares give \(H_2(X,D;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^E\). Let \(U=H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\). Product Künneth and the homology long exact sequence give
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(D;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow U\otimes U
\xrightarrow{\Phi}\mathbb Z^E
\longrightarrow H_1(D;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow U\oplus U\longrightarrow0.
\]
For oriented integer cycles \(z,w\) and an oriented edge \(e\), the coordinate of \(\Phi(z\otimes w)\) indexed by \(e\) is \(z_e w_e\). This follows directly by reading the coefficient of \(e\times e\) in the cellular cross product; no unverified homological cancellation is used.

Choose each of the \(b\) edge-disjoint simple cycles \(c_1,\ldots,c_b\), oriented arbitrarily. These are an integral basis of \(U\), since deleting one edge from each cycle leaves a spanning tree. When \(i\ne j\), their edge supports do not meet and \(\Phi(c_i\otimes c_j)=0\). On the diagonal, \(\Phi(c_i\otimes c_i)\) has coefficient one on every edge of the \(i\)-th cycle and zero elsewhere. These \(b\) vectors have disjoint supports and are primitive: selecting one edge per cycle gives a unimodular minor. Therefore \(\ker\Phi\) is freely generated by the \(b(b-1)\) off-diagonal tensors, and \(\operatorname{coker}\Phi\cong\mathbb Z^{E-b}\) is free. The exact sequence for \(H_1\) splits as a sequence of abelian groups because its quotient \(U\oplus U\) is free, giving \(\operatorname{rank}H_1=(E-b)+2b=E+b\). This proves all asserted integral groups, including absence of torsion.

The label swap exchanges product factors and reverses the orientation of every two-dimensional product square, so it acts on \(U\otimes U\) as \(z\otimes w\mapsto-w\otimes z\). It acts on the diagonal-square relative group \(\mathbb Q^E\) as minus the identity. Hence \(H_2(D;\mathbb Q)\) is the span of ordered off-diagonal tensors \(c_i\otimes c_j\), with \(\tau(c_i\otimes c_j)=-c_j\otimes c_i\); each unordered pair of distinct cycles contributes one positive and one negative eigenvector. The cokernel of \(\Phi\) is entirely negative, while \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)=U\oplus U\) has one copy of each swap sign. Maschke splitting over \(\mathbb Q\) gives \(b\) positive and \((E-b)+b=E\) negative dimensions in degree one. The claimed eigenspaces follow.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_cactus.py` using only Python's standard library. The script builds the complete signed product cellular boundary matrices for seven cactus fixtures, performs independent eliminations modulo \(2,3,5,7\), checks the predicted homology dimensions and the fixed-cell Lefschetz equality, and tests \(K_4\) separately as an example outside the cactus assumption. For the two-triangle shared-vertex graph, the cells number \((25,60,30)\), both boundary ranks are \((24,28)\) and Betti ranks are \((1,8,2)\) over all four fields. For three edge-disjoint triangular cycles in a chain, the cells number \((49,126,72)\), boundary ranks \((48,66)\), and Betti ranks \((1,12,6)\). These finite checks independently test orientation and counting; the integral and infinite-family conclusions come from the proof above rather than from finite fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Chettih and Lütgehetmann, arXiv:1612.08290 (first public 2016-12-25), introduced graph configuration spaces with sinks and treated basic source spaces; their principal homology theorem concerns ordinary configurations on trees with loops. Ramos, arXiv:1703.05535 (first public 2017-03-16), defines the general discrete sink model (Definition 3.1), proves its homotopy equivalence to the continuous sink space (Theorem 3.2), and determines integral homology for trees (Theorem D). Its general-graph results primarily concern homological stability with the number of particles. Neither inspected result supplies the fixed-two-particle cactus classification and the full label-swap character here. Published cycle or complete-graph special cases do not imply the edge-disjoint multi-cycle formula. The contribution is a complete infinite-family calculation whose dependence on graph combinatorics collapses to \(E\) and \(b\) within the cactus class.

## Limitations
The result concerns two **ordered** particles, all graph vertices designated sinks, and simple cactus graphs. It does not classify fundamental groups, integral \(\mathbb Z[C_2]\)-lattice decompositions, cohomology products or configurations of three or more particles. It does not imply the same Betti formulas for non-cactus graphs: shared cycle edges can enlarge the image of \(\Phi\). The homology statement is not a homotopy-equivalence classification. Literature overlap remains subject to independent audit; a failed search is not a proof that no earlier formulation exists.
