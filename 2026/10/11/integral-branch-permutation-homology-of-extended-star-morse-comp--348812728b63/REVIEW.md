# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The ordinary Morse complex of a tree equals its Hasse-incidence matching complex. Deleting the central incidences produces an \(n\)-fold join of two-component complexes, with a top integral generator transforming by the sign representation. The link of every central incidence includes into the deletion complex by a homotopy equivalence; the relative homology exact sequence identifies the top integral module as the kernel of a sign-twisted augmentation. The argument is uniform in every integer \(n\ge2\), includes boundary \(n=2\), and rules out integral torsion. A separately implemented signed-face trace and augmented mod-two boundary checker verifies the first four parameter values without substituting for the proof.

## Originality — PASS
Donovan–Scoville (2022 preprint, Theorem 15 and Corollary 17) gives the same complexes' nonequivariant wedge rank \(n-1\), but supplies no induced permutation character. Knowing the rank and the abstract group action is not sufficient to determine the integral module; no equivariant statement or character occurs in the inspected primary text. Focused searches of published mathematical records and primary literature found no statement implying this integral action. This does not prove complete global uniqueness; an equivalent unindexed representation calculation remains possible. The closely related earlier homotopy-and-strong-homotopy work was accessible here only as an abstract, so additional unpublished or inaccessible work is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The central geometric question is how relabeling the identical branches acts on the naturally occurring top cycles of a symmetric graph's Morse complex. The result determines the complete **integral** action for an infinite family, not merely an Euler characteristic or a known Betti number. The sign twist distinguishes the actual representation from the ordinary standard representation and determines the invariant homology of all symmetric arm quotients over characteristic zero. The exact equivariant augmentation sequence is a structural reason for the multiplicity and the action.

## Limits
No equivariant wedge splitting, unrelated-graph extension, or external verification is asserted; finite computations corroborate but do not certify the uniform theorem.
