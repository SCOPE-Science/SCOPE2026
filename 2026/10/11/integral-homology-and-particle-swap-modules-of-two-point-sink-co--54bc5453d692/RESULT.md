# Integral homology and particle-swap modules of two-point sink configurations on complete bipartite graphs

## Finding

Let \(m,n\ge2\), and let \(G=K_{m,n}\) have the usual cellular structure with one edge between each pair of vertices in opposite parts. Declare **all original vertices** to be sinks. The ordered configuration space \(Y_{m,n}=\operatorname{Conf}^{\mathrm{sink}}_2(G,V(G))\) consists of pairs of positions that can coincide at graph vertices, but cannot coincide at any interior point of an edge. The particles retain their labels.

Put \(E=mn\), \(b=(m-1)(n-1)\), and
\[
\rho(m,n)=\begin{cases}
1,&m=n=2,\\
\max(m,n),&\min(m,n)=2<\max(m,n),\\
mn,&m,n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, **for every** \(m,n\ge2\), the integral homology is torsion-free and
\[
H_i(Y_{m,n};\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^{\,2b+E-\rho(m,n)},&i=1,\\
\mathbb Z^{\,b^2-\rho(m,n)},&i=2,\\
0,&i>2.
\end{cases}
\]

The following describes the complete **rational** representations of the side-preserving vertex-symmetry group \(S_m\times S_n\) and the particle transposition \(\tau\). Write \(P_t=\mathbb Q^t\) for the permutation module, \(W_t=\ker(P_t\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q)\) for its standard submodule, \(L_t=\bigwedge^2W_t\), and, for \(t\ge3\),
\[
A_t=\ker\!\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^2 W_t\xrightarrow{\mu_t}P_t\bigr),
\qquad \mu_t(a\odot b)=(a_1b_1,\ldots,a_tb_t).
\]
Here \(A_3=0\), while for \(t\ge4\) this is the irreducible Specht module indexed by \((t-2,2)\). Denote by \(U=W_m\boxtimes W_n\) the outer tensor product, and by \(H_i^+\) and \(H_i^-\) the \(+1\) and \(-1\) particle-swap eigenspaces in \(H_i(Y_{m,n};\mathbb Q)\). All displayed outer products are over \(S_m\times S_n\).

When \(m,n\ge3\), one has
\[
H_1^+\cong U,\qquad H_1^-\cong U,
\]
\[
H_2^+\cong(L_m\boxtimes\operatorname{Sym}^2W_n)
\oplus(\operatorname{Sym}^2W_m\boxtimes L_n),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_2^-\cong{}&(A_m\boxtimes P_n)\oplus(P_m\boxtimes A_n)\\
&\oplus(A_m\boxtimes A_n)\oplus(L_m\boxtimes L_n).
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(m=2\) and \(n\ge3\), let \(\varepsilon_2\) be the sign character of \(S_2\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_1^+&\cong\varepsilon_2\boxtimes W_n,\\
H_1^-&\cong(\varepsilon_2\boxtimes W_n)\oplus(\varepsilon_2\boxtimes P_n),\\
H_2^+&\cong\mathbf1_2\boxtimes L_n,\\
H_2^-&\cong\mathbf1_2\boxtimes A_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(n=2<m\), interchange the two graph parts. For \(m=n=2\), with \(U=\varepsilon_2\boxtimes\varepsilon_2\),
\[
H_1^+\cong U,\qquad
H_1^-\cong(\varepsilon_2\boxtimes\mathbf1_2)
\oplus(\mathbf1_2\boxtimes\varepsilon_2)\oplus2U,
\qquad H_2=0.
\]
In every case \(H_0\) is the trivial representation, and the higher groups vanish. These formulas concern the natural side-preserving subgroup; when the graph parts have equal size, the additional automorphism interchanging the two parts is not separately decomposed.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is finite, simple, and connected, with \(m,n\ge2\), and the sink set is exactly its original vertex set, not an arbitrary subset. There are exactly two ordered particles. An edge interior is not a sink; two particles can simultaneously occupy different interiors of the same edge, but may not coincide there. The graph is given its usual intrinsic topology, not a discrete vertex topology. Only graph-part-preserving permutations and label transposition are represented in the stated module decomposition.

The result is an all-size statement. Computational checks for finite graph sizes are **not** offered as proof of its unbounded quantifiers; the proof below is integral and uniform.

## Proof

**1. Exact cellular reduction.** For any graph with every vertex a sink, the sink discretization theorem of Ramos (Theorem 3.2 of the referenced source, building on Chettih--Lütgehetmann) gives a homotopy equivalence from the cubical subcomplex \(D\subset G\times G\) consisting of all product cells except the open squares \(e\times e\), one for each edge \(e\). Consequently \(D\simeq Y_{m,n}\). Orient all graph edges from the first part to the second. The pair \((G\times G,D)\) has only relative degree-two cells, freely generated by \(e\times e\), so
\[
H_2(G\times G,D;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{E},
\qquad H_j(G\times G,D;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ne2).
\]
The cellular one-skeleton of \(D\) coincides with the connected one-skeleton of \(G\times G\), and \(D\) has no cells in degree above two.

**2. The relative exact sequence.** The graph cycle lattice \(C=H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\) has rank \(b\). By the integral Künneth theorem, \(H_2(G\times G;\mathbb Z)\cong C\otimes C\) and \(H_1(G\times G;\mathbb Z)\cong C\oplus C\). The long exact sequence of the pair gives
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(D;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow C\otimes C
\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }\mathbb Z^{E}
\longrightarrow H_1(D;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow C\oplus C\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(a=(a_e)\) and \(c=(c_e)\) are integral graph cycles, their exterior-product chain has coefficient \(a_ec_e\) on the deleted square \(e\times e\), hence
\[
\Phi(a\otimes c)=(a_ec_e)_{e\in E(G)}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the entire question reduces to the integral image of coordinatewise products of graph cycles.

**3. Integral image and saturation.** Identify a cycle on \(K_{m,n}\) with an integer \(m\)-by-\(n\) array with zero row and column sums. Rectangles with signed corner entries \(+1,-1,-1,+1\) form an integral basis. If \(m,n\ge3\), for any fixed entry \((i,j)\), choose two rectangles using that corner whose other row choices are distinct and whose other column choices are distinct. Their supports meet **only** in \((i,j)\); their coordinatewise product is its unit vector, up to sign. Therefore \(\Phi\) is onto \(\mathbb Z^{mn}\).

If \(m=2\) and \(n\ge3\), each zero-sum cycle has the form \((z,-z)\) where \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) has coordinate sum zero. Thus every product has two *equal* rows. For each column \(j\), select distinct other columns \(k,\ell\). The product of the two rectangles on column pairs \((j,k)\) and \((j,\ell)\) is the equal-row unit at column \(j\). Hence the image is **exactly** the saturated equal-row sublattice \(\{(z,z):z\in\mathbb Z^n\}\), of rank \(n\). The case \(n=2<m\) follows by interchanging parts. Finally, for \(m=n=2\), the cycle group is generated by one four-cycle, and its square is the primitive all-one vector in \(\mathbb Z^4\). Its image has rank one. In every case \(\operatorname{coker}\Phi\) is free abelian and \(\operatorname{rank}\Phi=\rho(m,n)\).

Sequence (1) now gives \(H_2(D;\mathbb Z)\cong\ker\Phi\cong\mathbb Z^{b^2-\rho}\). Since \(\operatorname{coker}\Phi\) and \(C\oplus C\) are free, the short exact sequence for \(H_1(D;\mathbb Z)\) splits as abelian groups, yielding the stated torsion-free rank \(2b+E-\rho\). Connectedness gives \(H_0=\mathbb Z\), and dimension two excludes higher homology.

**4. Equivariant structure and particle-swap signs.** The side-preserving group acts on the integral cycle lattice through the rational module \(U=W_m\boxtimes W_n\) and on deleted squares through \(P_m\boxtimes P_n\). Since product-cell orientations use the standard cross-product convention, transposing the particle factors acts as **minus the tensor-factor swap** on \(H_2(G\times G;\mathbb Q)=U\otimes U\), and as minus the identity on the diagonal-square relative module. On \(H_1(G\times G;\mathbb Q)=U\oplus U\), transposition simply exchanges the summands. The rational equivariant sequence (1) therefore implies
\[
H_2^+\cong\bigwedge^2U,
\quad H_2^-\cong\ker\!\left(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\xrightarrow{\Phi}P_m\boxtimes P_n\right),
\]
\[
H_1^+\cong U,\qquad H_1^-\cong U\oplus\operatorname{coker}(\Phi\otimes\mathbb Q).
\tag{3}
\]
Equivariant splitting here uses semisimplicity over \(\mathbb Q\), and is not asserted integrally.

By the symmetric- and alternating-square Cauchy identities,
\[
\bigwedge^2(W_m\boxtimes W_n)
\cong(L_m\boxtimes\operatorname{Sym}^2W_n)
\oplus(\operatorname{Sym}^2W_m\boxtimes L_n),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2(W_m\boxtimes W_n)
\cong(\operatorname{Sym}^2W_m\boxtimes\operatorname{Sym}^2W_n)
\oplus(L_m\boxtimes L_n).
\tag{4}
\]
The map \(\Phi\) vanishes on the \(L_m\boxtimes L_n\) term and restricts on the symmetric term to \(\mu_m\boxtimes\mu_n\), where \(\mu_t\) is coordinatewise multiplication. If \(t\ge3\), pick three distinct indices \(i,j,k\): the product \((e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_k)=e_i\) proves \(\mu_t\) surjective. Thus \(\operatorname{Sym}^2W_t\cong P_t\oplus A_t\) as rational \(S_t\)-modules, which yields the \(m,n\ge3\) formulas in the Finding. Standard representation theory identifies \(A_t\) with partition \((t-2,2)\) for \(t\ge4\); for \(t=3\) the kernel is zero.

For \(t=2\), \(W_2=\varepsilon_2\) and \(\mu_2\) has only the one-dimensional **trivial** submodule \(\langle(1,1)\rangle\) as image inside \(P_2\). Substituting this into (3) and (4) gives the thin-side formulas, including the \(m=n=2\) specialization. This proves the asserted rational representation decompositions for all graph sizes in the specified domain.

## Verification

A self-contained standard-library checker is provided as `verify.py`. It reconstructs the cubical cellular chains of \(D\), verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), computes boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\), independently checks ranks of the coordinatewise cycle-product map, and verifies character identities by signed Lefschetz traces under every side-preserving permutation combined with each of the two particle-swap choices.

The tested sizes are \((m,n)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4)\). Their degree-\(0,1,2\) Betti vectors are respectively \((1,5,0),(1,7,1),(1,10,5),(1,13,11),(1,8,7),(1,12,24),(1,18,65)\). Altogether the script checks \(2120\) combined symmetry cases and prints `VERIFY_OK`. Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the file. The computations are cross-checks of the uniform proof, not an exhaustive all-size certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Chettih--Lütgehetmann introduced sink configurations in their study of trees with loops; Ramos subsequently provided the general cubical sink model used in step 1. Their stated structural and explicit classification results do not provide the above bipartite all-size **integral ranks together with all rational side-permutation/particle-transposition isotypes**. The sharp image change when one graph part drops from three vertices to two is obtained here from the cycle-product lattice, not from the general existence of the cellular model.

Ramos--Yun calculated homology representations of ordinary, collision-free two-particle configurations of complete bipartite graphs. That is a **different space**: their coordinates cannot coincide even at graph vertices. For example, their ordinary \(K_{3,3}\) computation has one-dimensional second rational homology, whereas the sink configuration considered here has dimension seven. The cited ordinary configuration results therefore do not imply the stated sink-space modules.

The previously obtained sink-configuration calculation for a cycle graph covers the special case \(K_{2,2}\cong C_4\). It does not cover \(K_{2,n}\) for \(n\ge3\), which has overlapping cycles, or \(K_{m,n}\) with both parts at least three. No assertion of uniqueness against unpublished work is made.

## Limitations

All vertex collisions must be allowed as specified; different sink sets can change the homotopy type. The module decomposition is rational, even though the underlying homology groups are integral and torsion-free. The extra side-exchanging symmetry present when \(m=n\) is not separately analyzed. Nothing is proved here for three or more particles, other graph families, fundamental-group structure, or the cohomology ring. Searches cannot exclude an uncatalogued equivalent result; the particularly relevant recent complete-bipartite representation paper addresses ordinary collision-free rather than sink configurations. The finite checks do not substitute for the uniform proof.
