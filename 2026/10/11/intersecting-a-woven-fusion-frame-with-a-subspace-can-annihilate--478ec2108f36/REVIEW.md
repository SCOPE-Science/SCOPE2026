# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The two source families are identical coordinate-axis Parseval fusion frames on a two-point measure space, so every weaving has energy \(\|h\|^2\). Intersecting both coordinate axes with \(W=\operatorname{span}\{e_1+e_2\}\) gives zero subspaces, making every restricted weaving have zero lower energy.

The source proof's critical identity is false without commutation:
\[
P_{\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}}P_W
\ne
P_{\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}\cap W}.
\]
When \(P_W\) commutes with each fusion-subspace projection, the standard projection-product theorem gives equality with the intersection projection and the original universal bounds are preserved.

## Originality
PASS. Exact source, theorem-number, arXiv, DOI, intersection, projection-product, commutation, erratum, and counterexample formulations were checked against the relevant literature and indexed scientific records.

Earlier fusion-intersection theory explicitly assumes commuting projections, but does not state that the unrestricted continuous weaving theorem fails, and does not supply this two-atom counterexample. No checked indexed correction of Theorem 2.2 was found.

## Value
PASS. The theorem purports to preserve woven fusion-frame stability under restriction to an arbitrary closed subspace, a basic operation on subspace-valued data. The counterexample shows that the operation can destroy every analysis channel simultaneously. The repair pinpoints the exact invalid projection identity in the proof and replaces it with the standard structural hypothesis used in prior intersection theory.

## Closest literature and limitations
Bhandari--Mukherjee explicitly require commuting orthogonal projections in their fusion-intersection results. This is the closest structural precedent and supports the repair.

Commutation is sufficient rather than claimed necessary. Some noncommuting families can still survive intersection. No other result of the target paper is assessed.
