# Inverse classification of projection-width distributions for tangential symmetric polygons

## Finding
For every centrally symmetric convex polygon circumscribed about a Euclidean circle, the distribution of its widths in uniformly random directions remembers **exactly the multiset of successive tangent-normal gaps, but not their cyclic order**. More precisely, let \(P\) have \(2m\) sides, let \(r\) be its inradius, and let \(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m\) be the first half of the successive outward unit-normal angular gaps. Put \(\delta_j=\Delta_j/2\), and let \(U\) be uniform on unoriented directions. For normalized width \(T=w_P(U)/(2r)\),
\[
 \Pr(T>t)=\begin{cases}
 1,&t<1,\\
 \displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{j:\ t<\sec\delta_j}
 \arccos(t\cos\delta_j),&t\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every threshold \(\tau=\sec\delta_j>1\) is a square-root singularity of the density. Its location gives the gap \(2\delta_j\), and its amplitude gives the number of occurrences of that gap. The lower endpoint of the unnormalized width distribution is \(2r\), so the law determines the inradius as well. Two such polygons have identical width distributions **if and only if** their inradii and multisets of normal gaps agree.

The resulting complete congruence classification is as follows. The cyclic order of the \(m\) gaps modulo rotations and reversal determines the Euclidean congruence class, whereas the random-width law forgets that order. With \(m\le3\) any gap permutation is obtained by a rotation or reversal, so two tangential centrally symmetric quadrilaterals or hexagons with equal width laws are congruent. With pairwise distinct gaps and \(m\ge4\), exactly \((m-1)!/2\) noncongruent polygons share one law. The first ambiguity occurs among centrally symmetric octagons, where a generic law has three congruence classes.

The same law also fixes the polygon's area and perimeter:
\[
 \operatorname{Area}(P)=2r^2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tan\delta_j,
 \qquad \operatorname{Perimeter}(P)=4r\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tan\delta_j.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A polygon is a compact convex Euclidean polygon with nonempty interior. It is *tangential* here when every edge supports the same inscribed circle, and *centrally symmetric* when a half-turn about its unique center preserves it. By the symmetry, the circle's center is the polygon's center. All successive outward-normal angular gaps lie strictly in \((0,\pi)\); their full sum is \(2\pi\), and the ordered first half satisfies \(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Delta_j=\pi\). Conversely, any positive \(m\)-tuple summing to \(\pi\), with \(m\ge2\), produces such a polygon by intersecting the corresponding tangent halfplanes and their antipodal copies. The width in direction \(u\) is the length of the orthogonal projection onto the line generated by \(u\); it is \(h_P(u)+h_P(-u)\). Random direction means normalized Lebesgue measure on the space of unoriented lines, represented by \([0,\pi)\). Equality of laws is exact equality of Borel probability measures, not merely equality of finitely many moments or empirical histograms.

## Proof
**Support arc for one normal gap.** Translate the circle center to the origin and index outward normal angles cyclically by \(\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_{2m-1}\), with \(\theta_{j+m}=\theta_j+\pi\). Consecutive tangent lines at angles \(\theta_j\) and \(\theta_{j+1}\) meet at the vertex
\[
 v_j=r\sec(\Delta_j/2)(\cos\mu_j,\sin\mu_j),\qquad
 \mu_j=(\theta_j+\theta_{j+1})/2.
\]
For any direction angle \(\phi\in[\theta_j,\theta_{j+1}]\), this vertex maximizes the support scalar product, so
\[
 h_P(\phi)=r\sec(\Delta_j/2)\cos(\phi-\mu_j).
\]
Indeed, the polygon is the intersection of all tangent halfplanes, its vertices occur at consecutive line intersections, and the outer-normal cone at this vertex consists precisely of that interval. Every supporting side appears because the normals are distinct points of the strictly convex unit circle. By central symmetry, \(w_P(\phi)=2h_P(\phi)\), and only \(m\) such intervals need be counted modulo \(\pi\).

**Exact distribution.** For a fixed gap, write \(\delta=\Delta/2\). The ratio \(h_P/r\) runs from \(1\) at the two ends of the angular interval to \(\sec\delta\) at its midpoint. For \(1\le t<\sec\delta\), the set of angles in this interval with ratio exceeding \(t\) has Lebesgue length \(2\arccos(t\cos\delta)\); it is empty for \(t\ge\sec\delta\). Adding these lengths across one half-cycle and dividing by \(\pi\) yields the stated survival function. Since \(h_P\ge r\), the survival function is \(1\) for \(t<1\). The law has no atoms: in every interval its ratio is a nonconstant cosine, and the finite set of extrema has zero angular measure.

**Inverse recovery including repeated gaps.** For \(1<t<\max_j\sec\delta_j\), away from the finitely many thresholds, the density is
\[
 f_T(t)=\frac2\pi\sum_{j:\,t<\sec\delta_j}
 \frac{\cos\delta_j}{\sqrt{1-t^2\cos^2\delta_j}}.
\]
For any distinct threshold \(\tau=\sec\delta\), let \(k\) be the number of gaps equal to \(2\delta\). Then
\[
 \lim_{t\uparrow\tau}\sqrt{\tau-t}\,f_T(t)
 =\frac{k\sqrt{2\cos\delta}}{\pi}>0.
\]
Terms from larger thresholds are bounded at \(\tau\), while terms from smaller thresholds vanish on a neighborhood below \(\tau\). Thus the thresholds are precisely the points with one-sided unbounded density, and the displayed nonzero coefficients recover their multiplicities. Because \(\delta\mapsto\sec\delta\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\pi/2)\), the complete multiset of gaps follows. The essential infimum of \(w_P\) is \(2r\), independently recovering the scaling parameter. Conversely, the formula for survival depends only on these recovered data, establishing the equivalence.

**Congruence and first ambiguity.** Since center and inradius are intrinsic, a Euclidean isometry between two centered tangential polygons must send the cyclic set of tangent normals to a rotated or reversed cyclic set. It therefore sends the ordered half-cycle gap word to a cyclic permutation or reversal, and every such operation comes from a planar rotation or reflection. Hence the congruence classes at a fixed multiset are exactly its orbits under the dihedral group of the \(m\)-cycle. For \(m=2,3\), the dihedral group acts as the entire permutation group, giving uniqueness. For pairwise distinct gaps and \(m\ge4\), there are \(m!\) words and each dihedral orbit has size \(2m\), giving \(m!/(2m)=(m-1)!/2\) congruence classes. For an explicit first ambiguity take \(m=4\), \(r=1\), and gap words
\[
 (\pi/10,\pi/5,3\pi/10,2\pi/5)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (\pi/10,\pi/5,2\pi/5,3\pi/10).
\]
Both sum to \(\pi\) and have the same multiset, but the words are not related by cyclic permutation or reversal. They define noncongruent centrally symmetric tangential octagons with exactly the same width law.

**Area and perimeter.** A side at a tangent normal has endpoint tangent-segment lengths \(r\tan(\Delta_{j-1}/2)\) and \(r\tan(\Delta_j/2)\). Counting all \(2m\) sides gives \(\operatorname{Perimeter}(P)=4r\sum_{j=1}^m\tan(\Delta_j/2)\). Triangulating from the incenter, each side contributes half the product of its length and \(r\) to area. The displayed area formula follows. These are consequences of the recovered multiset, not independent assumptions or stronger reconstruction claims.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained finite-check program is `verification/check_width_spectrum.py` (Python 3 with NumPy). From explicit tangent-line intersections, the program computes polygon vertices and checks support widths by maximizing vertex dot products in \(262{,}144\) evenly spaced directions. For ten test profiles (including the two inequivalent octagons, small-dimensional cases, repeated gaps, and random gap vectors), it compares the geometric widths to the analytic probability law and compares independent shoelace area and polygonal perimeter to their formulas. One direct run returned `VERIFY_OK: 300 independent area/perimeter/support/CDF checks`. The code tests finite samples, and the exact law and inverse conclusion follow from the preceding analytic argument rather than from numerical enumeration.

To reproduce, execute `python verification/check_width_spectrum.py` with Python 3 and NumPy installed. There are no external data files. The random-number generator uses a fixed seed.

## Relationship to prior work
Kim, Yaskin and Zvavitch [1] introduced geometric tomography via distributions of central-section and projection sizes. Their two-dimensional Proposition 2 gives origin-symmetric examples with equal projection-length distributions but differing area; their Theorem 4 is a volume comparison involving an ellipsoid. Neither assertion recovers the complete normal-gap multiset or classifies all equidistributed centrally symmetric tangential polygons. This result supplies an exact, structurally constrained inverse theorem for that observable, together with its sharp nonuniqueness threshold. Work on random normal measurements [2] observes normal vectors sampled from boundary data, not the unordered distribution of projection widths. Polygon chord-length distributions [3] describe random affine-invariant chord lengths, a different sampling experiment.

## Limitations
The inverse classification is restricted to **centrally symmetric convex polygons tangent to one Euclidean circle**, with strictly positive normal gaps. It makes no uniqueness claim for arbitrary planar convex bodies, general tangential polygons lacking central symmetry, or noisy/finite-sample width histograms. The singularity reconstruction is exact-data only; near-colliding gap thresholds may make numerical inversion ill-conditioned. Broader equivalences under different tomography terminology in earlier literature have not been exhaustively ruled out. No independent audit has been performed.
