# Review of Inverse-square convergence of sharp cylinder-function Turán minima

## Correctness

PASS. The source gives, at the unique minimizer,
\\[
b(1+\\varsigma)=1
\\]
and
\\[
a^2
=
\\frac{(1+\\varsigma)(\\mu^2-\\varsigma^2)}{\\varsigma}.
\\]
These imply the exact identity
\\[
a^2(1-b)
+
\\left(\\frac{1-b}{b}\\right)^2
=
\\mu^2.
\\]
Since the source proves \\(b\\to1\\), the auxiliary variable is \\(O(a^{-2})\\), and therefore
\\[
1-b
=
\\mu^2a^{-2}+O(a^{-6}).
\\]
Classical fixed-order cylinder zero asymptotics and the fact that the minimizer lies between consecutive zeros give
\\[
a=\\pi n+O(1).
\\]
The asserted expansion and limit follow without an unproved interpolation or finite experiment.

## Originality

PASS. The fresh primary source proves only monotone convergence of the successive sharp minima to \\(1\\); it does not state an inverse-square rate or a leading coefficient. Exact-source, equivalent-formulation, and Bessel-Turán searches did not locate the limit
\\[
n^2(1-b_{\\mu,n})\\to\\mu^2/\\pi^2
\\]
or its phase-independent cylinder extension. The closest located Bessel records concern different complete-monotonicity and Fourier zero-free problems.

The residual risk is that this short asymptotic consequence may be implicit in older special-function literature under different notation. The searched current and classical references did not expose such a statement.

## Value

PASS. The recent theorem identifies a sequence of best constants but gives only qualitative convergence. The exact inverse-square law quantifies the sharpness landscape on all distant nodal intervals and shows that the entire one-parameter cylinder family has a universal leading deficit independent of phase. This is a natural structural invariant of the source's sharp inequalities, rather than a finite numerical refinement.
