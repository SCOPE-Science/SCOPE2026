# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

An explicit bijection between section faces and sign-crossed faces of the parent simplex proves the entire finite-dimensional face-vector formula. Haar-uniform central normals are normalized Gaussian vectors after removing the coordinate mean. The random-threshold empirical-count expansion has a stated grid-and-Bernstein justification; its iid influence summands have variance \(1/4-1/(2\pi)\). A symmetric binomial expansion gives the fixed-rank quadratic fluctuation, while DKW and Gaussian tails establish uniform moments and permit exact covariance limits. The finite mean uses the bivariate Gaussian quadrant identity. The included `verify.py` tested 125 explicitly constructed section hulls and 195,000 Gaussian samples; these are diagnostics, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with historical uncertainty

Compared by mathematical implication, Dirksen's central-section volume theorems do not determine a random section's face-vector fluctuations. Kabluchko–Thäle's full projection paper establishes only monotonicity of expected projection faces; its Gaussian-polytope correspondence does not assert the present centroidal-section law. The inspected 2024 high-dimensional Gaussian-polytope work concerns expected facets. Earlier projection papers from 1992 and 1994 remain possible historical overlap; in particular, only published descriptions, not full texts, were accessible for those papers. Related sample-above-mean limit theory is not claimed as an independent contribution. No equivalent joint rank-one chi-square geometric-section theorem was identified, which is not by itself proof of absolute priority.

## Value — PASS

The full \(f\)-vector is a fundamental combinatorial measure of section complexity. Classical expectation formulas do not describe fluctuations or cross-rank dependence. The result identifies a single asymptotic degree of freedom governing *all* fixed ranks, an explicitly non-Gaussian \(\chi^2_1\) limit, and sharp covariance exponents and positive constants. It supplies exact finite-dimensional calibration and a distribution-free-in-dimension Gaussian concentration control for vertices within this model. This is a structural characterization of a natural high-dimensional random geometry object, not merely an arbitrary finite enumeration.

## Prior-work scope and residual limitations

The assessed object is a centered regular-simplex **section**, not a Gaussian-polytope **projection** or a translated section. No growing-rank asymptotic is established. Full-text access to some historical projection articles was unavailable; hidden equivalence by duality cannot be ruled out absolutely. Numerical agreement is supportive but cannot replace analytic verification.
