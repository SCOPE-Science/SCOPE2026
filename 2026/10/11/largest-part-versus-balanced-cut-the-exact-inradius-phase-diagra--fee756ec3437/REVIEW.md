# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the exact complete-multipartite facet classification and polarity. After subtracting the mean, every facet-defining function becomes a polar vertex. Type-(i) squared norms are \(q-(2t-q)^2/N\), maximized on a largest part with the signs as balanced as parity allows. Type-(ii) squared norms are \(t(N-t)/N\), maximized by balanced level sizes. Independent feasible labelings attain both upper bounds, so the polar circumradius is their maximum and the inradius is its reciprocal square root. The graph-spread formula is the same statement divided by \(N\).

## Value
PASS. The source treats complete multipartite graphs as a natural family with an explicit but two-mechanism facet structure. The finding shows exactly which mechanism controls the canonical Euclidean inradius and, equivalently, the classical graph spread. The formula has a genuine phase competition between concentration inside one largest independent part and a globally balanced binary cut, with parity visible on the first branch.

A residual risk remains for an equivalent formula under graph-concentration terminology. The theorem is restricted to complete multipartite graphs with at least three parts and to the canonical Euclidean root realization.
