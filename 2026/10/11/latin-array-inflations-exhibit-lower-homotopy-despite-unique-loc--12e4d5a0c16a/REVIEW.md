# Review: Latin-array inflations exhibit lower homotopy despite unique local lifting

## Correctness — PASS
The combinatorial orthogonal-array hypothesis guarantees every \((n-2)\)-face omitting the final color occurs in exactly one top facet. Consequently, the explicit free-face collapses remove all top cells. The remaining complex has the lower connectivity of the full join, since their skeleta agree in dimensions through \(n-2\). Euler characteristic, the Hurewicz theorem, Whitehead's theorem, and the connected-graph case give the precise wedge formula. The corresponding nonflabby restriction is concretely the proper inclusion of global compatible assignments into boundary assignments. Exact boundary-matrix computation checks eleven zero-sum examples independently of the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest full text is Ayzenberg--Khoroshavkina, arXiv:2602.08694v1, Theorem 5.1, which assumes restriction surjectivity between **all nested opens**, and concludes a top-degree wedge; the paper explicitly distinguishes open-set sections from face diagrams. That theorem does not apply to these examples. Ayzenberg--Rukhovich, arXiv:2012.07600v1, studies unrestricted flabby edge and vertex inflations, not Latin-array-constrained top facets. Comparisons by exact Latin-array, quasigroup, parity-complex, sheaf, and wedge-homotopy formulations did not identify a statement implying the all-array formula. This is not a claim that an unsuccessful search proves novelty; older or unindexed constructions may contain an equivalent result.

## Value — PASS
The result isolates the gap between local stalk surjectivity and true flabbiness precisely where the recent finite-étale inflation theorem needs its global hypothesis. Unique completion of every codimension-one assignment is the strongest natural local extension condition; it still admits a uniform, arbitrarily large rank of homology exactly one degree below the flabby prediction. The result covers every finite Latin hypercube, including nongroup-based ones, not an arbitrary sample of small complexes.

## Limits
The argument proves ordinary homotopy equivalence only. It does not address automorphism representations or arbitrary nonflabby sheaves. Originality remains subject to possible unindexed equivalents. The eleven computational checks are finite corroboration rather than proof of the unbounded parameter range.
