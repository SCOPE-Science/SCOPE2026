# Scientific review: linear facet-angle stability

## Correctness — PASS
For every full-dimensional spatial polytope the even target surface-area measure is finitely supported at its unoriented facet normals. The proof explicitly inverts the spherical cosine transform on the finite even harmonic expansion of a nonnegative polynomial vanishing precisely on those normals. The inverse is perturbed around a strictly positive constant density; the explicit Legendre eigenvalues and addition-formula sup norm bound certify its nonnegativity. Integrating projected-area domination against this density gives the exact inequality \(\varepsilon_m\int g\,d\mu\le2\delta\), and projective separation turns \(g\) into a quadratic angular lower bound. A facet-splitting construction proves the exponent cannot be lowered within weak barriers. Critical eigenvalues were independently checked with exact rational polynomials; finite geometry tests complement rather than replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest general published result is Kiderlen's Corollary 1.4 (arXiv:2605.13449), whose polytope deficit exponent is less than one; it does not imply a linear estimate for arbitrarily small deficit. The published cube-specific linear result covers one special family but not arbitrary, nonsymmetric spatial polytopes. Steinerberger's 2025 result concerns planar opaque sets and the square. The complete general-polytope finite dual certificate, its explicit constant, and its sharp quadratic angular moment were not located in the searched source material. Unindexed equivalents remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result resolves the natural question whether the general weak-barrier angular concentration for every fixed spatial polytope has a linear Jones-deficit modulus. It gives a geometric constant from the number and separation of facet normal lines, a stronger integrated angular moment, and the sharp small-angle exponent. It requires no symmetry and directly strengthens the known small-deficit power.

## Closest literature and scope
Kiderlen, arXiv:2605.13449, pp. 2, 6--7 and 15--16, supplies the projection framework and a strictly weaker power for general polytopes. The published unit-cube record supplies linear stability only for coordinate facet directions, while Steinerberger, arXiv:2501.01004, supplies the planar analogue. The coefficient is not sharp and no new shortest opaque-barrier area bound is claimed.
