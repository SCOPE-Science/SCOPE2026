# Review: Link-blind integral Betti polynomials of ordered point configurations

## Correctness
PASS. Integral Alexander duality for \(L\sqcup Q\sqcup\{\infty\}\subset S^3\) gives cohomology ranks \(1,n,n+s\) in degrees \(0,1,2\). The deleted-point long exact sequence makes the old degree-two classes a primitive summand and identifies the new puncture spheres. The \(k-1\) global Gauss classes evaluate as the identity matrix on those puncture spheres; together with pulled-back classes of the ambient link complement they satisfy integral Leray–Hirsch. This proves the product formula and torsion-freeness for every smooth link and positive integer \(k\). The codimension-three diagonal argument identifies the full fundamental group with a direct power of the link group, making the unknot–trefoil example rigorous. There is no appeal to extrapolation from finite data.

## Originality
PASS with a stated residual risk of an unindexed equivalent formulation in the classical configuration-space literature. The closest contemporary work, Makri's arXiv:2610.04382v1, defines exactly the ordered marked-point fiber for trivial links and calculates fundamental groups, but not its integral cohomology. Bödigheimer–Cohen–Taylor (1989) explicitly study **unordered** configurations and include a mod-two knot-complement example, not the integral ordered all-link all-\(k\) formula. Knudsen's general-manifold calculation is likewise rational and unordered. Database searches under cohomology, Betti polynomial, link complement, knot type, Fadell–Neuwirth, and Leray–Hirsch formulations found no stronger record covering the claim. The proof's combination of Alexander duality, global angular classes, and integral Leray–Hirsch is not asserted to be a new technique.

## Value
PASS. The fiber introduced by the current mixed-configuration construction is a natural object for further topology. The theorem computes every integral Betti number uniformly for all particle counts and establishes the exact boundary of that additive invariant: it cannot detect knotting or linking, even when the configuration spaces are separated by their fundamental groups. This is a structural, motivated distinction between two tiers of invariants, not an arbitrary finite-parameter computation.

## Closest sources and scope limitations
Makri, arXiv:2610.04382v1, Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 3.8; Bödigheimer–Cohen–Taylor, *Topology* 28 (1989), Theorems A–C and Example 5.2; Knudsen, arXiv:1405.6696. The result is about integral additive cohomology of **ordered** configurations in complements of smooth links in Euclidean three-space. It neither claims cup-product-ring rigidity nor determines the total mixed-configuration-space cohomology. Further obscure or unindexed literature may contain an equivalent explicit formula.
