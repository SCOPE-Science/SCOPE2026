# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Parametrize the invariant graph foliation by \(\phi_t(0)=t\) as in the primary source and let \(P\) be projection along leaves. Equivariance gives \(P\circ f=\psi\circ P\), so area preservation makes \(\nu=P_*\operatorname{Leb}\) invariant under \(\psi\). For a short oriented leaf-parameter arc, the source's transverse Lipschitz inequality compares the gap of its boundary graphs at every horizontal coordinate to the parameter gap. Integrating the gap gives the area of the corresponding strip and proves \(K_{\mathrm{Fol}}^{-1}|I|\le\nu(I)\le K_{\mathrm{Fol}}|I|\). The cumulative distribution coordinate is therefore bi-Lipschitz. Invariance makes it conjugate \(\psi\) to a rigid rotation, and the rotation number is \(\alpha\). Theorem C then yields minimality.

No finite computation is used. Wrap-around arcs are treated on periodic lifts, and graph boundaries have zero area.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source explicitly retains bi-Lipschitz regularity of the conjugacy as a separate hypothesis and states that this is the main extra point of Theorem C. Its Lemma 3.17 proves that \(\psi\) is bi-Lipschitz, not that its conjugacy to \(R_\alpha\) is bi-Lipschitz. Statement-level searches for the source title, removal of the conjugacy hypothesis, transverse invariant measure, and circle-conjugacy aliases found no equivalent result. No equivalent published finding was located under the source identifier or the tested aliases.

The closest broader literature inspected was Arnaud--Zavidovique, arXiv:2011.01546. It treats an annulus integrability regime in which every leaf is individually invariant and therefore does not imply the present conclusion for a torus Dehn-twist map that permutes leaves through an irrational leaf-space action.

## Value
PASS. The result removes one of the two regularity hypotheses from a new minimality theorem and shows that the source's separate conjugacy assumption is forced by area and transverse foliation geometry. It leaves a mathematically meaningful single remaining regularity issue rather than merely weakening a constant or rephrasing a known condition.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest direct source is arXiv:2609.27102v1; the closest broader integrability source is arXiv:2011.01546. The theorem still assumes that the invariant foliation is Lipschitz in the transverse sense and does not prove that this property follows from Theorem A. It also uses area preservation essentially. A later revision of the very recent focal preprint or an unindexed measured-foliation observation remains a residual originality risk.
