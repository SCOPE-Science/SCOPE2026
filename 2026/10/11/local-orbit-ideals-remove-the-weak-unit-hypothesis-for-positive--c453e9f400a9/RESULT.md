# Local orbit ideals remove the weak-unit hypothesis for positive mean ergodic operators
## Finding
Let \(E\) be an order-continuous Banach lattice and let \(T:E\to E\) be positive, power-bounded, and mean ergodic. Write \(P\) for the mean ergodic projection and, for every integer \(n\ge 1\), define
\[
A_n=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^k.
\]
Then for every \(x\in E\),
\[
A_nx\xrightarrow{uo}Px
\]
in \(E\). In particular, the global weak-unit or quasi-interior-point assumption in the recent representation-free individual ergodic theorem is unnecessary for this conclusion.

## Assumptions and scope
The lattice \(E\) is real or complex and has order-continuous norm. The operator \(T\) is positive, power-bounded, and mean ergodic, meaning that \(A_ny\) converges in norm for every \(y\in E\). The symbol \(P\) denotes the resulting bounded projection. No separability, global weak unit, or global quasi-interior point is assumed.

The result concerns uo-convergence of Cesàro means. It does not assert order convergence inside \(E\), nor does it remove positivity, power boundedness, mean ergodicity, or order continuity of the norm.

## Proof
Fix \(x\in E\) and put
\[
g_k=T^k|x|\qquad(k\ge 0).
\]
Let \(F_x\) be the norm-closed ideal of \(E\) generated by \(\{g_k:k\ge0\}\). Define
\[
e_x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k-1}\frac{g_k}{1+\|g_k\|}.
\]
The series converges in norm, since the norm of its \(k\)-th term is at most \(2^{-k-1}\). Hence \(e_x\in(F_x)_+\). Moreover,
\[
g_k\le 2^{k+1}(1+\|g_k\|)e_x
\]
for every \(k\ge0\). Thus every generator \(g_k\) belongs to the principal ideal generated by \(e_x\). Because \(F_x\) is the closed ideal generated by the \(g_k\), the principal ideal generated by \(e_x\) is norm dense in \(F_x\). Therefore \(e_x\) is a quasi-interior point, equivalently a weak unit, of \(F_x\).

The ideal \(F_x\) is \(T\)-invariant. Indeed, if \(|y|\le \sum_{j=1}^m c_jg_{k_j}\) with \(c_j\ge0\), positivity gives
\[
|Ty|\le T|y|\le\sum_{j=1}^m c_jg_{k_j+1},
\]
so \(Ty\) remains in the algebraic ideal generated by the orbit. Continuity of \(T\) then yields \(T(F_x)\subseteq F_x\).

The restricted norm on \(F_x\) is order continuous: an ideal is a regular sublattice, so a decreasing net in \(F_x\) with infimum zero there also decreases to zero in \(E\), where order continuity of the norm applies. The restriction \(T|_{F_x}\) is positive and power-bounded. It is also mean ergodic. For \(y\in F_x\), every \(A_ny\) lies in \(F_x\), while \(A_ny\to Py\) in the norm of \(E\); since \(F_x\) is closed, \(Py\in F_x\), and the same norm convergence holds in \(F_x\). Thus the mean ergodic projection of the restriction is \(P|_{F_x}\).

Dobrick's individual ergodic theorem therefore applies to \(T|_{F_x}\), because \(F_x\) is an order-continuous Banach lattice with a weak unit. It gives
\[
A_nx\xrightarrow{uo}Px
\]
inside \(F_x\). Finally, ideals are regular sublattices, and uo-convergence is unchanged when passing between a regular sublattice and the ambient vector lattice. Hence the same uo-convergence holds in \(E\).

Since \(x\) was arbitrary, the assertion holds for every \(x\in E\).

## Verification
The proof is infinite and symbolic; no finite experiment is used to infer the conclusion. The critical steps are: construction of an explicit quasi-interior point for the orbit ideal; invariance of that ideal under positivity; inheritance of order continuity and mean ergodicity; application of the weak-unit individual ergodic theorem; and regular-sublattice transfer of uo-convergence.

For the transfer step, Gao, Troitsky, and Xanthos prove that uo-convergence is stable under passing to and from regular sublattices; they also note that every ideal is regular. The construction of \(e_x\) independently verifies the only missing local hypothesis needed for the weak-unit theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Dobrick's recent representation-free theorem proves uo-convergence of Cesàro means for positive, power-bounded, mean ergodic operators on order-continuous Banach lattices under a weak-unit hypothesis. The argument above localizes each orbit to a canonical closed invariant ideal carrying its own quasi-interior point, thereby removing the need for a weak unit in the ambient lattice.

Gao, Troitsky, and Xanthos provide the regular-sublattice invariance of uo-convergence used in the final passage from \(F_x\) back to \(E\). Their result is a transfer principle rather than the positive mean-ergodic theorem proved here.

## Limitations
The argument depends on positivity to make the orbit-generated ideal invariant. It also retains the source theorem's power-boundedness, mean-ergodicity, and order-continuity assumptions. It does not claim that the Cesàro means order-converge in \(E\), and it does not address nonpositive operators. An older equivalent formulation under different positive-operator terminology remains a residual literature risk, although searches under weak-unit removal, orbit-ideal localization, regular-sublattice transfer, and individual ergodic terminology found no covering statement.
