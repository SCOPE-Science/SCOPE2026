Loday's permutahedron, associahedron, and parallelepiped all have intrinsic Euclidean inradius \(\sqrt{n(n-1)}/2\), centered at their already-known common vertex barycenter. The permutahedron and associahedron have that incenter uniquely, while the parallelepiped acquires an \((n-3)\)-dimensional incenter family for \(n\ge4\).
