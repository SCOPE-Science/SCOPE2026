# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For every centrally symmetric regular Platonic solid, the sphere decomposes into congruent vertex normal cells and the support function on one cell is \(R\cos\theta\). The side distance satisfies \(\sin\delta=1/(2R)\) from the unit-edge chord relation. A sector decomposition of the regular spherical \(q\)-gon reduces the second support moment to an elementary antiderivative, yielding formula (9) in the result. Exact substitutions reproduce the cube, octahedron, icosahedron, and dodecahedron entries. The tetrahedron entry is the materially inspected published Finch evaluation. The bundled checker independently verifies coordinates, algebraic identities, one-dimensional sector quadrature, and direct spherical quadrature.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual classical-stereology risk.** Finch's full text evaluates only the tetrahedron and cube and explicitly invites analogous computations for other bodies. Kabluchko--Litvak--Zaporozhets provide a general higher-moment representation and explicit cube, simplex, and crosspolytope applications, but their full text has no dodecahedron or icosahedron occurrence. Exact-object, second-moment, projection-length, caliper/Feret, and exact-constant searches did not locate a complete Platonic table or the dodecahedral formula. Previously known or previously obtained individual entries are treated as components of the complete classification rather than re-claimed separately.

## Value
**PASS.** The five Platonic solids are the canonical complete family of regular convex polyhedra. Completing one natural random-projection invariant on the whole family is a natural classification, not an arbitrary sister-object computation. The proof also isolates one common normal-fan formula for all four centrally symmetric cases, making the dodecahedral completion structurally reusable.
