# Minimal two-shellable decompositions of Ziegler's ten-vertex ball
## Finding
Let \(B\) be the simplicial \(3\)-ball with the twenty-one tetrahedral facets listed by Ziegler:
\[
1234,1256,1569,2560,3678,4578,2367,1629,2670,3248,4137,
3478,1249,2730,3268,4157,4185,1489,2310,1859,2150.
\]
Ziegler proves that \(B\) is strongly non-shellable. For this fixed facet set, call an unordered partition
\[
\mathcal F(B)=A\sqcup C
\]
a **two-shellable decomposition** if the pure \(3\)-complexes generated by \(A\) and \(C\) are shellable and their intersection is a pure shellable \(2\)-complex.

There are exactly \(247\) two-shellable decompositions. Their complete block-size distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{unordered block sizes}&5+16&6+15&7+14&8+13&9+12&10+11\\
\hline
\text{number}&3&10&23&40&74&97
\end{array}.
\]
Thus the smaller block has at least five tetrahedra. Exactly three decompositions attain this minimum. Their five-tetrahedron blocks and their intersections are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{five-tetrahedron block}&\text{triangular facets of the intersection}\\
\hline
\{3678,2367,2670,2730,3268\}&\{023,026,238,378\}\\
\{4578,4157,4185,1489,1859\}&\{147,149,159,478\}\\
\{1256,1569,2560,1629,2150\}&\{012,026,129,159\}
\end{array}.
\]
Each displayed intersection is a shellable \(2\)-complex with four triangular facets.

## Assumptions and scope
Only the fixed abstract simplicial complex \(B\) above is considered. A shelling is the standard pure simplicial shelling: after the first facet, the intersection of each new facet with the preceding union is a nonempty pure codimension-one subcomplex of that facet's boundary. The phrase "two-shellable decomposition" is defined above; it is deliberately narrower than an arbitrary recursive constructibility decomposition. The result therefore does not claim to enumerate every possible constructibility tree for \(B\).

## Proof
For a nonempty set \(S\) of tetrahedral facets, let \(K(S)\) be the pure subcomplex generated by those facets. A finite pure simplicial complex is shellable if and only if either it has one facet, or it has a facet \(F\) such that the remaining facet-generated subcomplex is shellable and
\[
F\cap K(S\setminus\{F\})
\]
is nonempty and pure of codimension one. For a tetrahedron, any pure subcomplex generated by triangular boundary facets is itself shellable, so this gives an exact recursive test for \(K(S)\).

The verifier evaluates this recurrence for every one of the \(2^21-1\) nonempty tetrahedron subsets. It independently confirms that the complete twenty-one-facet complex is not shellable. It then checks every one of the \(2^20-1\) unordered complementary nontrivial facet partitions. A partition is retained only when both sides pass the exact shellability recurrence, the maximal faces of their intersection are all triangles, and that triangle complex passes the analogous exact shellability recurrence in dimension two.

Exactly \(247\) partitions survive. Direct counting by the smaller block size gives the six numbers in the displayed table. The smallest surviving block size is five; extracting all size-five survivors gives exactly the three blocks displayed above. Recomputing each intersection from the two generated subcomplexes gives exactly the four triangles displayed in the right column.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs all faces from the twenty-one source tetrahedra and first checks the face vector \( (10,38,50,21)\). It computes shellability of every nonempty tetrahedron-generated subcomplex by dynamic programming over facet subsets, enumerates all unordered complementary partitions, reconstructs each intersection, and recursively verifies shellability of each retained two-dimensional intersection.

Successful replay prints:

`VERIFY_OK f=10,38,50,21 shellable_full=no two_shellable_splits=247 size_distribution=5+16:3,6+15:10,7+14:23,8+13:40,9+12:74,10+11:97 minimum_block=5 minimal_splits=3 interface_triangles=4_each`

All arithmetic and set tests are exact. No random sampling or timeout conclusion is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Ziegler gives the exact twenty-one facets and proves that the resulting ten-vertex ball is strongly non-shellable. Hachimori's simplicial-complex library records the same ball as constructible but non-shellable. Lutz places Ziegler's ball in the hierarchy of vertex-decomposability, shellability and constructibility, and later work of Dao, Doolittle and Lyle identifies strong non-shellability of balls with their minimal Cohen--Macaulay property.

Those results establish the object and the qualitative gap between shellability and weaker assembly notions. They do not state how many decompositions into two shellable three-dimensional pieces exist, the sharp minimum size of a shellable child block in such a decomposition, or the three minimal witnesses above. The present statement is restricted to the explicitly defined two-shellable decompositions, so it neither conflicts with nor replaces broader recursive constructibility results.

## Limitations
The census concerns one labeled combinatorial ball, although the definition and counts are invariant under relabeling. It does not enumerate arbitrary constructibility trees whose children might themselves be constructible but non-shellable. The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed private computation or an unpublished implementation of Hachimori's constructibility test. No claim is made about geometric realizations beyond the established combinatorial model.
