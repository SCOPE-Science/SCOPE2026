# Minimum-support mod-two homology spectrum of the sixteen-vertex K3 triangulation
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the sixteen-vertex triangulation of the K3 surface in the Spreer--Kühnel labeling, obtained from the two facet seeds \((1,2,3,8,12)\) and \((1,2,5,8,14)\) under the order-\(240\) group described in the cited source. An **empty tetrahedron** is a four-vertex set \(T\) for which every triangle in \(\binom{T}{3}\) is a face of \(K\), but \(T\) itself is not a tetrahedron of \(K\). Because \(K\) is three-neighborly, this is equivalent to a four-set that is not a tetrahedron.

Write \(z_T\) for the mod-two sum of the four triangles of the tetrahedral boundary. Among the \(\binom{16}{4}=1820\) four-sets, exactly \(720\) are tetrahedra and \(1100\) are empty tetrahedra. Every empty-tetrahedron cycle \(z_T\) is nonzero in \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\), and the \(1100\) such cycles represent exactly \(508\) distinct homology classes. The number of empty tetrahedra representing a fixed one of these classes has distribution
\[
1^320,\qquad 3^80,\qquad 5^108.
\]
Equivalently, \(320\) classes have one four-vertex support, \(80\) have three, and \(108\) have five.

These \(508\) classes are precisely the nonzero elements of \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\) whose minimum vertex support has size four. No nonzero class has support on at most three vertices. The \(508\) classes span the full \(22\)-dimensional group \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\).

Under the full order-\(240\) automorphism group, the \(1100\) empty tetrahedra have seven orbits of sizes
\[
20,60,60,240,240,240,240,
\]
with lexicographically least representatives
\[
(1,2,3,4),\ (1,2,5,6),\ (1,2,9,10),\ (1,2,3,5),\ (1,2,3,6),\ (1,2,3,7),\ (1,2,5,11).
\]
The induced homology-class sets of these seven support orbits have sizes \(20,60,60,240,80,60,48\). The only overlap between two such class sets is the common \(60\)-class orbit arising from the support orbits represented by \((1,2,5,6)\) and \((1,2,3,7)\). Consequently the minimum-support classes themselves form exactly six automorphism orbits of sizes
\[
20,48,60,60,80,240.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns this specific sixteen-vertex K3 triangulation and mod-two simplicial homology. Vertex support of a homology class means the smallest cardinality of a vertex set \(S\) for which the class has a cycle representative contained in the induced subcomplex \(K[S]\). The orbit statement uses the automorphism group generated by the two permutations in the cited Spreer--Kühnel model.

No claim is made about integral minimum-support classes, about other triangulations of the K3 surface, or about a universal classification of tight triangulations.

## Proof
The exact facet construction gives face vector \((16,120,560,720,288)\). Over \(\mathbf F_2\), direct row reduction of the triangle-to-edge boundary matrix gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=105\), and direct row reduction of the tetrahedron-to-triangle boundary matrix gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3=433\). Hence
\[
\dim H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)=(560-105)-433=22.
\]

For each of the \(1100\) four-sets that is not a tetrahedron, the sum of its four triangular faces is a two-cycle. Reducing all \(1100\) cycles modulo \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) gives no zero remainder and exactly \(508\) distinct quotient representatives. Counting equal quotient representatives gives the multiplicity distribution \(1^320,3^80,5^108\).

To identify these as exactly the minimum-support classes, observe first that a complex on at most three vertices has no nonzero two-cycle. On exactly four vertices, the full two-skeleton has a one-dimensional two-cycle space, generated by the tetrahedral boundary. If the four-set is a tetrahedron of \(K\), that cycle belongs to \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) and is zero in global homology. If the four-set is empty, the exhaustive quotient computation above proves that its boundary is nonzero. Therefore the \(508\) classes just counted are exactly the nonzero classes with vertex support four.

Applying the full automorphism group to every empty tetrahedron gives the seven support orbits stated above. Passing each support orbit through the quotient map to homology gives class-set sizes \(20,60,60,240,80,60,48\); pairwise intersection testing shows exactly one overlap, of size \(60\), between the two specified support orbits. This yields six class orbits of sizes \(20,48,60,60,80,240\). Finally, row reduction of the \(508\) quotient representatives gives rank \(22\), so these minimum-support classes span all of \(H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs the order-\(240\) group and all \(288\) four-simplices from the two published seeds, recomputes the entire face vector, constructs both mod-two boundary matrices, enumerates every four-vertex set, computes the quotient classes modulo boundaries, checks the complete multiplicity and orbit decompositions, and verifies that the resulting classes span a \(22\)-dimensional space.

A successful replay prints:

`VERIFY_OK group=240 f=16,120,560,720,288 H2dim=22 empty_tetrahedra=1100 min_support_classes=508 multiplicity=1:320,3:80,5:108 tetra_orbits=20,60,60,240,240,240,240 class_orbits=20,48,60,60,80,240 span=22`

## Relationship to prior work
Casella and Kühnel constructed the sixteen-vertex K3 triangulation, proved the sixteen-vertex minimum, its complete two-skeleton, tightness, and its order-\(240\) symmetry. Spreer and Kühnel later restated the exact labeled model and explicitly used empty tetrahedra when studying slicings. Tightness explains why an induced spherical cycle can inject nontrivially into global homology, but it does not determine when distinct empty tetrahedra represent the same global class, how many distinct minimum-support classes occur, or their automorphism-orbit structure.

Codenotti, Santos, and Spreer study Betti numbers of induced subcomplexes and their relation to tightness and Stanley--Reisner invariants, providing a broader framework for induced-subcomplex homology. The exact \(508\)-class quotient spectrum, its \(1/3/5\) support multiplicities, and its six class orbits were not found in the inspected exact-object or broader sources.

## Limitations
The computation is exact for the published sixteen-vertex complex but does not classify other K3 triangulations. The original 2001 article was available during this review only through bibliographic/abstract-level material, while the later exact-object paper was inspected in full. An unindexed older computation could therefore contain an equivalent homology-class collision table. Search absence is not a proof of novelty.
