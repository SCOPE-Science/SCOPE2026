# Minkowski asymmetry and canonical-center polytopes of finite-poset order polytopes

## Finding

Let \(P\) be a finite nonempty partially ordered set of \(n\) elements, \(h\) the maximum number of elements in a chain, and
\[
\mathcal O(P)=\{x\in[0,1]^P:x_i\le x_j\text{ whenever }i<_Pj\}.
\]
For a full-dimensional convex body \(K\), its Minkowski asymmetry and set of minimizing centers are
\[
s(K)=\min_{c\in\mathbb R^n}\inf\{s\ge1:-(K-c)\subseteq s(K-c)\},\qquad
\mathcal M(K)=\{c:-(K-c)\subseteq s(K)(K-c)\}.
\]
Then \(s(\mathcal O(P))=h\), and **all** minimizing centers form the following explicitly defined polytope:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M(\mathcal O(P))=\left\{c:\ \frac1{h+1}\le c_i\le\frac h{h+1}\ (i\in P),\quad c_j-c_i\ge\frac1{h+1}\ (i\lessdot_P j)\right\}.}
\]
Let \(H=h+1\) and let \((H\mathcal O(P))^{(1)}=\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{int}(H\mathcal O(P))\cap\mathbb Z^P)\), the first interior-lattice-point hull at the Ehrhart codegree. The same center polytope has an exact arithmetic description:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M(\mathcal O(P))=\frac1H(H\mathcal O(P))^{(1)}.}
\]
Let \(C(P)\) be the union of the elements of all chains of cardinality \(h\). Then
\[
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M(\mathcal O(P))=n-|C(P)|.}
\]
In particular, the minimizing center is unique if and only if **every element is on some longest chain**. These conclusions include disconnected posets and the antichain case \(h=1\).

## Assumptions and scope

Throughout, \(P\) is finite and nonempty, with its strict partial order \(<_P\). A cover \(i\lessdot_Pj\) means \(i<_Pj\) and no intermediate poset element. The usual coordinates of \(\mathbb R^P\) are used; no normalization of Euclidean length is required, since Minkowski asymmetry is affine invariant. All interiors are ordinary \(n\)-dimensional interiors. The order polytope is full-dimensional.

For each \(i\in P\), define \(\ell(i)\) as the greatest cardinality of a chain ending at \(i\), and \(r(i)\) as the greatest cardinality of a chain beginning at \(i\). Consequently \(i\in C(P)\) exactly when \(\ell(i)+r(i)-1=h\). This provides a linear-time dynamic-programming test in the directed cover graph after a topological ordering.

## Proof

**1. Exact asymmetry feasibility.** All vertices of \(\mathcal O(P)\) are indicator vectors of upper sets, a standard consequence of the order-polytope description. For a proposed factor \(s\ge1\) and center \(c\), the defining inclusion is equivalent to
\[
\frac{(s+1)c-v}{s}\in\mathcal O(P)\qquad\text{for every vertex }v\text{ of }\mathcal O(P).
\]
For every \(i\), upper-set vertices with \(v_i=0\) and with \(v_i=1\) both exist; hence the coordinate inequalities give
\[
1\le(s+1)c_i\le s.
\]
For every cover \(i\lessdot_Pj\), \(\max_v(v_j-v_i)=1\), achieved by the upper set generated by \(j\), which excludes \(i\). Therefore the order inequality for the reflected vertices gives \((s+1)(c_j-c_i)\ge1\). Conversely these inequalities guarantee that every reflected vertex satisfies all defining halfspaces, proving the **if and only if** statement
\[
-(\mathcal O(P)-c)\subseteq s(\mathcal O(P)-c)
\iff
\begin{cases}
1\le (s+1)c_i\le s&\text{for every }i,\\
(s+1)(c_j-c_i)\ge1&\text{for every cover }i\lessdot_Pj.
\end{cases}
\]
Using covers, rather than every comparable pair, is sufficient by transitivity.

**2. Sharp optimum.** Any chain of \(h\) elements yields \(h-1\) cover steps after passing to a maximal chain containing it, together with a lower bound at its first vertex and an upper bound at its last. The feasibility inequalities imply \(s\ge h\): along a chain of \(h\) elements, \((s+1)(c_{\rm last}-c_{\rm first})\ge h-1\), but \((s+1)(c_{\rm last}-c_{\rm first})\le s-1\). Conversely set \(c_i=\ell(i)/(h+1)\). Then \(1\le\ell(i)\le h\), and any cover increases \(\ell\) by at least one. Thus \(s=h\) is feasible, proving \(s(\mathcal O(P))=h\) and the displayed exact center inequalities.

**3. Canonical lattice-interior hull.** Put \(u_i=Hc_i\). The center region becomes the difference-constraint polytope
\[
Q_P=\{u\in\mathbb R^P:1\le u_i\le h,\ u_j-u_i\ge1\text{ for every cover }i\lessdot_Pj\}.
\]
An integer vector is in the interior of \(H\mathcal O(P)\) exactly when \(1\le u_i\le h\) for all \(i\), and every cover difference is at least one. Indeed, the strict facet constraints \(0<u_i\) at minimal elements, \(u_i<H\) at maximal elements, and \(u_i<u_j\) at covers imply all the displayed inequalities for integers, and conversely. The coefficient matrix of \(Q_P\) comprises rows \(e_i\), \(-e_i\), and \(e_i-e_j\), so it is a directed-incidence matrix with unit-coordinate bounds and is totally unimodular. Since all right-hand sides are integers, every vertex of nonempty bounded \(Q_P\) is integral. Thus
\[
Q_P=\operatorname{conv}(Q_P\cap\mathbb Z^P)=(H\mathcal O(P))^{(1)},
\]
and the second displayed identity follows. The codegree is \(H\): any strictly increasing integer chain of \(h\) points inside a dilation \(t\mathcal O(P)\) needs \(t\ge h+1\), and \(u_i=\ell(i)\) shows that \(t=H\) suffices.

**4. Complete dimension of the center locus.** Augment the cover digraph by a bottom vertex \(\bot\), a top vertex \(\top\), edges from \(\bot\) to each minimal element and from each maximal element to \(\top\). Give every directed edge length one. Longest \(\bot\)-to-\(\top\) paths have length \(H=h+1\), and an element is on one of them precisely when it is in \(C(P)\). Every feasible \(u\in Q_P\) satisfies \(u_\bot=0\), \(u_\top=H\), and \(u_j-u_i\ge1\) on all augmented edges. Along a length-\(H\) path all these inequalities are equalities, so every \(i\in C(P)\) has the fixed coordinate \(u_i=\ell(i)\). This proves the upper dimension bound \(n-|C(P)|\).

For the reverse bound, fix an augmented edge \(e\) not belonging to any length-\(H\) path. Increase only its length from one to \(1+\varepsilon\), with \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Every \(\bot\)-to-\(\top\) path still has total weighted length at most \(H\), since a path using \(e\) originally had length at most \(H-1\). For each poset vertex \(i\), take \(u_i\) to be the maximum weighted length of a \(\bot\)-to-\(i\) path, and put \(u_\bot=0\), \(u_\top=H\). The augmented edge inequalities all hold, with strict slack on \(e\): for an edge ending at \(\top\), this follows because every path from \(\bot\) through that edge has length at most \(H\). Averaging these feasible vectors over all edges not on any length-\(H\) path produces one feasible point that has strict slack on every such edge. All inequalities incident to a noncritical coordinate are among these strict inequalities, since an edge on a maximum path has both endpoints critical if they belong to \(P\). Therefore every noncritical coordinate can be independently perturbed a sufficiently small amount, while the critical coordinates remain fixed. Hence the affine dimension is at least \(n-|C(P)|\), completing the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library Python program `verify.py` enumerates every forward-edge DAG presentation on at most five labeled vertices and 120 seeded presentations on six vertices. For each, it constructs the transitive closure and covers, computes \(h\), \(C(P)\), and all integer points of \(Q_P\), and checks by exact rational Gaussian elimination that their affine span has dimension \(n-|C(P)|\). It also checks reflected upper-set vertices against every coordinate and cover inequality. The run printed:

`PASS 1219 poset presentations, 3072 integer center points, exact affine ranks and vertex tests`

Different presentations may induce the same poset. This finite enumeration is not claimed to cover all posets or substitute for the arguments above. Reproduce using `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the embedded standalone file.

For perspective, a chain of length \(h\) together with \(k\) isolated elements, when \(h\ge2\), has a \(k\)-dimensional cube of minimizing centers. Conversely, the six-element poset comprising two three-element chains and one extra cover from the first element of one chain to the last element of the other has a unique center, even though it has a maximal chain of length two; thus unique center need not force purity of the poset.

## Relationship to prior work

Stanley introduced order and chain polytopes and their piecewise-linear transfer map [1]. Chappell, Friedl, and Sanyal [2] study double poset polytopes and explain that chain polytopes are stable-set polytopes of comparability graphs. Haase, Kohl, and Tsuchiya [3] study levelness and the interior lattice-point hull of order polytopes at codegree, including strict poset-labeling constraints. Von Bell et al. [4] study triangulations, volumes, circuits, and flips of order polytopes, including the generalized snake family. These papers provide relevant underlying constructions; the claim here is **not** that strict order maps, codegree, or the chain-polytope transfer are new. The asserted addition is the exact identification of all Minkowski-asymmetry minimizers with the first codegree interior hull and the affine dimension/uniqueness criterion in terms of maximum chains. No inspected primary result explicitly gives this combined Minkowski-center identification or dimension classification.

The chain polytope is generally only piecewise-linearly, not affinely, identified with its order polytope. Consequently, even a formula for a comparability-graph stable-set polytope would not automatically transfer to this order-polytope problem. This distinction prevents treating a nonlinear change of coordinates as an affine equivalence.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns Minkowski asymmetry and its minimizing centers, not Euclidean inballs, minimum enclosing ellipsoids, poset levelness, or the face numbers of the center polytope. The source comparison cannot exclude an older equivalent result phrased in terms of strict order maps, Hibi rings, canonical interior hulls, or generalized difference-constraint systems. The proof is self-contained apart from standard vertex and total-unimodularity facts, which are stated and used explicitly; the finite verifier is only supplementary.
