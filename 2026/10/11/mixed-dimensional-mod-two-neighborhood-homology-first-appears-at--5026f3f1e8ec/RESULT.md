# Mixed-dimensional mod-two neighborhood homology first appears at \(T_7\)
## Finding
Let \(T_n=L(K_n)\) be the triangular graph for \(n\ge3\), and let \(\mathcal N(T_n)\) be its Lovász neighborhood complex. For \(3\le n\le6\), the reduced mod-two homology of \(\mathcal N(T_n)\) is nonzero in exactly one degree: \(\widetilde H_1(\mathcal N(T_3);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2\), \(\widetilde H_1(\mathcal N(T_4);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2\), \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal N(T_5);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^{11}\), and \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal N(T_6);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^{91}\). At \(n=7\), mixed-dimensional homology appears for the first time: \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal N(T_7);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^{246}\) and \(\widetilde H_4(\mathcal N(T_7);\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^{7}\), with all other reduced mod-two homology groups zero.

Thus \(T_7\) is the first triangular graph whose Lovász neighborhood complex has reduced \(\mathbf F_2\)-homology in two different dimensions.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(n\ge3\), the triangular graph \(T_n=L(K_n)\) has one vertex for each two-element subset of \([n]\); two vertices are adjacent exactly when the corresponding edges of \(K_n\) meet. The Lovász neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(G)\) consists of the finite vertex sets that have a common neighbor in \(G\).

The claim is an exact finite computation for \(3\le n\le7\) with coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). It makes no assertion about integral torsion, the homotopy type, or the behavior for \(n\ge8\).

## Proof
Fix \(n\in\{3,4,5,6,7\}\). A vertex of \(T_n\) is an edge \(e\) of \(K_n\). Its open neighborhood consists of all edges of \(K_n\) that share exactly one endpoint with \(e\), so it has \(2(n-2)\) vertices. Therefore \(\mathcal N(T_n)\) is obtained exactly by taking every nonempty subset of every such open neighborhood and identifying repeated simplices.

The bundled verifier constructs those simplices directly. For each dimension \(k\), it forms the simplicial boundary map over \(\mathbf F_2\) by sending a \(k\)-simplex to the sum of its \(k+1\) codimension-one faces. Exact binary Gaussian elimination gives the boundary ranks. The mod-two Betti number is then
\[
\beta_k=\dim C_k-\operatorname{rank}(\partial_k)-\operatorname{rank}(\partial_{k+1}).
\]

The resulting Betti vectors, including \(\beta_0\), are
\[
(1,1),\quad
(1,1,0,0),\quad
(1,0,0,11,0,0),\quad
(1,0,0,91,0,0,0,0),
\]
for \(n=3,4,5,6\), and
\[
(1,0,0,246,7,0,0,0,0,0)
\]
for \(n=7\). Hence the reduced homology for \(n=3,4,5,6\) is supported in one degree, while for \(n=7\) it is supported in degrees \(3\) and \(4\). Since there are no intermediate values of \(n\), this proves the stated first-occurrence claim.

## Verification
For \(T_7\), the verifier reconstructs the complete face vector
\[
(21,210,1190,3780,5187,4410,2520,945,210,21)
\]
and the boundary ranks
\[
(20,190,1000,2534,2646,1764,756,189,21).
\]
These give \(\beta_3=246\) and \(\beta_4=7\), with all other reduced Betti numbers zero. The script also recomputes the full profiles for \(T_3,T_4,T_5,T_6\) and verifies the Euler characteristic independently from the face and homology data.

The computation is exhaustive: every simplex generated by every open neighborhood is included, and every boundary column is reduced exactly over \(\mathbf F_2\). No sampling, timeout inference, or floating-point computation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's neighborhood-complex paper provides the standard definition and the relation of neighborhood complexes to graph-topological invariants; its accessible full text contains no occurrence of “triangular” or “line graph.” Ralf Fröberg's 2026 open-access study computes neighborhood complexes and Stanley–Reisner data for several graph classes, including paths, stars, complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, and complete multipartite graphs; targeted full-text searches found no triangular-graph or line-graph treatment.

Semantic-index searches using the exact triangular-graph notation, the line-graph formulation \(L(K_7)\), the numerical ranks \(246\) and \(7\), and mixed-dimensional neighborhood homology returned no implication-equivalent result. The closest returned records concern neighborhood complexes of unrelated graphs or homology of different graph complexes.

## Limitations
Only mod-two homology is determined. Equality of Betti profiles over several other fields was used during stress testing but is not part of the accepted claim and does not certify torsion-freeness. The result does not determine attaching maps or a wedge decomposition. It also does not predict what happens for \(T_n\) with \(n\ge8\). Search noncoverage is not a proof of global novelty; older or unindexed literature under association-scheme or Hom-complex terminology remains a residual risk.
