# Mixed-dimensional Rips homotopy and full persistence of the Möbius–Kantor graph

## Finding
Let \(G=GP(8,3)\) be the 16-vertex Möbius–Kantor graph, with vertices \(u_i,v_i\) indexed modulo \(8\), and the edges given below. For its unit-edge shortest-path metric and the inclusive Vietoris–Rips complex \(K_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le}(G;r)\), the complete homotopy classification for all real \(r\ge0\) is
\[
K_r\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{16}\mathrm{pt},&0\le r<1,\\
\bigvee^9 S^1,&1\le r<2,\\
(\bigvee^9 S^2)\vee(\bigvee^2 S^3),&2\le r<3,\\
S^7,&3\le r<4,\\
\mathrm{pt},&r\ge4.
\end{cases}
\]
Over **every** field, its ordinary persistence barcode consists of fifteen degree-zero bars \([0,1)\), one degree-zero bar \([0,\infty)\), nine degree-one bars \([1,2)\), nine degree-two bars \([2,3)\), two degree-three bars \([2,3)\), and one degree-seven bar \([3,4)\). No other positive-length bars occur. In particular, the integral homology groups at each scale are torsion-free.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices are \(\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\}\). The unoriented edges are exactly \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+3}\), for every index modulo \(8\). Each edge has length one. Distances are shortest-path distances. At threshold \(r\), a nonempty vertex set is a simplex if and only if every pair of its distinct vertices has graph distance at most \(r\). The only changes in this finite filtration occur at the integers \(1,2,3,4\); conventions at equal thresholds are inclusive.

## Proof
**Distance and bipartition.** A direct breadth-first expansion from \(u_0\) gives distance layers
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
0&u_0\\
1&u_1,u_7,v_0\\
2&u_2,u_6,v_1,v_3,v_5,v_7\\
3&u_3,u_5,v_2,v_4,v_6\\
4&u_4
\end{array}
\]
and from \(v_0\) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
0&v_0\\
1&u_0,v_3,v_5\\
2&u_1,u_3,u_5,u_7,v_2,v_6\\
3&u_2,u_4,u_6,v_1,v_7\\
4&v_4.
\end{array}
\]
Rotation of all indices preserves adjacency, so these two tables establish every distance. The graph is bipartite with parts \(X,Y\) each of size \(8\). Within each part, the only distance-four pairs are the four opposite-index pairs, and all other distinct pairs have distance two. Across the bipartition, two vertices have distance one or three. In particular the eight forbidden distance-four pairs in the whole vertex set form a perfect matching.

**Thresholds zero, one, three, and four.** Below one, the complex consists of isolated vertices. At threshold one the complex is the graph itself, which has \(16\) vertices, \(24\) edges, is connected, and has no triangles; hence it is \(\bigvee^9S^1\). At threshold three, every pair is allowed except the eight disjoint distance-four pairs. Thus the clique complex is the join of eight two-point complexes:
\[
K_3\cong\underbrace{S^0*S^0*\cdots*S^0}_{8\text{ factors}}\cong S^7.
\]
At threshold four all vertex pairs are allowed, so the complex is a simplex.

**Threshold two: pure and mixed faces.** Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the induced subcomplexes of \(K_2\) on \(X\) and \(Y\). Each is the clique complex of the complete eight-vertex graph with four disjoint edges deleted. Accordingly each is the join of four zero-spheres and is a simplicial three-sphere:
\[
A\cong S^3,\qquad B\cong S^3.
\]
For either side \(X\), let \(D_X\) be the complex generated by the eight triangles \(N_G(y)\), with \(y\in Y\); define \(D_Y\) analogously. Let \(M\) be the subcomplex generated by all mixed simplices of \(K_2\). Its pure faces are precisely the faces of \(D_X\) and \(D_Y\): since the graph is bipartite, every across-part pair whose distance is at most two is actually an edge of \(G\), and a pure simplex extends to a mixed one if and only if its vertices have a common neighbor on the other side. Therefore
\[
K_2=A\cup M\cup B,\quad A\cap M=D_X,\quad B\cap M=D_Y,\quad A\cap B=\varnothing.
\]
The last equality is for geometric realizations; the empty simplex is included in each abstract simplicial complex.

**The mixed complex collapses to either neighbor complex.** Here is a complete acyclic matching argument. To retract \(M\) onto \(D_X\), take a simplex with nonempty \(Y\)-vertex set \(V\). Its \(X\)-vertex set \(U\) is contained in the common-neighbor set \(N_X(V)\), which is nonempty because the simplex is a face of a mixed simplex. Choose the least-index vertex \(x(V)\in N_X(V)\), and pair the face \(U\cup V\) without \(x(V)\) with the face \((U\cup\{x(V)\})\cup V\). Each face containing a \(Y\)-vertex belongs to exactly one such pair; the unmatched faces are exactly \(D_X\). A reversed incidence along a pair retains \(V\), and any subsequent downward unmatched incidence either strictly decreases \(V\) or stays at an upper member, never a lower member of a pair with the same \(V\). Thus no closed gradient path is possible. The matching is relative to the subcomplex \(D_X\), and the resulting standard acyclic-matching collapse yields a homotopy equivalence \(D_X\hookrightarrow M\). Interchange \(X,Y\) for \(D_Y\hookrightarrow M\).

**The two neighbor complexes are bouquets of nine circles.** Two different vertices on the opposite side cannot have two common neighbors: that would produce a four-cycle, impossible by the distance tables (equivalently by direct inspection of the defining edges). Therefore distinct generating triangles of \(D_X\) intersect in at most one vertex; no edges are shared by triangles. The complex has \(8\) vertices, \(24\) edges (all same-side pairs at distance two), and \(8\) triangles, because every distance-two pair has exactly one common neighbor. Remove one edge together with its unique triangle for each of the eight triangles: all eight operations are elementary collapses. A connected graph remains, with \(8\) vertices and \(16\) edges; its cycle rank is \(16-8+1=9\). Hence \(D_X\simeq\bigvee^9 S^1\), and likewise \(D_Y\simeq\bigvee^9 S^1\).

**Homotopy pushout.** All inclusions above are simplicial cofibrations. Their union is the homotopy pushout of the diagram \(A\leftarrow D_X\to M\leftarrow D_Y\to B\). Since \(D_X\to M\) and \(D_Y\to M\) are equivalences, the diagram is homotopy equivalent to a span \(A\leftarrow M\to B\). Both structure maps are null-homotopic: their source has the homotopy type of a connected graph, whereas \(A\) and \(B\) are simply connected, indeed two-connected, three-spheres. The homotopy pushout of two null maps out of a connected based space is \(A\vee B\vee\Sigma M\). Consequently
\[
K_2\simeq S^3\vee S^3\vee\Sigma\left(\bigvee^9S^1\right)
\simeq(\bigvee^2S^3)\vee(\bigvee^9S^2).
\]

**Persistence.** The only nonzero positive-degree reduced homology groups at the consecutive nontrivial stages occur in mutually distinct degrees: degree one at stage one, degrees two and three at stage two, and degree seven at stage three. All maps in positive degrees to the next threshold are therefore zero, independently of field. Connectivity occurs exactly at one. This proves every interval in the stated barcode; sphere wedges ensure the integral groups are free.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` and its neighboring `morse_certificate.json` require only the standard library. Invoke `python3 verify.py` from the folder containing both files. It independently reconstructs all 16 graph distances; the two bipartition classes; the eight distance-four antipodal pairs; every distance-two edge and unique common neighbor on either side; and all cliques of thresholds one, two, and three. Their respective face vectors are
\[
(16,24),\quad(16,72,112,48),\quad(16,112,448,1120,1792,1792,1024,256).
\]
At threshold two, the certificate records \(118\) elementary free-face pairs and \(12\) remaining critical faces (one vertex, nine triangles, and two tetrahedra), in an explicit chronological free-face or maximal-critical-face removal sequence; the verifier checks all incidences and removals. An independent mod-two boundary-rank calculation yields reduced Betti ranks \((0,0,9,2)\) in dimensions zero through three. These finite exact checks corroborate the structural homotopy proof, rather than replacing it.

## Relationship to prior work
Kawamura's 2011 bipartite-relation theorem supplies a general form of the middle-complex/neighbor-complex correspondence used in the proof; the new statement is the graph-specific all-threshold homotopy and persistence calculation and its explicit mixed-dimensional decomposition. Adamaszek (2011) gives broad graph-power clique-complex methods and cycle cases, but his simple large-girth collapse bound does not apply at radius two to this girth-six graph. Adamaszek–Adams (first posted 2015) classify Rips complexes of circles and circle subsets; the 16-point shortest-path graph metric here is not a circle metric covered by their classification. These results are essential context, not claims of novelty for their general methods.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns a fixed 16-vertex graph and inclusive graph-metric filtration, not all generalized Petersen graphs or all symmetric incidence graphs. It does not claim any new general discrete-Morse or homotopy-pushout theorem. Search of the named primary sources and the available mathematical research index found no statement implying this precise classification, but an unindexed or differently phrased equivalent calculation remains possible. Computational checks are finite exact corroboration; the homotopy classification follows from the stated structural proof.
