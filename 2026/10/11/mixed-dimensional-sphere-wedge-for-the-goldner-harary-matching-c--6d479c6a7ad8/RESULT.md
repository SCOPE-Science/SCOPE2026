# Mixed-dimensional sphere wedge for the Goldner–Harary matching complex

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the Goldner–Harary graph, constructed explicitly below, and let \(\mathcal M(G)\) be its ordinary graph matching complex. There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal M(G)\simeq\bigvee^{30}S^{2}\vee\bigvee^{48}S^{3}.
\]
In particular, the only nonzero reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal M(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{30}\) and \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{48}\). This is a statement of homotopy equivalence, not just an equality of field-valued Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
Construct a triangular bipyramid on vertex set \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with equatorial triangle \(0,1,2\) and nonadjacent apices \(3,4\). Its six triangular facets are, in order, \((3,0,1)\), \((3,1,2)\), \((3,2,0)\), \((4,0,1)\), \((4,1,2)\), and \((4,2,0)\). For each facet with index \(i\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), insert vertex \(5+i\) and join it to the three vertices of the facet. Retain the nine bipyramid edges and no others. The resulting simple graph \(G\) has eleven vertices and twenty-seven edges and is the Goldner–Harary graph, the Kleetope of the triangular bipyramid. This edge-list construction, rather than just a graph name, defines the scope of the claim.

A face of the ordinary matching complex \(\mathcal M(G)\) is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of \(G\), with the empty set understood as the empty face. Its vertices correspond to the twenty-seven graph edges. All chains and homotopy groups are taken integrally. There is no assertion about other maximal planar graphs or matching complexes of graph families.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive finite complex.** Represent each matching as a twenty-seven-bit integer using the lexicographic graph-edge order generated by `verify_goldner_harary.py`. Recursively append every permissible unused graph edge. Every matching is generated exactly once by strictly increasing edge index. The numbers of faces in dimensions zero through four are exactly \((27,219,613,510,72)\), giving \(1441\) nonempty faces. No matching has more than five graph edges because the graph has eleven vertices. The alternating Euler characteristic is \(-17\).

**2. Integral discrete-Morse reduction.** The file `MORSE_CERTIFICATE.json` records a fourteen-stage edge-toggle matching, with each pair specified by its lower and upper face bit masks. The checker `verify_goldner_harary.py` reconstructs the graph and every face before regenerating the proposed matching. The empty-face pair is excluded from the ordinary (unaugmented) face poset and its upper singleton is retained as the unique critical vertex. This leaves exactly \(662\) matched pairs and critical cells in dimensions \(0,2,3\), in respective numbers \(1,49,67\). The complete nonempty-face Hasse graph has \(4677\) cover relations. Orient each matched cover upward and every other cover downward. The checker constructs this entire directed graph and uses a topological sort to verify that all \(1441\) vertices are visited, certifying that the matching is acyclic.

**3. Signed Morse differential.** Orient each simplex by its increasing bit indices. For each critical three-simplex, compute its alternating simplicial boundary; recursively eliminate the paired two-simplices using their paired three-simplices with the signed coefficient of the pivot incidence. Because the directed matching is acyclic, this elimination terminates. It computes the integral cellular differential \(\partial_3\) of the Morse CW complex, a \(49\)-by-\(67\) integer matrix. The checker recalculates its full contents, checks the digest recorded in the certificate, and verifies that its rational rank is exactly \(19\). The certificate also identifies a \(19\)-by-\(19\) submatrix with integer determinant \(+1\), which is recomputed by an exact fraction-free determinant algorithm.

**4. From the matrix to homotopy.** Since the Morse CW complex has a single zero-cell and no one-cells, its two-skeleton is \(\bigvee^{49}S^2\). The homotopy classes of attaching maps of its \(67\) three-cells are completely determined by the integer degree vectors in \(\pi_2(\bigvee^{49}S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^{49}\). Rational rank \(19\), together with the unit maximal minor, implies that the integer matrix has precisely \(19\) nonzero Smith invariant factors and all are units. Unimodular row and column changes correspond to homotopy equivalences of the source and target wedges of two-spheres. Taking the homotopy cofiber of the resulting diagonal map cancels \(19\) sphere/three-cell pairs. The remaining \(49-19=30\) two-cells and \(67-19=48\) three-cells have trivial attaching maps. Thus the homotopy type is \(\bigvee^{30}S^2\vee\bigvee^{48}S^3\), as claimed.

## Verification
The package contains two standalone Python programs requiring only the Python standard library and one integer certificate:

`python verify_goldner_harary.py MORSE_CERTIFICATE.json`

`python verify_independent.py`

The first checks the graph, complete matching enumeration, all discrete-Morse pairs, all directed incidences, acyclicity, signed integer Morse differential, exact rank and unit maximal minor. The second independently constructs the same graph using a different enumeration algorithm, directly forms simplicial mod-two boundary matrices, and verifies their respective ranks \((26,193,390,72)\) from dimensions one through four. These give reduced mod-two Betti numbers \(30\) and \(48\) in dimensions two and three, with no others; the integral proof uses the signed Morse differential, not this field check. Both programs were executed on the packaged certificate and reproduced their asserted outputs.

## Relationship to prior work
The Goldner–Harary graph is a canonical eleven-vertex, twenty-seven-edge maximal planar non-Hamiltonian graph. Engström's 2005 independence-complex methods and Barmak's 2010 star-cluster techniques provide general topological tools and connectivity bounds, but do not imply these precise wedge multiplicities. Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega establish sphere-wedge decompositions for outerplanar graph matching complexes; the graph here is not outerplanar, since a simple outerplanar graph on eleven vertices has at most nineteen edges. Their broader planar-graph question motivates examining this non-outerplanar example, but the present positive example does not prove that conjecture. Bayer, Goeckner and Jelić Milutinović classify certain manifold matching complexes, whereas the present complex is nonpure and is not covered by that theorem. The complete integer homotopy decomposition is obtained from the explicit calculations above, not inferred from those earlier results.

## Limitations
The statement is only for the graph with the explicit edge construction above, with ordinary matching-complex conventions. Exact computational enumeration and an independently replayable discrete-Morse certificate establish the finite theorem; they do not imply any result for a general graph family. The separate mod-two computation is a consistency check, not an independent integral homotopy certificate. Literature comparison may miss equivalent unindexed or unpublished calculations. No independent scientific audit or independent formal certification has been performed.
