# Same-model scientific review

Claim assessed: for each finite affine plane of order \(q\ge2\), the inclusive scale-two Vietoris–Rips complex of its point–line incidence graph is \(\bigvee^{(q-1)^2(q+1)}S^2\vee\bigvee^{(q-1)^{q+1}}S^q\).

## Correctness: PASS
The proof determines all distance-two cliques; constructs subcomplexes \(U\) and \(V\) meeting in the original incidence graph; collapses \(U\) onto a point simplex and \(V\) onto the join \(B\) of line-parallel-class discrete sets; then uses the nullhomotopy of a graph mapping to the simply connected \(B\) to split the homotopy pushout as \(B\vee\Sigma G\). A direct edge/vertex count supplies the second wedge rank. All finite-plane quantifiers, the \(q=2\) overlapping dimensions, and inclusive scale conventions are explicit. Independent construction of orders \(2\) and \(3\) reproduces their complete mod-two homology, but those experiments are corroboration only.

## Originality: PASS
Closest published work: Adams–Karassev–Virk (arXiv:2512.23108) addresses topological metric thickenings; Larrión–Pizaña–Villarroel-Flores (2009) compares fundamental groups of bipartite graph squares; Kawamura (2011) provides relative homological comparison criteria. Full texts were inspected for possible stronger coverage. A public projective-plane scale-two computation obtains a wedge only of two-spheres, but cannot be specialized to affine incidence graphs because parallel lines are distance four, giving a noncontractible join that creates additional \(q\)-sphere summands. Indexed searches with Rips, graph-square, Levi-graph, affine-plane, and parallel-line aliases and a current historical-claim comparison found no equivalent or dominating assertion. A previously unindexed direct computation or broad theorem remains an originality risk, and absence from an index is not evidence of absolute uniqueness.

## Value: PASS
The result determines an entire family of homotopy types with integrally meaningful and sometimes mixed dimensions, rather than a single small graph or a field-dependent table. It resolves the effect of affine parallel classes on the Rips homotopy type and furnishes exact higher-dimensional topology for arbitrarily large prime-power orders. This differs materially from the projective-plane behavior.

## Limitations
The claim is conditional on the existence of a finite affine plane; it does not classify those planes. Computation independently checks the prime-field cases only. The theorem does not address arbitrary weighted graph distances or partial linear spaces, and no independent audit has been performed.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
