# Mod-two homology of four unordered points on \(K_{5,5}\)

## Finding
Let \(K_{5,5}\) be the complete bipartite graph with two vertex parts of size five, realized as a one-dimensional CW complex. For the space \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_4(K_{5,5})\) of unordered configurations of four distinct points, the complete mod-two Poincaré polynomial is
\[
\sum_{q\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb F_2}H_q(X;\mathbb F_2)t^q
=1+17t+528t^2+1278t^3+t^4.
\]
All higher homology groups with these coefficients vanish. In particular, the previously undetermined middle-degree dimensions in this natural four-particle example are \(528\) and \(1278\), and \(\chi(X)=-765\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the unsubdivided abstract simple graph \(K_{5,5}\), viewed with its topological realization. Configurations are **unordered**, with no collisions, and the coefficient field is precisely \(\mathbb F_2\). The graph braid-group convention agrees with the homology of this unordered configuration space. The conclusion concerns all homological degrees but only this graph and exactly four particles. It is not a statement about integral free ranks, integral torsion decompositions, homotopy type, or ordered configurations.

## Proof
Use the weight-four component of the reduced Świątkowski complex, which computes the homology of graph configuration spaces; see An, Section 2 and the original graph-configuration chain-model literature. There are ten vertices and twenty-five edges. Every vertex is incident to five edges. Select one reference half-edge \(h_v^0\) at each vertex. The reduced local factor is generated by the empty state in bidegree \((0,0)\) and the four differences \(h-h_v^0\) in bidegree \((1,1)\). A graph-edge variable has bidegree \((0,1)\). Over \(\mathbb F_2\),
\[
\partial(h-h_v^0)=e(h)+e(h_v^0),
\]
and the differential extends via the graded Leibniz rule. Edge variables commute. A degree-\(q\), weight-four basis element selects \(q\) distinct vertices, one of their four nonreference half-edge differences at each selected vertex, and an edge monomial of degree \(4-q\). Thus the complex vanishes above degree four, and its degree-\(q\) dimension is exactly
\[
c_q=\binom{10}{q}4^q\binom{28-q}{4-q},\qquad 0\le q\le4.
\]
This gives \((c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(20475,117000,234000,192000,53760)\).

The accompanying `verify.py` constructs **every basis element and every column** of each of the four boundary maps, using lexicographically enumerated vertices, incident edges, and repeated-edge monomials. It performs exact binary Gaussian elimination on integer bitsets. A pivot column is retained at its highest occupied row; XOR eliminates that pivot from subsequent columns. Elementary row independence shows this procedure computes the rank without approximation. The resulting ranks are
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,
\operatorname{rank}\partial_3,\operatorname{rank}\partial_4)
=(20474,96509,136963,53759).
\]
Because the differential squares to zero (each pair of local factors contributes twice and cancels in characteristic two), the homology dimension at degree \(q\) is \(c_q-\operatorname{rank}\partial_q-\operatorname{rank}\partial_{q+1}\), taking the endpoint ranks to be zero. Substituting the five chain dimensions and four ranks proves the asserted complete vector \((1,17,528,1278,1)\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3; no third-party modules or external data are needed. The script repeats the full exact elimination with two different reference-half-edge choices (first and last incident edge). Both give the same chain dimensions, ranks, and Betti vector. It also checks selected degree-two differential compositions directly and checks Euler characteristic independently with Gal's graph-braid formula:
\[
\chi(X)=[t^4]\frac{(1-4t)^{10}}{(1-t)^{25}}=-765.
\]
The computed alternating sum \(1-17+528-1278+1=-765\) agrees. The degree-zero answer agrees with connectedness, while the degree-four mod-two class agrees with An's Corollary 3.18, which predicts the top degree for balanced graphs in this family.

## Relationship to prior work
An's preprint *Torsion of every finite order in the homology of graph braid groups* (arXiv:2610.04305v1, initially public October 3, 2026, primary MSC 55R80 among its primary classifications) explicitly asks for lower Betti numbers when there are at least four particles (Question 8.1). Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.18 determine top-degree homology and degree-three torsion for the balanced case, but not the complete degree-two/degree-three mod-two dimensions computed here. This result settles a **single finite coefficient-field instance** of that wider problem, not the general question.

Maciążek–Sawicki, *Non-abelian Quantum Statistics on Graphs*, arXiv:1806.02846, Table 1 on PDF page 30, tabulates different graphs: \(K_4\), \(K_{3,3}\), and \(K_5\), not \(K_{5,5}\). Its Sections 5.5–5.6 treat \(K_{3,3}\) and \(K_{2,p}\). An–Drummond-Cole–Knudsen, *Asymptotic homology of graph braid groups*, arXiv:2005.08286, gives leading asymptotic Betti information as particle count grows rather than the exact four-particle dimensions. Thus neither supplies this pair of middle-degree dimensions as a direct implication.

## Limitations
The calculation is finite and exact over \(\mathbb F_2\); it does not lift the homology to integral coefficients or decide the splitting of degree-two torsion. Gaussian elimination gives dimensions but not a compact geometrically meaningful cycle basis. The result covers one natural graph and particle count; general formulas, optimal polynomial ranges, and the integral groups asked for in Question 8.1 remain open. Prior-literature coverage was checked in relevant accessible full texts and indexed records, but an unindexed equivalent computation is an originality risk.
