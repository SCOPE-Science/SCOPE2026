# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. In the inradius-one regular \(N=4n\) polygon, the polygonal norm is bounded above by the Euclidean norm and every polygonal unit vector has Euclidean length at most \(R=\sec(\pi/N)\). The Euclidean parallelogram identity gives the global upper bound \(C'_{\mathrm{NJ}}\le R^2\). Two adjacent vertices symmetric about a facet normal have sum norm \(2\) and difference norm \(2\tan(\pi/N)\); divisibility by \(4\) makes the difference direction another facet normal. Their quotient is \(1+\tan^2(\pi/N)=R^2\), so the bound is sharp for every \(n\ge1\).

## Originality
PASS. Searches used both \(C'_{\mathrm{NJ}}\) and the upper modified second von Neumann--Jordan terminology, regular-polygon aliases, exact dodecagonal/octet specializations and the secant-square candidate. The closest public regular-polygon result covers the octagon only. Full publisher text for the main 2022 and 2020 sources was inspected and contains no all-regular-\(4n\) formula. A 2023 paper on extreme absolute normalized norms is highly relevant but was inspectable only at metadata/abstract level; this is retained explicitly as a residual access risk rather than treated as full-text noncoverage.

## Value
PASS. This is an infinite exact family for a standard non-Hilbertian geometric constant, with a uniform equality mechanism and a sharp quadratic convergence rate to the Hilbert value. It subsumes isolated square, octagon and dodecagon values and provides a canonical benchmark for regular polyhedral approximations of the Euclidean plane.

## Closest literature and limitations
Zuo, Huang, Huang and Wang's 2022 work develops Gao-type calculations for absolute normalized planar norms and lists MSC \(46B20\). Ciesielski and Płuciennik identify the modified upper two-variable constant with \(C'_{\mathrm{NJ}}\) and calculate classical \(L^p/\ell^p\) families. Komuro, Mitani and Okazaki's 2023 extreme-norm paper remains the principal full-text access risk. The theorem requires \(4\mid N\) and does not assert the same formula for \(N\equiv2\pmod4\).
