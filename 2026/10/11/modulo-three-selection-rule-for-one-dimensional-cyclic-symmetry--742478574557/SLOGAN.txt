In the one-dimensional cosine-Fourier symmetry classes used for nonlinear diffusion, a simple critical residue pair \(\pm r\pmod n\) is forced to bifurcate by pitchfork whenever \(3r\not\equiv0\pmod n\), subject to the same doubled-sector Fredholm check used in the source. Thus transcriticality can escape the quadratic symmetry obstruction only in the unique pair \(r\equiv n/3\pmod n\) when \(3\mid n\), explaining the source's \(n=3\) versus \(n=2,5\) examples.
