# Neighborhood complexes of hypercubes collapse to explicit 2-sphere wedges
## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge 4\), let \(Q_d\) be the \(d\)-dimensional hypercube graph and let \(\mathcal N(Q_d)\) be its Lovász neighborhood complex. Then \(\mathcal N(Q_d)\) has exactly two connected components, exchanged by every parity-reversing cube translation. Each component simplicially collapses to a simply connected \(2\)-dimensional complex and is homotopy equivalent to
\[
\bigvee^{\mu_d}S^2,
\qquad
\mu_d=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal N(Q_d)\simeq
\left(\bigvee^{\mu_d}S^2\right)\sqcup
\left(\bigvee^{\mu_d}S^2\right).
\]
The first values for one component are \(\mu_4=7\), \(\mu_5=31\), \(\mu_6=111\), and \(\mu_7=351\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set of \(Q_d\) is \(\{0,1\}^d\), written additively over \(\mathbf F_2\); two vertices are adjacent exactly when their Hamming distance is one. The parity of a vertex is the parity of its Hamming weight. For a graph \(G\), the Lovász neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(G)\) has the nonisolated vertices of \(G\) as vertices, with a finite set forming a simplex exactly when its members have a common graph neighbor.

The claim is for \(d\ge 4\). It concerns the ordinary open-neighborhood complex, not a closed-neighborhood complex, a Vietoris–Rips complex, or a graph-power clique complex. The proof is symbolic for every allowed \(d\); finite computations are only corroborative.

## Proof
Because \(Q_d\) is bipartite by parity, every simplex of \(\mathcal N(Q_d)\) consists entirely of even vertices or entirely of odd vertices. Translation by any odd vector exchanges the two induced components, so it is enough to analyze the component \(K_d\) on the even vertices. It is connected because the even-weight subspace is generated by the vectors of Hamming weight two.

For each odd vertex \(y\), its neighborhood
\[
F_y=\{y+e_i:1\le i\le d\}
\]
is a facet of \(K_d\), hence a \((d-1)\)-simplex. If \(y\ne z\) are odd, then \(F_y\cap F_z\) has at most two vertices: two distinct cube vertices have common neighbors only when their Hamming distance is two, in which case they have exactly two common neighbors. Consequently every face of \(K_d\) with at least three vertices belongs to a unique facet.

Fix the first coordinate and put \(a_y=y+e_1\). Inside \(F_y\), retain the full \(1\)-skeleton together with every triangle containing \(a_y\). The whole simplex \(F_y\) collapses onto this retained subcomplex. Indeed, order all subsets \(\sigma\subseteq F_y\setminus\{a_y\}\) with \(|\sigma|\ge3\) by decreasing cardinality and collapse the pair
\[
\sigma\subset \sigma\cup\{a_y\}.
\]
When a pair is reached, every larger coface has already been removed, so \(\sigma\) is free. Since every face with at least three vertices lies in a unique facet and all shared faces are retained, these facetwise sequences concatenate to a global simplicial collapse
\[
K_d\searrow L_d,
\]
where \(L_d\) is \(2\)-dimensional.

Write
\[
g_j=e_1+e_j\qquad(2\le j\le d).
\]
The vertices of \(L_d\) are the even vectors. Its edges join pairs at Hamming distance two. Its retained triangles are exactly
\[
\{x,x+g_j,x+g_k\},
\qquad 2\le j<k\le d,
\]
for even \(x\).

The complex \(L_d\) is simply connected. An edge whose difference is \(e_j+e_k=g_j+g_k\), with \(j,k\ge2\), is the third edge of a retained triangle and is homotopic relative endpoints to two successive edges of types \(g_j\) and \(g_k\). Hence every edge loop is homotopic to a loop in the ordinary \((d-1)\)-cube graph generated by \(g_2,\ldots,g_d\). For each pair \(j<k\), the two retained triangles based at \(x\) and at \(x+g_j+g_k\) fill the corresponding coordinate square. Thus all coordinate-square relations of that cube graph hold in \(L_d\). The cubical \(2\)-skeleton of the \((d-1)\)-cube is simply connected, because attaching cells of dimension at least three to it produces the contractible full cube without changing its fundamental group. Therefore \(\pi_1(L_d)=0\).

The face numbers of \(L_d\) are
\[
f_0=2^{d-1},\qquad
f_1=2^{d-2}\binom d2,\qquad
f_2=2^{d-1}\binom{d-1}{2}.
\]
The edge count follows because the halved cube has degree \(\binom d2\). The triangle count follows from the displayed unique-facet description. Since \(L_d\) is connected and simply connected,
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_2(L_d;\mathbf Z)
=f_0-f_1+f_2-1
=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1=\mu_d.
\]
Because \(L_d\) is a finite simply connected \(2\)-complex, \(H_2(L_d;\mathbf Z)\) is free. Choosing maps \(S^2\to L_d\) representing a basis of \(H_2\) gives a homology equivalence from \(\bigvee^{\mu_d}S^2\) to \(L_d\); both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so Whitehead's theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. The collapse and the parity-swapping isomorphism prove the claim for both components.

## Verification
The standalone script `artifacts/verify.py` reconstructs one parity component directly from common neighborhoods for \(4\le d\le7\). For each tested dimension it verifies that distinct facets intersect in at most two vertices, that every face of size at least three has a unique facet, and that the explicit facetwise matching pairs every deleted face exactly once while leaving precisely the stated \(2\)-complex. For \(4\le d\le6\) it additionally builds the full directed Hasse graph of the matching and checks acyclicity.

The script independently computes mod-two boundary ranks for both the original neighborhood component and the collapsed complex. It obtains reduced homology only in degree two, with ranks \(7,31,111,351\) for \(d=4,5,6,7\), respectively. These finite calculations corroborate the proof; they are not used to infer the all-parameter statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's 2005 paper gives the common-neighbor definition of the neighborhood complex, proves that the barycentric subdivision of \(\mathcal N(G)\) collapses to the Lovász complex, and proves that \(\mathcal N(G)\) and \(\operatorname{Hom}(K_2,G)\) have the same simple-homotopy type for arbitrary graphs. It does not compute the hypercube family or the displayed multiplicity.

Shukla's later classification treats graphs of maximum degree at most three and a separate class of four-regular circulant graphs. That scope does not imply the all-dimensional hypercube theorem for \(d\ge4\), and the inspected full text contains no hypercube calculation. Exact searches under hypercube, Boolean-cube, common-neighbor, Lovász-neighborhood, and \(Q_d\) aliases found no statement implying this formula. The closest indexed hypercube records concern other complexes such as Morse or Vietoris–Rips complexes.

## Limitations
The theorem is about the homotopy type of the ordinary neighborhood complex and does not assert a homeomorphism or a canonical identification of individual sphere summands. The finite verifier covers only \(4\le d\le7\); the proof, not the finite replay, establishes the theorem for all \(d\ge4\). Search-based originality checks cannot exclude an older result stated under substantially different terminology, so that remains the main residual originality risk.
