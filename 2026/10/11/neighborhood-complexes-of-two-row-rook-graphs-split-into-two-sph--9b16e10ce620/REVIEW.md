# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs every maximal neighborhood simplex of \(R_n\), separates them into two subcomplexes, collapses those subcomplexes to the two boundary spheres, and identifies their intersection exactly with the crown graph \(K_{n,n}-M\). For \(n\ge4\), the two target spheres are simply connected, so both graph inclusions are null-homotopic and the homotopy-pushout calculation gives the claimed wedge. Exact chain calculations for \(4\le n\le8\) agree with the formula.

## Originality
PASS, with residual search risk. The comparison covered line-graph, rook-graph, Cartesian-product, categorical-product, Hom-complex, and crown-graph formulations. Matsushita supplies the general neighborhood-complex and graph-covering framework, while Nilakantan–Shukla study \(K_2\times K_n\) with \(\times\) explicitly categorical. Neither inspected source computes \(\mathcal N(K_2\square K_n)\) or implies the mixed-dimensional wedge formula.

## Value
PASS. The two-row rook graphs are a natural all-parameter line-graph family. The theorem gives a closed homotopy classification whose topology occurs simultaneously in dimensions \(2\) and \(n-2\), and it identifies the crown graph as the precise gluing mechanism producing the quadratic sphere multiplicity.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is T. Matsushita, *Fundamental groups of neighborhood complexes*, arXiv:1210.2803, and N. Nilakantan–S. Shukla, *Neighborhood Complexes of Some Exponential Graphs*, DOI 10.37236/5312. The latter paper uses the categorical product rather than the Cartesian product. The theorem is restricted to \(n\ge4\), and it establishes ordinary homotopy type rather than a global simple-homotopy collapse. An equivalent older formulation under different terminology remains a residual originality risk.
