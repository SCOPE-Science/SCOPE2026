# Review: Non-polydisc equality cases for complex fractional illumination

**Correctness — PASS.** The fractional covering measure is explicit, has mass \(2^n\), and covers every boundary stratum (including vanishing coordinates) with weight at least one. An independent phase-uniform boundary-point distribution limits the illumination probability of every direction to \(2^{-n}\); Tonelli yields the matching lower bound. The one-sided derivative of the two-coordinate \(\ell_1\) gauge is handled exactly at its nonsmooth coordinate axes. Complex-linear non-equivalence is proved by a finite-versus-continuum phase obstruction. No finite test is used as a substitute for a proof.

**Originality — PASS.** The closest paper is Rotem–Schejter–Slomka, *The Complex Illumination Problem*, arXiv:2410.12021v1; its later full open-access journal version was also examined. Its Conjecture 1.2 predicts polydisc-only equality, Theorem 1.3 computes the polydisc value, and Theorems 1.9–1.10 prove strictness only in the complex zonotope/zonoid classes. Their arguments do not state or imply equality for the two-coordinate complex \(\ell_1\)-ball product. Searches for the complex crosspolytope, fractional product coverings, and counterexamples located no prior equivalent calculation. An unindexed equivalent observation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem directly refutes the asserted equality classification in every dimension \(n\ge2\), while providing matched explicit fractional primal and dual witnesses for a natural polar-product body. It does not settle the universal fractional illumination upper bound, the classical illumination conjecture, or all equality cases.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
