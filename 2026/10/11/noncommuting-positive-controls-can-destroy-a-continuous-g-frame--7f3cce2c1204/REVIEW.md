# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The identity family on a one-point measure space is Parseval. The two control matrices are positive invertible, with exact spectra
\[
\{2,1\},
\qquad
\left\{\frac{39}{20},\frac1{20}\right\}.
\]
Nevertheless their mixed controlled quadratic form takes the exact value
\[
-\frac1{40}
\]
at a specified nonzero vector. This directly contradicts the defining lower controlled-frame inequality.

The repaired criterion is exact because the controlled quadratic form of an ordinary continuous \(g\)-frame is represented by
\[
Q S_\Lambda P.
\]

## Originality
PASS. The exact source theorem, universal-control corollary, arbitrary-control aliases, noncommuting-control formulation, mixed-operator formulation, earlier product-positivity theorem, and later controlled-\(g\)-frame characterizations were compared.

Earlier literature explicitly knows that a product of two positive invertible operators is positive only under commutation and proves the safe equal-control equivalence. The later checked paper repeats an unrestricted two-control equivalence. No checked source supplies the present counterexample or the exact repaired criterion.

## Value
PASS. The theorem being corrected is the central bridge between ordinary and two-control continuous \(g\)-frames in the source. The counterexample makes the mixed energy negative even though the original frame is Parseval, so the defect invalidates the property itself rather than merely its numerical bounds.

The repair is directly usable: require
\[
Q S_\Lambda P\in GL^+(H),
\]
or impose pairwise commutation as a simple sufficient condition.
