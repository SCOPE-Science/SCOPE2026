# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the exact functional gauge by rearrangement and an attaining phase-aligned vector, then proves the isometry criterion in both directions by order-statistic counting. It is valid for all \(p>0\), including the quasi-Banach range, and includes boundary equality. The disjointness and balanced-overlap consequences are direct and exact.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source, arXiv:2610.01043v1, gives only the balanced \(n=2\) example in Remark 1.1 and explicitly restricts its subsequent weak-\(L_p\) isometry analysis to surjective maps. The earlier Aminov--Chilin theorem also assumes surjectivity. Targeted graph, appended-functional, weak-Lorentz, and disjointness searches found no implication covering the full coefficient region. A residual risk remains that an older finite-dimensional Lorentz embedding theorem uses different terminology.

## Value
PASS. The theorem completely describes the canonical one-extra-coordinate obstruction that motivates the source's surjectivity restriction, quantifies exactly when it destroys disjointness, and gives the sharp common-overlap threshold in every finite dimension. This converts one isolated example into a complete structural boundary for a natural family.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is Fang--Gao--Huang--Sukochev's Remark 1.1 and the surjective symmetric-sequence-space isometry theory of Aminov--Chilin. The result is deliberately limited to graph maps that retain the source coordinates and append one scalar functional; it does not classify arbitrary nonsurjective isometries or noncommutative embeddings.
