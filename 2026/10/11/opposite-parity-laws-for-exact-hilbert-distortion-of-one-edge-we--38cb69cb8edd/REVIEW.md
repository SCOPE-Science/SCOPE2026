# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The unit ball is the projection of the \(\ell_1\) edge ball onto the orthogonal complement of the weighted cycle relation. Averaging an arbitrary optimal ellipsoid's quadratic form over the first-edge permutation group is a genuine unrestricted reduction, not an unproved symmetric ansatz. Explicit evaluation at the projected coordinate generators gives the primal extremum, and exhaustive parity classification of cube-section vertices gives the polar extremum. Elementary monotonicity in the remaining scalar parameter locates the unique optimum. The branch-switch comparison is algebraically nonnegative, including the critical endpoint. Exact-rational computations independently confirm 56 nontrivial parameter instances, but the infinite statement is established analytically.

## Originality: PASS
Finite graph transportation-cost quotients and their isometric classification are known; these are credited. The closest published cycle parity statements describe the **numerical index**, not Euclidean Banach--Mazur distance. General Banach--Mazur results on low-dimensional cubes versus cross-polytopes also do not solve the graph-weighted quotient ellipsoid problem. The compared full-text primary papers concern the quotient presentation, isometries, and comparison to \(\ell_1\), without a claim equivalent to the full one-edge Euclidean distortion formula. Residual risk: an equivalent weighted-zero-sum cube-section formula might appear in older polyhedral Banach-space literature not indexed under graph terminology.

## Value: PASS
The family is a natural one-edge deformation of a cycle metric; the exact optimal linear Hilbert distortion is a quantitative Banach-space invariant, not a routine edge-metric statistic. The strict *opposite* monotonicity for even and odd cycle sizes is structural and comes from different types of extreme points of the polar cube section. The theorem supplies every dimension and every real edge parameter in its scope, with explicit optimum and endpoint cases, rather than an isolated small graph computation.

## Scope and risks
The proof does not handle several distinct exceptional edge lengths or complex scalars. The singular limit \(\lambda=0\) is excluded because the metric collapses points. No independent validation is claimed.
