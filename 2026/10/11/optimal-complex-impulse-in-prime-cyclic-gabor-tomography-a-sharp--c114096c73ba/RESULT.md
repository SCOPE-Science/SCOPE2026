# Optimal complex impulse in prime-cyclic Gabor tomography: a sharp conditioning barrier

## Finding
For every prime \(p\ge5\), let \(g_z=\mathbf1+z\delta_0\in\mathbb C^{\mathbb Z_p}\), \(z\in\mathbb C\), and normalize \(h_z=g_z/\|g_z\|_2\). The full \(p^2\) Gabor intensity measurements generated by \(h_z\) define a linear lifting \(\mathcal A_z:M_p(\mathbb C)\to\mathbb C^{p^2}\). Equip its domain with the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and its range with the Euclidean norm.

The **sharp condition-number minimum over every complex impulse** is
\[
\boxed{\inf_{z\in\mathbb C}\kappa(\mathcal A_z)=\frac{\sqrt p}{\sin(\pi/p)}},
\]
with the convention \(\kappa=+\infty\) if the lifting is singular. Equality holds **if and only if** \(z=i\sqrt p\) or \(z=-i\sqrt p\). Both optimal windows give injective liftings, hence phase retrieval of every signal up to a global phase. At each optimum the largest and smallest singular values are respectively \(\sqrt p\) and \(\sin(\pi/p)\).

More generally, writing \(B=p+|z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}z\) and \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}\), the **complete singular-value multiset**, with all multiplicities, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sqrt p &&(1\text{ time}),\\
&\frac{\sqrt p}{B}\bigl||z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}z\bigr| &&(p-1\text{ times}),\\
&\frac{\sqrt p}{B}\bigl|p+2\operatorname{Re}z\bigr| &&(p-1\text{ times}),\\
&\frac{\sqrt p}{B}|z+\overline z\,\omega^j| &&(p-1\text{ times for each }1\le j<p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Coincident numerical values have their multiplicities added. Consequently, the best possible normalized smallest singular value within this two-level family is \(\sin(\pi/p)/\sqrt p\), which is asymptotic to \(\pi p^{-3/2}\). This is a sharp **restricted-family** stability barrier, not a lower bound for arbitrary Gabor windows.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(p\ge5\) be prime. For \(k,\ell,x\in\mathbb Z_p\) set \((T_k f)(x)=f(x-k)\) and \((M_\ell f)(x)=\omega^{\ell x}f(x)\). Define \(v_{k,\ell}=M_\ell T_k h_z\) and
\[
\mathcal A_z(X)=\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(Xv_{k,\ell}v_{k,\ell}^*)\bigr)_{k,\ell\in\mathbb Z_p}.
\]
This is complex linear on **all** matrices, not merely on rank-one Hermitian matrices. \(B=\|g_z\|_2^2=p+|z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}z\ge p-1>0\) for all complex \(z\). The analysis concerns the condition number of this full lifted map; nonlinear reconstruction stability in a different metric is not asserted.

## Proof
**1. Exact ambiguity amplitudes.** Define the normalized ambiguity coefficients
\[
A_z(u,v)=\langle h_z,M_vT_u h_z\rangle,\qquad u,v\in\mathbb Z_p.
\]
The elementary identity \(\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_p}\omega^{vx}=p\mathbf1_{v=0}\), applied to \(g_z=\mathbf1+z\delta_0\), gives
\[
B A_z(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
B,&u=v=0,\\
|z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}z,&u=0,\ v\ne0,\\
p+2\operatorname{Re}z,&u\ne0,\ v=0,\\
\overline z+z\omega^{uv},&u\ne0,\ v\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]
Because \(p\) is prime, every nonzero \(j\in\mathbb Z_p\) is attained exactly \(p-1\) times as a product \(uv\) with \(u,v\ne0\). Magnitudes of \(\overline z+z\omega^j\) have the same multiset as magnitudes of \(z+\overline z\omega^j\), by replacing \(j\) with \(-j\).

**2. Full singular values and the top endpoint.** The Weyl matrices \(W_{u,v}=p^{-1/2}M_vT_u\) form an orthonormal Hilbert–Schmidt basis. Weyl commutation gives, for each \(k,\ell\),
\[
\mathcal A_z(W_{u,v})_{k,\ell}=p^{-1/2}\omega^{vk-\ell u}A_z(u,v).
\]
The columns indexed by distinct \((u,v)\) are orthogonal under the two-dimensional Fourier transform and have norms \(\sqrt p|A_z(u,v)|\). This proves the entire stated multiset, including multiplicities. Since each time-frequency shift is unitary and \(\|h_z\|=1\), \(|A_z(u,v)|\le1\); equality holds at \((0,0)\). Hence \(s_{\max}(\mathcal A_z)=\sqrt p\) for all \(z\).

**3. The phase-radius upper bound.** For \(z=r e^{i\phi}\), \(r>0\), set \(\alpha=\pi/p\) and
\[
\mu_p(\phi)=\min_{1\le j<p}|\cos(\phi-j\alpha)|.
\]
The mixed-frequency branch alone yields
\[
\frac{s_{\min}(\mathcal A_z)}{\sqrt p}
\le\frac{2r\mu_p(\phi)}{p+r^2+2r\cos\phi}
\le\frac{\mu_p(\phi)}{\sqrt p+\cos\phi},
\]
where the second inequality follows from \(r+p/r\ge2\sqrt p\), with equality precisely at \(r=\sqrt p\). The denominators are positive for \(p\ge5\). For \(z=0\), the lifting is singular, so it cannot attain the optimum.

**4. Sharp angular inequality and rigidity.** The zeros of \(\phi\mapsto\mu_p(\phi)\) on the phase circle modulo \(\pi\) lie at \(\pi/2+j\alpha\), for \(1\le j<p\). They leave a single gap of length \(2\alpha\), so \(\mu_p(\phi)\le\sin\alpha\) for all \(\phi\), with equality only at \(\phi=\pi/2\pmod\pi\). When \(\cos\phi\ge0\) this proves
\[
\frac{\mu_p(\phi)}{\sqrt p+\cos\phi}\le\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sqrt p},
\]
with equality possible only at the stated quadrature phases.

For \(\cos\phi<0\) write \(\phi=\pi/2+\varepsilon\) with \(0<\varepsilon<\pi\); then \(\cos\phi=-\sin\varepsilon\). If \(\varepsilon\in[0,\alpha]\cup[\pi-\alpha,\pi]\), put \(d=\min\{\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon\}\). The nearest nonexcluded zero gives \(\mu_p(\phi)=\sin(\alpha-d)\) and \(\sqrt p+\cos\phi=\sqrt p-\sin d\). Direct differentiation shows
\[
\frac{d}{dd}\frac{\sin(\alpha-d)}{\sqrt p-\sin d}
=\frac{-\sqrt p\cos(\alpha-d)+\sin\alpha}{(\sqrt p-\sin d)^2}<0,
\]
because \(\sqrt p\cos\alpha>\sin\alpha\) for every \(p\ge5\). Thus this ratio is strictly below its value \(\sin\alpha/\sqrt p\) whenever \(d>0\).

In the remaining middle interval \(\alpha\le\varepsilon\le\pi-\alpha\), one of the allowed zero phases lies within \(\alpha/2\), so \(\mu_p(\phi)\le\sin(\alpha/2)\), while \(\sqrt p+\cos\phi\ge\sqrt p-1\). For \(p\ge5\),
\[
\frac{\sin(\alpha/2)}{\sqrt p-1}
<\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sqrt p},
\]
which follows from \(2(1-p^{-1/2})\cos(\pi/(2p))>1\): its left side is increasing in \(p\ge5\) and at \(p=5\) already exceeds one. We have proved the global upper bound \(s_{\min}/\sqrt p\le\sin\alpha/\sqrt p\), with equality requiring \(r=\sqrt p\) and \(\phi=\pi/2\pmod\pi\).

**5. Attainment and no missing smaller branch.** At \(z=\pm i\sqrt p\), \(B=2p\), \(|z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}z=p\), and \(p+2\operatorname{Re}z=p\). The two unmixed nontrivial amplitude branches equal \(1/2\). The mixed branches have smallest normalized amplitude
\[
\frac{2\sqrt p\sin(\pi/p)}{2p}=\frac{\sin(\pi/p)}{\sqrt p},
\]
which is less than \(1/2\) for \(p\ge5\). Therefore \(s_{\min}=\sin(\pi/p)>0\), all Weyl coefficients are nonzero, and exactly the two indicated parameters attain the sharp condition number. Injective lifting implies phase retrieval because equality of intensity data for two signals would imply equality of their rank-one projectors.

Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and NumPy. ## Relationship to prior work
Bojarovska–Flinth (2015, Theorem 2.3) already establishes the **general** Gabor-lifting singular-value/ambiguity relation; that general relation is **not** claimed as new. Feichtinger–Kozek–Luef (2007) develops finite Weyl spreading calculus. The contribution here is the explicit constrained **complex-parameter minimax**, its unique optimizers, and the exact sharp decay barrier for the natural one-impulse modification of a flat window. Prior positive-real-spike optimizations cover a proper subfamily and cannot imply the global complex-phase optimum: in particular, their best asymptotic condition number is twice the value obtained here. Li (2026) demonstrates much better normalized ambiguity margins for other, less restricted Gabor windows, underscoring that our barrier is particular to this two-level family and not globally optimal.

## Limitations
The minimax quantifies **only** \(g_z=\mathbf1+z\delta_0\) at prime lengths \(p\ge5\); it does not address arbitrary window design, general composite lengths, missing intensity measurements, full spark of the underlying vectors, or nonlinear robustness constants. The older general ambiguity diagonalization is used as a known ingredient. Historical priority for this exact complex-impulse minimax is not independently certified. A separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
