# Optimal \(m+1\)-cell polygonal generators for quasiperiodic–expanding toral maps

## Finding

For every irrational real number \(\alpha\) and every integer \(m\ge 2\), consider the measure-preserving affine map on the two-torus
\[
F_{\alpha,m}(\theta,y)=(\theta+\alpha,\,my)\pmod 1
\]
with Haar (Lebesgue) probability measure \(\lambda\). Let \(g(F)\) be the smallest cardinality of a finite measurable one-sided generating partition, and let \(g_{\mathrm{poly}}(F)\) be the analogous minimum with pieces bounded by finitely many line segments on a square fundamental domain. Then
\[
g(F_{\alpha,m})=g_{\mathrm{poly}}(F_{\alpha,m})=m+1.
\]

There is a flexible explicit construction. Given any Borel-measurable \(h:[0,1)\to[0,1]\) for which
\[
\widehat h(1):=\int_0^1h(t)e^{-2\pi it}\,dt\ne0,
\]
define a partition of the fundamental square by
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_\star&=\{(\theta,y):0\le y<h(\theta)/m\},\\
P_0&=\{(\theta,y):h(\theta)/m\le y<1/m\},\\
P_j&=\{(\theta,y):j/m\le y<(j+1)/m\},\quad 1\le j<m.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are \(m+1\) cells and they generate one-sidedly modulo \(\lambda\). In particular, \(h(t)=t\) gives a polygonal partition with a single slanted internal edge.

## Assumptions and scope

All torus coordinates are represented in \([0,1)\). The parameter \(\alpha\) is fixed, known and irrational, and \(m\) is an arbitrary integer at least two. Boundary choices are immaterial modulo Haar measure. A finite one-sided generator means that the sigma-algebra generated by the nonnegative inverse images of the partition is the completed Borel sigma-algebra, equivalently that the one-sided label itinerary determines almost every initial point. This is a measure-theoretic result; no topological pointwise generation claim is made. The general split profile need only be Borel; geometric regularity is asserted for the explicit profile \(h(t)=t\).

## Proof

**1. The product system is ergodic.** The circle transformation \(\theta\mapsto\theta+\alpha\) is ergodic for irrational \(\alpha\), and \(y\mapsto my\pmod1\) is mixing for Haar measure (its base-\(m\) digit process is the uniform one-sided Bernoulli shift). The product of an ergodic system and a mixing system is ergodic. Hence Birkhoff's theorem applies to integrable observables of \(F_{\alpha,m}\).

**2. Recover the expanding coordinate.** Merge labels \(\star\) and \(0\) into digit \(0\), and retain label \(j\) as digit \(j\) for \(1\le j<m\). The resulting itinerary is precisely the base-\(m\) digit sequence \(d_0,d_1,\ldots\) of \(y\), so for Haar-almost every \(y\)
\[
y=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_n}{m^{n+1}}.
\]
Points with ambiguous base expansions form a null set.

**3. Recover the irrational rotation phase.** Put \(E=P_\star\) and \(\varphi(\theta,y)=e^{-2\pi i\theta}\mathbf 1_E(\theta,y)\). Fubini's theorem gives the exact integral
\[
\int_{\mathbb T^2}\varphi\,d\lambda
=\frac1m\int_0^1h(t)e^{-2\pi it}\,dt
=\frac{\widehat h(1)}m\ne0.
\]
Let \(\omega_n\) be the cell label at \(F_{\alpha,m}^{n}(\theta,y)\), and let \(s_n=\mathbf 1_{\{\omega_n=\star\}}\). Since \(\theta_n=\theta+n\alpha\pmod1\),
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi i n\alpha}s_n
=e^{2\pi i\theta}\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\varphi(F_{\alpha,m}^{n}(\theta,y)).
\]
Birkhoff's ergodic theorem sends the rightmost average to \(\widehat h(1)/m\) for almost every initial point. Thus the labels recover
\[
e^{2\pi i\theta}
=\frac m{\widehat h(1)}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-2\pi i n\alpha}s_n.
\]
The phase determines \(\theta\) modulo one. Together with step 2 it proves one-sided generation. Taking \(h(t)=t\), direct integration by parts gives
\[
\widehat h(1)=\int_0^1t e^{-2\pi it}\,dt=-\frac1{2\pi i}=\frac i{2\pi}\ne0.
\]
All cells of this specialization have finitely many linear edges, proving \(g_{\mathrm{poly}}(F_{\alpha,m})\le m+1\).

**4. Sharp lower bound without boundary assumptions.** The entropy of the product equals the entropy of the expanding factor, \(h_\lambda(F_{\alpha,m})=\log m\), because the irrational rotation has zero entropy. For a generating partition into \(k\) cells, entropy is at most \(\log k\), hence \(k\ge m\). If \(k=m\), the entropy rate of its stationary label process is \(\log m\), forcing every finite block of labels to be uniformly distributed: for each block length \(n\), its entropy is at most \(n\log m\), while the entropy rate is \(\log m\). Therefore the label process is the uniform independent \(m\)-symbol process. Generation would make \(F_{\alpha,m}\) isomorphic to a one-sided Bernoulli shift and therefore mixing. But the nonconstant observable \(e^{2\pi i\theta}\) is a Koopman eigenfunction with unimodular eigenvalue \(e^{2\pi i\alpha}\ne1\), which rules out mixing. Hence \(k=m\) is impossible. Combining the lower bound with step 3 proves both asserted equalities.

## Verification

The proof relies only on a direct partition construction, ergodicity of a rotation times a mixing endomorphism, Birkhoff's theorem, the entropy inequality for a generating partition, and the explicit Fourier integral. All ranges and exceptional sets are stated. Symbolic integration independently simplifies \(\int_0^1t e^{-2\pi it}\,dt\) to \(i/(2\pi)\); this arithmetic check is not a substitute for the ergodic or entropy arguments. The one-sided decoder and the lower bound hold for every irrational \(\alpha\), without Diophantine assumptions.

## Relationship to prior work

Leuridan's 2026 paper studies generating partitions of linear toral group endomorphisms and boundary obstructions, with primary MSC \(37\mathrm{A}05\). The present maps are **affine** with an irrational translation, not the linear group endomorphisms in that paper; its results do not state the Fourier-visible split family or the optimal polygonal count here. Leuridan's earlier explicit generator for \([T,\mathrm{Id}]\) with irrational rotation treats a random-step skew product \((x,y)\mapsto(Sx,T^{x_0}y)\), which is one-sided Bernoulli, whereas the fixed direct product considered here retains a rotation eigenfunction and is not mixing. The classical finite-generator theorem concerns measurable existence rather than the present geometric construction or phase-recovery formula. A stationary first-coordinate variant does not imply the moving-phase recovery formula, because unweighted split frequencies erase the starting phase under irrational rotation.

## Limitations

Originality is assessed relative to the explicitly compared accessible literature and the recorded targeted searches, not established against every possible publication. A general affine-generator construction under another name might cover this result. The Fourier condition \(\widehat h(1)\ne0\) is sufficient, not necessary; no optimality is claimed for the class of split functions. The theorem concerns Haar-a.e. one-sided names rather than a continuous inverse or a finite-time reconstruction. It excludes rational rotations, variable expansion factors, and non-product skewing. The \(m+1\) minimum is sharp among finite measurable generators, not a statement about Markov partitions in an alternative sense.
