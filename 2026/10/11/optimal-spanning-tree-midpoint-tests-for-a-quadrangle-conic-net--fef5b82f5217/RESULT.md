# Optimal spanning-tree midpoint tests for a quadrangle conic net
## Finding
Let \\(Q\\) be a nondegenerate quadrangle with vertices \\(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\\) over a field \\(\\mathbb{{k}}\\) of characteristic different from \\(2\\). For a subset \\(S\\) of its six sides, let \\(G_S\\) be the graph on \\(\\{{1,2,3,4\\}}\\) whose edge \\(ij\\) records the tested side \\(L_{{ij}}\\in S\\).

Then \\(S\\) is a uniform midpoint-bisection test for membership in the quadrangle net exactly when \\(G_S\\) is connected: every conic \\(C=[H]\\) for which every side in \\(S\\) bisects \\(C\\) at its side midpoint belongs to \\(\\mathcal N(Q)\\) if and only if \\(G_S\\) is connected.

In particular, for every conic \\(C=[H]\\),
\[
C\in\mathcal N(Q)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{{the three sides of any fixed spanning tree of }}K_4\text{{ bisect }}C\text{{ at their side midpoints}}.
\]
Thus three side tests are best possible, and the minimal sufficient side sets are exactly the \\(16\\) spanning trees of \\(K_4\\).

There is also a six-incidence entanglement certificate. Let \\(Q_1\\) be nondegenerate and \\(Q_2\\) any quadrangle. Fix a spanning tree \\(T\\) among the sides of \\(Q_1\\), and fix two distinct pairs of opposite sides \\(\\mathsf A,\\mathsf B\\) of \\(Q_2\\). Then \\(Q_1\\) and \\(Q_2\\) have the same net if and only if every side in \\(T\\) bisects both line-pair conics \\([F_{{\\mathsf A}}]\\) and \\([F_{{\\mathsf B}}]\\) at that side's midpoint.

## Assumptions and scope
A nondegenerate quadrangle means four distinct vertices with no three collinear. Conics are understood in the affine-projective convention of Olberding--Walker: \\(C=[H]\\) for a nonzero polynomial \\(H\\in\\mathbb{{k}}[X,Y]\\) of degree at most \\(2\\), with degree-two homogenization. The net is
\[
\mathcal N(Q)=\mathbb P\!\left(\mathcal V(Q)\oplus\mathbb k\cdot 1\right),
\]
where \\(\\mathcal V(Q)\\) is the two-dimensional pencil space generated by two distinct pairs of opposite sides.

The connected-graph characterization is asserted only for nondegenerate quadrangles. Degenerate quadrangles can require tangency data at repeated vertices, so the graph-of-vertex-values reduction used here is not valid without modification.

## Proof
Let \\(P_2=\\mathbb{{k}}[X,Y]_{{\\le 2}}\\), and define the evaluation map
\[
E:P_2\longrightarrow\mathbb k^4,
\qquad
E(H)=\bigl(H(v_1),H(v_2),H(v_3),H(v_4)\bigr).
\]
For a side \\(L_{{ij}}\\), put \\(w=v_j-v_i\\) and \\(m_{{ij}}=(v_i+v_j)/2\\). If
\[
\phi(t)=H(v_i+t w),
\]
then \\(\\phi\\) has degree at most \\(2\\). Hence
\[
D_wH(m_{{ij}})=\phi'(1/2)=\phi(1)-\phi(0)=H(v_j)-H(v_i).
\]
Olberding--Walker's algebraic bisection criterion says that \\(L_{{ij}}\\) bisects \\([H]\\) at \\(m_{{ij}}\\) exactly when \\(D_wH(m_{{ij}})=0\\). Therefore a tested side imposes exactly the equality
\[
H(v_i)=H(v_j).
\]

Because \\(Q\\) is nondegenerate, a polynomial of degree at most \\(1\\) that vanishes at all four vertices is zero. Their pencil theorem says that every quadratic vanishing at all four vertices lies in \\(\\mathcal V(Q)\\). Consequently
\[
\ker E=\mathcal V(Q),
\qquad
\dim\ker E=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{{rank}}E=4.
\]
Let
\[
U=\mathcal V(Q)\oplus\mathbb k\cdot1.
\]
The source's net criterion for a nondegenerate quadrangle is exactly
\[
U=E^{-1}(\Delta),
\qquad
\Delta=\{(a,a,a,a):a\in\mathbb k\}.
\]

Now let \\(c(G_S)\\) be the number of connected components of \\(G_S\\), and let \\(D_S\\subset\\mathbb k^4\\) be the vectors constant on every connected component. The midpoint identity above gives
\[
W_S:=\{H\in P_2:\text{all tests in }S\text{ pass}\}=E^{-1}(D_S).
\]
Since \\(E\\) is surjective,
\[
\dim W_S=2+c(G_S).
\]
If \\(G_S\\) is connected, then \\(D_S=\\Delta\\), so \\(W_S=U\\): every conic passing the selected tests is in the net.

If \\(G_S\\) is disconnected, then \\(D_S\\supsetneq\\Delta\\), hence \\(W_S\\supsetneq U\\). Choose \\(H\\in W_S\\setminus U\\). If this \\(H\\) has degree at most \\(1\\), add any nonzero \\(F\\in\\mathcal V(Q)\\); the polynomial \\(H+F\\) still lies in \\(W_S\\setminus U\\) and has degree \\(2\\), because every nonzero member of \\(\\mathcal V(Q)\\) has degree \\(2\\). Thus there is a genuine degree-two conic that passes every test in \\(S\\) but is not in the net. This proves the if-and-only-if characterization.

A connected graph on four vertices needs at least three edges, and the connected three-edge subgraphs of \\(K_4\\) are exactly its spanning trees. Cayley's formula gives \\(4^{{4-2}}=16\\) such trees. Hence three is the optimal number of side tests.

For the entanglement certificate, let
\[
U_i=\mathcal V(Q_i)\oplus\mathbb k\cdot1.
\]
If the six stated bisections hold, the spanning-tree criterion gives \\(F_{{\\mathsf A}},F_{{\\mathsf B}}\\in U_1\\). Since the two distinct opposite-side polynomials form a basis of \\(\\mathcal V(Q_2)\\),
\[
U_2=\operatorname{{span}}\{1,F_{{\\mathsf A}},F_{{\\mathsf B}}\}\subseteq U_1.
\]
Both spaces have dimension \\(3\\), so \\(U_1=U_2\\), which is entanglement. The converse follows because equal nets make every side of \\(Q_1\\) bisect every conic in the common net at the side midpoint.

## Verification
The proof was checked at three levels. First, the midpoint identity \\(D_{{v_j-v_i}}H((v_i+v_j)/2)=H(v_j)-H(v_i)\\) was expanded directly for a general quadratic. Second, the dimension argument was checked against the source's statements that \\(\\mathcal V(Q)\\) has dimension \\(2\\), that its nonzero members have degree \\(2\\), and that net membership for a nondegenerate quadrangle is equivalent to equality of the four vertex values. Third, the accompanying exact-arithmetic script enumerates all \\(64\\) side subsets for one nondegenerate rational quadrangle and confirms that the sufficient subsets are exactly the connected side graphs, with exactly \\(16\\) minimal three-edge sets.

The script is only a finite sanity check. The theorem for all nondegenerate quadrangles and all fields of characteristic different from \\(2\\) is proved by the evaluation-map argument above, not inferred from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Olberding and Walker prove that a conic is in the net of a quadrangle exactly when all six sides bisect it at their side midpoints, and they explicitly sharpen this to a test on the four sides contained in two distinct pairs of opposite sides. Their Corollary 3.4 identifies net membership through common vertex values, while Proposition 4.1 identifies bisection with a directional-derivative condition. The connected-side-graph reduction above combines those two mechanisms in the nondegenerate case and shows that the four-side test is not minimal: any spanning tree of three sides suffices, and no disconnected side set can suffice uniformly.

The earlier Olberding--Walker work on bisector fields of quadrilaterals develops the bisection geometry and the relationship between quadrilaterals, conic pencils, and bisector fields, but the inspected statements do not give the connected-subgraph membership criterion or the optimal three-side test. Targeted searches for spanning-tree, connected-side-graph, and three-midpoint formulations found no covering statement.

## Limitations
The theorem intentionally excludes degenerate quadrangles, where repeated vertices introduce tangent conditions that are invisible to a simple graph of equality constraints. It does not claim that six scalar bisection incidences are globally minimal for deciding entanglement; the optimality statement concerns membership testing for one conic against one nondegenerate quadrangle. Literature searches were targeted rather than exhaustive, so a differently phrased historical equivalent remains a residual bibliographic risk.
