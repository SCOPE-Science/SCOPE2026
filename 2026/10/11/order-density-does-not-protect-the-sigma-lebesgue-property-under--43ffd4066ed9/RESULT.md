# Order density does not protect the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property under completion
## Finding
Let \(A\) be an uncountable set with the discrete topology, let \(A_\infty=A\cup\{{\infty\}}\) be its one-point compactification, fix a countably infinite set
\[
B=\{{b_j:j\in\mathbb N\}}\subset A,
\]
and write \(A'=A\setminus B\). On \(X=C(A_\infty)\), consider the locally convex-solid topology generated by
\[
q(f)=\sup_j|f(b_j)|,
\qquad
q_a(f)=|f(a)|\quad(a\in A').
\]
The focal source proves that \(X\) is \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue and that its topological completion is
\[
\widehat X=c\times\mathbb R^{{A'}},
\]
with the product of the supremum-norm topology on \(c\) and the pointwise topology on \(\mathbb R^{{A'}}\), via
\[
J(f)=\bigl((f(b_j))_{{j\in\mathbb N}},(f(a))_{{a\in A'}}\bigr).
\]
The canonical image has two additional order-theoretic properties not stated in the focal theorem:

1. \(J(X)\) is order dense in \(\widehat X\), and therefore it is a regular sublattice of \(\widehat X\).
2. \(J(X)\) is not super order dense in \(\widehat X\).

Consequently, ordinary order density and regularity of the canonical image do not protect the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property under nonmetrizable topological completion. The same counterexample pinpoints a genuine sequential gap: the canonical image is order dense but fails the stronger countable approximation property of super order density.

## Assumptions and scope
Order density is used in the standard vector-lattice sense: a sublattice \(Y\subset Z\) is order dense if every \(0<z\in Z\) dominates some \(0<y\in Y\). Super order density means that every \(0<z\in Z\) is the supremum of an increasing sequence from \(Y_+\) lying below \(z\).

The topology, completion, and the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue assertions are exactly those of Section 2 of arXiv:2609.29498v1. The new claim concerns the order position of that specific canonical image inside its completion. It does not assert that super order density would, by itself, force preservation of the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property for arbitrary nonmetrizable topological completions.

## Proof
First prove order density. Let
\[
y=(u,v)\in\bigl(c\times\mathbb R^{{A'}}\bigr)_+
\]
be nonzero.

If \(v(a)>0\) for some \(a\in A'\), choose \(0<t\le v(a)\). The singleton \(\{{a\}}\) is clopen in \(A_\infty\), so
\[
f=t\,\chi_{{\{{a\}}}}\in C(A_\infty)_+.
\]
Since \(a\notin B\),
\[
J(f)=\bigl(0,t e_a\bigr),
\]
where \(e_a\) is the coordinate vector in \(\mathbb R^{{A'}}\). Hence
\[
0<J(f)\le y.
\]

If instead \(v=0\), then \(u\in c_+\) is nonzero, so \(u_j>0\) for some \(j\). Choose \(0<t\le u_j\) and set
\[
f=t\,\chi_{{\{{b_j\}}}}.
\]
Again \(f\in C(A_\infty)_+\), and now
\[
J(f)=\bigl(t e_j,0\bigr),
\qquad
0<J(f)\le y.
\]
Thus \(J(X)\) is order dense. The general vector-lattice fact that every order-dense sublattice is regular then gives regularity; this implication is also recalled explicitly in Section 6 of the focal source.

To show that the image is not super order dense, consider
\[
y_0=\bigl(0,\mathbf 1_{{A'}}\bigr)\in\bigl(c\times\mathbb R^{{A'}}\bigr)_+.
\]
Suppose \(0\le J(f)\le y_0\). The first coordinate of \(J(f)\) is then zero, so \(f(b_j)=0\) for every \(j\). Since \(f(b_j)\to f(\infty)\), one has \(f(\infty)=0\). Continuity at \(\infty\) implies that for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
\bigl\{{a\in A: f(a)\ge 1/m}\bigr\}
\]
is finite. Therefore the positive support of \(f\) in \(A'\) is countable.

Now let \((f_n)\subset X_+\) be any sequence with \(0\le J(f_n)\le y_0\). The union of the supports of the second coordinates of the \(J(f_n)\) is countable. Because \(A'\) is uncountable, choose \(a_0\in A'\) outside that union. At coordinate \(a_0\), every \(J(f_n)\) is zero, whereas \(y_0(a_0)=1\). Hence no increasing sequence from \(J(X)_+\) can have supremum \(y_0\). Thus \(J(X)\) is not super order dense.

Finally, the focal source proves that \(X\) is \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue and that \(\widehat X\) is not: in the \(c\)-factor, the sequence whose first \(n\) coordinates are zero and whose remaining coordinates are one decreases to zero in order while retaining supremum norm one. Combining that theorem with the order-density argument above proves the stated separation.

## Verification
The proof uses only the explicit completion model and canonical embedding from the focal source. The two order-density cases were checked directly against the coordinate order on \(c\times\mathbb R^{{A'}}\). The obstruction to super order density uses only uncountability of \(A'\), the convergence \(f(b_j)\to f(\infty)\), and the defining continuity condition at the point at infinity.

The critical source statements were inspected in the full text: Lemma 2.3 establishes the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property of \(X\); Lemma 2.4 identifies the completion and the map \(J\); Theorem 2.5 records failure of \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue in the completion; and Section 6 recalls that order density of a sublattice implies regularity. No finite experiment, numerical approximation, or unproved computational step is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Muñoz-Lahoz, Taylor, and Tradacete answer Aliprantis's first completion question by proving that the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property can fail after nonmetrizable completion. Their Section 2 proves topological density of \(J(C(A_\infty)_+)\) in the positive cone of the completion, but does not state that the canonical image is order dense or classify its failure of super order density. Later in the same paper, order density and regularity are treated as separate completion properties, and the general implication from order density to regularity is recalled.

Aliprantis's metrizable theorem shows that metrizability restores preservation of the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property and, separately, ties the sequential property \((A,0)\) to order density and regularity of the canonical image. The present observation shows that merely imposing the latter two order properties does not recover the missing preservation theorem once metrizability is removed.

Super order density is a standard stronger countable approximation notion. Recent work on countability conditions in locally solid convergence spaces shows that it is precisely the form of density that transports \(\sigma\)-order convergence under suitable hypotheses, while classical Fatou-completion results also use super order density in positive sequential-completeness theorems. Those results do not cover this nonmetrizable topological completion, but they explain why the distinction between ordinary and super order density is mathematically natural here.

## Limitations
The result concerns one explicit completion model and does not establish a general preservation theorem under super order density. The non-super-order-density witness uses the uncountable coordinate factor, so it does not address what happens under additional countability assumptions. The originality comparison is literature-bounded, and an equivalent observation could appear under older terminology for sequential order density.
