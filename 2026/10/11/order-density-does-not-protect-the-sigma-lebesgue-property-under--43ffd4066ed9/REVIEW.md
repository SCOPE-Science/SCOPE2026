# Review of Order density does not protect the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property under completion

## Correctness
PASS. The completion and canonical embedding are taken from the focal full text. For every nonzero positive element of the completion, a positive singleton indicator at a coordinate where that element is positive gives a nonzero image element below it, proving order density. For the witness \(y_0=(0,\mathbf 1_{{A'}})\), every image element below \(y_0\) has countable positive support in \(A'\); a countable union of such supports cannot cover uncountable \(A'\), so no increasing sequence from the image can have supremum \(y_0\). The source independently supplies the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue and completion-failure assertions.

## Originality
PASS. Searches by the focal arXiv identifier, order-density and regularity aliases, super-order-density terminology, and completion/\(\sigma\)-Lebesgue implications located no statement that the Section 2 canonical image is order dense or that it fails super order density. The focal paper treats the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue counterexample in Section 2 and order density/regularity in Section 6, but does not combine them for this example. Broader literature on super order density concerns transfer of sequential order convergence or special Fatou completions and does not imply this nonmetrizable topological-completion statement.

## Value
PASS. The result removes a natural possible repair of the focal counterexample: requiring the canonical image to be order dense or regular still does not preserve the \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue property. The simultaneous failure of super order density identifies a mathematically motivated sequential boundary, directly matching the sequential nature of the property rather than adding an arbitrary side condition.

Closest literature and limitation: the focal preprint gives the completion and the failure of \(\sigma\)-Lebesgue; Aliprantis gives the metrizable preservation theorem; recent countability work explains why super order density is the relevant countable strengthening. None of the inspected sources states the new order-position classification. The main residual risk is equivalent older terminology for sequential order density.
