# Review: Parameter-free stationary activity calibration in the quadratic-perturbed Sprott E family

## Correctness
PASS. The proof derives all three conditional expectations directly from the invariant-measure generator identity. The activity law and fluctuation decompositions follow by exact conditional orthogonality, not by numerical averaging. Equality rigidity uses zero variance plus invariance of the support to force the unique equilibrium. The standalone symbolic check confirms the equilibrium and the algebraic channel identities.

## Originality
PASS. Searches covered the exact vector field, Sprott E aliases, invariant-measure language, time-average/moment language, conditional regressions, and the derivative-activity form. The defining 2013 full text and the 2014 same-system full text were inspected at the system-definition and stated-results level; they analyze chaotic behavior, bifurcation, dynamics at infinity, and synchronization rather than the accepted stationary-measure theorem. The closest indexed stationary-balance results concern different vector fields and do not imply the claim.

Residual risk: an unindexed or differently worded prior result could exist. This is a literature-search limitation, not a mathematical gap in the proof.

## Value
PASS. The exact law \(\mathbb E[y]=1/16+\mathbb E[\dot z^2]/16\) turns a natural activity observable into a parameter-free stationary calibration across the full quadratic perturbation family, while the equality case separates the equilibrium from every compact recurrent non-equilibrium state. The conditional laws and variance decompositions provide additional exact diagnostics for computations of periodic or chaotic regimes.

## Closest literature and limitations
Sprott (1994) supplies the base case E. Wei and Wang (2013) define the three-parameter quadratic perturbation and its equilibrium. Wang, Sun, Wei, and Xi (2014) analyze Hopf bifurcation, dynamics at infinity, and robust synchronization for the same family. None of the inspected sources states the conditional stationary laws or derivative-energy calibration. The theorem does not establish existence, ergodicity, mixing, or attraction of non-equilibrium compact states.
