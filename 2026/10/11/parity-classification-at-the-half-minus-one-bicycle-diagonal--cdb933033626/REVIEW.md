# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof was reconstructed from definitions and the imported local isosceles-trapezoid lemma. The decisive chain is: paired trapezoids force \(m\)-periodic exterior turns; periodic turns force opposite edge vectors and central symmetry; the side/diagonal parallelogram identity forces \(2\)-periodic squared radii; odd \(m\) collapses the two radii and equal chords force a regular polygon. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text comparison with Tabachnikov's foundational paper, Connelly--Csikós's first-order classification, and Tabachnikov--Tsukerman's later discrete-bicycle paper found neighboring but non-implicative results. The nearest own prior result is the single case \((10,4)\); it does not imply the all-\(m\) theorem. The principal residual risk is an unindexed elementary note. Csikós's 2007 article was available only at abstract level for this source review, so no whole-document noncoverage claim is made from that source alone.

## Value
PASS. The family \(k=n/2-1\) is a canonical arithmetic slice of the original classification problem. The result provides a global structural mechanism and a complete parity boundary, not merely another isolated polygon order.

## Closest literature and limitations
Tabachnikov's Lemma 7.1 is the critical published local input. His Theorem 10 already gives the even-\(m\) flexible side. Connelly--Csikós concerns infinitesimal flexibility of the regular polygon, while Tabachnikov--Tsukerman's two-circle theorem concerns \((4k,k)\), a distinct family. The present theorem assumes strict convexity and does not classify all flexible even-\(m\) solutions.
