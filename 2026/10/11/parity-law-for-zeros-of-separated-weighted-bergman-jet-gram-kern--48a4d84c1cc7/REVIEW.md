# Scientific review: parity law for separated jet Gram kernels

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is fully quantified for \(\alpha>-1\), \(p\ge0\), \(q-p=d\ge2\), and nonzero complex \(c\). The mixed-derivative identity follows from the explicitly normalized weighted half-plane Bergman kernel; normalization leaves a quadratic in \(t^d\). Strict gamma log-convexity proves \(B>A^2\), which makes the two quadratic roots distinct and avoids hidden degeneracies. Argument counting establishes all lower-half-plane roots, boundary cases, and their simplicity. A direct choice of \(z,w\) shows each lower scalar root is attained. Four high-precision integral comparisons and 560 root-count tests executed successfully; universal correctness rests on the analytic proof, not on the finite checks.

## Originality — PASS

The nearest original paper, Gehlawat–Jain–Sarkar, arXiv:2608.13193, gives a general unweighted differential-sequence series and treats single-support and factorial-support zero-free examples, but does not give the complete separated two-jet root count or weighted phase law. Bao–Guan–Sun, arXiv:2510.13144, studies related extremal differential-functional kernels without such a zero-count classification. Deng–Huang–Qian, arXiv:2009.02989, and Bonyo (2020) give base weighted Bergman formulas, not the root theorem. Searches for higher derivative Gram zeros, equivalent root polynomials, stronger weighted-jet coverage and tabulations disclosed no exact match among inspected sources. A yet-unindexed equivalent result remains possible; novelty is not inferred from unsuccessful search alone.

## Value — PASS

The structure of off-diagonal Gram-kernel zeros is central to the Lu Qi-Keng question for the new differential-functional kernels. This theorem gives the complete nonadjacent two-jet answer for infinitely many derivative pairs and continuously many weights and coefficients: it proves zero existence in every allowed case, classifies the number of distinct scalar zeros, and identifies a genuine parity-and-phase transition. The independence of weight and starting order makes this a structural classification, not a minor bound or unmotivated numerical example.

## Scientific limitations

The theorem counts distinct scalar values in the reciprocal-difference variable, not zeros of a fixed-point kernel slice. The adjacent-order case, more than two nonzero differential coefficients, and non-power weights are excluded. No statement of sharpness beyond the displayed exact count is made, and no independent replication or literature-exhaustiveness guarantee is claimed.
