# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The nearest-normal radial representation follows from the half-plane description of a regular polygon. Antipodal normals give the even law, while a half-step shift of opposite normals gives the odd law. Folding a uniform variable yields increasing quantile functions; a secant addition identity proves their rational transport. Direct integrations establish both exact moments, and a uniformly bounded Taylor remainder gives distributional and all-moment limits, with variance coefficients obtained from elementary uniform moments. These steps cover every integer \(n\ge3\) and radius \(R>0\). The included independent edge-intersection verifier executes successfully, although finite checks alone prove no infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Harutyunyan–Ohanyan (2009, DOI:10.1239/aap/1246886615) and Bäsel (2012, arXiv:1204.2707) derive regular-polygon chord laws for motion-invariant random lines \(dp\,d\phi\), not uniform directions of center-constrained lines. Their full texts, definitions and main formulas were inspected. The resulting offset-integrated chord law does not determine the angular law at the measure-zero offset \(p=0\). Neither source states the odd-to-double-side monotone rational transport or the parity-dependent scaled limit laws. Closest publicly indexed geometry results concern polygon symmetrizations, distances among polygon vertices, or extremal bisections, which do not imply the claim. An earlier treatment under alternative terminology or among currently unindexed sources remains possible; novelty is not inferred merely from negative searching.

## Value — PASS
Center-passing chords are canonical measurements of a convex body's radial and central-section geometry. The exact quantiles, full odd-to-even transport and universal two-scale fluctuation profiles provide more than elementary mean-value evaluation: they identify a structural parity defect in how regular polygons approximate the circle under isotropic radial section sampling. The exact factor-sixteen variance gap and the explicit coupling give usable quantitative benchmarks in central tomography and geometrical sampling without introducing arbitrary parameters.

## Literature and limits
The full primary papers above constitute the closest examined literature. The theorem does not extend to offset-random lines or nonregular polygons, and no independent audit has been performed.
