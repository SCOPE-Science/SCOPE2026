# Scientific review: parity-sharp simplex-normal plank covering

**Correctness — PASS.** For a centered regular unit simplex, the analysis map is an isometry up to factor \((d+1)/d\) onto the zero-sum subspace. Normalizing by the least absolute projection reduces the covering radius to minimizing the squared norm of a zero-sum vector whose coordinates all have magnitude at least one. For each possible positive/negative sign count, the sharp Cauchy--Schwarz lower bound is reached only by constant magnitudes within each sign group. The most balanced split yields exactly \(1/d\) or \(1/(d+2)\) for the squared covering threshold. Both the unit-ball-to-sphere reduction and all equality lines follow explicitly. The standard-library verifier tests dimensions \(2\le d\le50\) with rational arithmetic, as well as finite random samples; these tests are not used to extrapolate the all-dimensional theorem.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the documented residual literature risks.** Akopyan–Polyanskii (arXiv:2609.36163v1) state Ball's general total-relative-width lower bound; for \(d+1\) equal unit-ball planks that gives only \(t\ge1/(d+1)\), not the precise higher-dimensional fixed-frame threshold. Jiang–Polyanskii (arXiv:1703.10550) establish minimum total spherical-zone width, only yielding \(t\ge\sin(\pi/(2(d+1)))\), again without this threshold or equality classification. The complete inspected text of the Verreault survey (DOI:10.1112/blms.70230) summarizes those general facts; the Bezdek–Fodor–Vígh–Zarnócz article (DOI:10.1556/012.2024.04319) concerns zone multiplicity. The 2024 simplex-dot-product discussion at Mathematics Stack Exchange question 4894174 minimizes the **maximum** absolute projection. Its balanced equimodular odd-dimensional construction, combined with the tight-frame norm identity, *does* imply the odd-dimensional value and equality directions here; these are treated as known. The universal 2017 zone lower bound is attained by the \(d=2\) configuration, another known special case. Neither source supplies the unequal two-level extremizers and exact width for even \(d\ge4\). Record-index searches returned geometrically adjacent but non-covering results. The 2026 motivating note itself could not be read in full, and earlier unindexed work remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The exact threshold answers a natural constrained covering problem in Euclidean/spherical geometry for the canonical minimal redundant tight frame. It supplies an all-dimensional parity law, a closed expression for the zone-width obstruction, and the complete set and count of worst directions. These results give a geometric calibration for fixed-normal coverings which the general total-width theorem does not provide. The complete classification and parity transition, rather than an arbitrary numerical example, provide the mathematical reason to record the result.

Novelty of the joint parity statement rests on even \(d\ge4\), not on the already-covered odd and planar subcases. The theorem does not make a claim about globally optimal plank directions, noncentral translations, or independent verification. An inaccessible plausible source is an originality risk rather than evidence for uniqueness.
