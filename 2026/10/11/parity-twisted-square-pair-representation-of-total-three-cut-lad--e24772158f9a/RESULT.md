# Parity-twisted square-pair representation of total three-cut ladder homology

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), the rational top reduced homology of the total three-cut complex of the \(n\)-rung ladder carries a natural representation of the full graph automorphism group \(C_2\times C_2\). As a rational representation, it is the permutation representation on unordered pairs of the \(n-1\) elementary ladder squares, twisted by the sign of the row exchange when \(n\) is odd:
\[
\widetilde H_{2n-6}(\Delta^t_3(L_n);\mathbb Q)
\cong \varepsilon_S^{\,n\bmod2}\otimes\mathbb Q\!\left[\binom{F_n}{2}\right].
\]
Here the elementary squares \(F_n\) are fixed by row exchange \(S\) and reversed by column reflection \(R\), and \(\varepsilon_S(S)=-1\), \(\varepsilon_S(R)=1\). Writing
\[
B_n=\binom{n-1}{2},\qquad h_n=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
the four character values in the order \((1,S,R,SR)\) are exactly
\[
\boxed{\big(B_n,\ (-1)^n B_n,\ h_n,\ (-1)^n h_n\big).}
\]
In particular the row-exchange operator is the scalar \((-1)^n\), while the multiplicities of the positive and negative eigenvalues of column reversal are respectively \((B_n+h_n)/2\) and \((B_n-h_n)/2\). The rational representation isomorphism is abstract, not asserted canonical.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(L_n=P_n\square K_2\) have vertices \((a,i)\), for \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(0\le i<n\), with horizontal edges joining consecutive positions in a fixed row and vertical edges joining the two row vertices at each position. Let \(X_n=\Delta^t_3(L_n)\) be the simplicial complex generated by complements of three-element independent sets in \(L_n\). A subset \(\sigma\) is a face exactly when its complement contains an independent triple. The group acts by \(S(a,i)=(1-a,i)\) and \(R(a,i)=(a,n-1-i)\); these commute and generate \(\operatorname{Aut}(L_n)\) for \(n\ge3\). The set \(F_n\) is the \(n-1\) ordinary four-cycle faces between successive rungs.

The homotopy equivalence \(X_n\simeq\bigvee^{B_n}S^{2n-6}\) is the result of Chandrakar, Hazra, Rout and Singh [2, Theorem 1.1/Theorem 3.3]. Its consequence is that \(\widetilde H_j(X_n;\mathbb Q)\) vanishes for \(j\ne2n-6\), and is a \(B_n\)-dimensional rational vector space in that degree. This paper's nonequivariant homotopy theorem is **not** claimed as new. The new claim concerns the complete action on that vector space.

## Proof
**1. An equivariant complement formula.** Fix \(g\in\{1,S,R,SR\}\). Let \(m_g\) be the number of cycles of \(g\) acting on the \(2n\) graph vertices; let \(o_g(T)\) denote the number of complete vertex cycles that form a \(g\)-invariant subset \(T\). Call \(T\) *bad* when \(\alpha(L_n[T])\le2\), where \(\alpha\) is the independence number. Since \(L_n\) is bipartite, every set of at least five vertices contains an independent triple. Thus every bad set has at most four vertices. We claim
\[
\boxed{\chi_n(g)=(-1)^{m_g}\sum_{\substack{gT=T\\\alpha(L_n[T])\le2}}(-1)^{o_g(T)}.}\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the trace of \(g\) on an oriented simplex \(\sigma\) fixed as a set is the sign of the permutation induced on its vertices, namely \((-1)^{|\sigma|-o_g(\sigma)}\); its contribution to the reduced alternating simplicial trace is therefore \((-1)^{o_g(\sigma)+1}\). The reduced Hopf trace equals the top-homology character because \(2n-6\) is even and all other reduced rational homology vanishes. Taking complements \(T=V\setminus\sigma\) changes orbit-count parity by \(m_g\). The sum over all invariant complements vanishes, since it is \((1-1)^{m_g}=0\). Removing the complements with no independent triple leaves equation (1). The empty simplex is included in the reduced chain complex, making the sign consistent also at \(n=3\), when the relevant homology is reduced degree zero.

**2. Identity and row exchange.** A bad set of three vertices is exactly a triple containing an edge. The ladder has \(3n-2\) edges, is triangle-free, and has \(6n-8\) two-edge paths. By inclusion-exclusion the number of bad triples is
\[
(3n-2)(2n-2)-(6n-8)=6n^2-16n+12.
\]
A bad four-set is exactly a set supporting a perfect matching. Counting unordered pairs of disjoint edges and identifying the unique double-counting on a four-cycle gives
\[
\binom{3n-2}{2}-(6n-8)-(n-1)=\frac{9n^2-29n+24}{2}.
\]
Including the empty set, singletons, and pairs, the signed bad-set sum is
\[
1-2n+\binom{2n}{2}-(6n^2-16n+12)+\frac{9n^2-29n+24}{2}=B_n.
\]
Thus \(\chi_n(1)=B_n\). For row exchange, the \(n\) vertex cycles are the vertical rungs. The invariant bad sets are exactly the empty set, the \(n\) individual rungs, and all pairs of rungs: any union of three rungs has an independent triple in the same row. Their signed sum is \(1-n+\binom n2=B_n\); equation (1) gives \(\chi_n(S)=(-1)^nB_n\).

**3. Column reversal.** First take \(n=2h\) even. The \(m_R=2h\) orbits all have size two. At most two orbits can form a bad invariant set. Two selected orbits admit a perfect matching exactly if they are successive mirrored column-pairs in the same row (\(2(h-1)\) choices), or the mirrored pair in the opposite row (\(h\) choices). These cases are disjoint, giving \(3h-2\) bad two-orbit selections. Equation (1) yields
\[
\chi_n(R)=1-2h+(3h-2)=h-1.
\]
If \(n=2h+1\) is odd, reversal has \(2h\) size-two cycles and two fixed central vertices, hence \(m_R=2h+2\) even. The signed contributions of invariant bad sets are: empty \(+1\), one central vertex \(-2\), both central vertices \(+1\), one size-two orbit \(-2h\), one size-two orbit with one central vertex \(+2\) (only the orbit adjacent to the central vertex in its row works), and two size-two orbits \(+(3h-2)\). No other invariant set is bad, notably both central vertices with any size-two orbit have no perfect matching. The sum is \(h\). Therefore \(\chi_n(R)=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor=h_n\) for both parities.

**4. Simultaneous row exchange and reversal.** Every \(SR\)-orbit has size two, labelled \(i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) as \(\{(0,i),(1,n-1-i)\}\). Two distinct orbits produce a bad invariant four-set exactly when their labels satisfy \(|i-j|=1\) or \(i+j=n-1\), corresponding to a pair of disjoint horizontal or vertical edges. For \(n=2h\) these conditions give \((n-1)+h-1=3h-2\) distinct pairs (one pair satisfies both conditions); for \(n=2h+1\) they give \((n-1)+h=3h\) pairs (no overlap). The signed bad-set sum is therefore \(h-1\) when \(n\) is even and \(h\) when odd. Equation (1), whose outer sign is \((-1)^n\), proves \(\chi_n(SR)=(-1)^nh_n\).

**5. Representation identification.** The permutation character of the \(C_2\times C_2\) action on unordered pairs of distinct elements of \(F_n\) has values \((B_n,B_n,h_n,h_n)\): a reversal-invariant unordered pair of squares must be one of the \(h_n\) paired reflected squares. Tensoring with \(\varepsilon_S^{n\bmod2}\) gives precisely the four trace values just proved. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the four irreducible characters of \(C_2\times C_2\) are one-dimensional and characters determine representations; the asserted abstract isomorphism follows. The eigenspace multiplicities follow by applying the idempotents \((1\pm R)/2\). This completes the proof for every \(n\ge3\).

## Verification
The accompanying independent Python 3 script `artifacts/verify.py` implements two distinct finite trace calculations. For every \(3\le n\le40\) it enumerates all invariant vertex-orbit subsets of size at most four, checks the independence-number criterion directly, and verifies equation (1) against all four closed formulas. For \(3\le n\le7\), it independently builds **all faces of the simplicial complex** and evaluates the oriented simplicial-chain trace for all four automorphisms. For \(3\le n\le6\), it computes complete reduced mod-two boundary ranks and checks the predicted degree and Betti number. Running `python3 artifacts/verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`. These computations are independent finite corroboration, not a proof for arbitrarily large \(n\); the latter rests on the complete combinatorial orbit enumeration above together with [2].

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer and collaborators [1] defined and developed total cut complexes, including grid examples. Chandrakar, Hazra, Rout and Singh [2] proved the nonequivariant ladder formula \(\Delta^t_3(L_n)\simeq\bigvee^{B_n}S^{2n-6}\), which is used in the present Hopf-trace argument and is not new. Bayer and collaborators [3] studied equivariant homology for **ordinary** cut complexes of several graph families, rather than this total-three-cut ladder action. Carnero Bravo [4] studies Cartesian products of paths for the total **two**-cut case, not equivariant total three-cut. Related calculations for specific other grid graphs do not give the stated all-ladder character or parity twist. The contribution here is precisely the natural rational symmetry representation of the entire ladder family.

## Limitations
The result is over \(\mathbb Q\), and the displayed module isomorphism is abstract, not a canonical integral identification. The theorem does not claim an integral equivariant lattice, a stable equivariant homotopy equivalence, or a general formula for other cut parameters. The published nonequivariant homotopy concentration theorem [2] is essential. Literature comparison cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent statement. The finite verification does not certify the unbounded parameter range independently of the proof.
