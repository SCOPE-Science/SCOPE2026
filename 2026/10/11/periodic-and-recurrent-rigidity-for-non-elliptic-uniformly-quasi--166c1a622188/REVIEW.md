# Review of Periodic and recurrent rigidity for non-elliptic uniformly quasiregular ball maps

## Correctness

PASS. Fletcher's Theorem 1.2 is an exhaustive dichotomy. A finite periodic orbit cannot occur in the escaping branch. In the bounded branch, a cycle of prime period at least two cannot coexist with local-uniform convergence of the iterates to the unique fixed point, so it forces the uniformly quasiconformal homeomorphism and isometry branch.

After that branch is excluded, the bounded alternative has one globally attracting or super-attracting fixed point. Every periodic or recurrent point must equal it because the full forward orbit converges to it. In the escaping alternative every orbit converges to the boundary, so no interior orbit is relatively compact, periodic, or recurrent. The four stated diagnostics are therefore equivalent.

## Originality

PASS. The complete 2026 source was inspected at Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, Proposition 7.2, the stable-component theorem, the examples, and the open questions. Exact-text searches of the full paper found no use of “periodic point”, “recurrent”, or “cycle” for this ball-self-map consequence.

The 2020 Fletcher--Macclure paper was also inspected in full at its earlier Denjoy--Wolff theorem. That theorem requires properness and surjectivity in dimension three, whereas the new source removes those assumptions and works in every dimension. Broader periodic-point literature concerns globally defined or local uniformly quasiregular dynamics and does not imply the present all-orbit ball classification.

## Value

PASS. Periodic and recurrent points are basic dynamical invariants, and globally defined uniformly quasiregular maps can have abundant periodic dynamics. The result shows that the new ball Denjoy--Wolff theorem collapses that structure sharply outside the elliptic homeomorphism branch: one recurrent point already determines the entire global regime, and any nontrivial periodic cycle is a rigidity certificate for the isometric branch.

This is a natural structural corollary of the new theorem rather than an arbitrary specialization. It converts the source's metric/global convergence dichotomy into a simple orbit-theoretic diagnostic that is useful without knowing the adapted metric.
