The proof uses the exact alternatives of Fletcher's Denjoy--Wolff theorem.

If
\[
x
\]
has prime period
\[
q\ge2,
\]
then its orbit is finite and therefore relatively compact in
\[
\mathbb B^n.
\]
This excludes the escaping alternative.

In the bounded case, if
\[
f^m\to x_0
\]
locally uniformly, then
\[
f^m(x)\to x_0.
\]
A nonconstant periodic sequence cannot converge, so a cycle of length at least two excludes this branch. The source theorem therefore forces the uniformly quasiconformal homeomorphism branch, including the
\[
d
\]
-isometry and subsequential-identity conclusions.

Now exclude that homeomorphism branch. If the bounded case occurs, the source theorem gives one attracting or super-attracting fixed point
\[
x_0
\]
and local-uniform convergence of every orbit to
\[
x_0.
\]
Every periodic point and every recurrent point must therefore equal
\[
x_0.
\]

If the escaping case occurs, every orbit converges to a point of
\[
\partial\mathbb B^n.
\]
Thus no orbit is relatively compact in the open ball, and no periodic or recurrent interior point exists.

This proves the stated equivalence of fixed-point, periodic-point, recurrent-point, and relatively-compact-orbit diagnostics in the non-elliptic regime.

No computation or finite experiment is used. The unproved limits are exactly those stated in the result: the periodic and recurrent sets of the isometric homeomorphism branch are not classified, and no assertion is made for globally defined uniformly quasiregular maps.
