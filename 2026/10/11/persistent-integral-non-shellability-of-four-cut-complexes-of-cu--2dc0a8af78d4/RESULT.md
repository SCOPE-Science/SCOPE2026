# Persistent integral non-shellability of four-cut complexes of cubic paths

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge8\), the ordinary four-cut complex of the third power of a path satisfies
\[
\widetilde H_{n-6}(\Delta_4(P_n^3);\mathbb Z)\supseteq \mathbb Z
\]
as a **direct summand**. The complex is nonvoid, pure of dimension \(n-5\), and consequently is **not shellable**. The summand is integral and free; the result is not merely a statement modulo a prime.

## Assumptions and scope

The path-power graph \(P_n^3\) has vertex set \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and edges \(\{i,j\}\) whenever \(0<|i-j|\le3\). The ordinary \(4\)-cut complex \(\Delta=\Delta_4(P_n^3)\) is generated by the \((n-4)\)-element sets \([n]\setminus T\) for which the induced graph \(P_n^3[T]\) is disconnected and \(|T|=4\). Thus it is pure of dimension \(n-5\) whenever nonvoid. The set \(T=\{1,2,3,n\}\) is disconnected for all \(n\ge8\), so the complex is nonvoid. We use reduced integral simplicial (co)homology and the standard combinatorial Alexander dual on the **full** ambient vertex set \([n]\).

## Proof

**1. The Alexander dual.** Put \(K_n=\Delta^{\vee}\). Directly from the definition of the cut complex,
\[
K_n=\{S\subseteq[n]:\text{every four-element }T\subseteq S\text{ induces a connected graph in }P_n^3\}.
\]
For four distinct sorted vertices \(a<b<c<d\), the graph \(P_n^3[\{a,b,c,d\}]\) is connected **if and only if** the three successive gaps \(b-a,c-b,d-c\) are each at most \(3\). Indeed each larger gap disconnects the two sides, while successive short gaps give a spanning path. Every set of cardinality at most three belongs to \(K_n\).

**2. An integral three-cycle at eight vertices.** In \(K_8\), take the triangle-boundary cycle supported on vertices \(\{1,2,8\}\):
\[
u=[2,8]-[1,8]+[1,2].\]
On vertices \(\{3,4,5,6\}\) take the four-edge cycle
\[
v=[3,5]-[4,5]+[4,6]-[3,6].\]
Form the simplicial join chain \(z=u*v\), orienting every four-vertex simplex by ascending vertex order and using the corresponding permutation sign. This is a three-cycle because both \(u\) and \(v\) are one-cycles. Its support consists of exactly twelve tetrahedra. The gap criterion above verifies that **every** one of those tetrahedra is a face of \(K_8\). In other words, the join subcomplex \((\partial\Delta^2)*C_4\cong S^3\) is contained in \(K_8\).

Consider the distinguished oriented tetrahedron \(Q=[1,3,5,8]\). Its coefficient in \(z\) is \(-1\). It has **no coface of dimension four** in \(K_8\): the only possible extra vertices are \(2,4,6,7\), and the five-vertex extensions respectively contain disconnected four-sets
\[
\{1,2,3,8\},\quad\{1,3,4,8\},\quad\{1,5,6,8\},\quad\{1,5,7,8\}.
\]
Thus the integral degree-three cochain \(e_Q\) equal to \(1\) on \(Q\) and \(0\) on all other tetrahedra has zero coboundary. Since \(\langle e_Q,z\rangle=-1\), its class has infinite order and in fact spans a **split** infinite-cyclic summand of \(H^3(K_8;\mathbb Z)\): evaluation on \([z]\) maps this class to \(-1\in\mathbb Z\).

**3. Retraction onto the base complex.** For every \(n\ge8\), define \(r_n:[n]\to[8]\) by \(r_n(i)=\min(i,8)\). This vertex map is graph-Lipschitz: if \(|i-j|\le3\), then \(|r_n(i)-r_n(j)|\le3\). It induces a simplicial map \(K_n\to K_8\). Indeed, given a face \(S\in K_n\), choose any four distinct vertices of its image and one preimage of each in \(S\). The resulting four-vertex set induces a connected graph; its four distinct images remain connected because the vertex map preserves adjacency. The full image is therefore a face of \(K_8\). Inclusion \(K_8\hookrightarrow K_n\) is simplicial and its composite with \(r_n\) is the identity of \(K_8\).

Hence \(r_n^*:H^3(K_8;\mathbb Z)\hookrightarrow H^3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) is split injective, carrying the primitive class \([e_Q]\) into a direct summand \(\mathbb Z\) for every \(n\ge8\). Equivalently, it still evaluates to \(-1\) on the included integral cycle \(z\).

**4. Duality and shellability.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on \(n\) vertices yields
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{n-i-3}(K_n;\mathbb Z).
\]
Choosing \(i=n-6\) gives \(\widetilde H_{n-6}(\Delta;\mathbb Z)\cong H^3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\), which has the direct summand just constructed. Since \(\Delta\) is pure of dimension \(n-5\), this is nonzero homology **strictly below its top dimension**. A nonvoid pure shellable simplicial complex has reduced integral homology only in top dimension; therefore \(\Delta_4(P_n^3)\) is not shellable. \(\square\)

Run the standalone standard-library script `python3 artifacts/verify.py`. It constructs the oriented twelve-tetrahedron join cycle, checks its boundary is zero, checks each tetrahedron against the exact gap criterion, verifies that the chosen cochain has no possible degree-four coboundary, and enumerates all faces and the clamping map for each \(8\le n\le13\). Its success marker is `VERIFY_OK`. The finite enumeration is an independent stress test; the proof for all integers \(n\ge8\) is given above, including the integral splitting and the retraction argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Chauhan and Shukla (2026, arXiv:2603.23155v1) prove shellability of **three-cut** complexes of sufficiently large powers of **cycle** graphs. Bayer and collaborators establish that **all higher cut** complexes of **squared path** graphs are shellable in their range, as recalled and extended enumeratively by Zhang and Yang (2026, arXiv:2605.22808). The present four-cut complexes of **third-power paths** fall under neither result; the theorem establishes a persistent integral obstruction at the first path-power step beyond squared paths. The previously known existence of some nonshellable four-cut complexes of chordal graphs does not imply this uniform path-power statement or its explicit primitive homology class.

## Limitations

Only the existence of a direct integral summand is proved. The rank of the whole homology group, possible torsion, other homology degrees, complete homotopy types, and shellability of graph-power cut complexes outside the stated family are not determined. The reduction uses a specific eight-vertex subcomplex and is not a general formula for arbitrary path powers or cut sizes.
