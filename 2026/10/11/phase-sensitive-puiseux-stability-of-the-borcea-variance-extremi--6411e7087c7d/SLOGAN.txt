Near the regular-binomial equality model in Borcea's sharp variance inequality, every perturbation with nonzero linear coefficient opens a strictly positive \(t^{1/(n-1)}\) gap, rather than a first-order gap. Its leading coefficient has an exact phase-dependent range from \(\cos(\pi/(n-1))\) to \(\cos(\pi/n)\), and both endpoints are attained by explicit linear perturbations.
