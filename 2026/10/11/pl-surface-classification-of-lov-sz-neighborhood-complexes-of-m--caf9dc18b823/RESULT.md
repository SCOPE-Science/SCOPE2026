# PL surface classification of Lovász neighborhood complexes of Möbius and circular ladders

## Finding
Let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder on \(2n\) vertices, with \(n\ge2\), and let \(P_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the circular ladder, with \(n\ge3\). For the **open** Lovász neighborhood complex \(\mathcal N(G)\), the realizations have the following PL homeomorphism types. Write \(A=S^1\times[0,1]\) for the annulus and \(B\) for the Möbius band:

\[
|\mathcal N(M_{2n})|\cong_{\rm PL}
\begin{cases}
 S^2,&n=2,\\
 D^2\sqcup D^2,&n=3,\\
 A,&n\ge4\text{ even},\\
 B\sqcup B,&n\ge5\text{ odd};
\end{cases}
\]
\[
|\mathcal N(P_n)|\cong_{\rm PL}
\begin{cases}
 A,&n=3\text{ or odd }n\ge5,\\
 S^2\sqcup S^2,&n=4,\\
 A\sqcup A,&n\ge6\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the twisted and untwisted ladder families have distinct parity-dependent **surface** topology, not just distinct homology. All spaces in the statement are disjoint unions of closed surfaces or compact surfaces with boundary.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(M_{2n}\) has vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\); vertices \(i,j\) are adjacent precisely if \(j-i\equiv\pm1,n\pmod{2n}\). In \(P_n\), vertices are \((i,a)\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\); edges join \((i,a)\) to \((i\pm1,a)\) and \((i,1-a)\). These are finite, simple, loopless graphs, including the explicitly listed small orders.

By definition, a simplex of \(\mathcal N(G)\) is a set of vertices having a **common open neighbor** in \(G\). Thus \(\mathcal N(G)\) comprises exactly the subsets of the open-neighborhood sets \(N_G(v)\). No closed-neighborhood or flag-clique construction is used. PL homeomorphism means a piecewise-linear homeomorphism of their standard finite simplicial realizations, not merely a homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
**Möbius family, generic orders.** Assume \(n\ge4\) and reduce indices modulo \(2n\). Every open neighborhood is the triple
\[
\Delta_i=\{i-1,i+1,i+n\}.
\]
These are the distinct triangular facets. Each vertex appears in exactly three triangles; its simplicial link has three edges forming a path, in vertex order
\[
(i-2)\;--\;(i+n-1)\;--\;(i+n+1)\;--\;(i+2).
\]
For \(n\ge4\) these four link vertices are distinct. Therefore the complex is a compact triangulated 2-manifold **with boundary**, all vertices on the boundary. Directly counting faces gives
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(2n,4n,2n),\qquad \chi=0.
\]
The boundary edges are exactly \(\{i-1,i+1\}\). They form two disjoint cycles (even and odd vertex indices), each of length \(n\). The other two edges of \(\Delta_i\) are internal, shared with \(\Delta_{i+n-1}\) and \(\Delta_{i+n+1}\). Consequently, the dual adjacency graph of triangles is the cyclic step graph on \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\) generated by \(n-1\). Its number of connected components is
\[
\gcd(2n,n-1)=\gcd(2,n-1)=\begin{cases}1,&n\text{ even},\\2,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
If \(n\) is even, there is one surface of Euler characteristic zero and two boundary circles; the classification of compact surfaces makes it an annulus. If \(n\) is odd, triangles in each component have centers of one parity. Each of the two components contains exactly one of the boundary circles. Translation by one interchanges the components, so each has Euler characteristic zero. Each connected compact surface with one boundary circle and Euler characteristic zero is a Möbius band. This proves the generic formula, including nonorientability. The exceptional cases are immediate: \(M_4=K_4\), whose open-neighborhood complex is the boundary of a tetrahedron, and \(M_6=K_{3,3}\), whose neighborhood complex is two disjoint filled triangles.

**Circular family, generic orders.** Assume \(n=3\) or \(n\ge5\) and reduce indices modulo \(n\). The triangular facets are
\[
\Gamma_{i,a}=\{(i-1,a),(i+1,a),(i,1-a)\},\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
As in the first family, every vertex link is the three-edge path
\[
(i-2,a)\;--\;(i-1,1-a)\;--\;(i+1,1-a)\;--\;(i+2,a),
\]
which has four distinct vertices even when \(n=3\). There are \(2n\) vertices, \(4n\) edges, and \(2n\) triangles; thus the complex is a compact triangulated surface with boundary and Euler characteristic zero. The boundary edges are \(\{(i-1,a),(i+1,a)\}\). Each internal triangle edge joins \(\Gamma_{i,a}\) to \(\Gamma_{i\pm1,1-a}\).

For odd \(n\), this dual graph is connected, and the boundary consists of two \(n\)-cycles (one in each layer), so the surface is an annulus. For even \(n\ge6\), the dual graph has precisely two connected components, indexed by the parity of \(i+a\); the boundary is four cycles of length \(n/2\), with two cycles in each component. Translation by one interchanges the components; each therefore has Euler characteristic zero and two boundary circles. Both are annuli. At \(n=4\), instead, the vertex links are three-cycles and the triangles split into two tetrahedral boundaries, giving \(S^2\sqcup S^2\). This completes the classification in every stated range.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_ladder_neighborhood.py` from the directory containing this document and the standalone script. Its expected output, preserved in `verification_output.txt`, is

```
VERIFY_OK cases=57 mobius_n=2..30 prism_n=3..30 manifold_links=yes orientation=yes mod2_homology=yes
```

The verifier independently constructs the two graph families, enumerates all common-neighbor facets and their faces, verifies every vertex link is a path or a circle, finds connected components and boundary cycles, propagates triangle orientations, and computes exact mod-two boundary ranks. It checks each predicted invariant for all \(2\le n\le30\) in the Möbius family and \(3\le n\le30\) in the circular family. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the preceding all-parameter facet and surface-classification proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita [1] defines the same open neighborhood complex and studies its fundamental group via graph-theoretic fundamental groups. The general fundamental-group theorem does not identify the PL surfaces of the two ladder families. Shen–Song–Yu–Zhou–Zhuang [2, Theorem 4.6] **already prove** that \(\mathcal N(P_n)\simeq S^1\) for odd \(n\); that existing homotopy conclusion is not claimed as new. The present result strengthens that case to PL homeomorphism with an annulus and gives the remaining even-order, exceptional-order, and Möbius-ladder PL classifications. Their even-order \(S^0\) conclusion concerns the neighborhood complex of a *different induced independent-set graph*, not \(\mathcal N(P_n)\).

Agarwal–Basak [3, Problem 26] discuss independence and perfect-matching complexes of these same graph families and explicitly identify neighborhood complexes as a further subject of study; their results do not entail the above PL surface classification. A complete equivalent older surface classification under an alternate name has not been excluded.

## Limitations
The statement is only about open neighborhood complexes of the explicitly defined simple graphs, not arbitrary generalized Petersen graphs, graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complexes, independence complexes, matching complexes, or closed neighborhood complexes. All claimed PL types are proved symbolically; the finite script is a corroboration rather than an infinite proof. The literature comparison does not certify global novelty against inaccessible or unindexed results; an independent audit has not been performed.
