# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The key new step is the exclusion of persistent phase-scaling collinearity. If \(Y_0'=a\Lambda_\alpha Y_0\), then the profile lies on a single quasi-homogeneous scaling ray. Quasi-homogeneity converts the balance law on that ray to \(u'=-1/k+b e^{ku}\). Periodicity of the original profile forces periodicity of \(u\), impossible for a nonconstant scalar autonomous orbit. The focal source then gives the two distinguished multipliers \(1\) and \(e^{T_\eta}\); in dimension two these exhaust the Floquet spectrum and trigger its blow-up theorem.

Risk: the conclusion remains conditional on existence of a nonconstant periodic balance profile, positivity of \(p_\alpha\), and Assumption 4.2.

## Originality
**PASS.** The focal full text was inspected at the defining balance law, rank-dependent invariant-bundle proposition, multiplier corollary, blow-up theorem, numerical-computation remark, and planar example. It does not state that the rank condition is automatic in dimension two. The planar example still treats the Floquet multiplier as a computed quantity. Candidate-specific database searches using rank, radial-scaling, planar Floquet, and type-I blow-up aliases found no implication-equivalent theorem. Earlier primary compactification and normally-hyperbolic blow-up papers give geometric existence criteria at infinity but do not supply this balance-law rank reduction.

Residual risk: an equivalent transversality observation may exist in older quasi-homogeneous literature under different notation.

## Value
**PASS.** For planar applications the theorem removes an entire linearized-analysis stage from the fresh 2026 criterion: once a nonconstant periodic balance profile with the stated positivity is obtained, the necessary Floquet spectrum is forced. This is a reusable structural lemma rather than a numerical special case.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2610.05354v1 itself. Its Proposition 4.3 and Corollary 4.5 identify the phase/scaling multipliers under a full-rank hypothesis, while Theorem 5.1 separately assumes the required hyperbolicity. The present result supplies the missing planar rank implication. The result does not establish periodic-profile existence and does not control higher-dimensional quotient multipliers.
