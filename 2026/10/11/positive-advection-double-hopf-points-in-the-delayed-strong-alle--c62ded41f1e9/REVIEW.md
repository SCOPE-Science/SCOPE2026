# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The source characteristic equation is reconstructed exactly. At zero advection, the mixed Neumann--Dirichlet spatial spectrum gives \\(\\mu_2=9\\mu_1\\) and \\(\\mu_3=25\\mu_1\\). The scalar branch-difference function is positive at \\(x=1/25\\), tends to \\(-\\infty\\) as \\(x\\uparrow1/9\\), and is strictly decreasing on that interval, so it has a unique transverse zero. At that zero exactly two spatial modes can carry imaginary roots, and both conjugate pairs are simple. The gauge-transformed boundary eigenproblem has simple roots at \\(\\alpha=0\\), so the implicit-function theorem continues the transverse branch intersection to sufficiently small positive advection.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating preprint and journal article contain the same Hopf-branch formulas but assert that no Hopf--Hopf point exists when \\(d\\) and \\(\\tau\\) vary together. The constructed intersection uses a higher branch index and is not covered by their first-stability-boundary argument. Searches for the exact DOI, model title, characteristic equation, same-model Hopf--Hopf intersections, and correction/corrigendum language found no equivalent published correction. General double-Hopf reaction--diffusion papers establish that such codimension-two spectra can occur in other systems but do not imply this same-model construction.

## Value
PASS. The result changes the codimension-two bifurcation picture of the model: an asserted global exclusion fails, and positive-advection double-Hopf points persist near the zero-advection limit. This identifies a concrete omitted interaction between spatial mode number and Hopf branch index and opens a mathematically natural four-dimensional center-manifold problem for the same model.

The closest literature establishes double-Hopf theory for other delayed reaction--diffusion systems and a two-delay strong-Allee predator--prey system. The present result is narrower in model scope but directly addresses an incorrect exclusion in the motivating source. Its limitation is that the nonlinear double-Hopf normal form has not been computed.
