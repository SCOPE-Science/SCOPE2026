# Same-model mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof invokes the verified single-exception norm of Borwein–Fabian (1993, Theorem 4), and rotates it by Hilbert isometries with uniform norm-equivalence bounds. All summand norms are convex and Gâteaux smooth, and their nonnegative Bregman remainders have the common bound \(2M\|h\|_2\). This establishes an **exact** union formula for the Fréchet defect set of their positive summable combination. In the infinite case the finite-head norms have finitely many defects and so have dense Fréchet-smooth norming functionals on every closed subspace, by the Bishop–Phelps argument used in Bandyopadhyay–Gothwal (2026, Theorem 6). Uniform convergence of both norms and norm derivatives, with error at most \((M+1)2^{-N}\), transfers this density after perturbing away from the countable exceptional lines. The requisite Baire step is valid because the ambient subspace is complete of dimension at least two. Theorem 2(a) of the focal paper then proves hereditary MIP. The finite-dimensional and one-dimensional restrictions are covered. The countably dense example follows by radial homeomorphism of equivalent unit spheres. No infinite conclusion is based solely on a finite computation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to named residual risks

The strongest directly relevant publication, Bandyopadhyay–Gothwal (arXiv:2602.17153v1), expressly asks both the more-than-two-finite-point and infinite-point questions in Questions 1 and 2. Its Theorem 6 only addresses finite exceptional sets, and its cited Borwein–Fabian example has one antipodal pair. The Borwein–Fabian 1993 primary proof provides the one-line building block but does not discuss prescribed countable exceptional directions combined with hereditary MIP. Targeted indexed and open-web searches for countable singularities, positive norm superpositions, hereditary MIP, and the source's questions did not identify an equivalent statement. No directly covering earlier mathematical statement was identified in the reviewed records. Search absence is not a proof of absolute novelty. The full 2025 Gothwal thesis could not be accessed; its abstract credits the one-line construction, and it remains a residual coverage risk. Broader pre-2026 MIP-renorming and convex-smoothness literature could contain an implicit reduction. These risks do not establish a known implication covering the displayed theorem.

## Value — PASS

The theorem answers two explicitly posed questions about the boundary between Gâteaux smoothness, Fréchet smoothness, and hereditary MIP. It both supplies finite examples with more than two exceptions and shows that hereditary MIP is compatible even with *dense countable* exceptional sets. The uniform derivative approximation is the structural reason finite-defect MIP survives the infinite superposition. This is an infinite-dimensional classification over all prescribed countable projective direction sets, not an arbitrary numerical or normalization exercise.
