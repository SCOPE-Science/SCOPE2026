# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For arbitrary prescribed positive susceptible-tree densities, the nullcline elimination has the exact factored cubic displayed in `RESULT.md`; the hypotheses establish positivity of all parameters and infected components. The determinant identity gives positive–negative–positive signs at the ordered equilibria, and the uniform trace inequality gives sinks at the outer roots. The disease-free polynomial has exactly one positive root, and the implicit function theorem justifies open-parameter persistence. The checker independently evaluates the exact rational example against the original vector field and analytic Jacobian. Global basin separation, spatial stability, and absence of cycles are not proved.

## Originality — PASS
Liu–Zhang [1, Section 3] derive a cubic and give a sufficient stability theorem for an individual coexistence equilibrium; the full text does not supply the arbitrary-triple inverse realization with simultaneous sink–saddle–sink signs. Their Theorem 4 applies under \(r<\sigma\), whereas the exact demonstration here has \(r>\sigma\). The earlier related tree–insect paper [2] uses distinct equations; impulsive savanna bistability [3] and fire–tree-cover bistability [4] establish the broader phenomenon in different dynamical systems, so they are credited rather than treated as new. A prior mathematical record for the same motivating article concerns diffusion-mode instability at different parameters and does not imply this theorem. Literature coverage remains non-exhaustive.

## Value — PASS
The theorem moves beyond a noncommittal cubic root count: any triple of biologically positive equilibrium tree densities can be prescribed, the two endemic outer states are attracting, and this robust bistability survives small independent perturbations even if the fire-only equation has one positive equilibrium. That provides a structurally motivated mathematical mechanism for alternative endemic states. Its quantitative example is not an empirical forest calibration.
