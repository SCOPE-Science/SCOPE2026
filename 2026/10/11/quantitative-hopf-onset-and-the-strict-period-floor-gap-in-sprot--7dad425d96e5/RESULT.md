# Quantitative Hopf onset and the strict period-floor gap in Sprott Q

## Finding

**Theorem (stable Hopf branch, amplitude and period-gap asymptotics).** Fix any real \(a>0\). For the polynomial differential equation
\[
\dot x=-z,\qquad \dot y=x-y,\qquad \dot z=ax+y^2+bz,
\]
there is \(\varepsilon_a>0\) such that, for each \(0<b<\varepsilon_a\), the origin has a locally unique, nonconstant, orbitally asymptotically stable periodic orbit bifurcating from it. Its least period \(T_a(b)\), largest absolute second coordinate \(M_a(b)=\max_{0\le t\le T_a(b)}|y(t)|\), and time average \(\overline y_a(b)\) satisfy, as \(b\downarrow0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_a(b)&=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt a}\left(1+\frac{26a+5}{6a(8a+1)}b+O_a(b^2)\right),\\
M_a(b)^2&=\frac{2a(a+1)(4a+1)}{8a+1}b+O_a(b^{3/2}),\\
\overline y_a(b)&=-\frac{(a+1)(4a+1)}{8a+1}b+o_a(b).
\end{aligned}
\]
Furthermore, every nonconstant periodic orbit of this vector field, at any parameter \(b<a\), has \(T>2\pi/\sqrt{a-b}\). The local branch has the **strict asymptotic period-floor gap**
\[
T_a(b)-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a-b}}=
\frac{2\pi(a+1)}{3a^{3/2}(8a+1)}b+O_a(b^2)>0
\]
for sufficiently small positive \(b\). The origin is exponentially asymptotically stable when \(b<0\), and the crossing at \(b=0\) is a nondegenerate supercritical Hopf bifurcation. All assertions concern each fixed \(a>0\) and a sufficiently small neighborhood of the origin.

## Assumptions and scope

The independent variable is real time; the phase space is \(\mathbb R^3\); \(a\) is fixed and strictly positive, and \(b\) is a real bifurcation parameter. The vector field is smooth, with equilibria \((0,0,0)\) and \((-a,-a,0)\). The statement about a periodic branch concerns the origin only. \(O_a\) and \(o_a\) permit constants dependent on fixed \(a\); no uniformity as \(a\to0\) or \(a\to\infty\) is claimed. The amplitude is defined in the original second coordinate, not an arbitrary normal-form coordinate.

## Proof

Put \(s=y\), so \(x=s'+s\), \(z=-s''-s'\). This is a global linear coordinate equivalence between phase space and the third-order equation
\[
s'''+(1-b)s''+(a-b)s'+as+s^2=0. \tag{1}
\]
The Jacobian at the origin has characteristic polynomial
\[
(\lambda+1)(\lambda^2-b\lambda+a).
\]
For \(b=0\) its eigenvalues are \(-1,\pm i\omega\), with \(\omega=\sqrt a\), and the imaginary pair crosses the imaginary axis with real-part speed \(1/2\). In the original vector coordinates, choose \(q=(1+i\omega,1,a-i\omega)^\top\) and the complex left eigenrow \(\ell=(1,0,i/\omega)/[2(1+i\omega)]\). Then \(Aq=i\omega q\), \(\ell A=i\omega\ell\), \(\ell q=1\); this explicitly fixes the first-Lyapunov normalization. The sole quadratic multilinear component is \(B_3(u,v)=2u_2v_2\). Directly,
\[
A^{-1}B(q,\bar q)=(2/a,2/a,0)^\top,
\qquad[(2i\omega I-A)^{-1}B(q,q)]_2=\frac{2}{3a(1+2i\omega)}.
\]
The standard Hopf cubic expression therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{Re}\left\{\ell\left[B\bigl(\bar q,(2i\omega I-A)^{-1}B(q,q)\bigr)-2B\bigl(q,A^{-1}B(q,\bar q)\bigr)\right]\right\}
=-\frac{2(8a+1)}{a(a+1)(4a+1)},
\]
so the first Lyapunov coefficient is
\[
l_1(a)=-\frac{8a+1}{a^{3/2}(a+1)(4a+1)}<0.
\]
The Hopf theorem now establishes a unique local orbitally stable branch for \(b>0\) small and the stated stability change at the origin.

To compute the *physical-coordinate* asymptotics, let \(\epsilon>0\) denote the leading cosine amplitude of \(s\) and write \(\Omega=\omega+\delta\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^4)\), \(b=\beta\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^4)\). In the rotating phase \(\theta=\Omega t\), use
\[
s=\epsilon\cos\theta+\epsilon^2\left(c_0+c_2e^{2i\theta}+\bar c_2e^{-2i\theta}\right)+O(\epsilon^3).
\]
The zero and second harmonics of (1) give \(c_0=-1/(2a)\) and \(c_2=1/[12a(1+2i\omega)]\). The first-harmonic solvability condition at third order is
\[
-\delta\omega(1+i\omega)-\frac\beta2(-a+i\omega)+c_0+c_2=0.
\]
Taking real and imaginary parts yields the *exact* coefficients
\[
\beta=\frac{8a+1}{2a(a+1)(4a+1)},\qquad
\delta=-\frac{26a+5}{12a^{3/2}(a+1)(4a+1)}.
\]
Both are finite and \(\beta>0\). Inverting \(b=\beta\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^4)\) gives the asserted amplitude and period expansions; smooth analytic Hopf parametrization makes the period remainder \(O_a(b^2)\). Averaging the third equation and using \(\int x=\int y\) and \(\int z=0\) on a periodic orbit gives \(a\overline y+\overline{y^2}=0\). The leading Fourier harmonic fixes \(\overline{y^2}=\epsilon^2/2+o(\epsilon^2)\), proving the mean expansion.

Finally, multiply (1) by \(s'\), integrate over any nonconstant periodic orbit, and integrate by parts. All other terms vanish to leave
\[
\int_0^T (s'')^2\,dt=(a-b)\int_0^T(s')^2\,dt.
\]
Thus \(a>b\) necessarily and Wirtinger's inequality for the zero-mean periodic function \(s'\) gives \(T\ge2\pi/\sqrt{a-b}\). Equality would make \(s\) a single sinusoid plus a constant, which contradicts the nonzero second harmonic generated by \(s^2\) in (1); hence strict inequality holds. Expanding that floor and subtracting it from the derived local period gives the claimed strictly positive first-order gap.

## Relationship to prior work

Sprott's 1994 introductory paper presents this vector field among simple chaotic flows at representative numerical parameters, while the later parameter atlas maps numerical regimes [1,2]. The literature on equivalence of Sprott systems treats several forms of their jerk equations and chaotic attractors, without establishing the present bifurcation coefficients [3]. An important algebraic relationship is that, after translating and rescaling the height variable, the Sprott-S system at its positive-equilibrium Hopf threshold has the **same critical jerk equation** as (1) at \(b=0\). This explains why their critical Hopf normal forms are related. More precisely, a parameter-dependent time/coordinate equivalence exists nearby: for \(\kappa=1-b>0\), \(\alpha=(a-b)/\kappa^2>0\), and \(\sqrt B=a/[2(a-b)\kappa]\), setting \(U(\kappa t)=s(t)/\kappa^3\) transforms (1) exactly into the shifted Sprott-S jerk equation \(U'''+U''+\alpha U'+2\alpha\sqrt B\,U+U^2=0\) (primes now mean derivatives in the rescaled time). At \(b=0\), this becomes the shared critical jerk equation with \(B=1/4\). Along the Q unfolding, however, both \(\alpha\) and \(B\) vary and physical time is rescaled; the critical S Hopf coefficient and limiting period **alone** do not supply the Q period derivative or its gap. Those quantities are calculated independently here. The exact period-floor inequality is a necessary condition; the novel quantified gap states how far the *existing* stable branch is above it. No new assertion about classification of other periodic or chaotic attractors is made.

## Limitations

Only a local branch near the origin for each fixed \(a>0\) is proved. The theorem supplies neither a global uniqueness statement nor a numerical chaotic-attractor certificate and makes no assertion about other equilibria or parameter ranges. Its period and amplitude remainders are not uniform in \(a\). Historical priority cannot be ruled out by finite literature searches; in particular related critical jerk normal forms do not by themselves settle the different unfolding coefficients.
