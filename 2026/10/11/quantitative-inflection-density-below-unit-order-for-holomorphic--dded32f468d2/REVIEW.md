# Review of Quantitative inflection density below unit order for holomorphic curves

## Correctness

PASS. The primary source supplies a normalized entire representation whose components have order at most \(\rho\), the exact integral control of its growth by the Wronskian counting function, and the translation inequalities relating that counting function to \(N_1(r,f)\) and \(T(r,f)\).

The source's radius-selection lemma remains valid with an arbitrary dilation
\[
a>1
\]
in place of its fixed dilation. If the limsup ratio were smaller than the stated bound, one could choose
\[
\rho<\alpha<1
\]
and \(a\) sufficiently close to one so that the coefficient multiplying \(H_y\) stays strictly below one. The resulting polynomial growth on an unbounded sequence of radii forces every normalized coordinate to be a polynomial by Cauchy's estimates, contradicting transcendence.

The limiting integral is finite for every \(\rho<1\). The order-one-half evaluation and the asymptotic near order one follow directly from elementary changes of variable and tail comparison.

## Originality

PASS. The September 2026 primary source proves the sharp bound only at order zero. For general order below one, it proves the qualitative consequence that vanishing relative Wronskian count forces rationality, but it does not state a positive quantitative lower bound depending on \(\rho\).

The earlier Eremenko note formulates the qualitative small-ramification conjecture. The closest older lower-order defect-relation paper located in the search concerns deficiency relations and was not fully accessible; its published abstract does not report a Wronskian-density estimate. This inaccessible source is retained as an explicit residual risk.

Searches under exact-source, Wronskian-density, finite-order, and inflection-counting formulations did not locate a covering statement.

## Value

PASS. The fresh paper isolates order zero as the only quantitative case, while its subunit-order machinery already contains a stronger numerical consequence. The accepted result turns the qualitative statement
\[
N_1=o(T)\Longrightarrow\text{rational}
\]
into an explicit dimension-independent density bound for every ordinary order below one.

The formula gives a concrete scale across the entire interval, including the exact value
\[
4/(4+\pi)
\]
at order one half and the asymptotic linear decay near order one. This directly quantifies how much inflection mass is forced by transcendence.
