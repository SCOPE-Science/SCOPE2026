# Scientific review: curvature index of Steiner-symmetrized two-ball lenses

## Correctness — PASS
The proof covers arbitrary dimension, common positive radius, and all axis directions. Each positive fiber selects either one spherical cap or the midpoint of two; the exact spherical Hessian identity yields a positive curvature-deficit quadratic form on the hyperplane orthogonal to the two gradients' difference. Strict Jensen convexity gives the displayed exact transverse ratio, and inertia gives at most one sub-threshold principal curvature. Branch interfaces and regular vertical-tangent boundary points are handled by limits of the continuous shape operator. The published three-dimensional counterexample supplies sharpness; its principal minimum is reproduced by the standalone numerical checker. Numerical tests alone do not prove the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.6 and Proposition 4.9 of arXiv:2602.14090 were read in the primary full text: they establish planar preservation, one deficient normal curvature direction in dimension three, and unbounded loss in one direction. They do not establish this all-dimensional restriction on the **count** of deficient principal curvatures or the exact transverse formula. The corresponding discussion in arXiv:2505.09200 was inspected. Searches of published mathematical finding records for the curvature-index theorem, midpoint spherical graph formulations, and lens geometry found related mean-width and curvature results, none asserting or dominating this pointwise spectrum claim. General facts about Steiner symmetrization or convexity of the Euclidean norm are insufficient to state the lens theorem without the fiber-selection and metric-curvature comparison. Possible older or unindexed equivalent formulas remain a real but non-decisive residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The source raises a natural structural issue: how badly can Steiner symmetrization destroy the unit-ball curvature property? Severe loss of one sectional curvature need not entail simultaneous loss in many directions. The result determines the optimal number of deficient principal curvatures **exactly** for every two-ball lens and every ambient dimension, and identifies the guaranteed transverse geometry quantitatively. This distinguishes a rank-one failure of curvature from general failure of ball-body membership. The claim is not a parameter substitution, isolated decimal computation, or elementary rewording of the source's counterexample.

## Scientific limits
The assertion is restricted to two equal-radius balls and classically smooth boundary points. No global magnitude bound on the exceptional small curvature is claimed. Earlier unindexed midpoint-graph curvature arguments could overlap. Numerical evidence supplements an exact proof and does not constitute independent certification.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
