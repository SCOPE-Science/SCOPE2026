# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The focal finite theorem gives a convex decomposition of every Bessel Gram contraction into rank-one matrices generated by vectors in the unit polydisk. For every kernel vector, positivity of the convex summands forces each generator to be orthogonal to the kernel, so all generators lie in the rank-\(r\) range of the Gram matrix. The generating set inside that range is compact and connected. Its Hermitian ambient space has real dimension \(r^2\), while the real symmetric ambient space has dimension \(r(r+1)/2\). The connected-set refinement of Carathéodory therefore yields the stated atom counts. The zero-rank boundary case is handled separately.

## Originality
PASS. The focal source proves the sharp bounded-function representation and constructs finite simple functions, but it leaves the number of finite convex-decomposition terms unspecified and does not state a rank-sensitive support bound. Searches by source identifier, theorem wording, finite-support correlation aliases, Gram-rank language, polydisk decompositions, and Fenchel--Eggleston/Carathéodory terminology found no implication-equivalent published result. The closest retrieved frame/Gram findings concern coherence, tightness, or Riesz ratios rather than support complexity. The principal residual risk is an older equivalent statement in matrix convexity or cubature terminology.

## Value
PASS. The size of a finite probability model is a natural quantitative feature of the focal representation: the paper's proof already turns convex terms into atoms of a simple-function model. Replacing an unspecified ambient-size decomposition by \(r^2\) complex atoms or \(r(r+1)/2\) real atoms is structurally meaningful when \(N\) is large but the Bessel family spans a low-dimensional space. The proof also isolates the mechanism responsible for the compression: positivity forces every representing vector into the intrinsic Gram range.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2608.12280v1 itself, which supplies the convex representation and finite simple-function construction without the rank-sensitive atom count. Hanner--Rådström (1951) supplies the connected-set Carathéodory refinement used for the compression. No sharpness of the atom bounds is asserted, and the result does not extend the countable theorem to finite support.
