# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The target is the ordinary \(3\)-cut complex of a cycle power in the exact range of Chauhan–Shukla's Theorem 1.4. Its Alexander dual is the complex of subsets in which every vertex has at most one nonneighbor. The published wedge theorem supplies degree concentration and the exact Betti number; equivariant Alexander duality introduces the vertex-permutation sign. For every nonidentity rotation of order at least three, an orbit supplies two forbidden nonneighbors, making its nonempty fixed-simplex set empty. The half-turn fixes precisely the antipodal edges, each contributing positive one to the simplicial Lefschetz number. This proves the character and rational representation decomposition without extrapolating from finite data. Critical inequalities, the order-four exception, equality at the threshold, orientation signs, and rational semisimplicity were checked. Independently constructed finite chain complexes corroborate these conclusions.

## Originality — PASS, with limits
The closest result is the cyclic representation computation for \(\Delta_k(C_n)\) in Bayer et al., arXiv:2304.13675, Theorem 7.15, extended to the dihedral group for the same **unpowered** cycles in arXiv:2407.08158, Theorem 6.6. Those are not the powered graph family \(C_n^p\) for \(p\ge2\). Chauhan–Shukla, arXiv:2603.23155v1, Theorem 1.4, proves only shellability, homotopy type and Betti numbers in the exact parameter range; neither those data nor its specific shelling order determines rotation traces. Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak, arXiv:2406.01979, treats squared cycles without the cyclic representation. A separate earlier result concerns different exceptional cut parameters on squared cycles. Indexed search and full-text comparison disclosed no equivalent or logically stronger statement for these parameters, but cannot rule out an unindexed calculation.

## Value — PASS
The theorem upgrades a new two-parameter nonequivariant classification to a complete rational symmetry classification for infinitely many graph complexes. The antipodal element creates a new, parity-sensitive induced-sign summand with dimension growing in \(n\); its occurrence and all rotation traces cannot be reconstructed from the sphere count alone. This distinguishes topological symmetry types of the same naturally defined graph-complex family, without introducing arbitrary finite slices.

## Scientific limitations
The proof imports the published shellability theorem and is conditional on its verified statement. The result does not identify the integral module, reflection action, equivariant homotopy type, or any parameter outside the stated range. Numerical checks do not establish the universal statement. The remaining originality risk is an equivalent unindexed or unpublished result; no independent scientific validation is asserted.
