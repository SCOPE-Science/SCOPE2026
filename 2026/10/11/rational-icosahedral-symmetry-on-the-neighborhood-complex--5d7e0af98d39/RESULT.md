# Rational icosahedral symmetry on the neighborhood complex

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the icosahedral graph with twelve vertices and let \(\mathcal N(I)\) be its ordinary open-neighborhood complex. Its full graph-automorphism group is \(G\cong A_5\times C_2\), with central antipodal involution \(\iota\). Write \(\mathbf1\) for the rational trivial \(A_5\)-representation, \(V_5\) for its rational irreducible five-dimensional representation, \(W_6\) for the rational irreducible six-dimensional representation whose complexification is the sum of the two conjugate three-dimensional irreducibles, and \(\varepsilon\) for the nontrivial rational character of \(C_2\). Then the **entire rational homology representation** is

\[
\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(I);\mathbb Q)
\cong ((W_6\oplus V_5)\boxtimes\mathbf1_{C_2})
\oplus ((2\mathbf1\oplus2V_5)\boxtimes\varepsilon),
\]

and \(\widetilde H_d(\mathcal N(I);\mathbb Q)=0\) for every \(d\ne2\). In particular, the central involution has fixed-subspace dimension \(11\); the rotation subgroup \(A_5\) has fixed-subspace dimension \(2\), lying entirely in the antipodal \((-1)\)-eigenspace; and the whole group \(G\) has no nonzero invariant rational homology class in degree two.

## Assumptions and scope
Use vertices \(0,1,U_i,L_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\). The edges of \(I\) are exactly \(0U_i\), \(1L_i\), \(U_iU_{i+1}\), \(L_iL_{i+1}\), \(U_iL_i\), and \(U_iL_{i-1}\), for all five indices, with no loops or other edges. This is the usual icosahedral graph, with thirty edges and degree five. A nonempty set \(\sigma\) is a simplex of \(\mathcal N(I)\) precisely when \(\sigma\subseteq N_I(v)\) for some vertex \(v\), where \(N_I(v)\) is the *open* neighborhood. Thus the complex is not a graph-metric Vietoris--Rips complex or a closed-neighborhood complex.

The antipodal automorphism exchanges the uniquely distance-three-separated vertices. The index-two subgroup generated by all order-five graph automorphisms is the usual icosahedral rotational group \(A_5\). The displayed decomposition is over \(\mathbb Q\); the label \(W_6\) is important because the individual complex three-dimensional constituents are not defined over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**1. The rational homology is concentrated in degree two.** Enumerating all nonempty subsets of the twelve five-element open neighborhoods yields the complete face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(12,60,120,60,12).
\]
The simplicial boundary matrices, with bases ordered lexicographically and \(\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_d]=\sum_j(-1)^j[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_j,\ldots,v_d]\), have ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) of \(11,49,48,12\), in successive degrees \(1,2,3,4\); exact elimination modulo three gives the same four ranks as an independent check. Hence the mod-two Betti vector is \((1,0,23,0,0)\). Rational Betti numbers cannot exceed the corresponding mod-two dimensions by the universal coefficient theorem, and the rational Euler characteristic is
\[
12-60+120-60+12=24.
\]
Since the graph underlying the one-skeleton is connected, \(H_0\) has rank one; the other rational Betti numbers must therefore be \((1,0,23,0,0)\). This does not use any assumption that a finite homology computation alone proves a homotopy equivalence.

**2. The complete symmetry group and its Lefschetz traces.** A graph automorphism is uniquely determined by a permutation preserving the thirty specified edges. Exhaustive backtracking imposing adjacency *and nonadjacency* at every partial assignment yields \(120\) automorphisms. The antipode \(\iota\) is central and has order two. The subgroup \(R\) generated by all order-five automorphisms has sixty elements, and the remaining sixty are exactly \(\iota R\). Thus \(R\) is the familiar rotational group \(A_5\). Its conjugacy-class counts by element order \(1,2,3,5\) are respectively \(1,15,20,24\), with the order-five elements splitting into two classes of twelve.

For any simplicial automorphism \(g\), the trace on the oriented chain group \(C_d\) is the sum, over setwise \(g\)-fixed \(d\)-simplices, of the sign of the induced permutation of their vertices. Direct finite enumeration gives the following exact chain-trace table; for order five, each of the two rotation classes gives the same row.

| Element \(g\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_0\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_1\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_2\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_3\) | \(\operatorname{tr}C_4\) | \(\operatorname{tr}H_2\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| identity | 12 | 60 | 120 | 60 | 12 | 23 |
| \(r\in R\), order 2 | 0 | -4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| \(r\in R\), order 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |
| \(r\in R\), order 5 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 3 |
| \(\iota\) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |
| \(\iota r\), \(r\) order 2 | 4 | 0 | -8 | 4 | 4 | -5 |
| \(\iota r\), \(r\) order 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |
| \(\iota r\), \(r\) order 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |

The last column is \(\sum_{d=0}^4(-1)^d\operatorname{tr}C_d(g)-1\), because the Hopf--Lefschetz trace identity and Step 1 leave only \(H_0\) (on which every automorphism acts trivially) and \(H_2\). This proves the character values for every element of \(G\), not just representatives from a numerical sample.

**3. Character decomposition.** Project to the \(\pm1\) eigenspaces of \(\iota\) by \((1\pm\iota)/2\). The resulting \(A_5\)-characters on elements of orders \(1,2,3,5\) are, respectively,
\[
\chi_+=(11,-1,-1,1),\qquad \chi_-=(12,4,0,2).
\]
In each order-five class the displayed value is the same. The classical \(A_5\)-character table identifies
\[
\chi_+=\chi_{3}+\chi_{3'}+\chi_5,\qquad
\chi_-=2\chi_1+2\chi_5.
\]
The two three-dimensional characters are exchanged by \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)/\mathbb Q)\) and together form the irreducible rational representation \(W_6\). The other constituents are rational. Semisimplicity over characteristic zero establishes the displayed rational \(G\)-module isomorphism. Its eigenspace dimensions are \(11\) and \(12\). The two \(A_5\)-fixed vectors occur only in the negative eigenspace, giving the stated invariant dimensions.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's study of box complexes and their equivariant structure motivates using the natural graph-symmetry action, but does not calculate the icosahedral neighborhood homology representation. Shukla's neighborhood-complex classifications concern maximal degree at most three or certain four-regular circulants, and cannot contain this five-regular graph. A published finite calculation already gives the nonequivariant homotopy type \(\mathcal N(I)\simeq\bigvee^{23} S^2\). That is **not** claimed here; an ordinary nonequivariant homotopy type does not determine an \(A_5\times C_2\)-action on homology. The additional full-group character and rational module decomposition are the claim proved above.

## Limitations
The result determines the rational \(G\)-module, not the integral \(G\)-lattice or the equivariant homotopy type. It concerns one canonical five-regular graph, not all five-regular graphs. Modular boundary ranks are used only to deduce rational homology together with Euler characteristic, never to assert an integral Smith normal form. Originality remains subject to possible unindexed computations phrased as box or homomorphism complexes, despite the checked nearby results.
