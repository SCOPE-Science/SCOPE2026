# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The rank-one resolvent identity reduces non-spectral eigenvalues to the scalar equation \(\tau G(\lambda)=1\). The primary Fourier representation provides \(m(s)=(1/2-is)^{-1}\) and the constant-vector density \((\pi/2)\operatorname{sech}^2(\pi s)\). A direct beta-function substitution proves its exact Fourier transform. The three real-domain Laplace formulas are derived with correct signs and convergent boundary terms. Strict convexity of \(q(t)=t/(e^t-1)\), proved from an explicit positive second derivative, forces strict monotonicity on all three real components. Their limiting values give the exact \(\pi^2\)-thresholds. The spectral-circle points \(0\) and \(2\) are checked directly, not by extending a secular equation across the essential spectrum; the former is an eigenvalue only for \(\tau=-2\), while the latter is never an eigenvalue. The Fredholm essential spectrum is preserved by the rank-one perturbation. Analytic Taylor expansion yields the \(3/2\) crossing slope. The separate finite numerical checker passes but is not the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to literature uncertainty.** The complete spectral representation from Belli–Gül–Ross–Siskakis (arXiv:2604.19691v2, inspected sections 1–4 including spectrum, cyclic spectral density and resolvent) pertains to the unperturbed operator and contains no rank-one perturbation, sharp real feedback thresholds, or embedded eigenvalue transition. The full accessible 2026 follow-up *Crescents and the Real Variable Cesàro Operator* (sections 1–8, especially spectrum, point spectrum, and cyclicity) also studies unperturbed operators on \(L^p\). Generic rank-one resolvent algebra certainly implies an implicit secular equation but does not already supply the evaluated three monotone branches, exact \(\pi^2\) boundary constants, and the exceptional \(\tau=-2\) eigenvector. Targeted primary-literature, alias, and published mathematical-result searches found no dominating statement. An independently published or unindexed direct evaluation remains possible; only independent literature review could further reduce this risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The constant vector is the natural cyclic vector in the newly developed Fourier model, making its orthogonal rank-one projection canonical rather than arbitrary. The theorem determines the complete real spectral response of this specific nonnormal rank-one feedback family, including both birth/death thresholds at \(2\) and the unusual genuine eigenvalue embedded in the essential spectral circle at \(0\). The exact thresholds and the \(3/2\) crossing law are structural and falsifiable, rather than numerical parameter substitutions or a restatement of normality.
