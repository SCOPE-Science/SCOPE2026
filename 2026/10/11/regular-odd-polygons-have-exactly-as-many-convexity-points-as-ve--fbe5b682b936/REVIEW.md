# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof reconstructs the regular odd polygon's middle sets exactly. Their endpoints are the midpoints \(c_j\) of the longest diagonals, and their convex hull is a regular \(n\)-gon whose vertices are exposed, giving \(n\) convexity points by Schneider's exposed-point lemma. For the reverse inclusion, Schneider's exact middle-line criterion is combined with the continuous anti-periodic function \(q_z\). A noncorner point of the middle hedgehog makes every admissible zero of \(q_z\) a strict local extremum, while anti-periodicity forces a sign-changing zero in a half-turn; that zero violates the criterion. The argument covers all odd \(n\ge3\). The included finite checker is only an indexing sanity check and is not used to promote finite evidence to a universal proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 2015 primary source proves existence of at least three convexity points and supplies the characterization used here. The 2016 primary source says triangles and Reuleaux triangles have precisely three, proves that a typical planar convex body has infinitely many, and develops the polygonal middle hedgehog; it does not state the regular-odd exact count or the longest-diagonal description. published-finding corpus searches for exact counts, regular pentagons/heptagons, middle-hedgehog aliases, and reflection-union formulations found no covering statement. The closest owned result classifies quadrilaterals without parallel sides and does not imply the all-odd regular family. Residual risk is limited to an unindexed observation or note following Schneider's papers.

## Value
**PASS.** The result fills the explicit finite-cardinality gap between the source's examples with exactly three convexity points and its generic theorem with infinitely many. It provides a canonical family realizing every odd finite cardinality \(n\ge3\), with a geometric description by longest-diagonal midpoints and an exact radius. This is a structural classification for a natural symmetry family, not an arbitrary numerical slice.
