# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof first excludes centers on or outside the unit disk using the zero-mass angular gap seen from such a point. For an interior center, equal arclength mass of the basic sectors forces the ray-circle intersections to be the vertices of a regular \(3k\)-gon. The convexity of each \((k+1)\)-wedge is then exactly one determinant inequality, and rotation to the corresponding chord midpoint turns that determinant into
\[
2\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(k+1)}{3k}\right)
\left(c_k-O\cdot u_j\right).
\]
Thus the feasible centers are precisely the intersection of the stated regular family of half-planes. The inradius, circumradius, area, rotational union/intersection, and limits are standard consequences. The \(k=2\) degeneration is checked separately and agrees with the source's six-sector remark.

## Originality

PASS. The primary 2026 paper proves existence for every planar mass but does not classify the uniform circular mass or its center set. Its radial-symmetry argument uses a smooth comparison measure for degree computation rather than solving a radial example explicitly. The earlier introductory paper likewise gives existence/obstruction ranges rather than a center moduli space. Targeted searches for the exact circular, regular-polygon, and radius formulations found no covering statement.

## Value

PASS. A full exact model for the newly settled \((3k,k+1)\) regime is mathematically motivated by the source itself. The result does more than exhibit one fan: it classifies every convex spiral for the canonical circle mass, identifies the full center moduli polygon, recovers the rigid \(k=2\) endpoint, and shows convergence to a disk of radius \(1/2\).

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Roldán-Pensado, arXiv:2609.23746v1, which proves universal existence but no explicit all-center classification. The preceding paper by Espinosa-García, Martínez-Sandoval, and Roldán-Pensado introduces spiral equicoverings and the relevant arithmetic regimes.

The theorem is specific to normalized arclength measure on the unit circle. It does not imply an analogous polygonal moduli space for smooth radial densities or general planar masses. An older equivalent center-locus calculation under different fan-partition terminology remains the main bibliographic residual risk.
