# Regular simplices uniquely maximize the inradius of Makeev Δ-zonotopes
## Finding
Let
\[
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{a_0,\ldots,a_d\}\subset\mathbb R^d,
\qquad d\ge2,
\]
be a nondegenerate simplex whose barycenter is the origin:
\[
a_0+\cdots+a_d=0.
\]
Assume
\[
\operatorname{diam}\Delta\le1.
\]
Its associated \(\Delta\)-zonotope is
\[
R_\Delta=[0,a_0]+\cdots+[0,a_d].
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{r(R_\Delta)\le\frac12}.
\tag{1}
\]
In fact, for every pair \(i\ne j\),
\[
\boxed{\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert\ge2\,r(R_\Delta)}.
\tag{2}
\]

Hence if
\[
r(R_\Delta)\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},
\]
then every edge of \(\Delta\) has length in
\[
[1-\varepsilon,1].
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
r(R_\Delta)=\frac12
\]
if and only if \(\Delta\) is a regular simplex of edge length \(1\).

Equivalently, among all Makeev \(\Delta\)-zonotopes generated by simplices of diameter at most \(1\), the regular-simplex zonotope is the unique one that contains a Euclidean ball of radius \(1/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The inradius is the largest radius of a Euclidean ball contained in the convex body, with arbitrary center.

Because
\[
a_0+\cdots+a_d=0,
\]
the zonotope \(R_\Delta\) is centrally symmetric about the origin. For a centrally symmetric convex body, the largest inscribed Euclidean ball is centered at the symmetry center. Indeed, if
\[
c+rB_2^d\subset K
\]
and \(K=-K\), then also
\[
-c+rB_2^d\subset K,
\]
and convexity gives
\[
rB_2^d\subset K.
\]
Thus
\[
r(R_\Delta)=\min_{u\in S^{d-1}}h_{R_\Delta}(u).
\tag{4}
\]

The result is specific to the Euclidean generator-diameter constraint appearing in Makeev's universal-cover problem. It does not claim an extremal statement for arbitrary zonotopes with \(d+1\) generators under unrelated normalizations.

## Proof
For \(u\in S^{d-1}\), put
\[
t_k=u\cdot a_k.
\]
Since
\[
\sum_{k=0}^d a_k=0,
\]
we have
\[
\sum_{k=0}^d t_k=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The support function of the Minkowski sum is
\[
h_{R_\Delta}(u)
=
\sum_{k=0}^d\max\{0,t_k\}
=
\frac12\sum_{k=0}^d|t_k|.
\tag{6}
\]

Fix two distinct indices \(i,j\). The \(d-1\) vectors
\[
\{a_k:k\ne i,j\}
\]
are linearly independent. To see this, note that the only linear dependence among the \(d+1\) centered simplex vertices is a scalar multiple of
\[
a_0+\cdots+a_d=0;
\]
a dependence supported on only \(d-1\) vertices must therefore be trivial.

Choose a unit vector \(u_{ij}\) orthogonal to every \(a_k\) with
\[
k\ne i,j.
\]
Then
\[
u_{ij}\cdot a_k=0
\qquad(k\ne i,j).
\]
Equation (5) gives
\[
u_{ij}\cdot a_i=-u_{ij}\cdot a_j.
\]
Therefore (6) becomes
\[
h_{R_\Delta}(u_{ij})
=
|u_{ij}\cdot a_i|
=
\frac12
\bigl|u_{ij}\cdot(a_i-a_j)\bigr|.
\tag{7}
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
h_{R_\Delta}(u_{ij})
\le
\frac12\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert.
\tag{8}
\]

Since the inradius is the minimum support value,
\[
r(R_\Delta)
\le
h_{R_\Delta}(u_{ij}),
\]
so (8) yields
\[
2\,r(R_\Delta)
\le
\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert.
\]
This proves (2) for every edge.

Because every edge length is at most the diameter,
\[
\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert\le1.
\]
Combining this with (2) gives (1). If
\[
r(R_\Delta)\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},
\]
then (2) immediately gives (3).

Now suppose
\[
r(R_\Delta)=\frac12.
\]
Then (2) and the diameter bound imply
\[
\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert=1
\]
for every pair \(i\ne j\). Thus all edges of the simplex are equal, so \(\Delta\) is a regular simplex of edge length \(1\).

Conversely, suppose \(\Delta\) is regular of edge length \(1\). The standard regular-simplex description of the associated Makeev body is the intersection of the strips
\[
H_{ij}
=
\left\{
x:
\bigl|x\cdot(a_i-a_j)\bigr|
\le
\bigl|a_i\cdot(a_i-a_j)\bigr|
\right\}.
\]
For a centered regular simplex,
\[
\lVert a_i-a_j\rVert=1
\]
and
\[
a_i\cdot(a_i-a_j)=\frac12.
\]
Hence every bounding hyperplane of every strip has Euclidean distance
\[
\frac12
\]
from the origin. Therefore
\[
\frac12B_2^d\subset R_\Delta,
\]
while (1) already gives the reverse inradius inequality. Thus
\[
r(R_\Delta)=\frac12.
\]

Finally, translation cannot improve ball containment. If a translate
\[
t+R_\Delta
\]
contains a radius-\(r\) Euclidean ball, then \(R_\Delta\) contains a radius-\(r\) ball with some center; central symmetry and the argument preceding (4) move such a ball to the origin without decreasing its radius.

## Verification
The supplied `verify.py` checks the support-function identity (6) and the pairwise certificate (7) on deterministic rationally generated simplices in dimensions \(2\) through \(7\).

It also constructs regular simplices in their standard zero-sum realization, verifies that every edge has length \(1\), verifies the tight support value \(1/2\) in edge-difference directions, and checks the lower bound
\[
h_{R_\Delta}(u)\ge\frac12
\]
for deterministic unit directions using the regular-simplex tight-frame identity.

The computation is only a regression test. The theorem for all dimensions follows from the exact orthogonal-complement argument (7)--(8).

## Relationship to prior work
Hausel, Makai, and Szűcs formulate Makeev's universal-cover conjecture using the regular-simplex body obtained by intersecting unit-width strips orthogonal to the simplex edges, and prove the conjecture in dimension \(3\).

Živaljević later presents the equivalent \(\Delta\)-zonotope language
\[
R_\Delta=[0,a_0]+\cdots+[0,a_d]
\]
for an arbitrary centered simplex and states the corresponding all-dimensional Makeev conjecture under the generator-diameter constraint.

For a regular simplex, Živaljević also identifies \(R_\Delta\) with the intersection of the edge-normal strips. That result gives the regular body's inradius \(1/2\), but it does not compare this inradius with zonotopes generated by nonregular simplices of the same diameter.

Bezdek studied a different extremal problem for zonotopes with \(d+1\) generators: among those containing a fixed unit ball, the regular member minimizes intrinsic volumes. That fixes the inradius and optimizes size. The result here instead fixes the diameter of the generating simplex and maximizes the inradius; the pairwise estimate (2) directly links the generator geometry to ball containment.

## Limitations
This theorem does not prove Makeev's universal-cover conjecture in dimensions \(d\ge4\). It only identifies the unique generator that is optimal for containing the diameter-\(1\) Euclidean ball.

The stability statement (3) controls the edge lengths of the generating simplex. No quantitative Hausdorff or Banach--Mazur stability estimate for the zonotope itself is claimed.

Bezdek's fixed-inradius intrinsic-volume theorem is closely related background but has different hypotheses and objective. Its full text was not available through the bounded public retrieval used here; only its published abstract and later theorem summaries were inspected.

An equivalent short extremal observation may exist in older zonotope literature under minimum-width terminology. Exact-object and equivalent-formulation searches did not locate such a statement, but this remains the main historical residual risk.
