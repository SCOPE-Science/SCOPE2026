# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Dihedral symmetry reduces one vector to a single octagonal edge. The eight relative edge classes have a complete sign classification for \(N_8(x+y)-N_8(x-y)\). Same-edge pairs are strictly positive; the adjacent horizontal and vertical classes have only shared-endpoint zeros; the opposite classes follow by central symmetry; and on the decisive \(E_2\) class the two norms become strictly monotone functions of \(\sqrt2\pm d(a-b)\), so equality is exactly \(a=b\). Those points are precisely \(y=Rx\), with \(E_6\) giving \(y=-Rx\). Direct substitution gives both chord norms \(\sqrt2\).

## Originality
PASS. Searches covered the isosceles-orthogonality notation, regular-octagon aliases, quarter-turn and rotation formulations, and James-type language. No covering classification was found. The closest same-object records optimize different quantities. General James-type and polyhedral attainment results determine scalar extrema or existence of extremizers but do not identify every admissible unit pair. Current and immediate-prior own ledgers were checked; related rows give a scalar \(J_2\) identity, an \(H_2\) rotation optimization, and an \(\Omega\) extremum, none of which implies the full iff statement.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives the complete unit isosceles-orthogonality graph of a canonical polygonal Banach plane and a constant-chord consequence for every admissible pair. This is structural information beyond a routine exact-value computation and provides a natural benchmark for isosceles-based geometric invariants.

## Closest literature and limitations
Chen, Yang, Liu and Li introduced an isosceles-orthogonality James-type constant in 2022. Sain, Ghosh and Paul connected James attainment with isosceles orthogonality and proved polyhedral attainment results. Neither inspected source classifies all unit isosceles pairs in the regular octagon. The present theorem is restricted to unit vectors in this norm and does not assert the same rigidity for other regular polygons.
