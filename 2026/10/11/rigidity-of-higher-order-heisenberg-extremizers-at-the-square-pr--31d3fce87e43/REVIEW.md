# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The final claim is restricted to the square graph domain where the source identities extend by cutoff approximation. For even \(m\), equality in the final sharp inequality makes the entire chain \(0\le\|\mathcal E_bv\|_2^2\le0\) sharp. The only intermediate inequality is radial Cauchy, so equality also forces the tangential gradient of \(v\) to vanish. The remaining radial second-order equation has a one-dimensional smooth solution space at the origin and gives the source hypergeometric profile. For odd \(m\), equality gives \(\mathcal O_bv=0\). Writing \(w=\Delta v+bc_ov\) shows first that \(w\), then \(\Delta v\), then \(v\) are radial. The substitution \(v'=ry\) reduces the equation to a second-order radial equation with a one-dimensional smooth solution space, giving the source odd profile. The parameter identities were checked symbolically. No computation is used in place of proof.

## Value
PASS. The focal paper's main sharp inequality supplies an extremizer but does not say whether the constructed radial family is exhaustive. Equality classification is the natural next structural question for a sharp inequality, especially because future stability estimates require knowing the exact extremizer manifold. The result shows that no nonradial regular equality profile can occur and reduces the whole regular equality set to the already explicit hypergeometric family. This is a complete natural classification within a source-matched square domain, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a routine constant evaluation.
