# Rotation has order two or four on Möbius-ladder perfect-matching homology

## Finding
For each even integer \(n\geq 4\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder on \(2n\) vertices and let \(K_n=\mathcal M_p(M_{2n})\) be the simplicial complex generated by the graph's perfect matchings. Let \(\rho\) be the cyclic graph automorphism rotating all vertices one step. Put \(d=n/2-1\) and \(V_n=\widetilde H_d(K_n;\mathbb Q)\). Then \(V_n\cong\mathbb Q^2\), and the induced operator \(T=\rho_*\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T)=0,\qquad T^2=(-1)^d\operatorname{id}_{V_n}.
\]
Consequently, for \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), the rational rotation representation factors faithfully through a cyclic group of order two and splits into the trivial and sign lines. For \(n\equiv0\pmod4\), it factors faithfully through a cyclic group of order four and is the irreducible rational module \(\mathbb Q[t]/(t^2+1)\). Thus the homological order of a one-step geometric rotation is precisely two or four, although the rotation's graph order is \(2n\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an even \(n\geq4\). Label vertices of the graph by \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), and include perimeter edges \(e_i=\{i,i+1\}\) for all \(i\bmod 2n\) and opposite-vertex edges \(a_j=\{j,j+n\}\) for \(0\leq j<n\). An abstract vertex of \(K_n\) is a graph edge; a simplex is a collection of such edges contained in some perfect matching. This convention distinguishes \(K_n\) from the ordinary matching complex. The rotation \(\rho\colon i\mapsto i+1\) preserves the graph and its perfect matchings, so acts simplicially. Homology in the claim is reduced homology over \(\mathbb Q\); no statement about the integral representation is made.

The input from Agarwal and Basak, Theorem 21 of arXiv:2608.30601 (first released 31 August 2026), is
\[
K_n\simeq S^{(n-2)/2}\vee S^{(n-2)/2}\quad\text{for even }n.
\]
In particular, there is exactly one nonzero reduced rational homology group, in degree \(d\), of dimension two. The following character calculation is separate from that nonequivariant homotopy theorem.

## Proof
**Fixed-simplex enumeration.** The graph has \(3n\) edges: the \(2n\) perimeter edges and \(n\) opposite-vertex edges. The one-step rotation \(\rho\) permutes all perimeter edges transitively, forming an orbit of size \(2n\), and permutes all opposite-vertex edges transitively, forming an orbit of size \(n\). A simplex invariant under a permutation must be a union of entire edge orbits. The perimeter orbit is not a matching, while the opposite-vertex orbit \(R\) is a perfect matching. Thus the only \(\rho\)-invariant faces, including the augmented empty simplex, are \(\varnothing\) and \(R\); their numbers of constituent edge-orbits are respectively zero and one.

The two-step rotation \(\rho^2\) has exactly four edge orbits. Its opposite-vertex edges split into even and odd index classes \(R_0,R_1\), each of cardinality \(n/2\). Its perimeter edges split into even and odd index classes \(C_0,C_1\), each of cardinality \(n\). Every \(C_i\) is itself a perfect matching (alternating perimeter edges), hence cannot be combined with any other nonempty orbit into a graph matching. Both \(R_i\) are partial matchings, and their union is the opposite-vertex perfect matching. Therefore the complete list of invariant faces is
\[
\varnothing,\ R_0,\ R_1,\ R_0\cup R_1,\ C_0,\ C_1.
\]
The corresponding counts of entire edge-orbits are \(0,1,1,2,1,1\). No other invariant simplex exists, because every invariant edge subset is a union of whole orbits and each nonlisted union fails the matching condition.

**Reduced Lefschetz calculation.** Suppose a graph automorphism \(g\) fixes a simplex \(F\) setwise and permutes the \(k\) constituent abstract vertices in \(o\) cycles. On the oriented simplicial chain generated by \(F\), its sign is \((-1)^{k-o}\), since a cycle of length \(m\) has sign \((-1)^{m-1}\). In reduced degree \(k-1\), the signed Lefschetz contribution is \((-1)^{k-1}(-1)^{k-o}=(-1)^{o+1}\). The augmented empty simplex has \(k=o=0\) and contributes \(-1\), agreeing with reduced Lefschetz conventions. Hence
\[
L_{\mathrm{red}}(g)=-\sum_{\substack{F\in K_n\\gF=F}}(-1)^{o(F)},
\]
where the sum includes the empty face. The two fixed-face lists give
\[
L_{\mathrm{red}}(\rho)=-(1-1)=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
L_{\mathrm{red}}(\rho^2)=-(1-1-1+1-1-1)=2.
\]
Because reduced homology is supported only in degree \(d\), the Lefschetz trace formula yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T)=0,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(T^2)=2(-1)^d.
\]

**From traces to the operator identity.** Since \(\rho^{2n}=1\) on the graph, also \(T^{2n}=I\). In characteristic zero, a finite-order linear operator is diagonalizable over \(\mathbb C\), with eigenvalues on the unit circle. Thus the two eigenvalues of \(T^2\) lie on the unit circle. Their sum is either \(2\) or \(-2\); equality in the triangle inequality forces both to equal the same sign \((-1)^d\). It follows that \(T^2=(-1)^d I\). If \(d\) is even, trace zero forces the two eigenvalues of \(T\) to be \(1,-1\), and \(T\) has order two. If \(d\) is odd, its minimal polynomial is \(t^2+1\), so it has order four and affords the stated irreducible rational module. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
The standalone reproducibility program `verify_rotation.py` constructs every perfect matching and every simplex for \(n\in\{4,6,8,10\}\), explicitly computes all edge orbits under one- and two-step rotations, and verifies that there are respectively two and six fixed faces with the predicted orbit counts and Lefschetz numbers. It also computes complete reduced mod-two simplicial homology by exact bit-vector elimination for \(n\in\{4,6,8\}\). The expected nonzero mod-two Betti rank is two in degree \(d\), consistent with the cited homotopy input. These are finite consistency checks and do not establish the all-parameter theorem independently; the all-parameter steps are the orbit enumeration, the Lefschetz argument, and the cited homotopy theorem.

To reproduce, use Python 3 with its standard library and run `python3 verify_rotation.py`. The program prints `VERIFY_OK` only after its complete checks finish.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, Theorem 21, classify the nonequivariant homotopy type of the perfect-matching complex for even Möbius ladders; that theorem is used here and does not by itself determine the induced rotation action. The present calculation supplies a character and the complete rational cyclic representation, with a dichotomy modulo four.

Li proves equivariant log-concavity for the graded permutation modules spanned by graph matchings. Those modules and their comparison maps are not the homology representation of the subcomplex generated by perfect matchings, so that theorem does not imply the rotation formula. Bate, Everitt, Ford and Ramos study symmetric-group representations on matching-complex homology of complete hypergraphs; their hypotheses and complexes differ. The literature comparisons do not rule out all unindexed or unpublished equivalents.

## Limitations
This is a rational homology representation statement for even \(n\geq4\), not an equivariant homotopy equivalence or an integral lattice classification. It assumes the published nonequivariant homotopy classification; the included exact computations test only four finite parameter values. Odd values of \(n\), reflections, and higher symmetry actions are not classified. The claim of originality is relative to inspected searchable literature, not an assertion of mathematical uniqueness.
