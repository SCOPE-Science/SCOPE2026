# Saddle tangency makes the hyperbolic BRK logarithm sharp

## Finding

Consider the three-dimensional family of surfaces of revolution generated by
\[
g(a,t)=\frac{a}{t},
\qquad
1\le a\le\frac{100}{81},
\qquad
1\le t\le2.
\]
The surfaces used in the source are
\[
\Gamma_a
=
\left\{
(x,y,a/\sqrt{x^2+y^2}):
\frac32\le\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\le2
\right\}.
\]
This is a hyperbolic cinematic-curvature family.

Set
\[
a_0=\frac{100}{81},
\qquad
b_0=1,
\qquad
t_0=\frac{17}{10},
\qquad
q=\frac9{10},
\]
and translate \(\Gamma_{b_0}\) by
\[
\tau
=
\left(
\frac{17}{100},
0,
\frac{100}{1377}
\right).
\]
Then
\[
\left|
\Gamma_{a_0}(\delta)
\cap
\bigl(\Gamma_{b_0}(\delta)+\tau\bigr)
\right|
\asymp
\delta^2\log\frac1\delta
\qquad
(\delta\downarrow0).
\]

The recent source proves the upper bound
\[
\left|
(\Gamma_a(\delta)+\tau)\cap\Gamma_{\bar a}(\delta)
\right|
\lesssim
\frac{\delta^2\lvert\log\delta\rvert}
{\delta+\lvert\bar a-a\rvert}
\]
for hyperbolic cinematic-curvature families in \(\mathbb R^3\). The explicit pair above matches the logarithmic order for a fixed nonzero parameter gap. Hence the logarithmic factor in that hyperbolic theorem is genuinely necessary, already for the elementary power family \(g(a,t)=a t^{-1}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The notation \(\Gamma_a(\delta)\) denotes the Euclidean \(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(\Gamma_a\), exactly as in arXiv:2609.39059v1.

The generating interval is
\[
I=[1,2],
\]
so the source's truncated interval is
\[
I'=[3/2,2].
\]
The tangency constructed below occurs at source radii
\[
17/10
\quad\text{and}\quad
153/100,
\]
both strictly inside \(I'\). Thus boundary truncation plays no role in the local lower bound.

The statement is a sharpness result for Theorem 1.5(b), not a claim about every hyperbolic family and not a determination of the associated maximal-operator norm.

## Proof

Write
\[
u=\frac{17}{100},
\qquad
v=\frac{100}{1377}.
\]
Near the point with horizontal base coordinate
\[
P_0=(t_0,0),
\qquad
t_0=\frac{17}{10},
\]
the two relevant surfaces are graphs of
\[
\phi(x,y)
=
\frac{a_0}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}
\]
and
\[
\psi(x,y)
=
\frac1{\sqrt{(x-u)^2+y^2}}+v.
\]
Since
\[
t_0-u
=
\frac{153}{100}
=
q t_0,
\]
and
\[
a_0=q^{-2},
\]
one checks directly that
\[
\phi(P_0)=\psi(P_0)
\]
and
\[
\nabla\phi(P_0)=\nabla\psi(P_0).
\]
Thus the translated surfaces are tangent at the corresponding point in \(\mathbb R^3\).

Put
\[
F=\psi-\phi.
\]
For
\[
h_c(x,y)=\frac{c}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}},
\]
the Hessian at \((r,0)\) is
\[
D^2h_c(r,0)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2c/r^3&0\\
0&-c/r^3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently
\[
D^2F(P_0)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\lambda&0\\
0&-\lambda
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\lambda
=
\frac{1-q}{q^3t_0^3}>0.
\]
The tangency is therefore a nondegenerate saddle.

By the Morse lemma there is a fixed local \(C^1\) coordinate chart, with Jacobian bounded above and below, in which
\[
F=\xi^2-\eta^2.
\]
After the linear change
\[
U=\xi-\eta,
\qquad
V=\xi+\eta,
\]
we have
\[
F=UV.
\]
Choose a fixed small square
\[
|U|<\rho,
\qquad
|V|<\rho.
\]
For
\[
0<\delta<\rho^2,
\]
the exact planar area inside this square is
\[
\left|
\{|UV|\le\delta\}
\right|
=
4\delta
\left(
1+\log\frac{\rho^2}{\delta}
\right).
\]
Returning to the original base coordinates gives
\[
\left|
\{(x,y):|F(x,y)|\le\delta\}
\right|
\gtrsim
\delta\log\frac1\delta.
\]

For each such base point, the vertical segment
\[
[\phi(x,y)-\delta,\phi(x,y)+\delta]
\]
is contained in \(\Gamma_{a_0}(\delta)\), while
\[
[\psi(x,y)-\delta,\psi(x,y)+\delta]
\]
is contained in \(\Gamma_{b_0}(\delta)+\tau\). If
\[
|F(x,y)|\le\delta,
\]
these intervals overlap in length at least \(\delta\). Fubini's theorem therefore yields
\[
\left|
\Gamma_{a_0}(\delta)
\cap
(\Gamma_{b_0}(\delta)+\tau)
\right|
\gtrsim
\delta^2\log\frac1\delta.
\]

For the reverse inequality, the parameter gap is fixed:
\[
|a_0-b_0|=\frac{19}{81}.
\]
Theorem 1.5(b) of the source gives
\[
\left|
\Gamma_{a_0}(\delta)
\cap
(\Gamma_{b_0}(\delta)+\tau)
\right|
\lesssim
\frac{\delta^2\log(1/\delta)}
{\delta+19/81}
\lesssim
\delta^2\log\frac1\delta.
\]
This proves the two-sided order.

It remains only to verify that the model lies in the theorem's hyperbolic class. We have
\[
g_t(a,t)=-\frac{a}{t^2},
\qquad
g_{tt}(a,t)=\frac{2a}{t^3}.
\]
The map
\[
(a,t)\longmapsto(g_t,g_{tt})
\]
has the smooth inverse
\[
t=-\frac{2g_t}{g_{tt}},
\qquad
a=-g_t t^2
\]
on its compact image, so the cinematic-curvature lower bound is positive. For \(\bar t>t\), the determinant in the source's Definition 1.4 is
\[
D
=
\frac{2\bar a(a-\bar a)(\bar t-t)}
{\bar t^3t^3}.
\]
Hence
\[
D
\,[g_t(\bar a,t)-g_t(a,t)]
\,g_t(a,t)<0
\]
for \(\bar a\ne a\). Thus the family is hyperbolic. Smoothness supplies all remaining regularity assumptions.

## Verification

The primary paper was inspected at its definition of the surfaces \(\Gamma_a\), Definition 1.4, Remark 1.3, Theorem 1.5, the near-tangency proof, and its appendix level-set estimate.

The source explicitly identifies \(g(a,t)=a t^p\) with \(p<1\), \(p\ne0\), as the hyperbolic power model. Its Theorem 1.5(b) contains the additional logarithm in dimension three. The source's sharp maximal-operator theorem later invokes Theorem 1.5(a), not the hyperbolic part 1.5(b), so it does not itself supply a matching lower example for that logarithmic loss.

The lower bound here is independent of numerical computation. The chosen constants make the tangency and Hessian exact rational identities. The only qualitative input is the standard Morse lemma for a nondegenerate critical point.

A closely related source by the same authors proves general level-set estimates for hyperbolic functions and supplies the level-set theorem quoted in the BRK paper. That general analytic result explains why a logarithm can appear at a saddle, but it does not by itself identify a pair of admissible BRK surfaces whose neighborhood intersection saturates Theorem 1.5(b).

## Relationship to prior work

The classical BRK and Wolff literature develops intersection estimates and cinematic-curvature conditions for circles and related families. The recent source extends this framework to broad families of surfaces of revolution and separates the elliptic and hyperbolic three-dimensional cases.

For elliptic families, the paper obtains the same \(\delta^2/(\delta+|a-\bar a|)\) intersection scale as in higher dimensions. For hyperbolic families, it proves the weaker bound with one logarithm. The present example shows that this distinction is not an artifact of the proof: a permitted hyperbolic power family develops a genuine saddle tangency, and the familiar planar \(|UV|\le\delta\) geometry produces exactly the missing logarithm.

Targeted searches under the source identifier, hyperbolic cinematic-curvature terminology, logarithmic intersection sharpness, saddle tangency, and the power family \(a t^{-1}\) did not locate a published statement of this source-specific saturation example. The closest general level-set paper concerns the analytic sublevel-set mechanism rather than the BRK surface-neighborhood intersection.

## Limitations

The result proves sharpness of the logarithmic factor in the pairwise hyperbolic intersection theorem. It does not determine the sharp Wolff-type maximal-operator norm for hyperbolic surface families.

The explicit example uses the power \(p=-1\). No claim is made that every hyperbolic power \(p<1\), \(p\ne0\), admits the same displayed translation without modification, although the saddle mechanism is structurally stable.

The theorem establishes the asymptotic order up to positive constants, not an exact leading volume coefficient for the three-dimensional intersection.
