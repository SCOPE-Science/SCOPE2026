# Scale-four Čech nerve of the Tutte–Coxeter graph is a wedge of 144 two-spheres

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the 30-vertex Tutte–Coxeter graph with unit-length geometric edges, and let \(\mathcal N(T,r)\) denote the nerve of closed balls of radius \(r/2\) about the vertices in its intrinsic metric realization. Then
\[
\mathcal N(T,4)\simeq\bigvee^{144}S^2.
\]
In particular, \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(T,4);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{144}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The complex is pure of dimension nine and cannot be shellable. Since \(T\) has girth eight and cycle rank sixteen, the scale-four transition follows a graph-like homotopy type \(\bigvee^{16}S^1\) for every \(1\le r<4\), by the girth recovery theorem in [1]. The scale-four result makes no assertion about the induced persistence maps at this threshold.

## Assumptions and scope
Construct the point set \(P=\mathbb F_2^4\setminus\{0\}\) (fifteen vectors). On \(\mathbb F_2^4\), put the nondegenerate alternating form
\[
B(a,b)=a_0b_1+a_1b_0+a_2b_3+a_3b_2\pmod 2.
\]
The fifteen totally isotropic two-dimensional subspaces have nonzero-vector triples \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) with \(B(a,b)=0\). Form a bipartite graph with vertex classes these fifteen points and fifteen lines, joining a point to a line exactly when the vector lies in the line. This standard symplectic generalized-quadrangle incidence graph is the Tutte–Coxeter graph [3]. The standalone verifier constructs precisely this graph and checks its generalized-quadrangle incidences, cubic degree, 45 edges, diameter four, and girth eight. The statement concerns this specific finite graph and \(r=4\), not arbitrary generalized quadrangles, other graph complexes, or a classification of the entire filtration.

## Proof
**The exact nerve.** By the even-integer graph Čech lemma of Adams–Shukla–Singh [1, Lemma 2.2], \(\mathcal N(T,4)\) is the simplicial complex generated by the closed graph-distance-two neighborhoods \(N_2[v]=\{u:d_T(u,v)\le2\}\), for all thirty vertices \(v\). To see why the geometric-edge metric matters, an intersection of radius-two balls may be moved to an integer-distance vertex without losing its centers; hence the generating neighborhoods are *not* just pairwise-distance cliques. The thirty neighborhoods are distinct, each has ten vertices, and none contains another, so all are facets. The complex is pure of dimension nine.

**Exhaustive face construction.** Label points by nonzero binary vectors in ascending integer order, then label the isotropic lines by lexicographically sorted triples. Form each distance-two neighborhood as a 30-bit mask and insert every nonempty submask into a set. There are exactly 27,535 faces and the face vector in dimensions zero through nine is
\[
(30,435,2620,5625,7290,6255,3600,1350,300,30).
\]
Every face is included, because every simplex belongs to at least one neighborhood facet; the verifier also checks that every codimension-one face of every nonzero-dimensional simplex is in the enumerated set.

**A full acyclic matching.** Starting with the set of all nonempty faces, process vertices \(v=0,1,\ldots,29\) in that order. At stage \(v\), for every still-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\), pair it with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) if both are present and still unmatched; remove the two from subsequent stages. Pairing is along an actual codimension-one Hasse incidence. The standalone algorithm makes 13,695 such pairs and leaves exactly 145 critical faces: one vertex and 144 triangles. No other critical dimensions occur.

**Acyclicity certificate.** Build the *complete* Hasse diagram of these 27,535 nonempty faces: orient all unpaired codimension-one incidences from large face to small face, and reverse each paired incidence. There are exactly 144,210 incidences. Kahn's topological-sort algorithm removes every vertex of this directed graph, which proves that the matching has no closed alternating gradient path. This is the required combinatorial acyclicity condition, not an empirical estimate. Forman's discrete Morse theorem produces a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with exactly one zero-cell and 144 two-cells, and no cells of any other positive dimension. All attaching maps from the boundaries of those two-cells land in a single point, proving the displayed wedge homotopy type over the integers.

**Non-shellability.** A nonempty pure shellable simplicial complex has reduced homology concentrated in its top dimension (if any). This complex is pure nine-dimensional but has 144 independent classes in dimension two, so it is not shellable.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter; it uses no third-party modules and reconstructs every face and every matching pair directly from the finite symplectic incidence model. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK` followed by the exact graph parameters, the ten-entry face vector, 13,695 paired incidences, 144,210 Hasse edges, critical-cell counts \(\{0:1,2:144\}\), and Euler characteristic \(145\). As an independent chain-complex cross-check, exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\) computes \(\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_1=29\), \(\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_2=406\), and \(\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_3=2070\), giving reduced second Betti number 144. These finite-field ranks corroborate, but do not by themselves prove, the integral wedge assertion; the exhaustive acyclic matching proves that stronger claim. All computation concerns the finite 30-vertex complex, so no unproved infinite extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla, and Singh [1] establish the exact intrinsic Čech definition, the generating-neighborhood lemma, and the girth recovery range, but their higher-scale homotopy classifications concern hypercube graphs rather than the Tutte–Coxeter incidence graph. Previte [2] defines the equivalent \(D=\{0,1,2\}\) neighborhood construction and studies examples and classes such as cycles, trees and one-point unions, but her dissertation's results do not supply this graph's radius-two homotopy type. Graph-theoretic descriptions [3] identify the symplectic-quadrangle graph as the standard Tutte eight-cage. Known scale-three Vietoris–Rips homotopy calculations on generalized quadrangles use a different simplicial construction and do not determine this scale-four Čech nerve. The new contribution is the exact 144-sphere homotopy calculation and its complete finite discrete-Morse witness, not the preexisting construction or graph identity.

## Limitations
No classification is asserted at other scales, for generalized quadrangles of arbitrary order, or for Vietoris–Rips complexes. A systematic literature search can miss an equivalent unindexed result; the computation and proof are reproducible but have not undergone independent mathematical audit. The finite certificate is exhaustive for the stated thirty-vertex graph only. It does not establish any persistence-map assertion at scale four.
