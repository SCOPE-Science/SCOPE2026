# Scale-three Čech nerve of the Desargues graph is a wedge of 49 two-spheres

## Finding

Let \(D=GP(10,3)\) be the Desargues graph with 20 vertices and unit-length edges, realized as a metric graph. Let \(\mathcal N(D,r)\) denote the nerve of closed metric balls of radius \(r/2\) centered at its vertices. At the first critical scale beyond the girth-based graph-recovery range,

\[
\mathcal N(D,3)\simeq\bigvee^{49}S^2.
\]

In particular \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(D,3);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{49}\), all other reduced integral homology groups vanish, and \(\pi_1(\mathcal N(D,3))=0\). This nerve is a pure five-dimensional simplicial complex and is not shellable. The classification is a homotopy equivalence; no homeomorphism to the wedge is claimed.

## Assumptions and scope

Vertices of \(D\) are numbered \(0,\ldots,19\). For \(i\in\{0,\ldots,9\}\), with indices reduced modulo ten, there are three edge families: outer edges \(\{i,i+1\}\), inner edges \(\{10+i,10+(i+3)\}\), and spokes \(\{i,10+i\}\). This specifies the ordinary simple generalized Petersen graph \(GP(10,3)\), not the ten-vertex complement of the Petersen graph, which has occasionally shared the Desargues name. Its graph realization is assigned the intrinsic shortest-path metric. All balls are *closed*, centered at vertices, and their intersections may occur at edge midpoints.

The claim is restricted to the exact scale \(r=3\). It does not assert the homotopy type of any other high-scale nerve, a complete barcode, or an equivariant classification. The 2022 Čech-complex source is the owning source; graph terminology is fixed independently by the explicit edge list.

## Proof

**Step 1: the complete face set.** For a finite graph at odd integer scale three, the graph Čech nerve is generated by \(N_1[u]\cup N_1[v]\) over edges \(\{u,v\}\), where \(N_1[w]\) denotes the closed graph-distance-one neighborhood. This follows from the midpoint characterization of intersections of graph balls (Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2). The Desargues graph has exactly 30 edges, and each generated set has six vertices. Enumerating every nonempty subset of these 30 sets, then deduplicating, yields the complete face vector

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(20,150,440,410,180,30).
\]

There are thus exactly 1,230 nonempty faces, and the complex is pure of dimension five. Its Euler characteristic is \(20-150+440-410+180-30=50\).

**Step 2: elementary simplicial collapses.** On the complete face set, repeatedly select a codimension-one face \(\sigma\) having exactly one immediate coface \(\tau\), prioritizing the lexicographically largest triple \((|\tau|,\tau,\sigma)\), with faces represented by vertex-bitmasks. Delete the pair \((\sigma,\tau)\). Unique immediate coface guarantees that \(\tau\) is a maximal face; each deletion is an elementary simplicial collapse. Exactly 472 deletions leave the dimension-two subcomplex with face vector \((20,118,148)\). The procedure and all pair checks are given in `VERIFY.py`.

**Step 3: a complete acyclic Morse matching.** On the remaining one-skeleton, shuffle edges with pseudorandom generator seed one and take the first Kruskal spanning tree. This has 19 edges. Root at vertex zero and match each nonroot vertex with its parent tree edge. Next, among the 99 non-tree edges and 148 triangles, repeatedly select a triangle incident with *exactly one* as-yet-unmatched non-tree edge, and match that triangle to that edge (the deterministic pseudorandom choice is specified in `VERIFY.py`). This process matches all 99 non-tree edges to distinct triangles, leaving 49 triangles. Together with the elementary-collapse pairs, there are 590 disjoint matching pairs on the original face poset, with exactly one critical vertex and 49 critical triangles.

The entire directed Hasse graph of the original 1,230 nonempty faces is then constructed: a matched incidence is oriented from the lower face to the higher, and every other incidence from higher to lower. All 4,340 incidences are included. A complete topological sort processes all 1,230 vertices, proving the matching is acyclic—not merely pairwise valid. By Forman's discrete Morse theorem, the original complex is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell, no one-cells, and 49 two-cells. Every two-cell attaches to the singleton zero-skeleton by a constant map; hence the CW complex is a wedge of 49 two-spheres. This proves the finding over the integers, without an assumption that finite-field homology determines homotopy.

**Step 4: a shellability consequence.** The input complex is pure of dimension five, but its nonzero reduced homology is concentrated in dimension two. A pure shellable simplicial complex has the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres in its top dimension (or a contractible space). Therefore this five-dimensional nerve cannot be shellable.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` with the Python 3 standard library. It reconstructs the graph, generates the complete nerve, performs and verifies every elementary free-face collapse, reconstructs the complete discrete-Morse matching, and validates global gradient acyclicity by topological sorting. It independently checks mod-two boundary ranks 19 and 99 on the collapsed two-complex. Successful execution emits `VERIFY_OK` and the precise counts. The mathematical homotopy conclusion rests on the checked acyclic matching and Forman's theorem, not on the mod-two calculation alone.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Shukla and Singh supply the graph Čech definition and the odd-scale nerve generation lemma; their homotopy classifications concern hypercube graphs and do not cover \(GP(10,3)\). Published work on the Desargues graph's Vietoris–Rips filtration computes a different simplicial construction: a Rips complex is a clique complex on distance-bounded pairs, whereas the present Čech nerve records common intersection of vertex-centered balls. Other recorded Desargues graph invariants (matching, cut and independence complexes) have distinct defining faces. The Desargues graph is a distinguished cubic symmetric girth-six incidence graph, rather than an arbitrary graph selected for a numerical experiment.

## Limitations

This is a finite, exact computation for one canonical graph at one threshold. It does not establish a theorem for every cubic graph of girth six, determine the later Čech filtration, determine symmetry actions on homology, or exclude an unindexed equivalent calculation. The proof includes a reproducible exhaustive finite certificate; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
