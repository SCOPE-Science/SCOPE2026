# Scale-three Čech persistence survives vertex truncation of triangle-free graphs

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(H\) be a finite connected simple triangle-free graph with \(n\ge 2\) vertices and \(m\ge1\) edges. Subdivide every edge of \(H\) once and take the line graph of the subdivision; denote the resulting graph by \(T=L(S(H))\). Give every edge of \(T\) length one. Let \(\mathcal N(T,r)\) be the nerve of the closed intrinsic metric balls of radius \(r/2\), one centered at each vertex of \(T\). Then there is a sequence of elementary simplicial collapses, preserving the inclusion of the smaller complex,

\[
\mathcal N(T,3)\searrow\mathcal N(T,2).
\]

Both complexes have homotopy type

\[
\mathcal N(T,2)\simeq\mathcal N(T,3)\simeq\bigvee^{m-n+1}S^1.
\]

Moreover the map induced by the filtration inclusions

\[
H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(\mathcal N(T,2);\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(\mathcal N(T,3);\mathbb Z)
\]

is surjective at the first arrow and an isomorphism at the second. The kernel of the first arrow is canonically the direct sum of the cycle lattices of the cliques indexed by the original vertices:

\[
\ker\big(H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(\mathcal N(T,2);\mathbb Z)\big)
\cong\bigoplus_{v\in V(H)} H_1(K_{\deg_H(v)};\mathbb Z),
\]

of rank \(\sum_{v\in V(H)}\binom{\deg_H(v)-1}{2}\). Thus every first-homology class surviving scale two survives scale three, including its integral class, with no torsion and no homology above dimension one at either scale. The convention for zero summands is a point.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, and connected, and \(H\) contains no three-cycle; degree-one vertices and trees are allowed. The vertex set of \(T\) consists of **oriented incidences** \((v,w)\) for every edge \(vw\in E(H)\). Distinct incidences are adjacent in \(T\) exactly when they have the same first coordinate or are reversals \((v,w),(w,v)\). Write

\[
C_v=\{(v,w):w\in N_H(v)\}, \qquad
B_{vw}=C_v\cup C_w.
\]

The \(C_v\) are pairwise disjoint, nonempty vertex sets, each inducing a clique. The Čech nerve is formed in the **geometric realization of the unit-edge graph** and not from Euclidean balls or from balls restricted solely to its vertex metric. The scale parameter is normalized so that \(\mathcal N(T,1)=T\). We use integral, unreduced first homology, and elementary collapses mean removal of a free codimension-one face together with its unique containing facet.

## Proof

**1. Exact generating faces.** The graph Čech facet formula of Adams--Shukla--Singh (Lemma 2.2 in the first reference) gives at even scale two all closed graph-neighborhoods, and at odd scale three unions of closed neighborhoods across graph edges. For the incidence graph model above this yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_2:=\mathcal N(T,2)
 &=\left\langle C_v\cup\{(w,v)\}:v\sim_H w\right\rangle,\\
K_3:=\mathcal N(T,3)
 &=\left\langle B_{vw}:v\sim_H w\right\rangle
 \cup\left\langle C_v\cup\{(w,v),(z,v)\}:
 w\ne z\in N_H(v)\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here angle brackets mean the complex generated by the displayed sets (all their subsets). In particular, every vertex of both complexes is an incidence. Define the intermediate complex \(K_0=\langle B_{vw}:vw\in E(H)\rangle\). Plainly \(K_2\subseteq K_0\subseteq K_3\).

**2. Collapse \(K_3\searrow K_0\).** Fix a vertex \(v\) and two distinct neighbors \(w,z\), and put \(R=\{(w,v),(z,v)\}\). Because \(H\) has no triangle, the two vertices of \(R\) are in no generating simplex \(B_{ab}\): that would require the forbidden edge \(wz\). They occur together in exactly one generating simplex of the second kind, \(C_v\cup R\). Distinct pairs \((v;\{w,z\})\) have distinct such two-element markers \(R\). Choose \(a=(v,w)\in C_v\). Every face of \(K_3\setminus K_0\) with marker \(R\) is exactly \(R\cup S\) or \(R\cup S\cup\{a\}\), where \(S\subseteq C_v\setminus\{a\}\). Remove each pair

\[
(R\cup S,\;R\cup S\cup\{a\})
\]

in descending order of \(|S|\). Each lower face then has exactly one remaining coface, namely the paired upper face, and that upper face is maximal among remaining faces. This is an elementary collapse. The markers are exclusive, so these collapses can be carried out independently and remove precisely \(K_3\setminus K_0\).

**3. Collapse \(K_0\searrow K_2\).** Fix an edge \(vw\in E(H)\), set \(a=(v,w)\), \(b=(w,v)\), \(P=C_v\setminus\{a\}\), and \(Q=C_w\setminus\{b\}\). A face of \(B_{vw}\) is absent from \(K_2\) exactly when it has a nonempty subset of each of \(P\) and \(Q\). Each such face belongs to this one cross-edge generating simplex because \(H\) is simple and the \(C_v\) are disjoint. For every nonempty \(S\subseteq P\), nonempty \(U\subseteq Q\), and \(Y\in\{\varnothing,\{b\}\}\), match

\[
(S\cup U\cup Y,\;S\cup U\cup Y\cup\{a\}).
\]

Descending order of the lower-face cardinality again makes each pair an elementary collapse. This removes exactly \(K_0\setminus K_2\). If \(P\) or \(Q\) is empty, there are no pairs for this edge. Combining with Step 2 gives the claimed **relative simplicial collapse** \(K_3\searrow K_2\), so the actual inclusion \(K_2\hookrightarrow K_3\) is a simple-homotopy equivalence and induces integral homology isomorphisms.

**4. Collapse \(K_2\) to a graph.** Define

\[
R_0=\left\langle C_v:v\in V(H)\right\rangle
\cup\left\langle\{(v,w),(w,v)\}:vw\in E(H)\right\rangle.
\]

For each oriented incidence \((v,w)\) put \(a=(v,w)\), \(b=(w,v)\) and \(P=C_v\setminus\{a\}\). Every face of \(K_2\setminus R_0\) occurs uniquely in \(C_v\cup\{b\}\) and contains \(b\) together with a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq P\). The pairs \((\{b\}\cup S,\{a,b\}\cup S)\), again taken in descending order of \(|S|\), collapse \(K_2\) onto \(R_0\). This collapse **fixes the one-skeleton of \(T\)**: the latter consists of all edges inside the \(C_v\) and all reversal edges, and is exactly the one-skeleton of \(R_0\).

For each \(v\), choose an anchor \(a_v\in C_v\). In the simplex on \(C_v\), pair each subset \(S\subseteq C_v\setminus\{a_v\}\) of size at least two with \(S\cup\{a_v\}\), in decreasing cardinality order. These are elementary collapses and leave only a tree of edges from \(a_v\) to the other vertices of \(C_v\). Together with all reversal edges they form a connected graph \(J\); contracting the disjoint internal star trees in \(J\) gives the original graph \(H\). Graph forest contraction is a homotopy equivalence, so \(K_2\simeq H\simeq\bigvee^{m-n+1}S^1\).

**5. The integral persistence kernel.** In \(T\), each \(C_v\) spans \(K_{d_v}\), and the only other edges are reversals. Passing from \(T\) to \(R_0\) fills each \(K_{d_v}\) with a simplex without altering the reversal edges. Thus the induced \(H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(R_0;\mathbb Z)\) is surjective; its kernel is generated by cycles wholly inside the distinct \(C_v\). Because the \(C_v\) have disjoint vertex and edge sets, these cycle sublattices embed independently in the graph cycle lattice \(H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\), giving their direct sum, not merely an equality of ranks. The collapse \(K_2\searrow R_0\) fixes \(T\), so this is precisely the kernel for the inclusion \(T=K_1\hookrightarrow K_2\). A complete graph on \(d_v\ge1\) vertices has cycle rank \(\binom{d_v}{2}-d_v+1=\binom{d_v-1}{2}\). Step 3 transfers the same surviving integral classes to scale three. No infinite-dimensional approximation or finite-enumeration inference is used.

**Necessity of excluding triangles for the uniform assertion.** For \(H=C_3\), \(T=L(S(H))=C_6\). A direct complete boundary-matrix calculation gives \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(C_6,2);\mathbb F_2)=0\) but \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal N(C_6,3);\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2^2\). Therefore dropping the triangle-free hypothesis does not preserve the conclusion universally.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library `verify.py` reconstructs every generating face of the two Čech nerves directly from incidences and checks **every elementary collapse** by testing that the lower face has exactly one surviving immediate coface and the upper face has none. It checks all four collapse stages, equality of each resulting complex with its independently constructed target, final graph cycle rank, and the \(C_3\) obstruction by independent mod-two boundary elimination. The deterministic suite contains thirty-eight connected triangle-free examples, including paths, stars, cycles, \(K_{2,3}\), the cube, the Petersen graph, and thirty randomly constructed connected triangle-free graphs. It completes with `VERIFY_OK`. This is finite corroboration; the proof above supplies the general quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Shukla and Singh (arXiv:2212.05871, Lemma 2.2 and §3) define the **finite vertex-centered intrinsic graph Čech nerve** and give its even/odd generating-face formula. Their new homotopy calculations treat hypercube graphs, not the general class \(L(S(H))\) at scales two and three. Goyal, Shukla and Singh (arXiv:2009.12130, Corollary 3.3) establish that the **clique complex of a line graph** of a triangle-free graph has the homotopy type of the original graph; their complex is not the larger scale-three Čech nerve, and their statement alone does not imply the inclusion \(\mathcal N(T,2)\hookrightarrow\mathcal N(T,3)\) is a collapse equivalence. Adamaszek and collaborators (SoCG 2018, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2018.3) study metric gluings and a different, all-points version of metric Čech constructions; the present vertex-centered relative-collapse assertion does not follow from that treatment.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to line graphs of single-edge subdivisions of **triangle-free** graphs, and only the scale-one-to-three portion of the filtration is classified. It does not assert a collapse for arbitrary line graphs, arbitrary graph subdivisions, or scales four and above. The counterexample \(H=C_3\) shows the triangle condition cannot simply be discarded. The exact naturality is the inclusion at the specified scales; no claim about homotopy classification at unexamined parameters is made.
