# Scale-two Čech complexes of multinary Hamming graphs collapse to wedges of two-spheres

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(n\ge 3\) and \(q\ge 3\), the intrinsic graph Čech nerve at scale two of the Hamming graph \(H(n,q)=K_q^{\square n}\) has an explicit elementary simplicial collapse onto a simply connected two-dimensional complex. In particular,
\[
\mathcal N(H(n,q),2)\simeq\bigvee^{B(n,q)}S^2,
\qquad
B(n,q)=q^n-1-\frac{n(q-1)q^n}{2}+\frac{q^n}{2}\binom n2(q-1)^2+nq^{n-1}\binom{q-1}{2}.
\]
The integral reduced homology is free of rank \(B(n,q)\) in degree two and zero in every other degree. The examples \((n,q)=(3,4)\) and \((4,3)\) give \(783\) and \(836\) two-spheres, respectively. The \((3,3)\) specialization with \(134\) two-spheres was previously calculated and is not part of the originality claim.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix integers \(n,q\ge3\). Write \([q]=\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}\). The vertices of \(G=H(n,q)\) are words \(x\in[q]^n\), with an edge between words differing in exactly one coordinate; each edge is a unit interval in the geometric realization. The Čech nerve \(X=\mathcal N(G,2)\) has a simplex for a set of vertex-centered **closed radius-one** balls whose intersection in the geometric graph is nonempty. Let \(B_x=\{y\in[q]^n:d_H(x,y)\le1\}\), where \(d_H\) denotes Hamming distance. A *coordinate line* fixes all but one coordinate, and consists of \(q\) vertices. A free-face pair is a proper codimension-one face and its sole maximal coface, deleted together as an elementary simplicial collapse.

## Proof
**1. Facets and intersections.** Since each ball has radius one and every graph edge has length one, a common intersection of at least two vertex-centered balls in the interior of a graph edge can only involve balls centered at that edge's endpoints; that pair also intersects at a graph vertex. Therefore the nerve is the simplicial complex generated by the closed graph neighborhoods \(B_x\). For distinct centers \(x,y\), the vertex-set intersection \(B_x\cap B_y\) is their common coordinate line when \(d_H(x,y)=1\), consists of the two shortest-path intermediate vertices when \(d_H(x,y)=2\), and is empty when \(d_H(x,y)>2\). In particular, every face with at least three vertices that is not contained in a coordinate line belongs to a unique generating simplex \(\Delta(B_x)\).

**2. First explicit collapse.** Process each center \(x\), in any fixed ordering. Among subsets \(F\subseteq B_x\setminus\{x\}\) of cardinality at least three which are not contained in any coordinate line, process decreasing cardinality (breaking ties lexicographically), and delete \((F,F\cup\{x\})\). Each lower face is unique to \(\Delta(B_x)\); all larger non-line cofaces were removed earlier, while no line face contains \(F\). Consequently \(F\) has precisely the displayed coface and every deletion is an elementary simplicial collapse. Uniqueness also makes the pairs for different centers disjoint.

**3. Second explicit collapse.** For each coordinate line \(L\), select its lexicographically first vertex \(r_L\). Process all subsets \(F\subseteq L\setminus\{r_L\}\) with at least three vertices in decreasing cardinality, deleting \((F,F\cup\{r_L\})\). All cofaces off \(L\) were deleted in step 2, and distinct coordinate lines share at most one vertex; hence the same descending-order free-face argument proves these elementary collapses. The resulting complex \(K\) has dimension two and retains every original vertex and edge. Its triangles are precisely the following disjoint families:

(a) **Corner triangles:** for each \(x\), two distinct coordinate positions \(i,j\), and replacements \(a\ne x_i\), \(b\ne x_j\), retain \(\{x,x[i\mapsto a],x[j\mapsto b]\}\). Their number is \(q^n\binom n2(q-1)^2\).

(b) **Line-fan triangles:** for every coordinate line \(L\), retain \(\{r_L,u,v\}\) with distinct \(u,v\in L\setminus\{r_L\}\). Their number is \(nq^{n-1}\binom{q-1}{2}\).

The retained edges join exactly vertex pairs at Hamming distance one or two. Thus the numbers of vertices, edges, and triangles are
\[
V=q^n,\qquad E=\frac{q^n}{2}\left[n(q-1)+\binom n2(q-1)^2\right],\qquad
T=q^n\binom n2(q-1)^2+nq^{n-1}\binom{q-1}{2}.
\]

**4. Simple connectivity.** Every Hamming-distance-two edge is the third side of a corner triangle and can be homotoped to a two-edge path in \(G\). Thus any loop in the one-skeleton of \(K\) is homotopic to a loop in \(G\). Every triangle within one coordinate line bounds a combination of the surviving fan triangles. Each four-cycle from changing two different coordinates is filled by the two surviving corner triangles along its Hamming-distance-two diagonal. These are all the two-cell relations of the standard product CW structure on \(\prod_{i=1}^n\Delta^{q-1}\), whose one-skeleton is \(G\): its two-cells are triangles in one factor and squares in two factors. The product is contractible, and its two-skeleton therefore has trivial fundamental group. It follows that \(\pi_1(K)=0\).

**5. Homotopy type.** The connected finite two-complex \(K\) has no positive reduced homology outside degree two, and \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is free because it is a subgroup of a free cellular chain group. Euler characteristic gives its rank \(V-E+T-1=B(n,q)\). Choose maps representing a basis of \(\pi_2(K)\cong H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\), using Hurewicz. Their wedge induces a homology isomorphism from \(\bigvee^{B(n,q)}S^2\) to the simply connected CW complex \(K\); the homological Whitehead theorem makes this a homotopy equivalence. Composing with the two elementary-collapse sequences completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `verify_hamming_cech.py` is run with `python3 verify_hamming_cech.py`. It independently constructs the nerve as the downward closure of all radius-one vertex neighborhoods, carries out both specified stages, and checks **every** deletion by enumerating all immediate cofaces of the lower and upper face. It also compares the complete surviving edge and triangle sets to their structural predictions and computes both nontrivial simplicial boundary ranks exactly over \(\mathbb F_2\). On the parameter pairs \((2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2),(3,4),(4,3)\), the verifier reports `VERIFY_OK`, no first homology and the predicted second Betti numbers. In particular, \((3,4)\) has \(62{,}448\) initial nonempty faces, \(29{,}680\) deletion pairs and final face vector \((64,1152,1872)\); \((4,3)\) has \(38{,}907\) initial nonempty faces, \(17{,}739\) deletion pairs and final face vector \((81,1296,2052)\). The finite computations check the collapse implementation, not the infinite quantifiers in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1], Theorem 4.1, establish the sphere-wedge answer for binary hypercube graphs; their proof applies to \(q=2\) and does not cover genuinely multinary Cartesian powers. Matsushita [2], Example 3.5, gives the two-factor rook-graph case using the result of Antolín Camarena and Carnero Bravo [3] on independence complexes of certain categorical products. Those statements cover \(n=2\), rather than the claimed \(n\ge3,q\ge3\) region. The isolated ternary three-factor case \((3,3)\), including its \(134\)-sphere conclusion, was obtained in an earlier calculation; it is acknowledged as prior and is not represented as new. The contribution here is the full unbounded multinary, multifactor classification with explicit collapse instructions, covering such cases as \((3,4)\) and \((4,3)\) and all larger parameters. An equivalent result could exist under alternative terminology in unindexed literature.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only the scale-two intrinsic graph Čech nerve, not a Vietoris–Rips complex, larger Čech scales, persistence maps, or an equivariant representation. The reduction proves homotopy equivalence and integral homology, not an explicit minimal geometric sphere basis. Computation checks only the stated finite parameter pairs; the general conclusion is proved symbolically. Bibliographic novelty is qualified by the possibility of an undiscovered covering source. No independent scientific audit is asserted.
