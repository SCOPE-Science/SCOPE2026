# Scale-two Čech homotopy distinguishes the Shrikhande and rook graphs

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite simple strongly regular graph with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). Let \(\mathcal N(G,2)\) be the nerve of the closed radius-one balls centered at the sixteen graph vertices, with the balls taken in the geometric realization of \(G\) whose edges have unit length. There are exactly two possibilities, up to graph isomorphism:

\[
\mathcal N(G,2)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigvee^{23}S^2,&G\cong S\ \text{(the Shrikhande graph)},\\
\bigvee^{63}S^2,&G\cong R=K_4\square K_4\ \text{(the rook graph)}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, **the scale-two Čech homotopy type is not determined by strongly regular parameters or the adjacency spectrum**. Both complexes are pure six-dimensional and non-shellable. The new case is the Shrikhande graph; the rook-graph homotopy type was established previously [2].

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected, all graph edges have length one, and the ambient space for the closed balls is the full metric realization of the graph, not merely its discrete vertex set. The graph Čech scale \(r=2\) uses balls of radius \(r/2=1\). Label \(\mathbb Z_4^2\) by \(v(i,j)=4i+j\) for \(0\le i,j\le3\). The Shrikhande graph \(S\) joins \((i,j)\) to \((i,j)+(a,b)\) modulo four for each
\[
(a,b)\in\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\}.
\]
The rook graph \(R\) joins distinct \((i,j)\) and \((k,l)\) precisely when \(i=k\) or \(j=l\). Both graphs have degree six and exactly two common neighbors for every pair of distinct vertices. The induced graph on the six neighbors of a vertex is a six-cycle in \(S\), but two disjoint triangles in \(R\); hence the graphs are nonisomorphic.

The classification of all strongly regular graphs with these parameters into these two isomorphism classes is a classical result, stated explicitly in [3, §10.6]. The topological computation does not reprove that graph-classification theorem.

## Proof
**1. Identify the simplices.** By [1, Lemma 2.2], for an even integer scale the intrinsic graph Čech complex is generated by the closed graph-distance neighborhoods. Therefore the facets of each complex are the sixteen sets \(N_G[v]=\{v\}\cup\{w:vw\in E(G)\}\), each of size seven. They are distinct, so neither graph has additional maximal faces. All nonempty subsets of these facets can be enumerated exactly using sixteen-bit masks.

**2. Count the entire face posets.** In dimensions zero through six the face vectors are, respectively,
\[
 f(\mathcal N(S,2))=(16,120,400,512,336,112,16),
 \qquad f(\mathcal N(R,2))=(16,120,464,536,336,112,16).
\]
There are \(1512\) and \(1600\) nonempty faces, with Euler characteristics \(24\) and \(64\).

**3. Give exact acyclic Morse matchings.** For each of the two face sets, initialize the set of unmatched nonempty faces to all faces. For pivots \(v=0,1,\ldots,15\), visit remaining faces \(\sigma\) in increasing bit-mask order; whenever \(v\notin\sigma\) and both \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) remain, match that covering pair and remove both from the unmatched set. This deterministic finite rule produces \(744\) pairs for \(S\) and \(768\) pairs for \(R\). In either case the unmatched faces consist of exactly one vertex and only two-simplices: \(23\) for \(S\), or \(63\) for \(R\).

To certify acyclicity rather than assume it from the greedy construction, orient every Hasse covering incidence downward, except matched incidences, which are oriented upward. The independent directed-graph check implemented in `verify.py` builds all \(5952\) incidences for \(S\), respectively \(6240\) for \(R\), and removes every vertex of the resulting directed Hasse graph by Kahn's topological sorting algorithm. Exactly \(1512\), respectively \(1600\), simplices are removed, so neither directed graph has a cycle. Every matching pair is checked to be a genuine covering pair, and every face is checked to occur in at most one pair. Thus these are acyclic discrete-Morse matchings.

**4. Deduce the integral homotopy types.** Forman's discrete-Morse theorem yields a CW complex with one zero-cell, no one-cells, and respectively \(23\) or \(63\) two-cells, with no cells of any other dimension. Every attaching map of a two-cell lands in the sole zero-cell and is null. The CW complexes are precisely wedges of the indicated number of two-spheres up to homotopy. The difference in the second Betti numbers proves the non-equivalence. Since each original simplicial complex is pure six-dimensional with nonzero homology in degree two, neither is shellable.

**5. Classify the parameter class.** The established graph classification [3] says any strongly regular \((16,6,2,2)\) graph is isomorphic to \(S\) or \(R\). The two preceding computations therefore give all possible Čech homotopy types for this parameter class.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and its standard library; no external mathematical software or downloaded data are required. The script independently constructs both graphs from the adjacency definitions, checks all degrees and common-neighbor counts, confirms the contrasting local graphs, enumerates every nonempty face and performs both deterministic Morse matchings. It constructs every directed Hasse incidence and verifies acyclicity by exhaustive topological sorting. An independent mod-two boundary-matrix calculation gives ranks \((15,105,272,240,96,16)\) for \(S\) and \((15,105,296,240,96,16)\) for \(R\) in dimensions one through six, and Betti numbers \((1,0,23,0,0,0,0)\) and \((1,0,63,0,0,0,0)\). The executable ends with `VERIFY_OK` only after all assertions pass. The integral and homotopy conclusions follow from the Morse CW reductions, not from finite-field homology alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] define intrinsic graph Čech complexes and give the even-scale closed-neighborhood formula. Their homotopy classification concerns hypercube graphs, so it does not imply the present triangular Shrikhande case. Previte [4] introduced the equivalent \(D\)-neighborhood model and computed selected small graph examples, without a Shrikhande calculation in the inspected examples and main results. Matsushita [2, Example 3.5] determines the rook-graph case in the general family of Cartesian products of two cliques, giving \(63\) spheres when both clique sizes equal four. That case is cited as **known**, and is independently reconstructed here solely for a like-for-like comparison. The new Shrikhande calculation, together with [2] and the classical strongly regular graph classification [3], gives the two-case statement. Clique-complex homology and the ordinary open-neighborhood complex are different constructions; neither numerical data nor titles for those complexes imply the claim here.

## Limitations
The result is limited to the intrinsic scale-two Čech nerve and the strongly regular parameter class \((16,6,2,2)\). No conclusion is claimed for other scales, other strongly regular parameter sets or equivariant homology modules. This is an exact finite combinatorial proof with reproducible exhaustive checks, not a general structural formula for all graphs. The graph classification and the rook case are pre-existing results and are not newly proved. The bibliography and indexed search do not exclude an unindexed earlier equivalent Shrikhande calculation; an independent scientific audit has not been performed.
