# Scientific review: scale-two Čech homotopy of the Shrikhande and rook graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The graphs are fully specified on \(\mathbb Z_4^2\), and their strongly regular parameters are reconstructed directly. The even-scale graph Čech formula of Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2, identifies the exact simplicial facets. Exhaustive nonempty-face enumeration gives face vectors \((16,120,400,512,336,112,16)\) and \((16,120,464,536,336,112,16)\). A complete deterministic acyclic matching has \(744\) / \(768\) matched pairs and precisely one critical vertex plus \(23\) / \(63\) critical two-simplices. All \(5952\) / \(6240\) directed Hasse incidences are checked by topological sorting. The resulting CW models prove the integral homotopy equivalences. Independent mod-two chain ranks corroborate the result without being substituted for the homotopy proof. The classical two-graph classification is cited, not newly claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
The new conclusion is the exact Shrikhande scale-two homotopy type and its contrast with the known rook case. Adams–Shukla–Singh analyze hypercubes; Previte develops the equivalent \(D\)-neighborhood construction and other examples; Matsushita, Example 3.5, already covers all rook graphs but does not supply a Shrikhande classification. Brouwer–Van Maldeghem classify the parameter class as two graphs, without addressing Čech homotopy. Related work on clique and open-neighborhood complexes uses different simplicial face conditions. Targeted literal and semantic comparisons did not identify a stronger or equivalent published Shrikhande theorem. This is not a proof of global uniqueness: an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent may exist.

## Value — PASS
The pair is the exceptional nonisomorphic cospectral pair sharing parameters \((16,6,2,2)\), so testing whether a natural topological invariant distinguishes them is mathematically motivated. The calculation completes the scale-two homotopy classification of this entire parameter class, produces a difference of forty in second Betti number and supplies explicit integral wedge decompositions with compact replayable certificates. It is neither a routine substitution into the rook formula nor an arbitrary finite graph slice. It does not establish a general formula for all strongly regular graphs.

## Main literature and limitations
See Adams–Shukla–Singh, arXiv:2212.05871, Lemma 2.2; Matsushita, arXiv:2511.12608, Example 3.5; Brouwer–Van Maldeghem, *Strongly Regular Graphs*, §10.6; Previte, *The D-neighborhood complex of a graph*, Chapter 3. The calculation does not determine other scales, torsion phenomena for other parameter sets or symmetry representations. An independent audit has not been performed.
