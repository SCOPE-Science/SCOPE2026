# Review of Schwarz-gap rigidity for real Poisson extremizers

## Correctness

PASS. The source representation
\[
u=U\circ\omega
\]
is exact, with \(\omega\) a disk self-map fixing zero. For
\[
q=q_r(f),
\]
the rescaled map
\[
\psi(z)=\omega(rz)/(qr)
\]
is again a disk self-map fixing zero. Applying the same subharmonic Littlewood principle used in the primary theorem to
\[
z\mapsto |U(qrz)|^p
\]
gives
\[
\|P_r f\|_p^p\le M_p(qr).
\]

For
\[
p>1,
\]
the function
\[
|U|^p
\]
has positive Riesz mass in every annulus because
\[
\Phi'
\]
never vanishes. Hence \(M_p\) is strictly increasing. If \(q=1\), the analytic Schwarz quotient \(\omega(z)/z\) attains its unit bound at an interior point and is therefore a unimodular constant. Boundary uniqueness then gives exactly the rotated square-wave traces. All quantifiers and finite-radius hypotheses are explicit.

## Originality

PASS. The primary paper states that the square-wave trace attains the sharp real norm, but it does not classify all equality cases and does not state the contracted-radius estimate through \(q_r(f)\).

Classical Littlewood subordination gives only the non-strict comparison. The 2024 sharpening by Dmitrović and Karapetrović assumes a univalent subordinating symbol, whereas the disk map generated by arbitrary bounded real harmonic data need not be univalent. The classical harmonic Schwarz results are pointwise and do not imply the circular \(L^p\) extremizer classification.

Searches of the current and prior research ledgers, the published-finding index, and public literature under equality-case, square-wave, strict-subordination, and Schwarz-quotient formulations found no covering statement.

## Value

PASS. The source's exact operator norm identifies one extremizer but leaves its equality geometry implicit. The new result gives the complete extremizer orbit for every finite radius and every \(1<p<\infty\), together with a natural strictness parameter that measures how far the subordinate map is from a disk rotation.

Complete extremizer classifications are structurally useful in sharp inequalities, and the contracted-radius estimate is a reusable starting point for future stability estimates rather than a mere reformulation of norm attainment.
