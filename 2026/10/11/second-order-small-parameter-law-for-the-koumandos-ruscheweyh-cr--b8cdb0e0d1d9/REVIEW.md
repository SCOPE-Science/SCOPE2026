# Review of Second-order small-parameter law for the Koumandos–Ruscheweyh critical curve

## Correctness

PASS. The source's normalized root equation extends smoothly to
\[
\rho=0.
\]
At the endpoint,
\[
F(q,0)=q\operatorname{Si}(\pi)-\pi,
\]
so its root is
\[
\kappa=\pi/\operatorname{Si}(\pi),
\]
and
\[
F_q(\kappa,0)=\operatorname{Si}(\pi)>0.
\]
The implicit-function theorem therefore applies.

Direct differentiation of the exact source equation gives
\[
F_\rho(\kappa,0)
=
\kappa^2A-\pi\kappa B.
\]
All derivative integrals are absolutely convergent. The resulting formula
\[
q'(0)=\frac{\kappa^2}{S}(SB-A)
\]
and multiplication by \(\rho\) yield the claimed second-order expansion for
\[
\nu(\rho).
\]

## Originality

PASS with a named access risk. The fresh source proves the limiting root ratio and its global strict monotonicity but gives no endpoint derivative or second-order expansion.

The 2018 primary paper studies neighborhoods of
\[
\rho=1/3
\]
and a weaker result at
\[
\rho=2/3,
\]
rather than the small-\(\rho\) endpoint. Candidate-specific searches under the root equation, ratio limit, endpoint derivative, and second-order asymptotic formulations found no covering result.

The complete 2007 publisher paper could not be obtained through the lawful full-text route after open-access and authorized-access attempts, so an unindexed endpoint remark remains a residual risk. Its public abstract does not state such an expansion.

## Value

PASS. Proposition 6.1 of the new proof singles out
\[
q(\rho)=\nu(\rho)/\rho
\]
as the normalized quantity controlling the small-parameter regime and proves only its endpoint value and monotonicity.

The accepted result determines the tangent of that normalized critical curve. It therefore quantifies how rapidly the optimal parameter departs from its endpoint slope, providing a natural sensitivity invariant for the sharp parameter used throughout the source's small-\(\rho\) certificates.
