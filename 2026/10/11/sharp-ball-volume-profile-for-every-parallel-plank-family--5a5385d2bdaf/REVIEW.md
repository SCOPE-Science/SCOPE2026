# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** With all planks sharing one normal, their union inside the unit ball is encoded by a measurable set \(E\subset[-1,1]\) whose length is at most the total plank width. Fubini reduces covered volume exactly to the integral of the strictly symmetric-decreasing slice density \((1-t^2)^{(d-1)/2}\) over \(E\). One-dimensional rearrangement gives the centered interval as the unique maximizer up to null sets. The incomplete-beta formula follows by the substitution \(s=t^2\). In the no-waste case, removing \(\delta\) measure from the least costly part of the centered interval and adding it to the most valuable part of its complement gives the sharp layer-exchange deficit. The bundled checker independently tests the formulas, random interval unions, and equality constructions.

## Originality
**PASS, with an explicit folklore risk.** The primary 2013 source was materially inspected at the plank-volume question. It states the centered-plank configuration as the expected answer and records a stronger known theorem only in dimension three; it does not state an all-dimensional parallel-family theorem or a stability profile. Searches under plank, slab, partial covered volume, centered rearrangement, parallel families, and incomplete-beta formulations found no implication-equivalent published statement. A recent plank survey and a modern complex-plank paper concern width needed for complete coverage rather than this partial-volume optimization. Because the proof is a sharp one-dimensional rearrangement after alignment, an older unindexed observation remains a real residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The restriction is geometrically canonical rather than arbitrary: aligned planks are exactly the configuration conjectured to be extremal in the source problem. The result confirms the expected extremizer on that complete subclass in every dimension, supplies the exact target value used by the full open problem, classifies all equality families, and adds an optimal quantitative placement deficit. It also cleanly separates the one-dimensional part of the problem from the genuinely difficult interaction between different plank directions.
