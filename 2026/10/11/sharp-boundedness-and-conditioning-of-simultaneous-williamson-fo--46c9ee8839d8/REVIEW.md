# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proposed class, operator normalization, and all quantifiers are explicit. Every admissible normalizer makes \(JC\) similar to \(J(D\oplus D)\); regularizing its polar factor provides a norm bound even when \(C\) is injective but has spectrum accumulating at zero. The concrete multiplier formula and monotone essential-supremum limit give the sharp lower bound. A measurable reciprocal fourth-root squeeze attains that bound exactly when the ratio is essentially bounded. The noncoercive counterexample has positive coefficients almost everywhere. The accompanying finite checker is only supplementary; the proof does not extrapolate finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest full primary text is Kamat--Tonny (arXiv:2610.04583v1, Theorems 2.3, 3.1): simultaneous normal forms exist with an operator explicitly allowed to be unbounded, but neither the full multiplier-class boundedness equivalence nor the sharp infimum over arbitrary normalizers is established there. Bhat--John (arXiv:1804.03921v3, Theorem 4.3/Corollary 4.4) assumes strict positive invertibility; Babu--Mishra (2024, Sections 1--3) concerns finite-dimensional perturbation and orthosymplectic ambiguity. A hidden general condition-number characterization could imply the result; no guarantee of global novelty follows from absence of a match in searches.

## Value — PASS

The noncoercive infinite-dimensional case has a genuine structural dichotomy: even two bounded positive injective symplectically commuting multipliers can have no bounded simultaneous canonical-coordinate change. Where one exists, a sharp all-normalizer distortion, including arbitrary fiber mixing, is determined by an intrinsic stiffness ratio and is independent of how the energy splits between the two forms. This gives a complete quantitative answer for a natural functional-analytic model with atomic or continuous spectrum, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice. Its value does not rely on an unproved extension to general commuting pairs.

## Scientific limitations

No result for arbitrary nonaligned operator pairs, nontrivial common kernels, unbounded quadratic-form operators, or quantum implementability is claimed. Primary-source comparison is substantial but not exhaustive; potential implication from broader symplectic-similarity literature remains a risk. The primary theorem itself is proved analytically; the code checks finite examples only.
