# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The functional characterization of Chmieliński approximate orthogonality turns the optimal reverse parameter into the minimum absolute support-functional value at the reversed pair. Dihedral symmetry reduces the first vector to a smooth point of one edge or an adjacent vertex. For regular \(4m\)-gons, smooth points give residual strictly below \(\tan(\pi/N)\), while vertex Birkhoff sectors consist of two half-edges with support values \(\pm\tan(\pi/N)\); a vertex/perpendicular-edge-midpoint pair attains the bound. For regular \(4m+2\)-gons, the perpendicular of an edge is a vertex whose adjacent support values straddle zero, and the Birkhoff sector of a vertex is a single edge whose support annihilates the vertex. Hence the reverse residual is always zero.

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2020/2021 source was inspected at its arXiv metadata and publisher preview, including the \(C\)-approximate-symmetry framework, primary MSC \(46B20\), and regular-polyhedral context. Targeted searches covered the tangent formula, octagonal and dodecagonal exact values, support-functional equivalents, and the competing Dragomir notion; no covering statement was found. The exact-symmetry parity theorem covers the zero branch only. The 2018 global paper was accessible only at abstract level and remains an explicit residual risk rather than being treated as fully inspected.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives a natural sharp parameter across an infinite classical family, exposes a parity phase transition, identifies equality configurations, and quantifies the approach to Euclidean symmetry. It is stronger than merely showing that some approximation parameter exists.

## Closest literature and limitations
Chmieliński, Khurana and Sain established the relevant \(C\)-approximate-symmetry framework and qualitative polyhedral results. Earlier work of Chmieliński and Wójcik introduced global approximate symmetry and a related constant, while symmetric-point results identify the exact-symmetry parity class. The new content is the sharp positive defect \(\tan(\pi/N)\) for every regular \(4m\)-gon and its unification with the zero branch. The 2018 full text was inaccessible in this run, so an equivalent hidden formulation there remains a bibliographic risk.
