# Sharp complex lower bound for the sixth-order exponential Toeplitz determinant

## Finding

Let
\[
f(z)
=
z+a_3z^3+a_5z^5+\cdots
\]
be an odd normalized analytic function satisfying
\[
\frac{zf'(z)}{f(z)}
=
e^{v(z^2)},
\]
where
\[
v(\zeta)
=
v_1\zeta+v_2\zeta^2+\cdots
\]
is an arbitrary Schwarz function.

For the sixth-order symmetric Toeplitz determinant
\[
T_6(1),
\]
one has the sharp two-sided modulus bounds
\[
\frac{81}{256}
\le
|T_6(1)|
\le
\frac{625}{256}.
\]

The upper constant
\[
\frac{625}{256}
\]
is the sharp complex estimate proved in the cited 2026 source. The new lower bound is sharp as well:
\[
|T_6(1)|
=
\frac{81}{256}
\]
if and only if
\[
v(\zeta)=\zeta
\quad\text{or}\quad
v(\zeta)=-\zeta.
\]

Thus passage from real to complex Schwarz data changes the sharp upper endpoint but not the lower modulus floor. In particular,
\[
T_6(1)\ne0
\]
throughout the complex exponential class.

## Assumptions and scope

The symmetric Toeplitz determinant is the determinant of the matrix generated by the Taylor coefficients of the normalized odd function, with
\[
a_1=1.
\]
The cited source proves that the sixth-order determinant factors as the square of a reduced third-order determinant.

The complex exponential class here means exactly the source's complex extension:
\[
\frac{zf'(z)}{f(z)}
=
e^{v(z^2)}
\]
for an arbitrary Schwarz function
\[
v:\mathbb D\to\mathbb D,
\qquad
v(0)=0.
\]

No real-coefficient assumption is imposed on either
\[
f
\]
or
\[
v.
\]

The result concerns only
\[
T_6(1)
\]
for this exponential class. It does not claim an analogous complex lower bound for the full Janowski family.

## Proof

The source's coefficient calculation gives
\[
a_3=\frac{v_1}{2},
\qquad
a_5=\frac{v_1^2+v_2}{4}.
\]
Its parity factorization gives
\[
T_6(1)=D^2,
\]
where
\[
D
=
(1-a_5)
(1+a_5-2a_3^2).
\]

Put
\[
s=v_1^2
\]
and
\[
w=v_2.
\]
Then
\[
D
=
\frac{
(4-s)^2-w^2
}{16}.
\]

By the standard second-coefficient Schwarz estimate,
\[
|v_2|
\le
1-|v_1|^2.
\]
Since
\[
|s|=|v_1|^2,
\]
we have
\[
|w|
\le
1-|s|.
\]

Set
\[
t=|s|,
\qquad
0\le t\le1.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
16|D|
=
\left|
(4-s)^2-w^2
\right|
\ge
\left|
|4-s|^2-|w|^2
\right|.
\]
Moreover,
\[
|4-s|
\ge
4-|s|
=
4-t,
\]
while
\[
|w|
\le
1-t.
\]
Because
\[
4-t\ge1-t\ge0,
\]
it follows that
\[
16|D|
\ge
(4-t)^2-(1-t)^2.
\]
The right-hand side simplifies to
\[
15-6t,
\]
so
\[
16|D|
\ge
15-6t
\ge
9.
\]
Therefore
\[
|D|
\ge
\frac9{16},
\]
and hence
\[
|T_6(1)|
=
|D|^2
\ge
\frac{81}{256}.
\]

The cited source proves the sharp upper bound
\[
|T_6(1)|
\le
\frac{625}{256}.
\]
This completes the two-sided estimate.

It remains to identify equality in the lower bound. Equality in
\[
15-6t\ge9
\]
forces
\[
t=1.
\]
Then
\[
|w|\le1-t
\]
forces
\[
w=0.
\]
Equality in
\[
|4-s|\ge4-|s|
\]
with
\[
|s|=1
\]
forces
\[
s=1.
\]
Thus
\[
v_1^2=1,
\]
so
\[
v_1=\pm1.
\]
Equality in Schwarz's lemma then gives
\[
v(\zeta)=\pm\zeta.
\]
Conversely, either of these two Schwarz functions gives
\[
s=1,
\qquad
w=0,
\qquad
D=\frac9{16},
\]
and therefore
\[
T_6(1)=\frac{81}{256}.
\]

## Verification

The proof was replayed directly from the source factorization
\[
D
=
\frac{
(4-s)^2-w^2
}{16}
\]
and the Schwarz coefficient constraint
\[
|w|\le1-|s|.
\]

The lower bound uses only two elementary inequalities:
\[
|4-s|\ge4-|s|
\]
and
\[
|A-B|\ge\bigl||A|-|B|\bigr|.
\]
No optimization is hidden in a finite computation.

The equality analysis checks every inequality used in the chain. The terminal condition
\[
|s|=1
\]
forces
\[
w=0,
\]
and the radial triangle equality forces
\[
s=1.
\]
Schwarz's lemma then makes the extremizing Schwarz function linear.

Random admissible pairs
\[
(s,w),
\qquad
|w|\le1-|s|,
\]
were evaluated only as a consistency check. The proof and equality classification are exact and do not depend on sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Mahmood, Rasheed, Mihsin, and Atshan prove the sharp real exponential estimate
\[
\frac{81}{256}
\le
T_6(1)
\le
1
\]
and then treat the complex exponential class separately. Their complex proposition proves the sharp upper modulus estimate
\[
|T_6(1)|
\le
\frac{625}{256},
\]
attained by a purely imaginary linear Schwarz function.

The inspected full article does not state a nonzero complex lower modulus bound. Its abstract emphasizes that the real upper bound fails after complexification, while the complex section ends after proving the sharp upper estimate.

Earlier symmetric Toeplitz work for Ma--Minda, Sakaguchi, and related geometric subclasses treats different determinant orders or different function classes. The targeted searches did not locate a statement implying the complex lower bound above.

The result therefore completes the endpoint picture for the source's complex exponential specialization: the upper endpoint moves from
\[
1
\]
to
\[
\frac{625}{256},
\]
whereas the lower modulus endpoint remains
\[
\frac{81}{256}.
\]

## Limitations

The lower bound is a modulus statement because complex coefficients make
\[
T_6(1)
\]
complex-valued in general.

The argument uses the special exponential coefficient formulas and does not automatically extend to arbitrary Janowski parameters.

The source's sharp upper bound is prior input and is not reproved here.

No assertion is made about higher-order symmetric Toeplitz determinants.
