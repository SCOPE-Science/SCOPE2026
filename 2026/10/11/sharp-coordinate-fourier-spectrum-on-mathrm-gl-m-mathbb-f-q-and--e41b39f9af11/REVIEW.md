# Scientific review: coordinate Fourier spectrum on invertible matrices

## Correctness — PASS
The proof specifies all prime powers, every matrix size at least two, the nontrivial additive character, probability normalization, and the full scalar-coordinate family. The rank-zero/one/two Fourier sums follow by subspace Möbius inversion and agree with Li–Hu, Theorem 2.1. The rook-graph eigenspace decomposition exhausts the coefficient dimension. Explicit determinant factorizations establish positivity, strict eigenvalue ordering and the transition at exactly \(m=2q\). Seven exhaustive finite-field test cases provide independent finite corroboration but are not used as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The closest substantive prior statements are Li–Hu's matrix rank-dependent Gauss sum formula [1], the earlier Delsarte rank-metric scheme [2], and Kawamura's rank-orbit Fourier matrices [3]. Those are stronger **character-transform inputs** but do not state this compressed single-entry frame's eigenspace decomposition or the sharp Riesz-mode switch. Sustik et al. [4] concern harmonic/ETF existence conditions, rather than this constrained restriction. Scientific query comparisons against indexed published findings revealed no exact Gram-spectrum or cutoff statement. An older association-scheme compression giving the same threshold could still exist; index nonappearance is not a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact Riesz spectrum is a canonical stability invariant for the lowest-coordinate Fourier measurements on the nonlinear set of invertible matrices, whose ambient matrix rank-orbit harmonic analysis is classical. The field-dependent, sharp dimension threshold \(m=2q\) changes the worst-conditioned coefficient geometry from zero row/column-sum interactions to a constant/uniform mixed mode; its equality eigenspaces, positive lower bound and asymptotic conditioning rate are all explicit. This is a complete natural-family spectral classification, not a general claim about all Fourier bases.

## Limitations
No full-function Fourier basis, arbitrary coordinate degree, or independent priority review is claimed. Numerical checks are finite; the universal statement rests on the algebraic proof. The 1978 Delsarte full text was not obtained, so equivalence via historical association-scheme specializations remains a residual risk.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
