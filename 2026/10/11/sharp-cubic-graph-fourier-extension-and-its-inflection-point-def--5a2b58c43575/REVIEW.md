The proof explicitly fixes counting input and probability output normalization and uses the exact pair-sum character-orthogonality identity. For nonzero first-coordinate sum, \(x^3+y^3=(x+y)^3-3(x+y)xy\) uniquely determines an unordered pair. For zero sum, all antipodal pairs collide at the same output. Separating that sole exceptional fiber yields a complete fourth-moment identity, not an estimate. The subsequent decomposition into two completed squares, a triangle-inequality deficit and antipodal amplitude deficits has all nonnegative coefficients. Every equality condition is necessary and is simultaneously realized by a function with squared masses in the ratio \(3:2\). The algebra does not rely on experiments, and the polynomial-graph extension is an invertible affine change of coordinates. Direct exact convolution checks over prime fields and a quadratic extension corroborate the proof.

González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva prove exact inequalities for quadratic surfaces and cones, but do not treat this plane cubic graph in their inspected principal results. Published semantic searches for the cubic graph, its pair-sum collision, affine reductions, exact constants and phase/extremizer equations did not locate a dominating theorem.
