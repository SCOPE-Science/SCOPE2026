# Sharp degree spectrum and periodic census for homothetic holomorphic expanding tori

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge1\) and integer \(k\ge2\), a complex torus of complex dimension \(d\) admits a holomorphic expanding group endomorphism of topological degree exactly \(k\). More strongly, this endomorphism can act as an exact homothety of a flat Hermitian metric, with ratio \(k^{1/(2d)}\). Consequently the spectrum of topological degrees of holomorphic expanding maps on compact connected complex manifolds of each fixed positive dimension is precisely \(\{2,3,4,\ldots\}\), even after restricting the examples to holomorphic torus endomorphisms that are metric homotheties.

The construction also gives an exact fixed-point census. For each integer \(n\ge1\), writing \(g=\gcd(n,2d)\),
\[\#\operatorname{Fix}(f_{d,k}^{n})=|1-(-k)^{n/g}|^g.\]
These are all isolated fixed points. This formula is a specific explicit evaluation of the standard determinant formula for torus maps; the realization of *every* degree under simultaneous holomorphicity, arbitrary complex dimension, and exact metric homothety is the principal classification claim.

## Assumptions and scope
A compact complex torus is \((\mathbb R^{2d},J)/\mathbb Z^{2d}\), where \(J\) is a constant real-linear endomorphism with \(J^2=-I\). A flat Hermitian metric means a translation-invariant positive inner product preserved by \(J\). A holomorphic map is expanding if some Riemannian metric and constant \(\lambda>1\) satisfy \(\|Df_x(v)\|\ge\lambda\|v\|\) for every point and tangent vector. Topological degree is the nonnegative number of sheets of an orientation-preserving covering; a holomorphic covering preserves the complex orientation.

For each \(d,k\), take the integral companion matrix \(A\) of \(p(t)=t^{2d}+k\) on \(\mathbb R^{2d}\): \(Ae_j=e_{j+1}\) for \(1\le j<2d\) and \(Ae_{2d}=-k e_1\). Its characteristic polynomial is \(p\), its determinant is \(k\), and \(A^{2d}=-kI\). All its eigenvalues are simple, nonreal, and have absolute value \(r=k^{1/(2d)}>1\).

## Proof
**Complex structure and homothety.** The eigenvalues of the real matrix \(A\) form \(d\) nonreal complex-conjugate pairs. For each pair the real invariant plane admits an oriented real basis in which \(A\) is \(rR_\theta\), an ordinary planar rotation multiplied by \(r\). Define \(J\) on each invariant plane to be the standard quarter-turn; then \(J^2=-I\) and \(AJ=JA\). Declare these real planes pairwise orthogonal, with their displayed bases orthonormal. This gives a positive inner product \(h\) such that \(J\) is orthogonal and \(h(Au,Av)=r^2h(u,v)\). Translations preserve the constant \(J\) and \(h\), so both descend to the quotient \(X=(\mathbb R^{2d},J)/\mathbb Z^{2d}\). Since \(A\) has integral entries, it induces a map \(f:X\to X\), and \(AJ=JA\) makes that map holomorphic. The displayed metric identity gives \(\|Df(v)\|_h=r\|v\|_h\). The covering degree is \([\mathbb Z^{2d}:A\mathbb Z^{2d}]=|\det A|=k\). The choice of complex structure is allowed to vary with \(d,k\); there is no claim on one fixed complex torus.

**Periodic points.** For every \(n\ge1\), no eigenvalue of \(A^n\) is \(1\), since all eigenvalue moduli are \(r^n>1\). Hence fixed points of the induced torus endomorphism correspond exactly to the cosets of \((A^n-I)\mathbb Z^{2d}\), so their number is \(|\det(I-A^n)|\). Set \(m=2d\) and \(g=\gcd(n,m)\). The \(m\) distinct roots of \(z^m=-k\), when raised to the power \(n\), become each root of \(w^{m/g}=(-k)^{n/g}\) exactly \(g\) times. Taking the product of \((1-w)\) over these roots gives \(\det(I-A^n)=(1-(-k)^{n/g})^g\). This proves the displayed integer formula for every \(n\), without finite enumeration hypotheses.

**Necessity of degree at least two.** Let \(F:M\to M\) be any holomorphic expanding self-map of a compact connected complex manifold of dimension \(d\ge1\), with expanding metric \(h\) and \(\lambda>1\). Such a map is a smooth orientation-preserving finite covering of degree \(q\ge1\). Every singular value of its derivative is at least \(\lambda\), hence its positive volume Jacobian satisfies \(\operatorname{Jac}_hF\ge\lambda^{2d}>1\). The change-of-variables identity for a covering yields \(q\operatorname{vol}_h(M)=\int_M\operatorname{Jac}_hF\,d\operatorname{vol}_h\ge\lambda^{2d}\operatorname{vol}_h(M)\), whence integer \(q\ge2\). Together with the construction this proves the exact spectrum.

## Verification
An accompanying pure-Python integer checker constructs the companion matrix, verifies \(A^{2d}=-kI\), \(|\det A|=k\), and the fixed-count determinant identity for a stated finite range. These computations are independent tests, not replacements for the analytic proof above. The derivation via characteristic-polynomial roots and the finite-covering volume formula holds for all \(d,k,n\) in the quantified ranges.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhang, *Holomorphic expanding maps* (arXiv:2610.01525v1), proves a global holomorphic classification by complex infra-nilmanifold models; the present result instead solves a quantitative realization question inside the torus subclass, with arbitrary prescribed integer covering degree and exact homothety. Bauer and Herrig, *Fixed points of endomorphisms on two-dimensional complex tori* (arXiv:1508.05958v1), give the general eigenvalue determinant formula (Proposition 1.1), which fully explains the generic fixed-count mechanism used here. No originality is claimed for that generic mechanism alone. Hughes and de Jong, *Smoothness of Markov Partitions for Expanding Toral Endomorphisms* (arXiv:2604.00257v1), study differentiability of Markov boundaries, not the full degree realization problem. The simple integer companion matrix, its compatible complex structure, and the all-degree homothety classification are the specific content of this finding.

## Limitations
The complex torus and its complex structure depend on the selected degree. The theorem does not classify possible degrees for a *fixed* manifold or fixed complex structure, nor all fixed-point sequences of holomorphic expanding maps. The general determinant-to-periodic-count formula is classical; the special closed form here is a useful explicitly reproducible feature of the constructed realization, not an independent novelty claim. An equivalent all-degree companion construction may occur in older expanding-torus folklore or unindexed literature; literature searches do not prove its absence. The result has not undergone independent validation.
