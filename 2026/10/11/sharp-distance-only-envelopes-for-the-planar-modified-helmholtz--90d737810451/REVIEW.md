# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The upper estimate is an exact domain-comparison argument with the centered-disk Bessel solution, and the lower estimate is an exact comparison with the exponential solution determined by a supporting half-plane. The strong maximum principle gives the upper equality classification and strict lower inequality. Tangent disks provide an explicit lower-extremizing sequence, with the standard modified-Bessel asymptotic giving the limit.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating Nazarov paper proves a convexity criterion through the gradient bound and uses the half-plane barrier, but does not state the optimal pointwise envelope over all convex domains. Kuznetsov records the half-plane and disk model solutions and a large-parameter distance asymptotic, but not the finite-parameter extremal optimization or unique upper equality case. Targeted semantic and primary-literature searches under modified-Helmholtz, Yukawa, panharmonic, Bessel, distance-to-boundary, half-plane, and disk formulations did not locate an implication-equivalent theorem.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural complete extremal problem: it identifies the best possible pointwise information obtainable from boundary distance alone, classifies the unique upper extremizer, and identifies a sharp nonattained lower envelope. It gives a finite-parameter refinement of the distance asymptotic and organizes the canonical disk and half-plane model solutions into one optimal theorem rather than a routine parameter substitution.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Nazarov's 2026 convexity criterion, Kuznetsov's 2024 modified-Helmholtz convexity paper, and panharmonic/Yukawa potential work giving ball formulas. The result is planar and convex-domain specific. An equivalent elementary comparison theorem could exist in older potential-theory literature under different terminology.
