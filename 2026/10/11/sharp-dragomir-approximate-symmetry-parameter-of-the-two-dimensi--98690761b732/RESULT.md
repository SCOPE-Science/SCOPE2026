# Sharp Dragomir approximate-symmetry parameter of the two-dimensional Cesàro plane
## Finding
Let \(X=\mathrm{ces}_2^{(2)}\) be the real plane with
\[
\|(x,y)\|_X=
\left(x^2+\left(\frac{|x|+|y|}{2}\right)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
For \(\varepsilon\in[0,1)\), write \(u\perp_D^\varepsilon v\) when
\[
\|u+\lambda v\|_X\ge \sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\,\|u\|_X
\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
\]
Then the least \(\varepsilon\) for which \(x\perp_B y\) implies \(y\perp_D^\varepsilon x\) for all \(x,y\in X\) is
\[
\varepsilon_D(X)=\sqrt{\frac38}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\inf_{\substack{x,y\in S_X\\x\perp_B y}}\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}\|y+\lambda x\|_X=\sqrt{\frac58}.
\]
After the linear normalization \(M(s,t)=\sqrt{s^2+t^2+2|st|/\sqrt5}\), equality is attained by
\[
x=(1,0),\qquad y=\frac{(1/\sqrt5,1)}{\sqrt{8/5}},
\]
and its norm symmetries.

## Assumptions and scope
All scalars are real. Birkhoff--James orthogonality means
\[
x\perp_B y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\|x+\lambda y\|\ge\|x\|\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
\]
The approximate relation is
\[
u\perp_D^\varepsilon v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\|u+\lambda v\|\ge\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\,\|u\|
\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R).
\]
The theorem determines the best global parameter for the real two-dimensional exponent-\(2\) Cesàro plane. It does not address the distinct Chmieliński approximate relation.

## Proof
The Cesàro norm satisfies
\[
4\|(x,y)\|_X^2=5x^2+2|xy|+y^2.
\]
A linear coordinate change and constant rescaling reduce the problem to
\[
M(s,t)=\sqrt{s^2+t^2+2c|st|},\qquad c=\frac1{\sqrt5}.
\]
Since \(M(v)\ge\|v\|_2\), \(M\)-distance from a point to a line is at least Euclidean distance.

For unit \(x,y\) with \(x\perp_B y\), define
\[
d(x,y)=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}M(y+\lambda x).
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt{1-\varepsilon_D(X)^2}
=
\inf_{\substack{x,y\in S_M\\x\perp_B y}}d(x,y).
\]

Away from the coordinate axes the sphere is smooth. By sign and coordinate-swap symmetries, every smooth point is represented by
\[
x_z=\frac{(1,z)}{\sqrt{A(z)}},\qquad
A(z)=1+z^2+2cz,\qquad0<z\le1.
\]
The active quadratic form has gradient proportional to \((1+cz,z+c)\), hence its unique Birkhoff direction is generated by
\[
w_z=(-(z+c),1+cz).
\]
Put
\[
B(z)=M(w_z)^2
=
\frac8{25}\left(5z^2+4\sqrt5\,z+5\right),
\qquad
y_z=\frac{w_z}{\sqrt{B(z)}}.
\]
The Euclidean distance from \(y_z\) to the line spanned by \(x_z\) is
\[
\frac{A(z)}{\sqrt{B(z)(1+z^2)}}.
\]
The exact identity
\[
A(z)^2-\frac58B(z)(1+z^2)=\frac45z^2
\]
therefore gives
\[
d(x_z,y_z)\ge\sqrt{\frac58}.
\]

Now take a nonsmooth axis point \(e_1=(1,0)\). One-sided derivatives of
\[
\lambda\longmapsto M(e_1+\lambda(a,b))^2
\]
give
\[
e_1\perp_B(a,b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|a|\le c|b|.
\]
After scaling, every unit Birkhoff direction has the form
\[
y_r=\frac{(r,1)}{\sqrt{1+r^2+2c|r|}},
\qquad |r|\le c.
\]
Its Euclidean distance from the \(e_1\)-axis is
\[
\frac1{\sqrt{1+r^2+2c|r|}}
\ge
\frac1{\sqrt{1+3c^2}}
=
\sqrt{\frac58},
\]
so its \(M\)-distance is at least the same value.

For \(r=c\), let
\[
y_c=\frac{(c,1)}{\sqrt{8/5}}.
\]
Choosing \(\lambda=-(y_c)_1\) gives
\[
y_c+\lambda e_1=\left(0,\sqrt{\frac58}\right),
\]
whose \(M\)-norm is exactly \(\sqrt{5/8}\). Hence the sharp reverse line-distance is \(\sqrt{5/8}\), and
\[
\varepsilon_D(X)=\sqrt{1-\frac58}=\sqrt{\frac38}.
\]

## Verification
The proof exhausts all smooth points and all coordinate-axis points. At a smooth point the Birkhoff direction is the kernel of the unique norming functional; the displayed determinant/Euclidean-distance computation gives the exact lower bound. At an axis the complete Birkhoff cone follows from both one-sided derivatives, and a boundary direction realizes equality.

No numerical optimizer, finite sampling, timeout, or external certificate establishes the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Maligranda, Petrot, and Suantai study the identical two-dimensional Cesàro plane and calculate its classical James constant. Their paper predates the approximate-symmetry framework used here and does not discuss approximate Birkhoff--James symmetry.

Chmieliński and Wójcik study global approximate symmetry of Birkhoff orthogonality and introduce a related geometric constant. Chmieliński, Khurana, and Sain later distinguish the Chmieliński and modified Dragomir approximate relations and prove that every finite-dimensional Banach space is globally approximately symmetric in the modified Dragomir sense. Their inspected full text contains no Cesàro example and does not determine this sharp parameter.

Targeted searches using the Cesàro notation, the normalized absolute-quadratic norm, modified Dragomir terminology, and the radical \(\sqrt{3/8}\) did not locate an equivalent exact evaluation.

## Limitations
The result uses the modified Dragomir normalization \(\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\). It does not calculate the optimal parameter for the distinct Chmieliński relation or for higher-dimensional Cesàro spaces.

The full text of the 2018 global approximate-symmetry paper was not available through the checked open route; a differently notated calculation remains a bibliographic risk.
