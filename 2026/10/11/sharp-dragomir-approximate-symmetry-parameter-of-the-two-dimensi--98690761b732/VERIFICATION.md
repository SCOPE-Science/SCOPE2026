# Verification

After normalization,
\[
M(s,t)^2=s^2+t^2+\frac{2}{\sqrt5}|st|.
\]
At smooth points \(x_z=(1,z)/\sqrt{A(z)}\), the unique Birkhoff direction is generated by
\[
w_z=\left(-z-\frac1{\sqrt5},1+\frac z{\sqrt5}\right).
\]
With
\[
B(z)=M(w_z)^2=\frac8{25}(5z^2+4\sqrt5\,z+5),
\]
the squared Euclidean distance of \(w_z/\sqrt{B(z)}\) from the line through \(x_z\) is
\[
\frac{A(z)^2}{B(z)(1+z^2)}.
\]
The identity
\[
A(z)^2-\frac58B(z)(1+z^2)=\frac45z^2
\]
proves this is at least \(5/8\), and \(M\)-distance is no smaller than Euclidean distance.

At \(e_1=(1,0)\), Birkhoff directions are exactly those with
\[
|a|\le\frac{|b|}{\sqrt5}.
\]
Their unit Euclidean distance from the \(e_1\)-axis is at least \(\sqrt{5/8}\). The boundary direction \((1/\sqrt5,1)\), after normalization, attains equality because a horizontal translate lands on the other coordinate axis.

Thus the sharp reverse distance is \(\sqrt{5/8}\), and the least modified-Dragomir parameter is \(\sqrt{3/8}\). No numerical optimization is used.
