# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The unique \(\ell_p\) nearest-point map is obtained by a KKT displacement that is uniform across positive coordinates for every \(1<p<\infty\). Every active face gives an actual full-dimensional affine Jacobian, and the global \(\ell_4\) Lipschitz constant equals the largest norm of its linear mean-centering block. The externally established symmetric-quartic half-degree theorem reduces the resulting norm inequality to two-level coordinate vectors; two-variable Hölder duality and a one-variable derivative complete the proof. Exact symbolic identities and finite samples have been reproduced from the attached checker; these are not treated as proofs of the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with access risk

The full primary simplex projection paper [1] provides the soft-threshold map but not its sharp \(\ell_4\) Lipschitz constant. The full quartic symmetry paper [2] supplies an essential reduction but no simplex stability theorem. The closest plausible prior [3] treats linear hyperplane projections and may cover the constituent centering norm, yet its full text was unavailable; no claim that this paper is definitely noncovering is made. Searches for the nonlinear map, its analytic constant, and equivalent operator-norm statements did not identify a published exact match. Unindexed sources could alter this originality assessment.

## Value — PASS

An exact stability constant is directly motivated by perturbation sensitivity of the standard probability-simplex retraction. The all-dimensional formula, sharp dimension-free envelope, strict finite-dimensional gap, and independence of the best-approximation exponent provide substantive information not conveyed by the algorithm alone. The linear mean-centering subproblem is not asserted to be independently novel.

## Scope and limitations

Real coordinates, finite simplex dimension, \(\ell_4\) measurement, and \(1<p<\infty\) nearest-point minimization only. The result is not a general formula for arbitrary measurement exponents. Primary source [3] was only partially accessible, leaving a concrete bibliographic risk.
