# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every maximal fitted block is the scalar \(p\)-mean of its observations. Below the Hilbert exponent the scalar \(p\)-mean is \(\ell_\infty\)-nonexpansive, providing the upper \(n^{1/p}\) bound; above it the balanced residual powers force all scalar derivative weights to be at most \(1/2\), giving the sharp \((n/2)^{1/p}\) bound. A continuous finite selection of blockwise maps inherits their Lipschitz bound. Strictly complementary full pooling realizes the two lower mechanisms. Ordinary Hilbert projection and the two-coordinate arithmetic mean resolve the remaining cases. The proof establishes all real inputs and all admitted dimensions, and is not based on numerical experiments.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk

The closest full-text primary sources are Luss--Rosset (arXiv:1104.1779), Németh--Németh (arXiv:1503.08458), and Kyng--Rao--Sachdeva (arXiv:1507.00710). The first provides general convex block/KKT structure; the second covers order-preserving Euclidean cone projections; the third supplies all-exponent algorithms and error guarantees. None of their inspected theorems determines the exact input-data Lipschitz norm or its p-dependent sharp boundary. A historical article on best monotone \(L^p\) approximation, Swetits--Weinstein (1990), was not available as complete primary text; a corresponding prior theorem cannot be excluded solely on its abstract. Search results have limited coverage and do not certify novelty.

## Value — PASS

The classification computes the full sharp robustness profile of a standard shape-constrained best-approximation mapping, across all finite lengths and strictly convex exponents. Its sharp discontinuous transition at \(p=2\) and the different lower-bound mechanisms are geometric facts that are not consequences of algorithmic solvability alone. The statement excludes weights, general DAGs, infinite-dimensional cones, and endpoint exponents.
