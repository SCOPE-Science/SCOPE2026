# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof identifies all polar vertices using the rank/connected-tight-graph criterion, proves their integrality relative to the hub, gives an exhaustive four-state path automaton including adjacent pairs of zeros, and derives the centered squared norms without hidden normalization factors. The global maximum is determined by separating both-sign, one-sign, and zero-count cases; equality patterns are explicitly enumerated for every integer \(m\ge3\). The antipodal supporting inequalities establish the largest ball among all centers, not only central candidates. The contact-normal kernel argument proves the affine dimension of the *entire* center set. Exact rational independent finite tests are consistent for \(m=2,\ldots,10\) but are not relied upon as an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS with bounded residual risk.** Published primary work includes general symmetric edge polytope descriptions (D’Alì–Delucchi–Michałek), a maximal-bipartite-subgraph facet correspondence (Chen–Davis–Korchevskaia), and a connected-tight-edge description (Braun–Bruegge). Those supply the starting framework, not the fan-family Euclidean spectrum, closed inradius, complete contact classes or optimal-center dimensions. Actual searches tested fan/suspension aliases, discrete Lipschitz variance and broader facet formulas; no inspected source implied this package's full claim. Graph-Lipschitz extremal literature could contain an equivalent formulation, so absolute priority is not claimed.

## Value
**PASS.** Fan graphs are a canonical graph join with a path, not an arbitrary isolated construction. The distance spectrum is a complete metric invariant of *all* facets, compressed into a four-state polynomial transfer law. The parity discontinuity in closest facets and dimension of the entire incenter locus explains a geometric phenomenon inaccessible from facet counts or lattice equivalence alone. It is an infinite structural classification rather than a small computational increment.
