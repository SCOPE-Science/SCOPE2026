# Review

## Correctness
PASS. For every source column and every weaving block, the relative hypothesis gives a two-way Hilbert direct-sum coefficient norm comparison. The triangular estimate with factor \(1+\sqrt K\), squared and summed over the blocks, proves
\[
(1+\sqrt K)^{-2}S_j\preceq S_\sigma\preceq(1+\sqrt K)^2S_j.
\]
All sums exist by the Bessel assumptions. A single scalar index attains both sides and the frame bounds exactly for every \(K\ge0\) and finite \(m\ge2\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest exact theorem supplies an \(m\)-dependent lower weaving constant and an aggregate Bessel upper constant, rather than the all-anchor positive-order comparison. Its later module counterpart and the earlier synthesis-perturbation, operator-convergence and woven Parseval-identity literature were compared by hypotheses and implications. No checked source states the sharp factor and its simultaneous extremal family. An earlier implicit derivation remains possible.

## Value
PASS. The result gives sharp spectral equivalence and condition-number control across every assignment of sensors to finitely many source frames. The constants do not grow with the number of sources. Such quantitative operator equivalence is stronger than merely knowing that each assignment admits stable recovery and is directly relevant to uniform preconditioning.

## Closest literature and limitations
Li--Leng--Huang--Li's Theorem 3.5 and Rajput--Sahu--Mishra's Theorem 2.7 are the closest quantitative predecessors. Bemrose et al. address different synthesis perturbation metrics. Li--Zhu study convergence of already-woven frame operators, and Xu--Leng study separate weaving identities.

The new theorem requires the *all-subsets* relative coefficient hypothesis and is for ordinary Hilbert-space \(g\)-frames, not unrestricted Hilbert \(C^*\)-modules. The factor is universally sharp; some particular families have better constants.
