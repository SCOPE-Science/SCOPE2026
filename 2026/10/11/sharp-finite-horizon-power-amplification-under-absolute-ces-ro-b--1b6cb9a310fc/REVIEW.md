# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For each orbit, the hypothesis at all trailing time windows gives a positive-ratio recurrence for consecutive sums. Its product proves the universal bound including zero orbit terms, without divisibility hypotheses. For the explicit positive shift, the recursion for the complete orbit sum is exact; monotone finite orbit values and zero continuation establish every Cesàro inequality, which extends coordinatewise by Tonelli to arbitrary \(\ell^p\) vectors. The value \(K\) is attained on \(e_1\) at horizon two. Nonincreasing weights force all powers to have maximal product at the smallest indices, proving equality at every horizon. Exact rational checks in the accompanying script support normalizations only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The exact finite-horizon operator-norm envelope is not supplied by the classical polynomial estimate in Cohen–Cuny–Eisner–Lin, by the weighted-shift characterization and examples of Abbar–Arnold–Coine, or by Arnold's sharp \(q\)-self-improvement theorem. The known exponent is explicitly credited to prior work; the added content is the exact universal coefficient at each horizon and a common positive extremizer. Indexed published mathematics on \(q\)-endpoint feasibility and weak moments concerns distinct quantities. An unindexed earlier discrete extremal inequality could be equivalent, so independent literature verification remains valuable.

## Value — PASS
The claim turns qualitative power-growth estimates into a sharp usable bound for every finite observation horizon, with simultaneous attainment by a natural positive operator. Its Gamma asymptotic gives the exact universal leading coefficient and distinguishes genuinely worst-case transient amplification from a mere bound of the correct order. The result applies on arbitrary Banach spaces, not solely a selected shift or a finite list of numerical cases.
