# Review of finite-moment ellipsoid section reconstruction

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact codimension-one Jacobian under a diagonal linear map gives the inverse-square section volume as a spherical quadratic form. The half-parameter Dirichlet moment identity factorizes its generating function; formal inversion recovers the positive spectral roots and all semiaxes. The local minimality argument perturbs one freely variable elementary symmetric coefficient while preserving distinct positive roots. The separate known-volume cutoff follows by fixing the product. Exact arithmetic tests in dimensions two through five and an independently formed plane-restriction calculation in three dimensions support these steps, but are not substitutes for the analytic argument.

**Originality — PASS, with historical risk.** The closest full-text paper, Kim–Yaskin–Zvavitch (arXiv:1512.07289), already defines the same distributional section data and proves ellipsoid volume comparison, but does not derive a finite moment count for complete ellipsoid axis recovery or prove sharp insufficiency at one fewer moment. Its results on arbitrary convex bodies do not imply an optimal finite cutoff within ellipsoids. Hillier (2001) studies a related forward spherical quadratic-form density; its full text was not available for a complete historical exclusion, leaving a genuine residual risk. Møller (1988) uses three correlated parallel sections, not direction-forgotten random central sections. Unindexed classical moment-inversion formulas may also overlap. The explicit cutoffs, rather than the trivial observation that the full distribution describes a quadratic form, are the stated claim.

**Value — PASS.** The inverse problem asks how much direction-free section data suffice to identify an ellipsoid. An exact sufficient-and-necessary number of scalar population moments, in every dimension, is a natural finite-data invariant; the proof explicitly handles all axis multiplicities for recovery and gives matching generic lower bounds. The known-volume refinement separates scale information from anisotropy. No extension to nonellipsoidal bodies, noisy samples, or stable algorithms is claimed.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
