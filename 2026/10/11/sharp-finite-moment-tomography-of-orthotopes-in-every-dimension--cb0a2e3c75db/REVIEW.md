# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any positive orthotope facet-area tuple, the projection-volume identity is \(B(U)=\sum_iq_i|U_i|\). Gaussian polar decomposition makes the weighted sum of independent half-normal variables recoverable from spherical moments. The half-normal cumulant at every positive integer order is a nonzero rational polynomial in \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\), with nonzero highest-degree coefficient. Transcendence of \(\pi\) rules out all zeros and establishes the critical nonstandard lemma without extrapolation from finite experiments. Cumulant additivity and Newton identities then recover all facet areas. Constant-term perturbations prove sharp moment minimality; Rouché and an exact near-cube perturbation prove the \(1/d\) upper and lower stability exponents. These are analytic proofs; standalone numerical diagnostics are supplementary.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical uncertainty

Paouris–Pivovarov–Zinn (arXiv:1210.7012v1) study cube-projection central limit laws, not finite-moment reconstruction of variable-sided orthotopes. Kim–Yaskin–Zvavitch (arXiv:1512.07289v1) study unlabeled projection distributions and unrestricted-body failures of determination. Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets (arXiv:1511.08479v1, Section 3) establish higher moment methods for random **line widths** of regular polytopes and thus cover foundational Gaussian moment techniques and regular cases. The present claim does not reassert those known special cases: it establishes all-dimensional exact finite moment reconstruction, sharp number of moments, sharp inverse instability and verifies every needed half-normal cumulant is nonzero by a transcendence argument. The targeted source and alias searches and available full-text comparisons did not find this whole classification; equivalent earlier inverse-statistics literature remains possible.

## Value — PASS

A natural geometric tomography experiment reports projection sizes without directions. General convex bodies are not identifiable from these data, while arbitrary-dimensional orthotopes are a canonical meaningful restricted class. The theorem gives a dimension-exact minimal population-data requirement together with the precise unavoidable instability at equal-sided bodies. The all-order half-normal cumulant nonvanishing mechanism explains why finite identifiability persists in every dimension, despite higher cumulant sign changes. This is a structural inverse theorem, not a numerical table or a restatement of ordinary mean shadow width.

## Limits and risk

Positive finite side lengths and exact population moments are required; constants depend on dimension and compact side intervals. No empirical-noise or general-parallelotope reconstruction result is proved. The spherical Gaussian correspondence and root perturbation are classical; only the combined all-dimensional identification and sharpness are assessed. Historical overlap outside inspected literature cannot be excluded.
