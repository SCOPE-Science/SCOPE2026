# Review of Sharp finite-order coordinate Sidon constants

## Correctness
**PASS.** The denominator is exactly the circumradius of the Minkowski sum of the scaled regular \(m\)-gons. Minkowski perimeter additivity gives \(2m\sin(\pi/m)\sum_j|a_j|\), while the sum has at most \(mn\) sides. The sharp perimeter bound for a convex polygon in a radius-\(R\) disk then yields the stated constant. Equality forces a regular \(mn\)-gon, which in turn forces equal coefficient moduli and the stated interlacing of orientations. The proof treats every \(m\ge3\) and \(n\ge2\); finite enumeration is only a check.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest inspected literature gives qualitative or asymptotic Sidon information rather than this finite-order finite-size formula. Bourgain--Lewko prove general Sidonicity/Rademacher-Sidon statements, Hare--Ramsey relate finite-root Kronecker approximation to Sidonicity, and Seigner determines the infinite order-two Rademacher constant. Cartwright--Howlett--McMullen treat general extreme Sidon constants, but the available statement does not imply this intermediate coordinate-set value or equality class. Searches for the exact trigonometric formula, finite-order independent characters, cyclic coordinate characters, and the regular-\(mn\)-gon formulation found no covering published published-finding corpus record or primary statement. Residual risk remains for an older equivalent convex-geometric formulation.

## Value
**PASS.** Finite-order independent characters are a basic torsion model in abstract harmonic analysis. The result supplies an exact constant and all extremizers for every finite size and every order \(m\ge3\), and cleanly interpolates between a nontrivial fixed-order asymptotic constant \((\pi/m)/\sin(\pi/m)\) and the continuous-phase limit \(1\). The regular-polygon mechanism makes the dependence on both order and cardinality transparent rather than merely giving a bound.

## Closest literature and limitations
The theorem does not treat arbitrary Sidon sets, mixed character orders, dependencies, or the order-two case. The main originality risk is terminological: the same identity could conceivably occur in older planar vector-balancing or Minkowski-sum literature without being indexed as a Sidon-constant computation.

Same-model review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
