# Sharp finite-sample normalization of halfsphere-polytope facet deficits

## Finding

For every integer \(d\ge 2\) and \(n\ge d+1\), let \(P_{d,n}\) be the spherical convex hull of \(n\) independent uniform points in a fixed closed halfsphere of \(\mathbb S^d\), and put \(F_{d,n}=\mathbb E[f_{d-1}(P_{d,n})]\). Define
\[
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi^{k/2}}{\Gamma(k/2)},\qquad L_d=\frac{2\omega_d}{\omega_{d+1}}\frac{\pi^d}{d},\qquad
R_{d,n}=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{d(d+1)}\left(1-\frac{F_{d,n}}{L_d}\right).
\]
**The normalized facet deficit \(R_{d,n}\) is strictly increasing in the sample size \(n\)**, with the exact endpoints
\[
R_{d,d+1}=\frac{(d+2)(d+3)}{d(d+1)}\left(1-\frac{d+1}{L_d}\right),
\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty} R_{d,n}=\frac{(d-1)\pi^2}{6}.
\]
Equivalently, the following bounds are sharp in the indicated global senses:
\[
\frac{d(d+1)R_{d,d+1}}{(n+1)(n+2)}
\le 1-\frac{F_{d,n}}{L_d}
<\frac{d(d+1)(d-1)\pi^2}{6(n+1)(n+2)}.
\]
The left equality holds precisely at \(n=d+1\). The right coefficient cannot be decreased while retaining the bound for all \(n\), since the right-hand ratio approaches equality as \(n\to\infty\). This theorem concerns a strictly increasing *scaled error*, not the previously established monotonicity of the unscaled expectation.

## Assumptions and scope

The sphere \(\mathbb S^d\) is the Euclidean unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^{d+1}\). The halfsphere is any closed set cut out by a hyperplane through the origin. All sampled points have the rotation-invariant normalized surface-area distribution on that halfsphere, independently. Spherical convex hull means intersection of the unit sphere with the positive cone generated by the samples. Almost surely, \(P_{d,n}\) is a spherical \(d\)-polytope. Facets are its \((d-1)\)-dimensional faces. The integer dimension \(d\) is fixed when discussing monotonicity in \(n\); the result holds separately for every \(d\ge2\). The preexisting value \(L_d=2^{-d}d!\kappa_d^2\), with \(\kappa_d=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(1+d/2)\), is the limiting expected facet count.

## Proof

**1. Transform the exact expectation.** The facet-integral identity of Bárány–Hug–Reitzner–Schneider [1, Theorem 3.1] is
\[
F_{d,n}=\frac{2\omega_d}{\omega_{d+1}}\binom nd
\int_0^\pi\left(1-\frac{x}{\pi}\right)^{n-d}\sin^{d-1}x\,dx.
\]
Let \(U_n\sim\operatorname{Beta}(d,n-d+1)\) and \(q(z)=\sin(\pi\sqrt z)/(\pi\sqrt z)\) for \(0<z\le1\), with \(q(0)=1\). Substitution \(x=\pi u\) and \(\binom nd B(d,n-d+1)=1/d\) give the exact probability identity
\[
\frac{F_{d,n}}{L_d}=\mathbb E\left[q(U_n^2)^{d-1}\right].
\]
Define \(h_d(z)=(1-q(z)^{d-1})/z\) for \(0<z\le1\), extended continuously to \(h_d(0)=(d-1)\pi^2/6\). The beta second moment is
\[
\mathbb E[U_n^2]=\frac{d(d+1)}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
\]
Multiplication of a beta density by \(u^2\) changes its first shape parameter by two. Consequently,
\[
R_{d,n}=\mathbb E\left[h_d(V_n^2)\right],
\qquad V_n\sim\operatorname{Beta}(d+2,n-d+1).
\]

**2. Strict decrease of the deficit kernel.** The integral representation
\[
q(z)=\int_0^1\cos(\pi t\sqrt z)\,dt
\]
shows strict convexity on \((0,1)\). For \(a>0\), its integrand has the second derivative
\[
\frac{d^2}{dz^2}\cos(a\sqrt z)=\frac{a}{4z^{3/2}}
\bigl(\sin(a\sqrt z)-a\sqrt z\cos(a\sqrt z)\bigr).
\]
For \(0<a\le\pi\), \(0<z<1\), the bracket is strictly positive: \(\sin y-y\cos y\) vanishes at zero and has derivative \(y\sin y>0\) on \((0,\pi)\). The integrand is bounded near \(z=0\), allowing differentiation under the integral. Thus \(q\) is strictly convex; moreover \(q\ge0\) on \([0,1]\). Because \(d-1\ge1\), \(q^{d-1}\) is strictly convex there. Hence \(g_d(z)=1-q(z)^{d-1}\) is strictly concave with \(g_d(0)=0\). Its secant slope \(h_d(z)=g_d(z)/z\) is strictly decreasing for \(0<z\le1\). The limit at zero follows from \(q(z)=1-\pi^2z/6+O(z^2)\); \(h_d\) is continuous and bounded on \([0,1]\).

**3. Strict increase with sample size.** Holding the first beta shape parameter fixed, increasing the second from \(n-d+1\) to \(n-d+2\) strictly shifts the law left in first-order stochastic order. One direct proof uses the ratio of successive densities, proportional to \(1-v\), which is strictly decreasing in \(v\) and integrates to one after normalization. Since \(h_d(v^2)\) is strictly decreasing in \(v\in(0,1)\), the expectation \(R_{d,n}\) strictly increases. Also \(\mathbb E[V_n]=(d+2)/(n+3)\to0\), so bounded convergence in probability yields \(R_{d,n}\to h_d(0)\).

**4. Sharp endpoints.** With exactly \(d+1\) generic sampled points, the spherical polytope is a simplex almost surely and has exactly \(d+1\) facets, giving the displayed \(R_{d,d+1}\). Strict increase makes this the unique minimum. Its limiting value is the least upper bound of all the normalized deficits, establishing both strict upper bound and optimality of its coefficient.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` requires Python 3 with `mpmath`. It independently integrates the original published facet formula and the transformed beta formula at 55-digit working precision, then checks agreement, monotonicity, the initial simplex endpoint, and both proposed bounds for dimensions \(2\) through \(9\) and eighteen consecutive sample sizes in each dimension. The run printed `VERIFY_OK` for 144 dimension/sample pairs, with maximum absolute disagreement about \(4.18\cdot10^{-53}\). A further diagnostic tests positivity of the sinc-square-root second derivative at 99 interior points. These are finite diagnostic checks, **not** the proof of the universal quantifiers; the analytic argument above supplies that proof. Run with `python verify.py` from a directory containing the file.

## Relationship to prior work

Bárány, Hug, Reitzner and Schneider [1] proved the exact expected facet integral, its finite limit, and its leading \(n^{-2}\) asymptotic deficit. Bonnet and collaborators [2, Theorem 8] proved that the **unscaled** expected facet number increases in broad distribution families including the projected halfsphere case. Kabluchko [3] obtained exact combinatorial expectations for every face dimension. None of these inspected results asserts the present strict monotonicity of the second-order *normalized error* or its two simultaneously sharp global bounds. The exact expectation alone supplies numerical evaluations but does not immediately imply the parameter-ordering inequality; the new proof introduces the strictly decreasing convexity-deficit kernel and its beta-tilted stochastic ordering.

## Limitations

The statement is for uniform independent samples from an **exact halfsphere** and fixed dimension, not general hemispherical densities, proper caps, nonuniform sampling, or joint fluctuations. It relies on the exact expectation theorem [1], rather than deriving that integral from first principles. The sharpness at infinity is for each fixed \(d\); no dimension-uniform limiting exchange is asserted. Priority assessment of older equivalent inequalities cannot exclude unindexed presentations or equivalent results hidden within special-function identities. Verification is same-model only; no independent audit has been performed.
