# Sharp Fourier extension and phase transitions under arbitrary erasures of finite-field conics

## Finding

Let \(q\ge 3\) be an odd prime power and let \(\delta\in\mathbb F_q^\times\). Consider the affine conic
\[
C_\delta=\{u=(x,y)\in\mathbb F_q^2:Q(u)=x^2-\delta y^2=1\}.
\]
For an arbitrary nonempty retained set \(A\subseteq C_\delta\), let \(m=|A|\) and let \(p\) be the number of intact antipodal pairs \(\{u,-u\}\subseteq A\). The sharp counting-measure fourth-power extension inequality, using the fixed ambient normalization described below, is
\[
\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb F_q^2}|\mathcal E_Af(\xi)|^4
\le c(m,p)\left(q^{-1}\sum_{u\in A}|f(u)|^2\right)^2,
\qquad
c(m,p)=\begin{cases}2-m^{-1},&p\le1,\\3-\frac{3}{2p},&p\ge2.\end{cases}
\]
Its complete nonzero equality family is as follows. For \(p\le1\), the \(m\) coefficients have the same positive modulus and *arbitrary* phases. For \(p\ge2\), every unpaired point has coefficient zero; coefficients at the \(2p\) paired points have the same positive modulus; and the products \(f(u)f(-u)\) are one common complex number independent of the intact pair. Every such configuration is extremal.

Set \(h=|C_\delta|/2\). We have \(h=(q-1)/2\) when \(\delta\) is square and \(h=(q+1)/2\) when it is nonsquare. At fixed retained cardinality \(1\le m\le2h\), **every** integer
\[
\max(0,m-h)\le p\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor
\]
occurs. Consequently the displayed formula is the exact attainable spectrum of sharp extension constants across all erasure masks of size \(m\). In particular, the smallest and largest possible constants are obtained by evaluating \(c(m,p)\) at the lower and upper endpoint, respectively. A second intact pair triggers a discontinuous change: when \(p\ge2\), all unmatched surviving points are extinguished by every extremizer.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(\psi:\mathbb F_q\to\mathbb C\) be any nontrivial additive character. Define
\[
\mathcal E_Af(\xi)=\frac1q\sum_{u\in A}f(u)\psi(\xi_1u_1+\xi_2u_2).
\]
The counting measure on \(\xi\in\mathbb F_q^2\) and the displayed \(q^{-1}\)-normalization on retained frequencies are essential. Functions are arbitrary complex-valued assignments to \(A\), including zeros. The theorem does not assume \(\delta\) nonsquare, primality of \(q\), a special deletion pattern, or a prescribed number of erasures. The empty retained set is excluded. The count of antipodal pairs is well-defined because \(Q(u)=1\) implies \(u\ne0\), and odd characteristic gives \(u\ne-u\).

## Proof

Write the polarization \(B(u,v)=u_1v_1-\delta u_2v_2\) and \(Q(u)=B(u,u)\). First, on the conic, **every nonzero sum has a unique unordered representation**. Indeed, for \(u+v=s\ne0\), put \(d=u-v\), so \(u=(s+d)/2\), \(v=(s-d)/2\). The equations \(Q(u)=Q(v)=1\) are equivalent to
\[
B(s,d)=0,\qquad Q(d)=4-Q(s).
\]
If \(Q(s)\ne0\), the orthogonal complement of \(s\) is a one-dimensional nondegenerate quadratic space; its prescribed norm equation has at most two solutions \(d\) and \(-d\), defining the same unordered pair. If \(Q(s)=0\), the orthogonal complement is the isotropic line generated by \(s\), and then \(Q(d)=0\) contradicts \(Q(d)=4\). Thus no two distinct unordered pairs have the same nonzero sum. The zero sum consists exactly of ordered pairs \((u,-u)\) from the \(p\) intact antipodal pairs.

Write \(w_u=|f(u)|^2\), \(S=\sum_{u\in A}w_u\), and \(z_j=f(u_j)f(-u_j)\) for chosen representatives of the \(p\) intact pairs. Let
\[
T(f)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_q^2}\left|\sum_{u+v=s\atop u,v\in A}f(u)f(v)\right|^2.
\]
Every unequal unordered pair contributes four times the product of squared moduli to its nonzero fiber, and a diagonal pair contributes its fourth power. Correcting the zero fiber yields the exact identity
\[
T(f)=2S^2-\sum_{u\in A}w_u^2
+4\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^p z_j\right|^2-\sum_{j=1}^p|z_j|^2\right)
=2S^2-\sum_u w_u^2+8\operatorname{Re}\sum_{1\le i<j\le p}z_i\overline{z_j}.
\]
Character orthogonality gives \(\sum_\xi|\mathcal E_Af(\xi)|^4=q^{-2}T(f)\), while \((q^{-1}\sum|f|^2)^2=q^{-2}S^2\). Thus it remains to maximize \(T(f)/S^2\) over nonzero \(f\).

When \(p\le1\), the interaction term is empty. Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum w_u^2\ge S^2/m\), with equality exactly when all \(w_u=S/m>0\). This gives \(c(m,p)=2-1/m\), with unrestricted coefficient phases.

Suppose \(p\ge2\). For the \(j\)-th intact pair put \(X_j=w_{u_j}+w_{-u_j}\), and for the \(r=m-2p\) unmatched points put \(y_k=w_{v_k}\). Then \(P=\sum_jX_j\), \(R=\sum_ky_k=S-P\). The inequalities
\[
|z_j|\le\frac{X_j}{2},\qquad w_{u_j}^2+w_{-u_j}^2\ge\frac{X_j^2}{2}
\]
and \(\operatorname{Re}(z_i\bar z_j)\le|z_i||z_j|\) give
\[
T(f)\le2S^2+P^2-\frac32\sum_{j=1}^pX_j^2-\sum_{k=1}^r y_k^2
\le2S^2+\left(1-\frac3{2p}\right)P^2-\frac{R^2}{r}
\]
when \(r>0\); omit the last term when \(r=0\). Here \(a=1-3/(2p)>0\). Since \(0\le P\le S\), \(aP^2-(S-P)^2/r\le aS^2\) for \(r>0\): the left side is a convex function of \(P\), so its maximum on this interval occurs at an endpoint, and its value at \(S\), namely \(aS^2>0\), exceeds its value at \(0\), namely \(-S^2/r\). The \(r=0\) estimate is immediate. Therefore
\[
T(f)\le\left(3-\frac3{2p}\right)S^2.
\]
Equality forces \(R=0\), all \(p\) pair masses \(X_j\) equal, the two weights within every pair equal, and all nonzero products \(z_j\) of identical phase. Conversely these conditions attain equality. Because the \(X_j\) are equal and \(S>0\), the products all have equal positive modulus, hence are equal as complex numbers. This establishes all optimizers.

Finally, identify \(\mathbb F_q^2\) with the quadratic algebra \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^2-\delta)\), where \(Q(x,y)=(x+ty)(x-ty)\). If \(\delta\) is nonsquare, the algebra is \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\) and the norm-one group has order \(q+1\); if square, it is \(\mathbb F_q\times\mathbb F_q\), whose norm-one group has order \(q-1\). Thus the conic has \(h\) antipodal pairs. To choose a mask with \(p\) intact pairs and \(r=m-2p\) singletons, choose \(p\) pairs to retain completely and \(r\) of the remaining \(h-p\) pairs to retain once. Feasibility is exactly \(0\le p\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and \(r\le h-p\), equivalent to the claimed interval. The sharp constant is nondecreasing in \(p\), being constant for \(p=0,1\) and strictly increasing thereafter, proving the fixed-size extrema.

## Verification

The self-contained Python script `verify_conic_erasures.py` checks exact finite-field conic sizes, injectivity of all unordered nonzero pair sums, the convolution identity, sharp upper bounds for deterministic Gaussian-integer test vectors, and explicit extremizing vectors for **every nonempty retained subset** in each of ten cases. It covers split and nonsplit conics for \(q\in\{3,5,7,9,11\}\) (where \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[i]\)) and exhausts \(6,810\) masks. Running `python3 verify_conic_erasures.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`. The script is a finite diagnostic, not the justification for the theorem over arbitrary odd prime powers; that comes from the preceding algebraic argument.

## Relationship to prior work

González-Riquelme and Oliveira e Silva established sharp finite-field extension theory for several paraboloid, hyperbolic paraboloid and higher-dimensional cone regimes, including a discussion of exact extremizers. Their statements do not cover arbitrary masks on the present two-dimensional central conic. Koh and Shen studied optimal exponent ranges for extension estimates on broad classes of finite-field plane curves; their bounds do not give an exact mask-dependent best coefficient or classify all equality cases. The simple one-deletion phenomenon is only one boundary instance here; the proof treats both split and nonsplit conics, every retained size, every mask, and the exact phase transition when a second full antipodal pair survives.

## Limitations

This theorem is restricted to characteristic different from two, nondegenerate conics of the displayed norm-one form, and fourth moments. It makes no assertion about higher extension moments or nonconic curves. An unindexed general result for sets with uniquely represented nonzero two-sums could encompass part of the algebraic mechanism; therefore novelty of the mask classification should not be treated as externally certified. The finite checker does not verify fields beyond its enumerated cases. Independent validation has not been performed.
