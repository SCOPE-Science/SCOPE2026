# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Character orthogonality gives the exact weighted additive-energy identity with normalization \(N/m^2\). With unique unordered pair sums, the baseline energy is \(2T^2-Q\). A proper-parallelogram collision adds exactly \(8\operatorname{Re}(f(a)f(b)\overline{f(c)f(d)})\), while a midpoint collision adds exactly \(4\operatorname{Re}(f(a)^2\overline{f(b)f(c)})\). Sharp Cauchy--Schwarz and arithmetic--geometric mean bounds reduce the two cases to concave scalar optimizations, attaining \(9/4\) and \(15/7\) respectively. Equality in every step gives the stated support, modulus, and phase conditions. Odd order excludes a collision between two distinct diagonal pairs.

The packaged checker independently reconstructs ordered-pair convolutions for \(812\) one-defect subsets across five odd cyclic groups and performs \(1624\) exact energy comparisons, in addition to exact rational checks of the scalar optima. These computations support but do not replace the universal proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The inspected Mockenhaupt--Tao source supplies the finite restriction framework and even-exponent reduction to additive representation counts, but its representation-multiplicity estimate does not imply either exact one-defect constant or the equality classification. The inspected Lewko--Lewko source treats \(B_2[g]\), repeated pair sums, and \(\Lambda(4)\) structure, but does not state the sharp weighted optimization for a single collision.

Semantic searches for “single repeated sum”, “one additive relation”, “\(B_2[2]\) exact \(\Lambda(4)\) constant”, and the exact constants \(9/4\) and \(15/7\) did not surface a statement covering this dichotomy. Closest indexed results concern finite-field extremizers, additive-energy formulas for specific curves, phase-only fourth moments, or Sidon constants of fixed short character sets; none implies the abstract one-defect theorem.

Residual risk: an older equivalent statement may be hidden under different \(\Lambda(4)\), Sidon, or \(B_2[g]\) terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The hypothesis is the minimal structural departure from pair-sum uniqueness. The theorem shows that this first defect is not governed merely by a representation-number bound: the location of the duplicate fiber matters, producing two different universal constants and two different extremizer geometries. The result is ambient-independent, gives full equality cases, and can serve as a sharp local model for near-Sidon frequency sets and finite restriction problems.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary references inspected were Mockenhaupt--Tao, arXiv:math/0204234, and Lewko--Lewko, arXiv:1003.4561. The theorem is limited to odd-order groups and one double fiber; multiple additive defects and even-order diagonal phenomena remain outside its scope.
