# Scientific review: sharp fourth-moment extension on binary quadratic conics

**Correctness — PASS.** The nonzero affine conic has no contained line, so any nonzero pair sum has at most two ordered representations, exchanged by \(v\mapsto s-v\). Orthogonality gives the exact convolution fourth moment, and completing the weighted complex variance gives an explicit sum of nonnegative squares. Equality forces constant absolute value and a common opposite-pair product. The normalizations and the zero-input boundary are handled explicitly. Seven field instances, including two extension fields, and both Witt classes were checked with the embedded executable; these tests support but are not the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk.** Mockenhaupt–Tao establish the setting, Iosevich–Koh establish uniform sharp **exponent ranges** using quadratic pair geometry, and Koh–Shen generalize uniform extension inequalities to plane curves. Their inspected theorem statements do not directly give the exact \(3-3/M\) norm, all extremizers, or the weighted global deficit. The recent published three-dimensional anisotropic sphere phase dichotomy concerns a surface in \(\mathbb F_q^3\), not these conics. Equivalent formulations in older additive-energy or weak-Sidon literature may remain unidentified; public-record search cannot establish absolute novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The object is a canonical nonzero planar quadratic level and the invariant is its sharp endpoint Fourier-extension norm. The classification covers both split and nonsplit geometries across all odd prime powers, identifies the full positive-dimensional extremizer manifold, and decomposes the exact fourth-moment deficit into three geometrically meaningful sources: mass imbalance among antipodal pairs, imbalance within pairs, and phase-product mismatch. These are not consequences of a mere exponent-range bound or a numerical sharp constant.

**Closest sources and limits.** ArXiv math/0204234, 0804.4505 and 1003.4240 supply the historically relevant harmonic-analysis setup and weaker uniform bounds. ArXiv 2405.16647 and the documented three-dimensional-sphere phase classification are different surfaces. The complete theorem does not concern zero radius, characteristic two, degenerate binary forms, or higher extension exponents. No independent validation has been performed.
