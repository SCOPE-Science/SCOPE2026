# Review of Sharp fourth-moment phase range for four-frequency unimodular trigonometric polynomials

## Correctness
PASS. Parseval turns the fourth moment into a sum of squared pair-sum fibers. For four distinct integers, every nontrivial collision is either a three-term progression or the unique equal-sum partition of a centrally symmetric four-point set. Their contributions are computed exactly. The four-term-progression case reduces to \(28+4\cos\alpha+4\cos\beta+8\cos(\alpha+\beta)\); minimizing and maximizing this two-angle expression gives \(19\) and \(44\) with the stated equality conditions. The verifier independently reconstructs the collision sums on a substantial finite test family but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS, with a bounded folklore risk. Krenedits' full text states the even-integer majorant principle and discusses four-term idempotent constructions for non-even exponents, but it does not state the exact all-support fourth-moment range or the lower equality class. Anderson--Ash--Jones--Rider--Saffari study idempotent \(L^p\)-concentration, while Shao studies large additive energy in torsion-free groups; neither inspected source supplies the five four-point support types with the phase-sensitive ranges. Dedicated searches using the constants \(19\) and \(44\), fourth-moment terminology, additive-energy terminology, arithmetic-progression support, and unimodular coefficients did not locate a covering statement. The residual risk is an older equivalent small-cardinality result indexed only under Freiman or additive-energy language.

## Value
PASS. Four terms are the minimal cardinality featured by the classical idempotent majorant constructions outside even exponents, while \(p=4\) is the first nontrivial even endpoint. The theorem gives a complete endpoint geometry rather than a single example: it identifies every possible support relation type, the exact phase range for each, and both global equality classes. This provides a sharp finite baseline for phase interference at the endpoint used in majorant and interpolation arguments.
