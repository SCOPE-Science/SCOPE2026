# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The infinite-history convolution reduces exactly to a finite-dimensional \(2N\)-state flow. A real-quadratic shifted Hurwitz test is affine in the graph eigenvalue and rigorously proves the extremal-mode property. The one-mode derivative, endpoint-crossing sign computation, and characteristic-root equation yield the unique closed-form maximizing gain. The proof distinguishes the spectral exponent from a genuinely attained endpoint exponential bound when a repeated root is present. The three-vertex path example has exact rational discriminants; standalone finite numerical tests only supplement the proof.

**Originality: PASS, with residual risk.** The motivating 2026 Pignotti–Wang article proves that exponential consensus occurs, not the exact common-kernel gain-optimal exponent. Moradian–Kia optimize a discrete point-delay feedback parameter through a transcendental characteristic equation; Kempton–Herrmann–di Bernardo optimize graph weights for a different delay model. No identical theorem was found in the targeted mathematical-record and primary-source comparisons. The older Qiao–Sipahi homogeneous-delay paper was accessible only through an abstract, and equivalent control terminology might conceal further overlap.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result identifies a structural two-endpoint bottleneck valid on arbitrary finite connected weighted undirected graphs, gives an explicit globally optimal parameter, and shows a precise penalty for overcoupling. The rational path-graph instance makes the spectral balancing effect directly testable. The claim deliberately excludes heterogeneous memory, switching graphs, and control-effort optimization.
