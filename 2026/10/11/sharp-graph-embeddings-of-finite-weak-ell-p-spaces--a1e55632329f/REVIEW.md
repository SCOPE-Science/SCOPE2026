# Same-model review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof reduces the problem to the exact support function of the finite weak-\(\ell_p\) unit ball. Rearrangement gives the upper bound
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m a_j^*j^{-1/p},
\]
and a phase-aligned vector with coordinate moduli \(j^{-1/p}\) attains it. This equality vector makes the converse sharp because all original coordinates lie strictly above the new \((m+1)\)-st threshold. The disjointness statement is an exact support calculation on basis vectors.

## Originality

PASS. The focal paper's Remark 1.1 gives only one two-dimensional equal-coefficient example. Full-text inspection found no arbitrary coefficient-vector criterion, no all-dimensional converse, and no classification of which graph embeddings preserve disjointness. Targeted searches over weak-\(\ell_p\), Marcinkiewicz sequence spaces, graph embeddings, codimension-one isometries, identity-plus-functional maps, the exact focal coefficient, and disjointness returned no covering statement. Earlier symmetric-sequence-space isometry literature located in the search concerns surjective maps.

## Value

PASS. The finding explains the complete coefficient geometry behind the source's motivating counterexample. It identifies the source coefficient as a sharp boundary point in every positive exponent, extends the mechanism to all finite dimensions and arbitrary added functionals, and gives a clean structural criterion for exactly when support mixing occurs.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is K. Fang, Y. Gao, J. Huang, and F. Sukochev, arXiv:2610.01043v1, whose Remark 1.1 is the \(m=2\) equal-coefficient boundary case. Aminov--Chilin (2017) and Arazy (1985) concern surjective isometries of symmetric sequence spaces and do not imply the non-surjective graph classification. The result does not classify arbitrary embeddings into higher codimension or noncommutative analogues. An older equivalent finite-dimensional observation under different Lorentz/Marcinkiewicz terminology remains the principal residual originality risk.
