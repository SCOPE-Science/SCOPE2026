# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For bounded real inputs, uniform Taylor expansion of \(u|u|^\varepsilon\) at \(\varepsilon=0\) justifies differentiation of the best constant despite atoms at the mean. The global scalar inequality follows from a one-variable derivative with one positive maximum and its reciprocal-negative partner. Centered-unit-variance integration yields the sharp bound and rigid two-atom equality cases. The empirical rate follows from rounding an interior maximum and a second-order Taylor bound. The standalone finite verifier checked the stated numerical identities; no infinite claim depends on its mesh.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest inspected primary texts are Arnaudon–Miclo (arXiv:1301.7156), whose \(p\)-mean work develops an algorithm for fixed exponent, and Aldaz (arXiv:1203.4454), whose variance bounds govern arithmetic-minus-geometric means of nonnegative inputs. Neither supplies or implies the sharp signed-logarithmic moment bound or the exponent derivative constant. Schötz (arXiv:1812.08037) treats empirical convergence rates and was compared at the abstract level only; its full text was not inspected. Searches of published mathematical records for exponent derivative, centered signed logarithmic moment, and two-point extremality found no dominating result. A prior input-Lipschitz estimate is not the exponent derivative. Unindexed equivalent formulations remain a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS

The problem is a natural stability question for the canonical nonlinear \(L^p\) best-constant projection as the ambient geometry passes through the Hilbert exponent. The result gives an exact distribution-free optimal response coefficient, all extremal laws, and a nontrivial finite-empirical approximation rate; these are not routine differentiations of the optimality equation. The logarithmic-envelope proof is short but provides the decisive sharp constant and equality structure.

## Scientific limits

Only bounded real variables, derivative at \(p=2\), and asymptotic near-sharpness for equally weighted samples are proved. Finite empirical sharp constants remain undetermined. No independent mathematical validation is represented.
