# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof keeps Wackenhuth's normalization \(D(\Lambda_\mu)=i_\mu m_S(B_r(o))\) and uses exactly the two inequalities in the source proof. Equality of the density bound makes their endpoints identical, forcing \(\widehat\eta_\mu(\widehat F)=0\). Positivity then yields the support statement. The additional nonvanishing lemma is independent: a radial test supported below distance \(2r\) sees only diagonal pairs, and its Haar mass can be chosen small enough to make the reduced autocorrelation strictly positive.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of Wackenhuth's short paper and the broader homogeneous-space follow-up found the linear-programming inequality and reduced-diffraction identities but no equality theorem, zero-set support condition, or proof that positive-intensity hard-core packings have nonzero reduced diffraction. Targeted searches for complementary slackness, reduced diffraction, spherical zeros, and sharp hyperbolic packing witnesses found no covering result. Cohn--Elkies gives the Euclidean lattice analogue through Poisson summation, but that does not imply the invariant-random hyperbolic statement.

## Value
PASS. A sharp linear-programming construction must now pass a concrete structural test: its spherical transform must have a nontrivial zero capable of supporting reduced diffraction. This rules out every everywhere-positive witness from attaining the bound and gives a symmetric-space analogue of the zero mechanism behind Euclidean sharp auxiliary functions, without assuming periodicity or a dual lattice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Wackenhuth's two linear-programming papers, which provide the exact identities used here, and Cohn--Elkies, which supplies the Euclidean lattice precedent. The result is only a necessary equality condition in the compact-support direct class; it does not construct an optimizer, locate the required zero, or provide a quantitative deficit away from equality.
