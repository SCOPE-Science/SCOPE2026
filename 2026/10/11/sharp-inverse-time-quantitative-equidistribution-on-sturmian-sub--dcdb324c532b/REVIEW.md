# Review
## Correctness — PASS
The source's Theorem D gives the exact decomposition of every Lipschitz observable into a scalar multiple of \(\Phi-\alpha\) plus a Lipschitz coboundary, with the bounds \(|c_f|\le |f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}/(1-\theta)^2\) and \(|\widetilde f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\le\theta|f|_{\mathrm{Lip}}/(1-\theta)^3\). Sturmian balance gives \(|S_n(\Phi-\alpha)|<1\). Telescoping and \(\operatorname{diam}X_\alpha\le1\) therefore give the claimed all-\(n\) Birkhoff and Wasserstein bound. Equal-weight optimal transport is a permutation matching, giving the upper bound for \(F_n\). For the lower bound, balance plus the nonintegral invariant average \(n\alpha\) forces both adjacent digit counts to occur, and the 1-Lipschitz digit observable separates their empirical measures by exactly \(1/n\). Quantifiers and metric normalization were checked explicitly.

## Originality — PASS
Claim-level searches covered empirical Wasserstein convergence, the permutation-matching pseudometric, bounded Lipschitz Birkhoff discrepancy, cohomological rigidity, balance, and the exact \(n^{-1}\) endpoint. No published-finding corpus item states an implication-equivalent Sturmian theorem. The motivating paper states the cohomological rigidity theorem and the bounded digit discrepancy but does not state the empirical Wasserstein estimate or the two-sided \(F_n\) rate. Wang--Zheng's 2025 paper proves vanishing after multiplication by \(n^\tau\) for every \(\tau<1\) and discusses periodic-measure approximation; it does not give the uniform \(O(n^{-1})\) intrinsic matching bound or the matching lower bound.

## Value — PASS
The result identifies the precise endpoint order behind quantitative unique ergodicity of Sturmian systems. It upgrades a family of subcritical exponent statements to an explicit, slope-independent all-\(n\) estimate and simultaneously proves that the exponent cannot be improved to \(o(n^{-1})\). The same estimate controls every Lipschitz observable and every starting point, so it is a structural quantitative equidistribution law rather than a computation for a chosen observable or continued-fraction subsequence.

## Closest literature and limitations
The decisive source is arXiv:2610.03619v1. The closest quantitative comparison is Wang--Zheng, Journal of Differential Equations 418 (2025), DOI:10.1016/j.jde.2024.11.015. Classical Hecke--Ostrowski bounded-remainder theory covers individual clopen observables but does not, in the inspected modern statement, provide the uniform Lipschitz-unit-ball bound used here. A residual bibliographic risk remains that the all-Lipschitz Wasserstein corollary has been recorded elsewhere under bounded-remainder or Kantorovich terminology. The sharp multiplicative constant is not determined.
