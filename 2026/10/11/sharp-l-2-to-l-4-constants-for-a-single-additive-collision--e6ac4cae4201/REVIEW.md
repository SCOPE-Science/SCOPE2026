# Review of Sharp \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) constants for a single additive collision

## Correctness
**PASS.** Parseval applied to the square of the Fourier polynomial yields an exact pair-sum-fiber formula. Under the one-collision hypothesis, every nonexceptional fiber contributes the generic term \(2S^2-\sum x_a^2\), while the sole exceptional fiber contributes one explicit phase cross term. In the four-distinct-point case, arithmetic-geometric mean bounds give \(9S^2/4\); in the three-term-progression case, a sharp two-variable quadratic inequality gives \(15S^2/7\). Equality conditions were reconstructed at every inequality and give exactly the stated support concentration, modulus ratios, and phase alignment. The verifier checks representative and exhaustively enumerated small supports, but the universal statement rests on the analytic proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest archive-era source develops the even-exponent relation expansion and additive-independence criterion, but does not give the sharp norm after exactly one relation is introduced. The nearest published comparison fixes coefficient moduli, so it cannot imply the unrestricted amplitude optimization. A prior three-frequency result already covers the isolated three-term-progression value \(15/7\); it does not imply that arbitrary additional collision-free frequencies are harmless, nor does it contain the distinct disjoint-pair value \(9/4\) or the global extremizer classification. Exact-value, \(\Lambda(4)\), pair-sum-collision, autoconvolution, and one-relation searches located no implication-equivalent statement. Residual risk remains from older literature using different terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** Two-fold additive independence is the structural condition behind exact fourth-moment decoupling. Classifying the first possible defect is therefore a natural sharp benchmark: the theorem shows that the defect type alone fixes the global \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) constant and forces extremizers to concentrate on the minimal colliding subsystem. The difference between \(9/4\) and \(15/7\) records a genuine distinction between parallelogram and diagonal collisions rather than an arbitrary finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Neuwirth's 1997 paper supplies the additive-relation framework for even Fourier moments and the \(p/2\)-independence criterion. The closest indexed phase-only four-point result fixes coefficient magnitudes, and the closest prior exact three-frequency result treats only a three-element support. The present theorem is limited to torsion-free groups, exactly one nontrivial pair-sum collision, and exponent \(4\).
