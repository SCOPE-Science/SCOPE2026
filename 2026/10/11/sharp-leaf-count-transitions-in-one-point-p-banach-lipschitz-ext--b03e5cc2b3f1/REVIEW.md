# Review of the finite-star \(p\)-trace theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The stated domain has all leaf distances \(2\), and the added center has all leaf distances \(1\). Both leaf-center and arithmetic-center choices give the universal upper bound. A canonical real finite free \(p\)-space witness gives the reverse bound: coefficients outside the barycentric simplex cannot improve radius \(2\), and on the simplex Cúth–Raunig's rooted spanning-tree formula gives an exact expression for each radius. The ordered-simplex extreme-point argument is valid for every finite \(n\ge4\), and the \(p=1\) case, equality threshold and strict minimizer statements are checked separately. The proof is not an extrapolation from finite numerical trials.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Bíma's 2024 source defines the same trace but analyzes a different three-point geometry and broad extension bounds. Cúth–Raunig provide the finite free-space tree norm algorithm but not the sharp all-leaf-number optimization. Basso's universal sharp factor-two result does not imply the supercritical values below two. An already established exact three-leaf theorem is explicitly excluded. Searches for equivalent star, equilateral, Jung/Chebyshev and quasi-Banach trace formulations, plus review of several likely primary sources, found no theorem implying the full \(n\ge4\) transition. Unindexed equivalents and uninspected technical appendices are residual prior-art risks; no claim of uniqueness is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem determines a sharp established extension invariant for a natural full family of finite metrics. The transition \(p_n=\log(n-1)/\log n\) separates two different canonical minimizing geometries, tends to the Banach endpoint, and exhibits an exact sharp factor-two obstruction for fixed \(p<1\) and all sufficiently large finite stars, in contrast to every finite Banach case. The formula has universal target quantifiers, not just one numerical sample.

## Limits
The proof is for real \(p\)-Banach targets and a precisely specified leaf-to-center extension, not arbitrary domain trees or arbitrary target-optimal-center uniqueness. The finite labeled-tree script independently stress-tests small dimensions and sample centers but supplies no infinite proof. Independent scientific verification remains outstanding.
