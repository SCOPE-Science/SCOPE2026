# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof determines the exact rank-one metric projection onto each boundary line by duality in two dimensions; the continuous-selection lemma yields a global upper Lipschitz bound and a strict-complementarity construction realizes each boundary derivative on an open subset. The interior realizes the identity map. Hölder equality then gives the necessary and sufficient ray geometry. All quantifiers refer to arbitrary linearly independent nonzero real generators and every \(1<p<\infty\). The accompanying numerical verifier checked 3,675 independent constrained projections, 3,150 pairs and 210 local sharpness witnesses; computation was not used to infer the continuum theorem.

## Originality — PASS (with bibliographic risk)
Ivanov (arXiv:1501.02596) gives a relationship involving projections onto hyperplanes, not the sharp constant of a solid cone. Barrios–Ferreira–Németh (arXiv:1503.02757) study Euclidean simplicial-cone projection algorithms, and Németh–Németh (arXiv:1503.08458) classify Euclidean order-isotonicity, a different property. Domokos–Marsh (DOI:10.24193/fpt-ro.2018.1.12), examined in its complete author-hosted PDF, studies algebraic characterizations for Banach-cone projections; it does not state the present quantitative classification. The single-ray, orthant and symmetric-wedge facts provide special cases but do not alone supply a proof for all asymmetrically oriented wedges. Equivalent older presentations in approximation or Minkowski-plane terminology remain possible.

## Value — PASS
The family of all planar convex cones is a natural complete class of conic constraints. A sharp formula for the full map's worst-case perturbation amplification and an exact, discrete classification of nonexpansive orientations in non-Hilbert \(\ell_p\) geometry offer a structural endpoint beyond an isolated instance or numerical example. The result explains precisely which conic boundary normals cause failure of norm-one robustness.

## Scientific limits
The theorem concerns real two-dimensional pointed solid wedges and \(1<p<\infty\); it does not treat half-spaces, single rays, higher-dimensional cones or endpoint norms. The bibliographic comparison is not a proof of global historical uniqueness.
