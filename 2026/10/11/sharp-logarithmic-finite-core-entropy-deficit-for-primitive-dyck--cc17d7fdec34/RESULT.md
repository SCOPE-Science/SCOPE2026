# Sharp logarithmic finite-core entropy deficit for primitive Dyck excursions

## Finding

For each integer \(N\ge 1\), let \(\mathcal G_{\le N}\) consist of all binary first-return positive Dyck excursions of lengths at most \(2N\). Let \(X_N\) be the two-sided coded shift generated by their bi-infinite concatenations, and let \(h_N=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_N)\). Then \(h_N\) is the unique nonnegative solution of
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac1n\binom{2n-2}{n-1}e^{-2nh_N}=1.
\]
Moreover, as \(N\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{2N(\log 2-h_N)=\tfrac12\log N+\log\log N+\tfrac12\log\pi+o(1).}
\]
In particular, \(h_N\uparrow\log2\), but the entropy deficit is asymptotic to \((\log N)/(4N)\) and has a non-negligible \(\log\log N/(2N)\) correction. This is a statement about the **length-ordered cutoff** in the particular positive-excursion coded presentation, not an arbitrary order of generators.

## Assumptions and scope

Write the alphabet as \(\mathcal A=\{+,-\}\), assign increments \(\chi(+)=1\) and \(\chi(-)=-1\), and define the height \(H_j(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\chi(w_i)\) for a word \(w\) of length \(\ell\) and \(0\le j\le\ell\). A *primitive positive excursion* is a nonempty word satisfying \(H_\ell(w)=0\) and \(H_j(w)>0\) for every \(1\le j<\ell\). Every such word has even length \(2n\). Let \(\mathcal G_{\le N}\) contain exactly the excursions with \(1\le n\le N\). Define \(X_N\subset\mathcal A^{\mathbb Z}\) as the closure of all shifted bi-infinite concatenations from \(\mathcal G_{\le N}\); the time map is the left shift \(\sigma\). Entropy uses natural logarithms. The untruncated presentation is Hedges' positive Dyck code, whose coded shift is the full binary shift with entropy \(\log2\) [1].

## Proof

**1. Code counts and boundary structure.** Deleting the first \(+\) and final \(-\) from a primitive positive excursion of length \(2n\) bijects it with an ordinary Dyck word of semilength \(n-1\). There are therefore \(C_{n-1}=\frac1n\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\) words of length \(2n\). For any concatenated sequence, the height at every generator boundary equals the global minimum of its bi-infinite cumulative-height function, while at every strict interior position it is larger. Consequently boundary coordinates and the pointed code decomposition are unique; this is also established for the entire positive-excursion code in [1, Section 3.2]. Because the alphabet of allowed generators is finite, with word lengths uniformly bounded by \(2N\), the collection of pointed concatenations is already compact. Thus every point of \(X_N\) admits a unique pointed decomposition, and successive boundary gaps are between \(2\) and \(2N\).

**2. Exact entropy equation.** Let \(\nu\) be a shift-invariant measure on the finite full shift over generator names, and write \(r(g)=|g|\). Its normalized suspension is a \(\sigma\)-invariant measure on \(X_N\), with entropy \(h_\nu/\int r\,d\nu\) by the boundary inducing identity (or Abramov's formula). Conversely, every ergodic invariant measure on \(X_N\) lifts to the boundary section because boundary visits occur at least once every \(2N\) coordinates; its entropy has the same form. The variational principle and ergodic decomposition therefore give
\[
h_N=\sup_{\nu}\frac{h_\nu}{\int r\,d\nu}.
\]
For a generator-name distribution \(p_g\), entropy rate satisfies \(h_\nu\le-\sum_g p_g\log p_g\), with equality for its Bernoulli measure. Put \(Z_N(h)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_{\le N}}e^{-h|g|}\). Since \(Z_N(0)\ge1\), and \(Z_N(h)\) strictly decreases to zero, there is a unique \(h\ge0\) such that \(Z_N(h)=1\). At this root, set \(q_g=e^{-h|g|}\). Gibbs' inequality \(\sum_gp_g\log(p_g/q_g)\ge0\) gives \(-\sum_gp_g\log p_g\le h\sum_gp_g|g|\), so \(h_N\le h\). The Bernoulli distribution \(q_g\) achieves equality, so \(h_N=h\). Grouping generators by length yields the displayed finite Catalan root equation. For \(N=1\) its root is \(0\); for \(N>1\) it is positive. At \(h=\log2\) the sum is at most \(1/2\), so \(h_N<\log2\).

**3. The critical renewal weights.** Define \(a_n=C_{n-1}/4^n\). The Catalan generating function and Stirling's formula give
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}a_n=\tfrac12,
\qquad
 a_n=\frac1{4\sqrt\pi}\,n^{-3/2}\bigl(1+O(n^{-1})\bigr).
\]
Set \(t_N=2N(\log2-h_N)\), so the exact entropy equation becomes
\[
S_N(t_N):=\sum_{n=1}^Na_ne^{t_Nn/N}=1.
\]
For each fixed real \(c\), put \(T_N(c)=\tfrac12\log N+\log\log N+c\); these test values are positive for all sufficiently large \(N\). We claim
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}S_N(T_N(c))=\frac12+\frac{e^c}{2\sqrt\pi}.
\]
To prove this, first \(\sum_{n=1}^Na_n\to\tfrac12\). For \(n\le N/2\), the inequality \(e^u-1\le ue^u\) for \(u\ge0\) and \(\sum_{n\le N/2}na_n=O(\sqrt N)\) imply
\[
0\le\sum_{n\le N/2}a_n(e^{T_N(c)n/N}-1)
 \le O\!\left(\frac{T_N(c)e^{T_N(c)/2}}{\sqrt N}\right)=o(1).
\]
For \(N/2<n\le N\), the uniform Catalan estimate and a Riemann-sum bound (whose relative discretization error is \(O(T_N(c)/N)\)) give
\[
\sum_{N/2<n\le N}a_ne^{T_N(c)n/N}
 =\frac{1+o(1)}{4\sqrt{\pi N}}
 \int_{1/2}^1u^{-3/2}e^{T_N(c)u}\,du.
\]
Endpoint Laplace integration yields \(\int_{1/2}^1u^{-3/2}e^{Tu}\,du\sim e^T/T\) as \(T\to\infty\). Since \(e^{T_N(c)}/(T_N(c)\sqrt N)\to 2e^c\), the upper-half exponential contribution approaches \(e^c/(2\sqrt\pi)\). Its corresponding unsubtracted upper-half weight is \(O(N^{-1/2})\). Combining the ranges proves the claim.

**4. Sharp threshold and uniqueness of the limit.** The function \(S_N(t)\) is strictly increasing. The preceding limit equals one precisely when \(e^c=\sqrt\pi\), or \(c_*=\tfrac12\log\pi\). For any fixed \(\delta>0\), the limiting values of \(S_N(T_N(c_*-\delta))\) and \(S_N(T_N(c_*+\delta))\) lie strictly below and above one. For all large \(N\), therefore, \(T_N(c_*-\delta)<t_N<T_N(c_*+\delta)\). Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) shows
\[
t_N=\tfrac12\log N+\log\log N+\tfrac12\log\pi+o(1),
\]
which proves the asserted entropy deficit. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying `check_entropy.py` enumerates primitive excursions exactly for semilengths \(1\le n\le7\), verifying the Catalan counts, and solves the truncated renewal equation numerically for \(N=1,2,3,4,5,10,30,100,1000,10000,100000\). It reports 18 successful finite diagnostics. For example, \(h_{1000}=0.690065702674\ldots\) and \(h_{100000}=0.693102219219\ldots\). The code uses the finite recurrence \(a_{n+1}=a_n(2n-1)/(2n+2)\) with \(a_1=1/4\), and verifies residuals at floating-point accuracy. Numerical diagnostics are not an infinite proof; Steps 1–4 provide the analytic proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Hedges [1] introduced this exact positive-excursion presentation, proved its unique decoding and Catalan generator counts, and proved the qualitative finite-core exhaustion theorem and full-shift entropy \(\log2\). Those results, and the suspension/renewal entropy method, are antecedents rather than claims of novelty here. The contribution is the explicit length-cutoff entropy equation together with the asymptotically sharp three-term deficit, including the \(\log\log N\) correction and constant \(\tfrac12\log\pi\), which does not follow quantitatively from the prior qualitative exhaustion assertion alone. The broader computational coded-shift framework in Burr–Das–Wolf–Yang [2] and Kucherenko–López–Wolf [3] concerns computability and existence; no matching quantitative asymptotic was identified in the inspected materials. Priority risk remains for unindexed or incompletely accessible renewal-asymptotic literature.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to the binary *primitive positive-excursion code* with all generators of semilength at most \(N\); it does not assign this asymptotic to other coded shifts or arbitrary enumeration cutoffs. The displayed remainder is \(o(1)\) in the scaled deficit, not a uniform explicit finite-\(N\) bound. Root computations corroborate finite examples only. Literature searches cannot establish absolute priority, and the full text of [2] was not inspected.
