# Sharp logarithmic statistical dimension of regular simplex cones

## Finding

For each integer \(m\ge1\), let \(D_m\) be the \(m\)-dimensional cone generated by the edges at one vertex of a regular \(m\)-simplex. Equivalently, in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\) put
\[
D_m=\operatorname{pos}\{e_0-e_i:1\le i\le m\}\subset\{x:\textstyle\sum_i x_i=0\}.
\]
The generating vectors have Gram matrix entries \(\langle e_0-e_i,e_0-e_j\rangle=1+\delta_{ij}\). Write \(J_m\) for the intrinsic-volume random index of \(D_m\), so \(\Pr(J_m=j)=v_j(D_m)\). Then the following **sharp asymptotic law** holds:
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac{J_m}{2\log m}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\Pr}1,
\qquad
\delta(D_m)=\mathbb EJ_m=(2+o(1))\log m.}
\]
Thus, although \(D_m\) has dimension \(m\), its conic intrinsic-volume distribution is asymptotically concentrated at a logarithmic face dimension, with exact leading constant two.

This concerns the high-dimensional limit, **not** a new finite-dimensional intrinsic-volume formula: exact finite formulas for the encompassing family of regular-simplex cones are already available in Kabluchko and Seidel (2020), Theorem 2.4.

## Assumptions and scope

The cone \(D_m\) is endowed with the Euclidean metric of its linear span, and its conic intrinsic volumes have the standard Gaussian-projection normalization. The ambient representation in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\) is isometric to an \(m\)-dimensional cone. The asymptotic concerns integer \(m\to\infty\). The dual star order cone below serves only as an elementary tool to analyze the dimension of Gaussian faces; no arbitrary graph/poset, weighted model or non-Gaussian extension is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Polarity and a scalar projection equation.** Set
\[
C_m=\{(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in\mathbb R^{m+1}:x_0\le x_i\ (1\le i\le m)\}.
\]
Then \(D_m=C_m^\circ\), using the polar convention \(\langle y,x\rangle\le0\) for \(x\in C_m\). The cone \(C_m\) has one-dimensional lineality, while \(D_m\) is full dimensional in its \(m\)-dimensional span. Conic polarity in the full space implies \(v_j(D_m)=v_{m+1-j}(C_m)\), for \(j=0,\ldots,m\).

For independent standard Gaussian coordinates \(G_0,\ldots,G_m\), the orthogonal projection onto \(C_m\) is determined by a unique scalar \(T\) solving
\[
F_m(T):=T+\sum_{i=1}^m(T-G_i)_+=G_0;
\qquad
\Pi_{C_m}G=(T,\max\{G_1,T\},\ldots,\max\{G_m,T\}).
\]
Indeed, fixing the root at \(T\) forces each optimal leaf to \(\max\{G_i,T\}\), and the strictly convex scalar objective has the displayed derivative equation. There is almost surely no boundary tie \(G_i=T\). Exactly \(K_m=\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbf1_{\{G_i<T\}}\) independent defining inequalities are then active; the face of \(C_m\) has dimension \(m+1-K_m\). The polarity identity therefore gives the distributional equality \(J_m\stackrel{\mathrm d}=K_m\). This includes the extreme cases of no active leaf and all pooled leaves.

**2. The deterministic balance scale.** Write \(\varphi\) and \(\Phi\) for the standard normal density and cumulative distribution, \(\overline\Phi=1-\Phi\), and set \(q(u)=\varphi(u)-u\overline\Phi(u)\). As \(q'(u)=-\overline\Phi(u)<0\), there is a unique \(u_m>0\) satisfying
\[
mq(u_m)=u_m.
\]
Repeated integration by parts of the Gaussian tail yields
\[
q(u)=\frac{\varphi(u)}{u^2}(1+O(u^{-2})),\quad
\overline\Phi(u)=\frac{\varphi(u)}{u}(1+O(u^{-2})),\quad
\mathbb E[(-u-Z)_+^2]=O(\varphi(u)u^{-3}),
\]
for \(u\to\infty\) and \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). It follows directly from the defining equation that \(u_m\to\infty\), \(m\varphi(u_m)\sim u_m^3\), and hence
\[
u_m^2\sim2\log m.
\]

**3. Concentration of the projected face dimension.** Fix any real constant \(c\), write \(u=u_m\), and evaluate at \(t_c=-u+c/u\). The tail estimates, also at \(u-c/u\), imply
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{i=1}^m(t_c-G_i)_+=u e^c(1+o(1)),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m(t_c-G_i)_+\right)=O_c(1).
\]
As \(G_0/u\to0\) in probability, it follows that \((F_m(t_c)-G_0)/u\to e^c-1\) in probability. Since \(F_m\) is increasing, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
-u-\varepsilon/u<T<-u+\varepsilon/u
\]
with probability tending to one. Let \(N_m(t)=\#\{1\le i\le m:G_i<t\}\). Its binomial mean satisfies \(m\Phi(t_c)/u^2\to e^c\), with variance \(O_c(u^2)\), so \(N_m(t_c)/u^2\to e^c\) in probability. Monotonicity and \(K_m=N_m(T)\), followed by \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), therefore give \(K_m/u_m^2\to1\) in probability.

**4. Uniform integrability.** To pass from probability convergence to a mean law, set \(t_*=-u+8/u\) and \(s_*=-u+7/u\). For \(Y=N_m(s_*)\), the same tail estimate yields \(\mathbb EY/u^2\to e^7\), so \(\mathbb EY\ge100u^2\) eventually. A binomial Chernoff inequality gives \(\Pr(Y<6u^2)\le e^{-40u^2}\). If \(Y\ge6u^2\) and \(G_0\le4u\), then every counted leaf contributes at least \(1/u\) to \(F_m(t_*)\); consequently \(F_m(t_*)>G_0\), \(T<t_*\), and \(K_m\le N_m(t_*)\). Since \(\Pr(G_0>4u)\le e^{-8u^2}\) and \(u^2\sim2\log m\), the exceptional event has probability \(o(m^{-2})\). The binomial variable \(N_m(t_*)\) has expectation asymptotic to \(e^8u^2\) and second moment \(O(u^4)\). Using \(0\le K_m\le m\),
\[
\mathbb EK_m^2\le\mathbb EN_m(t_*)^2+m^2o(m^{-2})=O(u_m^4).
\]
Thus the normalized variables \(K_m/u_m^2\) are uniformly integrable; together with their convergence in probability this yields \(\mathbb EK_m/u_m^2\to1\). The distributional equality from step 1 proves the asserted law for \(J_m\), and the classical identity \(\delta(D_m)=\mathbb EJ_m\) completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` requires only standard Python and implements two independent numerical methods for projecting onto the polar star cone, plus exact three-coordinate angle checks and Gaussian-tail balance checks. Executing `python verify.py` produced `VERIFY_OK checks= 8267`. The geometric polar isometry follows directly from the Gram matrix \((1+\delta_{ij})\); the finite-dimensional intrinsic volumes are **known** independently through Kabluchko–Seidel's formulas. All limiting conclusions rest on the analytic concentration and uniform-integrability arguments, not simulations or an assumed infinite numerical experiment.

## Relationship to prior work

Kabluchko and Seidel, *Convex cones spanned by regular polytopes* (2020, arXiv:2006.09492), introduce the equicorrelated regular simplex cones \(C_m^{\triangle}(r)\) with generating Gram matrix \((r+\delta_{ij})\), and **Theorem 2.4 already computes their exact intrinsic volumes for all dimensions and parameters**. Our \(D_m\) is precisely their \(r=1\) case. This preexisting exact formula must not be counted as new; the point established here is the sharp high-dimensional **concentration scale and expectation coefficient** extracted through a direct Gaussian-projection argument. Their inspected article does not state or derive this logarithmic mean and in-probability limit.

McCoy and Tropp (2013, arXiv:1308.5265) establish the Gaussian interpretation and general concentration of conic intrinsic volumes, but leave this cone's mean unspecified. Amelunxen and Lotz (2015, arXiv:1512.06033) provide universal face-angle identities. Godland and Kabluchko (2020, arXiv:2005.06205) give a different Stirling-number law for total-order Weyl chambers. Chatterjee, Guntuboyina and Sen (2013, arXiv:1311.3765) analyze univariate chain-ordered risk, not this branching cone. General concentration around an unknown statistical dimension and an exact finite-angle expression do not directly identify the nontrivial logarithmic limit with coefficient two.

## Limitations

The new contribution is only the sharp asymptotic distributional location and statistical-dimension coefficient. Exact finite conic intrinsic volumes were established previously and are explicitly credited. This proof provides no limiting central limit theorem, sharp second-order mean correction, arbitrary equicorrelation parameter, general tree-poset extension, or independently certified novelty. A related asymptotic under older terminology may still exist.
