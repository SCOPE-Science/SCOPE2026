# Sharp maximizer bifurcation and cubic-root cusp for rank-one Davis–Wielandt radii

## Finding
For every nonzero rank-one operator \(T=a uv^*\) on a complex Hilbert space of dimension at least two, the Davis–Wielandt radius and all its maximizing rays admit a complete classification in terms of the operator norm \(a>0\) and overlap \(c=|\langle u,v\rangle|\in[0,1]\). The nonnormal case \(0<c<1\) has a unique maximizing ray determined by the unique positive root of an explicit cubic. At nilpotency \(c=0\), a symmetry-breaking transition occurs at \(a^2=1/2\): a circle of maximizing rays exists below this threshold, whereas a single ray maximizes at or above it. Approaching this point along the overlap parameter yields the sharp cubic-root response \(z_*(c)\sim c^{1/3}\) and the fractional-power radius cusp
\[
\operatorname{dw}(T)=\frac12+\frac34c^{4/3}+o(c^{4/3})\qquad (a=1/\sqrt2,\ c\downarrow0).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(H\) be a complex Hilbert space with \(\dim H\ge2\), and let \(T=a uv^*\), where \(a>0\) and \(u,v\in H\) are unit vectors. The convention is \(uv^*(x)=\langle x,v\rangle u\). Put \(c=|\langle u,v\rangle|\) and \(s=\sqrt{1-c^2}\). The spatial Davis–Wielandt radius is
\[
\operatorname{dw}(T)=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\bigl(|\langle Tx,x\rangle|^2+\|Tx\|^4\bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
Multiplying \(T\) by a unimodular scalar does not change this radius or its maximizing rays. We may therefore normalize the phase so that \(u=cv+sw\), where \(w\perp v\) is unit whenever \(c<1\). A maximizing *ray* means a one-dimensional complex subspace generated by a unit maximizing vector; phase multiples count as one ray.

**Interior overlap.** For \(0<c<1\), define
\[
G_{a,c}(z)=sc+(1-3c^2-2a^2)z-3scz^2-s^2z^3.
\]
There is exactly one positive root \(z_*=z_*(a,c)\), and the complete maximizing-ray set is the singleton generated by
\[
x_*=(v+z_*w)/\sqrt{1+z_*^2}.
\]
The exact value is
\[
\operatorname{dw}(T)^2=a^2\frac{(c+sz_*)^2+a^2}{(1+z_*^2)^2}.
\]
The function \((a,c)\mapsto z_*(a,c)\) is real analytic on \((0,\infty)\times(0,1)\).

**Boundary overlap.** If \(c=1\), the sole maximizing ray is \(\mathbb Cv\), and \(\operatorname{dw}(T)=a\sqrt{1+a^2}\). If \(c=0\) and \(a^2\ge1/2\), the sole maximizing ray is again \(\mathbb Cv\), and \(\operatorname{dw}(T)=a^2\). If \(c=0\) and \(0<a^2<1/2\), then
\[
\operatorname{dw}(T)=\frac{a}{2\sqrt{1-a^2}},
\quad x\in\operatorname{span}\{u,v\},\qquad |\langle x,v\rangle|^2=\frac{1}{2(1-a^2)}
\]
characterizes all maximizing unit vectors: their \(v\)- and \(u\)-components have these prescribed lengths and arbitrary relative phase, giving a circle of maximizing rays.

**Singular approach to nilpotency.** For fixed \(a>0\), as \(c\downarrow0\),
\[
z_*(a,c)\longrightarrow\sqrt{1-2a^2}\quad\text{if }a^2<\tfrac12;
\qquad z_*(a,c)\sim c^{1/3}\quad\text{if }a^2=\tfrac12;
\qquad z_*(a,c)\sim\frac{c}{2a^2-1}\quad\text{if }a^2>\tfrac12.
\]
At the critical norm the radius itself has the fractional-power expansion displayed above.

## Proof
First restrict maximizing vectors to \(E=\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}\). If \(x\) is unit, its orthogonal projection onto \(E\) has norm \(r\in[0,1]\). Writing the normalized projection as \(y\) when \(r>0\), one has \(\langle Tx,x\rangle=r^2\langle Ty,y\rangle\) and \(\|Tx\|^4=r^4\|Ty\|^4\). Hence the squared objective for \(x\) is \(r^4\) times that for \(y\); because \(T\ne0\) has positive radius, every maximizer has \(r=1\).

For \(0<c<1\), write any ray in \(E\) as \(x=(v+ze^{i\phi}w)/\sqrt{1+z^2}\) with \(z\ge0\), including the endpoint \(x=w\) separately. We have
\[
\|Tx\|^2=\frac{a^2}{1+z^2},\qquad
|\langle Tx,x\rangle|^2=\frac{a^2|c+sze^{i\phi}|^2}{(1+z^2)^2}.
\]
The maximum over phases is uniquely obtained at \(e^{i\phi}=1\) whenever \(z>0\), since \(c,s>0\). Thus the squared objective becomes
\[
F_{a,c}(z)=a^2\frac{(c+sz)^2+a^2}{(1+z^2)^2}.
\]
Direct differentiation gives the exact identity
\[
F_{a,c}'(z)=\frac{2a^2}{(1+z^2)^3}G_{a,c}(z).
\]
Here \(G_{a,c}(0)=sc>0\), \(G_{a,c}(z)\to-\infty\), and
\[
G_{a,c}''(z)=-6sc-6s^2z<0\quad(z\ge0).
\]
A strictly concave function that starts positive and tends to negative infinity has exactly one positive zero; its derivative there is strictly negative. Therefore \(F\) rises strictly until that root and decreases strictly afterward. The omitted endpoint \(x=w\) contributes zero; \(z=0\) is not stationary. This proves global uniqueness and the cubic formula. The implicit-function theorem, since \(G_z(z_*)<0\), proves real analyticity in the interior.

For \(c=0\), one has \(u=w\) and
\[
F_{a,0}(z)=a^2\frac{z^2+a^2}{(1+z^2)^2},\qquad
F_{a,0}'(z)=\frac{2a^2 z(1-2a^2-z^2)}{(1+z^2)^3}.
\]
Thus the maximizing coordinate is \(z=0\) when \(a^2\ge1/2\), and \(z=\sqrt{1-2a^2}\) when \(a^2<1/2\). In the latter case the objective is independent of the relative phase between \(v\) and \(u\). Evaluating gives respectively \(a^4\) and \(a^2/[4(1-a^2)]\) for the square of the radius. For \(c=1\), \(T\) is a scalar multiple of a rank-one orthogonal projection, so maximizing \(|\langle x,v\rangle|\) gives the asserted value and ray.

For the limits as \(c\downarrow0\), note \(s\to1\). If \(a^2<1/2\), the simple positive zero of the limiting polynomial \(z(1-2a^2-z^2)\) persists, proving convergence. If \(a^2>1/2\), the root approaches zero and division of \(G=0\) by \(c\) after setting \(z=c y\) gives \(y\to(2a^2-1)^{-1}\). At \(a^2=1/2\), set \(z=c^{1/3}y\) in \(G=0\); the leading balance is \(1-y^3=0\), and uniqueness forces \(y\to1\). At the same critical norm, a direct expansion along the maximizing root gives
\[
F_{1/\sqrt2,c}(z_*)=\tfrac14+\tfrac34c^{4/3}+o(c^{4/3}).
\]
Taking the square root yields \(\operatorname{dw}(T)=\tfrac12+\tfrac34c^{4/3}+o(c^{4/3})\), as required. The expansion follows by writing \(z_*=c^{1/3}(1+o(1))\), using the terms \(2cz-\tfrac12z^4\) at order \(c^{4/3}\) in the unscaled quotient and observing that all remaining terms are smaller.

## Verification
The proof above establishes the infinite-dimensional result analytically. The accompanying standard-library script `verify_rank_one_dw.py` independently checks the derivative polynomial identity using exact rational arithmetic on several rational Pythagorean parameter triples, the exact rational interior witness
\[
c=\tfrac35,\quad s=\tfrac45,\quad a^2=\tfrac{18}{25},\quad z_*=\tfrac14,\quad \operatorname{dw}(T)^2=\tfrac{156672}{180625},
\]
and the nilpotent endpoint calculation. It also numerically tests the three predicted scaling regimes. These finite checks are not substitutes for the quantified proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Bhattacharya, Kittaneh, Patra, and Satpathy (arXiv:2607.00719v1, first posted 1 July 2026, primary MSC 47A12) prove algebraic Davis–Wielandt-shell geometric properties and inequalities but not this rank-one maximizing-ray bifurcation or fractional cusp. Gao and Duan (early access 21 July 2026) derive inequalities for off-diagonal operator matrices, not this optimization. Earlier Li, Poon, and Sze describe the full Davis–Wielandt shell of quadratic operators as an ellipsoid and explicitly cover rank-one operators as members of that class; their stronger *shell-description theorem* is credited and may be used to recover the present rank-one optimization by a further nontrivial maximization. Their result, however, does not state the maximizing-ray phase transition, the exact cubic optimizer, or its singular \(c^{1/3}\) and \(c^{4/3}\) laws. Bhunia, Bhanja, Bag, and Paul determine radii of special block operators, including square-zero cases, which cover the \(c=0\) endpoint but do not give the transverse overlap bifurcation. The claimed contribution is the complete sharp optimizer response, not a new characterization of the pre-existing shell or a new square-zero endpoint radius.

## Limitations
This is a rank-one theorem, not a claim about arbitrary bounded operators or higher rank. The cusp is an asymptotic statement at fixed \(a=1/\sqrt2\); no uniform joint-error estimate in \((a,c)\) is claimed. The 2011 quadratic-shell theorem supplies geometric information from which related formulas might be derived, and an equivalent unindexed extremizer calculation cannot be ruled out. The September 2026 Orlicz-radius preprint (arXiv:2609.16652) was available only through an abstract in this comparison, so any potential overlap with its inaccessible full results is an explicit residual risk. Independent mathematical assessment has not been performed.
