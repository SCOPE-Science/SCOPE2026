# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. For any boundary trace whose Euclidean diameter is at most \(2\sin(\pi/7)\), monotonicity of chord length in circular separation and the inequality \(4\pi/7<\pi\) place the trace inside an arc of angular length at most \(2\pi/7\). If one of eight regions misses the outer circle, at most seven traces cover the circle, forcing the lower bound. The matching construction is a centered disk of radius \(\sin(\pi/7)\) plus seven radial annular sectors. Exact corner maximization of squared distance proves every piece has diameter at most the claimed value. The equality boundary structure follows from equality in the total arc-length cover.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source defines the same subdivision functional and gives a one-core, seven-outer-region disk example to show failure of the standard eight-partition, but it does not establish the sharp minimum over the entire one-core class. Searches under one-core, boundary-disjoint, seven-boundary-region, circle-cover, and \(2\sin(\pi/7)\) formulations found no statement implying this exact class optimum. Nearby results on bisections, two-center covering, and standard rotational partitions concern different optimization problems or different admissible classes.

Residual risk remains that the elementary boundary-cover lemma may have been recorded under classical circle-covering terminology and later applied to this subdivision problem without being indexed under the maximum-relative-diameter vocabulary.

## Value
PASS. The one-core topology is not an arbitrary parameter slice: it is the concrete mechanism used in the published first disk counterexample beyond the \(k\le6\) general theorem. Determining its exact optimum converts that qualitative construction into a sharp structural benchmark and isolates the outer-circle obstruction that any one-core improvement must meet. The result also gives the forced boundary geometry at equality while making no claim about the still broader unrestricted eight-subdivision problem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The principal comparison is A. Cañete, U. Schnell, and S. Segura Gomis, arXiv:1501.03907v1 / doi:10.1016/j.jmaa.2015.10.053, especially the subdivision definitions, the \(k\le6\) theorem, and Example 4.8. A. Cañete, doi:10.7153/mia-19-25, concerns standard partitions for multi-rotationally symmetric bodies rather than arbitrary one-core eight-subdivisions. The unrestricted eight-subdivision optimum of the unit disk is not determined here.
