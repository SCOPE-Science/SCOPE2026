# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Invariance makes the two coordinate marginals identical and turns the second map coordinate into the exact cubic moment identity
\[
\mathbb E[x^3]=\rho^2\mathbb E[x].
\]
On a nonnegative support, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E[x^2]^2\le\mathbb E[x]\mathbb E[x^3],
\]
which is exactly the sharp variance parabola. Equality in Cauchy-Schwarz restricts the positive support value to \(\rho\); forward invariance removes the off-diagonal candidates, leaving only the two fixed-point atoms. Odd symmetry and the periodic-orbit sign argument are exact consequences of the same identity.

Risk: the variance envelope is deliberately one-sided and does not apply to sign-spanning invariant measures after the merger crisis.

## Originality
PASS. The complete 1994 same-object paper was inspected at equation, attractor-geometry, and crisis level, and its full text was searched for invariant-measure and moment/statistical equivalents. It supplies the exact map and the quadrant-confined chaotic branches but not the accepted moment theorem. Later stochastic Holmes-map work concerns noise-induced escape rather than deterministic stationary moments.

Risk: the initial cubic moment identity is short, so an unindexed equivalent observation remains possible; the sharper one-sided variance envelope and equality classification were not found in the checked literature.

## Value
PASS. The result is matched to a published dynamical regime rather than an arbitrary sign restriction: immediately before the Holmes attractor-merging crisis, the two chaotic branches occupy the first and third quadrants. The theorem gives each such branch a parameter-explicit mean bound, a sharp variance envelope, and an exact fixed-point equality classification, all usable as invariant-statistics consistency tests.
