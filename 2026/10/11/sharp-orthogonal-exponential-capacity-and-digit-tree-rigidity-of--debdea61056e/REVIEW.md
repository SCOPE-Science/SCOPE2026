# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is precise for every odd prime and exponent at least one. Cyclotomic irreducibility prohibits Fourier zeros at modulus \(p\). For higher powers, a lift-by-\(p^{m-1}\) stationary-phase cancellation proves the zero condition outside square-product directions; two stationary residue classes and a nonzero quadratic Gauss sum prove nonvanishing in the remaining directions. The prime-power valuation reduction, sibling-digit equivalence, last-level exclusion, and injective \((u,v)\mapsto v-u\) map together establish the exact maximum. Recomputed exact-cyclotomic tests of 40,746 coefficient pairs across ten moduli and verified the attaining construction. The unlimited parameter range relies on proof, not enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Gurak (DOI: 10.5802/jtnb.665) and Salié supply the classical prime-power Kloosterman method and values; those are not novel. The full texts of Iosevich–Murphy–Pakianathan (arXiv:1405.7657) and Haessig et al. (arXiv:1507.05861) address related hyperbola Fourier spectra, Salem behavior, tiling, and finite-field phenomena, but the relevant examined statements do not give the maximal orthogonal-restriction capacity and necessary-and-sufficient digit-tree packing criterion in this prime-power family. Statement-level semantic and bibliographic searches did not yield an equivalent stronger theorem. Lack of a found match does not certify novelty; earlier harmonic-analysis or finite-ring graph literature might contain an equivalent reduction.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The bound is a sharp, uniform obstruction to Fourier spectrality for a canonical arithmetic curve, improving the mere observation that the restricted Fourier characters are not an obvious basis. The local tree equivalence classifies every orthogonal frequency set, including exact equality mechanisms, across infinitely many residue rings. The digit condition separates p-adic depth from finite-field quadratic geometry, a natural constraint for constructing exponential frames and partial spectra.

## Limits of review
Only the stated unweighted modular hyperbola is addressed; general finite local rings and even moduli are outside scope. The compatible-digit classification is algorithmic, not a closed enumeration of every allowable finite-field digit transversal. Residual historical-equivalence risk persists, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
