# Scientific review: sharp overlap transition for two projections

## Correctness — PASS
The rank-two projection and phase-alignment reductions are valid for arbitrary complex Hilbert spaces because both expectation values depend only on the projection onto the span of the two generating rays. The scalar function \(G_p\) has the derivative sign explicitly controlled by \(F(\theta)-(2p-1)\). The ratio \(F\) is a strict weighted mean of a strictly decreasing cotangent product, with limits \(+\infty\) and \((1+c)/(1-c)\), establishing exactly one or two global optimizing rays as claimed. The critical equality case, the orthogonal/coincident endpoints, and the quadratic \(p=2\) formula were checked separately. Exact rational checks and finite parameter tests support, but do not replace, the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, residual source-access risk
The published 2026 weighted joint-radius study establishes the natural modern setting, while its available abstract gives general estimates rather than the exact rank-one-pair symmetry-breaking profile. The full article was not accessible through the tested public routes or institutional retrieval, and this is a real residual risk, not evidence that its body lacks the result. The inspected relevant sections of the full 2024 two-operator article (notably Theorems 2.3 and 2.25) and the accessible 2023 p-joint projection/moment preprint (notably Section 2) address different implications: general inequalities or compressed coordinate-projection moment geometry, not the sharp overlap-vs-exponent classification. Searches for equivalent formulations, the critical parameter, and potentially dominating two-vector frame results did not yield a covering theorem. A later or unindexed exact calculation could still exist.

## Value — PASS
The two rank-one projections are the smallest noncommuting positive operator pair. The theorem gives a complete, all-real-exponent critical boundary, all extremizing rays, and an explicit algebraic Euclidean branch with a differentiability transition. The exact geometry distinguishes a balanced state from two symmetry-broken states and is a useful benchmark for multi-operator joint-radius bounds, beyond a routine renaming or a lone numerical counterexample.
