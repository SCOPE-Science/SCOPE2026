# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an identity-and-asymptotics claim with explicitly delimited parameters \(0<a<b<3a\), \(0<c<c_*\). The polynomial identity for the Yablonskii invariant curve was reconstructed and checked by exact multivariate polynomial arithmetic. Direct substitution gives the two branch velocities in unrescaled time; their signs follow from the unique positive minimum of \(F=m/(4R)\). These yield the period integral and its strictly positive \(c\)-derivative. The zero-parameter limit follows from an elementary cosine integral. A nondegenerate quadratic minimum of \(F\) at \(x_*\) proves the leading \(\delta^{-1/2}\) divergence and its explicit constant. The same rescaling, supplemented by a global tail estimate and bounded upper-branch time, proves the Cauchy occupation law. Numerical quadrature and exact identity replay in `verify.py` agree with these deductions. They do not replace the limiting proof. No assertion is made about other cycles or about \(c>c_*\).

## Originality — PASS, subject to recorded priority risk

The relevant sources are distinguished by logical implication, not merely by title. Yablonskii's 1966 oval, as reproduced in the full inspected 2019 and 2022 papers, already provides the orbit and admissible discriminant and is not new. The 2019 Cremona result gives geometric equivalence to a Qin circle through a time-changing birational construction. That equivalence does not itself compute the original-time period and normalized time-occupation; one must evaluate the transformed time factor. The 2005 stability article deals with Floquet hyperbolicity rather than physical-time period. The 2022 paper gives a uniqueness mechanism, not the stated sharp endpoint dynamics. Queries of published mathematical records for Yablonskii, its discriminant, Cremona/Qin equivalents, period monotonicity, square-root slowing, and Cauchy occupation did not identify a covering finding. An inaccessible full 1966 original, the 2004 quartic classification, and possible unlocated reductions leave publication priority uncertain; this review does not claim definitive global novelty or independent certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Period and time occupation are natural, physically observable quantities on an already-known isolated algebraic orbit. Its geometric oval persists while the vector field develops a single zero on it: the theorem supplies the exact critical parameter, a sharp nontrivial coefficient for the diverging original-time period, strict parameter response throughout the admissible interval, and a full limiting distribution on the correct square-root spatial scale. Those quantitative results are not ordinary orbit-counting consequences of the invariant algebraic curve and provide a meaningful benchmark for time changes under birational equivalence. They are an exact result for a natural two-parameter oscillator family, not a numerical slice or a small unsupported improvement.
