# Sharp positive Fourier sampling on three consecutive cyclic sites
## Finding
Let \(N\ge3\), \(G=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), and \(A=\{0,1,2\}\subset G\). A positive frequency rule is a set of distinct residues \(\Lambda\subset G\) with strictly positive real weights \(w_\lambda\) such that the restricted characters \(\sqrt{w_\lambda}\,e^{2\pi i\lambda a/N}\), for \(a\in A\), form a Parseval frame in \(\mathbb C^A\) with its counting inner product. Let \(\nu(N)\) be the smallest possible number of frequencies. Then
\[
\boxed{\nu(N)=\begin{cases}3,&3\mid N,\\4,&2\mid N\ \text{and}\ 3\nmid N,\\5,&\gcd(N,6)=1.\end{cases}}
\]
All three-frequency rules are translates of the order-three frequency subgroup, with weights exactly \(1/3\). Every even order not divisible by three admits the explicit four-frequency rule below. Every order coprime to six admits a five-frequency rule; on any specified five-node frequency set the rule, if it exists, has unique explicitly computable weights, given below by a necessary and sufficient strict-positivity test.

In particular, for every prime \(p\ge7\), adjustable positive weights reduce the number of Fourier measurements from the unweighted minimum \(p\) to exactly \(5\).

## Assumptions and scope
Use distinct frequencies, not a multiset, and allow arbitrary strictly positive real scalar weights. Normalize the Parseval bound to \(1\); another common positive scale gives the corresponding tight frame. With \(z_\lambda=e^{2\pi i\lambda/N}\), Parseval tightness is equivalent to
\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}w_\lambda=1,\qquad
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}w_\lambda z_\lambda=0,\qquad
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}w_\lambda z_\lambda^2=0.
\tag{1}
\]
The negative-moment equations follow by complex conjugation. The theorem also applies after translating \(A\) and replacing its common step by any unit of \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), since these operations relabel characters and/or multiply rows by unimodular scalars.

## Proof
**1. Moment reduction and three-node rigidity.** The Gram matrix of the weighted restricted-character columns has entries \(\sum_{\lambda}w_\lambda z_\lambda^{a-a'}\) for \(a,a'\in A\). Its diagonal is \(1\) exactly when the weights sum to \(1\), and its off-diagonal entries vanish exactly when the two complex moments in (1) vanish. Since the signal dimension is \(3\), at least three nonzero columns are required. With exactly three, the resulting \(3\times3\) analysis matrix is unitary. Its column norms force \(w_\lambda=1/3\). Orthogonality of two columns forces their frequency ratio \(u\) to satisfy \(1+u+u^2=0\), hence \(u\) is a nontrivial cubic root of unity. Consequently \(3\mid N\) and all three frequencies form a translated coset of \(\{0,N/3,2N/3\}\). Conversely that coset and equal weights give a unitary matrix, proving the three-node branch and its equality classification.

**2. Four-node obstruction when the order is coprime to six.** Suppose four distinct \(N\)-th roots \(z_1,\ldots,z_4\) have weights (not necessarily positive) satisfying all four homogeneous equations with exponents \(-2,-1,1,2\). Thus the \(4\times4\) matrix with rows \((z_j^{-2})_j,(z_j^{-1})_j,(z_j)_j,(z_j^2)_j\) is singular. Multiplication of its \(j\)-th column by \(z_j^2\) makes it the evaluation matrix for monomials of degrees \(0,1,3,4\). Singularity produces a nonzero polynomial
\[
P(X)=a_0+a_1X+a_3X^3+a_4X^4
\]
vanishing at all four distinct roots. It must be a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\prod_{j=1}^4(X-z_j)\), so comparison of the absent quadratic coefficient gives
\[
\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}z_i z_j=0.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a vanishing sum of six \(N\)-th roots of unity, counted with multiplicity. Lam and Leung's vanishing-sum theorem [3, Theorem 5.2] says that the length of such a sum belongs to the additive semigroup generated by the prime divisors of \(N\). If \(\gcd(N,6)=1\), every prime divisor is at least five, and \(6\) is not in this semigroup. This contradicts (2). Thus no four or fewer nodes work, even if complex signed weights were allowed.

**3. Four-node construction at the even orders.** If \(N\ge4\) is even and not divisible by three, let
\[
k=\lfloor N/4\rfloor,\quad \theta=2\pi k/N,\quad c=\cos\theta,\quad d=\cos(2\theta),\quad t=\frac1{2(1-d)}.
\]
Take the four frequencies \(\{0,k,N-k,N/2\}\) with respective weights
\[
\boxed{w_0=-t(c+d),\quad w_k=w_{N-k}=t,\quad w_{N/2}=t(c-d).}
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(N/6<k\le N/4\), so \(0\le c<1/2\), and \(d=2c^2-1<-c\). All weights in (3) are therefore strictly positive. The sum of the weights is \(2t(1-d)=1\); the first Fourier moment is \(-t(c+d)+2tc-t(c-d)=0\), and the second is \(-t(c+d)+2td+t(c-d)=0\). This is a positive Parseval rule. Three nodes are impossible by Step 1, proving the even branch.

**4. Five-node existence for orders coprime to six.** For each \(N\)-th root \(z\), form the point
\[
\Phi(z)=(\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Im}z,\operatorname{Re}z^2,\operatorname{Im}z^2)\in\mathbb R^4.
\]
The uniform average of these points over all \(N\)-th roots is the origin, because their first and second moments vanish for \(N\ge3\). Hence the origin is in their convex hull. Carathéodory's theorem in \(\mathbb R^4\) supplies a convex representation using at most five points. Removing any zero-weight points gives a strictly positive rule with at most five frequencies. Step 2 excludes four or fewer, hence precisely five are needed and exist.

**5. Complete weight criterion at five fixed nodes.** Fix five distinct \(N\)-th roots \(z_1,\ldots,z_5\). Let
\[
L_j(X)=\prod_{\ell\ne j}\frac{X-z_\ell}{z_j-z_\ell}
\]
be their Lagrange polynomials. There is exactly one complex vector satisfying the five moment equations with exponents \(-2,-1,0,1,2\), because the corresponding Vandermonde matrix is invertible. By extracting the coefficient of \(X^2\) in the interpolation identity for each polynomial of degree at most four, that vector is
\[
\boxed{w_j=z_j^2[X^2]L_j(X)=
\frac{z_j^2\sum_{\substack{k<\ell\\k,\ell\ne j}}z_kz_\ell}
{\prod_{\ell\ne j}(z_j-z_\ell)}.}
\tag{4}
\]
Complex conjugation of the moment equations and uniqueness imply that all \(w_j\) are real. Thus a specified five-frequency set supports a positive Parseval rule **if and only if** all five explicit real numbers in (4) are strictly positive; when it does, those are its unique normalized weights. This also determines all optimal frequency sets by an exact finite algebraic test. The theorem follows.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` constructs valid positive rules for every order \(3\le N<80\), verifies their first two moments numerically, and checks the five-node interpolation formula whenever applicable. It exhaustively tests the absence of a vanishing six-term symmetric function \(\sum_{i<j}z_iz_j\) for every four-node subset at orders \(5\le N<38\) coprime to six. The completed replay returned `VERIFY_OK`, with \(231\) moment identities, \(26\) five-node interpolation checks, and \(202467\) four-node nonexistence tests. These numerical checks are corroborative only; the theorem for all orders rests on the algebraic argument, Lam–Leung's theorem, and Carathéodory's theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Dutkay and Ranasinghe [1] construct weighted Parseval exponential frames on self-affine measures. Frederick and Mayeli [2] develop finite frame spectral pairs and Fourier-frame constructions, primarily with unweighted discrete character families. The exact positive-weight cardinality law for a fixed consecutive triple, its cyclotomic six-term obstruction, and its strict-positivity criterion are different from their stated results. The classical vanishing-sum theorem of Lam and Leung [3] is a critical, explicitly credited input; it supplies the arithmetic obstruction, not the positive quadrature classification. For an odd prime \(p\), the unweighted necessity of all \(p\) frequencies also follows directly from the cyclotomic minimal polynomial of \(e^{2\pi i/p}\); that baseline is not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The cardinality formula concerns a three-point arithmetic progression of unit step in a finite cyclic group; it does not determine the corresponding minimum for arbitrary three-point sets, longer progressions, constrained weight ratios, or unnormalized inverse-problem stability. Step 2 relies on the established Lam–Leung vanishing-sum theorem. The five-node positivity criterion is exact but is not a closed enumeration of five-node optimal supports for every \(N\). The computational sample is finite and does not independently prove any infinite statement. Related unindexed positive-quadrature and circle-design literature could contain an equivalent formulation.
