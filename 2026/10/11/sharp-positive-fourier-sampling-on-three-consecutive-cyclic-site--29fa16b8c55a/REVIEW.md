# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The weighted Parseval Gram identity is equivalent to vanishing moments of orders one and two. With three columns, unitarity forces equal weights and cubic-root ratios; this proves the entire three-node classification. For any four distinct roots, singularity of the four homogeneous moment rows forces vanishing of the elementary symmetric sum of degree two, a length-six vanishing sum of roots of unity. Lam–Leung Theorem 5.2 rules this out whenever all primes dividing the group order are at least five. The even-order weights are strictly positive by an explicit cosine inequality and satisfy both moments by direct algebra. Carathéodory gives five nodes in the remaining case, and Lagrange interpolation determines the unique possible weights at fixed five-node support. The exact scope is all integer orders at least three; computations provide independent finite corroboration but do not replace the external vanishing-sum theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
The closest weighted-frame source, Dutkay–Ranasinghe (arXiv:1710.01224), constructs self-affine weighted Parseval frames, not this exact minimum on a prescribed finite cyclic triple. Frederick–Mayeli (arXiv:2010.05667) treats general frame spectral pairs without the complete positive-weight cardinality trichotomy. The known three-point spectral classification covers the three-frequency orthogonal-basis case, but not the positive four- and five-frequency rules. A published prime-power minimum for *unweighted* tight frames imposes the stronger requirement of identical frequency coefficients: for prime orders it gives \(p\), while the present optimally weighted answer is five. Lam–Leung's classical theorem supplies the crucial six-term obstruction and is credited explicitly, not claimed as original. An unindexed quadrature/design reformulation is the main residual risk.

## Value — PASS
Three consecutive spatial samples are the smallest natural cyclic Fourier reconstruction problem whose tightness needs two nontrivial moments. The theorem gives the exact minimum sample budget for every group order, identifies divisibility phase transitions, exhibits explicit four-node formulas, and gives a complete fixed-support five-node positivity test. Its prime-order improvement from \(p\) unweighted characters to five weighted characters is unbounded as \(p\) grows, so the result is not merely a renaming or an incidental small-case computation.
