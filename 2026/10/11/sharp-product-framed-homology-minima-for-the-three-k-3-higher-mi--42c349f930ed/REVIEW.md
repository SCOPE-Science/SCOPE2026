# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The published product-framed Mayer--Vietoris formula splits first homology as the surface cokernel plus \(\mathbb Z/\gcd(p,q)\), and the adapted covering maps have cokernels \(\mathbb Z/p\) and \(\mathbb Z/q\). The surface cokernel maps surjectively onto their direct sum, forcing the lower divisor \(pq\gcd(p,q)\). The three explicit gluing actions are products of integral symplectic transvections; exact exhaustive maximal-minor calculations give surface determinantal divisors \(16\), \(9\), and \(12\), attaining the lower bound.

## Originality
PASS. The primary paper explicitly leaves the minimal-order Smith problem open for the three \(k=3\) model cases and records only the weaker \(\operatorname{lcm}(p,q)\) divisor. The new part is not merely the lower-bound corollary: explicit symplectic gluing certificates attain the stronger divisor in all three cases, yielding exact minima and exact minimizing groups. Targeted searches of the primary manuscript, the earlier \(k=1\) paper metadata/abstract, published finding records, and general web literature found no statement or certificate covering these three rectangular Smith computations.

## Value
PASS. These are the three model cases singled out by the source's open Question 13.1. Determining their exact minimum integral first-homology orders resolves the first sharp Smith-form benchmark for the higher rectangular regime and distinguishes it from the \(k=1\) determinant setting. The result also isolates a reusable product-framed divisor that is strictly stronger than the published universal \(\operatorname{lcm}(p,q)\) bound.

The claim is intentionally restricted to equatorially product-framed gluings; arbitrary framing terms and complete group classification remain open.
