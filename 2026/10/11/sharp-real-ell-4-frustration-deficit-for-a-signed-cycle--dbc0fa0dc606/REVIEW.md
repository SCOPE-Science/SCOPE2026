# Review of the sharp signed-cycle \(\ell_4\) asymptotic

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed limit is a theorem about real induced operator norms. The exact fourth-power identity reduces the norm to a minimization of nonnegative edge defects. Under the transformation \(g=x|x|\), explicit same-sign/opposite-sign factorizations establish a global Sobolev compactness bound and the sharper coefficient \(3/8\) away from zeros. A weak-\(H^1\) limit has antiperiodic boundary conditions and unit \(L^2\) norm; the sharp antiperiodic Poincaré inequality gives the lower bound. Sampling a transformed cosine gives the matching upper bound. A block-matrix isometry proves the equivalence with a signed-cycle adjacency norm. Finite diagnostics are not relied upon to establish a limit.

## Originality — PASS

The closest inspected results are Guo–Mohar's skew-circulant Hermitian eigenvalue formulas, Li–Li's quadratic signed-Laplacian frustration bounds, and Hameed–Biju–Stanić's exact signed-cycle spectral balance measure. The last work is particularly close in its graph family, but its exact formulas involve \(\ell_2\) eigenvalues and differences of spectra, not the real induced \(\ell_4\) norm. The latter is not obtained by the ordinary eigenvalue formula because Fourier diagonalization is not \(\ell_4\)-isometric. Related finite-dimensional \(\ell_4\) matrix inequalities and spectral results give no all-size leading coefficient. A residual risk is incomplete indexing of historical induced-norm or antiperiodic-shift results; the literature comparison is not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

Frustration prevents a signed cycle from attaining the positive cycle's norm \(2\). Determining the exact order-\(n^{-2}\) loss in a non-Hilbert space is a natural quantitative stability invariant of sparse signed operators, not an arbitrary finite entry or parameter choice. The exact coefficient differs from the computable Hilbert coefficient, and the proof identifies the effective continuum variational structure behind that difference. This does not require a finite-size closed formula or a downstream application to be mathematically substantive.

## Scope and remaining risks

The proof is real-scalar and first order in the finite-size deficit. There is no claim about complex \(\ell_4\), arbitrary weighted cycles, other exponents, or complete higher-order asymptotic expansions. No independent validation has been performed. The comparison sources' relevant sections were inspected in full text, but the entire historical literature is not guaranteed to have been retrieved.
