The Gegenbauer partial sums have a sharp zero-geometry transition at \(\alpha=1\): for \(\alpha\ge1\) every zero is eventually real, while for every \(0<\alpha<1\) the proportion of real zeros tends to \(0\). Below the threshold, the sums converge to a positive interior limit while their derivatives remain asymptotically real-rooted, forcing almost all zeros off the real axis.
