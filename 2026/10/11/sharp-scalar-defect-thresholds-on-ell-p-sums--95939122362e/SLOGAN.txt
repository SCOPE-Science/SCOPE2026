For \(X=\mathbb K\oplus_pY\) with \(Y\ne0\), the scalar-coordinate contraction \(T_\lambda(a,y)=(\lambda a,0)\), \(0\le\lambda<1\), has a norm-valued square-root defect exactly when \(\lambda=0\), or \(p=2\), or \(1<p<2\) and \(\lambda^2\le 2^{2/p}(p-1)/p\). Over complex spaces this is also the sharp isometric-dilation threshold in the sense of Jana–Pal–Roy: above the Hilbert exponent, every nonzero scalar-coordinate contraction fails, however small its norm.
