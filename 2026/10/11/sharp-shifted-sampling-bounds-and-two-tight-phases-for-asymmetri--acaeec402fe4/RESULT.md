# Sharp shifted sampling bounds and two tight phases for asymmetric two-sided exponentials

## Finding
For arbitrary \(a,b>0\), the shift-invariant space generated by the two-sided exponential \(g(t)=e^{at}\) for \(t<0\) and \(g(t)=e^{-bt}\) for \(t\geq0\) has an exactly solvable unit-density sampling problem. The optimal lower and upper \(L^2\) sampling constants on each translate of the integer lattice are given by two explicit endpoint expressions. There are precisely **two distinct offsets** in one period where the sampling operator is a scalar multiple of an isometry. They straddle the unique unstable offset, and the optimal lower bound vanishes quadratically at that offset. Among all translated grids, the integer-aligned grid uniquely maximizes the optimal lower bound.

Write \(r=e^{-b}\), \(s=e^{-a}\), and for \(0\le y<1\) put
\[
P_y=e^{-by}-rs e^{ay},\qquad Q_y=s(e^{ay}-e^{-by}).
\]
Define real constants \(u,v\) by the following formulas. When \(a\ne b\),
\[
\begin{split}
u&=\frac{a+b}{2(a-b)}\left[\frac{(1-r^2)(1+s^2)}{b}-\frac{(1-s^2)(1+r^2)}{a}\right],\\
v&=\frac{a+b}{a-b}\left[\frac{r(1-s^2)}{a}-\frac{s(1-r^2)}{b}\right].
\end{split}
\]
When \(a=b=c\), so \(r=s=e^{-c}\), use the continuous-limit formulas
\[
u=\frac{1-r^4}{c}-4r^2,\qquad
v=2r(1+r^2)-\frac{2r(1-r^2)}{c}.
\]
In all cases \(u>v>0\). Define
\[
E_+(y)=\frac{(P_y+Q_y)^2}{u+v},\qquad
E_-(y)=\frac{(P_y-Q_y)^2}{u-v}.
\]
For every \(f\) in the space, the sharp sampling inequality is
\[
\boxed{\min\{E_+(y),E_-(y)\}\,\|f\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2\ \le\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}|f(k+y)|^2\ \le\ \max\{E_+(y),E_-(y)\}\,\|f\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}^2.}
\]
Both constants are optimal for every \(y\), including the zero lower constant at the exceptional offset.

Let
\[
\lambda_\pm=\frac{u\pm\sqrt{u^2-v^2}}{v},\qquad
y_\pm=\frac1{a+b}\log\!\left(\frac{\lambda_\pm+s}{s(1+r\lambda_\pm)}\right),
\]
and
\[
y_0=\frac1{a+b}\log\!\left(\frac{1+s}{s(1+r)}\right).
\]
Then \(0<y_-<y_0<y_+<1\). The sampling inequality is **tight** (equal nonzero optimal bounds) exactly at \(y=y_-\) and \(y=y_+\). Stable sampling fails exactly at \(y=y_0\). The sharp lower bound reaches its unique global maximum over \(0\leq y<1\) at \(y=0\), with value
\[
\boxed{\max_{0\le y<1} A_y=A_0=\frac{(1-rs)^2}{u+v}.}
\]
Finally, defining \(h=(1+s)e^{-b y_0}>0\), the optimal lower bound obeys
\[
\boxed{A_y=\frac{(a+b)^2h^2}{u-v}(y-y_0)^2+O(|y-y_0|^3),\quad y\to y_0.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Fix arbitrary real numbers \(a,b>0\). The function \(g=g_{a,b}\) is defined pointwise by the preceding two-sided exponential formula, with \(g(0)=1\). Define
\[
V^2(g)=\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}c_jg(\cdot-j): (c_j)\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\right\}\subset L^2(\mathbb R).
\]
The series has its standard \(L^2\) meaning; point values are well-defined since \(g\) decays exponentially and \(\ell^2\) sequences yield absolutely convergent sample sums by Cauchy--Schwarz. The inequality uses **unweighted samples** \(\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}|f(k+y)|^2\), with no factor of lattice density. The frequency variable in the proof ranges over \(\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\); all assertion quantifiers cover every such \(\theta\), every coefficient sequence in \(\ell^2\), and every real \(a,b>0\). The two offsets are understood modulo integers.

## Proof
**Step 1 (the continuous energy Gramian).** Let \(C_n=\int_{\mathbb R}g(t)g(t-n)\,dt\), so \(C_{-n}=C_n\). Direct integration over the three intervals separated by \(0\) and \(n\) gives, for integers \(n\ge0\),
\[
C_n=\frac{a+b}{2(a-b)}\left(\frac{r^n}{b}-\frac{s^n}{a}\right)\quad(a\ne b),
\qquad C_n=r^n\left(n+\frac1a\right)\quad(a=b).
\]
Fourier summation of the two geometric series gives
\[
G(\theta):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}C_ne^{in\theta}
=\frac{u+v\cos\theta}{(1+r^2-2r\cos\theta)(1+s^2-2s\cos\theta)}.
\]
The Fourier transform \(\widehat g(\xi)=(a+b)/[(a-i\xi)(b+i\xi)]\) never vanishes on the real axis. Consequently its periodized squared modulus \(G(\theta)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}|\widehat g(\theta+2\pi m)|^2\) is strictly positive, with upper and lower positive finite bounds. Thus the integer translates of \(g\) form a Riesz basis for \(V^2(g)\), and \(u-v>0\). A direct simplification for \(a\ne b\) yields
\[
v=\frac{2rs(a+b)}{a-b}\left(\frac{\sinh a}{a}-\frac{\sinh b}{b}\right)>0,
\]
because \(\sinh t/t\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\). The limit \(a=b\) gives \(v=4r^2(\cosh a-\sinh(a)/a)>0\). Hence \(u>v>0\), as required.

**Step 2 (sampling multiplier).** Fourier diagonalization of the coefficient sequence uses
\[
Z_y(\theta)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}g(y-j)e^{ij\theta}
=\frac{e^{-by}}{1-re^{-i\theta}}+\frac{se^{ay}e^{i\theta}}{1-se^{i\theta}}.
\]
Multiplication of denominators gives the exact identity
\[
\frac{|Z_y(\theta)|^2}{G(\theta)}
=\frac{P_y^2+Q_y^2+2P_yQ_y\cos\theta}{u+v\cos\theta}.
\]
The squared \(L^2\) norm of \(f=\sum c_j g(\cdot-j)\) is the integral of \(|\widehat c(\theta)|^2G(\theta)\), while the sample energy is the integral of \(|\widehat c(\theta)|^2|Z_y(\theta)|^2\), each with measure \(d\theta/(2\pi)\). Their optimal ratio bounds are the essential minimum and maximum of the displayed continuous quotient. This quotient is fractional-linear in \(t=\cos\theta\in[-1,1]\), with positive denominator \(u+vt\), so its extrema occur at \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\). This proves the two explicit optimal constants. Optimality is sharp: choose coefficient Fourier transforms concentrated in progressively shorter arcs at the extremizing angle; they are legitimate \(L^2\) functions, and the quotient tends to the endpoint value.

**Step 3 (complete tightness classification).** For \(0<y<1\), one has \(P_y,Q_y>0\). The ratio
\[
\frac{Q_y}{P_y}=\frac{s(e^{(a+b)y}-1)}{1-rs e^{(a+b)y}}
\]
increases continuously and strictly from \(0\) to \(+\infty\). The continuous quotient in Step 2 is constant in \(t\) precisely if
\[
2u P_yQ_y=v(P_y^2+Q_y^2),
\]
i.e., if \(Q_y/P_y\) is one of the two reciprocal positive roots \(\lambda_\pm\) of \(v\lambda^2-2u\lambda+v=0\). They satisfy \(0<\lambda_-<1<\lambda_+\). Solving for \(y\) yields the stated \(y_\pm\), which are distinct and interior. At the unique offset \(Q_y=P_y\), the ratio vanishes at \(\theta=\pi\); solving yields \(y_0\). Thus \(y_-<y_0<y_+\) and the precise tightness and failure assertions follow. At the two tight offsets, the quotient is a positive constant at every frequency; at no other offset is it constant.

**Step 4 (global lower-bound optimum).** Both \(P_y+Q_y=(1-s)e^{-by}+s(1-r)e^{ay}\) and \(P_y-Q_y=(1+s)e^{-by}-s(1+r)e^{ay}\) have absolute values at most \(L=1-rs\) for \(0\le y\le1\). The former is strictly convex with equal endpoint values \(L\), so it is strictly less than \(L\) in the interior; the latter decreases strictly from \(L\) to \(-L\). Consequently
\[
A_y\le E_+(y)<\frac{L^2}{u+v}=A_0
\]
for every \(0<y<1\). At \(y=0\) one has \(P_0=L\), \(Q_0=0\), and the quotient decreases in \(\cos\theta\) because \(v>0\). This proves uniqueness of the maximizing phase.

**Step 5 (quadratic degeneration).** At \(y_0\), put \(h=(1+s)e^{-b y_0}=s(1+r)e^{a y_0}\). Differentiating \(P_y-Q_y\) gives the derivative \(-(a+b)h\) at \(y_0\). As \(E_+(y_0)>0\), the lower bound near \(y_0\) is exactly \(E_-(y)\). Taylor's theorem now gives the asserted sharp positive quadratic coefficient. No bound of larger order is possible.

## Verification
A standalone standard-library script, `verify_sampling.py`, checks the derived closed forms against independent truncations of the Zak and energy Fourier series for six parameter pairs; verifies that the two offsets yield equal optimal bounds, the unstable offset yields zero, the integer phase maximizes the lower bound over a grid, and confirms the local quadratic coefficient numerically. It also checks the symmetric closed forms numerically. These finite computations are **consistency tests**, not the proof of universality; Steps 1--5 provide the proof for every positive pair of parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
De Dios Pont--Gröchenig--Liehr--Shafkulovska--Taylor (arXiv:2608.04992v1, first submitted August 5, 2026, especially Section 5) prove general qualitative sampling stability for totally positive generators at shifted lattices except the Zak-zero shift. The present result treats the explicit two-sided exponential family and determines the exact best constants, the two tight sampling phases, the unique optimal lower-bound phase, and the sharp local rate of instability, none of which follow numerically from that qualitative theorem. Kloos--Stöckler (arXiv:1311.7359, Sections 3 and 5) analyze Zak zeros and near-critical **Gabor frame** lower bounds for even two-sided exponential windows; that time-frequency lattice perturbation is distinct from the translated critical-density sampling bounds proved here. Ulanovskii--Zlotnikov (arXiv:2402.03090) treat sampling density in quasi shift-invariant spaces for other meromorphic and entire function families, not this explicit exact phase profile. Classical Gramian diagonalization is used as a method and is not claimed as a novel lemma.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns one generator family with one kernel break point, one sample per unit interval, integer translates, and the unweighted \(L^2\) sampling norm. It does not address arbitrary totally positive generators, irregular sampling, weighted grids, or near-critical Gabor frame bounds. The symbol vanishes at one frequency at \(y=y_0\), which eliminates a positive lower frame bound; this alone does **not** exhibit a nonzero function having every integer-grid sample equal to zero. Although the stated primary sources do not cover the quantitative classification, an equivalent older formula in classical exponential-spline or sampling literature cannot be categorically excluded without exhaustive bibliographic review.
