# Sharp triaxial octahedron shadow bound and quadratic shape stability

## Finding
For any positive real numbers \(a,b,c\), let
\[
K(a,b,c)=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm a e_1,\pm b e_2,\pm c e_3\}\subset\mathbb R^3.
\]
Choose a plane uniformly with respect to rotation-invariant measure on the Grassmannian of two-dimensional linear subspaces, and let \(N(a,b,c)\) be the number of vertices of the orthogonal projection of \(K(a,b,c)\) onto this plane. Define \(p(a,b,c)=\Pr\{N(a,b,c)=6\}\). Then for **every** \(a,b,c>0\),
\[
p(a,b,c)\le p(1,1,1)=\frac4\pi\arctan\frac{\sqrt2}{5}
=0.350959312184\ldots,
\]
with equality **if and only if** \(a=b=c\). Equivalently, \(\mathbb E N(a,b,c)\le 4+(8/\pi)\arctan(\sqrt2/5)\), with the same equality condition.

This is a global optimization over both independent axis ratios, rather than over a single family with two equal axes. The optimum is quantitatively rigid: set \(\ell=(\log a,\log b,\log c)\), \(\bar\ell=(\log a+\log b+\log c)/3\) and \(\tau=(\sum_i(\ell_i-\bar\ell)^2)^{1/2}\). As \(\tau\to0\), uniformly in the overall scale and the direction of the anisotropy,
\[
p(1,1,1)-p(a,b,c)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\pi}\sum_{i=1}^3(\ell_i-\bar\ell)^2+O(\tau^3).
\]
Thus the quadratic coefficient is sharp and isotropic in centered log-side coordinates.

## Assumptions and scope
The Euclidean metric is fixed. The three axes of the polytope are pairwise perpendicular, and the axis lengths are strictly positive; there is no restriction on their ratios. This theorem concerns orthogonal, *not arbitrary oblique*, linear images of the regular octahedron. Projection planes carry normalized Haar measure, equivalently their unoriented normal lines are uniformly random on the sphere. Degenerate normal directions have zero measure. A generic planar projection has either four or six vertices.

Write \(L_{ab}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\), \(L_{ac}=\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\), and \(L_{bc}=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\), and introduce
\[
D=L_{ab}L_{ac}L_{bc}+a^2L_{bc}+b^2L_{ac}+c^2L_{ab}.
\]
The following exact formula is the useful geometric reduction, **not** the novelty claim by itself:
\[
p(a,b,c)=\frac4\pi\arctan\!\left(\frac{2abc}{D}\right).
\]

## Proof
**Projected-vertex criterion.** Let \(u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\) be a unit normal to the projection plane and let \(q_i\) be the projection of \(a_i e_i\), where \((a_1,a_2,a_3)=(a,b,c)\). These projected vectors satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\frac{u_i}{a_i}q_i=0.
\]
Almost surely all coordinates of \(u\) are nonzero and the projected vectors span the plane. For fixed \(i\), the pair \(\pm q_i\) is not a vertex pair of the centrally symmetric projected polygon exactly when the vector \(q_i\) lies in the rhombus \(\operatorname{conv}\{\pm q_j,\pm q_k\}\); using the displayed linear relation, this is equivalent to
\[
\frac{|u_i|}{a_i}\ge \frac{|u_j|}{a_j}+\frac{|u_k|}{a_k}.
\]
The events for different \(i\) are mutually exclusive except on null boundaries. Consequently all six vertices survive precisely when the positive triple \((|u_1|/a,|u_2|/b,|u_3|/c)\) satisfies all three strict triangle inequalities.

**Exact angular reduction.** Restrict to one spherical octant, say \(u_i>0\). The three triangle inequalities describe the intersection of the unit sphere with the simplicial cone generated by
\[
(a,b,0),\qquad (a,0,c),\qquad (0,b,c).
\]
For three nonzero vectors \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) spanning a pointed trihedral cone with positive pairwise dot products, its spherical area is
\[
\Omega=2\arctan\frac{|\det(v_1,v_2,v_3)|}
{|v_1||v_2||v_3|+(v_1\cdot v_2)|v_3|+(v_1\cdot v_3)|v_2|+(v_2\cdot v_3)|v_1|}.
\]
This standard solid-angle identity also follows from the spherical triangle half-angle formula; the denominator is positive for these vectors, so no branch choice is ambiguous. Their determinant has absolute value \(2abc\), while the denominator is \(D\). There are eight equally likely octants, hence \(p=8\Omega/(4\pi)=(4/\pi)\arctan(2abc/D)\).

**Sharp global inequality.** Elementary arithmetic-geometric mean inequalities give
\[
\frac{L_{ab}L_{ac}L_{bc}}{abc}\ge2\sqrt2,
\qquad
\frac{a^2L_{bc}+b^2L_{ac}+c^2L_{ab}}{abc}
\ge\sqrt2\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{bc}}+\frac{b}{\sqrt{ac}}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{ab}}\right)\ge3\sqrt2.
\]
For the first bound apply \(x^2+y^2\ge2xy\) to each pair. For the second first use \(\sqrt{b^2+c^2}\ge\sqrt{2bc}\), then the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the three terms whose product is one. Equality throughout holds only when \(a=b=c\). Thus \(D/(abc)\ge5\sqrt2\), strictly otherwise. Because the arctangent is increasing, the asserted sharp probability bound and equality classification follow. In particular \(\mathbb E N=4(1-p)+6p=4+2p\).

**Local sharp quadratic law.** The scale-free function \(F(\ell)=D/(abc)\) is real analytic and invariant under coordinate permutations and common translations of \(\ell_i\). On the plane \(\sum_i\ell_i=0\), its Hessian at the origin is therefore a scalar multiple of the Euclidean metric. It is enough to compute along \(\ell=(t,-t,0)\). Direct Taylor expansion from the displayed explicit radicals gives
\[
F(t,-t,0)=5\sqrt2+\frac{27\sqrt2}{4}t^2+O(t^4).
\]
Substitution into \(p=(4/\pi)\arctan(2/F)\) yields
\[
p(e^t,e^{-t},1)=p(1,1,1)-\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}t^2+O(t^4).
\]
Here \(\sum_i(\ell_i-\bar\ell)^2=2t^2\). Symmetry and the Taylor theorem complete the claimed invariant expansion and its uniformity with respect to centered log-shape directions. The strictly positive quadratic coefficient is exact.

## Verification
Run the standalone `verify.py` with Python 3, NumPy, and SciPy. The checked package version returned `VERIFY_OK 1527004`, including \(1{,}500{,}000\) independent Gaussian-direction triangle tests over five noncongruent shapes, \(2{,}000\) direct planar convex-hull vertex-count checks, \(25{,}000\) sampled anisotropic sharp-bound comparisons, and two log-direction Hessian tests (plus two constant checks). Checks compare the analytic formula with independently generated geometry. A separate symbolic series expansion in the direction \((t,-t,0)\) gives coefficient \(27\sqrt2/4\) for \(F\) and \(-\sqrt2/\pi\) for \(p\), matching the paper proof. These finite checks are not relied upon to prove the all-positive-parameter assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Finch [1], Section 2.3, gives the **regular** octahedron's two shadow-vertex probabilities; in particular the right-hand side of the sharp bound was already known. Böröczky and Henk [2] give expected face counts for randomly projected **regular** high-dimensional polytopes. Black and Criado [3] bound average random-shadow size for general fixed polytopes in terms of geometric and combinatorial parameters; their theorems are not a sharp all-axis-ratio classifier. The solid-angle identity used here is standard [4]. The global three-independent-axis maximum, equality classification and sharp local log-shape Hessian are the assertions under consideration; the known regular probability and the solid-angle identity are not presented as new discoveries. The finite-dimensional general probability reduction has also appeared in related treatments and is included here only to make the optimizing argument self-contained.

## Limitations
The theorem does not address general nonorthogonal affine octahedra, nonuniform direction distributions, projections to dimensions other than two, or higher-dimensional cross-polytopes. It does not claim that all origin-symmetric six-vertex bodies have maximized six-vertex shadow probability at the regular octahedron. The quadratic expansion is local in centered logarithmic side coordinates; no explicit globally valid uniform quadratic constant for arbitrary anisotropy is asserted. Numerical checking supplies diagnostics, not an independent mathematical audit. Historical literature may contain an equivalent optimization under different probabilistic or solid-angle terminology.
