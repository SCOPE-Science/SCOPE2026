# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The event that all six projected antipodal vertices remain extreme is exactly the event that three positive scaled absolute direction coordinates form a nondegenerate triangle. Their feasible directions constitute eight congruent simplicial spherical triangles. A positive-branch trihedral solid-angle formula yields the exact probability. Pairwise arithmetic-geometric mean inequalities and then a three-term arithmetic-geometric mean inequality show that the reciprocal arctangent argument has its unique minimum when all axes coincide. A permutation- and scale-invariant Taylor expansion supplies the exact positive log-shape Hessian. The independent convex-hull and Gaussian-direction diagnostics are consistent with, but are not substitutes for, the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual historical risk
Finch's inspected full paper gives the exact regular-octahedron probability but not a sharp extremality theorem throughout the two-dimensional triaxial parameter space. The full regular-polytope projection preprint of Böröczky and Henk focuses on regular polytopes. The complete arXiv HTML text of Black and Criado addresses general average-shadow bounds rather than a sharp formula with an anisotropy equality case. A previously available formula and a restricted one-parameter axial maximization do not logically imply the global maximization over all positive axis triples or its log-shape Hessian. The formula for the probability is not claimed new; the new comparison is global over two shape degrees of freedom. Searches cannot establish novelty, and an equivalent optimization could exist in earlier solid-angle literature.

## Value — PASS
The number of vertices of a random shadow controls geometric and algorithmic projection complexity. This theorem identifies the exact worst-case average combinatorial size in the natural full triaxial orthogonal-axis octahedron family, and gives uniqueness and an explicit perturbative loss. Going from a single stretch parameter to the full two-dimensional shape moduli is mathematically substantive: one-axis monotonicity cannot rule out off-axis maximizers. The proof explains the rigidity through a coupled sharp pairwise-product inequality rather than through a finite search.

Related average-shadow estimates appear in Böröczky–Henk's regular-polytope work and Black–Criado (arXiv:2406.06936).
