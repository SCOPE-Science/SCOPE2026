# Sharp two-harmonic area minimum for dihedrally invariant constant-width bodies

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge3\) and every \(w>0\), consider planar convex bodies invariant under the fixed dihedral group generated by reflection in the horizontal axis and rotation through \(2\pi/n\), having constant width \(w\) and support function of Fourier degree at most \(3n\). Every such body has support function
\[
h(\theta)=\frac w2+a\cos(n\theta)+b\cos(3n\theta).
\]
Put
\[
r_n=\frac{n^2-1}{9n^2-1},\qquad q_n=\sqrt{1-\frac{r_n}{3}},\qquad F_n=\frac{6(1+q_n)}{(2+q_n)^2}.
\]
The **sharp minimum area**, attained by exactly two bodies in these fixed coordinates, is
\[
\boxed{\min_K\operatorname{Area}(K)=\frac{\pi w^2}{4}-\frac{\pi w^2}{8(n^2-1)}F_n.}
\]
Explicitly, let
\[
s_n=\frac{1}{3(1+q_n)},\qquad
c_n=\sqrt{\frac{27s_n}{(1+3s_n)^3}}.
\]
The two and only two minimizing support functions are
\[
\boxed{h_\pm(\theta)=\frac w2\pm\left(-\frac{wc_n}{2(n^2-1)}\cos(n\theta)
+\frac{ws_nc_n}{2(9n^2-1)}\cos(3n\theta)\right).}
\]
They are interchanged by a rotation through \(\pi/n\). Their continuous radius-of-curvature densities vanish at exactly \(2n\) normal directions and are positive elsewhere. The exact disk-area deficit is \(F_n\) times the sharp deficit in the one-harmonic subclass, and \(F_n>4/3\) for every allowed \(n\). Thus admitting the second symmetric odd harmonic improves the attainable area reduction by more than one third.

## Assumptions and scope
A convex body means a compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^2\) with nonempty interior. Its support function is \(h(\theta)=\sup_{x\in K} \langle x,(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\rangle\). Constant width means \(h(\theta)+h(\theta+\pi)=w\) for all real \(\theta\). The dihedral action is fixed about the origin; translation terms are excluded by the prescribed rotation symmetry. The Fourier degree restriction is exactly \(3n\), not a smoothness assumption on arbitrary constant-width bodies. It is the first nontrivial spectral cutoff beyond the single frequency \(n\), since odd dihedral symmetry admits precisely \(n,3n,5n,\ldots\). Weak convexity (nonnegative curvature-radius density, with isolated zeros allowed) is included.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to two coefficients.** Rotation invariance removes Fourier modes not divisible by \(n\), reflection invariance removes sine modes, and constant width removes even frequencies and forces the constant term \(w/2\). Since \(n\) is odd, the cutoff leaves only the two displayed cosine modes. For twice-differentiable periodic \(h\), the necessary-and-sufficient support-function criterion is \(h+h''\ge0\). Define real normalized curvature coefficients
\[
\alpha=-\frac{2(n^2-1)a}{w},\qquad
\beta=-\frac{2(9n^2-1)b}{w}.
\]
Then
\[
h+h''=\frac w2\bigl(1+\alpha\cos(n\theta)+\beta\cos(3n\theta)\bigr).
\]
The oscillatory sum reverses sign under \(\theta\mapsto\theta+\pi\); hence convexity holds if and only if
\[
\|\alpha\cos t+\beta\cos(3t)\|_{L^\infty([0,2\pi])}\le1.
\]
The classical support-area identity, evaluated by trigonometric orthogonality, gives
\[
\operatorname{Area}(K)=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(h^2-(h')^2)\,d\theta
=\frac{\pi w^2}{4}-\frac{\pi w^2}{8(n^2-1)}(\alpha^2+r_n\beta^2).
\]
Therefore sharp area minimization is equivalent to maximizing \(J=\alpha^2+r_n\beta^2\) under the displayed uniform-norm constraint. Notice that \(0<r_n<1/9\).

**2. Reduction to a scalar parameter.** Simultaneous reversal of \((\alpha,\beta)\) leaves the objective and norm unchanged, so assume \(\alpha\ge0\). If \(\alpha=0\), feasibility gives \(J\le r_n<1\). If \(\alpha>0\) and \(\beta\ge0\), evaluating at \(t=0\) yields \(\alpha+\beta\le1\) and consequently \(J\le1\). In the remaining case put \(s=-\beta/\alpha>0\), and define
\[
m(s)=\|\cos t-s\cos(3t)\|_\infty.
\]
For this \(s\), the largest feasible objective is
\[
F(s)=\frac{1+r_n s^2}{m(s)^2},
\]
with equality only when \(\alpha=1/m(s)\) and \(\beta=-s/m(s)\). The identity \(\cos3t=4\cos^3t-3\cos t\) reduces the norm to the maximum absolute value on \([-1,1]\) of the odd cubic
\[
f_s(x)=(1+3s)x-4sx^3.
\]
For \(0\le s\le1/9\) this polynomial increases on \([-1,1]\), so \(m(s)=1-s\). For \(s\ge1/9\), the unique positive interior peak occurs at
\[
x_s=\sqrt{\frac{1+3s}{12s}}\in(0,1],\qquad
f_s(x_s)=\frac23(1+3s)x_s.
\]
It dominates endpoint absolute values: for \(s\le1\) this follows from interior maximality and \(1-s\ge0\); for \(s>1\) one has \(f_s(1/2)=s+1/2>s-1=|f_s(1)|\). Oddness accounts for negative \(x\). Consequently,
\[
m(s)^2=\begin{cases}(1-s)^2,&0\le s\le1/9,\\ (1+3s)^3/(27s),&s\ge1/9.\end{cases}
\]

**3. Exact optimization.** On the first interval, \(F(s)=(1+r_n s^2)/(1-s)^2\) is strictly increasing, since its derivative equals \(2(1+r_ns)/(1-s)^3>0\). For \(s\ge1/9\),
\[
F(s)=\frac{27s(1+r_ns^2)}{(1+3s)^3},\qquad
F'(s)=\frac{27(1-6s+3r_ns^2)}{(1+3s)^4}.
\]
The derivative has two positive zeros. The smaller is
\[
s_n=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-r_n/3}}{r_n}=\frac1{3(1+q_n)},
\]
which lies strictly between \(1/9\) and \(1/3\); the larger zero is a local minimum. Thus the only possible global maximum on the second interval is at \(s_n\) or in the limit as \(s\to\infty\), where \(F(s)\to r_n\). The critical equation implies \(r_ns_n^2=2s_n-1/3\), yielding
\[
F(s_n)=\frac{18s_n}{(1+3s_n)^2}=\frac{6(1+q_n)}{(2+q_n)^2}=F_n.
\]
Moreover \(F(1/6)=\frac43(1+r_n/36)>4/3\), so \(F_n>4/3>1>r_n\). Hence \(s_n\) is the unique global maximizing ratio. The only full coefficient maximizers are \((\alpha,\beta)=\pm(c_n,-s_nc_n)\), giving the two displayed support functions.

**4. Feasibility, contacts and the one-harmonic comparison.** The choice \(c_n=1/m(s_n)\) ensures the curvature-radius density is nonnegative, so each displayed function is a genuine support function. At \(s_n>1/9\), the odd cubic has exactly one negative interior minimum and its symmetric positive maximum; precisely two values of \(t\pmod {2\pi}\) realize the negative minimum. Thus each signed extremizer has exactly \(2n\) isolated zero-curvature normals over \(0\le\theta<2\pi\). The width remains \(w\) by oddness. In the one-harmonic subclass \(\beta=0\), the norm condition is \(|\alpha|\le1\), so the maximum objective is exactly \(1\). Comparing the area deficits gives the factor \(F_n>4/3\) claimed above. This completes the proof for all odd \(n\ge3\) and \(w>0\).

Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing that standalone script. The checker uses only Python's standard library and independently evaluates the exact cubic extrema, samples the constrained quadratic objective, verifies the positive curvature-radius density at its algebraic contact directions, and compares a direct trapezoidal integration of the support-area formula with the closed form for several odd symmetry orders. The finite evaluations corroborate, but do not replace, the preceding analytic global proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Falcó and Myroshnychenko (2026) prove that globally minimum-area constant-width bodies with odd dihedral symmetry are two orientations of the regular Reuleaux polygon; they establish the general Fourier area representation used here. Their unrestricted minimizers are not finite trigonometric polynomials, and that theorem does not determine the optimum or equality coefficients under the first two allowed Fourier modes. Kawohl and Sweers (2019) describe constant-width curves and approximation by analytic Fourier series, without presenting the sharp weighted two-harmonic constrained-area optimizer in the material inspected. Published sharp inequalities for constant 3-order width, sharp Hausdorff stability of ordinary constant-width bodies, and discrete Carathéodory–Fejér constants concern different constraints or objectives; their statements do not imply the exact formula above. The novelty claimed here is solely the sharp two-mode area, its optimizer, and its finite-band comparison, not the support-function identities or global Reuleaux minimality.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns a fixed dihedral symmetry axis and the exact two-frequency Fourier class; it gives neither the sharp area among all smooth bodies nor an error bound for arbitrary higher-frequency Fourier truncations. Its two optimizers have isolated zero radius-of-curvature directions and need not lie in the strictly positive-curvature subclass, although the same number is the infimum there by scaling the optimal nonconstant coefficients by factors increasing to one from below. No claim is made about the full cyclic-symmetry class, which permits independent harmonic phases. Priority against unindexed or unsearched historical finite-mode calculations remains subject to future independent literature examination.
