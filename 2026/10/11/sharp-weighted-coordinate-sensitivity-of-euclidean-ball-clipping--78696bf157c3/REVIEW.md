# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The derivative is exactly \(r^{-1}(I-uu^{\mathsf T})\) outside the Euclidean unit ball and the identity inside. Integrating along segments proves the global upper bound, while outside difference quotients approaching the boundary prove sharpness. A weighted absolute-row-sum calculation, Cauchy--Schwarz equality, and a two-variable symmetric eigenvalue maximization yield both the numerical constant and every maximizing normal. Operator duality transfers the result to the dual weighted sum norm. The scalar one-dimensional case is checked separately. The finite verifier corroborates each critical formula but is not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical uncertainty.** Goebel's radial-map Lipschitz bound is stated in the norm defining the ball, unlike Euclidean clipping evaluated in a different, weighted coordinate norm. Su--Yu--Fei-Fei study Euclidean projection algorithms onto intersecting norm balls, without the derived sensitivity invariant. Related linear orthogonal-projector estimates give potentially useful reductions, but the inspected results do not state the full sharp all-dimension weight profile or its equality normal classification. An equivalent formula in older matrix-projection or perturbation literature remains possible; the novelty assertion is limited to the inspected comparisons.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Coordinatewise error sensitivity is natural when an algorithm projects noisy coordinates onto an Euclidean constraint. The theorem gives one exact formula for every dimension and positive anisotropic error weighting, a complete classification of maximally amplifying outward normals, a sharp unique weight-balancing prescription, and an unavoidable \(\sqrt n\)-scale stability loss. These are complete quantitative structural facts rather than a numerical example. This finding does not claim a new algorithm or an extension to arbitrary convex sets.
