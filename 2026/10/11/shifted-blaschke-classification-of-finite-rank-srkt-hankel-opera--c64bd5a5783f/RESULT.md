# Shifted-Blaschke classification of finite-rank SRKT Hankel operators

## Finding

Let
\[
\psi\in\mathrm{BMOA}_0
\]
and suppose that
\[
H_{\overline\psi}:H^2\longrightarrow H^2_-
\]
is nonzero and has finite rank. Then
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}\|
=
\sup_{a\in\mathbb D}
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2
\]
holds if and only if
\[
\psi(z)
=
C\bigl(B(z)-B(0)\bigr)
\]
for some
\[
C\ne0
\]
and some nonconstant finite Blaschke product \(B\).

For every such symbol,
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_{\overline\psi}
=
\deg B,
\]
\[
\|\psi\|_*
=
\|\psi\|_G
=
\|H_{\overline\psi}\|
=
|C|,
\]
and
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2^2
=
|C|^2
\bigl(1-|B(a)|^2\bigr)
\qquad
(a\in\mathbb D).
\]
Thus the complete set of norming reproducing kernels is
\[
\{k_a:B(a)=0\}.
\]

There is also an exact uniform-norm dichotomy. Since a nonconstant finite Blaschke product maps the unit circle onto itself,
\[
\|\psi\|_\infty
=
|C|
\bigl(1+|B(0)|\bigr).
\]
Hence
\[
B(0)=0
\]
gives equality of all three symbol norms, while
\[
B(0)\ne0
\]
gives the strict chain
\[
\|\psi\|_G
=
\|\psi\|_*
<
\|\psi\|_\infty.
\]

In particular, this supplies bounded examples requested in the recent SRKT paper. If
\[
0<|a|<1
\]
and
\[
B_a(z)
=
\frac{a-z}{1-\overline a z},
\]
then
\[
\psi_a(z)
=
B_a(z)-a
=
-\frac{(1-|a|^2)z}{1-\overline a z}
\]
has rank one and satisfies
\[
\|\psi_a\|_*
=
\|\psi_a\|_G
=
1
<
1+|a|
=
\|\psi_a\|_\infty.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The Hardy-space Hankel operator and the norms
\[
\|\psi\|_*,
\qquad
\|\psi\|_G
\]
are used with the normalization of the recent primary source: \(\psi(0)=0\), and constants in a boundary symbol do not contribute to its negative Fourier part.

The theorem concerns nonzero finite-rank Hankel operators. It does not classify all compact or all bounded SRKT Hankel operators.

A finite Blaschke product is a function
\[
B(z)
=
\eta
\prod_{j=1}^{d}
\frac{a_j-z}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad
|\eta|=1,
\qquad
a_j\in\mathbb D.
\]
Its degree is \(d\), counting multiplicity.

## Proof

Assume first that
\[
H_{\overline\psi}
\]
has finite rank and obeys the strong reproducing kernel thesis.

Finite rank implies compactness. The normalized Hardy kernels
\[
k_a(z)
=
\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^2}}{1-\overline a z}
\]
converge weakly to zero as
\[
|a|\longrightarrow1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2
\longrightarrow0
\]
at the boundary. The map
\[
a\longmapsto
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2
\]
is continuous in the disk. Since the operator is nonzero and its norm equals the supremum over kernels, there is therefore some
\[
a\in\mathbb D
\]
such that
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2
=
\|H_{\overline\psi}\|.
\]

By the classical Kronecker theorem, finite rank forces the analytic coefficient-generating function \(\psi\) to be rational with its poles outside the closed disk. In particular,
\[
\psi\in H^\infty_0.
\]

Let
\[
\phi_a(z)
=
\frac{a-z}{1-\overline a z}.
\]
This disk automorphism is involutive and satisfies
\[
\phi_a(0)=a.
\]
Set
\[
g(z)
=
\psi(\phi_a(z))-\psi(a).
\]
Then
\[
g(0)=0.
\]

For bounded analytic symbols, the recent paper writes the Hankel norm as the quotient distance
\[
\|\psi\|_*
=
\inf_{h\in H^\infty}
\|\overline\psi-h\|_\infty.
\]
Boundary composition by \(\phi_a\) is an isometric bijection of
\[
L^\infty(\mathbb T)
\]
and maps
\[
H^\infty
\]
onto itself. Constants can be absorbed into the approximating \(H^\infty\) function. Hence
\[
\|g\|_*
=
\|\psi\|_*.
\]

The change of variables induced by \(\phi_a\) sends normalized arc measure to harmonic measure at \(a\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g\|_2^2
&=
\int_{\mathbb T}
|\psi(\phi_a(\zeta))-\psi(a)|^2\,dm(\zeta)
\\
&=
\mathcal P(|\psi|^2)(a)-|\psi(a)|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the Bonsall identity from the primary source,
\[
\mathcal P(|\psi|^2)(a)-|\psi(a)|^2
=
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2^2.
\]
Since \(k_a\) is norming,
\[
\|g\|_2
=
\|\psi\|_*
=
\|g\|_*.
\]

Thus the Hankel operator generated by \(g\) has minimal norm in the sense
\[
\|H_{\overline g}\|
=
\|g\|_2.
\]
The minimal-norm theorem for Hardy Hankel operators says that minimal norm implies maximal norm. Consequently,
\[
\|g\|_2
=
\|g\|_*
=
\|g\|_\infty.
\]
Equality of the \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) norms on normalized arc measure forces
\[
|g|
\]
to be constant almost everywhere on the unit circle. Hence
\[
g=CI
\]
for a nonzero constant \(C\) and an inner function \(I\). Because \(g\) is rational, \(I\) is a finite Blaschke product.

Undoing the automorphism gives
\[
\psi(w)-\psi(a)
=
C I(\phi_a(w)).
\]
Put
\[
B=I\circ\phi_a.
\]
Then \(B\) is again a finite Blaschke product, and
\[
\psi(w)
=
CB(w)+\psi(a).
\]
At \(w=0\),
\[
0
=
CB(0)+\psi(a),
\]
so
\[
\psi(w)
=
C\bigl(B(w)-B(0)\bigr).
\]

Conversely, suppose
\[
\psi=C(B-B(0))
\]
with \(B\) a nonconstant finite Blaschke product. The negative Fourier part is unchanged by subtracting the constant \(B(0)\). Since
\[
|B|=1
\]
almost everywhere on the circle, the upper estimate
\[
\|\psi\|_*
\le
|C|
\]
follows by using the constant correction in the quotient-distance formula.

For every \(a\in\mathbb D\), the Bonsall identity and cancellation of constants give
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2^2
=
|C|^2
\left(
\mathcal P(|B|^2)(a)-|B(a)|^2
\right)
=
|C|^2
\bigl(1-|B(a)|^2\bigr).
\]
A nonconstant finite Blaschke product has at least one zero in the disk. At each zero,
\[
\|H_{\overline\psi}k_a\|_2
=
|C|.
\]
Therefore
\[
|C|
\le
\|\psi\|_G
\le
\|\psi\|_*
\le
|C|,
\]
which proves
\[
\|\psi\|_G=\|\psi\|_*=|C|
\]
and the SRKT.

The same formula shows that equality is attained exactly when
\[
B(a)=0.
\]

Finally,
\[
H_{\overline\psi}
=
C H_{\overline B}
\]
because the subtracted constant has no negative Fourier part. The kernel of
\[
H_{\overline B}
\]
is exactly
\[
BH^2.
\]
For a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\),
\[
\dim(H^2\ominus BH^2)=d.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_{\overline\psi}=d.
\]

On the boundary, \(B\) maps the circle onto the circle, so
\[
\sup_{|\zeta|=1}
|B(\zeta)-B(0)|
=
1+|B(0)|.
\]
This yields the stated uniform-norm formula and the strict examples when
\[
B(0)\ne0.
\]

## Verification

The fresh primary source was inspected at the exact SRKT definition, the Bonsall kernel identity, the equality problem
\[
\|\psi\|_*=\|\psi\|_G,
\]
the bounded quotient-distance formula, and its explicit request for an example satisfying that equality while having
\[
\|\psi\|_G<\|\psi\|_\infty.
\]

The minimal-norm theorem used in the necessity direction was checked in the primary Hardy-Hankel literature: minimal norm implies maximal norm, and simultaneous minimal and maximal norm is equivalent to constant boundary modulus, hence to a constant multiple of an inner function.

The finite-rank input was checked against the classical Kronecker theorem in the Hardy-space formulation: a finite-rank Hankel matrix has a rational generating function with poles outside the disk.

A separate norm-attainment paper was inspected because it is a plausible stronger source. It characterizes general norm-achieved Hankel operators through unimodular symbols and Toeplitz kernels, but it does not state the finite-rank SRKT classification, the shifted-Blaschke form, or the exact set of norming reproducing kernels obtained here.

No numerical experiment is used.

## Relationship to prior work

The recent SRKT paper asks for a characterization of Hardy Hankel operators satisfying
\[
\|\psi\|_*=\|\psi\|_G.
\]
It notes that inner functions give easy equality cases and explicitly asks whether one can have that equality together with the strict inequality
\[
\|\psi\|_G<\|\psi\|_\infty.
\]
The shifted finite-Blaschke family supplies such examples whenever
\[
B(0)\ne0.
\]

A previously obtained polynomial classification shows that among polynomial symbols the SRKT symbols are exactly monomials. The present theorem is strictly broader: it classifies every finite-rank Hankel operator, equivalently the rational finite-rank class, and identifies every norming reproducing kernel. The polynomial result is recovered because a finite Blaschke product that is a polynomial is a unimodular monomial.

The classical Kronecker theorem classifies finite rank by rationality, not by the SRKT. The minimal-norm Hankel theorem classifies a different equality after normalization. Their combination does not itself state which rational Hankel symbols have a norming reproducing kernel; the Möbius normalization of the norming kernel supplies the bridge.

General norm-attainment results for Hankel operators describe arbitrary norm-achieved vectors via unimodular representatives. They do not identify the reproducing kernels that can be norming or reduce the finite-rank SRKT locus to shifted finite Blaschke products.

## Limitations

The theorem requires finite rank. It does not classify compact infinite-rank SRKT Hankel operators or the full \(\mathrm{BMOA}_0\) problem.

The exact shifted-Blaschke form uses rationality in the necessity direction. For a general compact symbol, the Möbius-normalized minimal symbol need not be rational, so the conclusion would involve a potentially infinite inner function and further compatibility with compactness would have to be analyzed separately.

The result answers the bounded-example question but not the fresh paper's separate request for an unbounded
\[
\mathrm{BMOA}_0
\]
example satisfying the SRKT.
