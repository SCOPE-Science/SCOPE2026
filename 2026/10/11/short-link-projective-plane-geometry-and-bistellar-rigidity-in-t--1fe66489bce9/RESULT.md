# Short-link projective-plane geometry and bistellar rigidity in the 13-vertex Wu manifold
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the 13-vertex triangulation of the Wu manifold \(SU(3)/SO(3)\) obtained from the six facet-orbit representatives printed by Köhler and Lutz under the affine group \(13{:}3\). Its 533 tetrahedra have link-length distribution
\[
3^{\times 91},\qquad 4^{\times 195},\qquad 5^{\times 195},\qquad 6^{\times 52}.
\]
Thus exactly 91 tetrahedra have a short one-dimensional link, meaning a triangular link. Under the full published affine action these 91 tetrahedra form exactly three orbits of sizes \(39,39,13\). Representatives and their link-vertex triples are
\[
\{1,2,3,10\}\leftrightarrow\{5,6,8\},\quad
\{1,2,4,8\}\leftrightarrow\{3,5,13\},\quad
\{1,2,5,7\}\leftrightarrow\{9,12,13\}.
\]
The 13-member orbit of \(\{1,2,5,7\}\) is a \(2\text{-}(13,4,1)\) design: every unordered vertex pair occurs in exactly one of its tetrahedra. Therefore these tetrahedra are the lines of a projective plane of order \(3\).

In addition, \(K\) admits no bistellar move of positive index \(i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\). With the convention that an index-\(i\) move in a 5-manifold replaces \(A*\partial B\) by \(\partial A*B\), where \(A\) is a \(5-i\)-simplex and \(B\) is an absent \(i\)-simplex satisfying \(\operatorname{lk}_K(A)=\partial B\), every such candidate fails. Hence any bistellar modification of this 13-vertex triangulation must first use an index-zero move and introduce a new vertex.

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns the exact labeled complex reconstructed from Köhler--Lutz Table 6. Vertices \(1,\ldots,13\) are identified with \(\mathbf F_{13}\) by subtracting one, and the affine group is generated by maps \(x\mapsto ax+b\) with \(a\in\{1,3,9\}\) and \(b\in\mathbf F_{13}\). The six facet representatives are \(123458\), \(123459\), \(123479\), \(123569\), \(12358\,10\), and \(12379\,12\); their orbit sizes are respectively \(39,39,39,13,13,13\).

A tetrahedron has a short link when its link is the three-cycle \(\partial\Delta^2\). Bistellar rigidity here means only the absence of positive-index Pachner moves in the given labeled 5-dimensional complex. It does not assert that the PL homeomorphism class has a unique triangulation, nor that no sequence can start after adding vertices.

## Proof
The affine orbit expansion produces 156 distinct 5-simplices. Taking all faces yields the face vector
\[
(13,78,286,533,468,156),
\]
matching the published value. In particular all \(\binom{13}{2}=78\) edges and all \(\binom{13}{3}=286\) triangles occur, independently confirming 3-neighborliness.

For every tetrahedron \(T\), the verifier forms the complete simplicial link from the facets containing \(T\). Each such link is checked to be a connected 2-regular graph, hence a cycle, and its number of edges is counted. Exhausting all 533 tetrahedra gives 91 links of length 3, 195 of length 4, 195 of length 5, and 52 of length 6. Applying all 39 affine permutations to the 91 short-link tetrahedra yields exactly three orbits of sizes \(39,39,13\), with the representatives stated above.

For the size-13 orbit, the verifier enumerates all \(13\binom{4}{2}=78\) block-pair incidences. There are exactly \(\binom{13}{2}=78\) distinct unordered vertex pairs and each occurs once. Thus the blocks form a \(2\text{-}(13,4,1)\) design. Since the parameters are \(13=3^2+3+1\) and \(4=3+1\), the standard incidence axioms give a projective plane of order \(3\).

For bistellar moves, for every \(i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) and every face \(A\) of dimension \(5-i\), the verifier constructs the full link \(\operatorname{lk}_K(A)\). It tests whether that link equals the boundary of an \(i\)-simplex \(B\) on exactly \(i+1\) vertices and whether \(B\) is absent from \(K\). The exhaustive candidate counts are zero for every index. For \(i=1\) and \(i=2\), 3-neighborliness already makes an absent edge or triangle impossible; the exhaustive link comparison supplies the nontrivial checks for the remaining indices.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. The program contains the six orbit representatives, regenerates the 39 affine permutations, reconstructs the full complex, validates the face vector and pseudomanifold ridge incidences, classifies every tetrahedral link, checks the projective-plane design property, and exhausts every positive-index bistellar center. The expected terminal line begins `VERIFY_OK` and reports the exact spectrum, orbit sizes, design parameter, and five zero move counts. `certificate.json` records the corresponding finite certificate data in a compact machine-readable form.

## Relationship to prior work
Köhler and Lutz prove that the reconstructed complex is a 3-neighborly triangulation of \(SU(3)/SO(3)\), give its face vector and affine \(13{:}3\) symmetry, and print the six facet-orbit representatives. Kühnel independently points out that the tetrahedron labeled \(\{0,1,4,6\}\) in the zero-based convention has a short one-dimensional link and uses that local fact to motivate tight handle attachment. In the present one-based convention this is \(\{1,2,5,7\}\), lying in the distinguished 13-element orbit above.

The inspected sources do not classify all short-link tetrahedra, do not identify their \(2\text{-}(13,4,1)\) incidence geometry, and do not state the absence of every positive-index bistellar move. The 2005 paper does note two 14-vertex triangulations of the same manifold that are bistellarly equivalent to the 13-vertex complex; this is compatible with the result because a bistellar route from the 13-vertex complex may begin by adding a vertex.

## Limitations
The result is finite and exact for this one labeled triangulation. It does not prove vertex-minimality or uniqueness among all 13-vertex triangulations of the Wu manifold, both of which are separate questions in the literature. It also does not classify sequences that first add one or more vertices. An earlier census article from 1999/2000 clearly contains the same 13-vertex manifold at the object level, but a searchable full text was not available for statement-by-statement comparison; this leaves a residual possibility that an unindexed finite observation about the same local census appeared there.
