# Review of the Sidon quartic-power phase theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The scope is all prime fields \(\mathbb F_p\) with \(p\ge5\) for the Sidon classification, and exactly \(p\in\{5,13\}\) for the phase-only fourth-moment equality classification. The Sidon necessity follows from counting unordered sums and leaves only three candidates; exact residues settle those cases. For the Fourier assertion, orthogonality gives a sum of squared two-sum fibers and the triangle bound is attained precisely at phase alignment. The two explicit maximal minors, of determinants \(-5\) and \(-13\), combined with class-indicator kernels and an independent modular character direction, determine both the connected rank and component count. The candidate phase families already provide every component at the full connected dimension, proving necessity as well as sufficiency. The standalone integer checker reproduced all finite histograms, full relation matrices, determinant certificates and modular ranks, reporting `CHECK_PASS`. The result does not claim the unrestricted operator norm or phase classification for other primes.

## Originality — PASS, with risk
The closest public quartic-graph calculation gives \(E(C_4)\) and the fourth moment for the constant function, but does not classify all unit-modulus phases at that moment. It follows from the current finite-field two-sum structure that every unit-modulus input is bounded by the same energy; equality classification requires the additional rank-and-torsion argument. González-Riquelme and Oliveira e Silva study other finite-field surfaces and do not cover the quartic graph. Biswas and Stovall work with real monomial curves and affine arclength measure, not finite-field graphs. Targeted searches of published research records and primary literature did not find the exact \(p=13\) four-torus, thirteen-component phase group. A previously unindexed equivalent result in character-lattice or multiplicative-Sidon literature remains possible; search absence is not a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS
The fourth-power-image Sidon property is a natural structural condition guaranteeing that a fourth-moment collision fiber fixes both quartic values. The exact classification shows this mechanism occurs only at \(p=5\) and \(p=13\), identifying the unique nontrivial prime \(p=13\) where a four-class image supports arbitrary phase freedoms. The complete \(13\)-component, four-dimensional equality geometry is not contained in the known scalar energy value and distinguishes general unimodular maxima from additive-character modulations. This is a natural complete equality problem, rather than an arbitrary fixed-parameter numerical improvement. The bounded prime test is justified by the structural counting theorem rather than by an arbitrary cutoff.
