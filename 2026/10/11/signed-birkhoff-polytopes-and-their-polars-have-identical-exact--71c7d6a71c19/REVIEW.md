# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The source gives every signed-permutation vertex and every signed row/column facet. Both families have Frobenius norm \(\sqrt n\). Antipodal vertex pairs force the minimum-enclosing radius \(\sqrt n\) and center \(0\). Opposite facet pairs force the inradius at most \(1/\sqrt n\); the origin attains it, and equality forces the center to annihilate a spanning family of facet normals. Under polarity, source facets become vertices and source vertices become facets, so the same two radii and uniqueness statements hold for \(\mathcal C(n)\).

## Originality
PASS. The first public type-\(B\) Birkhoff preprint and its replacement were checked, with the replacement inspected at the exact V- and H-descriptions and polar definition. A later full same-object paper devoted to faces and facets was inspected, including its PDF and targeted radius-term searches. None states the paired inradius/circumradius theorem or optimizer uniqueness. Candidate-specific published-result searches under direct signed-Birkhoff, type-\(B\), polar, Frobenius-radius, insphere, circumsphere, and Chebyshev-center formulations found no covering theorem.

## Value
PASS. This is a canonical full-dimensional Coxeter-type analogue of the Birkhoff polytope. The result gives a complete all-\(n\) Euclidean metric profile for both the polytope and its polar, including every inner and outer contact. The exact equality of radius profiles across the polar pair and the radius ratio \(n\) are natural geometric invariants complementary to the source's reflexive, Ehrhart, triangulation, and face theory.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources give reflexivity, regular unimodular triangulations, the complete vertex/facet descriptions, faces, graph diameter, Hamiltonian connectivity, and toric ideals. The theorem is specific to the standard Frobenius realization and does not address extremal ellipsoids or non-orthogonal unimodular images. A general centrally symmetric polar-pair theorem with equinorm contact data remains the main residual originality risk.
