# Six affine orbits of minimum-support torsion spheres in the thirteen-vertex Wu manifold
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the Köhler--Lutz thirteen-vertex triangulation of the Wu manifold
\[
SU(3)/SO(3).
\]
The published triangulation is \(3\)-neighborly, has face vector
\[
(13,78,286,533,468,156),
\]
and has
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]

The minimum vertex support of a nonzero integral simplicial \(2\)-homology class is \(4\). Exactly \(182\) four-vertex supports attain the minimum: the missing tetrahedra. Every missing four-set carries the tetrahedral two-sphere formed by its four triangular faces, and every such sphere represents the unique nonzero class in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\).

Under the published affine symmetry group \(13{:}3\), the \(182\) minimum-support carriers split into exactly six orbits:
\[
13,\ 13,\ 39,\ 39,\ 39,\ 39.
\]
Representatives may be chosen as
\[
\{1,2,4,10\},\quad
\{1,2,9,11\},\quad
\{1,2,3,11\},\quad
\{1,2,4,6\},\quad
\{1,2,4,12\},\quad
\{1,2,7,8\}.
\]

The affine group has two orbits on unordered vertex pairs. Label the pair type by whether the difference in \(\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z\), up to sign, lies in \(\{1,3,4\}\) or in \(\{2,5,6\}\). Counting the six edges of a carrier by these two pair types gives the signatures
\[
(3,3),\ (3,3),\ (4,2),\ (3,3),\ (3,3),\ (2,4)
\]
for the six carrier orbits in the order above.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices \(1,\ldots,13\) are identified with \(\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z\) by subtracting one. The affine group \(13{:}3\) is generated by translations and multiplication by \(3\); multiplication by \(3\) has order three modulo \(13\).

A missing tetrahedron is a four-vertex set that is not a tetrahedron although all four of its triangular faces are present. Since the complex is \(3\)-neighborly, every four-set has all four triangular faces, so every missing four-set carries a tetrahedral two-sphere.

The statement concerns vertex-support size for integral simplicial \(2\)-cycles. It does not claim that \(13{:}3\) is the full simplicial automorphism group, and it does not classify larger-support representatives of the torsion class.

## Proof
Köhler and Lutz give six facet-orbit representatives under \(13{:}3\):
\[
123458,\ 123459,\ 123479,\ 123569,\ 12358\,10,\ 12379\,12,
\]
with orbit sizes
\[
39,39,39,13,13,13.
\]
Expanding these affine orbits gives exactly \(156\) facets and reconstructs the published face vector
\[
(13,78,286,533,468,156).
\]

Because all \(\binom{13}{3}=286\) triangles are present, the complex is \(3\)-neighborly. Hence among the
\[
\binom{13}{4}=715
\]
four-vertex sets, exactly
\[
715-533=182
\]
are missing tetrahedra.

On a fixed four-vertex set the integral \(2\)-cycle group supported on its four possible triangles has rank one, generated by the oriented tetrahedral boundary. If the four-set is a tetrahedron of \(K\), that cycle is an integral boundary. Thus a nonzero class with four-vertex support can occur only on a missing tetrahedron. No nonzero \(2\)-cycle can be supported on at most three vertices, so four vertices is the first possible support size.

Over \(\mathbf F_2\), the boundary map from triangles to edges has rank \(66\), while the span of tetrahedral boundaries in the triangle chain group has rank \(219\). Therefore
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2} H_2(K;\mathbf F_2)=286-66-219=1.
\]
Exact reduction of all \(182\) missing tetrahedral boundaries modulo the tetrahedral-boundary space shows that every one is nonzero.

The published integral homology is
\[
H_*(K;\mathbb Z)=(\mathbb Z,0,\mathbb Z/2,0,0,\mathbb Z).
\]
Since \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)=0\), the universal coefficient theorem gives
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbf F_2
\cong
H_2(K;\mathbf F_2).
\]
Hence every missing tetrahedral boundary represents the unique nonzero integral class. This proves both the minimum and the complete set of minimum supports.

For the symmetry statement, act on all \(182\) missing four-sets by the \(39\) affine maps
\[
x\longmapsto 3^b x+a,
\qquad a\in\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z,\quad b\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]
The orbit decomposition is exactly
\[
13+13+39+39+39+39=182,
\]
with the six representatives displayed above. Finally, unordered pairs have two affine difference types, and direct counting of the six pairs in each representative gives the stated signatures.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs the complex only from the six published facet-orbit representatives and the affine action. It verifies every orbit size, the full face vector, three-neighborliness, the two mod-two boundary ranks, all \(182\) missing-tetrahedron homology classes, the complete six-orbit decomposition, and the pair-type signatures.

Its exact successful output is:

`VERIFY_OK f=13,78,286,533,468,156 H2_F2_dim=1 missing4=182 all_missing4_nonzero=yes affine_orbits=13,13,39,39,39,39 reps=12410|12911|12311|1246|12412|1278 pair_signatures=3:3|3:3|4:2|3:3|3:3|2:4`

The mod-two calculation is exhaustive. The passage to the integral torsion class uses the published integral homology together with the universal coefficient theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Kühnel and Lutz's census of tight triangulations places this thirteen-vertex Wu-manifold triangulation in the tight setting, and Köhler--Lutz later give the explicit \(13{:}3\) facet-orbit presentation and the homology \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z/2\). Tightness already makes small induced homology carriers structurally natural, so the present result does not claim novelty for the bare existence of a four-vertex torsion sphere.

The added statement is the complete minimum-support carrier census together with the exact affine orbit decomposition, explicit representatives, and pair-type signatures for all \(182\) carriers. The inspected exact-object sources do not provide this six-orbit table.

## Limitations
The orbit statement is for the published affine subgroup \(13{:}3\), not a claim about the full automorphism group. The result is specific to this triangulation and to minimum vertex support. An unindexed historical computation could contain the same six-orbit table.

An older exact-object tightness source was located with year- or month-level bibliographic dating but without a verified day in the inspected material.
