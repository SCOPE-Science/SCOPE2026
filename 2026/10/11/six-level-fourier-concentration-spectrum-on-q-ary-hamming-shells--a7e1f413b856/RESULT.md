# Six-level Fourier concentration spectrum on q-ary Hamming shells

## Finding

Let \(q\ge3\) be an integer, \(n\ge4\), and \(2\le k\le n-2\). On the finite abelian group \(G=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^n\) with uniform probability measure, let \(\mathcal H_{\le2}\) be the span of characters whose coordinate-frequency support has size at most two. Put \(S_k=\{x\in G:\#\{i:x_i\ne0\}=k\}\), \(P\) for orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_{\le2}\), and \(K=P M_{\mathbf 1_{S_k}}P\big|_{\mathcal H_{\le2}}\). Thus \(K\) measures the fraction of ambient Fourier energy concentrated on the shell.

Write
\[
 p=\frac{q-1}{q},\quad v=p(1-p),\quad
 a_j=\frac{(k)_j}{(n)_j}\ (j=1,2,3,4),\quad
 w=\frac{\binom nk(q-1)^k}{q^n},
\]
where \((m)_j=m(m-1)\cdots(m-j+1)\); in particular \((k)_j=0\) when \(j>k\). Every nonzero eigenvalue of \(K\) is \(w\) times one of the six following exact positive rational quantities, with the stated multiplicities (combine coincident eigenvalues):

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 j & L_j & \mathrm{multiplicity}\\ \hline
0 & 1+\dfrac{n(a_1-p)^2}{v}+\binom n2\dfrac{(a_2-2pa_1+p^2)^2}{v^2} & 1\\[2mm]
1 & \dfrac{a_1-a_2}{v}\left(1+\dfrac{(n-2)((k-1)/(n-2)-p)^2}{v}\right) & n-1\\[2mm]
2 & \dfrac{a_2-2a_3+a_4}{v^2} & \dfrac{n(n-3)}2\\[2mm]
3 & \dfrac{a_1}{p}\left(1+\dfrac{(n-1)((k-1)/(n-1)-p)^2}{v}\right) & n(q-2)\\[2mm]
4 & \dfrac{a_1}{p}\dfrac{(k-1)(n-k)}{(n-1)(n-2)v} & n(n-2)(q-2)\\[2mm]
5 & \dfrac{a_2}{p^2} & \binom n2(q-2)^2.
\end{array}
\]

The remaining eigenvalue is exactly zero, with multiplicity \(1+n(q-1)\). In particular,
\[
\operatorname{rank}K=\binom n2(q-1)^2,
\qquad
\ker K=\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{x_i\ne0}-k\right)h(x):h\in\mathcal H_{\le1}\right\},
\]
where \(\mathcal H_{\le1}\) is the Fourier-degree-at-most-one space. Consequently restriction to any interior shell is injective on the quotient \(\mathcal H_{\le2}/\ker K\), has exactly the positive squared singular values \(L_j\) under normalized shell measure, and has optimal quotient condition number \(\sqrt{\max_j L_j/\min_j L_j}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is the cyclic group of any integer order \(q\ge3\); it need not be a finite field or a prime power. All input and output scalar fields are complex. Ambient Fourier characters form an orthonormal basis for uniform probability measure on \(G\); shell restriction carries uniform probability measure on \(S_k\). The concentration operator uses the unrenormalized shell indicator and thus has eigenvalues \(wL_j\), whereas the normalized restriction Gramian has eigenvalues \(L_j\). The theorem requires exactly \(n\ge4\) and \(2\le k\le n-2\). It does not assert the same six-branch table at boundary shells.

## Proof

**One-coordinate decomposition.** Let \(I_i=\mathbf1_{x_i\ne0}\), and \(r_i=(I_i-p)/\sqrt v\). For each coordinate choose an orthonormal family \(c_{i,\alpha}\), \(1\le\alpha\le q-2\), spanning the functions that vanish at the zero symbol and have zero sum over the \(q-1\) nonzero symbols. These functions also have ambient mean zero, and their ambient norm equals one. Therefore \(\{1,r_i,c_{i,\alpha}\}\), with the indicated coordinate roles, is an orthonormal product basis of \(\mathcal H_{\le2}\) up to tensor degree two.

Conditional on \(x\in S_k\), the support vector \((I_1,\dots,I_n)\) is uniformly distributed among binary vectors of weight \(k\); conditioned on the support, the nonzero coordinate labels are independent and uniform. For distinct indices,
\[
\mathbb E_{S_k}[I_{i_1}\cdots I_{i_j}]=a_j.
\]
The normalized restriction Gramian splits orthogonally into: the radial tensor block generated by \(1,r_i,r_ir_j\); the blocks generated, for fixed \(i,\alpha\), by \(c_{i,\alpha}\) and \(c_{i,\alpha}r_j\) for \(j\ne i\); and the pairwise-contrast basis vectors \(c_{i,\alpha}c_{j,\beta}\) for \(i<j\). Cross terms between distinct blocks vanish by conditioning on independent, zero-mean nonzero labels.

**Radial sector.** Permutation symmetry decomposes the binary degree-two coordinate space into the Johnson harmonic degrees zero, one and two, with dimensions \(1,n-1,n(n-3)/2\) in the restricted shell space. Each appears with multiplicity at most one in the image, so each has one nonzero Gram eigenvalue. The constant test function has ambient coefficient correlations \(1\), \((a_1-p)/\sqrt v\) with each \(r_i\), and \((a_2-2pa_1+p^2)/v\) with each \(r_i r_j\). The sum of their squares is precisely \(L_0\).

For the standard coordinate-difference test \(h_1=I_1-I_2\),
\[
\mathbb E_{S_k}|h_1|^2=2(a_1-a_2).
\]
Its only nonzero ambient basis correlations are with \(r_1,r_2\), of respective magnitudes \((a_1-a_2)/\sqrt v\), and with \(r_1r_j,r_2r_j\) for \(j\ge3\), of magnitudes \((a_1-a_2)|b-p|/v\), where \(b=(k-1)/(n-2)\). Dividing the sum of squared correlations by \(\mathbb E_{S_k}|h_1|^2\) gives \(L_1\).

For the degree-two harmonic test \(h_2=(I_1-I_2)(I_3-I_4)\), let \(\delta=a_2-2a_3+a_4\). Then \(\mathbb E_{S_k}|h_2|^2=4\delta\); the only nonzero ambient basis correlations are with the four cross-pairs \(r_i r_j\), \(i\in\{1,2\}\), \(j\in\{3,4\}\), each of absolute magnitude \(\delta/v\). The resulting eigenvalue is \(L_2=\delta/v^2\). This computes all radial nonzero eigenvalues, including their multiplicities.

**One-contrast sectors.** Fix \(i,\alpha\). Every basis vector in its sector carries the factor \(c_{i,\alpha}\); conditional on nonzero \(I_i=1\), the remaining support has exactly \(k-1\) active positions among \(n-1\). Since \(\mathbb E_{S_k}|c_{i,\alpha}|^2=a_1/p\) and \(\sum_{j\ne i}r_j\) becomes the constant \(((k-1)-(n-1)p)/\sqrt v\), the constant direction of the sector has one Gram eigenvalue
\[
\frac{a_1}{p}\left(1+\frac{(n-1)((k-1)/(n-1)-p)^2}{v}\right)=L_3.
\]
For differences among the remaining \(n-1\) radial coordinates, the variance is \((k-1)(n-k)/((n-1)(n-2)v)\), giving eigenvalue \(L_4\) in \(n-2\) independent directions. The constant linear dependency on the shell leaves one null direction. There are \(n(q-2)\) such sectors, so the multiplicities are those in the table.

**Two-contrast sector.** Each \(c_{i,\alpha}c_{j,\beta}\) with \(i<j\) is orthogonal under shell measure to all other double-contrast vectors: any unequal pair or label index leaves a zero-mean independent label factor. Its conditional squared norm equals \(\mathbb P_{S_k}(I_i=I_j=1)/p^2=a_2/p^2\). These \(\binom n2(q-2)^2\) vectors give \(L_5\).

**Rank and complete nullspace.** All six displayed values are positive because \(2\le k\le n-2\) (in particular \(\delta=\mathbb P(I_1=1,I_2=0,I_3=1,I_4=0)>0\)). The positive multiplicities sum to \(\binom n2(q-1)^2\); subtraction from \(\dim\mathcal H_{\le2}=1+n(q-1)+\binom n2(q-1)^2\) gives nullity \(1+n(q-1)\).

Let \(R(x)=\sum_i I_i-k\). Multiplication by \(R\) sends \(\mathcal H_{\le1}\) into \(\mathcal H_{\le2}\) because functions of the same coordinate multiply without increasing coordinate support. It vanishes on \(S_k\), so \(R\mathcal H_{\le1}\subseteq\ker K\). If \(Rh=0\) globally, then \(h\) vanishes at the all-zero point and at every word with exactly one nonzero coordinate, since \(R\ne0\) there for \(k\ge2\). These values determine every degree-at-most-one function, proving \(h=0\). Hence the subspace of vanishing products has dimension \(1+n(q-1)\) and must equal the whole nullspace. Finally, multiplying the normalized Gramian by the shell density \(w\) converts the six levels to the eigenvalues of \(K\). This proves every assertion.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library `artifacts/verify.py` derives the exact integer Fourier-projection kernel on \(S_k\): if \(u_i=q\mathbf1_{x_i=y_i}-1\), then \(q^nP(x,y)=1+\sum_i u_i+\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j\). On four finite cases it checks the predicted integer eigenvalues, all exact spectral power sums up to the number of distinct levels, and an exact annihilating matrix polynomial. Cases: \((q,n,k)=(3,4,2),(3,5,2),(3,5,3),(4,4,2)\). Separately, normalized Fourier-basis Gram eigenvalues were compared numerically for nine parameter cases, including \(q=5\). These tests support but do not substitute for the preceding proof for all admitted parameters.

## Relationship to prior work

Filmus (arXiv:1406.0142v1) constructs an orthogonal basis for Boolean \(q=2\) slices using Johnson representations; the present problem treats all \(q\ge3\) and includes symbol-contrast sectors absent over the binary alphabet. Vasil'eva (arXiv:1412.3622v1) studies reconstruction of individual Hamming-graph eigenspaces from sphere data under supplemental conditions; this theorem instead gives all mixed-degree-at-most-two singular levels for every interior radius, and an explicit kernel and condition number. Levstein, Maldonado and Penazzi (2006, DOI:10.1016/j.ejc.2004.08.005) give a general Terwilliger-algebra decomposition for q-ary Hamming schemes, a broader representation-theoretic framework in which these calculations may be situated; the present statement is the concrete singular-value and nullspace classification for a specific Fourier band. No claim is made that the general representation-theoretic machinery is new.

## Limitations

Only Fourier degree at most two and interior Hamming shells are addressed. The theorem is an exact finite-group identity for each parameter triple and does not by itself establish new estimates at growing Fourier degree. A prior equivalent explicit classification expressed in Terwilliger-module or association-scheme terminology cannot be conclusively excluded without inspecting all historical literature. The finite experiments alone establish nothing outside their listed cases. The quotient condition number formula is exact but no simplified arithmetic tightness criterion is claimed for degree two.
