# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** For arbitrary \(n\ge5\), the Alexander dual has a complete one-skeleton, exactly \(6n\) triangles (the induced three-vertex paths), and exactly \(n\) tetrahedra (the ladder squares). Each ring two-step chord supplies a unique pivot row; three local rows per square supply a unit determinant block. Consequently the degree-two boundary has an integral unit minor of rank \(5n\), with \(n\) independent square-boundary cycles. The degree-three boundary has rank \(n\) and fills the full kernel. Integral duality, skeleton connectivity, Hurewicz, and Whitehead prove the exact wedge type. The embedded checker reconstructs and independently rank-checks instances \(5\le n\le9\). Limits: the proof excludes \(n=4\); finite tests alone do not prove the theorem.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** Bayer et al., arXiv:2304.13675, Section 7.3 treat prisms over **complete** graphs \(K_m\mathbin{\square}K_2\), not circular ladders. Chandrakar et al., arXiv:2408.07646, treat rectangular **open** grids rather than cycle products. Most importantly, Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak, arXiv:2512.04486, Conjecture 4.6(ii), explicitly state the **same integral homology formula as a conjecture**, supported by Table 5; the formula's original proposal is not claimed as new. Their paper establishes no proof of that conjecture or the stronger sphere-wedge homotopy type. No covering proved result was identified in the examined published texts or targeted literature searches. Subsequent uncatalogued proofs remain a residual possibility.

**Value — PASS.** The claim resolves a precise natural published conjecture for an entire infinite family of cyclic ladder graphs, replaces bounded computations with a uniform saturated-chain argument, and determines the stronger homotopy type. The wraparound edges distinguish this family topologically from the previously classified rectangular grids. This is not merely a new instance or a re-evaluation of known small data.
