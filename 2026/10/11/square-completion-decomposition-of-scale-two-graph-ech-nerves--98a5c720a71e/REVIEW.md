# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The even-scale graph Čech facet formula is taken from Adams–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2212.05871, Lemma 2.2), not assumed from a discrete metric substitute. Triangle-freeness and the two-common-neighbor bound make any two closed neighborhoods intersect in at most two vertices. Consequently every face with at least three vertices has a unique neighborhood generator, justifying all descending-cardinality free-face pairs. The second collapse removes exactly length-two chords with a unique center. Each remaining chord has exactly two common neighbors, and its four-cycle produces precisely one tetrahedral boundary; no extra faces survive. Choosing one disk per square realizes the square-completion CW complex, while each complementary disk has a null-homotopic attaching loop. The resulting homotopy wedge formula holds integrally without relying on numerical extrapolation. A standard-library checker verified all declared elementary free-face operations and independent mod-two ranks for seven larger graph examples and all 429 admissible labeled graphs on at most five vertices.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limitations

The closest owning article classifies scale-two Čech nerves for hypercubes and certain induced cube subgraphs; that result does not imply a classification for arbitrary graphs satisfying a local common-neighbor restriction, particularly toroidal grids with nonzero cup pairing. Previte's 2014 \(D=\{0,1\}\)-neighborhood thesis treats exactly the same simplicial construction. Its C4 and cube homology calculations and one-point-union homology theorem are known special cases, not new findings here; an edge-interacting network of square cycles on a torus cannot be reduced to that one-point-union theorem. Matsushita's later closed-neighborhood theory supplies general fundamental-group and independence-complex relations but does not state this square-completion plus one-sphere-per-square homotopy equivalence; fundamental group alone cannot recover the two-dimensional summands or cup product. Shukla's graph neighborhood-complex classification addresses the open-neighborhood object. Searches for alternative names, the closed-neighborhood formulation, square-completion decompositions, grid products, and equivalent published statements located no direct coverage. An unindexed or differently formulated equivalent remains possible. Published hypercube and previously studied prism/circular-ladder specializations are not claimed as original in isolation.

## Value — PASS

The theorem explains topology for an infinite class of graphs with arbitrary degree, nontrivial fundamental groups, and interacting square cycles. The torus-grid example is a natural benchmark that distinguishes a genuine torus cup product from wedges of spheres, while the additional two-spheres quantify redundancy created by closed-neighborhood thickening. The explicit collapses provide an independently checkable structural model, not merely an isolated Betti-number computation.

## Boundaries

No claim is made for graphs with triangles or a \(K_{2,3}\) subgraph, other Čech scales, persistence inclusion maps, or equivariant wedge naturality. Independent mathematical evaluation remains outstanding.
