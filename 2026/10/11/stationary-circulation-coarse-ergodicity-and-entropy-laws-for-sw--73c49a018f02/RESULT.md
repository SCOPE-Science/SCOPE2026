# Stationary-circulation, coarse-ergodicity and entropy laws for switching graph flows

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a nonempty finite directed multigraph in which every vertex lies on a directed cycle. Give each directed edge a geometric interval of length one, glue its endpoints to the incident vertices, and let \(X_G\) be the resulting compact metric graph. A permitted entire trajectory travels forward at unit speed on edges and, on reaching a vertex, may choose any outgoing edge; no waiting is allowed. Let \(\mathscr S_G\subset C(\mathbb R,X_G)\) consist of all such two-sided trajectories. A probability \(\mu\) on \(X_G\) is *stationary* when it is the time-zero marginal of a shift-invariant probability on \(\mathscr S_G\). Call it *strong-backward ergodic* if every Borel subset invariant under **all** backward continuations has \(\mu\)-measure zero or one (the strong-backward notion in Suda's framework).

Write \(\mathcal C\) for the strongly connected components of \(G\), let \(E_C\) denote the edges internal to \(C\), and let \(A_C\) be the edge-counting adjacency matrix of \(C\). Define the normalized nonnegative circulation polytope
\[
\mathcal P_G=\left\{w\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}:\sum_{e\in E}w_e=1,\quad
\sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}w_e=\sum_{e:\operatorname{head}(e)=v}w_e\ \text{for all }v\in V\right\}.
\]
Every stationary \(\mu\) is uniquely of the form \(\mu_w|_{e^\circ}=w_e\,dt\), \(\mu_w(V)=0\), for \(w\in\mathcal P_G\), and conversely every such \(w\) is stationary. Its extreme points are the length-normalized indicator circulations of directed simple cycles. If \(c=|\mathcal C|\), then
\[
\dim\mathcal P_G=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}(|E_C|-|V_C|)+c-1.
\]
**Exactly** those \(\mu_w\) whose positive edge weights are internal to a *single* strongly connected component are strong-backward ergodic. Each component face \(\mathcal P_C\) consists entirely of such measures and has dimension \(|E_C|-|V_C|\); mixtures with positive weights in multiple components are not strong-backward ergodic.

For the stationary trajectory laws projecting to \(\mu_w\), their **maximum possible time-one Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy** is
\[
\mathcal H(w)= -\sum_{e:\,w_e>0}w_e\log\frac{w_e}{p_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}},\qquad
p_v=\sum_{e:\,\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}w_e.
\]
For every \(C\in\mathcal C\), the values among its strong-backward ergodic stationary marginals fill **exactly** the interval
\[
\{\mathcal H(w):w\in\mathcal P_C\}=[0,\log\rho(A_C)].
\]
Here logarithms are natural; a component consisting of one vertex with one loop has the interval \([0,0]\). Thus strong-backward ergodicity of a stationary *marginal* imposes no entropy extremality: the one-vertex graph with \(m\ge2\) separate directed loops has an \((m-1)\)-simplex of strong-backward ergodic marginals and the complete entropy interval \([0,\log m]\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite and may have parallel directed edges and loops. Every vertex belongs to a directed cycle; this guarantees that every directed edge can be embedded in a two-sided permitted trajectory. Edge lengths and speeds are both exactly one. All trajectories are continuous in the natural metric graph; branch choice at a vertex is unrestricted, and no state may remain at a vertex for a positive time. Trajectories can use intercomponent edges transiently. The trajectory space is closed and equi-Lipschitz and hence compact, is invariant under every real time shift, and is closed under splicing at common positions. The definitions use **entire** trajectories and **all** backward continuations, not merely a chosen stationary Markov kernel. Entropy means the entropy of the time-one map on the trajectory space, maximized over stationary trajectory laws with the stated marginal; it is not entropy of a deterministic map on the geometric graph.

## Proof
**1. Suspension representation and conservation.** Choose a bi-infinite admissible edge itinerary \((e_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) with \(\operatorname{head}(e_j)=\operatorname{tail}(e_{j+1})\) and a phase \(u\in[0,1)\). Moving along \(e_0\) with initial coordinate \(u\) gives every entire trajectory, apart from the harmless multiple coding of phase-zero vertex positions. Real-time translation is the unit-roof suspension of the two-sided edge shift. In any translation-invariant trajectory probability, the phase is Lebesgue uniform and the edge itinerary is a stationary probability on the edge shift (the suspension disintegration). If \(w_e\) is the stationary probability that \(e_0=e\), then \(\sum_e w_e=1\). Since consecutive edge endpoints match, both the incoming and outgoing edge marginals at each vertex equal \(p_v\), proving Kirchhoff conservation. Uniform phase yields \(\mu|_{e^\circ}=w_e\,dt\), and vertices have zero mass. Conversely, for a circulation \(w\), set \(p_v=\sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}w_e\) and, whenever \(p_v>0\), choose edge \(e\) from \(v\) with probability \(w_e/p_v\). Kirchhoff gives a stationary bi-infinite edge process, and suspension with independent uniform phase projects to \(\mu_w\). At zero-mass vertices any outgoing probabilities may be chosen without changing the law. This also proves uniqueness of the state marginal associated with \(w\).

**2. Graph structure and extremality.** The condensation graph of strongly connected components is acyclic. A nonnegative circulation has zero flux along its intercomponent edges: remove a source of the condensation graph, use total conservation on its vertices to show all outgoing flux is zero, and induct on the remaining finite acyclic graph. Within a strongly connected component, every edge lies on a directed cycle. Consequently any nonzero circulation can be decomposed into nonnegative multiples of directed-cycle indicator vectors by repeatedly removing the smallest positive edge weight on a cycle. The extreme rays are exactly the indicator vectors of simple directed cycles, and the normalized extreme points give the claimed vertices of \(\mathcal P_G\). The oriented incidence matrix of a strongly connected \(C\) has rank \(|V_C|-1\). Because the sum of indicator vectors of cycles covering all internal edges gives a strictly positive circulation, the nonnegativity constraints do not diminish the affine dimension. Adding the components and the single total-mass normalization yields the stated dimension and \(\dim\mathcal P_C=|E_C|-|V_C|\).

**3. Exactly the strong-backward ergodic marginals.** Fix a component \(C\), and let \(B\) be a Borel set such that whenever \(x\in B\), every permitted entire trajectory through \(x\) belongs to \(B\) at every earlier time. If \(B\) contains one point \(x\) on an internal edge of \(C\), then it contains *every* point \(y\) of the subgraph corresponding to \(C\). Indeed the strong connectivity allows a finite directed path from \(y\) to \(x\); it can be extended to a two-sided trajectory using cycles in the component at either end. Since \(B\) is backward strongly invariant, \(y\in B\). Thus either \(B\) meets no edge interior of \(C\), or it contains all of them. As \(\mu_w\) has no vertex mass, every \(w\in\mathcal P_C\) has \(\mu_w(B)\in\{0,1\}\).

Now suppose \(w\) has positive circulation in at least two components. Choose one of those components \(C\) with no directed path to it from any **other positive-mass** component (choose a minimal positive component in the reachability partial order). Let \(B_C\) be the set of states from which some permitted forward continuation reaches \(C\). This is a Borel finite union of vertices and edge pieces, is backward strongly invariant by concatenation, and contains exactly the component \(C\) among the positive-mass components. Hence \(0<\mu_w(B_C)<1\). This proves both directions.

**4. Maximal entropy and its full interval.** The time-one entropy of the unit-roof suspension is the entropy rate of the stationary edge itinerary. The edge at time \(j+1\) must leave the head vertex of the edge at time \(j\); conditioning on the entire past can only decrease uncertainty. Hence for *any* stationary itinerary with one-edge probabilities \(w_e\),
\[
h\le\sum_v p_v H\left((w_e/p_v)_{\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}\right)=\mathcal H(w).
\]
The stationary vertex-Markov edge process from Step 1 attains equality, establishing the exact maximum, including faces with zero weights. Continuity of \(\mathcal H\) on \(\mathcal P_C\) follows from continuity of \(-x\log x\) at zero.

For \(C\), Perron--Frobenius gives a positive right eigenvector \(r\) of \(A_C\) with eigenvalue \(\rho>0\). For each individual edge \(e\) in \(C\), put \(q_e=r_{\operatorname{head}(e)}/(\rho r_{\operatorname{tail}(e)})\); the outgoing \(q_e\) sum to one at every vertex. Kirchhoff cancels the logarithmic \(r\)-terms, giving the exact nonnegative relative-entropy identity
\[
\log\rho-\mathcal H(w)=\sum_{v:p_v>0}p_v D\!\left((w_e/p_v)_{\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}\,\middle\|\,(q_e)_{\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}\right)\ge0.
\]
The Parry circulation \(w_e=\ell_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}r_{\operatorname{head}(e)}/(\rho\sum_v\ell_vr_v)\), with a positive left Perron eigenvector \(\ell\), attains equality. A simple-cycle circulation has \(\mathcal H=0\), so both endpoints are attained. Because \(\mathcal P_C\) is convex and connected and \(\mathcal H\) is continuous, its image is the full closed interval \([0,\log\rho(A_C)]\). Every point of this polytope is strong-backward ergodic by Step 3, completing the theorem.

## Verification
The entire-trajectory suspension proof checks normalization, the Kirchhoff laws, zero vertex mass, the strong-backward quantifier over *all* continuations, and the definition of entropy of the time-one map. The relative-entropy identity provides an independent equality-condition check for the entropy ceiling. Two finite examples are reproduced by the accompanying \(\texttt{verify.py}\): three self-loops at one vertex give a two-dimensional circulation simplex and maximal entropy \(\log3\); two parallel edges from the first vertex to the second and one return edge give a one-dimensional simplex and maximal entropy \(\tfrac12\log2\). The checks are illustrative; the proof applies to every finite directed multigraph under the stated assumptions.

## Relationship to prior work
Tang, Yang and Li (2026, Sections 3, 6 and 7) formulate stationary transition pairs for finite correspondences, their entropies and finite-state equilibrium examples. The accessible author abstract and detailed section-level synopsis do not report a classification by strong-backward invariant sets of the **geometric switching suspension**; the original full manuscript was inaccessible, so noncoverage in uninspected material is not asserted. Suda (2026, Definitions 2.7 and 3.2, Lemma 3.6 and Theorem 3.8) formulates strong-backward ergodicity of nondeterministic continuous-time trajectory families and notes systems where every invariant marginal is ergodic. His general results do not specify the exact circulation polytope, the SCC-by-SCC criterion, its affine dimensions, or the entropy intervals in the present finite-graph family. Standard cycle-decomposition and Perron--Frobenius facts supply ingredients for the new joint classification; these ingredients themselves are not claimed as new. Bédaride--Hilion--Lustig (2020) analyze invariant measures of graph towers and subshifts, a distinct setting. Related work on extreme invariant measures versus ergodic trajectory lifts addresses a different question: extremality of a marginal is not the strong-backward invariant-set criterion used here.

## Limitations
The classification requires finite graphs, unit edge lengths, deterministic unit speed on interiors, and arbitrary splicing of allowed paths at vertices. Variable travel times, waiting at vertices, incomplete two-sided trajectories, or constraints on branching can change the invariant polytope and its entropy envelope. Strong-backward ergodicity is *not* ordinary ergodicity of a chosen transition kernel: many nonextreme marginals satisfy it. The entropy formula is the maximum over invariant trajectory lifts, not the entropy of every lift. Literature searches and comparison of the complete Suda preprint found no equivalent theorem; full text of the Tang--Yang--Li preprint and some potentially relevant graph-flow literature was not accessible during this review, so an equivalent formulation in that literature cannot be ruled out. No independent validation is claimed.
