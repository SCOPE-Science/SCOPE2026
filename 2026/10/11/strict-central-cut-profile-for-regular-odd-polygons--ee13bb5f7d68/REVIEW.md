# Same-model review

## Correctness
**PASS.** With circumradius normalized to \(1\), the squared radial function of a regular \(n\)-gon is \(c^2\sec^2u\) on each side-normal sector and is \(2\pi/n\)-periodic. Because an odd-order central halfplane has angular width equal to an integer number of full radial periods plus one half-period, its area reduces exactly to one length-\(\pi/n\) residual integral. Evaluating that integral gives the stated formula. Its derivative is strictly positive in the fundamental chamber, and the endpoint identities verify both the unique edge-parallel minimum and the symmetry-axis bisection. No numerical experiment is used as proof.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest primary source, Rousseeuw--Ruts, already gives the endpoint maximal-depth value but explicitly says that edge-parallel optimality was verified numerically. The inspected source does not provide an analytic proof of that optimizer, strict uniqueness, or the full directional formula. Targeted published-finding corpus and literature searches over Tukey-depth, halfspace, central-cut, edge-parallel, Szarek-symmetry, and equivalent regular-polygon formulations returned no statement implying the new profile. The principal residual risk is a later unindexed proof under statistics terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The result closes an explicit numerical step in a published all-odd-order regular-polygon calculation and supplies the stronger full directional profile with equality classification. This is a structural statement for a canonical family in halfspace depth and convex-geometric symmetry, not an arbitrary numerical specialization. It clarifies exactly how the worst central cut changes with orientation and identifies all equality directions.

## Closest literature and limitations
Rousseeuw--Ruts is the decisive closest source: it supplies the known depth value and states numerical verification of the optimizer. Szarek supplies the convex-geometric symmetry/floating-body framework. The theorem is restricted to central cuts of odd regular polygons and does not claim the published endpoint value as new. An unindexed or differently phrased later analytic treatment remains possible.
