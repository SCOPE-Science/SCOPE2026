# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact volume calculation partitions the translated simplex difference body into orthants with independently bounded positive and negative coordinate sums. The polar computation uses each ordering chamber of the centered support coordinates; the chamber change-of-variables has a parameter-independent Jacobian, giving the exact denominator. The simplex permutation group fixes the unique Santaló point. Strict monotonicity follows from the **strict** Stieltjes inequality between positive Legendre and second-kind Chebyshev roots (in the fully accessible cited article, Theorem 4.22), the elementary comparison of Chebyshev roots with a linear grid, and the explicit sign of the paired quadratic log derivatives. The symmetry and curvature claims follow algebraically. Nontrivial examples and a finite exact/sampled verifier were independently replayed; these do not substitute for the all-dimensional proof. The polar normalization and affine invariance were checked explicitly. The boundary cases \(t=0\), \(t=1\), \(t\to\infty\), even/odd dimensions, and \(d=2\) are addressed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical priority risk

The closest inspected references are Kim–Reisner (simplex local minimality), Alonso-Gutiérrez–González–Jiménez (convolution body estimates), Artstein-Avidan–Putterman (unbalanced **primal** difference-body volume), the classical Stieltjes zero comparison as quoted by Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava, and a published record of higher-order unbalanced simplex **primal** volume unimodality. None of the inspected statements yields the centered **polar volume formula**, the exact product profile, and its unique global maximizing interpolation parameter. The much broader local-minimum theorem does not imply a maximum or monotonicity of this path. A relevant Barthe–Fradelizi symmetry paper was known through a review but could not be materially inspected in full: earlier equivalent formulas remain a residual risk. Searches cannot prove novelty or unpublished uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The volume product is a natural affine invariant whose extremizers form a central problem in convex geometry. This theorem determines the **entire** product curve for every simplex and every dimension, identifies the unique global optimum and its exact value, and supplies a sharp local curvature at that optimum. The dual-volume calculation and the strict root comparison together explain why maximizing the primal volume alone does not settle the genuine Mahler-product problem. The result is a structural exact extremal theorem, not a routine substitution or unmotivated finite calculation.

## Scope and limitations

The proof concerns the reflected-simplex family only. No universal claim for arbitrary convex bodies is made. The Stieltjes root comparison is pre-existing mathematics and explicitly credited. The unavailable Barthe–Fradelizi full text and alternate older naming are documented literature risks, and the verifier is only diagnostic.
