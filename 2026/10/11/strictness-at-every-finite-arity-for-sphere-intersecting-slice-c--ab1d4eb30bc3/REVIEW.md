# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The positive unit ball of the absolute norm \(N_m\) has exactly the nonzero vertices \(e_1,\ldots,e_{m+1}\) and \(v=(1/m,\ldots,1/m)\). Any at most \(m\) of these vertices share a facet because one coordinate index is absent from the selected standard-basis vertices. For each slice functional on the absolute \(c_0\)-sum, a maximizing positive vertex gives coordinate slices whose scaled product lies inside the original slice. The \(\mathrm{P3}\) property of \(c_0\) then supplies norm-one coordinate averages, and the common facet forces the assembled point to have norm one. For the reverse direction, \(m+1\) coordinate slices force all off-target coordinates below \(\alpha\); their equal average has norm at most \(m(1+m\alpha)/(m+1)<1\) when \(\alpha<m^{-2}\). All quantifiers and the boundary case \(m=2\) are covered.

## Originality
PASS. The focal 2026 preprint defines finite-arity properties and proves only the binary separation \(\mathrm{P3}^{(2)}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{P3}\), using exactly the three-summand member of the present family. Searches for consecutive-arity separation, finite-arity hierarchy, equivalent diameter-two formulations, and the explicit max-over-all-but-one norm found no statement implying strictness for every \(m\). The older sphere-intersection characterization concerns unrestricted convex combinations and does not imply a consecutive finite hierarchy. The current claim is therefore not a corollary merely by matching terminology or specializing a broader published theorem.

## Value
PASS. Whether a geometric property of convex combinations is determined by a bounded number of slices is the organizing question of the focal paper. The result gives a complete answer for the finite-arity behavior of \(\mathrm{P3}\): unlike \(\mathrm{P1}\), whose binary case collapses the entire hierarchy, \(\mathrm{P3}\) has a strict obstruction at every finite level. The construction is uniform and explains the mechanism through common facets of one explicit family of absolute norms, rather than adding a single isolated counterexample.

## Closest literature and limitations
The decisive comparison is arXiv:2608.11827v1, whose \(\mathrm{P3}^{(2)}\)-but-not-\(\mathrm{P3}\) space is exactly the case \(m=2\). arXiv:1703.04749 characterizes unrestricted sphere intersection in terms of attained diameter two, while arXiv:1701.06169 supplies the \(\mathrm{P1}\) property of \(c_0\). None states the all-\(m\) strict hierarchy. The result does not address \(\mathrm{P2}^{(m)}\), classification, or optimality of the displayed quantitative radius.
