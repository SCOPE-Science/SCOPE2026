# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Fu–Li's ball formulation reduces the modified constant to a supremum over the unit ball. For \(p\ge1\), the objective is separately convex, so the regular octagon reduces exactly to its eight extreme points in each variable. Dihedral enumeration leaves three shifted-norm patterns, and Jensen convexity shows the adjacent-vertex value dominates for all \(p\ge1\). The strict sub-Hilbert comparison with the unrestricted formula is exactly the strict two-coordinate \(\ell_p\)-to-\(\ell_q\) norm inequality for \((1,\sqrt2-1)\). The checker replays the finite octagon geometry with exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) arithmetic.

## Originality
PASS. The 2017 regular-octagon paper gives the unrestricted generalized constant, not the modified unit-sphere constant. Its \(p\ge2\) branch already covers the super-Hilbert modified value by implication, and that covered part is explicitly treated only as comparison. Fu–Li's 2022 full text develops the modified constant abstractly and computes classical \(\ell_r\)/\(L_r\) examples but contains no regular-octagon calculation. Targeted indexed-literature searches found no equivalent sub-Hilbert modified profile or exact equal-radius phase transition. The full text of the 2017 defining article was unavailable from the lawful sources checked, so a residual source-access risk is recorded rather than hidden.

## Value
PASS. The regular octagon is a canonical benchmark for exact Banach-space geometric constants, and its unrestricted exponent profile has been in the literature since 2017. The missing modified counterpart matters because the modified constant is precisely the equal-radius/unit-sphere version used in later geometric characterizations. The result supplies a complete natural sub-Hilbert profile and identifies the exact exponent at which equal-radius normalization stops changing the extremal value; this is a structural comparison rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Yang–Wang (2017), which computes the unrestricted exponent profile on the same octagon, and Fu–Li (2022), which studies the modified constant generally and supplies the ball formulation used in the proof. The result is restricted to the real regular octagonal plane and \(p\ge1\); no claim is made for other polygon families or the concave range \(0<p<1\).
