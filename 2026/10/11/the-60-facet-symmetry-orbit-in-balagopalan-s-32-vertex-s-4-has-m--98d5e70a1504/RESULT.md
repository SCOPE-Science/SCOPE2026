# The 60-facet symmetry orbit in Balagopalan’s 32-vertex \(S^4\) has mod-two homology concentrated in degree two
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the 32-vertex antipodal triangulation of \(S^4\) constructed by Sonia Balagopalan. The published construction states that the 300 facets form two orbits under \(C_2\times S_6\), of sizes 60 and 240. Let \(L\) be the subcomplex generated by the smaller orbit. Then
\[
f(L)=(32,210,480,270,60),
\]
and
\[
\widetilde H_i(L;\mathbf F_2)=0\quad(i\ne2),\qquad
\widetilde H_2(L;\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2^{91}.
\]
Thus the smaller symmetry orbit, although it is only one fifth of the facets of \(K\), already supports 91 independent mod-two two-dimensional homology classes.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex labels and four facet families are those in Balagopalan’s construction. Vertices are the six points \(e_i\), the twenty triangle barycentres \(\frac13(e_i+e_j+e_k)\), and the six facet barycentres \(\frac15(\mathbf 1-e_i)\). The first two displayed facet families in the source have 30 facets each and are exchanged by the antipodal involution; together they are the 60-facet \(C_2\times S_6\)-orbit defining \(L\). The claim concerns simplicial homology with coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). It does not assert the homotopy type or fundamental group of \(L\).

## Proof
Construct the two 30-facet families exactly from the published index formulas and close them under taking faces. Direct enumeration gives \(32\) vertices, \(210\) edges, \(480\) triangles, \(270\) tetrahedra, and \(60\) four-simplices.

There is then a deterministic sequence of 270 elementary simplicial collapses. At each step the lower-dimensional simplex is contained in a unique maximal coface, so deleting the pair is a genuine elementary collapse. After the 270 deletions the remaining complex is two-dimensional with face vector
\[
(32,210,270).
\]
Elementary collapse preserves homotopy type and hence homology.

For the remaining two-dimensional complex, exact Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbf F_2\) gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(\partial_1)=31,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\partial_2)=179.
\]
Therefore
\[
\beta_0=32-31=1,
\]
\[
\beta_1=210-31-179=0,
\]
and
\[
\beta_2=270-179=91.
\]
There are no chains in higher dimensions after the collapse. This proves the stated reduced homology. A direct computation on the unreduced 60-facet complex independently returns the same Betti vector \((1,0,91,0,0)\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs all four published facet families, checks that the full sphere description has face vector \((32,240,660,750,300)\), isolates the 60-facet orbit, verifies every elementary-collapse condition as it is used, and computes the mod-two boundary ranks by exact bitwise Gaussian elimination. Its expected terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK full_f=32,240,660,750,300 orbit_facets=60 f=32,210,480,270,60 collapses=270 core_f=32,210,270 ranks=31,179 betti=1,0,91`

No floating-point arithmetic or randomized step is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Balagopalan’s thesis constructs the 32-vertex antipodal \(S^4\) and explicitly states that its four displayed facet families become two families under \(C_2\times S_6\); their sizes are 30, 30, 120, and 120. The later journal article repeats these formulas and identifies the resulting sphere as the double cover of the 16-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{RP}^4\). Those sources determine the natural 60-facet orbit but do not state its homology or a collapse reduction. Focused searches for the exact object together with “facet orbit”, “homology”, “60 facets”, “91”, and equivalent small-orbit formulations found no prior statement implying the result.

## Limitations
The result is an exact statement about one canonical symmetry orbit and mod-two homology. It does not determine integral torsion, the fundamental group, or a wedge decomposition. The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed computation in old software archives or unpublished notes; no such covering computation was found in the inspected primary sources or indexed searches.
