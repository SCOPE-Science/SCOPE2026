# The antipodal half-turn representation on Möbius-ladder perfect-matching homology

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 2\), the antipodal half-turn on the perfect-matching complex of the Möbius ladder \(M_{2n}\) has reduced Lefschetz number

\[
\widetilde\Lambda(\tau;X_n)=
\begin{cases}
2(-1)^{n/2+1},& n\text{ even},\\
0,& n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

Its **entire reduced rational homology representation** in the unique nonzero homological degree, as a module for the cyclic group generated by \(\tau\), is

\[
\widetilde H_*(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q_{\mathrm{triv}}^{\oplus2},&n\text{ even},\\
\mathbb Q[C_2]^{\oplus2},&n\equiv3\pmod6,\\
\mathbb Q[C_2],&n\equiv1,5\pmod6\text{ and }n\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

The notation means that the displayed representation occupies the single degree given by Agarwal–Basak's nonequivariant classification: degree \(n/2-1\) for even \(n\), degree \(n/3\) for \(n\equiv3\pmod6\), degree \((n-1)/3\) for \(n\equiv1\pmod6\), and degree \((n+1)/3\) for \(n\equiv5\pmod6\). No positive or reduced homology occurs outside that degree. In particular, the even-\(n\) action is the identity on reduced integral homology as well (whose underlying abelian group is free of rank two).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(M_{2n}\) has vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), its \(2n\) rim edges join consecutive residue classes, and its \(n\) spoke edges join antipodal pairs \(\{i,i+n\}\). For \(n=2\), this graph is \(K_4\). The **perfect-matching simplicial complex** \(X_n=\mathcal M_p(M_{2n})\) has graph edges as simplicial vertices and has as faces exactly the sets of pairwise disjoint edges that extend to a perfect matching. This is *not* the ordinary matching complex and *not* the discrete Morse complex. Let \(\tau\) be induced by the graph automorphism \(i\mapsto i+n\). Reduced chains include the augmented empty simplex of degree \(-1\), on which \(\tau\) acts trivially.

All homology-representation statements below are over \(\mathbb Q\) except the explicitly stated even-case integral consequence. The non-equivariant sphere dimensions/ranks used in the representation step are those proved in Agarwal–Basak, arXiv:2608.30601, Main Theorem 1(b,c), not new here.

## Proof

**1. Orbit description of invariant faces.** Partition the graph vertices into \(n\) antipodal columns \(\{i,i+n\}\) indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\). The spoke in each column is fixed by \(\tau\); the rim edges fall into \(n\) two-edge orbits, the \(i\)-th orbit consisting of the two rim edges joining consecutive columns \(i\) and \(i+1\). For \(n=2\), there are *two distinct* such orbits joining the same two columns, so the column cycle is treated as a two-edge multigraph.

An invariant face is a collection of \(s\) spoke singletons and \(t\) entire rim-edge orbits. Because the face is a matching, these objects cover pairwise disjoint sets of columns, each spoke using one column and each rim-edge orbit using the two adjacent columns. Conversely every such disjoint monomer/dimer packing extends to a perfect matching by adding the spokes in all as-yet-uncovered columns. The correspondence includes the empty face and is bijective.

**2. Signed fixed-simplex trace.** Each rim-edge orbit in an invariant face contributes one transposition to the permutation of its oriented simplicial vertices, while a spoke contributes a fixed vertex. Therefore an invariant face with \(s+2t\) edge-vertices contributes \((-1)^t\) to the simplicial chain trace in degree \(s+2t-1\). The simplicial Lefschetz trace formula, including the augmented empty face, yields

\[
\widetilde\Lambda(\tau;X_n)
=-\sum_{\substack{D\text{ disjoint rim-orbit dimers}\\ S\text{ disjoint spoke monomers}}}(-1)^{|D|+|S|}.
\]

**3. Exact cancellation.** Fix a collection \(D\) of disjoint rim dimers with \(t\) orbits. There are \(n-2t\) available columns for monomers. Their sum of signs is \((1-1)^{n-2t}\); hence every term cancels unless \(n=2t\). If \(n\) is odd there is no term remaining. If \(n\) is even there are exactly two dimer tilings of the cycle of columns, including the two individually distinguished rim-orbit choices when \(n=2\). Each has sign \((-1)^{n/2}\). Thus the displayed reduced Lefschetz number follows for every \(n\ge2\), with no asymptotic or experimental assumption.

**4. Recovering the entire involution representation.** Agarwal–Basak prove that \(X_n\) has reduced integral homology concentrated in one degree and is free: rank two for even \(n\); rank four for \(n\equiv3\pmod6\); rank two for the other odd cases. If that degree is \(d\), homological Lefschetz gives \(\widetilde\Lambda=(-1)^d\operatorname{tr}(\tau_*\mid\widetilde H_d)\). For even \(n\), \(d=n/2-1\), so the trace is two; an order-two rational operator on a two-dimensional space must then be the identity. For odd \(n\), the trace is zero and the involution's \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces have equal dimension, giving the stated sums of regular rational representations. In even cases the integral action is the identity because it becomes the identity after tensoring the free integral homology with \(\mathbb Q\). No integral splitting claim is made for odd \(n\).

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 program `verify_ladder.py` uses only the standard library. It constructs the actual labeled graph, enumerates all perfect matchings and all their faces, computes the signed invariant-simplex trace *directly from the edge permutation*, and independently enumerates monomer/dimer packings. For every integer \(2\le n\le9\), the two computations agree and give the stated parity formula. For \(2\le n\le8\), an independent exact mod-two boundary-rank elimination computes the reduced Betti dimensions and matches the published nonequivariant theorem. Executing `python3 verify_ladder.py` on the delivered file prints `VERIFY_OK` after these checks. These finite computations are consistency tests; the all-parameter Lefschetz formula is proved by cancellation in Step 3, and the homology-rank input is a separately published theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs* (arXiv:2608.30601, first posted August 31, 2026, v2 September 14, 2026), determine the **nonequivariant** homotopy types for these ladder families, with the precise sphere counts and dimensions in Main Theorem 1(b,c). That theorem supplies the underlying ranks used here but does not, as stated or in the inspected proof, compute the half-turn's action on the resulting homology. The signed fixed-face calculation adds the missing representation information; neither the number of spheres nor their dimensions by itself determines whether a homological involution is trivial or has balanced \(+1/-1\) eigenvalues. Previous perfect-matching-complex work on honeycomb and polygonal-tiling graphs concerns different graph families. Searches for the same involution, its Lefschetz character, and equivariant perfect-matching homology found no dominating published formula, but database nonappearance is not a uniqueness proof.

## Limitations

The result determines only the action of the central order-two half-turn, not the full dihedral automorphism-group representation; for odd \(n\), it does not determine the corresponding integral lattice as a \(\mathbb Z[C_2]\)-module or equivariant homotopy type. The Betti ranks and sphere dimensions are invoked from the cited source, not independently reproved for all \(n\). An unindexed equivalent equivariant calculation remains a possible originality risk. Finite exact checks alone would not establish the universal formulas; that logical role belongs to the proof above.
