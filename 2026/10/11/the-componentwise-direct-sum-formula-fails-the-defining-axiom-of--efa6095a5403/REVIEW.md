# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source's own \(2\)-inner-product axiom says that \(\langle x,x\mid z\rangle=0\) exactly when \(x,z\) are linearly dependent. For nonzero \(a\) and \(b\), the vectors \(a\oplus2b\) and \(a\oplus b\) are independent, but each component pair is dependent, so formula (10) assigns squared \(2\)-inner product zero. This directly contradicts the defining axiom.

The Hilbert-induced comparison is also exact: the canonical \(2\)-inner product on an orthogonal Hilbert direct sum is the \(2\times2\) Gram determinant and includes cross terms absent from the source formula.

## Originality
PASS for the source-specific claim. Exact-source, theorem-number, direct-sum, componentwise-formula, and \(2\)-inner-product formulations were searched and compared. A prior result concerning a different tensor-product theorem in the same article addresses surjectivity versus invertibility of an operator and does not imply this direct-sum axiom failure. The closest indexed frame results concern different range, perturbation, duality, or convergence questions.

The first public PDF and the published full text were materially inspected. The published version retains formula (10) and the direct-sum theorem. An unindexed correction remains a residual literature risk.

## Value
PASS. The issue invalidates the claimed ambient \(n\)-Hilbert direct sum at the level of its defining geometry and therefore undercuts the article's direct-sum controlled-frame theorem as stated. The obstruction is universal for nontrivial \(2\)-inner-product factors, not a special numerical accident. The full Gram-determinant construction in the Hilbert-induced subclass identifies the missing structural terms.

## Limitations
The finding does not classify all direct sums of abstract \(n\)-inner-product spaces. The canonical repair is asserted only for Hilbert-induced \(2\)-inner products, and the source's frame theorem is not claimed to survive unchanged under that repair.
