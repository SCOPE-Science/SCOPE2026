# The \(D_4\)-character of square-grid three-cut homology

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\), and let \(G_n=P_n\square P_n\) have vertex set \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^2\), with edges between pairs at Manhattan distance one. Its ordinary three-cut complex \(\Delta_3(G_n)\) is the simplicial complex generated by complements of disconnected induced three-vertex subsets. Set \(N=n^2\), \(d=N-4\), and let \(D_4\) act on the grid, generated by the quarter-turn \(r(i,j)=(j,n-1-i)\) and axis reflection \(s(i,j)=(i,n-1-j)\).

The rational character \(\chi_n\) of \(\widetilde H_d(\Delta_3(G_n);\mathbb Q)\), evaluated on the five conjugacy classes \(\{1\},\{r,r^3\},\{r^2\},\{s,r^2s\},\{sr,r^3s\}\), is

\[
\chi_n=(B_n,0,H_n,X_n,0),\qquad
B_n=\frac{n^4-13n^2+20n-4}{2},\qquad
H_n=2-\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
\[
X_n=\begin{cases}
n-n^2/2,&n\text{ even},\\
(-1)^{(n-1)/2}(3-n),&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(u,v\in\{\pm1\}\), let \(\rho_{u,v}\) be the rational one-dimensional representation specified by \(\rho_{u,v}(r)=u\), \(\rho_{u,v}(s)=v\), and let \(V_2\) be the standard two-dimensional rational irreducible representation. Then

\[
\widetilde H_d(\Delta_3(G_n);\mathbb Q)
\cong\bigoplus_{u=\pm1}\left(\rho_{u,+1}^{\oplus a_n}\oplus\rho_{u,-1}^{\oplus b_n}\right)\oplus V_2^{\oplus c_n},
\]
\[
a_n=\frac{B_n+H_n+2X_n}{8},\quad
b_n=\frac{B_n+H_n-2X_n}{8},\quad
c_n=\frac{B_n-H_n}{4}.
\]
All multiplicities are nonnegative integers. The only nonzero reduced integral homology is free of rank \(B_n\) in degree \(d\); this nonequivariant part is an established result of Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Sundaram and Xue [1], not a new claim. The new determination is the complete square-grid dihedral character and its rational irreducible decomposition.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(P_n\) denotes the path with \(n\) vertices. All faces use the **ordinary** cut-complex convention: a facet is the complement of a **disconnected** induced three-vertex subgraph, not a total cut-complex facet. Homology is reduced simplicial homology; a group element acts via its permutation of vertices, including the induced orientation sign on each oriented simplex. The result applies to all integers \(n\ge3\). It does not assert an integral \(D_4\)-module splitting, an equivariant sphere-wedge equivalence, or statements for nonsquare grids.

## Proof

**1. Face complement structure and homology concentration.** Reference [1], Proposition 5.6, proves shellability of \(\Delta_3(P_m\square P_n)\); Proposition 5.8 determines its wedge-of-top-spheres homotopy type and the top Betti number. Specializing \(m=n\), its sole reduced homology is free in degree \(d=N-4\). Reference [1], Proposition 2.18, also identifies its face lattice as the truncated Boolean lattice of subsets of size at most \(N-3\), minus complements of connected triples and unit-square four-cycles. For completeness, a direct argument works in this grid: a subset \(S\) is a face exactly when its complement contains a disconnected triple. When the complement has at least five vertices, choose nonadjacent vertices \(x,y\); if every triple were connected, every other vertex would have to be adjacent to both \(x,y\), impossible because any pair in a square grid has at most two common neighbors. With four complementary vertices, every triple is connected precisely when the induced graph is a four-cycle (no triangles and at least two-vertex-connectivity); with three, failure occurs precisely for an induced connected path. No other faces are missing.

**2. Signed Hopf trace.** Fix \(g\in D_4\). Denote by \(\epsilon(g)\) the sign of its permutation of all \(N\) vertices, by \(f(g)\) its number of fixed vertices, and by \(t(g)\) the number of two-cycles in that permutation. Define

\[
p(g)=\sum_{T}\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_T),\qquad
q(g)=\sum_{Q}\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_Q),
\]
where \(T\) ranges over \(g\)-invariant induced connected three-vertex sets (paths) and \(Q\) over \(g\)-invariant unit-square vertex sets; if a set is not invariant it contributes zero. These are signed counts, not ordinary fixed-set counts. The reduced Hopf trace identity gives

\[
\boxed{\chi_n(g)=\epsilon(g)\left(1-f(g)+\binom{f(g)}2-t(g)-p(g)+q(g)\right).}
\]
Indeed, a fixed oriented face has trace equal to the sign of the induced permutation on its vertices, and for a fixed complement the product of that sign with the complement sign is \(\epsilon(g)\). The alternating trace over *all* subsets vanishes (it is the determinant \(\det(I-P_g)=0\), since every permutation matrix has eigenvalue one). Removing the nonfaces—complements of subsets of cardinality zero, one or two, connected triples, and unit squares—therefore yields precisely the displayed formula, with the global sign determined by the top degree \(d=N-4\). Since all reduced homology is in this degree, this alternating trace equals \((-1)^d\chi_n(g)\).

**3. Counts for the five conjugacy classes.** All values below concern permutations of the \(N\) grid vertices.

For the identity, \(f=N\), \(t=0\), \(\epsilon=1\), \(p=6n^2-12n+4\) (sum of \(\binom{\deg(v)}2\) over vertices), and \(q=(n-1)^2\). The formula yields \(B_n\) above, consistently with [1].

For a quarter-turn, if \(n\) is odd there is one fixed central vertex and no invariant connected three-vertex set or unit square; hence the bracket is \(1-1=0\). If \(n\) is even, there are no fixed vertices or two-cycles, and the unique invariant central unit square contributes \(-1\) (its vertices form a four-cycle); the bracket is \(1-1=0\). Hence \(\chi_n(r)=0\).

For a half-turn, if \(n\) is odd, \(f=1\), \(t=(n^2-1)/2\), \(p=-2\) from the two opposite-neighbor paths centered at the grid center, \(q=0\), and \(\epsilon=+1\). If \(n\) is even, \(f=0\), \(t=n^2/2\), \(p=0\), \(q=+1\) from the central square, and \(\epsilon=+1\). Both give \(\chi_n(r^2)=H_n\).

For an axis reflection and even \(n\), \(f=0\), \(t=n^2/2\), \(p=0\), \(q=n-1\) (squares crossing the reflection axis, each with positive orientation sign), and \(\epsilon=+1\). For odd \(n\), \(f=n\), \(t=n(n-1)/2\), \(p=(n-2)-n=-2\) (\(n-2\) axial paths fixed pointwise and \(n\) transverse paths with endpoints swapped), \(q=0\), and \(\epsilon=(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\). Thus \(\chi_n(s)=X_n\).

For a diagonal reflection, \(f=n\), \(t=n(n-1)/2\), \(p=-2(n-1)\) (all invariant three-vertex paths have exchanged endpoints), and \(q=-(n-1)\) (all invariant squares reverse orientation). The bracket is identically zero; hence \(\chi_n(sr)=0\). Conjugate group elements have identical traces.

**4. Rational representation.** The four one-dimensional \(D_4\)-characters take values \((1,u,1,v,uv)\), and the natural two-dimensional character takes values \((2,0,-2,0,0)\) on the chosen class representatives. The conjugacy-class sizes are \((1,2,1,2,2)\). Taking character inner products with \((B_n,0,H_n,X_n,0)\) gives \(a_n,b_n,c_n\) exactly as stated. Because this character is realized by rational homology, these are nonnegative integers. This completes the classification.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. Run `python3 verify.py`. It constructs the grid and all signed fixed connected triples/squares for every \(3\le n\le12\), checks the closed character formula, and verifies irreducible multiplicity integrality and the dimension identity. Independently of the deleted-strata argument, for \(n=3,4\) it enumerates **every face** directly from disconnected induced triples and computes the complete signed reduced chain supertrace under each of the five symmetries. It checks the formulas on these actual complexes and ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

For example, the character is \((10,0,-2,0,0)\) at \(n=3\), decomposing with multiplicities \((a_3,b_3,c_3)=(1,1,3)\); at \(n=4\) it is \((62,0,-6,-4,0)\), decomposing as \((6,8,17)\). These finite checks corroborate the infinite-family mathematical proof; they do not certify every order by enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer et al. [1], §§2.2 and 5, already determine the square-grid ordinary three-cut complex's face lattice, shellability, homotopy concentration and *dimension* of its top homology. Those results are used as premises. Section 6 of [1] studies equivariant homology of disjoint unions of complete graphs, paths and cycles, not the square-grid \(D_4\)-module. The present character calculation is a new, strictly finer invariant not determined by the published Betti number or nonequivariant sphere wedge. The initial cut-complex definitions and previous graph-family results are in [2]. Follow-up work [3] concerns, among other objects, total cut complexes of grids; the total construction is not the ordinary three-cut complex studied here.

## Limitations

The group action is classified **over \(\mathbb Q\)** only. This does not supply a simultaneous integral basis, a complete integral equivariant classification, or an equivariant simple-homotopy type. The proof relies on the previously published shellability theorem [1], not a new proof of shellability. Although the formulas were compared with targeted literature and known computational reports, an unindexed equivalent character calculation cannot be excluded; novelty remains subject to normal scholarly verification.
