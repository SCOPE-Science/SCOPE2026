# The degree-three Xiao monodromy has full Lagrangian unipotent image modulo \(3\)
## Finding
Let \(f_3:S(E,3)\to\mathbf P^1\) be Xiao's degree-three genus-two Lefschetz fibration, and let
\[
\bar\rho_3:\pi_1(\mathbf P^1\setminus\operatorname{Crit}(f_3))\longrightarrow \operatorname{Sp}(H_1(\Sigma_2;\mathbf F_3))
\]
be its mod-\(3\) homological monodromy. Let \(L\) be the \(\mathbf F_3\)-span of the four nonseparating vanishing-cycle classes. Then \(L\) is a Lagrangian two-plane. In a symplectic basis adapted to \(L\),
\[
\operatorname{im}\bar\rho_3=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}I_2&B\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}:B=B^{\mathsf T}\in M_2(\mathbf F_3)
\right\}.
\]
Thus the image is elementary abelian of order \(27\), and the four nonseparating local monodromies correspond exactly once to the four projective lines of \(L\cong\mathbf F_3^2\).
## Assumptions and scope
The input is the degree-three fibration treated by Szabó. The source gives seven vanishing cycles, four nonseparating and three separating, a global product of their Dehn twists equal to the identity, divisibility by \(3\) of every algebraic intersection between vanishing cycles, the Picard--Lefschetz action of a right Dehn twist on first homology, and \(\pi_1(S(E,3))\cong\mathbf Z^2\). The claim concerns only the induced action on \(H_1(\Sigma_2;\mathbf F_3)\); it makes no assertion about the full mapping-class-group monodromy.

For a genus-two Lefschetz fibration over \(S^2\), attaching the Lefschetz two-handles kills the homology classes of the vanishing cycles, so
\[
H_1(S(E,3);\mathbf Z)\cong H_1(\Sigma_2;\mathbf Z)/\langle[\delta_i]\rangle.
\]
Since the total-space fundamental group is \(\mathbf Z^2\), its abelianization is torsion-free of rank two. Consequently the lattice generated by all vanishing-cycle classes has rank two and is primitive. Separating cycles have zero first-homology class, so the four nonseparating classes generate this primitive rank-two lattice.
## Proof
Reduce the four nonseparating classes modulo \(3\), writing them as \(v_1,\ldots,v_4\) in the four-dimensional symplectic space \(V=H_1(\Sigma_2;\mathbf F_3)\). Primitivity of the integral rank-two vanishing lattice implies that their reductions span a two-dimensional subspace \(L\). Szabó's divisibility statement gives \(\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=0\) in \(\mathbf F_3\) for all \(i,j\). Hence \(L\) is isotropic, and because \(\dim L=2=\frac12\dim V\), it is Lagrangian.

For \(v\in L\), Picard--Lefschetz gives the transvection
\[
T_v(x)=x+\langle v,x\rangle v= (I+N_v)(x).
\]
Because \(L\) is isotropic, \(N_vN_w=0\) for \(v,w\in L\). Thus these transvections commute and their product is \(I+\sum N_v\). The three separating Dehn twists act trivially on first homology. Reducing the global monodromy relation modulo \(3\) therefore yields
\[
N_{v_1}+N_{v_2}+N_{v_3}+N_{v_4}=0.
\]

Choose a basis of \(L\). Its four projective lines have representatives
\[
(1,0),\quad(0,1),\quad(1,1),\quad(1,-1).
\]
The associated rank-one symmetric forms are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If their multiplicities among the four nonseparating cycles are \(a,b,c,d\), respectively, then the zero-sum relation gives
\[
a+c+d\equiv0,\qquad b+c+d\equiv0,\qquad c-d\equiv0\pmod3.
\]
Together with \(a+b+c+d=4\), these congruences force \(a=b=c=d=1\). Therefore every projective line in \(L\) occurs exactly once.

The first three displayed rank-one forms span the three-dimensional space \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbf F_3)\). In an adapted symplectic splitting \(V=L\oplus L^\vee\), the corresponding transvections generate exactly
\[
U_L=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_2&B\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}:B=B^{\mathsf T}\right\}.
\]
This group is additively isomorphic to \(\operatorname{Sym}_2(\mathbf F_3)\), so it has \(3^3=27\) elements and is elementary abelian.
## Verification
The finite \(\mathbf F_3\) step was independently replayed by `artifacts/verify_mod3_monodromy.py`. It enumerates the four projective lines, verifies that the unique four-term multiset of their rank-one forms summing to zero is the full set of four lines, verifies that those forms span all \(27\) symmetric two-by-two matrices, and checks the resulting adapted-basis transvections commute and multiply as claimed.

The structural part is not inferred from the computation: the rank-two primitive vanishing lattice follows from the standard first-homology quotient for a Lefschetz fibration together with the source's \(\pi_1(S(E,3))\cong\mathbf Z^2\), while isotropy follows from the source's intersection-divisibility theorem.
## Relationship to prior work
Szabó gives the explicit degree-three monodromy factorization, proves the intersection divisibility and total-space fundamental group statements used above, and checks the integral homological product relation. The source does not identify the resulting congruence image. Huang proves that Xiao's type \((4,3)\) factorization is equivalent to the Baykur--Korkmaz and Hamada factorizations, but does not state a mod-\(3\) homological image. Searches for congruence-monodromy, transvection-image, elementary-abelian, and \(\operatorname{Sp}(4,3)\) formulations found no published statement implying the result.
## Limitations
The conclusion is only about mod-\(3\) first-homology monodromy. It does not determine the integral symplectic image, the kernel of the mapping-class-group representation, or the full mapping-class-group monodromy. A recent independent preprint by Akhmedov studies the same Xiao fibration via braid monodromy; only its abstract and indexed summaries were accessible during comparison, so an unindexed equivalent observation in its full text remains a residual originality risk.
