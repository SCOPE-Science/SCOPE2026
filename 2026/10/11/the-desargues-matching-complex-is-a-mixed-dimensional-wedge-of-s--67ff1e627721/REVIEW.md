# Scientific review: Desargues matching complex

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite graph is specified as the incidence graph between the two- and three-subsets of a five-element set, and an explicit isomorphism to \(GP(10,3)\) is checked. A standalone exact enumerator constructs all 113,192 faces including the empty face. On the ordinary 113,191-face poset, the fully specified element-toggle matching contains 56,567 pairs and has one critical vertex, eight five-cells and forty-eight six-cells. Topological sorting of all 680,370 oriented incidences proves acyclicity. Exact signed gradient-path reduction gives an integral eight-by-forty-eight boundary whose image is the primitive rank-three summand generated by \(e_0-e_1\), \(e_3\) and \(e_6-e_7\), with explicit unit columns. Since all higher critical cells are in dimension six, the degree classification of maps into a wedge of five-spheres implies the claimed wedge after three unit cancellations. No numerical approximation enters the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest same-graph published result, *Mod-2 homology of the Desargues matching complex* (published-finding corpus identifier ending `91609797437d`), asserts only mod-two Betti ranks \(5,45\) and explicitly does not determine integral torsion, attaching maps or homotopy type. The full available Donovan–Scoville preprint determines matching-complex homotopy for paths, cycles and Dutch windmill graphs, not this graph. A separate published Möbius–Kantor matching-complex result concerns \(GP(8,3)\), not \(GP(10,3)\), and does not assert a family-wide theorem. Searches also used the bipartite Kneser and line-graph aliases. No stronger covering statement was found, but this cannot rule out unindexed or unpublished prior computations.

**Value — PASS.** The Desargues graph is a canonical cubic symmetric configuration graph. Its matching complex has homology in adjacent dimensions, so field-valued ranks alone leave the torsion and attaching-map questions unresolved. This result determines both the integral homology and the full homotopy type using a compact reproducible three-unit boundary certificate. It is a motivated completion of an independently documented partial calculation, not a mere change of coefficients or routine graph renaming.

**Limits.** The theorem is restricted to one explicitly defined finite graph and does not supply an equivariant splitting or parameter-uniform result. Published similarity searches cannot certify absolute historical uniqueness.
