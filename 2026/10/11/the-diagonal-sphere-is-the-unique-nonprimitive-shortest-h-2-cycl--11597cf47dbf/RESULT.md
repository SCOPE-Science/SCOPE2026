# The diagonal sphere is the unique nonprimitive shortest \(H_2\)-cycle in the natural ten-vertex \(\mathbb{CP}^{2}\)
## Finding
Let \(K=\mathbb{CP}^{2}_{10}\) be the ten-vertex triangulation of Bagchi--Datta. Among integral simplicial \(2\)-cycles representing nonzero classes in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), the minimum vertex support is four. Exactly \(35\) four-vertex supports attain this minimum, namely the missing tetrahedra. Of these, \(34\) tetrahedral-boundary cycles represent a primitive generator up to sign, while the unique induced diagonal sphere on \(\{x_{11},x_{22},x_{33},x_{44}\}\) represents twice a primitive generator up to sign. Under the full automorphism group \(A_4\), the \(35\) supports split into orbits of sizes \(1,4,6,12,12\); the singleton is exactly the diagonal double class, and the four nontrivial orbits are primitive.

Equivalently, after orienting every tetrahedral boundary by increasing vertex order and fixing the primitive generator detected in the verification, the \(35\) minimum-support classes have absolute coefficients \(1\) with multiplicity \(34\) and \(2\) with multiplicity \(1\). The coefficient-\(1\) distinction is independent of the orientation choice.

## Assumptions and scope
The complex \(K\) is the exact ten-vertex triangulation in Bagchi--Datta, with vertices \(x_{ij}\) for \(1\le i\le j\le4\). It is generated from the five published facet representatives by the published \(A_4\)-action. The source proves that this complex triangulates \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), has full automorphism group \(A_4\), is \(2\)-neighbourly, and has face vector \( (10,45,110,120,48)\). The statement here concerns integral simplicial homology of this labeled complex only.

## Proof
The packaged checker regenerates the \(48\) facets from the five published representatives and the two published permutations. It then constructs the integral simplicial boundary maps. A displayed unimodular \(36\)-minor of \(\partial_2\), together with \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=9\) and \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), proves \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=36\). A displayed unimodular \(73\)-minor of \(\partial_3\) proves rank at least \(73\) and makes \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) primitive in \(C_2\).

An explicit integral \(2\)-cocycle \(\phi\) annihilates every tetrahedral boundary and evaluates to \(1\) on one missing-tetrahedron boundary \(z_0\). Hence \(z_0\notin\operatorname{im}\partial_3\), so \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3\le73\); therefore its rank is exactly \(73\). Thus \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) has rank \(110-36-73=1\). Because the unimodular minor makes \(C_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) torsion-free, its subgroup \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is torsion-free, hence isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\). The equality \(\phi(z_0)=1\) makes \([z_0]\) a primitive generator.

A nonzero simplicial \(2\)-cycle cannot be supported on fewer than four vertices. On a fixed four-vertex set, the triangle-to-edge boundary equations have a nonzero kernel exactly when all four triangles are present, in which case that kernel is generated by the oriented boundary of the tetrahedron. If the tetrahedron itself is a face, this cycle bounds. Therefore the minimum-support nonzero classes are exactly the missing tetrahedra. Exhaustion gives \(35\) of them. Evaluating \(\phi\) on their boundaries gives absolute value \(1\) on \(34\) supports and \(2\) only on \(\{x_{11},x_{22},x_{33},x_{44}\}\). Finally, exhaustive action by the reconstructed \(A_4\) gives orbit sizes \(1,4,6,12,12\), with the diagonal set as the singleton.

## Relationship to prior work
Bagchi--Datta construct the exact complex used here, prove that it triangulates \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), give its full \(A_4\) automorphism group and face vector, and identify the complementary induced subcomplexes \(S^2_4\) and \(\mathbb{RP}^2_6\). Their \(S^2_4\) is the combinatorial child of the diagonal \(S^2\) in the twofold branched-cover construction. The inspected full text does not enumerate missing tetrahedra, classify minimum-support integral \(H_2\)-cycles, or state the \(34+1\) primitive/double spectrum or the orbit split proved here.

A separate published result for Kühnel's nine-vertex triangulation classifies its proper induced subcomplexes by collapse type; it concerns a different complex and does not imply this integral homology coefficient spectrum.

## Limitations
The theorem is exact for this labeled ten-vertex triangulation and its full automorphism group. It does not assert that the same \(34+1\) spectrum holds for other triangulations of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), nor does it classify cycles with more than four vertices. The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed finite computation or software table containing the same \(35\)-support census.
