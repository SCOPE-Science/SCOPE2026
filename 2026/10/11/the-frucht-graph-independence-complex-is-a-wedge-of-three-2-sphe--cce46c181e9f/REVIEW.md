# Review of The Frucht-graph independence complex is a wedge of three 2-spheres

## Correctness
PASS. The graph is fixed by an explicit edge list and independently identified with a standard Frucht LCF realization. Exhaustive enumeration gives \(168\) nonempty faces with face vector \((12,48,73,34,1)\). A complete check of all \(456\) Hasse covers proves that the stated \(82\)-pair matching is acyclic and leaves exactly one critical \(0\)-cell and three critical \(2\)-cells. The resulting CW model is therefore \(\bigvee^3S^2\). Signed chain calculations over three finite fields independently give Betti vector \((1,0,3,0,0)\).

## Originality
PASS. Exact searches under the Frucht name, identity-graph/asymmetric-cubic descriptions, Halin terminology, and the target wedge type found no covering statement. Full-text inspection of arXiv:2207.10363v1 and arXiv:1905.06926v1 shows that their explicit homotopy theorems concern square grids and other restricted graph constructions, not the Frucht graph. The closest checked same-object work concerns Vietoris--Rips complexes rather than the independence complex. Residual bibliographic risk from obscure or non-indexed sources remains.

## Value
PASS. The Frucht graph is a canonical smallest asymmetric cubic identity graph, making its independence complex a natural symmetry-free benchmark. The result compresses a \(4\)-dimensional, \(168\)-face complex to a perfect Morse model with three \(2\)-cells, providing an exact structural datum rather than a routine recomputation of a tabulated invariant.
