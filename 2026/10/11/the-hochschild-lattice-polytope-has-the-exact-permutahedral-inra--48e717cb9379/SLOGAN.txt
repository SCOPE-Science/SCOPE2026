The Pilaud--Poliakova \((1,n)\)-Hochschild polytope has exactly the same intrinsic Euclidean inradius \(\sqrt{n(n+1)}/2\) as the standard permutahedron, and its unique incenter is the uniform point \(((n+2)/2)\mathbf1\). The equality survives the Hochschild deformation because all tight subset-sum right-hand sides move weakly outward while the singleton constraints remain fixed.
