# The integral face–winding obstruction in toroidal total two-cut complexes

## Finding
Let \(m,n\ge4\) be integers, let \(G=C_m\square C_n\) be the Cartesian product of simple cycles, let \(N=mn\), and let \(K=\Delta^t_2(G)\) be the total two-cut complex: a subset of \(V(G)\) is a face precisely when its complement contains a pair of nonadjacent vertices. Let \(\Gamma\) be the group generated by independent rotations and reflections in the two cycle coordinates, together with interchange of the coordinates when \(m=n\). The translation subgroup is \(T=C_m\times C_n\).

There is a natural, \(\Gamma\)-equivariant short exact sequence of integral lattices
\[
0\longrightarrow \operatorname{sgn}_{V}\otimes\rho_{\mathbb Z}^{\vee}
\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{N-4}(K;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\otimes B^{\vee}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\rho_{\mathbb Z}\) is the rank-two signed coordinate-permutation action of \(\Gamma\) on the fundamental cycles of the torus, \(B=\varepsilon\otimes(\mathbb Z[F]/\mathbb Z\sum_{f\in F}f)\), \(F\) is the set of \(mn\) elementary torus squares, \(\varepsilon(g)=\det\rho_{\mathbb Z}(g)\), and \(\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\) is the sign of the permutation of graph vertices. Here \((\cdot)^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\cdot,\mathbb Z)\) has the contragredient action. **Restricted to \(T\), the extension class has exact additive order \(\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\)**. In particular, the extension does not split as an integral \(T\)-module.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), the extension splits as a representation of the finite group \(\Gamma\), giving
\[
\widetilde H_{N-4}(K;\mathbb Q)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_{V}\otimes\left[
\left(\varepsilon\otimes(\mathbb Q[F]/\mathbb Q\sum_{f\in F} f)\right)
\oplus \rho_{\mathbb Q}\right].
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish, and \(\widetilde H_{N-4}(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{N+1}\) as an abelian group. For each \(g\in\Gamma\), let \(A_g\) be its signed \(2\times2\) coordinate matrix and let \(f(g)\) be the number of elementary torus squares it fixes. Then the full rational homology character is
\[
\chi(g)=\operatorname{sgn}_{V}(g)
\bigl[\det(A_g)(f(g)-1)+\operatorname{tr}(A_g)\bigr].
\]
For any nonidentity translation, the character of the untwisted graph cycle space is \(1\), whereas at the identity its dimension is \(N+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The cycles \(C_m,C_n\) have the usual undirected edges between successive residues, with indices modulo \(m,n\). The lower bounds \(m,n\ge4\) ensure that the graph is simple and triangle-free, and that the standard square faces provide a cellular decomposition of the torus. The group \(\Gamma\) is the specified natural subgroup of graph automorphisms; no assertion is made that it equals the full automorphism group for exceptional parameters. Every module and Ext group is over the integral group ring of the indicated symmetry group. The extension order refers to its restriction to \(\mathbb Z[T]\), not to a scalar order in an unspecified extension category.

## Proof
**1. Complement duality and homological degree.** For any finite graph, a subset belongs to its total two-cut complex exactly when its complement is not a clique. Hence \(K=L^*\), the combinatorial Alexander dual of the clique complex \(L=\operatorname{Cl}(G)\). The graph \(G\) has no triangles, so \(L\) is precisely its one-dimensional realization. Equivariant integral combinatorial Alexander duality on the ordered \(N\)-vertex set gives, with the sign character induced by the ambient vertex permutation,
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_V\otimes \widetilde H^{N-i-3}(G;\mathbb Z).
\]
Since \(G\) is connected and one-dimensional, reduced cohomology is only in degree one, proving concentration at \(i=N-4\). There are \(N\) vertices and \(2N\) edges, so its integral first homology is free of rank \(2N-N+1=N+1\). The universal coefficient theorem identifies \(H^1(G;\mathbb Z)\) with \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)^\vee\) equivariantly.

**2. The face–winding extension.** Embed \(G\) as the one-skeleton of the standard \(m\)-by-\(n\) square cellulation of the oriented torus \(S=\mathbb R^2/(m\mathbb Z\oplus n\mathbb Z)\). Orient every face consistently with the ambient torus and every horizontal and vertical edge in the positive coordinate directions. The cellular chain inclusion and the identities \(H_2(S;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), \(H_1(S;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2\), \(C_2(S;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[F]\) yield the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{1\mapsto\sum F}
\mathbb Z[F]\xrightarrow{\partial_2}H_1(G;\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every \(g\in\Gamma\) acts on oriented face chains by a permutation of faces times the constant orientation sign \(\varepsilon(g)=\det(A_g)\); its induced action on the torus first homology is \(\rho_{\mathbb Z}(g)=A_g\). Thus the image of \(\partial_2\) is the \(\Gamma\)-module \(B=\varepsilon\otimes(\mathbb Z[F]/\mathbb Z\sum F)\), giving
\[
0\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow H_1(G;\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\pi}\rho_{\mathbb Z}\longrightarrow0.
\]
All underlying abelian groups are free. Taking integral duals and then twisting by \(\operatorname{sgn}_V\), followed by the equivariant duality in step 1, gives the asserted extension on the cut complex.

**3. Its exact integral order.** Under \(T=C_m\times C_n\), the oriented horizontal edges are one free transitive orbit and the oriented vertical edges are a second free transitive orbit. Hence every \(T\)-invariant integral one-chain is uniquely of the form \(aX+bY\), where \(X\) is the sum of all positively oriented horizontal edges and \(Y\) is the sum of all positively oriented vertical edges. Their boundaries vanish, and these are therefore all invariant elements of \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\). The winding map satisfies
\[
\pi(X)=n(1,0),\qquad \pi(Y)=m(0,1).
\]
Thus the image of \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)^T\) in \((\mathbb Z^2)^T\) is exactly \(n\mathbb Z\oplus m\mathbb Z\). The pullback of the face–winding extension along multiplication by \(q\) on its first rank-one quotient splits \(T\)-equivariantly if and only if \(q(1,0)\) has an invariant integral lift; this holds precisely when \(n\mid q\). The analogous second extension has exact order \(m\). By additivity of \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z[T]}(\mathbb Z^2,B)\) in its first variable, the order of the combined class is therefore \(\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\). Integral duality is an exact contravariant additive equivalence on the category of finite-rank free integral \(T\)-lattices, so the dual extension has the same order; tensoring with the one-dimensional vertex-sign lattice does not change it.

**4. Rational character.** Maschke's theorem splits the sequence over \(\mathbb Q\). Since \(B_{\mathbb Q}\) is a permutation-representation quotient twisted by a real one-dimensional sign, and \(\rho_{\mathbb Q}\) is a signed-permutation representation, both are self-dual over \(\mathbb Q\). Consequently the rational cut-homology module is the stated sum. On \(\mathbb Q[F]\), the trace of \(g\) is \(f(g)\); removing its trivial line gives \(f(g)-1\), and the orientation twist multiplies the trace by \(\det(A_g)\). The second summand has trace \(\operatorname{tr}(A_g)\), and Alexander duality supplies \(\operatorname{sgn}_V(g)\). This proves the formula for every element. A nonidentity translation fixes no square, has \(A_g=I\), and hence has graph-cycle trace \(-1+2=1\), as claimed.

## Verification
The standard-library Python program `verify_torus.py` constructs the graph and signed oriented-edge action directly for six toroidal grids, including square grids where coordinate interchange is allowed. For each of 912 natural symmetry elements, it checks the cellular formula \(\chi_{H_1(G)}(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1(G))-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0(G))+1\) against \(\det(A_g)(f(g)-1)+\operatorname{tr}(A_g)\). Separately, for all 208 elements on grids \(4\times4\) and \(4\times5\), it enumerates invariant vertex-orbit unions in the actual total two-cut complex and compares their signed reduced Lefschetz numbers to the duality prediction, including the vertex-permutation sign. It computes the translation edge orbits and their winding indices. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK group_character_checks 912 direct_cut_checks 208`. These are finite corroborations; the uniform integral extension-order statement is proved in step 3 rather than by enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced total cut complexes and studied several graph families (arXiv:2209.13503, first posted 2022-09-27). Carnero Bravo's *Total cut complexes and their duals* (arXiv:2602.21427, first posted 2026-02-24) studies powers of cycles, complete multipartite graphs, and Cartesian products of paths and complete graphs; it motivates considering a genuinely periodic Cartesian product. Chandrakar, Hazra, Rout and Singh's *Topology of total cut and cut complexes of grid graphs* (arXiv:2408.07646, first posted 2024-08-14) concerns rectangular path grids. The result here isolates the toroidal face–winding extension and computes its integral equivariant order, which is not supplied by nonequivariant sphere counts or the general abstract duality alone.

## Limitations
The integral nonsplitting assertion is for the translation subgroup \(T\); the rational character is for the stated natural group \(\Gamma\). It does not classify arbitrary graph automorphisms outside that subgroup or provide an equivariant homotopy decomposition. It uses \(m,n\ge4\); triangular factors have a different clique complex and are excluded. The equality of characters does not imply an integral direct-sum decomposition—in fact the explicit extension is nonsplit. Existing unindexed or inaccessible literature might contain an equivalent lattice-extension calculation.
