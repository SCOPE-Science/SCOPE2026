# The integral homotopy type of the six-cube graph Čech complex at scale three
## Finding
For the six-dimensional binary hypercube graph \(Q_6\), with every edge assigned length one, let \(\mathcal N(Q_6,3)\) be the nerve of the closed radius-\(3/2\) balls centered at all \(64\) graph vertices, with distances measured in the geometric realization of the graph. Then
\[
\mathcal N(Q_6,3)\simeq\bigvee^{49}S^3\ \vee\ \bigvee^{480}S^4.
\]
Consequently its reduced integral homology is free of rank \(49\) in degree \(3\), free of rank \(480\) in degree \(4\), and zero in every other degree. In particular, the two undetermined multiplicities in the six-dimensional, scale-three entry of the hypercube graph Čech table are determined.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(Q_6\) are the \(64\) six-bit words, represented by integers \(0\) through \(63\). Words are joined by an edge precisely when their binary representations differ in one bit. The graph's geometric realization is used for distances; midpoint intersections of radius-\(3/2\) balls are included. By the odd-scale graph Čech facet description of Adams, Shukla and Singh, the nerve is generated by
\[
F_{v,w}=N_1[v]\cup N_1[w],\qquad vw\in E(Q_6),
\]
where \(N_1[v]\) is the closed radius-one vertex neighborhood. This is not the flag/Vietoris–Rips complex. All homology groups are over \(\mathbb Z\) unless expressly stated otherwise.

## Proof
**Exact finite complex.** There are \(192\) graph edges. Each set \(F_{v,w}\) contains exactly \(12\) vertices and determines an \(11\)-simplex. Reconstructing every nonempty subset of every generating simplex, taking duplicates only once, gives exactly \(626000\) simplices. The complete face vector in dimensions \(0\) through \(11\) is
\[
(64,1312,11840,49520,111744,155008,145024,94080,42240,12672,2304,192).
\]
Its Euler characteristic is \(432\), consistent with the claimed wedge's Euler characteristic.

**A complete acyclic matching.** Process vertex labels \(v=0,1,\ldots,63\) in order. At stage \(v\), for every currently unmatched nonempty face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\), pair \(\sigma\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) if both are present, and delete the two matched faces from the active set. All pairs at a stage are disjoint. This deterministic rule produces exactly \(312686\) matched pairs, leaving precisely one critical vertex, \(98\) critical tetrahedra (dimension \(3\)), and \(529\) critical four-simplices (dimension \(4\)). The accompanying verifier proves acyclicity without relying on a heuristic: for each of the eleven adjacent-dimensional matching layers, it constructs the directed graph of possible gradient paths between matched lower faces and topologically sorts every vertex of that graph. All eleven directed graphs are acyclic. Therefore Forman's discrete Morse theorem supplies a CW complex with precisely those critical-cell counts.

**The integral Morse differential.** Orient each simplex by increasing binary-word labels. For every matched pair \(\sigma\subset\tau\) of dimensions \(3\) and \(4\), eliminate the unit incidence \([\tau:\sigma]\in\{+1,-1\}\) in the ordinary simplicial boundary. Explicitly, replace a matched lower three-face by the signed sum of the other three-faces of its matched upper four-face, and repeat until only critical three-faces remain. A three-face paired down with a two-face contributes zero to the critical degree-three boundary. The resulting integral Morse boundary \(\partial_4^{\mathrm M}\) is a \(98\)-by-\(529\) integer matrix with \(294\) nonzero entries, each \(+1\) or \(-1\). Its exact Smith normal form contains \(49\) diagonal entries equal to \(1\), with all others zero. All claims in this paragraph are rebuilt and checked from the original facets by the standalone program `verify_q6_cech.py`.

The Morse complex has no critical cells in degrees \(1\) or \(2\), and no critical cells above degree \(4\). Its only positive-dimensional differential is the displayed \(\partial_4^{\mathrm M}\). Hence
\[
\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{98-49}=\mathbb Z^{49},\qquad
\widetilde H_4\cong\mathbb Z^{529-49}=\mathbb Z^{480}.
\]
There is no integral torsion because the nonzero Smith invariants are units.

**From integral homology to homotopy type.** The acyclic matching gives a CW model with one zero-cell, no cells in degrees \(1\) or \(2\), and cells only in degrees \(3\) and \(4\). Its three-skeleton is a wedge of \(98\) three-spheres. By the Hurewicz theorem, its four-cell attaching maps are classified by their degree vectors in the free group \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{98}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{98}\), exactly the columns of \(\partial_4^{\mathrm M}\). Integral changes of bases realizing the Smith normal form correspond to homotopy equivalences of the three-skeleton and elementary rearrangements of four-cell attachments. Cancelling the \(49\) unit degree attachments leaves \(49\) three-spheres and \(480\) trivially attached four-cells, giving the stated homotopy equivalence.

## Verification
Run `python verify_q6_cech.py` using Python 3.10+ and SymPy. The script reconstructs all graph-edge-generated facets and all their faces, performs every matching operation, independently checks each gradient-path digraph for cycles, constructs the oriented integral Morse boundary, computes its full Smith normal form, and checks its mod-two rank independently. A complete successful replay produces the displayed critical counts, \(49\) unit Smith factors, and `OK` lines. The proof uses a finite exhaustive computation; it asserts nothing about other cube dimensions from that experiment.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] define these intrinsic graph Čech complexes, give the odd-scale facet description used here, prove vanishing of homology outside degrees \(3,4\) at scale three, and prove a dimension-four homotopy model. Their Table 1 computes the scale-three integral Betti numbers for dimensions \(4\) and \(5\), but leaves dimension \(6\) blank. Those results do not determine either multiplicity or the six-dimensional integral homotopy type. Feng's hypercube theorem [2] studies the **Vietoris–Rips** complex rather than the graph Čech nerve; its scale-three formula concerns a different simplicial complex and does not imply this finding.

## Limitations
The result is an exact statement for the single natural parameter pair consisting of cube dimension \(6\) and Čech scale \(3\). It does not establish a general formula in cube dimension, does not compute maps between Čech filtration stages, and does not classify Čech complexes at other scales. The computation is exhaustive but finite; the sphere-wedge conclusion uses a separate homotopy-theoretic argument and does not extrapolate experimentally.
