# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Writing \(f(t)=t/(1+t)\), the definition of \(J_2\) is exactly the supremum of \(f(\lVert x+y\rVert)\) over unit isosceles-orthogonal pairs. The published isosceles representation identifies the supremum of the untransformed quantity with \(J(X)\). Monotonicity and continuity of \(f\) therefore give \(J_2(X)=f(J(X))\), without assuming attainment. Independently applying the same monotone-transform argument to the definition of \(J_1\) yields \(J_1(X)=f(J(X))\), so the claimed universal coincidence follows.

## Originality
PASS. The defining 2022 paper was inspected at the \(J_2\) definition, its Hilbert computation, and Theorem 8. It proves only \(J(X)/3\le J_2(X)\le J(X)/2\) and treats \(J_1\) separately; it does not state the exact all-space identity or \(J_1=J_2\). A later full-text isosceles-profile paper states the needed James representation but does not, in the inspected material, formulate the collapse of the 2022 \(J_2\) invariant. Exact-formula, equality, alias, and implication searches did not locate a covering record.

## Value
PASS. This collapses two separately introduced James-type constants to the same classical one-parameter datum. The conclusion is not merely an exact value on a special space: it shows that imposing isosceles orthogonality before applying the metric transform does not change the resulting supremal invariant at all. Consequently every exact value, threshold, and comparison that depends only on \(J_1\) transfers verbatim to \(J_2\), and the source's two-sided \(J_2\) estimate can be replaced by equality.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest literature is Chen--Yang--Liu--Li, *Symmetry* 14 (2022), 405, especially Section 3 and Theorem 8, together with later literature explicitly recording the isosceles representation of the James constant. Residual risk is an unindexed observation of the same short consequence. The result does not claim pointwise equality of the two defining objective functions.
