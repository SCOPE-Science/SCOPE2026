# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the published exact formula \(C_{k,\ell}=a(A(r)-B(r)\log r)\) with \(a=\min\{k,\ell\}\) and \(r=\max\{k,\ell\}/a\). Equality of two coefficients gives a rational linear relation between logarithms of positive rationals and a rational constant. Hermite--Lindemann forces the constant term to vanish. The remaining two equalities reduce to equality of \(x/\sinh x\) at two positive arguments; strict monotonicity gives equality of the ratios and then equality of the scales. Every quantifier and endpoint used in the claim is covered.

## Originality
PASS. The focal paper proves recovery from the pair consisting of the first strict descent and \(C_{k,\ell}\), and explicitly states that it does not assert that either datum alone is sufficient. Its preceding exact-sequence paper proves recovery from the complete invariant sequence. Searches for leading-coefficient-only recovery, logarithmic-coefficient-only recovery, injectivity of the displayed coefficient formula, and equivalent asymptotic-rigidity formulations found no covering statement. The present theorem removes all auxiliary discrete or second-order data.

## Value
PASS. The leading tail coefficient is the most compressed asymptotic datum in the focal analysis. Showing that this single real scalar already encodes both integer exponents sharpens the source's rigidity theorem to a minimal-data form and identifies a non-obvious arithmetic role for the logarithmic term in the coefficient. This is structurally useful for classification, although no numerical stability claim is made.

The closest limitation is practical rather than mathematical: exact injectivity need not imply robust recovery from finite-precision estimates.
