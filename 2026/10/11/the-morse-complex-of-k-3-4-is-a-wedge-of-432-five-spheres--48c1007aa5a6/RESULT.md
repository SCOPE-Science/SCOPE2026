# The Morse complex of \(K_{3,4}\) is a wedge of 432 five-spheres
## Finding
Let \(G=K_{3,4}\) be the simple complete bipartite graph on vertex parts of sizes three and four. Its **ordinary discrete Morse complex**—the simplicial complex of all acyclic discrete vector fields on the one-dimensional simplicial complex \(G\)—satisfies
\[
\mathcal M(G)\simeq\bigvee^{432}S^5.
\]
Thus the only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_5(\mathcal M(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{432}\). All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. This is a homotopy classification, not merely a Betti-number computation.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has labeled vertices \(\{0,1,2\}\sqcup\{3,4,5,6\}\), with every edge joining the two parts and no other edges. A vertex of \(\mathcal M(G)\) is an incidence pair \((u,\{u,v\})\), represented by the arc \(u\to v\). A finite set of these 24 possible arcs forms a simplex precisely if no vertex is the tail of two arcs and the selected directed graph has no directed cycle. The latter includes forbidding both directions of the same edge. This is the usual Morse-complex condition that each cell occurs in at most one incidence pair and no closed gradient path exists. For a graph, these simplices are exactly its rooted spanning forests, with edges pointing toward roots (Chari–Joswig, Proposition 3.1).

Each face is coded by a 24-bit integer whose bit positions are ordered first by tail \(u\in\{0,\ldots,6\}\) and then by increasing neighbor \(v\). The scope is this fixed labeled graph and its ordinary nonempty-face Morse complex; this is **not** the two-matching complex on twelve edges, an algebraic Morse chain complex, or the generalized Morse complex allowing cyclic vector fields.

## Proof
**Exact face census.** Each vertex has either no outgoing arc or one outgoing arc. Enumerate the \(5^3 4^4=32,000\) choices of these seven outgoing states and reject exactly those in which following outgoing arcs revisits a vertex. The surviving face set is downward closed and has 12,800 elements, including the empty face. Its nonempty face vector (dimensions zero through five) is
\[
(24,234,1192,3357,4968,3024).
\]
No face has dimension greater than five because a rooted forest on seven vertices uses at most six edges. The enumeration's reduced Euler characteristic is \(-432\). An independent matrix–forest check uses the graph-Laplacian eigenvalues \(0,3,3,3,4,4,7\), obtaining \(\det(I-L_G)=(-2)^3(-3)^2(-6)=432=-\widetilde\chi(\mathcal M(G))\). This numerical consistency check does not by itself establish homotopy type.

**Exhaustive acyclic matching.** Start with the augmented face set, including the empty face. For each of the 24 arc bits in the fixed order, simultaneously pair each still-available face \(F\) not containing that bit with \(F\cup\{e\}\) when the larger face also remains available, and delete both faces before the next bit. The resulting 5,887 disjoint matched cover relations and 1,026 unmatched faces partition all 12,800 faces. The unmatched faces consist of exactly 297 dimension-four faces and 729 dimension-five faces. The empty face is matched to a zero-face; remove this one pair to obtain an ordinary discrete Morse matching with 5,886 pairs, one critical vertex, 297 critical four-faces and 729 critical five-faces.

For the acyclicity certificate, orient each Hasse cover downward unless it is matched, in which case orient it upward. An exhaustive topological sort visits all 12,800 augmented faces on 60,480 cover incidences, so there is no directed cycle. Removing the empty-face pair preserves acyclicity on the ordinary face poset. Forman's theorem gives a CW model with one zero-cell, 297 four-cells and 729 five-cells, and no cells in dimensions one through three.

**Integral attaching matrix.** Orient each simplex by its increasing arc-bit order. Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_{297}\) denote the critical four-cells. Define the projection \(P(F)\) of a four-face to the free abelian group generated by these cells as follows. A critical face maps to its basis vector; a face paired downward to dimension three maps to zero. If a four-face \(F\) is paired upward with a five-face \(U\), write \(\epsilon(F,U)=\pm1\) for its simplicial incidence sign and define recursively
\[
P(F)=-\epsilon(F,U)\sum_{F'\prec U,\ F'\ne F}\epsilon(F',U)P(F').
\]
The verified absence of directed cycles makes this recursion well founded. For each critical five-face \(T\), compute its Morse boundary as \(\sum_{F\prec T}\epsilon(F,T)P(F)\). This constructs the **integer**, not mod-two, 297-by-729 cellular boundary matrix. It has 4,162 nonzero entries, each initially \(1\) or \(-1\).

Integer row and column elimination repeatedly selects a \(\pm1\) entry and clears its column using unimodular integer row operations, then removes the pivot row and column. All 297 rows are eliminated by **297 unit pivots**, and no nonzero remainder survives; the largest absolute intermediate entry is two. Consequently, the matrix is equivalent over \(\mathbb Z\) to a block with 297 unit diagonal entries and 432 zero columns. Because the four-skeleton is a wedge of 297 four-spheres, the attaching maps of its five-cells are classified by their degrees in \(\pi_4(\bigvee^{297}S^4)\cong\mathbb Z^{297}\). Integral changes of basis correspond to homotopy-equivalent changes of these attaching maps; the 297 unit pairs cancel, leaving exactly 432 five-cells attached to a point. Therefore the CW model, and hence the original Morse complex, is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{432}S^5\).

## Verification
Two complete Python 3 standard-library programs are included: `verify_k34.py` and `verify_k34_mod2.py`. From the package directory, run `python3 verify_k34.py` and `python3 verify_k34_mod2.py`. The first independently reconstructs the graph and complete simplicial face set, checks the fixed element matching on all faces and all directed cover incidences, computes the integral Morse boundary by signed gradient paths, and performs full integer unit-pivot elimination. It exits with `VERIFY_OK` after checking all counts above. The second enumerates all faces by a separately written method, calculates every simplicial mod-two boundary rank, and checks the matrix–forest Euler value; it exits with `MOD2_OK`. Its ranks for the augmented dimensions zero through five are \((1,23,211,981,2376,2592)\), yielding mod-two reduced degree-five rank 432 and no other reduced homology. These finite checks are exhaustive for the **defined 24-vertex simplicial complex**, not a proof for an unspecified infinite graph family.

## Relationship to prior work
Chari and Joswig (2000/2005), Proposition 3.1, identify graph Morse complexes with rooted forest complexes and derive their face enumerations from Laplacians, but do not supply this complete-bipartite homotopy calculation. Scoville and Zaremsky (arXiv:2004.10481, Example 4.6) explicitly describe complete-bipartite graph Morse homotopy types as unknown to them and prove connectivity estimates; even the stronger general connectivity theorem does not imply an exact wedge or its multiplicity. Jojić (arXiv:1109.4475) proves shellability with a complete source and vertex decomposability for essentially-tree digraphs. The bidirected \(K_{3,4}\) has neither a complete source nor an underlying tree, so those cited hypotheses do not apply; the complete bidirected graph covered in the same paper is a different object.

Published related computations establish \(\mathcal M(K_{3,3})\simeq\bigvee^{80}S^4\) and \(\mathcal M(K_{2,n})\simeq\bigvee^{n^2-1}S^n\), but neither statement includes a graph with bipartition \((3,4)\). A computation of the two-matching complex of \(K_{3,4}\) is also different: it places a degree bound on subsets of undirected graph edges, not acyclic incidence pairs. These comparisons separate the present claim from stronger-family implications, not merely title matches. The topological method lies in the combinatorial simple-homotopy tradition exemplified by Kozlov's work (arXiv:math/0503613, primary MSC 57Q10), without claiming that that paper contains this graph's answer.

## Limitations
This theorem covers only the ordinary Morse complex for \(K_{3,4}\). It gives neither a classification for all \(K_{p,q}\) nor information about other higher-matching or generalized Morse complexes. The argument uses an exact finite computational acyclic-matching certificate; the included programs make every critical enumeration and integer reduction independently reproducible, but no formal proof-assistant certificate is asserted. Exhaustive comparison with all possibly unindexed publications is not possible; an independently discovered equivalent result remains a residual literature risk.
