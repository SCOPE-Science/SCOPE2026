# The neighborhood complexes of odd folded hypercubes are wedges of two-spheres

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(FQ_n\) be the simple graph whose vertices are antipodal pairs \([x]=\{x,x+\mathbf 1\}\) of binary words \(x\in\mathbb F_2^n\), with two distinct pairs adjacent when some representatives differ in exactly one coordinate. Let \(\mathcal N(G)\) denote the **open** Lovász neighborhood complex: a nonempty finite set \(\sigma\subseteq V(G)\) is a face precisely when all its vertices have a common neighbor in \(G\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N(FQ_n)\simeq\bigvee^{\,2^{n-3}(n^2-5n+8)-1}S^2.}
\]
In particular, for \(n=3,5,7,9\) the sphere multiplicities are respectively \(1,31,351,2815\). The complex initially has dimension \(n-1\), but admits a sequence of elementary simplicial collapses to dimension two. Its reduced integral homology is free and concentrated in degree two, with the displayed rank.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Throughout the proof, \(n\ge3\) is an **odd integer**, and \(\mathbf1\) denotes the all-one vector. The antipodal quotient contains \(2^{n-1}\) vertices, each of degree \(n\). Two vertices of a neighborhood complex are adjacent if and only if they have a common graph neighbor (equivalently, they are joined by a graph walk of length two). Homotopy equivalences concern ordinary geometric realizations and integral homotopy types, not merely homology over a field. No claim is made for even \(n\).

## Proof
**1. Common-neighbor rigidity.** Let \([x]\ne[y]\) be vertices of \(FQ_n\). Every length-two walk from \([x]\) to \([y]\) lifts, starting at \(x\), to a two-step walk of the ordinary cube \(Q_n\), ending at either \(y\) or \(y+\mathbf1\). These possible endpoints have respective Hamming distances \(d\) and \(n-d\) from \(x\). Since \(n\) is odd, they cannot both have distance two. If neither has distance two, the quotient vertices have no common graph neighbor. If one has distance two, there are exactly two intermediate cube vertices (the two orders of changing the differing coordinates). Their antipodal classes are distinct and give exactly two common neighbors. Thus every two distinct vertices of \(FQ_n\) have either zero or two common neighbors. In particular, two distinct open neighborhoods intersect in at most two vertices. As each neighborhood has size \(n\ge3\), the \(2^{n-1}\) open neighborhoods are distinct maximal faces of \(\mathcal N(FQ_n)\), and any face with at least three vertices occurs in exactly one maximal face.

**2. An explicit two-dimensional elementary collapse.** For each maximal face \(F=N(v)\), choose one distinguished vertex \(t_F\in F\). For \(k=n-1,n-2,\ldots,3\) in this order, and for each \(k\)-element subset \(\sigma\subset F\setminus\{t_F\}\), delete the face \(\sigma\) together with its coface \(\sigma\cup\{t_F\}\). These are valid elementary collapses: at that stage every proper coface of \(\sigma\), other than \(\sigma\cup\{t_F\}\), has already been deleted at a larger cardinality, and a face of size at least three cannot occur in another maximal neighborhood. (For \(n=3\) there are no such pairs.) After all these collapses every surviving face has cardinality at most three. Consequently \(\mathcal N(FQ_n)\) is homotopy equivalent to a finite two-dimensional simplicial complex.

**3. Simple connectivity.** Let \(Y_n\) be the cubical two-skeleton of the standard \(n\)-dimensional cube, including all vertices, edges, and square two-faces. It is simply connected: attaching the cube's cells of dimension three and higher does not affect its fundamental group, while the whole cube is contractible. The coordinatewise antipodal involution of the cube preserves \(Y_n\) and acts freely on its vertices, edges, and two-faces when \(n\ge3\). The quotient \(Y_n/\langle\mathbf1\rangle\) is a two-sheeted CW covering of a complex with one-skeleton \(FQ_n\); its two-cells project to the embedded four-cycles of \(FQ_n\). Indeed, an embedded four-cycle in \(FQ_n\) lifts to a four-step cube path; its endpoint cannot be antipodal to its starting point because four has even parity but \(n\) is odd. It is therefore a closed four-cycle in the cube and bounds a cubical square. Thus the quotient has the same fundamental group as the graph square-complex \(X_2(FQ_n)\), formed by filling its four-cycles. (Any degenerate length-four closed walks used in an alternate construction attach null-homotopic cells and do not change this fundamental group.) Since \(Y_n\) is simply connected, covering-space theory gives \(\pi_1(X_2(FQ_n))\cong\mathbb Z/2\).

A projected length-\(n\) cube path from \(x\) to \(x+\mathbf1\) represents the nontrivial deck transformation. Its length is odd, so the **even-length subgroup** of \(\pi_1(X_2(FQ_n))\) is trivial. By Matsushita's theorem identifying \(\pi_1(\mathcal N(G))\) with the even-length subgroup of the two-fundamental group \(\pi_1(X_2(G))\) for a loopless graph without isolated basepoint, \(\pi_1(\mathcal N(FQ_n))=0\). The neighborhood complex is connected: \(FQ_n\) is connected and has the odd cycle obtained by projecting an antipodal length-\(n\) cube path; hence any two graph vertices are connected by an even-length walk, which is a path in the one-skeleton of the neighborhood complex.

**4. Counting the spheres.** Write \(N=2^{n-1}\). The number of zero-faces is \(f_0=N\). Each edge of the neighborhood complex occurs in exactly two maximal faces, by Step 1, so \(f_1=N\binom n2/2\). For every \(3\le k\le n\), each \((k-1)\)-face belongs to a unique maximal neighborhood; hence \(f_{k-1}=N\binom nk\). Summing alternating face numbers gives
\[
\chi(\mathcal N(FQ_n))
=N\left(2-n+\frac12\binom n2\right)
=2^{n-3}(n^2-5n+8).
\]
By Steps 2 and 3, the complex is connected, simply connected, and homotopy equivalent to a two-dimensional CW complex. Its sole possibly nonzero reduced homology is then \(\widetilde H_2\), which is free abelian of rank \(\chi-1\). The Hurewicz isomorphism gives a map from a wedge of that many two-spheres inducing an integral homology equivalence. Since both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, the homology Whitehead theorem upgrades that map to a homotopy equivalence. This establishes the formula for every odd \(n\ge3\).

## Verification
The standalone program `verify.py` constructs \(FQ_n\) for \(n=3,5,7,9\), enumerates every simplex of the neighborhood complex, checks all pairwise common-neighbor intersections and the exact face-number formula, and verifies the sphere multiplicities \(1,31,351,2815\). For \(n=3,5,7\) it also replays the stated elementary collapses, checking that each deleted face has exactly one surviving proper coface. A second independently constructed quotient-cube program `crosscheck.py` checks the graph representation, common-neighbor distributions and four-cycle counts for \(n=3,5,7\). Both programs run using standard-library Python, with commands `python verify.py` and `python crosscheck.py`. The reproducible script outputs summarize the finite checks specified here. These finite checks corroborate but do not replace the uniform proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Lovász introduced neighborhood complexes as chromatic obstructions; their topology is generally difficult to determine. Matsushita's fundamental-group theorem supplies the link between graph square-complexes and neighborhood complexes, but does not give an integral homotopy classification of odd folded hypercubes. Shukla's explicit classifications concern graphs of maximal degree at most three and certain four-regular circulants; they do not cover the degree-\(n\) members of this family for odd \(n\ge5\). The classical Clebsch graph is the \(n=5\) member. General results for ordinary hypercube neighborhood complexes concern bipartite graphs and do not imply the connected odd-folded-quotient classification. The source on projective cubes supplies equivalent graph definitions, not a neighborhood-complex homotopy calculation. The sphere-wedge formula is compatible with, and makes sharp, the Lovász chromatic bound for this four-chromatic graph family.

## Limitations
The parity argument, the two-step common-neighbor property, and the covering-space proof all use odd \(n\); even-dimensional folded cubes are outside the theorem. The main claim is a proved homotopy equivalence, not a claim of homeomorphism or an equivariant splitting under graph automorphisms. Numerical verification addresses only the four specified finite dimensions; the proof for all odd dimensions is theoretical. An equivalent result might occur in literature not retrieved by the cited searches, and independent scientific validation has not been performed.
