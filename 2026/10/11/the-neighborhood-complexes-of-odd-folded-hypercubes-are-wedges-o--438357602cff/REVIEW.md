# Review of the odd folded-hypercube neighborhood-complex theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The proof makes the odd-dimension assumption explicit, derives the exact common-neighbor multiplicity by lifting two-step walks, and gives a free-face elementary collapse valid uniformly for all odd dimensions. The square-filled graph complex is the antipodal quotient of the simply connected cubical two-skeleton, with fundamental group \(\mathbb Z/2\); an odd deck-generator loop makes its even subgroup trivial. Matsushita's published identification then gives simple connectivity. The exact simplex counts and the homology Whitehead theorem yield the claimed *integral homotopy* wedge, not only field Betti numbers. Two distinct programs verify finite structural checks at \(\(\(n=3,5,7\),9\)\) and quotient-graph checks at \(\(n=3,5,7\)\); the uniform proof, rather than computation, covers all odd dimensions.

## Originality
PASS, with residual bibliographic risk. Matsushita's original theorem (arXiv:1210.2803, journal Theorems 1.1 and 3.1) computes fundamental groups in terms of graph loops, but not the homotopy type or the sphere count for this family. Shukla (arXiv:1802.04526) classifies degree-at-most-three and specific four-regular circulant families, excluding folded cubes of degree at least five. Chen–Naserasr–Sarti (arXiv:2406.10814) treat projective-cube definitions and homomorphism questions rather than neighborhood-complex homotopy. Published results on ordinary bipartite hypercubes, folded-cube independence complexes, and separate finite symmetric graphs do not imply the connected odd-quotient theorem. No matching statement was found in targeted literature and research-record comparisons, though equivalence in unindexed literature cannot be ruled out.

## Value
PASS. The result resolves the full integral homotopy type of a natural infinite, highly symmetric four-chromatic graph family. The original simplicial complexes have dimension growing with \(n\), yet collapse to dimension two; the formula supplies a closed integer sphere multiplicity and sharpens the interpretation of the Lovász chromatic obstruction. It unifies the Clebsch-graph instance with arbitrarily high-dimensional folded cubes and supplies a reusable square-complex covering argument. No equivariant claim is made.

## Limitations
The odd-parity hypothesis is essential to the proof. The finite scripts are checks, not exhaustive verification over all dimensions. No independent scientific audit has been performed; bibliographic priority remains subject to further literature verification.
