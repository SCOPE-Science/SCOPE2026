# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The graph and ordinary \(3\)-cut complex are defined explicitly. The verifier enumerates all \(364\) triples, finds \(42\) connected induced paths and \(322\) disconnected triples, then enumerates all \(16{,}235\) nonempty faces. The stated sequential element matching contains \(8{,}099\) disjoint cover pairs, leaves one critical \(0\)-cell and \(36\) critical \(10\)-cells, and its complete oriented Hasse diagram on \(112{,}924\) cover relations is acyclic. This proves the wedge decomposition by discrete Morse theory. Exact mod-\(2\) boundary ranks independently give reduced Betti number \(36\) only in dimension \(10\).

## Originality
**PASS.** The primary cut-complex paper gives the definition, a codimension-one-skeleton criterion, an Euler-characteristic formula, and a top-two-degree homology bound. For the Heawood graph those general results predict the signed Euler characteristic of magnitude \(36\), but they do not decide the possible degree-\(9\) group and do not state the Heawood homotopy type. Full-text inspection found no Heawood case. The companion total-cut paper treats a different subcomplex, defined using independent triples, and likewise does not state this ordinary-cut result. Exact and alias searches found the closest related records to concern the Heawood **total** \(3\)-cut complex or other graph complexes, not the ordinary \(\Delta_3(H)\) claim.

## Value
**PASS.** The Heawood graph is the smallest incidence graph of a projective plane and a standard symmetric cubic graph. It lies naturally just beyond the explicit families computed in the foundational ordinary-cut paper. The result sharpens that paper's general top-two-degree homology bound to a complete homotopy type in a canonical girth-six example, and it cleanly separates the topology of the ordinary and total cut constructions on the same graph. The fact is exact, self-contained, reproducible, and mathematically motivated rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs*, arXiv:2304.13675, especially Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2. The closest constructional neighbour is Bayer et al., *Total Cut Complexes of Graphs*, arXiv:2209.13503, which explicitly distinguishes total and ordinary cut complexes. Residual risk is limited to an unindexed or unpublished duplicate; no claim of exhaustive bibliographic uniqueness is made.
