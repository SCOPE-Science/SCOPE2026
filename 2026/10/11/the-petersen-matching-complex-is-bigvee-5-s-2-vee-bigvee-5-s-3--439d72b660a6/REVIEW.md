# Same-model review

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is the exact homotopy type of the ordinary matching complex of the Petersen graph. The proof was replayed from the graph definition and all 331 nonempty matching faces. The 160-pair discrete-Morse matching is acyclic on all 975 Hasse covers and leaves exactly one critical \(0\)-cell, five critical \(2\)-cells, and five critical \(3\)-cells. Exact integral gradient-path cancellation gives a zero Morse boundary from dimension \(3\) to dimension \(2\). Since the resulting \(2\)-skeleton is \(\bigvee^5S^2\), Hurewicz converts these zero boundary-coordinate vectors into null attaching classes. An independent full mod-\(2\) chain computation yields reduced Betti ranks five in degrees \(2\) and \(3\) and zero elsewhere.

## Originality — PASS
The closest literature and indexed findings were compared by implication, not by title alone. The equivalent formulation \(\operatorname{Match}(G)=\operatorname{Ind}(L(G))\) was searched. Donovan–Scoville covers paths, cycles, Dutch windmills, and related matching/Morse constructions but not the Petersen ordinary matching complex; Jelić Milutinović et al. studies trees and polygonal-line/honeycomb families; Barmak's claw-free theorem gives only connectivity. The closest indexed Petersen item concerns the generalized \(2\)-matching complex, a different object. No located source implies the claimed mixed wedge. Residual risk remains for an unindexed or differently phrased finite computation.

## Value — PASS
The Petersen graph is a canonical small cubic symmetric graph, so its ordinary matching complex is a natural exact test case rather than an arbitrary slice. The result exhibits a mixed-dimensional wedge \(\bigvee^5S^2\vee\bigvee^5S^3\), and the zero integral attaching matrix supplies information strictly beyond face counts or mod-\(2\) homology. This gives a reusable benchmark for methods aimed at exact matching-complex topology.
