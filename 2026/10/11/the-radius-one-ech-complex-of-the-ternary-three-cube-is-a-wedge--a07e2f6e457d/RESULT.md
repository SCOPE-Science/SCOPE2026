# The radius-one Čech complex of the ternary three-cube is a wedge of 134 two-spheres

## Finding

Let \(G=H(3,3)=K_3\square K_3\square K_3\) be the Hamming graph on the 27 ternary triples. Its graph Čech complex at scale \(2\), defined using radius-one closed balls in the geometric realization of \(G\), has exact homotopy type
\[
\mathcal N(G,2)\simeq\bigvee^{134} S^2.
\]
The integral reduced homology is \(\mathbb Z^{134}\) in degree \(2\), and vanishes in every other degree.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has vertex set \(V=\{0,1,2\}^3\). Two vertices are adjacent precisely when they differ in one coordinate. Fix lexicographic ordering of ternary triples, with zero-based labels \(0,\ldots,26\). For every center \(c\), the closed graph-distance-one neighborhood \(B(c)\) contains the center and all vertices differing in one coordinate; it has exactly seven members. The simplicial complex \(K\) is the complex generated by the 27 sets \(B(c)\). By the even-integer Čech description in Adams–Shukla–Singh [1, Lemma 2.2], this is \(\mathcal N(G,2)\) for the shortest-path metric on the geometric realization. All statements concern this graph and this single scale; no continuation in the alphabet size or dimension is asserted.

## Proof

Represent a nonempty simplex \(\sigma\subseteq V\) by the 27-bit integer with bit \(j\) set when vertex \(j\) lies in \(\sigma\). Generate all nonempty subsets of all 27 balls, deduplicating equal subsets. The resulting complex has exactly 2,889 nonempty simplices, with face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(27,243,891,945,567,189,27).
\]
Construct an edge matching on its face poset by iterating vertex indices \(j=0,\ldots,26\). At stage \(j\), for each still-unmatched simplex \(\sigma\) that omits \(j\), match it with \(\sigma\cup\{j\}\) exactly when that coface exists and is still unmatched. Traverse candidate integers in increasing order at each stage. Every pair is a codimension-one face–coface pair, and no simplex participates in two pairs.

For acyclicity, orient every complete Hasse cover \(\tau\subset\sigma\) downward, except matched covers, which are oriented upward. The standalone verifier independently enumerates all 11,097 such covers from the complete face collection and performs Kahn topological sorting on the resulting directed graph. The sort removes all 2,889 vertices, proving it has no directed cycle. The 1,377 pairs leave precisely one critical \(0\)-simplex and 134 critical \(2\)-simplices; there are no other critical cells.

Forman's finite discrete Morse theorem therefore supplies a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with one \(0\)-cell and 134 \(2\)-cells, and no \(1\)-cells or higher cells. Each \(2\)-cell attaches to the single point (the 1-skeleton), so the CW complex is \(\bigvee^{134}S^2\). This proves the integral homology assertion as well.

## Verification

Run `python3 hamming3_checker.py` with Python 3 standard library. The embedded program builds the graph from coordinates, reconstructs every face from the radius-one balls, creates the matching without loading any prior results, enumerates all Hasse incidences, and checks global acyclicity by directed topological sorting. Its actual successful output is in `CHECKS.txt`; 27 seven-element balls, 2,889 faces, 11,097 Hasse covers, 1,377 matching pairs, and the critical-cell vector \((1,0,134,0,0,0,0)\) are checked. Independent Euler counting from the face vector yields \(\chi(K)=135\), consistent with 134 two-spheres. The exhaustive finite enumeration, rather than a sampled computation, certifies this specific finite complex.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] develop this precise intrinsic Čech construction and compute the scale-two Čech homotopy type for binary hypercubes. The ternary three-cube is not a binary hypercube, so that theorem does not determine this answer. Matsushita [2, Example 3.5] determines the closed-neighborhood homotopy type for the two-factor rook graphs \(K_m\square K_n\), whereas the present graph is a three-factor product. Matsushita's general double-cover equivalence [2, Theorem 1.2] supplies a related reduction but not an explicit evaluation of this three-factor complex. These precise comparisons do not constitute a guarantee against other unlocated equivalent results.

## Limitations

This finding settles only the 27-vertex ternary three-cube at scale two. The checker is a finite exhaustive computation, not a proof of an all-dimension or all-alphabet theorem. Neither an equivariant homology decomposition nor the maps across filtration scales are established. Related work could conceivably contain the same computation under a different graph-complex terminology.
