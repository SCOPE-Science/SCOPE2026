# Same-model scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The seven-vertex ball rule follows from the graph metric and the integer-radius Čech lemma in Adams–Shukla–Singh. Reconstructing 27 distinct balls gives 2,889 distinct nonempty simplices. The supplied executable exhaustively checks all 11,097 Hasse covers, verifies 1,377 disjoint matching pairs and acyclicity, and confirms exactly one critical vertex plus 134 critical two-simplices. Forman's CW theorem then proves the wedge type integrally. The Euler characteristic 135 is an independent numerical consistency check; it is not used in place of acyclicity.

**Originality — PASS with residual literature risk.** Adams–Shukla–Singh treat binary hypercubes, which do not contain this 27-vertex ternary Hamming example. Matsushita's Example 3.5 covers two Cartesian factors, whereas this graph has three; Theorem 1.2 reduces a complex to the independence complex of a double cover but does not evaluate the resulting three-factor case. Specific Čech/Hamming/closed-neighborhood aliases and the public result index were checked without finding an exact or dominating evaluation. The lack of a matched indexed result is not by itself a proof of novelty; an unlocated alternative formulation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The first nonbinary three-fold Hamming product is a canonical test case for extending closed-neighborhood results from two factors and binary cubes. Determining its actual homotopy type, not only a mod-prime Betti vector, yields a concrete 134-sphere benchmark for higher Hamming-product Čech complexes. This is not a claim about arbitrary Hamming parameters.
