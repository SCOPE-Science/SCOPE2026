In the genuinely redundant range \(m\ge n+2\), the standard partial permutohedron has exactly the same Euclidean inball as its simplex envelope: the unique center is \(r\mathbf1\) with \(r=n(n+1)/(2(m+\sqrt m))\). Only the coordinate facets and the total-sum facet touch that ball; every proper subset-sum facet stays strictly farther away.
