# The Rips filtration of the complete-graph subdivision has two consecutive sphere wedges
## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(S(K_n)\) be the graph obtained by subdividing every edge of the complete graph \(K_n\) once. Give its vertices the shortest-path metric and use the inclusive convention
\[
\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(X;r)=\{\sigma\subseteq X:\operatorname{diam}(\sigma)\le r\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(S(K_n);1)\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{2}}S^1,
\qquad
\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(S(K_n);2)\simeq\bigvee^{\binom n3}S^2,
\]
and \(\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(S(K_n);3)\) is contractible. Since all graph distances are integral, these are the only positive-dimensional homology changes. Hence, over every coefficient field, positive-dimensional persistence consists exactly of \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) degree-one intervals \([1,2)\) and \(\binom n3\) degree-two intervals \([2,3)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(S(K_n)\) has point vertices \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\) and subdivision vertices \(e_{ij}\) for \(1\le i<j\le n\), with \(p_i\) adjacent to \(e_{ij}\) and \(p_j\) adjacent to \(e_{ij}\). All graph edges have unit length. The statement concerns the vertex set with graph distance, not the geometric realization of the graph as a metric graph. The threshold convention is diameter at most \(r\).

At scale zero the complex is a discrete set of \(n+\binom n2\) points. At scales at least three the complex is already contractible; for \(n\ge4\) the graph diameter is four, so contractibility at scale three is stronger than merely reaching the full simplex.

## Proof
At scale one the Rips complex is the graph \(S(K_n)\) itself, because the graph is triangle-free. It is connected, has \(n+\binom n2\) vertices and \(2\binom n2=n(n-1)\) edges. Therefore its cycle rank is
\[
n(n-1)-n-\binom n2+1=\binom{n-1}{2},
\]
so it is homotopy equivalent to the stated wedge of circles.

For scale two, first record the exact distance relations. Distinct point vertices have distance two. A point \(p_i\) has distance one from \(e_{jk}\) exactly when \(i\in\{j,k\}\), and otherwise distance three. Two subdivision vertices have distance two exactly when their corresponding edges of \(K_n\) meet, and distance four when those edges are disjoint.

Let \(X=\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(S(K_n);2)\). Every clique in the scale-two graph is contained in one of four kinds of simplices:
\[
P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\},
\]
\[
C_i=\{p_i\}\cup\{e_{ij}:j\ne i\},
\]
\[
D_{ij}=\{p_i,p_j,e_{ij}\},
\]
and
\[
T_{ijk}=\{e_{ij},e_{ik},e_{jk}\}
\qquad (i<j<k).
\]
Indeed, a clique with at least three point vertices cannot contain any subdivision vertex; a clique with exactly two point vertices can use only their common subdivision vertex; a clique with one point \(p_i\) can use only subdivision vertices incident with \(i\); and a pairwise-intersecting family of two-element subsets is either contained in one star or is exactly a triangle.

Let \(Z\subseteq X\) be the subcomplex generated by \(P\), all \(C_i\), and all \(D_{ij}\). We show that \(Z\) is contractible. Delete the subdivision vertices \(e_{ij}\) one at a time. At any stage, the link of a remaining \(e_{ij}\) is the union of a simplex with cone point \(p_i\), a simplex with cone point \(p_j\), and the edge \(\{p_i,p_j\}\), with the two simplices otherwise disjoint. This link deformation retracts onto that edge and is therefore contractible. Deleting a vertex with contractible link is a homotopy equivalence. After all \(e_{ij}\) are deleted, only the simplex \(P\) remains. Thus \(Z\) is contractible.

The only simplices of \(X\) not already in \(Z\) are the \(\binom n3\) triangles \(T_{ijk}\). Each is attached along its entire boundary: for example \(\{e_{ij},e_{ik}\}\subset C_i\), and similarly for the other two sides. Distinct such triangles have disjoint interiors. Because \(Z\) is a contractible subcomplex of the simplicial complex \(X\), collapsing \(Z\) to a point is a homotopy equivalence. The quotient is exactly a wedge of one two-sphere for each attached triangle. Hence
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{\binom n3}S^2.
\]

At scale three, every point vertex \(p_i\) is within distance three of every vertex of \(S(K_n)\). Thus \(p_i\) is a universal vertex in the scale-three Rips graph, and its clique complex is a cone. It is therefore contractible. The barcode statement follows immediately from the scale-one, scale-two, and scale-three homotopy types and from integrality of the graph distances.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs \(S(K_n)\) from its incidence definition for \(3\le n\le7\), computes every graph distance by breadth-first search, enumerates all maximal cliques at scale two, expands the exact scale-two flag complex, and computes its reduced mod-two homology by binary Gaussian elimination. It independently checks the distance taxonomy, the scale-one cycle rank, the scale-two maximal-clique census, the rank \(\binom n3\) in degree two, and the universal-vertex cone criterion at scale three.

The replay output is stored in `verification_output.txt`. The finite computation is a stress test of boundary cases and the combinatorial model; the quantified theorem rests on the symbolic proof above, not on extrapolation from those cases.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek and Adams develop Vietoris--Rips complexes as flag complexes of metric threshold graphs and emphasize persistent homology across varying scales. Their work supplies the standard Rips framework used here but studies subsets of the circle, not subdivision graphs of complete graphs. The inspected full text contains no occurrence of “crown,” “complete bipartite,” or “matching,” and targeted searches found no theorem for \(S(K_n)\).

A recent graph-theory paper on independent mutual-visibility coloring treats exactly the subdivision graphs \(S(K_n)\), but for visibility coloring and Ramsey-type partitions. The accessible full text contains no occurrence of “Vietoris” or “Rips.” Thus its exact-object results do not imply the scale-two flag-complex decomposition or the persistence calculation here.

Targeted searches under “subdivision graph,” “incidence graph of the complete graph,” “graph-metric Rips,” “square graph clique complex,” and the two binomial multiplicities found related computations for other graph families but no statement implying this all-parameter filtration.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the once-subdivided complete graph and the shortest-path metric on its vertices. It does not treat iterated subdivisions, weighted subdivisions, the continuous metric realization, or subdivision graphs of arbitrary base graphs. The originality assessment is based on focused semantic and exact-phrase searches plus inspection of the closest accessible full texts; an older result hidden under graph-power or flag-complex terminology remains a residual bibliographic risk.
