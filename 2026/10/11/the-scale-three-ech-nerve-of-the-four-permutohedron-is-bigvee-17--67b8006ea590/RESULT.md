# The scale-three Čech nerve of the four-permutohedron is \(\bigvee^{17}S^2\)

## Finding
Let \(P_4\) be the graph on the 24 permutations of \((1,2,3,4)\), with two permutations adjacent when one is obtained from the other by swapping entries in consecutive positions. Its abstract graph is the one-skeleton of the three-dimensional permutohedron (the truncated octahedron). Equip its geometric realization with unit edge lengths. Let \(\mathcal N(P_4,3)\) be the nerve of closed intrinsic balls of radius \(3/2\) centered at the 24 graph vertices. Then
\[
\mathcal N(P_4,3)\simeq\bigvee^{17}S^2.
\]
In particular its reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{17}\) in degree two and zero in other degrees. The complex is pure of dimension five but is not shellable. For a comparison using the same vertex metric and inclusive threshold three, the graph-metric Vietoris–Rips complex \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le3}(P_4)\) has second Betti number only \(1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), whereas the Čech nerve has second Betti number \(17\).

## Assumptions and scope
The permutations are numbered \(0,\ldots,23\) in lexicographic order. An edge joins permutations that differ by exactly one adjacent-position swap. In the Čech construction, balls are subsets of the *entire unit-edge geometric realization* and not Euclidean balls around an embedding of the polytope. A simplex of the nerve is any finite family of graph vertices whose closed radius-\(3/2\) balls have common intersection. The Vietoris–Rips comparison uses all pairs of graph vertices at shortest-path distance at most \(3\), and it concerns mod-two homology, not a claimed Rips homotopy classification. No generalization to larger symmetric groups or a full persistence barcode is asserted.

## Proof
**Metric-ball reduction.** For any finite simple unit-edge graph \(G\), the graph Čech description at the odd integer scale \(3\) gives a complex generated by
\[
N_G[u]\cup N_G[v],\qquad \{u,v\}\in E(G),
\]
where \(N_G[u]\) is the closed graph-distance-one neighborhood. This follows because intersections of closed radius-\(3/2\) vertex-centered balls can be witnessed at edge midpoints; it is also Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh [1]. The verifier reconstructs \(P_4\) directly from adjacent swaps, obtains exactly \(36\) edges and hence \(36\) distinct generating six-vertex facets. All are maximal, so the Čech nerve is pure of dimension five.

**Exhaustive combinatorics.** Enumerating every nonempty subset of these facets gives exactly \(1230\) simplices with face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(24,168,408,402,192,36).
\]
In `certificate.json`, each simplex is encoded as a 24-bit integer and every proposed matching pair is explicitly listed. The first \(502\) pairs are verified, in their recorded order, to be elementary simplicial collapses: the lower simplex is a proper face of exactly one remaining coface, its paired upper simplex. After these removals, \(226\) simplices remain. The remaining \(104\) matched pairs have codimension one and are disjoint from one another and from the first pairs. Together the \(606\) matched pairs partition all but \(18\) simplices, leaving exactly one critical vertex and \(17\) critical two-simplices.

**Acyclicity and homotopy.** Direct every incidence between a simplex and one of its codimension-one faces downwards except at a matched incidence, which is directed upwards. `verify.py` exhaustively constructs all \(4344\) such incidences and proves that the directed graph is acyclic by deleting vertices in a topological ordering. Thus the matching is an acyclic discrete-Morse matching. Forman's discrete Morse theorem gives a CW complex with one zero-cell and \(17\) two-cells and no one-cells or higher cells. Every two-cell attaches to the single point, proving the integral homotopy equivalence to the displayed wedge of spheres.

**Rips comparison and non-shellability.** Independently, the verifier constructs the distance-at-most-three adjacency graph, enumerates all of its cliques, and reduces simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\). Its Rips face vector is \((24,168,472,618,384,92)\), and the boundary ranks in positive degrees are \((23,145,326,292,92)\). These imply \(\beta_2(\operatorname{VR}_{\le3}(P_4);\mathbb F_2)=1\). For the Čech nerve, independent boundary ranks \((23,145,246,156,36)\) imply \(\beta_2=17\), corroborating the Morse certificate. Because a pure shellable five-dimensional simplicial complex has no reduced homology below degree five, the nonzero second homology also proves non-shellability.

## Verification
Both necessary stand-alone artifacts are `certificate.json` and `verify.py`. Run `python3 verify.py certificate.json` using Python 3 with no third-party dependencies. The verifier reconstructs the graph, checks all facets and faces, checks the \(502\) elementary collapses, verifies every incidence and the acyclicity of the complete matching, and independently computes the two mod-two homology groups from boundary matrices. Successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK` followed by the exact checked counts. The finite exhaustive certificate establishes the stated finite-graph claim; no inference about an unbounded graph family is made.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams–Shukla–Singh [1] define precisely the finite-vertex intrinsic graph Čech nerve and supply the odd-scale facet reduction, but their homotopy classifications concern hypercubes, not the permutohedral Cayley graph. Gasparovic and collaborators [2] analyze first-dimensional intrinsic Čech persistence using balls centered at all points of a metric graph, not the finite set of its original vertices, and do not classify its degree-two homotopy. Choudhary–Kerber–Raghvendra [3] use a Euclidean permutahedral lattice to approximate filtrations; their approximation theorems do not compute this graph-metric nerve. The previously investigated Rips filtration of the truncated-octahedral graph is a different complex; the mod-two comparison above is reconstructed independently and does not rely on that earlier computation. These differences are mathematical nonimplications, not a claim that a literature search proves global novelty.

## Limitations
The claim concerns this graph, this radius convention, and the single integer scale three. The Rips comparison is over \(\mathbb F_2\); no Rips integral homotopy classification is needed for the finding. The calculation does not classify other scales or other permutohedra. Although the specifically inspected sources do not cover the result, equivalent unindexed literature might exist.
