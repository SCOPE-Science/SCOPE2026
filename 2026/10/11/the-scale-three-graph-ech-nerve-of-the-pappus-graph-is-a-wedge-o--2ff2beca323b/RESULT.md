# The scale-three graph Čech nerve of the Pappus graph is a wedge of forty-four two-spheres

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the 18-vertex Pappus graph with unit-length geometric edges, and let \(\mathcal N(P,3)\) be the nerve of the closed radius-\(3/2\) balls in its geometric realization, one ball centered at each graph vertex. Then
\[
\mathcal N(P,3)\simeq\bigvee^{44}S^2.
\]
In particular, its reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{44}\) in degree two and zero in every other degree. The complex is pure of dimension five, so it is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope
The finite simple graph \(P\) is specified without reliance on a drawing. Its vertices are the residues \(0,1,\ldots,17\) modulo \(18\). Include the cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and the chords \(\{i,i+a_i\}\), with indices taken modulo \(18\) and \(a_i\) periodic of period six with values \((5,7,-7,7,-7,-5)\). Duplicate unordered edges are included only once. This is the standard LCF presentation \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\) of the Pappus graph. The resulting graph is connected, cubic, has \(27\) edges, girth \(6\), and diameter \(4\).

The graph carries the shortest-path metric on its unit-edge geometric realization, *not* only on its finite vertex metric. For \(r\geq0\), \(\mathcal N(P,r)\) is the nerve of closed balls of radius \(r/2\) centered at its eighteen vertices. The claim concerns \(r=3\). No assertion is made here about persistence maps at other scales or the homotopy type of different graph complexes.

## Proof
**1. Identify the entire nerve.** By Lemma 2.2 of Adams–Shukla–Singh, for an odd integer \(r=3\) the simplices of a finite graph Čech complex are generated by
\[
F_{uv}=N_P[u]\cup N_P[v],\qquad \{u,v\}\in E(P),
\]
where \(N_P[v]\) is the closed radius-one *vertex* neighborhood in \(P\). Every generating set \(F_{uv}\) in this cubic graph has six distinct vertices, as the adjacent endpoints have no common neighbors in a graph of girth six. Directly enumerating the \(27\) generators in the displayed LCF model establishes that they are pairwise distinct maximal simplices. Thus the nerve is pure of dimension five. Enumerating their nonempty subsets gives the full face vector, indexed by dimensions zero through five,
\[
(18,135,396,369,162,27),
\]
comprising \(1107\) nonempty simplices. The vertex, edge, neighborhood, facet and face enumerations are independently reconstructed and checked by the supplied verifier.

**2. Collapse to a two-complex.** The accompanying `certificates/pappus_cech3.json` lists \(422\) ordered pairs of face bitmasks. Read a mask as a subset of vertices according to its binary bits. Process the pairs in the displayed order. For every pair \((L,U)\), the supplied verifier independently constructs the current residual face set and confirms that \(U\) contains \(L\), has precisely one more vertex, and is the *only remaining proper superface* of \(L\). Thus \(L\) is a free face and deleting \((L,U)\) is an elementary simplicial collapse. The \(422\) pairs consist of \(26\) with one-dimensional lower faces, \(234\) with two-dimensional lower faces, \(135\) with three-dimensional lower faces and \(27\) with four-dimensional lower faces. These operations leave a down-closed two-dimensional complex \(K\) with face vector
\[
(f_0(K),f_1(K),f_2(K))=(18,109,136).
\]
In particular \(\mathcal N(P,3)\simeq K\) by a sequence of explicit elementary collapses.

**3. Prove simple connectivity, not merely homological vanishing.** The same certificate lists \(17\) edge masks that the verifier checks are edges of \(K\) forming a spanning tree of its eighteen vertices. Collapsing this tree in the one-skeleton gives the standard van Kampen presentation with \(109-17=92\) edge-loop generators and one relator from each triangle. A further ordered list of \(92\) pairs \((T,e)\) is provided. At each step \(T\) is an actual triangle of \(K\), and of its three boundary edges, precisely the designated edge \(e\) is not already trivialized by the spanning-tree edges or preceding steps. Its triangle relator therefore expresses that remaining generator as trivial. The verifier checks every such implication and that these steps account for all \(109\) one-simplices. It follows by van Kampen that \(\pi_1(K)=1\).

**4. Determine integral homotopy type.** The Euler characteristic of \(K\) is
\[
\chi(K)=18-109+136=45.
\]
Because \(K\) is connected and simply connected, its integral first homology vanishes. Since it is two-dimensional, \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) is a subgroup of the free abelian group of 2-chains and is therefore free; Euler characteristic gives \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{44}\). The integral Hurewicz theorem identifies \(\pi_2(K)\cong H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). Select sphere maps representing a basis and wedge them to obtain \(f:\bigvee^{44}S^2\to K\), an integral homology isomorphism. Both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, so the homological Whitehead theorem makes \(f\) a homotopy equivalence. This proves the stated wedge classification.

**5. Non-shellability.** A nonempty pure shellable simplicial complex of dimension five has reduced homology only possibly in top dimension five (it is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of five-spheres). Here the second reduced homology has rank \(44\ne0\). Therefore the pure five-dimensional nerve cannot be shellable.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1], originally public on 12 December 2022, introduce the precise metric-graph Čech nerve, prove the odd-scale facet formula used in Step 1 (Lemma 2.2), and establish graph recovery for parameters below half the girth (Lemma 2.3). Their higher-scale homotopy classifications address hypercube graphs; they do not give this Pappus-graph homotopy classification. The graph has girth six, so \(r=3\) is the first threshold not covered by their stated strict recovery bound; below it, the nerve is homotopy equivalent to the graph.

Previte [2] studies vertex-centered distance-neighborhood complexes, which coincide with appropriate *even*-scale graph Čech constructions but do not supply the present odd-scale edge-neighborhood union and its Pappus computation. Shukla [3] treats Lovász common-open-neighbor complexes, a different simplicial construction. Previously obtained Pappus-graph computations for Vietoris–Rips, matching and independence complexes concern distinct simplicial complexes. The Pappus graph identification is documented in [4]. Searches for equivalent formulations and stronger covering classifications have not located a prior result establishing this specific homotopy type. The absence of a search match is not a mathematical proof of priority.

## Limitations
The homotopy classification is proved only for the identified finite graph and the stated intrinsic Čech parameter. The proof uses a complete reproducible finite certificate rather than a uniform theorem for all cubic girth-six graphs. No canonical basis of its integral second homology, equivariant representation, interscale map or full persistence barcode is asserted. Some relevant literature may be unindexed or otherwise unavailable for inspection; an independently discovered equivalent result cannot be ruled out. Independent external mathematical validation has not been performed.
