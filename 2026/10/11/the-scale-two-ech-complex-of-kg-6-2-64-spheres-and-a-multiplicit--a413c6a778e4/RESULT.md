# The scale-two Čech complex of \(KG(6,2)\): 64 spheres and a multiplicity-free \(S_6\)-character
## Finding
Let \(G=KG(6,2)\), with vertices the fifteen two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\) and edges joining disjoint subsets. Give every graph edge unit length and take the intrinsic shortest-path metric on its geometric realization. Let \(X=\mathcal N(G,2)\) be the nerve of closed radius-one metric balls centered at the graph vertices. Then
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{64}S^2.
\]
In particular, \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{64}\), while all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. Although \(X\) is pure of dimension six, it is not shellable.

The natural action of \(S_6\) relabeling the six ground-set elements induces the following **multiplicity-free** rational homology representation:
\[
\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong_{\mathbb Q[S_6]}
S^{(4,2)}\oplus S^{(4,1,1)}\oplus S^{(3,3)}\oplus S^{(3,2,1)}
\oplus S^{(3,1,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,2,2)}\oplus S^{(2,2,1,1)},
\]
where \(S^{\lambda}\) is the irreducible rational Specht module indexed by partition \(\lambda\) of six. The dimensions of these seven summands are respectively \(9,10,5,16,10,5,9\), summing to \(64\).

## Assumptions and scope
The construction uses **closed** metric balls in the geometric graph, not open graph neighborhoods or vertex-only distance balls at nonintegral radii. At scale two the maximal simplices are the fifteen closed graph neighborhoods
\[
F_e=\{e\}\cup\{f\in\tbinom{[6]}2:f\cap e=\varnothing\},
\quad e\in\tbinom{[6]}2.
\]
Each has seven vertices. The symmetric-group representation is rational and is asserted only as an abstract module isomorphism; no canonical integral equivariant basis is claimed. The theorem concerns exactly this natural smallest Kneser graph with triangles and does not extrapolate to higher Kneser graphs.

## Proof
**Facet identification.** For even scale two, the intrinsic graph Čech formula identifies every simplex with a subset of some closed distance-one vertex neighborhood [1, Lemma 2.2]. Two vertices \(e,f\) in \(KG(6,2)\) are adjacent precisely when their underlying pairs are disjoint. This gives the displayed facet formula. All fifteen neighborhoods are distinct, of equal cardinality seven, and therefore maximal.

**An acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** Order the fifteen graph vertices lexicographically as two-subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,6\}\). Enumerate every nonempty face of the simplicial complex generated by the fifteen facets. At stage \(i\), among currently unmatched faces pair every \(\sigma\) not containing the \(i\)-th vertex with \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\), provided both are unmatched faces. Delete paired faces from the active set and move to the next index. The finite exact computation in `verify.py` independently reconstructs all faces, checks each matching pair is a codimension-one face inclusion, orients **all** Hasse incidences upward on matched pairs and downward on every unmatched incidence, and verifies acyclicity by a complete topological sort. It produces \(1,535\) nonempty faces, \(735\) matched pairs, and exactly one critical vertex and \(64\) critical two-simplices. There are no critical cells in other dimensions. The f-vector, in dimensions zero through six, is
\[
(15,105,455,525,315,105,15).
\]
The matching's directed Hasse graph has exactly \(5,985\) incidences, and the topological sort visits all \(1,535\) faces. These exhaustive assertions use the explicit finite domain, not sampling.

By Forman's discrete Morse theorem, \(X\) is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell, sixty-four two-cells, and no one-cells or other positive-dimensional cells. Every attaching map of a two-cell is a map from its boundary circle into the sole zero-cell, hence is constant. Consequently this CW complex is a wedge of sixty-four two-spheres, proving the integral statement.

**The symmetry representation.** The graph construction is \(S_6\)-invariant. For an element \(g\in S_6\), calculate the simplicial Lefschetz number from the oriented fixed faces:
\[
L(g)=\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in X\\g\sigma=\sigma}}
(-1)^{\dim\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_{\sigma}).
\]
Since \(H_0(X;\mathbb Q)\) is the trivial one-dimensional representation and there is no other positive-degree homology except degree two, the degree-two trace is \(L(g)-1\). For the eleven cycle types, listed in decreasing-part order, the traces are

| Cycle type | \(1^6\) | \(2,1^4\) | \(2^2,1^2\) | \(2^3\) | \(3,1^3\) | \(3,2,1\) | \(3^2\) | \(4,1^2\) | \(4,2\) | \(5,1\) | \(6\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Trace | 64 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -2 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 0 | -1 | 0 |

The verifier calculates these eleven traces exactly by signed fixed-face enumeration, then uses the Murnaghan–Nakayama rim-hook formula and conjugacy-class centralizer orders to compute every irreducible character inner product. Exactly the seven Specht types in the statement have multiplicity one; the other four have multiplicity zero. Rational representations of finite groups are semisimple, so character equality proves the claimed module decomposition.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. It reconstructs the graph, all face masks, the staged Morse pairing, all \(5,985\) directed Hasse incidences, and the full acyclicity check, then independently performs binary boundary elimination. Its mod-two boundary ranks, from degrees zero to six with zero boundary at the ends, are
\[
(0,14,91,300,225,90,15,0),
\]
which give mod-two Betti numbers \((1,0,64,0,0,0,0)\). This independent field check is corroborative only; the integral homotopy conclusion follows from the acyclic Morse matching. The verifier separately checks the eleven symmetric-group traces and all eleven Specht multiplicities, without assuming the claimed representation. Expected output includes `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] define intrinsic graph Čech complexes and give their closed-neighborhood facet formula at even scales. Their sphere-wedge classification is for binary hypercube graphs, not Kneser graphs. Nilakantan and Singh [2] compute the **open** Lovász neighborhood complex for Kneser graphs, using common adjacent vertices; for the graph considered here their theorem gives nineteen two-spheres, not the closed-neighborhood Čech complex or its \(64\)-dimensional homology. Matsushita [3] establishes general relationships for closed-neighborhood complexes, including independence complexes of canonical double covers, but does not state the present Kneser-graph homotopy or equivariant representation calculation in the inspected treatment. Thus neither an open-neighborhood result nor a functorial comparison alone gives the asserted classification.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification for \(KG(6,2)\), not a theorem for arbitrary \(KG(n,2)\). The certificate is a complete finite combinatorial proof for this graph, not an independent formal proof assistant certification. Distinct rational Specht summands need not split over the integers. Novelty is assessed against the inspected accessible literature and may be affected by an unindexed equivalent calculation. An independent audit has not been performed.
