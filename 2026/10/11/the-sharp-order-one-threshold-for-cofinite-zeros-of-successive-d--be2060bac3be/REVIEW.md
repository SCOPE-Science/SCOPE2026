# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The construction uses independent planar-uniform coefficient variables and deterministic slowly varying weights \(B_n\). For a compact annulus, the largest chosen monomial has amplitude at least \(B_n e^{|z|L_n-H_{n,R}}\), while the whole derivative has the global upper envelope \(2B_n e^{|z|L_{2n}}\). Conditioning on one variable gives the exact area small-ball bound. Exponential integration of its logarithmic tail, Jensen on a circle, and the zero-free harmonic mean-value identity yield a disk-hole estimate \(2e^{-\gamma L_n/2}\); this is summable because \(L_n\sim(\log n)^2\). The countable disk basis and Borel--Cantelli justify every quantifier in the cofinite assertion. The coefficient order formula gives exactly order one and infinite type, while the normalized-coefficient maximum argument excludes all finite-exponential-type nonpolynomials. Nothing depends on a finite experiment.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

Hou's full preprint establishes cofinite derivative zero-hitting for an **order-two, finite-type** random entire function, not order one. Its author-posted source also explicitly records the earlier order-two Gaussian routes of Almeida and Chojecki. The full Barth--Schneider predecessor covers a selected sequence of derivatives, not every sufficiently large derivative. Our statement adds the lower attainable order and the converse obstruction at finite exponential type. Targeted literature queries and independently indexed mathematical findings did not produce an equivalent lower-order result; this search outcome is not a proof of absolute novelty. The unexamined full Almeida PDF and unindexed earlier literature are recorded risks.

## Value — PASS

The result determines the smallest possible growth order for an explicitly motivated cofinite-zero phenomenon and explains why the positive construction must lie beyond finite exponential type. This is a sharp structural boundary in the central question of zeros of successive entire-function derivatives, not a routine special case or a numerical improvement.
