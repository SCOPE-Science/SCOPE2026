# Review: The six-character threshold for signed Walsh majorant reversal

## Correctness
PASS. Affine-frequency reduction is exact because frequency translation produces pointwise unit-modulus factors and linear coordinate changes permute the uniform group. Over characteristic two, the only circuit lengths relevant up to six points are four and six. All independent, rank-four and rank-three configurations are exhausted, with directly evaluated integer moment tables \((16,8)\), \((79/4,14)\), \((25,20)\), \((21,24)\) and \((30,20\text{ or }30)\). The rank-three six-point proof checks the parity split and Parseval before optimizing. The dimension-three seven/eight-point argument supplies the remaining quantifiers. A standalone exact-integer checker was replayed successfully. No infinite-dimensional conclusion rests on enumeration.

## Originality
PASS with bibliographic risk. The closest original source, Gressman–Guo–Pierce–Roos–Yung (arXiv:2106.12538, Theorems 1–3), handles real frequencies on continuous tori and general coefficient sizes, not finite Walsh groups with exponent-two relations and the exact sign-only \(L^3\) threshold. Krenedits (arXiv:1006.0409, Introduction and trigonometric constructions) exhibits earlier circle examples but not this all-dimensional Boolean classification. Published comparable records concern cyclic three-term majorant reversal, Walsh support-size spectra or phase minimax, not the six-circuit sign-majorant implication. The literature search cannot rule out older unindexed treatments on compact exponent-two groups, including the Fournier/Rains majorant literature, which remains the principal risk.

## Value
PASS. The first possible sign-only majorant reversal in the Boolean Walsh system, a natural restriction of the Hardy–Littlewood/Montgomery problem, has an exact support threshold, a complete frequency/sign characterization across all dimensions and sharp attained integer moments. The exceptional six-circuit geometry identifies the unique mechanism, and no smaller-dimensional Boolean system supports a reversal with any number of characters. This is not a numerical isolated example or a restatement of the continuous torus theory.

## Limits
The result is about \(\pm1\)-coefficients only. It does not assert results for complex phases, other real magnitudes, other exponents or general finite abelian groups. The three-dimensional exhaustive bound is proved separately. Equivalent older harmonic-analytic terminology remains a residual originality risk. Independent assessment is outstanding.
