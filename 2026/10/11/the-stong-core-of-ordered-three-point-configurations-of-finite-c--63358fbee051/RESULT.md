# The Stong core of ordered three-point configurations of finite crowns

## Finding
Let \(n\ge 2\) and let \(C_n\) be the \(2n\)-point finite crown whose minimal elements are \(a_i\), maximal elements are \(b_i\), and whose strict relations are \(a_i<b_i\) and \(a_i<b_{i+1}\), with indices modulo \(n\). Give the ordered three-point configuration space
\[
F_3(C_n)=\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in C_n^3:\ x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}
\]
the induced coordinatewise order (equivalently, the subspace Alexandrov topology). Write \(A_n\) for the induced subposet of triples whose three entries are **pairwise incomparable** in \(C_n\).

**Theorem.** For every \(n\ge3\), the inclusion \(A_n\hookrightarrow F_3(C_n)\) is a strong deformation retract and \(A_n\) has no beat points. Consequently \(A_n\) is the unique Stong core up to finite-space homeomorphism, with precisely
\[
|A_n|=2n(2n-4)(2n-5)
\]
points. The reduction consists of \(12n(2n-3)\) beat-point deletions, in four explicitly defined classes. For the exceptional \(n=2\), the entire \(24\)-point configuration space has no beat points: it is its own core, with two components each homeomorphic to the \(12\)-point crown.

## Assumptions and scope
The crown is a finite \(T_0\)-space, not a geometric circle or the ordinary configuration space of a manifold. A *down beat point* has a greatest strict predecessor, and an *up beat point* has a least strict successor. Deleting either is a strong deformation retract of finite spaces; a beat-point-free retract is a Stong core. The statement determines the **finite-space homotopy core**, rather than only the weak homotopy type of the order complex. All three particles are labeled, and the order is inherited from the Cartesian cube before restricting to injective triples.

## Proof
Fix \(n\ge3\). There are only three possible rank profiles: three lower points, three upper points, or a mixed triple with one or two lower points. For a mixed triple \(t\), let \(e(t)\) be the number of incidences \(a_i<b_j\) **between distinct entries of the triple**. Since every upper point has two lower neighbors and vice versa, \(e(t)\) belongs to \(\{0,1,2\}\). Denote by \(L_e\) the triples with two lower points and incidence count \(e\), and by \(U_e\) those with one lower point and incidence count \(e\). The desired \(A_n\) consists exactly of all-lower triples, all-upper triples, \(L_0\), and \(U_0\).

**Step 1: Delete \(L_1\) as down beat points.** For a triple with two lower points and an upper point incident to exactly one of them, the upper point can be lowered to either of its two lower neighbors; one choice repeats an occupied coordinate and is excluded. Thus there is exactly one strict predecessor in the configuration poset, namely an all-lower triple. The points within \(L_1\) are pairwise incomparable, so they may be deleted in any order without affecting these witnesses.

**Step 2: Delete \(U_1\) as up beat points.** Dually, a triple with one lower point and two upper points, one incident to that lower point, has exactly one strict successor: an all-upper triple obtained by raising the lower point to its other upper neighbor. The elements of \(U_1\) form an antichain, so this deletion is again valid one point at a time.

**Step 3: Delete \(L_2\) as up beat points.** The two lower points must be the two neighbors of the single upper point, say \(b_j\), so they are \(a_{j-1}\) and \(a_j\). To move strictly upward without repeating \(b_j\), either one lower coordinate is raised to its other upper neighbor, or both are raised. Any one-coordinate raise gives an element of \(U_1\): because \(n\ge3\), the other lower coordinate is not below that new upper point. Those intermediate successors were removed in Step 2. Raising both lower coordinates gives a unique all-upper successor, since \(b_{j-1}\), \(b_j\), and \(b_{j+1}\) are distinct when \(n\ge3\). Hence every point in \(L_2\) now has exactly one strict successor and is an up beat point. This class too is an antichain.

**Step 4: Delete \(U_2\) as down beat points.** Apply the rank-dual argument: the single lower point is incident to both upper points. Lowering exactly one upper point gives a deleted member of \(L_1\); lowering both gives a unique all-lower predecessor. Every point of \(U_2\) is therefore a down beat point, and the class is an antichain.

These four operations remove exactly the triples outside \(A_n\). To prove minimality, start with an all-lower triple, with three chosen lower indices forming a subset \(S\) of the \(n\)-cycle on lower indices (the upper point \(b_j\) corresponds to the edge joining its two lower neighbors). For \(n\ge4\), raising exactly one lower coordinate to an upper element incident with it but not with either other occupied lower element gives a strict successor **remaining in \(A_n\)**. These allowable single raises correspond exactly to edges having one endpoint in \(S\) and one outside. Since \(S\) is a nonempty proper subset of the cyclic graph, it has at least two boundary edges, which yield two distinct incomparable successors (both with two lower and one upper coordinate). If \(n=3\), instead raise all three lower coordinates simultaneously: the two assignments \(a_i\mapsto b_i\) and \(a_i\mapsto b_{i+1}\) (for all three indices) give two distinct, mutually incomparable, all-upper successors. In every case the all-lower triple has at least two incomparable successors and no strict predecessor. Thus it cannot be a beat point. An all-upper triple is dual. Consider a triple in \(L_0\). Lowering its sole upper entry to either of its two lower neighbors yields two incomparable strict predecessors in \(A_n\), because the original upper point is incomparable with both occupied lower coordinates. It also has two incomparable strict successors in \(A_n\): choose each of its two occupied lower coordinates in turn, raising it to an upper neighbor that is not incident to the other occupied lower coordinate. Such a choice exists because, for \(n\ge3\), two distinct lower elements share at most one upper neighbor. The old upper entry is distinct from either choice, by the \(L_0\) condition. The resulting two successors change different coordinates and hence are incomparable. The argument for \(U_0\) is order dual. Therefore no element of \(A_n\) is a down or up beat point. Stong's core theorem proves the asserted uniqueness.

For the cardinality, fix an upper element and count the mixed triples by the number of incident lower entries. Choosing the unique incident lower entry in \(L_1\) has \(2\) choices, choosing the nonincident lower entry has \(n-2\) choices, and permuting the three distinct coordinates has \(6\) choices. Thus \(|L_1|=12n(n-2)\), and dually \(|U_1|=12n(n-2)\). Both neighbors must occur in \(L_2\), so \(|L_2|=6n\), and dually \(|U_2|=6n\). Subtracting from the \((2n)(2n-1)(2n-2)\) injective ordered triples gives
\[
|A_n|=(2n)(2n-1)(2n-2)-24n(n-2)-12n=2n(2n-4)(2n-5).
\]
Equivalently, this counts ordered independent triples in the underlying \(2n\)-cycle of the crown. Every deletion is justified by a unique witness in the indicated direction, so the reduction is a finite sequence of strong deformation retractions.

For \(n=2\), the crown is the four-point complete height-two poset. Exhausting all \(4\cdot3\cdot2=24\) injective ordered triples gives a rank-two configuration poset where each point has exactly two opposite-rank neighbors and no other comparable points. Its Hasse graph consists of two cycles of length \(12\), checked by complete enumeration in the standalone verifier. Each cycle is the \(12\)-point crown and has no beat points, so no deletion is possible. (The \(n\ge3\) argument deliberately does not apply: the two other upper neighbors in Step 3 coincide when \(n=2\).)

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_core.py` with Python \(3\) and no third-party dependencies. The script constructs the entire injective product poset, classifies all mixed triples by incidence, and verifies each of the four prescribed stages using the actual currently surviving strict predecessors or successors. For every \(n\) from \(2\) through \(12\), it verifies the claimed counts and that no beat point survives in the proposed core. For \(n=2\), it also exhaustively checks both \(12\)-cycles and the absence of beat points. It terminates with `VERIFY_OK`. These computations test the formulas on finite examples; the unlimited \(n\ge3\) assertion follows from the uniform argument in the preceding section, not from sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak and Minian's *Minimal Finite Models* supplies the finite-space beat-point and Stong core theorems and the framework distinguishing finite-space homotopy from weak homotopy. Minian's *Morse theory of Bestvina-Brady type for posets and matchings* develops topological reductions for finite posets, including relative constructions. Neither gives this three-particle crown calculation. The published result *Two-point configurations of finite crowns retain the circle weak type* determines a projection weak equivalence for **two** labeled particles; it neither addresses triples nor gives the three-particle Stong core. Results about configuration spaces of geometric circles or simplicial complexes concern different topological objects and do not imply the present finite-poset statement. No broader finite-poset theorem found in the searched sources determines this four-stage deletion or the cardinality of the resulting core.

## Limitations
This theorem does not compute the general integral homology, the full fundamental group, or the equivariant homotopy type of \(F_3(C_n)\); it gives its exact minimal finite-space representative. There is no extension asserted to arbitrary posets, other particle numbers, or geometric graph configuration spaces. The literature search cannot rule out an unpublished or unindexed equivalent formula. The computation covers \(2\le n\le12\), while the symbolic proof covers all \(n\ge3\) plus the separately enumerated \(n=2\) case.
