# The threefold rotation character of pure strong Morse matching complexes of cycles

## Finding
Let \(n\ge6\), and let \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(C_n)\) be the pure complex of strong discrete Morse matchings of the \(n\)-cycle, with its cyclic edge-and-vertex rotation \(r\) generating \(C_n\). Define the **reduced equivariant Euler character**
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a)=\sum_{i\ge0}(-1)^i\operatorname{tr}\bigl(r^a\mid\widetilde H_i(X_n;\mathbb Q)\bigr).
\]
For every \(0\le a<n\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_n(r^a)=\begin{cases}5,&3\mid\gcd(n,a),\\-1,&3\nmid\gcd(n,a).\end{cases}}
\]
Here \(\gcd(n,0)=n\). Equivalently, as an element of the rational representation ring of \(C_n\),
\[
\boxed{\sum_i(-1)^i[\widetilde H_i(X_n;\mathbb Q)]=\begin{cases}
2[\mathbb Q[C_n/C_{n/3}]]-[\mathbf1],&3\mid n,\\
-[\mathbf1],&3\nmid n.
\end{cases}}
\]
In particular, if \(n=3k\ge9\), write \(V\) for the two-dimensional rational representation of \(C_n\) inflated from the nontrivial rational irreducible of \(C_3\). Then the **individual** nonzero reduced homology representations are
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbf1,\qquad \widetilde H_{2k-2}(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong V^{\oplus2}.}
\]
For \(n=6\) the sole nonzero group is \(\widetilde H_2(X_6;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbf1\oplus V^{\oplus2}\). If \(3\nmid n\) and \(n\) is odd, both the rank-one group in degree two and the rank-two group in degree \(2k-1\), where \(n\in\{3k+1,3k+2\}\), have trivial rotation action.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycle is a one-dimensional simplicial complex with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and edges \(e_j=\{j,j+1\}\). The vertices of the matching complex are its incidences, denoted \((j,+)\) for the pair \((j,e_j)\) and \((j,-)\) for \((j+1,e_j)\). The rotation sends \((j,\pm)\) to \((j+1,\pm)\). The pure complex is generated by the maximal-cardinality strong matchings; it is **not** the full strong-matching complex or the ordinary Morse-matching complex. All representations are over \(\mathbb Q\). The zero-th reduced homology is absent because \(n\ge6\) gives connected complexes by the cited homotopy theorem.

## Proof
**Facets.** The facet classification in Knudson--Owens--White, Proposition 8.3, follows from deleting the vertex whose two incident cycle edges are unmatched. Consequently a facet has exactly two consecutive absent edges \(e_b,e_{b+1}\). Reading the remaining \(n-2\) edges clockwise as \(e_{b+2},\ldots,e_{b+n-1}\), its incidence signs are \(+\) for the first \(j\) edges and \(-\) for the remaining \(n-2-j\), for exactly one \(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}\). There are \(n(n-1)\) facets. This description also follows by applying the paper's maximal-path matching description to the path remaining after the two unmatched edges.

**Fixed faces of nonidentity rotations.** Fix \(a\not\equiv0\pmod n\), set \(g=\gcd(n,a)\), and let \(d=n/g\ge2\) be the orbit size of every edge under \(r^a\). Any nonempty rotation-invariant face must be a union of complete \(d\)-element orbits of incidences. It cannot contain both signs: one occurrence of each sign in a fundamental block, repeated \(d\) times, yields at least four cyclically alternating occurrences of opposite signs, while every facet has one cyclic block of \(+\) signs followed by one of \(-\) signs. Thus each fixed nonempty face has a single incidence sign. Its edge support is the periodic lift of a nonempty set \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\). Such a face lies in a facet **if and only if** the complement of \(S\) contains two consecutive residues in the cyclic order; for \(g=1\), only \(S=\varnothing\) qualifies. Sufficiency uses the all-\(+\) or all-\(-\) facet with the two missing neighboring edges.

**Orientation and signed count.** If \(|S|=s\), the face has \(sd\) vertices. Rotation acts as \(s\) disjoint \(d\)-cycles on its ordered vertices, hence its oriented simplicial Lefschetz contribution is
\[
(-1)^{sd-1}\,(-1)^{s(d-1)}=(-1)^{s-1}.
\]
For each nonempty admissible \(S\) there are exactly two fixed signed faces, one for each sign. Subtracting the reduced empty-face trace, the reduced Lefschetz number is
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a)=-1+2\sum_{\substack{S\ne\varnothing\\\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\setminus S\text{ contains neighbors}}}(-1)^{|S|-1}.
\]
Let \(I_g(-1)\) be the alternating count of cyclic \(0\)-\(1\) strings of length \(g\) with no adjacent \(1\)'s, interpreting the sole residue at \(g=1\) as adjacent to itself. Since the total alternating sum of all subsets vanishes, the expression becomes
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a)=1+2(-1)^g I_g(-1).
\]
The standard two-state transfer matrix \(T=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}\) gives \(I_g(-1)=\operatorname{tr}(T^g)\), with eigenvalues \(e^{\pm\pi i/3}\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal L_n(r^a)=1+4(-1)^g\cos(\pi g/3)=1+4\cos(2\pi g/3),
\]
which equals \(5\) if \(3\mid g\) and \(-1\) otherwise. For \(a=0\), the same formula follows from Theorem 8.6 of Knudson--Owens--White: the wedge-of-spheres reduced Euler characteristic is \(5\) when \(3\mid n\) and \(-1\) otherwise. The Hopf trace theorem identifies the chain Lefschetz calculation with the alternating homology trace.

**Representation consequences.** If \(3\mid n\), the character just computed is \(2\operatorname{Ind}_{C_{n/3}}^{C_n}\mathbf1-\mathbf1\), since the three-point coset permutation character equals \(3\) exactly on rotations divisible by three. Rationally this character is \(\mathbf1\oplus V^{\oplus2}\). By the cited Theorem 8.6, if \(n=3k\ge9\), the only homology is one-dimensional in degree \(2\) and four-dimensional in even degree \(2k-2\). These are genuine rational summands of their (unsigned) direct sum \(\mathbf1\oplus V^{\oplus2}\). A one-dimensional rational representation of the cyclic group is trivial or (only for even \(n\)) the order-two sign; the displayed character has no order-two constituent. Thus degree-two homology is trivial and the higher group is \(V^{\oplus2}\). For \(n=6\), both degrees coincide, so the five-dimensional representation is \(\mathbf1\oplus V^{\oplus2}\). If \(3\nmid n\), the virtual character equals \(-\mathbf1\); for odd \(n\), the rank-one degree-two representation must be trivial, forcing the rank-two odd-degree group to be \(\mathbf1^{\oplus2}\).

## Verification
The self-contained `verify.py` enumerates all facets by independent choices of two unmatched neighboring edges and path-root position, generates every simplex, evaluates oriented simplicial traces of all rotations, and compares with the formula. An exact run verified \(51\) rotation-character instances for \(6\le n\le11\); the simplex counts were \(163,421,1057,2599,6281,14961\), respectively. The computational checks are finite examples and do **not** replace the all-parameter facet, transfer-matrix, and representation arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens--White (arXiv:2609.06844v1, first public September 6, 2026; primary MSC 57Q70) introduce the pure strong-matching complexes, classify their maximal matchings (Proposition 8.3), and determine their nonequivariant homotopy types (Theorem 8.6). Neither the full inspected paper nor Chari--Joswig's earlier study of **ordinary** discrete Morse complexes determines the new cyclic rotation character of the **pure strong** construction. The calculation here refines the existing homotopy classification rather than re-proving it. The ordinary pure cycle complex is a distinct complex and does not imply the stated group action.

## Limitations
The main equivariant Euler formula determines a virtual representation; the graded rotation action for even \(n\) not divisible by three is not completely resolved here. No reflection action, integral representation splitting, or equivariant homotopy decomposition is claimed. Literature comparisons may miss unindexed equivalent derivations.
