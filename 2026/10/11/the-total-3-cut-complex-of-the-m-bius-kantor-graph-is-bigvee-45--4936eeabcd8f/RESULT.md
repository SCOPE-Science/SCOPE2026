# The total \(3\)-cut complex of the Möbius–Kantor graph is \(\bigvee^{45}S^{10}\)
## Finding
Let \(M=G(8,3)\) be the Möbius–Kantor graph on vertices \(u_0,\ldots,u_7,v_0,\ldots,v_7\), with edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+3}\), where subscripts are modulo \(8\). For the total \(3\)-cut complex \(\Delta_3^t(M)\), whose facets are complements of independent triples, one has \(\Delta_3^t(M)\simeq\bigvee^{45}S^{10}\). Consequently this pure \(12\)-dimensional complex is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is defined explicitly, so no graph-database convention is needed. Its vertex set is
\[\{u_i,v_i: i\in\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\}\]
and its edges are \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+3}\). The total \(3\)-cut complex follows the definition of Bayer et al.: a face \(\sigma\) is present exactly when its complement contains an independent set of three vertices. The statement concerns ordinary homotopy type of the finite simplicial complex.

## Proof
There are exactly \(272\) independent triples in \(M\). Their complements are therefore \(272\) distinct facets, each with \(13\) vertices, so \(\Delta_3^t(M)\) is pure of dimension \(12\).

Enumerate every face as a bit mask and apply successive element matchings in the vertex order
\[u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_7,v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_7.\]
At each stage, among the faces not used earlier, pair a face \(\sigma\) not containing the current vertex \(x\) with \(\sigma\cup\{x\}\) whenever both are still present. This is exactly the sequential element-matching construction of Bayer et al.; their Theorem 7.5 states that the union of such stages is acyclic.

For this complex the exhaustive matching contains \(32{,}419\) pairs. The empty face is paired, and precisely \(45\) faces remain unmatched; every unmatched face has \(11\) vertices and therefore dimension \(10\). Bayer et al., Theorem 7.2, then gives a CW model with one \(0\)-cell and \(45\) cells of dimension \(10\), hence
\[\Delta_3^t(M)\simeq\bigvee^{45}S^{10}.\]

Since the original complex is pure of dimension \(12\) but has nonzero reduced homology in dimension \(10\), it cannot be shellable: a pure shellable \(12\)-complex is contractible or has homotopy type a wedge of \(12\)-spheres.

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` reconstructs \(M\) from the displayed edge rule, enumerates all independent triples, regenerates the entire face set, performs the sequential element matching, and separately verifies acyclicity by orienting all Hasse cover relations with matched edges reversed and running a full topological sort.

It checks \(64{,}883\) faces including the empty face and \(515{,}872\) oriented Hasse cover relations. The face vector is
\[(16,120,560,1820,4368,8008,11440,12870,11440,8008,4368,1592,272),\]
and its Euler characteristic is \(46\), consistent with a wedge of \(45\) even-dimensional spheres. The verifier reproduces exactly \(45\) critical \(10\)-cells and no critical cells in any other dimension.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al. introduced total cut complexes and computed them for several structured graph families, including complete bipartite graphs, cycles, prisms, and grids. Their complete-bipartite formula does not apply here: the Möbius–Kantor graph is sparse rather than complete bipartite, and its independent triples are not restricted to triples lying entirely in one bipartition class. Their paper does not list the Möbius–Kantor graph among its computed examples.

A later paper by Carnero Bravo develops general connectivity bounds and homotopy calculations for cycle powers, complete multipartite graphs, some disconnected graphs, and total \(2\)-cut complexes of certain Cartesian products. Those classes do not contain this total \(3\)-cut calculation for \(G(8,3)\). Searches using the aliases “Möbius–Kantor graph” and “generalized Petersen graph \(G(8,3)\)” found no published statement of the displayed homotopy type.

## Limitations
The result is for the single natural graph \(G(8,3)\) and the parameter \(k=3\); it does not assert a formula for all generalized Petersen graphs or all symmetric cubic graphs. The originality check is a literature comparison, not a proof that no inaccessible or unindexed source contains the same computation. The proof of homotopy type depends on the standard discrete-Morse element-matching theorem, while the finite enumeration and acyclicity of the concrete matching are independently regenerated by the bundled verifier.
