# The trivial unitary system has frame multiplicity \(1\) or \(\infty\) according to proper infiniteness

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal A\) be a nonzero unital \(C^*\)-algebra. On the standard **left** Hilbert module \(\mathcal H=\mathcal A\), use
\[
\langle x,y\rangle=xy^*.
\]
For the trivial unitary system \(\mathcal U=\{I_{\mathcal H}\}\), the frame multiplicity defined by Chi and Li has the exact dichotomy
\[
\mu(\mathcal U)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{if the unit of }\mathcal A\text{ is not properly infinite},\\
\infty,&\text{if the unit of }\mathcal A\text{ is properly infinite}.
\end{cases}
\]
Here the unit of \(\mathcal A\) is *properly infinite* when there are two elements \(v,w\in\mathcal A\) satisfying
\[
v^*v=w^*w=1,\qquad v^*w=0.
\]
Such elements are isometries with mutually orthogonal range projections.

In particular, taking \(\mathcal A=B(\ell^2(\mathbb N))\) yields **infinite** frame multiplicity while \(1\in\mathcal H\) is a complete wandering vector. This directly refutes the universal finiteness assertion in Theorem 5.3 of Chi--Li, *Characterization of frame vectors for \(\mathcal A\)-group-like unitary systems on Hilbert \(C^*\)-modules* (2023).

## Assumptions and scope
The source assumes a unital \(C^*\)-algebra, a finitely or countably generated left Hilbert module, and an \(\mathcal A\)-group-like unitary system with a complete wandering vector. All are satisfied: \(\mathcal H=\mathcal A\) is singly generated by its unit, and \(\{I\}\) is group-like because its generated group is itself and its independence condition is vacuous. The singleton \(\{1\}\) is an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal H\):
\[
\langle1,1\rangle=1,\qquad x=x1.
\]
Thus \(1\) is a complete wandering vector.

For the source's frame multiplicity, \(k\) is *admissible* when there exist individual complete Parseval frame vectors \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\) for \(\{I\}\) whose diagonal direct sum \(s_1\oplus\cdots\oplus s_k\) is a complete Parseval frame vector for the system \(\{I_{\mathcal H^{(k)}}\}\). Multiplicity is the supremum of admissible positive integers. No infinite direct sum is needed or claimed to be a standard frame here.

## Proof
**Step 1: characterize an individual Parseval vector.** For \(s\in\mathcal A\), the singleton Parseval identity is
\[
\langle x,s\rangle\langle s,x\rangle
=xs^*sx^*=xx^*\qquad(x\in\mathcal A).
\]
Setting \(x=1\) shows this holds only if \(s^*s=1\); that condition is also sufficient. Such a singleton is complete as an \(\mathcal A\)-module frame because
\[
x=(xs^*)s\qquad(x\in\mathcal A).
\]

**Step 2: characterize a diagonal Parseval vector.** For \(s=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\) and \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\), the diagonal one-vector Parseval condition is
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_is_i^*\right)
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_jx_j^*\right)
=\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_ix_i^*.
\]
Testing one-coordinate vectors gives \(s_i^*s_i=1\). For distinct \(i,j\), testing two-coordinate vectors with \(x_i=1\), \(x_j=1\) shows
\[
s_i^*s_j+s_j^*s_i=0.
\]
Using \(x_i=1\), \(x_j=\mathrm{i}1\) also shows
\[
-\mathrm{i}s_i^*s_j+\mathrm{i}s_j^*s_i=0.
\]
The two identities imply \(s_i^*s_j=0\). Conversely, the relations
\[
s_i^*s_j=\delta_{ij}1
\]
make the Parseval identity immediate. They also give complete generation: for any \(x=(x_j)_{j=1}^k\), put \(a=\sum_i x_is_i^*\). Then
\[
a s_j=x_j\qquad(1\le j\le k),
\]
so the single vector \(s\) generates \(\mathcal A^k\) as a left module.

Therefore \(k\) is admissible exactly when \(\mathcal A\) contains \(k\) isometries with pairwise orthogonal ranges.

**Step 3: prove the dichotomy.** If two such isometries \(v,w\) exist, define
\[
s_j=v^{j-1}w\qquad(j\ge1).
\]
Each \(s_j\) is an isometry. Whenever \(j>i\),
\[
s_i^*s_j=w^*v^{j-i}w=0
\]
since \(w^*v=0\), the adjoint of \(v^*w=0\). Thus every finite \(k\) is admissible, so \(\mu(\mathcal U)=\infty\). If no two orthogonal isometries exist, no \(k\ge2\) is admissible; \(k=1\) is always admissible via \(s_1=1\). Thus \(\mu(\mathcal U)=1\).

**Step 4: an explicit counterexample to Theorem 5.3.** Let \(e_n\), \(n\ge1\), denote the standard basis of \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), and define \(s_j\in B(\ell^2(\mathbb N))\) by
\[
s_je_n=e_{2^{j-1}(2n-1)}\qquad(j,n\ge1).
\]
Each map takes one orthonormal basis to an orthonormal family and is therefore an isometry. Every positive integer has exactly one power-of-two valuation, so the ranges for different \(j\) are mutually orthogonal. In particular
\[
s_i^*s_j=\delta_{ij}I.
\]
By Steps 1--3, \(\mu(\mathcal U)=\infty\), contrary to Theorem 5.3 despite the existing complete wandering vector \(1\).

## Verification
The definitions of complete wandering vector, Parseval frame vector, and frame multiplicity were checked directly against the primary article's Section 2 and Section 5. The full statements and proofs of Theorems 5.2 and 5.3 were inspected.

The proof of Theorem 5.3 arrives at an inequality of the form
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle s_j,s_j\rangle\le1
\]
and declares this contradictory for large \(k\) because every term is positive. In the explicit example,
\[
\langle s_j,s_j\rangle=s_js_j^*=p_j,
\qquad
p_ip_j=0\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_j\le1
\]
for **every** positive integer \(k\). Each \(p_j\) is a nonzero projection of norm \(1\), but these projections do not possess a common positive lower bound in the operator order. The alleged contradiction does not follow.

The direct computations in the proof, rather than finite experiments or external certificates, establish the assertion for every \(k\).

## Relationship to prior work
Theorem 5.2 of the same Chi--Li paper concerns orthogonal isometries as a criterion for a fixed finite diagonal multiplicity. It does not state the proper-infiniteness dichotomy or identify the impossibility of the following purported universal finiteness theorem. The proof above tests the definitions directly, independently of Theorem 5.2.

Mahmoudieh--Hosseinnezhad--Abbaspour Tabadkan (2019) study multi-frame **tuples whose orbit union is a frame of one Hilbert module**, with parameterizations by operators in the local commutant. This is a different multiplicity convention from the diagonal-amplification test on \(\mathcal H^{(k)}\) used in the 2023 paper. The earlier result does not assert finite diagonal frame multiplicity or this dichotomy.

The properly infinite unit of a \(C^*\)-algebra admits arbitrarily many mutually orthogonal isometries, whereas the absence of two such isometries prevents any diagonal multiplicity above one for the trivial system. This is the algebraic obstruction that the published argument overlooks.

## Limitations
The classification concerns the one-element unitary system on the canonical module \(\mathcal A\). It does not classify multiplicity for a nontrivial group-like unitary system or for an arbitrary Hilbert module \(\mathcal H\). Nor does it assert that a trace hypothesis is sufficient for a finite-multiplicity theorem in every other setting.

The counterexample employs a noncommutative properly infinite algebra, which is expressly permitted by the source's Section 2 assumptions. A special theorem restricted to commutative or otherwise finite coefficient algebras would be a different assertion.
