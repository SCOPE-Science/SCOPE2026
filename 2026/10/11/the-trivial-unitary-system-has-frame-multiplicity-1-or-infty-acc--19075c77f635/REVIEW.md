# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The left-module convention \(\langle x,y\rangle=xy^*\) and the source's definition of diagonal complete Parseval frame multiplicity were reconstructed. A singleton is Parseval and complete precisely when its element is an isometry. The diagonal Parseval identity forces orthogonality of all different isometries and conversely is satisfied by an orthogonal family; completeness is checked using an explicit left-module coefficient. Two orthogonal isometries yield arbitrarily many, proving the exact \(1\)-or-\(\infty\) dichotomy. The formula \(s_je_n=e_{2^{j-1}(2n-1)}\) verifies the source theorem's hypotheses and infinite multiplicity exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The precise source theorem, its preceding orthogonal-isometry criterion, earlier multi-frame-vector theory, equivalent Parseval and properly infinite formulations, and related indexed scientific results were compared. The earlier paper uses a distinct tuple-on-one-module multiplicity rather than the source's diagonal-amplification multiplicity. The 2023 theorem does not disclose the proper-infiniteness dichotomy or the explicit counterexample to its finiteness conclusion. The standard existence of orthogonal isometries is acknowledged as prior operator-algebra knowledge; the new point is its exact frame-multiplicity implication and contradiction of Theorem 5.3.

## Value
PASS. Universal finiteness is presented as an essential final theorem of the source article, yet it fails maximally: the simplest unitary system on a singly generated module can have arbitrarily large diagonal frame multiplicity. The dichotomy completely identifies the underlying operator-algebra boundary rather than producing only one negative instance. The published proof's inference from positive algebra elements to scalar growth fails for orthogonal nonzero projections.

## Closest literature and limitations

An unindexed correction or remark exploiting properly infinite algebras remains a possible priority risk.
