# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The six published difference cycles reconstruct a complex with face vector \((14,91,154,77)\). Exact row reduction over \(\mathbf F_3\) gives \(\dim H_1=1\). The displayed cocycle vanishes on every triangular face, is nonzero on an explicit empty-triangle cycle, and is evaluated exhaustively on all 210 empty triangles. The orbit and class counts are independently checked by direct translation enumeration. The lower bound of three edges for a simple cycle is immediate, so the detected 182 triangles prove the stated homological systole.

## Originality

PASS with residual risk stated below. The exact-object full text gives the difference-cycle description and topological type but no empty-triangle homology spectrum. Searches using the exact object, the 14-vertex parameter, missing/empty triangles, mod-three homology, shortest cycles, and combinatorial systole produced no statement implying the 13 essential orbits, the two null orbits, or the 182-cycle count. The broad systolic literature defines the invariant but does not supply this finite decomposition. The inaccessible full text of the 1985 antecedent remains the principal residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The empty triangles are the shortest possible cycles in a neighborly triangulation, and \(H_1(L(3,1);\mathbf F_3)\) is the torsion-sensitive coefficient group naturally detecting the lens-space generator. Classifying all 15 symmetry orbits therefore gives a sharp local description of how the global order-three homology is already visible on minimum-length cycles, while identifying the two exceptional empty-triangle orbits that fail to detect it.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest exact-object source is Spreer's cyclic-manifold classification, which records this 14-vertex \(L(3,1)\) and its difference cycles. The earlier Kühnel--Lassmann paper is the historical antecedent cited there, but its full text was not available in the material inspected. The claim is limited to mod-three homology of this particular triangulation.
