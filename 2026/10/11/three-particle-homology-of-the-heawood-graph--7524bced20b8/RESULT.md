# Three-particle homology of the Heawood graph
## Finding
Let \(H\) be the Heawood graph, realized as the incidence graph of the Fano plane. Write its vertices as points \(p_i\) and lines \(\ell_j\), indexed by \(i,j\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), and join \(p_i\) to \(\ell_j\) exactly when \(i-j\in\{0,1,3\}\). For the unordered configuration space \(B_3(H)\) of three distinct unlabeled points on \(H\),
\[
H_3(B_3(H);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
In addition, the Betti vectors in degrees \(0,1,2,3\) are
\[
(1,9,44,1) \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_2,
\qquad
(1,8,43,1) \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_3.
\]
For these coefficient fields, all homology groups in degrees at least \(4\) vanish.

## Assumptions and scope
The configuration space is the unordered space of three pairwise distinct points on the topological realization of the finite graph. The computation uses the full-vertex reduced Świątkowski complex in weight \(3\). For a trivalent vertex, after fixing one incident half-edge as a base, the local reduced degree-one generators are the two half-edge differences. The differential of such a difference is the corresponding difference of incident edge variables. This reduced complex computes graph-configuration homology by the comparison theorem and reduced-complex quasi-isomorphism of An, Drummond-Cole, and Knudsen.

The statement is specific to three particles on the Heawood graph. It does not assert an integral description of \(H_1\) or \(H_2\), nor a formula for other particle numbers.

## Proof
The Heawood graph in the stated model has \(14\) trivalent vertices and \(21\) edges. In weight \(3\), the reduced Świątkowski chain groups have dimensions
\[
\dim C_0=1771,\qquad
\dim C_1=6468,\qquad
\dim C_2=7644,\qquad
\dim C_3=2912,
\]
and no chain groups in higher degree. These counts come from choosing, respectively, three edge particles; one reduced vertex generator and two edge particles; two reduced vertex generators and one edge particle; or three reduced vertex generators.

The verifier constructs every basis element and every signed boundary from the rule
\[
\partial(h-h_0)=e(h)-e(h_0),
\]
checks \(\partial^2=0\) over the integers, and performs exact sparse Gaussian elimination over finite fields. The resulting boundary ranks are
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}\partial_3)
=(1770,4689,2911) \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_2,
\]
\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}\partial_3)
=(1770,4690,2911) \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_3.
\]
Substituting these ranks into \(\dim H_i=\dim C_i-\operatorname{rank}\partial_i-\operatorname{rank}\partial_{i+1}\) gives the two displayed Betti vectors.

For the integral top class, the supplied certificate is an explicit primitive vector \(z\in C_3\) supported on \(881\) basis elements, with every nonzero coefficient equal to \(1\) or \(-1\). Direct integer multiplication verifies \(\partial_3 z=0\). The mod-3 rank calculation gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3\ge 2911\) over \(\mathbb Q\), while the nonzero integral kernel vector gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3\le2911\). Hence the rational kernel has rank one. The integer kernel of a homomorphism between free abelian groups is saturated; since \(z\) is primitive, it generates that rank-one kernel. There is no \(C_4\), so \(H_3(B_3(H);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\).

## Relationship to prior work
An, Drummond-Cole, and Knudsen prove that the Świątkowski complex computes the homology of unordered graph configuration spaces and that the full-vertex reduced complex is quasi-isomorphic to it. Their paper supplies the chain model used here but does not state this Heawood three-particle calculation.

Maciążek and Sawicki develop homological methods for graph configuration spaces motivated by quantum statistics and, for simple graphs of degree at most three, show that second homology is generated by product cycles. That structural generation result does not determine the Heawood ranks above, the coefficient-characteristic jump, or the integral top class; the inspected article contains no Heawood instance.

An and Knudsen later give a generation theorem for second homology of planar graph braid groups. The Heawood graph is nonplanar, so that theorem does not imply the present computation; the inspected article also contains no Heawood instance.

## Limitations
The finite-field calculations prove the stated Betti vectors only for \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). The integral conclusion is only for \(H_3\). No Smith normal form for \(H_1\) or \(H_2\) is claimed. Literature searches and full-text inspections did not locate the exact Heawood result, but absence from searched sources is not a proof that no unindexed source contains it.
