# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The lower bound is reconstructed from the exact identity \(\sum_i\tau(\langle w_i,w_i\rangle_B)=\tau(1_B)\) for a shortest finite identity frame and the order bound \(\langle w_i,w_i\rangle_B\le1_B\). The upper bound follows independently from positivity of the inner product and of the trace. In the matrix model, the localization norm is a scaled Hilbert--Schmidt norm, the exact Hilbert--Schmidt/operator-norm ratio is attained by a maximal-rank partial isometry, and the frame-length formula follows from a rank lower bound plus an explicit block-partial-isometry construction.

## Originality
PASS. The focal recent paper was inspected at the level of the full statements of its finite-frame and localization results. It supplies the two separate ingredients but not the minimal-frame/localization-distortion theorem. Foundational module-frame literature and the closest open-access Heisenberg-module localization paper were also compared by implication. Database searches for localization norms, frame cardinality, Morita generator number, and the rectangular matrix formulas did not locate a covering statement.

## Value
PASS. The result connects the analytic distortion of trace localization to the algebraic complexity of finite projective generation. The rectangular matrix family is a natural Morita-equivalence test class and makes the integer obstruction exact for all matrix sizes, so the claim identifies a sharp structural relation rather than only a formal consequence of continuity.

## Closest literature and limitations
The nearest ingredients are Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.4 of arXiv:2609.37780v1, the general module-frame theory of Frank--Larson, and the Heisenberg-module localization/generator results of Austad--Enstad. None of the inspected statements gives the claimed shortest-frame bound or the exact rectangular matrix calibration. The universal lower bound need not be equality outside the matrix family. An older equivalent statement in finite-index or Morita-dimension terminology remains the main originality risk.
