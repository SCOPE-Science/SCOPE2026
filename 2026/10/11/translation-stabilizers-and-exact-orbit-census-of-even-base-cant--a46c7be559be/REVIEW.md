# Review: Translation stabilizers and exact orbit census of even-base Cantor spectra

## Correctness — PASS

For every even base and positive depth, the explicit factored Fourier transform proves that two frequency residues are orthogonal exactly when their first differing base digit has opposite parity. Saturation at \(2^n\) frequencies proves the full binary-tree classification; its independent digit choices give \(u^{2(2^n-1)}\) spectra. A period must have power-of-two order. The half-period fails the Fourier-zero condition if the base is divisible by four. Otherwise each power-of-two subgroup has pairwise-orthogonal cosets, and the saturated-prefix/coset argument counts every subgroup-fixed spectrum. Nested stabilizers and Burnside's lemma yield the stated exact strata and orbit counts. No step relies on numerical extrapolation. Independent exhaustive subset enumeration at small orders and 194 exact cyclotomic comparisons support the analytic argument.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk

The parity-labeled-tree idea is known from Dutkay–Han–Sun, *On the spectra of a Cantor measure*, arXiv:0804.4497, especially Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 5.1; it is not asserted as new in isolation. Their results concern spectra for an infinite base-four Cantor measure and recursion between them. Deng–Dong–Li (2019), Theorem 3.1, derives compatible-pair trees for infinite self-affine spectral measures, not finite cyclic orbit enumeration. Malikiosis, arXiv:2005.05800, studies spectrality and tiling in finite cyclic groups, including mask-polynomial zero sets, but its Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 establish existence/tiling implications, not the action of translations on the entire collection of spectra for a fixed finite Cantor digit set. The assessed new claim is the all-even-base arithmetic dichotomy and the complete fixed-subgroup, exact-stabilizer and orbit-count formulas. Comparisons with related published finite-set and Fourier-support classifications found different fixed sets and invariants. An unnoticed equivalent orbit computation, or a stronger specialized finite-tree theorem that immediately implies it, remains a residual historical-priority risk; this same-model assessment is not independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Translation of a frequency basis does not change its Fourier orthogonality, making orbit classes a canonical nonredundant measure of the family of all spectral partners. The explicit subgroup-fixed counts answer a natural symmetry-enumeration question for the finite approximants of a classical Cantor spectral measure. The contrast between full aperiodicity for \(4\mid b\) and all admissible power-of-two stabilizers when \(b\equiv2\pmod4\) and \(b>2\), with the single full spectrum at \(b=2\) is structural rather than an isolated numerical example. The complete exact orbit strata quantify the phenomenon at all depths; neither tree enumeration alone nor a bare Burnside restatement would carry this value.

## Scope and limitations

The result concerns finite cyclic spectra of the specific two-digit half-base set, not infinite fractal spectral completeness or arbitrary cyclic spectral pairs. The checkers examine finite dimensions only. The related infinite-measure spectral-labeling mechanism is prior art.
