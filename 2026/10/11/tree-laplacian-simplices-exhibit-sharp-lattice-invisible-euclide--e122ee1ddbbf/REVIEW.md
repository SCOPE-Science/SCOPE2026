# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. The barycentric facets of the full column-Laplacian simplex have exact normals \(L^\dagger e_i\); equality of all facet clearances yields the unique incenter and radius. A trace/Wiener argument with a proved tree-distance bound supplies the uniform lower bound. At least \((n+2)/2\) low-degree vertices force large cut currents and therefore a uniform upper bound. Direct zero-sum column solutions give the exact star and path formulas, and Riemann sums yield the exact asymptotic constants. The proof is all-order; finite exact checking is only corroborative.

## Originality
PASS. The primary Braun--Meyer source was inspected at the object definition and the entire tree-equivalence theorem; its conclusions are about lattice equivalence, Ehrhart properties and reflexivity rather than intrinsic Euclidean inradii. A same-object sequel and two plausible graph/simplex formulations were also inspected and compared by metric and implication. An inradius identity restricted to vertex-transitive graphs would not imply any tree-wide result, because nontrivial trees are not vertex-transitive. Generic pseudoinverse facet geometry is excluded from the novelty claim; the substantive finding is the sharp all-tree exponent window and the exact path/star witnesses.

## Value
PASS. All tree Laplacian simplices are lattice equivalent, yet the canonical Euclidean radii of their path and star representatives separate by a factor growing as \(n^{3/2}\). The universal bounds show these powers are optimal for the full class. This supplies a structural bridge between metric geometry, tree currents and Wiener bounds, explaining a natural phenomenon not visible to lattice invariants.

## Closest literature and limitations
Braun--Meyer, *Laplacian Simplices* (2017), Proposition 4.1; Meyer--Pllaha, *Laplacian Simplices II* (2018); Devriendt's effective-resistance simplex construction (2020); related simplex-induced metrics on Laplacian lattices. The claim is for unweighted trees in the inherited zero-sum Euclidean metric, and it does not assert exact fixed-order path/star extremizers. Other literature terminology and unindexed small-radius tables remain residual originality risks.
