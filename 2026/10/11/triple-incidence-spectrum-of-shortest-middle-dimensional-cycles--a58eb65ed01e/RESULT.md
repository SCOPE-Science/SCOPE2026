# Triple-incidence spectrum of shortest middle-dimensional cycles in the 15-vertex projective-plane-like 8-manifold
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the vertex-transitive fifteen-vertex Brehm--Kühnel combinatorial \(8\)-manifold \(M^8_{15}\) in the labeling of Köhler and Lutz. Let \(\mathcal B\) be the family of vertex supports of nonzero classes in \(H_4(K;\mathbf F_2)\) having minimum possible vertex support. Then \(\mathcal B\) consists exactly of the \(490\) complements of facets of \(K\), equivalently the \(490\) missing \(5\)-simplices. Every \(B\in\mathcal B\) has six vertices, and \(K[B]=\partial\Delta^5\).

The family \(\mathcal B\) is a \(2\)-\((15,6,70)\) design: every vertex belongs to \(196\) blocks and every unordered vertex pair belongs to exactly \(70\) blocks. For an unordered vertex triple \(T\), define
\[
\lambda(T)=|\{B\in\mathcal B:T\subseteq B\}|.
\]
The complete distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
\lambda&4&13&16&19&22&25&28&31\\
\#T&5&30&60&150&60&60&10&80.
\end{array}
\]
The five triples with \(\lambda(T)=4\) are
\[
\{1,6,8\},\ \{2,4,9\},\ \{3,7,11\},\ \{5,10,15\},\ \{12,13,14\}.
\]
They are pairwise disjoint, partition the fifteen vertices, and form one orbit under the published vertex-transitive \(A_5\) action. Thus the first nonuniform incidence layer of the shortest middle-dimensional homology carriers recovers an invariant five-block partition.

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the specific \(A_5\)-symmetric Brehm--Kühnel complex reconstructed from the fourteen published facet-orbit representatives and the two published permutation generators. Homology is simplicial homology with coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). A support is the set of vertices occurring in a simplicial cycle. The incidence assertions concern the six-vertex supports themselves, not oriented chains.

The identification with \(\mathbb H P^2\) is not needed for the combinatorial proof. Later work proves that this complex is PL homeomorphic to \(\mathbb H P^2\).

## Proof
The published fourteen facet-orbit representatives have orbit sizes
\[
60,60,60,15,15,30,30,60,60,20,10,30,30,10,
\]
which sum to \(490\). Generating these orbits under the published \(A_5\) permutations reconstructs exactly \(490\) facets and the face vector
\[
(15,105,455,1365,3003,4515,4230,2205,490).
\]
In particular every five-vertex subset is a face. Among the \(\binom{15}{6}=5005\) six-vertex subsets, exactly \(4515\) are faces; the remaining \(490\) are exactly the complements of the \(490\) facets.

An exact binary chain computation gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(\partial_4)=1001,\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\partial_5)=2001,
\]
where \(C_4\) has dimension \(3003\). Hence
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}H_4(K;\mathbf F_2)=3003-1001-2001=1.
\]
For each missing six-set \(B\), all of its five-subsets are faces, so the six \(4\)-simplices on \(B\) form the cycle \(\partial\Delta^5\). Reduction against a row-echelon basis for \(\operatorname{im}\partial_5\) verifies that each of these \(490\) cycles is nonzero in homology.

No nonzero simplicial \(4\)-cycle can be supported on at most five vertices. On exactly six vertices, the cycle equations for the complete \(4\)-skeleton force any nonzero \(4\)-cycle to be the full boundary \(\partial\Delta^5\). Therefore six is the minimum support size, and the missing six-sets give all minimum-support nonzero classes.

Counting incidences in these \(490\) supports gives vertex multiplicity \(196\) and pair multiplicity \(70\) uniformly. Exhausting all \(\binom{15}{3}=455\) triples gives the stated eight-value spectrum. Exactly five triples have multiplicity \(4\); they are the listed triples. Their union has fifteen vertices and they are pairwise disjoint. Applying all sixty elements generated by the published \(A_5\) permutations preserves the five-triple set and acts transitively on it.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` contains the fourteen facet-orbit representatives and the two published permutation generators. It regenerates the order-\(60\) group, verifies all orbit sizes and the full face vector, computes the binary boundary ranks, checks every missing six-set against the \(5\)-boundary span, exhausts all vertex, pair, and triple incidences, and checks the five-triple orbit and partition. A replay of the exact packaged file prints

`VERIFY_OK group=60 orbit_sizes=60,60,60,15,15,30,30,60,60,20,10,30,30,10 f=15,105,455,1365,3003,4515,4230,2205,490 H4_F2=1 ranks_d4_d5=1001,2001 missing6=490 design=2-(15,6,70) triple_spectrum=4:5,13:30,16:60,19:150,22:60,25:60,28:10,31:80 min_triples=1-6-8;2-4-9;3-7-11;5-10-15;12-13-14`.

## Relationship to prior work
Brehm and Kühnel introduced fifteen-vertex triangulations of \(8\)-manifolds like the quaternionic projective plane. Köhler and Lutz give the exact vertex-transitive \(A_5\) model used here, including its face vector, facet-orbit representatives, and group generators, and record complementarity and tightness. Balagopalan gives a detailed combinatorial construction of the associated family of \(490\) six-sets as a special maximal intersecting family. Gorodkov later proves that the Brehm--Kühnel complex is PL homeomorphic to \(\mathbb H P^2\).

Those results supply the object and its global structure. The statement here refines that structure at the incidence level of the minimum-support middle-dimensional homology carriers: the uniform pair parameter \(70\), the full eight-level triple spectrum, and especially the unique five-triple minimum stratum and its partition of the vertex set are not consequences merely of the face vector, complementarity, or the existence of the \(A_5\) action.

## Limitations
This is an exact theorem about one labeled Brehm--Kühnel triangulation, not a classification over all fifteen-vertex triangulations of \(\mathbb H P^2\). The homology-support statement is over \(\mathbf F_2\); no claim is made about minimum \(\ell^1\)-norm or geometric area of integral cycles. The theorem is combinatorial and mod-two; it does not assert an integral systolic classification, a geometric-area minimum, or a classification over the other fifteen-vertex triangulations of \(\mathbb H P^2\).
