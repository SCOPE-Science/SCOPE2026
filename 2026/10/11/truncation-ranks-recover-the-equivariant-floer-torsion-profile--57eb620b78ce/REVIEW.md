# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Huang defines the Borel complex over \(R=\mathbb F_2[[q]]\) and identifies the \(R/(q^N)\)-coefficient invariant as its chain-level truncation. Because the complex is finite free over the discrete valuation ring \(R\), the universal coefficient theorem applies. For each cyclic torsion summand \(R/(q^a)\), tensor and first Tor each have \(\mathbb F_2\)-dimension \(\min(a,N)\), giving the factor \(2\). Localization fixes the free rank at \(2^{c-1}\). The finite-difference reconstruction then follows exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The source paper introduces all finite truncations but contains no discussion of torsion, rank as a function of \(N\), discrete concavity, or inversion of the rank sequence. Hendricks--Lipshitz--Sarkar and Seidel--Smith provide localization and torsion-module background but no all-truncation tomography. Exact published-finding corpus searches and broader literature searches for truncation ranks, torsion exponents, universal-coefficient formulas, and eventual-slope formulations found no covering statement. The residual risk is that this DVR calculation may be implicit folklore in equivariant Floer computations.

## Value
PASS. Huang's theorem supplies one finite invariant for every truncation order, so it is natural to ask whether these values collectively retain the torsion information of the full completed theory. The result answers this exactly: scalar dimensions already determine every ungraded \(q\)-primary exponent and give immediately checkable concavity, parity, and stabilization constraints. This is a structural diagnostic for computations rather than a renaming or isolated numerical example.
