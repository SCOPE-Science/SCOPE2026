# Two adjacent affine layers force integral homology in two neighboring degrees

## Finding
For every \(n\ge2\) and \(q\ge2\), let \(K_{n,q}\) consist of all colorful partial assignments on \([n]\times\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) with fewer than \(n\) assigned coordinates, together with the full assignments satisfying \(\sum_i x_i\in\{0,1\}\pmod q\). Then
\[
\widetilde H_j(K_{n,q};\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=n-1,\\
\mathbb Z^{(q-2)q^{n-1}-(q-1)^n+1},&j=n-2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
All groups are torsion-free. The lower exponent is zero for \(n=2\) or \(q=2\), and positive when \(n,q\ge3\). In particular \(\widetilde H_1(K_{3,3};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and \(\widetilde H_2(K_{3,3};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\).

## Assumptions and scope
Set \(A=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) with no primeness requirement. The vertex set is \(\{(i,a):i\in[n],a\in A\}\). A face contains at most one vertex of any color \(i\). Every face on at most \(n-1\) colors occurs. A size-\(n\) face with entries \((x_1,\dots,x_n)\) occurs if and only if their sum in \(A\) is \(0\) or \(1\). Empty faces are included. Let \(J=A_1*\cdots*A_n\) be the complete join of \(n\) discrete \(q\)-point sets. Then \(K_{n,q}\subseteq J\) and both complexes have exactly the same skeleton through dimension \(n-2\).

## Proof
Orient every maximal face by increasing coordinate index. First, any codimension-one face of \(J\) has precisely two completions in \(K_{n,q}\), one of sum \(0\) and one of sum \(1\). Their oriented boundary coefficients at that face have the same sign, because the deleted coordinate index is the same. Hence a top-dimensional integral cycle has coefficients \(c_x\) satisfying \(c_x+c_y=0\) whenever the two allowed full assignments \(x,y\) agree in all but one coordinate.

The graph of these incidences is connected. Indeed, starting at any sum-\(0\) tuple, increasing coordinate \(i\) by one and then decreasing a different coordinate \(j\) by one traverses two edges and effects translation by \(e_i-e_j\). Such translations generate the entire sum-\(0\) subgroup of \(A^n\) for every \(n\ge2\). Every sum-\(1\) tuple is adjacent to a sum-\(0\) tuple. The incidence graph is bipartite by sum level; therefore all coefficients on the sum-\(0\) vertices must be a common integer \(c\), and all on sum-\(1\) vertices must be \(-c\). Conversely this gives an integral cycle. Since no simplices have dimension \(n\), this proves \(\widetilde H_{n-1}(K_{n,q};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Exactly the same argument over every field proves that the top homology has dimension one in every characteristic.

The colorful join \(J\) has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{(q-1)^n}\) in degree \(n-1\) and zero in every other degree: each discrete \(q\)-point factor has reduced \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z^{q-1}\), and the integral join Künneth formula applies. The pair \((J,K_{n,q})\) has only one relative nonzero cellular chain group, in degree \(n-1\), freely generated by the excluded \((q-2)q^{n-1}\) full transversals. Its long exact reduced homology sequence is therefore
\[
0\to\widetilde H_{n-1}(K_{n,q})\to\mathbb Z^{(q-1)^n}\to\mathbb Z^{(q-2)q^{n-1}}\to\widetilde H_{n-2}(K_{n,q})\to0,
\]
with every remaining reduced homology group zero. Taking ranks and using the top rank one gives the claimed formula for the degree \(n-2\) Betti number.

To rule out torsion in degree \(n-2\), repeat this same exact-sequence argument over \(\mathbb F_p\) for any prime \(p\). The incidence graph still gives top dimension one, and the join and relative chain ranks are unchanged, so the degree \(n-2\) dimension equals its rational rank for every prime. The universal coefficient theorem then excludes \(p\)-torsion for every \(p\) (the preceding lower integral homology group vanishes; when \(n=2\), reduced \(H_0\) is automatically free). Top homology was already identified integrally, completing the proof.

For \(n,q\ge3\), the lower rank is positive: at \(n=3\) it equals \((q-1)(q-2)\); increasing \(n\) multiplies the first term by \(q\) and the second by \(q-1\), so the strict inequality persists.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) reconstructs all faces and signed simplicial boundary matrices for \(2\le n\le4\), \(2\le q\le5\); computes exact ranks over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\); checks two completions for every codimension-one face; and verifies connectivity and the predicted Betti numbers in twelve cases. Running \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) prints \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). These finite tests are independent checks of the formulas; the all-parameter conclusion follows from the connected-incidence-graph and relative-homology proof above, not from extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Ayzenberg and Khoroshavkina, arXiv:2602.08694v1, develop generalized inflations and prove wedge decompositions under a global flabbiness hypothesis. Here local partial-coordinate restrictions have two completions, whereas the global completion constraint removes most full transversals when \(q>2\). The result determines the topology of this explicit nonflabby affine two-level family; it does not invoke the flabby theorem. Single-level Latin hypercube complexes have a different top homology profile (zero in degree \(n-1\)); adding the adjacent level creates a nonzero top class, and for \(n,q\ge3\) also leaves nonzero homology one degree lower. This distinction is not supplied by the single-layer calculation. The complete colorful join used in the proof is classical, and no novelty is claimed for its homology.

## Limitations
The theorem identifies integral homology, not homotopy types, attaching maps or multiplicative cohomology. Adjacent sum levels \(0,1\) are required: choosing two values with nonunit difference modulo composite \(q\) can change the incidence graph. It does not assert sheaf flabbiness, equivariance, or a classification of arbitrary unions of Latin hypercubes. Equivalent unindexed formulations in coding theory, contextuality or combinatorial topology are possible; independent audit has not been performed.
