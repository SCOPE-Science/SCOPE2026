# Two primitive homology directions exhaust the four-vertex \(H_2\)-systoles of a 12-vertex \(S^2\times S^2\)
## Finding
Let \(K\) be the 12-vertex vertex-transitive triangulation `manifold_4_12_124_1` of \(S^2\times S^2\) in the Köhler--Lutz/Lutz census. Among integral simplicial \(2\)-cycles representing nonzero classes in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), the minimum vertex-support size is four. Exactly \(60\) four-vertex supports attain this minimum: they are precisely the four-sets whose four triangular faces are present but whose tetrahedron is absent. Up to sign, their tetrahedral-boundary cycles represent exactly two primitive homology classes, with \(30\) supports in each class. An explicit \(240\)-element simplicial automorphism group acts transitively on all \(60\) shortest supports, so the two shortest homology directions lie in one symmetry orbit.

Thus the vertex-support \(2\)-systole of this triangulation is \(4\). The \(60\) minimizers do not spread through many lattice directions in middle homology: they occupy only two primitive rays, with equal support multiplicity, although the simplicial symmetry is transitive on the entire minimum layer.

## Assumptions and scope
The complex \(K\) is the explicit 12-vertex, 72-facet census complex `manifold_4_12_124_1`. The published census identifies it topologically as \(S^2\times S^2\) and records middle homology rank \(2\), so throughout this result
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
For an integral simplicial \(2\)-cycle, its vertex support is the set of vertices occurring in triangles with nonzero coefficient. The minimum is taken over cycles whose homology class is nonzero. A “four-vertex support” means the support set, not a choice of coefficient multiple; on such a support the cycle group is generated by the oriented tetrahedral boundary.

The facet reconstruction gives
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(12,60,160,180,72).
\]
The claim is specific to this census triangulation; it is not asserted for every 12-vertex triangulation of \(S^2\times S^2\).

## Proof
A nonzero simplicial \(2\)-cycle cannot be supported on fewer than four vertices. On exactly four vertices there are at most four oriented triangular faces. The only integral linear relation among the boundaries of all four faces of a tetrahedron is, up to an integer multiple, the oriented tetrahedral boundary. Consequently a four-vertex nonzero homology class can occur only when all four triangles are faces of \(K\) and the corresponding tetrahedron is absent.

Exhausting the \(\binom{12}{4}=495\) four-sets in the published facet complex gives exactly \(60\) such missing tetrahedra. Let \(c_1,c_2\) be the two integer \(2\)-cochains embedded in `verify.py`. Direct checking on all \(180\) tetrahedra gives
\[
\delta c_1=\delta c_2=0,
\]
so they define homomorphisms on \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). For each of the \(60\) oriented missing-tetrahedron boundaries \(z\), exhaustive evaluation gives
\[
(\langle c_1,z\rangle,\langle c_2,z\rangle)\in\{(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)\}.
\]
The signed counts are \(15,15,25,5\), respectively, and therefore the unoriented counts are \(30\) and \(30\).

Both \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) occur. Hence the map
\[
\Phi:H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad [z]\longmapsto(\langle c_1,z\rangle,\langle c_2,z\rangle)
\]
is surjective. Since the published topology gives \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), a surjective endomorphism onto \(\mathbb Z^2\) is an isomorphism. It follows that every one of the \(60\) boundaries is nonzero and primitive, and up to sign they occupy exactly two homology classes. Together with the four-vertex lower bound, this proves the systolic statement.

Finally, `verify.py` checks two explicit vertex permutations, closes the generated group, obtains exactly \(240\) simplicial automorphisms, and verifies that the orbit of one minimum support is all \(60\) supports. This proves the stated transitivity and shows that an automorphism interchanges the two primitive shortest directions.

## Relationship to prior work
Lassmann and Sparla classify three centrally symmetric cyclic 12-vertex triangulations of \(S^2\times S^2\), compute their automorphism groups, and discuss middle-homology generators and intersection forms. Their maximally symmetric model has automorphism group of order \(240\). The later Köhler--Lutz census gives the exact facet data used here; the associated Lutz tables identify `manifold_4_12_124_1` as \(S^2\times S^2\) with middle homology rank \(2\).

The inspected earlier paper contains structural descriptions of the triangulations, including octahedral subcomplexes and homology/intersection arguments, but no complete classification of four-vertex nonzero integral \(H_2\)-cycles, no count \(60=30+30\) of shortest supports by primitive homology direction, and no statement that the minimum-support layer is a single order-\(240\) symmetry orbit. published-finding corpus searches using the exact object name, missing-tetrahedron terminology, minimum-support and systolic formulations, the numerical \(60\) and \(30+30\) spectrum, and symmetry aliases found no covering result.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact theorem about one highly symmetric triangulation. The support metric counts vertices, not triangle area, number of triangles, or a geometric Riemannian mass. The two cocycles classify the shortest layer because the published topology supplies \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\); the result does not claim that longer cycles occupy only these two homology rays. An unindexed historical computation of the same finite census remains a residual originality risk.
