# Same-model scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Filakovský's Theorem 1 (arXiv:2602.11365) provides reduced rational homology concentrated in degree three with rank \(\binom{(m-1)(n-1)}2\). The additional representation statement follows from an exact signed augmented Lefschetz calculation. The proof distinguishes the two matching mechanisms under a row reflection and counts their overlap, and it identifies the unique double edge-orbit joining the central two vertex-orbits under the half-turn. These yield all three nonidentity traces; direct square-pair fixed-point counts match them. Since the group is \(C_2\times C_2\), equality of the four rational characters proves module isomorphism. Executable finite verification is supporting evidence, not a replacement for these arguments.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Full-text comparison of Filakovský's paper (Theorem 1 and Section 3) and Chandrakar–Hazra–Rout–Singh's grid cut-complex paper (main theorems and proofs) found nonequivariant homotopy classifications, not the three nonidentity reflection traces or the pair-of-plaquettes module. Targeted scientific-record searches for the equivalent formulation, its dual cut-complex form, stronger group actions, and exact character values yielded no covering statement. Published calculations of an unrelated ten-cut grid complex have different parameters and do not imply the result. An as-yet-unindexed equivalent representation calculation remains a residual risk; unsuccessful searches alone are not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** A wedge-of-spheres classification supplies dimension but no automorphism-group action. Here a natural infinite family has a complete four-character classification, with a geometrically simple abstract permutation representation. The result explains reflection traces and resolves the independent sign multiplicities uniformly; these quantities are not recoverable from rank alone. For nonsquare even rectangles the group is the full grid automorphism group. The restriction to the distinguished subgroup on squares and the absence of an integral canonical basis are genuine limits, not claimed advances.
