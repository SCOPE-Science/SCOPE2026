# Same-model review

## Correctness
PASS. Averaging over coordinate sign changes reduces every quadratic pullback to a diagonal one without worsening either comparison constant. The diagonal problem is exactly the strictly concave scalar profile \(F_{a,b}(t)=a t^{2/p}+b(1-t)^{2/q}\). Normalizing its endpoint minimum forces \(a,b\ge1\); any nonidentity normalized diagonal form is strictly larger at the interior maximizer of the identity profile, so the identity form is the unique diagonal optimum up to scale. Strict concavity gives the unique interior contact parameter, and an off-diagonal sign test at that contact pair proves uniqueness of the circle up to scale.

## Originality
PASS. The inspected archival Musielak--Orlicz source defines the relevant Luxemburg class and discusses Banach--Mazur distance, but its theorems concern equilateral dimension. Searches under Musielak--Orlicz, Nakano, variable-exponent, mixed-power, optimal-ellipse, and anisotropic-superellipse terminology found no equivalent theorem for \(2<p<q\). The closest exact-distance record concerns the different norm \((\|z\|_p^2+\lambda\|z\|_q^2)^{1/2}\), and the closest same-object result covers only the boundary with one exponent equal to \(2\).

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives an exact two-parameter Hilbert-distortion benchmark for the simplest genuinely anisotropic finite-dimensional Musielak--Orlicz family above the Hilbert exponent. The structural point is that coordinate anisotropy does not require an anisotropic optimal ellipse: the Euclidean circle is uniquely optimal, with the exact distortion and contact point determined by one intrinsic equation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kobos, arXiv:1305.6288, supplies the finite-dimensional Musielak--Orlicz Luxemburg framework and Banach--Mazur context but no optimal-ellipse formula. The result is restricted to two coordinates and \(2<p<q<\infty\); the subquadratic and mixed-side regimes are not addressed. An equivalent statement under different convex-body terminology remains a residual literature risk.
