# Unit-variance disintegration of a two-variable extremal Nevanlinna family

## Finding

Let \(\varphi\) be a nonconstant one-variable real rational Herglotz--Nevanlinna function and define
\[
h(z,w)
=
-\frac{1}{\varphi(z)+w-\frac1w}.
\]
The recent source proves that \(h\) has an extremal representing Nevanlinna measure \(\mu\).

That measure admits the following probability-kernel form:
\[
\mu(dt,dy)
=
\pi\,dt\,\kappa_t(dy)
\]
for Lebesgue-a.e. \(t\in\mathbb R\).

Put
\[
D(t)
=
\sqrt{\varphi(t)^2+4}
\]
and
\[
g_\pm(t)
=
\frac{-\varphi(t)\pm D(t)}2.
\]
Then
\[
\kappa_t
=
\alpha_+(t)\delta_{g_+(t)}
+
\alpha_-(t)\delta_{g_-(t)},
\]
where
\[
\alpha_\pm(t)
=
\frac{g_\pm(t)^2}{1+g_\pm(t)^2}
=
\frac12
\left(
1\mp\frac{\varphi(t)}{D(t)}
\right).
\]

The fiber law has exact moments
\[
\mathbb E_{\kappa_t}Y
=
-\varphi(t),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_t}(Y)
=
1,
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_{\kappa_t}
\left[
(Y+\varphi(t))^3
\right]
=
\varphi(t).
\]
Thus \(\varphi(t)\) is simultaneously the negative conditional mean and, because the variance equals one, the conditional skewness.

Moreover, each real boundary slice of \(h\) is exactly the Cauchy transform of the corresponding fiber law:
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{d\kappa_t(y)}{y-w}
=
-\frac{1}{\varphi(t)+w-\frac1w}
=
h(t,w),
\qquad
w\in\mathbb H.
\]

If
\[
m_k(t)
=
\int_{\mathbb R}
y^k\,d\kappa_t(y),
\]
then every conditional moment is generated by
\[
m_0(t)=1,
\qquad
m_1(t)=-\varphi(t),
\]
and
\[
m_k(t)
=
-\varphi(t)m_{k-1}(t)
+
m_{k-2}(t),
\qquad
k\ge2.
\]

In particular, the first-coordinate marginal of the full two-variable measure is exactly
\[
\pi\,dt.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The Herglotz--Nevanlinna function, representing measure, finite exceptional set, graph parametrization, and weights are those of Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 2.9 of arXiv:2610.02476v1.

All fiber statements are asserted for
\[
t\in\mathbb R\setminus S,
\]
where \(S\) is the finite exceptional set from the source. Since \(S\) has Lebesgue measure zero, the disintegration is unchanged if the kernel is defined arbitrarily on \(S\).

The statement is a structural description of the specific bidegree-\((m,2)\) extremal family. It does not classify arbitrary extremal Nevanlinna measures or arbitrary rational Herglotz--Nevanlinna functions.

## Proof

The source structure theorem gives
\[
\mu
=
\pi
\sum_{j=1}^{2}
(\gamma_j)_*
\left(
\alpha_j(t)\,dt
\right)
\]
for the family under consideration, with no vertical component.

Theorem 3.1 establishes
\[
g_1+g_2=-\varphi,
\qquad
g_1g_2=-1,
\]
and
\[
\alpha_j
=
\frac{g_j^2}{1+g_j^2}.
\]
It also proves
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1.
\]

For real \(t\notin S\), the two graph values are the distinct real roots of
\[
y^2+\varphi(t)y-1=0.
\]
Labelling them by sign gives
\[
g_\pm(t)
=
\frac{-\varphi(t)\pm\sqrt{\varphi(t)^2+4}}2.
\]
Since
\[
g_+g_-=-1,
\]
the source weights simplify to
\[
\alpha_\pm
=
\frac12
\left(
1\mp
\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt{\varphi^2+4}}
\right).
\]

Because the weights sum to one, define
\[
\kappa_t
=
\alpha_+\delta_{g_+}
+
\alpha_-\delta_{g_-}.
\]
The structure formula becomes
\[
\mu(dt,dy)
=
\pi\,dt\,\kappa_t(dy).
\]
This immediately shows that the first-coordinate marginal is
\[
\pi\,dt.
\]

To compute the conditional mean, use
\[
g_-= -\frac1{g_+}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\kappa_t}Y
&=
\frac{g_+^3}{1+g_+^2}
+
\frac{g_-}{1+g_+^2}
\\
&=
g_+-\frac1{g_+}
\\
&=
g_++g_-
\\
&=
-\varphi.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every support point satisfies
\[
Y^2+\varphi Y-1=0.
\]
Taking conditional expectation gives
\[
\mathbb E Y^2
=
1-\varphi\mathbb E Y
=
1+\varphi^2.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y)
=
1+\varphi^2-\varphi^2
=
1.
\]

Multiplying the support identity by \(Y^{k-2}\) and taking expectation yields
\[
m_k
=
-\varphi m_{k-1}+m_{k-2},
\qquad
k\ge2.
\]
In particular,
\[
m_2=1+\varphi^2
\]
and
\[
m_3=-\varphi^3-2\varphi.
\]
Expanding around the mean \(-\varphi\) gives
\[
\mathbb E
\left[
(Y+\varphi)^3
\right]
=
\varphi.
\]

Finally,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{d\kappa_t(y)}{y-w}
&=
\frac{\alpha_+}{g_+-w}
+
\frac{\alpha_-}{g_--w}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using
\[
\alpha_+g_-+\alpha_-g_+=0,
\]
together with
\[
g_+g_-=-1
\]
and
\[
g_++g_-=-\varphi,
\]
the right-hand side reduces to
\[
-\frac{w}{w^2+\varphi w-1}
=
-\frac{1}{\varphi+w-\frac1w}.
\]
This is precisely
\[
h(t,w).
\]

## Verification

The primary preprint was inspected at its general measure structure formula, its proof of Theorem 3.1, and its characterization of the bidegree-\((m,2)\) family. The source explicitly gives the two graph support, the product identity
\[
g_1g_2=-1,
\]
the sum identity
\[
g_1+g_2=-\varphi,
\]
and the weights
\[
\alpha_j=\frac{g_j^2}{1+g_j^2}.
\]

The source also proves
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1,
\]
which is the exact normalization needed for the probability disintegration.

The conditional mean, variance, third centered moment, moment recurrence, and fiber Cauchy-transform identity were each recomputed algebraically from these formulas. No numerical experiment or finite enumeration is used.

A closely related Clark-measure paper was inspected at its primary structure formula for rational inner functions on the bidisk. It likewise describes graph-supported measures and derivative weights, but the inspected material does not provide the present half-plane family, the unit-variance fiber law, or its moment recurrence.

## Relationship to prior work

Fagrell and Luger derive the explicit graph-supported representing measure and prove its extremality. Their proof uses the identities
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha_1}{g_1^2}
+
\frac{\alpha_2}{g_2^2}
=
1,
\]
together with
\[
g_1g_2=-1.
\]
They do not formulate the measure as a Lebesgue mixture of probability laws, identify a constant conditional variance, compute conditional skewness, or state that each boundary slice is exactly the Cauchy transform of the corresponding conditional law.

Earlier Clark-measure work gives graph parametrizations and derivative-weight structure for rational inner functions on the bidisk. That framework is an important analogue of the source construction, but it does not imply the displayed unit-variance law without the special quadratic relation and weights of the present family.

The new description therefore gives a probabilistic and moment-theoretic normal form for the source's extremal family:
\[
\text{base Lebesgue measure}
\quad+\quad
\text{two-point unit-variance fibers}.
\]
The parameter \(\varphi\) is recovered directly from the first or third centered conditional moment.

## Limitations

The probability-kernel description is specific to the source family
\[
h(z,w)
=
-\frac{1}{\varphi(z)+w-\frac1w}.
\]
It is not asserted for arbitrary bidegree-\((m,2)\) extremal measures.

The global measure \(\mu\) is generally infinite; only each conditional fiber \(\kappa_t\) is a probability measure.

The conditional formulas hold outside the source's finite exceptional set, which is sufficient for the measure disintegration.

The result does not by itself give a new extremality criterion; it reorganizes the exact extremal measure into a form that exposes its fixed conditional variance and moment recursion.
