# Review of Unit-variance disintegration of a two-variable extremal Nevanlinna family

## Correctness

PASS. The source structure theorem writes the representing measure as the sum of two graph pushforwards with weights \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\), and Theorem 3.1 proves
\[
\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1,
\qquad
g_1+g_2=-\varphi,
\qquad
g_1g_2=-1,
\qquad
\alpha_j=\frac{g_j^2}{1+g_j^2}.
\]
Thus the graph weights are genuinely conditional probabilities and the first marginal is \(\pi\) times Lebesgue measure.

The quadratic support identity
\[
Y^2+\varphi Y-1=0
\]
then gives the exact mean, variance, and moment recurrence. Direct partial fractions give the fiber Cauchy-transform identity. Every assertion is an algebraic consequence of source formulas that were inspected in their full theorem context.

## Originality

PASS. The fresh source computes support and weights and uses two weighted identities to establish extremality, but does not state the probability disintegration, constant conditional variance, conditional skewness, moment recurrence, or Cauchy-transform interpretation of each fiber.

The most relevant prior Clark-measure literature was compared at its graph-support and derivative-weight structure formula. It supplies a broad analogue but not this half-plane quadratic family or the unit-variance conditional law.

Targeted searches under conditional variance, probability disintegration, two-point fibers, Cauchy-transform fibers, and the exact source identifier did not locate an implication-equivalent statement.

## Value

PASS. The source's weights look initially like technical residue factors. The finding converts them into a canonical probabilistic normal form: every fiber has total mass one and the same variance, while the entire source parameter is carried by the conditional mean and skewness.

The identity
\[
h(t,w)
=
\int\frac{d\kappa_t(y)}{y-w}
\]
also makes the two-variable extremal into an exact family of one-dimensional Cauchy transforms. This provides a reusable bridge between the source's graph geometry, its Nevanlinna representation, and finite-support moment methods, rather than merely restating the residue formula.
