# Same-model mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reduces the spatial numerical radius over either scalar field to a smooth simplex maximum, uses weighted arithmetic–geometric mean to prove uniqueness of its unperturbed maximizer, computes the negative-definite tangent Hessian, and applies an analytic critical-point argument. Substituting the optimized tangent perturbation and the circulant factorization \((\alpha I+\beta S)(\alpha I+\beta S^{-1})=I-\alpha\beta L\) produces the precise Green-kernel quadratic coefficient. Fourier eigenvalues of the cycle Laplacian establish the exact zero-mode classification, including the two-edge-cycle convention. The independent computational replay checks examples but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
Chang and Wang (2012, doi:10.1016/j.jmaa.2012.04.039) address permutations maximizing numerical radius for weighted cyclic matrices in complex Hilbert space. Chien and Sheu (2013, doi:10.7153/oam-07-11) treat numerical radii of special Hilbert-space weighted shifts. Their results do not imply the \(p\)-dependent all-exponent Green-kernel formula, though the \(p=2\) specialization can be obtained from conventional symmetric-eigenvalue perturbation. Searches for equivalent weighted-cycle, spatial \(\ell_p\)-numerical-radius, and discrete Green-function formulations found no direct general antecedent. Residual risk: the 2012 archival PDF was not read in full, and historical nonlinear spectral-perturbation work may encode the same result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result computes a complete local invariant over all perturbation directions and finite dimensions, identifies an exact Hilbert-only flat mode and predicts structural arrangement sensitivity independent of the exponent. This is directly motivated by the classical weighted cyclic-shift permutation problem and is neither a one-point check nor merely a numerical estimate. The theorem is local in positive weights, does not solve arbitrary global permutation maximization, and asserts no uniqueness of complex maximizing phases.
