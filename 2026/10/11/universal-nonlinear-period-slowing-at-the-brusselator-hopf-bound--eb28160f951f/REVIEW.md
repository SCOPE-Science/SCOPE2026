# Scientific review: universal nonlinear period slowing at the Brusselator Hopf boundary

## Correctness — PASS

The analytic planar system has a unique positive equilibrium and a transverse Hopf crossing at \(B=1+A^2\). The exact scalar reduction and order-two and order-three Fourier solvability identities were rederived from the original vector field and verified by the accompanying executable checker. The amplitude relation \(\mu=(A^2+2)\rho^2/4+O_A(\rho^4)\) has strictly positive coefficient and so gives a nondegenerate supercritical stable branch. The frequency correction and its inversion are consistent, and \(4A^4-7A^2+4=4(A^2-7/8)^2+15/16\) proves strict positivity without numerical inference. Standard analytic Hopf reduction supplies local existence, local uniqueness up to phase, and the stated remainder. The constants need not be uniform as \(A\) approaches zero or infinity.

The 1984 and 1973 historical full texts remained unavailable, so undiscovered earlier equivalence remains a genuine residual priority risk, not evidence of originality by failed search alone. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The oscillator's linearized determinant is precisely \(A^2\), independent of the varied feed \(B\), so linear frequency cannot explain the post-onset period response. The finding supplies a closed first nonlinear coefficient and proves an unexpectedly uniform sign across the entire admissible \(A>0\) range. This distinguishes local nonlinear clock slowing from the large-amplitude period jumps studied numerically and provides a parameter-wide analytic direction-of-change test for weak chemical oscillations. A local derivative is not a theorem of global period monotonicity.
