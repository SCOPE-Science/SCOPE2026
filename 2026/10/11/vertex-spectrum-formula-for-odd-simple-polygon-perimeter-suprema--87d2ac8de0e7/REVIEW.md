# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Lángi's materially inspected theorem supplies the sharp triangle upper reduction for every odd \(m\). The new finite reduction is then a direct convexity argument on the product polytope: each pair distance is convex, their maximum is convex, and a convex function on a polytope has a maximum at an extreme point. The reverse inequality is reconstructed rather than assumed: any nondegenerate triangle admits simple odd zigzag polygons whose \(m-2\) alternating edges converge to its longest side while the last two edges converge to the other sides. Repeated vertex triples cannot maximize because a diameter pair plus any off-line vertex gives a strict triangle-inequality improvement. The phase formula is an exact comparison of finitely many affine functions. The regular-pentagon corollary is checked symbolically.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest source proves the continuous triangle reduction but does not state that polygonal containers reduce to finitely many extreme-vertex triples, does not describe the odd-index spectrum as a finite affine envelope, and does not state eventual stabilization to diameter triples. Its examples cover a square and a disk, not the regular pentagon. Searches over equivalent formulations, the broader unit-disk literature, exact-form queries, and implication-level comparisons found no covering statement. Residual risk remains that a computational-geometry note or unpublished observation may have recorded the same elementary finite reduction without using Lángi's terminology.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem converts a continuous extremal problem on a polygonal container into an exact finite computation over vertex triples and simultaneously describes the dependence on every odd side count. The eventual diameter phase is a structural consequence that is not visible from solving one fixed \(m\), while the regular pentagon gives a closed all-odd benchmark. This is more than a single parameter substitution or a numerical example: it supplies a reusable finite reduction and spectrum description for every convex polygon.
