# Scientific assessment

## Correctness
PASS. The closed-range direction is a direct consequence of the bounded inverse theorem and the scalar monotonicity estimate. For the nonclosed-range direction the finite-codimensional near-kernel lemma is justified by the closed-plus-finite-dimensional image argument. The directed family of annihilators converges weakly against every dual functional, while the factor-four scale separation yields positive cone increments and its norms tend to one. The normality equivalence is verified in both directions by explicit order-interval estimates. Exact-rational finite checks agree with the constructive inequalities but are not used to certify infinite-dimensional quantifiers.

## Originality
PASS, with residual risk. The relevant first-posted 2024 paper explicitly introduces the diagonal weighted-cone example and states that its weakly convergent increasing **net** behaviour is unknown; its subsequent theorem assumes reflexivity or a pointed bidual wedge, which does not apply to that non-normal Schur-space example. The present result settles that explicit case and gives a closed-range criterion for all operator-gauge cones, not a solution to the general open problem. Search and published-result comparison found no mathematical implication covering the net construction; absence from indexed results cannot rule out an older or unindexed equivalent statement. Full text of Wong–Ng's older Dini-related paper was not available for comparison.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A concrete unanswered case at the central sequence-versus-net distinction is resolved by a direct monotone weakly null construction on a Schur space. The closed-range equivalence gives a natural, complete structural classification across Banach-domain operators; the nonnormal-order example and its bounded-norm witness are substantive and are not merely restatements of the classical Schur property or the reflexive-case criterion.

## Closest literature and limitations
J. Glück, *Increasing sequences in ordered Banach spaces—new theorems and open problems* (arXiv:2409.19768v1), especially Example 6.1, Open Problem 6.2, Example 5.3(f), and Theorem 6.3. Only operator-gauge cones are covered; the unrestricted question remains unresolved, and unindexed literature may contain equivalent results. No independent audit has been conducted.
