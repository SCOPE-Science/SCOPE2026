# Weighted equilibrium-height defect for compact recurrence in a generalized Lorenz flow

## Finding
Consider the generalized Lorenz flow
\[
\dot x=-s_1x+s_2y,\qquad
\dot y=Rx+dy-xz,\qquad
\dot z=-qz+xy,
\]
under the parameter assumptions \(s_1>d>0\), \(q>0\), \(s_2\ne0\), and \(s_1d+s_2R>0\). Define
\[
z_*=R+\frac{s_1d}{s_2}.
\]
The two nonzero equilibria have the common \(z\)-coordinate \(z_*\).

For every compactly supported invariant probability measure \(\mu\),
\[
\boxed{\int x^2(z-z_*)\,d\mu
=\frac1{s_2}\int(-s_1x+s_2y)^2\,d\mu.}
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(\mu\) is supported on equilibria. Therefore every non-equilibrium ergodic invariant probability measure satisfies
\[
\frac{\int x^2z\,d\mu}{\int x^2\,d\mu}>z_*
\quad\text{when }s_2>0,
\]
and the inequality reverses when \(s_2<0\). Consequently, for \(s_2>0\) every non-equilibrium compact recurrent state carrying an ergodic invariant measure must put positive measure in \(z>z_*\); for \(s_2<0\) it must put positive measure in \(z<z_*\). Every nonconstant periodic orbit obeys the same strict crossing rule.

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns compactly supported invariant probability measures of the autonomous flow generated by the printed three-dimensional system. Compact support guarantees that the polynomial coboundary used below is integrable. The condition \(s_1d+s_2R>0\) is the source's saddle/homoclinic regime and ensures that the two nonzero equilibria are real. No existence of a chaotic invariant measure or periodic orbit is assumed; the conclusion applies whenever such a compact recurrent state exists.

The result is a finite-amplitude stationary-statistics law. It is not a local linearization, not a numerical time-average estimate, and not a claim about the existence of homoclinic orbits.

## Proof
Set
\[
H(x,y)=xy-\frac{s_1+d}{2s_2}x^2.
\]
Using the differential equations,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot H
&=\dot x\,y+x\dot y-\frac{s_1+d}{s_2}x\dot x\\
&=\frac1{s_2}(-s_1x+s_2y)^2-x^2(z-z_*).
\end{aligned}
\]
For an invariant probability measure with compact support, the integral of the Lie derivative of the polynomial \(H\) is zero. Integrating the displayed identity gives
\[
\int x^2(z-z_*)\,d\mu
=\frac1{s_2}\int(-s_1x+s_2y)^2\,d\mu.
\]

It remains to identify the equality case. Equality forces \(-s_1x+s_2y=0\) on the support of \(\mu\). The support is invariant, so along every trajectory in it \(x\) is constant and \(y=(s_1/s_2)x\) is constant. The second equation then yields
\[
x(z_*-z)=0.
\]
If \(x=0\), then \(y=0\), and the only compact invariant probability measure on the resulting scalar flow \(\dot z=-qz\) is the point mass at the origin. If \(x\ne0\), then \(z=z_*\), and the third equation gives
\[
x^2=\frac{q s_2 z_*}{s_1}
=\frac{q(s_1d+s_2R)}{s_1}>0.
\]
These are exactly the two nonzero equilibria. Thus equality holds exactly for probability measures supported on the three equilibria.

For a non-equilibrium ergodic measure, \(\int x^2\,d\mu>0\) and the square integral on the right is strictly positive. Dividing by \(\int x^2\,d\mu\) gives the weighted-height inequality. A strict weighted average on one side of \(z_*\) forces positive measure on that side, proving the crossing statement. The normalized invariant measure on a nonconstant periodic orbit is ergodic, so the periodic-orbit consequence follows.

## Relationship to prior work
Yang studies this exact generalized Lorenz system, proves sharp conditions for homoclinic orbits associated with the origin, and discusses chaos after the homoclinic pair breaks. The published paper gives the vector field, parameter assumptions, equilibrium/homoclinic analysis, and numerical chaotic example, but does not formulate invariant-measure or time-average identities of the type proved here [1].

The closest directly relevant time-average literature located concerns the classical Lorenz equations. Ovsyannikov, Rademacher, Welter, and Lu record exact proportionality relations among classical Lorenz time averages and study high-Rayleigh periodic attractors, but their identities are for the classical damping form and do not imply the derivative-energy defect above for the generalized system with positive \(d\) [2]. Earlier generalized-Lorenz work located on absorbing bounds uses Lyapunov functions to bound the attractor rather than an exact stationary weighted-height identity [3]. Canonical-form equivalences for generalized Lorenz systems are structural coordinate changes; no inspected source states a coordinate-free theorem that transports to the boxed identity.

## Limitations
The result constrains compact invariant probability measures; it does not prove that a non-equilibrium recurrent measure exists for any particular parameter value. The strict crossing conclusion is measure-theoretic and does not quantify residence time or excursion amplitude beyond \(z_*\). The equality classification uses compact support; no claim is made for invariant measures lacking the integrability needed for the polynomial identity. Bibliographic searches cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent derivation.
