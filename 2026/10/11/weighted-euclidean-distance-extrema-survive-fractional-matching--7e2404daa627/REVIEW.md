# Same-model scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The normalized feasible set uses unsigned degree inequalities and nonnegative coordinates. The active-constraint rank argument gives precisely disjoint odd half-cycles and unit matching edges at the fractional extreme points. Uniform near-perfect matching rounding on each odd cycle has the exact positive weighted gap shown in the theorem, not merely an empirical tendency; the positive-center restriction is essential and a triangle gives a counterexample without it. Coordinate clamping places every global Chebyshev minimizer in the nonnegative orthant, so equality of the complete enclosing balls follows. The strict quadratic midpoint identity proves unique centers. Repeating the farthest-point reduction in both arguments proves diameter equality and the two-matchings graph characterization. Exact-rational tests on 1,184 graph instances passed, including a signed-center boundary counterexample. Finite checks alone are not treated as general proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

The relevant full-text sections of Blanchard–De Loera–Louveaux (arXiv:2001.09575, pp. 1–4, 8–10, 17–19) study monotone paths and pivots for matching/fractional-matching polytopes, not weighted Euclidean circumcenters or diameters. Sanità (arXiv:1806.03397, pp. 1–4, §2 and appendix) treats shortest paths on the one-skeleton, a fundamentally different distance. Behrend (arXiv:1301.7356, introduction and incidence-rank propositions) provides structural combinatorics for fractional perfect matchings, not this rounding gap and metric invariance. Searches for equivalent formulations, including convex quadratic optimization, exact diameter, circumradius and Chebyshev center terms, surfaced no covering primary result in the inspected records. This is evidence of no identified coverage, not a proof of priority; an older separable convex maximization theorem might imply the statement without mentioning geometric balls. Prior known same-object John ellipsoid computations optimize inscribed volume rather than enclosing squared distance and do not imply the present all-graphs diagonal-weight theorem.

## Value — PASS

The result shows two distinct Euclidean metric invariants are *completely unaffected* by a substantial, in general strict, half-integral polytope relaxation, for arbitrary graph size and arbitrary positive diagonal weights. The quantitative strict gap and classification of all possible diametral contact types add structural content; this is not a single parameter computation, a restated vertex theorem, or a routine numerical case. It makes exact geometry available through matching pairs and reveals a precise disparity between combinatorial path diameters and Euclidean diameters.
