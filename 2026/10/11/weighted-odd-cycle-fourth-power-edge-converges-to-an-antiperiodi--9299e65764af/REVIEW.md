# Review of weighted fourth-power odd-cycle spectral limit

**Correctness — PASS.** The weighted edge/incident-mass normalization gives the exact defect identity (1). Global signed-square coercivity supplies the compact antiperiodic \(H^1\) limit; the sharper coefficient six holds away from zeros. Weighted weak lower semicontinuity gives the lower bound. The normalized first weighted antiperiodic eigenfunction has finitely many simple zeros, permitting a signed-square-root recovery sequence whose fourth-power mesh remainder is \(O(n^{-2})\). The resulting upper and lower limits coincide. Finite exact-rational checks are supporting algebra tests only.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest prior unweighted antiperiodic shift result determines only the constant-conductance special case; it does not imply the profile-dependent Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue \(\mu(a)\) for arbitrary positive smooth edge conductances and consistently normalized vertex masses. The inspected primary sources [1]–[4] concern lower Cheeger spectral bounds, nodal domains, signless cycles, or eigenpair computation, not this weighted continuum limit. Direct searches for weighted graph-top eigenvalues, inhomogeneous cycle limits and variable-conductance antiperiodic problems did not find a stronger formula. Such a formula may exist under another terminology.

**Value — PASS.** The finding identifies the exact effective continuum spectral problem for an infinite-dimensional class of heterogeneous cycles, with a nontrivial variable profile and normalization. It explains which macroscopic geometric property of conductance controls the nonlinear near-bipartite extremum and provides a strict strengthening of the constant-conductance case without changing the scientific bar.
