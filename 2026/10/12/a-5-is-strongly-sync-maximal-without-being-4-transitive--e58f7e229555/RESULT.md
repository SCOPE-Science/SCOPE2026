# \(A_5\) is strongly sync-maximal without being \(4\)-transitive
## Finding
Let \(A_5\) act naturally on
\[
Q=\{0,1,2,3,4\}.
\]
Then \(A_5\) is strongly sync-maximal but is not \(4\)-transitive.

More strongly, for every transformation
\[
f:Q\to Q
\]
of rank \(2\), \(3\), or \(4\), and every two distinct \(2\)-subsets \(S,T\subset Q\), there is a permutation \(g\in A_5\) such that exactly one of
\[
f(g(S)),\qquad f(g(T))
\]
is a singleton.

Consequently,
\[
\{\text{\(4\)-transitive permutation groups}\}
\subsetneq
\{\text{strongly sync-maximal permutation groups}\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
For a permutation group \(G\leq S_n\), strong sync-maximality means that for every transformation \(f:[n]\to[n]\) of rank \(r\) with
\[
2\leq r\leq n-1,
\]
all \(2\)-subsets are distinguishable in the transformation monoid generated by \(G\) and \(f\). Two distinct \(2\)-subsets are distinguishable if some generated transformation maps precisely one of them to a singleton.

The claim uses the natural degree-\(5\) action of \(A_5\). It settles the lower strictness question in the containment
\[
\text{\(4\)-transitive}\Longrightarrow\text{strongly sync-maximal}.
\]
It does not classify all strongly sync-maximal groups.

## Proof
There are two orbit types for ordered pairs of distinct \(2\)-subsets of a \(5\)-point set: the pairs either intersect in one point or are disjoint.

The group \(A_5\) is transitive on the intersecting type. Indeed, prescribe the images of the three points in the union. There are exactly two extensions of this prescribed bijection to a permutation of all five points, differing by a swap of the two unused points; exactly one extension is even.

The group \(A_5\) is also transitive on the disjoint type. Given two ordered pairs of disjoint \(2\)-subsets, first choose any bijection carrying the first source set to the first target set and the second source set to the second target set. If that bijection is odd, swap the two images inside one target \(2\)-set. This changes the parity but preserves both set images. Hence an even permutation always realizes the required ordered pair of set images.

Now fix a transformation \(f:Q\to Q\) of rank \(2\), \(3\), or \(4\).

For the intersecting orbit, choose distinct \(x,y\) in one fiber of \(f\), so
\[
f(x)=f(y).
\]
Because the rank is at least \(2\), choose \(z\) with
\[
f(z)\neq f(x).
\]
Then
\[
\{x,y\},\qquad \{x,z\}
\]
are intersecting \(2\)-subsets, and \(f\) maps the first to a singleton and the second to a \(2\)-set.

For the disjoint orbit, first suppose some fiber \(F\) of \(f\) contains at least three points. Choose distinct \(x,y,w\in F\), and choose \(z\notin F\). Then
\[
\{x,y\},\qquad \{w,z\}
\]
are disjoint, the first pair is collapsed by \(f\), and the second is not.

It remains to consider the case in which every fiber has size at most \(2\). Since \(f\) is not a permutation, some fiber \(F\) has size \(2\). The complement \(Q\setminus F\) has three points. If all three had the same \(f\)-image, then \(f\) would have rank \(2\) and its two fiber sizes would be \(2\) and \(3\), contradicting the assumption that every fiber has size at most \(2\). Therefore two points \(z,w\in Q\setminus F\) have distinct images. The disjoint pair
\[
F,\qquad \{z,w\}
\]
is again distinguished directly by \(f\).

Thus, for either orbit type, there is one ordered pair of \(2\)-subsets that \(f\) distinguishes. Since \(A_5\) is transitive on each orbit type, for arbitrary distinct \(S,T\) there exists \(g\in A_5\) carrying \((S,T)\) to an appropriate distinguished pair. The composition \(f\circ g\), which belongs to the generated monoid, distinguishes \(S\) and \(T\).

This proves that \(A_5\) is strongly sync-maximal.

Finally, \(A_5\) is not \(4\)-transitive. A \(4\)-transitive group of degree \(5\) has order divisible by
\[
5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2=120,
\]
whereas
\[
|A_5|=60.
\]
Hence the inclusion of \(4\)-transitive groups in the strongly sync-maximal groups is proper.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` beside `certificate.json`.

The verifier constructs all \(60\) even permutations on five points and checks directly that \(A_5\) is transitive on both ordered-pair orbit types for distinct \(2\)-subsets.

It then exhausts every non-permutation transformation of permitted rank:
\[
300\text{ of rank }2,\qquad
1500\text{ of rank }3,\qquad
1200\text{ of rank }4.
\]
For each of these \(3000\) transformations and every ordered pair of distinct \(2\)-subsets, it independently searches for an even permutation \(g\) such that applying \(g\) and then \(f\) collapses exactly one of the two subsets.

The verifier also reconstructs the local intersecting and disjoint witnesses used in the proof, so the finite computation checks the proof mechanism as well as the final quantified statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Hoffmann introduced strong sync-maximality after proving that ordinary sync-maximal permutation groups coincide with primitive groups. The paper proves
\[
\text{\(4\)-transitive}\Longrightarrow
\text{strongly sync-maximal}\Longrightarrow
\text{primitive}
\]
and explicitly leaves open whether the strong class is properly contained between the two surrounding classes.

The present result settles the lower-containment half of that question. The group \(A_5\) is not \(4\)-transitive, yet it satisfies the stronger one-group-element distinguishability property stated above.

A later paper of Rystsov and Szykuła studies a different question: when primitivity of an automaton implies synchronizability. Its accessible full text contains no occurrence of the strong sync-maximal property and does not decide the \(A_5\) case above.

Targeted searches for the exact group, the named strong property, the \(3\)-transitive formulation, rank-\(2\) through rank-\(4\) distinguishability, and the proper-containment question did not locate a covering published statement.

## Limitations
The result proves only that the lower inclusion is strict:
\[
\text{\(4\)-transitive}
\subsetneq
\text{strongly sync-maximal}.
\]
It does not classify strongly sync-maximal groups in general.

The proof is special to the natural action on five points; in particular, the parity-adjustment argument for disjoint pairs uses the small complement structure of this action.

An unindexed note, thesis, or unpublished computation could contain the same \(A_5\) observation.
