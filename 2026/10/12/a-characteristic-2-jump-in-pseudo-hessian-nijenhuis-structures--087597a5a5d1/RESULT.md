# A characteristic-\(2\) jump in pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis structures

## Finding

Let \(K\) be a field and let \(A_K\) be the two-dimensional pre-Lie algebra with basis
\[
\{e_1,e_2\}
\]
and nonzero products
\[
e_2\cdot e_1=-e_1,\qquad e_2\cdot e_2=e_2.
\]

A pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis structure is a pair \((B,N)\) for which \(B\) is a nondegenerate symmetric pre-Lie \(2\)-cocycle, \(N\) is an invertible Nijenhuis operator,
\[
B(Nx,y)=B(x,Ny),
\]
and
\[
B_1(x,y)=B(x,N(y))
\]
is again a pre-Lie \(2\)-cocycle.

Write
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\y&z\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]

If \(\operatorname{char}K\ne2\), then the complete classification is
\[
\boxed{
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\u&v\end{pmatrix},\quad u\ne0,
\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\mu\\0&\lambda\end{pmatrix},\quad \lambda\ne0.
}
\]
Thus the family displayed in Example 6.3 of Wang–Bai–Liu–Sheng is exhaustive whenever \(2\) is invertible.

If \(\operatorname{char}K=2\), there is a sharp enlargement:
\[
\boxed{
(B,N)\text{ is pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis}
}
\]
if and only if
\[
\boxed{
B\text{ is any nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
N\text{ is any invertible \(B\)-selfadjoint endomorphism}.
}
\]

Therefore, for every finite field \(\mathbf F_q\),
\[
\boxed{
\#\operatorname{PHN}(A_{\mathbf F_q})
=
\begin{cases}
q^2(q-1)^2,&\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q\ne2,\\[2mm]
q^4(q-1)^2,&\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q=2.
\end{cases}}
\]

In characteristic \(2\), there are
\[
\boxed{q^2(q-1)}
\]
nondegenerate symmetric forms and, for every one of them,
\[
\boxed{q^2(q-1)}
\]
invertible selfadjoint endomorphisms.

Over every extension field, the same formulas hold after replacing \(q\) by \(q^m\). Hence the point-count zeta functions are
\[
\boxed{
Z(T)=\frac{(1-q^3T)^2}{(1-q^4T)(1-q^2T)}
}
\]
when \(\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q\ne2\), and
\[
\boxed{
Z(T)=\frac{(1-q^5T)^2}{(1-q^6T)(1-q^4T)}
}
\]
when \(\operatorname{char}\mathbf F_q=2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The multiplication is exactly the two-dimensional example used in Wang–Bai–Liu–Sheng, Example 6.3.

The pseudo-Hessian cocycle identity is
\[
B(x\cdot y,z)-B(x,y\cdot z)
=
B(y\cdot x,z)-B(y,x\cdot z).
\]

The Nijenhuis identity is
\[
N(x)\cdot N(y)
=
N\!\left(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)\right).
\]

The primary source assumes an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\). The theorem here re-derives all defining equations over an arbitrary field and therefore does not infer positive-characteristic behavior by formal reduction of the source.

No quotient by algebra automorphisms is taken.

## Proof

For the symmetric form
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\y&z\end{pmatrix},
\]
evaluation of the pre-Lie cocycle identity on all basis triples gives one independent equation,
\[
\boxed{2x=0.}
\]

If the characteristic is not \(2\), this forces \(x=0\). Nondegeneracy then gives
\[
-y^2\ne0,
\]
so \(y\ne0\). Hence every pseudo-Hessian form is
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\u&v\end{pmatrix},
\qquad u\ne0.
\]

In characteristic \(2\), the cocycle equation vanishes identically, so every symmetric bilinear form is a cocycle and pseudo-Hessian means only nondegenerate.

For
\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\]
the complete basis-pair Nijenhuis torsion system is generated by
\[
\boxed{
2c^2=0,\qquad 2bc=0,\qquad 2b(a-d)=0.
}
\]

The selfadjointness equation
\[
N^TB=BN
\]
reduces to
\[
\boxed{
y(a-d)-bx+cz=0.
}
\]

Finally, the first diagonal entry of
\[
B_1=BN
\]
is
\[
ax+cy.
\]
For a symmetric form on this algebra, the cocycle criterion is twice that first diagonal entry. Therefore the \(B_1\)-cocycle condition is
\[
\boxed{2(ax+cy)=0.}
\]

Assume \(2\) is invertible. We already have \(x=0\) and \(y\ne0\). The \(B_1\)-cocycle equation gives \(c=0\). Selfadjointness then gives \(a=d\). Invertibility is exactly \(a\ne0\). Thus
\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a\ne0.
\]
Conversely, direct substitution shows that every such pair satisfies all four defining requirements.

Now assume characteristic \(2\). Both the pseudo-Hessian cocycle equation and the Nijenhuis torsion equations vanish identically. If \(N\) is \(B\)-selfadjoint, then \(B_1=BN\) is symmetric, and every symmetric form is automatically a cocycle. Therefore the complete condition is precisely that \(B\) be nondegenerate symmetric and \(N\) be invertible and \(B\)-selfadjoint.

For finite fields with \(2\) invertible, there are
\[
q(q-1)
\]
choices for \(B\), from \(u\ne0\) and arbitrary \(v\), and independently
\[
q(q-1)
\]
choices for \(N\), from \(a\ne0\) and arbitrary \(b\). This gives
\[
q^2(q-1)^2.
\]

Now let \(q\) be even. A symmetric matrix
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\y&z\end{pmatrix}
\]
is singular exactly when
\[
xz+y^2=0.
\]
If \(x\ne0\), then \(z=y^2/x\) is uniquely determined, giving \(q(q-1)\) singular forms. If \(x=0\), singularity forces \(y=0\), while \(z\) is arbitrary, giving \(q\) more. Thus there are \(q^2\) singular symmetric forms and
\[
q^3-q^2=q^2(q-1)
\]
nondegenerate ones.

Fix a nondegenerate \(B\). The \(B\)-selfadjoint endomorphisms form a three-dimensional vector space, so there are \(q^3\) of them. Exactly \(q^2\) are singular. Indeed, apart from the zero map, a singular selfadjoint map has rank one. If its kernel is a line \(L\), then selfadjointness gives
\[
\operatorname{im}N=L^\perp.
\]
Conversely, for every one of the \(q+1\) lines \(L\), there are exactly \(q-1\) nonzero maps
\[
\mathbf F_q^2/L\longrightarrow L^\perp.
\]
Thus the singular selfadjoint maps number
\[
1+(q+1)(q-1)=q^2.
\]
Therefore the invertible selfadjoint maps number
\[
q^3-q^2=q^2(q-1),
\]
and the total number of characteristic-\(2\) structures is
\[
q^4(q-1)^2.
\]

Replacing \(q\) by \(q^m\) and applying the exponential definition of the Hasse–Weil zeta function gives the two displayed rational functions.

## Relationship to prior work

Wang, Bai, Liu, and Sheng introduced pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis structures on pre-Lie algebras. Their paper assumes an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\). Example 6.3 uses exactly the multiplication studied here and displays
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\u&v\end{pmatrix},
\qquad u\ne0,
\]
together with
\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a\ne0,
\]
as pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis structures. It does not state a characteristic-free classification.

The 2026 low-dimensional classification of Nijenhuis operators treats raw complex Nijenhuis endomorphisms, not pseudo-Hessian–Nijenhuis pairs, and therefore does not imply the pair classification or the characteristic-\(2\) jump.

Guo and Ma develop related pseudo-Hessian and Nijenhuis pre-Lie constructions over a general field, but the inspected full text does not classify this fixed example in characteristic \(2\) or give the finite-field counts above.

Targeted searches for the exact multiplication, Example 6.3, characteristic \(2\), finite fields, selfadjoint Nijenhuis operators, and the counting polynomials did not locate a covering result.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only the two-dimensional pre-Lie algebra from Example 6.3.

It counts fixed-coordinate structures and does not quotient by algebra automorphisms.

The zeta statements record extension-field point counts; they do not assert that the structure spaces are affine spaces.

A specialized thesis or differently indexed note may contain the same characteristic-\(2\) calculation. Failed searches do not prove novelty.
