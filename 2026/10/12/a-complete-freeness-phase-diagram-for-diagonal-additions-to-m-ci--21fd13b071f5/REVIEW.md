# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is reduced to two exact geometric computations plus the cited addition theorem. In squared coordinates the component-orbits of the base arrangement become four concurrent lines; their intersections with the diagonal-conic parameter line give the six branch factors explicitly. Away from the common pencil line, each branch factor creates one simple two-to-one lift drop and an \(A_3\) tangency, yielding \(|B\cap C|=16-2r\). On the common pencil line, two pencil generators are local analytic coordinates at the four base points, making the five local branches a product of distinct linear forms and hence quasihomogeneous. The source theorem then gives the free, plus-one generated, and neither cases, and its resolution corollary gives the two displayed resolutions. The finite incidence computation was replayed from the packaged checker.

## Originality
PASS. The inspected source gives the general addition theorem and only two diagonal sample additions in Example 4.1; it does not classify the full diagonal parameter plane. Exact-equation searches, searches for the six branch factors, and searches for the new isolated free conic did not locate the phase diagram. The closest general prior result is Măcinic’s own theorem, which implies the freeness type only after the intersection cardinality and quasihomogeneity have been established; it does not imply the parameter discriminant without the new geometric calculation. A residual risk remains that an older or poorly indexed note computes the same invariant subfamily in different language.

## Value
PASS. The diagonal family is not an arbitrary slice: it is exactly the conic subspace fixed by the coordinate sign-change symmetry of the base arrangement and contains both diagonal additions highlighted by the source. The complete phase diagram reveals a one-dimensional free locus plus a previously unstated isolated free point, proves that the plus-one generated locus is exactly six points, and gives exact resolutions on the complementary strata. This is a natural finite classification of a symmetry-adapted moduli slice and provides reusable test cases for addition phenomena.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is A. Măcinic, arXiv:2408.11714, especially Theorem 3.7, Corollary 3.17, and Example 4.1. The result here specializes that theorem after a new exact computation of the full diagonal parameter stratification. It does not address arbitrary non-diagonal conics, and failed searches are not a proof of global novelty.
