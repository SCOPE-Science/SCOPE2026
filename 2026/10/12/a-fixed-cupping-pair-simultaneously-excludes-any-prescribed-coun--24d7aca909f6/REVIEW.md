# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Code \(B\) and the uniformly \(B\)-computable sequence \((E_n)\) into one set \(D\equiv_T B\). Cintioli's structural theorem applies because \(D\leq_T C\) and \(A\nleq_T D\). Its certificate mechanism permits a finite effective rule, so the designated-column diagonalization can be indexed by the target number \(n\), using coordinate \(q(n+1,m)\) at the requirement for \((n,e_0,e_1)\). The source verification then applies requirement by requirement. If some \(E_n\) were reducible to both final witnesses, the pair of witnessing reductions would appear in the enumeration and contradict its requirement.

## Originality

PASS. The recent source proves avoidance of one prescribed noncomputable target and explicitly states that its witnesses may depend on that target; it does not construct a fixed pair eliminating nonzero common lower bounds. Focused searches for fixed-pair, countable-family, designated-column, and simultaneous-cupping formulations found no prior statement of this countable prescribed-family strengthening. General simultaneous cone-avoidance results are not covering because their avoided sets must be noncomputable relative to the base oracle, whereas here all \(E_n\) lie uniformly below the cupping target \(B\).

## Value

PASS. The theorem addresses the exact structural limitation identified by the new source: dependence of the two witnesses on a single avoided degree. It shows that one pair can be selected once for an arbitrary prescribed countable family of nonzero degrees below the target, while preserving both cupping requirements. This is a meaningful intermediate step toward the stronger unresolved fixed-pair question, with a sharp statement of what remains unproved.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Cintioli's one-target designated-column lemma. Downey--Greenberg--Harrison-Trainor--Patey--Turetsky develop simultaneous cone avoidance for problems, but under a different relative noncomputability hypothesis that does not cover targets lying below the cupping oracle \(B\).

The uniform join hypothesis is essential to this coding proof. The conclusion excludes only the designated countable family and does not assert that the two witnesses form a minimal pair.
