# A four-conic characteristic-three boundary of the twelve-conic Hesse arrangement

## Finding

The twelve-conic Hesse (Chilean) configuration has a uniform, nonordinary degeneration in residue characteristic three. Specializing its explicit cyclotomic equations does **not** preserve twelve components: nine coincide as one smooth conic, and three other smooth conics remain distinct. The reduced divisor has exactly three transverse nodes and three singularities where three smooth conic branches share a tangent, with pairwise intersection multiplicities \(3,2,2\).

More precisely, for every algebraically closed field \(k\) with \(\operatorname{char}k=3\) and every \(t\in k\setminus\{0,1\}\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&=xy-tz^2,\\
 B&=xz-ty^2,\\
 C&=yz-tx^2,\\
 D&=x^2+y^2+z^2+(t+1)(xy+xz+yz).
\end{aligned}
\]
The product of the twelve conic forms of Dimca--Pokora--Sticlaru, §5, reduces, up to a nonzero scalar, to \(D^9ABC\). The **reduced support**, not the nonreduced degree-24 product, is the degree-eight divisor \(\mathcal R_t=V(ABCD)\subset\mathbb P^2_k\).

Its complete geometric singular support is
\[
\{[1:0:0],[0:1:0],[0:0:1],[t:1:1],[1:t:1],[1:1:t]\}.
\]
The coordinate vertices are ordinary nodes. The final three points each lie on exactly three smooth conics; the three unordered pairwise local intersection numbers are \(\{3,2,2\}\). In particular, none is an ordinary triple point. Every point displayed is rational over the field generated by \(t\), so \(\mathcal R_t\) has exactly six \(\mathbb F_{3^m}\)-rational singular points whenever \(t\in\mathbb F_{3^m}\setminus\{0,1\}\).

## Assumptions and scope

Use the explicit homogeneous quadratic forms \(f_1(t),\ldots,f_{12}(t)\) printed as the twelve conics in §5 of *On the Alexander polynomials of conic-line arrangements* (arXiv:2305.01450). Their coefficient ring is \(\mathbb Z[\omega,t]/(\omega^2+\omega+1)\). Reduction at the unique prime above three means sending \(\omega\) to \(1\). The polynomial parameter \(t\) survives, and the theorem concerns all geometric specializations \(t\ne0,1\), without assuming that the original elliptic curve has good reduction. In fact, the characteristic-zero Hesse cubic degenerates in characteristic three. The claim is about the divisor obtained by **this specified coefficientwise reduction** and its *geometric reduced support*. It does not address other integral models or the scheme structure of the nonreduced product.

## Proof

**1. Complete conic specialization.** Substitute \(\omega=1\) and reduce all coefficients modulo three in the twelve source equations. The forms with indices \(1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10,12\) become \(D\); forms \(3,7,11\) become \(A,B,-C\), respectively. Therefore the unreduced product equals \(-D^9ABC\). The accompanying independent checker contains all twelve source forms and verifies every coefficient identity over \(\mathbb F_3[t,x,y,z]\).

**2. Distinctness and smoothness.** The Hessian determinants of the four quadratics in characteristic three are, respectively,
\[
 \det\operatorname{Hess}(A)=\det\operatorname{Hess}(B)
 =\det\operatorname{Hess}(C)=-t,
 \qquad \det\operatorname{Hess}(D)=1-t^3=(1-t)^3.
\]
Since \(t\ne0,1\), each determinant is nonzero and all four conics are smooth. They are pairwise distinct: the three monomial conics have different nonzero mixed terms and \(D\) has all three nonzero square coefficients.

**3. Six displayed points.** The three coordinate vertices lie on precisely two of \(A,B,C\): \([1:0:0]\) lies on \(A,B\), \([0:1:0]\) on \(A,C\), and \([0:0:1]\) on \(B,C\). None lies on \(D\), and the excluded third monomial conic evaluates to \(-t\). At \([1:0:0]\), the gradients of \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,1,0)\) and \((0,0,1)\), so they meet transversely; coordinate symmetry gives the same conclusion at the other vertices.

The three points \(P_{AB}=[t:1:1]\), \(P_{AC}=[1:t:1]\) and \(P_{BC}=[1:1:t]\) each lie on \(D\) and exactly two of \(A,B,C\). For example, \(A(P_{AB})=B(P_{AB})=D(P_{AB})=0\), whereas \(C(P_{AB})=1-t^3=1-t\ne0\). Coordinate permutation proves the other cases. All six points are distinct for \(t\ne0,1\).

**4. Exact local contacts.** At \(P_{AB}\), work on the affine chart \(z=1\), write \(x=t+u\), \(y=1+v\), and compute in characteristic three:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&=u+tv+uv,\\
 B&=u+tv-tv^2,\\
 D&=(t-1)(u+tv)+u^2+v^2+(t+1)uv.
\end{aligned}
\]
All three have the same nonzero tangent direction \(u+tv=0\), because \(t\ne1\). On the smooth branch \(B=0\), \(u=-tv+tv^2\) exactly; substituting into \(A\) gives \(A=tv^3\). Hence \(I_{P_{AB}}(A,B)=3\), since \(t\ne0\).

On \(A=0\), solve \(u=-tv/(1+v)\); then
\[
 \left.D\right|_A =
 \frac{v^2\bigl((t-1)^2+(t-1)v+v^2\bigr)}{(1+v)^2}.
\]
On \(B=0\), solve \(u=-tv+tv^2\); then
\[
 \left.D\right|_B =
 v^2\bigl((t-1)^2+(t-t^2)v+t^2v^2\bigr).
\]
The leading coefficient in both restrictions is \((t-1)^2\ne0\), so \(I_{P_{AB}}(A,D)=I_{P_{AB}}(B,D)=2\). Permuting coordinates yields identical contact orders at \(P_{AC}\) and \(P_{BC}\).

**5. Exhaustion by Bézout.** Any two of these distinct conics have total geometric intersection number four, counted with multiplicity. Each pair among \(A,B,C\) already meets at one coordinate vertex with multiplicity one and one triple-branch point with multiplicity three. Each pair \((A,D)\), \((B,D)\), \((C,D)\) already has two triple-branch points of multiplicity two. In every case the known intersection multiplicities sum to four, leaving no further intersections, including over algebraic extensions or at infinity. Because each component is smooth, the displayed six intersections exhaust the singular support of the reduced divisor.

**6. Delta invariant and rational points.** For a reduced plane curve whose local branches are smooth, the local delta invariant equals the sum of pairwise local intersection multiplicities. Thus a node contributes one and each triple-branch point contributes \(3+2+2=7\). The sum is \(3+3\cdot7=24\), consistent with the arithmetic-genus equality \((8-1)(8-2)/2=24-4+1=21\) for four rational conic components. All six coordinates belong to the field containing \(t\), proving the finite-field count without extrapolation from experimental samples.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact polynomial arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_3[t]\). It checks all twelve coefficient reductions, four Hessian determinants, all six point incidences, the local substitution identities, the symmetry argument, and independently enumerates the projective singular support over \(\mathbb F_3\) and over \(\mathbb F_9\) for every admissible parameter in those fields. Running `python verify.py` with Python 3.11+ and SymPy 1.13+ returns `VERIFY_OK`. The symbolic identities plus Bézout, not the finite-field enumerations, establish the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Dolgachev--Laface--Persson--Urzúa, *Chilean configuration of conics, lines and points* (arXiv:2008.09627), construct and analyze the twelve-conic configuration in characteristics other than two and three, expressly excluding the characteristic addressed here. Dimca--Pokora--Sticlaru (arXiv:2305.01450), §5, print the twelve conic equations and study their complex incidence structure; their §6 studies **parameter** degenerations over \(\mathbb C\), not the residue-characteristic-three degeneration. This result uses those published equations as input and identifies a distinct arithmetic boundary: the four-component special fibre, its six singular points, and their exact nonordinary contact profile. The characteristic-zero configuration and its already-known incidence or symmetry groups are not claimed to be new.

## Limitations

The statement concerns the fixed cyclotomic model and the chosen residue homomorphism \(\omega\mapsto1\). The twelve-conic divisor specializes nonreduced, and only the singular support of its *reduction* is classified. No claim is made about the nonreduced singular scheme, wild-characteristic Milnor numbers, freeness, or alternate models; the values \(t=0,1\) are excluded because some smoothness or distinctness arguments fail there. No claim of formal priority or independent verification is made.
