# Review of A one-dimensional local-to-global modulus in minimal-Tjurina bitangent arrangements

## Correctness
PASS. The singular locus follows directly from subtracting two distinct conic equations and from the smoothness of each \(xz+a y^2\) for \(a\ne0\). The analytic classification is reconstructed in both directions: preservation of the unique common tangent forces the branch quadratic coefficients to transform affinely, while \(Y=y, Z=uz-vy^2\) realizes every affine change \(a\mapsto ua+v\). For the line-added family, the unique transverse branch is intrinsic and is preserved by the same map. Globally, the pencil \(\langle xz,y^2\rangle\) has unique singular members of ranks two and one, so any projective normalizer fixes both pencil parameters and acts by scaling. The packaged symbolic verifier checks all algebraic identities used in these steps and the explicit example.

## Originality
PASS. The lead paper classifies the two minimal-Tjurina families and gives their equations and nearly free status, but it does not state their embedded local analytic moduli or the one-dimensional local-to-global fiber. Aluffi--Faber already cover the multiplicative global stabilizer behavior, so that part is not claimed as new. Exact-object and alias searches for bitangent-pencil local analytic equivalence, common-tangent branch moduli, and projective-versus-analytic equivalence found no source stating that local classes are affine quotients while global classes are scaling quotients. The closest database results concern different conic-line arrangements or individual singularities and do not imply this comparison.

## Value
PASS. This gives a structural boundary for a complete extremal classification: even within the only two minimal-Tjurina conic-line families with a linear syzygy, degree, nearly freeness, global Tjurina number, embedded topological type, and the full embedded analytic type of every singular germ still leave one global projective modulus. The explicit degree-six and degree-seven examples make the failure concrete rather than merely dimension-theoretic.

## Closest literature and limitations
The lead source is Beorchia--Miró-Roig, arXiv:2303.04665, especially Example 3.3 and Theorem 3.6. Aluffi--Faber, DOI 10.5802/aif.1750, Lemma 3.1 is the closest global-symmetry comparison and is treated as prior coverage. The claim is restricted to the two bitangent families with \(m\ge3\), and an older general singularity-theory statement could potentially subsume the local affine-classification lemma under different language.
