# A periodic-gap criterion for irreducibility of binary free-semigroup endomorphisms fixing one generator

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{a,b\}\) and let \(\varphi_w:\Sigma^+\to\Sigma^+\) be the endomorphism defined by \(\varphi_w(a)=w\), \(\varphi_w(b)=b\), where \(w\in\Sigma^+\) contains at least one \(a\). Write its unique run decomposition as
\[
w=b^{r_0}a b^{r_1}a\cdots a b^{r_m},\qquad m=|w|_a\geq1,\quad r_0,\ldots,r_m\in\mathbb N_0.
\]
Then \(\varphi_w\) is rank-preserving. Its classification under composition into two rank-preserving non-automorphisms is as follows.

For \(m=1\), \(\varphi_w\) is reducible if and only if \(r_0+r_1\geq2\). If \(r_0+r_1=1\), it is irreducible. If \(r_0+r_1=0\), it is the identity automorphism.

For \(m\geq2\), \(\varphi_w\) is reducible if and only if either \(r_0+r_m>0\), or there is a proper divisor \(c\) of \(m\) such that \(2\leq c<m\) and
\[
r_{kc+j}=r_j\quad\text{for all }0\leq k<m/c\text{ and }1\leq j<c.
\]
The latter criterion compares each interior gap with its repetitions in \(m/c\) successive blocks; the \(m/c-1\) separating gaps \(r_c,r_{2c},\ldots,r_{m-c}\) are unrestricted. It is an exact divisor-and-equality decision test; the factorizations are constructed explicitly in the proof.

Two consequences sharpen the boundary. If \(m\geq2\) is prime, every word that starts and ends with \(a\) yields an irreducible \(\varphi_w\). In particular, for any \(m\geq2\), the word \(w=aba^{m-1}\) produces an irreducible endomorphism despite having upper-triangular incidence matrix with row corresponding to \(a\) equal to \((m,1)\) and the row for \(b\) equal to \((0,1)\). Thus the exclusive-reducibility assertion of Proposition 18(ii) of Bell–Foster–Reidenbach, arXiv:2603.15177v3 (also arXiv:2610.07122v1), fails already for \(w=aba\).

For a fixed prime \(m\geq2\), there are exactly \(\binom{\beta+m-2}{m-2}\) irreducible such morphisms with \(|w|_b=\beta\) and both word boundaries equal to \(a\). For \(m=4\), the corresponding count is \(\binom{\beta+2}{2}-(\lfloor\beta/2\rfloor+1)\). For each fixed composite \(m\geq4\), among boundary-free words with \(|w|_a=m\) and \(|w|_b=\beta\), the proportion yielding reducible morphisms tends to zero as \(\beta\to\infty\). These counts and asymptotics are consequences of the same exact criterion, not separate hypotheses.

## Assumptions and scope

Every image word of a free-semigroup endomorphism is nonempty. An automorphism of \(\{a,b\}^+\) is a permutation of the two letters. An endomorphism is rank-preserving when its image cannot be generated by one nonempty word. Following the composition definition in Bell–Foster–Reidenbach, a rank-preserving endomorphism is reducible when it is the composition of two rank-preserving non-automorphisms. The identity is a unit, not a nonunit irreducible element. No alphabet enlargement or factorization through a smaller alphabet is allowed. We include \(\beta=0\) and arbitrary nonnegative gap lengths.

To check rank preservation, observe that if a one-word semigroup generated both \(b=\varphi_w(b)\) and \(w=\varphi_w(a)\), its generator would have to be \(b\), contrary to the presence of \(a\) in \(w\). A rank-preserving composite has rank-preserving factors: a one-word factor image would force the composite image into a one-word semigroup.

## Proof

**Normalization of every factorization.** Suppose \(\varphi_w=\psi_2\circ\psi_1\), with both factors rank-preserving and non-automorphic. Since the positive word \(\psi_2(\psi_1(b))=b\) has length one, \(\psi_1(b)\) is a letter and its image under \(\psi_2\) is \(b\). If this letter is \(a\), compose \(\psi_1\) on the left and \(\psi_2\) on the right with the transposition of \(a,b\); this does not change their composition or unit status. Thus, without loss, \(\psi_1(b)=\psi_2(b)=b\). Because both factors preserve rank, their images of \(a\), say \(u=\psi_1(a)\) and \(v=\psi_2(a)\), contain \(a\). The entire factorization reduces to the exact word substitution
\[
w=v[u]=\text{the word obtained from }u\text{ by replacing every }a\text{ with }v\text{ and leaving }b\text{ fixed}.
\]
Set \(d=|u|_a\) and \(c=|v|_a\). Then \(m=cd\). Under this normalization a factor is an automorphism exactly when its image of \(a\) is \(a\).

**One occurrence.** If \(m=1\), then \(c=d=1\). Write \(u=b^\ell a b^t\) and \(v=b^p a b^q\); the total number of \(b\)'s in \(w=v[u]\) is \(\ell+t+p+q=r_0+r_1\). Each factor is a non-automorphism precisely when its pair of padding lengths has positive sum. Hence reducibility requires at least two \(b\)'s. Conversely, if \(r_0+r_1\geq2\), distribute the initial and final runs between \(u\) and \(v\) so that both receive at least one \(b\). This gives a nontrivial factorization. When \(r_0+r_1=0\), \(u=v=a\) is the only possibility.

**Boundary runs in the higher-occurrence case.** If \(m\geq2\) and \(r_0+r_m>0\), define two normalized factors by
\[
\psi_1(a)=b^{r_0}ab^{r_m},\quad\psi_1(b)=b,\qquad
\psi_2(a)=ab^{r_1}a\cdots ab^{r_{m-1}}a,\quad\psi_2(b)=b.
\]
The first is nonunit because of the nonzero boundary run; the second is nonunit because it contains at least two \(a\)'s. Substitution yields \(\varphi_w=\psi_2\circ\psi_1\).

**Boundary-free words.** Suppose now \(r_0=r_m=0\) and \(m\geq2\). In any normalized factorization \(w=v[u]\), both \(u\) and \(v\) start and end with \(a\); otherwise \(w\) would start or end with \(b\). If \(c=1\), then \(v=a\), and if \(d=1\), then \(u=a\); either case makes a factor a unit. A nontrivial factorization therefore requires \(c,d\geq2\), so \(c\) is a proper divisor of \(m=cd\). Write
\[
v=ab^{t_1}a\cdots ab^{t_{c-1}}a,\qquad u=ab^{s_1}a\cdots ab^{s_{d-1}}a.
\]
Then \(w=v b^{s_1}v\cdots b^{s_{d-1}}v\). The \(c-1\) interior gaps \(t_j\) of \(v\) occur in every copy, so \(r_{kc+j}=t_j=r_j\) for all \(0\leq k<d\) and \(1\leq j<c\). This proves necessity.

Conversely, given a proper divisor \(c\) satisfying the stated repeated-gap equalities, let \(d=m/c\), define \(v=ab^{r_1}a\cdots ab^{r_{c-1}}a\) and \(u=ab^{r_c}ab^{r_{2c}}a\cdots ab^{r_{(d-1)c}}a\). The repeated gaps give \(v[u]=w\). Both \(u\) and \(v\) contain at least two copies of \(a\), so both corresponding factors are rank-preserving non-automorphisms. This proves sufficiency and completes the classification.

**Enumerative consequences.** For a boundary-free word with \(m\geq2\), distributing \(\beta\) copies of \(b\) among \(m-1\) interior gaps gives \(\binom{\beta+m-2}{m-2}\) words. When \(m\) is prime there is no proper divisor, and all are irreducible. For \(m=4\), the only possible proper divisor is \(c=2\), so reducibility is exactly \(r_1=r_3\). The number of triples of nonnegative gaps with \(r_1=r_3\) and sum \(\beta\) is \(\lfloor\beta/2\rfloor+1\); subtraction gives the stated count.

For composite \(m=cd\) with \(c,d\geq2\), the count of gap vectors satisfying the \(c\)-periodic block condition has generating function
\[
\frac{1}{(1-z^d)^{c-1}(1-z)^{d-1}}.
\]
Its coefficient of \(z^\beta\) is \(O_m(\beta^{c+d-3})\), whereas the number of all boundary-free gap vectors is \(\Theta_m(\beta^{m-2})\). Since \((m-2)-(c+d-3)=(c-1)(d-1)>0\), the finite union of divisor events has density tending to zero. For the proposed smallest boundary \(w=aba\), there are two \(a\)'s, no nontrivial proper divisor, and no initial or final \(b\), hence \(\varphi_w\) is irreducible. The same argument applies to \(w=aba^{m-1}\) for all \(m\geq2\): the unique nonzero interior gap \(r_1\) cannot repeat in any nontrivial divisor block.

## Relationship to prior work

Bell, Foster and Reidenbach [1] introduce the rank-preserving composition problem, provide a general factor-basis decision criterion, and study upper-triangular incidence matrices in Section 5. Their Theorem 1 gives an abstract criterion that can in principle decide individual instances, but not the stated direct arithmetic classification of all words with one generator fixed. They explicitly leave a full binary upper-triangular classification unspecified. More sharply, their Proposition 18(ii) asserts exclusive reducibility for a matrix with \(\beta=1\), \(\alpha\geq2\), \(\gamma=1\), whereas \(\varphi_{aba}\) is an explicit irreducible endomorphism with that matrix. Their October conference version [2] repeats this assertion. Thus the new condition is not a corollary of that asserted sufficient test; it provides a structural correction on a natural whole family.

Honkala [3] studies commuting upper-triangular binary morphisms, a different equation involving a pair of morphisms; the supplied abstract does not furnish a composition-irreducibility classification. The earlier theory of simplifiable homomorphisms and indecomposable codes mentioned in [1] generally permits intermediate alphabets of different sizes and is not directly the same fixed-alphabet factorization notion. No equivalent periodic-gap criterion or encompassing exact census was established in the literature comparison.

## Limitations

The theorem treats only the fixed-generator subfamily \(\varphi_w(b)=b\), not all upper-triangular binary endomorphisms (which can send \(b\) to higher powers of \(b\)), nor larger alphabets. It addresses existence of a two-factor decomposition, not uniqueness or minimal factorization length. The asymptotic fixes \(m\) while \(\beta\to\infty\). The numerical verifier checks finite instances only. Although directly relevant full texts [1] and [2] were examined and focused overlap searches performed, older literature on upper-triangular morphisms could contain unrecognized equivalent formulations. Independent mathematical assessment remains outstanding.
