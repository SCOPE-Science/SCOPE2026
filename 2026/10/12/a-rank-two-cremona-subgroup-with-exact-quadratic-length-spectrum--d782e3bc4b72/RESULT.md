# A rank-two Cremona subgroup with exact quadratic-length spectrum \(\{0,1,5\}\)
## Finding
Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Consider the two extremal quartic de Jonquières maps singled out by Calabri--Nguyen:
\[
\phi_0([x:y:z])=[xz^3+y^4:yz^3:z^4],\qquad
\phi_1([x:y:z])=[xz^3+y^4+y^2z^2:yz^3:z^4].
\]
Then \(\langle\phi_0,\phi_1\rangle\cong\mathbf Z^2\), and for every \((m,n)\in\mathbf Z^2\),
\[
q(\phi_0^m\phi_1^n)=
\begin{cases}
0,&(m,n)=(0,0),\\
1,&m+n=0\text{ and }(m,n)\ne(0,0),\\
5,&m+n\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the exact quadratic-length spectrum of this subgroup is \(\{0,1,5\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, matching the hypotheses of arXiv:2609.39943v1. Here \(q(f)\) denotes the minimum number of quadratic plane Cremona maps in a decomposition of \(f\), with \(q(\mathrm{id})=0\). The statement concerns only the subgroup generated by the two displayed extremal normal forms; it does not classify quadratic length on larger triangular, de Jonquières, or Cremona subgroups.

## Proof
On the dense affine chart \(z=1\),
\[
\phi_0(x,y)=(x+y^4,y),\qquad
\phi_1(x,y)=(x+y^4+y^2,y).
\]
These are commuting triangular automorphisms. Hence for all integers \(m,n\),
\[
\phi_0^m\phi_1^n(x,y)
=
\bigl(x+(m+n)y^4+n y^2,y\bigr).
\]
Characteristic zero implies that this translation polynomial vanishes exactly when \(n=0\) and \(m+n=0\), hence exactly when \((m,n)=(0,0)\). Therefore the generated subgroup is free abelian of rank two.

Set \(a=m+n\) and \(b=n\). If \(a=0\) and \(b\ne0\), the map is the non-linear quadratic Cremona map
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[xz+b y^2:yz:z^2],
\]
so its quadratic length is exactly \(1\).

Assume now \(a\ne0\). The projective closure is
\[
F_{a,b}=[xz^3+a y^4+b y^2z^2:yz^3:z^4].
\]
If \(b=0\), precomposing by \([x:y:z]\mapsto[a x:y:z]\) and postcomposing by \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[a^{-1}X:Y:Z]\) sends \(F_{a,0}\) to \(\phi_0\).

If \(b\ne0\), choose \(\beta\in K^*\) with \(\beta^2=b/a\), and put \(\alpha=b^2/a\). Precompose by \([x:y:z]\mapsto[\alpha x:\beta y:z]\) and postcompose by \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[\alpha^{-1}X:\beta^{-1}Y:Z]\). The two non-leading coefficients become
\[
\frac{a\beta^4}{\alpha}=1,
\qquad
\frac{b\beta^2}{\alpha}=1,
\]
so \(F_{a,b}\) is equivalent to \(\phi_1\). Calabri--Nguyen prove that quadratic length is invariant under source/target projective automorphisms and that both normal forms \(\phi_0,\phi_1\) have quadratic length \(5\). Therefore \(q(F_{a,b})=5\) whenever \(a\ne0\), proving the formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Calabri--Nguyen classify quartic plane Cremona maps by quadratic length and identify exactly two equivalence classes attaining the maximum \(5\). Their paper treats individual quartic maps; searches within the full text for subgroup, iterate, and power yield no occurrence relevant to the subgroup restriction proved here. The present result instead computes the invariant on every element of the canonical subgroup generated by the two extremal representatives.

Blanc--Furter study a different invariant, the minimum number of de Jonquières factors, including its dynamical version under iteration. That literature motivates asking how a length behaves on powers and subgroups but does not determine this quadratic-length spectrum. Nguyen's 2020 thesis studies quadratic length for low-degree maps and bounds for de Jonquières maps; its public abstract does not state the rank-two subgroup computation above.

## Limitations
This result uses algebraic closedness to normalize the \(b\ne0\) coefficient by a square root and characteristic zero to make the integer-parameter embedding faithful. No claim is made in positive characteristic or over nonclosed fields. The originality comparison is necessarily subject to the possibility of an unindexed equivalent observation, especially in older long-form treatments; no such statement was found in the inspected source paper, public thesis abstract, published length literature, targeted web searches, or the checked published-finding database.
