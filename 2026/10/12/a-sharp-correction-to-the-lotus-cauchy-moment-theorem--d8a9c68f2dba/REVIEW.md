# Review: A sharp correction to the LOTUS Cauchy moment theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The tail comparison proves \(\mathbb E|X|^r=\infty\) for every positive integer \(r\). Algebraic inversion of the density gives a \(\pm\) pair and shows that the source's plus-only equation is false on half the support. A separate residue calculation proves \(\mathbb E[X^z]=(\mu+i\sigma)^z\) precisely on the absolute-integrability strip \(-1<\operatorname{Re}z<1\). The boundary failures are logarithmic, so the range is sharp.

## Originality
PASS for the correction as stated. The closest prior literature on complex fractional Cauchy moments predates the 2026 target paper and is explicitly excluded from the originality claim. Exact-title, theorem-language, inverse-density, fractional-moment, and analytic-continuation searches found no covering correction of DOI 10.47974/JSMS-1606. The most relevant earlier source, arXiv:2312.02698, supplies legitimate fractional-moment context rather than the invalid positive-integer conclusion.

## Value
PASS. The error affects the main population-moment claim and the dependent central-moment statement of a recent article. The correction is structurally useful: it identifies the lost inverse branch, restores the exact domain where the complex formula really is an expectation, and separates ordinary expectation from regularized or analytically continued quantities.

## Closest literature and limitations
Okamura and Otobe's fractional-calculus work is the closest mathematical predecessor and means that the fractional identity itself is not presented as new. Nadarajah's truncated-Cauchy work is consistent with the nonexistence of untruncated ordinary moments. A non-indexed or later correction to the 2026 article remains a residual originality risk. Generalized principal values and finite-part moments are outside the claim.
