# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The six pointed-valuation types are explicitly defined and witnessed on every irreflexive complete frame of size at least four. The formulas \(p\), \(\Delta p\), \(\neg\Delta(p\wedge\Delta p)\) and \(\neg\Delta(\neg p\wedge\Delta p)\) isolate the types, while the successor-type table is closed under \(\Delta\) and permits induction over all formula depths. Boundary sizes are treated separately. Exact finite-model verification exhausts valuations and world pointers for sizes one through nine, checks the partition and its modal closure, and never substitutes finite testing for the proof covering arbitrary sizes.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
Comparisons concerned exact statements and implications rather than titles: Wang–Zenger address generalized-path-condition proof theory; Fan provides neighborhood semantics, bisimulations, and frame-definability theory; Ding–Yang provide general bundle bisimulation expressivity. These results explain why modal equivalence partitions exist, but none of the accessible material states the present six-type normal form, exact \(64\)-class quotient, or minimal depth two on the complete irreflexive family. Literature and prior-record queries did not reveal a direct equivalence. The full Wang–Zenger preprint was unavailable for material reading, and an equivalent specialized formula-clone calculation could be indexed elsewhere. This is residual risk rather than proof of absence.

## Value — PASS
The complete irreflexive frame is a canonical highly symmetric test of how “knowing whether” loses truth polarity while retaining multiplicity information. The exact six-state semantic quotient yields a uniform constructive formula-normalization procedure and a sharp depth threshold, rather than a generic modal nondefinability observation. It is a precise new finite expressivity invariant at the intersection of modal equivalence, formula algebra and bounded-depth definability, and provides an explicit baseline against which extensions of the language or changes of accessibility can be compared.
