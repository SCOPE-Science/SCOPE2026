# A sharp five-element finite-basis cutoff for cubic-nilpotent semigroups with zero element squares
## Finding
Let \(S\) be a finite semigroup with an absorbing zero \(0\), with \(S^3=\{0\}\), \(S^2\neq\{0\}\), and \(s^2=0\) for every \(s\in S\). Put \(V=S\setminus S^2\) and form the irreflexive product digraph \(D_S\) on \(V\) with an arc \(u\to v\) precisely when \(uv\neq0\). For \(|S|\le5\), the full power semiring \(\mathcal P(S)\), including the empty set and taken in the constant-free union/multiplication signature, is finitely based **if and only if** \(D_S\) is transitive:
\[
(uv\neq0\ \text{and}\ vw\neq0)\ \Longrightarrow\ uw\neq0\qquad(u,v,w\in V).
\]
There are exactly \(16\) isomorphism classes of such semigroups of order \(5\), and exactly \(4\) have finitely based full power semirings. At order \(4\), the corresponding numbers are \(2\) and \(1\). The transitivity criterion has a sharp size boundary: at order \(6\), a nontransitive product digraph can nevertheless yield a finitely based full power semiring.

## Assumptions and scope
Multiplication is associative, and \(S^k\) denotes the set of products of \(k\) elements. The identity \(s^2=0\) is **elementwise**; it does not mean \(S^2=\{0\}\). The nilpotency index is exactly \(3\). For \(\mathcal P(S)\), addition is union and multiplication is setwise multiplication on *all* subsets of \(S\). Neither the empty subset nor \(\{0\}\) is a named constant. An equational basis is finite when all identities in this binary signature follow from finitely many identities.

The asserted classification concerns the nilpotent semigroups satisfying the displayed hypotheses, not all nilpotent semigroups of order five. 'Transitive' is graph-theoretic transitivity, including the requirement that every directed two-step walk has a shortcut; in a loopless digraph it forbids directed two-cycles.

## Proof
**Reduction to an atom digraph.** Since \(S^3=\{0\}\), each element of \(S^2\) annihilates \(S\) on either side. Set \(k=|S^2\setminus\{0\}|\ge1\). Every nonzero product is a product of two members of \(V=S\setminus S^2\), because an input in \(S^2\) would give zero. Wang–Yin–Wang's nilpotent criterion [1, Theorem 1.1] associates to \(S\) the relational structure \(\mathbf N(S)\) whose unary relation is all nonzero elements and whose binary relation is the set of ordered pairs with nonzero product, and proves:
\[
\mathcal P(S)\ \text{finitely based}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf N(S)\ \text{has finite homomorphism duality}.
\]
All elements of \(S^2\setminus\{0\}\) are isolated in its binary relation. Inclusion \(D_S\hookrightarrow\mathbf N(S)\) and a homomorphism back that fixes \(V\) and sends isolated output elements to any chosen vertex of \(V\) show homomorphism equivalence (the unary relation is everywhere). In particular, \(\mathbf N(S)\) has finite duality precisely when \(D_S\) does.

**The three-vertex digraph lemma.** Let \(D\) be any nonempty finite irreflexive digraph on at most three vertices with at least one arc. Then \(D\) has finite homomorphism duality if and only if it is transitive.

If \(D\) is transitive, it is acyclic; the vertices admit ranks by the lengths of longest incoming directed paths. Let \(h\in\{2,3\}\) be the largest number of vertices in a directed chain. Ranking gives a homomorphism from \(D\) to the transitively oriented complete digraph \(T_h\). A longest chain in \(D\) has all its shortcut arcs by transitivity, giving a homomorphism \(T_h\to D\). A finite directed graph maps to \(T_h\) exactly when it has no directed walk of length \(h\): absence of that walk excludes cycles and makes the incoming-longest-path rank at most \(h-1\). Thus the directed path with \(h\) arcs is a finite complete obstruction for \(D\).

For the converse, assume \(D\) is not transitive. First, if \(D\) contains a mutual pair of arcs, take its **mutual-edge graph** \(M\), with an undirected edge where both orientations occur. If \(M\) is triangle-free, it is bipartite because it has at most three vertices. Arbitrarily large odd cycles, with each edge directed both ways, do not map to \(D\); every proper induced subgraph is a union of bipartite paths and maps to the mutual pair. If \(M\) is a triangle, \(D\) contains the bidirected complete digraph on three vertices. Instead take the bidirected odd wheels: each has chromatic number \(4\) and hence does not map into the loopless three-vertex \(D\), whereas deletion of any vertex makes it \(3\)-colorable, hence homomorphic to the bidirected triangle in \(D\).

Now assume there is no mutual pair. If \(D\) contains a directed cycle, it must be the directed \(3\)-cycle. Directed cycles of arbitrarily large lengths not divisible by \(3\) cannot map into it (edge preservation advances colors modulo \(3\)), while deletion of any vertex leaves a disjoint union of directed paths that do map. In the remaining nontransitive case, \(D\) consists of the two consecutive arcs of a directed \(3\)-vertex path, possibly considered as the whole three-vertex digraph. For any integer \(j\ge2\), make an odd cycle of length \(2j+1\), orienting its successive edges with signs \(+,-,+,-,\ldots,+\) around the cycle. A homomorphism into the directed three-vertex path would require each directed edge to increase an integer level in \(\{0,1,2\}\) by exactly \(1\). Going around the cycle would make the total increment \(+1\), a contradiction. Deleting one vertex opens the cycle to paths whose oriented signed-prefix-sum range is at most \(2\), so the paths do map to the three-vertex path.

Each of the four witness families has unbounded order and every **proper induced subgraph** maps to \(D\). If finitely many obstructions described the homomorphism class of \(D\), take a witness larger than every obstruction. Some obstruction would map into the witness, but its image would be a proper induced subgraph of the witness, contradicting its homomorphism into \(D\). Thus \(D\) has no finite duality. This proves the lemma.

**Classification and counting.** Because all squares are zero, the product digraph has no loops. At order \(5\), \(|V|+k=4\), and a nonzero product requires \(|V|\ge2\). If \(k=1\), there are three atoms and one nonzero output \(z\). Every nonempty loopless directed graph on three vertices defines, and is recovered from, one multiplication table by assigning \(uv=z\) at each arc and making all other products zero. Relabeling the atoms corresponds exactly to graph isomorphism. Burnside's lemma gives \((2^6+3\cdot2^3+2\cdot2^2)/6=16\) graph isomorphism types including the empty graph, hence \(15\) nontrivial semigroups. Exactly four nonempty types are transitive: one edge, two edges with common source, two edges with common target, and the transitive three-vertex tournament. If \(k=2\), there are two atoms, and both opposite ordered products are nonzero and have different outputs to generate both outputs; this is a single additional isomorphism class, with a mutual pair and thus no finite basis. Therefore the total is \(15+1=16\), of which \(4\) are finitely based. At order \(4\), the two types are one arc and a mutual pair; exactly one is finitely based.

**Sharpness at six elements.** Let \(S_6=\{0,z,a,b,c,d\}\) and declare the only nonzero products to be \(ab=bc=bd=ad=z\). All triple products and all squares vanish. Its atom digraph has arcs \(a\to b\), \(b\to c\), \(b\to d\), \(a\to d\), but no \(a\to c\); hence it is not transitive. The coloring \(a\mapsto0\), \(b\mapsto1\), \(c,d\mapsto2\) is a homomorphism to \(T_3\), and the induced subdigraph on \(a,b,d\) is \(T_3\). These two homomorphisms show equivalence to \(T_3\), so finite duality and a finite equational basis still hold.

It also checks the graph-lemma case split and finite members of each infinite obstruction family, and checks both homomorphisms in the six-element boundary example. Its expected final lines are `VERIFY_OK`, `order4_classes=2 order4_finitely_based=1`, `order5_classes=16 order5_finitely_based=4`, and `order6_nontransitive_finitely_based_example=present`. The infinite-family arguments and the finite-basis equivalence are proved above, not inferred from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Wang, Yin, and Wang [1] prove the general equivalence between finite equational bases and finite relational duality, and give four- and six-element boundary examples (their Examples 4.4–4.5), but their published text does not enumerate this five-element elementwise-zero-square stratum, identify its exact \(16/4\) split, or state this transitivity cutoff with a sharp nontransitive six-element witness. The result here uses their general theorem; it does not reprove the broader nilpotent characterization. Distler and Mitchell [2] enumerate degree-three nilpotent semigroups, a broader combinatorial census, but do not stratify them by finite basability of full power semirings. The directed-path/transitive-tournament finite-duality pair is classical [3, Theorem 4] and accounts for the positive side of the graph criterion. The current claim is an exact small-semigroup boundary classification, not a new general duality theory.

## Limitations
The iff criterion is **restricted to order at most five and to vanishing element squares**; it fails at six elements as shown. The counts are for semigroup isomorphism, not anti-isomorphism, and refer only to nilpotency index exactly three. The construction supplies finite-basis existence via a published structural theorem, not an explicit minimal list of identities. Similar three-vertex digraph consequences may be implicit in older homomorphism-duality classifications; no claim of literature exhaustiveness or independent certification is made.
