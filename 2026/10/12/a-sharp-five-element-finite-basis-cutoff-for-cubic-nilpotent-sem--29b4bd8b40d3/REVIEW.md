# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The nilpotent finite-basis equivalence is used with its precise signature, nonempty multiplication relation and unary domain; all nonzero product outputs are isolated, making the atom digraph homomorphism equivalent to the original relational structure. The directed-graph dichotomy is proved by transitive-tournament colorings and by four arbitrarily large, induced-subgraph-critical obstruction families. The remaining classification is an exhaustive classification of the possible \(|S^2\setminus\{0\}|\) values at order five. The code independently checks semigroup tables and finite witness instances, but not the general theorem or the infinite obstruction argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest motivating source is Wang–Yin–Wang, arXiv:2610.01329v1, especially Theorem 1.1 and Examples 4.4–4.5. That paper supplies the broad reduction and two individual boundary examples; it does not state the exact five-element \(16/4\) split, the small-digraph transitivity equivalence, or the nontransitive six-element finite-basis boundary witness. Distler–Mitchell's arXiv:1201.3529 enumerates degree-three nilpotent semigroups but does not classify finite basability. Earlier homomorphism-duality work could implicitly contain parts of the small-digraph dichotomy; these parts are treated as ingredients, not separately claimed original.

**Value — PASS.** The first genuinely nontrivial small-order stratum of semigroups with vanishing element squares is fully classified for the difficult full-power-semiring finite-basis property, replacing a general computational duality test with the transparent exact three-vertex transitivity condition. The natural finite cutoff is shown sharp by a concrete order-six counterexample. The \(16/4\) partition is a naturally complete invariant for this stratum, rather than a single arbitrary numerical case.

**Limitations and remaining comparisons.** The published nilpotent criterion is assumed, and the result does not cover semigroups of index two, semigroups with a nonzero element square, or higher orders. Older digraph-homomorphism-duality sources may give equivalent small-core consequences without naming power semirings. Independent replication and comprehensive historical priority review remain outstanding.
