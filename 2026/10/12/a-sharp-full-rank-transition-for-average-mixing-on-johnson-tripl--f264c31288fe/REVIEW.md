# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The spectral projector \(E_3\) is independently reconstructed from the adjacency matrix, rather than inferred from a numerical table. Exact recurrence identities determine its four distance entries and the four rational eigenvalues of \(E_3\circ E_3\). For every integer \(n\ge7\), all denominators are positive and three explicitly expanded cubics have positive coefficients after the substitution \(h=n-7\); consecutive eigenvalue gaps prove that the positive \(\lambda_3\) is the least. The Schur product theorem transfers the lower bound to the complete average mixing matrix. At \(n=6\), complement-pair identical rows give rank at most ten, while the positive Schur-square sector has rank ten. The independent checker constructs full exact rational projectors for three small parameters and the complete average mixing matrix for the boundary case. Finite checks are not presented as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The existing spectral-projector identity (Godsil, 2011; Coutinho et al., 2017 and 2022), Krein nonnegativity (Godsil's association-scheme notes), and Burcroff's 2017 spectrum theorem for every Johnson-algebra element are general prerequisites. Burcroff's theorem already implies the *method* and resulting rational eigenvalues upon explicit substitution, so that spectrum computation by itself is not treated as novel. The evaluated contribution is the **strictly positive** single-projector square on all \(J(n,3)\), \(n\ge7\), plus the sharp antipodal rank exception. It supplies a defined family-level answer to a problem explicitly posed by Coutinho and Guo in 2024, rather than an alleged general answer. Searches and full-text comparisons found no explicit positivity/rank classification covering this family. An implicit consequence of previously developed Krein-parameter formulas or an unlocated general strict-positivity theorem remains a genuine coverage risk; no failed search is treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The problem is not an arbitrary parameter slice: rank of long-time quantum average mixing is a stated structural question for primitive distance-regular graphs. Johnson triple graphs form the first diameter-three Johnson family and exhibit a sharp transition from antipodal rank deficiency to strictly positive definite average mixing. The quantitative least-eigenvalue bound is a structural strengthening. The result does not prove the general open problem or assert rank information for higher Johnson diameters.

## Closest literature and limits
Coutinho and Guo, *Selected Open Problems in Continuous-Time Quantum Walks*, Section 4, Open Problem 4.1, gives the exact motivating question. Burcroff, *Johnson Schemes and Certain Matrices with Integral Eigenvalues*, Theorem 1.1/Corollary 7.5, gives a general algebraic spectral formula that dominates the pure eigenvalue-computation task but not an explicitly stated strict-positivity classification. Godsil's 2011 average-mixing paper and the 2017/2022 spectral papers provide the underlying mixing identity. The bound concerns adjacency-Hamiltonian long-time averaging only. The proof and checker have been inspected; independent validation has not been undertaken.
