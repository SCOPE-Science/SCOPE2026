# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a two-atom Poisson mixing distribution, the mass curvature has the exact sign factor \(2+2\cosh(\eta+kd)-kC\), where \(d=\log(b/a)\), \(C=(b-a)^2/(ab)\), and \(\eta=\log[(w/(1-w))e^{-(b-a)}]\). The strict-failure mixing-odds intervals have strictly decreasing upper endpoints and pairwise consecutive overlap; the arcosh derivative and strict concavity prove their union is a single half-line. A second strictly convex real-variable curvature function establishes a single finite block of failing indices. Boundary equality, pure-Poisson endpoints and escape of the first failure under vanishing contamination are proved separately. The extracted standalone verifier checks 2,240 polynomial identities and four exact-rational critical cases; no claim rests solely on finite enumeration. Proof assumptions: finite \(0<a<b\), \(0<w<1\), and integer \(k\ge1\).

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The closest inspected primary source, Valiquette et al. (arXiv:2305.17095v1, 2023), studies asymptotic tail classes of mixed Poisson distributions, including finite-upper-endpoint cases; its inspected theorems concern tail asymptotics, not the exact sharp global mass-log-concavity threshold for a two-atom mixing law. Puig (DOI:10.1111/sjos.12677), Section 3.2, studies weighted Poisson constructions and dispersion, which does not imply a necessary-and-sufficient ordinary mass-log-concavity phase diagram. A broad review by Karlis and Xekalaki (DOI:10.1111/j.1751-5823.2005.tb00250.x) was accessible at the abstract/bibliographic level only. Equivalent formulations in weighted-Poisson or strong-unimodality literature remain possible; independent novelty confirmation has not been performed. Le Cam’s undated manuscript on Poisson mixtures was inspected in selected pages on identifiability and statistical testing, without concluding full-manuscript noncoverage. The claimed contribution is the explicit one-sided cutoff, consecutive-block failure classification, boundary equalities, and vanishing-contamination discontinuity, not the elementary mixture identity itself.

## Value — PASS

Whether a mixed count distribution preserves global discrete log-concavity matters for discrete reliability, shape-constrained inference, and discrete unimodality-preserving arguments. The theorem supplies a complete exact parameter classification in the natural minimal nontrivial finite mixing family; it identifies an asymmetric stability phenomenon invisible to any fixed finite range of indices. The cutoff is not a routine algebraic restatement of overdispersion or tail asymptotics; the critical-index and overlapping-interval argument is essential. The result does not classify mixing laws with three or more intensities, does not assert ultra-log-concavity, and makes no numerical estimation guarantees.
