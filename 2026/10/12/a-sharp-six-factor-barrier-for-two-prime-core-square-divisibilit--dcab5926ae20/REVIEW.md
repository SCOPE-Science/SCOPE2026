# Review

## Correctness
PASS. For \(n=p^a q^b\) with \(p<q\), core-square divisibility forces the independent cross-divisibilities \(p^2\mid\sigma(q^b)\) and \(q^2\mid\sigma(p^a)\). The latter excludes \(a=1,2\). Under \(a+b\le5\), only \((a,b)=(3,2),(3,1),(4,1)\) remain. The first is eliminated by \(\sigma(p^3)=(p+1)(p^2+1)\); the second by \(p^2\mid q+1\) and a size comparison; the third by writing \(q+1=kp^2\), bounding \(k=1\), and observing that \(p^2-1\) is composite for odd \(p\). The exceptional small prime \(p=2\) is checked explicitly. Finally \(96\) verifies sharpness.

## Originality
PASS. The primary full text explicitly studies \(\gamma(n)^2\mid\sigma(n)\), completely handles support \(\{2,3\}\), and gives additional two-prime-support examples, but does not state a uniform total-multiplicity barrier. Searches using the exact divisibility, core/radical terminology, arbitrary two-prime support, \(\Omega\le5\), and the sharp witness returned no equivalent or dominating theorem. The closest indexed research results concern different sum-of-divisors valuation problems and do not imply this statement.

## Value
PASS. The source specifically notes that its \(\{2,3\}\) family does not exhaust the two-prime-support problem. Total multiplicity is a natural first structural invariant for the unresolved remainder. The theorem determines its exact minimum across all prime pairs and explains why the smallest source family begins at \(2^5\cdot3\). This is an unbounded structural obstruction, not a finite census.
