# Review of A single forbidden induced subgraph for \(3,d\)-perfection of complete multipartite graphs

## Correctness
PASS. In a complete multipartite graph every shortest path has at most two edges, and a three-vertex geodesic is exactly a \(2+1\) selection from two parts. The proof classifies when two colors avoid all such triples, computes the only clique-bound values needed for perfection, and exhibits \(K_{3,2,1}\) as a genuine gap with \(\chi_d^3=3\) and \(\omega_d^3=4\). The obstruction criterion is hereditary, so checking all induced subgraphs is built into the argument. Direct metric/hypergraph enumeration through order \(8\) agrees for \(d\in\{2,3,4\}\).

## Originality
PASS. The initiating 2026 paper defines \(k,d\)-perfection and explicitly asks which complete multipartite graphs are \(k,d\)-perfect. The 2025 complete-multipartite general-position coloring theorem determines the relevant coloring number but does not define or classify \(k,d\)-perfection and does not provide the clique-side obstruction. Targeted searches for the exact obstruction, equivalent \(3,2\)-perfect terminology, and broader complete-multipartite formulations found no covering statement. Residual risk is limited to unindexed or concurrent work on the recent invariant.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural complete classification answering a named open family from the defining paper. The result is structurally sharper than a parameter value: within the complete multipartite class the entire \(3,d\)-perfect family, for every \(d\ge2\), is characterized by exclusion of one six-vertex induced graph, \(K_{3,2,1}\). This also gives a restricted forbidden-induced-subgraph answer in the direction of the paper's broader perfection questions.
