# A trace law for cycle-count covariance and exact Cayley–Spearman coupling
## Finding
Let \(\Pi\) be uniformly distributed on \(S_n\), with \(n\ge2\), and let \(C_n\) be its number of cycles. For a deterministic real array
\[
A=(a_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le n},
\]
define the permutation linear statistic
\[
X_A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{i,\Pi(i)}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,X_A)
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}
-
\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^n a_{ij}.
}
\]
Thus \(C_n\) is uncorrelated with \(X_A\) exactly when the diagonal mean of \(A\) equals its grand mean.

For the squared rank displacement
\[
D_n=\sum_{i=1}^n(\Pi(i)-i)^2,
\]
the trace of its defining array is zero and
\[
\sum_{i,j=1}^n(i-j)^2=\frac{n^2(n^2-1)}6.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,D_n)
=
-\frac{n^2-1}{6}.
}
\]

Let
\[
K_n=n-C_n
\]
be the Cayley distance from the identity and let
\[
R_n
=
1-\frac{6D_n}{n(n^2-1)}
\]
be Spearman's no-ties rank coefficient. Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,R_n)=\frac1n,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,R_n)=-\frac1n.
}
\]
Writing
\[
H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j,
\qquad
H_n^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1{j^2},
\]
the classical cycle-count variance and the permutation-null Spearman variance give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C_n)
=
\operatorname{Var}(K_n)
=
H_n-H_n^{(2)},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(R_n)=\frac1{n-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(K_n,R_n)
=
-\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}
{n\sqrt{H_n-H_n^{(2)}}}.
}
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(K_n,R_n)
\sim
-\frac1{\sqrt{n\log n}}.
\]
This supplies an exact finite coefficient and the sharp first-order decay behind the asymptotic separation of Cayley-type cycle information and Spearman rank displacement.

## Assumptions and scope
The permutation is uniform on \(S_n\). The array \(A\) is deterministic and may be arbitrary; it need not be symmetric, centered, or generated by a metric.

The Cayley distance is the minimum number of transpositions needed to reach the identity, which equals \(n-C_n\). Spearman's coefficient is the standard complete-ranking statistic without ties.

The theorem does not assert independence. Vanishing covariance for a particular array is only a second-moment statement.

## Proof
For
\[
1\le i,j\le n,
\]
write
\[
E_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{\Pi(i)=j\}.
\]
Since \(\Pi\) is uniform,
\[
\mathbb P(E_{ij}=1)=\frac1n.
\]

If \(i=j\), conditioning on \(\Pi(i)=i\) leaves a uniform permutation of the remaining \(n-1\) labels and adds one fixed-point cycle. Hence
\[
\mathbb E[C_n\mid \Pi(i)=i]
=
1+H_{n-1}.
\]
If \(i\ne j\), contract the forced directed edge \(i\to j\). This gives a bijection from permutations satisfying \(\Pi(i)=j\) to permutations on \(n-1\) labels, and contraction preserves the number of cycles. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[C_n\mid \Pi(i)=j]
=
H_{n-1}.
\]
Since
\[
\mathbb EC_n=H_n=H_{n-1}+\frac1n,
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,E_{ij})
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{n-1}{n^2},&i=j,\\[6pt]
-\dfrac1{n^2},&i\ne j.
\end{cases}
\]

Now
\[
X_A=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}E_{ij},
\]
so linearity of covariance gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,X_A)
=
\frac{n-1}{n^2}\sum_i a_{ii}
-
\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}.
\]
Combining the diagonal and off-diagonal sums yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,X_A)
=
\frac1n\sum_i a_{ii}
-
\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}.
\]

For
\[
a_{ij}=(i-j)^2,
\]
the diagonal sum is zero. Also
\[
\sum_{i,j}(i-j)^2
=
2n\sum_i i^2-2\left(\sum_i i\right)^2
=
\frac{n^2(n^2-1)}6.
\]
Substitution proves
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,D_n)
=
-\frac{n^2-1}{6}.
\]
The Cayley and Spearman covariance formulas follow from their affine relations to \(C_n\) and \(D_n\).

Finally, the uniform cycle count has the same law as a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities
\[
1,\frac12,\ldots,\frac1n,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Var}(C_n)
=
\sum_{j=1}^n\left(\frac1j-\frac1{j^2}\right)
=
H_n-H_n^{(2)}.
\]
The standard permutation calculation for Spearman's coefficient gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(R_n)=\frac1{n-1}.
\]
Combining these variances with
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,R_n)=-\frac1n
\]
proves the exact correlation formula. Since
\[
H_n-H_n^{(2)}=\log n+O(1),
\]
the asymptotic relation follows.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` performs exact rational checks independent of the symbolic simplification above.

For every \(2\le n\le9\), it exhaustively enumerates all permutations and verifies the cycle-count mean and variance, the cycle/squared-displacement covariance, the two Cayley–Spearman covariances, the Spearman variance, and the correlation-squared identity.

For every \(2\le n\le8\), it also verifies the conditional cycle means for every matrix entry event \(\Pi(i)=j\) and checks the universal trace law on several nonsymmetric and symmetric integer arrays.

The finite computations are supplementary. The contraction argument proves the theorem for every \(n\ge2\) and every deterministic real array.

## Relationship to prior work
Jiang studies statistical dependence among metrics on the symmetric groups. The complete article explicitly places Cayley distance among the bi-invariant metrics, treats the Spearman family, motivates removal of redundant ranking information, and proves asymptotic independence of normalized Spearman statistics from broad classes of class functions. The article uses the number of cycles as a representative class statistic in this argument. It does not state the finite trace formula or the exact Cayley–Spearman covariance above.

Maples, Nikeghbali, and Zeindler study the total number of cycles of random permutations under generalized Ewens measures, including its mean, variance, and limiting behavior. Their focus is the cycle statistic itself rather than its covariance with permutation linear statistics.

Hultman develops character-theoretic formulas for expectations of permutation statistics on products of random permutations and includes cycle counts among the statistics considered. The accessible primary record does not state a covariance with Spearman displacement.

The present result complements the asymptotic metric literature by identifying an exact finite second-moment mechanism: cycle count interacts with every permutation linear statistic only through the difference between its diagonal mean and grand mean.

## Limitations
The trace law is specific to the uniform permutation measure. Under nonuniform measures, the conditional contraction law and matrix-entry probabilities generally change.

The result is a covariance identity, not a statement of finite independence. Its asymptotic Cayley–Spearman consequence is compatible with much stronger asymptotic-independence results.

Because the trace formula follows from an elementary contraction argument, an equivalent identity may occur in older unindexed work on random permutations, character theory, or permutation matrices under different notation.
