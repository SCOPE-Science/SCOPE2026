# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For non-induced copies of a motif without isolated vertices, incidence with an isolated host vertex vanishes inside the copy and is hypergeometric outside it. Summing over placements yields the exact covariance identity. The controlled logarithmic expansion of the falling-factorial ratio for growing trees gives \((1-1/\alpha)e^{2/\alpha}-1\), with exactly one nonzero root. The finite exact checker exhausts 113 graph/edge-count strata and confirms the analytic identities. It does not prove the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical risk

Itzkovitz et al. (2003) discuss expected motif counts in network ensembles; Ellis and Ferry (2010) analyze variance in independent-edge graphs; Sah (2022) proves distributional limit theorems for fixed subgraph counts in both sampling models. Janson (1994) develops broadly applicable random-graph-statistic methods. None of the materially inspected primary text states a universal growing-tree host-size threshold for *mixed isolation–motif covariance*. A general mixed-moment identity could entail the displayed finite formula, so the main additional claim is the growing-motif critical ratio. Janson's monograph was accessible only through bibliographic snippets; it remains a priority risk. A related finite fixed-edge formula for degree-one counts and motifs does not cover degree-zero counts for growing trees, and does not imply the specified critical ratio by a fixed-motif asymptotic.

## Value — PASS

Conditioning on exactly how many edges exist can reverse the sign of association between isolated vertices and a growing tree-motif count. Unlike a routine marginal moment computation, the result gives a shape-independent, exact all-edge-count sign rule and identifies a nontrivial universal transition \(\alpha_*=1.2550009749\ldots\) in a simultaneous growth regime. This directly informs model-dependent interpretation of motifs and network isolation. No claim about induced motifs or the critical boundary sequence is made.
