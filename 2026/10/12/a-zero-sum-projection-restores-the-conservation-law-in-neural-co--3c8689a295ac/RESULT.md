# A zero-sum projection restores the conservation law in neural-corrected SIR dynamics
## Finding
The normalized SIR model used by Annuš and Kmeť has the exact conservation law
\[
S(t)+I(t)+R(t)=1.
\]
The paper then augments a parameter-distorted SIR vector field by an unconstrained three-output neural residual:
\[
\dot x=f_{\mathrm{dist}}(x;\theta)+\lambda f_{\mathrm{NN}}(x,t),
\qquad
x=(S,I,R)^\top.
\]

Let
\[
\mathbf 1=(1,1,1)^\top.
\]
Since every closed-population SIR vector field satisfies
\[
\mathbf 1^\top f_{\mathrm{dist}}(x;\theta)=0,
\]
the hybrid total
\[
M(t)=S(t)+I(t)+R(t)
\]
satisfies
\[
\dot M(t)=\lambda\,\mathbf 1^\top f_{\mathrm{NN}}(x(t),t).
\]
Thus, for any nonzero neural coupling, exact population conservation is not inherited from the mechanistic baseline. It is guaranteed precisely when the neural correction is tangent to the SIR simplex.

The paper's residual target has the same structural issue. It is
\[
Y(t)=
\frac{d x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}}{dt}
-f_{\mathrm{dist}}(x_{\mathrm{obs}}(t);\theta),
\]
so
\[
\mathbf 1^\top Y(t)
=
\frac{d}{dt}
\mathbf 1^\top x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}(t).
\]
The stated data pipeline perturbs the three compartments and clips them componentwise to \([0,1]\); it does not impose a subsequent simplex normalization. Therefore fluctuations in the preprocessed observed total are presented to the network as a residual component normal to the SIR simplex.

For this controlled experiment, that normal component is unnecessary. Both the reference dynamics and the distorted dynamics are SIR systems, hence their exact vector-field difference is zero-sum.

The exact repair is the orthogonal projector
\[
P=I-\frac13\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top.
\]
Using
\[
P f_{\mathrm{NN}}
\]
in the hybrid ODE guarantees conservation for every \(\lambda\). Projecting the training targets by \(P\) has an additional optimality property: because the exact parameter-bias residual lies in the tangent plane, projection cannot increase its pointwise Euclidean estimation error.

## Assumptions and scope
The argument concerns the normalized, closed-population SIR experiment printed in the source:
\[
\dot S=-\beta SI,\qquad
\dot I=\beta SI-\gamma I,\qquad
\dot R=\gamma I.
\]
The true and distorted models use this same compartmental structure with different parameter values.

The neural correction is treated exactly as specified in the paper: an unconstrained three-component feedforward output added to the distorted vector field with scalar coupling \(\lambda\).

The Savitzky–Golay derivative operator is used componentwise, as stated in the preprocessing procedure. Only its linearity is needed.

The claim is about structural conservation of the continuous-time hybrid vector field. It does not assert that any particular numerical trajectory in the paper exhibits a specified amount of drift. A numerical integrator can also introduce its own small conservation error even when the vector field is tangent.

## Proof
For the distorted SIR field,
\[
f_{\mathrm{dist}}(S,I,R)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\beta SI\\
\beta SI-\gamma I\\
\gamma I
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathbf 1^\top f_{\mathrm{dist}}
=
-\beta SI+\beta SI-\gamma I+\gamma I
=0.
\]
Applying \(\mathbf 1^\top\) to the hybrid equation gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(S+I+R)
=
\lambda\,\mathbf 1^\top f_{\mathrm{NN}}(x,t).
\]
Hence a correction field satisfying
\[
\mathbf 1^\top f_{\mathrm{NN}}\equiv0
\]
preserves every affine mass leaf. Conversely, if the hybrid vector field is to preserve total population for every initial state and every positive coupling strength, its neural component must have zero sum at every state-time pair reached by those trajectories; global structural preservation is obtained by imposing the identity everywhere.

For the training target,
\[
Y=
\frac{d x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}}{dt}
-f_{\mathrm{dist}}(x_{\mathrm{obs}};\theta).
\]
Taking the component sum and using the zero sum of the SIR field gives
\[
\mathbf 1^\top Y
=
\mathbf 1^\top
\frac{d x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}}{dt}.
\]
Because Savitzky–Golay differentiation is linear and is applied componentwise,
\[
\mathbf 1^\top
\frac{d x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}}{dt}
=
\frac{d}{dt}
\mathbf 1^\top x_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{SG}}.
\]
Therefore any nonconstant total in the preprocessed observations is encoded directly as a nonconservative residual target.

Now compare with the exact residual generated by parameter distortion alone:
\[
r(x)=f_{\mathrm{true}}(x)-f_{\mathrm{dist}}(x).
\]
Both terms are SIR fields, so
\[
\mathbf 1^\top r(x)=0.
\]
Thus the target's normal component is not required to represent the controlled model discrepancy.

Define
\[
P=I-\frac13\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top.
\]
Then
\[
P^\top=P,\qquad
P^2=P,\qquad
\mathbf 1^\top P=0.
\]
Therefore \(P\) is the orthogonal projector onto
\[
T=\{v\in\mathbb R^3:\mathbf 1^\top v=0\}.
\]
Replacing the correction by \(P f_{\mathrm{NN}}\) yields
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(S+I+R)=0.
\]

Finally, let \(r\in T\). Orthogonal decomposition gives
\[
Y-r
=
P(Y-r)+(I-P)Y,
\]
and the two terms are perpendicular. Hence
\[
\|Y-r\|_2^2
=
\|PY-r\|_2^2+\|(I-P)Y\|_2^2,
\]
which proves
\[
\|PY-r\|_2\le\|Y-r\|_2.
\]
Thus the conservation projection is also pointwise non-worsening relative to the exact residual class of the paper's controlled parameter-bias experiment.

## Verification
The bundled `verification/verify.py` checks with exact rational arithmetic that
\[
P^\top=P,\qquad
P^2=P,\qquad
\mathbf 1^\top P=0,
\]
and verifies the Pythagorean error decomposition for representative rational residual targets and zero-sum exact residuals.

The checker also reconstructs symbolic rational SIR true and distorted derivatives at representative positive states and confirms that their difference is zero-sum.

These finite checks are only replays of the algebra. The general result follows from the identities in the proof and does not depend on simulation.

## Relationship to prior work
The motivating paper explicitly notes that SIR dynamics have conservation constraints, but its published hybrid equation adds a free three-output neural field. Its data pipeline perturbs and clips compartments independently, and the paper later identifies physical or regularization-based constraints on the learned residual as a direction for future work. The exact mass-drift identity, the target-normal-component identity, and the orthogonal non-worsening repair above are not stated there.

The general need for invariant-preserving neural dynamics is established prior art. Yu et al. describe unconstrained neural ODE drift away from conservation manifolds and propose invariant-by-construction architectures. Zinihi, Ehrhardt, and Sidi Ammi develop a structure-preserving Neural ODE framework and discuss exact conservation through Patankar-type extensions, including experiments on SIR dynamics.

Those works motivate conservation-preserving learning in general. The present result is narrower: it identifies the exact defect in the 2026 residual-correction SIR pipeline and proves that, because the paper's controlled discrepancy is only a parameter change within the same SIR family, orthogonal zero-sum projection cannot worsen pointwise Euclidean error to the exact residual.

## Limitations
The finding does not reconstruct the trained neural weights or claim a numerical lower bound on conservation drift in any published figure.

The projection
\[
P=I-\frac13\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top
\]
enforces the linear conservation law but does not by itself enforce nonnegativity of \(S\), \(I\), and \(R\). Positivity requires a separate structural or numerical mechanism.

For real epidemic data, births, deaths, migration, reporting corrections, and changing population denominators may make strict conservation inappropriate. The non-worsening statement is specific to the paper's controlled same-structure experiment, in which both reference and distorted vector fields are closed-population SIR systems.
