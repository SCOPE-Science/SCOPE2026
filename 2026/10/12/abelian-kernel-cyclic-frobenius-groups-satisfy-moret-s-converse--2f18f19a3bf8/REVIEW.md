# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. Fixed-point-freeness on the finite abelian kernel makes \(k\mapsto k^{-1}k^h\) bijective for every nonidentity complement element, so the induced action on nonprincipal kernel characters is semiregular. Each orbit therefore induces one irreducible character of degree \(m\), and the degree-squared sum exhausts \(\operatorname{Irr}(G)\). The induced kernel is exactly \(N_\theta=\bigcap_h\ker(\theta^h)\), giving codegree \(|K:N_\theta|\). Inflated codegrees are exact element orders in the cyclic complement, while induced codegrees have the same prime support as an explicitly available squarefree-order element of the abelian kernel. The packaged replay checks the mechanism across six structurally distinct kernels and complements.

## Originality

PASS. The primary 2021 source gives a negative answer to Moretó's question in general and builds counterexamples using nonabelian extraspecial structure. The closest Frobenius-codegree follow-up treats only the special case of exactly three codegrees. Searches under Moretó, Frobenius, abelian-kernel, cyclic-complement, and codegree-prime-support formulations found no theorem covering arbitrary finite abelian kernels. The main residual risk is an older equivalent statement presented purely as standard Frobenius character theory.

## Value

PASS. This gives a broad positive boundary for a question known to fail even among solvable groups. The theorem explains the positive behavior by a clean separation of character codegrees between kernel and complement and strengthens the desired containment to exact equality of prime supports. It covers non-elementary abelian kernels as well as familiar affine and generalized-dihedral examples.
