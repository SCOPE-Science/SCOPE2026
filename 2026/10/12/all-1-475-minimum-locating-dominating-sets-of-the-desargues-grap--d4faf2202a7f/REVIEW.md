# Review of the Desargues locating-dominating classification

## Correctness — PASS

The exact mathematical object is \(H(5,2)\) realized on left and right copies of the ten edges of \(K_5\), with cross-edges for disjoint pairs. Ordinary locating-domination requires all omitted vertices to have distinct nonempty open-neighborhood traces on the selected set. The lower-bound proof reduces any hypothetical seven-set to a \(3+4\) split, derives an equality case for six distinct three-bit traces, and rules out both possible pairwise-intersecting triple-edge shapes. It then exhibits five valid eight-vertex star codes. The count of \(1{,}475\) is obtained by an exhaustive \(2^{20}\)-mask decision, with two independent exact predicates on every mask. The complete automorphism group is reconstructed by the distance-two line graph on one bipartition side and the central swap; all thirteen orbit representatives, lengths, and coverage are verified. There is no appeal to random sampling, timeouts, or extrapolation. Residual risk: the finite count relies on the explicitly distributed code and its correct execution; the direct lower bound does not.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Hernando–Mora–Pelayo (arXiv:1506.03442v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03442) give the bipartite ordinary LD definition and general complements/global tools, but their inspected full text does not specify Desargues or this census. Stroppel–Stroppel (2012/017, university-hosted 25-page paper) establishes the same five-label incidence representation and the underlying \(S_5\) symmetries, without locating-domination. MathWorld's Desargues entry states the order, model and symmetry order, not minimum locating-dominating configurations. The distinct identifying-code literature (Bousquet et al., arXiv:1407.5833v1) imposes a strictly stronger vertex-distinguishing requirement, so an optimal identifying-code subset does not enumerate all ordinary LD codes. Hernando et al. (arXiv:1711.01951v1) give later general bipartite LD results but no graph-specific classification in the inspected text. Closest comparable results concern different graphs, parameters, or a proper subfamily of stronger codes. Remaining risk: equivalent historical statements under configuration-separating families, test covers, or older surveys may be unindexed or inaccessible. No evidence encountered supplies a stronger or equivalent complete \(1{,}475\)-set, thirteen-orbit classification.

## Value — PASS

The Desargues graph is the canonical twenty-vertex cubic distance-transitive bipartite incidence graph. Minimum location-domination gives the exact smallest number of landmarks necessary to cover and distinguish every unselected vertex, but a scalar optimum does not say how many placements exist or which geometrically inequivalent placements remain. The classification answers this natural finite optimization question completely: \(1{,}475\) optimum placements, a \(3+5/4+4/5+3\) side split, and thirteen symmetry inequivalent types. The structural seven-vertex obstruction provides a nontrivial reason for the optimum size. The answer is useful in discussing distinguishability and placement diversity for this benchmark graph, independently of any general family theorem. No significance beyond this finite complete classification is claimed.
