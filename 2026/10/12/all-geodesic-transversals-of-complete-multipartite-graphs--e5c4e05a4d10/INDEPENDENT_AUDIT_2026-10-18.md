# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In a connected complete multipartite graph, every length-two shortest path has its endpoints in one non-singleton part and middle vertex outside; these geodesics are maximal. A single edge is maximal exactly if it joins two singleton parts. The omitted-vertex set T contains no entire maximal geodesic iff either T lies within one non-singleton part, or T takes at most one vertex per non-singleton part and at most one singleton-part vertex total. Disjoint counting yields C_G(y)=(1+sy)product_(i in I)(1+n_i y)+sum_(i in I)((1+y)^(n_i)-1-n_i y); the maximum size of T is max(M,q+1_(s>0)), so gt=N-max(...). Counts of minimum sets follow from choosing full maximum parts versus sparse complements. Independent brute force reconstructed maximal shortest paths and checked the criterion and every coefficient in seven example part profiles (including all singleton and mixed parts). General classification is proved combinatorially, not by that finite enumeration.

## originality

PASS

The published graph-join theory provides scalar geodesic-transversal numbers, including the complete-bipartite case and potentially the full multipartite minimum by iteration; it does not supply the structural classification of every valid omitted set or its size polynomial in the inspected abstract. Earlier literature for geodetic, strong edge geodetic, and Steiner sets of complete multipartite graphs uses different covering definitions. The survivor is all-set enumeration, not first discovery of scalar gt.

## value

PASS

Complete size enumerator and exact basis classification for arbitrary part profiles are natural finite graph invariants with potential use in counting and optimization; they distinguish two mechanisms for maximal omitted sets and include stars, complete graphs, bipartite and Turán cases. The value does not depend on claiming a new scalar number derivable from joins.
