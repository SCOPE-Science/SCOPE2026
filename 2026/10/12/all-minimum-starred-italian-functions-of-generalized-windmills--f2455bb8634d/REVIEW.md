# Review of All minimum starred Italian functions of generalized windmills

## Correctness
PASS. If the zero set is nonempty and not dominating, some positive outer vertex has no zero neighbor; in a generalized windmill this forces the center and its entire clique blade positive. Weight \(r+1\) then fails the Italian condition on every other blade. At weight \(r+2\), a center label \(1\) permits at most one additional positive unit beyond the forced blade, so with at least three blades one completely zero blade remains and again violates the Italian condition. Thus the center is \(2\), the forced blade is all \(1\), and every other outer vertex is \(0\). The converse is immediate. Exhaustive verification of \(3{,}447{,}765\) ternary labelings across nine parameter pairs matches the complete minimum family.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational 2021 paper was inspected in searchable full text. Its Theorem 4.6 already gives the scalar value \(r+2\) after substituting \(G=mK_r\), so that value is explicitly treated as prior coverage rather than new. The inspected paper contains no friendship or windmill specialization and does not classify or count the equality functions for \(N_1+G\). Targeted exact web searches and semantic published-result searches under starred Italian, friendship, generalized windmill, Dutch windmill, and \(K_1\vee mK_r\) formulations found no equivalent optimizer classification.

## Value
PASS. The source theorem gives only the optimum weight for universal joins. The present result resolves the equality structure on a standard block-graph family: for at least three blades every optimum is forced onto exactly one blade, giving a closed optimizer count \(m\). This is a natural complete classification that distinguishes the multi-blade regime from the exceptional two-blade boundary.
