# All optimal ternary four-row perfect hash families are orthogonal arrays
## Finding
Let \(C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^4\) be a ternary four-row perfect hash family of strength three: for every three distinct columns \(x,y,z\in C\), some coordinate contains the three distinct symbols \(0,1,2\). Then \(|C|\le 9\). Equality holds exactly for orthogonal arrays \(\operatorname{OA}(9,4,3,2)\): every pair of coordinates projects \(C\) bijectively onto \(\{0,1,2\}^2\). There are exactly \(72\) labeled unordered equality cases. Under the natural group \((S_3)^4\rtimes S_4\), of order \(31{,}104\), all \(72\) lie in one orbit and each has stabilizer order \(432\).

One representative is
\[
\{0000,0111,0222,1012,1120,1201,2021,2102,2210\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Columns are distinct elements of \(\{0,1,2\}^4\), and a family is an unordered set. The perfect-hash condition is required for every three-element subset of columns. The equivalence group consists of independent permutations of the three symbols in each of the four coordinates, followed by a permutation of the four coordinates. No statement is made here for other numbers of rows, alphabet sizes, or strengths.

## Proof
Independent symbol permutations can send any chosen column to \(0000\). Therefore, if a family of size at least \(10\) existed, one of its translates under the natural group would contain \(0000\). An exact branch-and-bound search over the remaining \(80\) columns, using the defining three-column separation condition as the only compatibility test, proves that a family containing \(0000\) has size at most \(9\). The same search enumerates exactly eight size-nine families containing \(0000\).

Each of those eight normalized equality cases is checked directly to be an \(\operatorname{OA}(9,4,3,2)\). Conversely, every \(\operatorname{OA}(9,4,3,2)\) is a strength-three perfect hash family. Indeed, in a strength-two orthogonal array of index one, any two distinct columns agree in at most one coordinate. If three distinct columns had no coordinate carrying all three symbols, then in each of the four coordinates at least one of the three column-pairs would agree. By the pigeonhole principle, one pair would agree in at least two coordinates, a contradiction.

Finally, exhaustive application of all \(31{,}104\) elements of \((S_3)^4\rtimes S_4\) to one representative produces exactly \(72\) distinct families. Exactly eight of them contain \(0000\), matching the complete normalized census. Hence the orbit is the complete set of equality cases; orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer order \(31{,}104/72=432\).

## Relationship to prior work
Kim's 2003 thesis gives an explicit \(\operatorname{PHF}(4;9,3,3)\) constructed from two mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order three, so existence at size nine and the design construction are prior work. Walker and Colbourn's 2007 table for strength three records the entry \(k=9\) for \(N=4\) and alphabet size \(v=3\). The present result is the converse equality classification: every size-nine family is an orthogonal array, together with the exact count \(72\) and the single natural-equivalence orbit with stabilizer \(432\). The inspected sources do not state that equality classification.

## Limitations
The proof is a finite exact classification at one parameter. It does not supply a general equality theorem for perfect hash families. A 2003 thesis is the earliest inspected source that explicitly exhibits the same parameter, but the repository metadata exposes only the year; no calendar day is inferred from that record. The structured public-date field therefore uses the earliest fully day-resolved inspected source associated with the same parameter, while retaining the older year-only source in the references. An unindexed historical enumeration could still duplicate the equality census; targeted searches and inspection of the closest same-parameter sources found none.
