# All six \((-2)\)-curves on Roulleau’s explicit type \(S_2\) quartic descend to \(\mathbb Q\)
## Finding
Let \(Y/\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb P^3\) be the smooth quartic of Example 26 in Xavier Roulleau’s *On the geometry of K3 surfaces with finite automorphism group and no elliptic fibrations*. Then
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)
\]
acts trivially on
\[
\operatorname{{NS}}(Y_{{\overline{{\mathbb Q}}}}).
\]
In particular, each of the six geometric \((-2)\)-curves on \(Y\) is individually defined over \(\mathbb Q\). The three distinguished quadrics whose sections split into pairs of smooth rational quartics are also all defined over \(\mathbb Q\).

The source explicitly gives
\[
Q_1:xy+y^2+2xz+yz+z^2+3xt+3yt=0,
\qquad
Q_2:3xy-zt=0,
\]
and proves that each cuts \(Y\) into two smooth rational quartics. Define rational Segre parametrizations
\[
\phi_1([a:b],[c:d])=[ac+3ad+bc:-3ad-bc:-bc:ad+bc+bd]
\]
and
\[
\phi_2([a:b],[c:d])=[ac:bd:3ad:bc].
\]
Both coordinate transformations from \((ac,ad,bc,bd)\) have determinant \(3\), so they identify \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) with \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\), respectively. If \(F\) is the quartic equation of \(Y\), exact substitution gives
\[
F\circ\phi_1=-f_{{11}}f_{{13}},
\qquad
F\circ\phi_2=-3f_{{21}}f_{{23}},
\]
where
\[
\begin{{aligned}}
f_{{11}}={}&ac^3+9ac^2d+27acd^2+27ad^3+3bc^3+9bc^2d+9bcd^2,\\
f_{{13}}={}&27a^3c-648a^3d-318a^2bc-728a^2bd-41ab^2c+209ab^2d-11b^3c-8b^3d,\\
f_{{21}}={}&ac^3+9ac^2d+27acd^2+27ad^3-9bd^3,\\
f_{{23}}={}&9a^3c+27a^3d+3a^2bc+9a^2bd+9ab^2c+6ab^2d-24b^3c-8b^3d.
\end{{aligned}}
\]
The first and third factors have bidegree \((1,3)\), while the second and fourth have bidegree \((3,1)\). Thus each factor has degree \(4\) under the Segre embedding. Because the source proves that each \(Q_i\cap Y\) has exactly two smooth irreducible rational quartic components, these rational factors are precisely the two components. Hence the first four \((-2)\)-curves are individually defined over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the specific quartic of Example 26, not every member of the type \(S_2\) moduli space. The field-of-definition conclusion uses the source’s geometric results that this exact surface has geometric Picard number \(3\), Néron–Severi lattice \(S_2\), exactly six \((-2)\)-curves, and a third quadric section splitting into the final two curves.

“Defined over \(\mathbb Q\)” means descent of the corresponding closed curve or quadric as a subscheme; it does not assert that any of the rational curves has a \(\mathbb Q\)-rational point.

## Proof
The displayed factorizations show that the two components of \(Q_1\cap Y\) and the two components of \(Q_2\cap Y\) are each defined over \(\mathbb Q\). Let their classes be \(B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4\), and let \(H\) be the hyperplane class. Roulleau proves for Example 26 that these classes generate a lattice of type \(S_2\), and his reduction argument proves that \(Y_\mathbb C\) has Picard number \(3\). Proposition 24 then identifies the full geometric Néron–Severi lattice with \(S_2\). Consequently the sublattice generated by the \(\mathbb Q\)-defined classes \(H,B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4\) is the entire group
\[
\operatorname{{NS}}(Y_{{\overline{{\mathbb Q}}}}),
\]
so the absolute Galois action on this group is trivial.

The source also proves that a type \(S_2\) surface has exactly six \((-2)\)-curves. Let \(B\) be either of the two not already obtained from \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\), and let \(\sigma\) be an absolute Galois element. Triviality of the Néron–Severi action gives \([\sigma(B)]=[B]\). If \(\sigma(B)
e B\), then two distinct irreducible effective curves would have intersection
\[
B\cdot\sigma(B)=B^2=-2,
\]
which is impossible because distinct irreducible curves on a smooth surface have nonnegative intersection number. Hence \(\sigma(B)=B\) for every \(\sigma\), so each remaining curve descends to \(\mathbb Q\).

Finally, Roulleau uses the fact that a smooth rational quartic in \(\mathbb P^3\) lies on a unique quadric. The source’s third distinguished quadric \(Q_3\) contains either of the two remaining curves. Since that curve is defined over \(\mathbb Q\), Galois preserves its unique containing quadric. Therefore \(Q_3\) is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) as well. The printed \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\) already have rational coefficients, proving the assertion for all three quadrics.

## Relationship to prior work
Roulleau proves that type \(S_2\) K3 surfaces have three quadric sections, each a union of two smooth rational quartics, and that the six curves are the only \((-2)\)-curves. For Example 26 he prints \(Y\), \(Q_1\), and \(Q_2\), proves that the first four curves generate a lattice of type \(S_2\), establishes geometric Picard number \(3\), and concludes that a third quadric exists. His later atlas restates the complex-geometric type \(S_2\) description.

The refinement here is arithmetic: the two printed reducible sections split already over \(\mathbb Q\), which forces the entire geometric Néron–Severi group to have trivial Galois action and therefore forces the last two \((-2)\)-curves and the third distinguished quadric to descend to \(\mathbb Q\). Focused searches using field-of-definition, Galois-action, and Picard-group formulations found no implication-equivalent statement in the inspected sources or indexed records.

## Limitations
No explicit equation for the third quadric is claimed. The result does not determine \(Y(\mathbb Q)\), the Brauer group, the arithmetic automorphism group beyond what follows from the geometric source, or the fields of definition of other curves of positive genus. Literature searches cannot exclude an unindexed or unpublished observation of the same descent phenomenon.
