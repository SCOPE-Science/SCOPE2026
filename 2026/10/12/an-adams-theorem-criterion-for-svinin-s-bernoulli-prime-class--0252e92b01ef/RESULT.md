# An Adams-theorem criterion for Svinin's Bernoulli prime class
## Finding
Let \(p\ge 5\) be prime and write the Bernoulli number in lowest terms as
\[
B_{p-1}=\frac{A_p}{D_p},\qquad \gcd(A_p,D_p)=1.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{p-1}{2}\mid A_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{for every prime }q\mid\frac{p-1}{2},\;q-1\nmid p-1.
\]
Equivalently, for the function \(\chi\) introduced in arXiv:2607.18607v1,
\[
\chi(p)=2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{no prime }q\mid\frac{p-1}{2}\text{ satisfies }q-1\mid p-1.
\]
This exact criterion disproves Conjecture 2.1 of that preprint. The conjectured quadratic-form side consists, for primes \(p>3\), exactly of primes \(p\equiv 11\pmod{12}\). Every prime
\[
p\equiv 551\pmod{660}
\]
is therefore represented by \(-x^2+2xy+2y^2\), but it fails the Bernoulli divisibility because \(11\mid (p-1)/2\) and \(10\mid p-1\). Dirichlet's theorem consequently supplies infinitely many counterexamples.

The least counterexample is
\[
p=1871=-27^2+2\cdot27\cdot25+2\cdot25^2.
\]
Indeed, \((p-1)/2=935\) is divisible by \(11\), while \(10\mid1870\), so the von Staudt--Clausen theorem puts \(11\) in the denominator of \(B_{1870}\). Hence the reduced numerator of \(B_{1870}\) is not divisible by \(11\), and therefore is not divisible by \(935\).

## Assumptions and scope
Bernoulli numbers use the convention \(t/(e^t-1)=\sum_{n\ge0}B_n t^n/n!\). For a nonzero rational number, \(\operatorname{num}\) and \(\operatorname{denom}\) mean numerator and positive denominator after reduction. The theorem is about odd primes \(p\ge5\); the source's prime class also contains the small prime \(p=3\), which is harmless and can be checked separately.

The primary mathematical ingredients are the von Staudt--Clausen denominator theorem and Adams' theorem for Bernoulli numerators. The latter says that if an odd prime \(q>3\) and an integer \(e\ge1\) satisfy \(q^e\mid n\) and \(q-1\nmid n\), then \(q^e\) divides the numerator of \(B_n\).

## Proof
Put \(m=(p-1)/2\).

First assume \(m\mid A_p\). Let \(q\) be any prime divisor of \(m\). If \(q-1\mid p-1\), the von Staudt--Clausen theorem says that \(q\mid D_p\). Since \(A_p/D_p\) is reduced, this implies \(q\nmid A_p\), contradicting \(q\mid m\mid A_p\). Thus every prime \(q\mid m\) satisfies \(q-1\nmid p-1\).

Conversely, suppose every prime \(q\mid m\) satisfies \(q-1\nmid p-1\). Then \(q\ne2\), because \(1\mid p-1\), and \(q\ne3\), because \(2\mid p-1\). Hence each such \(q\) is an odd prime larger than \(3\). If \(q^e\Vert m\), then \(q^e\mid p-1\), and Adams' theorem gives \(q^e\mid A_p\). Multiplying over the distinct prime powers of \(m\) yields \(m\mid A_p\). This proves the criterion.

The source proves
\[
\chi(p)=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(p-1,A_p)}.
\]
The denominator \(D_p\) is even by von Staudt--Clausen, so \(A_p\) is odd. Therefore \(\chi(p)=2\) is equivalent to \(m\mid A_p\), giving the displayed classification of the class \(P_2\).

Now consider the quadratic form
\[
F(x,y)=-x^2+2xy+2y^2=3y^2-(x-y)^2.
\]
For a represented prime \(p>3\), reduction modulo \(3\) gives \(p\equiv2\pmod3\), and parity in the final expression gives \(p\equiv3\pmod4\). Thus \(p\equiv11\pmod{12}\). Conversely, if \(p\equiv11\pmod{12}\), then \((3/p)=1\), so \(p\) splits in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt3]\), whose discriminant is \(12\). Minkowski's bound is \(\sqrt{12}/2=\sqrt3<2\), hence this ring has class number one. A prime ideal above \(p\) is therefore generated by some \(u+y\sqrt3\) of norm \(\pm p\). Norm \(+p\) is impossible modulo \(4\), because \(u^2-3y^2\) never has residue \(3\pmod4\). Hence \(u^2-3y^2=-p\), or \(p=3y^2-u^2\); putting \(x=u+y\) gives \(p=F(x,y)\). Thus, for primes \(p>3\), representability is exactly \(p\equiv11\pmod{12}\).

For every prime \(p\equiv551\pmod{660}\), one has \(p\equiv11\pmod{12}\), so the form represents \(p\), while \(110\mid p-1\). Hence \(11\mid m\) and \(10\mid p-1\), violating the exact Bernoulli criterion. Since \(\gcd(551,660)=1\), Dirichlet's theorem gives infinitely many primes in this progression.

It remains to justify minimality of \(1871\). A represented counterexample \(p>3\) has \(p-1\equiv10\pmod{12}\) and, by the exact criterion, admits a prime \(q\mid m\) with \(q-1\mid p-1\). The congruences force \(q\equiv11\pmod{12}\): the factor \(q-1\) must have only one factor of \(2\) and cannot be divisible by \(3\). Thus \(q\ge11\). Since \(q(q-1)\mid p-1\), writing \(p-1=q(q-1)k\) and reducing modulo \(12\) gives \(k\equiv5\pmod6\). For \(q=11\) and \(p<1871\), the only possibilities are \(k=5\) and \(k=11\), giving \(p=551=19\cdot29\) and \(p=1211=7\cdot173\). For \(q\ge23\), already \(5q(q-1)\ge2530>1870\). Hence no smaller represented prime can fail the divisibility. The integer \(1871\) is prime, is represented by \((x,y)=(27,25)\), and fails through \(q=11\), so it is the least counterexample.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It checks primality of \(1871\), the displayed quadratic-form representation, the obstruction \(11\mid935\) and \(10\mid1870\), the factorisations of the two smaller forced candidates, and an independent finite enumeration of all represented-congruence primes below \(1871\) against the obstruction criterion. It also verifies the progression congruences for the infinite family. The script prints a single `VERIFY_OK` line on success.

These finite checks do not prove Adams' theorem, von Staudt--Clausen, Dirichlet's theorem, or the class-number argument; those are the stated mathematical inputs to the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Svinin's arXiv:2607.18607v1 derives \(\chi(p)=(p-1)/\gcd(p-1,\operatorname{num}(B_{p-1}))\), tabulates the first ten primes in \(P_2\), observes agreement with OEIS A141123, and states Conjecture 2.1 asserting equivalence between \((p-1)/2\mid\operatorname{num}(B_{p-1})\) and representation by \(-x^2+2xy+2y^2\). The source explicitly invites a proof or disproof.

OEIS A141123 records that the represented primes are \(2\) together with the primes congruent to \(11\pmod{12}\); it includes \(1871\), but it does not state the Bernoulli divisibility criterion or identify the failure at \(1871\). Thangadurai's 2004 paper restates the classical Adams theorem used here, but does not connect it to Svinin's 2026 prime class or quadratic-form conjecture. Focused published-finding corpus and web searches for the conjecture, the value \(1871\), Adams' theorem in this setting, and equivalent formulations did not locate the criterion, the infinite progression of counterexamples, or the least-counterexample statement.

## Limitations
The exact criterion classifies the Bernoulli divisibility in terms of prime divisors of \((p-1)/2\); it does not give a simple single congruence class for all primes in \(P_2\). The infinite counterexample progression \(551\pmod{660}\) is sufficient, not claimed to exhaust the counterexamples. The quadratic-form converse uses standard algebraic number theory for \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\). No claim is made about other classes \(P_\lambda\) from the source paper.
