# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(n\geq1\) male and \(n\) female founders, independent individual survival \(0<p<1\), and independent binary habitat suitability \(0<r\leq1\), the objective is exactly the complement of two site-failure probabilities. At fixed first-site population size, strict convexity of \(t^x\) for \(t=1-p\) enforces balanced sexes. The identity \((1-r\sqrt{uv})^2-(1-ru)(1-rv)=r(\sqrt u-\sqrt v)^2\) excludes all odd-sized releases. Paired allocations reduce to minimizing an explicitly displayed strictly concave quadratic in \(S=t^j+t^{n-j}\), so only concentration or nearest-even division can optimize. Algebraic comparison gives the precise tie/decision rule, and the even-budget bracket is strictly monotone and has exactly one root. All quantifiers and endpoint exclusions are explicit. Exact-fraction enumeration of 13,088 profiles at sixteen rational parameter pairs supplements, but does not replace, the arbitrary-size proof. No ecological persistence beyond initial one-step pair viability is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated residual risk

The strongest thematic antecedent is Grevstad (1999), which examines release number and size with environmental variability and Allee effects in a simulation-based establishment model. Jerde, Bampfylde and Lewis (2009) study the establishment of sexual species; Duncan et al. (2014) study one-population introduction size under stochastic establishment; Cassey et al. (2014) and Koontz et al. (2018) study alternative release regimes. None of the accessible material establishes the same two-site independent-suitability, fixed known-sex-founder *complete global allocation theorem* or its exact even-budget threshold. Published mathematical-result searches for equivalent allocation, insurance-versus-concentration and sex-pair feasibility did not identify broader dominating coverage. Multiple relevant articles were inspected only at abstract level; completeness of originality is therefore not independently established.

## Value — PASS

The ecological tradeoff is not a toy change of labels: placing all known-sex founders together insures against mate absence while dividing balanced sex pairs insures against site-specific catastrophe. This produces a complete global optimality classification, disqualifies every unequal partial division, supplies exact policies for odd budgets and an explicit one-crossing threshold for even budgets. The restriction to a one-step mate-availability proxy is scientifically material and clearly stated; future demographic work is needed before interpreting the theorem as reintroduction guidance.
