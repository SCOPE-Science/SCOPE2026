# An exact divisor parametrization of the semiprime layer of \(\varphi(n)\mid n^2-1\)
## Finding
Let \(\varphi\) be Euler's totient and let \(\Omega(n)\) count prime factors with multiplicity.

A composite integer \(n\) with
\[
\Omega(n)=2
\]
satisfies
\[
\varphi(n)\mid n^2-1
\]
if and only if \(n=pq\) for distinct odd primes \(p<q\) belonging to the following exact finite divisor parametrization.

Put
\[
u=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad v=\frac{q-1}{2},
\]
and write
\[
g=\gcd(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,
\]
where
\[
\gcd(a,b)=1,\qquad a<b.
\]
Then the divisibility holds exactly when
\[
g\mid a+b
\]
and
\[
h=\frac{g(a+b)+1}{ab}
\]
is a positive integer. Equivalently,
\[
(ha-g)(hb-g)=g^2+h.
\tag{1}
\]

Moreover,
\[
1\le h\le\left\lfloor\frac{3g+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus, for each fixed \(g\), all solutions arise from finitely many ordinary divisor pairs: choose an integer \(h\) in (2), factor
\[
g^2+h=rs,\qquad 0<r<s,
\]
require
\[
r\equiv s\equiv-g\pmod h,
\]
and put
\[
a=\frac{r+g}{h},\qquad b=\frac{s+g}{h}.
\]
Keeping exactly the cases with
\[
\gcd(a,b)=1,\qquad a<b,\qquad g\mid a+b,
\]
and with both
\[
p=2ga+1,\qquad q=2gb+1
\]
prime gives precisely the semiprime solutions.

Every such solution also satisfies
\[
q\le\frac{p^2+1}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

The first semiprime solutions are
\[
15,\ 35,\ 65,\ 91,\ 377,\ 559,\ 2059,\ 12851.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient set is the set of integers \(n\) for which
\[
\varphi(n)\mid n^2-1.
\]
The theorem classifies only the composite layer
\[
\Omega(n)=2.
\]
It does not classify integers with three or more prime factors counted with multiplicity, and it makes no claim about the infinitude of the prime pairs generated by (1).

The database sequence A097605 contains all integers satisfying the divisibility, while A098271 is its nonprime subsequence.

## Proof
First exclude the two factorization shapes that cannot occur.

If
\[
n=p^2
\]
for a prime \(p\), then
\[
\varphi(n)=p(p-1),
\]
whereas
\[
p\nmid p^4-1.
\]
Hence no prime square is a solution.

If
\[
n=2q
\]
with \(q\) odd prime, then
\[
\varphi(n)=q-1
\]
is even, while
\[
n^2-1
\]
is odd. Hence no even semiprime is a solution.

It remains to take
\[
n=pq
\]
with distinct odd primes
\[
p<q.
\]
Write
\[
u=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad v=\frac{q-1}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
\varphi(pq)=4uv.
\]
Put
\[
X=2uv+u+v.
\]
Since
\[
pq=(2u+1)(2v+1)=2X+1,
\]
one has
\[
(pq)^2-1=4X(X+1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\varphi(pq)\mid(pq)^2-1
\]
is equivalent to
\[
uv\mid X(X+1).
\]
Reducing \(X\) modulo \(uv\) gives
\[
X\equiv u+v\pmod{uv},
\]
so the condition is exactly
\[
uv\mid(u+v)(u+v+1).
\tag{4}
\]

Now write
\[
g=\gcd(u,v),\qquad u=ga,\qquad v=gb,
\]
with
\[
\gcd(a,b)=1,\qquad a<b.
\]
Condition (4) becomes
\[
g^2ab\mid g(a+b)\bigl(g(a+b)+1\bigr),
\]
or equivalently
\[
gab\mid(a+b)\bigl(g(a+b)+1\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
Because
\[
\gcd(ab,a+b)=1,
\]
condition (5) forces
\[
ab\mid g(a+b)+1.
\tag{6}
\]
Also
\[
\gcd\bigl(g,g(a+b)+1\bigr)=1,
\]
so (5) forces
\[
g\mid a+b.
\tag{7}
\]
Conversely, (6) and (7) imply (5). Thus (6)–(7) are exactly equivalent to the original divisibility.

Define
\[
h=\frac{g(a+b)+1}{ab}.
\]
Then \(h\) is a positive integer and
\[
hab=g(a+b)+1.
\tag{8}
\]
Expanding gives the exact factorization
\[
(ha-g)(hb-g)
=h^2ab-gh(a+b)+g^2
=g^2+h,
\]
which is (1). Both factors are positive because
\[
ha-g=\frac{ga+1}{b}>0,\qquad
hb-g=\frac{gb+1}{a}>0.
\]
Since \(a<b\), the first factor is strictly smaller.

Conversely, suppose \(g,h,r,s\) obey the divisor-pair construction in the finding, and set
\[
a=\frac{r+g}{h},\qquad b=\frac{s+g}{h}.
\]
Then
\[
(ha-g)(hb-g)=g^2+h
\]
is equivalent, after expansion and division by \(h>0\), to
\[
hab=g(a+b)+1.
\]
Together with
\[
\gcd(a,b)=1,\qquad g\mid a+b,
\]
this recovers (6)–(7), hence (4), hence the original totient divisibility. Primality of
\[
2ga+1,\qquad2gb+1
\]
then produces exactly one squarefree semiprime solution.

For the bound on \(h\), use \(a\ge1\) and \(b\ge2\):
\[
h=\frac ga+\frac gb+\frac1{ab}
\le g+\frac g2+\frac12
=\frac{3g+1}{2}.
\]
This proves (2), and therefore finiteness of the divisor search for each fixed \(g\).

Finally, reducing (4) modulo \(v\) yields
\[
v\mid u(u+1).
\]
Since \(v>u\),
\[
v\le u(u+1).
\]
Thus
\[
q=2v+1
\le2u(u+1)+1
=\frac{p^2+1}{2},
\]
which proves (3).

## Verification
The attached `verify.py` independently checks every integer
\[
2\le n\le2{,}000{,}000
\]
with
\[
\Omega(n)=2.
\]
It compares the direct divisibility test against the normalized conditions
\[
g\mid a+b,\qquad ab\mid g(a+b)+1,
\]
checks the factorization identity (1), the bound (2), and the prime bound (3), and confirms that prime squares and even semiprimes are rejected.

The computation is corroborative only. The unbounded classification follows from the exact algebraic equivalences in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A097605 is the exact sequence of integers satisfying
\[
\varphi(n)\mid n^2-1.
\]
It records that every prime is a member, that \(2\) is the only even member, and gives computational code and examples.

OEIS A098271 is the exact nonprime subsequence. Its listed terms begin
\[
1,\ 15,\ 35,\ 65,\ 91,\ 255,\ 377,\ 559,\ldots
\]
and it gives computational programs and a counting comment. The inspected entry does not state an exact semiprime classification, the normalized equation (8), the factor-pair identity (1), or the bound (3).

Targeted searches using the exact totient divisibility, semiprime terminology, the two database identifiers, the factor-pair formulation, and the larger-prime bound did not locate a published theorem equivalent to or stronger than this semiprime parametrization. Nearby results on totient congruences, \(\varphi\)-practical numbers, Knödel sets, and other two-prime divisibility equations concern different predicates and do not imply this statement.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify the \(\Omega(n)\ge3\) portion of A098271 and does not prove that infinitely many semiprime solutions exist.

Because the crucial reductions are elementary and the database sequences date from 2004, an equivalent observation could occur in an unindexed note, problem discussion, or private computation not surfaced by the searches. Failed searches are not treated as proof of novelty; the originality assessment rests on the exact database inspection, statement-level comparisons, and the absence of an implication from the closest located results.
