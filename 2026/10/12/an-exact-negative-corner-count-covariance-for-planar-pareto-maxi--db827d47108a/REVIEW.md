# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Sorting observations by one coordinate turns the maximal-point count into a sum of iid-sequence upper-record indicators. Conditional on the number of first-coordinate threshold exceedances, the northeast count involves only an initial segment of that sequence. The rank of a maximum is independent of the unordered observations; this yields the exact mixed term (5), the conditional covariance (1), and the binomial mixture (2). The telescoping sum gives the explicit positive error and exponential upper bound (4). Cases with zero or all first-coordinate exceedances, and strict interior sign, are included. Independent exhaustive rank-permutation enumeration checks 199 exact rational cells for sizes two through seven, with further rational parameter tests. Finite checking supplements rather than replaces the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical uncertainty

The inspected Bentley et al. (1978) full text treats expected maximal-point counts and a recurrence for their mean, not joint covariance with upper-right threshold occupancy. The inspected Bai et al. (2001) full text treats marginal and limiting laws of the maximal count across planar regions; its conditional spatial decompositions concern regional maxima, not the same fixed northeast occupancy statistic. Barbour–Xia (2001) was checked at abstract and bibliographic level only; no full-text noncoverage claim is made. The final result is narrower than a general record-process theorem but distinct from these marginal statements. A general historical multivariate record or empirical-process covariance identity might imply it and remains an explicit priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The exact strict sign combines two natural sample descriptors—Pareto-frontier size and upper-right threshold occupancy—whose separate coordinate projections are independent of frontier size. The negative covariance is a genuine interaction in the matched joint ordering, rather than a one-coordinate artifact. The universal nonzero limit and explicit exponential error quantify a finite-size spatial dependence missed by the separate marginal laws. These facts are not presented as a computational novelty certificate or as a causal effect.

## Limits

Independent continuous coordinates and fixed interior exceedance probabilities are necessary for the stated scope. No result is claimed for other dependence structures, tied coordinates, moving thresholds, or higher dimensions. The originality assessment relies on inspected available primary texts and searches, not proof of historical uniqueness. Independent audit remains outstanding.
