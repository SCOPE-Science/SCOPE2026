# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim covers exactly \(0<a,b<1\) and all \(U\in U(2)\), including the zero-determinant boundary. The explicit four-Kraus calculation gives the factorized normalized Choi-partial-transpose determinant; two-qubit positivity and the product-vector argument allow that determinant to detect the unique possible negative eigenvalue. Continuity handles equality. The packaged symbolic and direct numerical checks pass; the proof itself supplies the all-parameter quantifiers. The endpoints outside the claimed open parameter region are separately identified.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** Lami–Giovannetti (arXiv:1411.2517, Example 1) give the generalized-amplitude-damping entanglement-breaking condition and an unfiltered index, which cover an endpoint after a basis flip but not the all-unitary, unequal-stage parameter test. Cuevas et al. (arXiv:1707.00161, Secs. II–IV) experimentally and numerically explore rotated amplitude damping in a special equal-strength setting; the inspected complete five-page text does not state the general ordered-cascade threshold or prove its order reversal. Targeted searches did not identify the general claim. Equivalent characterization in the broader qubit-channel literature remains possible; search absence is not priority certification.

**Value — PASS.** The entire natural four-parameter problem (two damping strengths plus an arbitrary two-level intermediate unitary, reduced to one transition probability by covariance) receives an exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion, exposing a strict noncommutative order effect and a sharp equal-strength cutoff. These answer an operational question motivated by experiments on amendable channels, not an arbitrary finite slice or a recomputed numerical table.

**Limitations.** Only zero-temperature qubit amplitude damping with one intervening unitary is covered. Other noise classes and longer/noisy controls are not. The original generalized-damping criterion is credited, and independent assessment of priority and correctness remains outstanding.
