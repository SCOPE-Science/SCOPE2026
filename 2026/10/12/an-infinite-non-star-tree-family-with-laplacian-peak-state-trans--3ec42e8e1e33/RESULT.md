# An infinite non-star tree family with Laplacian peak state transfer
## Finding
For every integer \(b\ge2\), let \(T_b\) be the depth-two tree with a root \(r\), with \(2b-1\) children \(c_1,\ldots,c_{2b-1}\), and with exactly \(b\) pendant leaves adjacent to each \(c_i\). For the continuous-time quantum walk \(U(t)=e^{itL}\) generated by the combinatorial Laplacian \(L\), peak state transfer between \(r\) and any fixed child \(c_i\) occurs if and only if \(b\) is even. For even \(b\), the peak times are precisely the odd multiples of \(\pi\), the peak amplitude is
\[
B(L)_{r,c_i}=\frac{4b}{(b+1)(2b+1)},
\]
and the corresponding single-excitation transfer probability is
\[
\left(\frac{4b}{(b+1)(2b+1)}\right)^2.
\]
The trees \(T_b\) with even \(b\) are pairwise non-isomorphic and are not stars. They therefore give an infinite family of non-star trees with Laplacian peak state transfer, answering Open Problem 7.1 of Coutinho--Guo--Schmeits affirmatively.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple, finite, unweighted, and connected. The Laplacian is the combinatorial Laplacian, and peak state transfer has the meaning of Coutinho--Guo--Schmeits: the transition amplitude between two vertices attains the spectral triangle-inequality bound. The theorem classifies only root-to-child transfer in this family. It does not classify transfer between other vertex pairs, adjacency-matrix dynamics, or peak transfer in arbitrary generalized Bethe trees.

## Proof
Put \(a=2b-1\). Decompose the Laplacian representation according to the permutation symmetries of the \(a\) branches. There are three types of invariant subspaces.

First, leaf-difference vectors inside one branch have Laplacian eigenvalue \(1\). Every such vector vanishes at the root, so the corresponding spectral projectors have zero \((r,c_i)\)-entry.

Second, branch-standard vectors have root coordinate zero and satisfy the two-level eigenvalue equation
\[
\begin{pmatrix}b+1&-b\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their eigenvalues are the roots of
\[
\lambda^2-(b+2)\lambda+1=0.
\]
Again every vector in these eigenspaces has root coordinate zero, so none of these eigenvalues belongs to the mutual eigenvalue support of \(r\) and \(c_i\).

It remains to consider the fully symmetric subspace. In the orthonormal basis consisting of the root vector, the normalized sum of all child vectors, and the normalized sum of all leaf vectors, the Laplacian is
\[
Q_b=
\begin{pmatrix}
2b-1&-\sqrt{2b-1}&0\\
-\sqrt{2b-1}&b+1&-\sqrt b\\
0&-\sqrt b&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its characteristic polynomial factors exactly as
\[
\det(\lambda I-Q_b)=\lambda(\lambda-b)(\lambda-(2b+1)).
\]
The three spectral idempotents have root-to-one-child entries
\[
(E_0)_{r,c_i}=\frac{1}{b(2b+1)},\qquad
(E_b)_{r,c_i}=\frac{b-1}{b(b+1)},\qquad
(E_{2b+1})_{r,c_i}=-\frac{2b}{(b+1)(2b+1)}.
\]
All three are nonzero for \(b\ge2\). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{ev}^{+}_{L}(r,c_i)=\{0,b\},\qquad
\operatorname{ev}^{-}_{L}(r,c_i)=\{2b+1\}.
\]
Since
\[
\gcd(0,b,2b+1)=1,
\]
Corollary 5.5 of Coutinho--Guo--Schmeits applies directly. Its parity criterion requires the positive-support eigenvalues to be even and the negative-support eigenvalues to be odd after division by the gcd. The eigenvalue \(2b+1\) is always odd, so the criterion holds exactly when \(b\) is even. In that case the peak times are precisely the odd multiples of \(\pi\), with phase \(1\). If \(b\) is odd, the positive-support eigenvalue \(b\) has the wrong parity, so root-to-child peak state transfer does not occur.

Finally, summing the absolute values of the three nonzero projector entries gives
\[
B(L)_{r,c_i}
=\frac{1}{b(2b+1)}+\frac{b-1}{b(b+1)}+\frac{2b}{(b+1)(2b+1)}
=\frac{4b}{(b+1)(2b+1)}.
\]
For \(b=2\), the tree has ten vertices: a root with three children, each child having two leaves. This is exactly the shape of the nearly binary ten-vertex tree displayed in Figure 10 of the source paper. Thus the isolated computational example is the first member of the even family.

## Verification
The proof is analytic and applies to every integer \(b\ge2\). The accompanying exact-arithmetic script checks the quotient projectors, their signs, their sum, the eigenprojector identities, the parity criterion, and the peak-amplitude formula for \(2\le b\le24\). Those finite checks are corroborative only; the infinite statement follows from the displayed factorizations and closed formulas.

At \(b=2\), the theorem gives peak amplitude \(8/15\) and peak probability \(64/225\) at every odd multiple of \(\pi\), matching the source paper's unique non-star Laplacian-peak tree at order ten by graph shape.

## Relationship to prior work
Coutinho--Guo--Schmeits introduced continuous-time peak state transfer, proved the integer/parity characterization for Laplacian dynamics, computationally found that stars are the only Laplacian-peak trees through order eighteen except for one nearly binary tree on ten vertices, and explicitly asked whether infinitely many non-star trees admit Laplacian peak state transfer. Their current version does not give such a family.

Rojo--Medina give general Laplacian spectral machinery for generalized Bethe trees and related compound graphs. That work predates peak state transfer and does not state a state-transfer result. The present theorem uses the special parameter relation \(a=2b-1\) to force the three symmetric eigenvalues to be the integers \(0,b,2b+1\), and then couples their exact projector signs to the later peak-transfer parity criterion.

## Limitations
The transfer distance here is one edge, and the peak probability decays on the order of \(b^{-2}\). Thus this family resolves the stated existence question but does not supply a long-distance high-fidelity quantum wire. The theorem does not classify all peak-transfer pairs in \(T_b\), does not rule out peak transfer elsewhere when \(b\) is odd, and does not classify all depth-two generalized Bethe trees.
