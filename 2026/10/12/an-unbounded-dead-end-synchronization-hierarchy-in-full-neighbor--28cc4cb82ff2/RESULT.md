# An unbounded dead-end synchronization hierarchy in full neighborhood products

## Finding

For each integer \(n\geq1\), define the closed tri-modal formula
\[
\gamma_n:=\Box^n\bot\leftrightarrow
\bigl(\Box_1^n\bot\lor\Box_2^n\bot\bigr).
\]
Every full neighborhood product of two normal (possibly nonserial) neighborhood frames validates \(\gamma_n\), for every \(n\), without a finiteness or principal-filter assumption. Nevertheless, if \(L\) denotes the normal tri-modal logic obtained by combining three copies of \(\mathsf{K4}\) and the interaction schema
\[
\mathsf{mix}:\quad
\Box p\longrightarrow
(\Box_1\Box_2p\land\Box_2\Box_1p),
\]
then, for every \(n\geq1\),
\[
L+\{\gamma_k:1\leq k<n\}\not\vdash\gamma_n.
\]
The witnesses can be chosen as finite transitive Kripke frames on \(n+1\) worlds. Thus no finite subset of the displayed closed-formula family generates all of it over \(L\). This is not a claim that the full-product logic itself lacks every possible finite axiomatization.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(X,Y\) be nonempty sets. At each \(x\in X\) and \(y\in Y\), take nonempty filters \(\tau_1(x)\subseteq\mathcal P(X)\) and \(\tau_2(y)\subseteq\mathcal P(Y)\), upward closed and closed under finite intersection. Improper filters, containing \(\varnothing\), are allowed. The full product has horizontal and vertical neighborhood filters generated by \(U\times\{y\}\) and \(\{x\}\times V\), respectively, and the third, synchronous filter at \((x,y)\) consists of all sets containing some rectangle \(U\times V\), for \(U\in\tau_1(x)\) and \(V\in\tau_2(y)\). Denote these three boxes by \(\Box_1,\Box_2,\Box\). The formula \(\bot\) is false everywhere and powers of a box denote iteration.

For a subset \(A\subseteq X\), write \(B_1(A)=\{x:A\in\tau_1(x)\}\), and similarly \(B_2(B)\) for \(B\subseteq Y\). Write \(P(T)\) for the truth-set operation of the synchronous box on \(T\subseteq X\times Y\). All claims about the product apply to arbitrary valuations: the formulas themselves are variable-free.

For the separation, Kripke relations are treated as normal neighborhood filters of all supersets of their successor sets; the transitivity of each accessibility relation validates the corresponding \(\mathsf{K4}\) axiom.

## Proof

**Rectangular union identity.** For all \(A\subseteq X\) and \(B\subseteq Y\) define
\[
E(A,B)=(A\times Y)\cup(X\times B).
\]
We claim the exact identity
\[
P(E(A,B))=E(B_1(A),B_2(B)). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, for a fixed \((x,y)\), membership of \(E(A,B)\) in the product filter is witnessed by a rectangle \(U\times V\subseteq E(A,B)\) with \(U\in\tau_1(x)\) and \(V\in\tau_2(y)\). Such an inclusion holds precisely when \(U\subseteq A\) **or** \(V\subseteq B\): if both contain an element outside their indicated set, their Cartesian product contains a point outside \(E(A,B)\). Upward closure then gives \(A\in\tau_1(x)\) or \(B\in\tau_2(y)\). Conversely, either membership supplies a witnessing rectangle by pairing \(A\) or \(B\) with the whole opposite space; those whole spaces belong to their respective nonempty filters. This proves (1), including improper and nonprincipal cases.

Let \(A_0=B_0=\varnothing\) and \(A_{j+1}=B_1(A_j)\), \(B_{j+1}=B_2(B_j)\) for \(j\geq0\). Since \(E(\varnothing,\varnothing)=\varnothing\), induction using (1) yields
\[
P^n(\varnothing)=E(A_n,B_n)\qquad(n\geq0). \tag{2}
\]
The horizontal modality maps each cylinder \(A\times Y\) to \(B_1(A)\times Y\), and the vertical modality similarly maps \(X\times B\) to \(X\times B_2(B)\). Therefore \(A_n\times Y\) and \(X\times B_n\) are the truth sets of \(\Box_1^n\bot\) and \(\Box_2^n\bot\), respectively. Equation (2) proves \(\gamma_n\) on every full product.

**Strict separation at each depth.** Fix \(n\geq1\). Let
\[
W=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1,q\},\quad q=n,
\qquad
S=\{(i,j):0\leq i<j<n\}\cup\{(q,q)\}.
\]
Define \(R_1=R_2=S\) and \(R=S\cup\{(0,q)\}\). The relation \(S\) is transitive (a strict finite total order plus an isolated loop). The added arrow from \(0\) to the loop point \(q\) also leaves \(R\) transitive. Since \(S\circ S\subseteq S\subseteq R\), both \(R_1\circ R_2\subseteq R\) and \(R_2\circ R_1\subseteq R\). Hence the frame validates all instances of \(\mathsf{mix}\), as well as the three copies of \(\mathsf{K4}\).

At every world other than \(0\), the successor sets of all three relations coincide, even after any prescribed number of steps. Thus every \(\gamma_k\) is true there. At \(0\), for each \(1\leq k<n\), the strict chain admits a length-\(k\) path, so none of \(\Box^k\bot\), \(\Box_1^k\bot\), \(\Box_2^k\bot\) holds. At depth \(n\), the chain has no path of length \(n\), so \(\Box_1^n\bot\) and \(\Box_2^n\bot\) hold at \(0\). Yet \(0RqRq\cdots Rq\) supplies paths of every positive length under \(R\), so \(\Box^n\bot\) is false there. Consequently the frame validates \(\gamma_k\) for every \(k<n\) but refutes \(\gamma_n\). Soundness of normal modal derivations supplies the stated nonderivability. For any finite subset of the hierarchy, choose \(n\) larger than its largest index: the corresponding frame validates that subset but refutes \(\gamma_n\).

## Verification

The self-contained Python 3 script `verify.py` uses exact bit-set semantics to check (1) and (2) for all principal finite filters in sizes \((1,1)\), \((1,2)\), \((2,2)\), and \((3,2)\), together with \(300\) seeded cases in size \((3,3)\). Every finite filter is principal, so these tests include improper neighborhood filters. The script checked \(8{,}784\) pairs through depth \(7\). It also constructed the explicit transitive Kripke frames for \(1\leq n\leq16\), checked both composition inclusions and the modal values at every world, and reported `VERIFY_OK`.

Reproduce in a directory containing `verify.py` with `python3 verify.py`. These finite checks support, but do not replace, the symbolic arguments for arbitrary sets and every positive integer \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work

Aghamov, Kudinov, Nguyen and Piribauer (2026), *On Modal Logics of Full Products of Neighborhood Frames*, formalize the same triple of modal operators, prove that \(\mathsf{mix}\) suffices to axiomatize the serial full products for \(\mathsf D\) and \(\mathsf T\), and explicitly leave the \(\mathsf K\) and \(\mathsf{K4}\) full-product cases as future work. The present formulas are trivial in the serial cases, where all iterated boxes of falsity are false; their depth-by-depth separation is specific to allowing dead ends.

Kudinov (2014), *Neighbourhood Frame Product KxK*, and Kudinov (2018), *On neighbourhood product of some Horn axiomatizable logics*, give nonserial neighborhood-product principles involving **two** modal operators and variable-free formulas. The 2014 paper separately points to adding a third, synchronous modality as a further question. Neither checked source supplies the rectangular-union identity for that third modality or the transitive, depth-indexed separation of its nullary powers. The new statement is therefore not an immediate instance of the cited bimodal completeness formulas.

## Limitations

No axiomatization, completeness, finite-model property, decidability result, or non-finite-axiomatizability theorem for the **entire** full \(\mathsf K\) or \(\mathsf{K4}\) product logic is claimed. Only independence of the displayed family over the specific fusion-plus-mix base is proved. Literature searching cannot exclude unpublished or differently indexed equivalent statements. The infinite validity and independence rely on the proofs, not extrapolation from finite tests.
