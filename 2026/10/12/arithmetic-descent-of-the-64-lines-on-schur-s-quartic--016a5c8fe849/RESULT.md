# Arithmetic descent of the 64 lines on Schur's quartic

## Finding
Let \(S\) be the smooth quartic surface over \(\mathbb Q\)
\[
S:\qquad x_0^4-x_0x_1^3-x_2^4+x_2x_3^3=0\subset\mathbb P^3.
\]
For a geometric line \(\ell\subset S_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}\), its *minimal field of definition* means the subfield of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) generated by ratios of its Plücker coordinates. It is independent of the chosen Plücker representative. Among the complete set of 64 geometric lines, the distribution by exact minimal field is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
\text{Minimal field}&\mathbb Q&\mathbb Q(i)&\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)&
\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})&\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt3)\\\hline
\text{Number of lines}&6&2&2&34&20.
\end{array}
\]
Consequently their smallest common field of definition is \(K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt3)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\), with Galois group \(C_2\times C_2\). The action on all lines has six fixed points, 19 two-element orbits and five four-element orbits. The three nonidentity involutions of \(K/\mathbb Q\), acting on \((i,\sqrt3)\) by signs \((-1,+1)\), \((+1,-1)\), and \((-1,-1)\), fix respectively 8, 8 and 40 lines. In particular the displayed real quartic has exactly eight real lines.

The six \(\mathbb Q\)-lines consist of the four lines
\[
\{x_0=q x_1,\;x_2=p x_3\},\qquad p,q\in\{0,1\},
\]
and the two lines
\[
\{x_2=\epsilon x_0,\;x_1=\epsilon x_3\},\qquad\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]
The maximum number of pairwise skew \(\mathbb Q\)-lines on this fixed rational quartic is **four**, whereas the geometric maximum of 16 is a previously established result of Rojas and Lira.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb Q\), in characteristic zero, and the specific displayed \(\mathbb Q\)-equation (not an arbitrary twist or projectively equivalent model over \(\mathbb C\)) is fixed. A line is individually defined over a field when its point in the Grassmannian \(\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)\) is rational over that field. The result uses the already proved exhaustive list of 64 lines on Schur's quartic; its newly calculated content is their exact descent and the resulting rational skew-line maximum.

## Proof
Write \(r=\sqrt3\), \(\zeta=(-1+ir)/2\), \(U=\{1,-1,i,-i\}\), \(V=\{1,\zeta,\zeta^2\}\), and \(W=\{0\}\cup V\). Denote by \([v,w]\) the projective line spanned by two nonzero independent vectors. Rojas and Lira, Lemma 3.1, Corollary 3.5 and Lemma 3.6, give the following exhaustive 64 lines (the four labels denote disjoint parts of this set):
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(p,q)&=[(0,0,p,1),(q,1,0,0)],&&p,q\in W,\\
M_1(a,b)&=[(1,0,a,0),(0,a^3b,0,1)],&&a\in U,\ b\in V,\\
M_k(a,b)&=[(ra^3b,0,b,-2\zeta^{k-2}),
 (0,ra^3b,-b\zeta^{2-k},-1)],&&k=2,3,4,\ a\in U,\ b\in V.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are 16 plus 12 plus 36 distinct lines. Direct substitution of each parametrized line into the defining quartic gives zero; the cited exhaustive classification ensures there are no missing geometric lines.

Use ordered Plücker coordinates \((p_{01},p_{02},p_{03},p_{12},p_{13},p_{23})\). After scaling the tuples so that the indicated pivot coordinate equals one, they are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
L(p,q): &(0,pq,q,p,1,0),\\
M_1(a,b): &(a^3b,0,1,-b,0,a),\\
M_k(a,b): &(-ra^3b,b\zeta^{2-k},1,b,-2\zeta^{k-2},ra),\quad k=2,3,4.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the exact minimal fields are, respectively,
\[
\mathbb Q(p,q),\qquad \mathbb Q(a,b),\qquad
\mathbb Q(ra,b,\zeta^{k-2}).
\]
The last equality follows since \(a^4=1\), \(r/a^3=ra\), and \(ra\) and \(b\) occur directly among Plücker coordinates; \(\zeta^{k-2}\) also occurs directly. For \(k=2\) it is one, while for \(k=3,4\) it generates \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\). This coefficient recovery proves *minimality*, rather than merely giving fields containing some line equations.

Enumerating the four by four, four by three, and three further four by three parameter sets gives the field counts
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
&\mathbb Q&\mathbb Q(i)&\mathbb Q(r)&\mathbb Q(ir)&K\\\hline
L&4&0&0&12&0\\
M_1&2&2&0&4&4\\
M_2&0&0&2&6&4\\
M_3&0&0&0&6&6\\
M_4&0&0&0&6&6\\\hline
\text{Total}&6&2&2&34&20.
\end{array}
\]
For example, when \(k=2\), the value \(ra\) generates \(\mathbb Q(r)\) if \(a=\pm1\), and \(\mathbb Q(ir)\) if \(a=\pm i\); adding \(b\in\{\zeta,\zeta^2\}\) produces the stated composita. The existence of lines of field exactly \(K\) proves no proper subfield defines all 64 lines.

Since \(K/\mathbb Q\) is biquadratic, a line with minimal field of degree one, two or four has Galois orbit of that size. The totals give orbit profile \(1^6\,2^{19}\,4^5\). The involution fixing \(\mathbb Q(r)\) fixes the 6 rational and 2 \(\mathbb Q(r)\)-lines, hence 8 lines; the involution fixing \(\mathbb Q(i)\) likewise fixes 8; the involution fixing \(\mathbb Q(ir)\) fixes 6+34=40. Complex conjugation sends \(i\) to \(-i\) and fixes \(r\), so exactly eight lines are real.

For the rational skew maximum, the four \(L(p,q)\) with \(p,q\in\{0,1\}\) form a four-cycle of intersection edges. Both \(M_1(1,1)\) and \(M_1(-1,1)\) meet exactly the two vertices \(L(0,0)\), \(L(1,1)\) of that cycle, and are skew to one another. Hence no pairwise-skew rational subset can have more than two \(L\)-lines and two \(M_1\)-lines. Equality is achieved by \(L(0,1)\), \(L(1,0)\), \(M_1(1,1)\), \(M_1(-1,1)\). These incidences follow exactly from the vanishing of the Plücker polar form
\[
p_{01}q_{23}-p_{02}q_{13}+p_{03}q_{12}
+p_{12}q_{03}-p_{13}q_{02}+p_{23}q_{01}.
\]

## Verification
Run `python verify_schur_arithmetic.py` in this directory with Python 3.10 or later and SymPy 1.12 or later (`pip install -r requirements.txt`). The standalone verifier reconstructs 64 parametrized lines, checks their pairwise distinct Plücker points, verifies that each lies on the quartic by exact substitution, calculates the three Galois involution permutations and fixed-line counts, reconstructs all five exact minimal-field strata and the \(1^6,2^{19},4^5\) orbit distribution, and exhaustively checks all subsets of the six rational lines for the maximal skew size. These are exact symbolic checks, not numerical experiments. The exhaustiveness of the 64-line list is the cited primary theorem, not inferred from the 64 successful substitutions alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Schur's original 1882 construction and Bauer–Schmitz (2012) establish the rich 64-line geometry. Rojas–Lira (2019) give the full explicit line parametrization and prove the geometric maximum of 16 pairwise skew lines; those results are premises here, not claimed as new. The published line-intersection spectrum and common-transversal census concerns complex intersection combinatorics rather than individual arithmetic descent. Naskręcki–Pokora (2026) study a particular 24-line configuration defined over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\); this does not classify minimal fields of *all* 64 lines. The result here computes that complete arithmetic classification, the induced Galois representation, and the much smaller rational skew maximum for the fixed \(\mathbb Q\)-model.

## Limitations
This is arithmetic for one explicitly specified \(\mathbb Q\)-model of Schur's quartic, not for every rational form or twist. The field strata and rational skew maximum are not asserted to be intrinsic to its complex projective isomorphism class. The determination uses published line equations, so the arithmetic computation is finite and accessible from prior geometric data; no new enumeration of all possible quartic lines is claimed. Historical studies such as Segre's *On Arithmetical Properties of Quartic Surfaces* (1946) could contain overlapping implicit arithmetic information; its full text was not accessible for comparison. The 2019 construction has no independent validation beyond the cited mathematical literature, and no independent audit of the present result has been performed.
