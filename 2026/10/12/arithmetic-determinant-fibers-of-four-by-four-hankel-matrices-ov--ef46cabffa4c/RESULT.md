# Arithmetic determinant fibers of four-by-four Hankel matrices over finite fields

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be an arbitrary finite field, where \(q\) is any prime power. For \(a=(a_0,\ldots,a_6)\in k^7\), put \(H(a)=(a_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le3}\). Define the prescribed-determinant fiber \(N_q(t)=\#\{a\in k^7:\det H(a)=t\}\), and define \(\rho_m(t)=\#\{x\in k:x^m=t\}\) for \(m=2,4\), including \(t=0\). Then for **every** \(t\in k\),
\[
N_q(t)=q^6+q^3\bigl(\rho_4(t)-1+(q-1)(\rho_2(t)-1)\bigr).
\]
In particular, \(N_q(0)=q^6\). For even \(q\), every determinant value occurs exactly \(q^6\) times. For odd \(q\equiv3\pmod4\), the counts for nonzero squares and nonsquares are, respectively, \(q^6+q^4\) and \(q^6-q^4\). For odd \(q\equiv1\pmod4\), the counts for nonzero fourth powers, non-fourth-power squares, and nonsquares are, respectively,
\[
q^6+(q+2)q^3,\qquad q^6+(q-2)q^3,\qquad q^6-q^4.
\]
The refined arithmetic levels cannot be recovered from the usual rank distribution alone.

## Assumptions and scope
There are seven **independently and uniformly variable** coefficients \(a_0,\ldots,a_6\); no distinctness, nonzero coefficient, or characteristic restriction is imposed. Matrices are exactly the symmetric Hankel matrices \(H(a)\), not all symmetric matrices. The count is of ordered coefficient tuples, not equivalence classes. Equality and exponentiation refer to the given field, so the formula includes finite extension fields. Reversing the rows transforms the family into a Toeplitz family; at size four the reversing permutation has positive sign, so the determinant fibers agree. The symmetric Hankel matrix is also called the middle catalecticant of the corresponding binary sextic coefficient tuple (with no binomial coefficient reweighting).

## Proof
Write \(B=(a_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le2}\), \(v=(a_3,a_4,a_5)^T\), and \(\delta=a_0a_2-a_1^2\). The bordered determinant identity, valid without nonsingularity assumptions, is
\[
\det H=(\det B)a_6-v^T\operatorname{adj}(B)v.
\]
We partition the five-coefficient matrices \(B\) by their determinant, \(\delta\), and rank.

**Invertible \(B\).** Exactly \(q^5-q^4\) choices of \(B\) are invertible. For completeness, count singular \(B\): when \(\delta\ne0\) there are \(q^3-q^2\) triples \((a_0,a_1,a_2)\), and for each such triple \(a_3\) is arbitrary and \(a_4\) is uniquely determined by \(\det B=0\), giving \(q^4-q^3\) possibilities. When \(\delta=0\) and \(a_0\ne0\), write \(a_1=a_0\lambda\), \(a_2=a_0\lambda^2\); then \(\det B=-a_0(a_3-a_0\lambda^3)^2\), giving \(q^2(q-1)\) singular choices. When \(a_0=0\), \(\delta=0\) forces \(a_1=0\); the determinant is \(-a_2^3\), giving \(q^2\) singular choices. Their sum is \(q^4\). For each invertible \(B\) and each \(a_5\), exactly one \(a_6\) gives the prescribed \(t\). Thus this stratum contributes \(q^6-q^5\) to every fiber.

**Singular \(B\) with \(\delta\ne0\).** Such a \(B\) has rank two. Since its upper-left two-by-two minor is \(\delta\), its adjugate has the form \(\delta nn^T\), where \(n\) is its null vector normalized so that its last coordinate is one. Consequently
\[
\det H=-\delta(a_5-\beta)^2
\]
for some \(\beta\in k\) determined by \(B\). For each \(\delta\ne0\), the number of triples \((a_0,a_1,a_2)\) realizing it is
\[
m_q(\delta)=q(q-1)+q\rho_2(-\delta)
=q^2+q(\rho_2(-\delta)-1).
\]
Indeed \(a_0\ne0\) gives \(q(q-1)\) triples, while \(a_0=0\) requires \(-a_1^2=\delta\) and leaves \(a_2\) arbitrary. Each triple allows \(q\) values of \(a_3\), determines \(a_4\) from \(\det B=0\), and leaves \(a_6\) arbitrary. Hence this stratum contributes
\[
q^2\sum_{\delta\in k^\times}m_q(\delta)\rho_2(-t/\delta)
=q^4(q-1)+q^3(q-1)(\rho_2(t)-1).
\]
To evaluate the sum in odd characteristic, use \(\rho_2(s)=1+\chi(s)\), with the quadratic character extended by \(\chi(0)=0\), and \(\sum_{\delta\ne0}\chi(\delta)=0\). In characteristic two the squaring map is a bijection and both sides reduce to \(q^4(q-1)\). This derivation also works at \(t=0\).

**Singular \(B\), \(\delta=0\), rank two.** First suppose \(a_0=A\ne0\). Then \(a_1=A\lambda\), \(a_2=A\lambda^2\), and singularity forces \(a_3=A\lambda^3\). Let \(h=a_4-A\lambda^4\). Rank two means \(h\ne0\). The kernel is generated by \((-\lambda,1,0)^T\), the adjugate equals \(Ah\,nn^T\), and \(n^Tv=h\). Hence \(\det H=-Ah^3\). There are no such contributions for \(t=0\); for each \(t\ne0\), \(h\in k^\times\) and \(\lambda\in k\) determine the unique \(A=-t/h^3\), while \(a_5,a_6\) are free. This gives \(q^3(q-1)\) tuples for each nonzero target. Next suppose \(a_0=0\). We must have \(a_1=a_2=0\), and the rank-two condition is \(d=a_3\ne0\). In that case \(\operatorname{adj}(B)_{11}=-d^2\), and \(\det H=d^4\), independently of \(a_4,a_5,a_6\). The contribution is \(q^3\rho_4(t)\) for nonzero \(t\), and zero for \(t=0\) since \(d\ne0\).

**Rank at most one.** Every adjugate entry of \(B\) vanishes, so \(\det H=0\). There are exactly \(q^2\) rank-at-most-one matrices \(B\): the geometric progressions \(a_i=A\lambda^i\) with \(A\ne0\), contributing \(q(q-1)\), together with \((a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(0,0,0,0)\) and arbitrary \(a_4\), contributing \(q\). This stratum therefore contributes \(q^4\) at \(t=0\) and nothing elsewhere.

For \(t=0\), the first, second, and fourth strata sum to \(q^6-q^5+q^4(q-1)+q^4=q^6\). For \(t\ne0\), adding the first three strata and simplifying yields
\[
(q^6-q^5)+q^4(q-1)+q^3(q-1)(\rho_2(t)-1)+q^3(q-1)+q^3\rho_4(t)
=q^6+q^3(\rho_4(t)-1+(q-1)(\rho_2(t)-1)).
\]
Because \(\rho_2(0)=\rho_4(0)=1\), the same expression also holds at zero. The stated characteristic- and residue-class specializations follow from the cyclicity of \(k^\times\) and the bijectivity of finite-field Frobenius in characteristic two. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
The standard-library script `verify.py` enumerates **all** seven-tuples directly for field orders \(2,3,4,5,7\), computing determinants by independent field Gaussian elimination. Order four uses the explicit irreducible polynomial \(X^2+X+1\); all other checked orders are prime fields. The verified fibers for \(q=2,3,4,5,7\) are respectively `64,64`; `729,810,648`; `4096,4096,4096,4096`; `15625,16500,15000,15000,16000`; and `117649,120050,120050,115248,120050,115248,115248`. Each matches the formula, and each distribution totals \(q^7\). These finite computations corroborate the proof; they do not prove the theorem for arbitrary \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
The classical finite-field Hankel matrix results of Daykin (1960) and García-Armas, Ghorpade, and Ram (arXiv:1011.1760v1, Theorem 5.1) determine **rank multiplicities**, including \(q^6\) singular four-by-four Hankel matrices, but do not state the above nonzero-target determinant fibers. Dwivedi and Grinberg (arXiv:2109.05415v1, Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.7) refine the rank or zero-determinant census by prescribing initial coefficients; that is logically different from prescribing a nonzero determinant value. Hankel and Toeplitz aliases, and the binary-sextic catalecticant name, refer to the same determinant polynomial after coefficient relabeling (with the noted sign convention); the claim includes these descriptions rather than treating them as separate discoveries. Ballico and Elia (2014) investigate specialized determinantal varieties from linear-form Hankel families, not the full seven-parameter fiber census.

## Limitations
Only four-by-four Hankel matrices with independently variable entries on each skew diagonal are treated; no higher-size formula, nonuniform coefficient distribution, simultaneous minor constraints, or classification over nonfields is claimed. The zero fiber was already known from rank counts. While the cited primary texts were compared against the nonzero-value formula, obscure equivalent formulas in classical bordered-persymmetric or binary-invariant literature remain a bibliographic risk; inaccessible historical full text was not represented as having been read. Independent audit is not claimed.
