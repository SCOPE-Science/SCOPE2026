# Scientific review: arithmetic determinant fibers of four-by-four Hankel matrices

## Correctness — PASS
The result is an exact prescribed-value statement over all finite fields, not merely a rank count. The proof partitions every leading \(3\times3\) Hankel block into exhaustive, disjoint algebraic strata. The bordered-determinant identity determines the fiber in each stratum; quadratic-character orthogonality evaluates the only multiplicative convolution for odd characteristic, and Frobenius bijectivity covers even characteristic. An independent field-arithmetic enumerator fully checks seven coefficients over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\). The finite checks are corroboration rather than proof of the all-fields theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with recorded bibliographic risk
The closest inspected primary sources are García-Armas–Ghorpade–Ram, arXiv:1011.1760v1, especially Theorem 5.1 on ranks, and Dwivedi–Grinberg, arXiv:2109.05415v1, especially Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.7 on fixed-prefix rank and zero determinant. Their combined exact rank counts imply \(N_q(0)=q^6\) and the sum of the nonzero fibers, not the newly derived quadratic/fourth-power splitting. Ballico–Elia's specialized Hankel families do not cover the seven-variable determinant fibers. Searches also used Toeplitz, persymmetric, and binary-sextic catalecticant aliases. Daykin's 1960 bordered-persymmetric paper is potentially relevant but its original full text was inaccessible; its known rank theorem is credited and equivalent earlier fixed-value work remains a risk. Database searches alone do not establish priority.

## Value — PASS
Prescribing the value, rather than vanishing, of the determinant is a natural quantitative refinement of a classical structured-matrix statistic. The theorem completely resolves these fibers over an infinite class of finite fields and exposes the sharp contrast between characteristic-two uniformity and arithmetic splitting in odd characteristic. The square/fourth-power boundary is a structural feature unavailable from known rank counts; the result needs no hypothetical downstream application to be a meaningful exact classification.

## Limits and review status
Only four-by-four Hankel matrices with seven free coefficients are covered. No higher-size classification is asserted. Originality may be revisited if the inaccessible historical source or other classical literature contains equivalent statements.
