# Review

## Correctness
The characteristic-zero argument is a two-sided rank comparison. Rational decompositions from Lovitz give the upper bounds over any extension field. Any hypothetical shorter decomposition over an arbitrary characteristic-zero field uses only finitely many coefficients, hence descends to a finitely generated field over \(\mathbb Q\), which embeds into \(\mathbb C\); this would contradict Li's exact complex lower bound. The argument applies identically to scalar-weighted symmetric decompositions.

For positive characteristic, rank at most a fixed integer is expressed by a finite polynomial system. Nonexistence of the forbidden decompositions over \(\mathbb C\) gives rational Nullstellensatz certificates after faithful scalar descent. Clearing denominators leaves only finitely many primes, and the rational upper decompositions reduce outside another finite set. This proves exact equality over every field in every remaining characteristic. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
Li's primary theorem states the direct-sum ranks only over \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb C\). Lovitz proves the single-copy example over \(\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C\) and provides the rational upper decompositions, but does not treat the double and triple direct sums arithmetically. Earlier arbitrary-field counterexamples cited by Lovitz are different large-dimensional constructions and do not imply the exact ranks of Li's \(54\)- and \(81\)-dimensional examples. published-finding corpus searches for characteristic-zero field transfer, positive-characteristic reduction, aliases through spreading out, and exact rank data found no covering result.

Residual risk: the cofinite-prime consequence uses standard algebraic-geometric spreading-out and may be implicit to specialists even though it was not stated in the inspected sources. No inspected source states or dominates the exact field-persistence theorem for these tensors.

## Value
Tensor rank can depend on the ground field, so robustness across fields is a genuine structural question. The result shows that the first explicit low-dimensional Comon gaps larger than one are not peculiar to real or complex analysis: they survive over every characteristic-zero field and, after reduction, in almost every characteristic. This substantially broadens the arithmetic range of the new gap-two and gap-three examples without redoing their delicate rank lower bounds field by field.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest sources are Jianze Li, arXiv:2610.01660v1, and Benjamin Lovitz, arXiv:2609.28292v1. The result does not identify the exceptional primes and does not settle direct sums with more than three copies.
