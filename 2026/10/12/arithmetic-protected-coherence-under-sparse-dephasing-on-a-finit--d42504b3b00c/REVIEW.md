# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is exactly about the specified finite-dimensional adjacency-path Lindbladian with nonempty monitoring set and all rates strictly positive. The sine eigenbasis and Bézout divisibility give the exact protected eigenspace. The Hilbert–Schmidt dissipation identity forces peripheral eigenoperators to commute with every monitored projector; simplicity of the path spectrum and the nilpotence argument eliminate all other imaginary-axis operators. Contractivity excludes peripheral Jordan blocks, and the adjoint invariants fix every coefficient of the asymptotic state. A finite trigonometric-character sum yields the normalized Cesàro kernel. Boundary cases \(N=2\), \(g=1\) and \(g=2\), nonconvergence for \(g\ge3\), and the absence of a rate-uniform gap are all explicitly covered. The independent numerical implementation checked 83 monitor sets and 461 initial sites; the proof, not the finite test, establishes the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

Lidar and Whaley (arXiv:quant-ph/0301032, Section 5) already explain the general simultaneous-noise-eigenvector criterion for decoherence-free subspaces; the conceptual DFS mechanism is not presented as new. Solenov and Fedichkin (arXiv:quant-ph/0506096, pp. 1–4) monitor all cycle sites, and Bressanini, Benedetti and Paris (arXiv:2204.01836, Sections 2 and 4) analyze full-site dephasing rather than selected sites. Rodríguez-Rosario and collaborators (arXiv:0905.2942) provide the general quantum-stochastic-walk framework. Hoyer's 2013 Chapter 2 treats a site-dependent-rate diffusion model on infinite chains. None of the inspected statements supplies the complete exact open-path gcd criterion together with the operator-level asymptotic decomposition and closed Cesàro kernel. The strongest residual risk is an unlocated specialized treatment or an immediate but unstated consequence of more general Lindbladian peripheral-algebra results; searches cannot certify absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

The exact placement-dependent transition between full equilibration and persistent quantum memory is a natural physical and mathematical question in local environmental monitoring. The theorem treats every path length, every nonempty selection of monitored vertices and every fixed positive rate, with all-state asymptotics and all starting/target-position averages, rather than an arbitrary finite computation or routine model substitution. It exhibits a large qualitative difference between one endpoint monitor and one central monitor, and supplies an explicit experimentally interpretable probability kernel. No bound on finite-time relaxation rates or broad graph-level generality is claimed.

## Scientific limits and comparison

The Hilbert-space DFS criterion predates this theorem; the contribution assessed here is the exact arithmetic *classification and probability law*. Full-site classicalization and the infinite-chain transport limit are not treated as incompatible prior claims because their hypotheses differ. The gap for other selective-noise placements is not assumed to be novel without qualification. Independent literature and proof audit remain to be carried out.
