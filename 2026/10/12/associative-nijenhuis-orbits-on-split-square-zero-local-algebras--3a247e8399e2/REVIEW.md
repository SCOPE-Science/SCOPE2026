# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Comparing scalar coefficients in the Nijenhuis identity forces the radical to be invariant; the identity at the unit forces the distinguished vector to be an eigenvector. Conversely the three basis-pair checks suffice. The centralizer's eigenvector strata are indexed exactly by distinct block lengths, giving all orbit sizes. The weighted partition series derives the cumulative similarity-class formula. Complete brute-force identity checks and complete orbit enumerations over finite fields of orders \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\) support, but cannot replace, the proof for arbitrary \(q\) and \(n\). The field-arithmetic checker tests all linear maps in its declared finite cases, not just sampled operators.

**Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Cariñena–Grabowski–Marmo's original 2000 paper (arXiv version posted 2006-10-06) introduces associative Nijenhuis tensors but no finite-field local-algebra orbit census. Uchino's full text develops and constructs associative Nijenhuis operators but does not classify the operators of split square-zero local algebras up to automorphism. The full text of Zou–Gao–Kang–Lü gives complete parametrizations on three-dimensional complex associative algebras; this covers some small-dimensional operator families, which are not separately claimed. That paper explicitly lists automorphism classification as future work. Nishiyama–Ohta treats a more general matrix-plus-vector group action, giving relevant classical orbit-theoretic context, but neither it nor the other inspected sources was found to establish the stated *Nijenhuis-specific* reduction and finite-field all-dimensional cumulative orbit formula. The rank-one and centralizer ingredients are classical and not claimed as new. Closest published research records address Rota–Baxter, averaging, evolution dimension or short-algebra monomiality, not the Nijenhuis identity here. General enhanced-cone methods could conceivably contain an equivalent orbit formula; the residual risk is genuine.

**Value — PASS.** The classification identifies the exact solution variety and its full moduli under all algebra automorphisms for a natural infinite family of local rings. It provides both a closed count of raw operators and individual orbit sizes in all finite characteristics. The appearance of cumulative similarity-class numbers encodes an operator problem in a classical matrix invariant, useful for comparison and replication in other algebra families. It is not a numerical increment or a one-off finite field census.

**Scope and boundaries.** The theorem concerns split unital radical-square-zero algebras over finite fields, not general Artinian rings. All cited adjacent classifications are credited for their applicable ingredients and smaller-dimensional cases. Independent scrutiny and exhaustive bibliographic certainty are not claimed.
