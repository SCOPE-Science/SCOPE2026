# Automorphism-conjugacy classes of derivations of the three-dimensional null-filiform Leibniz algebra

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be an arbitrary finite field. For the one-generated three-dimensional nilpotent **left** Leibniz algebra \(L\) with basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) and nonzero products
\[
[e_1,e_1]=e_2,\qquad [e_1,e_2]=e_3,
\]
we classify **all** derivations up to conjugation by \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(L)\). This is classification of infinitesimal symmetries under changes of algebra coordinates, not classification of the algebra structures themselves.

Write \(D(a,b,c)\) for the unique derivation with \(D(e_1)=ae_1+be_2+ce_3\). For odd \(q\), the conjugacy orbits are exactly: \(q-1\) orbits \(D(a,0,0)\) (\(a\ne0\)) of size \(q^2\); \(q\) orbits \(D(0,1,\delta)\) (\(\delta\in k\)) of size \(q-1\); two orbits \(D(0,0,c)\), with representatives from the two classes in \(k^\times/(k^\times)^2\), each of size \((q-1)/2\); and the zero singleton. Thus there are precisely \(2q+2\) orbits.

For even \(q\), each fixed \(a\ne0\) instead supports **two** distinct orbits: \(D(a,0,0)\), size \(q\), and \(D(a,0,1)\), size \(q(q-1)\). They are separated by the vanishing or nonvanishing of \(ac+b^2\), respectively. In addition there are \(q\) orbits \(D(0,1,\delta)\), each size \(q-1\), one orbit \(D(0,0,1)\), size \(q-1\), and the zero singleton. Thus there are precisely \(3q\) orbits. Both descriptions cover all prime-power field sizes, including characteristics two and three.

## Assumptions and scope

The bracket is bilinear, and the stated basis products are the only nonzero ones. The left Leibniz convention is \([x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]]\). An ordinary derivation is a \(k\)-linear map \(D\) satisfying \(D[x,y]=[Dx,y]+[x,Dy]\). Conjugacy means \(D\mapsto gDg^{-1}\) for \(g\in\operatorname{Aut}_k(L)\). Matrices have columns equal to the images of the basis vectors. We do not classify other three-dimensional Leibniz isomorphism types or arbitrary higher-dimensional algebras.

## Proof

**Derivations.** Because \(L\) is generated by \(e_1\), any derivation is determined by \(D(e_1)=ae_1+be_2+ce_3\). Applying the derivation rule first to \([e_1,e_1]=e_2\) and then to \([e_1,e_2]=e_3\) gives
\[
D(e_2)=2ae_2+be_3,\quad D(e_3)=3ae_3,
\quad D(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\b&2a&0\\c&b&3a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The resulting map obeys the derivation equation on every pair of basis vectors, including all zero-product pairs. This argument holds without dividing by any integer, so it includes characteristics two and three.

**Automorphisms and the action.** The image of the generator must have a nonzero \(e_1\) component. Conversely, choosing \(g(e_1)=he_1+te_2+ue_3\), with \(h\ne0\), forces \(g(e_2)=h^2e_2+hte_3\) and \(g(e_3)=h^3e_3\), and defines an invertible algebra map. Therefore every automorphism is
\[
G(h,t,u)=\begin{pmatrix}h&0&0\\t&h^2&0\\u&ht&h^3\end{pmatrix},\quad h\in k^\times,\ t,u\in k.
\]
Direct multiplication gives the entire conjugation action:
\[
G(h,t,u)D(a,b,c)G(h,t,u)^{-1}
 =D\left(a,\ hb-\frac{at}{h},\ h^2c+a\left(\frac th\right)^2-\frac{2au}{h}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular \(a\) is invariant, and \(G\) has exactly \(q^2(q-1)\) elements.

**When \(a\ne0\).** Fix \(a\). In every characteristic, choosing \(t=h^2b/a\) sends the middle parameter to zero. If the characteristic is odd, the coefficient \(2a/h\) is invertible, so varying \(u\) independently sends the last parameter to zero. The full fixed-\(a\) fiber is therefore one orbit, of size \(q^2\).

In characteristic two, \(u\) disappears from (1); after eliminating \(b\), the last coordinate becomes \(h^2(c+b^2/a)\). Thus \(\Delta=ac+b^2\) is transformed into \(h^2\Delta\), so its vanishing is invariant. Since squaring is an automorphism of every finite field of characteristic two, all nonzero \(\Delta\) values are conjugate for fixed \(a\). Among the \(q^2\) triples \((b,c)\) with this \(a\), exactly \(q\) satisfy \(\Delta=0\) and \(q(q-1)\) do not. This proves both orbit representatives and both sizes.

**When \(a=0\).** Formula (1) simplifies to \((b,c)\mapsto(hb,h^2c)\). If \(b\ne0\), the invariant \(\delta=c/b^2\) is complete: choose \(h=1/b\) to obtain \(D(0,1,\delta)\). Its orbit has \(q-1\) members, for each of the \(q\) values of \(\delta\). If \(b=0,c\ne0\), the orbit set is \(k^\times/(k^\times)^2\). That quotient has two elements for odd \(q\), with orbit sizes \((q-1)/2\), and one element for even \(q\), with orbit size \(q-1\). Finally \(D(0,0,0)\) is fixed.

**Exhaustion and counts.** These cases partition all \(q^3\) derivations. In odd characteristic their sizes sum to \((q-1)q^2+q(q-1)+2(q-1)/2+1=q^3\) and their number is \((q-1)+q+2+1=2q+2\). In characteristic two the sizes sum to \((q-1)(q+q(q-1))+q(q-1)+(q-1)+1=q^3\) and their number is \(2(q-1)+q+1+1=3q\). The complete classification follows.

Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3, standard library only. The replay returns `VERIFY_OK`; tested orbit counts in that order are \(6,8,12,12,16,24,20\). These finite computations verify the implementation and small cases, not all field sizes; the unrestricted proof is above.

## Relationship to prior work

The matrix description of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\) for the algebra denoted \(Lei_1(3,F)\) is given in Theorem 4.1 of Kurdachenko–Pypka–Velychko; it is **not** claimed as new here. The recent papers of Kurdachenko–Pypka–Semko separately tabulate automorphism and derivation algebras of all three-dimensional non-Lie left Leibniz algebras. Earlier work also establishes derivation and automorphism formulas for null-filiform Leibniz algebras. This result instead determines the complete quotient of the derivation space by the **conjugation action** over finite fields, including its normal forms, exact orbit sizes, and the characteristic-two orbit-splitting boundary. The published table descriptions and accessible relevant full texts did not furnish those orbit distributions; the two recent preprints were accessible at abstract level but not fully inspected, which leaves a historical-coverage risk.

## Limitations

The result treats ordinary derivations, not local, higher, or generalized derivations. No assertion of novelty beyond the explicitly described conjugacy-orbit classification is made; equivalent prior terminology or coverage hidden in unavailable preprint sections remains possible. Self-review is not independent validation. Computational testing is finite and is not a substitute for the all-prime-power algebraic proof.
