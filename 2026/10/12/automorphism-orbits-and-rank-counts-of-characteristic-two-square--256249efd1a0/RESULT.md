# Automorphism orbits and rank counts of characteristic-two square-zero-variable algebra retractions

## Finding

For any field \(k\) of characteristic \(2\) and any integer \(n\geq1\), let
\[
A_n(k)=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1^2,\ldots,x_n^2).
\]
An algebra endomorphism is required to be \(k\)-linear, unital, and multiplicative. A **retraction** is an endomorphism \(F\) with \(F^2=F\), where the square denotes composition.

**Theorem.** Every retraction of \(A_n(k)\) is conjugate under \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(A_n(k))\) to exactly one of the \(n+1\) maps
\[
P_r(x_i)=\begin{cases}x_i,&1\leq i\leq r,\\0,&r<i\leq n,\end{cases}\qquad 0\leq r\leq n.
\]
The image is isomorphic to \(A_r(k)\), with \(A_0(k)=k\), and the rank of \(F\) as a \(k\)-linear transformation is \(2^r\). No other ranks occur.

For \(k=\mathbb F_q\), where \(q\) is any power of \(2\), put \(s=n-r\) and
\[
\eta_{n,r}=r(2^n-2^r-2s)+s(2^r-1).
\]
The **complete labeled rank enumerator** is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{n,q}(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}
\begin{bmatrix}n\\r\end{bmatrix}_q
q^{rs+\eta_{n,r}}X^{2^r}.}
\]
Here \(\begin{bmatrix}n\\r\end{bmatrix}_q\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient. In particular, the coefficient for each permitted rank is the number of distinct endomorphism maps of that rank, not an orbit count. The two extreme coefficients, at ranks \(1\) and \(2^n\), equal \(1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All assertions about conjugacy hold over arbitrary fields of characteristic \(2\), without assumptions of finiteness, algebraic closure, or perfection. The point counts require only \(q=2^e\), \(e\geq1\). The algebra is commutative, local, finite-dimensional, and has monomial basis \(\{x_S=\prod_{i\in S}x_i:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\}\). We do not impose a grading-preservation condition on any endomorphism or automorphism.

## Proof

**1. Endomorphisms and automorphisms.** Let \(\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\). It is the nilradical, and \(\dim_k A_n=2^n\). Every \(u\in\mathfrak m\) satisfies \(u^2=0\): the Frobenius square is the sum of the squares of positive-degree monomials, all of which vanish. Thus every tuple of \(n\) elements of \(\mathfrak m\) defines a unique unital algebra endomorphism by substitution. Conversely, each endomorphism preserves the nilradical, so arises in this way.

An endomorphism is an automorphism if and only if the induced linear map on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is invertible. Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, invertibility of the linear part implies that the images of the generators generate \(\mathfrak m\) as an ideal by Nakayama's lemma. It therefore yields a surjective unital endomorphism and hence an automorphism of the finite-dimensional algebra.

**2. Conjugating any retraction.** For a retraction \(F\), write \(T\) for its induced endomorphism of \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). Then \(T^2=T\). Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}T\), and choose a basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_r,w_{r+1},\ldots,w_n\) with the \(v_i\) in \(\operatorname{im}T\) and the \(w_j\) in \(\ker T\). Choose linear combinations \(\ell_i,h_j\) of the original generators representing these basis vectors. Define
\[
a_i=F(\ell_i)\quad (1\leq i\leq r),\qquad
b_j=h_j-F(h_j)\quad (r<j\leq n).
\]
All these elements lie in \(\mathfrak m\), and their linear parts form the chosen basis. They therefore define an automorphism \(\phi\) by \(\phi(x_i)=a_i\), \(\phi(x_j)=b_j\). Since \(F(a_i)=a_i\) and \(F(b_j)=0\), we have \(F\phi=\phi P_r\), or \(F=\phi P_r\phi^{-1}\). Different \(r\) cannot be conjugate because they have different ranks on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). The map \(P_r\) has image the monomial span on its first \(r\) generators, hence linear rank \(2^r\).

**3. Automorphism group and centralizer.** Now let \(k=\mathbb F_q\). The degree-at-least-two part of \(\mathfrak m\) has dimension \(2^n-1-n\). Each automorphism corresponds to an invertible \(n\times n\) linear part and \(n\) arbitrary higher-degree tails, so
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(A_n)|=|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|q^{n(2^n-1-n)}.
\]
Fix \(r\), put \(s=n-r\), and write \(B\) for the subalgebra generated by the first \(r\) variables and \(J\) for the ideal generated by the remaining \(s\) variables. The condition \(\phi P_r=P_r\phi\) is equivalent to
\[
\phi(x_i)\in B\cap\mathfrak m\ (i\leq r),\qquad
\phi(x_j)\in J\ (j>r).
\]
The invertible linear parts are exactly the block-diagonal matrices \(\operatorname{GL}_r(q)\times\operatorname{GL}_s(q)\). There are \(2^r-1-r\) admissible higher monomials for each of the first \(r\) images and \(2^n-2^r-s\) for each of the last \(s\) images. Since any tuple with this invertible linear part defines an automorphism, the entire centralizer has order
\[
|C_{\operatorname{Aut}}(P_r)|
=|\operatorname{GL}_r(q)||\operatorname{GL}_s(q)|
q^{r(2^r-1-r)+s(2^n-2^r-s)}.
\]

**4. Orbit-stabilizer.** Every retraction of rank \(2^r\) is in the single orbit of \(P_r\). Therefore its multiplicity equals \(|\operatorname{Aut}(A_n)|/|C_{\operatorname{Aut}}(P_r)|\). The identity
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|}{|\operatorname{GL}_r(q)||\operatorname{GL}_s(q)|}
=\begin{bmatrix}n\\r\end{bmatrix}_q q^{rs}
\]
and the exponent subtraction
\[
n(2^n-1-n)-r(2^r-1-r)-s(2^n-2^r-s)
=r(2^n-2^r-2s)+s(2^r-1)
\]
produce the stated polynomial. This proof counts every non-graded endomorphism, not just maps preserving the standard grading. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` independently enumerates all unital algebra endomorphisms for \(n=1,2,3\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), tests composition-idempotence, and computes their actual linear ranks. It additionally performs an exhaustive check for \(n=2\) over \(\mathbb F_4\), implemented with explicit field arithmetic. The predicted and enumerated distributions agree:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
q&n&\mathcal R_{n,q}(X)\\\hline
2&1&X+X^2\\
2&2&X+12X^2+X^4\\
2&3&X+448X^2+3584X^4+X^8\\
4&2&X+80X^2+X^4.
\end{array}
\]
The checker reports `VERIFY_OK` when every equality holds. The theorem for general \(n\) and even \(q\) rests on the proof above; finite enumeration is not invoked as an infinite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Epstein and Nguyen, *Algebra retracts and Stanley–Reisner rings* (2013), Theorem 6.4, already prove that **graded** algebra retracts of quotients by irreducible monomial ideals, including the ideal \((x_1^2,\ldots,x_n^2)\), possess a monomial base. Consequently the graded retract isomorphism types are prior knowledge, not a novelty claim here. The present theorem treats **all unital retractions**, including arbitrary higher-degree tails, by full automorphism conjugacy and gives exact multiplicities of the labeled maps at each rank.

Berkesch, Chan, Klein, Matusevich, Page, and Vassilev, *Differential operators, retracts, and toric face rings* (first public version 2021), develop a retract-based method for studying differential operators. Their toric face rings and radical monomial quotient setting are reduced and do not supply the nonreduced square-zero-variable algebra's retraction rank polynomial. The distinction is between a preexisting retract method and the present enumeration, not a claim that retracts themselves are new.

## Limitations

The orbit and count formulas are established in characteristic \(2\) only. In other characteristics arbitrary linear substitutions need not respect the square relations, so the same proof cannot simply be transferred. The enumeration records \(k\)-linear ranks, not the finer distribution of individual kernels as labeled ideals. Searches do not logically exclude an equivalent unpublished or differently named theorem. Independent external verification has not been performed.
