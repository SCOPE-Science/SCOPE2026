# Bad reduction of the Clebsch line configuration in characteristic \(5\)
## Finding
Let \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\) and let \(X\subset\mathbf P^4_k\) be the characteristic-\(5\) reduction of the Clebsch diagonal cubic, \(\sum_{i=0}^4 x_i=\sum_{i=0}^4 x_i^3=0\). Then \(X\) has the unique ordinary double point \(p=[1:1:1:1:1]\) and exactly \(21\) geometric lines, all defined over \(\mathbf F_5\). Under the natural coordinate-permutation action of \(S_5\), these lines form exactly two orbits: \(15\) lines avoiding \(p\), each with stabilizer of order \(8\) (a Sylow-\(2\) subgroup \(D_8\)), and \(6\) lines through \(p\), each with stabilizer of order \(20\) (the normalizer \(C_5\rtimes C_4\) of a Sylow-\(5\) subgroup). The \(15\)-line orbit is the reduction of the rational diagonal orbit on the smooth Clebsch surface. The smooth \(12\)-line double-six orbit, written with the golden-ratio parameter \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), specializes at the unique prime above \(5\) with \(\phi\mapsto3\) and collapses pairwise onto the \(6\)-line orbit through \(p\).

Equivalently, the bad reduction preserves the \(15\)-element diagonal orbit but folds the smooth \(12\)-element double-six orbit two-to-one. The six limiting lines are naturally indexed by the six Sylow-\(5\) subgroups of \(S_5\): the stabilizer of any such line has order \(20\) and is the normalizer of its unique Sylow-\(5\) subgroup. The fifteen lines away from the node are naturally indexed by the fifteen Sylow-\(2\) subgroups, because each stabilizer has order \(8\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\). The surface is the complete intersection
\[
H=\sum_{i=0}^4x_i=0,\qquad F=\sum_{i=0}^4x_i^3=0
\]
inside \(\mathbf P^4_k\), and the group considered is the evident coordinate-permutation copy of \(S_5\). No assertion is made that this copy is the full automorphism group scheme of the singular surface.

For the specialization statement, the smooth Clebsch surface and its double-six lines are taken over the quadratic integer ring generated by \(\phi\), localized away from \(2\). At the unique prime above \(5\), the polynomial \(\phi^2-\phi-1\) has the repeated root \(3\), so \(\phi\mapsto3\).

## Proof
A point of \(X\) is singular exactly when the gradients of \(F\) and \(H\) are proportional. Since \(\operatorname{char} k=5\ne2\), this means that all \(x_i^2\) are equal. After scaling, write \(x_i=\varepsilon_i\) with \(\varepsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}\). The hyperplane equation requires
\[
\sum_{i=0}^4\varepsilon_i=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_5.
\]
For five signs the integer sum is one of \(\pm5,\pm3,\pm1\), and only \(\pm5\) vanishes modulo \(5\). Thus all signs agree and there is one projective singular point,
\[
p=[1:1:1:1:1].
\]

On the chart \(x_4=1\), put \(x_0=1+a\), \(x_1=1+b\), \(x_2=1+c\), and use \(H=0\) to write \(x_3=1-a-b-c\). The quadratic part of the local cubic at \(p\) is
\[
3\bigl(a^2+b^2+c^2+(a+b+c)^2\bigr).
\]
Its symmetric matrix, up to the nonzero factor \(3\), is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&2&1\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(4\) in \(\mathbf F_5\). Hence \(p\) is an \(A_1\) ordinary double point.

A one-nodal cubic surface has \(21\) lines: six through the node and fifteen away from it. For this particular \(X\), the exact finite-field calculation in `verification/verify_clebsch_mod5.py` independently finds \(21\) distinct \(\mathbf F_5\)-lines. Since the geometric total is \(21\), every geometric line of \(X\) is already defined over \(\mathbf F_5\).

The fifteen classical diagonal lines are
\[
x_i=0,\qquad x_j+x_k=0,\qquad x_\ell+x_m=0,
\]
where \(\{i,j,k,\ell,m\}=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). There are \(5\cdot3=15\) distinct such lines in characteristic \(5\), none contains \(p\), and the verifier shows that they exhaust the fifteen lines away from \(p\).

For the remaining smooth Clebsch lines, Huybrechts writes the double-six using
\[
x_i+\phi x_j+x_k=0,\qquad
\phi x_i+x_j+x_\ell=0,\qquad
-\phi(x_i+x_j)+x_4=0,
\]
for \(0\le i<j\le3\) and the two orderings of the complementary pair \((k,\ell)\). Reducing \(\phi\) to \(3\) gives twelve labeled line equations of rank \(3\). Exact row reduction shows that these twelve specialize to only six distinct lines, each occurring exactly twice; all six contain \(p\). The verifier also checks that these are precisely the six lines of \(X\) through the node.

Finally, exhaustive action of the \(120\) coordinate permutations on the \(21\) lines gives orbit sizes \(15\) and \(6\). Orbit-stabilizer yields stabilizer orders \(8\) and \(20\). An order-\(8\) subgroup of \(S_5\) is Sylow-\(2\) and is isomorphic to \(D_8\). In an order-\(20\) stabilizer the Sylow-\(5\) subgroup is unique, hence normal; the stabilizer equals its order-\(20\) normalizer in \(S_5\), namely \(C_5\rtimes C_4\).

## Verification
The standalone verifier uses only Python's standard library. It enumerates all \(806\) two-dimensional subspaces of the hyperplane \(H\cong\mathbf F_5^4\), tests the cubic restriction exactly, obtains \(21\) lines, separates them as \(6+15\) by incidence with \(p\), matches the fifteen diagonal equations and the reduced twelve golden-ratio equations, and exhausts the \(S_5\) action and stabilizers.

A fresh replay produced:

`VERIFY_OK planes=806 lines=21 line_orbits=6+15 through_node=6 away=15 golden12_to_6_pairwise stabilizers=20,8 unique_A1_node=true all_lines_F5=true`

The finite enumeration is not used by itself to infer the geometric total over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_5\); that total is supplied by the one-node cubic-surface theorem. Its role is to show that all \(21\) geometric lines are already \(\mathbf F_5\)-rational and to verify the explicit orbit and specialization statements.

## Relationship to prior work
Dolgachev--Duncan state that Sylvester's equal-coefficient Clebsch form becomes singular in characteristic \(5\), and Busé--Nonkané compute discriminant \(-5\) and identify its characteristic-\(5\) singular point. These sources do not describe the line configuration in the bad fiber.

For a smooth Clebsch surface, standard references give the two \(S_5\)-orbits of \(15\) diagonal lines and \(12\) double-six lines; Huybrechts gives the explicit golden-ratio equations used above. Dolgachev--van Geemen--Kondō prove for a one-nodal cubic that there are \(21\) lines, six through the node and fifteen away, with each node line arising as a limit of a pair of smooth lines. The present result identifies exactly how those general statements meet in the Clebsch bad fiber: the rational \(15\)-orbit survives unchanged, while the golden-ratio \(12\)-orbit is the pairwise-collapsing part, and the resulting \(S_5\)-stabilizers are determined.

## Limitations
The result concerns the reduced set of geometric lines and their natural \(S_5\)-action. It does not compute the nonreduced local structure of the Fano scheme at the six node lines, nor does it identify the full automorphism group scheme of the singular cubic. The originality search did not locate an older source stating this exact \(15+6\) bad-reduction orbit decomposition or the pairwise golden-ratio collapse; an older treatment under classical line-configuration terminology remains a residual risk.
