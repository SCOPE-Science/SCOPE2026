# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong U\), the unique effective \((-2)\)-class \(O=e-f\), and the ample chamber \(y>x>0\). Thus a primitive degree-\(2d\) class is exactly a coprime factorization \(d=xy\). The Mayer--Saint-Donat base-divisor criterion reduces the non-base-point-free case to \(D=mF+O\), which is exactly \(x=1\). Saint-Donat's projective-model criterion then reduces non-very-ampleness among the primitive base-point-free classes to an elliptic curve of intersection \(2\); the only primitive nef isotropic class is \(F=f\), so this is exactly \(x=2\). The threshold and count formulas follow without extrapolation from finite experiments.

## Originality
PASS. The lead paper uses \(e+df\) as an ample polarization for every \(d>1\) but does not analyze its fixed curve, classify all primitive degree-\(2d\) classes, or give base-point-free/very-ample counts. Exact-source, Picard-\(U\), factorization, base-locus, and projective-model searches found no statement covering this complete specialization. General K3 theorems of Mayer and Saint-Donat are broader tools, not this arithmetic classification.

## Value
PASS. The source's main construction is explicitly organized by polarization degree. The finding identifies a geometric limitation of the uniform polarizations actually used there, gives the first degrees at which the same rank-two order-three surface admits morphic and embedded primitive models, and supplies exact counts in every degree. This is a natural complete classification for the source's canonical Picard lattice rather than an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is Festi's paper itself together with the classical Mayer--Saint-Donat linear-system criteria. The remaining originality risk is an older elliptic-K3 treatment that may encode the same specialization without referring to the order-three example or to Festi's notation.
