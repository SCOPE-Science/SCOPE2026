# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses a coefficientwise identity of integer formal power series: the proper-divisor recursion implies \(F=(2\prod(1-X_j)-1)^{-1}\). A distinguished maximal exponent \(A\) factors out exactly \(2^A\). Reduction modulo two is licensed because the remaining denominator has constant term one. The coefficient is the product of integer binomial coefficients, and the binary-submask classification follows from the factorization of \((1+X)^A\) over the field of two elements. Prime powers and repeated maximum exponents are included. Exact integer enumeration over 780 exponent signatures independently verifies the boundary examples and parity rule; this is not substituted for proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources

Fink's earlier Corollary 1 gives the lower bound alone. The 2023 paper *Number of ordered factorizations and recursive divisors* gives formulas for general values and expressly asks about the normalized sequence, but does not state the binary-submask sharpness criterion. The earlier two-prime-support formula of Chor–Lemke–Mador can be simplified to its two-exponent case, and is not claimed as novel. Unlike a direct substitution of the known lower bound, the present result extracts the first nonzero two-adic layer for every number of distinct prime factors from a multivariate rational generating series. Searches did not identify equivalent general coverage. A substantial residual risk is that a general arithmetic formula already gives the criterion after a short unrecorded modular reduction; a hidden equivalent classification remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The normalization by the largest prime exponent is a central published invariant and is explicitly raised as a question in Fink's 2023 paper. The theorem determines the entire parity locus of that normalized invariant for arbitrary prime support. This is a natural infinite classification distinguishing sharpness from extra divisibility, not an arbitrary finite slice or a recomputation of a table. It does not purport to determine the higher powers of two in non-sharp cases.
