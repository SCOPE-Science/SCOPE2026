# Biquadratic splitting fields for the 400 conic-pair planes on the Barth–Bauer quartic
## Finding
Consider the smooth projective quartic over \(\mathbb Q\)
\[
X:\quad \sum_{j=0}^{3}z_j^4-6\sum_{0\le j<k\le3}z_j^2z_k^2=0\subset\mathbb P^3.
\]
Let \(\mathscr H\) be the set of geometric projective planes whose scheme-theoretic intersection with \(X\) is a union of two distinct, irreducible conics. By Naskręcki's complete conic enumeration, \(\lvert\mathscr H\rvert=400\). For an embedded plane \(H\), its field of definition is the least field over which a homogeneous linear equation cutting out \(H\) can be written, up to a nonzero scalar.

Put \(K=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt5)\). The **exact** field-of-definition distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{minimal plane field}&\mathbb Q&\mathbb Q(i)&\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)&K\\
\hline
\#\text{ planes}&16&144&48&192
\end{array}
\]
with none having minimal field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})\). In particular, \(K\) is the minimal common splitting field of the complete embedded plane configuration. The \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\)-action has precisely \(16\) singleton orbits, \(96\) two-element orbits, and \(48\) four-element orbits.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb Q\) and the quartic equation is fixed **in the displayed coordinates**, rather than considered only up to complex projective isomorphism. A "conic-pair plane" means a plane whose geometric section is a sum of two distinct smooth rational degree-two curves. A plane rational over \(\mathbb Q\) may have a section that is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) even though it splits over an extension; the theorem does **not** identify the fields of the two individual conic components. The full list of geometric conics and the relevant projective automorphism matrices are the established inputs from Naskręcki [1].

## Proof
**Completeness of the finite plane set.** Naskręcki [1, Theorem 1.1] produces all \(800\) irreducible conics as the projective automorphism orbits of three explicitly defined conics, with conic-orbit lengths \(160,160,480\). His first two conic seeds occupy the same plane
\[
H_1:\ z_0+z_1+z_2=0,
\]
while the third seed occupies
\[
H_3:\ z_2+\frac{i(1+\sqrt5)}2z_3=0.
\]
Each irreducible conic in a smooth quartic hyperplane section has a complementary residual conic of degree two, and there are at most two such components in a quartic plane section. Naskręcki's independent Gröbner-basis enumeration [1, Corollary 3.1] confirms that there are precisely \(400\) distinct conic-cutting planes. Thus the complete set is obtained by taking dual projective orbits of \(H_1,H_3\); those orbits have sizes \(160,240\) respectively and are disjoint.

**Exact dual orbit construction.** For a plane equation \(v_0z_0+v_1z_1+v_2z_2+v_3z_3=0\), encode the coefficient row as \(v=(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)\). The projective group is generated by Naskręcki's four \(4\times4\) matrices [1, §1]: the sign change \(s_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)\), the coordinate swaps \(s_3=(01)\) and \(s_4=(12)\), and
\[
s_2=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&i&i\\
1&1&-i&-i\\
-i&i&1&-1\\
-i&i&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The row actions \(v\mapsto vs_j\) generate the same complete dual orbit as the contragredient inverse actions, since the group contains inverse elements. Normalize each nonzero row by making its first nonzero coefficient equal to \(1\). This yields an unambiguous representative of each projective plane, and the normalization commutes with field automorphisms.

Every group generator has coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(i)\), whereas the seed coefficients are in \(K\); hence all \(400\) planes are defined over \(K\). Use the basis \((1,i,\sqrt5,i\sqrt5)\) to carry out exact field arithmetic over \(K\). Its independent involutions \(\sigma_i:i\mapsto-i\) and \(\sigma_5:\sqrt5\mapsto-\sqrt5\) generate \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\cong C_2\times C_2\).

The exhaustive, duplicate-free orbit traversal given in `verify_planes.py` yields these **numbers of fixed projective planes**:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
 &\operatorname{Orb}(H_1)&\operatorname{Orb}(H_3)&\mathscr H\\
\hline
\sigma_i&16&48&64\\
\sigma_5&160&0&160\\
\sigma_i\sigma_5&16&0&16
\end{array}
\]
All field computations, normalization, and equality tests are exact rational arithmetic, not floating-point approximations. The source's complete conic classification ensures that the finite traversal is exhaustive for \(\mathscr H\), rather than a sample.

**Galois descent and minimality.** A normalized plane row has minimal field equal to the fixed field of its Galois stabilizer. The number fixed by **both** generators is \(16\), so these are the \(\mathbb Q\)-planes. The \(160\) planes fixed by \(\sigma_5\) are defined over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), giving \(160-16=144\) with exact field \(\mathbb Q(i)\). Similarly, the \(64\) fixed by \(\sigma_i\) give \(64-16=48\) with exact field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\). The composite fixes just the rational \(16\), so no plane has exact field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})\). The remaining \(400-16-144-48=192\) have trivial stabilizer and exact field \(K\). Orbit-stabilizer then gives \(16\) Galois singletons, \(96\) two-element orbits, and \(48\) four-element orbits. In particular \(K\) cannot be reduced as a common field.

For explicit representatives of all four types, respectively, one may use
\[
\begin{aligned}
&z_0-z_1-z_3=0,\\
&z_0-z_1+2iz_3=0,\\
&z_0+\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}z_1-z_2+\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}z_3=0,\\
&z_2+\frac{i(1+\sqrt5)}2 z_3=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each is in the exact dual orbit and has precisely the stated minimal coefficient field.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_planes.py`. This standard-library script constructs the field \(K\) as four-component rational tuples, implements its multiplication and Galois involutions, computes the two projective group orbits by breadth-first traversal and normalization, verifies their disjointness and Galois stability, and counts stabilizers for every one of the \(400\) rows. It asserts all orbit sizes, fixed counts, and exact field distributions. The expected terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK planes=400 first_orbit=160 third_orbit=240 Q=16 Qi=144 Qsqrt5=48 K=192 Galois_orbits=16+96+48 fixed_i=64 fixed_sqrt5=160 fixed_both=16`

The computation checks the **entire finite configuration**; its use as a complete proof depends on the cited independently established theorem that the two seed plane orbits account for all \(400\) conic-pair sections.

## Relationship to prior work
Naskręcki [1] gave the explicit 800 conics, their group generators, the conic-orbit sizes \(160,160,480\), and the complete 400-plane count. He stated an extension \(\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt2,\sqrt5)\) accommodating conic-component equations. The present claim concerns the **smaller field of the supporting hyperplanes** and gives exact minimal fields and their Galois permutation profile, rather than a new count of geometric conics or planes. Degtyarev [2,3,5] analyzes complex conic configurations and the Kummer/Barth–Bauer classification; Bonnafé–Sarti [4] supplies the underlying high-symmetry K3 geometry. The inspected relevant discussions do not specify this arithmetic 400-plane field distribution.

## Limitations
This is an arithmetic statement for one explicit embedded quartic over \(\mathbb Q\); no claim is made for arbitrary quartics, twists, or coordinates. A rational cutting plane need not have rational conic components; the proof does not classify their individual fields, real loci, or intersection points. The source's published conic completeness and generator matrices are used as premises; the supplied program independently verifies their exact plane-orbit consequences. As with any literature search, unindexed older equivalent arithmetic descriptions cannot be ruled out solely by failed searches.
