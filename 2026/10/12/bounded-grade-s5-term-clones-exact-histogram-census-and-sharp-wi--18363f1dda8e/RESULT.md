# Bounded-grade S5 term clones: exact histogram census and sharp width cutoffs

## Finding

Fix integers \(n\ge1\), \(r\ge1\), and \(K\ge1\). Let \(W=[n]\) carry the universal (reflexive) accessibility relation, and let \(\mathcal B_{n,K}\) be the Boolean algebra \(\mathcal P(W)\) enriched by each counting diamond \(D_j\), for \(1\le j\le K\), where \(D_j(A)=W\) when \(|A|\ge j\) and \(D_j(A)=\varnothing\) otherwise. Let \(\mathsf T_{n,r,K}\) be the family of all \(r\)-ary term operations \(\mathcal P(W)^r\to\mathcal P(W)\) of this algebra. Put \(m=2^r\).

For any input tuple \(\mathbf A=(A_1,\ldots,A_r)\), each world has a Boolean truth-vector (atom type) \(a\in\{0,1\}^r\). Write \(c_a(\mathbf A)\) for the number of worlds of type \(a\), and let \(v_a=\min\{K,c_a(\mathbf A)\}\). **A set operation belongs to \(\mathsf T_{n,r,K}\) if and only if membership of a world in its output depends solely on that world's atom type \(a\) and the clipped histogram \(v=(v_a)_a\).** Every such operation is definable by a formula of modal depth at most one, no matter how deeply the original formula nests counting modalities.

The number of term operations is exactly
\[
\boxed{\;|\mathsf T_{n,r,K}|=2^{E(n,m,K)}\;},
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
E(n,m,K)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\}^{m}\\\sum_a v_a=n}}
|\operatorname{supp}(v)|\\
&\quad+\sum_{\substack{v\in\{0,\ldots,K\}^{m}\\\max_a v_a=K,\ \sum_a v_a\le n}}
|\operatorname{supp}(v)|.
\end{split}
\]
The first sum has value zero when there are no admissible vectors. Equivalently, defining \(H_s(z)=m(z+\cdots+z^s)(1+z+\cdots+z^s)^{m-1}\) with \(H_0(z)=0\),
\[
E(n,m,K)=[z^n]H_{K-1}(z)+\sum_{j=0}^{n}[z^j]\bigl(H_K(z)-H_{K-1}(z)\bigr).
\]

Two sharp cutoffs follow. First, the term operations become **all permutation-equivariant** maps \(\mathcal P(W)^r\to\mathcal P(W)\) precisely when
\[
\boxed{\;K\ge\lceil n/2\rceil\;}.
\]
At and above that exact grade threshold,
\[
\boxed{\;|\mathsf T_{n,r,K}|=2^{\,m\binom{n+m-2}{m-1}}\;}.
\]
Second, at fixed \(r,K\), the number of term operations is independent of \(n\) as soon as \(n\ge mK\), and then
\[
\boxed{\;E(n,m,K)=m\bigl(K(K+1)^{m-1}-(K-1)K^{m-1}\bigr)\;}.
\]
The stabilization point is sharp: at \(n=mK-1\) the exponent is smaller by exactly \(m\) (because \(m\ge2\)). For \(K=1\), this recovers the ordinary, noncounting S5 operation census \(E(n,m,1)=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(n,m)}j\binom mj\), eventually \(m2^{m-1}\).

For example, on a five-world cluster with a single input proposition, the numbers of operations available at grades \(K=1,2,3\) are respectively \(16,256,1024\). Grade three already defines every permutation-equivariant operation, and no smaller maximum grade does.

## Assumptions and scope

The accessibility relation is universal on exactly \(n\) nonempty, labelled worlds and includes self-loops. The syntax has arbitrary Boolean connectives, \(r\) independent set variables, and all modalities \(\langle\ge j\rangle\) with integer \(1\le j\le K\); thresholds greater than \(K\) are not primitive. No proposition naming a particular world, global cardinality constant, converse relation, second accessibility relation, or infinitary Boolean operation is assumed. The enumerated operations are distinct functions on **all** assignments of the \(r\) input sets, not the number of truth sets generated by one fixed assignment. The theorem is about a single S5 equivalence class, not an arbitrary disjoint union of classes or a product of multiple equivalence relations.

## Proof

Let \(\delta_a\) be the propositional conjunction specifying Boolean atom type \(a\). For any formula \(\varphi\), induct on its construction to prove that its truth at a world is determined by the pair \((a,v)\). This is immediate for atoms and preserved by Boolean connectives. For a counting subformula \(\langle\ge j\rangle\psi\), with \(j\le K\), the induction hypothesis provides the same set \(B_v\) of satisfying local types in any two assignments with the same clipped histogram. Its number of satisfying worlds is \(\sum_{b\in B_v}c_b\), and
\[
\min\!\left\{K,\sum_{b\in B_v}c_b\right\}
=\min\!\left\{K,\sum_{b\in B_v}\min(K,c_b)\right\}.
\]
Therefore the comparison with \(j\) is determined by \(v\), completing the induction even for arbitrarily nested modalities.

Conversely, every clipped histogram \(v\) satisfying the displayed sum conditions is realized by a length-\(n\) list of local types. Indeed, a vector with no saturated coordinate must sum to \(n\); one with some \(v_a=K\) can absorb any extra mass in a saturated coordinate. Define the global test \(\chi_v\) by conjoining, for every atom type \(a\), the following: \(\neg\langle\ge1\rangle\delta_a\) if \(v_a=0\); \(\langle\ge v_a\rangle\delta_a\wedge\neg\langle\ge v_a+1\rangle\delta_a\) if \(0<v_a<K\); and \(\langle\ge K\rangle\delta_a\) if \(v_a=K\). This test is true everywhere exactly on assignments with clipped histogram \(v\). For an arbitrarily chosen output-bit \(\varepsilon(v,a)\) on every supported type \(a\), the disjunction of \(\chi_v\wedge\delta_a\) over those pairs where \(\varepsilon(v,a)=1\) realizes the requested operation at depth one.

Different output-bit choices define different operations because every realizable \(v\) admits an assignment containing each atom type in its support. Thus the number of free output bits equals the sum of support sizes over all realizable clipped histograms, yielding \(E\). The generating-function identity follows by weighting each nonzero histogram coordinate once and separating vectors containing \(K\) from vectors with no coordinate equal to \(K\).

Every permutation-equivariant operation is constant on all worlds of the same local atom type for a fixed exact histogram: the stabilizer of the input assignment permutes those worlds transitively. Conversely, the preceding depth-one construction defines every such operation when clipped histograms distinguish exact histograms. If \(n\le2K\), a histogram has either at most one coordinate larger than \(K\) (which is recovered by subtracting the other exact coordinates from \(n\)), or exactly two coordinates at least \(K\), forcing \(n=2K\) and both counts equal to \(K\). Therefore clipping is injective. If \(n\ge2K+1\), two distinct exact histograms, obtained from counts \((K,n-K,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((K+1,n-K-1,0,\ldots,0)\), have the same clipped histogram. A symmetric operation that selects the first atom type in one of those exact cases but not the other is consequently not definable with maximum grade \(K\). This proves the sharp cutoff.

For unrestricted equivariance the number of output bits is the sum of support sizes across all weak compositions of \(n\) into \(m\) parts. By symmetry this is \(m\binom{n+m-2}{m-1}\). For fixed \(K\), once \(n\ge mK\), exactly the clipped vectors containing a coordinate equal to \(K\) are realizable. Subtracting support-weight enumerations of \(\{0,\ldots,K-1\}^m\) from those of \(\{0,\ldots,K\}^m\) yields the displayed stable exponent. At \(n=mK-1\) the all-\(K\) vector alone is lost; it has support size \(m\), while no unsaturated vector sums to \(n\). Hence stabilization at \(mK\) is sharp.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library Python verifier, \(\texttt{verify.py}\), checks the census in three logically distinct ways: direct enumeration of exact histograms versus clipped histograms and the displayed formula; actual closure of the Boolean/counting term-operation clone on several small clusters; and evaluation of random nested formulas across many labelled valuations, grouped by clipped histogram. It additionally checks the two sharp threshold statements on finite ranges. Its final output is:

```text
VERIFY_OK histogram_comparisons 72 direct_term_closures 6 random_formula_checks 30960 sharp_plateau_cases 9 full_grade_cutoff_checks 308
```

The unrestricted theorem follows from the proof above; finite enumeration is supporting evidence, not an exhaustive test for all parameters.

## Relationship to prior work

Ahvonen, Heiman and Kuusisto (2024) develop graded multimodal types with counting-width parameters and connect them to counting message-passing automata. The truncated multiset viewpoint here uses that established framework. Otto (2019) establishes graded counting-bisimulation characterizations and graded quantifier-rank semantics. Neither inspected article states the finite single-cluster **entire term-function classification**, its closed exponent census, or both sharp cutoffs \(K=\lceil n/2\rceil\) and \(n=mK\). In particular the present claim concerns every syntactically definable \(r\)-ary operation, not just the equivalence classes of one pointed valuation or a single graph-colour refinement.

For \(K=1\), the result specializes to familiar monadic Boolean/S5 expressivity and should not be read as a claim that the ordinary S5 depth-collapse phenomenon or free-algebra viewpoint is new. Work on finite monadic Gödel free algebras (Castaño et al., 2020) addresses a different many-valued algebraic signature and does not supply the bounded counting-grade formula. A more remote antecedent in the algebraic study of S5 clones or finite counting logics remains possible.

## Limitations

The classification requires a universal equivalence relation; it does not extend unchanged to grids, arbitrary equivalence partitions, or mixed relational signatures. The formula counts term operations for a fixed finite domain, not formula sizes or minimal Boolean circuit complexity. The finite checker is not an independent proof of the universal statements. Independent mathematical and originality audits have not yet been performed.
