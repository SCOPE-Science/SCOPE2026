# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The nonstandard step is the bounded-map normal form. For maximal inputs, singleton successor cones force maximal outputs; a lower-to-lower image forces surjectivity on the entire upper antichain; a lower-to-upper image forces a constant upper map. For an upper antichain of size at least two, these cases are disjoint and exhaustive. At size one they merge into unrestricted choices from lower inputs. The three polynomials then follow from explicit surjection counts, idempotent fixed-point choices and functional-digraph decomposition. The rooted-forest determinant gives a general proof. A standalone exhaustive enumeration across 15 small cases, including a separate modal operator check through four worlds, supports rather than substitutes for this reasoning.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk
The 2020-first-public Shapirovsky source treats sums and p-morphisms as semantic tools, not exact endomorphism distributions. Closest published mathematical records for the same two-level posets calculate **all continuous order maps**, homotopy components and weak-order retraction supports; these are not the bounded-map monoid, and their statements do not imply the six rank polynomials without the missing successor-cone rigidity and cycle/forest refinements. Direct and semantic searches did not identify this exact classification. A differently indexed transformation-semigroup antecedent remains possible; negative searches alone do not prove priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The boundary \(q=1\) is a genuine structural transition in a natural basic family of S4 sum frames: it changes an endomorphism monoid with a full-transformation/permutation component and a constant ideal to a transformation monoid with a distinguished fixed point. The classification simultaneously settles attainable algebraic ranks, dual-frame retraction supports and the finer distinction between one-step and stabilized images for every size, with explicit deterministic verification. This supplies a tractable model for studying how boundedness differs from ordinary finite-space continuity; it is not a computation of a single arbitrarily selected small instance.

## Limitations
The theorem does not extend as stated to sparse bipartite posets, general preorders, or arbitrary modal algebras. It counts dual-frame supports rather than distinct embedded retract subalgebras. Stronger or equivalent unpublished or unindexed prior results cannot be excluded; no independent audit was performed.
