# Verification

The proof is symbolic. Construction of a clopen maximal box net uses only freeness, finitely many homeomorphisms and a countable clopen cover. At the centered point, the coordinate displacement of a neighbor is between \(-r\) and \(r\) in each coordinate, yielding the bound \(2nr\). A point in the group model has at most one marked predecessor with an offset in \([0,r]^n\): two would differ by a forbidden box displacement. This gives the continuous finite-alphabet labeling. The monoid problem is a predicate on one finite forward box as in Shinko–Weilacher–Yu, Definitions 3.5–3.6. The group generated by the monoid is abelian, the restricted Borel action is free and bounded-to-one, and Theorem 3.24 and Proposition 3.22 give the transfer. In the final decoding one label at a common future point cannot equal two different offsets. No finite experiment or numerical certificate is used.

**Why a binary common-base check does not suffice.** Let \(n=2,r=1\), and let the two maps be the positive coordinate shifts on
\[
X=\{(i,j):i,j\ge0\}\cup\{(i,j):i\ge1,\ j\ge-1\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2.
\]
This countable Borel action is free and its shifts are injective. The markers \(p=(0,0)\) and \(q=(1,-1)\) have no common predecessor in \(X\), but \(f_1(p)=f_2(q)=(1,0)\). More explicitly, take the binary marked set to be \(\{p,q\}\cup\{(2k,2\ell+1): k\ge1,\ell\ge0\}\). Every point reaches a lattice marker using at most two shifts in each coordinate and at most three in total. Among marked pairs, only \(p,q\) are within the forbidden coordinate box, and no forward window contains both. Thus common-base checks of observed marker pairs pass despite a forbidden collision. Rule 2 of the offset-label problem instead tests that collision by requiring different labels at the same point; this binary pattern cannot satisfy the new rule for both markers.
