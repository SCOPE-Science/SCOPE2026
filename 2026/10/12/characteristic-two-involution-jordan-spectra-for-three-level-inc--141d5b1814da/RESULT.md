# Characteristic-two involution Jordan spectra for three-level incidence algebras

## Finding

Let \(a,b,c\geq1\) be integers and let \(q\) be any power of \(2\). Let \(P_{a,b,c}\) be the ordinal sum of three antichains of sizes \(a,b,c\). Thus every element in an earlier level is below every element in a later level, and no two distinct elements of one level are comparable. Let \(I(P_{a,b,c},\mathbb F_q)\) be its unital incidence algebra. We count elements \(g\) with \(g^2=1\), **including the identity**, by the rank \(j=\operatorname{rank}(g-1)\), equivalently by the number of size-two Jordan blocks of \(g\) acting on \(\mathbb F_q^{a+b+c}\).

Define the Gaussian coefficient \(\binom{u}{r}_q\) to be the number of \(r\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^u\), with value zero outside \(0\leq r\leq u\). Define the number of rank-\(r\) linear maps from \(\mathbb F_q^v\) to \(\mathbb F_q^u\) by
\[
\mathcal M_{u,v}(r;q)=\binom{u}{r}_q\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^v-q^i),\qquad \mathcal M_{u,v}(0;q)=1.
\]
For \(r>\min(u,v)\), put \(\mathcal M_{u,v}(r;q)=0\). The complete rank-generating polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_{a,b,c;q}(T)=
\sum_{r=0}^{\min(a,b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\min(b-r,c)}
\sum_{t=0}^{\min(a-r,c-s)}
\mathcal M_{a,b}(r;q)\mathcal M_{b-r,c}(s;q)
q^{rc+(a-r)s}\mathcal M_{a-r,c-s}(t;q)T^{r+s+t}.}
\]
Precisely the coefficient of \(T^j\) is the number of these involutions whose Jordan normal form has \(j\) blocks of size \(2\) and \(a+b+c-2j\) blocks of size \(1\), with eigenvalue \(1\). This is an exact formula for all \(a,b,c\geq1\) and every even prime power \(q\); it does not require a bound on the level sizes.

The total number of solutions \(g^2=1\) is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_{a,b,c;q}(1)=q^{ac}
\sum_{r=0}^{\min(a,b)} \mathcal M_{a,b}(r;q)q^{(b-r)c}.}
\]
For \(a=b=c=2\) and \(q=2\),
\[
\mathcal J_{2,2,2;2}(T)=1+81T+630T^2+216T^3,
\]
so the six-dimensional natural representation has exactly \(928\) involutive incidence-algebra elements, distributed by the number of size-two Jordan blocks.

## Assumptions and scope

The poset and its three labeled levels are fixed; the formula counts distinct incidence-algebra elements, not similarity, conjugacy, or poset-isomorphism classes. The finite field has characteristic \(2\). All matrices represent the incidence algebra in a linear extension of the poset listing the three levels consecutively. Their diagonal blocks are diagonal, not full matrix algebras; the strict upper off-diagonal blocks between distinct levels have unrestricted entries. We define "involution" by \(g^2=1\), so the identity is counted (as in the motivating incidence-algebra enumeration literature).

## Proof

**Step 1 (exhaustive reduction).** In the ordered incidence-matrix realization, the diagonal entries of an element \(g\) with \(g^2=1\) each satisfy \(d^2=1\). In a field of characteristic \(2\), \((d-1)^2=d^2-1\), hence \(d=1\). All diagonal blocks are therefore identities. Such an element is uniquely
\[
g=1+N,\qquad
N=\begin{pmatrix}
0&A&C\\0&0&B\\0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with \(A\in M_{a\times b}(\mathbb F_q)\), \(B\in M_{b\times c}(\mathbb F_q)\), and \(C\in M_{a\times c}(\mathbb F_q)\). Here the zero diagonal blocks express incomparability within each antichain. Because \(2=0\), \(g^2=1\) if and only if \(N^2=0\), and direct block multiplication yields \(N^2=0\) if and only if \(AB=0\). This proves that all solutions, including the identity, appear exactly once in these data.

**Step 2 (ranks).** Fix \(r=\operatorname{rank}A\) and \(s=\operatorname{rank}B\). Since \(AB=0\), \(\operatorname{im}B\subseteq\ker A\), so \(0\leq s\leq\min(b-r,c)\). Let \(\pi:\mathbb F_q^a\to \mathbb F_q^a/\operatorname{im}A\) and write \(t=\operatorname{rank}((\pi C)|_{\ker B})\). Then \(0\leq t\leq\min(a-r,c-s)\). The image of \(N\) is generated by \((Ay,0)\) and \((Cz,Bz)\). Quotienting by \(\operatorname{im}A\) in the first coordinate gives the map \(z\mapsto(\pi Cz,Bz)\), whose rank is \(s+t\): the \(B\)-image has dimension \(s\), and the kernel contribution is exactly the rank \(t\) of \(\pi C\) on \(\ker B\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}N=r+s+t.
\]
Since \(N^2=0\), all Jordan blocks of \(N\) have size at most \(2\). Each size-two block contributes one to \(\operatorname{rank}N\), and replacing \(N\) by \(1+N\) makes every eigenvalue equal to \(1\). Thus \(r+s+t\) is exactly the claimed Jordan-block statistic.

**Step 3 (counting the fibers).** There are \(\mathcal M_{a,b}(r;q)\) choices of \(A\) with rank \(r\). For each such \(A\), a compatible \(B\) is a map \(\mathbb F_q^c\to\ker A\), a space of dimension \(b-r\). Exactly \(\mathcal M_{b-r,c}(s;q)\) such \(B\)'s have rank \(s\). Fixing \(A,B\), consider the linear restriction-and-quotient map
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb F_q^c,\mathbb F_q^a)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}(\ker B,\mathbb F_q^a/\operatorname{im}A),
\quad C\longmapsto(\pi C)|_{\ker B}.
\]
It is surjective, because a linear map on the subspace \(\ker B\) extends after choosing linear complements and a lift to \(\mathbb F_q^a\). Its target dimension is \((a-r)(c-s)\) and its domain dimension is \(ac\). Thus every target map has exactly
\[
q^{ac-(a-r)(c-s)}=q^{rc+(a-r)s}
\]
preimages \(C\). There are \(\mathcal M_{a-r,c-s}(t;q)\) rank-\(t\) target maps. Multiplying the four independent factors and summing the disjoint strata \((r,s,t)\) proves the full polynomial.

**Step 4 (unrefined count).** Alternatively, after choosing \(A\) of rank \(r\), any \(B:\mathbb F_q^c\to\ker A\) is allowed, yielding \(q^{(b-r)c}\) choices, while \(C\) is arbitrary with \(q^{ac}\) choices. Summing in \(r\) proves the total-count formula independently of the rank stratification.

## Verification

The standalone `checker.py` implements the rank-map formula and independently enumerates every strict three-block matrix over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4\) for ten specified small parameter choices. It checks \(N^2=0\) by matrix multiplication and the linear rank by Gaussian elimination, comparing each complete rank histogram with the formula. In characteristic \(3\), this tests only the characteristic-independent square-zero matrix counting lemma, **not** the characteristic-two involution interpretation. In characteristic \(4\), the arithmetic uses the irreducible polynomial \(X^2+X+1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Running `python3 checker.py` prints ten `PASS` lines followed by `VERIFY_OK`. In particular, the exhaustive \((a,b,c,q)=(2,2,2,2)\) case returns counts \((1,81,630,216)\) for ranks \((0,1,2,3)\). These finite tests corroborate the general, noncomputational proof; they are not used as infinite-field or all-parameter certificates.

## Relationship to prior work

Gargate and Gargate, *Involutions on Incidence Algebras of Finite Posets* (arXiv:1907.06805v1, first public 16 July 2019), develop an enumeration method for elements \(g\) satisfying \(g^2=1\) in incidence algebras, but explicitly impose characteristic different from \(2\); their diagonal-sign/recursion method uses inverses of \(2\) and does not supply the characteristic-two rank-refined formula. The present theorem addresses a different characteristic and the full Jordan multiplicity distribution for three antichain layers, crediting their motivation and terminology.

Fornaroli and Pezzott, *Classification of involutions on finitary incidence algebras of non-connected posets* (arXiv:2209.09690v1), use "involution" for an order-two algebra anti-automorphism, not for an incidence-algebra **element** squaring to the identity; their results are also stated in characteristic different from \(2\). They therefore answer a different question. Wu and Zhou, *Singular Cholesky Fibers over Finite Fields* (arXiv:2609.00533v1), announce a rank recursion for square-zero elements of the **entire** upper-triangular matrix algebra. The announced aggregate counts do not, by themselves, determine the rank distribution after imposing the fixed missing-entry pattern of a three-level incidence algebra. A full comparison with that latter paper's detailed results remains an explicit literature risk.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns complete three-level posets only, and does not classify arbitrary finite posets or calculate conjugacy-class sizes. It counts labeled matrices. No assertion is made about involutive anti-automorphisms. No absolute bibliographic uniqueness is claimed; broader parabolic-nilradical square-zero stratifications might contain equivalent formulas. The 2026 source noted above was compared from its public abstract only rather than from a fully inspected text. The mathematical proof is valid over every finite field of characteristic \(2\) for the stated posets, independently of experimental ranges.
