# Review: Classification of optimal binary length-six Z-channel codes

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is finite and exact. Compatibility was reconstructed both from the asymmetric-distance definition and from disjoint one-error Z-balls on all \(64\) words. Exhaustive maximal-clique enumeration gives maximum size \(12\) and exactly \(30\) maxima; an independent increasing-order enumeration recovers the same \(30\) twelve-cliques and finds no thirteen-clique. The explicit perfect-matching/parity construction generates exactly those \(30\) codes. The stated weight distribution, free-point count, one-orbit statement, and stabilizer order are all replayed from the supplied verifier.

## Originality
PASS. The inspected 1997 source supplies an explicit length-six twelve-word code, later literature records the scalar optimum and associated free-point/weight-distribution data, and general sphere-packing work covers bounds. None of the inspected sources states or implies the complete \(30\)-code census, the perfect-matching/parity characterization of every equality case, or the single \(S_6\)-orbit conclusion. The known optimum itself is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim.

## Value
PASS. Equality structure at this exact parameter is mathematically motivated by the later use of free points in two-stage Z-channel coding. The result turns a tabulated optimum and isolated constructions into a complete classification, explains why the standard example is representative of every optimum, and shows that every optimal code is automatically F-optimal. This is a natural exact invariant/classification rather than an arbitrary slice.

Closest literature and limitations: the closest inspected items are the 1997 explicit construction, the later exact small-parameter Z-channel tables and F-optimal framework, and general Z-channel sphere-packing bounds. The result is restricted to binary length \(6\) and one asymmetric error, and an obscure unindexed finite census remains a residual originality risk.
