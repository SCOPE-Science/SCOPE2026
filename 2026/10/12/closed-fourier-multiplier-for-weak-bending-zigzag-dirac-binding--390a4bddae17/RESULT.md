# Closed Fourier multiplier for weak-bending zigzag-Dirac binding
## Finding
Consider the planar zigzag Dirac operator in a strip of half-width \(a>0\) generated by a curve whose curvature is \(\beta\gamma\), under the regularity and decay assumptions used for the weak-bending theorem of Exner and Holzmann. Put
\[
\mu=\left(\frac{\pi}{2a}\right)^2,
\qquad
q=\frac{2a|\xi|}{\pi},
\]
and use the Fourier convention
\[
\widehat\gamma(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-i\xi s}\gamma(s)\,ds.
\]
The coefficient in the source asymptotic
\[
\sqrt{\mu-\epsilon(\beta)}=C_a[\gamma]\,\beta^2+O(\beta^3)
\]
has the exact one-dimensional multiplier representation
\[
C_a[\gamma]=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat\gamma(\xi)|^2
M\!\left(\frac{2a|\xi|}{\pi}\right)d\xi,
\]
where \(M(0)=1\),
\[
M(q)=\frac4{q^2}-\frac{16\sqrt{1-q^2}}{\pi q^4}
\cot\!\left(\frac\pi2\sqrt{1-q^2}\right),\qquad 0<q<1,
\]
\[
M(1)=4-\frac{32}{\pi^2},
\]
and
\[
M(q)=\frac4{q^2}-\frac{16\sqrt{q^2-1}}{\pi q^4}
\coth\!\left(\frac\pi2\sqrt{q^2-1}\right),\qquad q>1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
M(q)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}w_j\frac{A_j}{A_j+q^2},
\qquad
A_j=4j^2-1,
\qquad
w_j=\frac{256j^2}{\pi^2(4j^2-1)^3}.
\]
The weights satisfy \(\sum_jw_j=1\) and \(\sum_jw_jA_j=4\). Hence \(M\) is positive and strictly decreasing on \((0,\infty)\), \(q^2M(q)\to4\), and
\[
\frac3{3+q^2}\le M(q)\le\frac4{4+q^2}\qquad(q\ge0).
\]
Thus the leading weak-bending coefficient is an explicit positive low-pass quadratic form, with no remaining transverse-mode summation.

For the positive zigzag-Dirac threshold
\[
E_{\mathrm{th}}=c\sqrt{m^2c^2+\mu},
\]
and the positive discrete eigenvalue \(\lambda_1(\beta)=c\sqrt{m^2c^2+\epsilon(\beta)}\), the threshold gap therefore obeys
\[
E_{\mathrm{th}}-\lambda_1(\beta)
=\frac{c\,C_a[\gamma]^2}{2\sqrt{m^2c^2+\mu}}\,\beta^4+O(\beta^5).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The geometric hypotheses are exactly those of the source weak-bending theorem for a planar strip: the strip is generated by a sufficiently regular non-straight curve with scaled curvature \(\beta\gamma\), the required decay and integrability assumptions hold, and \(\beta\) is sufficiently small. The statement concerns the unique positive/negative pair of weakly induced zigzag-Dirac eigenvalues and the corresponding Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenvalue \(\epsilon(\beta)\). No claim is made for other boundary conditions, strong bending, or dimensions not covered by this planar calculation.

## Proof
For the normalized transverse Dirichlet basis
\[
\chi_n(u)=a^{-1/2}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi n(u+a)}{2a}\right),
\]
direct integration gives
\[
(\chi_n,u\chi_1)
=-\frac{8an\bigl((-1)^n+1\bigr)}{\pi^2(n^2-1)^2}.
\]
Thus only even modes contribute. Fourier transforming the exponential kernel in the source coefficient and using Parseval gives
\[
C_a[\gamma]=\frac1{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat\gamma(\xi)|^2M(q)\,d\xi,
\qquad q=\frac{2a|\xi|}{\pi},
\]
with
\[
M(q)=\frac{64}{\pi^2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}
\frac{(2j)^2}{\bigl((2j)^2-1\bigr)^2\bigl((2j)^2-1+q^2\bigr)}.
\]
Writing \(A_j=4j^2-1\) gives the weighted-resolvent form stated above. The identities
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{4j^2-r^2}
=\frac1{2r^2}-\frac\pi{4r}\cot\!\left(\frac{\pi r}{2}\right),
\]
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(4j^2-1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2-8}{16},
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(4j^2-1)^3}=\frac{32-3\pi^2}{64},
\]
together with partial fractions yield the trigonometric formula when \(r^2=1-q^2>0\). Analytic continuation \(r=i\sqrt{q^2-1}\) yields the hyperbolic formula for \(q>1\), and the limit at \(q=1\) gives \(4-32/\pi^2\).

The same summation identities give \(\sum_jw_j=1\) and \(\sum_jw_jA_j=4\). Termwise differentiation of \(A_j/(A_j+q^2)\) proves strict decrease for \(q>0\). Since \(A_j\ge3\), the lower bound follows termwise. For the upper bound, \(A\mapsto A/(A+q^2)\) is concave for \(q>0\), so Jensen's inequality and the weighted mean \(\sum_jw_jA_j=4\) give \(M(q)\le4/(4+q^2)\). Dominated convergence in the weighted series gives \(q^2M(q)\to4\).

Finally, the source asymptotic implies \(\mu-\epsilon(\beta)=C_a[\gamma]^2\beta^4+O(\beta^5)\). Exact rationalization of the relativistic dispersion relation gives
\[
E_{\mathrm{th}}-\lambda_1(\beta)
=\frac{c\bigl(\mu-\epsilon(\beta)\bigr)}{
\sqrt{m^2c^2+\mu}+\sqrt{m^2c^2+\epsilon(\beta)}} ,
\]
which yields the displayed quartic law.

## Verification
A standalone checker evaluates the transverse matrix elements by direct quadrature, independently sums the defining series, compares it with the closed trigonometric/hyperbolic formula at representative values on both sides of \(q=1\), verifies the two weight moments, checks the stated rational bounds, and checks the exact rationalization of the Dirac threshold gap. These computations corroborate the algebraic proof; they are not used to infer an infinite identity from finite data.

## Relationship to prior work
Exner and Holzmann give the exact Dirac-to-Dirichlet spectral correspondence and the weak-bending expansion from which the present calculation starts. Exner and Kovařík's monograph already rewrites the same weak-bending coefficient as a manifestly positive Fourier integral with an infinite transverse-mode sum; that positivity and Fourier representation are therefore prior work, not part of the novelty claimed here. The contribution here is the exact summation of that transverse series to the elementary multiplier \(M\), together with its strict monotonicity, explicit two-sided rational bounds, high-frequency constant, and the resulting closed quartic coefficient for the zigzag-Dirac threshold gap. Borrelli, Briet, Krejčiřík and Ourmières-Bonafos study relativistic waveguides with infinite-mass boundary conditions rather than the zigzag model and do not imply this multiplier identity.

## Limitations
The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed calculation of the same transverse series under another normalization. The two rational bounds are convenient structural bounds and are not claimed to be globally optimal. The result uses the source weak-bending hypotheses and does not improve the remainder term in the source Laplacian asymptotic.
