# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the source normalization and Fourier convention, computes the transverse matrix element exactly, evaluates the infinite mode sum by partial fractions and the classical cotangent expansion, and transfers the resulting Laplacian deficit to the zigzag-Dirac energy by exact rationalization. A separate checker confirms the matrix elements, weight moments, closed formula on both sides of \(q=1\), bounds, and dispersion rationalization. Finite checks are corroborative only.

## Originality
PASS. The exact same primary weak-bending theorem and the strongest cited predecessor were inspected. Exner--Kovařík already establish the positive Fourier representation with an unsummed transverse series, so that ingredient is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Searches over weak-bending, zigzag-Dirac, Fourier-multiplier and threshold-gap aliases found no covering statement of the elementary summed multiplier or its consequences. The closest relativistic-waveguide work found uses infinite-mass rather than zigzag boundary conditions. Residual risk remains that an unindexed calculation has performed the same classical series summation under another normalization.

## Value
PASS. The coefficient controls the first nonzero weak-bending binding scale and is presented in the predecessor literature as an infinite competition among transverse modes. The exact multiplier reveals its frequency response, strict monotonicity, explicit scale bounds and high-frequency constant, and gives a closed leading coefficient for the relativistic threshold gap. These are structural properties of the canonical weak-bending invariant.

## Closest literature and limitations
The primary source is Exner--Holzmann, arXiv:2112.08109v1. The closest prior formula is Exner--Kovařík, *Quantum Waveguides*, Chapter 6, especially Eq. (6.14), which already proves positivity via an unsummed Fourier series. The present result does not improve the source remainder and does not claim its rational multiplier bounds are optimal.
