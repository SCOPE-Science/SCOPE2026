# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof checks the full infinite claim. The finite base family contributes only \(O_{\mathcal A}(\log X)\) powers up to \(X\). Mertens' product estimate reduces the simultaneous raw shift family to at most \(\eta x\) surviving shifts after \(c_{\mathcal A}\) is chosen small. A power-free interval of length \(\gg_{\mathcal A}X/\log X\) absorbs the entire translation range because \(Qe^x=X^{2/3+o(1)}\), while the supplied auxiliary prime divisors are only \(X^{o(1)}\). This makes every positive prime candidate a proper composite difference; powers above the block give negative differences.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source proves the \(\log\log X\) lower bound for one fixed base and states the extension for each fixed \(a\ge2\), but does not state a common interval avoiding several fixed bases simultaneously. The recorded searches for finite-base, simultaneous, union, and common-gap formulations produced no dominating statement. Separate one-base gaps do not imply a common gap, so the synchronization step is not a formal corollary of their conclusions.

## Value
PASS. Finite unions are a natural next object for Romanoff-type sets. Showing that the same gap scale survives simultaneous avoidance demonstrates robustness of the recent large-shift sieve and supplies a structurally stronger conclusion than separate one-base estimates.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is A. Radomskii, *Large gaps between Romanoff numbers*, arXiv:2609.38408v1, especially Lemma 3.1 and Section 5. The theorem here fixes \(\mathcal A\) once and for all; it neither treats growing base families nor optimizes the implied constant, and it proves no matching upper bound.
