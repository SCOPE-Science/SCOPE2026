# Scientific review: binary order-four partial-word census

**Correctness — PASS.** Every candidate is represented uniquely by one of the \(27\) initial three-character strings followed by legal append operations. A new window is accepted precisely when the matched set is disjoint from all previously matched sets; a completed cover is therefore a partition of all \(16\) binary four-mers, and conversely every valid string survives each step. The strictly increasing coverage count bounds the depth by \(16\) and makes the exhaustive search complete. Separate integer-bitmask (C++17) and direct-string (Python 3) implementations agree on every one of the ten profile entries; the latter additionally rechecks each of the \(385\) strings and all reversal/complement orbits against the supplied complete list. Computation proves a finite census, not a statement for arbitrary factor length.

**Originality — PASS, with acknowledged residual risk.** Chen–Kitaev–Mütze–Sun's original full paper and its shorter conference treatment supply binary order-four examples and general existence/obstruction results, not the complete ten-term exact length-by-wildcard enumerator. The follow-up work by Goeckner and collaborators poses the number of universal partial words as an explicit enumerative question (Question 6.5). Published-result and mathematical-alias searches did not identify the proposed exact counts; the ordinary \(256\) de Bruijn strings were already known. Related material could still be present in unindexed computations or author-hosted datasets, and the entire follow-up PDF was not inspected.

**Value — PASS.** The total count at a specific smallest interesting binary order is a natural exact object explicitly motivated by the literature's enumeration question. The ten coefficients distinguish the available tradeoffs between wildcard count and physical sequence length; the \(32\) nontrivial symmetry types and complete list make the result a concrete benchmark for constructions and general enumeration methods. Its scope is deliberately finite; no unsupported larger-order claim is made.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
