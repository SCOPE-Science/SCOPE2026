# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is purely finite and the edge list defines a 24-vertex cubic graph without unspecified cases. Every possible dominating six-, seven-, and eight-set is checked by exact bitwise neighborhood unions; all 58,905 four-edge deletions and 376,992 five-edge deletions are checked against all 1,767 minimum dominating sets. Exactly 276 five-edge deletions destroy them all. The complete set is then checked for vertex stars; all graph automorphisms are generated by exhaustive adjacency-preserving backtracking and used to obtain the eight disjoint orbits. An actual nine-vertex dominating witness is checked for each orbit representative, forcing the uniform value nine. The separate critical-edge-bundle implementation agrees on the cardinality and supplies the 276-set table. Scientific boundary: computational exhaustion of a fixed finite graph is exact here; no infinite-family statement is made.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The strongest directly matching source is Srivastava, arXiv:2609.02477v1, Sections 3–5. It already gives the graph, its minimum domination number eight, its bondage number five, all 1,767 eight-vertex dominating sets, and one successful five-edge deletion. Those facts alone neither entail the newly counted 276 successful five-edge deletions nor their eight symmetry classes and compulsory complete star. The source's full HTML text and exact statement were compared; title/abstract-only review would have been insufficient. Exact-phrase and semantic searches for minimum deletion sets, automorphism orbits, and star extensions found no matching refinement. Residual risk: a later update, supplementary computation not surfaced in the text, or an unindexed equivalent enumeration could cover the result.

## Value — PASS

The motivating graph is a published cubic planar counterexample to a long-standing conjectured bondage bound. Identifying **every** minimum-cardinality destructive edge set determines the complete optimal fault landscape, not merely one certificate that the bound fails. The strong structural collapse—every five-edge witness contains a vertex star, each vertex has exactly thirteen extensions, and all witnesses fall into eight geometric symmetry types—is useful for studying criticality and robustness of that specific extremal graph, independently of generalization to other graphs. It is not a routine restatement of the previously known bondage number.
