# Complete equality-case classification for binary length-five edit-metric codes at distance three
## Finding
For binary words of length five with ordinary Levenshtein edit distance, the known optimum is \(E_2(5,3)=4\). There are exactly \(28\) labeled maximum codes. Every maximum is an affine \(2\)-flat in \(\mathbb F_2^5\). The maxima have exactly \(14\) direction subspaces, and each direction supports exactly two optimal cosets; the two are exchanged by global bit complement. The full automorphism group of the compatibility graph whose vertices are \(\{0,1\}^5\) and whose edges join words at edit distance at least \(3\) has order \(4\), generated by complement and word reversal. Thus the \(28\) maxima split into exactly eight equivalence classes, of sizes \(2,2,4,4,4,4,4,4\). Exactly \(18\) maxima have pair-distance multiset \(\{3,3,3,3,3,4\}\), and the remaining \(10\) have \(\{3,3,3,3,4,4\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The edit distance is the minimum number of single-symbol substitutions, insertions, and deletions needed to transform one finite binary string into another. Only fixed-length input words of length \(5\) are codewords. A code is feasible when every two distinct codewords have edit distance at least \(3\). "Affine" is with respect to coordinatewise addition in \(\mathbb F_2^5\). The automorphism statement concerns the complete compatibility graph for this exact parameter, not all edit-metric spaces at other lengths or distances.

## Proof
The computation enumerates all \(32\) binary length-five words and computes every pairwise edit distance by two independent exact recurrences. It forms the compatibility graph and checks all \(\binom{{32}}{{4}}\) four-subsets and all \(\binom{{32}}{{5}}\) five-subsets. Exactly \(28\) four-subsets are cliques and no five-subset is a clique, proving both the optimum \(4\) and the labeled census.

For each maximum code \(C\), choose any \(a\in C\) and form \(H=C+a\). Direct closure checks show that \(H\) contains \(0\), has four elements, and is closed under addition, so every maximum is an affine \(2\)-flat. Deduplication yields exactly \(14\) such direction subspaces. Each direction occurs for exactly two maximum cosets, and direct comparison shows that the two cosets are exchanged by adding \(11111\), equivalently by global bit complement.

For the compatibility graph, an exact graph-automorphism backtracking search preserves adjacency and nonadjacency and finds exactly four automorphisms. They are the identity, complement, reversal, and complement followed by reversal. Acting on the \(28\) maximum codes gives eight orbits with sizes \(2,2,4,4,4,4,4,4\). Direct distance tallying gives the two pair-distance multisets stated above.

## Relationship to prior work
Houghten, Ashlock, and Lenarz introduced the fixed-length edit-code extremal function and tabulated small binary parameters. Their table records \(E_2(5,3)=4\); the inspected technical-report text states that the tables give optimal sizes or bounds, and the maintained binary table still records the exact entry \(4\) at length \(5\), distance \(3\). The inspected source does not enumerate all equality cases, characterize them as affine planes, determine the full compatibility-graph automorphism group at this parameter, or classify maximum codes up to that group.

The 2005 technical report is only month-resolved in the inspected bibliographic evidence. The day-resolved source date stored here is the opening date, 2006-10-22, of the IEEE Information Theory Workshop at which the corresponding paper was presented. This date is used for cohort placement without inventing a day for the earlier month-only technical-report record.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite classification only for binary length \(5\) and minimum edit distance \(3\). It does not assert a structural theorem for larger lengths. The originality comparison is limited by indexing and access: the inspected 2005/2006 sources and maintained table give the optimum but not the equality-case census, and targeted database searches found no implication-equivalent classification; an obscure unindexed finite enumeration remains a residual risk.
