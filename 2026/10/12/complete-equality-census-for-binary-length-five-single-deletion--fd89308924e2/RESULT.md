# Complete equality census for binary length-five single-deletion codes
## Finding
For binary words of length \(5\), let \(D_1(x)\) be the set of distinct binary words of length \(4\) obtained by deleting one coordinate from \(x\). A code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^5\) corrects one deletion when \(D_1(x)\cap D_1(y)=\varnothing\) for every two distinct codewords \(x,y\in C\).

The maximum size is the known value \(6\), and there are exactly six labeled maximum codes. Under the natural order-four action generated by global bit complement and coordinate reversal, these six maxima form exactly three orbits, each of size two.

Exactly two maxima are perfect in the deletion-shadow sense: their shadows partition all \(16\) binary words of length \(4\). They are precisely the Varshamov--Tenengolts classes \(VT_0(5)\) and \(VT_3(5)\). The other four maxima are nonperfect and cover exactly \(14\) received words each.

The complete labeled list is:

1. \(\{\mathtt{00000},\mathtt{00011},\mathtt{01101},\mathtt{10010},\mathtt{11100},\mathtt{11111}\}\).
2. \(\{\mathtt{00000},\mathtt{00011},\mathtt{01110},\mathtt{10101},\mathtt{11000},\mathtt{11111}\}\).
3. \(\{\mathtt{00000},\mathtt{00111},\mathtt{01001},\mathtt{10110},\mathtt{11000},\mathtt{11111}\}\).
4. \(\{\mathtt{00000},\mathtt{00111},\mathtt{01010},\mathtt{10001},\mathtt{11011},\mathtt{11100}\}\).
5. \(\{\mathtt{00000},\mathtt{00111},\mathtt{01010},\mathtt{10001},\mathtt{11100},\mathtt{11111}\}\).
6. \(\{\mathtt{00011},\mathtt{00100},\mathtt{01110},\mathtt{10101},\mathtt{11000},\mathtt{11111}\}\).

The six maxima split into three shadow-size/coverage types, each occurring twice:
\[
(1,1,2,2,4,4;14),\qquad (1,1,2,2,3,5;14),\qquad (1,2,2,3,3,5;16).
\]
Here the first six entries are the sorted cardinalities \(\lvert D_1(x)\rvert\) of the six codewords and the final entry is the number of distinct received words covered by their union.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is binary, block length is exactly \(5\), and exactly one deletion must be correctable in the usual zero-error sense. Codewords are labeled binary strings; no quotient by symmetry is taken when counting the six maxima. The stated symmetry classification uses only the natural channel symmetries generated by bit complement and reversal. No claim is made that this order-four group is the full abstract automorphism group of the compatibility graph.

## Proof
Construct the compatibility graph \(G\) on all \(32\) binary words of length \(5\), joining two distinct words exactly when their one-deletion shadows are disjoint. A single-deletion-correcting code is exactly a clique of \(G\).

The verifier constructs \(G\) in two independent ways. First it compares the deletion shadows directly. Second it computes the longest common subsequence of each pair by dynamic programming. For equal-length words of length \(5\), two words have intersecting one-deletion shadows exactly when their longest common subsequence has length at least \(4\). The two constructions agree on every pair and give \(305\) compatibility edges.

The finite extremal step is exhaustive. The verifier checks all \(\binom{32}{6}=906192\) six-subsets and finds exactly six cliques. It also checks all \(\binom{32}{7}=3365856\) seven-subsets and finds none. Independently, a Bron--Kerbosch maximal-clique search gives maximal-clique size histogram
\[
3:12,\qquad 4:300,\qquad 5:288,\qquad 6:6,
\]
and exactly the same six maximum cliques. Therefore the maximum is \(6\) and the equality census is complete.

For each maximum, the verifier then applies complement and reversal, computes the resulting orbit, deletion-shadow sizes, total covered received words, and the weighted checksum \(\sum_{i=1}^5 i x_i\pmod 6\). Exactly two maxima cover all \(16\) received words, and their checksum is constant \(0\) or constant \(3\), identifying them as \(VT_0(5)\) and \(VT_3(5)\). The remaining four maxima each cover exactly \(14\) received words.

## Relationship to prior work
Sloane's survey states the exact optimum \(A(5,1)=6\), exhibits a size-six Varshamov--Tenengolts code, and explains that \(VT_0(n)\) is optimal for the small lengths then known. It does not enumerate all optimal length-five codes or classify their perfectness and natural-symmetry orbits. The same survey also notes that \(VT_0(n)\) ceases to be linear starting at \(n=5\), making this the first nonlinear block length in that classical family.

Kulkarni and Kiyavash formulate deletion correction as a hypergraph matching problem and give nonasymptotic upper bounds. Their general binary single-deletion bound specializes at length \(5\) to an upper bound strictly larger than \(6\), so it neither yields the sharp equality census nor implies the classification above. Their paper also distinguishes exact finite optimality from asymptotic optimality.

Perfect-code results do not subsume the finding: only two of the six maxima are perfect, while the other four are optimal but nonperfect. Thus a classification restricted to perfect Varshamov--Tenengolts classes cannot imply the complete equality census.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite classification only for binary length \(5\) and one deletion. It does not classify all perfect single-deletion codes at arbitrary lengths, does not settle universal Varshamov--Tenengolts optimality, and does not identify the full abstract automorphism group of the length-five compatibility graph. The literature comparison cannot rule out an obscure unindexed historical enumeration; no such enumeration was found in the inspected primary sources or targeted published-finding searches.
