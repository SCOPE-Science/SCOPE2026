# Complete equality census for binary length-seven two-deletion codes
## Finding
For binary two-deletion-correcting codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^7\), the known optimum is \(5\). The complete equality census contains exactly \(476\) labeled maximum codes.

Under the order-four group generated by word reversal and global bit complementation, the \(476\) maxima form exactly \(123\) orbits: \(115\) orbits have size \(4\), and \(8\) have size \(2\). Their total intersection is empty. Exactly \(94\) maxima contain neither `0000000` nor `1111111`.

For a maximum code, count the distinct length-five strings that can be received after two deletions from one of its codewords. Across all maxima, the exact coverage distribution is
\[
17:4,\;19:6,\;20:12,\;21:16,\;22:30,\;23:48,\;24:64,\;25:72,\;26:80,\;27:78,\;28:44,\;29:16,\;30:6.
\]
Thus the largest coverage among cardinality-optimal codes is \(30\). Exactly six maxima attain coverage \(30\), and those six form two orbits of sizes \(2\) and \(4\) under reversal and complementation.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(x=x_1\cdots x_7\), let \(D_2(x)\) be the set of distinct length-five strings obtained by deleting exactly two coordinates while preserving the order of the survivors. A code corrects two deletions precisely when \(D_2(x)\cap D_2(y)=\varnothing\) for all distinct codewords \(x,y\in C\). “Labeled” means that binary words retain their ordinary coordinates and symbols. The symmetry statement concerns only the explicit four transformations generated by reversal and common bit complement; it does not claim the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph.

The scalar optimum \(5\) is prior data. The contribution here is the equality census, orbit decomposition, endpoint-membership boundary, and exact output-coverage refinement.

## Proof
Construct a graph on all \(128\) binary words of length seven. Join two vertices exactly when their two-deletion descendant sets are disjoint. By definition, two-deletion-correcting codes are exactly cliques in this compatibility graph.

The bundled verifier constructs each descendant set twice: first by choosing the two deleted coordinates, and independently by choosing the five retained coordinates. It requires the two constructions to agree for every ambient word. The resulting graph has exactly \(2,955\) compatibility edges.

The verifier then performs an exhaustive increasing-vertex clique search. It enumerates every clique of size \(5\), obtaining exactly \(476\), and separately exhausts all branches that could contain a clique of size \(6\), finding none. This independently reproduces the known optimum \(5\) and proves that the stored list in `census.json` is the complete equality layer rather than a sample.

For every one of the \(476\) maxima, the verifier directly recomputes reversal and complement images, the membership of the two constant words, the intersection over all maxima, and the union of its deletion-descendant sets. The orbit sizes sum to \(115\cdot4+8\cdot2=476\). The coverage histogram also sums to \(476\), and the six coverage-\(30\) maxima split into one size-\(2\) and one size-\(4\) orbit.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `census.json`. The expected first terminal line is:

`VERIFY_OK vertices=128 edges=2955 maximum=5 labeled_maxima=476 orbits=123 orbit_hist=2x8,4x115 neither_constants=94 max_coverage=30 max_coverage_codes=6 max_coverage_orbits=2,4`

The second line reports the SHA-256 of the canonical complete census. The packaged value is `ef27d21e5565594f12116570dcdd31b8565bbb2b2d1504cf419fade24482618f`.

The finite search is exhaustive. No randomized sampling, timeout inference, or asymptotic extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A057591, created on 5 October 2000, records the maximum sizes of binary codes correcting two deletions and gives the length-seven value \(5\). Sloane’s associated independent-set challenge page identifies the exact `2dc.128` benchmark, states the same optimum, and gives one five-word example. On that same page the length-nine benchmark is explicitly accompanied by the statement that there are \(120\) maximum independent sets, whereas no corresponding all-maxima count is stated for `2dc.128`. The present result fills that finite equality-layer gap at length seven.

Kulkarni and Kiyavash formulate general deletion correction through pairwise-disjoint deletion sets and a hypergraph matching model, including multiple deletions. Their non-asymptotic framework supplies general bounds and optimization language, but the inspected full text does not state the \(476\)-maximum census, the \(123\)-orbit decomposition, or the coverage distribution above.

Targeted searches for the exact benchmark name, the count \(476\), all-maxima terminology, reversal/complement equivalence, and output coverage did not locate a publication or published-record result implying these classifications.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact result for binary length seven and exactly two deletions. It does not improve the already-known scalar optimum \(5\), does not classify larger lengths, and does not identify the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph. The coverage statistic counts distinct channel outputs reachable from a code and is a secondary refinement of cardinality-optimal codes; it is not a probability-of-error statement. An obscure or unindexed historical computational census remains a residual originality risk.
