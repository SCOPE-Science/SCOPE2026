# Complete equality census for binary length-six comma-free codes
## Finding
For binary fixed-length comma-free codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\), the classical maximum \(W_6(2)=9\) is attained by exactly \(1{,}972\) labeled codes. Under the order-four group generated by global bit complementation and reversal of every word, these maxima form exactly \(493\) orbits, all of size \(4\). In particular, no maximum is fixed by complementation, reversal, or their composition.

A lexicographically least maximum code is
\[
\{000001,000101,000111,100001,100101,100111,101001,101011,101111\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A code is a subset of the binary words of length \(6\). It is comma-free when, for every ordered pair \(x,y\in C\) and every shift \(1\le j\le5\), the word formed from the last \(6-j\) symbols of \(x\) followed by the first \(j\) symbols of \(y\) is not in \(C\). “Labeled” means that the two alphabet symbols and the six coordinate positions are fixed. The symmetry statement uses only the explicit four-element group generated by \(0\leftrightarrow1\) applied globally and reversal of the six coordinates.

## Proof
A word belonging to a comma-free code must be primitive under cyclic rotation: otherwise a nontrivial self-overlap reproduces the word. Two distinct rotations of the same primitive word also cannot both belong to a comma-free code. For binary words of length \(6\), Möbius inversion gives \(2^6-2^3-2^2+2=54\) primitive words, partitioned into exactly \(54/6=9\) primitive cyclic necklaces. Hence every comma-free code has at most \(9\) words.

The displayed code is checked directly from the definition, so the upper bound is attained. Every maximum code must therefore choose exactly one representative from each of the nine primitive necklaces. The standalone verifier enumerates exactly this finite search space. It recursively chooses one representative from each necklace and discards a partial choice as soon as a forbidden overlap already lies in that partial code. This pruning is logically safe because adding words cannot remove a forbidden overlap. The resulting exhaustive census contains exactly \(1{,}972\) codes, each independently rechecked against all ordered word pairs and all five nonzero shifts.

Global complementation and reversal preserve comma-freedom. The verifier applies the four resulting transformations to every one of the \(1{,}972\) maxima, verifies closure of the census under the action, and computes every orbit. There are \(493\) orbits and every orbit has size \(4\), so none of the three nonidentity transformations fixes a maximum.

## Relationship to prior work
Crick, Griffith, and Orgel introduced the comma-free coding idea in 1957. Golomb, Gordon, and Welch gave the fixed-length mathematical framework and the primitive-cyclic-class upper bound in 1958. Jiggs' 1963 Table I records the historical optimum \(W_6(2)=9\). The inspected historical and modern sources establish the model, upper bound, and optimum value, but they do not state the complete labeled equality census or the \(493\)-orbit decomposition asserted here. Modern computer-aided work continues to study exact values at other parameters, including \(W_6(3)\) and \(W_{12}(2)\).

## Limitations
The classification is for binary fixed word length exactly \(6\) and for the stated four-element complement/reversal symmetry group. It does not classify maximal nonmaximum codes, variable-length codes, larger alphabets, or equivalence under any additional transformation not explicitly proved to preserve comma-freedom. A short 1973 paper titled *On maximal comma-free codes* could not be retrieved in full after open-access attempts and an institutional-access attempt; its existence is therefore a residual literature risk, although the accessible bibliographic record and later citations do not supply the equality census here. An unindexed historical machine enumeration is also possible.
