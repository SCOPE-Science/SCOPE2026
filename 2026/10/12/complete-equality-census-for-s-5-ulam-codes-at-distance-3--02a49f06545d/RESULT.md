# Complete equality census for \(S_5\) Ulam codes at distance \(3\)
## Finding
Let \(S_5\) be the permutations of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), and let \(d_U\) be Ulam distance. Among all codes \(C\subseteq S_5\) satisfying
\[
d_U(\pi,\sigma)\ge 3
\]
for all distinct \(\pi,\sigma\in C\), the classical optimum is
\[
\lvert C\rvert=4.
\]
There are exactly \(4020\) labeled maximum codes, of which exactly \(134\) contain the identity permutation \(12345\).

Every maximum code is equidistant: each of its six unordered codeword pairs has Ulam distance exactly \(3\).

Consider the natural isometry subgroup generated by arbitrary relabelings of the five symbols and by reversal of every one-line permutation. It has order \(240\). On the \(4020\) maximum codes it has exactly \(24\) orbits, with orbit-size distribution
\[
60^{\times 5},\qquad 120^{\times 7},\qquad 240^{\times 12}.
\]
Thus the corresponding stabilizer orders inside this explicit subgroup are \(4\), \(2\), and \(1\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
For permutations \(\pi,\sigma\in S_5\), Ulam distance is the minimum number of single-symbol translocations needed to transform one permutation into the other. Equivalently,
\[
d_U(\pi,\sigma)=5-\operatorname{LCS}(\pi,\sigma),
\]
where \(\operatorname{LCS}\) denotes the length of a longest common subsequence.

A labeled code is a literal subset of \(S_5\), before quotienting by symmetry. The orbit statement concerns only the explicit order-\(240\) subgroup above. Equality with the full metric-isometry group or with the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph is not asserted.

## Proof
Construct the compatibility graph \(G\) on \(S_5\), joining two distinct permutations exactly when their Ulam distance is at least \(3\). The desired codes are precisely cliques in \(G\).

The verifier reconstructs all \(7140\) unordered pair distances in two independent ways. The first computes longest common subsequences by dynamic programming and uses \(d_U=5-\operatorname{LCS}\). The second constructs the graph whose edges are single-symbol translocations and computes all-pairs shortest-path distances by breadth-first search. The two distance matrices agree on every pair.

The verifier then exhaustively enumerates every four-clique in \(G\), obtaining exactly \(4020\). For each four-clique it intersects the four common-neighbor sets and finds the intersection empty. Hence no five-clique exists, so the maximum is \(4\). Independently counting the enumerated maxima that contain \(12345\) gives \(134\). The transitive symbol-relabeling action supplies the incidence check
\[
4020\cdot 4=134\cdot 120.
\]

For each maximum, the verifier recomputes its six pairwise distances and finds the common profile
\[
(3,3,3,3,3,3).
\]

Finally, the verifier explicitly builds all \(240\) transformations obtained from a symbol relabeling and an optional position reversal, checks that each transformation preserves the maximum-code set, and partitions all \(4020\) maxima into orbits. The resulting orbit sizes are exactly those stated above.

## Relationship to prior work
Farnoud, Skachek, and Milenkovic introduced translocation rank codes in the Ulam metric and connected the metric with deletion/insertion correction for permutations. Göloğlu, Lember, Riet, and Skachek later singled out the exact parameter \(n=5,d=3\): their Example III.1 gives an integer-programming upper bound \(5\), states that the actual value is \(4\), and their computational table again records \(4\). Their paper reports maximum sizes and Singleton-optimal existence, not a census of all equality cases.

Later sphere-size work studies Ulam balls and perfect-code nonexistence, while later multiple-deletion work develops asymptotic bounds and constructions. The inspected later full text contains no occurrence of the equality count \(4020\) and does not state an \(S_5,d=3\) extremizer classification.

Targeted semantic searches using the exact parameter, translocation/deletion aliases, the count \(4020\), the normalized count \(134\), and the final orbit/equidistance invariants found no implication-equivalent published result.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact classification for one classical small Ulam-code parameter. It gives no formula for extremizers at other lengths or distances. The symmetry reduction is with respect to the stated natural order-\(240\) subgroup only. A residual priority risk remains for an obscure or unindexed historical enumeration not found in the checked literature and semantic searches.
