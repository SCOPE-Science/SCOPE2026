# Complete equality classification of optimal length-five binary single-asymmetric-error codes

## Finding
For a binary word \(x\in\{0,1\}^5\), write
\[
d_A(x,y)=\max\!\left(\left|\{i:x_i=1,\ y_i=0\}\right|,\left|\{i:x_i=0,\ y_i=1\}\right|\right).
\]
For every code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^5\) satisfying \(d_A(x,y)\ge 2\) for all distinct \(x,y\in C\),
\[
|C|\le 6.
\]
Exactly \(120\) labeled six-word codes attain equality. Under the order-\(240\) group generated by all coordinate permutations and global bit complementation, the equality cases form exactly three orbits of sizes \(60\), \(30\), and \(30\), with stabilizer orders \(4\), \(8\), and \(8\).

Representatives of the three orbits are
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1&=\{00000,00011,01100,01111,10101,11010\},\\
C_2&=\{00000,00011,01100,10101,11010,11111\},\\
C_3&=\{00001,00110,01011,10101,11000,11110\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The channel is the binary Z-channel with at most one asymmetric error, so a transmitted \(1\) may change to \(0\) and a transmitted \(0\) is unchanged. A code corrects one such error exactly when the one-error downward balls centered at distinct codewords are disjoint. For binary words this is equivalent to the pairwise condition \(d_A(x,y)\ge 2\).

The result concerns arbitrary, not necessarily linear, length-five codes. “Labeled” means that the five coordinates and the binary symbols are fixed. The orbit statement uses only coordinate permutations and simultaneous complementation of every bit in every codeword.

## Proof
Construct the graph \(G\) on all \(32\) binary words of length five, joining \(x\ne y\) when \(d_A(x,y)\ge2\). A valid code is exactly a clique of \(G\). The supplied verifier independently constructs the same graph from disjoint one-error Z-channel balls and checks equality of the two adjacency relations.

An exact Bron--Kerbosch enumeration then visits every maximal clique of \(G\). Its largest cliques have size \(6\), and there are exactly \(120\) such cliques. This proves both the upper bound and the complete labeled count.

For the equality classification, the verifier applies all \(5!\) coordinate permutations and optional global complementation to every maximum code. The resulting order-\(240\) action partitions the \(120\) maxima into three orbits of sizes \(60\), \(30\), and \(30\). Orbit--stabilizer therefore gives stabilizer orders \(4\), \(8\), and \(8\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the package directory. The verifier uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs all \(32\) words, compares the asymmetric-distance and error-ball definitions for every ordered word pair, enumerates every maximal clique, checks the maximum and the \(120\)-code census, and recomputes the symmetry orbits.

The replay output ends with:

`VERIFY_OK maximum=6 labeled_maxima=120 orbits=3 orbit_sizes=30,30,60 stabilizers=4,8,8`

## Relationship to prior work
The classical finite tables for binary asymmetric codes already give the optimum value \(6\) at length five and asymmetric distance \(2\); this value is also recorded in OEIS A010101. Weber, de Vroedt, and Boekee give finite optimal-size tables for short asymmetric codes, while Zhou, Jiang, and Bruck review the Z-channel model, the disjoint asymmetric-ball formulation, and earlier upper-bound literature.

The contribution here is not the numerical optimum \(6\). It is the complete equality layer: exactly \(120\) labeled optima and exactly three symmetry types, with the orbit and stabilizer data above. The inspected classical table and the later Z-channel treatment do not state this equality census.

## Limitations
This is a finite classification only for binary length five and correction of one asymmetric error. It does not classify length-six or longer codes, multiple-error codes, list-decoding variants, or probabilistic maximum-likelihood optima. The literature search cannot exclude an obscure unpublished or unindexed historical census; the most plausible residual source is the 1989 thesis by J. H. Weber, whose bibliographic existence is known but whose full text was not available in the inspected material.
