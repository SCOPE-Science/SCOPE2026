# Complete extremizer classification for Kendall-tau codes in the five-symbol symmetric group at distance six
## Finding
For permutation codes \(C\subseteq S_5\) with minimum Kendall-tau distance at least \(6\), the known optimum \(P(5,6)=5\) is attained by exactly 168 labeled maximum codes. Every maximum is equidistant: all ten unordered codeword pairs have Kendall-tau distance exactly \(6\).

Under the explicit 240-element isometry group generated by arbitrary relabeling of symbols and simultaneous reversal of one-line positions, these 168 maxima form exactly two orbits, of sizes 48 and 120. Their stabilizer orders are respectively 5 and 2. Exactly seven maximum codes contain the identity permutation; exactly two of those seven are subgroups. Both subgroup maxima are cyclic of order five, with generators, up to inversion,
\[
(1\,2\,4\,3\,5)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(1\,2\,5\,3\,4).
\]
The 48-element orbit is exactly the disjoint union of the 24 left cosets of each of these two subgroup maxima. The maximum code displayed in Appendix D of the 2016 exact-parameter paper lies in the 120-element orbit, not in the cyclic-coset orbit.

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient set is \(S_5\), represented in one-line notation. The Kendall-tau distance between two permutations is the minimum number of adjacent transpositions required to transform one into the other. A code is any subset \(C\subseteq S_5\), with no linearity or subgroup assumption. The minimum-distance requirement is \(d_K(\sigma,\pi)\ge 6\) for every distinct \(\sigma,\pi\in C\).

The stated group action is deliberately explicit. Symbol relabeling sends \(\pi\) to \(\lambda\circ\pi\), for arbitrary \(\lambda\in S_5\). Position reversal sends \((\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_5)\) to \((\pi_5,\ldots,\pi_1)\). The claim concerns the 240 transformations generated by these operations; it does not assert that this is the full isometry group of the metric.

## Proof
Construct the graph \(G\) on the 120 permutations in \(S_5\), joining two vertices exactly when their Kendall-tau distance is at least \(6\). A code with the required minimum distance is exactly a clique of \(G\). Direct reconstruction gives 2,940 edges.

An exhaustive Bron--Kerbosch enumeration of all maximal cliques gives the size histogram
\[
300\text{ of size }2,\quad 3120\text{ of size }3,\quad 3960\text{ of size }4,\quad 168\text{ of size }5.
\]
Hence the maximum clique size is \(5\), and there are exactly 168 labeled maxima. As an independent completeness check, an ordered depth-first enumeration of every 5-clique produces the same 168 sets, and every one has empty common neighborhood, so none extends to size \(6\).

For each of the 168 maxima, direct calculation of its ten internal distances gives the single value \(6\). The 240 stated transformations are then constructed explicitly and checked on every unordered pair of permutations to preserve Kendall-tau distance. Acting on the complete set of maxima yields two orbits, of sizes 48 and 120. Orbit--stabilizer therefore gives stabilizer orders 5 and 2.

Normalizing by requiring the identity permutation leaves exactly seven maxima. Direct closure and inverse checks show that exactly two are subgroups. Each has five elements and is therefore cyclic. The verifier identifies the displayed generators. Enumerating all left cosets of both subgroup maxima gives 24 cosets from each subgroup, with the two coset families disjoint, and their union is exactly the 48-element orbit.

Finally, the five permutations printed in Appendix D of the 2016 paper are entered literally and checked against the reconstructed census. They form a maximum code and lie in the 120-element orbit.

## Relationship to prior work
Vijayakumaran's 2016 paper proves the exact value \(P(5,6)=5\) and prints one attaining code. Its conclusion explicitly raises the question of algebraic structure of the optimal codes. The inspected article does not enumerate all maximum codes, classify their symmetry orbits, identify subgroup maxima, or state the cyclic-coset decomposition above.

Targeted searches for the exact parameter, extremizer counts, orbit classifications, algebraic structure, and equivalent formulations located work on bounds and constructions but no source implying this complete 168-code census or the two-orbit classification. Thus the new content is the extremizer classification, not the already known optimum \(P(5,6)=5\).

## Limitations
The result is finite and specific to \(S_5\) at minimum distance \(6\). It does not classify maximum Kendall-tau codes at other parameters and does not claim that the explicit 240-element symmetry group is the full metric-isometry group. Originality searches cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed finite enumeration that was not recovered.
