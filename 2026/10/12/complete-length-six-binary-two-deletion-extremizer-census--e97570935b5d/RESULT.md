# Complete length-six binary two-deletion extremizer census
## Finding
For binary length-six two-deletion-correcting codes, \(L_2(6,2)=4\). There are exactly 16 labeled optimal codes. Every optimal code contains \(\mathtt{000000}\) and \(\mathtt{111111}\), and its other two codewords have Hamming weight \(3\). Under the standard equivalence generated by reversing every codeword and globally complementing bits, the 16 optima form exactly seven equivalence classes with orbit sizes \(1,1,2,2,2,4,4\). Exactly three labeled optima are basic in the dominant-vector sense, and they form exactly two equivalence classes, represented by \(\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{000111},\mathtt{111000},\mathtt{111111}\}\) and \(\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{001110},\mathtt{110001},\mathtt{111111}\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
For a binary word \(u\in\{0,1\}^6\), let \(D_2(u)\) be the set of distinct length-four subsequences obtained by deleting exactly two coordinates. A code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) corrects two deletions when \(D_2(u)\cap D_2(v)=\varnothing\) for all distinct \(u,v\in C\). The quantity \(L_2(6,2)\) is the largest possible cardinality of such a code. A word \(u\) is 2-dominant when there is a distinct word \(v\) with \(D_2(v)\subseteq D_2(u)\); a code is basic when none of its codewords is 2-dominant. Equivalence is exactly the four-element action generated by simultaneous reversal of all codewords and simultaneous bit complement, following the standard deletion-code equivalence used by Kolev.

## Proof
Construct the compatibility graph on all \(64\) binary words of length \(6\), joining \(u\) and \(v\) exactly when their two-deletion descendant sets are disjoint. Direct generation gives \(522\) edges. Exhaustive Bron--Kerbosch enumeration gives exactly \(217\) maximal cliques of size \(2\), \(276\) of size \(3\), and \(16\) of size \(4\), with none larger. Hence \(L_2(6,2)=4\), and there are 16 labeled optima. An independent increasing-order enumeration of every four-clique reproduces exactly those 16 codes.

Intersecting the 16 optimal codes gives the universal core \(\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{111111}\}\). In every optimum, each remaining codeword has Hamming weight \(3\). Restricting to the 20 weight-three words compatible with both constant words leaves exactly 16 compatibility edges, and those edges reproduce the 16 optima one-for-one.

For each of the 64 words, all two-deletion descendant sets are then compared by inclusion. Exactly three optimal codes contain no 2-dominant codeword:
\[
\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{000111},\mathtt{111000},\mathtt{111111}\},
\quad
\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{001110},\mathtt{110001},\mathtt{111111}\},
\quad
\{\mathtt{000000},\mathtt{011100},\mathtt{100011},\mathtt{111111}\}.
\]
Applying reversal and complement to all 16 optima produces seven complete orbits of sizes \(1,1,2,2,2,4,4\). On the three basic optima, the same action has two orbits of sizes \(1\) and \(2\), giving the two representatives stated above.

## Verification
`verify.py` reconstructs all descendant sets in two independent ways: by choosing the four surviving positions and by iterating two single deletions. It then reconstructs the full compatibility graph, runs exhaustive maximal-clique enumeration, independently enumerates all four-cliques, recomputes the common core, dominance relation, and all reversal/complement orbits. ## Relationship to prior work
Kulkarni and Kiyavash formulate deletion-correcting codes as pairwise-disjoint deletion sets and develop general non-asymptotic upper bounds and a hypergraph matching formulation. Kolev defines \(L_2(n,t)\), states classification of distinct optimal codes as the second central finite-parameter problem once \(L_2(n,t)\) is known, introduces reversal/complement equivalence and dominant vectors, and reformulates the structural problem as finding inequivalent basic optimal codes. The present finding gives a complete equality-case census for the concrete parameter \(n=6,t=2\), rather than a new general upper bound or a new code construction.

A recent independent-set search study by Weindel and Heckel uses the same binary multiple-deletion graph formulation. Its graph statistics give 64 vertices and 1,494 conflict edges for length six and two deletions, so the complementary compatibility graph has \(2016-1494=522\) edges, matching the reconstruction here. Its reported two-deletion code-size table starts at length seven and does not provide the length-six equality-case census.

The abstract of Landjev and Haralambiev reports computer searches constructing multiple-deletion codes for small lengths and recursive upper bounds, but does not state an equality-case census or classification at this parameter. That full text was not available during this comparison, so it remains a residual literature risk rather than positive coverage.

## Limitations
The claim is only for binary words of length \(6\) with exactly two deletions. The equivalence classification is for the standard four-element reversal/complement action; no assertion is made that this is the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph. Exhaustive finite enumeration proves only this finite parameter and does not imply a formula for other lengths or deletion counts. An obscure or unindexed earlier equality-case census could remain undiscovered.
