# Complete Pauli-magnitude spectrum of a single-edge-defective dense CCZ state

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), prepare the complete 3-uniform hypergraph state on \(n\) qubits but omit precisely one controlled-controlled-\(Z\) gate. The full distribution of the magnitudes of its \(4^n\) Pauli expectation values is given below by an exact five-term rank spectrum. All choices of the omitted triple are unitarily equivalent by permutations.

For \(a\in\mathbb F_2^n\), let \(r(a)\) be the rank over \(\mathbb F_2\) of the alternating bilinear form associated with the phase difference generated by translation by \(a\), and let \(N_r=|\{a:r(a)=r\}|\). The following are *multiplicity contributions*: when two displayed ranks coincide for small \(n\), their contributions must be added.

For **even** \(n\ge4\), write \(z=2^{n-4}\). The rank--count pairs are
\[
(r,N_r)\in\bigl\{(0,1),(2,z+6),(4,7z-7),(n-2,7z),(n-4,z)\bigr\}.
\]
For **odd** \(n\ge5\), they are
\[
(r,N_r)\in\bigl\{(0,1),(2,z+5),(4,7z-6),(n-1,2^{n-2}),(n-3,2^{n-2})\bigr\}.
\]
For each contribution \((r,N_r)\), exactly \(N_r2^r\) Pauli expectations have magnitude \(2^{-r/2}\), and all remaining Pauli expectations vanish. Consequently the *entire family* of Pauli--Liouville moments, for every real \(\alpha>0\), is
\[
m_\alpha=2^{-n}\sum_r N_r 2^{(1-\alpha)r},
\qquad M_\alpha=\frac{\log_2m_\alpha}{1-\alpha}\quad(\alpha\ne1).
\]
At order two this specializes to
\[
m_2=\begin{cases}11/256+77/2^{n+4},&n\text{ even},\\11/256+70/2^{n+4},&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
For every fixed \(\alpha>1\), the limiting moment is
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}m_\alpha=\frac{2^{2(1-\alpha)}+7\cdot2^{4(1-\alpha)}}{16}.
\]
In particular the missing-gate family has limiting second stabilizer Rényi entropy \(\log_2(256/11)\), whereas the intact complete 3-uniform hypergraph has limiting value \(3\). The order-two scalar for a *single* deletion is, in principle, also extractable from the previously published random-edge ensemble moment by differentiation; the claim of new information here is the **complete rank/magnitude distribution and its all-order consequences**, not the scalar order-two moment considered alone.

## Assumptions and scope
The input is the exact \(n\)-qubit pure state
\[
|\psi_n^-\rangle=2^{-n/2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_2^n}(-1)^{f_n(x)}|x\rangle,
\qquad f_n(x)=\binom{|x|}{3}+x_1x_2x_3\pmod2.
\]
Here \(\binom{|x|}{3}\) is the number of triples of occupied qubits, taken modulo two; adding the last term cancels the distinguished triple \(\{1,2,3\}\). The Paulis are \(P_{a,b}=i^{a\cdot b}X^aZ^b\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb F_2^n\). The normalization is \(m_\alpha=2^{-n}\sum_{a,b}|\langle P_{a,b}\rangle|^{2\alpha}\), with the usual convention that zero contributions vanish for \(\alpha>0\). No circuit noise, losses, approximations, or gate implementation imperfections other than omission of exactly one hyperedge are included.

## Proof
**1. Binary quadratic phase derivative.** Fix \(a\in\mathbb F_2^n\). The derivative \(q_a(x)=f_n(x+a)+f_n(x)\) is a quadratic Boolean polynomial. Let \(B_a\) be its alternating polar matrix. With \(s=\sum_i a_i\pmod2\), its entries for distinct \(i,j\) are
\[
(B_a)_{ij}=s+a_i+a_j+{\bf1}_{\{i,j\}\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}a_{\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i,j\}},
\qquad (B_a)_{ii}=0,
\]
where the final indexed term is the unique remaining coordinate of the distinguished triple. The character sums \(2^{-n}\sum_x(-1)^{q_a(x)+b\cdot x}\) are quadratic Walsh transforms. Squaring a Walsh transform, summing over a translated variable, and using orthogonality shows that precisely \(2^{r(a)}\) of the \(b\)'s yield a nonzero transform, each of absolute value \(2^{-r(a)/2}\). This establishes the magnitude rule and moment formula once the ranks are counted.

**2. Four-group rank reduction.** Write \(u=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\), \(v=(a_4,\dots,a_n)\), \(h=|u|\), \(w=|v|\), \(m=n-3\), \(\rho=w\pmod2\), and \(s=h+w\pmod2\). Partition the coordinates into four possibly empty groups \(T_0,T_1,O_0,O_1\), according to whether the coordinate lies in the distinguished triple (\(T\)) or outside (\(O\)), and whether the corresponding bit of \(a\) is zero or one. Their sizes are \(3-h,h,m-w,w\). Within the two \(T\)-groups and between them, every off-diagonal entry of \(B_a\) is \(\rho\). Between two outside groups the entry is \(s+\epsilon+\epsilon'\), where \(\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{0,1\}\) are their bit labels; within an outside group it is \(s\). Across a \(T_\epsilon\) and \(O_{\epsilon'}\) group it is \(s+\epsilon+\epsilon'\). All these expressions are in \(\mathbb F_2\).

To calculate the kernel without assuming the group sizes are positive, omit empty groups. For a nonempty group \(G\) of size \(k_G\), write \(Y_G=\sum_{i\in G}y_i\), denote its common off-diagonal within-group coefficient by \(d_G\in\{\rho,s\}\), and write the between-group coefficient as \(c_{GH}\). The kernel equation on any coordinate in \(G\) is
\[
d_Gy_i=d_GY_G+\sum_{H\ne G}c_{GH}Y_H.
\]
If \(d_G=0\), it imposes one equation \(\sum_{H\ne G}c_{GH}Y_H=0\) and leaves \(k_G-1\) independent within-group fluctuations. If \(d_G=1\), all \(y_i\) in the group coincide. When \(k_G\) is even this forces \(Y_G=0\); when \(k_G\) is odd the group condition is again \(\sum_{H\ne G}c_{GH}Y_H=0\). Therefore form a matrix \(K\) with one row/column per nonempty group: its \(G\)-row is the corresponding between-group coefficient row with zero diagonal, except if \(d_G=1\) and \(k_G\) is even, in which case its row has a single diagonal one. Exactly
\[
\dim\ker B_a=\sum_{G:d_G=0}(k_G-1)+\dim\ker K.
\]
This is a rank calculation using at most four group variables, with all coefficients explicitly specified above; no growing-size matrix rank or unverified global enumeration is necessary.

**3. Explicit small-matrix evaluation and counting.** Substituting the four sizes into the displayed kernel formula and eliminating at most four columns gives the following exhaustive cases. For odd \(n\):

| \(h\) | \(w\) and \(r(a)\) |
|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(w=0:0\); positive even \(w:2\); odd \(w:n-1\) |
| \(1\) | even \(w:n-3\); odd \(w:4\) |
| \(2\) | even \(w\in\{0,m\}:2\); other even \(w:4\); odd \(w:n-1\) |
| \(3\) | even \(w:n-3\); odd \(w:4\) |

For even \(n\):

| \(h\) | \(w\) and \(r(a)\) |
|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(w=0:0\); positive even \(w:2\); odd \(w:n-2\) |
| \(1\) | even \(w:n-2\); odd \(w<m:4\); \(w=m:2\) |
| \(2\) | \(w=0:2\); positive even \(w:4\); odd \(w:n-2\) |
| \(3\) | even \(w:n-4\); odd \(w<m:4\); \(w=m:2\) |

Here \(0\le h\le3\), \(0\le w\le m\), so unattainable parity/end-point combinations are simply absent. The number of shifts with any fixed \((h,w)\) is \(\binom3h\binom mw\). Summing those counts, separately over even and odd \(w\), gives the five rank--count contributions in the Finding section. Their total is \(2^n\) in both parity classes, including coincident small-size ranks.

**4. Derived moments and limiting effect.** Multiplying the rank contributions by \(2^{(1-\alpha)r}\) gives all positive-order moments. For \(\alpha=2\), simplification yields the parity-dependent finite formula above. For any fixed \(\alpha>1\), the contributions at ranks proportional to \(n\) tend to zero after division by \(2^n\), while the fixed rank-two and rank-four multiplicities approach the fractions \(1/16\) and \(7/16\); the stated limit follows. The published intact complete-state expression is \(m_2^{\mathrm{full}}=1/8+7/2^{n+1}\) for even \(n\) and \(1/8+7/2^{n+2}\) for odd \(n\). Hence
\[
m_2^{\mathrm{full}}-m_2^{\mathrm{defect}}=\frac{21}{256}\begin{cases}1-2^{4-n},&n\text{ even},\\1-2^{5-n},&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\]
which is zero at \(n=4,5\) and positive for every \(n\ge6\), proving the strict increase in order-two magic after the missing gate for \(n\ge6\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` included with this result uses Python standard-library integer and rational arithmetic. It independently checks the four-group kernel formula against direct binary-matrix elimination for *every* shift at each \(4\le n\le11\), checks the symbolic rank table through \(n=30\), and independently reconstructs the \(\alpha=2\) moment by direct Boolean phase correlations and Walsh transforms for \(4\le n\le8\). Run `python verify.py` to obtain `VERIFY_OK`. The analytic four-group reduction and counting, rather than these finite tests, prove the all-size assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Kagamihara and Tsuchiya, *Stabilizer Rényi entropy of 3-uniform hypergraph states*, establish the general \(\mathbb F_2\)-rank representation of all Pauli--Liouville moments (Theorem 1) and solve particular one-dimensional examples. Our proof credits and uses their rank correspondence; the new part is the exact all-size rank histogram for a complete hypergraph with exactly one omitted triple.

Chen, Yan, and Zhou, *Magic of quantum hypergraph states*, already calculate the intact complete-state second and half-order moments (Proposition 5). Their Theorem 5 gives an all-size formula for the order-two moment averaged over independently retained triples with arbitrary retention probability. Differentiating that **published** average at full retention is enough to recover the **scalar** order-two moment of any single-edge-deleted complete state, by permutation symmetry. Consequently the scalar formula above is explicitly *not* claimed as an independently original theorem. That average does not specify the binary-rank or Pauli-magnitude histogram of the defective state, which resolves all orders \(\alpha>0\) and the full spectral distribution here. General circuit fault tolerance or random-hypergraph entanglement work does not determine this histogram.

## Limitations
The state model has one exact omitted CCZ gate and no other faults; no robustness to coherent overrotation, stochastic noise, measurement errors, or multiple defects is proved. The finding classifies Pauli expectation magnitudes but not their signs, and does not establish a computational speed-up from this family. The finite checker is not a universal proof. The result has received self-review only; no independent audit or experimental validation is claimed.
