# Complete radius-one ball intersection profile in the looped graph node metric

## Finding
For every prime power \(q\ge 2\), every integer \(n\ge 4\), and all centers \(a,b\) in the \(q\)-ary edge-label space of the complete undirected graph with one loop at every vertex, the size of the intersection of their closed radius-one node-metric balls is determined exactly by the support graph of \(d=b-a\).

Write \(F=\operatorname{supp}(d)\), let \(\tau(F)\) be the minimum size of a vertex set meeting every supported edge (a loop must be met at its vertex), and write \(I(d)=|B_1(a)\cap B_1(b)|\). Set \(Q=q^n\).

* If \(F\) is empty, then
  \[I(d)=1+n(Q-1)-\binom n2(q-1).\]
* If \(\tau(F)\ge3\), then \(I(d)=0\).
* If \(\tau(F)=1\), then
  \[I(d)=\begin{cases}2Q-q,&F\text{ is one nonloop edge},\\Q+2(q-1),&F\text{ is two distinct nonloop edges with a common endpoint},\\Q,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\]
* If \(\tau(F)=2\), let \(\mathscr C(F)\) be the collection of all **unordered two-element vertex covers** of \(F\). Put \(c=|\mathscr C(F)|\), and let \(h\) count unordered distinct pairs \(\{S,T\}\subseteq\mathscr C(F)\) satisfying \(|S\cap T|=1\). Put \(m=1\) precisely if \(F\) consists of two disjoint nonloop edges, and \(m=0\) otherwise. Then
  \[\boxed{I(d)=2qc-2h+2m.}\]
In particular, the exceptional two-edge matching has \(I(d)=8q-6\). The formulas apply to every assignment of nonzero field values on the specified supports: the overlap is support-determined, not merely node-distance-determined.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(K_n^{\circ}\) have vertices \([n]\), one undirected coordinate \(\{i,j\}\) for each \(i<j\), and one loop coordinate \(\{i,i\}\) for each \(i\). Its edge-label space is \(\mathcal G=\mathbb F_q^{n(n+1)/2}\). For \(x\in\mathcal G\), its support graph includes precisely the nonzero-labeled coordinates. For a vertex set \(S\), let \(\operatorname{St}(S)\) denote all coordinates incident to \(S\), and let \(V_S\) be the linear subspace supported on \(\operatorname{St}(S)\). The node weight is \(\operatorname{wt}_N(x)=\tau(\operatorname{supp}(x))\), with \(\tau(\varnothing)=0\). Define \(d_N(x,y)=\operatorname{wt}_N(x-y)\), and \(B_1(x)=\{y:d_N(x,y)\le1\}\). Write \(V_i=V_{\{i\}}\); each \(V_i\) has dimension \(n\) and \(B_1(0)=\bigcup_i V_i\). All cardinalities count distinct graph words, not representations by subspaces.

## Proof
**Translation and ball volume.** Translation permits \(a=0\), \(b=d\). A coordinatewise nonzero scaling is an additive automorphism preserving each supported-edge set and hence every node distance; therefore the answer depends only on \(F\). For distinct \(i,j\), \(V_i\cap V_j\) is the one-dimensional line supported on their common nonloop edge. A nonzero vector lies in two distinct star spaces only if it is supported on exactly one nonloop edge, and it cannot lie in three distinct star spaces. The zero vector lies in all \(n\) spaces. Counting gives \(|B_1(0)|=1+n(q^n-1)-\binom n2(q-1)\). Since \(d_N\) is a metric, \(B_1(0)\cap B_1(d)=\varnothing\) when \(\tau(F)>2\).

**Distance one.** Suppose \(F\) has a vertex cover of size one. If \(F\) is one nonloop edge \(\{u,v\}\), then \(V_u\cup V_v\subseteq B_1(0)\cap B_1(d)\); no additional point can lie in the intersection because each ordered two-star decomposition of \(d\) has at least one of these two vertices. Its size is \(2q^n-q\). Otherwise the singleton cover is a unique vertex \(v\). Certainly \(V_v\) belongs to both balls. An intersection point outside \(V_v\) would yield \(x\in V_i\), \(d-x\in V_j\) with \(i,j\ne v\). Thus \(\{i,j\}\) must meet every edge of \(F\). If there is a loop in \(F\), or at least three distinct nonloop edges incident with \(v\), this is impossible. The remaining case is exactly two different nonloop edges \(\{v,u\},\{v,w\}\). The only off-\(V_v\) intersections lie in the two affine lines \(V_u\cap(d+V_w)\) and \(V_w\cap(d+V_u)\). Each line has \(q\) points, exactly one in \(V_v\), and their off-\(V_v\) portions are disjoint. They add \(2(q-1)\) points. All other distance-one configurations give exactly \(V_v\).

**Distance two: affine-line decomposition.** Assume \(\tau(F)=2\). If \(x\in V_i\cap(d+V_j)\), then \(\{i,j\}\) covers \(F\) and necessarily \(i\ne j\). Conversely, every ordered pair \((i,j)\) with \(\{i,j\}\in\mathscr C(F)\) yields a nonempty affine line
\[L_{ij}=V_i\cap(d+V_j),\qquad |L_{ij}|=q,\]
whose direction is the single coordinate \(\{i,j\}\). Existence follows by assigning each nonzero edge label of \(d\) to an endpoint from the covering pair. Therefore
\[B_1(0)\cap B_1(d)=\bigcup_{\{i,j\}\in\mathscr C(F)}(L_{ij}\cup L_{ji}),\]
a union of \(2c\) affine lines.

Consider two distinct lines \(L_{ij},L_{k\ell}\). If \(i=k\) but \(j\ne\ell\), their common point is uniquely specified by the equation \(d-x\in V_j\cap V_{\ell}\): outside the star of \(i\), all of \(d\) must be on the nonloop edge \(\{j,\ell\}\), whose label is necessarily nonzero since \(\tau(F)=2\). The same one-point intersection holds when \(j=\ell\) but \(i\ne k\). If both \(i\ne k\) and \(j\ne\ell\), an intersection would require \(x\) supported on \(\{i,k\}\), and \(d-x\) supported on \(\{j,\ell\}\). Since \(\tau(F)=2\), both edges must be nonzero, distinct, and disjoint. Therefore this situation can occur only when \(F\) consists of two disjoint nonloop edges.

**Generic two-cover pattern.** Suppose \(F\) is not that matching. For each pair of different unordered two-covers sharing one vertex, there are exactly two intersecting line pairs, namely the ordered versions with the common vertex in the same position (first or second). Every other line pair is disjoint. Moreover, no three lines pass through the same point: if three ordered-pair indices pairwise agree in their first or second component, they must all agree in the first or all agree in the second component. Three distinct two-covers sharing a fixed vertex \(i\) would force the support outside its star to lie simultaneously on three distinct edges of the form \(\{j,k\}\), which is impossible unless \(F\) itself is covered by \(i\). This contradicts \(\tau(F)=2\). Thus exactly \(2h\) distinct pairwise intersection points are subtracted from the \(2cq\) line elements, giving \(I(d)=2cq-2h\).

**Exceptional matching.** Now let \(F=\{\{u,v\},\{w,z\}\}\) with four distinct vertices and both labels nonzero. There are four two-covers, selecting one vertex from each edge, hence eight oriented lines. Four lines consist of vectors with the prescribed \(\{u,v\}\)-coordinate and a free cross-edge coordinate, all meeting at the vector supported just on \(\{u,v\}\). The other four lines similarly meet at the vector supported just on \(\{w,z\}\). The two groups are disjoint because their respective supported matching-edge labels differ. Each group has \(1+4(q-1)\) elements, yielding \(8q-6\). In this case \(c=4\), \(h=4\), so \(8q-6=2qc-2h+2\), as required. This completes all possibilities.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `verify.py` constructs the zero-centered one-ball independently as a union of finite star spaces, enumerates the intersection against translated copies, and compares its size with the claimed formulas. It checks every support pattern for \((n,q)=(3,2),(4,2),(4,3),(4,4),(5,2)\), and additional deterministic patterns for \((5,3)\) and \((6,2)\), totaling \(36504\) checks. Its \(q=4\) implementation uses the additive group of \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(q=3\) uses addition modulo three. A coordinatewise scaling justifies using one unit-valued difference vector per support pattern. Execute from this package's directory with `python3 verify.py`; the expected final message is `VERIFY_OK 36504`. These tests are finite cross-checks, not a proof for arbitrary \(n,q\); the line-arrangement argument above supplies the uniform proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Yohananov and Yaakobi introduced graph erasure codes and the relevant node-failure geometry in *Codes for Graph Erasures* (2017); Yohananov, Efron and Yaakobi subsequently developed double- and triple-node-erasure-correcting constructions. Zabokritskiy's *Binary Multiple-Node-Erasure-Correcting Codes over Complete Graphs: Constructions, q-Ary Metric Balls, and Duality* (first public 2026-09-01), especially Sections 5.2--5.3 and Corollary 26, proves the radius-one **single-ball** volume displayed above and intersections of unshifted star subspaces. The present result instead computes intersections of balls at **different centers**, which are unions of **affinely shifted** star intersections; those are not determined by two-unshifted-star intersection dimensions alone. It furnishes a complete second-order intersection profile, including an exceptional matching correction.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the looped undirected complete-graph node metric, not simple graphs, directed graphs, general crisscross covers, or balls of radii greater than one. It does not assert optimal code cardinalities, new packing inequalities, or any asymptotic code construction. The value of a ball intersection depends on detailed support patterns, not solely on \(d_N(a,b)\); indeed the distance-two overlap can be \(O(q)\), while distance-one overlaps are \(\Theta(q^n)\). A prior unindexed equivalent result cannot be excluded by literature search alone.
