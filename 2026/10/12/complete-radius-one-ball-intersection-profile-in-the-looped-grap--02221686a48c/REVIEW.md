# Scientific review of the complete radius-one node-ball intersection profile

**Correctness (PASS).** Translation invariance and supported-edge scaling reduce the question to difference supports. The proof classifies all affine one-star intersections, shows that each ordered two-cover gives a nonempty \(q\)-element affine line, counts all pairwise intersections, rules out triple intersections except for the separately treated matching pattern, and obtains the exact correction \(+2\). The singleton-cover and zero cases are handled separately. The complete support-pattern verifications and sampled larger parameters from `verify.py` agree, totaling 36,504 finite tests; these tests are not substituted for the uniform proof.

**Originality (PASS with residual risk).** Zabokritskiy, arXiv:2609.01474v2, Section 5.2, Theorem 24, Section 5.3 and Corollary 26 computes unshifted star-subspace intersections and single-center node ball sizes, not the affine intersections \(B_1(a)\cap B_1(b)\) at distinct centers. The 2017 and 2018/2020 graph-erasure papers introduce the metric and give erasure-correcting code constructions, not this second-order geometry. Searches of published mathematical records using node-metric intersection, vertex-cover metric, aliases such as graph-cover metric, ball intersection and affine-star arrangements did not identify a stronger covering result; similar results on ordinary graph metrics and codimension-two LRC incidence do not apply. This is an evidence-based comparison rather than a guarantee of global novelty; unindexed equivalent work remains a risk.

**Value (PASS).** The calculation completes a natural quantitative next step after the recent exact radius-one volume and addresses an intrinsic obstruction to sphere-intersection and distance-distribution arguments in graph codes. It separates center pairs of the same node distance by their support geometry and shows that distance-two overlaps stay bounded independently of graph order, apart from the finite-field factor, while distance-one overlaps scale like \(q^n\). The two-edge matching correction is a genuine structural exception, not a parameter relabeling or arbitrary finite case.

**Scope and limits.** No claim is made about multi-radius overlaps, optimal code sizes, or independent mathematical certification. The binary/quaternary/ternary computations test finite instances only.
