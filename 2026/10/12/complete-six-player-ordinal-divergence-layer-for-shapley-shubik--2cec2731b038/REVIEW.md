# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Independently generated every nonempty antichain of the 63 nonempty coalitions on six labeled players, i.e. all M(6)-2=7,828,352 nontrivial monotone games; repeated n=1..5 checks match Dedekind totals. Swings are counted exactly and each Shapley–Shubik score uses integer numerator sum |S|!(5-|S|)! with denominator 720. Exactly 2,540,340 six-player games differ in weak player rankings: zero strict reversals or Banzhaf-only splits, all are Shapley splits of a Banzhaf tie and all divergent games have no dummies. Two independent properness predicates agree; proper games=1,422,563 and proper divergences=573,960. The witness {ABC,ABDE,ADEF,BDEF} gives Banzhaf (10,10,6,6,6,4) and Shapley numerators (168,168,96,108,108,72). This is genuinely exhaustive for n<=6 only.

## originality

PASS

Tomiyama 1987 already proves that the two power notions need not be ordinally equivalent by six players, and Resultary had an earlier first-six-player witness; those threshold and existence facts are credited. Diffo Lambo–Moulen 2002 and Carreras–Freixas 2008 establish equality for swap-robust and weakly-linear subclasses, not the full six-player complement. The exact counts 2,540,340 and 573,960 and structural uniform one-way-tie-refinement theorem are extra. Tomiyama original full text remains inaccessible (Oxford worker unavailable), so historical unindexed tables are a substantial residual risk.

## value

PASS

The very first size exhibiting disagreement between two central power measures is a natural finite cutoff. An exact census of all 7.8 million simple games, including proper restricted incidence and universal no-reversal direction, explains the form, frequency and dummy behavior of the first possible ordinal mismatch. This is substantially richer than one witness and useful for small-game power-index theory.
