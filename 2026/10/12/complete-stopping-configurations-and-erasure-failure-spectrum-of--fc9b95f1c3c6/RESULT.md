# Complete stopping configurations and erasure-failure spectrum of the order-three projective plane

## Finding

For the point–line incidence design \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\), use its 13-by-13 binary incidence matrix \(H\), with **columns indexed by projective lines** and **rows indexed by projective points**. A subset \(S\) of columns is a *stopping set* if no row of \(H\) has exactly one nonzero entry in those columns; the empty set is included. The complete stopping-set weight enumerator is
\[
E(z)=1+78z^{6}+117z^{8}+247z^{9}+234z^{10}+78z^{11}+13z^{12}+z^{13}.
\]
There are exactly 769 stopping sets (768 nonempty), belonging to **nine orbits** under the projective collineation group of order 5616. Each nonempty stopping set contains one of the 78 six-column minimum stopping sets. Thus the stopping distance is six, as was previously known, and its complete all-size classification follows from the geometric characterization below.

For iterative single-erasure peeling with precisely this matrix \(H\), let \(B_k\) count erasure patterns of size \(k\) on which peeling terminates with at least one unknown variable. Then
\[
B(z)=78z^{6}+546z^{7}+1287z^{8}+715z^{9}+286z^{10}+78z^{11}+13z^{12}+z^{13}.
\]
In particular, exactly 3004 of the 8192 possible erasure patterns cause a residual failure. With independent erasure probability \(p\), the exact block-failure probability for peeling is \(\sum_{k=0}^{13}B_k p^{k}(1-p)^{13-k}\). At \(p=1/2\) this is \(751/2048\).

## Assumptions and scope

The projective plane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\) has 13 points and 13 lines, with four points on each line and four lines through each point. The binary parity-check matrix is the **full geometric point–line incidence matrix**, with no deleted or additional parity checks. Stopping-set polynomials depend on this chosen matrix, even for the same underlying binary code. The failure polynomial concerns synchronous or asynchronous peeling to a fixed point on a binary erasure channel, not maximum-likelihood decoding. No result for planes of order other than three is claimed.

In the dual plane, regard the 13 columns (original lines) as 13 dual points. Write \(T\) for the complement of a stopping set \(S\) in the dual point set. Since every dual line contains four points, the stopping condition is exactly
\[
\text{no dual line meets }T\text{ in exactly three points}.
\]
A *quadrangle* is a four-point set with no three collinear; a *triangle* is a noncollinear three-point set. This convention fixes all geometric classifications.

## Proof

**Lemma 1 (no five-point arcs).** An arc is a point set with no three collinear. Suppose five arc points existed. Every line through an arc point meets a second arc point, since there are four other arc points and exactly four lines through a given point. Hence no line contains exactly one arc point. At any external point, the four lines through it partition the five arc points into sets of sizes zero or two, contradicting parity. Thus every arc has at most four points.

**Lemma 2 (the complement classification).** Let \(T\) avoid three-point line intersections. If it contains no whole four-point line, it is an arc, hence it has zero through four points by Lemma 1. Thus it is empty, one point, two points, a triangle, or a quadrangle.

Otherwise take a whole line \(L\subseteq T\). Remove \(L\) to obtain the affine plane \(\mathrm{AG}(2,3)\), and let \(U=T\setminus L\). An affine line \(\ell\) is completed to a projective line by one point of \(L\). The forbidden three-point intersection condition is therefore equivalent to \(|\ell\cap U|\ne2\) for every affine line \(\ell\). Every pair of points of \(U\) consequently forces the third point of their affine line into \(U\). The affine closure generated by zero points, one point, a pair, or a noncollinear triple has size zero, one, three, or nine, respectively. Thus \(U\) is empty, one affine point, one full affine line, or all nine affine points. Accordingly \(T\) is a line, a line plus an external point, a union of two distinct projective lines, or the full thirteen-point plane. Conversely, each of the nine listed types avoids a three-point intersection: an arc meets lines in at most two points, and the affine-closure condition verifies all types containing a full line.

**Counting and orbits.** The arc cases occur with multiplicities \(1,13,\binom{13}{2}=78,\binom{13}{3}-13\binom{4}{3}=234,234\). For the quadrangle count, start with any noncollinear triangle; its three sides contain nine distinct points, leaving four possible fourth points. Every quadrangle is counted four times, so its number is \(234\cdot4/4=234\). The remaining cases—one whole line, one line plus one external point, two lines, and the whole plane—occur \(13,13\cdot9=117,\binom{13}{2}=78,1\) times. Translating \(|S|=13-|T|\) gives the stated \(E(z)\). The projective group acts transitively on points, pairs, noncollinear triangles, quadrangles (projective frames), lines, nonincident point–line pairs, and unordered pairs of distinct lines. Thus these nine types are precisely the nine orbits. The standalone verifier constructs all 5616 projectivities and independently checks the orbit partition.

**Minimum containment.** Every dual complement \(T\) of a nonempty stopping set lies in the union of two distinct lines. For an arc of size at most four, partition it into two subsets of size at most two and span each by a line. For the complement types containing one full line, the external portion has size zero, one, or three, so it lies on some second line. The union-of-two-lines case is immediate. In the full-plane case \(S\) is empty and is excluded. Conversely the complements of two-line unions are stopping sets of size \(13-7=6\). These are exactly 78 distinct minimum sets, and each is contained in every stopping set with the corresponding covered dual complement.

**Peeling failure.** Peeling terminates with an undecoded variable precisely when the residual erasure set is a nonempty stopping set. By the minimum-containment result, a pattern \(E\) fails exactly when its dual complement \(T\) is contained in a union of two distinct projective lines. If \(|E|\le5\), then \(|T|\ge8\), exceeding the seven-point capacity of such a union: there are no failures. For \(|E|=6\), the complement is exactly a two-line union, giving 78 patterns. For \(|E|=7\), a six-point complement must be a two-line union with one point omitted; there are \(\binom{13}{2}\cdot7=546\) choices. These are distinct: the two lines are recovered as the only lines meeting the six points in at least three points (their intersection is either omitted, giving two three-secants, or retained, giving one four-secant and one three-secant). For \(|E|=8\), its five-point complement contains a collinear triple by Lemma 1, and its other two points lie on another line; all \(\binom{13}{5}=1287\) patterns fail. For \(|E|\ge9\), any complement of at most four points can be partitioned into two pairs, each lying on a line; all \(\binom{13}{|E|}\) patterns fail. This gives the stated \(B(z)\).

## Verification

Two standalone implementations are supplied. Run `python3 verify.py`, then `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_independent.cpp -o verifier` and `./verifier`. Both independently construct the projective points from nonzero vectors in \(\mathbf F_3^3\), normalize by the first nonzero coordinate, form all thirteen dual line-incidence masks, and inspect all \(2^{13}=8192\) column subsets. The Python implementation additionally checks the forbidden-three criterion, the 78 minimum obstructions, peeling-vs-covering equivalence on every mask, and the projective group of order 5616. `orbits.csv` records one representative per projective orbit using the same lexicographic normalized point indexing. Every entry is checked against the generated classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Kashyap and Vardy (2003) introduced stopping sets for combinatorial designs, established the stopping distance six of the projective plane of order three, and discussed the full stopping-set weight enumerator as a more demanding quantity. Their inspected full text computes the enumerator for the Fano plane and partial enumerators for order four, not the order-three full polynomial or the decoder failure spectrum. Napolitano (2020) explicitly identifies the size-six configurations in order three as duals of symmetric differences of two lines and studies small stopping sets more generally. The size-six structure and minimum distance are therefore **not** asserted as original. The present contribution is the all-cardinality dual-complement classification, nine projective orbits, exact stopping-set polynomial, and exact peeling-failure polynomial together with a geometric covering criterion. The latter is stronger than knowledge of the stopping distance or minimal configurations alone.

## Limitations

This is a fixed small incidence design, not an asymptotic statement or a theorem for arbitrary projective planes. The polynomial for failed iterative decoding uses precisely the specified thirteen check rows; adding redundant parity checks may change it. Published small-stopping-set work has overlapping minimum-size conclusions. The complete text of Napolitano's closely related paper and Xia–Hu's finite-plane code paper could not be inspected, leaving a residual literature-priority risk for exact enumerations despite targeted searches and the full-text inspection of Kashyap–Vardy. No independent mathematical review is claimed.
