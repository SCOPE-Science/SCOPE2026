# Complete symmetry classification for consecutive pentagonal triples

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), put \(P(n)=n(3n-1)/2\) and let
\[
S_n=\langle P(n),P(n+1),P(n+2)\rangle
\]
be the additive numerical semigroup generated by three consecutive ordinary pentagonal numbers. Then
\[
\boxed{S_n\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in\{2,3,5,40\}.}
\]
In particular, \(40\) is the **last** symmetric index: all \(n\ge41\) are nonsymmetric. The Frobenius numbers at these four indices are respectively \(43,133,1699,258529\); the respective genera are \(22,67,850,129265\). Thus the last exceptional triple is
\[
\langle2380,2501,2625\rangle,
\]
whose scaled-generator relation is \(2501=7\cdot68+27\cdot75\), with \(2380=35\cdot68\) and \(2625=35\cdot75\).

## Assumptions and scope
The index \(n\) is an arbitrary integer at least \(2\). The notation \(\langle a,b,c\rangle\) means all nonnegative integer linear combinations. A numerical semigroup \(S\) of gcd \(1\) has Frobenius number \(F(S)\), its largest omitted nonnegative integer, and genus \(g(S)\), the number of omissions. It is *symmetric* if for every integer \(z\), exactly one of \(z\in S\) and \(F(S)-z\in S\) holds; equivalently \(2g(S)=F(S)+1\). All assertions are unconditional and concern precisely these three consecutive pentagonal generators.

## Proof
Write \(A=P(n)\), \(B=P(n+1)\), \(C=P(n+2)\). Direct expansion gives
\[
B-A=3n+1,\qquad C-B=3n+4,\qquad C-A=6n+5,
\]
with \(A<B<C\). The gcd identities needed below are
\[
\gcd(A,B)=\begin{cases}2&n\equiv3\pmod4,\\1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad
\gcd(B,C)=\begin{cases}2&n\equiv2\pmod4,\\1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\gcd(A,C)=5^{[5\mid n]}7^{[n\equiv5\pmod7]}\ \in\{1,5,7,35\}.
\]
Here \([Q]\) is \(1\) when \(Q\) holds and \(0\) otherwise. To prove the adjacent gcd assertions, any common odd prime divisor of \(A\) and \(B\) would divide both \(3n+1\) and either \(n\) or \(3n-1\), impossible. The same argument applies to \(B,C\). The parity pattern of the pentagonal numbers is odd, odd, even, even as the index runs through residues \(1,2,3,0\pmod4\); the difference of the two even adjacent terms is \(2\pmod4\), establishing the exact gcd. For the outer pair, \(C-A=6n+5\) is odd, and
\[
\gcd(n,3n-1)=1,\quad \gcd(n,6n+5)=\gcd(n,5),\quad
\gcd(3n-1,6n+5)=\gcd(3n-1,7).
\]
Hence \(\gcd(A,C)\mid35\). Modulo \(5\) the common divisor occurs precisely when \(n\equiv0\); modulo \(7\) precisely when \(n\equiv5\), proving the formula. In particular \(\gcd(A,B,C)=1\), so every \(S_n\) is numerical.

We use the standard three-generator symmetry criterion: for a *minimal* generating triple of gcd \(1\), symmetry is equivalent to the existence of a pair with gcd \(d>1\) such that the remaining generator is a nonnegative integer combination of the two divided by \(d\). This is Herzog's complete-intersection criterion in its three-generated form (see Fel, Section 2.1, and García-Sánchez--Viola, Theorem 10). If a generator is redundant, the resulting two-generated numerical semigroup is automatically symmetric.

Only \(n=2,5\) have a redundant generator. Indeed \(C<2A\) for \(n\ge6\), making all three distinct generators minimal. For \(n=3,4\), direct inspection of \((A,B,C)=(12,22,35)\) and \((22,35,51)\) proves minimality. At \(n=2\), \(22=2\cdot5+12\); at \(n=5\), \(70=2\cdot35\). Since the remaining pairs are coprime, both semigroups are symmetric.

Now suppose the triple is minimal. If the pair is \((A,B)\), its gcd can exceed \(1\) only when \(n\equiv3\pmod4\), with gcd \(2\). A relation \(C=uA/2+vB/2\) requires \(u+v\ge3\), hence \(2C\ge3A\). This is equivalent to \(3n^2-25n-20\le0\), forcing \(n\le9\). Only \(n=3,7\) remain. For \(n=3\), \(35=4\cdot6+11\), so the semigroup is symmetric. For \(n=7\), \(117\notin\langle35,46\rangle\), as checking \(v=0,1,2\) shows.

If the pair is \((B,C)\), its gcd can exceed \(1\) only when \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), again with gcd \(2\). Since \(A<B<C\), a relation \(A=uB/2+vC/2\) requires \(u+v=1\), so \(2A=B\) or \(2A=C\). The first would give \(3n^2-7n-2=0\), with nonsquare discriminant \(73\). The second gives \(3n^2-13n-10=0\), whose only positive integer root is \(n=5\), not congruent to \(2\pmod4\). Thus this pair never works.

For the outer pair \((A,C)\), its gcd \(d>1\) is one of \(5,7,35\). A symmetry relation must satisfy
\[
dB=uA+vC,\qquad u,v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad s=u+v.
\]
If \(s\le d-1\), then \(dB\le(d-1)C\), forcing
\[
3n^2+(11-6d)n+(10-8d)\le0.\tag{1}
\]
If \(s\ge d+1\), then \(dB\ge(d+1)A\), forcing
\[
3n^2-(6d+1)n-2d\le0.\tag{2}
\]
Both left sides are positive and strictly increasing for \(n\ge N_d\), where \(N_5=11\), \(N_7=15\), and \(N_{35}=71\). Hence \(n\ge N_d\) entails \(s=d\). In that case
\[
d(3n+1)=v(6n+5),\qquad
n=\frac{5v-d}{3(d-2v)}.
\]
Because \(n>0\), necessarily \(d/5<v<d/2\); all three values of \(d\) are odd, giving \(v\le(d-1)/2\), and therefore \(n\le(3d-5)/6<N_d\), a contradiction. Thus a solution with this outer pair must have \(n<11\) if \(d=5\), \(n<15\) if \(d=7\), or \(n<71\) if \(d=35\).

The gcd congruences leave only \(n=10\) for \(d=5\), only \(n=12\) for \(d=7\), and only \(n=5,40\) for \(d=35\). At \(n=10\), the required relation would be \(176\in\langle29,42\rangle\), which is false by checking the possible coefficient of \(42\), namely \(0\) through \(4\). At \(n=12\), it would be \(247\in\langle30,41\rangle\), false by checking the possible coefficient of \(41\), namely \(0\) through \(6\). The \(n=5\) case was already handled as a two-generated semigroup. At \(n=40\), \(2501=7\cdot68+27\cdot75\), proving symmetry and exhausting all indices.

For the four symmetric semigroups, the usual two-generator formula and its gluing extension give \(F=43,133,1699,258529\) respectively, and symmetry then gives the stated genera. In particular for \(n=40\), the formula is
\[
F=35(68\cdot75-68-75)+34\cdot2501=258529.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A069757 (initiated 2002-04-05) records the Frobenius numbers of exactly these consecutive pentagonal triples, including the value \(258529\) at \(n=40\), but its description and numerical table do not classify symmetric indices or genera. Fel's 2008 work classifies *Fibonacci and Lucas* triples and explains the general gcd-pair criterion; the present quadratic pentagonal family requires the new exact gcd formulas and finite obstruction argument. García-Sánchez and Viola (2015) likewise supply the general three-generator structure without a pentagonal-family specialization. The theorem is not an assertion that its familiar general criterion is new.

## Limitations
Only ordinary pentagonal numbers with three consecutive indices and \(n\ge2\) are considered. The theorem does not determine general Frobenius numbers or genera outside the four symmetric indices, and it does not classify other polygonal families. The published Frobenius-number table already contains the four exceptional Frobenius values; novelty concerns the exhaustive symmetry classification and the last exceptional index. An unindexed equivalent classification could still exist and would require human literature verification.
