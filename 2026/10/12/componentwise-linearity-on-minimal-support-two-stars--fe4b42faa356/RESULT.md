# Componentwise linearity on minimal support-two stars
## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field and let \(s\ge 1\). In the standard graded polynomial ring
\[
R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_s,y],
\]
fix positive integers \(a_i,b_i\) and consider the minimal support-two star ideal
\[
I=(x_i^{a_i}y^{b_i}:1\le i\le s).
\]
Its base graph is the star with center \(y\) and leaves \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\).

Then \(I\) is componentwise linear if and only if at most one \(a_i\) is greater than \(1\), and if an exceptional index \(e\) with \(a_e>1\) exists, then
\[
b_e\ge b_i\qquad\text{for every }i.
\]
Equivalently, writing \(d_i=a_i+b_i\), for every pair \(i\ne j\) with \(d_i\le d_j\) one has
\[
a_i=1\quad\text{and}\quad b_i\le b_j.
\]
In the affirmative cases, the minimal generators have linear quotients in nondecreasing degree: order all generators with \(a_i=1\) by increasing \(b_i\), and put the unique exceptional generator last if there is one.

## Assumptions and scope
All exponents are positive integers, so there is exactly one minimal monomial generator on each edge of the star. No restriction is placed on the characteristic of \(k\). The statement concerns componentwise linearity, not a single linear resolution of the generally non-equigenerated ideal. The one-generator case \(s=1\) is included: every principal ideal is componentwise linear and the criterion is vacuous apart from allowing its single leaf exponent to be arbitrary.

## Proof
We use two standard facts about componentwise linear monomial ideals. First, if an ideal generated in one degree has a linear resolution, multiplication by the graded maximal ideal preserves linearity, and hence so do its positive powers. Second, an ideal with linear quotients with respect to a nondecreasing-degree ordering of its minimal generators is componentwise linear. We also use the generator-deletion fact proved as Proposition 3.2 of Kumar--Nanduri--Saha: if a componentwise linear monomial ideal is given and one deletes every minimal generator divisible by a fixed variable, the remaining ideal is still componentwise linear.

We first classify two leaves. Let
\[
J=(x^a y^b,z^c y^e),\qquad d=a+b,\quad f=c+e,
\]
and suppose \(d\le f\). For \(d\le t<f\), the degree-\(t\) component ideal of \(J\) is \(x^a y^b\mathfrak m^{t-d}\), so it has a \(t\)-linear resolution. At degree \(f\), put
\[
A=x^a y^b\mathfrak m^{f-d},\qquad v=z^c y^e.
\]
Both \(A\) and \((v)\) have \(f\)-linear resolutions. The exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow A\cap(v)\longrightarrow A\oplus(v)\longrightarrow A+(v)\longrightarrow0
\]
shows that \(A+(v)\) is \(f\)-linear exactly when \(A\cap(v)\) is \((f+1)\)-linear. Here the colon can be computed exactly:
\[
A:v=(x^a y^{\max(b-e,0)}),
\]
because membership in \(x^a y^b\mathfrak m^{f-d}\) imposes no further degree condition once divisibility by \(x^a y^b\) is satisfied. Hence
\[
A\cap(v)=v(x^a y^{\max(b-e,0)})
\]
is principal of degree
\[
f+a+\max(b-e,0).
\]
Therefore the degree-\(f\) component is linear if and only if
\[
a+\max(b-e,0)=1,
\]
which is equivalent to \(a=1\) and \(b\le e\). If this condition holds, every component above degree \(f\) is a power of \(\mathfrak m\) times the degree-\(f\) component and is again linear. Thus, when \(d\le f\), the two-generator ideal \(J\) is componentwise linear exactly when \(a=1\) and \(b\le e\).

Now suppose the original star ideal \(I\) is componentwise linear. Fix two leaves \(x_i,x_j\). Repeatedly apply the generator-deletion proposition to the other leaf variables. The resulting two-generator ideal
\[
(x_i^{a_i}y^{b_i},x_j^{a_j}y^{b_j})
\]
is componentwise linear. The two-leaf classification therefore gives, whenever \(d_i\le d_j\),
\[
a_i=1,\qquad b_i\le b_j.
\]
Two indices with leaf exponent greater than \(1\) are impossible, since the one with smaller total degree would violate this condition. If \(a_e>1\) is the unique exceptional exponent, then \(d_e\) must be strictly larger than every other \(d_i\), and the pairwise condition gives \(b_i\le b_e\) for all \(i\). This proves necessity.

Conversely, assume the stated exponent condition. If all \(a_i=1\), order the generators by increasing \(b_i\). If \(a_e>1\), first order the other generators by increasing \(b_i\), then place the exceptional generator last. This is a nondecreasing-degree order because \(b_e\ge b_i\) and \(a_e>1\). For any earlier generator \(u_i=x_i y^{b_i}\) and later generator \(u_j=x_j^{a_j}y^{b_j}\), one has \(b_i\le b_j\), and therefore
\[
\frac{u_i}{\gcd(u_i,u_j)}=x_i.
\]
Consequently every colon
\[
(u_1,\ldots,u_{j-1}):u_j
\]
is generated by a subset of the leaf variables. Thus the displayed order has linear quotients, and because the degrees are nondecreasing, \(I\) is componentwise linear. This proves sufficiency.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` replays the exact two-leaf criterion for every exponent quadruple with each exponent between \(1\) and \(6\), and then exhausts every star with at most four leaves and all exponent pairs in \(\{1,2,3\}^2\). It checks that the pairwise criterion is equivalent to the stated global invariant and verifies the claimed linear-quotient order in every passing case. The run covers \(1296\) two-leaf cases and \(7380\) star cases and ends with `CHECK_OK`.

The finite replay is not used as an infinite proof. The proof above is symbolic for arbitrary positive exponents, arbitrary star size, and every field.

## Relationship to prior work
Kumar and Saha's 2026 preprint introduces support-two and minimal support-two monomial ideals, proves that componentwise linearity forces the base graph to be co-chordal, and completely classifies minimal support-two ideals having a single linear resolution. For initial degree greater than \(2\), their linear-resolution theorem forces the special form
\[
(x_i x_j^a:i\ne j),
\]
with one common exponent at the star center. It does not classify componentwise linearity on minimal stars with arbitrary exponent pairs on the edges.

Kumar, Nanduri, and Saha study componentwise linearity for vertex-weighted oriented edge ideals. Their theory includes a useful deletion proposition and gives equivalences with linear quotients for several graph classes, including bipartite graphs. However, a vertex-weighted oriented edge ideal has generators of the restricted form \(x_i x_j^{w(x_j)}\); exponents attached to the same vertex must be consistent across incident directed edges. The present family permits independent pairs \((a_i,b_i)\) on the different star edges, so it is not covered by that model in general. The 2025 follow-up on powers retains the same vertex-weighted-oriented restriction.

Dao and Suresh-Babu give broad criteria for sums of componentwise linear ideals and a complete description in two variables. Their results do not state this arbitrary-dimensional minimal-star exponent classification.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to minimal support-two ideals whose base graph is a star. It does not classify componentwise linearity for general co-chordal base graphs, for non-minimal support-two ideals, or for ideals with more than one generator on an edge. No claim is made about powers of the ideals in the new class. The originality comparison is necessarily subject to terminology risk: an equivalent arbitrary-edge-exponent star criterion could exist under a different monomial-ideal formalism, although targeted searches and the most directly overlapping full texts inspected did not reveal one.
