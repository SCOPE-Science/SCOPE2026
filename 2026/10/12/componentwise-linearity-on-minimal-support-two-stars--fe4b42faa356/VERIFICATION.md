# Verification

The proof has three independently checkable steps.

1. **Two-leaf slice.** For \(J=(x^a y^b,z^c y^e)\) with \(a+b\le c+e\), the degree-\(c+e\) slice is the sum of \(x^a y^b\mathfrak m^{c+e-a-b}\) and \((z^c y^e)\). Their intersection is the principal ideal generated by \(z^c y^e x^a y^{\max(b-e,0)}\). Its degree is one larger than \(c+e\) exactly when \(a=1\) and \(b\le e\). Lower and higher slices are obtained by multiplication with powers of \(\mathfrak m\).

2. **Necessity on a star.** Repeated generator deletion isolates every two-leaf subideal without losing componentwise linearity. Thus every ordered pair by total degree must satisfy the two-leaf criterion. This permits at most one leaf exponent greater than \(1\); if such an exponent occurs, its center exponent must dominate all the others.

3. **Sufficiency.** Sort the unit-leaf generators by increasing center exponent and place the unique heavy-leaf generator last. For every earlier/later pair, the monomial quotient by the gcd is exactly the earlier leaf variable. Hence every colon is generated by variables, giving linear quotients in nondecreasing degree and therefore componentwise linearity.

`verify.py` replays the exact arithmetic reduction on bounded families. Running

`python verify.py`

produces the bundled `verification_output.txt`, ending in `CHECK_OK`. The replay covers 1296 two-leaf exponent quadruples and 7380 star exponent tuples. It checks the pair criterion, its equivalence with the global invariant, and the asserted linear-quotient ordering. The finite computation is a regression check only and does not establish the unbounded quantifiers.
