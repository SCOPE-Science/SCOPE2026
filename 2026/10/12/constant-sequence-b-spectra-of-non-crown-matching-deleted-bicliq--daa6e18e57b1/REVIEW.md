# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. A mixed-shore independent color class in \(K_{{m,n}}-M_t\) can only be a single deleted matched pair. For \(q\ge3\) that class is too small. For \(q=2\), if such a pair is a realizing color class, both endpoints must be color-dominating, forcing every other color to occur on both shores and therefore forcing every color class to be a deleted matched pair; covering all vertices then forces the excluded equality \(m=n=t\). With only shore-pure classes, any coloring using at least three colors puts two colors on one shore, whose vertices cannot see each other’s color, so those classes have no color-dominating vertices. The two-color bipartition realizes exactly when \(q\le m\). Exhaustive finite replay agrees on \(66\) parameter profiles and \(24{,}043\) canonical proper colorings.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating paper defines sequence \(b\)-coloring and its spectra, but its inspected full text has no crown or complete-bipartite classification; its bipartite observation concerns a different one-extra-CDV sequence. Searches for the exact invariant together with matching-deleted bicliques, crowns, complete bipartite graphs, and multiple color-dominating vertices found no statement implying this theorem. The closest crown record returned by indexed search is for proper conflict-free coloring, while the closest \(b\)-chromatic record concerns classical one-CDV-per-color colorings. The undeleted biclique and balanced full-matching crown boundaries are expressly excluded from the claimed new statement.

## Value
PASS. Matching deletion is the canonical interpolation between a biclique and a crown graph. The theorem completely resolves every constant requirement \(q\ge2\) throughout the non-crown matching-deleted regime and identifies the exact structural reason no third color survives: a mixed class either cannot meet the CDV multiplicity or propagates to a full balanced matching. This is a natural robustness boundary for a newly introduced spectrum, not an arbitrary finite slice.
