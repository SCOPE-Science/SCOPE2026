# Coordinate rigidity for subalgebras of the Łukasiewicz-square interval

## Finding

Let \(\mathbf S=([0,1],\vee,\neg,\ast)\), with \(x\vee y=\max(x,y)\), \(\neg x=1-x\), and \(\ast x=\max(0,2x-1)\). **Every homomorphism between any two nonempty subalgebras \(A,B\le\mathbf S\) is the inclusion map:**
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=
\begin{cases}
\{\iota_{A,B}\},&A\subseteq B,\\
\varnothing,&A\not\subseteq B.
\end{cases}
\]
This covers finite, countably infinite, and arbitrary uncountable subalgebras. Hence the full category on these concrete subalgebras is a thin category isomorphic to their inclusion poset. Every endomorphism is the identity; every abstract isomorphism between concrete subalgebras is their common identity, so distinct subalgebras are not isomorphic.

For the standard finite reducts \(\mathbf S_n=\{0,1/n,\ldots,1\}\) (\(n\ge1\)) in the same signature,
\[
|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf S_n,\mathbf S_m)|=
\begin{cases}1,&n\mid m,\\0,&n\nmid m.\end{cases}
\]
The unique map, when present, fixes every rational coordinate. In particular, no standard finite square-reduct has a nonidentity endomorphism, even when it has proper nontrivial subalgebras.

## Assumptions and scope

A subalgebra is a nonempty subset of the real unit interval closed under all three displayed operations, with the operations inherited from \(\mathbf S\). Homomorphisms preserve \(\vee,\neg,\ast\), but no continuity, injectivity, or surjectivity is assumed. Finite \(\mathbf S_n\) are defined by their displayed rational grids. The theorem is about **concrete subalgebras of the standard interval**, not arbitrary algebras in the variety generated by \(\mathbf S\).

## Proof

**Step 1 (definable endpoints).** The unary term
\[
\tau(x)=\ast\bigl(\neg(x\vee\neg x)\bigr)
\]
is identically zero on \([0,1]\), because \(\neg(x\vee\neg x)=\min(x,1-x)\le1/2\). Thus every nonempty subalgebra contains \(0=\tau(a)\) and \(1=\neg 0\); every homomorphism preserves both, despite the absence of nullary symbols.

**Step 2 (all dyadic ramps are unary terms).** Put
\[
\sigma(x)=\ast x=\max(0,2x-1),\qquad
\lambda(x)=\neg\ast\neg x=\min(1,2x).
\]
For \(k\ge0\) and \(0\le j<2^k\), define terms recursively by
\[
R_{0,0}(x)=x,\quad
R_{k+1,2j}(x)=\lambda(R_{k,j}(x)),\quad
R_{k+1,2j+1}(x)=\sigma(R_{k,j}(x)).
\]
Induction gives the exact real-valued term functions
\[
\boxed{R_{k,j}(x)=\min\!\left(1,\max(0,2^k x-j)\right).}
\]
Indeed, both \(\lambda\) and \(\sigma\) commute with truncation to \([0,1]\) in the required manner: if \([y]_0^1=\min(1,\max(0,y))\), then \(\lambda([y]_0^1)=[2y]_0^1\) and \(\sigma([y]_0^1)=[2y-1]_0^1\).

**Step 3 (coordinate recovery without continuity).** Let \(h:A\to B\) be any homomorphism and let \(a\in A\). Since it preserves terms and the endpoints, \(h(R_{k,j}(a))=R_{k,j}(h(a))\). If \(a<j/2^k<1\), then \(R_{k,j}(a)=0\). Hence \(R_{k,j}(h(a))=0\), forcing \(h(a)\le j/2^k\). As dyadic rationals strictly above \(a\) approach \(a\), this implies \(h(a)\le a\). Likewise, if \(0<(j+1)/2^k<a\), then \(R_{k,j}(a)=1\); preservation forces \(h(a)\ge(j+1)/2^k\). Dyadic rationals below \(a\) approach \(a\), giving \(h(a)\ge a\). The endpoint cases were settled in Step 1. Therefore \(h(a)=a\) for every \(a\in A\).

Thus any homomorphism is literally inclusion, and inclusion preserves all operations whenever \(A\subseteq B\). The finite-grid formula follows because \(\mathbf S_n\subseteq\mathbf S_m\) if and only if \(n\mid m\) (for positive integers). The isomorphism, endomorphism, and category statements follow immediately.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library Python verifier is embedded as `verify_coordinate_rigidity.py`. It checks all \(131327\) dyadic ramp evaluations at denominators \(256\), through depth \(8\); confirms the definable-zero term; exhaustively screens \(92368\) endpoint-preserving monotone candidate maps between full grids of denominators \(1\) through \(9\); and screens \(10364\) analogous candidate maps over all \(324\) pairs of subalgebras of grids with denominators \(1\) through \(7\). The only homomorphisms found are the \(23\) and \(162\) predicted inclusions, respectively. The verifier independently checks that deleting either \(\ast\) or \(\vee\) invalidates the conclusion in small examples.

These computations are finite consistency checks, **not** a substitute for the unbounded proof in Step 3.

## Relationship to prior work

Coniglio, Esteva, Flaminio and Godo study exactly these \((\vee,\neg,\ast)\)-reducts, establish conditions for finite chains to recover ordinary Łukasiewicz implication, and classify the structure of finite subalgebras by their generating skeletons (Sections 3 and 5, especially Proposition 5.9 and Theorem 5.16). The latter theorem's proof constructs componentwise embeddings for finite representable chains; this is a particularly close antecedent to the **finite embedding** consequence above. The present statement instead classifies **all** homomorphisms (without assuming injectivity) among **arbitrary, including infinite**, concrete subalgebras, using a uniform dyadic term family. No equivalent categorical hom-set statement was found in the inspected primary text.

Ordinary full-signature MV-chain homomorphism theory yields divisibility for the full MV signature, but cannot by itself rule out new homomorphisms after deleting implication and strong conjunction: the same source shows that the reduced signature is not term-equivalent to the full signature for many denominators. The strengthened hom-set rigidity across the reducts therefore requires a separate argument.

## Limitations

The theorem does not assert that every abstract algebra in the associated variety embeds in \(\mathbf S\), nor does it classify maps between abstract nonrepresentable algebras. It does not compute the number of exotic finite subalgebras of each grid. The origin of a possible equivalent result in unindexed earlier literature is not excluded. The result has undergone internal proof and finite-computation review, but not an independent mathematical audit.
