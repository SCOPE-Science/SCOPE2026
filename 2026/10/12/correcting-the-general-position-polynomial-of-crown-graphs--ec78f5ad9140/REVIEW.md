# Review of Correcting the general position polynomial of crown graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The ordinary claim is forced by a short structural proof: in any bipartite graph a selected edge plus a third selected vertex violates general position, so every general position set of size at least three is independent; in a crown graph any independent set of size at least three lies in one bipartition class. This yields \(2(1+z)^n-1+n^2z^2\). General \(2\)-position is independently classified by the matched indices on the two sides, producing exactly the additional cubic and quartic terms. The foundational triangle-free equivalence with dissociation sets agrees. Exhaustive geodesic checks for \(3\le n\le9\) verify every coefficient. The \(n=3\) case gives an internal cross-check because \(\operatorname{Cr}_3=C_6\), whose ordinary cycle polynomial has only two cubic sets.

## Originality
PASS. The 2024 primary paper explicitly identifies \(T^*(r,2)\) as a complete bipartite graph without a perfect matching and displays the larger expression as its ordinary general position polynomial. Targeted searches for an erratum, correction, crown-graph general position polynomial, and general-\(2\)-position crown result located no published correction. The foundational 2020 source proves the triangle-free general-\(2\)-position/dissociation equivalence and a complete-bipartite scalar result but contains no crown treatment. Thus the correction and the identification of the published expression as the general-\(2\)-position polynomial are not implied by the inspected prior statements.

## Value
PASS. This is a correction to a named infinite family in the paper that introduced general position polynomials. It resolves a direct internal inconsistency at \(n=3\), replaces the ordinary polynomial by a simple exact formula for all connected crowns, and explains why the erroneous expression nevertheless has a natural meaning: it counts general \(2\)-position/dissociation sets exactly. The result therefore repairs the enumeration while preserving useful mathematical content of the published expression.
