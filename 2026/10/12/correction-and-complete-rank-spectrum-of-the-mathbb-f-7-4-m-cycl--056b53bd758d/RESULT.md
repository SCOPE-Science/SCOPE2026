# Two corrections to the \(\mathbb F_{7^4}\) cyclic benchmark

## Finding

Berhuy and Molina's Example 4.25 specifies \(\omega^4=\omega^2+1\) and calls \(\omega\) a generator of \(\mathbb F_{7^4}^{\times}\). A root of \(X^4-X^2-1\) generates \(L=\mathbb F_{7^4}\) as a field, but has multiplicative order \(32\), not \(2400\). The example also prints a message parametrization that does not lie in its stated cyclic ideal. Replacing the primitive-element requirement by the quartic-root requirement and using the basis \(g,xg\), where \(\alpha=4\omega^2-2\) and \(g=(x-1)(x-\alpha)\), fixes both issues. The printed tuple is rank-isometric to the corrected tuple, preserving the rank conclusions. For reference, the corrected code has projective rank multiplicities \((1,49,2352)\) at ranks \(1,2,3\), respectively, and
\[
W_C(z)=1+2400z+117600z^2+5644800z^3,
\qquad (M_1(C),M_2(C))=(1,3).
\]

## Field and code

In characteristic seven the relation gives \(\omega^8=3\omega^2+2\), then \(\omega^{16}=-1\). The order is therefore exactly \(32\). The degree over \(\mathbb F_7\) of a root of order \(32\) is the least \(d>0\) with \(7^d\equiv1\pmod{32}\): Frobenius to the \(d\)th power fixes the root exactly then. Here \(d=4\), proving irreducibility and validating the polynomial basis \(1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3\). Also \((4\omega^2-2)^2=-1\). Thus \(\alpha\notin\mathbb F_7\), \(\mathbb F_7(\alpha)=\mathbb F_{49}\), and \(g\) divides \(x^4-1\).

Use coefficient order \((1,x,x^2,x^3)\). The cyclic code is the ideal of \(g\), with \(L\)-basis \(g,xg\). Its actual coordinate vector for \(ag+bxg\) is
\[
c(a,b)=\big(a\alpha,\;-a(\alpha+1)+b\alpha,\;a-b(\alpha+1),\;b\big).
\]
In particular every coordinate sum is zero, as required by the root \(1\). The alternate tuple \(\big(a\alpha+b,-a(\alpha+1)+b\alpha,a-b\alpha,b\big)\) has coordinate sum \(2b\), so it is not a parametrization of this cyclic code. Subtracting its last coordinate from each of the first and third is an invertible \(\mathbb F_7\)-linear coordinate operation and recovers \(c(a,b)\); the two tuples therefore have the same coordinate-span rank for each \((a,b)\).

## Rank checks on the corrected benchmark

Rank means the dimension over \(\mathbb F_7\) of the span of the four coordinates. Each nonzero \(L\)-scalar multiple has the same rank. The projective representatives are \(c(t,1)\) for \(t\in L\) and \(c(1,0)\) at infinity. The zero sum of \(c(t,1)\) makes its second coordinate redundant; its coordinate span is exactly \(\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb F_7}(1,t\alpha,t-\alpha)\). The affine point has rank at most two if and only if there exist \(A,B\in\mathbb F_7\), not both zero, with \((A\alpha+B)t-B\alpha\in\mathbb F_7\). The coefficient \(A\alpha+B\) is nonzero. This forces \(t\in\mathbb F_{49}\); conversely every \(t\in\mathbb F_{49}\) gives rank at most two. Rank one forces \(t=\alpha+s\) with \(s\in\mathbb F_7\) and \(-1+s\alpha\in\mathbb F_7\), so it occurs exactly at \(t=\alpha\). Infinity has rank two since its coordinates span \(1,\alpha\). Therefore the ranks occur on \(1,48+1,2400-48\) lines, respectively. Each line contains \(2400\) nonzero words, giving the enumerator.

For the generalized rank weights, the full code is contained in the zero-sum hyperplane, which is defined over \(\mathbb F_7\) and is Frobenius invariant. Thus \(M_2\leq 3\). The Frobenius closure of an \(L\)-line has dimension equal to the rank of a generating vector (the Moore matrix rank identity). A line of rank three therefore gives \(M_2\geq 3\); the rank-one line gives \(M_1=1\).

## Prior coverage, verification and scope

Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py`. It verifies the field identities, constructs each projective representative from the coefficient vectors of \(g,xg\), checks both roots on every representative, and computes its exact coordinate rank. The result repairs the field generator specification and gives the exact rank distribution of the intended cyclic code; it is not a claim about all \(M\)-cyclic codes.

Berhuy and Molina, *On the rank weight hierarchy of M-codes*, Example 4.25, [arXiv:2507.00609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00609), DOI [10.3934/amc.2026035](https://doi.org/10.3934/amc.2026035), gives the benchmark and generalized rank weights. The ordinary enumerator is a check on the correction, not a new rank-enumeration result: eliminate the last coordinate using the zero sum to obtain a rank-isometric length-three code with dual generated by \((1+\alpha,2\alpha,-1+\alpha)\), of rank two. Its dual enumerator thus equals that of the length-three example in Gadouleau and Yan, [*MacWilliams Identity for Codes with the Rank Metric*](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0701097), Section III-F. Their Theorem 1 forces the same ordinary enumerator at \(q=7,m=4\).
