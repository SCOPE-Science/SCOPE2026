# Cotangent dimension bounds tensor-factor length of split local algebras
## Finding
Let \(F\) be an arbitrary field and let \(A\) be a nontrivial finite-dimensional local \(F\)-algebra whose residue division algebra is \(F\), so \(A/J(A)\cong F\). Write
\[
e(A)=\dim_F J(A)/J(A)^2.
\]
Choose an irreducible tensor factorization of \(A\) over \(F\), and let \(\tau(A)\) be its number of nontrivial irreducible factors. Sakurai's uniqueness theorem makes \(\tau(A)\) independent of the chosen factorization. Then
\[
\tau(A)\le e(A).
\]
Moreover, equality holds exactly for the truncated-polynomial products
\[
A\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{e(A)}F[t_i]/(t_i^{m_i}),\qquad m_i\ge2.
\]
When equality holds, the multiset \(\{m_1,\ldots,m_{e(A)}\}\) is uniquely determined by the isomorphism class of \(A\).

## Assumptions and scope
All tensor products are over \(F\). A factor is called nontrivial when it is not isomorphic to \(F\), and tensor-irreducible when it has no tensor decomposition into two nontrivial finite-dimensional \(F\)-algebras. The algebra \(A\) is assumed finite-dimensional, local, and split in the sense \(A/J(A)\cong F\). No restriction on the characteristic of \(F\) is imposed.

An irreducible factorization exists by repeatedly decomposing a reducible finite-dimensional algebra; dimensions strictly decrease in each nontrivial factor, so the process terminates. Sakurai proves uniqueness, up to permutation and isomorphism of the factors, for finite-dimensional local algebras over a splitting field. Thus \(\tau(A)\) is a genuine invariant in this setting.

## Proof
First consider two finite-dimensional split local \(F\)-algebras \(B\) and \(C\). Put
\[
R=J(B)\otimes C+B\otimes J(C)\subseteq B\otimes C.
\]
The ideal \(R\) is nilpotent, and
\[
(B\otimes C)/R\cong (B/J(B))\otimes(C/J(C))\cong F,
\]
so \(R=J(B\otimes C)\). Its square is
\[
R^2=J(B)^2\otimes C+J(B)\otimes J(C)+B\otimes J(C)^2.
\]
Consequently the natural maps from the two factors induce an isomorphism
\[
J(B\otimes C)/J(B\otimes C)^2
\cong J(B)/J(B)^2\oplus J(C)/J(C)^2.
\]
Hence cotangent dimension is additive:
\[
e(B\otimes C)=e(B)+e(C).
\]

Let
\[
A\cong P_1\otimes\cdots\otimes P_s
\]
be an irreducible tensor factorization. Sakurai's reduction shows that the factors are again split local algebras. Every nontrivial split local algebra \(P\) has \(e(P)\ge1\): if \(e(P)=0\), then \(J(P)=J(P)^2\), and nilpotence of \(J(P)\) forces \(J(P)=0\), whence \(P\cong F\). Additivity therefore gives
\[
e(A)=\sum_{i=1}^s e(P_i)\ge s=\tau(A).
\]

It remains to identify equality. Equality occurs precisely when \(e(P_i)=1\) for every factor. We use the following elementary one-generator lemma.

Suppose \(P\) is a nontrivial finite-dimensional split local algebra with \(e(P)=1\). Choose \(x\in J(P)\) whose class spans \(J(P)/J(P)^2\). For every \(q\ge1\), multiplication induces a surjection
\[
\bigl(J(P)/J(P)^2\bigr)^{\otimes q}\longrightarrow J(P)^q/J(P)^{q+1}.
\]
The source is one-dimensional, so each radical layer has dimension at most one and is spanned by the class of \(x^q\). If \(J(P)^q\ne0\), then \(J(P)^q/J(P)^{q+1}\ne0\), because equality \(J(P)^q=J(P)^{q+1}\) would, by nilpotence, force \(J(P)^q=0\). Thus, if \(m\ge2\) is the nilpotency index of \(J(P)\), the elements
\[
1,x,x^2,\ldots,x^{m-1}
\]
form an \(F\)-basis of \(P\), while \(x^m=0\). Hence
\[
P\cong F[t]/(t^m).
\]

Conversely, \(F[t]/(t^m)\) has cotangent dimension one and is tensor-irreducible. Indeed, a nontrivial decomposition into split local factors would give, by cotangent additivity, \(1=e(B)+e(C)\ge2\), a contradiction. Therefore \(\tau(A)=e(A)\) exactly when every irreducible factor is a one-variable truncated polynomial algebra, which gives the displayed tensor product.

Finally, Sakurai's uniqueness theorem identifies the irreducible factors up to reordering and isomorphism. Since \(\dim_F F[t]/(t^m)=m\), the multiset of exponents \(\{m_i\}\) is uniquely determined.

## Verification
The proof is symbolic and does not infer an infinite statement from a finite computation. The two critical reductions were checked independently inside the argument: the radical of a tensor product of split local algebras is the sum of the two extended radicals, and quotienting its square leaves exactly the direct sum of the two cotangent spaces. The equality case then reduces to the radical filtration of an algebra with one-dimensional cotangent space; nilpotence rules out a stationary nonzero radical layer, forcing every nonzero layer to be one-dimensional and generated by a power of a single lift.

The converse irreducibility check is also internal: cotangent additivity makes a nontrivial tensor decomposition of a cotangent-dimension-one split local algebra impossible. No machine computation, exhaustive search, or unproved classification is used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Sakurai proves unique irreducible tensor factorization for finite-dimensional local algebras over a splitting field and explicitly develops the cotangent space \(J/J^2\) as a functorial tool. The preprint does not state the factor-count bound above, does not identify the equality case, and contains no truncated-polynomial classification of maximally factorable algebras.

Earlier uniqueness results of Nüsken and cancellation results of Horst treat related tensor-factorization phenomena under narrower hypotheses. They motivate the comparison but, in the materials inspected, no statement was found that bounds the number of irreducible tensor factors by cotangent dimension or classifies equality. The present statement is an extremal structural consequence of the new all-characteristic split-local uniqueness theorem rather than a reformulation of uniqueness itself.

## Limitations
The result requires finite dimension, locality, and splitting of the residue division algebra over the ground field. It does not address nonsplit local algebras, nonlocal algebras, or the finer classification of algebras with \(\tau(A)<e(A)\). Historical tensor-factorization literature was not exhaustively available in full text; an older characteristic-zero source could contain an equivalent special-case corollary, although no such statement was located. Independent audit has not been performed.
