# Critical-scale fringe subtrees form a multitype spatial Poisson process

## Finding

**Theorem (uniform multitype spatial Poisson approximation).** Fix an integer \(r\ge 2\) and real numbers \(0<a<b<\infty\). For each sufficiently large integer \(n\), choose pairwise distinct integers \(k_{n,1},\ldots,k_{n,r}\) satisfying
\[
a\sqrt n\le k_{n,j}\le b\sqrt n\qquad(1\le j\le r).
\]
Build a binary search tree from a uniformly random insertion ordering of the keys \(1,\ldots,n\). For every \(j\), mark the starting key of each fringe subtree whose key set consists of \(k_{n,j}\) consecutive ranks. Let \(\Xi_n\) be the resulting point measure on \([0,1]\times\{1,\ldots,r\}\), assigning such a subtree with first key \(i\) a point at \((i/n,j)\).

Put
\[
p_k=\frac{2}{(k+1)(k+2)},\quad
\mathcal A_n=\{(i,j):1\le j\le r,\ 2\le i\le n-k_{n,j}\}.
\]
Let \(\Pi_n\) be the spatial marked Poisson point measure formed by independent Poisson multiplicities at the lattice positions \((i/n,j)\), for \((i,j)\in\mathcal A_n\), with means \(p_{k_{n,j}}\); it has no boundary points. There exists a finite constant \(C=C(r,a,b)\) such that, uniformly over all the permitted distinct choices of sizes,
\[
\boxed{\ d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\mathcal L(\Xi_n),\mathcal L(\Pi_n)\bigr)\le \frac{C}{\sqrt n}.\ }
\]
Here total variation refers to the laws of the entire finite marked point measures, not only their total counts.

If in addition \(k_{n,j}/\sqrt n\to c_j\in[a,b]\) for every \(j\), then \(\Xi_n\) converges weakly as a point measure to a marked Poisson point process whose marks \(j\) are independent and whose mark-\(j\) spatial intensity is \((2/c_j^2)\,dt\) on \([0,1]\). The limits \(c_j\) need not be distinct, although the finite sizes \(k_{n,j}\) must be. In particular, the fringe-subtree counts for the \(r\) selected sizes converge jointly to independent Poisson variables with respective means \(2/c_j^2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The randomness is the uniform permutation model for an ordinary binary search tree, with independent continuous insertion timestamps \(U_1,\ldots,U_n\); smaller timestamps mean earlier insertions. A *fringe subtree* consists of a node and all descendants below it, so its keys occupy a consecutive rank interval. A point is marked by the first key of that interval and by the index of its prescribed size. The integers \(r\) and constants \(a,b\) stay fixed as \(n\to\infty\). The total-variation estimate is against the discrete, matching-intensity Poisson field \(\Pi_n\), not against the continuum limiting process: lattice and continuous spatial laws are mutually singular for each fixed \(n\).

## Proof

**1. An exact local representation.** For \(1\le i\le n-k+1\), define \(A_{i,k}\) to be the event that the interval \(\{i,\ldots,i+k-1\}\) is a fringe subtree. Add deterministic sentinel timestamps \(U_0=U_{n+1}=-1\). The Cartesian-tree construction of a binary search tree gives the exact equivalence
\[
A_{i,k}=\left\{\max(U_{i-1},U_{i+k})<\min_{i\le s\le i+k-1}U_s\right\}.
\]
Indeed, the earliest timestamp in the interval is its prospective subtree root; the nearest ranks just outside must be inserted earlier to delimit its entire set of descendants. For interior starts \(2\le i\le n-k\), both sentinels are random; their timestamps must occupy the first two positions among \(k+2\) independent continuous timestamps. Thus
\[
\Pr(A_{i,k})=p_k=\frac{2}{(k+1)(k+2)}.
\]
At either extreme start \(i=1\) or \(i=n-k+1\), one delimiter is deterministic and the remaining delimiter must be the smallest of \(k+1\) timestamps, so \(\Pr(A_{i,k})=1/(k+1)\). In particular the exact previously known mean is recovered:
\[
\mathbb E X_{n,k}=(n-k-1)p_k+\frac{2}{k+1}
=\frac{2(n+1)}{(k+1)(k+2)},\qquad k<n-1.
\]
The restriction \(k<n-1\) is automatic for all prescribed sizes when \(n\) is sufficiently large.

**2. A joint local-dependence field.** Temporarily discard the two boundary starts for each size and write \(I_{i,j}=\mathbf1_{A_{i,k_{n,j}}}\) for \((i,j)\in\mathcal A_n\). This indicator is a measurable function only of the timestamps indexed by its *augmented interval*
\[
D_{i,j}=\{i-1,\ldots,i+k_{n,j}\}.
\]
For a site \(\alpha=(i,j)\), let \(B_\alpha\) comprise all sites \(\beta\) whose augmented intervals meet \(D_{i,j}\), including \(\alpha\). Then \(I_\alpha\) is independent of the sigma-field generated by all indicators outside \(B_\alpha\). In the Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon Poisson-process bound this makes its \(b_3\) term exactly zero, while
\[
b_1=\sum_\alpha\sum_{\beta\in B_\alpha}\Pr(I_\alpha=1)\Pr(I_\beta=1),\quad
b_2=\sum_\alpha\sum_{\substack{\beta\in B_\alpha\\\beta\ne\alpha}}\Pr(I_\alpha=I_\beta=1).
\]
There are at most \(rn\) sites, every neighborhood has at most \(r(2b\sqrt n+4)\) sites, and every interior event has probability at most \(2/(a^2n)\). Therefore \(b_1=O_{r,a,b}(n^{-1/2})\).

**3. Joint-event classification.** Two rooted fringe subtrees in one tree are either disjoint or one contains the other. Consequently two intervals partially overlapping without containment cannot give simultaneous events. If two disjoint intervals touch, simultaneous events are impossible too: the last key of the left interval must arrive before the first of the right interval to delimit the right subtree, while the first key of the right interval must arrive before the last of the left interval to delimit the left subtree, a strict contradiction. Disjoint intervals with at least two intervening ranks have disjoint augmented intervals and therefore contribute nothing to \(b_2\).

Suppose an interval of size \(k\) is strictly contained in an interval of size \(\ell>k\), without sharing either endpoint. Conditional on the outer interval being a fringe subtree, the relative ordering of its internal \(\ell\) timestamps is still uniform: the conditioning only requires both exterior timestamps to precede the internal ones. The conditional probability of the inner interval being a fringe subtree is therefore precisely \(p_k\). If the inner and outer intervals share one endpoint, one exterior delimiter of the inner interval has already been forced to precede all internal timestamps; the conditional probability is instead \(1/(k+1)\). For each fixed outer interval and candidate inner size there are at most \(b\sqrt n\) strictly internal starts and at most two endpoint-sharing starts. Hence the sum of its joint probabilities with nested inner candidates is bounded by
\[
p_\ell\left(b\sqrt n\,p_k+\frac{2}{k+1}\right)=O_{a,b}(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Summing over the at most \(rn\) outer sites and \(r\) inner-size labels gives \(O_{r,a,b}(n^{-1/2})\) for all nested ordered pairs, including when the two sizes differ by only one.

It remains to control disjoint intervals separated by exactly one intervening key. Conditional on that intervening timestamp taking value \(t\), the events involve disjoint remaining sets of timestamps, and their conditional probabilities are
\[
h_k(t)=t(1-t)^k+\frac{(1-t)^{k+1}}{k+1},\quad
h_\ell(t)=t(1-t)^\ell+\frac{(1-t)^{\ell+1}}{\ell+1}.
\]
This follows by integrating over the minimum internal timestamp of each interval and requiring its other exterior delimiter to be smaller. Consequently their joint probability is \(\int_0^1h_k(t)h_\ell(t)\,dt\). With \(m=\min(k,\ell)\),
\[
h_k(t),h_\ell(t)\le(t+m^{-1})e^{-mt},\quad
\int_0^1h_k(t)h_\ell(t)\,dt
\le \int_0^\infty(t+m^{-1})^2e^{-2mt}\,dt
=\frac{5}{4m^3}=O_a(n^{-3/2}).
\]
There are at most \(2r^2n\) such ordered neighboring site pairs. Thus their total contribution is again \(O_{r,a,b}(n^{-1/2})\). All configurations in \(b_2\) have now been accounted for, and \(b_2=O_{r,a,b}(n^{-1/2})\).

**4. Poisson process and boundary correction.** The process form of the Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon local-dependence theorem, applied to the entire labeled site field, bounds its total-variation distance to a field of independent Poisson variables with the same respective means by a universal multiple of \(b_1+b_2\) when \(b_3=0\). Mapping sites to positions \((i/n,j)\) cannot increase total variation. Reinstating the at most \(2r\) boundary sites changes the point measure with probability at most
\[
\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{2}{k_{n,j}+1}\le\frac{2r}{a\sqrt n}.
\]
This proves the stated uniform \(O_{r,a,b}(n^{-1/2})\) bound.

**5. Continuous spatial limit.** If \(k_{n,j}/\sqrt n\to c_j\), then \(np_{k_{n,j}}\to 2/c_j^2\). For every continuous test function on the compact marked space, the discrete intensity measures of \(\Pi_n\) converge by Riemann sums to \(\sum_j(2/c_j^2)\,dt\otimes\delta_j\). Hence the Poisson Laplace functionals converge to those of independent homogeneous marked processes. The total-variation approximation transfers this weak limit to \(\Xi_n\). Projecting onto mark counts proves the independent Poisson count limit, with no separation assumption on the constants \(c_j\). \(\square\)

## Verification

The included self-contained file `VERIFY.py` is run with `python3 VERIFY.py` using only Python's standard library. It independently builds binary search trees from every insertion permutation for orders two through seven and compares all subtree rank intervals with the stated timestamp indicator, verifying the exact mean identities. It then enumerates all \(9!\) insertion orders for nine keys, checking six joint-event identities (strict nesting, two endpoint-sharing nesting cases, touching exclusion, single-gap integration, and independence of disjoint augmented intervals) using exact rational arithmetic. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite tests independently check critical local identities; they do not prove the asymptotic theorem, which relies on the preceding estimates and the cited Poisson-process approximation theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Dennert and Grübel (2010, Section 4.2, page 576), after establishing exact covariances and process limits at the small- and linear-subtree-size ends, explicitly conjectured that counts at distinct critical scales become independent Poisson variables, and stated that they had no proof. The theorem here resolves that critical-band conjecture and strengthens it to a uniform, quantitative *spatial* marked point-process approximation. It also handles adjacent selected integer sizes converging to the same critical-scale constant. Their covariance formula alone does not imply a joint Poisson law. Feng, Mahmoud and Panholzer (2008), Fuchs (2008), and Holmgren and Janson (2014) established one-size critical Poisson limits; the 2014 work also establishes multivariate normal limits for *fixed* fringe-tree types, not the present multitype critical Poisson law. Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon (1989) provide the general local-dependence point-process theorem; the new step is the complete multiscale nesting/separation analysis for random binary search trees, not the abstract approximation inequality.

## Limitations

The number of marks \(r\) and the positive compact critical band \([a,b]\) are fixed. The theorem requires *distinct finite subtree sizes*, even when their rescaled limits coincide. No conclusion is asserted for the recursive-tree model, for growing numbers of marks, or for subtree sizes outside this band. The \(O(n^{-1/2})\) total-variation rate compares the finite marked tree to a matching-intensity discrete Poisson field; it is not a total-variation rate to a continuous point process. No independence or publication-priority certification beyond the stated mathematical comparison is claimed.
