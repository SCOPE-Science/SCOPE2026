# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A uniformly random binary search tree admits an exact timestamp criterion for a specified contiguous key interval to be a fringe subtree. The two boundary exceptions have total probability \(O(n^{-1/2})\). For interior intervals, direct integration yields the exact marginal \(2/((k+1)(k+2))\). The Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon local-dependence point-process theorem applies because indicators outside intersecting augmented intervals are independent. All intersecting augmented-window configurations are exhausted: incompatible partial overlap and touching; nested pairs with separate endpoint and interior conditional probabilities; one-gap disjoint pairs with an explicit shared-separator integral. Each nonzero local-pair contribution sums to \(O(n^{-1/2})\). This proves the field-level total-variation bound; an intensity Riemann-sum argument gives the continuous spatial limit. Verification reproduced \(5,912\) complete BST permutations and \(362,880\) exact nine-timestamp permutations, including six joint identities. Finite checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof. The only imported theorem is the published general Poisson-process bound of Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon (1989, Theorem 2).

**Originality — PASS, subject to independent historical priority review.** Dennert and Grübel (2010, Section 4.2, p. 576) explicitly conjecture asymptotic independent Poisson counts for multiple critical subtree-size parameters, and say they have no proof; their Proposition 2.1 supplies covariance formulas only. The present theorem supplies a stronger quantitative result for the complete rank-location marked point measure, including distinct integer sizes converging to the same scaled constant. Holmgren and Janson (arXiv:1406.6883v1, Section 1) explicitly allow varying size for one-size Poisson approximation but not for their different-size joint Gaussian result, while their Theorem 1.12 reproduces older covariance formulas. Fuchs (2008, Theorem 3) and Feng, Mahmoud and Panholzer (2008) establish univariate Poisson results, not the marked-field conclusion. The generic Poisson point-process bound from Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon (1989) does not itself supply the needed nested-fringe-tree overlap estimates. Targeted mathematical-literature searches and claim comparisons found no equivalent established theorem; this is a bounded historical comparison, not a uniqueness certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem resolves the explicitly stated 2010 joint-independence conjecture in the square-root critical range. It provides the stronger spatial placement law and a uniform \(O(n^{-1/2})\) total-variation error, valid for adjacent selected subtree sizes, which cannot follow merely from vanishing covariances or the known univariate Poisson theorems. This gives an explicit calibrated multiscale rare-fringe profile and a usable approximation across all rank windows simultaneously.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Closest is Dennert--Grübel (2010, Proposition 2.1 and Section 4.2); Holmgren--Janson (2014, Theorems 1.4 and 1.12) supplies one-size Poisson and exact covariance machinery; Fuchs (2008, Theorem 3) supplies another one-size approximation; Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon (1989, Theorem 2) supplies the general process bound. Full primary text of Dennert--Grübel, Holmgren--Janson and Fuchs was materially inspected. The Feng--Mahmoud--Panholzer article was assessed from its primary abstract, not falsely classified as fully read. Remaining risk: a different unlocated later paper might prove an equivalent joint critical limit; publication priority is not independently certified. The result is restricted to fixed finite sets of distinct sizes in a compact critical band, and only the discrete matching-intensity approximation has the stated total-variation rate.
