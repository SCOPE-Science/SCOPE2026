# Cusp and second-order tail of the sharp coherent-disagreement moment profile
## Finding
For \(\alpha>0\), let \(\mathcal C_{1/2}\) be the coherent laws generated by two posterior probabilities of the same Bernoulli event with prior \(1/2\), and define
\[
M(\alpha)=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal C_{1/2}}\int_{[0,1]^2}|x-y|^{\alpha}\,d\mu(x,y).
\]
Kravchenko's exact formula has a crossover \(\alpha_0\approx2.257507868922394\). The sharp value profile is continuous but has a genuine cusp there. If
\[
d_0=\frac{3\alpha_0+1-\sqrt{\alpha_0^2+6\alpha_0+1}}{2\alpha_0},
\]
then
\[
(\log M)'_-(\alpha_0)=-\log 2,\qquad (\log M)'_+(\alpha_0)=\log d_0,
\]
and hence the logarithmic slope jump is \(\log(2d_0)\approx0.391907779592366>0\). In addition,
\[
M(\alpha)=\frac{2}{e\alpha}\left(1-\frac{3}{2\alpha}+\frac{79}{24\alpha^2}+O(\alpha^{-3})\right)
\qquad(\alpha\to\infty).
\]
Thus the known first-order constant \(2/e\) has an explicit second-order approach, while the finite-exponent optimizer switch leaves a first-order nonsmooth signature in the exact optimal value.

## Assumptions and scope
A coherent pair is \(X=\mathbb P(E\mid\mathcal G)\), \(Y=\mathbb P(E\mid\mathcal H)\) for one event \(E\), with \(\mathbb P(E)=1/2\). The result concerns the sharp fixed-prior profile \(M(\alpha)\). It uses Kravchenko's global optimizer theorem as an independently published premise; it does not re-prove that theorem. The asymptotic statement is only for \(\alpha\to\infty\).

## Proof
Kravchenko's theorem gives
\[
M(\alpha)=2^{-\alpha}\quad(0<\alpha<\alpha_0),
\]
and, for \(\alpha\ge\alpha_0\),
\[
M(\alpha)=G(\alpha):=2a_\alpha d_\alpha^\alpha,
\]
where, with \(s_\alpha=\sqrt{\alpha^2+6\alpha+1}\),
\[
a_\alpha=\frac{\alpha+3-s_\alpha}{4},\qquad
d_\alpha=\frac{3\alpha+1-s_\alpha}{2\alpha}=\frac{1-2a_\alpha}{1-a_\alpha}.
\]
The parameter \(a_\alpha\) is the smaller root of \(2a^2-(\alpha+3)a+1=0\). Equivalently it is the interior stationary point of
\[
\log(2a)+\alpha\log\frac{1-2a}{1-a}.
\]
Consequently the chain-rule term through \(a_\alpha\) vanishes, giving the envelope identity
\[
(\log G)'(\alpha)=\log d_\alpha.
\]
Continuity at \(\alpha_0\) is part of the branch equality defining the crossover. The left logarithmic derivative is \(-\log2\), whereas the right one is \(\log d_0\). Since \(\alpha_0>2\) and
\[
d_\alpha>\frac12\iff 2\alpha+1>\sqrt{\alpha^2+6\alpha+1}\iff \alpha(3\alpha-2)>0,
\]
we have \(d_0>1/2\), so the two derivatives differ and the jump is \(\log(2d_0)>0\).

For the tail expansion put \(t=1/\alpha\) and \(S=\sqrt{1+6t+t^2}\). Direct algebra from the binomial series gives
\[
S=1+3t-4t^2+12t^3-44t^4+180t^5+O(t^6),
\]
so
\[
d_\alpha=1-t+2t^2-6t^3+22t^4-90t^5+O(t^6),
\]
and
\[
a_\alpha=t-3t^2+11t^3-45t^4+197t^5+O(t^6).
\]
Therefore
\[
\log d_\alpha=-t+\frac32t^2-\frac{13}3t^3+\frac{63}4t^4+O(t^5)
\]
and
\[
\log(a_\alpha/t)=-3t+\frac{13}2t^2-21t^3+O(t^4).
\]
Since \(G(\alpha)=2a_\alpha d_\alpha^\alpha\), these imply
\[
\log\frac{G(\alpha)}{2t/e}=-\frac32t+\frac{13}6t^2-\frac{21}4t^3+O(t^4).
\]
Exponentiating yields
\[
\frac{G(\alpha)}{2t/e}=1-\frac32t+\frac{79}{24}t^2-\frac{145}{16}t^3+O(t^4),
\]
which proves the stated expansion after discarding the displayed third correction.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` checks, using exact rational power-series arithmetic, the square-root series, the logarithmic series, and the coefficients \(-3/2\), \(79/24\), and \(-145/16\). It separately brackets the crossover numerically, evaluates \(d_0\), and confirms the positive slope jump. Those numerical evaluations corroborate the analytic proof; they are not used to establish the infinite asymptotic statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Cichomski and Osękowski proved the global first-order law \(\alpha\sup_{\mathcal C}\mathbb E|X-Y|^\alpha\to2/e\). Kravchenko later proved the exact fixed-prior \(1/2\) profile for every \(\alpha>0\), including the crossover \(\alpha_0\), and recovered the same first-order \(2/e\) asymptotic. The present result extracts two features not stated in those sources: the exact first-derivative cusp at the optimizer switch and the first two relative corrections to the high-moment tail. Arieli, Babichenko and Sandomirskiy cover the lower-branch regime used in Kravchenko's synthesis; that coverage does not imply the upper-branch derivative or the large-\(\alpha\) correction terms.

## Limitations
The result is an analytic refinement of an existing exact optimizer formula, not a new characterization of coherent laws. It is restricted to prior \(1/2\). The literature search found no statement of the cusp or the coefficients above, but short consequences of closed forms can be poorly indexed; an equivalent unpublished or unindexed derivation remains a residual originality risk.
