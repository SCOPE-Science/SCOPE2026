# Cyclic-quotient singularity baskets in the toric alpha-spectrum surface family

## Finding
Fix coprime positive integers \(B\le A\le 2B\). Consider the projective toric surface \(X_{A,B}\) associated to the fan of cones over proper faces of the lattice polygon
\[
Q_{A,B}=\{(d_0,d_1,d_2)\in\mathbb R^3:\ d_0+d_1+d_2=0,\ -A\le d_i\le B\}\subset N_{\mathbb R},\qquad N=\{d\in\mathbb Z^3:\textstyle\sum_i d_i=0\}.
\]
This is the two-dimensional subfamily of Wu's K-polystable toric \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano realizations; its alpha invariant is \(\alpha(X_{A,B})=B/(A+B)\).

**Complete singularity basket.** If \(A<2B\), write
\[
m_-=A(2B-A),\qquad m_+=B(2A-B).
\]
Choose integers \(s_-\) and \(s_+\) satisfying \(Bs_-\equiv-1\pmod A\) and \(As_+\equiv1\pmod B\). The three torus-fixed charts corresponding to the facets \(d_i=-A\) have cyclic quotient type \(\frac1{m_-}(1,q_-)\), and the three corresponding to \(d_i=B\) have type \(\frac1{m_+}(1,q_+)\), where
\[
q_-\equiv-1-(2B-A)s_-\pmod{m_-},\qquad
q_+\equiv-1+(2A-B)s_+\pmod{m_+}.
\]
For \(m_\pm>1\), take the unique representative \(1\le q_\pm<m_\pm\); it is coprime to \(m_\pm\). When \(m_\pm=1\) the corresponding chart is smooth and its quotient label is omitted. The types are independent of the chosen solutions of the congruences. These six charts contain all possible singularities. Thus \(X_{1,1}\) is smooth; for \(B\le A<2B\), every other allowed pair has exactly six singular points.

At the remaining endpoint \((A,B)=(2,1)\), the polygon has three vertices and \(X_{2,1}\) has exactly three singular points, all of type \(\frac13(1,2)\), equivalently du Val type \(A_2\). For every nontrivial chart \(\frac1m(1,q)\), the negative Hirzebruch--Jung continued fraction of \(m/q\) specifies the full exceptional-chain self-intersections of its minimal toric resolution. Therefore the formulas classify the entire resolution basket as well as the quotient-group orders.

For the infinite sequence \((A,B)=(B+1,B)\), \(B\ge2\), one has \(\alpha(X_{B+1,B})=B/(2B+1)\to1/2\), while the six cyclic quotient orders are three copies each of \(B^2-1\) and \(B^2+2B\). Thus quotient singularities of unbounded local group order occur arbitrarily close in alpha invariant to the smooth member \(X_{1,1}\) with \(\alpha=1/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); the chosen lattice is \(N\), identified with \(\mathbb Z^2\) by the first two coordinates. The fan contains the origin, the rays through all vertices of \(Q_{A,B}\), and the cones over edges of \(Q_{A,B}\). Cyclic quotient notation \(\frac1m(1,q)\) means the quotient of \(\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}\) by \(\mu_m\) acting with weights \((1,q)\), with \(\gcd(q,m)=1\). This theorem classifies singularities of precisely this toric surface family; it makes no assertion about general K-polystable surfaces or dimensions above two.

## Proof
First suppose \(A<2B\). Wu's explicit hyperplane-slice description of the polar polytope shows that the vertices of \(Q_{A,B}\) are exactly the six permutations of \((-A,B,A-B)\). Indeed, a vertex has at least two active coordinate bounds. Given the sum-zero constraint and \(B\le A<2B\), the remaining coordinate is necessarily \(A-B\), which lies in \((-A,B)\). These six vertices are distinct even when \(A=B\) (then they permute \((-A,0,B)\)). Each is a primitive lattice vector because \(\gcd(A,B)=1\).

Each edge is one of the six facets \(d_i=-A\) or \(d_i=B\). In lattice coordinates \((d_0,d_1)\), representative positively oriented pairs of primitive rays are
\[
(u_-,v_-)=((-A,B),(-A,A-B)),\qquad
(u_+,v_+)=((B,-A),(B,A-B)).
\]
Their determinants are, respectively, \(m_-=A(2B-A)\) and \(m_+=B(2A-B)\). Every edge lies in the permutation orbit of one of these two types, and coordinate permutations act by lattice automorphisms, so these determinants occur three times each. Every affine chart of a two-dimensional normal toric variety for a cone generated by primitive rays \(u,v\) is a cyclic quotient surface. The order of its quotient group is \(|\det(u,v)|\). The only potentially singular torus orbits of a normal toric surface are the zero-dimensional orbits; hence these six charts give the full singular locus.

To recover quotient *types* rather than only orders, let \(u,w\) be a positively oriented basis of the lattice with \(\det(u,w)=1\). If \(v=p u+m w\) and \(m>0\), then the affine toric cone has type \(\frac1m(1,-p\bmod m)\): in the index-\(m\) sublattice generated by \(u,v\), its smooth cover is \(\mathbb A^2\), and the deck-group generator \(w=(v-pu)/m\) acts with weights \((-p,1)\).

For the minus edge choose \(w_-=(s_-,t_-)\) with \(-A t_--B s_-=1\), which is possible exactly because \(Bs_-\equiv-1\pmod A\). Since \(p_-=\det(v_-,w_-)\), one has \(p_-=1+(2B-A)s_-\). Thus \(q_-\equiv-p_-\), giving the first formula. For the plus edge choose \(w_+=(s_+,t_+)\) with \(Bt_++As_+=1\), possible because \(As_+\equiv1\pmod B\). Then \(p_+=\det(v_+,w_+)=1-(2A-B)s_+\), giving the second formula. Altering either \(s_\pm\) by the modulus of its congruence changes \(p_\pm\) by a multiple of \(m_\pm\). Since the second primitive ray is primitive, \(\gcd(p_\pm,m_\pm)=1\).

In the strict case \(A>B\), coprimality forces \(B\ge2\), so both orders exceed one. If \(A=B\), coprimality forces \((A,B)=(1,1)\), and both determinants are one. At \(A=2B\), coprimality forces \((A,B)=(2,1)\), and \(Q_{2,1}\) is the triangle with vertices the permutations of \((-2,1,1)\). Any adjacent primitive pair has determinant three and can be transformed by a lattice unimodular map to \(((1,0),(1,3))\), so its quotient type is \(\frac13(1,2)\). There are precisely three torus-fixed charts.

Finally, the standard minimal-resolution theorem for cyclic quotient toric surfaces identifies the exceptional chain with the negative continued fraction of \(m/q\). For \(A=B+1\), direct substitution gives \(m_-=B^2-1\), \(m_+=B^2+2B\) and the displayed limit of the alpha invariant. This proves all assertions.

## Verification
`verify.py` enumerates the complete vertex sets, computes all facet-edge determinants, constructs unimodular bases using Bezout identities, checks the quotient residue formulas and primitivity, and verifies both boundary cases. It runs exact integer checks for all coprime parameters with \(1\le B\le80\), a total of 1967 pairs, without external packages. The program is a finite consistency check; the unrestricted theorem follows from the preceding symbolic argument.

For \((A,B)=(3,2)\), the basket is three \(\frac13(1,1)\) points and three \(\frac18(1,3)\) points. The corresponding minimal exceptional chains have self-intersections \([-3]\) and \([-3,-3]\), respectively. For \((A,B)=(2,1)\), all three chains are \([-2,-2]\).

## Relationship to prior work
Wu, *The alpha spectrum of K-polystable toric \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano varieties* (arXiv:2608.14115, first posted 2026-08-14), constructs \(Q_{n;A,B}\) and proves K-polystability and \(\alpha=B/(A+B)\). The later manuscript Section 5 gives vertex counts, Gorenstein and \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial criteria, and the **global** Weil class group, but does not classify the fixed-point cyclic quotient types or resolution baskets across its surface subfamily. Liu and Zhu (arXiv:2607.04005) provide the earlier alpha value \(2/(2n+1)\), which for \(n=2\) is the single parameter \((A,B)=(3,2)\). A published local-class-group computation for that special example gives local group orders three and eight; the present general formula agrees there, but determines quotient types and the entire two-parameter constrained family. Standard toric surface theory supplies the lattice-cone quotient algorithm and continued-fraction resolution interpretation, not the evaluated all-parameter basket.

## Limitations
The theorem is confined to \(n=2\) and the specified fan. It classifies geometric singularities, not deformation spaces, derived categories, or full integral cohomology. The Hirzebruch--Jung chain is specified algorithmically by an exact finite continued fraction rather than by a uniform closed list of its entries. The match to the existing \((3,2)\) calculation is a consistency comparison, not an independence claim.
