# Scientific review: derivation-rank polynomials of height-two incidence algebras

## Correctness — PASS

A complete derivation of the height-two incidence algebra has two uniquely specified field parameters on each arrow, one appearing as an inner idempotent perturbation and one as scalar action on the arrow. This is derived directly from the primitive-idempotent Leibniz identities, without requiring a characteristic restriction. Relative to the idempotent–arrow basis its operator matrix consists of a diagonally weighted oriented incidence block and a diagonal arrow block. Removing nonzero diagonal arrow pivots leaves a weighted incidence matrix on exactly the arrows for which the scalar action vanishes and the inner coefficient does not. Its rank over every field is the number of vertices minus the number of spanning connected components. Counting the three mutually exclusive edge states gives the exact rank polynomial; substituting into the subset expansion of the graphic Tutte polynomial gives the claimed closed specialization. Cyclic edge sets produce the first deviation from forest baseline precisely at degree one less than girth; shortest cycles contribute \((q-1)^g\) each, and no other edge state contributes to that coefficient. The unicyclic formula follows by correcting the unique dependent edge state.

The included `verify.py` is a direct finite checker, not a substitute for the general proof. It enumerates all derivation parameters over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) for selected graphs, constructs the resulting operator matrices, and independently computes their ranks. For a four-cycle it separately solves the entire linear Leibniz-equation space and confirms its dimension. The checker also verifies the shortest-cycle coefficient for several cyclic and acyclic graphs. It does not exhaust the infinite family or all prime-power fields; the proof uses only general field-linear algebra.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty

The closest antecedents are Kenneth Baclawski's 1972 characterization of incidence-algebra derivations and Zhankui Xiao's 2014 general normal-form results. Those structural lemmas encompass the existence and parameterization of derivations; this finding does not claim them as new. Neither inspected work develops the rank of each derivation as a linear operator, the exact finite-field distribution of those ranks, nor the induced Tutte specialization and girth-reconstruction coefficient. Krylov and Tuganbaev's later derivation-module study likewise concerns module decompositions and derivation classes rather than these enumerations. The standard graphic-matroid Tutte subset expansion is also pre-existing; novelty is asserted only for its precisely proved connection to the derivation-operator rank distribution and the resulting interpretation of the first cycle coefficient.

Statement-level checks distinguished the derivation-space dimension \(2|E|\) from the varying ranks \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}(\delta:A_G\to A_G)\). Likewise, graphical-group character-degree polynomials based on alternating matrices and quiver-extension bounds do not imply this associative-algebra derivation distribution. The published 1972, 2014, and 2024 source texts were examined for the actual formula and its equivalent formulations; searches for weighted incidence-rank distributions, Tutte specializations, finite-field tables, and shortest-cycle recovery did not disclose direct coverage. An equivalent result under a different name or in unexamined algebraic combinatorics literature remains a real residual risk; unsuccessful searches are not a proof of global publication priority.

## Value — PASS

The operator-rank spectrum is a natural invariant of the derivation representation of a finite-dimensional associative algebra, finer than the usual dimension of its derivation space. For an infinite family of incidence algebras, the finding gives every coefficient without enumerating the derivations individually and identifies exactly the underlying graph-matroid data controlling them. More importantly, it detects graph cycles and recovers two structurally important graph invariants — girth and number of shortest cycles — from algebraic operator ranks alone. The characteristic-free result is not an arbitrary small-instance census and gives a complete exact distribution across all simple bipartite height-two posets. It is mathematically motivated even though it does not assert graph reconstruction up to isomorphism or a general theorem for arbitrary posets.

## Limits and closest sources

The source-class date used in the accompanying record is the author-stamped public full-text upload of Baclawski's paper on 2014-06-07, not a fabricated exact original 1972 journal-release day. Xiao's arXiv first version is dated 2014-11-22. The absence of complete day-level publication data for the 1972 issue is recorded transparently. The general formula is proved for bipartite graphs without isolated vertices, not for arbitrary higher-height incidence algebras. Additional published characterizations and possible unindexed rank-generating formulations remain priority uncertainty.
