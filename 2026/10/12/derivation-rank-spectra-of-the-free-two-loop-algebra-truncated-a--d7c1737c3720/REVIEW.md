# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Applying Leibniz to the relation \(xyx=0\) eliminates all possible constant terms, independently of characteristic; every augmentation-ideal assignment to the two generators then gives a derivation. The resulting twelve-parameter block matrix has a tensor-square block whose rank is classified by a complete disjoint enumeration of \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices. The quotient-space counting lemma proves the Gaussian rank multiplicities, and their sums are \(q^{12}\). Direct independent Leibniz-system checks and exhaustive finite-field operator enumeration corroborate the proof, without being used to establish the infinite family.

**Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk.** The closest inspected sources are Strametz (2001, arXiv:math/0111060), Xu–Han–Jiang (2005, arXiv:math/0509202) and Sánchez-Flores (2010, arXiv:1004.2820). Their full-text material treats the Lie structure and dimensions of Hochschild cohomology, and the radical-square-zero loop setting; none of the inspected theorems states or supplies an equal-or-stronger operator-rank distribution for the seven-dimensional length-three algebra. The known parameterizations are acknowledged. The risk of an unindexed equivalent finite-field enumeration remains.

**Value — PASS.** The complete multiplicity distribution is a natural invariant of the smallest free two-generator associative algebra retaining all quadratic noncommutative words. It describes how many derivations have each kernel dimension and produces a sharp, structural collapse of the maximum possible rank in characteristic two. It cannot be recovered from the derivation-space dimension or from the commutative jet-algebra spectrum; the tensor-square action is essential.

**Limits.** The result does not classify conjugacy orbits of derivations, longer truncations, or arbitrary quivers. Exhaustive checks are finite; the all-fields assertion relies on the proof. A separate scholarly priority review remains useful.
