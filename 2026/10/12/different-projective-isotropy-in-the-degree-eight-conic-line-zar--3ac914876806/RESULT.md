# Different projective isotropy in the degree-eight conic-line Zariski pair
## Finding
For the explicit degree-eight conic-line arrangements of Amram--Shwartz--Sinichkin--Tan--Tokunaga,
\[
B_1=C_1+C_2+C_3+L_1+L_2,
\qquad
B_2=C_1+C_2+C_3+L_1+L_3,
\]
with
\[
C_1:X^2+Y^2-Z^2=0,\quad
C_2:\frac{X^2}{3}+Y^2-Z^2=0,\quad
C_3:X^2+\frac{Y^2}{3}-Z^2=0,
\]
and
\[
L_1:Y-X-2Z=0,\qquad L_2:Y+X+2Z=0,\qquad L_3:Y-X+2Z=0,
\]
the full projective stabilizers are
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb P^2}(B_1)\cong C_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb P^2}(B_2)\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]
More explicitly, the first group is generated by
\[
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[X:-Y:Z],
\]
while the second is generated by
\[
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[Y:X:Z]
\quad\text{and}\quad
[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[-X:-Y:Z].
\]
Hence the two members of this Zariski pair, although of the same combinatorial type, have different projective isotropy orders, namely \(2\) and \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The equations and the tangency pattern are those of Construction 2.2 of arXiv:2106.03507v1. A projective automorphism of an arrangement means an element of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb C)\) preserving the reduced union setwise; it may permute irreducible components of the same degree.

The claim concerns only these two displayed members. It does not classify the full realization space of their combinatorial type and does not assert that projective isotropy is a topological invariant.

## Proof
A projective transformation preserves the degrees of irreducible components, so an automorphism of either \(B_i\) permutes the three conics and separately the two lines. Among the three conics, \(C_1\) is characterized intrinsically by the published incidence pattern: it is tangent to both of the other conics, whereas \(C_2\) and \(C_3\) meet transversely. Therefore every arrangement automorphism fixes \(C_1\) and preserves the unordered pair \(\{C_2,C_3\}\).

Write the conics as quadratic forms with matrices
\[
Q_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),\quad
Q_2=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac13,1,-1\right),\quad
Q_3=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(1,\frac13,-1\right).
\]
First suppose that a projective transformation represented by \(T\in\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb C)\) fixes each of \(C_1,C_2,C_3\). Then there are nonzero scalars \(\lambda,\mu\) with
\[
T^{\mathsf T}Q_1T=\lambda Q_1,
\qquad
T^{\mathsf T}Q_2T=\mu Q_2.
\]
Consequently
\[
T^{-1}(Q_1^{-1}Q_2)T=\frac{\mu}{\lambda}Q_1^{-1}Q_2.
\]
The spectrum of \(Q_1^{-1}Q_2\) is \(\{1/3,1,1\}\), with multiplicities. The only scalar multiple having the same spectrum is itself, so \(\mu=\lambda\). Hence \(T\) commutes with \(Q_1^{-1}Q_2\) and preserves its one-dimensional \(1/3\)-eigenspace, the \(X\)-axis. Applying the same argument to \(Q_3\) forces preservation of the \(Y\)-axis. Thus \(T\) is diagonal. The equation \(T^{\mathsf T}Q_1T=\lambda Q_1\) then gives equal squares of its three diagonal entries, so projectively \(T\) is represented by a coordinate sign change.

Therefore the subgroup fixing \(C_2\) and \(C_3\) individually is the Klein four group of coordinate sign changes modulo overall scalar. The coordinate swap
\[
S:[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[Y:X:Z]
\]
fixes \(C_1\) and interchanges \(C_2\) and \(C_3\). It follows that the full stabilizer of the unordered conic triple is an order-eight group
\[
H\cong (C_2\times C_2)\rtimes C_2\cong D_8.
\]
Thus there are no projective symmetries of either arrangement outside these eight candidates.

To finish, apply the eight elements of \(H\) to the four common tangent lines
\[
L_1:Y-X-2Z=0,\quad L_2:Y+X+2Z=0,\quad L_3:Y-X+2Z=0,\quad L_4:Y+X-2Z=0.
\]
The setwise stabilizer of \(\{L_1,L_2\}\) consists exactly of the identity and \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[X:-Y:Z]\). Hence \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb P^2}(B_1)\cong C_2\).

The setwise stabilizer of \(\{L_1,L_3\}\) consists exactly of the identity, \(S\), the involution
\[
N:[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[-X:-Y:Z],
\]
and \(SN\). The two generators \(S\) and \(N\) commute and are distinct involutions, so this stabilizer is \(C_2\times C_2\). This proves the claim.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. It checks that no nontrivial scalar preserves the generalized-eigenvalue spectrum \(\{1/3,1,1\}\), enumerates the eight sign/swap elements of the conic-triple stabilizer, computes their induced permutation of the four tangent lines, and returns exactly two elements stabilizing \(\{L_1,L_2\}\) and four stabilizing \(\{L_1,L_3\}\).

The exhaustive finite check is used only after the symbolic argument has reduced the full projective stabilizer to the eight-element group \(H\); it is not used to infer completeness from a bounded search in \(\operatorname{PGL}_3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Construction 2.2 of Amram--Shwartz--Sinichkin--Tan--Tokunaga gives these exact equations, proves the stated conic tangencies, and uses fundamental groups to show that \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) form a Zariski pair. The source does not state their projective automorphism groups. Full-text searches of that paper for “automorphism”, “projective transformation”, and “symmetry” returned no occurrences.

A later paper of Bannai--Tokunaga on strong Ziegler pairs cites arXiv:2106.03507 and studies related conic-line Zariski phenomena through Jacobian-syzygy data; its inspected text likewise contains no automorphism computation. Searches in published-finding corpus for the exact source, equations, projective stabilizers, and aliases returned only stabilizer computations for different arrangements.

The present statement is therefore a geometric isotropy refinement of the explicit pair: the two same-combinatorics members have different stabilizer orders in the projective moduli problem.

## Limitations
The result is specific to the displayed arrangements and their projective embeddings. It does not compute the abstract automorphism group of the full combinatorial incidence structure, nor does it determine stabilizers of every point of the realization space. The originality comparison is limited by terminology: an older source could encode the same finite symmetry calculation without using “projective automorphism” or “stabilizer”.
