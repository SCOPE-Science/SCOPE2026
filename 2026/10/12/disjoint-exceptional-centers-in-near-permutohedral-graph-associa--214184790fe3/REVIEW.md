# Review of the disjoint-exceptional-center theorem

## Correctness — PASS

With lattice \(\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\), every nonempty proper subset labels a braid-fan ray; a ray is contracted precisely when its induced subgraph is disconnected. Two such divisors intersect if and only if their subsets are comparable. A complement of maximum degree one has only disconnected two-element subsets; a nonmatching complement has two missing edges sharing a vertex and therefore nested disconnected two- and three-element subsets (the three-element set is proper because the original graph is connected). When the complement is a matching, adjoining each missing pair gives a star subdivision at the cone generated by the two singleton rays. Direct inspection of the star quotient identifies the center's fan with the braid fan on the remaining \(n-2\) vertices, and distinct centers cannot meet. The codimension-two integral blowup formula gives the claimed Eulerian difference. The smallest case \(n=3\) has point centers and is included. Complete finite regression tests checked 770 connected graphs and 16 independent nested-tubing Betti profiles; these tests are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS with explicit residual risk

Compared implications, not just title strings. Postnikov–Reiner–Williams provide generic nested-set and \(h\)-vector machinery; these results recover the two ambient fans and permit an indirect calculation but do not themselves state which graph contractions have pairwise disjoint exceptional prime divisors, or identify all smooth centers uniformly. Da Rosa–Jensen–Ranganathan supply projective-space blowup realizations and the Losev–Manin connection, not this disjoint-center criterion for the canonical intermediate contraction. Suyama classifies Fano and weak Fano graphical toric varieties rather than these exceptional loci. A published mathematical record on acyclic orientation imbalance of complete graphs minus a matching treats a different invariant and does not imply toric blowup centers or integral Betti numbers. The residual novelty risk is an unindexed direct consequence in the literature on nested-fan contractions; this review does not certify worldwide uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The characterization selects **all** connected graphs for which the natural permutohedral-to-graph-associahedral birational contraction has nonintersecting exceptional divisors, not an arbitrary slice. It identifies each center geometrically as a smaller permutohedral variety and determines the full integral Betti profile, Euler characteristic and Picard rank across an infinite family by a uniform, reusable blowup factorization. This answers a natural birational and cohomological question motivated by the relationship of permutohedral toric spaces and graph associahedra with Losev–Manin compactifications. Standard fan and blowup facts are credited, and the new value is confined to the explicit classification and its structural consequences.

## Constraints and remaining risks

The result applies to the standard graphical nested fans over \(\mathbb C\); no cohomology-ring multiplication or arbitrary nonmatching contraction is classified. Generic published nestohedral formulas are stronger in scope for computing individual face vectors, but not a substitute for the stated disjoint-exceptional-locus characterization. The tests are finite. The originality conclusion is a literature-supported self-review only.
