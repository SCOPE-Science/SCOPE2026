# Distance-two extremizers among maximum binary length-nine non-overlapping codes
## Finding
Among binary length-nine non-overlapping (cross-bifix-free) codes of maximum cardinality \(14\), the largest possible minimum Hamming distance is \(2\). Exactly four labeled maximum codes have minimum Hamming distance \(2\), while the other \(1{,}148\) maximum codes have minimum distance \(1\). The four distance-two codes form a single orbit under the natural order-four action generated by global bit complementation and reversal of all coordinates.

A representative of the unique distance-two orbit is
\[
C=\{\texttt{000001111}, \texttt{000010111}, \texttt{000011011}, \texttt{000011101}, \texttt{000100111}, \texttt{000101011}, \texttt{000101101}, \texttt{000110011}, \texttt{000110101}, \texttt{001000111}, \texttt{001001011}, \texttt{001001101}, \texttt{001010011}, \texttt{001010101}\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A binary length-nine code is a subset \(C\subseteq\{{0,1\}}^9\). It is non-overlapping, equivalently cross-bifix-free, when no nonempty proper prefix of any codeword equals a suffix of any codeword, including the same codeword. The Hamming distance \(d_H(x,y)\) counts coordinates in which \(x\) and \(y\) differ, and the minimum distance is \(d(C)=\min_{{x
e y\in C}}d_H(x,y)\). The statement concerns only codes having the known maximum cardinality \(14\).

## Proof
Form the compatibility graph whose vertices are all bifix-free binary words of length nine and whose edges join mutually cross-bifix-free words. Direct generation gives \(148\) vertices. A code is non-overlapping exactly when its words form a clique.

The published exact computation of Chee, Kiah, Purkayastha, and Wang gives maximum clique size \(14\) at binary length nine. The bundled verifier independently constructs the same compatibility graph and exhaustively enumerates every size-\(14\) clique by an increasing-index recursive intersection search, so each clique is visited exactly once. It obtains \(1{,}152\) maximum codes. Computing \(d(C)\) for each gives the complete histogram
\[
1:1148,\qquad 2:4.
\]
Therefore the largest minimum Hamming distance within the cardinality-optimal layer is exactly \(2\).

Global bit complementation and coordinate reversal preserve both the non-overlap condition and Hamming distance. Applying the four resulting transformations to any of the four distance-two codes yields all four of them, with no nonidentity transformation fixing the displayed representative. Hence the four codes are one orbit of size \(4\).

## Verification
The standalone file `verify.py` generates all \(2^9\) binary words, filters the \(148\) bifix-free words, constructs all \(3{,}080\) compatibility edges, enumerates all \(1{,}152\) size-\(14\) cliques, recomputes every pairwise Hamming distance, and checks closure of the four distance-two codes under complement and reversal. The replay used for this result reported \(1{,}148\) maximum codes of minimum distance \(1\), four of minimum distance \(2\), and one order-four orbit among the latter.

## Relationship to prior work
Chee, Kiah, Purkayastha, and Wang define the same cross-bifix-free object and report the exact binary length-nine optimum \(14\), proved computationally by a maximum-clique search; their paper also motivates Hamming distance as relevant to synchronization in the presence of symbol errors. Stanovnik, Moškon, and Mraz later computed the full set of optimum solutions and the number of maximum non-overlapping codes for small parameters, including this regime. Their inspected article contains no Hamming-distance refinement. Blackburn uses the equivalent term “non-overlapping code” and supplies the standard alias and general framework. Thus the new statement is not the known optimum or the known count of optima, but the exact Hamming-distance refinement of that optimum layer.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact result only for the binary length-nine, size-\(14\) layer. Minimum distance \(2\) detects but does not correct an arbitrary single substitution, and no stronger synchronization-under-noise guarantee is asserted. The later optimum-solution data make the refinement mechanically derivable in principle; the inspected publications do not state the distance histogram or the unique symmetry orbit. An unindexed computation of the same secondary invariant remains a residual originality risk.
