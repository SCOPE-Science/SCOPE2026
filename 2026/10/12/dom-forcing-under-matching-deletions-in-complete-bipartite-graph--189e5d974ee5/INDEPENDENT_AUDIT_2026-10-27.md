## correctness

PASS

For 2≤m≤n, 0≤t≤m and connected K_{m,n}−M_t (only (2,2,2) excluded), each blue vertex has either p or p−1 white cross-shore neighbors when p vertices of A are white; hence any p≥3 cannot decrease, and likewise q≤2 in any successful zero-forcing initial complement. For t=0, three whites cannot all be removed, while a two-white A/B pair is forceable. For t=1, the sole missing edge permits three but not four whites: with a (2,2) white profile, a first force must use the single deleted pair and subsequently strands two whites on the opposite shore. For t≥2, four whites are forceable by {a1,a2,b1,b3}, through b2→a1→b3→a2→b1 (interpreting the middle terms as successive forces), giving Z=m+n−2−min(t,2). Domination at the Z threshold is obtained by explicitly complementary white sets except (2,2,1), m=2,t=2,n≥3, and m=n=3,t∈{2,3}. In m=2,t=2, any Z-set has no selected A vertex, leaving two undominated B vertices; in m=n=3,t=2 or 3, any dominating A/B selected pair must have two white neighbors per selected vertex, so forcing cannot start. Presented Z+1 witnesses dominate and force in each exception. All proof witnesses and lower-bound regimes were checked independently. These are infinite proof arguments, not inferred from finite exhaustive runs.

## value

PASS

Simultaneous domination and zero forcing is a natural hybrid invariant, and complete bipartite graphs are one of its foundational exact families. Deleting a matching is a canonical degree-balanced sparse defect: it is not an arbitrary slice. The result gives the full sharp transition as defect size t changes (two unit decreases in Z and subsequent saturation), together with a complete three-regime equality/failure classification for F_d=Z including small-shore obstructions. This structural robustness law is useful for interpreting how zero-forcing dynamics interact with static domination after sparse edge failure, and goes materially beyond recomputing a known table. Its individual standard-graph examples and t=0 case are not claimed as separate new results.
