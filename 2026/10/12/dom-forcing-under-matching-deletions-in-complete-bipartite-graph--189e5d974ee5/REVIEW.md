# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces ordinary zero forcing on \(K_{m,n}-M_t\) to the number of white vertices on each shore. Since any vertex loses at most one cross-shore neighbor, three white vertices on one shore can never be reduced. The cases \(t=0\), \(t=1\), and \(t\ge2\) then give sharp white-set maxima \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), respectively, with explicit forcing chains. The domination step is checked by explicit minimum witnesses outside the three exceptional low-shore regimes and by direct structural obstructions inside them. Exhaustive computation on 58 connected small profiles and 681 explicit-witness checks agrees with the theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating dom-forcing paper proves the undeleted complete-bipartite case \(F_d(K_{m,n})=m+n-2\), while its inspected full text contains no matching-deleted biclique theorem. A later forcing-and-domination paper gives the general equivalence \(F_d(G)=Z(G)\) iff a minimum zero-forcing set dominates, but does not determine \(Z\) or that existence question for \(K_{m,n}-M_t\). Targeted database and web searches under dom-forcing, zero forcing, matching-deleted complete bipartite graphs, and the notation \(K_{m,n}-M_t\) found no equivalent classification. Known isolated identifications with \(P_4\), \(C_6\), and \(Q_3\), and the undeleted \(t=0\) case, are explicitly treated as prior coverage rather than new subclaims.

## Value
PASS. The source literature makes complete bipartite graphs a basic exact family for the new invariant. Deleting a matching is the canonical degree-balanced defect of a biclique, and the theorem gives a complete robustness law rather than a single incremental case: the forcing threshold falls at the first two defects and then saturates, while domination disagrees with forcing only at exactly three low-shore boundaries. This isolates the mechanism behind the parameter change and supplies a reusable structural test for the full matching-defect family.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are the complete-bipartite theorem in Susanth–Dominic–Premodkumar and the general equality criterion in Jiang–Wan. Neither implies the matching-defect formula without the new shore-count analysis. The theorem does not extend to arbitrary deleted-edge sets, where a vertex can lose more than one neighbor and the proof's key bound fails. A residual risk remains that an equivalent matching-defect formula exists in literature indexed under different terminology.
