# Scientific review: star partial-transformation domain-rank census

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the full monoid of partial self-maps that carry inverse images of graph faces to faces or the empty set, not the forward graph-homomorphism monoid or the strict-continuity submonoid. For every integer \(n\ge1\), the proof partitions every map by whether its image contains the target center, then by the fiber of the source center or target center. The six parts are mutually disjoint and exhaust all possibilities. Their generating functions are partial-injection polynomials or small monomials. Inverse-image constraints are checked in both directions, and the totals agree with direct exhaustive verification for \(1\le n\le5\). Boundary \(n=1\), empty map, and the two-point center fiber are included. No finite experiment is used as the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with named residual access risk
The foundational Margolis–Rhodes paper (arXiv:2005.02606, published 2021), inspected in author-posted full text, gives definitions and the general singular/injective decomposition in Section 2 (Lemmas 2.5–2.6) and enumerates different graph families/examples; it does not present the star domain-rank polynomial. Their 2023 continuation, inspected through its relevant introduction and examples, concerns semigroup complexity and does not present the formula. The relevant accessible abstract for arXiv:2609.39370v1 (September 30, 2026) specifies enumerations for paths and cycles, not stars. Searches for equivalent forms under inverse-image-of-edge continuity, partial transformations, graph monoids, and rook/partial-injection polynomial terminology found no stronger star result, including published mathematical-result records. Distinct objects such as monotone maps on finite weak orders do not imply the claim. The inaccessible full text of the new preprint is a real uncertainty: the accessible abstract cannot exclude an unadvertised star calculation, so novelty is provisional in that specific respect. An equivalent older enumeration remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The recent path/cycle program makes the star—the basic family with arbitrarily large degree but only two graph-distance layers—a canonical complementary test case. The result completely counts a natural infinite family of transformation monoids by **both** domain and rank, not only their orders, and identifies exactly how branch geometry constrains singular fibers. These full coefficients provide inputs for later rank stratification and probability questions. It is not an arbitrary finite slice, a renamed known sequence, or solely a computational census; a uniform structural proof establishes all \(n\).

## Constraints and limitations
The theorem does not determine Green classes or the Krohn–Rhodes complexity; it is limited to stars. Publicly accessible text of arXiv:2609.39370v1 was restricted to abstract/metadata during literature verification; full-text equivalence remains the chief originality risk. Exhaustive cases through five leaves are corroboration only. No independent audit was performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
