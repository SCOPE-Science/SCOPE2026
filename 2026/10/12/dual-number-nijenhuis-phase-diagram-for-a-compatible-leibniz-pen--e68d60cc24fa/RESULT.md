# Dual-number Nijenhuis phase diagram for a compatible Leibniz pencil

## Finding

Let \(K\) be a field and let
\[
L=Ke_1\oplus Ke_2.
\]
For
\[
(u,v)\in K^2
\]
define a bilinear operation \(\mu_{u,v}\) by
\[
\mu_{u,v}(e_2,e_1)=u e_1,
\qquad
\mu_{u,v}(e_2,e_2)=v e_1,
\]
with all remaining products of basis elements equal to zero.

The full two-dimensional space
\[
\mathcal P=\{\mu_{u,v}:(u,v)\in K^2\}
\]
is a compatible left-Leibniz pencil: every linear combination of two members is again a left Leibniz bracket.

The member
\[
\mu_{1,1}
\]
is exactly the two-dimensional Leibniz algebra used in Example 2.2 of Mondal and Saha.

For a nonzero parameter
\[
(u,v)\ne(0,0)
\]
and a linear map
\[
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the Nijenhuis condition is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{c=0}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
(a-d)\bigl(v(a-d)-ub\bigr)=0.
}
\]

Consequently the projectivized pencil has one exceptional Nijenhuis fiber. At
\[
[0:1]\in\mathbf P^1(K),
\]
the Nijenhuis locus is the single affine plane
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_0=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
0&a
\end{pmatrix}
:a,b\in K
\right\}.
}
\]

For every
\[
[1:t]\in\mathbf P^1(K),
\]
the Nijenhuis locus is the union
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_0\cup\mathcal P_t,
}
\]
where
\[
\mathcal P_t=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
a&t(a-d)\\
0&d
\end{pmatrix}
:a,d\in K
\right\}.
\]
The two planes meet in the scalar line
\[
\{aI:a\in K\}.
\]

Over every finite field \(\mathbf F_q\), the exceptional projective fiber has
\[
\boxed{q^2}
\]
Nijenhuis operators, with rank distribution
\[
\boxed{
(1,\ q-1,\ q(q-1))
}
\]
in ranks \(0,1,2\).

Every other projective fiber has
\[
\boxed{2q^2-q}
\]
operators, with rank distribution
\[
\boxed{
(1,\ 3(q-1),\ 2(q-1)^2).
}
\]

There are \(q\) generic projective parameters and one exceptional parameter, so the complete projective incidence set
\[
\{([u:v],N):N\text{ is Nijenhuis for }\mu_{u,v}\}
\]
has exactly
\[
\boxed{2q^3}
\]
points.

There is also a basis-independent compatible-pair statement. If two parameter vectors
\[
(u,v),\qquad (u',v')
\]
are linearly independent, then an endomorphism is Nijenhuis for both brackets if and only if
\[
\boxed{
N=aI+bE_{12}.
}
\]
Thus the common Nijenhuis operators of any basis of the compatible pencil form the commutative algebra
\[
\boxed{
K[I,E_{12}]
\cong
K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
}
\]

For
\[
N=aI+bE_{12},
\]
the induced Nijenhuis bracket satisfies
\[
(\mu_{u,v})_N=\mu_{au,\,bu+av}.
\]
Identifying the parameter pair \((u,v)\) with the dual number
\[
u+v\varepsilon,
\]
this is exactly multiplication by
\[
a+b\varepsilon.
\]

Finally, over every extension field
\[
\mathbf F_{q^m},
\]
the projective incidence count is
\[
2q^{3m}.
\]
Hence the Hasse–Weil zeta function of the reduced projective-incidence set is
\[
\boxed{
Z(T)=\frac{1}{(1-q^3T)^2}.
}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Bhutia, Yadav, and Behera define compatible Hom–Leibniz algebras and define a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible pair to mean a map that is Nijenhuis for both brackets. Their Proposition 5.2 states equivalently that such a map is Nijenhuis for every linear combination of the two brackets.

The present pencil uses the untwisted specialization
\[
\alpha=I,
\]
so it is a compatible Hom–Leibniz algebra in their sense whenever the two chosen parameters span the pencil.

Mondal and Saha study Nijenhuis operators on left Leibniz algebras. Their Example 2.2 is
\[
[e_2,e_1]=[e_2,e_2]=e_1,
\]
which is \(\mu_{1,1}\) here. Their Example 2.10 classifies the Nijenhuis operators of that single fiber.

The two cited papers assume characteristic zero. The theorem here is reconstructed directly from polynomial identities and is valid over every field.

The zeta function is for the reduced field-valued incidence set; no claim is made about a nonreduced scheme structure.

## Proof

For
\[
x=x_1e_1+x_2e_2,
\qquad
y=y_1e_1+y_2e_2,
\]
the bracket is
\[
\mu_{u,v}(x,y)
=
x_2(uy_1+vy_2)e_1.
\]

For any
\[
x,y,z\in L,
\]
one has
\[
\mu_{u,v}\bigl(x,\mu_{u,v}(y,z)\bigr)
=
u x_2y_2(uz_1+vz_2)e_1.
\]
The first term on the right side of the left Leibniz identity vanishes because
\[
\mu_{u,v}(x,y)\in Ke_1,
\]
while
\[
\mu_{u,v}\bigl(y,\mu_{u,v}(x,z)\bigr)
=
u y_2x_2(uz_1+vz_2)e_1.
\]
Therefore every \(\mu_{u,v}\) is a left Leibniz bracket. Since the formula is linear in \((u,v)\), every linear combination stays in the same family, proving compatibility.

Now let
\[
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b\\
c&d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Nijenhuis torsion is
\[
T_N(x,y)
=
\mu_{u,v}(Nx,Ny)
-
N\!\left(
\mu_{u,v}(x,Ny)
+
\mu_{u,v}(Nx,y)
-
N\mu_{u,v}(x,y)
\right).
\]

Evaluation on the four ordered basis pairs gives equations generated by
\[
uc^2=0,
\qquad
vc^2=0,
\]
and, after \(c=0\),
\[
(a-d)\bigl(v(a-d)-ub\bigr)=0.
\]
Because \((u,v)\ne(0,0)\) and \(K\) is a field, the first two equations imply
\[
c=0.
\]
This proves the displayed criterion.

If
\[
u=0,
\]
then \(v\ne0\), so the second equation reduces to
\[
(a-d)^2=0.
\]
Hence
\[
a=d
\]
and the fiber is \(\mathcal P_0\).

If
\[
u\ne0,
\]
scale the projective parameter to
\[
[1:t],
\qquad
t=\frac vu.
\]
The equation factors as
\[
(a-d)\bigl(t(a-d)-b\bigr)=0,
\]
which gives the two planes \(\mathcal P_0\) and \(\mathcal P_t\). Their intersection has
\[
a=d,\qquad b=0,\qquad c=0,
\]
so it is the scalar line.

Over \(\mathbf F_q\), \(\mathcal P_0\) contains \(q^2\) matrices. Its zero matrix is the only rank-zero element; the \(q-1\) matrices with
\[
a=0,\qquad b\ne0
\]
have rank \(1\), and all \(q(q-1)\) matrices with \(a\ne0\) are invertible.

For a generic projective parameter, a change of variables identifies the second plane with the diagonal plane. Its rank profile is
\[
(1,\ 2(q-1),\ (q-1)^2).
\]
The intersection scalar line has rank profile
\[
(1,\ 0,\ q-1).
\]
Rankwise inclusion–exclusion with \(\mathcal P_0\) gives
\[
(1,\ 3(q-1),\ 2(q-1)^2).
\]

The total projective-incidence count is therefore
\[
q^2+q(2q^2-q)=2q^3.
\]

Now let two projective parameters be distinct. If one is \([0:1]\), its Nijenhuis locus is already \(\mathcal P_0\). Otherwise write them as
\[
[1:s],\qquad[1:t],
\qquad s\ne t.
\]
A matrix lying in both second planes would satisfy
\[
b=s(a-d)=t(a-d).
\]
Thus
\[
a=d,\qquad b=0,
\]
which lies in \(\mathcal P_0\). Consequently the intersection of the two complete Nijenhuis loci is exactly \(\mathcal P_0\).

Since
\[
E_{12}^2=0,
\]
the plane \(\mathcal P_0\) is the algebra
\[
K[I,E_{12}]
\cong
K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]

Finally, for
\[
N=aI+bE_{12},
\]
direct substitution into the induced bracket
\[
\mu_N(x,y)
=
\mu(x,Ny)+\mu(Nx,y)-N\mu(x,y)
\]
gives
\[
(\mu_{u,v})_N(e_2,e_1)=au\,e_1
\]
and
\[
(\mu_{u,v})_N(e_2,e_2)=(bu+av)e_1.
\]
Thus
\[
(u,v)\mapsto(au,bu+av),
\]
which is dual-number multiplication.

## Relationship to prior work

Bhutia, Yadav, and Behera provide the compatible Hom–Leibniz Nijenhuis framework and prove that common Nijenhuis operators are exactly those that remain Nijenhuis for every linear combination of a compatible pair. Their inspected full text contains no two-dimensional worked classification.

Mondal and Saha give the single Leibniz algebra
\[
\mu_{1,1}
\]
as Example 2.2 and classify its Nijenhuis operators in Example 2.10. Their result is exactly one generic fiber of the phase diagram above:
\[
c=0,\qquad
a=d
\quad\text{or}\quad
a-d=b.
\]
It does not give the exceptional projective fiber, the full compatible-pencil incidence, the common Nijenhuis algebra of two independent brackets, or the dual-number action on pencil parameters.

A current independent calculation of the \(\mu_{1,1}\) fiber reproduces that single-fiber classification and studies its automorphism orbits. The present theorem treats the entire compatible pencil and uses that single fiber only as a prior boundary check.

Targeted semantic-literature searches for the exact two-dimensional pencil, common compatible Nijenhuis operators, dual-number formulations, and finite-field counts did not locate the broader phase diagram.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the particular two-dimensional compatible pencil generated by
\[
[e_2,e_1]=e_1
\]
and
\[
[e_2,e_2]=e_1.
\]

It does not classify all compatible Leibniz pencils in dimension two.

The finite-field incidence zeta function is for the reduced set of field-valued pairs.

The generic fiber \([1:1]\) is already covered in the cited single-algebra literature; originality concerns the full pencil, its exceptional fiber, its common-Nijenhuis intersection, and the dual-number parameter action.

Failed searches do not prove absolute novelty.
