# Equality-case census for binary length-six three-symbol MDS codes
## Finding
For a binary word \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_5)\), write its cyclic three-symbol read vector as the six triples \((x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2})\), with indices modulo \(6\). The three-symbol distance \(d_3(x,y)\) is the Hamming distance between those two six-triple read vectors.

Among codes \(C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^6\) satisfying \(d_3(x,y)\ge 5\) for all distinct \(x,y\in C\), the maximum size is \(16\). There are exactly \(76\) labeled maximum codes. Under the explicitly stated \(768\)-element isometry subgroup generated by all translations \(x\mapsto x+t\), \(t\in\mathbb F_2^6\), together with the twelve coordinate symmetries of the six-cycle (six rotations and six reflections), the \(76\) maxima split into five orbits of sizes \(4,4,12,24,32\). Exactly \(44\) maxima are affine \(4\)-flats in \(\mathbb F_2^6\); all \(32\) nonlinear maxima form the unique orbit of size \(32\).

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\), the block length is exactly \(6\), the read-window width is exactly \(3\), and windows are cyclic. The code is not assumed linear or affine. “Labeled” means that the six coordinates and the two alphabet symbols are fixed. The stated \(768\)-element subgroup is an explicitly verified subgroup of the full metric-isometry group; no claim is made that it is the full automorphism group.

A code is called affine here when it is a translate of a linear subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^6\).

## Proof
First, \(|C|\le 16\) follows directly. If two binary words agree on any fixed block of four consecutive coordinates, then the two cyclic three-symbol windows wholly contained in that four-coordinate block are equal. Their three-symbol distance is therefore at most \(4\), contradicting \(d_3\ge5\). Projection onto those four fixed coordinates is consequently injective on \(C\), giving \(|C|\le2^4=16\).

The equality cases are finite and were exhaustively enumerated from first principles. The verifier constructs all \(64\) binary words and joins two words exactly when their cyclic three-symbol distance is at least \(5\). The resulting compatibility graph has \(1{,}632\) edges. A complete Bron--Kerbosch maximal-clique enumeration has \(100{,}772\) maximal cliques, with size histogram
\[
8:10712,\quad 9:33216,\quad 10:23936,\quad 11:19200,\quad 12:10688,\quad 13:2304,\quad 14:640,\quad 16:76.
\]
Thus precisely \(76\) labeled codes attain size \(16\). A second increasing-order fixed-cardinality search independently enumerates the same \(76\) sixteen-cliques.

For symmetry, the verifier constructs all \(64\) translations and all \(12\) rotations/reflections of the coordinate six-cycle, verifies that all \(768\) resulting maps are distinct and preserve \(d_3\) on every unordered pair of words, and computes their action on all \(76\) maxima. The resulting orbit sizes are exactly \(4,4,12,24,32\).

Finally, for each maximum \(C\), the verifier translates one codeword to zero and checks closure under binary addition. Exactly \(44\) maxima pass, so exactly \(32\) are nonlinear. Orbitwise checking shows that the nonlinear maxima are precisely the single orbit of size \(32\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and only the standard library. The expected final line is recorded in `VERIFY_OUTPUT.txt`. The program rebuilds the metric and graph rather than reading a stored graph or stored list of maxima. It checks the upper-bound projection on every enumerated maximum, recomputes the complete maximal-clique census, independently recomputes every size-\(16\) clique, checks every declared isometry on every unordered word pair, recomputes all five orbits, and rechecks affine closure code by code.

## Relationship to prior work
Yaakobi, Bruck and Siegel introduced the \(b\)-symbol read-channel framework for windows \(b>2\) and developed cyclic-code constructions and decoding. A public CaltechAUTHORS record for that work was created on 2016-05-02. Ding, Zhang and Ge subsequently established the general Singleton bound \(M\le q^{n-d_b+b}\) and studied constructions and existence of linear MDS \(b\)-symbol codes; their arXiv v1 was submitted on 2016-09-29. For \(q=2,n=6,b=3,d_b=5\), their bound gives \(M\le16\), matching the elementary four-coordinate projection proof above.

The inspected prior statements concern bounds, constructions, and especially linear MDS codes. They do not state a census of all nonlinear and linear/affine equality cases for this fixed binary parameter, do not imply that there are exactly \(76\) labeled maxima, and do not give the five stated orbits or the \(44/32\) affine/nonlinear split. Later work on generalized \(b\)-symbol weights likewise develops linear-code structure rather than an unrestricted finite equality-case census.

## Limitations
The classification is only for the exact parameter \(q=2,n=6,b=3,d_3=5\). The symmetry statement is only for the explicitly verified \(768\)-element translation--dihedral subgroup; a larger full isometry group is not ruled out. The literature comparison cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed finite enumeration that was not returned by the searches. The foundational 2016 Yaakobi--Bruck--Siegel source was inspected through its public repository abstract and metadata rather than through a full-text copy in this verification package.
