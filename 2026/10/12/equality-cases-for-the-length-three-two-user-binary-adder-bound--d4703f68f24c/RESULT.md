# Equality cases for the length-three two-user binary adder bound
## Finding
For the noiseless two-user binary adder channel at block length \(3\), let \(A,B\subseteq\{0,1\}^3\). The pair is uniquely decodable when the integer-coordinate sums \(a+b\in\{0,1,2\}^3\), for \((a,b)\in A\times B\), are all distinct. Then
\[
|A||B|\le 14.
\]
Equality holds if and only if, after possibly swapping the two users,
\[
A=\{u,\bar u\},\qquad B=\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{z\},
\]
where \(\bar u\) is the bitwise complement of \(u\) and \(z\in A\). Thus there are exactly \(16\) ordered extremal pairs. Under the natural action generated by a common permutation of coordinates, common complementation of coordinates, and swapping the users, all \(16\) extremizers form one orbit.

## Assumptions and scope
The channel is synchronous, noiseless, and adds the users' binary input symbols as ordinary integers. Both component codes are nonempty subsets of the full binary cube of block length \(3\). No linearity assumption is imposed. The claim concerns zero-error unique decodability and the product \(|A||B|\), not average-error capacity or asymptotic rates.

## Proof
For a uniquely decodable pair, let \(N_k\) be the number of ordered pairs \((a,b)\in A\times B\) at Hamming distance \(k\). Van Tilborg's two distance-class estimates give
\[
N_k\le {3\choose k}2^k
\quad\text{and}\quad
N_k\le {3\choose k}2^{3-k}.
\]
Summing the smaller bound over \(k=0,1,2,3\) yields
\[
|A||B|=\sum_{k=0}^3N_k
\le 1+6+6+1=14.
\]

Suppose equality holds. Since \(A\) and \(B\) are nonempty subsets of an eight-word cube and \(|A||B|=14\), their cardinalities are \(2\) and \(7\) in some order. Assume \(|A|=2\) and write \(A=\{u,v\}\), while \(B\) is the cube with one word removed.

Let \(r=d_H(u,v)\). A collision between a sum from the \(u\)-row and a sum from the \(v\)-row has the form
\[
u+b=v+b'.
\]
On every coordinate where \(u\) and \(v\) agree, one must have \(b=b'\). On every differing coordinate, \(b\) is forced to equal the corresponding bit of \(v\) and \(b'\) the corresponding bit of \(u\). Hence the coordinates outside the difference support can be chosen freely, producing exactly \(2^{3-r}\) pairwise disjoint collision pairs \(\{b,b'\}\) inside the full cube.

Because \(B\) omits only one word, it can hit every such collision pair only if \(2^{3-r}=1\), so \(r=3\). Thus \(u\) and \(v\) are antipodal. In that case the sole possible cross-row collision uses \(b=v\) and \(b'=u\), so unique decodability requires the missing word to be one of \(u,v\). Conversely, deleting either member of an antipodal pair removes that sole collision, while sums within either row are automatically distinct. This proves the characterization.

There are four unordered antipodal pairs in \(\{0,1\}^3\), two choices of which member is omitted from the seven-word code, and two choices of which user receives the two-word code. Therefore the number of ordered extremizers is \(4\cdot2\cdot2=16\). Common cube isometries are transitive on a marked antipodal pair, and user swap exchanges the two cardinality orientations, giving one orbit.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` exhaustively checks all \(255^2=65025\) ordered pairs of nonempty subsets of the binary three-cube. It verifies a maximum product of \(14\), exactly \(16\) ordered maximizers, the two size profiles \((2,7)\) and \((7,2)\), and the stated antipodal-minus-one structure. It also checks the distance-class upper-bound sum and an explicit witness. The recorded verifier output begins with `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Van Tilborg's 1983 paper defines unique decodability for this channel and proves the two distance-class bounds used above; summing them gives the classical product upper bound. The inspected full text develops general bounds and a length-five construction, but does not state the length-three equality classification proved here. The 1985 construction paper of Coebergh van den Braak and van Tilborg and the 1992 graph-theoretic construction paper of Guo and Watanabe are close literature on constructing code pairs, including linear-first-code methods. Their accessible abstracts do not state the global length-three equality classification. The latter full text was not available from an open source during this check, so no whole-document noncoverage claim is made for it.

## Limitations
This is a finite block-length classification at \(n=3\). It does not classify extremizers of the general van Tilborg bound at larger lengths and does not improve asymptotic zero-error rate bounds. A residual literature risk remains that an older poorly indexed source may have recorded the same small equality case, especially inside a table or worked example.
