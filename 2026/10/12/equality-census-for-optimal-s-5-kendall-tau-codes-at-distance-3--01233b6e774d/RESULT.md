# Equality census for optimal \(S_5\) Kendall-\(\tau\) codes at distance \(3\)
## Finding
Let \(S_5\) be the permutations of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), with Kendall-\(\tau\) distance \(d_K\). For codes \(C\subseteq S_5\) satisfying \(d_K(\pi,\sigma)\ge3\) for all distinct codewords, the classical optimum is \(P(5,3)=20\). There are exactly \(3192\) labeled maximum codes, exactly \(532\) of which contain \(12345\).

Under the explicit natural symmetry group generated by arbitrary relabelings of the five symbols and reversal of every one-line permutation, of order \(240\), the maxima form exactly \(24\) orbits with sizes
\[
6^1,\quad30^3,\quad48^2,\quad60^4,\quad120^5,\quad240^9.
\]
The corresponding stabilizer orders are \(40,8,5,4,2,1\). The size-\(20\) example printed by Vijayakumaran lies in a natural-group orbit of size \(120\).

## Assumptions and scope
Kendall-\(\tau\) distance is the minimum number of adjacent transpositions transforming one permutation into another; equivalently, it counts unordered symbol pairs whose relative order differs. A labeled code is a literal subset of \(S_5\), before quotienting by symmetry.

The orbit statement concerns only the displayed order-\(240\) isometry subgroup. Equality with the full metric isometry group or with the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph is not asserted.

## Proof
Form the graph \(G\) on \(S_5\), joining two permutations when their Kendall-\(\tau\) distance is at least \(3\). The required codes are exactly cliques of \(G\).

For each \(\pi\in S_5\), a unique relabeling of symbols sends \(\pi\) to \(12345\), and simultaneous symbol relabeling preserves all relative-order disagreements. Thus any clique of size \(21\) would have an isometric copy containing \(12345\).

The verifier reconstructs all pair distances twice: directly by pair-order disagreements and independently as shortest-path distances in the adjacent-transposition Cayley graph. The two definitions agree on all \(\binom{120}{2}=7140\) unordered pairs. A complete color-bounded search finds no size-\(21\) clique containing \(12345\). A separate target-size enumeration finds exactly \(532\) size-\(20\) cliques containing \(12345\). Hence \(P(5,3)=20\).

Applying all \(120\) symbol relabelings to the normalized maxima produces all labeled maxima. Double-counting a maximum code together with a chosen codeword gives
\[
N\cdot20=532\cdot120,
\]
so \(N=3192\). Direct canonical-set construction also yields \(3192\) distinct codes. Applying all relabelings and both reversal states then gives the stated \(24\)-orbit decomposition.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It checks all \(7140\) pair distances by two methods, excludes normalized size \(21\), enumerates all \(532\) normalized maxima, expands them to all \(3192\) labeled maxima, verifies every code, recomputes the natural-group orbits, and checks the published example.

The canonical normalized list has SHA-256 `502486838fdc6f3400e2372677ebb1ebf733bf9bdef71b690d7fad3c84d82eb2`; the canonical all-maxima list has SHA-256 `018236d2efcea3622ecab4ccd90d7334de08a6074a5669452884c2d8d500d2b3`.

## Relationship to prior work
Vijayakumaran proved the exact value \(P(5,3)=20\) and supplied one attaining code. The complete article states exact small-order values and gives instances rather than an equality-case census; its conclusion explicitly proposes investigating algebraic structure of optimal codes. Buzaglo and Etzion previously gave bounds and constructions for the same parameter family, not this equality census.

Jany and Ravagnani classify a different equality regime of \(t\)-balanced codes. At \(n=5\), size \(20=5!/3!\) corresponds numerically to \(t=3\), but their definition requires minimum distance strictly greater than \(\binom{3}{2}=3\). Their structural classification therefore does not cover the present distance-\(3\) extremizers. Later bounds work retains the known exact small-order value without giving this census.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact classification for one classical parameter pair. It does not give a general formula for equality cases at other lengths or distances. A residual historical-priority risk remains for an obscure or unindexed enumeration not found in the checked primary literature and semantic searches.
