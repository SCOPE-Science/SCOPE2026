# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The lower bound is forced by thirty-two unique length-five windows, only five of which a marked position can hit. The exact seven-set candidate criterion consists of initial and final endpoint bounds and inter-mark gaps of at most five. Each candidate hitting all windows automatically attracts longer factors, so only the thirty shorter binary factors need further testing. The Euler-circuit traversal is complete because each order-five cycle contains `00000` uniquely and corresponds bijectively to an anchored circuit. The packaged verifier checks all 2,048 cycles, all 32 cuts, all 120 seven-set candidates, and independently checks all nonempty factors for one witness per cut. The complete counts prove both the exact optimum and multiplicity extrema. The computational proof depends on successful compilation and execution of the included certificate, not on finite sampling.

**Originality (PASS, residual priority risk).** Mantaci et al., Proposition 41, gives general de Bruijn bounds rather than the universal exact order-five answer. Whittington, Theorem 29, constructs an optimal prime-order attractor only for a specified Lyndon-generated unfolding; the source also raises the all-de Bruijn question. Béal et al. present a greedy procedure and an experimental sequence, not an exhaustive classification. Published semantic result searches and prior claim comparisons showed no implication covering all cycles and all cuts. This cannot rule out an unindexed earlier census.

**Value (PASS).** This determines a well-motivated sharp invariant for every one of the 65,536 rooted unfoldings of a natural extremal-factor-complexity family, at the first binary order with a nonintegral length-five window-cover bound. The result is not merely a single-cycle sample: optimality holds for every cut, and the additional exact extrema show substantial diversity in the number of optimal attractors. It directly addresses the issue of how minimum full attractors vary over general de Bruijn sequences raised in Whittington's paper. The result does not purport to settle the unrestricted-order question.
