For every crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) with \(n\ge4\), the exact \(B\)-coloring number is \(\lceil n(n-1)/3\rceil\), except that \(\operatorname{Cr}_6\) needs \(11\) colors. The proof turns color classes into Mendelsohn triples, so deleting one perfect matching from \(K_{n,n}\) drops \(q_B\) from \(n^2\) to asymptotically \(n^2/3\), with the unique Mendelsohn exception at order \(6\).
