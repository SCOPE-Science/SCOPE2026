# Exact Boolean-target morphism profiles of finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattices

## Finding
Let \(L\) be a finite lattice of size \(n\), and let \(r\) be the number of join-irreducible elements of \(L\). Regard \(L\) only as the binary meet-semilattice \(L_\wedge=(L,\wedge)\), with no named constants. For \(e\ge 1\), put \(B_e^\wedge=(\mathcal P([e]),\cap)\). Then
\[
|\operatorname{Hom}(L_\wedge,B_e^\wedge)|=(n+1)^e,
\]
while
\[
|\operatorname{Emb}(L_\wedge,B_e^\wedge)|=
\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i\binom{r}{i}(n+1-i)^e.
\]
In particular, if \(L\) is nontrivial, the least \(e\) for which a meet-semilattice embedding \(L_\wedge\hookrightarrow B_e^\wedge\) exists is \(e=r\), and the number of embeddings at this least dimension is exactly \(r!\).

Tóth and Waldhauser proved that the finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattices are exactly the semilattice reducts of finite distributive lattices. Therefore the formulas above give an exact Boolean-target morphism profile for every finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattice.

## Assumptions and scope
A homomorphism here preserves only the single binary meet operation. It is not required to preserve the greatest element, the least element, or any nullary operation. This convention is essential: it is why constant maps are allowed and why the homomorphism count has \(n+1\), rather than a bounded-lattice count.

The counting theorem itself is slightly broader than the model-theoretic application: it holds for the meet reduct of every finite lattice, distributive or not. The polymorphism-homogeneous corollary uses the 2020 classification theorem of Tóth--Waldhauser.

## Proof
First consider a meet-homomorphism \(h:L_\wedge\to B_1^\wedge\), where \(B_1^\wedge\cong(\{0,1\},\wedge)\). If \(h^{-1}(1)=\varnothing\), then \(h\) is the constant-zero map. Otherwise \(F=h^{-1}(1)\) is a filter: it is closed under meets, and if \(x\in F\) and \(x\le y\), then \(x=x\wedge y\), so
\[
1=h(x)=h(x\wedge y)=h(x)\wedge h(y),
\]
forcing \(h(y)=1\). Every nonempty filter in a finite lattice is principal, so \(F=\uparrow a\) for a unique \(a\in L\). Conversely, for every \(a\in L\), the map
\[
h_a(x)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\le x
\]
preserves meet because \(a\le x\wedge y\) exactly when both \(a\le x\) and \(a\le y\). Thus there are exactly \(n+1\) scalar meet-homomorphisms: one for each \(a\in L\), plus constant zero.

A map to \(B_e^\wedge\cong(B_1^\wedge)^e\) is a meet-homomorphism exactly when each coordinate is one. Hence the coordinates may be chosen independently in \(n+1\) ways, giving \((n+1)^e\) homomorphisms.

Encode such a map by its \(e\) coordinate thresholds, allowing one extra symbol for the constant-zero coordinate. Let \(j\) be join-irreducible and let \(j_*\) be its unique lower cover. The only principal-filter coordinate that separates \(j\) from \(j_*\) has threshold exactly \(j\): if \(a\le j\) but \(a\nleq j_*\), then finiteness and uniqueness of the lower cover force \(a=j\). Therefore an injective homomorphism must use every join-irreducible threshold at least once.

Conversely, if every join-irreducible threshold occurs, equal images of \(x,y\in L\) imply that the same join-irreducibles lie below \(x\) and below \(y\). Every element of a finite lattice is the join of the join-irreducibles below it, so \(x=y\). Thus injectivity is equivalent to the requirement that all \(r\) distinguished threshold symbols occur among the \(e\) coordinates. Inclusion--exclusion over the missing join-irreducibles gives
\[
\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i\binom{r}{i}(n+1-i)^e.
\]
For \(e<r\) the condition is impossible. For \(e=r\), every coordinate must be a different join-irreducible threshold, so the exact count is \(r!\).

Finally, Tóth--Waldhauser's Theorem 4.1 identifies finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattices with semilattice reducts of finite distributive lattices, placing the formulas directly inside their classification.

## Relationship to prior work
Tóth--Waldhauser provide the classification of finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattices as semilattice reducts of finite distributive lattices. Classical lattice theory supplies the facts that finite filters are principal and that finite lattice elements are generated by join-irreducibles. Birkhoff representation gives a canonical embedding of a finite distributive lattice into a Boolean lattice indexed by its join-irreducibles.

The new point isolated here is the complete count of all meet-homomorphisms and all meet-embeddings into every Boolean cube, together with the structure-independence statement: the homomorphism profile depends only on \(n\), and the embedding profile only on \((n,r)\). The exact \(r!\) count at the minimal Boolean dimension refines the usual existence statement from representation theory.

## Limitations
The theorem counts morphisms into Boolean meet-semilattices only; it does not count homomorphisms between arbitrary finite polymorphism-homogeneous semilattices. It uses the binary meet signature without named constants, so changing the signature changes the count. The exact formulas are elementary consequences of standard lattice facts, which creates a residual folklore risk even though targeted searches did not locate the same enumeration.
