# Review
## Correctness
PASS. If an active prime \(p\in S\) occurred with exponent at least two in a preimage of an \(S\)-coprime target, every summand of \(D_S(n)\) would be divisible by \(p\), which is impossible. This forces an exponent-one active subset \(T\), after which \(D_S(mP_T)=mH(T)\) gives both necessity and sufficiency. The count, minimum, and maximum follow without a cutoff. The packaged finite regression agrees with the theorem on 135 coprime targets across four prime sets and on the explicit five-preimage example.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source, Emmons--Xiao (2022), solves inverse questions for the singleton partial derivative and explicitly names analogous finite-set arithmetic-subderivative questions as a natural next step. Merikoski--Haukkanen--Tossavainen (2019) introduces \(D_S\) and basic properties. Talukdar--Saikia (2025, 2026) treats generalized algebraic properties, iterates, and fixed points. None of the inspected statements gives the coprime-target subset parametrization, its exact fiber count, or its min/max branching. A residual risk remains that an unindexed source contains an equivalent corollary.

## Value
PASS. The coprime-target stratum is mathematically natural because coprimality with \(P_S\) forces a sharp squarefree-support reduction at the differentiated primes. The result gives a complete inverse problem there, not a bounded census: all fibers are explicit, every target has at least \(|S|\) preimages, and the extremal branching is computed exactly for every finite \(S\). This directly addresses the finite-set inverse direction identified in the motivating literature.

## Closest literature and limitations
The theorem does not classify targets divisible by primes in \(S\). The closest literature concerns singleton anti-partial derivatives, foundational arithmetic-subderivative properties, generalized algebraic identities, and iteration/fixed-point equations. Those results do not imply the present inverse-fiber formula.
