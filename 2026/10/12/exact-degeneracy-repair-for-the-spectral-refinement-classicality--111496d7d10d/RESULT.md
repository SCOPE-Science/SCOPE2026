# Exact degeneracy repair for the spectral-refinement classicality criterion
## Finding
Let \(\rho\) be a density operator on a finite-dimensional bipartite Hilbert space. Write the reduced states using spectral projections for their distinct eigenvalues,
\[
\rho_A=\sum_\alpha a_\alpha P_\alpha,
\qquad
\rho_B=\sum_\beta b_\beta Q_\beta.
\]
A **rank-one spectral refinement** means choosing an orthonormal rank-one resolution inside each eigenspace \(P_\alpha\) and \(Q_\beta\).

Then two statements hold.

First, \(\rho\) is classical-classical if and only if there exists a pair of rank-one spectral refinements \(\{p_i\}\) and \(\{q_j\}\) such that
\[
[\rho,p_i\otimes q_j]=0
\quad\text{for every }i,j.
\]

Second, this commutation condition holds for every pair of rank-one spectral refinements if and only if
\[
\rho=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}P_\alpha\otimes Q_\beta,
\qquad c_{\alpha\beta}\ge0,
\]
where positivity and normalization are supplemented by the marginal constraints
\[
\sum_\beta c_{\alpha\beta}\operatorname{rank}(Q_\beta)=a_\alpha,
\qquad
\sum_\alpha c_{\alpha\beta}\operatorname{rank}(P_\alpha)=b_\beta.
\]
Consequently, when a marginal spectrum is degenerate, the intrinsic rank-one spectral criterion for classicality has an existential quantifier. Requiring independence from all choices of eigenbasis is strictly stronger and has the exact block-scalar characterization above.

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns finite-dimensional bipartite density operators and two-sided classical-classical structure. A classical-classical state is one diagonal in some orthonormal product basis. No statement is made about one-sided classical-quantum states, infinite-dimensional spectral theory, or optimized correlation measures beyond the consequence for the original spectral-measurement construction.

The distinction matters only at degeneracy. If both marginals have simple spectra, their rank-one spectral projectors are unique up to phases and ordering, so the existential and universal formulations coincide.

## Proof
Suppose first that \(\rho\) is classical-classical. Then for some product basis \(\{|e_i\rangle\otimes|f_j\rangle\}\),
\[
\rho=\sum_{i,j}r_{ij}|e_i f_j\rangle\langle e_i f_j|.
\]
Taking partial traces shows that every \(|e_i\rangle\) is an eigenvector of \(\rho_A\) and every \(|f_j\rangle\) is an eigenvector of \(\rho_B\). Hence the rank-one projectors onto these vectors form spectral refinements of the marginals, and \(\rho\) commutes with all corresponding product projectors. Conversely, if one pair of rank-one spectral refinements has this commutation property, those mutually orthogonal product projectors form a complete rank-one resolution of the identity. Commutation with all of them makes \(\rho\) diagonal in that product basis, so \(\rho\) is classical-classical.

For the universal statement, assume the commutation condition holds for every pair of rank-one spectral refinements. Fix a pair \((\alpha,\beta)\). Every unit vector in \(P_\alpha\mathcal H_A\) can occur in some orthonormal refinement of \(P_\alpha\), and similarly every unit vector in \(Q_\beta\mathcal H_B\) can occur in a refinement of \(Q_\beta\). Therefore \(\rho\) commutes with every rank-one product projector supported in \(P_\alpha\otimes Q_\beta\). Such product rank-one projectors generate the full matrix algebra on that product subspace, whose commutant consists only of scalar multiples of the identity on the block. The same commutation also removes matrix elements between distinct \((\alpha,\beta)\) blocks. Hence
\[
\rho=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_{\alpha\beta}P_\alpha\otimes Q_\beta.
\]
Positivity gives \(c_{\alpha\beta}\ge0\), and partial traces give the displayed marginal constraints. Conversely, every state of this block-scalar form commutes with every rank-one refinement because each refinement projector lies inside a block on which \(\rho\) is scalar.

A two-qubit witness shows why the quantifier matters. Let
\[
\rho_{cc}=\frac12\bigl(|00\rangle\langle00|+|11\rangle\langle11|\bigr).
\]
Both marginals equal \(I/2\). The computational basis is a valid spectral refinement and leaves \(\rho_{cc}\) unchanged. The local \(X\)-basis is also a valid spectral refinement, but dephasing in the corresponding product basis gives \(I/4\). Thus an arbitrary choice of rank-one eigenbasis need not witness the classicality of a state even though a compatible eigenbasis does.

## Verification
The proof is exact finite-dimensional operator algebra. The existential direction was checked directly against the definition of a classical-classical state. The universal direction was checked by the commutant of the full matrix algebra generated by rank-one product projectors within every product eigenspace. The two-qubit witness can be verified entrywise: the computational dephasing is the identity on \(\rho_{cc}\), while all four local \(X\)-basis product outcomes have probability \(1/4\), so the postmeasurement state is \(I/4\).

For the mutual-information version of measurement-induced disturbance, the same witness has \(I(\rho_{cc})=1\) bit. The computational refinement therefore gives disturbance value \(0\), whereas the local \(X\)-basis refinement gives \(1\). This numerical ambiguity itself is not claimed as new; it is included only as a minimal illustration of the quantifier defect.

## Relationship to prior work
Luo's 2008 construction defines classicality through invariance under local projective measurement and then formulates the criterion using spectral decompositions of the reduced states. The full text was inspected through the classicality theorems and the spectral-measurement construction. Under degeneracy, a rank-one spectral decomposition is not unique, so a fixed arbitrary refinement cannot be used without an existence qualification.

Wu, Poulsen, and Mølmer subsequently emphasized that measurement-induced disturbance becomes ambiguous when reduced states are degenerate, and analyzed basis dependence for highly symmetric states. Girolami, Paternostro, and Adesso likewise exhibited the ambiguity for Bell-diagonal states with maximally mixed marginals and motivated optimization over local measurements. Those works cover the basis-dependence phenomenon illustrated by \(\rho_{cc}\). The present result isolates a different statement: the exact repair of the rank-one spectral classicality criterion and the necessary-and-sufficient block-scalar condition under which **every** spectral refinement is safe.

Targeted searches for the source theorem, degeneracy, spectral refinements, arbitrary eigenbases, classical-classical states, and block-scalar formulations did not locate an implication-equivalent statement. Failed search is not a uniqueness proof, and an obscure note or thesis may contain the same elementary commutant observation.

## Limitations
The algebraic argument is elementary; its value is the exact logical boundary of a published spectral criterion, not technical difficulty. It does not make the unoptimized spectral-measurement disturbance basis-independent. Rather, it identifies precisely when degeneracy is harmless and when an existence or optimization prescription is required.

The literature comparison was strongest for the defining paper and the two later papers that explicitly discuss degeneracy and measurement-basis ambiguity. A poorly indexed source could already state the same universal block-scalar characterization. No claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness is made.
