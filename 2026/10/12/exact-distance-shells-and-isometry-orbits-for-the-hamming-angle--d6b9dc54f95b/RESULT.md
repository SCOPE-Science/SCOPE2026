# Exact distance shells and isometry orbits for the Hamming-angle metric
## Finding
Let \(q\) be a prime power and let
\[
\delta([u],[v])=\min_{c\in\mathbb F_q^{*}}d_H(u,cv)
\]
be the Hamming-angle metric on \(\mathbb P(\mathbb F_q^n)\). If the center \([u]\) has Hamming weight \(w\), then its complete distance-shell enumerator depends only on \(w\). After fixing any ordering of the \(q-1\) nonzero field elements, it is
\[
E_{n,q,w}(z)=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{b=0}^{n-w}\binom{n-w}{b}(q-1)^b\!\sum_{a_0+\cdots+a_{q-1}=w}\binom{w}{a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1}}z^{\,b+w-\max(a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1})}-z^w\right).
\]
The coefficient \([z^r]E_{n,q,w}(z)\) is the exact size of the distance-\(r\) shell, so the exact radius-\(R\) ball size is \(\sum_{r=0}^{R}[z^r]E_{n,q,w}(z)\).

The shell profiles also determine the vertex orbits of the full isometry group: two projective points lie in the same isometry orbit if and only if they have the same Hamming weight. Hence the Hamming-angle projective space is generally not homogeneous; for every \(n\ge2\) it is not vertex-transitive.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(\mathbb F_q\) is arbitrary of prime-power order and \(n\ge1\). Hamming weight is well defined on a projective point because nonzero scalar multiplication does not change its zero pattern. The result concerns the metric on one-dimensional projective rays defined by minimizing ordinary Hamming distance over a scalar multiple. It is not the distinct Gabidulin--Simonis notion of a translation-invariant projective metric generated by a spanning family.

For a fixed center of weight \(w\), monomial transformations allow the representative to be normalized to
\[
u=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{w},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-w}).\]
This normalization preserves \(\delta\).

## Proof
Take a nonzero representative \(v\). Among the \(n-w\) coordinates outside the support of \(u\), let \(b\) be the number of nonzero coordinates of \(v\). These coordinates contribute exactly \(b\) disagreements for every nonzero scalar multiplying \(v\).

On the support of \(u\), let \(a_0\) be the number of zero entries of \(v\), and let \(a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1}\) be the multiplicities of the \(q-1\) nonzero field values in any fixed order. Then
\[
a_0+a_1+\cdots+a_{q-1}=w.
\]
For a chosen scalar \(c\), agreement with the normalized value \(1\) occurs exactly at positions where the corresponding entry of \(v\) equals \(c^{-1}\). As \(c\) ranges through \(\mathbb F_q^{*}\), these agreement counts range through \(a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1}\). Therefore
\[
\delta([u],[v])=b+w-\max(a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1}).
\]

For fixed \(b\), the outside-support coordinates can be chosen in
\[
\binom{n-w}{b}(q-1)^b
\]
ways. For fixed \((a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1})\), the support coordinates can be chosen in
\[
\binom{w}{a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1}}
\]
ways. Summing the associated powers of \(z\) counts all vectors \(v\in\mathbb F_q^n\). The unique zero vector occurs at \(b=0\), \(a_0=w\), and \(a_1=\cdots=a_{q-1}=0\); the displayed exponent assigns it \(z^w\), so that term must be removed. Every remaining projective point has exactly \(q-1\) nonzero vector representatives, and the exponent is scalar invariant. Division by \(q-1\) gives the stated enumerator.

It remains to determine the isometry orbits. Every monomial transformation induces an isometry of \(\delta\), and monomial transformations act transitively on projective points of each fixed Hamming weight. Thus every weight class is contained in one full-isometry orbit.

Conversely, isometries preserve the entire shell profile. If \(q>2\), a point at distance \(n\) must have zero entries on the support of \(u\) and nonzero entries in every coordinate outside it. Hence, when \(w<n\), the distance-\(n\) shell has
\[
\frac{(q-1)^{n-w}}{q-1}=(q-1)^{n-w-1}
\]
points, whereas for \(w=n\) it is empty. Because \(q-1>1\), these values distinguish every \(w\). If \(q=2\), the scalar group is trivial and \(\delta\) is ordinary Hamming distance on \(\mathbb F_2^n\setminus\{0\}\); the full binary cube has \(\binom nr\) vectors at distance \(r\), and deleting the zero vector removes exactly one point from the shell \(r=w\). Thus the profile is \(\binom nr-\mathbf 1_{r=w}\), which again determines \(w\). Therefore distinct weights cannot lie in the same isometry orbit, and the orbits are exactly the weight classes.

## Verification
The standalone verifier enumerates every projective point for \(q\in\{2,3,4,5\}\) and \(1\le n\le4\). It computes the metric directly by minimizing over field scalars and compares every shell coefficient with the composition formula. The \(q=4\) cases use an explicit arithmetic table for \(\mathbb F_4\), so the replay includes a non-prime field. It also checks total projective-space cardinalities, the radius-one formula, the distance-\(n\) shell, and representative ternary shell profiles. The replay terminates with `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Otal introduced the Hamming angle, proved that it descends to a metric on projective space, and gave a linear-time pointwise computation in terms of the largest scalar-agreement count. The present finding solves the separate global counting problem obtained by ranging the second projective point over the whole space: it gives every shell and ball size for every center and identifies the full isometry orbits from those profiles.

Riccardi and Sauerbier Couvée study sphere sizes for a different established notion of projective metric: a translation-invariant metric on a vector space arising from a projective spanning family, equivalently a quotient-weight construction. Their sphere-size theory does not state the shell distribution for Otal's scalar-orbit metric on \(\mathbb P(\mathbb F_q^n)\).

## Limitations
The formula is an exact finite enumerator, not an asymptotic estimate or a new coding bound by itself. It does not characterize the full isometry group internally; it determines its vertex orbits. The verifier exhausts only small fields and dimensions, while the all-parameter statement is supplied by the counting proof. No claim is made that the Hamming-angle metric is distance-regular; in fact the weight-dependent shell profiles obstruct vertex transitivity for \(n\ge2\).
