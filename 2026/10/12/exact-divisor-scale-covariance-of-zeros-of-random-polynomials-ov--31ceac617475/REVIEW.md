# Review of the finite-ring zero-count covariance theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** Conditioning on all coefficients except the independent constant makes one evaluation exactly uniform and independent of its difference from another evaluation. The free linear coefficients send the evaluation difference uniformly onto the ideal generated by the coordinate differences; all remaining polynomial differences lie in that ideal. This establishes the joint-zero probability even on the diagonal. Euler's totient divisor identity then gives the positive congruence-class covariance decomposition, and counting full-grid residue classes gives the exact variance. The argument covers all stated moduli, dimensions and monomial ensembles without approximation. Exact brute enumeration confirms the formulas in ten finite test ensembles. The verifier does not replace the universal proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Section 5, Conjecture 5.7 of the inspected full arXiv:2308.14580v1 states the prime-power expectation and univariate variance as conjectures. Its proven finite-field generating function does not cover rings with zero divisors; the ring covariance formula includes an additional divisor hierarchy. The inspected introductory results of arXiv:2101.09590 concern counts of actual roots over \(\mathbb Q_p\), not roots of a polynomial congruence over \(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z\). Evans (2006) likewise treats \(p\)-adic zero counts. Targeted published-record and literature queries found no equivalent covariance/variance theorem. The 2025 journal revision of Jain--Moon--Wu was identifiable by abstract but not materially inspected as full text, and an older equivalent finite-ring identity could exist.

## Value

**PASS.** The finding proves an explicitly motivated variance conjecture for every prime power, including the previously unrequired affine case; supplies the exact multivariate variance for **every** integer modulus; and resolves the full pairwise spatial covariance into positive residue-class scales. It distinguishes finite fields and finite rings sharply and allows exact uncertainty calculations for root counts in arbitrary subsets. This is a structural result, not a restatement of a generic moment identity. It does not solve the distribution-stabilization or complete generating-function questions.

## Scientific limits

The proof needs independent uniform constant and coordinate-linear coefficients. The supported positivity is covariance positivity for subset counts, not a general positive-association assertion. Full independent priority validation and independent proof audit remain to be performed.
