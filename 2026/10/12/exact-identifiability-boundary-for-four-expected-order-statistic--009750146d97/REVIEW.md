# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. For four expected order statistics, the source's binomial-moment equations determine the generator mean and variance explicitly. The feasibility defect is exactly the generator variance. Zero variance forces one degenerate generator and, through the source's one-to-one normalized generator/parent correspondence, one two-point parent; direct order-statistic gap formulas recover its atoms. Positive variance admits the displayed open family of two-atom generators, and the corresponding three-point parent is written explicitly. Direct computation verifies that its four expected order statistics are the prescribed values.

Risk: the proof relies on the published generator-to-parent correspondence for the general equivalence, although the explicit three-point construction is also checked directly.

## Originality
PASS. Okolewski and Papadatos give the general correspondence, the four-term feasibility inequality, the general singular-moment determinacy principle, and one symmetric unique example. Their inspected full text does not state the complete boundary-identification theorem, the arbitrary-boundary two-point formula, or an uncountable three-point family for every strict interior point. Targeted published-record and web searches found no covering statement.

Residual risk: Mallows's 1973 paper on distribution-function bounds from expected order statistics was identified as closely relevant classical background but was not materially inspected in full. An equivalent specialization may also exist in older truncated-moment literature under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. Four expected order statistics are the first nontrivial finite case in the motivating source. Classifying exactly when they identify the parent distribution, and showing that every interior point is already nonidentified among three-point laws, gives a natural structural phase diagram of the feasible region. The explicit formulas make the boundary and nonidentification mechanism usable without solving a fresh moment problem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is A. Okolewski and N. Papadatos, arXiv:2204.13949v1 / DOI:10.1007/s13171-024-00343-z. Mallows (1973), DOI:10.1214/AOP/1176996981, and Kadane (1974), DOI:10.2307/3212766, are classical background. The result is restricted to exactly four strictly increasing expected order statistics and does not enumerate all compatible interior parent laws.
