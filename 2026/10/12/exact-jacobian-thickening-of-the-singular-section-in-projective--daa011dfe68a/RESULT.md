# Exact Jacobian thickening of the singular section in projective K-regular quotient bundles
## Finding
Let \(A\) be a positive-dimensional complex abelian variety, \(\mathcal L\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)\) non-torsion, and \(d\ge3\). Define
\[X_d=\mathbb P_A(\mathcal O_A^{\oplus2}\oplus\mathcal L)/\mu_d,\]
where \(\mu_d\) scales the third summand and acts trivially on the first two. Let \(g=\dim A\), and let \(\Sigma\subset X_d\) be the image of the projective section \(\mathbb P_A(\mathcal L)\), canonically isomorphic to \(A\). For the intrinsic Jacobian ideal
\[J_d=\operatorname{Fitt}_{g+2}(\Omega^1_{X_d/\mathbb C}),\]
there is an equality of coherent ideal sheaves
\[\boxed{J_d=\mathcal I_\Sigma^{d-1}.}\]
The reduced singular locus is exactly \(\Sigma\). At each geometric point of \(\Sigma\), the normal Artin section of \(\mathcal O_{X_d}/J_d\) has graded Hilbert function \(dj+1\), in degrees \(0\le j\le d-2\), and transverse length
\[\boxed{\ell_d=\frac{(d-1)(d^2-2d+2)}2.}\]
In particular, these lengths are \(5,15,34,65\) for \(d=3,4,5,6\). The entire family is \(K_m\)-regular for every \(m\), by Satriano--Shen [1], so neither homotopy-invariant algebraic \(K\)-theory nor the smoothness of the reduced singular section imposes a bound on this intrinsic Jacobian thickness.
## Assumptions and scope
All schemes and differentials are over \(\mathbb C\). The quotient is the coarse geometric finite-group quotient. The Jacobian singular subscheme is defined by the Fitting ideal of order \(\dim X_d=g+2\), not by the radical or by the ideal generated by all first partials of a non-complete-intersection embedding. A transverse Artin section means the quotient by regular parameters on \(A\) in a local product chart. The result is uniform in the dimension of \(A\), its chosen line bundle, and all integers \(d\ge3\).
## Proof
First trivialize \(\mathcal L\) Zariski-locally on \(A\). The corresponding quotient chart is a product with the surface \(Y_d=\mathbb P(1,1,d)\). The unique singular point of \(Y_d\) is the weight-\(d\) vertex: its affine chart has coordinate algebra
\[R_d=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}\operatorname{Sym}^{dj}(\mathbb C^2)\simeq \mathbb C[z_0,\ldots,z_d]/I_d,\qquad z_i\mapsto x^{d-i}y^i,\]
where \(I_d\) is generated by all \(2\times2\) minors of the two-row Hankel matrix whose rows are \((z_0,\ldots,z_{d-1})\) and \((z_1,\ldots,z_d)\). Away from the vertex, the finite cyclic action has either trivial stabilizer or is a pseudo-reflection in a smooth local chart, so the coarse quotient is smooth; hence \(\Sigma\) is the full reduced singular locus.

Write \(\mathfrak m=(z_0,\ldots,z_d)\) in \(R_d\). The module of Kähler differentials has a presentation with \(d+1\) generators and a Jacobian relation matrix indexed by the quadratic generators of \(I_d\). Since \(\dim R_d=2\), \(\operatorname{Fitt}_2(\Omega^1_{R_d})\) is generated by the \((d-1)\times(d-1)\) minors of that matrix. Every entry is homogeneous of degree one, giving the inclusion
\[\operatorname{Fitt}_2(\Omega^1_{R_d})\subseteq\mathfrak m^{d-1}.\]

For \(0\le i\le d-2\), consider the actual quadratic relations \(f_i=z_0z_{i+2}-z_1z_{i+1}\). Their Jacobian submatrix with columns corresponding to \(z_2,\ldots,z_d\) is lower triangular, with all diagonal entries equal to \(z_0\). It has determinant \(z_0^{d-1}\), so
\[z_0^{d-1}\in\operatorname{Fitt}_2(\Omega^1_{R_d}).\]
The linear action of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb C)\) on binary forms preserves the ring and this intrinsic Fitting ideal. Its orbit sends \(z_0^{d-1}\) to the pure powers \((ax+by)^{d(d-1)}\). These span \(\operatorname{Sym}^{d(d-1)}(\mathbb C^2)= (R_d)_{d-1}\) in characteristic zero, by the Vandermonde/polarization argument. Therefore the ideal contains all of \((R_d)_{d-1}\), and hence contains \(\mathfrak m^{d-1}\). Equality follows.

For the product with a smooth \(g\)-fold, the differential module splits as the direct sum of its rank-\(g\) free smooth contribution and the pullback of \(\Omega^1_{R_d}\). The Fitting-sum identity therefore gives \(\operatorname{Fitt}_{g+2}=\operatorname{Fitt}_2(\Omega^1_{R_d})=\mathfrak m^{d-1}\) in every local chart at \(\Sigma\). Elsewhere the Fitting ideal is the unit ideal. This glues canonically to the stated equality with \(\mathcal I_\Sigma^{d-1}\).

Finally \(\dim_\mathbb C(R_d)_j=dj+1\), and the natural maximal-ideal filtration coincides with the standard Veronese grading. The transverse Artin algebra \(R_d/\mathfrak m^{d-1}\) therefore has successive lengths \(dj+1\) for \(0\le j\le d-2\). Summing yields \(\ell_d=(d-1)(d^2-2d+2)/2\), as claimed.
## Verification
The included self-contained Python/SymPy checker verifies the triangular Jacobian minor, the Veronese substitutions, the pure-power coefficient span, and the arithmetic length formula for \(d=3,4,5,6\); the general theorem uses the preceding symbolic argument and characteristic-zero polarization, not finite experimentation. Run with `python check_veronese_fitting.py` (requires SymPy).
## Relationship to prior work
Satriano and Shen [1, Theorem 1.2 and its proof, Section 5] construct exactly these quotients and prove \(K_m\)-regularity for all integers \(m\), as well as failure of the local-complete-intersection property when \(d\ge3\). Their explicit local description identifies fibers with \(\mathbb P(1,1,d)\), but the work does not state the intrinsic Jacobian Fitting ideal or its nilpotent thickening along the singular section. Classical work on Hankel determinantal rings [2] supplies related background on the rational-normal-curve cones; no assertion about the specific Fitting-power equality is inferred from its abstract. The present result computes the scheme structure and transverse length of the canonical singularity detector, beyond the reduced singular locus.
## Limitations
The equality is for the standard intrinsic Fitting/Jacobian singular subscheme; alternative singularity schemes defined using cotorsion sheaves or derived Jacobians are not computed. It does not establish that the local Veronese-cone identity was unknown in classical invariant-theoretic literature; a previously implicit equivalent identity remains a bibliographic risk. The infinite-family \(K_m\)-regularity is a cited theorem of [1], not reproved here. In positive characteristic the polarization step would require separate treatment.
