# Exact length-six generalized-Cayley code census
## Finding
For permutation codes \(C\subseteq S_6\) in the generalized Cayley metric \(\rho_{\mathrm{gC}}\), the exact maximum at minimum distance \(3\) is
\[
A_{\rho_{\mathrm{gC}}}(6,3)=6.
\]
There are exactly \(6{,}000\) labeled maximum codes. Every maximum is equidistant: each of its \(\binom{6}{2}\) distinct pairs has generalized Cayley distance exactly \(3\). Under left multiplication by \(S_6\), these maxima form exactly ten orbits, two of size \(120\) and eight of size \(720\). Exactly \(50\) maxima contain the identity. Exactly two of those identity-containing maxima are subgroups, both cyclic of order \(6\), generated up to inversion by \((1\,2\,4\,6\,5\,3)\) and \((1\,3\,2\,6\,4\,5)\).

## Assumptions and scope
A generalized transposition swaps two disjoint intervals of positions, with any intervening interval left between the swapped blocks. The generalized Cayley distance \(\rho_{\mathrm{gC}}(f,g)\) is the minimum number of generalized transpositions needed to transform \(f\) into \(g\) by post-multiplication. A code has minimum distance \(3\) when every two distinct codewords are at distance at least \(3\). “Labeled” means that the symbols and positions remain the fixed labels \(1,\ldots,6\); no quotient by an isometry group is taken. The orbit statement concerns the explicitly stated left-multiplication action only and does not assert that this is the full isometry group.

## Proof
The proof is a complete finite enumeration from the metric definition. There are \(70\) distinct nonidentity generalized transpositions in \(S_6\). Breadth-first search in the Cayley graph generated by them reaches all \(720\) permutations and gives the exact distance-from-identity distribution
\[
1,\ 70,\ 469,\ 180
\]
at distances \(0,1,2,3\), respectively. In particular the diameter is \(3\).

Build a graph on all \(720\) permutations, joining two vertices exactly when their generalized Cayley distance is at least \(3\). Left invariance reduces every distance test to the previously computed distance from the identity. The graph is \(180\)-regular with \(64{,}800\) edges. Thus codes of minimum distance \(3\) are exactly cliques in this graph.

A complete Bron–Kerbosch enumeration with pivoting produces \(758{,}976\) maximal cliques. Their size distribution is \(670{,}320\) of size \(4\), \(82{,}656\) of size \(5\), and \(6{,}000\) of size \(6\), with none larger. Hence the exact maximum is \(6\), and there are exactly \(6{,}000\) labeled maxima. Since the metric diameter is \(3\), every compatible distinct pair in a maximum code is at distance exactly \(3\), proving equidistance.

For the structural statements, the enumeration filters the maxima containing the identity and finds exactly \(50\). Closure under functional composition is checked for each of these, yielding exactly two subgroups. Each has two six-cycle generators that are inverses; choosing the lexicographically smaller cycle gives \((1\,2\,4\,6\,5\,3)\) and \((1\,3\,2\,6\,4\,5)\). Finally, all \(720\) left multiplications are applied to every maximum code. The resulting exact orbit partition has sizes \(120,120\) and eight copies of \(720\), accounting for all \(6{,}000\) maxima.

The computation is exhaustive over the finite space; no random sampling, timeout inference, or extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Chee and Vu introduced the generalized Cayley metric for permutation codes and developed breakpoint-based bounds and constructions. Their general upper bound specializes only to \(A_{\rho_{\mathrm{gC}}}(6,3)\le 24\), so it does not imply the exact value or any extremizer census. Yang, Schoeny, and Dolecek subsequently developed theoretical bounds and order-optimal constructions for the same metric. The inspected statements of these sources concern general constructions, rate, and bounds rather than an exhaustive fixed-parameter classification at \((n,d)=(6,3)\).

Targeted searches for the exact parameter, generalized-transposition aliases, finite tables, and implication-equivalent classifications did not locate a source giving the value \(6\), the count \(6{,}000\), or the ten-orbit decomposition. The closest indexed permutation-family results concern different relations or different metrics and do not imply this claim.

## Limitations
This result is only for \(S_6\) and minimum generalized Cayley distance \(3\). It does not claim a formula for other lengths or distances, nor that left multiplication is the full isometry group. An obscure or unindexed finite census could remain undiscovered; the originality conclusion is therefore limited to the inspected primary literature and targeted searches.
