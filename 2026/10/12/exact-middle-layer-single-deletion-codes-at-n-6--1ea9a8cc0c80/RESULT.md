# Exact middle-layer single-deletion codes at \(n=6\)
## Finding
Let \(D_1(x)\) be the set of distinct words obtained by deleting one coordinate from a binary word \(x\). For
\[
C\subseteq\{x\in\{0,1\}^6:\operatorname{wt}(x)=3\}
\]
with \(D_1(x)\cap D_1(y)=\varnothing\) for all distinct \(x,y\in C\), the exact maximum is
\[
|C|=5.
\]
Exactly \(8\) labeled maximum codes attain this value.

Every maximum code is invariant under reverse-complement. Under the order-\(4\) action generated by reversal and global bit complementation, the eight maxima split into exactly four orbits, each of size \(2\).

Among the eight maxima, exactly four have deletion-shadow union of size \(20\). These four partition the entire relevant received layer, namely all length-five binary words of Hamming weight \(2\) or \(3\). The other four maxima have deletion-shadow union of size \(18\).

## Assumptions and scope
A code corrects one deletion exactly when the deletion shadows of distinct codewords are disjoint. Because every transmitted word has length six and weight three, every received length-five word has weight two or three. There are exactly \(20\) such received words.

Codes are labeled literal subsets of the twenty length-six weight-three words. The symmetry statement uses only reversal and global bit complementation; no larger automorphism group is asserted.

## Proof
Consider the five length-five words
\[
H=\{\texttt{00011},\texttt{00110},\texttt{01100},\texttt{10101},\texttt{11000}\}.
\]
A direct insertion calculation partitions all twenty length-six weight-three words into five blocks according to the unique member of \(H\) appearing in their deletion shadow:

- `00011`: `000111`, `001011`, `010011`, `100011`;
- `00110`: `001101`, `001110`, `010110`, `100110`;
- `01100`: `011001`, `011010`, `011100`, `101100`;
- `10101`: `010101`, `100101`, `101001`, `101010`;
- `11000`: `110001`, `110010`, `110100`, `111000`.

Thus every length-six weight-three word has exactly one deletion descendant in \(H\). If two codewords belonged to the same block, their deletion shadows would intersect in that member of \(H\). Hence any single-deletion-correcting code contains at most one codeword from each block, so \(|C|\le5\).

The code
\[
\{\texttt{000111},\texttt{010101},\texttt{011100},\texttt{100110},\texttt{110001}\}
\]
has pairwise-disjoint one-deletion shadows, proving \(|C|=5\) is attainable.

For the equality classification, the verifier exhausts the \(\binom{20}{5}=15504\) candidate five-subsets and finds exactly eight valid codes. It independently checks all \(\binom{20}{2}=190\) word pairs using both direct shadow intersection and longest-common-subsequence distance. Applying reversal and complement to the eight maxima gives the four two-element orbits, and direct shadow unions give the distribution four of size \(18\) and four of size \(20\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs all twenty words, all deletion shadows, and the five-block upper-bound certificate. It compares direct deletion-confusability with a dynamic-programming longest-common-subsequence test on all \(190\) unordered word pairs. It then exhausts every five-subset, checks that no six-subset is valid, reconstructs the four symmetry orbits, and verifies the shadow-union distribution.

The canonical newline-terminated listing of all eight maxima in `MAXIMA.txt` has SHA-256 `6467d017b83742475c63ad33f708c912a1cc96363823f64828a16e97bb9a24ce`.

## Relationship to prior work
Cullina, Kulkarni, and Kiyavash introduced the constant-Hamming-weight induced deletion graphs as a deliberate construction framework. For one deletion they proved the exact chromatic number
\[
\chi(L_{1,n,k})=\omega(L_{1,n,k})=\max(k,n-k)+1.
\]
At \((n,k)=(6,3)\), this gives an optimal four-coloring of a twenty-vertex graph. Their paper explicitly distinguishes optimal colorings from maximum independent sets, so the chromatic result does not determine the exact equality cases classified here. The present five-block certificate gives the matching upper bound on the independence number, while the exhaustive classification determines all extremizers and their shadow geometry.

The longer treatment by the same authors studies weight partitioning and optimal colorings more broadly and reports computational optimality for unrestricted single-deletion codes at small lengths, not the present constant-weight equality census. Sloane's survey develops the classical unrestricted binary single-deletion problem and Varshamov--Tenengolts codes, but does not supply this middle-layer classification.

Targeted searches using the exact parameters, deletion-shadow language, longest-common-subsequence equivalence, reverse-complement symmetry, and the final counts found no statement implying the eight-code census or the four-orbit decomposition.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite result only for the balanced weight-three layer at length six. It does not classify constant-weight deletion codes at other lengths or weights, and it does not determine the full automorphism group of the finite compatibility graph. An obscure or unindexed historical finite enumeration remains a residual priority risk.
