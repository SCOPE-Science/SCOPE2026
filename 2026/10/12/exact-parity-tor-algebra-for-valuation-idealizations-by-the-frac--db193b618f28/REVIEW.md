# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The nilradical of \(R=D\ltimes(Q/D)\) is \(N=0\ltimes(Q/D)\), and divisibility of \(Q/D\) makes it a divided prime ideal. A nonzero nonunit of \(D\) gives a nonnil zero-divisor, so the ring is non-strong. Every element of \(N\) is killed by such a nonnil zero-divisor, while the general canonical-kernel inclusion gives \(K\subseteq N\); hence \(K=N\). The flat resolution \(0\to D\to Q\to Q/D\to0\), together with the torsion/divisible tensor vanishings, gives \(\operatorname{Tor}^{D}_{1}(Q/D,Q/D)\cong Q/D\). The published recurrence then yields the exact odd/even self-Tor pattern, and the mixed and quotient Tor groups follow by two explicit dimension shifts.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating 2026 preprint proves only that positive even self-Tor groups of the canonical kernel vanish and that odd groups are nonzero torsion-divisible modules. Its full text does not specialize to the idealization \(D\ltimes(Q/D)\) or identify the odd groups up to isomorphism. Searches for the exact valuation idealization, Clark's uniserial ring, canonical-kernel self-Tor, and the reduced-quotient Tor algebra found no statement of the complete parity table. The classical idealization and morphic-ring references establish that the object is natural and previously studied, but their advertised results concern ideal/module structure rather than this Tor computation.

## Value
PASS. The canonical kernel is the mechanism that drives the new \(\phi\)-weak-global-dimension trichotomy, but the general theorem leaves the odd Tor modules unspecified. The classical fraction-quotient idealization is a natural benchmark in which that recurrence closes exactly. The resulting full two-object Tor table and square-zero positive-degree Tor algebra give a concrete model for the obstruction rather than merely another nonvanishing example, and can be used to test refinements of the homological theory on non-strong \(\phi\)-Prüfer rings.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest result is Zhang--Qi, arXiv:2610.05567v1, Theorem 6.3, which determines only vanishing versus nonvanishing and torsion-divisibility for canonical-kernel self-Tor. Anderson--Winders survey idealizations, while Nicholson--Sánchez Campos and standard ring databases record the Clark-type valuation idealization without the parity-resolved Tor algebra. The main residual risk is an older, poorly indexed homological calculation of the same classical ring.
