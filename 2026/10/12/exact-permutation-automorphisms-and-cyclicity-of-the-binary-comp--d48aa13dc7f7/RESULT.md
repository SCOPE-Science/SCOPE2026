# Exact permutation automorphisms and cyclicity of the binary complement-pair codes

## Finding
For an even positive integer \(k\), define the binary length-\(2k\) code
\[
C_k=\{(u,u+\operatorname{par}(u)\mathbf 1_k):u\in\mathbb F_2^k\},\qquad \operatorname{par}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^k u_i\pmod2.
\]
Its generator is \([I_k\mid I_k+J_k]\), where \(J_k\) is the all-one matrix. For **every even \(k\ge6\)**, the full coordinate-permutation automorphism group is precisely
\[
\operatorname{PAut}(C_k)\cong (C_2)^{k-1}\rtimes S_k,
\qquad |\operatorname{PAut}(C_k)|=2^{k-1}k!.
\]
In the natural pairing \(P_i=\{i,k+i\}\), its elements arbitrarily permute the \(k\) pairs and independently exchange the coordinates within an **even** number of pairs. There is no cycle of length \(2k\), so these codes are not permutation-equivalent to cyclic codes, regardless of how \(x^k-1\) factors over \(\mathbb F_2\). Among positive even parameters, \(C_2\) is cyclic up to permutation, and \(C_4\) is not; thus the complete cyclic-equivalence classification is \(k=2\) only.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector coordinates and sums lie in \(\mathbb F_2\). A coordinate-permutation automorphism is a permutation of the \(2k\) coordinate positions taking \(C_k\) to itself. A linear code is permutation-equivalent to a cyclic code exactly when its permutation automorphism group contains a permutation consisting of one cycle on all its coordinates: conjugate that cycle to the usual one-step rotation. The group description is for even \(k\ge6\); the small cases \(k=2,4\) are determined separately. The parameter \(k\) is unrestricted above; no numerical enumeration is being extrapolated to an infinite claim.

## Proof
Put \(p=\operatorname{par}(u)\) and \(r=\operatorname{wt}(u)\). The two halves of \((u,u+p\mathbf 1_k)\) coincide if \(p=0\), and are bitwise complements if \(p=1\); hence
\[
\operatorname{wt}(u,u+p\mathbf 1_k)=
\begin{cases}2r,&r\equiv0\pmod2,\\ k,&r\equiv1\pmod2.\end{cases}
\]
For even \(k\ge6\), the weight-four words are **exactly** those with \(r=2\). Their supports are the \(\binom{k}{2}\) sets \(P_i\cup P_j\) for distinct \(i,j\). Two coordinate positions lie in the same \(P_i\) precisely when they belong to exactly the same weight-four supports. Indeed, coordinates in \(P_i\) have identical membership; distinct \(i,j\) are separated by a support \(P_i\cup P_h\) with \(h\notin\{i,j\}\). Because coordinate automorphisms preserve the family of minimum supports, they must preserve this intrinsic partition into pairs.

Conversely, every permutation of the pairs preserves the code: it merely permutes the coordinates of \(u\) and therefore preserves its parity. It remains to determine which within-pair swaps preserve the code. Let \(S\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}\) be the swapped pairs, write \(\mathbf1_S\) for its indicator vector, and consider \((u,u+p\mathbf1_k)\). After swapping, the first half is \(u'=u+p\mathbf1_S\); its true parity is \(\operatorname{par}(u')=p+p|S|\pmod2\). The difference between the second and first halves remains \(p\mathbf1_k\). Thus the transformed word belongs to \(C_k\) for every \(u\) if and only if \(|S|\) is even (an odd-parity \(u\) tests necessity). Such swaps form an invariant elementary abelian subgroup of order \(2^{k-1}\), normalized by all pair permutations, proving the asserted semidirect product and its order.

Any \(2k\)-cycle preserving a partition into \(k\) pairs must induce a single cycle of length \(k\) on those pairs. Following the point around this cycle for \(k\) steps changes which coordinate of its original pair it occupies if and only if the total number of within-pair flips is odd. An even number of flips instead gives two disjoint cycles of length \(k\). Our automorphism group allows only even flip parity, so it has no \(2k\)-cycle. This proves noncyclic equivalence for **every even \(k\ge6\)**.

For \(k=4\), the displayed weight formula gives minimum distance \(4\), while a binary cyclic code of length \(8\) and dimension \(4\) is unique: its generator is \((x+1)^4=x^4+1\) because \(x^8-1=(x+1)^8\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). This cyclic code has a weight-two codeword, so \(C_4\) cannot be permutation-equivalent to it. For \(k=2\), the code consists of zero, the all-one word, and two disjoint weight-two words; permuting the coordinates identifies it with the binary cyclic length-four dimension-two code generated by \(x^2+1\). These exhaust the positive even cases.

As one example outside the factorization hypothesis of García García--Martínez López--Rúa, at \(k=14\), \(x^{14}-1=(x+1)^2(x^3+x+1)^2(x^3+x^2+1)^2\); the two distinct cubics are reciprocal and not individually self-reciprocal. Nevertheless \(C_{14}\) is noncyclic by the intrinsic-pair obstruction.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs every codeword for \(k\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}\), checks the weight-four support characterization and intrinsic pair signatures for \(k\ge6\), enumerates all \(6!\,2^6=46080\) pair-preserving permutations at \(k=6\) and confirms precisely \(23040\) are automorphisms, and enumerates **all** coordinate permutations for the boundary cases \(k=2,4\), including the existence/nonexistence of a full-length cycle. Execute `python3 verify.py`; successful completion prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations are cross-checks, not the proof of the all-\(k\) assertion, which is the support-and-parity argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
García García, Martínez López, and Rúa (2026), Definition 5 and Propositions 3--5, study exactly the generator \([I_k\mid I_k+J_k]\) as an \(\omega\mid\overline\omega\) group code. They establish self-duality for even \(k\) and noncyclic equivalence **under** a self-reciprocal-factor hypothesis on \(x^k-1\). The present finding removes that hypothesis and identifies the full permutation automorphism group, rather than reproving self-duality. Mallik and Yildiz (2019/2021) discuss the same matrix through complete-graph adjacency and its minimum distance and self-duality. The \(k=6\) member is classically known as \(d_{12}^{+}\): Bouyuklieva, Willems, and Yankov describe the same matrix and the 15 two-pair weight-four supports. Thus neither that small code, its weight-four supports, nor related \(d_n\) enumerators are presented as new. The contribution is the uniform all-even-parameter full-group computation and unconditional cyclic-equivalence classification; it is not a new construction.

## Limitations
The statement concerns coordinate permutations, not the automorphisms of a chosen underlying group algebra or a different equivalence notion. It does not classify all self-dual binary codes, all group codes, or cyclic equivalence for odd \(k\). The self-contained proof resolves the stated family, but there is a residual historical-literature risk that a general automorphism theorem for the classical \(d_{2k}^{+}\)-type family already implies the group formula or its noncyclicity in a less recognizable notation. This risk is not removed by unsuccessful keyword searches. Independent audit has not been performed.
