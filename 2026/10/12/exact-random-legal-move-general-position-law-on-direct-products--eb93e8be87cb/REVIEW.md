# Scientific review: exact terminal law for uniform legal-choice general-position growth

## Correctness (PASS)
The argument identifies exactly every legal third move after a two-vertex row, column, or diagonal start; the case counts are \(n-2+2(m-1)\), \(m-2+2(n-1)\), and \((m-2)(n-2)+2\). An induced-three-vertex-path characterization is proved from diameter two and used to show that, after the third move, the process is confined to one of four distinct families and necessarily completes it. Dividing finite counts yields the four rational probabilities, which sum to one and are positive for \(m,n\ge3\). Automorphism transitivity establishes equiprobability within each terminal type. Both asymptotic limits follow from explicit rational expressions. The standalone verifier reconstructs distances, explores every reachable legal set with exact fractions, and reproduces the laws on six boards. Finite checks are only corroboration; no infinite step relies on enumeration.

## Originality (PASS with residual risk)
The 2021 achievement-game paper establishes legal moves and adversarial outcomes for other product operations. The 2022 avoidance-game sequel concerns optimal alternating play and complexity, not random uniform-legal play. The 2023 paper establishes the scalar lower general-position number on the direct product. The 2024 paper introduces the general-position polynomial; the 2026 work gives the **already known** exact all-subset polynomial of these direct products. The present result does **not** reassert that formula or the known set classification. The stochastic absorption probabilities, type-resolved terminal distribution, and distinct balanced/rectangular limiting behaviors require comparing legal extension counts at successive states and are not provided by these references. A direct search for the random general-position process and scientific-record comparisons found no equivalent; this is not proof against all historic terminology. The nearest connected structured records concern random greedy independent sets on different graph families, not this geodesic-free process.

## Value (PASS)
The precise success law measures a natural randomized alternative to an established combinatorial achievement game. Its four absorbing geometries refine both lower and upper general-position numbers. For large balanced boards, full maximum-size outcomes occur with probability approaching one but the rare four-corner stopping event produces a nonzero limiting **mean shortfall** and a linearly growing variance. With one dimension fixed, normalized size instead approaches a nontrivial Bernoulli law and expected approximation ratio \(1/m\). These phenomena demonstrate why all-set or maximal-set counts alone do not describe behavior of natural legal-choice algorithms. The theorem determines all finite-board terminal probabilities and all size moments, rather than an arbitrary sampled instance.
