# Exact semiprime fibers for a sum–difference totient divisibility
## Finding
Let
\[
\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d
\]
and let \(\varphi(n)\) be Euler's totient.

For a composite integer \(n\) with
\[
\Omega(n)=2,
\]
the divisibility
\[
\sigma(n)-\varphi(n)\mid \sigma(n)+\varphi(n)
\tag{1}
\]
holds if and only if
\[
n=pq
\]
for distinct odd primes
\[
p<q
\]
such that
\[
p+q\mid p^2-1.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus, fixing the smaller prime \(p\), the complete solution set is generated by the divisors
\[
d\mid p^2-1,\qquad d>2p,
\]
for which
\[
q=d-p
\]
is prime.

There is also a quotient-fiber parametrization. If
\[
k=\frac{\sigma(pq)+\varphi(pq)}
        {\sigma(pq)-\varphi(pq)},
\]
then
\[
2\le k<p
\]
and
\[
(p-k)(q-k)=k^2-1.
\]
Conversely, for every integer \(k\ge2\), every factor pair
\[
a<b,\qquad ab=k^2-1,
\]
for which
\[
p=k+a,\qquad q=k+b
\]
are prime produces a solution, and every squarefree semiprime solution arises uniquely in this way.

The first values in the two-factor layer are
\[
15,\ 35,\ 95,\ 119,\ 143,\ 209,\ 287,\ 319,\ 323,\ 377,\ldots
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The theorem concerns the complete multiplicity layer
\[
\Omega(n)=2
\]
inside the composite sequence defined by (1).

Hence the only possible shapes are
\[
p^2
\]
and
\[
pq
\]
with distinct primes.

No claim is made about integers with
\[
\Omega(n)\ge3,
\]
nor about the infinitude of prime pairs admitted by the divisor parametrization.

## Proof
First let
\[
n=p^2
\]
with \(p\) prime. Then
\[
\sigma(p^2)=p^2+p+1
\]
and
\[
\varphi(p^2)=p^2-p.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sigma(p^2)-\varphi(p^2)=2p+1
\]
and
\[
\sigma(p^2)+\varphi(p^2)=2p^2+1.
\]
If (1) held, then
\[
2p+1\mid2(2p^2+1).
\]
But
\[
2(2p^2+1)-(2p+1)(2p-1)=3.
\]
Hence
\[
2p+1\mid3,
\]
which is impossible because
\[
2p+1\ge5.
\]
Thus no prime square is a solution.

Now let
\[
n=pq
\]
with distinct primes
\[
p<q.
\]
Then
\[
\sigma(pq)=(p+1)(q+1)
\]
and
\[
\varphi(pq)=(p-1)(q-1).
\]
Consequently
\[
\sigma(pq)-\varphi(pq)=2(p+q)
\]
and
\[
\sigma(pq)+\varphi(pq)=2(pq+1).
\]
Condition (1) is therefore equivalent to
\[
p+q\mid pq+1.
\tag{3}
\]

Modulo \(p+q\), one has
\[
q\equiv-p,
\]
so
\[
pq+1\equiv1-p^2.
\]
Thus (3) is equivalent to
\[
p+q\mid p^2-1,
\]
which proves (2).

Put
\[
d=p+q.
\]
Because
\[
q>p,
\]
one has
\[
d>2p.
\]
Conversely, if
\[
d\mid p^2-1,\qquad d>2p,
\]
and
\[
q=d-p
\]
is prime, then \(q>p\), and the congruence above runs backward to give (3), hence (1). This proves the divisor parametrization.

The case \(p=2\) cannot occur, since then
\[
d\mid3
\]
but
\[
d>4.
\]
Therefore all semiprime solutions are odd.

For the quotient form, set
\[
k=\frac{pq+1}{p+q}.
\]
This is a positive integer on every solution. Also
\[
pq+1<p(p+q)
\]
because \(p>1\), so
\[
k<p.
\]
The value \(k=1\) would imply
\[
pq+1=p+q,
\]
or
\[
(p-1)(q-1)=0,
\]
which is impossible. Hence
\[
2\le k<p.
\]

Now
\[
(p-k)(q-k)
=
pq-k(p+q)+k^2
=
k^2-1.
\]
Thus
\[
a=p-k,\qquad b=q-k
\]
is a positive factor pair of
\[
k^2-1.
\]

Conversely, let
\[
k\ge2,\qquad a<b,\qquad ab=k^2-1,
\]
and suppose
\[
p=k+a,\qquad q=k+b
\]
are prime. Then
\[
pq+1
=
(k+a)(k+b)+1
=
2k^2+k(a+b)
=
k(p+q).
\]
Therefore
\[
p+q\mid pq+1,
\]
so (1) holds.

Uniqueness follows because \(k\) is the displayed quotient and then
\[
a=p-k,\qquad b=q-k
\]
are forced.

## Verification
The attached `verify.py` uses exact integer arithmetic.

It enumerates every prime square and every product
\[
pq\le2{,}000{,}000
\]
of distinct primes, checks the original divisibility directly, and independently checks both equivalent parametrizations:
\[
p+q\mid p^2-1
\]
and
\[
(p-k)(q-k)=k^2-1.
\]

The computation is corroborative only. The theorem is unbounded because the proof exhausts the two possible factorization shapes with
\[
\Omega(n)=2.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A061367 is the exact database sequence of composite integers satisfying
\[
\sigma(n)-\varphi(n)\mid \sigma(n)+\varphi(n).
\]
It supplies terms and direct computational tests but does not state a semiprime classification or either divisor parametrization above.

The two component functions are separately tabulated in OEIS A051612 and A065387. In particular, A065387 records the closed formula for
\[
\sigma(n)+\varphi(n)
\]
on semiprimes. That identity simplifies one side of the present condition but does not determine which semiprimes make the ratio integral.

De Koninck and Luca study the interaction of \(\sigma\) and \(\varphi\) through the composition quotient
\[
H(n)=\frac{\sigma(\varphi(n))}{\varphi(\sigma(n))}.
\]
Their paper has primary subject classification \(11A25\), contains explicit two-prime calculations involving
\[
\frac{\sigma(pq)}{\varphi(pq)},
\]
and gives broad analytic context for these two arithmetic functions. Its stated results concern orders, averages, and densities of the composition quotient rather than the sum–difference divisibility in (1).

Targeted searches using the exact divisibility, the database identifier, the semiprime reduction
\[
p+q\mid pq+1,
\]
the equivalent condition
\[
p+q\mid p^2-1,
\]
and the quotient factorization
\[
(p-k)(q-k)=k^2-1
\]
did not locate a published theorem equivalent to or stronger than the present complete two-factor classification.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify members with
\[
\Omega(n)\ge3.
\]
It also does not prove that infinitely many quotient fibers or smaller primes \(p\) yield a prime companion \(q\).

Because the proof is elementary and the defining sequence has been public since 2002, an equivalent observation may occur in an unindexed problem note or discussion not surfaced by the searches.
