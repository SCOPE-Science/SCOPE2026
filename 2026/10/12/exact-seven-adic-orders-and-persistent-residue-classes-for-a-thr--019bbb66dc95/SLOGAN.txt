For every integer \(n\ge1\), the exact \(7\)-adic valuation of \(1+2^n+3^n\) is \(0\) outside \(n\equiv2,4\pmod6\), is \(1+\nu_7(n)\) for \(n\equiv2\pmod6\), and is \(2+\nu_7(n)+\nu_7(n-1)\) for \(n\equiv4\pmod6\). The number of exponents in the common period \(6\cdot7^{k-1}\) giving divisibility by \(7^k\) is exactly \(2\), \(8\), or \(15\), according as \(k=1\), \(k=2\), or \(k\ge3\).
