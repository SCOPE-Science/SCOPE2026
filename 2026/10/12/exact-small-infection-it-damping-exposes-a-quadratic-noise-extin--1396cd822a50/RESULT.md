# Exact small-infection Itô damping exposes a quadratic-noise extinction gap in a stochastic SEI model
## Finding
Xu and Wang study the stochastic SEI model
\[
dS=\left[rS\left(1-\frac{S}{K}\right)-\frac{\beta SI}{1+mS}\right]dt
+S(\sigma_{11}+\sigma_{12}S)\,dB_1,
\]
\[
dE=\left[\frac{\beta SI}{1+mS}-(\alpha+\mu)E\right]dt
+E(\sigma_{21}+\sigma_{22}E)\,dB_2,
\]
\[
dI=(\alpha E-\mu I)dt+I(\sigma_{31}+\sigma_{32}I)\,dB_3.
\]
Their extinction proof uses
\[
P=\rho_1E+\rho_2I,
\qquad
\rho_1=\frac{\alpha K}{\mu(1+mK)(\alpha+\mu)},
\qquad
\rho_2=\frac{K}{\mu(1+mK)}.
\]

The exact Itô correction in \(d\log P\) coming from the \(E\)- and \(I\)-noises is
\[
Q(E,I)=
\frac{\rho_1^2E^2(\sigma_{21}+\sigma_{22}E)^2+
\rho_2^2I^2(\sigma_{31}+\sigma_{32}I)^2}{2P^2}.
\]
For all \(E,I>0\),
\[
Q(E,I)\ge
\delta_*:=
\frac{\sigma_{21}^2\sigma_{31}^2}
{2(\sigma_{21}^2+\sigma_{31}^2)},
\]
and this constant is sharp:
\[
\inf_{E,I>0}Q(E,I)=\delta_*.
\]
The quadratic noise intensities \(\sigma_{22}\) and \(\sigma_{32}\) do not appear in the exact global infimum because their contribution vanishes along infection states approaching the disease-free boundary.

The source's Theorem 4.2 instead uses
\[
\frac{\sigma_{22}^2\wedge\sigma_{32}^2}{4}
\]
as a state-independent damping constant in its extinction exponent. That substitution is not valid in general. For its Example 5.2,
\[
\sigma_{21}=\sigma_{31}=0.2,\qquad
\sigma_{22}=\sigma_{32}=1,
\]
so the source uses \(0.25\), whereas
\[
\delta_*=\frac{0.2^2\,0.2^2}{2(0.2^2+0.2^2)}=0.01.
\]
The paper reports \(R_0^E=-0.0651\). Keeping every other term in its displayed bound unchanged and replacing only the invalid damping term changes that value to
\[
-0.0651+0.25-0.01\approx0.1749>0.
\]
Thus the published Lyapunov estimate does not certify almost-sure exponential extinction in Example 5.2.

This is a statement about the theorem's uniform damping step and the resulting certificate. It does not assert that the stochastic system in Example 5.2 actually persists, and it does not contradict the paper's finite-time numerical trajectories.

## Assumptions and scope
All parameters are positive, as in the source. The conclusion concerns the exact Itô correction generated by the \(E\)- and \(I\)-diffusion coefficients and the use of that correction as a uniform state-independent negative drift in the source's Lyapunov proof.

No claim is made that quadratic noise has no dynamical effect. For moderate or large \(E\) or \(I\), the terms \(\sigma_{22}E\) and \(\sigma_{32}I\) increase \(Q(E,I)\). The point is narrower and exact: a theorem that needs one lower bound valid for every positive infection state cannot use the quadratic coefficients alone, because those terms vanish at first order as the infection approaches zero.

The source's simulation algorithm and finite-time trajectories are not used as evidence for or against almost-sure extinction.

## Proof
Set
\[
a=\frac{\rho_1E}{P},\qquad
b=\frac{\rho_2I}{P}.
\]
Then \(a,b>0\) and \(a+b=1\). Hence
\[
Q(E,I)
=\frac12\left[
a^2(\sigma_{21}+\sigma_{22}E)^2+
b^2(\sigma_{31}+\sigma_{32}I)^2
\right].
\]
Because all coefficients and states are positive,
\[
Q(E,I)\ge
\frac12\left(\sigma_{21}^2a^2+\sigma_{31}^2b^2\right).
\]
Writing \(b=1-a\), the right-hand side is a strictly convex quadratic. Its minimizer is
\[
a_*=\frac{\sigma_{31}^2}
{\sigma_{21}^2+\sigma_{31}^2},
\qquad
b_*=\frac{\sigma_{21}^2}
{\sigma_{21}^2+\sigma_{31}^2},
\]
with minimum
\[
\delta_*=
\frac{\sigma_{21}^2\sigma_{31}^2}
{2(\sigma_{21}^2+\sigma_{31}^2)}.
\]

It remains to prove sharpness for the full nonlinear expression, not only for its linear lower bound. Fix the minimizing composition and, for \(\varepsilon>0\), set
\[
E_\varepsilon=\frac{a_*\varepsilon}{\rho_1},
\qquad
I_\varepsilon=\frac{b_*\varepsilon}{\rho_2}.
\]
Then
\[
P_\varepsilon
=\rho_1E_\varepsilon+\rho_2I_\varepsilon
=\varepsilon,
\]
while \(a=a_*\) and \(b=b_*\) exactly. Therefore
\[
Q(E_\varepsilon,I_\varepsilon)
=\frac12\left[
a_*^2\left(\sigma_{21}+
\frac{\sigma_{22}a_*\varepsilon}{\rho_1}\right)^2
+b_*^2\left(\sigma_{31}+
\frac{\sigma_{32}b_*\varepsilon}{\rho_2}\right)^2
\right]
\longrightarrow\delta_*
\]
as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Hence
\[
\inf_{E,I>0}Q(E,I)=\delta_*.
\]

For Example 5.2, the source specifies
\[
\sigma_{21}=\sigma_{31}=0.2,\qquad
\sigma_{22}=\sigma_{32}=1.
\]
Thus \(a_*=b_*=1/2\) and \(\delta_*=0.01\). Along the explicit ray
\[
E_\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho_1},
\qquad
I_\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho_2},
\]
the correction \(Q\) tends to \(0.01\), so it cannot be bounded below by the paper's \(0.25\).

The paper's displayed extinction exponent for Example 5.2 equals approximately \(-0.0651\) and contains the term \(-0.25\). Replacing only that term by the exact guaranteed damping \(-0.01\) yields
\[
-0.0651+0.24\approx0.1749.
\]
Accordingly, the sign condition used to invoke the published theorem is lost.

## Verification
The bundled script `verification/verify.py` checks the convex minimization symbolically through its closed formula, evaluates the Example 5.2 constants, constructs the small-infection minimizing ray, and verifies numerically that \(Q(E_\varepsilon,I_\varepsilon)\) approaches \(0.01\) while remaining far below \(0.25\) for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). It also reproduces the \(+0.24\) correction to the source's reported exponent.

The proof of the exact infimum is analytic and does not depend on numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
The motivating paper explicitly writes the nonlinear perturbations as
\[
E(\sigma_{21}+\sigma_{22}E)\,dB_2
\quad\text{and}\quad
I(\sigma_{31}+\sigma_{32}I)\,dB_3,
\]
defines its extinction criterion with
\[
-(\sigma_{22}^2\wedge\sigma_{32}^2)/4,
\]
and concludes from that criterion that stronger second-order noise may lead to extinction. Its Example 5.2 is deliberately chosen with small linear coefficients and much larger quadratic coefficients.

Earlier stochastic epidemic literature studies second-order and general nonlinear perturbations, including Han et al. (2020) and Lu et al. (2021). Those works establish that nonlinear noise can affect stochastic epidemic dynamics in their respective models. They do not imply the source-specific lower bound above: the exact infimum here follows from the particular two-component Lyapunov combination \(P=\rho_1E+\rho_2I\) and the exact diffusion coefficients printed in the 2025 SEI model.

Searches for the motivating paper together with correction, extinction, quadratic-noise, and coefficient-index terms did not locate a published correction of this calculation. This absence is supporting evidence only; the originality assessment rests principally on direct comparison with the source's theorem, proof, examples, and conclusions.

## Limitations
The result does not prove that Theorem 4.2 is false as an abstract implication; it proves that the state-independent damping estimate used in the published proof is not valid in general and that the paper's Example 5.2 no longer satisfies the same displayed sufficient bound after that step is repaired.

The finding also does not determine the true top Lyapunov exponent of the infection subsystem in Example 5.2. Finite-time numerical extinction can occur even when this particular sufficient criterion fails, and quadratic noise can still influence nonlinear transients and global behavior away from the disease-free boundary.

A different extinction proof could potentially exploit state-dependent quadratic-noise dissipation without replacing it by a uniform constant. No such alternative proof is supplied here.
