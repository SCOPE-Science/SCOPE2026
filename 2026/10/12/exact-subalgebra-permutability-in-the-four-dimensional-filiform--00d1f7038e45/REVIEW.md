# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is restricted to one explicitly defined, class-three, four-dimensional Lie algebra over arbitrary finite fields. The proof classifies every subalgebra using its image in the two-dimensional abelianization and its intersection with the two-dimensional derived ideal. The crucial statement that an independent pair of projected generators forces the whole derived plane is proved using two successive brackets. Every nonpermutable pair then occurs in one of eight family pair types (fourteen oriented types); the displayed cross-bracket argument supplies conditions and symbolic counts. Expanding their sum gives the stated polynomial and the exact probability. No characteristic-sensitive division or field-order interpolation is used. Full RREF enumeration over fields of orders 2, 3, 4, 5, and 7 independently matches each entry, including a proper characteristic-two extension field. Reproducibility is furnished by `verify.py`. **Risk:** no independent human verification; enumeration alone cannot certify the infinite-field statement.

The August 2026 Towers–Zuleta–Gutierrez comaximal-graph manuscript includes the four-dimensional filiform algebra, but counts generation graph invariants, not whether a sum of two subalgebras is closed. Its full text could not be inspected, so a hidden equivalent comparison remains unresolved. The proof supplies new cross-bracket failure categories and an exact all-field formula not implied by any inspected stated theorem. ## Value — PASS
The four-dimensional standard filiform algebra is the first nilpotent class-three Lie algebra in its canonical maximal-class series. A complete count of the newly introduced subalgebra commutativity degree, rather than a chosen small numerical case, determines a natural lattice invariant for every prime power and all characteristics. The new formula has \(\operatorname{sd}(L_q)\sim 11/q^2\), whereas the studied four-dimensional central Heisenberg stabilization has limit \(1/4\); equal ambient dimension does not control asymptotic permutability. The classification of eight nonpermutability families provides additional structural explanation. The result does not assert broad classification of filiform or arbitrary solvable Lie algebras.
