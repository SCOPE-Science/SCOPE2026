# Exact ternary length-five two-deletion optimum and equality census
## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\). For \(x\in\Sigma^5\), let \(D_2(x)\subseteq\Sigma^3\) be the set of distinct length-three words obtained by deleting exactly two coordinates. A two-deletion-correcting code is a set \(C\subseteq\Sigma^5\) such that \(D_2(x)\cap D_2(y)=\varnothing\) for all distinct \(x,y\in C\).

The exact optimum is
\[
A_3(5,2)=6.
\]
Exactly \(478\) labeled maximum codes attain this value. Under the natural order-\(12\) action generated by all six alphabet permutations and coordinate reversal, these maxima form exactly \(46\) orbits: one orbit of size \(2\), two of size \(4\), eight of size \(6\), and thirty-five of size \(12\).

Because the deletion shadows in a code are disjoint, the number of distinct length-three received words covered by a maximum code is \(\sum_{x\in C}|D_2(x)|\). Among the \(478\) maxima, the coverage distribution is
\[
18:2,\quad 19:12,\quad 20:54,\quad 21:100,\quad 22:144,\quad 23:96,\quad 24:52,\quad 25:18.
\]
Thus no maximum code is perfect: every maximum misses at least two of the \(27\) possible received words.

For the same ternary alphabet and two deletions, the exact maxima at lengths \(2,3,4,5\) are \(1,3,3,6\). Hence length five is the first length at which the optimum exceeds the alphabet size.

## Assumptions and scope
Words are ordinary ordered strings over a three-symbol alphabet. Distinct deletion outcomes are counted once, even if several pairs of deleted positions yield the same descendant. Codes are unrestricted and need not be linear, systematic, constant-composition, or perfect.

For equal-length words \(x,y\in\Sigma^5\), two-deletion-shadow disjointness is equivalent to saying that their longest common subsequence has length at most \(2\). Equivalently, their insertion/deletion Levenshtein distance is at least \(6\), and therefore satisfies the usual two-deletion criterion of distance at least \(5\).

The symmetry statement concerns only the explicit natural group generated by symbol relabeling and reversal. It does not assert that this group is the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph.

## Proof
Construct the compatibility graph \(G\) on all \(3^5=243\) ternary words, joining two words exactly when their two-deletion shadows are disjoint. A subset of vertices is a two-deletion-correcting code exactly when it is a clique in \(G\).

The verifier constructs every deletion shadow directly. Independently, it computes longest common subsequence length by dynamic programming and checks all \(\binom{243}{2}=29403\) unordered pairs. For every pair, shadow disjointness agrees with the condition that the longest common subsequence has length at most \(2\).

A complete branch-and-bound maximum-clique search uses a greedy coloring of every remaining candidate subgraph as a rigorous upper bound. It returns clique number \(6\). A separate ordered fixed-cardinality recursion then counts every \(6\)-clique and every \(7\)-clique. It finds exactly \(478\) cliques of size \(6\) and none of size \(7\). The latter independently confirms that no larger code exists.

The \(478\) maxima are then acted on by the twelve explicitly generated transformations: six alphabet permutations, with or without coordinate reversal. Orbit enumeration gives sizes \(2^1,4^2,6^8,12^{35}\), whose weighted sum is \(478\). A Burnside check independently verifies that the fixed-set counts over the twelve group elements sum to \(46\cdot12\).

The same finite search at lengths \(2,3,4,5\) gives \(1,3,3,6\). The canonical serialization of all \(478\) maxima has SHA-256 digest `7dff591068a541f40fd02801d35ef2e263638e21eadbc7c0b9d9f1a71837a99d`.

## Relationship to prior work
Chee, Ge, and Ling define \(A_q(n,t)\), give the deletion-shadow and Levenshtein formulations, and study the design-theoretic regime \(t=n-2\). Their 2010 work treats perfect \((4,2)_q\) deletion-correcting codes and, at \(q=3\), yields the known length-four optimum \(A_3(4,2)=3\). Its stated main scope is the spectra of perfect \((3,1)_q\) and \((4,2)_q\) codes, not the unrestricted \((5,2)_3\) equality problem considered here.

Kulkarni and Kiyavash give a general hypergraph/linear-programming upper bound for arbitrary alphabet size and any fixed number of deletions. Their Theorem IV.1 specializes here to
\[
\sum_{z\in\Sigma^3}\frac{1}{|D_2(z)|}=3+18\cdot\frac12+6\cdot\frac13=14,
\]
so it does not determine the exact value \(6\), still less the \(478\)-code equality census or the \(46\) natural symmetry orbits.

Targeted searches using the aliases “two-deletion correcting,” “Levenshtein distance,” “longest common subsequence,” “ternary length five,” and the exact parameters did not locate a prior statement implying the finite result above. The closest published indexed results concern asymptotic \(q\)-ary deletion codes, perfect length-four deletion codes, or different length-five code notions.

## Limitations
The result is an exact finite classification for \(q=3\), length \(5\), and two deletions. It does not give a formula for larger alphabets or lengths, nor a structural description of all \(46\) natural-group orbit representatives beyond exhaustive reconstruction. The originality comparison cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed historical enumeration; no inspected source contained the exact value together with the equality census and orbit data.

Independent audit has not been performed.
