# Exact ternary length-three comma-free codes of index two
## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{{0,1,2\}}\). For \(x,y\in\Sigma^3\), define the two nontrivial splices
\[
T_1(x,y)=(x_2,x_3,y_1),\qquad T_2(x,y)=(x_3,y_1,y_2).
\]
A code \(C\subseteq\Sigma^3\) has comma-free index at least \(2\) when \(d_H(T_i(x,y),z)\ge 2\) for every \(x,y,z\in C\) and \(i\in\{{1,2\}}\).

The exact maximum is \(|C|=4\). There are exactly nine labeled maximum codes. More precisely, every maximum is uniquely of the form
\[
C_{{j,b}}=\{{x\in\Sigma^3:x_j=b,\ x_k\ne b\text{{ for every }}k\ne j}}\}},
\]
for one \(j\in\{{1,2,3\}}\) and one \(b\in\Sigma\). Thus there are \(3\cdot3=9\) maxima.

Under the natural order-\(12\) symmetry group generated by a simultaneous permutation of the three alphabet symbols and reversal of every word, the nine maxima form two orbits. The six codes with the fixed coordinate at an end form one orbit, with stabilizer order \(2\); the three codes with the middle coordinate fixed form the other, with stabilizer order \(4\).
## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is labeled \(\Sigma=\{{0,1,2\}}\), the block length is exactly \(3\), and the required comma-free index is at least \(2\). The classification counts labeled subsets of \(\Sigma^3\); the orbit statement uses only simultaneous alphabet relabeling and reversal, which preserve the splice condition.
## Proof
The comma-free-index condition is hereditary under taking subcodes. Therefore it is enough to rule out valid five-word codes and enumerate the valid four-word codes.

There are \(\binom{{27}}{{5}}=80{,}730\) five-word subsets and \(\binom{{27}}{{4}}=17{,}550\) four-word subsets of \(\Sigma^3\). The verifier first constructs, for every ordered pair \((x,y)\), the set of words \(z\) for which at least one of \(T_1(x,y)\) or \(T_2(x,y)\) lies at Hamming distance less than \(2\) from \(z\). A candidate code is valid exactly when none of its own words lies in any of these forbidden sets for any ordered pair of its codewords.

Exhausting all five-word subsets gives zero valid candidates. Exhausting all four-word subsets gives exactly nine. The verifier then constructs the nine explicit templates \(C_{{j,b}}\) above and checks equality of the two sets: every enumerated maximum is one of the templates, and every template satisfies the defining condition. This proves both the upper bound and the complete equality classification.

Finally, the verifier applies all \(6\cdot2=12\) simultaneous alphabet-permutation/reversal symmetries to all nine maxima. It obtains orbit sizes \(6\) and \(3\), hence stabilizer orders \(2\) and \(4\), and checks that the size-\(3\) orbit is exactly the middle-fixed family.
## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py`. The checker is standard-library-only, reconstructs the full \(27\)-word universe from the definition, exhausts all \(80{,}730\) five-subsets and \(17{,}550\) four-subsets, rechecks the nine structural templates, and recomputes the full order-\(12\) symmetry action. `census.json` records the nine labeled maxima independently in human-readable form.
## Relationship to prior work
Jiggs introduced comma-free codes of index \(r\), defined by Hamming separation from all nontrivial splices, and for length \(3\) proved a construction lower bound that gives four codewords over a three-symbol alphabet. The inspected paper does not give the matching upper bound or equality classification for this parameter.

Levenshtein's 2004 survey treats codes with prescribed comma-free index as the synchronization-with-errors generalization of ordinary comma-free codes, defines the general maximum-size problem, and reviews asymptotic and difference-system results. The inspected full text does not state the exact ternary length-three index-two value or classify its equality cases.

The present result closes this smallest ternary instance left as a lower-bound construction in the historical source and shows that all equality cases have a rigid coordinate/symbol template.
## Limitations
This is a finite exact classification only for alphabet size \(3\), block length \(3\), and comma-free index at least \(2\); it does not claim a formula for other alphabet sizes, lengths, or indices. The literature search cannot exclude an obscure unindexed historical statement of the same finite classification. The oldest directly inspected index-two source is Jiggs's 1963 paper, whose accessible metadata resolves the print publication only to the year.
