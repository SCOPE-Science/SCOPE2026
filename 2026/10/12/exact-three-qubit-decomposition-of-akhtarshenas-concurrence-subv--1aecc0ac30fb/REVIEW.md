# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reconstructs the qubit specialization of Akhtarshenas's Eq. (31), identifies the squared pair-subvector norm with \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{ij}\widetilde{\rho}_{ij})\), applies the two-qubit spin-flip trace identity and equality of complementary purities for a global pure state, and then uses the three CKW equalities. The normalization is checked on GHZ, W, and Bell-pair-product states. The Meyer-Wallach relation follows from its standard qubit normalization \(Q=(T_1+T_2+T_3)/3\).

## Originality
PASS. The defining paper proposes the pair-subvector norm as a pair contribution but does not decompose it into reduced-pair concurrence and three-tangle. A major review already warns that these entries are not the true reduced-pair concurrences, so that qualitative point is treated as prior coverage rather than novelty. The closest later identities concern concurrence of assistance, where \(C_a^2=c^2+\tau\), and a 2023 concurrence-vector paper concerns bipartition vectors. No inspected source stated or implied through a single broader theorem the exact Akhtarshenas-specific identities \(A_{ij}^2=c_{ij}^2+\tau/2\) and \(A_{\mathrm{tot}}^2=3Q/2\). The residual risk of an obscure explicit assembly remains recorded.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a precise structural meaning to a published quantity advertised as pairwise: genuine GHZ-type entanglement contributes a universal half-share to every pair label. It simultaneously explains the source's GHZ/W/EPR examples and shows that the total three-qubit scalar contains no ordering information beyond Meyer-Wallach \(Q\). This is a mathematically motivated boundary clarification, not a parameter-table recomputation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest prior literature is the qualitative review observation that Akhtarshenas's pair-labelled entries are not ordinary reduced-pair concurrences, CKW's decomposition of one-tangles, concurrence-of-assistance identities for pure three-qubit reductions, and the later coefficient-minor concurrence-vector framework. The theorem is limited to pure three-qubit states.
