# Review of the even-order internal congruence theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The generating-function interpretation and Chebyshev extraction identities match the formal definitions in Saikia–Talukdar, Sections 2 and 6. The proof reduces an infinite family to a shifted-polynomial lattice preserved by the linear operator after extracting exactly three factors of two. The recurrence for its monomial action is explicitly established, and the difficult initial divisibility and derivative conditions are calculated in closed form separately for odd and even half-orders. Exact integer computations independently reproduce the base derivative identities, operator valuations, and truncated generating-function coefficients. Computations are supporting evidence, not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the risks below.** Saikia–Talukdar formulate rather than prove the even-order three-power recursion in Conjecture 6.2 and state the congruence constants only conditionally. Their proven all-odd-order theorem has disjoint tuple-order scope. Adiga–Dasappa establish several pair-only congruences, and Saikia–Sarma–Sellers prove progression-specific and low-modulus statements (including quadruples), but neither gives a uniform theorem for every even tuple order with exact dyadic difference valuations for all admissible indices. Published numerical constants are not infinite results. No covering result was identified in the targeted literature and mathematical-results searches, although unindexed equivalent statements remain possible and some pair-order specializations overlap existing congruences.

**Value — PASS.** The result proves an explicitly posed general conjecture and identifies the complete sharp two-adic coefficient profile, not merely a single small parameter. It explains why the gain is three factors of two per dyadic extraction, supplies exact dependence on the two-adic order of the number of components, and pinpoints all sharpness indices.

**Scientific limits.** The result concerns only even tuple orders and successive dyadic-index differences. It does not classify valuations of individual coefficients. External independent mathematical audit remains outstanding, and literature indexing may be incomplete.
