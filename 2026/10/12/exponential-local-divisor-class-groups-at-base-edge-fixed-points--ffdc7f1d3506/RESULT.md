# Exponential local divisor-class groups at base-edge fixed points of friendship-graph toric varieties

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\). The *friendship graph* \(F_m\) consists of \(m\) triangles sharing a single central vertex \(o\), with the other vertices \(a_i,b_i\) in triangle \(i\). Let \(X_m\subset\mathbb P^{3m-1}_{\mathbb C}\) be the reduced projective edge-monomial toric variety whose homogeneous coordinates are
\[
 U_i=o a_i,\quad V_i=o b_i,\quad W_i=a_i b_i\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\]
For every \(i\), let \(p_i=[W_i]\) be the coordinate torus-fixed point corresponding to the base edge \(a_i b_i\). Put \(k=m-1\). At \(p_i\), the standard affine chart is the **normal Gorenstein toric complete intersection**
\[
 X_m\cap D_+(W_i)\ \cong\
 \operatorname{Spec}\frac{\mathbb C[x,y,u_1,v_1,w_1,\ldots,u_k,v_k,w_k]}{
 (u_jv_j-xyw_j:1\le j\le k)}.
\]
(The indexed variables on the right refer to the other \(k\) triangles, after relabelling.) The maximal ideal at \(p_i\) is generated by all displayed variables. Its associated graded local ring and Hilbert--Samuel series are
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}\mathcal O_{X_m,p_i}
 \cong\frac{\mathbb C[x,y,u_1,v_1,w_1,\ldots,u_k,v_k,w_k]}
 {(u_jv_j:1\le j\le k)},\qquad
 \sum_{n\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb C}(\mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1})z^n
 =\frac{(1+z)^k}{(1-z)^{2m}}.
\]
In particular, the tangent cone is the reduced union of \(2^{m-1}\) linear spaces of dimension \(2m\), while the germ itself is irreducible and normal, and
\[
 e(\mathcal O_{X_m,p_i})=2^{m-1},\qquad
 \boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p_i})\cong\mathbb Z^{\,2^m-2}.}
\]
Thus the local Weil-divisor non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality defect at these Gorenstein toric points grows exponentially in \(m\) while \(\dim X_m=2m\). No claim is made about the other torus-fixed points or the complete singular locus.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings and varieties are over \(\mathbb C\). A graph edge coordinate is the degree-two monomial in variables corresponding to the incident vertices. The projective edge variety is the closure of the resulting monomial map. The points \(p_i\) are exactly the \(m\) torus-fixed points indexed by the triangle bases \(W_i\), rather than the other \(2m\) fixed points indexed by spokes. \(\operatorname{Cl}\) is the Weil-divisor class group of the normal Noetherian local domain, and \(e\) is Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity with respect to its maximal ideal. Normality is proved below; no assumption of smoothness or factoriality is imposed.

## Proof
**1. Exact chart and primeness.** By permuting the triangles we may take \(i=m\). On \(D_+(W_m)\), define
\[
 x=U_m/W_m,\ y=V_m/W_m,\quad
 u_j=U_j/W_m,\ v_j=V_j/W_m,\ w_j=W_j/W_m\quad(1\le j<m).
\]
The edge-monomial parameterization gives \(u_jv_j=xyw_j\). Let \(A\) be the displayed polynomial ring and \(I=(u_jv_j-xyw_j)\). With a lexicographic order placing all \(u_j,v_j\) before \(x,y,w_j\), the generators have pairwise coprime leading monomials \(u_jv_j\); hence they are a Gröbner basis and a regular sequence. Their quotient \(A/I\) is free as a module over \(\mathbb C[x,y,w_1,\ldots,w_k]\), with standard monomials not containing any \(u_jv_j\), and therefore has no \(xy\)-torsion. After inverting \(xy\), each \(w_j=u_jv_j/(xy)\), so
\[
 (A/I)[(xy)^{-1}]\cong
 \mathbb C[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1},u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_k,v_k],
\]
a domain. Torsion-freeness proves \(I\) prime. Its codimension is \(k\), so \(\dim A/I=2+2k=2m\). The toric chart has the same dimension, since the graph is connected and nonbipartite and its edge exponents have affine rank \(2m\); alternatively \(x,y,u_j,v_j\) are \(2m\) algebraically independent monomials. The surjection from \(A/I\) onto the edge chart therefore has zero prime kernel. This proves the asserted scheme-theoretic isomorphism.

**2. The saturated toric cone and normality.** In the character lattice of the chart torus, \(x,y,u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_k,v_k\) form a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis: the only relations among the displayed exponent vectors are \(w_j=u_j+v_j-x-y\). The dual real cone has coordinates \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma_1,\delta_1,\ldots,\gamma_k,\delta_k)\) and is exactly
\[
 \alpha,\beta,\gamma_j,\delta_j\ge0,\qquad
 \gamma_j+\delta_j\ge\alpha+\beta\quad(1\le j\le k). \tag{1}
\]
The ring is a complete intersection and hence satisfies Serre's \(S_2\). Every invariant prime divisor corresponds to an extremal ray of (1). Along rays with \(\alpha>0\), respectively \(\beta>0\), a generic divisor point has \(x=0\), respectively \(y=0\), and in each remaining triangle exactly one of \(u_j,v_j\) is zero while the other and \(w_j\) are units. The rows of the Jacobian of \(u_jv_j-xyw_j\) are then independent. Along a ray with only one \(\gamma_j\) or \(\delta_j\) nonzero, \(w_j=0\), \(x,y\) are units, and the Jacobian again has full rank. Thus all invariant codimension-one points are smooth; since the singular locus of a toric variety is torus-invariant, \(R_1\) holds. The chart is normal by \(R_1+S_2\), and Gorenstein because it is a complete intersection.

**3. Exact ray census.** The primitive extremal rays of (1) are precisely
\[
 e_{\gamma_j},\ e_{\delta_j}\quad(1\le j\le k),
\]
and, for each function \(s:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\{\gamma,\delta\}\), the two vectors
\[
 e_\alpha+\sum_{j=1}^k e_{s(j),j},\qquad
 e_\beta+\sum_{j=1}^k e_{s(j),j}. \tag{2}
\]
To see exhaustiveness, take an arbitrary point of (1). For each \(j\), remove nonnegative slack from \(\gamma_j,\delta_j\) so their sum becomes \(\alpha+\beta\); the removed slack is a sum of the coordinate rays. The remaining pairs belong to the product of \(k\) line segments with this fixed sum. They decompose as nonnegative combinations of the binary choices in (2), splitting the total weight into its \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) parts. Every vector in (2) is extremal since its vanishing coordinate inequalities and the \(k\) equalities \(\gamma_j+\delta_j=\alpha+\beta\) leave a one-dimensional intersection. Thus there are exactly \(2k+2^{k+1}\) primitive rays.

**4. Integral divisor class group.** For any normal affine toric chart with character lattice \(M\), the torus-invariant divisors provide the exact sequence
\[
 M\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\#\text{rays}}
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}\longrightarrow0.
\]
All these divisors pass through the distinguished closed torus orbit \(p_i\), and the same sequence computes the local class group there. The map has rank \(2+2k\). It has *torsion-free* cokernel: among the primitive ray vectors are the \(2k\) coordinate vectors \(e_{\gamma_j},e_{\delta_j}\) and two vectors in (2), one with \(\alpha=1\) and the other with \(\beta=1\). These form a unimodular square minor of determinant \(\pm1\). Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p_i})
 \cong\mathbb Z^{(2k+2^{k+1})-(2+2k)}
 =\mathbb Z^{2^m-2}.
\]

**5. Tangent cone and multiplicity.** At the displayed maximal ideal, the initial homogeneous form of each chart equation is \(u_jv_j\), of degree two. The \(k\) initial forms involve disjoint pairs of variables, and form a regular sequence; hence they generate the entire maximal-ideal initial ideal. The tangent-cone equation is a squarefree monomial complete intersection and has \(2^k\) minimal primes, one choice from \((u_j)\) or \((v_j)\) for each \(j\). Its Hilbert series is
\[
 \frac{(1-z^2)^k}{(1-z)^{2+3k}}
 =\frac{(1+z)^k}{(1-z)^{2+2k}}.
\]
Evaluating the numerator at \(z=1\) yields \(e=2^k\). The proof holds for every \(m\ge2\), without reliance on finite experiments.

## Verification
The accompanying `check.py` uses exact SymPy arithmetic. For \(k=1,2,3\), it independently enumerates every extreme ray from the inequality matrix by checking all candidate intersections of \((2k+1)\)-tuples of active hyperplanes, and verifies that the resulting ray sets agree with (2), with respective counts \(6,12,22\), class ranks \(2,6,14\), and an explicit unimodular minor. It also verifies Gröbner-basis generation for \(k=1,\ldots,5\). `check_output.txt` records the actual successful execution. Finite checks validate examples and algebraic bookkeeping but cannot establish the all-\(m\) theorem; that is the role of the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi--Hibi's work on graph edge rings and even-closed-walk toric binomials provides the classical framework; it does not assert the above local fixed-point class-group formula or tangent-cone classification for friendship graphs. Higashitani--Matsushita prove general torsionfreeness and an abstract facet-count formula for class groups of homogeneous edge rings satisfying the odd-cycle condition. Their global affine-cone result differs from the local class group of a projective fixed-point chart calculated here, and their paper does not give this chart's explicit cone or exponential rank formula. The novelty claim is **only** the displayed local projective chart classification and exact local invariants, not the underlying general toric divisor-class-group machinery. A specialist may recognize this as a specialization of other general toric chart results that were not located by the searches; that is a residual literature risk.

## Limitations
The theorem treats the \(m\) base-edge fixed points of the friendship graph, not the remaining \(2m\) spoke-edge fixed points and not every singular orbit. The class group is local, not the global Picard group or class group of \(X_m\) or of its affine homogeneous cone. The scheme-theoretic tangent cone is reducible although the local ring is normal and irreducible. A self-review is not an independent mathematical audit. The searches do not exclude unindexed equivalent specializations.
