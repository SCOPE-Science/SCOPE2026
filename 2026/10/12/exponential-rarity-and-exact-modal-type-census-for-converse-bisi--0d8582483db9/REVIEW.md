# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The local criterion is proved in both directions: a forward-bisimilar sibling pair extends to a parent-coherent two-modal bisimulation, and any two-way-bisimilar vertices must have matching depth and hence a common parent before the sibling criterion applies. The exact type recursion uses a \(q\)-set rather than a multiset, and each possible type has the same number of positioned representatives. The logarithmic recurrence yields an absolutely convergent entropy series, with strict positivity of the decay constant certified by independent bottom-sibling and full-sibling events. Boundary cases \(h=0\), \(a<q\), and \(a=q\) are explicit. Exhaustive two-relation partition refinement independently confirms the small cases. No inference about all heights is drawn from those experiments.

## Originality

PASS with stated antecedent risk. Worrell's strongly extensional tree representation **already contains the structural no-bisimilar-siblings viewpoint**, which is excluded from the originality claim. The quantitative result counts actual colorings of a fixed complete regular tree, derives the exact modal-type recursion and its equal-fiber multiplicity, and establishes a sharp exponential probability law with an explicit series constant and threshold. The primary modal-semantic literature of Figueira–Goren-Roig and the contraction framework of Bolander–Burigana do not state those counts in the sections inspected. Targeted phrase, recurrence, and equivalent-object searches did not reveal a published all-parameter census. Older coalgebraic counting may hide an equivalent quantitative theorem; the research claim is not represented as independently certified.

## Value

PASS. The quantity is a natural measure of whether a finite modal model permits *any* bisimulation compression when observations are constrained to a fixed alphabet. The critical alphabet size and exponentially small fraction of successful colorings give a sharp, scalable trade-off relevant to modal abstraction/model reduction. The colored, positioned-object census and entropy rate are more informative than merely proving a possible labeling exists; they quantify robustness of distinguishability across every height.
