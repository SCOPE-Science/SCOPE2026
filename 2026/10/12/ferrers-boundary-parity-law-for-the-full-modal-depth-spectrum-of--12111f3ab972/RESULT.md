# Ferrers-boundary parity law for the full modal-depth spectrum of two-agent grids

## Finding

**Theorem (all finite Ferrers valuations).** Let \(m,n\ge1\), and let
\(n\ge \lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_m\ge0\) be integers. On the
\(m\times n\) grid \(W=[m]\times[n]\), let \(\Diamond_R\) and
\(\Diamond_C\) be the existential row and column modalities, and interpret
one propositional atom by
\[p(i,j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j\le\lambda_i.\]
Let \(\mathcal D_t\) be the Boolean algebra of all subsets of \(W\)
definable by formulas of modal nesting depth at most \(t\), using \(p\)
and both named modalities. Define \(r\) to be the number of *distinct*
row lengths \(\lambda_i\), and \(c\) to be the number of *distinct*
column heights \(h_j=|\{i:\lambda_i\ge j\}|\). Thus
\(|r-c|\le1\). For every integer \(t\ge0\), the complete depth spectrum is
\[
\boxed{\log_2|\mathcal D_t|=
\begin{cases}
\min\{r^2,(t+1)^2+1\},&r=c,\\[1mm]
\min\{rc,(t+1)^2+\mathbf 1_{\{t\ \mathrm{even}\}}\},&|r-c|=1.
\end{cases}}
\]
Here the indicator is one when \(t\) is even and zero otherwise.

In particular, the subalgebra generated by \(p\) under all Boolean and
modal operations has exactly \(2^{rc}\) elements. Its first stabilization
at the full type partition occurs at depth \(r-1\) when \(r=c\);
when \(|r-c|=1\), it occurs at depth \(0\) if
\(\min(r,c)=1\), and otherwise at depth \(\min(r,c)\).
All formulas count modal types (atoms of \(\mathcal D_t\)), not the
possibly much larger number of physical cells in the original grid.

Thus the entire spectrum is independent of the actual row/column
multiplicities and depends only on the two numbers of neighborhood types.
The unequal case exhibits a genuine parity alternation absent from the
balanced case.

## Assumptions and scope

The accessibility relations are *named* and reflexive: from \((i,j)\),
\(R\) reaches exactly \((i,j')\) for all \(j'\), and \(C\) reaches exactly
\((i',j)\) for all \(i'\). All propositional Boolean connectives,
including negation, are allowed; no counting, converse, diagonal,
identity, or global modalities are assumed. The Ferrers/chain-graph
hypothesis is essential: after permuting rows and columns, the marked
cells must form a Young diagram. Empty rows and empty columns are
permitted, and a constant valuation is included. A full Boolean algebra
with \(K\) atoms has \(2^K\) elements, explaining the logarithm above.

## Proof

**1. Modal signatures.** For a finite marked frame, let \(P_t\) be the
partition of worlds by formulas of depth at most \(t\). At depth zero,
\(P_0\) has the truth-value classes of \(p\). Given \(P_t\), the
\(P_{t+1}\)-type of \((i,j)\) consists precisely of its \(P_t\)-type,
the set of \(P_t\)-types occurring in row \(i\), and the set of
\(P_t\)-types occurring in column \(j\). Indeed, the two diamond
modalities test membership of a \(P_t\)-type in either successor set,
and arbitrary Boolean combinations of these tests distinguish all
such signatures. This is an exact equivalence, not an algorithmic
approximation or a counting-multiset refinement.

**2. Reduction to four strict staircase matrices.** Rows with the same
length and columns with the same height are interchangeable in all the
signatures just defined; duplicating either kind of row/column does not
alter a set of successor types. Collapse each twin group to one
representative. If \(E\) says that an empty row is present and \(Z\)
says that an empty column is present, the reduced Ferrers matrix is,
up to order-preserving relabeling, one of the following:

* \((E,Z)=(0,0)\): an \(s\times s\) matrix with consecutive row
  lengths \(s,s-1,\ldots,1\).
* \((E,Z)=(1,1)\): an \(s\times s\) matrix with consecutive row
  lengths \(s-1,s-2,\ldots,0\).
* \((E,Z)=(1,0)\): an \((s+1)\times s\) matrix with consecutive row
  lengths \(s,s-1,\ldots,0\).
* \((E,Z)=(0,1)\): an \(s\times(s+1)\) matrix with consecutive row
  lengths \(s,s-1,\ldots,1\).

The column-boundary cases above are understood with one final zero
column when \(Z=1\). This classification follows directly by grouping
adjacent columns according to constant column height: every proper
jump between distinct row lengths contributes exactly one distinct
nonzero column-height block. In the four cases the numbers of row and
column twin classes are respectively \((s,s)\), \((s,s)\),
\((s+1,s)\), and \((s,s+1)\). In particular \(|r-c|\le1\).

**3. The staircase peeling lemma.** On any of these reduced matrices,
consider the induced partitions of row and column indices by their
sets of \(P_{t-1}\)-types, with the trivial one-block partition at
\(t=0\). The following descriptions hold, with all block counts capped
at the number of available rows or columns.

In the two balanced cases, after depth \(t\), the row indices have
\(\min(s,t+1)\) consecutive blocks, and so do the column indices.
In the no-empty-boundary case, the first \(t\) indices at each end of
the relevant order are singled out from a remaining final interval;
in the two-empty-boundary case, the order is reversed. The precise
statement is that one boundary is peeled per round in each coordinate,
not that two boundaries are simultaneously peeled. For \(t<s-1\),
all intersections of the resulting row/column blocks are monochromatic
except for the single remaining central rectangle, which contains
both \(p\)-values. For \(t\ge s-1\), every row and column is separately
identified and no mixed rectangle remains.

For the \((s+1)\times s\) case (empty row, no empty column), put
\[
R_t=\min\{s+1,2\lfloor(t+1)/2\rfloor+1\},\qquad
C_t=\min\{s,2\lfloor t/2\rfloor+1\}.
\]
At depth \(t\), there are \(R_t\) row blocks and \(C_t\) column
blocks. New extreme rows are singled out in odd-numbered rounds, and
new extreme columns in even-numbered rounds. Before full separation
there is exactly one mixed row-block/column-block rectangle; at
\(t\ge s\) there is none. For the \(s\times(s+1)\) case the same
statement holds with rows and columns exchanged.

For completeness, these are **inductive block descriptions** rather
than assumptions: initially the valuation gives a single mixed
rectangle, except in the constant cases. In a balanced strict
staircase, the only row (respectively column) having a different set
of current marked/unmarked cell-types from the remaining rows
(respectively columns) is the boundary row (column) adjacent to the
already peeled zone. Removing that row and column leaves a strict
staircase of the same form with one fewer unresolved boundary level.
In the one-sided staircase, both its fully marked first row and its
unmarked last row have unique row type sets at the first step, whereas
its columns initially share one type set. With these two rows singled
out, its first and last columns acquire distinct type sets at the next
step. The unresolved middle is again a one-sided staircase, and
induction alternates the two-row and two-column splits. Each peeling
operation exposes all monochromatic peripheral rectangles while
leaving precisely the still-unresolved central rectangle mixed.
These observations prove the stated block counts and mixed-rectangle
criterion at every round, including the finite truncation cases.

**4. Counting types and stabilization.** Distinct row blocks and
column blocks give distinct modal types by their row and column
successor-type signatures. Within a block intersection, the only
possible extra distinction is the truth value of \(p\); by Step 3,
exactly one central intersection supplies such an extra type until
stabilization. In the balanced case this yields
\((t+1)^2+1\) before depth \(s-1\), and \(s^2\) thereafter.
In the unbalanced case, while \(t<s\), the block product is
\(R_tC_t\). For even \(t=2k\) before saturation this is
\((2k+1)^2\); for odd \(t=2k+1\), it is
\((2k+3)(2k+1)\). Adding the unique mixed intersection gives
\((t+1)^2+1\) in even rounds and \((t+1)^2\) in odd rounds.
At full stabilization the count is \(s(s+1)\). For \(s=1\)
the two truth-value classes are already stable at depth zero.
This proves the formulas and sharp stabilization depths. \(\square\)

## Verification

The bundled standard-library \(verify.py\) reconstructs modal
signatures for every weakly decreasing Ferrers profile on each
\(m\times n\) grid with \(1\le m,n\le7\), including empty and
full boundary cases. It checks the formula at all depths through
stabilization and beyond: **12,854 profiles and 182,954 depth
comparisons**. It additionally checks the proposed level-by-level
row/column peeling descriptions on **12,854 structural cases**.
An independent implementation directly computes
Boolean closures and the row/column modal images of all previously
definable unions for **62** smaller profiles, plus five larger
handpicked witnesses. It reports:

`VERIFY_OK 12854 Ferrers shapes; 182954 depth comparisons; 12854 structural wavefront tests; 62 direct Boolean-closure cases; 5 further witnesses`

These are finite consistency checks. The induction above, not the
finite census, establishes the theorem for all sizes.

## Relationship to prior work

Bezhanishvili and Meadors (arXiv:2312.16754, first public December 28,
2023; primary MSC 03B45) describe the bimodal \(\mathsf{S5}^2\)
Boolean-algebra setting and its relationship with finite generation.
Figallo and Mónica-Gomes (2023) classify finite diagonal-free
two-dimensional cylindric subalgebras via partitions, but do not give
a depth-indexed spectrum for a prescribed Ferrers generator. Groote,
Martens, and de Vink (first public March 2022) study lower bounds for
general bisimulation partition refinement, not this nested-neighborhood
family. Kiefer and McKay (2020) study **multiset** neighbor-counting
color refinement, whereas the modal signatures here use sets and
have different expressivity. The earlier established complete square
staircase case is recovered when \(r=c\) and neither boundary is
empty; the new result handles all Ferrers shapes, both boundary
statuses and arbitrary row/column multiplicities, including a new
parity law when \(|r-c|=1\). No claim is made to novelty of general
modal bisimulation, cylindric-algebra duality, or Ferrers graph theory.

## Limitations

The theorem treats ordinary (non-counting) named row/column modalities,
one marked atom, and Ferrers valuations only. It does not extend by the
same formulas to arbitrary binary matrices, counting modalities, or
additional named relations. The reviewed literature did not expose
an identical depth-spectrum theorem, but an implicit earlier
formulation in chain-graph refinement or finite algebraic logic
remains possible. Mathematical review and finite checks were carried
out without independent audit.
