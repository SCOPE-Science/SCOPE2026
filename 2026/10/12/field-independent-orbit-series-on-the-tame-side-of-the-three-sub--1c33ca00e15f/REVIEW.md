# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Ordered triples of subspaces are precisely injective representations of the inward \(D_4\) quiver. The classical \(D_4\) classification has twelve indecomposables, exactly three noninjective, leaving eight one-dimensional membership blocks and one two-dimensional generic block. Krull--Schmidt therefore identifies isomorphism types in ambient dimension \(d\) with nonnegative solutions of \(\sum_S m_S+2g=d\), yielding the displayed binomial sum and generating function. The dimension-quotient formulas isolate the nine multiplicities blockwise and are additive under direct sums.

## Originality

**PASS, with a substantial folklore caveat.** The primary Laskowski--Ulrich paper states the three-versus-four Borel-complexity contrast but contains no “orbit” occurrence and no finite-dimensional enumerator. Candidate-specific published-finding corpus searches, exact web searches for the binomial sum and rational generating function, broader searches under the three-subspace and \(D_4\) terminology, and both cumulative owned ledgers found no equivalent formula. However, the formula is a short generating-function consequence of a classical textbook-level classification, so an unindexed prior computation is plausible.

## Value

**PASS.** The formula turns the qualitative tame side of a modern classification-complexity threshold into a sharp, field-independent graded orbit profile. It gives a compact quantitative invariant—degree-eight polynomial growth with explicit rational series—against which the four-subspace jump can be compared.

## Closest literature and limitations

Laskowski--Ulrich explicitly say that three distinguished subspaces over a field have only countably many countable models and cite Prest's treatment of the subspace problem. The classical \(D_4\) classification supplies the nine injective indecomposables. Neither inspected source states the exact orbit series. The retained contribution is therefore the quantitative synthesis, not the underlying \(D_4\) classification.
