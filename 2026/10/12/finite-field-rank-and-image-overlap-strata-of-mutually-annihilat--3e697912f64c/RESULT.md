# Finite-field rank and image-overlap strata of mutually annihilating square-zero matrices

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) any prime power. For \(n\ge1\), let \(N_{n,q}(r,s,u)\) be the number of ordered pairs of \(n\times n\) matrices \((X,Y)\) over \(k\) satisfying
\[
X^2=Y^2=XY=YX=0,\quad
\operatorname{rank}X=r,\quad\operatorname{rank}Y=s,\quad
\dim(\operatorname{im}X\cap\operatorname{im}Y)=u.
\]
Set \(t=r+s-u\) and \(d=n-t\), and define
\[
S_q(a,b)=\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}(q^a-q^j)
\]
for integers \(0\le b\le a\), with \(S_q(a,b)=0\) if \(b>a\). The product is \(1\) when \(b=0\). For \(0\le u\le\min(r,s)\), \(t\le n\), and \(d\ge\max(r,s)\), the exact count is
\[
\boxed{N_{n,q}(r,s,u)=
\begin{bmatrix}n\\r\end{bmatrix}_q
\begin{bmatrix}r\\u\end{bmatrix}_q
\begin{bmatrix}n-r\\s-u\end{bmatrix}_q
q^{(r-u)(s-u)}S_q(d,r)S_q(d,s).}
\]
For every other triple of nonnegative integers, \(N_{n,q}(r,s,u)=0\). Here \(\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}_q\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient, counting \(b\)-dimensional subspaces of \(k^a\). The formula gives every joint rank/image-overlap stratum and hence, by summing, the complete count of ordered pairs satisfying the four annihilation equations.

When \(n=2m\) and \(r=s=m\), necessarily \(u=m\). The number of pairs in this maximal-rank stratum is
\[
\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}2m\\m\end{bmatrix}_q\lvert\operatorname{GL}_m(q)\rvert^2.}
\]
This is a boundary case of the same formula, not a separate classification result.

## Assumptions and scope
All counts concern ordered pairs on a *fixed, labeled* vector space of dimension \(n\) over the finite field \(k\), not simultaneous-conjugacy classes or equivalence classes of modules. The zero matrix is allowed. Define \(\operatorname{im}\) and \(\ker\) over \(k\). The local commutative algebra \(\Lambda=k[x,y]/(x,y)^2\) has radical generated by \(x,y\); a representation \(\Lambda\to M_n(k)\) is exactly such a pair. For the associated module \(M\), its radical \(JM=\operatorname{im}X+\operatorname{im}Y\) has dimension \(t=r+s-u\), so the overlap parameter refines the radical-layer data. No restriction on the characteristic is necessary.

## Proof
Write \(E=k^n\), \(U=\operatorname{im}X\), \(V=\operatorname{im}Y\), and \(W=U+V\). Because \(X^2=XY=0\), both \(U\) and \(V\) lie in \(\ker X\); similarly \(Y^2=YX=0\) implies \(W\subseteq\ker Y\). Thus \(X\) and \(Y\) factor uniquely through the same quotient \(E/W\) of dimension \(d=n-r-s+u\). Conversely, for **every** choice of subspaces \(U,V\subseteq E\), any pair of surjections \(E/W\twoheadrightarrow U\) and \(E/W\twoheadrightarrow V\), followed by inclusions in \(E\), gives exactly a pair in the required stratum. The constructions are mutually inverse because the images are prescribed, so there is no overcount from quotient bases.

Now count pairs \((U,V)\) with \(\dim U=r\), \(\dim V=s\), and \(\dim(U\cap V)=u\). There are \(\begin{bmatrix}n\\r\end{bmatrix}_q\) possibilities for \(U\). Pick \(T=U\cap V\) in \(\begin{bmatrix}r\\u\end{bmatrix}_q\) ways. The image \((V+U)/U\subset E/U\) has dimension \(s-u\), so it has \(\begin{bmatrix}n-r\\s-u\end{bmatrix}_q\) possibilities. For fixed \(T\) and this quotient image, every eligible \(V/T\) is the graph of a linear map from an \((s-u)\)-space to \(U/T\), giving precisely \(q^{(r-u)(s-u)}\) possibilities. This proves the subspace factor without identifying \(V\) with a chosen complement.

Finally, the number of surjections from a \(d\)-space onto a fixed \(r\)-space is \(S_q(d,r)\): count ordered independent rows of an \(r\times d\) coefficient matrix. The choice for \(Y\) is independent once \(U,V\) are fixed, giving \(S_q(d,s)\). Multiplying all factors proves the displayed identity. Nonvanishing is equivalent to the stated inequalities because each positive Gaussian binomial coefficient and valid surjection count is positive at every prime power.

For the final boundary, \(r=s=m\) and \(n=2m\) imply \(d=u\); surjectivity requires \(u\ge m\), whereas intersections satisfy \(u\le m\), so \(u=m\). The images coincide in a common \(m\)-subspace, which can be chosen in \(\begin{bmatrix}2m\\m\end{bmatrix}_q\) ways. Both quotient-to-image maps are isomorphisms, independently chosen in \(\lvert\operatorname{GL}_m(q)\rvert\) ways. The boundary formula follows.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently enumerates matrices over fields of orders \(2,3,4\), with extension-field arithmetic for \(q=4\). For dimensions and orders \((n,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2)\), it counts all pairs satisfying the relations, computes ranks and image intersections, and compares every nonempty stratum with the formula. It reports `VERIFY_OK` after all comparisons. Totals are respectively \(10,33,76,148,1665,8296\). These experiments test small instances only; the proof above establishes all \(n\) and prime powers.

## Relationship to prior work
Morrison [1], Section 5, studies the same three-dimensional radical-square-zero algebra and identifies its representations with pairs of maps from a top space to a radical space; his main results concern open orbits and irreducible components. Schröer [2] studies the more general annihilating-nilpotent matrix variety \(V(n,a,b)\), including \(a=b=2\), over an algebraically closed field, classifying components and degenerations. Bleher–Chinburg–Huisgen-Zimmermann [3] determine irreducible components and generic structure of radical-square-zero representation varieties. Oblak [4] classifies compatible Jordan forms for annihilating nilpotent pairs. Huang [5] enumerates arbitrary mutually annihilating pairs, with a different set of relations and without the joint square-zero rank/image-overlap frequencies. The result here is an explicit point-count distribution at fixed \((r,s,u)\), not a new orbit classification, component theorem, or formula for the unrestricted annihilation variety. The underlying quotient-map factorization is classical; the refinement is the complete simultaneous finite-field count.

## Limitations
The result does not count simultaneous similarity classes and does not assign orbit sizes or classify indecomposable modules. It does not assert that the strata are geometrically irreducible. Existing quiver Hall-algebra and representation-variety machinery may admit an equivalent indirect formulation; exhaustive bibliographic priority is not claimed. The proof assumes exact matrix relations, not approximate annihilation or noncommuting variants with only some products zero.
