# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** A stationary solution has the only possible infection-age profile \(i_*(a)=J_*S(a)\). Its norm is \(J_*m\), so the displayed endemic coordinates satisfy the stipulated \(L^1\) condition exactly when \(m<\infty\). The downstream equations and positive threshold reduce by exact algebra, not approximation. For integrable hazard, survival has a positive tail; for power-law hazard, direct integration gives the sharp \(k=1\) transition. Characteristics establish the transient mass identity and the conditional Tauberian-style ratio; no unproved convergence of infection incidence is assumed. The attached rational/symbolic consistency aid passed, but the analytic proof supplies the infinite-age justification.

**Originality (PASS, scoped).** The 2025 HIV transactivation paper's model (1.2), phase space and Theorem 2.1 were checked directly. The derived formal equilibrium is known; its failure to belong to the source's stated \(L^1\) space under integrable hazard is not accounted for in the theorem. Althaus–De Boer (2010) motivates age-dependent transactivation. Wang–Yang–Xu (2018) and Nelson et al. (2004) provide relevant earlier age-structured HIV equilibrium theory; their inspected death-rate settings are materially different. The general stationary renewal profile and characteristics are classical and excluded from the novelty claim. Database matching and literature searches cannot rule out unindexed work.

**Value (PASS).** Infection-age mass is a state-space requirement, not merely an algebraic coefficient: a finite total hazard leaves a positive fraction indefinitely in the unactivated compartment, and even certain almost-sure activation laws have infinite mean residence. The exact \(k=1\) threshold at unchanged \(\mathcal R_0\), with diverging total age mass, separates biologically interpretable finite-reservoir equilibria from misleading formal ones. The result states a necessary correction to an existing mathematical modeling framework. It does not judge the paper's particular discretized runs or global stability of trajectories.
