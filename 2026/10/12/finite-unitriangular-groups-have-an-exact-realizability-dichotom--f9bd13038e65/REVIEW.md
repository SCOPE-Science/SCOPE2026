# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. For even \(q\), the ring \(\mathbf F_2I_n\oplus N_n(\mathbf F_q)\) is closed, has nilpotent radical \(N_n(\mathbf F_q)\), and has units exactly \(I_n+N_n(\mathbf F_q)\). For \(n=2\), the group is the elementary abelian group \(C_p^f\), so Ditor's exact odd-order classification gives the Mersenne condition. For \(n\ge3\) in odd characteristic, the elementary matrices \(I+E_{12}\) and \(I+E_{23}\) do not commute, while every realizable finite odd-primary \(p\)-group is elementary abelian. The packaged checker directly verifies the even construction in representative prime and nonprime fields.

## Originality

PASS. The 2024 primary paper studies Fuchs' problem for special and affine linear groups but contains no unitriangular theorem in its searchable full text. The odd-primary \(p\)-group paper supplies the obstruction, while the 2-group paper shows that even-primary realizability is nontrivial but likewise contains no unitriangular classification. Searches under unitriangular, \(\mathrm{UT}_n(q)\), algebra-group, and group-of-units formulations found no covering theorem. The main residual risk is an older or unindexed observation using different algebra-group terminology.

## Value

PASS. The theorem completely classifies a standard all-ranks finite linear family in Fuchs' problem and gives an explicit local realizing ring of order exactly twice the group order throughout the even-field case. The exceptional size-two Mersenne boundary makes the classification sharp rather than merely sufficient.
