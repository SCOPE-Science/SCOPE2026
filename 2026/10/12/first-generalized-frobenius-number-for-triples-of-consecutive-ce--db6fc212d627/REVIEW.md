# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The three reduction identities are exact polynomial identities. They give an exhaustive, terminating reduction of all nonnegative pairs to a unique L-shaped normal form per residue. The second-minimum lemma is proved by reversing the **last** step of any reduction from an alternative pair: the last reverse step must be feasible from the normal form and costs one of \((n+1)a\), \((n+2)a\), \((2n+3)a\); each feasible move is a genuine witness. Therefore the formula for the entire second Apéry layer is exact, including multiplicities of distinct coefficient representations. The three corner maxima are compared symbolically, with explicit treatment of \(n=3,4,5\), giving the piecewise cubic. A separate finite weight-ordered coefficient-pair enumeration agrees with all the second Apéry values over \(3\le n\le80\); it is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected statements

Yin–Mu–Komatsu (2024) explicitly exclude the necessary centered-square generalized-star coefficient \(q=2\) from their \(p\)-Frobenius theorems. Chatterjee–Narula (2025) treat these same three centered-square generators but publicly report only ordinary Frobenius and Sylvester numbers; the full text was not accessible and so absence of a hidden \(p=1\) result is not asserted. In particular, their announced results do not imply this first generalized Frobenius classification. Earlier pair-only OEIS formulas, ordinary consecutive squares/cubes results and eventual-quasi-polynomial theory do not determine this exact second layer. Targeted mathematical-record and public-source searches did not uncover the piecewise \(p=1\) result. The inaccessible relevant paper and unknown/unindexed equivalents are recorded as residual risks.

## Value — PASS

Counting two representations is a standard intrinsic next threshold for a natural three-generator numerical semigroup, motivated by published \(p\)-Frobenius programs. The complete residuewise second Apéry layer is a structural calculation for an infinite family, and the exact threshold at \(n=6\) reveals a genuine switch of extremal normal form. This is a substantive classification rather than a renamed known \(p=0\) theorem or a small finite computation.

## Limitations and risks

Claims hold for \(n\ge3\) and only for the first generalized (one-representation) threshold. Finite checking does not establish the infinite result. The full text of the 2025 same-generator preprint was inaccessible; an unreported \(p=1\) statement there or elsewhere remains possible.
