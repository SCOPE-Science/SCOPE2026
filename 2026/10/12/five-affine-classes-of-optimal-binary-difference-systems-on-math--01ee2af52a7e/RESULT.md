# Five affine classes of optimal binary difference systems on \(\mathbb Z_{19}\)
## Finding
In \(\mathbb Z_{19}\), a binary index-one difference system of sets has the minimum possible redundancy \(6\) exactly when its two blocks are disjoint triples whose \(18\) ordered external differences contain every nonzero residue exactly once. There are exactly \(2{,}964\) ordered optimal systems. Under affine relabelings \(x\mapsto ux+t\), with \(u\in\mathbb Z_{19}^{\times}\), together with interchange of the two block labels, these systems form exactly five orbits, represented by \((\{0,1,2\},\{3,6,9\})\), \((\{0,1,2\},\{3,6,12\})\), \((\{0,1,2\},\{3,12,15\})\), \((\{0,1,4\},\{3,8,14\})\), and \((\{0,1,8\},\{2,11,15\})\). Their orbit sizes are \(684,684,684,684,228\), with stabilizer orders \(1,1,1,1,3\); the last stabilizer is generated by \(x\mapsto7x+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
A binary index-one difference system of sets on \(\mathbb Z_{19}\) is an ordered pair \((A,B)\) of disjoint subsets such that, among the ordered external differences \(a-b\) with the two entries taken from different blocks, every nonzero residue occurs at least once. The redundancy is \(|A|+|B|\). The equivalence considered here is exactly the action generated by affine relabelings \(x\mapsto ux+t\), where \(u\in\mathbb Z_{19}^{\times}\), and interchange of the two block labels. No claim is made about a larger, non-affine notion of equivalence.

## Proof
Levenshtein's lower bound for a difference system of sets with alphabet size \(q\), index \(\rho\), and length \(n\) is
\[
r\ge \sqrt{\frac{q\rho(n-1)}{q-1}}.
\]
For \((q,\rho,n)=(2,1,19)\), this gives \(r\ge6\). Equality holds only for a perfect regular system, so an optimal system must have \(|A|=|B|=3\). Conversely, two disjoint triples are optimal exactly when their \(2\cdot3\cdot3=18\) ordered external differences are the \(18\) nonzero residues once each.

The finite classification therefore reduces to all unordered pairs of disjoint triples in \(\mathbb Z_{19}\). The verifier examines every such pair directly from the definition, with no sampling or heuristic pruning. It finds \(1{,}482\) unordered systems, hence \(2{,}964\) ordered systems. It then applies all \(19\cdot18=342\) affine maps to the unordered census and all \(684\) combinations of an affine map with an optional block swap to the ordered census. The resulting ordered orbit sizes are \(684,684,684,684,228\), whose sum is \(2{,}964\). Orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(1,1,1,1,3\). For the last representative, the affine stabilizer is \(\{x\mapsto x,\ x\mapsto7x+1,\ x\mapsto11x+8\}\), so \(x\mapsto7x+1\) generates its order-three stabilizer.

## Relationship to prior work
Difference systems of sets were introduced for synchronization codes by Levenshtein. The 2004 survey treats the comma-free-code problems motivating difference systems and gives a day-resolved public bibliographic anchor. Tonchev and Wang give the sharp lower bound used above, an exhaustive general search algorithm, and printed tables of optimal systems; their printed binary table covers lengths \(7\) through \(12\) and gives representative solutions rather than an equality-case count or affine-orbit census at length \(19\). Zhou and Tang treat optimal and perfect difference systems explicitly as synchronization-code constructions. Chisaki and Miyamoto study regular systems whose blocks are triples and include a modulus-\(19\) construction, but the inspected \(19\)-point example has six blocks and index \(15\), so it does not imply the binary two-block classification here.

## Limitations
The novelty comparison is against the inspected published sources and semantic/exact-phrase searches described in the accompanying review. A historical extended online table mentioned by Tonchev and Wang was not available in the inspected article text; it could contain an optimal representative at length \(19\), which would not by itself imply the count or five-orbit classification but remains an originality risk. An obscure unindexed external-difference-family enumeration could also overlap the classification. The foundational 1971 source was verified only to year-level publication metadata, so no day has been invented for it; the structured date field uses the earliest directly relevant source for which an exact public date was verified.
