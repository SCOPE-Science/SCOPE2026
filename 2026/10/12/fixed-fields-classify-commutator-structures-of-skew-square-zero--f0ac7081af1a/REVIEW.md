# Scientific review: fixed-field classification of skew square-zero commutators

## Correctness
PASS. Multiplication in the Ore quotient gives the exact commutator formula. Solving its one-equation centralizer condition provides all \(q+2\) centralizers and their sizes, from which the commuting graph follows. Counting solutions of a nonzero linear functional proves each commutator fiber. For equal fixed fields the two difference maps have the same image; a complement construction produces an additive bijection intertwining them, and therefore *all* commutators. The radical and residue-field argument distinguishes ring isomorphism and anti-isomorphism. The finite verifier compared complete fibers, centralizers, graph component sizes and an explicit \(\mathbb F_{32}\) intertwiner. Formal validity does not rest on enumeration.

## Originality
PASS, with historical terminology risk. Kinyon (2026) proves an already-classical sharp bound but not the commutator classification. Raghavendran (1969, Theorem 10) classifies the underlying matrix rings, so the ring family and the distinctness of automorphism parameters are known ingredients. Dutta--Fasfous--Nath (2019, Theorem 2.1) proves the *generic* CC-ring clique-union theorem, so the abstract graph mechanism is covered; it does not supply the centralizer sizes for this family. Dutta--Basnet (2017) proves the generic isoclinism-to-graph implication, not the fixed-field isoclinism criterion. Dutta--Basnet--Nath (2018) treats commuting probabilities by other classifications, not the full commutator fiber distribution of this family. Searches for skew dual numbers, Galois-twist matrix rings, radical-square-zero centralizers, fixed-field isoclinism and commutator fibers found no corresponding class-specific statement. Historical equivalent coverage cannot be excluded solely by search.

## Value
PASS. This is an exact classification across all finite fields and nontrivial Galois twists, not an isolated count. The fixed field sharply delineates information retained by commutation and information lost by it. The explicit \(\mathbb F_{32}\) pair is a nonisomorphic, non-anti-isomorphic collision even under the full additive commutator word map; the extremal finite-zero-divisor context makes the distinction natural rather than an arbitrary slice. The computation also supplies the complete fiber law and centralizer profiles needed to study related graph and probability invariants.

## Limits and closest prior results
The older classifications and generic clique and isoclinism implications are already known and are not claimed as discoveries. The claim addresses only rings with a one-dimensional square-zero radical over a finite residue field. An older isoclinism-specific classification of this matrix family remains a residual risk. Only self-review was performed; it is not independent certification.
