# Fixed-observable multi-photon spectral reach extends through \(\min\{n,m-1\}\)
## Finding
For the rescaled fixed-observable hypothesis family \(H_g\) of arXiv:2608.01365v1, take integers \(m\ge2\), \(L\ge1\), and \(n\ge1\), and set
\[
d=\min\{n,m-1\}.
\]
There exists a real polynomial \(g\) of total degree at most \(n\) such that
\[
\mathcal P_{dL}\subseteq H_g,
\]
where \(\mathcal P_N\) denotes the real trigonometric polynomials of degree at most \(N\). Thus the source's constructive fixed-observable spectral reach can be strengthened from \(\min\{n,m-2\}L\) to \(\min\{n,m-1\}L\).

In particular, applying the same periodic Jackson inequality used in the source gives, for every \(K\)-times continuously differentiable periodic target \(f\),
\[
\inf_{h\in H_g}\|f-h\|_\infty
\le C_K\,\|f^{(K)}\|_\infty (dL)^{-K}.
\]
This proves a constructive gain from the \(m-1\)-st photon whenever \(n\) reaches that value. It does not prove that \(m-1\) is a saturation threshold.

## Assumptions and scope
The family \(H_g\) is the augmented fixed-observable family used by the source, which allows a real global rescaling coefficient. The statement therefore concerns that mathematical hypothesis family. It does not assert the same approximation statement for the unrescaled physical expectation-value family.

The source representation theorem identifies outputs with \(\pi_g(y)=g(y_1,\ldots,y_m)\), where \(g\) is a real polynomial of total degree at most the photon number and \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_m)\) lies in
\[
\Delta=\{y_j\in\mathcal P_L:y_j\ge0,\ \sum_{j=1}^m y_j=1\}.
\]
We use the same finite-dimensional open-mapping implication as the source: if \(y\) is in the relative interior of \(\Delta\), \(\pi_g(y)=0\), and the derivative on the affine tangent space is onto \(\mathcal P_N\), then \(\pi_g(\Delta)\) contains a neighborhood of zero in \(\mathcal P_N\); the allowed scalar rescaling then yields \(\mathcal P_N\subseteq H_g\).

## Proof
The case \(d=1\) is immediate. Put \(a=1/m\), take the constant interior point \(y_j=a\), and let \(g=x_1-a\). Then \(\pi_g(y)=0\), and on the tangent space \(\sum_jv_j=0\) the derivative is \(v_1\), which ranges over all of \(\mathcal P_L\).

Assume now \(d\ge2\). Put \(a=1/m\) and choose \(0<\varepsilon<a/\sqrt2\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_1(x)&=a+\varepsilon\cos(Lx),\\
y_2(x)&=a+\varepsilon\sin(Lx),\\
y_j(x)&=a,\qquad 3\le j\le m-1,\\
y_m(x)&=a-\varepsilon(\cos(Lx)+\sin(Lx)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every component is strictly positive and the components sum to one, so \(y\) lies in the relative interior of \(\Delta\).

Let \(T_r\) be the ordinary Chebyshev polynomial satisfying \(T_r(\cos t)=\cos(rt)\), and define
\[
g(x_1,\ldots,x_m)
=(x_1-a)^2+(x_2-a)^2-\varepsilon^2
+\sum_{j=3}^d (x_j-a)T_{j-1}\!\left(\frac{x_1-a}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]
The summand indexed by \(j\) has total degree \(j\), so \(\deg g\le d\le n\). At the displayed \(y\), all terms in the sum vanish and
\[
\pi_g(y)=\varepsilon^2(\cos^2(Lx)+\sin^2(Lx))-\varepsilon^2=0.
\]
At this point the nonzero partial derivatives are
\[
\partial_1g(y)=2\varepsilon\cos(Lx),\qquad
\partial_2g(y)=2\varepsilon\sin(Lx),
\]
and, for \(3\le j\le d\),
\[
\partial_jg(y)=T_{j-1}(\cos(Lx))=\cos((j-1)Lx).
\]
The extra \(x_1\)-derivatives of the Chebyshev summands vanish because their factors \(x_j-a\) vanish at the base point.

For arbitrary \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\in\mathcal P_L\), set the unused components to zero and choose \(v_m=-\sum_{j=1}^dv_j\). Since \(g\) is independent of \(x_m\), these tangent vectors show that the derivative image contains
\[
\cos(Lx)\mathcal P_L+\sin(Lx)\mathcal P_L
+\sum_{r=2}^{d-1}\cos(rLx)\mathcal P_L.
\]
The first two summands equal \(\mathcal P_{2L}\). Indeed the product-to-sum identities recover every sine and cosine frequency from zero through \(2L\). Inductively, if frequencies through \(rL\) are already present, then multiplying \(\cos(rLx)\) by \(\cos(jx)\) and \(\sin(jx)\), for \(0\le j\le L\), gives the frequencies \(rL+j\) up to \((r+1)L\), while the companion frequencies \(rL-j\) are already available. Hence the displayed span is exactly \(\mathcal P_{dL}\).

Therefore the derivative of \(\pi_g\) at \(y\), restricted to the tangent space of the simplex constraint, is onto \(\mathcal P_{dL}\). The source's open-mapping step gives a neighborhood of zero in \(\pi_g(\Delta)\), and the scalar rescaling allowed in \(H_g\) expands that neighborhood to all of \(\mathcal P_{dL}\).

## Verification
The proof above is analytic for every allowed \(m,n,L\). The accompanying checker independently builds the real Fourier coefficient vectors generated by the displayed derivative and verifies exact rational rank \(2dL+1\) for \(1\le L\le5\) and \(1\le d\le7\). Those finite checks corroborate the span identities but are not used as a proof of the quantified theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
The source paper proves a representation theorem for multi-photon quantum neural networks and attempts a fixed-observable construction with \(d=\min\{n,m-2\}\). A public technical review of arXiv:2608.01365v1 identifies a cancellation in that construction and gives a single explicit counterexample to the claimed threshold at \(m=3,n=2,L=1\): its circle construction realizes \(\mathcal P_2\). The same review says that a threshold at \(m-1\) appears plausible, but it does not prove an all-parameter inclusion.

The present statement strictly extends that isolated example: one polynomial template works for every \(m,n,L\) and realizes the full space \(\mathcal P_{\min\{n,m-1\}L}\). The paper's Sard dimension bound is only a necessary upper bound on possible coverage and does not imply this constructive inclusion.

## Limitations
The result does not prove optimality or saturation at photon number \(m-1\). In fact, the source's dimension-counting obstruction can leave room above degree \((m-1)L\). The theorem also inherits the source's augmented scalar-rescaling convention in \(H_g\); no claim is made that an unrescaled physical fixed observable approximates arbitrary-amplitude targets. Finally, the theorem is an existence result for a specially chosen observable polynomial, not a statement about generic or randomly chosen fixed observables.
