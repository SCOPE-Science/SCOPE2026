# Fixed-point local products and Catalan multiplicities for staircase Ferrers toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge3\), the projective staircase Ferrers toric variety admits **uniform algebraic product charts at every torus-fixed point**:
\[
D_+(x_{ij})\cong\mathbb A^{2(m+1-i-j)}\times C_{i-1}\times C_{j-1},
\qquad 1\le i,j\le m,\quad i+j\le m+1.
\]
Here \(C_t\) is the affine Ferrers cone defined below, and the designated fixed point is the product of the three origins. This product decomposition determines the **complete local Hilbert–Samuel series**:
\[
\sum_{d\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb C}(\mathfrak m_{ij}^d/\mathfrak m_{ij}^{d+1})z^d=
\frac{N_{i-1}(z)N_{j-1}(z)}{(1-z)^{2m-2}},
\]
where \(N_0(z)=1\) and \(N_t(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}t^{-1}\binom tk\binom t{k+1}z^k\) for \(t\ge1\). Consequently
\[
e(\mathcal O_{X_m,P_{ij}})=\operatorname{Cat}_{i-1}\operatorname{Cat}_{j-1},
\qquad
\dim T_{P_{ij}}X_m=2m-2+\binom{i-1}{2}+\binom{j-1}{2},
\]
with \(\operatorname{Cat}_t=(t+1)^{-1}\binom{2t}{t}\). Exactly four torus-fixed points are smooth. The largest fixed-point multiplicity, \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\), is attained precisely at the two extremal points \(P_{m,1}\) and \(P_{1,m}\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Write \(G_m\) for the Ferrers bipartite graph with edges \((p,q)\) for \(1\le p,q\le m\) and \(p+q\le m+1\), and define the projective toric variety
\[
X_m=\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb C[u_pv_q:(p,q)\in E(G_m)].
\]
The standard projective monomial coordinates are \(x_{pq}\). Its graph is connected, with \(2m\) vertices; hence \(X_m\) has dimension \(2m-2\). Each edge labels a torus-fixed point \(P_{ij}\). Define \(C_0=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\). For \(t\ge1\), define
\[
C_t=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[b_1,\ldots,b_t,\ a_rb_q:1\le r\le t,\ 1\le q\le t+1-r]\subset\mathbb A^{t(t+3)/2}.
\]
The generators displayed inside the semigroup algebra are given degree one for Hilbert-series purposes. It is a Ferrers edge ring for the diagram whose column lengths are \((t+1,t,\ldots,2)\), in the convention of Corso–Nagel. We study the local rings at the torus-fixed points only, not the full singular-locus scheme.

## Proof
**1. Coordinates on an affine chart.** Fix an admissible pair \((i,j)\), and let \(H=m+1-j\) and \(L=m+1-i\). On \(D_+(x_{ij})\), introduce Laurent coordinates \(a_p=u_p/u_i\) for \(p\ne i\), and \(b_q=v_q/v_j\) for \(q\ne j\), setting \(a_i=b_j=1\). The chart ring is the subalgebra generated by all available monomials \(a_pb_q\), namely those with \(p+q\le m+1\). These Laurent coordinates have the expected fraction field because the graph is connected; no unproved independence assumption is used.

Partition the generators into three disjoint variable blocks. The southwest block consists of \(b_q\) for \(1\le q<j\) and \(a_pb_q\) for \(H<p\le m\) and \(1\le q\le m+1-p\). Reindexing \(p=H+r\) identifies it with \(C_{j-1}\). The northeast block consists of \(a_p\) for \(1\le p<i\) and \(a_pb_q\) for \(L<q\le m\) and \(1\le p\le m+1-q\). Interchanging the two bipartition sides and reindexing identifies it with \(C_{i-1}\). The remaining primitive generators are the free variables \(a_p\) with \(i<p\le H\) and \(b_q\) with \(j<q\le L\); their number is \(2(m+1-i-j)\).

Any remaining allowed monomial \(a_pb_q\) must satisfy \(p\le H\) and \(q\le L\). Its individual factors are both in the displayed primitive lists (with \(a_i=b_j=1\)), so the monomial is redundant. Conversely every displayed primitive generator occurs as the ratio of an original edge coordinate to \(x_{ij}\). Each of the three lists uses disjoint Laurent variables, so their exponent semigroups form a direct sum: there are no cross-factor binomial relations. This proves the asserted isomorphism of complete affine charts, not merely their completions.

**2. Hilbert–Samuel series.** The southwest and northeast semigroup rings are standard graded, as is the polynomial block, and all their nonconstant monomials have positive degree; hence the maximal ideal of their product origin is the positive-degree ideal. Its associated graded ring is exactly the product graded ring. Corso–Nagel, Example 5.11, computes the Hilbert numerator for the Ferrers shape \((t+1,t,\ldots,2)\) and yields
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(C_t,z)=N_t(z)/(1-z)^{2t}.
\]
Indeed its numerator coefficient \(\binom{t-1}{k}^2-\binom{t-1}{k-1}\binom{t-1}{k+1}\) is \(t^{-1}\binom tk\binom t{k+1}\). Multiplying with the polynomial-factor series yields denominator exponent \(2(i-1)+2(j-1)+2(m+1-i-j)=2m-2\), as claimed. In particular the multiplicity is the numerator evaluated at one: \(N_t(1)=\operatorname{Cat}_t\), by that same classical Ferrers degree formula.

**3. Embedding dimension and extremal points.** The cone \(C_t\) has exactly \(t+t(t+1)/2\) distinct indecomposable edge-semigroup generators: none decomposes as a product of two positive-degree elements, because all displayed generators have degree one in the edge-semigroup grading (equivalently, insert a dummy row variable before dehomogenizing). Its dimension is \(2t\); thus the excess tangent dimension is \(\binom t2\). Add the factor contributions to derive the tangent-space dimension. Since \(X_m\) has dimension \(2m-2\), the point \(P_{ij}\) is smooth exactly if \(i,j\le2\). These give exactly four points when \(m\ge3\): \((1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(2,2)\), with the last point admissible when \(m\ge3\). Finally, the Catalan sequence is nondecreasing and strictly supermultiplicative when both positive indices sum to at least two; \(\operatorname{Cat}_a\operatorname{Cat}_b\le\operatorname{Cat}_{a+b}\le\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\) for \(a=i-1\), \(b=j-1\). At the bound \(a+b=m-1\), strictness holds when \(a,b>0\). Hence the only maximizers are the two extreme coordinates \((i,j)=(m,1)\) and \((1,m)\).

## Verification
The standalone script `ferrers_local_checker.py` uses Python standard libraries and verifies the semigroup factorization and the independently derived Jacobian tangent count in all 285 charts for \(2\le m\le11\). It separately verifies the Narayana numerator against direct enumeration of edge-semigroup monomials for \(1\le t\le6\), including the zero-degree and boundary cases. The recorded output is in `ferrers_local_checker_output.txt`. Run `python3 ferrers_local_checker.py` from the directory containing that script. These checks are bounded tests; all-parameter correctness rests on the direct-sum semigroup proof and the published Ferrers Hilbert-series formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Corso and Nagel compute global Hilbert series, multiplicity, and presentation data for Ferrers toric rings; those formulas are used, not claimed as new. Gonciulea and Lakshmibai determine qualitative singularity strata of broader ladder determinantal varieties (notably their Theorem 12.3); we do not claim a new general singular-support criterion. Ohsugi and Hibi supply general edge-polytope adjacency and smoothness tools. The present claim is the explicit all-fixed-point **algebraic chart product decomposition** and the resulting pointwise Hilbert–Samuel series, multiplicities, tangent dimensions, and extremal profile for the staircase family. A more general pre-existing local product formula, if identified, would subsume the claim; the searched literature did not settle that possibility absolutely.

## Limitations
Only torus-fixed local rings are classified. The theorem does not assert that the global singular locus is zero-dimensional, nor does it classify its nonfixed strata or nonreduced scheme structure. No claim of independent audit or exhaustive bibliographic novelty certification is made. The checker provides finite corroboration and does not certify an infinite theorem on its own.
