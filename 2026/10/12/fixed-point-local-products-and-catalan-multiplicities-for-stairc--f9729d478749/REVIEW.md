## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies an explicit generating set for every affine fixed-point chart in independent Laurent variables, partitions it into two Ferrers semigroups and a free polynomial block, and proves every omitted generator factors into the primitive list. The semigroup supports are disjoint, so no hidden mixed relations remain. The Hilbert–Samuel series then follows from the graded tensor product and Corso–Nagel Example 5.11. Tangent and boundary counts follow from primitive monomial counts; maximality follows from Catalan supermultiplicativity. Independent exact finite tests check 285 affine charts and sampled Hilbert functions, without replacing the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations
Corso–Nagel establishes **global** Ferrers Hilbert and multiplicity formulas, while Gonciulea–Lakshmibai gives broad qualitative ladder singular-stratum results. No global formula is presented as new. The precise explicit **local chart product** and **pointwise Hilbert–Samuel series** for the complete staircase fixed-point array are not stated in the inspected relevant passages. General toric local semigroup decompositions are classical; a stronger published theorem specializing automatically to these charts remains a residual coverage risk. Search covered Ferrers and ladder terminology, dual transpose shapes, Catalan/Narayana formulas, and comparisons with the available prior mathematical records. The indexed-search absence alone was not used as proof of originality.

## Value — PASS
The complete local germ decomposition determines the local Hilbert–Samuel functions and multiplicities at every torus-fixed point of a natural infinite family. It identifies exactly four smooth fixed points and the two unique, exponentially growing Catalan-multiplicity corners. This is pointwise geometric information not supplied by the global degree of the variety, and provides an explicit base for studying the surrounding singular strata and birational modifications.

The theorem does not characterize non-fixed singular points or the global singular scheme. Literature coverage is substantial but not exhaustive; independent audit remains unperformed.
