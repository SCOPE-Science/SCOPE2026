# Forest factorization gives exact finite null moments for the checkerboard score
## Finding
Let \(H\ge2\) denote a number of labeled sites and \(s\ge2\) the number of species. Fix occupancies
\[
1\le r_i\le H\qquad(1\le i\le s).
\]
Independently for each species \(i\), choose a uniformly random subset \(A_i\) of exactly \(r_i\) sites. No site (column) sums are prescribed.

For a species pair, write
\[
X_{ij}=|A_i\cap A_j|,
\qquad
G_{ij}=(r_i-X_{ij})(r_j-X_{ij}).
\]
The classical Stone--Roberts community checkerboard score is the arithmetic mean of the pairwise checkerboard-unit counts:
\[
C=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{1\le i<j\le s}G_{ij},
\qquad M=\binom{s}{2}.
\]

**Forest independence theorem.** For every simple forest \(F\) whose vertices are species, the entire family
\[
\{X_{ij}:\{i,j\}\in E(F)\}
\]
is mutually independent. Each component has its ordinary fixed-margin hypergeometric overlap distribution:
\[
\Pr(X_{ij}=x)
=\frac{\binom{r_i}{x}\binom{H-r_i}{r_j-x}}{\binom H{r_j}}
\]
for \(\max(0,r_i+r_j-H)\le x\le\min(r_i,r_j)\). In particular, because any two distinct unordered species-pair edges form a forest, all distinct pairwise checkerboard scores \(G_{ij}\) are **pairwise independent**.

Consequently the expectation **and variance** of the community-wide checkerboard score are exact finite sums of pairwise moments, even for heterogeneous species occupancy frequencies:
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[C]=\frac1M\sum_{i<j}\mu(r_i,r_j)},
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(C)=\frac1{M^2}\sum_{i<j}v(r_i,r_j)}.
\]
Here, defining the falling factorial \((z)_{\underline{k}}=z(z-1)\cdots(z-k+1)\), with \((z)_{\underline0}=1\), set
\[
t_k(a,b)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{(a)_{\underline{k}}(b)_{\underline{k}}}{(H)_{\underline{k}}},&0\le k\le H,\\
0,&k>H,
\end{cases}
\qquad K=a+b-1.
\]
Then
\[
\mu(a,b)
=ab-Kt_1+t_2
=\frac{ab(H-a)(H-b)}{H(H-1)}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
v(a,b)={}&(ab)^2+K(K-2ab)t_1\\
&+(2ab+K^2-4K+2)t_2\\
&+(4-2K)t_3+t_4-\mu(a,b)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
All \(t_k\) on the right use the same pair \((a,b)\). The formulas use exact rational arithmetic and need no randomized-matrix sampling.

The forest statement is sharp as to dependence: cycles can create higher-order dependence even though every two distinct edges remain independent.

## Assumptions and scope
The species labels are distinct, the sites are labeled and equiprobable for **every** species, and each row of the randomized binary presence--absence matrix has its prescribed positive sum. Rows are sampled independently and uniformly from all rows with their respective sums. Zero-occupancy species are excluded, although any selected row may contain all \(H\) sites.

The randomization fixes row totals but neither the column totals nor the absence of empty columns. This is the fixed-row, equiprobable-column model often called SIM2 or fixed--equiprobable (F--E) in ecological null-model literature. An observed matrix can be analyzed conditional on its row sums only when such an independently randomized null is substantively appropriate.

The variance is over **independently randomized complete matrices**, not the sample variance of pairwise scores within a single fixed matrix. The theorem does not calculate tail probabilities, a parametric limiting law, or effects of biological interaction.

## Proof
**(1) Pairwise overlap distribution.** Condition on any one chosen species set, say \(A_i\). Because the second species set is a uniform \(r_j\)-subset, its intersection with the fixed \(r_i\)-subset has exactly the hypergeometric law displayed above. Crucially, that distribution depends only on the **number** \(r_i\), not on the identity of the sites in \(A_i\).

**(2) Factorization on a forest.** Let \(F\) have at least one edge and choose a vertex \(\ell\) of degree one, with neighbor \(j\). Condition on every species set except \(A_\ell\). The variable \(X_{\ell j}\) has the same fixed hypergeometric distribution regardless of these conditioned species sets, because \(|A_j|=r_j\). Hence \(X_{\ell j}\) is independent of the complete sigma field generated by all the other species sets. In particular, it is independent of all overlap variables indexed by the remaining forest after removing the leaf edge. Delete the leaf and use induction on the number of forest edges. Isolated vertices cause no difficulty. The product-law result follows for every forest, including disconnected forests and forests with unequal occupancies.

Any two distinct unordered edges form a forest, so their overlaps are independent. Applying \(x\mapsto(a-x)(b-x)\) preserves independence and gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G_{ij},G_{k\ell})=0
\qquad\text{whenever }\{i,j\}\ne\{k,\ell\}.
\]
The indices may have one species in common.

**(3) Exact factorial moments.** For a hypergeometric intersection \(X\) of two uniform independent subsets with sizes \(a,b\), counting ordered \(k\)-tuples of common sites gives
\[
\mathbb E[(X)_{\underline{k}}]=t_k(a,b).
\]
The checkerboard contribution satisfies the identity
\[
G=(a-X)(b-X)
=ab-(a+b-1)(X)_{\underline1}+(X)_{\underline2}.
\]
With \(K=a+b-1\), this immediately yields \(\mu=ab-Kt_1+t_2\). Alternatively, count ordered pairs of distinct sites at which the two species occur separately to get
\[
\mu=\frac{ab(H-a)(H-b)}{H(H-1)}.
\]

For its square use the exact falling-factorial products
\[
(X)_{\underline1}^2=(X)_{\underline2}+(X)_{\underline1},
\]
\[
(X)_{\underline1}(X)_{\underline2}=(X)_{\underline3}+2(X)_{\underline2},
\]
\[
(X)_{\underline2}^2=(X)_{\underline4}+4(X)_{\underline3}+2(X)_{\underline2}.
\]
Expanding the square and taking expectations gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[G^2]={}&(ab)^2+K(K-2ab)t_1\\
&+(2ab+K^2-4K+2)t_2\\
&+(4-2K)t_3+t_4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Subtracting \(\mu^2\) yields \(v\). When \(H<4\), unavailable higher factorial moments are zero, as defined above; thus the same formulas hold down to \(H=2\).

**(4) Whole-matrix moments.** Linearity gives the displayed formula for \(\mathbb E[C]\). Expand \(\operatorname{Var}(\sum_{i<j}G_{ij})\). All cross terms vanish by step (2), leaving precisely the displayed sum of pairwise variances divided by \(M^2\). No large-census or many-species approximation is involved.

**(5) Necessity of the forest condition for full joint independence.** Take \(H=2\) and three species with \(r_1=r_2=r_3=1\). Each species chooses one of two sites independently with probability \(1/2\). Each overlap \(X_{ij}\) is a Bernoulli variable with probability \(1/2\) of being one. All three overlaps equal one precisely when all three species choose the same site, an event of probability
\[
\frac{2}{2^3}=\frac14
\ne\frac18=\left(\frac12\right)^3.
\]
Thus pairwise independence is not mistaken for unrestricted mutual independence.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_checkerboard_fe_moments.py` uses Python's standard library and exact fractions. It compares the displayed formulas with the full hypergeometric distribution for every \(2\le H\le12\) and every positive pair of row sizes, covering **649 pair instances**.

It also **enumerates all complete binary matrices** allowed by six heterogeneous or homogeneous row-degree models, totaling **30,665 matrices**. For each case it compares directly enumerated community-mean and community-variance values with the formulas, independently checks joint laws for every pair of overlap edges (including shared-species edges), tests joint independence of entire path-forest overlap families, and checks the triangle obstruction. Run `python3 verify_checkerboard_fe_moments.py`; the final line is `VERIFY_OK`.

For \(H=5\) and three species with occupancies \((2,3,4)\), the null mean and variance are
\[
\mathbb E[C]=\frac75,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(C)=\frac{49}{75}.
\]
This is an exact finite example, not an estimate. The all-parameter theorem follows from the preceding proof, not from the finite computational enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
The original checkerboard index of Stone and Roberts (1990) is used here without changing its normalization. Gotelli (2000) defined and compared nine matrix-randomization schemes including row-fixed, site-equiprobable SIM2, and assessed the C-score by randomized matrices; it provides the direct null-model motivation. Veech (2013) derives the exact single-pair overlap law as a non-simulation method for ecological co-occurrence, and Arita (2016) identifies that pairwise hypergeometric method with Fisher's exact test and F--E matrix randomization. These facts supply the correct pairwise marginal distributions and modeling context.

The new statement concerns the **joint structure across different species pairs**: all forest-indexed overlap counts factorize and all pairwise checkerboard contributions have zero covariance, leading to a closed-form **variance across complete randomized matrices**, not merely a per-pair overlap distribution. The full relevant sections of Gotelli (2000), Veech (2013), and Arita (2016) were compared for that exact implication; none of the inspected arguments or results states the forest product law or the resulting arbitrary-occupancy community C-score variance formula. Searches for C-score analytic variance, fixed--equiprobable covariance, and forest overlap independence likewise did not locate an exact covering ecological result. A general probability reference may contain an equivalent forest-independence observation under another name; see the limitation below.

## Limitations
This is a theorem conditional on independent row randomization with uniform site selection. It is not valid unchanged if column sums are also fixed, if sites have heterogeneous suitability, if species are ecologically dependent, or if one conditions on the absence of empty sites.

The formulas are exact for the variance across randomized matrices, not for the variance among pairwise scores in one observed matrix. Normal or Gaussian calibration and exact tail probabilities do not follow: pair contributions need not be mutually independent on cycles, even when their covariances are zero. The formula can yield zero variance (for example when all rows occupy all sites); dividing by its square root then produces an undefined standardized effect size.

The foundational pairwise hypergeometric law is known. The claimed contribution is its **forest-level factorization and aggregate C-score variance**, not a new pairwise Fisher test. An equivalent statement might appear implicitly in broader exchangeability, random-set U-statistic, or degenerate graph-kernel literature; no checked source was found to imply the specific result.
