# Free-orbit criterion and subgroup-Möbius census for finite Cayley S5 frames

## Finding
For any nontrivial finite group \(G\) generated by distinct nonidentity involutions \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\), and any number \(c\ge1\) of disjoint regular components, an ordered set of \(r\ge0\) propositions generates the entire finite Boolean modal algebra precisely when its component colorings occupy different **free** right-translation orbits of \(G\). Let \(q=2^r\). Writing \(\mu_G\) for the subgroup-lattice Möbius function and \(A_G(q)\) for the number of \(q\)-colorings of \(G\) with trivial right stabilizer, this yields
\[
 A_G(q)=\sum_{H\le G}\mu_G(1,H)q^{|G|/|H|},
 \qquad
 N_{G,c,r}=\prod_{j=0}^{c-1}\bigl(A_G(2^r)-j|G|\bigr).
\]
Here \(N_{G,c,r}\) counts ordered \(r\)-tuples of subsets of the whole frame that generate its full powerset algebra. The exact rank is \(\min\{r\ge0:A_G(2^r)\ge c|G|\}\).

More generally, for any such coloring, the atoms of its generated algebra correspond to the distinct rooted-color functions \(T_{a,g}:G\to[q]\), \(T_{a,g}(p)=f_a(pg)\). Thus, if \(\mathscr O\) is the set of different right-translation orbits among the component colorings, the number of atoms is \(\sum_{\mathcal O\in\mathscr O}|\mathcal O|\), and the algebra has \(2^{\sum_{\mathcal O\in\mathscr O}|\mathcal O|}\) elements.

For the dihedral group \(D_m\) of order \(2m\), generated by its two distinct reflections, \(m\ge2\), put \(P_q(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)q^d\) (primitive-word polynomial). Then
\[
 A_{D_m}(q)=P_{q^2}(m)-mP_q(m).
\]
For example, \(A_{D_2}(2)=8\), so two regular components of \(D_2\) admit exactly \(8(8-4)=32\) generating single-proposition colorings; \(A_{D_3}(2)=42\), giving seven free binary-color orbits on one six-state component.

## Assumptions and scope
Take \(G\) a **nontrivial finite group** with a specified list of distinct nonidentity involutions \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) that generate \(G\). The state space is \(W=[c]\times G\), with no edges between different components, and named agent equivalences
\[
 (a,g)R_i(a,h)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in\{g,s_i g\}.
\]
Each equivalence class therefore has exactly two elements. The logic is ordinary finitary multimodal propositional logic with Boolean connectives and one box or diamond per named agent; group words and group elements are not added to the formula language as operators. Every propositional valuation is a subset of \(W\). An ordered \(r\)-tuple gives, by its membership bits, a unique coloring \(f_a:G\to[q]\) where \(q=2^r\). The components are labeled, the propositions are ordered, and the value of \(r\) may be zero.

The functions \(f_a\) are acted on by right translations: \(f_a^h(g)=f_a(gh)\). Since the state involutions act by *left* multiplication, every right translation preserves each named S5 relation. The left/right convention is equivalent under group inversion to the standard right-Cayley frame convention in the cited literature.

For the subgroup lattice, define \(\mu_G(1,1)=1\) and recursively \(\sum_{K\le H}\mu_G(1,K)=0\) for each nontrivial subgroup \(H\). The sum for \(A_G\) is over all subgroups, not merely normal subgroups. The dihedral specialization uses \(D_m=\langle\rho,\tau\mid\rho^m=\tau^2=1,\tau\rho\tau=\rho^{-1}\rangle\), \(m\ge2\), with named agents \(\tau\) and \(\rho\tau\).

## Proof
**1. Modal-equivalence propagation.** For a fixed valuation, let \(\equiv\) denote agreement on all ordinary modal formulas. Because the frame is finite, \(\equiv\) is the stable partition obtained by iterating propositional colors and the sets of successor-block types. The \(i\)-th equivalence block of state \(x\) is exactly \(\{x,s_i x\}\). If \(x\equiv y\), the two blocks have identical sets of modal-equivalence classes. Since both include the common class \([x]=[y]\), equality
\[
 \{[x],[s_i x]\}=\{[y],[s_i y]\}
\]
forces \([s_i x]=[s_i y]\), including when one of these classes equals \([x]\). Repeating this propagation along the specified involutions yields that \(x\equiv y\) implies \(p x\) and \(p y\) have the same propositional color for every word \(p\) in the generators and hence for every \(p\in G\). Equivalently, for \(x=(a,g)\) and \(y=(b,h)\), we have \(f_a(pg)=f_b(ph)\) for all \(p\in G\).

Conversely, equality of these rooted-color functions is a color-preserving bisimulation: it is preserved under the identity and under each move \(s_i\), because \(T_{a,s_i g}(p)=T_{a,g}(ps_i)\). On the finite frame bisimilar states satisfy the same modal formulas. This proves the exact modal-equivalence classification. Every stable class is definable by some finitary modal formula (construct distinguishing formulas through finite partition refinement); therefore the atoms of the generated Boolean modal algebra are exactly these equivalence classes, not merely a set of abstract bisimulation states.

**2. Classification by free orbits.** For \(x=(a,g)\), the rooted color function \(T_{a,g}\) is the right translate \(f_a^g\). Thus the distinct modal types inside component \(a\) form exactly the right-translation orbit of \(f_a\). The orbit-stabilizer identity gives orbit size \(\lvert G\rvert/\lvert\operatorname{Stab}(f_a)\rvert\). Modal types from different components coincide exactly when their coloring orbits coincide. Since the powerset Boolean modal algebra has singleton atoms, full generation is therefore equivalent to all these orbits being free and mutually different. This also proves the formula for the number of atoms and generated-algebra cardinality. The same criterion is equivalent to rigidity of the colored frame: any color-preserving automorphism must preserve each deterministic left-involution map (uniquely determined by its named two-point equivalence blocks), so on connected components it is necessarily a right translation, optionally followed by a permutation of isomorphic colored components.

**3. Subgroup Möbius inversion.** For every subgroup \(H\le G\), the set of \(q\)-colorings fixed by all right translations in \(H\) has size \(q^{[G:H]}\), because its values are constant on the right \(H\)-orbits of \(G\). If \(E(K)\) denotes the number of colorings with stabilizer exactly \(K\), these fixed-point counts satisfy \(q^{[G:H]}=\sum_{K\ge H}E(K)\). Möbius inversion at the trivial subgroup yields \(E(1)=A_G(q)=\sum_{H\le G}\mu_G(1,H)q^{[G:H]}\). Every free orbit contains \(|G|\) colorings, hence there are \(A_G(q)/|G|\) available free orbits. Choose \(c\) different orbits in order and independently choose a representative coloring within each. The resulting number is
\[
 \left(\frac{A_G(q)}{|G|}\right)_{\!\underline c}|G|^c
 =\prod_{j=0}^{c-1}(A_G(q)-j|G|).
\]
If fewer than \(c\) free orbits exist, the falling factorial is zero, not negative; the product formula has a zero factor. Existence of any generating labeling is equivalent to \(A_G(q)\ge c|G|\), which gives the exact generator rank. The existence of some finite \(r\) is clear: color every state with a distinct element of a sufficiently large alphabet.

**4. Dihedral reduction.** For \(D_m=\langle\rho,\tau\rangle\), index the \(2m\) states as \(\rho^j\) and \(\rho^{-j}\tau\), \(j\bmod m\). Right multiplication by \(\rho\) simultaneously shifts both sequences one step, so a coloring is a cyclic word of length \(m\) over an alphabet of size \(q^2\). Requiring no nonidentity rotation to stabilize it is exactly primitiveness of this paired word. Elementary necklace-period Möbius inversion counts such words as \(P_{q^2}(m)\).

There are \(m\) reflections. If a rotation-free coloring is fixed by two different reflections, their product is a nonidentity rotation, contradiction; hence their fixed primitive sets are disjoint. For a given reflection \(t\), apply inclusion-exclusion across rotational subgroups to its fixed colorings. A subgroup comprising a rotation subgroup of index \(d\) together with \(t\) has index \(d\) in \(D_m\), so exactly \(q^d\) colorings are fixed by it. The rotation-primitiveness condition therefore leaves \(P_q(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)q^d\) \(t\)-invariant colorings. Subtracting the disjoint sets for all \(m\) reflections gives \(A_{D_m}(q)=P_{q^2}(m)-mP_q(m)\). This step uses the standard primitive-word polynomial; only its synthesis with the modal generation count is part of the present claim.

## Relationship to prior work
The frame objects are precisely a degree-two-per-agent subclass of the **Cayley S5 structures** developed by Canavoi and Otto (2017), whose paper explicitly constructs epistemic equivalence relations from involution-generated Cayley groups and studies bisimulation coverings and logical expressive completeness. Our statement does **not** claim these frames or general modal bisimulation principles as novel. Canavoi's 2018 dissertation develops the same framework and its expressive-completeness consequences. The finite-modal formula construction is an instance of standard partition refinement, closely related to the general distinguishing-formula algorithms of Wißmann, Milius and Schröder (2021/2022).

Free orbits, distinguishing colorings of group actions, primitive necklace polynomials, and subgroup-lattice Möbius inversion are classical tools. The proposed added result is their precise union into a full if-and-only-if criterion and exact ordered generating-tuple count **for these named-agent modal powerset algebras**, valid uniformly for arbitrary finite involution-generated groups and multiple components, together with the explicit dihedral specialization. Related distinguishing-group-action work of Ferrara and collaborators and symmetric-bracelet enumerations do not state the examined modal-algebra equivalence or its full finite-group count. The search does not exclude an implicit older antecedent.

## Limitations
Only ordinary Boolean multimodal formulas with the specified named pair-equivalence modalities are classified. No result about multi-agent common-knowledge modalities, finite nonregular frames, arbitrary group actions, or independent correctness/originality certification is asserted. All computations test finite parameter samples; neither the searches nor the computations can prove historical priority. The Canavoi dissertation is much broader in model-theoretic scope, and an unlocated formulation of the same enumeration within universal-algebra or distinguishing-coloring literature remains a possible originality risk.
