## Correctness — PASS
The source defines exactly the displayed homogeneous curve. The derivative equations at rational points force the exhaustive parametrization \(P_t\). In affine local coordinates, Frobenius expansions cancel the first- and degree-\(q\) terms at each singular point. The first surviving degree is \(\min(p^{v_p(r)},q)+1\). The calculation separately verifies all three cones and the exact collision equation. Squarefree cones imply ordinary multiple points, their delta invariants and rational normalization-branch counts. The repeated critical cones are asserted only as tangent-cone multiplicities; no unjustified analytic-branch classification is inferred from them. The fourteen exact coefficient checks support, but do not replace, the uniform algebraic proof.

## Originality — PASS

It does not supply the valuation trichotomy, degree-\(q+1\) tangent forms, sharp ordinary/nonordinary boundary, or rational tangent-splitting counts. Homma–Kim (2013) addresses minimum-degree smooth curves instead. Relevant mathematical-record, published-result, exact-formula and alias searches yielded no equivalent family-wide claim. This is an evidence-limited priority assessment, not a claim of uniqueness or independence from unindexed work.

## Value — PASS
The source explicitly distinguishes the difficult regime where the characteristic divides the exponent. This theorem resolves a natural local invariant for every rational singularity throughout that regime, exhibiting multiplicities that jump with the Frobenius valuation and a sharp collision on the critical valuation wall. It also determines a large rational-tangent splitting transition: at \(s>q\), all \(q+1\) branches split rationally, whereas at an ordinary critical point at most two do. This is a structural classification with consequences for local delta and normalization arithmetic, not a numerical example or a restatement of the singular-point criterion.
