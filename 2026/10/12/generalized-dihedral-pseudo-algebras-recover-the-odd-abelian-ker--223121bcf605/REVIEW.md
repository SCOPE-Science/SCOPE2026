# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. Odd order makes inversion free on the nonprincipal dual characters of \(A\). Clifford theory therefore gives one degree-\(2\) irreducible for every pair \(\{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\}\). Its restriction to \(A\) is \(\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\), it vanishes outside \(A\), and its kernel is exactly \(\ker\lambda\). Hence its codegree is the exact order of \(\lambda\), giving multiplicity \(N_A(d)/2\). The two linear codegrees are \(1\) and \(2\). The recovered \(p\)-power exact-order counts determine \(|A_p[p^k]|\), whose logarithmic successive differences recover the invariant-factor partition. The packaged checker verifies both formulas and reconstruction on eight examples.

## Originality

PASS. The 2022 primary paper introduces the pseudo-algebra and proves the analogous reconstruction only for abelian groups; its searchable full text contains no generalized-dihedral discussion. The 2024 follow-up gives nonrigidity examples and positive \(p\)-group results, again without this family. Searches under pseudo-algebra, generalized-dihedral, codegree-table, inversion-orbit, and element-order-spectrum formulations found no covering theorem. An older equivalent character-table computation without pseudo-algebra language remains the principal bibliographic risk.

## Value

PASS. The pseudo-algebra fails to determine order globally, so identifying a canonical nonabelian family on which it recovers the entire group is meaningful. The theorem also explains exactly why: nonlinear codegree multiplicities are half of the kernel's exact element-order counts. The explicit Sylow reconstruction makes the result directly reusable.
