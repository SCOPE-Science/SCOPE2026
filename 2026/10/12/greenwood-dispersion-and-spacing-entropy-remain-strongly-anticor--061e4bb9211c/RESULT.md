# Greenwood dispersion and spacing entropy remain strongly anticorrelated

## Finding

Let
\[
D=(D_1,\ldots,D_m)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad m\ge2.
\]
Equivalently, \(D\) is the vector of the \(m\) linear spacings generated by \(m-1\) independent uniform points on \((0,1)\), including the two boundary spacings.

Define Greenwood's sum-of-squared-spacings statistic
\[
G_m=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i^2
\]
and the Shannon entropy of the spacing vector
\[
H_m^{\mathrm{sp}}
=
-\sum_{i=1}^mD_i\log D_i.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})
=
-\frac{m-1}{(m+1)^2(m+2)}
<0.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The marginal variances are
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(G_m)
=
\frac{4(m-1)}
{(m+1)^2(m+2)(m+3)}
}
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})
=
H_{m+1}^{(2)}
-\frac{\pi^2}{6}
+\frac{\pi^2/3-2}{m+1}
-\frac1{(m+1)^2},
}
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
H_k^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^k\frac1{j^2}.
\]

Hence the exact correlation is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})
=
-\frac{\sqrt{(m-1)(m+3)}}
{2(m+1)\sqrt{(m+2)\operatorname{Var}(H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Although the covariance in (1) is of order \(m^{-2}\), the two statistics remain strongly coupled after standardization:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})
\longrightarrow
-\frac1{2\sqrt{\pi^2/3-3}}
=
-0.9286878573\ldots.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus Greenwood concentration and Shannon spacing evenness become almost opposite one-dimensional summaries of the random spacing profile without becoming perfectly collinear.

## Assumptions and scope

The logarithm is natural. Since Dirichlet coordinates are strictly positive almost surely, the entropy is well defined; the usual continuous extension \(0\log0=0\) may be adopted on the boundary.

The result concerns the uniform Dirichlet spacing vector. It applies directly to linear spacings from iid uniform points and, by the standard Dirichlet representation, to circular uniform spacings after fixing an origin.

Equation (5) is a correlation limit, not an assertion that one statistic is an affine function of the other.

## Proof

For nonnegative exponents \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), the Dirichlet moment identity is
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^mD_i^{a_i}
=
\frac{\Gamma(m)\prod_{i=1}^m\Gamma(1+a_i)}
{\Gamma\!\left(m+\sum_{i=1}^ma_i\right)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Differentiating (6) with respect to an exponent inserts a logarithm.

First,
\[
\mathbb EG_m=\frac2{m+1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Also
\[
\mathbb EH_m^{\mathrm{sp}}
=
\psi(m+1)-\psi(2)
=
H_m-1,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(\psi\) is the digamma function and
\[
H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1j.
\]

For the mixed moment, separate equal and unequal indices:
\[
\mathbb E[G_mH_m^{\mathrm{sp}}]
=
-m\,\mathbb E[D_1^3\log D_1]
-m(m-1)\mathbb E[D_1^2D_2\log D_2].
\tag{9}
\]
Differentiating (6) gives
\[
\mathbb E[D_1^3\log D_1]
=
\frac{6}{m(m+1)(m+2)}
\left(\psi(4)-\psi(m+3)\right)
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E[D_1^2D_2\log D_2]
=
\frac{2}{m(m+1)(m+2)}
\left(\psi(2)-\psi(m+3)\right).
\tag{11}
\]
Substitution into (9) yields
\[
\mathbb E[G_mH_m^{\mathrm{sp}}]
=
\frac{
2(m+2)H_{m+2}-(2m+9)
}
{(m+1)(m+2)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Subtracting the product of (7) and (8), and using
\[
H_{m+2}-H_m=\frac1{m+1}+\frac1{m+2},
\]
gives (1).

For Greenwood's variance,
\[
\mathbb EG_m^2
=
m\,\mathbb ED_1^4
+
m(m-1)\mathbb E[D_1^2D_2^2]
=
\frac{4(m+5)}{(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)}.
\tag{13}
\]
Subtracting the square of (7) gives (2).

For the entropy variance, differentiate (6) twice. The diagonal term is
\[
\mathbb E[D_1^2(\log D_1)^2]
=
\frac{2}{m(m+1)}
\left[
(\psi(3)-\psi(m+2))^2
+\psi_1(3)-\psi_1(m+2)
\right],
\tag{14}
\]
and for two distinct coordinates
\[
\mathbb E[D_1D_2\log D_1\log D_2]
=
\frac{1}{m(m+1)}
\left[
(\psi(2)-\psi(m+2))^2
-\psi_1(m+2)
\right],
\tag{15}
\]
where \(\psi_1\) is the trigamma function.

Expanding
\[
\mathbb E(H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})^2
=
m\,\mathbb E[D_1^2(\log D_1)^2]
+
m(m-1)\mathbb E[D_1D_2\log D_1\log D_2]
\]
and using
\[
\psi_1(k)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}-H_{k-1}^{(2)}
\]
gives (3).

Finally,
\[
H_{m+1}^{(2)}
=
\frac{\pi^2}{6}
-\frac1{m+1}
+\frac1{2(m+1)^2}
+O(m^{-3}),
\tag{16}
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{Var}(H_m^{\mathrm{sp}})
=
\frac{\pi^2/3-3}{m}
+O(m^{-2}).
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (1), (2), and (17) imply (5).

## Verification

The accompanying checker reconstructs every displayed moment from the Dirichlet gamma-moment formula, using high-precision digamma and trigamma evaluations. It compares the reconstructed mixed moment, covariance, both marginal variances, and exact correlation with the closed forms for a grid of dimensions.

It separately evaluates the correlation at increasing dimensions and verifies convergence toward the constant in (5).

The checker is supplementary. The all-\(m\) proof is the differentiated Dirichlet moment identity above.

## Relationship to prior work

Royen gives exact distributional representations for the square sum of Dirichlet coordinates and identifies the uniform-spacing specialization as Greenwood's statistic. The full public manuscript studies Greenwood's marginal distribution and exact quantiles. Searches within that manuscript for entropy, Shannon entropy, and covariance found no corresponding mixed law.

Classical sum-function-of-spacings theory, including Holst and later L'Ecuyer, studies broad classes of spacing statistics and their asymptotic normality. L'Ecuyer's full article places the sum of logarithms and the sum of squares of spacings side by side as goodness-of-fit statistics. The Shannon spacing entropy here is the different weighted-log functional
\[
-\sum_iD_i\log D_i,
\]
not the unweighted sum of logarithms.

A current related calculation for the same Dirichlet spacing model links Greenwood's statistic to the maximum spacing and obtains a positive covariance whose correlation tends to zero. That result does not imply (1)--(5): the entropy statistic is a global evenness functional, and its correlation with Greenwood's statistic instead converges to a nonzero negative constant.

Targeted searches for Greenwood--entropy covariance, sum-of-squared-spacings versus spacing entropy, and Dirichlet entropy versus square-sum covariance did not locate (1) or (5).

## Limitations

The exact formulas use the uniform Dirichlet parameter vector. They are not asserted for nonuniform Dirichlet parameters or for spacings from a nonuniform parent distribution before probability-integral transformation.

The result concerns Shannon entropy of the spacing vector, not the classical unweighted log-spacing statistic.

General Dirichlet mixed-moment formulas make the calculation accessible once this pair of statistics is chosen, so older compositional-data or spacing literature may contain an equivalent covariance under different terminology. That remains the principal originality risk.
