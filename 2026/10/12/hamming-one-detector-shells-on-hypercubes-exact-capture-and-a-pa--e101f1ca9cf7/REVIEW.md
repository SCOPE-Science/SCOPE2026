# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact claim follows from a fully specified finite-dimensional dissipative model. For every strictly positive loss strength, the captured subspace equals the cyclic \(H\)-span of detector vectors because its complement is loss-free and every eigenvalue on its reducing complement has strictly negative imaginary part. Independent Walsh-mode rank calculations give \(n^2-n+2-\mathbf1_{\{2\mid n\}}\). Layerwise affine-function geometry reconstructs the entire projector; the even-dimensional zero-energy radial Krawtchouk mode supplies the only missing direction. Both added-detector statements follow from the same projector. The packaged finite spectral checks using an alternative numerical construction reproduce all \(252\) sitewise cases and \(16\) rank cases on small cubes. They corroborate, but do not replace, the proof. Density matrices on the dark subspace may oscillate, so only survival norms have an asserted limit.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The standard spectral-cyclic subspace method is prior art, especially Razzoli–Paris–Bordone (2021). Krovi–Brun (2006) already explain degeneracy-induced trapping. Yamasaki–Kobayashi–Imai (2002) conjecture an odd-dimension-equivalent numerical formula for a single absorber on a different **discrete-time coined** hypercube walk; this coincidence is disclosed explicitly. Their operator, detector arrangement, and proof status do not imply the all-neighbor-shell continuous-time rank, even-dimensional correction, and detector redundancy/repair classification. Full-text inspections of the relevant sections of these and two additional papers found no statement implying the whole theorem. An undiscovered equivalent specialization is still possible; independent publication-priority validation has not taken place.

## Value — PASS
The immediate-neighbor shell is a canonical geometric vertex separator and a physically interpretable detector deployment. The result computes, in closed form for every dimension and every starting vertex, the exact fate of coherent transport under arbitrarily unequal positive loss rates. It identifies an unexpected parity-protected radial mode and the precise futility of adding the entire antipodal shell, alongside a one-site parity repair. These are structural infinite-family consequences, not a numerical census or a direct restatement of the general dark-subspace principle.

## Limitations and status
Uniform hypercube hopping, ideal positive localized losses, no interactions or disorder. No time-to-capture bound, robustness theorem, or arbitrary-detector optimization is claimed. A broader unpublished or unlocated proof is a residual originality risk.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
