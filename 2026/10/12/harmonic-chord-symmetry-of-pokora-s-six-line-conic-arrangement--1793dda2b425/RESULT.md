# Harmonic chord symmetry of Pokora’s six-line conic arrangement
## Finding
Let \(A\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\) be the reduced curve
\[
Q_6=xy(x^2+y^2-z^2)(y+x-z)(y-x-z)(y+x+z)(y-x+z)=0.
\]
Its full projective stabilizer is the dihedral group of order \(8\). It is generated by
\[
R[x:y:z]=[-y:x:z],\qquad S[x:y:z]=[-x:y:z],
\]
with \(R^4=S^2=1\) and \(SRS=R^{-1}\).

More intrinsically, the unique conic component \(C:x^2+y^2-z^2=0\) carries the harmonic four-set \(T=\{0,\infty,1,-1\}\) under
\[
\nu(t)=[2t:1-t^2:1+t^2].
\]
The six line components of \(A\) are exactly the six chords joining the six unordered pairs of points of \(T\). Thus the four ordinary quadruple points are the marked points \(\nu(T)\), and the three nodes are the three intersections of opposite chords.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\), and projective automorphisms mean elements of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb C)\) preserving the reduced curve \(A\) setwise. The equation is the arrangement denoted \(\mathcal{CL}_6\) in the cited source. No claim is made about abstract automorphisms of a resolution or of the complement.

## Proof
A projective automorphism preserves degrees of irreducible components. Since \(C\) is the unique component of degree \(2\), every automorphism of \(A\) preserves \(C\). Restriction to \(C\) is injective: a projective transformation fixing the nondegenerate conic pointwise is the identity. Conversely, every automorphism of \(C\cong\mathbb P^1\) extends uniquely to a projective automorphism of the conic plane through the quadratic Veronese representation.

Using \(\nu(t)=[2t:1-t^2:1+t^2]\), restriction of the six line equations gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_{x}=\{0,\infty\},\\
y&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_{y}=\{1,-1\},\\
y+x-z&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_1=\{0,1\},\\
y-x-z&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_2=\{0,-1\},\\
y+x+z&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_3=\{-1,\infty\},\\
y-x+z&=0 &&\Longleftrightarrow T_4=\{1,\infty\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence the line set is the complete set of chords of \(T=\{0,\infty,1,-1\}\). Therefore a projective automorphism preserves \(A\) exactly when its restriction to \(C\) preserves \(T\).

The cross-ratio \(\operatorname{cr}(0,\infty;1,-1)=-1\), so \(T\) is harmonic. Its Möbius stabilizer has exactly eight elements. It is generated by
\[
r(t)=\frac{t-1}{t+1},\qquad s(t)=-t,
\]
where \(r\) has order \(4\), \(s\) has order \(2\), and \(srs=r^{-1}\). Under the quadratic conic parametrization these become the displayed plane maps \(R\) and \(S\). Thus the full stabilizer is dihedral of order \(8\).

At each point of \(\nu(T)\), exactly three chord lines meet and the conic contributes a fourth smooth branch with a distinct tangent, giving four ordinary quadruple points. Among the fifteen pairs of chord lines, twelve pairs meet at those four vertices and the three disjoint-edge pairs meet at three distinct points away from \(C\), giving exactly three nodes.

## Verification
The accompanying exact-arithmetic verifier checks that the marked cross-ratio is \(-1\), enumerates all \(24\) permutations of the four marked points and finds exactly \(8\) realizable Möbius symmetries, verifies the dihedral relations for \(r\) and \(s\), checks that the six printed line factors restrict to all six unordered pairs of the marked set, and reconstructs the four triple-chord vertices plus the three opposite-chord intersections. It also verifies that \(R\) and \(S\) permute the six line factors and preserve the conic.

## Relationship to prior work
Pokora’s source gives the exact equation, weak combinatorics \( (n_2,n_3,n_4)=(3,0,4)\), total Tjurina number \(39\), and \(\operatorname{mdr}(Q_6)=2\), hence freeness. The later paper of Janasz and Pokora reuses the same arrangement as an example in the study of \(\mathscr{M}\)-arrangements. Neither inspected source states its full projective stabilizer or identifies the six lines as the complete chord arrangement of a harmonic four-set. The 2024 version of the lead paper includes a figure specifically meant to convey symmetry, but contains no automorphism statement.

## Limitations
The result concerns the embedded projective stabilizer of this marked plane arrangement. An older 1992 thesis of Mara Neusel is cited by the lead source as containing some related conic–line examples, but the exact occurrence of this equation and any automorphism calculation could not be verified from accessible full text; this remains the principal literature risk. The proof itself does not depend on that provenance question.
