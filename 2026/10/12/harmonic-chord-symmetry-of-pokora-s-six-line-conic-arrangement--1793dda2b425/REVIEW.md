# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the projective stabilizer to the Möbius stabilizer of the marked conic. The six line factors are checked directly against the quadratic parametrization, the marked cross-ratio is \(-1\), and an exhaustive exact-arithmetic permutation test gives eight stabilizers with dihedral relations. The incidence calculation also recovers the source’s four ordinary quadruple points and three nodes.

## Originality
PASS. The defining preprint gives the equation and freeness data but not the projective automorphism group. Its later revision only remarks visually on symmetry, and a later paper reusing the same equation does not state an automorphism result. Exact published-finding and own-ledger searches under stabilizer, automorphism, harmonic, complete-chord, and equation aliases found no covering statement. The main residual literature risk is the inaccessible 1992 Neusel thesis cited by the source as containing some related examples.

## Value
PASS. The result identifies the arrangement as the complete chord configuration of a harmonic divisor on a conic. This explains why the example has an order-eight symmetry jump and why its singularity pattern is exactly four quadruple points plus three nodes; the exact embedded stabilizer is a natural invariant of the classification example.

## Closest literature and limitations
Pokora’s arXiv:2312.13052v1 is the closest source: it defines \(Q_6\), proves freeness, and records \( (n_2,n_3,n_4)=(3,0,4)\). Janasz–Pokora arXiv:2512.24707v1 later reuses the same equation. Neither inspected text gives the order-eight projective stabilizer. The theorem is limited to the embedded plane arrangement, and historical overlap with Neusel’s 1992 thesis remains unresolved because the thesis full text was not available.
