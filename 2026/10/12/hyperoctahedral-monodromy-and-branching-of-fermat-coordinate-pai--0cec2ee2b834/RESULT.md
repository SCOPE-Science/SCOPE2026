# Hyperoctahedral monodromy and branching of Fermat coordinate-pair projections

## Finding
For integers \(d\ge4\) and \(n\ge2\), consider the complex Fermat hypersurface
\[
X_{d,n}=\left\{[x_0:\cdots:x_{n+1}]\in\mathbf P^{n+1}:\sum_{h=0}^{n+1}x_h^d=0\right\}.
\]
For two distinct indices \(i,j\) and \(\zeta^d=1\), let \(P=[e_i-\zeta e_j]\). Put \(m=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\). The rational projection \(\pi_P:X_{d,n}\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^n\) has degree \(2m\), and the Galois group of its function-field normal closure is
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{\mathbb C(X_{d,n})}/\mathbb C(\mathbf P^n))
\cong (\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^m\rtimes S_m=C_2\wr S_m,
\qquad |\operatorname{Gal}|=2^m m!.
\]
Its deck-transformation group is exactly \(C_2\), generated by exchanging the two selected coordinates after the \(\zeta\)-rescaling. In the one-parameter polynomial model below, the finite geometric branch-cycle types consist of one transposition and \(m-1\) products of two disjoint transpositions; infinity is a full \(2m\)-cycle. Consequently the degree-four projections from all 24 non-Galois quasi-Galois points of the Fermat quartic surface identified by Miura--Taki have normal-closure group \(C_2\wr S_2\cong D_4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\) and \(n\ge2\); the assertion is not made for positive-characteristic Fermat varieties or arbitrary projection centers. For even \(d\), \(P\notin X_{d,n}\), whereas for odd \(d\), \(P\in X_{d,n}\); in the latter case the generic projection degree is \(d-1\), not \(d\). Only the explicit coordinate-pair centers are covered. Monodromy means the Galois group of the normal closure of the induced finite extension of function fields, not the deck group.

## Proof
**1. Explicit projection polynomial.** Since \(\zeta^d=1\), rescale \(x_j=\zeta y_j\), preserving the Fermat equation, and set \(s=(x_i+y_j)/2\), \(t=(x_i-y_j)/2\). The point \(P\) becomes \(s=0\), \(t\ne0\), with every remaining coordinate zero. Projection from \(P\) is given by \([s:(x_h)_{h\ne i,j}]\). Over the chart \(s\ne0\), set \(T=t/s\), \(z_h=x_h/s\), and \(v=-\sum_{h\ne i,j}z_h^d\). The defining equation is
\[
p_d(T^2)-v=0,\qquad
p_d(U)=2\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom d{2r}U^r.
\]
The leading coefficient of \(p_d\) is nonzero, so the equation has degree \(2m\) in \(T\); the extension has this degree because \(p_d(T^2)-v\) has transitive geometric monodromy below. The sign change \(T\mapsto-T\) preserves the projection.

**2. The critical values of \(p_d\).** The identity
\[
p_d(z^2)=(1+z)^d+(1-z)^d,
\quad 2z\,p_d'(z^2)=d\big((1+z)^{d-1}-(1-z)^{d-1}\big)
\]
shows that every critical point of \(p_d\) arises from a nontrivial \((d-1)\)-st root of unity \(r=(1+z)/(1-z)\), excluding \(r=-1\) when it occurs. The inversion pair \(\{r,r^{-1}\}\) corresponds to the same \(U=z^2\), and different inversion pairs give different \(U\). There are exactly \(m-1\) such pairs; hence all critical points of the degree-\(m\) polynomial \(p_d\) are simple. At such a point,
\[
p_d(U_r)=\frac{2^d}{(1+r)^{d-1}}.
\]
These critical values are pairwise distinct: if two are equal, then their absolute values are equal, so \(|1+r|^2=2+r+r^{-1}\) agrees for the two roots of unity, which forces them to be equal or inverse. Also, \(|1+r|<2\) for every permitted \(r\), so every critical value has absolute value greater than \(2\). Thus \(p_d(0)=2\) is a regular value, and \(p_d\) is Morse with \(m-1\) distinct finite simple branch points.

**3. The exact monodromy of the polynomial cover.** The degree-\(m\) polynomial cover \(U\mapsto p_d(U)\) is connected. Its finite local monodromies are single transpositions, one at each critical value, and generate its entire geometric monodromy. A transitive permutation group generated by transpositions is \(S_m\); therefore \(\operatorname{Gal}(p_d(U)-v/\mathbb C(v))=S_m\). For the degree-\(2m\) cover \(T\mapsto p_d(T^2)\), the \(2m\) sheets are organized into \(m\) pairs \(\{+\sqrt{U_h},-\sqrt{U_h}\}\). Its monodromy \(G\) lies in \(C_2\wr S_m\) and maps surjectively onto \(S_m\). Since \(v=2\) is regular for \(p_d\) and exactly one simple \(U\)-root equals zero there, the local monodromy of \(p_d(T^2)\) at \(v=2\) flips just that one pair: it is a single transposition in the kernel. Conjugating by the surjective action on pairs supplies an independent flip of every pair; hence the kernel contains all of \(C_2^m\) and \(G=C_2\wr S_m\). At every other finite branch value, two nonzero critical points \(T=\pm\sqrt{U_r}\) produce two disjoint transpositions. The polynomial of degree \(2m\) is totally ramified at infinity, giving one \(2m\)-cycle.

**4. Base change to the Fermat projection.** Let \(K=\mathbb C(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\) and let \(v=-\sum z_h^d\). The map \(\mathbb A^n\to\mathbb A^1\) given by this polynomial has geometrically irreducible generic fiber when \(n\ge2\): over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb C(v)\), its projective closure is a smooth Fermat hypersurface of positive dimension, hence irreducible. Thus \(\mathbb C(v)\) is algebraically closed in \(K\); every finite separable extension of \(\mathbb C(v)\) is linearly disjoint from \(K\). The function-field Galois-closure group therefore survives this base change. The branch-cycle portrait remains a statement about the one-variable \(v\)-model; it is not an assertion that every inertia class at the boundary of the higher-dimensional target is unchanged.

**5. Exact deck group.** In the natural action of \(C_2\wr S_m\) on \(2m\) sheets, the stabilizer \(H\) of a given sheet fixes precisely the two sheets in its pair, because \(m\ge2\). Its normalizer must preserve that two-element fixed set, and \(N_G(H)/H\cong C_2\). This quotient is the automorphism group of the nonnormal degree-\(2m\) function-field extension. It is exactly the geometric sign involution \(T\mapsto-T\), proving the deck-group assertion. For \(d=4\), \(m=2\), so the normal closure is \(C_2\wr S_2\cong D_4\). Miura--Taki's Theorem 5.4 identifies precisely the 24 selected pair-centers among the quartic's non-Galois quasi-Galois points, giving the stated consequence.

## Verification
The standalone script `checks/verify.py` uses exact symbolic arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q\) to check the degree, squarefreeness of the critical-value discriminant, and regularity of \(p_d(0)=2\) for every integer \(4\le d\le25\); it reports `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite consistency tests only: the all-degree conclusion is the root-of-unity argument in Step 2, combined with the monodromy argument in Steps 3--5. The projective charts, degree parity, and group-theoretic stabilizer calculation have been checked independently in the proof, without reliance on the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Miura--Taki (arXiv:2608.25445v1, primary MSC 14J70, first public 2026-08-26) classify quasi-Galois points for quartic surfaces; their Propositions 3.4 and 5.2 and Theorem 5.4 identify the 24 coordinate-pair centers on the Fermat quartic but do not determine the Galois closures of their projections. Takahashi (2016, DOI 10.4171/RSMUP/135-3) proves a dihedral order-eight statement for projections from points of **plane quintic curves**, including 15 such points on the Fermat quintic. This covers a particular low-degree curve case of the monodromy statement; by ordinary regular base change, it can also yield its degree-five higher-dimensional specialization. The current assertion is the uniform all-degree formula, the explicit finite branch-cycle portrait and the quartic-surface consequence, not a claim of priority over that known quintic case. The general wreath-product property of a composition \(p(T^2)\) is standard when the requisite Morse and regular-value hypotheses hold; the substantive family-specific part is the proof that the explicit binomial \(p_d\) satisfies those hypotheses for every \(d\ge4\). A manuscript on Fermat quasi-Galois points cited by Miura--Taki as "in preparation" was not available for inspection and is a residual originality risk, not evidence of coverage or uniqueness.

## Limitations
No enumeration of **all** quasi-Galois points on Fermat hypersurfaces of arbitrary dimension and degree is asserted; only the specified pair-centers occur in the theorem. For \(d=4\), the **locations** of the 24 centers are already known. The degree-five plane-curve dihedral case is already published. The theorem is over \(\mathbb C\), requires \(n\ge2\), and does not determine the birational geometry of the full normal-closure variety. Branch-cycle descriptions refer to the natural one-parameter polynomial quotient; under the multivariate base map, branch divisors are inverse images of those values, without an assertion about their global irreducible components.
