# Idempotent derivations of cubic-truncated symmetric algebras over arbitrary fields

## Finding

Let \(K\) be any field, \(n\geq1\), and \(A=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^3\). An *idempotent derivation* means a \(K\)-linear associative derivation \(D:A\to A\) satisfying \(D^2=D\), where the square is composition.

**Theorem.** In characteristic different from two, \(D=0\) is the only idempotent derivation. In characteristic two, all such derivations, up to conjugation by \(K\)-algebra automorphisms, have exactly \(n+1\) distinct normal forms \(\delta_r\), for \(0\leq r\leq n\), uniquely determined by
\[
\delta_r(x_i)=\begin{cases}x_i,&1\leq i\leq r,\\0,&r<i\leq n.\end{cases}
\]
Every derivation in the orbit of \(\delta_r\) has linear rank \(r(n-r+1)\); these orbits are distinct even when two such ranks coincide.

For every finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) of even cardinality, set \(u=n-r\) and
\[
E(n,r)=r\left[\binom{r+1}{2}+\binom{u+1}{2}\right]+ru^2.
\]
Then the complete idempotent-derivation rank enumerator is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{\substack{D\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb F_q}(A)\\D^2=D}}t^{\operatorname{rank}D}
=1+\sum_{r=1}^{n}{n\brack r}_q\,q^{r(n-r)+E(n,r)}\,t^{r(n-r+1)}.}
\]
Here \({n\brack r}_q=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^{n-i}-1)/(q^{r-i}-1)\) is a Gaussian binomial coefficient. For odd \(q\) the polynomial is exactly \(1\). In particular, in characteristic two there are exactly \(n+1\) conjugacy classes, whereas in every other characteristic there is only one.

## Assumptions and scope

The algebra is commutative, unital, finite-dimensional, and has maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) with \(\mathfrak m^3=0\). Write \(V=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) and \(S=\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\). The vector-space decomposition \(A=K\oplus V\oplus S\) is induced by the standard grading, and multiplication on positive-degree elements is generated by \(vw\in S\) for \(v,w\in V\), with all degree-three terms zero. All classifications hold for any field; counting assertions require a finite field. The rank is the rank of \(D\) as a \(K\)-linear endomorphism of the entire algebra, including its unit.

## Proof

**Step 1: the characteristic boundary.** For any associative derivation, the Leibniz identity gives
\[
D^2(ab)=D^2(a)b+2D(a)D(b)+aD^2(b).
\]
If \(D^2=D\), comparison with \(D(ab)\) implies \(2D(a)D(b)=0\) for all \(a,b\). Suppose \(\operatorname{char}K\ne2\). Taking \(a=b=x_i\) shows \(D(x_i)^2=0\). For an element \(c+v+s\in K\oplus V\oplus S\), its square has constant part \(c^2\) and, when \(c=0\), quadratic part \(v^2\). Since \(v^2\ne0\) in the ordinary symmetric square for every nonzero \(v\in V\), the equality \((c+v+s)^2=0\) forces \(c=v=0\). Therefore \(D(V)\subseteq S\). As \(D(S)=D(VV)=0\), one gets \(D^2(V)=0\) and consequently \(D(V)=0\). This proves \(D=0\), independently of any restriction on the field other than characteristic.

**Step 2: all derivations in characteristic two.** Suppose \(\operatorname{char}K=2\). The relation \(x_i^3=0\) and Leibniz imply \(0=D(x_i^3)=x_i^2D(x_i)\). The coefficient of \(x_i^2\) forces the scalar part of \(D(x_i)\) to vanish. Thus each derivation is uniquely described by linear maps
\[
P:V\longrightarrow V,\qquad Q:V\longrightarrow S,\qquad D(v)=P(v)+Q(v).
\]
Conversely every such pair defines a derivation of \(A\): define it by Leibniz on polynomials, observing that the cubic ideal is stable because all derivatives of degree-three monomials still have degree at least three. On \(S\), the action equals the induced Leibniz map
\[
\delta_P(vw)=P(v)w+vP(w).
\]
In characteristic two, \(D^2\) is itself a derivation, so equality \(D^2=D\) holds on all of \(A\) if and only if it holds on \(V\). Comparing homogeneous parts on \(V\) yields the necessary and sufficient equations
\[
P^2=P,\qquad Q=QP+\delta_PQ.\tag{1}
\]

**Step 3: the conjugacy normal form.** Fix an idempotent \(P\), and write \(V=U\oplus W\), with \(U=\operatorname{im}P\) of dimension \(r\) and \(W=\ker P\) of dimension \(u=n-r\). The symmetric square decomposes as
\[
S=\operatorname{Sym}^2U\oplus(UW)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2W.
\]
The map \(\delta_P\) acts by zero on the first and third summands and by the identity on \(UW\). Equation (1) therefore says exactly
\[
Q(U)\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2U\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2W,
\qquad Q(W)\subseteq UW.\tag{2}
\]
For any linear \(H:V\to S\), the substitution \(\Phi_H(v)=v+H(v)\) extends to an algebra automorphism of \(A\). Indeed it fixes \(S\) pointwise, and its inverse is the substitution with \(-H\); in characteristic two \(\Phi_H^{-1}=\Phi_H\). Direct calculation on \(V\) gives
\[
\Phi_H\,\delta_P\,\Phi_H^{-1}(v)=P(v)+H(Pv)+\delta_PH(v).
\]
If (1) holds, taking \(H=Q\) produces precisely \(D\). Thus every \(D\) is automorphism-conjugate to \(\delta_P\), and a linear change of variables conjugates \(\delta_P\) to \(\delta_r\). The value \(r\) is intrinsic because it is the rank of \(D\) induced on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\), hence distinct \(r\) give distinct automorphism orbits.

**Step 4: rank.** On \(V\), \(\delta_P\) has rank \(r\); on \(S\), it has image \(UW\) of dimension \(ru\); and it kills \(1\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}\delta_P=r+ru=r(n-r+1)\). Conjugation preserves rank, proving the rank statement for every derivation.

**Step 5: finite-field census.** For fixed rank \(r\), the number of idempotent linear maps \(P:V\to V\) equals \({n\brack r}_q q^{ru}\): choose the \(r\)-dimensional image and one of the \(q^{ru}\) complementary kernels. The necessary and sufficient restrictions (2) are independent linear restrictions on \(Q\); their solution space has dimension
\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}(U,\operatorname{Sym}^2U\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2W)+\dim\operatorname{Hom}(W,UW)
=r\left[\binom{r+1}{2}+\binom{u+1}{2}\right]+ru^2.
\]
Consequently each \(P\) has exactly \(q^{E(n,r)}\) compatible quadratic parts. Multiply and sum by rank, with the unique \(r=0\) solution \(D=0\), to obtain the asserted polynomial. The orbit analysis proves the final conjugacy count.

## Verification

A standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` independently builds derivation matrices in the monomial basis and checks the equation \(D^2=D\) and ordinary matrix ranks. Direct exhaustive comparisons with the formula include \((n,q)=(1,2),(1,3),(1,5),(2,2),(2,3)\); full constant-term checks for the potentially exceptional characteristic-three one-generator case; a separate \(\mathbb F_4\) one-generator check; and sampled higher-dimensional tests plus constructed quadratic-shear conjugates. Run `python3 verify.py` (no external packages required); it prints `VERIFY_OK` upon successful completion. The finite tests confirm examples but are not offered as proofs for general \(n\) or arbitrary fields.

Two reference distributions for \(q=2\) are \(\mathcal P_{2,2}(t)=1+112t^2\) and \(\mathcal P_{3,2}(t)=1+269312t^3+28672t^4\). The second has exactly \(297985\) operators.

## Relationship to prior work

Idempotent derivations in characteristic two were previously related bijectively to \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\)-gradings; this observation is credited to Bahturin and Parmenter by Cojuhari–Gardner, Example 2.3. That general relation alone does not give the present exact finite-field counts, ranks, or automorphism normal forms for \(A\). Bahturin–Kochetov, Theorem 4.8, classifies gradings of \(O(m;1)=K[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_1^p,\ldots,x_m^p)\) over algebraically closed fields of characteristic \(p\), a different algebra from the cube-of-the-maximal-ideal quotient treated here. Suárez-Álvarez studies derivations and automorphisms of a different noncommutative algebra over characteristic-zero fields. Prior structural gradings theory is essential background, not a new assertion of this work.

## Limitations

This result applies to the symmetric cube-jet algebra as stated; it does not classify idempotent derivations for all local algebras with radical cube zero, nor does it classify arbitrary graded identities. In characteristic two the result uses the standard symmetric algebra, not the divided-power algebra. Existing general theory of gradings may yield alternative proofs or formulations. The finite verifications do not replace the symbolic proof. The cited studies do not rule out all possible equivalent prior treatments under different terminology.
