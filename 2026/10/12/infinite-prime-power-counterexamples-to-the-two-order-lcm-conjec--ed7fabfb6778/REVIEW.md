# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** The matrix representation proves that zero indices are exact multiples of the first index and the period-to-rank quotient is at most two. For \(a\equiv4\pmod6\), the first zero and period modulo \(2^{\nu_2(a)+1}\) are exactly four. The tripling identity \(U_{3r}=U_r(3+(a^2-4)U_r^2)\) guarantees an odd zero index modulo each \(3^j\); period reduction to modulus three forces zero count two. Least-common-multiple identities then prove the order-one product. A finite exact-arithmetic checker was executed; it is not the infinite proof.

The theorem does not classify all possible mixed pairs or the other table cells.
