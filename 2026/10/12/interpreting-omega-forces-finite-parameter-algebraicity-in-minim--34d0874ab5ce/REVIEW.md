# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Proposition 5.2 of the primary source gives a dimension-one interpretation of \((\omega,<)\) from any finite-dimensional one. Fixing the finite parameter tuple defining that interpretation, every quotient level is definable over the same tuple. Minimality forces all infinitely many pairwise disjoint levels to be finite, so every home-sort element is algebraic over that tuple.

## Originality

PASS. The primary paper states dimension reduction and finite levels but does not formulate the common finite-parameter algebraicity conclusion; its full text contains no \(\operatorname{acl}\) statement of this kind. The closest older almost-linear literature checked discusses particular examples and does not cover the general finite-dimensional consequence. Searches for finite algebraic generation, automorphism-orbit, and locally finite algebraic-closure equivalents found no dominating statement. The remaining risk is folklore duplication because the deduction is short.

## Value

PASS. The result converts interpretability of \((\omega,<)\) into a concrete algebraic-closure obstruction: locally finite algebraic closure rules out every finite-dimensional interpretation. This can eliminate candidate minimal structures without analyzing all possible definable quotient orders.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Jagiella, arXiv:2609.36313v2, especially Definition 2.9 and Proposition 5.2. Krupiński–Tanović–Wagner, arXiv:1201.5709, is relevant background on almost linear minimal structures. The theorem is only necessary, concerns the home sort, and is specific to the definability of individual positions in \((\omega,<)\).
