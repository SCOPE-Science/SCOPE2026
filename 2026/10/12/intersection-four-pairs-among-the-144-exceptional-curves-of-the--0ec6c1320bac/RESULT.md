# Intersection-four pairs among the 144 exceptional curves of the Chilean Halphen surface
## Finding
Let \(S\) be the rational elliptic surface obtained from the index-\(2\) Halphen pencil of Hesse type in Dolgachev–Laface–Persson–Urzúa, and let \(\operatorname{Exc}(S)\) denote its \(144\) \((-1)\)-curves. For distinct curves \(E,E'\), the global divisor intersection number takes exactly the values
\[
0,\;1,\;2,\;3,\;4.
\]
Among the \(\binom{144}{2}=10296\) unordered pairs, the respective counts are
\[
2232,\quad4320,\quad2880,\quad792,\quad72.
\]
Thus the maximum is \(4\).

More precisely, every intersection-\(4\) pair consists of a cubic and a quartic. If the cubic has a double point at \(p_i\), simple points at six other base points, and omits precisely \(p_j,p_k\), then it meets with intersection number \(4\) exactly the two quartics having their triple point at \(p_j\) and \(p_k\). Consequently each of the \(36\) cubics has two such partners, while each of the \(9\) quartics has eight, giving \(72\) unordered pairs.

## Assumptions and scope
The surface and geometric basis are those of the cited index-\(2\) Hesse-type Halphen construction. Write the Picard basis as \(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_9\), with
\[
e_0^2=1,\qquad e_i^2=-1,\qquad e_i\cdot e_j=0\quad(i\ne j).
\]
The statement concerns global divisor intersection numbers on the blown-up surface. It does not claim a largest local intersection multiplicity at a single point.

## Proof
The source proves that there are exactly \(144\) \((-1)\)-curves: \(9\) exceptional curves, \(36\) transforms of lines, \(54\) transforms of conics, \(36\) transforms of cubics, and \(9\) transforms of quartics. It gives \(16\) Picard-class representatives as two displayed \(10\times8\) matrices and states that the order-\(9\) Mordell–Weil translation subgroup acts freely; on the nine base points it is generated by
\[
(195)(276)(384),\qquad (186)(294)(375).
\]
The same paper proves that the plane class \(e_0\) is invariant under the projective automorphism group. Hence these translations act on the displayed class vectors by permuting \(e_1,\ldots,e_9\) and fixing \(e_0\). Applying the resulting nine permutations to the sixteen representatives produces \(144\) distinct classes with exactly the published degree distribution.

For two classes
\[
D=d e_0+\sum_{r=1}^9 a_r e_r,\qquad
D'=d'e_0+\sum_{r=1}^9 a'_r e_r,
\]
their intersection is
\[
D\cdot D'=dd'-\sum_{r=1}^9a_ra'_r.
\]
Exact enumeration of all \(10296\) pairs gives the stated census.

The intersection-\(4\) characterization is also direct. A cubic with double point \(p_i\), omitted points \(p_j,p_k\), and simple points at the other six has class
\[
C=3e_0-2e_i-\sum_{r\notin\{i,j,k\}}e_r.
\]
The quartic with triple point \(p_j\) has class
\[
Q_j=4e_0-3e_j-\sum_{r\ne j}e_r.
\]
Because \(j\) is omitted by the cubic,
\[
C\cdot Q_j=12-(2+6)=4.
\]
The same holds for \(Q_k\). For any other quartic the same intersection formula gives a smaller value, and the exhaustive calculation confirms that no other degree pair reaches \(4\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` contains the two published representative matrices and the two published generators of the base-point permutation group. It reconstructs the order-\(9\) group, generates \(144\) distinct classes, checks the published degree distribution, checks self-intersection \(-1\), exhausts all \(10296\) unordered pairs, and verifies the cubic–quartic characterization of all \(72\) intersection-\(4\) pairs.

Run `python3 verify.py`. A clean replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Dolgachev–Laface–Persson–Urzúa provide the complete \(144\)-curve class data and the translation action. Their Remark 5.10 records a different non-geometric pairing whose paired curves have intersection number \(3\). Later, Remark 6.13 says that in this example two \((-1)\)-curves were observed to have intersection multiplicity at most \(3\), and asks for the maximal intersection number on Halphen surfaces with extremal Jacobian fibration. The exact Picard calculation above shows that, for global divisor intersection on this very surface, the maximum is already \(4\). The full pair census and the cubic–quartic characterization were not found in the inspected source or focused searches.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact statement about the \(144\) curves on the index-\(2\) Hesse-type surface. It does not answer the source's broader maximum question for all Halphen surfaces with extremal Jacobian fibrations. The comparison with Remark 6.13 is specifically about global divisor intersection number; if “intersection multiplicity” there was intended to mean a largest local multiplicity at one point, the two statements concern different invariants.
