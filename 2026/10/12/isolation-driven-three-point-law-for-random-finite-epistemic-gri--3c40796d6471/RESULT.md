# Isolation-driven three-point law for random finite epistemic grids

## Finding

Let \(W_n=[n]\times[n]\), with two named equivalence modalities whose accessibility classes are respectively rows and columns. Give the single propositional atom \(P\) an independently sampled truth value at each cell, with probability
\[
\rho_n=\frac{\log n+c}{n},\qquad c\in\mathbb R\text{ fixed},
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\) for which \(0<\rho_n<1\). Let \(\mathcal A_n(P)\) be the Boolean subalgebra of \(\mathcal P(W_n)\) generated by the valuation of \(P\) and closed under both row and column existential modalities.

**Theorem.** Put \(\lambda=e^{-c}\). As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\bigl(|\mathcal A_n(P)|=4\bigr)&\longrightarrow e^{-2\lambda},\\
\Pr\bigl(|\mathcal A_n(P)|=8\bigr)&\longrightarrow 2e^{-\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda}),\\
\Pr\bigl(|\mathcal A_n(P)|=32\bigr)&\longrightarrow (1-e^{-\lambda})^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
and the probability of every other algebra cardinality tends to zero. With probability tending to one, the partition into modal-equivalence classes stabilizes at modal depth at most one. In particular, there are asymptotically only two, three, or five distinguishable states, even though the model contains \(n^2\) worlds.

For comparison, when the valuation probability is any fixed \(\rho\in(0,1)\), the generated algebra has **exactly four elements** with probability tending to one, with exponentially small exceptional probability. The size threshold is governed by independent Poisson limits for the numbers of completely unmarked rows and columns, rather than by counts of marked cells or by the size of the full powerset algebra.

## Assumptions and scope

The language is basic classical bimodal propositional logic, containing Boolean connectives, **one** atom \(P\), and existential modalities \(\langle R\rangle\), \(\langle C\rangle\). Relation \(R\) equates cells in a common row; \(C\) equates cells in a common column. Both are reflexive, symmetric, and transitive; the modalities are named and are not interchanged. Their operations on subsets commute. There are no equality/diagonal constants, propositional quantifiers, counting modalities, or built-in common-knowledge operators.

A modal type is an equivalence class of worlds satisfying the same formulas of the specified language. Since the model is finite, the Boolean algebra of all definable sets has one atom per stable modal type, and therefore has \(2^K\) elements when there are \(K\) stable types. The limiting theorem is for independent cell Bernoulli labels; it makes no assertion for correlated labels or a fixed number of marked cells.

## Proof

**1. Deterministic one-round classification.** Let \(M\) be a nonzero binary \(n\times n\) matrix. Call a row or column *active* if it contains at least one \(1\); let \(R_0,C_0\) be the sets of inactive rows and columns, and \(R_+,C_+\) their active complements. Assume that every active row has both a \(0\) and a \(1\) among its entries in active columns, and every active column has both a \(0\) and a \(1\) among its entries in active rows.

At depth zero there are precisely two types, given by the entry of \(M\). At depth one a world's type consists of its entry and the sets of depth-zero types seen through its row and column. An inactive row sees only \(0\); an active row sees \(\{0,1\}\), with the analogous statement for columns. Thus the distinct depth-one types are exactly the feasible triples
\[
(M_{ij},\mathbf1_{\{i\in R_0\}},\mathbf1_{\{j\in C_0\}}).
\]
All four combinations of row/column inactivity, when present, give a \(0\)-type; the active-row/active-column block gives in addition a \(1\)-type. Consequently the type count is
\[
K=2+a+b+ab,\quad a=\mathbf1_{\{R_0\ne\varnothing\}},\quad b=\mathbf1_{\{C_0\ne\varnothing\}}.
\]
This is \(2\), \(3\), or \(5\), according as neither side, exactly one side, or both sides have inactive classes.

Moreover, the row-successor set of depth-one types depends only on whether that row is active, not on the identity of the active row: every active row sees both colors in the active columns and all-zero entries in any inactive columns. The analogous assertion holds for column-successor sets. Therefore depth-two refinement makes no new distinctions; induction gives stabilization at depth one. The generated algebra has \(2^K\) elements, as claimed.

**2. The random matrix meets the deterministic assumptions.** For the sparse Bernoulli model, a row is inactive with probability \((1-\rho_n)^n\), so the expected number of inactive rows tends to \(\lambda\), and similarly for columns. Markov's inequality shows that the active row and column sets each have cardinality \(n-o_{\Pr}(n)\). Each row and column has binomial degree with mean \(\log n+c\), and a Chernoff or elementary binomial union bound gives
\[
\Pr\bigl(\text{some row or column has at least }n/2\text{ marked cells}\bigr)=o(1).
\]
With probability tending to one, at least \(3n/4\) rows and columns are active, while every row and column has fewer than \(n/2\) marked cells. Every active row then has a marked and an unmarked entry among active columns, and likewise for active columns. The deterministic classification applies with probability \(1-o(1)\).

**3. Joint Poisson limit, including row-column dependence.** Write \(X_n=|R_0|\), \(Y_n=|C_0|\). For fixed nonnegative integers \(a,b\), let \((z)_k=z(z-1)\cdots(z-k+1)\) denote a falling factorial. Choosing \(a\) distinct rows and \(b\) distinct columns leaves exactly \(n(a+b)-ab\) cells whose labels must all be zero. Thus
\[
\mathbb E\big[(X_n)_a(Y_n)_b\big]
=(n)_a(n)_b(1-\rho_n)^{n(a+b)-ab}
\longrightarrow\lambda^{a+b}.
\]
These are exactly the joint factorial moments of two independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\) variables; their distributions are moment-determinate. Hence \((X_n,Y_n)\) converges jointly to that independent pair. The probabilities of no inactive side, exactly one inactive side, and two inactive sides converge respectively to
\[
e^{-2\lambda},\qquad 2e^{-\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda}),\qquad (1-e^{-\lambda})^2.
\]
Combining this with Step 2 and the deterministic type count proves all three limiting algebra probabilities and excludes other sizes asymptotically.

**4. Fixed positive density.** If \(\rho_n=\rho\in(0,1)\), the probability that any of the \(2n\) rows or columns is monochromatic is at most
\[
2n\big(\rho^n+(1-\rho)^n\big).
\]
Outside this exponentially small event, each row and column sees both colors. The depth-zero partition is then already stable, with exactly two modal types; the generated algebra has four elements.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library verifier \(`verify_random_epistemic.py`\) enumerates all \(66{,}064\) binary matrices for square sizes \(2,3,4\) and checks the one-round theorem whenever its structural hypotheses hold. It compares an independently implemented iterative modal refinement with the formula for all \(32{,}440\) applicable matrices. Forty-three selected small cases also undergo direct closure of subsets under Boolean operations and both existential modalities. Another \(480\) Bernoulli matrices across three finite sizes undergo full iterative refinement; \(36\) exact finite factorial-moment evaluations check the moment identity and its limiting trend. Output:

```text
VERIFY_OK exhaustive_binary_matrices 66064 structural_cases 32440 direct_boolean_closures 43 random_refinements 480 factorial_moment_checks 36
```

Finite enumeration does **not** prove the probabilistic limit; that limit follows from the factorial-moment and high-probability arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Two-dimensional diagonal-free cylindric algebras and finite subalgebra lattices have a substantial established theory; see Figallo and Mónica-Gomes (2023). Bezhanishvili and Meadors (first preprint, 2023) discuss infinite one-generated \(\mathsf{S5}^2\)-algebras and the deterministic Erdős–Tarski construction. Feitosa, Matulovic, and Golzio (2023) give a related algebraic approach to a basic **different** bimodal epistemic logic, whose knowledge-belief comparison axioms are not imposed here. None of these cited structural results is asserted as new. The standard sparse random-bipartite-graph isolated-vertex Poisson threshold is likewise not new.

The present claim is the **joint critical-window distribution of the generated modal-algebra cardinality** and the deterministic depth-one mechanism connecting this distribution to the two independent sets of isolated rows and columns. It is not a rephrasing of generic modal completeness, of a subalgebra-lattice description, or of the classical Poisson limit alone.

## Limitations

The limit holds for fixed \(c\in\mathbb R\) and \(n\to\infty\), not uniformly over arbitrary sequences \(c=c_n\). The deterministic sufficient criterion does not classify every exceptional binary matrix. The result does not count all subalgebras, classify all generators, or treat graded/diagonal modalities; these enrichments can distinguish additional worlds. The closest finite Df2 subalgebra literature has been inspected, but an unindexed antecedent for the precise distribution remains possible. The proof and numerical tests have received self-review only, not independent mathematical audit.
