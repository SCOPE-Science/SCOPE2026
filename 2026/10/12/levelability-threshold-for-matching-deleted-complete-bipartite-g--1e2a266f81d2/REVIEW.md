# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The maximal independent sets of \(K_{a,b}-M_t\) are determined directly from the bipartition and the deleted matching. The positive cases are witnessed by explicit positive integer weight formulas. For \(t\ge2\) and \(a+b>4\), the maximal sets \(A,B,\{a_1,b_1\},\{a_2,b_2\}\) satisfy the strict-union obstruction from the initiating levelability paper. The only boundary left is \((2,2,2)\), where direct equations give all weights \((p,q,q,p)\). The checker independently enumerates all maximal independent sets through \(a,b\le6\).

## Originality
PASS. The initiating paper proves levelability for complete multipartite graphs, gives a general obstruction, and asks for classifications of possible weight functions, but its inspected full text contains no matching-deleted complete-bipartite classification. The closest general well-covered-vector-space paper gives algorithms rather than this symbolic positive-cone theorem. Semantic-index and web searches under levelable, positive well-covered, crown, and matching-deletion aliases found no covering statement. A prior same-family CIS result concerns a different property with no implication for weighted maximal-independent-set equality.

## Value
PASS. Matching deletion is the most local structured perturbation of complete bipartite graphs. The theorem gives a sharp stability boundary—zero or one deleted matching edge versus immediate failure at two, with one exact four-vertex exception—and simultaneously resolves the full positive-integer weighting cone in every surviving case. This directly addresses the initiating paper's stated program of classifying levelable families and possible weight functions, rather than recording an isolated numerical example.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Bhaskara et al., arXiv:2504.02065, especially Definition 1.1, Corollary 2.3, Lemma 2.7, and Remark 3.5. Milanič--Pivač, DOI 10.1007/s40314-023-02502-8, is broader algorithmically but does not state the family theorem. A residual risk remains that older weighted-well-covered literature contains an equivalent family under different notation.
