# Local class groups and the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein skeleton of odd-cycle stable-set toric varieties

## Finding
Let \(m\ge3\), \(n=2m+1\), and let \(X_m\) be the complex normal projective toric variety of the stable-set polytope \(P_m=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_S:S\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\text{ independent in }C_n\}\). For each \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), set \(F_i=\{x\in P_m:x_i=0,\ \sum_jx_j=m\}\) and \(Z_i=X_{F_i}\subseteq X_m\), the torus-orbit closure of this face. Then the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus and the non-Gorenstein locus agree, and both equal \(\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}Z_i\). The \(n\) irreducible components are distinct, each is a **linearly embedded** \(\mathbb P^m\) in the stable-set projective embedding, and each has codimension \(m+1\) in \(X_m\).

For \(1\le\delta\le2m\) and indices interpreted modulo \(n\), the **scheme-theoretic intersection** of two distinct components is a linear projective space:
\[
Z_i\cap Z_{i+\delta}\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb P^{q-1},&\delta=2q-1,\ 1\le q\le m,\\
\mathbb P^{m-q},&\delta=2q,\ 1\le q\le m.
\end{cases}
\]
At the generic point \(\eta_i\) of \(Z_i\), the local divisor-class group is \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,\eta_i})\cong\mathbb Z\), and the canonical class equals \((m-2)g\) for a primitive generator \(g\) (up to sign). This proves the failure of \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorensteinness along each component rather than merely the failure of Gorensteinness.

More precisely, if \(p_S\) is the torus-fixed point indexed by any independent set \(S\) with \(k=|S|\le m\), then
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p_S})\cong\mathbb Z^{\,k+\mathbf1_{k=m}},\qquad
[K_{X_m}]_{p_S}=
\begin{cases}
0,&k<m,\\
(m-2)[D_{\mathrm{odd}}],&k=m,
\end{cases}
\]
where \([D_{\mathrm{odd}}]\) is primitive in the latter free class group. The number of fixed points with \(|S|=k\) is \(1\) for \(k=0\), and \(\frac nk\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}\) for \(1\le k\le m\).

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\); \(m\ge3\) throughout. Edges of \(C_n\) are \(\{i,i+1\}\) with subscripts modulo \(n\). The polytope is full-dimensional of dimension \(n\), so \(\dim X_m=n\). Its Ehrhart ring equals the normal standard-graded stable-set toric ring: a cycle is almost bipartite, and its toric ideal admits a squarefree quadratic initial ideal [Matsuda--Ohsugi--Shibata, 2016, introductory item (5)]. The fan is the normal fan of \(P_m\). Divisor-class groups are those of **local rings**, not the global class group. The local canonical class is computed in the fan convention where each toric boundary divisor has coefficient \(-1\) in a torus-invariant representative of the canonical divisor.

## Proof
**1. Primitive facet normals.** The irredundant inequalities for the odd-cycle stable-set polytope are
\[
x_t\ge0,\qquad x_t+x_{t+1}\le1\quad(t\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z),\qquad \sum_tx_t\le m.
\]
This is the special case of the series-parallel stable-set facet theorem [Mahjoub, 1988]; each inequality is facet-defining. With inward-normal convention the primitive rays of the normal fan are
\[
u_t=e_t,\qquad v_t=-e_t-e_{t+1},\qquad w=-\sum_te_t.
\]
The normal cone at a face is generated by normals to its containing facets.

**2. Fixed-point class groups.** At \(p_S\), the active zero facets are \(u_t\) for \(t\notin S\); the active edge facets are \(v_t\) for edges incident with a vertex of \(S\), exactly \(2k\) since \(S\) is independent; the last facet \(w\) is active exactly for \(k=m\). Thus there are \(n+k+\mathbf1_{k=m}\) primitive rays. Among them the \(n\) normals
\[
\{u_t:t\notin S\}\ \cup\ \{v_s:s\in S\}
\]
form a lattice basis: the matrix is diagonal on coordinates \(t\notin S\), while \(v_s=-e_s-e_{s+1}\) has coefficient \(-1\) in the unique remaining coordinate \(s\); no two chosen \(s\)'s are adjacent. Consequently the divisor map from the character lattice is saturated, so the local toric divisor sequence gives a **torsion-free** class group of rank the ray count minus \(n\), as asserted.

To compute the canonical class, choose a lattice character \(h\) with coordinates \(h_t=-1\) for \(t\notin S\) and \(h_s=2\) for \(s\in S\). It pairs to \(-1\) with every active \(u\)- and \(v\)-ray. If \(k=m\), then
\[
\langle h,w\rangle=-(2m-(n-m))=1-m.
\]
Subtracting the principal divisor of \(h\) from the boundary representative \(K=-\sum D_\rho\) leaves precisely \((m-2)D_{\mathrm{odd}}\). The unimodular subset of rays also shows that the remaining \(D_{\mathrm{odd}}\) class is primitive. For \(k<m\), every ray pairs to \(-1\), so the local canonical divisor is principal. In particular, for \(k=m\) no nonzero multiple of \(K\) can be principal.

**3. The faces are unimodular simplices.** Rotate indices to take \(i=0\). The \(m+1\) maximal independent sets avoiding vertex \(0\) are exactly
\[
S_r=\{1,3,\ldots,2r-1\}\cup\{2r+2,2r+4,\ldots,2m\},\qquad 0\le r\le m.
\]
Their consecutive indicator-vector differences are \(\mathbf1_{S_{r+1}}-\mathbf1_{S_r}=e_{2r+1}-e_{2r+2}\). They form a basis of the rank-\(m\) face-difference lattice, so \(F_0\) is a unimodular \(m\)-simplex. The face coordinate ring is therefore polynomial on its \(m+1\) degree-one lattice points, and \(Z_0=\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb C[F_0]\cong\mathbb P^m\), embedded linearly. Rotations give all the components. Their defining faces are distinct; since \(\dim X_m=2m+1\), their codimension is \(m+1\).

**4. Generic local obstruction.** Every point of \(F_0\) satisfies precisely the facets with inward normals
\[
u_0,\quad v_1,v_3,\ldots,v_{2m-1},\quad w.
\]
Indeed \(\sum_{j=1}^m v_{2j-1}-u_0=w\). The first \(m+1\) normals form a basis of the saturated rank-\(m+1\) sublattice of their span: the columns \(0,1,3,\ldots,2m-1\) give an absolute-determinant-one minor. Hence the affine transverse cone has \(m+2\) rays and local class group \(\mathbb Z\), generated primitively by the class of the odd facet. Any lattice character that pairs to \(-1\) with the first \(m+1\) rays must pair to \(1-m\) with \(w\), giving \([K]=(m-2)[D_{\mathrm{odd}}]\ne0\). Because this class group has no torsion, \(K\) is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier at the generic point \(\eta_0\), and likewise at each \(\eta_i\).

**5. Equality of the two loci.** The non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus is closed, so Step 4 forces it to contain every \(Z_i\). Conversely, every Gorenstein local ring has Cartier canonical divisor, so the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus lies in the non-Gorenstein locus. Miyazaki's Theorem 3.4 [2022] proves that the latter locus of the affine Ehrhart cone is exactly the union of the homogeneous toric subvarieties defined by the face ideals for \(F_i\). Passing to \(\operatorname{Proj}\), where the punctured affine cone is locally a \(\mathbb G_m\)-bundle because the section ring is standard graded, gives precisely \(\bigcup_i Z_i\) for the projective non-Gorenstein locus. This proves equality without claiming Miyazaki's prior support theorem as new.

**6. Exact intersections.** Toric face-orbit closures obey the scheme-theoretic identity \(X_F\cap X_G=X_{F\cap G}\): their homogeneous monomial face ideals sum to the prime face ideal of \(F\cap G\). For \(F_0\), its vertices are \(\mathbf1_{S_r}\). A second zero constraint at \(i+\delta\) retains exactly \(q\) of these vertices when \(\delta=2q-1\), and exactly \(m-q+1\) when \(\delta=2q\). These retained vertices are affinely independent as a subset of a unimodular simplex, so their face varieties are the claimed linearly embedded projective spaces.

**7. Fixed-point counts.** Mark a starting element of an independent \(k\)-subset of a cycle and record the positive cyclic gaps between successive elements; each gap is at least \(2\), so after subtracting \(1\) the gaps are a positive composition of \(n-k\) into \(k\) parts. There are \(n\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}\) marked configurations, and each unmarked configuration has \(k\) marks. Divide by \(k\).

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library Python checker enumerates all stable sets for \(n=5,7,9,11,13,15\), verifies the primitive active facets and unimodular normal bases by exact Bareiss determinants, computes class-group rank distributions and canonical pairing discrepancies, checks every relevant face normal relation, and verifies every pairwise face-intersection count. The boundary \(n=5\) has zero canonical obstruction and is intentionally excluded from the main non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein theorem. Finite computation is supporting evidence, not a proof for all \(m\); the unbounded argument is Steps 1--7.

Run from a directory containing the embedded files with `python3 verify_odd_cycle.py`; the recorded result appears in `verify_output.txt`. The checker uses only the Python standard library and terminates successfully at the stated sizes.

## Relationship to prior work
The facet inequalities are classical [Mahjoub, 1988], and normality/degree-one generation follow from prior Gröbner theory [Matsuda--Ohsugi--Shibata, 2016]. **Miyazaki's Theorem 3.4 (2022) already completely determines the set-theoretic non-Gorenstein support** of the Ehrhart cone; that result is an explicit input, not an originality claim here. The added assertions are the equality with the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus, primitive canonical obstruction and full local class-group ranks at fixed points, unimodular simplex/linear component structures, and precise scheme-theoretic pairwise intersections. Hibi--Tsuchiya's prior global Gorenstein and Hilbert-series investigations do not, in the material inspected, present these local divisor-theoretic or intersection formulas.

## Limitations
The result concerns only odd **chordless** cycles of length at least seven, normal projective stable-set toric varieties, the specified generic-component and fixed-point local groups, and pairwise (not higher) component intersections. It does not classify all local analytic singularities or the nonreduced singular scheme. Although the direct primary-literature comparison did not find an equivalent full local-divisor theorem, an equivalent unindexed specialization of more general toric class-group methods or an overlooked passage remains a genuine originality risk.
