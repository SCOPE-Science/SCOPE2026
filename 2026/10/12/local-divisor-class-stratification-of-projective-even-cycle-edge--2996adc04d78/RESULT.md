# Local divisor-class stratification of projective even-cycle edge varieties

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\) be an integer, let \(C_{2m}\) be the simple even cycle, and let \(X_m\) be the complex projective toric variety associated to its edge semigroup with its degree-one embedding. Label the alternating edge coordinates by \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\) and \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\). Then
\[
X_m=V\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}u_i-\prod_{j=1}^{m}v_j\right)
\subset\mathbb P^{2m-1}.
\]
For a point \(p\in X_m\), define the two zero counts
\[
a(p)=\#\{i:u_i(p)=0\},\qquad b(p)=\#\{j:v_j(p)=0\}.
\]
The **Zariski local Weil-divisor class group** is determined at every complex point by
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p})
\cong \mathbb Z^{\,(a(p)-1)(b(p)-1)}}
\]
whenever \(a(p),b(p)\ge1\); it is zero when \(a(p)=b(p)=0\). In particular, every singular point is non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial, while every smooth point is factorial.

For \(m\ge3\), the **reduced and scheme-theoretic** singular locus has exactly
\[
\boxed{\binom m2^2}
\]
irreducible components, each a linear \(\mathbb P^{2m-5}\), indexed by two zero coordinates in each alternating class. At a general point of each component, the singularity is transversely a three-dimensional ordinary double point with local class group \(\mathbb Z\). Its local class rank grows at higher intersections of components according to the formula above.

The exact maximum of the local class rank on the projective variety is
\[
\boxed{\max_{p\in X_m}\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p})
=(m-1)(m-2)\qquad(m\ge3).}
\]
It is attained at precisely the \(2m\) coordinate torus-fixed points. For comparison, the vertex of the affine cone has local class rank \((m-1)^2\) and does not belong to the projective variety. For \(m=2\), \(X_2\) is a smooth quadric surface and all local class groups vanish.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The cycle has \(2m\) vertices and \(2m\) edges with \(m\ge2\). Its edge algebra is the image of the standard-graded map sending each edge variable to the corresponding product of its two endpoint variables; each edge variable has degree one in the source and image grading. The projective edge variety is the Proj of that edge algebra.

The local group \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,p})\) is the ordinary Weil-divisor class group of the **Zariski local normal domain** at \(p\), not a class group of a chosen resolution, a global class group, or an independently asserted completed-local class group. All zero counts use homogeneous coordinates, so vanishing is well-defined.

## Proof
**1. Toric hypersurface and normality.** The unsigned incidence matrix of a connected bipartite graph with \(2m\) vertices has rank \(2m-1\). Therefore the integer kernel for the \(2m\) edges of the single even cycle has rank one and is generated by the primitive alternating signed cycle vector. The homogeneous toric ideal is generated by the binomial
\[
F_m=\prod_{i=1}^{m}u_i-\prod_{j=1}^{m}v_j.
\]
The binomial is irreducible: viewed as a degree-one polynomial in \(u_1\), its two coefficients in the remaining variables are coprime. The resulting hypersurface has dimension \(2m-2\) and degree \(m\). Adjunction gives \(\omega_{X_m}\cong\mathcal O_{X_m}(-m)\), so these are Gorenstein Fano varieties. They are complete intersections, hence satisfy Serre's \(S_2\) condition.

**2. Reduced singular scheme and transverse model.** The Jacobian ideal in the homogeneous coordinate ring is generated by
\[
\left\{\prod_{k\ne i}u_k:1\le i\le m\right\}
\cup
\left\{\prod_{\ell\ne j}v_\ell:1\le j\le m\right\}.
\]
The equation \(F_m\) itself belongs to this ideal. Each displayed monomial is squarefree, and the two variable sets are disjoint. The squarefree-monomial ideal is exactly
\[
\bigcap_{\substack{1\le i<i'\le m\\1\le j<j'\le m}}
(u_i,u_{i'},v_j,v_{j'}).
\]
Consequently the singular subscheme is reduced. For \(m=2\), its only affine zero is the excluded origin, so the projective quadric is smooth. For \(m\ge3\), each coordinate prime in the intersection defines a distinct linear \(\mathbb P^{2m-5}\), giving the claimed number of components and codimension \(3\) in \(X_m\). In either case the hypersurface is regular in codimension one; together with \(S_2\), Serre's criterion makes it normal.

If \(p\) has \(a\ge1\) vanishing \(u\)-coordinates and \(b\ge1\) vanishing \(v\)-coordinates, invert all the remaining nonzero coordinates in a projective affine chart. Multiplying one vanishing variable by an invertible regular function changes the local equation to
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i=\prod_{j=1}^{b}y_j,
\]
with \(2m-a-b-1\) independent smooth parameters. Thus the point is singular precisely when \(a,b\ge2\). At the generic point of a component, \(a=b=2\), and the normal equation is \(x_1x_2-y_1y_2=0\), the ordinary threefold node.

**3. Local divisor classes.** For \(a,b\ge1\), consider the normal local binomial domain with equation
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{a}x_i=\prod_{j=1}^{b}y_j
\]
at a point where all displayed coordinates vanish, possibly with additional regular parameters. Inverting the product of the \(x_i\) also inverts every \(y_j\), yielding a localization of a Laurent polynomial ring, hence a UFD. The height-one primes meeting this localization's complement are exactly
\[
P_{ij}=(x_i,y_j),\qquad 1\le i\le a,\quad 1\le j\le b.
\]
Indeed, \(x_i=0\) forces at least one \(y_j=0\), and the quotient by each \(P_{ij}\) is a polynomial-domain localization of codimension one.

The divisor-class localization exact sequence therefore presents the local class group by the \(ab\) classes \([P_{ij}]\), modulo divisors of the invertible coordinate monomials. The relations are precisely
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{b}[P_{ij}]=0\quad(1\le i\le a),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{a}[P_{ij}]=0\quad(1\le j\le b).
\]
There are no other relations: after inversion the units, modulo original local units, are generated by the Laurent coordinate monomials; the relation \(\prod x_i=\prod y_j\) accounts for the single dependency between row and column relations. The relations eliminate every class in the last row and last column, leaving the \((a-1)(b-1)\) classes \([P_{ij}]\) with \(i<a\) and \(j<b\) freely. Equivalently, the integral presentation is the row/column incidence matrix of the connected complete bipartite graph \(K_{a,b}\); a spanning-tree minor is \(\pm1\), so there is no torsion. This proves the asserted **Zariski local** class group. If no coordinates vanish, the open torus is smooth and the local class group is zero.

**4. Sharp projective maximum.** At a projective point, the pair \((a,b)=(m,m)\) is impossible because all homogeneous coordinates would vanish. Every remaining pair with \(a,b\ge1\) is realized, by choosing the nonvanishing coordinates arbitrarily, since each product in the equation then vanishes. The function \((a-1)(b-1)\) is nondecreasing in each argument. For \(m\ge3\) its largest permitted value is \((m-1)(m-2)\), obtained exactly for \((a,b)=(m,m-1)\) or \((m-1,m)\). These are the \(2m\) projective coordinate points, with one coordinate nonzero. In the affine cone the origin has \((a,b)=(m,m)\), giving the strictly larger rank \((m-1)^2\).

## Verification
The bundled standalone checker `artifacts/verify.py` directly computes Smith normal forms for the complete-bipartite row/column relation matrices for all \(1\le a,b\le11\). It checks every nonzero diagonal invariant factor equals one, and that the cokernel rank is \((a-1)(b-1)\). It also enumerates all zero-count patterns for \(2\le m\le10\), checks the number of singular components, verifies the unique maximal-rank zero patterns and \(2m\) fixed points, and compares the Jacobian monomial ideal to the claimed radical coordinate-prime intersection on every squarefree monomial for \(2\le m\le8\). It completes \(87{,}506\) exact regression checks.

These computations are finite regression checks only. The all-parameter statements follow from the exact incidence-kernel argument, radical monomial-ideal identity, divisor-class localization sequence and elementary maximization proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Reyes, Tatakis, and Thoma establish foundational minimal-generator results for graph toric ideals; Bermejo, García-Marco, and Reyes classify and algorithmically recognize complete-intersection graph toric ideals. In particular, the single alternating even-cycle binomial is familiar graph-toric structure rather than a new defining equation.

Hosono, Lian, Takagi, and Yau explicitly study the **same cubic hypersurface for the special case \(m=3\)** under the notation \(\mathcal M_{3,3}\). Their Proposition 4.4 gives its six singular coordinate points and nine singular coordinate lines, and their Section 5 constructs resolutions using the local model \(xyz=uv\). Therefore those facts in the cubic special case are **previously established**, not an originality claim here.

Higashitani and Matsushita establish torsionfreeness and rank formulas for the **global affine edge-ring divisor class group**, with graph-theoretic descriptions of its rank. Their result does not state the pointwise local class-group rank for every projective coordinate zero pattern or the sharp projective maximum. The calculation above deliberately distinguishes the global affine cone class group, the local groups on the projective variety, and the earlier singular-locus description.

## Limitations
The result treats the elementary even-cycle edge family, not arbitrary graphs with complete-intersection toric ideals. The proposed originality lies in the **uniform local divisor-class stratification and its sharp projective maximum**, not in the cycle binomial, normality, or the known \(m=3\) nine-line singular locus. It does not classify crepant resolutions, deformations, Chow groups or completed-local divisor classes. Standard general toric-class-group methods could yield equivalent computations in unindexed literature; this remains a residual originality risk, and no independent external validation has been performed.
