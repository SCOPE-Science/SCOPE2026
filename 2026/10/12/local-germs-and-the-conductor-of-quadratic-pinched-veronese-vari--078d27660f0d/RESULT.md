# Local germs and the conductor of quadratic pinched Veronese varieties

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(X_n\) be the complex projective image of the quadratic Veronese map of \(\mathbb P^n\) after omitting the single mixed coordinate \(x_0x_1\). Then its normalization is \(\nu:\mathbb P^n\to X_n\), and the reduced singular locus is one line \(D\cong\mathbb P^1\), the image of \(L=V(x_2,\ldots,x_n)\). The restriction \(L\to D\) is the branched double cover \([a:b]\mapsto[a^2:b^2]\), with branch images \(q_0,q_1\).

The scheme-theoretic conductor of \(\mathcal O_{X_n}\subset\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}\) is the reduced ideal of \(D\); its pullback to the normalization defines the reduced line \(L\). In fact there is an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{X_n}\longrightarrow\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_D(-1)\longrightarrow0.
\]

For every closed point \(q\in D\) and every \(s\ge0\), the local Hilbert--Samuel function is
\[
\ell\bigl(\mathcal O_{X_n,q}/\mathfrak m_q^{s+1}\bigr)
 =2\binom{n+s}{n}-(s+1),
\qquad
\sum_{s\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb C}(\mathfrak m_q^s/\mathfrak m_q^{s+1})T^s
 =\frac{2}{(1-T)^n}-\frac1{1-T}.
\]
Thus every point of \(D\) has Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity two, embedding dimension \(2n-1\), and depth two. Since \(n\ge3\), the non-Cohen--Macaulay locus of the projective variety is exactly \(D\).

At the two branch images the completed local ring has the following explicit presentation. Set \(r=n-1\), with indices \(1\le i,j\le r\):
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,q_0}\simeq
\frac{\mathbb C[[y,z_1,\ldots,z_r,w_1,\ldots,w_r]]}
{(\,z_iw_j-z_jw_i,\;w_iw_j-yz_iz_j\mid1\le i,j\le r\,)}.
\]
The same holds at \(q_1\). At every \(q\in D\setminus\{q_0,q_1\}\), the completed local ring is instead
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,q}\simeq
\frac{\mathbb C[[u,a_1,\ldots,a_r,b_1,\ldots,b_r]]}
{(\,a_ib_j\mid 1\le i,j\le r\,)}.
\]
These are, respectively, the higher-dimensional pinch-type germs and the germs of two smooth \(n\)-fold branches meeting along a common smooth curve. These assertions together constitute one classification of the full local geometry of the singular line.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with ordinary scheme-theoretic images, completed local rings taken at closed complex points, and \(n\ge3\). Coordinates of the map are every monomial \(x_ix_j\) with \(0\le i\le j\le n\), except \(x_0x_1\). Thus the target is \(\mathbb P^{\binom{n+2}{2}-2}\). The notation \(D\) denotes the reduced coordinate line with only the coordinates \(x_0^2,x_1^2\) nonzero. The two branch images are its two coordinate endpoints. The ordinary Hilbert--Samuel grading concerns powers of the *local* maximal ideal, not the homogeneous coordinate-ring grading.

The boundary \(n=2\) is included solely for historical comparison: the two ordinary Whitney pinch points and normal-crossing double line of the projected Veronese surface were explicitly described in Fedorchuk--Smyth, Lemma 3.5. This result neither claims that boundary case as new nor claims to classify arbitrary projections.

## Proof

**1. Normalization and affine charts.** The map is everywhere defined, since it retains all squares. Wherever \(x_i\ne0\) for some \(i\ge2\), its surviving coordinates \(x_ix_j/x_i^2\) recover every parameter \(x_j/x_i\), giving a polynomial inverse on the corresponding standard affine chart. Hence it is an isomorphism outside \(L\). On \(L\), the only nonzero coordinates are \(x_0^2,x_1^2\), and the restriction is the indicated degree-two map. Every fiber is finite, and the map is proper, hence finite. Since it is birational from a normal variety, it is the normalization of its image.

On the chart \(x_0^2\ne0\), put \(t=x_1/x_0\) and \(z_i=x_{i+1}/x_0\) for \(1\le i\le r\); let \(y=t^2\) and \(w_i=tz_i\). All products \(z_iz_j\) already lie in the algebra generated by the \(z_i\). Thus the chart ring and its normalization are exactly
\[
 A=\mathbb C[y,z_1,\ldots,z_r,w_1,\ldots,w_r]
   =\mathbb C[t^2,z_1,\ldots,z_r,tz_1,\ldots,tz_r]
 \ \subset\ B=\mathbb C[t,z_1,\ldots,z_r].
\]
Here and below the first display for \(A\) means the subalgebra of \(B\), not an independent polynomial algebra. Set \(S=\mathbb C[y,z_1,\ldots,z_r]\). Since \(t^2=y\), the two parity sectors split as
\[
B=S\oplus tS,\qquad A=S\oplus t(z_1,\ldots,z_r)S,
\qquad B/A\simeq t\mathbb C[y].
\]
The quotient is annihilated by \((z_1,\ldots,z_r,w_1,\ldots,w_r)\); consequently that ideal is the conductor in \(A\). The conductor in \(B\) is the ideal \((z_1,\ldots,z_r)\). The symmetric chart \(x_1^2\ne0\) has the same form. Since the complement of these two charts misses no point of \(D\), the global conductor schemes are reduced \(D\) and \(L\).

Restricting the normalization to \(L\) gives \(f:L\simeq\mathbb P^1\to D\simeq\mathbb P^1\), \([a:b]\mapsto[a^2:b^2]\). The exact local quotients above identify \(\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}/\mathcal O_{X_n}\) with \(f_*\mathcal O_L/\mathcal O_D\). The double-cover decomposition \(f_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\) yields the stated global short exact sequence. For example, this decomposition follows from rank two, \(h^0(f_*\mathcal O_L)=1\), and \(\chi(f_*\mathcal O_L)=1\), or directly from the two affine coordinates \(t\) and \(t^{-1}\).

**2. Exact equations at the branch points.** Form the polynomial ideal \(J\) generated by \(z_iw_j-z_jw_i\) and \(w_iw_j-yz_iz_j\). Both types vanish under \(w_i\mapsto tz_i\) and \(y\mapsto t^2\). Conversely, the quadratic relations reduce any monomial to one with at most one \(w\). If exactly one \(w_i\) remains, the relations \(z_iw_j=z_jw_i\) allow its marked index to be transferred among the nonzero \(z\)-indices. Hence every residue has a linear normal form consisting of monomials \(y^az^\beta\) and \(y^aw_iz^{\beta-e_i}\) with \(|\beta|\ge1\) and a uniquely chosen nonzero index \(i\). Their images \(t^{2a}z^\beta\) and \(t^{2a+1}z^\beta\) are distinct monomials in \(B\), so no further relations exist. Thus \(J\) is the complete kernel, proving the asserted equations. Completing at the origin of this chart gives the presentation at \(q_0\), and exchanging \(x_0,x_1\) gives the same at \(q_1\).

**3. Formal transverse form away from the branch images.** At a point \(q\in D\) with \(y(q)=c\ne0\), choose a square root \(s\in\mathbb C[[y-c]]\) of \(y\), possible because \(c\ne0\). Set \(a_i=w_i+s z_i\) and \(b_i=w_i-s z_i\). This is an invertible linear change in the formal variables \(z_i,w_i\). The equations above become precisely \(a_ib_j=0\) for every pair of indices. Thus the completed local ring is the asserted fiber product of two regular \(n\)-dimensional formal power-series rings over their common one-dimensional base. It is reduced, has two branches, and their intersection is the smooth parameter line.

**4. Singular support and local Hilbert--Samuel functions.** Away from \(D\), part 1 gives smooth affine coordinate neighborhoods. At each point of \(D\), either displayed completed local presentation has \(2r+1=2n-1\) independent tangent directions and dimension \(n\), so every point of \(D\) is singular.

At \(q_0\), the monomial normal forms of part 2 also compute the powers of the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(y,z_i,w_i)\). The *maximal* number of generators in a factorization of \(t^{2a}z^\beta\) or \(t^{2a+1}z^\beta\) (the latter requiring \(|\beta|\ge1\)) is \(a+|\beta|\). Any pair of \(w\)-factors can be replaced by \(yz_iz_j\), which increases the factor count; after replacing all such pairs the indicated expression attains the maximal count. In the odd sector one \(w\) remains. Hence the number of degree-\(s\) classes in \(\mathfrak m^s/\mathfrak m^{s+1}\) is the number of degree-\(s\) monomials in \(S\), plus the number of these containing at least one \(z\):
\[
 \binom{n+s-1}{n-1}+\left(\binom{n+s-1}{n-1}-1\right)
 =2\binom{n+s-1}{n-1}-1.
\]
The same holds at \(q_1\). At all other points of \(D\), the two-branch fiber-product presentation immediately yields the same graded dimensions: two free regular \(n\)-variable branches minus their common one-variable axis. Summing these dimensions proves the claimed full Hilbert--Samuel formula and multiplicity two.

Finally localize the normalization exact sequence at any closed \(q\in D\). The normalization is a finite semilocal regular algebra of depth \(n\) as a module over \(\mathcal O_{X_n,q}\), while its quotient is a rank-one module over the regular local curve \(\mathcal O_{D,q}\), of depth one. The depth lemma therefore yields \(\operatorname{depth}\mathcal O_{X_n,q}=2\) for \(n\ge3\). Off \(D\) the local ring is regular, completing the classification of the non-Cohen--Macaulay locus.

## Verification

A standalone exact-integer semigroup checker (`checker.py`) reproduces the first five local associated-graded dimensions for \(2\le n\le6\). It tests all defining quadratic-binomial identities and compares the graded normalization defects in low degrees. Recorded results are in `checker_output.txt`; execute with `python checker.py`. In the checker, \(n=2\) is a boundary sanity check and not included in the novelty claim. The finite tests do not prove the general statement; steps 1--4 above do.

## Relationship to prior work

Fedorchuk--Smyth, *Stability of genus five canonical curves* (arXiv:1302.4804, Lemma 3.5), explicitly identifies the projected quadratic Veronese **surface** as a quartic with two pinch points and a normal-crossing double line. That \(n=2\) geometry is prior work. Greco--Martino, *Cohen--Macaulay Property and Linearity of Pinched Veronese Rings* (arXiv:1709.10461), gives homogeneous ring Hilbert series and a broad Cohen--Macaulay criterion. Maddox--Pandey, *Homological Properties of Pinched Veronese Rings* (arXiv:2111.05810, Theorem 2.3, Corollary 2.19, and Lemma 3.1), provides the normalization, semigroup cokernel, and homogeneous depth classification that substantially overlaps the ingredients used here. In particular, neither normalization nor the homogeneous depth result is claimed new.

The additional pointwise statement assessed here, in dimension \(n\ge3\), is a complete pair of completed-local-ring descriptions, uniform local Hilbert--Samuel functions, a scheme-theoretic conductor description, and the precise projective non-Cohen--Macaulay locus. These local data are not stated by the cited sources and require additional chart and local-filtration arguments beyond their homogeneous formulas. Schenzel's 1978 article on Veronese projections is a historical literature risk because its full original text could not be inspected.

## Limitations

The classification is specific to a **single deleted mixed quadratic monomial**; it does not address all Veronese projections, positive characteristic, or higher-degree pinches. The global normalization and graded depth were already determined in broader prior literature, and the \(n=2\) singularity structure was already described. The result is not independently audited. The historical Schenzel source is a residual originality risk; the unavailable original must not be represented as checked or as definitely not covering the claim. The checker only tests finitely many parameters and coefficients, not an infinite proof.
