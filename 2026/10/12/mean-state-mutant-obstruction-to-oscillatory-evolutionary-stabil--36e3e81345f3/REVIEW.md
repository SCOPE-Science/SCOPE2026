# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For every real payoff matrix and nonconstant periodic simplex path, its constant period-mean mutant is distinct and has the same period mean. Direct substitution in Definition 1a, Eq. (6), of arXiv:2601.01996v1 gives exactly \(p^{\mathsf T}Ap\) on both sides for every \(0<\epsilon<1\). The distinct interpolants \(m_\eta=(1-\eta)r+\eta p\) approach the resident uniformly and have the same mean; Definition 1c, Eq. (9), likewise has equality for every \(0<\eta<1\). For an actual replicator cycle, integrating \(\frac{d}{dt}\log r_i\) over the period proves \((Ap)_i=p^{\mathsf T}Ap\) on its support; the constant mean-state competitor is therefore a valid replicator trajectory. These are general proofs, not conclusions inferred from finite simulations. All quantifiers, endpoint restrictions, and boundary support cases are covered.

## Originality — PASS, narrowly scoped

The direct primary source [1] contains the strict universal tests and a claimed example of their satisfaction, but does not establish their universal incompatibility with nonconstant periodic states. Its Section III equal-mean caveat is explicitly attached to uniqueness propositions, not to Definition 1a. The classical statement that a replicator periodic orbit averages to a stationary equilibrium [3] is fully acknowledged as old; that result alone does not make the comparison with the later 2026 strict OESS test. Earlier periodic ESS analogues [2,4] use discrete maps and heterogeneity-weighted or information-theoretic functionals, not this bilinear two-stage average. Published mathematical findings queried using the exact definitions, time averages and mean-state mutants contained no directly covering claim; nearest related replicator records concern other dynamical systems. Search incompleteness and subsequent revisions of [1] remain residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The universally quantified mutant-stability test introduced in [1] is intended to distinguish evolutionarily stable periodic behavior from mere dynamical stability. The theorem shows a **structural logical obstruction**: every nonconstant periodic candidate fails both reported strict tests, independently of payoff coefficients or local stability. It supplies an exact dynamically admissible mutant even under a natural narrowing to actual replicator trajectories. Therefore, a non-vacuous strict definition requires a substantive change in admissible mutants or fitness functional. The result is not a mere numerical counterexample or a claim that stable cycles cannot exist.
