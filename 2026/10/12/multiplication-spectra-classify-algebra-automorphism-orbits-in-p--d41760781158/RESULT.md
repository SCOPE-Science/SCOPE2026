# Multiplication spectra classify algebra-automorphism orbits in prime cyclic group algebras
## Finding
Let \(\ell\) be a prime and \(q\) a prime power with \(\ell\nmid q\), and let
\[
A=\mathbb F_q[C_\ell].
\]
For \(a\in A\), let \(M_a:A\to A\) be multiplication by \(a\). Then
\[
a\text{ and }b\text{ are in the same }\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q\text{-alg}}(A)\text{-orbit}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_{M_a}(T)=\chi_{M_b}(T).
\]
Thus the characteristic polynomial of the regular multiplication operator is a complete invariant for element orbits under the full base-field algebra automorphism group.

Write \(f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)\) and \(m=(\ell-1)/f\). When \(f>1\), set
\[
H_f(q)=\frac1f\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}q^{\gcd(f,i)}
       =\frac1f\sum_{d\mid f}\varphi(d)q^{f/d}.
\]
Then the exact number of element orbits is
\[
q\binom{H_f(q)+m-1}{m},
\]
and the exact number of unit orbits is
\[
(q-1)\binom{H_f(q)+m-2}{m}.
\]
When \(f=1\), these counts are instead
\[
\binom{q+\ell-1}{\ell}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\binom{q+\ell-2}{\ell},
\]
respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The group \(C_\ell\) is cyclic of prime order \(\ell\), the coefficient field is \(\mathbb F_q\), and \(\ell\nmid q\). Automorphisms are unital \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra automorphisms. The result concerns ordinary algebra automorphisms, not Hopf, bialgebra, group-induced, or twisted automorphisms.

The nonmodular hypothesis makes \(T^\ell-1\) separable. For \(f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(q)\), every nontrivial \(q\)-cyclotomic orbit on the \(\ell\)-th roots of unity has size \(f\), so the cyclotomic factor \(\Phi_\ell(T)\) has exactly \(m=(\ell-1)/f\) irreducible factors, all of degree \(f\).

## Proof
By the standard realization and Chinese remainder theorem,
\[
A\cong \mathbb F_q[T]/(T^\ell-1).
\]
If \(f=1\), then \(T^\ell-1\) splits completely and
\[
A\cong \mathbb F_q^\ell.
\]
Every \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra automorphism permutes the \(\ell\) primitive factors, so the automorphism group is \(S_\ell\). An element is an \(\ell\)-tuple \((a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)\), and
\[
\chi_{M_a}(T)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(T-a_j).
\]
Hence equality of characteristic polynomials is exactly equality of coordinate multisets, which is exactly equality of \(S_\ell\)-orbits. Multisets of size \(\ell\) drawn from \(q\) values give \(\binom{q+\ell-1}{\ell}\) element orbits; restricting to nonzero coordinates gives \(\binom{q+\ell-2}{\ell}\) unit orbits.

Assume now \(f>1\), and put \(K=\mathbb F_{q^f}\). The factorization just described yields
\[
A\cong \mathbb F_q\times K^m.
\]
The one-dimensional factor is the unique simple factor of \(\mathbb F_q\)-dimension one, while the other \(m\) factors are mutually isomorphic of dimension \(f\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q\text{-alg}}(A)
\cong \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb F_q)^m\rtimes S_m,
\]
where each Galois group is generated by \(q\)-Frobenius. Thus an element
\[
a=(c,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m)
\]
is classified up to automorphism by its scalar \(c\in\mathbb F_q\) together with the multiset of Frobenius orbits of the \(\alpha_j\)'s.

For \(\alpha\in K\), let \(p_\alpha(T)\) be its minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb F_q\), of degree \(d\mid f\). Multiplication by \(\alpha\) on \(K\) has characteristic polynomial
\[
p_\alpha(T)^{f/d},
\]
because \(K\) has dimension \(f/d\) over \(\mathbb F_q(\alpha)\). Consequently
\[
\chi_{M_a}(T)=(T-c)\prod_{j=1}^{m}p_{\alpha_j}(T)^{f/\deg p_{\alpha_j}}.
\]
For every irreducible factor \(p\) of degree greater than one, its exponent determines the number of coordinates having minimal polynomial \(p\). For a linear factor \(T-r\), its exponent has the form
\[
f n_r+\mathbf 1_{r=c},
\]
where \(n_r\) is the number of \(K\)-coordinates equal to \(r\). Since \(f>1\), reduction of the exponent modulo \(f\) uniquely identifies \(c\), and then every \(n_r\) is recovered. Hence \(\chi_{M_a}\) recovers exactly the scalar coordinate and the multiset of Frobenius orbits. This proves both directions of the classification criterion.

It remains to count those multisets. The cyclic Frobenius group of order \(f\) acts on \(K\), and the \(i\)-th power of Frobenius fixes exactly \(q^{\gcd(f,i)}\) elements. Burnside's lemma therefore gives
\[
H_f(q)=\frac1f\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}q^{\gcd(f,i)}
\]
Frobenius orbits in \(K\). Choosing \(c\) and a multiset of size \(m\) from these \(H_f(q)\) orbit types gives the element-orbit formula. For units, \(c\ne0\) and no \(K\)-coordinate is zero; deleting the unique zero Frobenius orbit gives the unit-orbit formula.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently constructs the relevant finite fields in representative nonsplit cases, generates the full wreath-product action, and checks that the factored multiplication characteristic polynomial is constant on each orbit and distinct between different orbits. It verifies \((\ell,q,f,m)=(3,2,2,1),(5,2,4,1),(7,2,3,2)\), including both \(m=1\) and \(m>1\), and separately checks the split case \((\ell,q)=(2,3)\). Its final output is `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite computations are stress tests, not the proof of the all-parameter statement. The general proof is the decomposition, wreath-action, minimal-polynomial exponent recovery, and Burnside count above.

## Relationship to prior work
Brochero Martínez develops the cyclic-group-algebra decomposition \(\mathbb F_qC_n\cong\mathbb F_q[T]/(T^n-1)\) and its Chinese-remainder/Wedderburn factors as the input to finite dihedral group algebras. This supplies the same structural starting point but does not classify ordinary algebra-automorphism orbits of elements by regular multiplication spectra.

Davydov studies twisted automorphisms of group algebras, a different notion tied to monoidal/Hopf structure rather than the ordinary \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra automorphism action used here. Makhijani, Sharma, and Srivastava use Wedderburn decompositions to determine unit-group structure in finite dihedral and quaternion group algebras; their object and invariant differ from the orbit problem here.

Targeted searches for cyclic group algebra automorphism orbits, multiplication characteristic polynomials, Frobenius-wreath descriptions, and semisimple group-algebra unit orbits did not locate a statement implying the classification criterion or the two orbit formulas. The conclusion is therefore limited to the inspected literature and searches, not a claim that no equivalent formulation can exist elsewhere.

## Limitations
The theorem does not cover the modular case \(\ell\mid q\), composite cyclic groups, or noncommutative group algebras. For composite cyclic groups, repeated field degrees and several distinguished factor types can make a characteristic polynomial lose information that is recoverable in the prime-order case, so no extension is asserted.

The literature comparison found standard decomposition results and nearby automorphism/unit-group work, but not an exhaustive bibliography of every invariant-theoretic treatment of finite étale algebras. That is the principal residual originality risk.
