# No normalized-Laplacian perfect state transfer on fully branched depth-two trees

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge 2\) and every integer tuple \((p_1,\ldots,p_m)\) with \(p_i\ge1\), the depth-two tree \(T(p_1,\ldots,p_m)\) defined below has **no perfect state transfer between distinct vertices** for the continuous-time quantum walk generated by its normalized Laplacian \(H=I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\). There are no exceptions within this family, and the parameter sizes and multiplicities are unrestricted.

A stronger obstruction is established: any two pendant leaves attached to the same depth-one vertex fail strong cospectrality already at eigenvalue \(1\) of \(H\), even though an automorphism exchanges them.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, unweighted, and have no isolated vertices. Construct the rooted tree from a root \(c\), distinct neighbors \(h_1,\ldots,h_m\), and disjoint sets \(L_1,\ldots,L_m\) of pendant leaves with \(|L_i|=p_i\); its only edges are \(ch_i\) and \(h_i\ell\) for \(\ell\in L_i\). The conditions \(m\ge2\) and every \(p_i\ge1\) imply that the tree has diameter exactly four. In particular no neighbor of the root is itself pendant.

Write \(A\) for the adjacency matrix, \(D\) for its positive diagonal degree matrix, \(S=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}\), and \(H=I-S\). Perfect state transfer from distinct vertices \(u,v\) at time \(t\in\mathbb R\) means \(|(\exp(-itH))_{vu}|=1\). For each distinct eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(H\), let \(E_\lambda\) be its orthogonal spectral projector. Perfect state transfer implies strong cospectrality: \(E_\lambda e_u=\pm E_\lambda e_v\) for each \(\lambda\), and therefore implies cospectrality: \((H^k)_{uu}=(H^k)_{vv}\) for every nonnegative integer \(k\).

## Proof
**1. Different bipartition classes cannot be cospectral.** The bipartition classes are \(\{c\}\cup\bigcup_i L_i\) and \(\{h_1,\ldots,h_m\}\). Because the tree is connected, the normalized Laplacian has a simple eigenvalue \(0\) with eigenvector proportional to \(D^{1/2}\mathbf1\). Its projector therefore satisfies \((E_0)_{xx}=\deg(x)/\sum_y\deg(y)\). Any cospectral pair must have equal degrees.

The degrees are \(\deg(c)=m\), \(\deg(h_i)=p_i+1\ge2\), and \(\deg(\ell)=1\) for \(\ell\in L_i\). Thus a pair in opposite bipartition classes with equal degrees must consist of \(c\) and some \(h_i\) for which \(p_i=m-1\). For this pair we compare the second diagonal moment of \(S\), which equals that of \(I-H\) and must agree for cospectral vertices:
\[
(S^2)_{cc}=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1{p_j+1}\le\frac12,
\qquad
(S^2)_{h_i h_i}=\frac{p_i+1/m}{p_i+1}
=1-\frac1m+\frac1{m^2}\ge\frac34.
\]
These inequalities are strict against one another for every \(m\ge2\). Hence there is no cospectral pair across the bipartition, and therefore no perfect state transfer across it.

**2. Same-class transfers reduce to sibling leaves.** The tree result of Coutinho and Baptista, Corollary 8 of *Quantum Walks in the Normalized Laplacian* (arXiv:2209.04668), states that if a tree on at least three vertices has normalized-Laplacian perfect state transfer between vertices in the same bipartition class, they must be distinct leaves with the same parent. In this graph such candidates, if any, lie together in one set \(L_i\).

**3. Sibling leaves cannot be strongly cospectral.** Let \(E_1\) project onto the eigenspace \(\ker S=\ker(H-I)\). Form the real vector \(z\) with coordinate \(1\) at \(c\), coordinate \(-1/p_i\) at each leaf in \(L_i\), and coordinate \(0\) at every \(h_i\). At each hub the sum of the neighboring \(z\)-coordinates is \(1+p_i(-1/p_i)=0\); it is zero at all other vertices as well. Consequently \(Az=0\), and the nonzero vector \(f=D^{1/2}z\) satisfies \(Sf=0\), so \(E_1f=f\).

Take distinct sibling leaves \(u,v\in L_i\). Since their only neighbor is the same \(h_i\), the vector \(g=e_u-e_v\) also satisfies \(Sg=0\), hence \(E_1g=g\). The scalar products are
\[
\langle f,e_u+e_v\rangle=-\frac2{p_i}\ne0,
\qquad
\langle g,e_u-e_v\rangle=2\ne0.
\]
As \(f,g\) lie in the image of \(E_1\), these imply \(E_1(e_u+e_v)\ne0\) and \(E_1(e_u-e_v)\ne0\). Therefore \(E_1e_u\) equals neither \(E_1e_v\) nor \(-E_1e_v\). The pair is not strongly cospectral and cannot exhibit perfect state transfer.

Combining the three steps rules out every pair of distinct vertices and proves the theorem for all admissible integer parameter tuples.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` constructs the trees and checks the rational identity \(Az=0\), the nonzero sibling-leaf overlaps, and the exact unequal second moments for each degree-compatible root–hub pair. It checks every tuple with \(2\le m\le5\) and \(1\le p_i\le3\). Its reproducible output is `VERIFY_OK profiles=360 total_vertices=5274 sibling_pairs=2184 exact_rational_witnesses`. The infinite theorem follows from the symbolic proof above; this finite check is not an exhaustive proof for unbounded parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Coutinho and Baptista (arXiv:2209.04668, Corollary 8) supply the same-bipartition restriction and investigate the wider problem of normalized-Laplacian state transfer on trees; their result does not assert this full heterogeneous depth-two family exclusion. Monterde (arXiv:2111.01265, Theorem 3.9 and Corollary 3.14) gives general spectral criteria for twin strong cospectrality; the proof here exhibits an explicit second vector at the obstructing eigenvalue and combines it with a separate cross-bipartition moment obstruction. Alvir et al. (arXiv:1409.5840) exclude normalized-Laplacian perfect transfer on paths with at least four vertices, thereby covering particular members of this family but not its unrestricted branched cases. Coutinho, Juliano and Spier (arXiv:2305.10199) exclude perfect transfer on trees for the **adjacency** Hamiltonian, not for the normalized Laplacian.

## Limitations
The result establishes nonexistence of **perfect** state transfer, not nonexistence of pretty good state transfer, fractional revival, or other transport phenomena. It does not address trees in which some root neighbors are already leaves, the general diameter-four class with this feature, or all trees. The cited same-class reduction is an external published theorem, not independently re-proved here. The zero-eigenspace construction and rational moment comparison are proved for the exact family stated. Unlocated equivalent general implications in the broader strong-cospectrality literature remain a residual originality risk; independent audit is not performed.
