# Correctness
PASS. For a tuple \(\bar g\) whose generated subgroup lies in a variety \(\mathcal V\), local finiteness of Hall's group makes \(\langle\bar g\rangle\) finite, so evaluation from the relatively free group has finite-index kernel. Equal kernels give a coordinate-preserving isomorphism of the finite generated subgroups. Hall's inner ultrahomogeneity realizes that isomorphism by conjugation. Conversely, conjugation and arbitrary automorphisms preserve all word relations, so they preserve the kernel. Hall universality realizes every finite quotient. This establishes the exact orbit-kernel bijection.

The abelian exponent-\(m\) specialization is immediate from the relatively free object \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^n\). The displayed formula for \(S_{a,n}(p)\) is Birkhoff's subgroup-type formula specialized to the rectangular ambient type \((a^n)\), with \(r_i\) the column lengths of the subgroup type. The asymptotic follows from the exact degree identity \(\sum_i r_i(n-r_i)\), the Gaussian-binomial bounds, and a single midpoint subgroup type giving the matching lower bound.

The self-review checked three delicate points: arbitrary normal subgroups of the relatively free group give quotients still lying in the variety; full automorphism orbits cannot be coarser than kernel equality because automorphisms preserve every word relation; and primary decomposition of subgroups of \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^n\) really factors the count over prime powers.

# Originality
PASS, with explicit residual folklore risk. Hall's universality/conjugacy property is classical, Rzepecki packages it as inner ultrahomogeneity, Birkhoff counts subgroup types of finite abelian groups, and Galois numbers count vector subspaces. Those ingredients are prior and excluded from novelty. Targeted published-finding corpus, own-ledger, and web searches did not locate the general finite-index-normal-subgroup classification of Hall-group tuple orbits, the exact commuting exponent-\(m\) conjugacy profile, or its all-\(m\) quadratic logarithmic growth statement.

The closest conceptual own-ledger item is the immediately preceding generic 2-nilpotent Lie finding, which also uses kernels of marked free objects, but it concerns a different Fraïssé class, an exterior-square relation layer, and ordinary automorphism orbits. The Hall-group result uses inner conjugacy, arbitrary group varieties, and finite-index normal subgroups of relatively free groups, so neither claim implies the other.

Because the orbit-kernel step is a short corollary of a strong classical homogeneity property, the possibility that experts regard it as folklore cannot be eliminated by database search. The exact specialization and quantitative profile are therefore stated narrowly and the risk is disclosed.
