# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The coordinate equations are exactly the closed-neighborhood toggling constraints. Subtraction within a part forces constant press indicators. Their reduction to \((1+\epsilon_i)y_i+t=1\) is valid for all positive part sizes, including singleton parts, and exhaustively determines every solution. The homogeneous equations independently give nullity \(\max\{o-1,0\}\). The optimizer count follows because selecting more than one positive odd-sized part strictly increases cardinality over the smallest singleton choice. An independent finite verifier exhausts all \(579194\) press subsets for \(363\) profiles and compares binary ranks; these tests supplement, but do not replace, the algebraic proof.

## Originality — PASS with remaining literature risk

The 2026 primary study proves a general extremality criterion and describes matchings parity, rather than classifying every all-on solution on a multipartite graph. Ballard--Budge--Stephenson (2019, Section 3) determine nullity and a basis on the complete **bipartite** subfamily, but do not give the general multipartite weight enumerator. Berman--Borer--Hungerbühler (2021) establish generic linear-algebraic solvability, without the explicit arbitrary-part profile. A related indexed finding on odd vertex coloring of multipartite graphs concerns a different quantifier and different coloring object, so does not imply a Lights Out press-vector census. Equivalence with an unindexed result in parity domination or odd neighborhood domination remains possible. The generic linear-algebra facts and the bipartite special case are credited, not claimed as new.

## Value — PASS

The all-on solution family is a natural central object of the 2026 extremality question. Rather than one extremal/not-extremal bit, this gives an exact size distribution for **every** all-on solution across a graph class with unbounded part number and arbitrary part sizes, the minimum-press optimization and all its optimizers, and the universal-solvability boundary. The structure changes discontinuously between zero and one odd-sized part, exposing why large even parts become completely irrelevant to optimal presses once any odd part appears. Computing minimum presses is difficult on general graphs, so a complete structural classification in this class is mathematically motivated, not an arbitrary finite enumeration.

## Scope and residual risks

The theorem does not address general graphs, nonbinary rules, or the full weight distribution for arbitrary initial states. Prior equivalent formulations outside accessible/indexed literature cannot be fully ruled out. General affine-kernel characterizations are much older than the result.
