# Open-packing profiles and perfect partitions of circular ladders

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let \(L_n=C_n\square K_2\), with its two cyclic rails indexed by \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). An **open packing** is a vertex subset \(S\) such that \(N(u)\cap N(v)=\varnothing\) whenever \(u,v\in S\) are distinct. Write
\[
P_n(x)=\sum_{S\text{ open packing in }L_n}x^{|S|}.
\]
Then the ordinary length-generating function of every coefficient of these polynomials is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n\ge3}P_n(x)t^n
=-t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log D(t,x)-t-t^2,}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{D(t,x)=
(1-t-2xt^2-x^2t^3)\big((1+xt^2)^2-x^4t^6\big).}
\]
The logarithm means its formal power series at \(t=0\), so no analytic convergence is needed. In particular the entire open-packing polynomial, not merely its highest degree, is determined exactly.

There is also a closed coefficient formula. For every integer \(N\ge3\), put
\[
I_N(x)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/3\rfloor}
\frac{N}{N-2k}\binom{N-2k}{k}x^k.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{P_n(x)=\begin{cases}
I_{2n}(x),&n\text{ odd},\\
I_n(x)^2,&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\]
The reason is a concrete structural isomorphism of the **two-step graph** (vertices joined if they share a neighbor): it is \(C_{2n}^2\) for odd \(n\), and a disjoint union of two copies of \(C_n^2\) for even \(n\). Here the superscript \(2\) denotes the ordinary graph square; it is not an exponent of a numerical invariant.

Let \(\rho_n=\deg P_n\), and let \(M_n=[x^{\rho_n}]P_n(x)\), the number of maximum open packings. The sharp six-class formulas, valid for all \(n\ge3\), are:

| \(n\bmod6\) | \(\rho_n\) | \(M_n\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(0\) | \(2n/3\) | \(9\) |
| \(1\) | \(2(n-1)/3\) | \(n(2n+1)/3\) |
| \(2\) | \(2(n-2)/3\) | \((n(n+1)/6)^2\) |
| \(3\) | \(2n/3\) | \(3\) |
| \(4\) | \(2(n-1)/3\) | \(n^2\) |
| \(5\) | \((2n-1)/3\) | \(2n\) |

The same classification determines **perfect partitions into maximum open packings**, defined as unordered partitions of the entire vertex set in which every block is an open packing of size \(\rho_n\). Such a partition exists **exactly when** \(3\mid n\) or \(n\in\{4,8\}\). Its number is
\[
\boxed{Q_n=\begin{cases}
1,&3\mid n\text{ and }n\text{ odd},\\
6,&3\mid n\text{ and }n\text{ even},\\
24,&n=4,\\
1176,&n=8,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple; the graph \(C_n\) is the ordinary cycle of length \(n\ge3\). Every vertex of \(L_n\) has three distinct neighbors. Open-neighborhood disjointness, rather than closed-neighborhood disjointness or edge-open packing, is the defining property. Vertices within a rail are labeled by their cyclic index, so \(P_n(x)\) counts subsets of the actual labeled vertex set, not isomorphism types. The two independent colors of a column below are its two rail occupancies; they are not proper coloring labels.

## Proof
Encode the subset at cyclic column \(i\) by a two-bit integer \(c_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), where bit \(1\) selects the vertex on rail one, bit \(2\) selects its mate on rail two, and \(w(c_i)\) is the number of set bits. Direct examination of the three neighbors of each vertex shows that two selected vertices share a neighbor **if and only if** either they lie on opposite rails in adjacent columns or they lie on one rail two cyclic steps apart. This remains true for the triangular and square boundaries \(n=3,4\), where some cyclic descriptions coincide.

For a direct, noncomputational derivation of the coefficient formula, identify the vertex set with \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Two different vertices have a common neighbor precisely when their difference belongs to \(\{\pm h,\pm2h\}\), for \(h=(1,1)\); this follows by listing all possible two-edge walks, including paths that reverse direction. The order of \(h\) is \(2n\) when \(n\) is odd and \(n\) when \(n\) is even. Thus the two-step graph consists of one or two cosets of the cyclic subgroup generated by \(h\), respectively, and each coset induces the square of its cyclic graph. Open packings become independent sets in these components.

For an independent set of size \(k\ge1\) in \(C_N^2\), cyclically consecutive selected positions must be separated by at least three steps. The \(k\) cyclic gaps each have size at least three and sum to \(N\). Choosing the distinguished first vertex and a gap composition, and correcting for the \(k\) possible choices of first selected vertex, gives
\[
\frac Nk\binom{N-2k-1}{k-1}
=\frac{N}{N-2k}\binom{N-2k}{k}.
\]
The empty set gives the constant term. This proves the explicit \(I_N\) formula and the parity factorization without transfer matrices. The transfer calculation below gives a separately checkable compact length-generating function.

Define the compatibility relation \(R(a,b,c)\) by forbidding each of the following simultaneous bit occupancies: bit \(1\) in \(a\) with bit \(2\) in \(b\); bit \(2\) in \(a\) with bit \(1\) in \(b\); bit \(1\) in both \(a,c\); or bit \(2\) in both \(a,c\). On the sixteen states \((a,b)\in\{0,1,2,3\}^2\), form the weighted transfer matrix
\[
T_{(a,b),(b',c)}(x)=
\begin{cases}x^{w(c)},&b'=b\text{ and }R(a,b,c),\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Every closed length-\(n\) walk in the state graph corresponds bijectively to the cyclic word \((c_0,\ldots,c_{n-1})\) of one open packing, with total weight \(x^{\sum_i w(c_i)}\). Hence
\[
P_n(x)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(T(x)^n\bigr)\qquad(n\ge3).
\]
An exact finite calculation gives
\[
\det(I-tT(x))=(1-t-2xt^2-x^2t^3)
\bigl((1+xt^2)^2-x^4t^6\bigr)=D(t,x).
\]
This identity can be verified without symbolic-algebra packages: the attached verifier constructs all sixteen states and the polynomial matrix over integers, computes traces \(\operatorname{tr}(T^j)\) for \(1\le j\le16\), and uses the Newton identities \(c_0=1\) and
\[
c_k=-\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{k-j}\operatorname{tr}(T^j)
\quad(1\le k\le16)
\]
to recover every coefficient \(c_k=[t^k]\det(I-tT)\). All divisions are exactly integral. This is a finite exact certificate of the displayed determinant, not an extrapolation from sampled sizes.

For a matrix over the formal polynomial ring, the trace/determinant identity is
\[
-t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\det(I-tT)
=\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(T^n)t^n.
\]
The same finite matrix calculation gives \(\operatorname{tr}(T)=\operatorname{tr}(T^2)=1\); removing these two terms proves the generating function for precisely the admissible cycle lengths.

For completeness, extract its leading coefficients using the following explicit expansion, in which \(H_n(x)\) denotes the contribution of the first factor of \(D\):
\[
H_n(x)=n\sum_{\substack{a,b,c\ge0\\a+2b+3c=n}}
\frac{(a+b+c-1)!\,2^b}{a!b!c!}\,x^{b+2c}.
\]
The remaining two factors add to \(P_n\) the following terms: for \(n=2h\ge2\), a term \(2n(-1)^h x^h/h\); and, for every \(j\ge1,k\ge0\) with \(6j+2k=n\), a term
\[
\frac{n(-1)^k}{j}\binom{2j+k-1}{k}x^{4j+k}.
\]
These are direct binomial expansions of \(-2\log(1+xt^2)\) and \(-\log(1-x^4t^6/(1+xt^2)^2)\); in particular they are identities of formal power series.

Since \(L_n\) is cubic, the three open neighborhoods centered at distinct packed vertices are disjoint sets of size three. Therefore \(3|S|\le2n\). Write \(n=3m\), \(3m+1\), or \(3m+2\). For \(n=3m\), the coefficient of the maximum possible power \(x^{2m}\) in \(H_n\) is \(3\), with a further contribution \(6\) exactly when \(m\) is even. For \(n=3m+1\), the coefficient of \(x^{2m}\) in \(H_n\) is \(n(2m+1)\), with an additional \(nm\) exactly when \(m\) is odd; at \(m=1\) this additional term comes from \(-2\log(1+xt^2)\), and for larger odd \(m\) it comes from the final logarithm.

For \(n=3m+2\), the coefficient of the largest allowed power \(x^{2m+1}\) in \(H_n\) is \(2n\). It remains \(2n\) when \(m\) is odd, but is canceled by \(-2n\) from the final logarithm when \(m\) is even. In this latter case \(m\ge2\), the coefficient one degree lower contributed by \(H_n\) equals
\[
\frac{n(m+1)(2m+1)(2m+3)}6,
\]
and the correction equals \(n m(m-1)(m+1)/12\) (for \(m=2\), from the middle logarithm). Their sum is \(n^2(m+1)^2/4=(n(n+1)/6)^2>0\). This proves the maximum degrees and leading coefficients listed in all six cases without a computational extrapolation.

Finally, consider partitions entirely into maximum open packings. If \(n\) is odd, the two-step graph is \(C_{2n}^2\). Exact partitioning into sets of size \(\lfloor2n/3\rfloor\) requires \(\lfloor2n/3\rfloor\) to divide \(2n\); for odd \(n\ge3\), this forces \(3\mid n\). When it holds, every maximum set has all consecutive cyclic gaps exactly three, so the three residue classes modulo three constitute the unique partition.

If \(n\) is even, the two-step graph has two copies of \(C_n^2\). The class size is \(2\lfloor n/3\rfloor\), so divisibility of \(2n\) forces \(3\mid n\), \(n=4\), or \(n=8\). In the first case each component has the unique residue-class partition into three blocks, and the possible bijections pairing the blocks from the two components give \(3!=6\) partitions. For \(n=4\), each component is \(K_4\); the singleton pairings give \(4!=24\) partitions. For \(n=8\), partitioning one component \(C_8^2\) into four independent pairs is choosing a perfect matching of its complement. That complement is the eight-vertex Möbius ladder (an eight-cycle plus four opposite chords). Its seven perfect matchings comprise two using no opposite chord, four using exactly two adjacent opposite chords, and one using all four. Matching the four pairs from the two components gives \(7^2\cdot4!=1176\) partitions. This proves the final counting classification.

Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.9 or later. The standalone checker uses only the standard library. It independently constructs the polynomial transfer matrix, proves the displayed determinant through sixteen exact Newton identities, compares all coefficients of \(P_n\) against a different algorithm testing actual open-neighborhood intersections on every vertex subset for \(3\le n\le8\), and compares all listed extremal formulas and cycle-square independent-set coefficients against trace polynomials for \(3\le n\le60\). It separately enumerates exact covers by maximum packings at lengths \(3\) through \(8\). The exhaustive vertex-subset comparisons process \(87{,}360\) subsets. ## Relationship to prior work
Brešar, Gastineau, Klavžar and Togni, *Exact distance graphs of product graphs* (arXiv:1810.10198, first posted 24 October 2018), Theorem 2.2, express exact-distance graphs of Cartesian products. For triangle-free prisms this supplies the general two-step adjacency decomposition used here, but it does not identify the one-or-two squared-cycle cosets, count independent subsets or classify perfect maximum-packing partitions. The present result does not claim novelty for that general product theorem. Brešar, Klavžar and Rall, *Packings in bipartite prisms and hypercubes* (arXiv:2309.04963, v1 of 10 September 2023), Theorem 2.2, give \(\rho^{o}(G\square K_2)=2\rho_2(G)\) when \(G\) is bipartite. Applied to even cycles, this determines **only the maximum cardinality** here, not all coefficients, the exact number of extremizers, or odd-cycle prism cases. Their transformation is not a cardinality-preserving bijection on all open packings. Brešar, Samadi and Yero, *Injective coloring of graphs revisited* (arXiv:2106.09823), explain the two-step-graph encoding of open packings; the present enumeration is an explicit independence polynomial of that graph, not a claim of novelty for the encoding. Samadi, Soltankhah and Yero, *Injective coloring of product graphs* (arXiv:2306.01488), investigate coloring thresholds for various products, especially direct products of cycles; they do not give this exact cycle-prism packing enumeration in the inspected relevant results. These are mathematical comparisons, not a claim that all literature has been exhausted.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the cycle prism \(C_n\square K_2\), not arbitrary prism bases. It does not determine the number of injective colorings, which would require partitions into open packings rather than counts of single open packings. The determinant is established by a fully specified exact finite calculation; the infinite-size implication then follows from the algebraic trace identity. Independent review has not been performed. Some related graph-product chromatic literature, particularly papers about injective coloring of Cartesian products of cycles, was not fully accessible and remains a residual originality risk.
