# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete distribution is exactly the indicated integer induced-edge histogram divided by its positive binomial normalization. Tree isomorphism is characterized by canonical center-root branch encodings, and leaf augmentation is a provably complete generation of unlabeled tree types. The included standard-library verifier executes exhaustive integer checks for every tree order through ten, every vertex subset and every one- or two-depth experiment needed for the theorem. All assertions passed. It is an exhaustive finite proof rather than a sampled numerical experiment; the theorem has no infinite-order quantifier.

## Originality — PASS

Tittmann, Averbouch and Makowsky (2008/2011) already establish that the **complete** subgraph-component polynomial identifies nonisomorphic trees through order nine and fails at order ten. The full-data ten-vertex obstruction is therefore explicitly prior, not a new finding. The present addition is the exact minimum number of **cardinality-specific** distributions needed to recover all tree shapes: one for orders four through eight, two for nine, and the complete list of all five optimal nine-taxon pairs. Liao and Hou (2013/2014) discuss recovery by the full polynomial, not this complete fixed-depth sampling design in the inspected text. The closest tree-subgraph asymptotic article was available only by abstract; an unindexed coefficient table remains a residual prior-coverage risk. A failed search alone is not asserted to establish novelty.

## Value — PASS

Sampling effort affects inference about ecological interaction structure. The present theorem supplies an exact lower bound on how many different sizes of a uniform taxon subsample must be used to reconstruct every tree-shaped interaction network from **complete** sampled-link-count distributions. This directly contrasts a topology-blind expected count with one-depth identifiability for eight taxa, a necessary second depth for nine, and a complete-information obstruction for ten. The counts and optimal depth pairs are a naturally delimited complete classification, not arbitrary selected cases.

## Closest sources and limitations

The foundation is the graph polynomial of Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky (arXiv:0812.4147) and its recovery theory, with Liao–Hou (arXiv:1311.6856) as the strongest related accessible follow-up. Martinez et al. (1999) motivate the biological importance of sampling effort but do not establish the present idealized inverse experiment. The theorem requires exact probability laws, known network size, unweighted undirected tree structure and error-free sampling; it does not establish finite-data statistical reliability. The older ten-vertex result is cited as prior.
