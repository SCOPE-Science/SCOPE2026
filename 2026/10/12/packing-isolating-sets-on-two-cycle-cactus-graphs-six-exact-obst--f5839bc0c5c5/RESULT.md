# Packing-isolating sets on two-cycle cactus graphs: six exact obstructions
## Finding
Let \(B_{a,b,d}\) be the simple connected graph obtained from two vertex-disjoint cycles of lengths \(a\) and \(b\) by joining one specified vertex of each cycle with a path of length \(d\), whose internal vertices are new. For \(d=0\), identify the specified vertices instead. The cycles otherwise share no vertices or edges. Assume throughout \(3\le a\le b\) and \(d\ge0\).

A vertex set \(S\) is **packing-isolating** if the closed neighborhoods \(N[v]\), for \(v\in S\), are pairwise disjoint and the induced graph on \(V(B_{a,b,d})\setminus N[S]\) has no edges, where \(N[S]=\bigcup_{v\in S}N[v]\). Then \(B_{a,b,d}\) has a packing-isolating set **if and only if**
\[
(a,b,d)\notin\{(5,5,0),(5,5,1),(3,5,2),(5,5,3),(5,5,4),(5,5,7)\}.
\]
The six exceptions include two cycles of length five connected by bridge lengths \(0,1,3,4,7\), and a triangle joined to a five-cycle by a bridge of length \(2\). In particular, existence is not monotone in the connecting-path length.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, loopless, and simple. The two cycles are chordless and there are no additional vertices or edges beyond the cycles and joining path. For positive \(d\), the path has exactly \(d\) edges, not \(d\) internal vertices. When \(d=0\), the cycles meet at a single cut vertex, so the graph still has exactly two cycle blocks. A packing means a 2-packing: selected vertices are at graph distance at least three, equivalently their closed neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint. An isolating set means deleting its closed neighborhood leaves no edge; it need not dominate every vertex.

The theorem addresses **existence**, not a minimum cardinality or number of packing-isolating sets. A graph of either permitted shape has \(a+b+d-1\) vertices and \(a+b+d\) edges, including the shared-vertex convention for \(d=0\).

## Proof
**Lemma (three-subdivision preservation).** Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph with packing-isolating set \(S\), and form \(G'\) by replacing an edge \(uv\) with the length-four path \(u-p-q-r-v\) (three new vertices). Then \(G'\) has a packing-isolating set.

**Proof of lemma.** If neither endpoint belongs to \(S\), take \(S'=S\cup\{q\}\). Every old selected vertex is at distance at least one from each of \(u,v\); therefore its distance from \(q\), which is two from both endpoints along the replaced path, is at least three. Removing an edge cannot shorten any old pairwise distance. The new edges \(up,pq,qr,rv\) each touch \(N[q]\), and every old edge still touches \(N[S]\) because old adjacencies to selected vertices (other than the removed edge, whose endpoints were not selected) remain. Thus \(S'\) is packing-isolating.

If \(u\in S\), then \(v\notin S\). Set \(S'=S\cup\{r\}\), where \(r\) is adjacent to \(v\) in the new path. The distance between \(u\) and \(r\) is three; for every other \(s\in S\), \(r\) cannot be within distance two of \(s\), since a path of at most two from \(r\) to an old vertex other than \(v\) would imply that \(s\) is adjacent to \(v\) in the old graph, contrary to the old packing condition \(\operatorname{dist}_G(u,s)\ge3\). Thus \(S'\) remains a packing. The vertex \(v\), formerly dominated by \(u\), is now dominated by \(r\); all other previously dominated old vertices remain dominated. Each new edge has an endpoint in \(N[u]\cup N[r]\). Hence isolation also holds. The case \(v\in S\) is symmetric: add \(p\). \(\square\)

**Finite base certificate.** Consider all unordered cycle-length pairs \(3\le a\le b\le8\) and bridge lengths \(0\le d\le10\). There are \(21\cdot11=231\) such parameter triples. The plain-text file `seed_table.csv` gives, for every triple, either an explicit set of vertex indices or the symbol `NO`. The graph-indexing convention and tests are implemented in `build_seeds.py` and **independently reconstructed** in `verify_seeds.py`. The latter verifies that, for each of the 225 `YES` rows, the displayed selected vertices have disjoint closed neighborhoods and cover at least one endpoint of every edge by their union of closed neighborhoods. For each of the six `NO` rows it loops over every vertex subset (not only over a sample or a bounded search depth) and checks the same two predicates; it finds no valid subset. The six negative triples are exactly those displayed in the theorem. Therefore this finite base table is a complete exact finite proof when the supplied code is replayed.

**Lifting from the base.** For arbitrary \(a,b\ge3\), repeatedly subtract three from a length greater than eight. The resulting ordered lengths lie in \(\{3,4,5,6,7,8\}\), and any length that was greater than eight lands in \(\{6,7,8\}\). For \(d\ge11\), repeatedly subtract three until the bridge length lies in \(\{8,9,10\}\); otherwise leave \(d\) unchanged. This gives a base triple with \(3\le a'\le b'\le8\) and \(0\le d'\le10\). If the original triple is not one of the six exceptions, neither is the base triple: an exceptional base has cycle lengths at most five, forcing the corresponding original cycle lengths to have been at most eight and unchanged, and its bridge length at most seven, forcing the original bridge length to have been at most ten and unchanged. The base triple consequently has an explicit packing-isolating set. Restore the original parameters by subdividing suitable edges in the two cycles and/or the connecting path in groups of three vertices. By the lemma, a packing-isolating set persists after every restoration. Each of the six listed triples is already in the finite base and has no such set. This completes the if-and-only-if proof. \(\square\)

## Verification
Run `python3 build_seeds.py` in a directory containing the code to recreate `seed_table.csv`. It enumerates all \(231\) triples and uses exhaustive edge-coverage branching under the pairwise-distance restriction. Run `python3 verify_seeds.py` in the same directory to validate the entire table; it uses a different edge constructor, checks every positive witness explicitly, and enumerates all \(2^{|V|}\) subsets of each declared negative graph. All code uses only the Python standard library; no external numerical package or network access is required. The recorded outputs are `build_seeds.out` and `verify_seeds.out`.

The checker reports `verified_cases 231 positive_witnesses 225 exhaustively_negative 6` and ends with `FINITE_BASE_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The six negative graphs have respectively \(22,31,26,66,97,304\) packing subsets, and zero packing-isolating subsets. These finite computations prove the enumerated base only; the lemma and lifting argument are needed for the unbounded cycle and path lengths. The checker does not furnish independent human mathematical validation.

## Relationship to prior work
Boyer, Goddard, and Henning introduced the explicit simultaneous packing-isolation question in *On the Relationships between Domination, Isolation, and Packing* (arXiv:2606.18172, first posted June 16, 2026). Section 3 defines these sets, notes that \(C_5\) itself has none, and proves that every tree has one (Theorem 2). Its inspected material does not classify graphs with two cycle blocks. The present result addresses a different, naturally next, cyclic graph family and determines exactly where the two cycle obstructions can interact.

Radhika and Vijayan consider **connected** vertex-edge dominating sets of various graphs including certain barbell graphs. Because connectedness of the selected set differs essentially from 2-packing, those connected domination counts do not yield this simultaneous packing-isolation classification. The source abstract was inspected; its complete text was not available during this comparison.

## Limitations
Only two-cycle cactus graphs with no attached trees or extra chords are covered; the theorem does not classify general cactus graphs, graphs with three or more cycles, or arbitrary unicyclic graphs with tree attachments. The computational certificate is for the stated finite base, and claims for arbitrarily large parameters rely on the separately proved subdivision lemma. A search of relevant published literature found no covering theorem, but unindexed equivalent work and inaccessible material remain possible. No independent external audit has been performed.
