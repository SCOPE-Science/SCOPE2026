# Scientific review: Poisson extremes of smoothed alternating streaks

## Correctness — PASS

The exact identity \(Y_{i+1}-Y_i=(X_{i+2}-X_i)/2\) makes each alternating window two disjoint monotone rank chains. The specified-orientation probability is the product of two inverse factorials. Overlapping windows at displacement below their length force one longer alternation; those at displacement equal to the window length or one more share at most two innovations and are controlled by an explicit conditional rank-density estimate. The dependence-neighborhood Chen--Stein inequality then establishes the displayed finite total-variation estimate, with all constants taken conservatively. Its error tends to zero for growing windows of bounded expected frequency under \(r=o(\sqrt n)\). The union/disjoint-block tail sandwich and dyadic Borel--Cantelli argument rigorously yield the almost-sure \(\log n/\log\log n\) scale. Exact-rational exhaustive checks of 1,641,300 rank permutations support the finite identities; they do not certify the limiting results. Principal risk: human verification of the rank-density reduction and Chen--Stein normalization is still required independently.

## Originality — PASS (qualified, historical priority not independently certified)

Bandt and Shiha (2007), Proposition 7, give all fixed sign-word probabilities of equal-weight moving averages via combinatorial rank counting. Their result covers the parity-factorial marginal used here: this marginal is **not** presented as novel. Section 6, including the full proof and examples, was read in author-provided public full text and does not supply growing-pattern Poisson error estimates, a longest alternating-window rare-event law, or the almost-sure scale. Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon (1989) establish a general Poisson approximation theorem but do not calculate the dependence terms for this model. Bender, Helton and Richmond (2003) treat iid nearly periodic permutation patterns rather than smoothed signals, and Houdré and Restrepo (2010) treat noncontiguous subsequences. Published result searches using moving-average sign words, alternating runs, Chen--Stein, and length scales disclosed no implication-equivalent specialized claim. The key original comparison concerns the dependence structure and implication of the full Poisson-extreme conclusion, not source titles. Risk: a prior work on dependent permutation descent words, renewal-cell oscillations, or general rare pattern counting could contain an equivalent corollary; searches cannot prove priority.

## Value — PASS

Long contiguous alternating segments quantify sustained high-frequency oscillation of a smoothed time series. The proof shows that equal-weight two-point smoothing changes their extreme scale to \(\log n/\log\log n\), far below the logarithmic scale suggested by independent-sign heuristics. The explicit Poisson approximation approximates exact finite-sample exceedance probabilities with an error controlled by two distinct kinds of window overlap, and the almost-sure law holds distribution-free without moment assumptions. This is a motivated structural fluctuation statement, not merely a re-evaluation of already known fixed sign-word probabilities. Limits: no unequal-weight or higher-order claim, no uniform centered extreme-value limit, and historical priority remains unverified independently.
