# Positive-characteristic nilpotency for finite-dimensional binary Zinbiel algebras
## Finding
Let \(A\) be an \(n\)-dimensional right binary Zinbiel algebra over a field \(K\) with \(\operatorname{char}K\notin\{2,3\}\). Then
\[
A^{n+1}=0,
\]
for the left-normed powers \(A^1=A\) and \(A^{r+1}=A^rA\). Consequently every product of at least \(3n+1\) elements of \(A\), with arbitrary parenthesization, is zero. In particular every finite-dimensional binary Zinbiel algebra over every field of characteristic \(0\) or at least \(5\) is nilpotent, with nilpotency index at most \(3n+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
A right Zinbiel algebra satisfies
\[
a(bc)=(ab)c+(ba)c.
\]
A right binary Zinbiel algebra is an algebra for which every subalgebra generated by at most two elements is right Zinbiel. The dimension \(n\) is finite. No algebraic-closedness hypothesis is used.

The characteristic restriction is exactly the one needed by the defect identities used below: the proof divides by \(2\) and \(3\). Nothing here claims the same conclusion in characteristics \(2\) or \(3\).

## Proof
Define the Zinbiel defect
\[
\phi(a,b,c)=a(bc)-(ab)c-(ba)c.
\]
Ismailov--Mashurov--Smadyarov proved that over characteristic different from \(2\), binary Zinbiel algebras are characterized by the two-variable identities whose linearizations make \(\phi\) alternating. Smadyarov's recent Lemma 2.1 then derives
\[
\phi(ab,c,d)=a\phi(b,c,d)
\]
from an identity whose remaining term is
\[
3\bigl(\phi(ab,c,d)-a\phi(b,c,d)\bigr),
\]
and derives
\[
\phi(\phi(a,b,c),d,e)=0
\]
from an identity whose remaining term is \(2\phi(a,b,\phi(c,d,e))\). Therefore the same defect identities hold over every field with characteristic different from \(2\) and \(3\). The rest of Smadyarov's spanning, solvability, and minimal-ideal arguments is integral once these identities are available. The one place where nilpotency of an ordinary finite-dimensional Zinbiel algebra is invoked is characteristic-free by Towers' theorem.

Thus every minimal nonzero two-sided ideal \(M\) of \(A\) satisfies
\[
AM=MA=0,\qquad \dim M=1.
\]
This gives a quantitative induction. If \(n=1\), then \(A^2=0\). For \(n>1\), apply induction to the \((n-1)\)-dimensional quotient \(A/M\):
\[
(A/M)^n=0.
\]
Hence \(A^n\subseteq M\), so
\[
A^{n+1}=A^nA\subseteq MA=0.
\]
Therefore the left-normed nilpotency index is at most \(n+1\).

It remains to convert this to arbitrary parenthesizations. Smadyarov's Lemma 2.2 rewrites every homogeneous product in the free binary Zinbiel algebra as a linear combination of left-normed products in symbols from
\[
Z=X\cup\{\phi(x_i,x_j,x_k):i<j<k\}.
\]
Each generator from \(X\) has original degree \(1\), while each basic defect has original degree \(3\). Hence a homogeneous product of original degree \(d\) rewritten with \(m\) such factors satisfies
\[
d\le 3m,
\]
so \(m\ge\lceil d/3\rceil\). If \(d\ge 3n+1\), then \(m\ge n+1\), and every resulting left-normed term vanishes because \(A^{n+1}=0\). Thus every product of at least \(3n+1\) elements is zero.

## Relationship to prior work
Smadyarov's 2026 preprint proves finite-dimensional binary Zinbiel nilpotency over characteristic zero and supplies the defect identities, spanning lemma, and minimal-ideal proof used here. Its proof displays the only scalar divisions in the defect step, by \(3\) and \(2\). Ismailov--Mashurov--Smadyarov's earlier defining-identity theorem already treats binary Zinbiel algebras over fields of characteristic different from \(2\). Towers proves that finite-dimensional ordinary Zinbiel algebras are nilpotent over arbitrary fields.

The combination yields two additions not stated in the inspected sources: extension of the finite-dimensional binary theorem to every characteristic at least \(5\), and the explicit dimension bound \(3n+1\) for arbitrary parenthesizations.

## Limitations
No assertion is made in characteristics \(2\) or \(3\). The bound \(3n+1\) is a uniform bound extracted from the normal-form argument; it is not claimed to be sharp. The search did not identify a published source stating this positive-characteristic extension or this quantitative bound, but very recent or unindexed work remains a residual originality risk.
