# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The characteristic extension follows by tracking the exact scalar divisions in the recent defect-identity proof: only \(2\) and \(3\) must be invertible. The subsequent solvability and minimal-ideal arguments use no further characteristic-zero step, while the ordinary Zinbiel nilpotency input is available over arbitrary fields. The quantitative bound follows by induction on dimension for left-normed powers and by the degree bookkeeping in the published normal-form lemma.

## Originality
PASS. Searches for binary Zinbiel nilpotency in positive characteristic, characteristic \(5\), the bound \(3n+1\), and the left-power bound \(A^{n+1}=0\) found no result implying the claim. The recent source states characteristic zero. The older defining-identity paper covers characteristic different from \(2\) but does not supply the finite-dimensional nilpotency theorem. Towers treats ordinary Zinbiel algebras, not binary Zinbiel algebras.

## Value
PASS. The result removes the characteristic-zero hypothesis for all primes at least \(5\) and gives an explicit dimension-dependent nilpotency bound. This is a natural structural strengthening of a theorem whose motivating question was whether finite-dimensional binary Zinbiel algebras are nilpotent.

Closest literature and limitations: the proof is built from Smadyarov's 2026 defect/minimal-ideal framework, the 2023 binary defining identities, and Towers' characteristic-free ordinary Zinbiel theorem. Characteristics \(2\) and \(3\) remain outside the argument, and \(3n+1\) is not asserted to be optimal.
