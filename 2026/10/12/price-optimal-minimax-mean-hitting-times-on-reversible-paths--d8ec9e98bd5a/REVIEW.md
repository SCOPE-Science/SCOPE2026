# Scientific review: Price-optimal minimax mean hitting times on reversible paths

## Correctness — PASS

The exact claim is restricted to finite, irreducible continuous-time birth-and-death generators with prescribed positive stationary law, independently positive edge prices and a unit conductance expenditure. Multiplying first-step Poisson equations by the stationary masses and telescoping yields \(H_{i,i+1}=P_i/w_i\) and \(H_{i+1,i}=(1-P_i)/w_i\). The strong Markov property then makes the maximum over all ordered starting-target pairs exactly the maximum of the two endpoint directional sums. This is crucial: a commute-time or effective-resistance identity on its own does not distinguish the directions.

For every dual multiplier \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), weighted Cauchy–Schwarz supplies the lower bound \(S(\lambda)^2\), with a unique equality allocation. Its derivative is the difference of endpoint hitting expectations at that allocation. Strict decrease of \(G\) for \(n\ge3\) gives a unique maximizing multiplier, with the correct one-sided signs at either endpoint. At that multiplier, the Cauchy–Schwarz equality vector makes the active hitting expectation attain the lower bound; equality conditions force uniqueness. Direct algebra verifies the stationary-mass cancellation and the two three-state breakpoints. This is an analytical infinite-family proof, not an extrapolation from finite checks.

The supplied checker independently solved 220 finite exact-rational hitting-time Poisson systems (5,940 ordered entries), checked 800 variable-price optimized designs and 8,000 alternative feasible designs, and ran eight three-state phase probes. Its finite and numerical tests are corroboration, not complete verification of universal quantifiers. Scientific limits: no discrete-time departure caps, no other graph classes, no hitting-time tails, no independent audit.

## Originality — PASS, with residual prior-art risk

The classical electrical-network and birth-and-death results of Palacios–Gómez–Del Río (2014), Palacios–Quiroz (2016), and Aldous–Fill provide fixed-generator formulas and variational relations, not the simultaneous arbitrary-stationary-mass and arbitrary-edge-price *minimax* design. In particular, the entire available 2016 accepted article develops a conversion algorithm for given birth-and-death transition probabilities, stationary distributions, and hitting times, and does not optimize a conductance budget. The 2014 full article treats exact voltage formulas, the linear-graph identity, electrical inequalities and fixed random walks.

The highly relevant Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi 2006 conference paper (later SIAM Review 2008) actually allocates conductance on a path and mentions hitting/commute times. Its formal objective is the **sum of effective resistances**, rather than the largest one-way first-passage expectation with a fixed externally prescribed stationary law; its Section VII lists minimizing *maximum effective resistance*, again a symmetric objective that cannot imply the directed hitting-time minimax solution. This is a substantive objective/parameter distinction, not simply a different paper title. The closest identified published research-record hit, `2026/9/7/SCOPE009`, maximizes endpoint hitting time over a finite quarter-grid class of discrete eight-state chains, unlike the arbitrary-size, continuous-conductance, fixed-mass *minimization* here. The another known mathematical benchmark is an optimal *spectral gap* for arbitrary-mass priced paths; it optimizes \(L^2(\pi)\) relaxation, not the maximal directed mean hitting time. A distinct optimization problem is minimax **effective resistance on stars**, a symmetric and different network/objective.

The 1996 Palacios–Tetali article specifically on birth-and-death hitting times was available through its abstract and later source citations only. Its complete text was not compared; an access attempt did not produce a permitted PDF. An older specialized allocation theorem outside the inspected sources cannot be ruled out. Searches failing to retrieve an identical formula are not by themselves evidence of priority.

## Value — PASS

Worst directed mean hitting time is a natural robust latency criterion for a chain tasked with reaching arbitrary ordered endpoints. Its exact optimum answers a network-budgeting decision for **all path sizes**, independent positive stationary profiles and independently priced edges. The formula provides the unique rates through one strictly monotone scalar root rather than a generic multivariable optimization. The mass-cancellation regime gives a structural invariance not apparent from the unoptimized hitting formulas. The sharp three-state price interval demonstrates that a conductor may retain an equal allocation across unequal edge prices and then move to a different regime; the thresholds are exact natural boundaries, not an arbitrary finite benchmark. This is valuable as a complete resource-allocation characterization, without claiming a downstream mixing-time theorem.

## Residual limitations

Priority should be rechecked against inaccessible historical treatments (most notably Palacios–Tetali 1996), and against equivalent formulations of optimal hitting-time rather than maximum resistance. The result does not claim originality for the Poisson flux identity, Cauchy–Schwarz, or convex design frameworks.
