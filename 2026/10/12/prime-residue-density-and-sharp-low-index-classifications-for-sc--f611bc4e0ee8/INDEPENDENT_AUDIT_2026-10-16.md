# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For m-rough u, factor L_m(u) locally: for m<q≤2m, q^(b-1)(q-m)≥(1-m/q)q^(b/2), and for q>2m, q^(b-1)(q-m)≥q^(b/2), yielding c_m√u uniformly. For p>m, u=p-m; if u has a prime q≤m then L_m^2(p^a)=0 and D_m(p^a)=p^(a-1)u<p^a. If u is m-rough and sufficiently large, uL_m(u)>mp, so two iterates already exceed p^a for every exponent a≥2, and u+L_m(u)>p for a=1. CRT removes exactly one reduced class modulo each q≤m not dividing m; PNT in progressions gives the density. For m=2 and m=4 the small-p classifications use explicit L_m(u) floors and handle 3,5 and 5,11 respectively. Independently evaluated 240 small prime-power cases; finite tests do not establish the universal or density results.

## originality

PASS

Defant (2015) Theorem 18 proves infinitely many abundant prime bases working for all exponents (and the two-iterate estimate), not the exact full-prime-residue density or eventual all-class criterion. OEIS A241667/A241668 give integer sequences of iterates and examples, not the density or simultaneous exponent classification. The explicit dichotomy and m=2/4 finite-exception classifications are new best-of-knowledge consequences using uniform m-rough growth, not simply a rewording of Defant’s single-progression infinitude.

PASS

Exact natural relative densities of abundance among prime bases and complete no-exception classifications of the first two nontrivial even Schemmel parameters answer intrinsic questions about iterated arithmetical functions. The uniformity in exponent and absence of perfect prime powers at m=2,4 are substantive structural refinements, not small table recomputation.
