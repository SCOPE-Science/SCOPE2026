# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The result specifies fixed even \(m\), prime bases \(p\), every exponent \(a\ge1\), and prime-relative density. The proof checks the nonstandard \(m\)-rough lower bound at each prime power, uses the exact first-two-iterate expression, and separates all bad prime-residue classes. Dirichlet equidistribution counts the favorable classes. Exact small-parameter arguments cover all prime bases, including exceptional primes. A finite exact-rational verifier passed its specified checks, but does not substitute for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest primary work is Defant, *On Arithmetic Functions Related to Iterates of the Schemmel Totient Functions* (2015), particularly Theorem 18. It proves infinitely many favorable prime bases, simultaneously for all exponents, using a selected congruence progression; it does not state the exact density or exclude all bad classes. OEIS A241667 and A241668 contain numerical values of the same functions but no universal classification. The first-two-iterate identity is credited to the earlier work. An unindexed or overlooked derivation remains possible, and the argument is a close development of the source.

**Value — PASS.** The exact prime-residue law classifies a natural infinite distribution rather than one favored progression, and the \(m=2,4\) cases are unconditional complete prime-power classifications. It provides an exact zero-perfect-prime-power conclusion in those cases; it does not resolve general perfect iterate sums.

**Limitations.** Relative prime density is not integer density. The uniform threshold for general \(m\) is not claimed optimal; exceptional base primes for \(m\ge6\) may depend on exponent. Exact computations are finite diagnostics, not infinite certification.
