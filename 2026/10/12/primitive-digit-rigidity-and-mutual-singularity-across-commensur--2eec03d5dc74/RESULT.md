# Primitive-digit rigidity and mutual singularity across commensurable dice bases

## Finding

**Theorem (complete positive-weight classification for commensurable bases).** Fix an integer \(b\ge2\) and coprime positive integers \(m,n\) with \(m\ne n\). Put \(q=b^m\) and \(r=b^n\). Choose strictly positive probability vectors \(p=(p_0,\ldots,p_{q-1})\) and \(t=(t_0,\ldots,t_{r-1})\). Let \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) be the laws of the infinite base-\(q\) and base-\(r\) expansions generated by independent, identically distributed digits with respective distributions \(p\) and \(t\).

Exactly one of the following happens:

1. **Equality:** \(\mu=\nu\). This occurs if and only if there exists a **unique** strictly positive probability vector \(w=(w_0,\ldots,w_{b-1})\) such that, for every base-\(b\) word \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) and every base-\(b\) word \((c_1,\ldots,c_n)\), with leading zeros allowed,
   \[
   p_{\sum_{i=1}^m a_i b^{m-i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{a_i},\qquad
   t_{\sum_{j=1}^n c_j b^{n-j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{c_j}.
   \]
2. **Mutual singularity:** \(\mu\perp\nu\). Both measures nevertheless have topological support exactly \([0,1]\).

Every pair of distinct multiplicatively dependent integer bases admits a representation of this form (with a suitable \(b\) and coprime \(m,n\)); hence the theorem covers all such pairs with strictly positive digit weights.

The compatible pairs are parametrized by the \((b-1)\)-dimensional open probability simplex, rather than by arbitrary choices of the \(q\)- and \(r\)-digit laws.

## Assumptions and scope

An *iid base-\(q\) digit law* is the push-forward of a product probability measure on \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^{\mathbb N}\) under \((d_i)_{i\ge1}\mapsto\sum_{i\ge1}d_iq^{-i}\). The same convention applies to base \(r\). All one-digit probabilities are strictly positive; since \(q,r\ge2\), each is also strictly less than one. The only digit-expansion ambiguities occur at a countable set of rational endpoints, and this set has probability zero under either law. No independence between the two *draws* is assumed or needed: the theorem compares their marginal laws.

For a concrete equality example, set \(b=2\), \(m=2\), \(n=3\), and \(w=(2/5,3/5)\). Then
\[
p=(4,6,6,9)/25,\qquad t=(8,12,12,18,12,18,18,27)/125
\]
produce precisely the same, nonuniform, continuous probability measure in bases four and eight. If \(t\) is instead uniform on its eight digits while \(p\) stays as above, the two measures are mutually singular but each has support \([0,1]\).

## Proof

**1. Common-base reduction.** Write \(L=mn\) and \(Q=b^L=q^n=r^m\). Each base-\(Q\) digit corresponds to a word of \(L\) base-\(b\) digits. Regrouping \(n\) successive base-\(q\) digits gives the \(Q\)-digit probability
\[
P(a_1\cdots a_L)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{jm+i}b^{m-i}}.
\]
Regrouping \(m\) successive base-\(r\) digits similarly gives
\[
T(a_1\cdots a_L)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}t_{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{jn+i}b^{n-i}}.
\]
The two original real-valued distributions are therefore iid base-\(Q\) digit laws with one-digit vectors \(P\) and \(T\), respectively. Their expansions are almost surely unambiguous: for any fixed infinite digit sequence, its probability is bounded by a power of the largest one-digit probability, which tends to zero. A countable union deals with the ambiguous endpoints.

If \(P\ne T\), choose a \(Q\)-symbol \(s\) with \(P(s)\ne T(s)\). The strong law of large numbers asserts that the empirical frequency of \(s\) in successive \(Q\)-digits converges almost surely to \(P(s)\) under \(\mu\), and to \(T(s)\) under \(\nu\). These are disjoint measurable full-measure events (using the canonical expansion away from the null endpoint set). Consequently \(\mu\perp\nu\). If \(P=T\), every finite cylinder in base \(Q\) has the same mass under both measures, and the measures coincide.

**2. Coprime-block rigidity.** Assume \(P=T\), and regard the common law of one \(Q\)-symbol as the law of a vector \((Z_1,\ldots,Z_L)\) of base-\(b\) digits. Its consecutive length-\(m\) blocks are independent, each with distribution \(p\), and its consecutive length-\(n\) blocks are independent, each with distribution \(t\).

Fix any \(h\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\). Since \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), one of the \(n\)-block boundaries \(n,2n,\ldots,(m-1)n\) falls \(h\) positions after an \(m\)-block boundary. The coordinates to its left and right belong to independent length-\(n\) blocks. Hence the first \(h\) coordinates and the remaining \(m-h\) coordinates of that length-\(m\) block are independent. Every length-\(m\) block has the same distribution \(p\); therefore \(p\) factors across every proper initial/final split. By repeated splitting, its \(m\) individual base-\(b\) coordinates are jointly independent. (For \(m=1\), this is immediate.) Interchanging \(m\) and \(n\) similarly shows that \(t\) factors across its \(n\) individual coordinates.

Thus all \(L\) coordinates of \((Z_1,\ldots,Z_L)\) are independent. The law of coordinate \(Z_j\) depends only on its location modulo \(m\), by repetition of \(p\), and also only on its location modulo \(n\), by repetition of \(t\). As \(m,n\) are coprime, the Chinese remainder theorem supplies a coordinate realizing every pair of residues; hence all those one-coordinate laws are equal to a single vector \(w\). Consequently \(P=T=w^{\otimes L}\), \(p=w^{\otimes m}\), and \(t=w^{\otimes n}\), giving the displayed formulas. Strict positivity and normalization of \(w\) follow from those of \(p,t\). Uniqueness follows because \(w\) is the first primitive-digit marginal.

**3. Sufficiency and support.** Conversely, if \(p=w^{\otimes m}\) and \(t=w^{\otimes n}\), both infinite expansions arise by grouping the same iid base-\(b\) digits; thus \(\mu=\nu\). For either strictly positive digit law, every nonempty relatively open interval in \([0,1]\) contains a sufficiently small digit cylinder having strictly positive probability. Its support is therefore all of \([0,1]\).

**4. General multiplicative dependence.** If positive integers \(q,r>1\) obey \(q^a=r^c\) for some positive integers \(a,c\), the exponent vectors in their prime factorizations are proportional. Dividing their common vector of prime exponents by its maximal common divisor gives an integer primitive base \(b\) and positive exponents \(m,n\) with \(q=b^m\), \(r=b^n\), and \(\gcd(m,n)=1\). Distinct bases imply \(m\ne n\). Thus the theorem applies. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify_cross_base.py` performs exact rational checks of the compatibility identity \(P=T\) for primitive bases and coprime exponent pairs \((b,m,n)=(2,1,3),(2,2,3),(2,3,4),(3,2,3),(4,2,3)\). It checks that an incompatible positive law differs on the all-zero common-base cylinder. For \((b,m,n)=(2,2,3)\), it also computes the exact rank of all 64 linear relations among the logarithms of four- and eight-symbol weights: the rank is 10 in 12 variables, leaving precisely the two primitive log-weight degrees of freedom before normalization.

Run `python3 verify_cross_base.py` with the standard Python library. The script checks finite algebraic cases only; the result for every allowed integer base and every positive probability vector is proved analytically above, not by enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Pfeffer, Smith, and Severa, *Induced Distributions from Generalized Unfair Dice* (first posted 2023-09-14), establish mutual singularity for **two differently weighted dice with the same number of sides** in Theorem 3.3. They explicitly pose comparisons for **different numbers of sides** in Question 5.1, noting bases two and four among the non-coprime cases. Their same-base argument yields the present dichotomy *after* passing to the common base, but it does not characterize exactly when the induced common-base weights agree. Coprime-block rigidity supplies this missing equality classification.

Cornean, Herbst, Møller, Støttrup, and Sørensen study stationary digit processes and singular distributions for a fixed base in *Characterization of random variables with stationary digits* (2020) and *Singular distribution functions for random variables with stationary digits* (2022). Their fixed-base stationary/Markov and decomposition results do not give the simultaneous independent-block compatibility formula for two distinct commensurable bases.

## Limitations

The theorem requires strict positivity of every digit probability and commensurability of the two integer bases. It does **not** resolve comparisons between multiplicatively independent bases, nor does it classify pairs with zeros in their digit vectors. It asserts no quantitative distance or rate of statistical distinguishability. Mutual singularity is not disjointness of topological supports: the latter coincide here. While the source comparison identified the explicit open different-base setting, undiscovered equivalent statements under product-process or symbolic-dynamics terminology remain possible; independent priority validation has not been performed.
