# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source proves that the splitting-field witness has \\(D[x]/J\cong V\oplus V\\) and that the \\(D[x]\\)-action surjects onto the split central simple algebra \\(A\cong\operatorname{End}_K(V)\\). Hence \\(\operatorname{End}_{D[x]}(V)=K\\). Every nonzero proper submodule of the length-two semisimple module \\(V\oplus V\\) is a copy of \\(V\\), and its two coordinate maps are scalar endomorphisms, giving a unique point of \\(\mathbf P^1(K)\\). Pullback through the quotient gives exactly all overideals. Distinct projective lines have zero intersection and full sum. The infinitude of \\(K\\) follows because a centrally finite noncommutative division ring cannot have finite center.

## Originality
PASS. The 2026 source explicitly records composition length two but does not state the full submodule/overideal interval or a projective-line parametrization. Its full text was searched for the natural aliases. The 2024 precursor and the 2025/2026 cyclic-algebra paper establish nonmaximality by different explicit criteria and do not give this interval. Semantic literature-index searches for projective-line, length-two, intersection-of-maximals, and equivalent contraction formulations returned no matching published finding. The closest 2025 Chapman--Paran article was not available for full-text inspection; its abstract and the exact statement of its Proposition 2.3 reproduced in later primary literature were inspected, leaving a bounded residual risk that an uninspected remark contains the same interval observation.

## Value
PASS. The result sharpens the qualitative failure “the contraction is not maximal” to a complete lattice description. It shows uniformly across every excluded centrally finite division ring that the standard witness is a minimal length-two failure with infinitely many maximal overideals arranged as a projective line, and that any two already recover the contraction by intersection. This directly quantifies the gap between maximality failure and the older semi-maximality phenomenon and supplies a reusable structural invariant of the witness.

## Closest literature and limitations
The direct source is Paran, arXiv:2610.04317v1, especially Lemmas 3.1–3.2, Proposition 3.3, and the proof of Theorem 1.1. The older explicit precursor is arXiv:2412.06230v1, and the cyclic-algebra specialization is arXiv:2508.07451v2. The theorem concerns only the splitting-field witnesses from the centrally finite classification and does not classify arbitrary contractions or centrally infinite rings. Full text of Chapman--Paran (Journal of Algebra 679 (2025), 86–95) was not available for inspection.
