# Projective transport identity resolves equivalence of point-process distances
## Finding
Let \(E\) be a Polish space with Borel \(\sigma\)-field \(\mathcal B(E)\). Let \(\xi,\zeta\) be point processes with finite intensity measures, so that
\[
\mathbb E\xi(E)+\mathbb E\zeta(E)<\infty.
\]
Denote their probability laws on the space \(\mathcal N_f(E)\) of finite counting measures by \(P,Q\). For any two finite counting measures, set
\[
D(\mu,\nu)=|\mu-\nu|(E),\qquad
K(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{B\in\mathcal B(E)}|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(D\) is the **full variation norm** of the signed measure, not the set-supremum metric \(K\).

For pairwise disjoint Borel sets \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\), let \(T_{\boldsymbol A}\mu=(\mu(A_1),\ldots,\mu(A_k))\). Using Wasserstein distance for the \(\ell^1\) cost on integer vectors, define
\[
d_\pi(P,Q)=\sup_{k\ge1}\;\sup_{A_1,\ldots,A_k\,\mathrm{disjoint}}W_{1,\ell^1}((T_{\boldsymbol A})_\#P,(T_{\boldsymbol A})_\#Q).
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(\Pi(P,Q)\) denote all joint laws with marginals \(P,Q\). Define
\[
d_{KR}(P,Q)=\inf_{\Gamma\in\Pi(P,Q)}\int K(\mu,\nu)\,d\Gamma.
\tag{3}
\]
Then the projective distance has an **exact optimal-coupling representation**:
\[
\boxed{d_\pi(P,Q)=\min_{\Gamma\in\Pi(P,Q)}\int D(\mu,\nu)\,d\Gamma.}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{d_{KR}(P,Q)\le d_\pi(P,Q)\le2d_{KR}(P,Q),}
\tag{5}
\]
with both comparison constants optimal. This supplies the previously missing inequality between the two metrics identified as an open problem by Pianoforte and Turin.

For arbitrary finite intensity measures \(\lambda,\eta\), Poisson processes have the exact distance
\[
\boxed{d_\pi(\Pi_\lambda,\Pi_\eta)=|\lambda-\eta|(E).}
\tag{6}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Every Polish space is allowed; in particular, the locally compact second-countable Hausdorff spaces considered in the comparison in the motivating paper are covered. No compactness of \(E\), absence of atoms, simplicity of the processes, or coupling given in advance is required. The only moment hypothesis is finite expected total point count of each process. These hypotheses guarantee \(P,Q\) are supported on finite configurations and that all displayed transport costs are integrable.

The statement does not claim (4) for arbitrary measurable spaces lacking a suitable standard Borel/Polish structure, nor for processes of infinite total intensity.

## Proof
**Step 1: A separating sequence of continuity partitions.** Let
\[
\rho(B)=\mathbb E\xi(B)+\mathbb E\zeta(B)
\]
be the sum of the two finite intensity measures. Fix a complete separable metric generating the topology of \(E\). There is a countable basis \(B_1,B_2,\ldots\) of open metric balls with
\[
\rho(\partial B_j)=0\qquad(j\ge1).
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, use a countable dense family of centers and at each center a dense countable family of positive radii avoiding the at-most-countable set of radii for which the corresponding sphere has positive \(\rho\)-mass. Let \(\mathcal P_n\) be the finite partition of \(E\) into the nonempty atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\). Every partition-cell boundary is contained in a finite union of the null boundaries in (7). These partitions refine one another and separate any two distinct points eventually.

For \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal N_f(E)\), define
\[
c_n(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal P_n}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|.
\tag{8}
\]
Clearly \(0\le c_n\le c_{n+1}\le D\). Since the union of the supports of any two finite configurations is finite, all of its distinct points are separated by some \(\mathcal P_n\). Thus for each pair,
\[
c_n(\mu,\nu)\uparrow D(\mu,\nu).
\tag{9}
\]
The argument also handles repeated point masses.

**Step 2: Compactness and semicontinuity of coupling costs.** Equip \(\mathcal N_f(E)\) with the Polish topology of the disjoint union of the symmetric powers \(E^m/\mathfrak S_m\), for \(m=0,1,\ldots\), and its counting \(\sigma\)-field. The family \(\Pi(P,Q)\) is weakly compact: it is tight because the two marginals are fixed, and weakly closed by continuity of coordinate projections.

For each \(n\), let
\[
F_n(\Gamma)=\int c_n(\mu,\nu)\,d\Gamma(\mu,\nu).
\tag{10}
\]
This is lower semicontinuous on \(\Pi(P,Q)\). To prove this, suppose \(\Gamma_j\Rightarrow\Gamma\). By Skorokhod representation on the Polish product, realize configurations converging almost surely under these joint laws. A finite configuration converges in the symmetric-power topology by relabeling its finitely many atoms. Counts in each cell \(A\in\mathcal P_n\) therefore converge whenever the limiting configuration charges no boundary \(\partial A\). This holds almost surely under *all* couplings with the fixed marginals because
\[
P\{\mu(\partial A)>0\}+Q\{\nu(\partial A)>0\}\le\rho(\partial A)=0.
\]
Consequently \(c_n\) converges almost surely along the representation; Fatou gives
\[
F_n(\Gamma)\le\liminf_j F_n(\Gamma_j).
\tag{11}
\]
Also
\[
0\le F_n(\Gamma)\le\mathbb E\xi(E)+\mathbb E\zeta(E)<\infty,
\]
so \(m_n=\min_{\Gamma\in\Pi(P,Q)}F_n(\Gamma)\) exists.

**Step 3: The finite-dimensional minimum is exactly a projected Wasserstein distance.** Push each coupling through the count map for \(\mathcal P_n\). The resulting cost is the \(\ell^1\) transportation cost of the two finite-dimensional count laws, so
\[
m_n\ge W_{1,\ell^1}((T_{\mathcal P_n})_\#P,(T_{\mathcal P_n})_\#Q).
\]
Conversely, since finite counting configurations are standard Borel, regular conditional laws of configurations given their partition-count vectors exist. Every coupling of the two count vectors can be lifted using those conditional laws, preserving its cost. Hence equality holds:
\[
m_n=W_{1,\ell^1}((T_{\mathcal P_n})_\#P,(T_{\mathcal P_n})_\#Q).
\tag{12}
\]

**Step 4: Exchange increasing finite minima with the infinite limit.** Since \(F_n\) increases pointwise, \(m_n\) increases. Let
\[
M=\sup_nm_n<\infty.
\]
The sublevel sets
\[
C_n=\{\Gamma\in\Pi(P,Q): F_n(\Gamma)\le M\}
\]
are nonempty compact sets by Steps 2--3 and are nested. Compactness gives a common \(\Gamma_*\in\bigcap_nC_n\). By (9) and monotone convergence,
\[
\int D\,d\Gamma_*=\sup_nF_n(\Gamma_*)\le M.
\]
For every coupling \(\Gamma\), however,
\[
\int D\,d\Gamma\ge F_n(\Gamma)\ge m_n
\]
for all \(n\), hence \(\int D\,d\Gamma\ge M\). Therefore
\[
\min_{\Gamma\in\Pi(P,Q)}\int D\,d\Gamma
=\sup_nm_n.
\tag{13}
\]
This reasoning **does not** assume that a sequence of independently chosen optimal finite-dimensional couplings is consistent.

**Step 5: Identify the entire partition metric.** For each disjoint cell family, \(\sum_i|\mu(A_i)-\nu(A_i)|\le D(\mu,\nu)\) pointwise. Taking the best coupling and then the supremum over families shows
\[
d_\pi(P,Q)\le\inf_\Gamma\int D\,d\Gamma.
\]
The particular refining partitions \(\mathcal P_n\) are among the permitted families, so by (12)--(13)
\[
d_\pi(P,Q)\ge\sup_nm_n=\min_\Gamma\int D\,d\Gamma.
\]
This proves (4).

**Step 6: Compare the two total-variation conventions.** Write the Hahn--Jordan decomposition of \(\mu-\nu\) as positive mass \(a\ge0\) and negative mass \(b\ge0\). Then
\[
D(\mu,\nu)=a+b,
\qquad K(\mu,\nu)=\max\{a,b\},
\]
and hence
\[
K\le D\le2K.
\tag{14}
\]
Take infima over couplings and apply (4) to obtain (5). If \(\mu=0\) and \(\nu=\delta_x\), both costs equal one, establishing sharpness of the lower coefficient. If \(\mu=\delta_x\) and \(\nu=\delta_y\) with \(x\ne y\), then \(D=2\) and \(K=1\), establishing sharpness of the upper coefficient. These are deterministic point processes.

**Step 7: Poisson processes.** For finite measures \(\lambda,\eta\), decompose them into their common part \(\lambda\wedge\eta\) and residual measures \((\lambda-\eta)^+\) and \((\eta-\lambda)^+\), supported on disjoint Hahn sets. Couple the two Poisson processes by sharing a common Poisson process of intensity \(\lambda\wedge\eta\) and adding independently the two residual processes. Their difference is the difference of residual configurations on disjoint sets, giving
\[
\mathbb E D(\Pi_\lambda,\Pi_\eta)=|\lambda-\eta|(E).
\]
For any coupling of the same laws, convexity of the variation norm (or testing against bounded measurable functions of absolute value at most one) gives
\[
\mathbb E D(\Pi_\lambda,\Pi_\eta)\ge
|\mathbb E\Pi_\lambda-\mathbb E\Pi_\eta|(E)
=|\lambda-\eta|(E).
\]
The identity (4) now implies (6).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to solve a nontrivial two-by-two coupling problem for distributions on configurations of a three-site space. It compares the \(\ell^1\)-Wasserstein costs under all five set partitions and the variation-norm optimal coupling. It also checks the two sharp deterministic examples and a finite signed-intensity variation calculation. The expected terminal line is
`VERIFY_OK projective=1.250000000000 kr=1.000000000000 partitions=5`.

These computations test finite cases only. The infinite partition limit, compactness, semicontinuity, and Poisson-process assertions are justified by the analytic proof, not by finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Pianoforte and Turin introduce the partition metric \(d_\pi\), prove that it dominates total variation distance between *laws*, and prove \(d_\pi\le2d_{KR}\) for locally compact second-countable Hausdorff state spaces. They explicitly state that equivalence of \(d_\pi\) and \(d_{KR}\) remains open. Their proof of the existing inequality tests partition-cylinder functions against the process variation metric, but does not establish an opposite-direction comparison or a common optimal coupling for increasingly fine partitions.

The equality (4) identifies \(d_\pi\) as the Wasserstein-1 distance between laws of finite counting measures for the full signed-variation cost. It supplies the missing reverse inequality and the explicit optimal coupling. Its scope covers all Polish spaces, including the state spaces of the original comparison. Equation (6) is an additional exact Poisson benchmark in precisely that metric.

General compactness, disintegration, and projective finite-dimensional transport arguments are standard ingredients; the claim of distinction is their precise application to the metric introduced in the cited paper and to the formerly unresolved comparison. Related unbalanced-transport literature typically compares deterministic finite measures using different creation/deletion costs; that is not the same as comparing **laws of point processes** under \(d_\pi\) or \(d_{KR}\).

## Limitations
The finite-total-intensity hypothesis is used to ensure finite configurations almost surely and integrable transport costs. For general measurable spaces that are not standard Borel, the required compactness and disintegration need not be available. The theorem makes no claim for infinite-intensity locally finite processes in the absence of additional localization assumptions.

An abstract projective optimal-transport theorem could subsume the compactness step under different language, even though the inspected point-process literature does not state the \(d_\pi\)--\(d_{KR}\) equivalence. This is a residual bibliographic risk, not a mathematical assumption in the proof.
