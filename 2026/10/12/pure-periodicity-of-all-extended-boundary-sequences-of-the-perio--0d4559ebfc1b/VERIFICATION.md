# Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in the same directory as `certificate.json`. No third-party dependencies are required.

First, the program compares the first \(8192\) letters of the fixed point generated by the substitution with \(\nu_2(j+1)\bmod2\). Next it exhaustively checks the possible parity pairs for \(\nu_2(t),\nu_2(t+\Delta)\) over \(1\le\Delta\le128\) and \(1\le t\le8192\).

A separate symbolic boundary constructor fills ordinary letters by residues modulo \(P=2^{\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil}\) and uses the proved parity-pair lemma for at most two exceptional positions. The program compares the resulting boundary sets at separations \(d\) and \(d+2P\) for widths \(1\le\ell\le16\), over two full period-lengths of initial separations, and verifies the \(4P\) cardinality upper bound.

Finally, it constructs the actual word by the valuation formula and independently enumerates pairs of length-\(\ell\) blocks in \(65536\) starting positions for widths \(1\le\ell\le12\), checking exact agreement with the symbolic construction over one period of separations.

These are finite checks of arithmetic, enumeration and indexing. The universal theorem follows from the proof that, for every width, every residue class and every separation, the exact allowed two-hole parity pairs remain unchanged by a shift of \(2P\).
