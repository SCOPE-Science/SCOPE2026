# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Explicit strings have disjoint one-deletion shadows by a four-position shift obstruction. A 64-state automaton counts common outputs; a 4096-dimensional matrix evolution and a 4099-value exact integer certificate extend the identity to every length via Cayley–Hamilton. An independent direct-output enumerator checks the scanner against 43,942 unordered input pairs through length eight. The certificate is included as executable source, and no global extremal assertion is made.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Sala et al. (2016) consider the identical composed channel and an alternating-center example, but their center pair has FLL distance one and cannot form a single-deletion-correcting code. The 2021 radius-one FLL ball paper treats sizes of one ball, and the 2021 intersection paper treats an insertion ball intersected with a deletion ball, rather than two composed balls. The single-edits reconstruction paper uses a union rather than the same-length composition. Searches of mathematical records and relevant prior claims found no implication of the present distance-two formula. A specialized earlier pairwise intersection result could still be unindexed.

**Value — PASS.** The exact quadratic ambiguity persists when the two codewords have disjoint deletion shadows, providing a mathematically motivated obstruction to reconstruction under a mixed channel. This is an all-length theorem with a disclosed certificate, not a small enumerated example. It does not establish the global maximum over distance-two pairs or an optimal code rate.
