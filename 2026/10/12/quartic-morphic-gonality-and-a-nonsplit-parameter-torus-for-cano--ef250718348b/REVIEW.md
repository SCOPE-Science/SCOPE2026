# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
The normalization has degree-two hyperelliptic field \(k(x)\). If a map of degree at most \(4\) did not factor through \(x\), the associated birational curve of bidegree \((2,d)\) in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) would have arithmetic genus \(d-1\le3\), contradicting normalization genus \(g\ge4\). The nonsplit node local ring \(k+\mathfrak m_{P^-}\) forces a rational value at both conjugate branches; degree-two functions cannot meet this condition. The irreducible quadratic \(\phi\) and complementary linear section construct every degree-four map. Evaluation at \(\alpha\) proves the torus parametrization and the \(q+1\) count. Both the local-ring input and the genus bound are independently identified in the proof; arithmetic code only checks finite examples.

## Originality (PASS, residual risk documented)
The close primary source [1] constructs and classifies generalized gonality-three pencils, expressly observing that these need not be finite covers. Its Theorems 3.5 and 4.8 and local-ring proof have been read; they do not state an exact degree-four morphic gonality, its full moduli, or the \(q+1\) count. The earlier Rosa–Stöhr work [2] is closely relevant to nonremovable trigonal base points; only its accessible abstract and references were available, leaving the possibility of an implicit earlier cover classification. The smooth finite-field theorem [3] does not cover singular nodal sources. Public finding-record and literature queries for equivalent statements, nonsplit gluing, degree-four covers, and the torus did not identify stronger covering work; this absence cannot certify global novelty.

For an infinite, explicitly constructed family from a recent algebraic-geometric source, the invariant of *genuine* finite morphisms differs from the author's generalized linear-series gonality. The complete, naturally motivated minimal-cover classification identifies a nonsplit torus already intrinsic to the generalized Jacobian and counts its \(q+1\) rational classes. The result is more than a change of terminology or a single example. It does not calculate higher-degree covers or the generalized-Jacobian extension class.
