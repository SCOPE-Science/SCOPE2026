# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** For an ordered \(n\)-tuple of rank-one projectors, invertibility of the two factor matrices is forced by a strict dimension bound on the images and row spaces of positive words. With both factors invertible, each word corresponds to a directed walk in the normalized overlap matrix; the elementary matrices indexed by reachable ordered pairs are linearly independent and span precisely the generated algebra. The identity is already in that span, because inverse overlap coefficients vanish outside reachability. The minimum-generator construction uses an explicit three-dimensional seed and an invertibility-preserving Schur-complement induction. The dimension-three exact count follows from kernel Möbius inversion and a complete symbolic inclusion-exclusion over 63 directed cut families; the independent checker confirms the symbolic coefficients and all normalized matrices for several fields, including \(\mathbb F_4\). No finite experiment is substituted for the symbolic argument.

The latter's exact three-by-three count sums over all ordered matrix tuples; a restricted rank-one idempotent generating-tuple count cannot be recovered from that aggregate without new information. Earlier work by Krupnik, DOI:10.1080/00927879208824513, concerns arbitrary idempotent generators and remains a plausible priority risk: its complete text was not accessible. No precise statement covering the restricted graph criterion or the constrained three-dimensional count was identified in the inspected material. The original contribution assessed is the combined rank-one constrained theorem, not the unrestricted generation question or inclusion-exclusion methodology.

**Value — PASS.** Generation by rank-one idempotents is a natural restricted algebra-generation problem, substantially different from arbitrary generators: although full matrix algebras can be two-generated unrestrictedly, rank-one projector tuples need at least their ambient dimension. The sharp uniform threshold, the binary exception, and the complete polynomial enumeration in dimension three yield interpretable, reproducible invariants beyond a routine single-instance computation.
