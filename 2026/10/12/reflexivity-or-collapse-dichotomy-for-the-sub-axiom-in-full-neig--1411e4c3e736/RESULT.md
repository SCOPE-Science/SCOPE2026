# Reflexivity-or-collapse dichotomy for the sub axiom in full neighborhood products

## Finding

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be nonempty sets, each equipped with a normal neighborhood frame (nonempty, upward-closed, finite-intersection-closed neighborhood families, possibly containing the empty set). In the full three-modal product, consider the **sub interaction axiom**
\[
\mathrm{(sub)}:\qquad \Box_{\times}p\longrightarrow (\Box_1p\wedge\Box_2p).
\]
It is valid at every point for **all** valuations if and only if one of two mutually exclusive possibilities holds:

1. **Both factor frames validate the reflexivity axiom \(\mathrm T\)**: for every \(x\in X\), all its neighborhoods contain \(x\), and for every \(y\in Y\), all its neighborhoods contain \(y\).
2. **Both factor frames are everywhere improper**: each neighborhood family consists of the entire powerset of its carrier, including the empty set.

In particular, for factors satisfying the seriality axiom \(\mathrm D\) (no neighborhood family contains the empty set), the sub axiom is valid exactly when both factors validate \(\mathrm T\). This converse is valid even for infinite carriers and nonprincipal neighborhood filters.

For finite labeled carriers of respective positive sizes \(n,m\), the exact number of ordered pairs of normal neighborhood frames whose full product validates \(\mathrm{(sub)}\) is
\[
\boxed{2^{n(n-1)+m(m-1)}+1}.
\]
Out of the total \(2^{n^2+m^2}\) frame pairs, the proportion is \(2^{-(n+m)}+2^{-(n^2+m^2)}\).

## Assumptions and scope

Fix nonempty sets \(X,Y\). A normal neighborhood filter \(N(x)\subseteq\mathcal P(X)\) is nonempty, upward-closed, and closed under intersections of two members; likewise \(M(y)\subseteq\mathcal P(Y)\). An **improper** filter contains \(\varnothing\), equivalently is the entire powerset. A **proper** filter does not contain \(\varnothing\). Local reflexivity at \(x\) means \(x\in U\) for every \(U\in N(x)\).

For any valuation \(A\subseteq X\times Y\), at \((x,y)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Box_{\times}p &\iff (\exists U\in N(x))(\exists V\in M(y))\;U\times V\subseteq A,\\
\Box_1p &\iff (\exists U\in N(x))\;U\times\{y\}\subseteq A,\\
\Box_2p &\iff (\exists V\in M(y))\;\{x\}\times V\subseteq A.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the full-product neighborhood semantics of Aghamov et al. The factor carriers need not be finite or countable. The finite enumeration treats **labeled** factors and their ordered pairing; it does not count isomorphism classes.

## Proof

**Pointwise lemma.** At a fixed \((x,y)\), sub holds for every \(A\) precisely when either both \(N(x),M(y)\) are improper, or both are proper and locally reflexive at their designated points.

If both are improper, their empty neighborhoods witness \(\Box_1p\) and \(\Box_2p\) for every valuation. If both are proper and locally reflexive, any witness rectangle \(U\times V\subseteq A\) contains both the horizontal slice \(U\times\{y\}\) and vertical slice \(\{x\}\times V\), establishing sub.

For necessity, suppose exactly one of \(N(x),M(y)\) is improper. At \(A=\varnothing\), the empty neighborhood in that factor witnesses \(\Box_{\times}p\), whereas the corresponding slice modality associated with the **proper** factor is false: no nonempty neighborhood of that proper filter has an empty slice. Hence sub fails.

Suppose both filters are proper. If some \(U\in N(x)\) omits \(x\), choose any \(V\in M(y)\) and put \(A=U\times V\). Then \(\Box_{\times}p\) holds, but the section \(\{x\}\times Y\) of \(A\) is empty, so \(\Box_2p\) fails since \(M(y)\) is proper. Symmetrically, if some \(V\in M(y)\) omits \(y\), choose \(U\in N(x)\) and \(A=U\times V\); then \(\Box_1p\) fails. Thus both proper filters must be locally reflexive, proving the lemma.

**Global dichotomy.** Fix \(x_0\in X\), \(y_0\in Y\). By the lemma, at \((x_0,y_0)\) either both are improper or both are proper and locally reflexive. In the first case, pair \(x_0\) with every \(y\in Y\), and \(y_0\) with every \(x\in X\). The lemma forces every other local filter to be improper. In the second case, the same cross-pair comparisons force all local filters in both factors to be proper and locally reflexive. These are the only cases and they each imply validity at all points. Because both carriers are nonempty, the alternatives are disjoint.

**Finite census.** For a finite \(n\)-element labeled carrier, every normal filter \(N(x)\) is generated by the intersection \(R_x=\bigcap N(x)\), possibly empty, since the powerset is finite. There are \(2^n\) generators at each \(x\), hence \(2^{n^2}\) total frames. In a T-frame the generator at \(x\) must contain \(x\), giving \(2^{n-1}\) options independently for each \(x\), hence \(2^{n(n-1)}\) T-frames. Exactly one frame is everywhere improper, with all \(R_x=\varnothing\). Multiplying the T-frame counts for the two carriers and adding the one disjoint improper-improper pair yields the claimed formula. Division by \(2^{n^2+m^2}\) gives the proportion.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone standard-library Python program `verify_sub_dichotomy.py` enumerates **every normal neighborhood frame on each finite labeled carrier**, via principal generators. For sizes \((n,m)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,2),(2,3)\), it enumerates every ordered frame pair, every valuation of the product, and every product point, and directly checks equivalence with the dichotomy. Observed valid-pair counts are respectively \(2,5,17,257\) among \(4,32,256,8192\) total pairs, matching the formula. Run `python verify_sub_dichotomy.py`; success prints `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite enumeration tests these small sizes only; it is neither a proof for arbitrary finite size nor for infinite frames. Those cases are proved by the pointwise and global arguments above, without a finiteness assumption.

## Relationship to prior work

Aghamov, Kudinov, Nguyen and Piribauer (2026, arXiv:2606.31852) define the same full neighborhood products and sub axiom, prove its validity for T-factor products, and exhibit a counterexample among D-factor products. Their inspected results provide a **sufficiency statement** and a **non-T example**, rather than the necessary-and-sufficient characterization for each fixed pair of factors, the improper-filter exceptional case, or the finite census proved here.

Kudinov (2018, arXiv:1609.03232) studies a related but **bimodal** neighborhood-product logic, not the three-modal product/sub validity classification. Earlier topological/Kripke full-product analyses explain the reflexive sufficiency but do not, from the material inspected, provide this complete normal-neighborhood-factor dichotomy.

## Limitations

The two-factor theorem requires the standard **filter-based** normal neighborhood semantics; it is not asserted for arbitrary monotone neighborhood families without finite-intersection closure. The \(+1\) census term comes from permitting **improper** local filters; it disappears for serial frames. Enumeration is for labeled factor frames, not isomorphism classes, and no assertion is made about a broader model-theoretic complexity classification. An equivalent result under a different older formulation is a residual originality risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
