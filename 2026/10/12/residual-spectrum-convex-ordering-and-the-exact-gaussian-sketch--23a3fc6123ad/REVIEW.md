# Mathematical review\n\n**Correctness — PASS.** For an orthogonal fixed range basis and an independent real Gaussian test, the squared relative Frobenius error decomposes exactly as \(R=1+\sum_j\lambda_j\lVert G^\dagger h_j\rVert_2^2\). The Gaussian factors are independent, and conditional on \(G\) the energy variables are iid. Their exchangeability and majorization prove the finite-mean convex order; inverse-Wishart integration gives the rank-one beta-prime law and the universal \(q< (\ell-r+1)/2\) moment frontier. For finite variance, the exact conditional variance formula yields strict monotonicity in \(\sum_j\lambda_j^2\). Independent finite calculations corroborate the derivations; they are not proofs of the unrestricted assertions.\n\n**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The Gaussian projection factorization and its exact first mean are prior work: Tropp et al. (2017), Appendix A.3, and Nakatsukasa (2020), Section 3.4. Kressner and Lam (2024) reproduce the decomposition and first mean and study higher-moment-based tail bounds, but the inspected relevant results do not provide the stated spectrum-majorization convex-risk law and exact all-positive-order moment equivalence. Classical multivariate Student-t, beta-prime and Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya ingredients are not claimed new. An earlier specialized distributional treatment, or interpretation as a routine corollary of prior Gaussian-factor formulas, remains a priority risk.\n\n**Scientific value — PASS.** The result identifies residual spectral concentration as a sharp classifier of convex risk when the expected squared error is identical, provides exact rank-one calibration, and specifies the sketch oversampling needed for finite higher moments. This is a conditional exact-arithmetic robustness result, not a faster algorithm, finite-precision guarantee, or uniform quantile dominance.\n\n**Sources.** Tropp, Yurtsever, Udell and Cevher, *Practical Sketching Algorithms for Low-Rank Matrix Approximation*, SIAM J.
