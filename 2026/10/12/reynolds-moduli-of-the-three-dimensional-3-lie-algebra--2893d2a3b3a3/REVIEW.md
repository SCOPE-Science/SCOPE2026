# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The Reynolds defect is alternating and trilinear, and direct determinant expansion reduces it to \(\det(R)e_1=(\sigma_2(R)-\det(R))R(e_1)\). The resulting three cases are exhaustive and mutually exclusive. Finite-field counts follow from explicit disjoint strata, rational-canonical trace fibers, and standard matrix-rank counts. Exhaustive checks over \(\mathbf F_2\) and \(\mathbf F_3\) agree with the theorem.

## Originality

PASS. The primary source defines the Reynolds identity and uses the same three-dimensional algebra in its Nijenhuis example, but its Reynolds subsection gives general theory and infinite-dimensional examples rather than this classification. The later Reynolds \(n\)-Lie paper develops cohomology and constructions without the stated finite-field census. Semantic-database and web searches found no covering structural equation, rank table, or counting polynomial. Differently indexed specialized work remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. The algebra is the primary paper's canonical finite-dimensional \(3\)-Lie example. A characteristic-free collapse of the full Reynolds identity to one matrix equation, together with complete finite-field ranks and an exact invertible count, gives a natural benchmark for the Reynolds theory and materially extends the source's qualitative invertible-derivation correspondence.
