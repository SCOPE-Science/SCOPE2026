# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes precisely the set \(T\) of cycle vertices labeled \(2\) and checks all permissible center-leaf label pairs in the three possible neighborhood states. This gives a termwise bijection for the full polynomial. The four-state cyclic transition matrix directly realizes the same sum, and its displayed determinant produces the cubic generating denominator and recurrence. The minimum coefficient argument accounts for the factor \(2\) at undominated cycle vertices, including the \(n\equiv1\pmod3\) boundary; all cyclic-gap cases have been counted. Exact independent computations check all assignments for orders three through six and compare three implementations through larger orders. Finite computations are not substituted for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specific residual uncertainty
The 2011 corona paper gives the Roman-domination *number*, not an assessed full polynomial or multiplicity distribution in its accessible description. The complete 2021 Roman-domination-polynomial paper was materially inspected, especially Section 3 and Theorem 3.7 on \(K_n\circ K_1\); its complete-base case is different from \(C_n\circ K_1\) once \(n\ge4\). The 2021 cycle-polynomial work counts functions on \(C_n\) without pendant leaves. Published indexed claims concerning Roman polynomials for chain and complete-multipartite graphs cover different graph classes. Neither matching titles nor unsuccessful searches are used as novelty proofs: the implication comparison depends on these object and parameter differences. The 2011 linked full text and the original cycle-polynomial PDF were not accessible through their exposed endpoints, so an earlier equivalently expressed sunlet result remains a real risk.

## Value — PASS
The complete weight spectrum of feasible Roman defenses is not determined by the already known minimum cost. Sunlet graphs are a natural pendant-corona family, and the three-term positive recurrence, full coefficient extraction, and exact mod-three behavior of optimal-function multiplicities provide usable enumerative refinements. The minimum-weight equality is explicitly identified as prior work, not presented as the contribution.
