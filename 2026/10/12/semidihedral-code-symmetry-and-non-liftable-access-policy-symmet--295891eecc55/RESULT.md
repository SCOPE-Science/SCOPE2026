# Semidihedral code symmetry and non-liftable access-policy symmetries in a ternary cyclic example
## Finding

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb F_3^8\) be the cyclic code in Hernández--Vega Example 1(a), equivalently the code generated by
\[
g(x)=x^4+x^3+x^2+2x+1.
\]
For coordinates numbered \(0,1,\ldots,7\), its full coordinate-permutation automorphism group is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{perm}}(C)
=\langle r,s\mid r^8=s^2=1,\ srs=r^3\rangle
\cong C_8\rtimes_3 C_2,
\]
of order \(16\). Here one may take
\[
r=(0\;1\;2\;3\;4\;5\;6\;7),
\qquad
s=(1\;3)(2\;6)(5\;7).
\]
This is the order-\(16\) semidihedral (quasidihedral) group. Its element-order census is one identity, five involutions, six elements of order \(4\), and four elements of order \(8\).

Now designate coordinate \(0\) as dealer, exactly as in the source secret-sharing example. The source's \(15\) minimal access sets on participants \(1,\ldots,7\) have full hypergraph automorphism group
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A)\cong C_2\times C_2.
\]
It is generated, for example, by
\[
a=(1\;3)(2\;6)(5\;7),
\qquad
b=(1\;5)(3\;7).
\]
The participant orbits are
\[
\{4\},\qquad \{2,6\},\qquad \{1,3,5,7\}.
\]
The stabilizer of dealer coordinate \(0\) inside \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{perm}}(C)\) is only \(\langle s\rangle\) of order \(2\), and its induced action on participants is exactly \(\langle a\rangle\). Consequently the two elements \(b\) and \(ab\) are genuine symmetries of the access policy that do not lift to coordinate-permutation symmetries of \(C\) fixing the dealer.

## Assumptions and scope

The code is the exact source code with generator matrix obtained from four consecutive shifts of \(g(x)\), over \(\mathbb F_3\). "Automorphism" for the code means coordinate permutation only; coordinate-wise nonzero scalar multipliers are not included. The access hypergraph is the source's minimal-access family for dealer coordinate \(0\); its automorphisms are arbitrary permutations of the seven participant labels preserving that family.

The claim concerns this explicit code and this dealer choice. It does not classify monomial automorphisms, secret-sharing schemes from all equivalent dealer choices, or the whole infinite cyclic family in the source.

## Proof

The code generated by the source matrix has \(3^4=81\) words. Direct enumeration reproduces the source weight enumerator
\[
1+20z^4+32z^5+8z^6+16z^7+4z^8.
\]
A nonzero word is tested for minimality by checking every nonzero codeword whose support is contained in its support. Exactly the words of weights \(4\) and \(5\) are minimal, and after normalizing the dealer coordinate to \(1\), their access supports reproduce exactly the \(15\) sets printed in the source.

For code symmetry, exhaust all \(8!=40320\) coordinate permutations and retain those preserving the complete set of \(81\) codewords. Exactly \(16\) survive. The cyclic shift \(r\) and the involution \(s\) above both survive; their generated subgroup has all \(16\) surviving permutations, and direct composition gives
\[
srs=r^3.
\]
Thus the displayed presentation is exact, not merely a subgroup lower bound.

For access-policy symmetry, exhaust all \(7!=5040\) participant permutations against the exact \(15\)-set family. Exactly four survive: the identity, \(a\), \(b\), and \(ab\). The three nonidentity permutations are involutions and commute, proving \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal A)\cong C_2\times C_2\). Their action gives the three participant orbits stated above.

Finally, among the \(16\) code coordinate automorphisms, exactly two fix coordinate \(0\): the identity and \(s\). Restriction to participants therefore induces exactly \(\langle a\rangle\), proving that the other two access-hypergraph symmetries do not lift.

## Verification

The standalone verifier `artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the source code, checks its full weight enumerator and minimal access family, exhausts all \(40320\) coordinate permutations and all \(5040\) participant permutations, verifies the semidihedral presentation, and compares the dealer stabilizer with the full access-hypergraph group. Successful replay ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Hernández and Vega determine the cyclic-code family, give this exact \([8,4,4]_3\) example and generator matrix, determine which weights are minimal, and print all \(15\) minimal access sets for the corresponding seven-participant scheme. Their paper does not determine the full coordinate-permutation automorphism group, the full symmetry group of the resulting access hypergraph, or whether every policy symmetry lifts to a code symmetry.

The source also relates its covering structure to the perfect-nonlinear-function codes of Li--Ling--Qu. That older work determines covering structures and minimal access sets, so it is relevant to the support family, but the inspected bibliographic material and focused exact searches did not locate either automorphism group or the lifting comparison above. Exact searches by the generator polynomial, the \([8,4,4]_3\) parameters, semidihedral terminology, and the numerical access family likewise did not locate a prior statement of this symmetry split.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite symmetry classification for one newly highlighted cyclic-code secret-sharing example. A non-indexed classification of ternary \([8,4,4]\) codes may record an equivalent code's automorphism group under another presentation; no such coverage was found in the checked public literature and research records. The older perfect-nonlinear-function paper was available only through bibliographic/abstract material in this run, so an incidental symmetry calculation hidden in its full text remains a residual originality risk.

The non-lifting statement is about coordinate-permutation automorphisms, not the larger monomial automorphism group. No claim is made that the two non-liftable policy symmetries fail to lift after allowing coordinate scalings.
