# Seven symmetry classes of rowmotion cycles on the Fano incidence poset

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal P=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\) and let \(\mathcal L\) consist of its seven projective lines, each the three-point set \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\) for distinct nonzero vectors \(a,b\). Form the fourteen-element height-two poset \(Q=\mathcal P\sqcup\mathcal L\), ordered by \(p<\ell\) precisely when \(p\in\ell\). For each order ideal \(I\), define rowmotion \(\rho(I)\) as the ideal generated by the minimal elements outside \(I\).

**Theorem.** There are exactly \(458\) ideals of \(Q\). Rowmotion has precisely \(29\) orbits of length \(3\), \(35\) of length \(5\), and \(28\) of length \(7\); in particular its order as a permutation is \(\operatorname{lcm}(3,5,7)=105\). Under the incidence-preserving projective automorphism group \(\operatorname{GL}(3,2)\) of order \(168\), the \(92\) rowmotion cycles form exactly seven orbit-classes:

| Cycle length | Number of cycles in the class | Representative ideal mask | Ideals covered |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 1 | `0x0000` | 3 |
| 3 | 14 | `0x009f` | 42 |
| 3 | 14 | `0x01bf` | 42 |
| 5 | 7 | `0x0001` | 35 |
| 5 | 7 | `0x0007` | 35 |
| 5 | 21 | `0x0003` | 105 |
| 7 | 28 | `0x000b` | 196 |

Every entry of the table denotes an entire \(\operatorname{GL}(3,2)\)-orbit of rowmotion cycles, rather than an individual ideal. The accompanying `orbit_types.csv` records all states in the representative cycles.

## Assumptions and scope
Only the ordinary rowmotion action on the stated point-below-line incidence poset \(Q\) is considered. It is *not* rowmotion on the face lattice of the Fano plane, the rank-three Boolean lattice, or the Heawood graph viewed as a graph. The seven points are represented by nonzero binary integers \(1,\ldots,7\); the seven lines are the distinct sets \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\), ordered lexicographically. For reproducibility, an ideal is represented by a fourteen-bit integer: bit \(p-1\) records point \(p\), and bit \(7+j\) records line \(j\), where \(j=0,\ldots,6\) in lexicographic order. A recorded line requires all its points to be present. The symmetry group preserves the two ranks of the poset, so order-reversing dualities are not included.

## Proof
For any point set \(S\subseteq\mathcal P\), let \(C(S)=\{\ell\in\mathcal L:\ell\subseteq S\}\). Every ideal is uniquely \((S,T)\), with \(T\subseteq C(S)\). Consequently
\[
|J(Q)|=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal P}2^{|C(S)|}=458.
\]
This can be checked without enumerating the ideals: the contributions for point-set sizes \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\) are respectively \(1,7,21,42,63,84,112,128\). For size \(3\), seven of the \(35\) triples are lines and \(28\) are not. For size \(4\), seven subsets contain no line and the other \(28\) contain one. Every five-point set contains two lines; every six-point set contains four, and the full seven-point set contains all seven.

The minimal absent points of an ideal \((S,T)\) are exactly \(\mathcal P\setminus S\). The minimal absent lines are exactly \(U=C(S)\setminus T\). Taking their downward closure therefore gives the explicit transition
\[
\rho(S,T)=\left((\mathcal P\setminus S)\cup\bigcup_{\ell\in U}\ell,\ U\right),\qquad U=C(S)\setminus T.
\]
This formula is exact for every ideal and uniquely determines the finite permutation. All line sets, masks and transitions are generated from the seven nonzero vectors and binary addition, without externally supplied graph tables.

An order-preserving symmetry is induced by a nonsingular linear map of \(\mathbb F_2^3\). There are \(7\cdot6\cdot4=168\) such maps. Conversely, an incidence symmetry is determined by its action on any ordered noncollinear triple and there are at most \(7\cdot6\cdot4\) possible images, so this is the full automorphism group. Rowmotion commutes with each of these symmetries because the defining operation of taking minimal complementary elements and downward closure is intrinsic to the order.

For each of the seven representative masks in the theorem, the transition formula gives the short cycles explicitly recorded in `orbit_types.csv`. Acting by all \(168\) projective symmetries on the states in each representative cycle produces, in the table's order, sets of \(3,42,42,35,35,105,196\) *distinct* ideals. A direct exact bit-set comparison finds the seven resulting sets pairwise disjoint. Their cardinalities add to \(458\), the entire ideal count established above, so they exhaust all ideals. Since symmetry commutes with rowmotion, each set decomposes into cycles of its representative's length, giving the seven stated symmetry classes and the totals \(29,35,28\). Their least common multiple is \(105\), and each of the three lengths occurs.

This finite covering argument can be replayed with only seven short cycle representatives and the \(168\) invertible linear maps. Its disjointness and cardinality assertions are not inferred from sample enumeration: they are checked exhaustively by the supplied exact verifier. A second implementation directly tests every fourteen-bit subset for ideal validity and reconstructs all rowmotion orbits without using the rank-two transition formula.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using the standard Python library. It constructs \(458\) ideals, checks that rowmotion is bijective, checks equivariance on every ideal under all \(168\) linear automorphisms, and verifies that the seven explicit symmetry families form a disjoint cover. It writes `orbit_types.csv` deterministically, including every representative cycle state.

Independently, compile `verify_independent.cpp` with `c++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_independent.cpp -o verify_independent` and execute `./verify_independent`. This program enumerates all \(2^{14}=16384\) subsets, checks the downward-closure condition, computes minimal missing elements directly, and decomposes the permutation into cycles. Both verifiers completed with the same \((3:29,5:35,7:28)\) distribution. `VERIFICATION.md` records the exact checks and their limits.

## Relationship to prior work
Rowmotion is a classical poset dynamical system. Cameron and Fon-Der-Flaass (1995) analyze an equivalent antichain action and devote their specific orbit study to products of three chains; only their abstract and bibliographic record were available for comparison. Striker and Williams (arXiv:1108.1172, first posted 2011-08-04) give its standard order-ideal definition, its toggle interpretation, and orbit results for products of chains, classical root posets and other specialized posets. Striker (arXiv:1601.03710) develops generalized toggle groups and cover-closure. Pechenik (arXiv:2107.02679) treats not-relatively-prime rowmotion for minuscule posets and comparability-graph invariance of associated promotion-orbit cardinalities. These works supply the conceptual motivation, but the inspected statements and examples do not specify the Fano point-line incidence poset or imply its numerical cycle census. In particular, \(Q\) has seven minimal elements, whereas each poset in the minuscule classification of Pechenik has a unique minimal element, so that theorem does not apply. The claim here is precisely the seven-class spectrum, not the definition or general group-theoretic framework of rowmotion.

The result also illustrates boundaries of special-poset behavior: the Fano incidence poset has rank \(1\), but contains rowmotion cycles of lengths \(5\) and \(7\), relatively prime to \(3\), so it does not satisfy the not-relatively-prime condition for the one-layer case. Moreover, ideal cardinality is not constant in average across all cycles: there are three-cycles of constant cardinality \(6\), three-cycles of constant cardinality \(8\), and the three-cycle \(\varnothing\to\mathcal P\to Q\to\varnothing\), whose average cardinality is \(7\). These are consequences of the complete classification, not separate general claims.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite theorem about one fourteen-element poset. It does not establish an orbit formula for larger projective planes or other Steiner triple systems. Complete exact verification is provided, but there has been no independent external audit. Although targeted searches and material reading of highly relevant open primary sources found no prior equivalent class table, an unindexed earlier enumeration remains a genuine originality risk. The 1995 Cameron–Fon-Der-Flaass paper was available only at abstract level during this comparison; no whole-paper negative coverage claim is made for it. The term 'seven classes' refers to the action of rank-preserving incidence automorphisms, not the larger group obtained by adjoining a point-line duality.
