# Sharp binary weight-two pair-distance spectrum and the \(22\) first-threshold optima

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), let \(A_2(n,7)\) denote the maximum number of distinct binary length-\(n\) words, each containing exactly two ones, such that the cyclic symbol-pair distance between any two different words is at least \(7\). Then
\[
A_2(n,7)=\begin{cases}
1,&2\le n\le6,\\
2,&n=7,8,\\
3,&n=9,\\
\lfloor n/2\rfloor,&n\ge10.
\end{cases}
\]
For every even \(n=2m\ge10\), all maximum codes are exactly those arising from Euler circuits of connected, simple, \(4\)-regular graphs on \(m\) vertices with \(2m\) edges. At \(n=10\) the graph is necessarily \(K_5\), and there are precisely \(22\) different optimal five-word subsets of the \(45\) possible weight-two words at the labeled coordinate positions. Under the dihedral action on the ten cyclic positions, the \(22\) optimal codes constitute exactly three orbits, of sizes \(2,10,10\).

The precise equality enumeration and its dihedral classification are the focus: Eulerian graph methods for symbol-pair codes and general constant-pair-weight code bounds were previously known.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), the blocklength satisfies \(n\ge2\), and every word has **Hamming weight exactly two**, not merely pair-weight four. Define
\[
\pi(x)=((x_i,x_{i+1\bmod n}))_{i=0}^{n-1},\qquad d_{\mathrm p}(x,y)=|\{i:\pi(x)_i\ne\pi(y)_i\}|.
\]
For \(n\le6\), a singleton code meets the vacuous pairwise-distance requirement; a two-word code is impossible because the metric is at most \(n\).

The positions are labeled \(0,\ldots,n-1\). For length ten, two codes are **different labeled codes** if their sets of coordinate support pairs differ, irrespective of how the codewords are ordered. Only the dihedral group generated by a one-step cyclic coordinate shift and reversal is used for the three-orbit statement; neither individual coordinate permutations nor binary complements are allowed.

## Proof
**Distance and disjointness.** For any two binary words, their pair-distance equals the number of cycle edges incident to a differing coordinate. In particular, if two weight-two words share a one-position, their Hamming distance is \(2\) and their pair-distance is at most \(4\); hence any admissible code of size at least two has pairwise disjoint two-element supports. This immediately gives \(|C|\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). For disjoint supports \(P,Q\), let \(S=P\cup Q\). Since \(|S|=4\), counting incident cycle edges gives
\[
d_{\mathrm p}(P,Q)=8-e_{C_n}(S),
\]
where \(e_{C_n}(S)\) is the number of cyclic neighbor-pairs contained in \(S\). Thus a pair is compatible exactly when \(e_{C_n}(P\cup Q)\le1\).

**Small lengths.** Suppose \(m\ge2\) supports are disjoint, and let \(S\) be their union. Each pair of supports induces at most one cycle edge in their union. If no individual support has adjacent positions, every edge of \(C_n[S]\) joins two different supports, so \(e_{C_n}(S)\le\binom m2\). If one support has adjacent positions, every other support has no adjacent positions, and none of the edges joining this special support to any other support can occur; consequently \(e_{C_n}(S)\le1+\binom{m-1}{2}\le\binom m2\). Two distinct internally adjacent supports would themselves violate the distance condition. We therefore always have
\[
e_{C_n}(S)\le\binom m2.
\]
If \(h=n-2m\) positions are unused, deleting these \(h\) vertices removes at most \(2h\) edges of \(C_n\), whence \(e_{C_n}(S)\ge n-2h=4m-n\). At \((n,m)=(7,3),(8,3),(9,4)\), the resulting lower bounds \(5,4,7\) respectively exceed \(\binom m2=3,3,6\). Thus the respective upper bounds improve to \(2,2,3\). They are achieved by the following collections of supports (all coordinates are zero-based):
\[
\begin{aligned}
n=7:&\quad \{\{0,2\},\{4,6\}\},\\
n=8:&\quad \{\{0,2\},\{4,6\}\},\\
n=9:&\quad \{\{0,2\},\{3,5\},\{6,8\}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every two supports in each displayed collection induce at most one cycle edge, so the pair distances are at least \(7\). Together with the metric-length bound for \(2\le n\le6\), this proves all small cases.

**Uniform existence.** Fix \(m\ge5\). The circulant graph on vertex labels \(\mathbb Z_m\) with edges connecting labels differing by \(\pm1\) or \(\pm2\) is connected, simple, and four-regular. It has exactly \(2m\) edges and therefore admits a closed Euler circuit \(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{2m}=v_0\). Construct \(m\) binary words of length \(2m\), one per graph vertex: the word indexed by \(a\) has ones precisely at the two indices \(i\in\{0,\ldots,2m-1\}\) for which \(v_i=a\). Exactly two such occurrences exist because the graph has degree four, and at every occurrence one incoming and one outgoing tour edge are used. For distinct vertices \(a,b\), a cycle edge of the support union occurs precisely when the tour traverses graph edge \(\{a,b\}\). Simplicity of the graph ensures this occurs at most once. Therefore the resulting code has pair-distance at least seven and size \(m\), meeting the disjoint-support bound. If \(n=2m+1\ge11\), insert a common zero at any fixed cyclic coordinate into every such length-\(2m\) word. A previously compared cyclic pair \((a,b)\) versus \((c,d)\) is replaced by two comparisons \((a,0)\) versus \((c,0)\) and \((0,b)\) versus \((0,d)\); the number of differing new pairs is at least the number of differing old pairs. Hence the pair-distance does not decrease and the code still has \(m\) words. This proves the formula for all \(n\ge10\).

**All even-length equality cases.** Let an admissible code have \(m=n/2\ge5\) words. Their two-element supports partition all \(n=2m\) positions. If a support contained adjacent cyclic positions, one of the boundary edges of that two-position run would meet a position occupied by another codeword, creating at least two induced edges in the union of those two supports, a contradiction. Thus no two adjacent coordinates have the same codeword label. Label the coordinates by the codeword whose support contains them, giving a cyclic word \(v_0,\ldots,v_{2m-1}\) over \(m\) labels, each occurring exactly twice. The distance condition says that no unordered pair of distinct labels occurs as consecutive coordinates more than once. These consecutive unordered pairs therefore define a connected simple graph, whose degree at each label is four (two tour incidences per occurrence). The cyclic word is an Euler circuit of this graph. Conversely the construction in the preceding paragraph maps any Euler circuit of such a graph to an admissible code. Relabeling vertices exactly corresponds to permuting codeword names, leaving the unordered set of supports unchanged. This proves the claimed characterization.

**The exact first-threshold census.** With \(n=10\), the graph has \(m=5\) vertices. A simple four-regular graph on five vertices is \(K_5\). Count its closed Euler circuits by the finite recurrence
\[
F(v,E)=\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}F(w,E\setminus\{\{v,w\}\}),\quad
F(v,\varnothing)=\mathbf{1}_{v=0},
\]
where \(E\) is the set of unused edges and the start vertex is \(0\). An exact tabulation over the \(5\cdot2^{10}\) possible states yields \(F(0,E(K_5))=528\). Thus there are \(5\cdot528=2640\) labeled length-ten cyclic vertex sequences, since their initial symbol can be any of five labels. The action of \(S_5\) on these sequences by label permutation is free, and its orbits are exactly the distinct unlabeled support-code sets. Hence the number of optimal labeled-coordinate codes is
\[
\frac{5\cdot528}{5!}=22.
\]
The exhaustive support-pair verifier independently enumerates all \(22\) via a compatibility graph with \(45\) vertices and checks their exact orbit partition under all ten rotations and ten reflections. Three representative code sets and their orbit sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{r|l}
2&\{\{0,3\},\{1,8\},\{2,5\},\{4,7\},\{6,9\}\}\\
10&\{\{0,3\},\{1,5\},\{2,7\},\{4,8\},\{6,9\}\}\\
10&\{\{0,3\},\{1,5\},\{2,8\},\{4,7\},\{6,9\}\}
\end{array}
\]
where each displayed pair is the support of one codeword. The two computational routes are provided in separate standalone scripts and use different enumeration objects.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` and `python3 verify_independent.py` from the extracted package directory; both scripts use only the Python standard library and require no installed datasets or external services. The first exhaustively enumerates every clique of the length-ten pair-compatibility graph (\(45\) possible two-subsets), establishes that exactly \(22\) optima of size five exist, and directly evaluates all dihedral images to obtain orbit sizes \(2,10,10\). It also checks the maximum sizes for all lengths through ten. The second enumerates the closed Euler circuits of \(K_5\) by two exact methods (subset recurrence and direct enumeration) and maps them back to support codes. The nonfinite all-length statements rely on the explicit combinatorial arguments above; the programs do not purport to enumerate unbounded blocklengths.

## Relationship to prior work
The cyclic symbol-pair metric and the connection between pair-distance and ordinary Hamming distance come from Cassuto and Blaum and subsequent work. Chee, Ji, Kiah, Wang, and Yin (first public version 2012-11-07) already used Eulerian graphs, specifically \(K_5\) and a ten-edge Euler circuit, to produce other MDS symbol-pair codes; their construction is **not** new here. Elishco, Gabrys, and Yaakobi established an exact constant-pair-weight bound when the pair-distance equals twice the pair-weight; for pair-weight four, that result treats distance eight, not the distance-seven equality census here. Zhao and Chang (2025) addressed constant-pair-weight four and distance seven and reported optimal constructions for certain parameters, so the general optimal-size phenomenon may already follow from their work. The distinguishing contribution asserted here is the **complete classification of all extremizers at even full-support lengths and the count of \(22\) / three dihedral classes at the first such length \(10\)**, not priority for the Euler technique or for all previously known numerical bounds.

## Limitations
The 2012 Eulerian construction does not prove the converse that every extremizer must be Eulerian in this fixed-weight-two setting, and does not enumerate the \(22\) code sets. The 2025 Zhao–Chang article was available only as an introductory publisher preview; the relevant complete text could not be checked. Its abstract provides no equality classification, but the possibility of an uninspected overlap is material. The reported novelty assessment is literature-limited, not a claim of exhaustive bibliographic certainty. The code families use exactly Hamming weight two and minimum cyclic pair-distance seven; no statement about general binary codes, higher weights, the full pair-weight-four layer, or decoder complexity is claimed. The finite census is exact for the explicitly stated ten-coordinate domain only.
