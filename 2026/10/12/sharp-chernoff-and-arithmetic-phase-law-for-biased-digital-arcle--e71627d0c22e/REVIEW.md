**Correctness: PASS.** The exact graph-length identity is documented in Pfeffer–Smith–Severa, Theorem 4.2. Rationalization yields strict two-sided comparisons with product testing overlap; a finite-variance Chernoff tilt proves the exponential rate without assuming a large-deviation approximation. In the binary case, tilting gives an exact binomial average; a uniformly dominated lattice local limit proves the phase law. Two integrations by parts produce a beta-function Fourier transform that has no zeros on the required strip. Irrationality at the rational bias \(9/10\) finishes the precise nonconvergence claim. Separate finite calculations in `verify.py` exercise the critical identities without substituting finite evidence for proofs.

Broad semantic and literature searches found no source implying the stated entire family and Fourier-phase theorem, but older equivalent terminology may evade indexing; that residual risk is documented, and no independent novelty certification is claimed.

The result supplies a sharp universal-digit-family exponent, an exact binary local-limit profile, and a mathematically necessary lattice oscillation in the leading constant, giving substantially more information than convergence or a finite table. It is not a claimed new generic hypothesis-testing bound or a mere reparameterization of the finite length identity.
