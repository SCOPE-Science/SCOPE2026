# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The proof reconstructs the prime-power formula for the Dirichlet inverse, the normalization \(F_k(1)=6\), and the value at \(n=4\), then treats every composite exponent pattern. The difficult cube-free nonsquarefree negative-Möbius branch is controlled by the exact bound \(\sigma_k(a)/J_k(a)<5/2\) with separate \(b=2\) and \(b>2\) inequalities. Strictness excludes every second extremizer. Independent exact integer convolution recurrences and local formulas agreed in 24,000 finite checks; these are corroborative, not the infinite proof.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk
The complete September 2026 motivating paper proves the zero set, not the sharp composite gap. The closely related published stability note proves a continuous zero-set interval, and its proof controls signs but never minimizes \(|F_k(n)|\) or identifies the unique composite minimizer. Its zero-set claim does not imply the numerical bound: nonvanishing only excludes zero. Results on other arithmetic-function gaps concern different quantities. Searches of published findings, public literature, and earlier claims in the same topic found no implication-covering result. Equivalent unindexed statements remain possible; novelty is not inferred merely from search failure.

## Value (V): PASS
The residual magnitude is the natural quantitative separation invariant for a newly studied exact prime-characterization function. This theorem determines its optimum over **all composite integers** and identifies the unique sharp case, making its zero-set characterization quantitative and exhibiting a structural prime-exponent boundary. It is not a finite table or a rephrased zero-set test. It does not assert a speedup for factorization-based evaluation.
