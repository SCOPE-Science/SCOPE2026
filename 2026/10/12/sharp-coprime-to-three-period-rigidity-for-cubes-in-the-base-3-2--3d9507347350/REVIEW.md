# Scientific review: sharp coprime-to-three cube-period rigidity

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reconstructs the sequence definition from the unscaled sesquinary digit expansion and uses the exact recurrences published for it. The derived even-index subsequence satisfies equality at positions \(3m\) and \(3m+1\), forbidding four consecutive alternating symbols. A ten-letter alternating factor of the original word would force exactly such a forbidden four-letter factor. For a cube with period \(p\) prime to \(3\), aligned three-block positions visit all but at most one residue modulo \(p\), so two-edge overlap forces each adjacent edge of the period word to flip. Consequently \(p\) is even and the cube alternates; sharp length nine then forces \(p=2\). A separate argument excludes \(p=1\). The factor starting at index \(12\) verifies attainment, and a period-three fifth power starting at index \(18\) confirms the boundary. The attached reproducibility script completed its independent finite checks, which are not used as an infinite certificate.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature
Dekking (2023) supplies the defining block recurrences and proposes the unrestricted critical exponent \(5\), rather than an exact classification of coprime-to-three cube periods. Dekking–Keane (2023; preprint 2022) explicitly exhibits a fourth power but does not exclude larger nonternary periods. Cassaigne et al. (2026) prove recurrence, symmetry and frequencies, not a cube-period classification, as established from inspection of the article's statements, conclusions and targeted whole-document text searches. OEIS A357448 records the substitution and entries, not the result. Targeted research-record queries found related results on formal power series of the classical Thue–Morse sequence and other substitutions; those address different objects and do not imply this theorem. Residual risks are unindexed literature and inaccessible older rational-base combinatorics articles; a negative search is not a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS
The factor \(3\) is selected by the original three-letter block substitution, not by an arbitrary numerical slice. The result exactly classifies **every** triple repetition across the infinite set of periods coprime to \(3\), identifies the unique admissible period, and proves a sharp fractional exponent \(9/2\). It rules out fifth powers for two-thirds of residue classes of periods, a concrete structural reduction of Shallit's explicit 2023 critical-exponent conjecture. The remaining multiples-of-three periods remain open. This is a complete natural boundary lemma, not a claimed proof of the full conjecture.
