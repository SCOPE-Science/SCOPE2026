# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For arbitrary nonempty carriers, necessity follows directly from metric symmetry and triangle inequalities. The two constructive cases give genuine metrics with exactly the required strict relations and a distance range of at most \(\max\{3,\lfloor b/a\rfloor+1\}\) positive values. The infinite case is covered because all positive edge costs are bounded below and all direct routes have bounded cost, guaranteeing a shortest path. Three-point and path obstructions establish exact sharpness. A pure-stdlib checker independently tested 5,042 finite admissible pairs and explicit sharpness configurations. Finite checks are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit risk

The general representability direction is covered by Harding–Shapirovsky (2026), Lemma 4.2/appendix, and Kutz (2007), Theorem 4.4. Its unmodified restatement is not the contribution. The sharp cardinality-independent distance-alphabet formula, depending only on the floor of the ratio of two thresholds, and matching lower path examples were not stated in the inspected primary full-text material. Targeted semantic literature searches and published-source queries did not find matching claims. A prior quantitative reformulation in older distance-logic or graph-power literature could exist; searches are not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

Minimizing the number of metric distance values is a natural information-compression invariant for representing a qualitative two-radius modal frame. A single sharp universal formula applies to finite and infinite carriers, is independent of the number of worlds, and exhibits the first change in required levels at each integer ratio threshold. The explicit construction and sharp family distinguish the result from a merely routine representability corollary; the exact optimum answers a separate quantitative question.

Kutz, *Notes on Logics of Metric Spaces* (2007), DOI:10.1007/s11225-007-9023-3; and Kutz et al., *Logics of Metric Spaces* (2003), DOI:10.1145/635499.635504.
