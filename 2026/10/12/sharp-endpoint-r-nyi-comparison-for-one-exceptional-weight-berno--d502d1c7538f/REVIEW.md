# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof covers all independent fair-Bernoulli sums with \(n\ge1\) identical nonzero weights and one arbitrary real weight, after normalization. Noninteger and large integer offsets split into disjoint supports, while integral overlaps use exact binomial convolution coefficients. A central-mass induction treats small shifts, a modal-distance exponential tail bound treats larger shifts when \(n\ge20\), and a complete exact-integer certificate treats all \(190\) remaining \((n,k)\) cases. The certificate examines every relevant atom, not random samples. In particular the universal bound \(K M^2<1\) has no unproved limiting step. Risk: the computer-assisted finite part must be independently re-executed and the inequalities peer-checked.

## Originality — PASS (priority risk disclosed)

Li's 2026 Conjecture 1.1 states the unrestricted endpoint comparison; its Theorems 1.4/1.5 give different (positive-order or larger-variable-factor) comparisons and do not imply the complete present subclass. Jain--Sah--Sawhney (2021) prove a weaker general support/fiber relation. Searches for subset-sum support, largest atom, shifted-binomial convolution, equivalent Rényi-entropy endpoint statements, and published mathematical-record analogues did not identify direct coverage. The one-outlier result is a strict infinite-family case of the open conjecture. Unindexed prior equivalents remain possible; search failure alone is not a priority certificate.

## Value — PASS

The identical-weight binomial is a model sharpness family for the conjectured exponent \(2\). One exceptional coefficient creates new atom collisions exactly at integral offsets, so proving the sharp endpoint for all real exceptional values and every sample size is a natural structured extension. The result gives a complete theorem on this infinite family, with the limiting sharp exponent. It does not treat two independent exceptional coefficients, biased variables, or general weights.
