# Sharp finite normalized Nash-depth profile for surface cones of index at most 65

## Finding
For each integer \(q\) with \(2\le q\le65\), and each integer \(p\) with \(1\le p<q\) and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), consider the normal affine complex toric surface
\[
X_{p,q}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[\mathbb Z^2\cap C_{p,q}],
\qquad C_{p,q}=\mathbb R_{\ge0}(1,0)+\mathbb R_{\ge0}(p,q).
\]
Let \(d(p,q)\) be the minimum number of *simultaneous normalized Nash-blowup rounds* required for the resulting toric surface to become smooth. Every singular affine chart is blown up in a round; the round count is therefore the maximum over all descendant charts.

**Complete finite depth-profile theorem.** For every \(2\le q\le65\),
\[
\boxed{\max_{\substack{1\le p<q\\\gcd(p,q)=1}}d(p,q)
=\lceil\log_2 q\rceil,}
\]
and equality holds **exactly** for \(p=q-1\) among these standard representatives.

Equivalently, the smallest index of a cyclic quotient toric surface singularity requiring at least \(r\) normalized Nash rounds, for each \(1\le r\le7\), is respectively
\[
\boxed{2,3,5,9,17,33,65.}
\]
In particular, **index \(65\) is the sharp first seven-round threshold**, attained uniquely by the standard cone \(C_{64,65}\). No normal affine toric surface singularity of index at most \(64\) requires seven rounds.

There are exactly \(1307\) primitive parameter pairs \((p,q)\) in the stated range. The complete exact table \((q,p,d(p,q))\) appears in `artifacts/index_depth_profile.csv` and is regenerated by the bundled proof-checker.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\), and *normalized Nash blowup* means the normalization of the usual Nash blowup in characteristic zero. The index is the absolute determinant of the two primitive generators of the two-dimensional cone, equivalently the cyclic quotient-group order. Smooth surfaces have depth \(0\); in the standard cone parameterization the unique smooth index-one state is denoted \((0,1)\).

The theorem applies **only for index at most \(65\)**. The displayed logarithmic formula is not claimed outside that exact finite range. The integer \(65\) is not an arbitrary sampling cutoff: it is the first index realizing the seventh iteration, and the full range below it is checked exhaustively for sharp minimality.

## Proof
Every strongly convex two-dimensional rational cone with primitive generators and determinant \(q\) is integrally unimodularly equivalent to \(C_{p,q}\) for some \(0\le p<q\) with \(\gcd(p,q)=1\). For singular cones \(q\ge2\) we have \(1\le p<q\). Scanning these pairs without quotienting by further ray-exchange symmetries is therefore **exhaustive**, possibly redundant, rather than a sampling procedure.

The exact finite algorithm rests on the following two established properties of the normalized toric Nash blowup in dimension two (Atanasov–Lopez–Perry–Proudfoot–Thaddeus, Propositions 4.10–4.12). Let \(H=(v_0,\ldots,v_k)\) be the consecutive, counterclockwise Hilbert basis of \(C_{p,q}\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Its adjacent vectors satisfy \(\det(v_i,v_{i+1})=1\). If
\[
s_i=v_i+v_{i+1}\qquad(0\le i<k),
\]
then the affine charts of the normalized Nash blowup correspond to vertices of the unbounded polygon
\[
C_{p,q}+\operatorname{conv}\{s_0,\ldots,s_{k-1}\}.
\]
Its boundary consists of consecutive segments joining these sums and the two original exterior rays. Intermediate sums in a collinear boundary stretch do not contribute vertices.

**Finite Hilbert-basis certificate.** An indecomposable nonzero lattice element of \(C_{p,q}\) is either one of its primitive extreme rays or lies in the half-open fundamental parallelogram of those rays. Indeed, a point outside that parallelogram has a coefficient at least \(1\) on one generator, and subtraction of that generator gives a nontrivial decomposition inside the lattice semigroup. Hence every candidate indecomposable has
\[
0\le y\le q,\qquad0\le x\le p+1.
\]
The checker scans the slightly larger finite integer rectangle \(0\le y\le q\), \(0\le x\le p+q\), restricted by \(qx\ge py\), in increasing \(y\) then \(x\). An element is recorded precisely when subtracting no previously discovered indecomposable leaves a nonzero semigroup element. Every summand in a proper decomposition occurs earlier in this order, so induction proves this is the complete Hilbert basis, not a partial search. Exact rational sorting and adjacent determinant-one assertions provide independent consistency tests.

**Exact fan and depth recursion.** At each essential boundary sum the checker uses differences to the neighboring essential sums, or the appropriate exterior ray, as the two tangent rays. It primitive-reduces these rays and uses extended Euclid to transport the pair to standard form \((p',q')\); the determinant \(q'\) is exact. The fan procedure is precisely the cited toric Nash construction. Smooth descendants have depth \(0\), and every nonsmooth state obeys
\[
d(p,q)=1+\max_{(p',q')\in\mathcal N(p,q)}d(p',q'),
\]
where \(\mathcal N(p,q)\) is the exact finite collection of normalized Nash affine-chart states. Thus the recursive depth computation is exact. It terminates in all enumerated states, with no repeated singular state; the published toric-surface termination theorem independently guarantees eventual termination.

**Exhaustive arithmetic certificate.** `artifacts/verify.py` loops over each \(q=2,\ldots,65\) and each coprime \(p=1,\ldots,q-1\), independently regenerates the recursion and the complete \(1307\)-row certificate table, and checks for **every** \(q\) that the largest depth equals the integer bit-length \(\lceil\log_2 q\rceil\) and that the unique maximizing parameter is \(p=q-1\). These exhaustive assertions establish the theorem for the stated finite domain.

A readily inspected seven-step witness for index \(65\) is the chain of standard cone states
\[
(64,65)\to(63,65)\to(31,33)\to(15,17)\to
(7,9)\to(3,5)\to(1,3)\to(0,1).
\]
Each transition is a genuine affine chart in the corresponding normalized Nash blowup. The finite exhaustive upper bound rules out a longer branch and every lower-index seven-step example.

## Relationship to prior work
Atanasov, Lopez, Perry, Proudfoot, and Thaddeus established the Hilbert-basis / continued-fraction polyhedral description of normalized Nash blowups and proved eventual resolution for every toric surface. Their Section 4 gives the needed exact fan recursion, but does not state an index-by-index **round-depth** profile or the sharp index-65 seven-round threshold; their numerical tables concern three- and four-dimensional cone counts rather than the present two-dimensional depth invariant.

Castillo, Duarte, Leyton-Álvarez, and Liendo give later algorithms for toric Nash iteration and extensive computational evidence, but their dimension-two numerical study concerns the **non-normalized** Nash blowup and does not supply the claimed normalized surface depth table.

The present result is a new exact **finite extremal depth classification** derived by exhaustive arithmetic use of the earlier fan theorem. It is neither a claim that the algorithm itself is new nor a claim of an all-index complexity bound.

## Limitations
This does not determine the maximum normalized Nash depth for \(q>65\), does not address non-normalized Nash blowups, and does not give a complexity theorem in dimensions three or higher. The complete table retains unimodularly equivalent standard representatives; the parameter count \(1307\) is a count of coprime pairs, not of distinct isomorphism classes.

The underlying fan-construction method was known. The novelty claimed is restricted to the **computed and exhaustively certified sharp finite depth profile**. Equivalent unpublished machine tables or folklore observations remain a residual priority risk. The exact arithmetic checker, though reproducible, has not received an independent audit.
