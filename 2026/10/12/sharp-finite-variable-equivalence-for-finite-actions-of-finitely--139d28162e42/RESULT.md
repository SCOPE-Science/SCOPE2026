# Sharp finite-variable equivalence for finite actions of finitely generated groups

## Finding
For any fixed finitely generated group \(\Gamma\), let \(\mathrm{FO}^k\) be ordinary first-order logic with at most \(k\) variable names and no counting quantifiers. Write \(m_{[H]}(A)\) for the number of transitive components isomorphic to \(\Gamma/H\) in a finite \(\Gamma\)-set \(A\), with \([H]\) a conjugacy class of finite-index subgroups. For finite \(\Gamma\)-sets \(A,B\) and every \(k\ge1\),
\[
 A\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^k}B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \min(m_{[H]}(A),k)=\min(m_{[H]}(B),k)\ \text{for all }[H].
\]
For nonempty \(A\), its least isolating variable width among *finite \(\Gamma\)-sets* is exactly \(1+\max_{[H]}m_{[H]}(A)\). The empty action has width one.

## Assumptions and scope
\(\Gamma\) is fixed and finitely generated; choose a finite symmetric generating set \(S\). The language consists of the unary functions \(s\) for \(s\in S\), interpreted as the specified group actions. Words in \(S\) are terms and can have arbitrary finite length without consuming variables. Every structure considered is a finite \(\Gamma\)-set, so all relations defining \(\Gamma\) are assumed, not asserted to have a finite axiomatization. The signature has no additional predicates, names or counting quantifiers. The empty set is allowed. The subgroup types are counted up to conjugacy, not as chosen stabilizers. Although there may be infinitely many finite-index subgroup conjugacy classes, a fixed finite action contains only finitely many orbit types.

## Proof
**Finite quotients for comparing two models.** For two finite actions \(A,B\), the intersection \(N\) of the kernels of the homomorphisms \(\Gamma\to\operatorname{Sym}(A)\) and \(\Gamma\to\operatorname{Sym}(B)\) is finite-index and normal. Both actions factor through the same finite quotient \(Q=\Gamma/N\). Every orbit \(\Gamma/H\) that occurs is precisely the \(Q\)-orbit \(Q/(H/N)\). Distinct \(Q\)-orbit types are distinguished by the conjugacy classes of their point stabilizers.

**If truncated counts differ.** Choose an orbit type \(Q/J\) for which the capped multiplicities differ. For every subgroup \(L\le Q\), a one-variable quantifier-free formula \(\sigma_J(x)\) asserts \(q x=x\) for \(q\in J\) and \(q x\ne x\) for \(q\notin J\), where each \(q\) is expressed by a representative word over \(S\). For an orbit of type \(Q/J\), some points satisfy \(\sigma_J\); no orbit of another type does so. The two-variable quantifier-free formula \(\delta(x,y)=\bigwedge_{q\in Q}(q x\ne y)\) asserts that \(x,y\) lie in distinct orbits of the actions being compared. Thus, for any positive \(r\le k\), the sentence asserting the existence of \(r\) pairwise \(\delta\)-separated points satisfying \(\sigma_J\) uses exactly \(r\) variable names and says that there are at least \(r\) orbits of this type. Choose \(r\le k\) separating the two counts. The truth values differ, so the actions are not \(\mathrm{FO}^k\)-equivalent. Notice that the formulas may use \(Q\)-dependent words; the statement only requires *some* distinguishing sentence for each pair.

**If truncated counts agree.** Consider the infinite-round \(k\)-pebble game for the finite function structures. Maintain a bijection between the orbits currently occupied by pebbles in the two actions, together with a \(\Gamma\)-equivariant isomorphism on each matched pair of occupied orbits that maps all corresponding pebbled elements. When Spoiler moves a pebble, first release any orbit that now has no pebbles. If the chosen new point is in an already occupied orbit, use the maintained equivariant isomorphism (or its inverse) to reply. If it is in a fresh orbit of type \([H]\), select a fresh orbit of the same type on the other side and extend by an equivariant isomorphism carrying the new point to its mate. A total of at most \(k-1\) orbits are occupied before each move; if the type count is below \(k\), counts on both sides are identical, while if it is at least \(k\), at least one unused orbit remains on the answering side. Hence this strategy never fails. As it preserves all atomic equalities between unary-function terms in the pebbled variables, it preserves every \(\mathrm{FO}^k\)-formula by the standard pebble-game induction, and establishes the claimed equivalence.

**Exact isolating width.** Let \(A\ne\varnothing\) and put \(M=\max_{[H]}m_{[H]}(A)\). Adding one more orbit of a type occurring \(M\) times gives a nonisomorphic action \(B\) that is \(\mathrm{FO}^M\)-equivalent by the result just proved, so \(M\) variables cannot isolate \(A\). Conversely, the kernel \(N\) of the given finite \(\Gamma\)-action has finite index and is finitely generated by Schreier's finite-index subgroup theorem. Requiring each generator of \(N\) to fix every point is a finite one-variable condition and forces any competing \(\Gamma\)-set to factor through the finite quotient \(Q=\Gamma/N\). There are only finitely many orbit types for \(Q\), and, for each type, the same point-stabilizer and pairwise-distinct-orbit formulas express that its multiplicity is exactly the multiplicity in \(A\): use at least \(m\) and not at least \(m+1\), with the latter requiring at most \(M+1\) variable names. The conjunction isolates \(A\) among finite \(\Gamma\)-sets. If \(A=\varnothing\), the one-variable sentence \(\neg\exists x\,(x=x)\) suffices.

## Verification
The argument uses finite quotients for every comparison, an explicit family of distinguishing formulas, and a maintained equivariant orbit matching for the unbounded \(k\)-pebble game; these are full proofs for all finite actions, not extrapolations from finite experiments. The subgroup finiteness used in isolation is the standard Schreier theorem. As boundary checks, for the trivial group the statement specializes to the sharp \(k\)-variable distinction between sets of \(k\) and \(k+1\) elements; for a transitive nontrivial action the relative isolating width is two, independently of orbit cardinality.

## Relationship to prior work
Immerman and Lander (1990) establish the use of \(k\)-variable logic and the corresponding pebble game, including the exact obstruction supplied by \(k\) versus \(k+1\) indistinguishable isolated vertices. Feferman and Vaught (1959) investigate composition properties of first-order structures, but their full first-order product framework does not itself state the sharp *fixed-variable* multiplicity invariant for finite actions or the isolating-width formula. The present result applies that logical viewpoint to all finite actions of any finitely generated group, where transitive types are finite-index subgroups and a finite-index kernel must be imposed to isolate an action among competitors with other finite quotients. It is not a statement about graph color-refinement or counting logic.

## Limitations
The models must be genuine \(\Gamma\)-actions and the language must name finitely many generator maps with inverses expressible as words. The result does not claim finite axiomatizability of an arbitrary group's entire action theory, and does not address arbitrary infinite \(\Gamma\)-sets, other relations, counting quantifiers, or quantifier-rank bounds. The possibility of an equivalent general composition lemma in older finite-model-theory literature remains a residual originality risk.
