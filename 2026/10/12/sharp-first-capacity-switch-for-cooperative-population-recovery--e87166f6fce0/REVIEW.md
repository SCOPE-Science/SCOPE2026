# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim defines one continuous-time birth–death model with independent mate and vacancy draws and strictly positive rates. The probability of first reaching the recovery target before extinction follows from exact backward equations and a telescoping increment recurrence. The derivative \(d\log r_i(L)/dL=(L+1-2i)/((L-1)(L-i))\) proves the capacity bound uniformly in recovery target and mortality ratio. Exact coefficient comparison proves the first two singleton optima and rules out seven or more sites for target five; the quadratic \(44+226\gamma-253\gamma^2\) yields the unique switch. Independent rational backward-equation solves and coefficient checks reproduce the formulas. Finite enumeration is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS
The general birth–death recurrence is classical and is not itself the novelty. Windus and Jensen's primary paper shows qualitative habitat-reduction benefits for a *different nearest-neighbor spatial model* and simulation survival outcomes, not the present exact well-mixed all-capacity finite-target optimizer. Doering and collaborators derive extinction-time formulas, not this recovery-capacity phase theorem. Cuddington and collaborators address mate-location effects, not the same quantitative capacity tradeoff. Targeted searches of primary literature, mathematical aliases and published research records found no statement that covers the all-target cutoff plus first target-five switch. The remaining risk is an unlocated equivalent optimization theorem in general birth–death or ecological viability literature; independent auditing remains necessary.

## Value — PASS
Starting from two founders, a successful mate encounter competes with scarcity of empty sites. Optimizing the probability of ever reaching a finite rescue threshold over *all* integer habitat capacities is a natural conservation design question, and the first non-minimal optimal capacity is a substantive boundary of that tradeoff. The sharp polynomial threshold and finite universal cutoff are not merely a sampled numerical optimum. The result is a self-contained ideal-model benchmark; it does not establish recommendations for particular natural populations or permanence after first recovery.
