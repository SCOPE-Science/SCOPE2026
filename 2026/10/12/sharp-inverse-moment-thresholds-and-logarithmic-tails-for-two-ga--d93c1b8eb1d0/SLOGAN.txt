For a perfectly conditioned one-column tensor least-squares problem, a Gaussian sketch reusing two random factors has finite error moments only below \(\min(r_1,r_2)\), while independently resampling each of the same \(R=r_1r_2\) tensor rows preserves all moments below \(R\). Even the independent-row construction has an exact \(t^{-R}(\log t)^R\) error tail, versus \(t^{-R}\) for a dense Gaussian sketch, so identical row budgets can hide fundamentally different rare-error risks.
