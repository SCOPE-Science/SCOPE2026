# Sharp length-\(45\) obstruction for the palindromic formula \(\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\)
## Finding
Let \(f=\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\). Consider ternary words \(w\in\{0,1,2\}^{*}\) satisfying both conditions:

1. \(w\) is square-free;
2. \(w\) contains no occurrence \(h\) of \(f\) with
\[
|h(A)|=1,\qquad |h(B)|+|h(C)|\le 5,
\]
where \(h(B)\) and \(h(C)\) are nonempty.

Then the maximum possible length is exactly
\[
45.
\]
There are exactly \(18\) words of length \(45\) attaining the maximum. Under the group generated by arbitrary permutations of the three letters and reversal, the \(18\) extremizers form exactly two orbits, of sizes \(6\) and \(12\), represented by
\[
\mathtt{010212021012102120210201021012102120210121021}
\]
and
\[
\mathtt{012010201210120102012021201210120102012101201}.
\]

Thus the finite search certificate used in the published proof that \(\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\) has avoidability index \(4\) admits a sharp cutoff: length \(45\) is attainable, but length \(46\) is impossible.

## Assumptions and scope
A square is a nonempty factor \(uu\). A word is square-free if it has no square factor.

An occurrence of the formula \(f=\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\) is a nonerasing morphism \(h\) for which both words
\[
h(A)h(B)h(C)h(A)
\quad\text{and}\quad
h(A)h(C)h(B)h(A)
\]
occur as factors of the ambient word. The restriction studied here is exactly the finite certificate stated by Ochem and Rosenfeld: \(|h(A)|=1\) and \(|h(B)|+|h(C)|\le5\).

The claim is about this restricted certificate language, not about the longest unrestricted ternary word avoiding \(f\). The published theorem that the unrestricted formula has avoidability index \(4\) remains prior work.

## Proof
Both constraints are factorial, so every prefix of an admissible word is admissible. It is therefore enough to exhaust the prefix tree.

Since \(h(A)\) is one letter and \(h(B),h(C)\) are nonempty with total length at most \(5\), each fragment image has length between \(4\) and \(7\). There are exactly \(3834\) allowed triples \((h(A),h(B),h(C))\).

For every allowed triple write
\[
X=h(\mathtt{ABCA}),\qquad Y=h(\mathtt{ACBA}).
\]
If an admissible prefix becomes inadmissible after appending one letter, then either a new square is created or an occurrence of \(f\) is completed. Any factor created by one appended letter is a suffix. Hence a new square is detected by testing every even suffix \(uu\), while a new restricted formula occurrence is detected by testing each suffix of length \(4,5,6,7\): if the suffix equals \(X\) or \(Y\) for an allowed morphism, its paired fragment must occur somewhere in the extended word.

This suffix test is exact because any formula occurrence already present before the extension would have invalidated the previous prefix.

The complete survivor counts for lengths \(0,1,\ldots,46\) are
\[
1,3,6,12,18,30,42,54,54,60,66,78,90,96,102,108,114,126,138,150,162,174,132,120,120,120,120,120,120,126,132,132,132,132,132,126,96,90,84,78,84,66,36,30,24,18,0.
\]
Thus there are \(18\) words at length \(45\) and none at length \(46\). Factoriality then rules out every longer word.

For an independent check, a second enumerator ignores the suffix-map invariant. For every candidate it scans every factor for squares and directly tests all \(3834\) allowed morphisms by checking whether both fragment images occur. The two enumerators produce identical survivor sets at every length through \(46\).

Finally, applying all six alphabet permutations and reversal to the \(18\) boundary words gives exactly two orbits, of sizes \(6\) and \(12\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ochem and Rosenfeld proved that the palindromic pattern \(\mathtt{ABCADACBA}\), equivalently the formula \(\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\), has avoidability index \(4\). Their proof reduces a hypothetical recurrent ternary avoider to the square-free case and then states that a computer check rules out an infinite ternary square-free word even when only occurrences with \(|h(A)|=1\) and \(|h(B)|+|h(C)|\le5\) are forbidden.

That published statement is qualitative: it asserts that the restricted square-free language has no infinite branch. It does not state the maximum finite length, the number of longest words, or their symmetry types. The present result determines all three exactly.

A later paper by the same authors revisits \(\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\) as the established lower-bound example for palindrome-pattern avoidability and strengthens a related three-fragment formula, but it does not give a finite cutoff for the earlier restricted certificate.

Targeted searches for the exact value \(45\), the two boundary representatives, the palindromic-pattern name, and the restricted morphism inequalities located the original and follow-up papers but no published finite extremizer classification.

## Limitations
The value \(45\) concerns the restricted square-free certificate language used in the published proof. It is not asserted to be the maximum length of an arbitrary ternary word avoiding the unrestricted formula \(\mathtt{ABCA.ACBA}\).

The originality search cannot exclude unpublished backtracking output or an unindexed auxiliary file from the original computation. The proof here is independently reproducible from the definition and does not depend on source code from the prior papers.
