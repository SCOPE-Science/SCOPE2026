# Sharp length bound and symmetry types for ternary level-four Simon normal forms

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\). Write \(u\sim_4v\) when the two finite words have exactly the same scattered subsequences of lengths at most four. For a congruence class \(C\), set \(\ell(C)=\min\{|w|:w\in C\}\). Then
\[
\max_{C\in\Sigma^*/\!\sim_4}\ell(C)=18.
\]
Precisely eighteen classes achieve this maximum. Under the action generated by alphabet permutations and word reversal, the eighteen extremal classes split into exactly three orbits of six classes each. They are represented by

- `001110022220011100`;
- `001110022221100011`;
- `001110222000211122`.

This is a single sharp shortest-representative classification for the ternary, level-four quotient; it makes no claim of a general formula for arbitrary alphabet size or congruence level.

## Assumptions and scope
A scattered subsequence is obtained by deleting arbitrary positions without changing the order of the retained positions. We count the empty word as a class representative, but omit it from the binary signature because it occurs in every word. For \(w\in\Sigma^*\), let
\[
T(w)=\{u\in\Sigma^j:\ 1\le j\le4,\ u\text{ is a scattered subsequence of }w\}.
\]
There are \(3+3^2+3^3+3^4=120\) possible nonempty entries. Thus a signature is a subset of this 120-element set; equality of signatures is exactly \(\sim_4\)-equivalence. The stated action is by relabeling all letters simultaneously and by reversing the entire word; both operations preserve congruence and shortest-class length.

## Proof
**Exact transition lemma.** For every \(w\in\Sigma^*\) and every letter \(a\in\Sigma\),
\[
T(wa)=T(w)\cup\{a\}\cup\{ua:u\in T(w),\ |u|\le3\}.
\]
Indeed, a newly acquired nonempty scattered subsequence must use the appended last position; deleting its final letter gives either the empty word or an old subsequence of length at most three. Conversely, each displayed subsequence occurs in \(wa\). This proves both inclusions, without assumptions on the length of \(w\).

**Complete finite quotient computation.** Encode \(T(w)\) as a 120-bit unsigned integer with one fixed bit per possible subsequence. Begin with \(T(\varepsilon)=\varnothing\). Breadth-first search applies the proved transition to every reached signature for all three possible appended letters. Because every finite word is obtainable by successive appends, induction on word length proves that this search, once its queue is empty, has reached *every* realizable signature and hence every \(\sim_4\)-class. Breadth-first discovery makes the first word assigned to each signature a shortest representative. Processing the three letters in ascending order additionally chooses the shortlex representative.

**Orbit classification.** Enumerate the six permutations of the alphabet and the reversal of each image of the three displayed representatives. Convert each resulting word to its signature, then look up its shortlex representative in the exhaustive search. Each seed yields exactly six distinct extremal classes; the three sets are disjoint, and their union is exactly the set of all eighteen extremal signatures. This is an exhaustive certificate for the three-orbit statement, not an inference from visual patterns.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. The program implements the transition lemma directly, checks all \(1002606\) directed transitions, the complete eighteen-entry depth histogram, all eighteen extremal classes, and their three size-six symmetry orbits. As a separate check, each extremal word's 120-bit signature is recomputed using combinations of actual positions rather than the append recurrence. All eighteen also satisfy the published shortest-word ranker test: at every position, the shortest forward-ranker length plus shortest backward-ranker length equals \(5\), which confirms their length-minimality independently of the breadth-first distances. The all-class upper bound comes from the complete transition closure, not the finite list of witnesses alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Pach's 2017 online paper gives general length-minimal normal forms for Simon's congruence, building on his earlier level-four construction; Fleischer and Kufleitner (arXiv:1804.10459v1, 27 April 2018) give a linear-time shortlex normalization algorithm and the ranker minimality test used as an independent witness check. Fleischmann, Höfer, Huch and Nowotka (arXiv:2306.14192) solve the binary-class problem and study ternary factorization phenomena but do not state this ternary level-four depth census in the inspected text. A 2013 public MathOverflow computation already reports the index \(334202\); the index itself is **not** new here, and alone does not determine shortest-representative depths or orbit counts. The claimed contribution is the sharp eighteen-symbol universal bound and complete classification of its extremal classes.

## Limitations
The result concerns exactly the three-symbol alphabet and subsequences of lengths at most four. The finite certificate establishes no asymptotic bounds as the alphabet or level varies, and it does not classify every congruence class beyond the depth histogram. An earlier Pach level-four normal-form paper is available only through an indexed PDF preview in the present literature comparison; a hidden equivalent finite-depth computation there or in unindexed material remains a priority risk. Computational enumeration has been checked from the supplied executable, but no independent external audit has been performed.
