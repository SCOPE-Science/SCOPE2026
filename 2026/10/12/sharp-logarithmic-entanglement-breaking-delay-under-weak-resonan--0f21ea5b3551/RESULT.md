# Sharp logarithmic entanglement-breaking delay under weak resonant driving

## Finding
Let \(\Phi_{\Omega,t}=\exp(t\mathcal L_\Omega)\) be the qubit channel generated by spontaneous emission at rate one and a constant resonant transverse drive of amplitude \(\Omega\ge0\):
\[
\mathcal L_\Omega(X)=-i[(\Omega/2)\sigma_x,X]+\sigma_-X\sigma_+-\tfrac12\{\sigma_+\sigma_-,X\},
\qquad \sigma_-=|0\rangle\langle1|.
\]
Define \(T_{\rm EB}(\Omega)=\inf\{t\ge0:\Phi_{\Omega,t}\text{ is entanglement breaking}\}\), allowing an infinite value. Then \(T_{\rm EB}(0)=+\infty\), \(T_{\rm EB}(\Omega)<\infty\) for every \(\Omega>0\), and
\[
\boxed{\ T_{\rm EB}(\Omega)=4\log(1/\Omega)+o(1)\quad (\Omega\downarrow0).\ }
\]
More precisely, let \(J_{\Omega,t}=(\Phi_{\Omega,t}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\Phi_2\rangle\langle\Phi_2|)\) be the normalized Choi state with \(|\Phi_2\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Denote by \(\Gamma\) partial transposition of its reference factor, and by \(\lambda_{\min}\) the lowest eigenvalue. For each fixed real \(s\),
\[
\boxed{\ \lim_{\Omega\downarrow0}\Omega^{-4}\lambda_{\min}\!\left(J_{\Omega,\,4\log(1/\Omega)+s}^{\Gamma}\right)=\tfrac12(1-e^{-s}).\ }
\]
The sharp boundary profile implies the entanglement-breaking-time statement without an unspecified additive constant.

## Assumptions and scope
All matrices act on \(\mathbb C^2\). We use \(\sigma_x=|0\rangle\langle1|+|1\rangle\langle0|\), \(\sigma_y=-i|0\rangle\langle1|+i|1\rangle\langle0|\), and \(\sigma_z=|0\rangle\langle0|-|1\rangle\langle1|\). The decay rate and \(\hbar\) are one; times are dimensionless. The drive is coherent and continuous, not an instantaneous channel rotation or periodic reset. Entanglement breaking refers to loss of entanglement with an arbitrary reference for all possible inputs, equivalently separability of the normalized Choi state. Qubit-channel Choi separability is equivalent to positivity of its partial transpose. The asymptotic claim concerns \(\Omega\to0^+\) and each fixed finite \(s\); it does not assert a uniform approximation for \(s\) growing with \(\Omega^{-1}\).

## Proof
Write \(X=(I+x\sigma_x+y\sigma_y+z\sigma_z)/2\) for a state. The Bloch equation generated by \(\mathcal L_\Omega\) is \(\dot r=A_\Omega r+b\), where
\[
A_\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac12&0&0\\0&-\tfrac12&-\Omega\\0&\Omega&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=(0,0,1)^T.
\]
Its unique fixed Bloch vector is
\[
r_\infty=\left(0,-\frac{2\Omega}{1+2\Omega^2},\frac1{1+2\Omega^2}\right)^T.
\]
The stationary density matrix has determinant \(\det\rho_\infty=\Omega^4/(1+2\Omega^2)^2\), positive for \(\Omega>0\). The two-dimensional \(y,z\) block has trace \(-3/2\) and determinant \(1/2+\Omega^2\), so its eigenvalues have negative real parts, as does the independent \(x\) eigenvalue \(-1/2\). Therefore the affine map has Bloch form \(r\mapsto T r+c\) with \(T=e^{tA_\Omega}\), \(c=(I-T)r_\infty\), and converges exponentially at each fixed positive \(\Omega\) to the constant channel \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(X)\rho_\infty\). Its limiting Choi partial transpose is \(\rho_\infty\otimes I/2\), which is positive definite. The PPT criterion consequently proves finite \(T_{\rm EB}(\Omega)\).

For \(\Omega=0\), direct solution is the ordinary amplitude-damping channel. Its partially transposed normalized Choi state has eigenvalue \(-e^{-t}/2\) at each finite \(t\), establishing \(T_{\rm EB}(0)=\infty\).

To calculate the sharp small-drive boundary, fix \(s\in\mathbb R\), put \(\varepsilon=\Omega\), \(q=e^{-s/2}>0\), and set \(t=4\log(1/\varepsilon)+s\). In the \(y,z\) block the two eigenvalues of \(A_\varepsilon\) are
\[
\mu_\pm=-\frac34\pm\sqrt{\frac1{16}-\varepsilon^2},\qquad
\mu_+=-\frac12-2\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4),\qquad
\mu_-=-1+2\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4).
\]
For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), these distinct real eigenvalues yield the exact spectral projectors \(P_\pm=(B_\varepsilon-\mu_\mp I)/(\mu_\pm-\mu_\mp)\), with \(B_\varepsilon\) the displayed \(y,z\) block. Because \(\varepsilon^2|\log\varepsilon|\to0\), substitution in \(e^{tB_\varepsilon}=e^{t\mu_+}P_++e^{t\mu_-}P_-\) gives the entries needed through their stated orders:
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{xx}&=\varepsilon^2q,\\
T_{yy}&=\varepsilon^2q+O(\varepsilon^4|\log\varepsilon|),\\
T_{yz}&=-2\varepsilon^3q+o(\varepsilon^3),\quad
T_{zy}=2\varepsilon^3q+o(\varepsilon^3),\\
T_{zz}&=\varepsilon^4(q^2-4q)+o(\varepsilon^4).
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, \(e^{t\mu_+}=\varepsilon^2q(1+O(\varepsilon^2|\log\varepsilon|))\), while \(e^{t\mu_-}=\varepsilon^4q^2(1+O(\varepsilon^2|\log\varepsilon|))\). Inserting these entries into \(c=(I-T)r_\infty\), with the rational stationary vector above, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_x&=0,\\
c_y&=-2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^3(4+4q)+o(\varepsilon^3),\\
c_z&=1-2\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon^4(4+8q-q^2)+o(\varepsilon^4).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here each error term is with \(s\), hence \(q\), fixed. No finite-time approximation at \(\varepsilon=0\) has been substituted for the joint limit.

The normalized Choi partial transpose, in output-first tensor order, has the exact Pauli expansion
\[
J_{\varepsilon,t}^{\Gamma}=\frac14\left[(I+c\cdot\sigma)\otimes I+\sum_{i,j\in\{x,y,z\}}T_{ij}\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j\right].
\]
Partition this Hermitian \(4\times4\) matrix into two-dimensional blocks by the output qubit, writing \(J^\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}U&V\\V^\dagger&W\end{pmatrix}\). From the preceding expansions, \(U=I/2+O(\varepsilon^2)\) is invertible, \(V=O(\varepsilon)\), and the Schur complement \(K=W-V^\dagger U^{-1}V\) obeys
\[
\varepsilon^{-4}K=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1-q^2&0\\0&1+q^2\end{pmatrix}+o(1).
\]
For reproducibility, this identity follows by inserting the displayed coefficients of \(T\) and \(c\) into the explicit Pauli matrices; the accompanying finite-dimensional checker reconstructs the Choi matrix independently. To pass from the Schur complement to the small eigenvalues, for \(|\lambda|=O(\varepsilon^4)\) replace \(U^{-1}\) by \((U-\lambda I)^{-1}\) in the eigenvalue Schur equation. The difference has norm \(O(\varepsilon^6)\), since \(\|V\|=O(\varepsilon)\). The other two eigenvalues of \(J^\Gamma\) tend to \(1/2\); the two small eigenvalues are therefore \(\varepsilon^4(1\mp q^2)/2+o(\varepsilon^4)\). In particular the smallest scaled eigenvalue tends to \((1-q^2)/2=(1-e^{-s})/2\).

For fixed \(s<0\), that eigenvalue is negative for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), so \(\Phi_{\varepsilon,t}\) is not entanglement breaking. For fixed \(s>0\), all four partial-transpose eigenvalues are positive, so it is entanglement breaking. Finally, the semigroup property gives \(\Phi_{\Omega,t+u}=\Phi_{\Omega,u}\circ\Phi_{\Omega,t}\), and postprocessing cannot undo an entanglement-breaking channel. Thus the set of entanglement-breaking times is an upper interval; the two fixed-sign comparisons for arbitrary small positive \(s\) and negative \(s\) squeeze \(T_{\rm EB}(\varepsilon)-4\log(1/\varepsilon)\) to zero.

## Verification
The companion `verify.py` constructs the Lindblad superoperator in the four matrix-unit basis elements, exponentiates it numerically, builds the normalized Choi state and partially transposes the reference qubit. It checks the predicted scaled smallest eigenvalue at three positive drive strengths and two fixed offsets, checks the location of the earliest entanglement-breaking transition numerically at those strengths, and checks negative partial-transpose eigenvalues at zero drive. Run `python verify.py` with `requirements.txt` installed. These are finite-dimensional numerical corroborations, not a proof of the limit: the limiting statement follows from the analytic eigenprojector, Schur-complement and semigroup arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Driven Bloch relaxation for a resonantly forced dissipative qubit is analyzed by Zhou, Chu and Han (2006); the Bloch generator itself is not novel. Horodecki, Shor and Ruskai established the general Choi characterization of entanglement-breaking channels. Lami and Giovannetti studied entanglement-breaking indices and ordinary thermal generalized-amplitude-damping channels, including zero-temperature behavior, but their channel iteration without continuous coherent driving does not imply the present weak-drive profile. Hanson, Rouzé and França developed general eventual entanglement-breaking criteria for primitive semigroups; their criterion explains finite-time breaking for each fixed positive drive but does not supply the singular \(4\log(1/\Omega)+o(1)\) threshold. Bullock and collaborators studied undriven amplitude damping and environment-reset protocols rather than the joint weak-resonant-drive/long-time boundary derived here. Originality is assessed only relative to the literature inspected; undiscovered equivalent analyses remain possible.

## Limitations
Only a single qubit, exact resonance, continuous coherent transverse driving and zero-temperature Markovian spontaneous emission are treated. The result does not provide a closed finite-drive formula for the entire transition curve, handle detuning or extra dephasing, nor give an estimate uniform over unbounded offsets \(s\). The analytic proof makes no inference about finite precision experiments from the existence of an entanglement-breaking time. The possible existence of a previously published equivalent weak-drive specialization remains a research-priority risk.
