# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof is coefficientwise in \(\mathbb Z[[x]]\). Freshman's dream gives \(R_j\in1+p\mathbb Z[[x]]\); the elementary power-lifting lemma gives the precise factorwise moduli; the telescoping exponents are exact; division is only by formal units with constant term \(1\). For sharpness, the first nonconstant coefficient of the last ratio is isolated at degree \(p^{a-1}\), while all earlier factors contribute an additional p-adic order and \(B_a^{p^a}\) starts later. The embedded exact-integer verifier agrees on seven low-parameter cases. Risk: the computation samples only finite degrees, but no finite sample is used to justify the universal proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary source, arXiv:2610.01422v1, proves only modulus \(p^a\) for general \(t=p^a\), separately proves \(p^2\) for the prime case, and explicitly suggests a possible higher p-adic lift. The present statement gives \(p^{a+1}\) for every prime power and proves that exponent optimal via a uniform explicit witness. Searches for the same object under hook-length, t-core, formal-series, prime-power, and p-adic-lift formulations did not locate a theorem with this implication. Han--Ono 2011 concerns zero-equivalence for \(t=3\), not uniform coefficient congruences of this strength. Residual novelty risk remains because absence from searched literature cannot prove global nonexistence.

## Value
PASS. The finding answers the motivating paper's explicit higher-p-adic-lift direction in a uniform way and determines the exact p-adic strength rather than merely adding an isolated congruence. The sharp coefficient \(n=p^{a-1}\) shows the improvement cannot be iterated without extra hypotheses, giving a natural boundary as well as a positive theorem.
