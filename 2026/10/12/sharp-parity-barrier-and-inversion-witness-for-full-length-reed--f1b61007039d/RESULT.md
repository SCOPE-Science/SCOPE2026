# Sharp parity barrier and inversion witness for full-length Reed–Solomon subspace distances

## Finding
For every odd prime power \(q\ge5\), put \(k=(q+1)/2\) and let \(\mathcal R_q\) be the set of distinct, unscaled \([q,k]_q\) Reed–Solomon subspaces obtained by evaluating degree-\(<k\) polynomials at all elements of \(\mathbb F_q\) in arbitrary coordinate orders. With \(d_S(U,V)=\dim U+\dim V-2\dim(U\cap V)\), the exact diameter is
\[
\max_{U\ne V\in\mathcal R_q}d_S(U,V)=q-3.
\]
In particular, distinct members cannot intersect only in the constant vectors: a one-dimensional-center \((q,k,q;1)\) Reed–Solomon sunflower contains at most one distinct code.

At the first instance \(q=5\), there are precisely six distinct members of \(\mathcal R_5\); any two intersect in dimension two, while any three distinct members intersect precisely in the one-dimensional constant subspace. The six dual-normal projective points form the nonsingular six-point conic \(X^2+Y^2+Z^2+XY+XZ+YZ=0\) over \(\mathbb F_5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field order \(q\) is an odd prime power at least five, not necessarily prime. A code \(C_\alpha\) means \(\{(f(\alpha_x))_{x\in\mathbb F_q}:\deg f<k\}\), where \(\alpha\) is any permutation of the field, so coordinates are indexed by a fixed field enumeration. No nontrivial coordinate multipliers are allowed. Distances are between code subspaces, not between individual codewords under Hamming distance. Identical evaluation codes are identified as a single member.

## Proof
For every integer \(j\) with \(0\le j\le k-1\), the finite-field power sum \(\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}x^j\) vanishes. For \(j=0\), the sum is \(q=0\) in the field. For \(1\le j\le k-1<q-1\), multiply the sum by a nonzero field element whose \(j\)-th power is not one, or use the cyclicity of \(\mathbb F_q^\times\). Thus each \(C_\alpha\) lies in the same single-parity-check hyperplane \(H=\{v\in\mathbb F_q^q:\sum_xv_x=0\}\), of dimension \(q-1=2k-2\). By the dimension formula, any two \(k\)-spaces in \(H\) intersect in dimension at least \(2k-(q-1)=2\). Their subspace distance is therefore at most \(2(k-2)=q-3\), excluding a one-dimensional center.

For equality, index the coordinates by \(x\in\mathbb F_q\) and compare the evaluation vectors \(\alpha_x=x\) and \(\beta_x=x^{q-2}\), with \(\beta_0=0\). The second is a permutation because it inverts each nonzero field element. The first generator is the evaluations of \(1,x,\ldots,x^{k-1}\). The second generator is the evaluations of \(1,x^{q-2},x^{q-3},\ldots,x^{q-k}\): on nonzero inputs this follows from inversion; at zero each positive monomial vanishes on both sides. Since \(q=2k-1\), the displayed exponent sets intersect exactly in \(\{0,k-1\}\). Evaluations of the monomials of degrees \(0,1,\ldots,q-2\) are linearly independent: a polynomial of degree at most \(q-2\) cannot vanish at all \(q\) field elements unless it is zero. Therefore \(C_\alpha\cap C_\beta\) is exactly the two-dimensional span of the evaluation vectors of \(1\) and \(x^{k-1}\), and their distance is \(q-3\).

For the first-instance refinement, specialize to \(q=5,k=3\). Two evaluation permutations give the same degree-\(<3\) code exactly when one is obtained from the other by an affine field map: the dual code is spanned by \(\mathbf1\) and the evaluation vector \(\alpha\), since the first and second power sums vanish. Hence normalize each permutation uniquely to start with \((0,1)\). There are \(3!=6\) normalized tuples \((0,1,a,b,c)\), with \((a,b,c)\) a permutation of \((2,3,4)\). The common parity hyperplane has dimension four, and the annihilator of each three-space within it is represented by its normalized projective class \((1,a,b)\). These six points all satisfy \(Q(X,Y,Z)=X^2+Y^2+Z^2+XY+XZ+YZ=0\). The polar matrix for \(2Q\) is \(I+J\), of determinant \(4\) modulo five, so this conic is nonsingular; a nonsingular conic over \(\mathbb F_5\) has exactly six rational points and no three are collinear. Thus any two distinct annihilating hyperplanes inside the four-space are independent and their primal code intersection has dimension two; any three have independent annihilators modulo the shared constant vector and the primal intersection has dimension one. This proves every asserted incidence and enumeration, without relying on a computer census.

## Relationship to prior work
Con, Gruica, Montanucci and Zullo's *Sunflowers of Reed–Solomon Codes* (arXiv:2609.33512v1, 27 September 2026) introduces the relevant objects and subspace metric, gives the generalized \(V\)-matrix criterion, and determines the size of dimension-two Reed–Solomon sunflowers. The present boundary instead couples the **field order, full length and growing middle dimension** by \(\ell=q=2k-1\). Their construction and greedy asymptotics are for fixed \(k,\ell\) while the field grows; they do not imply a sunflower here. Its dual dimension-two counting result already explains the six distinct quintary codes and, with an extra duality argument, their pairwise intersection dimension; neither of those components alone is claimed as new. The new boundary result is the sharp all-odd-prime-power distance formula with an explicit inversion witness, together with the complete three-way quintary intersection/conic profile.

## Limitations
The conclusion is restricted to full-length **unscaled** Reed–Solomon evaluation codes and \(q\) odd with \(k=(q+1)/2\). It does not extend automatically to generalized Reed–Solomon codes with coordinate multipliers, shorter evaluation lists, or even field orders. The elementary projective conic and parity-space ingredients are classical; the assertion of novelty is the combined specialized sharp distance theorem and its incidence interpretation. Independent scientific review has not been performed.
