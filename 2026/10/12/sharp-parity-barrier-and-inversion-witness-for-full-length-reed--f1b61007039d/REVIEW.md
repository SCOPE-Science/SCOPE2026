# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The common parity-check hyperplane has dimension \(q-1=2k-2\) for every odd prime power \(q=2k-1\), so two \(k\)-spaces intersect in dimension at least two. Field inversion yields a pair of polynomial-evaluation spans whose exponent overlap is exactly two monomials; the evaluation root bound proves this overlap is all of the intersection. The quintary conic has nonsingular polar matrix, giving the exact pair/triple pattern. Executable finite checks reproduce the principal witness and the complete first instance.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** Con–Gruica–Montanucci–Zullo (arXiv:2609.33512v1) already introduce Reed–Solomon sunflowers and determine their dimension-two sizes. Their results cover the six-code count in the first quintary instance and can be combined with duality to recover the pairwise dimension, so those are not independently claimed novel. Their inspected complete preprint does not state the growing-dimension full-length obstruction, the sharp distance \(q-3\), its inversion witness, or the ternary-intersection conic classification. Semantic index and public literature searches found no equivalent or stronger result; unnoticed classical or unindexed prior formulations remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** The sharp distance is the precise obstruction at the diagonal boundary \(\ell=q=2k-1\) of the new Reed–Solomon sunflower question; it distinguishes full-length growing-dimension geometry from the fixed-length asymptotic existence regime and supplies an explicit distance-optimal pair for every odd prime power. The six-code conic gives the complete smallest-instance intersection profile rather than only a cardinality computation.

The result is restricted to unscaled full-length codes and is not a theorem about arbitrary MDS or generalized Reed–Solomon codes. Standard finite-field and projective-conic ingredients are acknowledged. An independently discovered older equivalent proof is a residual originality risk.
