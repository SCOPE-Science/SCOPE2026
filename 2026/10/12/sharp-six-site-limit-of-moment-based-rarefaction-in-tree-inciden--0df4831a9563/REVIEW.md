# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The event that a species is unobserved is exactly the event that its two habitat endpoints both lie among the unselected sites. Two distinct species share either three or four habitat endpoints, giving respectively \(p_3\) and \(p_4\) as their joint missing probabilities. Enumerating the \(W\) adjacent edge pairs yields the stated conditional variance with exact normalization. The positive coefficient for sample sizes \(1\le k\le n-3\) makes moment-equivalence precisely wedge equivalence. Tree excess degrees prove unique path/star bounds. The small-tree degree classification and explicit six-site, all-orders counterfamily prove the sharp onset. Direct all-subset enumeration over 18,244 labeled trees through order seven and separate family checks through order fifteen returned `VERIFY_OK`. No infinite conclusion is inferred from that finite experiment.

## Originality — PASS, with specified residual risk

Ugland–Gray–Ellingsen (2003), DOI 10.1046/j.1365-2656.2003.00748.x, already develop general conditional combinatorial richness variance; that formula is acknowledged and excluded from the novelty claim. Colwell–Mao–Chang (2004), DOI 10.1890/03-0557, provide complementary interpolation theory and distinguish estimator variance from conditional permutation variance. Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky, arXiv:0812.4147, develop a stronger full induced-subgraph component polynomial and distinguish trees through nine vertices using it; their full-distribution uniqueness is compatible with the present *moment-only* six-vertex ambiguity. The prior graph-degree extremum is also explicitly covered by Ferrer-i-Cancho, arXiv:1305.4561 (Section 2 and Appendix B). Therefore the path/star inequality is **not** counted independently as novel: the distinguishing contribution is the exact moment-information boundary and persistent ambiguity construction. The checked complete works do not state or imply that six sites are the first tree-incidence size at which every richness mean and variance agrees despite different full laws. Database searches cannot rule out unindexed degree-statistics or sampling-polynomial specializations; this is an explicit residual risk, not a proof of universal novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete sharp statement answers a natural inferential question for sample-based biodiversity accumulation: which aspects of a minimally connected, two-site-species habitat system are recoverable from the entire mean-and-variance rarefaction curves, and how soon can those curves mask a genuinely different chance of observing a given richness? The six-site cutoff and all-order witnesses show a consequential limitation of moment-based descriptions, while the exact simultaneous variance envelopes quantify what site configuration changes can achieve without changing the sampling budget. The tree-degree inequalities are elementary, but the combined ecological inference boundary is not merely a standalone rearrangement or table recomputation.

## Scope and evidence risks

Primary domain: uniform finite-site conditional rarefaction for minimally connected distinct-two-site species incidences (MSC2020 92D40). Do not interpret the conditional variability as estimator uncertainty, or extend the theorem without proof to more than two sites per species, multi-species duplicate habitat pairs, disconnected networks, unequal site inclusion probabilities, or nonlinear biodiversity indices. Author-hosted full text of the most relevant 2004 paper and the complete 2008 graph-polynomial preprint were inspected; residual unindexed/general-polynomial-prior-art risk remains.
