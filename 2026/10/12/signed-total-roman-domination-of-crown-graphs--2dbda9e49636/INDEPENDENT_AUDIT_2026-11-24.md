For any shore z with sum Z, the opposite open sums are Z−z_i. An all-negative shore is infeasible, so each sum is ≥2 and is ≥3 if the shore has a 2. A sum 2 means no 2, an even shore size n≥4, and at least one −1. Its opponent needs a 2 to defend this −1 and cannot itself contain −1; its sum is then ≥n+1, so the two sums cannot total ≤6. Hence total weight ≥6 and equality requires sums (3,3). At sums (3,3) every open sum is automatically ≥1. Two 2s per shore at odd n, or a shared unique 2 index at even n, give the stated feasible constructions. The two linear equations give a,b in terms of c.
