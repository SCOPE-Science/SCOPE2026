# Singular-component census of two-odd-cycle edge hypersurfaces

## Finding
Let \(a,b\ge1\) and \(\ell\ge0\) be integers. Construct \(G_{a,b,\ell}\) from simple cycles of lengths \(2a+1\) and \(2b+1\): for \(\ell=0\) identify one vertex of each cycle, and for \(\ell\ge1\) join distinct cycle vertices by a path of \(\ell\) edges with otherwise disjoint interior. Let \(X_{a,b,\ell}\subset\mathbb P^{E-1}_{\mathbb C}\), where \(E=2a+2b+\ell+2\), be the projective edge-monomial variety with homogeneous coordinates indexed by graph edges. This is an integral hypersurface of dimension \(E-2\).

There is a partition of its edge coordinates into two disjoint monomial supports, each with exponents in \(\{1,2\}\), for which the defining binomial is \(F=M_+-M_-\). Write \(s,t\) for the respective numbers of exponent-one variables and \(u,v\) for the numbers of exponent-two variables. The support statistics may be chosen as
\[
(s,t,u,v)=\begin{cases}
(a+b+1,a+b+1,\ell/2,\ell/2),&\ell\text{ even},\\
(a+b+2,a+b,(\ell-1)/2,(\ell+1)/2),&\ell\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

**Complete reduced singular-locus classification.** Choose independently on each side either a single exponent-two coordinate or an unordered pair of distinct exponent-one coordinates; set the selected coordinates to zero. Every resulting projective coordinate-linear subspace is an irreducible component of \(\operatorname{Sing}(X_{a,b,\ell})_{\mathrm{red}}\), all components arise exactly once in this way, and there are no other singular components. The numbers \(N_j\) of irreducible components of codimension \(j\) *inside the hypersurface* are
\[
N_1=uv=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell^2}{4}\right\rfloor,\qquad
N_2=u\binom{t}{2}+v\binom{s}{2},\qquad
N_3=\binom{s}{2}\binom{t}{2}.
\]
All these components are linear spaces of dimension \(E-j-2\), and no other codimensions occur. At a general complex point of a codimension-one, -two, or -three component, respectively, the transverse *complex analytic* equations, up to smooth factors and analytic coordinate changes, are
\[
xy=0,\qquad z^2-xy=0,\qquad xy-zw=0.
\]
Thus generic divisorial singularities are ordinary two-branch crossings; generic codimension-two strata are surface \(A_1\) singularities and generic codimension-three strata are ordinary threefold nodes. In particular, \(X_{a,b,\ell}\) is normal if and only if \(\ell\le1\). For \(\ell\ge2\), the exact number of irreducible divisorial components of its nonnormal locus is \(\lfloor\ell^2/4\rfloor\), independent of the two odd cycle lengths.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\) and the graph is finite, simple, connected, and contains no further edges beyond its two odd cycles and joining path. The case \(\ell=0\) denotes a one-vertex wedge, *not* two disjoint cycles. The projective variety is defined as the closure of the monomial parametrization \([t_v]\mapsto[t_pt_q]_{\{p,q\}\in E(G)}\); the term “edge-monomial toric variety” does **not** assume normality. Codimensions refer to \(X_{a,b,\ell}\), not the ambient projective space. Generic local normal forms are asserted only at general complex points where all unselected edge coordinates are nonzero.

## Proof
**Step 1 — unique primitive relation.** The unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix \(A_G\) has full row rank \(|V(G)|\) over \(\mathbb Q\): a left-kernel vector would satisfy \(c_p+c_q=0\) along every edge; traversing either odd cycle forces the starting value to vanish, and connectedness propagates zero to all vertices. In every case \(|E(G)|=|V(G)|+1\), so \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_G\) has rank one. Traverse the first odd cycle, then the joining path, then the second odd cycle, and finally the same path backwards. Alternating signs on this even closed walk give an integral relation \(\rho\) with coefficient \(\pm1\) on each cycle edge and \(\pm2\) on each path edge (the latter cancel from the vector when \(\ell=0\), because there is no path). A cycle coefficient has absolute value one, so \(\rho\) is primitive. Since the integer kernel is saturated of rank one, it is generated by \(\rho\). The edge-monomial kernel is therefore the principal prime binomial ideal \((M_+-M_-)\) from the positive and negative parts of \(\rho\). Its ordinary homogeneity follows from summing all incidence equations (each edge is counted twice), giving \(\sum_e\rho_e=0\).

**Step 2 — parity inventory.** If \(\ell=2k\), each cycle contributes its larger alternating class to a different side, so each monomial contains exactly \(a+b+1\) cycle-edge factors and \(k\) squared path variables. If \(\ell=2k+1\), the larger alternating classes of the two cycles lie on the same side; that monomial has \(a+b+2\) cycle-edge factors and \(k\) squared path variables, whereas the other has \(a+b\) cycle-edge factors and \(k+1\) squared path variables. This proves the displayed inventory for all \(\ell\ge0\), including the two boundary cases \(\ell=0,1\).

**Step 3 — exact Jacobian supports.** The supports of \(M_+\) and \(M_-\) are disjoint. Thus a gradient component associated with a variable on one side is simply the corresponding partial derivative of that monomial. For any monomial in distinct variables with exponents either one or two, *all* partial derivatives vanish at a point exactly when either (i) some exponent-two variable vanishes or (ii) at least two distinct variables vanish. Indeed, with no zero variable at least one derivative is nonzero, and with exactly one zero variable of exponent one its derivative is nonzero; all other zero patterns annihilate every derivative. The inclusion-minimal coordinate-zero sets satisfying this condition are precisely singletons of squared variables and pairs of unit-exponent variables. Taking one minimal zero set from each disjoint monomial support gives precisely the gradient-zero locus of \(F\). Since \(F\) is homogeneous in characteristic zero, Euler's identity also puts \(F\) in its gradient ideal; in particular the gradient-zero locus lies on the hypersurface. Each such set defines a projective coordinate subspace contained in \(X_{a,b,\ell}\); these subspaces are mutually incomparable and hence are *all* irreducible components of its reduced singular locus.

**Step 4 — dimensions and transverse types.** A selected singleton has one coordinate and a selected pair has two; consequently the three possible cardinalities are two, three, and four. A coordinate subspace defined by \(j+1\) vanishing coordinates has dimension \(E-j-2\), hence codimension \(j\) in the hypersurface. Independent choices from the two supports yield respectively \(uv\), \(u\binom t2+v\binom s2\), and \(\binom s2\binom t2\) components. At a general point on a component, every unselected coordinate is nonzero, so its factor in \(F\) is an analytic unit. Locally absorbing these units into the zero coordinates (taking analytic square roots where an exponent is two) yields respectively \(u_1^2-u_2^2\), \(z^2-xy\), and \(xy-zw\), times a smooth factor. Over \(\mathbb C\), the first equation is analytically equivalent to \(xy=0\). This verifies the stated generic analytic types.

**Step 5 — normality and sharp divisorial count.** An integral hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay and satisfies Serre's condition \(S_2\). By the Jacobian classification, its codimension-one singular locus has exactly \(uv\) components. Serre's criterion \(R_1+S_2\) gives normality if and only if \(uv=0\), which is equivalent to \(\ell\le1\). Substituting the parity inventory gives \(uv=\lfloor\ell^2/4\rfloor\) for every nonnegative \(\ell\), establishing the sharp count.

## Verification
Run `python3 checker.py` in the directory containing the provided standalone files; no third-party Python dependencies are required. The checker reconstructs the even closed walk and its primitive incidence-kernel relation, evaluates each polynomial partial derivative on every binary coordinate-zero support for fifteen small graph instances, and compares all inclusion-minimal singular supports with the predicted choices. It checked \(27{,}328\) binary support patterns across parameter families \((a,b)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,2)\) with joining lengths respectively \(0\ldots5\), \(0\ldots4\), and \(0\ldots3\). The exact output is stored in `checker_results.json`. The infinite classification is proved in Steps 1–5; finite tests alone cannot establish it.

## Relationship to prior work
Bermejo, García-Marco and Reyes (arXiv:1210.1950, first posted 2012-10-06) develop graph-theoretic characterizations and generators of complete-intersection toric ideals, with primary MSC 14M25. Their introduction and Section 6, including Theorem 6.19 quoting the established odd-cycle normality criterion, provide background for the principal binomial and normality boundary; neither the component counts nor the three transverse singularity types are asserted there. Tatakis and Thoma (arXiv:1110.1059) study complete-intersection toric ideals of graphs and their generators, which is relevant prior context but does not supply inspected full-text evidence for this singular-stratum count. Epure and Schulze (DOI:10.1112/blms.12614) develop a much broader theory of hypersurfaces with monomial Jacobian ideals and their normal-crossing singular loci. Their structural framework explains why monomial-support methods are natural here, but their cited theorems do not determine the explicit graph-dependent stratum counts or the three generic transverse normal forms. The previously known normality criterion and general binomial-hypersurface calculus are not claimed as original.

## Limitations
This result determines the **reduced** singular locus and the analytic types at general points of its irreducible components, not the nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, the singularity types at component intersections, the global normalization morphism, or singularities of arbitrary graphs with additional cycles or chords. The equality of analytic local forms uses the complex ground field and is not asserted in positive characteristic. The normalization boundary is prior art; the contribution is its precise codimension-by-codimension geometric refinement. Unindexed equivalent specializations remain a literature risk, and no independent audit is represented.
