# Singular strata and a small resolution for one-entry-deleted Segre toric varieties

## Finding

For integers \(a,b\geq 2\), let \(X_{a,b}\subset\mathbb P^{ab-2}_{\mathbb C}\) be the projective toric variety parametrized by the nonzero homogeneous monomials \(z_{ij}=x_i y_j\), \(1\leq i\leq a\), \(1\leq j\leq b\), except the coordinate \(z_{11}\). Equivalently, this is the projective edge toric variety of the bipartite graph obtained by deleting one edge from \(K_{a,b}\), or the closure of the projection of \(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\) from one point of its Segre embedding.

Define disjoint coordinate-linear spaces
\[
L_R=\{z_{ij}=0\text{ unless }i=1,\ j>1\}\cong\mathbb P^{b-2},\qquad
L_C=\{z_{ij}=0\text{ unless }i>1,\ j=1\}\cong\mathbb P^{a-2}.
\]
Then \(X_{a,b}\) is a normal projective variety of dimension \(a+b-2\), and its reduced singular locus is **exactly**
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_{a,b})_{\mathrm{red}}
= \begin{cases}L_R& a\geq3,\\\varnothing&a=2\end{cases}
\ \sqcup\
\begin{cases}L_C& b\geq3,\\\varnothing&b=2.\end{cases}
\]
Every affine chart with a row coordinate \(z_{1j}\ne0\), \(j>1\), is
\[U_{1j}\cong \mathbb A^{b-2}\times D_{2,a-1},\]
and every chart with a column coordinate \(z_{i1}\ne0\), \(i>1\), is
\[U_{i1}\cong \mathbb A^{a-2}\times D_{2,b-1},\]
where \(D_{2,m}\) is the affine variety of \(2\times m\) complex matrices of rank at most one. Charts \(z_{ij}\ne0\) with \(i,j>1\) are smooth affine spaces \(\mathbb A^{a+b-2}\). Thus, at every point of \(L_R\) for \(a\ge3\), the transverse singularity is the rank-one determinantal cone \(D_{2,a-1}\), its Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity is \(a-1\), and its Zariski local divisor-class group is \(\mathbb Z\). The analogous statements on \(L_C\) exchange \(a,b\). In particular, \(X_{a,b}\) is smooth precisely for \(a=b=2\), and globally Gorenstein (equivalently \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein) precisely when \(a,b\le3\).

The blowup \(B=\operatorname{Bl}_{([e_1],[e_1])}(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1})\) resolves the coordinate projection and defines a smooth projective birational morphism \(f:B\to X_{a,b}\). Its positive-dimensional fibers occur precisely above \(L_R\cup L_C\), and all such fibers are \(\mathbb P^1\). When \(a,b\ge3\), \(f\) is a projective small resolution (both in the sense of no divisorial exceptional components and in the stronger topological smallness sense). Among these small resolutions, it is crepant exactly for \(a=b=3\).

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes and local rings are over \(\mathbb C\), and all points under discussion are closed complex points. The projective scheme is the closure of the monomial map with its reduced, irreducible toric scheme structure, not the locus defined merely by an arbitrary selection of naive observed minors. The deletion pattern is **one** matrix coordinate, which can be moved to position \((1,1)\) by row and column permutations. The singular locus is stated with reduced scheme structure. A small resolution here is a proper birational map from a smooth variety that is an isomorphism over the smooth locus and has exceptional locus of codimension at least two. The stronger topological condition is also proved when \(a,b\ge3\).

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive affine charts.** The projective toric coordinate ring is the graded subalgebra of \(\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_a,y_1,\ldots,y_b]\) generated by \(x_i y_j\) for \((i,j)\ne(1,1)\). These coordinates cover every point of \(X_{a,b}\). On \(z_{ij}\ne0\) with \(i,j>1\), the degree-zero generators include every ratio \(x_p/x_i\) and \(y_q/y_j\), since edges \((p,j)\) and \((i,q)\) exist, and all other ratios are their products. Their exponent lattice is free of rank \(a+b-2\), giving a polynomial coordinate ring.

On \(z_{1j}\ne0\), \(j>1\), put
\[
s_i=\frac{x_i}{x_1}\ (2\le i\le a),\quad
w_i=\frac{x_i y_1}{x_1y_j}\ (2\le i\le a),\quad
t_q=\frac{y_q}{y_j}\ (q\in\{2,\ldots,b\}\setminus\{j\}).
\]
Each generator \(z_{pq}/z_{1j}\) is one of \(s_i,w_i,t_q,1\), or a product \(s_i t_q\). Conversely all displayed generators are present. The \(t_q\) are algebraically independent and disjoint in the exponent lattice from the \(s_i,w_i\). The latter give the subalgebra \(\mathbb C[s_i,\lambda s_i:2\le i\le a]\) of a polynomial ring in \(s_i,\lambda\), whose kernel is the prime ideal of all \(2\times2\) minors of the matrix whose first row is \((s_2,\ldots,s_a)\) and whose second row is \((w_2,\ldots,w_a)\). The equality of the kernel with that determinantal ideal follows directly from the standard polynomial parametrization of rank-one matrices (including its normal toric semigroup ring), not merely set-theoretic vanishing. This proves the row chart isomorphism. Transposition proves the column chart isomorphism.

**2. Singular sets, multiplicities, class groups, and canonical indices.** \(D_{2,1}=\mathbb A^2\) is smooth. For \(m\ge2\), the rank-one matrix locus \(D_{2,m}\) is smooth outside its origin: after inverting a nonzero matrix coordinate all remaining entries are expressed as products of one column and one row. At the origin all derivatives of the defining quadratic minors vanish, so the Zariski tangent dimension is \(2m\), greater than its dimension \(m+1\); hence the vertex alone is singular. Thus the row singular set is the free \(\mathbb A^{b-2}\) times the matrix origin, exactly \(L_R\cap U_{1j}\), and likewise for \(L_C\). These two loci are disjoint, and there are no singular points on the other charts.

For \(m\ge2\), \(D_{2,m}\) is the normal affine cone over the Segre embedded \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\), polarized by \(H_1+H_2\). Its local divisor-class group at the vertex is
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1})/\mathbb Z(H_1+H_2)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
with no torsion. The free affine factor does not change the local class group. Its cone multiplicity is the degree \(\binom m1=m\) of this Segre embedding. The canonical class descends to a rational Cartier class exactly if \(-2H_1-mH_2\) is rationally proportional to \(H_1+H_2\), i.e. precisely if \(m=2\). Since normal rank-one determinantal rings are Cohen–Macaulay, this also identifies the Gorenstein boundary. Apply this to \(m=a-1,b-1\). The only other charts are smooth; hence the global criteria follow.

**3. The projective resolution.** In the complete Segre embedding, omitting \(z_{11}\) is linear projection from the Segre point \(p=([e_1],[e_1])\). The remaining \(ab-1\) sections of \(\mathcal O(1,1)\) vanish simultaneously precisely at \(p\), and their local linear parts span its cotangent space (the sections \(x_i y_1\) and \(x_1 y_j\)). Thus the blowup at \(p\) resolves the projection, and the resulting map \(f\) is projective and birational, since the complete rank-one matrix is recovered from a generic observed nonzero \(2\times2\) rectangular submatrix.

A positive-dimensional fiber corresponds to a projective line through \(p\) whose intersection with the Segre variety contains another curve. Since the Segre ideal is generated by quadrics, if a line contains \(p\) and two further distinct rank-one points, every defining minor vanishes identically on that line. Every line in a two-factor Segre variety is a ruling line: one tensor factor is constant. Therefore all positive-dimensional fibers are exactly the strict transforms of ruling lines through \(p\), lying in the strict transforms of the two fibers \(\{[e_1]\}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\) or \(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\{[e_1]\}\). Projection on the first fiber becomes the standard morphism \(\operatorname{Bl}_{[e_1]}\mathbb P^{b-1}\to\mathbb P^{b-2}\) with \(\mathbb P^1\) fibers; the other is symmetric. These images are exactly \(L_R,L_C\), and no other fibers have positive dimension. The morphism is an isomorphism off these loci because it is proper, quasi-finite, birational, and the target is normal there. The exceptional locus dimensions are \(b-1\) and \(a-1\), while \(\dim B=a+b-2\); hence neither is a divisor exactly when \(a,b\ge3\). In that case the images have codimensions \(a,b\ge3>2\dim\mathbb P^1\), proving topological smallness. If \(a=b=3\), the target is Gorenstein, and absence of exceptional divisors makes the small resolution crepant. For other \(a,b\ge3\), the target is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein, so crepancy is not asserted.

## Verification

`checker.py` is a standalone Python 3 standard-library regression program. Run `python3 checker.py` in the directory containing it. `checker.out` records one actual successful run. It tests all 25 formats with \(2\le a,b\le6\), reconstructs 375 exact affine coordinate charts, compares bounded monomial semigroups and Hilbert functions through degree four, and uses exact rational Jacobian computations on the determinantal cones for \(1\le m\le6\). These are finite consistency tests only; the chart/kernel, singularity, divisor-class, and resolution assertions for all \(a,b\) are proved symbolically above.

## Relationship to prior work

Ohsugi–Hibi [1], Example 2 and Theorem 4, explain graph edge toric ideals and the complete bipartite graph's determinantal ideal, but do not classify these one-edge-deleted projective singular strata. Aluffi [2], especially its introduction, Section 2, and projection-degree theorems, treats projections of determinantal rank loci and computes degrees; its results provide the relevant projection framework, not the pointwise transverse cones, local divisor-class groups, or small resolution here. Kubjas–Rosen [3] and Kahle–Kubjas–Kummer–Rosen [4] study algebraic/semialgebraic conditions for matrix and tensor completion. Their projection images overlap the setup, but the conclusions investigated there concern completability and inequalities rather than this full complex projective birational/singularity classification. The known general rank-one determinantal singularity and incidence-resolution theories are used as mathematical inputs; the claim is their global identification and precise dichotomy for this deleted-edge family, not the general lemmas themselves.

## Limitations

The result treats exactly one missing coordinate and only the reduced singular locus, not nonreduced Jacobian schemes or arbitrary multiple-deletion patterns. An equivalent specialization under matrix-completion or toric projection terminology may exist outside the inspected sources. No independent mathematical audit has been performed. The finite checker does not certify an infinite family or replace any argument above.
