# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The exact six-row factorizations follow from the tetrahedral binomial formula and show the stated normalized generators. In each row, the pair gcd is \(h\), the remaining generator is coprime to \(h\), and an explicit nonnegative witness expresses it in the reduced pair. The rectangular Apéry lemma is proved, not inferred from data; it yields the exact Frobenius and genus formulas and symmetry. A separately implemented shortest-residue-path calculation verifies every index from \(2\) to \(140\). The infinite result rests on the symbolic argument; the limited calculation alone would not establish it.

**Originality: PASS, with residual risk.** The nearest directly examined source, Robles-Pérez and Rosales, arXiv:1706.04378 (Sections 3 and 4.3), treats the *four*-term tetrahedral semigroup and develops telescopic gcd identities and formulae; it does not assert the present **normalized three-term** six-residue Frobenius formula. OEIS A069761 lists four-term Frobenius numbers, not this invariant. Pascadi (arXiv:1712.02522) gives more general reduction-graph techniques applicable to similar questions, but the inspected application is not this six-case normalized triple. Generic gluing results imply a formula once the present six factorizations and all six membership witnesses have been obtained; those extra identities constitute the classification. Risk: the technical core is elementary, and an overlooked earlier specialization could subsume the claim.

**Value: PASS.** Gcd normalization is essential because consecutive tetrahedral triples do not always generate numerical semigroups without it. The classification determines an exact Frobenius number, genus and symmetry simultaneously for all indices, with six different cubic phases and four inequivalent limiting scales. The sharp separation of asymptotic constants distinguishes the triple problem from the classical four-generator formula; it is not merely a bounded computation or an arbitrary small slice.

Closest literature: OEIS A069761 (2002), Robles-Pérez–Rosales (2017; arXiv:1706.04378), Pascadi (2017; arXiv:1712.02522). The latter two first appeared later than the underlying 2002 recorded tetrahedral Frobenius problem. No exhaustive search can rule out all unindexed derivations. Finite verification is supporting evidence only.
