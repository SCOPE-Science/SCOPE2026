For the recursive divisor count \(\kappa_0(n)\), balancing two unequal positive prime exponents strictly increases the count, giving a strict majorization law. At every fixed \((\Omega(n),\omega(n))=(m,r)\), the unique minimal exponent signature is \((m-r+1,1,\ldots,1)\), while the unique maximal signature is as equal as possible; both sharp counts follow from an explicit finite binomial-difference formula already known in the literature.
