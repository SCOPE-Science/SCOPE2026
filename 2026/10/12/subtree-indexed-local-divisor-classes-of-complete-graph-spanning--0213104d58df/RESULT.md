# Subtree-indexed local divisor classes of complete-graph spanning-tree toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(E\) be the edge set of the complete graph \(K_n\), let
\[
P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_T:T\text{ is a spanning tree of }K_n\}\subset\mathbb R^E,
\]
and let \(X_n\) be the normal projective toric variety with the normal fan of \(P_n\). A spanning tree \(T\) indexes a torus-fixed point \(p_T\in X_n\). Define
\[
\mathcal C(T)=\{S\subsetneq V(K_n):2\le |S|\le n-1,\ T[S]\text{ is connected}\},\qquad c(T)=|\mathcal C(T)|.
\]
Then the local Weil-divisor class group, including its integral torsion, is completely determined:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p_T})\simeq\mathbb Z^{\,c(T)-n+2}.}
\]
In particular it has no torsion. If \(\tau(T)\) denotes the number of nonempty connected induced vertex sets of \(T\), including singletons and the whole tree, the rank is \(\tau(T)-2n+1\). The minimum and maximum over all torus-fixed points are attained, respectively, precisely by path trees and star trees:
\[
\boxed{\binom{n-1}{2}\ \le\ \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p_T})\ \le\ 2^{n-1}-n.}
\]
Consequently every torus-fixed point is non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial (and singular). The dependence on the tree type ranges from quadratic to exponential even within the same variety.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\). Write \(m=|E|=\binom n2\), and set
\[
M=\{(a_e)_{e\in E}\in\mathbb Z^E:\sum_ea_e=0\},\qquad N=\mathbb Z^E/\mathbb Z\mathbf1.
\]
These are the dual character and cocharacter lattices for the normal fan, with natural pairing. The affine lattice generated by differences of spanning-tree incidence vectors is precisely \(M\): basis exchanges give differences of unit vectors, and the graphic matroid of \(K_n\) is connected. No claim is made about local class groups at non-fixed points.

There is also an explicit integral presentation. Put \(E(S)=\{\{i,j\}\in E:i,j\in S\}\) and
\[
R_T=(E\setminus T)\sqcup\mathcal C(T).
\]
Then the following sequence is exact:
\[
0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\Phi_T}\mathbb Z^{R_T}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p_T})\longrightarrow0,
\]
where the two types of coordinates of \(\Phi_T(a)\) are
\[
(\Phi_T(a))_f=-a_f\quad(f\notin T),\qquad
(\Phi_T(a))_S=\sum_{e\in E(S)}a_e\quad(S\in\mathcal C(T)).
\]
Thus both a presentation and the abstract integral group are specified.

## Proof
**1. Facets of the spanning-tree polytope.** The graphic matroid of \(K_n\) is connected for \(n\ge4\); hence \(P_n\) has dimension \(m-1\). Its complete irredundant list of facets is
\[
x_f\ge0\quad(f\in E),\qquad
\sum_{e\in E(S)}x_e\le|S|-1\quad(2\le|S|\le n-1).
\]
This follows from the matroid base-polytope inequalities and the connected-restriction/connected-contraction facet criterion [1, Propositions 2.3, 2.4, 2.6]. Specifically, a nontrivial graphic flat of \(K_n\) consists of edges internal to blocks of a vertex partition. If more than one block has at least two vertices, its matroid restriction is disconnected, so it cannot give a facet. For a single proper nonsingleton block \(S\), the restriction is the cycle matroid of \(K_S\), and its contraction is the cycle matroid of the connected complete multigraph formed by collapsing \(S\); both are connected. Each nonnegativity inequality is a facet because deleting any one edge from \(K_n\) leaves a connected graphic matroid. The full rank equation is \(\sum_{e\in E}x_e=n-1\).

**2. Exact incident facets.** At the vertex \(\mathbf1_T\), a nonnegativity facet is tight exactly for \(f\notin T\). A subset facet is tight exactly when \(|T\cap E(S)|=|S|-1\), which, since \(T\) is a tree, is equivalent to \(T[S]\) being connected. Therefore the facets through this vertex are indexed without repetition by \(R_T\). There are \(m-n+1+c(T)\) of them.

**3. Integral class-group calculation.** The full-dimensional normal cone at \(\mathbf1_T\) has primitive rays
\[
-[\mathbf e_f]\quad(f\notin T),\qquad
[\sum_{e\in E(S)}\mathbf e_e]\quad(S\in\mathcal C(T))
\]
in \(N\); brackets mean classes modulo \(\mathbb Z\mathbf1\). Their primitiveness follows from the zero-one facet normals. The standard toric Weil-divisor sequence for the affine chart at \(p_T\), which computes its local class group, is exactly the sequence with map \(\Phi_T\) displayed above.

Crucially, the ray vectors generate \(N\) **integrally**. For every \(f\notin T\), the ray \(-[\mathbf e_f]\) occurs. For every \(e=\{i,j\}\in T\), the two-vertex set \(S=\{i,j\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal C(T)\), and its ray is \([\mathbf e_e]\). Thus all unit-coordinate classes occur up to sign; they generate \(N\). The dual divisor map therefore has saturated image: its cokernel has no torsion. Since its domain has rank \(m-1\), the group has rank
\[
(m-n+1+c(T))-(m-1)=c(T)-n+2.
\]

**4. Extremal tree shapes.** The count \(\tau(T)\) consists of \(c(T)\) proper connected sets of size at least two, \(n\) singletons, and the full tree. Hence \(c(T)=\tau(T)-n-1\). Every unordered pair of distinct vertices determines a distinct induced path, yielding at least \(n+\binom n2\) subtrees. Equality holds exactly for a path, since a branch vertex produces a connected non-path subtree. Conversely, delete a leaf adjacent to \(u\). Any subtree containing the deleted leaf is either that singleton or the leaf joined to a connected subtree containing \(u\). The latter count is at most \(2^{n-2}\). Induction gives \(\tau(T)\le2^{n-1}+n-1\), with equality exactly for a star. The path and star counts are \(\binom{n+1}{2}\) and \(2^{n-1}+n-1\), respectively. Substitution gives the stated sharp bounds. Since \(\binom{n-1}{2}>0\) for \(n\ge4\), every fixed-point local class group has positive free rank, precluding \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality.

## Relationship to prior work
The general matroid facet description and connected-flacet criterion of Feichtner--Sturmfels [1] are essential established inputs, **not new claims**. The usual toric affine divisor sequence is also standard [2]. Lasoń--Michałek [3] classifies which entire matroid base polytopes are smooth or Gorenstein but does not give the tree-indexed local integral class groups. Székely--Wang [4] concerns subtree enumeration, not local divisor theory. The contribution here is the explicit presentation and complete torsion-free, tree-indexed local class-group computation, together with the sharp change from path to star; the general matroid and toric tools themselves are not new.

## Limitations
The classification is for torus-fixed points of the normal projective toric variety of the **complete-graph spanning-tree** base polytope, not arbitrary graphic matroids or all torus orbits. It does not compute local analytic equations, global divisor class groups, or the exact distribution of ranks among unlabeled tree types. The proof concerns characteristic zero as formulated, although its lattice steps are characteristic-independent. The result is derived using powerful established general facet and toric-divisor theorems; possible earlier explicit specializations remain a bibliographic risk.
