# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The map from vertex-torus tuples to cycle-edge products is \(Q\)-to-one onto one multiplicative hypersurface; a dimension-\(d\) linear section can contain at most \(Q^d\) torus points. Matching odd/even edge groups attain that bound for every \(d\leq k\). Equality forces all coordinate functionals to be one-variable monomials, from the elementary nonvanishing linear-form lemma valid for every \(q>2\). Character orthogonality on \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) is not needed: elementary cyclicity yields exactly the residue-balanced blocks. Counting scalar assignments modulo block rescaling gives the claimed multiplicities. Every quantifier and boundary \(k\geq3\), \(q>2\), \(k\leq r<2k\) has been checked; the finite verifier corroborates nontrivial small fields and the field-stable cases.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
The closest recent source, Asensio et al. (arXiv:2608.30407v1, §4.2, Theorem 4.7), computes the distribution of weights of *individual* codewords. It does not optimize simultaneous zeros of \(r\) independent codewords or count minimizing \(r\)-subcodes. Earlier Patanker–Singh hypersimplex results concern the much larger code with all quadratic square-free monomials; the González Sarabia et al. projective complete-bipartite results concern a different evaluation construction (the isolated four-cycle coincidence is excluded by \(k\geq3\)). The original Jaramillo-Velez edge-code paper deals with basic parameters and trees. No inspected result gives the claimed all-field, modular-block extremizer classification. This search cannot exclude an unindexed broader theorem for toric linear sections.

## Value — PASS
Generalized support weights quantify minimum observation sets for linear subcodes and are not recoverable from the ordinary weight enumerator alone. Here both the full upper-half hierarchy and **all** extremizing subspaces have closed descriptions over every \(q>2\); the congruence-to-exact-balance transition \(q\geq k+2\) explains the extra small-field minimizers. The classification is uniform in cycle length and field size, not a parameter-specific census, and exhibits the exact dimension threshold for saturation of the linear-section projection bound.

## Scope and limits
Exact lower-half weights and nonminimum support multiplicities remain open. The verifier is exhaustive only in its explicitly reported finite cases, and the classification proof is mathematical, not externally certified. Novelty depends on inspected primary literature and indexed records rather than a complete census of all unpublished work.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
