# Tensor decomposability in cotangent dimension two

## Finding
Let \(k\) be a field and let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional associative unital local \(k\)-algebra satisfying
\[
A/J(A)\cong k,\qquad \dim_k J(A)/J(A)^2=2.
\]
Then \(A\) admits a nontrivial tensor decomposition over \(k\) if and only if there are integers \(m,n\ge2\) such that
\[
A\cong k[u,v]/(u^m,v^n).
\]
Moreover, the unordered pair \(\{{m,n\}}\) is uniquely determined by \(A\), and every nontrivial tensor decomposition is, up to order and isomorphism,
\[
A\cong k[t]/(t^m)\otimes_k k[s]/(s^n).
\]
Consequently every noncommutative split local algebra with two-dimensional cotangent space is tensor-indecomposable.

## Assumptions and scope
All algebras are finite-dimensional, associative, unital \(k\)-algebras, and tensor products are over \(k\). The condition \(A/J(A)\cong k\) is the split-local hypothesis. A tensor decomposition is nontrivial when both tensor factors have dimension greater than one.

Write
\[
e(A)=\dim_k J(A)/J(A)^2
\]
for the cotangent, or embedding, dimension. The theorem concerns exactly the first nontrivial case \(e(A)=2\). It imposes no restriction on the characteristic of \(k\).

## Proof
First note the cotangent-space additivity for split local tensor products. If \(B\) and \(C\) are split local, then
\[
J(B\otimes_k C)=J(B)\otimes_k C+B\otimes_k J(C),
\]
and after quotienting by the square of this radical one obtains
\[
J(B\otimes_k C)/J(B\otimes_k C)^2
\cong J(B)/J(B)^2\oplus J(C)/J(C)^2.
\]
Hence
\[
e(B\otimes_k C)=e(B)+e(C).
\]

Now suppose \(A\cong B\otimes_k C\) is nontrivial. Tensor factors of a split local algebra are again split local, and every nontrivial split local algebra has positive cotangent dimension. Since \(e(A)=2\), additivity forces
\[
e(B)=e(C)=1.
\]

It remains to classify split local algebras of cotangent dimension one. Let \(R\) be such an algebra and put \(J=J(R)\). Choose \(x\in J\) whose image spans \(J/J^2\). For every \(r\ge1\), products of representatives show that \(J^r/J^{r+1}\) is spanned by the class of \(x^r\). Let \(m\ge2\) be minimal with \(J^m=0\). For \(r<m\), equality \(J^r=J^{r+1}\) would iterate to \(J^r=J^m=0\), contradicting minimality. Therefore every quotient \(J^r/J^{r+1}\) for \(1\le r<m\) is one-dimensional and is generated by \(x^r\). It follows that
\[
1,x,x^2,\ldots,x^{m-1}
\]
is a basis of \(R\), and the homomorphism
\[
k[t]/(t^m)\longrightarrow R,\qquad t\longmapsto x,
\]
is an isomorphism. Thus
\[
B\cong k[t]/(t^m),\qquad C\cong k[s]/(s^n)
\]
for some \(m,n\ge2\), proving necessity. The converse is immediate because
\[
k[u,v]/(u^m,v^n)\cong k[u]/(u^m)\otimes_k k[v]/(v^n).
\]

Each algebra \(k[t]/(t^m)\) has cotangent dimension one and is tensor-irreducible: any nontrivial tensor factorization would express the positive integer \(1\) as a sum of two positive cotangent dimensions. Sakurai's uniqueness theorem for irreducible tensor factorizations of finite-dimensional local algebras over a splitting field therefore makes the unordered pair \(\{{m,n\}}\) unique. Finally, every algebra \(k[u,v]/(u^m,v^n)\) is commutative, so a noncommutative \(A\) satisfying the hypotheses cannot decompose.

## Verification
The argument is symbolic. The two critical points were checked independently from the radical filtration.

For cotangent additivity, expanding the radical square gives
\[
J(B\otimes C)^2=J(B)^2\otimes C+J(B)\otimes J(C)+B\otimes J(C)^2,
\]
so the classes coming from \(J(B)\otimes1\) and \(1\otimes J(C)\) survive independently and exhaust the quotient.

For the one-generator classification, nilpotence of \(J\) rules out a plateau \(J^r=J^{r+1}\neq0\). Since each successive quotient is at most one-dimensional and nonzero before the nilpotence index, the powers of one lift of a cotangent generator give exactly one basis vector in every radical layer. No commutativity hypothesis is inserted: commutativity is a consequence of generation by the single element \(x\).

As a boundary check, the three-dimensional algebra \(k[x,y]/(x,y)^2\) has cotangent dimension two but cannot decompose, consistently with the theorem because every rectangular algebra \(k[u,v]/(u^m,v^n)\) with \(m,n\ge2\) has dimension at least four.

## Relationship to prior work
Sakurai proves that irreducible tensor factorizations of finite-dimensional local algebras over a splitting field are unique without commutativity or characteristic restrictions, and develops the cotangent-space machinery used to compare tensor factors. The paper does not state the explicit classification above for cotangent dimension two. The present argument combines cotangent additivity with the elementary but decisive classification of cotangent-dimension-one split local algebras, converting the general uniqueness theorem into a complete decomposability criterion at the smallest nontrivial embedding dimension.

Horst's earlier cancellation theorem treats the characteristic-zero commutative setting. Nüsken's positive-characteristic theorem treats the narrower class of loop-resistant algebras. These results motivate the tensor-factorization problem but do not supply the all-characteristic split-local criterion above as located in the inspected material.

## Limitations
The split-local condition \(A/J(A)\cong k\) is essential to this statement; nonsplit local algebras are not classified here. The theorem is finite-dimensional and concerns tensor decompositions over the ground field. It does not classify tensor decomposability in cotangent dimension three or higher. Although targeted searches did not locate an earlier statement of this exact corollary, an older source could contain it under different terminology.
