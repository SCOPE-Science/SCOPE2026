# Review
## Correctness
PASS. For split local tensor products, the Jacobson radical is the sum \(J(B)\otimes C+B\otimes J(C)\), and quotienting its square gives the direct-sum cotangent formula. Therefore a nontrivial decomposition of an algebra with cotangent dimension two has two factors of cotangent dimension one. A split local algebra with one-dimensional cotangent space is generated by one radical element; nilpotence prevents any nonzero radical-power plateau, so it is exactly \(k[t]/(t^m)\). The converse decomposition is explicit. Sakurai's uniqueness theorem then makes the unordered truncation pair unique.

## Originality
PASS. The closest recent source proves general uniqueness of irreducible tensor factorization and provides the cotangent formalism, but its stated results do not give the explicit if-and-only-if classification in cotangent dimension two. Earlier Horst and Nüsken results address characteristic-zero commutative or loop-resistant positive-characteristic settings. Targeted searches for the equivalent rectangular-truncated-polynomial formulation and for cotangent-dimension-two tensor decomposability did not locate a covering theorem. The residual risk is that the corollary may appear in older literature under different terminology.

## Value
PASS. Cotangent dimension two is the smallest nontrivial local embedding dimension, so a complete decomposability criterion there is a natural structural boundary rather than an arbitrary special case. The theorem turns a general uniqueness result into a concrete recognition statement: decomposable objects are exactly rectangular truncated polynomial complete intersections, while every noncommutative example is automatically indecomposable.

## Closest literature and limitations
The principal source is Taro Sakurai, *Tensor factorization of local algebras*, arXiv:2609.38323v1. Earlier relevant work includes C. Horst's cancellation theorem for Artinian local algebras and M. Nüsken's positive-characteristic loop-resistant factorization theorem. The result is restricted to finite-dimensional split local algebras with two-dimensional cotangent space and tensor products over the ground field.
