# Verification
The proof was rechecked from the radical filtration rather than from examples.

For split local algebras \(B\) and \(C\), write \(I=J(B)\otimes C+B\otimes J(C)\). The quotient \((B\otimes C)/I\cong k\) is a field and \(I\) is nilpotent, hence \(I=J(B\otimes C)\). Expanding \(I^2\) shows that the map
\[
J(B)/J(B)^2\oplus J(C)/J(C)^2\longrightarrow I/I^2
\]
induced by \(b\mapsto b\otimes1\) and \(c\mapsto1\otimes c\) is an isomorphism. Therefore cotangent dimensions add exactly.

If a nontrivial split local algebra \(R\) has one-dimensional cotangent space, choose \(x\in J(R)\) generating \(J(R)/J(R)^2\). Multiplication shows that \(J(R)^r/J(R)^{r+1}\) is generated by \(x^r\). Before the nilpotence index, no layer can vanish or satisfy \(J(R)^r=J(R)^{r+1}\), because iterating such an equality to the nilpotence index would force \(J(R)^r=0\). Hence all preterminal layers are one-dimensional, and \(1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}\) is a basis. This gives \(R\cong k[t]/(t^m)\).

Sakurai's Theorem A was inspected in the full preprint and states uniqueness, up to order and isomorphism, of irreducible tensor factors for finite-dimensional local algebras over a splitting field. Since a cotangent-dimension-one split local algebra cannot factor into two nontrivial factors, the two truncated-polynomial factors above are irreducible and Sakurai's theorem applies.

The argument uses no exhaustive search or finite experiment. It does not address nonsplit local algebras or cotangent dimension at least three.
