# The 118-class ternary quotient of length-two palindromic duplication

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\), and let \(\equiv\) be the least monoid congruence on \(\Sigma^*\) generated by
\[
  uabv\equiv uabbav\qquad(u,v\in\Sigma^*,\ a,b\in\Sigma).
\]
For a word \(w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}\) of length \(n\ge2\), define its *signature* to consist of: (i) its ordered first two letters \((w_0,w_1)\); (ii) \(n\bmod2\); and (iii) the ordered list of pairs \((c,j_c\bmod2)\), where \(c\) runs through the distinct symbols of \(w\) in order of first appearance, and \(j_c\) is the least position containing \(c\) (positions begin at zero). The empty word and each singleton receive their own distinct signatures.

**Theorem.** Two ternary words are congruent if and only if their signatures agree. The quotient monoid \(\Sigma^*/{\equiv}\) therefore has exactly \(118\) elements. For the number \(C(n)\) of congruence classes represented by words of length \(n\),
\[
 C(n)=\begin{cases}
 1&n=0,\\
 3&n=1,\\
 9&n=2,\\
 21&n=3,\\
 39&n=4,\\
 51&n=5,\\
 57&n\ge6.
 \end{cases}
\]
A signature can be computed in time linear in word length, so this theorem gives a linear-time equivalence test without performing word rewrites.

## Assumptions and scope
Every generating step duplicates two adjacent symbols and inserts their reversal immediately after them. The congruence is the **symmetric** closure of these operations, allowing both expansions and contractions, with arbitrary contexts. It is not the directed descendant relation, nor the relation of intersecting forward-descendant cones. The alphabet has exactly three symbols, though a word need not use all three. All statements hold for *every* finite word, without length cutoff.

## Proof
**1. Necessity.** In \(uabv\mapsto uabbav\), two symbols are inserted after the retained source pair \(ab\). No new letter appears. The original first occurrences of the source symbols remain at their original positions; earlier first occurrences are unchanged, while first occurrences to the right move by exactly two positions. Thus the order of first appearances and each first-position parity are preserved. Both the first two letters and total length parity are also preserved. These properties hold in both directions. No generating move applies to an empty word or singleton.

**2. Finite representatives.** Every signature has a representative of length at most \(7\). Keep the original first two letters. There are at most two as-yet unseen symbols. Before writing each successive new symbol, insert either zero or one copy of an already encountered symbol, chosen to realize the prescribed parity of its first position. After the last new symbol, append zero or one previously encountered symbol to realize the prescribed total parity. This construction retains the first two letters and first-appearance order; its length is at most \(2+2\cdot2+1=7\). The cases of length below two are separate.

For each of the \(118\) distinct signatures represented among words of length at most \(7\), choose the shortest, then lexicographically first, representative \(r_s\). The count \(118\) also follows directly: excluding the empty word and three singletons, for either total-length parity there are \(3\) one-symbol signatures, \(18=6+12\) two-symbol signatures, and \(36=12+24\) three-symbol signatures. Thus each parity has \(57\) signatures and the total is \(4+2\cdot57=118\).

**3. Exact finite multiplication lemma.** For every such representative \(r_s\) and every \(a\in\Sigma\),
\[
 r_s a\equiv r_{\operatorname{sig}(r_s a)}.
\]
This is a finite lemma of \(118\cdot3=354\) explicitly decidable assertions. The accompanying `verify.cpp` proves every assertion by constructing the undirected graph with all \(7{,}174{,}453\) ternary words of lengths at most \(14\), and all \(8{,}370{,}198\) elementary expansion edges within that range. A disjoint-set computation tests graph connectivity of both words in each assertion. Connected vertices provide finite sequences of legal congruence steps. The checker recomputes the representative table, checks all \(354\) assertions, and aborts on any failure; there are no unverified oracle or externally supplied connectivity results. The bound \(14\) applies only to these *short representative transitions*, not to rewrites of arbitrary long words.

**4. Induction to unbounded length.** Equal signatures are compatible with appending a letter: if \(\operatorname{sig}(u)=\operatorname{sig}(v)\), then \(\operatorname{sig}(ua)=\operatorname{sig}(va)\) for every \(a\in\Sigma\). Indeed, the parity of the new position is determined by the old length parity; the appended letter either already occurs in both words or is a newly encountered final symbol in both. The other signature components behave identically. Proceed by induction on word length: if \(u\equiv r_{\operatorname{sig}(u)}\), then, using right congruence and the finite lemma,
\[
 ua\equiv r_{\operatorname{sig}(u)}a
    \equiv r_{\operatorname{sig}(ua)}.
\]
The empty prefix starts the induction. Hence every word is equivalent to its signature representative. Together with necessity, this proves the characterization for all lengths.

**5. Exact-length counts.** Direct enumeration of the finitely many signatures at lengths \(0\) through \(7\) gives \(1,3,9,21,39,51,57,57\). These numbers are checked independently of graph connectivity in `verify.cpp`. Every even-parity signature already occurs at length \(6\), and every odd-parity signature at length \(7\). Appending two copies of any existing symbol preserves the signature, so the count is \(57\) for every length at least \(6\).

## Verification
Compile `verify.cpp` as follows, using a C++17 compiler and standard library:

```sh
c++ -O2 -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra verify.cpp -o verify
./verify
```

On replay, the checker reports:

```text
VERIFY_OK ternary_classes=118 table_transitions=354 graph_vertices=7174453 graph_edges=8370198 invariant_edges=18045 stable_length_classes=57 length_bound=14
```

The graph enumeration is an exhaustive finite check of a multiplication lemma; the infinite statement follows only after applying the induction in Step 4. The code itself is supplied in full. In addition, the checker tests local invariant preservation on \(18{,}045\) edges and checks the exact counts for all lengths through \(7\).

## Relationship to prior work
Zeraatpisheh, Esmaeili, and Gulliver (2020) study code constructions, deduplication roots, and overlap patterns for palindromic duplication lengths two and three. Their results do not give this ternary symmetric-congruence classification. Yohananov and Schwartz (2025) prove a **sufficient** condition for common forward descendants via equality of summaries of disjoint length-two blocks and obtain zero-rate bounds. Their result does not give an if-and-only-if test for the symmetric congruence or the number \(118\). For example, `0120` and `0121` have different disjoint-pair summaries but are congruent by the finite lemma above. The known binary two-symbol restriction has \(19\) congruence classes; the ternary case additionally records ordering and parity information for the third symbol.

## Limitations
The finite graph cutoff \(14\) is a certificate only for the \(354\) representative transitions; it is **not** asserted to suffice for arbitrary input words. This theorem classifies symmetric equivalence, not common directed descendants, optimal duplication-correcting codes, or unrestricted alphabet sizes. The literature search is not a proof of priority: an equivalent presentation under a different semigroup or rewriting-system vocabulary may exist. No independent external verification or peer review is asserted.
