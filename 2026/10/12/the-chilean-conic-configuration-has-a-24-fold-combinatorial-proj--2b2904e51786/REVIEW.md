# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The source identifies the nine sections with \(\mathbb F_3^2\), the twelve Hesse components with affine lines, and explains that in the index-two translation any two parallel affine lines determine a conic component. Since a parallel class has three lines, each conic block is the complement of one affine line. This gives a direct finite model of the \((12_6,9_8)\) incidence structure. The exact verifier exhausts all \(9!\) point permutations and finds \(432\) incidence automorphisms, exactly the independently generated affine group. Pairwise common-point counts reconstruct four triangles. For the geometric half, Corollary 4.2 of Dolgachev–Laface–Persson–Urzúa gives the complete surface automorphism group \(G_{18}\), and its proof gives descent to \(\mathbb P^2\). A projective arrangement symmetry lifts through the nine multiplicity-eight base points, so no additional projective symmetry exists.

Risk: the proof relies on the source theorem that the complete automorphism group of every nondegenerate Hesse-type index-two Halphen surface is \(G_{18}\). The claim is therefore restricted to exactly that family and excludes degenerations.

## Originality
**PASS.** Exact-source and alias searches were made for the Chilean/Hesse conic arrangement, \(\operatorname{AGL}(2,3)\), \(G_{18}\), projective stabilizers, and the \((12_6,9_8)\) incidence group. The closest literature is Kohel–Roulleau–Sarti: it independently constructs the same conics, records the \(3\)-versus-\(4\) common-point pattern, and encounters a \(G_{432}\cong\operatorname{AGL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\) lattice symmetry for a natural K3 \(9\mathbf A_2\)-configuration. That is not the claimed comparison: the paper does not state that the plane point–conic incidence automorphism group has projective-realization kernel \(G_{18}\), nor the exact sequence induced by the four incidence-recoverable conic triples. The lead paper gives \(G_{18}\) for the Halphen surface and the affine-plane encoding, but likewise does not state this abstract-versus-projective symmetry theorem. Pokora–Szemberg and Dimca–Pokora–Sticlaru treat freeness/log invariants and Alexander polynomials, respectively.

Residual risk: the affine-plane identification makes the order \(432\) mathematically natural, and an older finite-geometry source could state an equivalent automorphism computation. The novelty asserted here is the combined plane-realization statement and exact kernel/quotient interpretation, not the standalone fact that \(\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{AG}(2,3))=\operatorname{AGL}(2,3)\).

## Value
**PASS.** The result measures a natural rigidity defect of a canonical algebraic configuration: it separates abstract incidence symmetry from projectively realizable symmetry and identifies the missing factor geometrically as the full \(S_4\)-permutation of the four reducible fibers. This is a structural invariant of the realization, not a relabeling or a recomputation of a published table. The incidence-only recovery of the fiber partition also makes the comparison intrinsic and reusable in projective-equivalence and degeneration questions.
