# The complete binary six-coordinate MDS symbol-pair code census

## Finding

Let a *binary six-coordinate code* be a subset \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^{6}\), with positions numbered \(0,1,\ldots,5\) cyclically. For any two words define the cyclic symbol-pair distance
\[
 d_{\mathrm p}(x,y)=\left|\left\{i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z:(x_i,x_{i+1})\ne(y_i,y_{i+1})\right\}\right|.
\]
All indices are reduced modulo six.

**Theorem (exhaustive finite classification).** Among all sixteen-word binary length-six codes with minimum symbol-pair distance at least \(4\), there are exactly **76 distinct labeled codes**. Of them, exactly **44** are affine four-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-cosets and exactly **32** are nonaffine. Under the group generated by translations \(x\mapsto x\oplus t\) for \(t\in\{0,1\}^{6}\) and the dihedral permutations of the six cyclic positions, these 76 codes form exactly **five orbits**. Their sizes and affine types are respectively
\[
 (4,\mathrm{affine}),\ (32,\mathrm{nonaffine}),\ (24,\mathrm{affine}),\ (12,\mathrm{affine}),\ (4,\mathrm{affine}).
\]
Every code in this classification meets the known symbol-pair Singleton bound with equality. The upper bound and the existence of such codes were previously established; the contribution here is the complete set-level census and the nonaffine/orbit distinction.

An integer \(m\in\{0,\ldots,63\}\) denotes the binary word with coordinate \(i\) equal to bit \(i\) of \(m\). The five lexicographically least representatives, obtained by sorting their sixteen encoded words, are shown below. Every other optimizer is a translate or dihedral coordinate rearrangement of exactly one of these:

| Orbit size | Affine? | Representative, in decimal word encoding |
| ---: | :---: | --- |
| 4 | yes | 0, 5, 10, 15, 17, 20, 27, 30, 34, 39, 40, 45, 51, 54, 57, 60 |
| 32 | no | 0, 5, 10, 15, 17, 20, 27, 30, 34, 39, 41, 44, 51, 54, 56, 61 |
| 24 | yes | 0, 5, 10, 15, 17, 20, 27, 30, 35, 38, 41, 44, 50, 55, 56, 61 |
| 12 | yes | 0, 5, 11, 14, 18, 23, 25, 28, 35, 38, 40, 45, 49, 52, 58, 63 |
| 4 | yes | 0, 7, 9, 14, 18, 21, 27, 28, 35, 36, 42, 45, 49, 54, 56, 63 |

The file `pair6_codes.txt` provides all 76 labeled codes, one sorted sixteen-integer code on each data line.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is precisely \(\mathbb F_2\), the block length is exactly six, and codewords are unlabelled elements of a set but their **coordinate positions are fixed and labeled** for the total of 76. No linearity, systematicity, cyclic invariance, or group-action invariance of the codes is imposed. A code is *affine* if it is a coset of a four-dimensional binary linear subspace; a nonlinear code here means one that is not affine. The group used solely to classify the 76 codes comprises arbitrary coordinatewise bit flips and the twelve rotations/reflections of their cyclic positions. Different words may be decoded without knowing any additional algebraic structure.

The integer-encoded catalogue is not a statement about orbit counts under larger groups, such as all six-coordinate permutations, which do not preserve the cyclic pair metric in general. A known erasure location is necessary for the stated adjacent-two-erasure interpretation; no substitution-error correction claim is made.

## Proof

**Lemma 1 (projection equivalence).** A binary six-coordinate code \(C\) has minimum cyclic pair-distance at least \(4\) exactly when, for each \(i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), deleting the two adjacent coordinates \(i\) and \(i+1\) is injective on \(C\).

*Proof.* Given two distinct words, let \(S\ne\varnothing\) be the support of their coordinatewise XOR. Their pair distance is the number of cyclic edges incident to \(S\): a pair differs precisely when at least one of its two coordinates is in \(S\). If \(S\) is a singleton then exactly two such edges occur; if \(S\) is a cyclically adjacent pair then exactly three occur. For any \(S\) containing at least three vertices, the number of incident edges is at least four: a run of three consecutive vertices touches four edges, and adding vertices cannot decrease this count. If \(|S|=2\) with nonadjacent vertices, their incident-edge union also has size four. Therefore pair-distance less than four is equivalent to having all differing positions contained in a cyclic adjacent two-position set. The latter is exactly failure of injectivity of the corresponding four-coordinate puncturing map. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2 (Singleton equality and exact cover).** If \(|C|=16\), the conditions in Lemma 1 are equivalent to saying that each of the six adjacent-two-coordinate erasure patterns maps \(C\) bijectively onto the sixteen possible words on the other four positions. In particular \(|C|\le16\) for pair-distance at least four.

*Proof.* Each puncturing map has a 16-element codomain. Injectivity implies the bound, and when \(|C|=16\), injectivity is equivalent to bijectivity. \(\square\)

**Lemma 3 (exhaustive classification certificate).** There are exactly 76 sixteen-subsets satisfying the six bijectivity conditions; 44 are affine, and the full bit-translation/dihedral action gives five orbits of the sizes and types listed in the theorem.

*Proof by finite exhaustive algorithms.* Number the original 64 possible words by their binary values. For each adjacent erasure index \(i\) and each surviving four-coordinate pattern \(v\), create one constraint labeled \((i,v)\). There are exactly \(6\cdot16=96\) constraints. Each word \(w\) satisfies exactly six of them, one for each \(i\). By Lemma 2, an admissible sixteen-word code is exactly an *exact cover* of all 96 constraints by sixteen word-rows.

The standalone Python program `pair6_verify.py` constructs these 96 labeled columns, recurses by choosing the first currently smallest-size uncovered column and trying every non-conflicting row covering it, and records each full exact cover. The recursion is exhaustive because an exact cover must choose precisely one of the rows covering that selected column. Rows overlapping any already covered column are impossible in an exact cover, so pruning them is sound. This search checks **685 recursive nodes** and produces **76 distinct sorted sixteen-word sets**.

Independently, `pair6_enum.cpp` builds the graph on all 64 words joining two exactly when their pair-distance is at least four. An admissible sixteen-word code is precisely a sixteen-clique, as the distance condition is pairwise. The program visits candidates in strictly increasing integer order and intersects the remaining candidate set with each new vertex neighborhood. Every clique has a unique increasing listing, so exactly one search path, and pruning a node when its present size plus candidate count is less than sixteen cannot remove any sixteen-clique. It visits **391,978 recursion nodes** and lists **76 solutions**. The 76 sorted solution lists are identical to the independently constructed exact-cover lists and to `pair6_codes.txt`.

To test affinity without presupposing linearity, take any \(c\in C\) and test whether \(x\oplus y\oplus c\in C\) for every pair \(x,y\in C\). This is equivalent to \(C\oplus c\) being closed under vector addition; since it contains zero and 16 elements, it is a four-dimensional subspace. Exactly 44 codes pass.

For each code, enumerate its images under all 64 binary translations, six rotations, and two reflections; these 768 transformations are graph isometries because they preserve which coordinate pairs are cyclic neighbors. Partitioning the list of all 76 codes into the resulting orbits produces the five listed in the theorem. The sizes add to \(4+32+24+12+4=76\), and the unique size-32 orbit is nonaffine. Because each image is one of the previously exhaustively listed codes, no orbit classification relies on assuming an unattested design. \(\square\)

Lemmas 1--3 prove the theorem. The finite computational step is exhaustive by the supplied complete search logic; it is **not** an extrapolation from random sampling, partial enumeration, or a timeout.

## Verification

The reproducible files are `pair6_enum.cpp`, `pair6_verify.py`, and `pair6_codes.txt`. With a C++17 compiler and Python 3, run:

```sh
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 pair6_enum.cpp -o pair6_enum
./pair6_enum > enum_output.txt
python3 pair6_verify.py
```

The C++ listing has a single comment/header line giving 391978 nodes and 76 solutions, followed by one line per labeled code. Compare the rest with `pair6_codes.txt`. The Python program independently enumerates exact covers, asserts the minimum pair distance of every code, checks the catalogue as a set, checks affinity, and reconstructs the five full orbits. It reports `EXACT_COVER_OK` and the expected counts.

## Relationship to prior work

Chee, Ji, Kiah, Wang, and Yin establish the Singleton-type symbol-pair bound and exhibit constructions realizing the distance-four case, including even lengths through an interleaving method; their Corollary 3.2(iv) implies existence for length six. These prior contributions are not part of the novelty claim. Ding, Ge, J. Zhang, T. Zhang, and Y. Zhang develop **linear** MDS symbol-pair constructions with larger distances; their overview explicitly attributes the distance-four existence results to Chee and collaborators. Neither inspected article enumerates all binary codes of the present size and length, their nonlinear fraction, or their complete symmetry classes.

The cyclic adjacent-two-erasure equivalence is used as a classification device, not a separate alleged novel theorem. The earlier unrestricted-code size bound is not strengthened; instead the equality cases are exhaustively determined in the full nonlinear domain.

## Limitations

The theorem is an exact, reproducible finite-parameter result for binary length six, not a statement for arbitrary block lengths or alphabets. Affine codes are counted by their labeled subsets, not by generator matrices; bit flips and dihedral symmetries are used only for the second classification. The literature comparison supports a claim of no identified previous complete census, but cannot rule out obscure or unindexed equivalent work. This is not an externally independently audited mathematical certificate.
