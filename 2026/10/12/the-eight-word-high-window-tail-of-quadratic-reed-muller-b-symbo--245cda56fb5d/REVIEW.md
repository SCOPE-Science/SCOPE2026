# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem quantifies every integer dimension \(m\ge3\) and each integer cyclic read width in the stated domain. Quadratic-form canonical reduction gives a rigorous Hamming threshold; affine fiber geometry classifies the eight and only eight short supports. The zero-window count yields all weight coefficients, and the code dimension accounts for every word. The independent checker verifies the exact enumerators in dimensions three to five and a distinct affine-coset census in dimensions three to eight. The proofs, not finite enumeration, establish the unbounded-parameter theorem. No unpublished computer calculation is required for the mathematical implication.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** The closest primary result is Pan–Ling–Liu (arXiv:2309.08920), Theorem 4.6, which fixes the minimum \(b\)-symbol distance for this exact code and coordinate order but not its coefficient distribution. Zhu–Li–Shi–Xia–Solé (arXiv:2404.02471) bounds the number of possible \(b\)-symbol weights, not their exact multiplicities for this family. Vega's explicit weight distributions are for semiprimitive irreducible cyclic codes, not the present length-power-of-two binary Reed–Muller codes. A previous first-order Reed–Muller enumerator concerns a strictly smaller evaluation space and does not imply the quadratic classification. Targeted independent literature and indexed-finding comparisons identified no equivalent or dominating statement; an unindexed or implicit prior equivalent remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The exact tail enumerator refines the published minimum-distance result to a complete all-coefficients theorem for an infinite classical-code family. The dimension-independent multiplicity eight and a sharp five-eighths transition explain a structural concentration phenomenon determined by the geometry of lexicographically embedded minimum-weight affine flats. The result is not a restatement of a minimum-distance inequality: it identifies precisely which and how many codewords are exceptional.
