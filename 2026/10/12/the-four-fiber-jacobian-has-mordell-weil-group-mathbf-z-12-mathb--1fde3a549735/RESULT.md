# The four-fiber Jacobian has Mordell–Weil group \(\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z\)
## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(5\). Consider the explicit Sun–Xu semistable fibration
\[
f:S\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1_k
\]
obtained from the pencil on \(\mathbb F_3\)
\[
F_0+tF_1=0,
\qquad
F_0=w\bigl(w+x(x-1)\bigr),
\qquad
F_1=\bigl(x^4-(x-1)^4\bigr)w-x(x-1)^5,
\]
after the twelve blow-ups resolving its two proper base points. Put \(K=k(t)\), let \(C=S_\eta\) be the smooth generic genus-\(2\) fiber, and let \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)\). Then
\[
J(K)\cong\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z.
\]
Equivalently, the Mordell–Weil rank is zero and the full Mordell–Weil group is cyclic of exact order \(12\).

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(C_0\) for the negative section and \(\Gamma\) for a ruling fiber on \(\mathbb F_3\). Let
\[
e_{0,1},\ldots,e_{0,6},e_{1,1},\ldots,e_{1,6}
\]
denote the total-transform exceptional classes of the six successive blow-ups above each of the two proper base points. Because \(S\) is the blow-up of the rational surface \(\mathbb F_3\) at these twelve successive points,
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(S)
=
\mathbf Z C_0\oplus\mathbf Z\Gamma
\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{6}\mathbf Z e_{0,i}
\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{6}\mathbf Z e_{1,i}.
\]
The intersection form is
\[
C_0^2=-3,
\qquad
C_0\cdot\Gamma=1,
\qquad
\Gamma^2=0,
\qquad
e_{a,i}^2=-1,
\]
with all other exceptional pairings zero.

Sun–Xu prove that the only singular fibers are over \(0,1,3,\infty\). The fibers over \(1\) and \(3\) are irreducible. The fiber over \(0\) has twelve rational components, and the fiber over \(\infty\) has two rational components. The argument below concerns only this explicit characteristic-\(5\) fibration.

## Proof
Every member of the resolved pencil has class
\[
F=2C_0+6\Gamma-
\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_{0,i}-
\sum_{i=1}^{6}e_{1,i}.
\]
For \(1\le i\le5\), the vertical exceptional components over the two base points have classes
\[
E_{0,i}=e_{0,i}-e_{0,i+1},
\qquad
E_{1,i}=e_{1,i}-e_{1,i+1}.
\]

At \(t=0\), Sun–Xu write the two original components as \(A:w=0\) and \(B:w+x(x-1)=0\). Their restrictions of \(F_1\) have contact orders \((1,5)\) and \((5,1)\) at the two proper base points. Consequently the strict transforms have classes
\[
A=C_0+3\Gamma-e_{0,1}-e_{1,1}-e_{1,2}-e_{1,3}-e_{1,4}-e_{1,5},
\]
\[
B=C_0+3\Gamma-e_{0,1}-e_{0,2}-e_{0,3}-e_{0,4}-e_{0,5}-e_{1,1}.
\]
These classes satisfy
\[
F=A+B+
\sum_{i=1}^{5}E_{0,i}+
\sum_{i=1}^{5}E_{1,i},
\]
and \(A^2=B^2=-3\), exactly as in the source's resolved fiber. At \(t=\infty\), one component is \(C_0\); the other is \(F-C_0\). Thus the subgroup \(V\subset\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) generated by all vertical divisors is generated by the thirteen classes
\[
F,\ C_0,\ A,\ E_{0,1},\ldots,E_{0,5},\ E_{1,1},\ldots,E_{1,5}.
\]
The irreducible fibers over \(1\) and \(3\) add no generator beyond \(F\).

Write these thirteen generators as columns in the displayed Picard basis. The resulting \(14\times13\) integer matrix has rank \(13\). Its fourteen maximal minors are
\[
0,24,-12,12,-12,12,-12,12,-12,12,-12,12,-12,12,
\]
whose greatest common divisor is \(12\). A specified \(12\times12\) minor has determinant \(1\). Therefore the Smith invariant factors are
\[
1,\ldots,1,12
\]
with twelve copies of \(1\), and hence
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(S)/V
\cong
\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z.
\]

Restriction to the generic fiber identifies this quotient with \(\operatorname{Pic}(C)\). Indeed, every divisor on the regular generic curve closes to a horizontal prime divisor on the regular surface, while a divisor on \(S\) restricts trivially precisely after subtracting a principal divisor if its remaining support is vertical.

The sixth exceptional curve above either proper base point is horizontal in the source. Its class is \(e_{0,6}\), respectively \(e_{1,6}\), and
\[
e_{0,6}\cdot F=e_{1,6}\cdot F=1.
\]
Thus each is a section of \(f\). In particular \(C(K)\neq\varnothing\), the degree map on \(\operatorname{Pic}(C)\) is split, and its degree-zero subgroup identifies with \(J(K)\). Since every torsion class has degree zero and the free rank-one summand maps nontrivially to \(\mathbf Z\), we obtain
\[
J(K)=\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)(K)\cong\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
Sun–Xu construct the characteristic-\(5\) fibration, resolve the two six-step base-point chains, determine all four singular fibers, and prove that the total surface is rational. Their paper does not state a Mordell–Weil group computation. The exact order-\(12\) result uses their complete integral component data, not just the numerical count of components.

The characteristic-zero work of Saito–Sakakibara develops higher-genus Mordell–Weil lattices on rational surfaces, and Gong–Xu later proves rank bounds including a positive-characteristic analogue. Those results supply broader rank/lattice context, but the checked statements do not give the integral quotient for this new characteristic-\(5\) reduction. In particular, a rank-zero conclusion would not determine the torsion subgroup.

Sun–Xu explicitly say that the pencil itself is reproduced from Nguyen and originates in Saito. Those older sources are therefore a genuine comparison risk. The accessible material confirms their characteristic-zero/higher-genus-rank context, but a complete text of the most directly relevant 1995 sources was not available through the checked lawful public routes. The originality claim is accordingly limited to the exact characteristic-\(5\) group above.

## Limitations
The computation is specific to the Sun–Xu reduction in characteristic \(5\). It does not determine the Mordell–Weil group of the characteristic-zero pencil, any other reduction, or a deformation of the four-fiber surface.

The proof uses the source's resolution data, including the contact orders at the two proper base points, the fact that the first five exceptional curves in each chain are vertical, the horizontality of the sixth exceptional curves, and the two-component fiber at infinity. It independently performs the integral lattice quotient from those data.

A residual literature risk remains because the 1995 Saito and Nguyen texts most closely tied to the original pencil were not fully accessible in the checked public sources. No located source states the characteristic-\(5\) order-\(12\) conclusion.
