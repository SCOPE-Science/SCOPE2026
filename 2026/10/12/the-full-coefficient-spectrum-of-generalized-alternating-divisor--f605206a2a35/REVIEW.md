# Self-review: full coefficient spectrum of generalized alternating divisor sums

## Correctness: PASS

The claim concerns one ordinary \(X^2\)-normalized limit for each fixed prime subset \(Q\), the complete range of those limits, and the exact zero-coefficient distinction between finite and infinite prime sets. The proof reconstructs the finite-set asymptotic using inclusion–exclusion, partial summation and the divisor convolution. A uniform inequality \(\sigma(pm)\le(p+1)\sigma(m)\) provides the missing infinite-set truncation estimate when prime reciprocals converge. When they diverge, positivity and monotone finite products prove the limiting weighted-free part is zero. An independently checked greedy product argument realizes every target, and odd-denominator arithmetic excludes zero cancellation for all finite sets. The executable finite checks succeeded but play no role as infinite certificates.

## Originality: PASS

Tóth's 2017 Theorem 57 assumes a finite set of primes; Open Problem 58 expressly asks for arbitrary fixed prime subsets for this divisor-sum asymptotic. His Proposition 56 allows an arbitrary subset in a Dirichlet-series identity, not in the asserted ordinary summatory limit. Zurita's 2020 Theorem 4 still states the finite-set formula. The new infinite-set limit, its complete coefficient spectrum, and the zero-leading-term finite/infinite separation are not proved in these sources. published-finding corpus searches for the equation, arbitrary-subset variant, Euler-product spectrum and cancellation found no implication-equivalent mathematical record. A residual risk is that a separately indexed general weighted prime-sieve/B-free mean-value theorem implies the same asymptotic; the searches performed cannot eliminate that bibliographic possibility. Results for other divisor functions and unrelated Euler products are not automatically equivalent.

## Value: PASS

The theorem provides the \(\sigma\)-specific answer to the natural infinite-subset part of Tóth's explicit Open Problem 58, without demanding restrictive density hypotheses. The achievable-coefficient interval shows that moving from finite to infinite exclusions changes the possible leading-term behavior: a precisely constructed infinite set achieves complete quadratic cancellation even though no finite prime set can. The prime-tail estimate also makes the limit stable under finite approximations when reciprocal sums converge. This is a structural asymptotic statement about a standard arithmetic function, rather than a numerical experiment or an isolated arbitrary input.
