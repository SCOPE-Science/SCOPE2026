# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The unique rational point forces every field-defined abstract automorphism to fix its hyperosculating point and hence the degree-one plane linear series. The complete linear-system argument reduces the entire abstract group to a projective stabilizer. Coefficient comparison proves necessity and sufficiency of all three matrix parameters in every characteristic; trace-fiber counting gives order \(s^3(s+1)\), and direct commutator calculations give the nonabelian Heisenberg kernel and the one-branch-point invariant field. Exact finite-field tests over degrees four, nine and twenty-five replay independently; only the symbolic argument establishes the infinite family.

**Originality — PASS, subject to a specific unresolved source risk.** Homma and Kim's October 2026 preprint, Theorem 7, gives the unique rational point, its inflexional tangent, and projective equivalence, not the full field-defined abstract automorphism group. The 2024 predecessor proves generic facts about Hermitian-relative curves and geometric equivalence but not this arithmetic group. Focused bibliographic comparisons did not locate the explicit triangular group law or one-branch-point Heisenberg quotient. The first-online-31-May-2026 conference chapter on projective equivalence is extremely relevant; its public abstract, but not its full text, was available. Thus an equivalent stabilizer computation within that chapter remains an explicit priority risk; public absence of a formula is not a proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The result computes a natural intrinsic arithmetic symmetry group across an infinite family of maximally sparse rational-point curves. It establishes the structure and order of the wild normal subgroup, not just its cardinality, and identifies the associated one-branch-point Galois function-field quotient, which are useful inputs for arithmetic quotients, ramification and automorphism classifications.

Independent confirmation of bibliographic priority and of the group calculation remains desirable; geometric automorphisms over an algebraic closure are outside the theorem.
