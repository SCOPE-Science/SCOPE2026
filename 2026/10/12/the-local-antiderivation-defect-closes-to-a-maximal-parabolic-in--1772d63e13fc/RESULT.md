# The local-antiderivation defect closes to a maximal parabolic in one triple step

## Finding
Let \(L\) be the four-dimensional complex Lie algebra with basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) and nonzero brackets
\[
[e_4,e_1]=e_1,\qquad [e_4,e_2]=-e_2,\qquad [e_4,e_3]=e_3.
\]
An antiderivation is a linear map \(D:L\to L\) satisfying
\[
D([x,y])=-[D(x),y]-[x,D(y)]
\]
for all \(x,y\in L\). Let \(\mathcal S=\operatorname{LocDer}_{-1}(L)\) denote the local antiderivations: for every \(x\in L\), there is an antiderivation \(D_x\) with \(T(x)=D_x(x)\).

Then the smallest Lie triple system in \(\operatorname{End}(L)\) containing \(\mathcal S\) is equal to the Lie subalgebra generated by \(\mathcal S\), and both are the \(13\)-dimensional maximal parabolic
\[
\mathfrak p=\{T\in\operatorname{End}(L):T(U)\subseteq U\},\qquad U=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}.
\]
More explicitly,
\[
\mathcal S=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
s&a&0&p\\
b&u&c&q\\
0&d&s&r\\
0&0&0&v
\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c,d,p,q,r,s,u,v\in\mathbb C
\right\}.
\]
Writing \(H=E_{11}-E_{22}\) and \(D_0=E_{11}+E_{33}\), the three missing directions are generated already by one triple-bracket layer:
\[
[[D_0,E_{12}],E_{21}]=H,\qquad
[[D_0,E_{12}],E_{23}]=E_{13},\qquad
[[D_0,E_{21}],E_{32}]=E_{31}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathcal S+\operatorname{span}[[\mathcal S,\mathcal S],\mathcal S]=\mathfrak p,
\]
and no further Lie or Lie-triple closure enlarges the space.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). Matrices are written in the ordered basis \((e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)\), with the \(j\)-th column equal to the coordinates of the image of \(e_j\). The Lie bracket on \(\operatorname{End}(L)\) is the commutator \([A,B]=AB-BA\), and a Lie triple system is a linear subspace closed under \([[A,B],C]\).

The statement concerns the particular four-dimensional Lie algebra used in the recent literature to demonstrate that local antiderivations need not themselves form a Lie triple system. It determines the complete closure defect of that example; it does not assert a corresponding closure theorem for arbitrary Lie algebras or arbitrary local \(\delta\)-derivations.

## Proof
Solving the antiderivation identity on the ordered basis pairs gives the eight-dimensional space
\[
\operatorname{Der}_{-1}(L)=
\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}
t&a&0&p\\
b&t&c&q\\
0&d&t&r\\
0&0&0&-2t
\end{pmatrix}
\right\}.
\]
This can be checked directly from the three nonzero brackets: the only diagonal degree of freedom is \((t,t,t,-2t)\), while the seven displayed off-diagonal entries are unrestricted.

We next reconstruct the local space without assuming its published matrix description. If \(T\) is local, evaluation at \(e_1\), \(e_2\), and \(e_3\) shows that its first three columns must have the zero pattern displayed in \(\mathcal S\). Evaluation at \(e_1+e_3\) forces equality of the \((1,1)\) and \((3,3)\) entries. These are exactly six independent linear constraints on \(M_4(\mathbb C)\), leaving dimension \(10\).

Conversely, every matrix of the displayed form is local. For \(x=x_1e_1+x_2e_2+x_3e_3+x_4e_4\), one constructs an antiderivation matching \(T(x)\) by cases. If \(x_4\ne0\), set the antiderivation diagonal parameter to \(-v/2\) and use the free fourth-column parameters to absorb the first three coordinate discrepancies. If \(x_4=0\) and \(x_2\ne0\), use the free \((1,2)\) and \((3,2)\) parameters to absorb the first and third coordinate discrepancies and the free \((2,1)\), \((2,3)\) parameters for the second. If \(x_4=x_2=0\) and \((x_1,x_3)\ne(0,0)\), choose the diagonal parameter to be \(s\) and use the free second-row parameters to match the remaining second coordinate. The zero vector is trivial. Hence the local space is exactly \(\mathcal S\).

The space \(\mathcal S\) already contains \(D_0,E_{12},E_{21},E_{23},E_{32}\). Direct matrix multiplication gives the three displayed triple brackets, so the Lie-triple closure contains \(H,E_{13},E_{31}\). Adding these directions to \(\mathcal S\) yields every matrix of the form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
*&*&*&*\\
*&*&*&*\\
*&*&*&*\\
0&0&0&*
\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is exactly \(\mathfrak p\), the stabilizer of \(U\). Since a stabilizer of a subspace is a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathfrak{gl}(L)\), \(\mathfrak p\) is in particular closed under triple brackets. Therefore it is the smallest Lie triple system containing \(\mathcal S\).

For ordinary Lie closure, the same missing directions arise already from pairwise commutators:
\[
[E_{12},E_{21}]=H,\qquad [E_{12},E_{23}]=E_{13},\qquad [E_{32},E_{21}]=E_{31}.
\]
Thus the Lie algebra generated by \(\mathcal S\) is also \(\mathfrak p\).

## Verification
The accompanying exact-arithmetic verifier reconstructs the eight-dimensional antiderivation space from the defining identity, checks the ten-dimensional local candidate, verifies the explicit triple-bracket identities, computes both the ordinary Lie closure and the Lie-triple closure, and confirms that each has dimension \(13\) and equals the stabilizer of \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\). Its output ends with `CHECK_OK`.

The finite integer-grid checks in the verifier are regression checks for the constructive local-witness formula; they are not used as an infinite proof. The proof of locality for all complex vectors is the case analysis above, and the closure statement follows from exact symbolic matrix identities plus the Lie-subalgebra property of the stabilizer.

## Relationship to prior work
Hao and Chen exhibit this same Lie algebra and compute both its antiderivations and local antiderivations. Their example uses \(E_{12},E_{22},E_{21}\) to prove that the local-antiderivation space is not closed under triple brackets. The inspected version does not determine the smallest Lie triple system containing that space, its dimension, or its identification with a maximal parabolic.

Related recent work on local antiderivations of Jacobi--Jordan algebras proves closure phenomena in a different algebraic variety and does not imply the present statement for this nonabelian Lie algebra. Searches for the exact matrix example, its triple closure, parabolic closure, and equivalent reflexive-hull formulations did not locate a published statement giving the \(13\)-dimensional closure identified here.

## Limitations
The result is specific to this four-dimensional complex Lie algebra and to antiderivations. It does not classify Lie-triple closures of local antiderivations in other Lie algebras. Originality assessment is limited by the recency of the motivating preprint and by the possibility of unindexed or later material using different terminology. No independent audit has been performed.
