# Scientific review: Möbius–Kantor Kempe reconfiguration

**Correctness — PASS.** Every unlabeled edge-coloring is a partition of the twenty-four edges into three perfect matchings. A lowest-uncovered-vertex matching recursion proves the completeness of the thirty-three-matching census; pair-complement enumeration then proves the twenty-factorization census. All Kempe moves are generated by the connected components of the three bichromatic two-factors in each factorization. The verified explicit vertex bijection from the sixteen-coloring component onto the original graph maps all twenty-four component edges onto exactly the original edge set. An independently written edge-by-edge coloring search finds the same twenty factorizations and twenty-seven switch edges. This proves a *finite fixed-graph* statement only.

**Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk.** Belcastro–Haas (arXiv:1209.1730, 2012) define Kempe equivalence and prove general decomposition results; their 2017 Australasian Journal paper studies the relevant normalized reconfiguration graphs, including a *fourteen*-vertex generalized Petersen example, but does not exhibit \(GP(8,3)\) or the self-copy-plus-claw classification. Goedgebeur–Östergård (arXiv:2105.01363) census all cubic bipartite graphs of this order; their general algorithm and class-count table might recover the **number of classes**, which is therefore not claimed independently new. Their printed results do not specify the twenty-vertex adjacency graph, explicit self-isomorphism or the componentwise bichromatic profiles. The closest same-base-graph records inspected concern the matching complex and identifying codes, with no implication for Kempe adjacency. Residual priority risk: published or unindexed program output may contain the same graph-specific classification, and the broader census could allow its reconstruction.

**Value — PASS.** Reconfiguration graph structure is a natural invariant, studied explicitly in the 2017 source. In this classical symmetric cubic graph, one of the two Kempe classes is a graph isomorphic to the *original base graph*, an exact self-reproduction phenomenon rather than merely an aggregate count. The disconnected three-leaf-star component is uniquely separated by its alternating-cycle profiles. Explicit adjacency and isomorphism certificates give a reusable benchmark for edge-Kempe dynamics.
