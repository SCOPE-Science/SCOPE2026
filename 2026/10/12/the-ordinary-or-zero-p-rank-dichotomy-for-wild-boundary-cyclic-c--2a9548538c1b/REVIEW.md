# Mathematical review: the ordinary-or-zero \(p\)-rank dichotomy

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is precisely smooth projective curves over an algebraically closed odd-characteristic field admitting the specified wild cyclic subgroup. The cited classification [1, Theorem 4.8] exhausts eight normal forms. Deuring--Shafarevich gives \(\gamma(X)=p\gamma(Z)+(p-1)(r-1)\), and the rational-base pole numbers give five ranks. For the three positive-genus bases the explicit birational transformations, Hasse coefficients and nilpotent genus-two Hasse--Witt matrix prove \(\gamma(Z)=0\); one branch pole gives the remaining ranks. A replayable exact checker verifies model identities, Hasse calculations, Riemann--Hurwitz genera, and all eight arithmetic rows. The universal proof rests on the general formula and quotient computations, not on tests alone.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The initiating paper [1] gives a complete equation list but does not give the evaluated \(p\)-ranks. Pries--Zhu [2] already implies the five rational-base row values once those equations are known. Booher--Cais [3] gives the general cover formula, but the three explicit nonrational quotient ranks and full boundary invariant classification are not supplied there. The two characteristic-three genus-six families have equal \((p,N,g)\) and different ranks. Published general formulas make the calculations accessible; an older implicit or explicit rank table cannot be categorically excluded. Comparisons included matching the exact equations, parameters, and theorem implications rather than title-only matching.

## Value — PASS
The entire natural wild cyclic boundary locus is shown to be ordinary or of \(p\)-rank zero, with an exact list and a decisive invariant separating the two order-twelve genus-six families. The rank is intrinsic to the Jacobian and can govern its behavior in moduli. The result is a complete and motivated invariant calculation, not a classification of all Newton polygons or of higher cyclic orders.
