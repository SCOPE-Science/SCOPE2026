# The RPSL zero mass contradicts its Lagrange equation
## Finding
Consider the rational-power shifted Lagrangian (RPSL) generator
\[
g(z)
=
c+(1-c)
\left(
\frac{1-\theta}{1-\theta z}
\right)^\gamma,
\qquad
0<c<1,\quad 0<\theta<1,\quad \gamma>0,
\]
and let \(\psi(u)\) be the smallest solution of
\[
\psi(u)=u\,g(\psi(u)).
\]

The defining equation itself forces
\[
\boxed{\psi(0)=0.}
\]
Therefore the power series of \(\psi\) has no constant term. By Lagrange inversion,
\[
\psi(u)
=
\sum_{n\ge1}p_nu^n,
\qquad
p_n
=
\frac1n[z^{n-1}]g(z)^n,
\]
which is exactly the positive-integer formula used in the source.

Hence the source's additional assignment
\[
\Pr(X=0)=g(0)
=
c+(1-c)(1-\theta)^\gamma
\]
cannot belong to the probability law generated by \(\psi\). The same conclusion follows directly from the classical two-function Lagrange-distribution formula: the positive coefficients above correspond to choosing \(f(z)=z\), so the zero mass is \(f(0)=0\), not \(g(0)\).

There is also a sharp normalization phase. Put
\[
m=g'(1)
=
\frac{(1-c)\gamma\theta}{1-\theta}.
\]
Because \(g\) is a probability-generating function, the coefficients \(p_n\) are the finite-total-progeny probabilities of a Galton-Watson process with offspring PGF \(g\). Their total mass is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\ge1}p_n=q,
}
\]
where \(q\) is the smallest solution in \((0,1]\) of
\[
q=g(q).
\]

For
\[
m\le1,
\]
one has
\[
q=1.
\]
Thus the proper law generated by the paper's functional equation is already normalized on
\[
\{1,2,\ldots\},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\Pr(X=0)=0.}
\]
Adding the proposed atom \(g(0)>0\) makes the total claimed mass
\[
1+g(0)>1.
\]

For
\[
m>1,
\]
one has
\[
q<1.
\]
Then the coefficients \(p_n\) describe finite total progeny and leave mass
\[
1-q
\]
at infinity. A proper finite-count distribution is obtained only after conditioning on finiteness:
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(X=n\mid X<\infty)=\frac{p_n}{q},
\qquad n\ge1.
}
\]
Again there is no zero atom generated by \(\psi\).

At the simulation parameters emphasized in the source,
\[
(\gamma,\theta,c)=(1.5,0.4,0.3),
\]
the offspring mean is
\[
m
=
\frac{0.7\cdot1.5\cdot0.4}{0.6}
=
0.7<1.
\]
Hence the positive Lagrange coefficients already sum to one. Yet
\[
g(0)
=
0.3+0.7(0.6)^{1.5}
=
0.625331\ldots.
\]
The source's claimed full PMF would therefore have total mass
\[
1.625330\ldots,
\]
not one.

The source's reported mean near \(3.33\) at these parameters is instead consistent with the positive-support subcritical total-progeny law:
\[
\mathbb E[X]
=
\frac1{1-m}
=
\frac{10}{3}.
\]
It is incompatible with simultaneously adding the advertised zero mass without a further renormalization that would change the mean.

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns the exact RPSL construction in which \(\psi\) is defined as the smallest root of
\[
z=ug(z)
\]
and the positive probabilities are obtained from the corresponding Lagrange coefficients.

The generator \(g\) is a genuine PGF: it is the mixture of an atom at zero of weight \(c\) and a negative-binomial-type PGF of weight \(1-c\). This makes the branching interpretation exact rather than heuristic.

At the critical point \(m=1\), the positive coefficients still sum to one but their mean total progeny is infinite. For \(m<1\), their mean is \(1/(1-m)\). For \(m>1\), the unconditioned total progeny takes the value \(+\infty\) with probability \(1-q\).

The claim does not reassess the paper's fitted regression coefficients or goodness-of-fit values. It identifies a normalization defect in the stated probability model and the simulation interpretation built from its claimed zero mass.

## Proof
Setting \(u=0\) in
\[
\psi(u)=u g(\psi(u))
\]
gives
\[
\psi(0)=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
[u^0]\psi(u)=0.
\]

Lagrange inversion applied to
\[
\psi=u g(\psi)
\]
gives
\[
[u^n]\psi(u)
=
\frac1n[z^{n-1}]g(z)^n,
\qquad n\ge1.
\]
These are exactly the source's positive-index coefficients.

The classical Consul-Shenton Lagrange family uses two PGFs \(g\) and \(f\):
\[
\Pr(X=0)=f(0),
\]
and, for positive \(n\),
\[
\Pr(X=n)
=
\frac1{n!}
\left.
\frac{d^{n-1}}{dz^{n-1}}
\{g(z)^n f'(z)\}
\right|_{z=0}.
\]
The RPSL positive formula omits any factor other than \(g(z)^n\), so it is the case
\[
f'(z)=1,
\qquad f(z)=z,
\]
with
\[
f(0)=0.
\]
Replacing this zero mass by \(g(0)\) is not licensed by that theorem.

Next,
\[
g(1)=1,
\qquad
g'(1)
=
\frac{(1-c)\gamma\theta}{1-\theta}
=m.
\]
The coefficients of \(g\) are nonnegative, so \(g\) is an offspring PGF. The recursive decomposition of a Galton-Watson family tree gives the total-progeny PGF equation
\[
\psi(u)=u g(\psi(u)).
\]
At \(u=1\), the finite-total-progeny mass is therefore the smallest fixed point
\[
q=g(q).
\]

For a nondegenerate offspring PGF, standard convexity gives
\[
q=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m\le1,
\]
and
\[
q<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m>1.
\]
This proves both normalization regimes.

Finally, at
\[
(\gamma,\theta,c)=(1.5,0.4,0.3),
\]
direct substitution gives
\[
m=0.7
\]
and
\[
g(0)=0.3+0.7(0.6)^{1.5}=0.625331\ldots.
\]
Since \(m<1\),
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}p_n=1,
\]
so the claimed extra zero atom raises the total mass to
\[
1+g(0)=1.625330\ldots.
\]

## Verification
The accompanying checker evaluates the source's generator and its Lagrange coefficients directly.

For the source's simulation parameter triple, it verifies
\[
g(0)=0.625331\ldots,
\qquad
g'(1)=0.7,
\]
and numerically accumulates the positive Lagrange coefficients toward one.

For a supercritical example it solves
\[
q=g(q)
\]
for the smallest fixed point, verifies \(q<1\), and accumulates the positive coefficients toward that same value.

The checker also verifies symbolically at the functional-equation level that the constant coefficient of \(\psi(u)\) is zero. The numerical coefficient sums are consistency checks; the normalization phase follows analytically from the PGF fixed-point argument.

## Relationship to prior work
The RPSL source explicitly defines \(\psi(u)\) as the smallest root of
\[
z=ug(z),
\]
prints the positive Lagrange coefficients, then separately claims that \(g(0)\ne0\) implies
\[
\Pr(X=0)=\psi(0)=g(0).
\]
Those statements cannot all hold: the functional equation gives \(\psi(0)=0\) immediately.

The source cites Consul and Shenton's foundational Lagrange-expansion paper as justification. That paper uses two functions: the zero mass is \(f(0)\), while the positive coefficients contain \(f'(z)\). The RPSL positive formula is precisely the \(f(z)=z\) case, whose zero mass is zero.

The branching interpretation supplies the second correction. The equation
\[
\psi=u g(\psi)
\]
is the standard total-progeny PGF equation. It explains why the paper's finite-mean condition
\[
(1-c)\gamma\theta<1-\theta
\]
is exactly the subcritical condition \(m<1\), and it exposes a supercritical defective phase that is absent from the stated count-law normalization.

Focused searches using the exact DOI, RPSL name, zero-mass statement, normalization, extinction-probability terminology, and Lagrange-equation aliases did not locate a published correction of this defect.

## Limitations
The result does not determine whether every numerical simulation in the paper used the written PMF exactly. The source instructs numerical normalization of a truncated PMF in its simulation section, which can mask a normalization defect while generating a different law from the analytic one.

No claim is made that the positive-index coefficient formula itself is wrong. The correction concerns its normalization, the added zero atom, and the parameter regimes in which it defines an ordinary finite-valued count distribution.

For \(m>1\), the conditioned finite-total-progeny law is a valid distribution after division by \(q\), but its inferential properties are not developed here.
