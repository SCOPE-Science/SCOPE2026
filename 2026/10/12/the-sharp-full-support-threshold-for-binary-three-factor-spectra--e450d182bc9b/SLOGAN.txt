For binary strings of every length \(n\ge14\), observing that **all eight** possible consecutive three-bit patterns occur leaves strictly more possible strings than any other presence/absence pattern. The threshold is sharp: at length \(13\) the two most ambiguous spectra each omit a constant triple, while from length \(14\) onward the all-present spectrum is uniquely most ambiguous, with an exact all-length inclusion-exclusion count.
