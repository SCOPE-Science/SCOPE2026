# The symmetric group \(S_3\) has \(129\) averaging maps and \(51\) pointed ones

## Finding

Let \(G\) be a group. Following Das, an averaging operator is a set map
\[
\mathcal A:G\to G
\]
satisfying
\[
\mathcal A(g)\mathcal A(h)\mathcal A(g)^{-1}
=
\mathcal A\!\left(\mathcal A(g)h\mathcal A(g)^{-1}\right)
\]
for all \(g,h\in G\). It is pointed when
\[
\mathcal A(e)=e.
\]

There is a useful structural reformulation.

Put
\[
H=\langle \mathcal A(G)\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A\text{ is averaging}
\iff
\mathcal A(khk^{-1})=k\mathcal A(h)k^{-1}
\text{ for all }k\in H,\ h\in G.
}
\]

Thus an averaging operator is exactly a map that is equivariant for the conjugation action of the subgroup generated by its image.

For the smallest nonabelian group
\[
G=S_3,
\]
this gives a complete finite classification.

There are exactly
\[
\boxed{129}
\]
averaging maps \(S_3\to S_3\).

Stratifying by
\[
H=\langle\mathcal A(S_3)\rangle,
\]
the counts are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
H&1&C_2&C_3&S_3\\ \hline
\#\mathcal A&1&45&80&3.
\end{array}}
\]

Exactly
\[
\boxed{51}
\]
of these maps are pointed. Their corresponding stratum counts are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
H&1&C_2&C_3&S_3\\ \hline
\#\mathcal A\text{ pointed}&1&21&26&3.
\end{array}}
\]

Since
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)=\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)\cong S_3,
\]
we may also quotient by automorphism conjugacy,
\[
(\phi\cdot\mathcal A)(g)
=
\phi\!\left(\mathcal A(\phi^{-1}(g))\right).
\]

The \(129\) averaging maps form exactly
\[
\boxed{60}
\]
automorphism-conjugacy classes, distributed as
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\langle\mathcal A(S_3)\rangle&1&C_2&C_3&S_3\\ \hline
\#\text{ classes}&1&15&41&3.
\end{array}}
\]

The \(51\) pointed averaging maps form exactly
\[
\boxed{25}
\]
classes, distributed as
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\langle\mathcal A(S_3)\rangle&1&C_2&C_3&S_3\\ \hline
\#\text{ pointed classes}&1&7&14&3.
\end{array}}
\]

For all averaging maps, the orbit-size distribution is
\[
\boxed{
6\text{ classes of size }1,\qquad
39\text{ classes of size }2,\qquad
15\text{ classes of size }3.
}
\]
For pointed averaging maps it is
\[
\boxed{
6\text{ classes of size }1,\qquad
12\text{ classes of size }2,\qquad
7\text{ classes of size }3.
}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The definition is the conjugation-based averaging identity introduced by Das in 2024.

This is not the distinct stronger group identity
\[
\mathcal A(g)\mathcal A(h)
=
\mathcal A(\mathcal A(g)h)
=
\mathcal A(g\mathcal A(h))
\]
introduced later by Zhang and Gao. Their paper explicitly distinguishes that identity from Das's pointed averaging identity.

The count concerns all set maps \(S_3\to S_3\), not only homomorphisms, power maps, or constant maps.

The conjugacy quotient is by automorphisms of the group \(S_3\).

## Proof

Start from Das's defining identity:
\[
\mathcal A(g)\mathcal A(h)\mathcal A(g)^{-1}
=
\mathcal A\!\left(\mathcal A(g)h\mathcal A(g)^{-1}\right).
\]
For fixed \(g\), this says precisely that
\[
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal A(g)}\circ\mathcal A
=
\mathcal A\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathcal A(g)}.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal A\) commutes with conjugation by every element of its image.

If a map commutes with \(\operatorname{Ad}_x\) and \(\operatorname{Ad}_y\), then it also commutes with
\[
\operatorname{Ad}_{xy}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_x\operatorname{Ad}_y
\]
and with
\[
\operatorname{Ad}_{x^{-1}}
=
\operatorname{Ad}_x^{-1}.
\]
Therefore it commutes with conjugation by every element of
\[
H=\langle\mathcal A(G)\rangle.
\]
This proves the forward implication of the structural criterion. The reverse implication follows immediately by taking
\[
k=\mathcal A(g)\in H.
\]

Now specialize to \(S_3\).

### The trivial generated-image subgroup

If
\[
H=1,
\]
then the image is \(\{e\}\). There is exactly one map.

### A generated-image subgroup \(C_2\)

Fix a transposition \(t\), and put
\[
H=\langle t\rangle.
\]
Conjugation by \(t\) has four orbits on \(S_3\):
\[
\{e\},\qquad
\{t\},\qquad
\{\text{the other two transpositions}\},\qquad
\{\text{the two }3\text{-cycles}\}.
\]

Since \(H\) is abelian, its conjugation action on itself is trivial. An \(H\)-equivariant map
\[
S_3\to H
\]
is therefore exactly a function constant on those four domain orbits. There are
\[
2^4=16
\]
such functions.

Exactly one has image \(\{e\}\). Hence
\[
2^4-1=15
\]
maps generate this chosen \(C_2\).

There are three subgroups of order \(2\), so this stratum contributes
\[
3\cdot15=45.
\]

For pointed maps, the value at \(e\) is forced to be \(e\). Thus there are
\[
2^3-1=7
\]
pointed maps for each \(C_2\), giving
\[
3\cdot7=21.
\]

### The generated-image subgroup \(C_3\)

Let
\[
H=A_3=\langle r\rangle\cong C_3.
\]
Conjugation by \(H\) has four orbits on \(S_3\):
\[
\{e\},\qquad
\{r\},\qquad
\{r^2\},\qquad
\{\text{all three transpositions}\}.
\]

Again \(H\) is abelian, so an \(H\)-equivariant map \(S_3\to H\) is an arbitrary choice of one value of \(H\) on each of these four domain orbits. Thus there are
\[
3^4=81
\]
equivariant maps.

Only the constant identity map fails to generate \(H\). Therefore this stratum contributes
\[
3^4-1=80.
\]

In the pointed case, the identity orbit is fixed to \(e\), so the number is
\[
3^3-1=26.
\]

### The generated-image subgroup \(S_3\)

An \(S_3\)-equivariant map is determined by its values on the three conjugacy classes.

At the identity, equivariance forces the image into
\[
Z(S_3)=\{e\}.
\]

For a transposition \(t\), its image must lie in
\[
C_{S_3}(t)=\{e,t\}.
\]

For a \(3\)-cycle \(r\), its image must lie in
\[
C_{S_3}(r)=A_3=\{e,r,r^2\}.
\]

Hence there are
\[
1\cdot2\cdot3=6
\]
fully conjugation-equivariant maps.

If the transposition representative maps to \(t\), equivariance sends the three transpositions to themselves under conjugacy, so the image contains all three transpositions and generates \(S_3\). This gives
\[
3
\]
maps, according to the three possible values on the \(3\)-cycle representative.

If the transposition representative maps to \(e\), then the two nontrivial choices on the \(3\)-cycle class generate \(C_3\), and the remaining choice is the trivial map. Thus exactly
\[
3
\]
maps generate \(S_3\).

All three are pointed.

Adding the four generated-image strata gives
\[
1+45+80+3=129
\]
and
\[
1+21+26+3=51
\]
in the pointed case.

### Automorphism-conjugacy classes

Every automorphism of \(S_3\) is inner.

The trivial stratum gives one class.

For a \(C_2\)-generated map, the generated subgroup is one of the three conjugate \(C_2\)'s. The normalizer of a chosen \(C_2\) is that \(C_2\) itself, and the map is already equivariant under that subgroup. Hence no two distinct maps inside the chosen \(C_2\) stratum become identified by its normalizer. Therefore the \(45\) maps form
\[
15
\]
global classes, each of size \(3\). In the pointed case,
\[
21
\]
maps give
\[
7
\]
classes.

For the \(C_3\) stratum, \(A_3\) is normal. Conjugation by \(A_3\) fixes every \(A_3\)-equivariant map, so only the quotient
\[
S_3/A_3\cong C_2
\]
acts effectively. A transposition acts on \(A_3\) by inversion.

Among the \(80\) generating maps, exactly two are fixed by this involution. Indeed, fixedness forces the values on \(e\) and on the transposition orbit to be \(e\), while the values on \(r,r^2\) are determined by choosing a nontrivial value for \(r\) and the inverse value for \(r^2\). Burnside's lemma gives
\[
\frac{80+2}{2}=41
\]
classes.

For pointed maps, the same two fixed generating maps occur, so
\[
\frac{26+2}{2}=14
\]
classes.

Finally, each of the three \(S_3\)-generated maps is fully conjugation-equivariant, hence fixed by every inner automorphism. They give three singleton classes.

Therefore
\[
1+15+41+3=60
\]
classes in total and
\[
1+7+14+3=25
\]
pointed classes.

The orbit-size distributions follow at once:
\[
6+39\cdot2+15\cdot3=129
\]
and
\[
6+12\cdot2+7\cdot3=51.
\]

## Verification

The included program `artifacts/verify.py` represents \(S_3\) explicitly as permutations of three letters.

It exhaustively checks every one of the
\[
6^6=46656
\]
set maps \(S_3\to S_3\) against the defining averaging identity.

The direct exhaustive count is
\[
129,
\]
of which
\[
51
\]
are pointed.

The program independently computes
\[
H=\langle\mathcal A(S_3)\rangle
\]
for every accepted map and obtains the stratum counts
\[
1,\ 45,\ 80,\ 3
\]
and
\[
1,\ 21,\ 26,\ 3
\]
for all and pointed maps, respectively.

It then computes the full action of
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(S_3)=\operatorname{Inn}(S_3)
\]
on the accepted maps and obtains exactly
\[
60
\]
orbits overall and
\[
25
\]
pointed orbits, with all orbit-size distributions matching the proof.

The program also verifies the general subgroup-equivariance criterion for every one of the \(46656\) maps on \(S_3\).

It returns `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite enumeration is exhaustive and is used only as an independent replay of the conceptual counting proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Das introduced the conjugation-based averaging identity for groups in 2024, proved structural characterizations in terms of racks and graphs of maps, and listed broad families of examples such as constants, power maps, and maps into the center. The inspected full text does not give a finite-group census or an \(S_3\) classification.

A later paper of Zhang and Gao introduced a different, stronger group averaging identity
\[
\mathcal A(g)\mathcal A(h)
=
\mathcal A(\mathcal A(g)h)
=
\mathcal A(g\mathcal A(h)).
\]
That paper explicitly distinguishes its identity from Das's pointed conjugation identity and proves that its pointed averaging groups satisfy Das's condition. It does not supply the \(S_3\) census above.

Targeted searches for averaging operators on \(S_3\), averaging operators on symmetric groups, exact finite-group counts, pointed counts, and conjugacy classes did not locate the numbers
\[
129,\qquad51,\qquad60,\qquad25
\]
or the generated-image stratification.

## Limitations

The result concerns Das's conjugation-based definition, not every later notion called an averaging operator on a group.

The structural equivariance criterion is general, but the exact numerical classification is only for \(S_3\).

No classification is claimed for \(S_n\) with \(n\ge4\).

A specialized or unindexed finite-group calculation may exist. Failed searches do not prove novelty.
