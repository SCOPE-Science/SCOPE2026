# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof separates published geometric premises from the new finite and lattice deductions. The source identifies the complete nineteen-curve weighted dual graph and the alternate fibration in which \(a_1\) and \(b_4\) are the zero and two-torsion sections, the pairs \(\{L_3,R_1\}\) and \(\{R_2,b_5\}\) are the two \(I_2\) fibers, and the Nikulin involution is fiberwise translation by the two-torsion section. Those facts force the graph action to swap \(a_1,b_4\) and preserve each indicated fiber pair. Exhaustive weighted-graph enumeration leaves exactly one such automorphism and gives the displayed permutation. Exact intersection arithmetic then verifies the primitive difference relation, rank \(8\), and determinant \(2^{10}\). The standard Nikulin anti-invariant lattice has determinant \(2^8\), so the index is exactly \(2\).

Risk: the doubled graph edges are read as intersection multiplicity two exactly as in the source diagram; changing that graph data would alter the automorphism and determinant computations. The claim is restricted to the source's general-member configuration.

## Originality

PASS. The 2019/2023 generalized-Inose paper gives the graph and the van Geemen--Sarti involution but does not state its complete labeled action on the nineteen curves or compute the lattice generated by the nine paired differences. Kondō's 1989 paper is stronger prior art than a title-level search would suggest: it computes dual-graph symmetries and a symplectic involution in the corresponding finite-automorphism setting. Accordingly, no novelty is claimed for the abstract Klein-four symmetry or for existence of a Nikulin involution. The new claim is restricted to the exact modern labels and, substantively, to the primitive relation and index-two comparison with \(E_8(-2)\). Focused semantic searches for the exact source, the labeled involution, the fixed curve, and the anti-invariant difference lattice returned no implication-equivalent finding.

Residual risk: an older explicit relabeling of Kondō's diagram or a detailed table in the finite-automorphism literature may already encode the same permutation. The index-two difference-lattice statement is less likely to be tabulated, but search failure is not a proof of global novelty.

## Value

PASS. The anti-invariant lattice \(E_8(-2)\) is a canonical integral invariant of every Nikulin involution. For this explicit projective K3 family, the most visible anti-invariant classes are the differences of paired rational curves. Showing that these classes span the correct rational space yet miss the integral anti-invariant lattice by exactly one factor of two is a structural integrality fact, not a relabeling or a routine orbit count. It records precisely how the concrete curve configuration fails to capture the full Nikulin lattice and can be used when comparing quotient, lattice-polarized, or elliptic-fibration descriptions of the surface.

## Closest literature and limitations

Clingher--Hill--Malmendier, arXiv:1908.09578, is the direct source for the graph and elliptic-fibration data. Kondō, Nagoya Math. J. 116 (1989), is the closest older source for abstract dual-graph automorphisms and the symplectic involution. Nikulin supplies the universal anti-invariant lattice \(E_8(-2)\). Roulleau's atlas is a broader modern comparison for finite-automorphism K3 curve configurations. The result is not a classification of special members and does not choose a canonical generator for the index-two saturation.
