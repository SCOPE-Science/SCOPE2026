# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For precisely three plant-extinction indicators, the raw moment identities have common mean and common triple-product probability under the stated exchangeable primary-deletion law, because all pollinators appear in at least one neighborhood. Subtraction rigorously yields \(\Delta m_3=3(1-\mu)\Delta V\). The six-vertex lower bound is a fully specified enumeration of all binary plant–pollinator incidence matrices with no isolated vertices, with complete conditional distributions compared inside every pair of matched degree signatures. Its delivered checker reports exactly 387 connected labeled matrices across 44 signatures and has no counterexample. The two seven-vertex examples are connected bidegree-matched trees; exact pair-union calculations give the advertised variance gap and cubic-driven third-moment crossover. The root is unique by strict monotonicity. No analogous minimum is claimed for graphs beyond the bounded exclusion or for nonmatching degree profiles.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The baseline one-step all-neighbors-lost coextinction model is Memmott–Waser–Price (2004), DOI 10.1098/rspb.2004.2909. Burgos et al. (2006), arXiv:q-bio/0609005, study mutualistic network nestedness and average attack-tolerance curves. Jones–Zurell–Wiesner (2024), DOI 10.1002/ecm.1601, give analytic mean-extinction predictions, noting the extension toward variance as a future problem. Their relevant accessible full texts were inspected. Those results do not explicitly contain, and no implication was found forcing, the three-plant third-central-moment comparison combined with the seven-species bidegree-matched sharpness result. The elementary indicator moment expansion is standard and is not claimed as new in isolation. Prior published bidegree-preserving reliability enumerations or small-network cumulant identities might imply the sharp example; that remains a residual coverage risk. A failed keyword search would not alone justify originality.

## Value — PASS

The third central moment is a natural descriptor of the asymmetry of the distribution of secondary extinctions, beyond expected loss and variance. In two networks whose guild sizes, both degree multisets, and expected plant loss agree, the sharp smallest-network witness proves that variance ordering does not fix third-moment ordering as a function of primary-loss intensity. The universal identity shows that this discrepancy is driven by the common expected extinction count crossing one, rather than a reversal of the variance order. The precise seven-species limit distinguishes this structural effect from an arbitrary unmotivated small-example computation and offers a reproducible benchmark for stochastic ecological network comparisons.

## Scientific and access limitations

The result assumes perfectly functional binary interactions and one-step secondary extinctions; no empirical ecological outcome or standardized-skewness claim is established. Only the stated fixed-bidegree comparison and three-plant moment identity are proved. Published mathematical and ecological literature is extensive, so residual possibility of a stronger, equivalent specialized prior theorem remains despite direct primary-text comparison.
