# Thirty-seven absorption classes at the fourth realizability level
## Finding

For Kihara's absorption preorder on finite quantifier patterns over
\[
\{\exists,\forall,\exists^\infty,\forall^\infty\},
\]
the \(\Sigma_4\) patterns have exactly \(37\) mutual-absorption classes, and the \(\Pi_4\) patterns also have exactly \(37\). After contracting adjacent repeated ordinary quantifiers there are exactly \(61\) normalized patterns on each side, with maximum normalized length \(7\).

Because Kihara proves that absorption implies realizability-theoretic di-many-one reducibility, the open semantic counts satisfy
\[
N_m(\Sigma_4)\le37,\qquad
N_m(\Pi_4)\le37,\qquad
N_{dm}(\Sigma_4)\le37.
\]
By duality \(N_{dm}(\Pi_4)\le37\) as well. The theorem does not claim that any of these inequalities is an equality.

## Assumptions and scope

A pattern is a finite word in \(\exists,\forall,\exists^\infty,\forall^\infty\). Hierarchy levels are those of Kihara's Definition 2.6. Absorption is generated by
\[
\exists^\infty\to\forall\exists,\qquad
\forall^\infty\to\exists\forall,\qquad
\exists\exists\to\exists,\qquad
\forall\forall\to\forall,
\]
plus arbitrary insertion of a quantifier. Two patterns are mutually absorbable when each absorbs into the other.

The claim classifies this syntactic equivalence only. Distinct absorption classes may still become equivalent under the stronger semantic reductions studied by Kihara.

The checker uses `E`, `A`, `I`, `J` for \(\exists,\forall,\exists^\infty,\forall^\infty\), respectively.

## Proof

Adjacent copies of \(\exists\) and of \(\forall\) may be contracted, and the reverse step is supplied by arbitrary insertion. Hence every pattern is mutually absorbable with its run-collapsed normal form.

Apply Definition 2.6 recursively from the right. The exhaustive normalized counts are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
\text{length}&1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\
\hline
\text{level at most }4&4&14&38&56&40&14&2&0\\
\Sigma_4&0&1&10&23&19&7&1&0\\
\Pi_4&0&1&10&23&19&7&1&0.
\end{array}
\]
Prepending a quantifier never decreases the numerical hierarchy level. Therefore the zero in the length-\(8\) column proves that no longer normalized fourth-or-lower-level pattern exists. Summing either exact fourth-level row gives
\[
1+10+23+19+7+1=61.
\]

For a normalized pattern \(P\), let \(\mathcal R(P)\) be the finite set obtained by independently leaving each infinite quantifier unchanged or applying its one expansion
\[
\exists^\infty\mapsto\forall\exists,\qquad
\forall^\infty\mapsto\exists\forall,
\]
then contracting adjacent equal ordinary quantifiers. The absorption relation has the exact normal form
\[
P\to^*Q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\exists R\in\mathcal R(P))\ R\text{ is a subsequence of }Q.
\]

The reverse direction first performs the selected expansions and contractions and then inserts the missing symbols of \(Q\). For the forward direction, trace descendants of the symbols originally in \(P\). Each original infinite quantifier is either retained or expanded exactly once. Expanding a symbol that was itself inserted is only another form of insertion; a contraction involving inserted ordinary material cannot remove the last traced occurrence of that ordinary symbol. After deleting the inserted material from the trace, the surviving descendants contain a member of \(\mathcal R(P)\) in their original order. Thus some \(R\in\mathcal R(P)\) is a subsequence of \(Q\).

The bundled checker computes this finite relation on all \(61\) normalized \(\Sigma_4\) patterns and takes its symmetric part. It obtains exactly \(37\) classes. The same independent computation for \(\Pi_4\) also gives \(37\). As a source-level sanity check, the hierarchy generator produces exactly \(17\) normalized \(\Sigma_3\) patterns, matching Kihara's Example 2.5.

Finally, Kihara's Observation 2.22 converts absorption into di-many-one reducibility. Therefore each mutual-absorption class lies inside one di-many-one class, proving the displayed upper bounds.

## Relationship to prior work

Kihara defines absorption, proves that it implies realizability-theoretic reducibility, and completely classifies the corresponding quantifier-pattern structures through the third arithmetical level. His publicly available 2026 journal-version manuscript still asks for the numbers of many-one classes of \(\Sigma_4\) and \(\Pi_4\) patterns and di-many-one classes of \(\Sigma_4\) patterns.

The August 2026 collection *Open Problems in Mathematical Logic* repeats those fourth-level counting questions and the broader problem of deciding reducibility between two patterns. The present result does not solve those semantic questions; it completely computes the source-defined absorption quotient and therefore supplies a finite upper bound for them.

Targeted searches for fourth-level absorption, mutual absorption, the counts \(61\) and \(37\), and realizability-theoretic fourth-level reductions did not locate this quotient in the checked literature.

## Limitations

The number \(37\) is not claimed as the exact number of many-one or di-many-one classes. Further semantic reductions can merge distinct absorption classes.

No general reducibility decision procedure is proved. The theorem is specific to the fourth hierarchy level.

Search non-detection cannot exclude equivalent unpublished or unindexed observations.
