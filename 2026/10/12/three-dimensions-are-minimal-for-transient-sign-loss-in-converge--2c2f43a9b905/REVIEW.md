# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The residual identity \(I-AX_k=(I-\alpha A)^{2^k}\) gives the exact polynomial \(X_k=q_{2^k}(A)\). For even \(m\), the scalar function \(q_m\) is positive and strictly decreasing on the full convergence interval. In dimension two, functional calculus therefore writes \(q_m(A)=c_0I+c_1A\) with \(c_1\le0\); Stieltjes off-diagonals then become nonnegative, while positive definiteness gives positive diagonals. The three-dimensional witness is checked exactly: both \(A\) and \(2I-A\) are positive definite, \(A^{-1}>0\), \(X_1\ge0\), and \((X_2)_{13}=-1/500\). Risk: no claim is made outside the stated symmetric scalar-initialized setting.

## Originality
PASS. The classical Newton–Schulz method, convergence criterion, scalar initialization, approximate-inverse preconditioning use, and structure-aware inverse iterations are prior. The closest inspected full text (Soleymani--Sharifi--Shateyi 2014) explicitly covers Schulz inversion, \(V_0=\alpha I\), convergence, sparsity, and preconditioning but contains no entrywise-positivity, Stieltjes, or \(M\)-matrix theorem. Hackbusch--Khoromskij--Tyrtyshnikov 2005/2008 discuss loss of computational tensor/hierarchical structure, not sign structure. Published-index and semantic searches did not locate the dimension-two preservation theorem or a convergent three-dimensional Stieltjes counterexample. Residual risk: older monotone matrix-iteration literature may use terminology not captured by these searches.

## Value
PASS. Approximate inverses are used as preconditioners and are often generated by Newton–Schulz because it uses matrix products and converges quadratically. For Stieltjes systems the true inverse is nonnegative, so knowing whether intermediate approximations preserve that sign structure is a natural structural question. The finding gives a complete minimal-dimension boundary and an exact, robust three-dimensional witness rather than a numerical anecdote.

## Closest literature and limitations
The result does not challenge convergence or stability theory. It isolates a separate entrywise property of convergent intermediate inverse approximations. It does not extend to nonsymmetric \(M\)-matrices, non-scalar starts, dropping strategies, or other matrix functions.
