# Three node-type families in theta-graph edge toric varieties
## Finding
Consider three internally vertex-disjoint paths of even lengths \(2r_1,2r_2,2r_3\), where \(r_i\ge1\), with common distinct endpoints. Their union is the simple bipartite theta graph \(\Theta_{\mathbf r}\). Let \(X_{\mathbf r}\subset\mathbb P^{2R-1}_{\mathbb C}\), with \(R=r_1+r_2+r_3\), be its projective edge toric variety, defined by the degree-two monomials in graph-vertex coordinates indexed by the \(2R\) edges. Its dimension is \(2R-3\).

Set \(c_i=\binom{r_i}{2}\). If all \(r_i=1\), then \(X_{\mathbf r}\cong\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\) under its Segre embedding and is smooth. Otherwise, its singular locus has codimension exactly three. The total number of irreducible codimension-three singular components is
\[
N(\mathbf r)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_i^2+2\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}c_ic_jr_k+2r_1r_2r_3\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_i,
\]
where \(k\) is the index outside \(\{i,j\}\). These are respectively three explicit geometric types I, II, and III. At the general point of every component, a transverse three-dimensional analytic slice is the ordinary node \(xy-zw=0\). Components in higher codimension are not determined here.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\), and each path has length at least two (so the graph is simple). Number the edge coordinates along path \(i\) consecutively, and let \(A_i\) be the product of the \(r_i\) odd-positioned coordinates and \(B_i\) the product of its \(r_i\) even-positioned coordinates. All six monomial blocks have pairwise disjoint sets of variables. The homogeneous edge toric ideal is
\[
I_{\mathbf r}=(A_1B_2-B_1A_2,\ A_1B_3-B_1A_3,\ A_2B_3-B_2A_3).
\]
The equalities express the three simple even-cycle binomials. Generation by the simple even-cycle binomials is the standard edge-ring theorem for connected bipartite graphs; here every simple cycle is a union of two of the three paths. In particular the displayed ideal is prime of height two. Equivalently, \(X_{\mathbf r}\) is the rank-at-most-one locus of the monomial-entry matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}A_1&A_2&A_3\\B_1&B_2&B_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The three component types are specified precisely as follows. For each occurrence, unspecified coordinates are generically nonzero.

**Type I:** choose a path \(i\), two distinct coordinates in \(A_i\) and two distinct coordinates in \(B_i\); set these four coordinates to zero. On the resulting coordinate subspace impose the irreducible equation \(A_jB_k-B_jA_k=0\) in the other two paths, where \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\). There are \(\sum_i c_i^2\) components.

**Type II:** choose one row (either all \(A\)-blocks or all \(B\)-blocks), and two distinct paths \(i,j\); set two distinct coordinates to zero in each chosen row-block for \(i,j\), and one coordinate to zero in that row-block for the remaining path \(k\). The resulting five-coordinate linear subspace lies in \(X_{\mathbf r}\). There are \(2\sum_{i<j}c_ic_jr_k\) components.

**Type III:** choose a row (either \(A\) or \(B\)) and a distinguished path \(i\). In that row set two distinct coordinates to zero in path \(i\), and one coordinate to zero in each of the other two paths. In the opposite row set one coordinate to zero in path \(i\). This five-coordinate linear subspace also lies in \(X_{\mathbf r}\). There are \(2r_1r_2r_3\sum_i c_i\) components.

These types have different generic supports of the matrix, or different distributions of zeros within the blocks; all specified irreducible subvarieties are distinct.

## Proof
**1. Equation and dimension.** An even closed walk in a connected bipartite graph gives an edge-toric binomial, and the edge toric ideal is generated by the binomials of simple even cycles. Here there are exactly three simple cycles, obtained by selecting two of the three paths; the corresponding binomials are precisely the three minors above. The connected bipartite graph has \(2R-1\) vertices and \(2R\) edges, so the edge-incidence rank is \(2R-2\), the toric ideal has height two, and the projective variety dimension is \(2R-3\).

**2. Monomial support stratification.** Fix a coordinate-zero pattern with \(z\) zero edge coordinates, and restrict to the dense open locus of the corresponding coordinate stratum where every other coordinate is nonzero. Each monomial \(A_i\) or \(B_i\) survives exactly when its block contains no zero coordinate. Because the matrix has rank at most one, its set of nonzero entries is either empty or a rectangular set \(U\times V\), with \(U\subseteq\{A,B\}\) and \(V\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\) nonempty. When this support is nonempty, the nonzero minor equations on the torus impose \((|U|-1)(|V|-1)\) independent equations. Independence holds because all six monomial blocks use disjoint variables and each nonzero monomial is a primitive product: one coordinate in each active block can be varied independently. Thus the codimension inside \(X_{\mathbf r}\) of the stratum is
\[
z+(|U|-1)(|V|-1)-2.
\]
If all six blocks vanish, then \(z\ge6\), and the entire stratum has codimension at least four; it cannot supply a component of codimension at most three. Therefore it remains only to analyze nonempty supports and \(z\le5\).

**3. Jacobian at rank-one matrices.** The ambient \(2\times3\) rank-one determinantal variety is smooth away from its zero matrix, with normal space of dimension two. For a matrix \(u v^{\mathsf T}\), the linearized equations are the normal components of the variations of the six entries. A nonzero monomial block is a submersive coordinate near the stratum. A zero monomial block has nonzero linear differential exactly when **one** of its coordinate factors is zero; its differential vanishes when two or more factors are zero. The minors have Jacobian rank two, and the edge variety is smooth, precisely when the surviving differentials span both normal directions.

For completeness, the possible support shapes and their low-codimension outcomes are:

- \(|U|=2,|V|=3\): no coordinate zeros; the two independent toric equations give smoothness.
- \(|U|=2,|V|=2\): there is one missing column and at least two zero coordinates. Smoothness fails in codimension at most three exactly when both missing monomial blocks each have at least two zero factors. Then \(z=4\), giving type I.
- \(|U|=2,|V|=1\): at least four zero coordinates; for \(z\le5\) the missing-column differentials span the two independent normal directions, so all these strata are smooth.
- \(|U|=1,|V|=3\): all blocks in the missing row vanish. For \(z\le5\), failure of rank two occurs precisely when two of the three missing-row blocks each contain two zero coordinates, and the third contains one. This is type II, with \(z=5\).
- \(|U|=1,|V|=2\): one full matrix column is zero and all three blocks in the missing row vanish. With \(z\le5\), rank failure occurs precisely when the missing-row block in the missing column has two zero factors, that column's other block has one, and the missing-row blocks in the two active columns each have one. This is type III, with \(z=5\).
- \(|U|=1,|V|=1\): there are at least five zero coordinates, and when \(z=5\) the three missing-row blocks provide two independent normal directions, so the stratum is smooth.

All additional support shapes have more than five zero variables. The classification proves that there are no singular strata of codimension less than three, and that **every** singular stratum of codimension three belongs to exactly one of I, II, III.

**4. Irreducibility, count, and maximality.** Type II and type III are coordinate linear spaces (all three minors vanish identically), hence irreducible. Type I is the product of a coordinate linear space with the nontrivial two-path cycle hypersurface: the binomial in the other two paths is irreducible because the disjoint squarefree monomials have primitive exponent difference. Each type has dimension \(2R-6\): type I imposes four linear equations plus one binomial in projective space, and types II and III impose five coordinate equations. No larger-dimensional singular component exists, and the listed components are distinct, so these are exactly the irreducible components of codimension three. The factors count as described under the assumptions, yielding the stated formula. If all \(r_i=1\), the defining minors have six independent linear entries and give the smooth Segre variety. Otherwise \(\sum c_i>0\), and type III is nonempty, proving singular codimension exactly three.

**5. Generic transverse ordinary nodes.** At a generic type-I point the two remaining columns are nonzero, and their rank-one equation is smooth; solving that equation and using nonzero block coordinates as units reduces the other minors to one hypersurface equation equating products of two zero coordinates each. At a generic type-II point the nonzero-row products are units. The one-zero-factor block in the opposite row is eliminated using one minor, and the remaining equation equates two products of two zero factors. At a generic type-III point the nonzero blocks of the two active columns are units; their minor eliminates one single-zero variable, and the remaining equation equates the product of two single-zero variables to the product of the two zeros in the doubled block. In each case independent analytic coordinate changes give a smooth factor times the hypersurface \(xy-zw=0\) in four transverse coordinates. This is a three-dimensional ordinary double point.

## Verification
A standalone exact Python checker (`theta_singularity_checker.py`) reconstructs the three minors and their integer Jacobian matrix at each nonempty projective coordinate-support stratum with at most five coordinate zeros. For each pattern it checks the monomial-support compatibility equations, calculates the codimension, and computes whether the Jacobian rank is two using exact integer determinants. It checks all sorted triples \((r_1,r_2,r_3)\) with \(1\le r_i\le4\) and \(r_1+r_2+r_3\le7\), verifying 6,793 compatible support strata. In every nonsmooth case of codimension three the checker finds exactly the predicted count; the ten counts (lexicographic triple order) are \(0,5,27,84,20,64,161,144,63,161\). It finds no singular stratum below codimension three. Execute `python3 theta_singularity_checker.py` with the standard Python library. Finite enumeration is regression evidence, not the proof of the infinite theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Gitler–Reyes–Villarreal (arXiv:math/0603718, first publicly posted 2006-03-30) establish structural results on bipartite graph edge ideals, and explicitly recall the generating cycle-binomial theorem in their Section 3. Ohsugi–Hibi (arXiv:1108.2850, first publicly posted 2011-08-14) study when families of adjacent two-minors are graph toric ideals. The standard rank-one determinantal geometry is also prior knowledge; the smooth \(K_{2,3}\) case is classical. None of those general descriptions supplies the three infinite parameter-dependent *projective singular component families*, their exact enumerative formula, or the generic transverse-node assertion. Brown–Lakshmibai (arXiv:0707.2392) find codimension-three singular loci for different Bruhat–Hibi toric varieties; their varieties and indexing combinatorics are distinct. The present result does not treat those known results as new.

## Limitations
The classification determines only the **codimension-three** irreducible components of the reduced singular locus, not components occurring solely in codimension four or higher, nor the scheme-theoretic singular locus at intersections. The analytic node statement holds at general points of the listed components, not necessarily at their intersections. The argument is over \(\mathbb C\), with no assertion of an analogous classification in positive characteristic. Published mathematical coverage by literature not identified in the targeted searches remains possible. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
