# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Sarkar's dimension-at-least-three proof supplies the needed descent after contraction of all \((-1)\)-hypersurfaces. Since a complex abelian variety has no rational curves, it has no such hypersurfaces; thus automorphisms of the blow-up descend to automorphisms of \(A\) preserving the center. The remaining proof is exact: every automorphism is affine, the group-automorphism part is countable, and for each \((g,\sigma)\) the triple-stabilizer locus is a proper closed kernel unless both entries are identities. The explicit involutions prove sharpness for one and two points.

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary literature is Sarkar's arXiv:2608.07983v1. It proves eventual triviality and gives the general threshold \(d+1\) for an abelian \(d\)-fold through Theorem 3.1, but it does not state the sharp constant \(3\) for all \(d\ge3\). Searches of the exact blow-up statement, equivalent triple-stabilizer formulation, inversion lower bound, and source-specific threshold returned no implication-equivalent published result. A residual risk remains that an unindexed result about finite-set stabilizers of abelian varieties may contain the same theorem under different language.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural complete cutoff for the initiating paper's automorphism-killing problem on a canonical class of varieties. It improves a dimension-dependent sufficient bound to a dimension-independent exact answer and identifies the geometric obstruction at smaller center sizes.

## Closest literature and limitations
The source's Theorem A says “sufficiently many,” while Theorem 3.1 yields \(d+1\) for an abelian \(d\)-fold. Remark 5.1 separately shows that “general” cannot simply replace “very general” in an abelian example. The present result is over \(\mathbf C\), for dimension at least \(3\), and does not classify special triples or positive-characteristic behavior.
