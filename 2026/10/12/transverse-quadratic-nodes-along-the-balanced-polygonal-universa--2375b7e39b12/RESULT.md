# Transverse quadratic nodes along the balanced polygonal universal-adjoint torus line

## Finding
For each convex real \(n\)-gon with \(n\geq5\), assign positive cyclic determinant weights \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\) as below. The scheme-theoretic singular locus of its universal-adjoint hypersurface on the dense projective coordinate torus is **empty** except when
\[
4\mid n,\qquad \prod_{i\ \mathrm{odd}}c_i=\prod_{i\ \mathrm{even}}c_i.
\]
In that exceptional case the singular subscheme on the torus is exactly a **smooth reduced punctured projective line**, isomorphic to \(\mathbf G_m\). Every point along it has the same analytic transverse ordinary quadratic double-point singularity of rank \(n-2\): the germ is a product of a smooth curve with a nondegenerate quadric hypersurface germ.

This assertion includes a converse and a scheme-theoretic, local-analytic classification of the exceptional case of the polygonal criterion in [1, Section 7]; it does not classify singularities supported on coordinate hyperplanes.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbf C\) for the hypersurface, and let \(P\) be any convex real polygon with \(n\geq5\) edges. Choose nonzero outward ray generators \(v_i\in\mathbf R^2\), cyclically ordered around its normal fan, and require consecutive pairs to be linearly independent. Put \(v_{n+1}=v_1\) and \(c_i=|\det(v_i,v_{i+1})|>0\). The homogeneous universal adjoint [1, Eq. (2)] is
\[
F_c(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_i\prod_{j\notin\{i,i+1\}}x_j,
\qquad [x_1:\cdots:x_n]\in\mathbf P^{n-1}.
\]
Indices are cyclic. Set \(T=\{[x]\in\mathbf P^{n-1}:x_1\cdots x_n\ne0\}\), and give the singular locus its Jacobian-ideal scheme structure.

## Proof
**1. Inversion and the singular scheme.** On \(T\), set \(y_i=x_i^{-1}\); homogeneous coordinatewise inversion is an algebraic automorphism of \(T\). Then
\[
F_c(x)=\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^n x_j\Bigr)Q_c(y),\qquad Q_c(y)=\sum_{i=1}^nc_i y_i y_{i+1}.
\]
The monomial factor is invertible on \(T\). A unit multiplication and a nonsingular coordinate change preserve the singular **subscheme** of the zero hypersurface: the ideals generated by each polynomial together with its differential are identified. For a homogeneous degree-\(n-2\) polynomial in characteristic zero, Euler's relation puts the polynomial into its partial-derivative ideal, and the same holds for the homogeneous quadratic \(Q_c\). Thus the scheme of interest is the projective torus intersection of the linear Jacobian scheme \(H_c y=0\), where \(H_c\) is the symmetric cyclic matrix with \((H_c)_{i,i+1}=(H_c)_{i+1,i}=c_i\) and every other entry zero.

**2. Complete rank classification.** If \(n\) is odd, the only nonzero determinant terms of the cycle matrix come from the two oriented Hamiltonian cycles (an odd number of vertices cannot be covered by two-cycles). Consequently
\[
\det H_c=2\prod_{i=1}^n c_i\ne0.
\]
If \(n=2m\) is even, reorder the coordinates so that odd indices precede even indices. Then
\[
H_c=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad B_{r,r}=c_{2r-1},\quad B_{r,r-1}=c_{2r-2}
\]
with the second index cyclic modulo \(m\). The only two permutations contributing to the determinant of \(B\) are the identity and cyclic shift, so
\[
\det B=\prod_{r=1}^m c_{2r-1}+(-1)^{m-1}\prod_{r=1}^m c_{2r}.
\]
Positivity of all \(c_i\) therefore gives singularity of \(B\) exactly when \(m\) is even and the two alternating products coincide, equivalent to the displayed exceptional condition. In that case the leading \((m-1)\)-square submatrix of \(B\) is triangular with nonzero diagonal, hence \(\operatorname{rank}B=m-1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}H_c=n-2\). Otherwise \(H_c\) is invertible.

**3. Explicit line and reducedness.** Under the exceptional condition \(\ker H_c\) is two-dimensional. The two summands come from one-dimensional kernels of \(B\) and \(B^{\mathsf T}\); let \(u\) be supported on odd indices and \(v\) on even indices. All of their supported entries are nonzero because the kernel recurrences use nonzero edge weights. Hence the all-nonzero points of \(\mathbf P(\ker H_c)\) are exactly \([a u+b v]\) for \(ab\ne0\), a copy of \(\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,\infty\}\cong\mathbf G_m\). Under coordinate inversion, multiplication of the inverse coordinates by \(ab\) gives
\[
x_i=b/u_i\ \text{for odd }i,\qquad x_i=a/v_i\ \text{for even }i.
\]
Their image is the projective line spanned by the two fixed vectors \(U\) and \(V\), where \(U_i=1/u_i\) for odd \(i\) and \(U_i=0\) for even \(i\), with \(V\) defined by the complementary rule. Since the Jacobian equations of \(Q_c\) are independent linear equations cutting out exactly \(\mathbf P(\ker H_c)\), this is a **reduced** smooth singular scheme, not merely a set of singular points. The inverse torus automorphism preserves its scheme structure.

**4. Analytic transverse type.** Over \(\mathbf C\), a quadratic form of rank \(n-2\) in \(n\) variables splits linearly as a nondegenerate sum of \(n-2\) squares and two null directions. Locally in projective space around any point on \(\mathbf P(\ker H_c)\), one null coordinate supplies a nonzero affine chart, and the other null coordinate is a smooth parameter along the singular line. The local equation is exactly an ordinary nondegenerate quadratic form in the \(n-2\) transverse affine variables, independent of that parameter. Inversion and multiplication by the nowhere-vanishing monomial do not alter the hypersurface's analytic isomorphism type. Hence the transverse quadratic rank is precisely \(n-2\), everywhere along the punctured line.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_cycle.py` is standalone Python/SymPy code performing exact integer and symbolic checks: ranks for constant-weight cycles of lengths \(4,5,6,8,10,12\); the alternating-product transition for two weighted eight-cycles; and direct vanishing of the degree-six octagon universal-adjoint polynomial and its gradient at a torus point, together with Hessian rank \(6\). Run `python verify_cycle.py` after installing SymPy. The recorded output is `verification_output.txt`. These examples **illustrate** the theorem, while the preceding determinant and scheme arguments prove the assertion for every integer \(n\geq5\) and all admissible positive weights.

## Relationship to prior work
Telen [1, Theorem 7.11] establishes the no-off-boundary-singularity condition when \(n\) is not divisible by four or the alternating products are unequal; [1, Example 7.12] supplies a special balanced octagon with an additional singular line. Neither statement gives the **uniform converse on the torus**, its **scheme-theoretic reducedness**, nor the **transverse analytic singularity type** for all balanced cyclic positive weights. The present local classification follows from inverting torus coordinates to a weighted quadratic cycle and computing its entire Jacobian kernel. The quadrilateral case is excluded because [1, Section 2.1] already analyzes its reducible quadric.

## Limitations
The theorem is confined to the coordinate torus and does not determine the singular components or scheme structure on the coordinate boundary. The analytic normal form is asserted over \(\mathbf C\); positivity of determinant weights is essential in the parity classification. The code is a finite exact consistency check and does not replace the general proof. The classification does not imply that the full projective singular locus consists only of this line.
