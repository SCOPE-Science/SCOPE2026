# Twenty symmetry classes and an exact census of optimal five-ary constant-composition codes

## Finding
Consider codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1,2,3,4\}^{13}\) in which every word has exactly one occurrence of each nonzero symbol \(1,2,3,4\) and nine zero positions, and every two distinct words have Hamming distance at least \(7\). The established optimal cardinality is \(13\) [1]. The structural equality cases correspond to proper four-edge-colorings of the unique projective plane of order three. The new **exact counting and symmetry classification** is:

**For each fixed projective plane on thirteen labeled positions there are exactly \(1,935,360\) labeled four-colorings. Hence there are exactly \(2,145,927,168,000\) optimal codes on thirteen labeled positions with the four nonzero symbols distinguished. Under all coordinate permutations and a common permutation of the four nonzero symbols, there are exactly \(20\) equivalence classes.**

For a fixed projective plane, quotienting the colorings by the global four-symbol permutation produces \(80,640\) normalized colorings. The projective collineation group, of order \(5,616\), partitions these into \(12\) orbits of size \(5,616\), seven of size \(1,872\), and one of size \(144\). The corresponding collineation stabilizer orders of normalized colorings are respectively \(1\), \(3\), and \(39\). A representative of every class is included in `orbit_representatives.txt`.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with zero distinguished. The four nonzero symbols each appear exactly once in each codeword; the code is an unordered set of distinct thirteen-coordinate words, and Hamming distance counts positions with unequal entries. The coordinates are labeled for the exact total count. For equivalence classes we allow coordinate permutations and **one common global permutation** of nonzero symbols, never an independently chosen permutation at each coordinate. The target cardinality is the known sharp value \(13\) [1], not a newly claimed optimal-size theorem. No classification is claimed outside this exact length, composition and distance.

A proper four-edge-coloring of the incidence graph assigns colors \(1,2,3,4\) to its fifty-two line–point incidences, with all four colors distinct around each line and each point. Each colored line gives a codeword: the symbol on each of its four incident points, zero elsewhere.

## Proof
**1. Equality forces a projective plane.** Two weight-four codewords sharing two or more nonzero support positions have Hamming distance at most \(6\), even if they disagree at every shared position. Thus their supports meet at most once. If two distinct codewords share a position bearing the same nonzero symbol, their distance is at most \(6\); therefore each coordinate occurs with any particular nonzero symbol in at most one codeword. In a thirteen-word code there are exactly \(13\cdot4=52\) nonzero incidences, while thirteen coordinates each have capacity four. Consequently every coordinate occurs in exactly four words, and all four symbols occur once there. The number of intersections of pairs of supports, counted by columns, is \(13\binom{4}{2}=78=\binom{13}{2}\). Because each pair meets at most once, every pair of codeword supports meets exactly once.

Let \(B\) be the \(13\times13\) zero–one line–point incidence matrix, with codewords as rows and coordinates as columns. Every row and column sum is four, and every two different rows intersect once, so \(BB^{\mathsf T}=3I+J\). This matrix is invertible. Since \(B\mathbf1=B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=4\mathbf1\), multiplying \(BB^{\mathsf T}=3I+J\) through the invertible \(B\) yields \(B^{\mathsf T}B=3I+J\). Hence every pair of coordinates occurs together in exactly one support. This is a symmetric \(2\)-\((13,4,1)\) design, equivalently a projective plane of order \(3\). The order-three projective plane is unique up to isomorphism [2]. The prohibition on repeating a symbol at a point makes its incidence edges properly four-colored. Conversely any proper four-coloring of this plane produces thirteen admissible codewords: two lines meet in one point and have distinct symbols there, so their Hamming distance is \(4+4-1=7\).

**2. Finite exact census for a fixed plane.** A concrete model takes projective points and lines as the thirteen normalized nonzero triples over \(\mathbb F_3\); point \(p\) belongs to line \(\ell\) exactly when \(\ell\cdot p=0\) in \(\mathbb F_3\). Fix one line and prescribe its four incident colors in increasing point order. Every four-coloring has exactly one global recoloring meeting this normalization. `planes.cpp` enumerates all remaining assignments with a complete backtracking over the twenty-four permutations of four colors on each remaining line, rejecting precisely those repeating a color at a point. It gives exactly \(80,640\) normalized colorings, hence \(24\cdot80,640=1,935,360\) labeled four-colorings per fixed plane.

A structurally independent check uses the cyclic incidence model with point set \(\mathbb Z_{13}\) and lines \(i+\{0,1,3,9\}\). A four-coloring is an ordered decomposition into four perfect matchings of the 4-regular bipartite graph. The stand-alone `matchings.py` enumerates exactly \(3,852\) perfect matchings. Among ordered disjoint first-two matching choices, the number with respectively \(1,2,3\) residual even-cycle components is \(473,472\), \(89,856\), and \(78,624\). Alternating the last two colors around these cycles yields, independently,
\[
473472\cdot2+89856\cdot4+78624\cdot8=1,935,360.
\]

**3. Labeled incidence planes and orbit classification.** For the unique projective plane, its full collineation group is \(\operatorname{PGL}(3,3)\), of order \(5,616\), by the fundamental theorem of projective geometry, since \(\mathbb F_3\) has no nontrivial field automorphisms. There are \(13!/5,616=1,108,800\) distinct placements of that incidence plane on thirteen labeled coordinates. Since different supports recover different code sets, multiplying by the four-coloring count gives
\[
(13!/5616)\,1935360=2,145,927,168,000.
\]

The four nonzero symbols act freely by global recoloring, allowing the distinguished-line normalization. The collineation group acts on its \(80,640\) normalized colorings followed by unique recoloring. `gens.py` and `make_mapping.py` construct the induced action from four elementary transvections. Direct group closure gives all \(5,616\) collineations. `orbits.cpp` builds the exact permutation action on all normalized colorings and exhaustively joins generated orbits: \(12\times5616+7\times1872+1\times144=80640\), so there are \(20\) classes. Independently, `groupconj.py` enumerates the group and its twelve conjugacy classes, and `burnside.cpp` tests their fixed colorings directly. The weighted fixed-point sum is \(112,320\); Burnside's lemma gives \(112320/5616=20\). Thus the orbit decomposition and the total count are exhaustive, not samples.

## Verification
Run `sh run_checks.sh` in a directory containing the included source files, Python 3 and a C++17 compiler. It generates the incidence model, the exact normalized-coloring binary table, all group actions and class representatives. The key recorded results are: `normalized_coloring_count=80640`, `group_order=5616`, `orbit_count=20`, `BURNSIDE_SUM=112320`, and `VERIFY_MATCHINGS_OK` with `total_labeled_edge_colorings 1935360`. `verify_codes.py` independently builds and verifies all twenty representative codebooks by directly checking the exact composition and every pairwise Hamming distance. Its final marker is `VERIFY_CODES_OK representatives=20 words_per_code=13 min_distance_at_least=7`.

Each enumerator has an explicit finite universe and explores it completely: twelve unfixed lines with twenty-four local color choices, all distinct first-two perfect matching pairs, or the finite collineation group. This is a finite exact exhaustive computation supplemented by the structural reduction above; it is not an inference from a heuristic or a partial search. The generated binary table is reproducible and does not need to be supplied as an input.

## Relationship to prior work
Chee, Dau, Ling and Ling [1] give constructions and exact cardinality bounds for these constant-composition parameters: their Table I obtains size \(13\) for length \(13\), weight four and distance seven, and Table III records the stabilization length threshold \(N_{\mathrm{ccc}}=13\), not the code size. This result does **not** re-claim that upper bound or a thirteen-word construction. The structural reduction to properly colored symmetric incidence designs is a known/elementary coding-theoretic framework, and is not claimed as new. The new assertions are the complete count of all labeled equality cases and the twenty-class classification under the specified natural equivalence. The uniqueness of the order-three projective plane [2] is a known input, not new. General edge-coloring and factorization results alone guarantee existence rather than these numerical and orbit counts.

## Limitations
The enumeration and orbit sizes are supported by reproducible exhaustive finite code, not by closed-form hand derivations of each integer. The finite projective-plane uniqueness and the fundamental theorem of projective geometry are invoked as standard external theorems. An obscure or unindexed historical census of four-edge-colorings of this specific incidence graph, perhaps framed as a one-factorization classification, could overlap the new enumeration despite the targeted literature and database comparisons. No claim is made for other compositions, alphabet sizes or code lengths. Independent external audit has not been performed.
