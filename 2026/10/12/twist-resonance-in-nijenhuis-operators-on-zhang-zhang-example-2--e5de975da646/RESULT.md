# Twist resonance in Nijenhuis operators on Zhang–Zhang Example 2.6

## Finding

Let \(K\) be a field with \(\operatorname{char}K\ne2\), let \(\mu\in K^\times\), and let \(A_\mu\) be the three-dimensional Hom-Lie antialgebra from Zhang–Zhang Example 2.6. Its even part is \(K\varepsilon\), its odd part is \(Ka\oplus Kb\), and
\[
\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon=\varepsilon,\qquad
\varepsilon\cdot a=\frac{\mu}{2}a,\qquad
\varepsilon\cdot b=\frac{\mu^{-1}}{2}b,\qquad
[a,b]=\frac12\varepsilon.
\]
The twists are
\[
\alpha(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon,\qquad
\beta(a)=\mu a,\qquad
\beta(b)=\mu^{-1}b.
\]

Write
\[
\Phi_0(\varepsilon)=\lambda\varepsilon,\qquad
\Phi_1=P=\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\q&s\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\Phi\text{ is Nijenhuis}\iff P\beta=\beta P\ \text{and}\ \det(P-\lambda I)=0.}
\]

If \(\mu^2\ne1\), twist commutation forces \(q=r=0\), so
\[
P=\operatorname{diag}(p,s),\qquad
\boxed{(p-\lambda)(s-\lambda)=0.}
\]
Hence the Nijenhuis variety is two affine planes meeting in the scalar line.

If \(\mu=\pm1\), then \(\beta=\pm I\) and the commuting condition disappears:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Nij}(A_\mu)\cong
\mathbf A^1\times\{Q\in M_2(K):\det Q=0\},\quad Q=P-\lambda I.}
\]
Thus the variety jumps from dimension \(2\) to dimension \(4\).

The trivial deformation generated by \(\Phi\) is
\[
\omega_0(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)=\lambda\varepsilon,
\]
\[
\omega_1(\varepsilon,a)=\frac{\lambda\mu}{2}a,\qquad
\omega_1(\varepsilon,b)=\frac{\lambda\mu^{-1}}{2}b,
\]
and, with \(\rho=\operatorname{tr}P-\lambda\),
\[
\omega_2(a,b)=\frac{\rho}{2}\varepsilon.
\]
So the extra resonant matrix directions are partly invisible to the induced deformation: it remembers only \(\lambda\) and \(\rho\).

Over odd \(\mathbf F_q\), the nonresonant raw count is
\[
\boxed{2q^2-q}
\]
with rank profile
\[
\boxed{\left(1,2(q-1),2(q-1),(q-1)(2q-3)\right)}
\]
on the total three-dimensional graded space.

At \(\mu=\pm1\),
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Nij}(A_\mu)|=q^4+q^3-q^2}
\]
with rank profile
\[
\boxed{\left(1,(q-1)(q+1)^2,q(q^2-1),q(q-1)(q^2-2)\right)}.
\]

The automorphism group fixes \(\varepsilon\) and acts on the odd plane by
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A_\mu)=\{G\in\operatorname{SL}_2(K):G\beta=\beta G\}.
\]
Thus over odd \(\mathbf F_q\) it has order \(q-1\) off resonance and \(q(q^2-1)\) at resonance.

Off resonance, every Nijenhuis odd block is diagonal, so the automorphism torus fixes every Nijenhuis operator. Hence there are
\[
\boxed{2q^2-q}
\]
conjugacy classes.

At resonance, for fixed \(\lambda\), the singular matrix \(Q=P-\lambda I\) has exactly \(q+2\) \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_q)\)-orbits, represented by
\[
0,
\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(\tau,0)\quad(\tau\in\mathbf F_q^\times),
\qquad
E_{12},
\qquad
\nu E_{12},
\]
where \(\nu\) is any nonsquare. Therefore
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Nij}(A_{\pm1})/\operatorname{Aut}(A_{\pm1})|=q(q+2).}
\]

Over \(\mathbf F_{q^m}\), the raw counts are \(2q^{2m}-q^m\) off resonance and \(q^{4m}+q^{3m}-q^{2m}\) at resonance. Hence
\[
\boxed{Z_{\mathrm{nonres}}(T)=\frac{1-qT}{(1-q^2T)^2}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{Z_{\mathrm{res}}(T)=\frac{1-q^2T}{(1-q^3T)(1-q^4T)}}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Zhang and Zhang introduce Hom-Lie antialgebras and their Nijenhuis operators. Example 2.6 gives the exact one-parameter three-dimensional family used here. Definition 5.1 gives the Nijenhuis conditions, and Theorem 3 gives the associated trivial deformation.

The primary preprint was first public on 10 January 2019 and lists \(17D99\) first among its Mathematics Subject Classification codes.

The source assumes an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\). The argument here uses only \(2\ne0\) and \(\mu\ne0\). Finite-field conjugacy formulas are stated for odd fields.

## Proof

The source requires
\[
\Phi_0\alpha=\alpha\Phi_0,\qquad
P\beta=\beta P.
\]
The even equation is automatic. Since
\[
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0\\0&\mu^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
\]
one has
\[
P\beta-\beta P=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&r(\mu^{-1}-\mu)\\
q(\mu-\mu^{-1})&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(\mu^2\ne1\) forces \(q=r=0\), while \(\mu=\pm1\) imposes no odd-block restriction.

The even-even Nijenhuis equation is automatic because \(\varepsilon^2=\varepsilon\). On the odd part, left multiplication by \(\varepsilon\) equals \(\beta/2\). Therefore the mixed Nijenhuis equation cancels once \(P\beta=\beta P\).

Write
\[
[y,z]=\frac12\Omega(y,z)\varepsilon,\qquad
\Omega((y_1,y_2),(z_1,z_2))=y_1z_2-y_2z_1.
\]
For every \(2\times2\) matrix \(P\),
\[
\Omega(Py,z)+\Omega(y,Pz)=\operatorname{tr}(P)\Omega(y,z),
\]
and
\[
\Omega(Py,Pz)=\det(P)\Omega(y,z).
\]
Hence the odd-odd Nijenhuis equation is
\[
\lambda\operatorname{tr}(P)-\lambda^2=\det(P),
\]
equivalently
\[
\det(P-\lambda I)=0.
\]

The deformation formulas follow directly from the source's three displayed formulas for \(\omega_0,\omega_1,\omega_2\), using \(P\beta=\beta P\) and the trace identity above.

For nonresonant finite-field counting, each \(\lambda\) admits \(2q-1\) diagonal pairs \((p,s)\), giving \(2q^2-q\). The rank profile follows by rankwise inclusion-exclusion of the two diagonal affine planes.

At resonance, translation by \(\lambda I\) identifies each fiber with the singular \(2\times2\) matrices. Their number is
\[
q^4-|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_q)|=q^3+q^2-q,
\]
giving \(q^4+q^3-q^2\) total. The displayed rank profile follows by separating \(\lambda=0\) from \(\lambda\ne0\); for each nonzero \(\lambda\), exactly \(q(q+1)\) odd blocks have rank \(1\).

An automorphism fixes \(\varepsilon\), commutes with \(\beta\), and preserves \([a,b]=\varepsilon/2\), hence its odd block lies in \(\operatorname{SL}_2(K)\cap C(\beta)\).

At resonance, singular \(Q\) with nonzero trace \(\tau\) is rank-one semisimple and conjugate to \(\operatorname{diag}(\tau,0)\). Nonzero trace-zero singular \(Q\) is nilpotent; over odd \(\mathbf F_q\), the nonzero nilpotents split into two \(\operatorname{SL}_2\)-orbits distinguished by square class, represented by \(E_{12}\) and \(\nu E_{12}\). This gives \(q+2\) classes per \(\lambda\).

## Relationship to prior work

The primary paper gives Example 2.6 and the abstract Nijenhuis definition but no worked Nijenhuis classification. Its Nijenhuis section contains no low-dimensional example.

The twist parameter \(\mu\) is present in the source example, but the resonance \(\mu^2=1\), the determinantal description, finite-field counts, and automorphism quotient are not analyzed there.

Targeted semantic and web searches for the exact example, ordinary Lie-antialgebra naming, Nijenhuis classification, twist resonance, determinant equations, and finite-field orbit counts found no covering result.

Older Lie-antialgebra literature concerns cohomology, representations, and enveloping algebras rather than this Nijenhuis phase diagram.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the parity-preserving Nijenhuis notion of Zhang and Zhang and one three-dimensional Hom-Lie antialgebra family.

Characteristic \(2\) is excluded. Finite-field orbit formulas are for odd \(q\).

Failed searches do not prove absolute historical novelty.
