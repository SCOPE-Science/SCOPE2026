# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The central finite equivalence is the relational recombination clause precisely when every conditioning fiber is a Cartesian rectangle, including empty fibers. Counting the unique pair of nonempty projections gives the stated reliability polynomial. In the sparse window, a fiber's dominant violations are two edges in distinct rows and columns; the probability of three or more edges is negligible at the necessary scale. The dense regime is controlled by the full-team event, because a non-full rectangle has an empty row or column. The intermediate-density bound separates sparse fibers, opposite-block witnesses, and dense missing-cell obstructions. Independent exact finite tests confirm the formula and semantics on their stated ranges.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The contribution assessed is the **two-sided Poisson phase diagram** for Bernoulli sampling of a *relational* ternary team, not the familiar rectangle definition or the equiprobable count derived from it. The 2017 semantics article introduces the relevant atom; the 2018 probabilistic-team article concerns weights on a fixed team and logical expressiveness, not the distribution of a Bernoulli-selected support. The 2023 dimension article studies independence-family dimensions and contains relevant combinatorial representations, but available evidence does not establish either of the critical exponents or the intervening zero law. Older random-database work and inaccessible full text leave a genuine equivalence risk, not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete sharp phase diagram for a natural satisfaction invariant from team and database semantics. The constraint is nonmonotone under addition of rows: it holds with high probability for almost-empty and nearly complete teams, yet fails throughout the entire region between two **unequal** critical powers. Each boundary has a different finite nontrivial Poisson limit, useful when interpreting satisfiability under sparse insertion noise and near-complete deletion noise. The exact finite polynomial makes the transition computable without numerical simulation.

## Limits of assessment
The full 2023 dimension paper could not be read from an authorized complete-text source despite attempts at preprint and institutional OA routes; indexed substantive statements were compared and the omission is retained as a residual originality risk. Other variants of the random-database problem might restate these limits. The verifier is finite corroboration, not proof of convergence.
