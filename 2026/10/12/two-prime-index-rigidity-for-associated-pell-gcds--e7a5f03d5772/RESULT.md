# Two-prime index rigidity for associated Pell gcds

## Finding

Let the associated Pell sequence be \(Q_0=Q_1=1\) and \(Q_{n+2}=2Q_{n+1}+Q_n\). For \(k\ge1\), set \(G(k)=\gcd(k,Q_k)\). We determine \(G(k)\) for **every index with at most two distinct prime divisors**:

\[
G(1)=1,\qquad G(p^a)=1\quad(p\text{ prime},\ a\ge1),
\]

and, for distinct primes \(p<q\) and \(a,b\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{G(p^a q^b)=\begin{cases}q^b,&q\mid Q_{p^a},\\1,&q\nmid Q_{p^a}.\end{cases}}
\]

Thus the smaller indexed prime never divides the associated Pell term; the larger prime either does not appear in the index gcd or appears to the full exponent of the index. The criterion is computable by reducing just \(Q_{p^a}\) modulo \(q\), not by computing the enormous term at the product index.

The proposed rank-of-apparition equivalence in Conjecture 32 of [1] fails for the infinite family

\[
G(3\cdot2^a)=1\qquad(a\ge2),
\]

even though \(3\mid3\cdot2^a\) and the entry point \(e_Q(3)=2\) divides \(3\cdot2^a\). For example, \(Q_{12}=19601\) and \(\gcd(19601,12)=1\). Conversely, the nontrivial family

\[
G(2\cdot3^b)=3^b\qquad(b\ge1)
\]

shows that a large prime-power block can survive intact.

## Assumptions and scope

All indices are positive integers; the variables \(p,q\) are rational primes with \(p<q\), and \(a,b\) are arbitrary positive integers. The valuation \(\nu_\ell(z)\) is the usual exponent of an odd prime \(\ell\) in a nonzero integer. This theorem does not assert an evaluation of \(G(k)\) for indices having three or more distinct prime factors. The entry point \(e_Q(\ell)\), when used, is the least positive \(n\) for which \(\ell\mid Q_n\).

## Proof

**1. Frobenius congruence.** Work in the commutative quadratic order \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\), with \(\alpha=1+\sqrt2\) and \(\beta=1-\sqrt2\). These satisfy \(\alpha\beta=-1\) and \(\alpha+\beta=2\), while the integral identity

\[
2Q_m=\alpha^m+\beta^m
\]

holds for every \(m\ge0\). For any odd prime \(\ell\), the binomial Frobenius congruence gives

\[
2Q_{\ell m}=\alpha^{\ell m}+\beta^{\ell m}
\equiv(\alpha^m+\beta^m)^\ell=(2Q_m)^\ell
\equiv2Q_m\pmod\ell.
\]

Since \(2\) is invertible modulo \(\ell\),

\[
Q_{\ell m}\equiv Q_m\pmod\ell.\tag{1}
\]

Applying this with \(\ell=p\) repeatedly gives \(Q_{p^a}\equiv Q_1=1\pmod p\) for every odd \(p\). At the remaining prime \(2\), every \(Q_m\) is odd by its recurrence. This proves \(G(p^a)=1\).

**2. Exact odd-prime lifting.** We need a strengthened form of (1). If \(\ell\) is odd and \(\ell\mid Q_m\), then

\[
\nu_\ell(Q_{\ell m})=\nu_\ell(Q_m)+1.\tag{2}
\]

To prove (2), put \(A=\alpha^m\), \(B=\beta^m\), and \(A+B=\ell^e c\), where \(e=\nu_\ell(2Q_m)\ge1\) and the integer \(c\) is not divisible by \(\ell\). Because \(AB=(-1)^m\), both \(A\) and \(B\) are units modulo \(\ell\) in the quadratic order. Substituting \(A=-B+\ell^e c\) into \(A^\ell+B^\ell\) produces the leading term

\[
\ell^{e+1}B^{\ell-1}c.
\]

For every intermediate binomial term the coefficient contributes another factor of \(\ell\) and at least \(2e\) comes from the binomial argument; the final term has valuation at least \(e\ell\ge e+2\). Consequently, the remaining sum is divisible by \(\ell^{e+2}\), whereas the displayed leading coefficient is nonzero modulo \(\ell\). Thus \(\nu_\ell(2Q_{\ell m})=e+1\), proving (2), since \(\ell\) is odd. Iterating (2) shows that whenever \(\ell\mid Q_m\),

\[
\nu_\ell(Q_{m\ell^b})=\nu_\ell(Q_m)+b\qquad(b\ge1).\tag{3}
\]

When \(\ell\nmid Q_m\), iteration of (1) instead proves \(\ell\nmid Q_{m\ell^b}\).

**3. The smaller prime is absent.** If \(p=2\), parity already gives \(p\nmid Q_{p^a q^b}\). Let \(p\) be odd. Repeated application of (1) reduces the question to whether \(p\mid Q_{q^b}\). We show this is impossible.

In \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\), choose \(s\) with \(s^2=2\), put \(\alpha=1+s\), \(\beta=1-s\), and \(u=\alpha/\beta\). The denominator is invertible because \(\alpha\beta=-1\). The Frobenius map either fixes \(s\) or sends \(s\) to \(-s\), according to whether \(2\) is a square modulo \(p\). Hence

\[
u^p=u\qquad\text{or}\qquad u^p=u^{-1}.
\]

Thus the multiplicative order \(r\) of \(u\) divides \(p-1\) or \(p+1\), in particular \(r\le p+1<q\). Suppose \(p\mid Q_{q^b}\). As \(2Q_{q^b}=\alpha^{q^b}+\beta^{q^b}\), this would give \(u^{q^b}=-1\). Because \(\gcd(q,r)=1\), exponentiation by \(q^b\) is an automorphism of the cyclic group generated by \(u\); hence \(u\) itself has order \(2\), so \(u=-1\). This contradicts \(\alpha+\beta=2\ne0\) in odd characteristic. Therefore \(p\nmid Q_{q^b}\) and, by (1), \(p\nmid Q_{p^a q^b}\).

**4. The larger prime is all or nothing.** Let \(m=p^a\). For \(q\nmid Q_m\), (1) immediately gives \(q\nmid Q_{mq^b}\). For \(q\mid Q_m\), (3) gives

\[
\nu_q(Q_{mq^b})=\nu_q(Q_m)+b\ge b+1.
\]

Thus \(\gcd(Q_{mq^b},p^aq^b)\) has exactly \(q^b\) as its \(q\)-part. Step 3 ruled out the \(p\)-part, completing the asserted dichotomy.

**5. Infinite obstruction and positive families.** The recurrence gives \(Q_2=3\), \(Q_4=17\), and the doubling identity

\[
Q_{2m}=2Q_m^2-(-1)^m.
\]

Every \(2^a\) with \(a\ge2\) is even, so \(Q_4\equiv2\pmod3\) and the doubling identity yields \(Q_{2^a}\equiv1\pmod3\) for all \(a\ge3\). Applying the dichotomy with \(p=2,q=3\) proves \(G(3\cdot2^a)=1\) for all \(a\ge2\). However \(e_Q(3)=2\) because \(Q_1=1\) and \(Q_2=3\), so each such index meets the proposed but insufficient rank condition. Finally \(3\mid Q_2\), and taking \(p=2,q=3,a=1\) gives \(G(2\cdot3^b)=3^b\) for all \(b\ge1\). This finishes the proof.

An accompanying dependency-free `verify.py` implements an exact modular fast-doubling evaluation of the associated Pell recurrence. Its output is `verification_output.txt`. It compares the modular evaluator against the ordinary recurrence, verifies the formula at every qualifying index up to \(30000\), tests seven explicit much larger indices, and checks the two displayed infinite families over substantial finite ranges. The computation is **not** used to infer any statement at untested indices; the proof above establishes the universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

Mbirika, Schrader, and Spilker [1], Section 6.2, Question 31 explicitly request closed expressions for \(\gcd(Q_k,k)\) and five related sequences. Their Theorem 28 leaves such an index gcd unevaluated. Their Conjecture 32 proposes a rank-of-apparition criterion for nontrivial \(\gcd(Q_k,k)\), but the family in Step 5 disproves its sufficient direction. The theorem here classifies the natural entire one- and two-prime-support domain, not the general case of three or more indexed primes.

Fiebig, Mbirika, and Spilker [2] analyze entry points and periods in general Lucas pairs, including companion sequences; their period equalities do not supply the present index-gcd formula. Flórez, Higuita, and Mukherjee [3] classify strong divisibility for polynomial Fibonacci-type sequences, a different question comparing sequence terms at two sequence indices rather than comparing a sequence term to its ordinary integer index. Known elementary divisibility identities for companion Lucas sequences motivate, but do not alone establish, the precise smaller-prime exclusion and full prime-power occurrence proved here.

## Limitations

The exact gcd dichotomy is restricted to indices with at most two distinct prime factors. It does not classify general index-gcds or correct the rank conjecture at arbitrary supports. Novelty is assessed relative to the identified literature and searched records; unindexed equivalent results or implicit classical corollaries may exist. Computational checks are finite, and independent validation has not been performed.
