# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact finite Curie--Weiss magnetization sum was expanded by excitation sector after normalization by the two fully magnetized ground states. The limiting zeros are simple, so the implicit-function expansion is valid. The generic paired-sector calculation was not applied across its degeneracies: \(n=2\), \(n=3\), and the unpaired zero-magnetization sector at \(n=4\) were each checked separately. The packaged finite computation is corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS
The closest inspected source, arXiv:2208.00917, proves strict monotone motion of the same finite-size Curie--Weiss zeros and their convergence to \((2k-1)\pi/(2n)\), but its proof stops at a ground-state-pair limit and does not give the exponential rate. The primary source arXiv:2203.05533 studies global large-degree Lee--Yang asymptotics with fixed inverse temperature. arXiv:2002.01269 studies finite-size scaling toward thermodynamic criticality. Searches covering fixed-size, low-temperature, strong-coupling, unitary-Hermite, convergence-rate, and excitation-sector aliases did not locate a covering statement. Residual risk remains that an older low-temperature expansion contains an equivalent specialization under another normalization.

## Value — PASS
Quantitative strong-coupling motion is a natural continuation of the published zero-motion theorem. The coefficients identify which physical magnetization sectors control relaxation and explain a finite-size degeneracy at \(n=4\). The result supplies a structural Arrhenius law and its next excitation correction rather than a small numerical improvement.
