# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The two-task objective is an exact two-piece linear concave function on a Euclidean ball. The weaker-task support point gives the first branch; its zero-progress and equal-progress events yield two different thresholds. Projection onto the equal-progress hyperplane gives the second branch, and exact algebra proves the safe-range criterion.

Risk: the unequal-norm theorem intentionally covers the regime where the mean gradient harms the weaker task.

## Originality

PASS. The defining full text gives the general primal, dual, two-task picture, and convergence range but not the closed two-task phase diagram. Focused published-record searches found no implication-equivalent common-descent threshold, balancing threshold, one-third barrier, or equal-norm no-rotation law.

Residual risk: one closely related later source was only partially accessible, and a later clipped-CAGrad variant contains related two-objective algebra.

## Value

PASS. CAGrad is designed to trade average-gradient fidelity against worst-task improvement. The result gives exact tuning boundaries for two different conflict-resolution goals and identifies a sharp norm-imbalance regime where the stronger goal is impossible under the source's convergence-safe parameter restriction.
