# Review of “Under multiple choice, Galois-type bases already characterize choice without Foundation”

## Correctness
PASS. The argument was reconstructed from Theorems A and B of arXiv:2609.20140v2. The only inheritance step is that if \(K\subseteq F\subseteq L\) and \(L/K\) is normal, separable, and algebraic, then \(L/F\) has the same three properties; the source itself uses this fact in the proof of Theorem A. The restricted basis principle therefore supplies exactly the hypotheses Theorem A needs.

## Originality
PASS. Corollary 7.1 of the source uses the broader statement that every vector space over every well-orderable characteristic-zero field has a basis. Targeted semantic-database, web, alias, and implication searches did not identify the restricted normal-separable-algebraic characterization. The closest semantic hits concerned unrelated uses of normal bases or fields. Historical searches also found work on bases over fixed or specific fields; accessible statements use broader all-vector-space hypotheses. One thematically close 2013 article was available only at abstract level and is recorded as a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The result identifies a natural and substantially smaller class of vector spaces sufficient for the foundation-free choice argument once multiple choice is assumed. It clarifies which part of the basis principle is structurally active in the source proof rather than merely rephrasing the broad equivalence.

The main limitation is explicit: the theorem retains \(\mathsf{MC}\), and it does not determine whether the restricted basis principle alone has any stronger choice-theoretic consequences.
