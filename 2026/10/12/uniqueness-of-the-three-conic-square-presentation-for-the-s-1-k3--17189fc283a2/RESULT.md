# Uniqueness of the three-conic square presentation for the \(S_1\) K3 sextic
## Finding
Let \(B=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\) be the smooth branch sextic of a K3 double plane \(\pi:X\to\mathbb P^2\) whose Néron--Severi lattice is of type \(S_1\) in Roulleau's classification. Then \(B\) has exactly three smooth conics whose intersection divisor with \(B\) is everywhere even. Equivalently, it has exactly three smooth six-tangent conics.

Fix a homogeneous sextic equation \(F\) for \(B\). Suppose
\[
F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2=q'_1q'_2q'_3-(f')^2,
\]
where all six quadratic forms define smooth pairwise distinct conics within their respective triples. Then there are a permutation \(\sigma\in S_3\), nonzero scalars \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\in\mathbb C^*\) with \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3=1\), and a sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) such that
\[
q'_i=\lambda_i q_{\sigma(i)}\quad(1\le i\le3),\qquad f'=\varepsilon f.
\]
In addition,
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb C)}(B)=\{1\}.
\]
Thus the displayed presentation has no ambiguity beyond permutation, product-one rescaling of the conic equations, and the sign of the cubic square root.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The K3 surface \(X\) has Néron--Severi lattice exactly of type \(S_1\), hence Picard rank \(3\). The conics considered in the uniqueness statement are smooth and pairwise distinct. A six-tangent conic means a smooth conic whose local intersection multiplicity with \(B\) is even at every intersection point; multiplicity-two tangency at six distinct points is the generic case.

The statement concerns a fixed branch sextic and a fixed homogeneous equation \(F\). Since a defining equation is only determined up to a nonzero scalar, any other choice can first be normalized to \(F\) over \(\mathbb C\) by absorbing a scalar into one quadratic factor and a square root into the cubic factor.

## Proof
Roulleau proves that an \(S_1\) surface contains exactly six \((-2)\)-curves \(A_1,\ldots,A_6\), that the unique square-two polarization \(D\) gives the double-plane map, and that
\[
A_1+A_4=2D,\qquad A_2+A_5=2D,\qquad A_3+A_6=2D.
\]
The three sums are the pullbacks of three smooth six-tangent conics. He also proves that the deck involution exchanges \(A_j\) with \(A_{j+3}\), with indices modulo \(6\).

Now let \(C\subset\mathbb P^2\) be any smooth six-tangent conic. Since \(C\cong\mathbb P^1\) and the divisor \(B|_C\) is even, the restriction of the sextic equation is a square in \(H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(6))\), up to a nonzero scalar. Hence the double cover \(\pi^{-1}(C)\to C\) splits into two components, each mapping isomorphically to \(C\). Each component is a smooth rational curve on the K3 surface and therefore is a \((-2)\)-curve. The two components are exchanged by the deck involution.

There are only six \((-2)\)-curves on \(X\), and the deck involution has exactly the three orbits
\[
\{A_1,A_4\},\qquad\{A_2,A_5\},\qquad\{A_3,A_6\}.
\]
Therefore every smooth six-tangent conic is one of the three already obtained from these orbits. This proves completeness of the three conics.

For uniqueness of the square presentation, each equation \(F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2\) makes every \(V(q_i)\) a six-tangent conic, because on \(V(q_i)\) one has \(F=-f^2\). Completeness therefore implies, after a permutation, that \(q'_i=\lambda_iq_i\) for nonzero scalars \(\lambda_i\). Put \(c=\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\) and \(Q=q_1q_2q_3\). Subtracting the two expressions for \(F\) gives
\[
(f'-f)(f'+f)=(c-1)Q.
\]
If \(c\ne1\), both factors on the left are nonzero homogeneous cubics. Unique factorization in \(\mathbb C[x,y,z]\) forces every irreducible factor of each cubic to be one of the three irreducible quadrics \(q_1,q_2,q_3\). But any product of a subset of these quadrics has even degree, whereas a nonzero factor \(f'\pm f\) has degree \(3\). This is impossible. Hence \(c=1\), and then \((f'-f)(f'+f)=0\), so \(f'=f\) or \(f'=-f\).

Finally let \(g\in\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb C)\) preserve \(B\). For a representative linear change of coordinates, \(g^*F=\alpha F\) for some \(\alpha\in\mathbb C^*\). Choosing \(\beta\in\mathbb C^*\) with \(\beta^2=\alpha\), the rule on the weighted double-plane model \(w^2=F\) given by applying \(g\) to the plane coordinates and multiplying \(w\) by \(\beta\) lifts \(g\) to an automorphism of \(X\). Roulleau proves \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong C_2\), generated by the deck involution. Both elements of this group descend to the identity on the plane, so the lifted automorphism can descend to \(g\) only when \(g=1\). Thus the projective stabilizer of \(B\) is trivial.

## Verification
The source statements used in the proof were checked in the full text of arXiv:1909.01909v5: the six-curve intersection matrix and completeness of the \((-2)\)-curve list occur in Section 3.1; the three relations summing opposite curves to \(2D\), the double-plane description, the deck action, and \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong C_2\) occur in Proposition 8 and its surrounding discussion. Section 5.1 gives the construction \(F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2\) and the dimension count for the family.

The accompanying verifier checks the displayed six-curve matrix, the three deck orbits, and the elementary factor-degree obstruction used in the uniqueness argument. The lifting argument for projective stabilizers is algebraic and does not rely on numerical experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Roulleau proves existence of three six-tangent conics and writes the generic branch sextic in the form \(F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2\). Theorem 20 obtains the expected moduli dimension by quotienting the evident continuous rescalings, but the source does not state that the three conics exhaust all smooth six-tangent conics, does not identify the complete fiber of the square presentation, and does not state triviality of the projective stabilizer of the branch sextic.

The later atlas studies configurations of \((-2)\)-curves for K3 surfaces with finite automorphism group and provides broader context, but no covering uniqueness statement for this \(S_1\) three-conic presentation was located. The new point is the exact fiber: the finite and scalar ambiguities visible in the formula are all of them.

## Limitations
No claim is made for branch sextics whose double covers have Néron--Severi lattice strictly larger than \(S_1\); additional splitting conics or projective symmetries can then occur. Reducible or nonreduced quadratic factors are outside the statement. The result concerns projective automorphisms of the plane sextic, not abstract automorphisms of the genus-ten curve that need not extend to \(\mathbb P^2\).
