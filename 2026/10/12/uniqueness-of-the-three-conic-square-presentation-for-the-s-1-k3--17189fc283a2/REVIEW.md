# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The lead source gives the complete six-element set of \((-2)\)-curves, the three opposite-pair relations over the double-plane polarization, the deck action, and \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong C_2\). For any further smooth six-tangent conic, evenness of the branch divisor on \(\mathbb P^1\) forces its pullback to split into two rational curves; adjunction makes them \((-2)\)-curves, so completeness leaves only the three published deck orbits. The uniqueness of \(F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2\) then follows from unique factorization and the parity obstruction that a nonzero cubic cannot be a product of the irreducible quadratic factors. The projective-stabilizer assertion follows because every plane automorphism preserving the branch lifts to the double cover, while the full K3 automorphism group is only the deck group.

## Originality
PASS. Full-text inspection of the lead paper found existence of three six-tangent conics, the exact six-curve configuration, the automorphism group, and the construction \(F=q_1q_2q_3-f^2\), but not completeness of the smooth six-tangent conics, exact uniqueness of the square presentation, or triviality of the plane sextic stabilizer. Searches for the exact presentation, uniqueness, six-tangent-conic completeness, and equivalent projective-rigidity formulations found no covering statement. The later atlas is broader but does not supply the claimed exact fiber for the \(S_1\) presentation.

## Value
PASS. The source constructs a seventeen-dimensional \(S_1\) family from tuples of three quadrics and one cubic and removes the evident continuous rescaling in its dimension count. Determining the exact fiber is a natural moduli question: the result shows that no additional smooth splitting conic and no hidden projective isotropy enlarges that fiber. This converts a parameter count into a rigid presentation statement on the \(S_1\) locus.

The result does not extend to higher-Picard specializations, where additional splitting curves or symmetries can appear.
