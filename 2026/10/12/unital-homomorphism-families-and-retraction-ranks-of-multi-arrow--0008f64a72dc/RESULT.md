# Unital homomorphism families and retraction ranks of multi-arrow Kronecker path algebras

## Finding
For every field \(K\) and integers \(r,s\ge1\), all **unital \(K\)-algebra homomorphisms** between the multi-arrow Kronecker path algebras \(\mathcal K_r(K)\) and \(\mathcal K_s(K)\) fall into exactly three explicitly parametrized types. Their finite-field count, and the exact labeled retraction-rank distribution and automorphism-conjugacy classes, are determined. Specifically,
\[
|\operatorname{Hom}_{K\text{-alg},1}(\mathcal K_r(\mathbb F_q),\mathcal K_s(\mathbb F_q))|=q^{s(r+1)}+q^s+2.
\]
For the idempotent endomorphisms of \(\mathcal K_n(\mathbb F_q)\), the complete **linear-rank** polynomial is
\[
\mathcal R_{n,q}(T)=2T+\sum_{d=0}^{n}{n\brack d}_q q^{(d+1)(n-d)}T^{d+2}.
\]
There are exactly \(n+3\) conjugacy classes under unital algebra automorphisms. The family of image algebras is precisely \(K\), \(K\times K\), and \(\mathcal K_d(K)\) for \(1\le d\le n\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(K\) be an **arbitrary field**, and let \(r,s\ge1\). Define \(\mathcal K_r(K)\) to be the associative unital algebra on the vector space \(Ke_1\oplus Ke_2\oplus V_r\), with \(\dim_KV_r=r\), where
\[
 e_i e_j=\delta_{ij}e_i,\quad e_1v=v=ve_2\ (v\in V_r),\quad e_2v=ve_1=0,\quad V_rV_r=0.
\]
The unit is \(1=e_1+e_2\). Equivalently, \(\mathcal K_r(K)\) is the path algebra of two vertices connected by \(r\) parallel arrows, equivalently the algebra of matrices \(\begin{psmallmatrix}a&v\\0&b\end{psmallmatrix}\) with \(a,b\in K\), \(v\in V_r\). Define \(\mathcal K_0(K)=K\times K\) only to describe possible image algebras. All homomorphisms below are \(K\)-linear and unit preserving. A retraction means an algebra endomorphism \(F\) with \(F^2=F\), not an element-idempotent or module endomorphism. The rank is the dimension of the linear image. All formulas hold for finite fields of every characteristic, including characteristic two.

## Proof
**1. Radical and quotient.** An element \(ae_1+be_2+v\) is nilpotent if and only if \(a=b=0\), since its image in \(K\times K\) has diagonal coordinates \(a,b\); conversely every \(v\in V_r\) squares to zero. Therefore the radical \(J_r=V_r\) is the entire nilpotent set and is preserved by any algebra homomorphism. Any unital \(K\)-algebra map therefore induces a map \(K\times K\to K\times K\), determined by the image of the idempotent \(e_1\) and the condition \(e_1+e_2=1\). The diagonal of the image must be one of \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,0)\). An element \(ae_1+be_2+u\) is idempotent if and only if \(a^2=a\), \(b^2=b\), and \((a+b-1)u=0\). Thus the lifted images of \(e_1\) in the first two cases are respectively \(e_1+u\) and \(e_2+u\), for arbitrary \(u\in V_s\), while in the latter two cases they are precisely \(1\) and \(0\). There is no characteristic restriction in these equations.

**2. Peirce compatibility and the three families.** Since \(v=e_1ve_2\), the arrow image must lie in \(F(e_1)J_sF(e_2)\). This space equals \(J_s\) when \(F(e_1)=e_1+u\) and \(F(e_2)=e_2-u\), but equals zero for the reversed diagonal orientation and for either constant diagonal orientation. In the first case, the image on \(V_r\) is an arbitrary \(K\)-linear map \(L:V_r\to V_s\). The defining relations show directly that every such choice is multiplicative. Hence all homomorphisms are, uniquely:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(P)}& e_1\mapsto e_1+u,\ e_2\mapsto e_2-u,\ v\mapsto L(v);\\
\text{(R)}& e_1\mapsto e_2+u,\ e_2\mapsto e_1-u,\ v\mapsto0;\\
\text{(S)}& ae_1+be_2+v\mapsto a1\quad\text{or}\quad ae_1+be_2+v\mapsto b1.
\end{array}
\]
Here \(u\in V_s\), and for (P) \(L\in\operatorname{Hom}_K(V_r,V_s)\). The four quotient types are distinct, so these parametrizations have no duplication. Over \(\mathbb F_q\) their sizes are respectively \(q^sq^{sr}\), \(q^s\), and two. This proves the Hom count.

**3. Idempotent endomorphisms.** Specialize \(r=s=n\). The map on \(K\times K\) induced by a retraction must itself be composition-idempotent; the reversed map interchanges the two quotient factors and squares to the identity, so is not idempotent. The scalar quotient maps are idempotent, and both augmentations in (S) square to themselves. For type (P), direct substitution gives \(F_{u,L}^2(e_1)=e_1+u+L(u)\), \(F_{u,L}^2(e_2)=e_2-u-L(u)\), and \(F_{u,L}^2(v)=L^2(v)\). Thus \(F_{u,L}^2=F_{u,L}\) if and only if \(L^2=L\) and \(L(u)=0\). The image of such an \(F_{u,L}\) is generated by \(e_1+u\), \(e_2-u\), and \(\operatorname{im}L\), and these satisfy the defining relations for \(\mathcal K_d(K)\), with \(d=\operatorname{rank}L\). Its vector-space dimension is \(d+2\); the two scalar maps have image \(K1\) of rank one.

**4. Finite-field enumeration.** For \(0\le d\le n\), the number of idempotent linear operators \(L: \mathbb F_q^n\to\mathbb F_q^n\) of rank \(d\) equals \({n\brack d}_q q^{d(n-d)}\): choose their image \(U\) and a complementary kernel \(W\), equivalently a graph of one of \(q^{d(n-d)}\) linear maps from a fixed complementary \((n-d)\)-space into \(U\). Once \(L\) is chosen, \(u\in\ker L\) has \(q^{n-d}\) choices. Multiplication gives \({n\brack d}_q q^{(d+1)(n-d)}\), proving the polynomial after adding the two scalar maps. Here \({n\brack d}_q=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(q^{n-j}-1)/(q^{d-j}-1)\), with empty product one. For example, \(\mathcal R_{2,2}(T)=2T+4T^2+12T^3+T^4\), for a total of 19 retractions.

**5. Automorphism conjugacy.** Every automorphism must preserve \(J_n\). Its induced automorphism of \(K\times K\) cannot swap the two factors: the Peirce space \(e_1J_ne_2\) has dimension \(n>0\) but \(e_2J_ne_1=0\). Thus the automorphisms are exactly maps \(F_{w,M}\) of type (P) with \(M\in\mathrm{GL}(V_n)\). Conjugating a retraction \(F_{u,L}\) by \(F_{w,I}\) changes \(u\) by a vector in \((I-L)V_n\), so, since \(u\in\ker L=(I-L)V_n\), its shear can be set to zero. A linear change of basis then conjugates the idempotent \(L\) to the projection \(\operatorname{diag}(I_d,0)\). Hence there is one conjugacy class for each \(0\le d\le n\). The two scalar maps are fixed under automorphisms because the two quotient characters are fixed; they constitute two additional distinct classes. Thus the total is \(n+3\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `checker.py` exhaustively enumerates all possible basis-image matrices over five small prime-field Hom cases, tests unit preservation and every multiplication-table relation, tests \(F^2=F\) for endomorphisms, and independently computes their ranks. The tests include \((r,s,q)=(2,2,2)\), giving exactly 70 homomorphisms and 19 retractions with rank counts \(2,4,12,1\) at ranks \(1,2,3,4\). Further integer checks test Gaussian-coefficient formulas. Run `python3 checker.py`; expected final line is `VERIFY_OK`. Finite enumerations corroborate but do not replace the arbitrary-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Iovanov and Sistko, *Maximal Subalgebras of Finite-Dimensional Algebras* (arXiv:1705.00762, first public 2017-05-02), study maximal-subalgebra types and explicitly the two-arrow Kronecker case in their Example 4.15. That is a genuine structural precursor but their results do not give the set of all unital algebra homomorphisms between arbitrary multi-arrow Kronecker path algebras, their composition-idempotent maps, or the rank-enumeration polynomial here. Facchini and Heidari Zadeh, *Algebras with a bilinear form, and Idempotent endomorphisms* (arXiv:2210.08230), discuss general rank-one splitting and idempotent endomorphisms in other algebra settings; the basic notion of a rank-one retraction is not claimed as new. Kinser and Oswald, *Hopf actions of some quantum groups on path algebras* (arXiv:2010.15197), address actions rather than this explicit Hom and retraction census. Known centralizer/commuting-probability counts for this path algebra are different invariants and do not imply its Hom cardinality or retraction-rank multiplicities.

## Limitations
All homomorphisms are unital and \(K\)-linear; neither general ring homomorphisms nor representation-module endomorphisms are included. The source and target must have at least one arrow; for zero-arrow sources or targets the quotient-automorphism case distinctions change. The theorem gives exact finite-field counts, not general finite-ring counts. The literature search did not establish a dominating prior theorem, but an equivalent classification under the terminology of triangular matrix algebras or one-point extensions remains a bibliographic possibility. The automated finite checks use prime fields; the arbitrary-field claims rest on the algebraic proof. No independent audit has been performed.
