# Universal covariance rigidity of cyclic marginal purities

## Finding
For an arbitrary pure state of \(n\ge3\) qudits of local dimension \(p\ge2\), consider the vector of purities of the \(n\) cyclic intervals of length \(1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Its affine hull has dimension \(n\) when \(2k<n\), and dimension \(k\) when \(2k=n\). Every affine relation in the balanced case is generated by equality of the purities of complementary intervals. In particular, **every full-support pure-state ensemble has exactly this covariance rank**, regardless of its density or symmetry. These conclusions concern exact linear dependencies, not statistical independence or lower bounds on nonzero eigenvalues.

## Assumptions and scope
Put \(V=\mathbb Z_n\), \(A_s=\{s,s+1,\ldots,s+k-1\}\subset V\), with all site labels taken modulo \(n\), and let \(\mathcal H=(\mathbb C^p)^{\otimes n}\). For any unit vector \(\psi\in\mathcal H\), let \(\rho_{A_s}=\operatorname{Tr}_{V\setminus A_s}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\) and \(P_s(\psi)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{A_s}^2)\). An ensemble has full support when every nonempty open set in the projective pure-state space has positive probability. Haar measure is one such ensemble, but no Haar invariance is assumed. All asserted covariance ranks are for the covariance of the real vector of \(n\) purities \((P_s)_s\), not for the covariance of density-matrix entries or for an empirical finite sample.

## Proof
**1. Covariance and universal affine relations.** For any real vector \(c\), the covariance quadratic form is \(\operatorname{Var}(\sum_s c_sP_s)\). If this vanishes for a full-support ensemble, continuity of the purity functions makes \(\sum_s c_sP_s\) constant on every pure state: its deviation from the almost-sure value is a continuous function, so a nonzero value anywhere would persist on an open set of positive measure. Conversely every universal affine relation gives a covariance null vector.

**2. Finite pure-state probes.** In a fully product state, all purities equal one. Place a maximally entangled \(p\)-level pair on sites \(j\) and \(j+h\), leaving the other sites in pure product states. Then for each interval
\[
P_s=1-(1-p^{-1})B_h(j,s),\qquad
B_h(j,s)=\mathbf1\{\text{exactly one of }j,j+h\text{ belongs to }A_s\}.
\]
Thus any universal affine relation forces \(B_hc=0\) for every pair displacement \(h\). It is enough to use translations of \(h=1\) and, when \(2k<n\), of \(h=k+1\).

**3. Fourier elimination.** The matrices \(B_h\) are circulant under simultaneous shifts of \(j,s\). Let \(z\) be any complex \(n\)th root of unity and test a Fourier vector \(c_s=z^s\). For adjacent sites \((j,j+1)\), an interval separates the pair precisely when it starts at \(j+1\) or at \(j-k+1\). Consequently
\[
(B_1c)_j=z^{j-k+1}(1+z^k).
\]
If \(2k<n\), the sites \(j\) and \(j+k+1\) cannot share any length-\(k\) interval. The intervals containing exactly one of them start in the disjoint ranges \(j-k+1,\ldots,j\) and \(j+2,\ldots,j+k+1\). Therefore
\[
(B_{k+1}c)_j=z^{j-k+1}(1+z^{k+1})\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}z^r.
\]
The first expression can vanish only when \(z^k=-1\), which implies \(z\ne1\). In that case \(\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}z^r=2/(1-z)\) and \(1+z^{k+1}=1-z\); the second expression equals \(2z^{j-k+1}\ne0\). Thus the two probe families have no common Fourier kernel and their stacked cut-incidence matrix has full column rank \(n\). All affine relations are trivial if \(2k<n\).

If \(2k=n\), the Schmidt spectra of complementary reductions agree, hence \(P_{s+k}=P_s\) for every state. The adjacent-pair probe matrix has precisely the Fourier null modes \(z^k=-1\), namely the odd frequencies of the \(n\)-cycle, a \(k\)-dimensional space. These modes are exactly the coefficient vectors obeying \(c_{s+k}=-c_s\). There are no further relations, so the affine dimension and every full-support covariance rank are exactly \(k\).

**4. Affine hull.** Subtract the all-ones purity vector of the product probe from the probe purity vectors. Their spans are nonzero scalar multiples of the rows of the matrices just analyzed. Hence even this explicit finite set of states already has affine dimension \(n\) in the unbalanced case and \(k\) in the balanced case. The whole pure-state image can have no smaller dimension; the previous kernel classification shows it cannot have larger dimension in the balanced case. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library script `verify.py` constructs exact finite cut-incidence matrices for all \(3\le n\le20\) and every allowed \(k\), checks their ranks modulo a large prime, and separately reconstructs the Haar covariance from all \(24\) four-replica permutations for small local dimensions and system sizes. In its finite tests it reports `VERIFY_OK incidence_cases=99 four_replica_cases=116 covariance_cases=38`. The analytic Fourier and full-support arguments, not the bounded calculations, establish the theorem for all admissible parameters.

For independent cross-checking against Haar-specific literature, let \(N=p^n\), \(q_d=\max(0,k-d)+\max(0,k-n+d)\), \(\mu=(p^k+p^{n-k})/(N+1)\), and \(D=N(N+1)(N+2)(N+3)\). Direct four-replica expansion gives the cyclic covariance entry
\[
C_d=\frac{p^{4n-2k}+2p^{3n}+4p^{3n-2k}+8p^{2n}+p^{2n+2k}+4p^{n+2k}+2p^{2n-2k+2q_d}+2p^{n+2k-2q_d}}{D}-\mu^2.
\]
For \(n=4\), \(p=2\), \(k=2\), the variance of the *average* cyclic interval purity is approximately \(0.0030049171371334914\), agreeing with the known Haar benchmark. This four-replica expression is a reproducibility cross-check, **not** the asserted new ensemble-independent conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work
Mirsohi (arXiv:2608.28914v1) introduces planar interval purity and obtains its complete Haar two-interval covariance kernel and variance. The present statement addresses a different question: exact universal affine dependencies and covariance nullspaces for **every** full-support ensemble, without Haar invariance; its proof uses product and Bell-pair witness states rather than Haar integration. Doroudiani and Karimipour (2020) introduce planar maximally entangled states, and Wang (2021) introduces planar \(k\)-uniform states. Their maximally mixed marginal criteria motivate the interval family but are not used to establish covariance rigidity. Equality of complementary purities is classical Schmidt-decomposition background, and no novelty is claimed for that identity itself.

## Limitations
The theorem provides only rank and nullspace, not positive lower eigenvalue bounds, conditioning or finite-sample statistical guarantees. A discrete or restricted ensemble without full support can have additional dependencies. The proof applies to pure global states with identical local dimensions, fixed length \(k\), and cyclic intervals; it does not classify covariance ranks for arbitrary families of disconnected subsystems or mixed global states. The most directly related 2026 preprint's complete body was unavailable for statement-by-statement comparison; overlap with an unexamined appendix remains a literature risk and needs independent verification before any claim of definitive priority.
