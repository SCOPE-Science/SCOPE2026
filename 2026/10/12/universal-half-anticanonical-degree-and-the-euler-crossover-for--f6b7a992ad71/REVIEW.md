# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. For a simply connected semisimple \(G\), the Weyl vector \(\rho\) is an integral strictly dominant weight, so \(L_\rho\) is very ample. Borel--Weil and Weyl's dimension formula give
\[
h^0(G/B,L_\rho^{\otimes m})=(m+1)^N,
\]
hence
\[
\deg_{L_\rho}(G/B)=N!.
\]
The Bruhat decomposition gives
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(G/B)=|W|.
\]

Writing the Weyl exponents as \(e_i\), one has
\[
|W|=\prod_i(e_i+1),\qquad N=\sum_i e_i.
\]
The inequality \(e_i+1\le2^{e_i}\) gives \(|W|\le2^N\), while \(N!>2^N\) for \(N\ge4\). Direct inspection of the four semisimple root systems with \(N\le3\) yields the stated three sub-equality cases and the unique equality \(A_2\).

## Originality
PASS. The inspected archival flag-variety sources supply the homogeneous-line-bundle and Schubert-cell inputs, and the Weyl formulas supply the representation-theoretic numerical ingredients. ## Value
PASS. The half-anticanonical polarization is canonical, and its degree being determined solely by dimension rather than Lie type is a strong uniformity phenomenon. The Euler comparison then separates nearly every semisimple root system from the exceptional low-dimensional cases and identifies \(A_2\) as the unique equality type.
