# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact intersection probability for any \(k\) designated inclusion-defect vertices is \(q^{nk}(1-q^{n-k})^k\); this uses nonoverlapping row trials after columns are forced empty. Expanding the product of defect indicators proves the full distribution, not just its mean. Pairwise covariance is nonpositive, yielding the large-mean failure bound; normalized factorial moments tend to those of \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) whenever the defect mean tends to a nonzero finite limit. The bounds for the two critical windows and the intermediate regime are proved for moving Bernoulli parameters, with endpoints covered. An actual standalone exhaustive verifier checks all binary teams through order four and rational weights, including all distribution coefficients and moments. It does not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS in assessed scope, with residual risk

The full texts of Hannula–Hella, arXiv:1903.10706v1, and Hannula–Kontinen–Link, arXiv:2101.02466v1, were inspected for the same logical atom, implication coverage, and probabilistic counting. They cover complexity, repairs, implication and axiomatization; neither contains the Bernoulli exact law or the re-entrant probability limit. Indexed-record searches for inclusion, zero-indegree/positive-outdegree digraphs and random-relation Poisson limits did not identify a covering result. Classical degree-count Poisson theorems (Kordecki 1996, abstract inspected) mean an older equivalent graph-theoretic formulation cannot be excluded without further independent literature work. A name/parameter mismatch is not treated as evidence of novelty, and a search miss alone is insufficient.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Unary inclusion is a natural fundamental atom of team logic and a database integrity dependency. Its satisfaction probability is nonmonotone under a changing relation density. The theorem does more than supply two limits: it gives the complete finite defect distribution and a single universal intensity criterion covering arbitrary, potentially oscillatory sequences along convergent-intensity subsequences, with a negative-dependence variance bound and explicit interior-failure classification. This is a structurally informative reliability profile for a defined logical property rather than an arbitrary finite invariant.

## Closest literature and limits

Hannula–Hella (2019) gives the semantics and complexity baseline; Hannula–Kontinen–Link (2021) establishes dependency interactions; Kordecki (1996) is closest in general rare-degree Poisson methodology but not in the inspected inclusion-specific claim. Equivalent prior graph-theoretic enumeration is the principal residual risk. The proof assumes independent pair sampling, inclusion of loops, and one unary atom; it establishes neither arbitrary-team input models nor multiple-atom interaction limits.
