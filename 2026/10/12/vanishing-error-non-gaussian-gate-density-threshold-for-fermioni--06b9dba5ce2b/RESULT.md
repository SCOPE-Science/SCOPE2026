# Vanishing-error non-Gaussian gate-density threshold for fermionic codeword preparation
## Finding
Fix an integer \(q\ge 4\). Consider a sequence of fermionic codes \(\mathcal C_j\) with parameters \([[n_j,k_j,d_j]]_{\mathrm F}\) such that
\[
n_j\to\infty,\qquad \frac{k_j}{n_j}\to R>0,\qquad \frac{d_j}{n_j}\to\Delta>0.
\]
For a circuit with \(g_j\) bounded-weight non-Gaussian gates, let \(F_j=F^{(n_j)}_{g_j,q}(\mathcal C_j)\) be the doped-Gaussian codeword fidelity of Tang et al. Define
\[
\gamma_0=\frac{\Delta}{q-2},\qquad
\gamma_*=\frac{\Delta}{q-2}+\frac{R}{q}.
\]
There are two asymptotic obstruction regimes.

If \(g_j/n_j\to\gamma<\gamma_0\), then
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_j}\log F_j
\le
\frac{\Delta-(q-2)\gamma}{2}
\log\!\bigl(1-H_2^{-1}(R)\bigr)<0.
\]
Thus the maximum attainable codeword fidelity is exponentially small.

If \(\gamma_0<\gamma<\gamma_*\), define \(\sigma=q(\gamma-\gamma_0)\). Then \(0<\sigma<R\) and
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}F_j
\le
1-H_2^{-1}\!\left(\frac{R-\sigma}{1-\sigma}\right)<1.
\]
Thus the fidelity remains uniformly separated from one.

The threshold statement does not require convergence of \(g_j/n_j\). For every fixed \(\eta>0\), if
\[
\frac{g_j}{n_j}\le\gamma_*-\eta
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\), then
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}F_j
\le
1-H_2^{-1}\!\left(\min\left\{\frac R2,\frac{q\eta}{4}\right\}\right)<1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
F_j\to1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{g_j}{n_j}
\ge
\frac{\Delta}{q-2}+\frac{R}{q}.
\]
Hence the leading non-Gaussian gate density forced by the exact-preparation lower bound remains necessary for vanishing preparation error.

## Assumptions and scope
The gate model is exactly the \((g,q)\)-doped Gaussian model of Tang et al.: fermionic Gaussian states and unitaries are free, while each counted non-Gaussian unitary is generated by a Hermitian Majorana product of even weight at most the fixed integer \(q\ge4\). The code distance is Majorana distance. The binary-entropy inverse \(H_2^{-1}\) is taken on the increasing branch with range \([0,1/2]\).

The result is conditional on a positive-rate, positive-relative-distance family; it does not assert existence of such a family under additional geometric, locality, or hardware constraints. It concerns state-dependent preparation of at least one codeword through the fidelity quantity of the source theorem, not a universal coherent encoder.

## Proof
Tang et al. define
\[
r_j=\left\lfloor\frac{d_j-3}{q-2}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
s_j=q\max\{g_j-r_j,0\},
\]
and
\[
t_j=\left\lfloor\frac{d_j-(q-2)\min\{g_j,r_j\}-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
Their Theorem 2 proves
\[
F_j\le
\left[
1-H_2^{-1}\!\left(
\frac{\max\{0,k_j-s_j\}}
{\max\{n_j-s_j,n_j-k_j\}}
\right)
\right]^{t_j},
\]
with \(t_j\ge1\).

First suppose \(g_j/n_j\to\gamma<\gamma_0\). Since \(r_j/n_j\to\gamma_0\), eventually \(g_j<r_j\). Hence \(s_j=0\), the entropy argument equals \(k_j/n_j\to R\), and
\[
\frac{t_j}{n_j}\to\frac{\Delta-(q-2)\gamma}{2}.
\]
Taking logarithms of the source bound and then the upper limit gives the exponential-rate inequality in the finding.

Next suppose \(\gamma_0<\gamma<\gamma_*\). Eventually \(g_j\ge r_j\), and
\[
\frac{s_j}{n_j}\to\sigma=q(\gamma-\gamma_0),
\qquad 0<\sigma<R.
\]
Therefore the entropy argument converges to
\[
\frac{R-\sigma}{1-\sigma},
\]
because \(1-\sigma>1-R\). Since \(t_j\ge1\), dropping all but one factor yields the stated constant upper bound on \(F_j\).

For the robust threshold, fix \(\eta>0\) and assume \(g_j/n_j\le\gamma_*-\eta\) eventually. If \(g_j<r_j\), then \(s_j=0\), so the entropy argument is \(k_j/n_j\ge R/2\) for all sufficiently large \(j\). If \(g_j\ge r_j\), use \(r_j/n_j\to\gamma_0\) and \(k_j/n_j\to R\). For all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\frac{r_j}{n_j}\ge\gamma_0-\frac{\eta}{2},
\qquad
\frac{k_j}{n_j}\ge R-\frac{q\eta}{4}.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{s_j}{n_j}
=q\left(\frac{g_j}{n_j}-\frac{r_j}{n_j}\right)
\le R-\frac{q\eta}{2},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{k_j-s_j}{n_j}\ge\frac{q\eta}{4}.
\]
Because \(\max\{n_j-s_j,n_j-k_j\}\le n_j\), the entropy argument is at least \(q\eta/4\) in this branch. Combining the two branches gives the lower bound
\[
\min\left\{\frac R2,\frac{q\eta}{4}\right\}
\]
for the entropy argument, and \(t_j\ge1\) gives the uniform fidelity gap. If \(F_j\to1\) while \(\liminf g_j/n_j<\gamma_*\), some subsequence would lie below \(\gamma_*-\eta\) for a fixed \(\eta>0\), contradicting that gap.

## Verification
The derivation uses the source theorem with its exact floor functions before taking limits. The critical facts checked directly from the source proof are that \(r_j\) has the stated definition, that \(s_j=q\max\{g_j-r_j,0\}\), and that \(t_j\ge1\) for every admissible code. The limiting steps use only continuity and monotonicity of \(H_2^{-1}\) on the source branch and the elementary facts \(r_j/n_j\to\Delta/(q-2)\) and \(k_j/n_j\to R\).

No finite experiment is used as evidence for the asymptotic theorem. Endpoint rates \(\gamma=\gamma_0\) and \(\gamma=\gamma_*\) are intentionally not assigned to the two displayed open regimes; the robust statement is what yields the necessary lower threshold for sequences with fidelity tending to one.

## Relationship to prior work
Tang et al. prove the finite-size fidelity inequality above and deduce the exact-preparation lower bound
\[
g\ge
\left\lfloor\frac{d_{\mathrm F}-3}{q-2}\right\rfloor
+
\left\lceil\frac{k}{q}\right\rceil.
\]
They summarize this as \(g=\Omega(d_{\mathrm F}+k)\) for fixed \(q\). The present result extracts the asymptotic fidelity phase structure of that inequality and shows that the same leading density threshold survives when exact preparation is relaxed to any sequence of errors tending to zero.

Mudassar, Schuckert, and Gottesman construct high-rate Majorana-code architectures and fault-tolerant gadgets. That work supplies nearby coding context but is not used to prove the gate-density theorem here. No claim is made that the threshold is achievable, or that it is optimal under a broader gate or preparation model.

## Limitations
The theorem is a consequence of the doped-Gaussian fidelity bound and inherits that model's assumptions. It does not provide an achievability construction above \(\gamma_*\), does not prove a converse for arbitrary non-Gaussian resource measures, and does not address universal encoding channels. The exponential-rate formula requires convergence of \(g_j/n_j\) strictly below \(\gamma_0\); the constant-gap formula requires a limiting rate strictly between \(\gamma_0\) and \(\gamma_*\). The final liminf threshold is one-sided and remains valid without either convergence assumption.
