# Weyl-monodromy isotropy of Shimada's cubic-surface Zariski pairs
## Finding
Let \(W=W(E_6)\) act on the \(27\) lines of a smooth cubic surface through the permutation representation printed by Shimada. For the four minimal representatives
\[
S_1=\{1,2,3,4,5\},\qquad S_2=\{1,2,3,4,21\},
\]
\[
T_1=\{1,2,3,4,5,27\},\qquad T_2=\{1,2,3,4,21,26\},
\]
the setwise stabilizers are
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_W(S_1)\cong\mathfrak S_5,\qquad
\operatorname{Stab}_W(S_2)\cong C_2\times\mathfrak S_5,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_W(T_1)\cong\operatorname{Stab}_W(T_2)\cong\mathfrak S_5.
\]
The stabilizer action on the marked lines is faithful for \(S_1,T_1,T_2\). For \(S_2\), its pointwise kernel has order \(2\) and is generated by
\[
\sigma_6=(5\,6)(10\,11)(14\,15)(17\,18)(19\,20)(26\,27).
\]
Hence \(S_2\) is the unique one of Shimada's four representatives with nontrivial Weyl-monodromy isotropy invisible on the arrangement itself.

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient group is the Weyl group \(W(E_6)\) acting on the \(27\) line classes, not the projective automorphism group of a fixed general cubic surface. The representatives and numbering are exactly those of Shimada: \(S_1,S_2\) are the two five-line representatives and \(T_1,T_2\) the two six-line star representatives. All calculations are over the finite permutation representation generated by the six reflections in equation (2.5).

For \(T_i\), the distinguished central line of the six-line star is fixed by every combinatorial automorphism and the five leaves may be permuted arbitrarily, so the abstract incidence-graph automorphism group is \(\mathfrak S_5\). For each \(S_i\), the five lines are pairwise disjoint, so their abstract permutation group is also \(\mathfrak S_5\).

## Proof
Shimada proves that deformation classes of line arrangements on a general cubic surface are the \(W(E_6)\)-orbits in the power set of the \(27\) lines, and gives six explicit permutations generating \(W(E_6)\). His Section 3.2 gives the four representatives above and orbit sizes
\[
|W\cdot S_1|=432,\quad |W\cdot S_2|=216,\quad
|W\cdot T_1|=|W\cdot T_2|=432.
\]
Since \(|W(E_6)|=51840\), orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(120,240,120,120\).

The exact permutation calculation then records the induced actions on the marked lines. In all four cases the induced image has order \(120\), hence is the full \(\mathfrak S_5\) allowed by the abstract incidence graph. The pointwise-kernel orders are, respectively,
\[
1,\quad 2,\quad 1,\quad 1.
\]
This already identifies the three order-\(120\) stabilizers with \(\mathfrak S_5\).

For \(S_2\), direct substitution in the six printed generators shows that \(\sigma_6\) fixes \(1,2,3,4,21\) pointwise, so it generates the kernel. Exhaustive multiplication in the \(240\)-element stabilizer verifies that this kernel is central. To determine the extension, let \(H=\operatorname{Stab}_W(S_2)\). The exact commutator subgroup has order \(60\). The involution
\[
\tau=(1\,4)(7\,13)(8\,16)(10\,19)(11\,20)(22\,25)
\]
lies in \(H\), and adjoining \(\tau\) to \([H,H]\) gives a subgroup of order \(120\) whose action on \(S_2\) is faithful and is all of \(\mathfrak S_5\). This subgroup does not contain \(\sigma_6\). Therefore the central kernel splits off:
\[
H=\langle\sigma_6\rangle\times\langle[H,H],\tau\rangle
\cong C_2\times\mathfrak S_5.
\]

## Verification
Run `python artifacts/verify.py` with Python and SymPy. The script reconstructs all six permutations from Shimada's equation (2.5), verifies \(|W(E_6)|=51840\), exhausts all group elements, recomputes the four setwise stabilizers and their pointwise kernels, checks that each induced image has order \(120\), verifies that \(\sigma_6\) is central in the \(S_2\) stabilizer, and verifies the order-\(120\) complement used in the splitting argument. Its final line is `VERIFY_OK`.

The verification is a complete finite enumeration in the stated permutation representation. No numerical approximation or unproved extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Shimada's Theorem 1.5 classifies exactly two Zariski pairs. Section 3.2 prints the four representatives and their orbit sizes, and Remark 3.3 recalls the classical result that five pairwise skew lines have two \(W(E_6)\)-orbits. The source uses lattice parity and complement homology to distinguish the two Zariski pairs. It does not state the isomorphism types of the four setwise stabilizers or identify the pointwise kernel of \(S_2\).

The associated `ZPcubic.txt` computation data records minimal representatives, orbit numbers, and orbit sizes. In the relevant data and searchable file there is no stated stabilizer or group-structure output. The present result uses those published representatives and the published \(W(E_6)\) generators but adds the isotropy-group classification and the faithful-versus-hidden-kernel distinction.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns monodromy isotropy inside \(W(E_6)\). It does not assert that these groups are projective automorphism groups of a fixed cubic surface. The calculation is specific to Shimada's four representatives, though the same method applies to other line-arrangement orbits. Classical literature on the \(27\)-line configuration is extensive, so a differently indexed older subgroup computation remains a residual originality risk; targeted searches and the inspected source/data did not locate one stating this four-case classification.
