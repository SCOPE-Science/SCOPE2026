# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The order-\(p\) subgroups are exactly the \(p+1\) lines in the \(p\)-torsion plane. There is one elementary abelian order-\(p^2\) subgroup and exactly \(p\) cyclic order-\(p^2\) subgroups, all sharing the same order-\(p\) subgroup. This gives precisely a \((p+2,p)\)-pineapple. Two white leaves or two white noncentral clique vertices are permanent forcing obstructions, while at most one white vertex in each class can always be cleared, with the center handled first by a blue leaf if necessary. This proves the all-set classification and the factored polynomial. The packaged verifier exhausts all subsets for four primes and reconstructs the actual subgroup graphs for \(p=2,3\).

## Originality

PASS. The 2009 pineapple minimum-rank theorem and later pineapple zero-forcing-number citation cover the scalar values and are explicitly treated as prior. The finite-abelian intersection-graph literature studies graph isomorphism and structural properties, not zero forcing enumeration. The zero forcing polynomial literature defines the invariant and treats several standard families. Searches under the subgroup-intersection, pineapple, parameter, factorized-polynomial, and coefficient-sequence formulations did not locate the complete polynomial or the all-set characterization. The main residual risk is an unindexed source treating pineapples under another name.

## Value

PASS. \(C_{p^2}\times C_p\) is the smallest noncyclic, non-elementary finite abelian \(p\)-group family, and its subgroup lattice naturally mixes a projective line of order-\(p\) subgroups with cyclic order-\(p^2\) overgroups. The full zero forcing enumerator records more than the already-known scalar value: it gives every forcing-set count and all minimum sets in closed form, providing a clean benchmark for the substantially harder subgroup-intersection graphs of larger finite abelian \(p\)-groups.
