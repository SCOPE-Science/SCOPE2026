# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The published blow-up theorem gives six independent false-twin classes with sizes
\[
\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,
\lambda_2\lambda_3,\lambda_1\lambda_3,\lambda_1\lambda_2
\]
and quotient \(B_3\). Two white vertices in one class form an immediate fort. Conversely, if at least one local factor is not a field, every profile with at most one white vertex in each class forces; the proof explicitly handles the only difficult boundary where two local factors are fields. This gives the factorized polynomial and every minimum set. The quotient adjacency matrix has determinant \(-1\), so its blow-up gives a rank-six witness; the zero-forcing lower bound makes the rank exact. Root magnitudes recover the ordered two smallest local ideal counts and the cube root of their total product recovers the third.

The packaged verifier reconstructs actual prime-power chain-ring ideal graphs, exhaustively checks three boundary regimes, checks all \(64\) white-class profiles in the least-redundant case, and verifies exact rational ranks.

## Originality

PASS for the final strengthened claim. The classical co-maximal papers supply the graph and its Boolean blow-up structure but do not study zero forcing. A 2026 modular-compression theorem gives a generic type-profile formula for zero forcing polynomials, so the false-twin compression mechanism itself is broader prior coverage and is not claimed as new. No searched source states that the zero forcing polynomial of a three-local co-maximal ideal graph recovers the three local proper-ideal counts, determines the graph within this class, or has exact real minimum rank six.

The closest internal result on the same graph family concerns total and paired domination, not zero forcing, minimum rank, or polynomial reconstruction. Searches under Boolean blow-up, co-maximal ideal graph, zero forcing polynomial, minimum rank, and parameter recovery formulations did not locate an implication of the final reconstruction theorem.

## Value

PASS. The finding turns a general graph polynomial into a complete invariant on a natural algebraic graph class and gives an explicit inverse map from polynomial roots to local ideal counts. For products of chain rings this becomes recovery of the nilpotency-length multiset, a standard structural datum. The result also proves a uniform rank equality and classifies every minimum forcing set, rather than merely evaluating one numerical invariant.
