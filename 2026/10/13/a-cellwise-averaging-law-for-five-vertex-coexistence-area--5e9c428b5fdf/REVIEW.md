# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source assumptions imply \(a_i=\alpha_i^2<c_j=\beta_j^{-2}\) for every pair. The published boundary has \(s(R)\) strictly decreasing from \(1\) to \(0\), and the identity for \(1-s-t\) proves that it lies below \(s+t=1\). The area integral is therefore oriented correctly. Expanding \(-s'(R)\) and \(t(R)\) separates the integral into pair terms, and the rescaling \(R=a_i x\) leaves only \(q_{ij}=a_i/c_j\). Direct partial fractions give the stated \(I(q)\), hence the area formula. The argument covers arbitrary finite periods \(m_1,m_2\) under the stated strict small-\(r\) hypothesis.

## Originality
PASS. Kenyon--Prause Section 3.4 gives the higher-period coexistence boundary but not its area or a pairwise averaging identity. Their simply-periodic predecessor gives the one-cell hyperbolic boundary but likewise does not state the higher-period averaging law. Aggarwal treats pure-state existence for a broader ferroelectric six-vertex setting but has no staggered cellwise area decomposition. Targeted database searches for the source identifier, coexistence-area terminology, the exact \(q\log q\) expression, and cellwise averaging returned no covering result.

## Value
PASS. The source emphasizes that higher periods create richer phase diagrams and more intricate boundaries. The new identity extracts a natural global phase-size invariant from that geometry and shows an unexpected exact additivity: the full higher-period coexistence area is determined solely by the arithmetic mean of one-cell contributions. This allows immediate comparison across periodic environments without reconstructing or numerically integrating each phase boundary.
