# Scientific review: a codimension-one barrier to photon-coincidence suppression

## Correctness — PASS

The claim distinguishes (a) the exact rank-\(N\) and rank-\(N-1\) qualitative regimes, (b) existence of a zero-producing interferometer when two photons are identical up to phase, and (c) a separate quantitative inequality requiring full-support dependence. The proof derives the one-photon-per-output amplitude \(\Psi_U=\sum_\sigma a_\sigma P_\sigma V\) without a missing factorial and uses \(\det U\ne0\) to ensure a nonzero path. An exact codimension-one expansion into independent repeated-label tensor sectors forces sign reversal under specified star transpositions. Any two distinct star generators create a three-column minor whose all-six signed permutation monomials violate the row-ratio conditions. This establishes positivity for all dimensions, not just tested ones. At full support, the kernel is exactly the sign line; projection of the unitary path vector and the established Egorychev–Falikman permanent inequality give a strictly positive bound. The identity \((N!)^2>N^N\) for every \(N\ge3\) is proved directly. The balanced beamsplitter is an exact boundary witness. Independent finite calculations corroborate but do not prove these infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The closest primary comparison is Shchesnovich, arXiv:1410.1506, Section III.C: it defines the general permutation-Gram probability and explicitly proves the \(N=3\), two-dimensional internal-span impossibility, then states a much wider zero-output conjecture. A proved three-photon case does **not** imply the present arbitrary-\(N\) codimension-one classification: the proof requires the multiset-sector decomposition and a two-generator three-column argument for arbitrary \(N\). The general source conjecture is not a proof. Its formalism supplies premises rather than the new statement. Shchesnovich, arXiv:1712.03191, gives general photon-counting generating functions but neither the specific rank-\(N-1\) no-zero theorem nor the all-unitary Gram spectral gap. Crespi, arXiv:1502.06372, gives exact suppression conditions in a restricted Sylvester-network family with different particle assumptions; such a restricted-network theorem cannot imply positivity for every unitary with dependent but pairwise nonproportional internal states. Gurvits, arXiv:math/0504397, is explicitly credited for the permanent bound. Additional indexed and meaning-based comparisons did not identify equivalent or dominating coverage. An equivalent result in unindexed multiphoton interferometry or a later specialization of general representation-theoretic machinery remains a genuine residual risk; searches are not novelty certificates.

## Value — PASS

Exact zero probability is the sharp operational signature in the motivating photon-suppression question. A one-dimensional deficit of internal-state rank is the first singular departure from linearly independent photon labels, and the theorem identifies a complete boundary there rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. It extends a meaningful three-photon observation to every photon number and provides a positive, interferometer-independent quantitative certificate for the canonical full-support dependence class. The sign-representation kernel connects internal linear dependence with path-interference cancellation; the strict permanent gap then proves robustness against any choice of a lossless interferometer. This gives a practically interpretable obstruction to claiming perfect destructive interference from merely linearly dependent spectra.

## Closest literature and limits

The closest source is Shchesnovich (2014), arXiv:1410.1506, Section III.C, equations (80)–(83). Related general counting methods occur in Shchesnovich (2017), arXiv:1712.03191. Crespi (2015), arXiv:1502.06372, treats symmetry-based suppression in Sylvester devices. The theorem is only about independent pure single-photon inputs and the full collision-free pattern, with ideal detectors and an exactly unitary square interferometer. It makes no claim for lower-rank spans, mixed or entangled inputs, other output events, or uniform gaps across changing photon states. The spectral lower bound may not be optimal and independent priority validation has not occurred.
