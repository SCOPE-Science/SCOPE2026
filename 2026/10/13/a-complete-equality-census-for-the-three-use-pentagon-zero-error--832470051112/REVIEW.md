# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The defining confusability relation was reconstructed directly. Exhaustive search found \(31{,}328\) size-\(10\) codes through a fixed word and no size-\(11\) code through that word; vertex transitivity then gives exactly \(391{,}600\) labeled maxima. Every enumerated maximum was rechecked pairwise. A separate natural-group pass produced \(85\) canonical classes and recomputed every stabilizer, whose orbit sizes sum independently to \(391{,}600\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest inspected primary literature proves \(\alpha(C_5^{\boxtimes 3})=10\) or classifies different odd-cycle-power families, but does not state the labeled equality count, the fixed-word count, the \(85\) natural-group orbits, or the stabilizer distribution. Exact-count, alias, parameter, and implication searches found no equivalent published statement. The 1974 Sonnemann--Krafft full text remained inaccessible after bounded lawful attempts, so it is an explicit residual risk rather than negative evidence.

## Value
PASS. The pentagon channel is a foundational zero-error example, and equality structure for its third strong power is a natural structural question. The census shows that the classical optimum \(10\) has a surprisingly rich equality space rather than a single canonical construction; the exact orbit table is a reusable benchmark for extremal and symmetry-based investigations.

## Closest literature and limitations
Baumert et al. supply the original exact value and an exhaustive exclusion of sizes \(11\) and \(12\), while Badalyan--Mnatsakanyan give a later proof. Bohman--Holzman--Natarajan classify different power families. This result is limited to three uses and to the stated natural channel-isometry group; it does not claim a new Shannon-capacity value or a classification for arbitrary powers.
