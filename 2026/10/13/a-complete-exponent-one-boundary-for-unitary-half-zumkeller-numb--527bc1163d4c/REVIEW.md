# Scientific review: exponent-one unitary half-Zumkeller boundary

**Correctness (PASS).** Each number has exactly seven proper unitary divisors, producing the complete signed equation \(N+CD=0\). The inequalities separately cover \(a=1,b\ge2\), \(b=1,a\ge3\), and \((a,b)=(2,1)\), while the shared \((1,1)\) branch has an explicit partition for every \(q^c\). Each of the six remaining cases has an explicit balanced partition. The unitary-Zumkeller distinction follows from exact unitary divisor sums. No finite test is substituted for an infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS relative to inspected literature).** The 2024 primary article gives the definitions, generic subset-sum condition, and examples but not this full classification. OEIS A290467 is a finite table of membership, not an all-parameter nonexistence theorem. The closest published mathematical result classifies the different *full* unitary-Zumkeller condition: it contains \(6q^c,60,90\) but not the four half-only cases. Targeted searches for this exact family, equivalent signed-sum formulations, and a dominating three-support classification found no equivalent theorem; an unindexed result remains a risk.

**Value (PASS).** This is a natural first boundary for the genuinely richer proper-unitary-divisor problem on three prime supports. It gives a complete classification for both exponent-one faces, proves uniform exclusion beyond finite thresholds, and isolates four known small examples as the **only** additional half-only members throughout two infinite parameter families. The value is the completeness and full-versus-half distinction, not novelty of the individual listed integers.

**Limits.** The case in which both exponents of \(2\) and \(3\) exceed one is open here; no priority is asserted over unindexed sources. The included finite tests supplement the symbolic proof.
