# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The tangent exact sequence and Gauss--Bonnet give
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)
=
\left(\prod_i d_i\right)
[t^n]\frac{(1+t)^{n+c+1}}{\prod_i(1+d_it)}.
\]
If the total degree is divisible by \(4\), the conclusion is immediate. With one even degree congruent to \(2\) modulo \(4\), reduction modulo \(2\) leaves \(\binom{n+2}{2}\). With all degrees odd, replacing them by their signs modulo \(4\) gives
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)
\equiv
(-1)^f(n+1+2fn)
\pmod4.
\]
These cases exhaust all multidegrees and prove the stated parity and exact-\(\nu_2=1\) criteria.

The bundled checker independently reconstructs the Chern-series coefficient over a broad finite grid. Finite computation is regression evidence only.

## Originality
PASS. The archival characteristic-class source supplies the complete-intersection virtual tangent framework but contains no matching mod-four, parity, two-adic, or divisibility statement in targeted full-text searches. A modern full-text source on quadratic Euler characteristics of same-degree complete intersections likewise does not state the result. Database searches found nearby complete-intersection arithmetic for dual degrees and other enumerative invariants, not the ordinary Euler characteristic.

The principal residual risk is older literature devoted directly to Euler characteristics whose full text was not searchable during this comparison.

## Value
PASS. Euler characteristic is the basic integer controlling the middle-cohomology size of a smooth projective complete intersection once Lefschetz fixes the remaining cohomology. The theorem gives its complete residue modulo \(4\) for arbitrary multidegree and a sharp first two-adic stratification with simple geometric conditions.
