# A complete ordinary-multiple-point census for a twelve-degree Galois-point curve

## Finding
Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_{11}\). Define three binary forms of degree twelve by homogenizing to degree twelve the following polynomials with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_{11}\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A(t)&=t^{12}+t^{10}-t^9+5t^8-2t^7-4t^5-2t^4+3t^3+5t^2-2,\\
 B(t)&=t^{12}-2t-1,\\
 D(t)&=t^{11}-t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Denote the corresponding homogeneous forms by \(A_h(S,T)=T^{12}A(S/T)\), \(B_h(S,T)=T^{12}B(S/T)\) and \(D_h(S,T)=T^{12}D(S/T)\). Let \(C\subset\mathbb P^2_k\) be the image of
\[
 \phi:\mathbb P^1_k\longrightarrow\mathbb P^2_k,
 \quad [S:T]\longmapsto[A_h(S,T):B_h(S,T):D_h(S,T)].
\]
Then \(C\) is an integral rational curve of degree twelve. The two points \([1:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0]\) are outer Galois points, with respective Galois groups cyclic of order twelve and isomorphic to \(A_4\). The **entire geometric singularity basket** is
\[
 \boxed{25\text{ ordinary double points}+6\text{ ordinary triple points}+2\text{ ordinary quadruple points}.}
\]
Every singular point is defined over \(\mathbb F_{121}\). Exactly five are \(\mathbb F_{11}\)-rational, and all five are nodes: four are \([2:1:0],[4:1:0],[6:1:0],[8:1:0]\), and the fifth is \([1:1:1]\). The remaining twenty nodes, six triple points and two quadruple points form respectively ten, three and one Frobenius-conjugate pairs. As an additional consequence, \(\#C(\mathbb F_{11})=9\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_{11}\); all ordinary-multiple-point assertions are geometric, not merely assertions about rational branches. The curve is the **specific trace-coordinate model** above. Its construction is associated with the characteristic-eleven \(A_4\)-versus-cyclic group pair used by Fukasawa and Waki [1]. No assertion is made that every plane model attached to the same pair has this basket.

Use the standard fractional-linear convention: a matrix \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) acts on \(t\in\mathbb P^1\) by \(t\mapsto(at+b)/(ct+d)\). Let \(G\) be generated in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_{11})\) by
\[
 \sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \eta=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\1&4\end{pmatrix}
\]
and let \(H\) be generated by \(\xi=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). The displayed binary forms are the trace-coordinate forms of these groups. All integers in polynomial and matrix entries are understood modulo eleven.

## Proof
**1. Group actions and quotient functions.** Exact projective matrix multiplication gives \(|G|=|H|=12\), \(G\cap H=\{1\}\), \(G\cong A_4\) and \(H\cong C_{12}\). Indeed, the generators \(\sigma,\tau\) generate a Klein four-group and \(\eta\) has order three; the generated group has order twelve. The cyclic group generated by \(\xi\) has order twelve. Both groups act regularly on \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{11})\); the exact enumeration is reproduced by the attached verifier.

Write \(f_G(t)=\sum_{g\in G}g(t)\) and \(f_H(t)=\sum_{h\in H}h(t)\) as sums of rational functions. Each is invariant under its corresponding group. Since the action on the twelve rational points is regular, each summand has one distinct simple pole and together the pole divisors are exactly \(\sum_{P\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{11})}P\). Thus the quotient functions each have degree twelve. Exact division of \(D(t)=t^{11}-t\) by the linear denominators gives \(f_G=A/D\) and \(f_H=B/D\), using the stated coefficients. Consequently \(k(f_G)=k(t)^G\) and \(k(f_H)=k(t)^H\).

The composite field \(k(f_G,f_H)\) is \(k(t)^{G\cap H}=k(t)\), by finite Galois correspondence. Therefore \(\phi\) is birational onto its image. The homogeneous forms have no common zero: at any finite zero of \(D\), both \(A\) and \(B\) are nonzero, because each quotient function has an actual simple pole; at \([1:0]\) both \(A_h\) and \(B_h\) have leading coefficient one. Hence \(\phi\) is a morphism and, because the pullback of a line has degree twelve and \(\phi\) is birational, \(\deg C=12\). Projecting from \([1:0:0]\), respectively \([0:1:0]\), recovers \(f_H\), respectively \(f_G\), so these are outer Galois points with the claimed groups. They are off \(C\) because the shared pole set has nonvanishing \(A_h,B_h\).

**2. Complete exact arithmetic census and transversality.** Put \(K=\mathbb F_{11}[u]/(u^2-2)\). Since two is nonsquare modulo eleven, \(K=\mathbb F_{121}\). Enumerate all 122 parameters in \(\mathbb P^1(K)\). For finite \(t\), if \(D(t)\ne0\), use the image coordinates \((A(t)/D(t),B(t)/D(t))\); at a zero of \(D\), use the infinity chart \((A(t)/B(t),D(t)/B(t))\). At the source point \(t=\infty\), the target is \([1:1:0]\). Grouping equal image coordinates yields **exactly** twenty-five fibers of size two, six of size three and two of size four; all other fibers in this finite enumeration are singletons.

The attached standalone verifier recomputes the group matrices, both trace numerators, every image, and all tangents using only exact \(\mathbb F_{11}\) and \(K\) arithmetic. For any two distinct parameter values in the same multiple fiber, the tangent determinant is nonzero. In the finite affine target chart, the tangent vector is
\[
 \left(\frac{A'D-AD'}{D^2},\frac{B'D-BD'}{D^2}\right);
\]
in the chart at infinity with \(B\ne0\), it is
\[
 \left(\frac{A'B-AB'}{B^2},\frac{D'B-DB'}{B^2}\right).
\]
At the source point \(\infty\), these derivatives are taken with source coordinate \(w=1/t\) and the homogeneous forms; the tangent vector is \((0,1)\). Each vector at a multiple fiber is nonzero, and all pairs are linearly independent. Therefore the displayed collisions are ordinary double, triple or quadruple points.

**3. Exhaustiveness over the algebraic closure.** The arithmetic genus of an integral degree-twelve plane curve is \((12-1)(12-2)/2=55\). Its normalization is \(\mathbb P^1\), so its geometric genus is zero and the sum of all local delta invariants equals 55. An ordinary \(r\)-fold point contributes \(\binom r2\). The exact finite-field census alone contributes
\[
 25\binom22+6\binom32+2\binom42=25+18+12=55.
\]
Since every singularity contributes a positive delta invariant, no other geometric singularity or additional branch of a listed singularity can exist, including over higher extension fields. This is why the finite enumeration proves an assertion over the entire algebraic closure: it **saturates the algebraic genus bound**, rather than extrapolating from a finite sample.

**4. Arithmetic.** Exactly four of the nodes occur on the target line at infinity, at the points stated above, and one further rational node is \([1:1:1]\). All other multiple-fiber images occur in nontrivial conjugate pairs over \(\mathbb F_{11}\), yielding the stated rationality counts. All twelve \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{11})\) source points map to the line at infinity, where four identifications of pairs leave eight distinct rational images. The one rational affine node has two conjugate, nonrational normalization preimages; every smooth rational point must have a rational normalization preimage. Hence \(\#C(\mathbb F_{11})=8+1=9\).

## Relationship to prior work
Fukasawa and Waki [1, Theorem 1(a), Lemmas 1–2, Proposition 1] establish existence of degree-twelve rational plane curves with \(A_4\) and cyclic-order-twelve outer Galois groups in characteristic eleven. They exhibit the group generators and the common regular orbit, but do not classify the singularities of the explicit trace-coordinate model above. The present claim is the exact geometric and arithmetic singularity basket of **one specified embedding**, not the existence of the Galois groups.

Fukasawa and Waki [2] construct different explicit plane models with cyclic and dihedral Galois groups. The general results of Fukasawa and Higashine [3] concern existence of two-Galois-point embeddings, including quotient curves, and not this singularity census. The classification in Fukasawa and Speziali [4] applies when the two groups form a semidirect product. Here \(\langle G,H\rangle\cong\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_{11})\) has order 1320, not the order 144 of a semidirect product of groups of order twelve, so that theorem does not imply this result.

## Limitations
The complete singularity classification applies to this explicitly normalized trace model. Different choices of invariant quotient coordinates can lead to different plane models. The result does not classify all embeddings associated with these Galois groups, nor does it determine the full projective automorphism group. Exact finite-field computations provide exhaustive certificates because of the genus-bound saturation; absent that argument they would not prove the geometric completeness statement. Priority remains subject to any unindexed earlier calculations.
