# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact first-moment identity fixes the sole candidate hopping coefficient. The second-moment identity saturates a weighted Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; this forces the marked Laplacian vector into the span of the stationary initial vector and target. Explicit coordinate comparison gives precisely constant entrance from every nonmarked state. Conversely that equality closes a two-dimensional invariant subspace and gives all success times, including necessity. The argument covers arbitrary finite irreducible reversible chains with optional self-loops. The standalone rational verifier checks 41,592 small cases and two weighted examples, but the proof does not rely on an exhaustive finite test.

## Originality — PASS
Mograby–Balu–Okoudjou–Teplyaev develop the weighted quantum-search setting and the complete-graph example without the all-reversible-chain iff condition. Ide–Narimatsu's equitable quotient result concerns adjacency quantum search and its distinct initial-state assumptions. Chakraborty–Novo–Roland analyze asymptotic optimality rather than exact finite-time probability-one attainment. Previously known unweighted cone-over-regular-graph characterizations are a proper special case: the weighted cone over a path in the finding realizes exact transfer despite a nonregular base. An alternate-indexed equivalent reversible-chain theorem remains possible, and novelty is not inferred merely from failed searches.

## Value — PASS
The result gives a complete universal structural criterion and the unique tuning/entire exact-time set in a natural weighted quantum-search model. Its conditional-equilibrium interpretation suggests explicit choice of reversible edge conductances to enable search, while examples also show how weights on a fixed topology can prevent it. The result does not supply approximate robustness estimates or generalize to nonreversible chains.
