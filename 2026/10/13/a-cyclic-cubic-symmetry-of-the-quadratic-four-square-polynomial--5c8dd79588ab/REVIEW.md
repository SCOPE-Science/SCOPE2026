# Review: A cyclic cubic symmetry of the quadratic four-square polynomial triple

## Correctness
PASS. The source supplies the exact normal form, the irreducibility of \(G\), the reality of all three roots, and uniqueness of the parameter for a distinguished entry. Exact polynomial arithmetic verifies \(G(\tau(T))=512G(T)/(67T-36)^3\), \(\tau^3(T)=T\), and the two entry-renormalization identities. The field-discriminant argument constructs an explicit integral element giving a rank-three integral lattice of discriminant \(91^2\), then uses independent Eisenstein tests at \(7\) and \(13\) to force the full tame discriminant contribution. No finite numerical experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2610.05565v1, already observes that the three roots correspond to the three distinguished entries and records a square polynomial discriminant. Those statements cover the existence of three equivalent normalizations and, together with classical cubic theory, essentially cover abstract cyclicity. They do not provide the explicit fractional-linear Galois generator, identify its orientation on the entries, or determine the maximal field discriminant. Searches in the published published-finding corpus corpus for four-square triples, cubic Galois symmetry, root permutation, and cyclic parameter fields returned no statement implying this combined claim.

## Value
PASS. The source's three-root ambiguity is structural rather than incidental: the explicit Galois generator shows that the three normalizations are one arithmetic orbit, and the exact discriminant identifies the arithmetic field carrying that orbit. This removes the need to treat the three roots as unrelated real parameters and gives a reusable exact symmetry for subsequent arithmetic study of the unique quadratic triple.

The proof does not classify higher-degree four-square triples and does not claim a new general criterion for cyclic cubic fields.
