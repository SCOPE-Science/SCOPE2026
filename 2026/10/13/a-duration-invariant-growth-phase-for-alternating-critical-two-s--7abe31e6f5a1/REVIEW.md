# Scientific review: duration-invariant critical two-stage growth phase

## Correctness — PASS
The isolated matrix has characteristic polynomial \((\lambda-1)(\lambda+jf)\). Cayley–Hamilton supplies an exact formula for every positive integer matrix power. A trace–determinant calculation reduces \(\det(I-A^mB^n)\) to a positive scalar multiple of \((j_A-j_B)(f_A-f_B)\). Strict positivity and Perron–Frobenius control the nonprincipal eigenvalue, so the determinant sign gives the exact growth phase. The convex-average and rank-one long-spell identities have separate derivations. The standalone exact rational checker reproduces the finite tests. Risk: these hypotheses are essential; no stochastic or general-product conclusion is justified.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest inspected papers are Caswell–Trevisan (1994), Cushing (2006), Cushing–Ackleh (2011), and Caswell (2019). Their studied classes cover general periodic matrix products, reproduction-number formulas, and phase-sensitive demographic bifurcations, but the inspected portions did not give the specialized uniform-all-spell-length, individually-neutral two-stage sign classification. The Cushing–Ackleh period-two formulas provide a natural route to a fixed-duration special case, not the explicit invariant sign for every positive pair of block lengths. Caswell–Trevisan (1994) was accessible only by abstract; stronger unlocated prior coverage remains possible. A generic spectral criterion for arbitrary matrices is not equated to an explicit uniform parameter phase law.

## Value — PASS
Contrary to applying isolated environmental growth factors, which are all exactly one in this setting, the switching prognosis can be supercritical or subcritical. The classification isolates a measurable, ecologically motivated survival–fertility tradeoff and proves that changing the duration of either homogeneous season cannot reverse its sign. This is a precise population-viability benchmark rather than a generic small perturbation or a simple numerical example. Limits: only a stylized two-stage deterministic population is addressed.
