# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

On squarefree three-prime products, \(\lambda(pqr)\) is the least common multiple of the three shifted primes. Writing \(d=(pq+1)/(r-1)\) is justified by the final-prime congruence, and \(r>q\) forces \(1\le d<p\). The middle-prime congruence gives the exact factorization \(q-1\mid(p+1)(p+d)\), whence the explicit upper bound. The converse preserves both residual divisibilities; it does not incorrectly invert \(d\) modulo \(q-1\). The direct and parametric finite enumerations agree for both stated fibers. No computational extrapolation is used for the general bound.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk

McNew 2012 studies the minus-shift equations \(\lambda(n)\mid n-1\) and \(\operatorname{rad}(\varphi(n))\mid n-1\), not the positive-shift equation. Zheng 2022 defines the present \((1,-1)\) terminology but proves infinitude for the distinct \((-1,1)\) case \(p+1\mid n-1\). Exact-condition and implication-sensitive searches, including related published mathematical records, found no covering statement of the divisor bound or complete least-prime fibers. Such negative searches are not proof of worldwide novelty; an unindexed elementary proof remains a risk.

## Value — PASS

The new result provides an effective finite divisor classification for **every fixed smallest prime** in a natural mixed-sign Korselt equation. This explains the finite nature of each three-prime fiber without an arbitrary size cutoff and resolves the first two smallest-prime fibers exactly. Those explicit local classifications are useful reference data for further questions on prime-support structure, even without proving any global asymptotic theorem.
