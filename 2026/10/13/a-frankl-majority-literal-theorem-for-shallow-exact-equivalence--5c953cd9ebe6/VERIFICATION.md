# Verification

The primary modal source was checked at the publisher's open-access full text. Its verified metadata gives publication online on 06 May 2026 and primary MSC 03B45. The proof uses its definitions of state descriptions, closure, canonical representatives and canonical representations, together with its canonical-model and completeness lemmas.

For a finite vocabulary of \(q\) variables, the canonical representative counts were recomputed directly. Degree \(0\) has \(R_0=2q\) representatives. Degree \(1\) adds two signed modal representatives for each nonempty subset of \(C_0\), giving \(R_1=R_0+2(2^{R_0}-1)\). For \(n\ge1\), only state-description representations using at least one degree-\(n\) representative produce genuinely new degree-\(n+1\) literals, giving \(R_{n+1}=R_n+2(2^{R_n}-2^{R_{n-1}})\). Thus one variable gives \(R_0=2\), \(R_1=8\), \(R_2=512\), while two variables already give \(R_1=34\).

For a normal form, closure adjoins conjunctions of every nonempty selection of original disjuncts. Canonical representation sends such conjunctions to unions, so the distinct closure representations form exactly the union closure generated by the original disjunct representations. Conversely, choosing one state description for each member of a nonempty union-closed family realizes that family. The source's canonical-model lemma distinguishes unequal representation families, and equal state-description representations are provably equivalent; therefore the family is an exact-equivalence invariant.

The external combinatorial premise was checked in Vučković--Živković's published primary article: Frankl's conjecture is proved for union-closed families on universes of size at most \(12\). The underlying computer-assisted exhaustive computation was not independently replayed.
