# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved by conditioning on a specified degree-one vertex and observing that every motif with minimum degree two must lie in the complementary \(n-1\)-vertex graph. Exact fixed-edge factorial moments and the conditional \(G(n-1,m-1)\) law give the covariance. All finite boundary cases, the feasibility range, the nonpositive-ratio branch, the real cutoff, the triangle integer cutoff, and the fixed-motif sparse asymptotics were reconstructed. Independently enumerating host graphs for seven motifs and using exact rational threshold tests supports, but does not replace, the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

The inspected Goldstein article [1] concerns degree-count marginal behavior in the independent-edge model. The inspected Stark–Wormald article [2] supplies the general fixed-edge motif expectation and subgraph-count clusters, not the degree-one/motif mixed covariance and its sign classification. General graph-model conditioning may make parts of the formula obtainable from known factorial-moment machinery; the exact sign classification and model contrast were not identified in these full texts or the additional targeted searches. Novelty relative to the full historical literature is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

Degree-one vertices quantify pendant structure, whereas triangles, cycles, and other degree-at-least-two motifs quantify local cyclic density. Conditioning on total edge count couples these otherwise distinct statistics. The exact threshold exhibits a quantitative sign reversal absent in the binomial model; for triangles its integer location is unexpectedly precise, and for all motifs the sign depends only on two basic structural parameters. This is a natural complete classification rather than an arbitrary numerical example.

## Limits and nearest prior literature

The older motif-count expectation in [2] is used as prior input. Prior binomial-model degree/subgraph covariance identities are not a fixed-edge result. Possible implicit derivations through classical mixed random-graph moment formulas remain an originality risk; no independent priority search or audit has certified uniqueness. The restrictions \(n\ge h+1\), non-induced copies, and minimum motif degree two are essential.
