# Scientific review: fixed-order three-power zero sums

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof converts an arbitrary modular three-term zero sum at order \(n\) into a trinomial and a nonzero integral resultant when \(3\nmid n\). The complex-unit-vector argument shows the resultant cannot vanish, and the product formula yields the uniform \(3^n\) bound. The infinite construction proves the cyclotomic factor divides the three-term polynomial, establishes exact order with finitely many nonzero resultants, and separately rules out shorter sums for odd and even orders. The packaged exact-arithmetic checker exercises representative finite cases; the symbolic argument proves the unbounded quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources.** Jacob–Külshammer's question about \(m(q,e)=3\) motivates the problem, but its Proposition 11 imposes divisibility by a cyclotomic value and a length threshold. Breuer–Héthelyi–Horváth–Külshammer (2021) treats prime orders or prime-power moduli in the relevant results. Lam–Leung's complex-root weight classification proves a different statement; it does not assert a bound or a finite/infinite dichotomy for actual modular zero sums. A prior specialized order-four classification implies some cases of the finite half but not the theorem for all orders. No exact equivalent was found in the targeted mathematical-record searches. An unindexed equivalent remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** Prescribed multiplicative order is the organizing parameter in a published problem asking when three powers suffice. A complete all-order finite/infinite threshold with an explicit modulus bound, including an infinite family for even orders requiring composite moduli, is a natural structural result, not a single numerical example or an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Limitations.** The theorem neither lists all moduli below the bound nor makes a sharpness claim for the bound. The constructive threshold depends on finitely many nonzero resultants. Bibliographic completeness and independent proof audit are open.
