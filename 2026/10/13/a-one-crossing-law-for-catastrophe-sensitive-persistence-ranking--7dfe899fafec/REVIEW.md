## Originality — PASS, qualified

Closest relevant sources: Lande (1993) gives extinction-time analysis under random catastrophes for a single density-dependent population; Etienne and Heesterbeek (2001) optimize colonization/extinction interventions for a metapopulation without an accessible statement of the present regional-shock comparison; Johst and Drechsler (2003) analyze disturbance spatial correlation in a different spatial model; Drake and Griffen (2013) study experimental source–sink extinction. Their actual statements, not just titles, were compared. The new claim classifies *all* positive two-patch designs with two matched total rates, with at most one catastrophe-intensity ranking reversal and a sharp rational-rate example. The known finite-state first-step method is acknowledged as prior technique. Incomplete access to two closely related historical papers limits the exclusion of hidden equivalent earlier formulas; novelty is assessed rather than independently certified.

## Value — PASS

Conservation comparisons often hold a total intervention budget constant while reallocating protection and recolonization among habitat patches. The theorem shows that such designs cannot be ranked uniformly from the two sums alone: their relative mean persistence can change with the severity rate of shared regional shocks. At the same time, the exact one-crossing bound prevents arbitrary repeated reversals, and gives a necessary-and-sufficient condition and a feasible witness. This is a structural comparison in a natural finite model, not a renamed elementary identity or a negligible numerical improvement. It does not claim generality for multi-patch networks, correlated partial shocks, empirical populations, or nonlinear population dynamics.
