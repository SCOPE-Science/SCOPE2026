# A rotation profile and limiting law for lower-plus-upper Wythoff representations
## Finding
Let \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), let
\[
u(h)=\lfloor h\varphi\rfloor,\qquad v(k)=\lfloor k\varphi^2\rfloor,
\]
and define
\[
r(n)=\#\{(h,k)\in\mathbb N^2:u(h)+v(k)=n\}\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Set
\[
\alpha=\varphi^{-1},\qquad \beta=\varphi^{-2}=1-\alpha,\qquad \theta_n=\{n\alpha\}.
\]
Define the continuous \(1\)-periodic function
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=
\begin{cases}
\beta-\theta,&0\le\theta<\beta,\\
\theta-\beta,&\beta\le\theta<2\beta,\\
\beta,&2\beta\le\theta<1.
\end{cases}
\]
Then uniformly for \(n\ge2\),
\[
r(n)=n\Lambda(\theta_n)+O(\log n).
\]
Thus the large fluctuations of the representation count are governed, to relative error \(o(1)\), by one golden-rotation phase.

More precisely, the empirical distributions of \(r(n)/n\) converge weakly to
\[
\mu=2\,\mathbf 1_{[0,\beta]}(y)\,dy+(1-2\beta)\,\delta_\beta.
\]
The continuous part has density \(2\) on \([0,\beta]\), while a point mass \(1-2\beta=\sqrt5-2\) sits at the maximal profile value \(\beta\). In particular,
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}\frac{r(n)}n\longrightarrow
\int_0^1\Lambda(t)\,dt
=\beta-\beta^2
=\varphi^{-3}
=\sqrt5-2,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{n\le N}r(n)=\frac{\sqrt5-2}{2}N^2+O(N\log N).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The variables \(h,k,n\) are positive integers. The representation count is ordered by the two different summand families: \(h\) indexes the lower Wythoff sequence and \(k\) indexes the upper Wythoff sequence. Since both sequences are strictly increasing, a decomposition \(n=m+(n-m)\) with the first summand lower Wythoff and the second upper Wythoff corresponds to exactly one pair \((h,k)\).

The \(O(\log n)\) constant is absolute. No effective best constant is asserted. The weak limit concerns the uniform empirical measure on indices \(1\le n\le N\).

## Proof
For an integer \(m\ge1\), the number of lower Wythoff terms not exceeding \(m\) is
\[
\#\{h:\lfloor h\varphi\rfloor\le m\}
=\left\lfloor\frac{m+1}\varphi\right\rfloor.
\]
Hence the indicator \(A(m)\) of the lower Wythoff set is
\[
A(m)=\lfloor(m+1)\alpha\rfloor-\lfloor m\alpha\rfloor.
\]
Because \(0<\alpha<1\), this is \(1\) exactly when
\[
\{m\alpha\}\ge1-\alpha=\beta.
\]
The lower and upper Wythoff sets partition the positive integers, so the upper indicator is \(B(m)=1-A(m)\), equivalently
\[
B(m)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{m\alpha\}<\beta.
\]

For \(n\ge2\),
\[
r(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}A(m)B(n-m).
\]
Write \(y=\{m\alpha\}\) and \(\theta=\theta_n=\{n\alpha\}\). The two indicator conditions become
\[
y\in[\beta,1),\qquad \{\theta-y\}<\beta.
\]
Thus \(r(n)\) is the number of points \(\{m\alpha\}\), \(1\le m\le n-1\), in
\[
K_\theta=[\beta,1)\cap\{y\in[0,1):\{\theta-y\}<\beta\}.
\]
A direct circle-interval calculation gives
\[
|K_\theta|=
\begin{cases}
\beta-\theta,&0\le\theta<\beta,\\
\theta-\beta,&\beta\le\theta<2\beta,\\
\beta,&2\beta\le\theta<1,
\end{cases}
=\Lambda(\theta).
\]
At the two breakpoints no orbit ambiguity occurs, because equality would force a nonzero integer multiple of the irrational \(\alpha\) to be integral.

It remains to control the discrepancy of the golden rotation. The continued fraction of \(\alpha\) has all partial quotients equal to \(1\), so its convergent denominators are Fibonacci numbers. For a convergent \(p/q\), the \(q\)-point orbit block
\[
\{\,\{x+j\alpha\}:0\le j<q\,\}
\]
has interval-count discrepancy bounded by an absolute constant, uniformly in \(x\): compare it with the translated \(q\)-grid generated by \(p/q\). The rational grid has interval discrepancy at most \(2\), and the convergent bound moves each grid point by less than one grid spacing, so only a bounded number of points near the two interval endpoints can change membership. Every \(N\) is a sum of nonconsecutive Fibonacci denominators, with \(O(\log N)\) summands. Splitting the first \(N\) orbit points into the corresponding translated convergent blocks therefore yields, uniformly over intervals \(I\subset[0,1)\),
\[
\#\{1\le m\le N:\{m\alpha\}\in I\}=N|I|+O(\log(N+2)).
\]
Applying this with \(N=n-1\) and \(I=K_{\theta_n}\) gives
\[
r(n)=(n-1)\Lambda(\theta_n)+O(\log n)
=n\Lambda(\theta_n)+O(\log n).
\]

Since \(\alpha\) is irrational, \(\theta_n=\{n\alpha\}\) is equidistributed on the circle. The preceding uniform error is \(o(n)\), and \(\Lambda\) is continuous on the circle. Therefore the empirical distribution of \(r(n)/n\) is the pushforward of Lebesgue measure by \(\Lambda\). The first two branches each map an interval of length \(\beta\) linearly onto \([0,\beta]\), giving density \(2\), while the plateau \([2\beta,1)\) contributes an atom of mass \(1-2\beta\) at \(\beta\). This proves the displayed weak limit and the mean.

For the summatory formula, the same convergent-block argument gives \(O(\log N)\) discrepancy for Birkhoff sums of the bounded-variation function \(\Lambda-\int_0^1\Lambda\). Abel summation then gives
\[
\sum_{n\le N}n\left(\Lambda(\theta_n)-\int_0^1\Lambda\right)=O(N\log N).
\]
Together with \(\sum_{n\le N}O(\log n)=O(N\log N)\) and
\[
\int_0^1\Lambda(t)\,dt=\beta-\beta^2=\varphi^{-3}=\sqrt5-2,
\]
this yields
\[
\sum_{n\le N}r(n)=\frac{\sqrt5-2}{2}N^2+O(N\log N).
\]

## Verification
The accompanying verifier independently generates the lower and upper Wythoff sets with high-precision arithmetic, forms their exact finite convolution through \(n=5000\), checks the first \(77\) published values of OEIS A259598, and compares the exact counts with the phase profile. These finite checks test the algebra and normalization but are not used to prove the infinite statements.

The proof's only asymptotic input is the reconstructed \(O(\log N)\) interval-discrepancy estimate for the golden rotation, obtained from Fibonacci convergent blocks.

## Relationship to prior work
Ashburn's recent theorem for A259598 proves the complete exceptional classifications \(r(n)=0\), \(r(n)=1\), and \(r(n)=2\). It does not give a general asymptotic formula for \(r(n)\), a normalized limiting distribution, or a summatory main term.

Shallit earlier showed that the lower-plus-upper representation counts admit a finite Fibonacci linear representation, so exact values are algorithmically available. That exact representation is the closest prior coverage: it determines the sequence, but the inspected paper does not extract the fractional-part phase profile, the weak limiting law, or the coefficient \((\sqrt5-2)/2\) in the summatory function. The present argument instead obtains those global features directly from the rotation coding of complementary Beatty sequences.

The OEIS entry A259598 gives the generating function and the exceptional-value conjectures but no comparable asymptotic profile. Earlier Wythoff-sumset work classifies representability and related sumsets rather than this quantitative distribution of representation multiplicities.

## Limitations
The result gives the first-order scale \(n\Lambda(\theta_n)\) with logarithmic error, not an exact closed formula for every \(r(n)\), and it does not optimize the discrepancy constant. The exact Fibonacci linear representation from earlier work may contain finer lower-order information not extracted here. The literature search did not locate another statement of this phase profile or limiting law, but specialized work on Beatty representation functions remains a residual source-risk.
