# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The transport threshold is an exact lattice statement: displacement strictly below \(2/3\) forces nearest-integer rounding. The binomial preimages of rounded values one, two, and three produce a coefficient defect \(a_1a_3-a_2^2\) equal to a positive factored polynomial for every integer \(n\ge2\). Ordinary log-concavity is necessary for Poisson-binomial masses, yielding the lower bound without an asymptotic argument. At \(n=2\), a separately written exact coupling to \(\operatorname{Bin}(4,1/2)\) attains the lower constant. The \(n=1\) boundary is checked using a real-rooted quadratic and an unavoidable lattice distance.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk

Tang and Tang's 2019 preprint sets up this rational-scaling transport question, gives isolated small-size exact values and a floor-rounding obstruction, but the examined Section 4 and Appendix A do not give the all-size nearest-integer coefficient identity or its uniform lower bound. The published 2023 main article delegates that material to a supplement, which could not be accessed; this is a specific, unresolved coverage risk, not evidence of absence. Standard Newton/log-concavity results supply a known necessary condition, but do not imply the new inequality without the explicit rounded-law calculation. A related asymptotic statement allowing error sublinear in sample size would not imply or contradict the claimed fixed positive barrier.

## Value — PASS

The motivating literature asks for a lower bound for Poisson-binomial approximation of a scaled binomial. The theorem settles a sharp, dimension-free lower constant across every multiple-of-three sample size at once. Its numerical value is attained in a natural smallest nontrivial instance, while the general proof identifies an intrinsic exact finite-mass obstruction. The result is meaningful even though the corresponding global upper-bound problem remains open.

## Limits

Only the stated integer family and the uniform lower constant are proved. The checker examines finitely many parameters and is not a proof of the infinite claim. An inaccessible published supplement leaves residual priority risk; no independent assessment has been performed.
