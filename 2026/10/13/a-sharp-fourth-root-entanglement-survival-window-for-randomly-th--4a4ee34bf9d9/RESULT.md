# A sharp fourth-root entanglement-survival window for randomly thinned cycle graph states

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge 10\), prepare the \(n\)-qubit graph state \(|C_n\rangle\) of the unweighted cycle. Independently retain each qubit with probability \(r\in[0,1]\), and trace out the others. Let \(E_n\) denote the event that the reduced state on the retained qubits is **not fully separable** (with the empty subsystem understood to be separable). Let \(F_n\) denote the event that some four cyclically consecutive sites survive. Then for every \(n\ge 10\) and \(r\in[0,1]\),

\[
\Pr(F_n)\le \Pr(E_n)\le\Pr(F_n)+16nr^5.
\]

If \(r=r_n\) and \(n r_n^4\longrightarrow\lambda\in[0,\infty)\), then

\[
\Pr(E_n)\longrightarrow 1-\exp(-\lambda).
\]

More generally, when \(n r_n^4\to\infty\), \(\Pr(E_n)\to1\). Consequently the critical scale is \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/4}\); at \(r_n=c n^{-1/4}\), for each fixed \(c\ge0\), the survival probability tends to \(1-e^{-c^4}\). The claim concerns residual entanglement, not genuine multipartite entanglement, Bell nonlocality, or recoverability of encoded quantum information.

## Assumptions and scope

Write the cycle vertices as \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its commuting graph-state stabilizer generators are

\[
K_i=Z_{i-1}X_iZ_{i+1},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]

For a retained set \(A\), the density operator is \(\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_{A^c}|C_n\rangle\langle C_n|\). It is fully separable if it lies in the convex hull of tensor products of one-qubit density operators on \(A\). Retention events of distinct physical vertices are independent with the same probability \(r\), and lost particles are **traced out**, not individually measured with recorded outcomes.

Define \(H_n\) to mean that some five retained vertices occur in cyclic order with each of their four intervening cyclic gaps belonging to \(\{1,2\}\). The stronger deterministic statement proved below is

\[
F_n\subseteq E_n\subseteq F_n\cup H_n.
\]

## Proof

**1. Four consecutive surviving vertices witness entanglement.** Take \(B=\{i,i+1,i+2,i+3\}\). Since \(n\ge10\), the stabilizer subgroup supported inside these four sites is generated by \(a=Z_iX_{i+1}Z_{i+2}\) and \(b=Z_{i+1}X_{i+2}Z_{i+3}\), with identity operators understood on unspecified sites. Consequently

\[
\rho_B=\frac1{16}(I+a)(I+b).
\]

Partial transpose on the qubit at \(i+1\) leaves \(a\) and \(b\) invariant but reverses the sign of \(ab\). Its operator \(\rho_B^{\Gamma_{i+1}}=(I+a+b-ab)/16\) has the eigenvalue \(-1/8\) on the nonempty common \((-1,-1)\) eigenspace of \((a,b)\). Thus \(\rho_B\) is entangled. Any \(\rho_A\) with \(A\supseteq B\) is therefore not fully separable, because partial trace preserves full separability. This four-site certificate is already explicit in Han–Zhang–Zhang's cycle analysis; the new probabilistic and structural conclusions do not claim that certificate as new.

**2. Stabilizer constraints and separation by two losses.** The full stabilizer group is \(\{\prod_iK_i^{u_i}:u_i\in\mathbb F_2\}\). A product survives the partial trace over \(A^c\) precisely when \(u_j=0\) and \(u_{j-1}+u_{j+1}=0\) for every lost vertex \(j\notin A\). The former equality removes its \(X\) factor, and the latter removes its \(Z\) factor. Therefore the surviving subgroup is determined by this explicit linear system over \(\mathbb F_2\), and the reduced state equals its normalized stabilizer sum.

Suppose there is a pair of consecutive lost vertices. Split the retained vertices into maximal nonempty *clusters*: successive vertices within one cluster have cyclic gaps one or two, while different clusters are separated by at least two lost sites. Each cluster is a linear ordered sequence, and the constraints above decouple between clusters. At either end of a cluster, the nearest of the two successive lost sites imposes \(u=0\) on the endpoint retained vertex. Every one-site hole within a cluster imposes equality of the two adjacent retained \(u\)-coordinates. Thus the surviving stabilizer subgroup is the direct product of the cluster-supported subgroups, and \(\rho_A\) is the tensor product of the associated cluster density operators.

For any cluster containing at most three retained vertices, the two endpoint equations leave at most one free binary coordinate. A four-vertex cluster with **no four consecutive retained sites** has at least one internal hole, which imposes a further independent equation on its two interior coordinates. Its supported stabilizer subgroup also has dimension at most one. Any stabilizer marginal with a zero- or one-dimensional stabilizer subgroup is fully separable: it is either the normalized identity or \((I+P)/2^k\) for a tensor-product Pauli observable \(P\), and both are diagonal in a product of single-qubit Pauli eigenbases. Consequently, when no cluster has more than four vertices and \(F_n\) fails, \(\rho_A\) is fully separable.

If \(H_n\) fails, any cluster contains at most four retained vertices by definition. If there is no pair of successive losses, every two consecutive retained vertices have gap one or two, and for \(n\ge10\) at least five vertices survive: hence \(H_n\) necessarily occurs. Therefore on \(F_n^c\cap H_n^c\) there exists a double loss, all clusters satisfy the preceding sufficient separability criterion, and \(\rho_A\) is fully separable. This proves \(E_n\subseteq F_n\cup H_n\).

**3. Finite-size probability bound.** There are \(n\) possible initial vertices and \(2^4=16\) sequences of successive gaps from \(\{1,2\}\) for a candidate five-vertex pattern. Their five vertices are distinct for \(n\ge10\). Each pattern is retained with probability \(r^5\), so \(\Pr(H_n)\le16nr^5\) by the union bound. Combine with the deterministic sandwich.

**4. Poisson critical window and both sides.** Write \(N_4\) for the number of cyclic four-consecutive retained windows. If \(nr_n^4\to\lambda<\infty\), then \(r_n\to0\). For each fixed integer \(m\ge1\), the \(m\)th factorial moment of \(N_4\) is \(\lambda^m+o(1)\): the contribution of \(m\) mutually nonoverlapping windows is \((nr_n^4)^m+o(1)\); tuples containing overlapping windows can be grouped into \(c<m\) connected window components, in which case they have at least \(4c+(m-c)\) distinct required retained sites, and hence total contribution \(O_m((nr_n^4)^c r_n^{m-c})=o(1)\). Consequently \(N_4\) converges in distribution to a Poisson random variable with mean \(\lambda\), including the degenerate case \(\lambda=0\). Because \(F_n=\{N_4\ge1\}\), \(\Pr(F_n)\to1-e^{-\lambda}\). The upper-bound correction obeys \(16nr_n^5=16(nr_n^4)r_n\to0\), proving the claimed limit for \(E_n\).

Finally, if \(nr_n^4\to\infty\), partition the first \(4\lfloor n/4\rfloor\) sites into disjoint four-vertex blocks. Independence gives

\[
\Pr(F_n^c)\le(1-r_n^4)^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\longrightarrow0,
\]

hence \(\Pr(E_n)\to1\). If instead \(nr_n^4\to0\), the union bound gives \(\Pr(E_n)\le nr_n^4+16nr_n^5\to0\). This covers all asserted regimes.

## Verification

The accompanying pure-Python file `verify.py` constructs the exact surviving graph-state stabilizer subgroup by binary elimination, partitions retained vertices at two-site losses, and checks the component factorization and low-rank separability certificate. It exhaustively examines every retention mask for \(n=10,11,12,13,14,15\), a total of 64,512 masks. It finds 26,120 masks with a four-run entanglement witness and verifies separability certificates in 22,297 masks satisfying \(F_n^c\cap H_n^c\), checking 53,569 cluster rank certificates. It also checks the explicit \(-1/8\) partial-transpose eigenvalue. Invoke `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3. The infinite-dimensional asymptotics follow from the analytical proof, not from these finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Han, Zhang, and Zhang (arXiv:2606.08561v1, 2026) developed the stabilizer-marginal framework and explicitly proved the four-site NPT certificate used here, while establishing non-resistance in the worst-case fixed-number-loss setting for cycles of order at least seven. The present theorem addresses a different, *random independent retention* question: it gives a deterministic near-characterization of surviving entanglement and the exact critical probability law for arbitrary growing cycle size. In contrast to worst-case resistance, the entanglement survival probability tends to a nontrivial limit in the fourth-root retention window.

Silberstein and Arnon-Friedman (2023) considered Bell-inequality robustness of graph states under qubit loss, a stronger and distinct operational property; Bell violation is not used here. Sajjad and coauthors (arXiv:2404.09014, published in *Quantum* in 2026) developed coherent-information bounds for graph states under pre-entangling depolarization, a different noise mechanism and target observable. The result here is neither a claim about those models nor an implication of their stated results. Standard Poisson approximation for rare overlapping word occurrences supplies the probabilistic tool; the quantum separability sandwich linking that tool to the event \(E_n\) is the structural contribution.

## Limitations

Only the ideal unweighted cycle graph state, independent identical Bernoulli retention, and loss modeled by partial trace are covered. The theorem gives the asymptotic probability of *some* residual entanglement, not its amount, spatial range, multiparty depth, Bell violation, or usefulness for communication. The \(16nr^5\) correction need not be small for arbitrary finite \(n,r\); it is asymptotically negligible in the critical window. The four-site NPT mechanism itself is prior work. There remains a possibility of an equivalent formulation not indexed under the searches considered; no priority conclusion follows from unsuccessful searches alone.
