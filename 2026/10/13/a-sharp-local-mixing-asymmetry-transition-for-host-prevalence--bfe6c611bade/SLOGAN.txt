In the normalized two-patch Ross–Macdonald model, balanced host movement is always a strict local minimum of vector prevalence, but its host-prevalence curvature changes sign on one explicit abundance–connectivity curve. The transition is determined by the unique positive root of \(y^3+2(1+q)y^2-4q=0\), explaining why slight mixing asymmetry can raise host prevalence near threshold yet lower it at larger vector abundance.
