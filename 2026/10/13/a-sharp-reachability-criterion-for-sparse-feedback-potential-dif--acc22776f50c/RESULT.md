# A sharp reachability criterion for sparse-feedback potential difference games
## Finding
Consider an \(N\)-player deterministic discounted difference game with uncoupled physical dynamics
\[
x_{t+1}^j=f_t^j(x_t^j,a_t^j),\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,
\]
and a fixed sparse feedback information structure. Player \(j\)'s admissible feedback at time \(t\) may depend only on the state coordinates indexed by a set \(O_j\). Form the directed observation graph with an edge \(r\to j\) exactly when \(r\in O_j\), and let \(R_i\) be the vertices reachable from \(i\), including \(i\).

Suppose player \(i\)'s stage reward has the decomposition
\[
L_t^i(x,a)=P_t(x,a)+c_t^i\!\left((x^j,a^j)_{j\in K_i}\right),
\]
where \(K_i\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{i\}\). If
\[
K_i\cap R_i=\varnothing\qquad\text{for every player }i,
\]
then every maximizer of the discounted potential objective generated by \(P\) over the admissible sparse-feedback class is a feedback Nash equilibrium, provided the relevant discounted payoffs are finite.

The graph condition is sharp as a universal guarantee based only on the supports \(K_i\) and the observation graph. If \(K_i\cap R_i\neq\varnothing\) for some \(i\), then there is a smooth discounted infinite-horizon game with open scalar state and action spaces, uncoupled physical dynamics, that observation graph, and a reward decomposition of the displayed form for which a potential-maximizing admissible feedback profile is not a feedback Nash equilibrium.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix a discount factor \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). The state and action trajectories induced by every policy profile considered below are assumed well-defined, and the discounted payoff differences used in the Nash comparison are finite. The feedback information sets \(O_j\) are fixed ex ante. A directed edge \(r\to j\) means that player \(j\)'s action is permitted to react to coordinate \(x^r\); it does not mean that the physical state equation for \(x^j\) is coupled to \(x^r\).

The sufficiency statement is deliberately support-based: the nuisance term \(c_t^i\) may be an arbitrary function of the listed coordinates. The necessity statement is correspondingly universal rather than pointwise: failure of the graph condition does not say that every particular game with that support fails to be potential; it says that no guarantee valid for all such nuisance functions can survive that failure.

The result concerns potential maximizers and feedback Nash equilibrium. It does not assert existence or uniqueness of maximizers, nor does it assert reduction of the associated coupled Hamiltonian system.

## Proof
Fix player \(i\), a baseline admissible feedback profile \(\psi\), and a unilateral deviation \(\widetilde\psi^i\). Compare the two trajectories from the same initial state while all opponents keep their feedback rules fixed.

We first prove that every state and action coordinate indexed by a vertex outside \(R_i\) is unchanged by the unilateral deviation. At time \(0\), the state vectors coincide. Assume that at time \(t\) the state coordinates outside \(R_i\) coincide. If \(j\notin R_i\), then every coordinate observed by player \(j\) also lies outside \(R_i\). Indeed, if some \(r\in O_j\) were reachable from \(i\), then the edge \(r\to j\) would make \(j\) reachable from \(i\), a contradiction. Hence player \(j\)'s fixed feedback rule receives the same observed state under the two trajectories and chooses the same action. Because the physical dynamics are uncoupled, the next state \(x_{t+1}^j=f_t^j(x_t^j,a_t^j)\) is also the same. Induction gives equality of the state-action coordinates of every \(j\notin R_i\) for all times.

When \(K_i\cap R_i=\varnothing\), every coordinate on which \(c_t^i\) depends is therefore invariant under a unilateral deviation by player \(i\). Consequently
\[
J_i(\widetilde\psi^i,\psi^{-i})-J_i(\psi)
=
J_P(\widetilde\psi^i,\psi^{-i})-J_P(\psi),
\]
where \(J_P\) is the discounted objective generated by \(P\). If \(\psi\) maximizes \(J_P\) over the admissible feedback class, the right-hand side is nonpositive for every unilateral deviation. Repeating the argument for all players proves that \(\psi\) is a feedback Nash equilibrium.

For sharpness, suppose \(k\in K_i\cap R_i\). Choose a simple directed path
\[
v_0=i\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_m=k,
\]
with \(m\ge1\). Take \(X^j=A^j=\mathbb R\), initial state zero, and dynamics \(x_{t+1}^j=a_t^j\). For \(0\le t\le m\), let
\[
P_t(x,a)=-\sum_{j=1}^N(a^j)^2,
\]
and put \(P_t=0\) for \(t>m\). Define a baseline feedback profile that is zero except that, at time \(r\), player \(v_r\) uses the admissible rule
\[
a_r^{v_r}=x_r^{v_{r-1}},\qquad r=1,\ldots,m.
\]
The baseline trajectory is identically zero, so its potential value is \(0\), the global maximum because every potential stage is nonpositive.

Let player \(i\)'s nuisance term be zero except at time \(m\), where
\[
c_m^i=C a^k
\]
for some \(C>0\). If player \(i\) deviates by choosing \(a_0^i=\delta>0\) and zero thereafter, the fixed opponent feedback rules propagate \(\delta\) along the path, giving \(a_r^{v_r}=\delta\) for \(r=1,\ldots,m\). The payoff change to player \(i\) is exactly
\[
-\delta^2\sum_{r=0}^{m}\alpha^r+\alpha^m C\delta.
\]
It is positive whenever
\[
0<\delta<\frac{\alpha^m C}{\sum_{r=0}^{m}\alpha^r}.
\]
Thus the potential-maximizing baseline profile is not a feedback Nash equilibrium. All displayed dynamics, policies along the witness path, potential stages, and nuisance terms are smooth.

The criterion strictly enlarges the own-state-only feedback class. For example, with three players, take \(O_1=\{1\}\), \(O_2=\{1,2\}\), and \(O_3=\{3\}\). Player \(2\) may react to player \(1\)'s state, so the class is not own-state-only. Nevertheless, choosing \(K_1=\{3\}\), \(K_2=\{3\}\), and \(K_3=\{2\}\) gives \(K_i\cap R_i=\varnothing\) for every \(i\).

## Verification
The proof has two independent parts. The sufficiency part is an induction on time using only graph reachability and the uncoupled state equations. The sharpness part is an explicit path-propagation construction whose payoff difference is a one-variable quadratic expression.

The accompanying verifier checks the strict three-player extension example, simulates the length-two sharpness witness at \(\alpha=1/2\), and verifies that the concrete deviation \(\delta=1/20\) has positive gain. These finite checks support the explicit examples; the general theorem is established by the analytic induction and path construction above.

## Relationship to prior work
Márquez-Prado, Hernández-Lerma, and Jasso-Fuentes show that for uncoupled dynamics the open-loop decomposition \(L_t^i=P_t+c_t^i\) need not remain a potential decomposition under unrestricted feedback, because opponents' feedback actions can react to the deviating player's state. They recover invariance by restricting every player's feedback to depend only on that player's own state. The present criterion identifies the more general obstruction: a nuisance coordinate matters only if it lies in the directed transitive closure of the deviating player's informational influence. Thus own-state-only feedback is sufficient but not necessary.

Guo and Zhang characterize dynamic potential games through a value-function decomposition into a potential term and a residual depending only on other players' policies, and develop distributed games as an important class. The reachability theorem here is a primitive-data criterion for when a stagewise nuisance term remains invariant under sparse feedback, together with a matching universal counterexample when the graph condition fails.

Prasad, Mohapatra, and Reddy study finite-horizon feedback potential difference games and give conditions for an optimal-control representation. Only their abstract and bibliographic metadata were available for comparison, so an unrecognized overlap with a more specialized full-text theorem remains the main originality risk.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes uncoupled physical dynamics. With physically coupled state equations, a player's deviation may propagate outside the observation graph even when no feedback edge carries that information, so the stated reachability set is no longer sufficient.

The graph condition is exact only as a universal support-level guarantee. Particular nuisance functions can have cancellations or symmetries that preserve potentiality even when their support intersects a reachability set. Conversely, the theorem does not attempt to identify all such exceptional functions.

The result addresses the Nash implication of potential maximization, not the existence of an optimizer, differentiability of value functions, or Hamiltonian-system reduction.
