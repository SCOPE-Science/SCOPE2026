# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The number distribution follows by normalizing creation operators on the diagonal thermal Fock law; its probability-generating function is \(G(z)=z^m/[1+\beta(1-z)]^{m+1}\). Independently reconstructing silence of one bin and a pair gives the exact click covariance and a strictly positive denominator. All parameter boundaries are accounted for: finite \(m\ge1\), \(\beta\ge0\), \(N\ge2\), \(0<\eta\le1\), with the isolated \(h=1/2\) endpoint treated separately. The sign criterion is an exact logarithmic inequality. The unique thermal ceiling and order thresholds follow from a strictly increasing logarithmic ratio. A monotone derivative-ratio argument proves the cutoff dependence on detector parameter. A two-sided positive bound of the finite-order supremum yields the claimed essential-singularity coefficient; no numerical limit is used as proof. The standalone program independently constructs finite click-count distributions and confirms 240 numerical instances and 120 cutoff identities. The verifier is corroborating evidence, not infinite certification.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority risk
The fundamental sub-binomial witness and its click-covariance representation are due to Sperling, Vogel and Agarwal (arXiv:1205.4669, Section II); their thermal example uses one added photon. The full \(m\)-photon-added thermal photon-number distribution is prior work (Barnett et al., arXiv:1806.09475). It combines with the established covariance identity to imply the unoptimized \(Q_B\) expression. Lee et al. (arXiv:1606.04826) assess the different unbalanced click witness and a single-photon-added example. Gebhart and Bohmann (Phys. Rev. Research 2, 023150, 2020) tabulate click distributions for multiple added photons and test classifiers, but the inspected article does not state the all-order thermal ceiling or optimized essential-singularity exponent. General click moment-matrix results (arXiv:1701.07642) also do not directly establish these two optimizations. The claimed contribution is the sharp all-order resource barrier and asymptotic maximum-contrast collapse, not a new witness, nonclassicality of photon-added thermal light, or the base distribution. Unlocated stronger theorems remain possible; this is a self-review, not an independent publication-priority guarantee.

## Value — PASS
A sharp detector-dependent cutoff separates thermal inputs for which finite-order photon addition can produce a negative second-order click witness from inputs for which it never can. The least useful order is exact, and the essential exponential vanishing of the *best* negative contrast explains why a formally attainable sign can be practically fragile near the boundary. These results are mathematically nontrivial optimization and singular-limit facts motivated by a standard optical witness, not an arbitrary parameter slice. No finite-sample detection guarantee, global nonclassicality no-go, or extension to dark counts is asserted.
