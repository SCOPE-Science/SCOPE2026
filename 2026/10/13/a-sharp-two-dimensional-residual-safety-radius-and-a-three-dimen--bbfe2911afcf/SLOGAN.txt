Newton–Schulz residual squaring has a sharp low-dimensional divide: in \(2\times2\), spectral radius at most \(1/2\) guarantees first-step Frobenius residual nonexpansion, and this threshold is exact. In \(3\times3\), a nilpotent initial residual with spectral radius zero can spike by an arbitrarily large factor and then vanish exactly on the next step, even when the target matrix is \(I\).
