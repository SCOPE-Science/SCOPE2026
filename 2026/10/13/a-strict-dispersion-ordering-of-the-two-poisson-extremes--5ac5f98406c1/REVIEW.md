# Scientific comparison and review

## Correctness — PASS
The new universal inequality reduces exactly to \(b>\lambda c^3\), where \(b=e^{-2\lambda}I_1(2\lambda)\) and \(c=e^{-2\lambda}(I_0(2\lambda)+I_1(2\lambda))\). Poisson size bias establishes the separate mean and variance identities. The beta-gamma conditional-expectation coupling proves the strict bound \(c<(1+2\lambda)^{-1/2}\); at every hypothetical first zero of \(b-\lambda c^3\), modified-Bessel differentiation makes the derivative strictly positive, a contradiction. This covers all positive intensities and both zero and infinite limiting boundaries. Independent distributional computations passed seventeen intensity tests, but constitute checks rather than a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The earliest public lead source, Badiella–del Castillo–Puig (June 26, 2023), establishes ultra-log-concavity and separate underdispersion, not the relative ranking of minimum and maximum. The earlier Steutel–Thiemann formula and Lederman–Schein's 2025 Section 3/Figure 4 give exact moment and limiting-dispersion information but do not state or prove the all-intensity inequality. Existing exact moment formulas do not determine the sign without a further nontrivial special-function inequality. Earlier related results address large-intensity asymptotics or the maximum's variance relative to its Poisson parent, neither of which implies the comparison for all finite intensities. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches revealed no equivalent or stronger theorem. The inaccessible 1989 primary full text and any older alias of the cubic Bessel inequality are priority risks, not verified negative evidence.

## Value — PASS
The pair of Poisson extrema is a natural two-rank count model, and the variance-to-mean ratio is its standard underdispersion index. A global strict ordering resolves the otherwise ambiguous choice of rank at any unknown intensity, even though the maximum has larger ordinary variance and both indices have the same Gaussian-limit value. The proof connects Poisson order statistics, beta-gamma convex order, and a nontrivial modified-Bessel inequality. The result does not claim a classification across all sample sizes or heterogenous Poisson parameters.
