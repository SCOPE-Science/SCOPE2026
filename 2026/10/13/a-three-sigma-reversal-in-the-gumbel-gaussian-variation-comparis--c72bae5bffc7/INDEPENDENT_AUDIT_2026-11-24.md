PASS

For x=exp(-gamma-ac), a=pi/sqrt(6), the exact outside mass exceeds 1-exp(-x)>x/(1+x); integration of the Gaussian density bounded by t/c times itself gives T_N(c)<2 phi(c)/c. For their bounds' ratio R(c), (log R)'=c+1/c-a+ax/(1+x)>2 on [3,infinity). At 3 the lower bound exceeds 1/244 by gamma<1, a<4/3 and e<3, while the Mills upper bound is smaller than exp(-9/2)/3<1/244 since 134217728>2187*244^2 and e>8/3. This proves R(3)>1 for the bounds themselves, not just an ordering of the true tails at 3. The Gumbel left tail is negligible relative to exp(-gamma-ac), and the Gumbel right tail and the normal Mills equivalent give the stated asymptotic including its prefactor. Continuity and the opposite strict sign at radius one from Sun–Hu–Sun give at least one crossing in (1,3), not uniqueness. Bibliographic correction: the one-sigma result is in arXiv:2304.11459v2, Sec. 2.2, Theorem 2.2, not v1 cited in the record; the mathematical premise itself is supported.

## originality

PASS

Support is for the permanent symmetric two-tail reversal with the explicit uniform cutoff at 3 and its quantified divergence. The one-sigma inequality and the separate tail asymptotics are antecedents or standard, not the novelty. The strongest inspected indexed discussions concern other distributions or convergence to Gumbel at radius one. The reported older one-sided three-sigma observation is close but does not imply the explicit all-radii symmetric result as stated.

## value

PASS

Three sigma is a conventional normal benchmarking radius, not a freely chosen extreme sample point. The uniform bound gives a concrete failure of extending the published one-sigma advantage into an all-radius concentration order, with a usable finite cutoff and without claiming it is sharp. The routine separate asymptotics alone would not justify the value assessment.
