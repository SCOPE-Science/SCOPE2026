# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the exact Gumbel outside-central probability to a one-sided lower bound and compares it with the Gaussian Mills upper bound. The resulting ratio is strictly increasing from radius three onward, and elementary constant estimates certify it is already greater than one at radius three. Exact tail equivalents give the divergent ratio. The finite numerical checker is supplementary only.

## Originality
PASS. The direct primary source proves the Gumbel variation inequality at one standard deviation and does not state an all-radius concentration ordering. Candidate-specific searches found no symmetric central-mass theorem valid for every radius at least three. A secondary text contains a related one-sided three-standard-deviation comparison attributed to Gumbel (1958), so the inaccessible original source is explicitly retained as a residual risk rather than being declared non-covering.

## Value
PASS. The motivating literature explicitly invokes the familiar one-, two-, and three-standard-deviation rules. Showing that the Gumbel-versus-normal ordering changes sign and remains reversed throughout the three-sigma-and-beyond regime is therefore a natural structural boundary of the variation-comparison question, strengthened by an explicit tail-ratio law.

## Closest literature and limitations
Sun, Hu and Sun establish the Gumbel inequality at radius one. The present theorem proves the opposite ordering for every radius at least three, but does not claim that three is the first reversal radius or that the crossover in the interval from one to three is unique. The classical source behind a secondary one-sided three-sigma comparison was not materially inspected.
