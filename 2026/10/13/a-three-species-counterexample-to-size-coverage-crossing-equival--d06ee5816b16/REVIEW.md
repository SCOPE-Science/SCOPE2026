# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The two three-species vectors are positive and normalized. Strict majorization and strict convexity give the claimed inequality between size-based expected richnesses for every real effort greater than one, not merely a finite range. Six exact rational inequalities at efforts two, three, seven, eight, and eleven bracket the two opposite rankings under equal expected coverage. Strictly increasing richness and coverage and continuity imply a crossing; the argument also works for polygonal interpolation of integer-effort curves. The attached exact-arithmetic script independently confirms the decisive fractions and the original sampling expectations for small efforts. Limits: no unique crossing count or empirical confidence statements are proved.

## Originality — PASS, with literature risk

The strongest primary source is the 2012 article of Chao and Jost and its Appendix G, Theorem G1. The inspected theorem expressly claims the implication refuted by the example. The paper's main text repeats it; Appendix G's stronger crossing-count assertion is likewise contradicted because the size-based curves have zero crossings while the coverage-based curves have at least one. This is a claim-to-prior contradiction, not a paraphrase of existing examples. Chao and Jost's Appendix H discusses monotonicity and expected-coverage curves without imposing an assumption that would exclude these vectors. Lande, DeVries, and Walla (2000) addresses size-based accumulation crossings rather than the implication about equal coverage. Searches for named counterexamples, corrected versions, and majorization formulations uncovered no identified prior correction. This does not establish absolute priority: a less visible published correction could exist.

## Value — PASS

An exact counterexample to a widely used theoretical assertion about ecological rarefaction is substantive because the promised equivalence underlies a claim that changing the sampling standard cannot change qualitative rankings except at corresponding crossings. The two communities even have equal true richness and a strict majorization relation, so the phenomenon is not driven by unequal species numbers or ambiguous finite-sample estimates. It gives a reproducible boundary to the interpretation of coverage-standardized comparisons, while leaving intact their broader practical motivations.
