# A totient law for one-leaf quantum absorption on perfect rooted trees

## Finding

Consider the continuous-time one-excitation quantum walk on a homogeneous perfect rooted tree. A single irreversible trap at a leaf captures a precisely calculable fraction of the maximally mixed input, and its absorbing rank is governed by Euler's totient summatory function rather than by the exponentially growing number of vertices.

For every integer \(b\ge2\) and \(h\ge1\), with \(N=(b^{h+1}-1)/(b-1)\), the eventual absorption probabilities at a fixed leaf \(\ell\) and at the root \(o\) are

\[
P_{\mathrm{cap}}(\ell)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q=2}^{h+2}\varphi(q),\qquad
P_{\mathrm{cap}}(o)=\frac{h+1}{N}.
\]

In particular, moving the only trap from root to leaf changes the bright-subspace rank from \(h+1\) to \(\sum_{q=2}^{h+2}\varphi(q)=3(h+2)^2/\pi^2+O(h\log h)\), regardless of the branching number \(b\). The asymptotic statement concerns rank as depth grows, not a parameter-uniform physical decay rate.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(T_{b,h}\) has root \(o\), exactly \(b\) children per nonleaf vertex, and all leaves at distance \(h\) from \(o\). It is finite, simple and undirected. Let \(A\) be its adjacency matrix on \(\mathbb C^N\), let \(J\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\gamma>0\), and choose one site \(v\). The no-jump evolution is generated by

\[
K_v=JA-i\gamma|v\rangle\langle v|,\qquad
\rho(t)=e^{-itK_v}(I_N/N)e^{itK_v^\dagger}.
\]

The eventual captured probability is \(1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}\rho(t)\); equivalently it is the total integrated norm loss. The maximally mixed input is a uniform incoherent mixture of all vertex-localized states, not a uniform coherent superposition. The results hold separately for every leaf and for the root, but not for arbitrary nonleaf placements.

Define the bright cyclic subspace \(\mathcal B_v=\operatorname{span}\{A^k|v\rangle:0\le k<N\}\). Let \(\varphi(q)\) denote Euler's totient, with \(\varphi(q)=\#\{1\le j<q:\gcd(j,q)=1\}\).

## Proof

**1. The single-site loss is governed exactly by cyclic rank.** For any finite real symmetric \(A\), \(\mathcal B_v\) is \(A\)-invariant, and its orthogonal complement \(\mathcal D_v\) is also invariant and lies in the kernel of \(\langle v|\). Thus \(K_v\) acts as the Hermitian \(JA\) on \(\mathcal D_v\). On \(\mathcal B_v\), any eigenpair \(K_v u=\lambda u\) satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Im}\lambda\,\|u\|^2=-\gamma|\langle v,u\rangle|^2.
\]

If \(\operatorname{Im}\lambda=0\), then \(\langle v,u\rangle=0\) and \(Au=(\lambda/J)u\). Consequently \(\langle A^kv,u\rangle=0\) for every \(k\), contradicting \(u\in\mathcal B_v\setminus\{0\}\). Every eigenvalue of \(K_v\) restricted to the finite-dimensional bright subspace therefore has strictly negative imaginary part, so its propagator decays to zero, even in the presence of Jordan blocks. The dark propagator is unitary. Orthogonality gives

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}\rho(t)=\frac{\dim\mathcal D_v}{N},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{cap}}(v)=\frac{\dim\mathcal B_v}{N}.
\]

This step uses \(\gamma>0\) and does not assert a decay rate uniform in \(b\) or \(h\).

**2. Factor the full tree spectrum.** Define monic polynomials by

\[
P_0(x)=1,\quad P_1(x)=x,\quad P_{k+1}(x)=xP_k(x)-bP_{k-1}(x).
\]

Schur complements at the root of the tree and its \(b\) descendant subtrees give the rational resolvent recursion \(F_0(x)=x\), \(F_h(x)=x-b/F_{h-1}(x)\), hence \(F_h=P_{h+1}/P_h\). Successively multiplying descendant determinants yields the exact polynomial identity

\[
\det(xI_N-A)=P_{h+1}(x)
\prod_{k=1}^{h}P_k(x)^{(b-1)b^{h-k}}.
\]

These are polynomial identities, initially calculated away from poles and then extended to all \(x\). The recurrence is a rescaled Chebyshev recurrence:

\[
P_k(x)=b^{k/2}U_k\!\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt b}\right),\qquad
\operatorname{zeros}(P_k)=\left\{2\sqrt b\cos\frac{\pi j}{k+1}:1\le j\le k\right\}.
\]

Every \(P_k\) for \(1\le k\le h+1\) occurs with positive multiplicity. Because \(\cos\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,\pi)\), distinct eigenvalues of \(A\) correspond one-to-one to reduced fractions \(j/q\) in \((0,1)\) with denominators \(2\le q\le h+2\). There are exactly

\[
R_h=\#\operatorname{spec}(A)=\sum_{q=2}^{h+2}\varphi(q).
\]

**3. Every energy is visible from any chosen leaf.** Let \(E_\lambda\) denote the orthogonal spectral projection onto the eigenspace at any eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(A\). No nonzero eigenvector can vanish on every leaf: the eigen-equations at the leaves force all their parents to vanish, and induction inwards along the tree forces the entire vector to vanish. Hence \(E_\lambda\) is not supported entirely away from the leaves, so

\[
\sum_{\ell\text{ a leaf}}\langle\ell,E_\lambda\ell\rangle>0.
\]

The tree automorphism group acts transitively on its leaves and commutes with \(A\), hence also with \(E_\lambda\). All leaf diagonal entries above are equal. Positivity of their sum therefore implies \(E_\lambda|\ell\rangle\ne0\) for every particular leaf and every \(\lambda\). In the finite-dimensional spectral decomposition,

\[
\mathcal B_\ell=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(A)}
\operatorname{span}\{E_\lambda|\ell\rangle\},
\]

so \(\dim\mathcal B_\ell=R_h\). Notice that the eigenspaces may have large multiplicity: a single sink sees exactly one direction from each distinct eigenvalue, not the entire eigenspace.

**4. Root comparison.** Starting at the root, the adjacency evolution remains in the \((h+1)\)-dimensional span of the normalized uniform level vectors. In that ordered basis the restricted adjacency is a Jacobi matrix with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries \(\sqrt b\). Its simple spectrum has \(h+1\) eigenvalues, and the first basis vector is cyclic because all Jacobi off-diagonal entries are nonzero. Thus \(\dim\mathcal B_o=h+1\), proving both capture formulas by Step 1.

**5. Large depth.** The classical summatory-totient estimate \(\sum_{q\le X}\varphi(q)=3X^2/\pi^2+O(X\log X)\) applied at \(X=h+2\) gives \(R_h=3(h+2)^2/\pi^2+O(h\log h)\). The ratio of capture probabilities is exactly \(R_h/(h+1)\), and therefore grows linearly in the depth, although the total captured fraction tends to zero exponentially for fixed \(b\ge2\).

## Verification

The standalone script `verification/verify.py` computes exact integer adjacency-action Krylov sequences and their ranks over two large prime fields, checking both root and individual-leaf ranks for multiple branching factors and depths. It independently computes the predicted totient sum from the arithmetic definition. A separate symbolic rational-polynomial check confirmed the characteristic-polynomial identity for \((b,h)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)\). The analytic proof above, not these finite tests, establishes the result for all admissible \(b,h\). No finite check establishes publication priority or parameter-uniform convergence rates.

## Relationship to prior work

Farhi and Gutmann (1997) introduced continuous-time quantum walk algorithms on trees. Novo and collaborators (2015) developed Krylov-space dimensional reduction for transport and explicitly analyzed a binary tree with its sink at the root; the generic Krylov principle in Step 1 is not new. Aryal and Kettemann (2020), and Ostilli, Bezerra and Viswanathan (2021 preprint), describe characteristic polynomials and eigenmodes of closely related finite Cayley-tree hopping models; the Chebyshev recurrence and related spectral decompositions are prior work. Mareš, Novotný, Štefaňák and Jex (2019 preprint) studied trapping on Cayley trees using *discrete-time Grover coined walks*, a different Hilbert space and evolution law. Lin and Zhang (2013) studied location-sensitive trapping times for *classical* walks on dendrimers. The newer source-and-drain Bethe-lattice transport analysis by Hatano, Katsura and Kawabata concerns multiple peripheral sources and a central drain. The assessed statement is the combined **single fixed leaf versus root** exact capture-rank law for continuous-time homogeneous adjacency evolution, especially its totient evaluation across every height and branching factor. It is not asserted that the general ingredients are new.

## Limitations

The result assumes a loop-free perfect rooted tree with uniform nonzero nearest-neighbor hopping and one strictly positive local loss at one vertex. It does not extend unchanged to arbitrary site energies, random hopping, multiple sinks, nonperfect trees, discrete-time coined walks, or continuous-time Lindblad dynamics with recycling. It describes the eventual total capture from a specified incoherent input and does not provide finite-time loss rates or capture from an arbitrary localized input. The spectral ingredients are classical and may permit an equivalent derivation in specialized literature not located in the inspected sources; independent priority review is not performed.
