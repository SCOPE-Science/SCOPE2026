# Scientific assessment: totient law for one-leaf quantum absorption

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an exact, all-integer-height theorem with explicit model and strictly positive absorption. The polynomial factorization follows from the recursive Schur complement of the homogeneous rooted tree, with all factors having positive multiplicities; Chebyshev zeros are counted once by reduced rational angles. Transitivity of leaf automorphisms and uniqueness of an eigenvector vanishing on all leaves prove every distinct energy contributes precisely one cyclic direction at a specified leaf. Dissipativity excludes purely real modes on the bright sector, yielding exact loss from the maximally mixed density matrix. Root invariance and Jacobi cyclicity complete the comparison. Integer-rank experiments over two prime fields and four exact symbolic characteristic polynomials corroborate the algebra; they are explicitly not an infinite proof. The asymptotic uses the classical summatory-totient theorem. Risks: finite-time loss rates and disorders are outside the claim.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

The proof does not claim the recurrence or Krylov transport method as new. Novo et al. study a binary-tree root sink, whereas this statement evaluates a *one-leaf sink*, shows it sees every distinct spectral energy, counts the distinct energies by all reduced denominators, and compares the ensuing absorption fractions. Ostilli et al. and Aryal–Kettemann supply relevant tree spectra without identifying this sink-placement capture observable in inspected text. Mareš et al. analyze a Grover *coined discrete-time* walk; Lin–Zhang classical trap locations concern mean first-passage times. The accessible Hatano–Katsura–Kawabata material studies central drainage fed by many peripheral sources. Searches for totient spectral rank and quantum capture on perfect trees did not reveal an equivalent theorem. This is not proof of global novelty: an alternative historical derivation could be unindexed or hidden in an unexamined source, particularly at the intersection of known spectrum and known Krylov formula.

## Value — PASS

The rank is a natural operational transport invariant for a homogeneous dendrimer subjected to a single irreversible detector. The exact leaf/root placement law is unexpectedly independent of branching before normalization; its totient structure gives quadratic versus linear bright-rank growth and a quantified contrast in long-time capture from a uniform incoherent input. It fills the motivated location-dependence question with an exact natural all-height classification rather than an arbitrary finite enumeration. It does not supply thermalization, general graph trapping or uniform finite-time bounds.
