# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The statistic's generating function is derived directly by requiring all smaller odd parts and excluding the next odd part. Jacobi and Gauss product identities give an exact square factorization; extracting the half-product modulo two identifies odd-divisor parity, and Frobenius squaring cancels every non-odd-square term. The coefficientwise congruence follows for all nonnegative indices. The density-one conclusion uses a genuine norm-form obstruction at primes congruent to five or seven modulo eight and the standard divergence of the corresponding reciprocal prime sums. A separate verifier checks the formulas and direct partition enumerations for finite ranges; those checks are not substituted for proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to usual bibliographic uncertainty. Mukherjee's Theorem 1.12 gives the generating function but no congruence modulo eight, triangular exact valuation criterion, odd-square correction, or density-one statement; its complete Section 6 proof was compared. A closely related earlier minimal-excludant paper treats unrestricted partitions, not distinct-even partitions. A four-regular partition sequence table lists unweighted coefficients and does not state the weighted theorem. Targeted semantic and literature searches did not identify a stronger covering result. Because a known generating function contains the full coefficients implicitly, the scientific distinction is the nontrivial dyadic factorization and precise number-theoretic consequences, not a claim of a new generating function. Residual risk: an equivalent dissection may be unindexed.

## Value

PASS. This is a complete modulo-eight residue law for an independently motivated weighted partition statistic, sharpening it to an exact triangular parity threshold and an arithmetic representation test for the remaining nonzero level. The nonobvious density-one divisibility assertion gives a global arithmetic consequence not available from the source's generating function without additional work. The result does not assert a higher-power valuation law.
