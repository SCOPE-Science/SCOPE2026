# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is quantified over all \(k\ge1\) and odd primes \(p<q\). The identity \(a=M(p+1)-(p-M)q\) follows from \(\sigma(2^k p q)=M(p+1)(q+1)\). Binary subset sums turn each bounded three-coefficient abundance representation into a semiperfect witness. Lemma 1's quotient bounds and transformed representation are exact and valid at \(q=2M\) (although the latter is even). Lemma 2 counts \(x\) distinct residues in a universe allowing only \(x-1\) outside \([0,M]\). Both cases of the resulting threshold split give strict bounds. The lower bound \(a>M\) is explicitly attributed to Iannucci. Finite program output supports, but does not substitute for, the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS
Iannucci (arXiv:1504.02761v1) establishes the basic identity and necessary conditions, and conjectures the substantially stronger coefficient near one third. The closest inspected published result proves only \(a<\frac34M^2+M\), which cannot imply the stricter \(\frac23\)-coefficient bound; its proof uses a weaker residue-packing inequality. Searches for aliases, two-thirds bounds, semiperfect obstructions, and the same parameterization did not find a covering theorem. Some literature records the intermediate observation \(q>2M\), which is **not** claimed as a new stand-alone theorem. Residual risk: unindexed earlier mathematical arguments, including implicit deductions within constructions.

## Value — PASS
The theorem gives a nontrivial, uniform quantitative improvement for a natural family tied to a published conjecture. Unlike an arbitrary finite subcase, it holds for every exponent and prime pair; compared with the known coefficient \(3/4\), it closes a positive fraction of the gap to Iannucci's conjectured scale. It does not claim optimality or new infinitude results.
