# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Euclid's one-to-one parametrization and the parity of the perimeter force \(2m=(2u)^k\) and \(m+n=v^k\). Coprimality and the wedge inequalities are necessary and sufficient. The bounded dilated wedge has explicitly computed area \(Y\log2/(2k)\), and an odd-coordinate Möbius sieve gives the Euler-product factor \(8/\pi^2\) with error \(O_k(\sqrt Y\log(2Y))\). This proves the asymptotic for each fixed exponent, not merely for tested bounds. The embedded standalone exact-integer verifier cross-checks two independently implemented finite enumerations.

**Originality — PASS, with a disclosed residual risk.** The older unrestricted triangle-perimeter count is not a perfect-power subcount. Yiu's square-perimeter normal form and OEIS tables do not establish the all-support asymptotic. The 2026 fixed-prime-support theorem has a different domain and a logarithmic rate at each prescribed support; its fixed-support estimates cannot be summed without controlling a growing support family. Searches for square, cube, perfect power, and lattice-asymptotic variants yielded no statement with the same domain and constant. Earlier unindexed derivations remain possible. The result's elementary geometric sieve relies on classical techniques and is not claimed as a new counting method.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Perfect-power perimeters are a natural, thin Diophantine subclass for which total-perimeter asymptotics alone give no counting answer. The finding determines the power-law order, exact constant, error, and infinite occurrence simultaneously for all fixed integer exponents, with unrestricted prime support. The constant's inverse-exponent dependence is a structural feature, not a fitted statistic.

**Scientific limits.** The exponent is fixed; no uniformity with growing exponent is shown. Triangles, not distinct perimeter values, are counted. The reported experiments are finite checks of the bijection. Complete historical novelty is not certified.
