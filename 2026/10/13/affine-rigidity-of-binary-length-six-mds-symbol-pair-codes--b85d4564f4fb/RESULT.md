# Affine rigidity of binary length-six MDS symbol-pair codes
## Finding
For binary words of length \(6\), define the cyclic symbol-pair distance by
\[
d_p(x,y)=\left|\left\{i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z:(x_i,x_{i+1})\ne(y_i,y_{i+1})\right\}\right|.
\]
Among all, not necessarily linear, codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) with \(d_p(C)\ge5\), the maximum size is \(8\). Exactly \(32\) labeled maximum codes attain this size, and every one is an affine three-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^6\).

Exactly four maxima contain \(000000\). These four are linear and form one orbit under the dihedral group of rotations and reflections of the six cyclic coordinates. Consequently, under the natural order-\(768\) group generated by the \(64\) binary translations and the order-\(12\) dihedral coordinate group, all \(32\) maxima form one orbit; a maximum has stabilizer order \(24\).

For every maximum code, its \(28\) unordered codeword pairs have the same distance distribution: \(24\) pairs at pair-distance \(5\) and \(4\) pairs at pair-distance \(6\).

## Assumptions and scope
The coordinate pairs are cyclic, so the final pair is \((x_5,x_0)\). Codes are arbitrary subsets of \(\{0,1\}^6\); linearity is a conclusion, not an assumption. “Labeled” means that the six coordinate positions and the two binary symbols are fixed. The symmetry statement concerns the explicit natural group generated by word translations and cyclic-coordinate dihedral symmetries; no claim is made about the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph.

## Proof
Construct the graph whose \(64\) vertices are the binary words of length \(6\), with an edge between two words exactly when their pair-distance is at least \(5\). A code of the stated type is exactly a clique in this graph.

An exact branch-and-bound maximum-clique search with greedy-coloring upper bounds gives clique number \(8\). An independent fixed-cardinality recursion enumerates every \(8\)-clique and finds exactly \(32\).

Translation by any binary word preserves pair-distance. Of the \(32\) maxima, exactly four contain \(000000\). Direct closure checks show that each of these four is closed under binary addition and hence is a three-dimensional linear subspace. If \(C\) is any maximum and \(c\in C\), then \(C+c\) is a maximum containing zero; exhaustive comparison places it among those four linear codes. Therefore every maximum is an affine translate of one of the four linear codes.

One representative linear maximum has generator rows
\[
001011,\qquad 010101,\qquad 100111.
\]
Applying all twelve cyclic-coordinate rotations and reflections to this representative yields exactly the four zero-containing maxima. Applying in addition all \(64\) translations yields exactly the full set of \(32\) maxima. Orbit-stabilizer therefore gives stabilizer order \(768/32=24\).

For a zero-containing maximum, the seven nonzero codewords have pair-weights \(5,5,5,5,5,5,6\). In an affine three-flat, each nonzero difference occurs in exactly four unordered pairs, giving \(24\) pairs at distance \(5\) and \(4\) at distance \(6\).

## Relationship to prior work
Cassuto and Blaum introduced the symbol-pair channel and its metric framework [1]. Chee, Ji, Kiah, Wang and Yin established the Singleton-type bound for symbol-pair codes [2], which here gives \(|C|\le 2^{6-5+2}=8\). Ding, Ge, Zhang, Zhang and Zhang then studied linear MDS symbol-pair codes and proved that a linear binary MDS code with pair-distance \(5\) exists for every length \(5\le n\le7\) [3]. Their scope is explicitly linear; their result supplies existence, not an unrestricted equality-case classification. The finding here classifies all binary length-\(6\) equality cases and shows that allowing nonlinear codes creates no additional maxima.

A focused search of current published-result records for the exact parameters, nonlinear equality cases, affine classification, and the numerical census found no statement implying this classification. The closest current record found concerns binary cyclic symbol-pair codes and a different parameter family; restricting to cyclic linear codes does not imply an enumeration of all arbitrary length-\(6\) maxima.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite classification only for the binary parameter triple length \(6\), minimum pair-distance \(5\), maximum size \(8\). It does not classify longer MDS symbol-pair codes, nonbinary alphabets, or the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph. The literature comparison cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed historical enumeration, although the most directly relevant construction paper and targeted exact-parameter searches were inspected.
