# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The classification is finite and completely specified. Every nine-point subset can be translated to contain zero; the search enumerates all strictly increasing eight-element completions after zero, rejecting only irrevocable pair-sum collisions or impossible cardinality bounds. The full set of \(216\) normalized solutions is checked independently against the affine orbit of a directly verified representative; both finite exhaustive programs agree, and the orbit has all \(1344\) possible elements of the affine group. This proves completeness and trivial stabilizer. No infinite assertion rests on finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The earlier minimum modulus \(56\) and the displayed example are *already published* (Haanpää–Huima–Östergård, OEIS A004135 and A260998), and no originality is attributed to either fact. The inspected tables and the full recent coding paper do not give the stronger classification that every optimal set belongs to one affine orbit, nor the total count \(1344\) and trivial stabilizer. Modular Sidon sets with repeated-index restrictions are strictly stronger and do not imply this count. The complete 2004 article was inaccessible beyond four annotated original-table scans; an existing unindexed or uninspected class enumeration remains a material priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The previously tabulated optimum \(Q_{9,2}=56\) has a natural unresolved rigidity question: how many distinct optimal nine-motif pair-sum label systems exist once changes of cyclic coordinates are factored out? The answer is unexpectedly rigid—a *single free orbit*. This classifies all design freedom in the exact shortest cyclic-syndrome alphabet relevant to the 2025 composite-DNA coding framework. Unlike reproducing an optimum entry, this answers an independent complete-structure question with an explicit finite certificate. It does not claim that the complete error-correcting codes are unique.
