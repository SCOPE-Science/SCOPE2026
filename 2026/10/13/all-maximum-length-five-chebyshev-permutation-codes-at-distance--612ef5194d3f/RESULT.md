# All maximum length-five Chebyshev permutation codes at distance three
## Finding
Let \(d_\infty(\pi,\sigma)=\max_{1\le i\le5}|\pi(i)-\sigma(i)|\) on \(S_5\). The known optimum for minimum distance at least \(3\) is \(10\). There are exactly \(192\) labeled maximum codes \(C\subset S_5\) of size \(10\).

Every maximum code has a compact structural description. For a codeword \(\pi\), let \(L(\pi)\) be the two positions occupied by the symbols \(1,2\), and let \(H(\pi)\) be the two positions occupied by \(4,5\). In a maximum code, every two-subset of the five positions occurs exactly once as an \(L\)-set and exactly once as an \(H\)-set. Thus the code induces a bijection \(f\) on the ten two-subsets such that \(f(L)\cap L=\varnothing\). Equivalently, \(f\) is a perfect matching in the bipartite double cover of the Petersen graph, whose vertices are the two-subsets and whose adjacency relation is disjointness.

There are exactly \(60\) such Petersen-supported bijections. As permutations of the ten two-subsets, they have exactly three cycle types: \(6\) have type \(2+2+2+2+2\), \(24\) have type \(5+5\), and \(30\) have type \(2+8\). For each such bijection, assigning the internal order of \(1,2\) on \(L\) and \(4,5\) on \(H\), with \(3\) in the remaining position, yields respectively \(4\), \(2\), and \(4\) admissible distance-three codes. Hence
\[
6\cdot4+24\cdot2+30\cdot4=192.
\]

Under the explicit isometry group generated by arbitrary coordinate permutations and the global value reflection \(x\mapsto6-x\), of order \(240\), the \(192\) maximum codes form exactly three orbits of sizes \(24,48,120\). Their stabilizer orders are \(10,5,2\), and their unordered pair-distance profiles \((N_3,N_4)\) are respectively \((30,15)\), \((25,20)\), and \((28,17)\). Every maximum is position-symbol balanced: in every coordinate, each of the five symbols occurs exactly twice.

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient objects are all one-line permutations of \(1,2,3,4,5\). Distance is the Chebyshev metric above, and a code is an arbitrary subset, with no group, linearity, or construction-family assumption. “Labeled” means that coordinates and symbols are not factored by symmetry. The stated order-\(240\) action is an explicitly verified isometry group; no claim is made that it is the full graph-automorphism group.

## Proof
If two codewords have the same set of positions occupied by \(1,2\), then in those two positions their values differ by at most \(1\), while in the remaining three positions both values lie in \(\{3,4,5\}\) and therefore differ by at most \(2\). Their Chebyshev distance is at most \(2\). Consequently the map \(\pi\mapsto L(\pi)\) is injective on any distance-three code, so every such code has at most \(\binom52=10\) words. A size-ten code exists, and the exhaustive verification described below independently reconstructs all of them.

For equality, the injective map onto the ten two-subsets is bijective. Applying the same argument to the symbols \(4,5\) shows that \(\pi\mapsto H(\pi)\) is also bijective. Since a permutation cannot place low and high symbols in the same position, \(L(\pi)\cap H(\pi)=\varnothing\). Hence a maximum code defines a bijection \(f:L\mapsto H\) supported on disjoint pairs of two-subsets. Conversely, once such a bijection is fixed, each \(L\) has four possible internal orientations: the two orders of \(1,2\) and the two orders of \(4,5\); the remaining position contains \(3\). The verifier checks the pairwise-distance condition during a complete pruned search over these orientations. It finds exactly the cycle-type and orientation counts stated above, and the resulting \(192\) codes coincide exactly with an independent exhaustive enumeration of all ten-cliques in the full compatibility graph.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the artifact directory. The script uses only the Python standard library. It constructs all \(120\) permutations and all \(5340\) compatible pairs at distance at least \(3\), proves the ten-word upper bound by the low-position signature, exhaustively enumerates every ten-clique, independently enumerates all \(60\) Petersen-supported bijections and their admissible orientations, and compares the two code sets for exact equality. It then verifies the order-\(240\) action, orbit sizes, stabilizers, pair-distance profiles, cycle types, and the position-symbol balance condition. `census.json` contains all \(192\) labeled maxima and one representative of each stated orbit.

## Relationship to prior work
Kløve, Lin, Tsai and Tzeng introduced and developed permutation arrays under the Chebyshev distance and investigated the difficult near-diagonal regime \(P(d+r,d)\). Their 2009 paper reported the value \(9\) for the \(P(5,3)\) table entry. Bereg, Haghpanah, Malouf and Sudborough later identified that entry as a computational error and proved \(P(n,n-2)=10\) for all \(n\ge5\), in particular establishing the already-known optimum \(P(5,3)=10\). Tamo and Schwartz independently developed the same metric for limited-magnitude rank-modulation codes. The inspected primary texts provide the metric, constructions, bounds, and the exact optimum, but not the complete set of equality cases, the Petersen-supported bijection description, or the three-orbit census above.

## Limitations
The classification is finite and specific to length \(5\) and minimum Chebyshev distance \(3\). The symmetry statement concerns the explicitly defined order-\(240\) isometry group only. The originality comparison covered the principal Chebyshev-permutation-array literature and targeted database searches, but an obscure or unindexed prior finite enumeration remains a residual risk.
