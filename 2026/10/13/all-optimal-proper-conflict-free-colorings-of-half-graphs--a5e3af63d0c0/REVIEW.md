# Scientific assessment of the optimizer classification

**Correctness.** Two open neighborhoods of size two and the four cross-edges force a four-color core. Once these colors are fixed, the nested neighborhoods restrict both shores to binary sequences except for one endpoint on each shore. In a binary sequence with two distinct initial colors, the third position fixes a majority; a prefix can keep a singleton only by repeating that majority thereafter. The final endpoint either extends the repetition or uses the one available color from the opposite shore. Those endpoint uses do not violate properness. Both interior blocks are nonempty for \(n\ge4\), giving four independent binary choices, hence 16 normalized and 384 named optima. Exhaustion at \(n=4,5\) corroborates the all-order proof.

**Originality.** The scalar value four and the particular construction with a unique color at each universal vertex follow from Pradhan and Sharma, arXiv:2608.10874v2, §4.3, Observation 3 and Theorem 8. The assertion being assessed is the complete optimum family, including optima that reuse a color across the shores, and its count. The cited theorem and algorithm do not exhaust or enumerate this family. Differently indexed equivalent enumerations remain a possible priority risk.

**Value.** The characterization explains why the number of optimal colorings modulo color permutation remains fixed despite the growing graph. It supplies an exact structural benchmark for optimizers on a canonical nested-neighborhood family, without claiming a new scalar invariant or an extension to all chain graphs.
