# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is proved directly from the definition of strong resolution. For \(n\ge4\), omitted centers are limited to one by the mutually-maximally-distant center pairs, and omitted rim vertices are limited to an adjacent pair because every nonadjacent rim pair is mutually maximally distant. Every allowed omitted pair or triple is then explicitly resolved by a selected vertex. The triangular-rim cases are proved separately. An exhaustive direct-definition replay over \(1\le m\le4\), \(3\le n\le7\) agrees with the classification and every enumerator coefficient.

## Originality
PASS. The closest same-object primary paper is Kusmayadi–Kuntari–Rahmadi–Lathifah, DOI 10.17654/MS099091325. Its relevant full-text section defines the same \(t\)-fold wheel, proves the known scalar strong metric dimensions, and gives one minimum construction. It does not state the complete strong-resolving-set family, the factorized cardinality enumerator, or the number of all bases. The foundational vertex-cover reduction, DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2006.06.009, is general and does not imply the claimed formulas without computing the generalized-wheel obstruction structure. Searches under the aliases “generalized wheel,” “t-fold wheel,” “multiple hub wheel,” and \(C_n\vee\overline{K_m}\) found no stronger statement. Residual risk remains for poorly indexed equivalent literature.

## Value
PASS. The same-object literature records only the optimum size. The present theorem determines every feasible set, not just one optimum, and gives a compact product enumerator. This immediately exposes the complete basis multiplicity and the total population of strong resolving sets. The result is a natural structural refinement of a standard graph family rather than an arbitrary finite slice.
