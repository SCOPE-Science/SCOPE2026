# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. With \(H(x)=x^pL(x^p)\), regular variation gives \(H(b_n)\sim n\). Potter bounds and Cesàro uniform integrability control the truncated quadratic variance, while eventual almost increase controls the tail \(p\)-moment. Independent maximal inequalities finish the positive implication. If almost increase fails, the selected two-scale pairs yield independent symmetric three-point rows with weighted Cesàro mass \(1/m\), occurrence probability tending to zero, and normalized maximal \(p\)-moment growing at least on the order of \(m^2\). The inverse-scale equivalence and the explicit slowly varying oscillatory example were checked separately.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating preprint poses a maximal mean-convergence problem. The same author's later article already answers it for increasing weights bounded below by one, and that known answer is not presented as new here. The final theorem instead gives a necessary-and-sufficient characterization of all slow weights for which this upgrade holds uniformly over independent arrays. The later article gives per-array sufficient/necessary tail criteria and one decreasing-weight counterexample, but not the complete weight-class boundary or the bounded-below oscillatory obstruction. Searches found no dominating published statement. Older quasi-monotone or Orlicz formulations remain a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The criterion locates the exact structural threshold at which a natural regular-variation weak law becomes a strong \(L^p\) mean law, resolving the remaining shape-dependence of the slow moment weight. The obstruction is robust enough to use finite-support independent variables and weights bounded below by one; the positive side includes nonmonotone weights. This is an all-weight classification rather than an isolated counterexample.

## Closest literature and limitations
The motivating work is arXiv:2207.06715v1. Its direct subsequent answer for increasing weights is DOI 10.1007/s13398-023-01540-5, especially Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 and Example 4.2. The theorem here is universal over rowwise independent arrays and makes no assertion for arbitrary dependent arrays. The principal bibliographic risk is an equivalent statement in unindexed monotone-envelope or Orlicz literature.
