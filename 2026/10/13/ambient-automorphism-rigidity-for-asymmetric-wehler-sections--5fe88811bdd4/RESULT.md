# Ambient automorphism rigidity for asymmetric Wehler sections

## Finding
Let \(m\geq6\) and let \(P=(\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf C})^m\). Let \(F\subset P\) be any smooth hypersurface in \(|\mathcal O_P(2,\ldots,2)|\). Fix integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\geq3\) that are **not all equal** and let \(G\subset P\) be a hypersurface in \(|\mathcal O_P(d_1,\ldots,d_m)|\), with \(X=F\cap G\) smooth of codimension two. Define \(S_{\mathbf d}=\{\sigma\in S_m:d_{\sigma(i)}=d_i\text{ for all }i\}\). Then restriction induces an isomorphism
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf C))^m\rtimes S_{\mathbf d}}(F,X)\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf C}(X).
\]
Thus every abstract automorphism of \(X\) extends **uniquely** to an ambient product automorphism that preserves \(F\) and permutes factors only among equal entries of \(\mathbf d\). No genericity is imposed on the smooth sections. In particular, the theorem applies when \(\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_m)=1\), where \(K_X\) is a primitive multigraded polarization.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties and morphisms are over \(\mathbf C\). Smoothness of \(F\) and \(X\), the bound \(m\geq6\), and all \(d_i\geq3\) are hypotheses, not conclusions. The word ``automorphism'' means a biregular automorphism of \(X\), without a chosen polarization or embedding. Write \(h_i=c_1(\operatorname{pr}_i^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(1))\) on \(P\) and \(H_i=h_i|_X\). Let \(\mathbf e_i\) be the \(i\)-th standard multidegree vector. Smooth examples satisfying these assumptions exist by Bertini, since both defining line bundles are very ample. The explicit nonuniform multidegree \((3,3,3,3,3,4)\) is primitive and is included.

## Proof
**1. Intrinsic recovery of the ruling classes.** As \(F\) and \(X\) are successive smooth ample hypersurfaces and \(\dim X=m-2\geq4\), integral weak Lefschetz gives
\[
H^2(P,\mathbf Z)\simeq H^2(X,\mathbf Z),\qquad H^4(P,\mathbf Z)\hookrightarrow H^4(X,\mathbf Z).
\]
Indeed, \(\dim F=m-1\geq5\), so degree-four restriction to \(F\) is an isomorphism; restriction to \(X\) is injective even when \(m=6\). Hodge decomposition and the exponential sequence identify \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) with \(H^2(X,\mathbf Z)\), hence \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\bigoplus_i\mathbf Z H_i\). In the integral ring
\[
H^*(P,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z[h_1,\ldots,h_m]/(h_1^2,\ldots,h_m^2),
\]
write \(D=\sum_i a_iH_i\). Injectivity in degree four implies
\[
D^2=0\iff a_ia_j=0\quad\text{for every }i\neq j.
\]
Consequently the primitive nonzero square-zero classes are exactly \(\{\pm H_i:1\leq i\leq m\}\). Each \(H_i\) is nef and nontrivial, whereas \(-H_i\) is not nef: its intersection with a sufficiently high power of the ample class \(A=\sum_iH_i\) is strictly negative. Thus every automorphism of \(X\) permutes the \(H_i\) without signs.

**2. The coordinate pencils are complete.** Let \(\mathbf f=(2,\ldots,2)\) and \(\mathbf d=(d_1,\ldots,d_m)\). The Koszul resolution of the ideal sheaf of \(X\), twisted by \(\mathbf e_i\), is
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathcal O_P(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf f-\mathbf d)
\longrightarrow\mathcal O_P(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf f)\oplus\mathcal O_P(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf d)
\longrightarrow\mathcal I_X(\mathbf e_i)\longrightarrow0.
\]
By the Künneth formula and the cohomology of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(a)\), the first middle summand is acyclic (it has one coordinate of degree \(-1\)); the second has cohomology only in degree \(m\), and the left summand likewise has cohomology only in degree \(m\). In particular \(H^0(P,\mathcal I_X(\mathbf e_i))=H^1(P,\mathcal I_X(\mathbf e_i))=0\). Restriction therefore identifies
\[
H^0(P,\mathcal O_P(\mathbf e_i))\simeq H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(H_i))\simeq\mathbf C^2.
\]
Given \(\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\), its permutation of these complete two-dimensional linear systems produces \(T\in(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf C))^m\rtimes S_m\) with \(T|_X=\varphi\). This extension is unique: an ambient transformation acting trivially on \(X\) induces the identity permutation on the pairwise distinct \(H_i\), and its \(i\)-th projective transformation is the identity because \(\operatorname{pr}_i(X)=\mathbf P^1\).

**3. The factor permutation preserves the multidegree.** Adjunction yields \(K_X=\sum_i d_iH_i\). Any automorphism preserves the canonical class, so its permutation of \(\{H_i\}\) preserves the vector \(\mathbf d\). Hence \(T\) belongs to \((\operatorname{PGL}_2)^m\rtimes S_{\mathbf d}\).

**4. The Wehler hypersurface is uniquely determined by \(X\).** Twist the Koszul sequence by \(\mathbf f\):
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_P(-\mathbf d)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O_P\oplus\mathcal O_P(\mathbf f-\mathbf d)
\longrightarrow\mathcal I_X(\mathbf f)\longrightarrow0.
\]
All coordinates of \(\mathbf f-\mathbf d\) are negative, so it has no global sections. Also \(H^1(P,\mathcal O_P(-\mathbf d))=0\) by Künneth since \(m\geq6\). Thus \(h^0(P,\mathcal I_X(\mathbf f))=1\), generated by the equation of \(F\). Since \(T\) preserves \(X\) and preserves the multidegree \(\mathbf f\), it preserves this one-dimensional equation space; hence \(T(F)=F\). Conversely, each such ambient stabilizer restricts to an automorphism of \(X\), and uniqueness makes this restriction injective. This proves the isomorphism.

## Verification
The proof is uniform in the integer \(m\) and in every permitted multidegree; it does not use finite experiments as a substitute for Lefschetz or Künneth. The companion program `verify.py` performs exact Künneth-cohomology and cup-product consistency checks for the boundary dimension \(m=6\) and further small dimensions, using several asymmetric vectors including \((3,3,3,3,3,4)\). Run it using `python3 verify.py` and require `VERIFY_OK`. It is not a certification of the general Lefschetz theorem or of originality.

## Relationship to prior work
Ana Quedo, *Automorphisms of hypersurfaces in polarized varieties*, arXiv:2610.00709v1, Corollary 7.16, proves ambient extension for smooth \(X\in|\mathcal O_F(d,\ldots,d)|\) with one scalar \(d>2\), in a Wehler variety of dimension at least three. That **uniform-degree case is established prior work** and is intentionally excluded here. In contrast, the present theorem treats every smooth nonuniform multidegree, including primitive vectors such as \((3,3,3,3,3,4)\) for which Quedo's scalar-power hypothesis cannot apply. Quedo's Theorem 1.1 concerns powers of a single normally generated polarization; it does not by itself identify the individual coordinate pencils or the unique Wehler equation in the asymmetric case. General extension results for complete intersections in ordinary projective space, including work of Benoist, have a different ambient geometry. Their conclusions are not invoked as a proof of this multiprojective assertion. The present argument instead uses integral square-zero cup products and the multigraded Koszul ideal.

## Limitations
The claim concerns smooth complex complete intersections only, with \(m\geq6\) and all \(d_i\geq3\), and does not assert optimality of either numerical threshold. It does not compute the finite stabilizer group of an individual pair of equations, nor does it classify birational self-maps. A possible older, more general automorphism-extension result for ample complete intersections in products of projective spaces remains a specific bibliographic risk. The numerical tests in `verify.py` do not establish any infinite statement.
