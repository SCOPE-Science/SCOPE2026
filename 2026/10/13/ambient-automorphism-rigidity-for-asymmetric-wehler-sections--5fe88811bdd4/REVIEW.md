# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has explicit smoothness, complex-ground-field, dimension, and multidegree assumptions. Successive integral weak Lefschetz comparisons identify the integral Picard lattice and inject degree-four cup products into the smooth complete intersection. Primitive square-zero classes intrinsically recover the coordinate pencils. The full two-section property follows from the displayed Koszul resolution and factorwise cohomology of line bundles on \(\mathbf P^1\). Adjunction forces degree-preserving factor permutations, and the quadratic ideal piece of \(X\) is one-dimensional, forcing preservation of the Wehler hypersurface. There are no unstated general-position assumptions. The small-dimensional exact checks corroborate the sheaf-cohomology boundary cases but are not used to infer the infinite theorem. No argument establishes the cases \(m\leq5\) or defining degrees below three.

## Originality — PASS
Quedo, arXiv:2610.00709v1, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 7.16, proves an extension theorem for scalar powers of a polarization and, for Wehler varieties, for the **uniform** multidegrees \((d,\ldots,d)\) with \(d>2\). Uniform multidegrees are excluded from the present claim. A primitive asymmetric vector such as \((3,3,3,3,3,4)\) cannot be a scalar power with exponent greater than one of any integral multigraded line bundle, so the cited scalar-power result does not imply this claim. General complete-intersection automorphism papers by Benoist and Lyu–Zhang concern ordinary projective space rather than a product of projective lines. Targeted mathematical searches and the compared indexed findings did not produce an exact theorem or a stronger applicable statement. This does **not** establish exhaustive bibliographic uniqueness. Residual risk: an older multiprojective or toric complete-intersection extension theorem, expressed in different language, could imply the result.

## Value — PASS
The result addresses the specific gap between scalar-polarized sections and asymmetric multigraded sections of Wehler Calabi–Yau varieties. It determines the **entire abstract biregular automorphism group** for every smooth section in an infinite nonuniform family, not just the generic stabilizer, by an explicit simultaneous ambient stabilizer. The coordinate-pencil and quadratic-equation recovery are structural results, and primitive canonical vectors are naturally beyond the scalar-power hypothesis in the initiating paper. Smooth nonuniform examples exist by Bertini. This is not a classification of individual stabilizer orders, birational maps, or low-dimensional exceptions.
