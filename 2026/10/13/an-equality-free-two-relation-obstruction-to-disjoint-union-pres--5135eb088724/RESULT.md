# An equality-free two-relation obstruction to disjoint-union preservation syntax

## Finding

The conjectured universal-plus-guarded-existential-positive normal form for first-order sentences preserved under disjoint unions **fails even when the equality predicate is absent**. The failure already occurs over finite structures with two unrestricted binary predicate symbols, and the same witness establishes failure over arbitrary structures.

Fix a relational signature \(\{E,Q\}\) containing two binary predicates, with no constants, functions, or identity symbol. Define
\[
U(x):=E(x,x)\land\neg Q(x,x),\qquad T(x):=Q(x,x),
\]
\[
B:=\exists x\exists y\,\bigl(U(x)\land U(y)\land\neg E(x,y)\bigr),
\]
\[
C:=\forall x\forall y\,\bigl((T(x)\land T(y))\to E(x,y)\bigr),
\]
and the equality-free sentence
\[
\boxed{\Psi:=(\exists x\,U(x))\land(B\lor C).}
\]

**Theorem.** Every disjoint union of a nonempty family of models of \(\Psi\) is again a model of \(\Psi\). Yet no sentence \(\forall x\,\alpha(x)\), with \(\alpha\) in the grammar specified below, is equivalent to \(\Psi\), even if equivalence is required only on finite structures.

## Assumptions and scope

A structure has a nonempty domain. Both binary relations are arbitrary (not assumed reflexive, transitive, symmetric, or functional). A disjoint union interprets each relation componentwise and has no cross-component relation tuples. The family being united is nonempty; an empty union is not an allowed nonempty-domain structure.

The comparison grammar starts from atomic formulas and their negations, allows \(\land\), \(\lor\), and **unrestricted** \(\exists\), and allows \(\forall y\) only as a guarded quantifier
\[
\forall y\bigl(R(z,y)\to\beta\bigr)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\forall y\bigl(R(y,z)\to\beta\bigr),
\]
where \(R\in\{E,Q\}\), and the two variable symbols \(y,z\) are distinct. The matrix \(\beta\) is generated recursively by the same grammar. No unrestricted universal quantifier is permitted **inside** \(\alpha\). We test sentences whose outermost quantifier is the one unrestricted \(\forall x\). We allow every number of free and bound variables allowed by the grammar; the result is not a bounded-rank claim.

## Proof

**1. Preservation under disjoint unions.** Let \((\mathfrak M_i)_{i\in I}\) be a nonempty family with \(\mathfrak M_i\models\Psi\) for every index. Every component contains at least one \(U\)-element. If \(I\) is a singleton, the union is isomorphic to its unique member. If \(I\) has at least two indices \(i\ne j\), select a \(U\)-element \(u\in\mathfrak M_i\) and a \(U\)-element \(v\in\mathfrak M_j\). In the disjoint union, both still satisfy \(U\), while \(E(u,v)\) is false because the elements lie in different components. Hence \(B\) holds; \(\exists x\,U(x)\) also holds. Thus the union satisfies \(\Psi\), regardless of whether \(C\) survives. No finiteness assumption is used.

**2. Two finite structures.** Define \(\mathfrak A\) on \(\{a,b\}\), and \(\mathfrak B\) on \(\{a,b,c\}\), with relations exactly
\[
E^{\mathfrak A}=\{(a,a),(b,b)\},\quad Q^{\mathfrak A}=\{(b,b)\},
\]
\[
E^{\mathfrak B}=\{(a,a),(b,b),(c,c)\},\quad Q^{\mathfrak B}=\{(b,b),(c,c)\}.
\]
In each structure, \(a\) is the only \(U\)-element, so \(B\) fails. In \(\mathfrak A\), the sole \(T\)-element is \(b\), and \(E(b,b)\) holds, so \(C\) holds: \(\mathfrak A\models\Psi\). In \(\mathfrak B\), both \(b,c\) satisfy \(T\) but \(E(b,c)\) fails, so \(C\) fails: \(\mathfrak B\not\models\Psi\).

**3. The guarded-fragment transfer lemma.** Suppose \(f:\mathfrak X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak Y\) is an induced-substructure embedding, such that its image is *relation-closed*: for each \(R\in\{E,Q\}\) and each pair \((s,t)\in R^{\mathfrak Y}\), whenever one endpoint belongs to \(f[X]\), the other belongs too. Then for every matrix \(\alpha\) generated by the comparison grammar and every assignment \(\sigma\) of its free variables into \(X\),
\[
\mathfrak X,\sigma\models\alpha
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak Y,f\circ\sigma\models\alpha.
\]
Proof is by structural induction. Atomic and negated atomic formulas have the same truth value in the induced substructure. The two binary Boolean connectives preserve the implication. For \(\exists y\,\beta\), copy any witness in \(X\) through \(f\), then apply the induction hypothesis. For a guarded universal with guard \(R(z,y)\), assume \(\mathfrak X,\sigma\models\forall y(R(z,y)\to\beta)\). If \(t\in Y\) satisfies \(R(f(\sigma(z)),t)\), relation-closure forces \(t=f(s)\) for some \(s\in X\). The induced-substructure condition gives \(R(\sigma(z),s)\), hence the original guarded universal gives \(\beta\) at \(s\), and induction gives \(\beta\) at \(t\) in \(\mathfrak Y\). The reversed guard \(R(y,z)\) is identical with the endpoints swapped. Variable shadowing is treated by extending the assignment before induction. This proves the lemma for formulas of any finite quantifier rank.

**4. Covering the nonmodel.** There are two embeddings of \(\mathfrak A\) into \(\mathfrak B\):
\[
f_0(a)=a,\ f_0(b)=b,\qquad f_1(a)=a,\ f_1(b)=c.
\]
Their images \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\) are induced and relation-closed, since every nonempty relation tuple of \(\mathfrak B\) is a diagonal pair. Their images cover all of \(\mathfrak B\). If \(\mathfrak A\models\forall x\,\alpha(x)\), choose either embedding whose image contains an arbitrary \(t\in B\), and choose its preimage \(s\in A\). Then \(\mathfrak A\models\alpha(s)\), so the transfer lemma yields \(\mathfrak B\models\alpha(t)\). Since \(t\) was arbitrary, \(\mathfrak B\models\forall x\,\alpha(x)\). But \(\mathfrak A\models\Psi\) and \(\mathfrak B\not\models\Psi\), contradicting equivalence. Both structures are finite; equivalence on any larger class of structures also fails. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` exhaustively generates all \(260\) labeled \(\{E,Q\}\)-structures with domains of size one or two (\(4\) on one point and \(256\) on two). Exactly \(81\) satisfy \(\Psi\). It checks disjoint-union preservation on all \(81^2=6561\) ordered pairs of those satisfying structures, and directly evaluates the two- and three-element separating structures and both embeddings. Run with `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. These are finite cross-checks; the universal conclusions are established by the proof, notably the induction in Step 3.

## Relationship to prior work

Van Benthem, ten Cate, and Yang (2026), Section 3.4, recount a conjecture from van Benthem's *Modal Logic and Classical Logic* (1985) about the above preservation fragment, provide a counterexample **using identity**, and explicitly leave the **identity-free** case open (arXiv:2603.12171v1, p. 14). Their construction counts distinct elements satisfying unary diagonal conditions using identity; it is not an identity-free first-order formula over arbitrary structures. Here the witness substitutes a genuinely binary nonedge test for multiplicity and a complete-relation requirement for the opposing obstruction; its two separating structures are covered by relation-closed induced images. The argument is therefore not an identity-free restatement of the printed formula.

Related research concerns disjoint-union invariance, guarded fragments, and generated-substructure preservation. The well-known **invariance** characterization (truth of union iff truth of components) is stronger than one-way **preservation** and does not subsume this result.

## Limitations

The result is confined to two *arbitrary* binary predicates; it makes no single-predicate minimality claim and does not assert a complete replacement syntax. It concerns the precise guarded comparison grammar from the cited conjecture, not all possible normal forms. The enumerator does not prove the unbounded formula-separation theorem. An equivalent earlier counterexample might exist under another formulation; the cited original 1985 book was not independently inspected in full.
