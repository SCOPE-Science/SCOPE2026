# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The result includes the exceptional \(n=2\) and \(n=3\) cases and proves the even-order obstruction by invariant cardinality. For odd \(n\ge5\), the displayed reset word is evaluated exactly. The independent lower bound assigns a potential to every nonempty cyclic interval; the full inverse-image formula treats the seam, the boundaries \(L=1,2,n-1\), and the full set, and a complete parity/wrap case analysis proves that one inverse letter increases potential by at most one. All quantified states, letters, and cycle orders are explicit. A standard-library checker confirms all cases through \(n=19\) by exact subset BFS and evaluates much larger witnesses and inverse transitions. These finite checks corroborate rather than replace the symbolic proof.

**Originality: PASS (with residual priority risk).** Gusev's Eulerian construction has the **same exact polynomial** at odd orders, so the result makes no claim of a new numerical threshold. However, Gusev's two letters are nonpermutations; here \(a\) is an actual full permutation cycle, so neither automaton is conjugate as a labeled transition system, and the cited Gusev theorem is not a theorem about this family. The full public exposition of Gusev's maps and proof was examined. Eppstein's interval algorithm supplies a broad method rather than this sharp closed form. Rystsov–Szykuła's one-point-contraction hypothesis excludes the present multiple-pair collapse. Volkov's 2018 idempotent family and Zhu's 2024 rank assumptions also do not yield it. Related indexed records, including an idempotent cyclic pair-compression model, do not cover the nonidempotent present transition. The original Gusev journal text was only partially accessible; a full secondary technical account supports the map comparison. Undiscovered equivalences remain a risk.

**Value: PASS.** This fully characterizes synchronizability and exact shortest reset words for a natural one-parameter family combining a genuine permutation cycle with simultaneous alternating successor mergers. The sharp change between even and odd sizes comes from a single wrap-around defect. This is a substantive structural boundary, not a new bound for arbitrary automata nor a minor numerical improvement over known Gusev thresholds.
