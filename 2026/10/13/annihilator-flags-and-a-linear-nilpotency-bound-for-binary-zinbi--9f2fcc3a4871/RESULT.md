# Annihilator flags and a linear nilpotency bound for binary Zinbiel algebras

## Finding
Let \(A\) be an \(n\)-dimensional right binary Zinbiel algebra over a field of characteristic \(0\), where \(n\ge 1\). Then there is a complete flag of two-sided ideals
\[
0=I_0\subset I_1\subset\cdots\subset I_n=A,
\qquad \dim I_j=j,
\]
such that
\[
A I_j+I_j A\subseteq I_{j-1}
\qquad (1\le j\le n).
\]
In particular, if the left-normed powers are defined by \(A^1=A\) and \(A^{r+1}=A^rA\), then
\[
A^{n+1}=0.
\]
Every product of \(d\ge 3n+1\) elements of \(A\), with arbitrary placement of parentheses, is zero. Hence the nilpotency index in the arbitrary-bracketing sense is at most \(3n+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The multiplication is bilinear and need not be unital. “Right binary Zinbiel” means that every subalgebra generated by at most two elements is a right Zinbiel algebra. The characteristic-zero hypothesis is essential to the cited defect identities used in the current structural theory. The quantitative bound is not asserted to be sharp.

The source paper uses the right convention and defines the Zinbiel defect by
\[
\phi(a,b,c)=a(bc)-(ab)c-(ba)c.
\]
Its Lemma 2.2 states that the free binary Zinbiel algebra is spanned by left-normed products whose factors are either free generators or alternating three-generator defects. Its Corollary 4.3 states that every minimal nonzero two-sided ideal \(M\) satisfies \(AM=MA=0\) and \(\dim M=1\).

## Proof
We first construct the flag by induction on \(n\). The case \(n=1\) follows from Corollary 4.3: the only nonzero minimal ideal is \(A\) itself, so \(A^2=0\).

Assume \(n>1\). Choose a minimal nonzero two-sided ideal \(M\). By Corollary 4.3,
\[
AM=MA=0,
\qquad \dim M=1.
\]
Let \(\pi:A\to \overline A=A/M\). By induction, \(\overline A\) has a flag
\[
0=J_0\subset J_1\subset\cdots\subset J_{n-1}=\overline A,
\qquad \dim J_r=r,
\]
with
\[
\overline A J_r+J_r\overline A\subseteq J_{r-1}.
\]
Set \(I_0=0\), \(I_1=M\), and, for \(2\le j\le n\), set
\[
I_j=\pi^{-1}(J_{j-1}).
\]
Then \(\dim I_j=j\). For \(j=1\), the desired containment is exactly \(AM+MA=0\). For \(j\ge2\), quotienting by \(M\) gives
\[
\pi(AI_j+I_jA)
\subseteq
\overline A J_{j-1}+J_{j-1}\overline A
\subseteq J_{j-2},
\]
so \(AI_j+I_jA\subseteq I_{j-1}\). This proves the flag.

Now \(A=I_n\) and \(I_jA\subseteq I_{j-1}\). Repeated right multiplication therefore gives
\[
A^r\subseteq I_{n+1-r}
\qquad (1\le r\le n+1),
\]
where \(I_0=0\). Thus \(A^{n+1}=0\).

It remains to pass from left-normed products to arbitrary bracketings quantitatively. Apply Lemma 2.2 to a homogeneous word of original degree \(d\). Because the reduction identities are homogeneous, every left-normed summand has factors from \(Z=X\cup Y\), where a factor from \(X\) has original degree \(1\) and a factor from \(Y\) is a defect of original degree \(3\). If a summand has \(a\) factors from \(X\) and \(b\) factors from \(Y\), then
\[
d=a+3b,
\qquad m=a+b.
\]
Hence \(d\le 3m\), so
\[
m\ge \left\lceil \frac d3\right\rceil.
\]
For \(d\ge3n+1\), every such summand has \(m\ge n+1\) left-normed factors, and therefore evaluates into \(A^m=0\). By multilinearity, every arbitrarily bracketed product of \(d\ge3n+1\) elements of \(A\) is zero.

## Verification
The proof was checked against the full text of arXiv:2610.02852v1. The required source inputs are: the left-normed power convention in equation (2.1), the homogeneous spanning construction in Lemma 2.2, the one-dimensional annihilating-minimal-ideal statement in Corollary 4.3, and the qualitative induction in Theorem 4.4. The new flag induction was replayed symbolically, including the pullback step at \(j=2\). The degree bookkeeping was checked from \(d=a+3b\) and \(m=a+b\), which gives \(m\ge\lceil d/3\rceil\). No finite experiment is used as evidence for the universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Smadyarov proves that every finite-dimensional right binary Zinbiel algebra over characteristic zero is nilpotent, but Theorem 4.4 only produces an unspecified exponent and then invokes Lemma 2.2 qualitatively to remove parentheses. The explicit annihilator flag, the bound \(A^{n+1}=0\), and the arbitrary-bracketing bound \(3n+1\) are not stated there.

The earlier defining-identities paper of Ismailov, Mashurov, and Smadyarov explicitly leaves finite-dimensional binary-Zinbiel nilpotency open in Remark 1.5, so it does not cover the present quantitative strengthening. Towers proves nilpotency for ordinary Zinbiel algebras, a strictly smaller class. In that ordinary setting stronger dimension bounds are known; they do not imply the present result for binary Zinbiel algebras. Benayadi, Kaygorodov, and Mhamdi prove a uniform bound for symmetric Zinbiel superalgebras, again under strictly stronger identities.

## Limitations
The coefficient \(3\) comes from the current free-algebra spanning set, whose auxiliary defect factors have original degree \(3\). The argument does not prove optimality, and a sharper normal form could improve \(3n+1\). The result is restricted to characteristic zero because the cited binary-Zinbiel structure theory uses characteristic-zero divisions. The flag is an existence statement and need not be canonical.
