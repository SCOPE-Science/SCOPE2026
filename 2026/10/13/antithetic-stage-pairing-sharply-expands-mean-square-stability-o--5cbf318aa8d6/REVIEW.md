# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The scalar scheme multiplier is derived from the published update, and complementary stages multiply to \(m^2-b^2V^2\). Exact second and fourth uniform moments yield the stated factor; independent pairs yield the full even/odd mean-square formulas. Strict dominance uses the globally valid bound \(b/m\le4\). The Sturm calculation has exactly one positive root, with rational endpoint certificates, proving sharpness including neutral and divergent boundaries. The pathwise exponent follows from the strong law for independent pairs and integrable logarithms. The included verifier reconstructs the algebra and root counts.

**Originality — PASS, subject to external priority risk.** The closest original source, Bochacik et al., arXiv:2006.12131v1, Secs. 2 and 5, assumes independence across every stage and proves only the independent-stage \(1.59607\ldots\) stability threshold; it does not compare complementary dependent stages. Bochacik–Przybyłowicz, arXiv:2404.19059, Sec. 3, also assumes independent stages and addresses different implicit multipliers. Targeted research-index and literature searches for antithetic/correlated randomized Runge–Kutta pairing did not identify the explicit factor or degree-seven boundary. This is evidence of noncoverage by inspected statements, not a guarantee against unindexed equivalents.

**Value — PASS.** Dependence control is a cost-neutral design choice for a randomized integrator: no additional stage evaluations are needed. The construction extends the mathematically sharp mean-square step-size limit by approximately \(18.8\%\), creates a nonempty stability-recovery interval where independent stages diverge in mean square, and exactly separates mean-square improvement from the unchanged almost-sure exponent. The sharp boundary gives a reproducible benchmark for coupling designs. Its restriction to scalar autonomous decay and lack of nonlinear error guarantees are material limits.
