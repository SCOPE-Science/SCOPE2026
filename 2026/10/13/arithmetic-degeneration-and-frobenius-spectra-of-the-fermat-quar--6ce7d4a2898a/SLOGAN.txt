The \(28\) bitangents of the Fermat plane quartic preserve their \(288\)-double-point, \(15\)-quadruple-point incidence structure modulo every odd prime except exactly \(3\), \(5\), and \(17\). In characteristic \(5\), all \(31\) points of the rational projective plane become intersections—\(16\) triple and \(15\) quadruple—and the generic Frobenius spectrum has \(303\), \(63\), or \(9\) rational intersections according to the prime modulo \(8\).
