# Arithmetic derivation jumps in odd dihedral quandle algebras

## Finding

Let \(n\geq3\) be odd, and let \(K\) be an arbitrary field whose characteristic does not divide \(n\). On the \(K\)-vector space \(A=K[R_n]\) with basis \(e_i\), indexed modulo \(n\), consider the nonassociative product
\[
e_i e_j=e_{2j-i}.
\]
The Lie algebra of all \(K\)-linear derivations \(D\) satisfying \(D(xy)=D(x)y+xD(y)\) is abelian. It vanishes in characteristic zero and in characteristic two.

For an odd prime characteristic \(\ell\nmid n\), define for each divisor \(m>1\) of \(n\)
\[
h_m=\operatorname{ord}_m(2),\qquad H_m=\langle2,-1\rangle\leq(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]
Set \(\epsilon_\ell(m)=1\) in exactly the following cases: (i) \(-1\notin\langle2\rangle\) modulo \(m\) and \(\ell\mid(2^{h_m}-1)\), or (ii) \(-1\in\langle2\rangle\) modulo \(m\) and \(\ell\mid(2^{h_m/2}+1)\); otherwise set \(\epsilon_\ell(m)=0\). In case (ii) the order \(h_m\) is even. Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\dim_K\operatorname{Der}_K(A)=\sum_{\substack{m\mid n\\m>1}}\frac{\varphi(m)}{|H_m|}\,\epsilon_\ell(m).}
\]
All divisibility tests concern ordinary positive integers, not residue classes modulo \(n\).

The first nonzero example with \(\ell\nmid n\) is \(n=11\), \(\ell=3\): its derivation space is one-dimensional. Writing \(\chi\) for the quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_{11}\), with values \(0,1,-1\) interpreted in \(\mathbb F_3\), a generator over any characteristic-three coefficient field is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle D(e_i)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z}\chi(j-i)e_j.}
\]
This formula does not assert anything for fields whose characteristic divides the quandle order or for even-order dihedral quandles.

## Assumptions and scope

The basis is indexed by the additive cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). All scalars belong to \(K\). An algebra derivation is a linear map obeying the Leibniz identity for the displayed nonassociative product, with no assumption of associativity, commutativity, or a unit. The commutator of two derivations is the usual matrix commutator. If the characteristic is zero, the notation \(\ell\) is not used as an integer prime. For \(\ell>0\), it is prime; \(\ell\nmid n\) guarantees a separable cyclic Fourier transform over a field extension.

## Proof

**Step 1: exact Fourier multiplication.** Extend scalars to a field \(F\) containing a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\). Such a field exists because the characteristic does not divide \(n\). Since \(n\) is invertible, the vectors
\[
E_a=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}\zeta^{ai}e_i
\]
form an \(F\)-basis. Direct substitution into the product, using orthogonality of cyclic characters, gives
\[
E_aE_b=\begin{cases}E_{-a},&b=-2a,\\0,&b\ne-2a.\end{cases}
\]
Every index here is taken modulo \(n\).

**Step 2: all derivations are Fourier-diagonal.** Write \(D(E_a)=\sum_u d_{u,a}E_u\). For \(b\ne-2a\), Leibniz applied to \(E_aE_b=0\) yields
\[
0=d_{-b/2,a}E_{b/2}+d_{-2a,b}E_{-a}.
\]
The two displayed basis vectors are distinct for the stipulated \(b\). Thus \(d_{-b/2,a}=0\). Given any \(u\ne a\), choose \(b=-2u\) to conclude \(d_{u,a}=0\). Consequently \(D(E_a)=t_aE_a\) for uniquely determined scalars \(t_a\).

**Step 3: scalar compatibility.** The nonzero products give the exact condition
\[
t_{-a}=t_a+t_{-2a}\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z).
\]
Replacing \(a\) by \(-a\) gives \(t_{2a}=t_a-t_{-a}\); doing the same starting from \(-a\) gives \(t_{-2a}=t_{-a}-t_a=-t_{2a}\). As doubling permutes the index set, the last identity forces \(t_{-a}=-t_a\) for every \(a\), whence \(t_{2a}=2t_a\). Conversely these two relations imply the original one. Taking \(a=0\) yields \(t_0=0\). Hence the entire derivation space, after scalar extension, is the space of functions \(t\) with
\[
t_0=0,\qquad t_{-a}=-t_a,\qquad t_{2a}=2t_a.
\]
This also shows that any two derivations commute, as both are diagonal in the same basis.

**Step 4: characteristic and orbit count.** In characteristic two, \(t_{2a}=0\) and the bijectivity of doubling imply \(t=0\). In characteristic zero, \(2^{h_m}t_a=t_a\) for every index \(a\) of exact additive order \(m\), which again forces \(t=0\).

In odd characteristic \(\ell\), each nonzero index \(a\) has exact additive order \(m>1\) for one divisor \(m\mid n\). The unit subgroup \(H_m\) generated by multiplication by \(2\) and \(-1\) acts freely on the indices of order \(m\): a unit fixing \(a\) is congruent to one modulo \(m\). Since there are \(\varphi(m)\) such indices, there are \(\varphi(m)/|H_m|\) orbits. Within an orbit the values are prescribed by \(2\mapsto2\in K^\times\) and \(-1\mapsto-1\in K^\times\). This assignment is consistent exactly when it respects every relation in \(H_m\). With \(h_m=\operatorname{ord}_m(2)\), if \(-1\notin\langle2\rangle\), the sole nontrivial scalar consistency condition is \(2^{h_m}=1\) in \(K\). If \(-1\in\langle2\rangle\), its unique occurrence in that cyclic subgroup is \(2^{h_m/2}\), and consistency is exactly \(2^{h_m/2}=-1\) in \(K\). A consistent orbit supplies one independent parameter; an inconsistent one forces its parameter to zero. Summation gives the stated formula.

The derivation equations are a finite homogeneous linear system over \(K\). Field extension does not alter its nullity. Thus the dimension and commutativity proved over \(F\) descend to the original, possibly nonsplitting, field \(K\).

**Step 5: the first jump and an explicit certificate.** For odd \(n\in\{3,5,7,9\}\), every possible nontrivial consistency prime divides \(n\), so the theorem gives zero in the stated prime-to-\(n\) range. For \(n=11\), the cyclic subgroup generated by \(2\) has order \(10\), and \(2^5\equiv-1\pmod{11}\); since \(2^5+1=33\), characteristic three supplies exactly one orbit parameter. The displayed quadratic-character operator is nonzero. Direct substitution verifies Leibniz in characteristic three: its coefficient at \(e_k\) in \(D(e_i e_j)-D(e_i)e_j-e_iD(e_j)\) is
\[
\chi(k-2j+i)-\chi(2j-k-i)-\chi((k+i)/2-j),
\]
which is zero modulo three for all \(i,j,k\) modulo eleven. Equivalently, after Fourier transform its eigenvalues are proportional to \(\chi(a)\), and the two required relations hold because \(\chi(-1)=-1\) and \(\chi(2)=-1=2\) in characteristic three.

## Verification

Run \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) with a standard Python 3 interpreter. The script independently constructs the \(n^3\) families of coefficient equations for a derivation, calculates their rank by modular Gaussian elimination, and compares the resulting nullities with both a group-orbit consistency algorithm and an independently implemented divisor-sum formula for the theorem. It covers every pair with odd \(n\in\{3,5,7,9,11,13,15\}\), prime \(\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}\), and \(\ell\nmid n\). It separately checks the quadratic-character map by all \(11^3\) basis-level Leibniz coefficient tests in characteristic three. Successful output ends with \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\), \(\texttt{LEGENDRE_N11_P3_OK}\), and \(\texttt{ARITHMETIC_FORMULA_OK}\). Such finite tests are consistency checks, not a proof for unbounded \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work

Elhamdadi, Makhlouf, Silvestrov, and Zappala (2021) introduced the derivation problem for quandle algebras. Their general structure-constant criterion applies here; their Theorem 3.9 proves the odd-dihedral vanishing statement in characteristic zero, and Section 4.2 tabulates positive-characteristic cases of orders at most six. Those antecedents are not claimed as new. The present all-odd-order arithmetic dimension formula, abelian-Lie conclusion in positive characteristic, and the first prime-to-order exceptional derivation at order eleven go beyond the explicit scope of those results.

Sangare and Ta (2026) study idempotents, automorphisms, and commutator widths of these algebras, including the odd dihedral family; their motivating paper does not state the above derivation dimension formula. Elhamdadi, Ta, and Virgin (2026) developed a Fourier perspective for idempotents of quandle algebras; the Fourier transform itself is therefore not presented as new. The latter paper's complete text was not available for comparison. The derivation classification is a different invariant from idempotent and automorphism formulas, although related methods may yield equivalent formulations.

## Limitations

The theorem excludes characteristic dividing \(n\) and even \(n\), where the separable Fourier argument may fail or doubling of indices is not invertible. It neither classifies automorphism groups nor establishes a scheme-theoretic smoothness statement. Its arithmetic expression is a complete classification only for the specified subfamily. Previously unlocated equivalent derivation calculations remain a residual priority for independent literature review; absence in the searches made is not a proof of global novelty.
