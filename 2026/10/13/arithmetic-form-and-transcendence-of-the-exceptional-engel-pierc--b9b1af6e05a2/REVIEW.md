# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Ahn's normalization gives the Pierce series and identifies \(C\) with eventually consecutive tails. Splitting after a finite prefix reduces the claim to the tail \(T_m\). The beta integral is exact, integration by parts gives \(T_m=1-(m-1)T_{m-1}\), and the closed form with derangement numbers follows by induction. The \(e^{-1}\)-coefficient is \((-1)^{k+m}(m-1)!/P\neq0\), so transcendence follows from the classical transcendence of \(e\). The executable verifier checks the recurrence and representative prefix gluings but is not used as a substitute for the proof.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating full text defines \(C\), proves it countable dense, and identifies \(\Theta(\mathbb I)=\mathbb I\setminus C\), but does not give the arithmetic form or transcendence of \(C\). Exact web searches for “eventually consecutive” Pierce digits, derangements, \(e^{-1}\), and transcendence found the motivating paper and general Pierce references but no statement implying this theorem. Shallit's inspected 1984 paper concerns a different quadratic family. Varona's inspected 2017 theorem proves transcendence only for Pierce series generated by a specific nonlinear recurrence, which the consecutive-tail cumulative denominators do not satisfy in general. Indexed searches for the source identifier, eventual consecutiveness, and derangement / inverse-exponential aliases returned no close matching finding.

## Value
PASS. The result assigns arithmetic content to the exact countable dense set removed by Ahn's new homeomorphism: the exceptional points are not merely topologically exceptional but are all transcendental and admit a closed formula. The corollary that every algebraic irrational survives in the homeomorphic image is a natural structural consequence of the source theorem, not a finite table or arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest sources are Ahn's arXiv:2610.00946v1, Shallit's 1984 explicit Pierce-expansion paper, and Varona's 2017 transcendental Pierce-series theorem. The inclusion in \(\mathbb Q+\mathbb Q^{\times}e^{-1}\) is not claimed to characterize that whole affine set, nor are irrationality exponents or algebraic-independence properties asserted.
