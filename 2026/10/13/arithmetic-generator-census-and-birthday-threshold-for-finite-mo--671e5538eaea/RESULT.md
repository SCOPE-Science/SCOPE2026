# Arithmetic generator census and birthday threshold for finite monadic MV powers

## Finding

Let \(m,n\geq1\) and \(r\geq0\). Let \(\mathbf L_m=\{0,1/m,\ldots,m/m\}\) be the finite Łukasiewicz MV-chain, and give \(A_{m,n}=\mathbf L_m^n\) the coordinatewise MV operations together with global existential and universal operations. Given an ordered generating tuple \(\mathbf g=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\), define the **coordinate words**
\[
 w_i=(m g_1(i),\ldots,m g_r(i))\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}^{r},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,
\]
and the **arithmetic content** \(d(\mathbf g)=\gcd\bigl(m,\{m g_j(i):1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq r\}\bigr)\), with the empty-set convention \(d(\varnothing)=m\).

**Complete criterion.** The tuple generates every element of \(A_{m,n}\) if and only if its coordinate words are pairwise distinct and \(d(\mathbf g)=1\).

**Exact census.** If \(N_{m,n,r}\) counts *ordered* generating tuples, and \((x)_{\underline n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)\) is zero for nonnegative integer \(x<n\), then
\[
 \boxed{N_{m,n,r}=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)\left((m/d+1)^r\right)_{\underline n}.}
\]
Here \(\mu\) is the classical number-theoretic Möbius function.

**Sharp generator rank.** The least number of algebra generators is
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{rank}(A_{m,n})=
 \begin{cases}
 \lceil\log_2 n\rceil,&m=1,\\
 \max\{1,\lceil\log_{m+1} n\rceil\},&m\geq2.
 \end{cases}}
\]
In particular, for every \(m\geq2\), a single many-valued proposition generates all \((m+1)^n\) truth functions as long as \(1\leq n\leq m+1\); the exceptional Boolean singleton \(A_{1,1}\) is generated by the empty tuple.

**Random-generator threshold.** For fixed \(m\geq2\), sample the \(nr\) coordinates of the tuple independently and uniformly from \(\mathbf L_m\); put \(Q=(m+1)^r\). Along any sequence with \(n\to\infty\) and \(r\to\infty\),
\[
 \boxed{\Pr(\langle\mathbf g\rangle=A_{m,n})\longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}e^{-\lambda^2/2},& n/\sqrt Q\to\lambda\in[0,\infty),\\
 0,&n/\sqrt Q\to\infty.
 \end{cases}}
\]
Thus the worst-case existence threshold is about \(\log_{m+1}n\) generators, while uniform random generation has the sharp birthday-paradox scale \(2\log_{m+1}n\). These thresholds concern the specified finite functional monadic algebras, not all monadic MV-algebras.

## Assumptions and scope

The MV operations on \(\mathbf L_m\) are \(\neg a=1-a\) and \(a\oplus b=\min(1,a+b)\). Meets and truncated differences are their standard term operations. Both the given generators and generated terms are *functions on the same labeled \(n\)-point domain*. The monadic operations are
\[
 \exists f=\overline{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}f(i)},\qquad
 \forall f=\overline{\min_{1\leq i\leq n}f(i)},
\]
where an overbar denotes the corresponding constant function. The signature contains \(0\), and hence also \(1=\neg0\), so empty generation is meaningful. No constants for intermediate rational truth values are assumed. Labels matter in the census, and the enumeration is of ordered tuples, including tuples with coincident generator vectors.

## Proof

**1. Necessity of distinct words.** Terms in the coordinatewise MV operations preserve equality between two coordinates whenever all generators agree there. Each monadic operator has a constant output and thus also preserves this equality. Induction on terms shows that equal coordinate words force equal evaluations of *every* generated element. Since the full power separates any two distinct coordinates, all words must be distinct.

**2. Necessity of primitive arithmetic content.** Put \(d=d(\mathbf g)\). The subchain of rational values with numerators divisible by \(d\) contains \(0,1\) and every generator entry and is closed under \(\neg\), \(\oplus\), minimum and maximum. Applying either monadic operation extracts one of these already admissible values. Therefore every generated function takes values in \(\{0,d/m,2d/m,\ldots,1\}\). If \(d>1\), the constant \(1/m\) is absent, so full generation is impossible.

**3. Crisp interpolation at every chain point.** For each \(a\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), the continuous piecewise-integer-affine function
\[
 h_a(x)=\max\{0,1-m|x-a/m|\},\qquad 0\leq x\leq1,
\]
is a unary MV term function by the classical McNaughton theorem. Its restrictions to the finite chain satisfy \(h_a(b/m)=1\) for \(b=a\) and \(0\) for every other integer \(b\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Consequently, if \(r\geq1\) and the coordinate words are distinct, the term
\[
 e_i=\bigwedge_{j=1}^r h_{m g_j(i)}(g_j)
\]
is the crisp selector of coordinate \(i\): it has value \(1\) at \(i\) and \(0\) elsewhere. This is the only use of the nontrivial standard McNaughton representation theorem; the particular peak functions also admit direct finite-chain term searches.

**4. Extracting all truth constants.** For each generator entry \(g_j(i)\), the term \(\exists(e_i\wedge g_j)\) is the constant function with value \(g_j(i)\). Hence the generated algebra contains all constant values in the MV-subalgebra of \(\mathbf L_m\) generated by the scalar entries. In integer numerators, truncated subtraction \(x\ominus y=\max(0,x-y)\) implements the Euclidean algorithm. Together with repeated truncated sums, this proves that the scalar subalgebra generated by the entry numerators and \(m\) consists exactly of multiples of \(d(\mathbf g)\). When \(d(\mathbf g)=1\), all \(m+1\) truth constants are available. Given any target vector \(f\in A_{m,n}\), the term \(\bigvee_{i=1}^n(e_i\wedge\overline{f(i)})\) equals \(f\). This proves sufficiency for \(r\geq1\). If \(r=0\), only the two constant endpoint functions are generated; the criterion holds in this case as well, because it is true precisely for \(m=n=1\).

**5. Exact count and rank.** A generator tuple is an \(n\)-tuple of words in an alphabet of \((m+1)^r\) possibilities. The number with pairwise distinct words is the falling factorial \(((m+1)^r)_{\underline n}\). For each divisor \(d\mid m\), the requirement that all entry numerators are divisible by \(d\) restricts every letter to the \(m/d+1\) multiples of \(d\). Applying Möbius inversion to the common gcd of the entries and \(m\) gives the stated census. Necessity of distinct words implies \(n\leq(m+1)^r\). When \(r\geq1\) and this holds, choose \(n\) distinct words including \((1,0,\ldots,0)\); then the arithmetic content is \(1\). This proves the rank formula and its endpoint convention.

**6. Sharp random threshold.** There are \(Q=(m+1)^r\) equally likely coordinate words. Their birthday-collision-free probability is \((Q)_{\underline n}/Q^n\), understood as zero for \(n>Q\). The arithmetic failure event is contained in the union, over primes \(p\mid m\), of the events that all \(nr\) sampled numerators are multiples of \(p\). Thus
\[
 0\leq\frac{(Q)_{\underline n}}{Q^n}-\frac{N_{m,n,r}}{Q^n}
 \leq\sum_{p\mid m}\left(\frac{m/p+1}{m+1}\right)^{nr}.
\]
For fixed \(m\) the error tends to zero when \(n,r\to\infty\). If \(n/\sqrt Q\) has finite limit, expanding \(\sum_{a=0}^{n-1}\log(1-a/Q)\) yields \(-n(n-1)/(2Q)+O(n^3/Q^2)\), giving \(e^{-\lambda^2/2}\). If \(n/\sqrt Q\to\infty\), the elementary bound \((Q)_{\underline n}/Q^n\leq\exp(-n(n-1)/(2Q))\) (for \(n\leq Q\)) tends to zero. This finishes the asymptotic theorem.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` independently constructs generated subalgebras under \(\neg,\oplus,\exists,\forall\), tests the word-and-gcd criterion and Möbius census against exhaustive small cases, performs separate signature enumeration, checks unary peak terms in low-valued chains, and evaluates illustrative finite birthday probabilities. Its successful output is recorded in `VERIFICATION.md`. Exhaustive tests are finite checks, not a replacement for the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Castaño, Díaz Varela and Savoy (2024, arXiv:2408.04757v1), Example 2.1 and Theorems 2.9 and 2.16, establish the relevance of precisely these functional finite monadic MV powers in S5-modal Łukasiewicz semantics and quasivariety generation. **Generating a variety by a family of algebras is different from generating one specified algebra by an ordered tuple.** The inspected theorem/proof sections give the former, not the criterion, ordered-tuple count, rank or random threshold above.

McNaughton's classical term-function theorem, finite MV-chain arithmetic and the birthday principle are established ingredients, not claimed contributions. The exact census combines those ingredients with the *global quantifier's ability to extract individual truth constants*. This contrasts with Gödel-chain powers, where ordinary Gödel operations cannot synthesize missing intermediate values and coordinate separation depends on zero patterns instead of full truth values. The work does not claim a new interpolation theorem or a new probability principle.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns full finite function powers with both global extremum quantifiers. It does not classify arbitrary subalgebras of these powers, finite monadic MV-algebras without functional completeness, MV-algebras without the global quantifier, nor continuous-domain function powers. Its random asymptotic assumes fixed truth denominator \(m\) and independent uniform entries. The possibility of an antecedent in older finite MV generator literature has not been eliminated; one older subvariety-classification paper was located by abstract but not fully inspected.
