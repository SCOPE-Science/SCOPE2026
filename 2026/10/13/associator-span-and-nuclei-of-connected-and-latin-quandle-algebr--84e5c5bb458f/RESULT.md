# Associator span and nuclei of connected and Latin quandle algebras

## Finding

For every connected quandle \(Q\) and field \(k\), the quandle algebra \(A=k[Q]\) has associator span equal to its augmentation ideal \(I=\ker\varepsilon\). Consequently, its maximal associative quotient is \(k\). The left nucleus is \(kS\) if \(Q\) is finite and \(0\) if \(Q\) is infinite; here \(S=\sum_{x\in Q} e_x\). If \(Q\) is also Latin, both its middle and its right nuclei have the same description. In particular, all three nuclei coincide over any field, without assumptions on the field characteristic or the size of its prime subfield.

## Assumptions and scope

A quandle is a set \(Q\) with an operation \(x*y\) satisfying \(x*x=x\), bijectivity of each right translation \(R_y:x\mapsto x*y\), and right self-distributivity \((x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)\). It is **connected** when the group generated by all \(R_y\) acts transitively on \(Q\), and **Latin** when each left translation \(L_x:y\mapsto x*y\) is also bijective. No finiteness assumption is imposed unless stated explicitly. The algebra \(A\) is the direct-sum (finite-support) free \(k\)-module with basis \(e_x\) and product \(e_x e_y=e_{x*y}\). The augmentation \(\varepsilon:A\to k\) sums coefficients and satisfies \(\varepsilon(uv)=\varepsilon(u)\varepsilon(v)\).

The **left**, **middle**, and **right** nuclei are respectively the sets of \(u\in A\) for which \((u,v,w)=0\), \((v,u,w)=0\), and \((v,w,u)=0\) for all \(v,w\in A\), where \((u,v,w)=(uv)w-u(vw)\). We do not assert that the middle/right nuclei have the displayed form when \(Q\) is connected but non-Latin.

## Proof

**Associator span.** Since augmentation is multiplicative, every associator lies in \(I\). For arbitrary \(t,y\in Q\), right-translation bijectivity allows \(x\) with \(x*y=t\). Idempotence of \(y\) then gives
\[
(e_x,e_y,e_y)=e_{(x*y)*y}-e_{x*(y*y)}=e_{t*y}-e_t.
\]
Thus the vector space spanned by associators contains every edge difference \(e_{R_y(t)}-e_t\). The span also contains their inverse-edge differences, since \(R_y\) is bijective. By connectedness, a finite word in right translations and their inverses sends any \(s\) to any \(t\); telescoping the corresponding edge differences yields \(e_t-e_s\). These differences span \(I\) (for infinite \(Q\), each element still has finite support). Therefore the associator span equals \(I\), which is a two-sided ideal because \(\varepsilon\) is multiplicative. Any map from \(A\) into an associative \(k\)-algebra kills every associator, hence all of \(I\), and factors uniquely through \(A/I\cong k\). This is precisely the universal associative quotient.

**Left nucleus.** If \(u\) belongs to the left nucleus, substitute \(v=w=e_y\) to obtain \((u e_y)e_y=u(e_y e_y)=u e_y\), or \(R_y^2(u)=R_y(u)\). Since \(R_y\) permutes the basis, its extension to \(A\) is invertible, and hence \(R_y(u)=u\) for all \(y\). Transitivity forces all coefficients of \(u\) to be equal. With finite support this forces \(u=0\) for infinite \(Q\); with finite \(Q\) it yields \(u\in kS\). Conversely, on finite \(Q\) every \(R_y\) is a permutation, so \(S e_y=S\). Consequently \(S v=\varepsilon(v)S\) and \((S,v,w)=0\) for all \(v,w\).

**Right nucleus in the Latin case.** If \(u\) is in the right nucleus, set \(v=w=e_x\) to get \((e_xe_x)u=e_x(e_xu)\), equivalently \(L_x(u)=L_x^2(u)\). The Latin hypothesis makes \(L_x\) invertible; therefore \(L_x(u)=u\) for every \(x\). For any \(a,b\in Q\), right-translation bijectivity supplies \(x\) with \(x*a=b\), so the left translations act transitively on the basis indices. Again coefficient constancy implies \(u\in kS\) in the finite case and \(u=0\) in the infinite case. Conversely, for finite Latin \(Q\), \(e_x S=S\), so \(vS=\varepsilon(v)S\), showing that \(S\) belongs to the right nucleus.

**Middle nucleus in the Latin case.** Right self-distributivity makes each \(R_z\) an algebra automorphism. If \(u\) is in the middle nucleus, for every \(x,z\),
\[
(e_x u)e_z=e_x(u e_z)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(e_{x*z}-e_x)R_z(u)=0.
\]
Apply the inverse algebra automorphism \(R_z^{-1}\):
\[
(e_x-e_{R_z^{-1}(x)})u=0.
\]
Connectivity and telescoping along translation edges give \(Iu=0\). Conversely, \(Iu=0\) implies the middle nucleus identity by reversing these steps, so the middle nucleus equals the right annihilator \(\operatorname{Ann}_r(I)\). For Latin \(Q\), write \(u=\sum_{t\in Q}c_t e_t\). The equation \(Iu=0\) says \(e_xu=e_yu\) for all \(x,y\). Given any \(a,b\in Q\), choose \(x\) with \(x*a=b\), and compare the coefficient of \(e_b\) in \(e_xu=e_bu\). Bijectivity of \(L_x\), plus \(b*b=b\), yields precisely \(c_a=c_b\). Thus the coefficients of \(u\) are constant, giving \(u\in kS\) for finite \(Q\) and \(u=0\) for infinite \(Q\). For finite Latin \(Q\), \(e_xS=S\) shows \(IS=0\), so all \(kS\) belongs to the middle nucleus.

Therefore the three nuclei have the asserted form. Their intersection, the full nucleus, has the same form. There is no division by \(|Q|\), so the argument remains valid when the field characteristic divides \(|Q|\).

Run \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) using Python 3 with no third-party dependencies. The verifier reconstructs the multiplication tables of dihedral quandles of orders three and five, a four-element affine quandle, a nonmedial Latin Galkin quandle of order fifteen, and the connected non-Latin transposition-conjugacy quandle of order six. It checks the quandle properties, connectedness, and, for relevant cases, Latinity. It calculates the ranks of all associators and the nullities of the three nucleus condition matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). In all checked cases the associator rank equals one less than the quandle order, and the left-nucleus dimension equals one; for the Latin examples, the middle/right nuclei also have dimension one. The finite computations provide reproducibility checks, not a proof for arbitrary fields or infinite quandles, which is given above.

## Relationship to prior work

The systematic quandle-ring construction of Bardakov, Passi, and Singh (2019) supplies the foundational algebraic framework. Sangare and Ta (2026, arXiv:2608.01550v1) established the finite-Latin total-sum multiplication property in Proposition 3.3 and computed the **left nucleus** of odd dihedral quandle algebras in Proposition 4.7. Both are prior results and are not claimed as new. The contribution here is the exact associator-span/universal-associative-quotient theorem for **all connected quandles**, the left-nucleus theorem for general finite and infinite connected quandles, and the middle/right nucleus classification for the entire Latin class, including nonmedial examples. No earlier theorem establishing this complete combined statement was identified in the searched literature; absence from searches does not constitute proof of novelty.

## Limitations

The middle and right nuclei are not classified for arbitrary connected non-Latin quandles. Every quandle-algebra element has finite support, so the formal total basis sum does not exist as an element for infinite \(Q\). In positive characteristic dividing the order of a finite quandle, \(S\) may itself lie in the augmentation ideal; this does not invalidate the nucleus statements. Relevant older treatments of quandle rings and recent related manuscripts may use different terminology for equivalent structural conclusions. No independent audit has been performed.
