# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A Lie endomorphism maps the commutator generator to a scalar multiple. Idempotence splits into the identity and central-killing maps whose quotient component is a projection onto an isotropic image. Central shears remove the optional linear central functional. The stabilizer action on the image–kernel decomposition reduces to the restricted alternating rank on the kernel; a symplectic splitting proves completeness in all characteristics. Counting isotropic subspaces, complementary graphs, alternating forms of each rank, and compatible central functionals yields each orbit size. Exact small-field enumeration and explicit automorphism-generated orbit traversal corroborate the formula. The finite tests are not treated as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** Searches for Heisenberg retractions, Lie-idempotent conjugacy, finite-field endomorphisms, paired isotropic spaces and symplectic complements did not identify the exact all-prime-power orbit-size census. The closest broad canonical-form theorem (Sergeichuk, 1990) likely encompasses the paired-subspace normal-form input for odd characteristic; that input is not presented as intrinsically original. Lorand–Weinstein (2015) classify two isotropic spaces, which does not cover the present generally nonisotropic kernel. Rojas (2012) treats faithful representations, and related fixed-point/commutativity literature does not count idempotent Lie endomorphism orbits. An older specialized retraction count with equivalent parameters may exist: literature search is not novelty proof.

**Value — PASS.** Idempotent endomorphisms are precisely retractions onto Lie subalgebras together with specified kernels. Their complete automorphism-orbit census distinguishes the rank of the complementary symplectic restriction, not just the dimension of the image. The exact class sizes and total count hold for every rank and every finite characteristic, including characteristic two not covered by classical odd-characteristic paired-subspace canonical forms. This is a natural complete classification of a structural Lie-algebra invariant rather than an arbitrary small parameter slice.

**Limits and source overlap.** The proof uses classical alternating-form congruence theory and symplectic Grassmannian counting. The geometric subtype may follow from broader paired-subspace classifications, but the finite-field Lie-retraction multiplicity census is not directly supplied by the inspected sources. Primary comparisons include arXiv:1206.5867, arXiv:1503.00169, and Sergeichuk's author-hosted paper. Finite checks cover only small prime fields. Bibliographic priority is not independently established.
