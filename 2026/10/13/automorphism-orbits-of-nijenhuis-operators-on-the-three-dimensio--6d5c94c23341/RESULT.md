# Automorphism orbits of Nijenhuis operators on the three-dimensional null-filiform algebra

## Finding

Let \(A\) be the three-dimensional null-filiform associative algebra with basis
\[
\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}
\]
and multiplication
\[
e_1^2=e_2,\qquad e_1e_2=e_2e_1=e_3,
\]
with all omitted products zero.

A linear map \(N:A\to A\) is Nijenhuis when
\[
N(x)N(y)
=
N\!\left(xN(y)+N(x)y-N(xy)\right)
\]
for all \(x,y\in A\).

Using the convention that matrix columns are the coordinates of the images of the basis vectors, every Nijenhuis operator is uniquely
\[
\boxed{
N_{a,b,c,d}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&0&0\\
b&a&0\\
c&d&a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a,b,c,d\in K.
}
\]

Every algebra automorphism is
\[
\boxed{
\phi_{u,v,w}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u&0&0\\
v&u^2&0\\
w&2uv&u^3
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u\in K^\times,\quad v,w\in K.
}
\]

Conjugation acts by
\[
\boxed{
(a,b,c,d)
\longmapsto
\left(
a,\,
ub,\,
u^2c+v(2b-d),\,
ud
\right).
}
\]

Over \(\mathbb C\), this gives a complete and nonredundant set of orbit representatives.

First, the scalar operators are
\[
\boxed{
S_a=aI_3.
}
\]

Second, the nonzero operators with \(b=d=0\) have representatives
\[
\boxed{
T_a=aI_3+E_{31}.
}
\]

Third, if \((b,d)\ne(0,0)\) and
\[
2b-d\ne0,
\]
there is exactly one orbit for each projective direction
\[
[b:d]\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)\setminus\{[1:2]\}.
\]
A convenient set of representatives is
\[
\boxed{
G_{a,t}
=
aI_3+E_{21}+tE_{32},
\qquad
t\in\mathbb C\setminus\{2\},
}
\]
together with
\[
\boxed{
G_{a,\infty}=aI_3+E_{32}.
}
\]

Finally, on the exceptional projective direction
\[
d=2b,\qquad b\ne0,
\]
the quantity
\[
\boxed{
\tau=\frac{c}{b^2}
}
\]
is invariant, and the representatives are
\[
\boxed{
H_{a,\tau}
=
aI_3+E_{21}+\tau E_{31}+2E_{32},
\qquad
\tau\in\mathbb C.
}
\]

Thus \(d=2b\) is a genuine symmetry-jump locus. Away from it, the shear parameter \(v\) removes \(c\); on it, the shear action on \(c\) vanishes and an extra affine modulus survives.

The stabilizer dimensions over \(\mathbb C\) make the jump explicit:
\[
\dim\operatorname{Stab}(S_a)=3,\qquad
\dim\operatorname{Stab}(T_a)=2,
\]
\[
\dim\operatorname{Stab}(G_{a,t})=
\dim\operatorname{Stab}(G_{a,\infty})=1,
\qquad
\dim\operatorname{Stab}(H_{a,\tau})=2.
\]

The same equations are valid over every field. In particular, over \(\mathbf F_q\) the set of Nijenhuis operators has exactly
\[
\boxed{q^4}
\]
elements. The number of automorphism-conjugacy classes is
\[
\boxed{
q\bigl(2q+1+\gcd(2,q-1)\bigr).
}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
q(2q+3)
}
\]
for odd \(q\), and
\[
\boxed{
q(2q+2)
}
\]
for even \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope

The complex orbit classification concerns the algebra denoted \(A_2\) in Abdujabborov--Karimjanov--Kodirova. Their convention writes the coordinate row of each \(N(e_i)\) as a row of the displayed matrix; transposing their \(A_2\) table gives the column convention used here.

The finite-field extension concerns the same multiplication law after base change to \(\mathbf F_q\). The proof does not use algebraic closure.

The equivalence relation is conjugacy by automorphisms of the fixed associative algebra:
\[
N\sim \phi N\phi^{-1}.
\]

No claim is made about the other three-dimensional nilpotent associative algebras in the source.

## Proof

Write a general linear map in column convention as
\[
N=
\begin{pmatrix}
x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It is enough to impose the Nijenhuis identity on all ordered pairs of basis vectors.

From the pair \((e_3,e_3)\), the \(e_2\)-coordinate gives
\[
x_{13}^2=0,
\]
hence
\[
x_{13}=0.
\]

With \(x_{13}=0\), the pair \((e_1,e_3)\) gives
\[
x_{23}^2=0,
\]
so
\[
x_{23}=0.
\]

Then \((e_2,e_2)\) gives
\[
x_{12}^2=0,
\]
hence
\[
x_{12}=0.
\]

The \(e_2\)-coordinate of the \((e_1,e_1)\) identity now reduces to
\[
(x_{11}-x_{22})^2=0,
\]
so
\[
x_{22}=x_{11}.
\]

Finally, the \(e_3\)-coordinate of the \((e_1,e_2)\) identity becomes
\[
(x_{11}-x_{33})^2=0,
\]
giving
\[
x_{33}=x_{11}.
\]

No equation remains on
\[
x_{21},\qquad x_{31},\qquad x_{32}.
\]
Putting
\[
a=x_{11},\qquad b=x_{21},\qquad c=x_{31},\qquad d=x_{32}
\]
gives exactly \(N_{a,b,c,d}\). Direct substitution verifies that every such matrix is Nijenhuis.

For automorphisms, the algebra is generated by \(e_1\). If
\[
\phi(e_1)=u e_1+v e_2+w e_3,
\]
then multiplicativity forces
\[
\phi(e_2)=\phi(e_1)^2=u^2e_2+2uv\,e_3
\]
and
\[
\phi(e_3)=\phi(e_1)^3=u^3e_3.
\]
The map is invertible exactly when
\[
u\ne0.
\]
This gives the displayed automorphism group.

Direct matrix multiplication yields
\[
\phi_{u,v,w}
N_{a,b,c,d}
\phi_{u,v,w}^{-1}
=
N_{a,\,ub,\,u^2c+v(2b-d),\,ud}.
\]

The scalar \(a\) is therefore an invariant, and the projective direction
\[
[b:d]
\]
is invariant whenever \((b,d)\ne(0,0)\).

If
\[
b=d=0,
\]
then
\[
c\longmapsto u^2c.
\]
Over \(\mathbb C\), this gives the scalar orbit \(c=0\) and one nonzero orbit represented by \(c=1\).

Suppose next that
\[
(b,d)\ne(0,0)
\]
and
\[
2b-d\ne0.
\]
After a scalar normalization of \((b,d)\), choose \(v\) to solve
\[
u^2c+v(2b-d)=0.
\]
Thus \(c\) can be removed uniquely once the projective representative is fixed. This gives the \(G\)-families.

On the exceptional line
\[
d=2b,
\]
necessarily \(b\ne0\). Normalizing \(b\) to \(1\) also normalizes \(d\) to \(2\), and the shear parameter \(v\) disappears from the transformation of \(c\). Since
\[
b\longmapsto ub,\qquad c\longmapsto u^2c,
\]
the ratio
\[
c/b^2
\]
is invariant. This gives the \(H\)-family and proves uniqueness.

For the finite-field count, fix \(a\in\mathbf F_q\).

If
\[
b=d=0,
\]
the action on \(c\) is multiplication by nonzero squares. Hence this stratum contributes
\[
1+\gcd(2,q-1)
\]
orbits: one zero orbit and \(\gcd(2,q-1)\) nonzero square classes.

There are
\[
q+1
\]
projective directions \([b:d]\). Exactly one is exceptional:
\[
[1:2].
\]
Each of the remaining \(q\) directions contributes one orbit because \(c\) is removable.

On the exceptional direction, after normalizing \(b=1\), the invariant
\[
\tau=c/b^2
\]
runs through all \(q\) field elements. Thus this direction contributes \(q\) orbits.

For each fixed \(a\), the total number is
\[
1+\gcd(2,q-1)+q+q
=
2q+1+\gcd(2,q-1).
\]
Multiplying by the \(q\) choices of \(a\) proves the formula.

## Verification

The included program `artifacts/verify.py` reconstructs the Nijenhuis equations directly from the multiplication table.

It verifies, without assuming the published table, that every solution over a field is of the form
\[
N_{a,b,c,d}.
\]

It independently derives the automorphism matrix from the image of \(e_1\), symbolically computes
\[
\phi N\phi^{-1},
\]
and checks the stated parameter action.

For finite fields it exhaustively enumerates all \(q^4\) Nijenhuis operators and the effective automorphism action for
\[
q=2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7.
\]
The observed orbit counts are respectively
\[
12,\ 27,\ 65,\ 119,
\]
which equal
\[
q\bigl(2q+1+\gcd(2,q-1)\bigr).
\]

The program returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite-field enumeration is used only as a replay check; the formula is proved symbolically.

## Relationship to prior work

Abdujabborov, Karimjanov, and Kodirova classify Rota-type operators on all three-dimensional nilpotent complex associative algebras. Their \(A_2\) multiplication is exactly the three-dimensional null-filiform law used here. Their Nijenhuis table gives the four-parameter raw family \(N_{a,b,c,d}\), but it does not quotient that family by the automorphism group.

Karimjanov, Kaygorodov, and Ladra treat null-filiform associative algebras in arbitrary dimension, but their Nijenhuis results are explicitly restricted to operators homogeneous for the natural grading. In degree zero they obtain only scalar operators, and the other theorems similarly impose homogeneity. Hence those results do not cover the full four-parameter family or its automorphism quotient.

Arzikulov, Karimjanov, and Umrzaqov determine the general automorphism form for null-filiform associative algebras. Specializing their formula to dimension three agrees with the direct automorphism calculation above, but their work concerns automorphisms, local automorphisms, and \(2\)-local automorphisms rather than Nijenhuis orbit spaces.

Focused searches for Nijenhuis operators on the three-dimensional null-filiform algebra, automorphism conjugacy, normal forms, and finite-field orbit counts did not locate the orbit decomposition or the exceptional \(d=2b\) symmetry jump.

## Limitations

The complex normal forms classify only the \(A_2\) Nijenhuis family, not the other nilpotent associative algebras in the 2021 table.

The finite-field formula is for conjugacy by algebra automorphisms of the fixed multiplication.

The existence of the raw four-parameter Nijenhuis family is known from the 2021 source; the new content assessed here is the automorphism quotient, its exceptional symmetry stratum, and the finite-field orbit count.

A specialized or unindexed source may contain an equivalent quotient calculation. Failed searches do not prove novelty.
