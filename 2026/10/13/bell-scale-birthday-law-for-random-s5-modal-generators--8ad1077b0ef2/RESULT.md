# Bell-scale birthday law for random S5 modal generators

## Finding

Let \(n\ge1\), and choose an equivalence relation \(R_n\) uniformly from all \(B_n\) equivalence relations on \([n]\). Independently, choose \(r\ge0\) propositional valuations uniformly, so that each world receives an independent, uniformly distributed word in an alphabet of size \(Q=2^r\). Interpret the ordinary S5 diamond by \(\Diamond U=\{x:\exists y\,(xR_ny\land y\in U)\}\). Write \(\mathcal A_n\) for the Boolean modal algebra generated by the valuations.

**Exact finite census.** For every \(n\ge1\) and every integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\Pr\bigl(\mathcal A_n=\mathcal P([n])\bigr)
=\frac{n!}{B_n Q^n}[z^n]\prod_{s=1}^{\min(n,Q)}(1+z^s)^{\binom Qs}.
\]
For any fixed equivalence relation with \(c_s\) classes of size \(s\), the exact number of ordered generating \(r\)-tuples is
\[
\prod_{s\ge1}(s!)^{c_s}\bigl(\tbinom Qs\bigr)_{\underline{c_s}},
\]
where a falling factorial with too many factors is zero.

**Sharp probabilistic law.** Let \(w_n>0\) solve \(w_ne^{w_n}=n\), and allow \(r=r_n\) to vary. If \(Q_n/(n w_n)\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), then the number \(Z_n\) of unordered distinct-world pairs satisfying the same ordinary modal formulas obeys
\[
Z_n\ \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}\ \operatorname{Poisson}\!\left(\frac1{2\lambda}\right),
\qquad
\Pr\bigl(\mathcal A_n=\mathcal P([n])\bigr)\longrightarrow e^{-1/(2\lambda)}.
\]
If \(Q_n/(n w_n)\to0\), the generation probability tends to zero; if this ratio tends to infinity, it tends to one. In particular, the random-generation scale is \(Q\asymp n\log n\), or \(r\approx\log_2(n\log n)\). The limit is asserted only along sequences of allowable powers of two with the stated ratio; no continuous interpolation of \(r\) is assumed.

## Assumptions and scope

The frame is chosen uniformly **among labeled equivalence relations**, not by selecting a random graph edge independently. The propositional words at distinct worlds are independent and uniform on all \(Q\) available words. Relations are reflexive, symmetric, and transitive; \(\Diamond\) sees the whole equivalence class, including the current world. Terms use Boolean operations and the ordinary, nongraded S5 diamond; there are no world-naming constants. The counts include ordered proposition tuples, and \(B_n\) is the \(n\)-th Bell number. The empty tuple of propositions is permitted.

## Proof

**1. A depth-one normal form.** For a Boolean word \(a\in\{0,1\}^r\), let \(\chi_a\) be its propositional characteristic formula. For each world \(x\), write \(c(x)\) for its word and \(S(x)=\{c(y):yR_nx\}\) for its class palette. Every Boolean/modal term is constant on worlds having the same pair \((c(x),S(x))\): a Boolean operation preserves this property; and \(\Diamond\varphi\) depends only on which palette colors in the class satisfy \(\varphi\), hence only on the class palette. Conversely, the depth-one formula
\[
\chi_a\ \land\ \bigwedge_{b\in S}\Diamond\chi_b\ \land\ \bigwedge_{b\notin S}\neg\Diamond\chi_b
\]
defines precisely the worlds with signature \((a,S)\), for any feasible \(a\in S\subseteq\{0,1\}^r\). Therefore **arbitrary modal nesting does not refine** the partition defined by these depth-one signatures. Full generation is equivalent to all these signatures being distinct.

Within a fixed equivalence class, distinct worlds have the same palette, so their signatures are different if and only if their words are different. Under this condition, two different equivalence classes contribute a common signature precisely when their palettes coincide. Thus full generation occurs exactly when every class has pairwise different colors and all classes have distinct palettes. In particular, classes of different sizes cannot share a palette when the first condition holds.

**2. Exact census.** For a fixed partition with \(c_s\) blocks of cardinality \(s\), assign distinct size-\(s\) subsets of the \(Q\)-letter palette to its ordered blocks; there are \((\binom Qs)_{\underline{c_s}}\) ways. Then biject the \(s\) vertices in each block to its selected colors, in \(s!\) ways. Multiplying over sizes yields the conditional enumeration.

Alternatively, selecting the palettes used by all blocks corresponds to selecting a squarefree family of nonempty subsets of the \(Q\) letters, with total size \(n\). The coefficient of \(z^n\) in \(\prod_s(1+z^s)^{\binom Qs}\) counts these families. Ordering the resulting \(n\) colored positions and bijecting them to the \(n\) labeled worlds contributes \(n!\), yielding exactly the number of pairs consisting of a labeled equivalence relation and a successful labeled coloring. Divide by \(B_nQ^n\).

**3. Pair density of a uniform random partition.** Let
\[
H_n=\sum_{C\in[n]/R_n}\binom{|C|}{2},\qquad
T_n=\sum_{C\in[n]/R_n}\binom{|C|}{3}.
\]
For any prescribed distinct vertices, forcing \(j\) identifications gives \(B_{n-j}\) partitions. Hence the exact factorial moments are
\[
\mathbb EH_n=\binom n2\frac{B_{n-1}}{B_n},\quad
\mathbb E(H_n)_{\underline2}=\binom n2\left(\binom n2-1\right)\frac{B_{n-2}}{B_n},\quad
\mathbb ET_n=\binom n3\frac{B_{n-2}}{B_n}.
\]
The classical Bell-number ratio asymptotics, for fixed \(j=1,2\), state \(B_{n-j}/B_n\sim(w_n/n)^j\), where \(w_ne^{w_n}=n\). It follows by computing the variance from the displayed moments that
\[
\frac{H_n}{nw_n}\xrightarrow{\Pr}\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_C|C|^3=O(nw_n^2).
\]
Indeed \(\sum_C|C|^3=n+6H_n+6T_n\).

**4. Conditional birthday limit.** Conditional on the random partition, let \(X_n\) count pairs of vertices belonging to the same class and having the same color. It is a sum of \(H_n\) indicators, each with probability \(1/Q_n\). Events for disjoint vertex pairs are independent, so the indicators possess a dependency graph whose edges join pairs sharing a vertex. The Stein--Chen bound for dependency graphs implies
\[
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(\mathcal L(X_n\mid R_n),\operatorname{Poisson}(H_n/Q_n)\right)
\le \frac{C}{Q_n^2}\sum_{C\in[n]/R_n}|C|^3
\]
for a universal finite constant \(C\) (a factor at most one from the usual bound can be dropped). At the critical scale, taking expectations makes the right side \(O(1/n)\). Together with \(H_n/Q_n\to1/(2\lambda)\) in probability, this yields \(X_n\Rightarrow\operatorname{Poisson}(1/(2\lambda))\).

There remains the possibility that two distinct equivalence classes have the same palette, causing cross-class indistinguishability. Let \(I_n\) be the number of singleton classes. For two classes of sizes \(1\) and \(1\), palette equality has probability \(1/Q_n\); for a pair of classes of sizes \(s\le t\) with \(t\ge2\), the probability of palette equality is at most \((s/Q_n)^t\le st/Q_n^2\), provided \(Q_n\ge n\). Thus, by a union bound,
\[
\Pr(\text{two classes share a palette})
\le \frac{\mathbb E\binom{I_n}{2}}{Q_n}+\frac{n^2}{2Q_n^2}.
\]
Two specified vertices are singleton classes with probability \(B_{n-2}/B_n\), so \(\mathbb E\binom{I_n}{2}=\binom n2B_{n-2}/B_n=O(w_n^2)\). At or above the critical scale this bound tends to zero. On its complement, \(Z_n=X_n\); hence the Poisson limit for \(Z_n\), and the formula for generation probability because full generation is equivalent to \(Z_n=0\).

**5. Both sides of the threshold.** If \(Q_n/(nw_n)\to\infty\), Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(X_n>0)\le\mathbb EH_n/Q_n\to0\), while the preceding cross-class bound also tends to zero. If \(Q_n/(nw_n)\to0\), the conditional probability that every class is internally injectively colored is at most \(\exp(-H_n/Q_n)\) (zero whenever some class is larger than \(Q_n\)). Because \(H_n/(nw_n)\to1/2\) in probability, this probability tends to zero; full generation is a subevent.

## Relationship to prior work

Sliusarev studies uniformly random labeled S5 frames and almost-sure **frame validities**. His finite S5 models are uniformly distributed equivalence relations, as in the present setup, but the inspected manuscript does not address independent random valuations, the probability that they generate every singleton, or a Poisson law for pairs with identical modal theories. Shekarriz and collaborators study distinguishing graph colorings and disconnected graph distinguishing thresholds; these provide the relevant symmetry-breaking language, but the inspected article does not contain the combined random-partition/Bell-scale model or the present limit theorem. Ahmadi, Alinaghipour and Shekarriz (2020) introduce enumerative distinguishing-coloring indices and a coloring-partition perspective. Those general indices could subsume portions of the finite fixed-frame census, and are acknowledged as antecedents. General birthday collision and Bell-number ratio tools are classical and are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The random relation is sampled uniformly from all labeled equivalence relations and not from independently generated edges, exchangeable paintbox partitions, or weighted block-size models. The propositional color distribution is uniform; nonuniform distributions may yield different thresholds. The modal language has only one, nongraded S5 accessibility modality. The exact finite census and asymptotics are proved under these conditions. Independent mathematical and originality audit has not been performed. The distinguishability criterion and elementary counting ingredients could have older precursors in graph symmetry-breaking; the claim to originality concerns the combined S5-generation model, exact colored-Bell coefficient and Bell-scale Poisson threshold.
