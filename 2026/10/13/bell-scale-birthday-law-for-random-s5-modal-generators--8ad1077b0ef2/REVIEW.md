# Scientific review: Bell-scale birthday law for random S5 modal generators

**Correctness — PASS (self-review).** The criterion that depth-one signatures consist of local color plus the containing class's palette is exact for a finite equivalence relation. Local injectivity plus distinct class palettes characterizes the singleton atoms of the generated algebra. Two independent counts give the coefficient formula. The exact Bell ratios for prescribed identifications and the standard Bell asymptotic imply concentration of the number of intra-class pairs; Stein--Chen gives the Poisson limit, and cross-class palette equalities are negligible via a separate union bound. Both threshold extremes follow from Markov and conditional birthday inequalities. Exhaustive finite enumeration and direct closures were run, without confusing them with asymptotic proof.

**Originality — PASS (self-review).** Sliusarev's arXiv:2406.10770 treats almost-sure *validity of formulas on frames*, not generation of all singleton propositions by random valuations; the full inspected HTML S5 section makes this distinction explicit. The full text of *Distinguishing threshold of graphs* (DOI:10.1002/jgt.22923) studies symmetry-breaking colorings and disconnected-graph thresholds, not the Bell-uniform random-frame probability or Poisson critical window. Ahmadi--Alinaghipour--Shekarriz (arXiv:1910.12102), Theorem 2.3, already contains a two-part palette inequality count, which is credited as a structural precursor. Relevant indexed research on independent occupancy and equality-modal semantics does not imply this combined result. Existing graph-coloring enumeration might imply the finite palette factor without matching its modal or random-partition presentation. An equivalent historical result on random automorphism-breaking colorings remains a residual originality risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS (self-review).** The natural finite S5 model generator problem has an exact finite solution and a nontrivial random threshold. Uniformly random equivalence relations introduce \(\Theta(n\log n)\) same-block pairs; the relevant palette scale is not the usual all-pairs birthday scale. The Poisson law identifies the complete critical transition, while the coefficient makes finite comparison reproducible and yields a sharp depth-one expressive characterization.

**Limitations.** Only one S5 modality, uniform labeled equivalence relations and independent uniform propositional words are treated. Standard Bell saddle-point estimates and a dependency-graph Poisson bound are used as known results. The computational checks are finite. Possible older work on color-distinguishing unions of cliques was not exhaustively ruled out.
