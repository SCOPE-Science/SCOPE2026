# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The metric parameterization follows by conjugating the intertwining equation into the simple real eigenbasis, where commutation with a diagonal matrix forces a positive diagonal matrix. The condition-number identity follows from the equality of the nonzero spectra of the two Gram products. The pairwise lower bound uses only metric orthogonality and the extremal eigenvalue inequalities. The two-dimensional optimum follows from a one-variable diagonal scaling and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. The checker independently reconstructs the algebra on representative finite instances.

## Originality
PASS. The 2006 two-level paper supplies the complete metric family but does not optimize its spectral condition number. The 2007 paper supplies general quasi-Hermitian metric structure. The 2018 “minimally anisotropic” paper optimizes Hilbert-Schmidt distance and explicitly separates that choice from other possible objectives; it does not state the diagonal-Gram condition-number reduction, universal pairwise bound, or the exact two-dimensional spectral-condition optimum. The 2012 Riesz-basis paper supplies qualitative motivation for metric singularity but not this finite-dimensional quantitative bound. Focused published-finding corpus and literature searches for condition-number, eigenvector-overlap, diagonal-scaling, and best-conditioned-metric formulations found no implication-equivalent result. Residual risk from older Riesz-basis or matrix-scaling literature is retained.

## Value
PASS. Metric nonuniqueness is a central practical issue in quasi-Hermitian quantum mechanics, and spectral condition number is a scale-invariant measure of the worst distortion between the reference and physical norms. The theorem turns that choice into a standard diagonal Gram-scaling problem in arbitrary finite dimension and gives an unavoidable pairwise obstruction. The exact two-level law quantifies the blow-up forced by eigenvector coalescence, complementing qualitative exceptional-point and singular-metric discussions.

The closest literature optimizes a different, normalization-dependent Hilbert-Schmidt objective. The result does not claim the known metric family, Riesz-basis criterion, or qualitative singularity mechanism as new, and it does not extend to defective matrices or infinite-dimensional unbounded metrics.
