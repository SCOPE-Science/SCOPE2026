# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The necessary strict interval-support chain follows directly from Bonzio–Pra Baldi, Theorem 3.15; finite Boolean intervals have power-of-two sizes with strictly decreasing exponents. The converse is a concrete restriction-system construction satisfying Theorem 3.17; the canonical Boolean action proves uniqueness and identifies the exact automorphism stabilizer. The rigid-size characterization follows from the vanishing of all nontrivial symmetric factors. Checks reconstruct the full Bochvar operations for the smallest ranks, and enumerate the Boolean-support systems through rank seven.

**Originality — PASS, with a material caveat.** The 2023 representation source does not state a binary cardinality normal form or the automorphism/rigidity profile. The 2018 linearly indexed involutive-bisemilattice enumeration studies the strictly larger reduct category, not Bochvar expansions. The later arXiv:2412.14911 equivalence of Bochvar algebras with Boolean/meet-subsemilattice pairs is broader structural machinery and may make the claimed finite-chain invariants a relatively immediate *unstated corollary*. The explicitly determined fine spectrum and group profile were not found in the inspected manuscripts, indexed results, targeted web searches; residual unindexed/folklore overlap is possible.

**Value — PASS.** The natural, previously studied linearly indexed Płonka subclass has an unexpectedly rigid classification once Bochvar external operations are imposed. The cardinality alone recovers the fiber profile, full isomorphism type, automorphism group and exact rigid sizes; by contrast, counting the analogous involutive-bisemilattice reducts is substantially more involved. This is a concise finite classification rather than an independent new representation theory.

**Scope and limits.** Every cardinality includes the trivial one-element algebra; change to sizes at least two when excluding it. This result does not classify nonchain-indexed Bochvar algebras. Exhaustive computer checks are finite and do not supply an independent audit of the theorem.
