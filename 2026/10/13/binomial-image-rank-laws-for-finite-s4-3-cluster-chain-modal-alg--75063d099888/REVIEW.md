# Mathematical review: finite S4.3 cluster-chain rank laws

## Correctness — PASS

The claim defines one precise frame class and two ranked classes of endomorphisms. The proof checks the back/forth identity at every successor cone, so there is no silent replacement by ordinary monotone maps. The last-block surjectivity lemma is derived in both directions, the product factors count independent choices, and the idempotent case follows by fixing the full terminal suffix. The algebraic image cardinality is correctly distinguished from the world-image cardinality. Direct brute-force function enumeration covers 65 finite cluster profiles and 3,192,243 candidates; comparisons agree with both polynomial families, and direct modal-algebra operation checks cover selected small profiles. The general result depends on the proof, not an extrapolation from those computations.

## Originality — PASS, with stated uncertainty

The arXiv preprint *A non-uniform view of Craig interpolation in modal logics with linear frames* (Kurucz, Wolter, Zakharyaschev; 2023) uses the ordered-cluster architecture but addresses interpolant existence. Its accessible full text was examined at the principal structural sections and searched for bounded morphisms and endomorphisms, with no equivalent rank census found. Thomason's 1975 frame/modal-algebra duality is background; it does not supply the specific enumerations. Bull's 1966 finite-model-property theorem concerns logics rather than counts of concrete morphisms. Existing work on weak-order **poset** maps or distinct modal-frame geometries does not imply this cluster-preorder bounded-map theorem. An unindexed semigroup description of the same maps could nevertheless be an antecedent; missing full older articles are not regarded as proof of absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Chains of reflexive clusters are the smallest natural finite S4.3 families beyond strict chains and individual equivalence clusters. The result settles the full arbitrary-profile census and exhibits a nonobvious probability law: for equal cluster sizes, bounded-morphism image length is shifted binomial and idempotents are exponentially rare. The exact last-block surjection condition is the mechanism that distinguishes these morphisms from arbitrary monotone finite-space maps. The claim is a natural quantitative classification, not merely a new name for Stirling numbers.

## Limitations and sources

The theorem does not cover finite K4.3 frames with irreflexive singleton clusters, nonlinearly ordered clusters, or infinite descriptive frames. Counts are of algebraic images under inverse image, with exponents that are powers of two. None of the computations or source searches is an independent mathematical audit.
