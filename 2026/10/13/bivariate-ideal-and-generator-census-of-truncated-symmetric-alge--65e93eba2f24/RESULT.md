# Bivariate ideal and generator census of truncated symmetric algebras

## Finding
For each prime power \(q\) and each integer \(d\geq1\), let
\[
A=\mathbb F_q[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1,\ldots,x_d)^3,
\qquad h=\binom{d+1}{2}.
\]
For an ideal \(I\) let \(\mu(I)\) be the least number of generators as an \(A\)-module. In particular \(\mu(0)=0\), while \(\mu(A)=1\). Define the complete joint ideal enumerator
\[
F_{d,q}(X,Y)=\sum_{I\triangleleft A}X^{\dim_{\mathbb F_q} I}Y^{\mu(I)}.
\]
Writing \({n\brack k}_q\) for the Gaussian binomial coefficient, and \(b_r=\binom{d-r+1}{2}\), one has
\[
\boxed{F_{d,q}(X,Y)=X^{1+d+h}Y+\sum_{r=0}^{d}{d\brack r}_q
\sum_{j=0}^{b_r}{b_r\brack j}_q q^{r(b_r-j)}X^{r+h-b_r+j}Y^{r+j}.}
\]
Thus the formula simultaneously determines the number of ideals of every vector-space dimension and every minimal-generator number. It includes every nonhomogeneous ideal, all finite characteristics, and the zero and unit ideals. The number of nonzero proper principal ideals is
\[
{h\brack1}_q+{d\brack1}_q q^{\binom d2},
\]
while the unit ideal supplies one further principal ideal. For \(d=2\), \(q=2\), the full polynomial is
\[
1+7XY+7X^2Y^2+6X^3Y+X^3Y^3+3X^4Y^2+X^5Y^2+X^6Y.
\]
In particular, even at the same dimension \(3\), the count distinguishes six principal ideals from the unique three-generated ideal.

## Assumptions and scope
The algebra is commutative, associative, unital, finite-dimensional, and split over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) satisfies \(\mathfrak m^3=0\). As vector spaces, \(\mathfrak m=V\oplus W\), where \(V\) is spanned by the linear monomials and \(W=\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\) by the quadratic monomials; \(\dim V=d\) and \(\dim W=h\). Multiplication on \(\mathfrak m\) is \((v,w)(v',w')=(0,vv')\). Every proper ideal belongs to \(\mathfrak m\), since an element with nonzero constant term is a unit.

The family is the free commutative split local length-three truncation on \(d\) generators. Every commutative split local \(K\)-algebra with radical cube zero and radical-mod-square dimension \(d\) is a quotient of its counterpart \(K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1,\ldots,x_d)^3\), by choosing lifts of a radical basis. The claim is only about ideals of the free truncation, not arbitrary quotients.

## Proof
Fix any proper ideal \(I\subseteq\mathfrak m\). Denote by \(U\leq V\) its projection under \(V\oplus W\to V\), and by \(C=I\cap W\) its central intersection. Let \(r=\dim U\). Since \(\mathfrak m I=VU\) (all higher products vanish), the ideal condition is exactly \(VU\subseteq C\). Conversely, any vector subspace \(I\leq V\oplus W\) satisfying this containment is an ideal: it is stable under multiplication by every generator in \(V\), hence by the algebra.

For given \(U,C\), the possible ideals form graphs of unique \(K\)-linear maps \(f:U\to W/C\): every graph is the inverse image in \(V\oplus W\) of the graph in \(V\oplus(W/C)\). This construction is bijective, with no choice of complement required. In particular, the number of lifts is \(q^{r\dim(W/C)}\).

Choose a complement \(U'\) of \(U\) in \(V\). Multiplying symmetric tensors yields the canonical quotient
\[
W/VU\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2(V/U),
\]
so \(\dim(W/VU)=b_r=\binom{d-r+1}{2}\). This holds in characteristic \(2\) as well, since monomials, rather than divided squares, constitute the basis. There are \({d\brack r}_q\) choices of \(U\). The intermediate spaces \(C\), with \(VU\subseteq C\subseteq W\), are in bijection with subspaces \(C/VU\leq W/VU\); choosing \(j=\dim(C/VU)\) gives \({b_r\brack j}_q\) choices. Then
\[
\dim C=h-b_r+j,\qquad \dim(W/C)=b_r-j,
\]
and consequently there are \(q^{r(b_r-j)}\) lifts, each with
\[
\dim I=r+h-b_r+j.
\]
Finally, the local Nakayama lemma gives
\[
\mu(I)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(I/\mathfrak m I).
\]
Here \(\dim(\mathfrak m I)=\dim(VU)=h-b_r\), whence \(\mu(I)=r+j\). Summing the disjoint parameters \((r,j)\) proves the double sum. The unique ideal outside \(\mathfrak m\) is \(A\), of dimension \(1+d+h\), generated by the unit, giving the first term.

For the principal-ideal corollary, \(\mu(I)=1\) forces \((r,j)=(0,1)\) or \((1,0)\) among proper ideals. These contribute \({h\brack1}_q\) central lines and \({d\brack1}_q q^{b_1}\) other ideals, where \(b_1=\binom d2\). Both are nonzero, and \(A\) is counted separately. This also proves the displayed specialization by direct substitution.

## Verification
The standalone file `check_ideals.py` requires Python 3 with no third-party packages. Run `python3 check_ideals.py`. It independently enumerates row-reduced echelon bases for every vector subspace of the radical, directly tests stability under multiplication by every linear generator, and computes the minimal number of generators by Gaussian elimination on the span of the products. It separately computes the formula from Gaussian coefficients. Full exhaustive comparisons were run for \((d,q)=(1,2),(1,3),(2,2),(2,3)\); the tool printed `VERIFY_OK`. The numbers of ideals in those cases are \(4,4,27,46\), respectively. Exhaustive finite checks support, but do not replace, the unrestricted bijective proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Short local algebras with cube-zero radical are studied in Ringel's `arXiv:2211.16885` (first posted 30 November 2022, primary MSC 16G10) and Ringel--Zhang `arXiv:1912.02081`. Their theorems address reflexive and Gorenstein-projective modules, with specific Hilbert types, not an all-ideal and minimal-generator counting polynomial for the free commutative truncation. The distinction is substantive: knowing the Hilbert type or the homological behavior of some ideals does not determine the count of all embedded ideals with their generator numbers. Existing ideal-zeta and finite-field submodule enumeration methods are related conceptually; a presently unidentified equivalent specialization remains a possible novelty risk.

## Limitations
This is an exact count of ideals in the specified free length-three truncation, not a classification of ideals up to algebra automorphism, and not an enumeration of ideals in its arbitrary quotients. The general proof is purely algebraic; the reproducibility program checks only the listed small prime-field cases. The evidence for originality is comparative, not a proof that no equivalent formula was published under another description. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
