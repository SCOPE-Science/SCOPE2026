# Self-review

The proof was checked in four pieces. First, Gvozdevsky's Corollary 3.4 applies to arbitrary infinite first factors and gives isotypy of all \(A\times\mathbb Z\). Second, Lemma 3.6 makes the finite-distance quotient an isomorphism invariant recovering \(A\). Third, the converse characterization of the isotypy class uses only formulas already forced by finite tuple types: immediate predecessor/successor in arity one and arbitrarily many intervening points for coordinates chosen from different fibers. These force every finite-distance class to be \(\mathbb Z\) and force infinitely many such classes. Fourth, the standard coding map \(A\mapsto A\times\mathbb Z\) is continuous and reduces countable-linear-order isomorphism to isomorphism within the class.

The Borelness argument was checked separately. Finite distance is a countable union of finite-cardinality interval conditions. The requirements that every point have immediate predecessor and successor within its finite-distance class and that there be arbitrarily many pairwise inequivalent points are countable Boolean combinations of Borel coordinate conditions. The resulting class is invariant under isomorphism.

Rast's complete-theory result was treated as adjacent rather than equivalent: it places maximal complexity on all countable models of a non-\(\aleph_0\)-categorical theory of linear order, whereas the present claim restricts to one isotypy class, a strictly finer semantic invariant.
