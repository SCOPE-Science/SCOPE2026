# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof classifies every subalgebra by its projection to the two-dimensional quotient, its intersection with the center, and a graph map. The closure condition is checked separately in quotient dimensions \(0,1,2\). For pairs, the only possible failures are distinct quotient lines, where the condition reduces exactly to \(z\notin C+D\). The graph-of-functional count yields the weighted intersection sum \(J_r(q)\), and the Gaussian finite sum follows from the standard count of disjoint subspaces. The parity limit follows by a leading-degree comparison. The independent checker reproduces the exact formula by brute-force subspace enumeration in five small cases.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating preprint covers \(r=0\). A published comparison record covers \(r=1\). Published higher-rank Heisenberg formulas concern a different structural family with one-dimensional center and growing symplectic quotient. Searches for the arbitrary central stabilization, equivalent Heisenberg-direct-product language, and subgroup-commutativity analogues did not locate a result implying the all-\(r\) formula or the parity law. The remaining group-theoretic alias risk is stated explicitly.

## Value
PASS. Central stabilization is a natural structural operation, and the exact all-\(r\) formula answers how a newly studied invariant behaves under repeated stabilization. The parity oscillation is qualitatively stronger than a one-step calculation: even and odd central dimensions have different large-field limits indefinitely. The result also separates stabilization behavior from the previously studied higher-rank Heisenberg behavior.
