# Characteristic-sensitive multiplicity of the central component of graph first-jet schemes

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph with no isolated vertices and at least one edge, and let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Set
\[
X_G=\operatorname{Spec}\Bigl(k[x_v:v\in V]/(x_ix_j:\{i,j\}\in E)\Bigr).
\]
In the first jet scheme \(J_1(X_G)\), let \(P=(x_v^{(0)}:v\in V)\) be the ideal of jets whose constant terms vanish. This is a minimal prime. Define \(b_k(G)\) to be the number of connected components when \(\operatorname{char}(k)=2\), and the number of bipartite connected components when \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\). The local ring at the generic point of \(V(P)\), with residue field \(K=k(x_v^{(1)}:v\in V)\), is
\[
\mathcal O_{J_1(X_G),P}\ \cong
K[\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{b_k(G)}]/(\epsilon_1^2,\ldots,\epsilon_{b_k(G)}^2).
\]
Consequently the component \(V(P)\) has scheme-theoretic generic multiplicity exactly \(2^{b_k(G)}\). In characteristic unequal to two, that component is generically reduced exactly when all connected components of \(G\) are nonbipartite; in characteristic two, it is generically nonreduced for every nonempty graph. For a connected triangle, the generic multiplicity is \(1\) in characteristic unequal to two and \(2\) in characteristic two.

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the first jet scheme, i.e. maps from \(\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^2)\) into \(X_G\). Here a jet has coordinates \(u_i=x_i^{(0)}\) and \(v_i=x_i^{(1)}\). The condition that every graph vertex lies on an edge ensures that the generic local algebra is Artinian. The graph may be disconnected; its connected components are counted with multiplicity one. Bipartite means having no odd cycle, so a component consisting of one edge is bipartite. The coefficient field \(K\) is the rational-function field of the independent first-order coordinates. No smoothness hypothesis is imposed on \(X_G\).

## Proof
Write \(S=k[u_i,v_i:i\in V]\). By taking coefficients modulo \(t^2\) of the graph edge equations, the first-jet ideal is exactly
\[
J=\bigl(u_i u_j,\ u_i v_j+v_i u_j:\{i,j\}\in E\bigr)\subset S.
\]
The ideal \(P=(u_i:i\in V)\) contains \(J\), and every \(v_i\notin P\) is invertible in \(S_P\). Put \(K=k(v_i:i\in V)\) and \(z_i=u_i/v_i\). Dividing edge equations by the units \(v_iv_j\) gives the exact local presentation
\[
(S/J)_P\ \cong
\left(K[z_i:i\in V]\big/(z_i+z_j,\ z_i z_j:\{i,j\}\in E)\right)_{(z_i:i\in V)}.
\]
Fix a connected component and a root vertex \(r\). Each linear relation along an edge reads \(z_j=-z_i\). Propagating along any path expresses every coordinate of that component as either \(z_r\) or \(-z_r\). If the component is bipartite, all path parities are consistent, leaving one parameter \(t=z_r\). If the component has an odd cycle, some two paths of opposite parity yield \(2t=0\). Hence, if \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\), a nonbipartite component contributes no parameter; if \(\operatorname{char}(k)=2\), its signs coincide and it contributes one parameter just as a bipartite component does.

For each surviving component, one of its edges becomes the nonzero relation \(\pm t^2=0\), where the scalar sign is a unit in every characteristic. Every other edge in that component yields either the same relation up to sign or zero after this substitution. Distinct connected components involve disjoint variables and impose no cross-component relations. Therefore the quotient, before the final localization, is precisely
\[
K[t_1,\ldots,t_b]/(t_1^2,\ldots,t_b^2),\qquad b=b_k(G).
\]
This finite-dimensional algebra is local with maximal ideal generated by the nilpotent variables, so localization does not change it. It has \(K\)-basis given by all squarefree products of the \(t_i\), including \(1\); thus its length is \(2^b\). Because \(P\) localizes to the unique prime of a zero-dimensional local ring, \(P\) is a minimal prime of \(J\). The length is precisely the generic component multiplicity by the standard definition of the fundamental cycle of a finite-type scheme. The reducedness and characteristic conclusions follow immediately.

## Verification
The companion standalone Python program `verify_graph_first_jets.py` exactly row-reduces the unsigned edge-incidence matrix over each of \(\mathbf F_2\), \(\mathbf F_3\), and \(\mathbf F_5\). It then substitutes a basis of the resulting nullspace into all edge quadrics, verifying that the surviving quadratic ideal consists exactly of independent square relations. It exhaustively checks all simple graphs without isolated vertices on \(2\) through \(5\) labeled vertices and five additional labeled six-vertex examples, for **2,457** graph/field test instances. The output is `EXACT_IDEAL_CHECK_OK`. These finite tests are corroboration; the proof above establishes the theorem for all finite graphs and algebraically closed fields, including characteristic zero.

## Relationship to prior work
Xie (2026) examines off-diagonal Gram maps; its scalar case is the complete-graph edge ideal and its jet dimensions are studied, but the generic scheme multiplicity calculated here is not provided there. Goward and Smith (2006) determine the irreducible-component structure of jets of monomial schemes and emphasize characteristic-independent descriptions of their radicals; such descriptions alone do not determine the nilpotent generic local structure. Galetto, Iammarino, and Yu (2025), especially their Question 8.2, explicitly identify primary components and multiplicities of general graph and clutter jets as a problem; the present result answers one natural component's multiplicity uniformly across all graphs, rather than all their primary components. Yuen's multiplicity results concern jets of simple normal crossing divisors, i.e. single monomial hypersurfaces, not the interacting edge families of arbitrary graphs.

## Limitations
The theorem determines only the central constant-zero component \(V(P)\), not the full primary decomposition or multiplicities of other components. It does not cover isolated vertices in the stated form and does not concern jets of order greater than one. The calculation gives scheme-theoretic generic lengths, not degrees of projective closures or dimensions of higher jet schemes. A broader prior theorem with an implicit equivalent computation remains a bibliographic possibility; an independent literature review has not been carried out.
