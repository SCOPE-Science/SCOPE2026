# Chen–Ruan Hodge diamond of the generic family \(2.19\) forgetful fiber
## Finding
Let \(V\subset\mathbf P^5_{\mathbf C}\) be a general smooth complete intersection of two quadrics, and let \(\mathcal K_V\) be the fiber over the residual gerbe \(B\operatorname{Aut}(V)\) of the forgetful K-moduli-stack map from Fano family \(2.19\) to family \(1.14\). Under Zhao’s canonical identification \(\mathcal K_V\cong[A/G]\), where \(A\cong F_1(V)\) is an abelian surface and \(G=A[2]\times\{\pm1\}\) acts by translations and inversion, the Chen–Ruan orbifold Hodge numbers are \(h^{0,0}_{\mathrm{CR}}=h^{2,0}_{\mathrm{CR}}=h^{0,2}_{\mathrm{CR}}=h^{2,2}_{\mathrm{CR}}=1\), \(h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{CR}}=20\), and all others vanish. Equivalently, the orbifold Betti numbers are \((1,22,1)\) in degrees \(0,2,4\), so the orbifold Poincaré polynomial is \(1+22t^2+t^4\) and the orbifold Euler characteristic is \(24\). The sixteen nontrivial twisted sectors are residual gerbes \(B(\mathbf Z/2)\), all of age \(1\), and their sixteen degree-two classes are exactly the increment from the untwisted invariant value \(h^{1,1}=4\) to \(h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{CR}}=20\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbf C\).  The threefold \(V\subset\mathbf P^5\) is assumed general enough for Zhao's Lemma 8.5: \(F_1(V)\) is identified with an abelian surface \(A\), and \(\operatorname{Aut}(V)\cong(\mathbf Z/2)^5\) acts as \(G=A[2]\times\{\pm1\}\) by translations and inversion.  The stack \(\mathcal K_V\) is the residual-gerbe fiber in Zhao's Lemma 8.7.  The statement concerns Chen–Ruan cohomology of this generic Deligne–Mumford quotient stack only; it does not assert an analogous formula for singular or nongeneral \(V\), nor for the total K-moduli stack.

## Proof
Zhao identifies \(\mathcal K_V\) with \([A/G]\), where \(|A[2]|=16\) and \(|G|=32\).  Write an element of \(G\) as \((T,\epsilon)\), with \(T\in A[2]\) and \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}\).  If \(\epsilon=+1\) and \(T\ne0\), translation by \(T\) has no fixed point.  If \(\epsilon=-1\), fixed points solve \(2x=T\).  The multiplication-by-two map on a complex abelian surface has kernel \(A[2]\), so each such equation has exactly \(16\) solutions.  Translation by \(A[2]\) acts simply transitively on this solution set.  Hence for each of the \(16\) elements \((T,-1)\), the quotient of its fixed locus by the centralizer \(G\) is a single residual gerbe with stabilizer of order \(2\).  Because \(G\) is abelian, these are \(16\) distinct twisted sectors.

At every such fixed point, the derivative of \(x\mapsto-x+T\) is \(-I\) on the two-dimensional tangent space.  Both eigenvalues have fractional part \(1/2\), so the age is \(1\).  Fantechi–Göttsche's global-quotient description therefore shifts the cohomology of each zero-dimensional twisted sector by two in total degree, and by \((1,1)\) in the Hodge bidegree.

For the identity sector, translations act trivially on \(H^*(A,\mathbf C)\), while inversion acts by \((-1)^{p+q}\) on \(H^{p,q}(A)\).  Thus the invariant Hodge numbers are \(h^{0,0}=h^{2,0}=h^{0,2}=h^{2,2}=1\) and \(h^{1,1}=4\), with the odd-total-degree groups killed.  Adding one \((1,1)\)-class from each of the \(16\) age-one sectors gives \(h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{CR}}=20\).  Summing by total degree yields orbifold Betti numbers \((1,22,1)\) and orbifold Euler characteristic \(24\).

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_orbifold_hodge.py` checks the finite-group orders, fixed-locus orbit size, stabilizer order, age bookkeeping, invariant abelian-surface Hodge diamond, Chen–Ruan Hodge numbers, Betti numbers, and Euler characteristic using exact integer arithmetic.  Its recorded output ends in `VERIFY_OK`.  The script verifies only the finite and Hodge bookkeeping; the geometric premises are taken from the cited primary sources and were checked directly there.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhao proves the exact quotient-stack identification \(\mathcal K_V\cong[F_1(V)/\operatorname{Aut}(V)]\), the standard \(A[2]\times\{\pm1\}\) action for general \(V\), and the coarse Kummer surface with sixteen \(A_1\)-singularities.  He also identifies the nontrivial automorphism locus by the equation \(2x=T\).  The paper does not compute Chen–Ruan cohomology of this fiber.  Fantechi–Göttsche give the general fixed-locus and age formalism for finite global quotients.  Combining these yields the stated exact orbifold invariant of this newly identified K-moduli fiber.  The resulting Hodge diamond agrees with that of the crepant Kummer K3 resolution, as expected from the McKay/crepant-resolution picture, but no ring isomorphism is claimed here.

## Limitations
The calculation is for a general smooth \((2,2)\)-complete intersection and uses complex coefficients.  It determines the graded Hodge vector space, not the full Chen–Ruan product.  Classical Kummer-orbifold calculations in other moduli contexts make the numerical profile unsurprising; the contribution here is the exact transfer of that profile to Zhao's generic residual-gerbe K-moduli fiber, together with the sector/stabilizer interpretation.  No claim is made about nongeneral automorphism groups or boundary fibers.
