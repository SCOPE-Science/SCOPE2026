# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof starts from the exact definition and the published same-open-neighborhood observation. Every multipartite part is monochromatic. Global color totals then give exact balance equations for each part. Comparing two differently colored parts forces them to be singleton parts, separately for \(k=2\) and \(k\ge3\); every remaining part is then forced to be singleton as well. The surviving complete graph case is balanced exactly when \(k\mid r\), and the multinomial count follows. The standalone verifier independently checks \(583627\) labeled assignments on all tested small part profiles.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating general-\(k\) paper states the complete-graph criterion and the same-neighborhood lemma but contains no occurrence of “multipartite” in targeted full-text inspection. The earlier two-color paper gives the complete-bipartite boundary \(K_{m,n}\), not arbitrary complete multipartite graphs. Semantic literature searches under exact, alias, and neighborhood-twin formulations found no covering same-invariant result.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives a natural complete classification for a canonical graph family under a recent invariant: all non-complete complete-multipartite graphs are excluded for every \(k\ge2\), while the entire positive case has an exact divisibility criterion and labeled count. This turns a known biclique obstruction and a known clique example into a sharp all-multipartite boundary.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are arXiv:2510.16666v1 and the two-color predecessor arXiv:2412.02800 / DOI:10.1007/s00373-025-02950-5. The theorem is restricted to finite connected complete multipartite graphs. An unindexed equivalent formulation remains a residual literature risk.
