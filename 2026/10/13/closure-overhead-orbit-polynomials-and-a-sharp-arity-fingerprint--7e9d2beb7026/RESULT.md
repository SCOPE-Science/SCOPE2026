# Closure-overhead orbit polynomials and a sharp arity fingerprint for homogeneous meet-trees

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal T_k\) be the countable homogeneous generic meet-tree of arity at most \(k\), for each integer \(k\ge1\); let \(\mathcal T_\infty\) be the generic meet-tree without an arity bound. These are the Fraïssé limits identified by Kaplan, Rzepecki and Siniora. For an \(n\)-element subset \(S\) of one of these structures, write \(\langle S\rangle_\wedge\) for its closure under binary meet and define its *closure overhead* by \(w(S)=|\langle S\rangle_\wedge|-n\). Let \(g^{(k)}_{n,w}\) count automorphism orbits of such subsets with overhead \(w\), and put

\[
F_k(z,u)=\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{w\ge0}g^{(k)}_{n,w}z^nu^w,\qquad P_{k,n}(u)=[z^n]F_k(z,u).
\]

**Theorem (orbit calculus, arity fingerprint, and extremal ranks).** For every \(k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}\), an orbit is uniquely encoded by an unlabelled, non-plane rooted tree with two types of vertices: exactly \(n\) marked vertices, and \(w\) unmarked vertices, each unmarked vertex having at least two children. All leaves are marked, and all vertices have at most \(k\) children when \(k\) is finite. In particular, set

\[
M_k(F)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}Z(\mathfrak S_j)\big(F(z,u),F(z^2,u^2),\ldots,F(z^j,u^j)\big)
\]

for finite \(k\), where \(Z(\mathfrak S_j)\) is the standard symmetric-group cycle-index polynomial, \(Z(\mathfrak S_0)=1\), and

\[
M_\infty(F)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{F(z^j,u^j)}{j}\right).
\]

Then \(F_k\) is the unique formal power series with zero constant term satisfying

\[
\boxed{F_k=zM_k(F_k)+u\big(M_k(F_k)-1-F_k\big).}
\]

The whole family has the **sharp finite-subset arity fingerprint**

\[
\boxed{P_{k,n}(u)=P_{\infty,n}(u)\quad(1\le n\le k),\qquad
P_{\infty,k+1}(u)-P_{k,k+1}(u)=u.}
\]

Thus the first subset size at which the arity-\(k\) orbit polynomial differs from the unrestricted orbit polynomial is *exactly* \(k+1\), and the first difference consists of precisely one orbit of closure overhead one.

Finally, for every \(k\ge2\) (including \(\infty\)) and every \(n\ge1\), the possible closure overheads are **exactly** \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\). The constant coefficient \(g^{(k)}_{n,0}\) is the number of unlabelled rooted trees with \(n\) vertices and maximum child-degree at most \(k\). The maximal-overhead coefficient \(g^{(k)}_{n,n-1}\) is independent of \(k\ge2\) and equals the number of unlabelled non-plane full binary rooted trees with \(n\) leaves (the Wedderburn–Etherington count). For \(k=1\), instead, \(F_1(z,u)=z/(1-z)\), so there is one orbit for every subset size and every closure overhead is zero.

For example, \(\mathcal T_2\) has \(1,2,5,15,48,166\) subset orbits in sizes \(1\) through \(6\), while \(\mathcal T_3\) has \(1,2,6,20,72,282\). The unrestricted coefficients are the **previously known** rooted-Greg-tree totals \(1,2,6,21,78,313\). At size six its refined polynomial is

\[
\boxed{P_{\infty,6}(u)=20+72u+103u^2+80u^3+32u^4+6u^5.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

A *meet-tree* is a semilinearly ordered set in which every pair has a greatest lower bound, written \(\wedge\). A finite meet-tree has a unique least element and its cover relation makes it a rooted, non-plane tree. Here *arity at most* \(k\) means that there cannot be \(k+1\) pairwise incomparable elements with a common pairwise meet, equivalently every node of every finite meet-substructure has at most \(k\) children. The ambient structures are the ultrahomogeneous Fraïssé limits **in the language containing the meet operation**, not the existentially closed pure partial-order trees. An orbit is under the full automorphism group of the corresponding meet-tree, acting on *unordered* finite subsets (not injective ordered tuples). All counts concern concrete finite subsets and their meet-generated substructures, not finite induced substructures in a pure order reduct.

The variable \(z\) marks elements of the selected subset; \(u\) marks additional meet-points created by taking closures. The coefficient range is \(0\le w\le n-1\). An occurrence of a bounded-degree tree in the theorem refers to its maximum number of children **at any vertex**, not only at the root.

## Proof

**1. Orbit–decorated-tree correspondence.** Let \(S\) be finite and nonempty. Because meet is associative, commutative and idempotent, \(\langle S\rangle_\wedge\) is generated by pairwise meets of elements of \(S\). In particular it is a finite meet-tree. Color the selected vertices black (marked) and the other meet-points white (unmarked). Every leaf must be black: a white leaf cannot be the meet of selected vertices. More generally, if a white point had only one child in the finite closure, all selected points above it would lie in the same child-branch; meets of those points would be inside that branch, while meets involving any selected point outside that branch would lie below the white point. It could therefore not be a generated point. Thus every white point has at least two children. The ambient arity bound transfers to the maximum child-degree of the finite closure.

Conversely, take any finite rooted, unordered tree having black leaves and white vertices of at least two children, with maximum child-degree at most \(k\). Regard it as a meet-tree using the ancestor order and lowest common ancestor as meet. Every white vertex is the meet of two black leaves taken from distinct child branches. Hence the black vertices generate the *entire* meet-tree. By the Fraïssé age property, this finite meet-tree embeds into \(\mathcal T_k\) (or \(\mathcal T_\infty\)); its black vertex set has exactly the specified closure. Finally, ultrahomogeneity extends any isomorphism of two such finitely generated colored closures to an ambient automorphism. Such an extension sends black points to black points and therefore carries one selected subset onto the other. This proves a bijection between subset orbits and the stated unlabelled decorated-tree types.

**2. Exact generating equation.** At a rooted decorated tree, child subtrees form a finite unordered multiset of decorated trees, with at most \(k\) members. The ordinary generating function for multisets with precisely \(j\) members is the cycle-index substitution \(Z(\mathfrak S_j)(F(z,u),F(z^2,u^2),\ldots,F(z^j,u^j))\). Thus \(M_k(F)\) counts allowable child multisets. A black root adds one selected element and permits any child multiset, giving \(zM_k(F)\). A white root adds one unselected meet-point and must have *at least two* children; excluding empty and singleton multisets gives \(u(M_k(F)-1-F)\). Adding these cases proves the boxed identity. The infinite-arity exponential expression follows from the classical cycle-index identity for arbitrary multisets. For uniqueness, order monomials by total degree in \((z,u)\): every root contributes positive degree, so the coefficient of any fixed total degree is determined by strictly lower degrees. Hence the equation recursively determines a unique zero-constant-term formal series. At \(k=1\) the white-root contribution vanishes, yielding \(F_1=z(1+F_1)=z/(1-z)\).

**3. Exact arity-detection threshold.** In any decorated closure with \(n\) marked vertices, each child branch at a vertex contains at least one marked descendant: otherwise an unmarked leaf would exist. Distinct child branches have distinct marked descendants. Hence every vertex has at most \(n\) children. Thus for \(n\le k\) the sets of allowable decorated types for \(\mathcal T_k\) and \(\mathcal T_\infty\) coincide, giving equality of all overhead coefficients.

Now put \(n=k+1\). A type occurring in the unrestricted model but not the arity-\(k\) model has a vertex with \(k+1\) children. These child branches exhaust all \(k+1\) marked vertices, one in each branch. Each child branch must therefore be a *single marked leaf*: any further unmarked branching would require more marked leaves, and unmarked unary vertices are forbidden. The high-degree vertex cannot be marked, since that would consume another marked vertex, and cannot have an ancestor, since any ancestor would have to be marked or have at least two child branches, again requiring another marked vertex. Consequently it is the unmarked root of one \((k+1)\)-leaf star. This gives exactly one extra orbit, of overhead one, and proves the second boxed identity and its sharpness.

**4. Complete overhead range and endpoints.** Let a decorated rooted tree have \(L\) leaves. For any finite rooted tree, summing \(\deg^+(v)-1\) over nonleaf vertices gives \(L-1\). Each white vertex has at least two children and therefore contributes at least one; hence \(w\le L-1\le n-1\). For every \(k\ge2\) and every \(0\le w\le n-1\), construct a non-plane full binary rooted tree with \(w+1\) black leaves and \(w\) white internal vertices. If \(n>w+1\), place \(n-w-1\) extra black unary vertices on a chain above its root. The resulting decorated tree has exactly \(n\) black and \(w\) white points, maximum degree at most two, and every white vertex binary. Thus every listed overhead occurs.

When \(w=0\), all vertices are black, so the allowed types are exactly bounded-degree unlabelled rooted trees. When \(w=n-1\), equality in both bounds implies \(L=n\), *all* black vertices are leaves, and *each* white vertex has exactly two children, with no other internal vertices. The allowed shapes are therefore precisely unlabelled non-plane full binary rooted trees with \(n\) leaves. For \(k\ge2\) this family is unaffected by increasing \(k\). This concludes the proof.

## Verification

The included standalone Python program `verify.py` implements two separate exact finite enumerators: (a) a Pólya cycle-index formal-power-series expansion of the displayed equation, and (b) generation of every canonical unlabelled two-colored rooted tree with up to six black vertices and its allowed white overhead. It independently reconstructs each shape's meet-closure from all pairwise lowest common ancestors of black vertices. It tests every arity cap from one through six, compares all available bivariate coefficients, checks the published unrestricted rooted-Greg-tree totals, verifies the sharp first arity difference and each overhead stratum, and checks the two endpoint sequences. A successful replay prints:

`VERIFY_OK 740 exact coefficient and combinatorial assertions; 421 independent rooted shapes; 6 arities`

The program uses only the Python standard library. Run it with `python3 verify.py`. This is an exhaustive check **only for marked sizes through six**, not an independent proof of the universal identities. The general theorem rests on the argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Kaplan–Rzepecki–Siniora prove that the finite meet-trees and finite bounded-arity meet-trees form Fraïssé classes and establish ultrahomogeneity of their limits (Fact 2.16); they also note that a subset's meet-closure consists of its pairwise meets (after Fact 2.19). Those structural inputs are used here, not claimed as new. Their manuscript concerns generic automorphisms rather than the graded orbit profiles on finite subsets.

The unrestricted ungraded sequence \(P_{\infty,n}(1)\) is **already** OEIS A052300, rooted unlabelled Greg trees; it is not a new sequence or a new combinatorial interpretation of the classical multiset cycle-index. OEIS A048160 records the two-variable refined counts for **labelled** rooted Greg trees, not unlabelled subsets under a meet-tree automorphism group. Hoenen, Eger and Gehrke (2017) analyze **root-degree** refinements of **labelled** Greg trees; restricting the root degree is different from restricting the maximum degree at every node in the generic arity-\(k\) meet-tree. The proposed contribution is the simultaneous oligomorphic-orbit interpretation for every bounded arity, the closure-overhead polynomial classification, its sharp one-orbit first-difference law and the exact overhead endpoints. Some statements follow naturally once the known Fraïssé and classical combinatorial ingredients are combined; prior implicit derivations remain possible.

## Limitations

The full bivariate coefficient arrays beyond six selected vertices are not independently enumerated here. The Pólya recurrence is an exact implicit characterization, not a claimed closed elementary formula for its general coefficients. No asymptotic result for finite \(k\) is claimed. OEIS A052300 and classical rooted-tree or Wedderburn–Etherington numbers are explicitly prior art. The literature search cannot exclude unindexed work that has already combined the same generic meet-trees with unlabelled Greg-tree enumeration. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
