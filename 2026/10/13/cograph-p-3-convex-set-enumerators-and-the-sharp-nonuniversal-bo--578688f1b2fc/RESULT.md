# Cograph \(P_3\)-convex-set enumerators and the sharp nonuniversal bound

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every finite nonempty cograph \(G\), the cardinality enumerator
\[
\mathcal C_G(z)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V(G)\\ \forall v\notin S:\,|N_G(v)\cap S|\le1}}z^{|S|}
\]
has the following exact cotree recurrences. Define \(i(H)\) as the number of isolated vertices of \(H\), and define
\[
Q_H(z)=\prod_{C\in\operatorname{Comp}(H)}(1+z^{|C|}),
\]
where the product is over connected components of \(H\). For disjoint nonempty graphs \(A,B\),
\[
\mathcal C_{A\sqcup B}(z)=\mathcal C_A(z)\mathcal C_B(z),\qquad Q_{A\sqcup B}(z)=Q_A(z)Q_B(z),\qquad i(A\sqcup B)=i(A)+i(B).
\]
For the join \(A\vee B\), writing \(a=|A|\) and \(b=|B|\), if \(a,b\ge2\), then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_{A\vee B}(z)=1+(a+b)z+i(A)i(B)z^2+z^{a+b}.}
\]
If \(A=K_1\), for every nonempty \(B\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_{K_1\vee B}(z)=1+bz+zQ_B(z).}
\]
The symmetric formula holds when \(B=K_1\); in every join \(i(A\vee B)=0\) and \(Q_{A\vee B}(z)=1+z^{a+b}\). The leaf value is \(\mathcal C_{K_1}(z)=Q_{K_1}(z)=1+z\), with \(i(K_1)=1\).

In particular, if \(G\) is a connected cograph on \(n\ge4\) vertices **without a universal vertex**, then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_G(1)\le n+2+\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor.}
\]
Equality holds precisely for \(K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}\). Thus there is a sharp quadratic cap on convex-set counts in connected cographs with no universal vertex, in contrast to the exponential star case.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected; joins and unions use disjoint vertex sets. A cograph is a graph containing no induced four-vertex path. Equivalently, it can be constructed recursively from one-vertex graphs by disjoint unions and joins. The \(P_3\)-convexity used here is generated by **all** three-vertex paths, not only induced ones: \(S\subseteq V(G)\) is convex exactly if each vertex outside \(S\) has at most one neighbor in \(S\). In particular, the empty set, every singleton, and the full vertex set are convex. A universal vertex is adjacent to every other vertex. For the upper bound the graph is connected, has at least four vertices, and has no universal vertex; no such restriction is imposed on the polynomial recurrences.

## Proof

**Join classification.** Put \(S_A=S\cap V(A)\) and \(S_B=S\cap V(B)\), and let their cardinalities be \(s_A,s_B\). In a join, every vertex of \(B\setminus S_B\) has all \(s_A\) vertices of \(S_A\) as neighbors. If \(s_A\ge2\), convexity therefore forces \(S_B=V(B)\). If also \(s_B\ge2\), it likewise forces \(S_A=V(A)\), hence \(S=V(A\vee B)\). If \(s_A\ge2\) but \(s_B=1\), then \(B\) must be a singleton, and every vertex of \(A\setminus S_A\) already sees the selected singleton. Therefore it must have no selected neighbors inside \(A\). Equivalently, \(S_A\) is a union of entire connected components of \(A\). The case \(s_A\ge2,s_B=0\) is impossible because an unselected vertex of \(B\) has at least two selected neighbors. Symmetric arguments apply after exchanging factors.

The only other nontrivial possibility has \(s_A=s_B=1\). Each unselected vertex in \(A\) already sees the selected vertex of \(B\); thus the selected vertex of \(A\) must be isolated in \(A\). The same is required in \(B\). Conversely, any such pair is convex. When both factors have size at least two, the entire classification consists of the empty set, \(a+b\) singletons, the \(i(A)i(B)\) crossing pairs of isolated factor vertices, and the full vertex set. Their sizes prove the first join formula without double counting, since \(a+b\ge4\).

When one factor is \(K_1\), write its vertex as \(u\). If \(u\notin S\), the condition at \(u\) forces \(|S|\le1\). If \(u\in S\), the condition on outside vertices of \(B\) says exactly that no edge of \(B\) crosses between \(S\setminus\{u\}\) and its complement, so the selected vertices of \(B\) are a union of connected components. These choices have generating polynomial \(zQ_B(z)\), including \(\{u\}\) and the entire graph. The remaining choices are the empty set and \(b\) singleton vertices of \(B\), giving the second join formula. Convexity splits componentwise under disjoint union, giving the product recurrence. In a join of nonempty factors there are no isolated vertices and the graph is connected, which proves the auxiliary updates. A binary cotree now proves all recurrences by induction.

**Sharp bound and equality.** Every connected cograph on at least two vertices admits a join decomposition \(A\vee B\) with both factors nonempty. If \(G\) has no universal vertex, neither factor can be a singleton (such a singleton would be universal in \(G\)). Therefore the first join formula applies, and
\[
\mathcal C_G(1)=n+2+i(A)i(B)\le n+2+|A||B|\le n+2+\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
Equality in the first inequality forces every vertex of each factor to be isolated in that factor, so both factors are edgeless; equality in the second forces their orders to differ by at most one. These conditions give exactly the balanced complete bipartite graph. Conversely that graph attains equality and has no universal vertex for \(n\ge4\).

**Arithmetic complexity.** At every cotree node maintain \(\mathcal C_H(z)\), \(Q_H(z)\), \(|H|\), and \(i(H)\). Polynomial multiplication occurs only at union nodes, where the factor degrees are the two child orders. Summing their pairwise products over a binary cotree counts any pair of leaves at most once, and sums of node degrees are at most quadratic. Join and leaf updates are linear in their polynomial degree. Thus the complete coefficient vector requires \(O(n^2)\) coefficient arithmetic operations given a cotree; the scalar \(\mathcal C_G(1)\) requires \(O(n)\) arithmetic operations using component counts instead of polynomials. Bit complexity is not claimed to be linear.

## Verification

The complete independent executable `verify.py` checks the recurrence against brute-force enumeration of every vertex subset on all \(6{,}039\) labeled cographs of orders one through six; the exhaustive enumeration also checks the sharp bound and equality characterization whenever applicable. It further tests \(1{,}000\) random binary cotrees with at most twelve vertices and four balanced/unbalanced complete bipartite coefficient anchors. The included `verify.out` records successful execution; run `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing these two artifact files. The universal theorem rests on the proof above, not on these finite checks.

For example, \(\mathcal C_{K_{2,3}}(z)=1+5z+6z^2+z^5\). If \(H=K_{2,2}\sqcup K_2\), then \(K_1\vee H\) has \(\mathcal C(z)=1+7z+z^3+z^5+z^7\), illustrating component-size-sensitive coefficients in a cone that is not a block graph.

## Relationship to prior work

Dourado, Grippo, Lin, and Protti (2026, arXiv:2603.04706v1) introduced the counting problem studied here, gave a general extremal bound for connected graphs, and provided formulas/algorithms for trees and threshold graphs. Their Theorems 11 and 14 do not give the complete cograph cardinality polynomial nor the sharp **no-universal-vertex** extremal theorem here. The star \(K_{1,n-1}\) is a boundary covered by their global extremal result; substituting its cotree into the formula gives \(\mathcal C_{K_{1,n-1}}(1)=2^{n-1}+n\), as already known.

Campos, Sampaio, Silva, and Szwarcfiter (2015) studied hull, convexity, Carathéodory, and Radon **numbers** under \(P_3\)-convexity using cograph and related decompositions; the inspected parts of that paper do not count all convex subsets by cardinality. Courcelle, Makowsky, and Rotics (2001) give general polynomial-time countability of vertex-set properties expressible in monadic second-order logic on graph classes of bounded clique-width with suitable decompositions. Thus polynomial-time computability alone is *not* claimed as original here: the stated contribution is the explicit positive join/union polynomial, a full structural classification of its terms at join nodes, and the sharp restricted extremum with equality graphs.

## Limitations

The theorem does not apply to arbitrary graphs with induced four-vertex paths. It counts convex sets under all length-two paths, not the distinct induced-path or geodesic convexities. The linear statement concerns arithmetic operations for scalar evaluation given a cotree, not bit complexity or cotree recognition. Finite verification is not a proof of the infinite claim. Primary accessible literature has been checked, but unindexed or unpublished equivalent formulations remain possible; no independent external scientific audit has been performed.
