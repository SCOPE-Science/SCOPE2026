# Complete bounded-synchronization-delay spectrum of binary five-bit capacity codes

## Finding

For a binary length-five block code \(C\), define its **block-count synchronization delay** \(\delta(C)\) as in the next section. Among all maximum-cardinality codes of finite delay, precisely \(9{,}204\) labeled codes exist. The complete count by delay is

\[\begin{array}{c|rrrr|r}\delta&1&2&3&4&\infty\\hline \text{codebooks}&86&5{,}546&3{,}116&456&6{,}421\end{array}\]

The final entry counts the remaining primitive-necklace selections; codes in that column do not have finite delay. In particular, there are \(9{,}118\) **non-comma-free** maximum-capacity codebooks with finite synchronization delay; exactly \(456\) attain delay four, and no finite delay larger than four is possible at capacity six. The \(86\) delay-one codebooks and their classification were known previously and are included solely to provide the complete delay spectrum.

Under the four transformations generated by global bit complementation and word reversal, the new designs have \(1{,}395\) classes at delay two, \(784\) at delay three and \(114\) at delay four. Their orbit-size decompositions are respectively \(17\) orbits of size two plus \(1{,}378\) of size four; \(10\) of size two plus \(774\) of size four; and \(114\) of size four.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the binary alphabet \(\{0,1\}\). Every codeword has length five, the channel is error-free, and concatenation is unrestricted. Write \(C^d\) for exactly \(d\) concatenated words and \(C^+\) for a nonempty concatenation. Define

\[\delta(C)=\min\{d\ge1:\ \forall u,v,w\in\{0,1\}^*,\ (uvw\in C^+\land v\in C^d)\Longrightarrow uv\in C^+\}.\]

If the set is empty, put \(\delta(C)=\infty\). This condition guarantees resynchronization after encountering \(d\) consecutive complete *candidate* codewords, even if the candidate boundary is shifted relative to the true framing. It does **not** give a bound of \(d\) raw bits and makes no substitution/deletion correction claim. A comma-free code satisfies \(\delta=1\).

Words are labeled by their actual five-bit sequences: no quotient is taken when counting \(9{,}204\). The separate orbit counts quotient only by global complementation and simultaneous reversal of each complete word.

## Proof

**Capacity upper bound.** A code of finite delay contains no word of period one and cannot contain two members of the same cyclic-rotation orbit. Indeed, repeating a periodic word yields two indefinitely admissible framings; so does repeating two different rotations of a common primitive word. In each case, an arbitrarily long shifted string of complete codewords violates the displayed definition. Since length five is prime, the thirty nonconstant words form exactly \((2^5-2)/5=6\) disjoint primitive rotation necklaces. Thus any finite-delay code has at most six words. The code of the six lexicographically least primitive rotations (Lyndon representatives), `00001,00011,00101,00111,01011,01111`, attains cardinality six and has delay two by the calculation below.

**Exact reduction.** At size six every code picks exactly one word from each of the six necklaces; there are exactly \(5^6=15{,}625\) possibilities. For a code \(C\) and each offset \(r\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), define a directed graph \(G_r(C)\) with vertex set \(C\) and edge \(x\to y\) precisely when the last \(5-r\) bits of \(x\) concatenated with the first \(r\) bits of \(y\) form a word in \(C\). A false frame containing \(d\) complete codewords occurs precisely when some \(G_r(C)\) has a directed walk of \(d\) edges: its vertices are the \(d+1\) true-framed neighboring words. Conversely every such walk is realized by concatenating its vertices, with an appropriate initial length-\(r\) prefix and terminal suffix. Hence \(\delta(C)=\infty\) exactly when one such graph contains a directed cycle. Otherwise

\[\delta(C)=1+\max_{1\le r\le4}\{\text{length of a longest directed path in }G_r(C)\}.\]

There are six vertices, but exhaustive evaluation sharpens the bound to four for the finite-delay six-word cases.

**Exhaustive classification.** The standalone `delay_verify.py` enumerates the six rotation necklaces and all their Cartesian selections; it determines acyclicity and longest path through tri-color depth-first search. An independent `delay_verify.cpp` uses integer rotation arithmetic and a six-round Boolean-reachability propagation. Reaching six edges on a six-vertex graph is equivalent to a directed cycle; when it does not occur the reachability rounds directly determine the longest path. Thus the two programs implement logically distinct termination tests. Both enumerate exactly \(6{,}421\) cyclic/unsynchronizable choices and respectively \(86,5546,3116,456\) choices at delays one through four. They independently create a byte-identical full `catalogue.csv` of every finite-delay codebook.

**Symmetry census.** Both global bit complementation and reversal preserve every directed path delay, since they act on the same framed-code condition. The Python verifier computes each orbit under their four-element group and finds the orbit sizes reported above; the orbit totals reproduce every unquotiented count. No additional symmetries are silently identified.

## Verification

Requires Python 3 standard library and a C++17 compiler; no third-party packages or external data. From this directory, run `python3 delay_verify.py python_catalogue.csv` and compile `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 delay_verify.cpp -o delay_verify`, then run `./delay_verify cpp_catalogue.csv`. Both programs independently assert their full delay counts; `cmp python_catalogue.csv cpp_catalogue.csv` and `cmp python_catalogue.csv catalogue.csv` confirm complete catalogue equality. The embedded catalogue has exactly \(9{,}204\) data rows plus one header. Its SHA-256 is `8f42b6607925826293bfdc79878c2a8c0a11051d1e1cc45f6dc28e8d382e8ea6`.

As short checks: the six Lyndon representatives listed above have \(\delta=2\); the codes `00001,00011,00101,00111,01101,11011` and `00001,00011,00101,01101,10111,11100` have delays three and four respectively.

## Relationship to prior work

Golomb and Gordon introduced the foundational bounded-synchronization-delay problem. Cummings's 1987 survey discusses the primitive-necklace upper bound and bounded delay; Cummings's 1988 paper proves that the lexicographically minimal primitive rotations yield a maximal-size bounded-delay code and investigates its geometry. The 1998 de Bruijn-graph paper studies related constructions and their synchronization properties. These results concern capacity and special constructions, not the displayed complete spectrum of every binary size-six length-five codebook.

The comma-free \(\delta=1\) stratum, exactly \(86\) labeled optimum codes, was classified earlier. We expressly **exclude** this known part from the new contribution. The difference between a comma-free code and one of finite delay two, three or four is the number of complete false-framed words that can persist, not the maximum word count. Later work by Vohl, Quimper and Dubé concerns inserting synchronization control patterns into compressed streams with different length and delay parameters; it does not furnish an implication to this equality-case enumeration.

## Limitations

The theorem is an exhaustive finite classification for binary length-five, size-six codebooks. It does not imply a closed formula for longer block lengths, a raw-symbol delay optimum, a decoding-performance guarantee under noise, or novelty of the previously known comma-free stratum. Some early specialist publications could not be examined in complete text; the remaining bibliographic risk of an unindexed finite enumeration or alternative delay normalization is genuine.
