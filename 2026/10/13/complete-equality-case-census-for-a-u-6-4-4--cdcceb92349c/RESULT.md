# Complete equality-case census for \(A_U(6,4)=4\)
## Finding
For permutation codes \(C\subseteq S_6\) with minimum Ulam distance at least \(4\), the known optimum \(A_U(6,4)=4\) has exactly \(53{,}640\) labeled equality cases. Every equality case is equidistant: all six pairwise Ulam distances are exactly \(4\). Under the explicitly checked \(1440\)-element subgroup generated by arbitrary relabeling of the six symbols and reversal of the six one-line positions, these maximum codes split into exactly \(57\) orbits: seven orbits of size \(360\), twenty-nine of size \(720\), and twenty-one of size \(1440\). None of the maximum codes is a left coset of a subgroup of \(S_6\).

## Assumptions and scope
A permutation is written in one-line form. For \(\pi,\sigma\in S_6\), the Ulam distance is \(d_U(\pi,\sigma)=6-L(\pi,\sigma)\), where \(L(\pi,\sigma)\) is the length of a longest common subsequence. The classification is for unrestricted labeled subsets of \(S_6\) having minimum Ulam distance at least \(4\). The stated symmetry group is only the explicit subgroup generated by symbol relabeling and position reversal; no claim is made that it is the full isometry group.

## Proof
The Ulam metric is left invariant, so every nonempty code can be relabeled to contain the identity permutation. Among the \(720\) permutations, exactly \(132\) lie at Ulam distance at least \(4\) from the identity. Form a compatibility graph on those \(132\) candidates, joining two candidates when their mutual Ulam distance is at least \(4\). Direct construction gives \(612\) edges.

A four-word code containing the identity is exactly a triangle in this compatibility graph. Direct triangle enumeration gives exactly \(298\) triangles. For every one of those triangles, the intersection of the three corresponding neighbor sets is empty, so no triangle extends to a four-clique. Hence no five-word code exists, while the triangles give four-word codes. This independently establishes \(A_U(6,4)=4\) and gives exactly \(298\) normalized maxima containing the identity.

Applying all \(720\) symbol relabelings to all normalized maxima and deduplicating produces exactly \(53{,}640\) labeled maxima. Direct distance evaluation on every one of them gives the unique six-distance profile \((4,4,4,4,4,4)\).

For the declared symmetry subgroup, all \(720\) relabelings and their compositions with position reversal are applied to each maximum code. The resulting orbit partition has \(57\) blocks, with size distribution \(360^7,720^{29},1440^{21}\). Finally, each of the \(298\) normalized maxima is tested for closure under permutation composition; none is a subgroup. If a maximum code were a left coset of a subgroup, normalizing any member to the identity would yield a subgroup maximum, so no maximum is such a coset.

## Relationship to prior work
Farnoud, Skachek, and Milenkovic introduced translocation rank codes, proved that translocation distance equals Ulam distance, and derived general size bounds. Göloglu, Lember, Riet, and Skachek later reported computer-search values for small parameters; their Table I records the exact maximum \(A_U(6,4)=4\). Their published table records only the maximum size, not the number or structure of equality cases. The later sphere-size work of Kong gives exact Ulam sphere formulas and nonexistence of nontrivial perfect permutation codes, but does not classify the \((6,4)\) equality cases. The present claim is therefore the complete labeled and symmetry-refined census at a previously solved optimum, rather than a new value of \(A_U(6,4)\).

## Limitations
The result is finite and specific to length \(6\) and minimum distance \(4\). The \(1440\)-element action is an explicitly verified natural isometry subgroup, not asserted to be the full automorphism group of the Ulam metric space. The literature comparison found no published equality-case census, but an obscure or unindexed enumeration remains a residual originality risk.
