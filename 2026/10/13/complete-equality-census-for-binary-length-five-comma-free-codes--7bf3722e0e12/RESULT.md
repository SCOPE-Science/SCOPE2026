# Complete equality census for binary length-five comma-free codes
## Finding
For binary fixed-length comma-free codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^5\), the classical maximum size is \(6\), and exactly \(86\) labeled codes attain it. Every maximum contains exactly one representative from each of the six primitive cyclic necklaces and therefore has Hamming-weight multiset \((1,2,2,3,3,4)\). Under the natural order-four action generated by global bit complementation and reversal of each word, the \(86\) maxima form exactly \(25\) orbits: \(18\) orbits of size \(4\) and \(7\) of size \(2\). Exactly \(10\) maxima are complement-invariant, none is reversal-invariant, and exactly \(4\) are reverse-complement-invariant; equivalently, among the seven size-two orbits, five are stabilized by complement and two by reverse-complement.

## Assumptions and scope
A binary word has length five. For words \(u=u_1\cdots u_5\) and \(v=v_1\cdots v_5\), an overlap at shift \(s\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) is the length-five word \(u_{s+1}\cdots u_5v_1\cdots v_s\). A code is comma-free when no such overlap of any ordered pair of codewords, including a word paired with itself, is itself a codeword. Labeled codes are literal subsets of \(\{0,1\}^5\), without quotienting by symmetries. The symmetry statement concerns only the explicit natural group generated by reversing every word and complementing every bit; no assertion is made that this is the full automorphism group of the compatibility structure.

## Proof
Any codeword in a comma-free code must be primitive under cyclic rotation. Indeed, if a length-five word has a nontrivial cyclic period, then a nontrivial boundary overlap in the concatenation of the word with itself reproduces the word. Likewise, two distinct rotations from the same primitive cyclic necklace cannot both lie in a comma-free code: one of them occurs as a boundary overlap of the other with itself.

Because five is prime, the only nonprimitive binary length-five words are \(00000\) and \(11111\). Thus there are \(30\) primitive words, partitioned into six cyclic necklaces of five rotations each. Every comma-free code has at most one representative from each necklace, so its size is at most \(6\). The classical length-five theorem of Golomb, Gordon, and Welch attains this bound for every alphabet, and hence the binary optimum is \(6\). Therefore every binary optimum chooses exactly one representative from every primitive necklace.

The equality cases were then enumerated exhaustively in two independent finite ways. First, all \(5^6=15625\) choices of one rotation from each of the six necklaces were tested directly against the comma-free overlap definition; exactly \(86\) choices pass. Second, all \(\binom{30}{6}=593775\) six-subsets of primitive words were tested using a separately constructed pair-compatibility relation; exactly the same \(86\) subsets pass. Since every optimum must be a transversal of the six primitive necklaces, either enumeration is exhaustive, and their agreement supplies an independent implementation cross-check.

For the weight profile, a primitive necklace of weight \(r\) has five words. Hence the number of primitive necklaces of each weight is \(\binom{5}{r}/5\) for \(r\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), namely \(1,2,2,1\). Every optimum contains one word from every necklace, forcing the weight multiset \((1,2,2,3,3,4)\).

Global complement and reversal preserve the comma-free property. Direct action on the complete list of \(86\) optima gives fixed-set counts \(10\), \(0\), and \(4\) for complement, reversal, and reverse-complement respectively. Burnside's lemma therefore gives \((86+10+0+4)/4=25\) natural-group orbits. Explicit orbit construction gives eighteen orbits of size four and seven of size two; of the size-two orbits, five have complement in their stabilizer and two have reverse-complement in their stabilizer.

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` regenerates all \(32\) words, determines primitivity from cyclic rotations, reconstructs the six primitive necklaces, performs both exhaustive equality-case enumerations, and requires exact set equality between them. It then checks the forced weight profile, all three nonidentity fixed-point counts, the \(25\)-orbit decomposition, and stabilizer types against `census.json` and the full canonical list in `maxima.txt`. The verification is exact finite arithmetic and set manipulation; there are no randomized steps, numerical tolerances, or external data dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work
Crick, Griffith, and Orgel introduced comma-free coding in 1957 in the genetic-coding setting. Golomb, Gordon, and Welch formalized comma-free dictionaries, proved the primitive-necklace upper bound, and proved that it is attained for length five; this yields the classical binary optimum \(6\). Jiggs' later survey records extremal sizes and contemporary constructions. The present finding concerns a different layer of information: the complete set of equality cases at the binary length-five parameter, their forced weight profile, and their quotient under the elementary complement/reversal symmetries. The inspected classical sources establish the optimum and constructions but do not state this \(86\)-code equality census or its \(25\)-orbit decomposition. A separate modern result on length-five non-overlapping codes concerns the stricter cross-bifix/non-overlap condition and has binary optimum \(2\), so it does not imply this comma-free classification.

## Limitations
This is a finite equality-case classification for the binary length-five comma-free problem. It does not classify maxima for larger alphabets or other lengths, and it does not claim that the order-four complement/reversal group is the full automorphism group of the finite compatibility structure. Originality searches cannot rule out an obscure or poorly indexed historical enumeration; the claim is limited to the absence of an implication-equivalent statement in the inspected primary literature, focused database searches, and the checked prior findings.
