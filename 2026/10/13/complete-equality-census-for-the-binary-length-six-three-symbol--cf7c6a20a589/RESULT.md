# Complete equality census for the binary length-six three-symbol MDS boundary
## Finding
For binary cyclic \(3\)-symbol codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) with minimum \(3\)-symbol distance \(6\),
\[
|C|\le 8.
\]
Equality is attainable. Exactly \(776\) labeled eight-word codes attain equality. Of these, exactly \(232\) are affine \(3\)-flats in \(\mathbb F_2^6\) and \(544\) are nonlinear.

Under the natural order-\(768\) group generated by all translations \(x\mapsto x+a\) in \(\mathbb F_2^6\) together with the dihedral symmetries of the six cyclic coordinate positions, the \(776\) maxima split into exactly ten orbits. Their orbit sizes are
\[
8,\ 24,\ 24,\ 32,\ 48,\ 64,\ 96,\ 96,\ 192,\ 192,
\]
with stabilizer orders
\[
96,\ 32,\ 32,\ 24,\ 16,\ 12,\ 8,\ 8,\ 4,\ 4.
\]
The affine maxima are exactly the six orbits of sizes
\[
8,\ 24,\ 24,\ 32,\ 48,\ 96,
\]
so the remaining four orbits, totaling \(544\) codes, are nonlinear.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_5)\in\{0,1\}^6\), indices are read modulo \(6\). Its cyclic \(3\)-symbol read vector is the sequence of triples
\[
(x_i,x_5,x_6),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.
\]
The \(3\)-symbol distance \(d_3(x,y)\) is the Hamming distance between these two six-term read vectors. A code is admissible when \(d_3(x,y)\ge6\) for every two distinct codewords.

“Labeled” means that coordinates and binary symbols are not factored out. The orbit statement is only for the explicitly stated translation-dihedral subgroup.

## Proof
The Singleton bound for \(b\)-symbol codes gives
\[
M\le q^{n-d_b+b}.
\]
At \(q=2\), \(n=6\), \(b=3\), and \(d_b=6\), this yields \(M\le 2^3=8\).

The supplied verifier independently constructs the complete \(64\times64\) distance matrix in two ways. First it compares the six cyclic length-three windows directly. Second it computes the support of the difference word and counts exactly those cyclic starting positions whose length-three windows intersect that support. The two distance matrices are required to agree entrywise.

The compatibility graph has the \(64\) binary words as vertices and joins two words exactly when their \(3\)-symbol distance is \(6\). A complete Bron--Kerbosch traversal finds maximum clique size \(8\) and exactly \(776\) maximum cliques. An independent fixed-size increasing-vertex clique census reproduces exactly the same \(776\) eight-cliques and finds no nine-clique.

For each maximum code, affine structure is checked directly after translation to contain zero: the translated eight-word set must be closed under binary addition. This yields exactly \(232\) affine maxima and \(544\) nonlinear maxima.

Finally, the verifier generates all \(64\) binary translations and all \(12\) dihedral coordinate symmetries, applies the resulting \(768\) transformations to every maximum, and computes the complete orbit partition and stabilizers stated above.

## Relationship to prior work
Yaakobi, Bruck, and Siegel introduced the \(b\)-symbol read-channel framework for \(b\ge3\), extending the earlier symbol-pair model. Ding, Zhang, and Ge then proved the general Singleton bound for \(b\)-symbol codes and constructed linear MDS families. Their construction theorem includes binary linear MDS \(3\)-symbol codes with \(d_3=6\) at every length \(n\ge6\), so in particular the numerical optimum \(8\) at the present parameter is prior work.

The contribution here is the complete all-code equality layer at the smallest length covered by that family: the exact count \(776\), the split into \(232\) affine and \(544\) nonlinear optima, and the ten translation-dihedral symmetry types. The inspected MDS paper explicitly focuses on linear \(b\)-symbol codes and does not state this arbitrary-code census.

A nearby finite result for ordinary symbol-pair distance at length six has a different compatibility relation: it has only \(32\) maxima and all are affine. Direct comparison of the two pairwise admissibility predicates on all \(\binom{64}{2}\) word pairs finds \(96\) pairs on which they differ, so that result does not imply the present \(3\)-symbol classification.

## Limitations
This is a finite classification only for binary length six, cyclic window size three, and minimum \(3\)-symbol distance six. It does not classify other lengths, other alphabet sizes, other read-window sizes, shortened or noncyclic window models, or larger minimum distances.

The literature comparison cannot rule out an obscure or unindexed finite census of the same parameter. The directly relevant full-text MDS source supplies the bound and linear existence, not the arbitrary-code equality enumeration.
