# Complete equality census for the binary length-six three-symbol channel

## Finding
For binary cyclic \(3\)-symbol codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) with minimum \(3\)-symbol distance \(6\),
\[
|C|\le 8.
\]
Equality is attainable. Exactly \(776\) labeled eight-word codes attain equality. Of these, exactly \(232\) are affine \(3\)-flats in \(\mathbb F_2^6\) and \(544\) are nonlinear (not affine).

Under the natural order-\(768\) group generated by all translations \(x\mapsto x+a\) in \(\mathbb F_2^6\) together with the dihedral symmetries of the six cyclic coordinate positions, the \(776\) maxima split into exactly ten orbits. Their orbit sizes are
\[
8,\ 24,\ 24,\ 32,\ 48,\ 64,\ 96,\ 96,\ 192,\ 192,
\]
with stabilizer orders
\[
96,\ 32,\ 32,\ 24,\ 16,\ 12,\ 8,\ 8,\ 4,\ 4.
\]
The affine maxima are exactly the six orbits of sizes
\[
8,\ 24,\ 24,\ 32,\ 48,\ 96,
\]
so the remaining four orbits, totaling \(544\) codes, are nonlinear.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_5)\in\{0,1\}^6\), indices are read modulo \(6\). Its cyclic \(3\)-symbol read vector is the sequence of triples
\[
(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z.
\]
The \(3\)-symbol distance \(d_3(x,y)\) is the Hamming distance between these two six-term read vectors. A code is admissible when \(d_3(x,y)\ge6\) for every two distinct codewords.

“Labeled” means that coordinates and binary symbols are not factored out. The orbit statement is only for the explicitly stated translation-dihedral subgroup.

## Proof
Every fixed window projection is injective on an admissible code: agreement in that window would give distance at most five. There are \(2^3=8\) possible window values, proving the upper bound. The eight words \((a,b,c,a,b,c)\), with \(a,b,c\in\{0,1\}\), attain it, since each consecutive window reads a cyclic permutation of \((a,b,c)\). At equality every window projection is bijective.

The supplied verifier constructs the \(64\times64\) distance matrix in two ways. First it compares the six cyclic length-three windows directly. Second it computes the support of the difference word and counts exactly those cyclic starting positions whose length-three windows intersect that support. The two distance matrices are required to agree entrywise.

The compatibility graph has the \(64\) binary words as vertices and joins two words exactly when their \(3\)-symbol distance is \(6\). A complete Bron–Kerbosch traversal finds maximum clique size \(8\) and exactly \(776\) maximum cliques. A separate fixed-size increasing-vertex clique census reproduces exactly the same \(776\) eight-cliques and finds no nine-clique. Each code occurs once in the fixed-size search because its vertices have a unique increasing order and every extension is tested against all earlier vertices.

For each maximum code, affine structure is checked directly after translation to contain zero: the translated eight-word set is a linear subspace precisely if it is closed under binary addition. This yields exactly \(232\) affine maxima and \(544\) nonlinear maxima.

Translations preserve window disagreements, and the dihedral coordinate maps permute the windows and may reverse the entries of each window. The verifier generates all \(64\) binary translations and all \(12\) dihedral coordinate symmetries, applies the resulting \(768\) transformations to every maximum, and computes the orbit partition and stabilizers stated above.

## Relationship to prior work
Yaakobi, Bruck, and Siegel introduced the \(b\)-symbol read-channel framework for \(b\ge3\), extending the earlier symbol-pair model. Ding, Zhang, and Ge then proved a general Singleton bound for \(b\)-symbol codes and constructed linear MDS families. Their results include the bound and its attainment at these parameters; the numerical optimum \(8\) is not new.

The contribution is the complete all-code equality layer in the shortest binary length permitting \(d_3=6\): the exact count \(776\), the split into \(232\) affine and \(544\) nonlinear optima, and the ten translation-dihedral symmetry types. The linear MDS construction does not classify all codes whose six window projections are bijective.

A nearby census for cyclic symbol-pair distance at length six, with minimum pair distance \(5\), has \(32\) eight-word maxima and all are affine. Its admissible pairs are a proper subset of those for the present \(3\)-symbol distance. Indeed, a length-three agreement forces two length-two agreements. Conversely, if a difference mask has two length-two agreements but no length-three agreement, its cyclic zero pattern must have two separate runs of exactly two zeros. The three rotations of `001001` are the only such masks (0 denotes agreement). Each mask accounts for \(64/2\) unordered pairs, giving exactly \(96\) pairs on which the two admissibility predicates differ.

## Limitations
This is a finite classification only for binary length six, cyclic window size three, and minimum \(3\)-symbol distance six. It does not classify other lengths, other alphabet sizes, other read-window sizes, shortened or noncyclic window models, or larger minimum distances.

The literature comparison cannot rule out an unindexed finite census of the same parameter. The directly relevant MDS source supplies the bound and linear existence, not the arbitrary-code equality enumeration.
