# Complete equality classification for binary length-eight double-asymmetric-error codes
## Finding
For arbitrary binary codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^8\) correcting up to two one-direction asymmetric errors, equivalently satisfying minimum asymmetric distance \(\Delta(C)\ge 3\) for \(\Delta(x,y)=\max\{|\{i:x_i=1,y_i=0\}|,|\{i:x_i=0,y_i=1\}|\}\), the maximum cardinality is \(|C|=7\). There are exactly \(181{,}440\) labeled unordered maximum codes. Under coordinate permutations \(S_8\), they form five orbits of sizes \(20{,}160,40{,}320,40{,}320,40{,}320,40{,}320\), with stabilizer orders \(2,1,1,1,1\). Under the order-\(80{,}640\) group generated by coordinate permutations and global bit complementation, they form exactly three orbits of sizes \(20{,}160,80{,}640,80{,}640\), with stabilizer orders \(4,1,1\).

A representative of the orbit of size \(20{,}160\) is
\[
\{00000000,00000111,00111000,01011011,10101101,11010100,11111111\}.
\]
Representatives of the two orbits of size \(80{,}640\) are
\[
\{00000000,00000111,00111000,01011011,10101101,11010100,11110111\}
\]
and
\[
\{00000001,00001110,00110011,01011101,10111111,11010000,11100110\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A binary word is identified with its support. For \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^8\), define
\[
N(x,y)=|\{i:x_i=1,\ y_i=0\}|,
\qquad
\Delta(x,y)=\max\{N(x,y),N(y,x)\}.
\]
The one-direction two-error descendant ball of \(x\) is the set of words obtained by changing at most two of its \(1\)-coordinates to \(0\). The claim concerns arbitrary nonlinear codes and does not assume constant weight, linearity, systematic form, or a particular construction.

## Proof
For two words \(x\) and \(y\), their radius-two one-direction descendant balls intersect exactly when there is a word \(z\subseteq x\cap y\) with \(|x\setminus z|\le2\) and \(|y\setminus z|\le2\). Such a \(z\) exists exactly when \(|x\setminus y|\le2\) and \(|y\setminus x|\le2\), which is exactly \(\Delta(x,y)\le2\). Therefore a code corrects every pattern of at most two one-direction asymmetric errors if and only if every distinct pair has \(\Delta\ge3\).

Construct the compatibility graph on all \(2^8=256\) binary words, joining two words when \(\Delta\ge3\). Codes in scope are precisely cliques. The standalone verifier performs an exhaustive ordered branch-and-bound clique search with an initial seven-word lower bound. It visits \(16{,}853{,}807\) search nodes, proves that no clique of size \(8\) exists, and enumerates exactly \(181{,}440\) cliques of size \(7\).

For equality classification, the verifier acts on the complete maximum-code set by the seven adjacent coordinate transpositions, which generate \(S_8\). Breadth-first orbit closure gives five coordinate-permutation orbits with sizes \(20{,}160\) and four copies of \(40{,}320\). Adding global complementation merges two pairs of the \(40{,}320\)-orbits and leaves the \(20{,}160\)-orbit invariant, giving three orbits of sizes \(20{,}160,80{,}640,80{,}640\). Orbit-stabilizer then gives the stated stabilizer orders. Every generated image is asserted to belong to the exhaustive maximum-code set.

## Relationship to prior work
Radonjic–Bala–Vujicic study the practically motivated problem of correcting single and double asymmetric errors and give necessary and sufficient syndrome conditions for their integer-code construction; their inspected full text does not optimize unrestricted binary length-eight codes or classify equality cases. Constantin–Rao is a foundational general source on binary asymmetric error-correcting codes. Fang–van Tilborg explicitly classify optimal codes for minimum asymmetric distance \(2\) at several small lengths, showing that equality classification is a natural question in this literature, but their published abstract states results only for distance \(2\), not the distance-\(3\) problem considered here.

Targeted searches for the exact parameter, aliases involving two Z-channel errors, the count \(181{,}440\), and orbit-classification language did not locate a statement implying the present equality census. This search evidence is not a proof of novelty; the residual risk is an older unindexed table, thesis, or full-text classification not exposed by the searched records.

## Limitations
The result is a finite exact classification at one parameter pair. The upper bound and census are computational, although the channel-to-metric equivalence is proved directly and the verifier is exhaustive with no randomized choices, heuristic cutoff, solver dependency, or timeout. The work does not claim a formula for other lengths or error radii, nor does it claim that coordinate permutations plus global complementation are the entire abstract isometry group of the metric.
