# Complete equality classification of \(p_3(3,4)=9\) perfect hash families
## Finding
Let \(C\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}^3\). Call \(C\) a three-row strength-three perfect hash family when, for every three distinct columns \(x,y,z\in C\), at least one coordinate takes three distinct values on \(x,y,z\). Equivalently, \(C\) is a \(\mathrm{PHF}(3;|C|,4,3)\).

The exact maximum is
\[
p_3(3,4)=9.
\]
There are exactly \(4608\) literal maximum column sets, with the three coordinates and four symbols in each coordinate labeled. Exactly \(648\) of these maxima contain \(000\).

Under the natural group
\[
S_4^3times S_3,
\]
of order \(24^3\cdot 6=82944\), generated by independent symbol permutations in each coordinate and permutations of the three coordinates, all \(4608\) maxima lie in one orbit. Thus the stabilizer of every maximum in this group has order \(18\).

Every maximum has symbol multiplicities \((3,2,2,2)\), up to order, in each coordinate. Among its \(inom{9}{2}=36\) unordered column pairs, exactly \(18\) have Hamming distance \(2\) and exactly \(18\) have Hamming distance \(3\).

One representative is
\[
\{	exttt{000},	exttt{011},	exttt{022},	exttt{103},	exttt{131},	exttt{213},	exttt{232},	exttt{323},	exttt{330}\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Columns are distinct and are treated as an unordered set. “Literal” means before quotienting by row permutations or symbol relabelings; it does not mean that the nine columns themselves are ordered. The equality classification is only for length \(3\), alphabet size \(4\), and strength \(3\).

The symmetry statement is for the explicit natural action \(S_4^3times S_3\). No claim is made that this is a larger abstract automorphism group of a derived constraint hypergraph, although it is the standard row/symbol equivalence group for these arrays.

## Proof
There are \(4^3=64\) possible columns. A triple of columns is forbidden precisely when none of the three coordinates contains three distinct symbols. Direct enumeration gives \(8640\) forbidden triples.

The natural symbol action is transitive on the \(64\) columns, so any nonempty perfect hash family can be moved so that one selected column is \(000\). The verifier therefore performs an exhaustive strictly increasing backtracking search over families containing \(000\), pruning a candidate as soon as it completes a forbidden triple with two already chosen columns. It finds no size-\(10\) family and finds exactly \(648\) size-\(9\) families containing \(000\). This proves the upper bound \(p_3(3,4)\le 9\), while any enumerated size-\(9\) family gives the matching lower bound.

For an independent count of all literal maxima, apply the \(64\) coordinatewise translations modulo \(4\) to the \(648\) normalized maxima and deduplicate. Every maximum occurs because translating any one of its nine columns to \(000\) produces a normalized maximum. The resulting set has exactly \(4608\) members, and the incidence identity
\[
4608\cdot 9=648\cdot 64
\]
checks the same number without relying on deduplication multiplicities.

Finally, the verifier applies all \(6\cdot24^3=82944\) natural row/symbol transformations to one representative. Its orbit is exactly the complete set of \(4608\) maxima. Orbit-stabilizer therefore gives stabilizer order \(82944/4608=18\). Direct checks on all \(4608\) maxima give the asserted coordinate-multiplicity and Hamming-distance profiles.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It reconstructs all \(64\) columns and all \(8640\) forbidden triples, excludes a normalized size-\(10\) family, enumerates the \(648\) normalized size-\(9\) maxima, expands them to all \(4608\) literal maxima, reconstructs the full natural orbit, and verifies the structural profiles for every maximum.

A separate mixed-integer formulation with one binary variable per column and one inequality for each forbidden triple independently returned optimum \(9\) during review; the packaged dependency-free verifier supplies the exhaustive replay used for the result.

## Relationship to prior work
Walker and Colbourn define \(\mathrm{PHF}(N;k,v,t)\), develop direct and recursive constructions, use tabu search, and give the first general tables of best-known family sizes. Their computational section explicitly describes the tabulated entries as maximum values of \(k\) “for which PHFs are found,” and their table-generation section is an existence-table workflow; the accessible full text does not give an equality-case enumeration or an equivalence classification for \(\mathrm{PHF}(3;9,4,3)\).

Shangguan and Ge later make \(p_3(3,q)\) a central extremal function, prove the asymptotic relation \(q^{2-o(1)}<p_3(3,q)=o(q^2)\), and connect the problem to multipartite hypergraph extremal theory. Those results do not determine the finite \(q=4\) equality structure.

A semantic search of published findings for the exact parameter, the aliases “three-row perfect hash family” and “separating hash family,” and the final counts \(4608\), \(648\), and stabilizer \(18\) found no implication-equivalent classification. The closest published structured result concerns group-developed three-row perfect hash families, which imposes an additional translation-invariance hypothesis and does not classify all \(q=4\) maxima.

## Limitations
This is a finite exact equality classification at the first alphabet size strictly larger than the strength. It does not give a general formula for \(p_3(3,q)\) or classify maxima for other alphabet sizes. The historical `phftables.com` site named in the 2007 paper was not accessible in this run; the paper itself states that the site stores explicit constructions, not a complete isomorphism census. An obscure or unindexed earlier equality enumeration remains a residual priority risk.
