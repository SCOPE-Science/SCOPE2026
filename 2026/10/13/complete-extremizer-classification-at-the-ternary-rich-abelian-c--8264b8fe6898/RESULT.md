# Complete extremizer classification at the ternary rich abelian-cube boundary
## Finding
A finite ternary word is called rich when a word of length \(n\) has exactly \(n+1\) distinct palindromic factors, including the empty word. An abelian cube is a factor \(xyz\) with
\[
|x|=|y|=|z|>0
\]
and with \(x,y,z\) having the same Parikh vector.

Among rich abelian-cube-free words over \(\{0,1,2\}\), the maximum length is \(180\), as reported by Andrade and Mol. At that maximum there are exactly \(24\) words. They form exactly four orbits under permutations of the alphabet and exactly two orbits under the group generated by alphabet permutations and reversal.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1,2\}\). Richness uses the standard convention counting the empty word, so a word of length \(n\) is rich when it has \(n+1\) distinct palindromic factors.

Two words are in the same alphabet-permutation orbit when one is obtained from the other by a permutation of \(0,1,2\). For the second orbit count, reversal is also allowed. The claim classifies only the equality cases at the published extremal length \(180\).

## Proof
The search uses the characterization cited by Andrade and Mol: a finite word is rich if and only if every prefix has a unioccurrent palindromic suffix. When a rich prefix is extended by one letter, it therefore suffices to test whether the new prefix has a unioccurrent palindromic suffix. A finite word has at most one such suffix, necessarily its longest palindromic suffix, so the test is exact.

Similarly, when an abelian-cube-free prefix is extended by one letter, every newly created forbidden factor must end at the new final position. For each possible block length \(m\), the verifier compares the three Parikh vectors of the suffix blocks of length \(m\). Thus every branch retained by the recursion is rich and abelian-cube-free, and every rejected branch violates one of the defining properties.

Alphabet relabeling preserves both properties. Every nonempty ternary word has a unique restricted-growth representative obtained by relabeling symbols in order of first appearance: the first occurring symbol becomes \(0\), the next previously unseen symbol becomes \(1\), and the third becomes \(2\). The exhaustive recursion generates exactly these representatives.

The exact census gives four normalized words at length \(180\) and none at length \(181\). Hence no longer word exists, since any longer word would have an admissible prefix of length \(181\). All four length-\(180\) representatives use all three letters, so each has six distinct alphabet relabelings. Therefore the number of labeled extremizers is
\[
4\cdot 6=24.
\]
Canonicalizing the reversals sends classes \(1\leftrightarrow3\) and \(2\leftrightarrow4\). Each resulting permutation-plus-reversal orbit has size \(12\), proving that there are exactly two such orbits.

## Relationship to prior work
Andrade and Mol report that the longest ternary rich word avoiding abelian cubes has length \(180\), obtained by backtracking. Their full paper states the extremal length but does not give the number of length-\(180\) words, their normalized representatives, or their symmetry-orbit structure.

Targeted searches for the exact extremal count, equality-case terminology, restricted-growth classes, and the first representative above did not locate a published classification. The authors' cited 2024 undergraduate thesis is a plausible source of additional computational detail, but its repository PDF was not accessible during this comparison, so it remains a residual originality risk rather than evidence of coverage.

## Limitations
The result classifies equality cases only for the ternary abelian-cube-free rich-word extremum. It does not address the much larger binary abelian-fourth-power-free extremum reported in the same paper, nor does it imply an infinite avoidance theorem.

The main residual originality risk is inaccessible computational detail in the cited undergraduate thesis or an unindexed data file. The exhaustive computation proves the mathematical classification independently of that access limitation.
