# Complete extremizer classification for the binary undirected-cube obstruction
## Finding
Let \(u\simeq v\) mean that \(v=u\) or \(v=u^{R}\). A binary word avoids the unary pattern \(\mathtt{xxx}\) in the undirected sense when it has no factor \(ABC\) in which \(A,B,C\) are nonempty equal-length blocks and
\[
A\simeq B\simeq C.
\]

The published maximum length of such a binary word is \(9\). Exactly six words attain that maximum:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathtt{001101100},\quad
\mathtt{001101101},\quad
\mathtt{010010011},\\
&\mathtt{101101100},\quad
\mathtt{110010010},\quad
\mathtt{110010011}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Under the group generated by reversal and binary complementation, these six words form exactly two orbits:
\[
\{\mathtt{001101100},\mathtt{110010011}\}
\]
and
\[
\{\mathtt{001101101},\mathtt{010010011},
\mathtt{101101100},\mathtt{110010010}\}.
\]
The first orbit consists of two complementary palindromes.

## Assumptions and scope
For words \(u,v\) of the same length, \(u\simeq v\) means equality up to reversal. An instance of the unary pattern \(\mathtt{xxx}\) up to \(\simeq\) is therefore a concatenation \(ABC\) of three nonempty blocks having the same length, where each of \(B\) and \(C\) is either \(A\) or \(A^{R}\).

The claim is a complete equality-case classification for the binary finite obstruction used in the source proof that the undirected avoidability index of \(\mathtt{xxx}\) is \(3\). It does not claim a new avoidability-index value; the maximum length \(9\) is prior work.

## Proof
Avoidance of \(\mathtt{xxx}\) up to \(\simeq\) is factorial: every factor of an avoiding word also avoids the pattern. Hence an exhaustive prefix tree over the binary alphabet is complete.

Suppose an avoiding prefix \(w\) is extended by one bit. Any newly created instance must end at the new last position. For every positive integer \(m\) with \(3m\le |w|+1\), take the suffix of length \(3m\) and split it into blocks \(A,B,C\), each of length \(m\). Reject the extension exactly when
\[
B\in\{A,A^{R}\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
C\in\{A,A^{R}\}.
\]
This is precisely the definition of an undirected instance of \(\mathtt{xxx}\), so the test has neither false positives nor false negatives.

Starting with the empty word, the exact numbers of surviving prefixes at lengths \(0,1,\ldots,10\) are
\[
1,2,4,6,10,16,18,12,10,6,0.
\]
Therefore the maximum length is \(9\), there are exactly six equality cases, and no longer binary avoider exists. Reading the six survivors gives the list in the finding.

As an independent boundary check, every factor of each of the six length-\(9\) words is scanned directly for every possible block length \(m\), without using the incremental-prefix argument. All six pass. Closing the six-word set under reversal and complementation gives one orbit of size \(2\) and one orbit of size \(4\), exactly as stated.

## Relationship to prior work
Currie and Mol introduced undirected pattern avoidance and, in the extended version of their work, prove the complete unary-pattern avoidability-index classification. Their proof states that a computer backtracking search finds the longest binary word avoiding \(\mathtt{xxx}\) in the undirected sense to have length \(9\), which supplies the binary lower-bound obstruction needed for the case \(\lambda_{\simeq}(\mathtt{xxx})=3\).

The source gives the scalar maximum \(9\), but it does not list all maximizing words or classify the equality cases under the natural reversal/complement symmetries. The earlier WORDS 2019 version states only that backtracking proves the required binary nonavoidability bound, without the length-\(9\) equality classification.

Targeted semantic searches, exact searches for the six representatives, and searches under the equivalent phrases “undirected cube” and “pattern \(\mathtt{xxx}\) up to reversal” located the source but no published six-word classification.

## Limitations
The maximum length \(9\) and the resulting avoidability-index conclusion are prior work. The new result is only the complete equality set and its two symmetry orbits.

The originality search cannot exclude an unpublished backtracking log, private computation, or unindexed auxiliary file containing the same six words. No claim is made about extremizers for longer unary patterns or for binary patterns on two variables.
