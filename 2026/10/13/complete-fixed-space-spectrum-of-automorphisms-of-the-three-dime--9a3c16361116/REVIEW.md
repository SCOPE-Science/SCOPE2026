# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
Every automorphism is derived from an arbitrary matrix \(A\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\) on the quotient by the center and an arbitrary central covector; bracket preservation scales the center by \(\det A\). The fixed-point equations reduce to \((A-I)v=0\) plus one scalar equation. Splitting \(A\) into five exhaustive strata according to \(\det A=1\) and \(\dim\ker(A-I)\) gives the stated four multiplicities. They sum to \(|\operatorname{Aut}(H_3(\mathbb F_q))|\), and the weighted Burnside sum equals \(3\). Exhaustive checks over \(q=2,3,4,5,7\), including \(\mathbb F_4\), agree exactly.

## Originality — PASS
The closest published same-object result located gives only the fixed-point-free coefficient \(N_0(q)=q^3(q+1)(q-2)^2\); that coefficient is explicitly credited. Fisher--Gray--Hydon give the classical automorphism matrix form for the real three-dimensional nilpotent algebra \(A_{3,1}\), but not a finite-field fixed-space distribution. Searches using fixed-space, fixed-set-size, theta-value, and exact-fiber formulations did not locate the remaining three coefficients or a theorem implying the complete spectrum. A 2018 paper by Liu and Tang is directly about automorphism groups of Heisenberg Lie (super)algebras. Its accessible full article was inspected: it constructs inner, central, and involutionary automorphism subgroups over a commutative ring and does not enumerate finite-field fixed-point or fixed-space fibers.

Residual risk remains that another finite-field source tabulates the same distribution under different terminology. The new claim is the complete four-fiber spectrum and its Burnside consequence, not the known automorphism parametrization, named automorphism subgroups, or the already published dimension-zero fiber.

## Value — PASS
Counting automorphisms by fixed-set size is a natural refinement of fixed-point-free enumeration. Here the full distribution is uniform over all finite fields, handles the characteristic-two boundary, and turns the isolated known zero-dimensional coefficient into a complete theta spectrum. The Burnside identity gives an intrinsic interpretation through the three element-orbits, so the formulas form a coherent structural invariant rather than a disconnected finite census.
