# Complete Galois-hull profile of scaled generalized Gabidulin codes
## Finding

Let \(N=2m\), let \(K=\mathbb F_{q^N}\), and let \((\alpha,\lambda)\) be a scaled trace-self-dual \(\mathbb F_q\)-basis of \(K\), so
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_q}(\lambda\alpha_i\alpha_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\]
Fix \(s\) with \(\gcd(s,N)=1\), let \(1\le k\le m\), and put
\[
C=G_{k,s}(\alpha).
\]
For \(0\le e<N\), write \(u\) for the inverse of \(s\) modulo \(N\), put \(t\equiv eu\pmod N\) with \(0\le t<N\), and set
\[
\delta_N(t)=\min\{t,N-t\}.
\]
For the \(e\)-Galois product
\[
\langle x,y\rangle_e=\sum_{r=1}^{N}x_r y_r^{q^e},
\]
define the right and left duals by
\[
C^{\perp_{e,R}}=\{y:\langle c,y\rangle_e=0\text{ for every }c\in C\},
\qquad
C^{\perp_{e,L}}=\{y:\langle y,c\rangle_e=0\text{ for every }c\in C\}.
\]
Then
\[
C^{\perp_{e,R}}=G_{N-k,s}\!\left(\alpha^{q^{(k-t)s}}\right),
\qquad
C^{\perp_{e,L}}=G_{N-k,s}\!\left(\alpha^{q^{(k+t)s}}\right),
\]
and both hull conventions have the same dimension
\[
\dim_K\bigl(C\cap C^{\perp_{e,R}}\bigr)
=\dim_K\bigl(C\cap C^{\perp_{e,L}}\bigr)
=\min\{k,\delta_N(t)\}.
\]
All Frobenius exponents in the locator vector are interpreted modulo \(N\).

Consequently the Euclidean hull, corresponding to \(e=0\), is trivial. For the Hermitian involution \(e=m\), one has \(t=m\), hence the hull has dimension \(k\); this recovers the Hermitian self-orthogonality in the source and shows that the case \(k=m\) is Hermitian self-dual. More generally, the entire Galois-hull profile depends on \(s\) only by the permutation \(e\mapsto es^{-1}\pmod N\).

## Assumptions and scope

The length is exactly \(N=2m\), matching the setting in which scaled trace-self-dual bases are used in the source. The step \(s\) is coprime to \(N\), and \(1\le k\le N/2\). The code is the generalized Gabidulin code generated by the first \(k\) \(q^s\)-Frobenius rows of the basis vector \(\alpha\).

The result concerns Galois duality for these scaled-basis representatives. It does not assert that every generalized Gabidulin representative has the same hull dimensions under arbitrary rank-metric equivalence.

## Proof

For cyclic indices \(i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), define
\[
v_i=\alpha^{q^{is}}.
\]
Because \(\gcd(s,N)=1\), the vectors \(v_0,\ldots,v_{N-1}\) are the rows of a full Moore matrix in permuted Frobenius order, hence form a \(K\)-basis of \(K^N\).

Duy Ho's Lemma 4.1 proves that for a scaled trace-self-dual basis,
\[
S_r:=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\alpha_a\alpha_a^{q^r}
\]
satisfies \(S_0=\lambda^{-1}\) and \(S_r=0\) for every nonzero residue \(r\pmod N\). Using this identity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle_e
&=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\alpha_a^{q^{is}}\alpha_a^{q^{js+e}}\\
&=\left(S_{(j-i)s+e}\right)^{q^{is}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle_e\ne0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(j-i)s+e\equiv0\pmod N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
j\equiv i-t\pmod N.
\]
Thus the \(e\)-Galois pairing matrix in the basis \((v_i)\) is a nonzero diagonal scaling of a cyclic permutation matrix.

Now
\[
C=\operatorname{span}_K\{v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}\}.
\]
A basis vector \(v_j\) lies in the right dual \(C^{\perp_{e,R}}\) exactly when \(j\) is not one of
\[
-t,1-t,\ldots,k-1-t\pmod N.
\]
The complementary cyclic interval is
\[
k-t,k+1-t,\ldots,N-1-t,
\]
which gives
\[
C^{\perp_{e,R}}=G_{N-k,s}\!\left(\alpha^{q^{(k-t)s}}\right).
\]
Reversing the arguments gives \(\langle v_j,v_i\rangle_e\ne0\) exactly when \(j\equiv i+t\pmod N\), so the left dual is
\[
C^{\perp_{e,L}}=G_{N-k,s}\!\left(\alpha^{q^{(k+t)s}}\right).
\]

For the right hull, the common rows are those \(v_i\) with \(i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) that avoid the translated interval \(\{-t,\ldots,k-1-t\}\). The left hull replaces \(-t\) by \(t\), which has the same circular distance from zero. Since \(k\le N/2\), two cyclic intervals of length \(k\) separated by circular distance \(d=\delta_N(t)\) overlap in exactly \(\max\{0,k-d\}\) positions. Hence
\[
\dim_K(C\cap C^{\perp_{e,R}})
=k-\max\{0,k-d\}
=\min\{k,d\}.
\]
This proves both dual formulas and the common hull-dimension formula.

For \(e=m\), every unit modulo the even integer \(N=2m\) is odd, so \(ms^{-1}\equiv m\pmod{2m}\). Therefore \(\delta_N(t)=m\ge k\), giving Hermitian self-orthogonality. For \(e=0\), \(t=0\), giving a trivial Euclidean hull.

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` checks the cyclic-index deduction exhaustively for every even \(N\le40\), every unit \(s\pmod N\), every \(1\le k\le N/2\), and every \(0\le e<N\). For each tuple it independently constructs the forbidden pairing indices, the proposed right and left dual intervals, and both hull intersections, and compares them with the two closed formulas.

The script also checks the scaled trace-self-dual zero pattern directly in the explicit \(\mathbb F_9/\mathbb F_3\) example used in the source. Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite checks are not used as a proof for arbitrary parameters; the infinite statement follows from the Moore-basis argument and Lemma 4.1 above.

## Relationship to prior work

Islam and Horlemann computed all \(e\)-Galois hull dimensions for the ordinary Gabidulin code \(G_k(\alpha)\) when \(\alpha\) is a trace-self-dual basis. In the range \(k\le N/2\), their piecewise formula is equivalent to
\[
\min\{k,\min(e,N-e)\}.
\]
Their Theorem 2.2 also gives an explicit \(e\)-Galois dual using the trace-dual basis, with its generator-matrix computation corresponding to the source-first (right-dual) orientation above. That result is the \(s=1\), unscaled predecessor of the formula above.

Ho introduced scaled trace-self-dual bases, proved that they exist without the parity restriction of ordinary trace-self-dual bases, and used their Frobenius orthogonality to prove Hermitian self-orthogonality for generalized Gabidulin codes \(G_{k,s}(\alpha)\). The source treats the Hermitian twist \(e=m\); it does not state the complete all-\(e\) right/left dual or hull profile for all \(e\) and all generalized steps \(s\).

Focused searches for generalized-Gabidulin Galois hulls, scaled trace-self-dual Galois hulls, explicit right/left \(e\)-Galois duals, and equivalent circular-distance formulations found the ordinary \(s=1\) theorem and the 2026 Hermitian endpoint, but no statement covering the all-\(e\), all-\(s\) formula above.

## Limitations

The proof uses the scaled trace-self-dual identity from Ho's Lemma 4.1 as an input. It does not reprove existence of scaled trace-self-dual bases.

Only full-length generalized Gabidulin codes of length \(2m\) generated from such a basis are covered. Shortened locator sets and arbitrary non-scaled bases can have different hull behavior. The result determines dimensions and dual generators; it does not classify the equivalence classes of the resulting hull subcodes.
