# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
Ordinary derivations of \(V\oplus\bigwedge^2V\) are exactly the block maps with central action \(R(C)\). Every local derivation preserves the central bracket ideal. The key necessity is proved by an explicit all-field exterior-square tangent lemma: off-diagonal coefficients are glued by decomposable vectors sharing an endpoint; after subtracting them, four-point relations make the diagonal coefficients additive. The latter reconstruction divides by \(2\), explaining the characteristic boundary. The reverse implication constructs an ordinary derivation at every given vector. The parameter dimensions and ordinary criterion follow injectively. The separate finite-field equation-rank verifier returned `VERIFY_OK` for three cases, but is not used to infer the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, with risk
The author-provided complete June 2026 manuscript by Hao and Chen proves general Lie-closure and related existence implications, rather than explicit ranks for free two-step Lie algebras. Their December 2025 extensive small-dimensional computations cover the six-dimensional rank-three example but not the rank-at-least-four family. The complete arXiv:2405.04209 provides existence results for all class-two nilpotent Lie algebras, not a classification of all local derivations. Burde–Dekimpe–Verbeke's free nilpotent results apply to almost-inner derivations, a stricter class of ordinary derivations. Searches for the same exterior-square tangent action, free-class-two local derivations, and reflexive-hull aliases did not identify an implication-equivalent family formula in the searched sources or mathematical record database. Historical equivalence under a different terminology remains possible, especially through general Grassmannian infinitesimal-preserver theorems.

## Value — PASS
Determining the complete local-derivation space is a natural local-to-global rigidity problem. A uniform description over all odd-characteristic fields for arbitrarily large free nilpotent Lie algebras, with a sharp \(n^2\)-dimensional defect and exact finite-field populations, resolves more than a single example or an existence test. The proof also isolates the exact geometric source of the restriction, rather than merely enumerating instances.
