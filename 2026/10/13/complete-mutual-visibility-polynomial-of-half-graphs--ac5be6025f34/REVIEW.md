# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The proof reduces all pair geodesics to the index bounds defining the half graph. The cases of the extreme selected indices \(q<p\), \(q=p\), and \(q>p\) are exhaustive and disjoint. Each has a necessary-and-sufficient visibility condition and an exact weighted count. Direct arithmetic summation yields the total-set count, and a separate exact graph-distance enumeration agrees with the complete coefficient vector for \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8\). The unrestricted proof is combinatorial, not extrapolated from finite observations.

## Originality: PASS
The 2021 introduction of mutual visibility and the later 2024 introduction of the visibility polynomial are acknowledged. Complete-bipartite enumerators do not cover half graphs with strictly nested neighborhoods. A 2023 distance-hereditary-graph algorithm determines an optimum but does not state or imply a coefficient enumerator; the 2025 enumerations considered other graph families and operations. The half-graph optimum and its unique maximum set were already established previously; they are explicitly excluded from this work's originality claim. Direct and semantic literature searches, mathematical-research record comparisons, and previous own scientific claims yielded no equivalent all-cardinality formula. This is not proof against undiscovered or unindexed prior work.

## Value: PASS
The formula gives every coefficient for an infinite natural bipartite graph family, including all set sizes and an exact total-set count. The three-case boundary characterization identifies precisely how shortest paths interact with the strict neighborhood order; it is stronger than an isolated maximum-size evaluation and provides a reusable counting decomposition for related chain-graph questions.

## Closest literature and limitations
G. Di Stefano (arXiv:2105.02722) defines the invariant and examines complete bipartite graphs. Cicerone and Di Stefano (arXiv:2307.10661) give a distance-hereditary optimization algorithm. Bujtás, Klavžar, and Tian (arXiv:2412.03066) and Tonny and Shikhi (arXiv:2507.01851, arXiv:2509.22571) define or study other visibility polynomials. None of the inspected sections provides the claimed half-graph enumerator, although a reduction outside those sections or unindexed paper cannot be excluded. The result does not claim the previously known maximum-cardinality or uniqueness statement as original.
