# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim fixes arbitrary prime-power field order, dimension at least two, positive word length, and ordered nonzero rank-one square-zero factors. Every nonzero factor is represented by an isotropic dyad modulo scalar rescaling. For a fixed nonzero target, decompositions into a left square-zero dyad and an arbitrary nonzero rank-one right factor are parametrized uniquely by two dual linear equations. Splitting by the right factor's trace yields a completely explicit two-by-two transition matrix; its displayed eigenvectors and eigenvalues reconstruct both trace fibers, including the length-one and characteristic-two boundaries. A constant nonzero-product continuation count yields the zero fiber; the two class cardinalities give the total-variation formula. Exhaustive direct matrix-multiplication enumeration in the attached `verify.py` program agrees on every zero or rank-one target over eight field-and-dimension pairs and multiple word lengths, including an extension field. Finite enumeration is corroborative rather than a proof of universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The strongest relevant older factorization results located are existence and rank criteria for nilpotent or square-zero products (Sullivan 2008, Botha 2016, and Hattingh's 2018 synthesis) and characterization of *commuting* nilpotent products (Kokol Bukovšek et al. 2007). These statements do not furnish exact finite-field multiplicities or conditional trace-mixing rates; in particular, commuting nilpotent products are nilpotent, whereas the unrestricted products here can have nonzero trace. Mohammadian's 2012 abstract concerns rank bounds under common-image and common-kernel hypotheses, rather than an all-length count. Earlier rank-one-idempotent product multiplicities involve a different factor orbit and are not logically equivalent. Residual risk: the full publisher versions of several closely titled factorization papers were not available to inspect; a separately phrased exact enumeration elsewhere remains possible. Search failure is not proof of bibliographic uniqueness.
