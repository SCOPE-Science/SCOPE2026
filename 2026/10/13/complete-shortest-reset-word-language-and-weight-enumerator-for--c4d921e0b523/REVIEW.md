# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The all-length proof fixes the transition maps and a precise zero-based position convention. The unique terminal state of a shortest reset word is proved from predecessor counts, and the Frobenius obstruction supplies the sharp lower bound. Two carefully chosen initial trajectories force the complete \(a\)- and \(b\)-position sets; an explicit shrinking family of cyclic intervals then verifies sufficiency at every unforced position. Distinct position sets yield the exact binomial enumerator. The boundary case \(n=2\) is checked separately. Independent word-level enumeration through \(n=6\), subset-image dynamic programming through \(n=12\), and interval inclusion checks through \(n=40\) passed. Finite checks are ancillary to the unrestricted proof.

**Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk.** In the primary full text of Ananichev–Gusev–Volkov (arXiv:1005.0129 and arXiv:1302.5793, Wielandt-family theorem), the available statement and proof establish the length and exhibit a witness, but do not determine all shortest input strings or their binomial count. Gusev–Pribavkina (arXiv:1403.3992) extend reset thresholds to related families without this complete language classification. Ryzhikov–Szykuła (MFCS 2018) explicitly identify this Wielandt family as the minimized decoder of a binary prefix code, but the inspected entire article only uses its known quadratic reset length, not a classification or count of shortest words. Computation-oriented shortest-word work addresses algorithmic discovery and does not imply the closed form. Related published finite automaton records concern different transition systems. Search coverage cannot exclude an unindexed prior enumeration; this is a bibliographic risk, not an asserted proof of unique priority.

**Value — PASS.** The complete shortest-reset-language is a natural structural invariant of a canonical extremal synchronizing family. The result provides all feasible inputs, an all-parameter closed form for their number, and a letter-composition polynomial; the already-known reset length and one extremal witness do not supply these distinctions. This is a complete family-wide classification, not a renamed threshold calculation or an arbitrary finite test case.

**Scientific limitations.** This result describes labeled shortest input strings, not distinct induced transformations or longer reset words. An unindexed older source might preempt the novelty claim. The computational tests do not independently certify the infinite theorem.
