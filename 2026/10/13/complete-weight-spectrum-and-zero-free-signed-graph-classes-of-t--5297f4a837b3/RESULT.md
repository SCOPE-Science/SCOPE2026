# Complete weight spectrum and zero-free signed-graph classes of the ternary \(\Delta_{5,2}\) code

## Finding

Let \(C\) be the ternary hypersimplex toric code defined below. It has parameters \([32,10,8]_3\), and its **entire** weight enumerator, not just the first two nonzero coefficients, is given by the following table. The third column counts natural signed-graph orbits, not arbitrary monomial-equivalence classes.

| Weight \(w\) | Codewords \(A_w\) | Signed-graph orbits |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 8 | 120 | 1 |
| 12 | 720 | 3 |
| 14 | 2240 | 2 |
| 16 | 4500 | 7 |
| 18 | 8000 | 5 |
| 20 | 10992 | 11 |
| 22 | 12384 | 8 |
| 24 | 10320 | 11 |
| 26 | 6240 | 5 |
| 28 | 2880 | 4 |
| 30 | 480 | 1 |
| 32 | 172 | 3 |

Among the \(172\) words with **no zero coordinates**, exactly three signed-graph classes occur. Every such word is obtained from one of these three forms by permuting variables, changing individual variable signs, and multiplying by \(\pm1\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 f_E&=x_1x_2,\\
 f_{C_4}&=x_1x_3+x_1x_4+x_2x_3-x_2x_4,\\
 f_{K_5}&=\sum_{1\le i<j\le5}x_ix_j.
\end{aligned}
\]

The three classes have exactly \(20\), \(120\), and \(32\) distinct coefficient vectors, respectively. The full code has \(62\) orbits under the natural signed-graph action of order \(3840\).

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb F_3\), whose nonzero elements are \(\{1,-1\}\). For every symmetric edge-label vector \(a=(a_{ij})_{1\le i<j\le5}\in\mathbb F_3^{10}\), set

\[
 f_a(x)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le5}a_{ij}x_ix_j,
 \qquad C=\bigl\{(f_a(x))_{x\in\{\pm1\}^5}:a\in\mathbb F_3^{10}\bigr\}.
\]

The coordinates are the \(32\) distinct torus points in any fixed order. The natural action on edge labels is generated by variable permutations (the symmetric group on five variables), variable sign changes (one global change acts trivially on quadratics), and multiplication of the entire polynomial by \(-1\). Its effective order is \(120\cdot16\cdot2=3840\). No assertion is made that this is the entire monomial automorphism group of the code.

## Proof

**Injectivity and dimension.** The ten sign characters \(x\mapsto x_ix_j\), indexed by edges of \(K_5\), have pairwise orthogonal integer evaluation columns. Their squared norms are \(32\), which is \(2\ne0\) modulo \(3\). Consequently they are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_3\), the evaluation map is injective, and \(\dim C=10\). Thus every one of the \(3^{10}=59049\) edge-label vectors represents one distinct codeword.

**Zero-free forms.** A form has weight \(32\) exactly when it is nowhere zero on the sign cube. We classify these forms recursively for up to five variables. Isolate a variable and write \(f(y,t)=g(y)+tL(y)\), where \(g\) is homogeneous square-free quadratic, \(L\) is linear, and \(t\in\{\pm1\}\). Since the only nonzero field values are \(\pm1\), both \(g(y)+L(y)\) and \(g(y)-L(y)\) are nonzero if and only if

\[
 L(y)=0\Longrightarrow g(y)\ne0,
 \qquad L(y)\ne0\Longrightarrow g(y)=0.
\tag{1}
\]

Up to sign changes in the earlier variables, the \(r\) nonzero coefficients of \(L\) all equal \(1\), where \(0\le r\le4\). In the \(r=0\) case we reduce to one fewer variable. For \(r=1\), the linear form never vanishes, so \(g\) vanishes on the entire sign cube. Independence of its distinct sign characters yields \(g=0\), giving one nonzero edge.

For \(r=2\), normalize \(L=y_1+y_2\), which vanishes precisely at \(y_1=-y_2\). Expand \(g\) in the sign-character basis on the other variables. Its vanishing for all \(y_1=y_2\) forces the edge \(y_1y_2\) coefficient and all edges among the remaining variables to vanish; the only possible terms are \((y_1-y_2)H\), where \(H\) is a linear form in the remaining variables. Condition (1) reduces to \(H\) being nowhere zero on their sign cube. With at most two remaining variables, this holds exactly when \(H\) has one nonzero term. Thus \(f\) is a four-cycle with the product of its four edge signs equal to \(-1\).

For \(r=3\), normalize \(L=y_1+y_2+y_3\). It vanishes at the two configurations with all three signs equal. The other six configurations force every quadratic coefficient within these three variables and every coefficient joining them to another variable to be zero: in both cases the linear system obtained by evaluating at the three representatives having one minus sign has determinant \(4\equiv1\pmod3\). Coefficients on edges of the remaining variables are zero by character independence. Hence \(g=0\), contradicting (1) on the two zeros of \(L\).

For \(r=4\) (possible only with five variables), write \(L=y_1+y_2+y_3+y_4\) and \(g=\sum_{i<j\le4}b_{ij}y_iy_j\). The condition that \(g\) vanish at all sign configurations except those with two plus and two minus signs says that its value at all plus signs and at each one-minus-sign configuration is zero. Equivalently, \(\sum_{j\ne i}b_{ij}=0\) for every vertex \(i\). At a balanced configuration with plus-sign pair \(\{i,j\}\), these equations reduce the value of \(g\) to \(b_{ij}\). Therefore (1) says every one of the six \(b_{ij}\) is nonzero. Each vertex has three incident coefficients in \(\{\pm1\}\) summing to zero modulo \(3\), forcing all three to be equal. Connectivity of \(K_4\) then forces all six coefficients to share one common sign. These are exactly the two signed \(K_5\) possibilities after normalizing the four edges incident to the isolated vertex. The recursive base case of two variables is one nonzero edge, so the cases above exhaust all zero-free forms in five variables.

An edge contributes \(\binom52\cdot2=20\) labeled forms. A negative-sign-product four-cycle contributes \(\binom54\cdot3\cdot2^3=120\): choose four vertices, one of three undirected cycles, and one of eight frustrated signings. The \(K_5\) forms comprise the \(2^4\) different vertex-switchings of each of the all-plus and all-minus edge assignments, giving \(32\). All three representatives are zero-free: the edge is nonzero on the torus; the four-cycle becomes one of two nonzero terms depending on whether \(x_3=x_4\); and for \(K_5\),

\[
 2f_{K_5}(x)=\left(\sum_i x_i\right)^2-5,
\]

where the square is \(0\) or \(1\) in \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(5\equiv2\), so the right side is never zero. The three patterns are inequivalent because their numbers of nonzero edge coefficients are \(1,4,10\). These arguments prove the exhaustive \(172=20+120+32\) classification without relying on the full weight census.

**Full finite weight spectrum.** For each coefficient vector, split the final variable as \(f(y,t)=g(y)+tL(y)\). The exact number of zero evaluations is

\[
 Z(f)=\sum_{y\in\{\pm1\}^{4}}
 \left(2\,\mathbf1_{\{g(y)=0,\,L(y)=0\}}+
 \mathbf1_{\{g(y)\ne0,\,L(y)\ne0\}}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

The weight equals \(32-Z(f)\). Enumerating all \(3^{10}\) coefficient vectors by the ten independent edge coordinates, then using (2), yields exactly the table in the Finding section. The enclosed `verify.py` computes this tally twice, using (2) and direct evaluation at all \(32\) torus points. It also partitions all coefficient vectors into orbits of the signed-permutation generators. Each orbit has a constant weight, yielding the third table column.

Independently, Burnside's lemma checks the total orbit count. Every natural symmetry acts on the ten edge coordinates as a signed permutation. For each edge permutation cycle, fixed ternary labels contribute three choices if the product of signs along the cycle is \(+1\), and only the zero choice otherwise. Summing \(3^{\#\text{positive cycles}}\) over all \(3840\) symmetries gives \(238080\); dividing by \(3840\) gives \(62\) orbits, agreeing with the explicit orbit partition.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. The verifier rederives the \(32\)-point generator matrix from the ten edges, checks the orthogonality certificate for injectivity, traverses all \(59049\) coefficient vectors in two independent zero-counting methods, checks all expected weight and orbit totals, verifies the \(172\) zero-free split by number of nonzero monomials, and independently computes the \(3840\)-element Burnside sum. Successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK` and numerical summaries. The mathematical proof of the zero-free orbit classification above is independent of this finite weight census.

## Relationship to prior work

Carvalho and Patanker's paper [1], first publicly posted on 25 February 2025 and subsequently published in 2026, studies coefficients of minimum and next-to-minimum weight in hypersimplex toric codes, with its main structural regime \(q\ge4\), \(d\ge3\). It defines the same class of objects but does not assert the complete weight enumerator or the nowhere-zero quadratic classification for \((q,s,d)=(3,5,2)\). The previously known minimum-distance formula [1,2] gives \(8\) in this instance, a consistency check rather than a new result. The earlier generalized-weight investigation [3] concerns support minima of subcodes, not full counts at every ordinary Hamming weight or signed-graph orbits. Neither minimal-distance nor generalized-weight formulas alone imply the present enumeration. A literature search cannot exclude unindexed code-equivalent tables or previous computations.

## Limitations

Only the specified ternary, five-variable, degree-two code and the stated natural symmetry action are classified. No formula for general \(q\), \(s\), or \(d\), no claim of the full monomial automorphism group, and no independent external validation is asserted. The finite enumeration is exhaustive for the specified \(59049\) coefficient vectors; it is not evidence about an infinite family. Older equivalent computations may exist outside the inspected literature.
