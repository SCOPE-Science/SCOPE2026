# Condition two is the sharp positivity frontier for conjugate gradients on positive diagonal systems

## Finding
Let
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n),
\qquad
0<\mu\le\lambda_i\le L,
\qquad
b\ge0,
\]
and apply exact-arithmetic conjugate gradients to
\[
Ax=b
\]
from \(x_0=0\). Put
\[
\kappa=\frac{L}{\mu}.
\]
Then
\[
\kappa\le2
\]
is sufficient for every CG iterate to remain componentwise nonnegative in every dimension, and the constant \(2\) is sharp as a dimension-free guarantee.

There is also a low-dimensional rigidity phenomenon: every two-dimensional positive diagonal system is positivity-safe for every condition number. For every \(\kappa>2\), however, a three-dimensional system can have a negative coordinate already at the second CG iterate. Thus dimension three and iteration two are jointly minimal for sign loss.

The same iterates are produced by every well-defined exact-line-search quasi-Newton method from the one-parameter Broyden family when its initial Hessian approximation is the identity. Hence the same positivity theorem applies to that equivalent family, including exact-line-search BFGS.

## Assumptions and scope
Arithmetic is exact. The system matrix is positive diagonal in the coordinate basis in which componentwise nonnegativity is tested. The right-hand side satisfies \(b\ge0\), and the initial iterate is \(x_0=0\).

Coordinates with \(b_i=0\) remain zero and may be removed. On the remaining active coordinates,
\[
x_i^\star=\frac{b_i}{\lambda_i}>0.
\]
The proof applies until exact CG convergence; after convergence the iterate is \(x^\star\).

The quasi-Newton corollary is restricted to exact line search, identity initial Hessian approximation, and update choices for which the one-parameter Broyden scheme is well defined.

## Proof
Write the CG residual as
\[
r_k=b-Ax_k.
\]
Because \(x_k\) belongs to the \(k\)-th Krylov space generated by \(A\) and \(b\), there is a degree-\(k\) polynomial \(p_k\), normalized by
\[
p_k(0)=1,
\]
such that
\[
r_k=p_k(A)b.
\]
CG residual orthogonality gives
\[
r_k\perp
\operatorname{span}\{b,Ab,\ldots,A^{k-1}b\}.
\]
Therefore, for \(j=0,\ldots,k-1\),
\[
\sum_i b_i^2\lambda_i^j p_k(\lambda_i)=0.
\]
Thus \(p_k\) is the degree-\(k\) orthogonal polynomial, with normalization \(p_k(0)=1\), for the positive discrete measure
\[
\sum_i b_i^2\delta_{\lambda_i}
\]
on the active spectrum.

For completeness, its roots are real, simple, and lie strictly between active spectral extremes until convergence. Indeed, if a degree-\(k\) orthogonal polynomial had fewer than \(k\) sign-changing roots in the convex hull of a positive measure, multiplying it by the product of those sign-changing factors would produce a polynomial of degree below \(k\) whose product with \(p_k\) has one sign on the support and is not identically zero, contradicting orthogonality. Denote these roots by
\[
\theta_1^{(k)},\ldots,\theta_k^{(k)}.
\]
They are the usual Ritz values, and
\[
p_k(\lambda)
=
\prod_{j=1}^k
\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\theta_j^{(k)}}\right).
\]

Every active eigenvalue and every Ritz root lie in
\[
[\mu,L].
\]
If \(\kappa\le2\), then
\[
0<\frac{\lambda_i}{\theta_j^{(k)}}\le\frac{L}{\mu}\le2.
\]
Hence every factor satisfies
\[
-1\le
1-\frac{\lambda_i}{\theta_j^{(k)}}
<1,
\]
so
\[
|p_k(\lambda_i)|\le1.
\]
Since
\[
r_{k,i}=b_i-\lambda_i x_{k,i}
=b_i p_k(\lambda_i),
\]
we obtain
\[
x_{k,i}
=
\frac{b_i}{\lambda_i}
\bigl(1-p_k(\lambda_i)\bigr)
\ge0.
\]
This proves the all-dimensional sufficiency.

In dimension two there are at most two active eigenvalues. The first CG iterate is
\[
x_1=\alpha_0b
\]
with \(\alpha_0>0\), and CG reaches the exact solution after at most two active eigenmodes. Therefore every two-dimensional iterate is nonnegative for arbitrary conditioning.

For sharpness, fix \(\kappa>2\) and set
\[
A_\kappa
=
\operatorname{diag}
\left(1,\frac{\kappa}{2},\kappa\right),
\qquad
b_\varepsilon=(1,1,\varepsilon)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the first two CG steps are well defined for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the active spectral measure for the first two steps converges to the two-point measure supported at
\[
1,\qquad\frac{\kappa}{2}.
\]
The corresponding degree-two residual polynomial therefore converges coefficientwise to the exact two-point annihilator
\[
p_2^{(0)}(\lambda)
=
(1-\lambda)
\left(1-\frac{2\lambda}{\kappa}\right).
\]
At the third curvature,
\[
p_2^{(0)}(\kappa)
=
(1-\kappa)(1-2)
=
\kappa-1>1.
\]
By continuity,
\[
p_2^{(\varepsilon)}(\kappa)>1
\]
for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\). Consequently
\[
(x_2)_3
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa}
\left(1-p_2^{(\varepsilon)}(\kappa)\right)<0.
\]
Because \(x_0=0\) and \(x_1=\alpha_0b_\varepsilon>0\), iteration two is the first possible failure. The two-dimensional argument shows that dimension three is minimal.

Finally, on a positive-definite quadratic, exact line search along parallel directions produces the same next point. The one-parameter Broyden family with identity initial Hessian approximation generates directions parallel to CG whenever the update is well defined. Therefore those quasi-Newton schemes have exactly the same iterates and inherit the same positivity theorem.

## Verification
The embedded `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to replay the three-dimensional witness and a two-dimensional termination example.

For
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,4),
\qquad
b=(1,1,1/10)^{\mathsf T},
\]
the first two CG steps satisfy
\[
\alpha_0=\frac{201}{304},
\qquad
\alpha_1=\frac{8588}{13467},
\]
and
\[
x_2=
\left(
\frac{64}{67},
\frac{143}{268},
-\frac{83}{2680}
\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The third coordinate is negative, while
\[
x^\star=(1,1/2,1/40)^{\mathsf T}>0.
\]
The third CG step reaches \(x^\star\) exactly.

The computation is only a witness replay. The universal positivity statement follows from the orthogonal-polynomial/Ritz-root proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Forsgren and Odland study the connection between conjugate gradients and exact-line-search quasi-Newton methods on positive-definite quadratics. Their full text states the Krylov-space characterization of CG, finite termination, residual/gradient orthogonality, and the fact that well-defined one-parameter Broyden updates generate directions parallel to CG and therefore identical iterates under exact line search.

Those results imply the quasi-Newton transfer once a property of CG iterates is known, but they do not state componentwise positive-orthant invariance, a condition-number frontier, or a minimal-dimensional sign-loss example.

Classical CG convergence theory controls energy norms, residual norms, and polynomial approximation. Those metrics do not imply coordinatewise feasibility: an iterate can improve globally while crossing a coordinate face. The theorem here uses the location of every Ritz root to turn the residual polynomial into a coordinatewise sign certificate.

Targeted searches using conjugate-gradient, Ritz-polynomial, nonnegative-iterate, positive-orthant, sign-change, BFGS, Broyden, and diagonal-quadratic terminology did not locate an implication-equivalent theorem.

## Limitations
The diagonal hypothesis is essential because positivity is coordinatewise. A general symmetric positive-definite change of eigenbasis does not preserve the nonnegative orthant.

The theorem assumes exact arithmetic and the zero starting point. Finite precision can perturb CG orthogonality and Ritz values, while a nonzero starting point changes the normalized coordinate argument.

The boundary \(2\) is dimension-free, not a claim that every problem above it fails. In particular, every two-dimensional positive diagonal problem remains safe for arbitrary condition number.

Residual originality risk remains that older CG, orthogonal-polynomial, or positivity-preserving iteration literature may encode the same coordinatewise consequence without using modern cone-invariance terminology.
