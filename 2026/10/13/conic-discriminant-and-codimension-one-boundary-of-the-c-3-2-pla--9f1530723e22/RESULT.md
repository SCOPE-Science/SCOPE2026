# Conic discriminant and codimension-one boundary of the \(C_3^2\) plane-sextic normal form

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\) or characteristic greater than \(21\), and consider
\[
C_{a,b,c}:\quad X^6+Y^6+Z^6+aX^3Z^3+bY^3Z^3+cX^3Y^3=0.
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=4-a^2-b^2-c^2+abc.
\]
Then \(C_{a,b,c}\) is smooth if and only if
\[
(4-a^2)(4-b^2)(4-c^2)\Delta\ne0.
\]
On this smooth locus, the coordinate-cube map
\[
q:[X:Y:Z]\longmapsto[X^3:Y^3:Z^3]
\]
is the quotient by the diagonal subgroup \(G\cong C_3^2\) generated by \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[X:\zeta_3Y:Z]\) and \([X:Y:Z]\mapsto[X:Y:\zeta_3Z]\). Its image is the smooth conic
\[
Q_{a,b,c}:\quad x^2+y^2+z^2+axz+byz+cxy=0,
\]
so \(C_{a,b,c}/G\cong\mathbf P^1\). The branch divisor consists exactly of the six points \(Q_{a,b,c}\cap\{xyz=0\}\), and every branch point has inertia order \(3\).

The three coefficient parameters are recovered directly from the three branch-point pairs on the coordinate sides. For example, on \(x=0\), writing \(t=y/z\), the two points satisfy \(t^2+bt+1=0\), hence their reciprocal-pair trace is \(t+t^{-1}=-b\); cyclically the three traces are \(-a,-b,-c\).

The four displayed factors in the smoothness discriminant also distinguish the two generic codimension-one degenerations. If exactly one side factor vanishes, for example \(b=\pm2\) while \(\Delta(4-a^2)(4-c^2)\ne0\), then \(C_{a,b,c}\) has exactly three singularities, all of type \(A_2\), and no others. If instead \(\Delta=0\) while \(4-a^2\), \(4-b^2\), and \(4-c^2\) are nonzero, then \(Q_{a,b,c}\) is the union of two distinct lines meeting away from the coordinate triangle; correspondingly \(C_{a,b,c}\) is the union of two smooth diagonal cubics meeting transversely in exactly nine ordinary nodes.

## Assumptions and scope
The displayed family is the exceptional \(C_3^2\)-invariant plane-sextic normal form appearing in Badr--Bars. Their automorphism classification supplies conditions under which this diagonal subgroup is the full automorphism group. The smoothness criterion and boundary statement above apply to the entire three-parameter displayed family. When the full automorphism group is larger than \(G\), the map \(q\) is still the quotient by the distinguished subgroup \(G\), but it need not be the full automorphism quotient.

Only the generic codimension-one boundary is classified here. Intersections of two or more discriminant components can have more degenerate singularities and are not included in the boundary classification.

## Proof
Write
\[
Q=x^2+y^2+z^2+axz+byz+cxy,
\]
so that the sextic equation is exactly \(F(X,Y,Z)=Q(X^3,Y^3,Z^3)\). The partial derivatives are
\[
F_X=3X^2(2X^3+aZ^3+cY^3),\quad
F_Y=3Y^2(2Y^3+bZ^3+cX^3),\quad
F_Z=3Z^2(2Z^3+aX^3+bY^3).
\]
No coordinate vertex lies on \(C_{a,b,c}\). At a point with \(XYZ\ne0\), put \(x=X^3\), \(y=Y^3\), and \(z=Z^3\). Singularity is equivalent to a nonzero kernel vector for
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}2&c&a\\c&2&b\\a&b&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det M=2\Delta.
\]
If the three side factors are nonzero, a kernel vector when \(\Delta=0\) has all three coordinates nonzero and therefore lifts through the cube map. Conversely, when \(\Delta\ne0\) no singularity occurs in the torus.

On \(X=0\), the remaining derivative equations are governed by \(\begin{pmatrix}2&b\\b&2\end{pmatrix}\), so a singularity occurs there exactly when \(4-b^2=0\). The analogous calculations on \(Y=0\) and \(Z=0\) give \(4-a^2\) and \(4-c^2\). This proves the exact smoothness product.

The action of \(G\) fixes the two rational functions \(Y^3/X^3\) and \(Z^3/X^3\), and these generate the invariant function field. Hence \(q\) is the degree-\(9\) quotient map by \(G\). The conic matrix has determinant \(\Delta/4\), hence the image conic is smooth on the smooth sextic locus. Among the four order-three subgroups of \(G\), three fix the coordinate lines pointwise, while the fourth has only the coordinate vertices as fixed points; those vertices are not on the sextic. Thus ramification occurs exactly above the coordinate-side intersections. Each side cuts the smooth conic in two distinct points because the corresponding side factor is nonzero, giving six branch values, all with inertia order \(3\). Riemann--Hurwitz gives
\[
2\cdot10-2=9(-2)+6\bigl(9-9/3\bigr),
\]
confirming quotient genus \(0\).

For the side boundary, take \(b=2\). The quotient conic is tangent to \(x=0\) at \([0:-1:1]\), whose cube-map inverse on \(X=0\) consists of three points. At one such point, after setting \(Z=1\), \(Y=-1+s\), the lowest terms are
\[
9s^2+(a-c)X^3.
\]
Moreover \(\Delta|_{{b=2}}=-(a-c)^2\), so \(\Delta\ne0\) makes the cubic coefficient nonzero. The local analytic type is therefore \(u^2+v^3=0\), namely \(A_2\). The cases \(b=-2\) and the other coordinate sides follow identically by signs and permutation. The exact smoothness analysis excludes any additional singularities.

Finally suppose \(\Delta=0\) and all three side factors are nonzero. Then the conic has rank exactly \(2\), so it is a pair of distinct lines, and its unique intersection point has no zero coordinate. Pulling each line back under \(q\) gives a diagonal cubic with all three coefficients nonzero, hence a smooth cubic. The cube map is etale over the torus, and the two quotient lines meet transversely there. Their inverse images therefore meet transversely in the nine points of the degree-\(9\) fiber, giving exactly nine ordinary nodes.

The accompanying exact symbolic checker `verify.py` reconstructs the pullback identity, the three partial derivatives, the determinant \(2\Delta\), the three side discriminants, and the six tangency/discriminant intersection identities. It also checks the local leading terms on the \(b=\pm2\) boundary, a rank-two witness on the \(\Delta=0\) boundary away from the coordinate triangle, and the Riemann--Hurwitz equality.

A fresh replay returned:

`VERIFY_OK sympy=1.14.0 pullback=conic smoothness_product=exact branch=6x3 boundary=tangency:3A2,conic-discriminant:9nodes`

The checker is finite symbolic verification. The statements that a rank-two conic splits into two distinct lines and that etale pullback preserves transverse crossings are standard algebraic-geometric arguments supplied in the proof, not inferred from a finite experiment.

## Relationship to prior work
Badr--Bars give the displayed sextic normal form and the \(C_3^2\) automorphism subgroup, together with conditions identifying the exact automorphism stratum. The later complete stratification paper retains this normal form in its classification. Neither inspected source states the coordinate-cube conic quotient, the factorized smoothness discriminant, or the two generic boundary singularity patterns described above.

The abstract quotient passport is not claimed as new: the LMFDB Higher Genus database already contains genus-\(10\) curves with group \(C_3^2\), quotient genus \(0\), and signature \([0;3,3,3,3,3,3]\). The contribution here is the explicit identification for this plane-sextic normal form, including its branch divisor, coefficient recovery, exact discriminant, and codimension-one degeneration geometry.

## Limitations
No assertion is made that the abstract signature \([0;3,3,3,3,3,3]\) is new. No classification is given for higher-codimension intersections among \(4-a^2=0\), \(4-b^2=0\), \(4-c^2=0\), and \(\Delta=0\). The statement also does not classify projective equivalences between different parameter triples. On parameter loci where the full automorphism group exceeds \(G\), the conic remains the quotient by \(G\), not necessarily by the full automorphism group.
