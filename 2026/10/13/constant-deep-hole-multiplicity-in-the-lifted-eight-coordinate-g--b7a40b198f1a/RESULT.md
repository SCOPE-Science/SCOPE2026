# Constant deep-hole multiplicity in the lifted eight-coordinate Golay family
## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge 2\), put \(Q=3^m\). Let \(\mathscr G_{8,m}\) be the \(m\)-lift of the three-times-punctured ternary Golay code \(\mathscr G_{8,1}=[8,6,2]_3\), using the same parity-check matrix over \(\mathbb F_Q\). Then every deep-hole coset contains exactly \(24\) vectors of Hamming weight \(2\). Hence
\[
\mathscr G_{8,m}\text{ is a }(2,24)\text{-APMCF code for every }m\ge2.
\]
This proves the \(\mathcal D_5\) branch of the constant-multiplicity conjecture posed by Davydov, Marcugini, and Pambianco.

## Assumptions and scope
An \(m\)-lift keeps a parity-check matrix \(H\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) and regards it over \(\mathbb F_{3^m}\). A coset is represented by its syndrome \(s=Hv^T\); its distance from the code is the minimum Hamming weight of an error vector \(e\) satisfying \(He^T=s\). A deep hole is a coset at covering radius. An \((R,\lambda)\)-APMCF code has exactly \(\lambda\) codewords at distance \(R\) from every deep hole.

The primary source proves that the family \(\mathcal D_5\) consists of the codes \(\mathscr G_{8,m}=[8,6,2]_{3^m}\), that their covering radius is \(2\) for \(m\ge2\), and that the cases \(m=2,3\) have multiplicity \(24\). It then conjectures the same multiplicity for all \(m\ge2\).

## Proof
Let \(H\) be any \(2\times8\) parity-check matrix of \(\mathscr G_{8,1}=[8,6,2]_3\). Its rows generate the dual code \(\mathscr G_{8,1}^\perp=[8,2,6]_3\).

No column of \(H\) is zero: otherwise the corresponding unit vector would lie in \(\mathscr G_{8,1}\), contradicting minimum distance \(2\). There are exactly four projective one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_3^2\). For any nonzero row vector \(a\in\mathbb F_3^2\), the zeros of the dual word \(aH\) are exactly the columns of \(H\) lying on the projective point \(\ker(a)\). Since every nonzero dual word has weight at least \(6\), each projective point occurs among the columns at most twice. Eight nonzero columns distributed among four projective points therefore force multiplicity exactly \(2\) at every projective point.

Now regard the same matrix over \(\mathbb F_Q\), where \(Q=3^m\) and \(m\ge2\). Write the four embedded \(\mathbb F_3\)-lines as \(L_1,\ldots,L_4\); each contains exactly two physical columns of \(H\). A nonzero syndrome on one of these lines has a weight-one representative. A syndrome \(s\) outside their union has no weight-one representative. Any two distinct lines \(L_i,L_j\) are generated by linearly independent vectors already over \(\mathbb F_3\), so they remain independent over \(\mathbb F_Q\). Thus for each unordered pair \(\{i,j}\) there is a unique decomposition
\[
s=\alpha u_i+\beta u_j
\]
with \(u_i\in L_i\setminus\{0\}\), \(u_j\in L_j\setminus\{0\}\). Because \(s\) lies on neither line, both coefficients are nonzero.

Fixing the line pair, either of the two physical columns on \(L_i\) and either of the two physical columns on \(L_j\) may be chosen. Their column scalars merely rescale the unique nonzero error coefficients, so the four coordinate pairs give four distinct weight-two errors with syndrome \(s\). A pair of coordinates from the same projective line cannot yield \(s\), because its syndrome remains on that line. Consequently the total number of weight-two representatives is
\[
\binom{4}{2}\cdot2\cdot2=24.
\]
For \(m\ge2\), \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_Q)\) has \(Q+1>4\) points, so syndromes outside the four embedded lines exist. The preceding argument also shows that every syndrome has distance at most \(2\), while those outside the union have distance exactly \(2\). Hence the covering radius is \(2\), and every deep-hole coset has exactly \(24\) nearest codewords.

## Verification
The standalone verifier reconstructs a canonical parity-check matrix with two columns on each of the four points of \(\mathrm{PG}(1,3)\). It checks that its ternary row code has parameters \([8,2,6]_3\), verifies that every pair of projective directions is independent, and confirms the symbolic count \(\binom{4}{2}\cdot4=24\).

As an independent finite anchor it works in \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[u]/(u^2+1)\), enumerates all syndromes and all weight-one and weight-two errors, finds exactly \(48\) deep syndromes, and verifies that each has zero weight-one representatives and exactly \(24\) weight-two representatives. This reproduces the source's \(m=2\) table entry. The all-\(m\) conclusion is proved algebraically above rather than inferred from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Davydov, Marcugini, and Pambianco introduce the lifted families and prove that \(\mathcal D_5\) is a completely regular near-MDS family with covering radius \(2\). Their Table 2 computes \(\lambda_{8,2}=\lambda_{8,3}=24\), and Remark 5.5(ii) explicitly conjectures that \(\lambda_{8,m}=24\) for every \(m\ge2\). The argument here supplies the missing all-parameter proof by reducing the covering multiplicity to the four projective column classes of the rank-two parity-check matrix.

Focused searches under the exact code parameters, APMCF terminology, the \(\mathcal D_5\) label, the symbols \(\lambda_{8,m}\), lifted-Golay terminology, and the equivalent four-projective-line syndrome formulation located the source conjecture and its finite \(m=2,3\) computations, but no later statement proving the all-\(m\) value. Searches for broader deep-hole multiplicity results found results for other code families whose hypotheses and syndromes do not specialize to this lifted eight-coordinate family.

## Limitations
The theorem settles only the \(\mathcal D_5\) branch of the source's conjecture. It does not settle the proposed constants for \(\mathcal D_3\) or \(\mathcal D_4\), nor the separate conjecture comparing covering multiplicities of lifted codes with their duals. Literature search is evidence against prior coverage, not a proof that no equivalent statement exists outside the checked sources.
