# Scientific review: finite regular \(L\)-algebra morphisms

## Correctness — PASS

The precise scope is finite nonempty downsets in \(\mathbb N^m\), which represent finite regular \(L\)-algebras by the published negative-cone theorem. The proof does not infer a classification of homomorphisms from the published classification of isomorphisms. Instead, it uses direct defining-operation identities. Cross-axis identities force disjointness of positive coefficients in each output coordinate. Adjacent-vector identities force exact integer increments, and finite downward closure allows their integration from zero. The positive-homogeneity of truncated subtraction proves the converse. Checking maximal source vectors is sufficient because coordinate maps are monotone and targets are downsets.

For a full rectangular target the one-coordinate bound is exactly \(k r_i\le t_j\); no coupling remains. The homogeneous-box monoid, rank polynomial, idempotent polynomial, and embedded-retract polynomial follow by explicit mappings to pointed transformations and partitions. Degenerate singleton diagrams and the distinction between abstract types and embedded retract subsets are spelled out. An independent finite enumeration agrees with all tested formulas, but the written proof, not that enumeration, establishes their full quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks

The main source, Dietzel–Menchón–Vendramin, *On the enumeration of finite \(L\)-algebras*, arXiv:2206.04955, was inspected in its accessible full arXiv HTML version: Section 2 defines homomorphisms; Section 4 counts linear-algebra isomorphism classes by Bell numbers; Section 5 establishes representation of regular algebras as finite Young diagrams and identifies isomorphisms with coordinate permutations. The claimed complete **arbitrary-homomorphism** normal form, rectangular-target hom-set formula, and pointed-transformation/retract spectra do not appear in the inspected text. The new theorem is derived from, not a replacement for, its representation theorem.

Additional targeted web searches used equivalent descriptions including positive-part difference, truncated subtraction, BCK morphisms, and direct products of chains. The accessible abstracts of Rump's foundational 2008 work and Zhan–Borumand Saeid–Davvaz's 2024 operator paper show structural background and operator questions respectively, but no explicit formula matching this claim. published-finding corpus semantic queries for the full coordinate classification, box hom-set census, pointed-transformation monoids, and retract rank polynomials found no directly equivalent record. Current and preceding owned ledgers were searched; one earlier finding explicitly bijects **linear** \(L\)-algebras with partitions, and another counts embedded retracts of an unrelated full-team inquisitive algebra. Neither entails the finite-regular morphism classification.

Residual risks remain: Rump's 2008 full text was not available for exhaustive inspection; an unstated consequence of his structure-group machinery or unindexed BCK-algebra literature might provide antecedents. Search absence alone is not used as a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete morphism classification for a natural, independently established class of algebraic-logical structures, rather than a small computed example. It makes hom-set evaluation algorithmic for arbitrary finite diagrams using only coordinate choices and maximal vectors, proves unexpected indifference to source diagram shape for rectangular codomains, and identifies an infinite family of endomorphism monoids with classical pointed transformation monoids. Its three closed rank spectra distinguish all endomorphisms, idempotents, and embedded retracts. The result can be used in categorical and computational studies of finite regular \(L\)-algebras; it is not a claim of a new ambient representation theorem.
