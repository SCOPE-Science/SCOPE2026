# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The quotient relations have independent supports on the cycle–vertex incidence forest; their span contains no singleton. One direction of the hull identity follows from each cycle relation, and the other from terminal-cycle pruning of an alleged linear dependence supported on a closed vertex set plus one point. The resulting matroid is loopless, so independent sets are simultaneously convexly, Helly and Radon independent. All finite parameter boundaries and the forest case are included; the proof does not infer an infinite theorem from tests. An executable finite checker uses two distinct computations to confirm both closure procedures and all three independence maxima on 15 profiles and 3576 subsets.

## Originality — PASS (with identified uncertainty)
The motivating full text arXiv:2609.34236v1 proves graph-class classifications only at near-maximal values and computational hardness; its statements do not entail a full all-cactus matroid closure identity. Earlier IWOCA 2026 work determines rank in particular classes, notably forests, cycles and chordal graphs, and those special cases are already covered; the accessible abstract does not imply the binary-matroid representation for every cactus. Searches of the available literature and published mathematical-result index for cactus rank, cycle-convex Helly/Radon, matroid closure, incidence-vector quotient and equivalent formulations returned no covering all-cactus theorem. However, one relevant extended technical report was inaccessible; an unindexed equivalence cannot be excluded.

## Value — PASS
The all-cactus theorem gives more than an invariant count: an explicit linear representation and exchange structure of an entire convexity, with a rank formula valid for an unbounded number of cycles, bridges and articulation vertices. The three exact extremal invariants follow from this structural reduction; the invariant is naturally motivated by a recent program studying their separation and complexity. It is not merely a numerical test or routine specialization of the near-extremal classifications.

## Scope and limits
Only cactus graphs are covered. Published forest, cycle and chordal special cases are acknowledged. Full-text comparison with the 2025 extended version of the IWOCA 2026 rank paper remains a residual novelty risk. The finite checker is supporting evidence only; the universal conclusion rests on the proof.
