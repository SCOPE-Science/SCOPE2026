# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim quantifies over exactly the noncomplete labeled \(r\)-partite graphs with \(r\ge3\), not over arbitrary graphs. Starting with \(xUxV\), the proof derives both the shared cross-part order and opposite within-part order; a three-letter sandwich contradiction forces contiguity. Remaining same-part symbols necessarily nest across the external block sequence. Thus every word is cyclically converted to the displayed block normal form. The converse follows by checking two letters from the same versus different blocks. Counting is corrected for all \(2r\) block-boundary rotations, and the absence of a nontrivial cyclic period follows from the presence of a nonedge. The finite checker independently enumerates canonical words up to seven labels and full labeled words for eight profiles. Residual scientific limit: no proof is asserted for \(r=1\), for singleton-only complete graphs, or for arbitrary repeated multiplicity above two.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Representing words are nonunique and cyclically related, making the **complete labeled enumeration** of representations of a fixed graph a natural finite invariant. The theorem proves a global rigidity condition: although each pair of parts separately has a two-block representation, the pairwise projections must be compatible with **one common cyclic order of all parts**, a property not supplied by the bipartite result. It resolves the entire noncomplete complete-multipartite family with three or more parts, rather than one isolated parameter. The exact normalization also offers a finite reference family for testing word-graph representation algorithms and chord-diagram enumeration, but no downstream theorem is claimed.
