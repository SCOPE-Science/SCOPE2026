# Verification

The theorem uses one finite certificate and two standard infinite inputs: Dirichlet's theorem and Defant's Lemma 2.2.

The finite certificate is stored in `cover_certificate.json`. Its rows encode exponent residue \(a\), modulus \(m\), prime \(q\), and prime-progression residue \(c\).

The attached `verify.py` checks exactly that every \(q\) is prime, that
\[
\operatorname{ord}_q(3)=m,
\]
and that
\[
3^ac+2\equiv0\pmod q.
\]
It then confirms that the ten exponent classes cover every residue modulo \(48\), that
\[
M=1620742097628261335
\]
is the product of the ten primes, and that
\[
C=206033543146003779
\]
is the simultaneous CRT residue with
\[
\gcd(C,M)=1.
\]

Finally, it checks that \(11\) is absent from the subgroup generated by \(3\) modulo \(41\). Therefore a prime
\[
p\equiv13\pmod{41}
\]
cannot equal
\[
3^s+2.
\]

The finite verifier does not establish infinitude. Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many prime terms of the coprime progression \(C\pmod M\). Defant's Lemma 2.2 then converts the two all-\(t\) conditions into the all-\(\alpha\) Schemmel-nontotient conclusion.
