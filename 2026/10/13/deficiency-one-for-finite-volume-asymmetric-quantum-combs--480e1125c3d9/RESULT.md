# Deficiency one for finite-volume asymmetric quantum combs
## Finding
Consider a metric comb with spine vertices \(c_k\), \(k\ge1\). Join \(c_k\) to \(c_{k+1}\) by an edge of length \(\ell_k>0\). At every \(c_k\), attach two degree-one vertices \(u_{k,+}\) and \(u_{k,-}\) by edges of arbitrary lengths \(a_{k,+}>0\) and \(a_{k,-}>0\). No relation between \(a_{k,+}\) and \(a_{k,-}\) is imposed.

If
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}\ell_k+\sum_{k\ge1}\bigl(a_{k,+}+a_{k,-}\bigr)<\infty,
\]
then the minimal Kirchhoff Laplacian \(H_0\) on this infinite quantum graph has
\[
n_+(H_0)=n_-(H_0)=1.
\]
In particular, the finite-total-length comb has exactly one boundary degree of freedom at its unique incomplete end.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is locally finite and has exactly the stated comb topology. The operator is the minimal Kirchhoff Laplacian: on each open edge it acts as \(-d^2/dx^2\), functions are continuous at vertices and satisfy Kirchhoff flux balance, and the minimal operator is the closure of compactly supported edgewise \(W^{2,2}\) functions satisfying those vertex conditions.

The edge lengths may be completely asymmetric from tooth to tooth. The only metric hypothesis is finite total length. The conclusion concerns deficiency indices, not a classification of spectra of particular self-adjoint extensions.

## Proof
Exner--Kostenko--Malamud--Neidhardt associate to a metric graph the weighted vertex measure
\[
m(v)=\sum_{e\sim v}|e|
\]
and edge conductances \(b(u,v)=|e_{uv}|^{-1}\). Their Theorem 3.5 identifies the deficiency indices of the quantum-graph operator with those of the corresponding minimal weighted discrete Laplacian \(h_0\). It therefore suffices to prove that, for each \(z\in\{i,-i\}\), the solution space of
\[
h_0^*f=zf
\]
in \(\ell^2(V;m)\) is one-dimensional.

Write \(f_k=f(c_k)\) and \(f_{k,\sigma}=f(u_{k,\sigma})\). At a tooth vertex, the deficiency equation is
\[
\frac{f_{k,\sigma}-f_k}{a_{k,\sigma}^2}=z f_{k,\sigma},
\]
so
\[
f_{k,\sigma}=\frac{f_k}{1-z a_{k,\sigma}^2}.
\]
Because \(z=\pm i\), one has \(|1-z a^2|\ge1\) for every \(a>0\).

For the spine, set
\[
p_k=\frac{f_k-f_{k+1}}{\ell_k}
\]
and
\[
q_k(z)=m(c_k)+\sum_{\sigma\in\{+,-\}}
\frac{a_{k,\sigma}}{1-z a_{k,\sigma}^2}.
\]
Eliminating the two tooth values gives, at the first spine vertex,
\[
p_1=zq_1(z)f_1,
\]
and for every \(k\ge2\),
\[
p_k-p_{k-1}=zq_k(z)f_k,
\qquad
f_{k+1}=f_k-\ell_k p_k.
\]
Hence a deficiency solution is uniquely determined by the single complex number \(f_1\). If \(f_1=0\), then \(p_1=0\), and induction gives the zero solution. Thus the deficiency space has dimension at most one.

It remains to show that the solution generated by \(f_1=1\) belongs to \(\ell^2(V;m)\). Finite total length implies
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}m(c_k)<\infty,
\qquad
\sum_{k\ge1}a_{k,+}+
\sum_{k\ge1}a_{k,-}<\infty.
\]
Moreover,
\[
|q_k(z)|\le m(c_k)+a_{k,+}+a_{k,-},
\]
so \(\sum_k|q_k(z)|<\infty\). For \(k\ge2\), put
\[
X_k=|f_k|+|p_{k-1}|.
\]
The two recurrences give
\[
X_{k+1}
\le
\bigl(1+r_k\bigr)X_k,
\qquad
r_k=\ell_k+|q_k(z)|+\ell_k|q_k(z)|.
\]
Since \(\sum_k\ell_k<\infty\) and \(\sum_k|q_k(z)|<\infty\), also \(\sum_k r_k<\infty\). The convergent infinite-product bound for \(\prod_k(1+r_k)\) therefore shows that \((f_k)\) and \((p_k)\) are bounded. The tooth formula then shows that all \(f_{k,\sigma}\) are bounded as well.

Finally,
\[
\sum_{v\in V}m(v)=2\sum_{e\in E}|e|<\infty.
\]
A bounded vertex function is therefore in \(\ell^2(V;m)\). The recursively generated nonzero solution satisfies the formal equation and hence belongs to the maximal adjoint domain in the locally finite weighted-graph setting. Thus each deficiency space has dimension at least one. Combining the upper and lower bounds gives
\[
n_+(h_0)=n_-(h_0)=1.
\]
Theorem 3.5 of the primary source now yields
\[
n_+(H_0)=n_-(H_0)=1.
\]

## Verification
The proof is analytic and does not rely on a finite truncation. The accompanying `verify.py` independently checks the tooth elimination and the spine recurrence with exact Gaussian-rational arithmetic for asymmetric geometric edge lengths at both \(z=i\) and \(z=-i\). It verifies forty-eight tooth equations and twenty-four spine equations exactly. The finite replay is only a consistency check; the infinite conclusion comes from the summable transfer bound above.

## Relationship to prior work
Example 4.18 of Exner--Kostenko--Malamud--Neidhardt modifies their comb by shrinking its vertical edges. Under finite total length they prove non-self-adjointness and explicitly state that they expect the deficiency indices to equal one. Their Theorem 3.5 supplies the quantum-to-discrete deficiency-index correspondence used here, but Example 4.18 does not prove the index count.

Kostenko--Mugnolo--Nicolussi later connect deficiency indices to finite-volume graph ends. For a finite-volume one-ended graph their general theorem gives the lower bound \(n_\pm(H_0)\ge1\), with equality requiring an additional maximal-domain Sobolev property. They also prove equality for symmetry-rich family-preserving metric graphs without horizontal edges. That result covers symmetric specializations when the metric preserves the required family symmetry, but it does not imply the present arbitrary asymmetric-tooth statement. The recurrence above supplies the missing upper bound without any paired-length symmetry.

Breuer--Levi develop the decomposition theory for family-preserving metric graphs. Their symmetry hypothesis is likewise not assumed here.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to a single infinite spine with two terminal teeth at each spine vertex. It does not assert deficiency one for arbitrary one-ended finite-volume quantum graphs: later work shows that even one-ended finite-volume graphs can have larger, including infinite, deficiency indices. No assertion is made about spectral asymptotics, the Friedrichs extension, or other vertex couplings.

A residual originality risk is that the same asymmetric-comb recurrence may appear in weakly indexed extension-theory literature under Jacobi-operator or finite-decoration terminology. Targeted searches did not locate such a statement.
