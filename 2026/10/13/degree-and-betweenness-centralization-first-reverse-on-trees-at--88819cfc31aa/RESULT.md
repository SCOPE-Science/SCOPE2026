# Degree and betweenness centralization first reverse on trees at eight vertices
## Finding
Freeman's graph centralization construction compares the most central vertex with every other vertex and divides the resulting sum of gaps by its maximum possible value at the same graph order.

For a connected simple graph \(G\) of order \(n\), let
\[
C_D(G)
=
\frac{\sum_v(d_{\max}-d(v))}
{(n-1)(n-2)}
\]
be Freeman degree centralization.

Let \(b(v)\) be ordinary vertex betweenness, counting for each unordered pair of other vertices the fraction of geodesics through \(v\). Then
\[
C_B(G)
=
\frac{\sum_v(b_{\max}-b(v))}
{(n-1)\binom{n-1}{2}}.
\]
This is the same normalized graph centralization as Freeman's ordered-pair convention, because numerator and denominator are both multiplied by \(2\).

Restrict now to trees. For every
\[
n\le7,
\]
there is no pair of \(n\)-vertex trees that the two centralizations rank in opposite strict orders.

At
\[
n=8,
\]
strict ranking reversal occurs for the first time. Among the \(23\) unlabeled eight-vertex trees, exactly \(8\) unordered pairs reverse.

A compact witness is given by two spiders. Let
\[
A=S(4,1,1,1)
\]
be the tree with one center and four arms of lengths \(4,1,1,1\), and let
\[
B=S(3,2,2)
\]
have three arms of lengths \(3,2,2\).

Their degree sequences are
\[
(4,2,2,2,1,1,1,1)
\]
and
\[
(3,2,2,2,2,1,1,1).
\]

Their Freeman centralizations are
\[
C_D(A)=\frac37,
\qquad
C_B(A)=\frac{11}{21},
\]
while
\[
C_D(B)=\frac5{21},
\qquad
C_B(B)=\frac47.
\]

Therefore
\[
C_D(A)>C_D(B)
\]
but
\[
C_B(A)<C_B(B).
\]

Degree centralization judges the four-arm broom-like spider more centralized because one vertex has degree \(4\). Betweenness centralization judges the three-arm spider more centralized because its center brokers more vertex pairs, and its subordinate branch vertices preserve a larger gap between the leading broker and the rest.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, connected, and acyclic.

Comparisons are only between trees of the same order. The normalization constants therefore do not affect the sign of a ranking comparison, but the standard Freeman normalizations are retained.

Betweenness counts unordered endpoint pairs and divides equally among multiple geodesics. On trees every endpoint pair has a unique geodesic.

The statement is about strict ranking reversal. The weak orderings begin to differ earlier through ties: this does not contradict the sharp strict threshold at eight vertices.

## Proof
For degree centralization, if
\[
\Delta=\max_v d(v),
\]
then
\[
C_D(T)
=
\frac{\sum_v(\Delta-d(v))}
{(n-1)(n-2)}.
\]
Since every tree has
\[
\sum_v d(v)=2(n-1),
\]
the numerator is exactly
\[
n\Delta-2(n-1).
\]
Thus at fixed order degree centralization is determined solely by the maximum degree.

For tree betweenness there is also an exact component formula. Delete a vertex \(v\), and let the resulting component sizes be
\[
s_1,\ldots,s_k.
\]
Every path between vertices lying in different components passes uniquely through \(v\), so
\[
b(v)=\sum_{i<j}s_i s_j.
\]

For the witness
\[
A=S(4,1,1,1),
\]
the maximum degree is \(4\), hence
\[
\sum_v(4-d(v))
=
18
\]
and
\[
C_D(A)=\frac{18}{42}=\frac37.
\]

The vertex betweennesses along the long arm and at the hub are
\[
15,\ 12,\ 10,\ 6,
\]
and the four leaves have betweenness \(0\). Thus
\[
\sum_v(15-b(v))
=
77,
\]
so
\[
C_B(A)=\frac{77}{147}=\frac{11}{21}.
\]

For
\[
B=S(3,2,2),
\]
the maximum degree is \(3\), hence
\[
\sum_v(3-d(v))
=
10
\]
and
\[
C_D(B)=\frac{10}{42}=\frac5{21}.
\]

Its betweennesses are
\[
16,\ 10,\ 6,\ 6,\ 6,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_v(16-b(v))
=
84
\]
and
\[
C_B(B)=\frac{84}{147}=\frac47.
\]

This proves the displayed strict reversal.

To prove minimality, the verifier exhausts every unlabeled tree through order \(8\). Every labeled \(n\)-vertex tree is generated by a Prüfer sequence of length \(n-2\). Each generated tree is reduced to a canonical unrooted-tree code using its center or bicenter and recursively sorted rooted branch codes. This is a complete isomorphism invariant for trees.

The resulting unlabeled-tree counts are
\[
1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6,\ 11,\ 23
\]
for orders
\[
3,4,5,6,7,8.
\]
Exact pairwise comparison yields strict-reversal counts
\[
0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 8.
\]
Hence eight vertices are sharp.

## Relationship to prior work
Freeman's foundational centrality paper distinguishes degree centrality as potential communication activity from betweenness centrality as potential control of communication. It defines network centralization as the normalized sum of gaps from the most central point and gives the degree- and betweenness-based graph centralization formulas.

Freeman also calculates all centrality measures for all \(34\) five-point graph types and explicitly observes that degree-, betweenness-, and closeness-based graph centralizations differ in their rankings of intermediate forms. In particular, his five-point table includes the star, fork, and chain communication trees.

That literature establishes both the measures and the fact that their rankings can disagree on general graphs. It does not answer the tree-specific finite question addressed here: how large must an acyclic communication network be before degree and betweenness centralization rank two trees in opposite strict orders?

Targeted searches using degree centralization, betweenness centralization, trees, communication trees, ranking reversal, minimum order, and the exact spider signatures did not locate the eight-vertex threshold or the eight-pair first layer.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to trees. General connected graphs already exhibit degree-versus-betweenness ranking disagreement among the five-point forms tabulated by Freeman.

The result uses Freeman's classical geodesic betweenness. Flow, current-flow, random-walk, and weighted betweenness measures can behave differently.

The finite classification establishes the first strict reversal and its boundary count; it does not classify all reversing tree pairs at larger orders.

An unindexed exercise, software test, or specialist note could contain the same eight-vertex spider witness even though the sharp tree threshold was not located.
