# Review of the exact degree-conditioned Wiener regression

## Correctness — PASS
Under the uniform labelled-tree law, Prüfer's weighted generating polynomial is \((m+z)^{m-1}\). The weighted marked-path contraction identity gives exact distance-survival functions for a fixed root-to-other pair and a fixed nonroot pair. Coefficient extraction yields both conditional distance components and therefore the full Wiener regression. The proof establishes strict decline at degrees \(2,3,\ldots,n-1\) by an exact difference formula and positive rational induction, then proves the positive leaf-to-degree-two jump using uniform-edge subdivision and the tree Wiener bound \(W\ge(m-1)^2\). All boundary cases are specified. The packaged exact-rational Prüfer enumerator verifies every degree cell through \(n=8\) (\(280392\) trees); its role is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS
Full-text inspections covered Entringer–Meir–Moon–Székely (1994, §§3–4, labelled-tree means), Janson (2003, §§2–7, Wiener asymptotics and internal path length) and Berzunza Ojeda–Janson (2021/2022, §§4–13, root modifications and distance profiles). These do not state a finite-size degree-conditioned Wiener regression or classify its optimizing degree. Targeted semantic database queries and comparison with prior related exact tree-index formulas revealed no equivalent or stronger coverage. Residual risk: an unindexed historical bivariate marked-degree generating function may contain an implicit equivalent; a failed search is not historical uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
The Wiener index is a canonical global distance invariant, and a distinguished degree is its most elementary local structural statistic. The exact conditional mean is nonmonotone, has an all-size uniquely degree-two maximizer, and is not determined by their ordinary covariance, which is identically zero by exchangeability. The result supplies a complete, naturally posed finite-sample regression rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations
Uniform labelled Cayley trees only; expectations, not conditional stochastic ordering. The literature comparison has residual historical-indexing risk. No independent validation is asserted.
