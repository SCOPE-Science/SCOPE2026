# Scientific review: power-free odd legs in primitive right triangles

**Correctness — PASS.** Euclid's primitive parametrization maps bijectively to ordered positive odd coprime factors \(u<v\); the odd leg is their product and the hypotenuse is half their sum of squares. The claimed local probability at every odd prime excludes exactly three disjoint possibilities, giving \((1-p^{-2})(1-2/[p^{k-1}(p+1)])\). The finite-prime Chinese remainder argument and uniform-in-\(X\) elementary tail bound after fixing a prime cutoff prove convergence in the circular sector. The boundary-area normalization gives \(X/(2\pi)\). Two independently written finite-enumeration parametrizations agree in all 15 tested cases. Risk: the proof supplies only \(o_k(X)\), not a uniform estimate for growing \(k\).

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Zelator (2008) gives squarefree-factor obstructions for specified Pythagorean legs; Overmars–Ntogramatzidis (2015) gives a parametrization; Ochieng (2019) gives formulas for triples with a prescribed leg; Burson (2020) notes squarefree-factor properties. These do not state or quantitatively imply the all-hypotenuse density and explicit infinite product on their own. The generic elementary CRT/sieve technique applies to coprime lattice points, so an earlier treatment of the same special case could exist; searches of published records and topic-specific pages found no stronger covering assertion. No novelty is claimed for the factorization or local sieve in isolation.

**Value — PASS.** Squarefreeness and higher power-freeness of a triangle's unique odd leg are natural arithmetic properties of primitive Pythagorean triangles. The even leg is always divisible by \(4\), so the asymmetry gives a substantive reason to study the odd leg rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. The theorem determines an explicit, nontrivial global density across all hypotenuses and all fixed exponents, rather than a table or isolated numerical observation. The value is this exact naturally motivated distributional invariant; no downstream theorem is asserted.

Closest works: Zelator (2008), Overmars–Ntogramatzidis (2015), Ochieng (2019), Burson (2020). Residual risk is an unindexed earlier application of squarefree-pair densities to the same geometric counting problem. The assertions concern primitive triangles and fixed exponents only.
