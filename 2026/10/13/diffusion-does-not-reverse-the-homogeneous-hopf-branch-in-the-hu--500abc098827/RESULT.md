# Diffusion does not reverse the homogeneous Hopf branch in the hunting-cooperation model

## Finding

Consider
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_t&=d_1u_{xx}+\sigma u(1-u)-u(1+av)v,\\
v_t&=d_2v_{xx}+u(1+av)v-v
\end{aligned}
\]
on \((0,\pi)\) with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions.

The spatially constant subspace
\[
\mathcal H=\{(u,v):u(x)\equiv U,\ v(x)\equiv V\}
\]
is invariant because the diffusion terms vanish identically on it. The restriction to \(\mathcal H\) is exactly
\[
\dot U=\sigma U(1-U)-U(1+aV)V,
\qquad
\dot V=U(1+aV)V-V.
\]
Therefore the spatially homogeneous Hopf branch of the PDE has exactly the same direction in the bifurcation parameter \(a\) as the Hopf branch of the ODE.

The source's Theorem 2.1 states that the ODE Hopf at
\[
a_0=\frac{(1+\sigma)^2}{\sigma}
\]
is supercritical. Its Theorem 3.2 nevertheless states that under \((H_1)\) the spatially homogeneous PDE Hopf is subcritical. These statements cannot both describe the same homogeneous branch.

An exact witness lies in the source's own \((H_1)\) regime:
\[
\sigma=1,\qquad a_0=4,\qquad d_1=10,\qquad d_2=\frac15.
\]
For this choice,
\[
\frac{\sqrt{a_0\sigma}-1}{d_2\sqrt{a_0\sigma}}=\frac52,
\]
so \(m=1\), and the source's threshold is
\[
r_1=2.
\]
Thus \(d_1>r_1\), exactly the \((H_1)\) case.

At the Hopf point the first nonconstant cosine mode has
\[
T_1=-\frac{51}{5},\qquad D_1=-\frac{12}{5}.
\]
It therefore has one positive real eigenvalue and makes the homogeneous periodic branch transversely unstable. Diffusion changes stability, but not direction.

Direct normal-form reconstruction of the planar restriction at \(\sigma=1\), \(a=4\) gives
\[
\left.\frac{d}{da}\operatorname{Re}\lambda(a)\right|_{a=4}=\frac1{16}>0
\]
and
\[
l_1=-5\sqrt2<0.
\]
Hence the homogeneous periodic branch exists for \(a>4\): it is supercritical.

The same witness also contradicts the statement immediately before Theorem 3.2 that
\[
D_j(a_0)>0
\]
for every positive mode \(j\), since \(D_1(a_0)=-12/5\).

## Assumptions and scope

The domain is the source's interval \((0,\pi)\), the boundary conditions are Neumann, and \(d_1,d_2>0\). The correction concerns the spatially homogeneous Hopf branch generated by the zero Laplacian mode.

The term “direction” refers to the parameter side on which the small periodic branch exists. “Transverse stability” refers to perturbations in nonconstant spatial modes. These are distinct properties.

The result does not classify spatially inhomogeneous periodic branches and does not challenge the source's Turing threshold away from the Hopf point.

## Proof

If the initial condition is spatially constant, then
\[
u_{xx}=v_{xx}=0
\]
for all time as long as the solution remains in the constant subspace. The PDE vector field therefore restricts exactly to the planar ODE. In particular, every periodic orbit of the ODE is a spatially homogeneous periodic solution of the PDE at the same parameter value. Diffusion coefficients do not enter this restricted vector field, so they cannot reverse the side of this branch.

For the exact witness, \(\sqrt{a_0\sigma}=2\). The \((H_1)\) mode-count expression is
\[
\frac{2-1}{(1/5)2}=\frac52,
\]
which lies between \(1^2\) and \(2^2\). For \(k=1\), the source's threshold formula gives
\[
r_1=
\frac{2(2-1)^2/4+(1/5)(1/2)}{(1/2-1/5)}
=2.
\]
Thus \(d_1=10>2\).

The source's modal formulas give at \(a=4\)
\[
T_k=-\frac{51}{5}k^2
\]
for \(k\ge1\), and
\[
D_k=rac12+10\left(\frac15k^2-\frac12\right)k^2+rac1{10}k^2.
\]
At \(k=1\),
\[
D_1=\frac12-3+\frac1{10}=-\frac{12}{5}.
\]
Hence the first spatial mode is hyperbolically unstable.

For completeness, the Hopf coefficient of the homogeneous restriction can be reconstructed independently. Translating the equilibrium
\[
(U,V)=\left(\frac12,\frac14\right)
\]
to the origin at \(\sigma=1,a=4\) gives linear part
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\frac12&-\frac32\\
\frac12&\frac12
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose eigenvalues are \(\pm i/\sqrt2\). Applying the standard multilinear Hopf formula to the exact quadratic and cubic terms gives
\[
l_1=-5\sqrt2.
\]
The real-part crossing speed is \(1/16\). Therefore the reduced radial equation has a stable nonzero branch for positive \(a-4\), so the branch is supercritical.

Because the positive transverse eigenvalue at \(a=4\) is separated from zero, the corresponding transverse Floquet exponent stays positive for sufficiently small homogeneous periodic orbits. Thus the corrected local picture under this \((H_1)\) witness is a supercritical but transversely unstable homogeneous Hopf branch.

## Relationship to prior work

Miao and He state both ingredients needed for the contradiction: their Theorem 2.1 calls the local-model Hopf supercritical, while the text before Theorem 3.2 explicitly says the spatially homogeneous PDE Hopf is the ODE dynamics. Theorem 3.2 then changes the direction to subcritical under \((H_1)\).

Wu and Zhao previously analyzed stability, Hopf bifurcation, and Turing instability for the same diffusive hunting-cooperation model. Their published abstract does not supply the source-specific correction above. Ryu and Ko later studied global dynamics and Hopf phenomena in a related diffusive hunting-cooperation system; that broader work likewise does not state that diffusion reverses the side of an invariant homogeneous Hopf branch.

Searches by the exact DOI and title, Theorem 3.2, the \((H_1)\) terminology, and “subcritical/supercritical” correction language did not locate a published erratum resolving the contradiction.

## Limitations

The finding corrects the direction of the spatially homogeneous branch. It does not calculate the direction of any genuinely spatially inhomogeneous Hopf branch.

The transverse-instability conclusion is local near the Hopf point. It does not characterize the eventual nonlinear attractor after a nonconstant perturbation.

A non-indexed author correction could exist outside the checked records.
