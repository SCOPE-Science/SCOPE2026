# Disjoint Segre singular strata of complete multipartite edge toric varieties
## Finding
For integers \(r\ge2\) and \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\ge1\), put \(N=\sum_i a_i\) and let \(G=K_{a_1,\ldots,a_r}\). In the projective toric variety \(X_G\) of its edge monomials, for \(i<j\) set \(m_{ij}=N-a_i-a_j\). Denote by \(Z_{ij}\) the reduced coordinate subvariety in which the only potentially nonzero coordinates correspond to edges joining parts \(i,j\).

The \(Z_{ij}\) are pairwise disjoint Segre varieties \(\mathbb P^{a_i-1}\times\mathbb P^{a_j-1}\), and
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_G)_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigsqcup_{i<j:\,m_{ij}\ge2} Z_{ij}.
\]
Every affine chart at a coordinate edge joining parts \(i,j\) is \(\mathbb A^{a_i+a_j-2}\times D_{2,m_{ij}}\), where \(D_{2,m}\) is the rank-at-most-one determinantal variety of \(2\times m\) matrices (smooth for \(m=0,1\)). For \(m_{ij}\ge2\), at each closed point of \(Z_{ij}\) the transverse germ is the rank-one determinantal cone, with Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity \(m_{ij}\), local divisor class group \(\mathbb Z\), and singular-stratum codimension \(m_{ij}+1\). It is Gorenstein exactly if \(m_{ij}=2\); for \(m_{ij}>2\) its canonical class is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier. In particular \(X_G\) is normal.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over the algebraically closed field \(\mathbb C\). The graph is connected, simple, complete multipartite, with every part nonempty. Fix formal coordinates \(t_v\) for its vertices. Define
\[
X_G=\overline{\{[t_ut_v]_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}: t_v\in\mathbb C^*\}}\subset \mathbb P^{|E(G)|-1}.
\]
The closure is reduced with its toric scheme structure; \(Z_{ij}\) carries the reduced induced structure. A \(2\times0\) rank-one variety means a point, and \(D_{2,1}\) is \(\mathbb A^2\). "Transverse" refers to the indicated product affine chart; all local multiplicity and divisor-class assertions concern closed points of the projective variety. No statement about scheme-theoretic reducedness of the Jacobian singular scheme is implied.

## Proof
**Affine chart calculation.** Fix an edge \(uv\), with \(u\) in part \(i\) and \(v\) in part \(j\). On the affine chart \(z_{uv}\ne0\) introduce, for the remaining vertices in the two chosen parts,
\[
x_{u'}=z_{u'v}/z_{uv}=t_{u'}/t_u,\qquad y_{v'}=z_{uv'}/z_{uv}=t_{v'}/t_v.
\]
For each vertex \(w\) in the remaining \(m_{ij}\) vertices set
\[
p_w=z_{wv}/z_{uv}=t_w/t_u,\qquad q_w=z_{uw}/z_{uv}=t_w/t_v.
\]
Every chart coordinate is among these variables or is a product of two: an edge inside the chosen two parts gives \(x_{u'}y_{v'}\), a chosen-part/outside edge gives \(x_{u'}q_w\) or \(y_{v'}p_w\), and any edge between two outside parts gives \(p_wq_{w'}\). Thus the chart coordinate ring is generated by the indicated \(x,y,p,q\).

Its complete kernel consists of the minors \(p_wq_{w'}-p_{w'}q_w\). Indeed, equality of the Laurent monomials forces equal exponents of each \(x_{u'}\) and \(y_{v'}\); it also fixes the total exponent of each outside vertex \(w\), and the total number of \(p\)-factors. Any two allocations of the same outside multiset into \(p\) and \(q\) with the same \(p\)-count differ by swaps \(p_wq_{w'}\leftrightarrow p_{w'}q_w\). This proves both the asserted presentation and absence of additional relations. Consequently
\[
U_{uv}\cong\mathbb A^{a_i+a_j-2}\times\operatorname{Spec}\frac{\mathbb C[p_w,q_w:\,w\notin C_i\cup C_j]}{(p_wq_{w'}-p_{w'}q_w:\,w,w')}.
\]
The second factor is the normal rank-one determinantal semigroup variety \(D_{2,m_{ij}}\). Its normality may also be seen directly: its semigroup is the set of all nonnegative row and column exponent vectors with equal totals, which is saturated in its group. Hence the affine cover proves \(X_G\) normal.

**Entire singular locus.** For \(m=0,1\) the determinantal factor is smooth. For \(m\ge2\), the \(2\times2\)-minor scheme has rank-one locus smooth of dimension \(m+1\) away from the zero matrix: after inverting one nonzero entry, solve for the remaining entries in that row or column. At the zero matrix the equations have no linear terms while the embedding dimension is \(2m>m+1\), so the origin is singular. Within \(U_{uv}\), its singular locus is precisely \(p_w=q_w=0\) for all outside vertices \(w\), which is \(Z_{ij}\cap U_{uv}\). Varying \(uv\) covers all points of \(X_G\). The \(Z_{ij}\) are Segre varieties since the surviving coordinate matrix has entries \(t_ut_v\) across two parts; their supports are disjoint nonempty coordinate sets, so distinct \(Z_{ij}\) have empty intersection. This establishes the reduced singular-locus decomposition.

**Local invariants.** The smooth factor has dimension \(a_i+a_j-2\) and \(D_{2,m}\) has dimension \(m+1\), giving \(\dim X_G=N-1\) for \(r\ge3\) and \(\operatorname{codim}(Z_{ij},X_G)=m_{ij}+1\); for \(r=2\), \(X_G\) is the smooth Segre variety and no singular stratum is present. For \(m\ge2\), the affine cone \(D_{2,m}\) has projectivization \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\). Its degree, and hence the local Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity of its vertex, is \(m\). Equivalently its Hilbert series is \((1+(m-1)z)/(1-z)^{m+1}\).

The local toric divisor class group at that vertex is the free quotient
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1})/\langle\mathcal O(1,1)\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
The anticanonical bundle of the projectivization has bidegree \((2,m)\); modulo \((1,1)\), its class is \((m-2)\) times a generator. Since the quotient is torsion-free, the canonical class is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier precisely for \(m=2\), which is exactly the Gorenstein case. Adding the smooth affine factor leaves the indicated completed local transverse model, multiplicity, divisor class group, and \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier test unchanged. All claimed local invariants follow.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3, no third-party packages. It independently checks the monomial-generation and degree-at-most-four kernel assertions for \(100\) graph-edge charts across \(12\) complete multipartite shapes, including complete graphs and unbalanced parts. It also checks rank \(m-1\) of the minor Jacobian at \(28\) sample nonzero rank-one matrices with \(2\le m\le8\), and zero Jacobian at the origin. These are finite regression tests; the infinite assertion uses the preceding semigroup-presentation and determinantal proofs.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi--Hibi (2000) study compressed edge polytopes, Ehrhart data and initial ideals of complete multipartite graphs. Ohsugi--Hibi (2008), in their classification of simple graph edge polytopes, already establish the resulting smoothness classification; claiming that old criterion as new would be incorrect. Huh--Sturmfels (2013) provide the archival algebraic-geometric language for projective monomial models, Segre and determinantal loci. Lasoń--Michałek (2020) classify Gorenstein matroid base polytopes, a stronger result concerning a different **global section-ring** property, not the pointwise singular strata described here. Higashitani--Matsushita (2020) investigate global class groups of affine complete multipartite edge rings, not local class groups of all singular points in the projective edge variety. The proposed contribution is the **uniform, entire projective singular-stratum decomposition and transverse local models**, with multiplicity, local class group and \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein threshold.

## Limitations
Only complete multipartite graphs over \(\mathbb C\) are covered; no claims for arbitrary graph edge varieties, nonreduced Jacobian singular schemes, positive-characteristic singularity classifications, or entire affine-cone global class groups are made. The 2000 source was accessible in indexing/abstract form rather than a complete original article; an undiscovered explicit formulation there or elsewhere remains a literature-overlap risk.
