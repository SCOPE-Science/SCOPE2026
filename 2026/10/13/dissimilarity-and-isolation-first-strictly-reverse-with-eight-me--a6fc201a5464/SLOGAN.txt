With two equally sized groups in two tracts, dissimilarity is \(D=d/N\) while isolation is \(I=1/2+d^2/[2t(2N-t)]\), so isolation also reacts to tract-size imbalance. The first strict ranking conflict occurs at \(N=8\) members per group, uniquely up to symmetries: \((1,0)+(7,8)\) is less segregated by dissimilarity but more segregated by isolation than \((5,3)+(3,5)\).
