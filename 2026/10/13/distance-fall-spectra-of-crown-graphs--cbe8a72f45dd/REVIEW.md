# Review

## Correctness
PASS. In \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) for \(n\ge3\), the deleted partner \(b_i\) is the unique vertex at distance \(3\) from \(a_i\), and symmetrically. Every independent set is a shore subset or one deleted matched pair. Hence a nonempty independent set is distance-\(2\) dominating exactly when its size is at least two. The spectrum bounds and constructions then follow directly. The radius-\(3\) branch uses the exact diameter, and the radius-\(1\) branch follows from the independent dominating sets. Exhaustive checks on small crowns independently reproduce all asserted spectra.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating distance-fall paper proves existence of a distance-\(2\) fall \(3\)-coloring for every connected \(3\)-colorable graph, but that result supplies only one feasible color count for crowns and does not characterize their color classes or spectrum. Its full text contains no crown or perfect-matching treatment. Exact searches under distance-fall, crown, matching-deleted biclique, fall spectrum, and independent distance-domination formulations did not locate the radius-\(2\) characterization. The ordinary fall endpoint \(\{2,n\}\) is treated as prior context, not as new content.

## Value
PASS. Crowns are a canonical diameter-\(3\) bipartite family, so distance \(2\) is the first nonautomatic radius in the new distance-fall framework. The theorem upgrades a general existence result to a complete spectrum and an if-and-only-if description of every admissible color class, and it identifies a sharp transition from the disconnected classical fall spectrum \(\{2,n\}\) to the interval \(\{2,\ldots,n\}\), then to the full proper-coloring interval at radius \(3\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is Goddard--Mafunda, arXiv:2508.21232v1, which defines distance-fall coloring and proves the general distance-\(2\), three-color existence theorem. Classical fall-coloring literature supplies the radius-\(1\) context. The result does not count colorings or address general matching-deleted bicliques. A residual bibliographic risk remains that the short crown calculation appears under older independent-distance-domination terminology.
