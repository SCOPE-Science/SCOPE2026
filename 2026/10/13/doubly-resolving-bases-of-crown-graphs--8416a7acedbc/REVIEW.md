# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

**PASS.** In the connected crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}-M\), \(n\ge3\), the exact graph-distance table reduces vertex-pair differences to three symmetry types. Omitting two matched pairs fails a same-side test. Adjacent cross pairs have two complementary transversal difference-level sets; avoiding them forces at least three selected vertices in each bipartition side for a size-\(n-1\) set. This is feasible exactly for \(n\ge7\). At smaller orders, the two types of size-\(n\) bases follow from the same level sets. A separate exact checker tested all 142,924 subsets through the claimed optimum for \(3\le n\le9\), confirming every stated basis count and the classification. These finite tests corroborate, rather than replace, the general proof.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** The original parameter and broad bounds occur in Cáceres et al. (2007); complete-bipartite values occur in Jannesari (2021). The cocktail-party graph treated by Liu et al. (2020) is a different matching-deleted graph, and the 2026 Kneser treatment is not the bipartite Kneser crown. Published same-invariant complete-multipartite and nested chain-graph classifications do not specialize to crowns. A published edge-metric crown basis theorem addresses a distinct vertex-to-edge statistic and does not imply the side-balance obstruction. Known small special cases are not independently new. An unindexed equivalent crown result, including a thesis or appendix, remains a genuine priority risk.

## Scientific value

**PASS.** This is a natural complete optimum-landmark classification of a canonical infinite graph family. The sharp transition at \(n=7\), the exact structural description, and the enumerations strengthen a bare scalar result and clarify how doubly resolving sets differ from edge-metric bases. It does not enumerate nonminimum sets or imply analogous classifications for arbitrary bipartite graphs.

This is a mathematical assessment only. Formal verification and expert attestation are not asserted.
