# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The edge-conflict table follows by directly listing the third-edge bridges between horizontal and spoke edges of \(C_n\square K_2\). Two partitions of the \(3n\) edges into conflicting triples give the universal upper bound \(n\). Equality forces a three-periodic spoke pattern and an alternating horizontal-layer assignment, which exists precisely when \(6\mid n\). The six-periodic template, the explicit seam conditions, and the \(n=3\) exception attain the required lower bounds for all remaining orders. The independent standard-library checker validates the complete conflict table in eleven dimensions, 178 witnesses, exact small optima and equality counts. Code checks complement rather than replace the unbounded proof. Risk: any omitted boundary bridge would invalidate both steps; it is controlled by the explicit complete conflict table and separate enumeration.

## Originality — PASS (residual historical risk)
Brešar–Samadi's \(|E|/\delta\) bound is sharp at certain graphs, but does not imply the complete prism residue formula, or the six packing census. Brešar–Dravec–Hedžet–Samadi give Cartesian-product **lower** bounds and three-packing results, not this formula. The injective edge *chromatic index* is the number of EOP classes in a cover of all edges and does not determine the largest individual class. A foundational study by Chelladurai and collaborators considers other graph families. Searches by graph alias (circular ladder, prism, generalized Petersen graph \(G(n,1)\)) and invariant alias (edge-open packing, injective-edge-color class, maximum EOP) found no equivalent. A potentially overlooked formula in specialized injective coloring or generalized Petersen literature remains a genuine risk; novelty is not logically proven by absence of a search hit.

## Value — PASS
This is an exact invariant for an infinite elementary three-regular Cartesian-product family, not a relabeling of a known coloring index. The sharp six-residue transition, constructive optimum at each order, and rigidity/census of all equality cases refine the prior global degree bound. This precisely identifies when a prism is extremal for that bound and gives all certificates when it is; these are natural structural questions for edge-open packing.
