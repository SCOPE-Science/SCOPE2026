# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The full-domain/partial-bijection distinction is maintained: the inverse submonoid always includes the global identity. The proof of the two-generator lemma uses componentwise exponent shifts, rank monotonicity, and a strict rank drop for exponent magnitude at least two; the cycle-only case uses the order of a cyclic permutation group on its support. Separately implemented symbolic labeled-component coefficient extraction and explicit inverse-submonoid closure enumeration through six vertices agree on every parameter cell. All displayed formulas are exact for integer \(n\ge1\), \(k\ge1\); finite tests are not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

The closest full-text work is Elliott–Levine–Mitchell (2023), arXiv:2303.12387, whose Theorem 1 explicitly counts **non-isomorphic** monogenic inverse submonoids and whose Lemmas 8–10 classify abstract structures, not exact labeled embedded subsets. The Preston (1986) full-text study concerns representations and classification up to isomorphism. The new classification of the actual generating fibers and the exact multivariate embedded enumerator answer a different enumerative question. Comparisons against relevant published material and recorded database searches found no dominating formula, but the literature search cannot exclude an unpublished or differently titled equivalent calculation.

## Value — PASS

The distinction between abstract inverse monoids and their literal occurrences as submonoids of \(I_n\) is natural in finite semigroup enumeration. The finding supplies complete embedded multiplicities, not just a finite table, and its unexpectedly uniform two-generator rigidity yields a short exact total and an asymptotic dominance result. These facts can be used to benchmark inverse-monoid enumeration algorithms and investigate distribution among embeddings.
