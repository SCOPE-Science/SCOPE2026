# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The source equations give the BSC boundary \(R=1-h_2(p)\), the PSC/Holevo boundary \(p=1/2-\sqrt{h_2^{-1}(R)(1-h_2^{-1}(R))}\), and the QD-HDQI relation \(p=(1-\operatorname{sech}\beta)/2\). Their composition yields the two exact inverse-temperature boundaries. The identity \(\operatorname{asech}(2\sqrt{q(1-q)})=\operatorname{atanh}(1-2q)\) follows on \(0<q<1/2\). Standard entropy expansions at \(0\) and \(1/2\) give the endpoint laws with the displayed constants. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The anchor paper states the channel capacities, the threshold-to-temperature map, and finite-rate numerical Shannon/Holevo bounds, but not the dual endpoint classification. Earlier BPQM work studies the BSC-versus-pure-state-channel capacity advantage in optical communication, including a weak-signal capacity-ratio divergence, but does not translate it through the Gibbs inverse-temperature map or derive these rate-zero and rate-one temperature asymptotics. Semantic searches for equivalent inverse-temperature, binary-entropy-inverse, and endpoint formulations found no covering statement. A recent binary-code rate-bound paper using the same channel pair was only available at abstract-level detail during this review, so differently phrased overlap there remains a stated residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The source presents the Shannon and Holevo curves as the information-theoretic temperature limits of classical and quantum decoding. The new endpoint laws reveal that their separation is qualitatively different at the two rate extremes: an unbounded multiplicative gain at high rate versus an unbounded additive but relatively vanishing gain at low rate. This is a natural asymptotic invariant of the source's temperature frontier and clarifies where a finite-rate percentage comparison is structurally misleading.

## Limits
The result is conditional on the uniformly coupled commuting QD-HDQI channel reduction and concerns capacity ceilings. It does not establish decoder achievability, finite-blocklength performance, or optimality among all Gibbs-state-preparation algorithms.
