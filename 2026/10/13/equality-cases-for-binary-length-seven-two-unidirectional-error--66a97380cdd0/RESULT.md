# Equality cases for binary length-seven two-unidirectional-error codes
## Finding
Let \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^7\) be a binary block code that corrects every pattern of at most two unidirectional errors: for a transmitted word, up to two bits may change, and all changed bits in that word have the same direction. The known optimum is \(|C|=4\). Among labeled subsets of \(\{0,1\}^7\), exactly \(1680\) codes attain this optimum.

Under the order-\(10080\) group generated by arbitrary coordinate permutations and global complementation, these \(1680\) maxima form exactly two orbits. One orbit has size \(1260\), stabilizer order \(8\), and sorted codeword-weight profile \((2,3,4,5)\). The other has size \(420\), stabilizer order \(24\), and sorted weight profile \((1,3,4,6)\). Representatives are
\[
\{0000011,0011100,1100101,1111010\}
\]
and
\[
\{0000001,0001110,1110010,1111101\},
\]
respectively. The union of the four disjoint radius-two unidirectional error balls has size \(72\) for every code in the first orbit and \(80\) for every code in the second orbit.

The unordered directional-difference multiset \(\{\{N(x,y),N(y,x)\}:\{x,y\}\subset C\}\) is also rigid on each orbit. It equals
\[
\{(1,3),(1,3),(1,4),(2,3),(2,3),(2,3)\}
\]
for the \(1260\)-element orbit and
\[
\{(0,5),(1,3),(1,3),(1,4),(1,4),(2,3)\}
\]
for the \(420\)-element orbit.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^7\), let \(N(x,y)\) be the number of coordinates with \(x_i=1\) and \(y_i=0\). A pair is comparable when one word covers the other coordinatewise. The unidirectional distance used here is
\[
d_u(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
N(x,y)+N(y,x),&\text{if }N(x,y)=0\text{ or }N(y,x)=0,\\
2\max\{N(x,y),N(y,x)\},&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
The classical necessary-and-sufficient condition says that correction of at most two unidirectional errors is equivalent to pairwise \(d_u\ge 5\). The classification is only for binary length seven and does not assert that the displayed order-\(10080\) group is the full graph automorphism group.

## Proof
The maximum size \(4\) is classical: Weber, de Vroedt and Boekee prove \(A_3(2t+3,t)=4\) for every positive integer \(t\); specializing to \(t=2\) gives the length-seven optimum.

For the equality classification, construct the compatibility graph on all \(128\) binary words, joining two words exactly when their radius-two unidirectional error balls are disjoint. Independently compute \(d_u\) for every pair and verify that ball disjointness is equivalent to \(d_u\ge5\). The graph has \(2976\) edges.

An exhaustive increasing-label clique enumeration finds exactly \(1680\) four-cliques. For every one of these four-cliques, the intersection of the four neighbor sets is empty, so no five-clique exists. Sorting the four Hamming weights yields only two profiles, with multiplicities \(1260\) and \(420\). Acting on one representative of each profile by all \(7!\) coordinate permutations and global complementation produces disjoint orbits of exactly those two sizes, and their union is the complete set of \(1680\) maxima. Orbit-stabilizer then gives stabilizer orders \(8\) and \(24\). Direct evaluation of the pairwise directional counts and of the four disjoint error-ball sizes gives the two stated rigid profiles and coverage totals.

## Relationship to prior work
Bose and Rao developed the basic theory of binary unidirectional-error correction and proved the necessary-and-sufficient pairwise condition. Weber, de Vroedt and Boekee later established the exact small-length theorem \(A_3(2t+3,t)=4\), including the case considered here. Their inspected papers give distance conditions, constructions, bounds and exact optimum cardinalities; they do not state an enumeration or equivalence classification of all optimal length-seven, two-error codes. Targeted searches for the exact parameter, its distance formulation, equality cases and equivalent single-direction-error terminology likewise found no implication-equivalent classification.

## Limitations
This is a finite, computer-assisted equality-case classification at one parameter. The claimed two-orbit statement is with respect to coordinate permutations and global complementation only; a larger abstract automorphism group, if present, is not ruled out. The literature search cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed historical enumeration, although the two highly relevant primary papers were inspected in full and no such enumeration was found.
