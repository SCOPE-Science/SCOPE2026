# Equality cases for the length-six binary adjacent-transposition bound
## Finding
For a binary word \(x\in\{0,1\}^6\), let \(B(x)\) be the closed one-error ball consisting of \(x\) and every word obtained by swapping one adjacent coordinate pair. Swapping equal neighboring bits leaves the word unchanged. A code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) corrects one adjacent transposition exactly when the balls \(B(x)\), \(x\in C\), are pairwise disjoint.

The known optimum is \(20\). There are exactly \(7056\) labeled optimum codes. Every optimum has Hamming-weight layer sizes
\[
(1,2,4,6,4,2,1),
\]
and the numbers of optimal layer choices at weights \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6\) are respectively
\[
(1,6,14,1,14,6,1).
\]
Consequently the number of full optimum codes is
\[
1\cdot 6\cdot 14\cdot 1\cdot 14\cdot 6\cdot 1=7056.
\]
The weight-three layer is unique:
\[
\{\mathtt{000111},\mathtt{001110},\mathtt{011100},\mathtt{100011},\mathtt{110001},\mathtt{111000}\}.
\]
Under the natural order-four action generated by global complement and reversal of all six coordinates, the \(7056\) maxima form exactly \(1810\) orbits: \(4\) singleton orbits, \(86\) orbits of size \(2\), and \(1720\) orbits of size \(4\). This statement concerns that explicit natural action only; it does not assert that this order-four group is the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is binary, the block length is exactly \(6\), and the only channel error is one adjacent transposition; there is no end-around swap. The received word has the same length and Hamming weight as the transmitted word. Codes are arbitrary subsets, not assumed linear or constant-weight.

“Labeled” means that the six coordinate positions and the symbols \(0,1\) are fixed. The orbit count quotients only by the explicitly stated complement-and-reversal action. No claim is made for any other block length or for a larger equivalence group.

## Proof
An adjacent transposition preserves Hamming weight. Hence if \(x\) and \(y\) have different Hamming weights, then \(B(x)\cap B(y)=\varnothing\). The optimization therefore splits exactly into the seven constant-weight layers.

For each weight \(k\), define a graph whose vertices are the binary length-six words of weight \(k\), with an edge joining two words when one is obtained from the other by swapping an adjacent unequal pair. Two words can occur together in a correcting code exactly when their graph distance is at least \(3\): their closed radius-one graph neighborhoods are then disjoint. The included verifier checks this equivalence directly against error-ball intersection for every same-weight pair.

The verifier then exhaustively enumerates all subsets at the candidate optimum size and all subsets one larger in each layer. It obtains layer maxima
\[
(1,2,4,6,4,2,1)
\]
with exact numbers of maximizing subsets
\[
(1,6,14,1,14,6,1).
\]
For every layer it also finds zero valid subsets of cardinality one larger. Summing the layer maxima proves the global upper bound \(20\). Because different layers never interfere, every independent choice of a maximizing subset in each layer gives a full optimum code, and every full optimum arises uniquely this way. Multiplying the seven counts gives \(7056\).

The same enumeration finds a single maximizing weight-three subset, namely the six displayed words. Finally, the verifier applies complement and reversal to all \(7056\) maxima and computes the resulting orbit partition, obtaining sizes \(1\) with multiplicity \(4\), \(2\) with multiplicity \(86\), and \(4\) with multiplicity \(1720\).

## Verification
Run

```text
python3 artifacts/verify.py
```

from the package directory. The script reconstructs all \(64\) binary words and all one-transposition balls from the definition. Independently, it constructs every constant-weight token graph and computes graph distances by breadth-first search. It requires exact agreement between ball disjointness and distance at least \(3\) on every same-weight pair.

It exhaustively checks all subsets at the claimed layer maximum and at one larger cardinality, constructs all \(7056\) full maxima by Cartesian product of the layer maxima, validates every full code from the original error-ball definition, checks the unique weight-three layer, and recomputes the natural-group orbit partition. The packaged replay output is stored in `artifacts/verification_output.txt`.

The coverage distribution of the \(7056\) disjoint-ball unions is also recomputed as a secondary check; it ranges from \(46\) to \(58\) received words and is recorded in `artifacts/certificate.json`. This profile is not needed for the main claim.

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A057608 records the maximum size of a binary length-\(n\) code correcting one adjacent transposition and gives the length-six value \(20\). Sloane's challenge-problem page gives the equivalent \(64\)-vertex independent-set benchmark `1tc.64` and explicitly notes that the graph decomposes by Hamming weight.

Gómez Soto, Leaños, Ríos-Castro, and Rivera identify the constant-weight version with packing in token graphs and determine the packing number for the weight-two family. Their formulation supplies the exact graph-distance interpretation used here, but the inspected paper determines maximum cardinalities rather than enumerating all maximizing sets. The later study of three-token path graphs likewise addresses packing numbers and constructions rather than the complete length-six equality census.

The contribution here is therefore not the known optimum \(20\), but the complete labeled equality-case count, the layer-by-layer factorization of all maximizers, the forced central layer, and the orbit census for the stated natural action.

## Limitations
The result is a finite exact classification for length \(6\) only. It does not imply a formula for the number of maximizers at other lengths. The order-four action used for orbit counting is a declared channel symmetry and is not claimed to be the full compatibility-graph automorphism group.

The originality comparison cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed historical enumeration of all `1tc.64` optima. No inspected source states or implies the \(7056\) count, the layer-choice vector, the forced weight-three layer, or the stated natural-action orbit partition.
