# Equality classification of seven-bit codes correcting a deletion or adjacent transposition

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal{C}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{7}\) be a binary block code capable of uniquely correcting **either exactly one deleted coordinate or at most one adjacent-symbol transposition**, with the receiver knowing the received length. The largest possible code has \(16\) words. There are exactly \(28\) unordered, labeled optimal codes of size \(16\). Under the order-four symmetry group generated by reversing all coordinate positions and complementing every bit, there are precisely \(8\) optimal-code equivalence classes: \(6\) orbits of size \(4\) and \(2\) orbits of size \(2\).

For comparison, exactly \(408\) distinct labeled \(16\)-word codes correct a single deletion without the transposition requirement. Each of the eight classical length-seven Varshamov–Tenengolts (VT) syndrome classes modulo \(8\) has \(16\) words, yet **none** corrects the mixed deletion-or-transposition channel.

## Assumptions and scope

For each \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_7)\in\{0,1\}^{7}\), let \(D(x)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{6}\) be the set of all distinct words obtained by removing exactly one coordinate of \(x\). Let \(T(x)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{7}\) consist of \(x\) itself and all distinct words obtained from \(x\) by swapping one pair of adjacent coordinates. Swapping identical bits leaves \(x\) unchanged. An unordered code \(\mathcal{C}\) is valid precisely when, for every pair \(x\ne y\) in \(\mathcal{C}\), both \(D(x)\cap D(y)=\varnothing\) and \(T(x)\cap T(y)=\varnothing\). This does **not** claim correction of a deletion and a transposition occurring in succession, a substitution, or two transpositions.

The \(128\) binary words are labeled by their seven-character bit strings; no coordinate or alphabet symmetry is factored out when reporting \(28\). For the orbit count, only *global* reversal \(x_1\cdots x_7\mapsto x_7\cdots x_1\) and *global* bit complementation \(x\mapsto x\oplus1111111\) are permitted. These operations commute and preserve each permitted error operation.

## Proof

**Lemma (finite reduction).** Form the graph \(G\) on the \(128\) seven-bit strings by joining distinct \(x,y\) exactly when their two deletion descendant sets are disjoint and their two transposition-output sets are disjoint. Then valid codes are exactly the cliques of \(G\). This follows directly by applying the unique-decoding requirement pairwise, since the two possible received word lengths are different.

**Exhaustive maximum and equality argument.** The program `verify_direct.py` constructs the graph directly from literal deletion and transposition outputs, with all outputs represented as actual strings. It explores cliques by depth-first branching and a proper greedy coloring of the remaining induced graph. Each color class is an independent set, so a clique can contain at most one vertex of each color, giving a mathematically valid upper bound on any extension. Every branch with a sufficient bound is searched: a vertex is chosen and recursively intersected with its adjacency set, then removed from the candidate set. Therefore every clique is considered exactly once as a possible terminal code, except branches whose correct coloring upper bound rules out reaching the current maximum. The search **computes** its maximum rather than assuming \(16\), and returns a maximum of \(16\) with precisely \(28\) maximizing sets.

An independent upper-bound and counting route is supplied in `verify_deletion_filter.cpp`. For every seven-bit word, its deletion descendants are encoded as a \(64\)-bit incidence mask; its transposition outputs are encoded as two \(64\)-bit masks. The C++ graph joins words based **only** on disjoint deletion masks. Its independently implemented exhaustive clique search proves that the deletion-only graph has no clique of size \(17\) and has exactly \(408\) distinct cliques of size \(16\). All those \(408\) cliques are then tested, without additional search-pruning assumptions, against the two transposition-mask intersections. Precisely \(28\) pass. A clique of size greater than \(17\) would contain a clique of size \(17\); thus the nonexistence check proves the global upper bound. The explicit \(28\) witnesses attain \(16\) in the mixed channel. The two complete enumerations agree on the mixed-channel optimum and labeled equality count despite distinct graph constructions and search targets.

**Equivalence classes.** The first program applies the four group elements (identity, reversal, complement, reversal followed by complement) to every maximizing set; it verifies that the image is another maximizing set and partitions the \(28\) sets into the eight orbits. The file `representatives.tsv` lists the lexicographically least labeled representative of each orbit, with its orbit size. The verifier reconstructs and checks the entire table. The sizes add to \(6\cdot4+2\cdot2=28\), and every member is accounted for.

**VT comparison.** For \(a\in\{0,\ldots,7\}\), put \(V_a=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{7}:\sum_{i=1}^{7}i x_i\equiv a\pmod8\}\). Direct computation confirms \(|V_a|=16\) for each \(a\), but also finds at least one pair \(x\ne y\) in each \(V_a\) with \(T(x)\cap T(y)\ne\varnothing\). This identifies a concrete incompatibility. The eight explicit conflicts are recorded in `vt_obstructions.tsv` and automatically checked by `verify_direct.py`. These conflicts do not contradict the known deletion-correction property of VT codes.

## Verification

Place `verify_direct.py`, `verify_deletion_filter.cpp`, `representatives.tsv` and `vt_obstructions.tsv` in the same directory. Run:

```sh
python3 verify_direct.py
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_deletion_filter.cpp -o verify_deletion_filter
./verify_deletion_filter
```

Both programs ran successfully with these outputs:

```text
VERIFY_OK optimum=16 labeled=28 orbit_sizes={2: 2, 4: 6} classes=8 VT_classes_failing=8 nodes=159080
VERIFY_OK length=7 deletion_size17=0 deletion_size16=408 mixed_size16=28 nodes=511342
```

The exhaustive programs are the finite proof certificate; the stored table is not assumed as the source of the count. The first program also confirms every representative, every orbit, and failure of all eight VT classes. The second program separately establishes the deletion-only upper bound and independently counts all mixed optima as a filter of deletion-only extremizers.

## Relationship to prior work

Gabrys, Yaakobi and Milenkovic (first preprint 2016) introduced codes correcting a single deletion *or* a single adjacent transposition, and gave construction/redundancy results (Section III). Gabrys, Guruswami, Ribeiro and Wu (2021) developed an improved asymptotic construction and explicitly discussed limitations of the earlier redundancy argument. These are the same channel but neither inspected work supplies the exact length-seven maximizing-code census or its reversal/complement orbit decomposition. No claim of novelty is made for the error model, VT codes, the definition of compatibility graphs, or generic clique enumeration.

Single-deletion results, including No (2019), give the deletion-only numerical upper bound at length seven and establish that VT codes are optimal for single deletion. That upper bound alone does not prove attainability with the additional transposition constraint or determine which deletion-optimal codes survive it. The present result resolves those remaining exact finite questions.

## Limitations

The theorem applies **only to binary length seven** and the stated alternative-error channel. An exhaustive computer search proves this finite statement but gives no infinite-length theorem or efficient construction for general length. Classification is under the explicitly stated four-element group, not under arbitrary coordinate permutations, which need not preserve adjacency. The research literature comparison may have missed an unindexed finite census; no claim of independence or third-party audit is made. The 2016 paper's asymptotic argument is not used in the proof; the later 2021 paper notes that its claimed constant-gap redundancy argument was flawed.
