# Equivariant formal conicality at the deep vertices of elementary-symmetric quartics

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(e_j\) be the degree-\(j\) elementary symmetric polynomial in \(n\) variables, and set
\[
X_n=V(e_4(x_1,\ldots,x_n))\subset \mathbb P^{n-1}_{\mathbb C}.
\]
At each coordinate vertex \(p\) of \(X_n\), writing \(m=n-1\), there is an \(S_m\)-equivariant formal automorphism, tangent to the identity and preserving every coordinate-support subspace, and an invertible symmetric formal series, taking a local equation of \(X_n\) to \(e_3(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\). In particular,
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,p}\cong
\mathbb C[[z_1,\ldots,z_m]]/(e_3(z_1,\ldots,z_m)).
\]
This is an equivalence of **entire completed germs**, not just equality of tangent cones, multiplicities, or Hilbert functions.

The reduced singular locus is the union of the \(\binom n2\) coordinate projective lines. At any nonvertex point of this locus, an analytic transverse ordinary quadratic double point occurs, and the completed local ring is
\[
\mathbb C[[s,z_1,\ldots,z_{n-2}]]/
\left(\sum_{1\le i<j\le n-2}z_i z_j\right).
\]
The local multiplicity is three at coordinate vertices and two at other singular points. The respective associated-graded Hilbert–Samuel series are
\[
\frac{1-T^3}{(1-T)^{n-1}},\qquad
\frac{1-T^2}{(1-T)^{n-1}}.
\]
The projective hypersurface is integral and normal.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is \(\mathbb C\), the characteristic-zero formal power-series topology is the maximal-ideal topology, and all algebraic projective points are closed. Coordinate vertices have exactly one nonzero homogeneous coordinate. Nonvertex points on coordinate lines have exactly two nonzero homogeneous coordinates. Completed local rings are completed at the corresponding closed-point maximal ideals. The support-preserving automorphism sends each ideal generated by a selection of variables to itself.

## Proof

**1. The local equation and reduced singular support.** On the chart \(x_1=1\), the equation is exactly \(e_3(z)+e_4(z)=0\), where \(z\) comprises the other \(m\) coordinates. To identify the singular support, observe that \(\partial_{x_i}e_4=e_3(\widehat{x_i})\). At a critical point, summing these derivatives gives \((n-3)e_3(x)=0\). Since \(e_3(x)=e_3(\widehat{x_i})+x_i e_2(\widehat{x_i})\), all nonzero coordinates satisfy \(e_2(\widehat{x_i})=0\). If their number is \(r\ge3\), summation over the nonzero coordinates gives \((r-2)e_2(x)=0\); thus \(e_2(x)=0\). The deletion formula now implies \(x_i(e_1(x)-x_i)=0\), hence every nonzero \(x_i\) equals \(e_1(x)\), a contradiction on summing unless all vanish. Conversely, support of size at most two annihilates every first derivative. Hence the singular support is precisely the stated union of coordinate lines.

**2. The equivariant formal elimination lemma.** Put \(R=\mathbb C[[z_1,\ldots,z_m]]\) and \(S=R^{S_m}\cong\mathbb C[[e_1,\ldots,e_m]]\), interpreted with the ordinary variable-degree filtration. Let \(I=(e_3,\ldots,e_m)S\): this is exactly the invariant ideal of series vanishing on every coordinate subspace with at most two potentially nonzero variables. For \(2\le r\le m-2\), define
\[
H_r=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_i^{r}\frac{\partial e_3}{\partial z_i}.
\]
The coordinate-preserving derivation defining \(H_r\) is tangent to each such coordinate subspace, so \(H_r\in I\). Writing \(p_j=\sum_i z_i^j\) and using \(\partial_i e_3=e_2-e_1z_i+z_i^2\), one has
\[
H_r=e_2p_r-e_1p_{r+1}+p_{r+2}.
\]
Newton's identities, reduced modulo \((e_3,\ldots,e_{r+1})\), give the **triangular congruence**
\[
H_r\equiv(-1)^{r+1}(r+2)e_{r+2}\pmod{(e_3,\ldots,e_{r+1})S}.
\]
Indeed, no term depending only on \(e_1,e_2\) can survive, because \(H_r\) vanishes on all two-coordinate planes; the only new generator of weighted degree \(r+2\) is \(e_{r+2}\), whose coefficient is the power-sum coefficient \((-1)^{r+1}(r+2)\). All these coefficients are invertible in \(\mathbb C\). It follows inductively that
\[
I=(e_3,H_2,\ldots,H_{m-2})S.
\]
This is the essential nonstandard formal-rigidity computation.

**3. Elimination in every degree.** Suppose a symmetric formal equation has the form \(F=e_3+P_d+O(\mathfrak m^{d+1})\), where \(d\ge4\), \(P_d\) is homogeneous of degree \(d\), and \(F\in I\). By the generator identity from step 2, homogeneity gives
\[
P_d=a e_3+\sum_{r=2}^{m-2}b_rH_r,
\]
where \(a\) and each \(b_r\) are symmetric homogeneous polynomials of degrees \(d-3\) and \(d-r-2\), respectively; omit coefficients of negative degree. The substitution
\[
z_i\longmapsto z_i-\sum_{r=2}^{m-2}b_r(z)z_i^r
\]
is tangent to the identity, commutes with the permutation group, preserves every coordinate-support plane, and changes the degree-\(d\) part of \(e_3\) by \(-\sum b_rH_r\). A symmetric unit \(1-a+O(\mathfrak m^{d-2})\) cancels the remaining \(a e_3\) term. The resulting equation is \(e_3+O(\mathfrak m^{d+1})\) and still belongs to \(I\). Induction over all \(d\ge4\) produces convergent (maximal-ideal-adic) automorphisms and units, yielding the claimed contact equivalence. The maps are genuine formal coordinate changes: their linear terms are the identity. No finite truncation is invoked to deduce the infinite statement.

**4. Generic line points.** At a point with coordinates \([1:a:0:\cdots:0]\), \(a\ne0\), the exact local equation in the remaining \(n-2\) variables \(z\) is \(a e_2(z)+(1+a)e_3(z)+e_4(z)\). The Hessian of \(e_2(z)\) has eigenvalues \(n-3\) once and \(-1\) with multiplicity \(n-3\), so it is nonsingular. The formal Morse lemma over the coefficient ring \(\mathbb C[[a-a_0]]\), after absorbing the invertible parameter \(a\), eliminates all terms of order at least three in \(z\) and gives the indicated quadratic presentation. The parameter direction is smooth.

**5. Multiplicity, Hilbert series and normality.** At a vertex the maximal-ideal initial form is the nonzero homogeneous cubic \(e_3\); elsewhere on the singular support it is the nondegenerate homogeneous quadratic \(e_2\). The hypersurface tangent-cone formula gives both displayed Hilbert series and multiplicities. For irreducibility: \(e_3\) in four variables is a primitive linear polynomial in the last variable because \(\gcd(e_2(x_1,x_2,x_3),x_1x_2x_3)=1\). Likewise \(e_4\) in five variables is primitive linear in the fifth, with relatively prime coefficients \(e_3\) in four variables and their product. Inductively for more variables, \(e_4\) in the first \(n-1\) is irreducible and cannot divide the lower-degree coefficient \(e_3\). Gauss's lemma gives irreducibility for all \(n\ge5\). Finally \(X_n\) is a hypersurface and hence satisfies Serre's \(S_2\); its singular lines have codimension \(n-3\ge2\) inside \(X_n\), so \(R_1\) and normality follow.

## Verification

The standalone `checker.py` requires Python 3 and SymPy. Running `python checker.py` checks fifteen triangular Newton identities, three first-degree formal eliminations, seven transverse Hessian ranks, and 3,159 exact integer specializations of the singular-support criterion. The recorded output is in `checker_output.txt`. These finite checks probe the critical identities but are not substitutes for steps 1–5, which prove the statement in every \(n\ge5\) and at every stated point.

## Relationship to prior work

The coordinate-support description of the singular locus of elementary-symmetric hypersurfaces is already known, as demonstrated by the 2017 MathOverflow proof and earlier symmetric-hypersurface and derivative-locus literature; **no novelty is claimed for that support alone**. Cafure–Matera–Privitelli study singular dimensions and conditions on symmetric hypersurfaces, especially in finite characteristic. Xie studies derivative and multiplicity loci in positive characteristic. The present claim concerns a stronger property absent from the materially inspected portions of those works: an all-orders, permutation-equivariant, support-preserving formal contact equivalence at every deep vertex, together with the two formal local types for this quartic family. Generic ordinary double points, integrality, and normality provide context and are not presented as independent novel breakthroughs.

## Limitations

No algebraic (Zariski-neighborhood) coordinate equivalence at the vertices is asserted; the main equivalence is formal. The theorem is characteristic zero, concerns \(e_4\) only, and does not compute the full nonreduced projective Jacobian scheme or the local class groups. An equivalent formal result in older singularity literature cannot be ruled out by searches alone. Independent validation has not been performed.
