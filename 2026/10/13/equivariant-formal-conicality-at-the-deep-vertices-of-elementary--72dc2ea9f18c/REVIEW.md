# Self-review

## Correctness: PASS

The affine vertex equation is exactly \(e_3+e_4\), not an asymptotic approximation. The invariant ideal of all symmetric functions vanishing on every two-coordinate plane is \((e_3,\ldots,e_m)\). Newton's identities prove the triangular generator relation \(I=(e_3,H_2,\ldots,H_{m-2})\), with nonzero pivot \((-1)^{r+1}(r+2)\). Degreewise equivariant coordinate substitutions and symmetric units eliminate every higher homogeneous term; their orders rise and the maps converge formally. The remaining singular-line normal form follows from the invertible Hessian of \(e_2\). The Jacobian support argument, irreducibility induction, Serre criterion, and Hilbert-series calculations close the remaining claims. A standalone exact checker reproduces small-degree critical algebra but is not the general proof.

The absence of a retrieved match is not a proof of historical uniqueness; older general equivariant singularity theory remains a risk.

Formal conicality is stronger than a known tangent cone, numerical multiplicity, reduced singular support, or a generic node. It identifies the complete local ring at every high-incidence vertex of a natural infinite family and explains why higher-degree corrections do not change analytic/formal geometry, with an explicit symmetry-compatible construction. The distinction between deep cubic germs and transverse nodes is geometrically intrinsic. No general theorem outside this family or novel numerical singular-support formula is required for the result's significance.
