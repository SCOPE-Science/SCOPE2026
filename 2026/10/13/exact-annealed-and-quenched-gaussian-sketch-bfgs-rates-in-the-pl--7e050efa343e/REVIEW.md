# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact inverse-BFGS recursion on a fixed SPD \(2\times2\) matrix is conjugated to a rank-one orthogonal-projection sandwich. This implies the **pathwise exact product**, not merely a simulated rate. The Gaussian angle has the Poisson-kernel law with Fourier coefficients \(r^{|m|}\); the stationary ergodic theorem plus the integrable logarithmic cosine series gives the almost-sure factor \(\bigl((1+r^2)/2\bigr)^4\). For squared-error expectation, the positive compact transfer operator has rank five and an even leading eigenfunction; its restricted basis matrix is the stated \(3\times3\) matrix, with rationally checked characteristic polynomial. The leading spectral coefficient is positive for every nonzero symmetric initial error. Exact rational checks and numerical tests corroborate these independent derivations. No finite simulation is asserted to prove an infinite-time statement.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded priority risk
Gower–Richtárik (arXiv:1602.01768, Sections 6.2 and 8.8) established the same matrix update and a squared-error upper bound, not its exact Gaussian two-dimensional rate; the gap is decisive even for the isotropic instance: the cited sufficient factor \(1/2\) versus the exact \(3/8\). Kovalev–Gower–Richtárik–Rogozin (arXiv:2002.11337, Sections 1–4 and Appendix B.3) proved bounds for a coupled optimization/matrix Lyapunov function, without the present exact Gaussian fixed-Hessian rate or almost-sure exponent. The accelerated system of arXiv:1802.04079 is a different iteration. Broader random-projection theory supplies abstract tools, but the identified statements do not imply these explicit anisotropy-dependent expressions without the separate angular and finite-rank calculation. Public mathematical-index searches for the same rate, aliases, and equivalent projector products did not yield an explicit equivalent. An unindexed special-case calculation remains possible; this review does not claim priority certification.

## Value — PASS
The stochastic matrix inversion method is a published numerical primitive for quasi-Newton optimization. Exact contraction factors give calibration that a general sufficient bound does not: \(\Lambda(0)=3/8\) rather than the published \(1/2\), while the typical sample-path squared-error factor is \(1/16\). The anisotropic case is structurally solved through an explicit Perron cubic and both rates worsen monotonically with condition anisotropy. The finite-dimensional result is motivated by precise interpretation of published rate bounds, not by arbitrary parameter selection. It does not generalize to changing Hessians or assert downstream optimization convergence.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
