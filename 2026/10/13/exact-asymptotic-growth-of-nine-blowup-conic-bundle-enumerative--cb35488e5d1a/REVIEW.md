# Scientific review: nine-blowup conic-bundle growth

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes the indexing shift \(N=n+1\) and uses the generating functions from Finashin–Kharlamov's Sections 7.1–7.2. Their second-moment lattice-shell definition separately verifies the factor \(1/4\) multiplying the logarithmic derivative. Jacobi's quartic identity yields both \(F=\theta_2^4\theta_3^4\) and the exact sign-cusp suppression \(F(-q)=-\theta_2^4\theta_4^4\). The eta transformation provides the dominant \(\exp(\pi^2/w)\) factor. A direct lower bound for the logarithmic product outside the two candidate saddle neighborhoods and a Gaussian calculation establish the exponent, powers and constants. The standalone integer checker corroborates the theta coefficients, derivative normalization, and decreasing finite-index error, but numerical trends are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty
The most closely related source is Finashin–Kharlamov, arXiv:2610.02445v1, which states exact theta/partition generating series and a finite table but does not supply the large-conic-degree limits or the distinction between the two dominant-cusp candidates. Their earlier arXiv:2203.10503v2 studies different del Pezzo enumerative invariants and their growth when anticanonical degree increases. Bryan–Leung supply the fixed-curve partition factor, not the bidegree-aggregated real invariant. Indexed searches for both \(\theta_2^4\theta_3^4\)-eta expressions and the specific constants did not identify a covering mathematical statement. The analytic method is classical, so an unindexed or implicit modular-form specialization remains possible and independent bibliographic confirmation is appropriate.

## Value — PASS
The precise asymptotic quantifies growth of a deformation-invariant real enumerative quantity on rational surfaces at the zero-canonical-square boundary. The exact prefactor and polynomial exponent distinguish it from merely reporting the exponential rate; comparison with the complex Gromov–Witten total yields the nonconstant square-root ratio. The cancellation at the apparent \(-1\) cusp explains why parity does not split the leading constants. This is a natural limiting regime left untreated in the initiating paper, not an arbitrary finite table extension.
