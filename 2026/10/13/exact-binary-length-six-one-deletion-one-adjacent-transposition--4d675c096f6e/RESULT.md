# Exact binary length-six one-deletion/one-adjacent-transposition code census
## Finding
For binary words \(x\in\{0,1\}^6\), define \(B(x)\) by first applying at most one adjacent transposition of unequal neighboring symbols and then applying at most one deletion; either operation may be absent. A code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\) corrects this combined error pattern exactly when \(B(x)\cap B(y)=\varnothing\) for every two distinct \(x,y\in C\).

The exact maximum is \(6\). There are exactly \(26\) labeled maximum codes. Every maximum contains the two constant words \(000000\) and \(111111\). Under the four-element group generated by reversal of the six coordinates and global bit complement, the maxima form exactly \(10\) orbits, whose sizes are
\[
1,1,2,2,2,2,4,4,4,4.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is binary and the block length is exactly \(6\). The channel is the simultaneous-error model introduced for deletion and adjacent-transposition correction: at most one adjacent transposition is followed by at most one deletion. The zero-transposition and zero-deletion possibilities are included. No claim is made for the alternative “one deletion or one transposition” channel, for longer lengths, for nonbinary alphabets, or for unrestricted Damerau–Levenshtein balls.

## Proof
Construct a compatibility graph on all \(2^6=64\) binary words. Two vertices are adjacent precisely when their combined-error balls are disjoint. A correcting code is therefore exactly a clique of this graph.

The verifier constructs every combined-error ball directly from the definition. For each word it includes the unchanged word and every result of one adjacent swap of unequal neighboring bits; from each such intermediate word it includes both that word and every length-five word obtained by deleting one coordinate. It then compares every unordered pair of length-six words and obtains a compatibility graph with \(928\) edges.

A complete Bron–Kerbosch enumeration with pivoting visits the finite compatibility graph without pruning by an unproved bound. It enumerates all \(2921\) maximal cliques, with size histogram
\[
3:218,\qquad 4:1861,\qquad 5:816,\qquad 6:26.
\]
Hence no clique has size greater than \(6\), and exactly \(26\) labeled cliques attain size \(6\).

Intersecting the vertex sets of all \(26\) maxima gives exactly \(\{000000,111111\}\). Finally, reversal and global bit complement are checked directly to preserve every compatibility relation. Applying their four generated maps to all maxima gives the stated ten-orbit decomposition.

## Relationship to prior work
Gabrys, Yaakobi, and Milenkovic introduced the binary deletion/adjacent-transposition coding problem and, in their simultaneous-error section, defined the ball obtained from at most \(\ell\) adjacent transpositions followed by at most one deletion. They constructed families correcting one deletion together with adjacent transpositions; their result is constructive and asymptotic in length rather than a complete fixed-length extremizer census.

Ye and Ge later developed upper bounds on the maximum size of deletion/transposition codes. Their introduction explicitly notes that the earlier works predominantly concerned constructions and that cardinality upper bounds remained unknown apart from a different asymmetric setting. Their fixed-error upper bounds are asymptotic and do not state the exact \(n=6\) value, the number of maximizers, the universal constant-word core, or the finite symmetry decomposition proved here.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification at one natural small parameter value. It does not establish a formula for larger lengths or classify maxima under the full automorphism group of the compatibility graph. The originality search cannot exclude an obscure or unindexed finite enumeration that was not found, although the closest primary papers inspected do not contain or imply the claimed census.
