# Exact binary ranks and ancillary-qubit budgets for mixed-parity Hamming boxes

## Finding

**Theorem (mixed-parity Hamming-box rank and resource law).** For every integer \(d\ge2\) and all integers \(q_1,\ldots,q_d\ge2\) with at least one even and at least one odd alphabet size, put
\[
G=K_{q_1}\square\cdots\square K_{q_d},\qquad N=\prod_{i=1}^d q_i.
\]
Let \(A\) denote the adjacency matrix of \(G\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Write
\[
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{i:q_i\text{ is even}\},& O&=\{i:q_i\text{ is odd}\},\\
Q&=\prod_{i\in E}q_i,& H&=\prod_{i\in E}(q_i-2),\\
P&=\prod_{i\in O}q_i,& R&=\prod_{i\in O}(q_i-2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\ker A=\frac{(Q+H)(P+(-1)^dR)}4},\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2} A=PQ-\frac{(Q+H)(P+(-1)^dR)}4}.
\]
Orient every edge in precisely one direction (without retaining undirected edges), arbitrarily. For the mixed-graph stabilizer extension of Riera--Parker--Stănică, the exact minimum number of ancillary qubits is
\[
\boxed{e(G)=\frac{PQ}{2}-\frac{(Q+H)(P+(-1)^dR)}8}.
\]
The required ancilla budget is independent of the orientations. The expressions are integers, including when some even alphabet sizes equal \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(K_q\) is the simple loopless complete graph of order \(q\), and \(\square\) is the Cartesian graph product: vertices are coordinate tuples, with an edge precisely when two tuples differ in exactly one coordinate. All matrix ranks and kernels are over \(\mathbb F_2\), not over the real or complex numbers. Each coordinate size is at least two, and both parity classes must be nonempty. The graph is connected and simple. Every undirected edge is replaced by exactly one directed arc to obtain a fully oriented mixed graph, rather than retaining the original undirected edge. The ancilla quantity is the minimum *append parameter* in the stabilizer-commutation completion defined in the cited quantum-state framework; it is not a claim about gate counts or physical hardware cost outside that model.

## Proof

**Step 1: A Kronecker-sum identity.** Write \(V_i=\mathbb F_2^{q_i}\), let \(J_i\) be its all-ones operator, and use the tensor identification \(\mathbb F_2^N=\bigotimes_i V_i\). The adjacency matrix of \(K_{q_i}\) is \(J_i+I_i\) in characteristic two. Therefore, denoting the action of \(J_i\) in the \(i\)-th tensor component by the same letter,
\[
A=(d\bmod2)I+\sum_{i=1}^d J_i.
\]

**Step 2: Separate odd coordinate sizes.** If \(q_i\) is odd, then \(J_i^2=J_i\). Its 1-eigenspace is the line spanned by the all-ones vector, and its 0-eigenspace has dimension \(q_i-1\). Thus simultaneous decomposition of all odd coordinates gives sectors indexed by subsets \(S\subseteq O\). The sector in which \(J_i\) has eigenvalue 1 exactly for \(i\in S\) occurs with multiplicity \(\prod_{i\in O\setminus S}(q_i-1)\). On this sector the remaining operator is
\[
A_S=(d+|S|\bmod2)I+D,\qquad D=\sum_{i\in E}J_i
\]
on \(\bigotimes_{i\in E}V_i\). Consequently, only sectors with \(|S|\equiv d\pmod2\) may contribute to the kernel, because otherwise \(A_S=I+D\) and \((I+D)^2=I\), as verified below.

**Step 3: Nilpotent even-coordinate differential.** If \(q_i\) is even, then \(J_i^2=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}J_i=1\). Choose a vector \(v_i\) with coordinate sum 1 and put \(u_i=J_iv_i\ne0\). Because \(J_iu_i=0\), the 2-space generated by \(v_i,u_i\) is an acyclic two-term complex; select an additional \((q_i-2)\)-dimensional complement inside \(\ker J_i\), on which \(J_i=0\). On the tensor product of the even-coordinate spaces,
\[
D=\sum_{i\in E}J_i,\qquad D^2=\sum_{i\in E}J_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}J_iJ_j=0.
\]
Each tensor summand containing an acyclic 2-space factor is itself contractible: if \(h\) satisfies \(J_ih+hJ_i=I\) on that factor, then \(Dh+hD=I\), because operators on the other tensor factors commute with \(h\) and cross terms vanish in characteristic two. The sole noncontractible summand is the tensor product of the \((q_i-2)\)-dimensional zero-differential factors. Hence
\[
\dim\bigl(\ker D/\operatorname{im}D\bigr)=H.
\]
Since \(D^2=0\), \(Q=\operatorname{rank}D+\dim\ker D\) and \(H=\dim\ker D-\operatorname{rank}D\). Therefore
\[
\dim\ker D=\frac{Q+H}{2}.
\]
This calculation remains valid when an even alphabet size equals 2, in which case its zero-differential factor is zero-dimensional and \(H=0\).

**Step 4: Count the surviving odd sectors.** Define the polynomial
\[
F(z)=\prod_{i\in O}((q_i-1)+z).
\]
Its coefficient of \(z^r\) is the total multiplicity of sectors with exactly \(r\) odd-coordinate 1-eigenvalues. Because \(F(1)=P\) and \(F(-1)=\prod_{i\in O}(q_i-2)=R\), the total multiplicity of sectors with \(r\equiv d\pmod2\) is
\[
\frac{P+(-1)^dR}{2}.
\]
Multiplying by the nullity from Step 3 gives the asserted binary nullity.

**Step 5: Orientation-independent append cost.** For a fully oriented underlying graph, the directed adjacency matrix \(B\) obeys \(B+B^{\mathsf T}=A\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) because each underlying edge occurs once, in one direction. Lemma 4.1 of Riera--Parker--Stănică identifies the minimal append parameter as half the binary rank of this symmetric commutator matrix; Lemma 4.2 makes the parameter orientation-independent. Thus \(e(G)=\operatorname{rank}(A)/2\), as claimed. Since \(A\) is alternating over \(\mathbb F_2\), its rank is even, verifying integrality of the resource count.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in the same folder as this document. The standard-library verifier constructs the graph directly from its coordinate-adjacency definition, performs exact \(\mathbb F_2\) Gaussian elimination using integer bitsets, and compares the computed nullity against the displayed closed formula. It checks all ordered tuples with alphabet sizes \(2\) through \(6\), lengths \(1\) through \(5\), and at most \(350\) vertices: 1,222 complete cases, of which 1,034 have mixed parities. These are *finite computational consistency checks*, not proofs for unbounded dimensions or alphabet sizes. The proof is Steps 1–5.

For orientation-independent concrete examples: \(K_2\square K_3\) has 6 vertices, nullity 2, rank 4 and append cost 2; \(K_3\square K_4\) has 12 vertices, nullity 6, rank 6 and append cost 3; \(K_2\square K_2\square K_3\) has 12 vertices, nullity 2, rank 10 and append cost 5; and \(K_2\square K_3\square K_5\) has 30 vertices, nullity 6, rank 24 and append cost 12.

## Relationship to prior work

The quantum framework is due to Riera, Parker and Stănică (2021 early access), who prove the general identity \(e=\tfrac12\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(B+B^{\mathsf T})\). That identity alone does not evaluate rank for heterogeneous Hamming boxes. Fish, Key and Mwambene (2009) already determined the binary rank of **homogeneous** Hamming graphs \(K_q^{\square d}\), for both odd and even \(q\), in their Proposition 1; no novelty is asserted for that homogeneous special case. Fish, Key, Mwambene and Rodrigues (2019) address related Hamming adjacency codes. Fish, Key and Mwambene (2023) study Cartesian product codes and show conditional spectral rank bounds or LCD conditions; the available page-ten excerpt explicitly remarks that Cartesian-product adjacency rank is not directly given by the product construction. The even-order clique factor falls outside the product-RLCD hypothesis because its binary adjacency matrix is invertible while its reflexive matrix has rank one. In contrast, the present theorem evaluates the rank **exactly** for arbitrary mixed-parity coordinate sizes via a nilpotent tensor-complex calculation, and translates it into the sharp quantum append parameter. A direct general modular-rank theorem for arbitrary Cartesian clique products might still subsume this result; this remains a literature risk.

## Limitations

The formula concerns the binary rank of the ordinary adjacency matrix, not the nullity over \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\). The quantum resource statement requires all underlying edges oriented; arbitrary mixtures of directed and retained undirected edges need not have commutator matrix equal to \(A\). The theorem does not optimize alternative state-preparation architectures or robustness to noise. Equal-alphabet Hamming graphs were already classified in the coding literature; the mathematical contribution is the fully heterogeneous mixed-parity case. A complete full-text check of the closely related 2023 Cartesian-product paper was not available; its accessible excerpt and abstract do not settle the precise arbitrary-size mixed-parity formula.
