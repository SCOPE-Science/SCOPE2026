# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Circular factor means a substring of one conjugate with length at most 22 (no repeated winding). The 20/11 threshold is checked by integer inequality 11L>20p for a period p. The prefix property is factorial, so extending all ternary prefixes and rejecting newly introduced high-exponent suffixes exhausts every linearly admissible word. An independently written prefix search, checking every relevant period, found 3042 length-22 threshold-survivors. Independent complete doubled-word scan of every 3042 candidate with starts 0..21 and lengths 1..22 found exactly 132 circular words with CE<=20/11; canonical rotation gave precisely six classes, 22 rotations each. Computing exact least periods for these candidates confirms CE=20/11 and no smaller value; symmetry by alphabet relabeling/reversal gives one orbit. The complete finite search proves the single length 22 domain, not all word lengths. Two distinct circular critical factors have length 20 and least period 11 in the first class.

## originality

PASS

Currie–Mol–Rampersad 2019 proves that 22 is one of the last lengths without a 7/4^+-free circular ternary word; their full-text proof and exceptional-length list do not determine the numerical length-22 minimax CE or all minimizers. Shur proves existence of ternary squarefree circular words and related bounds, implying only a weaker <2 upper bound. The submitted 20/11 and six equality classes resolve a quantitative and combinatorial gap at the exceptional endpoint, not a repackaging of the prior threshold.

## value

PASS

Length 22 is the **last exceptional ternary circular length** in the 7/4^+ threshold theorem, hence singled out mathematically, not chosen arbitrarily. The exact minimax exponent and complete six-class equality census quantify that exception and characterize extremizers, a natural precise fact in repetition avoidance. A finite computation is warranted because the domain is a mathematically motivated natural boundary.
