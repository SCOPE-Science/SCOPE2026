# Exact diameter-code census in the block-transposition metric on \(S_5\)
## Finding
For the block-transposition metric on \(S_5\), the maximum size of a code with minimum distance at least \(3\) is \(4\). There are exactly \(540\) labeled maximum codes. Because the diameter is \(3\), every maximum code is equidistant: every two distinct codewords are at distance exactly \(3\).

Under the full \(1440\)-element isometry group of the block-transposition Cayley graph, the \(540\) maxima split into exactly two orbits of sizes \(180\) and \(360\), with stabilizer orders \(8\) and \(4\). Orbit representatives in one-line notation are
\[
\{12345,15432,52413,53142\}
\]
and
\[
\{12345,21543,35241,53142\}.
\]
Exactly \(4\) of the \(18\) maxima containing the identity are subgroups; each is cyclic of order \(4\). Their \(30\) right cosets per subgroup are pairwise disjoint as families of codes, giving exactly \(120\) coset maxima. All \(120\) lie in the \(360\)-element isometry orbit, so the other \(420\) maximum codes are not right cosets of these cyclic subgroup maxima.

## Assumptions and scope
A block transposition is specified by integers \(0\le i<j<k\le5\). On a one-line permutation \(\pi\), it exchanges the adjacent position blocks \(\pi[i:j]\) and \(\pi[j:k]\), leaving the remaining positions fixed. The distance \(d_{\mathrm{BT}}(\pi,\rho)\) is the shortest-path distance in the Cayley graph generated by all \(20\) such moves. A code is any subset \(C\subseteq S_5\), with no linearity or subgroup assumption.

The claim is finite and exact for \(S_5\) and minimum distance \(3\) only. No formula for larger symmetric groups is asserted. The phrase “full isometry group” uses the published fact that the full automorphism group of the \(S_5\) block-transposition Cayley graph has order \(1440\), together with a direct construction and verification here of \(1440\) distinct distance-preserving maps.

## Proof
The verifier constructs all \(120\) permutations and all \(20\) block-transposition generators directly from the definition. Breadth-first search from the identity gives the exact distance distribution
\[
1,20,68,31
\]
at distances \(0,1,2,3\), respectively. Thus the diameter is \(3\), agreeing with the published small-order distance table.

Form a compatibility graph on the \(120\) permutations, joining two vertices exactly when their block-transposition distance is at least \(3\). It has \(1860\) edges and is \(31\)-regular. A code of minimum distance at least \(3\) is exactly a clique in this graph.

An exhaustive Bron--Kerbosch enumeration finds exactly \(60\) maximal cliques of size \(2\) and exactly \(540\) maximal cliques of size \(4\), with no maximal clique of any other size. Hence the maximum code size is \(4\) and the labeled maximum-code count is \(540\). A separate increasing-order enumeration of all four-cliques independently returns the same \(540\) sets.

For symmetry, the verifier implements the six toric maps and the reverse map using the standard formulas for block transpositions. They generate \(12\) distance-preserving maps fixing the identity. Composing these with all \(120\) right translations gives \(1440\) distinct distance-preserving maps. The published automorphism-group order is also \(1440\), so these maps exhaust the full isometry group. Direct orbit enumeration of the \(540\) maxima yields orbit sizes \(180\) and \(360\).

Finally, among the \(18\) maxima containing the identity, direct closure tests find exactly \(4\) subgroups. Their element-order multisets are all \(\{1,2,4,4\}\), so each is cyclic of order \(4\). Enumerating all right cosets gives \(30\) distinct maximum codes from each subgroup; the four coset families are pairwise disjoint, giving \(120\) in total. Orbit membership is checked directly.

## Relationship to prior work
Korchmaros introduced and analyzed the Cayley graph generated by block transpositions and described its automorphism structure. The later thesis gives the small-order distance table; for \(n=5\) it records the distance distribution \(1,20,68,31\) and states that the full automorphism group has order \(1440\). Those facts determine the ambient metric and its full symmetry group, but they do not determine the clique number of the distance-three compatibility graph, the number of maximum codes, their two full-isometry orbits, or the cyclic-coset decomposition above.

Full-text checks of the thesis found no occurrences of “code”, “packing”, “independent set”, or “maximum clique”. Searches for the same parameter under block-transposition-code, diameter-packing, and independence-graph formulations did not locate a source whose statement implies the \(540\)-code census or its orbit/coset classification. A later paper on a “block permutation metric” concerns a different metric and therefore does not cover this claim.

## Limitations
The exhaustive computation proves only the finite \(S_5\) statement. It does not extrapolate the maximum, census, or orbit structure to \(S_n\) for \(n>5\). The orbit claim depends on the published full automorphism-group order \(1440\); the verifier independently constructs and checks \(1440\) distance-preserving maps but does not re-prove the external theorem that no further graph automorphisms exist. An obscure or unindexed prior finite census remains a residual originality risk.
