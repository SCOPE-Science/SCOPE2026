# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces every terminal coloring to an exact domination criterion and then supplies explicit parity-controlled strategies for both players in every case. The finite minimax replay agrees with the formulas on all tested profiles, but the analytic strategies, not the experiment, establish the universal claim.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating source gives exact values for complete and complete-bipartite graphs, while later same-invariant papers give general bounds and monotonicity results. Targeted searches across complete-split, join, complete-multipartite, and universal-vertex formulations found no arbitrary-\(u\) exact theorem. A prior owned result for \(K_{1,1,m}\) is a strict special case; it does not imply the present two-parameter classification.

## Value
PASS. The theorem closes the noncomplete complete-split family and turns the previously isolated \(u=2\) parity reversal into a full law: odd \(u\) gives equality of the two game numbers, while even \(u\) gives a one-unit gap whose direction flips with \(m\)-parity. This is a natural structural classification, not a routine small-parameter extension.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest exact results are the complete-graph and complete-bipartite formulas of Hartnell--Rall. English--Swan supplies general bounds and starting-player comparisons, and the 2026 deletion/palette paper supplies monotonicity facts; none determines the exact complete-split values. The theorem does not address complete multipartite graphs with two or more non-singleton parts, and poorly indexed equivalent literature remains a residual originality risk.
