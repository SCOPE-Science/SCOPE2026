# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. For adjacent \(x,y\), common neighbors are exactly the nonzero vectors in \(\langle x,y\rangle^\perp\), after removing whichever endpoints are themselves isotropic. Nondegeneracy gives the orthogonal-complement dimension and therefore the exact formula. The dimension-three impossibility of independent orthogonal isotropic endpoints follows from a two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace being forced inside a one-dimensional orthogonal complement. The edge-class and triangle counts then follow from exact degree sums. Representative graphs over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_4\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_7\) were exhaustively reconstructed and matched the formulas.

## Originality

PASS. The founding paper proves diameter and girth, the 2016 full finite-field treatment proves vertex degrees, regularity behavior, isomorphism rigidity, domination, clique and independence results, and the 2020 sequel gives dimension-two component decompositions. Full-text inspection of the strongest direct sources did not locate an all-dimensional common-neighbor formula or the edge-triangle defect classification. Searches also used the orthogonality and polarity aliases. The rank-three projective quotient is classical and is treated as prior context, not as the originality claim. The principal residual risk is terminology mismatch elsewhere in finite-geometry literature.

## Value

PASS. Girth three says only that at least one triangle exists. The accepted theorem determines the number of triangles through every edge from intrinsic linear-algebraic data, proves that edge triangle multiplicity is always nonconstant, and isolates \(TD(\mathbb F_2,3)\) as the only dimension-at-least-three case containing edges that lie in no triangle. The exact dimension-three triangle count is a natural global corollary. These are structural local invariants, not a parameter substitution or recomputation of a known table.

Closest literature: Badawi (2015), Mollahajiaghaei (2016), Abdulla--Badawi (2020), Saleh--Megahed (2020), together with orthogonal-polarity literature for the rank-three projective quotient. The theorem does not cover general finite coefficient rings or arbitrary bilinear forms.
