# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces every possible forcing trajectory to local white counts in the blades and the color of the center. The center-black and center-white cases are both necessary and sufficient, including the boundary \(\ell=m\) and the maximum threshold \(\ell=rm\). The polynomial and minimum formulas are counts of those exact cases. An independent exhaustive program checks all subsets for \(81\) parameter triples with \(2\le r,m\le4\) and \(1\le\ell\le rm\), agreeing with the theorem.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational \(k\)-forcing paper supplies the same rule, general bounds, and some special-family results but no windmill/friendship theorem in its searchable full text. Cut-vertex zero-forcing work can cover scalar information at \(\ell=1\), and a recent propagation-number paper can overlap singleton thresholds, but neither accessible statement determines every \(\ell\)-forcing set. The closest published-result record for exact \(k\)-forcing sets concerns complete multipartite graphs, which does not contain the generalized windmill family for \(r,m\ge2\). A same-family generalized-windmill record concerns a different invariant.

Residual risk remains from inaccessible or poorly indexed literature, and no novelty claim is made for the scalar friendship zero-forcing specialization.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a complete propagation profile on a natural graph family rather than a single minimum. It exposes sharp structural transitions when \(\ell\) crosses \(m\) and successive multiples of \(m\), and it supplies a closed cardinality enumerator and exact minimum-set counts. These formulas provide a compact benchmark for \(k\)-forcing on clique bouquets and articulation-driven propagation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main comparison is D. Amos, Y. Caro, R. Davila, and R. Pepper, “Upper bounds on the \(k\)-forcing number of a graph,” arXiv:1401.6206v1 / Discrete Applied Mathematics 181 (2015). D. D. Row's 2012 cut-vertex technique is relevant to the ordinary zero-forcing specialization. V. L. Banot and M. Baldado Jr. (2026) study singleton propagation thresholds for several graph operations. The present theorem is limited to equal-sized clique blades and does not treat unequal windmills or other forcing variants.
