# Exact finite covariance between Greenwood and Moran spacing tests
## Finding
Let
\[
(D_1,\ldots,D_m)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad m\ge2,
\]
the joint law of the \(m\) ordinary uniform spacings generated by \(m-1\) iid points on \((0,1)\). Define Moran's log-spacing statistic
\[
M_m=-\sum_{i=1}^m\log(mD_i)
\]
and, for every real \(a>0\), the power-spacing sum
\[
P_{m,a}=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i^a.
\]
Write
\[
A_{m,a}=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(a+1)}{\Gamma(m+a)}.
\]
Then the exact finite-sample covariance is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(P_{m,a},M_m)
=
mA_{m,a}
\left\{
m[\psi(m+a)-\psi(m)]
-[\psi(a+1)-\psi(1)]
\right\}.
}
\]
Here \(\psi\) is the digamma function.

For Greenwood's statistic
\[
G_m=P_{m,2}=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i^2,
\]
the formula collapses to the elementary expression
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,M_m)
=
\frac{m-1}{(m+1)^2}.
}
\]
Thus the two classical spacing tests are positively coupled under the exact null for every finite \(m\ge2\).

Their marginal variances are
\[
\operatorname{Var}(G_m)
=
\frac{4(m-1)}{(m+1)^2(m+2)(m+3)}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(M_m)
=
m\frac{\pi^2}{6}-m^2\psi_1(m),
\]
where \(\psi_1\) is the trigamma function. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,M_m)^2
=
\frac{(m-1)(m+2)(m+3)}
{4(m+1)^2\left[m\pi^2/6-m^2\psi_1(m)\right]}.
}
\]
For example,
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(G_2,M_2)=0.8844926873\ldots,
\qquad
\operatorname{Corr}(G_{10},M_{10})=0.6992531303\ldots.
\]
The finite dependence is therefore substantially stronger at small and moderate sample sizes than its limiting value.

Using
\[
\psi_1(m)=\frac1m+\frac1{2m^2}+O(m^{-3}),
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,M_m)
\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi^2/6-1}}
=0.6226046291\ldots
}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,M_m)^2
\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{4(\pi^2/6-1)}
=0.3876365241\ldots.
}
\]
The final constant is exactly the classical first-order Greenwood/log-spacings asymptotic squared-correlation constant reported in the spacing-test efficiency literature. The formulas above supply its finite-sample origin and extend the covariance calculation to the complete power family \(P_{m,a}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The parent distribution is continuous and the null is transformed to uniformity, so the ordinary spacings have the symmetric Dirichlet law displayed above.

The Moran statistic is written with \(mD_i\) inside the logarithm. Removing the factor \(m\) changes only an additive constant and therefore leaves all covariance and variance formulas unchanged.

The general power formula assumes \(a>0\). The same Dirichlet differentiation argument extends to \(a>-1\) whenever the required logarithmic moment is finite, but that larger parameter range is not needed for the goodness-of-fit interpretation here.

The result concerns joint second moments. Positive covariance does not imply that Greenwood and Moran statistics are functions of one another or jointly Gaussian at finite \(m\).

## Proof
For a symmetric Dirichlet vector with unit parameters,
\[
\mathbb E D_i^a
=
\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(a+1)}{\Gamma(m+a)}
=A_{m,a}.
\]
Differentiating the corresponding Dirichlet moment with respect to the exponent of the same coordinate gives
\[
\mathbb E[D_i^a\log D_i]
=
A_{m,a}[\psi(a+1)-\psi(m+a)].
\]
For \(j\ne i\), differentiating with respect to the Dirichlet exponent of the other coordinate gives
\[
\mathbb E[D_i^a\log D_j]
=
A_{m,a}[\psi(1)-\psi(m+a)].
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\left[P_{m,a}\sum_{j=1}^m\log D_j\right]
&=
mA_{m,a}
\left[
\psi(a+1)+(m-1)\psi(1)-m\psi(m+a)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Also
\[
\mathbb EP_{m,a}=mA_{m,a}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^m\log D_j
=
m[\psi(1)-\psi(m)].
\]
Subtracting the product of the means and changing sign because
\[
M_m=-m\log m-\sum_j\log D_j
\]
proves
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(P_{m,a},M_m)
=
mA_{m,a}
\left\{
m[\psi(m+a)-\psi(m)]
-[\psi(a+1)-\psi(1)]
\right\}.
\]

At \(a=2\),
\[
A_{m,2}=\frac{2}{m(m+1)},
\]
while the digamma recurrence gives
\[
\psi(m+2)-\psi(m)=\frac1m+\frac1{m+1}
\]
and
\[
\psi(3)-\psi(1)=\frac32.
\]
Substitution yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,M_m)=\frac{m-1}{(m+1)^2}.
\]

For the Greenwood variance, the Dirichlet fourth moments give
\[
\mathbb EG_m^2
=
\frac{4(m+5)}{(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)},
\]
and
\[
\mathbb EG_m=\frac2{m+1},
\]
which simplifies to the stated variance.

For Moran's statistic, standard Dirichlet log-moments give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\log D_i)=\psi_1(1)-\psi_1(m),
\]
and for \(i\ne j\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\log D_i,\log D_j)=-\psi_1(m).
\]
Summing all diagonal and off-diagonal terms yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}(M_m)
=
m\psi_1(1)-m^2\psi_1(m)
=
m\frac{\pi^2}{6}-m^2\psi_1(m).
\]
Combining the exact covariance with the two variances proves the finite correlation formula.

Finally, the standard trigamma expansion gives
\[
m\frac{\pi^2}{6}-m^2\psi_1(m)
=
m\left(\frac{\pi^2}{6}-1\right)+O(1),
\]
which yields the displayed limiting correlation.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` performs two supplementary checks that do not replace the analytic proof.

First, for \(m=2\) and \(m=3\), it directly integrates the Dirichlet density at several noninteger and integer values of \(a\) and compares the resulting covariance with the gamma-digamma formula. These low-dimensional integrations do not use the moment differentiation identities from the proof.

Second, it checks the Greenwood specialization, both marginal variances, the closed correlation formula, and convergence to the classical limiting squared correlation over thousands of finite values of \(m\).

The general theorem for all \(m\ge2\) and \(a>0\) is established by the Dirichlet moment calculation above.

## Relationship to prior work
The spacing-test literature treats Greenwood's quadratic statistic and the log-spacings statistic as canonical but differently optimal goodness-of-fit procedures. Mirakhmedov's complete 2010 article states that the asymptotic quality of a spacing test is governed by its asymptotic correlation with Greenwood's statistic. For first-order log-spacings, the article gives the squared asymptotic correlation numerically as \(0.3876\), matching exactly the limit
\[
\frac{1}{4(\pi^2/6-1)}
\]
obtained above.

That article works at the asymptotic-correlation level: its displayed relation between a general spacing statistic and Greenwood is explicitly an asymptotic equality. The finite covariance
\[
\frac{m-1}{(m+1)^2}
\]
and the exact finite correlation formula above are not stated in the inspected text.

Penev and Ruderman subsequently emphasize that sample size and centering matter for moderate-sample spacing tests and explicitly contrast Moran and Greenwood behavior with asymptotic conclusions. Their study motivates a finite-sample comparison but does not state the joint null covariance derived here.

The present result therefore sharpens a known asymptotic relationship rather than replacing it: the classical efficiency constant is recovered as the limit of an explicit finite correlation, and the same calculation supplies an exact covariance with every power-spacing sum.

## Limitations
The formulas use the ordinary symmetric uniform-spacing law. Higher-order or overlapping spacings have different dependence structures and are not covered.

The power-family covariance identity is elementary once the relevant Dirichlet logarithmic derivatives are written down. An equivalent finite formula may occur in older spacings or Dirichlet-moment literature under different notation; the inspected highly relevant sources emphasize asymptotic efficiency and marginal approximations rather than this exact joint law.

No finite-sample independence is claimed, and the covariance alone does not determine comparative power against alternatives.
