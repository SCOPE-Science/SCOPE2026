# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The same-coordinate and cross-coordinate logarithmic Dirichlet moments follow by differentiating the exact beta-function moment. Summing them gives the power/Moran covariance for every \(m\ge2\) and \(a>0\). The Greenwood specialization uses only digamma recurrences, while the two marginal variances follow independently from fourth Dirichlet moments and the Dirichlet log-covariance matrix. Direct low-dimensional integration and wide finite replay confirm the normalizations and algebra.

## Originality
PASS, with a specific older-literature risk. The closest complete primary source makes asymptotic correlation with Greenwood the central efficiency quantity and explicitly reports squared asymptotic correlation \(0.3876\) for first-order log-spacings. Its displayed relation is asymptotic and the inspected text does not state the finite covariance \((m-1)/(m+1)^2\) or the finite correlation formula.

A moderate-sample comparison paper directly contrasts Moran and Greenwood and motivates finite-sample analysis but does not expose the joint null moment in the accessible material. Exact-form and semantic searches did not locate the finite identity. The asymptotic constant itself is treated as prior art, not as part of the originality claim.

## Value
PASS. Greenwood and Moran are canonical competitors in spacing-based goodness-of-fit testing, and the literature explicitly warns that moderate-sample behavior can differ from asymptotic conclusions. The exact law quantifies their finite null redundancy and explains the classical asymptotic efficiency constant as a limit. The power-family extension gives the result structural reuse beyond the single Greenwood exponent.

## Closest literature and limitations
The strongest comparison is the complete 2010 Greenwood efficiency paper, which gives an asymptotic correlation relation and the first-order log-spacings squared limit \(0.3876\). The new contribution is the exact finite covariance/correlation and its power-spacing extension. The formulas are restricted to ordinary symmetric uniform spacings and do not cover higher-order or overlapping spacings.
