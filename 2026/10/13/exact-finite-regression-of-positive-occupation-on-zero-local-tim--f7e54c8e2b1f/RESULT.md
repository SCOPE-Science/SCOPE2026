# Exact finite regression of positive occupation on zero local time
## Finding
Let
\[
S_0=0,\qquad S_j=\varepsilon_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_j,
\]
where the increments are independent and satisfy
\[
\mathbb P(\varepsilon_j=1)=\mathbb P(\varepsilon_j=-1)=\frac12.
\]
Fix \(n\ge1\), and define the zero local time, excluding the initial time, by
\[
Z_{2n}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\mathbf 1\{S_j=0\}.
\]
Define the strict positive and negative occupation times by
\[
A_{2n}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\mathbf 1\{S_j>0\},
\qquad
B_{2n}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\mathbf 1\{S_j<0\}.
\]
Finally let
\[
\mathcal R_{2n}
=
\sigma(|S_0|,\ldots,|S_{2n}|)
\]
be the sigma-field generated by the reflected path.

Then the positive occupation time has the exact finite conditional regression
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[A_{2n}\mid\mathcal R_{2n}]
=
n-\frac12 Z_{2n}.
}
\]
Since \(Z_{2n}\) is measurable with respect to \(\mathcal R_{2n}\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[A_{2n}\mid Z_{2n}]
=
n-\frac12 Z_{2n}.
}
\]
Thus the least-squares regression is not merely linear: the full conditional mean is exactly affine for every finite horizon.

Put
\[
p_n=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}
\]
and
\[
\mu_n=(2n+1)p_n-1.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E Z_{2n}=\mu_n
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2n})
=
2n-3\mu_n-\mu_n^2.
}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{2n},A_{2n})
=
-\frac12\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2n})
=
-\frac12\left(2n-3\mu_n-\mu_n^2\right).
}
\]
The covariance is strictly negative for every \(n\ge1\).

Using the central-binomial asymptotic,
\[
p_n
=
\frac1{\sqrt{\pi n}}
\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right),
\]
one obtains
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{2n},A_{2n})
\sim
-\left(1-\frac2\pi\right)n.
}
\]

Because
\[
A_{2n}+B_{2n}+Z_{2n}=2n,
\]
the same regression identity is equivalently
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(
Z_{2n},
A_{2n}-B_{2n}
\right)=0.
}
\]
Thus the entire finite covariance between zero local time and one-sided occupation comes from the deterministic loss of nonzero time slots when the walk visits zero, not from a covariance with the signed occupation imbalance.

## Assumptions and scope
The walk is the one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk started at zero and observed at the even horizon \(2n\).

The local time used here is the number of visits to zero at positive integer times. The occupation time is strict: visits to zero count in neither \(A_{2n}\) nor \(B_{2n}\).

The theorem is an exact finite second-moment and conditional-mean statement. It does not assert independence between local time and signed occupation, nor between the reflected path and excursion signs as random elements beyond the explicit conditional sign symmetry used in the proof.

## Proof
Condition on the complete reflected path
\[
(|S_j|)_{0\le j\le2n}.
\]
Its nonzero portions decompose into excursions away from zero, with the last excursion possibly unfinished at time \(2n\).

For any fixed reflected path, independently reflecting any one excursion across zero produces another simple-walk path with the same reflected path and the same probability. Therefore, conditional on the reflected path, each excursion sign is symmetric. In particular, at every time \(j\) for which
\[
|S_j|>0,
\]
the conditional probability that \(S_j>0\) is \(1/2\).

Exactly
\[
2n-Z_{2n}
\]
of the positive times \(1,\ldots,2n\) are nonzero. Hence
\[
\mathbb E[A_{2n}\mid\mathcal R_{2n}]
=
\frac12(2n-Z_{2n})
=
n-\frac12Z_{2n}.
\]
Taking conditional expectation once more with respect to \(Z_{2n}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E[A_{2n}\mid Z_{2n}]
=
n-\frac12Z_{2n}.
\]
The covariance identity
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{2n},A_{2n})
=
-\frac12\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2n})
\]
follows immediately.

It remains to compute the local-time variance. Let
\[
a_k=\mathbb P(S_{2k}=0)
=
\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{4^k}.
\]
Since the walk cannot be at zero at odd times,
\[
\mathbb E Z_{2n}
=
\sum_{k=1}^n a_k.
\]
The elementary telescoping identity
\[
(2k+1)a_k-(2k-1)a_{k-1}=a_k
\]
gives
\[
\sum_{k=0}^n a_k=(2n+1)a_n,
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathbb E Z_{2n}
=
(2n+1)a_n-1
=
\mu_n.
\]

For \(1\le i<j\le n\), the Markov property yields
\[
\mathbb P(S_{2i}=0,S_{2j}=0)
=
a_i a_{j-i}.
\]
The generating function
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}a_kz^k=(1-z)^{-1/2}
\]
implies the convolution identity
\[
\sum_{i=0}^j a_i a_{j-i}=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[Z_{2n}(Z_{2n}-1)]
=
2\sum_{j=2}^n\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}a_i a_{j-i}
=
2\sum_{j=2}^n(1-2a_j)
=
2n-4\mu_n.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E Z_{2n}^2
=
2n-3\mu_n,
\]
which proves
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2n})
=
2n-3\mu_n-\mu_n^2.
\]

Finally,
\[
a_n
=
\frac1{\sqrt{\pi n}}
\left(1+O(n^{-1})\right)
\]
implies
\[
\mu_n
=
2\sqrt{\frac n\pi}-1+O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
and thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{2n})
=
2\left(1-\frac2\pi\right)n+O(\sqrt n).
\]
Multiplication by \(-1/2\) gives the stated covariance asymptotic.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` exhaustively enumerates every sign path through \(2n\) steps for \(1\le n\le8\). It verifies the exact local-time mean, variance, covariance with positive occupation, and the conditional mean given each realized value of zero local time.

It also groups paths by their entire reflected trajectory and verifies
\[
\mathbb E[A_{2n}\mid\mathcal R_{2n}]
=
n-\frac12Z_{2n}
\]
directly on every reflected-path fiber.

A separate exact-rational loop checks the telescoping and convolution identities and the resulting variance formula through \(n=2000\). These computations supplement, rather than replace, the all-\(n\) proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Classical random-walk fluctuation theory treats occupation time, local time, maxima, and last-zero variables as closely related path functionals.

Mishchenko gives a complete finite-path treatment of several such functionals for the simple symmetric random walk. In particular, the paper defines the strict positive occupation time and a downcrossing-based discrete analogue of local time, and proves a path-transform equality of conditional joint distributions involving occupation time, that downcrossing count, the endpoint, the maximum, and the argmax. The paper does not use the visit-count local time \(Z_{2n}\) in the finite theorem and does not state the conditional regression or covariance above.

Csáki, Csörgő, Földes, and Révész later define local time at zero exactly as the number of zero visits and define leg occupation times for random walks on a spider. Their two-leg symmetric model specializes to the simple symmetric random walk on the line. Their article develops strong approximations and limit theorems for these local and occupation times; it does not state a finite covariance or conditional regression between the zero-visit count and one-sided occupation.

The present identity is therefore a finite excursion-sign complement to those distributional and asymptotic results.

## Limitations
The exact slope \(-1/2\) uses symmetric excursion signs. It changes for skew random walks or asymmetric increments.

The result uses strict positive occupation. Alternative conventions that assign zero times to the preceding or following sign lead to different finite formulas.

The excursion-sign proof is elementary, so an equivalent identity may occur in older fluctuation-theory literature under a different occupation-time convention. That remains the principal historical originality risk.
