# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the normalized order statistic to a linear form in a \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)\) vector by the independent exponential spacing representation. The sample total is independent of those normalized proportions, giving the independence statement. Exact Dirichlet first and second moments give the finite formulas, and ordinary Riemann sums give the asymptotics. The median monotonicity step is an exact one-line rational identity.

## Originality
PASS. The closest complete primary paper, Lin–Wu–Ahmad (1980), treats the same quantile-to-mean ratio asymptotically but does not give the finite exponential formulas. Scannell et al. (2000) already plot the expected exponential median-to-mean ratio for small samples and state the \(\log2\) limit, so those plotted expectation values are treated as prior coverage. The surviving claim is the exact all-\(k\) finite moment theorem, its exact variance, independence from the sample mean, and the all-odd-\(n\) harmonic form.

## Value
PASS. Quantile-to-mean ratios are established scale-free skew diagnostics, and a directly relevant applied paper uses the median/mean version under an exponential model. Exact finite-sample mean and variance formulas give a rigorous calibration where prior literature is asymptotic or graphical, with a transparent small-sample bias correction.

## Closest literature and limitations
The strongest coverage risk is the 1980 asymptotic quantile/mean-ratio article; it was inspected completely and remains asymptotic. The 2000 exponential application partially covers the median expectation through a plotted finite-sample curve. Classical spacing and gamma–Dirichlet facts make the proof short, and an unindexed historical formula remains possible. No claim is made beyond iid exponential samples or ordinary order statistics.
