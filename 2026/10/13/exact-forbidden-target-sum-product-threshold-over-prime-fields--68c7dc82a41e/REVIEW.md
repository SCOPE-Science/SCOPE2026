# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For p>=5 prime and prescribed a!=0 in F_p, an admissible subset is an independent set in the graph whose sum involution is s(x)=a-x and product involution is i(x)=a/x on nonzero x, including loops at a/2 and square roots of a. The sum matching alone gives (p-1)/2 upper bound. Each nondegenerate component is an alternating path or even cycle, plus collapsed double-colored edges; saturation fails by exactly one only if two distinct product-loop roots are endpoints in the same path. With a=u²!=4, f=s∘i has projective matrix [[a,-a],[1,0]], is semisimple and u is not an eigenline. The f-orbit of u has length its PGL2 order m, and the two roots u,-u lie together iff m is even by the involution-reversal symmetry. At a=4 the matrix is nontrivial unipotent of odd projective order p, so no defect. Hence M_p(a)=(p-1)/2 - 1{a quadratic residue and m even}. Independently computed PGL2 matrix powers and brute-force enumerated ALL subsets for all nonzero a over p=5,7,11,13 (32 complete prime-target tests), matching the formula. The infinite-prime conclusion relies on the component and group-action proof, not those finite examples.

## originality

PASS

Tang (arXiv 2603.29992, 2026) proves a sharp (p-1)/2 construction avoiding only target 1 and classifies its two-involution graph using the special six-cycle anharmonic group generated by x↦1-x and x↦1/x. For arbitrary a the forbidden sum maps by λ but forbidden product by λ², so a cannot generally be normalized simultaneously to 1. The PGL2 order criterion varies with a (e.g. M_7(1)=3 but M_7(2)=2), genuinely extending rather than restating that theorem. Exact graph independent-set extremum for all nonzero a was not identified by Resultary or finite-field literature comparisons. Tang’s existing general |A|>p/2 bound is weaker than the exact target-specific one-unit defect.

## value

PASS

The exact maximal density for avoiding a fixed nonzero common sum/product target is a natural refinement of Tang’s counterexample to a named finite-field conjecture. The one-unit drop is controlled by nontrivial PGL2 orbit parity, and it explicitly shows why the target-1 construction cannot be uniformized to all a. A sharp complete formula is valuable independently of any general sum-product theorem.
