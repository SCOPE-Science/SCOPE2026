# Exact free-algebra growth for Olshanskii's four-element prime ring
## Finding
Let \(A\) be the four-element nonassociative ring over \(\mathbb F_2\) with basis \(e,f\) and multiplication
\[
e^2=f,\qquad ef=f,\qquad fe=0,\qquad f^2=f.
\]
For every integer \(n\ge 1\), let \(F_n\) be the \(n\)-generated free ring in the variety \(\operatorname{var}(A)\). Then
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}F_n=4^n+n-1,
\qquad
|F_n|=2^{4^n+n-1}.
\]
Equivalently, writing an input as \(x_i=(a_i,b_i)\in\mathbb F_2^2\), the \(f\)-coordinates generated by product terms are exactly all Boolean functions on \(A^n\) that vanish at the all-zero input, while the \(e\)-coordinate of an arbitrary term is an arbitrary \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear form in \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\). In particular,
\[
\operatorname{pt}(A)=4.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The ring language is \((+, -, 0, \cdot)\), with no multiplicative identity symbol. Since \(A\) has characteristic \(2\), every algebra in \(\operatorname{var}(A)\) is naturally an \(\mathbb F_2\)-vector space under addition. The statement concerns the specific four-element specialization of the two-dimensional nonsimple prime algebra exhibited by Olshanskii; it does not claim an exact formula for arbitrary finite prime rings.

The free algebra \(F_n\) is identified with the algebra of \(n\)-ary term functions on \(A\): two terms define the same element of \(F_n\) exactly when they agree on every point of \(A^n\).

## Proof
Write \(x=(a,b)\) and \(y=(c,d)\) relative to the basis \(e,f\). The multiplication table gives
\[
xy=(0,ac+ad+bd).
\]
Thus every product has zero \(e\)-coordinate. It follows inductively that the \(e\)-coordinate of any term function is a linear form \(\sum_i \lambda_i a_i\), and every such linear form occurs from the corresponding sum of variables. These forms span an \(n\)-dimensional space.

Let \(R\) be the Boolean function algebra on \(A^n\), equivalently
\[
R=\mathbb F_2[a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_n,b_n]/(a_i^2-a_i,b_i^2-b_i:1\le i\le n).
\]
Let \(W\subseteq R\) be the set of \(f\)-coordinates of finite sums of product terms. For each \(i,j\), the product \(x_ix_j\) contributes
\[
q_{ij}=a_i a_j+a_i b_j+b_i b_j.
\]
If \((0,P)\) is represented by a term with \(P\in W\), then
\[
(0,P)x_i=(0,Pb_i),
\qquad
x_i(0,P)=(0,(a_i+b_i)P).
\]
Adding these two term functions gives \((0,a_iP)\). Hence \(W\) is closed under multiplication by every coordinate function \(a_i\) and \(b_i\), so \(W\) is an ideal of \(R\) containing every \(q_{ij}\).

In particular,
\[
q_{ii}=a_i+a_i b_i+b_i.
\]
As a function on \(\mathbb F_2^2\), \(q_{ii}\) is zero precisely at \((0,0)\) and is one at the other three points. Therefore the common zero set of \(q_{11},\ldots,q_{nn}\) is only the all-zero point of \(A^n\). Since \(R\) is the full function algebra \(\mathbb F_2^{A^n}\), the ideal generated by these \(q_{ii}\) is exactly the set of all functions that vanish at that one point. Its dimension is \(4^n-1\). Thus \(W\) contains every such function.

Conversely, every product term vanishes at the all-zero input, so every element of \(W\) does too. Hence \(W\) is exactly the codimension-one ideal of functions vanishing at the origin and
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}W=4^n-1.
\]

Every ring term is a sum of variables and product subterms, so its term function lies in the sum of the \(n\)-dimensional variable span and the \(f\)-only space \(W\). Their intersection is zero: if \(\sum_i\lambda_i x_i\) has zero \(e\)-coordinate, then \(\sum_i\lambda_i a_i\) is the zero function, forcing every \(\lambda_i=0\). Therefore
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}F_n=n+(4^n-1)=4^n+n-1,
\]
and the order formula follows. Finally,
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\log |F_n|)^{1/n}=4,
\]
so the potential is \(4\), independently of the logarithm base.

## Verification
The symbolic proof above establishes the formula for every \(n\ge1\). The bundled script `CHECKER.py` separately constructs the term-function algebra by finite closure for \(n=1,2,3\). It obtains dimensions \(4,17,66\), exactly matching \(4^n+n-1\), and checks that \(a+ab+b\) vanishes only at \((0,0)\). The script ends with `CHECK_OK`.

This finite computation is a regression check only; it is not used to infer the infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Olshanskii proves that a finite nonassociative ring has maximal potential exactly when it is prime, gives two-sided bounds of the form \(c_1|A|^n<\log|F_n|<c_2|A|^n\), and exhibits the multiplication table above as a nonsimple prime algebra. The inspected paper does not state an exact formula for \(|F_n|\) for this example.

Bekbaev's 2026 classification and Verhulst's finite-field enumeration address isomorphism classes of two-dimensional algebras, including characteristic two. They do not compute relatively free algebras, term-function spaces, or identity-growth functions for this multiplication table. Searches for the exact formula, its dimension form, and the multiplication-table aliases found no prior statement among the checked sources. The closest retrieved exact free-algebra result concerns the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra over finite fields, a different signature and multiplication, and does not imply this formula.

## Limitations
The argument is specific to the displayed four-element algebra. It does not classify exact growth for all two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-algebras or all finite prime nonassociative rings. A residual originality risk remains that older clone, polynomial-function, or identity-theory literature could encode the same term-function algebra under a different presentation not exposed by the searches performed.
