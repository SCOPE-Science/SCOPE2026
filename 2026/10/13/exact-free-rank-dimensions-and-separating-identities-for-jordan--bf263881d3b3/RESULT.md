# Exact free-rank dimensions and separating identities for Jordan-extended affine Lie algebras

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, \(s\ge 2\), and \(J_s=I_s+N_s\), where \(N_s\) is a single nilpotent Jordan block of length \(s\). Define the metabelian Lie algebra
\[
\mathfrak g_s=kt\ltimes k^s,\qquad [t,v]=J_sv,\qquad [k^s,k^s]=0.
\]
For every integer \(n\ge1\), the relatively free Lie algebra \(F_n^{(s)}\) on \(n\) generators in the \(k\)-Lie variety generated by \(\mathfrak g_s\) has
\[
\boxed{\dim_k F_n^{(s)}=n+s(n-1)(q^n-1).}
\]
Its derived ideal has the exact operator-valued description given below. Moreover, the varieties form a strictly ascending chain
\[
\operatorname{var}_k(\mathfrak g_1)\subsetneq
\operatorname{var}_k(\mathfrak g_2)\subsetneq
\operatorname{var}_k(\mathfrak g_3)\subsetneq\cdots,
\]
where \(\mathfrak g_1\) is the familiar two-dimensional nonabelian affine Lie algebra. These varieties nevertheless have the same free-dimension exponential growth rate:
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\dim_k F_n^{(s)}\bigr)^{1/n}=q.
\]
A concrete identity distinguishes successive varieties: for \(D_x=\operatorname{ad}x\),
\[
\bigl(D_x^q-D_x\bigr)^s[y,z]=0
\]
is an identity of \(\mathfrak g_s\), but not of \(\mathfrak g_{s+1}\). Here the powers denote repeated composition of adjoint operators acting on the commutator \([y,z]\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field has \(q=p^e\) elements, for any prime \(p\) and \(e\ge1\), with no restriction on \(s\) relative to \(q\). All Lie algebras are over \(k\), including the characteristic-two alternating bracket convention. Write \(V_s=k^s\) with a basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) and let \(N_s e_1=0\), \(N_s e_j=e_{j-1}\) for \(j>1\). Thus \(J_s\) is invertible and its minimal polynomial is \((T-1)^s\).

The relatively free algebra means the quotient of the free \(k\)-Lie algebra on \(n\) formal generators by all polynomial identities of \(\mathfrak g_s\). For comparison, \(s=1\) recovers the previously established dimension formula for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra; the new statement and strictness claim concern nontrivial Jordan lengths \(s\ge2\).

## Proof
**1. Term-function realization.** For a finite algebra, a relatively free algebra is the algebra of term functions on the corresponding Cartesian power, generated by the coordinate projections. Write each input to \((\mathfrak g_s)^n\) as \(x_i=a_i t+u_i\), with \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in k^n\) and \(u_i\in V_s\). Brackets satisfy
\[
[x_i,x_j]=J_s(a_i u_j-a_j u_i),
\]
where the right side belongs to the abelian ideal \(V_s\). Bracketing a \(V_s\)-valued function with \(x_\ell\) multiplies it by \(a_\ell J_s\), up to an inessential sign. Brackets of two \(V_s\)-valued functions vanish. Hence the derived term functions are exactly linear combinations of
\[
P(a_1J_s,\ldots,a_nJ_s)J_s(a_i u_j-a_j u_i),
\quad 1\le i<j\le n,
\]
as \(P\) ranges over ordinary polynomials in \(n\) commuting indeterminates. Both inclusions follow: all these expressions are iterated brackets, and every bracket term has this form.

**2. Simultaneous operator interpolation.** Put \(B_s=k[J_s]\cong k[\eta]/(\eta^s)\), a commutative \(k\)-algebra of dimension \(s\). For each \(a\in k^n\), consider the ring map
\[
\epsilon_a:k[Z_1,\ldots,Z_n]\longrightarrow B_s,
\qquad P\longmapsto P(a_1J_s,\ldots,a_nJ_s).
\]
If \(a\ne0\), one coefficient \(a_i\) is nonzero, so \(\epsilon_a\) is surjective: \(J_s=a_i^{-1}\epsilon_a(Z_i)\) generates \(B_s\). If \(a=0\), the image is the scalar subalgebra \(kI_s\).

The radical of \(\ker\epsilon_a\) is the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m_a=(Z_1-a_1,\ldots,Z_n-a_n)\). Indeed, \(\epsilon_a(Z_i-a_i)=a_iN_s\) is nilpotent; conversely, the scalar quotient \(B_s/(N_s)\cong k\) evaluates \(P\) at \(a\). The kernels for distinct rational points have distinct maximal radicals and are therefore pairwise comaximal. The Chinese remainder theorem consequently permits the values \(\epsilon_a(P)\) to be independently prescribed at every point of \(k^n\), with the stated image algebras. This operator-valued interpolation remains valid when \(s>q\): ordinary pointwise polynomial interpolation without nilpotent jets would not suffice.

**3. Every derived fiber.** Fix \(a\ne0\). Because \(J_s\) is invertible, the coefficient vectors of the basic commutators span over \(B_s\) the kernel of
\[
B_s^n\longrightarrow B_s,\qquad (T_1,\ldots,T_n)\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_iT_i.
\]
If \(a_r\ne0\), the \(n-1\) vectors \(a_r\mathbf e_j-a_j\mathbf e_r\), \(j\ne r\), form a \(B_s\)-basis for this kernel. Thus the fiber has \(k\)-dimension \(s(n-1)\). At \(a=0\), every basic commutator is zero. The Chinese remainder theorem from step 2 separates all nonzero-point fibers, so the derived ideal consists **exactly** of functions
\[
(a;u_1,\ldots,u_n)\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_i(a)u_i,
\]
where \(T_i(a)\in B_s\), \(T_i(0)=0\), and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_iT_i(a)=0\qquad\text{for all }a\in k^n.
\]
The action of \(B_s\) on \(V_s\) is faithful, so this parametrization is injective. Hence
\[
\dim_k[F_n^{(s)},F_n^{(s)}]=s(n-1)(q^n-1).
\]

**4. Free abelian quotient.** The \(n\) coordinate generators are linearly independent in the term-function algebra because their \(kt\) projections are the independent scalar coordinates \(a_i\). Their span intersects the derived ideal trivially, whose \(kt\) projection is identically zero. Every term is a sum of a linear term and bracket terms. Therefore the full dimension is \(n+s(n-1)(q^n-1)\), including \(n=1\).

**5. Strictness and an explicit polynomial identity.** The invariant span of \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\) in \(V_{s+1}\) identifies \(\mathfrak g_s\) with a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g_{s+1}\). Thus the generated varieties are nested. On their respective abelian ideals, \(D_x\) acts as \(aJ_s\). Since \(a^q=a\), and since \((I+N_s)^q=I+N_s^q\) in characteristic \(p\),
\[
D_x^q-D_x=a(N_s^q-N_s)=-aN_s(1-N_s^{q-1}).
\]
The right side has nilpotency index at most \(s\), proving the displayed identity on all commutators. In \(\mathfrak g_{s+1}\), substitute \(x=y=t\) and \(z=e_{s+1}\). The image of \([y,z]=J_{s+1}e_{s+1}\) under \((J_{s+1}^q-J_{s+1})^s\) is nonzero: it equals \((-1)^sN_{s+1}^s\) times an invertible polynomial in \(N_{s+1}\), applied to \(J_{s+1}e_{s+1}\). Hence this identity fails in \(\mathfrak g_{s+1}\), and every inclusion is strict. Finally the exact dimension formula gives the common exponential rate \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
Bahturin and Olshanskii's *Locally finite varieties of nonassociative algebras* (2026), Example 7, analyzes asymptotic free-dimension growth for a semidirect product with an **irreducible** abelian module and proves that the growth exponent is \(q\) even for arbitrarily large irreducible modules. Its argument does not treat indecomposable nonsemisimple Jordan modules or give the exact dimension formula above. Olshanskii's subsequent *Finite groups and rings generating varieties of rapid growth* (2026), Example 4, gives a lower-growth construction for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra, but not the Jordan-extension hierarchy.

The publicly available result *Exact free-algebra growth for the two-dimensional affine Lie algebra over finite fields* (2026) establishes the \(s=1\) formula \(n+(n-1)(q^n-1)\). The present result requires the additional operator-valued interpolation, covers every \(s\ge2\), and separates all Jordan lengths by explicit identities. The free-algebra formula in the \(s=1\) case is prior work, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The result concerns only split one-dimensional Lie extensions whose action is a **single unipotent Jordan block**. It does not classify relatively free algebras for arbitrary direct sums of Jordan blocks or arbitrary semisimple eigenvalues. A historical paper by Bahturin on identities in free polynilpotent Lie algebras was identified but not available for full-text comparison; an implicit older equivalent description cannot be excluded without that source. The prime-field computations do not constitute proof over all extension fields.
