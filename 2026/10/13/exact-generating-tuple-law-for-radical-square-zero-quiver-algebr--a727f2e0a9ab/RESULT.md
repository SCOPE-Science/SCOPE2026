# Exact generating-tuple law for radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding
Let \(Q\) be any finite loopless directed multiquiver with \(v\geq1\) vertices and arrow multiplicities \(m_{uw}\) from \(u\) to \(w\) for \(u\ne w\). Over any finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \(A=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the arrow ideal. For each integer \(r\geq1\), the number \(G_r(A)\) of ordered \(r\)-tuples generating \(A\) as a **unital** \(k\)-algebra is exactly
\[
G_r(A)=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{v-1}(q^r-i)\right)\prod_{u\ne w}\left(q^{m_{uw}}\prod_{j=0}^{m_{uw}-1}(q^{r-1}-q^j)\right).
\]
All empty products equal one. The expression is zero when \(v>q^r\) or some \(m_{uw}>r-1\). Dividing by \(q^{r(v+|E(Q)|)}\) gives the exact probability for \(r\) independently uniform elements:
\[
\Pr(\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle_{k,1}=A)
=\prod_{i=0}^{v-1}(1-iq^{-r})\prod_{u\ne w}\prod_{j=0}^{m_{uw}-1}(1-q^{j+1-r}).
\]
Consequently the minimum number of unital algebra generators is
\[
d(A)=\max\!\left\{\left\lceil\log_q v\right\rceil,\ 1+\max_{u\ne w}m_{uw}\right\}
\]
when an arrow exists, and \(d(A)=\lceil\log_q v\rceil\) without arrows (in particular \(d(\mathbb F_q)=0\)).

## Assumptions and scope
A loopless quiver has no arrows whose start and end coincide; parallel arrows, opposite arrows, oriented cycles, and disconnected components are allowed. In the radical-square-zero quotient every product of two arrows vanishes. The coefficient field is an arbitrary finite field, including nonprime fields and characteristic two. The \(r\) inputs are ordered, repetitions allowed, and generation always includes the original identity. The formula counts literal tuples, not automorphism orbits.

## Proof
Write \(1=\sum_{u\in V}e_u\) and decompose every generator as
\[
x_i=\sum_{u\in V}a_{iu}e_u+\sum_{u\ne w} b_{i,uw},\qquad b_{i,uw}\in J_{uw}:=e_uJe_w\cong k^{m_{uw}}.
\]
Let \(a_u=(a_{1u},\ldots,a_{ru})\in k^r\), and let \(B\) be the unital subalgebra generated by the inputs. Modulo \(J\), the images of \(x_i\) are coordinate functions on the finite subset \(\{a_u\}\subset k^r\). Multivariate polynomial interpolation shows that \(B\to A/J\cong k^V\) is onto exactly when all \(v\) labels \(a_u\) are distinct. There are precisely \(\prod_{i=0}^{v-1}(q^r-i)\) ways to choose these ordered vertex labels.

Fix such a distinct label family and two distinct vertices \(u,w\). Put \(\delta_{uw}=a_u-a_w\in k^r\setminus\{0\}\). The arrow component of the associative commutator is
\[
[x_i,x_j]_{uw}=\delta_{uw,i}b_{j,uw}-\delta_{uw,j}b_{i,uw}.
\]
Define \(\beta_{uw}:k^r\to J_{uw}\) by \(\beta_{uw}(c)=\sum_i c_i b_{i,uw}\), and put
\[
W_{uw}=\beta_{uw}(\ker\delta_{uw}).
\]
These commutator components span \(W_{uw}\): the vectors \(\delta_{uw,i}e_j-\delta_{uw,j}e_i\), for all input indices \(i,j\), span \(\ker\delta_{uw}\), and their images under \(\beta_{uw}\) are exactly the displayed commutator components. Because \(\delta_{uw}\ne0\), the kernel has dimension \(r-1\).

We claim \(B=A\) exactly when \(W_{uw}=J_{uw}\) for every ordered pair. Necessity: if any \(W_{uw}\ne J_{uw}\), the direct sum \(W=\bigoplus_{u\ne w}W_{uw}\) is a two-sided ideal of \(A\), since \(J^2=0\). All input commutators belong to \(W\); hence the images of the generators commute in \(A/W\), making the generated quotient commutative. But \(A/W\) itself is noncommutative: a nonzero surviving arrow from \(u\) to \(w\) has nonzero commutator with \(e_u\), as \(u\ne w\). Thus the inputs cannot generate \(A\).

Sufficiency: since the labels are distinct, for each vertex \(u\) choose a polynomial \(P_u\) in the \(r\) generators whose image modulo \(J\) is \(e_u\). Let \(p_u=P_u(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in B\). For each commutator \(c=[x_i,x_j]\in B\cap J\), the element \(p_ucp_w=e_uc e_w\) lies in \(B\cap J_{uw}\), since \(J^2=0\). These isolated commutator components span \(W_{uw}=J_{uw}\); therefore \(J\subseteq B\). Combined with surjectivity to \(A/J\), this proves \(B=A\).

For fixed distinct labels, the coordinates \((b_{1,uw},\ldots,b_{r,uw})\) determine a linear map \(k^r\to k^{m_{uw}}\). Its restriction to the fixed \((r-1)\)-dimensional kernel of \(\delta_{uw}\) is surjective in exactly \(\prod_{j=0}^{m_{uw}-1}(q^{r-1}-q^j)\) ways. On a complementary one-dimensional summand its image is arbitrary, giving \(q^{m_{uw}}\) additional choices. Arrow coordinates in distinct ordered-pair components are independent. Multiplication of these counts proves the formula. The probability and minimum-generator claims follow by division and by the necessary and sufficient nonvanishing thresholds.

## Verification
A standard-library Python script, `verify.py`, exhaustively enumerates candidate tuples for eleven small examples over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_4\), including parallel arrows, a two-cycle, and a disconnected quiver. Its independent algorithm repeatedly closes the span under multiplication by the input generators; it does not use the commutator-rank criterion. Run `python3 verify.py`. All eleven predicted counts match and the program prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Kravchenko–Mazur–Petrenko study probabilities and minimal numbers of algebra generators, including semisimple matrix-algebra families, but their cited quantitative results do not enumerate radical-square-zero quiver tuples. Iovanov–Sistko classify maximal subalgebras of finite-dimensional quiver algebras: vertex identifications and omitted arrow directions are the conceptual obstructions that appear here, although their classification does not present this explicit all-arity probability product. Sercombe–Shalev establish general bounds and asymptotic probabilities for generating finite associative algebras; the formulas here give an exact special family rather than those general bounds. No claim is made that the structural classification of maximal subalgebras is new.

## Limitations
Loops are excluded: self-loops can survive in a commutative quotient, invalidating the necessity argument. The radical must have square zero. These formulas count unital generation only and do not classify generating tuples up to automorphisms. The proof holds for all finite fields and all finite loopless directed multiquivers, while the executable checks sample only eleven small instances. Possible equivalent exact enumerations elsewhere in the literature have not been ruled out exhaustively.
