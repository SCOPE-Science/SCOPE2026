# Exact generator rank and census for finite monadic Gödel powers

## Finding

Let \(k\ge2\), \(n\ge1\), and let \(C_k=\{c_0=0<c_1<\cdots<c_{k-1}=1\}\) be the finite Gödel (linearly ordered Heyting) algebra. On the Cartesian power \(A_{k,n}=C_k^n\), use coordinatewise meet, join and Gödel implication \(a\to b=1\) when \(a\le b\) and \(a\to b=b\) otherwise. Extend it by the *global* monadic operations
\[
 \forall f=(\min_i f(i),\ldots,\min_i f(i)),\qquad
 \exists f=(\max_i f(i),\ldots,\max_i f(i)).
\]
These operations give a finite monadic Gödel algebra.

For an ordered tuple of \(r\ge0\) potential generators \(g_1,\ldots,g_r\in A_{k,n}\), define its positivity signature at coordinate \(i\) by
\[
 b_i=(\mathbf1_{\{g_1(i)>0\}},\ldots,\mathbf1_{\{g_r(i)>0\}})\in\{0,1\}^r.
\]
**Exact generation criterion.** The tuple generates the full algebra \(A_{k,n}\) if and only if (i) the \(n\) signatures \(b_i\) are pairwise distinct, and (ii) every intermediate chain element \(c_1,\ldots,c_{k-2}\) occurs among the \(nr\) generator entries.

Let \(N_{k,n,r}\) count ordered generating \(r\)-tuples, and interpret \(0^0=1\). Then the complete finite census is
\[
\boxed{
 N_{k,n,r}=n!\sum_{h=0}^{k-2}(-1)^h\binom{k-2}{h}
 [z^n]\prod_{j=0}^r\bigl(1+z(k-1-h)^j\bigr)^{\binom rj}.
}
\]
In particular \(N_{2,n,r}=(2^r)_n\), the falling factorial; the formula simultaneously accounts for Boolean coordinate separation and coverage of genuinely many-valued truth levels.

To express the sharp generator rank, set \(T_r(n)=-\infty\) for \(n>2^r\), and otherwise let \(T_r(n)\) be the sum of the \(n\) largest Hamming weights among all binary vectors of length \(r\), including repetitions of weights according to their binomial multiplicities. The minimal number of generators is exactly
\[
\boxed{
 d(A_{k,n})=\min\{r\ge0:n\le2^r\text{ and }k-2\le T_r(n)\}.
}
\]
For an entirely explicit evaluation, choose \(w\) so that
\[
 \sum_{j=w+1}^r\binom rj<n\le\sum_{j=w}^r\binom rj.
\]
Then
\[
 T_r(n)=\sum_{j=w+1}^r j\binom rj+w\left(n-\sum_{j=w+1}^r\binom rj\right).
\]
The rank formula includes the zero-generator case \(A_{2,1}\) and is valid without asymptotic restrictions.

For illustration, \(A_{5,2}\) has no single generator, but exactly \(24\) ordered generating pairs. It has five truth levels at each of two coordinates; hence \(25\) algebra elements.

## Assumptions and scope

The carrier \(C_k\) is a *chain*, not an arbitrary finite Gödel algebra, and the monadic operations are the coordinate-global minimum and maximum displayed above. The coordinate set is labeled, and tuples of generators are ordered. The language includes \(0,1,\wedge,\vee,\to,\forall,\exists\). The theorem concerns generating the full specified algebra under these operations; it does not assert a formula for arbitrary monadic Gödel algebras or free-algebra sizes.

## Proof

**(1) Two necessary invariants.** Every value appearing at any coordinate of a term in the generators belongs to
\[
 S=\{0,1\}\cup\{g_j(i):1\le j\le r,1\le i\le n\}.
\]
This follows by induction: lattice operations return an argument, Heyting implication returns either \(1\) or its consequent, and global min/max return one of the existing coordinate values. Thus generating all diagonal intermediate constants forces every \(c_1,\ldots,c_{k-2}\) to occur in \(S\) as a generator entry.

For any \(f\in A_{k,n}\), write \(Z(f)=\{i:f(i)=0\}\). The elementary identities
\[
 Z(f\wedge g)=Z(f)\cup Z(g),\quad Z(f\vee g)=Z(f)\cap Z(g),\quad
 Z(f\to g)=Z(g)\setminus Z(f)
\]
show that the zero set of a term formed by pointwise connectives belongs to the Boolean algebra generated by the initial zero sets \(Z(g_j)\). For the global quantifiers, \(Z(\forall f)\) and \(Z(\exists f)\) are each either the entire coordinate set or the empty set. Consequently all definable zero sets lie in this same Boolean algebra. If two coordinates have the same positivity signature, no definable zero set can separate them; in particular the crisp coordinate selector \(e_i\), equal to \(1\) at just one of them and \(0\) elsewhere, is not term-definable. Generation therefore forces the signatures to be distinct.

**(2) Sufficiency by explicit terms.** Define Gödel negation \(\neg a=a\to0\). It is crisp: \(\neg a=1\) exactly when \(a=0\), and \(\neg\neg a=1\) exactly when \(a>0\). If the signatures are distinct, the coordinate selector
\[
 e_i=\bigwedge_{j:g_j(i)=0}\neg g_j\ \wedge\ 
 \bigwedge_{j:g_j(i)>0}\neg\neg g_j
\]
is a term in the generators, with empty meets interpreted as \(1\). For each generator entry \(g_j(i)=c_t\), the term \(\exists(e_i\wedge g_j)\) is the diagonal constant \(c_t\). The assumed coverage therefore gives every scalar constant \(\overline{c_t}\). For an arbitrary target \(f\in C_k^n\), the expression
\[
 f=\bigvee_{i=1}^n(e_i\wedge\overline{f(i)})
\]
is a term. Thus the conditions suffice.

**(3) Exact census.** Fix an injective assignment of binary positivity words \(b_1,\ldots,b_n\). It has \(W=\sum_i |b_i|\) positive generator slots. Each positive slot may carry any of the \(k-1\) nonzero chain elements. Inclusion-exclusion over the \(k-2\) required intermediate levels shows that the number of fillings is
\[
 \sum_{h=0}^{k-2}(-1)^h\binom{k-2}{h}(k-1-h)^W.
\]
For a fixed \(h\), summing \((k-1-h)^W\) over ordered injective assignments is \(n!\) times the coefficient of \(z^n\) in the product over all \(2^r\) words \(b\) of \(1+z(k-1-h)^{|b|}\). Exactly \(\binom rj\) words have weight \(j\), giving the boxed coefficient formula.

**(4) Sharp rank.** An injective choice of \(n\) binary signatures exists if and only if \(n\le2^r\). Once chosen, exactly \(W=\sum_i|b_i|\) generator slots are nonzero. Covering \(k-2\) distinct intermediate levels is possible if and only if \(W\ge k-2\); the remaining nonzero slots can be assigned \(1\). Maximizing \(W\) over distinct words means taking the \(n\) largest Hamming weights, hence exactly \(T_r(n)\). This proves the rank formula and its displayed binomial-tail evaluation.

## Verification

The accompanying independent standard-library program `verify.py` directly closes generating tuples under the seven basic operations, without using the zero-signature criterion, for \(8{,}800\) small tuples across \(17\) distinct parameter combinations, comparing generation against the theorem. It additionally compares the coefficient census against an independent enumeration of distinct signatures in \(102\) parameter cases and verifies \(272\) rank-boundary instances. Its final marker is `VERIFY_OK`. The proof above applies to all parameter values and does not depend on the finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Castaño, Cimadamore, Díaz Varela and Rueda (arXiv:2006.10180v1, first posted June 17, 2020) develop monadic Gödel algebras, characterize their finite chains and free algebras in the subvariety generated by such chains, and study generation of individual *chain* expansions in Section 4.3. Their Lemmas 1.1--1.2 supply the monadic background. The claim here concerns a different concrete family: powers of a chain with the global coordinate quantifiers, which generally are not themselves chains, and counts ordered tuples generating the *entire named power*. The inspected relevant parts of that source do not supply this positivity-code criterion or its rank/census formula. General nonmonadic Gödel-algebra free-spectrum and coproduct studies are different invariants. Earlier work on Heyting-power idempotent retractions concerns morphism images rather than monadic generating tuples.

## Limitations

This is a self-review with computational corroboration, not an independent audit. The literature searches and inspected full-text primary source did not reveal an equivalent generator-census statement, but hidden antecedents in universal-algebra generation theory remain possible. The theorem is restricted to globally quantified finite powers and makes no extension claim for arbitrary monadic structures, nonchains, or products equipped with different quantifiers.
