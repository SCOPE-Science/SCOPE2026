# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The one-factor generating-count formula follows by partitioning ordered tuples according to the dimension of their linear span: the only non-generating two-dimensional spans are Lie-subalgebra planes. For \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), the number of such planes is \(q+1\) both for the nonsingular-conic argument in odd characteristic and for the Heisenberg algebra in characteristic two. In odd characteristic the Lie algebra is perfect and simple, with automorphism group of order \(q(q^2-1)\), so Lie Goursat and free automorphism orbits give a falling product. In even characteristic its direct power is two-step nilpotent with a nondegenerate block commutator map from its \(2k\)-dimensional abelianization onto its \(k\)-dimensional center; surjectivity of generating tuples to the quotient is necessary and sufficient. The verification program independently checks Lie closures, plane counts, generating automorphism orbits, and quotient-rank counts for multiple finite fields, including \(\mathbb F_4\). Infinite quantifiers rest on proof, not enumeration.

## Originality — PASS with limits

The 2007 Bois paper (arXiv:0708.1711, full text inspected) studies existence/generating varieties for simple algebras over algebraically closed fields and direct-sum existence (Corollary 2.2.5), not ordered finite-field multiplicities or this characteristic-two transition. The published three-dimensional plane-count result already supplies \(q+1\), and that step is explicitly treated as prior work. The 2025 dynamical-Lie direct-power paper concerns constructing quantum-circuit Lie algebras and not enumerating Lie-generating tuples over finite fields. The analogous finite simple group direct-power generating-graph theory is also credited. A 1976 semisimple-generator paper could not be read in full in the available sources; no priority certainty is asserted. The matched statement is the exact all-arity Lie direct-power enumeration, not the underlying standard Goursat lemma.

## Value — PASS

The count is a natural finite-field generator invariant and identifies the *exact* maximal size of a direct power generated by a fixed number of Lie elements. It exhibits a discontinuity invisible in the identical single-factor enumerators: for two generators, \(q^2(q-1)\) factors in odd characteristic but only one in characteristic two. This measures a structural effect of the degeneration of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) from simple to Heisenberg, and goes beyond a small table or renaming of the single-factor count.

## Remaining limitations

Only unrestricted ordered generating tuples are counted; constrained tuple distributions are not determined. The external access shortfall for one relevant historical publication and the possibility of older generic formulations remain bibliographic risks. No independent mathematical assessment was made.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
