# Reproducibility and proof checks

Use `python verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter; it uses only `math.isqrt` and integer arithmetic. The accompanying `verification_output.txt` was generated by executing this standalone file.

The program sieves Euler's totient function through 200010 and checks every initial value from 1 through 200000, confirms the first repeated state lies on one of three claimed cycles, evaluates the entire eleven-state transition table, and tests the prime/composite descent alternatives for odd indices from 27 through 49999. These bounded checks succeeded. They are finite regression tests, not an exhaustive proof over all positive integers.

The unrestricted proof rests on three directly verifiable observations: the least prime divisor of a composite number does not exceed its square root, so its totient does not exceed its value minus its square root; two odd twin primes greater than 3 must be followed by a multiple of 3; and the resulting strict three-step descent cannot continue indefinitely among positive odd integers. The final eleven-state table then gives an exact finite classification. The initial values 1 and 2 are separately routed through 4 and 5.

Independent audit was not performed. No external certificate or computation over an infinite domain is claimed.
