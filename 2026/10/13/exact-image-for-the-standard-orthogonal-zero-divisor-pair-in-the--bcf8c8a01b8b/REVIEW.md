# Review

## Correctness
PASS. In the split model, left multiplication by \(p_0\) or \(n_0\) forces the quaternion component into \(Fp_0+Fn_0\), while the sum of their right-multiplication contributions fills the entire \(M_2(F)\omega\) component. This gives the global containment in a six-dimensional space. For \(x_0=E_{11}+E_{21}\), direct Cayley--Dickson multiplication gives an explicit linear slice whose six decisive coordinates form a determinant-one system, proving the reverse inclusion over every field in scope. The symbolic replay reconstructs the product formula and confirms the determinant-one minor.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2610.04466v1, explicitly states only that the image spans a six-dimensional space for linearly independent orthogonal zero divisors and says the image is a vector subspace except possibly in that case. Its fixed split model uses exactly \(p_0=E_{11}\), \(n_0=E_{12}\), but it does not give the exact image for this pair. published-finding corpus searches for the canonical pair, aliases, exact-image language, and the six-dimensional subspace found no statement implying this claim. The older \(M_2\) theorem covers an associative algebra and does not imply the octonion calculation.

## Value
PASS. The pair \(p_0,n_0\) is not an arbitrary coordinate slice: it is the standard zero-divisor pair fixed in the motivating paper's split model and lies in the paper's only case where vector-space structure of the image is left unresolved. Identifying its exact image and exhibiting a single rank-six slice gives a concrete structural foothold on that boundary without pretending to settle the entire case.

Closest literature and limitations: arXiv:2610.04466v1 gives the six-dimensional linear span in the general exceptional case but not exact image equality; arXiv:2312.13865v1 proves vector-space images only for \(2\times2\) matrices. The present result is restricted to the canonical pair and makes no general orbit-reduction claim.
