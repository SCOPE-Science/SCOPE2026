# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The mathematical claim follows from the published exact finite-sample reduction with strict interior equality (Wu--Chen, Proposition 2.1), an independently recomputable exact polynomial identity, and a strict positive lower bound on the polynomial remainder for every separation in the claimed open interval. The checker enumerates the entire finite count/rank sample space and compares symbolic coefficients, not only numerical instances. The sharp probability is attained at the interior symmetric vector and every other admissible vector has strictly greater correct-selection probability. The result is for exactly two observations from each of four populations.

## Originality — PASS

Wu--Chen's Section 6 explicitly leaves even-sample balanced exact centering as a conjecture, while Table 2 reports a numerical check at one gap. Their Corollary 2.4 concerns two rather than four populations; their Theorem 2.3 is eventual in sample size rather than an exact two-trial result. The new all-gap four-population classification and its sharp value are not consequences of those theorems. Searches for equivalent binomial slippage, ranked subset selection, four-population two-trial centering, and tie-breaking aliases did not reveal an explicit covering theorem. This is a literature-supported novelty assessment, not proof of worldwide priority.

## Value — PASS

The result resolves the first balanced multi-population finite-sample case explicitly singled out by a recent conjecture, for the full continuum of gap values and every admissible preference-zone parameter vector. The exact extremal probability and strict uniqueness are useful structural benchmarks for exact ranking-and-selection design; this is not a chosen numerical instance or a routine substitution into the asymptotic theorem.

## Closest literature and risks

Closest: Y. Wu and P. Chen, *Least-Favorable Location for Binomial Top-t Selection* (arXiv:2609.03466v1), especially Proposition 2.1, Corollary 2.4, Theorem 2.3, Table 2, and Section 6. That work supplies the reduction and conjecture but not the present uniform finite-sample proof. Possible overlooked equivalent statements in pre-2026 statistical selection literature remain a priority risk. The theorem does not imply the unrestricted even-sample conjecture, and does not establish a result for other tie rules.
