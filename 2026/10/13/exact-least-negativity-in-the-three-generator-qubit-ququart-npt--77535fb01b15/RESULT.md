# Exact least negativity in the three-generator qubit–ququart NPT simplex
## Finding
In \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^4\), define the three orthonormal nearest-neighbor antisymmetric vectors
\[
|\psi_i\rangle=\frac{|0,i+1\rangle-|1,i\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad i=0,1,2,
\]
and the probability-simplex family
\[
\rho_{x,y,z}=x|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0|+y|\psi_1\rangle\langle\psi_1|+z|\psi_2\rangle\langle\psi_2|,
\qquad x,y,z\ge0,\qquad x+y+z=1.
\]
For the standard negativity
\[
\mathcal N(\rho)=\frac{\|\rho^\Gamma\|_1-1}2,
\]
the unique global minimizer on the full closed simplex is
\[
x=y=z=\frac13,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\min\mathcal N(\rho_{x,y,z})=\frac{\sqrt5-1}6}.
\]
At the minimizer, \(\rho^\Gamma\) has inertia
\[
(\nu_+,\nu_0,\nu_-)=(5,1,2).
\]
In particular the least-negative point of this diagonal simplex is not a maximal-inertia point: it lies on the boundary where the middle \(2\times2\) partial-transpose block acquires a zero eigenvalue.

## Assumptions and scope
The claim concerns only convex mixtures diagonal in the displayed three-vector basis. It does not minimize negativity over every density operator supported on their span, and it does not claim an all-dimensional formula. Partial transposition \(\Gamma\) is taken on the qubit factor; the spectrum is unchanged if the other factor is transposed instead.

These vectors are the \(2\times4\) specialization of the standard nearest-neighbor completely entangled/NPT-subspace construction appearing in the literature on large completely entangled and NPT subspaces. The problem here is quantitative: determine the exact least negativity on the canonical diagonal probability simplex.

## Proof
In the ordered product basis, partial transposition splits \(\rho_{x,y,z}^\Gamma\) into two positive singleton entries \(x/2\) and \(z/2\), together with three independent blocks
\[
B_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&-x\\-x&y\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_1=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}x&-y\\-y&z\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_2=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}y&-z\\-z&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathcal N(\rho_{x,y,z})=
\frac{\sqrt{y^2+4x^2}-y}4+
\max\!\left\{0,\frac{\sqrt{(x-z)^2+4y^2}-x-z}4}\right\}+
\frac{\sqrt{y^2+4z^2}-y}4.
\]
Fix \(y\), hence fix \(x+z=1-y\). If \(y>0\), the function \(t\mapsto\sqrt{y^2+4t^2}\) is strictly convex on \([0,\infty)\), so the sum of the two endpoint-block contributions is uniquely minimized at \(x=z=(1-y)/2\). The middle contribution is also no smaller there because
\[
\sqrt{(x-z)^2+4y^2}\ge2y.
\]
If \(y=0\), the negativity is \(1/2\), already larger than the value obtained below. Hence every global minimizer must satisfy
\[
x=z=\frac{1-y}2.
\]
Put
\[
s(y)=\sqrt{y^2+(1-y)^2}.
\]
The problem reduces exactly to
\[
F(y)=\frac{s(y)-y}2+\max\!\left\{0,\frac{3y-1}4}\right\},\qquad 0\le y\le1.
\]
For \(0\le y\le1/3\),
\[
F'(y)=\frac{2y-1}{2s(y)}-\frac12<0.
\]
For \(y\ge1/3\),
\[
F'(y)=\frac{2y-1}{2s(y)}+\frac14.
\]
This is positive for \(y\ge1/2\). On \(1/3\le y<1/2\), positivity is equivalent to \(s(y)>2(1-2y)\). Squaring is legitimate and gives
\[
s(y)^2-4(1-2y)^2=-14y^2+14y-3>0,
\]
because the roots are \((7\pm\sqrt7)/14\), which bracket the interval \([1/3,1/2]\). Thus \(F\) decreases up to \(1/3\) and increases thereafter. Its unique minimum is at \(y=1/3\), forcing \(x=z=1/3\).

Finally,
\[
F\!\left(\frac13\right)=\frac12\left(\frac{\sqrt5}3-\frac13\right)=\frac{\sqrt5-1}6.
\]
At this point the middle block has eigenvalues \(0\) and \(1/3\), while each endpoint block has one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Together with the two positive singleton entries this gives inertia \((5,1,2)\).

## Verification
The bundled `verify_min_negativity.py` reconstructs the \(8\times8\) density matrix and partial transpose using exact SymPy expressions, checks the three-block decomposition, verifies the uniform-point spectrum and inertia, and symbolically checks the claimed minimum value. It also evaluates the one-variable formula on exact rational points on both sides of \(1/3\). These finite checks support the algebra; the global minimum is proved analytically above.

## Relationship to prior work
Walgate and Scott study maximal completely entangled subspaces and their genericity. Johnston later proves that an \((m-1)(n-1)\)-dimensional subspace can be chosen so that every supported state is NPT, and gives the nearest-neighbor spanning construction used here. Rana proves the universal upper bound \((m-1)(n-1)\) on the number of negative partial-transpose eigenvalues. Chen and Đoković give explicit \(2\times N\) NPT families with prescribed numbers of negative eigenvalues.

Those results establish existence, dimension, and inertia bounds. The present claim instead solves the trace-norm minimization problem on the complete diagonal simplex generated by the three canonical \(2\times4\) nearest-neighbor vectors. The inspected sources do not state the minimum \((\sqrt5-1)/6\), its unique uniform minimizer, or the fact that this minimizer lies on the \((5,1,2)\) inertia boundary.

## Limitations
No claim is made about the minimum over coherent density matrices supported on the same three-dimensional subspace. The result is specific to \(2\times4\); higher-dimensional diagonal simplices lead to a longer coupled minimization problem. A poorly indexed source could contain the same special minimization, although targeted semantic and exact-expression searches found none.
