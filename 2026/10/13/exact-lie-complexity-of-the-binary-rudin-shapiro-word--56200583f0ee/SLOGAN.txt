The binary Rudin–Shapiro word has Lie complexity supported, apart from the exceptional non-dyadic lengths \(3,5,6\), only at powers of two: every length \(2^k\) with \(k\ge3\) has exactly four cyclic-conjugacy classes whose every rotation occurs, and all non-dyadic lengths \(n\ge7\) have none. An exact length-eight starting-parity obstruction and two injective desubstitutions prove this sparse spectrum.
