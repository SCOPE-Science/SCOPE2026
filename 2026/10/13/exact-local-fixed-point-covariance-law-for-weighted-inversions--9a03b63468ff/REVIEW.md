# Review
## Correctness
PASS. Conditioning on \(\Pi(i)=i\) leaves every inversion comparison not incident to \(i\) exchangeable and hence unbiased. The two incident directions have exact deviations \(\pm(n+1-2i)/(2(n-1))\), which yields the arbitrary-weight theorem. Ordinary inversion count, aggregate covariance, and correlation follow by exact algebra. Exhaustive permutation enumeration through \(n=9\) supplies an independent finite replay.

## Originality
PASS, with an explicit older-literature risk. The closest full text found conditions inversion number on the total number of fixed points and gives exact pair-order probabilities under that count conditioning. It does not state the specified-position event \(\Pi(i)=i\), the parabolic local profile, or the arbitrary weighted-inversion theorem. Archive-era sources separately treat inversion statistics under conjugacy-class averaging and the random fixed-point set.

An equivalent aggregate identity may occur in older Mahonian enumeration, and a local form could be hidden under pinned-permutation terminology. The aggregate covariance is therefore presented as a corollary; the main originality claim is the local arbitrary-weight law.

## Value
PASS. Fixed points and inversion number are fundamental permutation statistics. The result identifies exactly how a fixed point at a specified location perturbs all weighted pairwise-order scores, exposes central-position neutrality, and explains the aggregate negative dependence through a position-resolved mechanism.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest recent work studies inversion statistics conditional on a prescribed fixed-point count. The present theorem instead conditions on the location of one fixed point. It applies only to uniform permutations and controls first conditional moments and covariance, not independence or a full conditional law.
