# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Kummer's borrow-count theorem reduces the exact least-valuation count to an exhaustive list of permissible borrow runs. For one borrow, all possible ending positions are listed and the congruence \(m\mid k\) is solved exactly. For the exceptional \(p=m-1\) case, the two-borrow patterns split into the four disjoint enumerated families, with every gap and initial-position boundary handled. The resulting counts are positive in every permitted case. Independent exact Legendre and digital valuation computations agree with the construction in 169 exhaustively scanned parameter cases, with 11,534 constructive witnesses verified across the larger parameter sample. No finite scan is used to infer completeness at arbitrary parameters.

## Originality — PASS

Fairfax-Ball (arXiv:2609.37754v1) gives the least valuation of the restricted gcd, but does not count all coefficients attaining it. The corrected McTague paper (arXiv:1510.06696v5) and the partially accessible Wu preprint (arXiv:2606.20940v2) develop restricted gcd formulas, not the full two-digit minimizer census. The structurally nearest prior research finding establishes the ordinary-branch *gcd valuation* only; knowing a minimum does not determine its number of attaining indices. Relevant semantic queries and prior claim comparisons found no complete classification. Residual risk: an unindexed or implicit equivalent counting argument, and the unexamined portions of Wu's preprint.

## Value — PASS

The cardinality of the least-valuation stratum of an arithmetic-progression subrow measures how many individual binomial coefficients realize the gcd's prime-adic order, an invariant not encoded by the gcd itself. The formula identifies a sharp contrast between the ordinary and exceptional prime-modulus branches and an unexpected stabilization at gap three, while classifying the entire natural family and all minimizers. This is a motivated exact invariant and is not simply a restatement or renaming of the known valuation criterion.
