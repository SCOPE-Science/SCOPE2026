# Exact monogenic subalgebra spectrum of the canonical strict-2-finite Heyting algebra

## Finding
Let \(H=X_2^*\) be the Heyting algebra of clopen upsets of the Esakia space \(X_2\) from the strict-finiteness construction. For \(U\in H\), let \(\langle U\rangle\) denote the subalgebra generated by \(U\), including the named constants \(0\) and \(1\). Then
\[
\{{|\langle U\rangle|:U\in H\}}=\{{2,3,5,9\}}.
\]
The multiplicities are also exact: exactly two elements generate size \(2\), exactly thirty generate size \(5\), exactly four generate size \(9\), and every other element generates size \(3\). The four size-\(9\) generators are
\[
L_2^0\setminus\{{x_0^j\}}\qquad (j=1,2,3,4).
\]
In particular, the sharp monogenic bound for this canonical strict-\(2\)-finite algebra is \(9\).

## Assumptions and scope
For \(n=2\), each level \(L_2^i=\{{x_i^0,\ldots,x_i^4\}}\) has five points. The order has minimum \(x_\infty\) and, apart from equality,
\[
x_i^l\le x_{i'}^{{l'}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\ge i'+2\ \text{{ or }}\ (i=i'+1\text{{ and }}l'\ne l+1).
\]
The topology is the one-point compactification topology from Definition 4.1 of the source paper. Hence a clopen upset is either the whole space or a finite upset omitting \(x_\infty\). In the Esakia dual, for clopen upsets \(A,B\),
\[
A\to B=X_2\setminus\downarrow(A\setminus B).
\]
No assertion is made here about the exact spectrum of two-generated subalgebras.

## Proof
Write \(L_0=L_2^0\) and \(L_1=L_2^1\). If a finite upset \(U\) meets some level \(L_2^r\) with \(r\ge2\), then every point of \(L_0\) lies above each point of \(L_2^r\). Thus \(L_0\subseteq U\), so \(\downarrow U=X_2\), equivalently \(\neg U=0\). Any nonzero proper dense element \(a\) generates exactly \(\{{0,a,1\}}\). Therefore every generator of rank at least \(2\) gives a 3-element subalgebra.

Now consider rank \(1\). A point \(x_1^l\) lies below every point of \(L_0\) except possibly \(x_0^{{l+1}}\); for \(l=4\) it lies below all of \(L_0\). Consequently, a rank-1 upset fails to contain all of \(L_0\) only in the four cases
\[
E_j=(L_0\setminus\{{x_0^j\}})\cup\{{x_1^{{j-1}}\}},\qquad j=1,2,3,4.
\]
All other rank-1 upsets are dense and generate size \(3\). Directly from the implication formula, \(\neg E_j=\{{x_0^j\}}\), \(\neg\{{x_0^j\}}=E_j\), and \(E_j\vee\{{x_0^j\}}\) is dense. The five sets \(0,1,E_j,\{{x_0^j\}},E_j\vee\{{x_0^j\}}\) form the generated subalgebra, so each \(E_j\) generates size \(5\).

It remains to analyze rank \(0\), so let \(U=S\subseteq L_0\), \(S\ne\varnothing\). If \(|S|\ge2\), then no point of \(L_1\) can avoid every member of \(S\), hence
\[
\neg S=L_0\setminus S.
\]
For a singleton \(\{{x_0^j\}}\) with \(j\ge1\), the unique extra point not lying below it is \(x_1^{{j-1}}\), while \(x_0^0\) has no such exception. These observations give all cases. The full set \(L_0\) is dense and generates size \(3\). If \(2\le|S|\le3\), or if \(S=L_0\setminus\{{x_0^0\}}\), or if \(S\) is a singleton, the pair \(S,\neg S\), together with their join and \(0,1\), closes to exactly five elements.

The only remaining rank-0 sets are
\[
a_j=L_0\setminus\{{x_0^j\}},\qquad j=1,2,3,4.
\]
For each such \(a_j\), put \(b=\neg a_j\), \(c=a_j\vee b\), \(d=\neg b\), \(e=b\vee d\), \(f=d\to a_j\), and \(g=d\vee f\). The nine elements
\[
0,1,a_j,b,c,d,e,f,g
\]
are pairwise distinct and are closed under \(\wedge,\vee,\to\); this is checked symbolically by the accompanying exact verifier directly from the displayed order. Hence \(|\langle a_j\rangle|=9\).

There are \(31\) nonempty rank-0 upsets: one gives size \(3\), four give size \(9\), and the other \(26\) give size \(5\). There are \(35\) rank-1 upsets: four give size \(5\) and the other \(31\) give size \(3\). Together with \(0\) and \(1\), which each generate the two-element algebra, this yields the claimed global multiplicities.

## Verification
The script `verify_monogenic_x2.py` implements the order of Definition 4.1 and the Esakia implication exactly for finite clopen upsets. It exhaustively enumerates all \(31\) nonempty rank-0 upsets and all \(35\) rank-1 upsets, closes each under \(0,1,\wedge,\vee,\to\), identifies the four 9-element generators and the four exceptional rank-1 generators, and verifies closure of the explicit nine-term family in every exceptional case. It also checks the structural deep-rank lemma across representative levels. The replay returns `VERIFY_OK 1300 exact assertions`.

The infinite tail is not inferred from finite enumeration: the proof that every rank at least \(2\) element is dense follows uniformly from the order definition.

## Relationship to prior work
Hyttinen, Martins, Moraschini, and Quadrellaro construct \(X_n\), prove that \(X_n^*\) is infinite and \((n+1)\)-generated, and prove a uniform finite upper bound for its \(n\)-generated subalgebras. Their paper does not state the exact monogenic spectrum of \(X_2^*\); full-text searches for “one-generated” and “monogenic” in the article returned no occurrence. The classical Rieger–Nishimura theorem classifies finite one-generated Heyting algebras in general, but it does not determine which of those algebras occur as singly generated subalgebras of this particular \(X_2^*\).

The present calculation is therefore a fine-structure result about the canonical counterexample itself: it replaces mere finiteness at the first lower generator arity by an exact four-value spectrum, a sharp bound, and exact generator multiplicities.

## Limitations
The result is specific to \(X_2^*\). It does not determine the exact two-generated bound in \(X_2^*\), nor the corresponding monogenic spectra for every \(X_n^*\). Originality searches cannot exclude an unindexed observation or folklore computation. The published article appeared online after the middle-window cutoff, so the dating of the finding uses the earlier June 2023 preprint of the same construction.
