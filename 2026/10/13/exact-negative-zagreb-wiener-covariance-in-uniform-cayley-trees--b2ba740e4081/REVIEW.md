# Scientific review — exact negative Zagreb–Wiener covariance in uniform Cayley trees

## Correctness (C): PASS
The probability model is the uniform unrooted labeled tree, not a recursive or unlabelled tree. The forest-extension count gives the exact marked-pair distance distribution, weighted Prüfer coding gives the conditional squared-degree expectation, and a finite telescoping identity yields the mixed moment. The boundary case where the marked path spans the tree is separately included. Positivity of a four-term partial sum proves the covariance strictly negative at all sizes \(n\ge4\), with direct degeneracy at \(n=2,3\). The exact finite rational enumeration of every labeled tree through \(n=8\) agrees with all formulas. Janson's result supplies only the separately identified large-size Wiener mean asymptotic.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk
Janson (2003, DOI 10.1002/rsa.10074), inspected in complete institutional full text, derives Wiener and internal-path-length moments, not the Zagreb mixed moment. Hollas (2005, DOI 10.1007/s10910-005-6474-5), also inspected in complete full text, studies independent random marks on trees and does not address the two deterministic indices. Wagner (2014, DOI 10.1017/S0963548314000443) supplies additive small-toll limit theory rather than the exact nonlocal cross-covariance. Feng–Hu (2011, DOI 10.1239/jap/1324046027) treat a different random-recursive-tree law; its full paper remained inaccessible in the attempted authorized route. Search comparisons covered the aliases first Zagreb index, squared-degree sum, Cayley/uniform labeled tree, Wiener/total pairwise distance, covariance/cross-moment, and distance-conditioned regression. An earlier conditioned *single endpoint degree* law cannot imply the present global sum-of-squared-degrees covariance without conditional information about all other vertices. No covering stronger statement was identified, but the literature may contain an older joint-index enumerator under another name.

## Value (V): PASS
The first Zagreb and Wiener indices are classical complementary local-degree and global-distance statistics of the same random tree. The exact negative cross-dependence and its nontrivial conditional-distance mechanism quantify their interaction across all tree sizes, including a strict sign boundary and a leading asymptotic scale. This is not a deterministic inequality between indices nor merely a restatement of their individual marginal moments.

## Limits
Exact rational tree enumeration is finite corroboration and does not establish the infinite theorem by itself. The proof is self-contained except for the clearly designated known Wiener mean asymptotic. The inaccessible Feng–Hu full text and possible unindexed classical cross-index identities remain originality risks. Independent validation has not been performed.
