# Scientific review: exact odd-copy activation hierarchy

**Correctness — PASS.** The analytic comparison treats every pair of product eigenvectors in every positive tensor power, not a finite sample: the parity of occupation count equals total excitation parity, so odd energy gaps have maximal first-level-count difference equal to the largest odd integer at most the copy number, and even gaps give a weaker requirement. Explicit energy-gap-one inversions prove necessity, while a logarithmic comparison proves sufficiency. Strict inequalities, ties, the uniform/Gibbs case and even-copy padding are included. The exact-arithmetic verifier checks 1,430 finite parameter/copy instances and four activation thresholds; none of these finite tests is offered as the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the stated literature risk.** The closest prior work is Skrzypczyk–Silva–Brunner, arXiv:1412.5485, section IV equation (9). Its mixed-virtual-temperature witness is a sufficient activation construction and, when specialized, can produce the same upper bound; that part is not independently novel. Its full text does not prove the reverse all-pairs condition or identify the exact closed population wedge and odd/even plateau for *all* equispaced qutrit states. Perarnau-Llobet et al., arXiv:1502.07311, address energy/entropy extremizers, and Salvia–Giovannetti, arXiv:1912.02099, study energy bounds and structural stability. A qutrit activation example in the QFT.org pedagogical chapter has only fixed copy counts. Searches under finite-copy passivity, qutrit virtual temperature, tensor-power activation, logarithmic population ratios, and equivalent lattice inequalities did not locate a stronger covering classification. Residual risk: an implicit or explicitly published consequence of broader lattice-order/thermomajorization results might duplicate the theorem, and one relevant thesis was not completely accessible.

**Value — PASS.** Minimal copy number is an operationally natural exact invariant: it measures the finite collective resource required for cyclic work extraction. The result characterizes an entire two-parameter family, including all equality boundaries, constructs the first realizing work-extraction transposition and explains why every even copy step is redundant. Its divergence near common virtual temperatures quantifies how near-Gibbs states can resist work extraction for arbitrarily many copies. No implication is asserted for general spectra or maximal ergotropy.

**Limitations.** Only equispaced, positive-population, energy-diagonal passive qutrits and arbitrary global cyclic unitaries are covered. The literature comparison is not independent priority certification.
