# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies the six-vertex unweighted cycle, adjacency-generated evolution, a single common observation-time distribution, exact probability weights, and nonnegative times. The distance-class formulas follow by Fourier diagonalization and are verified symbolically. Substitution of the explicitly stated radical cosine nodes and weights yields all four equalities to \(1/6\); the four distances exhaust every matrix entry. Distinct positive support points are guaranteed by elementary inequalities. The sole-time lower bound checks the exhaustive roots of the difference between adjacent distance classes. Periodicity follows from all adjacency eigenvalues being integers. The proof is finite exact algebra and a periodicity argument, not an empirical extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The primary inspected preprint of Baptista–Coutinho–Marques, arXiv:2308.16378, supplies general arbitrary-time sampling and two path examples but not this explicit six-cycle theorem or matching two-atom lower bound. The inspected Adamczak et al. arXiv:0708.2096 treats instantaneous or conventional Cesàro cycle mixing, distinct from an optimized atomic time law. The 2022 and 2017 spectral-framework papers do not assert this optimal sampling support in the inspected relevant portions. Search comparisons included precise aliases such as six-cycle, C6, two-time randomized sampling, six-by-six circulant mixing, exact uniform mixing, and alternative time distributions; named published-finding corpus closest matches were unrelated random walks or Fourier constants. A differently worded later result or a general constructive criterion could subsume the theorem; absence of an indexed match is not proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS

Exact uniform probability output from one common observation-time distribution is a natural quantum transport and sampling criterion. The six-cycle is a fundamental small cycle where standard Cesàro averaging is not uniform. Determining the *minimum* number of observation times required (two), with explicit algebraic parameters and an arbitrarily-late realization, answers a concrete natural complexity question, rather than a renamed identity, a single numerical experiment, or an arbitrary unmotivated slice. The result is deliberately limited to this six-cycle and need not imply a family-wide theorem to remain a complete minimal-support fact.
