# Exact optimum and equality census for \(\mathbb Z_5^3\) Lee codes of distance three
## Finding
For arbitrary, not necessarily linear, codes \(C\subseteq\mathbb Z_5^3\), equipped with the coordinatewise Lee metric
\[
d_L(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^3 \min\{(x_i-y_i)\bmod 5,(y_i-x_i)\bmod 5\},
\]
the largest possible cardinality subject to \(d_L(C)\ge 3\) is
\[
|C|=15.
\]
There are exactly \(200\) labeled maximum codes. Under the natural Lee-isometry group generated by translations in \(\mathbb Z_5^3\), independent reflections in the three cyclic coordinates, and permutations of the three coordinates, all \(200\) maxima form one orbit. This group has order
\[
5^3\,2^3\,3!=6000,
\]
so the stabilizer of a maximum has order \(30\).

Every maximum is symbol-balanced in every coordinate: each symbol in \(\mathbb Z_5\) occurs exactly three times in each coordinate. Among the \(\binom{15}{2}=105\) unordered pairs of codewords, exactly \(45\) have Lee distance \(3\), exactly \(45\) have distance \(4\), and exactly \(15\) have distance \(6\).

The linear optimum at the same parameters is known to be \(5\); hence the unrestricted optimum is three times larger.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_5\), and the Lee weight of a symbol \(a\) is \(\min\{a,5-a\}\) for the representative \(a\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). No linearity, additive closure, or systematic form is assumed.

The equality census is labeled: two subsets are counted separately when they are different subsets of \(\mathbb Z_5^3\), even if a Lee isometry maps one to the other. The asserted transitivity concerns the explicit order-\(6000\) natural isometry group above; no larger abstract automorphism group is needed.

## Proof
Form the compatibility graph \(G\) on the \(125\) words of \(\mathbb Z_5^3\), joining two words exactly when their Lee distance is at least \(3\). Then the desired codes are precisely the cliques of \(G\).

The exact verifier performs a complete bit-set branch-and-bound maximum-clique search. At each node it greedily colors the candidate-induced graph; the number of colors is an upper bound on the size of any clique extension. A branch is discarded only when the current clique size plus that certified coloring bound cannot exceed the incumbent. The search visits the complete remaining tree and returns clique number \(15\), while an explicit fifteen-word witness attains the bound.

Translation is an isometry and acts transitively on the \(125\) ambient words. The verifier therefore enumerates all maximum cliques containing \(000\), again with exact coloring bounds, and obtains exactly \(24\). Double-count the pairs \((C,c)\) in which \(C\) is a maximum code and \(c\in C\). On one hand there are \(15N\) such pairs if \(N\) is the number of labeled maxima. On the other hand every ambient word lies in exactly \(24\) maxima by translation symmetry, giving \(125\cdot24\). Thus
\[
N=\frac{125\cdot24}{15}=200.
\]
The verifier also constructs these \(200\) sets explicitly by translating the normalized maxima, so the double count is independently realized as a set computation.

Finally, the verifier applies all coordinate permutations, all independent coordinate reflections, and all translations to one maximum. Its orbit contains exactly \(200\) distinct maximum codes, so it equals the complete census. Direct exact tallies over all \(200\) maxima give the stated coordinate balances and the common pair-distance distribution.

## Verification
Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter. It reconstructs all \(125\) ambient words and the Lee metric from definitions. As an independent compatibility check, it also constructs every radius-one Lee ball, each of size \(7\), and verifies for every pair of centers that distance at least \(3\) is equivalent to disjoint radius-one balls.

The maximum-clique branch-and-bound then proves the upper bound \(15\), not merely by finding a large clique. A separate target-size enumeration through \(000\) gives the \(24\) normalized maxima. The script explicitly generates all \(200\) translated maxima, checks the complete natural-isometry orbit, and recomputes every balance and pair-distance tally. Successful replay begins with:

`VERIFY_OK vertices=125 ball_size=7 maximum=15 normalized_maxima=24 labeled_maxima=200 natural_group=6000 orbits=1 orbit_size=200 stabilizer=30 distance_profile=3x45,4x45,6x15 balanced=3_each_symbol_per_coordinate max_nodes=149652 enum_nodes=337597`

## Relationship to prior work
Astola and Tabus study linear Lee codes and give a tight table for \(q=5\). At \(n=3\) and minimum distance \(3\), their Table 1 gives linear dimension at most \(1\), hence at most \(5\) codewords. Their result is restricted to linear codes and therefore does not cover the unrestricted optimum \(15\) or its equality cases.

Alderson and Huntemann explicitly study general, not necessarily linear, Lee codes and develop Singleton- and Plotkin-type bounds. Their \(q=5\) special arguments concern integral-dimension equality regimes; they do not state the exact unrestricted value for \(\mathbb Z_5^3\) at distance \(3\), nor an equality census.

Polak later defines the same unrestricted parameter \(A_q^L(n,d)\) and gives semidefinite-programming bounds. The displayed table of new \(q=5\) bounds starts with \(n=4\); it does not state the \(q=5,n=3,d=3\) value or classify its maximizers. Searches using the exact parameter, Lee-code aliases, and nonlinear/unrestricted terminology found no source stating the \(200\)-code census, the one-orbit classification, or the universal balance and distance profile.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-parameter theorem for \(\mathbb Z_5^3\) at minimum Lee distance \(3\). It gives no formula for other lengths, alphabets, or distances, and it does not improve asymptotic Lee-code bounds. The upper bound and census are computer-assisted finite proofs whose validity depends on the included deterministic verifier and ordinary integer/bit-set operations; no independent audit has yet been performed. A residual bibliographic risk remains that an obscure or unindexed historical computation may contain the same finite census.
