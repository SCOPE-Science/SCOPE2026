# Exact prime-density distribution of rational singularities in a plane-filling curve family

## Finding

For each prime \(p\ge5\), the explicitly defined degree-\(p+3\) plane-filling curve \(C_p\) below has exactly \(N_p\) singular points rational over \(\mathbb F_p\), where \(N_p\) is the number of roots of the same integer polynomial \(h(T)=T^7+T^3-1\) modulo \(p\). The splitting field of \(h\) has Galois group \(S_7\), so the following is the **complete natural-density distribution of rational singular-point counts** as \(p\) varies over rational primes:

| Number \(j\) of rational singular points | Prime density |
| --- | --- |
| \(0\) | \(1854/5040=103/280\) |
| \(1\) | \(1855/5040=53/144\) |
| \(2\) | \(924/5040=11/60\) |
| \(3\) | \(315/5040=1/16\) |
| \(4\) | \(70/5040=1/72\) |
| \(5\) | \(21/5040=1/240\) |
| \(6\) | \(0\) |
| \(7\) | \(1/5040\) |

In particular, the rational-point locus of \(C_p\) contains no singular point for a set of primes of density \(103/280\). This is a statement about rational singular points, **not** geometric smoothness over an algebraic closure.

## Assumptions and scope

All primes here are rational primes \(p\ge5\). Let \(C_p\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb F_p}\) have homogeneous equation
\[
 F_p=X^2(X^pY-XY^p)+Y^2(Y^pZ-YZ^p)+(Z^2+X^2)(Z^pX-ZX^p)=0.
\]
Every \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational projective point lies on \(C_p\), since each Frobenius binomial vanishes there. We count singular projective points whose three homogeneous coordinates are rational over \(\mathbb F_p\). The **natural density** of a set of primes means the limit of its counting function divided by the number of primes up to \(x\), as \(x\to\infty\).

## Proof

Define the homogeneous cubics
\[
 A=X^2Y-Z^3-X^2Z,\quad B=Y^2Z-X^3,\quad C=XZ^2+X^3-Y^3.
\]
Direct expansion yields the exact polynomial identities
\[
 F_p=AX^p+BY^p+CZ^p,\qquad XA+YB+ZC=0.
\]
At an \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational projective point \(P\), Frobenius gives \((X^p,Y^p,Z^p)=(X,Y,Z)\). Differentiating the first identity in characteristic \(p\), and then differentiating the second identity, shows
\[
 (\partial_XF_p,\partial_YF_p,\partial_ZF_p)(P)=-(A,B,C)(P).
\]
Consequently \(P\) is singular if and only if \(A(P)=B(P)=C(P)=0\). These three equations have no point on any coordinate axis: if \(X=0\), then \(A=-Z^3\) forces \(Z=0\) and \(C=-Y^3\) forces \(Y=0\); if \(Y=0\), then \(B=-X^3\) forces \(X=0\); if \(Z=0\), then \(B=-X^3\) forces \(X=0\). Normalize \(Y=1\), write \(X=u\). The equation \(B=0\) forces \(Z=u^3\); substitution in \(C\) gives \(u^7+u^3-1=0\), and then \(A=u^2(1-u^7-u^3)=0\). Thus the rational singular points are in bijection with roots of \(h(T)=T^7+T^3-1\) through \(u\mapsto[u:1:u^3]\). This bijection also holds at the finitely many primes dividing the discriminant.

To determine the number-field Galois group, reduce \(h\) modulo \(2\). The complete monic irreducible polynomials of degrees at most three over \(\mathbb F_2\) are
\[
 T,\ T+1,\ T^2+T+1,\ T^3+T+1,\ T^3+T^2+1.
\]
The respective remainders on division into \(h\) are \(1,1,T,T+1,T^2+1\), all nonzero. A reducible polynomial of degree seven has a factor of degree at most three, so \(h\) is irreducible modulo \(2\), and hence irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Its degree-seven Galois group is thus transitive and, because seven is prime, primitive.

A second exact finite-field factorization is
\[
 h(T)\equiv(T+123)(T+135)(T+150)(T+163)(T+196)(T^2+167T+111)\pmod{467}.
\]
The five linear factors are pairwise distinct and the quadratic discriminant is a nonsquare modulo \(467\). This squarefree factorization has cycle type \((2,1,1,1,1,1)\), so by the Frobenius factorization theorem the Galois group contains a transposition. A primitive permutation group containing a transposition is the full symmetric group: the normal subgroup generated by conjugates of that transposition has edge-components forming an invariant partition, which is connected by primitivity, and connected graph transpositions generate the full symmetric group. Hence the group is \(S_7\).

Outside finitely many ramified primes, the count \(N_p\) is the number of fixed points of Frobenius acting on the seven roots. By Chebotarev's density theorem, conjugacy-invariant conditions on Frobenius have density equal to their proportion in \(S_7\). A permutation on seven letters with exactly \(j\) fixed points is specified by selecting those \(j\) letters and deranging the other \(7-j\) letters. The number is \(\binom7jD_{7-j}\), with \(D_0=1\), \(D_1=0\), and \(D_n=(n-1)(D_{n-1}+D_{n-2})\). The eight counts are exactly \(1854,1855,924,315,70,21,0,1\), which sum to \(7!=5040\); this proves the table and the density \(1854/5040=103/280\).

## Verification

The attached self-contained `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It performs the exact polynomial divisions modulo \(2\), the exact factorization and nonsquare test modulo \(467\), the derangement count and density calculations, and an exhaustive check of all projective \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational singular points for thirteen sample primes from \(5\) to \(47\). These finite checks support the identities; the proof of the all-primes density is the group-theoretic and Chebotarev argument above, **not** finite sampling. Execute with `python3 verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca's *Plane-filling curves of small degree over finite fields* (arXiv:2307.03072, first submitted 6 July 2023), notably its construction and smoothness/root criteria, motivates and supplies the family. Their existence results allow parameters to depend on the finite field. The present statement instead fixes the same parameter throughout varying prime characteristics and computes the entire distribution of rational singularities, including the exact density of the root-free condition. Their *Tangent-filling plane curves over finite fields* (arXiv:2302.13420) studies a different tangent-filling family. The factorization and Chebotarev calculation here are explicitly supplied rather than attributed to those sources.

## Limitations

The conclusion concerns **singular points rational over \(\mathbb F_p\)**. It does not assert that the curve is geometrically smooth, or exclude singularities over higher extensions; no such conclusion follows from the density calculation alone. The finite-field tests are not a substitute for the all-primes proof. The novelty comparison cannot exclude unindexed prior Galois computations or arithmetic-density reformulations, and no independent review has been performed.
