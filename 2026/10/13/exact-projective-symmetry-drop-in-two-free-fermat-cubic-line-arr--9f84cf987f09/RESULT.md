# Exact projective symmetry drop in two free Fermat cubic-line arrangements
## Finding
Let \(\omega\) be a primitive cube root of unity. Consider the reduced plane curves
\[
D_6=V\!\left((x^3+y^3+z^3)(x^3+y^3)\right)
\]
and
\[
D_7=V\!\left((x^3+y^3+z^3)(x^3+y^3)(y+z)\right)
\]
over \(\mathbb C\). The first is the degree-six Fermat cubic plus three concurrent inflectional tangents, and the second is the degree-seven arrangement obtained by adding the inflectional tangent \(y+z=0\).

Their full embedded projective stabilizers are
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_3}(D_6)\cong C_3\times S_3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_3}(D_7)\cong C_3.
\]
In particular, the stabilizer orders are \(18\) and \(3\), respectively. Every element stabilizing \(D_6\) has, up to a common scalar, one of the forms
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[\omega^a x:\omega^b y:\omega^c z]
\]
or
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[\omega^a y:\omega^b x:\omega^c z],
\]
with \(a,b,c\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). The central \(C_3\) fixes all three line components individually, while the quotient \(S_3\) gives their full permutation group. For \(D_7\), the only surviving transformations are generated by
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[\omega x:y:z].
\]
Thus adding a single inflectional tangent produces an exact factor-six projective-symmetry drop while the arrangement remains free.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties and projective automorphisms are over \(\mathbb C\). The stabilizer is the setwise stabilizer of the reduced embedded divisor in \(\mathbb P^2\), not the abstract automorphism group of a normalization or complement. The result concerns exactly the two displayed equations. No statement is made for arbitrary choices of three or four flex tangents on a smooth cubic.

## Proof
Write
\[
F=x^3+y^3+z^3,\qquad G=x^3+y^3.
\]
For \(D_6=V(FG)\), the cubic component \(V(F)\) is the unique irreducible component of degree three, so every projective stabilizer preserves it and therefore also preserves the union \(V(G)\) of the three line components. Those three lines have the unique common point \(P=[0:0:1]\), hence \(P\) is fixed.

A projective transformation fixing \(P\) can be represented as
\[
(x,y,z)\longmapsto\bigl(A(x,y),\ell(x,y)+\mu z\bigr),
\]
where \(A\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb C)\), \(\mu\ne0\), and \(\ell\) is linear. Preservation of the three-line divisor gives \(G(A(x,y))=\lambda G(x,y)\) for some \(\lambda\ne0\). Preservation of the Fermat cubic gives
\[
G(A(x,y))+\bigl(\ell(x,y)+\mu z\bigr)^3
=\kappa\bigl(G(x,y)+z^3\bigr).
\]
The coefficient of \(z^2\) forces \(\ell=0\), after which comparison gives \(\lambda=\kappa=\mu^3\).

The three roots of the binary cubic \(G\) form a three-point subset of \(\mathbb P^1\). Its projective stabilizer is the full \(S_3\), generated in the coordinate \(t=x/y\) by \(t\mapsto\omega t\) and \(t\mapsto1/t\). Therefore every lift to \(\mathbb P^2\) is represented by one of the two displayed monomial forms. Modulo simultaneous addition of the same exponent to \(a,b,c\), there are \(9\) diagonal and \(9\) anti-diagonal elements.

The diagonal subgroup is \(C_3^2\). In exponent coordinates relative to the \(z\)-coordinate, swapping \(x\) and \(y\) fixes the diagonal line and negates the anti-diagonal line. Hence
\[
C_3^2\rtimes C_2\cong C_3\times(C_3\rtimes C_2)\cong C_3\times S_3.
\]
The central factor is represented by simultaneous scaling of \(x\) and \(y\) relative to \(z\); it fixes every line in \(V(G)\). The complementary \(S_3\) acts faithfully on the three lines.

For \(D_7\), the four line components contain exactly three lines through \(P\); the fourth line \(L=V(y+z)\) is the unique line component not through \(P\). Thus every stabilizer of \(D_7\) fixes \(L\) and lies in the stabilizer of \(D_6\). For a diagonal element, preservation of \(L\) is equivalent to equality of the exponents on \(y\) and \(z\). No anti-diagonal element preserves \(L\). After dividing by a common scalar, the surviving elements are exactly
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[\omega^a x:y:z],\qquad a\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,
\]
which proves the second assertion.

## Verification
The standalone verifier enumerates the \(18\) projective monomial normal forms, checks their action on the three factors of \(x^3+y^3\), identifies the order-three componentwise kernel and the six-element permutation quotient, verifies the direct-product decomposition, and checks that imposing preservation of \(y+z=0\) leaves exactly three elements and excludes every anti-diagonal form. The upper-bound argument in the proof is symbolic and does not rely on the finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Talar gives the exact degree-six and degree-seven equations above and proves their freeness, with the degree-six arrangement having \(\operatorname{mdr}=2\) and the degree-seven arrangement having \(\operatorname{mdr}=3\). The article does not state their projective automorphism groups, and full-text checks found no use of the terms “automorphism”, “symmetry”, or “stabilizer”. Dimca--Ilardi--Pokora--Sticlaru independently place the degree-six equation in the general construction obtained from a Fermat curve by adding one concurrent family of inflectional tangents; their broader construction likewise does not determine the embedded projective stabilizer. The present result is the exact isotropy calculation for these displayed divisors, together with the symmetry drop caused by Talar’s additional tangent.

## Limitations
The calculation is over \(\mathbb C\) and concerns embedded projective symmetries. It does not classify abstract automorphisms of the complements, braid monodromy, or stabilizers of arbitrary flex-line subsets. An older treatment of the same configurations in a projectively equivalent normal form could contain an equivalent subgroup computation without using the searched terminology.
