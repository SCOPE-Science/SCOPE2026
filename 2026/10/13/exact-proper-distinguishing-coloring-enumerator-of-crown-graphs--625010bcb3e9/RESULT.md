# Exact proper distinguishing-coloring enumerator of crown graphs and the mixed-palette threshold

## Finding

Let \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}-\{a_i b_i:1\le i\le n\}\), with \(n\ge3\). Let \(P_n(q)\) count *proper distinguishing colorings* \(\kappa:V(\operatorname{Cr}_n)\to[q]\), with colors labeled, unused colors permitted, and every automorphism required to preserve colors individually. For nonnegative integers \(a,b,r\), put
\[
U(a,b;r)=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\sum_{j=0}^{b}(-1)^{a+b-i-j}\binom ai\binom bj(ij)_{\underline r},
\]
where \((x)_{\underline r}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1)\), with value \(1\) for \(r=0\). Empty sums have value \(0\). Then, for every \(n\ge3\) and nonnegative integer \(q\),
\[
\boxed{P_n(q)=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\binom nt(q)_{\underline t}
\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge1\\a+b\le q-t}}
\frac{(q-t)!}{a!\,b!\,(q-t-a-b)!}\,U(a,b;n-t).}
\]
The summand indexed by \(t\) counts colorings with exactly \(t\) distinct colors occurring on both bipartition classes.

Write \(k=\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil\), the *previously known* distinguishing chromatic number of this graph. For every \(n\ge4\), a proper distinguishing coloring with \(k\) available colors can have a color occurring on both sides if and only if
\[
\boxed{n=\left\lfloor\frac{(k-1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor+1.}
\]
In this exceptional case such a coloring has exactly one cross-side color, and its number among all labeled \(k\)-palette colorings is
\[
\boxed{M_n=k!\,n!\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge1\\a+b=k-1\\ab=n-1}}\frac1{a!\,b!}.}
\]
Otherwise \(M_n=0\). For example, \(M_5=3600\), \(M_7=604800\); the exceptional \(n=3\) allows two shared colors and has \(P_3(4)=408\), of which \(192,144,72\) use respectively \(0,1,2\) shared colors.

## Assumptions and scope

Graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The crown graph has two identified sides \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) and \(B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\); every \(a_i b_j\) with \(i\ne j\) is an edge. A coloring is **proper** if endpoints of every edge have different colors. It is **distinguishing** if the only graph automorphism fixing all vertex colors is the identity. Palette labels are fixed, not identified modulo permutations. The theorem concerns \(n\ge3\) and integers \(q\ge0\), with \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). Neither the known minimum palette size nor the general notion of distinguishing coloring is presented as new.

## Proof

**Automorphism and color structure.** For \(n\ge3\), the connected bipartite graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) has automorphism group generated by simultaneous permutations \(a_i\mapsto a_{\pi(i)},b_i\mapsto b_{\pi(i)}\), \(\pi\in S_n\), and global interchange of its sides. Because each \(a_i\) has exactly one nonneighbor on the opposite side, \(b_i\), these are all automorphisms. Write \(\alpha_i=\kappa(a_i)\) and \(\beta_i=\kappa(b_i)\). By properness, any color used on both sides can appear only as \(\alpha_i=\beta_i\) for one index \(i\); in particular these diagonal colors are pairwise distinct and appear nowhere else. Other colors occur exclusively on one side. Distinct matched indices with the same ordered color pair induce a nonidentity simultaneous transposition preserving the coloring. Conversely, if all pairs \((\alpha_i,\beta_i)\) are distinct, no nonidentity side-preserving automorphism fixes the colors. If at least one pair is nondiagonal, its first color is exclusive to side \(A\) and its second exclusive to \(B\); a side-interchanging automorphism cannot preserve the first color. If every pair is diagonal, global side exchange preserves the coloring. Thus the coloring is proper and distinguishing precisely when the ordered pairs are pairwise distinct and at least one is nondiagonal (together with the stated properness condition).

**Exact enumeration.** Fix the number \(t\) of diagonal pairs. Choose their indices in \(\binom nt\) ways and assign distinct shared colors in \((q)_{\underline t}\) ways. There are \(r=n-t\ge1\) remaining matched pairs. Their first and second coordinates must use disjoint palettes of \(a\ge1\) and \(b\ge1\) exclusive colors. Choosing these ordered palettes from the \(q-t\) unused colors gives \((q-t)!/[a!b!(q-t-a-b)!]\) possibilities. Every remaining index must receive a distinct cell from the \(a\)-by-\(b\) rectangle. Using every selected row and column is enforced by inclusion-exclusion: \(U(a,b;r)\) is exactly the number of injective assignments of these \(r\) labeled indices to rectangle cells with no unused row or column. This gives the formula, with no overcount because the number of shared colors and the two used exclusive palettes are uniquely determined by the coloring.

**Mixed-color optimal palette.** Suppose \(k=\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil\), \(n\ge4\), and \(t\ge1\). Necessarily
\[
n-t\le ab\le\left\lfloor\frac{(k-t)^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]
Since \(\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil=k\), we have \(n\ge \lfloor(k-1)^2/4\rfloor+1\). For \(k\ge5\), the function \(t+\lfloor(k-t)^2/4\rfloor\) over integers \(1\le t\le k-2\) has its maximum at \(t=1\) or the endpoint \(t=k-2\); the latter is \(k-1\), strictly below \(\lfloor(k-1)^2/4\rfloor+1\) for \(k\ge5\), except \(k=5\) where it is \(4<5\). Moreover, for \(2\le t\le k-2\), the expression is strictly below the \(t=1\) value (directly check \(k=5,6\), and use the decreasing difference thereafter). For \(k=4\), the only permissible \(n\ge4\) is \(n=4\), while any \(t\ge1\) has capacity at most \(3\). Therefore mixed optimal colorings require \(t=1\) and \(n=1+\lfloor(k-1)^2/4\rfloor\). Equality then forces \(a+b=k-1\), \(ab=n-1\), so \(a,b\) must be balanced, and \(U(a,b;n-1)=(n-1)!\): every cell is occupied exactly once. In the general formula the corresponding term becomes \(\binom n1 k\,(k-1)!\,(n-1)!/(a!b!)=k!n!/(a!b!)\). Summing all admissible ordered \((a,b)\) proves the stated count. Conversely, assign all cells bijectively and choose the single diagonal index and shared color to construct such a coloring.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` uses only Python standard library. Running `python verify.py` produces:

`VERIFY_OK exhaustive_orders=3,4 palette_ranges=0..5 assignments= 483494 proper_checked= 37328 threshold_orders=4..23 order3_optimal=408 order5_mixed_optimal=3600 order7_mixed_optimal=604800`

For \(n\in\{3,4\}\) and every \(0\le q\le5\), all \(q^{2n}\) labeled assignments are examined. Properness is tested from the graph edges and distinguishingness is tested against all \(2n!\) automorphisms, without using the ordered-pair criterion as an oracle. The program compares the entire shared-color distribution against the formula. For \(4\le n\le23\), independent arithmetic checks test the optimal-palette threshold and the mixed-color count. Exhaustive finite checks corroborate but do not replace the combinatorial proof for arbitrary \(n,q\).

## Relationship to prior work

Laflamme and Seyffarth (2009), Theorem 1 and its proof in Section 2, already establish \(\chi_D(\operatorname{Cr}_n)=\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil\), identify repeated ordered-pair obstruction, and show that an optimal coloring can be chosen with disjoint color palettes on the two sides. Those results are used as background. The present formula instead enumerates **all** labeled proper distinguishing colorings, including those with shared colors, and identifies exactly when such shared colors occur among minimum-palette colorings. It is not a new proof of the known chromatic threshold. Cavers and Seyffarth (2013) concern graphs with large distinguishing chromatic number, not complete palette enumerators for crowns. Fisher and Isaak (2006) study identity edge colorings of complete bipartite graphs and their associated matrix automorphisms, chiefly deciding existence; their statements do not supply a count of labeled proper vertex colorings of crowns. A later study of the ordinary distinguishing number of crown graphs treats the nonproper parameter, and hence does not imply this proper-coloring count.

## Limitations

The result is an elementary, specialized enumeration of the family \(K_{n,n}-M\); it does not determine comparable polynomials for general bipartite graphs or prove a new bound on the already-known distinguishing chromatic number. A differently indexed earlier exact palette enumeration may exist; no universal novelty certification is asserted. The exception \(n=3\) is outside the sharp mixed-color-optimal criterion and is recorded separately. The formula counts *labeled* colors with unused labels permitted, not classes modulo color renaming. Independent review is not claimed.
