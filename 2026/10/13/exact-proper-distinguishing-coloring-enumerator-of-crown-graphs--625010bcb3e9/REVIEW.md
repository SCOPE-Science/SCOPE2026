# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)

For the connected crown graph of order \(2n\) with \(n\ge3\), the automorphism group is precisely simultaneous index permutations and the optional exchange of sides. The color-sharing rule follows immediately from adjacency. An injective list of ordered matched-pair colors removes all side-preserving symmetries; the presence of a nondiagonal pair removes side-exchange symmetry. Inclusion-exclusion over occupied exclusive palette rows and columns is an exact finite combinatorial bijection with no unnamed color-renaming quotient. The minimum-palette mixed-color threshold follows from the sharp product capacity and the known minimum palette size. Exhaustive independent automorphism tests for \(n=3,4\) and \(0\le q\le5\) match the formula. The mathematical argument, not enumeration alone, covers arbitrary \(n\ge3,q\ge0\).

## Originality (PASS with residual risk)

The most relevant predecessor is Laflamme–Seyffarth, *Distinguishing Chromatic Numbers of Bipartite Graphs*, 2009, especially Section 2. Its Theorem 1 establishes the minimum number \(\lceil2\sqrt n\rceil\), recognizes pair repetitions as obstructions, and eliminates shared colors in one minimum-color construction; the article does not state or evaluate the complete count of all labeled proper distinguishing colorings, its shared-color refinement, or the exact mixed-optimal count. Thus the minimum palette number and basic obstruction are *not* claimed as new, while the evaluated bivariate refinement is not supplied by the known statement. Fisher–Isaak (2006) analyze identity edge-colorings of complete bipartite graphs through arrays under permutations of rows and columns; that existence theory is not a count of properly coloring the vertices of a crown with fixed palette labels. Cavers–Seyffarth (2013) classify high distinguishing chromatic number; the 2026 crown study concerns ordinary, nonproper distinguishing number. The residual risk is an earlier equivalent counting result indexed under symmetry-breaking colorings, and the formula is a relatively direct but nontrivial enumeration from prior structure. Absence in searched sources is not proof of first discovery.

## Value (PASS)

The known extremal parameter alone does not quantify the number or diversity of feasible symmetry-breaking proper colorings. The formula gives *all palette sizes* and resolves the seemingly paradoxical fact that although a minimum coloring can always avoid cross-side colors, other minimum colorings sometimes require one such color by their chosen assignment pattern. It identifies exactly when that phenomenon is possible and provides full exact labeled counts. These are natural enumerative invariants of a standard highly symmetric graph family, with a transparent finite proof and a standalone independent checker; they are not arbitrary parameter slices or repetitions of the old chromatic bound.

## Limits and risk

The result is restricted to the crown family, with labeled available colors and no quotient by palette permutations. Nonproper distinguishing enumerations and the known minimum threshold are not results of this work. A complete earlier enumeration under different terminology remains possible, particularly in the vicinity of the 2009 construction; the full 2009 Section 2 was inspected for substantive implication rather than just title matching. Independent external mathematical validation has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
