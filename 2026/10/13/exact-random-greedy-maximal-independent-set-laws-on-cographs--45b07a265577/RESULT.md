# Exact random-greedy maximal-independent-set laws on cographs
## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite cograph. Choose a uniformly random ordering of \(V(G)\) and scan the vertices in that order, accepting a vertex exactly when it has no previously accepted neighbor. The terminal set is a maximal independent set.

Fix any cotree of \(G\). For a cotree node \(x\), let \(G_x\) be the induced cograph represented below \(x\), let \(n_x=|V(G_x)|\), let \(m_x\) be the number of maximal independent sets of \(G_x\), and let \(p_x(I)\) denote the probability that the random greedy process outputs a particular maximal independent set \(I\) of \(G_x\). Then the entire output law is determined by the following exact recurrences.

For a leaf, \(n_x=m_x=1\) and the unique maximal independent set has probability \(1\).

For a disjoint-union node with children \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\),
\[
m_x=\prod_{i=1}^s m_{x_i},
\qquad
p_x(I)=\prod_{i=1}^s p_{x_i}(I_i),
\]
where \(I=I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_s\) is the unique decomposition of a maximal independent set into maximal independent sets of the child graphs.

For a join node with children \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\),
\[
m_x=\sum_{i=1}^s m_{x_i},
\]
and every maximal independent set is contained in exactly one child. If \(I\) is a maximal independent set of child \(x_i\), then
\[
p_x(I)=\frac{{n_{x_i}}}{{n_x}}\,p_{x_i}(I).
\]

Consequently, the greedy output law on \(G_x\) is uniform over all maximal independent sets if and only if the following recursive criterion holds:

- a leaf is uniform;
- a disjoint-union node is uniform exactly when all its children are uniform;
- a join node is uniform exactly when all its children are uniform and
\[
\frac{{n_{x_1}}}{{m_{x_1}}}=\frac{{n_{x_2}}}{{m_{x_2}}}=\cdots=\frac{{n_{x_s}}}{{m_{x_s}}}.
\]

Thus a cotree gives both the exact terminal distribution and a bottom-up exact-uniformity test. Given a cotree, the latter needs only \(n_x\), \(m_x\), and one Boolean flag per node; aside from the bit complexity of the integer counts, it uses linear many cotree operations and never enumerates maximal independent sets.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. A cograph is a graph obtained from single vertices by repeated disjoint union and join; equivalently, it is an induced-\(P_4\)-free graph. A join of graphs adds every edge between distinct joined factors. A cotree records these two operations. The theorem concerns the distribution generated by a uniformly random vertex order, not an arbitrary distribution on priorities and not adaptive greedy rules.

The term “uniform” means that every maximal independent set of the graph is produced with the same probability. The theorem does not assert that those maximal independent sets have the same cardinality.

## Proof
Assign every vertex an independent continuous priority and inspect vertices in increasing priority order. This is equivalent to a uniformly random permutation.

At a disjoint-union node, the greedy decisions in one child graph never depend on vertices of another child. The relative priority orders induced on the disjoint child vertex sets are independent and uniform. Hence the terminal maximal independent set is the union of the child outputs, the maximal-independent-set count multiplies, and the output probabilities multiply. If the product distribution is uniform, fixing all child outputs except one shows that the remaining child distribution must itself be uniform; the converse is immediate.

At a join node, every vertex of one child is adjacent to every vertex of every other child. The globally earliest vertex is accepted. If it lies in child \(x_i\), it permanently blocks every vertex outside that child, so all later accepted vertices lie in \(G_{x_i}\). The probability that the global earliest vertex lies in \(x_i\) is \(n_{x_i}/n_x\). Conditional on that event, the relative vertex order inside \(x_i\) is still uniform: for each fixed relative ordering of the child, the same number of global interleavings place the global minimum in that child. Therefore the conditional terminal law is exactly the ordinary random-greedy law on \(G_{x_i}\), proving
\[
p_x(I)=\frac{{n_{x_i}}}{{n_x}}p_{x_i}(I).
\]
Since a maximal independent set in a join is a maximal independent set of exactly one child, the counts add.

Now suppose the join-node law is uniform. Within any one child, the multiplicative factor \(n_{x_i}/n_x\) is constant, so that child's law must be uniform. Its individual output probability is then \(1/m_{x_i}\), and equality of the global probabilities for outputs from two children is equivalent to
\[
\frac{{n_{x_i}}}{{m_{x_i}}}=\frac{{n_{x_j}}}{{m_{x_j}}}.
\]
Conversely, uniform child laws together with equality of these ratios make every global maximal independent set have the same probability. Induction up the cotree proves all assertions.

## Verification
The bundled `artifacts/verify.py` is an exact finite stress test independent of the symbolic induction. It enumerates every labeled graph through order \(6\), recognizes cographs recursively through graph/complement connectivity, computes the cotree law with exact rational arithmetic, and independently enumerates every vertex permutation to obtain the brute-force greedy law. For every cograph it checks equality of the two distributions and equality between the recursive uniformity criterion and direct equality of all output probabilities.

The replay checks \(6039\) labeled cographs and \(4020821\) vertex permutations. The cograph counts by order are \(1,2,8,52,472,5504\), providing an internal census check. Finite enumeration is corroborative only; the universal claim follows from the cotree induction above.

## Relationship to prior work
Fairfax-Ball's 2026 preprint studies the same random-order greedy maximal-independent-set law on trees, proves that exact uniformity among trees occurs only for the one-vertex tree and the single edge, and explicitly identifies classification beyond trees as a natural open direction. The present theorem answers that direction on cographs, a canonical hereditary class with a cotree decomposition.

Krivelevich, Mészáros, Michaeli, and Shikhelman develop a broad local-limit framework for asymptotic greedy independent-set densities. Their framework studies asymptotic density and does not supply the exact finite terminal law or the cograph uniformity criterion above.

Corneil, Perl, and Stewart record the unique cotree representation of cographs and a linear-time recognition/construction algorithm. That structural machinery supplies the natural decomposition used here but does not address random greedy output laws.

Focused published-finding corpus searches found three especially close published records: an exact random-greedy law on threshold graphs, an exact law on chain graphs, and a variance theorem for complete multipartite graphs. The threshold-graph result is a special subclass case; the complete-multipartite result is a special cograph family and concerns an aggregate statistic rather than the full cograph law. Chain graphs are not a covering class for cographs. None of these results implies the union/join recursion and uniformity criterion for all cographs.

## Limitations
This result is confined to cographs. It does not classify exact greedy uniformity for all graphs, distance-hereditary graphs, or all modular-decomposition classes. The law assumes a uniformly random permutation. The algorithmic statement counts arithmetic operations on cotree nodes; the integers \(m_x\) can have nonconstant bit length.

The principal originality risk is terminological or folkloric: the two recurrences are natural consequences of disjoint union and join, so an equivalent statement could be implicit in modular-decomposition or random-priority literature under different language. Focused searches under cograph, cotree, join/union, terminal-law, permutation-fibre, and uniformity formulations did not locate such a theorem.
