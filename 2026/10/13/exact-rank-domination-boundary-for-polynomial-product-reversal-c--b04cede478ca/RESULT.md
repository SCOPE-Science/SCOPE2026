# Exact rank-domination boundary for polynomial product-reversal compositions
## Finding
Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\). Let \(p,q\in K[t]\) be nonzero, with \(p\) nonconstant, and define
\[
f=p(xy),\qquad g=q(yx)\in K\langle x,y\rangle.
\]
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1. There exists a constant \(C>0\) such that for every \(n\ge 1\) and every \(X,Y\in M_n(K)\),
\[
\operatorname{rank} q(YX)\le C\,\operatorname{rank} p(XY).
\]
2. The same inequality holds with \(C=1\).
3. One has \(p\mid q\), and in addition either \(p(0)\ne0\) or \(tp\mid q\).

Under these equivalent conditions,
\[
q(yx)\in (p(xy))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
tp\mid q.
\]
Consequently, within this two-polynomial product-reversal ansatz, the rank-domination condition fails to detect two-sided ideal membership exactly when
\[
p(0)\ne0,\qquad p\mid q,\qquad q(0)\ne0.
\]
The counterexample \(p(t)=q(t)=t-1\) is the first member of this complete family.

## Assumptions and scope
The algebra \(K\langle x,y\rangle\) is the free associative algebra. The ideal \((p(xy))\) is the two-sided ideal generated by \(p(xy)\). Matrix rank is ordinary rank over \(K\). The characteristic-zero assumption matches the setting of the motivating rank-Nullstellensatz literature. The proof of the rank-domination classification itself only uses algebraic closedness for the divisibility argument; the sufficient directions and the explicit witnesses are characteristic-free whenever the required roots lie in the field.

## Proof
Assume first that there is some \(C>0\) with the stated rank domination. Put \(X=I_n\) and \(Y=T\). Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}q(T)\le C\,\operatorname{rank}p(T)
\]
for every square matrix \(T\).

Let \(\alpha\in K\) be a root of \(p\) of multiplicity \(a\). Take \(T=J_a(\alpha)\), a single Jordan block. Since \(p(T)=0\), the rank inequality forces \(q(T)=0\). Writing \(q(t)=(t-\alpha)^b h(t)\) with \(h(\alpha)\ne0\), the matrix \(h(T)\) is invertible, so \(q(T)=0\) implies \(b\ge a\). Thus every root of \(p\) occurs in \(q\) with at least the same multiplicity. Since \(K\) is algebraically closed,
\[
p\mid q.
\]

Suppose now that \(p(0)=0\). Write
\[
p(t)=t^a u(t),\qquad q(t)=t^b v(t),
\]
where \(a\ge1\), \(b\ge a\), and \(u(0)v(0)\ne0\). If \(b=a\), take \(n=a+1\), let \(J=J_{a+1}(0)\), let
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,0),
\]
and set \(X=J\), \(Y=D\). Then \(XY=JD\) is \(J_a(0)\oplus0\), while \(YX=DJ=J_{a+1}(0)\). Therefore
\[
p(XY)=0,
\]
whereas
\[
q(YX)=J^a v(J)\ne0,
\]
because \(v(J)\) is invertible and \(J^a\ne0\). This contradicts rank domination. Hence \(b\ge a+1\), which is equivalent to \(tp\mid q\).

This proves necessity of condition 3.

For sufficiency, first suppose \(p(0)\ne0\) and write \(q=pr\). For any square \(X,Y\),
\[
Yp(XY)=p(YX)Y.
\]
If \(v\in\ker p(XY)\) and \(Yv=0\), then \(XYv=0\), hence \(p(XY)v=p(0)v\); therefore \(v=0\). Thus \(Y\) injects \(\ker p(XY)\) into \(\ker p(YX)\). Interchanging \(X\) and \(Y\) gives the reverse nullity inequality, so
\[
\operatorname{rank}p(XY)=\operatorname{rank}p(YX).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{rank}q(YX)
=\operatorname{rank}\bigl(p(YX)r(YX)\bigr)
\le\operatorname{rank}p(YX)
=\operatorname{rank}p(XY).
\]

Now suppose \(tp\mid q\), say \(q(t)=t\,p(t)r(t)\). In the free algebra,
\[
q(yx)=y\,p(xy)\,x\,r(yx),
\]
because \(y\,p(xy)\,x=(yx)p(yx)\). Hence both
\[
q(yx)\in(p(xy))
\]
and, after every matrix evaluation,
\[
\operatorname{rank}q(YX)\le\operatorname{rank}p(XY).
\]
Thus condition 3 implies condition 2, and condition 2 trivially implies condition 1.

It remains to classify ideal membership under rank domination. If \(tp\mid q\), the displayed factorization already proves membership. Conversely, assume rank domination holds but \(tp\nmid q\). The criterion already proved forces
\[
p(0)\ne0,\qquad p\mid q,\qquad q(0)\ne0.
\]
Because \(p\) is nonconstant and \(K\) is algebraically closed, choose a nonzero root \(\lambda\) of \(p\). On a countably infinite-dimensional \(K\)-vector space with basis \(e_1,e_2,\ldots\), let \(L\) and \(R\) be the left and right unilateral shifts, so
\[
LR=I,\qquad RL=I-P,
\]
where \(P\) is projection onto \(Ke_1\). Evaluate \(x\) at \(\lambda L\) and \(y\) at \(R\). Then
\[
p(xy)=p(\lambda I)=0,
\]
but
\[
q(yx)e_1=q(\lambda RL)e_1=q(0)e_1\ne0.
\]
Any element of the two-sided ideal generated by \(p(xy)\) vanishes under every algebra homomorphism that kills \(p(xy)\). Hence \(q(yx)\notin(p(xy))\). This proves the ideal-membership equivalence and the final counterexample classification.

## Verification
The proof has three independent certificates. First, root multiplicities are forced by evaluating on single Jordan blocks. Second, when the constant term of \(p\) vanishes, the pair \(X=J_{a+1}(0)\), \(Y=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,0)\) gives an explicit obstruction unless \(q\) has one additional zero factor. Third, the free-algebra identity
\[
t\,p(t)r(t)\big|_{t=yx}=y\,p(xy)\,x\,r(yx)
\]
proves the positive ideal-membership direction.

The accompanying `verify.py` exhaustively checks representative domination and failure cases over small prime fields and verifies the matrix identities used in the proof. These finite checks are consistency tests only; the theorem is proved symbolically above.

## Relationship to prior work
Yan and Yang give the counterexample
\[
f=xy-1,\qquad g=yx-1
\]
to Volčič's proposed rank Nullstellensatz, proving equality of the two ranks for every finite matrix size while showing \(g\notin(f)\). Their argument treats this single pair.

Shamovich and Volčič previously proved that equality of ranks of two free polynomials at all matrix tuples is equivalent to stable association, and they explicitly note rank-equivalence of \(xy+1\) and \(yx+1\). That result concerns equality rather than the one-sided rank-domination preorder classified here.

Lippert and Strang proved the classical product-reversal fact that for a polynomial \(h\) with \(h(0)\ne0\), the nullities of \(h(AB)\) and \(h(BA)\) agree, and they characterize the possible discrepancy at the zero eigenvalue. The present result uses exactly that boundary mechanism but combines it with polynomial divisibility and two-sided ideal membership to classify all rank-domination counterexamples of the form \(p(xy),q(yx)\).

## Limitations
The theorem classifies only the natural two-polynomial product-reversal family \(p(xy),q(yx)\); it does not classify arbitrary noncommutative generators and targets. Algebraic closedness is used to turn Jordan-block tests at all roots into polynomial divisibility and to obtain a nonzero root for the nonmembership witness. No claim is made about a finite matrix-size bound sufficient to test the universal rank inequality.
