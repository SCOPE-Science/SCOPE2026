The revised statement counts distinct positive solutions of the displayed scalar equation. Multiplying by the positive denominator is reversible. Exact signs of the quartic at four disjoint rational brackets give all its roots and their simplicity. The sign of the cubic discriminant, the exceptional factorization at \(b=1\), and the exclusion of negative and triple roots at the other endpoints give the global count.

The comparison with turning points is a mathematical distinction between two conditions: existence of stationary points of the profile and intersection of that profile with the line at \(a=6/5\). The example \(b=18\) distinguishes them. The re-entrant multiplicity intervals do not contradict eventual large-\(b\) multiplicity, and no claim of a contradiction with specific wording in a particular version of the original paper is needed.

The substantive result is the exact disconnected multiplicity set and its separation from the turning-point region. It does not extend to all parameter specializations, all Gibbs measures, extremality, or stability. Comparison with the exact theorem wording in the cited papers remains limited by access to their bodies.
