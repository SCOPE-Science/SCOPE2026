# Exact smooth spectra of affine pencil line arrangements
## Finding
Let \(P_n\subset\mathbf C^2\) be an affine pencil of \(n\ge2\) distinct complex lines: all \(n\) lines pass through one point, and there are no other singular points. For \(2\le k\le n\), let \(p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,k)\) denote the least prime threshold in Harris's Definition 3.10: \(P_n\) is \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^k\)-anti-admissible for every prime \(p\ge p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,k)\).

Then
\[
p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,2)=
\begin{cases}
2,& n\ \text{even},\\
3,& n\ \text{odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,k)=2\quad(3\le k\le n).
\]
Equivalently, the ordered smooth spectrum
\[
\bigl(p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,2),\ldots,p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,n)\bigr)
\]
is \((2,\ldots,2)\) for even \(n\), and \((3,2,\ldots,2)\) for odd \(n\).

This reverses the parity pattern of Harris's near-pencil example: for a near pencil of \(n\) lines, the source gives \((2,\ldots,2)\) when \(n\) is odd and \((3,2,\ldots,2)\) when \(n\) is even.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\) for the meridians. Since \(P_n\) is affine,
\[
H_1(\mathbf C^2\setminus P_n;\mathbf Z)\cong\mathbf Z^n.
\]
Fix a prime \(p\) and put \(V=\mathbf F_p^k\). If \(g_i=\phi(\mu_i)\) and
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^n g_i,
\]
then the pencil has only one singular point, so Harris's anti-admissibility condition is exactly:
the \(g_i\) span \(V\), and \(g_i\) and \(S\) are linearly independent for every \(i\).

No assertion is made about projective pencils, non-elementary-abelian target groups, or other incidence types.

## Proof
First take \(k=2\) and \(p=2\). The three nonzero vectors of \(\mathbf F_2^2\) are \(a,b,a+b\). If an anti-admissible map exists, then \(S\ne0\), and no \(g_i\) can lie in the one-dimensional subspace generated by \(S\). Hence all \(g_i\) are the other two nonzero vectors, say \(a\) and \(b\). Surjectivity forces both to occur. If their multiplicities are \(r\) and \(s\), then
\[
S=(r\bmod2)a+(s\bmod2)b.
\]
For \(S\) to be independent from both \(a\) and \(b\), it must equal \(a+b\). Thus both \(r\) and \(s\) are odd, so \(n=r+s\) is even. Conversely, when \(n\) is even, send one meridian to \(a\) and the other \(n-1\) meridians to \(b\). Then \(S=a+b\), so all required pairs are independent. Therefore \(P_n\) is \((\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^2\)-anti-admissible exactly when \(n\) is even.

Now let \(k=2\) and \(p\ge3\). Choose
\[
r=
\begin{cases}
1,& n\not\equiv1\pmod p,\\
2,& n\equiv1\pmod p.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(n\ge2\), this gives \(1\le r\le n-1\); in the second case \(n\ge4\). Send \(r\) meridians to \(e_1\) and the remaining \(n-r\) meridians to \(e_2\). The sum is
\[
S=r e_1+(n-r)e_2.
\]
Both coefficients are nonzero modulo \(p\): the first by \(r\in\{1,2\}\), and the second by the choice of \(r\). Hence \(S\) is independent from both \(e_1\) and \(e_2\), and the images span \(\mathbf F_p^2\). Thus every pencil is \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^2\)-anti-admissible for every prime \(p\ge3\). Combining with the characteristic-two argument gives the asserted value of \(p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,2)\).

Finally take \(3\le k\le n\) and any prime \(p\). Send the first \(k\) meridians to \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\), and every remaining meridian to \(e_1\). Then
\[
S=(n-k+1)e_1+e_2+\cdots+e_k.
\]
The \(e_2\)- and \(e_3\)-coordinates of \(S\) are both \(1\), so \(S\ne0\) and \(S\) is not a scalar multiple of any basis vector. Every image \(g_i\) is a basis vector, so every pair \(\{g_i,S\}\) is linearly independent, and the images span \(\mathbf F_p^k\). This works for every prime \(p\), proving \(p_{\mathbb A}(P_n,k)=2\).

## Verification
The proof was checked directly against Harris's Definition 2.3 and Definition 3.10. The unique singular point of a pencil reduces all local conditions to independence from the single sum \(S\). The characteristic-two necessity also agrees with Harris's Lemma 3.4, since an odd pencil has one odd-multiplicity point.

As a finite stress test of the explicit formulas, the displayed constructions were evaluated for \(2\le n\le14\), every \(2\le k\le n\), and primes \(2,3,5,7,11\); every claimed construction was surjective and satisfied the required independence condition. The proof itself is uniform and does not depend on this finite test.

## Relationship to prior work
Harris introduces the smooth spectrum and computes it for arrangements in general position and for near pencils. The source does not state the affine-pencil spectrum. Its Lemma 3.4 supplies the characteristic-two obstruction for odd multiplicity, and Lemma 3.6 shows anti-admissibility can be extended to larger target rank, but neither determines the exact pencil spectrum. The new point is the all-primes rank-two construction together with the uniform rank-\(k\) construction, which closes every entry of the spectrum.

Harris's 2025 thesis treats smooth branched covers for several projective arrangements, including near pencils, but the inspected relevant sections do not state the affine-pencil smooth spectrum above.

## Limitations
The result is deliberately restricted to affine pencils and elementary abelian targets. It does not classify spectra of general supersolvable arrangements or projective pencils. The argument is elementary once the new smooth-spectrum invariant is available; consequently, the main originality risk is an unindexed observation in the very recent anti-admissibility literature.
