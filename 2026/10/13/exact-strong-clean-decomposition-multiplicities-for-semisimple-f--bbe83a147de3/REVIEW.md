# Review

## Correctness
PASS. A strongly clean decomposition of \(A\) is equivalent to a commuting idempotent \(E\) with \(A-E\) invertible. Semisimplicity identifies the centralizer with a product of full matrix algebras over the extension fields \(\mathbb F_{q^{d_f}}\). The invertibility test forces exactly one idempotent on the \(t\)- and \(t-1\)-summands and permits every idempotent on all other summands. The rank-\(k\) idempotent count \({m\brack k}_Q Q^{k(m-k)}\) follows from choosing image and complementary kernel. The packaged finite checker reproduces the predicted counts in several split and nonsplit examples.

## Originality
PASS with residual literature risk. The inspected foundational and general matrix-ring papers concern existence and factorization criteria for strong cleanness; the triangular uniqueness paper concerns ring-wide uniqueness in a different class. Targeted database and web searches for exact decomposition multiplicities, commuting idempotents, semisimple centralizers, and uniqueness found no statement implying the product formula. The main residual risk is an equivalent count expressed in older finite-linear-group or centralizer language.

## Value
PASS. Strong cleanness is usually treated as an existence property. The formula upgrades that qualitative question to an exact multiplicity invariant on the natural semisimple stratum, handles nonsplit irreducible factors uniformly, and gives a sharp uniqueness characterization. This is a structural refinement rather than a parameter substitution or isolated numerical census.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest broad source is Borooah--Diesl--Dorsey, which supplies the invariant-summand characterization and extensive matrix-ring existence criteria. Chen--Yang--Zhou treats the \(2\times2\) local-ring case. Chen--Gurgun--Kose treats uniquely strongly clean triangular matrix rings. None of the inspected material states the semisimple finite-field multiplicity product. The result does not cover nonsemisimple matrices.
