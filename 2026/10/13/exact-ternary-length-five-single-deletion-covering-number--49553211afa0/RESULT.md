# Exact ternary length-five single-deletion covering number

## Finding

The minimum number of ternary words of length five whose one-deletion descendants cover **every** ternary word of length four is
\[
D(3,5,1)=21.
\]
More precisely, there is a set of 21 centers whose distinct one-deletion shadows *partition* the 81 length-four words. Conversely, an exact nonnegative integral shadow-weight certificate shows that every covering set has at least 21 centers.

## Assumptions and scope

Put \(\Sigma=\{0,1,2\}\). Words have ordered coordinates, and subsequences preserve their order. For \(v=v_1\cdots v_5\in\Sigma^5\), define its one-deletion shadow to be the **set**
\[
S(v)=\{v_1\cdots v_{i-1}v_{i+1}\cdots v_5:1\le i\le5\}\subseteq\Sigma^4.
\]
Repeated descendants arising from deleting different positions count only once. A deletion-covering code is a set \(C\subseteq\Sigma^5\) with \(\bigcup_{v\in C}S(v)=\Sigma^4\), and \(D(3,5,1)\) is the minimum possible cardinality. This is **not** the minimum size of an insertion-covering code with length-five target words, nor the maximum size of a deletion-correcting code.

## Proof

**Upper bound: an explicit exact partition.** Use these seven length-five prototype words:

`00121`, `00200`, `01010`, `01022`, `01112`, `02120`, `02221`.

For each prototype \(b\), include the three words \(b+a\), where adding \(a\in\{0,1,2\}\) means adding the same symbol modulo three in *each* coordinate. All 21 resulting words are distinct.

To check coverage without a long incidence table, normalize any length-four word \(u\) by subtracting its first symbol modulo three from every coordinate; write \(\nu(u)\) for the resulting word beginning with zero. There are exactly 27 such normalized words, each representing an orbit of three words under common symbol translation. The distinct normalized one-deletion descendants of the seven prototypes are as follows:

| Prototype | Normalized one-deletion descendants |
|---|---|
| `00121` | `0011`, `0012`, `0021`, `0121` |
| `00200` | `0000`, `0020`, `0200` |
| `01010` | `0010`, `0100`, `0101`, `0110`, `0202` |
| `01022` | `0022`, `0102`, `0122`, `0211` |
| `01112` | `0001`, `0111`, `0112` |
| `02120` | `0120`, `0201`, `0210`, `0212`, `0220` |
| `02221` | `0002`, `0221`, `0222` |

These 27 normalized descendants are pairwise distinct and exhaust all words whose first symbol is zero. The deletion map commutes with common symbol translation. Thus the three translates of each prototype contribute exactly the corresponding orbits of length-four descendants, without any overlap. They partition \(\Sigma^4\), yielding \(D(3,5,1)\le21\). The 21 centers comprise 9 words with three distinct descendants, 6 with four, and 6 with five, giving \(9\cdot3+6\cdot4+6\cdot5=81\) distinct outputs.

**Lower bound: a ten-orbit integral dual certificate.** Consider the action on length-four words generated by arbitrary permutations of the ternary alphabet and reversal of coordinate order. Assign to each word \(u\) a nonnegative integer \(w(u)\) which is constant on each orbit, as specified in this full table. Representatives are lexicographically least in their orbits:

| Representative | Number of words | \(w(u)\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `0000` | 3 | 10 |
| `0001` | 12 | 8 |
| `0010` | 12 | 5 |
| `0011` | 6 | 7 |
| `0012` | 12 | 6 |
| `0101` | 6 | 3 |
| `0102` | 12 | 4 |
| `0110` | 6 | 7 |
| `0112` | 6 | 6 |
| `0120` | 6 | 4 |

The ten orbits are disjoint and contain all 81 words. The sum of their weights, counting all words in each orbit, is
\[
\sum_{u\in\Sigma^4}w(u)=468.
\]
We claim that **every** five-symbol word obeys
\[
\sum_{u\in S(v)}w(u)\le23.
\]
The group generated by alphabet permutations and reversal has 25 orbits on \(\Sigma^5\), and its action commutes with the deletion operation. Hence it suffices to check one canonical representative per orbit. The following complete table gives the sums for those representatives:

| Representative | Sum | Representative | Sum | Representative | Sum |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| `00000` | 10 | `00001` | 18 | `00010` | 23 |
| `00011` | 15 | `00012` | 22 | `00100` | 20 |
| `00101` | 23 | `00102` | 23 | `00110` | 19 |
| `00112` | 19 | `00120` | 20 | `00121` | 23 |
| `00122` | 19 | `01001` | 23 | `01002` | 23 |
| `01010` | 23 | `01012` | 23 | `01020` | 22 |
| `01021` | 21 | `01102` | 23 | `01110` | 23 |
| `01112` | 22 | `01120` | 23 | `01201` | 19 |
| `01210` | 23 | | | | |

Each displayed sum is computed over a *set* of distinct deleted words; the included standard-library verifier also exhausts all 243 five-symbol words directly. Therefore, for any deletion cover \(C\), nonnegativity of \(w\) and coverage imply
\[
468=\sum_{u\in\Sigma^4}w(u)
\le\sum_{v\in C}\sum_{u\in S(v)}w(u)
\le23|C|.
\]
Consequently \(|C|\ge\lceil468/23\rceil=21\), matching the construction. This proves the theorem.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It regenerates all ten length-four symmetry orbits (all 81 targets), verifies the integer weights and total weight 468, and checks the shadow-weight inequality for **each of the 243** potential length-five centers, including its 25-orbit consistency and exact score histogram. It independently expands the seven prototypes into 21 distinct words, verifies coverage of all 81 targets, and verifies the integer lower bound. Execute `python3 verify.py`; successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK` and the summary counts. The finite classification is exhaustive; no probabilistic computation, numerical optimizer, or unproved infinite extrapolation is used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Xie, Sun and Ge, *New bounds for covering codes under insertions or deletions* (arXiv:2606.15379v1, posted 13 June 2026), define \(D(q,n,R)\), prove asymptotic large-alphabet covering bounds, and develop nonbinary deletion-covering constructions; their results do not specify this fixed ternary length-five minimum. Lenz, Rashtchian, Siegel and Yaakobi, *Covering Codes using Insertions or Deletions* (arXiv:1911.09944), give general lower/upper bounds and construction methods without the value 21. Our use of an integral weighted covering certificate is an explicit finite specialization of standard hypergraph duality, not a claim to have invented linear-programming duality.

The constructed partition also makes the 21-word set a perfect single-deletion-*correcting* code. Chee, Ge and Ling, *Spectrum of Sizes for Perfect Deletion-Correcting Codes* (arXiv:1008.1343), study the different spectrum problem for one deletion at length three and two deletions at length four; those parameters do not imply the present minimum covering cardinality at length five. Afrati and collaborators' earlier terminology calls length-five codewords covering length-four targets an *insertion-covering* code; the definitions, rather than this terminological distinction, control the comparison. Their general constructions do not supply this exact certificate.

## Limitations

The claim is only for alphabet size three, five-symbol centers, and exactly one deletion. It neither classifies all optimum codes nor determines optimum deletion-correcting code *sizes* or longer-length covering numbers. Literature searches cannot exclude an unindexed earlier equivalent finite calculation; a historical thesis on perfect ternary deletion codes could not be examined in full. This is a finite exact certificate; it does not establish an asymptotic theorem. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
