# Exact ternary length-three \(\overline{3}\)-separable codes
## Finding
For ternary length-three \(\overline{3}\)-separable codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^3\), the exact maximum is \(|C|=6\). Exactly \(135\) labeled unordered codes attain equality. Under the natural group \((S_3)^3\rtimes S_3\) of independent symbol permutations in the three coordinates together with coordinate permutations, the maxima split into exactly two orbits of sizes \(27\) and \(108\), with stabilizer orders \(48\) and \(12\), respectively. A representative of the size-\(27\) orbit is \(\{000,001,012,022,102,202\}\); in every coordinate its symbol multiplicities are \((4,1,1)\), and each two-coordinate projection has multiplicities \((2,1,1,1,1)\). A representative of the size-\(108\) orbit is \(\{000,011,101,122,212,220\}\); in every coordinate its symbol multiplicities are \((2,2,2)\), and every two-coordinate projection consists of six distinct ordered pairs.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is \(Q=\{0,1,2\}\), and a code is an unordered subset \(C\subseteq Q^3\) with no repeated codeword. For a nonempty subset \(A\subseteq C\), its descendant is
\[
\operatorname{desc}(A)=A(1)\times A(2)\times A(3),
\]
where \(A(j)\) is the set of symbols appearing in coordinate \(j\). The code is \(\overline{3}\)-separable when distinct nonempty subsets \(A,B\subseteq C\) with \(|A|,|B|\leq 3\) always have different descendants. The equivalence group used here is the full natural Hamming-isometry group generated by an independent permutation of \(Q\) in each coordinate and by permutation of the three coordinates.

## Proof
Encode a descendant by the three nonzero three-bit masks recording its coordinate symbol sets. In a lexicographic depth-first enumeration, maintain the exact map from descendant signatures to all already selected subsets of sizes one, two, and three. When a new word \(v\) is added, the only new subsets are \(\{v\}\), the pairs \(\{u,v\}\), and the triples \(\{u,w,v\}\) with previously selected words \(u,w\). Therefore rejecting an extension precisely when one of these new signatures duplicates an old or another new signature is equivalent to rejecting exactly the non-\(\overline{3}\)-separable extensions; no heuristic pruning is used.

This exhaustive search visits every valid code in increasing-word order. Its largest depth is six and it produces exactly \(135\) distinct six-word codes. As a separate upper-bound check, every one-word extension of every one of those \(135\) six-word codes is tested directly from the definition; none is valid. Any seven-word valid code would contain a valid six-word subset, so this independently excludes size seven and proves the maximum is six.

Next enumerate all \(6^3\cdot 6=1296\) natural isometries. Applying every isometry to all \(135\) maxima preserves the census and gives exactly two orbits, of sizes \(27\) and \(108\). Orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(1296/27=48\) and \(1296/108=12\). Direct coordinate counts on every member of the two orbits give the stated multiplicity profiles and the displayed representatives.

## Relationship to prior work
Cheng, Jiang, Li, Miao, and Tang define \(\overline{3}\)-separable codes of length three and identify the exact binary case \(M(\overline{3},3,2)=3\). For \(q>2\) they record the older \(q^2\) upper bound, but their sharper theorem \(M(\overline{3},3,q)\leq\lfloor 3q^2/4\rfloor\) is explicitly stated only for \(q\geq 4\); thus the ternary boundary case is not settled by that theorem. Their full text also supplies a forbidden-configuration characterization, but it does not state the ternary optimum or an equality census.

Zhang, Jiang, and Cheng later prove that for length three and \(q\geq3\), \(\overline{3}\)-separability and strongly \(\overline{3}\)-separability are equivalent. Their inspected length-three analysis does not state the exact ternary optimum or classify its equality cases. Exact-parameter and alias searches using separable-code, strongly-separable-code, descendant-code, and multimedia-fingerprinting terminology did not locate a source implying the \(135\)-code census or the two natural-equivalence classes.

## Limitations
This is a finite classification only for alphabet size three and length three; it does not improve asymptotic bounds or the published \(q\geq4\) theory. The originality assessment is necessarily literature-bounded: an obscure or unindexed finite enumeration could exist, although no inspected exact-parameter source states or implies this classification. The computational proof is exhaustive for the stated finite domain and makes no claim for larger alphabets or lengths.
