# Exact three-coordinate asymmetric Lee codes and their two optimal geometries

## Finding

Let \(Q=\{00,01,10,11\}\), and give it the asymmetric Lee (ALD) distance at \(\lambda=1\) introduced by Gabrys, Kiah and Milenkovic. On distinct symbols the distance is one between \(01\) and \(10\), four between \(00\) and \(11\), and two for every other pair. For length-three words the distance is the sum of the three single-coordinate costs.

A code is a subset \(C\subseteq Q^3\) whose distinct words have distance at least three. Its sharp maximum size is
\[
A_1(3,3)=19.
\]
Exactly **32** labeled subsets attain this maximum. Under the group generated by permutations of the three coordinates and, independently in every coordinate, the symbol swaps \(00\leftrightarrow11\) and \(01\leftrightarrow10\), they form precisely **two orbits**, of sizes **8** and **24**, with stabilizers of orders **48** and **16**, respectively. Their distributions of unordered word-pair distances \(3,4,5,6,7,8\) are, respectively,
\[
(24,69,0,24,24,30),\qquad(28,61,4,24,24,30).
\]
These distributions distinguish the two orbits even without using a particular representative.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet symbols are represented by the integers \(0=00\), \(1=01\), \(2=10\), \(3=11\). The local distance matrix in this order is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&2&2&4\\2&0&1&2\\2&1&0&2\\4&2&2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All statements concern exactly three symbol positions, integer parameter \(\lambda=1\), and minimum distance at least three. The 32 codes are counted as distinct labeled subsets of the fixed alphabet-and-coordinate space, not modulo equivalence. The displayed symmetry group has order \(4^3\cdot3!=384\).

## Proof

**1. Reduction to a finite graph.** Let \(V=Q^3\), of cardinality 64. Construct the simple compatibility graph \(G\) on \(V\) by joining distinct words exactly when their ALD distance is at least three. A code with the prescribed minimum distance is precisely a clique of \(G\), and conversely. Direct evaluation of the displayed matrix yields exactly **1,752 edges**. Index a word \((s_0,s_1,s_2)\) by \(i=s_0+4s_1+16s_2\); a labeled code is then represented by the unsigned 64-bit integer whose bit \(i\) is one exactly when word \(i\) is included.

**2. Exhaustive maximum-clique certificate.** The standalone `verify_ald.py` independently computes the local distance directly from Equation (1) of the original paper (without relying on the displayed matrix), constructs all graph edges, and enumerates *every maximal clique* through Bron–Kerbosch recursion with exact pivoting. At a recursive state \((R,P,X)\), vertices in \(R\) form a clique, \(P\) are unprocessed potential extensions, and \(X\) are processed potential extensions. If both \(P\) and \(X\) are empty, \(R\) is maximal and is recorded. Otherwise a pivot \(u\in P\cup X\) is chosen, and each branch adds one vertex from \(P\setminus N(u)\). This restriction is exhaustive: any as-yet-unreported maximal clique extending \(R\) must contain a vertex outside \(N(u)\), or it could be extended by the pivot (and, when the pivot is processed, would already have been excluded). Each branch replaces \(P,X\) by their intersections with the new vertex neighborhood, then moves the branch vertex from \(P\) to \(X\). Therefore every maximal clique appears once.

The resulting numbers of maximal cliques of sizes 10 through 19 are, in increasing order,
\[
128,\ 2344,\ 32360,\ 177712,\ 300744,\ 86648,\ 16168,\ 2464,\ 24,\ 32.
\]
Their sum is \(618624\). No maximal clique has size 20 or more; exactly 32 have size 19. Since every clique extends to a maximal clique, this establishes both the upper bound and the complete labeled census. An independent C++17 verifier, `verify_ald.cpp`, rebuilds the graph using the distance matrix and performs a bitset maximum-clique search with a valid greedy-coloring upper bound. It independently obtains maximum 19 and 32 equality cases.

**3. Equality orbits.** The two masks

`2492490490292492` and `2492490490492492` (base 16)

represent valid 19-word codes under the stated indexing. Each coordinate independently admits two commuting swaps, \(00\leftrightarrow11\) and \(01\leftrightarrow10\), both preserving the local distance matrix; the three coordinate permutations also preserve the total distance. Applying all \(384\) operations to the two displayed masks yields disjoint orbits of sizes 8 and 24. Both orbits lie among the 32 previously enumerated optimizers, so their union is the complete equality set. The stabilizer orders follow from orbit–stabilizer. Direct pairwise distance counting for each representative yields the two spectra in the finding. The number of distance-three pairs differs, proving the orbits are distinct.

## Verification

From a directory containing the three accompanying artifacts, run:

```sh
python3 verify_ald.py
g++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_ald.cpp -o verify_ald
./verify_ald
```

The Python verifier is dependency-free beyond the standard library and checks the entire maximal-clique size histogram, the 32 optimizers, the two symmetry orbits and both pairwise-distance distributions. The C++ verifier uses different graph and search implementations and checks the optimum and labeled equality count. `orbit_reps.json` gives the full words of both representative codes. Both programs terminate after a finite traversal of a 64-vertex graph; no probabilistic or timeout-based inference is involved.

## Relationship to prior work

Gabrys, Kiah and Milenkovic introduced the ALD and its weighted four-symbol model, along with linear-programming bounds and constructions. In the version of *Asymmetric Lee Distance Codes for DNA-Based Storage* available as arXiv:1506.00740v5, Table V gives, for \(n=3\), \(\lambda=1\) and required distance three, a lower and upper bound of **6 and 30**. The new assertion identifies the exact intermediate value **19** and enumerates all equality cases; neither fact follows from those inequalities. Their 2015 ITW paper, *Asymmetric Lee Distance Codes: New Bounds and Constructions*, also develops LP inequalities and constructions but does not classify these equality cases. The standard Lee distance is not interchangeable with the ALD: in ALD the two opposite-symbol transitions have respective costs one and four.

## Limitations

Only the length-three, \(\lambda=1\), distance-three instance is proved. Nothing here asserts an optimum for longer words, a different \(\lambda\), or a stronger decoding model. The equality enumeration is an exhaustive finite proof, not a general formula in word length. Prior-literature searches cannot rule out an unindexed earlier enumeration. The two stated orbits are under the explicitly defined order-384 symmetry group; no classification of all possible non-coordinatewise isometries is claimed.
