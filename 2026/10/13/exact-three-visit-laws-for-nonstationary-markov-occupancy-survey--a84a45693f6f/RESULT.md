# Exact three-visit laws for nonstationary Markov occupancy surveys

## Finding

Consider a fixed-occupancy site with three consecutive binary detection opportunities. Under the precisely specified Markov observation model below, three visits are necessary and sufficient to identify occupancy probability, initial detectability and both state-dependent detection probabilities from the entire distribution of detection histories. No stationarity of the initial detection state is required. The theorem also gives three exact observable equations and an inequality that completely characterize which positive three-history laws are compatible with the model.

Let \(q_{ijk}=\Pr(Y_1=i,Y_2=j,Y_3=k)\) for \(i,j,k\in\{0,1\}\). Put
\[
u=q_{100}+q_{101}+q_{110}+q_{111},\qquad r=q_{010}+q_{011}.
\]
Then, for laws in the model,
\[
b=\frac{q_{110}+q_{111}}{u},\quad
a=\frac{q_{101}}{q_{100}+q_{101}},\quad
\psi=u+\frac{r}{a},\quad
\pi=\frac{u}{\psi}.
\]
A strictly positive probability vector \((q_{000},\ldots,q_{111})\) is generated by exactly one parameter quadruple in \((0,1)^4\) if and only if the four reconstructed parameters are in \((0,1)\) and
\[
q_{111}=ub^2,\qquad q_{011}=rb,\qquad q_{001}=r(1-a).
\]
For every interior quadruple, the two-visit distribution is shared by infinitely many distinct interior quadruples.

## Assumptions and scope

Sample units are independent and identically distributed, homogeneous and closed during the three ordered surveys. At a randomly sampled unit, \(Z\in\{0,1\}\) indicates true site occupancy, with \(\Pr(Z=1)=\psi\). If \(Z=0\), every \(Y_t=0\) (no false positive). Conditional on \(Z=1\), the observed binary sequence itself, rather than an additional hidden local-availability sequence, is a homogeneous first-order Markov chain with
\[
\Pr(Y_1=1\mid Z=1)=\pi,\quad
\Pr(Y_{t+1}=1\mid Z=1,Y_t=0)=a,\quad
\Pr(Y_{t+1}=1\mid Z=1,Y_t=1)=b.
\]
All four parameters \(\psi,\pi,a,b\) lie strictly between zero and one. Initial stationarity is not imposed. In the ecological interpretation, site-scale false negatives can result from the species being unavailable or not recorded at a particular replicate. The theorem excludes additional conditional observation errors superimposed on the binary Markov detection process.

## Proof

**Three-history expansion.** Define \(u=\psi\pi\) and \(r=\psi(1-\pi)a\). Conditional Markov transitions give the seven positive nonzero-word probabilities:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
\text{word}&111&110&101&100&011&010&001\\\hline
\Pr(\text{word})&ub^2&ub(1-b)&u(1-b)a&u(1-b)(1-a)&rb&r(1-b)&r(1-a)
\end{array}
\]
The final all-zero probability is \(q_{000}=1-u-r(2-a)\). The seven formulas and normalization are exact for every interior parameter tuple.

**Reconstruction.** From these expansions, \(\Pr(Y_1=1)=u\), \(\Pr(Y_1=0,Y_2=1)=r\), and the observed conditional probabilities after an initial detection determine \(b\) and \(a\):
\[
b=\Pr(Y_2=1\mid Y_1=1),\qquad
a=\Pr(Y_3=1\mid Y_1=1,Y_2=0).
\]
Because \(u,r,a>0\), solving \(u=\psi\pi\) and \(r=\psi(1-\pi)a\) gives \(\psi=u+r/a\), \(\pi=u/\psi\). All denominators are strictly positive, giving global uniqueness on the stated open parameter domain.

**Exact feasibility of an arbitrary three-history law.** For any strictly positive normalized eight-cell law, compute \(u,r,b,a,\psi,\pi\) as stated. The sums defining \(u,r\) and the ratios defining \(a,b\) automatically match the row sums for the transitions from first observed 1 and from the word 10. The three additional equalities \(q_{111}=ub^2\), \(q_{011}=rb\), \(q_{001}=r(1-a)\) enforce the only remaining independent entries. If all four reconstructed parameters are in \((0,1)\), their Markov expansion therefore reproduces all seven nonzero words; normalization supplies \(q_{000}\). Conversely, every interior model law obeys all equalities and inequalities. These are necessary and sufficient conditions, not merely moment tests.

**Two visits never suffice in the interior.** For two observed occasions let \(u=\Pr(Y_1=1)\), \(r=\Pr(Y_1=0,Y_2=1)\), and \(b=\Pr(Y_2=1\mid Y_1=1)\). The four two-word probabilities are \(ub,u(1-b),r,1-u-r\) for \(11,10,01,00\), respectively. For any \(\alpha\) in a sufficiently small open interval around the original \(a\), put
\[
a'=\alpha,\quad b'=b,\quad \psi'=u+\frac r\alpha,\quad \pi'=\frac u{\psi'}.
\]
Interior inequalities persist by continuity, and every quadruple has the same two-history law. This proves the sharp necessity statement for all interior parameter points, not just for one example.

For an exact witness, \((\psi,\pi,a,b)=(3/5,2/5,1/3,2/3)\) and \((12/25,1/2,1/2,2/3)\) both produce two-history probabilities \((q_{00},q_{01},q_{10},q_{11})=(16/25,3/25,2/25,4/25)\); their three-history laws differ.

## Relationship to prior work

MacKenzie et al. (2002) introduced site occupancy inference with imperfect detection. Hines et al. (2010) developed occupancy models for Markov-correlated spatial replicates, including a separate detectability layer, and their discussion recommends retaining at least three segments for practical surveys. Hines et al. (2014) broadened such correlated models and the initial replicate parameterization; Wright, Irvine and Rodhouse (2016) fitted a Markov detection model and studied diagnostics. These papers motivate the observation process but do not present the stated exact three-cell-history feasibility characterization with unrestricted initial detectability and a global inverse map. The 2010 three-segment practical guideline anticipates the number in the present minimum, but does not supply its two-visit impossibility for every interior point or a sufficient three-visit theorem under the particular assumptions here.

Browning and Carro (2013) establish a general sufficient four-wave bound for mixtures of two unrestricted binary Markov chains; that statement does not settle a sharp three-wave threshold in the special structural-zero mixture here. Its complete proof was not accessible for comparison, so unrecognized special-case coverage remains a residual literature risk. The mathematical novelty claim is limited to the exact structural characterization and sharp theorem, not to introducing Markov occupancy likelihoods.

## Limitations

This is structural identification from an ideal population distribution across arbitrarily many comparable sites. It provides no numerical stability bounds or finite-sample precision guarantee, and extreme probabilities cause near-zero history cells. Additional replicate-level detection errors, false positives, time-inhomogeneous transitions, occupancy change during the window or site heterogeneity are not covered. Boundary cases with zero transitions/detections are not claimed identifiable. A general two-component mixture of arbitrary Markov chains is not covered. Full prior-publication coverage cannot be established from indexed searches alone; the inaccessible full proof of Browning and Carro (2013) remains a comparison risk.
