**Correctness — PASS.** The statement fixes linear overlapping two-grams, all four counts, the binary alphabet, and all lengths \(n\ge3\). The positive-run composition bijections separately count the equal- and unequal-transition cases, including constant words. Strict discrete log-concavity balances self-loop counts, a closed ratio comparison determines the maximizing cross-transition counts and the occasional parity tie, and direct comparisons exclude opposite-endpoint configurations. The limiting coefficient follows by central binomial estimates. The bundled independent checks exhaust words through length 18 and four-count profiles through length 80, reproducing all modal profiles and total counts; these computations are supplementary to the all-length proof. The main risks are possible transcription errors in the ratio comparisons or omissions at the short-length boundary; both have been explicitly checked.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic residual risk.** The closest inspected primary full texts are Jacquet–Knessl–Szpankowski (2010) and Kiah–Puleo–Milenkovic (arXiv 2014/2015). The former explicitly enumerates the *number* of first-order Markov types and recalls the prior fixed-type population formula. The latter studies the number of distinct substring profiles and code constructions. Neither text, including the sections presenting their main results, supplies the exact all-length *largest* binary linear bigram-count fiber and its complete equality cases. The 1955 Whittle article is materially related because it calculates fixed-profile distributions, but only the abstract could be inspected; its full text was inaccessible, so an earlier implicit or explicit modal classification cannot be definitively ruled out. Several neighboring public records concern multinomial modes or matrix fibers but not these sequence fibers. Novelty means the additional global extremization and equality characterization, not a new fixed-profile counting formula.

**Value — PASS.** An unordered count spectrum of adjacent pairs is a canonical lossy observation in string reconstruction, DNA-profile storage and finite-order Markov modeling. The maximum number of messages consistent with one perfectly observed spectrum is the exact worst-case list size; this is a motivated all-length natural invariant. The theorem gives its closed form, complete maximizing transition-count profiles, finite parity effects and asymptotic collision probability. Neither the fixed-type count nor the number of distinguishable spectra alone supplies the worst-case list size. Scope is restricted to the full binary linear two-gram count channel, not cyclic words or higher-order spectra.
