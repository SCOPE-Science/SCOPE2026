# Exactly two closure components for the Gaussian reciprocal-fourth-power divisor sum

## Finding
For every nonzero \(z\in\mathbb Z[i]\), define the reciprocal-fourth-power Gaussian divisor sum
\[
\delta_{-4}(z)=\sum_{[d]:\,d\mid z}|d|^{-4},
\]
where \([d]\) ranges over divisors modulo multiplication by units \(\{\pm1,\pm i\}\). Let \(\chi_{-4}\) be the primitive character modulo \(4\), and put
\[
Z=\zeta(2)L(2,\chi_{-4})=\frac{\pi^2}{6}\,\beta(2),\qquad C=\frac34Z,
\]
where \(\beta(2)=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k/(2k+1)^2\) is Catalan's constant. Then
\[
\boxed{\overline{\delta_{-4}(\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\})}=[1,C]\cup[5/4,Z].}
\]
There are exactly two connected components. Their unique complementary gap inside the convex hull is \((C,5/4)\), with \(C=1.1300272574422387\ldots\) and \(Z=1.5067030099229850\ldots\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ring \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is a unique factorization domain. Gaussian primes are counted up to association; the two conjugate prime factors above a rational prime congruent to \(1\pmod4\) are distinct. For a Gaussian prime \(\pi\), let \(N(\pi)=|\pi|^2\). All closures are in the usual real topology. The exact interval statement is unconditional. The explicitly cited bound for primes in arithmetic progressions is used only for the large-norm overlap criterion.

## Proof
**1. Euler factors and the ramified prime.** Unique factorization and the definition give
\[
\delta_{-4}(z)=\prod_{\pi^a\parallel z}S_a(N(\pi)^{-2}),
\qquad S_a(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^a.
\]
There is exactly one Gaussian prime above \(2\), namely \(1+i\) up to association, with norm \(2\). Every other Gaussian prime has norm equal to a rational prime \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) (two separate primes of that norm) or \(q^2\) for a rational prime \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) (one prime of that norm). The full limiting Euler product is
\[
\prod_{[\pi]}(1-N(\pi)^{-2})^{-1}=\zeta_{\mathbb Q(i)}(2)=Z.
\]
Omitting the prime of norm \(2\), we obtain the nonramified limiting product \(C=(1-2^{-2})Z=3Z/4\).

**2. Interval-filling lemma.** Let \(0<t_1,t_2,\ldots<1\) have convergent product \(M_j=\prod_{k\ge j}(1-t_k)^{-1}\) and \(M_j\to1\). If for every \(j\),
\[
M_{j+1}\ge1+t_j,
\]
then the closure of the set of all finite products \(\prod_j S_{a_j}(t_j)\) with nonnegative integral exponents and finite support equals \([1,M_1]\). Indeed \(S_{a+1}(t)/S_a(t)\le1+t\) for every \(a\ge0\). Thus the intervals \(S_a(t)[1,M]\) overlap successively when \(M\ge1+t\), and their closed union is \([1,M/(1-t)]\). For any target in \([1,M_1]\), choose an exponent at stage \(j\) so that the residual lies in \([1,M_{j+1}]\). These residual intervals shrink to \(\{1\}\); finite truncations approach the target. An infinite exponent, if encountered at an endpoint, is approximated by finite exponents. The upper inclusion follows from the Euler product.

**3. Exact overlap for all nonramified prime norms below \(29\).** List the nonramified Gaussian prime norms in nondecreasing order with multiplicity:
\[
5,5,9,13,13,17,17,29,29,37,37,41,41,49,53,53,\ldots.
\]
For each of the first seven entries (both norm-\(5\) entries, norm \(9\), both norm-\(13\) entries, and both norm-\(17\) entries), the exact finite product
\[
\prod_{\substack{k>j\\N_k\le113}}(1-N_k^{-2})^{-1}>1+N_j^{-2}
\]
holds. Here \(N_k\) are all Gaussian prime norms through \(113\), including multiplicity; the complete finite list is regenerated by the accompanying verifier. The tightest case is the second norm-\(5\) entry, where the exact product of the 28 factors of norms \(9\) through \(113\) is strictly greater than \(26/25\). All comparisons are made with exact rational arithmetic.

**4. All remaining overlap inequalities.** For every real \(x\ge29\), there is a rational prime \(q\equiv1\pmod4\) with \(x<q<\sqrt2 x\). First, for \(29\le x<8\cdot10^9\), the verifier checks an explicit increasing chain of \(66\) such primes, starting at \(29\), ending above \(8\cdot10^9\), and satisfying \(q_{j+1}^2<2q_j^2\). Each chain entry is verified prime by integer trial division. For \(x\ge8\cdot10^9\), Theorem 1.2 of Bennett--Martin--O'Bryant--Rechnitzer gives, for every \(y\ge8\cdot10^9\),
\[
\left|\theta(y;4,1)-\frac y2\right|<\frac{y}{840\log y},
\]
so in particular the weaker denominator \(160\) bound holds. Let \(y=\sqrt2 x\). Since \(\log x>1\), \(\sqrt2>7/5\), and \(\sqrt2+1<5/2\), we have
\[
\theta(y;4,1)-\theta(x;4,1)
>\frac{(\sqrt2-1)x}{2}-\frac{(\sqrt2+1)x}{160\log x}>0.
\]
The asserted rational prime follows. Now take any nonramified Gaussian prime of norm \(n\ge29\). This \(q\) yields **two** later Gaussian prime factors of norm \(q\). Their total contribution to the limiting tail exceeds
\[
(1-q^{-2})^{-2}>1+2q^{-2}>1+n^{-2},
\]
because \(q^2<2n^2\). Combined with the previous exact seven checks, the interval-filling lemma applies to the entire nonramified Euler product. Hence the closure of the range of \(\delta_{-4}\) on Gaussian integers coprime to \(1+i\) equals \([1,C]\).

**5. Reintroducing the ramified prime.** Fixing the exponent \(a\ge0\) of \(1+i\) multiplies the nonramified interval by \(S_a(1/4)\), giving \([S_a(1/4),C S_a(1/4)]\). At \(a=0\) this is \([1,C]\). For \(a\ge1\), the adjacent ratios are at most \(S_2(1/4)/S_1(1/4)=21/20\). But
\[
C\ge(1-5^{-2})^{-2}=(25/24)^2>21/20,
\]
so all intervals with \(a\ge1\) overlap into the single closed interval from \(S_1(1/4)=5/4\) to \(\lim_{a\to\infty}C S_a(1/4)=4C/3=Z\). Finally \(\beta(2)<1\) and \(\pi^2<10\) imply \(C<\frac34\frac{10}{6}=5/4\). Therefore the two intervals are disjoint, and they are exactly the two connected components. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Defant's *An Extension of the Abundancy Index to Certain Quadratic Rings* (2015 public preprint, arXiv:1506.05416, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2) defines the Gaussian divisor function and establishes multiplicativity, but studies solitariness rather than the topological closure of the fourth-reciprocal-power range. Defant's separate arXiv:1506.05432 gives a density criterion for divisor sums on the **positive integers**, not on \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Sanna's arXiv:1507.08091 computes connected components for ordinary integer divisor sums; its Euler-factor framework motivates, but does not state, the exact Gaussian two-component result. The unconditional progression estimate used here is Bennett--Martin--O'Bryant--Rechnitzer (arXiv:1802.00085), Theorem 1.2, for the modulus \(4\).

## Limitations
This theorem does not compute the relative frequencies of Gaussian integers whose divisor sums lie in either component, nor prove an analogous formula in other quadratic rings or at other exponents. The prime-chain file supports the finite part of the proof only, and an earlier unindexed derivation of the same range is a residual originality risk.
