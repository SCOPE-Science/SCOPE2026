Exact rational comparisons verify every nonramified overlap below norm \(29\); a verified chain of \(66\) primes and the unconditional explicit modulus-\(4\) Chebyshev bound establish every remaining overlap. The interval-filling argument reconstructs the entire closure and the final ramified-prime interval comparison gives exactly two components. The proof explicitly distinguishes the infinite theorem from its finite certificate.

Defant (arXiv:1506.05432) and Sanna (arXiv:1507.08091) study divisor sums on the ordinary positive integers; they do not state the Gaussian two-interval formula, and their theorems alone do not imply its split-prime norm overlap verification. Related published results for unitary and alternating divisor sums concern different functions. Direct claim, equation, parameter, and alias searches found no stronger covering theorem. Unindexed prior derivations remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The precise number of connected components and both exact endpoints are natural topological invariants of a divisor sum defined in foundational work on imaginary quadratic rings. The proof shows how Gaussian split primes fill the entire unramified spectrum, while the ramified prime creates one and only one gap. This addresses a meaningful distinction between integer and Gaussian divisor-function ranges rather than merely listing a numerical observation.

**Limitations.** No asymptotic distribution between the intervals, other exponent, or other ring is claimed. The cited theorem for primes in progressions is an external published ingredient; the packaged exact checker verifies the explicit finite bridge but does not reprove that theorem.
