In a ratio of independent centered Gaussian products, any numerator factor forces a single central density mode, whereas a pure reciprocal product has exactly two nonzero modes. With \(N\) unit-normal denominator factors those modes shrink as \(\exp[-N(\log2-\gamma_{\mathrm E})/2-3\zeta(3)/\pi^2]\) while a typical absolute observation grows as \(\exp[N(\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\log2)/2]\).
