# Example 4.7 has a unique rational Steiner hexad
## Finding
For each of Elsenhans--Jahnel's two Example 4.7 plane quartics \(C_{F,5}\) and \(C_{F,-5}\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with
\[
F(T)=T^6-24T^5+152T^4-340T^3+335T^2-150T+25,
\]
the Galois action on the \(63\) nonzero classes of \(J(C)[2]\) has orbit sizes
\[
1,6,16,16,24.
\]
Consequently
\[
J(C)(\mathbb Q)[2]\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\]
and the distinguished Steiner hexad constructed in the source is the unique Galois-invariant Steiner hexad. The singleton orbit is the nonzero two-torsion class defining that hexad. The same conclusion holds for both signs because the source proves that the two bitangent Galois images are conjugate in \(\operatorname{Sp}_6(\mathbb F_2)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The calculation is over characteristic zero and concerns only the two explicit smooth plane quartics of Example 4.7. For a smooth plane quartic, the \(28\) bitangents are identified with the odd theta characteristics, while the \(63\) Steiner hexads are naturally indexed by the nonzero classes of \(J(C)[2]\). The source constructs a distinguished Galois-invariant Steiner hexad and identifies its stabilizer with the standard subgroup \(U_{63}\subset\operatorname{Sp}_6(\mathbb F_2)\). It also states that the Galois group acting on the twelve bitangents of this hexad is the degree-twelve transitive group 12T31 of order \(48\).

The conclusion does not assert anything about odd-primary torsion, the full Mordell--Weil group of the Jacobian, or quartics outside these two explicit examples.

## Proof
Use the standard symplectic vector space \(V=\mathbb F_2^6\), with quadratic form
\[
q_0(a_1,a_2,a_3,b_1,b_2,b_3)=a_1b_1+a_2b_2+a_3b_3.
\]
The \(28\) vectors \(m\in V\) satisfying \(q_0(m)=1\) model the odd theta characteristics. For each nonzero \(v\in V\), define
\[
H_v=\{m:q_0(m)=q_0(m+v)=1\}.
\]
Each \(H_v\) has twelve elements, partitioned into six pairs \(\{m,m+v\}\), and the \(63\) sets \(H_v\) are distinct. Thus they model all Steiner hexads.

Fix the vector \(v=e_1\) and enumerate its symplectic stabilizer through transvections. Its induced permutation group on the \(28\) odd characteristics has order \(23040\), as required for \(U_{63}\), and its restriction to the twelve elements of \(H_v\) is faithful.

A standard permutation representative of 12T31 is generated by
\[
(1\ 10\ 7\ 4)(3\ 6\ 9\ 12),
\qquad
(1\ 5\ 9)(2\ 6\ 10)(3\ 7\ 11)(4\ 8\ 12).
\]
This group has exactly one invariant partition into six pairs, namely
\[
\{1,7\},\{2,8\},\{3,9\},\{4,10\},\{5,11\},\{6,12\}.
\]
After identifying these blocks with the six Steiner pairs in \(H_v\), both generators lift uniquely through the faithful restriction of \(U_{63}\). The lifted group has order \(48\), and its action on the \(28\) odd theta characteristics has orbit sizes \(12,16\), reproducing the bitangent orbit decomposition stated in the source.

Applying the same lifted generators to all \(63\) hexads \(H_u\), equivalently to all nonzero vectors \(u\in V\), gives orbit sizes
\[
1,6,16,16,24.
\]
The common fixed set in \(V\setminus\{0\}\) is exactly \(\{v\}\). Hence the Galois-fixed subgroup of \(J(C)[2]\) has two elements, so it is \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). By the source's bijection between nonzero two-torsion classes and Steiner hexads, the distinguished hexad is therefore the unique Galois-invariant one.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` performs the complete finite calculation using exact binary arithmetic and permutations only. It constructs all \(28\) odd theta characteristics and all \(63\) Steiner hexads, enumerates the stabilizer of the distinguished class, generates 12T31 from the displayed permutations, proves that its six-pair block system is unique, lifts its generators, and exhaustively computes the induced orbits on both the \(28\) bitangents and the \(63\) nonzero two-torsion classes.

A successful run ends with:
`VERIFY_OK Uv=23040 H=48 bitangent_orbits=12+16 torsion_orbits=1+6+16+16+24 rational_2torsion=2 unique_steiner=true`

No floating-point calculation, probabilistic test, or partial enumeration is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Elsenhans and Jahnel establish the correspondence between Steiner hexads and nonzero two-torsion classes, construct the Galois-invariant distinguished hexad, identify the relevant point stabilizer \(U_{63}\), and in Example 4.7 determine the order-\(48\) group 12T31 on its twelve bitangents. They also show that the two quartics obtained from the two signs have conjugate bitangent Galois images. Their stated conclusions therefore imply existence of a nonzero rational two-torsion class, but they do not give the complete orbit decomposition on the other \(62\) nonzero classes or state uniqueness of the rational Steiner hexad.

The LMFDB entry for 12T31 supplies a standardized degree-twelve permutation representative and its generators. That database entry does not attach this group to the Jacobian two-torsion of these quartics or compute the \(63\)-point action.

## Limitations
The computation determines only the rational two-torsion subgroup and the Galois orbit structure on nonzero two-torsion. It does not determine \(J(C)(\mathbb Q)\), odd-primary torsion, or the full arithmetic of either Jacobian. The originality comparison cannot exclude an older or unindexed calculation that encodes the same \(63\)-point action in different theta-characteristic notation.
