# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. Burnside's basis theorem gives \(d(G)=2n\) because \(\Phi(G)=Z(G)\) and \(G/Z(G)\) has dimension \(2n\). For \(n=1\), noncommutation is equivalent to linear independence in the two-dimensional quotient, so every noncommuting pair generates and \(\Xi(G)\) is empty. For \(n\ge2\), no pair generates; the commutator form is nondegenerate alternating, so adjacency is exactly projective nonorthogonality after grouping scalar multiples and central lifts. Direct hyperplane counting gives the strongly regular base parameters. Tensoring its adjacency matrix with \(J_{p(p-1)}\) gives the claimed spectrum. The packaged checker independently verifies the boundary and representative higher-rank cases.
