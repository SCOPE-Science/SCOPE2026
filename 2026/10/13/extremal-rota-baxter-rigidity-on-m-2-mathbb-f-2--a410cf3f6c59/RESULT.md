# Extremal Rota–Baxter rigidity on \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\)
## Finding
Let \(A=M_2(\mathbb F_2)\), with ordered matrix-unit basis \((E_{11},E_{12},E_{21},E_{22})\). A linear map \(R:A\to A\) is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_2\) when
\[
R(x)R(y)=R\bigl(R(x)y+xR(y)+\lambda xy\bigr)
\]
for all \(x,y\in A\).

For both possible weights \(\lambda\in\{0,1\}\), the maximum degree of the minimal polynomial of \(R\) is exactly \(3\). There are exactly \(6\) extremal weight-zero operators and exactly \(24\) extremal weight-one operators.

Under conjugation by \(\operatorname{Aut}(A)\), the weight-zero extremals form two orbits, each of size \(3\). Transpose interchanges those two orbits, so they form one class under automorphisms and transpose. The weight-one extremals form four automorphism orbits, each of size \(6\). Transpose pairs them into two \(12\)-element classes, and
\[
\phi(R)=-R-\operatorname{id}=R+\operatorname{id}
\]
interchanges those two classes. Thus there is exactly one extremal class for each weight under the equivalence used in the recent characteristic-zero theorem.

The exhaustive census also gives exactly \(28\) weight-zero and \(110\) weight-one Rota–Baxter operators in total. The six weight-zero extremals all have minimal polynomial \(X^3\). Among the twenty-four weight-one extremals, exactly \(12\) have minimal polynomial \(X^2(X+1)\), and exactly \(12\) have minimal polynomial \(X(X+1)^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\), the algebra is the associative matrix algebra \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\), and all \(\mathbb F_2\)-linear operators are included. Since \(\mathbb F_2\) has only the weights \(0\) and \(1\), this covers every Rota–Baxter weight on this algebra.

The word *extremal* means that the minimal polynomial has the largest degree occurring among Rota–Baxter operators of the specified weight. The computation proves that this degree is \(3=2\cdot2-1\) for both weights. The equivalence relation is generated by conjugation with algebra automorphisms, transpose, nonzero scalar multiplication in weight zero, and the involution \(\phi\) in nonzero weight. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), nonzero scalar multiplication is trivial.

## Proof
There are exactly
\[
2^{16}=65536
\]
linear endomorphisms of the four-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-space \(A\). For a fixed linear map \(R\), the Rota–Baxter defect
\[
D_R(x,y)=R(x)R(y)-R\bigl(R(x)y+xR(y)+\lambda xy\bigr)
\]
is bilinear in \((x,y)\). Therefore testing the \(16\) ordered pairs of matrix units is logically equivalent to testing the identity on all of \(A\times A\).

Exhaustion of the \(65536\) maps gives \(28\) solutions at weight \(0\) and \(110\) at weight \(1\). For every accepted map, the verifier independently checks all \(16^2=256\) ordered element pairs. The minimal polynomial is then found by exact linear dependence among \(\operatorname{id},R,R^2,R^3,R^4\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). The degree distributions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\lambda&1&2&3\\ \hline
0&1&21&6\\
1&2&84&24
\end{array}.
\]
Hence the maximal degree is \(3\) in both weights, with \(6\) and \(24\) extremals respectively.

The verifier enumerates all invertible unital multiplicative linear maps \(A\to A\) and obtains exactly \(6\) algebra automorphisms. Independently, conjugation by the six invertible matrices in \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\) gives the same six maps. Acting on the extremal sets yields automorphism-orbit sizes
\[
(3,3)
\]
at weight \(0\), and
\[
(6,6,6,6)
\]
at weight \(1\). Adding transpose gives orbit sizes \((6)\) and \((12,12)\), respectively. Adding \(\phi(R)=R+\operatorname{id}\) at weight \(1\) gives one orbit of size \(24\).

The canonical characteristic-zero operators from the motivating paper reduce modulo \(2\) to the maps
\[
B_0(E_{11})=E_{12},\quad B_0(E_{12})=0,\quad B_0(E_{21})=E_{11},\quad B_0(E_{22})=0,
\]
and
\[
B_1(E_{11})=E_{22},\quad B_1(E_{12})=E_{12},\quad B_1(E_{21})=0,\quad B_1(E_{22})=0.
\]
The exhaustive orbit computation places \(B_0\) in the unique full weight-zero class and \(B_1\) in the unique full weight-one class.

## Verification
The standalone file `artifacts/verify.py` is a complete finite certificate. It constructs the multiplication of \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\) directly from matrix coordinates, enumerates every linear endomorphism, tests the Rota–Baxter identity, rechecks every accepted map on every ordered pair of elements, computes minimal polynomials, exhausts the algebra automorphism group in two independent ways, and computes the stated orbit partitions. Its recorded output ends with `VERIFY_OK` and reports the counts and orbit sizes stated above.

No inference from sampling is used. The only computational search spaces are finite and fully exhausted.

## Relationship to prior work
Gubarev's 2026 paper *Extremal Rota–Baxter operators on matrix algebras* classifies extremal operators on \(M_n(F)\) in characteristic zero. Its Theorem 1.1 gives one class for each weight under automorphisms, transpose, and the additional standard symmetries; the paper explicitly assumes characteristic zero throughout and identifies extremal degree \(2n-1\). The same paper lists primary MSC 2020 code 16W99 and was first submitted on September 4, 2026.

Earlier classifications of Rota–Baxter operators on \(M_2\) do not cover the present field. Benito, Gubarev, and Pozhidaev explicitly assume characteristic different from two. Tang, Zhang, and Sun treat the four-dimensional complex simple associative algebra. Panasenko's later refinement of the weight-zero \(M_2\) classification likewise states characteristic different from two. Thus these results do not imply the characteristic-two census or its orbit structure.

The present result shows that the recent extremal rigidity conclusion survives in the smallest characteristic excluded by those works, but with a nontrivial refinement: automorphism conjugacy alone is not unique. Transpose is essential already at weight \(0\), and transpose together with \(\phi\) is essential at weight \(1\).

## Limitations
This is an exact theorem only for \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\). It does not prove a characteristic-two analogue for \(M_n(\mathbb F_2)\) with \(n>2\), nor for \(M_2(F)\) over arbitrary larger fields of characteristic two. The finite census establishes existence, maximal degree, counts, and equivalence classes, but does not by itself explain whether the characteristic-zero structural proof can be adapted in general positive characteristic.

Literature search noncoverage is evidence rather than a proof of absolute novelty. An unindexed characteristic-two classification could in principle exist, although the directly relevant classifications inspected above explicitly exclude characteristic two or work over \(\mathbb C\).
