# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is a finite exhaustive theorem. Every one of the \(2^{16}\) linear maps on \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\) is tested. Bilinearity makes the basis-pair Rota–Baxter test exhaustive, and a second pass checks every accepted map on all \(256\) ordered element pairs. Minimal-polynomial degree is computed exactly from powers of the operator. The six algebra automorphisms are obtained both by exhaustive multiplicativity testing and by inner conjugation, after which every relevant orbit is explicitly generated.

## Originality
PASS. The directly motivating 2026 extremal theorem is explicitly characteristic zero. The 2018 quadratic-algebra paper and the 2025 Panasenko refinement explicitly exclude characteristic two, while the 2014 full \(M_2\) classification is over \(\mathbb C\). Focused searches for \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\), characteristic-two matrix Rota–Baxter classification, the exact counts \(28\), \(110\), \(6\), \(24\), and the transpose/\(\phi\) orbit profile found no equivalent or stronger statement. The closest finite-field Rota–Baxter database item concerns the four-dimensional exterior algebra rather than \(M_2\). Residual indexing risk is recorded in the structured audit.

## Value
PASS. This is the smallest matrix algebra over the smallest field excluded by the nearby classifications, so the case is a natural structural boundary rather than an arbitrary finite slice. The result both confirms that extremal degree \(2n-1\) and one full equivalence class survive at \(n=2\) in characteristic two and shows exactly why the broader equivalence is needed: automorphism conjugacy alone splits into two and four classes. That orbit refinement is directly relevant to any attempt to extend the characteristic-zero rigidity theorem to positive characteristic.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Gubarev, arXiv:2609.05647v1, whose Theorem 1.1 proves characteristic-zero extremal rigidity and whose first page lists MSC 16W99, 16S50. The older \(M_2\) classifications inspected either assume characteristic different from two or work over the complex numbers. The present theorem is only for \(\mathbb F_2\) and does not settle larger characteristic-two fields or matrix sizes.
