# Ferrers rigidity and the exact one-generator census of finite two-agent epistemic grids

## Finding

Let \(m,n\ge 1\). On \(W=[m]\times[n]\), let the two named equivalence relations be equality of the row coordinate and equality of the column coordinate. For an arbitrary marked subset \(P\subseteq W\), let \(\mathcal A(P)\) be the Boolean subalgebra of \(\mathcal P(W)\) generated by \(P\) and closed under both existential accessibility operators. Write \(B_P\) for its \(m\)-by-\(n\) binary incidence matrix.

**Classification theorem.** The following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(\mathcal A(P)=\mathcal P(W)\); equivalently, each singleton world is definable from the one proposition \(P\).
2. \(B_P\) is **Ferrers** (the sets of marked columns in its rows are linearly ordered by inclusion), and no two rows and no two columns of \(B_P\) are identical.
3. After independent row and column permutations, \(B_P\) is exactly one of these canonical shapes, expressed by its successive row lengths: if \(m=n=k\), either \((k-1,k-2,\ldots,0)\) or \((k,k-1,\ldots,1)\); if \(m=n+1\), \((n,n-1,\ldots,0)\); if \(n=m+1\), \((m,m-1,\ldots,1)\). No shape exists when \(|m-n|\ge2\).

Therefore the exact number \(F(m,n)\) of labelled one-proposition generating sets is
\[
F(m,n)=\begin{cases}
2(m!)^2,&m=n,\\
m!n!,&|m-n|=1,\\
0,&|m-n|\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, on the \(k\)-by-\(k\) grid precisely \(2(k!)^2\) of its \(2^{k^2}\) one-proposition valuations generate the full algebra. Their marked-set cardinalities are exactly \(k(k-1)/2\) and \(k(k+1)/2\); thus the sharp minimum support is \(k(k-1)/2\). For \(|m-n|=1\), every generating support has size \(\binom{\max(m,n)}2\). The result includes the one-cell case.

## Assumptions and scope

The accessibility relations are *named*, reflexive equivalence relations on the entire finite Cartesian product. Formally, for \(S\subseteq W\),
\[
\Diamond_r(S)=\{(i,j):\exists j'\,(i,j')\in S\},\quad
\Diamond_c(S)=\{(i,j):\exists i'\,(i',j)\in S\}.
\]
Boolean complements are relative to \(W\). No diagonal, global nominals, counting modalities, constants naming rows, or additional propositional variables are supplied. The rows and columns are labelled in the enumeration, and independent row permutations and column permutations preserve the two *named* modalities (swapping the modalities is not allowed).

## Proof

**Finite modal types.** Start with the two colours \(P\) and \(W\setminus P\). Recursively refine the vertex partition by giving a cell its previous colour together with the *set* of previous colours visible in its own row and the set visible in its own column. Since \(W\) is finite, this process stabilizes at the greatest colour-respecting two-modal bisimulation equivalence. By induction on modal formula depth, the resulting classes are precisely the atoms of \(\mathcal A(P)\): at every finite stage, each refinement class is definable by finite Boolean combinations of existing class formulae and the two diamonds; conversely the partition respects all operations at stabilization. Consequently \(\mathcal A(P)=\mathcal P(W)\) iff every stable class is a singleton.

**(1) implies Ferrers: a non-Ferrers obstruction.** If the marked-neighbourhoods of two rows are incomparable, there are two rows and two columns displaying a checkerboard (the two diagonal cells marked, the other two unmarked, or its complement). Set \(R_*\) and \(C_*\) initially to these two rows and columns. Repeatedly adjoin any outside row whose entries on \(C_*\) contain both colours, and any outside column whose entries on \(R_*\) contain both colours. This monotone saturation terminates. Every row of \(R_*\) and every column of \(C_*\) remains mixed on the opposite core, whereas each outside row is constant on \(C_*\) and each outside column is constant on \(R_*\).

Assign every cell \((i,j)\) the triple \((\rho(i),\kappa(j),B_P(i,j))\), where \(\rho(i)=*\) for \(i\in R_*\) and equals the individual identity \(i\) otherwise, and similarly \(\kappa(j)=*\) for \(j\in C_*\), its identity otherwise. Equality of these triples is a colour-respecting bisimulation equivalence: for each equal-triple pair, sets of triple-classes seen within its row coincide, as do those within its column. Indeed, core rows/columns see both colours within the opposite core, while exterior columns/rows are constant on the core; all other coordinates are individually fixed. The initial two marked checkerboard cells share the triple \((*,*,1)\), and are distinct. Thus a non-Ferrers marking never generates the full algebra.

**(1) implies distinct profiles.** If two rows have identical binary words, their transposition, fixing all other rows, is a nonidentity valuation-preserving automorphism of both named relations; no generated formula separates its exchanged cells. The same reasoning applies to two identical columns. Thus full generation requires pairwise distinct row profiles and pairwise distinct column profiles.

**(2) implies (1): definable peeling.** Consider any nonempty active rectangle \(R\times C\) all of whose row profiles and column profiles are distinct when restricted to its active coordinates. Its Ferrers property yields a monochromatic row or column. If the shortest row is empty, it is all-zero; otherwise any column that this shortest row marks is all-one. Distinctness of the active profiles ensures a monochromatic row/column of a given fixed colour is unique on that side. Unless \(|R|=|C|=1\), choose a monochromatic side with at least two surviving indices, so its removal leaves a nonempty rectangle (if one side has just one index, all members of the other side are monochromatic, and distinctness forces that side to have at most two indices).

Suppose the selected index is a unique all-zero row of the active rectangle \(A=R\times C\). The corresponding slice is definable by \(A\cap\neg\Diamond_r(A\cap P)\); for an all-one row use \(A\cap\neg\Diamond_r(A\setminus P)\), and use \(\Diamond_c\) for pure columns. Starting from \(A=W\), the selected slice is definable. Applying the corresponding full-grid diamond to that slice defines the entire named row/column, whose complement can be intersected with \(A\) to obtain the next definable active rectangle. Removal of a monochromatic row subtracts the *same* entry from every surviving column profile; hence their pairwise distinctness persists; row distinctness and the Ferrers order also persist. The column case is symmetric. This induction isolates every complete row and complete column, and their intersections are definable singletons. Thus the whole powerset algebra is generated.

**(2) iff (3): classification and exact census.** Put a Ferrers matrix in nonincreasing row-length order \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_m\) with each \(0\le\lambda_i\le n\). Distinct rows require \(m\) different integers from \(\{0,\dots,n\}\). Distinct columns mean that for each \(j=1,\dots,n-1\), some row has length exactly \(j\); otherwise columns \(j\) and \(j+1\) would be identical. Hence the lengths \(1,\dots,n-1\) are present, and at most two further lengths, \(0\) and \(n\), are available. This forces \(m\in\{n-1,n,n+1\}\). For \(m=n\), exactly one of \(0,n\) is present; for \(m=n+1\), both occur; for \(m=n-1\), neither occurs, giving exactly the four shapes above (the last case expressed with \(n=m+1\)).

Each canonical shape has distinct row and column profiles, so independent permutations yield exactly \(m!n!\) different labelled matrices; none has a nontrivial stabilizer under these permutations. The two balanced shapes are disjoint because they have different numbers of marked entries. Multiplying by the number of shapes proves \(F(m,n)\) and the minimum-support assertions. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying standalone standard-library Python program, `verify.py`, independently computes ordinary modal set-based partition refinement. In a successful run it checked **all 74,954 binary matrices** of sizes \(1\le m,n\le4\), covering 16 rectangular parameter cases, and found exact agreement with the structural criterion and the factorial counts. It separately validated 648 explicit checkerboard-core bisimulation witnesses, compared seven small cases with direct closure of the Boolean modal algebra, and checked 1,680 larger random matrices plus planted generating staircases. The program prints a `VERIFY_OK` line and exits with assertions enabled on any mismatch. These tests corroborate the finite semantics; the proof above establishes the unrestricted theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Figallo and Mónica-Gomes (2023) describe the subalgebra lattice of a finite diagonal-free two-dimensional cylindric algebra using compatible partitions of the atoms. Their general partition correspondence supports the finite-algebra viewpoint but, in the full text inspected, does **not** enumerate which individual binary subsets of a specified labelled rectangular grid generate the entire algebra. Erdős, Faber, and Larson (1981) exhibit that an upper-triangular relation generates all singletons in the countably infinite two-dimensional cylindric set algebra; consequently the staircase *idea and sufficiency paradigm are prior art*. The contribution claimed here is the necessary exclusion of all other finite markings by a canonical checkerboard-core bisimulation, and the resulting all-rectangle iff classification and exact labelled census. G. M. Bergman's 1982 work on the rank of two-dimensional cylindric set algebras is a potentially relevant antecedent whose complete text was not available in this comparison; hidden overlap remains possible.

## Limitations

The statement is restricted to complete Cartesian rectangular frames with *two* named row/column modalities and precisely *one* freely chosen propositional truth set. The enumeration is of labelled supports, not isomorphism classes of subalgebras. No claim is made for arbitrary equivalence-relation frames, multi-generator constructions, or counting/diagonal modalities. Finite experiments are not used as an infinite proof. An independent mathematical audit and complete historical-priority audit have not been performed; the unavailable Bergman text leaves a specific originality uncertainty.
