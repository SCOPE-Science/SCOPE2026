# Fibonacci and idempotence-graded spectra of finite negatively generated De Morgan chains

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal{N}\) be the class of **finite, totally ordered, negatively generated De Morgan monoids**, where negative generation means generation by the elements below or equal to the fusion identity. Isomorphisms preserve the full involutive residuated-lattice signature. Write \(c_n\) for the number of isomorphism classes having \(n\) elements, and let \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), \(F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\).

**Finite spectrum theorem.** For every \(t\ge1\),
\[
c_{2t-1}=1,\qquad c_{2t}=F_{t+1}.
\]
More precisely, let \(H_{2t}(u)\) enumerate the \(2t\)-element isomorphism types by the number of **non-idempotent elements**, i.e. elements \(x\) with \(x\cdot x\ne x\). Then
\[
\boxed{H_{2t}(u)=1+\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}F_d u^d.}
\]
All odd-cardinality types are fully idempotent, and the constant term in the even polynomial is the unique idempotent type.

The enumeration has a finer invariant. For every non-idempotent type, let \(k\) be the radius of the odd Sugihara skeleton in the generalized Sugihara monoid inside its canonical reflected interval (so that this skeleton has \(2k+1\) elements). Define \(k=0\) for the unique idempotent even type. The joint generating function by half-cardinality, number of non-idempotent elements, and skeletal radius is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H(z,u,v)=\sum_{t\ge1}z^t\sum_{[A]:|A|=2t}u^{d(A)}v^{k(A)}
=\frac{z}{1-z}\left(1+\frac{uz}{1-uz-u^2vz^2}\right).}
\]
Equivalently, the coefficient for fixed non-idempotent count \(d\ge1\) and radius \(k\) is
\[
\boxed{\binom{d-k-1}{k}\quad\text{when }\;0\le k\le\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor,}
\]
provided \(t\ge d+1\), and is zero otherwise. In particular, every positive number of non-idempotent elements from \(1\) through \(t-1\) occurs at even order \(2t\).

There is also an asymptotic shell law. Among non-idempotent isomorphism types of cardinality \(2t\), let \(s\) be the number of pairs of idempotent elements outside the canonical central reflected interval. If \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), then for each fixed \(j\ge0\),
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Pr(s=j)=\varphi^{-j-2}.
\]
This is a probability distribution because \(\sum_{j\ge0}\varphi^{-j-2}=1\).

For illustration, \(c_2,c_4,c_6,c_8,c_{10},c_{12},c_{14},c_{16}\) are \(1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34\), while the idempotence-refined polynomial at cardinality \(12\) is
\[
H_{12}(u)=1+u+u^2+2u^3+3u^4+5u^5.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

A De Morgan monoid is a commutative square-increasing involutive residuated lattice \(\langle A;\cdot,\wedge,\vee,\neg,e\rangle\). The order is the lattice order, and the residual is \(x\to y=\neg(x\cdot\neg y)\). The class here requires \(A\) to be a finite nonempty **chain** and requires that \(A\) be generated by \(A^- =\{x\in A:x\le e\}\). It does **not** count all semilinear algebras, arbitrary products of chains, all De Morgan monoids, or all generalized Sugihara monoids outside the totally ordered setting.

The parameters \(d\) and \(k\) are intrinsic, not arbitrary presentation labels. The canonical decomposition and uniqueness are justified in the proof. For \(t=1\), the inner sum is empty and there is one type of order two. The trivial one-element chain is included among odd orders.

## Proof

**Step 1: canonical central reflection and exterior shell.** Theorems 7.19 and 7.22 of Wannenburg–Raftery, with their Theorem 7.11, classify every non-idempotent member of the stated class as
\[
A\cong S_{2s+1}[R(D)],
\]
where \(s\ge0\), \(S_{2s+1}\) is the unique \((2s+1)\)-element odd Sugihara chain, \(D\) is a finite totally ordered generalized Sugihara monoid, \(R(D)\) is its reflection, and square brackets denote rigorous extension replacing the middle element of the odd Sugihara chain with the reflection. The case \(s=0\) means the central reflection alone. The reflection \(R(D)\) has \(2|D|+2\) elements, and the exterior shell contributes \(2s\) more, whence
\[
|A|=2(|D|+s+1).
\]
Reflection creates \(|D|\) non-idempotent elements: its reversed copy \(D'\) has square equal to its greatest bound; the original copy and two new bounds are idempotent. Rigorous extension adds only idempotent elements. Thus \(d(A)=|D|\ge1\).

No overcounting occurs. Within non-idempotent \(A\), the interval \([\neg(f^2),f^2]\), with \(f=\neg e\), recovers \(R(D)\) intrinsically (Theorem 7.19). Collapsing that interval recovers the odd Sugihara quotient, and hence \(s\) (Theorem 7.16). In a reflection the original \(D\) is the internal lower copy between the new bottom and the inverted upper copy; it is recovered, with its operations, from \(R(D)\). Consequently \(s\) and the isomorphism type of \(D\) are unique. The all-idempotent cases are disjoint from these by their defining equation \(x^2=x\). Theorem 5.3, with the trivial case added, gives one fully idempotent Sugihara chain of every positive cardinality.

**Step 2: classification of finite generalized Sugihara chains.** For \(|D|=d\ge1\), Theorem 6.2 expresses \(D\) as a lexicographic sum of nonempty finite interval fibers \(X_i\) over a unique odd Sugihara skeleton \(S_{2k+1}\), indexed by \(i=-k,\dots,0,\dots,k\). Theorem 6.1 makes fusion and residual completely determined by the skeleton and those ordered fibers. By Corollary 6.10, negative generation requires every fiber \(X_i\) with \(i>0\) to be a singleton. The other \(k+1\) fibers have arbitrary positive sizes \(p_{-k},\dots,p_0\). Hence
\[
d=k+\sum_{i=-k}^{0}p_i,\qquad p_i\ge1,\qquad 0\le k\le\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
The skeleton and all fiber cardinalities are intrinsic: recover them using the term operation \(x\mapsto x^{**}\), where \(x^*=x\to e\). Since finite chains have exactly one order type for each length, distinct \((k,p_{-k},\dots,p_0)\) give nonisomorphic \(D\)'s. Conversely all such data construct a generalized Sugihara chain. For fixed \(d,k\), the number of positive compositions into \(k+1\) parts is
\[
\binom{(d-k)-1}{(k+1)-1}=\binom{d-k-1}{k}.
\]
Let \(g_d\) be the number of isomorphism types \(D\) of size \(d\). Then
\[
\sum_{d\ge1}\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{d-k-1}{k}v^k x^d
=\frac{x}{1-x-vx^2},
\]
where invalid binomial coefficients are zero. This follows by writing the \(k+1\) nonempty fibers as compositions: the contribution for fixed \(k\) is \(x^{2k+1}(1-x)^{-k-1}\), and summing the geometric series yields the rational function. At \(v=1\), the denominator \(1-x-x^2\) gives \(g_d=F_d\).

**Step 3: convolution and the three-variable formula.** At cardinality \(2t\), choose the number \(d=|D|\) and the outer-shell size \(s\) with \(d+s+1=t\). Thus \(1\le d\le t-1\), and for each such \(d\) the number of distinct types is exactly \(g_d=F_d\). Add the unique fully idempotent Sugihara chain. This proves \(H_{2t}(u)=1+\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}F_du^d\) and
\[
H_{2t}(1)=1+\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}F_d=F_{t+1}.
\]
Odd cardinalities have no non-idempotent rigorous extensions because \(|R(D)|+2s\) is even, giving \(c_{2t-1}=1\). Summing over all shell sizes \(s\ge0\) and over the independent parameters \(d,k\) proves the trivariate generating function:
\[
\sum_{t\ge1}z^t\left(1+\sum_{d=1}^{t-1}\sum_k \binom{d-k-1}{k}u^dv^k\right)
=\frac{z}{1-z}\left(1+\frac{uz}{1-uz-vu^2z^2}\right).
\]

**Step 4: limiting shell distribution.** At cardinality \(2t\), there are \(F_{t+1}-1\) non-idempotent types, of which exactly \(F_{t-j-1}\) have shell size \(j\), for \(0\le j\le t-2\). Therefore
\[
\Pr_t(s=j)=\frac{F_{t-j-1}}{F_{t+1}-1}.
\]
By the classical Fibonacci ratio limit \(F_{n-r}/F_n\to\varphi^{-r}\), this approaches \(\varphi^{-j-2}\). The geometric limiting masses sum to one, finishing the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` constructs, independently of the generating-function identity, all positive fiber-compositions for each eligible skeletal radius, their generalized Sugihara operation tables, the reflected central algebras, and their rigorous extensions. It checks identity, involution, commutativity, order-monotonicity, square-increase and every finite residuation instance on all generated examples of cardinality at most \(16\); associativity and generation from the negative cone are also checked on examples through cardinality \(12\). It compares full operation-table signatures for noncollision through order \(16\) and the predicted exact counts through order \(21\). It additionally counts all admissible compositions through \(|D|=20\) against the Fibonacci recurrence. The replay result is `VERIFY_OK 304 parameter identities, exact operation tables and finite axioms`. These checks are finite corroboration, **not a substitute for the preceding all-size proof**.

## Relationship to prior work

Wannenburg and Raftery supply the essential reflection, generalized Sugihara, and rigorous-extension representation theorems (Theorems 5.3, 6.1–6.2, 6.10–6.11, 7.11, 7.16, 7.19, 7.22). Those structural theorems are **established antecedents**, not new claims here. In particular the existence of the decomposition is not asserted to be original. The contribution claimed here is its exact joint **cardinality / element-idempotence / skeleton-radius classification**, the corresponding rational multivariate generating function, and the limiting shell distribution. The Fibonacci sequence and positive-composition identity in isolation are also established facts; their occurrence as this specific graded algebra spectrum is the subject of the claim. Gil-Férez, Jipsen and Metcalfe (2020) **do** give exact counts in surrounding classes: \(2^{n-2}\) for *all* \(n\)-element commutative idempotent residuated chains, and Catalan counts for conservative commutative residuated lattices; their published results do not count the *non-idempotent De Morgan* chains of the present theorem. Galatos–Raftery (2015) supplies important category-equivalence background and remains a possible source of an implicit generalized Sugihara fiber count.

## Limitations

The exact count applies only to **finite chains** in the negatively generated semilinear De Morgan variety. No claim is made for arbitrary finite De Morgan monoids, nonchain products, or for the number of labeled operation tables. The numerical sequence itself is classical. An unindexed or implicit prior enumeration in earlier category-equivalence/structure work cannot be ruled out, although the explicit counts of Gil-Férez–Jipsen–Metcalfe (2020) concern different, idempotent classes. Computational testing reaches bounded finite orders, and no independent mathematical audit has yet been conducted.
