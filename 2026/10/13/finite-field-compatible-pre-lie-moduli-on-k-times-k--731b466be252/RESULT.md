# Finite-field compatible pre-Lie moduli on \(K\times K\)

## Finding

Let \(K\) be a field and let \(A=K\times K\) have basis
\[
\{e_1,e_2\}
\]
and first pre-Lie product
\[
e_1\cdot e_1=e_1,\qquad e_2\cdot e_2=e_2,
\]
with the mixed products equal to zero.

Write an arbitrary second bilinear product as
\[
e_1*e_1=a e_1+b e_2,
\]
\[
e_1*e_2=c e_1+d e_2,
\]
\[
e_2*e_1=r e_1+s e_2,
\]
and
\[
e_2*e_2=g e_1+h e_2.
\]

Then \((\cdot,*)\) is a compatible pre-Lie pair if and only if
\[
\boxed{
r=c,\quad s=-b,\quad g=-c,\quad d=-b,
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
b(a+b+c-h)=0,\qquad c(a+b+c-h)=0.
}
\]

Thus the affine variety of compatible second products is exactly
\[
\boxed{
H\cup P,
}
\]
where
\[
H=\{h=a+b+c\}\cong\mathbb A^3
\]
and
\[
P=\{b=c=0\}\cong\mathbb A^2,
\]
with
\[
H\cap P=\{b=c=0,\ h=a\}\cong\mathbb A^1.
\]

The automorphism group of the fixed first product is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot)\cong S_2,
\]
generated by the transposition
\[
\tau(e_1)=e_2,\qquad \tau(e_2)=e_1.
\]
On the four free coordinates it acts by
\[
\boxed{
(a,b,c,h)\longmapsto(h,-c,-b,a).
}
\]
Its fixed locus is
\[
a=h,\qquad c=-b,
\]
which is an affine plane.

Consequently, over every finite field \(\mathbf F_q\), the number of compatible second products on the fixed algebra is
\[
\boxed{q^3+q^2-q},
\]
and the number of automorphism-conjugacy classes is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{q(q^2+2q-1)}2.
}
\]

More precisely, there are exactly
\[
\boxed{q^2}
\]
singleton classes and
\[
\boxed{\frac{q(q^2-1)}2}
\]
two-element classes.

These formulas hold without excluding characteristic \(2\). In characteristic \(2\), the signs in the involution disappear, but the fixed locus still has dimension two and the same counts result.

## Assumptions and scope

A compatible pre-Lie pair means that \((A,\cdot)\), \((A,*)\), and \((A,\cdot+*)\) are pre-Lie algebras.

The first product is fixed throughout. It is the two-dimensional semisimple commutative associative algebra \(K\times K\), denoted \(\mathcal C_{07}\) in the cited complex classification.

The finite-field count concerns compatible second products on this fixed first algebra, and the quotient is by automorphisms of the first product. It is not a count of all two-dimensional compatible pre-Lie algebras over \(\mathbf F_q\).

The cited classification is over \(\mathbb C\). The equations and finite-field quotient here are proved directly over an arbitrary field.

## Proof

The pre-Lie identity is
\[
(x*y)*z-x*(y*z)=(y*x)*z-y*(x*z).
\]
Compatibility with the fixed product \(\cdot\) means that the same identity also holds for \(\cdot+*\).

Substituting the basis triples into the mixed part of the compatibility identity gives the four linear relations
\[
r=c,\qquad s=-b,\qquad g=-c,\qquad d=-b.
\]
Hence every compatible candidate has the form
\[
e_1*e_1=a e_1+b e_2,
\]
\[
e_1*e_2=e_2*e_1=c e_1-b e_2,
\]
and
\[
e_2*e_2=-c e_1+h e_2.
\]

Substitution into the remaining pre-Lie identity reduces all residual equations to
\[
b(a+b+c-h)=0,
\]
\[
c(a+b+c-h)=0.
\]
Because \(K\) is a field, either
\[
a+b+c-h=0
\]
or
\[
b=c=0.
\]
This proves the decomposition
\[
H\cup P.
\]
The intersection is obtained by imposing both conditions, giving
\[
b=c=0,\qquad h=a.
\]

Now determine the symmetry group of the fixed first product. The identity element
\[
1=e_1+e_2
\]
is fixed by every algebra automorphism, and \(e_1,e_2\) are the two primitive nonzero orthogonal idempotents summing to \(1\). Therefore every automorphism either fixes them both or swaps them. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot)=\{1,\tau\}.
\]

Conjugating the second product by \(\tau\) gives
\[
(a,b,c,h)\longmapsto(h,-c,-b,a).
\]
The fixed conditions are
\[
a=h,\qquad c=-b.
\]
They automatically lie in \(H\), so the fixed locus is an affine plane.

Over \(\mathbf F_q\), inclusion-exclusion on \(H\cup P\) gives
\[
|H\cup P|=q^3+q^2-q.
\]
The nontrivial automorphism fixes exactly
\[
q^2
\]
of these products. Burnside's lemma therefore gives
\[
\frac{(q^3+q^2-q)+q^2}{2}
=
\frac{q(q^2+2q-1)}2
\]
orbits.

Every orbit of the order-two group has size one or two. Hence the number of singleton orbits is \(q^2\), and the number of two-element orbits is
\[
\frac{(q^3+q^2-q)-q^2}{2}
=
\frac{q(q^2-1)}2.
\]

## Verification

The included checker exhaustively verifies the defining compatible pre-Lie identities over
\[
\mathbf F_2,\qquad \mathbf F_3,\qquad \mathbf F_5.
\]

For each field it enumerates all \(q^8\) bilinear second products on the fixed two-dimensional vector space and tests the pre-Lie identity for \(*\) and for \(\cdot+*\) on all ordered basis triples.

It independently verifies that the resulting products are exactly those satisfying the displayed equations. It then enumerates all invertible \(2\times2\) matrices, filters the algebra automorphisms of \((A,\cdot)\), obtains exactly the identity and swap, and constructs every orbit on compatible second products.

The exact outputs are
\[
q=2:\quad 10\text{ products},\ 7\text{ classes},\ 4\text{ singleton classes};
\]
\[
q=3:\quad 33\text{ products},\ 21\text{ classes},\ 9\text{ singleton classes};
\]
and
\[
q=5:\quad 145\text{ products},\ 85\text{ classes},\ 25\text{ singleton classes}.
\]

The checker returns `VERIFY_OK`.

Finite enumeration is not used to prove the arbitrary-field equations or the general finite-field formulas.

## Relationship to prior work

Abdelwahab, Kaygorodov, and Makhlouf classify complex two-dimensional compatible pre-Lie algebras. Their method first fixes a pre-Lie algebra and then classifies compatible products under its automorphism group. For the first product \(\mathcal C_{07}\), they compute an automorphism group of order two and split the compatible complex products into the families denoted \(\mathcal C_{38}\) and \(\mathcal C_{39}\), with the corresponding swap equivalences.

The equations above recover those two branches geometrically as the affine three-space \(H\) and affine plane \(P\), meeting in an affine line. The new finite-field statement counts the full fiber and its automorphism quotient over every \(\mathbf F_q\), including characteristic \(2\).

Liu and Chen develop deformation, cohomology, Nijenhuis-operator, and extension theory for compatible pre-Lie algebras, but their work does not provide this two-dimensional finite-field orbit census.

Targeted literature and semantic-database searches for the \(\mathcal C_{07}\) fiber, finite-field compatible pre-Lie counts, automorphism orbits, and the displayed closed formulas did not locate this result.

## Limitations

The result treats one distinguished first-product fiber, not the full finite-field classification of all two-dimensional compatible pre-Lie pairs.

The orbit count is for the action of automorphisms of the fixed algebra \((A,\cdot)\); it does not identify pairs after exchanging the two compatible products.

A specialized or unindexed finite-field treatment may contain an equivalent census.

Failed searches do not prove novelty.
