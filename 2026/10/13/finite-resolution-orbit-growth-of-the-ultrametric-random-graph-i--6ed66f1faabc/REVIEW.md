# Review

## Correctness
The source represents the ultrametric limit by branches whose distance is determined by the first level at which they differ, and proves strict metric homogeneity. Therefore a finite injective tuple below a fixed resolution is encoded by the successive equivalence relations “agree through level \(j\),” which form a refinement multichain from the one-block partition to the discrete partition. Conversely every such chain gives a labeled ultrametric with the allowed dyadic distances.

For the graph example, the age is the class of all finite looped graphs and the source's extension property realizes arbitrary finite graph extensions while preserving the metric. Thus the off-diagonal graph bits are independent of the metric hierarchy, giving the factor \(2^{\binom n2}\). Strict homogeneity then identifies labeled finite metric-graph patterns exactly with ordered-tuple automorphism orbits.

The polynomial-in-resolution statement follows by compressing every weak partition chain to its strict transitions. A strict chain with \(k\) transitions can place those transitions in \(\binom{h+1}{k}\) positions. The top binomial-basis coefficient is the number of maximal chains in the partition lattice, \(\prod_{k=2}^n\binom k2=n!(n-1)!/2^{n-1}\), yielding leading coefficient \(n!/2^{n-1}\).

## Value
The theorem turns a non-oligomorphic action with infinitely many metric spheres into a controlled family of finite-resolution profiles. For each arity, the number of visible orbits grows by an exact polynomial in the number of scales, and the leading constant is explicit. This quantifies how orbit complexity accumulates across the inverse-limit levels rather than merely saying that infinitely many orbits occur.

The binomial-basis formula also separates structural contributions by strict partition-chain length, making the profile interpretable in ordinary partition-lattice combinatorics.
