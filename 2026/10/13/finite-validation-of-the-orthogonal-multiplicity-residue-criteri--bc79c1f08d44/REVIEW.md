# Review: Finite validation of the orthogonal-multiplicity residue criterion through degree 22

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is deliberately finite and its complete domain is enumerated. The continuant recurrence gives an exact positivity predicate for \(m(f)\); squarefreeness and coprimality are exact polynomial-gcd/evaluation tests; every non-linear factor is recovered exactly; and each \(\psi_P(f)\) is evaluated by exact inversion and trace in \(\mathbb F_2[T]/(P)\). The packaged replay checks 1,856,852 cases with zero mismatches. Distinct-continuant counts for degrees \(15\)–\(22\) also reproduce the published counts exactly.

## Originality
PASS, with a stated residual literature risk. arXiv:2610.02352v1 states the same residue criterion as an unproved identity and reports exhaustive squarefree verification only through degree \(14\). Targeted semantic-database searches found no result stating the degree-\(22\) cutoff or a stronger theorem that implies it. Blackburn's paper predates the new residue invariant; Lauder's work contains related continued-fraction criteria, but the motivating 2026 paper cites that work while still identifying its equation (4) as conjectural. Full text of Lauder's thesis was not available in this check, so an unnoticed equivalence is the principal residual risk.

## Value
PASS. The verified criterion is not an incidental identity: it is explicitly the remaining conjectural local input in the motivating source's probabilistic model for positive orthogonal multiplicity. Extending the complete squarefree prime-to-linear verification from degree \(14\) to degree \(22\) adds eight full degrees and 1,856,852 exact cases. Degree \(22\) is a natural cutoff because it passes the degree-\(21\) historical multiplicity table that the source identifies as the previous published boundary.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2610.02352v1 itself: its equation (4) is exactly the criterion checked here, but its exhaustive verification stops at degree \(14\). Blackburn (1998) supplies the orthogonal-multiplicity structure and historical degree-\(21\) tabulation; Lauder (1999 and thesis) supplies related continued-fraction criteria. This finding remains a finite verification, not an arbitrary-degree proof, and excludes repeated factors and the two linear factors.
