# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The digit-sum identity \(t_q(qn+a)=(t_q(n)+a)\bmod q\) gives a globally bounded centered prefix sum. The explicit high-digit replacement gadgets preserve every prescribed arithmetic residue while changing digit sum through all \(q\) phases. Consequently any nonzero residue sampled at a fixed progression of block boundaries witnesses nonconstant centered sums. This excludes arbitrarily long equal-sum blocks unless the length is divisible by \(q\). Positive witnesses are concatenations of aligned permutation blocks. Finite checker output independently corroborates the prefix identity, phase saturation in specified small cases, and sampled block orders; it is not an infinite certificate.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** Popoli–Shallit–Stipulanti (arXiv:2410.02409v1, Sections 3.1.2 and 5) address ternary generalized Thue–Morse additive complexity and Tribonacci additive-power orders, but not the complete all-base fixed-order unbounded-exponent spectrum. Chen–Wen (arXiv:1809.06554, Theorem 1 and Lemmas 1–2) classify abelian complexity and give Parikh decompositions but no theorem on arbitrarily high additive powers at a prescribed order. The older critical-exponent result arXiv:0710.4031 treats exact-word repetitions, not equal integer sums. No equivalent statement appeared in targeted published-result comparisons. This is evidence of an unaddressed implication gap, not a proof of priority; unindexed literature is a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Additive-power orders are an explicit research theme of the 2024 source. The theorem gives a complete classification simultaneously for all digit bases and all block orders, distinguishing the general existence of arbitrarily long additive powers from the far stronger property of their existence at one fixed order. The modular-gadget lemma provides a reusable arithmetic structure, and the exclusion bound is uniform over starting positions. The work does not claim sharp finite exponents or resolution of the distinct Tribonacci problem.
