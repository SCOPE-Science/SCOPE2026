# Fixed-point class groups and the sharp Gorenstein boundary of two-deletion Segre toric varieties

## Finding
For all integers \(a,b\ge3\), let \(G_{a,b}\) be the bipartite graph obtained from \(K_{a,b}\) by deleting the two vertex-disjoint edges \((1,1)\) and \((2,2)\). Define \(X_{a,b}\) to be the projective edge toric variety whose homogeneous coordinates are \(z_{ij}=x_i y_j\) for the \(ab-2\) allowed edges. It is a normal projective variety of dimension \(a+b-2\), embedded in \(\mathbb P^{ab-3}_{\mathbb C}\).

Each allowed edge \((i,j)\) gives a torus-fixed point \(p_{ij}\). The Zariski local divisor-class groups, smoothness and canonical Cartier conditions are completely determined as follows:

| Fixed-point type | Number | \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_{a,b},p})\) | Gorenstein precisely when |
|---|---:|---|---|
| \(i,j\ge3\) | \((a-2)(b-2)\) | \(0\) | always (smooth) |
| \(i\in\{1,2\},\ j\ge3\) | \(2(b-2)\) | \(\mathbb Z\) | \(a=3\) |
| \(i\ge3,\ j\in\{1,2\}\) | \(2(a-2)\) | \(\mathbb Z\) | \(b=3\) |
| \((i,j)=(1,2),(2,1)\) | \(2\) | \(\mathbb Z^2\) | \(a=b=3\) |

At these fixed points, \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein is equivalent to Gorenstein. Hence \(X_{a,b}\) is globally Gorenstein, equivalently globally \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein, exactly when \(a=b=3\). The singular fixed-point count is \(2(a+b-3)\). In particular, deleting the second disjoint monomial introduces two fixed points with a rank-two local class group, not present in the one-deletion model.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\); \(a,b\) are arbitrary integers at least three. An edge label is an ordered pair of row and column indices. The homogeneous coordinate semigroup has exponent vectors \(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf f_j\) for every allowed edge. Since the graph is connected and bipartite, its edge ring is normal by the Ohsugi--Hibi normality criterion. The local groups in the table are for Zariski local rings at **torus-fixed closed points**, not an assertion about all closed points. Their Gorenstein conditions concern the projective toric variety, not the homogeneous edge ring or the lattice polytope being Gorenstein in the Ehrhart-ring sense.

For a fixed allowed edge \((i,j)\), use lattice coordinates \(R_p=\mathbf e_p-\mathbf e_i\) for \(p\ne i\) and \(C_q=\mathbf f_q-\mathbf f_j\) for \(q\ne j\). The local affine chart has saturated monoid cone \(K_{ij}\) generated by the vectors \(R_p+C_q\) of all other allowed pairs, with \(R_i=C_j=0\). These are lattice coordinates in rank \(d=a+b-2\). The formulas below describe the *tangent semigroup cone* and its **facet normals**; these must not be confused with the tangent-cone generating rays when calculating local divisor classes.

## Proof
**1. Lattice and normality.** Every allowed grid exponent vector is a vertex of the product-of-simplices exponent polytope, so the allowed edges index all torus-fixed points. The edge graph is bipartite and connected; Ohsugi--Hibi's odd-cycle criterion gives normality of the homogeneous edge semigroup ring, and therefore of each degree-zero chart. At an ordinary-ordinary vertex, all \(R_p\) and \(C_q\) occur as generators, so their lattice is full. At any special-row/ordinary-column vertex, every \(R_p\) and every \(C_q\) except \(C_i\) occurs, and \(C_i\) is the difference \((R_p+C_i)-R_p\). The transposed assertion holds for ordinary-row/special-column vertices. At a cross vertex, the generators include \(R_p\), \(C_q\) for \(p,q\ge3\), \(R_j+C_q\), \(R_p+C_i\), and \(R_j+C_i\); subtraction recovers the remaining \(R_j,C_i\). Thus the lattice generated by the affine chart semigroup is the full stated coordinate lattice in every case.

**2. Explicit tangent cones.** Write all coordinates with respect to the basis in the previous paragraph.

If \(i,j\ge3\), every coordinate-axis generator is present; all other vectors are sums of two axes. Thus \(K_{ij}\) is the nonnegative orthant in dimension \(d\).

If \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and \(j\ge3\), write \(r_p\) for the coordinates of \(R_p\), and \(c_q\) for those of \(C_q\). The generators are precisely the row axes \(R_p\), the column axes \(C_q\) for \(q\ne i,j\), and \(R_p+C_i\), up to decomposable elements. Therefore the cone is cut out by
\[
r_p\ge0\ (p\ne i),\qquad c_q\ge0\ (q\ne j),\qquad \sum_{p\ne i}r_p-c_i\ge0.
\]
For sufficiency, allocate the amount \(c_i\) among row coordinates \(r_p\), producing nonnegative coefficients on the generators \(R_p+C_i\); put unused row mass on \(R_p\) and all other column mass on their corresponding axes. Such an allocation exists exactly when \(0\le c_i\le\sum_{p\ne i}r_p\). The transposed argument gives the ordinary-row/special-column cone with the extra inequality \(\sum_{q\ne j}c_q-r_j\ge0\).

At a cross vertex, by symmetry take \((i,j)=(1,2)\). Put \(x=r_2\), \(y=c_1\), and retain \(r_p,c_q\) for \(p,q\ge3\). Its generators, after discarding decomposable sums, are the axes \(R_p,C_q\) for \(p,q\ge3\), together with \(R_2+C_1\), \(R_2+C_q\) and \(R_p+C_1\). Consequently
\[
\begin{split}
&x,y,r_p,c_q\ge0\quad (p,q\ge3),\\
&-x+y+\sum_{q=3}^{b}c_q\ge0,\\
&x-y+\sum_{p=3}^{a}r_p\ge0.
\end{split}
\]
Necessity holds on every listed generator. For sufficiency, choose a nonnegative coefficient \(t\) on \(R_2+C_1\) satisfying
\[
\max\!\left(0,x-\sum_{q\ge3}c_q,y-\sum_{p\ge3}r_p\right)\le t\le\min(x,y).
\]
The two displayed inequalities are exactly what makes this interval nonempty. Allocate \(x-t\) across the available \(R_2+C_q\) and \(y-t\) across \(R_p+C_1\), and put all remaining row and column mass on the available axes. This proves the exact cone equality without an appeal to finite enumeration.

**3. All inequalities really are facets.** Every coordinate inequality listed above is a facet: setting its coordinate to zero leaves a full-dimensional cone inside that hyperplane, as witnessed by the other generators. The additional special-row inequality is a facet because the \(R_p+C_i\), together with the unrelated column axes, span its hyperplane; likewise after transposition. At a cross vertex each of the two additional inequalities has an independent collection of saturating generators of rank \(d-1\); coordinate inequalities do as well. Their coefficients are primitive integral vectors. No additional facets exist because Step 2 has proved equality with the stated intersection of half-spaces.

**4. Local divisor classes.** For a saturated affine toric cone \(K\) in the character lattice, its torus-invariant prime divisors correspond to primitive **facet normals** of \(K\). The toric divisor sequence gives \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,p})\) as the cokernel of the character-to-divisor pairing. All chart facet-normal lists contain the \(d\) coordinate covectors, an integral basis of the one-parameter subgroup lattice. Thus the sequence has torsion-free cokernel and its rank is exactly the number of additional facet normals: zero, one, one and two in the four cases. The affine class group and the class group localized at the unique closed torus orbit agree, since all invariant prime divisors contain that point. A nonzero class-group rank forces the corresponding fixed point to be singular; the orthant charts are smooth.

**5. Canonical divisor and global threshold.** Normal affine toric coordinate rings are Cohen–Macaulay, so Cartier canonical divisor is equivalent to Gorensteinness. The canonical Weil divisor of a normal affine toric variety is \(-\sum_\rho D_\rho\). It is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier precisely if there is a rational character taking value one on **every primitive facet normal**. Because the coordinate normals already form a lattice basis, the only candidate has all coordinate values equal to one, and it is automatically integral if it works. At a special-row/ordinary-column point the extra facet normal evaluates to \((a-1)-1=a-2\), so the condition is exactly \(a=3\); at the transposed point it is \(b=3\). At a cross point the two extra normals evaluate to \(b-2\) and \(a-2\), respectively. Both equal one precisely when \(a=b=3\). Orthant charts always satisfy the condition. The collection of torus-fixed affine charts covers the complete projective toric variety, proving its global Gorenstein/\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein criterion. Finally, adding the three non-smooth point counts gives \(2(b-2)+2(a-2)+2=2(a+b-3)\).

## Verification
The included file `verify.py` checks the exact exponent-difference generators, the proposed primitive facet inequalities, their rank-\(d-1\) supporting sets, the presence of an integral coordinate-normal basis, the class-group ranks, and the Gorenstein tests for all \(3\le a,b\le6\). It uses Python 3 and SymPy. Run `python verify.py` from the directory containing this file; `verification_output.txt` records the observed output. This finite validation does not replace the constructive cone and lattice proof for arbitrary \(a,b\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi--Hibi (1998) prove normality of bipartite edge semigroup rings, and Ohsugi--Hibi (2008, Lemma 1.4 and Theorem 1.8) give general edge-adjacency and global simplicity criteria for graphical edge polytopes. In particular, those results already imply the *adjacency counts* of the present polytope; adjacency alone is not claimed as novel. Bigdeli--Herzog--Lu (arXiv:1508.01290, §7) analyze deformation/rigidity for a complete bipartite graph with **one** deleted edge, a different invariant and family. Established one-coordinate-deletion Segre computations yield only local class groups of rank at most one, whereas the two-disjoint-deletion cross charts here have rank two and a new two-parameter canonical obstruction. General graph-Ehrhart-ring Gorenstein criteria (including work on perfectly matchable subgraph polytopes) apply to different rings and do not automatically determine canonical Cartier conditions of these individual projective charts. The result is a pointwise toric class-group and Cartier classification, not a claim of discovering the general toric divisor sequence.

## Limitations
The theorem describes torus-fixed **Zariski local rings** and the global \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein property, not the complete singular or non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein stratification away from those points. No minimal free resolutions, singularity deformations, or characteristic-positive extensions are claimed. The computation specializes well-known toric divisor tools; an unindexed earlier specialization to the same two-deletion polytope cannot be ruled out solely by unsuccessful searches.
