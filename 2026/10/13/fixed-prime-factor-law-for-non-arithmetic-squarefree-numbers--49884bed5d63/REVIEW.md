# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Ore's squarefree characterization gives an exact bijection between
non-arithmetic squarefree integers with \(r\) prime factors and integers
\[
2m
\]
where \(m\) is squarefree with \(r-1\) prime factors, each congruent to
\(1\pmod4\). Prabhu's Theorem 1.3 gives the asymptotic density
\(2^{-(r-1)}\) of a prescribed all-\(1\pmod4\) residue tuple inside the
fixed-\((r-1)\)-prime Landau layer. Replacing the height by \(x/2\) contributes
the additional factor \(1/2\). The case \(r=2\) is the ordinary prime number
theorem in arithmetic progressions. Dividing by Landau's squarefree
\(r\)-prime asymptotic gives the stated relative deficit.

The finite checker directly verifies the structural criterion and finite
counts. No finite enumeration is used to justify the asymptotic.

## Originality
PASS. The exact structural criterion and the restricted-prime Landau theorem
are prior results and are explicitly treated as such. The closest global
squarefree-arithmetic result has a Landau--Ramanujan-scale secondary term after
summing over all prime-factor layers; it does not imply the asymptotic for any
fixed layer. A separate two-prime-power-block density theorem excludes the
squarefree equal-exponent setting. Searches for fixed-\(\omega\),
fixed-prime-factor, \(r\)-almost-prime, and squarefree arithmetic-number
formulations did not locate the displayed layer law or its relative constant.

Residual risk remains that this short synthesis may have appeared as an
unstated corollary or exercise in literature not indexed under arithmetic
numbers.

## Value
PASS. The fixed-\(r\) theorem resolves how the global density-one phenomenon
looks inside every natural squarefree almost-prime layer. It exposes a
different scale from the unrestricted Landau--Ramanujan secondary term and
gives an explicit \(r\)-dependent relative deficit. This is a natural
stratification by prime-factor complexity, not a bounded census or arbitrary
parameter slice.
