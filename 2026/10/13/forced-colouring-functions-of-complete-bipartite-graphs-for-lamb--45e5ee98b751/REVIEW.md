# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For λ≥3, any first forcing step on one shore of Kmn witnesses λ−1 distinct colours on the opposite shore. Properness forces all initial colours on target shore to be the sole missing colour; after target shore fills, it is monochromatic and cannot force any missing vertex back because λ−1≥2. Thus the opposite shore was fully coloured and surjects onto λ−1 colours. Both orientations are exhaustive, and all total proper colourings are counted by P(Kmn;λ)−λQ_m(λ−1)−λQ_n(λ−1) after subtracting overlap; the two overlap families are disjoint for λ≥3. The chromatic polynomial follows by number of distinct colours on A. Independent direct state simulation compared exact rational forcing probabilities with formula for λ=3,4,5 at 11 small (m,n) profiles including cases with no forcing beyond total assignments; all matched. This is an all-λ proof, not inference from enumerated profiles.

## originality

PASS

Prior published Resultary theorem exactly proves the λ=3 specialization and its first-forcing mechanism: that part is NOT NEW. The new whole-parameter theorem is not fully covered: λ≥4 has λ−1-colour surjections and total-colouring configurations with varying allocations between shores. Separate published complete multipartite theorem assumes palette equals χ=r≥3, and so cannot imply λ>2 on bipartite graphs. Exact uniform λ≥4 formula survives as a genuinely broader result, although its first-move proof extrapolates a known argument; Farr 2026 full text remains inaccessible and is recorded as a material priority risk.

## value

PASS

A closed probability polynomial for EVERY palette size λ≥4 on every complete bipartite graph is a natural complete classification in the hard-colour regime; it systematically handles surjective saturation and previously absent (a,λ−a) total colour allocations, not only the known three-colour small examples. This supplies reusable exact benchmark values for an invariant known generally computationally hard. The partial prior λ=3 case is explicitly not counted as novel value.
