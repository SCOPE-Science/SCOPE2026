# Four dimensions are minimal for Grün failure in multilinear algebras
## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field. By a \(k\)-algebra here we mean a \(k\)-vector space with one bilinear multiplication; no associativity, commutativity, or multiplicative identity is assumed. Such an algebra is an expansion of an abelian group and hence is a semiassociative Mal'cev algebra with Mal'cev term
\[
m(x,y,z)=x-y+z.
\]
Write
\[
Z_{\mathrm{TC}}(A)=0/\zeta_A,\qquad Z^{\mathrm{TC}}_2(A)=0/\zeta_2
\]
for the zero blocks of the term-condition center and second center. Among finite-dimensional perfect \(k\)-algebras, the minimum dimension for
\[
Z^{\mathrm{TC}}_2(A)\supsetneq Z_{\mathrm{TC}}(A)
\]
is \(4\).

A sharp example has basis \(a,b,c,d\) and only the nonzero basis products
\[
b^2=a,\qquad bc=b,\qquad cb=c,\qquad c^2=d,\qquad ab=d.
\]
It satisfies
\[
A^2=A,\qquad Z_{\mathrm{TC}}(A)=kd,\qquad Z^{\mathrm{TC}}_2(A)=ka\oplus kd.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(k\) is arbitrary, including characteristic \(2\). The algebra is a single-sorted multilinear algebra with operations \(+,-,0\), scalar multiplications, and one bilinear product. Perfectness and centrality are the term-condition-commutator notions used for semiassociative Mal'cev algebras.

For a \(k\)-algebra \(A\), set
\[
A^2=\operatorname{span}_k\{xy:x,y\in A\},\qquad
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=\{z\in A:zA=Az=0\}.
\]
The result is a minimum-dimension statement in this multilinear class. It does not claim uniqueness of the four-dimensional witness.

## Proof
First identify the commutator and center in any bilinear algebra.

For the total congruence, Wires's Proposition 2.10 says that the zero block of the term-condition commutator is generated by values of binary polynomials absorbing \((0,0)\) to \(0\). Multiplication itself is such a polynomial, so the block contains \(A^2\). Conversely, for an absorbing binary polynomial \(c(x,y)\), bilinearity permits expansion into bracketed monomials. Since
\[
c(x,y)=c(x,y)-c(x,0)-c(0,y)+c(0,0),
\]
every surviving monomial contains both variables and hence contains a multiplication; all its values lie in \(A^2\). Therefore
\[
0/[1_A,1_A]=A^2.
\]
Thus \(A\) is perfect exactly when \(A=A^2\).

Wires's Corollary 2.11 similarly gives
\[
Z_{\mathrm{TC}}(A)=\operatorname{Ann}(A).
\]
Indeed, the absorbing polynomials \(xy\) and \(yx\) force a central element to annihilate on both sides. Conversely, if \(z\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)\), the same mixed-monomial expansion shows that every absorbing polynomial vanishes when one input is \(z\): at the first multiplication node meeting the \(z\)-leaf, the monomial becomes \(0\).

Applying this center identity to the quotient gives
\[
Z^{\mathrm{TC}}_2(A)=
\{z\in A:zA+Az\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(A)\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the displayed algebra, the products \(b^2,bc,cb,c^2\) are respectively \(a,b,c,d\), so \(A^2=A\) and \(A\) is perfect. If
\[
x=\alpha a+\beta b+\gamma c+\delta d,
\]
then
\[
xb=\beta a+\gamma c+\alpha d.
\]
Hence \(xA=0\) forces \(\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0\), while \(d\) annihilates all basis elements on both sides. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Ann}(A)=kd.
\]
Modulo \(kd\),
\[
xb\equiv\beta a+\gamma c,\quad
xc\equiv\beta b,\quad
bx\equiv\beta a+\gamma b,\quad
cx\equiv\beta c.
\]
Equation (1) therefore holds exactly when \(\beta=\gamma=0\), so
\[
Z^{\mathrm{TC}}_2(A)=ka\oplus kd.
\]
Since \(ab=d\ne0\), the element \(a\) is not central.

For minimality, suppose \(A\) is finite-dimensional, perfect, and has strict second center. Put \(C=\operatorname{Ann}(A)\). A witness outside \(C\) maps to a nonzero annihilator element of \(A/C\), so \(C\ne0\). The quotient \(B=A/C\) remains perfect because \(A=A^2\).

No nonzero perfect algebra of dimension at most \(2\) has nonzero annihilator. In dimension \(1\), a nonzero annihilator forces zero multiplication. In dimension \(2\), if \(0\ne L\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(B)\), choose \(u\notin L\); every product in \(B=ku+L\) is a scalar multiple of \(u^2\), so \(\dim B^2\le1\), contradicting \(B^2=B\). Hence
\[
\dim(A/C)\ge3.
\]
Since \(C\ne0\), one gets
\[
\dim A\ge4.
\]
The example attains the bound.

## Verification
The standalone `check_grun.py` reconstructs the multiplication table, verifies perfectness, solves the annihilator equations, solves the second-center equations modulo the annihilator, and checks the same identities over several small finite fields as a characteristic sanity check. The recorded output in `verification_output.txt` ends with `CHECK_OK`.

The finite computation is only a table cross-check. The lower bound over all fields is the symbolic quotient argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Wires proves a conditional Grün theorem for perfect semiassociative Mal'cev algebras, explicitly states that multilinear algebras lie in the class, and asks which perfect semiassociative Mal'cev algebras satisfy an unconditional Grün lemma. The inspected paper gives unconditional positive results for groups and multiplicative Lie algebras and discusses skew-brace failures, but it does not give a multilinear-algebra counterexample or a minimum dimension.

Aichinger and Mudrinski provide the absorbing-polynomial commutator machinery used in the general theory. Tsang proves failure for perfect skew braces, whose signature has two group laws and a derived brace operation; that does not imply the sharp dimension cutoff for a vector-space algebra with one bilinear product.

## Limitations
The theorem gives the first possible dimension and one explicit witness. It does not classify all four-dimensional witnesses or determine which additional identities restore the Grün property.

Targeted searches found no prior statement of the four-dimensional minimum or the displayed table in this Grün context. This is not a global novelty certificate: an uncrawled small-algebra catalogue could contain an isomorphic multiplication table without the same term-condition interpretation.
