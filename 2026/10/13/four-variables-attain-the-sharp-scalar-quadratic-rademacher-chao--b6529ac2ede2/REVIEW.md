# Review: Four variables attain the sharp scalar quadratic Rademacher-chaos constant

## Correctness
**PASS.** The quadratic chaos becomes a degree-one-plus-degree-two Walsh polynomial on three independent signs. Orthogonality to the constant and cubic characters forces each four-point parity class to have zero total. The sharp four-number inequality \(\|y\|_1\ge\sqrt2\|y\|_2\) for a zero-sum real four-vector then yields \(\|h\|_2\le2\|h\|_1\) after exact normalization. Its equality conditions, combined with equality in \(a+b\ge\sqrt{{a^2+b^2}}\), force support on exactly two reduced cube points in one parity class; cube symmetries turn this into the stated product factorization. The two- and three-variable constants follow from the same zero-sum argument.

Risk: the argument is specifically real scalar. It does not extend automatically to complex or normed-space coefficients because the signed positive/negative decomposition in the zero-sum lemma is real-valued.

## Originality
**PASS.** The full Ivanisvili–Tkocz paper was inspected at its theorem and Remarks 6–8. It records the conjectured degree-two constant \(2\) and the product witness but does not state the complete four-variable scalar theorem or equality classification. Candidate-specific searches in the published-finding index and public literature used the four-variable quadratic-chaos, finite-dimensional Pełczyński, and homogeneous Walsh formulations and found no covering result. The closest indexed Walsh findings concern phase flatness or sparse Fourier support and do not imply an \(L^1\)-to-\(L^2\) bound.

Risk: Oleszkiewicz's 1999 chapter is the historical source cited for the conjecture. Only bibliographic material and later citations were available in the inspected public sources, not its full text, so a finite-four-variable observation there remains a genuine residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** Four variables are not an arbitrary slice: the conjectured extremizer for degree two already uses exactly four coordinates. The theorem shows that this first potentially sharp dimension satisfies the conjectured scalar constant, identifies every equality case, and distinguishes it from the smaller constants \(1\) and \(\sqrt2\) in dimensions two and three. It therefore isolates the first nontrivial finite-dimensional geometry of the open moment-comparison problem.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Ivanisvili–Tkocz, arXiv:1807.04358 / DOI:10.4310/ARKIV.2019.V57.N1.A7. It gives improved general bounds and records Pełczyński's degree-two conjecture but not the finite-four-variable theorem proved here. The result is restricted to real scalar coefficients and does not settle the arbitrary-dimensional or vector-valued conjecture.
