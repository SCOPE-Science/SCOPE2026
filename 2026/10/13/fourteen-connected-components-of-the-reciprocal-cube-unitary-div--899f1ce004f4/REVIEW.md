# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The multiplicative prime-power identity is exact. The closure from primes at least \(11\) equals the interval \([1,P]\): five finite rational inequalities cover primes \(11\) through \(23\); Nagura's theorem controls subsequent prime ratios; and the complete-subsums argument covers the entire interval. Prime exponents above explicit cutoffs overlap the tail interval, leaving thirty-two closed intervals whose maximal merge has fourteen components. The verifier checks the complete finite merge by strict rational enclosures, including all gaps. The original prime-interval theorem is inspected independently. The proof applies to all positive integers, not merely a numerical range.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Defant's original unitary-range paper (arXiv:1507.02654, §§1–3) establishes a connectedness threshold but no exact component count or endpoints at \(r=3\); its future-directions section highlights the component-count problem. A published exact calculation at \(r=2\) concerns a different parameter and does not imply the fourteen-component determination at \(r=3\). Searches for the reciprocal-cube unitary range, equivalent Euler-product spectra, and explicit component counts found no covering published theorem. Unindexed or differently formulated results remain a possible overlap risk; search absence alone is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
The count \(14\) is an exact topological invariant of the naturally defined, unrestricted arithmetic-function range at the next integer exponent beyond the disconnected \(r=2\) case. The result gives all component endpoints, not just the count, and exhibits how the first four small primes govern the gaps while all larger primes form an interval. The exponent is motivated by the established threshold problem rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. No general theorem for all exponents is claimed or required.

## Sources and limitations

The exact finite arithmetic is reproducible but is not a replacement for the infinite argument.
