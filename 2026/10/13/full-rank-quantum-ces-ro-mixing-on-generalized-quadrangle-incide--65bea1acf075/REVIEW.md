# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof determines the entire adjacency spectrum of the generalized-quadrangle incidence graph from its point graph, derives the distance matrix polynomials from the intersection array, and obtains the five Cesàro eigenvalues via the normalized spectral-projector entries. The minus branch has a strictly positive square-plus-positive decomposition. Exact symbolic identities for all eigenvalues and all distance-class entries, together with independent numerical graph reconstructions for orders two and three, substantiate the argument; the unrestricted proof is analytical.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The projector-Schur-square identity (Godsil 2011; Coutinho et al. 2018), general association-scheme evaluation (Coutinho and Guo 2024), and GQ incidence parameters (Huang 2014) are prior inputs. The inspected sources did not exhibit this entire parameter-uniform Cesàro spectrum, strict full-rank result, or its thin-boundary drop. Prior symmetric-design incidence results do not apply to generalized quadrangles. A broader unlocated Krein-matrix evaluation might nevertheless imply these expressions; searches alone cannot establish uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem completely evaluates a natural infinite incidence-graph class of quantum transport networks, including every distance-resolved transition probability and a sharp rank transition. The result makes no claim to resolve the full-rank question for primitive distance-regular graphs, because this family is bipartite and imprimitive.

The scope is conditional on existence of generalized quadrangles of balanced order with at least two points beyond the thin case; unbalanced geometries, instantaneous mixing, and discrete-time quantum walks remain outside the proof. Finite tests corroborate but do not prove universal formulas.
