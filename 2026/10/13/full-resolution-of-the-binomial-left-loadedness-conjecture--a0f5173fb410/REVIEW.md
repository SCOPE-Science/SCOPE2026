# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The argument covers every boundary and quantifier in the claim. The symmetric boundary \(n=2m\), small means \(m=1,2\), and the general case \(m\ge3\) are separated. In the general case the exact mass-ratio identity forces a U-shaped sequence, while a proved endpoint lemma gives the required terminal sign. Simmons' classical inequality supplies the initial sign. Exact finite checks are supplementary and are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating paper explicitly states the full \(n\ge2m\) assertion as Conjecture 4.1 and reports only \(m\in\{1,2\}\) and \(4\le m\le n/3\) as established binomial ranges. Searches for the conjecture number, left-loadedness aliases, symmetric mass ratios, and the endpoint mechanism found no covering full-range theorem. A prior result for \(m=3\) is a strict special case and is therefore subsumed rather than counted as independent novelty.

## Value
PASS. Resolving the complete domain of an explicit published conjecture is mathematically substantive. The proof also isolates the missing structural ingredient: an all-parameter endpoint inequality that lets the source's U-shape strategy operate beyond its published range.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Fokkink–Papavassiliou–Pelekis (2022), which defines left-loadedness and states the target conjecture. Simmons' theorem supplies only the first coefficient inequality. A public MathOverflow question asks for the stronger \(L_2\) property; this finding does not settle that stronger question for \(m\ge3\). The theorem is confined to integer-mean binomial laws with success probability at most one half.
