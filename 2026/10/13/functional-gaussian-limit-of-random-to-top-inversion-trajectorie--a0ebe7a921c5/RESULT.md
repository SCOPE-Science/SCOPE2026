# Functional Gaussian limit of random-to-top inversion trajectories

## Finding

Start with the identity permutation of the labels \(1,\ldots,n\). At each discrete time select a position uniformly and move its card to the first position. Let \(I_{k,n}\) count inversions of the resulting permutation at step \(k\). For every fixed \(T\in(0,\infty)\), set
\[
Y_n(t)=n^{-3/2}\bigl(I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor,n}-\mathbb E I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor,n}\bigr),\quad 0\le t\le T.
\]
Then \(Y_n\) converges in distribution in \(D([0,T])\), equipped with the \(J_1\) topology, to the unique continuous solution of
\[
dY(t)=-2Y(t)\,dt+\sqrt{\nu(t)}\,dW(t),\quad Y(0)=0,\qquad \nu(t)=\frac{4+8e^{-3t}-9e^{-4t}}{36}.
\]
For every \(0\le s\le t\le T\), the covariance is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(Y(s),Y(t)\bigr)=e^{-2(t-s)}V(s),\qquad V(s)=\frac{1+8e^{-3s}-9(1+s)e^{-4s}}{36}.
\]
In particular, every finite collection of times converges jointly to this Gaussian law. The deterministic centering has the exact expression
\[
\mathbb E I_{k,n}=\frac{n(n-1)}4\left(1-\left(1-\frac2n\right)^k\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The deck starts in increasing label order. The selected positions are independent and uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\); equivalently, the selected labels are independent and uniform. No assumption of stationarity is imposed. The conclusion is for each fixed finite \(T\), in particular it is not a convergence assertion on time intervals growing with \(n\). The theorem covers all fixed times including \(t=0\). All conditional variances and martingales below refer to the natural filtration generated by the selected cards.

## Proof

**1. Exact martingale regression.** For a permutation \(\pi\), write \(I(\pi)=\#\{i<j:\pi_i>\pi_j\}\). If its card at position \(j\) is moved to the top, its inversion increment is
\[
h_j(\pi)=\#\{i<j:\pi_i<\pi_j\}-\#\{i<j:\pi_i>\pi_j\}.
\]
Indeed, all unaffected pairs keep their relative ordering and the chosen card reverses its order with exactly the preceding cards. Summing over \(j\) gives
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n h_j(\pi)=\binom n2-2I(\pi).
\]
Thus the conditional mean increment is \(m_n(\pi)=(n-1)/2-2I(\pi)/n\). If \(\xi_{k+1}=I_{k+1,n}-I_{k,n}-m_n(\pi_{k,n})\), then \(\xi_{k+1}\) is a martingale difference, \(|\xi_{k+1}|\le2n\), and \(Z_k=I_{k,n}-\mathbb E I_{k,n}\) satisfies the **exact** recursion
\[
Z_{k+1}=\left(1-\frac2n\right)Z_k+\xi_{k+1}.
\]
Solving the same recursion for the mean proves the asserted centering formula.

**2. Deterministic empirical-permutation limit.** Put \(b=e^{-t}\), \(a=1-b\). At step \(k_n\), where \(k_n/n\to t\), the number \(K_n\) of distinct selected labels has \(K_n/n\to a\) in probability: its expectation is \(n[1-(1-1/n)^{k_n}]\) and its variance is \(O(n)\), by bounded differences (or negative correlation of occupancy indicators). Conditional on the selected-label set, the selected labels appear first, ordered by their last selection times, and the remaining labels are increasing. By exchangeability, conditional on \(K_n\), the selected set is a uniform subset of that size and its front ordering is a uniform permutation, independent of the subset.

Consider the empirical probability measure \(\gamma_n=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{(j/n,\pi_{k_n,n}(j)/n)}\). Standard hypergeometric variance bounds for a uniform subset, order-statistic concentration for its increasing complement, and variance bounds for a uniform front permutation show that, for every rectangle with rational endpoints, its empirical mass converges in probability to that of
\[
\gamma_a(dx,du)=\mathbf1_{0<x<a}\,dx\,du+b\,\delta_{x=a+bu}\,du,\qquad (x,u)\in[0,1]^2.
\]
Here the second term denotes the image of \(b\,du\) under \(u\mapsto(a+bu,u)\). To see the two components directly, the front block of relative size \(a\) has asymptotically uniform independent position and label coordinates, whereas each unselected label \(u\) occupies asymptotic position \(a+bu\). A finite rational-rectangle approximation gives \(\gamma_n\Rightarrow\gamma_a\) weakly in probability. The same argument includes \(t=0\), when the first component vanishes and the limit is the diagonal measure.

**3. Exact limiting predictable quadratic variation.** Let \(H_j=h_j(\pi_{k_n,n})/n\). In empirical-measure form, the signed lower-left count \(H_j\) converges, in second empirical moment, to
\[
H(x,u)=\int_{x'<x}\operatorname{sgn}(u-u')\,\gamma_a(dx',du').
\]
The inversion density and squared increment are two- and three-fold bounded integrals against \(\gamma_n\), with indicator kernels whose discontinuities lie in equality-coordinate hyperplanes. Since both coordinate marginals of \(\gamma_a\) are Lebesgue measure, all these hyperplanes have zero product-measure mass. Weak convergence of the empirical measures therefore justifies the limiting integrals, including the diagonal component of \(\gamma_a\).

On the uniformly scrambled front block \(0<x<a\), \(H(x,u)=x(2u-1)\). On the increasing tail \(x=a+bu\), \(H(x,u)=a(2u-1)+bu=(2-b)u-a\). Consequently
\[
\frac1{n^3}\sum_{j=1}^n h_j(\pi_{k_n,n})^2\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\ \int H^2\,d\gamma_a
=\frac{a^3}{9}+b\int_0^1((2-b)u-a)^2du
=\frac{1+2b^3}{9}.
\]
The inversion density converges to \((1-b^2)/4\): pairs within the front block contribute \(a^2/4\), front-to-tail pairs contribute \(ab/2\), and tail-to-tail pairs contribute zero. Therefore \(m_n(\pi_{k_n,n})/n\to b^2/2\). Subtraction of its square gives the conditional martingale-increment variance limit
\[
\frac1{n^2}\mathbb E(\xi_{k_n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_{k_n})\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\ \frac{1+2b^3}{9}-\frac{b^4}{4}=\nu(t).
\]
All normalized quantities are bounded independently of \(n\), so convergence also holds in \(L^1\). This triangular-array result is valid for **every** deterministic sequence \(k_n/n\to t\). Compactness and contradiction therefore upgrade it to the uniform-in-\(k\) assertion \(\sup_{0\le k\le nT}\mathbb E\lvert n^{-2}\mathbb E(\xi_{k+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_k)-\nu(k/n)\rvert\to0\): otherwise select offending indices, extract a subsequence of their rescaled times, and contradict the triangular-array limit.

**4. Functional martingale limit and drift identification.** Define \(M_n(t)=n^{-3/2}\sum_{k<nt}\xi_{k+1}\). The preceding uniform expectation bound and Riemann summation imply \(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert\langle M_n\rangle(t)-\int_0^t\nu(v)dv\rvert\to0\) in probability. Its jumps have size at most \(2/\sqrt n\), hence the conditional Lindeberg criterion holds. The standard martingale functional central limit theorem yields
\[
M_n\Rightarrow \int_0^{\cdot}\sqrt{\nu(v)}\,dW(v)
\quad\text{in }D([0,T]).
\]
From the exact centered recursion,
\[
Y_n(t)=M_n(t)-\frac2n\sum_{k<nt}Y_n(k/n).
\]
Discrete Gronwall bounds the supremum of \(Y_n\) on \([0,T]\) by \(e^{2T}\sup|M_n|\), and the displayed recursion is the Euler discretization of the continuous integral equation \(Y=M-2\int_0^{\cdot}Y(v)dv\). Continuous mapping at continuous driving paths gives the asserted Gaussian diffusion.

**5. Covariance.** The unique solution is \(Y(t)=\int_0^t e^{-2(t-v)}\sqrt{\nu(v)}\,dW(v)\). Itô isometry gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y(s),Y(t))=e^{-2(t-s)}\int_0^s e^{-4(s-v)}\nu(v)dv\). Integration produces \(V(s)=[1+8e^{-3s}-9(1+s)e^{-4s}]/36\). In particular \(V(0)=0\) and \(V'(t)+4V(t)=\nu(t)\). Positivity of the diffusion intensity follows from \(4+8b^3-9b^4\ge3\) for \(0\le b\le1\).

## Verification

The accompanying `checks/check_inversions.py` is a reproducible standard-library exact-arithmetic check. It exhausts all permutations for the conditional drift identity through seven cards; it also enumerates the exact shuffle distribution at sizes two through six and time steps zero through seven, comparing all resulting variances with an independently derived closed formula. The finite checks test the combinatorial identities and normalizations, not the infinite-time scaling limit; the infinite theorem rests on the deterministic permuton limit, martingale functional CLT, and continuous mapping proof above. The closed finite-variance formula used by the checker is
\[
\operatorname{Var}(I_{r,n})=\frac{n(n-1)(2n+5)}{72}+\frac{2(n)_3}{9}\left(1-\frac3n\right)^r+\frac{(n)_4}{16}\left(1-\frac4n\right)^r-\frac{(n)_2^2}{16}\left(1-\frac2n\right)^{2r},
\]
where \((n)_j=n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)\), zero when \(j>n\); here the zero-th power has its usual value \(1\).

## Relationship to prior work

Alexander Clay, *On the statistics of random-to-top shuffles*, arXiv:2603.09008 (first posted 9 March 2026), establishes the Gaussian **one-time** inversion-count limit and variance \(V(t)\) at fixed positive time, using the random-subset and uniform-front-permutation representation. It does not state a convergence theorem for the whole temporal trajectory or its two-time covariance. The present result recovers its one-time variance, but the functional limit requires the predictable quadratic-variation analysis and an explicit martingale equation. John Pike, *Eigenfunctions for Random Walks on Hyperplane Arrangements* (2013), identifies the inversion-count eigenfunction/first-moment regression but does not provide the present functional fluctuation law. Established functional martingale CLTs are used as a standard probability theorem, not claimed as novel. Earlier stronger functional shuffle results could imply this claim even without naming inversions; priority is not independently adjudicated.

## Limitations

No rate of functional convergence is proved. The starting deck is the identity, and times are rescaled by the deck size with a fixed compact horizon; arbitrary initial permutations and horizons increasing with the number of cards are excluded. The process is Gaussian **only in the asymptotic limit**, not at finite deck size. Literature searches and self-review cannot certify priority or replace an independent audit.
