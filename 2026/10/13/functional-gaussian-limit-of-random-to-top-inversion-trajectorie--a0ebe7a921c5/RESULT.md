# Functional Gaussian limit of random-to-top inversion trajectories

## Finding

Start with the increasing permutation of \(1,\dots,n\). At each step choose a position independently and uniformly and move that card to the front. Let \(I_{k,n}\) count inversions at step \(k\). For each fixed \(0<T<\infty\), the right-continuous process
\[
Y_n(t)=n^{-3/2}\bigl(I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor,n}-\mathbb E I_{\lfloor nt\rfloor,n}\bigr),\qquad 0\le t\le T,
\]
converges in \(D([0,T])\) with the \(J_1\) topology to the continuous Gaussian solution of
\[
dY(t)=-2Y(t)\,dt+\sqrt{\nu(t)}\,dW(t),\quad Y(0)=0,\qquad \nu(t)=\frac{4+8e^{-3t}-9e^{-4t}}{36}.
\]
The exact centering and the limiting covariance, for \(0\le s\le t\le T\), are
\[
\mathbb E I_{k,n}=\frac{n(n-1)}4\left[1-\left(1-\frac2n\right)^k\right],\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(Y(s),Y(t))=e^{-2(t-s)}\frac{1+8e^{-3s}-9(1+s)e^{-4s}}{36}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Equivalently the selected labels are independent and uniform. The filtration is generated by these selections. The deck starts at identity, and the convergence includes time zero. There is no assertion for arbitrary initial states, stationary sampling, or growing rescaled time windows.

## Proof

**Regression and centering.** From a permutation \(\pi\), moving the card at position \(j\) to the front changes the inversion count by
\[
h_j(\pi)=\#\{i<j:\pi_i<\pi_j\}-\#\{i<j:\pi_i>\pi_j\}.
\]
Thus \(\sum_j h_j=\binom n2-2I(\pi)\), so the conditional mean change is \(m_n(\pi)=(n-1)/2-2I(\pi)/n\). With \(\xi_{k+1}=I_{k+1,n}-I_{k,n}-m_n(\pi_{k,n})\) and \(Z_k=I_{k,n}-\mathbb E I_{k,n}\),
\[
Z_{k+1}=(1-2/n)Z_k+\xi_{k+1},\qquad |\xi_{k+1}|\le 2n.
\]
The \(\xi_{k+1}\) are martingale differences, and solving the corresponding mean recursion from zero gives the stated centering.

**Empirical permutation at a deterministic time.** For integers \(k_n\) with \(k_n/n\to t\), put \(b=e^{-t}\), \(a=1-b\). The number \(K\) of distinct selected labels satisfies \(K/n\to a\) in probability: its mean is \(n[1-(1-1/n)^{k_n}]\), and its variance is at most \(n\), by nonpositive covariance of the unseen-label indicators. The selected labels occupy the first \(K\) positions in order of last selection, and the others are increasing. Relabeling symmetry implies that, conditional on \(K\), the front is a uniform ordered subset. In particular, conditional on the selected set, its ordering is uniform.

Let \(C_n(u)\) be the number of selected labels at most \(nu\), divided by \(n\). Hypergeometric variance bounds give \(C_n(u)-(K/n)u\to0\) in probability at each \(u\), and a finite grid and monotonicity make this convergence uniform. An unselected label \(j\) therefore occupies normalized position \(K/n+j/n-C_n(j/n)=a+bj/n+o_{\mathbb P}(1)\), uniformly over such labels. Their empirical label measure tends to \(b\,du\). In the front, the count of labels at most \(nu\) in the first \(\min(\lfloor nx\rfloor,K)\) slots, conditional on the selected set, is hypergeometric with sample proportion tending to \(u\) if \(a>0\). Its normalized variance is \(O(1/n)\) and its normalized mean tends to \(\min(x,a)u\); if \(a=0\) the whole front has vanishing mass. Thus the empirical measure of normalized position and label converges weakly in probability to
\[
\gamma_a(dx,du)=\mathbf 1_{0<x<a}\,dx\,du+b\,\delta_{a+bu}(dx)\,du.
\]
The same proof gives the diagonal measure when \(t=0\).

**Limiting bracket.** With respect to this limit measure, the signed normalized count of preceding labels is
\[
H(x,u)=\int\mathbf 1_{x'<x}\operatorname{sgn}(u-u')\,\gamma_a(dx',du').
\]
The empirical inversion density and normalized conditional second moment of the increment are bounded integrals against the second and third powers of the empirical measure. Their discontinuities are on equal-coordinate hyperplanes, which have zero product mass since both marginals of \(\gamma_a\) are uniform. Hence weak convergence gives their limiting integrals. The inversion density is \(a^2/4+ab/2=(1-b^2)/4\), and \(m_n(\pi_{k_n,n})/n\to b^2/2\). In the front, \(H=x(2u-1)\); in the tail at \(x=a+bu\), it is \((2-b)u-a\). Therefore
\[
\frac{1}{n^3}\sum_j h_j(\pi_{k_n,n})^2\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\ 
\frac{a^3}{9}+b\int_0^1((2-b)u-a)^2du=\frac{1+2b^3}{9}.
\]
Subtracting the squared conditional mean yields \(n^{-2}\mathbb E(\xi_{k_n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_{k_n})\to(1+2b^3)/9-b^4/4=\nu(t)\) in probability and in \(L^1\), by boundedness. This holds along every deterministic sequence \(k_n/n\to t\). By compactness of \([0,T]\) and continuity of \(\nu\), the \(L^1\) error relative to \(\nu(k/n)\) tends to zero uniformly over \(0\le k\le \lfloor nT\rfloor\); otherwise a subsequence of violating indices would have rescaled times converging in that compact interval.

**Functional convergence.** Define the càdlàg martingale \(M_n(t)=n^{-3/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}\xi_{k+1}\), with an empty sum at time zero and filtration \(\mathcal F_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\). Its bracket is \(n^{-3}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}\mathbb E(\xi_{k+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_k)\). The preceding uniform \(L^1\) error bound, summation and a Riemann approximation show that the bracket converges uniformly in probability to \(\int_0^t\nu(v)dv\). The martingale jumps are bounded by \(2/\sqrt n\); in particular the conditional Lindeberg condition holds. The martingale functional central limit theorem gives \(M_n\Rightarrow M=\int_0^\cdot\sqrt{\nu(v)}dW(v)\) in \(J_1\).

The discrete recursion sums exactly to
\[
Y_n(t)=M_n(t)-\frac2n\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}Y_n(k/n)=M_n(t)-2\int_0^{\lfloor nt\rfloor/n}Y_n(v)\,dv.
\]
Discrete Gronwall gives \(\|Y_n\|_\infty\le e^{2T}\|M_n\|_\infty=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\). Replacing the integral's upper limit by \(t\) has uniform error at most \(2\|Y_n\|_\infty/n\). The solution map for \(y=m-2\int_0^\cdot y(v)dv\) is Lipschitz for the uniform norm by Gronwall, and is \(J_1\)-continuous at continuous inputs. It follows that \(Y_n\Rightarrow Y\), the asserted diffusion. The intensity is positive: \(b^3(8-9b)\ge-1\) for \(0\le b\le1\). The solution is \(\int_0^t e^{-2(t-v)}\sqrt{\nu(v)}dW(v)\); the covariance follows from Itô isometry and integration.

## Verification

The accompanying `checks/check_inversions.py` uses exact arithmetic for small decks to check the conditional regression and the exact mean and variance. These checks are supplemental rather than a proof of functional convergence. For analytic verification of the finite variance used there, let \(S_{ij}\) be the sign of the relative order of labels \(i<j\), positive when \(i\) is first, and write \(u_j=1-j/n\). Each sign has mean \(u_2^r\) at step \(r\). For two disjoint pairs the sign product has mean \(u_4^r\): conditional on the pair membership of each request, the most recently requested label in each hit pair is fair within that pair and independent of the labels chosen in the other pair. If neither pair is hit, the product is one. For a triple \(i<j<l\), let \(B_r\) be the expectation of the sum of its three sign products. If no triple card is moved, the sum is unchanged. Moving respectively \(i,j,l\) gives new sums \(1+2S_{jl},-1,1-2S_{ij}\), where the signs on the right are from time \(r\). Their expectations sum to one since \(\mathbb E S_{jl}=\mathbb E S_{ij}\). Thus \(B_{r+1}=u_3 B_r+1/n\), \(B_0=3\), and \(B_r=(1+8u_3^r)/3\). Since \(I_{r,n}=(\binom n2-\sum_{i<j}S_{ij})/2\), expanding its variance gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(I_{r,n})=\frac{n(n-1)(2n+5)}{72}+\frac{2(n)_3}{9}u_3^r+\frac{(n)_4}{16}u_4^r-\frac{(n)_2^2}{16}u_2^{2r}.
\]
Here \((n)_j=0\) for \(j>n\), and a zeroth power is one.

## Relation to prior work and limitations

Clay, *On the statistics of random-to-top shuffles* (arXiv:2603.09008, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 7.1), proves the identity-start one-time Gaussian limit with the same variance and gives the exact mean. Pike, *Eigenfunctions for Random Walks on Hyperplane Arrangements* (2013), identifies the centered inversion count as an eigenfunction. In particular, the regression already yields the two-time covariance from the earlier-time variance by multiplication by \((1-2/n)^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-\lfloor ns\rfloor}\). The additional conclusion here is the path convergence on each fixed compact window, for which the conditional bracket and tightness argument is needed. No convergence rate is established.
