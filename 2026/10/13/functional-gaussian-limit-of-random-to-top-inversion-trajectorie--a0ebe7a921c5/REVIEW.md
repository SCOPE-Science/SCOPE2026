# Scientific review: functional Gaussian limit of random-to-top inversion trajectories

## Correctness — PASS

The single-card inversion increment has an exact linear conditional expectation, leading to a centered martingale regression. The permutation's touched-label uniform-prefix structure supplies a deterministic empirical-measure limit; its triple-integral squared increment is \((1+2e^{-3t})/9\), and subtracting the squared conditional mean gives the displayed \(\nu(t)\). Conditional variance convergence is uniform in time in expected absolute error; vanishing normalized jumps allow the martingale functional CLT. The centered regression maps its limit into the stated time-inhomogeneous Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation. The resulting variance agrees with the established marginal result. The accompanying exact checker verifies small-deck drift and moment identities, not infinite convergence.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

Clay (arXiv:2603.09008), Theorem 1.3 and Sections 4/7, proves a one-time critical-regime normal limit. Pike (2013 dissertation) gives the inversion eigenfunction and mean but not functional weak convergence. Actual searches in mathematical records and primary sources targeted covariance, functional CLT, Gaussian Markov and move-to-front aliases and identified no directly covering statement. The additional empirical-permutation predictable-variance computation and tightness are not furnished by single-time convergence. A stronger or unindexed trajectory theorem could affect priority. Such risk cannot be excluded by failed searches alone.

## Value — PASS

The new result gives the full temporal joint fluctuation law, explicit covariance for distinct sampling times, and a nonstationary Gaussian Markov limit. The distinction between single-time normality and temporal correlation is substantial for stochastic evolution of shuffle statistics. The scope is only the identity-started uniformly selected random-to-top walk on bounded time windows; arbitrary starts, rates, and growing horizons remain open here.
