# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(3/4<p<1\), the memory martingale \(W_n/A_n\) is square-integrable and converges, its deterministic normalization has regular-variation exponent \(q\), and the alternating drift correction converges in \(L^2\) by a paired square-summable martingale series plus an absolutely summable drift. Conditional martingale FCLTs under each positive-probability finite history establish mixing of the Gaussian process relative to the complete label history; that is what supplies independence from the two measurable limiting memory variables, rather than an unjustified inference from separate marginal limits. The variance recursion gives the displayed memory-scale variance. The new covariance sign is derived by exact martingale projection and a strictly decreasing positive sequence; all boundary hypotheses and normalization constants are explicit. Exact finite-history tests replay from the packaged checker (28712 assertions), but are not used to justify the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS (priority uncertainty retained)

The complete principal preprint arXiv:2604.04922v1 proves the marginal Brownian FCLT, martingale decomposition and convergence of the correction, not the joint Gaussian/non-Gaussian independence or cross-covariance sign. Bercu arXiv:1707.04130v1 determines the ordinary elephant memory's superdiffusive marginal behavior. Shibata arXiv:2511.10347v1 treats a different bipartite rule using separate parity histories. Searches of mathematical records for alternating memory, joint functional limits, stable convergence, covariance and dihedral aliases found no covering theorem; the closest recorded elephant result addresses a critical cover-time question. Standard stable martingale CLTs give one ingredient but not the new combined claim and covariance sign. An unindexed formulation or a general urn-fluctuation theorem specialized to the exact model could alter priority; no absolute novelty claim is made.

## Value — PASS

The motivating paper juxtaposes complete-history reinforcement and signed group position but does not determine whether their limiting random effects remain statistically coupled. The two-scale result answers that structural dependence question: a Gaussian factor decouples from an entire non-Gaussian/history-dependent memory pair, while the two residual variables exhibit provably strict anticorrelation at every interior superdiffusive parameter. The latter sign goes beyond a textbook marginal central limit deduction and distinguishes independence *at the Gaussian scale* from genuine residual dependence. This directly describes the joint limit of two natural observables of a single non-Markovian walk, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.
