# Generic cusp locus of the diminished-Fermat quintic companion map
## Finding
Let \(\Lambda\) be the base-point-free quintic linear system in Kabat--Strycharz-Szemberg generated by
\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1&=a^2(5c^3-a^3), & u_2&=b^2(b^3-5c^3),\\
u_3&=c^2(c^3-5a^3), & u_4&=c^2(5b^3-c^3),\\
u_5&=5b^2(a^3-c^3), & u_6&=5a^2(c^3-b^3).
\end{aligned}
\]
Write
\[
\phi([a:b:c])=[u_1:u_2:u_3:u_4:u_5:u_6]
\]
and let \(X=\phi(\mathbb P^2)\). Then the non-immersive locus of \(\phi\) is exactly the coordinate triangle
\[
T=\{abc=0\}.
\]
More precisely, the projective differential has rank \(2\) on \(\mathbb P^2\setminus T\), rank \(1\) at every nonvertex point of \(T\), and rank \(0\) at the three coordinate vertices.

At a generic point of each of the three coordinate lines, the completed local ring of the image has the transverse ordinary-cusp form
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,\phi(p)}\cong
\mathbb C[[t,p,q]]/(q^2-p^3),
\]
with normalization parameterization \(p=x^2\), \(q=x^3\). Hence each coordinate-line image is generically a cuspidal nonnormal curve of the companion surface.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The source paper proves that \(\Lambda\) is base point free, so \(\phi\) is a morphism. The rank statement is global. The analytic statement is generic along each coordinate line: in the three affine charts used below, it is proved away from the coordinate vertices and away from the finite set where \(t^3=-1\). Those exceptional local analytic types are not classified here. The result also does not assert that the three cuspidal image curves exhaust the entire singular locus of \(X\).

## Proof
Let \(J\) be the \(3\times6\) matrix whose rows are the partial derivatives of the six \(u_i\) with respect to \(a,b,c\). Exact expansion of all \(3\times3\) minors gives greatest common divisor \(250abc\). To exclude any additional common zero of these minors on the torus \(abc\ne0\), three minors suffice. After dividing by \(abc\), they include
\[
-750bc^2(a^3+c^3)^2,
\qquad
750ac^2(b^3+c^3)^2,
\]
and
\[
250\bigl(2a^6b^3-a^6c^3-15a^3b^3c^3+3a^3c^6-5b^3c^6-2c^9\bigr).
\]
If all three vanished with \(abc\ne0\), the first two would force \(a^3=b^3=-c^3\), while the parenthesis in the third would become \(-18c^9\), a contradiction. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}J=3\) off \(T\).

On \(a=0\), the greatest common divisor of all nonzero \(2\times2\) minors of the restricted Jacobian is \(25bc\); on \(b=0\) it is \(25ac\); and on \(c=0\) it is \(25ab\). Hence \(J\) has rank exactly \(2\) at every nonvertex point of \(T\). Direct substitution at the three vertices gives rank \(1\). Euler's degree-five identity sends the radial source vector to \(5(u_1,\ldots,u_6)\), so passing from the affine cone differential to projective tangent spaces subtracts one from these ranks. This proves the global rank statement.

For the local analytic statement, consider first \(a=0\) on the chart \(c=1\). Put \(a=x\), \(b=t\), and use \(u_3\) as a nonzero target coordinate. Every normalized target coordinate is a formal function of \(t,x^2,x^3\). Put \(y=x^3\). Two normalized coordinates may be chosen so that the determinant of their derivatives with respect to \(t,y\), at \(y=0\), is
\[
-50t(t+1)(t^2-t+1)=-50t(t^3+1).
\]
Thus, for \(t\ne0\) and \(t^3\ne-1\), the formal inverse-function theorem recovers \(t\) and \(y=x^3\) from target coordinates. Another target coordinate is a unit times \(x^2\). Therefore the completed image subring is exactly
\[
\mathbb C[[t,x^2,x^3]]
\cong
\mathbb C[[t,p,q]]/(q^2-p^3).
\]
The same calculation on \(b=0\) and \(c=0\) gives determinants proportional to \(t^3+1\) (with the expected additional unit powers of \(t\) coming from the chosen affine charts), and again a target coordinate is a unit times \(x^2\).

For completeness, the coordinate-line restrictions are generically one-to-one: for example
\[
\phi([0:t:1])=[0:t^2(t^3-5):1:5t^3-1:-5t^2:0],
\]
and \(t\) is recovered rationally from the fourth, fifth and third coordinates. Moreover, outside \(a=0\), the equations \(u_1=u_6=0\) have only finitely many solutions; analogous statements hold cyclically. Hence a generic point of each displayed image curve has a unique preimage. Since \(\phi\) is proper and no curve is contracted (any contracted curve would lie in the rank-drop triangle, while each coordinate-line restriction is nonconstant), \(\phi\) is finite. The completed target local ring at such a generic image point is therefore the cusp ring displayed above.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reconstructs the six quintics, differentiates them exactly, checks all Jacobian-minor gcd statements, verifies the three torus minors used in the proof, checks the vertex ranks, and verifies the three formal cusp charts. A fresh execution returned:

`VERIFY_OK nonimmersive=abc=0 vertex_projective_rank=0 generic_cusp=C[[t,x^2,x^3]] exceptions=t^3+1=0 axis_gcds=25*b*c,25*a*c,25*a*b`

The checker uses exact symbolic arithmetic. The cusp conclusion also uses the formal inverse-function theorem and the elementary fact that a proper quasi-finite morphism is finite; these are proof steps, not numerical extrapolations.

## Relationship to prior work
Kabat and Strycharz-Szemberg give the six quintics, prove base-point-freeness, and explicitly state that it would be interesting to investigate properties of the resulting companion surface. Their paper does not analyze the differential or the local singularity type of this image.

A later paper by Di Gennaro, Ilardi, Miró-Roig, Szemberg and Szpond studies companion varieties attached to root systems and to standard Fermat configurations. Its Fermat section uses a different linear system and different companion maps; it does not state the rank-drop locus or the cusp normal form for the six-coordinate diminished-Fermat quintic map above.

A closely related indexed computation for this exact six-coordinate map establishes finiteness, birationality and degree \(25\). That statement is compatible with, but does not imply, the exact non-immersive locus or its transverse cusp type. Full material for that indexed record was not accessible in this run, so possible overlap hidden in its longer files remains a residual originality risk.

## Limitations
The finitely many nonvertex points where the chosen cusp-chart determinant vanishes, characterized in the displayed charts by \(t^3=-1\), are not analytically classified here. The three coordinate vertices are also not classified beyond the proved fact that the projective differential has rank \(0\) there. No assertion is made that every singular point of \(X\) lies on the three coordinate-line images.
