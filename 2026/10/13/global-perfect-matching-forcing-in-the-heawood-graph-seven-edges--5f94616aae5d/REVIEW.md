# Review of the Heawood global-forcing theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The definition uses restriction of the perfect-matching edge-indicator vector to an edge test set. Injectivity is exactly the transversal condition on all 276 pairwise matching differences, reduced to 178 distinct masks. There are 24 matchings, separately checked by exact bipartite permanent. The 812 size-six hexagon transversals cover every possibility for a six-edge candidate that might work; five group orbits with explicit disjoint nice decagons exclude all of them. The full 116,280 seven-edge layer yields 8064 solutions, with an explicit mask 8795, and the signature predicate independently confirms the count. The 336 incidence automorphisms yield 30 exact orbits of sizes 168 (12 times) and 336 (18 times). Every proof loop has a complete finite and explicitly reproducible range, not a sampling cutoff.

## Originality — PASS, subject to normal priority uncertainty

The 2007 grid and benzenoid literature, the comprehensive 2011 forcing survey, and the 2020 global/anti-forcing relations paper establish definitions, general bounds, and different graph classes, not an exact Heawood value or a multiplicity/orbit classification. Ye–Zhang's 2009 Heawood-Pfaffian paper studies embeddings and matching orientation, not edge-test injectivity. The 2014 anti-forcing paper concerns deleting edges to isolate one designated matching, a weaker and differently quantified task. Known counts of 24 matchings and 28 hexagons do not establish seven, 8064, or the decagon obstruction. Published result searches and previously published scientific records were compared on the precise graph and invariant, including equivalent terminology total forcing and nice-cycle hitting sets. Risk remains that an unindexed earlier computer census includes the same exact numbers; source retrieval of the 2007 original PDF was only partial. No claim of independent verification or definitive priority is made.

## Value — PASS

Global forcing is a natural exact distinguishability measure on all perfect matchings; the Heawood graph is the cubic girth-six cage and an exceptional nonplanar Pfaffian brace. The precise value and all optimum configurations are a meaningful natural classification, not an arbitrary subcase of a known table. The unexpected failure of the hexagon-only test identifies the mathematical role of conformal decagons. Thirty distinct orbits give geometric information that a scalar value cannot provide; no further theorem about larger graphs is claimed.
