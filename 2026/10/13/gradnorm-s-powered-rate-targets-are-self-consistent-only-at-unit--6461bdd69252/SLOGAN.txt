GradNorm normalizes the relative training rates before raising them to \(\alpha\), so for heterogeneous tasks the powered targets have the correct mean only at \(\alpha=1\). The resulting auxiliary loss has the sharp unavoidable normalized residual \(\left|1-T^{-1}\sum_i r_i^\alpha\right|\), and dividing the powered targets by their own mean removes this structural mismatch without changing their relative priorities.
