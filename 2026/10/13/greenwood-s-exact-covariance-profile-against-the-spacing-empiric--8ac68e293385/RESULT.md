# Greenwood's exact covariance profile against the spacing empirical CDF
## Finding
Let
\[
D=(D_1,\ldots,D_m)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad m\ge2,
\]
so that \(D_1,\ldots,D_m\) have the law of the ordinary spacings generated by \(m-1\) independent uniform points on \([0,1]\). Define Greenwood's statistic
\[
G_m=\sum_{i=1}^m D_i^2
\]
and the empirical distribution function of the normalized spacings by
\[
\widehat F_m(x)=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbf 1\{mD_i\le x\},
\qquad 0\le x\le m.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))
=
\frac{x(m-1)}{m^2(m+1)}
\left(1-\frac{x}{m}\right)^{m-1}
\left(2-\frac{m+2}{m}x\right).
}
\]
Consequently, for interior thresholds,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))
\begin{cases}
>0,&0<x<\dfrac{2m}{m+2},\\[4pt]
=0,&x=\dfrac{2m}{m+2},\\[4pt]
<0,&\dfrac{2m}{m+2}<x<m.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the unique nontrivial sign change occurs at a normalized spacing threshold tending to \(2\), twice the mean normalized spacing.

Writing
\[
p_m(x)=\left(1-\frac{x}{m}\right)^{m-1},
\qquad
q_m(x)=\left(1-\frac{2x}{m}\right)_+^{m-1},
\]
the exact variance of the spacing empirical CDF is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat F_m(x))
=\frac1m p_m(x)(1-p_m(x))
+\frac{m-1}{m}\bigl(q_m(x)-p_m(x)^2\bigr).
}
\]
Together with
\[
\operatorname{Var}(G_m)
=\frac{4(m-1)}{(m+1)^2(m+2)(m+3)},
\]
this gives the exact correlation at every finite \(m\) and threshold \(x\).

For every fixed \(x>0\),
\[
m^2\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))
\longrightarrow x(2-x)e^{-x},
\]
\[
m\operatorname{Var}(\widehat F_m(x))
\longrightarrow e^{-x}-(1+x^2)e^{-2x}.
\]
Hence, whenever the limiting variance is positive,
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))
\longrightarrow
\frac{x(2-x)e^{-x}}
{2\sqrt{e^{-x}-(1+x^2)e^{-2x}}}.
\]
Finally,
\[
\boxed{
\int_0^m\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))\,dx=0.
}
\]
The positive and negative parts of the covariance profile therefore compensate exactly.

## Assumptions and scope
The vector \(D\) is the uniform-simplex spacing vector. Equivalently, it is the vector of ordinary spacings from iid uniform observations after including the two boundary gaps. The threshold variable \(x\) is expressed on the normalized-spacing scale, so \(mD_i\) has mean one.

The result concerns the joint finite-sample law of Greenwood's quadratic dispersion statistic and the pointwise empirical CDF of normalized spacings. It does not assert the same formula for nonuniform parent distributions, transformed spacings under composite nulls, circular spacings, or Dirichlet laws with shape parameter different from one.

## Proof
Set
\[
t=\frac{x}{m},
\qquad
N_t=\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbf 1\{D_i>t\}.
\]
Then
\[
\widehat F_m(x)=1-\frac{N_t}{m}.
\]
For a single coordinate of a uniform-simplex vector,
\[
\Pr(D_1>t)=(1-t)^{m-1}.
\]
Condition on \(D_1>t\). By translating the first coordinate by \(t\) and rescaling the remaining simplex by \(1-t\), one obtains
\[
D_1=t+(1-t)Y_1,
\qquad
D_j=(1-t)Y_j\quad(j\ge2),
\]
where
\[
Y\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1).
\]
Using
\[
\mathbb EY_1=\frac1m,
\qquad
\mathbb EY_1^2=\frac{2}{m(m+1)},
\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^mY_j^2=\frac{2}{m+1},
\]
we get
\[
\mathbb E[G_m\mid D_1>t]
=t^2+\frac{2t(1-t)}m+\frac{2(1-t)^2}{m+1}.
\]
By exchangeability,
\[
\mathbb E[G_mN_t]
=m(1-t)^{m-1}
\left(
 t^2+\frac{2t(1-t)}m+\frac{2(1-t)^2}{m+1}
\right).
\]
Also
\[
\mathbb EG_m=\frac2{m+1},
\qquad
\mathbb EN_t=m(1-t)^{m-1}.
\]
Subtracting gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,N_t)
=
\frac{t(m-1)}{m+1}(1-t)^{m-1}igl((m+2)t-2\bigr).
\]
Since \(\widehat F_m(x)=1-N_t/m\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,\widehat F_m(x))
=-\frac1m\operatorname{Cov}(G_m,N_t).
\]
Substituting \(t=x/m\) proves the displayed covariance formula and its sign classification.

For the variance, write
\[
p=(1-t)^{m-1},
\qquad
q=(1-2t)_+^{m-1}.
\]
The one- and two-coordinate probabilities are
\[
\Pr(D_1>t)=p,
\qquad
\Pr(D_1>t,D_2>t)=q.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(N_t)=mp(1-p)+m(m-1)(q-p^2),
\]
and division by \(m^2\) proves the variance formula for \(\widehat F_m(x)\).

The exact variance of Greenwood's statistic follows from the Dirichlet moments
\[
\mathbb ED_1^4=\frac{24}{m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)},
\]
\[
\mathbb ED_1^2D_2^2=\frac4{m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)}.
\]

For fixed \(x\), elementary logarithmic expansions give
\[
\left(1-\frac{x}{m}\right)^{m-1}\to e^{-x},
\]
while expanding the joint-exceedance term one order further gives
\[
m\left(q_m(x)-p_m(x)^2\right)\to -x^2e^{-2x}.
\]
Substitution yields the two asymptotic formulas and the correlation limit.

Finally, for every realization,
\[
\int_0^m\bigl(1-\widehat F_m(x)\bigr)\,dx
=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\int_0^m\mathbf 1\{mD_i>x\}\,dx
=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i
=1.
\]
Taking covariance with \(G_m\) gives the zero-integral identity.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. It verifies the closed covariance formula independently from the one-coordinate conditional integral, verifies the empirical-CDF variance from one- and two-coordinate exceedance probabilities, checks the sign-change classification on a dense rational threshold grid, and checks the zero-integral identity for every \(2\le m\le80\).

The computational checks are finite consistency tests. The proof above establishes the formulas for every admissible dimension and threshold.

## Relationship to prior work
The empirical distribution function of normalized spacings is a classical object in goodness-of-fit testing. Jammalamadaka and Taufer compare an empirical distribution built from normalized exponential spacings with the empirical distribution of the original observations and develop the associated asymptotic theory. Their complete article does not introduce Greenwood's sum of squared spacings jointly with that empirical process or state the covariance profile above.

Koziol's work studies goodness-of-fit tests based directly on the empirical distribution function of uniform spacings, including Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Cramér–von Mises constructions. The accessible bibliographic and summary material makes it a particularly relevant historical comparison, but the complete article was not available in the sources inspected here. It is therefore retained as a residual literature risk rather than treated as definitely noncovering.

Greenwood's statistic is itself a classical spacing-dispersion statistic. Separate exact results for its covariance with the maximum spacing or with spacing entropy do not imply this pointwise empirical-process profile: the present theorem identifies a threshold-dependent sign reversal, an exact finite-sample empirical-CDF variance, and a nondegenerate fixed-threshold correlation limit.

## Limitations
The formulas use the uniform-simplex law exactly. They are not distribution-free after arbitrary transformations away from the uniform-spacing null.

The literature on spacings is extensive. In particular, the full 1977 article on empirical spacing-distribution goodness-of-fit statistics was not available for direct inspection, so an equivalent historical identity cannot be ruled out completely. The checked complete 2003 article on normalized-spacing empirical processes does not contain the claimed Greenwood covariance profile.
