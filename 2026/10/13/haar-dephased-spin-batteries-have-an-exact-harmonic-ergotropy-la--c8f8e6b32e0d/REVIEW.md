**Originality — PASS, with named residual risk.** Primary published sources were compared by mathematical implication. The 2026 Floquet paper examines driven interacting dynamics and gives a qualitative large-disorder storage/dephasing result; only its indexed title and abstract were readable, leaving its complete treatment uninspected and a genuine possible priority risk. Wang and Dou (2025) analyze incoherent ergotropy and random states using Hilbert–Schmidt and engineered ensembles (supplement, random-sampling section), not the exact Haar-pure Dirichlet(1) spin spectrum and square-root constant. Hovhannisyan, Simon and Anders (2024) already prove general concentration of ergotropy for a different Hilbert–Schmidt mixed-state ensemble; their concentration methodology is credited and not original here. Caravelli et al. (2020) investigate randomized dynamical work in closed batteries rather than passive reordering of a dephased Haar-pure ensemble. Exact ordered-Dirichlet expectation is classical; the asserted original mathematical-physics result is the complete finite-size battery law, limiting constant, and loss-of-efficiency classification in one natural dephasing benchmark. No matching published claim was identified by targeted searches, but absence of a hit is not a proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** Separating *stored energy* from *unitarily extractable work* after a dephasing storage step is a motivated problem in quantum batteries. For a standard chaotic-equilibration idealization, the theorem quantifies how a full-rank diagonal ensemble can retain extensive energy but only a sharply quantified \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) amount of globally recoverable work. A rational all-\(n\) expression, a nontrivial Gaussian-quantile constant, and an exponentially concentrated performance scale give more than a routine finite example. It does not predict the dynamics of a specific driven interacting battery and assumes experimentally unconstrained collective work extraction.
