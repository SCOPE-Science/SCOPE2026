# Homotopy classes of real error-transparent code-switching paths

## Finding

Let the real distance-three codes of the source be
\[
\mathcal Q_n=\{[A]:q_n(A)=0\}\subset \mathbb P(\mathcal M(n)_\mathbb R),
\]
where the real quadratic form \(q_n\) is nondegenerate and has indefinite signature \((p,q)\), with \(p,q\ge1\) and \(m=p+q\ge3\). Then
\[
\mathcal Q_n\cong
\bigl(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\bigr)/
\bigl((u,v)\sim(-u,-v)\bigr).
\]
The fundamental group is
\[
\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&\min(p,q)=1,\ m=3,\\
0,&\min(p,q)=1,\ m\ge4,\\
\mathbb Z^2,&p=q=2,\\
\mathbb Z,&\min(p,q)=2,\ \max(p,q)\ge3,\\
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&p,q\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
For two fixed points of \(\mathcal Q_n\), choosing one reference path identifies the endpoint-fixed homotopy classes of paths with a torsor for \(\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n)\). The source proves that every continuous path of distance-three codes gives an error-transparent switch. Therefore these groups classify homotopy classes of **real distance-three encoder trajectories** that can be used by that construction.

For the survey rows in arXiv:2610.05189v1 that contain both a real Clifford code and a real icosahedral code and are marked as switchable, the resulting groups are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
n&13&17&19&23&25&27&29\\ \hline
\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n)&
\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z^2&0&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z^2&\mathbb Z.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the real thirteen-qubit switching circle has integer winding classes; the real nineteen- and twenty-seven-qubit manifolds are tori with two independent winding numbers; and the real twenty-three-qubit manifold is a sphere, so any two real switching trajectories with the same endpoints are homotopic through real distance-three trajectories.

## Assumptions and scope

The statement assumes exactly the real-encoder setting of Theorem 14 of arXiv:2610.05189v1: \(q_n\) is a real, nondegenerate, indefinite quadratic form on the \(m\)-dimensional real encoder space. The classification concerns paths constrained to \(\mathcal Q_n\). It does not claim that distinct real path classes remain distinct after allowing arbitrary complex paths in the larger set \(\mathcal L(n)\), nor that different trajectory classes necessarily yield physically inequivalent switching unitaries or distinct holonomies.

The source's Theorem 11 supplies an error-transparent switch for every continuous path in \(\mathcal L(n)\), and its real connectivity theorem places \(\mathcal Q_n\) inside that set. The present result refines the topology of the real switching manifold beyond connectedness.

## Proof

By Sylvester's law of inertia, choose real coordinates \(x=(u,v)\in\mathbb R^p\times\mathbb R^q\) in which
\[
q_n(x)=\lVert u\rVert^2-\lVert v\rVert^2.
\]
A projective null line has a representative of Euclidean norm one. On the null cone this forces
\[
\lVert u\rVert^2=\lVert v\rVert^2=\frac12.
\]
Rescaling each factor by \(\sqrt2\) identifies the normalized null cone with \(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\). Projectivization identifies exactly \((u,v)\) and \((-u,-v)\), proving
\[
\mathcal Q_n\cong
\bigl(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\bigr)/\mathbb Z_2
\]
for the free diagonal antipodal action.

If \(p=1\) (or symmetrically \(q=1\)), write the first sphere as \(S^0=\{\pm1\}\). The map
\[
[(\varepsilon,v)]\longmapsto \varepsilon v
\]
is a homeomorphism from the quotient to \(S^{m-2}\). Hence the fundamental group is \(\mathbb Z\) for \(m=3\) and trivial for \(m\ge4\).

If \(p=q=2\), write the two circle angles as \((\theta,\phi)\). The quotient adds the identification
\[
(\theta,\phi)\sim(\theta+\pi,\phi+\pi).
\]
On the universal cover \(\mathbb R^2\), the deck lattice may be generated by \((2\pi,0)\) and \((\pi,\pi)\). It has rank two, so the quotient is a torus and its fundamental group is \(\mathbb Z^2\).

If \(p=2\) and \(q\ge3\), the universal cover is \(\mathbb R\times S^{q-1}\). The full quotient is generated by
\[
g(t,v)=(t+\pi,-v).
\]
Because \(S^{q-1}\) is simply connected, this is the universal covering action, and its deck group \(\langle g\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\) is the fundamental group. The case \(q=2,p\ge3\) is symmetric.

Finally, if \(p,q\ge3\), the product \(S^{p-1}\times S^{q-1}\) is simply connected. Its diagonal antipodal quotient is a regular two-sheeted cover, so
\[
\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

For a path-connected space, after fixing a reference path from \(A\) to \(B\), endpoint-fixed homotopy classes of paths from \(A\) to \(B\) are a torsor for \(\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n,A)\). Substituting the source's survey signatures \((2,1),(1,2),(2,2),(1,3),(3,2),(2,2),(2,3)\) for \(n=13,17,19,23,25,27,29\) gives the table above.

## Verification

The topology proof is analytic and does not rely on finite enumeration. A small exact checker accompanies the result only to verify the case split and the transcription of the source survey signatures into the displayed fundamental groups. The checker is not used as a proof of any covering-space statement.

The source's Theorem 14 explicitly identifies the normalized real null set as a product of spheres modulo simultaneous sign and proves connectedness from that model. Its Theorem 11 gives an error-transparent switch along every continuous distance-three path. Its survey table supplies the signatures used in the seven concrete rows.

## Relationship to prior work

arXiv:2610.05189v1 proves the path-existence criterion for error-transparent switching, identifies the real distance-three locus as a projective quadratic zero set, models its normalized null cone as a product of spheres modulo simultaneous sign, and uses this only to decide connectedness. The paper does not state the fundamental groups above, classify endpoint-fixed path homotopy classes, or extract the winding-group table from its survey. Its conclusion explicitly frames broader fault-tolerant deformation questions as questions about the *shape* of polynomially defined code sets.

The topology of nondegenerate real projective quadrics and the covering-space arguments used here are classical mathematics; no novelty is claimed for those facts in isolation. Likewise, holonomic quantum computation has long studied loops in control-parameter manifolds and their holonomies. Those works do not identify the real permutation-invariant distance-three manifold of arXiv:2610.05189v1 or imply the signature-by-signature switching-trajectory classification above.

## Limitations

The result classifies the real distance-three trajectory space only. A nontrivial class in \(\pi_1(\mathcal Q_n)\) may become trivial in the larger complex code set \(\mathcal L(n)\). No claim is made that two nonhomotopic real encoder paths produce inequivalent endpoint unitaries, different Kato holonomies, different logical gates, or different physical control costs. The source itself leaves synthesis of the switching operator into bounded physical controls open.

The theorem also assumes nondegeneracy and indefiniteness of \(q_n\). Degenerate quadratic forms, higher-distance code families, and gate groups for which the error Gram matrix is not constant require separate analysis.
