# Hypercube gluing and the 113-dimensional torus in a magic-square boundary Jacobian

## Finding

The center-zero hyperplane section \(\mathcal D\) of the complex magic-square-of-squares surface is a connected nodal curve with sixteen smooth elliptic quartic components and exactly \(128\) ordinary nodes. Its dual graph has the sixteen vertices of the four-dimensional cube, with four parallel edges between each adjacent pair and no other edges. In particular, its connected generalized Jacobian fits into the semiabelian exact sequence
\[
1\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\mathcal D)
\longrightarrow E^{16}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
T\simeq(\mathbb G_m)^{113},\qquad E:y^2=x^3-x.
\]
Let \(G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^4\) act by independently changing signs in the four indicated opposite-square pairs. For every \(S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}\), write \(\chi_S(g)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}g_i}\). The complete rational isotypic decomposition of the torus character group is
\[
X^*(T)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q
\cong\bigoplus_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}}
\chi_S^{\oplus(4|S|-1)}.
\]
In particular, the trivial representation does not occur. The asserted sequence is not claimed to split as an algebraic-group extension.

## Assumptions and scope

All varieties and algebraic groups are over \(\mathbb C\). In projective coordinates \([A:B:C:D:M:E:F:G_0:H]\) (the symbol \(G_0\) denotes a coordinate, not the group of sign changes), let \(V\) be the locus where the nine coordinate squares form a \(3\times3\) magic square. Equivalently, there exist \(x,y,z\) such that the row-major coordinate squares are
\[
(A^2,B^2,C^2,D^2,M^2,E^2,F^2,G_0^2,H^2)
=(z-y,x+y+z,z-x,y-x+z,z,x-y+z,z+x,z-x-y,z+y).
\]
This is the singular complete intersection of six quadrics studied by Auel and Singer, with ordinary double points on the surface. Set \(\mathcal D=V\cap\{M=0\}\) scheme-theoretically. The proof below establishes that this scheme is reduced. The sign-change group \(G\) is generated by independent sign changes in \(A,B,C,E\), fixing \(D,F,G_0,H,M\).

## Proof

**1. A common elliptic normalization and sixteen components.** The opposite-square relations on \(M=0\) are
\[
A^2+H^2=B^2+G_0^2=C^2+F^2=D^2+E^2=0.
\]
With \(i^2=-1\), every point lies on one of the sixteen curves \(\mathcal E_{\epsilon}\), indexed by \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}^4\), defined by
\[
M=0,\quad A=\epsilon_1 iH,\quad B=\epsilon_2 iG_0,
\quad C=\epsilon_3 iF,\quad E=\epsilon_4 iD,
\]
and the common pair of quadrics in \([F:H:D:G_0]\in\mathbf P^3\):
\[
q_1=D^2-H^2+F^2=0,\qquad
q_2=G_0^2+H^2+F^2=0. \tag{1}
\]
Call their intersection \(\mathscr E\). The two gradients of (1) are independent at every projective common zero: if both \(D\) and \(G_0\) are nonzero their respective columns certify independence; if either vanishes, a rank drop would force two of \(F,H,D,G_0\) to vanish, which (1) forbids in projective space. Thus \(\mathscr E\) is a smooth complete intersection of two quadrics, so it is a connected genus-one quartic. Projection from \(\mathcal E_{\epsilon}\) to \(\mathscr E\) is an isomorphism. The original surface equations, together with (1), give all scheme equations of \(\mathcal D\). Locally over \(\mathscr E\), the four extra coordinates satisfy the four monic equations \(A^2+H^2=B^2+G_0^2=C^2+F^2=E^2+D^2=0\). Hence \(\mathcal D\to\mathscr E\) is finite locally free of rank \(16\), generically a union of sixteen distinct sheets. Torsion-freeness over the smooth base rules out nilpotents, proving that \(\mathcal D\) is reduced with exactly these irreducible components. Auel--Singer, Proposition 3.3, independently identifies their common elliptic isomorphism class as \(E:y^2=x^3-x\).

**2. Pairwise intersections and local analytic type.** No two of the four coordinates \(F,H,D,G_0\) can vanish simultaneously at a point of (1). For each one of these four coordinates, setting it equal to zero in (1) yields exactly four distinct projective points, since each of the remaining square-root choices is nonzero; the vanishing is simple. Therefore, for distinct sign vectors \(\epsilon,\eta\),
\[
\#\bigl(\mathcal E_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal E_{\eta}\bigr)=
\begin{cases}4,&\text{if their Hamming distance is }1,\\0,&\text{if it is at least }2.
\end{cases}
\]
An intersection with \(\epsilon_1\ne\eta_1\), for example, has \(M=A=H=0\); the other three base coordinates and their paired coordinates are nonzero. In a local chart one may solve all those nonvanishing coordinates analytically by the implicit function theorem. The only transverse surface relation is the exact diagonal-quadratic identity
\[
A^2+H^2=2M^2.
\]
Thus the surface has a local ordinary double point and its hyperplane section \(M=0\) has analytic equation \(A^2+H^2=0\), an ordinary nodal crossing between exactly two components. The other three sign directions have the identical model using the pairs \((B,G_0)\), \((C,F)\), \((E,D)\). Outside these intersections, \(\mathcal D\) is locally a single smooth sheet. There are \(4\cdot8\cdot4=128\) nodes, each belonging to precisely two components. The labelled dual multigraph \(\Gamma\) is \(4Q_4\), i.e. four parallel edges over every edge of a four-dimensional cube. It is connected, with \(16\) vertices and \(128\) edges, so
\[
b_1(\Gamma)=128-16+1=113.
\]
An independent adjunction consistency check is \(p_a(\mathcal D)=1+(64+3\cdot64)/2=129\), using \(\deg V=2^6=64\) and \(K_V=3H\). The normalization formula gives the same number \(16+128-16+1=129\).

**3. The generalized Jacobian and its sign character.** The normalization of \(\mathcal D\) is the disjoint union of its sixteen elliptic curves. The standard normalization exact sequence for the Picard scheme of a connected nodal curve (Caporaso--Viviani, Section 3.0.4) identifies the torus character lattice with \(H_1(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)\) and gives the displayed semiabelian extension. Its torus rank is \(113\).

For the equivariant refinement, write \(g_j\in G\) for the generator reversing the sign of the \(j\)-th paired coordinate. The action on the sixteen components is the regular action of \(G\). For each direction \(j\) there are four distinct base branch points, hence four \(G\)-orbits of nodes; the stabilizer of a node in any such orbit is \(\langle g_j\rangle\), which exchanges its two branches. Consequently the oriented graph-chain modules over \(\mathbb Q\) satisfy
\[
C_0(\Gamma,\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[G],\qquad
C_1(\Gamma,\mathbb Q)\cong
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{4}
\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle g_j\rangle}^{G}\operatorname{sgn}\right)^{\oplus4}.
\]
The sign representation in the edge module is essential: the stabilizing involution interchanges the two endpoint branches and hence reverses the oriented edge. Since \(\Gamma\) is connected, the boundary map \(C_1\to C_0\) surjects onto the augmentation kernel. A character \(\chi_S\) appears once in \(C_0\) and appears \(4|S|\) times in \(C_1\). It therefore appears \(4|S|-1\) times in \(H_1(\Gamma,\mathbb Q)\) for nonempty \(S\); the trivial character appears zero times in \(C_1\) and in \(H_1\). This is the asserted decomposition of \(X^*(T)\otimes\mathbb Q\), and its total dimension is
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{4}\binom4k(4k-1)=113.
\]

## Verification

The companion Python program `check_hypercube.py` performs two independent exact checks: it enumerates the projective \(\mathbb F_{17}\)-points of the base intersection (1), verifies its Jacobian rank and the four simple zeros of each of the four coordinate functions; it constructs the entire labelled \(128\)-edge multigraph, verifies connectedness and its first Betti number, and computes all sixteen sign-character multiplicities by integer Fourier sums. The program prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite-field and combinatorial checks corroborate but do not replace the characteristic-zero smoothness, local-analytic, and normalization arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Auel--Singer (arXiv:2609.09351v1), Section 3.2, already proves the decomposition of the center-zero hyperplane section into sixteen smooth elliptic quartics, and Section 5 studies intersection products of their strict transforms in a much larger Picard-lattice computation. The new claim is not that decomposition, their elliptic isomorphism class, or the general intersection formula; it is the complete ordinary-node gluing multigraph of that fixed hyperplane section and the resulting full equivariant torus character of its generalized Jacobian. The accompanying published Magma repository provides component equations and a larger intersection matrix but is not cited here as a source of this torus-character formula. Caporaso--Viviani supply the established general nodal-Jacobian exact sequence and character-lattice description, not the present specialized graph. No priority or exhaustive bibliographic uniqueness is claimed.

## Limitations

The result concerns only the reduced center-zero section over \(\mathbb C\), not other coordinate sections, the full Picard group of the ambient surface, or all possible degenerations. It does not describe the semiabelian extension class, prove that the extension splits, or classify its compactified Jacobian. The exact number of nodes is proved in characteristic zero; the finite-field check is not an assertion about arbitrary characteristics. Novelty remains subject to independent inspection of the complete associated Magma tables and further literature.
