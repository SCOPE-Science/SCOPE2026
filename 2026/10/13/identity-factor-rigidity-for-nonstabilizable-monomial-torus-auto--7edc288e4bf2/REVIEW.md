# Scientific review: identity-factor rigidity for monomial torus automorphisms

## Correctness — PASS
The proof isolates the only additional complication caused by an identity factor: periodic prime divisors \(V(Q(y))\). The Laurent-support argument proves that no divisor involving two distinct moving-coordinate exponent vectors can be periodic. The algebraic-stability orbit criterion therefore excludes contraction of all nonvertical prime divisors by a hypothetical birational model. After deleting its finitely many contracted vertical divisors, the remaining invariant open set has factorial coordinate ring and explicitly computable units. Its boundary-divisor localization sequence has kernel with eigenvalues those of \(A\) and additional copies of \(1\). Effective birational pullback preserves the cone spanned by boundary prime divisors. Algebraic stability bounds its spectral radius by the monomial map's first dynamical degree \(|\alpha|\), and Perron--Frobenius prohibits the irrational peripheral complex eigenangle. The proof explicitly treats every integer \(r\geq1\) and arbitrary smooth projective birational models, rather than testing a family of compactifications. Exact integer calculations separately check the exhibited matrix's hypotheses. Finite checks are not used to certify the birational obstruction.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The initiating source, Kuznetsova, arXiv:2610.02536v1 (Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.9, Question 1.5), proves the unstabilized rank-three case and products with multiplicatively independent translations; its identity-product question is explicitly left open in general. Taking translation multipliers equal to one violates Corollary 5.9's essential hypothesis and introduces precisely the invariant vertical divisors addressed here. Lin--Wulcan, arXiv:1206.4925v2, and Jonsson--Wulcan, arXiv:1001.3938v2, concern toric stabilizations and obstructions; these do not rule out arbitrary smooth projective nontoric models. Full HTML source materials were checked at the relevant theorems and proofs. Targeted research-database queries and checks against preceding and current work found no equivalent claim. Searches alone do not prove uniqueness: an implicit, differently phrased general theorem about products could subsume the result, and independent bibliographic examination remains desirable.

## Value — PASS
The theorem resolves a concrete and naturally posed stabilization question for an infinite family of rank-three dynamical systems in every higher torus dimension. The issue is not an automatic insertion of identity coordinates: fixed coordinates create infinitely many periodic vertical divisors and prevent direct use of the source's divisor-orbit argument. The proof introduces an invariant localization and an enlarged fixed-unit kernel that preserve the Perron--Frobenius obstruction. This determines nonexistence of an algebraically stable model with no toric restriction, a geometric distinction that older toric-only obstructions cannot make.

## Scope and limitations
The result is over \(\mathbb C\), requires the specified rank-three irreducible spectrum, and concerns products with identity automorphisms of algebraic tori. It does not answer Kuznetsova's general product question for arbitrary maps and factors, nor characterize all potential nontoric projective models of other monomial maps. No external independent audit has been performed.
