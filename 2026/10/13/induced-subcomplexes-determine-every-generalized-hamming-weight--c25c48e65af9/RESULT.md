# Induced subcomplexes determine every generalized Hamming weight of simplicial face codes
## Finding

Let
\[
\Delta
\]
be any simplicial complex on vertex set
\[
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\]
For a prime \(q\), form the code of Lorite López, Camazón Portela, and López Ramos by using the nonzero characteristic vectors of faces as generator columns:
\[
C_\Delta^*
=
\left\{
\bigl(u\cdot\chi_\sigma\bigr)_{\sigma\in\Delta\setminus\{\varnothing\}}
:
u\in\mathbb F_q^k
\right\}.
\]

For
\[
T\subseteq[k],
\]
write
\[
\Delta|_T
=
\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\subseteq T\}
\]
for the induced subcomplex on \(T\).

Then every generalized Hamming weight has the exact formula
\[
d_r(C_\Delta^*)
=
|\Delta|
-
\max_{\substack{T\subseteq[k]\\|T|=k-r}}
|\Delta|_T|,
\qquad
1\le r\le k.
\]

Equivalently,
\[
d_r(C_\Delta^*)
=
\min_{\substack{S\subseteq[k]\\|S|=r}}
\left|
\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\cap S\ne\varnothing\}
\right|.
\]

In particular, the entire generalized Hamming-weight hierarchy is independent of the prime field size \(q\). The source proves this independence only for the ordinary minimum distance \(d_1\).

As a closed infinite-family consequence, consider the distance-optimal family in source Theorem 5.2 obtained by taking the cone over the \((N-1)\)-skeleton of an \(N\)-simplex. Its parameters are
\[
[2^{N+2}-3,N+2,2^{N+1}-2]_q.
\]
For every
\[
1\le r\le N+2,
\]
its generalized Hamming weights are
\[
d_r
=
2^{N+2}-2-2^{N+2-r}.
\]

Thus the entire hierarchy of that distance-optimal family is explicit, not only its minimum distance.

## Assumptions and scope

A simplicial complex on vertex set \([k]\) contains every singleton
\[
\{i\},
\qquad 1\le i\le k,
\]
and is downward closed under taking subsets.

Each face
\[
\sigma\in\Delta
\]
is represented by its characteristic vector
\[
\chi_\sigma\in\mathbb F_q^k.
\]
The punctured code \(C_\Delta^*\) omits only the zero column corresponding to the empty face.

Because all singleton faces occur, the generator contains
\[
e_1,\ldots,e_k.
\]
Hence
\[
\dim C_\Delta^*=k.
\]

The theorem is stated in the prime-field setting of the source. The proof itself uses only linear algebra over a field and characteristic vectors with entries \(0\) and \(1\).

This construction should not be confused, for \(q>2\), with the different notion of a simplicial complex inside \(\mathbb F_q^m\) used in some defining-set-code literature, where every vector whose support lies in a face is included. For \(q=2\), the two notions coincide.

## Proof

Let
\[
D^*
=
\{\chi_\sigma:\sigma\in\Delta,\ \sigma\ne\varnothing\}
\subseteq\mathbb F_q^k.
\]
The code length is
\[
n=|D^*|=|\Delta|-1.
\]

Let
\[
U\le\mathbb F_q^k
\]
be an \(r\)-dimensional message subspace and let
\[
L=U^\perp.
\]
Then
\[
\dim L=k-r.
\]

A coordinate indexed by a nonempty face \(\sigma\) vanishes on the entire subcode generated by \(U\) if and only if
\[
u\cdot\chi_\sigma=0
\]
for every \(u\in U\), equivalently
\[
\chi_\sigma\in L.
\]
Therefore
\[
d_r(C_\Delta^*)
=
(|\Delta|-1)
-
\max_{\substack{L\le\mathbb F_q^k\\\dim L=k-r}}
|D^*\cap L|.
\]

It remains to evaluate the maximum exactly.

Fix an arbitrary \(s\)-dimensional subspace
\[
L\le\mathbb F_q^k.
\]
Choose an information set
\[
T\subseteq[k],
\qquad |T|=s,
\]
for \(L\); equivalently, choose \(s\) coordinate columns forming a nonsingular minor of a basis matrix of \(L\). The coordinate projection
\[
\pi_T:L\longrightarrow\mathbb F_q^T
\]
is injective.

For any face characteristic vector
\[
\chi_\sigma\in D^*\cap L,
\]
projection gives
\[
\pi_T(\chi_\sigma)=\chi_{\sigma\cap T}.
\]
Since \(\Delta\) is downward closed,
\[
\sigma\cap T\in\Delta|_T.
\]
Because \(\pi_T\) is injective on \(L\), distinct vectors of \(D^*\cap L\) have distinct projected characteristic vectors. Hence
\[
|D^*\cap L|
\le
|\Delta|_T|-1.
\]

Conversely, for every \(T\subseteq[k]\) with \(|T|=s\), the coordinate subspace
\[
\mathbb F_q^T
=
\operatorname{span}\{e_i:i\in T\}
\]
has
\[
D^*\cap\mathbb F_q^T
=
\{\chi_\sigma:\varnothing\ne\sigma\in\Delta|_T\}.
\]
Thus
\[
|D^*\cap\mathbb F_q^T|
=
|\Delta|_T|-1.
\]

Therefore
\[
\max_{\substack{L\le\mathbb F_q^k\\\dim L=s}}
|D^*\cap L|
=
\max_{\substack{T\subseteq[k]\\|T|=s}}
\bigl(|\Delta|_T|-1\bigr).
\]
Substituting
\[
s=k-r
\]
gives
\[
d_r(C_\Delta^*)
=
|\Delta|
-
\max_{\substack{T\subseteq[k]\\|T|=k-r}}
|\Delta|_T|.
\]

For the equivalent form, put
\[
S=[k]\setminus T.
\]
A face fails to lie in \(\Delta|_T\) exactly when it meets \(S\). Therefore
\[
|\Delta|-|\Delta|_T|
=
|\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma\cap S\ne\varnothing\}|,
\]
which proves the second formula.

For the distance-optimal family of source Theorem 5.2, let \(B\) be the \(N+1\) base vertices and let \(v\) be the cone vertex. The cone over the \((N-1)\)-skeleton contains every subset of
\[
B\cup\{v\}
\]
except
\[
B
\]
and
\[
B\cup\{v\}.
\]
Hence
\[
|\Delta|=2^{N+2}-2.
\]
For every
\[
1\le r\le N+2,
\]
one can choose a set \(T\) of size
\[
N+2-r
\]
whose induced subcomplex is a full simplex, so
\[
\max_{|T|=N+2-r}|\Delta|_T|
=
2^{N+2-r}.
\]
The displayed closed hierarchy follows.

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library.

First, for every simplicial complex on a full vertex set of size at most \(4\), it computes generalized Hamming weights directly from all message subspaces over both
\[
\mathbb F_2
\]
and
\[
\mathbb F_3.
\]
It compares those direct values with the induced-subcomplex formula above.

Second, it reconstructs source Example 4.2, whose maximal faces are
\[
\{0,1\},\qquad
\{0,2,3\},\qquad
\{4,5,6,7\}.
\]
The source gives parameters
\[
[24,8,2]_q.
\]
The verifier obtains the complete hierarchy
\[
(2,6,8,9,17,21,23,24)
\]
both from direct binary subcode enumeration and from induced-subcomplex maxima.

Finally, for several values of \(N\), it constructs the cone-over-skeleton complex of source Theorem 5.2 and checks
\[
d_r
=
2^{N+2}-2-2^{N+2-r}
\]
for every allowed \(r\).

Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Lorite López, Camazón Portela, and López Ramos introduce the characteristic-face construction used here. Their Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 determine the ordinary minimum distance through vertex-link data and show that this minimum distance is independent of the prime field. They do not give a formula for the higher generalized Hamming weights. Their Theorem 5.2 gives the parameters and distance optimality of the cone-over-skeleton family, but not its complete hierarchy.

Liu, Zheng, Lu, and Wang study weight hierarchies for a related defining-set construction from simplicial complexes inside \(\mathbb F_q^m\). Their paper gives general intersection formulas and then evaluates special cases with one, two, or three maximal elements and several disjoint-support configurations. They explicitly note that the general maximal-intersection problem is difficult. For
\[
q=2,
\]
their defining set agrees with the characteristic-face set used here. The induced-projection argument therefore gives the arbitrary-simplicial-complex binary hierarchy in one formula.

For
\[
q>2,
\]
the two constructions differ: the older defining set contains all nonzero field vectors whose support lies in a face, while the present construction contains only the \(0\)-\(1\) characteristic vector of each face. No implication from the older \(q>2\) formulas is asserted.

Focused searches under generalized-Hamming, weight-hierarchy, induced-subcomplex, characteristic-face, and exact-source terminology did not locate the formula in the Finding.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the characteristic-face code \(C_\Delta^*\) defined by the 2026 source. It does not solve the distinct \(q>2\) vector-downset construction of the earlier simplicial-complex literature.

The formula determines generalized Hamming weights, not the complete support-size distribution of every subcode.

For the source's cone-over-skeleton family, the result supplies the complete hierarchy but does not classify the minimizing subcodes up to automorphism.

A combinatorics or matroid source using different terminology could contain an equivalent projection lemma; no such coding-theoretic hierarchy formula was found in the inspected literature.
