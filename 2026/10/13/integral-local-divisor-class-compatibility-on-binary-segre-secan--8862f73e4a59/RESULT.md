# Integral local divisor-class compatibility on binary Segre secants

## Finding
Let \(n\geq4\), let \(Y=(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C})^n\) have its Segre embedding, and let \(X=\operatorname{Sec}(Y)\) denote its projective secant-line variety. For each pair \(1\leq i<j\leq n\), write \(S_{ij}\) for the irreducible component of \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}\) obtained by allowing the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th factors to vary inside their \(\mathbb P^3\) secant and retaining decomposability in the other factors. The established singular-locus description gives \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigcup_{i<j}S_{ij}\) with \(S_{ij}\cap S_{kl}=Y\) for distinct pairs.

**Theorem (local divisor-class compatibility).** For every closed complex point \(x\in X\), the Weil divisor class group of its algebraic local ring is
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,x})\cong
\begin{cases}
0,&x\notin\operatorname{Sing}(X),\\
\mathbb Z,&x\in\operatorname{Sing}(X)\setminus Y,\\
\mathbb Z^n,&x\in Y.
\end{cases}
\]
All these groups are torsion-free. More precisely, fix \(p\in Y\), and denote the generic point of \(S_{ij}\) by \(\eta_{ij}\). For each pair, choose the generator of \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,\eta_{ij}})\cong\mathbb Z\) induced by the toric divisor corresponding to the facet \(\sum q_i=2\) in the secant-cumulant chart through \(p\). The localization map
\[
\rho_p:\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,p})\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{i<j}\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,\eta_{ij}})
\cong\mathbb Z^{\binom n2}
\]
is injective, and its image consists **exactly** of the integer edge labels \((w_{ij})_{i<j}\) on the complete graph satisfying, for all distinct \(i,j,k,l\),
\[
w_{ij}+w_{kl}=w_{ik}+w_{jl}=w_{il}+w_{jk}.
\]
Consequently the cokernel of \(\rho_p\) is free of rank \(\binom n2-n\). The coordinates and generator choices depend on the chosen Segre chart, but the abstract local groups and the saturation of the restriction image do not.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\); all class groups are **algebraic** Weil divisor class groups of normal noetherian local rings, not class groups of completions. The number of binary factors is any integer \(n\geq4\). The map \(\rho_p\) is defined by localization at the prime ideals of the generizations \(\eta_{ij}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{X,p})\); there is no asserted map between two unrelated closed-point local rings. The secant chart, normality, and published description of the singular components are used as established input, not claimed as discoveries here.

## Proof
**1. Toric chart and integral lattice.** The secant-cumulant coordinate theorem of Michałek–Oeding–Zwiernik gives an affine chart through a coordinate Segre point, isomorphic to \(\mathbb A^n\times T_{2,n}\). The normal affine toric variety \(T_{2,n}\) has semigroup generated by
\[
(1,\mathbf 1_I)\in\mathbb Z^{n+1},\qquad I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad |I|\geq2.
\]
These generators span the full lattice \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\): differences between triple and pair generators give the coordinate vectors \((0,e_i)\), and a pair generator then supplies \((1,0)\). The homogenized truncated-cube polytope has irredundant inequalities \(0\leq q_i\leq1\) and \(\sum q_i\geq2\) for \(n\geq4\), so its cone has primitive inward facet rays
\[
 r_i^0=(0,e_i),\qquad r_i^1=(1,-e_i),\qquad r_*=(-2,1,\ldots,1).
\]
This affine chart and these rays are prior results (Michałek–Oeding–Zwiernik, Sections 4–5). The class-group computations and integral restriction comparison below are the asserted contribution.

**2. Divisor classes at the Segre stratum.** Let \(A_i=[D_i^1]\) and \(B=[D_*]\) be the classes of the invariant prime divisors from \(r_i^1\) and \(r_*\). The toric Weil-divisor exact sequence \(M\to\mathbb Z^{\sigma(1)}\to\operatorname{Cl}\to0\) (Cox–Little–Schenck, Theorem 4.1.3) gives, on pairing characters with the displayed primitive rays,
\[
[D_i^0]=A_i-B,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n A_i=2B.
\]
The normals \(r_1^0,\ldots,r_n^0,r_1^1\) form an integral basis of \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\). Thus no finite torsion survives and
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X,p})\cong
\frac{\mathbb Z\langle A_1,\ldots,A_n,B\rangle}
{\mathbb Z\langle A_1+\cdots+A_n-2B\rangle}\cong\mathbb Z^n.
\]
Affine-space factors do not alter the local toric class group. The class group of an affine normal toric variety at its closed torus orbit equals the toric divisor presentation used here, since every invariant divisor passes through that orbit.

**3. Classes along the other singular points.** The published description of the toric singular locus shows that away from its closed orbit it is the disjoint union of \(\binom n2\) punctured torus-orbit lines. For the line indexed by \(\{i,j\}\), the associated face is the vertex \(q_i=q_j=1\), \(q_k=0\) otherwise. Its incident rays are exactly
\[
\{r_i^1,r_j^1\}\cup\{r_k^0:k\notin\{i,j\}\}\cup\{r_*\}.
\]
The first \(n\) listed coordinate rays form a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of the saturated hyperplane \(\{(a,c):a+c_i+c_j=0\}\) and
\[
r_*=-r_i^1-r_j^1+\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}r_k^0.
\]
Applying the toric exact sequence to this face gives a free rank-one local class group with generator \(t_{ij}=[D_*]\), and identities
\[
[D_i^1]=[D_j^1]=t_{ij},\qquad
[D_k^0]=-t_{ij}\quad(k\notin\{i,j\}).
\]
At a smooth point, the regular local ring has trivial class group. The group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2^n\) is transitive on \(Y\) and also on each \(S_{ij}\setminus Y\): the corresponding \(2\times2\) matrix in the two varying factors has rank two and lies in one left-right group orbit. Consequently the toric-chart calculations apply at every closed point of each indicated stratum. Since the published singular components meet only along \(Y\), the local description is exhaustive.

**4. Exact restriction lattice, including integrality.** In the coordinates of Step 2, localization along \(S_{ij}\) kills the classes of toric divisors not containing its orbit. Thus
\[
\rho_p(A_k)_{ij}=\mathbf1_{k\in\{i,j\}},\qquad
\rho_p(B)_{ij}=1.
\]
Equivalently an integral expression \(\sum_k a_kA_k+bB\) maps to
\[
w_{ij}=a_i+a_j+b.
\]
The defining relation \(\sum A_i-2B=0\) maps to zero. The four-cycle equalities follow by cancellation.

For the converse, let integer labels \(w_{ij}\) obey all four-cycle equalities. For each \(i\), choose distinct \(j,k\ne i\) and define
\[
t_i=w_{ij}+w_{ik}-w_{jk}.
\]
The four-cycle identities show that \(t_i\) is independent of the chosen pair: replacing one auxiliary index changes this expression by a four-cycle difference, and the graph of auxiliary pairs is connected because \(n\geq4\). Using the same third index in the definitions for \(i\) and \(j\) yields \(t_i+t_j=2w_{ij}\). Consequently all \(t_i\) have one common parity. Choose an integer \(b\) with that parity, set \(a_i=(t_i-b)/2\), and obtain \(w_{ij}=a_i+a_j+b\) exactly over \(\mathbb Z\). If all \(w_{ij}\) vanish, then \(a_i\) is a common integer \(c\) and \(b=-2c\), precisely the single relation in Step 2; hence \(\rho_p\) is injective. Its image is the integer kernel of the quadrilateral-difference matrix, so its cokernel embeds in the free abelian group generated by these differences. In particular the cokernel is torsion-free and, by the rank calculation, is \(\mathbb Z^{\binom n2-n}\). This proves the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Michałek, Oeding and Zwiernik (arXiv:1212.1515, first publicly submitted 2012-12-07, Sections 4–5) prove the normal secant-cumulant toric chart, provide the precise truncated-cube facet list, identify the nonsimplicial vertex cones, and classify the singular components. They explicitly show failure of local \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality and identify exceptional Gorenstein cases; none of those prior statements is presented as new here. The present theorem refines this input by computing **integral** local class groups at every stratum and the simultaneous integral restriction image, with its four-cycle equations and torsion-free cokernel. The latter is not the same assertion as existence of nonsimplicial cones. The computation uses the standard toric class-group sequence (Cox–Little–Schenck, 2011, Theorem 4.1.3).

Michałek and collaborators' `Flexible affine cones and flexible coverings` (arXiv:1612.01144, first posted 2016-12-04; subsequent to the historical seed) extends toric coverings to Segre–Veronese secants. Its inspected Section 2 concerns toric-chart flexibility and does not state the integral compatibility lattice computed here. Searches for aliases including toric truncated-cube class groups, tensor border-rank-two Cartier defects and four-cycle balancing did not identify a stronger published result. This is not a claim that absence from a search database proves historical novelty.

## Limitations
The groups are for algebraic local rings over \(\mathbb C\), not asserted to coincide with analytic or completed-local divisor class groups. The generators of generic component groups require a chosen secant-cumulant chart through the selected Segre point. The result does not compute global \(\operatorname{Cl}(X)\), the global Picard group, or higher secants. It is built on the established polyhedral model, so a different presentation of the same integer restriction calculation could occur in unindexed toric literature; no claim of independent validation or historical priority is made.
