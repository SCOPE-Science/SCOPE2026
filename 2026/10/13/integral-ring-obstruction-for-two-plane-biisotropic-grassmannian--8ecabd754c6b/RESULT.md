# Integral ring obstruction for two-plane biisotropic Grassmannians

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\), let \(X_n=\operatorname{S_2Gr}(2,2n+1)\) denote the variety of two-planes in \(\mathbb C^{2n+1}\) that are isotropic for both members of a general pencil of alternating forms, and put \(Y_n=\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n)\). Write \(d=4n-4\) and let \(h_X\) and \(h_Y\) be the positive primitive integral Plücker classes in degree two. Then
\[
\int_{X_n}h_X^d=C_{2n-1},\qquad
\int_{Y_n}h_Y^d=C_{2n-2},\qquad
\frac{C_{2n-1}}{C_{2n-2}}=\frac{4n-3}{n}>1,
\]
where \(C_m=\frac{(2m)!}{m!(m+1)!}\) is the \(m\)-th Catalan number. Hence the graded integral singular cohomology rings are not isomorphic. The graded integral Chow rings are likewise not isomorphic. Nonetheless, Fonarev--Kuznetsov prove that the integral singular cohomology *groups* are isomorphic degree by degree.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with ordinary singular cohomology with \(\mathbb Z\)-coefficients and Chow groups with integral coefficients. The pencil is general as in Fonarev--Kuznetsov, so \(X_n\) is a smooth connected projective variety of dimension \(d=4n-4\). The named ring isomorphisms are *graded* and integral. Neither a presentation of either full ring nor any distinction between rings with rational coefficients is claimed. For \(n=1\), the common zero locus is a point and the comparison Grassmannian is also a point, so the strict lower bound is sharp.

## Proof

**1. Codimension-two linear-section model.** On \(G_n=\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n+1)\), the Plücker line bundle is \(\mathcal O_{G_n}(1)=\det(\mathcal U)^\vee\) for the tautological rank-two subbundle \(\mathcal U\). An alternating form \(\omega\) on the ambient vector space restricts to a section of \(\wedge^2\mathcal U^\vee=\mathcal O_{G_n}(1)\). Since the two defining alternating forms in the general pencil are linearly independent, their vanishing loci are two Plücker hyperplanes. Their scheme-theoretic intersection is \(X_n\). The source's smoothness theorem and dimension \(2\cdot2(n+1-2)=4n-4\), compared with \(\dim G_n=2(2n-1)=4n-2\), establish the expected codimension two.

**2. Primitive classes.** Each of the two standard generators of the pencil displayed by Fonarev--Kuznetsov is a rank-\(2n\) alternating form (one-dimensional radical). Its Plücker hyperplane section of \(G_n\) is smooth: if such a section were singular at a two-plane \(U\), the differential of the evaluation \(\omega|_U\) would vanish on every \(\operatorname{Hom}(U,V/U)\), forcing \(U\subseteq\operatorname{rad}(\omega)\), impossible for a one-dimensional radical. The second section cuts out a smooth \(X_n\). Since \(\dim X_n=d\ge4\), the integral weak Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, successively applied to these smooth sections, identifies \(H^2(G_n,\mathbb Z)\cong H^2(X_n,\mathbb Z)\). The restriction of the primitive Plücker generator is therefore the primitive generator \(h_X\). The same is standard for the Grassmannian \(Y_n\), giving \(H^2(Y_n,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z h_Y\).

**3. The two top intersection numbers.** Intersecting with two Plücker hyperplanes preserves projective degree, so, writing \(H=c_1(\mathcal O_{G_n}(1))\),
\[
\int_{X_n}h_X^{4n-4}=\int_{G_n}H^{4n-2}=\deg\operatorname{Gr}(2,2n+1).
\]
By the Pieri rule, \(\deg\operatorname{Gr}(2,N)\) is the number of saturated Young-diagram chains from the empty diagram to the rectangle with two rows of length \(N-2\); by the ballot reflection principle this equals \(C_{N-2}\). Thus the displayed number is \(C_{2n-1}\). Applied to \(Y_n\), the same rule gives \(\int_{Y_n}h_Y^{4n-4}=C_{2n-2}\). Factorial cancellation gives \(C_m/C_{m-1}=2(2m-1)/(m+1)\), hence the exact ratio \((4n-3)/n\). It exceeds one for all \(n\ge2\).

**4. Obstruction to ring isomorphism.** Both spaces are smooth projective complex varieties of the same dimension, with \(H^{2d}(-,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), generated by the positive point class. A graded integral cohomology-ring isomorphism must send the unique primitive degree-two generator \(h_X\) to \(\pm h_Y\) and the top integral generator to its positive or negative. The absolute integer coefficient of the top power of a primitive degree-two generator is therefore a graded-ring invariant. Since \(d=4n-4\) is even and the coefficients \(C_{2n-1}\) and \(C_{2n-2}\) differ, no such isomorphism exists.

Finally, the source proves an algebraic cell decomposition of \(X_n\), while \(Y_n\) has Schubert cells. Both therefore have \(\operatorname{CH}^1\cong\mathbb Z\) generated by the Plücker class and \(\operatorname{CH}^{d}\cong\mathbb Z\) generated by a point. The identical top-intersection argument in the Chow rings excludes any graded integral Chow-ring isomorphism.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3. Its implementation independently counts Plücker degrees through lattice-path dynamic programming rather than using the closed Catalan formula as the algorithm, and independently enumerates the two-plane fixed-point cell dimensions from the formula in the source. The checks cover \(2\le n\le20\) for degree and ratio and \(2\le n\le10\) for cell-rank agreement, plus the sharp boundary \(n=1\). The code is a finite consistency check, not a substitute for the uniform weak-Lefschetz, Pieri, and ring-invariant arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Fonarev and Kuznetsov, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2 of arXiv:2610.03309v1, prove equality of the graded integral singular cohomology *groups* of \(\operatorname{S_2Gr}(k,2n+1)\) and \(\operatorname{Gr}(k,2n+2-k)\), but explicitly distinguish this from the unresolved multiplicative structure. The rank-two isotropic equations as Plücker hyperplanes follow from their Definition 2.1. The Catalan degree formula for ordinary two-plane Grassmannians is classical; neither that formula nor weak Lefschetz is asserted here as new. The claim is the uniform integral ring nonisomorphism, which supplements rather than contradicts the published additive comparison. An earlier additive motivic comparison also does not determine multiplicative structures.

## Limitations

The calculation only treats \(k=2\), \(n\ge2\), not the full range \(1\le k\le n+1\). It distinguishes graded rings over \(\mathbb Z\); rational coefficient rings may behave differently, and their comparison is not assessed. The special case \(n=2\) is a classical low-degree Grassmannian-section geometry; older literature could encode this observation without using the biisotropic terminology. There is no claim to a presentation of cup products beyond the exhibited top power.
