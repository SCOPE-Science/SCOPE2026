# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reconstructs all stationary points for simple-spectrum symmetric data using the nondegenerate trace pairing, identifies the squared distances and the unique disjoint-difference decomposition of each pair of critical indices, and derives all discriminant multiplicities with no genericity shortcuts. The irreducibility argument takes place explicitly in the universal ring of symmetric spectral coefficients; it uses transitivity of the hyperplane orbit plus the non-alternating sign test. A direct hyperplane-avoidance argument proves that the additional components are nonempty on the simple-spectrum locus and gives the exact generic double-collision count. An extracted, standalone, standard-library checker returns `VERIFY_OK` for \(4\le n\le7\), all \(k\), and two distinct spectra per case. Finite checks are corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The full October 2026 Friedman–Hoşten–Rosana primary paper determines where the *number* of stationary points changes, by the eigenvalue discriminant. Its result does not imply that every critical squared distance is distinct off that locus: diagonal data with eigenvalues \((0,1,2,3)\) have all six stationary points but two share value \(10\). The January 2026 Friedman–Rosana–Sturmfels paper studies chordal distance and GD degrees, without identifying the new resonance-factor decomposition. The open-access Chapter 7 of Breiding–Kohn–Sturmfels (2024) supplies the general **known** bisector/offset discriminant framework (Theorem 7.20), but not these projection-Grassmannian irreducible components, degrees, multiplicities, and generic collision profiles. Focused searches of published scientific results showed no statement implying this exact family; an older additive-compound/resolvent formulation remains possible and is a real residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Exact critical-point counts do not control value collisions. This theorem gives the complete additional hypersurface stratification on which the same well-conditioned spectral problem has different stationary solutions at exactly equal squared distance, with explicit irreducibility, degrees, multiplicities and generic collision numbers for every \(n,k\). It distinguishes equal stationary values from the true eigenvalue-collision ED discriminant and from ties among global minima. The degree-three extra equation for \(\operatorname{pGr}(2,4)\) is a concrete boundary witness, not an arbitrary one-off example.

Limits and risks: conclusions are over \(\mathbb C[e_1,\ldots,e_n]\) and make no irreducibility claim after pullback to the full matrix-entry ring; the real medial axis is not classified. The generic double-value statement is only at a general point of one resonance component, not at multiple intersections. Full independence has not been assessed.
