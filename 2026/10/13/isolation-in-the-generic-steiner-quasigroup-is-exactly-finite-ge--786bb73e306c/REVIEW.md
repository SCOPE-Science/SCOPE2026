# Review

## Correctness
PASS. If a tuple has finite generated Steiner subquasigroup, embed that finite substructure into the locally finite Fraïssé limit; quantifier elimination transfers the tuple's complete type, and Proposition 4.4 isolates every tuple type realized in the prime Fraïssé limit. Conversely, an isolated type is realized in every model, in particular in the prime model; equality of complete types gives an isomorphism of generated term substructures, so local finiteness forces the original generated subquasigroup to be finite. Corollary 5.5 identifies generated substructure with algebraic closure. The low-arity and continuum nonisolated consequences then follow from the Steiner identities and Theorem 3.3.

## Originality
PASS with residual folklore risk. The primary paper states all major ingredients separately but the inspected text does not state the isolation/finite-closure biconditional or the exact nonisolated-type arity spectrum. published-finding corpus searches under isolated-type, finite-generated-closure, and prime-model formulations found no close record. Exact web searches likewise returned the source and unrelated Steiner material, not this synthesis. The own ledger already contains the total type spectrum for the same theory, but that result does not separate isolated from nonisolated types.

## Value
PASS. The criterion translates a topological/model-theoretic notion—an isolated point of the Stone type space—into a concrete algebraic invariant of a realization. It explains structurally why arities one and two are tame and why the wild behavior at arity three is genuinely nonprincipal rather than merely a large collection of isolated types.
