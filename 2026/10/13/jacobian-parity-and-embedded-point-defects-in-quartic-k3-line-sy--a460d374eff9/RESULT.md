# Jacobian parity and embedded-point defects in quartic K3 line systems

## Finding
Let \(X\subset\mathbf P^3_{\mathbf C}\) be a very general smooth quartic K3 surface containing a line \(L\). Write \(H\) for the hyperplane class. Fix integers \(m\ge2\) and \(1\le k\le2m-1\), and define
\[
a=\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\qquad r=m-a\ge1,\qquad B=kH+aL.
\]
For a general divisor \(D\in|kH+mL|\), the fixed component is exactly \(rL\), and \(D=rL+A\) with \(A\in|B|\) smooth and irreducible. Its intrinsic Jacobian singular subscheme \(J(D)\) has, as an ideal on \(X\), the exact description
\[
\mathcal I_{J(D)}=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal I_L^{r-1},& k\text{ even},\\
\mathcal I_L^{r-1}\mathcal I_q,& k\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\]
where in the odd case \(q\) is the unique reduced point of \(A\cap L\). We set \(\mathcal I_L^0=\mathcal O_X\). In particular, if \(k\) is odd and \(r\ge2\), the Jacobian scheme has a single length-one embedded-point defect over the uniform \((r-1)\)-fold thickening of \(L\). When \(r=1\), this says the even case is smooth and disconnected, whereas the odd case has one ordinary node.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes are over \(\mathbf C\). The quartic is chosen very generally in the family of smooth quartics containing a line; thus the Néron–Severi group is generated by \(H,L\) with \(H^2=4\), \(H\cdot L=1\), and \(L^2=-2\). The singular subscheme of a Cartier curve \(D\subset X\) is defined intrinsically by \(\operatorname{Fitt}_1(\Omega^1_{D/\mathbf C})\), then viewed as an ideal sheaf on \(X\). The theorem concerns a general member of each indicated complete linear system, not every special member.

## Proof
For every integer \(t\) with \(0\le t<r\), the class left after removing \(tL\) satisfies
\[
(kH+(m-t)L)\cdot L=k-2(m-t)<0.
\]
Any effective divisor with negative intersection with the irreducible curve \(L\) must contain \(L\) as a component. Induction forces \(rL\) into every member of \(|kH+mL|\). Multiplication by the canonical section of \(\mathcal O_X(rL)\) therefore identifies the residual complete system with \(|B|\).

For even \(k=2a\), \(B\cdot L=k-2a=0\); for odd \(k=2a+1\), \(B\cdot L=1\). The smooth-irreducible existence result for classes \(jH+bL\) with \(j\ge2b\) on this very general quartic (Castorena–Vite, Lemma 2.2 and Corollary 2.3) applies to \(B\) because \(k\ge2a\). Hence a general \(A\in|B|\) is smooth irreducible, and since \(B\neq L\), it contains no \(L\). This proves that \(rL\) is precisely the fixed part. The intersection number proves that \(A\) is disjoint from \(L\) in the even case and meets it at one reduced transverse point \(q\) in the odd case: distinct smooth curves meeting with total intersection number one meet once transversely.

For a Cartier divisor with local defining equation \(F\) on a smooth surface with étale local coordinates \(u,v\), the cotangent presentation \(\mathcal O_D\xrightarrow{(F_u,F_v)}\mathcal O_D^{\oplus2}\to\Omega^1_D\to0\) gives the intrinsic first Fitting ideal generated by \(F_u,F_v\) in \(\mathcal O_D\). Pulling this ideal back to \(\mathcal O_X\) adds \(F\), which is already in the derivative ideal by Euler's local product identity for the specific local equations below.

At any point of \(L\setminus A\), choose \(u=0\) for \(L\). Up to a unit, \(D\) has local equation \(F=u^r\), and its Jacobian ideal is \((u^{r-1})\) in characteristic zero. In the odd case, at \(q\) the transversality gives regular coordinates \(u,v\) with \(L=(u)\) and \(A=(v)\), so \(F=u^rv\). Its derivatives give the *exact* ideal
\[
(F,F_u,F_v)=(ru^{r-1}v,u^r)=u^{r-1}(u,v).
\]
At points of \(A\setminus L\), the curve \(A\) is smooth and hence the Jacobian scheme is empty. These local descriptions glue to the claimed global ideals. Finally, when \(r\ge2\), the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal I_L^{r-1}\mathcal I_q
\longrightarrow\mathcal I_L^{r-1}
\longrightarrow\mathbf C_q\longrightarrow0
\]
shows that the embedded excess has length one; locally the relevant quotient is \((u^{r-1})/(u^{r-1}(u,v))\cong\mathbf C\). Because \((u^{r-1}(u,v))\) has associated prime \((u,v)\) in addition to \((u)\), this is an embedded associated point. In the case \(r=1\), the formula simply records the isolated transverse node \(q\).

## Verification
The symbolic calculation \((\partial_u(u^rv),\partial_v(u^rv))=u^{r-1}(rv,u)\) is valid for every positive integer \(r\) in characteristic zero. An auxiliary standalone Python script checks sample parameter ranges and these polynomial identities, but the proof above covers all integers in the stated domain without reliance on sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Castorena and Vite, *Curves contained in a quartic determinantal surface containing a line* (2026), introduce the quartic K3 line classes and explicitly discuss the incomplete picture of general divisors \(|kH+mL|\) when \(m\ge2\), \(1\le k\le2m-1\). Their smooth-irreducible existence theorem for the boundary residual class is used here. The fixed-component argument is elementary once that input is known; the particular full-family **intrinsic Jacobian-ideal parity formula and its embedded length-one defect** are the additional geometric statements. More general local calculations for products of smooth curves can imply the local derivative formulas; no claim of novelty is made for this local algebra in isolation.

## Limitations
The conclusion assumes a very general quartic and general divisors. It does not classify singular schemes for special residual members or quartics with larger Picard group. The proof does not identify the global Hilbert component or prove a new smooth-irreducible existence theorem. A covering published statement about the same Jacobian parity formula could exist beyond the literature inspected; independent verification has not been performed.
