# Scientific review: joint Novikov line–idempotent spectrum

## Correctness — PASS

The claim applies to every prime \(p\), every \(q=p^s\), and every \(a\in\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^p)\). The Novikov identities follow by direct expansion. The key reduction distinguishes units, valuation-one elements, and valuation at least two; the derivative leading term is nonzero for \(1\le r<p\). The canonical coefficient recurrences have invertible multipliers \(1,\ldots,p-1\). These yield a complete disjoint line classification and idempotent counts. The map from the top two coefficients of \(a\) to the canonical obstruction pair is triangular with diagonal entries one, giving exact parameter fibers. The packaged direct finite-field enumeration produces `VERIFY_OK`; no finite test is treated as a general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

Zhelyabin–Zakharov (2024), DOI 10.1134/S0037446624030169, explicitly develops the same general truncated differential Novikov construction and proves a structural representation theorem. It does not state the all-parameter finite-field *joint* line/idempotent spectrum in the inspected main text. Kolesnikov–Nesterenko (2023), DOI 10.1134/S0037446623030084, treats embeddings and conformal envelopes rather than these finite-field counts. Pozhidaev–Zhelyabin (2025 online; 2026 volume), DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2025.09.017, has a broader automorphism-theoretic abstract but no accessible full-text comparison here. Osborn (1992), DOI 10.1080/00927879208824486, is closely relevant to *existence and structure of idempotents in simple Novikov algebras*; full text was not accessible and might contain stronger statements about the individual idempotent locus. No searched published statement or available result proves the uniform four-row joint parameter census. This establishes a reasoned originality assessment, not an absolute novelty certificate.

## Value — PASS

The exact joint distribution is a natural invariant for the entire classical \(p\)-dimensional Gelfand–Dorfman family, not a selected small parameter slice. It unifies one-dimensional subalgebra geometry, idempotent abundance, and finite-field deformation parameters. The uniform-fiber phenomenon is a substantive structural statement; in particular, both the number of idempotents and the number of line subalgebras genuinely vary over a full, unrestricted family. These results are not supplied by the structural classification alone.

## Limitations

The enumeration counts labeled parameters and embedded lines; it is not a classification of higher-dimensional subalgebras, ideals, or isomorphism classes. Earlier idempotent literature not available in full leaves residual bibliographic uncertainty. Direct computations are finite checks and do not establish all-prime coverage. No external validation is claimed.
