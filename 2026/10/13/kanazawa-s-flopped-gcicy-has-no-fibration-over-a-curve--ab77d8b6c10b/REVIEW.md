# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Kanazawa proves that \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_-)/\operatorname{tors}\) has basis \(H_-,F_-\) and computes
\[
H_-^3=7,\qquad H_-^2F_-=8,\qquad H_-F_-^2=4,\qquad F_-^3=0.
\]
For \(D=aH_-+bF_-\), the condition \(D^2=0\) forces
\[
8a(a+b)=0,\qquad 7a^2+16ab+4b^2=0.
\]
If \(a=0\), then \(b=0\); if \(b=-a\), then \(-5a^2=0\), so again \(a=b=0\). Thus no nonzero rational divisor has square zero. Any nonconstant morphism to a projective curve would pull back a positive-degree divisor to a nonzero square-zero divisor, giving a contradiction.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating paper gives the entire numerical input but does not state the no-curve-base conclusion. Its companion paper treats natural K3 and genus-one fibrations and says the non-Gorenstein minus phase has a single genus-one fibration of index \(4\); that statement has a two-dimensional base and therefore does not imply absence of morphisms to curves. Targeted source, alias, square-zero, K3-fibration, surface-fibration, and implication searches found no covering statement. Earlier gCICY references predate the specific minus phase.

## Value
PASS. The result gives a clean birational-chamber distinction that is stronger than loss of one named projection: the plus phase has a K3 fibration over \(\mathbf P^1\), whereas the minus phase has no morphism to any projective curve. The proof turns the newly computed full intersection form into a global structural obstruction and is directly relevant to fibration behavior under Calabi--Yau flops.

The conclusion is restricted to morphisms on \(X_-\). It does not exclude rational maps with indeterminacy or fibrations appearing after a further birational modification.
