# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim follows from exterior supercommutativity, the exact decomposition of the normalized commutator into its bivector and top-degree parts, the \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-torsor count of decompositions of a fixed nonzero simple bivector, and the surjective trivector functional. The zero-bivector case is counted separately. The centralizer ranks \(0,1,4\) and sharp width-two obstruction are proved by explicit linear maps. The included `verify.py` has been run successfully for the prime fields of orders \(3\) and \(5\), with exact target counts and rank checks; the symbolic argument—not small-field experimentation—establishes the theorem for all odd prime powers.

## Originality — PASS (with residual bibliographic risk)
The relevant 2009 Grassmann polynomial-identity and central-polynomial results supply the algebraic framework and central-commutator identities, but not this exact single-commutator fiber law. The complete 2009 journal article by Koshlukov–Krasilnikov–da Silva was read. Its principal theorems concern generators of central-polynomial T-spaces over infinite fields. Bekh-Ochir–Rankin's directly finite-field 2009 preprint/2011 article was identified from its abstract and bibliographic record but its full text could not be inspected; this is an explicit residual risk. The 2025/2026 automorphism-stable-subspaces article describes invariant linear subspaces and subalgebras, rather than preimages of individual values of the bilinear commutator map. No uniqueness follows merely from failed searches; classical Plücker decomposability, standard exterior identities, and the linear commutator span are credited.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete exact and naturally defined word-map invariant for the smallest exterior algebra in odd characteristic where single commutators cease to exhaust their linear span. It identifies the Klein-quadric obstruction, proves the optimal width-two correction, and quantifies every target fiber and centralizer type uniformly over finite extension fields. Such exact fibers distinguish attainable from unattainable commutators and determine commuting probabilities; they are not an arbitrary restriction to a few examples.
