# Large repetition subcodes inside the new distance-five BCH family
## Finding

Let
\[
s\ge2,\qquad n=(2^{2s}+1)(2^s-1),
\]
and let
\[
C=\mathcal C_{(2,n,5,1)}
\]
be the binary narrow-sense BCH code of designed distance \(5\).

Define
\[
r_s=
\begin{cases}
n/5,&s\not\equiv2\pmod4,\\
n/51,&s\equiv2\pmod4.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(C\) contains \(r_s\) pairwise disjoint codewords of weight \(5\). Their span
\[
R_s\subseteq C
\]
is, after deletion of coordinates that are identically zero on \(R_s\) in the second congruence class, isomorphic to
\[
[5,1,5]_2^{\oplus r_s}.
\]
Hence
\[
\dim R_s=r_s,
\qquad
W_{R_s}(z)=(1+z^5)^{r_s}.
\]

The ambient BCH code therefore satisfies the coefficientwise lower bounds
\[
A_{5j}(C)\ge\binom{r_s}{j}
\qquad(0\le j\le r_s),
\]
and its generalized Hamming weights obey
\[
d_j(C)\le5j
\qquad(1\le j\le r_s).
\]
In particular, \(C\) contains at least \(r_s\) pairwise disjoint minimum-weight codewords.

When
\[
s\not\equiv2\pmod4,
\]
the subcode occupies every coordinate and is itself cyclic, generated by
\[
1+X^{n/5}+X^{2n/5}+X^{3n/5}+X^{4n/5}.
\]

For example, at \(s=3\), the recent source gives the code \([455,431,5]_2\); the result here exhibits inside it a \(91\)-dimensional direct sum of fivefold repetition blocks and yields
\[
A_{5j}(C)\ge\binom{91}{j}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Wang, He, Yi, and Zheng prove that
\[
C=\mathcal C_{(2,n,5,1)}
\]
has parameters
\[
[n,n-8s,5]_2
\]
for every \(s\ge2\). Their proof uses the standard binary criterion that a polynomial represents a codeword of this designed-distance-five BCH code once it vanishes at a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\beta\) and at \(\beta^3\).

Their Lemma 6 supplies two explicit root-of-unity constructions. If
\[
s\not\equiv2\pmod4,
\]
then \(5\mid n\) and the five elements are the complete subgroup of order \(5\). If
\[
s\equiv2\pmod4,
\]
then \(51\mid n\) and their construction uses a primitive \(51\)-st root together with exponent pattern
\[
E=\{0,3,21,33,44\}.
\]
The finding below extracts the full translate family hidden in those two constructions.

## Proof

Let \(\beta\) be a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity.

First suppose
\[
s\not\equiv2\pmod4.
\]
The source proves \(5\mid n\). Put
\[
r=n/5,
\qquad
\omega=\beta^r.
\]
Then \(\omega\) has order \(5\), so
\[
1+\omega+\omega^2+\omega^3+\omega^4=0.
\]
Multiplication by \(3\) permutes the residue classes modulo \(5\), hence also
\[
1+\omega^3+\omega^6+\omega^9+\omega^{12}=0.
\]
For every
\[
0\le t<r,
\]
define
\[
c_t(X)=X^t\left(1+X^r+X^{2r}+X^{3r}+X^{4r}\right).
\]
Then
\[
c_t(\beta)=0,
\qquad
c_t(\beta^3)=0,
\]
so \(c_t\in C\). Its support is
\[
\{t,t+r,t+2r,t+3r,t+4r\}.
\]
The \(r\) such supports are pairwise disjoint and partition all \(n\) coordinates, because each coordinate has a unique residue modulo \(r\). Thus their span is a direct sum of \(r\) binary repetition codes of length \(5\). Since the displayed generator divides \(X^n-1\), this span is also the cyclic subcode generated by that polynomial.

Now suppose
\[
s\equiv2\pmod4.
\]
The source proves \(51\mid n\) and constructs an element \(\theta\) of order \(51\) such that, for
\[
E=\{0,3,21,33,44\},
\]
we have
\[
\sum_{e\in E}\theta^e=0,
\qquad
\sum_{e\in E}\theta^{3e}=0.
\]
Put
\[
r=n/51.
\]
The subgroup of order \(51\) in the cyclic group of \(n\)-th roots is unique, so for some integer \(a\) with
\[
\gcd(a,51)=1
\]
we have
\[
\theta=\beta^{ar}.
\]
For \(0\le t<r\), define
\[
c_t(X)=\sum_{e\in E}X^{t+r(ae\bmod51)}.
\]
Then
\[
c_t(\beta)=\beta^t\sum_{e\in E}\theta^e=0,
\qquad
c_t(\beta^3)=\beta^{3t}\sum_{e\in E}\theta^{3e}=0,
\]
so again \(c_t\in C\).

Every support of \(c_t\) lies in the coordinate residue class \(t\) modulo \(r\). Hence the \(r\) supports are pairwise disjoint. Each has five coordinates because multiplication by \(a\) permutes the residues modulo \(51\) and the five source exponents are distinct. Thus their span is again isomorphic to
\[
[5,1,5]_2^{\oplus r}.
\]

In either case, a sum of exactly \(j\) of the disjoint generators has weight exactly \(5j\), and there are
\[
\binom{r_s}{j}
\]
such sums. This proves
\[
W_{R_s}(z)=(1+z^5)^{r_s}
\]
and the ambient weight-spectrum lower bound.

Finally, the span of any \(j\) chosen generators is a \(j\)-dimensional subcode supported on exactly \(5j\) coordinates. By the definition of generalized Hamming weight,
\[
d_j(C)\le5j.
\]

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` uses only the Python standard library.

It independently constructs \(\mathbb F_{16}\) and \(\mathbb F_{256}\). In \(\mathbb F_{16}\) it finds the four order-five elements and checks directly that the complete order-five subgroup has zero first and third power sums. In \(\mathbb F_{256}\) it searches all elements of exact order \(51\) and verifies that eight of them satisfy both source equations for the exponent set
\[
\{0,3,21,33,44\}.
\]
Thus the two nontrivial finite-field patterns used by the proof are replayed without importing source computations.

The verifier also checks the divisibility alternatives and the disjoint-support construction for every
\[
2\le s\le200,
\]
including exact support and weight-enumerator checks for representative cases \(s=2,3,4,6\). The finite sweep is a stress test; the infinite divisibility statements are proved algebraically above.

Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Wang, He, Yi, and Zheng settle the minimum-distance conjecture by constructing one weight-five codeword for each \(s\). Their Lemma 6 gives the order-five and order-fifty-one patterns, and their Theorem 7 concludes only the exact parameters
\[
[n,n-8s,5]_2.
\]
The paper does not enumerate translates, state a repetition subcode, give lower bounds on \(A_{5j}\), or discuss generalized Hamming weights for this family.

Chen, Xie, and Ding previously proved the minimum-distance-five cases when \(5\mid n\) and posed the remaining congruence class as a conjecture. Their paper likewise does not state the direct-sum repetition subcode or the resulting weight-spectrum and generalized-weight consequences.

Focused searches using the exact length, weight-five support language, disjoint cyclic translates, repetition-subcode terminology, weight-enumerator lower bounds, and generalized-Hamming-weight terminology found no prior statement of this structural consequence.

## Limitations

The result gives a certified subcode and therefore lower bounds on the ambient weight distribution; it does not determine the complete weight enumerator of \(C\).

The generalized Hamming inequalities are upper bounds, not exact values of \(d_j(C)\).

For
\[
s\equiv2\pmod4,
\]
the constructed repetition subcode occupies only \(5/51\) of the coordinates. No claim is made that the lower bound \(r_s=n/51\) is the maximum possible number of pairwise disjoint minimum-weight codewords.
