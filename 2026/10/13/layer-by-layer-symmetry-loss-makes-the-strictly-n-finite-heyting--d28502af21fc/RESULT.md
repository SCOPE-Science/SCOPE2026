# Exact automorphism groups of the finite level truncations of the canonical Esakia spaces

## Finding

Let \(X_n\) be the canonical Esakia space in the construction of strictly \(n\)-finite varieties of Heyting algebras [1], for \(n\ge0\). Put \(q=2^n+1\), the number of points on each finite level \(L_n^i\), with labels \(x_i^0,\ldots,x_i^{q-1}\). For \(d\ge0\), let \(T_{n,d}=\bigcup_{i=0}^dL_n^i\) with the induced order. Then
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(T_{n,d})\cong S_{\max(q-d,1)}.
\]
The least truncation depth with trivial automorphism group is \(q-1\) (exactly \(q\) levels). More precisely, the group permutes the missing-comparability paths that meet all retained levels, freely and arbitrarily when there is more than one such path; all other paths are fixed pointwise.

This is a statement about finite induced posets, not a claim that these truncations are the duals of particular subalgebras or quotients. It applies equally to any finite window of consecutive levels by shifting the level indices.

## Proof

By [1, Definition 4.1], the only missing comparabilities from level \(i\) to level \(i-1\) are the pairs \((x_i^l,x_{i-1}^{l+1})\) for \(0\le l<q-1\). All lower points lie below all upper points whenever the level gap is at least two. Since \(q\ge2\), each point on a non-top level lies below at least one point of the preceding level. It follows by iteratively deleting maximal elements that the levels of each truncation are intrinsic and preserved by all automorphisms.

Make an undirected graph on the retained vertices, putting edges precisely at the missing adjacent-level comparabilities. A tuple of level permutations is a poset automorphism if and only if it preserves this graph: same-level, reversed-level and long-gap relations are already determined by the levels. In the graph, each edge preserves \(s=i+l\). For each \(0\le s\le d+q-1\), the vertices with this sum form a path, possibly a single vertex, meeting each level of the integer interval
\[
J_s=[\max(0,s-q+1),\min(d,s)]
\]
in one vertex. Consecutive levels in \(J_s\) are joined by the corresponding missing pair, so these paths are exactly the graph components. A level-preserving graph automorphism can only exchange paths with the same interval, and each such exchange is uniquely determined by its permutation of those paths. Conversely every permutation within a class of paths with identical interval preserves the entire graph and hence the order.

For \(0\le d\le q-1\), precisely the indices \(d\le s\le q-1\) give the interval \([0,d]\), a class of size \(q-d\). All other intervals are distinct: if the upper endpoint is below \(d\) it recovers \(s\), and if the lower endpoint is positive it recovers \(s\). For \(d\ge q\) no interval equals \([0,d]\), and the same endpoint argument proves they are all distinct. This proves the formula, including \(n=0\) and \(d=0\), and gives the exact least rigid depth.

## Relation to the infinite witness

The full-space rigidity of \(X_n\) and the rigidity of its dual Heyting algebra \(X_n^*\) are separately proved in [2]. They also follow from the finite theorem, but are not claimed here as distinct new results. The unique minimum is fixed, maximal-layer deletion once again preserves every finite level, and any two finite-level points are contained in a rigid truncation by choosing \(d\) large enough. Thus every order automorphism of the infinite space is trivial, and Esakia duality transfers this to the dual algebra. Strict \(n\)-finiteness describes the variety generated by the infinite \((n+1)\)-generated witness algebra; this proof concerns its explicit dual space and says nothing about all varieties or about endomorphisms.

[2] determines fixed labels by propagating along the missing-upper maps in the infinite space. It does not determine which finite permutations lift, nor give the exact automorphism group in the presence of the truncation's bottom boundary. The interval classification above supplies both the upper bound and the independent free permutations in finite depth.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` checks widths \(2\le q\le7\) and depths \(0\le d\le q+1\). For each top permutation it constructs the only possible lower-level permutations if they exist, then checks every vertex-pair order comparison. This is a finite check; the proof by path intervals establishes the unbounded claim.
