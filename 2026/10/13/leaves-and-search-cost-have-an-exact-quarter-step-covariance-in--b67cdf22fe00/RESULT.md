# Leaves and search cost have an exact quarter-step covariance in random recursive trees

## Finding

Let \(T_n\) be the standard random recursive tree on the labels \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with \(n\ge2\). Equivalently, for each \(j\ge2\), vertex \(j\) chooses its parent independently and uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\).

Let \(L_n\) be the number of leaves, where a leaf means a vertex of outdegree zero, and let
\[P_n=\sum_{v=1}^n d(v)\]
be the total internal path length, where \(d(v)\) is the distance from the root \(1\) to \(v\).

Then
\[\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,P_n)=-\frac{n-2}{4}.}\tag{1}\]
Thus
\[\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(L_{n+1},P_{n+1})-\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,P_n)=-\frac14,\qquad n\ge2.}\tag{2}\]

The mechanism passes through the leaf-depth sum
\[Q_n=\sum_{v\text{ leaf}}d(v),\]
whose expectation is
\[\boxed{\mathbb E Q_n=\frac n2H_{n-1}-\frac{n-2}{4}.}\tag{3}\]

For \(n\ge3\), the classical identity \(\operatorname{Var}(L_n)=n/12\) makes the least-squares slope of total path length on leaf count
\[\boxed{\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(P_n,L_n)}{\operatorname{Var}(L_n)}=-3\frac{n-2}{n}.}\tag{4}\]
In particular, the projection coefficient tends to \(-3\).

Using the classical asymptotic
\[\operatorname{Var}(P_n)\sim\left(2-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\right)n^2,\]
equation (1) also gives
\[\boxed{\operatorname{Corr}(L_n,P_n)\sim-\frac{\sqrt3}{2\sqrt{\,2-\pi^2/6\,}}\,n^{-1/2}.}\tag{5}\]
So leaf abundance and search cost are negatively coupled at every nontrivial size, while their standardized dependence vanishes at rate \(n^{-1/2}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The tree is the uniform recursive tree generated by independent uniform parent choices. The root has depth zero. Leaves are vertices of outdegree zero, so for \(n\ge2\) the root is not a leaf.

The covariance result is finite and exact for every integer \(n\ge2\). Equation (5) uses a known asymptotic variance of total path length; the exact covariance in (1) does not depend on that asymptotic result.

The statistic \(Q_n\) is an auxiliary bridge: it is the sum of leaf depths, not merely the number of leaves.

## Proof

Grow \(T_n\) to \(T_{n+1}\) by choosing a parent \(V_n\) uniformly from the \(n\) existing vertices. Let \(D_n=d(V_n)\) and \(J_n=\mathbf 1\{V_n\text{ is a leaf}\}\). Then
\[L_{n+1}=L_n+1-J_n,\tag{6}\]
\[P_{n+1}=P_n+D_n+1.\tag{7}\]
Conditional on \(T_n\),
\[\mathbb E[D_n\mid T_n]=\frac{P_n}{n},\qquad \mathbb E[J_n\mid T_n]=\frac{L_n}{n},\tag{8}\]
and
\[\mathbb E[J_nD_n\mid T_n]=\frac{Q_n}{n}.\tag{9}\]

Write \(c_n=\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,P_n)\), \(\ell_n=\mathbb E L_n\), \(p_n=\mathbb E P_n\), and \(q_n=\mathbb E Q_n\). From (8),
\[\operatorname{Cov}(L_n,D_n)=\frac{c_n}{n},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(J_n,P_n)=\frac{c_n}{n},\]
so these terms cancel when (6)--(7) are expanded. By (9),
\[\operatorname{Cov}(J_n,D_n)=\frac{q_n}{n}-\frac{\ell_np_n}{n^2}.\]
Therefore
\[\boxed{c_{n+1}=c_n-\frac{q_n}{n}+\frac{\ell_np_n}{n^2}.}\tag{10}\]

The standard recursive-tree first moments are
\[\ell_n=\frac n2\qquad(n\ge2),\tag{11}\]
and
\[p_n=nH_{n-1}-(n-1).\tag{12}\]

When the new vertex is attached, it becomes a leaf at depth \(D_n+1\); if its parent was a leaf, that parent ceases to be one. Hence
\[Q_{n+1}=Q_n+(1-J_n)D_n+1.\tag{13}\]
Using (8)--(9),
\[q_{n+1}=\left(1-\frac1n\right)q_n+\frac{p_n}{n}+1=\left(1-\frac1n\right)q_n+H_n.\tag{14}\]
Since \(q_1=0\), multiplying by \(n\) and telescoping gives
\[(n-1)q_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}kH_k.\tag{15}\]
The harmonic identity
\[\sum_{k=1}^{m}kH_k=\frac{m(m+1)}2H_m-\frac{m(m-1)}4\tag{16}\]
proves (3).

Substituting (3), (11), and (12) into (10) gives
\[-\frac{q_n}{n}+\frac{\ell_np_n}{n^2}=-\frac14.\tag{17}\]
Thus \(c_{n+1}=c_n-1/4\). At \(n=2\), both \(L_2\) and \(P_2\) are deterministic, so \(c_2=0\). Iteration proves (1).

Equation (4) follows from the classical variance \(\operatorname{Var}(L_n)=n/12\), and (5) from the classical total-path-length variance asymptotic.

## Verification

The accompanying exact checker enumerates every recursive tree on \(n\) labeled vertices for \(2\le n\le10\). There are
\[\sum_{m=1}^{9}m!=409113\]
such trees in the replay.

For each tree it computes parent depths, leaf count, total path length, and the sum of leaf depths directly. Using exact rational arithmetic, it checks (1), (3), the first moments (11)--(12), the leaf variance used in (4), and the exact least-squares slope.

The exhaustive enumeration is supplementary. The proof for arbitrary \(n\) is the growth-step calculation (6)--(17).

## Relationship to prior work

Dobrow and Fill study total path length in random recursive trees, including moment recurrences and the non-Gaussian limiting distribution. Their work establishes the search-cost side of the present calculation.

Leckey and Neininger study both the number of leaves and internal path length in the broader Hoppe-tree family. Their full public manuscript treats these quantities in separate sections: the leaf count through an urn/martingale analysis and internal path length through depth recurrences and contraction methods. Targeted full-text searches found no covariance or joint-distribution statement connecting the two quantities.

Lundblad and Wagner study path length together with external path length, the sum of depths of leaves. Their work is directly relevant to the auxiliary statistic \(Q_n\), but its stated objective is comparison of average vertex depth with average leaf depth rather than leaf-count/internal-path-length covariance.

Zhang studies the number of leaves in a random recursive tree, including normal approximation and large deviations. That marginal leaf theory does not by itself determine a mixed moment with total path length.

The result here is the exact finite mixed law between two canonical global functionals: leaf abundance and total search cost.

## Limitations

The quarter-step law uses uniform attachment. Weighted recursive trees and Hoppe trees generally change the parent-selection averages and leaf dynamics, so (1) should not be transferred to those models without a new calculation.

Negative covariance does not imply negative association of the full tree statistics.

The first moments and marginal variance facts used here are classical. The claimed contribution is the mixed covariance identity and its exact one-step mechanism.

Older recursive-tree literature is extensive. Although the closest full source treating both leaves and internal path length was inspected, an equivalent mixed identity could exist under terminology such as terminal vertices and search cost.
