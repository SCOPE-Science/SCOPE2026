# Length \(15\) is the first four-attractor generalized pseudostandard layer
## Finding
Let a binary generalized pseudostandard prefix be generated from the empty word by repeatedly appending one bit and taking the shortest palindrome or antipalindrome having the result as a prefix. Among all distinct such prefixes, the smallest length at which the minimum string-attractor size can equal \(4\) is
\[
15.
\]
Exactly two length-\(15\) prefixes have minimum attractor size \(4\):
\[
\mathtt{010011010110010}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathtt{101100101001101}.
\]
They are bitwise complements. The first is generated by
\[
\Delta=0000,\qquad \Theta=ERER,
\]
and the second by \(\Delta=1111\) with the same antimorphism directive \(\Theta=ERER\). In zero-based positions, each has the minimum string attractor
\[
\{0,4,7,11\}.
\]
No binary generalized pseudostandard prefix of length at most \(14\) needs four attractor positions.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(R\) be reversal and let \(E\) be reversal followed by bit complementation. For a finite word \(u\), its \(R\)-closure or \(E\)-closure is the shortest word fixed by the corresponding antimorphism and having \(u\) as a prefix. Starting with \(w_0=\varepsilon\), a finite generalized pseudostandard directive produces
\[
w_{i+1}=(w_i\delta_{i+1})^{\vartheta_{i+1}},
\]
where \(\delta_i\in\{0,1\}\), \(\vartheta_i\in\{E,R\}\), and the superscript denotes shortest pseudopalindromic closure.

For a finite word \(w\), a string attractor is a set \(\Gamma\) of positions such that every distinct nonempty factor of \(w\) has an occurrence crossing some position of \(\Gamma\). Write \(\gamma(w)\) for the minimum size of a string attractor.

The classification is by distinct generated prefix words, not by directive descriptions. A word can have more than one directive representation.

## Proof
Every closure step strictly increases word length: before closure one new bit is appended, and closure never removes a prefix. Consequently any generated prefix of length at most \(15\) occurs after at most \(15\) directive steps. It is therefore enough to perform a breadth-first traversal from \(\varepsilon\), applying all four choices \((0,E),(0,R),(1,E),(1,R)\), discarding outputs longer than \(15\), and deduplicating equal words.

The numbers of distinct reachable prefixes at lengths \(1,2,\ldots,15\) are
\[
2,4,4,8,6,12,10,18,12,24,16,34,18,40,28.
\]
This traversal is exhaustive because every omitted transition strictly leaves the length window and can never return to it.

For each reached word \(w\) and each distinct factor \(f\), let \(U(f)\) be the union of all positions covered by all occurrences of \(f\) in \(w\). A set \(\Gamma\) is a string attractor exactly when
\[
\Gamma\cap U(f)\ne\varnothing
\]
for every distinct nonempty factor \(f\). Thus \(\gamma(w)\) is the minimum hitting-set size of this finite family of position sets. Exhaustive hitting-set search gives the following distributions, where each row lists counts by minimum attractor size:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
|w|&\gamma=1&\gamma=2&\gamma=3&\gamma=4\\
\hline
1&2&0&0&0\\
2&2&2&0&0\\
3&2&2&0&0\\
4&2&6&0&0\\
5&2&4&0&0\\
6&2&10&0&0\\
7&2&8&0&0\\
8&2&12&4&0\\
9&2&10&0&0\\
10&2&18&4&0\\
11&2&14&0&0\\
12&2&24&8&0\\
13&2&14&2&0\\
14&2&24&14&0\\
15&2&22&2&2
\end{array}
\]
Hence four positions are unnecessary below length \(15\), and exactly two words require four at length \(15\).

For the first exceptional word, the successive closures under \((0,E),(0,R),(0,E),(0,R)\) are
\[
\mathtt{01},\quad
\mathtt{010},\quad
\mathtt{01001101},\quad
\mathtt{010011010110010}.
\]
Complementing every directive bit gives the other exceptional word. Direct occurrence checking verifies that \(\{0,4,7,11\}\) covers every distinct factor of each word, while every one of the \(\binom{15}{3}=455\) three-position sets misses at least one factor. Therefore both minimum attractor sizes are exactly \(4\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` beside `certificate.json`.

The verifier reconstructs shortest \(R\)- and \(E\)-closures from their definitions and exhausts the complete closure graph through length \(15\). It computes minimum string-attractor size twice. One engine converts factors to unions of covered positions and solves the resulting finite hitting-set problem. A second engine tests factor occurrences directly and independently searches candidate position sets. The engines agree for every reached word.

The replay checks every distribution entry in the table, the absence of size-four minima below length \(15\), the exact two-word first layer, both directives, complement symmetry, and the displayed minimum attractor.

## Relationship to prior work
Dvořáková and Hendrychová introduced the relevant generalized-pseudostandard string-attractor problem. They prove minimum attractor size \(2\) for the standard complementary-symmetric Rote subclass and size \(3\) for the all-antipalindromic pseudostandard subclass, then single out \(4\) as the next natural threshold by conjecturing an upper bound of four for arbitrary binary generalized pseudostandard prefixes.

Kutsukake, Matsumoto, Nakashima, Inenaga, Bannai, and Takeda prove that the \(n\)-th Thue--Morse word has minimum attractor size \(4\) for \(n\ge4\). Under the standard convention \(|t_n|=2^n\), this supplies generalized-pseudostandard-related size-four examples from length \(16\) onward, but it does not classify the shortest size-four prefix in the full mixed-closure class.

Targeted searches using the exact two words, minimum-length language, generalized-pseudostandard terminology, pseudopalindromic-prefix terminology, and size-four string-attractor formulations located no statement of the length-\(15\) onset or its complete two-word equality layer.

## Limitations
The result is a sharp finite classification only for the onset of minimum attractor size \(4\). It does not give a universal bound on attractor size for generalized pseudostandard prefixes, classify later size-four prefixes, or characterize larger attractor numbers.

The enumeration is by distinct finite words; it does not enumerate all directive pairs generating each word. An unindexed finite computation or thesis table could contain the same length-\(15\) classification despite the targeted searches.
