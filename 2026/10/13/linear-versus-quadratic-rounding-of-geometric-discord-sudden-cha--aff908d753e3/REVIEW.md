# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact formula for first-subsystem squared Hilbert–Schmidt discord follows by orthogonal projection onto each measurement-fixed subspace and maximization of \(u^{\mathsf T}(xx^{\mathsf T}+TT^{\mathsf T})u\). Both physical families have explicitly computed two-by-two Bloch Gram matrices. Their nonzero offdiagonals prove analyticity for every positive preparation error and finite time. The scaled square-root expansions have strictly positive limiting radicands, ensuring differentiated, locally uniform limits, with precise centering and width. The all-time full-rank PPT criterion establishes separability. The package verifier checks actual matrices, partial transposes, both formulas and both limits independently. Finite tests are not the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Compared with the general spectral formula of Dakić–Vedral–Brukner (arXiv:1004.0190), Bell-diagonal dephasing studies of Yao et al. (arXiv:1303.4827), eigenvalue-crossing criteria of Yu–Li–Fan (arXiv:1309.0934), and entropic-discord imperfection examples of Pinto–Karpat–Fanchini (arXiv:1309.7945), the final mathematical statement concerns neither a generic discord formula nor mere disappearance of a kink. It quantifies two inequivalent *physical* perturbation directions with equal Hilbert–Schmidt amplitudes, proves different linear versus quadratic transition widths, and obtains the same explicit limiting derivative profile. The general spectral formula permits this derivation but the complete comparison is not stated in those inspected texts. A relevant non-X state/decoherence paper by Chen–Fu–Fang (doi:10.1007/s10773-014-2094-9) could only be assessed from its abstract. Priority therefore carries residual risk; search absence is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

Abrupt changes of a correlation measure are frequently inferred from idealized Bell-diagonal preparations. The finite perturbation that smears such a change is a natural robustness question. Two equal-distance, fully separable state families have transition widths differing by an entire power of error magnitude, even while both obey a universal crossover curve. This distinguishes local magnetization imperfections from cross-correlation imperfections in a directly testable mathematical model, and provides exact timescale predictions. It is not a normalization identity or an arbitrary numerical slice. The result neither asserts discord-based computational advantage nor claims applicability to entropic discord.

## Literature and limitations

The foundational spectral expression is due to Dakić–Vedral–Brukner; sudden-change behavior is established in later primary papers. Full or material sections of four preprints were inspected. The Chen–Fu–Fang full text remained inaccessible, so the statement of that paper was not conclusively excluded. No independent validation or priority certification has been performed.
