# Local divisor-class isogeny across the singular axis of elliptic-cone quotients

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\). Form the Nakamura–Shibata affine threefold \(X_m\) as in [1] from an elliptic curve \(E/\mathbf C\), a degree-\(m\) ample line bundle \(L\), a point \(P\in E\) of exact order \(m\), an order-\(m\) linearization of translation by \(P\) on \(L\), and a primitive \(m\)-th root \(\zeta\) acting on the extra affine coordinate. Put \(G=\langle P\rangle\), \(q:E\to B=E/G\), and let \(s=t^m\) be the invariant coordinate on the image of the cone-vertex axis. Denote by \(o\) its point at \(s=0\), and by \(x_s\) its point at each \(s\in\mathbf C^\times\).

**Theorem.** Let \(A\) be the degree-one line bundle on \(B\) descended from \(L\). Let \(M\) be the line bundle associated with the one-dimensional weight-\(\zeta\) representation of the principal \(G\)-bundle \(q\). Then \(M\) has exact order \(m\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\), and the smooth modification \(r:W_m\to X_m\) of [1] has the explicit form
\[
W_m\simeq\operatorname{Tot}_B(A^{-1}\oplus M),\qquad F=\operatorname{Exc}(r)\simeq\operatorname{Tot}_B(M).
\]
There are isomorphisms of groups
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_m)\simeq\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,o})\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^0(B),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,x_s})\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)\quad(s\ne0).
\]
The *two localization maps from the global class group* identify, respectively, with the identity and the dual isogeny \(q^*:\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)\). The kernel of the latter is precisely \(\langle M\rangle\) of order \(m\). The canonical divisor class of \(X_m\) is \([M^{-1}]\) in the first identification; thus the global-to-local map at a nonzero point kills precisely the cyclic subgroup generated by that class. Each local class group has non-torsion elements; in particular, \(X_m\) is not \(\mathbf Q\)-factorial at any point of this axis. Moreover, under \(r|_F:F\to\mathbf A^1_s\), the zero fiber is the Cartier divisor \(mB\) and every nonzero closed fiber is isomorphic to \(E\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbf C\). The curve \(E\) is an elliptic curve with origin; the translation point has *exact* order \(m\); the linearization satisfies the order-\(m\) cocycle condition; and the extra affine-line representation is primitive. The normal Gorenstein affine cone is \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}\bigoplus_{n\ge0}H^0(E,L^{\otimes n})\). The quotient is \(X_m=(Y\times\mathbf A^1_t)/G\). The points \(x_s\) are closed points on the singular axis; no assertion about local groups at nonclosed points or the completed local rings is made. The claimed maps compare local class groups only by localization from the *same global group*: they are not asserted to be morphisms between local rings at distinct points.

## Proof
**1. Equivariant descent.** The translation action on \(E\) is free, so \(q:E\to B\) is a connected étale cyclic isogeny of degree \(m\). The chosen linearization descends \(L\) to a line bundle \(A\) on \(B\), with \(q^*A\simeq L\) and \(\deg A=\deg L/m=1\). The trivial line on \(E\) with the weight-\(\zeta\) linearization descends to \(M\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\). If \(M^{\otimes j}\) is trivial, an equivariant trivialization upstairs differs from the original one by a nowhere-vanishing regular function on the complete connected curve \(E\). Such a function is constant; equivariance forces \(\zeta^j=1\). Conversely \(M^{\otimes m}\) is trivial. Hence the order is exactly \(m\).

**2. Smooth model and exceptional fibers.** The standard cone contraction \(\widetilde Y=\operatorname{Tot}_E(L^{-1})\to Y\) is proper and birational, and is an isomorphism away from the vertex [1, Example 3.1 and proof of Theorem 3.2]. Quotienting \(\widetilde Y\times\mathbf A^1_t\) by the free diagonal \(G\)-action, descent of both line bundles identifies the quotient with \(W_m=\operatorname{Tot}_B(A^{-1}\oplus M)\). It is smooth. The unique exceptional prime divisor \(F\) is the zero locus of the first vector-bundle coordinate, so \(F=\operatorname{Tot}_B(M)\) and \(\mathcal O_{W_m}(F)\simeq\pi^*A^{-1}\), where \(\pi:W_m\to B\). The exceptional image \(Z\simeq\mathbf A^1_s\) has codimension two, and \(r\) is an isomorphism on \(W_m\setminus F\). For \(s=0\), the reduced fiber of \(F\to Z\) is the zero section \(B\), with scheme fiber \(mB\), since \(s=t^m\) pulls back to a function vanishing to order \(m\). For \(s\ne0\), the \(m\) roots of \(t^m=s\) form a simply transitive \(G\)-set, whence \(r^{-1}(x_s)\simeq E\), with projection to \(B\) equal to \(q\).

**3. Global class group.** The vector-bundle homotopy invariance of Picard groups gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(W_m)=\pi^*\operatorname{Pic}(B)\). Smoothness identifies this with \(\operatorname{Cl}(W_m)\). Divisor localization on removing the single prime \(F\), followed by the fact that removal of the codimension-two locus \(Z\) does not alter Weil class groups, gives
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(X_m)\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(B)/\mathbf Z[A].
\]
As \(\deg A=1\), each quotient class has a unique degree-zero representative \(N\otimes A^{-\deg N}\). Therefore this group is \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\).

**4. Detecting a local class by a contracted fiber.** Localization of a normal Noetherian domain surjects on Weil class groups, hence \(\operatorname{Cl}(X_m)\to\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_m,x})\) is onto for each closed point \(x\). Represent a global class by \(N\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\). If the class is Cartier at \(x\in Z\), then in a neighborhood of \(r^{-1}(x)\) its strict transform differs by an integer multiple of \(F\) from a principal Cartier divisor. Restricting the corresponding line-bundle equality to the proper contracted fiber gives
\[
\pi^*N|_{r^{-1}(x)}\simeq\mathcal O_{W_m}(aF)|_{r^{-1}(x)}
\]
for some \(a\in\mathbf Z\) (the sign of \(a\) is immaterial). At \(x=o\), this says \(N\simeq A^{-a}\) on \(B\). Since \(\deg N=0\) and \(\deg A=1\), we have \(a=0\) and \(N\) trivial. Thus the global-to-local class-group map at \(o\) is injective as well as surjective.

At \(x=x_s\) with \(s\ne0\), the analogous restriction is \(q^*N\simeq (q^*A)^{-a}\simeq L^{-a}\) on the contracted fiber \(E\). Taking degrees again forces \(a=0\), so the localization kernel is *contained* in \(\ker(q^*:\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(E))\).

**5. The precise nonzero-point kernel.** Since \(q\) is a connected cyclic torsor, an element of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\) trivialized by \(q^*\) is represented by a character of \(G\): a trivialization of its pullback upstairs has a constant cocycle because \(H^0(E,\mathcal O_E^\times)=\mathbf C^\times\). Hence \(\ker q^*=\langle M\rangle\), a group of order \(m\). On the open set \(s\ne0\), the quotient \(Y\times\mathbf G_m\to X_m|_{s\ne0}\) is finite étale; the source is Gorenstein, so the target is Gorenstein there. In particular \(K_{X_m}\) is Cartier at all \(x_s\). Now \(K_{W_m}\simeq\pi^*(A\otimes M^{-1})\), by the canonical-bundle formula for the total space of a rank-two vector bundle over the elliptic curve \(B\). Under the global divisor-class quotient by \([F]=[A^{-1}]\), \([K_{X_m}]\) corresponds to \([M^{-1}]\). Therefore \(\langle M\rangle\) is contained in the nonzero-point localization kernel. Together with the preceding containment this gives equality. The dual isogeny \(q^*\) is surjective over \(\mathbf C\); consequently the indicated local group is \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)\).

**6. Non-Q-factoriality.** Each elliptic Picard group in the result has non-torsion points. In a local normal ring, \(\mathbf Q\)-factoriality would make every class in the Weil divisor group torsion. Thus no point of \(Z\) is \(\mathbf Q\)-factorial. This completes the theorem.

## Verification
The argument is symbolic and applies to all integers \(m\ge2\); no finite enumeration is used as an infinite proof. Its principal checkpoints are: \(\deg A=1\); the exact order of \(M\) from connected equivariant descent; \(\mathcal O_{W_m}(F)=\pi^*A^{-1}\); the codimension-two localization sequence; and restriction of a locally principal divisor to contracted fibers of degrees \(1\) and \(m\). The nonzero-point converse uses the Gorenstein property of a finite étale quotient, not the original canonical-index computation. For \(m=2\) and \(m=3\) the character-kernel sizes are respectively \(2\) and \(3\), consistent with the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Nakamura and Shibata [1] supply the quotient construction, smooth modification, and the exact Cartier index of the canonical class. Their Claim 3.3 determines the order of the canonical line on the exceptional surface and their proof recalls \(\operatorname{Pic}(F)\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(B)\). The present theorem does **not** claim those established facts as new: it determines *all* global and local Weil divisor classes at *every* closed singular-axis point and the exact localization isogeny, which are not stated in [1]. The Picard invariance and Weil-divisor localization used in the proof are standard tools [2,3]. Results on finite divisor class groups at toric fixed points concern different varieties, and a previously recorded result for this same elliptic-cone family treats the singular stratum and canonical-index jump, not these complete local groups. No conclusive older covering calculation was identified.

## Limitations
These are algebraic class groups of the stated ordinary local rings over \(\mathbf C\), not class groups of completions, étale or strict-henselian localizations. The proof does not classify all singularities away from the vertex axis, determine the full class-group functor under deformation, or claim novelty for the original minimal-log-discrepancy and canonical-index results. General divisor-class theorems could make the calculation implicit in unexamined literature; no independent audit has been performed.
