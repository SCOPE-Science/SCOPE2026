# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The result is about the explicit finite graph on the noncollinear triples of the seven-point Fano plane, with disjointness adjacency. The proof has no infinite extrapolation. The necessary-and-sufficient criterion says a subset of a matching is forcing precisely when it intersects the difference with every other perfect matching. A smallest-unmatched-vertex recursion enumerates all \(84\) matchings without duplicates; all \(16{,}384\) subsets of each are tested. An independently coded extension-count recurrence in C++ confirms the exact coefficient vector for a canonical matching. Full graph-automorphism enumeration and the explicitly realized group of order \(336\) establish transitivity on matchings and verify the \(51\) quartet orbits. Every alternating cycle arises as a symmetric-difference component; all \(74\) are enumerated, and the unique eight-cycle meets every ten-cycle. Thus three vertex-disjoint alternating cycles cannot occur, while a disjoint pair is witnessed. The code checks both sides. The stated limitations distinguish machine-exhaustive finite proof from broader unproved claims.

## Originality (O): PASS, with priority risk
The full inspected 2009 forced-matching paper defines forcing subsets and proves results for grids and other graph products; its planar-bipartite packing theorem cannot imply the corresponding equality for the nonplanar Coxeter graph. The Coxeter graph's order, symmetries and \(84\) perfect matchings are known, including the latter in recent matching-polynomial literature; those facts do not determine the full local forcing-subset polynomial, quartets' group orbits or alternating-cycle packing. Search aliases included forced matching, local forcing subsets, forcing polynomial, F028A, Coxeter matching, alternating-cycle transversals, and symmetry orbits. No equivalent or dominating statement was located in the checked material. A recently published matching-polynomial article was accessible only as indexed source excerpts; it is a specific residual literature risk. Absence of a search result is not treated as an independent novelty certificate.

## Value (V): PASS
Forcing perfect matchings is a natural discrete-identification invariant. For this highly symmetric classical nonhamiltonian cubic graph, the complete per-matching forcing polynomial, equality of all \(84\) profiles, and \(51\) structural optimal orbits are a meaningful complete classification. The strict gap \(4\) versus \(2\) separates alternating-cycle transversals from packings beyond the planar-bipartite setting, giving a concrete boundary to a standard minimax theorem. This is not a routine naming change or an arbitrary enumeration of a graph unrelated to the invariant.
