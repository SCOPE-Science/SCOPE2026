With calibrated positive false-detection probability \(f>0\), the information-optimal number of repeat visits per site for estimating vanishingly rare occupancy grows as \(\log(1/\psi)/D(\operatorname{Bern}(p)\|\operatorname{Bern}(f))\), and the maximal Fisher information per visit is asymptotic to \(D(\operatorname{Bern}(p)\|\operatorname{Bern}(f))/[\psi\log(1/\psi)]\). At the singular boundary \(f=0\), one visit becomes uniquely optimal for sufficiently rare occupancy.
