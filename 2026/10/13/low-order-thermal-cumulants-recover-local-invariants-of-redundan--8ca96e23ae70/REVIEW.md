# Self-review
## Correctness
PASS. Redundancy-freeness removes every trace word with an odd multiplicity. The surviving fourth- and sixth-order multiplicity patterns were evaluated exactly from Pauli commutation signs, reorganized into the weighted graph sums \(E_x\), \(Q_x\), \(W_x\), and \(T_x\), and then converted to cumulants by the logarithm series. The proof is uniform in graph size and real couplings. A finite exhaustive checker corroborates, but does not establish, the theorem.

## Originality
PASS. Searches covered low-order spectral moments, high-temperature cumulants, anticommutation and frustration graphs, edge/triangle recovery, noncontextual Pauli Hamiltonians, and free-fermion solvability. The closest primary source, arXiv:2610.03697v1, provides the redundancy-free framework, graph encoding, and general partition-function/paired-word expansions but does not state the closed fourth/sixth graph invariants or the equal-coupling regular inversion. Full-text checks of arXiv:2506.19778v1 and arXiv:2610.03557v1 did not reveal a covering implication. Residual risk remains from older literature phrased in different high-temperature-series terminology and from contemporaneous work not yet indexed.

## Value
PASS. The formulas identify the first local anticommutation-graph information visible in thermodynamics. At fourth order they recover total weighted anticommutation mass; at sixth order they add an incident-edge/triangle combination. For equal couplings on regular graphs, the first two graph-dependent cumulants recover degree and triangle count exactly, giving a direct non-isospectrality test for graphs that differ in either invariant. The statement is structural and parameter-uniform rather than a finite-instance census.

## Closest literature and limitations
The result assumes redundancy-freeness and Pauli involutions. Algebraic relations among distinct terms can add trace words. The general weighted sixth coefficient does not separate wedges from triangles, and triangle recovery needs equal-magnitude couplings plus regularity. No full graph reconstruction, higher-order classification, or new complexity threshold is claimed.
