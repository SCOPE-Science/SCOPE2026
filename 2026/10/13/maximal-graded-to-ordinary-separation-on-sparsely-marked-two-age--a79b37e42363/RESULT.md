# Maximal graded-to-ordinary separation on sparsely marked two-agent S5 grids

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(W_n=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^2\), with two named reflexive equivalence relations \(R\) and \(C\) connecting cells in the same row and in the same column, respectively. Interpret one propositional atom by
\[
p(i,j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (j=i)\ \text{or}\ (j=i-1).
\]
Exactly \(2n-1\) of the \(n^2\) cells satisfy \(p\); the marked row-column incidence graph is an alternating Hamiltonian path.

Let \(B_n\) be the number of ordinary modal-bisimulation classes and let \(G_n\) be the number of graded (successor-counting) modal-bisimulation classes. Then
\[
B_n=2,\qquad G_n=n^2.
\]
More precisely, let \(K_t(n)\) be the number of distinct depth-at-most-\(t\) graded modal types, with unbounded finite counting thresholds. The **complete modal-depth spectrum** is
\[
K_t(n)=
\begin{cases}
2,&t=0,\\
(t+1)^2+3,&1\le t\le n-2,\\
n^2,&t\ge n-1.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus depth \(n-1\) is both necessary and sufficient to define every singleton. The Boolean algebra generated by \(p\) using all graded row and column operators contains \(2^{n^2}\) sets, while the ordinary bimodal algebra generated by the same \(p\) contains only four sets.

The ratio is **extremal among all one-bit valuations on the same grid**:
\[
\max_{V(p)\subseteq W_n}\frac{G_n(V)}{B_n(V)}=\frac{n^2}{2}.
\]
Here both quotients refer to the same valuation and the same two relations. The claimed result is this explicit maximal separation and its exact graded-depth census, not the established generic connection between graded modalities and counting partition refinement.

## Assumptions and scope

- \(n\ge3\); the two row/column accessibility relations are named and are inclusive of the current cell. No diagonal or global modality is used.
- Ordinary modal logic has Boolean connectives and existential row/column modalities. Graded modal logic additionally permits \(\langle R\rangle_{\ge k}\) and \(\langle C\rangle_{\ge k}\) for every positive integer \(k\). Modal depth is the maximum number of nested modal operators.
- For a finite structure, a depth-\(t\) type records the propositional value and, at each subsequent round, the sets of previously available successor types (ordinary case) or the counts of successors of each type (graded case). These are precisely formula-equivalence classes at depth \(t\).
- \(K_t(n)\) counts types, so the corresponding depth-bounded Boolean algebra of definable subsets has \(2^{K_t(n)}\) elements.

## Proof

**1. Ordinary collapse.** Each row and each column contains at least one marked and at least one unmarked cell when \(n\ge3\). Consequently the relation identifying all \(p\)-true cells with one another and all \(p\)-false cells with one another is an ordinary bisimulation: for either accessibility relation, any cell has a successor of each propositional value. The two values are already distinguished by \(p\), so exactly two ordinary types exist at every modal depth. The ordinary generated algebra therefore has four elements.

**2. Graded type recursion.** Let \(T_t(i,j)\) denote the graded depth-\(t\) cell type. Let \(\mathcal R_t\) partition the row indices by their multisets of \(T_t\)-types, and \(\mathcal C_t\) analogously partition the column indices. These are row and column *histogram partitions*, not additional modalities.

At depth zero, \(T_0(i,j)\) is the truth value of \(p(i,j)\). At depth \(t\ge1\), the graded cell type is determined exactly by
\[
\bigl(p(i,j),\ [i]_{\mathcal R_{t-1}},\ [j]_{\mathcal C_{t-1}}\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
To justify the word “exactly,” begin with the standard recursion that attaches to \(T_{t-1}(i,j)\) the two multisets of \(T_{t-1}\)-successors. The induction hypothesis recovers the preceding cell type from \(p\) and the previous row and column histograms, since each histogram at time \(t-1\) refines its histogram at time \(t-2\). Conversely these data recover the entire new cell signature. This proves (1) by induction, including the base \(t=1\).

It follows that \(\mathcal R_{s+1}\) is obtained from \(\mathcal R_s\) by further distinguishing rows according to the **number of marked entries in every class of \(\mathcal C_s\)**. The analogous statement holds for columns. Indeed the number of all entries in each old class is fixed, so the unmarked counts are determined by the marked counts.

**3. Exact alternating peeling.** Represent the \(p\)-relation as a bipartite graph with row vertices \(r_i\), column vertices \(c_j\), and edges \(r_ic_i\) for all \(i\), plus edges \(r_ic_{i-1}\) for \(i\ge1\). This graph is the path
\[
r_0-c_0-r_1-c_1-\cdots-r_{n-1}-c_{n-1}.
\]
At depth zero, the histogram partition isolates \(r_0\) among the rows and \(c_{n-1}\) among the columns, because their marked degrees are one and every other row/column has marked degree two.

For \(0\le s\le n-2\), put
\[
a_s=1+\lfloor s/2\rfloor,\qquad b_s=\lfloor(s+1)/2\rfloor.
\]
We claim that \(\mathcal R_s\) consists of singleton rows with indices below \(a_s\), singleton rows with indices at least \(n-b_s\), and one central consecutive interval \([a_s,n-b_s-1]\). Similarly, \(\mathcal C_s\) has singleton columns below \(b_s\), singleton columns at least \(n-a_s\), and one central interval \([b_s,n-a_s-1]\). The central interval has \(n-s-1\) indices on either side.

This is true for \(s=0\). If \(s\) is even, then \(a_s=b_s+1\). Exactly the rightmost central row sees a marked neighbor in a previously isolated column to the right; exactly the leftmost central column sees a marked neighbor in an isolated row to the left. All other central rows/columns have the same two marked neighbors within their respective central opposite-side blocks. Therefore the next refinement peels the rightmost central row and the leftmost central column. If \(s\) is odd, \(a_s=b_s\), and the symmetric argument peels the leftmost central row and the rightmost central column. No other split occurs. This proves the claim for all \(s\) by induction. At \(s=n-2\), both histogram partitions are discrete.

**4. Cell-type census.** Fix \(1\le t\le n-2\), and set \(s=t-1\). The row and column histogram partitions \(\mathcal R_s,\mathcal C_s\) each have \(s+2=t+1\) blocks. All except one are singleton blocks; each central block contains \(m=n-t\ge2\) indices. By (1), each of the \((t+1)^2\) nonempty Cartesian products of these blocks gives one cell type, **plus one extra type for every product containing both values of \(p\)**.

Exactly three products contain both values. One is the central-row by central-column product: its induced marked edges form a path with \(2m-1\) marked cells among \(m^2\), so both values occur for \(m\ge2\). The second is the central-row block crossed with the unique adjacent peeled column at the active path boundary, where exactly one of its \(m\) cells is marked. The third is the corresponding peeled row crossed with the central-column block. All remaining blocks are monochromatic: a singleton-by-singleton product consists of one cell, and all other singleton-by-central products have no marked cells. Hence
\[
K_t(n)=(t+1)^2+3.
\]
At \(t=0\) the two propositional values give \(K_0(n)=2\). At \(t=n-1\), both histogram partitions from time \(n-2\) are discrete. By (1), all \(n^2\) cells have different types. No further split is possible. Since \((n-1)^2+3<n^2\) for \(n\ge3\), the depth \(n-1\) threshold is sharp.

**5. Optimality of the separation ratio.** For any nonconstant valuation there are at least two ordinary modal types, while there can be no more than \(n^2\) graded types. Thus \(G/B\le n^2/2\). For a constant valuation every cell has the same graded and ordinary type, giving \(G/B=1\). The displayed Hamiltonian-path marking attains \(B=2\) and \(G=n^2\), proving the exact maximum.

## Verification

The self-contained, standard-library Python program `verify.py` checks the recursion through \(n=70\) (68 grid sizes, 2,482 graded depth levels), compares both terminal quotients with an independent pairwise back-and-forth algorithm using explicit bipartite perfect matching for counting bisimulation on sizes \(2\) through \(7\), and constructs Boolean algebras of definable sets directly from their powerset semantics for sizes \(2\) through \(4\).

The program was executed and returned:

`VERIFY_OK 68 grid_sizes  2482 graded_levels  12 independent_pair_games  3 direct_boolean_semantics`

All finite checks passed. They support the exact formulas but do not prove the unrestricted all-\(n\) statement; that is established by the induction above. For example, at \(n=5\) the graded type counts by depth are \(2,7,12,19,25\), whereas every ordinary depth has exactly two types.

## Relationship to prior work

Ahvonen, Heiman and Kuusisto (2024) formally develop graded multimodal types and the equivalence with multiset-counting message-passing refinement. Their general semantic machinery supports the recursion used here, but their stated theorems do not specialize to the marked two-agent S5 grid or calculate its exact depth counts and extremal graded-to-ordinary ratio. Comer (2024) develops graded modal equivalence through homomorphism-count profiles, another general representation not itself an enumeration of this grid family. Otto (2019) establishes the general counting-bisimulation perspective. Prior quantitative work on ordinary row/column grid types and on graded/ordinary separation in outward-directed regular trees concerns different valuations/frames and gives neither the path construction nor the present depth census.

The proposed contribution is the simultaneous explicit sparse witness, exact \((t+1)^2+3\) depth spectrum, sharp full-distinction threshold, and attainment of the maximal possible \(n^2/2\) ratio. General graded-bisimulation theory, modal expressiveness facts and generic color refinement are **not** claimed as new.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to the particular two-agent finite S5 grid and to unrestricted positive integer counting thresholds. It does not claim an optimal number of marked cells, a classification of all maximizing valuations, or a new modal expressiveness theorem for arbitrary frames. An implicit presentation of this exact path construction in earlier graph-refinement or modal literature cannot be fully excluded. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
