# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Let \(d=\operatorname{gon}(\Gamma)\) and choose a degree-\(d\) positive-rank divisor on the metric banana path. Corollary 5.3 of Mascuch--Teixidor i Bigas gives a smooth proper curve \(X\) with skeleton \(\Gamma\), with \(g(X)=g(\Gamma)\), and with a degree-\(d\) positive-rank divisor. Hence \(\operatorname{gon}(X)\le d\). Conversely, every positive-rank divisor on \(X\) specializes to a positive-rank divisor of the same degree on \(\Gamma\), so its degree is at least \(d\). Therefore \(\operatorname{gon}(X)=d\). The source's Theorem 1.1 identifies the same \(d\) with geometric gonality.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating source states genus-preserving lifting of positive-rank divisors on banana paths, but does not state exact equality between the gonality of the chosen algebraic lift and the gonality of the skeleton. Exact, alias, source, and broader-coverage searches found no such universal same-genus statement. The closest older general theorem allows increasing vertex genera; the same literature also shows that gonality-preserving lifting can fail in augmented settings.

## Value
PASS. The equality rules out a genuine possible loss of information: lifting a minimal tropical pencil only proves an algebraic upper bound until specialization is used to exclude a smaller algebraic pencil. Simultaneous preservation of genus and gonality is a structural property of the banana-path class and makes the prescribed-gonality families in the initiating paper immediately realizable by same-genus smooth curves of exactly that gonality.

The theorem is existential. It does not say that every curve with the same skeleton has the same gonality, nor does it preserve the full gonality sequence.
