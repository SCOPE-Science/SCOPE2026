# Minimum spacing flips covariance at the power-one boundary
## Finding
Let
\[
(D_1,\ldots,D_m)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad m\ge2,
\]
be the ordinary uniform-spacing vector. Define the minimum spacing
\[
M_m=\min_{1\le i\le m}D_i
\]
and, for \(a>0\), the power-spacing statistic
\[
P_{m,a}=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i^a.
\]
Then the covariance changes sign exactly at the linear-power boundary:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,P_{m,a})
\begin{cases}
>0,&0<a<1,\\
=0,&a=1,\\
<0,&a>1.
\end{cases}
}
\]

For every integer \(q\ge1\), there is also an exact finite formula:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,P_{m,q})
=
\frac{q!(m-1)!}{(m+q)!}
\left(
\sum_{r=0}^q m^{-r}-1-\frac qm
\right).
}
\]
Equivalently, for \(q\ge2\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,P_{m,q})
=
\frac{q!(m-1)!}{(m+q)!}
\left(
\sum_{r=2}^q m^{-r}-\frac{q-1}{m}
\right),
\]
which makes strict negativity immediate.

For Greenwood's statistic
\[
G_m=P_{m,2}=\sum_{i=1}^mD_i^2,
\]
the covariance and correlation reduce to
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,G_m)
=-\frac{2(m-1)}{m^3(m+1)(m+2)}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Corr}(M_m,G_m)
=-\sqrt{\frac{(m+1)(m+3)}{m^2(m+2)}}.
}
\]
Hence
\[
\sqrt m\,\operatorname{Corr}(M_m,G_m)\longrightarrow-1.
\]

The dependence is visible already at the threshold-conditioned level. For
\[
0\le t<\frac1m,
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(M_m>t)=(1-mt)^{m-1}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[G_m\mid M_m>t]
=
\frac1m+
\frac{m-1}{m(m+1)}(1-mt)^2.
}
\]
Thus requiring every spacing to exceed \(t\) moves the expected Greenwood statistic quadratically from its null mean \(2/(m+1)\) toward its deterministic minimum \(1/m\).

More generally, for each fixed integer \(q\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt m\,\operatorname{Corr}(M_m,P_{m,q})
\longrightarrow
-\frac{q!(q-1)}
{\sqrt{(2q)!-(q!)^2(q^2+1)}}.
}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The spacing vector is exactly uniform on the \((m-1)\)-simplex, equivalently Dirichlet with all parameters equal to one. This is the null spacing law generated by a uniform sample after including the two boundary spacings.

The sign theorem applies to every real power \(a>0\). The closed covariance formula is asserted for integer powers \(q\ge1\). The Greenwood specialization corresponds to \(q=2\).

No independence statement is made. In particular, the vanishing covariance at \(a=1\) is trivial because \(P_{m,1}=1\) deterministically.

## Proof
Let
\[
e=\left(\frac1m,\ldots,\frac1m\right).
\]
For \(0\le t<1/m\), the event \(M_m>t\) is the translated simplex
\[
D_i>t,
\qquad
\sum_iD_i=1.
\]
By the affine change of variables
\[
D=t\mathbf 1+(1-mt)X,
\]
where
\[
X\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1),
\]
the conditional distribution of \(D\) given \(M_m>t\) is exactly that affine image of the original simplex. The volume ratio gives
\[
\mathbb P(M_m>t)=(1-mt)^{m-1}.
\]

Write
\[
u=1-mt.
\]
Then
\[
D=(1-u)e+uX.
\]
For fixed nonuniform \(X\), as \(u\) decreases from one to zero the vector \((1-u)e+uX\) becomes more equal in the majorization order. Therefore the strictly Schur-convex function
\[
\Phi_a(d)=\sum_i d_i^a
\]
strictly decreases with \(t\) when \(a>1\), while the strictly Schur-concave function \(\Phi_a\) strictly increases when \(0<a<1\). Since a Dirichlet vector is nonuniform almost surely,
\[
\mu_a(t):=\mathbb E[P_{m,a}\mid M_m>t]
\]
is strictly decreasing for \(a>1\) and strictly increasing for \(0<a<1\).

For any integrable \(Y\) and nonnegative \(M_m\), Fubini's theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,Y)
=
\int_0^{1/m}
\mathbb P(M_m>t)
\left(
\mathbb E[Y\mid M_m>t]-\mathbb EY
\right)
\,dt.
\]
Taking \(Y=P_{m,a}\), and noting that \(\mu_a(0)=\mathbb EP_{m,a}\), proves the sign theorem.

For the exact integer formula, let \(q\ge1\). Since the first coordinate of \(X\) has the beta law
\[
X_1\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,m-1),
\]
its moments are
\[
\mathbb EX_1^k
=
\frac{k!(m-1)!}{(m+k-1)!}.
\]
Using exchangeability and expanding the integer power,
\[
\mathbb E[P_{m,q}\mid M_m>t]
=
m\sum_{k=0}^q
\binom qk
 t^{q-k}(1-mt)^k
\frac{k!(m-1)!}{(m+k-1)!}.
\]
Integrating this against the survival probability yields
\[
\mathbb E[M_mP_{m,q}]
=
\frac{q!(m-1)!}{(m+q)!}
\sum_{r=0}^q m^{-r}.
\]
Also
\[
\mathbb EM_m=\frac1{m^2}
\]
and the Dirichlet moment formula gives
\[
\mathbb EP_{m,q}
=
\frac{m!(q!)}{(m+q-1)!}.
\]
Subtracting the product of means proves the exact covariance formula.

For \(q=2\), direct simplification gives the stated Greenwood covariance. The minimum-spacing variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}(M_m)
=
\frac{m-1}{m^4(m+1)},
\]
while the standard Dirichlet fourth-moment calculation gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(G_m)
=
\frac{4(m-1)}{(m+1)^2(m+2)(m+3)}.
\]
Combining these three formulas gives the exact correlation.

The conditional Greenwood mean follows by expanding
\[
\sum_i\left(t+(1-mt)X_i\right)^2
\]
and using
\[
\sum_iX_i=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_iX_i^2=\frac2{m+1}.
\]

Finally, for fixed integer \(q\ge2\), the exact variance
\[
\operatorname{Var}(P_{m,q})
=
\frac{m(m-1)!(2q)!+m(m-1)(m-1)!(q!)^2}{(m+2q-1)!}
-
\left(
\frac{m!(q!)}{(m+q-1)!}
\right)^2
\]
has asymptotic form
\[
\operatorname{Var}(P_{m,q})
\sim
\left((2q)!-(q!)^2(q^2+1)\right)m^{1-2q}.
\]
Together with
\[
\operatorname{Var}(M_m)\sim m^{-4}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M_m,P_{m,q})
\sim
-q!(q-1)m^{-q-2},
\]
this proves the limiting correlation constant.

## Verification
The accompanying `checker.py` uses exact rational arithmetic for the integer-power formulas. It checks the covariance, variance, sign, Greenwood specialization, and threshold-conditioned Greenwood identity for broad finite ranges.

As an independent low-dimensional replay, it integrates \(M_mP_{m,q}\) directly over the simplex for \(m=2\) and \(m=3\), partitioning by the identity of the minimum coordinate, and compares those integrals with the all-\(m\) closed form.

The universal real-power sign theorem is established analytically by the affine conditional law and majorization; the finite checks are supplementary.

## Relationship to prior work
Minimum spacing is a classical extreme-spacing statistic. Lao and Mayer's treatment of extreme-value analogues of U-statistics includes the smallest spherical distance and notes that in dimension two it is exactly the minimal spacing between circular order statistics. Their result is asymptotic and does not compare minimum spacing with Greenwood or other power-spacing sums.

Greenwood's statistic is a classical spacing goodness-of-fit statistic. Mirakhmedov's complete treatment studies its asymptotic efficiencies and its role relative to additive spacing tests. The inspected text contains no minimum-spacing statistic and no finite mixed moment with an extreme spacing.

The present result links these two classical directions at finite sample size. It shows that the minimum spacing has opposite covariance signs with concave and convex power-spacing functionals, gives a closed formula for every integer power, and identifies the exact threshold-conditioned Greenwood regression.

## Limitations
The affine conditioning argument depends on the uniform-simplex law. It does not transfer verbatim to nonuniform Dirichlet parameters or to spacings under fixed nonuniform alternatives before probability-integral transformation.

The exact integer-power formula does not by itself provide a closed elementary covariance for arbitrary noninteger \(a\), although the sign theorem covers every real \(a>0\).

Older spacings or Dirichlet order-statistic literature may contain equivalent mixed moments under different notation; targeted searches did not locate one.
