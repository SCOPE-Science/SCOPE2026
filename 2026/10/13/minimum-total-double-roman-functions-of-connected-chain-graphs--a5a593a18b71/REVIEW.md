# Review of Minimum total double Roman functions of connected chain graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The universal opposite-side pair gives weight \(6\). If both sides contain a zero, each side must contribute at least weight \(3\), and equality forces a single label \(3\) on each side; totality and zero defense then force both vertices to be universal. If only one side contains zeros, the opposite side is entirely positive, and the label-
\(1\) support rule plus zero defense force total weight at least \(7\). If no zeros occur, weight \(6\) would force all labels to be \(1\), which violates double Roman support. Exhaustive testing of \(1{,}662{,}976\) quaternary labelings on \(46\) canonical chain types agrees exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The accessible 2019 primary full text establishes general total-double-Roman bounds and selected graph classes, including \(\gamma_{tdR}(G)\le3\gamma_t(G)\), but contains no chain-graph or half-graph theorem. Semantic database searches and exact literature searches under chain, half, Ferrers, and universal-pair formulations did not locate the accepted equality classification. Nearby stored results concern ordinary Roman, total Roman, quasi-total Roman, or other double-Roman variants and do not imply the present minimum-function theorem.

## Value
PASS. Connected chain graphs are a canonical bipartite class with nested neighborhoods. The general theory supplies only the weight-
\(6\) upper bound from total domination; the theorem identifies exactly when that bound is attained and classifies every optimum by the universal twin classes. The exact multiplicity \(|U_X||U_Y|\) and unique half-graph corollary are natural structural data beyond a scalar calculation.
