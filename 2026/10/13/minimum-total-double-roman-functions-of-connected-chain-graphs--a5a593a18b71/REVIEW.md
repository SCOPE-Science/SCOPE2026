# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Connectivity and nested neighborhoods give a vertex universal to the opposite shore on each side, so labeling such a pair 3 is feasible. In any total double Roman function the positive support is nonempty and meets both sides. If both sides have a zero, defending it requires at least 3 weight from the opposite side; total weight at most 6 means exactly one 3 per side. Totality makes them adjacent, and the zero conditions give adjacency to every other vertex on their opposite sides. If just one side has zeros, the all-positive side needs at least 5 weight: defending the zero adds at least 2 above the all-positive baseline, whether using one 3 or two 2s. Thus the other side cannot contribute at least 2 at weight at most 6. But if it contributes only 1, every vertex on the all-positive side needs label at least 2, giving total weight at least 7. With neither side containing a zero, weight at most 6 would make all vertices 1, impossible. Therefore the product count follows, as does uniqueness on the half graph. The argument actually needs only both side sizes at least 3 and nonempty opposite-universal classes, rather than the chain ordering itself.
