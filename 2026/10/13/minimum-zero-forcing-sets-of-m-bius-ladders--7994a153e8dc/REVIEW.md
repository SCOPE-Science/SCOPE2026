# Correctness
For \(n\ge4\), any first force from a minimum four-set in the cubic Möbius ladder leaves exactly the closed neighborhood of the forcing vertex plus one extra blue vertex. Normalizing the forcing vertex to \(0\), a direct neighborhood calculation shows that a second force is possible only when the extra vertex lies in \(\{-2,2,n-1,n+1\}\). In each allowed case, reflection reduces to two configurations; one immediately has two consecutive full antipodal rungs and the other creates the second full rung with one force. Consecutive full rungs then propagate around the whole ladder. The twelve rooted local patterns collapse to five disjoint dihedral orbits with sizes \(2n,4n,4n,2n,n\), yielding \(13n\). The boundary case \(M_6\cong K_{3,3}\) has exactly \(9\) minimum sets. An independent exhaustive checker agrees for every \(3\le n\le12\).

# Originality
The foundational source proves only the scalar value \(Z(M_{2n})=4\). The 2020 circulant paper again treats the scalar number and torus-product extensions, not an enumeration of minimum bases. The zero forcing polynomial paper supplies the counting framework but contains no Möbius-ladder, Mobius-ladder, ladder, or circulant occurrence in the inspected full text. Searches under Möbius-ladder and circulant aliases, including \(C_{2n}(1,n)\), did not locate the five-orbit classification or the coefficient \(z(M_{2n};4)=13n\). The closest own prior finding classifies minimum zero forcing sets for circular ladders, a distinct graph family; its statement does not imply the Möbius-twisted classification.

# Value
The Möbius ladder is a canonical cubic circulant whose zero forcing number has been known since the foundational literature, but the scalar minimum does not describe the space of optimal forcing configurations. The exact five-type classification supplies the leading coefficient of the zero forcing polynomial and records how the twist changes the minimum-basis geometry. This is a natural complete classification of optimal sets on a standard family rather than an arbitrary finite slice.

The main residual risk is bibliographic: an unindexed thesis or computation could contain an equivalent minimum-set census. The proof itself does not depend on absence of such a source.
