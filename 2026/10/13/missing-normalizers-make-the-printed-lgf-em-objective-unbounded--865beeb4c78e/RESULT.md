# Missing normalizers make the printed LGF EM objective unbounded in frailty shape
## Finding
Consider the logarithmic–gamma frailty model with
\[
\theta,\lambda>0,
\qquad
0<\eta<1.
\]
For one observation, the hierarchy is
\[
P(N=n)=\frac{\eta^n}{-n\log(1-\eta)},
\]
\[
f_Z(z)=\frac{\lambda^\lambda}{\Gamma(\lambda)}z^{\lambda-1}e^{-\lambda z},
\]
and
\[
f_{Y\mid N,Z}(y\mid n,z)=n\theta z e^{-n\theta zy}.
\]
Hence the exact complete-data contribution is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\eta^n}{-n\log(1-\eta)}
\frac{\lambda^\lambda}{\Gamma(\lambda)}z^{\lambda-1}e^{-\lambda z}
(n\theta z)e^{-n\theta zy}.
}
\]
After the two factors of \(n\) cancel, its log is, up to terms independent of all model parameters,
\[
\boxed{
n\log\eta
-\log[-\log(1-\eta)]
+\log\theta
+\lambda\log\lambda
-\log\Gamma(\lambda)
+\lambda\log z
-\lambda z
-n\theta zy.
}
\]

The published complete-data formulas omit both parameter-dependent normalizers
\[
-\log[-\log(1-\eta)]
\quad\text{and}\quad
-\log\Gamma(\lambda).
\]
Equation (4.5) additionally changes the gamma power from
\[
\lambda\log\lambda
\]
to
\[
(\lambda+1)\log\lambda.
\]
These terms cannot be absorbed into an additive constant when \(\eta\) and \(\lambda\) are optimization variables.

Let the E-step be frozen and define
\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^m E(N_i\mid y),
\qquad
B=\sum_{i=1}^m E(\log Z_i\mid y),
\]
\[
C=\sum_{i=1}^m E(Z_i\mid y),
\qquad
D=\sum_{i=1}^m y_iE(N_iZ_i\mid y).
\]
The corrected EM auxiliary function is
\[
\boxed{
Q(\theta,\lambda,\eta)
=
A\log\eta
-m\log[-\log(1-\eta)]
+m\log\theta
+m\{\lambda\log\lambda-\log\Gamma(\lambda)\}
+\lambda(B-C)
-\theta D
+K,
}
\]
where \(K\) is independent of the new parameters.

By contrast, Eq. (4.5), interpreted literally, gives
\[
Q_{\mathrm{print}}(\theta,\lambda,\eta)
=
A\log\eta
+m\log\theta
+m(\lambda+1)\log\lambda
+\lambda(B-C)
-\theta D
+K'.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\mathrm{print}}(\theta,\lambda,\eta)\to+\infty
\qquad(\lambda\to\infty).
}
\]
Even the preceding Eq. (4.4), which has \(m\lambda\log\lambda\) rather than \(m(\lambda+1)\log\lambda\), is unbounded for the same reason.

Thus the joint three-parameter M-step has no finite frailty-shape maximizer as printed. A finite box constraint merely makes the selected value depend on the imposed upper box boundary.

The corrected frailty-shape score is
\[
\boxed{
m\{\log\lambda+1-\psi(\lambda)\}+B-C=0,
}
\]
where \(\psi\) is the digamma function.

The corrected logarithmic-mixing score is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{A}{\eta}
-
\frac{m}{(1-\eta)[-\log(1-\eta)]}=0,
}
\]
which is exactly the term structure appearing in the source's Eq. (4.9). That equation is therefore internally incompatible with Eq. (4.5): Eq. (4.9) differentiates a normalizing term that Eq. (4.5) does not contain.

The corrected frailty-shape objective is coercive. As \(\lambda\downarrow0\),
\[
-\log\Gamma(\lambda)\sim\log\lambda\to-\infty.
\]
As \(\lambda\to\infty\), Stirling's formula gives
\[
\lambda\log\lambda-\log\Gamma(\lambda)
=
\lambda+\frac12\log\lambda+O(1).
\]
Moreover, for every positive random variable,
\[
\log z-z+1\le0,
\]
with strict inequality away from \(z=1\). The conditional frailty distributions in the E-step are nondegenerate, so
\[
m+B-C
=
\sum_{i=1}^m E\{1+\log Z_i-Z_i\mid y\}<0.
\]
Hence the corrected \(Q\) tends to \(-\infty\) also as \(\lambda\to\infty\), and it has at least one finite maximizer.

## Assumptions and scope
The result uses the model's stated parameter domain
\[
\theta,\lambda>0,
\qquad
0<\eta<1,
\]
and the hierarchy printed in Section 2.

The claim concerns the joint three-parameter EM formulation in Section 4. It does not challenge the observed-data likelihood in Eq. (4.1).

The simulation study fixes \(\lambda\) and explicitly states that a corrected complete-data log-likelihood was implemented. The real-data analysis uses direct maximization of the observed-data likelihood rather than EM. Therefore no claim is made that the published simulation table or real-data estimates were generated by the unbounded printed joint-\(\lambda\) objective.

## Proof
Multiplying the logarithmic PMF, gamma-frailty density, and conditional exponential-minimum density yields the exact complete-data density displayed above. The factors \(n^{-1}\) and \(n\) cancel, but the factors
\[
[-\log(1-\eta)]^{-1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Gamma(\lambda)^{-1}
\]
do not: both depend on parameters being optimized.

Taking conditional expectation with the E-step distribution frozen gives the corrected auxiliary function. Differentiation in \(\eta\) and \(\lambda\) gives the two corrected score equations.

For the printed Eq. (4.5), all terms other than
\[
m(\lambda+1)\log\lambda
\]
are at most linear in \(\lambda\) when the E-step is frozen. Thus its positive \(m\lambda\log\lambda\) term dominates and forces divergence to \(+\infty\).

For the corrected objective, Stirling's formula cancels the \(\lambda\log\lambda\) growth through \(-\log\Gamma(\lambda)\). The remaining linear coefficient is
\[
m+B-C<0
\]
by \(1+\log z-z\le0\) and nondegeneracy. This proves the right-endpoint decay. The gamma pole proves left-endpoint decay.

## Verification
The accompanying checker uses the positive sample
\[
(0.18,0.42,0.95,1.7,3.2)
\]
and freezes the E-step at
\[
(\theta,\lambda,\eta)=(1.1,2.3,0.45).
\]
Posterior sums over the latent count are evaluated on a sufficiently long geometric tail and the conditional gamma moments are used exactly.

The checker verifies numerically that the corrected \(\eta\)- and \(\lambda\)-score formulas agree with centered finite differences of the corrected auxiliary function.

For this frozen E-step, the corrected frailty-shape objective has a finite maximizer at approximately
\[
\lambda=2.49636001.
\]
The printed Eq. (4.5) instead places the numerical maximizer at the upper endpoint when optimized over each of the boxes ending at \(5\), \(10\), \(30\), and \(100\), matching the analytic unboundedness result.

## Relationship to prior work
The primary article's Eq. (4.2) starts from the correct factorized complete-data likelihood, but Eqs. (4.3)–(4.5) omit the logarithmic and gamma normalizers. Equation (4.9) then restores the derivative of the missing logarithmic normalizer. Section 5 further says that the simulation used a corrected complete-data log-likelihood, but it fixes \(\lambda\), so it does not supply or test a corrected joint frailty-shape M-step.

A closely related 2025 gamma-frailty model by the same author also prints a complete-data log-likelihood without the term \(-\log\Gamma(\lambda)\), but its subsequent \(\lambda\)-score explicitly contains \(-\psi(\lambda)\), which is the derivative that the omitted normalizer requires. That earlier paper therefore supports the corrected score convention but does not state the present model's missing logarithmic normalizer, its extra \(+\log\lambda\), or the unboundedness of Eq. (4.5).

Focused searches using the target DOI, model name, complete-data likelihood, gamma normalizer, digamma score, and unbounded-auxiliary terminology did not locate a published correction that states the present claim.

## Limitations
The result diagnoses the formulas as printed. It does not infer the contents of unavailable numerical code.

The corrected auxiliary function is proved to have at least one finite frailty-shape maximizer, not necessarily a unique one for every frozen E-step.

The source's genuine weak-identifiability problem for \(\eta\) can coexist with this algebraic EM defect; the result does not claim that all numerical instability in the model is caused by the missing normalizers.

A recent or unindexed correction could exist outside the sources examined.
