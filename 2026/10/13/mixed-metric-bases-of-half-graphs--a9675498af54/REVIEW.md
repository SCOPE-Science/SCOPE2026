# Review of Mixed metric bases of half graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The vertex–incident-edge comparisons force \(a_n\) and \(b_1\). For every internal prefix, the elements \(a_1,a_1b_2,\ldots,a_1b_i\) can be separated only by threshold coordinates from selected \(A\)-vertices and singleton coordinates from selected \(B\)-vertices in that prefix, forcing at least \(i-1\) such landmarks. A symmetric suffix argument gives the complementary inequalities. Together they prove the lower bound \(n\) and force exactly one choice from each internal diagonal pair at equality. The explicit distance comparisons prove every such transversal resolves all vertex–vertex, edge–edge, and vertex–edge pairs. The independent checker exhausts all subsets of size at most \(n\) for \(2\le n\le8\) and matches the theorem exactly.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational mixed-metric paper was inspected in full searchable text: its exact-family section treats cycles, complete bipartite graphs, trees, and grids, and targeted searches found no half, chain, or Ferrers terminology. The closest object-specific paper located is a 2021 chain-graph paper whose accessible abstract concerns ordinary metric dimension and restricted threshold dimension rather than mixed metric dimension. Semantic and exact searches under half graph, chain graph, bipartite chain graph, Ferrers graph, and difference graph found no same-invariant theorem. Related ordinary, strong, and edge-metric half/chain results do not imply the additional vertex–edge separation proved here. The unavailable full text of the 2021 chain-graph article is recorded as a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. Half graphs are the canonical strictly nested chain graphs. The theorem gives both the exact mixed metric dimension and every basis, with a simple \(2^{n-2}\) count. The forced endpoints and diagonal-transversal law show how simultaneous vertex-and-edge resolution changes the structure relative to ordinary or edge-only resolvability. This is a natural benchmark family rather than an arbitrary finite slice.
