# Modal operation spectra reconstruct finite full successor trees

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(b,h\) be integers with \(b\geq1\), \(h\geq1\). Let \(T_{b,h}\) be the rooted directed tree whose root-to-leaf height is \(h\), every non-leaf has exactly \(b\) distinct children, and whose only edges are the downward immediate-child edges. Equip the Boolean algebra \(\mathcal A_{b,h}=\mathcal P(T_{b,h})\) with the ordinary existential successor operation
\[
\Diamond U=\{v:\text{a child of }v\text{ belongs to }U\}.
\]
Write \(\mathrm{Clo}_r(\mathcal A_{b,h})\) for the set of distinct \(r\)-ary term operations generated by Boolean constants and operations, the variables, and \(\Diamond\), where \(r\geq0\). Put \(q=2^r\) and define
\[
 t_0(q)=q,\qquad
 t_{j+1}(q)=q\sum_{k=1}^{b}\binom{t_j(q)}{k}
 \quad(0\leq j<h),\qquad
 E_{b,h}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{h}t_j(q).
\]
As usual, \(\binom{m}{k}=0\) for integers \(k>m\geq0\). Then
\[
\boxed{\bigl|\mathrm{Clo}_r(\mathcal A_{b,h})\bigr|=2^{E_{b,h}(2^r)}.}
\]
The expression \(E_{b,h}(q)\) is a polynomial of degree
\[
 n=1+b+\cdots+b^h=|T_{b,h}|,
\]
with leading coefficient
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{lc}(E_{b,h})=(b!)^{-(n-1)/b}.}
\]
Consequently, if two full successor trees \(T_{b,h}\) and \(T_{b',h'}\), both of positive height, have the same numbers of Boolean modal term operations **in every arity**, then \(b=b'\) and \(h=h'\). Thus the full arity-wise operation spectrum determines the frame in this natural family. More precisely,
\[
\log_2\bigl|\mathrm{Clo}_r(\mathcal A_{b,h})\bigr|
\sim (b!)^{-(n-1)/b}\,2^{nr}\qquad(r\to\infty).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The frame is *irreflexive* and uses only immediate-child accessibility, not the ancestor order, reflexive closure, backwards parent edges, graded modalities, or fixed-point operators. The algebra has underlying set \(\mathcal P(T_{b,h})\). Inputs are arbitrary subsets of vertices and terms can use \(\varnothing\), the entire frame, union, intersection, complement, and \(\Diamond\). An operation is identified by its action on **all** \(r\)-tuples of subsets of the same fixed frame. For \(r=0\) the single propositional color is the empty truth vector. This statement concerns precisely the homogeneous complete successor trees with \(b,h\ge1\); it makes no classification claim for arbitrary directed trees or extra modal operators.

A valuation of the \(r\) input variables gives each vertex one of the \(q=2^r\) colors. A *pointed modal color type* is the equivalence class of a colored vertex under satisfaction of every Boolean modal formula over these \(r\) variables, evaluated over all such valuations and all vertices of the frame.

## Proof

**1. Finite pointed types.** A vertex with exactly \(j\) edges on every longest downward path to a leaf has height \(j\). For height zero, a pointed type consists solely of its color, giving \(t_0(q)=q\). For \(j\ge1\), the data visible to ordinary successor modality are its own color and the **nonempty set** \(S\) of distinct types realized by its \(b\) children; their multiplicities are not visible to existential or universal modalities. By induction on \(j\), each of the \(t_{j-1}(q)\) child types is realizable in a height-\(j-1\) complete subtree. Since the child subtrees are disjoint, any set \(S\) of size between one and \(b\) can be realized simultaneously by choosing one representing subtree coloring for each type and repeating some type if necessary. Distinct \(S\) (or distinct root colors) are separated by an existential subformula using a child-type characteristic formula. This establishes exactly
\[
 t_j(q)=q\sum_{k=1}^{b}\binom{t_{j-1}(q)}{k}.
\]

**2. Explicit formula definability and disjoint heights.** Define the leaf-height test
\[
 H_j=\Diamond^j\top\ \wedge\ \neg\Diamond^{j+1}\top,
\]
with \(\Diamond^0\top=\top\). On this complete finite tree it holds precisely at vertices of height \(j\), so types of different heights are separated. For a color \(c\), let \(P_c\) be the conjunction of the \(r\) literals specifying its complete truth vector (the empty conjunction is \(\top\) when \(r=0\)). Starting from \(\chi_{0,c}=H_0\wedge P_c\), define recursively, for each height \(j\) type \(\tau=(c,S)\),
\[
\chi_{j,c,S}=H_j\wedge P_c\wedge
\bigwedge_{\sigma\in S}\Diamond\chi_{j-1,\sigma}
\wedge\Box\bigvee_{\sigma\in S}\chi_{j-1,\sigma},
\qquad \Box\varphi:=\neg\Diamond\neg\varphi.
\]
Every \(S\) is nonempty. The formula expresses *exactly* the root color and realized child-type set, so it isolates \(\tau\), uniformly across all valuations and vertices of this fixed frame. Hence the \(E_{b,h}(q)\) types counted over all heights are mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive, and individually definable.

**3. Count all operations, not only formulas.** Every term defines a Boolean-valued function of the pointed type: this holds by induction on Boolean connectives and \(\Diamond\), because successors are determined up to type and existential modality is insensitive to multiplicities. Conversely, any subset of the finite set of \(E_{b,h}(q)\) types is defined by the disjunction of its characteristic formulas, including the empty subset. Every type is realized somewhere in the frame under some input valuation, so distinct subsets define distinct \(r\)-ary operations. Thus the number of operations is exactly \(2^{E_{b,h}(q)}\).

**4. Degree, leading coefficient, and inverse theorem.** For positive integer \(b\), the summand \(\binom{t}{b}\) has degree \(b\) in \(t\) and leading coefficient \(1/b!\), and all other summands have smaller degree. Since \(t_0=q\) has degree one with leading coefficient one, induction shows
\[
\deg t_j=1+b+\cdots+b^j,
\qquad
\operatorname{lc}(t_j)=(b!)^{-(1+b+\cdots+b^{j-1})}.
\]
This applies when \(b=1\), interpreting the displayed sums normally (the empty sum for \(j=0\) is zero). The degree of \(t_j\) strictly increases with \(j\), so \(E_{b,h}\) has the degree and coefficient claimed.

If two trees have equal arity spectra, taking base-two logarithms gives equality of the corresponding \(E\)-polynomials at every \(q=2^r\), an infinite set. The polynomials therefore coincide, which first recovers their common degree \(n\) and then gives equality of their leading coefficients. For \(n>1\), the function \((b!)^{1/b}\) is **strictly increasing** on positive integers \(b\), because adjoining the new factor \(b+1\), larger than the geometric mean of \(1,\ldots,b\), strictly increases that geometric mean. Therefore the coefficient \((b!)^{-(n-1)/b}\) uniquely determines \(b\), including the case \(b=1\) where it equals one. Once \(b\) and \(n\) are known, the strictly increasing formula \(n=\sum_{i=0}^h b^i\) uniquely determines \(h\). The asymptotic is the leading term of the polynomial evaluated at \(2^r\). This proves all assertions.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python program `verify.py` independently enumerates every coloring of sufficiently small frames, canonically computes the pointed modal types, compares the observed type counts with the stated recurrence, and directly generates the unary Boolean-modal operation clone for three small examples. It also checks the symbolic leading-coefficient formula for additional parameters. Execute with `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing this result and the script. Recorded output:

```
VERIFY_OK type_censuses 51 direct_closures 3 equal_order_reconstruction 31 leading_binary 1/32768 leading_fiveary 1/2985984000000
```

A concrete separation at equal frame order is \(T_{2,4}\) versus \(T_{5,2}\), each with \(31\) vertices. For \(r=1\), the respective exponents \(E_{b,h}(2)\) are \(3{,}265{,}298\) and \(132\). Their leading coefficients are respectively \(1/32768\) and \(1/2985984000000\). The direct computations test finite examples only; the general theorem is proved by the preceding induction and polynomial argument.

## Relationship to prior work

The finite-state Hennessy--Milner equivalence of modal indistinguishability and bisimulation, and the existence of modal formulas characterizing finite behavioral classes, are foundational; they are not claimed as new. Wißmann, Milius and Schröder give generic procedures for constructing such distinguishing formulas. Ten Cate and Koudijs investigate finite characterization and local tabularity of modal languages. Colcombet, Doumane and Kuperberg study expressive power in a much broader tree-algebra setting. These accounts do not state the complete full-successor-tree **arity-wise term-operation census** above or its reconstruction of the branching factor and height from polynomial degree and leading coefficient. The contribution asserted here is precisely this numerical clone spectrum and inverse classification on full trees, not a new bisimulation theorem.

## Limitations

No claim is made about arbitrary rooted trees, ancestor reachability, ordered-child modalities, weighted or graded branching, or the complexity of an optimal formula implementing each term function. The arity-wise inverse theorem assumes equality for every nonnegative integer arity; no bound on finitely many determining arities is provided. The formula does not count syntactically distinct formulas. Prior literature on finite modal algebras, bounded branching and tree-type enumeration may contain an equivalent earlier formulation; no claim of independent originality certification is made. Finite verifications are corroborating checks and do not replace the general proof.
