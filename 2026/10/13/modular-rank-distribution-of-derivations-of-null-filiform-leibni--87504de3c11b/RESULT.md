# Modular rank distribution of derivations of null-filiform Leibniz algebras

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq1\) and every finite field \(K=\mathbf F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), let \(N_n(K)\) be the right Leibniz algebra with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) and products \([e_i,e_1]=e_{i+1}\) for \(1\leq i<n\), all other basis products being zero. For a derivation \(D\), write \(\nu(D)=\dim_K\ker D\), and put \(m=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\). The **complete nullity enumerator** of its derivations is

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_{n,q}(T)&=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{Der}(N_n(K))}T^{\nu(D)}\\
&=T^n+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(q-1)q^{n-k-1}T^k\\
&\quad+\begin{cases}
(q-1)q^{n-1},&m=0,\\[2pt]
(q-1)^2\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}q^{n-k-1}T^k+(q-1)q^{n-m}T^m,&m\geq1.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}}
\]

Empty sums are zero. Thus the number of invertible derivations is \((q-1)q^{n-1}\) when \(n<p\), and zero when \(n\geq p\). In the latter range every derivation is singular, but all nullities from \(1\) through \(n\) occur. The result is valid for all prime powers \(q\), including characteristic two.

This enumeration has a constructive rank criterion. Write the unique parameter vector of a derivation as \((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\). If \(a_1=0\), its nullity is the smallest positive \(j\) with \(a_{j+1}\ne0\), or \(n\) if none exists. If \(a_1\ne0\) and \(n\geq p\), an explicit triangular gauge recursion defined in the proof produces \(b_1,\ldots,b_{m-1}\), and the nullity is the smallest \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) with \(b_j\ne0\), or \(m\) if none exists. The gauge coordinates are uniformly distributed when the remaining derivation parameters are uniformly distributed.

## Assumptions and scope

The multiplication is **right Leibniz**, with \([x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]-[[x,z],y]\). Rank and nullity refer to the underlying \(K\)-linear transformation \(D:N_n(K)\to N_n(K)\), not the adjoint action of the derivation Lie algebra and not the dimension of the derivation space. Distinct derivations are counted as distinct linear maps on the fixed labeled algebra, not modulo automorphisms. The characteristic is the prime \(p\), while \(q\) may be any power of \(p\). No assumption of algebraic closedness, odd characteristic, or lower bound on \(p\) is made. The presentation and proof also handle \(n=1\).

For convenience, define \(\operatorname{ord}_s b\) to be the least positive exponent with nonzero coefficient in \(b(s)\), with value \(+\infty\) when \(b=0\). For \(m=1\), the gauge polynomial is necessarily zero and the corresponding nullity equals one.

## Proof

**1. All derivations and their matrices.** The algebra is generated by \(e_1\), because \(e_{i+1}=[e_i,e_1]\). Write
\[
D(e_1)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j e_j.
\]
The derivation identity gives, inductively,
\[
D(e_i)=i a_1e_i+\sum_{j=2}^{n-i+1}a_j e_{i+j-1},\qquad1\leq i\leq n.
\]
Conversely, these formulas satisfy the derivation identity on every basis pair: for second input \(e_j\) with \(j>1\), both bracket terms vanish; for \(e_1\), the displayed induction is precisely the required identity (the terminal case gives zero). The parameter vector is unique, so \(|\operatorname{Der}(N_n(K))|=q^n\) in every characteristic.

Identify \(e_i\leftrightarrow t^{i-1}\) in \(R_n=K[t]/(t^n)\). Put \(\delta=t\,d/dt\) and \(h(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j+1}t^j\). The associated linear operator is
\[
D=a_1(1+\delta)+M_h,
\]
where \(M_h\) means multiplication by \(h\). This equality is an equality of linear maps; it does not identify the Leibniz multiplication with multiplication in \(R_n\).

**2. The branch \(a_1=0\).** Then \(D=M_h\). When \(h\) first has a nonzero coefficient in degree \(k\), where \(1\leq k<n\), its image is \(t^kR_n\) and its kernel has dimension \(k\). There are \((q-1)q^{n-k-1}\) such coefficient vectors. The zero polynomial contributes the single zero derivation, of nullity \(n\). This yields the first two terms of \(\mathcal C_{n,q}(T)\).

**3. Remove the incomplete characteristic block.** Assume \(a_1=a\ne0\), write \(n=pm+r\) with \(0\leq r<p\), and first suppose \(m=0\). The matrix of \(D\) is triangular with diagonal \(a,2a,\ldots,na\), all nonzero, so \(D\) is invertible.

For \(m\geq1\), the ideal \(I=t^{pm}R_n\) is \(D\)-stable. On \(I\), the matrix of \(D\) is triangular with diagonal \(a,2a,\ldots,ra\), after reduction modulo \(p\), and is therefore invertible. The induced map on \(R_n/I\cong K[t]/(t^{pm})\) has exactly the same kernel dimension: every kernel vector of the quotient has a unique correction in \(I\), obtained using invertibility of \(D|_I\). Hence all nullity calculations reduce to the case \(n=pm\). The \(r\) omitted high-degree coefficients of \(h\) remain free in the final count.

**4. A normalized gauge lemma in characteristic \(p\).** Work in \(R_{pm}=K[t]/(t^{pm})\) with \(a\ne0\). There exist unique polynomials
\[
u(t)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{pm-1}u_jt^j,\quad u_{pj}=0\ (1\leq j<m),\qquad
b(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}b_js^j,
\]
such that
\[
a t u'(t)=(b(t^p)-h(t))u(t)\pmod{t^{pm}}.\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, solve coefficient-by-coefficient in increasing degree \(j\). When \(p\nmid j\), the coefficient on the left is \(aj u_j\), a nonzero scalar multiple of the as-yet-undetermined \(u_j\), and no new \(b\)-coefficient appears. When \(p\mid j\), the coefficient on the left vanishes; choose \(u_j=0\) and determine the new \(b_{j/p}\), which occurs on the right with coefficient \(u_0=1\). Earlier choices are unchanged. This proves existence and uniqueness without dividing by \(p\).

Multiplication by the unit \(u\) conjugates \(D\) to
\[
M_u^{-1}DM_u=a(1+\delta)+M_{b(t^p)},
\]
by the product rule and (1). Let \(S=K[s]/(s^m)\). Then
\[
R_{pm}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}t^iS,\qquad \delta(t^if(s))=i t^if(s).
\]
On the summand \(t^iS\), the conjugated map is multiplication by \(a(i+1)+b(s)\). For \(i<p-1\), this is a unit of \(S\), while for \(i=p-1\) it is multiplication by \(b(s)\). Consequently,
\[
\dim_K\ker D=\min\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_s b,m\bigr).\tag{2}
\]
Since \(b(0)=0\), this nullity lies between \(1\) and \(m\).

**5. Uniformity of resonance coordinates.** Fix \(a\in K^\times\). The degree-\(pj\) equation in (1) has the form
\[
b_j=a_{pj+1}+F_j(a_2,\ldots,a_{pj};b_1,\ldots,b_{j-1}),\qquad1\leq j<m,
\]
where \(F_j\) is determined by the preceding gauge recursion. In particular, the new parameter \(a_{pj+1}\) occurs with coefficient one and does not occur in earlier equations. Thus, for each prescribed vector \((b_1,\ldots,b_{m-1})\), the number of choices of \((a_2,\ldots,a_{pm})\) is \(q^{pm-m}\). Including the \(r\) unused final parameters gives \(q^{n-m}\) choices. Equivalently, the resonance vector is uniform on \(K^{m-1}\) for each fixed nonzero \(a\).

For \(1\leq k<m\), exactly \((q-1)q^{m-1-k}\) resonance vectors have first nonzero coefficient at index \(k\). Multiplying by \((q-1)q^{n-m}\), to allow every \(a\ne0\) and its fibers, gives \((q-1)^2q^{n-k-1}\) derivations of nullity \(k\). The single zero resonance vector contributes \((q-1)q^{n-m}\) derivations of nullity \(m\). Combined with Steps 2 and 3, this proves the displayed enumerator and all consequences. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone `verify_derivation_nullfiliform.py` uses only the Python standard library. Run `python verify_derivation_nullfiliform.py`. It independently assembles each derivation matrix from its coefficients, computes ranks by finite-field Gaussian elimination, reconstructs the normalized gauge coefficients, checks the coefficient identity (1), and compares the resulting complete nullity histograms with the formula. Its finite fields are \(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_3,\mathbf F_4,\mathbf F_5\); \(\mathbf F_4\) is implemented directly as \(\mathbf F_2[\alpha]/(\alpha^2+\alpha+1)\). The exhaustive ranges are \(n\leq8,6,5,5\), respectively. The program prints `VERIFY_OK` only after every check succeeds. This finite enumeration corroborates the theorem but is not the proof for arbitrary \(n\) and \(q\); Steps 1–5 supply that proof.

For instance, in characteristic two, \(n=4\) and \(q=2\), the enumerator is \(8T+6T^2+T^3+T^4\). For the nonzero leading derivation parameter in this example, nullity two occurs precisely when \(a_1a_3=a_2^2\), illustrating the first characteristic-resonance equation.

## Relationship to prior work

The standard null-filiform multiplication and its grading/automorphism viewpoint appear in Calderón–Camacho–Kaygorodov–Omirov, first posted as arXiv:1708.01753v1 on August 5, 2017 (Sections 2 and 3). Adashev–Kurbanbaev, arXiv:2010.01904v2, Section 3.5, prove a result about almost-inner derivations of the same algebra; this is a different subspace-level question. Normurodovna (2026), Theorem 1, explicitly describes all derivation matrices over \(\mathbf C\). That known matrix form is acknowledged; it does not provide the positive-characteristic rank stratification or finite-field histogram. The new statement counts linear-map ranks, not gradings, automorphisms, derivation-space dimensions, or almost-inner subspaces. The modular gauge lemma explains why the triangular matrix form develops nontrivial resonance at the characteristic threshold.

## Limitations

The enumerator counts labeled derivations on a fixed algebra. It is not an orbit count under conjugation by automorphisms, a classification of derivations of other filiform Leibniz algebras, or a claim about distributions on the entire moduli of Leibniz algebras. The mathematics proves the assertion for every finite field and every \(n\geq1\); computational checks address only the explicitly stated finite cases. Earlier equivalent results under different terminology for Euler differential operators or positive-characteristic derivation strata remain a literature-overlap risk; neither a failed search nor finite computation can eliminate that risk.
