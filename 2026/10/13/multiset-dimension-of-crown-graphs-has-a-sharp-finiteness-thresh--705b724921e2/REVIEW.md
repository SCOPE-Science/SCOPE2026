# Review of Multiset dimension of crown graphs has a sharp finiteness threshold

## Correctness
PASS. For fixed landmark counts in the two bipartition classes, the multiset representation of \(a_i\) is determined exactly by the two membership indicators of the deleted matched pair \(a_i,b_i\). Hence all \(n\) matched-pair patterns must be distinct, forcing \(n\le4\). At \(n=4\), all four patterns occur and the unique \((0,0)\) pair gives identical representation \(\{\!\{1,1,2,2\}\!\}\) to its two vertices. At \(n=3\), the four possible three-pattern collections can be checked exactly: two fail by a matched-pair collision and the other two give six pairwise distinct vertex representations. Exhaustive direct-distance verification for \(3\le n\le8\) agrees with the proof.

## Originality
PASS. The 2017 foundational full text proves the cycle value and general finiteness obstructions but contains no crown or bipartite treatment. The 2024 full text proves equivalence with ID-colorings, NP-completeness, king-grid results, and a strong-product theorem; targeted full-text searches found no crown or complete-bipartite occurrence. Exact and semantic literature searches under crown, deleted-perfect-matching, \(K_{n,n}-M\), ID-coloring, and multiset-resolving formulations found no all-\(n\) crown theorem. A 2026 census may include isolated small crowns computationally, but its accessible abstract does not state the arbitrary-\(n\) threshold and its full text was unavailable during this comparison.

## Value
PASS. Finiteness is a central structural issue for ordinary multiset dimension because symmetric graphs can have no resolving set at all. Crown graphs are a standard connected regular bipartite family of diameter \(3\), so neither the foundational diameter-\(2\) obstruction nor its large-twin-class obstruction decides them. The theorem identifies an exact family-wide threshold, explains it through a four-state bottleneck, and completely classifies the finite boundary.
