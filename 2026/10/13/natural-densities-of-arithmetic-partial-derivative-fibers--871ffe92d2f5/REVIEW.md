# Scientific review: natural densities of arithmetic partial-derivative fibers

## Correctness — PASS
For each positive output \(x=p^a m\), the displayed inverse formula follows bijectively from writing the exponent of \(p\) in a preimage as \(k=d p^b\). Each divisor \(d\mid m\) in the finite set \(\mathcal D_{p,a}\) supplies one preimage; different \(b\) cannot produce the same \(d\), because \(b\mapsto d p^b+b\) is strictly increasing. Fixing \(a\) makes the fiber count periodic in \(m\) modulo \(L_{p,a}\), with \(p\nmid L_{p,a}\), so elementary residue counting supplies its exact local probability. Contributions from valuations \(a\ge A\) occupy at most the density \(p^{-A}\); this proves existence and the uniform truncation bound before any infinite summation. Inclusion--exclusion gives the exact image probability without any independence assumption. For \(a<p-1\), the sole divisor is \(a+1\), so the comparison with the logarithmic power series has rigorously bounded tail \(p^{1-p}\). Positivity for positive fiber sizes uses Emmons--Xiao, Theorem 21, together with the new periodic-residue argument; the zero fiber is witnessed by all integers of \(p\)-valuation \(p-1\). The symbolic proof covers all primes, outputs, and multiplicities.

The packaged Python verifier checked exact rational bounds for five primes; independent source-image membership for \(180{,}000\) tested output/prime pairs; inverse multiplicities for three primes, 30 valuations and up to 300 coprime co-factors; and exact periodic multiplicity counts. These are supporting finite tests, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS
The closest work is Emmons--Xiao, *The Arithmetic Partial Derivative* (arXiv:2201.12453; Journal of Integer Sequences 25 (2022), Article 22.4.7). Its Lemma 14 and Theorem 17 give finite anti-partial-derivative parameterizations; Theorem 21 gives infinitely many outputs with every positive finite inverse count. That is prior art and is acknowledged in the proof. Its relevant full-text sections do not give natural densities of the inverse-count fibers, an explicit density series for the positive image, a geometric tail bound, or the uniform large-prime asymptotic. In particular, infinite cardinality does not imply positive density. The 2023 Emmons--Xiao sequel proves related results for local fields and number fields; its discussion of the image of a *logarithmic derivative* on fields is not a density result for the image of the original integer-valued \(D_p\). The 2012, 2016 and 2017 related sources concern derivative equations and existence, not these distributional statements in the portions examined. Semantic published-record comparisons also concerned other arithmetic functions or the full arithmetic derivative rather than this fixed-prime range. All comparisons concern implications, not just matching titles. Searches cannot exclude an unindexed prior treatment, particularly a reformulation of the inverse criterion as a density law.

## Value — PASS
For an integer-valued arithmetic function, the natural density of its image is a basic invariant measuring how frequently its equation is solvable. The foundational anti-partial-derivative work emphasizes existence and exact counts but does not quantify frequency. Here the *entire* inverse-count distribution is shown to exist, with every atom positive, and each density is approximable to rigorous arbitrary precision by finite rational arithmetic. The image-density constant has a striking uniform analytic approximation whose error decays as \(p^{1-p}\), exposing the typical deficit \(1/(2p)\) as the prime grows. This is a natural distributional completion of the previous existence results and gives an explicit output-frequency invariant for future work. It is not a table extension or a parametrically arbitrary slice.

## Closest literature and remaining limitations
The essential literature comparison is with Emmons--Xiao 2022 (arXiv:2201.12453, Lemma 14, Theorems 17 and 21) and Emmons--Xiao 2023 (arXiv:2307.04912, partial-derivative extension and logarithmic derivative image). Broader background includes Kovič 2012, Haukkanen--Merikoski--Tossavainen 2016 and Pandey--Saxena 2017. The result is about positive integral preimages; it neither supplies elementary closed forms for the densities nor settles the image of the full arithmetic derivative. The positive-fiber existence input is the cited external theorem. An unindexed equivalent distribution theorem is the principal bibliographic risk.
