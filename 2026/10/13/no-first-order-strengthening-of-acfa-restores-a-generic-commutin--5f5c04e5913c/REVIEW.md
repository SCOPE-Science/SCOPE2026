# Review

## Correctness

PASS. ACFA is model-complete, and model-completeness is inherited by any stronger theory in the same language. For an arbitrary consistent \(R\supseteq\mathrm{ACFA}\), choose a completion \(R'\supseteq R\). The recent Theorem 1.2 says \((R')_\tau\) has no model companion because \(R'\) is a completion of ACFA. Corollary 6.2 says that if the model-complete theory \(R\) had \(R_\tau\) with a model companion, then every completion of \(R\), including \(R'\), would inherit one. The contradiction proves the claim with all quantifiers and language restrictions explicit.

## Originality

PASS. The recent paper states the obstruction for every completion of ACFA and separately states the general completion-transfer result, but the inspected text does not formulate the closure theorem for all consistent, possibly incomplete, same-language strengthenings of ACFA. Targeted searches for “every extension,” “first-order strengthening,” “model-complete extension,” and “refinement” formulations did not locate an equivalent published statement. Earlier several-automorphism and generic-automorphism literature gives different obstruction criteria or non-elementary frameworks and does not subsume this exact hereditary conclusion.

## Value

PASS. The source resolves the long-standing completion-by-completion obstruction. The present consequence shows that completeness is not a boundary at all: no additional first-order information in the original difference-field language can repair the failure. This eliminates an entire natural strategy for recovering a generic commuting automorphism and turns the source's pointwise theorem over completions into a hereditary obstruction over the whole cone of consistent theories above ACFA.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is Beyarslan--Kowalski (2026), whose Theorem 1.2 supplies the all-completions obstruction and whose Corollary 6.2 supplies the transfer from a model-complete theory to its completions. Kikyo's earlier general results use instability plus further hypotheses such as amalgamation, while Hyttinen--Kangas study several commuting automorphisms in a non-elementary framework.

The theorem is restricted to extensions in the original \(L_\sigma\) language and makes no assertion about richer expansions before adjoining \(\tau\).
