# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Writing
\[
A=\sigma(p^a)=p^a+U,\qquad B=\sigma(q^b)=q^b+V
\]
turns the harmonious equation into the exact identity
\[
UV=p^a q^b,
\]
where
\[
U=\sigma(p^{a-1}),\qquad V=\sigma(q^{b-1}).
\]
The congruences \(U\equiv1\pmod p\) and \(V\equiv1\pmod q\) handle both the equal-prime case and the prime-support forcing. Positive exponents \(a=1\) or \(b=1\) are explicitly excluded by the same valuation argument. For distinct primes and \(a,b\ge2\), one obtains
\[
U=q^b,\qquad V=p^a.
\]
Substitution yields
\[
(p-1)(q-1)p^{a-1}=p^{a-1}-1,
\]
whose two sides have incompatible sizes. All primes, exponents, and boundary cases are covered.

## Originality
PASS. The complete archival harmonious-pairs manuscript was inspected across all sections, including its main counting theorem, discordant-number machinery, computation section, and concluding application. Targeted full-text searches for prime-power language found no statement equivalent to the present exclusion. The paper's cited Borho consequence bounds the product in terms of total prime-factor multiplicity but does not force either member to have mixed prime support.

The exact harmonious-pair sequence records give definitions and examples, not this structural obstruction. A related indexed theorem classifies pairs consisting of a power of two and a mixed-support partner of the form \(2^bq\); because such partners are not prime powers, its positive families neither cover nor contradict the new statement.

Residual novelty risk is nonzero because the proof is short and could have appeared in unindexed notes.

## Value
PASS. Prime powers are the minimal multiplicative building blocks for divisor-sum problems. Showing that a harmonious equation cannot pair two such blocks gives a sharp support boundary: every harmonious pair must have at least one genuinely mixed-prime member. The conclusion applies uniformly to all primes and all positive exponents and is not a bounded census.
