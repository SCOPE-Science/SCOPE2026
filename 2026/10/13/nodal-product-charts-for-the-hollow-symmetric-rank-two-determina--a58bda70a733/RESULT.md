# Nodal-product charts for the hollow symmetric rank-two determinantal locus

## Finding
Fix an integer \(n\ge3\). Let \(R=\mathbb C[x_{ij}:1\le i<j\le n]\), and let \(M=(m_{ij})\) be the symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix with \(m_{ii}=0\) and \(m_{ij}=m_{ji}=x_{ij}\) for \(i\ne j\). Put \(J=I_3(M)\), the ideal generated by **all** \(3\times3\) minors, including nonprincipal minors, and \(X_n=\operatorname{Proj}(R/J)\).

**Theorem (complete scheme and local-germ classification).** Every principal projective chart \(x_{ab}\ne0\) admits a scheme isomorphism
\[
X_n\cap D_+(x_{ab})\ \simeq\ \operatorname{Spec}\frac{\mathbb C[u_i,v_i:i\notin\{a,b\}]}{(u_iv_i:i\notin\{a,b\})}.
\]
Consequently \(X_n\) is a reduced, equidimensional, locally complete-intersection projective scheme of dimension \(n-2\). Its exactly \(2^{n-1}-1\) irreducible components are Segre varieties indexed by unordered nontrivial bipartitions \(A\mid B\) of \([n]\):
\[
X_{A\mid B}\cong\mathbb P^{|A|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|B|-1},\qquad
\deg X_{A\mid B}=\binom{n-2}{|A|-1}.
\]
In particular, if \(P_{A\mid B}\subset R\) denotes the prime ideal generated by all same-side edge variables and all \(2\times2\) minors of the cross-block \((x_{ij})_{i\in A,j\in B}\), then the **projectively saturated** ideal satisfies
\[
J^{\mathrm{sat}}=\bigcap_{A\mid B}P_{A\mid B}.
\]
The total degree is \(\deg X_n=\tfrac12\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\).

At a closed point \(p\in X_n\), let \(z(p)\) be the number of identically zero rows of the represented hollow matrix. Then \(0\le z(p)\le n-2\), and the complete local ring is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,p}\cong
\frac{\mathbb C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{n-2-z},u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_z,v_z]]}{(u_1v_1,\ldots,u_zv_z)},\qquad z=z(p).
\]
Its number of local irreducible branches and its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity both equal \(2^{z(p)}\); its tangent-cone Hilbert series is
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_p}\mathcal O_{X_n,p};t)
=\frac{(1+t)^{z(p)}}{(1-t)^{n-2}}.
\]
Thus \(p\) is smooth if and only if no row vanishes; the reduced singular locus is the union of the \(n\) zero-row subvarieties, each isomorphic to \(X_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge4\). The maximal local multiplicity is \(2^{n-2}\), attained exactly at the coordinate points supported on one edge. The normalization of \(X_n\) is the disjoint union of its smooth Segre components.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes are over \(\mathbb C\); the matrix is symmetric with a structural zero diagonal, not skew-symmetric. The projective scheme is defined by **every** \(3\times3\) minor. The scheme-theoretic statement is about \(\operatorname{Proj}R/J\) and the saturation \(J^{\mathrm{sat}}\); no assertion that the unsaturated affine-cone ideal \(J\) is radical at the irrelevant vertex is needed. Terms such as local branches and multiplicity refer to completed complex local rings.

## Proof
1. **A rank-two pivot exists at every projective point.** If a nonzero symmetric matrix with zero diagonal had rank one, it could be written \(cww^{\mathsf T}\), with \(c\ne0\). Its zero diagonal would force \(w_i^2=0\) for every index, a contradiction. Thus every point of \(X_n\) has rank exactly two and some nonzero off-diagonal coordinate \(s=x_{ab}\). These coordinates cover \(X_n\) projectively.

2. **Exact localized determinantal ideal.** On the localization \(R_s\), order the pivot indices first, and write
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}B&C\\ C^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&s\\s&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
C_{\bullet i}=\begin{pmatrix}u_i\\v_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(B\) is invertible, invertible row and column operations over \(R_s\) reduce \(M\) to the block diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(B,S)\), where
\[
S_{ij}=x_{ij}-\frac{u_iv_j+v_iu_j}{s}\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
S_{ii}=-\frac{2u_iv_i}{s}.
\]
All \(3\times3\) minors vanish modulo the entries of \(S\); conversely each \(S_{ij}\) is a unit multiple of a suitable minor with the two pivot rows and columns. More precisely, the \(3\)-minor ideal of \(\operatorname{diag}(B,S)\) is exactly \((S_{ij})\) after inverting \(s\), since a nonzero \(2\times2\) pivot determinant \(-s^2\) times \(S_{ij}\) is a \(3\)-minor. Thus this is an **ideal equality**, not just a rank calculation on points. Eliminating the off-pivot \(x_{ij}\), and using that \(2\) is invertible, gives
\[
R_s/JR_s\cong\mathbb C[s,s^{-1},u_i,v_i:i\notin\{a,b\}]/(u_iv_i).
\]
After taking the degree-zero part (equivalently setting \(s=1\)) one obtains the asserted projective affine chart.

3. **Reducedness, complete intersection, components.** Each local ideal is generated by the regular sequence of squarefree monomials \(u_iv_i\) on disjoint variable pairs. This is a radical complete intersection: it equals the intersection of the \(2^{n-2}\) coordinate prime ideals obtained by choosing one variable from each pair. The chart is therefore reduced, and every irreducible component has dimension \(n-2\). Choosing a component amounts to assigning each remaining vertex to the side of \(a\) or of \(b\). As the pivot varies, these choices glue to precisely the unordered bipartitions \(A\mid B\) of the full vertex set. On a fixed bipartition the cross-block has rank one and same-side entries vanish, giving exactly the Segre embedding. Every bipartition contains a pivot across its sides and hence contributes a genuine component. Since projective schemes are determined by their standard affine charts, reducedness and the prime-component identification yield the stated saturation equality.

4. **Germs and singular support.** Fix \(p\) and a nonzero pivot \(x_{ab}(p)\). For each remaining index \(i\), the row indexed by \(i\) is zero at \(p\) precisely when \(u_i(p)=v_i(p)=0\); the pivot formula also forces every off-pivot entry in that row to vanish then. If the row is nonzero, exactly one of \(u_i,v_i\) is a unit near \(p\), so the relation \(u_iv_i=0\) eliminates the other variable smoothly. If the row is zero, the pair remains a node \(u_iv_i=0\). Completing gives the displayed product-of-nodes ring, with \(n-2-z\) free variables. It has \(2^z\) minimal primes; the equations are homogeneous quadrics in a regular local ring, so the associated-graded Hilbert series is \((1+t)^z/(1-t)^{n-2}\) and the multiplicity is \(2^z\). It is regular exactly when \(z=0\). The zero-row strata are naturally smaller rank-two hollow schemes \(X_{n-1}\). All Segre components are normal, so their disjoint union is the normalization of the reduced scheme.

5. **Degree.** The Segre component indexed by \(A\mid B\) has degree \(\binom{n-2}{|A|-1}\). Summing degrees over unordered bipartitions counts each ordered nontrivial side \(A\) twice, giving
\[
\deg X_n=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\binom na\binom{n-2}{a-1}=\frac12\binom{2n-2}{n-1},
\]
by Vandermonde's convolution. The maximal possible number of zero rows is \(n-2\), attained at a coordinate-edge point; conversely a rank-two matrix with only two nonzero rows must be supported on their unique edge.

## Verification
The standalone `CHECK.py` checks all \(3\times3\) minors after the chart substitution, by exact integer polynomial coefficient calculations, for \(n=3,4,5,6\): respectively \(1,16,100,400\) minors. It also recovers every nodal generator from the pivot principal minor and independently evaluates the degree identity, component count and maximal multiplicity for \(3\le n\le11\). Run `python CHECK.py` in a Python environment with SymPy installed. The verbatim output is in `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt`. These computations test only bounded instances; Steps 1--5 above supply the proof for every \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Grood, Harmse, Hogben, Hunter, Jacob, Klimas and McCathern (2014), Theorem 2.3 and Proposition 2.2, establish the complete-bipartite **support-graph classification** for rank-two real symmetric zero-diagonal matrices (after removing isolated zero rows). This covers the set-theoretic pattern underlying the component labels and is **not** an originality claim here. Their paper does not identify the complex projective minor scheme, its ideal saturation, universal nodal-product local rings, multiplicities or Hilbert--Samuel series. Bruns--Römer--Wiebe's earlier generic rectangular determinantal-ring framework likewise does not give these symmetric zero-diagonal local equations. The added content is the full **scheme-theoretic and pointwise local** classification across every size, including intersections of all components.

## Limitations
The theorem makes no assertion about affine embedded components at the homogeneous irrelevant vertex before saturation, and gives no classification in characteristic two. It does not determine the minimal homogeneous free resolution or the global scheme structure of the singular subscheme (only its reduced support). It is not an assertion that the classical degree of symmetric rank-two matrices or the complete-bipartite support criterion was previously unknown. An equivalent unindexed prior local-scheme calculation remains possible.
