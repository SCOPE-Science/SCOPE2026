# Review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The induction has been reconstructed from the definitions. The central-intersection step uses only normality, subgroup closure, the fact that the quotient is abelian, and extension closure. The fixed-point calculation in the quotient is exact, and Burnside counting converts the inductive fixed-point divisibilities into the desired identity-term divisibility. The \(\pi\)-separable corollary follows because normal-series intersection and splicing prove subgroup and extension closure.

Risk retained: the theorem assumes finiteness at every stage; no profinite analogue is claimed.

## Originality
**PASS.** The closest source is Sabatini, arXiv:2610.03151v1, which proves precisely the soluble special case and whose proof supplied the structural clue. The new claim is not a cardinality corollary of that theorem: divisibility of a subset cardinality does not pass to the larger \(\mathcal C\)-generated set. The full proof must be rerun with the class axioms.

The comparison also inspected Akbari et al., arXiv:2511.11544v1, whose broader-class remarks concern adapting computations, and Garonzi--Lucchini--Otmen, DOI:10.1007/s00605-025-02112-y, which studies generalized generated-pair sets with different closure hypotheses and measure conclusions. Targeted semantic searches for solubilizer/solvabilizer, formation/F-solubilizer, \(p\)-solvable, \(\pi\)-separable, normalizer divisibility, and extension-closed classes returned no equivalent statement. Closest result-database records concerned unrelated divisibility problems.

Residual risk: formation or Fitting-class literature may contain an equivalent result under different terminology. The claim is deliberately limited to the exact theorem proved here and does not assert a literature-wide uniqueness result.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem turns a recent solubilizer-specific arithmetic statement into a reusable closure principle and supplies natural new consequences for \(\pi\)-separable and \(p\)-solvable generated subgroups. These classes are central in finite-group theory and can include nonsoluble groups, so the consequence is not a renaming of the soluble case. The proof also gives a conceptual boundary: bounded derived length lacks the extension closure used at the decisive maximal-subgroup step, consistent with the failure recorded in the motivating paper.
