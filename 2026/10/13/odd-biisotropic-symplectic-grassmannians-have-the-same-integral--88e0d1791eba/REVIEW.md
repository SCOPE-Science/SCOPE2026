# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The initiating source proves that \(\mathrm{S}_2\mathrm{Gr}(k,2n+1)\) is smooth and projective, has a \(\mathbb G_m\)-action with isolated fixed points, and has affine Białynicki--Birula cells. Its Theorem 1.1 matches the number of \(d\)-dimensional cells with the number of \(d\)-dimensional Schubert cells in \(\mathrm{Gr}(k,2n+2-k)\) for every \(d\). Brosnan's integral motivic Białynicki--Birula theorem decomposes the motive of a smooth projective \(\mathbb G_m\)-variety into Tate twists of fixed-component motives. Since all fixed components here are points, the twist multiplicities are exactly the cell counts. The two motives therefore have identical integral Tate decompositions. Additivity of the Grothendieck ring across the same affine stratifications gives the class equality.

## Originality
PASS. Fonarev--Kuznetsov state only the resulting isomorphism of graded integral singular cohomology groups and explicitly separate the ring-structure problem. Their text contains no motivic or Grothendieck-class statement. Brosnan gives the general decomposition theorem but no specialization to this 2026 family. Searches for the exact family together with Chow-motive, Tate-motive, Grothendieck-class, and ordinary-Grassmannian comparison formulations found no implication-equivalent published statement.

## Value
PASS. The motivic conclusion is strictly stronger than equality of Betti groups: it identifies the entire integral Chow motive as a pure Tate object with the same multiplicity profile as the ordinary Grassmannian. This controls integral Chow-group ranks and every additive realization of the Chow motive, and the Grothendieck-class identity records the same cell equality at the level of varieties. At the same time, it cleanly marks what remains unresolved: multiplicative structure is not determined.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest statement is Theorem 1.1 of Fonarev--Kuznetsov, which gives the dimension-preserving cell count and graded cohomology-group equality. The critical general tool is Brosnan's Theorem 3.5. An in-preparation manuscript cited by the initiating source treats \(\mathrm{S}_2\mathrm{Gr}(3,7)\), but no public full text was located; possible overlap in that special case remains a residual literature risk.
