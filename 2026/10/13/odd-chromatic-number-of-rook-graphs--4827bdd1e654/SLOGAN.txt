The rook graph \(K_m\square K_n\), with \(1\le m\le n\) and \(n\ge2\), has odd chromatic number \(n\) when \(m<n\), but the balanced graph \(K_n\square K_n\) has the sharp parity surcharge \(n+2\). The obstruction at \(n+1\) colors is that row-missing and column-missing colors have identical multiplicity profiles, forcing one cell whose row and column see exactly the same color set.
