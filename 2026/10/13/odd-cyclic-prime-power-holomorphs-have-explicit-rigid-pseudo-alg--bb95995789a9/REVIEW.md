# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof uses the published explicit monomial irreducible representations of the odd cyclic prime-power holomorph. The shift permutation forces the complement exponent to be divisible by \(\varphi(p^k)\); comparing two diagonal entries then forces the cyclic-kernel exponent to be divisible by \(p^k\), and the remaining scalar condition is controlled exactly by the order of \(\omega\). This gives kernel size \(p^{2(n-k)-s}\) and nonlinear codegree \(p^{k+s}\). Summing root-of-unity parameters gives nonlinear multiplicity \(p^{t-1}\) at codegree \(p^t\). Adding the linear characters of the cyclic quotient yields the displayed pseudo-algebra. The top codegree and its multiplicity recover \(p\) and \(n\). The packaged checker verifies the parameter counts and degree-squared identity for several primes and ranks.

## Originality

PASS. The primary 2021 representation paper gives the full irreducible parametrization and explicit matrices but does not discuss codegrees or pseudo-algebras. Moretó and Lewis--Yan provide the pseudo-algebra framework without a holomorph specialization. The closest semantic-database theorem is the scalar affine Frobenius formula, which covers the boundary \(n=1\) only. The accepted claim deliberately starts at \(n=2\). Targeted searches under holomorph, metacyclic, cyclic prime-power, codegree, and pseudo-algebra formulations found no covering formula. Older metacyclic codegree literature remains a residual bibliographic risk.

## Value

PASS. The family is a standard, structurally rich metacyclic family with known irreducible representations. Determining its complete pseudo-algebra supplies a clean test of how much kernel information the invariant retains and proves exact parameter rigidity. The new \(p\)-power codegree tower also explains the transition from the affine \(n=1\) case to higher prime powers.
