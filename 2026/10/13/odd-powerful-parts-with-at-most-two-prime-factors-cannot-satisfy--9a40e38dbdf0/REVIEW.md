# Review
## Correctness
PASS. The nonunitary Euler function and divisor-sum decomposition are independently reconstructed from the prime-power formulas. For a one-prime odd powerful part the ratio is at most one-third. For two odd prime-power blocks, the exact expansion and monotonicity show a sharp maximum \(113/225\) at \(3^2 5^2\). Parity of the factors in \(B(t)\) forces the squarefree part of any solution to be either \(1\) or \(2q\) with prime \(q\equiv1\pmod4\). The required ratio is then respectively \(1\) or at least \(5/9\), contradicting the sharp bound.
## Originality
PASS, with residual indexing risk. Ligh--Wall Theorem 7 excludes just the one-prime powerful part and Conjecture 4 explicitly posits evenness. No inspected full primary source, OEIS identity, or relevant semantic search established the new two-distinct-odd-primes obstruction. Other similar prime-support theorems concern different divisor functions. The original 1987 formulas and the older one-prime exclusion are prior inputs, not novelty claims.
## Value
PASS. An explicit historic conjecture singles out odd powerful parts; Theorem 7 leaves the two-prime stratum as its first possible counterexample class. Its complete exclusion is a meaningful unbounded structural advance, strengthened by a sharp local ratio estimate with a unique equality case. No claim is made to resolve the higher-support conjecture.
