# Review of Optimal fractional \(k\)-resolving functions of square-page book graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The resolving-neighborhood table is derived directly from the book graph distances. Summing the constraints for \(R\{a_i,a_j\}\) gives the sharp lower bound \(rk/2\), while the uniform internal weight \(k/4\) attains it. Equality forces zero spine weight, equality in every page-pair constraint, and therefore page sums \(k/2\). The two remaining resolving-neighborhood families yield exactly the two cross inequalities stated in the theorem, proving both necessity and sufficiency. The uniqueness consequences follow algebraically from this polytope description. An exact-arithmetic verifier independently reconstructs every resolving neighborhood for \(4\le r\le10\), checks the lower-bound certificate, and stress-tests uniform and asymmetric optima.

## Originality
PASS. The foundational fractional \(k\)-metric paper gives general inequalities, proportionality conditions, and several exact graph families but no book or generalized-theta treatment was found in the inspected text. Earlier Cartesian-product bounds already cover the scalar fractional metric value of \(B_r\cong K_{1,r}\square K_2\), and together with the general proportionality criterion can recover the scalar \(rk/2\) formula once the uniform resolving function is supplied. Those results do not characterize all minimum functions. Targeted semantic and exact-phrase searches under book, generalized theta, fractional resolving function, optimizer, and Cartesian-product aliases did not locate the page-sum-and-cross-inequality optimizer classification.

## Value
PASS. Weighted resolving functions model redundant landmark allocation, so knowing all minimum allocations is materially stronger than knowing only the optimum value. The classification identifies the complete optimum polytope for a standard Cartesian-product/generalized-theta family, proves a unique optimum at \(r=4\) and at \(k=4\), and exhibits broad asymmetric optimum regimes. This is a natural structural refinement of the fractional \(k\)-metric framework rather than a recomputation of a known scalar invariant.
