# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses only finite-support spectral projection for \(\operatorname{ad}_X\), translation finite differences over a characteristic-zero field, and the direct coset decomposition of the skew group ring. Each step preserves two-sided ideal membership, and the central contraction-extension identities imply the claimed prime and maximal-ideal correspondences.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating 2026 paper classifies graded ideals and gives graded-simplicity and simplicity criteria, but not all ordinary ideals in the nonsimple translation case. The closest older skew-group and strongly graded results located establish nonzero intersection with a center or commutant, not generation of every ideal by that intersection. A 2001 crossed-product abstract is retained as a residual risk because its full text was unavailable, but its displayed subgroup construction does not visibly specialize to \(\ker c\) in this translation setting.

## Value
PASS. The result converts a newly introduced non-simple but graded-simple family from a noncommutative ideal problem to the full commutative ideal theory of \(k\ker c\), including primes and maximals. For free abelian groups this becomes an explicit Laurent-polynomial classification, and the characteristic-\(p\) example gives a sharp structural boundary rather than a cosmetic hypothesis.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest direct inputs are the 2026 affine skew-group realization and graded-ideal theorem, together with older ideal-intersection results for abelian skew group rings and strongly graded rings. The theorem is not asserted for nonabelian acting groups or positive characteristic. Full-text comparison with the 2001 crossed-product article was unavailable, and that access limitation remains a stated originality risk.
