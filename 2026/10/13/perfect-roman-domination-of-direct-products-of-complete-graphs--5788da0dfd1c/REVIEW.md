# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The fixed-support canonicalization is exact: if \(A\) is the label-two set, the unique lowest weight for that support is \(mn+|A|-q(A)\), where \(q(A)\) counts outside vertices adjacent to exactly one selected vertex. The three two-support arrangements all have strictly positive excess. For supports of size at least three, a row-wise count bounds \(q(A)\) by both \(n+2m-3\) and \(m+2n-3\). Those inequalities establish the infinite lower bound and force any non-singleton equality into the \(3\times3\) board with three selected cells. The equality case is exactly the six permutation supports. The included verifier independently exhausts 1,188,352 supports and 551,124 ternary assignments on specified small boards; these are consistency checks, not the proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to stated risk.** The closest inspected same-graph primary theorem [1, Proposition 2.1] evaluates ordinary rather than perfect Roman domination, giving \(5\) or \(6\). The inspected perfect Roman Cartesian-product paper [2] treats paths and cycles with a different graph adjacency. The general regular-graph source [3] gives no identified exact formula for this two-parameter family. Searches also compared tensor/categorical product aliases, the complement-of-rook-graph formulation, related published database findings, and the classification of minima. No checked stronger theorem was found that entails both the formula and optimum classification. An indexed search is not proof of global novelty; alternate nomenclature and unindexed literature are residual risks.

**Value — PASS.** The formula and full optimum census apply to all products of two complete graphs of order at least three; the exceptional \(3\times3\) permutation configurations are completely classified. In contrast with ordinary Roman domination, whose number stays at \(6\) when both factors have order at least four [1], the exact perfect Roman domination number is \(m+n\), so the additive separation grows unboundedly. This is a structural difference rather than a numerical increment.

**Limitations.** Products with a factor smaller than three and with more than two factors are outside scope. The search may miss equivalent older work. The independent scientific audit is not yet performed.
