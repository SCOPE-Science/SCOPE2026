**Correctness: PASS.** The support reduction gives the exact weight \(W(F)=N+|F|-U(F)\). The decisive combinatorial bound \(U(F)\le4(n-4)\) for supports of size at least two is proved in both the disjoint-pair and intersecting-pair cases. The two candidate constructions are explicit, and every equality case is identified; this proves the optimum and the full minimum-function census for all \(n\ge7\). Exhaustive bounded searches checked orders seven, eight, and nine, and a distinct count implementation corroborated the local weight relation. The finite tests do not constitute the infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** The 2018 regular-graph paper provides bounds rather than the present exact Kneser case. The 2022/2023 Kneser paper analyzes ordinary, total, and signed Roman variants, none equivalent to the exact-one-neighbor condition. The 2023 perfect Roman paper treats general complexity and several other graph classes. No exact coverage emerged from the recorded published-finding corpus and synonym searches. A potentially unindexed source could nevertheless contain equivalent results; novelty is not mathematically certified by a negative search.

**Value: PASS.** The exact minimum is accompanied by a full classification and multiplicity enumeration, including two adjacent phase transitions \(7\to8\to9\) for a classical Kneser family. The distinguishing constraint on uniquely served vertices is essential, not merely a reformulation of ordinary Roman domination. The argument identifies a natural sharp unique-neighborhood bound and applies it for every \(n\ge7\), rather than fitting small numerical tables.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Henning–Klostermeyer 2018, Zec–Grbić 2023, and Fernau–Mann 2023 are fully named and scoped in the accompanying result. Neither the general bound nor the ordinary Roman formula implies this exact perfect Roman census. Unknown historical equivalents and other Kneser parameters remain outside verified novelty scope.
