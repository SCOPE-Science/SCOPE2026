# Scientific review: square perimeters of sixty-degree primitive triangles

## Correctness — PASS
The conic parameter \(t=(a+c)/b\) reconstructs the unique reduced pair \((m,n)\), and a direct prime-gcd analysis proves the normalization divisor is precisely one or three. The square case with normalization divisor one is ruled out modulo three. In the surviving divisor-three case, coprimality and prime valuations yield a disjoint exhaustive pair of square-factor families. For each wedge the area is \(Y\log2/4\), the admissible coprime residue density is \(3/(4\zeta(2))\), and ordinary slicing plus Möbius inversion gives the claimed error. A separate perimeter-factor enumeration agrees with the parametrization at six complete finite bounds through \(10^7\). The asymptotic conclusion rests on the symbolic proof, not these tests.

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature limits
A full-text comparison with Zelator's 2008 complete parametrization and Zimhoni's 2019 matrix-tree treatment did not identify a square-perimeter counting theorem. Gordon and Wells study unrestricted perimeter counts, which do not imply this sparse subset's asymptotic. The A350038 perimeter sequence is a set of possible values rather than an asymptotic count of triangles with square perimeters. Earlier Gilder/Burn and Gordon articles and the complete Gordon–Wells text were not available for conclusive page-by-page exclusion; an earlier equivalent result could exist. Related results on right-angled triangles address a different norm form.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact classification and an asymptotic law for a natural arithmetic restriction on a classical infinite family. The exclusion of the normalization-one branch modulo three, the pair of inequivalent residue wedges, and the exact leading constant with a nontrivial power-saving counting error make the result more than a table entry or direct restatement of known parametrizations. It does not resolve counting for unrestricted angles and does not assert uniqueness of a perimeter value among triangles.
