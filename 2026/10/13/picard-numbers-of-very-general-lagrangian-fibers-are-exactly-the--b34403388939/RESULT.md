# Picard numbers of very general Lagrangian fibers are exactly the divisors of the fiber dimension
## Finding
Let \(f:X\to B\) be a non-isotrivial Lagrangian fibration from a primitive symplectic complex variety of dimension \(2g\), and let \(F=X_b\) be a very general smooth complex fiber. Then
\[
\rho(F)\mid g.
\]
More precisely, if the very general fiber is isogenous to the product of \(d\) Hodge-generic factors supplied by Varvak's classification, then
\[
\rho(F)=d.
\]
Conversely, every positive divisor \(d\mid g\) occurs for a non-isotrivial primitive-symplectic Lagrangian fibration of relative dimension \(g\). Hence the set of possible Picard numbers of very general complex fibers is exactly the set of positive divisors of \(g\).

## Assumptions and scope
A primitive symplectic variety is used in the sense of Varvak: it is compact, \(\mathbf Q\)-factorial, log-terminal, has \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\), and has a symplectic form unique up to scaling. The fibration is assumed non-isotrivial. A very general fiber means a smooth complex fiber outside a countable union of proper closed Hodge loci.

The claim concerns the Picard number of a very general closed complex fiber. It does not identify the Picard number of the scheme-theoretic generic fiber over the function field of \(B\).

## Proof
Varvak's Corollary 1.3 states that there is a positive divisor \(d\mid g\) such that the very general fiber \(F\) is isogenous to
\[
A_1\times\cdots\times A_d,
\]
where the \(A_i\) are pairwise non-isogenous Hodge-generic Abelian varieties of dimension \(h=g/d\). Equivalently, on rational first cohomology the Mumford--Tate group is isogenous to
\[
\bigl(\mathbf G_m\times \operatorname{Sp}_{2h}^{\,d}\bigr)/\{\pm1\}.
\]

Write \(V_i=H^1(A_i,\mathbf Q)\). By Künneth,
\[
H^2\!\left(\prod_i A_i,\mathbf Q\right)
\cong
\bigoplus_i \Lambda^2 V_i
\;\oplus\!
\bigoplus_{i<j} V_i\otimes V_j.
\]
For the standard representation of \(\operatorname{Sp}_{2h}\), the invariant subspace of \(\Lambda^2V_i\) is one-dimensional, generated by the symplectic form. For \(i\ne j\), the tensor product \(V_i\otimes V_j\) has no invariant vector under the independent factors \(\operatorname{Sp}(V_i)\times\operatorname{Sp}(V_j)\). Therefore the space of rational Hodge classes of degree two on \(\prod_iA_i\) has dimension exactly \(d\).

By the Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem, rational degree-two Hodge classes on a complex Abelian variety are exactly \(\operatorname{NS}(-)\otimes\mathbf Q\). Thus
\[
\rho\!\left(\prod_i A_i\right)=d.
\]
Picard number is invariant under isogeny because an isogeny induces an isomorphism on rational cohomology compatible with Hodge classes. Hence \(\rho(F)=d\), proving the divisibility assertion.

For sharpness, fix any positive divisor \(d\mid g\) and set \(h=g/d\). Varvak's Proposition 2.8 constructs a primitive symplectic variety \(X\) and a Lagrangian fibration whose very general fiber has Mumford--Tate group
\[
\bigl(\mathbf G_m\times(\operatorname{Sp}_{2h})^d\bigr)/\{\pm1\}.
\]
The construction is the \(d\)-th symmetric power of a varying Beauville--Mukai Lagrangian fibration; its very general fiber is a product of \(d\) distinct Hodge-generic Abelian varieties, so the resulting fibration is non-isotrivial. The preceding invariant calculation gives Picard number \(d\). Since \(d\) was arbitrary, every positive divisor of \(g\) occurs.

## Verification
The initiating source was inspected at its definition of Hodge-genericity, Corollary 1.3, and Proposition 2.8. Hodge-generic means Mumford--Tate group \(\operatorname{GSp}_{2h}\); Corollary 1.3 supplies the divisor \(d\mid g\) and the product-isogeny decomposition; Proposition 2.8 realizes every factorization \(g=hd\).

The remaining argument is representation-theoretic. In degree two, each standard symplectic factor contributes exactly its polarization class, and cross terms contribute no invariant Hodge classes. The Lefschetz \((1,1)\)-theorem converts this exact Hodge-class count into the Néron--Severi rank. No finite experiment is used as evidence for an infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Varvak classifies the Mumford--Tate group and gives the product-isogeny description and attainability, but does not state the resulting classification of Picard numbers of very general fibers. The exact Picard-number conclusion requires taking degree-two invariants of the classified Mumford--Tate representation.

Oguiso proves a different rank-one theorem: for an abelian-fibered hyper-Kähler manifold over projective space with a rational section, the Picard number of the scheme-theoretic generic fiber over the function field is one. Oguiso explicitly notes that the Picard numbers of smooth closed fibers may all be at least two. Thus that theorem does not imply, and is not contradicted by, the present classification for very general closed complex fibers.

For smooth hyper-Kähler total spaces the attainable intermediate factor counts remain subtler: Varvak asks which intermediate pairs can occur smoothly. The sharp attainability statement here is therefore intentionally made for primitive symplectic varieties, where singular symmetric-power examples realize every divisor.

## Limitations
The result assumes non-isotriviality and concerns very general smooth closed complex fibers. It does not classify Picard numbers in the isotrivial cases, special fibers, or the scheme-theoretic generic fiber. The construction realizing every divisor may have singular total space when the factor count is intermediate, so this does not solve the corresponding smooth hyper-Kähler attainability problem.

The originality comparison is limited by the searchable literature inspected here. The deduction is short once the new Mumford--Tate classification is available, so an implicit occurrence in unindexed notes or discussion remains a residual risk.
