# Point-removal dimension has exact finite convex-choice degree
## Finding

Marcone and Volpi introduce a strengthened point-removal principle for poset dimension, denoted
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+.
\]

Viewed as a represented multivalued problem, an input consists of a countable poset \(P\), a distinguished point \(x_0\), and a finite realization
\[
(L_j)_{j<r}
\]
of
\[
P\setminus\{x_0\}.
\]

A valid output is an \((r+1)\)-element realization
\[
(L'_j)_{j<r+1}
\]
of \(P\) such that
\[
L_j
=
L'_j\!\upharpoonright(P\setminus\{x_0\})
\qquad(j<r-1).
\]

Let \(\mathsf{XC}_1\) denote closed choice on nonempty closed convex subsets of \([0,1]\), equivalently nonempty closed intervals, with the standard negative-information representation. Let \((\cdot)^*\) denote finite parallelization.

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{DB}_p^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1^*.
}
\]

There is a sharper fixed-arity form. For each fixed integer
\[
r\ge1,
\]
let
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\]
be the restriction in which the input realization contains exactly \(r\) linear orders. Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^{r-1}.
}
\]

Here the zero-fold product is the computable unit. Consequently,
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,1}^+
\]
is computable,
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,2}^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1,
\]
and, for arbitrary fixed \(r\), the point-restoration task has exactly the product degree of \(r-1\) independent interval-choice instances.

This identifies the uniform computational content hidden inside the source's reverse-mathematical equivalence: every original realization that must be preserved costs one separator choice, while the last input linearization can be replaced by two canonical extensions without any choice oracle.

## Assumptions and scope

A realization of a poset is a finite family of linear extensions whose intersection is the partial order.

The problem
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\]
uses standard effective presentations of countable posets and linear orders on subsets of \(\mathbb N\). The finite integer \(r\) is fixed externally for the fixed-arity statement and is part of the finite input code for the unbounded problem.

The source's separation problem \(\mathsf{LS}\) takes a linear order \(L\) and sets \(I,F\subseteq L\) with every element of \(I\) below every element of \(F\); a solution is a downward-closed separator containing \(I\) and disjoint from \(F\).

The notation
\[
\mathsf{LS}^{m}
\]
means the Cartesian product of \(m\) independent copies of \(\mathsf{LS}\). The notation
\[
\mathsf{LS}^{*}
\]
means the disjoint union over all finite arities, with the arity supplied as part of the instance.

The reductions are ordinary Weihrauch reductions. No claim of strong Weihrauch equivalence is made.

No claim is made that the powers
\[
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^m
\]
form a strict hierarchy in \(m\). The theorem gives an exact expression for each slice without asserting pairwise inequivalence of those expressions.

## Proof

We first show the fixed-arity equivalence with the separation problem.

### From point restoration to \(r-1\) separators

Fix
\[
r\ge1
\]
and an instance
\[
(P,x_0,(L_j)_{j<r})
\]
of
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+.
\]

Define
\[
I=\{x\in P:x<_P x_0\}
\]
and
\[
F=\{x\in P:x_0<_P x\}.
\]

For every
\[
j<r,
\]
all elements of \(I\) precede all elements of \(F\) in \(L_j\).

Marcone--Volpi's forward construction uses the final input order
\[
L_{r-1}
\]
to build, computably, two linear extensions of \(P\): one places \(x_0\) immediately above the initial segment generated by \(I\), and the other places \(x_0\) immediately below the final segment generated by \(F\).

For each of the remaining orders
\[
L_j
\qquad(j<r-1),
\]
the construction needs exactly one separator
\[
B_j
\]
for the \(\mathsf{LS}\) instance
\[
(L_j,I,F).
\]

Given these \(r-1\) separator sets, insert \(x_0\) between \(B_j\) and its complement, preserving the old order on
\[
P\setminus\{x_0\}.
\]

Together with the two computable extensions obtained from \(L_{r-1}\), this gives the required \(r+1\) realizing orders.

Every preprocessing and postprocessing step is uniform and computable. Therefore
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\le_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{LS}^{r-1}.
\]

For
\[
r=1,
\]
there are no separator calls: the two canonical extensions of the sole input order already form the required output.

### From \(m\) separators to point restoration with \(m+1\) input orders

Now fix
\[
m\ge0
\]
and \(m\) independent \(\mathsf{LS}\) instances
\[
(L_j,I_j,F_j)_{j<m}.
\]

For
\[
m=0,
\]
there is nothing to solve. Assume
\[
m>0.
\]

The converse direction of Marcone--Volpi's Theorem 5.3 first applies a computable normalization. It replaces the orders by computably isomorphic copies on a common domain so that all lower sets become one common set \(I\) and all upper sets become one common set \(F\).

It then introduces one additional computable linear order
\[
L_m
\]
that places the common \(I\)-part first, the neutral part next, and the common \(F\)-part last.

Intersecting
\[
L_0,\ldots,L_m
\]
and adjoining a new point \(0\) with
\[
I<_P0<_PF
\]
produces a poset \(P\) for which
\[
(L_j)_{j<m+1}
\]
is a realization of
\[
P\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Apply
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,m+1}^+.
\]

Its preservation clause applies exactly to
\[
j<m.
\]

Hence, in each of those first \(m\) output orders, the restriction away from \(0\) is the corresponding original normalized order. Define
\[
B'_j
=
\{x:x<_{L'_j}0\}.
\]

Because
\[
I<_P0<_PF,
\]
each \(B'_j\) contains \(I\), avoids \(F\), and is downward closed in \(L_j\). Undoing the computable normalization yields a separator for each original \(\mathsf{LS}\) instance.

Thus
\[
\mathsf{LS}^{m}
\le_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{DB}_{p,m+1}^+.
\]

Putting
\[
m=r-1
\]
gives
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{LS}^{r-1}.
\]

### Replace linear separation by interval choice

Proposition A.1 of Marcone--Volpi proves
\[
\mathsf{LS}
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1.
\]

Taking a fixed finite Cartesian product preserves Weihrauch equivalence, so
\[
\mathsf{LS}^{r-1}
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^{r-1}.
\]

Therefore
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^{r-1}.
\]

Finally, taking the disjoint union over all finite \(r\ge1\) converts the two fixed-arity reductions into
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{LS}^{*}
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1^{*}.
\]

## Verification

The proof was checked against the full primary preprint.

The definition of
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+
\]
requires \(r+1\) output orders from an \(r\)-order realization and preserves exactly the first \(r-1\) original orders.

In the forward half of Theorem 5.3, the authors apply finite parallelized linear separation only to the sequence indexed by
\[
j<r-1.
\]
The final input order is instead converted directly into two linear extensions.

In the converse half, a sequence of \(m\) separation instances is augmented by one extra computable order. The resulting point-removal instance therefore has exactly
\[
m+1
\]
input realization orders. The output preservation requirement applies to exactly the first
\[
m
\]
orders, and their cuts below the new point recover the \(m\) separators.

These two arity counts are the load-bearing facts behind the fixed-slice equivalence.

The same paper's Proposition A.1 gives explicit computable reductions in both directions between \(\mathsf{LS}\) and one-dimensional convex closed choice \(\mathsf{XC}_1\).

No finite experiment is used. The result follows from direct uniform reconstruction of the source reductions.

## Relationship to prior work

Marcone--Volpi prove over \(\mathsf{RCA}_0\) that their finite-parallel separation principle
\[
\mathsf{LS}^{*}
\]
is equivalent, as a reverse-mathematical statement, to
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+.
\]

In a separate appendix, they prove the Weihrauch equivalence
\[
\mathsf{LS}
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1.
\]

The paper does not state a Weihrauch degree for
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+,
\]
does not combine these two results into
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
\mathsf{XC}_1^*,
\]
and does not state the fixed-arity law
\[
\mathsf{DB}_{p,r}^+
\equiv_{\mathrm W}
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^{r-1}.
\]

Kihara--Pauly's work on finite and convex choice gives broader context for the Weihrauch behavior of convex closed choice, but it does not treat the poset point-removal problem.

Targeted searches for the strengthened point-removal principle together with Weihrauch reducibility, fixed realization arity, products of \(\mathsf{XC}_1\), and finite parallelization did not locate an equivalent classification.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the strengthened point-removal problem
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p^+,
\]
whose output is required to preserve the specified old linearizations. The weaker principle
\[
\mathsf{DB}_p
\]
does not impose that correlation, and no Weihrauch classification of it is claimed here.

The exact expression
\[
(\mathsf{XC}_1)^{r-1}
\]
does not by itself assert that different values of \(r\) yield different Weihrauch degrees.

The result uses ordinary Weihrauch reducibility, not strong Weihrauch reducibility.

The theorem classifies uniform computational content; it does not alter the source's reverse-mathematical induction bounds for computable truth.
