# Polynomial Boolean-basis elimination on Euclidean Kripke frames

## Finding
Fix any finite collection \(B\) of Boolean truth functions, each of finite arity. Let \(\mathrm{ML}[B]\) be the one-modal propositional language with the functions in \(B\), propositional variables and constants, and \(\Box,\Diamond\). Let \(\mathrm{ML}_{\mathrm{DM}}\) use only \(\neg,\wedge,\vee\), constants, and the same modal operators. There are constants \(c_B>0\) and \(d_B\in\mathbb N\) such that **every** \(\varphi\in\mathrm{ML}[B]\) of ordinary syntax-tree size \(n\ge1\) has a formula \(\widehat\varphi\in\mathrm{ML}_{\mathrm{DM}}\) of size at most \(c_B n^{d_B}\), with the same original propositional variables, and
\[
 \forall (W,R,V)\ \forall w\in W:\quad
  \bigl[R\text{ Euclidean}\bigr]\Longrightarrow
 \bigl((W,R,V),w\models\varphi\iff (W,R,V),w\models\widehat\varphi\bigr).
\]
Here Euclidean means \(\forall x,y,z\in W\), \(xRy\land xRz\Rightarrow yRz\). The statement permits infinite and nonserial frames. In particular, adding bi-implication does not provide a superpolynomial formula-size advantage on the full frame class of modal logic \(\mathsf K5\). The polynomial depends on the fixed basis, not on the formula, frame, or valuation.

## Assumptions and scope
Formula size counts all syntax-tree nodes, including repeated subformula occurrences; it is **not** directed-acyclic-graph or circuit size. All Boolean connectives are fixed finite-arity truth functions and interpreted classically at each world. Both \(\Box\) and \(\Diamond\) have the standard Kripke semantics. No reflexivity, seriality, transitivity, finiteness, cardinality restriction, or restriction on propositional valuations is assumed. The proof uses two published, quantified polynomial translation theorems: the fixed-finite-basis propositional Boolean formula-balancing theorem and the fixed-finite-basis \(\mathsf S5\) translation theorem of Berkholz, Kuske and Schwarz (Theorem 4.4 and Lemma 4.7 of their 2025 full paper).

## Proof
**Successor-cluster lemma.** Fix an arbitrary Euclidean relation \(R\), a world \(w\) with a successor \(y\), and set \(C=R(y)\). Euclideanness implies \(yRy\), because \(wRy\) twice. If \(z,t\in C\), then \(yRz\) and \(yRt\) give \(zRt\). Hence the restriction of \(R\) to \(C\) is the total relation, in particular \(\mathsf S5\). Moreover \(R(z)=C\) for every \(z\in C\): inclusion \(C\subseteq R(z)\) follows from \(yRz,yRt\Rightarrow zRt\); if \(zRu\), then \(zRy\) (since \(yRz\land yRy\Rightarrow zRy\)), and \(zRy\land zRu\Rightarrow yRu\), so \(u\in C\). Every \(z\in R(w)\) belongs to \(C\), by \(wRy\land wRz\Rightarrow yRz\); therefore all such \(z\) see exactly \(C\). If \(R(w)=\varnothing\), the last assertion is vacuous. This proof does not need transitivity or finiteness.

**S5 substitution.** From the published \(\mathsf S5\) polynomial translation theorem, for each \(\beta\in\mathrm{ML}[B]\) choose \(T_B(\beta)\in\mathrm{ML}_{\mathrm{DM}}\), equivalent to \(\beta\) at every state in every equivalence-relation Kripke model, with size \(\le C_B |\beta|^{e_B}\). For any Euclidean model and any \(z\in R(w)\), the generated submodel of \(z\) is exactly the complete \(\mathsf S5\) cluster \(C\). Structural induction on formulas establishes that evaluation of \(\beta\) at \(z\) in the original model agrees with evaluation in the restriction to \(C\). Thus \(\beta\) and \(T_B(\beta)\) agree at **every immediate successor of every world**, even if they do not agree at a nonreflexive root.

**Top-level Boolean skeleton.** In the syntax tree of \(\varphi\), identify its maximal modal subformula occurrences \(\nabla_i\beta_i\), with \(\nabla_i\in\{\Box,\Diamond\}\), meaning that no ancestor of this occurrence is modal. Replace each such entire occurrence by a distinct formal propositional letter \(q_i\). The remaining formula \(F\) is a purely propositional formula over the original letters and the \(q_i\), using the finite basis \(B\), with \(|F|\le n\). By the fixed-basis propositional balancing/compilation theorem, obtain a De Morgan formula \(F_{\mathrm{DM}}\), equivalent to \(F\) under every Boolean assignment, of size at most \(A_Bn^{b_B}\). Replace every occurrence of \(q_i\) in this compiled skeleton with the modal formula \(\nabla_i T_B(\beta_i)\). Call the resulting formula \(\widehat\varphi\). Every substituted modal atom has, at any world \(w\), exactly the truth value of the corresponding original \(\nabla_i\beta_i\), by the preceding paragraph; propositional Boolean equivalence therefore gives \(\widehat\varphi\equiv\varphi\) on all Euclidean frames. No fresh propositional variable remains in \(\widehat\varphi\).

**Polynomial bound.** There are at most \(n\) maximal modal occurrences and \(|\beta_i|<n\). The compiled Boolean skeleton contains at most \(A_B n^{b_B}\) formal letters, each substituted by a formula with at most \(1+C_B n^{e_B}\) nodes. Hence \(|\widehat\varphi|\le A_B n^{b_B}(2+C_B n^{e_B})\), a fixed polynomial for each \(B\). The same construction works for nullary truth functions, edgeless roots, and arbitrary modal nesting: nesting is confined to subformulas translated *once* by the published S5 theorem rather than recursively expanded in the Euclidean translation.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 file `verify.py` enumerates **all** labeled Euclidean relations on \(1,2,3,4\) worlds: respectively \(2,7,39,306\) relations. For each, it verifies the successor-cluster conclusion, then evaluates four instances of sound immediate-successor substitution with all \(2^{|W|}\) one-variable valuations. It reports `VERIFY_OK` and exhibits a three-world Euclidean relation that is not transitive, showing that this covers frames outside \(\mathsf{K45}\) and \(\mathsf S5\). Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing this file. Exhaustive tests on four worlds **do not** establish the unbounded theorem; that conclusion follows from the successor-cluster argument and the two cited published translation bounds.

## Relationship to prior work
Berkholz, Kuske and Schwarz (2025, Theorem 4.4) prove polynomial elimination of all fixed finite Boolean bases on \(\mathsf S5\) frames; they also show exponential separation for reflexive frames. Pfau (2026, Theorem 17 and Section 4.3) extends the positive result to **transitive, symmetric** frames, while proving exponential separation for transitive reflexive and for symmetric reflexive frames. Euclidean relations need be neither transitive nor symmetric, and may have a root with only a proper subset of an otherwise complete S5 cluster as its successor set. Thus neither positive frame-class theorem covers all Euclidean frames. The transfer in the proof isolates this non-S5 root as a Boolean skeleton and translates each modal scope *at the successors*, where S5 semantics is valid.

Georgiev (2017) studies modal-depth reduction on K45 and K5 (at most one or two modal levels, respectively). A bounded modal depth by itself does not bound the size required to eliminate non-locally-monotone connectives, so it is a different assertion. The available abstract does not resolve whether the full work additionally contains the present succinctness theorem.

## Limitations
The result is an upper bound, not a claim about an optimal polynomial degree, an efficient implementation of the published S5 constructor, or a corresponding lower bound. It says nothing about all transitive-only or all symmetric-only frames, where established exponential separations apply. The literature search is not a uniqueness proof: an earlier equivalent transfer for Euclidean frames under different terminology may exist, particularly in modal-depth or Boolean-basis work. The proof depends on the published S5 polynomial translation theorem and propositional balancing theorem, not on the finite checker. No independent audit or independent validation has been performed.
