# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim quantifies over every fixed finite Boolean basis, every arbitrary-size Euclidean Kripke frame, every valuation and every formula, with a uniform polynomial formula-size bound. A Euclidean successor is contained in a generated complete reflexive cluster: a direct relation argument establishes this without finiteness, transitivity or seriality. This justifies using the published Berkholz–Kuske–Schwarz (2025) S5 polynomial translation for *modal operands*, not incorrectly for formulas at the original root. A propositional balancing theorem compiles the remaining Boolean skeleton once; replacing its placeholders multiplies two fixed polynomials. The primary S5 theorem and its quantitative proof were read. The standalone finite checker corroborates the cluster lemma and modal substitution equations but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (residual coverage risk)
The 2025 S5 theorem and Pfau's 2026 transitive–symmetric theorem apply to different, smaller frame classes; the actual present statement is polynomial elimination on the entire Euclidean class K5, including nontransitive and nonsymmetric frames. K5 modal-depth reduction discussed in Georgiev (2017) does not by itself imply polynomial formula-tree size. published-finding corpus and literature searches for Euclidean/K5, biconditional, Boolean-basis simulations and aliases found no explicit dominating result. Georgiev's full paper was inaccessible, and earlier equivalent formulations cannot be excluded. This is a same-model originality determination, not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
This resolves a natural Boolean-basis succinctness case left between strongly contrasting 2025–2026 results. The theorem is all-size, applies to arbitrary propositional valuations, and identifies a reusable transfer mechanism: the non-S5 root is handled by a Boolean skeleton while every modal scope enters an S5 cluster. It goes beyond finite table recomputation or a mere restatement of the S5 theorem.

## Scope and sources
The result does not determine the optimal polynomial exponent, lower bounds, or the behavior of all arbitrary frames. The decisive sources are Berkholz, Kuske and Schwarz, *Boolean basis, formula size, and number of modal operators* (2025, Theorems 3.2 and 4.4, Lemma 4.7); Sebastian Pfau, *Boolean Basis and Succinctness of Modal Logic via Hella-Vilander Games* (2026, Theorem 17 and Sections 4.1–4.3); and Georgiev, *Modal Depth of Formulas in K45 and K5* (2017, abstract only).
