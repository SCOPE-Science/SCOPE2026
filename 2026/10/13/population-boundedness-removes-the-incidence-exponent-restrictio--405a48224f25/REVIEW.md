# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The source's conservation law gives a uniform bound on the infected density. The positive Neumann resolvent fixes constants, so the nonlocal infectiousness field has the same uniform bound. On that reachable interval,
\[
\frac{u^p}{1+\kappa u^q}\le \max\{1,N^{p-1}\}u
\]
for every \(p\ge1\), independently of \(q\). This supplies exactly the integrability estimate needed in the source's proof. The remaining extinction and convergence arguments do not use \(p\le q+1\).

## Originality

PASS. The primary theorem and proof were inspected in full. The printed restriction is used at the global incidence estimate, while the accepted state-restricted replacement is not stated there. Searches by title, DOI, theorem number, exponent aliases, correction terminology, and implication-level formulations did not locate a published result covering this source-specific extension. The closest epidemic records concern different models and different thresholds.

## Value

PASS. The result closes the long-time theorem over the full exponent range \(p\ge1,\ q\ge1\) that the model itself permits. In particular, it shows that the previously excluded \(p>q+1\) regime still has extinction and positive residual susceptibility because bounded population, rather than the asymptotic tail of the incidence law, controls the realized dynamics.

## Closest literature and limitations

Related spatial-epidemic work motivates the model but does not imply this exact removal of the exponent relation. The claim does not cover \(p<1\), does not provide a decay rate, and does not refit the Puerto Rico data. An unindexed correction remains a residual literature risk.
