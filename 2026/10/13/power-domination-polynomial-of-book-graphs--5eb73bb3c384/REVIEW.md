# Review: Power domination polynomial of book graphs

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof separates sets meeting the spine from page-only sets. In the first case, the domination step observes both spine vertices and one vertex on every page, after which each missing page mate is the unique unobserved neighbor of its observed mate. In the page-only case, any touched page brings both spine vertices under observation; the process then succeeds exactly when at most one page remains untouched. With two untouched pages, both spine vertices have at least two unobserved page neighbors and no observed page vertex can enter an untouched page, so propagation is permanently blocked. These cases are exhaustive and give the polynomial by disjoint counting. The standalone verifier exhausts all subsets for \(2\le n\le7\) and checks the closed coefficients. Risk: finite replay does not prove the infinite family, so correctness rests on the structural argument.

## Originality
**PASS.** The 2018 paper introducing the power domination polynomial was inspected in full searchable text: it contains no “book” or “Cartesian” occurrence, and its conclusion invites counting power dominating sets for additional nontrivial families. The 2025 entropy paper was also inspected in full: it defines the book graph and uses its ordinary domination polynomial, while its power-domination section lists other families and gives no book-graph power domination polynomial. Broad web searches for “power domination polynomial” together with “book graph” and for the Cartesian description \(K_{1,n}\mathbin{\square}P_2\) did not surface a covering source. The closest semantic comparison located concerns minimum power dominating sets of connected threshold graphs, a different graph class and only minimum sets. Risk: absence from searched sources is not a proof of novelty; an unindexed or differently named equivalent formula may exist.

## Value
**PASS.** Book graphs are a standard, parameterized graph family and appear explicitly in recent work comparing domination-based entropy measures. The result gives substantially more than the scalar power domination number: it classifies every power dominating set, gives every coefficient of the power domination polynomial, identifies the unique minimum locations for all \(n\ge3\), and supplies the exact total count. The structural dichotomy—spine access versus at most one untouched page—also exposes the propagation mechanism that the polynomial records. Risk: the family is specialized, so the value is as a complete exact model case rather than a general Cartesian-product theorem.
