# Review: Power domination polynomial of crown graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The proof gives necessary and sufficient conditions, not only constructions. Mixed-side sets are exhausted by the \((1,1)\), \((1,\ge2)\), and \((\ge2,\ge2)\) cases. One-side sets reduce to the exact count of remaining unobserved neighbors after the domination step. The boundary \(n=3\) is handled separately. An exhaustive checker independently matched the theorem for every subset of \(\operatorname{Cr}_n\) for \(3\le n\le8\).

## Originality
PASS with a stated access risk. The 2018 paper defining the power domination polynomial was inspected in full and contains no “crown” occurrence. The closest same-object 2019 paper is visibly minimum-parameter focused; only its indexed first-page material was readable, so the scalar minimum values are excluded from the originality basis and the inaccessible remainder is recorded as a residual risk. Searches also covered the aliases “biclique crown” and “\(K_{n,n}\) minus a perfect matching.”

## Value
PASS. The result upgrades a natural minimum-parameter question to a complete classification of all power-dominating placements and an exact generating polynomial for every \(n\ge3\). The formula simultaneously yields every cardinality count, total count, and minimum multiplicity and has a short structural proof tied to the deleted matching.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest sources are Brimkov–Patel–Suriyanarayana–Teich, *Power domination polynomials of graphs* (arXiv:1805.10984v1), and Chandrasekaran–Sulthana, *k-Power Domination of Crown Graph* (2019). The theorem here is restricted to ordinary power domination and does not treat generalized \(k\)-power domination or propagation times. The inaccessible portion of the 2019 paper is the main residual literature risk.
