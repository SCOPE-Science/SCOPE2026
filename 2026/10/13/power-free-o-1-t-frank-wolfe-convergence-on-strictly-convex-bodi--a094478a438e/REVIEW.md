# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Strict convexity and compactness give a unique, continuous support minimizer and a positive separation modulus at every fixed distance. The proof combines this with the standard smoothness descent estimate, the vanishing distance to the compact optimum set, and a two-scale dichotomy for reciprocal objective-gap increments. Every sufficiently late reciprocal increment exceeds every fixed threshold, establishing \(o(1/t)\). The planar superflat witness is strictly convex because its lower profile is strictly convex and its upper profile strictly concave; the boundary support ratio tends to zero faster than any power. All nonstandard steps are justified in RESULT.md; no numerical sampling is substituted for the infinite-time proof.

**Originality — PASS (same-model, residual prior-literature risk).** Pokutta, arXiv:2604.28006v3, Definition 3.1 and Corollary 3.8, require one fixed power-type local dual sharpness inequality; Remark A.4 explicitly separates strictly convex superflat bodies from this assumption. The present qualitative support modulus provides a genuinely weaker geometric hypothesis and proves the omitted conclusion. Kerdreux–d'Aspremont–Pokutta (PMLR 130, 2021), despite defining arbitrary curvature moduli, formulate rate theorems using power-type moduli and additional gradient/growth conditions. Dunn (1979, 1980) gives historical non-power exposure theorems in the nonzero-gradient exposed-optimum regime. Their abstract-stated optimum-gradient modulus vanishes at a zero-gradient minimizer; the new theorem includes this missing case, but the historical complete texts were inaccessible, an important originality risk. Published-result searches did not identify the full all-objectives conclusion. Negative search results do not establish global novelty; a historical qualitative-modulus result remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The result closes a mathematical gap visibly identified by the motivating work: a canonical superflat compact strictly convex set has no finite power exponent, yet still has an unconditional asymptotic improvement for both standard descent-based Frank–Wolfe rules. It supplies the structural separation between merely qualitative support exposure and quantitative polynomial curvature. No new algorithm, uniform numerical speedup, or open-loop theorem is asserted.
