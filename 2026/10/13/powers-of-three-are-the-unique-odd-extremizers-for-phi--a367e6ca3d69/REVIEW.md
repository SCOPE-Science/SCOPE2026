# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. For an odd input, maximality forces the maximizing admissible integer to be even and not a power of two. The condition \(\varphi(M)\mid2n\) then limits the \(2\)-adic valuation of \(\varphi(M)\) to at most one, forcing \(M=2q^e\). The exact ratio \(M/\varphi(M)=2q/(q-1)\) gives \(M\le6n\), with equality only at \(q=3\); comparing \(2\cdot3^e=6n\) gives \(n=3^{{e-1}}\). The converse follows from \(\varphi(2\cdot3^{{a+1}})=2\cdot3^a\). Boundary case \(n=1\) is included.

## Originality
PASS. Ozeki proves the odd upper bound and analyzes the maximizing integer's factorization, while Pelizzari–Punch exhibit powers of three as sharpness witnesses. Neither inspected full text states the converse equality classification. Targeted literature and database searches for the equality locus, aliases, and the defining inverse-totient maximum found no covering result. The closest records concern different totient functions or unrelated extremal problems and do not imply this equality classification.

## Value
PASS. The constant \(6\) is singled out in the recent literature as the sharp odd-input constant. Determining its full equality locus is therefore a natural extremal classification, and it distinguishes genuine extremizers from merely asymptotically or numerically large values of \(\Phi(n)/n\). The result is exact and applies to every odd positive integer.

The closest literature supplies the upper bound and one infinite family of equality examples; it does not classify equality. The main limitation is that the proof is an equality analysis of an existing sharp bound and says nothing about even inputs.
