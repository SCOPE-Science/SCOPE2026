Conditioning on all vertex labels produces exactly \(q\), \(q^2\), or zero linear-equation solutions independently on each arrow. Expansion by edge subsets establishes all prescribed-target fiber counts, without assumptions about characteristic, orientation, cycles, or simple edges. Target support is realizable precisely under a proper \(q^2\)-coloring; concatenating the diagonal-label pairs from \(r\) commutators proves both directions of the width equivalence. Looplessness is essential. The included verifier independently checks small prime-field examples and exact graph identities; finite checks are not substituted for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS, with stated risk.** The multivariate Tutte/Fortuin--Kasteleyn spin expansion itself is classical, and the all-zero target formula for general radical-square-zero quivers is already available; these are expressly *excluded* from the novelty claim. The closest accessible result about exact commutator fibers is the two-vertex Kronecker case, recovered by specialization. The complete target-dependent coefficient distribution, its support-graph membership classification, and the chromatic commutator-width formula for general loopless quivers are neither consequences of an aggregate commuting probability nor of the reviewed published Kronecker result. Searches using alternative quiver, ring-word-map, and graph-polynomial vocabulary identified no stronger source supplying these three together. A logically equivalent result under incidence-form or coloring terminology might still exist and has not been excluded globally.

**Value — PASS.** Commutator width and realizability are intrinsic algebra invariants. A full, exact, arbitrary-target distribution is strictly finer than commuting probability and reveals sharp non-surjectivity for graph chromatic thresholds. The exact width grows without bound along complete-graph quivers at each fixed finite field, and the graph-theoretic classification is a motivated structural bridge rather than a numerical case calculation. It is useful as an explicit obstruction and construction tool for square-zero algebras and as a starting point for studying higher radical layers.

No conclusions extend to nonzero length-two paths, vertex loops, or group commutators. A possible independently discovered equivalent remains a bibliographic risk.
