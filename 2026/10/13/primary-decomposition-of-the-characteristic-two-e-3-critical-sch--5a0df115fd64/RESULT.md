# Primary decomposition of the characteristic-two \(e_3\) critical scheme in four variables
## Finding
Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), let \(R=K[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\), and let
\[
e_3=x_1x_2x_3+x_1x_2x_4+x_1x_3x_4+x_2x_3x_4.
\]
Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by the four first partial derivatives of \(e_3\). Then the order-two zero scheme and the critical scheme coincide, and \(I\) has the irredundant primary decomposition
\[
I=Q_1\cap Q_2\cap Q_3\cap Q_4,
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=(x_2+x_4,\ x_3+x_4,\ x_4^2),\\
Q_2&=(x_1+x_4,\ x_3+x_4,\ x_4^2),\\
Q_3&=(x_1+x_4,\ x_2+x_4,\ x_4^2),\\
Q_4&=(x_1+x_3,\ x_2+x_3,\ x_3^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each \(Q_i\) is primary to the prime \(P_i=(x_j:j\neq i)\) of the \(i\)-th coordinate axis, and
\[
R/Q_i\cong K[x_i,t]/(t^2).
\]
Thus the scheme has exactly four irreducible components, each coordinate axis occurs with generic multiplicity \(2\), the total degree is \(8\), and there are no embedded associated primes. In particular, this gives the full primary decomposition in the cubic four-variable characteristic-two case of the smaller-dimensional regime left open in the source.
## Assumptions and scope
The field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\). The statement concerns the affine critical scheme of the degree-three elementary symmetric polynomial in four variables and, equivalently here, its multiplicity-at-least-two scheme. It does not claim a primary decomposition for general \((n,d,p)\), nor does it classify all smaller-dimensional derivative loci in the source paper.

For the source notation, \(n=4\), \(d=3\), \(L=n-d+1=2\), and \(Q=2^{v_2(L)}=2\). The source therefore places this example in the smaller-dimensional but nonreduced regime and proves generic multiplicity \(2\) along each coordinate-axis component.
## Proof
Write \(f_i=\partial e_3/\partial x_i\). Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=x_2x_3+x_2x_4+x_3x_4,\\
f_2&=x_1x_3+x_1x_4+x_3x_4,\\
f_3&=x_1x_2+x_1x_4+x_2x_4,\\
f_4&=x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
In characteristic \(2\), one has \(f_4=f_1+f_2+f_3\), so \(I=(f_1,f_2,f_3)\). Euler's identity gives
\[
3e_3=\sum_{i=1}^4x_if_i.
\]
Since \(3=1\) in characteristic \(2\), it follows that \(e_3\in I\). Hence the scheme cut out by \(e_3\) and its first derivatives is exactly the critical scheme \(V(I)\).

Xie's exact dimension formula gives \(\dim V(I)=1\) for \((n,d,p)=(4,3,2)\). Thus \(I\), generated by three quadrics in the regular ring \(R\), has height \(3\). It is therefore a complete intersection, hence Cohen--Macaulay and unmixed, of degree \(2^3=8\).

The four coordinate axes are components. Xie's local-ring theorem gives generic transverse length \(Q=2\) on each of them, so these four axes already contribute total degree \(4\cdot2=8\). Consequently there are no further minimal components. Because \(I\) is unmixed, there are no embedded associated primes.

It remains to identify the isolated primary component on each axis. For the first axis, exact saturation gives
\[
I:x_1^\infty=(x_2+x_4,\ x_3+x_4,\ x_4^2)=Q_1,
\]
and the analogous three saturations give \(Q_2,Q_3,Q_4\). Each quotient is visibly isomorphic to \(K[x_i,t]/(t^2)\), so \(Q_i\) is \(P_i\)-primary. Since the four \(P_i\) are all minimal primes and there are no embedded primes, the primary decomposition is their intersection.

The package includes an independent exact polynomial-arithmetic replay over \(\mathbf F_2\): it computes Gröbner bases by Buchberger's algorithm, computes ideal intersections by elimination, verifies \(e_3,f_4\in(f_1,f_2,f_3)\), and verifies
\[
(f_1,f_2,f_3)=Q_1\cap Q_2\cap Q_3\cap Q_4.
\]

## Relationship to prior work
Xie determines all derivative-locus and higher-multiplicity dimensions for elementary symmetric polynomials in positive characteristic, classifies the extremal larger-dimensional components, and computes the generic local rings along coordinate components in the smaller-dimensional regime. The paper explicitly states that the smaller-dimensional and embedded components are not classified and that primary decompositions and global reducedness require additional information. The present result supplies that missing global scheme structure for the concrete case \((n,d,p)=(4,3,2)\).

Orzel's earlier work studies the dimension of the order-two zero locus for formula-complexity applications and does not give this primary decomposition. The earlier MathOverflow discussion identifies the reduced coordinate-support picture in characteristic zero and explicitly notes that the argument fails in positive characteristic; it does not contain the characteristic-two scheme decomposition above.
## Limitations
This is a single exact low-dimensional case. It does not provide a general formula for the primary decomposition of \(C_q(e_d)\) or \(Z_s(e_d)\). The proof uses Xie's dimension and generic-local-ring theorems for the structural degree argument; the explicit ideal equality itself is independently replayed by the bundled exact checker.

The result is scheme-theoretic over algebraically closed fields of characteristic \(2\). No assertion is made about arithmetic over non-algebraically-closed base fields beyond base change to an algebraic closure.
