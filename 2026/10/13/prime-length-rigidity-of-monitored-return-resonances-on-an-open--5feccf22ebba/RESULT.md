# Prime-length rigidity of monitored return resonances on an open quantum chain

## Finding

For every odd prime \(p\ge3\), the return of a quantum walker to the endpoint of a uniform open chain with \(p-1\) vertices obeys a complete three-branch mean first-detection formula at positive stroboscopic sampling times. The exceptional sampling times form \(((p-1)/2)^2\) **pairwise disjoint arithmetic ladders**; there are no multi-gap resonances. For \(p=3\), only two of the three branches have nonempty time sets.

Put \(m=(p-1)/2\) and \(a_r=2\cos(\pi r/p)\), for \(1\le r\le m\), and define
\[
\mathcal R_1=\bigcup_{r=1}^m\left\{\frac{2\pi k}{2a_r}:k\in\mathbb N\right\},\qquad
\mathcal R_2=\bigcup_{1\le r<s\le m}\bigcup_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
\left\{\frac{2\pi k}{a_r+\epsilon a_s}:k\in\mathbb N\right\}.
\]
Each denominator is positive. The individual ladders in this union are disjoint for \(\tau>0\), and the mean return-measurement count obeys
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[T_\tau]=\begin{cases}
p-2,&\tau\in\mathcal R_1,\\
p-3,&\tau\in\mathcal R_2,\\
p-1,&\tau\notin\mathcal R_1\cup\mathcal R_2.
\end{cases}}
\]
The return is almost sure for every \(\tau>0\). The first branch consists of a single collision between the sampled phases of opposite-energy eigenstates, and the second consists of exactly two distinct two-state phase collisions. In particular, the mean cannot fall below \(p-3\) at positive sampling times, except that \(\mathcal R_2\) is empty for \(p=3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The one-particle Hilbert space is \(\mathbb C^{p-1}\), with orthonormal site vectors \(|x\rangle\), \(x=1,\dots,p-1\). The Hamiltonian is the unweighted path adjacency operator, \(H|x\rangle=|x-1\rangle+|x+1\rangle\), omitting absent boundary sites. Units set the nearest-neighbor hopping rate and \(\hbar\) to one. A walker starts at \(|1\rangle\). For a fixed \(\tau>0\), each interrogation consists of applying \(U_\tau=e^{-i\tau H}\), measuring the rank-one projector \(P=|1\rangle\langle1|\), and stopping on the first positive outcome. A negative outcome is followed by the next unitary step and measurement. The random variable \(T_\tau\in\{1,2,\dots\}\) counts interrogations, not elapsed physical time. No continuous loss or nonprojective monitoring is assumed.

## Proof

**1. Endpoint spectral support.** Let \(n=p-1\). The sine transform diagonalizes the open path:
\[
E_j=2\cos\frac{\pi j}{p},\qquad
\langle x|v_j\rangle=\sqrt{\frac2p}\sin\frac{\pi jx}{p},\qquad 1\le j\le n.
\]
Every endpoint spectral weight \(w_j=|\langle1|v_j\rangle|^2=(2/p)\sin^2(\pi j/p)\) is strictly positive. If \(r_\tau\) is the number of *distinct* phases in \(\{e^{-i\tau E_j}:1\le j\le n\}\), then the cyclic space generated by \(|1\rangle\) under \(U_\tau\) has dimension precisely \(r_\tau\). Within this space, each eigenspace of \(U_\tau\) is one-dimensional and its eigenvector has nonzero endpoint overlap.

**2. The monitored-return counting identity.** Work in that \(r_\tau\)-dimensional cyclic space \(C\). With \(\psi=|1\rangle\) and \(Q=(I_C-P)U_\tau\), the probability of no detection during the first \(t\) interrogations is \(\|Q^t\psi\|^2\) (including \(t=0\)). The operator \(Q\) has spectral radius strictly below one: its norm is at most one, and if \(Qx=\lambda x\) with \(|\lambda|=1\), equality in the projection norm forces \(U_\tau x=\lambda x\) and \(\langle\psi,x\rangle=0\). This contradicts the nonzero endpoint component of every \(U_\tau\)-eigenvector in \(C\). Consequently \(Q^t\to0\). Since \(QQ^\dagger=I_C-P\), iterating gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{N}Q^t P(Q^\dagger)^t
=I_C-Q^{N+1}(Q^\dagger)^{N+1}.
\]
Taking traces and sending \(N\to\infty\) gives \(\sum_{t\ge0}\|Q^t\psi\|^2=r_\tau\). The tail-sum identity for an integer-valued nonnegative return time therefore yields \(\mathbb E[T_\tau]=r_\tau\). Moreover, \(\|Q^t\psi\|\to0\) proves eventual detection with probability one. This is also an independent finite-dimensional derivation of the earlier general spectral-support return formula of Grünbaum, Velázquez and the Werners.

**3. Rational independence of the positive energies.** The spectrum is the set of \(m\) distinct numbers \(a_1>\dots>a_m>0\) and their negatives. Write \(\zeta=e^{\pi i/p}\). Suppose \(\sum_{j=1}^m c_j a_j=0\) with \(c_j\in\mathbb Q\). Then
\[
F(X)=\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\bigl(X^{m+j}+X^{m-j}\bigr)
\]
vanishes at \(\zeta\), has degree at most \(2m=p-1\), and has zero coefficient of \(X^m\). The minimal polynomial of the primitive \(2p\)-th root \(\zeta\) is
\[
\Phi_{2p}(X)=1-X+X^2-\cdots+X^{p-1},
\]
which has degree \(p-1\) and a *nonzero* coefficient of \(X^m\). Thus a nonzero \(F\) could only be a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\Phi_{2p}\), an impossibility. Hence \(F=0\), and its distinct outer coefficients give \(c_j=0\) for all \(j\). Therefore \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) are rationally independent.

**4. Exhaustive phase-resonance classification.** Apart from signs, all possible positive pairwise differences between distinct eigenvalues belong to the types \(2a_i\), \(a_i+a_j\), and \(a_i-a_j\) with \(i<j\). Their coefficient vectors relative to the rationally independent basis \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) are \(2e_i\), \(e_i+e_j\), and \(e_i-e_j\). Two vectors from different listed types, or with different indices, are never proportional over \(\mathbb Q\). Therefore ratios of two distinct listed positive gap types cannot be rational. If a common positive sampling time merged two different gap types, it would satisfy \(\tau\Delta=2\pi k\) and \(\tau\Delta'=2\pi k'\) for positive integers \(k,k'\), forcing the forbidden rational ratio \(\Delta/\Delta'=k/k'\). Thus all \(m+2\binom m2=m^2\) resonance ladders are pairwise disjoint for positive time.

A ladder of type \(2a_i\) merges exactly the states with energies \(a_i\) and \(-a_i\), reducing the phase-support count from \(n\) to \(n-1=p-2\). A ladder of type \(a_i-a_j\) merges the pairs \((a_i,a_j)\) and \((-a_i,-a_j)\). A ladder of type \(a_i+a_j\) merges \((a_i,-a_j)\) and \((a_j,-a_i)\). Both latter types therefore reduce the count by two, to \(n-2=p-3\). Outside these ladders every phase is distinct, giving \(n=p-1\). Combining with Step 2 proves the theorem, including all positive sampling times and the \(p=3\) boundary case.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py`, using NumPy and SciPy, checks all \(m^2\) predicted ladder representatives at \(\tau=2\pi/\Delta\) for odd primes \(p\in\{3,5,7,11,13,17,19\}\). It checks the expected phase-collision multiplicities and tests 43 small-system instances against an independently computed discrete Lyapunov return-time sum. Output:

`VERIFY_OK distinct_resonance_ladders=220 pair_collisions=406 direct_lyapunov_means=43`

These finite floating-point checks are corroboration; Step 3 and Step 4 are exact arguments proving the assertion for every odd prime, every integer ladder multiple, and all nonresonant positive times.

## Relationship to prior work

The general integer-quantization formula equating finite-state mean monitored return time to spectral-support cardinality is established in Grünbaum, Velázquez, A. H. Werner and R. F. Werner (arXiv:1202.3903); it is not claimed here as new. Sinkovicz et al. (arXiv:1411.0568) give general open-dynamics recurrence theory and examples. Friedman, Kessler and Barkai (arXiv:1603.02046) analyze measurement-induced critical sampling on periodic and infinite tight-binding lattices. Riddell and Bertini (arXiv:2404.12100) use cyclotomic methods for rational independence in other hopping systems. The present specialized statement resolves the *complete positive-sampling resonance set and its nonoverlap* for every prime-length open path, including the three possible return means and the exact number of distinct exceptional ladders. This arithmetic classification is not supplied by a general spectral-support identity alone.

## Limitations

The result concerns the first **return** to an endpoint, not an arbitrary initial-site-to-target **hitting** time. The path must have exactly \(p-1\) vertices with \(p\) an odd prime, uniform zero-on-site-energy nearest-neighbor hopping and ideal equally spaced projective interrogations. Composite lengths, nonuniform hopping, decoherence, and off-endpoint monitoring are not classified. The mean counts measurements; the physical mean duration equals \(\tau\mathbb E[T_\tau]\). The result does not give variance or full return-time distribution. Existing general monitored-return quantization results imply its integer-valued *format* but not the explicit cyclotomic resonance classification presented here. Independent literature priority assessment has not been performed.
