# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof separates primes according to whether they contain \(D\). The quotient case is exactly \(R/D\cong k[x,y,z]\); the complementary case is forced to a unique coordinate kernel by the mutually orthogonal idempotents \(\delta_\omega\). Minimality, maximality, topology, and dimension then follow from the explicit classification. The idempotent proof uses the infinite fibers to force a polynomial part taking only the values \(0\) and \(1\) on a Zariski-dense set, hence the polynomial part is constant.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating 2026 paper constructs the ring and proves its annihilator properties but does not state its prime spectrum, minimal-prime set, Krull dimension, irreducible-component decomposition, or idempotent classification. The 2009 minimal-spectrum paper supplies general context for annihilator conditions but predates the example. Targeted searches for the same function-ring construction, aliases involving finite-support ideals, prime pullbacks, and idempotents found no result that states or clearly implies the complete classification for this ring. Residual risk remains that a general theorem about rings with orthogonal-idempotent ideals could subsume some steps.

## Value
PASS. Minimal-prime topology is part of the established motivation for annihilator conditions, so computing it for the newly constructed sharp counterexample is intrinsic rather than an arbitrary invariant. The result explains the example geometrically: a three-dimensional irreducible polynomial component coexists with countably many isolated minimal-maximal components, yet the idempotent Boolean algebra is only finite–cofinite. That contrast is useful for understanding why the construction has abundant coordinate annihilators without decomposing as an infinite product.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is arXiv:2610.00116v1, which provides the construction \(R=\Delta B+D\), the Zariski-dense set, and the annihilator properties. Hong–Kim–Lee–Nielsen (2009) study minimal-prime spectra and compactness for annihilator-condition rings in general. Neither inspected source gives the displayed spectrum or idempotent classification. The finding is intentionally limited to the specific Tran–Nguyen ring.
