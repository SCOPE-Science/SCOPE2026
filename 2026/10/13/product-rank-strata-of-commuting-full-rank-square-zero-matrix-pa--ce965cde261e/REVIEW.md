# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The canonical form for a maximal-rank square-zero matrix is standard and is proved via image/kernel equality. The commuting partner's block form, the equation \(AB+BA=0\), and the identification of product rank with \(\operatorname{rank}A\) follow by direct block multiplication. The quotient \(\ker A/\operatorname{im}A\) detects exactly the rank of the second square-zero factor; in canonical blocks this is invertibility of \(S\). A direct count of image/kernel flags, quotient isomorphisms, and free block entries gives the closed formula with no characteristic assumptions. Separate finite enumerations and nonprime-field arithmetic cross-check the proof. The finite experiments do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (subject to bibliographic uncertainty)
The closest same-object full text is Levy's 2006 preprint *Varieties of Modules for Z/2Z x Z/2Z*. Its Sections 1–3 and Lemma 2.1 treat algebraically closed geometric components and describe rank-labeled strata, not the exact finite-field fibers requiring both operator ranks maximal and a specified product rank. Britnell–Wildon's 2010 preprint is about commuting similarity-class existence and centralizer types, not labeled pair frequencies. Basili–Iarrobino's 2007 study is about Hilbert functions and generic Jordan forms for commuting pairs over infinite fields. A known formula for mutually annihilating square-zero pairs applies only to the product-zero boundary. Targeted direct searches and mathematical implication comparisons did not identify an already published formula covering the complete positive-product distribution. These comparisons do not prove global priority: an equivalent historical enumeration may have escaped the inspected sources.

## Value — PASS
The product \(XY\) is a natural invariant of a module over \(\mathbb F_q[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)\); nonzero product distinguishes genuinely different structures from modules on which the radical square acts trivially. The finding determines **all** attainable product ranks for the maximal-rank pair locus and every finite-field fiber in all even dimensions, exhibiting the sharp one-quarter rank cutoff and an explicit rational-point distribution. Unlike an arbitrary small numerical sample, it treats a complete natural stratum and includes every characteristic and extension field. The positive-product counts do not follow from the prior mutually annihilating formula.
