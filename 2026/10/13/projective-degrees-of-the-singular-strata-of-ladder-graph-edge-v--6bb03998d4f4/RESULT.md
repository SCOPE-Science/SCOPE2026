# Projective degrees of the singular strata of ladder-graph edge varieties

## Finding
Fix an integer \(m\ge3\). Let \(L_m=P_2\square P_{m+1}\) be the simple ladder graph with \(m\) square faces. Its projective edge-monomial toric variety \(X_m\subset\mathbb P^{3m}_{\mathbb C}\) has dimension \(2m\) and is the complete intersection
\[
X_m=V(f_1,\ldots,f_m),\qquad f_i=v_{i-1}v_i-u_iw_i\quad(1\le i\le m),
\]
where the homogeneous coordinates are \(v_0,\ldots,v_m,u_1,\ldots,u_m,w_1,\ldots,w_m\).

The reduced singular locus \(S_m=\operatorname{Sing}(X_m)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is pure of codimension three in \(X_m\), with exactly \(5(m-1)\) irreducible components. Precisely \(m+3\) of them have projective degree \(2^{m-2}\) and the remaining \(4(m-2)\) have degree \(2^{m-3}\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\deg S_m=(3m-1)2^{m-2}.}
\]
Each component is generically a locus of transverse ordinary threefold nodes (analytically \(xy-zw=0\) over \(\mathbb C\)). This claim concerns the reduced support and its projective degree, not the scheme structure of the Jacobian ideal.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\), the embedding is the standard edge-monomial embedding with one coordinate per graph edge, and \(m\) ranges over all integers \(m\ge3\). The variables \(v_i\) correspond to rungs and \(u_i,w_i\) to the two rails in the \(i\)-th cell. A component's degree is its degree in \(\mathbb P^{3m}\), with reduced structure. Because all singular components have dimension \(2m-3\), their degrees add to the degree of the reduced singular locus.

## Proof
**1. Coordinate ring.** The ladder has \(2m+2\) vertices, \(3m+1\) edges and cycle rank \(m\). Its only chordless cycles are the \(m\) square faces. It is a bipartite ring graph obtained by attaching successive three-edge paths to a starting square, so the graph-toric ideal is generated by the \(m\) square binomials \(f_i\) and is a prime complete intersection of height \(m\). This is the ring-graph theorem and foliation criterion of Gitler–Reyes–Villarreal [1, Corollaries 3.4 and 3.7]. The dimension is consequently \(3m-m=2m\).

**2. Exhaustion of singular points.** The \(i\)-th Jacobian row is supported in the four columns \((v_{i-1},v_i,u_i,w_i)\), with respective entries \((v_i,v_{i-1},-w_i,-u_i)\). At any point of the complete intersection, singularity is equivalent to Jacobian rank \(<m\). If any row \(i\) is zero, the point lies in
\[
R_i=V(v_{i-1},v_i,u_i,w_i)\cap X_m.
\]
For \(i>1\), the neighboring equation \(f_{i-1}\) reduces to \(u_{i-1}w_{i-1}=0\); similarly \(f_{i+1}\) reduces to \(u_{i+1}w_{i+1}=0\) when \(i<m\). Choosing one zero rail from each existing neighbor produces \(2^{\mathbf1_{i>1}+\mathbf1_{i<m}}\) coordinate components, denoted \(R_{i,\alpha,\beta}\).

Suppose instead every row is nonzero and a nontrivial relation \(\sum_i\lambda_i\operatorname{row}_i=0\) exists. Since the \(u_i,w_i\) columns occur in no other row, \(\lambda_i\ne0\) forces \(u_i=w_i=0\). The equation \(f_i=0\) then gives \(v_{i-1}v_i=0\), and the nonzero-row assumption makes exactly one of these rungs nonzero. Thus every participating row is supported at exactly one rung column. Two rows can cancel only if they are adjacent, say \(i,i+1\), both supported in \(v_i\). The point consequently lies in
\[
J_i=V(v_i,u_i,w_i,u_{i+1},w_{i+1})\cap X_m,\qquad 1\le i<m.
\]
Conversely, each \(R_{i,\alpha,\beta}\) has row \(i\) zero and each \(J_i\) has the two rows \(i,i+1\) linearly dependent (or zero). Hence the union of these loci equals the singular locus set-theoretically.

**3. Irreducibility and dimensions.** After fixing the zero coordinates of a component \(R_{i,\alpha,\beta}\), the equations \(f_{i-1},f_i,f_{i+1}\) that exist vanish identically; the remaining equations split into disjoint shorter chains of ladder quadrics. Their quotient rings are polynomial extensions and tensor products of prime graph-toric rings of ladder segments, hence domains over \(\mathbb C\). For an endpoint \(i=1\) or \(i=m\), the coordinate-linear subspace has codimension five and retains \(m-2\) independent quadrics; an interior \(i\) fixes six coordinates and retains \(m-3\) independent quadrics. Both have codimension \(m+3\) in \(\mathbb P^{3m}\), i.e. codimension three in \(X_m\).

For \(J_i\), five coordinates vanish, \(f_i,f_{i+1}\) vanish identically, and the \(m-2\) residual equations again split into shorter independent ladder chains. Their quotient is a domain. Hence \(J_i\) also has codimension \(5+(m-2)=m+3\) in the ambient projective space. Each listed component has a dense toric stratum with support precisely its complementary nonzero coordinates, so the loci are distinct and none contains another. There are \(2+2+4(m-2)=4m-4\) row-zero components and \(m-1\) adjacent-row components, totaling \(5(m-1)\).

**4. Degree computation.** A projective complete intersection of \(q\) quadrics inside a coordinate projective subspace has degree \(2^q\). The shorter ladder equations remain regular sequences because they define prime complete intersections and involve disjoint variable blocks. Each of the four end-row components and each of the \(m-1\) loci \(J_i\) has \(m-2\) surviving quadrics, hence degree \(2^{m-2}\). Every interior-row component has \(m-3\) surviving quadrics, hence degree \(2^{m-3}\). Therefore the degree multiset has \(m+3\) copies of \(2^{m-2}\) and \(4(m-2)\) copies of \(2^{m-3}\). Summing gives
\[
(m+3)2^{m-2}+4(m-2)2^{m-3}=(3m-1)2^{m-2}.
\]

**5. Generic transverse analytic type.** At a general point of a row-zero component, take all non-forced coordinates nonzero. Each neighboring equation can be solved for the coordinate killed by its chosen rail option, since the other rail variable is invertible; all remaining ladder equations are smooth in independent variables. The only transverse equation left is the rank-four quadric \(v_{i-1}v_i-u_iw_i=0\). At a general point of \(J_i\), both \(v_{i-1}\) and \(v_{i+1}\) are nonzero, so \(f_i\) eliminates \(v_i\), while \(f_{i+1}\) becomes \(u_iw_i\,v_{i+1}/v_{i-1}-u_{i+1}w_{i+1}=0\). An invertible rescaling gives \(xy-zw=0\). The remaining equations provide smooth factors. This proves the generic transverse node claim.

## Verification
The included dependency-free file `ladder_degree_checker.py` enumerates binary coordinate supports satisfying the chain quadrics; for every support it computes the Jacobian rank exactly modulo the prime \(101\). It extracts maximal singular supports, compares them with the predicted coordinate components, and recomputes all component degrees by the number of surviving regular quadrics. It checks \(m=2,3,4,5,6\) (the first is a boundary regression). Among the checked cases, the singular-support enumerations cover respectively \(11,89,591,3619,21199\) singular representatives. For \(m=3,4,5,6\) the asserted component counts are \(10,15,20,25\), and the total projective degrees are \(16,44,112,272\). Run `python3 ladder_degree_checker.py` beside the checker file. The output is embedded as `ladder_degree_checker_output.txt`. These finite checks do not establish the theorem for arbitrary \(m\); Steps 1–5 give the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Gitler–Reyes–Villarreal [1] established the prime complete-intersection graph-toric description used in Step 1. Wagner [2] developed a broad projective Hibi-variety singular-orbit criterion in terms of contracted-poset fibers. Lakshmibai–Mukherjee [3] determine the general singular locus of affine Hibi toric varieties, and Gonciulea–Lakshmibai [4] study certain matrix-ladder determinantal singular loci. These general descriptions provide related and in part stronger qualitative singular-support coverage. The contribution assessed here is specifically the **complete projective degree distribution and total degree** for the standard embedded chain of graph-square quadrics, with an elementary explicit component decomposition used to obtain the degrees; it does not claim a first general singular-locus criterion. The two-square case is deliberately outside the theorem.

## Limitations
No claim is made about the nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, embedded components of that scheme, nongeneric singular germs, resolutions, or independent validation. Known general Hibi-orbit results may imply the component supports by specialization; they do not directly supply the asserted degrees in the inspected portions. A complete comparison with Wagner's original full text could not be made, so possible unindexed equivalent degree formulas are a residual originality risk.
