# Projective realization of every automorphism on the two most algebraic Enriques sextics

## Finding

Over \(\mathbb C\), let \(S_{\mathrm I}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{II}}\) be the Enriques sextic models displayed by Shimada--Veniani for the two most algebraic Enriques surfaces. Choosing \(\alpha=15/16\) in their type-I equation and \(\alpha=63\) in their type-II equation, the full projective stabilizers are
\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_4}(S_{\mathrm I})\cong D_8,\qquad
\operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_4}(S_{\mathrm{II}})\cong S_4.
\]
More strongly, the natural lifts from the two projective stabilizers to the corresponding smooth Enriques surfaces are surjective. Thus every automorphism of either Enriques surface is linear on this sextic model.

For type I, put
\[
K=\left\langle
\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,1),
\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1)
\right\rangle\cong C_2^2
\]
inside \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbb C)\), and let
\[
s[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[x_3:x_1:x_2:x_0].
\]
Then \(\langle K,s\rangle\) has order \(8\) and is isomorphic to \(D_8\).

For type II, let
\[
u[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[x_0:x_2:x_1:x_3],\qquad
v[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]=[x_0:x_1:i x_3:-i x_2].
\]
Then \(\langle K,u,v\rangle\) has order \(24\) and is isomorphic to \(S_4\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The type-I model is the \(\alpha=15/16\) member of Shimada--Veniani's displayed Enriques sextic; the alternative \(\alpha=17/16\) in their construction differs only by a relabelling, and no additional statement about that presentation is needed here. The type-II model uses \(\alpha=63\).

After clearing denominators and moving the right-hand side to the left, the equations used in the verification are
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm I}={}&-x_0^2x_1^2x_2^2+8x_0^2x_1^2x_3^2+8x_0^2x_2^2x_3^2-x_1^2x_2^2x_3^2\\
&-8x_0^3x_1x_2x_3+9x_0x_1^3x_2x_3-7x_0x_1x_2^3x_3-8x_0x_1x_2x_3^3,
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{II}}={}&-x_0^2x_1^2x_2^2+x_0^2x_1^2x_3^2+x_0^2x_2^2x_3^2+63x_1^2x_2^2x_3^2\\
&-x_0^3x_1x_2x_3+x_0x_1^3x_2x_3+x_0x_1x_2^3x_3-x_0x_1x_2x_3^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The conclusion concerns these particular projective models, not every Enriques sextic of Nikulin--Kondo type I or II.

## Proof

Direct substitution shows that every generator listed above preserves the corresponding polynomial exactly. Projective closure of the type-I generators contains \(8\) elements. Its element orders are
\[
1^1,\quad 2^5,\quad 4^2,
\]
which identifies the group as the dihedral group \(D_8\). Projective closure of the type-II generators contains \(24\) elements, with element orders
\[
1^1,\quad 2^9,\quad 3^8,\quad 4^6,
\]
the order profile of \(S_4\); equivalently, the normal subgroup \(K\cong V_4\) and the two displayed lifts act on its three nonidentity elements as the full \(S_3\), giving \(V_4\rtimes S_3\cong S_4\).

A projective automorphism of either sextic induces a birational self-map of the smooth Enriques surface represented by that model. Since an Enriques surface is minimal of nonnegative Kodaira dimension, this birational self-map is biregular. The resulting homomorphism from the projective stabilizer to the automorphism group of the smooth Enriques surface is injective: an element acting trivially on the smooth model acts trivially on a dense open subset of the sextic and hence is the identity in \(\operatorname{PGL}_4\).

Shimada--Veniani prove
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(Y_{\mathrm I})|=8,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(Y_{\mathrm{II}})|=24.
\]
The explicit projective subgroups above already have these orders. Therefore the injective homomorphisms are isomorphisms, proving both the full-stabilizer statements and the assertion that every automorphism is projectively realized.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` performs exact symbolic substitutions in \(F_{\mathrm I}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{II}}\), forms the projective groups generated by the displayed matrices, and computes their element-order distributions. It checks orders \(8\) and \(24\) and profiles \(1^1 2^5 4^2\) and \(1^1 2^9 3^8 4^6\), respectively. No numerical approximation is used.

The upper-bound step is independent of the finite-group enumeration: it uses the published exact automorphism-group orders of the two smooth Enriques surfaces. The computation therefore certifies the new geometric realization, while the literature supplies the exhaustion argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Shimada--Veniani classify Enriques involutions on singular K3 surfaces of small discriminant, prove that the two quotients of the discriminant-\(7\) K3 surface have automorphism groups of orders \(8\) and \(24\), and in Section 6 give the explicit sextic equations used here. They explicitly describe their automorphism result as lattice-theoretic and state that a more geometric description on a projective model would be useful.

The abstract group structures are not claimed as new. Kondō's classification and later work of Brandhorst--Shimada identify type I with a dihedral group of order \(8\) and type II with \(S_4\). The refinement here is that, for the particular most-algebraic sextic equations of Shimada--Veniani, every automorphism is already induced by a projective linear transformation, with concrete generators.

Focused searches of published-finding corpus for the exact sextics, the type-I/type-II projective stabilizers, the parameters \(\alpha=15/16\) and \(\alpha=63\), and the phrase "all automorphisms realized projectively" found no implication-equivalent published finding.

## Limitations

This result does not classify projective stabilizers across the full one-dimensional families of Nikulin--Kondō type I and II surfaces. It does not claim that the abstract identifications \(D_8\) and \(S_4\) are new. It also does not identify which lattice-theoretic generator in the published Borcherds description corresponds to each displayed projective matrix.

The principal originality risk is an older or unindexed geometric treatment of these same sextic equations that may already write the automorphisms projectively. Searches located the classical abstract classification and later lattice/cone descriptions, but no such equation-level realization.
