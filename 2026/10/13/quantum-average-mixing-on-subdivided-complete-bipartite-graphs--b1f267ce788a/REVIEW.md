# Scientific assessment: subdivided complete bipartite graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is proved for all positive integers \(a,b\). The argument derives the four singular sectors (including zero), treats the only nonzero-eigenvalue collision at \(a=b\) by combining its projectors before taking Schur squares, and obtains a sum of positive-semidefinite matrices. Every zero-mode equation is explicitly solved for the five small-part regimes. The exact checker reconstructs the average independently by polynomial spectral projectors and verifies nine representative instances, including both coincident- and distinct-singular-value regimes. The checker is supplementary and does not prove the infinite classification.

## Originality — PASS (with stated residual risks)

Godsil's foundational work supplies the average-projector identity and path cases. Coutinho–Godsil–Guo–Zhan give the commutant/rank formulation and a simple-spectrum bipartite bound; their bound does not apply as a classification of this repeated-spectrum family. Coutinho and collaborators establish general exact computation for integral Hamiltonians, but no dimension-uniform formula of this kind in the inspected treatment. Chen's thesis provides spectral subdivision relations and studies the Cesàro limit for a different discrete-time walk, rather than the continuous-time adjacency Hamiltonian used here. A prior complete-graph subdivision result concerns \(S(K_n)\), not the family \(S(K_{a,b})\). The two smallest path cases are explicitly preexisting. Search is incomplete by nature, and an equivalent unpublished or hidden published specialization of a broader theory cannot be ruled out.

## Value — PASS

The calculation isolates a sharp and natural structural threshold: subdivided bicliques have full-rank long-time quantum transport exactly when neither original part has size one or two. Beyond a qualitative threshold, it computes all six boundary nullity regimes, identifies the actual kernel directions and provides a universal exact rational matrix. This distinguishes multiplicity-induced rank defects from full-rank two-parameter interiors and supplies reproducible data for a canonical incidence-graph operation.
