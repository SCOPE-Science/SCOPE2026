# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Honzik states that Rado's Conjecture implies \(\mathfrak c\le\omega_2\). Pelayo-Gómez proves in ZFC that \(\mathfrak d=\omega_1\) implies \(\mathfrak a=\omega_1\), and explicitly derives \(\mathfrak c\le\omega_2\Rightarrow\mathfrak a\le\mathfrak d\). Their composition proves \(\mathrm{RC}\Rightarrow\mathfrak a\le\mathfrak d\). In Honzik's consistency model, \(\mathfrak d=\omega_1\), so Pelayo-Gómez forces \(\mathfrak a=\omega_1\); the remaining displayed invariants are exactly those stated by Honzik.

## Originality

PASS. The Pelayo-Gómez primary text checked for the theorem and continuum-\(\omega_2\) corollary contains no Rado-Conjecture application, while Honzik's public statement predates the new \(\mathfrak d=\omega_1\Rightarrow\mathfrak a=\omega_1\) theorem and does not list \(\mathfrak a\) in its advertised random-algebra pattern. Searches combining Rado's Conjecture with the almost-disjointness number, maximal almost disjoint families, \(\mathfrak d<\mathfrak a\), and \(\mathfrak a\le\mathfrak d\) found no equivalent published statement.

Residual risk: Honzik's full text could not be retrieved through the available primary-source route, so an uninspected passage might determine \(\mathfrak a\) in the specific forcing model by another argument. This does not affect the global new implication obtained by combining his continuum bound with Pelayo-Gómez's later theorem.

## Value

PASS. Honzik explicitly frames his result as a new cardinal-invariant pattern compatible with Rado's Conjecture. The new deduction both excludes a natural outstanding pattern, \(\mathfrak d<\mathfrak a\), from every model of Rado's Conjecture and completes the concrete random-algebra pattern by fixing the previously unstated MAD invariant. It is therefore a motivated structural consequence of two recent advances rather than an arbitrary substitution.

## Closest literature and limitations

Honzik (2026) is the closest source on Rado's Conjecture and cardinal invariants; Pelayo-Gómez (2026) is the decisive source on the \(\mathfrak a\)-versus-\(\mathfrak d\) inequality. Older Rado-Conjecture literature supplies compactness and forcing context but does not contain the 2026 theorem needed for this deduction.

The consistency statement inherits Honzik's strongly compact cardinal hypothesis, and no claim is made that Rado's Conjecture fixes either \(\mathfrak a\) or \(\mathfrak d\) separately.
