# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The centralizer condition forces a cyclewise partial injection with independent rotation phases; nilpotency is exactly the absence of directed cycles on the cycle labels. A labeled directed path of length \(j\) contributes \(j-1\) arrows, \(\ell^{j-1}\) rotation weights, and \(\ell(j-1)\) domain points. The exponential formula gives all rank/index coefficients, while the path-size composition criterion proves the rank support and sharp bound. The independent enumeration program tests all partial injections for each cycle type on at most six points and a separate eight-point family. The mathematical proof, not finite sampling, establishes the infinite families.

## Originality: PASS, with recorded literature-access uncertainty
Gomes–Howie (1987) supply the path/nilpotency-index theorem and are not displaced. Konieczny's 2013 Theorem 3.4 gives a broader cyclewise centralizer membership criterion, but no joint enumeration in the read sections. The 2004 enumerative papers cover standard unconditioned Lah-number counts; the present theorem is a weighted, bounded-index joint distribution **inside arbitrary permutation centralizers** with complete rank support. The 2020 finite-centralizer paper concerns precisely related centralizers but was available only through its abstract, not its full text; equivalent coverage in that article or the inaccessible 2004 full texts remains possible. Search absence is not proof of novelty.

## Value: PASS
The exact joint distribution and rank support are natural finite inverse-semigroup invariants: they describe the nilpotent filtration and domain-rank strata of the centralizer of *every* permutation, not a hand-picked numerical slice. The formula disentangles the effects of cycle lengths, multiplicities, nilpotency bound and rank, and yields sharp attainable ranks, including cases with missing intermediate ranks. These are nontrivial refinements of the known whole-monoid nilpotent counts and the structural centralizer classification.

## Limits and closest literature
The result assumes a full permutation and a partial **injective** commuting map. It does not cover centralizers of general nonpermutation partial maps. The 2013 and 1987 publisher PDFs were materially inspected. The 2020 and 2004 relevant full papers remained inaccessible in the available reading routes; their abstracts were used only to identify scope and risk, never to assert exhaustive exclusion.
