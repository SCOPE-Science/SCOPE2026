# Scientific review: rank-one derivations of bilinear-form Leibniz algebras

## Correctness — PASS

The one-dimensional derived ideal forces every rank-one derivation to annihilate that ideal. The noncentral rank-one derivation identity forces the spanning image direction to lie in the radical of the symmetrization of the defining nondegenerate bilinear form, and conversely this condition is sufficient. The proof counts all projective directions and the central fiber and then uses Frobenius linearization of the diagonal function in characteristic two. Its exact formula counts distinct maps, not choices of representatives. Eleven directly enumerated finite examples over \(\mathbf F_2\), \(\mathbf F_3\), and \(\mathbf F_4\) validate coefficients and the characteristic boundary; direct enumeration is corroboration rather than a proof of universal scope. The argument covers arbitrary fields and all dimensions at least two.

## Originality — PASS

Mancini and La Rosa, *Two-step nilpotent Leibniz algebras* (arXiv:2106.12904, first public 2021-06-24), classify the algebra structures in terms of bilinear forms. La Rosa and Mancini, *Derivations of two-step nilpotent algebras* (arXiv:2301.11058), give general derivation matrices in selected canonical classes, principally in characteristic different from two; those matrices can imply some fixed odd-characteristic rank-one statements. The examined literature does not provide the uniform all-form rank-one derivation locus with finite-field fibers or the characteristic-two idempotent criterion. Semantic searches of published mathematical research records yielded nearby automorphism-scheme and adjoint-rank results, but none mathematically implied the new all-characteristic claim. Remaining risk: alternative terminology in bilinear-form isometry and derivation literature, and coverage not indexed in the searches.

## Value — PASS

The rank-one part of the derivation algebra is a complete, naturally motivated structural stratum. The criterion identifies when minimal-rank derivations are square-zero or idempotent, and the exact finite-field census shows a sharp transition absent in characteristic different from two. Two characteristic-two forms with equal symmetrization radical can have different idempotent counts depending on whether the diagonal restriction vanishes, so the result adds a meaningful discriminating invariant.

## Scope and limits

Only nondegenerate bilinear forms with a one-dimensional derived ideal and rank-one derivations are treated. The result does not count higher-rank derivations or classify underlying Leibniz algebras up to isomorphism. The accompanying verification is same-model only. Published literature may contain previously unrecognized equivalent formulations; originality is assessed against inspected sources and actual queries, not established by their absence alone.
