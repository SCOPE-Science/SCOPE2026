# Scientific review: rank spectra of square-zero derivations on truncated polynomial algebras

## Correctness — PASS
For every field of characteristic two, the derivations of \(k[t]/(t^n)\) are uniquely parametrized by their value on \(t\). For even and odd adjacent lengths, the two coefficient normalizations each give a unique pair \((E,H)\in(k[x]/(x^s))^2\). The equation \(D^2=0\) reduces, with no missing characteristic-dependent terms, to \(EH=H^2=0\). Image-pivot valuations give the same rank formula \(s-\min(v_x(E),v_x(H))\) in both lengths. The valuation split at \(\lceil s/2\rceil\) yields the two coefficient regimes, the zero term, and the total. The proof works for all \(s\ge1\); the standalone finite-field matrix enumerator is an independent corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The original text of Cong Chen, *Nilpotent varieties of some finite dimensional restricted Lie algebras* (arXiv:1910.00954v1, 2019), discusses restricted Lie algebras and nilpotent varieties; the materially inspected Zassenhaus-envelope analysis works in characteristic greater than three rather than giving the characteristic-two ordinary one-variable truncated polynomial derivation spectrum. The original text of Alexander Premet, *Regular derivations of truncated polynomial rings* (arXiv:1405.2426v3, 2014), concerns regular derivations and characteristic greater than two. None of their inspected theorems implies the finite-field two-regime square-zero rank distribution or adjacent-length bijection. Broader semantic, exact-phrase and algebra-alias literature searches, plus checks of published mathematical records, yielded no matching implication. Nevertheless an equivalent result may occur in classical restricted Witt-algebra literature or as an unstated corollary; a search failure alone does not establish novelty.

## Value — PASS
A square-zero derivation is a natural point of the restricted-power nilpotent variety of a derivation Lie algebra. The complete finite-field rank polynomial for every embedding length is a nontrivial arithmetic refinement, and the equality between neighboring truncations is a structural bijection rather than an isolated count. The theorem identifies exactly where two valuation regimes meet and supplies closed multiplicities, which are useful for comparing restricted-power strata over finite fields.

## Limits of this review
The formulas do not classify conjugation orbits, higher nilpotence exponents, or multivariate truncations. The standalone checker covers finitely many small fields and lengths. Possible equivalent prior formulations and human independent assessment remain outstanding.
