# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof diagonalizes the two-particle hypercube Hamiltonian in binary characters, decomposes into total-momentum sectors, and counts detector-visible energies. For unequal rational speeds, collision classes are primitive coprime lattice chains, yielding the exact finite binomial deficit; the nondecaying subspace equals the loss-free Hamiltonian eigenspace by dissipativity. Independent dense position-space spectra and exact integer-grid enumeration corroborate the analytic result. The argument assumes positive loss at every coincidence vertex and does not give capture times.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority risk
The 2007 two-particle meeting problem (arXiv:0705.1985) concerns discrete-time walks on a line. Carson–Loke–Wang (arXiv:1511.04828) analyze interacting walker entanglement. Thiel et al. (arXiv:1906.08112) provide general bright/dark detection principles, but not the sharp binary-cube, full-coincidence rank or rational-rate hierarchy. Targeted mathematical-record and literature comparisons found no equivalent count. The first two source comparisons did not inspect full papers, and an unlocated broader theorem could imply this result; priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS
A complete arithmetic stratification of two-particle reaction ranks on all hypercubes is a motivated structural result. The binomial onset at cube dimension equal to the numerator-plus-denominator of the reduced speed parameter distinguishes a whole family of mobility resonances from the known equal-speed resonance.

## Scientific limits
Two distinguishable noninteracting walkers, homogeneous positive real hopping, and complete strictly positive coincidence absorption are essential. Sitewise reaction probabilities, disorder, nonuniform hopping, finite-time stability, and incomplete detector arrangements are not classified.
