# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The hazards are explicit, strictly positive, real-analytic, and sum to \(1\). The cumulative-hazard identity gives the exact exponential minimum. The alternating intervals give explicit bounds proving \(\liminf R_i(x)/x=0\) for each factor, while complementary intervals prove properness. The exponential-moment lower bound then proves heavy-tailedness for every positive moment parameter.

## Originality
PASS. The closest source, arXiv:2505.08954, proves universal existence but uses alternating frozen cumulative-risk functions. The follow-up arXiv:2603.06452 characterizes segmented heavy tails and gives piecewise constructions; its inspected text does not provide analytic positive hazards. The original 2023 AIMS Mathematics example uses alternating factorial-interval tail formulas. Focused semantic searches for smooth, analytic, positive-hazard, density, and exact-exponential-minimum formulations found no covering result.

Residual risk: an unindexed or unpublished equivalent analytic regularization may exist. The claim is therefore limited to the explicit regularity refinement and does not assert priority over the general minimum-factorization phenomenon.

## Value
PASS. The construction clarifies the mathematical meaning of the newly emphasized segmented-tail condition: it is compatible with full local analytic regularity and strictly positive hazards. It removes several plausible artifacts of the earlier constructions while preserving an exact canonical light-tailed minimum.
