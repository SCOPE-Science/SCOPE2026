# Reciprocal-series coordinates split rational-normal secant charts

## Finding

Let \(C_d\subset\mathbb P^d_{\mathbb C}\) be the rational normal curve with coordinates \([a_0:\cdots:a_d]=[1:t:\cdots:t^d]\), and let \(\Sigma_{r,d}=\sigma_r(C_d)\) be the closure of the union of spans of \(r\) points. For every pair of integers \(r\ge2\) and \(d\ge2r\), the entire affine chart \(a_0\ne0\) has an explicit **scheme-theoretic algebraic product decomposition**

\[
\Sigma_{r,d}\cap D_+(a_0)\;\cong\;\mathbb A^1\times\operatorname{Cone}(\Sigma_{r-1,d-2}),
\]

where the cone is the standard affine cone in \(\mathbb A^{d-1}\), and \(\Sigma_{r-1,d-2}\subset\mathbb P^{d-2}\) is the corresponding smaller rational-normal secant variety. The identification is **polynomial in both directions**; no completion, normalization, blowup, or removal of a smaller locus is needed. It identifies \(C_d\cap D_+(a_0)\) with the line obtained by setting all cone coordinates to zero. Thus, at every point of \(C_d\), the completed local ring of \(\Sigma_{r,d}\) is the formal completion of a smooth one-dimensional factor times the affine-cone vertex of \(\Sigma_{r-1,d-2}\).

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). The secant indexing \(\sigma_r\) means spans of \(r\), rather than \(r+1\), points. We use the classical scheme-theoretic catalecticant presentation: for \(d\ge2r\), the homogeneous ideal of \(\Sigma_{r,d}\) is generated by all \((r+1)\)-minors of

\[
H=(a_{i+j})_{0\le i\le r,\ 0\le j\le d-r}.
\]

On \(a_0=1\), define the truncated generating series

\[
A(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^d a_j z^j,\qquad
C(z)=A(z)^{-1}\pmod{z^{d+1}}=1+\sum_{j=1}^d c_jz^j.
\]

Set \(u=a_1\) and \(b_j=-c_j\) for \(2\le j\le d\). These are coordinates on \(\mathbb A^1\times\mathbb A^{d-1}\).

## Proof

**Polynomial coordinate change.** The recursion for coefficients of a reciprocal series reads

\[
c_n=-\sum_{k=1}^n a_k c_{n-k}\quad(1\le n\le d),\qquad c_0=1.
\]

Consequently, \(c_n=-a_n+P_n(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1})\); the coordinates \((a_1,c_2,\ldots,c_d)\) are triangular polynomial coordinates. Inverting the reciprocal series a second time gives their polynomial inverse, and \(c_1=-u\).

**Exact matrix identity.** Define square lower-unitriangular Toeplitz matrices of the row and column sizes of \(H\):

\[
L_{ik}=\begin{cases}c_{i-k}&i\ge k,\\0&i<k,\end{cases}
\qquad
R_{j\ell}=\begin{cases}c_{j-\ell}&j\ge\ell,\\0&j<\ell.\end{cases}
\]

Here the indices start at zero, with \(0\le i,k\le r\) and \(0\le j,\ell\le d-r\). Treating the coefficients of any formal series beyond order \(d\) as independent placeholders, the bivariate Hankel generating identity is

\[
\sum_{i,j\ge0}a_{i+j}s^it^j
=\frac{sA(s)-tA(t)}{s-t}.
\]

Multiplication by \(C(s)C(t)\), followed by coefficient extraction in the range \(i+j\le d\), yields

\[
\sum_{i,j\ge0}(LHR^{\mathsf T})_{ij}s^it^j
=\frac{sC(t)-tC(s)}{s-t}
=1+\sum_{i,j\ge1}b_{i+j}s^it^j
\]

within the finite rectangular index range. Therefore the following is an **exact identity over the polynomial coordinate ring**:

\[
LHR^{\mathsf T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&K\end{pmatrix},
\qquad K=(b_{i+j})_{1\le i\le r,\ 1\le j\le d-r}.
\]

Both \(L\) and \(R\) are invertible over that ring, with determinant one. Therefore the ideals of \((r+1)\)-minors of \(H\) and the displayed block diagonal matrix coincide. The ideal of \((r+1)\)-minors of \(\operatorname{diag}(1,K)\) equals the ideal of \(r\)-minors of \(K\): every \(r\)-minor of \(K\) is obtained by adjoining the leading \(1\), while minors not meeting this leading entry lie in the same ideal by cofactor expansion.

Reindexing \(K\) by subtracting one from both row and column indices gives the Hankel matrix

\[
K=(b_{(i'+j')+2})_{0\le i'\le r-1,\ 0\le j'\le d-r-1}.
\]

Its \(r\)-minors are precisely the homogeneous equations of \(\Sigma_{r-1,d-2}\), now written in the affine cone coordinates \(b_2,\ldots,b_d\). The variable \(u\) is unconstrained. This proves the product **as schemes**, not merely on points or after localization at a smooth point.

**The curve and completed germs.** On the curve's chart, \(a_j=u^j\), hence \(A(z)\equiv(1-uz)^{-1}\) and \(C(z)=1-uz\) modulo \(z^{d+1}\). Thus \(b_j=0\) for all \(j\ge2\). Every point of \(C_d\) can be transported to this chart's distinguished point by an automorphism of the rational normal curve induced by \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb C)\), giving the completed-local-ring assertion.

## Verification

The standalone dependency-free script `checker.py` reads the exact integer coefficients, constructs the reciprocal coordinate change, multiplies the triangular matrices explicitly, and tests both the block identity and equality of matrix ranks by exact rational Gaussian elimination. Reversing the reciprocal series also tests polynomial invertibility. The recorded output `checker_output.txt` reports **612** successful integral specializations, **22,168** checked transformed entries, with \(4\le d\le14\) and \(2\le r\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor\). This finite verification is not used as an infinite proof; the preceding generating-function and determinantal-ideal arguments prove the theorem for every stated \((r,d)\).

## Relationship to prior work

The rational-normal secant catalecticant equations are classical (see Conca–Mostafazadehfard–Singh–Varbaro, introductory discussion, and the literature cited there). Vermeire established qualitative normality and smoothness away from lower secant strata for curves, while Ein–Niu–Park, Proposition 5.10, determines pointwise **multiplicities** of secant varieties; their multiplicity formulas are not claimed as new here. The product decomposition is a stronger explicit isomorphism of **the entire affine chart and its coordinate ring**, rather than equality of dimensions, Hilbert–Samuel multiplicities, tangent cones, or smoothness loci. No matching algebraic chart-product statement was located in the pertinent sections inspected, but a historically known equivalent coordinate reduction remains a genuine possibility.

## Limitations

The isomorphism is asserted only for the standard chart \(a_0\ne0\), not as a global projective product, and only in the specified nontrivial range \(d\ge2r\ge4\). It does not classify completed local rings at all points of higher secant strata or give new secant degrees. It relies on the established determinantal presentation of rational-normal secants. An exhaustive historical originality guarantee is not claimed, and the mathematical review is not an independent expert certification.
