# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The five axioms are stated with full adjunction direction and the product Gödel residual. From the Galois unit, interiority and meet Frobenius law one gets an extensive idempotent closure \(g\); the other axioms give the shared fixed subalgebra and force its floor/ceiling operations. The axis-point case uses implication into bottom to generate both complete axes and hence a rectangle. In the axis-free case the two Frobenius laws force the ceiling of both unit axes to be top, yielding injective coordinate projections and strictly synchronized cores. Explicit case splits establish all axioms for both candidate classes. Vandermonde's identity supplies the complete count. Two differently organized exhaustive finite verifiers agree for specified small pairs; no finite data are used to infer the unrestricted theorem.

## Originality: PASS with residual risk

Wang--Shi--Wang (arXiv:2609.09529v1) introduces the Frobenius-adjoint algebraic setting. Castaño et al. (arXiv:2006.10180v1, Lemma 1.1 and the discussion immediately afterward) explicitly establish the *older* shared-quantifier-range and relatively-complete-subalgebra framework. This review does **not** credit those ingredients as new. The new point is the precise rectangle/strict-diagonal dichotomy plus explicit two-chain-product enumeration. Inspected primary literature and searches of published mathematical findings did not locate that specialization. Because the older general criterion is powerful, an earlier direct specialization could exist; absent records cannot rule it out. Independent literature validation is outstanding.

## Value: PASS

The smallest non-chain finite Gödel products are a natural first benchmark for Frobenius/Galois expansions. Their possible cross-coordinate behavior is nontrivial: cores may factor into independent coordinate choices or synchronize the two chains, and these mechanisms contribute different closed-form counting terms. The result is an exact all-size classification useful for finite semantic model generation, and is not a small arbitrary numerical slice.

## Scope and risk

Only \(m,n\geq2\), the coordinate-labelled two-chain product with meet as multiplication, and the five stated axioms are covered. The result does not count isomorphism types or classify products with three factors. Older equivalent formulas could have been presented under monadic Gödel/Heyting terminology. The code provides finite cross-checks, not independent mathematical review.
