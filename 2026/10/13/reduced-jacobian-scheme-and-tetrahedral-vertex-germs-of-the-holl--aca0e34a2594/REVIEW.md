With P=af, Q=be, R=cd, the homogeneous determinant equals (P−Q−R)²−4QR. At a=1, w=f−be−cd is a polynomial coordinate with polynomial inverse. The chart derivatives in the new coordinates generate (w,cde,bde,bce,bcd); the change-of-variables Jacobian is invertible, and Euler's homogeneous identity supplies the missing homogeneous derivative because F itself lies in this ideal. A monomial in b,c,d,e vanishes on every coordinate two-plane iff its support has at least three variables. This gives the six-prime decomposition as an equality of ideals, ruling out nilpotents and embedded components there. Index permutations are transitive on the six edges, so the computation covers all of projective space. On a≠0, setting one variable from each of the complementary pairs {b,e},{c,d} to zero gives four coordinate planes; setting b=e=0 or c=d=0 gives the chart of one of the indicated quadrics. Cycling the complementary pairs exhausts the eight distinct planes and three quadrics; each quadric is a smooth rank-four quadric on its coordinate P³. All components are surfaces of degrees 1 and 2 respectively, so the top-dimensional degree is 14. The multiplicity is 6. An independent exact replay formed all six homogeneous partials and compared Gröbner bases over Q on each of the six charts, then checked the series and multiplicity. This is compatible with base change to C and complements the all-degree argument.
