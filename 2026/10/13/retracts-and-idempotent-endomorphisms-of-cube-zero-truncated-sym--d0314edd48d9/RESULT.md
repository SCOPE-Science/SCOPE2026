# Retracts and idempotent endomorphisms of cube-zero truncated symmetric algebras

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field, \(r\ge 1\) an integer, and
\[
A_r(K)=K[x_1,\ldots,x_r]/(x_1,\ldots,x_r)^3.
\]
All homomorphisms below are unital \(K\)-algebra maps. A retraction is an endomorphism \(f\) satisfying \(f^2=f\), and a retract subalgebra means its image \(f(A_r(K))\), counted as an actual embedded subalgebra rather than only by isomorphism type.

Write \(V=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\), \(W=\mathfrak m^2\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\), \(s=\binom{r+1}{2}\), and \(s_k=\binom{k+1}{2}\). Every algebra endomorphism has a unique expression on generators
\[
f(v)=P(v)+Q(v),\qquad P:V\to V,\quad Q:V\to W.
\]
It is idempotent precisely when
\[
P^2=P,\qquad Q=QP+(\operatorname{Sym}^2P)Q.
\]
For \(U=\operatorname{im}P\) of dimension \(k\), and \(T=\ker P\), the second equation is equivalent to
\[
Q(U)\subset\ker(\operatorname{Sym}^2P),\qquad
Q(T)\subset\operatorname{Sym}^2U.
\]
Thus the fiber of quadratic terms above each rank-\(k\) linear projection has dimension
\[
d_{r,k}=k(s-s_k)+(r-k)s_k.
\]
Every rank-\(k\) retract subalgebra is isomorphic over \(K\) to \(A_k(K)\) (with \(A_0(K)=K\)); hence its \(K\)-dimension is \(1+k+s_k\). Every such image is uniquely specified by a \(k\)-plane \(U\subset V\) and a linear map \(U\to W/\operatorname{Sym}^2 U\).

For every prime power \(q\) and every \(0\le k\le r\), the **numbers of retraction maps and of distinct retract subalgebras**, respectively, whose image has cotangent dimension \(k\), are
\[
\boxed{I_{r,k}(q)={r\brack k}_q\,q^{\,k(r-k)+d_{r,k}}},
\qquad
\boxed{R_{r,k}(q)={r\brack k}_q\,q^{\,k(s-s_k)}}.
\]
Here \({r\brack k}_q=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(q^{r-j}-1)/(q^{k-j}-1)\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient. Each embedded rank-\(k\) retract is the image of exactly
\[
\boxed{q^{\,(r-k)(k+s_k)}}
\]
different retraction maps. In particular, the total idempotent-endomorphism count is \(\sum_{k=0}^r I_{r,k}(q)\), with precisely one map for each of \(k=0\) and \(k=r\).

The rank-\(k\) retraction locus is a smooth geometrically irreducible vector bundle over the rank-\(k\) idempotent-linear-map variety; its dimension is \(2k(r-k)+d_{r,k}\). The space of embedded rank-\(k\) retract images is a vector bundle over \(\operatorname{Gr}(k,V)\) of dimension \(k(r-k)+k(s-s_k)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The algebra is the full total-degree-three truncation of a *commutative polynomial ring*; no restriction is imposed on the characteristic, algebraic closedness, or finiteness of \(K\). The formulas counting maps and images assume \(K=\mathbf F_q\). The ranks \(k\) include \(0\) (augmentation) and \(r\) (identity), and the number \(r\) is any positive integer. No quotient by automorphisms is taken. Symmetric squares are ordinary symmetric powers, including in characteristic two; no averaging or division by two occurs.

## Proof
**Step 1 (normal form).** The unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) consists of the nilpotent elements of \(A_r(K)\), so each endomorphism preserving \(K\) sends \(\mathfrak m\) into itself. As a vector space, \(A_r(K)=K\oplus V\oplus W\) with \(VW=W^2=0\) and \(W=\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\). Every choice of \(P:V\to V\) and \(Q:V\to W\) extends uniquely to a unital algebra endomorphism: a product of three images in \(\mathfrak m\) is zero, and the original defining relations are exactly those triple products. On \(W\), this map acts as \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(P)\); quadratic parts of the images cannot affect degree two. Applying it twice to \(V\) yields \(P^2\) as the degree-one part and \(QP+(\operatorname{Sym}^2P)Q\) as the degree-two part, giving the displayed necessary-and-sufficient equations.

**Step 2 (quadratic fibers).** If \(P^2=P\), write \(V=U\oplus T\) where \(P\) is identity on \(U\) and zero on \(T\). Then
\[
W=\operatorname{Sym}^2U\oplus(U\cdot T)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^2T
\]
and \(\operatorname{Sym}^2P\) is the projection onto the first summand. Restricting \(Q=QP+(\operatorname{Sym}^2P)Q\) to \(U\) gives \((\operatorname{Sym}^2P)Q|_U=0\); restricting to \(T\) gives \(Q|_T=(\operatorname{Sym}^2P)Q|_T\). The choices are therefore independent vector spaces of dimensions \(k(s-s_k)\) and \((r-k)s_k\). This proves \(d_{r,k}\) over every field, including characteristic two.

**Step 3 (images and embedded retracts).** For any such \(f\), let \(B=f(A_r(K))\). Its degree-two portion equals \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\), and its degree-one projection is \(U\). Moreover, it is generated by \(k\) elements of the form \(u+\beta(u)\), with \(u\in U\) and \(\beta:U\to W\); the class of \(\beta(u)\) in \(W/\operatorname{Sym}^2U\) depends only on \(B\). Thus the image is determined by \((U,\overline\beta)\). Conversely, for an arbitrary \(U\subset V\) and an arbitrary \(\overline\beta:U\to W/\operatorname{Sym}^2U\), choose a complement \(T\), set \(P\) to the projection with image \(U\) and kernel \(T\), lift \(\overline\beta\) uniquely into \(\ker\operatorname{Sym}^2P\), and set \(Q|_T=0\). Step 2 shows this defines a retraction with the prescribed image. Different pairs have different degree-one parts or different graphs, hence different images. Their generators have independent leading linear parts, and their quadratic products span \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\); no relation of total degree less than three can arise, so \(B\cong A_k(K)\).

**Step 4 (finite-field counts).** Over \(\mathbf F_q\), there are \({r\brack k}_q\) choices of \(U\). For each, complements \(T\) correspond to maps from a fixed complementary \((r-k)\)-space to \(U\), hence number \(q^{k(r-k)}\). Above each projection, Step 2 gives \(q^{d_{r,k}}\) quadratic choices: this proves \(I_{r,k}(q)\). Independently, each \(U\) supports exactly \(q^{k(s-s_k)}\) graph maps \(\overline\beta\), yielding \(R_{r,k}(q)\). For fixed \((U,\overline\beta)\), each choice of complement \(T\) uniquely determines the admissible \(Q|_U\) lifting the graph; \(Q|_T\) is arbitrary in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\), giving \(q^{k(r-k)+(r-k)s_k}\) maps per image. Multiplying by \(R_{r,k}(q)\) reproduces \(I_{r,k}(q)\). For \(k=0,r\), these expressions equal one.

**Step 5 (geometry).** The rank-\(k\) projections form the homogeneous variety \(\operatorname{GL}(V)/(\operatorname{GL}_k\times\operatorname{GL}_{r-k})\), parametrizing ordered complementary subspaces; it is smooth and geometrically irreducible of dimension \(2k(r-k)\). Step 2 exhibits the quadratic terms as the kernel of a constant-rank vector-bundle homomorphism, with rank \(d_{r,k}\). Step 3 identifies retract images with total space of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal U,\operatorname{Sym}^2V/\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal U)\) over the Grassmannian; its fiber rank is \(k(s-s_k)\). The asserted geometry follows.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 file `verify.py` uses exact finite-field linear algebra. First it exhaustively enumerates all rank-\(r\) linear maps \(P\) and solves the full quadratic constraint matrix to obtain the number of \(Q\)'s, comparing it with the formula for \((r,q)=(1,2),(2,2),(2,3),(2,5),(3,2),(3,3)\). Second, for \((r,q)=(2,2),(2,3)\), it exhaustively enumerates all possible \(P,Q\), verifies the actual full degree-two endomorphism square, computes canonical images from the full induced matrix, and checks both the map and image counts. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` and `DIRECT_VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for arbitrary fields and ranks.

For example, when \(r=2\), the rank-one retraction count equals \((q+1)q^4\), while the number of rank-one retract subalgebras equals \((q+1)q^2\). For \(q=2\) these numbers are \(48\) and \(12\), and for \(q=3\) they are \(324\) and \(36\). The total idempotent-endomorphism counts are therefore \(50\) and \(326\), respectively.

## Relationship to prior work
Gupta–Nagamine (2023, arXiv:2301.12681) establish a structural classification of retracts of Laurent polynomial rings over domains. Their Theorem 1.2 concerns reduced infinite-dimensional Laurent polynomial rings, not nilpotent Artinian truncations, and does not count idempotent maps or embedded retracts. Chakraborty–Dasgupta–Dutta–Gupta (2019, arXiv:1910.11023), especially Sections 5–6, study polynomial-ring retracts, polynomial fibrations, and general quotient-descent properties; their Lemma 6.1(ii) permits an existing polynomial retraction to descend modulo an ideal in its kernel but does not classify all endomorphisms of the cube-zero quotient. Chakraborty–Pal (2025, arXiv:2504.14382) classify special monomial/binomial retractions of untruncated polynomial rings and distinguish retraction maps sharing images. Their result has different hypotheses and does not imply the complete finite-field counts for the truncated local algebra. The present theorem treats *all* retractions of a specific natural Artinian local algebra, with characteristic-free normal forms, an embedded-image classification, and exact arithmetic fibers.

## Limitations
Only the truncation at total degree three is proved; no general truncation of degree four or higher is classified here. Counts refer to \(K\)-algebra maps fixing scalars and individual embedded subalgebras, not automorphism-orbit classes. The searches and full-text comparisons above do not rule out equivalent formulations under other terminology, especially via formal-smoothness or jet retraction theory. No independent verification or audit is claimed.
