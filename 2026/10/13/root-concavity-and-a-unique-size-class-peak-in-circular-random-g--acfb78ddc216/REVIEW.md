# Scientific review: radius profile of size-specific circular components

**Correctness — PASS.** Cyclic uniform spacings give a normalized simplex-volume formula with two lower-bounded exterior gaps and \(k-1\) upper-bounded interior gaps. Affine nesting of the convex slices and Brunn–Minkowski prove \((n-1)\)-root concavity for the full radius interval. A Vandermonde argument rules out a constant polynomial interval, so the maximizer is unique. Exact inclusion-exclusion and compact-uniform exponential limits yield \(nr_{n,k}\to\log((k+1)/2)\). The checker supplies rational identities and independent simulation, not infinite-domain certification.

**Originality — PASS, with substantial explicit prior coverage.** Huillet (2003) derives a joint circular-spacings law that covers the exact mean formula and conditional component sizes; these are not claimed as original. Decreusefond–Ferraz (2010, Section 4.2) address Poisson total cluster counts on a circle; Penrose–Yang (2022, introduction and main theorem sections) address component-size frequencies in different asymptotic regimes. Comparison of their statements and implications did not identify the finite-sample root-concavity and unique size-specific optimum at every radius. The potential for an implicit prior Brunn–Minkowski specialization remains a genuine historical risk; novelty is restricted to the complete root-concavity/optimization statement.

**Value — PASS.** A tunable connection radius controls both merging and formation of components of a prescribed vertex count. The theorem proves a global structural shape constraint for every finite sample size and each proper non-singleton component size, not just a few numerical modes. It also locates the unique optimal radius at the sharp \(1/n\) scale with a closed logarithmic constant. The result gives a full classification for a natural component-abundance observable; it is not a special-case recomputation of the classical total-number-of-gaps distribution.

**Scope and risks.** The model is exactly uniform points on a unit circle with shorter-arc adjacency, \(n\ge3\), \(2\le k<n\), and \(0<r<1/2\). The mean formula is classical implied data, not a new result. No claim is made for higher dimensions, growing \(k\), nonuniform sampling, or sample-path concavity. Independent historical-priority validation is not available.
