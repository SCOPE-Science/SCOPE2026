# Serial consistency and positive introspection preserve one-world completeness in weak closure semantics
## Finding

For every selection \(\Lambda\subseteq\{\mathsf M,\mathsf C,\mathsf N,\mathsf K,\mathsf D,\mathsf 4\}\), where \(\mathsf N\) is Nishimura's axiom \(\Box\top\) and no modal inference rule \(\mathsf E\) or \(\mathsf G\) is present, extend Nishimura's closure-frame conditions by \((\mathsf D)\): no \(\varphi\) and \(\neg\varphi\) both belong to \(\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\), and by \((\mathsf 4)\): \(\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\) implies \(\Box\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\). Then the Hilbert logic generated by the selected axiom schemata is strongly complete for the class of closure frames satisfying the corresponding selected conditions. Moreover, every non-theorem has a finite countermodel with exactly one world and a finite generator. Thus all \(2^6\) no-rule axiom selections retain the singleton finite-model property, including every combination containing both \(\mathsf D\) and Nishimura's \(\mathsf N\).

The point of the statement is that \(\mathsf D\), although its semantic condition is negative rather than Horn-like, does not force a multiworld canonical construction in the no-rule hierarchy. The proof uses a proof-consequence closure rather than an iterative syntactic closure.

## Assumptions and scope

A closure frame is a triple \((W,G,\operatorname{cl})\) in Nishimura's broad sense: \(W\) is nonempty, \(G(w)\) is a set of modal formulas, and \(\operatorname{cl}\) is an arbitrary map on sets of modal formulas; extensivity, monotonicity, and idempotence are not assumed.

For the four conditions already treated by Nishimura, use
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathsf M)&:\ \varphi\wedge\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\Rightarrow \varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w)),\\
(\mathsf C)&:\ \varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\Rightarrow \varphi\wedge\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w)),\\
(\mathsf N)&:\ \top\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w)),\\
(\mathsf K)&:\ \varphi,\varphi\to\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\Rightarrow\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Add
\[
(\mathsf D):\ \neg\bigl(\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\ \text{and}\ \neg\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\bigr)
\]
and
\[
(\mathsf 4):\ \varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w))\Rightarrow\Box\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(G(w)).
\]
These are exactly the membership conditions validating the axiom schemata \(\mathsf D:\neg(\Box\varphi\wedge\Box\neg\varphi)\) and \(\mathsf 4:\Box\varphi\to\Box\Box\varphi\). Kogure and Kurahashi use the same two membership conditions in their more restrictive closure-operator semantics.

Let \(L_\Lambda\) contain classical propositional logic, Modus Ponens, uniform substitution, and exactly the selected modal axiom schemata from \(\Lambda\). There is no modal inference rule such as replacement of equivalents or necessitation.

## Proof

We first prove strong completeness. Suppose \(\Gamma\nvdash_\Lambda\theta\). Extend \(\Gamma\cup\{\neg\theta\}\) to a maximally \(L_\Lambda\)-consistent set \(\Delta\) over the full modal language and put
\[
G=\{\chi:\Box\chi\in\Delta\}.
\]
For every set \(X\) of formulas define the proof-consequence closure
\[
\operatorname{cl}_\Lambda(X)=
\left\{\chi:\text{for some finite }X_0\subseteq X,\quad
L_\Lambda\vdash
\left(\bigwedge_{\xi\in X_0}\Box\xi\right)\to\Box\chi}\right\}.
\]
For \(X_0=\varnothing\), the displayed conjunction means \(\top\). This is a legitimate closure in Nishimura's broad sense because no closure-operator axioms are required.

At the single world with generator \(G\), one has the exact modal truth bridge
\[
\chi\in\operatorname{cl}_\Lambda(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Box\chi\in\Delta.
\]
The right-to-left implication uses the one-element support \(\{\chi\}\subseteq G\). For the converse, a finite witness \(X_0\subseteq G\) has every \(\Box\xi\) in \(\Delta\); Modus Ponens with the displayed theorem gives \(\Box\chi\in\Delta\). Together with the usual Boolean maximal-consistency argument, this gives the truth lemma for every modal formula. Hence the unique world forces every member of \(\Gamma\) and refutes \(\theta\).

It remains to check the selected frame conditions. Each check is a finite-support composition of the corresponding axiom. For example, witnesses for \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\) can be united; the \(\mathsf C\) axiom then yields a witness for \(\varphi\wedge\psi\). The arguments for \(\mathsf M\), \(\mathsf N\), and \(\mathsf K\) are identical in form. If \(\mathsf4\in\Lambda\) and a finite support proves \(\Box\varphi\), composition with \(\Box\varphi\to\Box\Box\varphi\) proves that \(\Box\varphi\) is itself in the closure.

For \(\mathsf D\), suppose both \(\varphi\) and \(\neg\varphi\) belonged to the closure. Unite their finite supports into \(X_0\subseteq G\). From the support one derives both \(\Box\varphi\) and \(\Box\neg\varphi\), while the \(\mathsf D\) axiom gives \(\neg(\Box\varphi\wedge\Box\neg\varphi)\). But every support formula \(\Box\xi\) lies in \(\Delta\), contradicting the consistency of \(\Delta\). Thus the semantic \(\mathsf D\) condition holds. This proves strong completeness for every selected combination.

For the finite-model property, start with a single non-theorem \(\theta\) and take a maximally consistent set only over Nishimura's finite augmented subformula set for \(\theta\). Let
\[
G_\theta=\{\chi\in\mathsf{Sub}^{*}(\theta):\Box\chi\in\Delta_\theta\}.
\]
Then \(G_\theta\) is finite. Use exactly the same proof-consequence closure above. The modal truth bridge is needed only for boxed formulas in the finite augmented subformula set; if closure membership gives a derivation of such a box from the finite support, maximal consistency forces that boxed formula into the finite set. The global frame-condition checks above use only the support boxes, all of which belong to the finite maximally consistent set. Therefore the resulting one-world frame has a finite generator, satisfies every selected condition, and refutes \(\theta\).

## Verification

The load-bearing point is the definition of \(\operatorname{cl}_\Lambda\). Nishimura explicitly permits an arbitrary set operator, so proof-consequence closure need not be shown extensive, monotone, or idempotent. Every semantic closure condition is checked only at the actual generator of the one-world frame.

The \(\mathsf D\) check does not assume that the closure itself preserves syntactic consistency. Instead, any hypothetical pair \(\varphi,\neg\varphi\) in the closure comes with two finite proof supports. Their union would make a finite subset of the maximally consistent set derive the conjunction forbidden by the \(\mathsf D\) axiom. This is the only non-Horn step.

The finite-model statement does not claim that \(\operatorname{cl}_\Lambda(G_\theta)\) is finite. Nishimura's definition of a finite closure model requires only finitely many worlds and a finite generator at every world; an infinite generated closure is allowed.

No computational experiment, bounded enumeration, or asymptotic inference is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Nishimura proves strong completeness and a singleton finite-model construction for all no-rule combinations of \(\mathsf M,\mathsf C,\mathsf N,\mathsf K\), and explicitly lists \(\mathsf D\) and \(\mathsf4\) among the first modal principles whose closure-semantics completeness remains to be extended. Nishimura also emphasizes that the closure operation is deliberately broader than an ordinary closure operator.

Kogure and Kurahashi earlier studied \(\mathsf D\) and \(\mathsf4\) in closure semantics for conjunctively closed provability predicates. Their framework requires an extensive, monotone, idempotent closure operator, and their symbol \(\mathsf N\) denotes the necessitation rule, not Nishimura's axiom \(\Box\top\). They prove broad completeness results for combinations of necessitation, conjunction closure, consistency principles, and \(\mathsf4\); when necessitation and \(\mathsf D\) occur together, their finite-model question is substantially different. Those results supply the natural semantic forms of \(\mathsf D\) and \(\mathsf4\) but do not imply the theorem here for Nishimura's arbitrary-operator, no-inference-rule hierarchy with \(\mathsf M\) and \(\mathsf K\).

Targeted searches for singleton closure completeness with \(\mathsf D\) and \(\mathsf4\), no-inference-rule weak modal closure semantics, and arbitrary-operator closure models did not locate a statement covering all selected combinations above.

## Limitations

The theorem deliberately excludes modal inference rules \(\mathsf E\) and \(\mathsf G\). It does not treat \(\mathsf T\), \(\mathsf B\), or \(\mathsf5\), whose natural semantic conditions may involve truth or other structure. The proof-consequence closure is not asserted to give a primitive-recursive decision procedure, and no complexity bound is claimed.

The theorem is semantic and proof-theoretic; it does not provide an arithmetical interpretation of these logics.
