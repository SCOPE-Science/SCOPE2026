# Review: sharp domination-polynomial log-concavity cutoff in trivially perfect graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The structural recursion is exact: every connected trivially perfect graph has a universal vertex; every disconnected one is a disjoint union of such connected graphs. Adding a universal vertex maps a polynomial \(Q\) to \(Q+z(1+z)^{n-1}\). Disjoint union maps component polynomials to their product. Exhausting integer partitions therefore exhausts every possible polynomial image rather than merely sampling graphs. The attached code replays the full exhaustive finite proof: \(230337\) order-15 polynomials all pass every log-concavity inequality, and among \(230337\) connected order-16 polynomials exactly one fails. The disconnected order-16 case is covered by the standard log-concavity-preserving convolution theorem because each component has smaller order and all domination polynomials have interval support. Independent graph-level brute force checks all labeled graphs through five vertices and all \(65536\) vertex subsets of the windmill witness. The failing vector and degree-two defect are independently reconstructed from the known windmill formula. Finite computation proves only this finite cutoff; the infinite windmill threshold follows from an exact inequality, not extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Omar's 2026 full-text paper proves log-concavity for threshold graphs, a proper subclass, but does not determine the sharp cutoff for all trivially perfect graphs or the exceptional polynomial image. Rather's 2025 full-text work already gives an explicit domination-polynomial formula for generalized friendship and clique-join graphs, which directly covers the exhibited windmill; the formula itself and the elementary degree-two computation are acknowledged as prior mathematical consequences. The new theorem requires an exhaustive and structurally complete comparison across *all* trivially perfect graphs through order 16 and identifies the unique failing coefficient vector. Other directly relevant published studies of domination polynomials for chain graphs or general-position polynomials for windmills do not imply this classification. Searches under trivially perfect, quasi-threshold, universal-vertex, windmill, and log-concavity terminology found no covering theorem. Search incompleteness, alternate terminology, and graph-polynomial collisions remain documented risks; uniqueness is not claimed for graph isomorphism types.

## Value — PASS
This closes a natural sharp boundary question prompted by the established threshold-graph log-concavity theorem and by open shape questions for clique-join domination polynomials. The result is more than a lone counterexample: it provides a complete order cutoff for an established hereditary graph class, a uniqueness classification of the first exceptional polynomial, positive unimodality throughout order 16, and an infinite three-blade obstruction family with an exact transition. The complete polynomial-image certificate is reusable for subsequent finite structural investigations. It does not purport to settle log-concavity or unimodality for all trivially perfect graphs in every order.

## Closest literature and limitations
Omar (arXiv:2601.14494v1) treats threshold graphs; Rather (DOI 10.61091/jcmcc126-19) already supplies the clique-join formula; Drange–Pilipczuk supplies the graph class recursion; Johnson–Goldschmidt treats log-concavity under convolution. No accessible relevant primary text stated the finite order-16 uniqueness conclusion. Residual risks include a pre-existing equivalent result under rooted-forest terminology and the lack of an independent external audit. The certificate is finite and auditable, not a mathematical classification at all orders.
