# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claimed policy dominates any history-adaptive competitor in an explicit monotone coupling of the two missing-vertex processes. Alternation of graph sides gives independent complete and initially-one-collected geometric coupon clocks; their integer completion times produce the two parity-specific exact CDFs. A gamma-function product for the geometric Laplace transforms gives the critical log-gamma law, including the no-steering boundary. Monotone integral estimates and a Lyapunov ratio establish the Gaussian component regime, and an independent continuous-mapping argument gives the non-Gaussian maximum-of-two-normal limit. The embedded standalone checker passed 906 finite-rational and transform checks; the universal claims are grounded in the analytical proof.

**Originality — PASS, with stated priority risks.** Bukh–Dubroff (arXiv:2605.18712v1) and the earlier complete treatments of time-dependent steering (arXiv:2006.02475 and arXiv:2412.13109) establish general cover-time bounds or algorithms, not the full sharp biclique CDF and the two fluctuation phases. Nakata–Kubo (DMTCS, 2006) is particularly close in the Gumbel-to-normal vocabulary but uses recursively acquired bonuses, not an independent per-step chance of targeting an unvisited vertex; its theorem does not imply the stated finite law or the negative-log-gamma crossover. Targeted mathematical-record searches, searches under equivalent coupon terminology, and existing claims revealed no matching theorem. An unindexed equivalent formulation remains possible. The full text of Tung–Ueltzen (arXiv:2607.25016v1) was not accessible; its publicly described result concerns general graph decompositions and improves cover-time *bounds*, with no explicit biclique distribution in the accessible description. This access risk is not treated as a complete-text noncoverage proof.

**Value — PASS.** The problem has a natural role in the recently active study of optimal intermittent steering of graph exploration. This complete family-level distribution gives a rare exact stochastic optimum against all adaptive policies and reveals a sharp \(\varepsilon\asymp1/m\) transition that expected-time order bounds cannot detect. The two interlaced coupon processes produce a maximum of two log-gamma limits, followed by a maximum-of-two-Gaussians law: a distinction essential for cover-time quantiles. The finding does not extend optimality to general graphs, and it does not claim a Gaussian limit for the whole cover time.

**Limits and risks.** The checker tests finitely many graphs and values, not the limiting assertions by enumeration. The model presumes independent steering opportunities and uniform uncontrolled neighbors. The strongest residual priority risk is an unindexed probability-ordering theorem or previously overlooked exact solution of a forced-discovery coupon process with the same affine hazards.
