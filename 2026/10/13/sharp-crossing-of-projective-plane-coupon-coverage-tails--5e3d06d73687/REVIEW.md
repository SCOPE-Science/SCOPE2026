# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The claimed probabilities refer to independent block draws with replacement, and the exact comparison is made at each integer horizon \(t\ge0\). Standard missing-point inclusion-exclusion reduces survival probabilities to finite signed sums of powers of block-avoidance counts. Canonical projective-coordinate enumeration exhausts all \(127\) nonempty point subsets for \(q=2\) and all \(8191\) for \(q=3\). Exact arithmetic reproduces the signed coefficient tables, the rational values at horizons \(4,5,6\), and the two strict positive threshold inequalities. A decreasing-power argument proves the remaining *infinite* range; finite tests are not treated as an infinite proof. The two cases have been reconstructed from their assumptions through the claimed boundary strictness; no unverified nonstandard lemma is required. Independent scientific audit remains outstanding.

## Originality: PASS, subject to priority risk

Barak-Pelleg and Berend, *Fano Geometry and Slow Coupon Collecting* (arXiv:2606.28216v1), give exact **mean** cover times for these models, not the statement that the full pointwise survival comparison holds for every horizon. The nearby general coverage work of Grunbaum and Yaakobi (arXiv:2510.25305) discusses expectation-based extremality, alongside stochastic comparisons between *other* coverage geometries, not this pair of projective/full block laws. Berend and Sher (arXiv:2509.17201) investigate expected-time extremality in different package families. The novelty claimed here is specifically the exact finite-horizon reversal at \(q=3\) and its provably unique location, contrasted with usual stochastic domination at \(q=2\). Exhaustive avoidance counting and expectation comparison are credited as known methods. A prior equivalent, possibly differently phrased tail comparison has not been excluded independently.

## Value: PASS

Comparing expected completion times does not determine their horizon-by-horizon probabilities or their stochastic order. The result classifies these order relations completely for the two smallest finite projective planes, revealing a sharp order reversal for the natural order-three test case. This identifies a meaningful boundary to proposed fair-mechanism extremality and shows why a positive mean gap may coexist with an early completion advantage. The result is intentionally limited to the two specified geometries; neither a general projective-plane theorem nor a larger-dimensional classification is asserted.
