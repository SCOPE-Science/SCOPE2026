# Scientific review of the one-vector deterministic row-sampling positivity frontier

## Correctness — PASS

For every real unit trial vector, every row subset with nonzero selected mass, and every real symmetric matrix bounded between \(\mu I\) and \(LI\), the sketched scalar is the trace pairing of the input matrix with a symmetric rank-at-most-two operator. The two eigenvalues of this operator are \((1\pm 1/a)/2\). Splitting the input into \(\mu I+(L-\mu)B\), with \(0\preceq B\preceq I\), gives the exact upper and lower endpoints. The proof constructs endpoint matrices, covers the degenerate full-mass case, and explains why intermediate values are attainable. Averaging coordinate energies proves the top-row upper bound, while the flat vector gives a matching lower bound against every rule that observes only the trial vector. The exact strict positivity inequality and equality cases follow. The package's rational and numerical tests corroborate, without replacing, these steps. There are no limiting approximations or unverified nonstandard lemmas in the universal argument.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit residual risk

Bergermann's 2026 deterministic-sketch paper defines the row-sampled Rayleigh–Ritz method, gives one-column DEIM/Q-DEIM selection, and notes spurious Ritz values and the empirical benefit of oversampling. Those are credited. The examined full text does not give the exact minimax interval for matrices constrained by two spectral endpoints, nor the basis-only all-vector row-budget classification or the sharp positivity fraction. Nakatsukasa–Tropp already analyze sketched Ritz methods and their failure to retain self-adjoint structure, but not this deterministic row-budget threshold. Drmač–Gugercin analyze basis-dependent subset conditioning, not the sketched eigenvalue's all-matrix positivity frontier. Published related results about Lanczos curvature and row-coherence are different implication problems and do not subsume the theorem. A broad two-sided Rayleigh-quotient inequality might already contain the fixed-row extremum; novelty therefore rests on the source-motivated **sharp composition with minimax row selection**, not an assertion that trace duality or positive-definite cross terms are newly discovered. Older equivalents under oblique Petrov–Galerkin or interpolation terminology remain a literature risk. Independent audit has not been performed.

## Value — PASS

The exact spectral location of a sketched Ritz value matters for eigensolvers applied to positive-definite problems; a negative value is qualitatively misleading. The motivating method selects a small deterministic coordinate sketch to preserve its trial subspace. The theorem resolves a natural all-dimension resource question: given a worst-case input condition number, what fraction of coordinates is both necessary and sufficient to rule out nonpositive one-vector sketched Ritz values, even using the optimal trial-vector-only row rule? The bound is sharp for every \(n,s\), includes an explicit zero crossing and strict negative construction, and distinguishes the ideal top-row budget from heuristic oversampling. It is not a dimension-specific arithmetic exercise or a restatement of the motivating paper's qualitative observation. No higher-dimensional eigensolver guarantee is asserted.

## Boundaries and residual risks

The result applies to exact arithmetic, real SPD matrices with a known enclosing interval, one trial vector, and row-only selection based on the trial vector. It provides no normwise eigenpair residual or backward-error certificate and does not claim that an oversampling heuristic matches ideal top-row selection for \(s>1\). Literature search is not a proof of novelty; an equivalent older oblique numerical-range or two-sided Rayleigh-quotient bound may exist. The scientific conclusion needs a separate independent audit before any assertion of external certification.
