# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete simple-game domain through five players is generated from minimal-winning antichains and independently filtered for properness. Banzhaf swing counts are computed from losing-to-winning and winning-to-losing transitions; Johnston scores are computed by coalition-centered and player-centered loops. Exact rational outputs agree. The full five-player quotient is canonicalized under every player permutation, and the package replays all nine divergent classes.

Risk: the theorem is finite and compares only weak rankings.

## Originality
**PASS.** Prior work explicitly studies ordinal equivalence of Johnston, Banzhaf, and Shapley–Shubik and proves equality of rankings throughout the semicomplete class. That structural theorem is treated as prior. Searches by aliases, exact player number, proper-simple-game restriction, exact count \(480\), fraction \(96/529\), and isomorphism language did not locate the sharp proper-game boundary, nine-class census, or universal tie-refinement property.

Risk: failed search is not proof of bibliographic uniqueness. An unindexed historical enumeration or software table may contain the same finite census.

## Value
**PASS.** The literature treats ordinal equivalence of these indices as a substantive question because both are classical criticality-based measures but allocate coalition credit differently. The smallest proper voting body where the rankings separate is therefore a natural structural boundary. The first layer is unusually informative: all \(480\) divergences are concentrated in nine symmetry types and every separation refines a Banzhaf tie rather than reversing an existing strict comparison.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
