## Originality
**PASS.** The inspected literature directly compares the two runoff ideas and fixes their definitions, so no novelty is claimed for the rules or for their qualitative difference. Freeman, Brill, and Conitzer characterize STV and Coombs as plurality- and veto-based runoff scoring rules with parallel-universes tie-breaking. Miller compares Alternative Vote and Coombs on three-candidate elections and studies their Condorcet behavior. The exact first singleton-winner disagreement layer—no conflict below five voters, exactly \(180\) labeled conflicts at five voters, one anonymous candidate-relabeling orbit, and universal Condorcet selection by Coombs within that layer—was not located in the checked sources or targeted searches.

Risk: failed search is not proof of bibliographic uniqueness. An unindexed enumeration or teaching example may contain the same finite census.

## Value
**PASS.** STV and Coombs are especially close elimination rules: for three candidates each performs exactly one elimination and then the same pairwise runoff, differing only in whether first-place weakness or last-place opposition determines who is removed. The sharp smallest electorate where that sole design choice changes a unique winner is therefore a natural structural boundary. The fact that all minimal conflicts collapse to one symmetry type and always sacrifice the Condorcet winner under STV gives the boundary a clear social-choice interpretation rather than merely a numerical count.
