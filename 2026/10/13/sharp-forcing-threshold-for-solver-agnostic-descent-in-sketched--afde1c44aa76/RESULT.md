# Sharp forcing threshold for solver-agnostic descent in sketched Levenberg-Marquardt steps
## Finding

Consider a symmetric matrix
\[
B\succeq0,
\]
a regularization parameter
\[
\mu>0,
\]
and a nonzero right-hand side
\[
g\in\operatorname{range}(B).
\]
Suppose an approximate inner solve returns \(s\) and residual \(\rho\) satisfying
\[
(B+\mu I)s=-g+\rho,
\qquad
\|\rho\|\le\eta\|g\|.
\]

Let
\[
a=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(B)+\mu,
\qquad
b=\lambda_{\max}(B)+\mu,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{b}{a},
\]
where \(\lambda_{\min}^{+}(B)\) denotes the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(B\).

Then the exact uniform frontier for solver-agnostic strict descent is
\[
\eta_*=
\frac{2\sqrt{ab}}{a+b}
=
\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa}}{\kappa+1}.
\]

More precisely:

1. If
\[
0\le\eta<\eta_*,
\]
then every residual satisfying the displayed norm bound gives
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s<0.
\]

2. If
\[
\eta=\eta_*,
\]
then every admissible residual gives
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s\le0,
\]
and equality is attainable.

3. If
\[
\eta>\eta_*,
\]
then a two-eigenvalue instance admits an admissible residual for which
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s>0.
\]

For the sketched Levenberg-Marquardt model in Bellavia--Malaspina--Morini, one has
\[
B=MJ^{\mathsf T}JM^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
g=MJ^{\mathsf T}F.
\]
Because
\[
\operatorname{range}(MJ^{\mathsf T})
=
\operatorname{range}(MJ^{\mathsf T}JM^{\mathsf T}),
\]
the hypothesis
\[
g\in\operatorname{range}(B)
\]
holds automatically.

The source obtains descent for its LSMR-generated step by an additional Krylov orthogonality:
\[
s^{\mathsf T}\rho=0.
\]
That property gives
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s
=
-s^{\mathsf T}(B+\mu I)s<0
\]
for every nonzero step, independently of the condition-number frontier above.

Thus the forcing test
\[
\|\rho\|\le\eta\|g\|,
\qquad
\eta<1,
\]
is safe in the paper because it is coupled to the LSMR construction. If LSMR is replaced by an arbitrary inner solver while retaining only that residual test, the sharp condition becomes
\[
\eta<
\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa}}{\kappa+1}.
\]
For large \(\kappa\), this is asymptotic to
\[
\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}},
\]
so an apparently mild residual tolerance can fail to preserve descent on ill-conditioned regularized models.

## Assumptions and scope

The result is finite-dimensional and deterministic. It concerns one fixed inner linearized subproblem.

The matrix \(B\) may be singular. Only its positive spectral subspace matters because \(g\in\operatorname{range}(B)\). If \(B\) has rank one, then \(a=b\) and
\[
\eta_*=1.
\]
Hence the usual condition \(\eta<1\) is solver-agnostically sufficient in that special case.

The result does not claim that the source algorithm is incorrect. The source explicitly adopts LSMR and proves descent using the orthogonality generated by its Krylov least-squares construction. The finding identifies exactly what changes if one tries to treat the residual bound alone as a portable stopping rule for another inner solver.

## Proof

Set
\[
H=B+\mu I.
\]
The approximate solve is
\[
Hs=-g+\rho,
\]
so
\[
s=H^{-1}(-g+\rho).
\]
For fixed \(g\), the largest possible directional derivative over all residuals with
\[
\|\rho\|\le\eta\|g\|
\]
is
\[
\max_{\|\rho\|\le\eta\|g\|}
g^{\mathsf T}s
=
-g^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}g
+
\eta\|g\|\|H^{-1}g\|.
\]
Indeed, equality is attained by taking \(\rho\) parallel to \(H^{-1}g\).

Therefore strict descent for every admissible residual is equivalent to
\[
\eta<
R(g):=
\frac{g^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}g}
{\|g\|\|H^{-1}g\|}.
\]
Uniform strict descent over all nonzero \(g\in\operatorname{range}(B)\) holds exactly when
\[
\eta<
\inf_{0\ne g\in\operatorname{range}(B)}R(g).
\]

Restrict \(H\) to \(\operatorname{range}(B)\). Its eigenvalues lie in
\[
[a,b].
\]
Let
\[
A=H^{-1}
\]
on that subspace, whose eigenvalues lie in
\[
[\alpha,\beta]
=
\left[\frac1b,\frac1a\right].
\]
Normalize \(\|g\|=1\), and define
\[
m_1=g^{\mathsf T}Ag,
\qquad
m_2=g^{\mathsf T}A^2g.
\]
Then
\[
R(g)^2=\frac{m_1^2}{m_2}.
\]

For every scalar \(t\in[\alpha,\beta]\),
\[
(t-\alpha)(\beta-t)\ge0,
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
t^2\le(\alpha+\beta)t-\alpha\beta.
\]
Applying this eigenvalue-wise gives
\[
m_2\le(\alpha+\beta)m_1-\alpha\beta.
\]
Hence
\[
R(g)^2
\ge
\frac{m_1^2}{(\alpha+\beta)m_1-\alpha\beta}.
\]
The right-hand side is minimized for
\[
m_1=\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta},
\]
where its value is
\[
\frac{4\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
R(g)
\ge
\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}{\alpha+\beta}
=
\frac{2\sqrt{ab}}{a+b}.
\]

This bound is sharp. Take a two-dimensional positive spectral subspace on which
\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(a,b)
\]
and choose the unit vector
\[
g=
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{a}{a+b}},
\sqrt{\frac{b}{a+b}}
\right).
\]
A direct calculation gives
\[
R(g)=\frac{2\sqrt{ab}}{a+b}.
\]
Taking
\[
\rho
=
\eta\,
\frac{H^{-1}g}{\|H^{-1}g\|}
\]
attains the worst residual direction. Thus the directional derivative is zero at \(\eta=\eta_*\) and positive for every \(\eta>\eta_*\).

The sharp example is realizable as a least-squares model. Given positive numbers
\[
\lambda_1=a-\mu,
\qquad
\lambda_2=b-\mu,
\]
choose
\[
M=I,
\qquad
J=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\sqrt{\lambda_2}),
\]
and choose \(F\) so that
\[
J^{\mathsf T}F=g.
\]
Then
\[
B=J^{\mathsf T}J
\]
and the constructed \(g\) is exactly the sketched gradient.

Finally, LSMR is different from an arbitrary residual-producing solver. For the source Krylov step,
\[
s^{\mathsf T}\rho=0.
\]
Multiplying
\[
Hs=-g+\rho
\]
by \(s^{\mathsf T}\) gives
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s
=
-s^{\mathsf T}Hs+s^{\mathsf T}\rho
=
-s^{\mathsf T}Hs<0
\]
for every nonzero step because \(H\succ0\). This explains why the source can allow every forcing term below one without invoking the condition-number frontier.

## Relationship to prior work

The primary paper defines its reduced Levenberg-Marquardt equation and the residual test
\[
\|\rho_k\|\le\eta_k\|M_k\nabla f(x_k)\|,
\qquad
\eta_k<1.
\]
Its descent lemma does not use the residual norm alone: it invokes the LSMR Krylov property
\[
\widehat s_k^{\mathsf T}\rho_k=0.
\]
The finding above quantifies exactly how much stronger a residual-only rule must be if that orthogonality is absent.

The authors' earlier inexact Gauss-Newton work likewise constructs trial steps in Krylov subspaces and proves nonascent using residual orthogonality. That predecessor does not state a solver-agnostic forcing threshold.

Classical inexact Newton forcing-term literature studies residual tolerances below one for convergence and efficiency, while the LSMR literature explains the minimum-residual Krylov structure used by the source. The inspected sources do not state the condition-number-dependent sharp frontier
\[
\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa}}{\kappa+1}
\]
for preserving descent under an arbitrary residual vector in a regularized normal equation.

Targeted searches by source title, residual criterion, Levenberg-Marquardt forcing term, inexact Gauss-Newton descent, Krylov orthogonality, and Kantorovich-angle terminology did not locate an equivalent source-specific result.

## Limitations

The sharp threshold is for arbitrary residual vectors satisfying only the Euclidean norm test. Particular solvers may enforce additional geometric conditions and can therefore allow larger forcing terms.

The result concerns descent of the linearized-model direction through the sign of
\[
g^{\mathsf T}s.
\]
It does not replace the source's random-embedding assumptions, global probability analysis, Armijo acceptance analysis, or local convergence theory.

The threshold depends on the extreme positive eigenvalues of the regularized sketched normal matrix. Estimating those eigenvalues cheaply and safely in a production solver is a separate numerical question.
