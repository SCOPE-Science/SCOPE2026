# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The domain and quantifiers are finite disjoint unions of directed cycles with arbitrary valuations, and the successor modality is immediate, not transitive. Forward-prefix locality and its positive monotonic refinement follow from a structural induction. The displayed positive least-common-multiple formula is evaluated at every permitted cycle, and congruence-defined ordered valuation pairs prove sharpness. Fine–Wilf supplies the classical prefix sufficiency; the explicit two-cycle valuations of lengths \(n\) and \(n-1\) supply the matching strict obstruction. The \(n=1\) boundary is settled separately. The reproducibility script checks 127 length-set cases, with an aggregate of 5,828 enumerated valuations, without treating finite tests as infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** Figallo–Sarmiento–Pezzini prove tense algebraic completeness, not exact future-only elimination depth. Dvorkin's Lyndon-positivity results do not bound the depth of predecessor on cyclic frames. Kurtonina–de Rijke provide general positive simulation characterizations and finite-degree criteria, but no numeric lcm thresholds for this class. Fine–Wilf's periodic agreement theorem is explicitly reused for the Boolean upper bound; the full exact-depth modal classification, the positive formula, and the sharp monotone obstruction are additional. Searches for equivalent successor/past temporal and periodic-word formulations and exact tables found no decisive coverage. A differently named deterministic temporal-logic result could remain uncatalogued; the primary 1965 proof was not itself read, but its standard numerical statement was independently cross-checked.

## Value

**PASS.** The locality cost of eliminating past is a basic structural invariant of modal languages. On the same natural bounded-cycle family, the optimal modal nesting grows linearly with full Boolean operations and quadratically in the negation-free language. The general exact positive formula for arbitrary finite permitted period sets identifies the combinatorial mechanism behind the gap. The result is a uniform classification with matching constructive lower bounds, not a small finite-table computation.

## Scope limits

The claim does not concern transitive future operators, cycles with unbounded lengths, arbitrary Kripke frames, or minimum formula size. Finite enumeration supplements rather than replaces the proof. Bibliographic comparisons cannot exclude every unlocated equivalent formulation.
