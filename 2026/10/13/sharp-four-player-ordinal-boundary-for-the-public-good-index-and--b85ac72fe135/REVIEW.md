# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete proper-simple-game domains through four players are exhausted. Public Good is computed in two exact formulations. Nucleoli are obtained by exact rational successive least-core optimization on every canonical class, and the two divergent classes also have direct structural certificates. Orbit expansion gives exactly \(18\) labeled divergences.

Risk: the theorem is finite and ordinal only.

## Originality
**PASS.** Prior work establishes the Public Good definition and the nucleolus as a stability-oriented solution for voting games. Those facts are treated as prior. Searches by Public Good/Holler aliases, nucleolus terminology, exact player number, exact count \(18\), and both weighted representatives did not locate the sharp proper-game boundary or the two-orbit first-layer classification.

Risk: failed search is not proof of bibliographic uniqueness. An unindexed historical table or software enumeration could contain the same census.

## Value
**PASS.** Public Good and the nucleolus embody two notably different interpretations of power allocation: participation in minimal winning coalitions versus lexicographic coalition stability. Determining the smallest proper voting game where these viewpoints rank players differently is therefore a natural structural boundary. The result is strengthened by a complete first-layer quotient, a dummy-free refinement, and an explicit demonstration that properness delays divergence from three to four players.
