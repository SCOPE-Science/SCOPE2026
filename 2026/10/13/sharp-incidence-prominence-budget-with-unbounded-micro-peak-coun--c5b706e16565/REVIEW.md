# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The accepted result is the joint sharp logarithmic prominence budget and the smooth decreasing-transmission counterfamily with unlimited unthresholded incidence peaks and increasing prevalence. Starting from the published SIR incidence-growth identity, direct integration over ordered disjoint intervals proves the bound. Its coefficient is sharp because narrow positive Gaussian pulses of prescribed logarithmic transmission-loss mass concentrate the incidence decline into arbitrarily short intervals, while the bounded positive state term contributes vanishing integrated growth there. For the all-count construction, explicit comparison estimates establish a strictly positive incidence-growth state term between pulses, and the pulse centers have negative incidence-growth derivative; the corresponding alternating extrema are strict by analyticity. The population remains positive and infectious prevalence strictly increases because the effective reproduction factor stays greater than one. Finite rational and sampled trajectory checks support but do not replace these uniform proofs.

Scientific boundaries: the budget assumes a nonnegative susceptible–infectious growth contribution; no conclusion about substantial multi-wave epidemics or peaks after smoothing is made. The highly localized construction has no lower bound on its second transmission derivative as the pulse width vanishes.

## Originality — PASS, restricted claim

The motivating arXiv:2610.05556v1 already proves the incidence derivative identity (equation 21), Proposition 2 on maxima and minima, and Corollary 1 asserting any finite peak count under unrestricted transmission. Those statements are expressly excluded from the claimed contribution. The new statement is the **combined sharp prominence-versus-variation law and fixed-state analytic counterfamily** satisfying simultaneous monotone forcing, arbitrarily small total forcing loss, uniform bound on its first logarithmic derivative, and strictly increasing infectious prevalence. The full motivating paper discusses prominence thresholds but gives no sharp budget or this constrained construction. Published inverse-SIR papers establish flexible reconstruction but do not impose these simultaneous constraints. The Gaussian-pulse paper uses pulses in transmission, not pulses exclusively in its negative logarithmic derivative. No directly dominating theorem was identified in the examined material; possible unindexed prior work is a live residual risk. The budget inequality alone is a direct consequence of the published identity, so novelty is not assigned to that elementary calculation in isolation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating problem is how epidemic incidence peak count interacts with forcing complexity and minimum-prominence rules. The theorem distinguishes a **quantitatively controlled aggregate feature** (sum of logarithmic peak-to-trough drops) from an **uncontrolled count** (strict local extrema) under strictly decreasing transmission. The optimal coefficient and explicit regularity-constrained witness rule out an interpretation that small forcing amplitude and a bounded first logarithmic slope alone impose a finite incidence-peak count. It gives a rigorous reason to specify a prominence threshold or higher-order regularity before treating peak counts as effective model complexity. The examples are not a claim about real outbreak waves or clinical policy.
