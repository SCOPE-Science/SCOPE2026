# Sharp linear-atom trace preservation for Boolean algebras with LFI operators
## Finding

**Trace-compression theorem.** Let \(B\) be any nontrivial Boolean algebra with LFI operators (BALFI), let \(v\) be a valuation, and let \(\Phi\) be a nonempty finite set of formulas closed under taking subformulas. Suppose some \(\varphi\in\Phi\) satisfies \(v(\varphi)\ne1\). Put
\[
S=\{v(\psi):\psi\in\Phi\},\qquad T=S\cup\{1\},\qquad r=|T|\ge2.
\]
There is a finite BALFI \(B'\) whose Boolean reduct is \(\mathcal P(J)\), with \(1\le|J|\le r-1\), a valuation \(v'\), and a Boolean homomorphism \(\pi:C\to\mathcal P(J)\) defined on a finite Boolean subalgebra \(C\) of the Boolean reduct of \(B\), such that \(S\), both unary images of \(S\), and \(1\) lie in \(C\), the restriction \(\pi|_T\) is injective, and
\[
v'(\psi)=\pi(v(\psi))\quad\text{for all }\psi\in\Phi.
\]
In particular, \(v'(\varphi)\ne1\). For a single formula with \(t\) distinct syntactic subformulas, every BALFI refutation has a countermodel with **at most \(t\) Boolean atoms** (and hence at most \(2^t\) algebra elements). The bound \(r-1\) is **optimal as a uniform bound for injective Boolean-homomorphic preservation of arbitrary \(r\)-element traces containing \(1\)**. This optimality statement is *not* a lower bound for the smallest BALFI countermodel of an arbitrary formula.

## Assumptions and scope

A BALFI is an algebra with Boolean reduct \((B,\wedge,\vee,\to,0,1)\) and two unary functions \(N,C_0:B\to B\) satisfying, for every \(a\in B\),
\[
a\vee N(a)=1,\qquad a\wedge N(a)\wedge C_0(a)=0.
\]
Here \(\to\) is material implication in the Boolean reduct. The operators \(N\) and \(C_0\) interpret the nonclassical negation and consistency connective of RmbC, respectively; \(C_0\) must not be confused with the finite Boolean subalgebra \(C\) in the theorem. A valuation preserves the indicated Boolean operations and both unary operators. A formula is refuted when its algebra value differs from \(1\). The theorem is semantic and applies to a valuation into an arbitrary, potentially infinite, BALFI; it does not assume that the input algebra is finite or atomic.

## Proof

**1. A finite Boolean carrier containing the observed data.** Let \(S\) and \(T\) be as in the theorem. In the Boolean reduct of \(B\), form the Boolean subalgebra
\[
C=\langle S\cup N[S]\cup C_0[S]\rangle_{\mathrm{Bool}}.
\]
It is finite because a Boolean algebra generated by a finite set is finite. The Boolean constants belong to \(C\), as do all Boolean subformula values and the unary outputs needed at arguments from \(S\). Write \(Q\) for the finite nonempty set of atoms of \(C\), identifying \(C\) with \(\mathcal P(Q)\).

**2. Greedy separation of the actual trace.** Since \(v(\varphi)<1\), there is an atom \(q_0\in Q\) below \(1\wedge\overline{v(\varphi)}\). Start with \(J=\{q_0\}\). Its coordinate restriction already distinguishes \(1\) from \(v(\varphi)\), so the partition of \(T\) by identical restrictions has at least two classes. Whenever two distinct \(a,b\in T\) still agree on every selected atom, the symmetric difference \(a\triangle b\) is nonzero. Choose an atom of \(C\) contained in \(a\triangle b\); it cannot belong to \(J\), and adding it strictly increases the number of trace classes. No subsequent addition merges classes. At most \(r-2\) further atoms are required because there are \(r\) trace elements. Thus \(1\le|J|\le r-1\) and the map
\[
\pi:C\longrightarrow\mathcal P(J),\qquad\pi(a)=\{q\in J:q\le a\}
\]
is a Boolean homomorphism injective on \(T\), with \(\pi(v(\varphi))\ne J\).

**3. Absorption of the arbitrary unary behavior off the trace.** For each \(x\in\pi(S)\), define
\[
N'(x)=\pi(N(s)),\qquad C_0'(x)=\pi(C_0(s))
\quad\text{where }s\in S\text{ is the unique element with }\pi(s)=x.
\]
Uniqueness follows from injectivity on \(S\). These outputs exist in \(\mathcal P(J)\), because \(N[S]\cup C_0[S]\subseteq C\). At every other input \(x\in\mathcal P(J)\setminus\pi(S)\) set
\[
N'(x)=J\setminus x,\qquad C_0'(x)=J.
\]
At an input in \(\pi(S)\), applying the Boolean homomorphism \(\pi\) to the two BALFI equations in \(B\) proves \(x\cup N'(x)=J\) and \(x\cap N'(x)\cap C_0'(x)=\varnothing\). At every other input, these equations follow immediately from complementation. Therefore the powerset algebra equipped with \(N'\) and \(C_0'\) is a BALFI.

**4. All subformulas, not just the final truth value, are preserved.** Give each variable appearing in \(\Phi\) the value \(\pi(v(p))\), and assign arbitrary values to other variables. Induct on formula structure. Variable values agree by definition; Boolean connectives commute with \(\pi\); and for a unary subformula \(N\alpha\) or \(C_0\alpha\), its argument has original value in \(S\), so the corresponding unary operation was explicitly preserved in step 3. It follows that \(v'(\psi)=\pi(v(\psi))\) for every \(\psi\in\Phi\). The selected atom \(q_0\) witnesses \(v'(\varphi)\ne J\). For \(\Phi=\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)\), one has \(r\le t+1\), proving the linear atom bound.

**5. Optimality for arbitrary Boolean traces.** For any integer \(r\ge2\), take the Boolean algebra \(\mathcal P([r-1])\) and its \(r\)-element trace
\[
T_r=\{[r-1]\}\cup\{[r-1]\setminus\{j\}:j\in[r-1]\}.
\]
Each proper coatom in this trace differs from the top at precisely its own atom. Every Boolean homomorphism from \(\mathcal P([r-1])\) to a Boolean algebra with \(m\) atoms has at most \(m\) Boolean characters as output coordinates. Every character comes from a source atom. To separate the top from each of the \(r-1\) coatoms requires all \(r-1\) distinct source-atom characters, hence \(m\ge r-1\). The trace can be realized as valuations of \(r-1\) variables in a BALFI by using, for example, Boolean complementation for \(N\) and the constant-top operation for \(C_0\). Thus no smaller uniform homomorphic trace-separation bound exists.

## Verification

Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py` with Python 3 and the standard library. It prints \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\), exhaustively checks all 6 BALFIs on a one-atom Boolean reduct and all 1,296 BALFIs on a two-atom Boolean reduct, evaluates five nested formulas over every two-variable valuation, and checks 1,000 reproducible random BALFIs on three atoms. In total, it checks 108,800 formula instances, 36,841 nonvalid projections, Boolean coherence, both pointwise operator axioms, and functionality on the actual trace. It also enumerates missing-coordinate obstructions for the sharp trace family with \(2\le r\le9\). The code examines finite cases only; the general theorem rests on the five proof steps above.

## Relationship to prior work

Coniglio and Mallea (2026, Sections 4–6) establish finite BALFI filtration and an exponentially large **atom** allowance, and formulate a coNEXPTIME upper bound for RmbC validity. Their Absorption Lemma 6.3 is close to step 3, but does not provide an injective Boolean trace projection or the sharp \(r-1\)-atom bound. Carnielli, Coniglio and Fuenmayor (2020, Theorem 6.7) interpret RmbC faithfully in the fusion of two minimal non-normal modal logics \(\mathsf E\oplus\mathsf E\). Dalmonte and Mazzullo (2023, Theorem 3) prove coNP-completeness for this type of non-normal fusion and construct polynomial-sized neighborhood countermodels. Combining those already published theorems gives a coNP upper bound for RmbC **independently of the present theorem**. Accordingly, **coNP-completeness is not claimed as a new result here**. The specific quantitative, homomorphically trace-faithful linear-atom reduction and its exact \(r-1\) bound are the subject of this finding; the earlier polynomial-size modal countermodel construction allows up to quadratic components and does not state this optimal trace bound.

## Limitations

The optimality concerns preserving **the identity pattern of a prescribed Boolean trace through a homomorphism**, not minimum countermodel size for a given formula. The construction preserves values and operation graphs on a finite subformula trace, not arbitrary equations throughout the whole original BALFI and not axiomatic extensions whose additional global identities might be violated by the off-trace completion. The similarity of the coordinate-separation argument to standard finite Boolean-algebra techniques is a residual originality risk; the exact combined statement was not identified in the inspected literature. A full independent audit has not been performed.
