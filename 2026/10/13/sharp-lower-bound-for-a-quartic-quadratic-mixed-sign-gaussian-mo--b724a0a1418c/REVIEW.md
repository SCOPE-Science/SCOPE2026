# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every centered unit-variance Gaussian triple and \(0<u<1\), the exact conditional even-moment computation yields a quadratic form in the coefficients of the third variable. Its comparison with \((1-u)(3-u)\) is a block-positive-semidefiniteness calculation. The leading principal entry is strictly positive, the determinant has an explicitly nonnegative Bernstein factor, and the remaining diagonal entry is nonnegative. These sign statements hold over the entire parameter rectangle, including the singular boundary. Equality requires \(Y=\pm X\) and \(Z\perp X\); joint normality gives independence. The self-contained exact-rational verifier checks 207 identities and cases. Finite tests alone are not the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The originating bivariate opposite inequality (Russell–Sun, arXiv:2205.10231) cannot itself compare three factors. Zhou et al. (arXiv:2308.13740, Proposition 1.6) supply a three-dimensional lower bound for \((4,2)\) with covariance factor \((1-a^2)^2(1-b^2)\), which is zero at the equality configuration and does not imply the strictly positive sharp infimum. Lan–Ouimet–Sun (arXiv:2505.09976, Theorem 1) extend conditional-covariance lower estimates without stating this endpoint. The separately recorded \((2,2)\) covariance formula and positive-power \((2,2,4)\) stability bound concern different integrands. Searches for the sharp constant, mixed-sign aliases, and quartic–quadratic moments found no covering statement, but negative searches are not proof of priority. Risk remains from earlier equivalent estimates or general unnamed extremal principles.

## Value — PASS
The mixed-sign three-factor Gaussian product problem is specifically motivated by published incomplete quantitative bounds. The first unequal even-positive-power case has a sharp **strictly positive covariance-independent lower constant** and a complete equality classification despite earlier general lower estimates degenerating to zero. This gives a natural exact extremal invariant for three-factor Gaussian moments, not a normalization check or arbitrary parameter cutoff. It does not settle unrestricted mixed-sign Gaussian product inequalities.
