# Scientific review: sharp Weyl-mixture divisibility classification

## Correctness — PASS

The statement is about a complete family of \(q+1\) symplectic directions over \(\mathbb F_q\), with \(q=p^r\ge3\), one common positive rate, and arbitrary nonnegative convex weights. The proof constructs the exact eigenvalues on every nonidentity Weyl operator, inverts their logarithmic derivatives using orthogonal Weyl characters, and identifies a diagonal canonical Lindblad matrix. The negative-rate necessity is established by a first-order Choi-state argument; finite-dimensional time-dependent GKLS suffices when all rates are nonnegative. The weight criterion uses the monotonicity of \(a/(1+ay)\); the single-crossing claim uses a strictly positive derivative of the normalized least-rate function. Boundary supports, equal weights, and critical equality are treated explicitly. Exact computation in a qutrit example corroborates the root. A separate direct-character implementation of finite-field Weyl operators supports the Fourier normalization with 182400 finite checks in dimensions 3, 4, 5, and 7. Finite tests are not used to prove the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Xu–Jagadish, arXiv:2605.23852v1, Section V.B, Eq. (30) and Appendix F, supplies the qutrit four-way rate and identifies a uniform Markovian neighborhood and nonuniform non-Markovian examples; the present proof credits it rather than claiming the rate derivation. The inspected original does not state the global if-and-only-if reciprocal-odds inequality, the single onset without reentrance, or the arbitrary prime-power field-spread case. Jagadish–Srikanth–Petruccione (arXiv:1910.04114) classifies the qubit case; it is not a result in dimensions \(q\ge3\). Siudzińska–Chruściński (arXiv:1706.10041) treats general channel eigenvalues and memory kernels, not this evaluated fixed-semigroup mixing simplex. Li–Xu–Wang–Zheng (arXiv:2309.04903) addresses stationary semigroup spectra and decompositions, not CP-divisibility of the nonsemigroup mixed evolution. Xu–Li–Zheng (Phys. Rev. A 109, 012410, 2024) mixes generalized Weyl dephasing channels to obtain stationary semigroups, a different direction. The claim is a new complete parameter classification within a well-motivated source problem; an unindexed equivalent or unnoticed implicit specialization of general rates remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS

For a natural family of nontrivial quantum channels, the theorem replaces point examples and neighborhood arguments with a computable exact condition on the whole probability simplex. It determines whether time-dependent open-system dynamics is CP-divisible for all times, gives an exact unique time of permanent departure when not, and characterizes the sharp difference between interior and face mixtures. The finite-field extension includes composite prime-power dimensions where modular Weyl coordinates are insufficient. The result is substantive as a complete physical parameter-space decision theorem; it does not claim arbitrary rate or noise generality. The boundary asymptotics and an exact rational qutrit onset provide nonroutine structural information and readily testable predictions.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary statement is Xu–Jagadish (2026), Eq. (30): a qutrit instantaneous-rate identity, with incomplete weight-region classification. Its implication to the evaluated criterion requires the new monotonicity argument and endpoint analysis. The qubit Markovian simplex and earlier generalized Pauli/GKLS results are baseline rather than equivalent claims. The theorem assumes complete field-line spreads, a common constant constituent relaxation rate, and time-independent weights. The 2024 generalized Weyl paper's channel constituents and target stationary-semigroup property differ. A prior unindexed equivalent reduction cannot be excluded; no external independent scientific audit is claimed.
