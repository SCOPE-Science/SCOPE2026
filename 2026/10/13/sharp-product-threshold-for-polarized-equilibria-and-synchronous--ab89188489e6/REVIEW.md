# Scientific review — Sharp product threshold for two contrarian communities

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact three-person sampling polynomial conjugates to two negative odd cubics. Squaring decouples them into two increasing, strictly concave maps, each with a unique nonzero positive fixed point exactly when the product slope exceeds one. This yields the complete polarized/even-odd attractor classification and the critical power law. A standalone exact-rational checker confirms the essential algebra on finite witness examples, not the general theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with limitations.** Galam (2026, doi:10.3390/e28040443) develops the single-population ratio-dependent contrarian law; Lambiotte, Ausloos and Hołyst (2007, doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.75.030101) develop coupled ordinary-majority networks, and Nettasinghe et al. (2025, doi:10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf082) use asynchronous cross-party thresholds. Their stated results do not give the distinct four-parameter cross-coupled contrarian product threshold or prove coexistence of two polarized fixed points with a reversing two-cycle. The scalar concavity argument is known, and a generally formulated two-map theorem or unindexed model could subsume this application.

**Value — PASS.** The exact global threshold and sign-based basins distinguish stable polarized intergroup opposition from persistent synchronous majority reversal, and show they may both attract for identical social-interaction parameters. This closes a natural two-community problem, rather than adding a small parameter correction.

**Limitations.** Applies only to deterministic synchronous, independent cross-community triad sampling and the stated monotone high-contrarian domain. Independent expert assessment, the finite-population stochastic process, asynchronous networks, and neighboring parameter regimes remain unproved.
