For the globally stable scalar flow \(\dot u=-a u-bu^2\), the standard degree-\(N\) Carleman error is exactly \([R(1-e^{-at})]^N\) relative to the solution, with \(R=bu_0/a\). The boundary \(R=1\) is therefore sharp for universal long-time use of the unshifted mechanism: it gives only \(O(N^{-1})\) uniform absolute error at criticality, while every \(R>1\) eventually causes late-time truncation divergence and loss of lifted dissipativity at sufficiently high order.
