# Sharp secant-safeguard boundary for a subspace conjugate-gradient direction

## Finding
Consider the Case-I direction in Li et al. (2026):
\[
d=-g+\frac{g^{\mathsf T}y-g^{\mathsf T}s}{s^{\mathsf T}y}s,\qquad y=Bs,
\]
where \(B\) is symmetric positive definite, \(s\neq0\), and the direction is accepted when \(s^{\mathsf T}y\ge\xi\|y\|^2\) for \(0<\xi<1\). Across all such accepted inputs in dimension at least two, the **largest valid uniform coefficient** \(C\) in \(g^{\mathsf T}d\le-C\|g\|^2\) is
\[
C_*(\xi)=1-\frac1{4\xi}.
\]
Thus a positive uniform descent constant exists exactly when \(\xi>1/4\). At the boundary \(\xi=1/4\), the accepted direction is strictly descending for every fixed nonzero residual but no positive uniform coefficient exists. For \(0<\xi<1/4\), an accepted direction can be an ascent direction. A fully specified two-dimensional monotone affine example below realizes ascent after a valid first line-search and projection update, and the next line search then has no admissible step.

## Assumptions and scope
The theorem concerns the **Case-I** branch of equations (6) and (10), where the subspace is spanned by the current negative residual and the latest displacement, and the secant relation \(Bs=y\) is imposed. Let \(n\ge2\), \(B=B^{\mathsf T}\succ0\), \(s\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}\), \(y=Bs\), \(g\in\mathbb R^n\), and \(0<\xi<1\). The branch is taken whenever \(s^{\mathsf T}y\ge\xi\|y\|^2\). The coefficient is uniform over these inputs; the equality case of a fixed input is separate from uniformity. No claims are made about the source's Case-II/III formulas, or about a modified algorithm that explicitly checks descent.

## Proof
Write \(e=s/\|s\|\), \(b=e^{\mathsf T}Be>0\), and \(h=(I-ee^{\mathsf T})Be\), so \(e^{\mathsf T}h=0\). Decompose \(g=ue+v\), with \(e^{\mathsf T}v=0\). Secancy gives
\[
d=-\frac{u-h^{\mathsf T}v}{b}e-v,\qquad
-g^{\mathsf T}d=\frac{u^2}{b}-\frac{u\,h^{\mathsf T}v}{b}+\|v\|^2.
\]
The guard is precisely \(b\ge\xi(b^2+\|h\|^2)\). The quadratic form \(-g^{\mathsf T}d\) is the identity on perpendicular directions and on the plane generated by \(e,h\), if \(h\ne0\), has matrix
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1/b&-\|h\|/(2b)\\-\|h\|/(2b)&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(c=1-1/(4\xi)\). The guard implies \(b\le1/\xi\) and \(\|h\|^2\le b/\xi-b^2\). Therefore \(1/b-c\ge\xi-c=(2\xi-1)^2/(4\xi)\ge0\) and
\[
\det(Q-cI)\ge(1/b-c)(1-c)-\frac{b/\xi-b^2}{4b^2}
=\left(\frac1{4\xi}-\frac12\right)^2\ge0.
\]
Also \(1-c=1/(4\xi)>0\); hence \(Q-cI\succeq0\), establishing the asserted inequality for all inputs, including the case \(h=0\). Sharpness is witnessed in two dimensions by \(s=e_1\) and
\[
B_b=\begin{pmatrix}b&H_b\\H_b&1+H_b^2/b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H_b=\sqrt{b/\xi-b^2},\quad 0<b<1/\xi.
\]
This matrix is positive definite with determinant \(b\), and the guard holds at equality. The least eigenvalue of \(Q\) tends to \(c\) as \(b\downarrow0\), so no larger uniform coefficient is possible. For \(\xi=1/4\), every fixed guarded pair satisfies \(\|h\|^2\le4b-b^2<4b\) and \(Q\succ0\); nevertheless its uniform infimum is zero. If \(\xi<1/4\), taking \(0<b<\min\{1/\xi,1/\xi-4\}\) gives \(H_b^2>4b\), so \(Q\) has a negative eigenvalue. Choosing \(g\) in its negative eigendirection gives genuine ascent.

**Reachable two-dimensional example.** Set \(F(x)=Ax\), \(\Omega=\mathbb R^2\), and
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}99/100&3\\3&999/100\end{pmatrix}\succ0,
\quad r=1/100,\quad B=A+rI=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\3&10\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Choose the source's valid numerical parameters \(\xi_1=1/100\), \(\xi=2/25\) (trial size), \(\rho=1/2\), \(\sigma=3/10\), \(\kappa=1\), and \(\varepsilon=1/1000\). Start with \(F(x_0)=(-1,-300/251)^{\mathsf T}\), \(x_0=A^{-1}F(x_0)\), \(x_{-1}=x_0\), and \(d_0=-F(x_0)\). The first trial \(\alpha_0=2/25\) passes equation (11). It gives \(F(z_0)=(-198901/313750,0)^{\mathsf T}\) and the standard hyperplane projection with \(\kappa=1\) gives \(x_1-x_0=s_0=(2/25,0)^{\mathsf T}\). Then
\[
g_1=F(x_1)=(-1151/1250,-5994/6275)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
y_0=F(x_1)-F(x_0)+rs_0=(2/25,6/25)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The guard is satisfied strictly, because \(s_0^{\mathsf T}y_0-\xi_1\|y_0\|^2=18/3125>0\). Equation (6) gives \(a=-8991/251\) and
\[
d_1=(-610199/313750,5994/6275)^{\mathsf T},\quad
g_1^{\mathsf T}d_1=86467011299/98439062500>0.
\]
Because \(A\succ0\), for every \(\alpha>0\), \(F(x_1+\alpha d_1)^{\mathsf T}d_1=g_1^{\mathsf T}d_1+\alpha d_1^{\mathsf T}Ad_1>0\). Thus the source's line-search inequality (11) fails at *every* positive \(\alpha\).

## Relationship to prior work
Li et al. (2026), equations (6), (10), (11), and Lemma 1, provide the secant Case-I direction, safeguard and claimed all-parameter sufficient descent. The theorem above supplies a sharp guard threshold and exact optimal coefficient, and gives a reachable counterexample at a parameter allowed by that paper. Tang et al. (2025) study subspace minimization for monotone equations with a different direction formula and descent argument, not this Case-I secant safeguard. General known descent safeguards do not imply the sharp \(1/4\) boundary without the present secant coupling and extremizers.

## Limitations
The finding does not assert failure of every run or every branch of the source method. It addresses equations (6) and (10) under the paper's stated positive-definite secant premise and exactly one reachable first-update trajectory. The statement does not prove a replacement global-convergence theorem for \(\xi>1/4\), because other branches and hypotheses would still need scrutiny. A sharper comparison with all historical subspace methods remains an originality risk; no independent validation has been performed.
