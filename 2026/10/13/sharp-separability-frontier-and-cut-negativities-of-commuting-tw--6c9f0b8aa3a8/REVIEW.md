# Self-review

**Claim assessed.** Every mutually commuting, one- and two-local qubit Pauli Hamiltonian is locally Clifford equivalent to a tensor sum of a classical diagonal sector and disjoint Bell dimers. Each dimer has four explicit Gibbs probabilities; full separability is equivalent to each maximum probability being at most one half, and logarithmic negativity across any cut is the sum of the crossing-dimer contributions. Under a coefficient bound \(J\) and any permitted term-overlap maximum degree \(\Delta\ge2\), the largest universal fully-separable interval is exactly \(0\le\beta J\le(\log3)/4\).

## Correctness — PASS
The centralizer of two commuting two-body Pauli strings that disagree on one site's axis forces their shared support to be the same pair and confines all other nontrivial terms on those sites to three pair generators. Remaining sites are simultaneously one-axis diagonalizable by local Clifford gates. The Gibbs state thus factors into classical diagonal and Bell-diagonal sectors. The Bell eigenvalue-sign product is \(-1\), partial-transpose eigenvalues are \(1/2-q_s\), and the two-qubit PPT criterion is both necessary and sufficient for separability. Multiplicativity of the partial-transpose trace norm gives exact logarithmic negativity across every cut. Every pair of Bell energies differs by at most \(4J\), establishing the universal sufficient threshold. The explicit antiferromagnetic Heisenberg dimer \(J(XX+YY+ZZ)\) has overlap degree two and crosses the PPT boundary at exactly the same threshold. No numerical limit has been used to prove an infinite statement. The packaged program verified 9,957 commuting subsets, 6,600 two-qubit partial transposes, and 3,960 full bipartition norms; finite checks are corroborative only.
