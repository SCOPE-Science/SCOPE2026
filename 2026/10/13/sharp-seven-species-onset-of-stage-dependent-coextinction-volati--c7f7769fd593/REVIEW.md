# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact moment formula follows by expanding extinction indicators, with no asymptotic assumptions. A complete finite certificate enumerates every feasible nonisolated incidence matrix up to seven total vertices after harmless secondary-guild relabeling: 97 degree-multiset profiles and 1,600 incidence matrices. Independent direct survivor-set enumeration matches exact neighborhood-union moments for every matrix and every stage. The sole seven-vertex obstruction is structurally classified into a star, path and doubled-edge-plus-disjoint-edge case; their exact variances are derived both ways. The exhaustive finite classification makes no assertion about larger graphs.

**Originality — PASS within the investigated literature, subject to stated residual risk.** Memmott et al. (2004) establish the ecological random-extinction setup. Burgos et al. (2006) treat nestedness/robustness. Bane et al. (2018) analyze the distribution of sequence-wide robustness, a distinct random variable; their conclusions about topology and variance do not imply the seven-species stagewise optimizer reversal. Jones et al. (2024) give expected secondary extinctions and explicitly leave uncertainty in extinction curves as a further question. No inspected source supplies the same minimum-size incompatibility, its three structural types, or its stage-specific sharp variance ordering. A broader combinatorial or ecological treatment not found by the searches could still cover it; database search results alone cannot prove global uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem isolates a precise non-monotonic risk-ranking limitation of widely used random knockout models while holding every secondary species at the same degree. It shows that identical expected robustness does not force a common largest fluctuation risk; the strict phase reversal at the smallest possible network size is a motivated structural boundary rather than an arbitrary graph computation. Its value is as an exact null-model diagnostic, not as a prediction of real population dynamics.

**Scientific limitations and risks.** No larger-network optimality theorem is claimed, no probabilistic model with changing interaction weights is analyzed, and ecological outcomes need not follow the one-step knockout rule. Unindexed work on extinction-count covariances or convex-order couplings might contain a covering theorem; this remains an originality risk. There was no independent audit.
