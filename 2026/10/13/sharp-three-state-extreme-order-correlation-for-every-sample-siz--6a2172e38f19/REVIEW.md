# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For independent uniform draws from exactly three distinct atoms, the joint law of the sample extrema gives the stated exact first, second, and cross moments. Positive affine normalization leaves their correlation invariant. Algebra reduces the squared correlation to \(C(z)^2/P(z)\) for \(z=t(1-t)\), and the sign of its derivative to an affine polynomial \(H(z)\). The proof computes both endpoint values exactly and provides a uniform positive lower bound for the critical endpoint factor for every integer \(n\ge4\). Hence the global and unique optimum occurs at the midpoint; it is not inferred from a grid or a numerical limit. The standalone verifier checks the two nonstandard symbolic factorizations and multiple exact finite joint distributions. Scope: exactly three distinct equally likely support atoms and every sample size \(n\ge4\). No probabilistic approximation is needed for the finite-sample inequality.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk
Papadatos's 2022 paper (arXiv:2205.14360v1, Theorems 1.2 and 2.1) proves lattice extremality for **two draws** and raises the sample-size extension explicitly in its concluding discussion. The general bound of Székely and Móri, as reproduced in that paper, gives only \(1/n\) for the correlation between extrema, and does not imply the strict three-state coefficient \(R_n\). López-Blázquez and Salamanca-Miño (2021) treat discrete-parent maximal correlation via automatic differentiation; its first-page preview and abstract were inspected, but the full paper could not be retrieved, so it is an explicitly recorded priority risk. A publicly indexed result on **two draws from three equally spaced atoms with variable probabilities** addresses a different extremum and cannot specialize to fixed uniform masses with \(n\ge4\). Available prior three-draw special cases do not cover the unbounded set of sample sizes in this result. Search included alternative descriptions as lattice-optimal monotone transforms, extremal order-statistic correlation, and discrete Terrell characterization, as well as numerical aliases of the displayed bound. No logically covering statement was found in the inspected work. This is a searched originality assessment, not an independently established priority certificate: older results under different terminology remain possible.

## Value — PASS
The proof settles a natural infinite-parameter slice of a question stated in the principal 2022 primary reference: the three-support-point case for **all** sample sizes at least four, including sharp constants and unique geometry, rather than a single further finite case. It also identifies the exact exponentially decaying sharp scale \((2/3)^n\), which is far smaller than the universal order-statistic bound \(1/n\). The result is deliberately limited to fixed equal masses, so it neither generalizes the probability-vector conjecture nor asserts a general characterization for all population sizes.
