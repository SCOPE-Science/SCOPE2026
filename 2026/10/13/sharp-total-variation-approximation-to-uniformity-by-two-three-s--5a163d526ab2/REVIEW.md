# Scientific review: three-sided-dice Manhattan projection

## Correctness — PASS
The central nonnegative polynomial identity is checked both by direct algebraic expansion and by a degree-determining 4096-point exact grid. A two-case dual-potential argument proves the lower bound globally without discretizing the probability simplices. Equality in the same inequalities forces an explicitly characterized output distribution; equality in the polynomial identity then forces one factor to have vanishing middle mass, from which all factors are recovered. Attainment is verified exactly for a full parameter interval, including endpoints. The standalone checker passes 9469 exact assertions. Independent verification has not been performed.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority limitations
The lead paper (Metya–Mukherjee, arXiv:2609.38243v1, Section 4.3) states the Manhattan lower bound for every \(n\ge3\) as Conjecture 4.7 and already constructs equality candidates. This theorem supplies the previously conjectural lower bound for \(n=3\) and proves no additional minimizers exist. Asgarli–Hartglass–Ostrov–Walden (arXiv:2304.08501v2, Theorem 1) solve a different squared-Euclidean projection and do not entail the Manhattan optimization. Search comparisons found no covering published record, but absence of a matched record does not prove historical priority; differently indexed arguments remain a risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete solution of the first nontrivial case of an explicit, recently published optimal approximation problem. It identifies a sharp observable statistical discrepancy and a nontrivial continuum of all optimal factorizations. The convolution determinant inequality and total-variation dual certificate have independent structural content for the finite-probability model. The work does not resolve higher support sizes.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
