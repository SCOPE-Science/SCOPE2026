# Sharp two-qubit Pauli-flat contrast with a separable nonstabilizer endpoint

## Finding
For the 15 nonidentity Hermitian two-qubit Pauli matrices \(P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes2}\setminus\{II\}\), consider a density operator \(\rho\) satisfying \(|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P)|=\gamma\) for the same real \(\gamma\ge0\) and every \(P\). Such an operator exists **if and only if** \(0\le\gamma\le1/3\). Moreover, **every** allowed contrast is attained by a **separable** two-qubit density operator with \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P)=+\gamma\) for all 15 nonidentity Paulis.

At the sharp contrast \(\gamma=1/3\), the explicit separable attaining state has rank two and purity \(2/3\). For \(1/5<\gamma\le1/3\), the same separable family lies outside the convex hull of two-qubit pure stabilizer states. Consequently, entanglement is unnecessary either for maximal equal Pauli contrast or for the nonstabilizer character of this family. The assertion concerns stabilizer-polytope membership, not a claim about an operational mixed-state magic monotone.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(I\), \(X\), \(Y\), and \(Z\) be the standard Hermitian single-qubit Pauli matrices, with \(XY=iZ\). Tensor-factor order is fixed, and a density operator means a positive semidefinite \(4\times4\) complex Hermitian matrix of trace one. Its 15 nonidentity Pauli expectations are necessarily real. No assumption is made about purity, entanglement, symmetry, or the individual signs of these expectations. In particular, the theorem optimizes over **all** of the \(2^{15}\) sign assignments, rather than fixing a chosen one.

For the attaining family, write \(A=X+Y+Z\), \(S=A/\sqrt3\), and define rank-one local projectors \(\Pi_{\pm}=(I\pm S)/2\), since \(S^2=I\). Put
\[
q_{\pm}=\frac{3\pm\sqrt3}{6},\qquad
\omega=q_+\Pi_+\otimes\Pi_++q_-\Pi_-\otimes\Pi_-,
\qquad
\rho_{\gamma}=(1-3\gamma)\frac{I_4}{4}+3\gamma\,\omega.
\]
The coefficients are probabilities whenever \(0\le\gamma\le1/3\). Here \(I_4=I\otimes I\). A two-qubit pure stabilizer state is a common eigenstate of two independent commuting signed Pauli matrices; the stabilizer polytope is their convex hull.

## Proof
**Universal obstruction by contextuality.** Use the standard Peres–Mermin array
\[
\begin{matrix}
XI&IX&XX\\
IY&YI&YY\\
XY&YX&ZZ
\end{matrix}.
\]
Every row and column is a commuting triple. The operator product of each row and of the first two columns is \(+I_4\), while the product of the final column is \(-I_4\). Denote these six products by \(PQR=\varepsilon I_4\), with five \(\varepsilon=+1\) and one \(\varepsilon=-1\). Every array entry belongs to exactly two triples.

For any sign assignment \(r_P\in\{+1,-1\}\) on all 15 nonidentity Paulis, the product of the six numbers \(r_Pr_Qr_R\) from the array is \(+1\), whereas the product of the six contextual signs \(\varepsilon\) is \(-1\). Therefore some commuting context has \(r_Pr_Qr_R=-\varepsilon\). Choose its first two independent observables \(P,Q\), and form the rank-one projector
\[
E=\frac{(I_4-r_PP)(I_4-r_QQ)}4.
\]
Because \(P\) and \(Q\) commute, their joint eigenspaces are one-dimensional, and \(E\) is positive with trace one. In this joint eigenspace, \(P\) and \(Q\) have expectations \(-r_P\) and \(-r_Q\). The third observable equals \(R=\varepsilon PQ\), so its expectation is \(\varepsilon r_Pr_Q=-r_R\). The other 12 nonidentity Pauli expectations in \(E\) vanish by Pauli orthogonality to the four-element stabilizer group generated by the two signed operators.

Write the arbitrary putative flat state as
\[
\rho=\frac14\left(I_4+\gamma\sum_{P\ne I_4}r_PP\right).
\]
This expansion follows from \(\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=4\delta_{P,Q}\). Taking its expectation in \(E\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho)=\frac{1-3\gamma}{4}.
\]
Since both \(E\) and \(\rho\) are positive semidefinite, this is nonnegative. Hence \(\gamma\le1/3\), for **every** sign vector.

**Sharp separable construction.** For each \(R\in\{X,Y,Z\}\), the single-qubit expectations are \(\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_\pm R)=\pm1/\sqrt3\). Using \(q_++q_-=1\) and \(q_+-q_-=1/\sqrt3\), one obtains
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega(R\otimes I))=\operatorname{Tr}(\omega(I\otimes R))=\frac13,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega(R\otimes T))=\frac13
\]
for all \(R,T\in\{X,Y,Z\}\). Thus all 15 nonidentity expectations of \(\omega\) equal \(+1/3\), and every nonidentity expectation of \(\rho_\gamma\) equals \(+\gamma\). The expression for \(\rho_\gamma\) is a convex combination of the separable states \(I_4/4\) and \(\omega\). This proves both feasibility and separability throughout the complete interval. The two product projectors in \(\omega\) are orthogonal, with strictly positive weights, so \(\omega\) has rank two and purity \(q_+^2+q_-^2=2/3\). Since any flat state at \(\gamma=1/3\) has purity \((1+15\gamma^2)/4=2/3<1\), rank one is impossible: the construction attains the least possible endpoint rank.

**Nonstabilizer segment.** Define \(W=\sum_{P\ne I_4}P\). Every pure two-qubit stabilizer state has precisely three nonidentity Pauli expectations of magnitude one, with all other twelve zero, and hence \(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma W)\le3\). By convexity the same holds for every state in the stabilizer polytope. Yet \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\gamma W)=15\gamma>3\) whenever \(\gamma>1/5\). Consequently the constructed, separable \(\rho_\gamma\) is nonstabilizer throughout \(1/5<\gamma\le1/3\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library program `artifacts/verify_pauli_flat.py` checks all six contextual operator products, their commutation and multiplicity, and exhaustively confirms that each of the \(2^{15}=32768\) sign vectors frustrates at least one context. For selected sign vectors it constructs the contextual stabilizer projector and checks all 15 Pauli expectations directly. It also verifies that the all-positive endpoint has trace one, all 15 expectations equal \(1/3\), and purity \(2/3\), and checks the exact characteristic identity for the signed Pauli sum.

Run `python3 artifacts/verify_pauli_flat.py` with Python 3; no third-party dependencies, hidden data, or downloads are needed. The reported successful output begins with `VERIFY_OK`. The finite census checks the sign-space aspect exhaustively, but the universal density-operator result rests on the analytic projector argument, not on eigenvalue sampling. The rank, separability, and nonstabilizer conclusions also follow analytically from the explicit decomposition and witness.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu, Low, and Yin (2025, arXiv:2502.17550) study **pure** two-qubit states maximizing stabilizer Rényi entropy and identify a Weyl–Heisenberg MUB-fiducial family. Cuffaro and Fuchs (2024, arXiv:2412.21083) characterize pure-state saturation of SIC-associated stabilizer-entropy bounds. Neither result gives a sharp contrast interval for **mixed** two-qubit states with all 15 Pauli expectations of equal modulus. The Peres–Mermin square (Mermin 1990) provides the known state-independent parity contradiction; the present proof uses this standard structure to obtain a quantitative density-operator positivity bound.

A particularly close contemporaneous study, Cieśliński (arXiv:2610.07172v1, posted 2026-10-05), explicitly defines mixed Pauli-flat states, displays a **different, entangled** two-qubit example at contrast \(1/3\), and reports that value in a best-found table with an SDP formulation. Its inspected statement does not establish the all-sign global ceiling through a positivity witness, nor present the all-positive **separable** sharp family. The endpoint numerical value and existence of a nonstabilizer mixed state at it are therefore **not claimed as new in isolation**. This result is the conjunction of the universal sharp obstruction and a separable rank-two attaining family, including its exact nonstabilizer interval. Priority of an equivalent unstated derivation remains uncertain.

## Limitations
The theorem is a complete answer only for **two** qubits and equal absolute expectation magnitudes for **all** 15 nonidentity Hermitian Pauli strings. It makes no optimality claim for higher numbers of qubits, for approximate flatness or only selected Pauli weights, or for states subject to locality/noise/preparation constraints. Nonmembership in the stabilizer polytope is demonstrated by a witness; no claim is made that separability alone implies or forbids operational computational speedup. The endpoint values do not establish uniqueness or classify all sign-pattern equivalence classes. The classical Peres–Mermin structure and the previously exhibited entangled contrast-\(1/3\) state remain prior work.
