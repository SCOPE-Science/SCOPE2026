# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The stopping rule and missing-place definition agree exactly with the cited sibling model. The range restriction is valid because the terminal type appears for its first time on the last draw. The geometric time for the deliberately rare type has the exponential limit claimed; conditional multinomial variance gives simultaneous concentration for all fixed \(N-1\) common types; positive continuous limiting counts make completion before or at the rare arrival overwhelmingly likely. The continuous mapping step has no threshold atom, and boundedness gives convergence of the first two moments. The resulting two-point variance reaches the universal range bound. The uniform-vector limit follows from almost-sure finiteness of the original completion time. A separate exact-rational finite-state recursion verified the stated finite comparison. No finite computation is used to establish an infinite limit.

## Originality

**PASS with explicit priority risk.** Full text of Doumas–Spektor [1] was inspected and addresses mean, not variance, maximization. A different 2026 Long result [5] optimizes the variance of completion time, not the number of missing types. Public materials for Long [2] take \(N\to\infty\) at fixed sibling indices, whereas this theorem fixes \(N\) and takes \(j\to\infty\). Long [3] proves PGF and binomial-moment radial order; those statements do not compare variance, whose centered first-moment subtraction is essential; the exact \(N=j=3\) example demonstrates the distinction. Public-result and literature searches found no implication covering the two-point triangular-array law or sharp variance supremum. The full versions of [2] and [3] could not be inspected, and an equivalent unpublished or unindexed theorem cannot be ruled out. Failure to find one is not treated as a proof of global priority.

## Value

**PASS.** The extremal variance of a naturally defined coupon-collection deficit is a substantive second-moment question suggested by the established mean-extremality theory. The result gives the exact sharp limit for every fixed number of types, identifies an explicit near-extremizing family of admissible positive coupon distributions, produces the full two-point limiting law, and explains why uniform probabilities that maximize mean deficit have asymptotically zero variance. The analytic consequence does not reduce to known PGF comparisons or to a gratuitous finite parameter slice. Rates and exact finite-index maximizers remain open.
