# Shortest atypicality witnesses for the three generating Thue–Morse atypical words
## Finding
Let \(\mathbf t\) be the Prouhet–Thue–Morse word, let \(\mu(a)=ab\), \(\mu(b)=ba\), and let \(\xi\) exchange \(a\) and \(b\). For a finite factor \(w\) of \(\mathbf t\), define its atypicality witness length
\[
\kappa(w)=\min_{\varphi} |\varphi(w)|,
\]
where the minimum ranges over nonerasing endomorphisms \(\varphi:\{a,b\}^{+}\to\{a,b\}^{+}\) such that \(\varphi(w)\) is again a factor of \(\mathbf t\), but
\[
\varphi\notin\{\mu^n,\xi\circ\mu^n:n\ge 0\}.
\]

For the three words that generate all atypical factors in the classification of Almeida and Klíma,
\[
\kappa(\mathtt{aabab})=13,\qquad
\kappa(\mathtt{abaaba})=8,\qquad
\kappa(\mathtt{aabbaab})=13.
\]
Up to applying \(\xi\) simultaneously to both morphism images, the unique minimizing witnesses are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
w&\varphi(a)&\varphi(b)\\
\hline
\mathtt{aabab}&\mathtt a&\mathtt{bbaab}\\
\mathtt{abaaba}&\mathtt a&\mathtt{bb}\\
\mathtt{aabbaab}&\mathtt a&\mathtt{bab}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus every one of the three generating atypical words has a unique shortest local witness up to letter exchange. In particular, for \(\mathtt{aabab}\) the shortest witness has image length \(13\), improving the length-\(17\) witness displayed in the source classification.

## Assumptions and scope
An endomorphism is nonerasing because the source works with the free semigroup \(\{a,b\}^{+}\). A factor means a contiguous finite segment of \(\mathbf t\).

The excluded morphisms are exactly the globally language-preserving morphisms for \(\mathbf t\): Almeida and Klíma prove that their monoid is generated by \(\xi\) and \(\mu\), and the two morphisms commute. The quantity \(\kappa(w)\) therefore measures the shortest image that witnesses atypicality locally rather than merely applying a global symmetry or Thue–Morse substitution.

The claim concerns precisely the three words \(\mathtt{aabab}\), \(\mathtt{abaaba}\), and \(\mathtt{aabbaab}\) in Theorem 4.3 of the source, whose variants and factors comprise all atypical words. It does not claim analogous minima for every atypical factor.

## Proof
The three displayed morphisms give the upper bounds. Their images are, respectively,
\[
\mathtt{aabbaababbaab},\qquad
\mathtt{abbaabba},\qquad
\mathtt{aababbaababab}
\]
with the last expression understood without spaces as \(\mathtt{aababbaababab}\). For reproducibility, the certificate uses the binary encoding \(a=0\), \(b=1\) and records the exact images
\[
\mathtt{0011001011001},\quad
\mathtt{01100110},\quad
\mathtt{0010110100101}.
\]
They occur in \(\mathbf t\), and none of the three morphisms is a power of \(\mu\) or its composition with \(\xi\).

For the lower bounds, let \(r=|\varphi(a)|\ge1\) and \(s=|\varphi(b)|\ge1\). If \(w\) contains \(A\) copies of \(a\) and \(B\) copies of \(b\), then
\[
|\varphi(w)|=Ar+Bs.
\]
Consequently, any witness shorter than or tied with the claimed minimum lies among finitely many pairs \((r,s)\). For each such pair there are exactly \(2^{r+s}\) possible ordered image pairs \((\varphi(a),\varphi(b))\).

Membership in the Thue–Morse factor language is also finite and exact. Proposition 2.1 of the source states that, for a candidate word \(q\) of length \(L\ge3\), \(q\) is a factor of \(\mathbf t\) if and only if it is a factor of
\[
\mu^{\,2+\lceil\log_2(L-1)\rceil}(a).
\]
The lengths in this claim are at most \(13\), so only a bounded prefix generated through \(\mu^6(a)\) is needed.

Exhausting every nonempty image pair through the claimed minima tests \(228\) morphisms for \(\mathtt{aabab}\), \(12\) for \(\mathtt{abaaba}\), and \(36\) for \(\mathtt{aabbaab}\). After excluding the morphisms \(\mu^n\) and \(\xi\circ\mu^n\), no valid witness occurs below the claimed minima. At each minimum exactly two witnesses remain, and they are exchanged by \(\xi\). This proves both the three values of \(\kappa\) and uniqueness up to letter exchange.

It applies the exact factor test from Proposition 2.1 and independently reconstructs Thue–Morse bits as the parity of binary digit sums, checking that both factor-generation methods agree for every length through \(13\).

## Relationship to prior work
Almeida and Klíma prove that, up to reversal and letter exchange, every atypical factor is a factor of one of
\[
\mathtt{aabab},\qquad \mathtt{abaaba},\qquad \mathtt{aabbaab}.
\]
They also give one non-global endomorphism witnessing atypicality for each of these three words. Their witnesses map \(b\), respectively, to \(\mathtt{bbabaab}\), \(\mathtt{bb}\), and \(\mathtt{bab}\), while fixing \(a\). The first of those produces an image of length \(17\); the latter two produce images of lengths \(8\) and \(13\).

The source does not minimize image length or classify shortest witnesses. Its global result that the language-preserving endomorphism monoid is generated by \(\xi\) and \(\mu\) identifies which morphisms must be excluded, but does not imply any of the three local minima.

Targeted searches under “atypical word”, “shortest image”, “minimal endomorphism”, the three generating words, and the explicit new \(\mathtt{bbaab}\) image found no statement of these minima or their uniqueness.

## Limitations
The result is a complete shortest-witness classification only for the three generating atypical words in the published theorem. A factor of one of these words may have a different value of \(\kappa\), and no formula for all atypical factors is asserted.

The originality search cannot exclude an unindexed computation or unpublished note. The proof uses the published exact factor-recognition bound for the Thue–Morse word; the bundled verifier independently checks the finite factor sets involved but does not reprove the general proposition.
