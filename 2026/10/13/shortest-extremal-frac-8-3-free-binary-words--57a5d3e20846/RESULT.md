# Shortest extremal \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free binary words
## Finding
A binary word is called extremal \(\beta\)-free when it contains no factor of exponent at least \(\beta\), while inserting either binary letter at any of its \(|w|+1\) positions always creates a factor of exponent at least \(\beta\).

For the boundary value \(\beta=\frac{8}{3}\), the shortest extremal words have length
\[
30.
\]
There are exactly two such words:
\[
\mathtt{011011001101100110010011001001}
\]
and
\[
\mathtt{100100110010011001101100110110}.
\]
They are complements and reversals of one another, so they form one orbit under the group generated by reversal and binary complementation.

## Assumptions and scope
The exponent of a nonempty finite word \(x\) is \(|x|/p(x)\), where \(p(x)\) is its least positive period. Thus \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free means that every factor \(x\) satisfies
\[
\frac{|x|}{p(x)}<\frac{8}{3}.
\]
Extremality concerns every single-letter insertion, including insertion before the first and after the last position.

The claim classifies only the shortest boundary-threshold examples. It does not settle whether extremal \(\beta\)-free binary words exist for any \(\beta>\frac{8}{3}\).

## Proof
Fix a positive integer period \(p\). A factor of period \(p\) has exponent at least \(\frac{8}{3}\) as soon as its length is at least
\[
L_p=\left\lceil\frac{8p}{3}\right\rceil.
\]
Conversely, if any factor has exponent at least \(\frac{8}{3}\), taking its least period \(p\) and then its prefix of length \(L_p\) produces a factor of length \(L_p\) with period \(p\). Hence testing these threshold factors for all \(p\) is equivalent to testing \(\frac{8}{3}\)-freeness.

The exhaustive search starts from the empty word and appends \(0\) and \(1\). Because each retained prefix is already \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free, a newly created forbidden factor must end at the appended letter. The threshold test above therefore gives an exact pruning rule and enumerates every \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free binary word once.

The resulting counts for lengths \(1\) through \(30\) are recorded in `certificate.json`. They end
\[
34880,\ 48020,\ 66102
\]
at lengths \(28,29,30\), respectively. Every retained word of each length \(n<30\) has at least one insertion that remains \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free. At length \(30\), exactly the two displayed words fail this extendability test, and every one of their \(62\) single-letter insertions contains a factor of exponent at least \(\frac{8}{3}\). This proves both minimality and the complete length-\(30\) classification.

## Verification
Compile and run `verify.cpp`. The verifier regenerates all free words through length \(30\), checks the recorded population at every length, and tests extremality for every survivor. It also independently checks the two final witnesses directly from the definition: for every factor it computes the least period, and for every one-letter insertion it verifies that some factor has exponent at least \(\frac{8}{3}\).

The expected terminal output is:

`VERIFY_OK shortest=30 free30=66102 extremal30=2 symmetry_orbits=1`

The exhaustive minimality proof is finite. No inference from sampling, timeout, or a partial search is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Mol, Rampersad, and Shallit define extremal \(\beta\)-free words and prove that arbitrarily long extremal \(\beta\)-free binary words exist for every extended threshold from \(2^+\) through \(\frac{8}{3}\). Their Proposition 4.2(e) supplies an explicit morphic construction at the upper endpoint. In that construction the two fixed boundary words have length \(26\), the uniform morphism has block length \(34\), and the internal square-free ternary word is nonempty, so the displayed family begins at length \(86\). The paper does not claim a minimum length or classify the shortest \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free extremals.

The related paper on extremal ternary square-free words classifies admissible lengths for a different alphabet and threshold. It does not imply the binary \(\frac{8}{3}\) classification here.

Searches under “shortest extremal 8/3-free binary word”, “extremal 8/3-free binary words minimum length”, “extremal repetition threshold binary 8/3 finite words”, the exact witness string, and equivalent \(\beta\)-free terminology found no result implying the length-\(30\) classification.

## Limitations
This result does not improve the open upper-threshold conjecture above \(\frac{8}{3}\), and it does not classify extremal \(\frac{8}{3}\)-free words of lengths greater than \(30\). The originality comparison cannot exclude an unindexed computational note containing the same shortest pair.
