# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

Every factor identity follows from the explicit rank-one spectrum of the complete-graph adjacency matrix. Tensorization is exact because Cartesian adjacency summands commute. The coefficient formula uses only the Cesàro integral of nonzero integer-frequency exponentials. Orthogonal projector ranks provide full multiplicities without assuming generic eigenvalues. Since every Laurent coefficient has nonnegative combinatorial weights, zero versus positivity is decided by an exact signed-balance test. For the three-factor case the only newly surviving sector has exactly two balanced sign assignments when the larger nonbinary size exceeds the smaller by two. The domain is all \(d\ge1\) and \(n_i\ge2\); zero coefficients at binary singletons and repeated factor sizes are included. Twelve exact rational reconstructions independently check \(9218\) matrix entries, but are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Godsil's original spectral-projector treatment and Coutinho--Godsil--Guo--Zhan's commutant formula are methodological foundations, not an explicit mixed-alphabet coefficient law. The full-text discussion of Cartesian bunkbeds in Lo--Rajaram--Schepens--Sullivan--Tamon--Ward analyzes path--circulant products and asymptotic average-uniformity, not the present complete graph-factor tensor rank transition. Chan's Hamming-graph treatment concerns instantaneous uniform mixing in an equal-alphabet setting. The exact-computation algorithm of Coutinho and coauthors is more general as an algorithm but does not state the signed-frequency nonvanishing criterion or the \(|m-n|=2\) rank jump. These are statement-versus-implication comparisons, not searches for matching words alone. Related previously considered homogeneous products and hypercube-by-single-clique products do not determine what happens when two *different* nonbinary alphabet sizes interact. A stronger unlocated association-scheme or Cartesian-product rank formula might still subsume the theorem; searching cannot prove universal novelty.

## Value — PASS

Long-time quantum transport can retain or annihilate tensor sectors when eigenfrequency collisions occur. The theorem gives all sectors and exact multiplicities for an unrestricted mixed-alphabet Cartesian family, and it produces a sharp arithmetic rank-restoration boundary on a three-factor graph that is not explained by separate factor ranks. For instance the additional positive sector on \(K_2\square K_3\square K_5\) has dimension \(8\), whereas the neighboring \(K_2\square K_3\square K_4\) has no such sector. The signed-balance rule is reusable for large products where direct eigenprojector calculations are costly. The novelty/value claim is for the nontrivial mixed-size resonance and classification, not for elementary tensorization by itself.
