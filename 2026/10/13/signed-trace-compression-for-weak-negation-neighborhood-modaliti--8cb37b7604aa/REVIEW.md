# Review of signed-trace compression for weak-negation neighborhood modalities

## Correctness (PASS)
The statement fixes unrestricted signed membership semantics and distinct modal operands. The trace lemma adds a world only when it separates two previously colliding sets, increasing the number of observable trace classes. Separate application to positive and negative operand sets yields the bound \(2k-1\). The neighborhood reconstruction is well-defined because trace equality within a sign implies full-set equality for the finitely many operands; signed structural induction covers atomic, weak negation, Boolean and both modal polarities. The sharpness example forces \(2k-1\) selected points specifically for the prescribed *joint trace-separation* problem. A polynomial signed-column certificate is necessary by compression and sufficient by explicit neighborhood synthesis. The coNP hardness reduction treats weak negation carefully: classical negation is implemented as positive implication into a fresh atom, not by the weak negation symbol.

Scripts `verify_polarity.py` and `verify_certificates.py` passed 2,700 finite-model reconstruction trials, 1,500 finite certificate checks, and 9,600 Boolean translation evaluations. These are supplementary sanity tests, not proof of arbitrary-cardinality cases. The model bound is not claimed minimal among arbitrary equivalent models.

## Originality (PASS, with risks)
The relevant 2026 paper of Vaz and Skurt introduces the four-neighborhood weak-negation membership semantics, extensionality and correspondence, but the inspected Sections 2–3 and their main results do not state a finite signed-trace model-size theorem or complexity characterization. Drobyshevich and Skurt (2021) instead use neighborhoods of ordered signed proof pairs with FDE-fixed rejection operations. Dalmonte, Olivetti and Negri (2018) prove finite-model existence for classically negated nonnormal systems using bi-neighborhood pairs, not a sharp simultaneous two-polarity operand-trace compression theorem. Dalmonte and Mazzullo (2023, Theorem 3) already prove coNP-completeness for classically negated combinations of nonnormal modal logics, so **coNP as an abstract complexity outcome is not claimed novel**; their modal proof and model extraction are not the stated \(2k-1\) signed-preservation theorem for the four independent weak-negation clauses. Searches using CLoN, bi-neighborhood, signed filtration, extensionality witnesses, coNP and alias terminology did not identify identical coverage in published mathematical results.

Residual risk: a general elementary-neighborhood or multi-sorted translation theorem might entail the signed compression without this exact terminology; absence from inspected sources does not establish worldwide priority. Equivalence claims are therefore restricted to the explicitly formulated semantics. The proof does not transfer to arbitrary frame restrictions.

## Value (PASS)
The 2026 semantic formalism is sufficiently new that a finite-model bound for *simultaneous verification and rejection* under nested modalities is mathematically motivated. Single-polarity filtration loses negative information needed by weak negation, while signed trace separation repairs the obstruction with a linear number of worlds and a sharp extremal bound for that representation task. The construction is constructive, supports a compact certificate for all finite-premise semantic consequences, and separates these unrestricted weak-negation cases from extensions requiring additional closure conditions. The value is the explicit structural preservation theorem and its algorithmic realization, not simply a restatement of the established coNP class for classical E.

## Scope and independent status
The scripts check finite samples; symbolic proofs establish the theorem. Published-database and literature comparison may not index every equivalent generalization. No statement is made about restricted CLuN/CLaN, global consequence, arbitrary frame axioms or optimal minimum model size.
