# Singular-curve arrangement and jumping local class groups of cycle cut varieties

## Finding
Let \(C_n\) be the simple graph cycle with \(n\ge4\), and let \(X_n\) be its projective cut toric variety over \(\mathbb C\). There are \(2^{n-1}\) torus-fixed points and the reduced singular locus is a pure one-dimensional arrangement
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigcup_{e\in E(P_n)} V(e),\qquad
\#E(P_n)=2^{n-2}\binom n2,
\]
where \(P_n\) is the polytope of even-parity binary words, and \(V(e)\cong\mathbb P^1\) is the toric invariant curve belonging to the edge \(e\). Two components intersect only at a torus-fixed point; exactly \(\binom n2\) components pass through each fixed point.

At each non-fixed point of a singular component, a Zariski neighborhood has the product model \(\mathbb G_m\times D_{2,n-2}\), where \(D_{2,m}\subset\mathbb A^{2m}\) is cut out by all \(2\times2\) minors of a generic \(2\times m\) matrix. Thus its transverse germ is the affine Segre cone over \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-3}\). Its local divisor-class group is \(\mathbb Z\) and its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is \(n-2\).

At each fixed point \(p\), the full affine coordinate neighborhood is
\[
U_p\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[z_i z_j:1\le i<j\le n],\qquad
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p})\cong\mathbb Z^n.
\]
For \(n=4\), all local rings are Gorenstein (the generic transverse singularity is a threefold ordinary double point). For \(n\ge5\), every singular local ring is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein. All stated local class groups are torsion-free.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over the algebraically closed field \(\mathbb C\). The cut variety means the projective variety defined by the homogeneous cut ideal of Sturmfels--Sullivant, not the edge-monomial toric variety of the same graph. Write
\[
P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n:\sum_i\epsilon_i\equiv0\pmod2\},
\quad L=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i x_i\equiv0\pmod2\}.
\]
A cut of \(C_n\) crosses an even number of edges, and every even set of edges uniquely determines an unordered vertex bipartition. Hence the cut variety is the projective toric variety of \(P_n\) in its intrinsic lattice \(L\). We use the reduced underlying set of the singular scheme, not its nonreduced structure. A torus-fixed point is indexed by an even-parity vertex of \(P_n\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (all vertex charts).** The coordinate reflection \(x_i\mapsto x_i\) or \(1-x_i\), according to a chosen even-parity vertex, permutes the vertices of \(P_n\) and identifies their tangent semigroups. It is therefore enough to study the chart of the zero vertex. Its exponent semigroup is generated by all nonzero even-weight binary vectors. Every such vector is a sum of weight-two vectors with disjoint supports. Consequently
\[
\mathbb C[U_0]=\mathbb C[\chi^{e_i+e_j}:1\le i<j\le n]
\cong R_n:=\mathbb C[z_i z_j:1\le i<j\le n].
\]
The group generated by these exponents is exactly \(L\). Its real cone is
\[
C_n'=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\ x_i\ge0,\quad x_i\le\frac12\sum_jx_j\ \ (1\le i\le n)\right\}.
\]
Indeed this is the cone of nonnegative degree sequences of loopless multigraphs on \(n\) labeled vertices. An integral sequence in \(C_n'\cap L\) can be realized by pairing its integral half-edges without loops: the largest degree never exceeds the sum of the others. Thus the edge semigroup is saturated in \(L\), proving that all charts and \(X_n\) are normal. The weight-two vectors are exactly the extremal rays of \(C_n'\). It follows that the edges of \(P_n\) join parity words at Hamming distance two.

**Step 2 (singularity of the common affine chart).** Put \(q_{ij}=z_i z_j\) and \(W_n=\operatorname{Proj}R_n\). In the homogeneous chart \(q_{12}\ne0\) of \(W_n\) set
\[
a_i=q_{1i}/q_{12},\qquad b_i=q_{2i}/q_{12}\quad(3\le i\le n).
\]
All remaining homogeneous ratios are \(q_{ij}/q_{12}=a_i b_j\). The complete relations among these \(2(n-2)\) coordinates are \(a_i b_j-a_j b_i=0\) for \(3\le i<j\le n\). This follows either from the prime ideal of a generic rank-one matrix or by factoring a rank-one matrix into a column and a row. Hence this chart is exactly \(D_{2,n-2}\), not just a subset of it. This determinantal variety is smooth at matrices of rank one and singular only at its zero matrix, as follows from the Jacobian of its \(2\times2\) minors. The charts \(q_{ij}\ne0\) cover \(W_n\), so its singular set consists of precisely the \(\binom n2\) coordinate points, one for each \(q_{ij}\).

Localizing the affine cone \(U_0=\operatorname{Spec}R_n\) at \(q_{ij}\) gives \(\mathbb G_m\times D_{2,n-2}\); the \(\mathbb G_m\) factor is the invertible degree-one coordinate \(q_{ij}\). Thus away from its vertex, the singular locus of \(U_0\) consists exactly of the \(\binom n2\) coordinate axes. The origin is also singular: the tangent-space dimension is \(\binom n2>n\), since the \(q_{ij}\) are a minimal generating set and the equations have no linear terms. Therefore the reduced singular locus of \(U_0\) is exactly those axes, including the origin.

**Step 3 (global gluing and counting).** Every chart is isomorphic to \(U_0\). The singular axes on it are torus orbits for the extremal rays \(e_i+e_j\), which correspond precisely to edges of \(P_n\) incident with its defining vertex. Their global orbit closures are \(\mathbb P^1\). Distinct invariant curves may meet only at fixed points, and the affine charts cover all of \(X_n\). Double-counting curve endpoints gives
\[
\#\{\text{irreducible singular curves}\}=
\frac{2^{n-1}\binom n2}{2}=2^{n-2}\binom n2.
\]
This proves the entire reduced singular-locus statement, not merely an inclusion.

**Step 4 (local class groups and multiplicity).** The affine Segre cone \(D_{2,m}\), for \(m\ge2\), has local class group \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1})/\mathbb Z[(1,1)]\cong\mathbb Z\) at its vertex. Its degree, hence Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity, is \(m\). Taking \(m=n-2\) proves the invariants along the non-fixed portions of singular curves.

At the origin of \(U_0\), the cone \(C_n'\) has exactly \(2n\) facets, with primitive supporting linear forms
\[
f_i(x)=x_i,\qquad g_i(x)=\tfrac12\sum_jx_j-x_i\quad(1\le i\le n).
\]
For \(n\ge4\), each form cuts out a facet: the first is generated by edges of the complementary complete graph and the second by the edges of the star centered at \(i\). The toric class-group sequence for this normal affine chart gives
\[
0\longrightarrow L\xrightarrow{\ x\mapsto(f_i(x),g_i(x))_{i=1}^{n}\ }
\mathbb Z^{2n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p})\longrightarrow0.
\]
The cokernel has rank \(n\) and no torsion: if a rational \(x\in L\otimes\mathbb Q\) has all \(f_i(x),g_i(x)\) integral, then \(x_i\in\mathbb Z\) and \(\frac12\sum_i x_i\in\mathbb Z\), so \(x\in L\). Hence the image is saturated and \(\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p})\cong\mathbb Z^n\).

**Step 5 (canonical class).** A toric canonical divisor is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier at the fixed point exactly when a rational \(x\in L\otimes\mathbb Q\) pairs to one with all \(2n\) primitive normals. The \(f_i\) conditions give \(x_i=1\) for all \(i\), and the \(g_i\) conditions become \(n/2-1=1\), which holds precisely when \(n=4\). For \(n=4\), this solution belongs to \(L\), so the local canonical class is Cartier in every fixed chart and \(X_4\) is Gorenstein. For \(n\ge5\), the local Segre cone \(D_{2,n-2}\) is likewise not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein: its canonical class is \((n-4)\) times a generator of its infinite-cyclic class group. Every singular point is either fixed or belongs to a non-fixed singular curve, proving the asserted failure everywhere on the singular locus.

## Verification
A standalone script \(\texttt{checks/check_cycle_cut.py}\) uses exact modular Jacobian ranks, integer Smith normal forms, bounded integral-semigroup enumeration, and polyhedral linear programming as independent finite regression tests. With \(\texttt{python3 checks/check_cycle_cut.py}\), the recorded \(\texttt{checks/check_output.json}\) reports PASS. Explicit curve totals for \(n=4,5,6,7\) are \(24,80,240,672\). Smith normal forms of the local class maps for \(4\le n\le10\) have only unit invariant factors. These bounded tests are not used as an infinite proof; the preceding algebraic argument supplies the all-parameter result. Dependencies are recorded in \(\texttt{checks/requirements.txt}\).

## Relationship to prior work
Sturmfels--Sullivant introduced graph cut toric varieties and exhibited the four-cycle complete intersection and cut polytope (arXiv:math/0606683, Example 1.2). Donten-Bury--Michałek proved projective normality for the two-state group-based phylogenetic model on arbitrary trees, including its equivalent claw/parity-polytope presentation (arXiv:1011.3236, Proposition 3.12). Ohsugi characterized which cut polytopes have Gorenstein toric rings (arXiv:1302.2899); thus the global Gorenstein boundary is prior work, recovered locally here and not separately claimed as a new criterion. Chimani--Juhnke-Kubitzke--Nover--Römer classified simplicity of cut polytopes (arXiv:1903.01817), and Mori treated related parity polytopes and Gorensteinness (arXiv:2210.17147). The rank-one determinantal cone and its divisor class group are classical.

The specific contribution is the **global reduced singular-curve arrangement of the projective cycle cut variety**, its explicit multiplicities and toric incidences, and the **pointwise jump** from \(\mathbb Z\) to \(\mathbb Z^n\) of local divisor-class groups. Existing smoothness, simplicity, normality, Gorenstein, and determinantal theorems do not alone specify that combined pointwise classification. The equivalent \(\mathbb Z_2\) claw-model presentation was included in the literature comparisons; the result is not asserted to be novel solely because its coordinates differ.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns characteristic zero, although several combinatorial parts are characteristic-independent. It determines the reduced singular locus, not the nonreduced singular scheme, embedded components of a Jacobian ideal, or the full intersections of scheme-theoretic singular subschemes. The transverse \(D_{2,n-2}\) description applies at non-fixed singular points; the fixed-point germs are the different, larger affine cones \(\operatorname{Spec}R_n\). The source comparison cannot exclude unindexed work presenting an equivalent classification under group-based phylogenetic terminology. A relevant article by Brennan and Chen was available in abstract but not in complete text.
