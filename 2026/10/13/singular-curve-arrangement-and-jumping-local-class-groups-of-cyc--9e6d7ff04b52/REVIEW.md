# Scientific assessment: singular-curve arrangement of cycle cut varieties

## Correctness — PASS
The statement distinguishes the cut toric variety from the edge toric variety. The proof derives the intrinsic parity lattice, the monomial semigroup of every vertex chart, and its saturated degree-sequence cone. It identifies the projectivized chart ring with the rank-one two-row determinantal variety on standard opens, then proves that its only singular points are coordinate points. Homogeneous localization gives exactly the coordinate-axis singularities of each full affine cone. These axis orbits glue to **all** toric invariant edge curves and no other singular strata. The incidence and count follow from explicit Hamming-distance-two adjacency. The fixed-point class groups come from all \(2n\) primitive facets and an explicit saturation argument, not merely from ranks of experimental matrices. The generic cone multiplicity, class group, and canonical criteria are reconstructed from Segre geometry. The finite tests include edge detection, bounded degree semigroups, modular Jacobian ranks, and Smith normal forms; they do not prove the infinitely quantified parts.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks
The cycle cut construction, four-cycle quadratic complete intersection, normality of the equivalent two-state claw model, cut-polytope simplicity, general Gorenstein classification, and determinantal singularities are documented prior results. The new combined result is a global projective singular-locus description: \(2^{n-2}\binom n2\) explicit singular curves with their full incidence at fixed points and a local-class-group rank jump from one to \(n\). A known description of the projective edge ring of a complete graph concerns the **projectivization** of a vertex chart, rather than the entire cut variety; extracting the stated torus-orbit arrangement and local class groups requires the separate chart identifications and gluing proof. Full text was consulted for the closest open sources, including the alternative claw-tree terminology, and no statement with the same combined scope was found. The Brennan--Chen paper was only accessible in abstract, and an unindexed equivalent treatment under phylogenetic terminology remains a substantive literature risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete singular-stratum and local divisor-class classification for an infinite family of natural, previously studied projective toric varieties. The surprising pure one-dimensional singular set in arbitrarily large dimension, explicit curve incidence, generic multiplicity, and rank jump at fixed-point intersections are natural birational and divisor-theoretic data; they provide a specific basis for questions about small resolutions, \(\mathbb Q\)-factorializations, and singularity invariants. The result is more than recomputation of known smoothness or normality criteria. It is not claimed to classify the nonreduced singular scheme.

Donten-Bury--Michałek, arXiv:1011.3236, handles the equivalent two-state claw model's projective normality. Buczyńska--Wiśniewski, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/90, treats principally trivalent phylogenetic trees, a different fan and scope. Ohsugi, arXiv:1302.2899, controls Gorensteinness; Chimani et al., arXiv:1903.01817, polytope simplicity; Mori, arXiv:2210.17147, related parity polytope and Gorenstein results. Brennan--Chen, DOI 10.1137/070703247, is an unresolved full-text coverage risk. The all-parameter proof is symbolic, supported by bounded computational checks, with no external validation.
