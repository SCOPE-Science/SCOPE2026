# Six-word extremum and complete equality classification for one-error-correcting binary comma-free codes

## Finding
For any set \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^6\), let \(d_H(C)\) denote the minimum pairwise Hamming distance, with \(d_H(C)=+\infty\) for sets of at most one word. The set is **comma-free** if, for every ordered pair \(u,v\in C\) (including \(u=v\)) and every \(1\le j\le5\), the length-six word formed by the last \(6-j\) bits of \(u\) followed by the first \(j\) bits of \(v\) does not belong to \(C\).

Among **all**, not necessarily linear or maximal, binary comma-free codes of length six satisfying \(d_H(C)\ge3\), the exact largest cardinality is
\[
\max |C|=6.
\]
There are precisely \(16\) distinct six-word extremizers, with the six coordinate positions and the two alphabet symbols fixed. These extremizers form exactly **four orbits of size four** under the group generated by reversing every word and complementing every bit. For every extremizer, its \(15\) unordered word pairs have Hamming-distance multiset \(\{3^8,4^7\}\) (eight distances three and seven distances four). The complete list of all \(16\) labeled extremizers is `optimal_codes.txt`.

This is an exact small-block instance of the jointly motivated synchronization/aligned-substitution problem: comma-freedom recognizes error-free word boundaries, while minimum distance three corrects one substituted bit **once the boundary is known**.

## Assumptions and scope
Words are indexed from left to right, have length exactly six, and are drawn from the fixed alphabet \(\{0,1\}\). A code is a **set**, so duplicate words are excluded. The symbol positions are not interchangeable in the counting: only global bit complementation and wordwise reversal are used for the stated symmetry classes. No linearity, constant-composition, circular buffer, or error-resynchronization assumption is imposed. The distance convention is ordinary Hamming distance at the same alignment.

Write \(S_j(u,v)=u_{j+1}\cdots u_6v_1\cdots v_j\) for \(1\le j\le5\), and \(C\) is comma-free exactly when \(S_j(u,v)\notin C\) for every \(u,v\in C\). The associated maximum is the binary length-six, minimum-distance-three, comma-free-index-at-least-one extremal quantity. The conclusion does **not** assert that one substitution can be corrected during loss of framing: this would require a stronger separation index.

## Proof
**Primitive-word reduction.** A member \(w\in C\) must have six distinct cyclic rotations. Otherwise there exists \(1\le j\le5\) such that \(S_j(w,w)=w\), contradicting comma-freedom. A six-bit integer \(m\in\{0,\ldots,63\}\) represents its standard six-bit expansion. Enumerating all rotations yields exactly \(54\) primitive words; this is also verified independently by two standalone programs.

**Extension lemma.** Suppose \(T\) is already a feasible code and \(x\) is a primitive word with larger integer value than every word of \(T\). Then \(T\cup\{x\}\) is feasible if and only if:

1. \(d_H(x,y)\ge3\) for every \(y\in T\); and
2. \(S_j(u,v)\notin T\cup\{x\}\) for every ordered \(u,v\in T\cup\{x\}\) and \(1\le j\le5\).

These are literally the defining inequalities, so there are no unstated assumptions. Failure at a node is monotone: appending more words cannot repair a distance violation or a forbidden splice. Thus an increasing-word depth-first enumeration over the \(54\) primitive candidates, adding **every** feasible extension and discarding **only** extensions violating one of these explicitly checked conditions, visits each feasible code exactly once. Completeness follows by reading the elements of any feasible code in increasing integer order; each prefix is feasible and is necessarily reached.

**Exhaustive certificate.** `verify_enumeration.py` implements the extension lemma using integer bit operations and tests all ordered splices. The independently implemented `verify_independent.cpp` instead represents words as literal six-character strings, generates splices by substrings, and independently generates primitive candidates. Both traverse the entire (unpruned-by-optimum) feasible search tree and return exactly the following numbers of feasible codes by cardinality \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6\):
\[
(1,54,814,3042,2480,324,16).
\]
They report **no** feasible code with seven or more words. The total number of visited nodes is \(6731\). The sixteen recorded size-six codes are directly compared against `optimal_codes.txt`, and two independent implementations check the four symmetry orbits and the fifteen-distance multiset of each optimizer. This finite, exhaustive and logically complete enumeration establishes the bound and the entire equality classification.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_enumeration.py` in the directory containing `optimal_codes.txt`. Independently compile `verify_independent.cpp` with a C++17 compiler, then run the executable with `optimal_codes.txt` as its argument, for example `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_independent.cpp -o verify_independent && ./verify_independent optimal_codes.txt`. Both executions must exit successfully and print `VERIFY_OK`.

The Python checker visits every lexicographically increasing feasible subset of all six-bit primitive words and checks exact full integer splices. The C++ checker independently enumerates strings, literal concatenation splices, distance, and equality cases. Their cross-check is not a statistical test. All arithmetic is integer/string exact; no floating-point tolerance is involved. The proof of completeness lies in the extension lemma and monotonicity, not in test output alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Golomb, Gordon, and Welch (1958) introduced comma-free codes and the cyclic-word upper bound; their classification does not impose minimum Hamming distance three. Levenshtein (1969) defined and motivated simultaneously error-correcting and synchronizing codes through Hamming and comma-free separation; his 2004 survey discusses general separation/index bounds and constructions. Neither statement alone yields the sharp finite six-bit classification above. Churchill (2009) analyzes other restrictions and generalizations of comma-free codes; they do not logically determine this unrestricted distance-three extremum. Published studies of **non-overlapping/cross-bifix-free** codes use a stronger condition and likewise do not imply the exact extremum for general comma-free codes. The result claims neither the classical definition nor the general coding bounds as new.

## Limitations
The claim is only for \(n=6\), binary alphabets, strict comma-freedom and aligned Hamming distance at least three. It neither determines higher block lengths nor guarantees correction of a framing error. Exact computation is exhaustive because the search space is finite and every feasible increasing code is visited; it does not establish an infinite-parameter theorem. The historical literature search may miss unindexed tables, unpublished computations or less obviously equivalent small-parameter treatments. The review is not an independent external audit.
