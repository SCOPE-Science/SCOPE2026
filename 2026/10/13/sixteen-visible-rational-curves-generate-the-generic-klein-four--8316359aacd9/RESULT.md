# Sixteen visible rational curves generate the generic Klein-four quartic quotient lattice
## Finding
Let \(X\) be a very general member of the degree-four family no. 3 in Benedetta Piroddi's study of symplectic Klein-four actions on K3 surfaces:
\[
X:\quad \sum_{i=0}^{3}a_i x_i^4+\sum_{0\le i<j\le3}b_{ij}x_i^2x_j^2+x_0x_1x_2x_3=0.
\]
The two displayed generators act by
\[
\tau(x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3)=(-x_0:-x_1:x_2:x_3),
\qquad
\varphi(x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3)=(-x_0:x_1:-x_2:x_3).
\]
Write
\[
Q(z)=\sum_i a_i z_i^2+\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}z_i z_j.
\]
Piroddi's total-quotient model is the quartic
\[
S:\quad z_0z_1z_2z_3+Q(z)^2=0\subset\mathbb P^3.
\]
For each coordinate edge \(L_{ij}\), the singular locus of \(S\) contains exactly two points, with ratio \(t=z_i/z_j\) equal to a root of
\[
a_i t^2+b_{ij}t+a_j=0.
\]
These are all the singularities of \(S\). Thus \(S\) has twelve ordinary nodes, two on each of the six coordinate edges.

The coordinate-plane section \(z_i=0\) is scheme-theoretically twice the conic
\[
D_i=\{z_i=0,\ Q=0\}.
\]
For a very general member the four conics \(D_i\) are smooth. Let \(\pi:\widetilde S\to S\) be the minimal resolution, let \(C_i\) be the strict transform of \(D_i\), and let \(E_{jk}^{\pm}\) be the two exceptional curves over the two nodes on \(L_{jk}\). Then all sixteen curves are smooth rational \((-2)\)-curves, the \(C_i\) are pairwise disjoint, and
\[
C_i\cdot E_{jk}^{\pm}=
\begin{cases}
1,&i\notin\{j,k\},\\
0,&i\in\{j,k\}.
\end{cases}
\]
Hence every pair among \(C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3\) is joined by two exceptional curves, and the incidence graph is the subdivision of a doubled \(K_4\).

If \(H\) is the pullback of a hyperplane and
\[
F_i=\sum_{j<k,\ i\notin\{j,k\}}\left(E_{jk}^{+}+E_{jk}^{-}\right),
\]
then
\[
H=2C_i+F_i\qquad (i=0,1,2,3).
\]
The lattice \(L\) generated by the sixteen curves has rank \(13\) and discriminant \(2^8\). For the very general member, \(L=\operatorname{NS}(\widetilde S)\). Thus the abstract rank-thirteen quotient lattice in Piroddi's classification is generated by sixteen curves that are directly visible in the quartic equation.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The statement concerns family no. 3 in Piroddi's degree-four projective models. “Very general” means outside the countable union of proper loci where the Picard rank jumps and outside the proper algebraic loci where the source quartic or one of the four ternary quadrics \(Q|_{z_i=0}\) is singular. The latter open condition is nonempty: for example, the choice \(a_i=1\) and \(b_{ij}=0\) makes all four face conics smooth, and the quartic \(\sum_i x_i^4+x_0x_1x_2x_3=0\) is smooth.

The result does not assert that the four face conics remain irreducible on every special member. The twelve quotient nodes, however, remain distinct for every smooth source member of this displayed family.

## Proof
First consider one of the six coordinate lines in the source, say the line on which only \(x_i,x_j\) may be nonzero. The restriction of the quartic equation is
\[
a_i x_i^4+b_{ij}x_i^2x_j^2+a_jx_j^4.
\]
If \(a_i=0\) or \(a_j=0\), the corresponding coordinate vertex lies on \(X\) and all first derivatives vanish there. If
\[
\Delta_{ij}=b_{ij}^2-4a_i a_j=0,
\]
the binary quartic has a repeated root in \(t=(x_i/x_j)^2\); at the corresponding point its derivative along the coordinate line vanishes, while all transverse derivatives vanish because each transverse monomial contains one of the two absent coordinates. Either situation makes \(X\) singular. Therefore smoothness of \(X\) forces
\[
a_i\ne0,
\qquad
\Delta_{ij}\ne0
\]
for all \(i<j\). The six coordinate lines consequently meet \(X\) in twenty-four distinct fixed points, four on each line.

Passing to the full Klein-four quotient identifies the two sign choices over each root of \(a_i t^2+b_{ij}t+a_j\). On the quotient edge \(L_{ij}\), the two resulting points satisfy \(Q=0\). Since two coordinates vanish there, every first derivative of \(z_0z_1z_2z_3+Q^2\) also vanishes. Piroddi's general fixed-locus description gives twenty-four source points with nontrivial stabilizer, paired into twelve quotient orbits, so there are no further quotient singularities. The stabilizer of each point has order two and acts symplectically on the tangent plane, hence the quotient germ is \(A_1\). Equivalently, near a node on \(L_{jk}\), if \(u=z_i\) and \(v=z_l\) are the two missing coordinates and \(w=Q\), the simple edge root makes \(w\) a local coordinate and the equation has analytic form
\[
uv+w^2=0
\]
after multiplying by analytic units.

Now set \(z_i=0\) in the quotient equation. It becomes \(Q^2=0\), so the coordinate hyperplane section is twice the reduced conic \(D_i\). Each \(D_i\) passes through the six nodes on the three coordinate edges contained in its plane. At a node shared by \(D_i\) and \(D_l\), the local model above identifies the two reduced curves with \(\{u=w=0\}\) and \(\{v=w=0\}\). Their strict transforms meet the exceptional \((-2)\)-curve at distinct points and do not meet each other. Thus the four \(C_i\) are pairwise disjoint and have precisely the asserted incidence with the twelve exceptional curves. Since \(\widetilde S\) is a K3 surface and every \(C_i\) is a smooth rational curve, adjunction gives \(C_i^2=-2\).

For the divisor relation, the local Cartier divisor \(u=0\) in the \(A_1\) model pulls back as twice the strict transform of \(\{u=w=0\}\) plus the exceptional curve. Summing this over the six nodes of \(D_i\) gives
\[
H=2C_i+F_i.
\]
Let \(A\) be the lattice generated by the twelve pairwise disjoint exceptional curves. Then \(A\cong A_1^{\oplus12}\), so \(|\operatorname{disc}(A\oplus\mathbb ZH)|=2^{12}\cdot4=2^{14}\). Each relation above says
\[
C_i=\frac{H-F_i}{2}.
\]
Modulo \(2A\), the four incidence vectors \(F_i\) have rank three and satisfy the single relation
\[
F_0+F_1+F_2+F_3=0,
\]
because every exceptional curve lies on exactly two face conics. Hence adjoining the four \(C_i\) enlarges \(A\oplus\mathbb ZH\) by index \(2^3\). Therefore
\[
|\operatorname{disc}L|=\frac{2^{14}}{(2^3)^2}=2^8,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}L=13.
\]

Piroddi's Proposition 2.4.1 shows that the exceptional lattice \(M_{2,2}\) is an index-four overlattice of \(A_1^{\oplus12}\), hence \(|\operatorname{disc}M_{2,2}|=2^8\). For the degree-four family no. 3, Theorem 3.2.3 gives the very general quotient lattice as the index-two overlattice of \(M_{2,2}\oplus\langle4\rangle\), whose discriminant is again \(2^8\). Thus \(L\subseteq\operatorname{NS}(\widetilde S)\) has the same rank and discriminant as \(\operatorname{NS}(\widetilde S)\), so the inclusion has index one.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` checks the doubled-\(K_4\) incidence data, the rank-three binary span of the four face vectors, the index \(2^3\) enlargement, and the resulting discriminant \(2^8\). It independently recomputes the discriminant \(2^8\) of the source's very general quotient lattice from the two successive overlattice indices. It also checks the local intersection arithmetic
\[
(2C_i+F_i)^2=4
\]
using \(C_i^2=-2\), six transverse intersections with exceptional curves, and six exceptional self-intersections.

The checker is a finite exact arithmetic verification of the lattice and incidence calculations. The assertions that there are no additional quotient singularities and that the quotient germs are \(A_1\) use the finite-group quotient geometry and the local analytic argument in the proof, not numerical experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Piroddi proves abstractly that a symplectic \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\)-action has twenty-four points with nontrivial stabilizer, describes the exceptional lattice \(M_{2,2}\), classifies the rank-thirteen Néron--Severi lattices of the resolved total quotients, and in the final projective section writes the explicit family \(Q_4\) and the quartic quotient model \(S_4\). Those ingredients cover the abstract number and type of quotient singularities and the target lattice. They do not identify the twelve singular points of \(S_4\) as two points on every coordinate edge, do not identify the four doubled coordinate-plane sections with visible conics, and do not state that the strict transforms of those conics together with the twelve exceptional curves generate the entire very general Néron--Severi lattice.

Schaffler studies a different four-dimensional family of K3 surfaces with symplectic Klein-four action, realized through covers of \(\mathbb P^2\). That work provides important background on explicit curve generators but does not contain Piroddi's seven-dimensional quartic family or the coordinate-face configuration above.

## Limitations
The equality with the full Néron--Severi lattice is a very-general statement. On Noether--Lefschetz specializations the sixteen curves remain in the Néron--Severi group when the relevant conics remain smooth, but additional divisor classes can appear. If a face conic degenerates, its strict transform must be replaced by its irreducible components and the displayed sixteen-curve description is no longer the appropriate generating set.

The originality comparison is limited to the searched literature and database records. An unpublished thesis computation or an unindexed coordinate treatment could contain the same geometric identification.
