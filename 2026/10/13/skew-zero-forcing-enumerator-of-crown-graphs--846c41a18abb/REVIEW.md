# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof reduces the skew dynamics to the current white-index sets. The exact white-neighbor counts \(|J|-\mathbf 1_{\{r\in J\}}\) and \(|I|-\mathbf 1_{\{r\in I\}}\) prove necessity when either side has at least three white vertices. For the converse, the two-white and one-white cases give explicit forces, and the permission for a white vertex to force is used exactly in the two-white case. Counting the independent omission choices gives the stated square polynomial. Exhaustive replay for every subset through order sixteen agrees with the symbolic criterion and coefficients.

## Originality
PASS. The closest broad source inspected defines skew zero forcing and studies minimum sets and matching structure, but the accepted claim is stronger and differently shaped: it classifies every skew zero-forcing set of the crown graph and identifies the complete enumerator. A separate ordinary zero-forcing polynomial framework does not imply the skew classification. Exact searches under crown, \(K_{n,n}\) minus a perfect matching, skew forcing, minimum sets, and polynomial/enumerator aliases did not produce a covering all-cardinality crown statement. The scalar minimum is not relied on as the novelty component.

## Value
PASS. Crown graphs are a canonical matched deletion of complete bipartite graphs, and skew forcing differs from ordinary forcing solely by whether a white vertex may perform the unique-neighbor force. The theorem isolates that distinction exactly: all coefficients agree except the minimum layer, and the added configurations have a simple matched-pair description. This gives a reusable boundary example for comparing forcing variants rather than an arbitrary finite computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
DeAlba's arXiv:1404.1618v1 supplies the skew rule, primary MSC \(05C50\), and broad matching-based context for minimum skew forcing. Boyer et al., arXiv:1801.08910v1, supplies the ordinary all-cardinality polynomial framework. Earlier matching/minimum-skew-rank theory may imply the scalar minimum, and nonindexed specialized sources remain a residual risk; neither issue covers the retained all-set classification and one-layer defect.
