# Review of Stable local edge metric bases of square-page book graphs

## Correctness
PASS. The proof explicitly calculates the three possible local edge resolving-neighborhood types. The page-pair neighborhoods force at least one selected non-spine vertex on all but at most one page, giving the lower bound \(r-1\). At equality there are no spine vertices and exactly one choice on every nonempty page. The two orientation-specific neighborhood families force at least two selected \(a\)-vertices and at least two selected \(b\)-vertices, and those same inequalities suffice to hit every neighborhood. The counting formula then follows by excluding the four orientation boundary classes. Direct exhaustive computation for \(5\le r\le8\) agrees with the dimension, classification, and count.

## Originality
PASS. The 2020 introducing paper was inspected in full and treats paths, ladders, cycles, stars, and wheels while explicitly posing further special-family and graph-operation problems; it does not treat books or generalized theta graphs. The 2024 same-parameter continuation states corona-product, edge-corona-product, and integer-programming results, not Cartesian products such as \(K_{1,r}\square K_2\). Targeted searches under book, square-page book, generalized theta, and Cartesian-product formulations found no equivalent local-edge-metric theorem. Existing results for other book-graph resolving invariants do not imply the local adjacent-edge neighborhood classification.

## Value
PASS. The theorem gives a sharp stable threshold, a complete structural description of every minimum local edge metric generator, and an exact exponential basis count on a natural generalized-theta family. The proof identifies the precise reason the stable regime begins at five pages: equality requires two landmarks of each orientation after one page is omitted. This directly advances the special-family question posed by the foundational local-edge-metric work rather than recomputing a previously known scalar invariant.
