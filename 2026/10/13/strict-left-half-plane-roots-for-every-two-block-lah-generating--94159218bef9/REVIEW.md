# Scientific assessment: two-block Lah zero location

**Correctness — PASS.** The stated probability polynomial has precisely the normalization \(n!(n-1)\) multiplying the difference of rising factorials, and its \(z^2\) coefficient is positive. For nonzero \(z\) with nonnegative real part, every \(h\ge1\) satisfies \(|2z+h|^2-|z+h|^2=3|z|^2+2h\operatorname{Re}z>0\); this proves strict separation of the product moduli and the nonvanishing assertion for all degrees. A finite exact-arithmetic verifier corroborates the coefficient identity and an illustrative nonreal example, without being treated as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (with priority risk).** Kabluchko–Marynych, arXiv:2105.11365, Conjecture 3.11 asks for negative real parts of the zeros, while Proposition 3.12 supplies selected negative integer zeros only. Their Lemma 3.1 supplies the identity from which the present proof starts, but contains no corresponding modulus comparison. The related r-Lah source (arXiv:2212.12277) proves log-concavity and limit results, not zero exclusion for the target polynomial; a later paper about Hurwitz-stable Hankel determinants of associated Lah polynomial sequences treats different objects. Searches using the names, identities and alternative formulations found no encompassing statement, but do not establish exclusive priority. The source conjecture must be interpreted after excluding the unavoidable origin factor.

**Value — PASS.** The first nontrivial block number \(k=2\) is a natural full infinite subfamily of a specifically formulated zero-location conjecture; all integer degrees are covered. The resulting stability claim is stronger than coefficient log-concavity or identification of finitely many negative integer roots, and genuinely addresses complex nonreal roots (already at degree eight). It informs the applicability of root-location approaches to Lah probability generating functions, without claiming new central limit laws or the unrestricted conjecture.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Kabluchko–Marynych (2021, revised 2022) remains closest, with Kabluchko–Steigenberger (2022, revised 2023) providing adjacent generalization. The claim is restricted to two blocks and ordinary Lah laws; other block numbers and generalized r-parameters are open here. A previous equivalent root-stability argument could exist under rising-factorial or Stirling-convolution terminology.
