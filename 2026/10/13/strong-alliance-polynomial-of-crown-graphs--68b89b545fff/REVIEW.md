# Correctness
The crown graph is \((n-1)\)-regular, so each selected vertex needs at least \(h=\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil\) selected neighbors. A selected \(a_i\) has exactly \(b-\mathbf 1_{i\in J}\) selected neighbors, and a selected \(b_j\) has exactly \(a-\mathbf 1_{j\in I}\). This gives the two exact regimes according to whether \(I\cap J\) is empty. For \(n\ge4\), every numerically feasible regime is connected: it is either a complete bipartite graph or a complete bipartite graph with independent matching edges removed and both sides of size at least three. The parity formulas then follow by a complete count of the only disjoint boundary size pairs. Exhaustive direct verification through \(n=8\) matched the structural criterion and every coefficient.

# Value
The result isolates a natural perturbation of a classical exact family: deleting one perfect matching from \(K_{n,n}\). The perturbation is not invisible to alliances; it creates an overlap constraint on matched indices and a sharp parity transition. For even \(n\), the minimum size is unchanged but the minimum family collapses from all half-by-half choices to complementary halves. For odd \(n\), the degree parity change lowers the minimum by two. The full polynomial records this phenomenon at every cardinality, giving a reusable exact classification rather than only a scalar computation.
