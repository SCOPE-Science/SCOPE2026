# Review of Supercritical symmetry breaking at \(\tau_2(k)\) for 4-periodic SOS boundary laws

## Correctness
PASS. The accepted claim is local and follows from a two-variable analytic reduction of the published boundary-law system. Passing to \(s=(a+b)/2\), \(d=(a-b)/2\), and \(u=d^2\) removes the exchange symmetry without dividing away the critical branch. At the source's second shared-root value, the \((s,\tau)\)-Jacobian is explicitly nonzero. The implicit-function theorem therefore produces the unique local solution curve in \(u\), and the computed derivative \(C_k\) is strictly positive for every \(k\ge2\). Inverting \(\tau-\tau_2(k)=C_kd^2+O(d^4)\) proves both the supercritical side and the stated square-root amplitude. The proof does not infer an infinite statement from finite computation.

## Originality
PASS. The motivating paper arXiv:2110.10078 derives the all-\(k\) system and the intersection value \(\tau_2(k)\), but its Remark 2 says the unequal problem remains open for \(k\ge4\); no local pitchfork, side-of-bifurcation sign, or onset amplitude is stated. Full-text inspection of arXiv:2203.11446 found the same height-periodic family and small-degree classifications, but not this local normal form. The earlier arXiv:1902.04909 fully analyzes the relevant 4-periodic family only for \(k=2\). A 2023 generalized-SOS paper uses a different parity-dependent interaction and its explicit standard-form reductions do not supply this theorem. Exact-source, alias, bifurcation, and implication searches found no covering statement. Residual risk is an equivalent local calculation in weakly indexed literature.

## Value
PASS. The source singles out \(\tau_2(k)\) as the second contact between the symmetric and unequal-cycle algebra and explicitly leaves \(k\ge4\) unresolved. Showing that the contact is a uniformly supercritical symmetry-breaking bifurcation gives genuine existence information for every previously open degree and an exact onset coefficient, while carefully avoiding any unsupported global classification. This is structural phase-branch information, not an arbitrary parameter slice or numerical refinement.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is arXiv:2110.10078 itself: it supplies the system and shared-root threshold but not the local continuation proved here. arXiv:2203.11446 supplies the cyclic-shift identification and later small-period context. The result is local; other unequal branches may exist away from the neighborhood, and no statement about their number is made.
