# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Independent central edge basis elements force the complete determinant system on every graph edge. The support-component proof gives all free centralizer parameters and no others; the separate projective-line count proves the commuting formula without assuming odd characteristic. The rank-nullity step and central-factor multiplicities are explicit. An executable exhaustive checker tests the determinant system versus the support and projective counts in several fields, including \(\mathbf F_4\); computations are supporting samples and do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS (with limitations).** Barrionuevo–Tirao (arXiv:2206.10572v1, §§2–3) presents the graph algebra and classifies rigidity, not the centralizer rank spectrum or commuting-pair polynomial. Shamsaki–Erfanian–Parvizi (arXiv:2406.10064v1, §3) gives a general commutativity-degree identity, but does not evaluate it on graph Lie algebras. The polynomial \(Q\) itself, including its weighted-colouring model and tree evaluations, is prior work of Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky (arXiv:0812.4147v2, §4.2); it is not claimed new. The distinctive contribution is the all-graph exact centralizer/rank description and identification of the Lie count with that established polynomial. Studies of graph Lie rigidity, differently labeled graph Lie algebras, and noncommuting graphs were compared. A possible equivalent unlocated treatment of graph-Lie centralizers is a residual risk; absence of a search match is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The all-dimensional, all-graph centralizer classification is a structural Lie-theoretic invariant rather than an arbitrary isolated numeric example. It simultaneously yields the entire adjoint-rank distribution and exact commutativity degree in every finite characteristic and gives a practical forest evaluation. It answers a natural specialization of the finite-field commutativity-degree problem; the old \(Q\) theory alone does not formulate the Lie centralizers. No novelty is claimed for pre-existing graph-polynomial formulae.

**Closest literature and limits.** The three principal antecedents are graph Lie algebras (Barrionuevo–Tirao), finite-field Lie commutativity degree (Shamsaki–Erfanian–Parvizi), and the subgraph component polynomial (Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky). This result covers only simple graphs with independent edge-center generators; it does not extend without change to central labels shared across distinct edges, and finite tests do not establish infinite quantifiers.
