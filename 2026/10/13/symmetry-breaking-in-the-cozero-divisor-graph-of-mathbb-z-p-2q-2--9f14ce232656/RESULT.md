# Symmetry breaking in the cozero-divisor graph of \(\mathbb Z/(p^2q^2)\mathbb Z\)

## Finding

Let \(p\ne q\) be primes and let \(\Gamma'=\Gamma'(\mathbb Z/(p^2q^2)\mathbb Z)\) be the cozero-divisor graph on the nonzero nonunits. Put \(N=p^2q^2-pq(p-1)(q-1)-1\), and define \[W=\{(p-1)q(q-1),\ q(q-1),\ p(p-1)(q-1),\ p(p-1),\ (p-1)(q-1),\ p-1,\ q-1\}.\] Then \(\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma')\cong\prod_{w\in W}S_w\), with repeated factors interpreted as independent symmetric groups attached to the seven gcd classes, \[\operatorname{Det}(\Gamma')=N-7=p^2q^2-pq(p-1)(q-1)-8,\qquad D(\Gamma')=(p-1)(q-1)\max\{p,q\}.\]

The seven symmetric-group factors correspond to the seven nontrivial proper gcd classes. Although the unweighted seven-vertex quotient has four automorphisms, the arithmetic class sizes destroy all three nonidentity quotient symmetries when \(p\ne q\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let
\[
R=\mathbb Z/(p^2q^2)\mathbb Z
\]
for distinct primes \(p,q\). The cozero-divisor graph \(\Gamma'(R)\) has as vertices the nonzero nonunits, with two distinct vertices \(x,y\) adjacent exactly when
\[
x\notin Ry
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y\notin Rx.
\]

For \(x\in R\), the principal ideal \(Rx\) is determined by
\[
\gcd(x,p^2q^2).
\]
Thus the nonzero nonunit vertices split into seven gcd classes, indexed by the exponent pairs
\[
(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,2),(2,1).
\]

Write these classes respectively as
\[
A,B,C,D,E,F,G.
\]

## Proof

For exponent pairs \(u=(i,j)\) and \(v=(k,\ell)\), ideal containment is coordinatewise:
\[
(p^i q^j)\subseteq(p^kq^\ell)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
i\ge k,\ j\ge\ell.
\]
Consequently two gcd classes are joined completely in the cozero-divisor graph exactly when their exponent pairs are incomparable coordinatewise.

The quotient on \(A,\ldots,G\) therefore has edge set
\[
\{AC,AD,BC,BD,BE,BF,DE,DG,FG\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every gcd class is an independent set, and all vertices inside it have the same open neighborhood. The class sizes are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{class}&\text{size}\\ \hline
A&(p-1)q(q-1)\\
B&q(q-1)\\
C&p(p-1)(q-1)\\
D&p(p-1)\\
E&(p-1)(q-1)\\
F&p-1\\
G&q-1.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
These follow from the standard count
\[
|\{x:\gcd(x,p^2q^2)=d\}|=\varphi(p^2q^2/d).
\]

The quotient graph in (1) has exactly four automorphisms. Besides the identity, they are generated by the coordinate-swap symmetry and may be written
\[
(A\,C)(B\,D)(F\,G),
\tag{3}
\]
\[
(A\,F)(C\,G)(B\,D),
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
(A\,G)(C\,F).
\tag{5}
\]
The quotient has no pair of false twins or true twins, so the seven gcd classes are exactly the maximal twin classes of the full graph.

None of (3)--(5) preserves the weights in (2) when \(p\ne q\). Indeed, (3) would force
\[
|F|=|G|,
\]
hence \(p=q\). Symmetry (4) would force
\[
|A|=|F|,
\]
hence \(q(q-1)=1\), impossible for a prime \(q\). Symmetry (5) would force
\[
|A|=|G|,
\]
hence \(q(p-1)=1\), again impossible.

Therefore every automorphism of the full graph fixes each gcd class setwise. Conversely, every permutation inside any one gcd class is an automorphism because that class consists of false twins. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma')
\cong
S_{|A|}\times S_{|B|}\times S_{|C|}
\times S_{|D|}\times S_{|E|}\times S_{|F|}\times S_{|G|}.
\tag{6}
\]

The number of vertices is
\[
N=p^2q^2-\varphi(p^2q^2)-1
=
p^2q^2-pq(p-1)(q-1)-1.
\tag{7}
\]

For a determining set, in a false-twin class of size \(w\), at least \(w-1\) vertices must be fixed; otherwise a transposition of two unfixed twins survives. Taking all but one vertex from every gcd class fixes all seven internal symmetric groups, and the weighted quotient is rigid. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Det}(\Gamma')
=
\sum_{\text{seven classes}}(w-1)
=
N-7.
\tag{8}
\]

For a distinguishing labeling, every vertex in a false-twin class must receive a distinct color. Hence
\[
D(\Gamma')\ge \max W.
\]
Using a common palette of \(\max W\) colors and labeling every gcd class injectively destroys all internal permutations; weighted quotient rigidity leaves no further automorphism. Therefore equality holds.

From (2),
\[
\max W
=
(p-1)(q-1)\max\{p,q\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (6), (8), and (9) prove the finding.

## Relationship to prior work

Afkhami and Khashyarmanesh study the cozero-divisor graph for finite commutative rings and its planarity, outerplanarity, and ring-graph behavior. Their article lists \(13A99\) before the graph-theory classification and was published on 9 November 2012.

Mathil, Baloda, and Kumar later give an explicit structural decomposition of \(\Gamma'(\mathbb Z_n)\) by proper-divisor classes and study Laplacian spectra, connectivity, and Wiener-type questions. In particular, their divisor-class description implies the seven-class blow-up used here for \(n=p^2q^2\). The inspected full article does not state automorphism groups, determining numbers, or distinguishing numbers.

The result above uses that prior divisor-class architecture as input. Its additional content is the exact quotient symmetry calculation, the arithmetic proof that all nonidentity quotient symmetries are destroyed by class sizes for distinct primes, and the resulting full graph automorphism, determining, and distinguishing formulas.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to the balanced exponent pattern \(p^2q^2\) with distinct primes.

If the two prime parameters were formally equal, the coordinate-swap quotient symmetry would survive; this is excluded by the hypothesis.

For general \(p^a q^b\), the divisor quotient is a larger grid incomparability graph and can have different weighted symmetries.

The formulas concern graph automorphisms, not the image of ring automorphisms inside the graph automorphism group.
