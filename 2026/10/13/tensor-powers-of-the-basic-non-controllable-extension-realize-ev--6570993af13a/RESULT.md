# Tensor powers of the basic non-controllable extension realize every positive ideal-gap
## Finding
Let \(k\) be a perfect field with \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\). Let \(A=kQ\), where
\[
Q:\quad 1\xrightarrow{\alpha}2,\qquad 1\xrightarrow{\beta}3,
\]
and let \(B\subset A\) be the unital subalgebra generated by
\[
e_1,\qquad e_2+e_3,\qquad \alpha+\beta.
\]
For every integer \(r\ge1\), set
\[
A_r=A^{\otimes r},\qquad B_r=B^{\otimes r}.
\]
Then
\[
A_rJ(B_r)=J(A_r)\triangleleft A_r,
\qquad
A_r/A_rJ(B_r)\cong k^{3^r},
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{gldim}(A_r,B_r)=r.
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{gldim}(A_r,B_r)
-\operatorname{gldim}\!\left(A_r/A_rJ(B_r)\right)=r.
\]
Thus every positive integer occurs as the gap for an extension satisfying \(AJ(B)\triangleleft A\), over every perfect field of characteristic different from \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All tensor products are over \(k\). Relative projective dimension and relative global dimension are those of Hochschild relative homological algebra for a unital extension \(B\subset A\).

The source paper works over perfect fields and proves its tensor-product results in Section 4 under \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\). The present claim uses those tensor-product theorems, so it retains that characteristic restriction. No assertion is made here in characteristic \(2\).

Write \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) for the primitive idempotents of \(A\), with path-algebra convention
\[
e_2\alpha=\alpha=\alpha e_1,\qquad
e_3\beta=\beta=\beta e_1.
\]
The source's Example 5.1 proves
\[
AJ(B)=J(A),\qquad
\operatorname{gldim}(A,B)=1,
\]
and exhibits an \(A\)-module \(M\) with
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{(A,B)}M=1.
\]

## Proof
The new point is that the regular bimodule of Example 5.1 has relative projective dimension exactly \(1\).

Consider the relative bar multiplication map
\[
\mu:A\otimes_B A\longrightarrow A,\qquad x\otimes y\longmapsto xy.
\]
Balancing over the three-dimensional algebra
\[
B=k e_1\oplus k(e_2+e_3)\oplus k(\alpha+\beta)
\]
reduces \(A\otimes_BA\) to the following nine-element basis:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&e_1\otimes e_1,\\
&e_2\otimes e_2,\ e_2\otimes e_3,\ e_2\otimes\alpha,\ e_2\otimes\beta,\\
&e_3\otimes e_2,\ e_3\otimes e_3,\ e_3\otimes\alpha,\ e_3\otimes\beta.
\end{aligned}
\]
On these classes, \(\mu\) has five independent nonzero images
\[
e_1,\quad e_2,\quad \alpha,\quad e_3,\quad \beta,
\]
so its kernel is four-dimensional, with basis
\[
e_2\otimes e_3,\qquad
e_2\otimes\beta,\qquad
e_3\otimes e_2,\qquad
e_3\otimes\alpha.
\]
As an \(A\)-bimodule this kernel is
\[
\ker\mu
\cong
Ae_2\otimes_k e_3A
\ \oplus\
Ae_3\otimes_k e_2A.
\]
Indeed the first summand maps onto the span of
\(e_2\otimes e_3,e_2\otimes\beta\), and the second maps onto the span of
\(e_3\otimes e_2,e_3\otimes\alpha\). Each summand is an ordinary projective \(A^e\)-module, hence also \((A^e,B^e)\)-relatively projective.

Moreover \(A\otimes_BA\cong A^e\otimes_{B^e}B\) is \((A^e,B^e)\)-relatively projective, and the sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\ker\mu
\longrightarrow A\otimes_BA
\xrightarrow{\mu}A
\longrightarrow0
\]
is \(B^e\)-split: \(a\mapsto1\otimes a\) is a \(B\)-bimodule section of \(\mu\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{(A^e,B^e)}A\le1.
\]
The general inequality
\[
\operatorname{gldim}(A,B)
\le
\operatorname{pd}_{(A^e,B^e)}A
\]
together with the source computation \(\operatorname{gldim}(A,B)=1\) gives
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{(A^e,B^e)}A=1.
\]

Now apply the source's tensor-product results repeatedly. Corollary 4.10 gives
\[
\operatorname{gldim}(A_r,B_r)\le r.
\]
On the other hand, Theorem 4.9 applied to the source module \(M\) gives
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{(A_r,B_r)}(M^{\otimes r})
=
r\,\operatorname{pd}_{(A,B)}M
=r.
\]
Hence
\[
\operatorname{gldim}(A_r,B_r)=r.
\]

It remains to verify the ideal condition. Since
\[
B/J(B)\cong k^2,\qquad A/J(A)\cong k^3,
\]
their semisimple quotients are separable. The standard radical formula for tensor products therefore gives
\[
J(B_r)=
\sum_{i=1}^r
B^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes J(B)\otimes B^{\otimes(r-i)}
\]
and the analogous formula for \(J(A_r)\). Using \(AJ(B)=J(A)\) and \(AB=A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_rJ(B_r)
&=
\sum_{i=1}^r
A^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes AJ(B)\otimes A^{\otimes(r-i)}\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^r
A^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes J(A)\otimes A^{\otimes(r-i)}\\
&=J(A_r).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(A_rJ(B_r)\) is a two-sided ideal and
\[
A_r/A_rJ(B_r)\cong (A/J(A))^{\otimes r}\cong k^{3^r},
\]
whose ordinary global dimension is \(0\). The claimed gap is therefore exactly \(r\).

## Verification
The standalone checker `check_base.py` reconstructs \(A\), \(B\), and the balanced tensor product \(A\otimes_BA\) over the rationals from their multiplication tables. It verifies exactly that the balancing-relation space has rank \(16\), that the nine displayed tensor classes form a quotient basis, that the multiplication kernel has dimension \(4\) with exactly the four displayed basis classes, and that the two indicated \(A^e\)-projective summands map isomorphically onto that kernel. It terminates with `CHECK_OK`.

The checker is a finite verification of the fixed base extension only. The theorem for every \(r\) is not inferred from finite experiments; it follows symbolically from the base bimodule calculation, Theorem 4.9, Corollary 4.10, and the radical tensor formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Iusenko and Primolan's Example 5.1 is exactly the base extension above. They prove \(AJ(B)=J(A)\triangleleft A\), semisimplicity of the quotient, and relative global dimension \(1\). Their Section 4 proves the tensor-product projective-dimension equality and the global upper bound used here. However, their Theorem 4.12 gives additivity only for homologically controllable extensions, whereas Example 5.1 is explicitly non-controllable.

Their Proposition 5.5 constructs, for every \(n\ge1\), other non-controllable extensions with gap \(n\), but Remark 5.6 explicitly states that it is unknown whether every positive integer can be realized while also requiring \(AJ(B)\triangleleft A\). The tensor powers above satisfy precisely that missing ideal condition. The additional base calculation
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{(A^e,B^e)}A=1
\]
is what makes Corollary 4.10 sharp for these non-controllable tensor powers.

Primolan's 2023 dissertation contains the same three-vertex non-controllable example as Example 5.5.3 and earlier tensor-extension material, but the inspected portions do not state the regular-bimodule calculation above or the resulting all-positive ideal-gap family.

## Limitations
The proof uses the source's Section 4 tensor-product theorems, which are stated under \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\). Characteristic \(2\) is not settled here.

The construction answers only the positive-gap part of Remark 5.6 under this characteristic hypothesis. The same remark's question about negative gaps remains open.

The originality assessment is based on the full motivating preprint, the directly relevant dissertation material, targeted web searches, and semantic database searches. It is not a claim that no equivalent observation exists in unindexed literature.
