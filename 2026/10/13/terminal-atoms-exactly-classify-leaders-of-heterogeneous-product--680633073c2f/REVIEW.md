# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. All cases in the terminal-atom classification have complete analytic proofs. A top-vector hit gives the strong positive case. A measurable sample-maximizing index, independent of other coordinate samples, gives the uniform positive bound when only one top atom is missing. When two are missing, a threshold event bound and a diagonal choice of upper-tail probabilities force a subsequence of leader probabilities to vanish, regardless of ties. Dominated convergence justifies the maximum-index probability tending to zero. Exact rational verification replays selected finite cases without substituting for the infinite argument.

## Originality
PASS, with residual risk. The direct 2022 paper defines the same positive-lower-limit leader property and explicitly poses the independent-product questions. Its inspected relevant propositions treat restricted finite/continuous or regular atomic settings and do not give the heterogeneous terminal-atom iff classification. The same-author follow-ups studied different dependence or mapped-geometric settings. Candidate-specific searches for the exact structural criterion and its common-argmax equivalent found no published covering statement. An equivalent theorem might still occur in broader argmax or occupancy terminology.

## Value
PASS. This settles a natural complete structural question posed in the motivating work, identifying exactly which one-dimensional marginal endpoint features govern leader persistence in any fixed dimension. The rare-upper-tail obstruction works without regularity or atom restrictions, and the atomless-exception formula gives a sharp quantitative boundary.
