# The 144 edge one-factorizations of the Tutte–Coxeter graph form a single symmetry class

## Finding

Let \(T\) be the thirty-vertex Tutte–Coxeter graph, identified with the bipartite incidence graph of the fifteen duads (two-element subsets) and fifteen synthemes (partitions into three duads) of a six-element set. Then \(T\) has exactly \(144\) unordered one-factorizations, or \(864\) proper edge-colorings with three named colors. **Every** one-factorization has a distinguished matching whose complement is one Hamiltonian \(30\)-cycle, while the complements of its other two matchings each comprise one \(10\)-cycle and one \(20\)-cycle. Thus every named proper three-edge-coloring has exactly one Hamiltonian pair of colors. The automorphism group of order \(1440\) acts transitively on the \(144\) one-factorizations (stabilizer order \(10\)).

Equivalently, all \(288\) perfect matchings split into exactly two automorphism orbits, each of size \(144\), distinguished by the complementary two-factor's cycle type: \((30)\) versus \((10,20)\). Every matching of the first kind lies in exactly one one-factorization; every matching of the second kind lies in exactly two. The scalar numbers \(288\) perfect matchings and \(144\) Hamilton cycles were reported earlier [2,3] and are not asserted as discoveries here. The new classification concerns the two-factor cycle spectrum, the decomposition into edge-colorings, the forced one-versus-two Hamiltonian-pair pattern, and the symmetry actions.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(T\) has left vertices \(d_{ab}\), one per two-element subset \(\{a,b\}\subset\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), and right vertices \(s_P\), one per partition \(P\) of the six symbols into three unordered pairs. An edge joins \(d_{ab}\) to \(s_P\) exactly when \(\{a,b\}\in P\). Labels in the verification files are lexicographic: duads are \(0,\ldots,14\), synthemes are \(15,\ldots,29\). Every vertex has degree three; the graph has \(45\) edges. A perfect matching (one-factor) is a set of \(15\) disjoint edges covering all vertices. A one-factorization is an unordered triple of disjoint perfect matchings partitioning all \(45\) edges. A proper three-edge-coloring with fixed named colors is an ordered version of that triple.

A two-factor is a spanning disjoint union of cycles, and \(T\setminus M\) denotes the two-factor of edges outside a perfect matching \(M\). All counts refer to the one fixed abstract labeled graph described above, not to graph-labeling counts or drawings.

## Proof

**(1) Exhaustive perfect-matching census.** Construct all \(15\) synthemes by recursively pairing the smallest unused \(K_6\) symbol with each remaining symbol. Enumerate perfect matchings of \(T\) by choosing the least unmatched graph vertex \(v\) and branching once for each unmatched neighbor \(w\), removing \(v,w\). The recursion is exact because every perfect matching contains exactly one such edge. In particular, setting \(P(\emptyset)=1\) and \(P(U)=\sum_{w\in N(v)\cap U}P(U\setminus\{v,w\})\) for the least \(v\in U\), the independent counting recurrence gives \(P(V(T))=288\). The verifier also retains all \(288\) distinct matching edge sets.

**(2) Exact two-factor partition.** Because \(T\) is cubic, removing a perfect matching leaves degree two at every vertex; because \(T\) is bipartite, all components are even cycles. For every one of the \(288\) matchings, the verifier deletes its \(15\) edges and visits every remaining component by a complete graph traversal. It finds precisely \(144\) single cycles of length \(30\) and \(144\) pairs of cycles with lengths \(10\) and \(20\). The standalone C++ program independently reconstructs the incidence graph, enumerates all matchings as bijections from the \(15\) duad vertices to adjacent synthemes, and obtains the identical partition. This is an exhaustive finite proof, not a sample.

**(3) Automorphism completeness and the two matching orbits.** Any automorphism preserving the two bipartition classes induces an automorphism of the disjointness graph on the \(15\) duads, since two duads have a common syntheme neighbor exactly when they are disjoint. A maximal pairwise-intersecting family of two-subsets of a six-element set has size at most five; if it has more than three members, they all share a symbol. Thus the six five-element stars are intrinsically determined, so every side-preserving automorphism induces a permutation of the six underlying symbols. Conversely, all \(6!=720\) permutations induce side-preserving graph automorphisms, and their action on synthemes is determined by their neighborhoods. The explicit permutation `DUAL` in `verify.py` interchanges the two sides and preserves all \(45\) edges. Consequently the full automorphism group has exactly \(1440\) elements, generated by the above \(720\) permutations and their compositions with `DUAL`. Apply each to two representative perfect matchings in `representatives.csv`: their disjoint orbits have size \(144\) each and contain all \(288\) matchings. Their complementary cycle profiles are respectively \((30)\) and \((10,20)\).

**(4) All one-factorizations.** Fix a perfect matching \(M\). On each even cycle of \(T\setminus M\), the other two named colors must alternate; they have exactly two possible starting parities, independently over all cycles. Therefore \(M\) admits exactly \(2^{c(M)}\) ordered completions, where \(c(M)\) is the number of complementary cycles. Summing over every choice of the first named color gives \(144\cdot2+144\cdot4=864\) proper three-edge-colorings. Relabeling the three distinct color classes acts freely, so there are exactly \(864/6=144\) unordered one-factorizations. This also gives one factorization containing any matching of cycle type \((30)\), and two containing any matching of type \((10,20)\).

**(5) Symmetry and the uniform bichromatic pattern.** Compute the unordered triple orbit of the explicit one-factorization given in `representatives.csv` under the \(1440\) graph automorphisms. It has size \(144\) and therefore equals the entire set of factorizations from (4). Direct complementary-component checks for the representative show exactly one member of profile \((30)\) and two of profile \((10,20)\). Automorphisms preserve cycle lengths, so the same is true of every factorization. Since a pair of the three colors has as its complement exactly the third color class, precisely one of the three bichromatic spanning two-factors is Hamiltonian and the other two are disjoint \(10\)- and \(20\)-cycles. The stabilizer of any factorization consequently has size \(1440/144=10\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` and `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_independent.cpp -o verify_independent && ./verify_independent` with the two source files extracted into the same directory. The Python program uses only the standard library; it independently checks a deletion recurrence and full backtracking, all perfect matchings, all complementary component sizes, every one of the \(1440\) displayed group permutations against every graph edge, both matching orbits, all factorizations, and the single factorization orbit. It deterministically regenerates `representatives.csv`. The independent C++ program uses left-to-right bipartite assignment and bitwise matching-set complements, then checks all factorization triples, with no dependence on the Python output. Both were run successfully before release.

## Relationship to prior work

John Baez [1] explicitly described the duad–syntheme construction and outer \(S_6\) symmetry of this graph in an article publicly dated 15 August 2015; this provides the archival starting point and object definition. Balasubramanian [2, Table 1, pp. 10–13] computed the ordinary matching polynomial, including its known constant coefficient \(288\), but did not classify complementary two-factor cycle types or edge-coloring factorizations. Garcia [3, Section 4] independently reported that the graph has \(144\) Hamilton cycles, in a study of the Erdős–Gyárfás problem; the paper does not give the other complementary cycle type or three-edge-coloring orbit theorem. Dejter [4, Example 4 and Section 7] studies girth-tight edge colorings of derived prism graphs and other families, including an obstruction from the Tutte eight-cage, but not the complete ordinary edge-coloring census or symmetry classification of the cage itself. These earlier scalar counts and constructions do not logically determine the asserted uniform bichromatic pattern or transitivity.

## Limitations

The statement is a finite exact classification for this particular thirty-vertex graph; it is not a theorem for all cages, cubic symmetric graphs, or generalized quadrangles. Its proof contains a reproducible exhaustive finite calculation and a separate structural automorphism argument. Nothing here asserts independent auditing, publication priority, or an unproved extrapolation. Unindexed prior classifications of Tait colorings, particularly under alternate names for the incidence geometry, remain a priority risk despite targeted literature and research-record searches.
