# The 45 tritangents of a cyclic cubic surface lie over the Hesse affine plane
## Finding
Let \(E=V(f)\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf C}\) be a smooth plane cubic and let
\[
X_f=V\!\left(w^3-f(x,y,z)ight)\subset\mathbf P^3
\]
be its smooth cyclic cubic surface. Write \(\Omega\) for a generator of the deck group. The \(27\) lines of \(X_f\) split into nine \(\Omega\)-orbits of size three, indexed by the nine flexes of \(E\), equivalently by \(E[3]\cong\mathbf F_3^2\).

For a tritangent plane, project its three constituent lines to their deck-orbits. Then the complete set of \(45\) tritangent planes has the following structure:
\[
45=9+36.
\]
Exactly nine tritangents project to singletons. These are the planes over the nine flex tangents, each containing the three lines in one deck-orbit, and each is fixed by \(\Omega\). The other \(36\) tritangents use three distinct deck-orbits. Their projections are precisely the twelve affine lines in \(E[3]\cong\mathbf F_3^2\), and every affine line supports exactly three tritangents, cyclically permuted by \(\Omega\). Thus the projected triples form the Hesse Steiner triple system on the nine flexes: every pair of flexes occurs in exactly one projected triple.

The monodromy group computed by Medrano Martín del Campo,
\[
H_3(\mathbf F_3)times\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3),
\qquad |G|=648,
\]
therefore has exactly two orbits on the \(45\) tritangents, of sizes \(9\) and \(36\). Their stabilizer orders are \(72\) and \(18\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbf C\). The branch cubic \(E\) is smooth, so \(X_f\) is a smooth cubic surface. The result concerns the incidence local system of its \(27\) lines and \(45\) tritangent planes together with the deck action. The identification of the nine deck-orbits with \(E[3]\) is the one induced by the nine inflection points described in the cited monodromy paper; changing the affine origin merely relabels the affine plane.

The phrase “non-deck-fixed” is intentionally used for the \(36\)-element class. On a generic cyclic cubic surface these tritangents are not Eckardt planes, but special cyclic cubic surfaces can have extra Eckardt points. No universal assertion that the \(36\) planes are non-Eckardt is made.

## Proof
Use the standard Picard marking of a smooth cubic surface with basis \(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_6\), intersection form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,\ldots,-1)\), and the \(27\) line classes
\[
E_i=e_i,\qquad F_{ij}=e_0-e_i-e_j,\qquad G_i=2e_0-\sum_{k
e i}e_k.
\]
Medrano Martín del Campo gives an explicit integral matrix for the deck transformation \(\Omega\) in this marking. Exact application of that matrix to the \(27\) classes gives nine cycles of length three. The cited geometric construction identifies those nine cycles with the nine inflection points of the branch cubic.

Two distinct line classes on a smooth cubic surface meet exactly when their Picard intersection number is \(1\). Hence a tritangent plane is represented combinatorially by a triple of pairwise meeting line classes. Exhaustive exact enumeration of the \(\binom{27}{3}\) triples gives exactly \(45\) such triangles. Projecting each triangle to its set of \(\Omega\)-cycles gives exactly nine singleton projections, each with multiplicity one, and twelve three-element projections, each with multiplicity three.

For the twelve three-element projections, exact pair counting shows that all \(\binom{9}{2}=36\) pairs of deck-orbits occur exactly once. Consequently these twelve blocks form a Steiner triple system on nine points. Under the source identification of the nine points with \(E[3]\cong\mathbf F_3^2\), the monodromy quotient acts by affine transformations generated by translations and \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\). The invariant twelve-block Steiner system is therefore the twelve affine lines of the Hesse affine plane. Equivalently, this is the standard \((9_4,12_3)\) Hesse incidence structure.

The deck generator fixes the nine singleton tritangents. It cannot fix any tritangent whose three lines belong to three distinct deck-orbits: on each such orbit it moves the unique selected line. Since each projected affine line has exactly three lifts, \(\Omega\) cycles those three lifts. The affine monodromy is transitive on the nine points and on the twelve affine lines, and the central deck action is transitive on the three lifts over each affine line. Thus the full order-\(648\) monodromy has exactly two tritangent orbits, of sizes \(9\) and \(36\). Orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(648/9=72\) and \(648/36=18\).

## Verification
The accompanying verifier uses only exact integer linear algebra and finite enumeration. It checks that the published deck matrix preserves the cubic-surface intersection form, permutes the standard \(27\) line classes in nine \(3\)-cycles, and that there are exactly \(135\) intersecting line pairs and \(45\) pairwise-intersecting line triples. It then proves that projection to deck-cycles has profile nine singleton blocks of multiplicity one plus twelve three-point blocks of multiplicity three, and that each of the \(36\) unordered pairs of deck-cycles occurs in exactly one three-point block. Finally it computes the deck action on the \(45\) tritangents and obtains nine fixed tritangents plus twelve \(3\)-cycles.

The finite checker certifies the line-incidence and deck-action calculation. The identification of deck-cycles with branch flexes and the order-\(648\) monodromy group are imported from the cited source, and the monodromy-orbit conclusion then follows formally from those source results and the verified block structure.

## Relationship to prior work
Medrano Martín del Campo constructs the cyclic cubic surface family, relates the nine branch inflection points to the \(27\) lines, computes the deck permutation, and proves that the monodromy image is the order-\(648\) centralizer of the deck action. The inspected source does not state the induced organization of all \(45\) tritangent planes into a nine-element fixed class and a three-sheeted lift of the twelve Hesse affine lines.

Brundu, Logar, and Polli give an explicit classification of symmetric cubic surfaces, enumerate the \(45\) tritangent planes in standard line labels, and for their generic nine-Eckardt-point family identify the nine coplanar Eckardt points as a Hesse configuration. Their inspected discussion of that family gives projective stabilizer permutations but does not state the deck-projection Steiner system or the monodromy orbit decomposition proved here.

Targeted published-record searches for “cyclic cubic surface tritangent planes,” “deck transformation tritangent planes,” “Steiner triple system,” “Hesse affine plane,” and the numerical \(9+36\) formulation found no statement implying the complete claim. The closest indexed finding concerns sixers on the Fermat cubic surface and is not a statement about cyclic-cover tritangents.

## Limitations
The result is a combinatorial and monodromy statement for the cyclic cubic family over \(\mathbf C\). It does not classify projective automorphism orbits on every individual member. In particular, special fibers can have projective symmetry or extra Eckardt points beyond the generic nine. The proof does not claim a new classification of cubic surfaces, and it does not identify scheme structures on parameter-space degeneracy loci. A residual literature risk remains that an older classical treatment of trihedral pairs encodes the same twelve projected triples in different terminology.
