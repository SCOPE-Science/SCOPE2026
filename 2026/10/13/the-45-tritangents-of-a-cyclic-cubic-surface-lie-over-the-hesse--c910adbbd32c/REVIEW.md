# Review of The 45 tritangents of a cyclic cubic surface lie over the Hesse affine plane

## Correctness
PASS. The proof uses the source's explicit integral deck matrix on the standard Picard lattice and independently reconstructs all \(27\) line classes, their incidence relation, and all \(45\) tritangent triples. Exact enumeration gives nine singleton deck-fiber projections and twelve three-fiber projections, each of the latter with exactly three lifts. Every pair among the nine deck-fibers occurs in exactly one three-point block, proving the Steiner/Hesse affine-plane structure. The source supplies the identification of the nine deck-fibers with branch flexes and the order-\(648\) monodromy group; transitivity then yields monodromy orbit sizes \(9\) and \(36\), with stabilizers \(72\) and \(18\).

## Originality
PASS. The closest primary monodromy source organizes the \(27\) lines into nine flex-associated triples and determines the monodromy centralizer, but it does not state the induced structure on all \(45\) tritangent planes. The closest symmetric-cubic source explicitly handles lines, tritangents, and the nine-Eckardt Hesse configuration, but its inspected nine-Eckardt discussion does not give the deck-projection Steiner system or this family-monodromy orbit decomposition. Targeted published-record searches using cyclic-cover, tritangent, Hesse, affine-plane, Steiner-system, deck-action, and \(9+36\) formulations found no covering statement.

## Value
PASS. Tritangent planes are one of the two canonical finite configurations attached to a cubic surface alongside its \(27\) lines. The source explains the cyclic-cover action on the lines; the new result completes that picture on all \(45\) tritangents and shows that the classical Hesse affine plane is not only visible in the branch flexes but controls the entire nonfixed tritangent system via a natural three-sheeted lift. The result also gives a compact monodromy invariant \(9+36\) with exact stabilizers.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest literature is arXiv:1910.09593 / DOI:10.5802/aif.3481 for the cyclic family, deck action, flex correspondence, and monodromy group, and arXiv:2207.04493 for explicit symmetric cubic surfaces, tritangents, and the nine-point Hesse configuration. The claim is about monodromy of the family, not the projective automorphism group of each member. Special members may have extra Eckardt points, so no claim that all \(36\) non-deck-fixed tritangents are geometrically non-Eckardt is made. Older classical trihedral-pair terminology remains a residual originality risk.
