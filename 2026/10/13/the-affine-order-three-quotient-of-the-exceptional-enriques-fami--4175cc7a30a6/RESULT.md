# The affine order-three quotient of the exceptional Enriques family

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let \(\alpha\in k\). Katsura--Schütt's exceptional family of supersingular Enriques surfaces with a cohomologically trivial automorphism of order three has an affine normal-form chart
\[
y^2=t x^4+\alpha t^5x^2+t^7x+t^3,
\]
with
\[
\phi(t,x,y)=(\zeta t,\zeta^2x,y),\qquad \zeta^3=1,\quad \zeta\ne1.
\]
For this affine chart, the categorical quotient by \(\langle\phi\rangle\) is the complete intersection
\[
u^3=sv,\qquad y^2=uv+\alpha s u^2+s^2u+s
\]
in invariant coordinates \(s=t^3\), \(u=tx\), and \(v=x^3\). It has exactly one singular point, \(s=u=v=y=0\), and the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity at that point is \(3\). On \(s\ne0\), eliminating \(v\) gives
\[
s y^2=u^4+\alpha s^2u^2+s^3u+s^2.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The statement concerns the affine normal-form chart appearing in the cited classification theorem, not a global compactification or a resolution of the quotient of the smooth Enriques surface. The ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\); in particular, the cyclic group of order \(3\) is linearly reductive. The parameter \(\alpha\) is arbitrary.

## Proof
Write
\[
A_\alpha=k[t,x,y]/(R),\qquad R=y^2+t x^4+\alpha t^5x^2+t^7x+t^3.
\]
The action has weights \(1\), \(2\), and \(0\) modulo \(3\) on \(t\), \(x\), and \(y\). An invariant monomial \(t^a x^b\) satisfies \(a+2b\equiv0\pmod3\). If \(a\ge b\), it is a product of a power of \(u=tx\) and a power of \(s=t^3\); if \(b\ge a\), it is a product of a power of \(u\) and a power of \(v=x^3\). Hence
\[
k[t,x,y]^{\langle\phi\rangle}=k[s,u,v,y]/(u^3-sv).
\]
Because \(3\) is invertible in characteristic \(2\), taking invariants is exact. Moreover, \(R\) itself is invariant, so the contraction of the principal ideal \((R)\) to the invariant ring is generated by the invariant image of \(R\). Substitution gives
\[
R=y^2+uv+\alpha s u^2+s^2u+s.
\]
This proves the displayed quotient equations.

For the singular locus, set
\[
F_1=u^3+sv,\qquad F_2=y^2+uv+\alpha s u^2+s^2u+s.
\]
In characteristic \(2\), the nonzero columns of the Jacobian rows are
\[
(v,u^2,s),\qquad (1+\alpha u^2,v+s^2,u)
\]
with respect to \((s,u,v)\). The \((u,v)\)-minor is
\[
u^3+sv+s^3,
\]
which equals \(s^3\) on \(F_1=0\). Thus a singular point has \(s=0\), and then \(F_1=0\) gives \(u=0\). The \((s,u)\)-minor then becomes \(v^2\), so \(v=0\), and \(F_2=0\) gives \(y=0\). Conversely, the Jacobian has rank \(1<2\) at the origin, so the origin is singular and is the only singular point.

Finally, \(F_2\) has linear term \(s\), so in the completed local ring it solves uniquely for \(s\), with
\[
s\equiv y^2+uv\pmod{\mathfrak m^4}.
\]
Substituting into \(F_1\), the resulting hypersurface in \(k[[u,v,y]]\) has cubic initial form
\[
u^3+u v^2+v y^2.
\]
Therefore its order, and hence its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity, is \(3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Katsura and Schütt classify all Enriques surfaces carrying a cohomologically trivial order-three automorphism and give precisely the displayed one-parameter normal form and diagonal action. Their paper does not give the invariant coordinate ring or the singularity calculation for the affine categorical quotient. Their introduction also explains that the earlier work of Dolgachev--Martin left the order-three existence case open, so that earlier classification cannot contain this source-specific quotient model. The present statement starts from the published normal form and computes the complete invariant ring rather than merely the invariant function field.

## Limitations
The quotient described here is the affine categorical quotient of the published normal-form chart. No claim is made about the global singularity basket of a projective compactification, the minimal resolution of the global quotient, or the analytic ADE type of the unique affine singularity. The multiplicity statement is local and exact; the displayed hypersurface after eliminating \(v\) is asserted only on \(s\ne0\).
