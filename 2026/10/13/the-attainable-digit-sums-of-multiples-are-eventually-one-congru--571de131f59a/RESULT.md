# The attainable digit sums of multiples are eventually one congruence class

## Finding
For integers \(b\ge2\) and \(n\ge1\), let
\[
D_{b,n}=\{s_b(kn):k\ge1\},
\]
where \(s_b\) denotes the base-\(b\) digit sum. Define
\[
g=\gcd(n,b-1),\qquad q=\frac{b-1}{g},\qquad T=\max_{1\le j\le q}s_b(jn).
\]
Then
\[
D_{b,n}\subseteq g\mathbb N,
\]
and every positive multiple \(t\) of \(g\) with \(t\ge T\) lies in \(D_{b,n}\).

In addition, \(D_{b,n}\cup\{0\}\) is closed under addition. Consequently
\[
\{0\}\cup g^{-1}D_{b,n}
\]
is a numerical semigroup, with conductor at most \(\lceil T/g\rceil\).

Thus the complete infinite tail of the digit-sum spectrum of multiples of \(n\) is determined by the single necessary congruence condition modulo \(\gcd(n,b-1)\); only finitely many smaller admissible digit sums can fail to occur.

## Assumptions and scope
The notation \(s_b(N)\) means the sum of the digits in the ordinary base-\(b\) expansion of the nonnegative integer \(N\). The set \(D_{b,n}\) uses positive multiples of \(n\).

The theorem gives an explicit sufficient tail threshold \(T\). It does not claim that \(T\) is the least possible threshold. The exact finite exceptional set below \(T\) can depend on \(b\) and \(n\).

## Proof
For every integer \(N\ge0\),
\[
s_b(N)\equiv N\pmod{b-1}.
\]
Hence, if \(N=kn\), then \(g\mid N\) and \(g\mid b-1\), so
\[
g\mid s_b(kn).
\]
This proves the necessary divisibility condition.

Now let \(t\ge T\) be a positive multiple of \(g\). Since
\[
\gcd\!\left(\frac ng,\frac{b-1}{g}\right)=1,
\]
there is a unique residue class \(j\pmod q\) such that
\[
jn\equiv t\pmod{b-1}.
\]
Choose its representative with \(1\le j\le q\), and put
\[
u=s_b(jn).
\]
Then \(u\le T\le t\), while
\[
u\equiv jn\equiv t\pmod{b-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
t=u+r(b-1)
\]
for some integer \(r\ge0\).

Clokie, Lidbetter, Molina Lovett, Shallit, and Witzman proved that whenever one multiple of \(n\) has digit sum \(u\), then for every \(r\ge0\) another multiple has digit sum \(u+r(b-1)\). Applying that result to the multiple \(jn\) gives a positive integer \(k\) with
\[
s_b(kn)=t.
\]
This proves that every sufficiently large multiple of \(g\) occurs.

It remains to prove additive closure. Suppose
\[
s_b(k_1n)=u_1,
\qquad
s_b(k_2n)=u_2.
\]
Choose \(L\) larger than the number of base-\(b\) digits of \(k_1n\). Then the base-\(b\) digit blocks of \(k_1n\) and \(b^Lk_2n\) are disjoint, so
\[
s_b\!\left((k_1+b^Lk_2)n\right)=u_1+u_2.
\]
Thus \(D_{b,n}\cup\{0\}\) is an additive submonoid of the nonnegative integers. After division by \(g\), its complement is finite by the tail theorem above, so it is a numerical semigroup. If
\[
u\ge\left\lceil\frac Tg\right\rceil,
\]
then \(gu\ge T\), hence \(u\in g^{-1}D_{b,n}\); this gives the stated conductor bound.

Finite replay is not the proof of the infinite statement. The infinite result follows from the congruence argument and the published progression theorem, with additive closure proved directly above.

## Relationship to prior work
Clokie, Lidbetter, Molina Lovett, Shallit, and Witzman study the minimum digit sum of a multiple and related sturdy/flimsy questions. Their Theorem 4 proves the progression principle used here: if a digit sum \(u\) occurs for a multiple of \(n\), then every \(u+r(b-1)\) occurs. They also explicitly note that in base \(3\) the attainable sums need not begin at the minimum; for example, \(n=13\) has minimum digit sum \(3\), but digit sum \(4\) does not occur.

The present theorem combines the progression principle with the complete residue set generated by the first \((b-1)/g\) multiples and with additive block concatenation. This yields a global description not stated in the focal paper: after a finite threshold, the attainable spectrum is exactly the positive multiples of \(\gcd(n,b-1)\), and after scaling it is a numerical semigroup.

Targeted searches for “attainable digit sums” of multiples, cofinite digit-sum spectra, and numerical-semigroup formulations did not locate this conclusion. Older work on digit sums of multiples and the OEIS entries cited by the focal paper concern minima, witnesses, or special equalities rather than the full eventual spectrum.

## Limitations
The threshold
\[
T=\max_{1\le j\le(b-1)/g}s_b(jn)
\]
is explicit but need not equal the true conductor after scaling. Determining the exact finite set of missing admissible digit sums below this bound remains a separate finite problem for each \((b,n)\).

The originality comparison is strongest against the full text of the focal paper and targeted indexed searches. A short unpublished or unindexed corollary of its progression theorem remains a residual risk.
