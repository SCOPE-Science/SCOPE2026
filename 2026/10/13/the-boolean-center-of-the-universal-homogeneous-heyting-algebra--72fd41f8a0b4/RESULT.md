# The Boolean center of the universal homogeneous Heyting algebra is atomless, with dense automorphism shadow
## Finding
Let \(L\) be the Fraïssé limit of all finite nontrivial Heyting algebras and let
\[
Z(L)=\{a\in L:a\vee\neg a=1\}.
\]
Then \(Z(L)\), with the operations inherited from \(L\), is the countable atomless Boolean algebra. Restriction gives a continuous homomorphism
\[
\rho:\operatorname{Aut}(L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(Z(L))
\]
with dense image in the pointwise-convergence topology. Consequently, for every \(n\ge0\), the number of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\)-orbits on ordered \(n\)-tuples from \(Z(L)\) is
\[
\boxed{2^{2^n}-1}.
\]
The first values are \(1,3,15,255,65535\) for \(n=0,1,2,3,4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The center means the complemented elements of a Heyting algebra, equivalently the elements satisfying \(a\vee\neg a=1\). Complemented elements form a Boolean Heyting subalgebra with inherited operations. Yamamoto proves that \(L\) is countable, locally finite, universal for finite nontrivial Heyting algebras, and ultrahomogeneous. No claim is made that \(\rho\) is injective or surjective.

## Proof
Take \(0<c\in Z(L)\). Local finiteness gives a finite Heyting subalgebra \(A\le L\) containing \(c\). Write \(A\cong\operatorname{Up}(P)\) for its finite dual poset. The nonempty upset representing \(c\) contains a maximal point \(m\) of \(P\). Evaluation at \(m\), given by \(h(U)=1\) iff \(m\in U\), is a Heyting homomorphism \(h:A\to\mathbf 2\): at a maximal point the upset implication condition reduces to Boolean implication. Moreover \(h(c)=1\).

Form \(B=A\times\mathbf2\) and the graph embedding \(e(a)=(a,h(a))\). Then \(d=(0,1)\) is central in \(B\) and \(0<d<e(c)\). Embed \(B\) into \(L\). By ultrahomogeneity, postcompose with an automorphism of \(L\) so that the composite fixes \(A\) along \(e\). The image of \(d\) is then a central element strictly between \(0\) and \(c\). Thus \(Z(L)\) is atomless; since it is countable, it is the countable atomless Boolean algebra.

Every automorphism of \(L\) preserves the defining equation of the center, so restriction defines a continuous homomorphism \(\rho\). To prove density, fix \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(Z(L))\) and finite \(F\subseteq Z(L)\). Let \(C\) be the finite Boolean subalgebra generated by \(F\). The map \(\sigma|_C:C\to\sigma(C)\) is an isomorphism of finite Heyting subalgebras of \(L\), hence extends by ultrahomogeneity to \(g\in\operatorname{Aut}(L)\). Therefore \(\rho(g)\) agrees with \(\sigma\) on \(F\), proving density.

For an ordered central tuple \(\bar b=(b_1,\dots,b_n)\), define its Boolean cells by
\[
c_\varepsilon=\bigwedge_{i=1}^n b_i^{\varepsilon_i},\qquad b_i^1=b_i,\quad b_i^0=\neg b_i,
\]
for \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\). The nonzero cells form a nonempty support \(S(\bar b)\). Equal supports give a cell-by-cell isomorphism between the finite Boolean subalgebras generated by the tuples, and ultrahomogeneity extends that isomorphism to \(L\). Conversely, atomlessness lets one partition \(1\) into any prescribed finite number of nonzero central pieces, so every nonempty support \(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\) is realizable. Hence the number of orbits is the number of nonempty subsets of a \(2^n\)-element set: \(2^{2^n}-1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Yamamoto constructs two Boolean companions of \(L\). One is an ambient countable atomless Boolean algebra with an interior operator whose open part is \(L\), and automorphisms of \(L\) extend uniquely to it. The other is a Boolean algebra on the double-negation fixed points. Yamamoto explicitly states that the latter is not a substructure of \(L\) and proves that its induced automorphism image is non-dense.

The center considered here is different: it is the canonical complemented-element Heyting subalgebra of \(L\). The new conclusion is that this intrinsic center is atomless and that the restriction image is dense. General facts about Heyting centers and the countable atomless Boolean algebra are classical and are not claimed as new on their own. Candidate-specific semantic-index searches, exact web searches, and full-text inspection of Yamamoto's paper did not locate the combined center/density theorem or its center-tuple orbit consequence.

## Limitations
The dense restriction image may be proper; the result does not determine the kernel or surjectivity of \(\rho\). The orbit formula concerns only tuples entirely contained in the center and does not contradict the global non-Roelcke-precompactness of \(\operatorname{Aut}(L)\). The deductions are short enough that an equivalent formulation may exist as unindexed folklore. No independent audit has been performed.
