# Review of The Bouderbala--Karras divisor discrepancy has infinite global amplitude

## Correctness
PASS. The proof rewrites the discrepancy exactly as an additive sum over exponent coordinates. The \(k=1\) case follows term-by-term, including its equality condition. For \(k\ge2\), a prime divisor of \(k\) supplies a repeated-exponent family with strictly positive per-coordinate discrepancy, while exponents congruent to \(1\) modulo \(k\) supply a prime-power family tending to negative infinity. No finite computation is used to establish an infinite quantifier.

## Originality
PASS. The full focal paper introduces the same \(f_k\), proves only envelope bounds between \(\omega\) and \(\Omega\), and explicitly leaves the amplitude of the difference from \(\log_2\tau\) for later study. Exact and alias searches for the discrepancy, signed ranges, gcd-exponent formulation, and broader divisor-function comparisons produced no checked source implying the theorem. The closest retrieved records concern different gcd maps or unrelated discrepancy problems. A differently indexed prior result remains a residual search risk.

## Value
PASS. The theorem answers the most basic global form of a question explicitly highlighted by the introducing paper and reveals a clean structural split: \(k=1\) is one-sided with a complete equality class, whereas every \(k\ge2\) is unbounded in both signs. The witnesses are natural exponent patterns tied directly to divisors and residue classes of \(k\).

## Closest literature and limitations
Bouderbala and Karras, Notes on Number Theory and Discrete Mathematics 28(3) (2022), 575–580, DOI 10.7546/nntdm.2022.28.3.575-580, is the closest source. The result interprets “amplitude” as a global supremum over all \(n\) for each fixed \(k\). It does not determine finite-\(x\) extremal growth, distribution, or all zeros for \(k\ge2\).
