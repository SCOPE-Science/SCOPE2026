# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The quantifiers are proper and upward monotone. The dominance proof separates into two exact applications of the source's smallness/completeness lemma: \(P_0\) is \(\omega\)-small while the cofinite \(Q\) is \(\omega\)-complete, and \(Q^d\) is \(\omega_1\)-small while \(P_1^d\) is \(\omega_1\)-complete. Duality and union preservation then give \(P\triangleright Q\). The lower witness relation is in \(P\cdot Q\) but admits no \(P\)-by-\(Q\) rectangle because any such rectangle would contain a forbidden diagonal point. The upper witness relation is in \(Q^d\cdot P^d\) but admits no \(Q^d\)-by-\(P^d\) rectangle because infinitely many selected rows force omission of infinitely many \(X\)-points, contradicting \(|X\setminus B|\le1\). Engström's countable theorem makes \(\aleph_1\) minimal.

## Originality

PASS. The closest result is Engström's Theorem 7.2, which proves failure of the branching dichotomy on a domain of cardinality \(\aleph_2\). His countable theorem supplies the opposite boundary but does not close the remaining \(\aleph_1\) case. The 2005 Altman–Peterzil–Winter theorem characterizes the countable case rather than producing an uncountable branching counterexample. Searches for the source title together with “\(\aleph_1\),” “branching product,” “scope dominance,” “least uncountable,” and “minimum domain cardinality” did not locate an equivalent or stronger \(\aleph_1\) theorem. The new witness is not a special case of the source's \(\aleph_2\) construction: it changes the small component to a two-point witness condition and the inner quantifier to the cofinite filter.

## Value

PASS. The source isolates a central yes/no branching question, proves it positively on countable domains, and refutes it by an \(\aleph_2\) example. Determining the exact first cardinal where the dichotomy fails is therefore a natural structural boundary. The construction closes the only cardinal gap between those two stated results and shows that no continuum-size or almost-disjoint-family mechanism is needed at the first failure.

## Closest literature and limitations

Engström (2026) is the direct source for the branching formulation, the structural lemmas, the countable positive theorem, and the \(\aleph_2\) counterexample. Altman, Peterzil, and Winter (2005) give the earlier countable-domain characterization. The present result does not address counterexamples where both quantifiers are filters, does not impose permutation invariance, and does not classify all dominance pairs on \(\aleph_1\).
