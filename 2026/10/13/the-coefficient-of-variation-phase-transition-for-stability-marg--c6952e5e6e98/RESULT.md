# The coefficient-of-variation phase transition for stability-margin-optimal stochastic heavy ball

## Finding
Consider scalar stochastic heavy ball applied to randomly sampled, centered positive-curvature quadratics. Let the independently sampled curvature \(A_k>0\) have mean \(\mu=\mathbb E A_k>0\), variance \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(A_k)<\infty\), and coefficient of variation \(v=\sigma/\mu\). For each momentum \(0\le\beta<1\), the exact mean-square convergence interval from **every** deterministic pair of initial iterates is
\[
0<\eta\mu<\tau_v(\beta),\qquad
\tau_v(\beta)=\frac{2(1-\beta^2)}{(1-\beta)+(1+\beta)v^2}.
\]
Convergence means \(\mathbb E(x_k^2+x_{k-1}^2)\to0\). Both interval endpoints are excluded. Its **stability-margin-optimal momentum**, maximizing the admissible upper stepsize endpoint rather than minimizing the within-interval convergence rate, has a sharp noise transition:
\[
\boxed{\begin{array}{ll}
0<v<1:&\displaystyle \beta_*=\frac{1-v}{1+v},\quad \max_{0\le\beta<1}\tau_v(\beta)=\frac4{(1+v)^2};\\[4pt]
v\ge1:&\displaystyle \beta_*=0,\quad \max_{0\le\beta<1}\tau_v(\beta)=\frac2{1+v^2}.
\end{array}}
\]
Every displayed maximizer is unique. For \(v=0\), \(\tau_0(\beta)=2(1+\beta)\) increases strictly: its supremum \(4\) is not attained because \(\beta<1\). Thus strictly positive momentum enlarges the maximum admissible constant stepsize **exactly** when \(0\le v<1\) (with no attained best momentum at \(v=0\)); when \(v\ge1\), momentum strictly reduces the stable stepsize ceiling.

In the natural uniform two-component case with distinct curvatures \(a,b>0\), \(v=|a-b|/(a+b)\) and \(\beta_*=\min(a,b)/\max(a,b)\). For \(\{a,b\}=\{1,2\}\), this gives \(\beta_*=1/2\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}=3/2\), compared with the no-momentum ceiling \(6/5\). Conversely, when \(A_k=1\) with probability \(3/4\) and \(A_k=9\) with probability \(1/4\), \(v^2=4/3>1\), so the unique stability-margin optimum is \(\beta=0\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix a scalar optimum at zero. At each integer \(k\ge0\), sample \(A_k>0\) independently and identically with finite positive first moment and finite second moment. The update is
\[
x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta A_kx_k+\beta(x_k-x_{k-1}),\qquad
\eta>0,\quad 0\le\beta<1,
\]
from arbitrary deterministic \((x_0,x_{-1})\in\mathbb R^2\). All random variables are independent of the initial condition. The scalar gradient estimate \(A_kx_k\) is unbiased for the gradient \(\mu x_k\) of \(f(x)=\mu x^2/2\), with multiplicative, rather than additive, gradient noise. The distribution may be continuous or discrete, provided its second moment exists. Nothing is assumed about higher moments or symmetry. The finite-sum two-component example samples indices independently with replacement.

## Proof
Put \(m=1+\beta-\eta\mu\), \(s^2=\eta^2\sigma^2\) (this symbol is a noise variance, not the normalized stepsize), and \(z_k=(x_k,x_{k-1})^\mathsf T\). Conditional independence gives the exact symmetric-moment update
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb Ex_{k+1}^2\\ \mathbb Ex_{k+1}x_k\\\mathbb Ex_k^2\end{pmatrix}
=T\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb Ex_k^2\\\mathbb Ex_kx_{k-1}\\\mathbb Ex_{k-1}^2\end{pmatrix},\quad
T=\begin{pmatrix}m^2+s^2&-2\beta m&\beta^2\\m&-\beta&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct expansion yields
\[
\det(I-T)=(1+\beta)\eta\left[2\mu(1-\beta)-\eta\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\mu^2+\sigma^2\right)\right].\tag{1}
\]
First assume \(0<\beta<1\). Introduce the quadratic form
\[
V(x,y)=x^2-\frac{2\beta m}{1+\beta}xy+\beta^2y^2.
\]
Its determinant and leading principal minor are
\[
\det P=\frac{\beta^2\eta\mu[2(1+\beta)-\eta\mu]}{(1+\beta)^2},\qquad P_{11}=1.
\]
Therefore \(V\) is positive definite whenever \(0<\eta\mu<2(1+\beta)\). Since \(\sigma^2\ge0\), the proposed strict stability bound implies that inequality. Expanding the conditional square directly and using \(\mathbb E(A_k-\mu)=0\) gives the **exact**, rather than estimated, conditional drift
\[
\mathbb E[V(x_{k+1},x_k)\mid x_k,x_{k-1}]-V(x_k,x_{k-1})
=-\eta\left[2\mu(1-\beta)-\eta\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\mu^2+\sigma^2\right)\right]x_k^2.\tag{2}
\]
The coefficient is strictly positive throughout the claimed stability interval. Telescoping expectations of (2), using the lower bound \(V\ge0\), proves \(\sum_{k\ge0}\mathbb Ex_k^2<\infty\). Hence \(\mathbb Ex_k^2\to0\) and also \(\mathbb Ex_{k-1}^2\to0\). If \(\beta=0\), the update instead gives directly
\[
\mathbb Ex_{k+1}^2=[1-2\eta\mu+\eta^2(\mu^2+\sigma^2)]\mathbb Ex_k^2,
\]
whose factor lies in \([0,1)\) exactly on the claimed interval.

For necessity, at \(\eta\) equal to the displayed cutoff (1) vanishes and \(T\) has eigenvalue \(1\); above the cutoff (1) is negative. Since \(\det(\lambda I-T)\to+\infty\) as real \(\lambda\to+\infty\), the latter implies a real eigenvalue \(\lambda>1\). Thus in either case \(T^k\not\to0\). The three deterministic initial moment vectors generated by \((x_0,x_{-1})=(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) span the real vector space of symmetric two-by-two moment arrays. If all deterministic starts converged in mean square, positivity and Cauchy--Schwarz would imply each of those three moment vectors tends to zero, forcing \(T^k\to0\), a contradiction. This proves the strict necessary-and-sufficient cutoff including its boundary.

Now normalize by \(v=\sigma/\mu\) and write
\[
\tau_v(\beta)=\frac{2(1-\beta^2)}{1+v^2+(v^2-1)\beta}.
\]
For \(v>0\), its derivative factors as
\[
\frac{d\tau_v}{d\beta}=
-\frac{2[\beta(v-1)+v+1][\beta(v+1)+v-1]}{[1+v^2+(v^2-1)\beta]^2}.
\]
On \(0\le\beta<1\), the first bracket is strictly positive. If \(0<v<1\), the second changes sign exactly once, from negative to positive, at \(\beta_*=(1-v)/(1+v)\). If \(v\ge1\), the derivative is negative for every \(\beta>0\) (and at \(\beta=0\) it vanishes only when \(v=1\)). Substitution gives both optimal ceilings. At \(v=0\), elementary simplification yields \(2(1+\beta)\), proving the unattained supremum. This completes all endpoints and uniqueness cases.

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` (Python 3 with SymPy) independently expands (1), the Gram determinant, and (2), factors the derivative, checks the exact optimized expressions, and compares the analytical drift against directly enumerated conditional expectation under four strictly positive finite-support curvature laws, seven stepsizes, six momentum values, and 25 starting pairs. Its run returns `VERIFY_OK` with 3,528 exact-rational checks. Those finite checks are supplementary: the proof above establishes the infinite-time and arbitrary-distribution statements without enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Yang, Lin and Li (arXiv:1604.03257v1, publicly submitted 2016-04-12) introduce unified stochastic momentum convergence analysis; their Theorems 1--4 use general smoothness/bounded-noise assumptions and horizon-dependent stepsizes, not an exact all-initialization constant-stepsize phase diagram. Gadat, Panloup and Saadane (arXiv:1609.04228v1, publicly submitted 2016-09-14) study stochastic heavy ball with decreasing gains and exponential/polynomial-memory scaling; their theorem and quadratic analysis do not imply the present scalar constant-parameter boundary.

**Important exact precedence.** Kidambi, Netrapalli, Jain and Kakade (arXiv:1803.05591, Appendix A, Lemma 4 and Remark 8) derive the *same algebraic necessary stepsize bound* \(2(1-\beta^2)/[c+(c-2)\beta]\) with \(c=\mathbb EA^2/(\mathbb EA)^2\) for their stochastic regression construction, specifically under \(c\ge2\). That inequality and the high-variance instability obstruction are established prior work, **not new discoveries here**. Their particular construction excludes the low-noise regime \(1<c<2\) and they do not give the exact every-initialization sufficiency or the unique stability-margin-optimal momentum there. The narrowly assessed contribution is the complete low- and high-noise optimized phase transition for arbitrary positive scalar curvature laws, the exact sufficiency proof, and the explicit low-noise two-component rule.

Paquette and Paquette (arXiv:2106.03696, Sections 2 and Appendix E.4) analyze high-dimensional random least squares through a limiting stochastic diffusion/Volterra equation; their large-sample equivalence of stochastic heavy ball to suitably scaled SGD is different from the finite scalar, distribution-uniform constant-step stability-margin optimization here. Relevant indexed findings cover one-step random-curvature SGD/OSGM and deterministic heavy-ball spectral barriers, not this exact optimization of stochastic momentum against curvature variation.

## Limitations
The maximizing parameter concerns the **width of the exact mean-square stable stepsize interval**, not the fastest contraction factor at an interior stepsize, transient peak, almost-sure convergence rate, or general multi-dimensional acceleration. The result requires iid scalar multiplicative curvature noise and a common quadratic minimizer with no persistent additive gradient noise. Negative momentum, curvature-dependent sampling, adaptive parameters, noncommuting Hessians and nonlinear objectives are not covered. The fixed-parameter cutoff's necessary inequality in the high-variance case is old work; only the explicitly identified low-noise optimization and necessary-and-sufficient extension are assessed as new. An earlier unlocated equivalent analysis of scalar random-coefficient second-order recurrences remains a genuine originality risk.
