# The complete Cartier lattice and class group of odd dihedral product quotients

## Finding

For each odd integer \(n\ge5\), put \(S=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf C}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf C}\). Fix a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta_n\), and let the dihedral group \(D_n\) of order \(2n\) act on the affine product coordinates by
\[
r(x,z)=(\zeta_n x,\zeta_n^2 z),\qquad s(x,z)=(x^{-1},z^{-1}).
\]
Write \(Y_n=S/D_n\). Then, using the two natural equivariant degree-one line bundles and the sign character as coordinates,
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)=\mathbf Z^2\oplus\mathbf Z/2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)=(2n\mathbf Z)^2\oplus0.
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)/\operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)
\cong (\mathbf Z/2n)^2\oplus\mathbf Z/2.
\]
The unique nonzero torsion divisor class is non-Cartier at each of the four \(A_1\) points, but Cartier at the two cyclic quotient points. The quotient ruling morphisms \(\phi_1,\phi_2:Y_n\to\mathbf P^1\), characterized upstairs by \(x^n+x^{-n}\) and \(z^n+z^{-n}\), pull back \(\mathcal O(1)\) to the two primitive generators of the Cartier lattice. Thus the polarization \(A=(n-4)\phi_1^*\mathcal O(1)+\phi_2^*\mathcal O(1)\) is primitive in \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes and representations are over \(\mathbf C\); \(n\) is odd and at least five. The class group is the *integral Weil divisor class group*, not just its numerical quotient. The displayed subgroup \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)\subset\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)\) uses explicit linearizations and so carries more information than abstract isomorphism types. The theorem refers specifically to this dihedral action, not arbitrary actions of a dihedral group on a ruled surface.

## Proof

**1. Fixed loci and equivariant descent.** Every nonidentity rotation \(r^j\) has nontrivial multipliers in both factors because \(n\) is odd; its fixed locus consists of the four coordinate corners. Each reflection \(r^js\) acts on the factors as \(x\mapsto\zeta_n^j/x\) and \(z\mapsto\zeta_n^{2j}/z\), so it has exactly four fixed points. Thus all nontrivial stabilizer loci have codimension two. Let \(U\subset S\) be the complement of their union, and let \(V=U/D_n\). The quotient \(U\to V\) is an étale \(D_n\)-torsor. Because the complement of \(U\) has codimension two in smooth \(S\), restriction identifies \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) with \(\operatorname{Pic}(U)\), and equivariant restriction is also an isomorphism: extensions of invariant line bundles and their isomorphisms across codimension two are unique. The normal surface \(Y_n\) has \(V\) as its smooth locus: every nontrivial point stabilizer acts nontrivially on both tangent directions and has no pseudo-reflection. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)=\operatorname{Pic}(V)
=\operatorname{Pic}^{D_n}(U)=\operatorname{Pic}^{D_n}(S).
\]
The first equality is normality plus removal of codimension-two points, and the second is descent for a finite étale torsor.

**2. Split computation of the equivariant Picard group.** Since \(D_n\) preserves the two factors, it acts trivially on \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbf Z^2\). Let \(h=(n+1)/2\), so \(2h\equiv1\pmod n\). On homogeneous coordinates of the first factor, the matrices
\[
R_1=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_n^h,\zeta_n^{-h}),
\qquad T=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
define an actual linear representation of \(D_n\), projectivizing to \(x\mapsto\zeta_n x\) and \(x\mapsto1/x\). The analogous second-factor representation takes \(R_2=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_n,\zeta_n^{-1})\) and the same \(T\). Hence \(\mathcal O_S(1,0)\) and \(\mathcal O_S(0,1)\) admit genuine \(D_n\)-linearizations. They split the degree map of equivariant Picard groups. A linearization of the trivial line bundle is a character, since \(H^0(S,\mathcal O_S^*)=\mathbf C^*\). For odd \(n\), the character group of \(D_n\) is \(C_2\), generated by the character trivial on rotations and negative on reflections. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Pic}^{D_n}(S)\cong\mathbf Z^2\oplus C_2.
\]
Represent its elements by \((a,b,\epsilon)\), where \(a,b\in\mathbf Z\) and \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\).

**3. Cartier descent at all stabilizers.** A linearized bundle on \(S\) descends to an invertible bundle on the coarse quotient if and only if each geometric stabilizer acts trivially on its fiber (the tame finite-group descent criterion). At the four corners, the cyclic rotation stabilizer has on the two degree-one factors eigencharacters with exponents independently equal to \(\pm h\) and \(\pm1\), up to simultaneously reversing all signs by dualizing the fibers. Thus triviality at all corners says
\[
ha+b\equiv ha-b\equiv-ha+b\equiv-ha-b\equiv0\pmod n.
\]
Subtracting and using odd \(n\) forces \(a\equiv b\equiv0\pmod n\), and conversely these congruences suffice at the corners.

For any fixed reflection \(r^js\), its action on the two homogeneous two-dimensional representations is an involution with two distinct eigenlines, of eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\). The four fixed points on their product realize the four independent pairs of eigenline signs. Consequently the action on the fiber of a class \((a,b,\epsilon)\) is of the form \((\pm1)^a(\pm1)^b(-1)^\epsilon\), with independently variable signs. Triviality at all such points is equivalent to \(a,b\) both even and \(\epsilon=0\). Combining the corner and reflection restrictions, and using that \(n\) is odd, gives precisely
\[
(a,b,\epsilon)\text{ Cartier}\iff
2n\mid a,\quad2n\mid b,\quad\epsilon=0.
\]
There are no other types of stabilizers, so this condition is necessary and sufficient globally.

**4. Interpretation.** By construction, \(\phi_1\) and \(\phi_2\) pull back the base hyperplane divisor to degrees \((2n,0)\) and \((0,2n)\) on \(S\). Their descended bundles have the canonical stabilizer-trivial equivariant structures, hence correspond to the two positive basis vectors of the Cartier lattice. The sign character is trivial at both rotation (cyclic) singularities but nontrivial at each reflection (\(A_1\)) singularity. Finally the coefficient vector of \(A\) in this Picard basis is \((n-4,1)\), which is primitive.

## Verification

A standalone pure-Python program `verify.py` enumerates the four corner characters and all four reflection-eigenline sign pairs for representative odd orders, verifying that the resulting conditions are exactly \(2n\mid a,b\) and \(\epsilon=0\) modulo \(2n\). This is a finite arithmetic sanity check, not a replacement for the uniform proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Malara–Merta–Szpond–Zieliński (2026, §6) construct the quotient, describe its six singularities and the two quotient ruling morphisms, and study irrational Seshadri constants. The statement here strengthens the available divisor information to the *full integral Weil class group*, its torsion, the *entire Cartier sublattice*, and a primitive polarization criterion. General equivariant-divisor methods, such as Hashimoto's work, supply the conceptual framework but do not themselves compute these particular group invariants.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to odd \(n\ge5\) and characteristic zero. It does not compute Cox rings, nef cones of resolutions, or the analogous even-order quotient. The argument is self-contained on this action; an equivalent computation elsewhere under a different quotient presentation remains a possible bibliographic risk.
