# The complete saturation spectrum of two-use seven-cycle zero-error codes

## Finding

In the seven-symbol zero-error channel with confusability graph the cycle \(C_7\), there are exactly **83,181 inclusion-maximal two-use codebooks** on the labeled alphabet \((\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^2\). Their size-generating polynomial is

\[
M(z)=28z^7+9016z^8+73157z^9+980z^{10}.
\]

Here *inclusion-maximal* means that no further input pair can be adjoined while retaining zero-error distinguishability. This differs from maximum possible cardinality, which is ten and was already known. The **82,201 inclusion-maximal nonmaximum** codes have sizes seven, eight, or nine.

Under the natural group \((D_{14}\times D_{14})\rtimes S_2\) generated by cyclic translations and reflections of each coordinate and interchange of the coordinates (order 392), the codebooks form **229 orbits**. By code size, the orbit counts are respectively 1, 27, 198, and 3. The exact orbit-size multiplicities are:

| Code size | Orbit size | Number of orbits |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 28 | 1 |
| 8 | 196 | 8 |
| 8 | 392 | 19 |
| 9 | 49 | 1 |
| 9 | 98 | 2 |
| 9 | 196 | 18 |
| 9 | 392 | 177 |
| 10 | 196 | 1 |
| 10 | 392 | 2 |

The known 980 maximum-size codes and their three symmetry orbits appear only as a consistency baseline; the new finite classification concerns all terminal codebooks, including the 226 nonmaximum orbits.

## Assumptions and scope

Identify the seven channel inputs with \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\). Two distinct single-use symbols are confusable exactly when their residues differ by \(1\) or \(-1\) modulo seven. Two length-two inputs \((x,y)\) and \((u,v)\) are confusable if and only if both coordinate differences belong to \(\{0,1,-1\}\) modulo seven. A codebook is an arbitrary subset of the 49 indexed length-two inputs. No linearity, constant composition, or symmetry constraint is imposed. Distinct labeled subsets count separately.

For a codebook \(C\), *zero error* means no distinct two elements of \(C\) are confusable. *Inclusion maximal* means every input pair outside \(C\) is confusable with at least one member of \(C\). In particular, a cardinality-seven maximal codebook is **not** an optimum-size codebook. The symmetry classes use only the specified order-392 natural channel-isometry group; no claim is made about unrelated isomorphisms.

## Proof

Let \(V=\{0,\ldots,48\}\), with vertex number \(7x+y\) representing the ordered pair \((x,y)\). Define the **compatibility graph** \(H\) on \(V\) by joining distinct vertices precisely when at least one of their two circular coordinate differences is one of \(2,3,4,5\) modulo seven. Thus \(H\) is the complement of the usual confusability graph \(C_7\boxtimes C_7\). Zero-error codebooks correspond exactly to cliques of \(H\); addition of a codeword is possible exactly when the clique can be extended. Consequently the required codebooks are exactly the maximal cliques of \(H\), with no excluded cases.

Both embedded independent enumerators construct all 49 adjacency masks directly from this condition. They execute the following exhaustive pivoted maximal-clique recurrence, starting with \(R=\varnothing\), \(P=V\), and \(X=\varnothing\). The invariant is that \(R\) is a clique, the potential extensions lie in \(P\), and processed extensions lie in \(X\). If \(P=X=\varnothing\), output \(R\). Otherwise choose a pivot \(u\in P\cup X\); for every \(v\in P\setminus N_H(u)\), recursively visit

\[
(R\cup\{v\},\ P\cap N_H(v),\ X\cap N_H(v)),
\]

then move \(v\) from \(P\) to \(X\). This is exhaustive because any maximal clique extending \(R\) either contains the pivot or contains a vertex outside its neighborhood; the \(X\) discipline prevents duplicates. It outputs only maximal cliques, because a terminal \(R\) has no admissible extending vertex in either set. Each output is also checked directly: every two selected vertices are compatible and every unselected vertex fails compatibility with some selected vertex.

The two separately written enumerators agree on all 83,181 **sorted 49-bit masks**, not merely on their histogram. Their counts by popcount are 28, 9,016, 73,157, 980, with no masks outside sizes seven through ten. The SHA-256 of the canonical uncompressed list is `362d213751756f339a3319847ce2bc6b580fd2ddc34d124b265e0a1dd417de13`.

For the orbit statement, apply five permutations of \((x,y)\): add one modulo seven to either coordinate, negate either coordinate, and swap the two coordinates. They generate exactly the stated group. They preserve confusability and cardinality. Both programs partition the *complete verified catalogue* by breadth-first orbits under these generators. All images are found in the catalogue, and the disjoint orbit sizes sum to the cardinality counts above. The orbit-size table is therefore exhaustive. The unique size-seven orbit contains the explicit codebook

\[
\{(0,5),(1,3),(2,1),(3,6),(4,4),(5,2),(6,0)\}.
\]

## Verification

The accompanying files `verify_a.py` (Python 3 standard library), `verify_b.cpp` (C++17 standard library), and `catalogue.txt.gz` reproduce the result. From one directory, execute:

```sh
python3 verify_a.py
g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_b.cpp -o verify_b
./verify_b catalogue_cpp.txt
cmp catalogue.txt catalogue_cpp.txt
gzip -dc catalogue.txt.gz | cmp - catalogue.txt
sha256sum catalogue.txt
```

The files encode each codebook as an unsigned 49-bit mask. Bit \(7x+y\) denotes pair \((x,y)\). The canonical catalogue is sorted numerically and serialized as exactly 13 lowercase hexadecimal characters followed by a line feed per mask, with no headers. The compressed catalogue contains **83,181** such lines. The SHA-256 above is for the **decompressed** list. Both programs perform direct independent-set/maximality checks, compute all maximal sets, and compute the symmetry-orbit histogram.

## Relationship to prior work

The classical odd-cycle strong-product independence-number theorem already gives the maximum size ten, and later exact enumeration gives 980 maximum-size two-use codebooks. Those results do not enumerate inclusion-maximal codebooks that are *smaller* than maximum. Klobučar (2005) considers independent sets and independent domination in strong products of paths and cycles; Yang and Zhang (2018, Theorem 7) prove that the independent domination number of \(C_7\boxtimes C_n\) is \(n\) for \(n\ge7\), which already implies the **minimum terminal size seven** at the present parameters. Neither result as inspected yields the numbers 28, 9016, or 73157 or the orbit classification. Hildebrant (2026) computes the *independence polynomial* of this exact graph, including the coefficient 980 at size ten. The ordinary independence polynomial counts every independent set, whereas the present saturation polynomial counts only those with no permissible extension; one does not determine the other without additional structural data.

Thus the newly classified object is the distribution and symmetry of *all inclusion-maximal zero-error codebooks*, not the previously known minimum or maximum size, nor the previously known maximum-size multiplicity.

## Limitations

This is a finite exhaustive result for exactly seven input symbols and two channel uses. It does not determine three-use saturation spectra, any asymptotic Shannon capacity, or optimal greedy construction strategies. It counts codebooks, not transmission probabilities. The 2005 original PDF could not be retrieved for complete comparison, although later full texts containing the most relevant independent-domination theorem were examined; an older unindexed enumeration of the entire terminal spectrum cannot be categorically excluded. The proof depends on the exhaustive enumerators and their exact adjacency rule, which are provided for independent reproduction. No external independent audit has been performed.
