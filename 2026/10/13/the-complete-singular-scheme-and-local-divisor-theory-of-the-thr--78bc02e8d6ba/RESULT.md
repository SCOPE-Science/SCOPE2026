# The complete singular scheme and local divisor theory of the three-by-three Birkhoff toric variety

## Finding
Let \(B_3\) be the polytope of \(3\times3\) doubly stochastic matrices and let \(X_{B_3}\) be its projective complex toric variety in the embedding by all lattice points of \(B_3\). Partition the six homogeneous coordinates, indexed by permutation matrices, into triples \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) (even permutations) and \(y_1,y_2,y_3\) (odd permutations). Then
\[
X_{B_3}=V(x_1x_2x_3-y_1y_2y_3)\subset\mathbb P^5.
\]
Its *scheme-theoretic Jacobian singular locus* is **reduced**, with homogeneous ideal
\[
J=(x_1x_2,x_1x_3,x_2x_3,y_1y_2,y_1y_3,y_2y_3).
\]
It is the projective Stanley--Reisner curve of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,3}\): precisely the nine lines
\[
L_{ij}=\mathbb P(\operatorname{span}(e_{x_i},e_{y_j}))\qquad(1\le i,j\le3).
\]
Three lines meet at each of the six coordinate points; no other two lines meet. This curve is Cohen--Macaulay, of degree \(9\), Hilbert polynomial \(9d-3\), and arithmetic genus \(4\). The toric fourfold \(X_{B_3}\) is normal. At a closed point of a singular line away from the six coordinate points, the germ is a smooth one-dimensional factor times a three-dimensional ordinary double point \(uv-wz=0\), and its local Weil divisor-class group is \(\mathbb Z\). At any one of the six coordinate points the affine chart is
\[
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[a,b,c,d,e]/(ab-cde),
\]
and its local divisor-class group is \(\mathbb Z^2\). Thus the class-group rank jumps from one to two at the six triple intersections.

## Assumptions and scope
The field is \(\mathbb C\) (the scheme-theoretic Jacobian assertion also holds over fields of characteristic other than \(3\), and indeed the displayed ideal can be checked in every characteristic). A Birkhoff lattice point means an integer nonnegative matrix with all row and column sums one; it is a permutation matrix. The natural lattice is the affine row/column-sum lattice, of dimension four. The **scheme-theoretic singular locus** is defined by the homogeneous hypersurface equation and its six partial derivatives, followed by projective saturation. Degree and arithmetic genus refer to the induced projective embedding in \(\mathbb P^5\). Local class groups are divisor-class groups of the local normal rings at the stated closed points; they are not merely completions or numerical ranks.

## Proof
**The cubic equation.** The six permutation matrices have affine dimension four. The sum of the three even permutation matrices equals the sum of the three odd ones, while the six augmented exponent columns have rank five. Thus the integral relation lattice has rank one, generated by this primitive relation (all coefficients have absolute value one). The toric ideal is the principal prime binomial ideal \((x_1x_2x_3-y_1y_2y_3)\), agreeing with the explicit presentation in Haase--Paffenholz [1]. The fourfold is an irreducible cubic hypersurface.

**The singular scheme and incidences.** The partial derivatives are the three products of two distinct \(x\)-coordinates and the negatives of the three products of two distinct \(y\)-coordinates. Their ideal \(J\) already contains the defining cubic, because each triple product is divisible by one of these quadrics. Hence the singular scheme is \(\operatorname{Proj}\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,y_1,y_2,y_3]/J\). The ideal is squarefree and saturated: its minimal prime decomposition is
\[
J=\bigcap_{i=1}^3\bigcap_{j=1}^3
(x_k:k\ne i,\;y_\ell:\ell\ne j).
\]
Each prime defines the indicated coordinate line, so the scheme is reduced without embedded components. Distinct lines meet exactly when they share their \(x\)-index or their \(y\)-index, and then meet only at that coordinate point. Thus the incidence graph of components and six triple points is the edge--vertex incidence graph of \(K_{3,3}\), with nine lines and six triple points.

**Cohen--Macaulayness and degree.** The ring factors as \(A_x\otimes_\mathbb C A_y\), where \(A_x=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]/(x_ix_j:i<j)\) and \(A_y\) is the analogous ring. Each one-dimensional factor is Cohen--Macaulay: it is the reduced union of three coordinate axes, and the sum of its three coordinate functions is a nonzerodivisor. Their tensor product is therefore Cohen--Macaulay of Krull dimension two. The standard graded Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{(1+2t)^2}{(1-t)^2}.
\]
For every \(d\ge1\), its degree-\(d\) piece has dimension \(6+9(d-1)=9d-3\). Therefore the projective curve has degree nine and arithmetic genus \(1-(9\cdot0-3)=4\).

**Local analytic types and normality.** On a line \(L_{ij}\) away from its endpoints, \(x_i\) and \(y_j\) are units in a suitable affine chart. The cubic becomes a unit multiple of \(uv-wz\) in the four remaining transverse coordinates; the ratio of the two nonzero line coordinates supplies one smooth local parameter. Thus the transverse singularity is an ordinary threefold node. At, say, the point where \(x_1\ne0\) and all other coordinates vanish, setting \(x_1=1\) gives the chart \(ab-cde=0\), with \(a=x_2,b=x_3,c=y_1,d=y_2,e=y_3\). Symmetry covers all six points. Since the singular scheme has dimension one in the dimension-four hypersurface, the hypersurface is regular in codimension one. Hypersurfaces are Cohen--Macaulay; Serre's \(R_1+S_2\) criterion establishes normality.

**Local divisor-class groups.** The node transverse to a line has four invariant prime divisors and a rank-three saturated character lattice, giving divisor class group \(\mathbb Z\). This remains the local class group after adjoining the smooth line parameter and localizing at a closed point. At a coordinate point, consider \(R=\mathbb C[a,b,c,d,e]/(ab-cde)\). Its six torus-invariant height-one prime divisors are
\[
D_{a,c},D_{a,d},D_{a,e},D_{b,c},D_{b,d},D_{b,e},
\]
where, for example, \(D_{a,c}\) has ideal \((a,c)\). The principal character-divisor relations are
\[
\operatorname{div}(a)=\sum_{z\in\{c,d,e\}}D_{a,z},\qquad
\operatorname{div}(b)=\sum_{z\in\{c,d,e\}}D_{b,z},\qquad
\operatorname{div}(z)=D_{a,z}+D_{b,z}\ (z=c,d,e).
\]
The five listed relations have rank four because their sole dependence is \(\operatorname{div}(a)+\operatorname{div}(b)=\sum_z\operatorname{div}(z)\). Eliminating \(D_{b,z}=-D_{a,z}\) leaves three free generators \(D_{a,c},D_{a,d},D_{a,e}\) with their sum zero. Hence the quotient is \(\mathbb Z^2\), with no torsion. For the affine toric fixed point the toric divisor sequence computes the class group of the local ring as well, since all invariant prime divisors pass through that point.

## Verification
The included `check_birkhoff.py` uses only the Python standard library. It verifies the exponent-matrix rank and primitive relation; enumerates all \(64\) binary coordinate-support patterns, identifying exactly six singular one-coordinate strata and nine singular two-coordinate strata; checks the Hilbert function through degree \(12\); computes the rank and saturation of the six-divisor relation matrix by exact rational row reduction and maximal minors; and checks nondegeneracy of the transverse quadratic form. Run `python3 check_birkhoff.py`. The recorded exact output is supplied in `check_output.txt`. These finite calculations are independently reproducible checks of key algebraic steps; the proof above establishes the infinite statements about the homogeneous ideal, local rings, and all closed points.

## Relationship to prior work
Haase--Paffenholz [1, Section 1.3] gives the cubic toric ideal and the two regular unimodular triangulations of \(B_3\). Paffenholz [2] studies the face lattice of Birkhoff polytopes, including this four-dimensional polytope. The standard toric divisor sequence used for the class-group computations is explained in [3]. The present statement concerns the **full Jacobian singular scheme** of the associated toric fourfold, its nine-line intersection geometry, its Hilbert polynomial and arithmetic genus, and its pointwise local class groups; none of these is asserted as a new description of the Birkhoff polytope, the cubic equation, or the general divisor sequence. A known Stanley--Reisner \(K_{3,3}\) curve can occur in unrelated geometric constructions; abstract coincidence of that graph is not a claim of uniqueness.

## Limitations
No classification of projective or crepant resolutions is asserted, and no claim is made about all higher-dimensional Birkhoff varieties. The local divisor-class groups are for the complex toric cubic at its singular closed points. The argument does not establish that no unpublished or differently named literature contains the same combined specialization. No independent audit has been performed.
