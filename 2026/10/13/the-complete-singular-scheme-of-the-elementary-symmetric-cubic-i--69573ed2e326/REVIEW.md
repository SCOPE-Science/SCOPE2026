# Self-review: elementary symmetric cubic singular schemes

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns the third elementary symmetric cubic in \(n\ge5\) projective variables over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two, with \(n\not\equiv2\pmod4\). The identity \(\partial_i e_3=x_i^2+s_1x_i+s_2\) reduces every geometric critical point to at most two coordinate values. The case of one nonzero value produces exactly the indicator lines of weight \(1\pmod4\), and the two-distinct-nonzero-value case is excluded by an explicit parity and binomial-coefficient comparison. The Hessian polar form of the local quadratic term \(e_2(y)+e_2(z)\) is nonsingular when \(n\) is odd; when \(4\mid n\), its one-dimensional radical is anisotropic for the quadratic form, so the tangent quadric is still smooth. For \(4\mid n\), the relation \(\sum_i\partial_i e_3=0\), zero-dimensional projective support, and regular-sequence criterion give a complete intersection of degree \(2^{n-1}\). The support consists of \(2^{n-2}\) points, each with nonzero Jacobian tangent direction and hence Artin length at least two; the degree bound forces each length to equal two. For odd \(n\), full Hessian rank gives reduced local points. The proof is universal; the exact \(\mathbf F_4\) census and rank tests in `verify.py` are supplementary only.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk
Xie's arXiv:2610.00259v1 Theorems 1.1–1.4 determine dimension, extremal positive-dimensional partition components, and generic local structure **along coordinate subspaces**, but Section 7 explicitly leaves smaller-dimensional components and primary decompositions open. This theorem specifies all projective points, including non-coordinate indicator points, and their local Artin structures for cubic degree and the indicated congruence classes. Orzel's arXiv:2509.05009v3 gives dimension bounds rather than this singular-scheme classification. The present theorem deliberately excludes \(n=4\) and supplies a uniform statement starting at five variables. Semantically related published results on singular schemes and positive-characteristic quadratic degeneracy concern different hypersurfaces. The key unresolved bibliographic risk is an older equivalent exact classification outside the searched terminology; the older general partition-space paper was inspected through its abstract and its description in Xie's source, not claimed read in full.

## Value — PASS
The complete projective singular subscheme is a natural, previously unsettled invariant of a symmetric cubic family. The new classification distinguishes two sharply different schemes—reduced nodes for odd \(n\), curvilinear doubled nodes forming an exact complete intersection when \(4\mid n\)—while showing that every hypersurface tangent quadric remains smooth. The length \(2^{n-1}\) in the even case explains scheme-theoretic multiplicity not visible from the dimensions and coordinate-axis local rings alone. This is a complete family-level singular-scheme classification, not a generic table recomputation or an arbitrary low-dimensional example.
