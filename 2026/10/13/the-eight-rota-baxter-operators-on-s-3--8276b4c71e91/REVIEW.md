# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof uses the descendant-group homomorphism to restrict kernel and image sizes to four possibilities. Kernel-coset constancy solves the two nontrivial proper-kernel cases, and exact factorization verifies the surviving maps. For trivial kernel, the inverse crossed homomorphism reduces the problem to endomorphisms of \(S_3\), proving that inversion is the only bijective case. The automorphism and involution quotients then follow from the transitivity of conjugation on transposition subgroups and direct formulas. An exhaustive standalone checker independently confirms every claim.

## Originality

PASS. The primary group Rota–Baxter paper supplies the general tools and explicitly motivates classification modulo automorphisms and the standard involution, but its \(S_3\) discussion is about extension examples rather than a full census. A same-authors follow-up gives one representative from each of the two non-elementary \(S_3\) orbit types and observes their cyclic descendant groups, but does not prove exhaustion. Focused semantic-database and web searches found no eight-map table or the stated orbit quotients. An unindexed small-group calculation remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. \(S_3\) is the smallest nonabelian group and appears explicitly as a test case in the foundational literature. The theorem closes its natural finite classification problem, identifies all non-elementary operators as proper splitting maps, and explains why two distinct automorphism types nevertheless produce the same cyclic descendant group.
