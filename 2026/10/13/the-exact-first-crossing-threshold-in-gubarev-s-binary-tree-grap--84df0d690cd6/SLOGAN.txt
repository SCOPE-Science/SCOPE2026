The first-crossing cutoff in Gubarev's binary-tree graph is exactly \(6\cdot4^{n-1}-3\), because a crossing-free path of one fewer edge exists and no longer one can. This lowers the uniform exponent in the source's first-crossing nilpotency theorem from \(2^{2n+1}\) to \(6\cdot4^{n-1}-3\), without claiming that the matrix nilpotency exponent is itself always sharp.
