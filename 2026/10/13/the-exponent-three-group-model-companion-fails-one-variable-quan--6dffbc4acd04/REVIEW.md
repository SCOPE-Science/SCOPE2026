# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Example 5.1 supplies the exact two algebraic behaviors needed. In every exponent-three extension of \(G=B(3,3)\), the element \(a=[x,y]\) satisfies \([a,b,c]=1\) for all \(b,c\), while in \(A*_{\mathcal V_3}F_{\mathcal V_3}(v,w)\) the same element satisfies \([a,v,w]\ne1\). The model-companion property embeds each group into a model of \(T_3^*\), preserving these equations and inequations. Both images of \(a\) are nonidentity elements of order \(3\). Every parameter-free one-variable group term reduces to a power, so the two elements have identical quantifier-free one-types. Their disagreement on \(\forall y\,\forall z\,([x,y,z]=1)\) therefore proves failure of quantifier elimination. Since \(T_3\) is universal, a model completion would eliminate quantifiers, so \(T_3^*\) is not one.

## Value

PASS. Quantifier elimination is a central structural distinction among model companions. The result gives an explicit, minimal-arity obstruction: a single parameter-free one-variable formula separates two elements that no quantifier-free one-variable formula can distinguish. It therefore locates a sharp qualitative boundary of the newly constructed model companion and explains concretely why the exponent-three result stops at model companionship rather than model completion.
