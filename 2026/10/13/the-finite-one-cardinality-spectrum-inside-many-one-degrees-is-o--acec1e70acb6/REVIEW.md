# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The recursive case is exhaustive: \(\varnothing\) and \(\mathbb N\) each form singleton many-one degrees, and all other recursive sets lie in one common many-one degree. Richter--Stephan--Zhang show that this nontrivial recursive degree contains exactly three finite-one degrees. Cintioli proves that every nonrecursive many-one degree contains exactly one or infinitely many finite-one degrees, while Richter--Stephan--Zhang bound the number inside any many-one degree by countable. Therefore the infinite case is exactly \(\aleph_0\), giving the spectrum \(\{1,3,\aleph_0\}\).

## Originality

PASS. Richter--Stephan--Zhang explicitly left other finite cardinalities open. Cintioli later closes the nonrecursive finite-cardinality case but states his result only for nonrecursive degrees. Candidate-specific published-finding and literature searches did not locate the combined global spectrum statement or another source stating that \(3\) is the unique finite cardinality above \(1\).

## Value

PASS. The number of finite-one degrees inside a many-one degree is the central cardinal invariant posed in the 2026 structural study. The accepted theorem gives its complete spectrum and identifies the unique exceptional finite case. This turns an open-ended cardinality question into a closed trichotomy and cleanly separates the recursive exception from the nonrecursive dichotomy.

## Closest literature and limitations

Cintioli (2026) supplies the nonrecursive one-or-infinite theorem. Richter--Stephan--Zhang (2026) supply the recursive three-degree exception, the countable upper bound, and the original open question.

The result is a synthesis rather than a new priority construction, and it does not classify countably infinite order types.
