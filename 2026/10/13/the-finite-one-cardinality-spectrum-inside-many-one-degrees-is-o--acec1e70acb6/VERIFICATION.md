# Verification

The proof is a complete case split.

For recursive many-one degrees, \(\varnothing\) and \(\mathbb N\) each form singleton degrees. Every other recursive set is many-one equivalent to every other nontrivial recursive set by mapping membership to fixed in/out witnesses.

Richter--Stephan--Zhang prove that this nontrivial recursive many-one degree contains exactly three finite-one degrees.

For nonrecursive many-one degrees, Cintioli's Corollary 7 proves:
\[
|F(\mathbf d)|=1
\]
or
\[
|F(\mathbf d)|\text{ is infinite}.
\]

Richter--Stephan--Zhang prove the global upper bound
\[
|F(\mathbf d)|\le\aleph_0.
\]
Therefore the infinite alternative is exactly
\[
\aleph_0.
\]

The cases are exhaustive and yield
\[
|F(\mathbf d)|\in\{1,3,\aleph_0\}.
\]

## Limits

No claim is made about the order type of \(F(\mathbf d)\) in countably infinite cases.

No new priority construction is required or asserted.
