# The first connected twin-free incidence networks indistinguishable by complete richness sampling laws

## Finding

For a binary incidence matrix of species against sampling sites, the *complete richness sampling law* is the number \(L_k(j)\) of \(k\)-site subsets on which exactly \(j\) distinct species appear, for each sampling depth \(k\) and each richness \(j\). Suppose that every row and every column is nonzero, no two rows coincide, no two columns coincide, and the associated bipartite incidence graph is connected. Then the complete richness sampling law reconstructs the matrix, up to independent row and column permutations, whenever the smaller guild has at most three members. Reconstruction first fails at four species and four sites.

In the first counterexample, index four sites by \(0,1,2,3\). Interpret a positive integer as the binary encoding of a species' occupied sites. The two species-neighborhood lists are

\[
A=(1,3,6,14),\qquad B=(1,3,6,13).
\]

Both have species degree multiset \(\{1,2,2,3\}\) and site degree multiset \(\{1,2,2,3\}\), but are nonisomorphic. Their common richness count table, whose rows correspond to \(k=0,1,2,3,4\) and columns to \(j=0,1,2,3,4\), is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0&0\\
0&1&2&1&0\\
0&0&1&3&2\\
0&0&0&1&3\\
0&0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus even every exact finite-sample probability, not just its expectation or variance, can be insufficient to recover ecological interaction structure.

## Assumptions and scope

An incidence matrix has \(r\ge1\) labeled species and \(n\ge1\) labeled sites, with entries zero or one. Sampling is uniformly without replacement among all \(k\)-subsets of the \(n\) sites; probability \(L_k(j)/\binom{n}{k}\) is the observed-richness law. Rows (species) and columns (sites) are individually unlabeled for reconstruction, but the two guilds cannot be interchanged. *Twin-free* means all rows are pairwise distinct and all columns are pairwise distinct. There are no isolated vertices in either guild and the incidence graph is connected. The result addresses deterministic, perfectly observed incidence and does not assume an ecological generative model.

## Proof

Encode each species neighborhood as a nonzero \(n\)-bit integer \(m\). Distinct species neighborhoods give a subset \(\mathcal M\subseteq\{1,\dots,2^n-1\}\), of size \(r\). For a sampled-site mask \(s\), the number of detected species is exactly

\[
R(s)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}{\bf1}_{m\mathbin{\&}s\ne0},
\qquad
L_k(j)=\sum_{s=0}^{2^n-1}{\bf1}_{|s|=k}{\bf1}_{R(s)=j}.
\]

Here \(\&\) denotes bitwise intersection of the encoded sets. The finite classification is performed by the included self-contained `verify.py`; its loops enumerate **every** distinct nonempty species-neighborhood family for \(n\le3\), and, using the equivalent site-neighborhood encoding, **every** family for \(r\le3\). The twin-free condition bounds the opposite guild by \(2^n-1\) or \(2^r-1\), respectively, so these loops omit no admissible size. Every candidate is tested for nonisolated columns, connectedness, and distinct columns, and its entire law is generated by enumerating all site subsets. A collision is reported only after checking all site permutations for a row-multiset match; row permutations are incorporated by sorting. No collisions occur for either small-guild census. The largest one has only seven members in the other guild.

For the displayed witnesses, exact subset enumeration gives the same complete table above, while both remain connected and twin-free. The first matrix has a four-cycle: species of masks \(6\) and \(14\) share two sites. In the second matrix no two species share two sites, so it has no four-cycle; its connected eight-vertex, eight-edge incidence graph has a six-cycle. Graph girth is preserved by any guild-respecting isomorphism. The witnesses are therefore nonisomorphic, proving sharpness.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using standard Python only. The checker generates all finite candidates, computes full probability-count laws with integer arithmetic, and tests guild-preserving isomorphism by all site permutations. It reports `VERIFY_OK`, the case counts, and the witness law. The smallest-guild census includes all admissible other-guild sizes, rather than stopping at an arbitrary enumeration cutoff. The checker is an exhaustive finite proof certificate for this finite boundary, not a statistical simulation.

## Relationship to prior work

Colwell, Mao, and Chang (2004) develop incidence-based sample-rarefaction statistics; their discussion and Table 2 explain that distinct incidence patterns can yield identical *mean* accumulation curves when species frequencies agree. Identical full richness distributions for every sample size are a strictly stronger equivalence than identical means. Boros, Gurvich, and Zverovich (2008) study neighborhood hypergraphs of bipartite graphs, a related structural representation; the exact sampling-law reconstruction boundary is not established by the accessible abstract. The precise complete-law threshold above is the assessed contribution, not the known expectation formula or the general fact that a single average does not identify a matrix.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes noiseless complete incidence data, connectedness and distinct species and site neighborhoods. It does not claim uniqueness from a finite number of empirical samples, identify abundance-weighted ecological networks, or classify all collisions beyond the sharp onset. Previously published descriptions of equivalently distributed incidence systems under different terminology remain possible.
