# Review of the Boolean F-preconditional negation fibre

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Chen's eight algebraic clauses are stated explicitly. P1/P2 fix the top row, P3/P4 reduce every row to the antecedent, identity fixes its top entry, and P5 eliminates precisely the forbidden pair-row value. For three atoms exactly six independent flags remain with three values each, and every resulting operation is checked symbolically against P1--P5 and the F-axioms. Burnside counting yields 138 unlabelled cases. A standalone exhaustive checker verifies 729 full tables, all 138 orbit classes via fixed-point counts, and the two four-element F-preconditionals. Finite verification supports but does not replace the proof.

**Originality (O): PASS with residual literature risk.** Chen (2026) gives F-algebra semantics, not a fibre enumeration. Holliday (2023) already gives the specific null negation and one drastic conditional, so **their existence is not new**; his classification table concerns algebras with negation rather than all conditional expansions over a fixed negation. Holliday (2025), Proposition 4, forces a unique Sasaki conditional under involutive negation, a hypothesis which the present fibre violates. Searches for fixed-negation preconditional enumeration, 729/138 counts, Boolean expansions, and related aliases produced no equivalent result. Prior results for chain-ordered carriers would not by themselves imply the classification of this nonchain Boolean carrier. A more obscure unpublished or differently indexed table could still duplicate the result.

**Value (V): PASS.** The natural forgetful map from F-preconditional algebras to their fundamental negation reduct is injective on implication expansions up to the four-element Boolean carrier and first fails injectivity at eight elements. Giving the exact first nontrivial fibre and its automorphism-orbit structure identifies how much conditional information the negation reduct loses, a structural issue directly motivated by the completeness and translation frameworks. The bound is the **sharp first threshold**, not an arbitrary sample size; it supplies the smallest fully enumerated testbed for distinguishing conditional identities with a fixed negation. This does not assert a global classification or a downstream general theorem.

**Closest sources and limits.** The key full texts inspected are Chen's Sections 3, 6, and conclusion; Holliday (2023), Sections 3 and 6; and Holliday (2025), Sections 2.1--2.5 including Proposition 4. The stated equality of all 729 flattening instances is specific to this finite fibre. Independent review remains pending.
