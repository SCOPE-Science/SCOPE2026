# The five-cube has exactly eighty maximum edge-open packings

## Finding
For the five-dimensional Boolean cube \(Q_5\),
\[
\rho_e^o(Q_5)=12.
\]
There are exactly \(80\) edge-open-packing sets of cardinality \(12\). These sets form one orbit under coordinate permutations and translations of \(\mathbb F_2^5\). In each such set, the selected edges induce four vertex-disjoint copies of \(K_{1,3}\), whose four centers lie in one bipartition class.

## Assumptions and scope
The finite simple graph \(Q_5\) has vertex set \(\mathbb F_2^5\), with two binary vectors adjacent if they differ in one coordinate. Thus it has \(32\) vertices and \(80\) edges. For a simple graph \(G\), an **edge-open packing** is a set \(B\subseteq E(G)\) such that any two distinct edges \(e,f\in B\) have no third edge \(h\in E(G)\setminus\{e,f\}\) joining an endpoint of \(e\) to an endpoint of \(f\). Its maximum cardinality is \(\rho_e^o(G)\). This definition allows edges of a packing to share an endpoint when no third edge joins them; it is not an induced matching.

All numbers refer to the labeled graph with the fixed Boolean-coordinate vertex set, rather than to isomorphism classes. The result addresses this one natural finite cube, not a formula for all cube dimensions.

## Proof
**A twelve-edge construction.** Split the five coordinates into the first three and last two. Write \((a,b)\in\mathbb F_2^3\times\mathbb F_2^2\). Choose the four star centers
\[
(000,00),\quad(000,11),\quad(111,01),\quad(111,10),
\]
and at each center choose the three edges obtained by flipping one of the first three coordinates. These are four mutually disjoint stars, each with three edges. No edge of \(Q_5\) joins endpoints belonging to different stars: within a common first-coordinate pattern, the last-coordinate patterns differ in two bits, while across the two first-coordinate patterns their last-coordinate patterns differ in one bit and their first-coordinate patterns differ in three bits; flipping at most one first-coordinate bit at either end leaves endpoint Hamming distance at least two. Edges within a star have no common third edge because \(Q_5\) has no triangles. Hence these twelve edges are an edge-open packing.

**Exact upper bound and census.** Index all \(80\) undirected cube edges by the endpoint pairs \((x,x\oplus 2^k)\), where bit \(k\) of \(x\) is zero. Form the conflict graph \(H\) on these \(80\) edges: two vertices of \(H\) are adjacent exactly when their corresponding cube edges admit a distinct connecting third edge. As \(Q_5\) is triangle-free, incident cube edges do not conflict; disjoint cube edges conflict precisely if some endpoints are adjacent in \(Q_5\). The two definitions are evaluated separately in the supplied `verify.py` and agree for every pair. The resulting conflict graph is \(28\)-regular and has \(1120\) edges.

The cube automorphism group generated by coordinate permutations and binary translations acts transitively on cube edges. Therefore fix the selected edge \(e_0=\{00000,00001\}\), represented in the program by integer endpoints \((0,1)\). Exactly \(51\) other cube edges do not conflict with \(e_0\). Form their compatibility graph \(J\): two vertices are adjacent precisely if their corresponding cube edges do not conflict. A packing containing \(e_0\) and having \(s+1\) edges is exactly an \(s\)-clique in \(J\).

The fully specified, self-contained exhaustive verifier `verify.py` proves
\[
\#\{\text{12-cliques in }J\}=0,
\qquad
\#\{\text{11-cliques in }J\}=12.
\]
For completeness, its recursion enumerates every candidate clique by branching on the last vertex in a fixed greedy-color ordering of the current candidate set. Each color class is an independent set, so a subproblem with \(r\) already chosen vertices and at most \(c\) available colors cannot contain a target clique of size greater than \(r+c\). That inequality justifies the **only** search pruning; every remaining branch is recursively visited. Each clique has one last vertex and therefore appears exactly once. All adjacency and pruning decisions use finite integer bitsets with exact operations, not numerical tolerances or a time cutoff. The source code and its captured output are included, making the finite enumeration reproducible.

The absence of a 12-clique prohibits any packing of 13 edges containing \(e_0\). Edge transitivity then prohibits a 13-edge packing anywhere. The explicit construction proves the exact optimum \(12\). For the number \(N\) of maximum packings, the double count of pairs \((e,B)\) with \(e\in B\), \(|B|=12\), yields
\[
12N=80\cdot 12,
\qquad N=80.
\]
Finally, applying all \(32\) translations and all \(5!\) coordinate permutations to the exhibited construction produces exactly \(80\) different maximum packings (verified by exact set comparison). Since this equals their entire census, they form one orbit. Every image of the construction consists of four disjoint three-edge stars with centers of one parity, proving the claimed extremal structure.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3. The reproducible output is contained in `verification_output.txt`. In particular, it reports \(51\) candidates after fixing an edge, zero 12-cliques (\(59\) search nodes), twelve 11-cliques (\(265\) search nodes), and an orbit of size \(80\). The program builds the cube explicitly, cross-checks two conflict predicates for every edge pair, independently validates the exhibited construction, and enumerates all claimed symmetry images. The finite census is exhaustive by the recursion argument above; its speed is not evidence of completeness.

## Relationship to prior work
Brešar, Dravec, Hedžet and Samadi, *Induced matching vs edge open packing: trees and product graphs* (arXiv:2406.04020, first publicly available 2024-06-06), establish results for graph products and give in their Table 1 only a lower bound \(\rho_e^o(Q_5)\ge10\). Their Theorem 5.10 determines the hypercube parameter in dimensions that are powers of two; dimension five is outside that family. The same work gives the general bound \(\rho_e^o(Q_5)\le\alpha(Q_5)=16\). Thus the present \(12\), its multiplicity and its orbit structure do not follow from those displayed claims. Brešar and Samadi, *Edge open packing: complexity, algorithmic aspects, and bounds* (arXiv:2403.00750), prove general upper bounds and complexity results rather than an exact five-cube census. The characterization framework of Pandey and Santra (arXiv:2508.01935) does not state an exact five-cube number or maximum-packing census. Equivalent results indexed under injective edge coloring or induced-star packing cannot be categorically excluded.

## Limitations
This is a finite, reproducible exact computation with a fully specified exhaustive proof search; it is not an infinite-dimensional cube theorem. The correctness of the finite upper bound and enumeration depends on the auditable `verify.py` code and its exhaustive branching invariant, not solely on the stored output. There has been no independent third-party audit. A formulation of the same numerical fact or orbit classification under different terminology may exist outside the inspected literature.
