# The ideal-image equational probability spectra of finite medial Latin quandles

## Finding
Let \(M\) be a nonzero finite abelian group, \(T\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)\), and assume that \(1-T\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)\). The Alexander operation
\[
x\star y=T(x)+(1-T)(y)
\]
is a medial Latin quandle. Write \(R=\mathbb Z[T]\subseteq\operatorname{End}(M)\), the commutative unital ring generated by \(T\), and for any ideal \(I\triangleleft R\) set \(IM=\sum_{r\in I}r(M)\). The probabilistic equational spectrum of the **single-binary-operation algebra**, with no named constants or division symbols, is exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(M,\star)=\{\,1/|IM|:I\triangleleft R\,\}.}
\]
For \(|M|>1\), minimality of that spectrum has a complete criterion:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(M,\star)=\{1,1/|M|\}\iff R\text{ is a field}.}
\]
Equivalently, there is a prime \(p\) such that \(M\) is elementary abelian of exponent \(p\) and the minimal polynomial of \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) is irreducible. The corresponding irreducible block may occur with arbitrary positive multiplicity. By the classical affine representation of medial Latin quandles, this applies to every finite medial Latin quandle, up to isomorphism.

When \(M\) is elementary abelian of exponent \(p\), write the elementary divisors of \(T\) as \(f_i^{\lambda_{ij}}\) with distinct irreducible polynomials \(f_i\) of degrees \(d_i\), and set \(e_i=\max_j\lambda_{ij}\). The general ideal formula becomes the explicit finite set
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(M,\star)=\left\{p^{-\sum_i d_i\sum_j\max(\lambda_{ij}-a_i,0)}:0\le a_i\le e_i\text{ for all }i\right\}.}
\]
Duplicate probability values appearing for different tuples are included only once.

## Assumptions and scope
An equation in \(k\ge1\) variables is a pair of terms constructed exclusively from the binary symbol \(\star\) and variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\). Its probability is the number of satisfying \(k\)-tuples, divided by \(|M|^k\). The spectrum is the set over all such equations and all finite \(k\). No constants, inverses, additional module operations, or division operations belong to the signature. The finite group may have mixed prime-power order; no semisimplicity hypothesis is made for the ideal formula. In the irreducible-polynomial statements alone, \(M\) is assumed to have prime exponent. The one-element quandle has spectrum \(\{1\}\) and is excluded from the two-value criterion.

## Proof
**1. Terms realize every affine coefficient vector.** Every term operation in \(k\) variables is necessarily an affine map \(\sum_{i=1}^k r_i x_i\) where the \(r_i\in R\) and \(\sum_i r_i=1\), by induction on its syntax. Conversely, let \(S_k\) be the set of coefficient vectors so realized and let
\[
H_k=\{(r_1,\ldots,r_k)\in R^k:\sum_i r_i=1\}.
\]
The coordinate vectors belong to \(S_k\), and \(S_k\) is closed under \(u\star v=Tu+(1-T)v\). Fix the last coordinate vector \(e_k\); the set \(U=S_k-e_k\) contains zero and is closed under the same operation, now inside the additive module \(W_k=\{(r_i):\sum_i r_i=0\}\). Because \(T\) and \(1-T\) are bijections of the finite group \(M\), they have finite multiplicative order as endomorphisms. Their inverses therefore are positive powers lying in \(R\); both are units of \(R\). The maps \(u\mapsto Tu\) and \(u\mapsto(1-T)u\) map finite \(U\) into itself injectively, hence onto itself. Thus for any \(u,v\in U\) their inverse images belong to \(U\), and
\[
(T^{-1}u)\star((1-T)^{-1}v)=u+v\in U.
\]
So \(U\) is an additive subgroup (finiteness supplies additive inverses), stable under multiplication by \(T\) and by integers, hence an \(R\)-submodule. It contains each \(e_i-e_k\), so it equals all \(W_k\). Therefore \(S_k=H_k\) for every \(k\), even though the signature has only \(\star\).

**2. Every equation has an ideal-image probability.** For an equation between terms with coefficient vectors \((r_i)\) and \((s_i)\), put \(d_i=r_i-s_i\) and \(I=(d_1,\ldots,d_k)\triangleleft R\). The equation is the kernel of the group homomorphism
\[
\psi:M^k\to M,\qquad \psi(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=\sum_i d_i x_i.
\]
Its image is \(\sum_i d_i M=IM\), since the coefficients commute with the entire ring action. By the finite-group homomorphism theorem, the fraction of solutions equals \(1/|IM|\).

**3. Every ideal is witnessed by a genuine equation.** Any ideal \(I\) of the finite ring \(R\) is generated by finitely many elements \(r_1,\ldots,r_m\). By step 1 there is a term in \(m+1\) variables realizing the coefficient vector
\[
(r_1,\ldots,r_m,1-\sum_i r_i).
\]
Equating it with its last variable yields the homomorphism image \(IM\) and probability \(1/|IM|\). The zero ideal is witnessed by an identity. This establishes the exact spectrum formula, not merely one inclusion.

**4. Minimal-spectrum characterization.** If \(R\) is a field, its only ideals are \(0\) and \(R\), giving the two displayed probabilities for nontrivial \(M\). Conversely, if \(R\) is not a field, a finite commutative unital ring \(R\) has a nonzero nonunit \(r\). Because \(R\) acts faithfully as a subring of \(\operatorname{End}(M)\), its image \(rM\) is nonzero. If \(rM=M\), then \(r\) is a bijection of the finite set \(M\), so some positive power of \(r\) is the identity, making \(r\) a unit of \(R\), a contradiction. Hence \(1<|rM|<|M|\), giving an intermediate probability. Finally, a field \(R\) has prime characteristic \(p\), forcing the abelian group \(M\) to have exponent \(p\), and \(R=\mathbb F_p[T]\cong\mathbb F_p[X]/(\mu_T)\) is a field precisely when the minimal polynomial \(\mu_T\) is irreducible.

**5. Elementary-divisor formula.** The module structure theorem over \(\mathbb F_p[X]\) writes \(M\) as a direct sum of cyclic modules \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(f_i^{\lambda_{ij}})\). The endomorphism ring generated by \(T\) is \(R\cong\prod_i\mathbb F_p[X]/(f_i^{e_i})\). Its ideals correspond to \((f_i^{a_i})\), with \(0\le a_i\le e_i\). On the cyclic summand indexed by \((i,j)\), the image of this ideal has \(\mathbb F_p\)-dimension \(d_i\max(\lambda_{ij}-a_i,0)\). Adding these dimensions and applying the first formula proves the displayed explicit spectrum.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` builds coefficient rings from finite matrices or scalar maps, enumerates binary term-coefficient clones (and ternary clones for the smaller examples), constructs all ideal-generated image subgroups, and independently enumerates solution probabilities for every binary-term equation in two variables. It verifies the cases \(\mathbb Z/5\) with \(T=2\), \(\mathbb Z/9\) with \(T=2\), the irreducible two-dimensional binary example, a two-dimensional Jordan block over \(\mathbb F_3\), a four-dimensional double irreducible binary block, and a five-dimensional split binary example. Its output ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

For \(M=\mathbb Z/9\), \(T=2\), the spectrum is exactly \(\{1,1/3,1/9\}\). For the binary module \(\mathbb F_2^5\) with one irreducible block of degree two and one of degree three, the spectrum is \(\{1,1/4,1/8,1/32\}\); for the irreducible binary degree-two block alone, it is \(\{1,1/4\}\). These finite checks validate the implementation and examples, not the unbounded theorem; the proof above is independent of exhaustive computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Cardó introduced the equational probability spectrum and proved that finite nondegenerate groupoids with minimal spectrum must be quasigroups, while leaving classification of quasigroups open (arXiv:2603.19487, Theorem 4 and concluding discussion). Related work developed general spectra and primality but did not state the present ideal-image law (arXiv:2604.08446). Ta proved categorical equivalence of medial Latin quandles with affine modules and constructed their free objects (arXiv:2602.08875, Theorem 5.3 and Proposition 6.1). Those results supply and motivate the affine module viewpoint; the present contribution computes equation probabilities, classifies minimality for this class, and gives the complete elementary-divisor spectrum. The established affine representation and term-clone framework are explicitly used, not claimed as new. Earlier work on affine and medial quasigroups is represented by Stanovský and Vojtěchovský and by the references in Ta's paper. A standard term-function description alone does not state how ideal images exhaust the probability spectrum, but it does supply an essential ingredient.

## Limitations
This theorem is restricted to finite **medial Latin** quandles, each represented by a finite abelian group with \(T\) and \(1-T\) invertible. It does not classify spectra for nonmedial or non-Latin quandles, nor the number or minimal syntactic depth of equations attaining a given probability. The elementary-divisor formula assumes prime exponent; mixed-exponent modules remain covered by the ideal-image formula, but their spectra are not tabulated here using a universal elementary-divisor display. The proof has been self-reviewed; no independent validation or audit has been performed. Earlier universal-algebra treatments of affine clone operations could contain an implicit equivalent probability computation; the present literature comparison does not certify global novelty.
