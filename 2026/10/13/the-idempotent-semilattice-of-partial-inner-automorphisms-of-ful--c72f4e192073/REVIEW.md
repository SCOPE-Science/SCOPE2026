# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof begins from the defining generators \(\phi_{g,h}\), gives their exact source and target as rectangular matrix algebras, and shows that each restriction and preimage is rectangular using the induced isomorphisms of image and quotient factors. This is the essential step: closure of *initial* generating domains alone would not suffice. Rank-one matrices then construct every pair of proper nonzero subspaces from actual generated partial identities. Recovery of image span and common kernel establishes no collisions, while Gaussian coefficients and a rank-one square test give the enumerative refinements. The verifier reproduces generator-domain closure over three small finite fields and the entire generated partial map monoid over \(\mathbb F_2\) in dimension two. Boundary domains \(S\) and \(\{0\}\) are treated separately. Arbitrary fields are handled by finite-dimensional linear algebra, not by extrapolating data.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

Araújo et al., arXiv:2605.00041v1, Definition 3.8 and §§3–7, give the general construction but leave the linear endomorphism-monoid characterization as Problem 7.9. The older related manuscript arXiv:2301.04252v3 concerns natural conjugacy rather than the present exact idempotent semilattice; the 2026 manuscript acknowledges historical content overlap with earlier versions of that work. Real semantic searches for partial inner automorphisms, idempotent domains, matrix endomorphism monoids, and two-subspace Gaussian counts did not locate an implication-equivalent determination or a stronger covering theorem. In particular, classifications for full transformation monoids do not assert the subspace pair domains or their algebra multiplication. Residual risk includes unpublished results, older editions, and alternate terminology; database nonhits alone are not treated as a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

Idempotents determine the intrinsic semilattice of an inverse monoid. Computing every idempotent for the previously uncharacterized linear endomorphism case of a specifically posed open problem is a natural structural invariant, not an arbitrary field-size experiment. The classification detects rectangular matrix subalgebras beyond ordinary corners, with a sharp zero-product boundary and complete finite-field domain-size distribution. It leaves genuinely harder non-idempotent structure open.
