# Scientific review: inverse Hadamard line decompositions of a smooth coordinate-tangent quadric

## Correctness — PASS
The nonzero coordinate sections of a smooth quadric's Segre parametrization belong to the four-dimensional space of biforms of bidegree \((1,1)\). Tangency of each fixed coordinate plane forces each to have tensor rank one. Factoring gives two line maps whose product is the Segre embedding. Every competing product line pair also gives an isomorphism of the Segre surface, so uniqueness of tensor factors and the two rulings exhaust all pairs. A reciprocal-scaling orbit cannot meet its switched orbit, because the Hadamard square of one line spans at most a plane. Stabilizers vanish by injectivity of the product map and existence of a full-coordinate line point. These steps cover **every** smooth coordinate-tangent quadric over \(\mathbf C\), not merely general members. The embedded code replays an exact example but is not the basis for the theorem.

## Originality — PASS
The closest work, Antolini–Ballico–Oneto, arXiv:2502.18813, identifies which quadrics arise and their coordinate tangencies, and proves that the **quadric** is determined by its singular coordinate locus; it explicitly leaves recovering the **factor lines** as Question 3.12. The published dimension-three parameter-fiber computation is compatible with, but does not give, exactly two free torus orbits. The 2016 identifiability result of Bocci–Carlini–Kileel concerns point factorizations from a fixed linear space, not all unknown line pairs over a quadric. Ballico's cancellation and real-ghost results likewise concern different implications. Semantic searches for inverse factor recovery, Segre section factorization, ruling swap, diagonal torus orbits and generic parameter fibers found no dominating statement in the examined indexed literature. Searches do not prove absolute novelty; a differently indexed equivalent Segre interpretation and the partly inaccessible 2024 monograph are residual risks.

## Value — PASS
The result answers the exact natural reconstruction question posed by the initiating paper and upgrades the known dimension of the solution family to a complete, explicit classification of the ambiguity for **all** smooth coordinate-tangent quadrics. It supplies a constructive procedure, proves its exhaustiveness, and distinguishes ordered from unordered identifiability. This goes beyond a numerical confirmation or an arbitrary special instance.

## Scope and limits
Only algebraically closed characteristic-zero geometry is claimed. No assertion is made about scheme-theoretic reducedness of the fiber, degenerations to singular quadrics, other curve degrees or real descent.
