# The Martinez-Maure constant-width octic has singularity basket \(3E_6+6A_1+2A_6\)
## Finding
Let \(C\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\) be the projective closure of the affine constant-width curve introduced by Yves Martinez-Maure from the support function \(h(\theta)=8-\sin(3\theta)\). Writing \(s=x^2+y^2\) and \(q=y(y^2-3x^2)\), its affine equation is
\[
F=(s^2+8q)^2+432q(351-10s)-567^3-28s^3-486s(67s-567\cdot18)=0.
\]
Then \(C\) is an irreducible rational plane octic whose normalization is \(\mathbb P^1\), and its complete complex singularity basket is
\[
3E_6+6A_1+2A_6.
\]
The three \(E_6\) points are real and form the orbit of \((0,9)\) under rotation by \(2\pi/3\). The six \(A_1\) nodes are nonreal and form the two conjugate rotation orbits generated by \((0,-9+6i\sqrt3)\) and \((0,-9-6i\sqrt3)\). The two \(A_6\) cusps are the points at infinity \([1:i:0]\) and \([1:-i:0]\). Their delta invariants sum to
\[
3\cdot3+6\cdot1+2\cdot3=21,
\]
which equals the arithmetic genus \((8-1)(8-2)/2\) of a plane octic.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The source curve is the degree-eight curve defined by Equation (3) in Martinez-Maure's article, with first verified public appearance on 2021-07-05. ADE notation is used in the standard plane-curve sense: \(A_1\) is an ordinary node, \(A_6\) has Puiseux pair \((2,7)\), and \(E_6\) has Puiseux pair \((3,4)\). The claim concerns the reduced projective closure of this exact equation, not arbitrary degree-eight curves of constant width.

## Proof
Introduce complex coordinates \(u=x+iy\) and \(v=x-iy\). With \(z=e^{i\theta}\), the support-function parametrization gives
\[
u= -iz^4+8z-2iz^{-2},\qquad v=2iz^2+8z^{-1}+iz^{-4}.
\]
After homogenizing in \([S:T]\in\mathbb P^1\), this is the base-point-free degree-eight morphism
\[
[U:V:W]=[-iS^8+8S^5T^3-2iS^2T^6:\ 2iS^6T^2+8S^3T^5+iT^8:\ S^4T^4].
\]
Direct substitution into the homogenization of \(F\) gives zero. On the affine parameter chart, eliminating \(z\) from
\[
uz^2+iz^6-8z^3+2i=0,\qquad vz^4-2iz^6-8z^3-i=0
\]
returns exactly the equation \(F\) expressed in \((u,v)\). The penultimate subresultant is linear in \(z\), with its \(z\)-coefficient nonzero at the point corresponding to \(z=1\); hence the generic fiber has one point. Therefore the map is birational, so \(C\) is irreducible and its normalization is \(\mathbb P^1\). This agrees with the general properness result for constant-width curves with a nonzero even Fourier coefficient in Bardet--Bayen.

The polynomial is invariant under rotation by \(2\pi/3\). Solving the exact affine Jacobian system \(F=F_x=F_y=0\) gives nine points. They are the three-point rotation orbit of \((0,9)\) and the two conjugate three-point orbits generated by \((0,-9\pm6i\sqrt3)\). At a nonreal representative, the Hessian determinant is nonzero, so all six nonreal singularities are ordinary nodes of type \(A_1\).

At the real representative \((0,9)\), write the normalization parameter as \(z=i+t\). The expansions begin
\[
x=12t^3+18it^4-30t^5+\cdots,
\qquad
y-9=-9t^4-18it^5+\cdots.
\]
Thus the branch has coprime characteristic orders \((3,4)\), hence type \(E_6\). Rotation gives the other two real \(E_6\) points.

The degree-eight homogeneous part of \(F\) is \((x^2+y^2)^4\), so the only points of \(C\) at infinity are \([1:i:0]\) and \([1:-i:0]\). Near the first, use the chart \(V=1\) and local parameter \(r=S/T\). If \(a=U/V\) and \(b=W/V\), then
\[
a=-2r^2-24ir^5+195r^8+\cdots,
\qquad b=-ir^4+8r^7+66ir^{10}+\cdots.
\]
After the analytic coordinate change \(c=b+\frac{i}{4}a^2\),
\[
c=-16r^7-273ir^{10}+\cdots.
\]
Hence the characteristic orders are \((2,7)\), giving type \(A_6\). Complex conjugation gives the same type at the other point at infinity.

Finally, \(\delta(E_6)=3\), \(\delta(A_1)=1\), and \(\delta(A_6)=3\), so the displayed singularities contribute total delta invariant \(21\), exactly the arithmetic genus of a degree-eight plane curve. This independently exhausts the genus defect and confirms that no further singularities can occur.

## Relationship to prior work
Martinez-Maure proves that the displayed octic is a noncircular convex algebraic curve of constant width \(16\), derives its explicit parametrization, and obtains its implicit equation by elimination. Bardet--Bayen prove general degree and properness statements for algebraic constant-width curves; in particular, their results account for irreducibility/proper parametrization and show that degree \(8\) is the minimum possible noncircular degree. Neither inspected full text gives the singularity basket above: searches in the lead article found no occurrence of “singular,” “cusp,” “node,” “normalization,” or “rational,” while Bardet--Bayen discuss singular points only abstractly in the proof of properness and do not classify the singularities of this specialization. Panraksa--Washington study other real algebraic constant-width constructions and isolated real points, not this later equation.

## Limitations
The originality comparison is literature-based rather than an exhaustive theorem that no equivalent coordinate presentation exists. Older or less-indexed work on rational support-function curves could conceivably contain the same singularity calculation under another normalization. The computation is over \(\mathbb C\); the real convex locus sees only the three \(E_6\) points, while the six nodes and two \(A_6\) points are not finite real points. No claim is made about equisingular deformations or about all minimal-degree constant-width octics.
