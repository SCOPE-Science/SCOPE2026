# The multiplicative order gives the smallest Hermitian-cover saturation bound
## Finding
Let \(N\ge3\), let \(p\nmid N\) be prime, and assume that
\[
p^\nu\equiv-1\pmod N
\]
for some \(\nu\ge1\). Put
\[
d=\operatorname{ord}_N(p)
\]
and let \(X_N^{2m}\subset\mathbf P^{2m+1}_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p}\) be the smooth degree-\(N\) Fermat hypersurface, where \(m\ge1\).

Then \(d\) is even and the least positive exponent \(\nu\) satisfying \(p^\nu\equiv-1\pmod N\) is exactly \(d/2\). Consequently the smallest Hermitian variety in Harashita's diagonal covering construction is obtained from
\[
q_0=p^{d/2},\qquad e_0=\frac{q_0+1}{N},
\]
and gives a finite quotient map
\[
\pi_0:\mathcal H(2m+1,q_0^2)\longrightarrow X_N^{2m}
\]
of degree
\[
e_0^{2m+1}.
\]

Let \(\Lambda_{N,p}\subset\mathrm N^m(X_N^{2m})\) be the lattice generated by the classes \(\pi_{0*}[P]\), where \(P\) ranges over the maximal totally isotropic \(m\)-planes on the Hermitian variety. Then
\[
\boxed{p^{md/2}\,\mathrm N^m(X_N^{2m})\subset
\Lambda_{N,p}\otimes\mathbf Z_{(p)}}.
\]
The \(p\)-primary part of the discriminant group
\[
\Lambda_{N,p}^{\vee}/\Lambda_{N,p}
\]
is annihilated by the same power \(p^{md/2}\). For every prime \(\ell\ne p\) with \(\ell\nmid e_0\), one also has
\[
\Lambda_{N,p}\otimes\mathbf Z_\ell
=
\mathrm N^m(X_N^{2m})\otimes\mathbf Z_\ell,
\]
and this lattice is unimodular.

Among all diagonal Hermitian covers obtained by choosing a power \(q=p^\nu\) with \(N\mid q+1\), the choice \(q=q_0\) has the smallest cover degree and gives the smallest annihilator bound \(q^m\) supplied by Harashita's descent theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(\mathrm N^m(X_N^{2m})\) denotes codimension-\(m\) algebraic cycles modulo numerical equivalence, modulo torsion, equipped with the middle intersection pairing. The lattice \(\Lambda_{N,p}\) is defined from the specific minimal diagonal Hermitian cover above.

The restriction \(N\ge3\) is essential for the arithmetic normalization used in the proof: then \(-1\) is a nontrivial element of order two in \((\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times\). The hypothesis that some \(p^\nu\) is congruent to \(-1\) modulo \(N\) is the classical supersingularity criterion used by Shioda--Katsura and by Harashita for even-dimensional Fermat hypersurfaces.

The displayed power \(p^{md/2}\) is an annihilator bound for the \(p\)-localized saturation defect. It is not asserted to be the actual exponent of the quotient, and it can be non-sharp for a particular Fermat variety.

## Proof
Because \(p^\nu\equiv-1\pmod N\), the element represented by \(p^\nu\) in the cyclic subgroup \(\langle p\rangle\subset(\mathbf Z/N\mathbf Z)^\times\) has order two. The subgroup \(\langle p\rangle\) has order
\[
d=\operatorname{ord}_N(p).
\]
A cyclic group has a unique element of order two. Since \(N\ge3\), the residue class of \(-1\) is nontrivial of order two, so \(d\) is even and
\[
p^{d/2}\equiv-1\pmod N.
\]
Moreover every exponent with value \(-1\) satisfies
\[
\nu\equiv\frac d2\pmod d.
\]
Thus the least such exponent is \(d/2\), and every other one has the form
\[
\nu=\frac d2+kd\qquad(k\ge0).
\]

Set \(q_0=p^{d/2}\) and \(e_0=(q_0+1)/N\). Harashita's Lemma 7.7 gives the finite surjective morphism
\[
[x_0:\cdots:x_{2m+1}]\longmapsto[x_0^{e_0}:\cdots:x_{2m+1}^{e_0}]
\]
from \(\mathcal H(2m+1,q_0^2)\) onto \(X_N^{2m}\), of degree \(e_0^{2m+1}\). Since \(q_0\) is a power of \(p\), the integer \(q_0+1\) is prime to \(p\); hence \(e_0\) and the cover degree are prime to \(p\).

Harashita's Proposition 7.9 applies to this cover. It states that if \(\Lambda_Z\) is generated by the pushforwards of the Hermitian generators, then the \(p\)-part of \(\Lambda_Z^\vee/\Lambda_Z\) is annihilated by \(q^m\), and
\[
q^m\mathrm N^m(Z)\subset\Lambda_Z\otimes\mathbf Z_{(p)}.
\]
Taking \(q=q_0\) and \(Z=X_N^{2m}\) gives
\[
q_0^m=p^{md/2},
\]
which proves both \(p\)-local assertions. The same proposition gives unimodularity at every prime \(\ell\ne p\) not dividing the cover degree; because that degree is \(e_0^{2m+1}\), this is equivalent to \(\ell\nmid e_0\).

Finally, every other admissible power is
\[
q=p^{d/2+kd}\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
Both \(q\) and \((q+1)/N\) increase with \(k\). Hence the cover degree
\[
\left(\frac{q+1}{N}\right)^{2m+1}
\]
and Harashita's bound \(q^m\) are simultaneously minimized at \(k=0\). This proves the optimality statement within the diagonal Hermitian-cover construction.

## Verification
The proof has two independent parts. The arithmetic part is the uniqueness of the order-two element in the cyclic group \(\langle p\rangle\). The geometric part is a direct specialization of Harashita's Lemma 7.7 and Proposition 7.9 to the least admissible power \(q_0=p^{d/2}\).

As consistency checks, for \(N=3\) and \(p=2\) one has \(d=2\), so the method gives the bound \(2^m\); for the Fermat cubic fourfold \(m=2\), the resulting bound is \(4\). For \(N=5\) and \(p=2\), one has \(d=4\), hence the bound is \(2^{2m}\). These examples are illustrations only; the universal proof does not depend on finite enumeration.

The source was checked at its supersingular Fermat criterion, the explicit quotient map and degree, and the full \(p\)-adic descent proposition. The source does not introduce \(\operatorname{ord}_N(p)\) or optimize over admissible powers \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
Harashita proves that whenever \(N\mid q+1\), the Fermat hypersurface is a finite quotient of the corresponding Hermitian variety and that, for a cover of degree prime to \(p\), the pushforward-generator lattice has \(p\)-primary saturation defect killed by \(q^m\). The paper leaves \(q=p^\nu\) as an arbitrary admissible power.

The observation here converts that family of bounds into a canonical one depending only on the residue class of \(p\) modulo \(N\): the least admissible exponent is half the multiplicative order. This also identifies the unique smallest diagonal Hermitian cover and minimizes its induced saturation bound.

Shimada's earlier lattice theorem treats the extremal Hermitian/Fermat case of degree \(q+1\), proving that the lattice generated by middle-dimensional linear spaces has full rank and \(p\)-power discriminant. It does not provide the arbitrary-degree Fermat quotient together with the multiplicative-order optimization used here.

Targeted searches for the exact multiplicative-order bound, its saturation formulation, and the equivalent minimal-Hermitian-cover formulation did not locate an implication-equivalent statement.

## Limitations
The result optimizes the bound delivered by Harashita's diagonal Hermitian-cover method; it does not prove that \(p^{md/2}\) is the exact exponent of the actual saturation quotient. A different set of algebraic cycles may give a smaller exponent.

The statement concerns the \(p\)-local middle numerical cycle lattice. At primes dividing \(e_0\), Harashita's descent theorem makes no unimodularity assertion.

No claim is made for \(N=2\), where \(-1\equiv1\pmod2\) and the order-two argument degenerates.
