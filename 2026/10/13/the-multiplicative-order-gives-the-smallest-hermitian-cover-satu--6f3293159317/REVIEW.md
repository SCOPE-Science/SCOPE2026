# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Let \(d=\operatorname{ord}_N(p)\). Because \(N\ge3\), the class of \(-1\) has order two. If \(p^\nu\equiv-1\pmod N\), then \(-1\) lies in the cyclic group generated by \(p\), whose unique element of order two is \(p^{d/2}\). Hence \(d\) is even and every admissible exponent is congruent to \(d/2\) modulo \(d\). The least one is therefore \(d/2\).

Harashita's Lemma 7.7 gives the finite quotient from the Hermitian variety at \(q_0=p^{d/2}\), with degree \(e_0^{2m+1}\), where \(e_0=(q_0+1)/N\). This degree is prime to \(p\). Proposition 7.9 then gives exactly the claimed inclusion
\[
p^{md/2}\mathrm N^m(X_N^{2m})\subset\Lambda_{N,p}\otimes\mathbf Z_{(p)}
\]
and annihilation of the \(p\)-primary discriminant group by the same power. Its prime-to-\(p\) statement gives unimodularity when \(\ell\nmid e_0\). Since all other admissible exponents have the form \(d/2+kd\), both the cover degree and the source bound \(q^m\) are minimized at \(k=0\). No step infers an infinite statement from a finite experiment.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating paper was inspected in full at the supersingular Fermat subsection and the complete \(p\)-adic descent subsection. It states the bound for an arbitrary admissible \(q=p^\nu\), but it does not introduce \(\operatorname{ord}_N(p)\), select the least admissible \(q\), or state the optimized bound \(p^{md/2}\). Shimada's classical lattice paper treats the degree-\(q+1\) Fermat/Hermitian case, not this arbitrary-degree quotient optimization. Searches for the exact formula, saturation language, minimal-cover language, and multiplicative-order aliases found no implication-equivalent statement. The current and prior ledgers contain Fermat geometry findings, but none concerns positive-characteristic numerical cycle saturation or this source.

## Value
PASS. Harashita's new result turns Hermitian generators into quantitative integral information for arbitrary supersingular Fermat quotients, but its bound depends on a noncanonical choice of admissible power \(q\). The present result removes that choice and produces a canonical, computable \(p\)-local saturation bound depending only on \(m\), \(N\), and the residue class of \(p\). This is a natural invariant for explicit integral cycle calculations and simultaneously identifies the smallest cover in the construction. The claim carefully stops at optimality within the method and does not overstate the true lattice exponent.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest new source is Harashita's Proposition 7.9 together with Lemma 7.7. The closest older source is Shimada's degree-\(q+1\) Fermat lattice theorem. Neither inspected statement gives the multiplicative-order optimization for general \(N\).

The actual saturation quotient may have smaller exponent, and primes dividing \(e_0\) are outside the prime-to-\(p\) unimodularity assertion.
