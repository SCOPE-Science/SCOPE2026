# The Nash threefold family has a divisibility-controlled singular-curve and canonical-index transition

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\) and every algebraically closed field \(k\) of positive characteristic \(p\mid n\), consider the normal Gorenstein toric hypersurface
\[
X_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,c,w]/(w^n-abc).
\]
Write \(Y_n\) for its **first normalized Nash blowup**. Then \(Y_n\) has three mutually isomorphic affine normal toric charts. Each chart has one torus-fixed singular point and has a four-ray defining cone. Put \(g_2=\gcd(n,2)\) and \(g_3=\gcd(n,3)\). Its four two-dimensional faces, in cyclic order, have lattice indices
\[
\frac{n}{g_2},\quad \frac{n}{g_2g_3},\quad \frac{n}{g_2g_3},\quad \frac{n}{g_2}.
\]
An index greater than one means the corresponding one-dimensional torus orbit belongs to the singular locus. Hence each chart's singular locus is **isolated for \(n=2\)**, contains exactly **two irreducible torus-invariant curves for \(n=3\) or \(n=6\)**, and contains exactly **four such curves for \(n\ge4\) with \(n\ne6\)**. The fixed point belongs to those curves whenever they exist.

More sharply, the canonical divisor of \(Y_n\) obeys the complete trichotomy
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{condition on }n&\text{canonical divisor of }Y_n\\\hline
6\mid n&\text{Cartier (Gorenstein)}\\
n\text{ odd},\ 3\nmid n&\mathbb Q\text{-Cartier of exact index }n\\
\text{otherwise}&\text{not }\mathbb Q\text{-Cartier}.
\end{array}
\]
These assertions concern the first **normalized** Nash blowup, not an infinite sequence of Nash modifications or the Nash blowup without normalization.

## Assumptions and scope

The integer \(n\ge2\) is arbitrary and the field \(k\) is algebraically closed with \(\operatorname{char}k=p>0\) and \(p\mid n\). No smoothness assumption is made on \(X_n\). The terms *face index* and *canonical index* are distinct: the former is the index of the two primitive generators of a rank-two cone in the saturated lattice of their real span; the latter is the least positive integer making the canonical Weil divisor Cartier. A nonregular two-dimensional toric cone generates a one-dimensional singular torus orbit in a normal toric threefold.

## Proof

**1. Toric presentation and applicability of the Nash formula.** Work in \(M=\mathbb Z^3\), with monomial exponents
\[
\gamma_a=(1,0,0),\quad\gamma_b=(0,1,0),\quad
\gamma_w=(0,0,1),\quad\gamma_c=(-1,-1,n).
\]
They satisfy \(n\gamma_w=\gamma_a+\gamma_b+\gamma_c\). The monomial ring is precisely \(k[a,b,c,w]/(w^n-abc)\). It is normal: the defining polynomial is irreducible, the hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay, and its singular locus is contained in the union of the three coordinate axes \(w=0\) (codimension two). Alternatively, the semigroup is the intersection of its cone with \(M\), as is seen using the fractional-lattice description \(\mathbb Z^3+\mathbb Z(1/n,1/n,1/n)\) in positive-octant coordinates. Therefore the positive-characteristic logarithmic Jacobian blowup theorem of Duarte–Jeffries–Núñez-Betancourt applies.

The four three-column minors, in the natural order, have absolute values \(1,n,1,1\). Because \(p\mid n\), the characteristic-dependent monomial ideal contains precisely the terms with the other three triple sums. Their exponents, in a fixed order, are
\[
v_1=(1,1,1),\qquad v_2=(0,-1,n+1),\qquad v_3=(-1,0,n+1).
\]
Equivalently, the ordinary hypersurface Jacobian ideal is \((ab,ac,bc)\); the toric logarithmic Jacobian ideal differs by the common nonzerodivisor \(w\), so it gives the same blowup. Permuting \(a,b,c\) permutes the three ideals' generating monomials and the three Newton vertices. Each is indeed a vertex: a valuation that is smallest on \(v_1\) comes from \((0,0,1)\), and symmetry handles the remaining two. Thus the normalized blowup has exactly three maximal affine charts, all isomorphic.

**2. A complete chart and its four rays.** On the chart corresponding to \(v_1\), the unnormalized affine semigroup is
\[
\Gamma+\mathbb N(v_2-v_1)+\mathbb N(v_3-v_1),
\]
where \(\Gamma=\mathbb N\gamma_a+\mathbb N\gamma_b+\mathbb N\gamma_w+\mathbb N\gamma_c\). Taking the saturation replaces it by the intersection with \(M\) of the character cone generated by
\[
A=(1,0,0),\quad B=(0,1,0),\quad C=(-1,-2,n),\quad D=(-2,-1,n).
\]
Indeed \(\gamma_c=B+C=A+D\) and \(\gamma_w=(3A+3B+C+D)/(2n)\), so the four displayed vectors generate the full chart cone over \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\); each spans an extremal ray. Its dual cocharacter cone \(\sigma_n\subset N_{\mathbb R}\) has precisely the four primitive rays
\[
\begin{split}
r_0&=(0,0,1),\\
r_x&=(n/g_2,0,2/g_2),\\
r_m&=(n/g_3,n/g_3,3/g_3),\\
r_y&=(0,n/g_2,2/g_2).
\end{split}
\]
They are cyclically ordered \(r_0,r_x,r_m,r_y\). Pairing with \(A,B,C,D\) verifies that each is nonnegative on the character cone and vanishes on the two independent defining generators of the appropriate facet. These are all facets, since the character cone has exactly four extremal rays. Primitivity follows from the definitions of \(g_2,g_3\).

**3. All codimension-two singular strata.** For primitive vectors \(s,t\in\mathbb Z^3\), the index in the saturated rank-two lattice of their plane is the greatest common divisor of the coordinates of \(s\times t\). Applying this to the cyclic pairs of the four primitive rays gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{dual face}&\text{lattice index}\\\hline
(r_0,r_x)&n/g_2\\
(r_x,r_m)&n/(g_2g_3)\\
(r_m,r_y)&n/(g_2g_3)\\
(r_y,r_0)&n/g_2.
\end{array}
\]
A normal toric variety is smooth at the generic point of the orbit of a face exactly when that face is regular. All one-dimensional faces are generated by primitive vectors and hence regular. The full three-dimensional cone cannot be regular because it has four rather than three rays, so its unique closed orbit is singular. The two-dimensional nonregular faces yield the listed singular curves. Since \(n/g_2=1\) holds among \(n\ge2\) only for \(n=2\), and \(n/(g_2g_3)=1\) holds precisely when \(n\mid6\), the claimed count of irreducible singular curves follows. In particular, the chart is isolated-singular only for \(n=2\).

**4. Canonical \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier criterion and exact index.** For a normal affine toric variety, the canonical Weil divisor is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier exactly when a rational character \(m\in M\otimes\mathbb Q\) pairs to \(1\) with every primitive ray of the cone. Write \(m=(u,v,t)\). The equations for \(r_0,r_x,r_y\) uniquely give
\[
t=1,\qquad u=v=(g_2-2)/n.
\]
The fourth equation for \(r_m\) becomes
\[
(2g_2-1)/g_3=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_3=2g_2-1.
\]
Since \(g_2\) is either \(1\) or \(2\) and \(g_3\) either \(1\) or \(3\), this happens exactly when \(n\) is odd and prime to \(3\), or when \(6\mid n\). In the first case the unique rational support function has \(m=(-1/n,-1/n,1)\), so its smallest integral multiple is exactly \(n\). In the second it has \(m=(0,0,1)\), proving Cartierness. In the remaining cases the compatibility equation fails, proving that the canonical divisor is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier. As the three charts are isomorphic and cover \(Y_n\), these statements hold globally. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The independent arithmetic checker `verify.py` uses exact integer and rational arithmetic for every \(2\le n\le512\). It recomputes the four toric rays by oriented cross products of all pairs of character-cone generators, identifies their facets, measures saturated lattice indices, and solves the canonical support-function equations. It also checks the singular-orbit counts and Cartier indices. The resulting output is `VERIFY_OK: independent dual-ray enumeration, lattice indices, and canonical index for n=2..512`. The parameter-uniform proof above, not the finite tests, establishes the theorem for all \(n\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Duarte, Jeffries and Núñez-Betancourt established the characteristic-\(p\) monomial Jacobian/Nash formula (Theorem 1.9) and analyzed **the isolated exponent \(n=2\)** in their higher-dimensional example, including explicit chart semigroups and nonresolution after one normalized Nash blowup. Those facts and the algorithm are prior results. The claimed contribution is instead the **complete all-exponent singular-stratum and canonical-index classification**: the indices for arbitrary \(n\), the switch from isolated to curve singularities, the exceptional \(n=3,6\) cases, and the exact \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein/Cartier arithmetic trichotomy. The later characteristic-free toric paper of Castillo and collaborators revisits the same \(n=2\) cone as an example and develops general criteria, but its studied criteria do not state this arithmetic classification. Their subsequent computational study treats distinct four-dimensional hypersurface examples and algorithmic searches.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to algebraically closed fields with \(p\mid n\). It does not assert the behavior for \(p\nmid n\), describe the unnormalized Nash blowup's singular scheme, count **global** irreducible curves after identifying chart overlaps, or settle termination of repeated modifications. The singular-stratum counts are per affine chart, not additive across all three charts. The earliest source date is the first preprint submission, not journal publication. Independent validation of the mathematical claim has not been performed.
