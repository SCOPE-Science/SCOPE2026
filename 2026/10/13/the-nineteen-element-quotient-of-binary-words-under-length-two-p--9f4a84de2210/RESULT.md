# The nineteen-element quotient of binary words under length-two palindromic duplication

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma=\{0,1\}\). On the free monoid \(\Sigma^*\), define \(\equiv\) to be the least monoid congruence containing the relations
\[
  uabv\equiv uabbav\qquad
  (u,v\in\Sigma^*,\ a,b\in\Sigma).
\]
In particular, one may insert or delete the reversal of a contiguous length-two factor immediately after that factor, with no limit on the number of moves and either direction allowed.

**Theorem (complete congruence classification).** For all \(x,y\in\Sigma^*\), one has \(x\equiv y\) if and only if exactly one of the following holds:

1. Both words are empty.
2. Both are the same one-letter word.
3. Both have length at least two, have the same length parity and first letter, and have matching *initial-run type*, defined by the maximal initial constant run \(a^r\) as follows:
   - **constant:** all letters are \(a\);
   - **one:** the word is mixed and \(r=1\);
   - **even:** the word is mixed and \(r\ge2\) is even;
   - **odd:** the word is mixed and \(r\ge3\) is odd.

Consequently, the quotient monoid \(\Sigma^*/{\equiv}\) has **exactly 19 elements**: one empty class, two one-letter classes, and sixteen classes indexed by a first letter, a length parity, and one of four initial-run types. The number of equivalence classes intersecting words of length \(n\) is
\[
 c(n)=\begin{cases}
 1&n=0,\\
 2&n=1,\\
 4&n=2,\\
 6&n=3,\\
 8&n\ge4.
 \end{cases}
\]
For every \(n\ge4\), selecting one word of length \(n\) from each of the eight classes gives a binary code correcting an arbitrary number of length-two palindromic duplications. **The maximum possible size of such a code is not determined here**: words within the same undirected congruence class need not have intersecting *forward* descendant cones.

## Assumptions and scope
The empty word and length-one words are included. An elementary duplication replaces \(ab\) by \(abba\), even when \(a=b\), and a deduplication performs the inverse replacement. Here the quotient is for the **symmetric, context-closed equivalence** generated by those moves; it is not a classification of the directed descendant reachability preorder. Each move changes length by exactly two. For a mixed word \(a^rbs\), \(b\ne a\) is its first change of symbol, \(r\ge1\), and \(s\in\Sigma^*\) is the remaining suffix.

## Proof
**Step 1 (invariants).** Length parity and the initial letter are preserved by each move. The empty word and each one-letter word admit no move. A constant word stays constant because a duplicated constant pair contributes only the same symbol, and the inverse deletion cannot introduce a different symbol. For a mixed word, let \(r\) be its maximal initial run length. A duplication wholly within that run adds exactly two to \(r\); every other duplication leaves \(r\) unchanged. More precisely, a duplication straddling the first symbol change begins with the same terminal letter of the initial run and inserts the opposite symbol before the inserted copy of that terminal letter, so it does not lengthen the run. By reversing this reasoning, an allowed deduplication preserves whether the word is mixed and, when mixed, whether \(r=1\), \(r\ge2\) even, or \(r\ge3\) odd. Thus all stipulated data are invariants of \(\equiv\).

**Step 2 (a nontrivial local relation).** The following two chains are forward length-two palindromic duplications; each arrow is directly verified by inserting the reverse of one existing adjacent pair:

`010 -> 01001 -> 0110001 -> 011010001 -> 01101100001`

`011 -> 01101 -> 0110101 -> 011011001 -> 01101100001`.

The common endpoint proves \(010\equiv011\). Simultaneously exchanging zero and one proves \(101\equiv100\). Consequently, for any *distinct* binary symbols \(a,b\),
\[
 aba\equiv abb,\qquad baa\equiv bab.
\]

**Step 3 (two-letter suffix erasure).** Using one elementary duplication and the two Step 2 relations,
\[
 ab\equiv abba\equiv abaa\equiv abab
 \quad (a\ne b).
\]
More generally, for every \(u,v\in\{a,b\}\), the first three positions of \(abuv\) allow the third symbol to be changed to \(a\) by \(aba\equiv abb\); then the last three positions of \(abaa\) allow the fourth symbol to be changed to \(b\) by \(baa\equiv bab\). Therefore
\[
 abuv\equiv abab\equiv ab \qquad(a\ne b,\ u,v\in\{a,b\}).
\]
These relations hold within arbitrary left and right contexts by definition of a monoid congruence.

**Step 4 (complete reduction).** Let a mixed word be \(w=a^rbs\), where \(b\ne a\) and \(r\ge1\). Apply Step 3 to the factor consisting of its last initial \(a\), its first \(b\), and the next two suffix letters. Repeat to remove all pairs from \(s\). If \(|s|\) is even, the result is \(a^rb\). If \(|s|\) is odd, one trailing symbol remains, which can be changed to \(a\) using Step 2 on the terminal three-letter factor; the result is \(a^rba\). Since \(aa\equiv aaaa\), blocks of four consecutive \(a\)'s can be shortened to two until \(r\) is \(1\), \(2\) or \(3\), as dictated by its initial-run type. Thus every mixed word reduces to one of \(ab,aba,aab,aaba,aaab,aaaba\), with its first letter, parity, and run type selecting a unique one of these six patterns per choice of \(a\), except that the length-two and length-three minimal cases have no extra restrictions.

A constant word \(a^n\), \(n\ge2\), reduces in the same way to \(aa\) if \(n\) is even and to \(aaa\) if \(n\) is odd. The empty and singleton words remain unchanged. Any two words with identical invariant data thus reduce to exactly the same canonical word, so they are congruent. Together with Step 1, this proves the classification.

**Step 5 (exact count).** For each of the two first letters, the four run types occur for both parities: constants are represented by \(aa,aaa\); run-one cases by \(ab,aba\); even-run cases by \(aaba,aab\); and odd-run cases by \(aaab,aaaba\), with the pairs listed in even/odd order when possible. This gives eight mixed-or-constant classes per first letter, i.e. sixteen length-at-least-two classes, plus the empty and two singleton classes. At length \(2\), only constant and run-one types are possible; at length \(3\), additionally even-run type is possible; from length \(4\) onward all four occur. This gives the displayed formula for \(c(n)\).

For the code observation, any two words with distinct invariants cannot reach a common descendant via forward duplications, because every forward step is also a step in \(\equiv\). Hence one representative from each fixed-length class has pairwise disjoint descendant cones. This does not prove that eight is the optimal code size.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` checks each step of the two 4-duplication chains, including its bit-complement version; it checks preservation of the stated invariant on all binary words of lengths \(2\) through \(13\). It constructs the graph with all binary words of lengths \(2\) through \(14\) and all available duplication edges, then checks that the induced components contain exactly the signature classes on each length slice \(2\) through \(9\). It also checks the total 19 canonical signatures and fixed-length counts through length \(9\). Execute `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3 and no additional dependencies. These finite checks are **not** a proof of the theorem for arbitrary lengths; the proof is Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work
Zeraatpisheh, Esmaeili, and Gulliver (2020, Section III-A) introduce length-two palindromic roots, show that such roots need not be unique, and construct unlimited-error codes using priority deduplication and joint irreducibility; the result here instead classifies the symmetric congruence of *every binary word*, including words without the special roots. Yohananov and Schwartz (first posted in 2023; Section 5 in the 2024 arXiv version) prove zero asymptotic rate for fixed duplication length at least two and arbitrary finite alphabets, a statement that neither specifies nor entails this exact finite quotient. Later work on reverse-complement and palindromic duplications likewise addresses error-correcting constructions and not the 19-state binary quotient. The current theorem is intentionally not presented as a sharp maximum-code theorem, nor does it contradict the earlier capacity-zero result.

## Limitations
The equivalence relation here allows both duplications and deduplications, whereas actual storage channels ordinarily permit only forward errors. The congruence classification therefore supplies a **sufficient** invariant-based construction of disjoint descendant cones, not a necessary criterion for forward cone intersection. No exact optimal number of codewords, shortest path lengths, or classification over alphabets with more than two letters or other duplication lengths is claimed. The possibility of an unpublished or differently formulated earlier equivalent result remains a priority risk.
