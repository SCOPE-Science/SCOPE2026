# The order-two quasi-Galois configuration of the Wiman sextic is the line graph of the Tutte–Coxeter graph
## Finding
Let \(C\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf C}\) be the Wiman sextic. For each quasi-Galois point \(P\) with \(|G[P]|=2\), write \(\tau_P\) for the nontrivial element of \(G[P]\) and \(\ell_P=F[P]\setminus\{P\}\) for its fixed axis. Then:

\[
\#\{P:|G[P]|=2\}=45.
\]

The \(45\) axes \(\ell_P\) are exactly the \(45\) Wiman reflection lines, while the \(45\) centers \(P\) are exactly the \(45\) quadruple points of that line arrangement. For distinct \(P,Q\),

\[
P\in\ell_Q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \tau_P\tau_Q=\tau_Q\tau_P.
\]

Consequently every axis contains exactly four quasi-Galois centers and every center lies on exactly four axes. After labeling a center and its axis by the common involution, the center-axis incidence graph is the commuting graph of the \(45\) double transpositions of \(A_6\). Identifying a double transposition with the incident pair consisting of its fixed duad and the syntheme formed by its two transposed pairs together with that fixed duad identifies this commuting graph with the line graph of the Tutte–Coxeter graph, equivalently the Levi graph of the duad-syntheme model of \(GQ(2,2)\).

There are exactly \(30\) Klein-four subgroups generated by commuting involutions. They split into two \(A_6\)-orbits of size \(15\): a duad type, in which all three involutions have the same fixed duad, and a syntheme type, in which their three fixed duads form one syntheme. Each quasi-Galois center lies in exactly one reflection triangle of each type.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbf C\). The curve is the classical smooth Wiman sextic with its Valentiner projective \(A_6\)-action. The phrase “order-two quasi-Galois point” means a point \(P\in\mathbf P^2\) with \(|G[P]|=2\) in the sense of Fukasawa–Miura–Takahashi. The Wiman arrangement means the orbit of a projective involution's fixed line under this \(A_6\)-action.

The finite-geometry identification is combinatorial: the Tutte–Coxeter graph is taken in its standard duad-syntheme realization, with the \(15\) two-subsets of a six-set on one side and the \(15\) partitions into three pairs on the other, incidence being containment.

## Proof
Fukasawa–Miura–Takahashi prove for the Wiman sextic that \(\operatorname{Aut}(C)=G_2(C)\cong A_6\). Their involution argument also shows that an involution of a smooth plane curve in characteristic different from two is represented projectively by a homology: it has one isolated fixed point and one fixed line. For every involution \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(C)\), the involution argument in Proposition 2.6 identifies its isolated projective fixed point \(P_\sigma\) as a quasi-Galois point with even \(|G[P_\sigma]|\). For the Wiman sextic Theorem 2.7 excludes order \(6\), so \(|G[P_\sigma]|=2\). Conversely, every order-two quasi-Galois point supplies one involution. If two distinct involutions had the same center \(P\), both would lie in the cyclic group \(G[P]\) of order \(2\), which is impossible. Since \(A_6\) has exactly \(45\) involutions, there are exactly \(45\) centers.

Bauer–Di Rocco–Harbourne–Huizenga–Seceleanu–Szemberg describe the Wiman arrangement as the orbit of the fixed line of an involution of the projective \(A_6\)-action. Thus the fixed axes of the \(45\) quasi-Galois involutions are exactly its \(45\) lines.

Now take distinct order-two quasi-Galois points \(P,Q\). If \(P\in\ell_Q\), then \(\tau_Q\) fixes \(P\). Therefore conjugation by \(\tau_Q\) preserves the cyclic group \(G[P]=\langle\tau_P\rangle\), so \(\tau_Q\tau_P\tau_Q^-1=\tau_P\). Conversely, if \(\tau_P\) and \(\tau_Q\) commute, then \(\tau_Q\) preserves the fixed locus of \(\tau_P\). The isolated fixed point of \(\tau_P\) is unique, so \(\tau_Q(P)=P\). Since \(P\neq Q\), the point \(P\) lies on the fixed axis \(\ell_Q\). This proves the center-axis/commutation equivalence.

In \(A_6\), an involution has cycle type \((2,2,1,1)\). Its centralizer in \(A_6\) has order \(8\) and contains exactly five involutions, itself and four others. Hence each Wiman axis contains exactly four quasi-Galois centers. Bauer et al. prove independently that the Wiman arrangement has exactly \(45\) quadruple points and that every Wiman line contains four of them. The \(45\) distinct quasi-Galois centers are therefore exactly those quadruple points.

For the finite-geometry identification, label the six symbols by \(1,\ldots,6\). An involution \((ab)(cd)\) fixes the duad \(\{e,f\}\), where \(\{a,b,c,d,e,f\}\) is the full six-set, and determines the syntheme
\[
\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\},\{e,f\}\}.
\]
This is an incidence edge between a duad and a syntheme. It is a bijection from the \(45\) double transpositions to the \(45\) edges of the duad-syntheme Levi graph. Two distinct double transpositions commute exactly when their edges share the duad endpoint or the syntheme endpoint. Hence the commuting graph is exactly the line graph of that Levi graph, which is the Tutte–Coxeter graph.

Finally, three pairwise commuting nonidentity involutions form a Klein four-group. In the duad-syntheme graph, the corresponding three edges are exactly the star of one vertex. There are therefore \(15+15=30\) such groups, in two \(A_6\)-orbits according to which bipartition class the star center belongs to. Simultaneous diagonalization of a projective Klein four-group shows that its three isolated centers are noncollinear and each involution's axis is the line through the other two centers. Thus every Klein four-group gives a reflection triangle, and each involution-edge has exactly two endpoints, so every center belongs to exactly one triangle of each type.

It checks all \(45\) double transpositions, the four commuting neighbors of each, all \(30\) Klein four-groups, their \(15+15\) conjugacy-orbit split, the duad-syntheme edge bijection, the equivalence “commute if and only if the corresponding incidence edges share an endpoint,” and the connected cubic \(30\)-vertex incidence graph of girth \(8\). The projective-geometric premises—\(\operatorname{Aut}(C)=G_2(C)\cong A_6\) and the Wiman arrangement census—are sourced from the cited papers rather than inferred from the finite computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Fukasawa–Miura–Takahashi Part I proves \(\operatorname{Aut}(C)=G_2(C)\) for the Wiman sextic, while Part II develops arrangement methods and explicitly enumerates the sextic case for quasi-Galois groups of order \(3\). Part II does not state the order-two Wiman configuration above. Bauer et al. give the classical \(45\)-line Wiman arrangement, its \(45\) quadruple points, their \(D_8\) stabilizers, and the fact that each line contains four quadruple points, but do not identify those points as quasi-Galois projection centers or identify the pole-axis incidence with the line graph of the Tutte–Coxeter graph.

Thus the new content is the bridge between the quasi-Galois projection structure and the classical Valentiner/Wiman reflection arrangement, together with the exact duad-syntheme/Tutte–Coxeter interpretation and the two \(15\)-element families of reflection triangles. The classical raw counts of Wiman lines and multiple points are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The argument is for the complex Wiman sextic and uses its standard projective \(A_6\)-action. It does not classify order-two quasi-Galois configurations for arbitrary sextics. The finite verifier certifies the \(A_6\) combinatorics, not the cited algebro-geometric theorems. Older Valentiner literature may encode the pole/mirror or duad-syntheme incidence under classical terminology; no such source was found in the inspected modern literature, so that is the main residual originality risk.
