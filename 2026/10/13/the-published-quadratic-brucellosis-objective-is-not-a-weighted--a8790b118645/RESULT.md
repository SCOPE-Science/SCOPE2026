# The published quadratic brucellosis objective is not a weighted-sum scalarization

## Finding

The brucellosis article first defines four economic-loss objectives as the linear integral functionals
\[
J_1=\int_0^T D_0kI\,dt,
\]
\[
J_2=\int_0^T\left[D_1(I_1+I_2)+D_2(C_1+C_2)\right]dt,
\]
\[
J_3=\int_0^T B_0S\theta\,dt,
\qquad
J_4=\int_0^T\left(B_1S_1\varphi_1+B_2S_2\varphi_2\right)dt.
\]

A weighted-sum scalarization of these objectives is therefore
\[
J_{\rm WS}=\omega_1J_1+\omega_2J_2+\omega_3J_3+\omega_4J_4.
\]

Equation (16) in the article is a different functional. It squares the instantaneous rates before integration:
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_Q=\int_0^T\Bigl[&\omega_1(D_0kI)^2
+\omega_2\{[D_1(I_1+I_2)]^2+[D_2(C_1+C_2)]^2\}\\
&+\omega_3(B_0S\theta)^2
+\omega_4\{(B_1S_1\varphi_1)^2+(B_2S_2\varphi_2)^2\}\Bigr]dt.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus equation (16) is not a weighted-sum scalarization of equations (12)–(13). It defines a new quadratic-cost optimal-control problem.

The difference changes the control structure. For the genuine weighted sum \(J_{\rm WS}\), the Hamiltonian is affine in each of the bounded controls. Except on singular switching surfaces, the optimal controls are therefore at their bounds. The smooth interior formulas in equation (19) instead come from the quadratic penalties introduced in equation (16).

Consequently, the article's Theorem 4.3 and its numerical optimal-control figures solve the quadratic problem actually written in equation (16), not the advertised weighted sum of the four original economic-loss objectives.

## Assumptions and scope

The claim uses the controlled state system (14), the four objective functionals in (12)–(13), the quadratic functional (16), and the bounded control set (11) exactly as printed.

No claim is made that quadratic running costs are intrinsically inappropriate. They are common and can be useful. The issue is equivalence: replacing each original running loss by its square changes the optimization problem and the meaning of the weights.

The result does not assert that every numerical trajectory in the article is wrong. It identifies which objective those trajectories optimize, assuming the displayed optimality system was used.

The result does not reassess the paper's reproduction-number, equilibrium, or global-stability results.

## Proof

Write the four original objectives as
\[
J_i=\int_0^T\ell_i(t)\,dt.
\]
By definition, assigning weights to the objective functionals and summing gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_iJ_i
=\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_i\int_0^T\ell_i(t)\,dt
=\int_0^T\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_i\ell_i(t)\,dt.
\]
There is no squaring operation in this identity.

The article's equation (16) replaces the first, third, and fourth running losses by their squares. For the second objective it goes further: it replaces
\[
D_1(I_1+I_2)+D_2(C_1+C_2)
\]
by the sum of the two separate squares. Hence equation (16) is neither \(J_{\rm WS}\) nor a fixed scalar multiple of it.

Nor can the quadratic functional be recovered as a monotone transformation of each original objective value. On \([0,1]\), let
\[
\ell_A(t)=1\quad(0\le t\le1/2),
\qquad
\ell_A(t)=0\quad(1/2<t\le1),
\]
and let
\[
\ell_B(t)=3/5.
\]
Then
\[
\int_0^1\ell_A\,dt=1/2<3/5=\int_0^1\ell_B\,dt,
\]
whereas
\[
\int_0^1\ell_A^2\,dt=1/2>9/25=\int_0^1\ell_B^2\,dt.
\]
Thus integration and pointwise squaring can reverse the ordering of nonnegative cost streams. This example proves a functional non-equivalence; it is not used as an attainable-state construction.

Now form the Hamiltonian for the actual weighted sum. The only terms depending on \(\theta\) are
\[
\omega_3B_0S\theta-\lambda_1S\theta+\lambda_3S\theta.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{\partial H_{\rm WS}}{\partial\theta}
=S(\omega_3B_0-\lambda_1+\lambda_3).
\]
Since \(0\le\theta\le\bar\theta\), minimization gives
\[
\theta^*=0
\]
when the switching coefficient is positive and
\[
\theta^*=\bar\theta
\]
when it is negative; a zero coefficient is the singular case.

Similarly,
\[
\frac{\partial H_{\rm WS}}{\partial\varphi_1}
=S_1\left[\omega_4B_1+\beta I(\lambda_4-\lambda_5)\right],
\]
and
\[
\frac{\partial H_{\rm WS}}{\partial\varphi_2}
=S_2\left[\omega_4B_2+\epsilon\beta I(\lambda_7-\lambda_8)\right].
\]
Hence these controls are likewise bang-bang away from their switching surfaces.

Equation (19) instead divides adjoint differences by quantities proportional to the current susceptible population. Those interior expressions are precisely the first-order conditions generated by the squared running costs in equation (16), not by the original linear economic-loss objectives.

## Verification

The bundled `verify.py` checks three independent pieces.

First, it verifies the ranking reversal
\[
1/2<3/5
\]
but
\[
1/2>9/25.
\]

Second, symbolic differentiation reconstructs the three weighted-sum Hamiltonian switching coefficients.

Third, it symbolically differentiates the source's quadratic vaccination term and confirms that its stationarity condition is an interior formula proportional to
\[
\frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_3}{2\omega_3B_0^2S},
\]
which is the structure printed in equation (19).

No numerical optimal-control solve is used to establish the mismatch.

## Relationship to prior work

The motivating brucellosis article explicitly says that the weighted-sum method transforms its four-objective problem into equation (16). Its Figure 9 later describes the economic loss using the four integrals in equation (12), while the displayed optimality system was derived from equation (16).

Zarei, Vahidian Kamyad, and Effati give a directly relevant multiobjective epidemic-control treatment. They define weighted-sum scalarization as multiplying each objective functional by a weight and summing the weighted objectives. The same article explicitly distinguishes quadratic drug-cost models from linear drug-cost models and notes that the latter lead to on-off treatment structure. This is consistent with the bang-bang distinction proved above.

Marler and Arora's multiobjective-optimization survey, which the brucellosis article itself cites, treats weighted sums as scalarizations of the objective functions rather than pointwise squaring of their running costs.

Targeted searches by the brucellosis DOI, exact title, equation terminology, weighted-sum language, quadratic-cost language, and correction or erratum terminology did not locate a published correction of this source-specific mismatch.

## Limitations

The finding does not say that the quadratic functional is mathematically invalid. It says that it is a different objective from the four economic-loss functionals stated immediately beforehand.

A new modeling paper could deliberately redefine the objectives to be the quadratic running-cost functionals and then legitimately derive equation (19). That would not recover a weighted-sum solution of the original linear objectives.

The functional ranking-reversal example is used only to prove that pointwise squaring before integration does not preserve objective ordering in general; it is not asserted to be an attainable pair of trajectories of the brucellosis system.
