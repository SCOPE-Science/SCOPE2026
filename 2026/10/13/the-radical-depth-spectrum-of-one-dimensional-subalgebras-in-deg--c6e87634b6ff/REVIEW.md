# Scientific assessment: radical-depth spectra of exterior-algebra lines

## Correctness — PASS

The algebra is the degree-four quotient, the radical is exactly the positive-degree summand, and a multiplication-closed one-dimensional space is shown to be either the scalar line or a line generated by a square-zero radical element. Exterior graded commutation gives the precise degree-three and degree-four square conditions. The alternating-bivector normal form proves, in all odd characteristics, that vanishing of the degree-four square is **equivalent** to decomposability; the annihilating degree-one vectors form its two-plane. Exact finite-field Gaussian counts yield all four depth coefficients with denominators counting projective lines. Independently coded truncated exterior multiplication exhaustively checks the indicated small prime-field cases. The proof, not extrapolation from these tests, treats all dimensions and prime powers.

Classical Grassmannian point counts are acknowledged as ingredients rather than asserted novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The number and position in the Loewy filtration of one-dimensional subalgebras is a natural exact invariant of a local algebra; the formula classifies it for every generator number and every finite field, not for a chosen small example. Degree four is the earliest truncation at which squaring a bivector detects the Plücker cone, which explains both the Gaussian-binomial terms and the abrupt change in characteristic two. The full depth spectrum is stronger than the total point count and is suitable as a boundary datum for higher-dimensional subalgebra enumeration. No unsupported wider classification or downstream theorem is claimed.
