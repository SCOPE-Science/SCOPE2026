# The Section 5 threefold is equivariantly the suspension \(\operatorname{Susp}(\mathbf A^2,y_2)\)
## Finding
Let \(K\) be a field and let
\[
X=V(u_1v-y_1y_2,\;u_2v-(y_1+1)y_2,\;u_1(y_1+1)-u_2y_1)\subset \mathbf A^5_K.
\]
Give \(X\) the action
\[
t\cdot(u_1,u_2,v,y_1,y_2)=(tu_1,tu_2,t^{-1}v,y_1,y_2).
\]
Then \(X\) is \(\mathbb G_m\)-equivariantly isomorphic, over the quotient \(\mathbf A^2_{y_1,y_2}\), to the suspension
\[
\operatorname{Susp}(\mathbf A^2,y_2)=V(wv-y_2)\subset \mathbf A^2_{y_1,y_2}	imes\mathbf A^2_{w,v}.
\]
The isomorphism is given by
\[
w=u_2-u_1,\qquad u_1=y_1w,\qquad u_2=(y_1+1)w,\qquad y_2=wv.
\]
In particular, the degree-one homogeneous component of the coordinate ring is
\[
A_1=wK[y_1,y_2],
\]
so it is cyclic. Thus the displayed Section 5 example in Arzhantsev--Shakhmatov does not establish the claimed failure of the fiber-structure characterization of suspensions.

## Assumptions and scope
The algebraic calculation works over every field \(K\), and therefore in the algebraically closed characteristic-zero setting of the cited source. The conclusion concerns only the displayed Section 5 example and the argument based on its allegedly non-cyclic degree-one component. It does not prove the proposed general characterization of suspensions.

## Proof
Set
\[
w=u_2-u_1.
\]
Subtracting the first defining equation from the second gives
\[
(u_2-u_1)v=y_2,
\]
hence \(wv=y_2\). The third defining equation rewrites as
\[
u_1-y_1(u_2-u_1)=0,
\]
so \(u_1=y_1w\). Adding \(w\) then gives \(u_2=(y_1+1)w\).

Define
\[
\Phi:\mathbf A^3_{w,v,y_1}\longrightarrow X,
\qquad
(w,v,y_1)\longmapsto (y_1w,(y_1+1)w,v,y_1,wv).
\]
Substitution shows that all three defining equations of \(X\) vanish on \(\Phi\). Conversely,
\[
\Psi:X\longrightarrow\mathbf A^3_{w,v,y_1},
\qquad
(u_1,u_2,v,y_1,y_2)\longmapsto(u_2-u_1,v,y_1)
\]
is polynomial. The identities above show that \(\Phi\circ\Psi\) and \(\Psi\circ\Phi\) are the identity maps. Hence \(X\cong\mathbf A^3\), with equation \(wv=y_2\) when viewed over the quotient coordinates \((y_1,y_2)\).

Under the given action, \(w\) has weight \(1\), \(v\) has weight \(-1\), and \(y_1\) has weight \(0\), so the isomorphism is equivariant. Its invariant ring is
\[
K[w,v,y_1]^{\mathbb G_m}=K[y_1,wv]=K[y_1,y_2].
\]
Finally,
\[
u_1=y_1w,\qquad u_2=(y_1+1)w,\qquad w=u_2-u_1,
\]
so the source's displayed generators \(u_1,u_2\) generate exactly the principal module \(wK[y_1,y_2]\).

## Relationship to prior work
Arzhantsev and Shakhmatov introduce the displayed threefold in Section 5 to show that natural fiber properties of a quotient map do not characterize suspensions. Their argument states that the degree-one component generated by \(u_1,u_2\) is not cyclic over the quotient ring. The identities above show the opposite: both generators are multiples of \(w=u_2-u_1\), and \(w\) itself belongs to their span.

The checked current source version, the indexed publication record, exact-equation searches, and searches under the aliases “suspension,” “cyclic homogeneous component,” and the explicit coordinate equations did not locate a prior correction of this Section 5 example.

## Limitations
This finding invalidates the displayed counterexample, not the paper's factoriality or flexibility theorems and not any claim about the existence of some other counterexample. It also does not establish that the fiber-structure conditions are sufficient to characterize suspensions. A later correction not present in the checked source version or indexed literature would supersede the originality claim.
