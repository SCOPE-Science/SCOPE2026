# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact polynomial identities \(F(S\mathbf x)=F(T\mathbf x)=F(\mathbf x)\) and \(F(U\mathbf x)=49F(\mathbf x)\) transport the explicitly squared initial bitangent restriction. Exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_7]\) gives 28 distinct lines and checks the six cyclotomic Galois fixed counts. The greatest-common-norm and polynomial-gcd calculation excludes every possible collision for odd primes other than seven, while the Jacobian equations prove smoothness in the claimed range. Combining these with the classical 28-bitangent count and unramified Frobenius establishes all quantifiers. The standalone executable reconstructed those checks successfully. Eight finite-prime independent censuses are corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
Janasz–Pokora–Zieliński prove the complex 28-line incidence structure and list bitangents of a different Klein model. Bethea–Brazelton show the automorphism group is transitive on the bitangents of this rational model, provide numerical coefficients and identify four real bitangents. These results are genuine near-prior work. They do not state the **cyclotomic Galois** orbit distribution, the minimal common field and individual minimal fields, or the exact Frobenius fixed-line counts at every odd prime away from seven. We exclude the known 28 lines, complex incidences, geometric transitivity and four-real-line count from the original claim. Classical computations or unindexed arithmetic tables could still imply some/all arithmetic conclusions; the search is evidence of uncovered coverage, not proof of worldwide novelty.

## Value — PASS
The joint field-of-definition and good-prime classification answers a natural arithmetic question for a canonical highly symmetric quartic: it distinguishes rational, real-cubic and six-degree bitangents and determines precisely which reductions expose all 28. Uniform collision exclusion matters because a cyclotomic presentation alone does not justify reducing an entire bitangent configuration at every prime. The result is a complete natural finite configuration classification rather than a numerical sample or generic restatement of complex orbit transitivity.
