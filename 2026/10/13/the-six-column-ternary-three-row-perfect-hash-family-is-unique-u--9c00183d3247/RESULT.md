# The six-column ternary three-row perfect hash family is unique up to natural equivalence
## Finding
Let \(A\) be a \(3\times k\) array over \(\{0,1,2\}\). Call \(A\) a \(\operatorname{PHF}(3;k,3,3)\) when, for every three distinct columns, at least one row contains all three symbols. Then \(k\le 6\). At equality there are exactly \(36\) unordered column sets with fixed row and symbol labels, or \(25{,}920\) ordered arrays.

Under the natural group generated by permutations of the three rows and independent permutations of the three symbols in each row, the \(36\) unordered maxima form one orbit. The group has order \(1296\), the orbit has size \(36\), and the stabilizer of a maximum has order \(36\). A representative has columns
\[
000,\;011,\;102,\;121,\;212,\;220.
\]

Every maximum has a structural description: in each row every symbol occurs exactly twice, so each row defines a perfect matching on the six column positions by joining equal-symbol columns. The three row matchings are edge-disjoint and together form \(K_{3,3}\). Conversely, any row-labeled one-factorization of a labeled \(K_{3,3}\), with the three symbols assigned to the three pairs in each row, is a maximum \(\operatorname{PHF}(3;6,3,3)\).
## Assumptions and scope
Rows and symbols are labeled unless an equivalence is explicitly applied. Columns are distinct. The count \(36\) regards a code as an unordered set of six ternary columns; multiplying by \(6!\) gives the ordered-array count \(25{,}920\). The equivalence group used here is \(S_3\ltimes (S_3)^3\) in the evident action: permute rows and independently relabel symbols within each resulting row.
## Proof
The finite universe consists of the \(27\) ternary columns. A complete search tests every seven-subset and every six-subset against the defining condition. No seven-subset is a perfect hash family, while exactly \(36\) six-subsets are. Hence the maximum is \(6\) and the literal census is exact.

For every one of the \(36\) maxima, direct inspection from the defining array shows that each row has multiplicity pattern \(2,2,2\). Joining the two columns bearing each symbol therefore gives three perfect matchings, one per row. The verifier checks that their nine edges are distinct, every column position has degree \(3\), and the resulting graph is bipartite with parts of size \(3\); it is therefore \(K_{3,3}\). Conversely, in a one-factorization of \(K_{3,3}\), any three vertices cannot contain one edge from each of the three factors, because that would form a triangle. Thus among any three columns some row has no equal-symbol pair, hence has three distinct symbols, which is precisely the perfect-hash condition.

There are \(10\) labeled bipartitions of six positions into two parts of size \(3\), each \(K_{3,3}\) has \(2\) one-factorizations, the three factors can be assigned to rows in \(3!\) ways, and symbols can be assigned within each row in \((3!)^3\) ways. This gives \(10\cdot2\cdot3!\cdot(3!)^3=25{,}920\) ordered arrays, agreeing with the exhaustive census. Exhausting the natural group of order \(3!\cdot(3!)^3=1296\) on one maximum produces all \(36\) unordered maxima and gives stabilizer order \(1296/36=36\).

## Relationship to prior work
The six-column optimum itself is not new. Kim's thesis displays a \(\operatorname{PHF}(3;6,3,3)\) and attributes that construction to earlier hash-family work; Walker and Colbourn's later existence table records \(6\) as the maximum number of columns for three rows and three symbols at strength three. The contribution here is the complete equality structure: the exact \(36\)-family labeled census, the single natural-equivalence class, and the \(K_{3,3}\) one-factorization characterization. Searches for the exact parameter, its perfect-hash-code aliases, uniqueness, counts, and the \(K_{3,3}\) formulation did not locate a prior statement implying that classification.
## Limitations
This is a finite equality classification for one natural parameter. It does not give new asymptotic bounds or constructions for larger alphabets. An earlier 1996 dissertation cited by the accessible thesis is not openly readable in full from the inspected repository; it remains a residual originality risk because it could contain an unindexed equality classification, although the accessible later sources use it for a construction rather than for such a classification. The exact maximum \(6\) is prior work and is not claimed as new.
