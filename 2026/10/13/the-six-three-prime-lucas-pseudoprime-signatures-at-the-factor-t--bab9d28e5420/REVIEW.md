# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Williams' squarefree local criterion reduces the three-prime question to three explicit divisibility conditions. Two positive quotients satisfy \(k\in\{1,2\}\) and \(j\le13\) or \(j\le7\); eliminating the final prime gives a Diophantine identity with no vanishing-denominator case. It forces \(q\le21\) and \(r\le65\). Both independent exhaustive finite sieves were replayed using standard-library exact arithmetic and produce precisely the six listed signed rows. Dirichlet's theorem and the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions rigorously yield realization and density.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence.** Williams (1977) proves the general Korselt-like *criterion*, not an exact cross-discriminant complete list with an explicit small-prime cutoff. Helmreich and Webster (2023 preprint; expanded 2024 publication) develop algorithms and finite tabulations for individual discriminants, not all prime discriminants simultaneously. Carmichael and Lucas–Carmichael statements account for the two uniform-sign rows but not the four mixed-sign rows. Comparable published mathematical records on Lucas–Carmichael three-prime families restrict different sign patterns or factor-shapes. Explicit equivalent tabulations outside accessible indexed literature remain a bibliographic risk. The new contribution is the exact specialized classification, not the prior criterion itself.

**Value — PASS.** The least prime factor \(3\) is the first natural three-factor boundary of this Carmichael-type problem. Resolving it completely across infinitely many discriminants produces six and only six signed examples, including four mixed-sign cases invisible to all-plus/all-minus theories; it also provides an explicit independent benchmark for cross-discriminant tabulation and a uniform density law. The result is a finite but natural complete classification, not an arbitrary computed slice.

The published general criterion, its broader algorithmic coverage, and the limits of the special-case contribution are explicitly acknowledged. Finite verification is exhaustive only because the proof first establishes the finite cutoff; it does not establish the infinitude/density part empirically. Independent mathematical assessment remains outstanding.
