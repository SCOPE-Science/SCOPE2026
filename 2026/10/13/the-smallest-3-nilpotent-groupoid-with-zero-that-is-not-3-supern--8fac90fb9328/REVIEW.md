# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The four-element table has exact-depth value sets \(D_1=\{0,a,b\}\), \(D_2=\{0,a\}\), and \(D_3=\{0\}\), so Theorem 4.7 of Đurđev–Mudrinski gives \(3\)-nilpotency. Since \(b=c\cdot c\in G^2\) and \(b\cdot b=a\neq0\), Proposition 5.3 gives failure of \(3\)-supernilpotency. For minimality, a nonzero \(w=uv\) with \(u,v\in H^2\) is forced by Proposition 5.3; \(3\)-nilpotency makes \(w\) a two-sided annihilator. If \(u\neq v\), four distinct elements \(0,u,v,w\) are immediate. If \(u=v\), an order-at-most-three algebra cannot produce \(u\) from a product without forcing the contradictory identities \(u^2=u\) and \(u^2=w\).

## Originality
PASS. The motivating 2026 paper gives an eight-element separator at \(n=3\) as the \(H_3\) instance of its general construction, but the inspected full text does not state a minimum-cardinality separator. Targeted searches for the minimum, four-element separators, and the condition \((G^2)^2\neq\{0\}\) found no covering result. Earlier semigroup-with-zero papers concern the associative setting, where nilpotency and supernilpotency coincide.

## Value
PASS. Minimum counterexample size is a natural sharp invariant for the paper’s first noncoincidence phenomenon. Replacing the available eight-element witness by a provably smallest four-element witness isolates the obstruction to the single two-step chain \(c^2=b\), \(b^2=a\), and shows that no additional combinatorial bulk is needed.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Đurđev–Mudrinski, arXiv:2610.03348v1, especially Proposition 5.3 and Example 5.4. The result is restricted to groupoids with absorbing zero and \(n=3\); no higher-level minimum is asserted. A residual risk remains that a small-example catalogue not surfaced by the targeted searches could contain an isomorphic table without emphasizing this exact minimum statement.
