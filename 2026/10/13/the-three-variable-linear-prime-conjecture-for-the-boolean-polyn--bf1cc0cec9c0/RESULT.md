# The three-variable linear-prime conjecture for the Boolean polynomial semiring
## Finding
Let \(\mathbb B=\{0,1\}\) be the Boolean semifield, with \(1+1=1\), and let \(R=\mathbb B[x,y,z]\). If an ideal \(I\subset R\) is generated by linear polynomials and does not contain all three variables \(x,y,z\), then \(I\) is prime if and only if
\[
I=(x_i:i\in S)
\]
for some subset \(S\subseteq\{x,y,z\}\).

Thus the three-variable case of the linear-prime conjecture posed by Bishop, Eppolito, Green, Jun, Norwood, and Thackrah holds. This is the first dimension beyond their complete two-variable classification.

## Assumptions and scope
A polynomial over \(\mathbb B\) is identified with its finite monomial support in \(\mathbb N^3\). Addition is union of supports and multiplication is Minkowski sum. A linear polynomial is a nonzero polynomial whose support is a nonempty subset of
\[
\{(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}.
\]
There are exactly \(15\) such polynomials.

The statement concerns only ideals generated by linear polynomials and only those for which at least one of \(x,y,z\) is absent from the ideal. It does not classify linear-generated prime ideals containing all three variables, and it does not classify arbitrary prime ideals.

## Proof
For a finite support \(F\subset\mathbb N^3\) and a set \(\mathcal G\) of linear supports, write \((\mathcal G)\) for the ideal they generate. The following support criterion is exact:
\[
F\in(\mathcal G)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall a\in F\ \exists G\in\mathcal G,\ u\in\mathbb N^3:
a\in u+G\subseteq F.
\]
Indeed, any expression of \(F\) as a Boolean sum of polynomial multiples of generators expands into a union of monomial shifts \(u+G\). Since Boolean addition has no cancellation, every used shift must lie inside \(F\), and every monomial of \(F\) lies in at least one used shift. Conversely, choosing one such shift for each \(a\in F\) and taking their union gives an ideal expression for \(F\).

Now enumerate the \(2^{15}=32768\) subsets of the \(15\) linear polynomials. For a generating set \(\mathcal G\), let \(C(\mathcal G)\) be the set of all linear polynomials that belong to \((\mathcal G)\). The support criterion computes \(C(\mathcal G)\) exactly. Moreover,
\[
(\mathcal G)=(C(\mathcal G)),
\]
because \(\mathcal G\subseteq C(\mathcal G)\subseteq(\mathcal G)\). Hence the linear closure \(C(\mathcal G)\) is a complete canonical key for the ideal.

The exhaustive calculation gives \(1748\) distinct linear-generated ideals. Exactly \(1728\) of them are proper and omit at least one of \(x,y,z\). Among these, \(7\) are generated by a proper subset of \(\{x,y,z\}\). For each of the other \(1721\) ideals, the attached certificate supplies finite supports \(F,G\) such that
\[
F\notin I,\qquad G\notin I,\qquad FG\in I.
\]
Thus every one of those \(1721\) ideals is nonprime. All certificate factors have degree at most \(3\), and all certificate products have degree at most \(6\).

It remains to prove that the \(7\) variable-generated ideals are prime. Let
\[
I=(x_i:i\in S)
\]
with \(S\subsetneq\{x,y,z\}\). A polynomial belongs to \(I\) exactly when every one of its monomials is divisible by at least one variable in \(S\). If \(f\notin I\) and \(g\notin I\), choose monomials \(m\in\operatorname{Supp}(f)\) and \(n\in\operatorname{Supp}(g)\) avoiding every variable in \(S\). Then \(mn\) also avoids every variable in \(S\), so \(fg\notin I\). Hence \(I\) is prime.

## Verification
The file `verify.py` reconstructs the \(15\) linear supports, implements the proved support-membership criterion, enumerates all \(32768\) generating subsets, and recomputes the \(1748\) distinct closures. It then verifies all \(1721\) explicit non-primality witnesses in `witnesses.json`.

The replay output is:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{distinct linear-generated ideals}&=&1748,\\
\text{nonprime target closures}&=&1721,\\
\text{checked witnesses}&=&1721,\\
\text{maximum factor degree}&=&3,\\
\text{maximum product degree}&=&6.
\end{array}
\]
The replay ends with `CHECK_OK`.

The finite computation is exhaustive because every linear-generated ideal has a generating subset of the fixed \(15\)-element set, and the support criterion is an if-and-only-if membership test, not a degree-truncated search.

## Relationship to prior work
Bishop, Eppolito, Green, Jun, Norwood, and Thackrah classify the prime ideals of \(\mathbb B[x,y]\) that are generated by linear polynomials and then state a conjecture for \(\mathbb B[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\): if such an ideal does not contain all variables, it should be prime only when generated by a subset of the variables. The result above proves the next case \(n=3\).

Mincheva and Sakran classify prime ideals of the one-variable Boolean polynomial semiring and exhibit factorization phenomena that illustrate why primeness in this setting is subtler than ordinary monomial divisibility. Their classification does not imply the three-variable linear-generated statement.

Targeted searches for the three-variable statement, its “generated by a subset of variables” formulation, and the exact conjecture did not locate a prior proof. This search evidence supports but cannot logically certify novelty.

## Limitations
The result does not address \(n\ge4\). The witness certificate proves non-primality only for the \(1721\) three-variable closure classes under the stated hypothesis. No claim is made about ideals containing all three variables, arbitrary nonlinear generating sets, or prime congruences.

The originality comparison is limited by discoverability: an older theorem phrased in substantially different semiring language could conceivably subsume the finite classification, although no such theorem was found in the inspected sources or targeted searches.
