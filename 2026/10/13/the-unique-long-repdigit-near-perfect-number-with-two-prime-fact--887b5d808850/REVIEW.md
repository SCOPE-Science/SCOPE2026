# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Ren–Chen exhausts all two-prime near-perfect integers. The proof checks each of three parametric families by parity, coprime odd factors, the classical square-repunit classification, a uniform 2-adic bound, and exact binary-exponent contradictions. The exceptional 40 is analyzed separately; its arithmetic and base-three digits are explicit. The finite verifier supplies regression checks, not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources
The 2022 paper's repdigit discussion is restricted to a finite range of bases, and the directly relevant published 2026 result treats every even base but does not classify unbounded odd bases. The complete two-prime near-perfect classification supplies inputs, not the repdigit bound. Searches for equivalent all-base classifications produced no dominating source. A differently phrased, unindexed equivalent theorem remains a residual bibliographic risk; non-detection is not proof of uniqueness in worldwide literature.

## Value — PASS
The length-three-or-greater classification resolves a natural all-base question arising from repdigit near-perfect research, separates a genuinely exceptional base-three example, and uses structural constraints to settle arbitrarily large odd bases without finite cutoffs. It does not pretend to solve larger-prime-support cases.
