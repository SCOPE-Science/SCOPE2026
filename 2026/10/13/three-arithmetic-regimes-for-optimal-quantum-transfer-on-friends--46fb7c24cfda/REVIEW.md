# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The complete orthogonal spectral reduction yields the exact transition amplitude, checked against independently constructed adjacency matrices. For nonsquare discriminants, density of the two-frequency orbit identifies the global supremum with a compact two-torus maximum; upper and lower first-order expansions agree. For square discriminants, the exact base-gap identity forces every maximizing phase into a \(D^{-1}\)-scale neighborhood of the four known peaks, permitting a locally uniform one-dimensional limiting optimization. Strict convexity establishes unique defining constants. The limits are analytical; the finite verifier does not claim to certify an infinite statement.

## Originality: PASS with residual risk
The general spectral-arithmetic theory in *Irrational quantum walks* and the explicit spectral join formula and \(O(m^{-1})\) bounds in *Quantum walks on join graphs* are acknowledged. Their respective inspected treatments do not identify the three asymptotic coefficients of the global squared transfer deficit, and the difference requires singular-scale optimization together with a rational/irrational phase-space distinction. The 2023 Dutch-windmill thesis is a highly relevant possible overlap: only its abstract was available; its entire full text was not examined. The original claim is not an unconditional global novelty certificate.

## Value: PASS
A natural unweighted quantum network has asymptotically nearly perfect transport along a peripheral edge, but the optimal fidelity loss has a detectable arithmetic discontinuity at triangular counts of triangles. The distinction between unrestricted peak probability, coarse join perturbation bounds, and the two types of resonant finite-dimensional phase locking is mathematically motivated and quantitatively nontrivial. The problem is not an arbitrary small-instance computation or a parameter restatement.
