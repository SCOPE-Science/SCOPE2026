# Three couples are the first incompatibility between rank-maximality and minimum regret
## Finding
Consider the classical Stable Marriage problem with \(n\) men and \(n\) women, complete strict preference lists, and stability defined by the absence of a blocking man--woman pair.

For a stable matching \(M\), let
\[
p(M)=(p_1(M),\ldots,p_n(M)),
\]
where \(p_r(M)\) is the number of the \(2n\) participants whose partner is their \(r\)-th choice. A stable matching is **rank-maximal** when \(p(M)\) is lexicographically maximal over all stable matchings.

Let
\[
g(M)=\max_{a}\operatorname{rank}_a(M(a))
\]
be the regret of \(M\). A stable matching is **minimum-regret** when it minimizes \(g(M)\).

Write \(P(I)\) for the rank-maximal stable matchings of instance \(I\), and \(G(I)\) for its minimum-regret stable matchings.

For every instance with at most \(2\) agents on each side,
\[
P(I)=G(I).
\]

This is sharp. Among all
\[
6^6=46656
\]
labeled strict preference profiles with \(3\) men and \(3\) women, the exact optimizer-set relation is
\[
\begin{array}{c|r}
\text{relation} & \text{profiles}\\ \hline
P(I)=G(I) & 37716\\
P(I)\subsetneq G(I) & 6984\\
P(I)\cap G(I)=\varnothing & 1956
\end{array}
\]
and the relations
\[
G(I)\subsetneq P(I)
\]
and
\[
\varnothing\ne P(I)\cap G(I)\subsetneq P(I),G(I)
\]
never occur.

Hence the first incidence of outright incompatibility is
\[
\frac{1956}{46656}=\frac{163}{3888}.
\]

Under independent relabeling of men and women and optional exchange of the two sides, the \(1956\) incompatible profiles form exactly
\[
29
\]
isomorphism classes. Their orbit-size distribution is
\[
26\times72,\qquad 2\times36,\qquad 1\times12.
\]

Every incompatible profile has a unique minimum-regret stable matching. Of the \(1956\) incompatible profiles, \(1728\) have exactly two stable matchings and \(228\) have exactly three. The rank-maximal optimizer is unique in \(1872\) incompatible profiles and has size two in the remaining \(84\).

A concrete witness uses men \(m_0,m_1,m_2\) and women \(w_0,w_1,w_2\) with preferences
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
m_0&w_0\succ w_1\succ w_2\\
m_1&w_0\succ w_1\succ w_2\\
m_2&w_0\succ w_2\succ w_1
\end{array}
\]
and
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
w_0&m_0\succ m_1\succ m_2\\
w_1&m_2\succ m_1\succ m_0\\
w_2&m_1\succ m_2\succ m_0.
\end{array}
\]

It has exactly two stable matchings. The matching
\[
M_G=\{m_0w_0,m_1w_1,m_2w_2\}
\]
has profile
\[
p(M_G)=(2,4,0)
\]
and regret
\[
g(M_G)=2.
\]
The matching
\[
M_P=\{m_0w_0,m_1w_2,m_2w_1\}
\]
has profile
\[
p(M_P)=(4,0,2)
\]
and regret
\[
g(M_P)=3.
\]
Thus \(M_G\) is the unique minimum-regret stable matching and \(M_P\) is the unique rank-maximal stable matching.

## Assumptions and scope
Preferences are complete, strict, and two-sided. Every stable matching is perfect.

The profile is two-sided: ranks of both men and women contribute to \(p(M)\).

Lexicographic comparison starts at first choices, then second choices, and so on.

Regret is the worst individual partner rank, not the sum of ranks.

The theorem concerns optimizer-set compatibility, not merely whether the two criteria assign different objective values.

The exact census is complete only through \(3\times3\). No larger-market frequency or asymptotic statement is made.

## Proof
For \(1\times1\), there is one matching and the statement is immediate.

For \(2\times2\), the verifier exhausts all
\[
(2!)^{4}=16
\]
preference profiles. Fourteen have one stable matching and two have two stable matchings. In all sixteen profiles the rank-maximal and minimum-regret optimizer sets coincide.

For \(3\times3\), there are
\[
(3!)^{6}=46656
\]
labeled preference profiles. The verifier enumerates all six perfect matchings for every profile and retains exactly those with no blocking pair.

The stable-matching-count distribution is
\[
34080\text{ profiles with one stable matching},
\]
\[
11484\text{ profiles with two stable matchings},
\]
and
\[
1092\text{ profiles with three stable matchings}.
\]

For each stable matching, its two-sided rank profile and worst rank are computed exactly. Lexicographic maximization gives \(P(I)\), while minimization of the worst rank gives \(G(I)\). Direct set comparison yields
\[
37716,\quad 6984,\quad 1956
\]
for equality, proper containment \(P(I)\subsetneq G(I)\), and disjointness respectively, with zero profiles in the other two relation types.

For the isomorphism census, the natural symmetry group is generated by independent permutations of the three men and three women together with side exchange. It has order
\[
2(3!)^2=72.
\]
Canonicalization under all \(72\) transformations sends the \(1956\) incompatible profiles into \(29\) classes. Orbit counts are \(26\) of size \(72\), \(2\) of size \(36\), and \(1\) of size \(12\), whose total is
\[
26\cdot72+2\cdot36+12=1956.
\]

The displayed witness can be checked directly. Both listed matchings are stable and the remaining four perfect matchings are blocked. Their profiles and regrets are
\[
(2,4,0),\ 2
\]
and
\[
(4,0,2),\ 3,
\]
so the lexicographic and minimax objectives select different stable matchings.

## Relationship to prior work
The Stable Marriage literature has long treated several inequivalent notions of fairness.

Gusfield's 1987 work gives a polynomial-time algorithm for a minimum-regret stable marriage, explicitly interpreting regret as the welfare of the worst-off participant.

Irving, Leather, and Gusfield's 1987 minimum/maximum-weight framework can encode lexicographic profile objectives by exponentially separated weights. Later profile-based work makes the resulting two-sided rank-maximal criterion explicit: maximize the number of first-choice assignments, then subject to that maximize second choices, and so on.

These results establish that minimum regret and rank-maximality are standard but conceptually different stable-matching objectives. They do not determine whether their optimizer sets must intersect in small markets.

The checked profile-based full text discusses rank-maximality and minimum regret as distinct criteria, gives algorithms for each, and studies profile-based optimization. It does not state the sharp optimizer-compatibility boundary above, the \(1956\)-profile first incompatibility count, or the \(29\)-class quotient.

Targeted searches using rank-maximal, minimum-regret, optimizer intersection, disjointness, three-couple, and exact small-profile formulations did not locate an equivalent boundary theorem or census.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to complete strict Stable Marriage.

The rank-maximal profile is two-sided. One-sided applicant-only rank-maximality is a different objective.

The exact census stops at \(3\times3\).

The full 1987 weighted-optimization article was not accessible during the comparison; its bibliographic record and abstract were checked, while the later open full text explicitly attributes the rank-maximal exponential-weight construction to that paper.

A thesis appendix, software corpus, or unindexed small-instance table could contain the same finite boundary.
