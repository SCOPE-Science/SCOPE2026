# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The governing equations and threshold come from equations (1)–(4) and (19) of Mohamed (2026). All fourteen parameters are strictly positive, and the exact example lies in the source's feasible density bound. The first positive equilibrium is verified by substitution. A reversible scalar elimination yields the factored equilibrium equation with a strictly positive denominator; Descartes' rule and two rational endpoint evaluations give exactly one additional positive root. The full characteristic polynomials establish local rumor-free stability and an unstable direction at the rational positive equilibrium. Nondegeneracy and strict inequalities establish parameter robustness. The executable exact-rational checker corroborates the algebra but is not substituted for those analytic steps.

**Originality — PASS with stated residual risk.** The complete 2026 primary paper was inspected, including all ten pages, its formula for \(\mathcal R_0\), Theorem 3.3, and the numerical sections. It gives no corresponding subunit reproduction-number example or three-equilibrium census, and in fact asserts the opposite global conclusion. Cui, Mu, and Wan (2008) establish the *general* possibility of subthreshold multiple equilibria under saturated recovery in an earlier epidemic model; that phenomenon itself is not claimed as original. The present result is for the specifically published four-compartment dual-saturation ODE, includes strictly positive residual transmission by the debunked class, and supplies an exact parameter point plus a complete root count. The older article's full text was unavailable; an undiscovered stronger abstract framework remains a residual overlap risk.

**Value — PASS.** Global elimination under a reproductive threshold is a core practical interpretation of the model. A complete, open-parameter subthreshold coexistence construction gives a mathematically substantive boundary to that interpretation, with all compartments present and rates positive. It sharpens rather than merely rephrases the standard observation that saturated treatment *may* produce backward effects. Two positive equilibria alone are not called two attractors.

**Closest literature and limits.** Mohamed (2026), DOI `10.65904/3083-1733.2026.2.2`, is the directly compared source. Cui, Mu, and Wan (2008), DOI `10.1016/j.jtbi.2008.05.015`, report saturated-recovery multiple equilibria for a different model. van den Driessche and Watmough (2002), DOI `10.1016/S0025-5564(02)00108-6`, give the general next-generation threshold framework. No global classification beyond the exact equilibrium census, no stable endemic equilibrium, and no empirical rumor prediction are asserted.
