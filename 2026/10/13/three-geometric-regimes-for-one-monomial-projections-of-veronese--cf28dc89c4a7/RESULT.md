# Three geometric regimes for one-monomial projections of Veronese surfaces

## Finding
For every integer \(d\geq3\), omit exactly one degree-\(d\) monomial \(M=x^a y^b z^c\) from the complete degree-\(d\) Veronese coordinate system of \(\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\), and write \(X_{d,M}\subset\mathbb P^{\binom{d+2}{2}-2}\) for the reduced projective image. Precisely three, mutually exclusive, geometric regimes occur:

1. **Pure omitted monomial.** If \((a,b,c)\) is a permutation of \((d,0,0)\), the rational map has exactly one base point. Its image is a smooth embedded blowup \(\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2\simeq\mathbb F_1\), polarized by \(dH-E\). Its degree and Hilbert polynomial are
   \[\deg X_{d,M}=d^2-1,\qquad \chi(X_{d,M},\mathcal O_{X_{d,M}}(t))=\frac{(d^2-1)t^2+(3d-1)t}{2}+1.\]
2. **Tangent omitted monomial.** If \((a,b,c)\) is a permutation of \((d-1,1,0)\), the map is a finite birational normalization \(\nu:\mathbb P^2\to X_{d,M}\). The image is smooth except for one isolated nonnormal point \(p\), where
   \[\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_{d,M},p}\cong\mathbb C[[v,u^2,uv,u^3]]\subset\mathbb C[[u,v]].\]
   The normalization quotient has length one, the conductor is \((v,u^2)\mathbb C[[u,v]]\), and the local ring has dimension two, embedding dimension four, depth one and Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity two. For every integer \(q\geq0\),
   \[\ell\!\left(\mathcal O_{X_{d,M},p}/\mathfrak m_p^{q+1}\right)=2\binom{q+2}{2}-1.\]
   Its degree is \(d^2\) and its Hilbert polynomial is \(\binom{dt+2}{2}-1\).
3. **All remaining omitted monomials.** The morphism \(\mathbb P^2\to X_{d,M}\) is an isomorphism. Thus \(X_{d,M}\) is a smooth embedded projective plane with polarization \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(d)\), degree \(d^2\), and Hilbert polynomial \(\binom{dt+2}{2}\).

For each fixed \(d\), these cases occur for respectively \(3\), \(6\), and \(\binom{d+2}{2}-9\) omitted monomials. In particular, removing a single coordinate can produce a smooth blowup, an isolated non-Cohen–Macaulay singularity, or a smooth model of \(\mathbb P^2\), depending only on the exponent pattern. Here the Hilbert polynomial refers to the intrinsic projective Hilbert polynomial \(\chi(\mathcal O_X(t))\), not necessarily the Hilbert function of an unnormalized homogeneous coordinate ring.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes and rings are over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(V_d=H^0(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(d))\) with its monomial basis. The projection deletes one basis coordinate, not a general linear combination. Define \(X_{d,M}\) as the reduced closure of the image of the resulting rational map, so the pure-power case is permitted to have a base point. By a vertex chart one means the principal open at a surviving coordinate \(x^d\), \(y^d\) or \(z^d\); by a completed local ring one means completion at its maximal ideal. Smoothness is a statement about the projective variety, not about projective normality of its homogeneous coordinate ring. No assertion is made for \(d=1,2\) or arbitrary nonmonomial projection centers.

## Proof
Write \(N=\binom{d+2}{2}\). A variable permutation reduces the omission to one of three patterns. In each case below, coordinate ratios on the indicated opens give *equalities of rings*, not just set-theoretic descriptions.

**The morphism cases.** Suppose none of the three pure powers is omitted. Then their three nonvanishing loci cover both source and image. On the chart where \(x^d\ne0\), put \(u=y/x\) and \(v=z/x\). The target affine coordinate algebra is the monomial subring of \(\mathbb C[u,v]\) generated by all \(u^iv^j\) with \(0\leq i+j\leq d\), except the ratio corresponding to \(M\). Its normalization is contained in \(\mathbb C[u,v]\), which is the source-chart ring. If the omitted exponent of \(x\) is not \(d-1\), then both \(u\) and \(v\) survive as generators; hence the ring equals \(\mathbb C[u,v]\). The same argument applies on the other two coordinate charts. Consequently, if the omission is not a permutation of \((d-1,1,0)\), all three affine rings equal the source-chart rings, and the map is an isomorphism globally.

Now let \(M=x^{d-1}y\). Only the \(x^d\)-chart loses a degree-one generator, \(u=y/x\); the \(x^{d-1}z\)-coordinate supplies \(v=z/x\). Among the surviving ratios are \(u^2,uv,u^3\). Every other monomial \(u^iv^j\) of total degree at most \(d\), except \(u\), is generated by those four elements: with \(j\ge1\) use \(v\) or \(uv\) and \(u^2,u^3\), and with \(j=0\), every exponent \(i\ge2\) is a nonnegative combination of 2 and 3. Therefore the coordinate algebra is exactly
\[R=\mathbb C[v,u^2,uv,u^3]=\mathbb C+(v,u^2)\mathbb C[u,v]\subset B=\mathbb C[u,v].\]
On the other two coordinate charts \(R=B\). Since \(u\) is integral over \(R\) via the monic equation \(T^2-u^2=0\), \(B\) is a finite integral extension of \(R\); both share the fraction field because \(u=(uv)/v\). The map is therefore finite birational, and the source \(\mathbb P^2\), being normal, is the normalization. The two rings differ by the one-dimensional vector space spanned by the residue class of \(u\), supported at the origin \((u,v)=(0,0)\). This proves uniqueness of the nonnormal point, and establishes the completion and conductor formulas (the conductor is \((v,u^2)B\), not \((u,v)B\)). The embedding dimension is four because the four monomials \(v,u^2,uv,u^3\) are linearly independent modulo the square of the maximal ideal.

For the exact Hilbert–Samuel function let \(\mathfrak m=(v,u^2)B\subset R\). For every \(n\ge1\), one has \(\mathfrak m^n=(v,u^2)^nB\) as an ideal of \(R\), since it is generated by products of \(v,u^2,uv,u^3\) and absorbs all monomials in the stated ideal. The inclusion \(R\subset B\) gives a length-one quotient \(B/R\); moreover \(\mathfrak m^n\subset R\). Since \(v,u^2\) is a regular sequence in the regular local ring \(B_{(u,v)}\),
\[\ell(R/\mathfrak m^n)=\ell(B/(v,u^2)^n)-\ell(B/R)=2\binom{n+1}{2}-1.\]
This gives multiplicity two. The finite extension exact sequence \(0\to R\to B\to\mathbb C\to0\), localized at the origin, together with the depth lemma yields \(\operatorname{depth}R=1\). Thus the point is non-Cohen–Macaulay and fails Serre's \(S_2\) condition. Because \(\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}/\mathcal O_X\) is a single length-one skyscraper sheaf and \(\nu^*\mathcal O_X(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(d)\), the projection formula and Euler characteristics imply \(\chi(\mathcal O_X(t))=\binom{dt+2}{2}-1\). Finite birationality also gives degree \(d^2\).

**The pure-power case.** Let \(M=x^d\). The remaining coordinates vanish simultaneously only at \(p=[1:0:0]\). They form the complete linear system of degree-\(d\) plane curves through \(p\), which becomes base-point-free on \(Y=\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2\), with divisor class \(L=dH-E\). Near the exceptional divisor use \(u=y/x\), \(v=z/x\). In the blowup chart with exceptional direction \(u\), put \(w=v/u\). Dividing all permitted monomial coordinates by \(x^{d-1}y\), one obtains a polynomial chart in \(u,w\): the ratios \(x^{d-2}y^2/(x^{d-1}y)=u\) and \(x^{d-1}z/(x^{d-1}y)=w\) generate its ring, and every other ratio is a nonnegative monomial in these. The other exceptional chart is symmetric. Away from \(p\), the two surviving pure-power charts \(y^d\ne0\) and \(z^d\ne0\) are ordinary full affine-plane charts. These four isomorphisms of affine coordinate rings glue to an isomorphism of \(Y\) with the projective image. Finally, \(H^2=1\), \(E^2=-1\), \(H\cdot E=0\) and \(K_Y=-3H+E\), so \(L^2=d^2-1\) and \(-K_Y\cdot L=3d-1\). Riemann–Roch on the smooth rational surface gives \(\chi(\mathcal O_Y(tL))=\tfrac12((d^2-1)t^2+(3d-1)t)+1\).

The exhaustive count follows from the distinct permutations of the two exceptional exponent patterns, leaving \(N-9\) others.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` implements an independent, exact integer-semigroup reconstruction of affine coordinate chart generators for every degree from \(3\) through \(16\), including exceptional blowup charts. It checks all \(959\) deleted monomials and \(2919\) affine charts. It separately tests the tangent-case conductor ideal powers and the Hilbert–Samuel lengths for \(1\le n\le10\) with bounded exponents. It also compares \(60\) exact graded-semigroup Hilbert values with the three projective Hilbert polynomials for degrees \(3\) through \(7\). The output in `verify_output.txt` is reproducible using Python 3 and the standard library. These tests are finite supporting evidence; the preceding algebraic chart and length arguments prove the statements for every \(d\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
The projection and secant viewpoint is classical. Muñoz, Sierra and Solá Conde analyze smooth projections of quadratic Veronese models and secant-defective varieties. Caviglia and Conca classify the Koszul property of projections of the **cubic** Veronese surface; their Theorem 4.1 explicitly covers pure-power and tangent projection centers in degree three but concerns minimal equations/Koszulness, not the claimed all-degree projective local rings, conductor and pointwise Hilbert–Samuel polynomials. Colarte-Gómez, Miró-Roig and Nicklasson study monomial projections, arithmetic Cohen–Macaulayness, quadratic generation and related ring-theoretic questions; their full text does not supply this three-regime scheme-theoretic projective classification. Schenzel's 1978 paper is a relevant historical antecedent on Cohen–Macaulay/Buchsbaum properties at the vertex of the affine cone; its full text was not obtained, so complete prior coverage cannot be excluded. The results here do not claim that projective smoothness or the existence of these projection regimes was unknown, only the joined explicit all-degree local-geometric/invariant description under the stated monomial-omission hypothesis.

## Limitations
The conclusion is specific to a single deleted degree-\(d\) **monomial coordinate** and to complex projective surfaces, for \(d\ge3\). It does not classify arbitrary linear projections, multiple omissions or affine-cone singularities at their vertices. In the third regime a smooth projective variety can still have a nonnormal homogeneous coordinate ring (as in the pinched cubic Veronese); the local regularity of \(X_{d,M}\) must not be conflated with projective normality. The historical comparison with Schenzel's 1978 original remains incomplete, and an equivalent formulation in earlier work would eliminate an originality claim. No independent external mathematical audit has been undertaken.
