# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is established by a complete affine-atlas computation for every degree \(d\ge3\). When all pure powers survive, the three standard affine opens cover the projective variety. Their coordinate rings are either full polynomial rings or, in the tangent case, exactly \(\mathbb C[v,u^2,uv,u^3]\); this establishes the scheme-theoretic map and normality comparison. Deleting a pure power has a unique base point, and both standard exceptional charts of its blowup have polynomial coordinate rings generated by ratios of permitted degree-\(d\) monomials. The proof identifies the finite length-one normalization quotient, the conductor and every maximal-ideal power, and applies the regular-sequence length formula and Riemann–Roch with the stated quantifiers. The standalone exact checker passed all \(959\) omissions and \(2919\) affine charts for \(3\le d\le16\) tested the local filtration through the tenth power, and checked \(60\) graded Hilbert values. Finite checks are not the infinite proof; the coordinate identities provide that proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Prior work already studies one-coordinate and general Veronese projections; none of that premise is asserted new. The closest full text, Caviglia–Conca (arXiv:1203.1949, Theorem 4.1), classifies Koszul/nonquadratic projected cubic coordinate rings, including the pure-power and tangent-center strata. It does not state the arbitrary-degree three-way **projective local** classification, completed tangent local ring, conductor, depth, full Hilbert–Samuel function and projective Hilbert-polynomial transition. Colarte-Gómez–Miró-Roig–Nicklasson (arXiv:2303.09582) considers monomial projections in depth, but primarily addresses arithmetical Cohen–Macaulayness, quadratic/Koszul properties and the combinatorics of generators, not this local geometric result. Muñoz–Sierra–Solá Conde (arXiv:0707.1429) treats smooth projections and secant defective varieties, in particular degree-two Veronese models, not the present degree-at-least-three singular local rings. The historical Schenzel (1978) work and Gröbner (1965) are plausible stronger antecedents on arithmetic projection properties; their complete originals were not inspected. Exact equivalence or stronger historical specialization remains possible, and is a genuine residual risk. A targeted review of accessible mathematical literature identified no implication-equivalent statement.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete natural trichotomy for the simplest possible monomial perturbation of a complete projective linear system (deleting one basis section), not an arbitrary parameter slice. It distinguishes three **different projective geometries** and determines the entire nonnormal local algebra in the delicate case, including its depth defect and every Hilbert–Samuel length. It separates intrinsic projective smoothness from the possible failure of homogeneous-coordinate-ring normality, a practical distinction in questions about projected Veronese varieties. The count of exceptional omitted monomials is secondary to the geometric and local invariant classification; no downstream theorem or independent validation is claimed.

## Limits and closest literature
The all-degree local-ring presentation requires only \(d\ge3\), but does not apply to general projection centers, several deleted monomials, or affine-cone vertices. A comparison of the unavailable full text of Schenzel (1978) with the exact local statement remains desirable; its absence does not establish uniqueness. Full published coverage of the all-degree local invariant formulas would defeat originality even without matching terminology.
