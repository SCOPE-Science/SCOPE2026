# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The total Roman definition was reconstructed from the primary source. The proof derives the crown minimum value, classifies all minimum functions, converts edge deletions into a loopless directed graph, proves the sharp lower bound by forced arcs, and supplies an isolate-free witness of matching size. The executable checker independently confirms the first two nontrivial orders and the algebraic counting inequality.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating same-invariant source was inspected at full-text level and covers undeleted complete bipartite graphs but not crowns or matching-deleted bicliques. Searches using crown, \(K_{n,n}-M_n\), one-factor-complement, exact-value, and matching-deletion formulations found no covering statement. The closest crown edge-deletion theorem concerns ordinary total bondage and yields no implication of the exact total Roman value.

## Value
PASS. The result measures how a canonical perfect-matching deletion changes a newly introduced robustness invariant, from the published undeleted value \(n\) to \(n-1\), and the proof supplies a complete optimizer structure rather than only a scalar evaluation.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is F. Khosh-Ahang Ghasr and S. Nazari-Moghaddam, “Total Roman bondage number of a graph,” arXiv:2602.08758v1. Its Corollary 9 gives the complete-bipartite value and its exact-family list does not include crown graphs. The theorem here is limited to \(n\ge4\) crowns and does not classify arbitrary \(K_{r,s}-M_t\). Database noncoverage cannot prove absolute novelty, so an uncommon-alias indexing risk remains.
