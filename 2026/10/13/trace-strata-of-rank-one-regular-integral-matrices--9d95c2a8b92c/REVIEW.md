# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof first converts regularity to content \(1\) using the 2026 theorem, then uses Smith normal form to obtain a primitive rank-one factorization and a unimodular basis giving \(B(t,b)\). For \(t\ne0\), any integral conjugator must preserve the unique nonzero-eigenvalue line, forcing the displayed upper-triangular form and the exact congruence invariant \(b\bmod |t|\) up to sign. The converse conjugator is explicit. The trace-zero case is handled separately. The fine-locus step uses the exact 2016 criterion and similarity invariance. No bounded computation is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with a stated residual risk. The 2026 paper gives the regularity criterion and one non-fine witness, not a trace-stratified classification. The 2016 paper gives fineness only for the row normal form. Searches for rank-one integral similarity, regular-fine matrices, and trace-residue criteria located general integral-similarity literature, including the 2006 second-order paper and the 2019 algorithmic paper, but no source stating the coupled result that the regular rank-one trace stratum is \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) with exactly one fine orbit. The 2006 full text was not available in the inspected source, so possible prior coverage of the bare similarity normal form remains a residual risk; it does not by itself cover the later fine-locus coupling.

## Value
PASS. The 2026 paper establishes failure of the regular-fine property of \(M_2(\mathbb Z)\) using one example. The finding identifies the entire rank-one obstruction geometrically and arithmetically: it gives exact trace-stratum class counts, singles out the unique fine orbit, identifies the sharp first obstruction at trace magnitude \(5\), and shows that along prime traces the proportion of fine classes tends to \(0\). This is a natural complete classification of the rank-one regular locus relevant to the motivating property, not an arbitrary parameter slice.
