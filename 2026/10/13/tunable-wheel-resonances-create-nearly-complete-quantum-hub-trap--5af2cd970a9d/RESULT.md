# Tunable wheel resonances create nearly complete quantum hub trapping

## Finding
Consider a one-excitation continuous-time quantum walk on a wheel with a cycle of \(n\ge3\) rim sites and one central hub. The hopping between consecutive rim sites is \(1\), and each hub--rim hopping is the same real \(g>0\). A fixed rim site \(0\) is an irreversible absorber with an arbitrary rate \(\gamma_0>0\). Let \(\mathcal B_g\) denote the eventually absorbed (bright) subspace and let \(S_h(g)\) be the eventual probability of survival for a walker initially at the hub.

Define, for integers \(1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\),
\[
\lambda_k=2\cos(2\pi k/n),\qquad
\mathcal K_n=\{k:\lfloor n/4\rfloor<k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad
g_k=\sqrt{\lambda_k(\lambda_k-2)/n}\quad(k\in\mathcal K_n).
\]
All \(g_k\) are positive and pairwise different. Writing \(\mathcal G_n=\{g_k:k\in\mathcal K_n\}\), the exact rim-absorber bright rank is
\[
\dim\mathcal B_g=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2-\mathbf1_{\{g\in\mathcal G_n\}}.
\]
In particular, there are precisely \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor n/4\rfloor\) exceptional couplings. With an additional arbitrary positive-rate absorber at the hub, the bright rank is \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\) for **every** \(g>0\). The corresponding eventual capture probabilities for the maximally mixed initial state are these ranks divided by \(n+1\).

Let \(d_k=1\) if \(n\) is even and \(k=n/2\), and \(d_k=2\) otherwise. The survival probability from the hub with only the rim absorber is exactly
\[
S_h(g)=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{d_k\lambda_k(\lambda_k-2)}{d_k\lambda_k(\lambda_k-2)+(d_k+1)\lambda_k^2},&g=g_k\text{ for some }k\in\mathcal K_n,\\[5pt]
0,&g\notin\mathcal G_n.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently an arbitrarily small detuning from a resonant \(g_k\) changes **eventual** hub survival discontinuously from a positive value to zero. For \(n\ge5\), taking \(k_n=\lfloor n/4\rfloor+1\) gives an exceptional \(g_{k_n}=O(n^{-1})\) with
\[
S_h(g_{k_n})\ge1-3\pi/n,
\]
so the hub excitation becomes asymptotically **almost entirely protected** against absorption at a directly connected rim vertex. This concerns infinite-time capture, not finite-time continuity.

## Assumptions and scope
The Hilbert space has orthonormal basis \(\{|h\rangle,|0\rangle,\ldots,|n-1\rangle\}\), and indices on rim sites are modulo \(n\). The selfadjoint tight-binding Hamiltonian is
\[
H=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(|j\rangle\langle j+1|+|j+1\rangle\langle j|)
+g\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(|h\rangle\langle j|+|j\rangle\langle h|).
\]
For an absorbing set \(A\subseteq\{0,h\}\), its unnormalized no-jump evolution is generated by \(K=H-i\sum_{a\in A}\gamma_a|a\rangle\langle a|\), with every \(\gamma_a>0\). For initial density \(\rho\), eventual survival is \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{tr}(e^{-iKt}\rho e^{iK^*t})\). Its complement is eventual capture. Every limit exists, and the formulas are independent of the positive rates. We use the adjacency, rather than graph-Laplacian, Hamiltonian, and assume no onsite potentials, flux, other dissipation, or coupling disorder.

## Proof
**1. Absorption and dark eigenvectors.** For any finite-dimensional Hermitian \(H\), the subspace \(\mathcal D\) spanned by all \(H\)-eigenvectors orthogonal to every absorber is reducing for \(K\), and evolution on it is unitary. The imaginary part of \(\langle v,Kv\rangle\) is \(-\sum_{a\in A}\gamma_a|\langle a,v\rangle|^2\). If an eigenvalue of \(K\) is real, its eigenvectors therefore lie in \(\mathcal D\). The restriction to \(\mathcal D^\perp\) has every eigenvalue strictly in the open lower half-plane; its evolution decays exponentially in finite dimension (possibly with polynomial Jordan factors). Therefore eventual survival for any \(\rho\) is \(\mathrm{tr}(P_\mathcal D\rho)\), and the bright dimension is the sum, over distinct \(H\)-eigenvalues, of the ranks of the absorber evaluation maps on their eigenspaces. This also covers degeneracies; simply counting eigenvectors with nonzero amplitude in an arbitrarily chosen degenerate basis would be invalid.

**2. Spectrum and all possible collisions.** The rim Fourier modes with nonzero wave number have eigenvalues \(\lambda_k=2\cos(2\pi k/n)\) for \(1\le k<n\). They are unaffected by the hub, because their rim amplitudes sum to zero. In the orthonormal two-dimensional space spanned by \(|h\rangle\) and \(|s\rangle=n^{-1/2}\sum_j|j\rangle\), the Hamiltonian is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&g\sqrt n\\g\sqrt n&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \lambda_\pm=1\pm\sqrt{1+ng^2}.
\]
As \(g>0\), \(\lambda_+>2\) cannot meet any rim eigenvalue, while \(\lambda_-<0\) can meet precisely the negative \(\lambda_k\). The equation \(\lambda_-=\lambda_k\) is equivalent to \(g=g_k\), with \(k\in\mathcal K_n\). The numbers \(\lambda_k\) decrease strictly as \(k\) increases over \(1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\); the function \(x(x-2)\) decreases strictly on \(x<0\). Thus the exceptional positive couplings are all different. No two different rim-frequency levels collide.

**3. One rim absorber and two absorbers.** For each \(1\le k<n/2\), the twofold rim eigenspace consists of a cosine standing wave nonzero at \(0\), and a sine standing wave zero there. If \(n\) is even, the alternating \(k=n/2\) rim mode is one-dimensional and nonzero at \(0\). Therefore each distinct nonuniform rim eigenvalue contributes exactly one bright dimension for a single rim sink. There are \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) such values, plus two hub--uniform levels \(\lambda_\pm\), each having nonzero amplitude at rim site \(0\). In the absence of collisions the rank is \(R_n=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\). At a unique collision of \(\lambda_-\) with a rim level, the single rim absorber evaluates the merged eigenspace by one functional rather than two, lowering the rank by one. With the hub also absorbing, the hub and rim evaluations are independent on the merged eigenspace: the former sees the hybrid eigenvector but vanishes on rim Fourier modes, while the latter sees a rim standing wave. Hence the lost rank is fully restored; at noncolliding levels the hub adds no additional rank. This proves both bright-rank formulas.

**4. Hub survival at a collision.** Put \(\lambda=\lambda_k<0\) and \(M=ng^2+\lambda^2\) at \(g=g_k\). A normalized hybrid eigenvector at \(\lambda\) is
\[
|u\rangle=(g\sqrt n|h\rangle+\lambda|s\rangle)/\sqrt M.
\]
In the corresponding rim eigenspace choose a normalized cosine (or alternating) vector \(|v\rangle\) with \(|\langle0|v\rangle|^2=d_k/n\) and \(\langle h|v\rangle=0\). The projection of the absorber \(|0\rangle\) onto \(\operatorname{span}\{|u\rangle,|v\rangle\}\) has squared coordinates \(\lambda^2/(nM)\) and \(d_k/n\), respectively. Its orthogonal complement in that plane is dark. The hub's projection onto \(|u\rangle\) has squared norm \(ng^2/M\); it has no projection onto any other dark eigenvector. The resulting dark probability is
\[
\frac{ng^2}{M}\frac{d_k/n}{\lambda^2/(nM)+d_k/n}
=\frac{d_kng^2}{d_kng^2+(d_k+1)\lambda^2}.
\]
The resonance identity \(ng^2=\lambda(\lambda-2)\) yields the stated formula. Off resonance, both hybrid eigenvectors occupy distinct energies and are separately seen by the rim absorber; the hub lies entirely in the bright subspace, giving survival zero.

**5. High-protection sequence.** For \(n\ge5\), \(k_n=\lfloor n/4\rfloor+1<n/2\), so \(d_{k_n}=2\). Write \(\varepsilon=-\lambda_{k_n}>0\). Since \(0<k_n-n/4\le1\),
\[
0<\varepsilon=2\sin(2\pi(k_n-n/4)/n)\le4\pi/n.
\]
The hub survival at this resonance is \((4+2\varepsilon)/(4+5\varepsilon)\), giving \(1-S_h=3\varepsilon/(4+5\varepsilon)\le3\pi/n\). Also \(g_{k_n}^2=\varepsilon(\varepsilon+2)/n=O(n^{-2})\), as claimed.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` independently diagonalizes the full real symmetric \((n+1)\times(n+1)\) Hamiltonian for wheel sizes \(3\le n\le30\), builds the absorber-visible projector by singular-value decompositions within numerical eigenspaces, and checks both sink placements, every exceptional coupling, generic couplings, and the hub-survival formula. The checks use spectral tolerance and numerical arithmetic; the unbounded all-\(n\) theorem follows from the preceding proof, not these tests. Reproduce using `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt` and `python verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Solenov and Fedichkin (first public arXiv version June 13, 2005) analyze continuous-time transport on the unaugmented cycle, with monitoring-induced decoherence; their Hamiltonian has no spoke coupling or central vertex. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (arXiv:2011.13794, first public November 27, 2020) establish the general spectral/Krylov framework for absorption on several graph families. Their treatment principally uses graph-Laplacian hopping and does not classify adjacency-weighted wheels or the exceptional hub-initial-state protection. The standard unweighted wheel adjacency spectrum also supplies the Fourier-block starting point, not the parameter-dependent absorber classification. The evaluated new statement here is the complete positive-coupling resonance set, exact rank jumps, exact hub-initial-state survival and asymptotically near-total trapping, with rank restoration by a second sink. The general fact that absorption depends on spectral support is not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The effect concerns the infinite-time limit; capture at any fixed finite time varies continuously with \(g\), and near a resonance the decay can be very slow. The exceptional couplings form a finite discrete set for each \(n\) and require exact tuning. The theorem does not treat arbitrary rim disorder, magnetic flux, diagonal onsite energies, photon interactions, multiple excitations, or non-Markovian sinks. A broader or unlocated published calculation may imply the special-purpose formula; novelty is not independently certified.
