# Tutte-polynomial formula for commuting probabilities of radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding

Let \\(Q\\) be a finite loopless quiver with vertex set \\(V\\), arrow multiset \\(E\\), and let \\(A_Q=\\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\\), where \\(J\\) is the arrow ideal. Let \\(G_Q\\) be the underlying undirected multigraph, and for \\(F\\subseteq E\\) let \\(k(F)\\) be the number of connected components of the spanning multigraph \\((V,F)\\), including isolated vertices. Then the number of ordered commuting pairs in \\(A_Q\\) is
\\[
C_Q(q)=q^{|E|}\\sum_{F\\subseteq E}(q-1)^{|F|}q^{2k(F)}.
\\]
Equivalently,
\\[
\\operatorname{Pr}(A_Q)=q^{-2|V|-|E|}\\sum_{F\\subseteq E}(q-1)^{|F|}q^{2k(F)}.
\\]
If \\(c=k(E)\\), this is the Tutte evaluation
\\[
\\operatorname{Pr}(A_Q)=q^{-2|V|-|E|}q^{2c}(q-1)^{|V|-c}T_{G_Q}\\!\\left(1+\\frac{q^2}{q-1},q\\right).
\\]
Hence the commuting probability is independent of the orientation of the quiver and depends only on its underlying multigraph. For the \\(r\\)-Kronecker quiver this specializes to \\(q^{-2}+(q^2-1)q^{-r-2}\\).

Thus the commuting-pair count of a radical-square-zero quiver algebra is a random-cluster/Potts partition function of the underlying multigraph, evaluated at \\(Q=q^2\\) spin states and edge weight \\(q-1\\). In particular, reversing any subset of arrows leaves the commuting probability unchanged.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \\(Q=(V,E)\\) be a finite quiver without loops; parallel arrows are allowed. Let \\(J\\) denote the ideal generated by the arrows and set
\\[
A_Q=\\mathbb F_qQ/J^2.
\\]
Thus all products of two arrows vanish. The underlying multigraph \\(G_Q\\) has the same vertices and one undirected edge for every arrow of \\(Q\\), retaining multiplicity and forgetting orientation.

For a finite ring \\(R\\),
\\[
\\operatorname{{Pr}}(R)=\\frac{{|\\{{(x,y)\\in R^2:xy=yx\\}}|}}{{|R|^2}}.
\\]
For a spanning edge submultiset \\(F\\subseteq E\\), \\(k(F)\\) counts connected components of \\((V,F)\\), including isolated vertices. The Tutte polynomial uses the standard multigraph convention
\\[
T_G(X,Y)=\\sum_{{F\\subseteq E}}(X-1)^{{k(F)-k(E)}}(Y-1)^{{|F|-|V|+k(F)}}.
\\]

## Proof

Write each element uniquely as
\\[
x=\\sum_{{v\\in V}}d_v e_v+\\sum_{{a\\in E}}\\xi_a a,
\\qquad
y=\\sum_{{v\\in V}}s_v e_v+\\sum_{{a\\in E}}\\eta_a a.
\\]
For an arrow \\(a:u\\to v\\), the relation \\(J^2=0\\) gives
\\[
[x,y]_a=(d_u-d_v)\\eta_a-(s_u-s_v)\\xi_a.
\\]
There is no diagonal part in the commutator. Fix the two diagonal vectors \\((d_v)_v\\) and \\((s_v)_v\\), and assign to each vertex the color
\\[
c(v)=(d_v,s_v)\\in\\mathbb F_q^2.
\\]
For one arrow \\(a:u\\to v\\), the commuting equation is one homogeneous linear equation in \\((\\xi_a,\\eta_a)\\). If \\(c(u)\\ne c(v)\\), at least one coefficient is nonzero and there are exactly \\(q\\) solutions. If \\(c(u)=c(v)\\), the equation vanishes identically and there are \\(q^2\\) solutions. Therefore, if \\(m(c)\\) is the number of arrows whose endpoints receive the same color, the radical coordinates contribute
\\[
q^{{|E|+m(c)}}.
\\]
Summing over all \\(q^{2|V|}\\) colorings gives
\\[
C_Q(q)=q^{{|E|}}\\sum_{{c:V\\to\\mathbb F_q^2}}q^{{m(c)}}.
\\]
Now expand each monochromatic-edge weight as
\\[
q^{{m(c)}}=\\prod_{{a\\in E}}\\left(1+(q-1)\\mathbf 1_{{c(u_a)=c(v_a)}}\\right).
\\]
After expanding the product and interchanging the two finite sums,
\\[
\\sum_c q^{{m(c)}}
=\\sum_{{F\\subseteq E}}(q-1)^{{|F|}}N(F),
\\]
where \\(N(F)\\) is the number of \\(\\mathbb F_q^2\\)-colorings constant on every edge of \\(F\\). Such a coloring is exactly a free choice of one color on each connected component of \\((V,F)\\), so
\\[
N(F)=q^{{2k(F)}}.
\\]
This proves
\\[
C_Q(q)=q^{{|E|}}\\sum_{{F\\subseteq E}}(q-1)^{{|F|}}q^{{2k(F)}}.
\\]
Since \\(|A_Q|=q^{|V|+|E|}\\), division by \\(|A_Q|^2\\) gives the probability formula.

Finally, the multivariate sum is the Fortuin--Kasteleyn random-cluster partition function \\(Z_G(q^2,q-1)\\). The standard identity
\\[
Z_G(Q,v)=Q^{{k(E)}}v^{{|V|-k(E)}}T_G\\left(1+\\frac Qv,1+v\\right)
\\]
yields the displayed Tutte evaluation. Because the right-hand side uses only \\(G_Q\\), orientation is invisible.

For two vertices joined by \\(r\\) parallel arrows, \\(k(F)=2\\) for \\(F=\\varnothing\\) and \\(k(F)=1\\) otherwise, so
\\[
\\sum_{{F\\subseteq E}}(q-1)^{{|F|}}q^{{2k(F)}}=q^4+q^2(q^r-1),
\\]
and hence
\\[
\\operatorname{{Pr}}(A_Q)=q^{-2}+(q^2-1)q^{-r-2}.
\\]

## Verification

The included `verify.py` reconstructs multiplication in \\(\\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\\) directly and exhaustively counts commuting pairs for six small quivers over \\(\\mathbb F_2\\) and \\(\\mathbb F_3\\). The cases include a single arrow, two parallel arrows, an oriented length-two path, a triangle orientation, and a directed three-cycle. The same program independently evaluates the spanning-subgraph formula and asserts equality in every case. It also checks the Kronecker specialization symbolically for \\(1\\le r\\le6\\) and \\(q\\in\\{{2,3,5\\}}\\).

Finite enumeration is a consistency check only; the theorem is proved by the exact color-expansion argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen's 2011 paper studies artin algebras with radical square zero and makes their valued quiver a central structural invariant; its arXiv first version is dated 20 April 2011 and lists MSC 16E50. Nasr-Isfahani's 2016 paper explicitly uses the standard quiver presentation \\(kQ/J^2\\) for finite-dimensional radical-square-zero algebras. Neither paper studies commuting probabilities or Tutte/Potts evaluations.

A published result on generalized Kronecker path algebras gives
\\[
\\operatorname{{Pr}}(A_{{r,q}})=q^{{-2}}+(q^2-1)q^{{-r-2}}
\\]
for the two-vertex, \\(r\\)-parallel-arrow family, together with finer centralizer and commutator-fiber information. The theorem here strictly broadens that probability calculation to every finite loopless quiver and identifies the controlling invariant as the full random-cluster/Tutte polynomial of the underlying multigraph. The earlier Kronecker formula is recovered as the two-vertex parallel-edge specialization; its finer centralizer classification is not claimed here.

Targeted searches for radical-square-zero quiver algebras together with commuting probability, Potts/random-cluster partition functions, Tutte polynomials, and orientation-invariant commutator counts did not locate this general formula. The closest general commuting-probability literature treats finite rings through centralizers, subrings/quotients, or bilinear maps rather than through a quiver-to-Tutte identity.

## Limitations

The theorem is stated for split basic radical-square-zero algebras of the explicit form \\(\\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\\). It does not cover arbitrary nonsplit finite-dimensional algebras with radical square zero, where division-algebra vertex factors and bimodule-valued arrows replace scalar vertex colors.

The count determines only the zero commutator fiber. Prescribed nonzero commutator fibers depend on the support and linear relations of the target radical element and are not claimed to be Tutte evaluations in this form.

A mathematically equivalent formulation may exist in the literature under bilinear-map, isoclinism, or Potts-partition terminology not captured by the searches.
