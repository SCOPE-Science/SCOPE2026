# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. In \(\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\), each arrow coordinate of a commutator is exactly \((d_u-d_v)\eta_a-(s_u-s_v)\xi_a\). For fixed diagonal data, that equation has \(q^2\) radical-coordinate solutions exactly on monochromatic edges of the induced \(\mathbb F_q^2\)-vertex coloring and \(q\) solutions otherwise. Expanding the monochromatic weight gives the spanning-subgraph sum with \(q^{2k(F)}\) colorings constant on each component. The Tutte conversion is the standard random-cluster identity. The packaged exhaustive checker independently reconstructs the commutation equations and matches the formula in seven small quiver/field cases.

## Originality
PASS. The closest published same-object result is the generalized Kronecker two-vertex family, whose commuting-probability formula is exactly recovered by the present theorem. Its statement does not cover arbitrary quivers or identify a Tutte/random-cluster invariant. The archive radical-square-zero sources use valued quivers and the presentation \(kQ/J^2\) for homological and singular-equivalence questions, not commuting-pair enumeration. Targeted database and web searches using quiver, radical-square-zero, commuting probability, Tutte, Potts, random-cluster, bilinear-map, and orientation-invariance formulations did not locate a broader covering statement. Residual risk remains for equivalent language in bilinear-map/isoclinism literature.

## Value
PASS. The formula turns an algebraic probability for every finite radical-square-zero quiver algebra into a classical graph polynomial evaluation. It yields orientation invariance, imports deletion-contraction and Tutte-equivalence tools, and strictly generalizes a published Kronecker calculation rather than merely recomputing a small example.
