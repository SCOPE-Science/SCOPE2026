# Review: Two additional exact fourth-power exceptions in a cubic-unit family

## Correctness
PASS. The coefficient map for squaring a norm-one cubic unit follows directly from elementary symmetric functions. Applying it twice to \((C,D)=(0,3)\) and \((1,13)\) reproduces exactly the published minimal-polynomial coefficients of \(\eta_5\) and \(\eta_{96}\). The source's index formulas give \(D_{K_5}=-135\) and \(D_{K_{96}}=-2104\). The root brackets \(3<\gamma_5<4\) and \(12<\gamma_{96}<13\), combined with Artin's inequality, exclude every proper prime power of either \(\gamma_b\), so both are fundamental.

## Originality
PASS. The primary source states that \(b=5\) and \(b=96\) are square-exceptions and reports only that the displayed examples have even unit order. Its exact higher-power examples in Remark 5.6 are \(b=-75\), \(b=-3\), and \(b=9\), not these two parameters. Exact-phrase, polynomial, regulator, and implication searches found no published item asserting either \(\eta_5=\eta_0^4\) or \(\eta_{96}=\eta_0^4\). General cubic-field tables can identify fields or discriminants, but do not by themselves supply the relation between the family element \(\eta_b\) and a fundamental unit.

## Value
PASS. This is not merely another verification that the two parameters are squares: it determines the exact unit-group exponent and exact regulator at two of the first positive Pell exceptions. It also shows that repeated squareness is not confined to the exceptional discriminant-bound failure \(b=-75\) highlighted by the source; it occurs at \(b=5\) and \(b=96\) as well. The result sharpens a half-log regulator bound to an exact quarter-log identity at two natural parameters already singled out by the source's square-exception sequence.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest source is Kimura--Umemoto, arXiv:2609.37389v1, especially Theorem 5.2, the numerical paragraph immediately following it, and Remark 5.6. The present claim is deliberately limited to \(b=5\) and \(b=96\); no completeness statement for all fourth-power exceptions is made.
