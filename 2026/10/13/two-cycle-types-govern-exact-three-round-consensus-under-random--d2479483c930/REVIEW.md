# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Each agent’s dependency support at most doubles per simultaneous averaging round, requiring three rounds. At that cutoff, full eight-element support is equivalent to exact uniform average because all contributing coefficients are positive and each of the eight initial states occurs exactly once in an averaging tree. Contracting the first matching gives a loopless degree-two multigraph on four vertices, whose only possibilities are two doubled edges or a four-cycle. Inclusion-exclusion gives 60 edge-disjoint second matchings, split as 12 and 48; the corresponding last-step perfect matchings number 24 and 4. These steps yield the exact count 480 and probability \(32/735\). Exact-integer enumeration independently checks the counts and normalizations. The argument is universal in the initial real state and conditional on every first matching by label symmetry.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk.** The nearest methodological precursor is the block-gossip formulation in Loizou–Richtárik (arXiv:1905.08645, §3.4); it identifies random subgraph averaging and supplies contraction bounds, but not this exact law. Ko’s finite-time average-consensus thesis (2010, Chapter 4) proves the hypercube construction and necessary power-of-two restrictions; it does not enumerate iid perfect matchings. Shi–Anderson–Johansson (arXiv:1112.1336, Theorem 5 and Appendix C) prove almost-sure finite-time existence under a different random-graph model, not the exact three-round probability. Boyd–Ghosh–Prabhakar–Shah (2006) study gossip averaging time with different sampling mechanisms. Dedicated published-index and web searches for the probability, the matching cycle types, and alternate formulations did not identify a covering statement. Search absence does not certify novelty: the main residual risk is a specialized, non-indexed combinatorial count of optimal matching schedules.

**Value — PASS.** Eight agents form the first nontrivial power-of-two parallel-averaging case in which two edge-disjoint successive matchings have distinct alternating-cycle topologies. The exact minimal-round success probability is a natural reliability invariant of fully synchronous random gossip, and the sharp 24-versus-4 completion count isolates a sixfold scheduling effect. This is a complete classification of an unforced, mathematically natural finite-time event, not a numerical tolerance bound or an arbitrary parameter table. The result makes no claim about larger networks, work per round, practical runtime, or data-dependent earlier consensus.
