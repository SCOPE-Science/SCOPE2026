# Review of the two-level Grassmann coloring spectrum

## Correctness

PASS. The witness has the exact Moore-determinant expansion obtained by polarization of the cubic seventh-power map; the determinant is nonzero for every binary independent triple. The scalar action gives a structural orbit accounting: the nine scalar subfield three-spaces form one exceptional orbit, and all remaining \(1386\) spaces form \(22\) size-\(63\) orbits. The finite orbit allocation, with four free orbits above the norm-one coset and three over each of the other six, was checked by complete RREF enumeration and independent triple-subspace enumeration. The same code compares the polynomial witness to the Moore determinant on every space and verifies adjacency constraints through all \(651\) binary two-spaces. Neither partial testing nor an asymptotic conclusion is used.

## Originality

PASS on the inspected source set, subject to ordinary literature-indexing risk. Carlet supplies the identity and sum-free property, D'haeseleer–Taranchuk give the determinant coloring, and Heering–Kaspers–Taranchuk provide its witness and coding context. Their theorems establish valid coloring and chromatic bounds, not the size of every fiber or the norm-one distinction. The MRD paper treats distinct classes of colorings. Searches for the \(21/29\) split, determinant fibers, finite-field aliases, and scalar/coset reductions did not identify an implication proving the two-level spectrum. The independently published statement of a \(31\)-coloring is a stronger chromatic upper bound but not a determination of this specific \(63\)-coloring's fibers.

## Value

PASS. The first nontrivial self-dual three-space Grassmann instance admits several different algebraic colorings; their class-size distributions matter for assessing how they partition constant-dimension codes, even when not optimal for chromatic number. The natural Moore construction is **not equisized**: precisely nine norm-one classes are larger by eight, and this deviation is accounted for by the unique subfield orbit. This is a complete structural and computational description of a natural partition, rather than an arbitrary restricted example or a marginal chromatic-number claim.

## Scope and risk

The claim is limited to the seventh-power coloring over \(\mathbb F_{64}\). It does not classify optimal colorings or offer a general extension-degree formula. The original determinant and coloring theorems are credited; an unindexed earlier exact fiber computation is a residual originality risk.
