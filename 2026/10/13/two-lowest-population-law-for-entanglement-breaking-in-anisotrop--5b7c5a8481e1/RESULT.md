# Two-lowest-population law for entanglement breaking in anisotropic reset dynamics

## Finding

For every integer \(d\ge3\), every faithful density matrix \(\sigma\) on \(\mathbb C^d\) having at least three distinct eigenvalues, and every \(0\le s\le1\), consider the identity–replacement channel
\[
\Phi_{s,\sigma}(X)=sX+(1-s)\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma.
\]
Write the eigenvalues of \(\sigma\), in nondecreasing order and counting multiplicity, as \(0<p_{(1)}\le p_{(2)}\le\cdots\le p_{(d)}\), and put \(r_\sigma=\sqrt{p_{(1)}p_{(2)}}\). Then the following are equivalent:

1. \(\Phi_{s,\sigma}\) is entanglement breaking;
2. its normalized Choi state is separable;
3. its normalized Choi state has positive partial transpose;
4. \(s\le r_\sigma/(1+r_\sigma)\).

Thus, within this high-dimensional anisotropic family, the generally insufficient positive-partial-transpose test is in fact exact. An explicit finite product-state decomposition establishes sufficiency, without using any generic high-dimensional PPT-to-separability implication.

For every \(\gamma>0\), the reset semigroup generated by
\[
  \mathcal L_\sigma(X)=\gamma\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma-X\bigr)
\]
consequently has the exact first entanglement-breaking time
\[
  T_{\mathrm{EB}}(\sigma,\gamma)=\gamma^{-1}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{(1)}p_{(2)}}}\right).
\]
Its dependence on the entire stationary spectrum enters only through the two smallest populations. In particular, for a Gibbs state \(\sigma_\beta=e^{-\beta H}/Z_\beta\) with \(\beta>0\) and at least three distinct energy levels, its two highest energies \(E_{(d)}\ge E_{(d-1)}\) give
\[
  T_{\mathrm{EB}}=\gamma^{-1}\log\left(1+Z_\beta e^{\beta(E_{(d)}+E_{(d-1)})/2}\right).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The Hilbert space is \(\mathbb C^d\) with \(d\ge3\). The output state \(\sigma\) is positive definite, has trace one, and has at least three distinct eigenvalues. The parameter \(s\) lies in the entire closed interval \([0,1]\). These restrictions single out anisotropic stationary targets beyond the two-level-spectrum shifted-depolarizing family and the ordinary unital depolarizing channel. The reset rate \(\gamma\) is any strictly positive real number. Entanglement breaking means that \((\operatorname{id}\otimes\Phi)(\rho)\) is separable for every bipartite input and any finite ancillary dimension; finite-dimensional equivalence with separability of the normalized Choi state is standard [1].

Fix an eigenbasis of \(\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_d)\). The normalized maximally entangled state uses \(|\Omega\rangle/\sqrt d\), where \(|\Omega\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^d|ii\rangle\). Below \(J\) denotes the unnormalized Choi matrix; the physical state is \(J/d\). Partial transpose \(\Gamma\) acts on the output tensor factor. Changing to a different eigenbasis corresponds to local-unitary changes and does not alter the claims.

## Proof

**Step 1: Choi matrix and the necessary threshold.** Linearity gives
\[
  J=s|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|+(1-s)I_d\otimes\sigma.
\]
It has trace \(d\) and is positive semidefinite. Its partial transpose is
\[
  J^\Gamma=s\sum_{i,j}|ij\rangle\langle ji|+(1-s)I_d\otimes\sigma.
\]
Each one-dimensional sector \(|ii\rangle\) has eigenvalue \(s+(1-s)p_i>0\). For distinct indices \(i,j\), the invariant sector spanned by \(|ij\rangle,|ji\rangle\) has matrix
\[
  \begin{pmatrix}(1-s)p_j&s\\s&(1-s)p_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its trace is nonnegative, and its determinant is \((1-s)^2p_i p_j-s^2\). Therefore \(J^\Gamma\ge0\) precisely when \(s\le(1-s)\sqrt{p_i p_j}\) for all distinct \(i,j\), equivalently \(s\le r_\sigma/(1+r_\sigma)\). The partial transpose of a separable state is positive, so this inequality is necessary for entanglement breaking. Strict violation supplies a negative-eigenvalue certificate.

**Step 2: finite separable decomposition at every PPT point.** Suppose \(0\le s\le r_\sigma/(1+r_\sigma)\). Set \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\). For every \(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_d)\in\{0,1,2\}^d\), define unnormalized one-particle vectors
\[
  u_\theta=\sum_i p_i^{-1/4}\omega^{\theta_i}|i\rangle,\qquad
  v_\theta=\sqrt s\sum_j p_j^{1/4}\omega^{-\theta_j}|j\rangle.
\]
The positive, explicitly separable operator
\[
  S=3^{-d}\sum_{\theta\in\{0,1,2\}^d}
  |u_\theta\rangle\langle u_\theta|\otimes|v_\theta\rangle\langle v_\theta|
\]
is a finite sum of product projectors. Independence of the cubic phases implies the exact identity
\[
  S=s|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|+
  s\sum_{i\ne j}\sqrt{p_j/p_i}\,|ij\rangle\langle ij|.
\]
Indeed, the phase moment is zero unless its positive and negative index multisets agree modulo the independent cubic roots; the surviving terms are the diagonal-sector coherences \(|ii\rangle\langle jj|\) and the population projectors \(|ij\rangle\langle ij|\). The common \(|ii\rangle\langle ii|\) cases are counted only once in the displayed identity.

Subtracting \(S\) from the unnormalized Choi matrix yields
\[
  J-S=(1-s)\sum_i p_i|ii\rangle\langle ii|+
  \sum_{i\ne j}\left((1-s)p_j-s\sqrt{p_j/p_i}\right)|ij\rangle\langle ij|.
\]
Every coefficient is nonnegative: for \(i\ne j\),
\[
   (1-s)p_j\ge s\sqrt{p_j/p_i}
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\le(1-s)\sqrt{p_i p_j},
\]
precisely the PPT condition already proved. Thus \(J\) is a finite sum of unnormalized positive product projectors. Dividing by \(d\) makes a separable state. The standard Choi criterion [1] shows that \(\Phi_{s,\sigma}\) is entanglement breaking. This argument works for every allowed dimension, including the threshold equality case, and requires no finite-dimensional enumeration.

**Step 3: continuous-time first crossing.** The replacement map \(\mathcal R_\sigma(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma\) satisfies \(\mathcal R_\sigma^2=\mathcal R_\sigma\). Hence
\[
  e^{t\mathcal L_\sigma}=e^{-\gamma t}\operatorname{id}+(1-e^{-\gamma t})\mathcal R_\sigma.
\]
It is a legitimate quantum Markov generator: taking the Lindblad jump operators \(L_{ij}=\sqrt{\gamma p_i}|i\rangle\langle j|\) yields \(\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}XL_{ij}^\dagger=\gamma\operatorname{Tr}(X)\sigma\) and \(\sum_{i,j}L_{ij}^\dagger L_{ij}=\gamma I\). Since \(e^{-\gamma t}\) decreases strictly from one to zero, substitution into the exact threshold gives the stated first time. For Gibbs targets with \(\beta>0\), the two least-populated eigenstates are those with the two highest energies, giving the displayed expression.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_reset.py` (Python 3 and NumPy) independently constructs the Choi matrix in the tensor-product basis, partial-transposes it, and computes every term in the finite cubic-phase product-vector sum. It checks exact residual formulas numerically and their nonnegativity at and below the predicted threshold for four distinct nonuniform spectra in dimensions \(3\), \(4\), and \(5\), and verifies a negative PPT eigenvalue immediately above threshold. The verifier also tests the predicted first crossing for the exponential semigroup. Its recorded output is `VERIFY_OK fullrank_PPT_and_phase_decomposition=16 Lindblad_thresholds=3`. These deterministic finite calculations are consistency checks, not the proof for all dimensions; that proof is Steps 1–3.

## Relationship to prior work

Horodecki, Shor and Ruskai [1] provide the general Choi-separability characterization of entanglement-breaking channels. Hanson, Rouzé and Stilck França [2] analyze eventual entanglement breaking for Markov semigroups and illustrate the ordinary unital depolarizing case, for which the well-known exact threshold gives \(T_{\mathrm{EB}}=\log(d+1)/\gamma\). Trinh [3] derives Bell-mixing thresholds for **two-qubit** separable noise, including the replacement-product specialization; hence the dimension-two formula is antecedent, not asserted to be new. Baitenov [4] investigates a shifted depolarizing channel whose invariant state has at most **two** distinct eigenvalues and establishes parameter-dependent occurrence or nonoccurrence of entanglement breaking; the partial material inspected does not supply the all-spectrum theorem here. The present statement concerns faithful spectra with at least three distinct values in arbitrary dimension at least three, for which the exact two-smallest-population threshold and an explicit phase-averaged separability certificate are obtained simultaneously. An independent literature-priority review has not been performed.

## Limitations

This is an exact theorem only for an identity–replacement interpolation and its corresponding single-rate reset semigroup; it does not cover arbitrary Lindblad dynamics, coherent Hamiltonian perturbations, multirate thermalization, or temperature-dependent rate changes. All-dimensional correctness follows analytically; numerical experiments do not establish it on their own. The chosen domain excludes rank-deficient stationary states, two-level spectra, and the maximally mixed target, although the proof algebra admits straightforward boundary checks. An equivalent all-dimensional theorem in unlocated specialized literature remains a priority risk. In particular, full-text inspection of the later sections of [4] was incomplete, so no claim is made that its entire paper has been exhaustively ruled out as containing an equivalent broader lemma.
