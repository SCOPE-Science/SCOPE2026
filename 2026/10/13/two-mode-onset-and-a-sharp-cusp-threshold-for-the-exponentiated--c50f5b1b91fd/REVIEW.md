# Mathematical assessment of the small-exponentiation Haq density theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The density and logarithmic derivative are reconstructed from the stated 2025 Haq CDF, not from a plotted or discretized approximation. Writing the log-density derivative as \(A_\tau(y)+\varepsilon B_\tau(y)\) makes its zeros the exact stationary points. The analytic extension of \(yB_\tau(y)\) through the origin proves the first mode and its leading location. The simple positive zeros of \(A_\tau\) persist by the implicit-function theorem when \(2\tau(\tau+2)<1\). At equality, the double zero at one becomes two roots, by strict concavity and the explicit quadratic Taylor term. Compact isolation and uniform negative tail bounds rule out further roots. Symbolic identities and sample numerical roots were checked with the bundled standalone script. No numerical root scan is treated as exhaustive proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limits

Alharthi (2025), pages 22499–22501, derives the exponentiated density and plots shape examples without the theorem's complete near-one stationary-point count or square-root critical expansion. The original Haq paper (Ahsan-ul-Haq, 2022, Section 2.1) **already derives the baseline critical mode formula and its underlying threshold**; no priority is claimed for that threshold in the unexponentiated model or for general CDF exponentiation. The 2023 power Haq distribution is a transformation of the lifetime variable, not the CDF-exponentiated law examined here. Targeted searches for exact and equivalent modal conditions, generic exponentiation results, and matched numerical thresholds did not reveal a prior proof of the combined model-specific statement. Non-indexed equivalent work remains a priority risk; absence of a retrieved match is not evidence of uniqueness by itself.

## Value — PASS

The density modality of a proposed lifetime distribution is a basic modeling feature, but the existing shape discussion does not reveal its change near the natural nesting parameter \(\delta=1\). The theorem shows when a second strict interior density peak **must** emerge, when it **cannot** emerge locally in parameter space, and why the critical parameter requires a square-root rather than a regular perturbative expansion. It is useful in evaluating shape flexibility and preventing single-peak interpretations of this model. The result is not a global all-parameter classification, does not determine which local mode is globally higher, and makes no assertion about the likelihood surface.
