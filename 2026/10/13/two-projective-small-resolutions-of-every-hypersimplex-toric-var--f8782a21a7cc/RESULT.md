# Two projective small resolutions of every hypersimplex toric variety
## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\) and \(2\le k\le n-2\), let \(X_{k,n}\) be the normal projective toric variety of the hypersimplex
\[
\Delta_{k,n}=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in[0,1]^n:\sum_{i=1}^n x_i=k\}.
\]
There are exactly \(\binom nk\) singularities, one at each torus-fixed point; they are isolated and their affine charts are the rank-one determinantal cones \(D_{k,n-k}\). Two explicit smooth projective toric varieties give *small birational resolutions* of \(X_{k,n}\). Both are crepant exactly for \(n=2k\). The two constructions differ in their exceptional fiber dimensions, and the orientation with fiber dimension \(\min(k,n-k)-1\) is also small in the intersection-cohomological sense.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). The lattice of polytope differences is \(M=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i z_i=0\}\); the lattice of one-parameter subgroups is \(N=\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). We use *inward* normal cones for the fan. The rational parameter is any
\[
0<\epsilon<\frac1{n-1}.
\]
Define the rational full-dimensional box slices
\[
\Delta^+_{k,n}(\epsilon)=\{\textstyle\sum_i x_i=k,\ 0\le x_i\le1+\epsilon\},
\qquad
\Delta^-_{k,n}(\epsilon)=\{\textstyle\sum_i x_i=k,\ -\epsilon\le x_i\le1\}.
\]
Multiplying by a common denominator gives lattice polytopes without changing their normal fans. Denote the associated toric varieties by \(Y^+_{k,n}\) and \(Y^-_{k,n}\). A *small birational map* here means that it contracts no divisor. The additional, stronger, Goresky--MacPherson smallness condition is stated separately.

## Proof
**1. The fixed-point affine charts.** A vertex of \(\Delta_{k,n}\) is indexed by a subset \(S\subset[n]\) with \(|S|=k\). In the chart at \(S\), the ratio of a monomial indexed by \(T\) to that indexed by \(S\) has exponent \(e_T-e_S\). By exchanging elements of \(S\) for elements outside \(S\), each such exponent is a sum of elementary swaps \(e_j-e_i\), with \(i\in S\) and \(j\notin S\). Conversely the elementary swaps arise by replacing a single element. All integral points of the cone are nonnegative integral sums of swaps: for any vector with nonpositive entries on \(S\), nonnegative entries off \(S\), and coordinate sum zero, match the positive and negative units to obtain an integral transportation decomposition. The affine chart is therefore the semigroup algebra generated by the \(k(n-k)\) elements \(u_iv_j\), equivalently
\[
U_S\simeq D_{k,n-k}:=\operatorname{Spec}\frac{\mathbb C[z_{ij}:i\in S,j\notin S]}{(z_{ij}z_{i'j'}-z_{ij'}z_{i'j})}.
\]
The rank-one determinantal cone is smooth away from its zero matrix and singular at zero whenever \(k,n-k\ge2\): at a nonzero matrix one can solve locally for one factorization coordinate, whereas at zero its tangent space has dimension \(k(n-k)>n-1\). The vertex charts cover \(X_{k,n}\); their origins are precisely the \(\binom nk\) torus-fixed points. Hence the complete singular locus is this set of isolated points.

**2. Vertex formulas for the perturbations.** A vertex of either box slice has all but one coordinate at a bound. For \(\Delta^+\), feasibility and the bound on \(\epsilon\) force exactly \(k-1\) coordinates to equal \(1+\epsilon\), one free coordinate to equal \(1-(k-1)\epsilon\), and all others to be zero. Thus vertices are indexed by a pair \((U,f)\) with \(|U|=k-1\), \(f\notin U\). For \(\Delta^-\), a vertex has exactly \(k\) coordinates equal to \(1\), exactly \(n-k-1\) equal to \(-\epsilon\), and its one free coordinate equal to \((n-k-1)\epsilon\). Hence \(\Delta^-\) vertices are indexed by a \(k\)-subset \(S\) and \(f\notin S\). These formulas follow directly by setting \(n-1\) coordinates to endpoints, solving the coordinate-sum equation, and checking that all other endpoint counts put the remaining coordinate outside the prescribed interval.

Each new vertex has precisely \(n-1\) active box facets. Their primitive inward normals are the coordinate classes \(\pm\bar e_i\) for all \(i\ne f\). Such classes form an integral basis of \(N\), because omitting any one coordinate class from \(\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\) leaves a basis. Both normal fans are therefore unimodular; \(Y^+\) and \(Y^-\) are smooth and projective.

**3. Fan refinement and fibers.** For a generic linear functional with pairwise distinct coordinate weights, maximizing it on \(\Delta_{k,n}\) chooses the \(k\) highest-weight coordinates. The same greedy rule on \(\Delta^+\) assigns the upper bound to the first \(k-1\) coordinates and the remaining positive mass to the \(k\)-th. On \(\Delta^-\) it assigns the upper bound to the first \(k\) coordinates and the remaining mass to the \((k+1)\)-st, with the rest at the lower bound. Thus every cone of either perturbed normal fan is contained in a cone of the original normal fan. Both furnish projective toric birational morphisms
\[
\pi^\pm:Y^\pm_{k,n}\longrightarrow X_{k,n}.
\]
No new one-dimensional fan rays are introduced: all rays are coordinate inward normals already present in the original fan. Therefore neither map contracts any divisor. Every positive-dimensional face of \(\Delta_{k,n}\) is obtained by fixing some coordinates at zero or one, leaving at least two free; its inward normals are a proper subset of the coordinate basis classes, so these cones are already smooth. The morphisms are isomorphisms away from the isolated singular fixed points.

Over the fixed point indexed by \(S\), the \(\pi^+\)-preimage has one torus-fixed point for each choice of free coordinate \(f\in S\). On the affine chart \(U_S\simeq D_{k,n-k}\), the fan subdivision is the standard resolution
\[
\{(Z,[u])\in D_{k,n-k}\times\mathbb P^{k-1}:\operatorname{im}Z\subset\mathbb C u\}\longrightarrow D_{k,n-k},
\]
namely the total space of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{k-1}}(-1)^{\oplus(n-k)}\) mapping to the rank-one matrix cone. Its zero fiber is \(\mathbb P^{k-1}\). For \(\pi^-\) the transposed incidence resolution has zero fiber \(\mathbb P^{n-k-1}\), described using the row line. To verify the fan identification rather than just count fixed points, on the incidence chart where \(u_i\ne0\), take \(a_h=u_h/u_i\) for \(h\in S\setminus\{i\}\) and \(b_j=u_i v_j\) for \(j\notin S\). These are \(n-1\) affine coordinates with torus exponents \(e_i-e_h\) and \(e_j-e_i\), respectively. Their dual cone is generated by the inward normals \(-\bar e_h\) for \(h\in S\setminus\{i\}\) and \(+\bar e_j\) for \(j\notin S\), exactly the cone at the perturbed vertex with free coordinate \(i\). This exhibits the local affine isomorphism to the incidence chart. Interchanging the row and column factors gives the corresponding verification for \(\pi^-\). The morphisms are isomorphisms over all nonzero rank-one matrices.

**4. Canonical and smallness thresholds.** At the cone for \(S\), the primitive rays are \(-\bar e_i\) for \(i\in S\) and \(+\bar e_j\) for \(j\notin S\), subject to the single relation
\[
\sum_{i\in S}(-\bar e_i)=\sum_{j\notin S}\bar e_j.
\]
The affine toric canonical divisor is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier if and only if there is a rational functional taking value \(1\) on each primitive ray. The relation requires \(k=n-k\); when that equality holds, a functional with entries \(-1\) on \(S\) and \(+1\) off \(S\) lies in the integral character lattice and supplies the required Cartier datum. Thus the singularities are Gorenstein exactly for \(n=2k\), and are non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein otherwise. In the Gorenstein case both small birational smooth resolutions are crepant: their relative canonical divisors could be supported only on exceptional prime divisors, of which there are none.

The exceptional fibers have respective dimensions \(k-1\) and \(n-k-1\). Since all singular strata are points of codimension \(n-1\), choosing the smaller dimension gives
\[
2\bigl(\min(k,n-k)-1\bigr)<n-1,
\]
which is precisely the Goresky--MacPherson small-map inequality. The opposite orientation need not satisfy that stronger inequality. Both have no divisorial exceptional locus because \(k,n-k\ge2\).

## Verification
The exact-arithmetic regression script `CHECKER.py` exhaustively reconstructs the vertices of both box slices for every \(4\le n\le9\) and \(2\le k\le n-2\), using rational \(\epsilon=1/(3n)\). It checks that the explicit vertex lists agree with the full all-but-one-at-bound enumeration, that each vertex lies on exactly \(n-1\) box facets, and that each unperturbed \(k\)-subset has exactly \(k\) upper-resolution or \(n-k\) lower-resolution fixed points above it. The recorded test covers \(42\) parameter/orientation cases and \(7540\) candidate vertices. This finite calculation is not an infinite proof; Steps 1--4 give the general argument.

Additional immediate invariants are the multiplicity \(\binom{n-2}{k-1}\) and divisor-class group \(\mathbb Z\) at each singular point, by the degree and Picard quotient of the Segre variety \(\mathbb P^{k-1}\times\mathbb P^{n-k-1}\). The smooth resolutions have respectively \(k\binom nk\) and \((n-k)\binom nk\) torus-fixed points; these numbers count their topological Euler characteristics. The smaller of these agrees with the known intersection-cohomology Euler characteristic, rather than constituting a new formula for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Cox--Little--Schenck develops the general toric fan, projective-morphism, divisor, and resolution machinery that underlies the construction (in particular, Chapters 3, 7 and 11). Jojić (2007) determines toric \(h\)-vectors of dual hypersimplices using shellings. Wang--Yu (2017, Theorem 2.2) proves explicit intersection-cohomology ranks and describes the hypersimplex face lattice. Their previously known intersection-cohomology and toric \(h\)-vector formulas are **not** claimed as discoveries here. The assertion assessed here is the uniform **explicit two-sided rational box-slice construction** giving smooth projective small birational resolutions, both exceptional fiber models, the perverse-small orientation, and the exact crepant boundary. Standard local incidence resolutions of rank-one determinantal cones are also acknowledged as established constructions; the new step is their simultaneous coherent projective globalization across all hypersimplex fixed points. Targeted literature and record comparisons did not locate this full stated package. Undetected equivalent globalizations remain possible.

## Limitations
This statement treats only normal toric varieties of uniform-matroid hypersimplices, not arbitrary matroid polytopes, and only integers in the displayed range. "Small" as used for the birational morphisms means no divisors are contracted; the more restrictive intersection-cohomological inequality holds only for the selected orientation. The known intersection-cohomology values are prior art. Projective smooth resolutions may exist in other forms; no uniqueness is claimed. The comparative literature search is not a proof of novelty, and an independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
