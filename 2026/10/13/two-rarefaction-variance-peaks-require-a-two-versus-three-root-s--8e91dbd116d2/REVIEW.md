# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Rooted diversity is expressed exactly as the sum of branch lengths whose descendant sets meet the sampled taxa. Subset enumeration yields the variance quadratics, verified coefficient-by-coefficient by an independently structured rational checker. Two root-one-versus-four shapes exhaust that topology and each has an explicitly positive expression for the third-minus-second variance. The remaining shape has two explicit necessary-and-sufficient inequalities, and the positive-edge integer witness satisfies both. For fewer than five tips no two separated strict interior peaks exist. No finite test is misused as proof for the all-length statement.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** Nipperess and Matsen's established general covariance formula covers computation of conditional rarefaction variance, but does not give the five-tip root-topology modal obstruction, the full two-versus-three branch-age region, or the positivity certificates excluding both root-one-versus-four forms. Chao and colleagues focus on rarefaction/extrapolation and estimator uncertainty; O'Dwyer, Kembel and Green study sampling laws of metacommunities. Searches for variance unimodality, two local maxima, ultrametric five-tip trees, and equivalent formulations found no dominating classification. A specialized tree-statistics inequality or overlooked supplementary statement remains a risk; the existence of the general variance formula is stated openly and is not itself claimed as novel.

**Value — PASS.** The shape of sampling uncertainty is directly relevant to comparing phylogenetic diversity at different sampling depths. The theorem shows that uncertainty need not have one sampling-depth peak even on strictly ultrametric trees, identifies the smallest taxon pool capable of this behavior, and isolates the topology and exact conditions that make it possible. This is an exact natural classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice. It is not an empirical design recommendation or a general theorem for trees with more than five tips.
