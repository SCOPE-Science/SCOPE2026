# Two symmetry types of maximum binary length-four \(B_2\) codes
## Finding
For binary \(B_2\) codes \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^4\), the maximum cardinality is \(7\), and exactly \(240\) labeled codes attain it. Under the standard Hamming-isometry group \((C_2)^4\rtimes S_4\) of order \(384\), these maxima form exactly two orbits of sizes \(192\) and \(48\), with stabilizer orders \(2\) and \(8\). Representatives are \(C_A=\{0000,0001,0010,0100,0111,1001,1110\}\) and \(C_B=\{0000,0001,0010,0111,1011,1100,1111\}\). Their pairwise Hamming-distance profiles \((N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4)\) are respectively \((4,10,6,1)\) and \((4,8,8,1)\), so the profiles distinguish the two extremal types.

Exactly \(105\) of the \(240\) labeled maxima contain \(0000\). The orbit containing \(C_A\) contributes \(84\) of these normalized representatives and the orbit containing \(C_B\) contributes \(21\).

## Assumptions and scope
A binary length-four \(B_2\) code is a set \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^4\) for which ordinary coordinatewise addition is injective on unordered pairs from \(C\), with repetition allowed. Thus if \(x,y,u,v\in C\) and \(x+y=u+v\), then \(\{x,y\}=\{u,v\}\). For binary words, allowing repeated pairs or restricting to distinct pairs gives the same off-diagonal condition: a diagonal sum has every coordinate in \(\{0,2\}\), while a sum of two distinct binary words has at least one coordinate equal to \(1\).

“Labeled” means that the four coordinate positions and the symbols \(0,1\) in each coordinate are fixed. The equivalence group used here is the standard Hamming-isometry group generated by coordinate permutations and independent bit complements, namely \((C_2)^4\rtimes S_4\), of order \(16\cdot24=384\).

## Proof
There are only \(16\) binary words of length four. The supplied verifier checks every one of the \(\binom{16}{8}=12870\) eight-word subsets and finds no \(B_2\) code of size eight. Since the \(B_2\) property is hereditary under taking subsets, this excludes every larger code. It then checks all \(\binom{16}{7}=11440\) seven-word subsets and finds exactly \(240\) satisfying the defining injectivity condition. In particular, the displayed sets \(C_A\) and \(C_B\) are witnesses, so the exact maximum is \(7\).

The verifier constructs all \(384\) Hamming isometries explicitly. Acting on the \(240\) maxima yields exactly two disjoint orbits. The orbit of \(C_A\) has size \(192\), and the orbit of \(C_B\) has size \(48\); together they exhaust the full census. Direct stabilizer counts are \(2\) and \(8\), agreeing with orbit–stabilizer: \(384/2=192\) and \(384/8=48\).

For an extremal code \(C\), let \(N_d\) be the number of unordered pairs of distinct codewords at Hamming distance \(d\). Direct calculation gives
\[
(N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4)(C_A)=(4,10,6,1),\qquad
(N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4)(C_B)=(4,8,8,1).
\]
The verifier checks that each profile is constant on its full orbit. Since the two profiles differ, they provide a simple invariant distinguishing the two equivalence classes.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify.py` uses only exact integer arithmetic and the Python standard library. It independently constructs ternary encodings of all ordinary pair sums, checks the complete size-eight upper-bound layer and the complete size-seven equality layer, generates the full order-\(384\) Hamming-isometry group, partitions the maxima into orbits, counts stabilizers, and checks the distance profiles on every maximum.

## Relationship to prior work
Lindström introduced the binary pair-sum problem and studied its asymptotic growth. His 1969 paper defines the same binary ordinary-sum uniqueness problem, and his 1972 paper formulates the corresponding \(B_2\)-sequence language for vectors. Those papers establish asymptotic bounds rather than a finite equality classification at length four.

OEIS A309370 records the exact small value \(7\) at dimension four and displays \(C_A\) as one maximum example. That database entry does not give the number of maximum codes, their Hamming-isometry orbit decomposition, stabilizer orders, or a second inequivalent extremal type. A modern treatment of binary \(B_2\) and separable codes records the equivalence of these notions in the binary setting and develops bounds and constructions, but the inspected text does not supply this length-four equality census.

The contribution here is therefore the complete finite equality structure at this canonical small parameter: literal count, natural-equivalence classification, stabilizers, and a compact invariant separating the two types.

## Limitations
The classification is only for binary length four. It does not assert a pattern for larger dimensions. Equivalence is taken under the standard Hamming-isometry group; no claim is made here about a larger abstract automorphism group of the pair-sum incidence structure. The originality comparison covered the cited primary sources, the exact-value database entry, and targeted semantic searches, but an obscure unindexed historical census remains a residual literature risk.
