# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. Specializing the published anticanonical volume identity to \(\mathbf P(1,1,a,b)\) gives
\[
(a+b+2)^3=64ab.
\]
With \(s=a+b\), AM--GM gives \((s+2)^3\le16s^2\), which fails for every \(s\ge9\). Hence \(2\le s\le8\). Since \(ab=(s+2)^3/64\) is integral and \(4\le s+2\le10\), divisibility by \(2^6\) forces \(s+2\in\{4,8\}\). The resulting sum-product pairs are exactly \((1,1)\) and \((2,4)\), up to order. Both surviving weighted projective spaces are known to admit the required smoothing.

## Originality
PASS. The initiating paper proves several broader results under additional gcd or \(A\)-type assumptions but does not state the unrestricted two-unit-weight theorem. The primary predecessor gives the volume equation, a non-exhaustive computer list, and the smoothable \(\mathbf P(1,1,2,4)\) example, but no exhaustion theorem. Exact-form and implication searches found no covering statement. A recent highly relevant degeneration preprint could not be inspected in full and remains a named residual risk.

## Value
PASS. This is a natural complete classification of an infinite subfamily central to the weighted-projective degeneration problem. It removes local singularity hypotheses and finite-search dependence and turns the general volume obstruction into an exact if-and-only-if theorem on the two-unit slice.

The result does not address the one-unit family \(\mathbf P(1,a,b,c)\), where the current classification problem remains substantially harder.
