# Review of the projective symmetry of the eleven-tacnode family

## Correctness: PASS

All quantifiers are over \(r\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,\pm1,\pm i\}\). The five quadratic difference factorizations and one mixed product show that precisely one unordered conic pair has nodes; it has two distinct nodes and one tacnode. The two-node secant, tacnode and tangent impose a triangular normal form on every projectivity. The node coordinates force the first coordinate to have no shear and square-unit scaling; the mixed-term coefficient forces the third-coordinate scaling to be a sign. Conversely the four sign changes preserve all components. These steps prove completeness over \(\mathbb C\) and do not rely on finite enumeration. The actual packaged verifier independently checks the polynomial identities over the full parameter ring.

## Originality: PASS, with access risk

Măcinic--Pokora Theorem 3.1 gives the explicit four-conic family and the special member's plus-one generation, not the uniform ambient projective stabilizer. Bromboszcz--Jarosławski--Pokora Theorem 4.2 addresses the same combinatorics but not its symmetry group. Searches for the exact eleven-tacnode contact type, parameter equations, projective stabilizer, and component-permutation group did not identify an implication-equivalent prior result in the accessible material. The full text of Megyesi's earlier classification was unavailable despite locating the legitimate journal open-access entry. That noninspection is a genuine residual originality risk and not evidence of absence; the classification of the parameter family by itself does not imply the stabilizer conclusion.

## Value: PASS

The eleven-tacnode configuration is a natural extremal four-conic arrangement, and the result determines the **entire** projective symmetry group for its complete displayed parameter family, rather than one sampled curve. It resolves possible accidental projective symmetries uniformly and distinguishes abstract pairwise-contact permutations from geometric projectivities. The geometric node--tangent flag furnishes a reusable structural argument; the finding is not a statement about arbitrary hand-picked coefficients.

## Scientific limitations

Only ambient projective automorphisms over \(\mathbb C\) are classified; degenerate parameters, all abstract curve automorphisms, cross-parameter isomorphisms and general syzygy behavior are not addressed. An earlier equivalent symmetry computation might exist in uninspected work.
