# Scientific assessment

**Correctness (PASS).** The base-\(p\) digit-complement identities give identical digit sums for the two lower arguments and the top argument. Legendre's formula gives exact valuation \(a\). A separately reconstructed unit-factorial identity, together with Wilson's theorem, gives the common normalized residue \(-1\pmod p\). The proof explicitly handles \(p=2\), \(a=1\), and both endpoints of the multiplier range. The reproducibility program directly checks 825 admissible triples by two integer routes, but the proof does not depend on that experiment.

**Originality (PASS, with residual risk).** The motivating paper's full text proves a gcd-one criterion and a local binary prime-divisibility statement. The known exact gcd-valuation refinement determines only minimum valuations, not the residue of each individual member. The statement is not implied by either; one can preserve a gcd and all member valuations while changing member unit residues. Searches for prime-power binomial leading-unit congruences, factorial digit-complement identities, and the exact family found no directly covering result. Classical unindexed binomial congruences remain a real risk.

**Value (PASS).** The prime-power boundary is a canonical extremal subfamily of the binomial-progression gcd question. Identifying the first nonzero \(p\)-adic digit **for every** allowed multiplier supplies complete modular data at the first sharp layer, substantially finer than determining the gcd. The constant residue offers a usable uniform congruence for linear combinations of the progression and a fixed baseline for any higher-order lifting, rather than an arbitrary numerical test.

**Relationship and limits.** The result does not replace the exact gcd classification for arbitrary parameters. Its further \(p\)-adic digits and its analogues for composite parameters remain outside scope. No exhaustive claim about all unindexed literature is made.
