# Uniform longitudinal fields preserve partial-transpose inertia in charge-conserving thermal dimers

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal H_A\) and \(\mathcal H_B\) be finite-dimensional. Let \(Q_A\) and \(Q_B\) be Hermitian local charges and suppose a bipartite Hamiltonian \(H_0\) conserves their sum,
\[
[H_0,Q_A\otimes I+I\otimes Q_B]=0.
\]
For a uniform field \(h\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
H(h)=H_0-h(Q_A\otimes I+I\otimes Q_B),
\qquad
\rho_h(T)=\frac{e^{-H(h)/T}{Z_h(T)},
\]
where \(T>0\). With
\[
K_A=e^{hQ_A/(2T)},\qquad K_B=e^{hQ_B/(2T)},
\]
one has the exact partial-transpose congruence
\[
\rho_h(T)^{T_B}
=
\frac{Z_0(T)}{Z_h(T)}
(K_A\otimes K_B^{\mathsf T})
\rho_0(T)^{T_B}
(K_A\otimes K_B^{\mathsf T}).
\]
Because the prefactor is positive and both local filters are invertible for finite \(h\) and positive \(T\), Sylvester's law of inertia gives
\[
\operatorname{In}\!\left(\rho_h(T)^{T_B}\right)
=
\operatorname{In}\!\left(\rho_0(T)^{T_B}\right),
\]
where \(\operatorname{In}\) records the numbers of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues.

Thus a uniform field conjugate to a conserved additive charge can change the magnitude of the negativity but cannot change whether it vanishes. More strongly, it cannot change the number of negative partial-transpose eigenvalues or move any temperature at which the partial transpose acquires a zero eigenvalue.

For the spin-one dimer of Strečka et al.,
\[
H_0=J_{xy}(S_1^xS_2^x+S_1^yS_2^y)+J_zS_1^zS_2^z+D\bigl((S_1^z)^2+(S_2^z)^2\bigr),
\]
and the longitudinal Zeeman term is \(-h(S_1^z+S_2^z)\). The exchange term preserves total \(S^z\), so the theorem applies for all real \(J_{xy},J_z,D\). Therefore the entire positive-temperature NPT/PPT sign pattern is longitudinal-field independent. In particular, the field-independent threshold reported later for selected anisotropies is not a parameter-specific numerical coincidence, and the temperature of any kink caused by a partial-transpose eigenvalue crossing zero is likewise field independent.

## Assumptions and scope

The result requires finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces, finite \(h\), positive \(T\), and exact conservation of the additive charge \(Q_A\otimes I+I\otimes Q_B\) by \(H_0\). The partial transpose is taken in any fixed local basis; the transpose on \(K_B\) in the congruence makes the statement basis-covariant.

For the spin-one application the field is longitudinal, along the conserved \(z\)-axis. A transverse field generally violates the commutation hypothesis and is outside the theorem. The theorem concerns PPT/NPT and negativity support. In a \(3\times3\) system, PPT does not in general imply separability, so no equivalence between zero negativity and separability is asserted.

## Proof

Write
\[
M=Q_A\otimes I+I\otimes Q_B.
\]
The commutator hypothesis gives \([H_0,M]=0\), hence
\[
e^{-H(h)/T}=e^{-H_0/T}e^{hM/T}.
\]
Since
\[
e^{hM/(2T)}=K_A\otimes K_B,
\]
and this operator commutes with \(e^{-H_0/T}\),
\[
e^{-H(h)/T}
=(K_A\otimes K_B)e^{-H_0/T}(K_A\otimes K_B).
\]
After normalization,
\[
\rho_h(T)=\frac{Z_0(T)}{Z_h(T)}(K_A\otimes K_B)\rho_0(T)(K_A\otimes K_B).
\]
Partial transposition on subsystem \(B\) gives
\[
\rho_h(T)^{T_B}
=
\frac{Z_0(T)}{Z_h(T)}
(K_A\otimes K_B^{\mathsf T})
\rho_0(T)^{T_B}
(K_A\otimes K_B^{\mathsf T}).
\]
The matrix \(K_A\otimes K_B^{\mathsf T}\) is invertible for finite \(h\) and \(T>0\), and the scalar \(Z_0(T)/Z_h(T)\) is positive. Hermitian matrices related by an invertible congruence have identical inertia, proving the claim.

For the spin-one XXZ dimer,
\[
S_1^xS_2^x+S_1^yS_2^y
=\frac12\left(S_1^+S_2^-+S_1^-S_2^+\right),
\]
so every off-diagonal exchange transition preserves \(S_1^z+S_2^z\); the remaining terms are diagonal in that charge. Hence the hypothesis holds exactly.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_field_ppt.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to check two algebraic ingredients on the spin-one product basis. It verifies that every exchange transition generated by \(S_1^+S_2^-\) or \(S_1^-S_2^+\) preserves total \(S^z\), and it checks entry by entry that partial transpose commutes with the corresponding two-sided local filtering in the required congruence form. The recorded output is

`VERIFY_OK transitions=8 supported_entries=19 pt_entries=81`

These finite exact checks are supplementary. The theorem for arbitrary finite dimensions follows from the analytic exponential factorization and Sylvester's law of inertia, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Strečka et al. give the archive-era spin-one dimer Hamiltonian, its longitudinal field term, and the exact spectrum. Ghannadan and Strečka later compute the negativity for the same model class and explicitly observe from selected parameter plots that the threshold temperature above which negativity vanishes is independent of magnetic field; they also identify a temperature kink caused by a partial-transpose eigenvalue changing sign. The present result proves that both phenomena are forced by an exact local-filter congruence for every real exchange and single-ion anisotropy parameter whenever the field is longitudinal.

Invertible local filtering and its relation to entanglement classes are standard prior theory. The new point claimed here is not preservation of PPT under a given local filter by itself, but the identification of the entire positive-temperature uniform-field Gibbs family of any additive-charge-conserving bipartite Hamiltonian as one such invertible local-filter orbit, with the resulting field invariance of the full partial-transpose inertia and all sign-change temperatures.

The closest own prior result establishes a field-independent negativity threshold for one pure-biquadratic spin-one dimer and derives its explicit negativity formula. That special case does not imply the present all-Hamiltonian congruence or the full inertia statement; conversely, the present theorem does not reproduce that prior formula.

## Limitations

Negativity values themselves are not invariant. Only the inertia of the partial transpose is. The result does not apply to nonuniform fields, transverse fields, fields coupled to nonadditive operators, or Hamiltonians that fail to conserve the coupled charge.

The positive-temperature hypothesis is essential. As \(T\downarrow0\) or \(|h|\to\infty\), the local filter can become singular in the limit, so a field-driven product ground state can coexist with NPT Gibbs states at every sufficiently small but positive temperature. No statement about PPT entanglement or distillability is made.
