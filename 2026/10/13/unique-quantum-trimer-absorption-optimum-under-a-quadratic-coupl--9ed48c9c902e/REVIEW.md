# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
With strictly positive links the non-Hermitian excitation generator is exponentially stable. Direct multiplication of the explicitly reconstructed full integrated-survival Gramian verifies its Lyapunov equation for every positive real value of the couplings and loss. The lower-right entry gives the exact absorption mean. Substituting the fixed squared-coupling budget reduces joint optimization to a sharp two-factor AM–GM inequality and the identity \(x+1+2/x+2/x^2-9/2=(x-2)^2(2x+1)/(2x^2)\). Equality is unique. The standalone exact symbolic verifier checks the identities; finite instances do not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The known finite uniform-coupling clean-chain first-transfer formula of Zhang et al. (2017), especially their Eq. (17), is explicitly acknowledged and is not itself claimed as novel. Riberi's 2026 semi-infinite Lindblad walk supplies the first-passage context but not the finite constrained trimer optimization. Contreras-Pulido et al. (2014) treat pumped triple-dot steady currents, and use equal couplings for their analytical parameter study. Peter et al. (2024) treat emitter-chain trapping under a different dissipative model; only indexed article materials were available for inspection. Targeted mathematical-record and source searches showed no exact constraint/equality classification. Searches cannot rule out earlier implicit or inaccessible results, particularly in the broad quantum-transport literature. The assessment concerns the precisely specified unique global coupling-and-loss optimum, not a generic optimization of a trap.

## Value — PASS
The trimer is the minimal linear system permitting nontrivial redistribution between two hopping amplitudes. Fixed squared amplitude is a natural finite hopping-Hamiltonian resource. The sharp optimum is asymmetric, fully characterized in closed form, and saves the exact fraction \(1-\sqrt3/2\) of mean transfer time relative to the best homogeneous-link device. This supplies a quantitative design boundary for a motivated physical objective rather than a numerical curiosity. The result does not claim universal experimental advantage under other constraints or environmental effects.

## Limitations
Only one initial excitation, three resonant sites, strictly positive coherent nearest-neighbor couplings, an ideal site-one Markovian loss jump and the quadratic budget are covered. No results are asserted about multiple excitations, other decay, injection, finite-time capture probability, measurement backaction or larger networks. Publication priority remains provisional pending human literature review.
