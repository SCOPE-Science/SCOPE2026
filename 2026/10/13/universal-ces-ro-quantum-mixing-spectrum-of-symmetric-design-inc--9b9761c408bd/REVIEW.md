# Scientific self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is quantified over all symmetric \(2\)-\((v,k,\lambda)\) designs with \(v\ge3\), \(2\le k\le v-1\), or equivalently connected regular bipartite graphs with exactly four adjacency eigenvalues. The fundamental singular-value relation \(DD^{\mathsf T}=D^{\mathsf T}D=(k-\lambda)(I-J/v)\) fixes all spectral projectors. Their Schur squares yield the stated rational block matrix. The constants and two orthogonal zero-mean singular sectors give all eigenvalues and multiplicities. The inequality \(|v-2k|\le v-4\), together with \(k-\lambda\ge1\), proves strict positivity on the zero-mean sector for every nontrivial design, while \(k=v-1\) is handled separately. Both graph-to-design and design-to-graph directions are established. Exact computations independently corroborate five designs; finite testing is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (residual risk)
Godsil's general projector-Schur-square identity, the commutant perspective of Coutinho–Godsil–Guo–Zhan, and the exact arbitrary-graph calculation framework of Coutinho et al. predate the finding and are not claimed. Their inspected statements do not give the symmetric-design parameter-uniform average-mixing spectrum, its exact degeneracy boundary, or the eigenvalue gap. Abiad–Dalfó–Fiol establish four-eigenvalue bipartite distance regularity but not these Cesàro mixing quantities. Specific design datasets and the Heawood graph adjacency spectrum similarly do not imply a tabulated full answer without the projector calculation. An unlocated broad association-scheme result might imply this answer; this is a residual risk, not proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete, natural classification of the long-time quantum transport invariant for an entire maximal spectral class: connected regular bipartite graphs with four adjacency levels. It determines every transition probability, eigenvalue, multiplicity, rank, exact kernel and positive spectral gap; all are independent of whether a symmetric design is Desarguesian or highly symmetric. The abrupt crown-graph nullity boundary and design-parameter-independent trace are structural, testable consequences, rather than arbitrary finite computations. The invariant is directly motivated by average quantum transport and by the established interest in average-mixing rank.
