# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The kick--drift recurrence, second-moment linearization, and determinant factorization are derived algebraically from the specified oscillator. The nondegenerate-step condition is exactly equivalent to the strict positivity of a fourth-moment square expectation because the step is almost surely strictly positive. Sign-changing the real cubic across one gives a real eigenvalue beyond one. A cone-preserving adjoint eigenmatrix exists at the spectral radius; its possible rank-one case would force a forbidden real eigenline for one-step elliptic matrices, making it strictly positive definite. This closes the all-deterministic-initializations exponential lower bound. The constant-step invariant quadratic form verifies the other direction. `verify.py` reproduces the critical identities and rational samples. Fixed-step endpoint cases and step distributions containing zero are correctly excluded.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Skeel and Gear's 1992 article (abstract accessible; full text unavailable) addresses the loss of symplectic invariants under variable steps. Skeel's complete 1993 article establishes existence of destabilizing specially constructed variable-step sequences for the Störmer/Verlet method, rather than universal i.i.d. mean-square instability, and gives no fourth-moment determinant identity. Quinn et al.'s 1997 full paper studies adaptive leapfrog in simulations. Abdulle and Garegnani's full 2018/2020 preprint establishes weak/mean-square accuracy and finite-horizon approximate energy preservation with step-scaled randomization, which does not imply an all-time fixed-variance stability result. The directly inspected portions of those original results and appendices do not assert the present specific quantified dichotomy. General random-product theory may imply portions of the qualitative growth statement, but the sharp moment identity and every-deterministic-initial-state expected-energy estimate are not identified there. Residual risk remains from unlocated specialized literature on random elliptic symplectic matrices; a failed search is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The question tests whether stable symplectic one-step maps remain energetically reliable when a time-step scheduler is randomized. The theorem draws a natural sharp boundary—any nonzero step variability under strictly positive stable steps—without restricting to a particular distribution. The exact four-moment determinant certificate is inexpensive to evaluate, and the positive-definite dual witness establishes failure for all deterministic initial states instead of a selected unstable trajectory. The zero-step counterboundary shows why positivity is a meaningful assumption, not merely an algebraic convenience. This is a substantial numerical-stability qualification despite first-order simplicity.

It is not an almost-sure growth-rate theorem, a correlated-timestep theorem, or a universal guarantee for other symplectic methods.
