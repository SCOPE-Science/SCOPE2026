# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For iid continuous product coordinates the decreasing first-coordinate order is independent of the second-coordinate values. Pareto maxima are upper records, and dominated hypervolume is an integral of their prefix maxima weighted by independent order-statistic gaps. Relative ranks through index \(k\) are independent of the prefix maximum. For a later index \(j=k+r\), conditioning on that maximum gives the exact record probability \((1-u^r)/r\) with \(u=F_Y(B_k)\). Each resulting covariance equals a negative multiple of the covariance of two nondecreasing functions of a \(\operatorname{Beta}(k,1)\) variable, strictly negative under nondegeneracy. The finite-sample formula, strict boundaries \(n\ge2\), and uniform harmonic reduction have been reconstructed. Independent exact-rational permutation enumeration confirms 54 finite model cases; 98 more exact comparisons confirm the uniform formula. These finite checks are not used as proofs of an infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, residual priority risk

Bai–Hwang–Liang–Tsai (2001, doi:10.1214/EJP.v6-76) established planar maxima marginal limit theory, including the rectangular record model. Hwang–Tsai–Chen (2011, arXiv:1111.6224v1) established skyline cardinality and generalized-dominance estimates. Bai–Devroye–Hwang–Tsai (2005, doi:10.1002/rsa.20053) concerns maxima marginal normal approximation and variance. The inspected primary statements do not yield the mixed covariance of Pareto-front cardinality and the *Lebesgue union area of all dominated rectangles*. A separate point-count-in-a-quadrant covariance does not imply the corresponding coverage-indicator covariance: \(\operatorname{Cov}(M,K)<0\) alone gives no sign for \(\operatorname{Cov}(M,\mathbf1\{K>0\})\), which is what area integration needs. The proof establishes the latter directly, including all product continuous marginals. The literature search is non-exhaustive; an implicit record-process identity remains the main risk.

## Value — PASS

Pareto-front cardinality measures the diversity of nondominated options; dominated hypervolume is a standard geometrical measure of objective-space coverage. Their two marginal expectations cannot determine dependence. The result resolves, under a natural product-sampling null model, whether a larger random Pareto front tends to correspond to more or less covered area in covariance, and supplies both a universal strict sign and a closed finite-\(n\) benchmark of order \((\log n)/n\). The model has a fixed origin and two independent objectives; extrapolation to dependent objectives or three dimensions is not justified.
