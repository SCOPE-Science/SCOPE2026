# Vandermonde witnesses make finite-dimensional dividing bounds linearly sharp

## Finding

In the symmetric generic-bilinear theory \(T_\infty\) of Dobrowolski and Hoffmann, fix the characteristic of the algebraically closed scalar field to be zero or odd. For every \(k\ge2\) and \(r\ge1\), there is a formula \(\varphi(x;b)\) with exactly \(r\) vector-sort coordinates in \(b\), and a finite base \(A\), such that an \(A\)-indiscernible witness to \(k\)-dividing is contained in a model of \(T_{r(k-1)}\). Every \(k-1\) instances along that witness are consistent, even inside the displayed finite-dimensional model. Every \(A\)-indiscernible witness to \(k\)-dividing for the same formula and parameter type has vector span of dimension at least \(r(k-1)\), whether or not the containing substructure is required to contain \(A\). Thus the exact minimum finite witness dimension for this family is \(r(k-1)\).

The general upper bound in Dobrowolski–Hoffmann, Proposition 4.4 (journal version), is \(2k|V(b)|\) over a countable base. The example shows that the linear order in \(k|V(b)|\) is unavoidable. In any universal asymptotic bound \(Ck|V(b)|+o(k|V(b)|)\), the constant \(C\) is at least \(1\); no optimality of the upper-bound constant \(2\) is asserted.

## Assumptions and construction

Use an algebraically closed scalar field \(K\) of the chosen characteristic with an infinite transcendence basis, and a model of \(T_\infty\) whose vector space has a countably infinite orthonormal basis for the symmetric form \([\cdot,\cdot]\). For \(1\le j\le r\), choose disjoint blocks \(e_{j,0},\ldots,e_{j,k-2}\) from this basis. Let \(E_j\) be the span of block \(j\), and let \(E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^r E_j\), of dimension \(r(k-1)\). In particular **the restriction of the form to \(E\) is nondegenerate**. Take \(A\) to be the finite set of all displayed basis vectors. Choose distinct \(t_i\), \(i<\omega\), from a transcendence basis of \(K\) over its prime field. Define
\[
v_j(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}t^\ell e_{j,\ell},\qquad b(t)=(v_1(t),\ldots,v_r(t),t),
\]
and use the formula, in vector variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\),
\[
\varphi(x;b(t))=\bigwedge_{j=1}^r [x_j,v_j(t)]=t^{k-1}.
\]
Thus \(|V(b)|=r\); the one scalar parameter does not count towards \(|V(b)|\) in the upper bound. The result concerns the symmetric theory just specified, not its possible characteristic-two analogues or the alternating theory.

## Proof

**Indiscernibility.** A permutation of the transcendence basis sending a given finite subtuple of distinct \(t_i\)'s to another extends to an automorphism of \(K\). Extend this to the vector sort by applying it to coordinates in the orthonormal basis and fixing each basis vector. This preserves the field and vector operations and the form, whose basis Gram entries are zero or one. The resulting automorphism fixes \(A\) and carries the first tuple of \(b(t_i)\)'s to the second. In particular the sequence is \(A\)-indiscernible, also when the model is embedded elementarily in a monster model.

**Exact inconsistency threshold.** For distinct scalars \(s_1,\ldots,s_q\) with \(q\le k-1\), the vectors \(v_j(s_a)\), \(1\le a\le q\), are linearly independent for each \(j\) by the Vandermonde determinant on the first \(q\) powers. Nondegeneracy of \(E\) makes the \(q\) linear functionals \(x\mapsto[x,v_j(s_a)]\) on \(E\) independent. The resulting map to \(K^q\) is surjective, so for each \(j\) we can choose \(x_j\in E\) attaining the targets \(s_a^{k-1}\). This proves the consistency assertion. For \(k\) distinct scalars, the polynomial
\[
P_j(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}[x_j,e_{j,\ell}]t^\ell-t^{k-1}
\]
would have to vanish at all \(k\) points. Its leading coefficient is \(-1\) and its degree is \(k-1\), a contradiction. Thus any \(k\) instances are inconsistent.

**Minimum dimension.** Consider any \(A\)-indiscernible witnessing sequence of realizations of \(\operatorname{tp}(b(t_0)/A)\). By indiscernibility its scalar coordinates are either all equal or pairwise distinct. If the scalar coordinates are all equal, the type equations for \(v_j(t)\) force the vector coordinates to be equal as well. The single repeated instance is consistent, so this cannot be a witness. Otherwise take the first \(k-1\) distinct scalars. In each block the Vandermonde matrix is invertible, expressing every \(e_{j,\ell}\) as a linear combination of the sequence's vector parameters with coefficients in the field generated by these scalars. Thus any finite-dimensional substructure containing the sequence contains these basis vectors, even if the definition does not require it to contain \(A\). Their independence forces dimension at least \(r(k-1)\). Conversely the substructure with vector sort \(E\) and field sort \(K\) contains the sequence. Its form is nondegenerate, so it is a model of \(T_{r(k-1)}\).

Taking \(k\) to infinity (and, if desired, \(r\) to infinity along with it), the ratio of the necessary dimension to \(kr\) tends to one. This gives the claimed lower bound on the leading constant.

## Verification

The packaged `verify.py` reports 198 exact rational rank assertions for \(2\le k\le10\), \(1\le r\le5\), using initial segments of the integer sample points \(0,\ldots,k-1\). The finite computations check coefficient versus augmented ranks and block dimension arithmetic. The general field statement, indiscernibility and minimum witness dimension follow from the proof above, not from those computations.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

The journal paper defines finite-dimensional dividing witnesses and proves the general sufficient bound \(2k|V(b)|\). The result here is a lower-bound family for that notion; varying the witness cannot evade the span constraint because the actual parameter coordinates generate the basis of \(E\). The proof does not improve the universal upper bound or decide its optimal leading constant. An uncatalogued interpolation-based antecedent remains possible.
