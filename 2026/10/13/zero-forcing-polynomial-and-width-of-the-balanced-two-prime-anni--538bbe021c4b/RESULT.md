# Zero forcing polynomial and width of the balanced two-prime annihilating-ideal graph

## Finding

Let \(p\ne q\) be primes and let \(G=\operatorname{AG}(\mathbb Z/(p^2q^2)\mathbb Z)\) be the standard annihilating-ideal graph. Then \[\mathcal Z(G;u)=4u^2+24u^3+33u^4+21u^5+7u^6+u^7,\qquad Z(G)=\operatorname{tw}(G)=\operatorname{pw}(G)=2.\] The four minimum zero forcing sets are exactly \(\{(q),(q^2)\}\), \(\{(q),(pq)\}\), \(\{(p),(pq)\}\), and \(\{(p),(p^2)\}\).

Thus the complete standard zero forcing enumerator is independent of the numerical values of the two distinct primes.

## Assumptions and scope

Let
\[
R=\mathbb Z/(p^2q^2)\mathbb Z,
\]
where \(p\ne q\) are primes. The annihilating-ideal graph \(G=\operatorname{AG}(R)\) has the nonzero annihilating ideals as vertices, with distinct ideals adjacent exactly when their product is zero.

Since \(R\) is Artinian, every nonzero proper ideal is a vertex. Write the ideal generated by
\[
p^i q^j
\]
as the exponent pair \((i,j)\). The vertex set is therefore
\[
V(G)=
\{(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}.
\]

## Proof

For exponent pairs \(x=(i,j)\) and \(y=(k,\ell)\),
\[
(p^i q^j)(p^kq^\ell)=(0)
\]
in \(R\) exactly when
\[
i+k\ge2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
j+\ell\ge2.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the graph has seven vertices and its adjacency is determined completely by (1), independently of \(p\) and \(q\).

The four claimed two-vertex sets have the following chronological forcing sequences, written in exponent-pair notation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{(0,1),(0,2)\}:&
\quad
(0,1)\to(2,1)\to(1,1),\\
&
\quad
(0,2)\to(2,0)\to(1,2)\to(1,0);
\end{aligned}
\]
equivalently, in chronological order,
\[
(0,1)\to(2,1),\ 
(0,2)\to(2,0),\ 
(2,0)\to(1,2),\ 
(2,1)\to(1,1),\ 
(1,2)\to(1,0).
\tag{2}
\]
The remaining three minimum sets force by the symmetric sequences recorded by the verifier.

Hence
\[
Z(G)\le2.
\]
The graph is not a path: the vertices \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\) each have degree four. Since a graph with standard zero forcing number one is a path, it follows that
\[
Z(G)=2.
\tag{3}
\]

For the decomposition widths, the following is a path decomposition:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_1&=\{(0,1)\},\\
B_2&=\{(0,1),(0,2)\},\\
B_3&=\{(0,1),(0,2),(2,1)\},\\
B_4&=\{(0,2),(2,0),(2,1)\},\\
B_5&=\{(1,2),(2,0),(2,1)\},\\
B_6&=\{(1,0),(1,2),(2,1)\},\\
B_7&=\{(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Every edge is contained in one of these bags and the bags containing any fixed vertex form a consecutive interval. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{pw}(G)\le2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
(0,2),(2,0),(1,2),(2,1)
\]
induce a four-cycle, so \(G\) is not a forest. Hence
\[
\operatorname{tw}(G)\ge2.
\]
Using
\[
\operatorname{tw}(G)\le\operatorname{pw}(G),
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{tw}(G)=\operatorname{pw}(G)=2.
\tag{5}
\]

It remains to count every zero forcing set. The standalone verifier enumerates each of the
\[
2^7=128
\]
vertex subsets, computes its deterministic zero forcing closure, and groups the successful sets by cardinality. It obtains
\[
z(G;2)=4,\quad
z(G;3)=24,\quad
z(G;4)=33,\quad
z(G;5)=21,\quad
z(G;6)=7,\quad
z(G;7)=1,
\]
and zero at smaller cardinalities. Therefore
\[
\mathcal Z(G;u)
=
4u^2+24u^3+33u^4+21u^5+7u^6+u^7.
\tag{6}
\]
The same exhaustive classification shows that the four sets displayed in the finding are all the minimum zero forcing sets.

## Verification

It independently builds the seven-vertex exponent-pair graph from (1), exhausts all \(128\) vertex subsets, verifies the complete polynomial (6), and checks the four minimum sets and a chronological forcing sequence for each.

## Relationship to prior work

Visweswaran and Parejiya classify commutative rings whose annihilating-ideal graphs have independence number at most four. Their article has primary Mathematics Subject Classification \(13A15\), and its accepted author version was posted online on 25 February 2016. The balanced product of two length-two prime-power factors lies naturally at this small-independence boundary, but the inspected full text contains no occurrence of “zero forcing,” “treewidth,” or “pathwidth.”

Nikandish, Maimani, and Izanloo study \(\operatorname{AG}(\mathbb Z_n)\) directly. They prove weak perfectness for all \(n\), give the general divisor-ideal representation, and work out a neighboring two-prime-power example. Their full article has graph-theoretic primary classification and does not compute zero forcing enumerators or decomposition widths.

General zero forcing polynomial theory defines and develops the counting invariant and gives formulas for several standard graph families. The inspected article contains no small-order database or table covering this seven-vertex annihilating-ideal graph. A current graph-statistics database records conventional zero forcing number as a graph invariant, so the bare numerical value \(Z(G)=2\) is not used as the originality basis. The accepted content is the arithmetic identification, the complete polynomial and minimum-set classification, and the exact width certificates.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the standard annihilating-ideal graph of the balanced exponent pattern \(p^2q^2\).

The graph depends only on the exponent pattern, so neither the polynomial nor the widths recover the numerical prime values.

The polynomial coefficients are established by complete finite enumeration after a symbolic seven-vertex reduction; no closed formula for arbitrary exponent pairs \(p^a q^b\) is asserted.

A general graph database may contain the numerical zero forcing number of an isomorphic unlabeled seven-vertex graph. The complete zero forcing polynomial and ring-specific identification were not located in the checked sources.
