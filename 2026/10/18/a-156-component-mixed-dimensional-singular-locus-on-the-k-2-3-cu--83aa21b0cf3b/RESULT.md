# A 156-component mixed-dimensional singular locus on the \(K_{2,3}\) cut sixfold

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Let \(P\) be the cut polytope of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{2,3}\), in the affine lattice generated by its cut vertices, and let \(X_P\) be the associated **normal-fan** projective toric sixfold. It is also the normal projective cut variety in the standard sixteen cut coordinates.

Write \(A=\{(\sigma,0):\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^3\}\) and \(B=\{(0,\tau):\tau\in\{\pm1\}^3\}\) in \(\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^3\). In the affine integral coordinates specified below, \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A\cup B)\). Let \(E_A,E_B\) range independently over the twelve edges of the two cubes, and let \(S\) range over their twelve square faces (six from each cube). With \(V(F)\) denoting the torus-orbit closure indexed by a face \(F\) of \(P\), the **whole reduced singular locus** is
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_P)_{\mathrm{red}}
 =\bigcup_{E_A,E_B}V(\operatorname{conv}(E_A\cup E_B))
 \;\cup\!\bigcup_S V(S).
\]
The union has exactly **156 distinct irreducible components**: 144 threefolds isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^3\) and twelve surfaces isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). Generic transverse germs along the threefolds are ordinary threefold nodes \(uv-wz=0\); those along the surfaces are affine cones over the Segre-embedded \((\mathbb P^1)^3\subset\mathbb P^7\). In particular the singular locus is non-equidimensional, with components of codimensions three and four, and the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus has the same support. All statements hold in every characteristic.

## Assumptions and scope
Choose the two left vertices of \(K_{2,3}\) to be \(L_0,L_1\), and the three right vertices to be \(R_1,R_2,R_3\). Normalize a cut by declaring the membership bit of \(L_0\) to be zero, and write \(s\in\{0,1\}\) for the bit of \(L_1\) and \(r_i\in\{0,1\}\) for that of \(R_i\). In six edge coordinates the cut vectors are
\[
(u_1,u_2,u_3,v_1,v_2,v_3)
 =(r_1,r_2,r_3,\ s\oplus r_1,s\oplus r_2,s\oplus r_3).
\]
Here \(\oplus\) is binary exclusive-or. There are sixteen distinct vertices; their differences span a rank-six lattice. Apply the integral affine change of real coordinates \(y_i=u_i+v_i-1\), \(z_i=u_i-v_i\). It maps the cuts with \(s=0\) to \(A\) and the cuts with \(s=1\) to \(B\). Their vertex-difference lattice becomes
\[
M=\{(y_1,y_2,y_3,z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb Z^6:
 y_1\equiv\cdots\equiv z_3\pmod2\}.
\]
Differences inside \(A\) generate \(2e_{y_i}\), those inside \(B\) generate \(2e_{z_i}\), and one cross-cube difference has all six coordinates odd; this proves the displayed intrinsic parity-lattice formula. The coordinate change is not asserted to be unimodular in ambient \(\mathbb Z^6\); **this intrinsic lattice is essential** to all smoothness and local-germ statements. Its dual lattice is
\[
N=\{q\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z^6:\textstyle\sum_iq_i\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The following proof treats the normal projective toric variety attached to the polytope, not the toric variety of its polar and not a projective model formed using the larger ambient edge lattice.

## Proof

**1. Polytope and faces.** The two three-cubes lie in complementary coordinate subspaces, each containing zero in its interior. Thus their convex hull is their free sum. A direct supporting-functional calculation gives precisely thirty-six facets
\[
\epsilon y_i+\delta z_j\le1,
\qquad 1\le i,j\le3,\quad \epsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
Indeed the restriction to each cube must have maximum at most one, and a facet of their free sum joins a square face of each cube. Every proper face is either one proper face of a single cube, or the join of proper faces \(F_A,F_B\) of the two cubes. If \(\dim F_A=a\), \(\dim F_B=b\), its join has dimension \(a+b+1\), where \(a,b\in\{0,1,2\}\). A single-cube face has dimension \(a\in\{0,1,2\}\).

A cube has eight vertices, twelve edges, and six square faces. Hence the face numbers in dimensions zero through six are
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)
 =(16,88,204,240,144,36,1).
\]
For example, the 240 three-faces comprise 144 edge–edge joins and 96 vertex–square joins. The twelve unmixed square faces are the other distinguished candidates.

**2. Intrinsic primitive normal rays.** In the lattice \(N\), the primitive outward normal to the facet indexed by \((i,j,\epsilon,\delta)\), up to the harmless common choice of inward or outward sign, is
\[
\rho_{ij}^{\epsilon,\delta}
 =\tfrac12(\epsilon e_{y_i}+\delta e_{z_j}).
\]
Its primitivity follows by pairing with \(2e_{y_i}\in M\), which gives \(\epsilon\). For a mixed face of type \((a,b)\), exactly \((3-a)(3-b)\) facet normals are active, while its normal cone has dimension \(5-a-b\). Thus the cone is nonsimplicial when \((a,b)\) is \((0,0)\), \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\), or \((1,1)\). In contrast, the types \((0,2)\), \((2,0)\), \((1,2)\), \((2,1)\), and \((2,2)\) have the number of rays equal to cone dimension.

For type \((0,2)\), after changing signs, the three rays are \(\rho_{1j},\rho_{2j},\rho_{3j}\) for one fixed \(j\). The pairing map from \(M\) to \(\mathbb Z^3\) is onto: take the relevant \(y_i\) coordinates to be twice any desired integers and all other coordinates zero. Therefore the rays form part of an integral basis of \(N\) and the cone is smooth. Its faces, which include the normal cones of types \((1,2)\) and \((2,2)\), are smooth. The \((2,0)\) case is symmetric.

A single-cube face of dimension \(a\) has \(6(3-a)\) active primitive rays and a normal cone of dimension \(6-a\); for each \(a\le2\) it is nonsimplicial. Every single-cube face lies in some square face of that cube; every remaining nonsimplicial mixed face lies in an edge–edge join. The latter joins (dimension three) and the single-cube squares (dimension two) are mutually maximal among nonsmooth faces. By the toric orbit criterion for smoothness and the orbit–face closure order, their orbit closures are **exactly** the irreducible components of the reduced singular support. This proves the count \(12^2+2\cdot6=156\). Since all and only singular cones are nonsimplicial, the support also equals the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus.

**3. Geometry of the components.** A join of two cube edges has four affinely independent cut vertices. It is an intrinsic-lattice unimodular tetrahedron: relative to any of its first-cube vertices, its three edge-difference generators are \(2e_{y_i}\), \(2e_{z_j}\), and an odd-parity vector connecting the two cubes. Any point of \(M\) in their real span has integral coefficients in these three generators, because its nonvarying coordinates determine the coefficient of the odd vector and the parity condition determines the other two. Hence its orbit closure has the fan of the standard lattice tetrahedron and is \(\mathbb P^3\). An unmixed square is an intrinsic-lattice unit square (the edge steps are \(2e_i\) in its saturated affine sublattice), so its orbit closure is \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). Distinct faces have distinct dense torus orbits, so the 156 components are pairwise distinct.

**4. Generic transverse affine toric germs.** For an edge–edge join, normalize the two fixed coordinate signs in each cube. The four active normal rays are the grid \(\rho_{ip}\), \(\rho_{iq}\), \(\rho_{jp}\), \(\rho_{jq}\), satisfying
\[
\rho_{ip}+\rho_{jq}=\rho_{iq}+\rho_{jp}.
\]
Modulo the tangent lattice of its orbit, a dual lattice point has integer pairings \(h_{ab}\ge0\) with these four rays. The image is **all** nonnegative integer \(2\times2\) tables satisfying \(h_{11}+h_{22}=h_{12}+h_{21}\): the parity-lattice condition makes the pairings integral, and any integer table with the relation has an even-coordinate preimage. This semigroup is generated by its two row indicators and two column indicators, with the single relation that their two respective sums coincide. Its semigroup ring is \(k[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\). This is the normal transverse threefold node, independently of characteristic.

For a square face in the first cube, the six rays are \(\rho_{ij}^{+,+}\), \(\rho_{ij}^{+,-}\) for \(j=1,2,3\), up to sign changes. Its dual semigroup, after dividing out the orbit-direction lattice, is
\[
\{(a,b_1,b_2,b_3)\in\mathbb Z^4:
 a\equiv b_1\equiv b_2\equiv b_3\pmod2,
 \ a\ge |b_j|\text{ for each }j\}.
\]
Every element is a sum of \(a\) sign vectors \((1,\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)\), because each \(b_j\) can be written as the sum of \(a\) signs, independently across \(j\). These eight degree-one generators give the homogeneous Segre coordinate ring of \((\mathbb P^1)^3\). Thus the transverse singularity is its four-dimensional affine cone. The other cube is symmetric. Local toric product decompositions along each dense orbit give the claimed generic transverse germs.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `check_cut23.py` constructs the sixteen cut vertices in free-sum coordinates and the thirty-six facet supports; closes them under intersections, obtaining exactly 729 nonempty faces; verifies the face vector, all active-facet counts, all singular face dimensions, and maximal nonsimplicial faces. It checks both combinatorial families and the primitive-ray parallelogram identities. Run with `python3 check_cut23.py`; the expected last lines begin with `VERIFY_OK` and report 144 three-faces and twelve square faces. The script was executed from this package after construction. The general lattice-semigroup proofs above, not the finite enumeration, establish the statements for every algebraically closed field.

## Relationship to prior work
Sturmfels–Sullivant (first public preprint 2006-06-27), *Toric geometry of cuts and splits*, defines these projective cut varieties; its computations include nineteen quadratic generators and degree 72 for \(K_{2,3}\), and its Corollary 2.4 characterizes when cut varieties are smooth. Ohsugi's *Gorenstein cut polytopes* (2013) determines a much broader Gorenstein criterion. Chimani–Juhnke-Kubitzke–Nover–Römer (2019) classifies simple and simplicial cut polytopes, explicitly warning that the ambient edge lattice can differ from the cut-vertex lattice. Sakamoto (2020) determines the full Ehrhart \(h^*\)-polynomial for \(K_{2,m}\), including \(K_{2,3}\). These results motivate and constrain the computation but do not, in the material inspected, state the 156-component reduced singular support or distinguish its two generic transverse cone types. The present claim is **not** a new cut-ideal generator count, degree, Hilbert series, normality theorem, Gorenstein criterion, or global smoothness criterion.

## Limitations
This determines the **reduced** singular support and the analytic/étale-local toric type at each component's generic point. It does not determine the scheme structure of the Jacobian singular subscheme, analytic singularities along component intersections, conductor ideals, or a resolution. The originality comparison is best-knowledge rather than an exhaustive theorem of absence: implicit coverage in other treatments of free sums, cube-cut varieties, or lattice toric singularities remains a literature risk.
