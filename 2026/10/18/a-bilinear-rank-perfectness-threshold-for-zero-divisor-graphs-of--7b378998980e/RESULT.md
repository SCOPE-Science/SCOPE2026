# A bilinear-rank perfectness threshold for zero-divisor graphs of short local rings

## Finding

Let \(R\) be a finite commutative local ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) such that \(\mathfrak m^3=0\) and \(\dim_k\mathfrak m^2=1\), where \(k=R/\mathfrak m=\mathbb F_q\). Multiplication defines a symmetric bilinear form
\[
 B:(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\times(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\longrightarrow\mathfrak m^2\cong k.
\]
Write \(r=\dim_k\operatorname{rad} B\), \(t=\operatorname{rank}B\), \(c=q^{r+1}-1\) and \(b=q^{r+1}(q-1)\). Necessarily \(t\ge1\). Define the ordinary simple zero-divisor graph \(\Gamma(R)\) to have all nonzero elements of \(\mathfrak m\) as vertices, with distinct vertices adjacent exactly when their product is zero.

**Perfectness dichotomy.** The graph \(\Gamma(R)\) is perfect if and only if \(t\le2\). If \(t\ge3\), it contains an explicitly constructible induced five-cycle, even when \(R\) has mixed characteristic or a degenerate multiplication form.

The perfect cases have complete decompositions:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 t=1:&\quad \Gamma(R)\cong K_c\vee\overline{K_b},\\
 t=2:&\quad \Gamma(R)\cong K_c\vee\bigl(\alpha K_b\ \sqcup\ \beta K_{b,b}\bigr),\qquad \beta=(q+1-\alpha)/2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\vee\) is graph join and \(\alpha\) is the number of isotropic points of the nondegenerate two-dimensional symmetric quotient form. For odd \(q\), \(\alpha=2\) in the split case and \(\alpha=0\) in the anisotropic case. For even \(q\), \(\alpha=q+1\) in the alternating case and \(\alpha=1\) otherwise. In particular, in every perfect case \(\chi(\Gamma(R))=\omega(\Gamma(R))\); their common value is \(c+1\) for \(t=1\), \(c+b\) for \(t=2,\alpha>0\), and \(c+2\) for \(t=2,\alpha=0\).

Among rings in this family with nonperfect zero-divisor graph, the smallest possible order is \(32\), attained over \(\mathbb F_2\) by the rank-three diagonal multiplication form.

## Assumptions and scope

No coefficient-field embedding, regularity of the multiplication form, restriction on the characteristic, or restriction on the radical dimension is assumed. The square ideal is nonzero and one-dimensional over the residue field. The graph uses the Anderson–Livingston convention excluding zero and all units. Perfect means that every induced subgraph has clique number equal to chromatic number.

Fixing an identification of the one-dimensional \(k\)-space \(\mathfrak m^2\) with \(k\) rescales \(B\), but leaves the rank, isotropic points and zero-product relation unchanged.

## Proof

**Step 1: the intrinsic bilinear model.** Since \(\mathfrak m^3=0\), the product of two elements of \(\mathfrak m\) depends only on their residue classes modulo \(\mathfrak m^2\). Scalar multiplication by \(k\) on both quotient spaces is well-defined, and multiplication induces the displayed symmetric \(k\)-bilinear map. Because \(\mathfrak m^2\) is generated by products of elements of \(\mathfrak m\), the form is not identically zero, so \(t\ge1\). Its radical lifts to the socle \(\operatorname{Ann}_R(\mathfrak m)\), which has \(q^{r+1}\) elements: \(r\) coordinates from the form's radical and one from \(\mathfrak m^2\). Thus the \(c\) nonzero socle elements are precisely the universal vertices. Any non-socle vertex has some nonzero product with an element of \(\mathfrak m\) outside its own vertex, so it is not universal (also follows directly from the nondegenerate quotient model and \(t\ge1\)).

Let \(W=(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)/\operatorname{rad}B\) be the nondegenerate quotient of dimension \(t\). The product of vertices outside the socle vanishes exactly when their nonzero images in \(W\) are orthogonal. Each projective point \([w]\) of \(\mathbb P(W)\) lifts to precisely \(b=q^{r+1}(q-1)\) vertices. The class of \([w]\) is a clique if \(B(w,w)=0\), and an independent set otherwise. Two distinct classes are completely joined if and only if their projective points are orthogonal.

**Step 2: ranks one and two.** When \(t=1\), a nonzero vector is never orthogonal to another nonzero vector, producing an independent set of \(b\) outside the universal clique. For \(t=2\), perpendicularity maps each point of the projective line to one point and is an involution. Its fixed points are precisely the isotropic points. Therefore every fixed point contributes one complete component \(K_b\), and every transposed pair contributes one complete bipartite component \(K_{b,b}\). Over odd fields, a nondegenerate binary quadratic form has either zero or two projective zeros; over even fields, an alternating form has all \(q+1\) points isotropic, while a nonalternating form has exactly one (its diagonal quadratic polynomial is the square of a nonzero linear form). This proves the decompositions. Complete and complete bipartite graphs are perfect; disjoint union and joining a clique preserve perfectness. Their maximum clique and coloring formulas follow immediately.

**Step 3: a five-cycle in every higher rank.** First suppose that the nondegenerate form on \(W\) admits three mutually orthogonal vectors \(e_1,e_2,e_3\), each of nonzero self-pairing. Put \(a=B(e_1,e_1)\), \(d=B(e_2,e_2)\), \(c_0=B(e_3,e_3)\), all nonzero. The five vectors, in cyclic order,
\[
 e_3,\quad e_2,\quad e_1+e_3,\quad (c_0/a)e_1+e_2-e_3,\quad e_1-(c_0/d)e_2
\]
have consecutive pairings zero; their five nonconsecutive pairings are, up to ordering, \(c_0,-c_0,d,-c_0,a\), all nonzero. They therefore lift to an induced five-cycle in \(\Gamma(R)\). All five vectors represent distinct projective points.

Every nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form over a field of odd characteristic has an orthogonal basis of nonisotropic vectors, so this applies whenever \(t\ge3\) in odd characteristic. In characteristic two, it also applies to any *nonalternating* nondegenerate symmetric form of rank at least three: split off a nonisotropic line repeatedly; if an alternating two-plane remains, combine that plane with one nonisotropic line. For example, with \(B(e,e)=B(u,v)=1\), and all other pairings among \(e,u,v\) zero, the vectors \(e+u,e+v,e+u+v\) are mutually orthogonal with self-pairing one. Field scalings reduce the general nonzero coefficients to this example.

It remains to consider alternating nondegenerate forms in characteristic two. Then \(t\) is even, and \(t\ge3\) implies \(t\ge4\). Choose two hyperbolic pairs \(e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2\), with \(B(e_i,f_i)=1\) and all other basis pairings zero. The following five vectors, in cyclic order, have pairings zero exactly on cycle edges and one on every nonedge:
\[
 f_2,\quad f_1,\quad e_2,\quad e_1+f_1+e_2,\quad e_1+f_2.
\]
Their lifts again induce \(C_5\). An induced \(C_5\) is not perfect, since its chromatic number is three while its clique number is two. This proves the converse.

**Step 4: sharp order.** The length filtration gives \(|R|=q^{r+t+2}\). Nonperfectness forces \(t\ge3\), hence \(|R|\ge2^5=32\). Equality is attained by \(R=\mathbb F_2\oplus(\mathbb F_2^3)\oplus\mathbb F_2z\), with \(z\mathfrak m=0\) and \(e_ie_j=\delta_{ij}z\); adjoin the indicated unit and let multiplication by scalars be ordinary. This is a commutative associative local ring, its maximal ideal has cube zero and square dimension one, and the displayed diagonal five-cycle occurs.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone script `verify.py` constructs split square-zero-extension rings through their maximal-ideal multiplication tables over prime residue fields. It explicitly enumerates every nonzero zero divisor and every pairwise product. In thirteen independent configurations, it checks universal-vertex counts, all low-rank component decompositions, and the presence of an induced \(C_5\) for higher rank. The suite includes both characteristic-two bilinear-form types, odd-characteristic split and anisotropic binary forms, and forms with nontrivial radical. Running `python verify.py` produced `VERIFY_OK 13 independent configurations`.

These finite tests check representative instances only. The theorem for arbitrary finite residue fields, mixed-characteristic local rings, unbounded radical dimension, and unbounded rank is established by the preceding symbolic proof, not by extrapolation from computation.

## Relationship to prior work

Asensio, García-Marco and Knauer, *Rings are χ-bounded* (arXiv:2609.17401, first posted September 15, 2026), show a general chromatic bound for associative rings and construct short commutative rings from vector-valued alternating bilinear maps. Their Section 3.1 provides the multiplication-to-orthogonality relationship, but not this one-dimensional-square-ideal perfectness threshold or the rank-two graph decomposition.

Arunkumar, Cameron, Kavaskar and Tamizh Chelvam, *Induced subgraphs of zero-divisor graphs* (arXiv:2207.11741), show that local-ring zero-divisor graphs realize arbitrary finite induced subgraphs, while principal-maximal-ideal rings have threshold graphs. This does not characterize perfectness among the one-dimensional-square-ideal rings considered here.

The extraspecial-group commuting-graph literature establishes a similar symplectic rank threshold. Its alternating-form case parallels a subcase of the present construction; it does not address nonalternating forms in odd or even characteristic, the radical-lift structure of local rings, or the full low-rank decomposition. Prior small-order local-ring classifications may cover individual examples, including size \(32\).

## Limitations

The assertion concerns perfectness, not equality of the clique and chromatic numbers for the full graph in every high-rank case. The theorem makes no statement about rings with \(\dim_k\mathfrak m^2>1\) or \(\mathfrak m^3\ne0\), and does not claim that the graph determines the ring up to isomorphism. High-rank five-cycles are constructed explicitly, but no complete higher-rank graph spectrum is determined. Independent external validation has not been performed.
