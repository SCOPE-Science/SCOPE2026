# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The hypothesis \(\mathfrak m^3=0\) makes multiplication factor through a symmetric residue-field bilinear form, and one-dimensionality of \(\mathfrak m^2\) makes that form scalar-valued. Its nondegenerate quotient controls adjacency outside the socle. A projective-line perpendicularity involution gives the entire rank-two graph. For higher rank, the proof supplies five vectors with all ten orthogonality decisions explicitly checked, treating nonalternating forms and characteristic-two alternating forms separately. These deductions work without a coefficient-field embedding. The thirteen finite test configurations independently confirm the predicted component structures and five-cycle existence.

The only external mathematical ingredients are standard finite-field bilinear-form linear algebra and the elementary closure properties of perfect graphs under clique joins and disjoint unions. The symbolic proof, rather than the finite checks, establishes unrestricted parameter ranges.

## Originality — PASS, self-review

Asensio–García-Marco–Knauer (arXiv:2609.17401, Section 3.1) use vector-valued alternating bilinear forms to build rings with large chromatic-number gaps; their explicit zero-product model motivates the question but does not present the perfectness threshold for a one-dimensional square ideal or the complete binary-rank decomposition. Arunkumar–Cameron–Kavaskar–Tamizh Chelvam (arXiv:2207.11741, Section 2.3) show unrestricted local-ring induced-subgraph universality and principal-ideal threshold graphs, not the rank threshold for this intermediate family.

A published mathematical result on extraspecial-group commuting graphs already covers an analogous *alternating/symplectic* perfectness boundary; that partial overlap is not claimed as independent discovery here. The general local-ring statement also encompasses odd-characteristic symmetric and characteristic-two nonalternating forms and arbitrary radicals; it is not implied by that group-theoretic result. A prior classification of individual local-ring graphs of orders \(p^4\) and \(p^5\) may contain the smallest example without covering the unrestricted theorem. An earlier algebraic-graph result reconstructs graded socle-two Gorenstein rings from their zero-divisor graph degree multisets; its degree formulas neither establish perfectness of every rank-two graph nor exhibit the five-cycle obstruction uniformly at all higher ranks. Another related result classifies bilinear forms vanishing on zero-product pairs, a different invariant.

Residual risk: an exact or stronger perfectness criterion under the terminology of orthogonality graphs, Gorenstein local algebras, or polar graphs could exist in literature not found by these searches; the 2013 small-order paper was available only by abstract.

## Value — PASS

The result draws a sharp structural boundary within finite short local rings, a natural class interpolating between principal-ideal threshold graphs and general local-ring graph universality. Rank at most two gives a complete decomposition and exact coloring formulas; rank three immediately forces a five-cycle, with the first possible nonperfect ring in this class of order \(32\). The distinction between the alternating and nonalternating cases and the absence of a coefficient-field assumption are substantive structural features rather than arbitrary finite parameter slices.

## Limitations

The theorem does not determine chromatic numbers in higher rank and does not apply when the square of the maximal ideal has dimension greater than one. Independent external validation has not occurred.
