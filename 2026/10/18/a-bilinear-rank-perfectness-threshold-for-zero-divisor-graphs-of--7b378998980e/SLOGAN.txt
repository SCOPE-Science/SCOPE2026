For every finite commutative local ring with \(\mathfrak m^3=0\) and one-dimensional nonzero \(\mathfrak m^2\), its zero-divisor graph is perfect exactly when the multiplication pairing on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) has rank at most two. At rank two the whole graph splits into a universal clique joined to projective-point cliques and paired complete bipartite components; from rank three onward an explicit induced five-cycle always exists.
