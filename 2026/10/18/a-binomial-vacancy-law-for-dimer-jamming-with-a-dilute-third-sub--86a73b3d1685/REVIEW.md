# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A full derivation identifies the exponential/edge-priority embedded chain, bounds survival of each rare-part vertex by a telescoping product, and couples all rare-part attachment choices to independent fair coins. The terminal unmatched count is exactly the absolute binomial imbalance on the good coupling event. The scaling follows from the elementary explicit bound and binomial CLT. Finite exact state computations and an independent labeled-vertex enumeration verify small systems and the bound. The bound is not claimed optimal.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk.** The same random-edge greedy algorithm appears in Dyer–Frieze (1991), but its identified primary subject is general performance, especially sparse graphs and forests; nothing accessible establishes this complete-tripartite dilute-class distribution. Turban's full 2019 preprint analyzes complete one-type substrates and adsorption times, not three-color vacancy defects. Arnosti's full 2021 manuscript concerns bipartite random graphs rather than a complete tripartite fixed substrate. Published mathematical-result index and topical searches for complete-tripartite dimer adsorption, random greedy matching, binomial defects, and equivalent random-walk/half-normal statements found no dominating theorem. A potentially relevant old general-matching paper remained inaccessible in full, which limits certainty; failure to locate an equivalent is not proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The question probes robustness of perfect dimer packing on a fully connected balanced two-class substrate after adding a small third class. The perfect-matchable \(K_{n,n,2}\) has a limiting \(1/2\) chance of leaving two vertices uncovered, an effect not visible in limiting coverage fraction. The distributional theorem for every fixed \(r\), an effective convergence estimate, and the half-normal dilute crossover explain the mechanism beyond a one-off example. This concerns nonequilibrium jamming, not a random matching optimization guarantee or trivial parity test.

**Remaining scientific limits.** Full text of Dyer–Frieze (1991) was not accessible. Other synonyms and specialized unpublished work could contain equivalent results. The geometry is graph-based, uniform rates are essential to the stated probability law, and growing \(r\) is restricted to \(o(n^{1/3})\) for the Gaussian statement.
