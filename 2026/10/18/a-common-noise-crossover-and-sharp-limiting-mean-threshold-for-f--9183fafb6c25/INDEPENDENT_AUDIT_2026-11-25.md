Independent Gaussian maxima at each positive mesh point divided by √(2 log N) converge to √t by the two-sided normal tail asymptotics. The Brownian reflection bound plus the union bound over N paths and a fixed mesh gives maximum normalized increments ≤ε with probability tending to one when mesh width δ<ε². This also handles t=0 and proves uniform convergence in probability on compacts. The exact minimum process is ℓ+√(2Dρ_N)B₀−2√(Dq_N)Y_N. If q_N→a<∞ then ρ_N→1 and the limit starts positive and hits in finite time, no later than the ordinary Brownian hit. Strong Markov and local Brownian downward oscillations at the first hit dominate the local O(h) barrier increment, ensuring continuity of the first-crossing map. In the divergent regime, rescaling time by ℓ²/(4Dq_N) gives a common noise coefficient at most (2q_N)⁻¹ᐟ² and the uniform deterministic limit 1−√u, whose crossing is strict. For survival of T_a set b=√(2a), c=ℓ/√(2D); logarithmic time makes the boundary b−ce^(−s/2) for the stationary OU process. The killed generator is unitarily equivalent to the Dirichlet confining Schrödinger operator −∂²/2+x²/8−1/4. This gives discrete spectrum and a positive ground state. Moving the wall gives continuous eigenvalues via translation and the variational principle, and strict domain monotonicity follows because a zero extension cannot be the positive ground state on the larger domain. The upper survival bound uses the fixed wall b−ε from a sufficiently late time. The lower bound starts at a finite time in a compact interval above b, with positive prior survival probability (use a Brownian tube around a steep linear path starting at zero and staying strictly above b√r−c); the initial surviving submeasure is dominated by the stationary Gaussian and has positive ground-state overlap. Killing at b then gives a bound C t^(−λ(b)), which is essential at the critical exponent. Lower and upper bounds yield the claimed logarithmic exponent. On (1,∞), x²−1 is a positive Dirichlet eigenfunction with eigenvalue 1, so monotonicity gives finite mean precisely at b>1, including divergence at equality.
