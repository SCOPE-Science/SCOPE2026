# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies every admissible upper index exactly as \(1\le k\le\lfloor\beta n\rfloor\), derives the crucial exact congruence \(\{v(k)\alpha\}=\beta\{k\alpha\}\), and partitions all nonboundary phases into three exhaustive cases. The rotation discrepancy bound follows from bounded continued-fraction coefficients and Fibonacci-block decomposition. The uniform threshold error treats the plateau separately, avoiding the apparent large boundary interval at the distributional atom. Saturation outside the plateau is confined to two shrinking phase windows and is negligible with logarithmic-squared error. Integer-arithmetic checks compare two distinct counting implementations. Remaining mathematical scope limitations are stated in the result.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Ashburn (arXiv:2610.04842v1), Section 3, classifies the counts zero, one and two but does not provide this macroscopic limiting law. Shallit (arXiv:2006.04177v1), Section 11.2, gives a general rank-19 pointwise evaluation procedure, but not an empirical probability law, positive saturation density, or a uniform error bound. OEIS A259598 presents the sequence, generating function and rare-count conjectures, not the limiting measure. Targeted queries for equivalent distribution, Beatty convolution and saturation statements did not identify a covering mathematical result. A prior indirect derivation via the rank-19 representation or other unpublished work remains possible; mathematical database search is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The distribution of the entire naturally normalized additive representation function is distinct from classifying the rare counts zero, one and two. An explicit positive-mass atom at complete saturation is an unexpected structural feature of the arithmetic convolution, and the uniform \(O((\log X)^2)\) counting-error theorem makes the phenomenon quantitative rather than simply numerical. The result is a complete continuous-and-atomic law for a classical natural invariant, with an independent elementary proof based on rotation discrepancy.
