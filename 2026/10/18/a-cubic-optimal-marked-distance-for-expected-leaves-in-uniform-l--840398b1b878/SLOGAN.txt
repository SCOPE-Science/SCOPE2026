In a uniform labeled Cayley tree, the expected **total** number of leaves conditioned on the distance between two marked vertices peaks at a distance of order \((2n)^{1/3}\), even though a typical marked distance is of order \(\sqrt n\). The exact finite optimizer is determined by the sign change of \(2(n-1)-k(k+1)^2\), including infinitely many two-distance ties, and its excess over the unconditioned leaf mean tends to \(e^{-1}\).
