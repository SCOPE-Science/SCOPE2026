# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A facet corresponds exactly to a centered integral potential with a connected tight-edge subgraph. Cactus blocks therefore give independent edge-gradient words: symmetric bridge signs, balanced full signs on even cycles, and balanced signs with one zero on odd cycles. Their exact covariances are \(1\), \(\frac r{r-1}(I-J/r)\), and \(I-J/r\), respectively. The cactus cycle space splits orthogonally, so these covariances equal \(CP\) for the gradient-cut projection. The pseudoinverse calculation proves \(\operatorname{Cov}(U)=L_c^+\) in all dimensions, and the weighted Matrix--Tree Theorem proves the determinant. Exact enumeration with a separate code path corroborates ten finite examples, but is not claimed to certify the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks

The closest full primary text, Braun–Bruegge (arXiv:2201.13303), already supplies the facet criterion, cycle classifications, and their counts; those are treated strictly as prerequisites. It does not state the facet-normal covariance as a modified Laplacian Green kernel, its even-cycle resistance correction, the determinant product, or graph reconstruction. The geometric and Lipschitz-free sources arXiv:1810.11278 and arXiv:1911.10642 and the cactus-related Ehrhart source arXiv:2008.08621 concern different consequences. Related stochastic graph-height and electrical-network formulations may contain an unindexed equivalent; bibliographic uniqueness is not independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem turns exponential facet enumeration into a single sparse inverse-Laplacian computation. Its exact resistance law, parity-sensitive edge correction, graph-recovery property, and cycle-multiset determinant invariant are structural, motivated invariants of an established family of metric Banach balls rather than isolated numerical experiments. It provides second-moment information unavailable from counts alone and a natural link between polyhedral and electrical geometry.

## Boundaries

Unweighted cactus graphs, uniform facet measure, real scalars and canonically centered normals. No statement for overlapping cycles or arbitrary edge weights. No third-party verification is asserted.
