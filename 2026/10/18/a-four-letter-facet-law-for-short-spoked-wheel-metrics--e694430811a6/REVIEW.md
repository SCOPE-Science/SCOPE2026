# Review: A four-letter facet law for short-spoked wheel metrics

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof fixes the normalized metric exactly and identifies the dual Lipschitz polytope. Active graph-edge incidences have rank equal to the vertex count minus the number of tight connected components, giving an exact and exhaustive vertex criterion. For strictly short spokes, all rim coordinates in a connected tight graph must be in \(\{r,r-1,-r,1-r\}\). There is no additional possible edge tightness beyond the two asserted pair types. The local four-label rules are necessary and sufficient for connectedness. A \(14\)-state transfer matrix therefore counts all facets without rotational identification. Its exact characteristic polynomial was checked using integer Newton identities, and independent exact-rational active-basis enumeration recovered all vertices in small cases. The proof covers all \(m\ge4\), not only those tested.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to stated residual risk.** D'Alì, Delucchi and Michałek, *Many faces of symmetric edge polytopes*, arXiv:1910.05193v1, §4.3, Proposition 4.22, obtain the known **unweighted** wheel count, whose \(m=4\) value is \(26\); it does not give the strictly intermediate spoke-ratio value \(38\). Chen, Davis and Korchevskaia, arXiv:2107.12315v1, study fixed unweighted edge-root adjacency polytopes. Braun and Bruegge, arXiv:2201.13303v1, likewise study counts for unweighted sparse graphs. Jevtić, Timotijević and Živaljević, arXiv:1812.00397v1, Theorem 1.1, study weighted **cycles without a hub**. An accessible contemporary complete-multipartite Lipschitz-free facet result requires *all* cross-part distances equal, which fails for the present \(m=4\) metric and generally for its wheel deformation. The closest scientific claims, equivalent descriptions, metric reductions, and publicly indexed research were compared by implication, not only by their titles. Equivalents outside the inspected literature remain possible.

## Value

**PASS.** The exact number of facets is a natural Banach-space invariant controlling the extreme norming functionals and the polyhedral complexity of finite optimal-transport geometry. The wheel is a canonical family treated in a published paper; varying its single spoke-to-rim ratio is a geometrically motivated deformation, not an arbitrary slice. A stable new interior combinatorial type exhibits both endpoint jumps and a distinct exponential growth base, determined from a nontrivial tight-graph classification and constant-size transfer determinant. Only the interior result is presented as new; the endpoint and general rank facts are acknowledged known.

## Remaining limitations

No claim is made for nonuniform spoke profiles, higher face counts, or complex scalar fields. Exact finite tests do not establish the infinite theorem separately from the analytic argument. Indexing may miss an equivalent weighted-wheel result. This work has not received independent mathematical validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
