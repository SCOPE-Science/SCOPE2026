# A four-world convex-neighborhood separation of the binary inquisitive conditional

## Finding

The binary strict inquisitive neighborhood conditional \(\Rrightarrow\) is **not definable** from the universal and existential unary neighborhood modalities \(\boxplus\) and \(\mathsf E\), even when all models are required to have convex families of nonempty neighborhoods. This gives a negative answer to the second question of Open Problem 4.6 in Ciardelli's *Inquisitive Neighborhood Logic* [1].

More precisely, write \(\mathcal L_{\boxplus,\mathsf E}\) for the inquisitive propositional language generated by propositional letters, falsum, conjunction, inquisitive disjunction, implication, and arbitrarily nested instances of \(\boxplus\) and \(\mathsf E\). There exist two inhabited, convex neighborhood models on the same four worlds, with the same valuation of **every** propositional letter, such that **every** formula in \(\mathcal L_{\boxplus,\mathsf E}\) has exactly the same support at **every** information state in both models, but the formula
\[
\neg q\;\Rrightarrow\;(\neg p\wedge\neg q)
\]
is true at every world of the first model and false at every world of the second. Consequently no unary-fragment formula can define this binary-conditional instance uniformly on convex models.

## Assumptions and scope

We use inhabited inquisitive neighborhood semantics [1]. A model is \(M=(W,\Sigma,V)\) with nonempty \(W\); \(\Sigma(w)\) is a family of **nonempty** subsets of \(W\), possibly empty as a family; each propositional letter has an arbitrary truth set \(V(r)\subseteq W\). Information states are arbitrary subsets \(s\subseteq W\), including the empty state. Support satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
M,s\models r&\iff s\subseteq V(r),\\
M,s\models\bot&\iff s=\varnothing,\\
M,s\models\varphi\wedge\psi&\iff M,s\models\varphi\ \text{and}\ M,s\models\psi,\\
M,s\models\varphi\mathbin{\sqcup}\psi&\iff M,s\models\varphi\ \text{or}\ M,s\models\psi,\\
M,s\models\varphi\to\psi&\iff \forall t\subseteq s\,\bigl(M,t\models\varphi\Rightarrow M,t\models\psi\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sqcup\) denotes inquisitive (non-splitting) disjunction, \(\neg\varphi\) abbreviates \(\varphi\to\bot\), and modal formulas are truth-conditional: support at \(s\) is truth at every world of \(s\). The world-truth clauses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
M,w\models\boxplus\varphi&\iff\forall t\in\Sigma(w):M,t\models\varphi,\\
M,w\models\mathsf E\varphi&\iff\exists t\in\Sigma(w):M,t\models\varphi,\\
M,w\models\varphi\Rrightarrow\psi&\iff\forall t\in\Sigma(w):\bigl(M,t\models\varphi\Rightarrow M,t\models\psi\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
A neighborhood family is convex when \(A,B\in\Sigma(w)\) and \(A\subseteq C\subseteq B\) with \(C\ne\varnothing\) imply \(C\in\Sigma(w)\). A model is convex if this holds at every world.

The result applies to the whole unary language at unbounded modal nesting depth; it is not merely a bounded-depth or propositional-fragment separation. It does not resolve Conjecture 4.11 about **convex-invariant full-language formulas**.

## Proof

**Construction.** Let \(W=\{a,a',b,c\}\). Interpret \(p\) only at \(b\), \(q\) only at \(c\), and every other propositional letter as false everywhere. Set
\[
U=\{a\},\qquad T=\{a,b\},\qquad V_0=\{a',b,c\}.
\]
Let \(M_-\) and \(M_+\) have this same carrier and valuation, with **constant local neighborhood families** at every world:
\[
\Sigma_-(w)=\{U,V_0\},\qquad
\Sigma_+(w)=\{U,T,V_0\}\quad (w\in W).
\]
Both families are convex. The two members of \(\Sigma_-\) are incomparable. In \(\Sigma_+\), the only proper inclusion between distinct listed members is \(U\subset T\), and no nonempty set is strictly between them. The set \(V_0\) is incomparable with both.

**Type-support lemma.** For a state \(s\), let \(\pi(s)\) be the set of propositional valuation types realized by its worlds, relative to **all** propositional letters. Here there are exactly three types: \(\alpha\) at \(a,a'\), \(\beta\) at \(b\), and \(\gamma\) at \(c\). For every \(\theta\in\mathcal L_{\boxplus,\mathsf E}\) there is a downward-closed family \(\mathcal D_\theta\) of subsets of \(\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}\), containing the empty set, such that for **both** \(M_-\) and \(M_+\), and every \(s\subseteq W\),
\[
M_-,s\models\theta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi(s)\in\mathcal D_\theta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M_+,s\models\theta.
\]
Prove this by structural induction. Atomic support and falsum depend only on \(\pi(s)\), and conjunction/inquisitive disjunction preserve this property. For implication, every subset of \(\pi(s)\) is the type set of some substate of \(s\). Therefore its universal quantification over substates depends only on \(\pi(s)\). All resulting predicates are downward-closed by persistence of support.

For the modal induction steps, note that
\[
\pi(U)=\{\alpha\}\ \subset\ \pi(T)=\{\alpha,\beta\}\ \subset\
\pi(V_0)=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}.
\]
All neighborhoods of either model have profiles between the two endpoints. If the subformula's support is the same downward-closed predicate \(\mathcal D\) of profiles in the two models, then at **every** world of either model,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf E\varphi\text{ is true}&\iff\pi(U)\in\mathcal D,\\
\boxplus\varphi\text{ is true}&\iff\pi(V_0)\in\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, any supported neighborhood forces support at the smaller-profile \(U\), and support at the larger-profile \(V_0\) forces support at all neighborhoods. As both endpoint neighborhoods occur in both models, the displayed equivalences hold exactly. These two modal truth values are constant across the worlds and agree between the models. The new modal formula thus supports either every state or only the empty state, uniformly in the two models, completing the induction.

**The conditional separates.** Put \(\varphi=\neg q\) and \(\psi=\neg p\wedge\neg q\). Their supports are truth-conditional: a state supports \(\varphi\) precisely when it contains no \(c\); it supports \(\psi\) precisely when it contains neither \(b\) nor \(c\). Thus \(U\) supports both; \(V_0\) does not support \(\varphi\); and \(T\) supports \(\varphi\) but not \(\psi\). The conditional \(\varphi\Rrightarrow\psi\) therefore holds at every world of \(M_-\) and fails at every world of \(M_+\). The type-support lemma rules out any unary-fragment equivalent, even one with arbitrary nested unary modalities and additional propositional letters. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 program `verify_convex.py` reconstructs the two models as bit masks, checks the convexity definition by enumerating all intermediate nonempty states, and computes the three valuation-type profiles. It enumerates **all 19 downward-closed predicates containing the empty type profile** on the three-type state lattice and checks that universal and existential quantification over either neighborhood family agree (38 unary-quantification comparisons). It also checks the implication support clause on all 16 information states and all 19-by-19 pairs of these predicates (5,776 comparisons), and checks the claimed conditional's opposite truth values.

An independent evaluator in `direct_semantic_test.py` explicitly interprets 2,500 deterministically generated unary formulas of varying nesting depth and compares their truth at all 16 states in both models (40,000 paired comparisons). These finite checks validate the countermodel's explicit data. The unbounded-language claim relies on the preceding structural induction, **not** on finite formula enumeration.

To reproduce, run `python3 verify_convex.py` and `python3 direct_semantic_test.py` from a directory containing this file and both scripts. Their output tokens are `VERIFY_OK` and `DIRECT_SEMANTICS_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Ciardelli [1, Sections 3–4, especially Open Problem 4.6] establishes expressivity properties of the unary fragments on unrestricted models and asks, separately, whether the two unary modalities define the binary conditional over **convex** models. Unrestricted-model nondefinability does not entail nondefinability on a restricted frame class. The present construction places **both** models within that class, answering the second question negatively. The already-known downward-monotone definition [1, Proposition 4.5] is not contradicted: neither neighborhood family here is downward-monotone.

An earlier separation for the **existential-only** language over upward-monotone frames addresses the other question of Open Problem 4.6. It does not imply the present result because the larger language also includes the universal modality, while the present class is convex rather than upward-monotone.

Anttila and Brinck Knudstorp [2] study expressively complete convex **team-support properties**, rather than restricting neighborhood assignments to convex sets; those representation theorems have different primitives and do not yield this frame-restricted separation. Ding and Yang [3] study modal bundled operators with classical one-world truth conditions over a distinct convex-neighborhood semantics; their results do not assert definability of an inquisitive binary conditional from these two unary inquisitive operators.

## Limitations

This is an expressivity separation, not a complete expressive characterization of the unary fragment on convex models. No claim is made that four worlds are **minimal** for an arbitrary separating pair. No claim is made about Conjecture 4.11's convex-invariant formulas. The literature comparison used available primary text and indexed records and does not certify absolute priority; unindexed or later equivalent results remain possible.
