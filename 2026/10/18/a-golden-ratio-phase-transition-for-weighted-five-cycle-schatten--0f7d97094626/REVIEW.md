# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The exact Schatten-fourth trace expansion is valid for arbitrary complex five-cycle weights and has no angular winding term because the fourth power of a five-cycle has zero trace. The optimization reduces to a real quadratic form on a five-simplex. The eigenvalue threshold \(\sqrt5-1\) is the reciprocal of \(\cos(\pi/5)\). Below and at it, the Fourier spectral argument proves global minimality and classifies the nullspace equality set. Above it, explicit negative-curvature zero-sum tangent vectors rule out any support of size four or five; the two possible types of support of size three are solved directly. Boundary cases, \(q=2\), phases, and norm scaling are treated. The standard-library diagnostic program supports but does not replace these arguments.

## Originality

PASS under the currently inspected material, with publication-priority risk. Pal–Chandok's 2026 article develops weighted Schatten numerical radius inequalities rather than the exact five-cycle optimization and its critical eigenspace. A previously available four-cycle minimax formula has a different winding term, and an indexed sharp nilpotent-shift formula addresses paths, not a closed five-cycle. Older weighted-shift numerical-range results concern different radii or restricted weights. No directly covering five-cycle phase diagram was located by the stated mathematical searches. Earlier quadratic-simplex literature may contain an equivalent disguised formulation; the complete manuscripts for some highly relevant indexed records were not accessible, so the absence of overlap cannot be certified.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a natural global minimax problem for a new Schatten-radius functional, yielding the sharp constant for every weighting parameter and *all* equality cases. The spectral threshold is not merely a numerical crossing: it produces a two-dimensional continuum of minimizers and an abrupt change from five-support to three-support optimizers, followed by an endpoint degeneracy. In particular the closed odd cycle behaves structurally differently from four-cycles (which have winding terms) and nilpotent paths, giving a motivated quantitative boundary in operator-radius geometry.

## Limitations

The result is for weighted five-cycles at Schatten exponent four. It does not classify arbitrary cycle lengths, general matrices, or other Schatten exponents. Publication priority is not independently certified, and numerical diagnostics do not replace the proof.
