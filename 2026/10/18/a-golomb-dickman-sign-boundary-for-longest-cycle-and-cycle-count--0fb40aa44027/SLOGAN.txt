For a uniform random permutation, the number of cycles of size \(r\) is negatively correlated with its longest cycle length exactly when \(r\) is below the mean longest-cycle length, and positively correlated exactly when \(r\) exceeds it. The unique macroscopic sign boundary converges to the Golomb–Dickman fraction \(0.6243299885\ldots\), and every super-half-length cycle count has the exact covariance \(1-\mathbb E[M_n]/r\).
