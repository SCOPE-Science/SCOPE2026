# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The intrinsic facet-normal lengths are computed explicitly, and a strictly positive grid-overlap dual certificate proves a universal lower bound for nonnegative arrays with prescribed mixed differences. The covariance of two integer-part processes evaluates its minimum to a closed expression involving the greatest common divisor. Explicit coupled-grid recursions achieve equality; complementary slackness forces precisely the same recursions, which classify the entire incenter locus and its contacts. Exact rational tests cover every pair of dimensions from 2 through 12 and 1,806 parameter profiles. Principal risk: the proof is not independently certified; finite computations are only checks.

## Originality
**PASS (with residual bibliographic risk).** The published rectangular Monge-polytope work of Erickson and Kretschmann proves the required facet classification but no Chebyshev-center theorem. The published square-Monge inball theorem (`b3dc6b94166a`) completely covers the specialization \(p=q\) and is expressly not a newly claimed case. Its equal-dimension matching argument does not imply a general greatest-common-divisor law for unequal dimensions. Erickson and Jones's more recent same-family work concerns volumes and Ehrhart series. published-finding corpus comparisons and targeted source/alias searches did not reveal a rectangular covering theorem. A different terminology for the same mathematical statement remains a real residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** Coprimality gives an exact, previously unavailable geometric uniqueness criterion and general common divisors give a complete nontrivial family of optimal centers. The change in contact multiplicity at cube boundaries and the exact mass correction link Euclidean convex geometry to unequal-grid couplings. This is an infinite structural classification of a standard matrix polytope, not a routine small-sample invariant or a renamed square formula.

## Scope and limitations
The result is restricted to nonnegative, sum-one, nonsymmetric Monge matrices with the intrinsic Frobenius metric. It gives optimal contained balls, not maximum-volume ellipsoids. Square specializations are established prior science; external priority and independent validation are not certified.
