# A lattice-phase local law with a first correction for two-generator union-closed team properties

## Finding
Fix a labelled universe \(U_n\) of \(n\) valuations. Consider the finite collection \(\mathfrak F_n\) of all families \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(U_n)\) that contain \(\varnothing\) and are generated under finite unions by teams of cardinality at most two. Equip \(\mathfrak F_n\) with the uniform probability measure, and let \(K_n\) denote the number of singleton teams in a uniformly chosen family.

Let \(q_n\geq0\) solve \((q_n+1)2^{q_n}=n\), put \(m_n=\lfloor q_n\rfloor\) and \(\theta_n=q_n-m_n\), and for \(\theta\in[0,1]\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z\) define
\[
F_\theta(j)=j\theta-\frac{j(j-1)}2,\qquad
Z_\theta=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}2^{F_\theta(j)},\qquad
\pi_\theta(j)=\frac{2^{F_\theta(j)}}{Z_\theta},\qquad
\mu_\theta=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}F_\theta(j)\pi_\theta(j).
\]
Then, with \(\mathbb P(K_n=m_n+j)=0\) outside the possible range, there is an absolute constant \(C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\boxed{\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\left|\mathbb P(K_n=m_n+j)-\pi_{\theta_n}(j)\left(1+\frac{F_{\theta_n}(j)-\mu_{\theta_n}}{m_n+1}\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(m_n+1)^2}.}
\]
The corrected expression is a signed first-order approximation; it is not asserted to be a nonnegative probability distribution at remote tail indices. In particular, the uncorrected lattice-phase law has total-variation error \(O(1/\log n)\). Along every subsequence on which \(\theta_n\to\theta\), the centered variable \(K_n-m_n\) converges in total variation to \(\pi_\theta\), a nondegenerate non-Gaussian discrete law. Moreover, \(m_n=\log_2 n-\log_2\log_2 n+O(1)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(K_n)=O(1)\).

The exact finite law used in the proof is
\[
\mathbb P(K_n=s)=\frac{\binom ns 2^{-\binom s2}}{\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk2^{-\binom k2}},\quad 0\leq s\leq n.
\]
The finite coefficient formula and its lacunary-polynomial normalizer are already known; novelty is claimed solely for the **uniform first-order local distributional expansion** in the indicated team-property model.

## Assumptions and scope
The union operation is ordinary set union of teams, not logical disjunction in general. The universe is labelled, families are counted as distinct subsets of \(\mathcal P(U_n)\), and the empty team is included. A family is eligible precisely when its members can be generated by unions from eligible teams of size zero, one, or two; no downward-closure, union-universe, or topological axiom is assumed. No statement is made about uniform sampling of isomorphism classes or about all union-closed team properties without the generator-size bound.

## Proof
**Canonical generators and the finite weights.** The union-irreducible nonempty members of any finite union-closed family are intrinsic and form its unique inclusion-minimal union-generating family. In the specified class they are singleton and doubleton teams. Let \(S\subseteq U_n\) be the set of valuations whose singleton teams belong to \(\mathcal F\). A doubleton \(\{u,v\}\) with both endpoints in \(S\) is redundant because it is the union of two singletons. Every other doubleton may independently be present or absent as a union-irreducible generator. Conversely these choices determine distinct eligible families: singleton and eligible doubleton membership recovers every choice, and higher-order teams are their unions. Thus, conditional on \(|S|=s\), the number of families equals \(2^{\binom n2-\binom s2}\). Multiplication by \(\binom ns\) and normalization gives the displayed exact law.

**Exact weight ratios.** After cancelling the common factor \(2^{\binom n2}\), put \(w_{n,s}=\binom ns2^{-s(s-1)/2}\). The successive ratio is
\[
\frac{w_{n,s+1}}{w_{n,s}}=\frac{n-s}{s+1}2^{-s}.
\]
The function \(x\mapsto(x+1)2^x\) is strictly increasing for nonnegative \(x\), so \(q_n\) is unique. Put \(m=m_n\), \(a=m+1\), \(\theta=\theta_n\), and \(F=F_\theta\). For an integer \(i\) with the displayed weights defined, the ratio about \(m\) can be written exactly as
\[
\frac{w_{n,m+i+1}}{w_{n,m+i}}
=2^{\theta-i}\frac{a+\theta}{a+i}\left(1-\frac{m+i}{n}\right).
\]
For positive \(j\), multiply this identity from \(i=0\) to \(j-1\); for negative \(j\), multiply its reciprocals from \(i=j\) to \(-1\). Since the oriented sum of \(\theta-i\) over either range equals \(F_\theta(j)\), Taylor expansion of \(\log(a+\theta)-\log(a+i)\) yields, uniformly on \(|j|\leq a/2\),
\[
\log\frac{w_{n,m+j}}{w_{n,m}}
=(\log 2)F_\theta(j)+\frac{F_\theta(j)}a
+O\!\left(\frac{(|j|+1)^3}{a^2}+\frac{(|j|+1)(m+|j|)}n\right).
\]
The terms with negative \(j\) obey the same oriented-product formula. The displayed bound is used only where the indices are valid.

**Uniform tails and normalization.** The ratios of successive \(w_{n,s}\) decrease in \(s\). The preceding exact expression implies absolute-constant bounds \(w_{n,m+j}/w_{n,m}\leq C_0 2^{-c_0j^2}\) on all valid indices \(j\), with \(c_0>0\). For positive \(j\), bound each successive ratio above by a constant multiple of \(2^{-i}\); for negative \(j\), use the corresponding inverse ratios, which are bounded by a constant multiple of \(2^{-|i|}\). The same Gaussian tails hold for \(2^{F_\theta(j)}\), uniformly in \(\theta\in[0,1]\).

Take \(|j|\leq L\sqrt{\log a}\), with fixed sufficiently large \(L\). In this window the exponential of the previous logarithmic formula has the expansion
\[
\frac{w_{n,m+j}}{w_{n,m}}=2^{F_\theta(j)}\left[1+\frac{F_\theta(j)}a+O\!\left(\frac{(1+|j|)^6}{a^2}\right)\right],
\]
where \(m/n\) is exponentially small in \(m\), because \(n=(q_n+1)2^{q_n}\). Uniform Gaussian domination makes the sum over \(j\) of the absolute remainders \(O(a^{-2})\), and the contribution from indices outside this window is also \(O(a^{-2})\), for sufficiently large \(L\). Consequently,
\[
\sum_j\frac{w_{n,m+j}}{w_{n,m}}=Z_\theta\left(1+\frac{\mu_\theta}a+O(a^{-2})\right).
\]
Division gives the claimed \(\ell^1\) expansion. The absolute implicit constants can be made uniform over all \(\theta\in[0,1]\) because \(Z_\theta\) is continuous and bounded above and below by positive constants there.

**Consequences.** Removing the first correction leaves \(O(a^{-1})\) total-variation error. The exponential tails establish uniform integrability of fixed moments. Therefore phase-convergent subsequences have the stated limits and bounded variance. Taking binary logarithms of \((q_n+1)2^{q_n}=n\) gives \(q_n=\log_2n-\log_2\log_2n+O(1)\). The ratio \(\pi_\theta(j+1)/\pi_\theta(j)=2^{\theta-j}\) makes the limit law explicitly discrete and non-Gaussian.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_phase.py` exhaustively generates all families from singleton and doubleton generators through four labelled valuations, independently deduplicates the families, and compares the resulting singleton profiles to the exact counting formula. It also compares exact finite distributions computed from logarithmic gamma functions against both the leading and first-corrected lattice-phase approximations for six selected values of \(n\). These checks returned `VERIFY_OK`. They are finite sanity checks, not a substitute for the uniform infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Yang's 2020 paper establishes the semantic significance of union-closed team properties and expressive completeness of the corresponding propositional team logics. The coefficient sum \(\sum_s\binom ns2^{-\binom s2}\) appears in OEIS A079491; its graph interpretation was also recorded there, while A339832 gives the unlabelled graph counterpart. Brown–Dilcher–Manna studied the corresponding expected-independence polynomial. Gawronski–Neuschel (2013/2014) and Paris (2014/2015) established asymptotics, including theta oscillations, for **the sum of these coefficients**. Their inspected primary texts do not state an \(\ell^1\)-local limit for the coefficient-index random variable or its explicit normalized first-order correction. The present assertion does not claim any new coefficient count, graph correspondence, or asymptotic leading term of the normalizing polynomial.

## Limitations
The class restricts the union-generating team sizes to at most two; all higher-rank union-closed properties remain outside the theorem. The bound is an asymptotic statement with unspecified universal constant and sufficiently-large threshold. The leading-phase limit must be indexed by the fractional part \(\theta_n\); there is not a single phase-independent centered limit. The signed first-correction approximation need not be pointwise nonnegative in the far tails. Related general saddle-point expansions might imply alternative formulations of the local expansion; no claim of globally certified novelty or independent external validation is made.
