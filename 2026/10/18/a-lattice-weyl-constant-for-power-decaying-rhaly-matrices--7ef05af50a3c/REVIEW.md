# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The weighted prefix-sum embedding is exactly converted to a discrete Dirichlet-energy quotient. A codimension-one tail argument reduces low eigenvalue counts to finite strings, and rank-one edge interlacing bounds the geometric block error. The Neumann path eigenvalues \(4\sin^2(\pi k/(2h))\) give the exact phase density. The resulting counting law is inverted without assuming finite simulations certify infinite rank. Finite Sturm recurrences and the gamma identity were checked separately.

## Originality

**PASS, with identified residual risk.** The complete 2025 Rhaly paper was compared at its approximation-number and Schatten theorems: they give comparison bounds rather than the exact phase integral. published-finding corpus research records for geometric Rhaly singular spectra, trace-class criteria, and damped generalized Cesàro matrices were compared by implication and do not establish this polynomial-weight coefficient. The full June 2026 paper and some general unbounded-Jacobi works could not be fully inspected. Their potential stronger coverage remains an explicit publication-priority risk; neither was declared not covering from an abstract alone.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result determines a naturally motivated approximation-number invariant, the precise discrete-versus-continuous dispersion coefficient, all quasi-Schatten thresholds, and the critical logarithmic residue, throughout the complete power-decay range \(\gamma>1\). The sharp leading constant is not supplied by qualitative compactness or Schatten membership criteria.

## Limitations and comparison

There is no uniform estimate at \(\gamma=1\), no finite-rank remainder bound, and no claim for arbitrary factorable matrices. Most relevant: Bellavita, Dellepiane and Stylogiannis (2025, arXiv:2503.21930v3); their 2026 weighted-Hardy extension (arXiv:2606.15875, abstract inspected); the Hardy-type tree approximation-number results of Evans, Harris and Lang (2001); and general unbounded-Jacobi work by Janas and Naboko (2004). The last two were only accessible through abstracts and are not certified as noncovering. Human literature verification and independent mathematical audit remain outstanding.
