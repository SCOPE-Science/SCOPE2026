# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The affine action is a genuine tournament automorphism action: the multiplier subgroup \(Q=\{1,3,4,5,9\}\) preserves the quadratic-residue arc differences. The unique linear order on any transitive subtournament makes simplex stabilizers pointwise, so orbits are valid quotient cells with the usual signed orbit boundaries. Two independent orientation conventions give the same exact quotient Smith factors. For the full group, the complete cellular matrices have ranks \((0,1,1)\) and the two-cell with degree-one attaching map forms a contractible subcomplex. Collapsing it leaves a sphere with one degree-two three-cell, proving the Moore homotopy equivalence, not merely a homology match. Original-complex Smith forms and all chain identities are also checked. Four standalone programs reconstruct the finite calculations.

## Originality — PASS
The original eleven-vertex Moore-space wedge is explicitly **known**: Govc [2], Proposition 3.4, describes \(M(\mathbb Z/2,2)\vee\bigvee^{10}S^2\). The new claim instead computes the topological quotients under the canonical affine group and two natural subgroups, including the precise full-orbit homotopy type. The original paper's relevant full-text discussion treats nonequivariant homotopy types, not these quotient complexes. Govc–Levi–Smith [1] treats tournament filtrations rather than the quotient calculation. A separate order-nineteen affine homology-character calculation has different objects, group and degrees and gives no formula implying the order-eleven quotient. Searches of literature and mathematical research records did not identify an encompassing theorem for these exact orbit spaces. Residual risk: unindexed equivalent equivariant or quotient computation.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A previously known complex with both ten free two-dimensional generators and genuine two-torsion is a motivated test case for the effect of symmetry on homotopy. The affine quotient preserves exactly the Moore two-torsion summand while suppressing all free homology, and the translation quotient exhibits new eleven-torsion in degree one. The result is an integral homotopy classification of a natural quotient and two complete subgroup-homology profiles; it is not a numerical re-enumeration of the existing nonequivariant homology or a generic degree computation. This isolates a structural distinction among canonical group quotients that neither nonequivariant homotopy data nor rational representation characters imply.
