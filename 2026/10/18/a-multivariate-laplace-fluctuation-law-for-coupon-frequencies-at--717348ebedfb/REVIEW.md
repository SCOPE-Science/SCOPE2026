# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** Independence of the Poisson coupon streams gives an exact conditional multinomial profile with one compulsory singleton; the conditional Lindeberg theorem gives independent Gaussian coordinates after scaling. The beta-distributed minimum of uniform transforms gives the common exponential mixing variable. The independent global Poisson clock has mean-square discrepancy \(H_n/n\), which vanishes even after the polynomial-in-logarithm centering derivatives are included. The full domain, fixed dimension, and weak-limit quantifiers are explicit in the proof. Exact rational checks and bounded simulations are sanity checks, not a replacement for the proof.

**Originality — PASS (subject to documented residual search risk).** Foata–Han–Lass (2001), Proposition 4.1, provides an exact multivariate probability generating function of terminal coupon-multiplicity counts; it does not state or asymptotically analyze the observed-time-centered second-order Gaussian-mixture law. This is a strong finite-law predecessor, rather than an equivalent limiting theorem. Myers–Wilf (2003) provides the exact singleton distribution, not the multivariate second-order limit. Saunders (arXiv:2005.08915, Theorem 1.1) and Ilienko (arXiv:2002.00650, Theorem 1) provide first-order common-exponential limits, which do **not** imply the normalized order-\((\log n)^{r/2}\) fluctuations. Mahmoud–Smythe (2012) explicitly excludes superlinear sampling and handles nonrandom sample-size phases. Searches for stopping-time conditional Gaussian and Laplace limits and relevant published mathematical research found no statement that dominates this theorem. An unlocated equivalent result remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** First-order counts co-fluctuate through an exponential factor of order \((\log n)^r\); this theorem characterizes the previously hidden fluctuations remaining after removing that factor. It gives the full joint variance profile across any fixed collection of multiplicity counts, explains conditional independence versus unconditional dependence, and identifies an explicit non-Gaussian multivariate Laplace law. This is a structural second-order answer for a canonical occupancy stopping time, not an arbitrary finite calculation.

**Scope and risk.** No claim of an independently audited proof or priority is made. The conclusion is restricted to fixed multiplicities and equal coupon weights. A future literature search may uncover equivalent implicit second-order asymptotics in another parametrization.
