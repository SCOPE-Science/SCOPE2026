# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** For a generic hyperplane, section vertices and crossed cube edges correspond bijectively. The Boolean edge-isoperimetric lower bound \(|A|\log_2(2^n/|A|)\), with the degree upper bound \(n|A|\), controls the logarithmic section complexity. The required set is a halfspace in the Boolean cube generated by independent Gaussian magnitudes. Conditioning invariant hyperplanes on intersection changes the Gaussian normal-direction density by the cube width; its normalized tilt tends to one in total variation. A quenched weighted-Rademacher upper and lower large-deviation argument identifies the rate function and yields the convergence. The endpoint coefficients follow from explicit expansions of the Legendre transform. The new checker reproduces the exact finite combinatorics and rate-function endpoint behavior. Numerical evidence is not used to justify infinite-dimensional assertions.

## Originality

**PASS relative to evidence inspected, with residual historical-priority risk.** Lonke proves fixed-central-section expectations; Swan already proves the exact mean under random translation for fixed normal, and it is not counted as a discovery. De Loera and collaborators treat possible vertex-count values and maxima, not a probability distribution; Cho–Kim treat clipped volumes; Bárány–Domokos treat mean vertices of planar sections of three-dimensional polytopes. The closest relevant published records concern **central** hyperplanes or other polytopes. Neither an implication nor an exact equivalent of the random exponential-limit law was identified. The 2008 Calkin et al. probabilistic cube-cut paper and the 1991 Chakerian–Logothetti article could not be inspected in full; this may conceal related results. No definitive priority claim or independent validation is made.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A natural and fully specified invariant geometric sampling law produces a complete, nondegenerate, dimension-free distribution for the exponential growth rate of section complexity. The theorem sharply separates the typical and expected section sizes even though the mean is fixed exactly by a classical identity. Endpoint exponents quantify both near-central and near-supporting hyperplane regimes, providing structural information unavailable from mean counts or maximal-section bounds.

## Scope and limitations

The claim concerns equal-side cubes, codimension-one affine hyperplanes, and a weak limit of logarithmic section counts; it neither generalizes to arbitrary polytopes nor provides finite-dimensional convergence rates. Uninspected historical literature leaves priority risk. Independent mathematical assessment is not part of this package.
