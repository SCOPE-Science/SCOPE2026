Splitting a fixed habitat area into two initially occupied patches has either a strict all-horizon survival advantage or exactly one switch from short-horizon advantage to long-horizon disadvantage, with the dividing recolonization rate given explicitly. The sharp all-horizon recolonization threshold exceeds the formal mean-persistence threshold by exactly the original single-patch extinction rate, regardless of the extinction-area exponent; the switching horizon diverges with an explicit reciprocal law at that boundary.
