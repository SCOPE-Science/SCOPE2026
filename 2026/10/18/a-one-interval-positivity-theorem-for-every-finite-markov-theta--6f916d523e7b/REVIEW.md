# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite-state Markov renewal decomposition yields the exact identity \(R(s)_{ii}=\{1+r_i\mathbb E[s(1-e^{-T_i/s})]\}^{-1}\), including defective return times. Pointwise strict increase of \(s(1-e^{-t/s})\) for every \(t>0\) or \(t=\infty\) establishes strictly decreasing resolvent diagonals without spectral or reversibility assumptions. The Cayley/theta matrix algebra leaves only diagonal positivity constraints, giving a sharp closed initial interval for each generator and parameter. Transient and recurrent cases follow from the corresponding return-time expectation and the stationary renewal-reward identity. Time reversal preserves resolvent diagonals. An exact-rational verifier replayed 197 examples; finite checks are not substituted for the universal argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Horváth (1998) gives problem-class Runge–Kutta positivity criteria, not this chain-specific single-interval law. Fallat–Tsatsomeros (2002) gives the underlying Cayley identity and matrix class facts, not return-time monotonicity. Doyle (2009) develops general Markov potential/resolvent background rather than integrator positivity. The closest inspected public result, *A resolvent-return law for Crank–Nicolson positivity on reversible Markov chains*, proves monotonicity from reversibility and explicitly does not classify nonreversible positivity windows. The current theorem genuinely extends that restriction to every finite generator, all theta parameters, transient classes, and time reversal. An equivalent result in the much larger Markov renewal or numerical positivity literature may remain unindexed.

**Value — PASS.** For stiff Markov master equations, positivity is a structural requirement independent of stability. This theorem rules out reentrant positivity for the entire first-order theta family even when nonreversible semigroups exhibit oscillatory returns, and supplies exact graph-recurrence conditions for when arbitrarily large steps are admissible. The transient-state obstruction and time-reversal invariance add useful, nontrivial consequences. No general closed-form root is claimed.
