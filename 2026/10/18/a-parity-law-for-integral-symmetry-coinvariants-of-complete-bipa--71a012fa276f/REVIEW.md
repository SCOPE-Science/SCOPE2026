# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The conclusion follows from an equivariant chain presentation for the top homology of the full simplex skeleton, with pure-part chains killed. The oriented-face permutation coinvariants are exactly order-two modules because each \((r+1)\)-element mixed face has a same-part transposition in its stabilizer. The entire relation matrix arises from boundaries of \((r+2)\)-element faces, giving the stated parity laws, including saturated endpoint cases. The proof addresses all \(m,n\ge2\), \(2\le r<m+n\); no conclusion is inferred from finite trials alone. Independent finite implementations of original-chain and reduced color-orbit presentations agree in 70 parameter cases, and the closed orbit formula agrees on the larger finite rectangular test range. The homotopy ranks cited as background are separately established in Deshpande–Singh, Theorem 3.5.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with residual risk)
Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, Theorem 3.5) completely determines the nonequivariant homotopy ranks for multipartite graph higher independence complexes. It does not state the present integral part-permutation coinvariants, so these are not corollaries of its ranks alone: two torsion-free lattices of the same rank can have different coinvariants. Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150) proves vertex decomposability for trees and supplies no group-coinvariant classification of cyclic complete-bipartite graphs. Targeted web and indexed mathematical-record searches for the exact invariant, synonymous symmetry-module statements, subgroup coinvariants and broader coverage did not identify a dominating result. Indexed related records on complete-bipartite Morse-matching and graph configuration spaces concern different complexes. Unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The invariant records integral obstructions to symmetrizing top homology, which are erased by rational representations and cannot be reconstructed from nonequivariant sphere counts. The theorem simultaneously settles every bipartition size and every nontrivial higher-independence level, with exact two-primary boundary behavior and a sharp even/odd stable-rank transition. This is a structural, motivated classification rather than a new name for a known Betti number or a single arbitrary numerical example.
