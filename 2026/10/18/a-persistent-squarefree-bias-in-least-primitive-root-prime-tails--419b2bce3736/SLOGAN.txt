Among integers that fail the primitive-root test modulo every prime up to a growing cutoff, the fraction that are squarefree tends to \(K/\zeta(2)\approx0.35970\), rather than the ordinary squarefree density \(1/\zeta(2)\approx0.60793\). An explicit convergent Euler product gives the persistent bias \(0.59166<K<0.59168\), and a uniform squarefree-sieve theorem makes this rigorous up to cutoffs below \((\log N)/4\).
