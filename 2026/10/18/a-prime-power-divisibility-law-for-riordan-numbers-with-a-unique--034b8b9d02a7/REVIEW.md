# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Laurent constant-term identity is deduced from the Catalan binomial transform. The first-order Frobenius congruences give exact noncancellation for all odd primes except a specific five-adic branch. A second-order polynomial congruence and five explicit coefficients give a nonzero class modulo 25 after exactly one factor of five. This settles all positive exponents. `verify.py` checks 96 binomial-transform cases and 24 prime-power valuations, plus five mod-25 towers; the infinite proof does not depend on these finite checks.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risks)
The classical recurrence and Catalan representation are credited. Deutsch–Sagan treat low-prime congruences, Krattenthaler–Müller give general ternary-power congruence algorithms and separate binary congruence formulas. Neither covers the evaluated all-odd-prime exact valuation with its unique five-adic parity exception. Indexed mathematical records, accessible historical claims, OEIS and relevant full-text papers did not identify an equivalent theorem. Unindexed earlier equivalent results or algorithms specialized to this case remain possible; no independent originality audit was performed.

## Value — PASS
Prime-power indices are singled out naturally by the recurrence factor, and the exact exceptional prime and exponent parity represent genuine second-order arithmetic structure. The classification is uniform over every odd prime and every positive exponent, sharper than a bare divisibility bound or a finite table.

## Scientific limits
No formula is claimed for the prime two, other indices or high normalized residues. Published sources may contain an unlocated equivalent statement. Reproduction tests support but do not certify all infinite quantifiers.
