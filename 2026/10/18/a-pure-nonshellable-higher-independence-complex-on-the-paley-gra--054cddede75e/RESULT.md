# A pure, nonshellable higher-independence complex on the Paley graph of order \(13\)

## Finding

Let \(P_{13}\) be the Paley graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z\) and edge set defined by the nonzero quadratic residues modulo \(13\). The two-independence complex \(X=\operatorname{Ind}_2(P_{13})\) is a pure three-dimensional simplicial complex with \(78\) tetrahedral facets, but

\[
X\simeq S^2\vee S^3\vee S^3.
\]

Consequently \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. The pure complex \(X\) is not Cohen–Macaulay over any field and therefore is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,12\}\) and \(R=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}\). Join distinct residues \(u,v\in V\) when \((u-v)\bmod 13\in R\). For each \(\sigma\subseteq V\), let \(P_{13}[\sigma]\) be the induced graph. Define

\[
X=\{\sigma\subseteq V:\text{ every connected component of }P_{13}[\sigma]\text{ has at most two vertices}\}.
\]

Equivalently, the induced graph has maximum degree at most one. The result concerns this single labeled \(13\)-vertex graph, with ordinary non-equivariant homotopy equivalence and integral coefficients. The empty simplex is included in the enumeration but omitted from the ordinary \(f\)-vector.

## Proof

**Face enumeration and purity.** Enumerating the \(2^{13}=8192\) subsets and retaining precisely those with induced maximum degree at most one gives the ordinary face vector

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)=(13,78,143,78),
\]

with no faces of dimension above three. Every lower-dimensional face extends to at least one of the \(78\) tetrahedra, so the complex is pure of dimension three. The one-skeleton is the complete graph on \(13\) vertices, since every two-vertex induced graph has components of size at most two.

**Simply connected two-skeleton.** Use the \(12\) edges \(\{0,i\}\), for \(1\le i\le12\), as a spanning tree of the complete one-skeleton. The fundamental group of the two-skeleton is generated by the \(66\) remaining oriented edges, with one triangular relator for each two-simplex. The \(33\) triangles containing vertex \(0\) each kill one distinct generator. For any other triangle with two trivial edge generators, its triangular relation kills the third. A complete ordered sweep through the remaining \(110\) triangles kills all \(33\) remaining generators. The embedded checker constructs the actual triangles and executes this elimination, including checking the initial \(33\) and final \(66\) counts. Thus \(\pi_1(X^{(2)})=1\), and \(\pi_1(X)=1\).

**Exact integral boundary.** Orient each simplex by increasing vertex labels. The simplicial boundary \(\partial_3:C_3(X;\mathbb Z)\to C_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) is a \(143\times78\) integer matrix with columns having four alternating \(\pm1\) entries. The embedded verifier applies explicitly implemented unimodular row and column operations, each with a unit pivot. It reaches exactly \(76\) diagonal entries equal to \(1\), with the two remaining columns zero and no residual nonzero block. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{SNF}(\partial_3)=\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{76},0,0),
\]

where the rectangular zero padding is understood. In particular, the image of \(\partial_3\) is primitive in \(C_2\) and no torsion is hidden in the degree-two homology. The complete one-skeleton has \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=12\); the verified simple connectivity gives \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=0\), hence \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=78-12=66\). It follows that

\[
\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_2=143-66-76=1,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_3=78-76=2.
\]

The checker additionally verifies \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\) with exact integers.

**Upgrading homology to homotopy.** The simply connected two-skeleton has free second homology of rank \(143-66=77\), no higher homology, and is consequently homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{77}S^2\) (Hurewicz and the homology form of Whitehead's theorem). The \(78\) three-cells attach by maps \(S^2\to\bigvee^{77}S^2\), determined completely by integral degree vectors. Under an integral change of bases on the wedge summands and attaching spheres, the attaching map has the same \(76\) unit diagonal entries found above. Those unit attachments cancel \(76\) two/three-cell pairs; the remaining one two-sphere and two trivially attached three-spheres give the asserted equivalence. Such integral basis changes are realized by homotopy equivalences of finite wedges of two-spheres.

Since a pure three-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay complex must have vanishing reduced second homology over the coefficient field, while \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), the complex is not Cohen–Macaulay over any field. Pure shellability would imply Cohen–Macaulayness, and hence is also impossible.

## Relationship to prior work

Deshpande and Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, first public version January 15, 2020) define higher independence complexes and classify complete multipartite, fully whiskered, path, cycle, and perfect-ary-tree families. Deshpande, Shukla, and Singh (arXiv:2001.06775) prove connectivity bounds for general graphs and wedge-decomposition statements for chordal graphs. Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863) identify the equivalent connected-triple hypergraph model and analyze shellability for trees and related chordal families. These general and family-specific statements do not determine the integral homotopy type of this nonchordal Paley graph.

The ordinary open-neighborhood complex of the same graph is a different simplicial construction; likewise, its ordinary independence complex, cut complexes, and complexes of different graphs are not interchangeable with \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P_{13})\). The present statement is an exact single-graph classification, not an assertion that no other equivalent computation exists.

## Limitations

Only \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P_{13})\) is classified. No formula is claimed for other Paley orders, other higher-independence parameters, or equivariant homotopy. The homotopy splitting is established up to ordinary integral homotopy equivalence, not simplicial isomorphism. Literature indexing can miss prior equivalent computations; this proof does not establish publication priority.
