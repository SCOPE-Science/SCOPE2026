# Sharp near-resonance linewidth and survival for a corner trap on a weighted rook graph

## Finding

Let \(m,n\ge2\) be integers, fix \(\beta,\gamma>0\), and vary \(\alpha=(n\beta+\delta)/m>0\) near \(\delta=0\). On the vertex space of \(K_m\square K_n\) put
\[
H_0=\alpha(J_m-I_m)\otimes I_n+\beta I_m\otimes(J_n-I_n),\qquad
H=H_0-\tfrac{i\gamma}{2}|0,0\rangle\langle0,0|.
\]
Within the four-dimensional invariant product-band sector generated by the four separate band projections of the corner vertex, precisely one eigenvalue approaches the real axis as \(\delta\to0\); the other three have decay rates bounded below for sufficiently small detuning. Write \(z_{\rm slow}(\delta)\) for that eigenvalue. Its population decay rate is
\[
-2\operatorname{Im}z_{\rm slow}(\delta)=\mathcal K\delta^2+O(|\delta|^3),\qquad
\mathcal K=\frac{4mn(m-1)(n-1)}{\gamma(m+n-2)^3}.
\]
For a vertex \((r,c)\), define \(R=\mathbf 1_{r=0}\), \(C=\mathbf 1_{c=0}\) and the weights
\[
Q_{r,c}=\left(R+\frac{1-R}{m-1}\right)\left(C+\frac{1-C}{n-1}\right),\qquad
D_{r,c}=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)}{mn(m+n-2)}
\left(\frac{nC-1}{n-1}-\frac{mR-1}{m-1}\right)^2.
\]
For each fixed \(s>0\), the survival probability has the limit
\[
\lim_{\delta\to0,\,\delta\ne0}\big\|e^{-iHs/\delta^2}|r,c\rangle\big\|^2
=1-Q_{r,c}+D_{r,c}e^{-\mathcal K s}.
\]
Here \(Q_{r,c}\) is the eventual absorbed weight away from the collision and \(D_{r,c}\) is the weight lost from eventual absorption at exact resonance. Those static weights (including the corresponding mean capture change from \(4/(mn)\) to \(3/(mn)\)) are a singleton case of the earlier rectangular-detector theorem [1], not new claims. The result concerns the decay coefficient and the resolved time dependence of the metastable weight.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is finite, with positive homogeneous hoppings in each factor and exactly one lossy corner. The convention \(-i\gamma/2\) is part of the definition of \(\gamma\). All estimates hold at fixed \(m,n,\beta,\gamma\); no uniformity is claimed as the dimensions grow or the detector strength approaches zero or infinity. The limit concerns nonzero detuning and fixed strictly positive rescaled time, not the value at \(t=0\). No repeated measurements or multiple detectors are included.

## Proof

Put \(q=|0,0\rangle\), \(U_k=J_k/k\), \(V_k=I_k-U_k\). The four product projectors are \(U_m\otimes U_n,U_m\otimes V_n,V_m\otimes U_n,V_m\otimes V_n\). Their energies are \(\alpha(m-1)+\beta(n-1)\), \(\alpha(m-1)-\beta\), \(-\alpha+\beta(n-1)\), and \(-\alpha-\beta\), denoted respectively by \(E_+,E_1,E_2,E_-\). The middle gap is \(E_1-E_2=\delta\); the extreme energies stay distinct from the middle ones at resonance. For each of these four projectors \(P_j\) use the unit vector \(\phi_j=P_j q/\sqrt{w_j}\), where \(w_j=\|P_jq\|^2\). The extreme weights are \(w_+=1/(mn)\), \(w_-=(m-1)(n-1)/(mn)\). The middle weights are \(u=w_1=(n-1)/(mn)\), \(v=w_2=(m-1)/(mn)\); set \(W=u+v\).

The span \(B\) of the four \(\phi_j\) is independent of detuning. Its orthogonal complement is exactly the permanent dark sector: it is invariant and the absorber vanishes there, so its evolution is unitary. This follows directly from the product projectors and holds even when a pair of their energies coincide. At resonance, inside \(B\), the unit vector
\[
d=(\sqrt v\,\phi_1-\sqrt u\,\phi_2)/\sqrt W
\]
is detector-orthogonal at energy \(E_*=E_1=E_2\). For every eigenvector \(x\) of \(H\), the identity \(\operatorname{Im}z\,\|x\|^2=-(\gamma/2)|\langle q,x\rangle|^2\) forces a real eigenvalue's eigenvector to be orthogonal to \(q\) and an eigenvector of \(H_0\). All remaining real eigenspaces are outside \(B\): the other middle direction and the extreme directions have nonzero detector overlap. The orthogonal complement of \(d\) inside \(B\) is invariant at resonance because both \(H_0\) and the absorber preserve it. Its eigenvalues lie strictly below the real axis. This shows that the root at \(E_*\) is simple in \(B\), independent of any degeneracy in the permanent dark sector.

On \(B\), the diagonal energy matrix is perturbed by \(-i(\gamma/2)qq^*\). To locate the eigenvalue near \(E_*\), put \(y=z-E_2\) and
\[
F(z,\delta)=\frac{w_+}{z-E_+}+\frac{w_-}{z-E_-}.
\]
The determinant lemma gives the secular factor \(1+i(\gamma/2)(u/(y-\delta)+v/y+F(z,\delta))\). After clearing the two middle factors (the extreme factors are nonzero in a neighborhood), the equation is
\[
W y-v\delta=\big(2i/\gamma-F(z,\delta)\big)y(y-\delta).
\]
The derivative of the difference of the two sides with respect to \(y\) is \(W\) at \((y,\delta)=(0,0)\). There is a unique analytic root there; consequently \(y=(v/W)\delta+O(\delta^2)\). Substitution again yields
\[
y=\frac{v}{W}\delta-\frac{uv}{W^3}\big(2i/\gamma-F(E_*,0)\big)\delta^2+O(|\delta|^3).
\]
Since \(F(E_*,0)\) and \(E_2\) are real, multiplication of its imaginary part by \(-2\) gives \(4uv/(\gamma W^3)\) as the coefficient, equal to \(\mathcal K\). The other three eigenvalues remain uniformly below the real axis for sufficiently small real detuning by spectral continuity.

To take the survival limit, let \(P_{\rm slow}(\delta)\) be the spectral projection in \(B\) around the simple root. It converges to \(|d\rangle\langle d|\) by the contour formula for the projection. The fast part of the propagator on \(B\) is uniformly \(O(e^{-\eta t})\) for some \(\eta>0\): enclose its spectrum in a fixed compact contour entirely below \(\operatorname{Im}z=-\eta\), and bound the resolvent on this contour for small \(\delta\). This argument also covers repeated fast eigenvalues. The bright-sector norm is therefore the slow squared norm plus vanishing transients at \(t=s/\delta^2\). Direct row and column projector overlaps give \(\|P_B|r,c\rangle\|^2=Q_{r,c}\). In the middle two directions, \(\langle r,c|\phi_1\rangle=\sqrt u\,(nC-1)/(n-1)\) and \(\langle r,c|\phi_2\rangle=\sqrt v\,(mR-1)/(m-1)\), hence \(|\langle d|r,c\rangle|^2=D_{r,c}\). The permanent dark sector contributes its constant norm squared \(1-Q_{r,c}\), with zero cross term against \(B\). Finally, \(e^{2\operatorname{Im}z_{\rm slow} s/\delta^2}\to e^{-\mathcal K s}\), proving the limit.

## Verification and relation to prior work

The mathematical proof above needs no numerical input. The accompanying `artifacts/verification.py` checks the static weights against exact spectral-projector calculations and the linewidth against full-matrix eigenvalues for a finite selection of parameters. `artifacts/test_survival.py` checks the finite-detuning approximation at representative rescaled times using full matrix exponentials. These are finite corroboration, not proofs of a general or uniform limit.

The rectangular-detector capture law [1] already completely specializes to the two static sitewise formulas and the four-to-three rank drop for a singleton detector. The general connection between eventual transport efficiency and the trap-generated Krylov space was developed in [2]. Dark-state trapping and perturbative survival are established phenomena; the lossy-star law [3] also has an explicit quadratic scale, but concerns a different perturbation and a different spectral cluster. The claim here is restricted to the explicit weighted-rook coefficient and the site-resolved time limit, with no claim to the general quadratic mechanism or absolute historical priority.
