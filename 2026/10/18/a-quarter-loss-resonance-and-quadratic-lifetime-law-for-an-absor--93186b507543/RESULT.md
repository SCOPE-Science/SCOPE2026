# A quarter-loss resonance and quadratic lifetime law for an absorbing weighted rook graph

## Finding

Let \(m,n\geq 2\) be integers and \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\). On the vertex Hilbert space of the Cartesian-product graph \(K_m\square K_n\), define the hopping Hamiltonian and a single irreversible corner absorber by
\[
H_0=\alpha(J_m-I_m)\otimes I_n+\beta I_m\otimes(J_n-I_n),\qquad H=H_0-\frac{i\gamma}{2}|0,0\rangle\langle0,0|.
\]
Starting from a basis vertex \(v=(r,c)\), the survival probability is \(S_v(t)=\|e^{-itH}|r,c\rangle\|^2\); the eventual absorption probability is \(P_v=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}S_v(t)\). Write \(R_r=\mathbf 1_{\{r=0\}}\), \(C_c=\mathbf 1_{\{c=0\}}\), and the hopping detuning \(\delta=m\alpha-n\beta\). Define
\[
Q_{r,c}=\left(R_r+\frac{1-R_r}{m-1}\right)\left(C_c+\frac{1-C_c}{n-1}\right),
\]
and
\[
D_{r,c}=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)}{mn(m+n-2)}
\left(\frac{nC_c-1}{n-1}-\frac{mR_r-1}{m-1}\right)^2.
\]
**Exact absorption and resonance theorem:** For every starting vertex, the answer off resonance is \(P_v=Q_{r,c}\) if \(\delta\neq0\); on resonance, \(P_v=Q_{r,c}-D_{r,c}\) if \(\delta=0\). In particular, averaging uniformly over the \(mn\) initial vertices gives exactly
\[
\overline P_{\delta\neq0}=\frac4{mn},\qquad
\overline P_{\delta=0}=\frac3{mn}.
\]
Thus the anisotropy resonance creates a universal *one-quarter reduction* in mean absorption across all sizes, independent of absorber strength.

**Sharp slow-mode and survival theorem:** Fix \(m,n,\beta,\gamma\) and vary \(\alpha=(n\beta+\delta)/m\) with \(\delta\to0\). Apart from the permanent dark eigenspaces there is exactly one active eigenvalue whose decay rate vanishes at this resonance. If \(z_{\rm slow}(\delta)\) denotes that eigenvalue, then
\[
-2\,\operatorname{Im} z_{\rm slow}(\delta)
=\mathcal K\delta^2+O(|\delta|^3),\qquad
\mathcal K=\frac{4mn(m-1)(n-1)}{\gamma(m+n-2)^3}.
\]
For every vertex \(v\) and every fixed \(s>0\),
\[
\boxed{\lim_{\delta\to0,\,\delta\neq0}S_v(s/\delta^2)
=1-Q_{r,c}+D_{r,c}\exp(-\mathcal K s).}
\]
Both formulas are exact statements for arbitrary fixed positive \(\beta,\gamma\) and arbitrary fixed finite \(m,n\). The limiting survival profile isolates a metastable population of weight \(D_{r,c}\); sources having \(D_{r,c}=0\) do not contribute to that slow population.

## Assumptions and scope

The matrices \(J_k\) and \(I_k\) are respectively the \(k\)-by-\(k\) all-ones and identity matrices. Row indices range from \(0\) to \(m-1\), column indices from \(0\) to \(n-1\). All hopping amplitudes are real and positive, the graph is finite and undirected, and the absorber sits at exactly one vertex. Time evolution is generated by the non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian \(H\), with total absorption flux \(\gamma|\langle0,0|\psi(t)\rangle|^2\). These definitions fix the convention that the absorbing term is \(-i\gamma/2\). The asymptotic assertions hold with \(m,n,\beta,\gamma\) fixed while the detuning tends to zero; no growing-volume or varying-detector-strength limit is claimed. All long-time statements are for \(t\ge0\) and normalized basis initial data.

## Proof

**1. Bright and dark modes.** Let \(U_m=J_m/m\) and \(V_m=I_m-U_m\), with analogous definitions for \(n\). The four mutually orthogonal spectral projectors of \(H_0\), before possible collisions, are
\[
P_{UU}=U_m\otimes U_n,\quad P_{UV}=U_m\otimes V_n,\quad
P_{VU}=V_m\otimes U_n,\quad P_{VV}=V_m\otimes V_n.
\]
Their respective eigenvalues are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
E_{UU}=\alpha(m-1)+\beta(n-1),&E_{UV}=\alpha(m-1)-\beta,\\
E_{VU}=-\alpha+\beta(n-1),&E_{VV}=-\alpha-\beta.
\end{array}
\]
Positivity of \(\alpha,\beta\) guarantees that only the two middle bands can collide, and \(E_{UV}-E_{VU}=m\alpha-n\beta=\delta\). Let \(q=|0,0\rangle\). The squared norms \(w_j=\|P_jq\|^2\) are, in the order just displayed,
\[
w_{UU}=\frac1{mn},\quad w_{UV}=\frac{n-1}{mn},\quad
w_{VU}=\frac{m-1}{mn},\quad w_{VV}=\frac{(m-1)(n-1)}{mn}.
\]
Every \(w_j\) is strictly positive. The rank-one imaginary perturbation vanishes on each subspace \(\operatorname{ran}(P_E)\cap q^\perp\). Conversely, any real eigenvalue of the dissipative \(H\) has an eigenvector orthogonal to \(q\): multiplying its eigenvalue equation by its adjoint gives \(\operatorname{Im} z\,\|\psi\|^2=-\gamma|\langle q,\psi\rangle|^2/2\). Such a vector is then an eigenvector of \(H_0\). Since \(e^{-itH}\) is a contraction, no nontrivial Jordan block can lie on the real axis. Hence the dark components persist, and every component in the spectral cyclic subspace generated by \(q\) decays to zero. The latter subspace is the direct sum of the lines spanned by \(P_Eq\) over *distinct* energies \(E\). Therefore
\[
P_v=\sum_{E\ \mathrm{distinct}}\frac{|\langle v,P_Eq\rangle|^2}{\langle q,P_Eq\rangle}.
\]

**2. Evaluate absorption and the resonance drop.** At \(\delta\neq0\) the four energies are distinct. The scalar overlaps factor into the row and column projector overlaps. Summation over the four products gives the displayed \(Q_{r,c}\). At \(\delta=0\) the two middle projections merge into \(P_{UV}+P_{VU}\). The loss of squared bright projection on merging two bands is the exact weighted Cauchy identity
\[
\frac{|a|^2}{u}+\frac{|b|^2}{v}-\frac{|a+b|^2}{u+v}
=\frac{uv}{u+v}\left|\frac au-\frac bv\right|^2,
\]
where \(a=\langle r,c|P_{UV}q\rangle\), \(b=\langle r,c|P_{VU}q\rangle\), \(u=w_{UV}\), and \(v=w_{VU}\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
\frac au=\frac{nC_c-1}{n-1},\qquad
\frac bv=\frac{mR_r-1}{m-1};
\]
substitution yields \(D_{r,c}\) with no approximation. For a uniformly mixed initial basis state, the mean bright projection is the dimension of the bright subspace divided by \(mn\). The bright dimension is exactly four off resonance and three on resonance, proving the quarter-loss identity.

**3. Quadratic resonance linewidth.** Use the orthonormal band vectors \(\phi_j=P_jq/\sqrt{w_j}\) spanning the four-dimensional cyclic space. At \(\delta=0\), the middle-band subspace has a normalized detector-orthogonal state
\[
|d\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{w_{VU}}|\phi_{UV}\rangle-\sqrt{w_{UV}}|\phi_{VU}\rangle}{\sqrt{W}},
\quad W=w_{UV}+w_{VU}=\frac{m+n-2}{mn},
\]
and a normalized detector-active companion
\[
|b\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{w_{UV}}|\phi_{UV}\rangle+\sqrt{w_{VU}}|\phi_{VU}\rangle}{\sqrt W}.
\]
The dark vector is an isolated simple eigenvector *within this cyclic space*. Detuning has a real off-diagonal matrix element from \(d\) to \(b\) of magnitude
\[
h=\delta\frac{\sqrt{w_{UV}w_{VU}}}{W}.
\]
For the three-dimensional bright complement at resonance, take the \(bb\)-entry of the resolvent at the common middle energy \(E_*\). The rank-one resolvent identity, applied to the three distinct energies, gives
\[
\langle b|(E_*-H_{\mathrm{bright}})^{-1}|b\rangle
=\frac1W\left[\frac{w_{UU}}{E_*-E_{UU}}+\frac{w_{VV}}{E_*-E_{VV}}\right]
-\frac{2i}{\gamma W}.
\]
Its real component affects only the real second-order energy shift. The imaginary component, combined with the Schur complement for the simple dark eigenvalue, gives
\[
\operatorname{Im}z_{\rm slow}(\delta)
=-\frac{2w_{UV}w_{VU}}{\gamma W^3}\,\delta^2+O(|\delta|^3).
\]
Using the explicit weights gives the stated \(\mathcal K\) after multiplication by \(-2\). The eigenvalue and its spectral projection vary analytically for sufficiently small detuning because the resonant dark eigenvalue is simple within the cyclic space and the other three bright eigenvalues have strictly negative imaginary parts.

**4. Double-scale survival.** The squared overlap \(|\langle d|r,c\rangle|^2\) is exactly the Cauchy gap \(D_{r,c}\). All three other bright modes decay exponentially on a finite time scale that stays bounded away from zero as \(\delta\to0\). The permanently dark complement contributes \(1-Q_{r,c}\), independently of the nonzero detuning. At \(t=s/\delta^2\), the slow mode contributes \(D_{r,c}\exp(-\mathcal Ks)\); the cross terms with permanent dark modes vanish identically by orthogonality of the invariant spectral dark and cyclic sectors, and the remaining transients vanish. This proves the displayed limit for each fixed \(s>0\).

## Verification

The standalone `artifacts/verification.py` constructs the *full* finite vertex Hamiltonian and its distinct spectral projectors by matrix-polynomial interpolation, instead of inserting the theorem's tensor-factor projector formulas. It checks 170 exact-rational source absorptions and bright-rank identities across nine different board dimensions, both at and away from resonance. It also checks 24 small-detuning linewidths by direct numerical diagonalization of the full lossy Hamiltonian.

The separate `artifacts/test_survival.py` evaluates 32 long-time matrix exponentials at the prescribed detuning-dependent time scale, for several board sizes, source orbits and rescaled times. All comparisons passed with stated numerical tolerances. These finite tests are corroboration; the arbitrary-size statements follow from the proof above. Run both scripts with Python and the dependencies in `artifacts/requirements.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Krovi and Brun established degeneracy-based quantum dark spaces and infinite hitting times in 2006. Varbanov, Krovi, and Brun analyzed continuous-time quantum-walk hitting questions using measurement-based definitions. Krapivsky, Luck, and Mallick analyzed survival under quantum traps and avoiding modes, particularly in one-dimensional lattices. Razzoli, Paris, and Bordone (2020 preprint; 2021 publication) use exactly the same kind of non-Hermitian single-trap dynamics and establish that transport efficiency equals projection onto the trap-generated Krylov space; they evaluate complete and bipartite graphs, strongly regular graphs, joined cliques, and clique simplices, and its strongly regular type-I class can already cover the unweighted \(3\)-by-\(3\) rook graph as a special case. Their treatment does not cover general rectangular anisotropy, the parameter-driven middle-band collision, or its detuning linewidth. These foundational descriptions and general spectral mechanisms are prior mathematics.

The present finite weighted-rook-graph result is the evaluated all-size *site-resolved absorption discontinuity*, the exact dimension-free one-quarter reduction in mean capture at the middle-band collision, and the sharp detuning-squared linewidth and survival crossover with their explicit \(m,n,\gamma\) coefficients. The general dark-space projector criterion alone does not supply these evaluated formulas. Relevant general graph-search reductions and resonance phenomena in other quantum graphs concern different Hamiltonians and observables. Searches were not exhaustive, so an equivalent treatment in less accessible quantum transport literature remains possible.

## Limitations

The absorption model is a non-Hermitian effective single-sink evolution, not repeated projective measurement or a full system-and-environment quantum master equation. The result does not address multiple sinks, disorder, non-complete graph factors, arbitrary site-dependent couplings, or simultaneous large-volume and small-detuning limits. The quadratic asymptotic has a remainder for fixed positive parameters and does not assert a parameter-uniform error bound as detector strength tends to zero or infinity. At exact resonance the slow state is permanently dark; limits in large time and vanishing detuning do not commute. The work has no independent scientific audit.
