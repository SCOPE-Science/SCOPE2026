# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The four product spectral projectors of the weighted rook Hamiltonian have strictly positive detector weights. At a collision exactly two projections merge, and the weighted Cauchy identity evaluates the ensuing bright-projection loss. Contraction of the single-sink semigroup identifies the permanent dark complement exactly. An explicit rank-one resolvent and simple-eigenvalue perturbation determine the linewidth; spectral separation gives the survival limit. Full-matrix exact arithmetic checks and independent evolution/eigenvalue comparisons reproduce the formulas, without being used in place of the arbitrary-size proof.

## Originality — PASS subject to historical uncertainty
Krovi and Brun (arXiv:quant-ph/0606094) already describe dark quantum-walk states arising from degeneracy; Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (arXiv:0803.3446) treat measured continuous-time hitting, and Krapivsky, Luck and Mallick (arXiv:1311.6298) analyze trap survival and avoiding modes. These general principles are not claimed new. The compared texts do not provide the evaluated single-corner weighted-rook formulas, a quarter-loss population average, or the exact resonant quadratic linewidth and double-scale survival profile. published-finding corpus searches found related Cartesian-product corner search reductions, magnetic-cycle sink perturbations, and quantum-graph resonances, but not an implication of these exact formulas. A more general unexamined spectral-theory application or a historical paper may cover the result; search incompleteness is not evidence of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact resonance law quantifies a singular trap-efficiency change on every product of two complete graphs, not simply the existence of dark states. Its source dependence identifies which starts excite the critical mode; the size-dependent quadratic linewidth and full long-time profile quantify the metastable transport transition. These are directly relevant to detector placement, coherent transport, and the distinction between static dark subspaces and near-dark lifetimes.

## Closest material and limitations
The four foundational papers above have different primary objectives, and previous cyclic weak-flux sink results concern a distinct graph with different spectral collisions. This theorem is restricted to a homogeneous weighted Cartesian product, exactly one Markovian sink, and fixed finite size. The literature comparison is not exhaustive; independent validation and historical priority review have not been performed.
