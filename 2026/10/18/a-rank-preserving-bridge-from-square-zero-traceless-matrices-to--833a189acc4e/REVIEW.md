# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** A nonzero traceless matrix has a unique minimum-entry valuation and therefore a unique primitive trace-zero quotient matrix. Scalar multiplication by the corresponding uniformizer power is independent of lifts. Cayley--Hamilton and the Smith invariant factors prove that square-zero is equivalent to a precise upper bound on the primitive matrix's image exponent, and multiplication preserves image cardinality. The elementary valuation counts use the smooth nonzero locus of the quadratic \(a^2+bc\) in every characteristic. Direct complete finite enumerations independently corroborate the coefficient table. The proof's quantifiers include all finite commutative chain rings, including mixed characteristic.

**Originality: PASS (correspondence only).** Carnevale--Shechter--Voll (arXiv:1709.02717, Example 1.3) already provide precisely the same rational coefficient series for primitive trace-zero matrix images. Hence the numerical formula by itself is NOT a new finding. The inspected paper's Theorem 1.2, Example 1.3 and §2.2 treat elementary divisors and image growth and do not identify their strata with square-zero trace-zero matrices at a bounded ring length. The proved canonical rank-preserving map is a different mathematical statement, not implied by a coincident series. Britnell--Wildon (arXiv:1001.0811) address field-level commuting types, and Stasinski--Voll (arXiv:1104.1756) address representation zeta phenomena. Targeted mathematical-record searches found no equivalent correspondence. An earlier valuation-scaling bijection in other terminology remains a genuine priority uncertainty.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The map structurally transfers a published local image-zeta count to a nonlinear square-zero locus and explains a previously non-obvious stabilization of square-zero image-size fibers across ring length **and ring characteristic**. It is a uniform identification for all finite chain rings, not merely a recomputation for a small ring; it identifies which primitive quotient strata contribute to each exact nilpotent rank and may be used to transport further primitive-matrix statistics in this two-dimensional setting. No broader consequence or higher-dimensional theorem is asserted.

**Limitations.** No extension to non-chain rings or larger matrices has been established; complete historical priority cannot be guaranteed. Finite verification does not prove the universal theorem.
