# A relative simplicial collapse between the pure Morse complexes of a tetrahedron and its boundary

## Finding
For the three-simplex \(\Delta^3\) on four labeled vertices and its boundary \(\partial\Delta^3\), let \(\mathcal M_P(K)\) be the pure complex of acyclic matchings on the nonempty-face Hasse diagram of \(K\): its facets are precisely the maximum-cardinality acyclic matchings, and all their submatchings are faces. The natural simplicial inclusion is a **simplicial collapse**:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3)\searrow\mathcal M_P(\partial\Delta^3).}
\]
An explicit, machine-checkable sequence has exactly \(3428\) elementary free-face collapses, retaining **every** boundary-pure face. It follows that this particular natural inclusion is a simple-homotopy equivalence and induces isomorphisms of integral homology, compatible with the natural tetrahedral permutation action. No equivariant collapse sequence is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
All simplicial faces of the original tetrahedron and its boundary are nonempty. Label the four vertices \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). A Hasse incidence is an ordered pair \((\sigma,\tau)\) with \(\sigma\subset\tau\) and \(|\tau|=|\sigma|+1\). A matching uses no Hasse-diagram vertex twice. Orient unmatched Hasse edges downward, matched edges upward; the matching is **acyclic** precisely when this directed graph has no directed cycle. The simplicial vertices of \(\mathcal M(K)\) are individual Hasse incidences. The *pure* subcomplex \(\mathcal M_P(K)\) is generated by its top-dimensional (maximum-cardinality) acyclic matchings. For \(\Delta^3\) such matchings have seven incidences, and for \(\partial\Delta^3\) they have six. These pure complexes are distinct from both the ordinary all-acyclic complex \(\mathcal M(K)\) and the generalized all-matching complex \(\mathcal{GM}(K)\).

The claim is for precisely this four-vertex tetrahedron and its boundary, not for all simplices. It identifies the *actual* inclusion, rather than merely asserting an abstract homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
**Step 1: exhaustive finite descriptions.** Enumerate the \(15\) nonempty tetrahedral faces and their \(28\) cover incidences, or the \(14\) nonempty boundary faces and their \(24\) incidences. Traverse increasing incidence indices; retain a set only if neither Hasse endpoint is reused and the reversed-arrow orientation is acyclic (checked by a complete topological-sort test). At cardinalities seven and six, respectively, there are exactly \(256\) matchings in each case. All subsets of these optimal matchings give the two pure complexes. Independently reconstructed face vectors, from dimension zero upward, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f(\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3))&=(28,300,1544,3680,3672,1600,256),\\
 f(\mathcal M_P(\partial\Delta^3))&=(24,216,896,1680,1152,256).
\end{aligned}
\]
The natural correspondence of incidences confirms that every boundary-pure face occurs in the full pure complex, with the same simplicial vertices.

**Step 2: relative cone decomposition.** The full Hasse diagram has four additional incidences, each pairing a triangular facet with the tetrahedral three-face. Denote their corresponding simplicial vertices by \(a_0,\ldots,a_3\). No discrete vector field can contain two of these incidences because they share the tetrahedral three-face. Consequently every face of the full pure complex not in the boundary pure complex contains exactly one \(a_i\). The certified links \(L_i=\operatorname{lk}_{\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3)}(a_i)\) lie entirely in the boundary pure complex, and each has the nonempty face vector
\[
(21,162,500,630,336,64).
\]
There are \(1714\) faces in each link when the empty face is included.

**Step 3: explicit free-face eliminations.** The supplied `collapse_certificate.json` lists \(3428\) ordered pairs of bitmasks of Hasse incidences. `verify_collapse.py` reconstructs both pure complexes *independently of the certificate*, starts from every face of the full pure complex, and checks each proposed \((\alpha,\beta)\) in sequence: both are present; neither belongs to the boundary pure subcomplex; \(\alpha\) has codimension one in \(\beta\); \(\beta\) has no coface; and \(\alpha\) has exactly one immediate coface (namely \(\beta\)). The pair is therefore an elementary simplicial collapse. The verifier removes those two faces, updates coface counts, and ultimately checks **equality** of the remaining face set with the original boundary pure face set. In particular the sequence removes \(6856\) distinct faces and leaves \(4224\) nonempty faces untouched.

There is also a structural reading of the certificate: each \(L_i\) is collapsed to a vertex by \(856\) elementary pairs; lifting these pairs to the cone \(a_i*L_i\), then removing the last apex edge together with \(a_i\), requires \(857\) relative pairs per cone. Because a face contains at most one apex vertex, these cone removals do not interfere. Thus \(4\cdot857=3428\) verified elementary collapses produce precisely \(\mathcal M_P(\partial\Delta^3)\).

A finite enumeration is an exhaustive proof here: all tetrahedral nonempty faces, all possible matchings of maximum cardinality, every pure-complex face, and every proposed collapse are enumerated. The result does not extrapolate any finite sample to an infinite family.

## Verification
The self-contained standard-library files `verify_collapse.py` and `collapse_certificate.json` are integral to the proof; `verification_output.txt` records the expected output. From a directory containing both files, execute:

```text
python3 verify_collapse.py collapse_certificate.json
```

The verifier independently computes the maximum matching counts \(256\) and \(256\), exact pure face vectors, and all four links before processing the certificate. It asserts the relative free-face conditions for **all \(3428\) pairs**, then confirms exact equality with the \(4224\)-nonempty-face target. It performs no numeric approximation, randomized search or reliance on external packages. The ordered bitmasks, not the recorded program output alone, form the reproducible finite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Scoville, *The complex of discrete Morse matchings of the \(n\)-simplex: homotopy types and structural results* (2026), defines these pure complexes and establishes, by separate homology and connectivity calculations, \(\mathcal M_P(\Delta^3)\simeq\mathcal M_P(\partial\Delta^3)\simeq\bigvee^{81}S^3\) (Theorem 22). Its Table 1 gives the two full face vectors, and Proposition 9 gives the maximal matching count correspondence. Those homotopy types and face counts are prior results, not the contribution here. The new statement is stronger in a different direction: the **particular natural inclusion** is realized by a concrete simplicial collapse, hence by a simple-homotopy equivalence. In the same article, Example 2.2 establishes an analogous collapse for the two-simplex, but Theorem 22 for the tetrahedron does not provide a relative collapse. Section 6 treats generalized (possibly cyclic) matchings; its noncollapsible cone link must not be confused with the acyclic *pure* links here.

Chari and Joswig introduced the complex of discrete Morse matchings; Donovan, Lin and Scoville studied homotopy and strong homotopy techniques for other base complexes. No claim about a general-simplex collapse, a collapse of the full ordinary Morse complex, or the generalized matching complex is made.

## Limitations
This is a finite, certificate-dependent simple-homotopy statement about \(\Delta^3\) only. It does not prove the corresponding statement for higher-dimensional simplices, settle any general conjecture, or supply an equivariant sequence of free-face eliminations. The four-label permutation action preserves the source, target and inclusion; the induced integral homology isomorphism commutes with this action, but an \(S_4\)-equivariant collapse is neither checked nor asserted. Scientific originality is subject to the possibility of an unindexed equivalent relative collapse or a stronger already-published general result. Independent verification beyond the attached executable same-study checks has not been performed.
