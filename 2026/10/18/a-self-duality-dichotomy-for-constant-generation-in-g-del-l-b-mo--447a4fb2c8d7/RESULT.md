# A self-duality dichotomy for constant generation in Gödel–Löb modal fragments
## Finding

Let \(C\) be any Boolean clone, understood as a clone of positive-arity Boolean operations, and consider the simple modal fragment generated by the Boolean connectives in \(C\), both ordinary modalities \(\Box,\Diamond\), and propositional variables, with no freely supplied Boolean constants. Write \(\mathsf{GL}\) for Gödel–Löb provability logic and \(\mathsf D\) for the clone of all self-dual Boolean operations, those satisfying
\[
f(\neg a_1,\ldots,\neg a_r)=\neg f(a_1,\ldots,a_r).
\]
Then the fragment defines at least one of the propositional constants \(\bot,\top\) up to \(\mathsf{GL}\)-equivalence **if and only if** \(C\not\subseteq\mathsf D\).

More precisely:

1. If \(C\subseteq\mathsf D\), **every** formula in the fragment is contingent over finite transitive irreflexive Kripke frames: it is true at some pointed model and false at another. Thus neither constant is definable.
2. If \(C\not\subseteq\mathsf D\), a constant is defined by a formula using one propositional variable, one Boolean connective from \(C\), and modal nesting depth at most two. Its truth value is determined explicitly by a witness to failure of self-duality.

For a Boolean clone \(C\) which is **constant-free** propositionally (neither constant Boolean operation belongs to \(C\)), this equivalence decides exactly when adjoining \(\Box,\Diamond\) produces a *new definable Boolean constant*. It does not claim a full classification of all newly definable nonconstant Boolean operations.

The depth bound is attained: for the clone generated by conjunction, \(\bot\) first becomes definable at modal depth two; for the clone generated by disjunction, \(\top\) first becomes definable at modal depth two.

## Assumptions and scope

A Boolean clone contains projections and is closed under composition; operations may have any positive arity. A formula of the fragment is built from propositional variables, connectives denoting members of \(C\), and \(\Box,\Diamond\), without adding \(\bot,\top\) unless expressible through the specified connectives. Semantic definability of a constant means equivalence to that constant at every world in every \(\mathsf{GL}\)-model under every valuation.

We use the standard finite-frame completeness of \(\mathsf{GL}\): its theorems are exactly the formulas valid on all finite transitive irreflexive Kripke frames. Such frames may have any finite nonzero cardinality. The formula variables need not be distinct; substitution can repeat one variable.

Write \(\tau(p)=\Box\Diamond p\) and \(\nu(p)=\Diamond\Box p\). On every finite transitive irreflexive frame, \(\tau(p)\) holds precisely at worlds with no successor, and \(\nu(p)\) holds precisely at worlds with a successor, independently of the valuation of \(p\).

## Proof

**1. Complementary terminal/nonterminal selectors.** Let \((W,R)\) be a finite transitive irreflexive frame. A terminal world has no \(R\)-successor, so \(\Box\Diamond p\) is vacuously true and \(\Diamond\Box p\) false. Any nonterminal world \(w\) has an \(R\)-maximal successor \(z\): take a longest finite path from \(w\) and use transitivity to obtain \(wRz\). This \(z\) has no successor. Therefore \(z\not\models\Diamond p\) but \(z\models\Box p\). Consequently \(w\not\models\Box\Diamond p\) and \(w\models\Diamond\Box p\). Thus \(\tau,\nu\) are complementary and valuation-independent.

**2. Constructing a constant from failure of self-duality.** Suppose some \(r\)-ary \(f\in C\) is not self-dual. By the Boolean law of excluded middle, there is a bit vector \(a\in\{0,1\}^r\) and \(c\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
f(a)=f(\neg a)=c.
\]
For each \(i\), set \(\sigma_i(p)=\tau(p)\) when \(a_i=1\) and \(\sigma_i(p)=\nu(p)\) when \(a_i=0\). The vector of their truth values is \(a\) at a terminal world and \(\neg a\) at a nonterminal world. Hence
\[
f(\sigma_1(p),\ldots,\sigma_r(p))\equiv_{\mathsf{GL}}c,
\]
where \(c\) denotes \(\bot\) for zero and \(\top\) for one. The fragment contains the displayed formula, its modal depth is exactly two, and only \(p\) is used.

**3. Contingency for all self-dual bases.** Assume every \(f\in C\) is self-dual. We prove by structural induction that every fragment formula \(\varphi\) has both a satisfying pointed finite transitive irreflexive model and a refuting one. For the induction step, first obtain both kinds of pointed models for every distinct formula \(\psi\) appearing immediately as the scope of a modal subformula of \(\varphi\); these are proper subformulas and hence covered by induction. Take disjoint copies of those witness models and add a new root \(r\) with an arrow to **every** world of every copy. The new frame remains finite, transitive, and irreflexive. At \(r\), each immediately occurring subformula \(\Box\psi\) is false (its scope has a refuting successor), while each \(\Diamond\psi\) is true (its scope has a satisfying successor). The same construction works when no modal scope occurs, by taking an isolated root.

Compare this new root with an isolated terminal world \(t\). Assign arbitrary Boolean values to the propositional variables at \(t\), and assign their complements at \(r\). Each variable then has complementary truth at the two worlds. Each modal subformula also has complementary truth: at \(t\), boxes are true and diamonds false; at \(r\), boxes are false and diamonds true. Finally, any application of a self-dual Boolean connective to complementary argument values gives complementary outputs. Induction through the Boolean skeleton of \(\varphi\) therefore makes its truth values at \(t\) and \(r\) opposite. Consequently \(\varphi\) has both truth values, closing the structural induction. In particular no such formula can be globally true or globally false.

**4. Sharpness of nesting depth.** In the conjunction-only fragment, every depth-at-most-one formula evaluates to true on a pointed two-world chain \(rRs\) when all propositional variables are true at both worlds: a conjunction-only scope is true, and each depth-one box or diamond of such a scope is true. Thus no such formula defines \(\bot\). Yet \(\Box\Diamond p\land\Diamond\Box p\) is identically false by Step 1. Likewise, every depth-at-most-one formula in the disjunction-only fragment evaluates to false at \(r\) in the same two-world chain when every variable is false: each modal-free disjunction and each depth-one box or diamond of it is false. Thus no such formula defines \(\top\), whereas \(\Box\Diamond p\lor\Diamond\Box p\) is identically true. Both depth-two bounds are sharp.

The equivalence and all stated boundaries follow.

## Verification

Run \(`python3 gl_constant_checker.py`\) from the extracted package. The standalone checker enumerates all finite transitive irreflexive labelled frames on one through four worlds, all valuations of one propositional variable, and every pointed world. It checks the two modal selectors against the primitive successor relation; this gives 242 frames and 28,996 elementary selector assertions.

It also tests the explicit witness criterion on all Boolean truth tables of arity one, two, and three (254 are non-self-dual), and builds positive and negative finite models constructively for six sample formulas over self-dual Boolean operations. The checker reports `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite checks support, but do not replace, the structural induction and arbitrary-arity proof. The full theorem rests on the proof above and finite-frame completeness of \(\mathsf{GL}\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bezhanishvili, ten Cate, Ganguly and Meier (2026) classify simple modal fragments by Boolean clones and prove their generalized Makinson trichotomy. For Type C logics, including \(\mathsf{GL}\), their Theorem 4.15 gives general upper and lower bounds on definable propositional operations but not an exact self-duality test for constants; their Corollary 4.17 leaves general expressive containment for Type C as an open issue. Their Example 4.11 separately notes a \(\top\)-definition using \(\lor,\Box,\Diamond\). The present criterion identifies *all* Boolean clones that can generate some constant in \(\mathsf{GL}\) and gives an explicit, uniform depth-two construction, as well as the converse contingency theorem.

Tanaka (2026) supplies a proof calculus for the positive fragment of \(\mathsf{GL}\) with primitive constants; it does not address constant-free clone bases or the self-dual/non-self-dual dichotomy. The older Rata--Rusu work on expressibility systems for provability logic is a related source whose full text could not be inspected; it is not assumed to lack an equivalent result.

## Limitations

This is a dichotomy for definability of **at least one Boolean constant**, not a theorem identifying exactly which of the two constants is generated by every clone, and not an answer to the full Type C expressive containment problem. It uses both modalities; the one-modality fragments are not classified here.

The construction uses finite transitive irreflexive frames and standard finite-frame completeness of \(\mathsf{GL}\). For other Type C logics, terminal/nonterminal selectors may fail to partition their frames. For infinite frames, finite-frame completeness is the reason semantic calculations suffice for provability in \(\mathsf{GL}\).

Related older functional-expressibility literature may contain an equivalent formulation, and independent external review has not been carried out.
