# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof is independent of finite sampling. Every finite transitive irreflexive frame partitions into terminal and nonterminal points, and the two formulas \(\Box\Diamond p\) and \(\Diamond\Box p\) exactly recognize that partition. A non-self-dual Boolean function takes an equal value on some complementary input pair; substituting the two selectors gives a constant. Conversely, for any self-dual basis, structural induction provides both a satisfying and a falsifying pointed finite GL model for every formula, by combining prior positive/negative models under a new transitively connected root. The two-world depth-one countermodels establish optimality for the conjunction and disjunction bases. The packaged executable test corroborates the semantics and finite witness construction.

## Originality — PASS, subject to documented access risk

The closest comparison is Bezhanishvili et al., *Modal Fragments* (2026), particularly Example 4.11 and Theorem 4.15: they provide one disjunction-based definition and a Type C bound, not the complete self-duality dichotomy or its converse contingency proof. Tanaka (2026) studies GL positive proof systems with constants already present. Prior published-literature comparisons showed no equivalent theorem. The full Rata–Rusu (2000) GL-expressibility paper was not accessible; that older source and unindexed results remain originality risks. Lack of a match is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The result gives a complete and naturally motivated yes/no classification for **whether** adjoining both modalities to a constant-free propositional basis generates any Boolean constant. The partition is by the established self-dual clone of Post's lattice, with explicit bounded-depth witnesses and a strong nondefinability converse, and it directly refines a recognized gap in the study of Type C modal fragments. It deliberately does not claim a full solution to the broader containment problem.
