# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The accepted claim is the exact bridge-retention threshold and the optimal value for a book graph under worst single-edge failure, including universal bridge-weight determination across unrestricted optimal assignments. Concavity of the minimum surviving spectral gap justifies automorphism averaging. A direct four-dimensional quotient yields the cubic characterizing the unique sub-two Fiedler eigenvalue after a spoke failure, and the hub-edge failure has the strictly larger gap. Simple-eigenvalue perturbation establishes strict increase and strict concavity in the bridge-to-spoke ratio. Its scalar optimality equation then has one positive solution below a sharp price and no positive optimum at or above the price. The invariant bridge weight of any unrestricted optimum survives averaging. The exact threshold, special case, asymptotic and intact-network contrast follow algebraically. The included verification program passed six symbolic identities and 1,295 accumulated matrix, spectral, scalar, and asymmetric comparisons. Computation is corroboration, not the source of the infinite-family theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The inspected 2006 Sun–Boyd–Xiao–Diaconis full text formulates the nominal, linearly budgeted spectral-gap problem, but contains no edge-outage minimax objective. The inspected Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao 2007 preprint treats symmetry and intact complete-bipartite fastest mixing, not fault-contingent book-graph bridge pricing. The accessible abstract of Sydney–Scoglio–Gruenbacher 2013 concerns discrete edge rewiring of an intact graph, not a continuous-priced single-outage criterion. Semantic published-results searches for book graphs, priced backup edges, one-edge failure, algebraic connectivity and hub-bridge thresholds found graph-theoretic and nominal-network statements with different invariants, not an implication of this exact threshold. Strong general semidefinite formulations could of course recover individual numerical instances; they do not state the price classification. An unindexed equivalent specialization is possible, and the rewiring paper was not fully accessible.

## Value — PASS

One-edge survivability is an intrinsically motivated design objective for communication and diffusion networks. The result proves a qualitative inversion between intact and fault-tolerant design: an edge that cannot improve the nominal spectral gap becomes mandatory under one-failure risk, even when it costs substantially more than a spoke. The precise finite-size threshold and the asymptotic \(2/m\) cost scale quantify when paying for redundant hub communication is justified, for every book size rather than a collection of numerical examples. The result is intentionally a complete exact criterion for one natural family, not a general theorem for arbitrary networks.

## Limits and closest literature

The stated theorem does not classify every asymmetric spoke allocation and does not treat multiple outages. Prior-work searches cannot prove uniqueness against unseen publications, and the 2013 edge-rewiring paper's full text remained unavailable.
