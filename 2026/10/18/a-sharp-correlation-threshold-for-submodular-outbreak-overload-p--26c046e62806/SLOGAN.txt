For a one-generation star outbreak with shared Beta-distributed infectivity, the vaccination benefit for preventing at least \(r\) secondary infections has diminishing returns exactly when \((r-1)\beta-(d-r)\alpha\ge d-1\) in the interior range \(2\le r\le d-1\); it never does at \(r=1\) and always does at \(r=d\). At fixed mean infectivity, increased positive correlation can cross this sharp boundary even though the expected infections prevented remain unchanged.
