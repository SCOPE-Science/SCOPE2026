# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement is an all-parameter identification classification for the explicitly defined path-power graphs. In the positive direction, the complete-graph case is immediate, path layers give the positive case at power one, and the other positive cases have at most three two-vertex degree cells, with matching connections forming a forest. The degree-cell quotient and the matching-tree label-propagation argument reconstruct every comparison graph. In the negative direction, for every permitted parameter the three distinct stable two-vertex classes support three parallel matchings; switching one matching preserves colour refinement but changes the number of triangles meeting all three classes from two to zero. All boundary inequalities were checked symbolically and through both packaged finite sanity-check programs. Finite checks are corroboration, not the proof of the infinite parameter statement.

## Originality — PASS (same-model, subject to priority verification)

The general theory of amenable graphs, colour refinement and counting logic is established in Arvind–Köbler–Rattan–Verbitsky (2015), Sections 2–5 and Theorem 9, and Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman (2015), Theorem 17. Their criteria explain the general switching mechanism but the inspected passages do not state a closed-form threshold for powers of paths. The contribution is the uniform explicit criterion \(k=1\) or \(n\leq k+4\), the sharp boundary, and the transparent two-edge obstruction. Targeted searches found no overlapping indexed finding; this is not a proof of worldwide priority. The main risk is an overlooked special-family corollary in the literature.

## Value — PASS

This identifies exactly where a standard low-width and highly structured graph family stops being definable up to isomorphism by two-variable counting logic against arbitrary graphs. The parameter boundary is sharp for each power, with an explicit inexpensive witness and an all-size argument. The distinction between identification within the path-power family and identification among all graphs is essential and potentially useful in comparisons of logical and algorithmic graph symmetries.
