For every proper \(n+1\)-palette colouring of the square rook graph \(K_n\square K_n\), the exact number of vertices lacking a uniquely occurring neighbour colour is \(\sum_s a_s^2\), where \(a_s\) counts rows missing palette symbol \(s\). The defect cells form disjoint balanced bicliques, at least \(n\) vertices must fail, this is sharp, and all minimum-defect failures form a perfect matching; the underlying numerical chromatic threshold was already known through adjacent-vertex-distinguishing edge-colouring theory.
