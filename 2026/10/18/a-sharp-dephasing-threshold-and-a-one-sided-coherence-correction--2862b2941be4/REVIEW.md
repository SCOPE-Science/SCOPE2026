# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The claimed four-site result is fixed by explicit Lindblad jump operators and by a closed sixteen-real-variable stationary covariance system. Hermitian uniqueness follows from the boundary-damping energy identity and propagation through the connected hopping chain. The stationary matrix determinant factorizes into strictly positive factors for all real tilt and nonnegative dephasing, and the current numerator is supplied exactly. The tridiagonal correction is a rational function with nonnegative factors and a strict factor of the squared tilt. The derivative numerator has negative coefficients at powers four through twelve; its four low coefficients have ordered unique positive parameter zeros proved by exact rational brackets and Descartes' rule. This proves a unique global dephasing optimum above the exact threshold. The independent full-Fock-space Lindblad checks agree at five parameter pairs. An exact standalone symbolic checker reconstructs all pivotal polynomial identities. These are finite verifications of an analytical proof, not substitutes for an infinite-domain argument.

## Originality (PASS, with priority risk)
Jacob et al. (arXiv:2407.21715, Secs. 2–3 and discussion) establish the physical tilted boundary-driven transport problem, exact correlation equations, exact two- and three-site currents and an approximate all-size tridiagonal current. Their visible full text explicitly leaves exact size-four formulas and exact positive-optimum criteria unprinted. Thus the *general covariance method and dephasing-enhancement phenomenon* are prior art. The new comparison concerns a specific nontrivial consequence of that method: a closed sharp tilt threshold with a global one-max theorem and a strict sign for closure error at the minimal first-uncaptured size. Coates–Lovett–Gauger (arXiv:2106.12567, model and discussion) use extraction and distributed reinjection, which is not the present two-reservoir boundary drive. Contreras-Pulido et al. (arXiv:1407.0924) derive dephasing assistance in a three-dot system, not the present four-site optimization. Searches for four-site exact tilted-chain dephasing optimum and one-sided tridiagonal-error inequalities did not disclose an equivalent claim. A general open-quantum-system theorem or less prominent appendix may imply the special inequality; independent literature priority review is warranted.

## Scientific value (PASS)
A matched four-site wire is the smallest linearly tilted geometry in which the nearest-neighbor closure omits independent long-range coherence. The proved uniform sign of its error explains a systematic bias in an approximation used to estimate transport. The exact transition separates a whole regime in which *no amount* of dephasing enhances the current from a regime with precisely one optimal positive rate, not just a weak-noise derivative test. Such a cutoff has direct meaning for finite quantum-transport devices. The theorem is limited to fixed unit hopping and injection/extraction rates, maximal bias and four sites; it does not claim universal behavior for arbitrary chains or reservoir conventions.

## Closest literature and limitations
Jacob et al., *Dephasing-assisted transport in a tight-binding chain with a linear potential*, arXiv:2407.21715, is the closest same-model source and supplies the general solution machinery. Coates et al., *Localisation determines the optimal noise rate for quantum transport*, arXiv:2106.12567, uses a different reinjection protocol. Contreras-Pulido et al., *Dephasing-assisted transport in linear triple quantum dots*, arXiv:1407.0924, studies a different three-dot process. No unrestricted finite-chain or thermodynamic-limit monotonicity result is claimed. The 2024 paper's linked supplementary material was not independently available as a separate readable document; a hidden same-model equivalence remains possible.
