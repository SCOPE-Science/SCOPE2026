## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limits

Relevant full primary texts were compared by mathematical implication, not by title alone. Chao–Jost (2012) concerns sample-size/coverage standardization; MacKenzie–Royle (2005) optimizes precision in occupancy studies; Carvalho et al. (2015) optimize multispecies monitoring-site choice; Moore–McCarthy (2016) optimize single-species detections across sites under variable costs. Their models do not directly imply the exact sharp six-individual classification or the explicit unbounded reset identity under two finite hypergeometric samples. Heck et al. (1975) supplies a foundational rarefaction formula, acknowledged as prior. Historical findings concerning single-community abundance majorization, rarefaction variance, and singleton counts address different objects, not allocation nesting. Targeted bibliographic and research-index searches found no equivalent exact result. The full Heck (1975) technical note was not accessible, but its verified abstract describes a single-collection rarefaction calculation; an overlooked formulation in older survey-design or maximum-coverage literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result identifies a planning pathology of a natural fixed-budget biological survey problem: optimal design at budget \(m\) may have no extension optimal at \(m+1\), and the necessary revision can be arbitrarily large. The sharp minimum and complete first-layer classification separate a structural phenomenon from a vague counterexample. The disjoint-species control theorem identifies exactly an important setting where budget-consistent planning is possible. This matters to survey programs considering incremental funding without assuming that the new optimum can be obtained by adding a sample to a previous plan.

## Boundaries and future validation

The result is mathematical and model-conditional; observed occupancy, estimation uncertainty, heterogeneous detectability, nonuniform costs, and adaptive survey policies may behave differently. The uniqueness assertion is explicitly about a forced adjacent-budget gap, not every globally nonnested census. Existing general nonnested maximum-coverage phenomena and unavailable or differently indexed biodiversity-planning works pose residual priority risks, so specialist bibliographic review is warranted before any broader originality claim.
