# Scientific review: two-group renewal-booster allocation

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem fixes the contact matrix, renewal laws, immunity curve, per-capita dose-rate budget and the closed allocation domain before asserting a spectral claim. The renewal-reward identity gives the stationary susceptibilities exactly; the two-by-two characteristic polynomial gives a smooth positive Perron root near fairness; and its Taylor coefficient yields the strict local-minimum/maximum threshold. An explicit algebraic inequality, with rational bounds on the exponential, proves that a concentrated split has strictly smaller spectral radius than the otherwise locally minimizing fair split. The exponential-clock comparison is an exact matrix identity plus monotonicity in the allocation magnitude. The standalone verification program reconstructs the critical identities and high-precision comparison. This is a full proof of the stated spectral results, not a claim about nonlinear epidemic trajectories.

**Originality — PASS, subject to independent priority verification.** Foutel-Rodier, Charpentier and Guérin derive renewal-based susceptibility, the two-group next-generation matrix and fixed recurrent vaccine-rate allocation; their Proposition 6 proves fairness for assortative mixing, not the sharp disassortative Erlang-2 curvature threshold or the exhibited fair-local-minimum-but-inferior-to-concentration example. Delmas, Dronnier and Zitt already prove disassortative spectral concavity and related concentration strategies when vaccine cost is linear in effective susceptible fractions. Their result does not transfer to the nonlinear Erlang-2 map from mean booster administration rates to susceptible fractions. Duijzer and colleagues study final epidemic size in noninteracting SIR populations, a different objective. Relevant public research-record searches produced no direct counterpart; lack of a search result cannot establish absolute novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The motivating vaccine-allocation literature explicitly asks which distribution of a fixed recurrent vaccine supply best blocks endemicity. The new exact boundary determines the local fairness response as both assortativity and vaccination intensity vary. The nonlocal counterexample shows a practical optimization pitfall: checking local curvature at an equal split may certify a local minimum without identifying the best rate allocation. Contrasting Erlang-2 and exponential boosters isolates the role of the renewal-clock shape while holding the mean dose budget and immunity curve fixed. The theorem does not assert a globally optimal allocation in the Erlang-2 example, a general epidemic-control recommendation or benefits beyond the stated threshold surrogate.

**Closest literature and residual risks.** Primary mathematical comparisons: Foutel-Rodier, Charpentier and Guérin, arXiv:2306.13633, Sections 3–5 and Proposition 6; Delmas, Dronnier and Zitt, arXiv:2112.08756, Section 4 and Proposition 4.1; Duijzer et al., DOI:10.1111/poms.12788 (accessible abstract and article overview). A broader theorem about nonlinear allocations under recurrent renewal dose-rate budgets could still subsume this special case. Neither a full external proof audit nor exhaustive publication-priority certification has been performed.
