For independently fair-colored equivalence structures with \(c_n\to\infty\) equal classes of size \(s_n\to\infty\), every first-order sentence probability converges exactly when each rare-color mean \(c_n2^{-s_n}\binom{s_n}{j}\) has an extended limit. The zero-one law holds precisely when those limits are all zero or infinite; a single critical profile gives two independent Poisson counts, and oscillating means destroy first-order convergence despite an unchanged uncolored limit theory.
