# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The mass formulas \(a=v\), \(b=3(u-v)\), \(c=1-3u+2v\) are exact. The discriminant is \((3u-v)^2-4v\); its derivative is strictly negative in \(v\) at fixed \(u\). A fully justified Lagrange-multiplier maximization on the five-coordinate sphere gives the sharp upper bound for the centered third power sum, attained by a one-heavy/four-equal vector at fixed second moment. The resulting rational polynomial factors as \(48t^3H(t)\) with \(H(t)\ge2/5\) throughout the admissible interval. The explicit six-category law has negative discriminant \(-1439/810000\). The checker verifies the exact polynomial certificate and finite diagnostics, but the continuous proof is independent of them. Boundary cases with zero masses or zero quadratic coefficient are included.

## Originality — PASS on inspected sources
O'Neill's full open-access article studies occupancy for uniform allocations and related mixtures, not an arbitrary-probability real-rootedness support threshold. Tang and Tang's full article supplies the general real-rootedness characterization, but that theorem alone neither gives the special five-label inequality nor predicts the sharp six-label obstruction. The relevant published occupancy/covariance/exposed-point results found through mathematical-record searching do not imply the present threshold. A possible equivalent formulation in unindexed earlier occupancy or symmetric-polynomial literature remains the principal risk; absence of a search hit is not proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete, exact, small-sample classification by the **natural** complexity parameter, the number of possible species or boxes, rather than a selected numerical curiosity. The threshold identifies when a nonuniform richness law has an independent-Bernoulli convolution representation, and demonstrates the first alphabet support size at which this structural property can fail. The five-variable cubic extremum explains the boundary and the six-variable construction proves sharpness; neither part is a routine substitution into a known formula.
