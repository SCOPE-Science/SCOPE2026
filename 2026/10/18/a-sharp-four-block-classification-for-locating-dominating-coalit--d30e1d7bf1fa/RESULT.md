# A sharp four-block classification for locating-dominating coalitions of complete multipartite graphs

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) be a connected complete multipartite graph with \(r\ge2\), positive integer part sizes, and \(N=\sum_i n_i\ge3\). Let \(s\) be the number of parts of size one. The locating-dominating coalition number satisfies
\[
C_L(G)=\begin{cases}4,&N\ge4,\ \max_i n_i\le2,\ s\le2;\\3,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the parameter is bounded by four throughout the infinite class, and the exact boundary depends on the sizes of the independent twin classes and the number of universal vertices, not on the number of parts.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and connected. A *locating-dominating set* (LD-set) is a subset \(S\subseteq V(G)\) such that every vertex in \(V(G)\setminus S\) has a neighbour in \(S\), and the sets \(N(v)\cap S\) for distinct vertices outside \(S\) are different. An *LD-coalition partition* is a partition into nonempty blocks, no block itself an LD-set, such that every block has another block with which its union is an LD-set. Its maximum number of blocks is \(C_L(G)\). Unlike domination-coalition variants permitting one-vertex dominating blocks, this definition permits no LD block on its own.

For each part \(P_i\) of order at least two, all its vertices are false twins (identical open neighbourhoods). The union \(U\) of all singleton parts consists of universal vertices, which are true twins and have identical signatures on any set not containing them. Write \(p\) for the number of parts of size two when all part sizes are at most two.

## Proof
**Twin obstruction.** If \(S\) is an LD-set, at most one vertex of every non-singleton part \(P_i\) lies outside \(S\): two omitted vertices there have identical signatures on \(S\). Likewise, \(|U\setminus S|\le1\), because two omitted universal vertices have the same signature \(S\). These are necessary conditions; they are not asserted to be sufficient for arbitrary \(S\).

**A three-block partition always exists.** Choose distinct twins \(u,v\): take two from a non-singleton part if one exists, otherwise take two universal vertices (then \(G\) is complete). The partition \(\{V(G)\setminus\{u,v\},\{u\},\{v\}\}\) works. Its large block is not an LD-set because \(u,v\) are indistinguishable from outside; no singleton is an LD-set in a graph of order at least three. Each union of the large block with a singleton has size \(N-1\), hence is an LD-set in a connected graph. Thus \(C_L(G)\ge3\).

**Every partition with at least four blocks has disjoint coalition pairs.** Given such a partition, form its coalition graph \(H\) on the partition blocks: join two blocks when their union is an LD-set. The graph \(H\) has no isolated vertex. A pairwise-intersecting family of edges covering at least four vertices is a star (the alternative triangle covers only three vertices). Suppose therefore that all coalition edges meet a central block \(B\), with at least three distinct neighbours \(L_1,L_2,L_3\).

Consider one false-twin class \(P_i\), or the true-twin class \(U\). Put \(a=|P_i\setminus B|\), or \(a=|U\setminus B|\), respectively. Since each \(B\cup L_j\) is an LD-set, the twin obstruction gives \(|L_j\cap(P_i\setminus B)|\ge a-1\) (respectively for \(U\)). The three disjoint leaf blocks therefore give \(a\ge3(a-1)\), forcing \(a\le1\). So \(B\) contains all but at most one vertex of every false-twin class and of \(U\).

If \(G\) has at least two non-singleton parts, \(B\) meets both and is already an LD-set: every outside vertex is dominated, and distinct omitted vertices in different parts have distinct signatures because \(B\) meets their parts. The same conclusion holds if \(B\) contains a universal vertex, or if \(G\) is complete. The only possible failure is that \(G\) is a star, with a unique universal hub outside \(B\), and one leaf also outside \(B\). Indeed, all other leaves belong to \(B\); the omitted leaf is undominated by \(B\). To make \(B\cup L_j\) an LD-set one must add the hub or this omitted leaf, which occupy at most two distinct leaf blocks. Hence three different leaf blocks cannot all partner with \(B\). This contradicts the star assumption and proves that \(H\) has two disjoint coalition edges.

**Necessary profile.** Let these edges correspond to disjoint pairs \(B_1\cup B_2\) and \(B_3\cup B_4\), each an LD-set. A non-singleton part of size \(q\) contributes at least \(q-1\) vertices to each disjoint union. Therefore \(2(q-1)\le q\), or \(q\le2\). Likewise, the universal twin class of size \(s\) contributes at least \(s-1\) vertices to each disjoint union, so \(s\le2\). Consequently, the four-block case is possible only if every part has size at most two, there are at most two singleton parts, and \(N\ge4\).

**Five or more blocks are impossible.** Under the necessary profile, every two-vertex part contributes exactly one vertex to each of \(B_1\cup B_2\) and \(B_3\cup B_4\). Therefore any fifth nonempty block \(E\) consists solely of universal singleton-part vertices. There is at least one such vertex. If \(s=2\), one is in \(E\). Both disjoint LD unions omit it and must contain the other universal vertex, which is impossible as their vertex sets are disjoint. Thus \(s=1\) and \(E\) is its sole universal vertex. For \(E\) to have a coalition partner \(B_j\), the partner must contain a vertex from every two-vertex part. But \(B_j\) belongs to one of the two disjoint LD unions, in which each two-vertex part contributes exactly one vertex. The other block in that union would consequently contain no two-vertex-part vertices and no universal vertex (the latter is in \(E\)), contradicting the nonemptiness of every partition block. Hence \(C_L(G)\le4\).

**Sharp four-block constructions.** Assume the necessary profile and \(N\ge4\). If \(s=0\) or \(s=1\), there are at least two two-vertex parts. Divide those parts into nonempty groups \(I,J\). In each part of \(I\), put one vertex in \(B_1\) and the other in \(B_2\). In each part of \(J\), similarly split the pair between \(B_3,B_4\). If \(s=1\), put the universal vertex in \(B_1\). Every cross-group union \(B_i\cup B_j\), \(i\in\{1,2\}\), \(j\in\{3,4\}\), is an LD-set: it meets every two-vertex part once, misses at most one universal vertex, and meets at least two distinct original parts, ensuring domination and distinct signatures. None of its constituent blocks is an LD-set because each omits an entire two-vertex part in the other group.

If \(s=2\), there is at least one two-vertex part. Put one vertex of every such part in \(B_1\) and its partner in \(B_2\), and let \(B_3,B_4\) be the two universal singletons. Every union of a block from \(\{B_1,B_2\}\) with a block from \(\{B_3,B_4\}\) is an LD-set. Neither \(B_1\) nor \(B_2\) is an LD-set because it omits both true-twin universal vertices; neither singleton block is an LD-set. This completes the four-block construction and the theorem.

## Verification
The accompanying independent program `verify_ld_coalitions.py` builds the adjacency matrix directly and tests the domination and outside-signature conditions for every vertex subset. It enumerates every unordered vertex partition by restricted-growth strings, computes the largest valid coalition partition, and compares this to the theorem. It checks every connected complete multipartite size profile of orders \(3\) through \(9\), plus eight selected profiles of order \(10\), a total of \(94\) profiles. All comparisons passed. The theorem for arbitrary size follows from the preceding proof, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Chellali, Dobrynin, Foucaud, Golmohammadi and Valenzuela-Tripodoro introduced locating-dominating coalition partitions and developed general bounds, exact values for paths and cycles, and characterizations of graphs satisfying \(C_L(G)=|V(G)|\) in arXiv:2602.18760. In particular, their Proposition 2.8 gives \(C_L(G)\le N-\gamma_L(G)+2\), and Proposition 4.1 includes small cases such as \(C_4\) and \(K_4-e\). These results do not give the uniform bound four and its exact multipartite threshold; for many two-vertex parts the general bound grows with the number of parts. A separate publicly indexed result on *self-* and *solid-* locating domination of complete multipartite graphs concerns different set definitions and does not evaluate coalition partitions.

## Limitations
The statement assumes connected complete multipartite graphs with at least three vertices and the strict coalition convention (no individual LD-set blocks). It does not enumerate every maximizing partition or address coalition variants based on domination alone. The literary priority comparison includes the accessible initiating primary preprint and targeted record/phrase searches; equivalent work under an unindexed synonym remains possible. No external independent scientific audit has been performed.
