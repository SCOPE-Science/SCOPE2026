# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The twin-class obstruction limits the complement of any locating-dominating set. A coalition graph with at least four blocks cannot be a star: three distinct partners force its centre to be an LD-set, except in a star graph where only two outside vertices can repair the failure. Two disjoint coalition edges force all non-singleton parts to have order two and the universal twin class to have at most two vertices. An extra fifth block would contain only a universal vertex and cannot have a partner. Explicit four-block partitions attain the bound in all permitted profiles, while two twin vertices generate a three-block partition in every remaining case. Independent exhaustive enumeration agrees on 94 size profiles through order ten.

## Originality
PASS within available evidence. The initiating full-text paper arXiv:2602.18760v2 introduces this coalition invariant, evaluates paths and cycles, and supplies general upper bounds and small-order classifications, but not a connected complete-multipartite all-parameter classification. Proposition 4.1 overlaps at four-vertex examples and is explicitly credited. Its size-dependent bound does not imply the new uniform bound for arbitrarily many two-vertex parts. Publicly indexed results on self- and solid-locating domination address different variants. Searches for locating-coalition, LD-coalition, complete multipartite, complete bipartite, universal-vertex, and twin-class formulations did not identify a covering theorem; indexing omissions remain a real risk.

## Value
PASS. The result determines a newly introduced partition invariant on the entire natural class of connected complete multipartite graphs, including arbitrarily many twin classes and universal vertices. Its sharp jump between three and four blocks isolates a structural boundary that cannot be read from order or domination number alone. The constant ceiling four is independent of graph size, considerably sharper than the known general bound on this class.
