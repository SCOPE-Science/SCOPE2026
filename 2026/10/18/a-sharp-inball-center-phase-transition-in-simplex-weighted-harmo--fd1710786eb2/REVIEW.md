# Review of the simplex-weighted harmonic-polytope inball theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete harmonic facet structure is supplied by Ardila–Escobar's Proposition 3.8. Positive Minkowski coefficients preserve the normal fan; each facet slack at the symmetry-fixed center and each projected normal norm are computed explicitly. An endpoint comparison for affine functions in the weight establishes the global lower envelope and all tangent-facet equality cases. The high-weight center-locus derivation checks every facet, not only the active ones. The self-contained finite checker independently enumerates facet cardinalities, confirms the cardinality identities, and verifies the original Minkowski support functions.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** The known unweighted inradius theorem already implies the specialization at weight one and is explicitly excluded from novelty. The principal new mathematical content is the positive-weight phase threshold, the loss of center uniqueness despite a constant normal fan, and a complete higher-dimensional center-locus formula. The inspected original harmonic-polytope paper provides facets but no metric phase result; the inspected bipermutahedron paper studies a different, finer deformation fan. Searches did not reveal an earlier all-weight solution, though alternative descriptions in general Minkowski-interpolation literature remain a genuine risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The exact center-dimension jump from zero to \(n-1\), at a sharp weight scaling quadratically in the dimension, separates metric structure from unchanging polytope combinatorics. It also explains why the inradius of these simplex-weighted Minkowski sums eventually saturates and supplies every optimal center rather than a single symmetry point. This is an independently motivated geometric phenomenon, not solely a recomputation of the known unit-weight radius.

## Sources and limitations
Ardila–Escobar, *The harmonic polytope*, arXiv:2006.03078 (2020), Proposition 3.8; Ardila, *The bipermutahedron*, arXiv:2008.02295 (2020); published 2026 note *Exact Euclidean inradius and incenter transition of the harmonic polytope*. The result concerns only the standard orthogonal-coordinate metric, positive diagonal-simplex weight, and dimensions \(n\ge3\). General asymmetric weights and the singular zero-weight endpoint are not treated; absolute literature uniqueness cannot be certified from searches.
