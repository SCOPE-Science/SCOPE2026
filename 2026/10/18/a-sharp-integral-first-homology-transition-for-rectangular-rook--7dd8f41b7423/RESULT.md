# A sharp integral first-homology transition for rectangular rook neighborhood complexes

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(m,n\ge3\), let \(R_{m,n}=K_m\square K_n\) be the rook graph on the cells \([m]\times[n]\), with adjacency when two distinct cells share a row or column. Let \(\mathcal N(R_{m,n})\) denote its **open** Lovász neighborhood complex. Then
\[
H_1(\mathcal N(R_{m,n});\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^2,&m=n=3,\\
\mathbb Z,&\min(m,n)=3<\max(m,n),\\
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&m,n\ge4.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the first integral homology changes sharply from a free group to nontrivial two-torsion precisely when both board dimensions reach four, and the torsion persists at every larger rectangular size.

## Assumptions and scope
A nonempty set of vertices \(\sigma\subseteq[m]\times[n]\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(R_{m,n})\) exactly when some cell \(y\notin\sigma\) shares a row or column with each vertex of \(\sigma\). The center is excluded: this is not the closed-neighborhood, independence, matching, or clique complex. All homology is unreduced integral simplicial homology. The statement concerns exactly dimensions \(m,n\ge3\), without an upper cutoff; permutations of the two coordinates do not alter the complex.

## Proof
**1. Presenting first homology by triangle boundaries.** Fix \(m,n\ge3\) and write \(v=mn\). Every two distinct cells have a common rook neighbor: for cells in a shared row or column use a third cell there, and otherwise use either other corner of their rectangle. Hence the one-skeleton of \(\mathcal N(R_{m,n})\) is the complete graph on \(v\) vertices. The integral cycle lattice of this skeleton has rank \(\binom{v-1}{2}\), with basis \(z_{ab}=e_{0a}+e_{ab}-e_{0b}\) for \(1\le a<b<v\), using row-major vertex indices. For each increasing triangle \((a,b,c)\) admitting a common rook neighbor, its oriented boundary has coordinates \(z_{bc}-z_{ac}+z_{ab}\), omitting those terms whose first index is zero. Consequently \(H_1\) is the cokernel of this explicit integer matrix \(B_{m,n}\). Higher-dimensional simplices cannot affect first homology.

**2. Three finite bases with exact integral witnesses.** The accompanying file `base_certificates.json` specifies, without random seeds, actual triangles and cycle-basis rows for square minors of these matrices. The standalone `verify_rook_h1.py` regenerates every triangle from the rook rule and uses integer-only fraction-free elimination to verify that the minor for \((3,3)\) has order \(26\) and determinant \(-1\), that for \((3,4)\) has order \(54\) and determinant \(+1\), and that for \((4,4)\) has order \(105\) and determinant \(+2\). In these cases the full cycle-basis ranks are respectively \(28,55,105\). These exact minors prove that the first two cokernels are torsion-free once their rational ranks are shown to be at most \(2\) and \(1\), while the last is finite of order dividing two.

For the free-rank bounds, write \(\vartheta(i,j)=0\) if \(i=j\), \(+1\) when \(j-i\equiv1\pmod3\), and \(-1\) otherwise. Whenever there are exactly three rows, define the skew integral edge cochain
\[
\omega((i,a),(j,b))=\vartheta(i,j)\begin{cases}-2,&a=b,\\1,&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]
Each triangle with a common rook center has vertices on the two legs of that center. For three rows, its row labels are either all equal, consist of two equal labels, or are three distinct labels. In each case the sum of \(\omega\) around the triangle vanishes (in the last case, \(-2+1+1=0\) times the cyclic row-orientation sign). Therefore \(\omega\) is an integral cocycle, and it evaluates to \(-6\) on the oriented three-edge cycle using all three cells in a fixed column. For the \((3,3)\) board, transposing rows and columns gives a second cocycle; evaluation on the three-edge cycles of a full row and full column yields two independent cohomology functionals. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}B_{3,3}\le26\), and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}B_{3,4}\le54\). Together with the unit minors, this gives \(H_1(\mathcal N(R_{3,3});\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2\) and \(H_1(\mathcal N(R_{3,4});\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\).

For four rows and four columns, define the mod-two edge cochain \(\epsilon(u,v)=1\) precisely when the two cells are in **different rows and different columns**. A triangle witnessed by one center has zero or two cross-leg pairs, so \(\epsilon\) vanishes on every triangle boundary. On the three-edge cycle with successive vertices \((0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\), it evaluates to one. Hence the first homology of the \((4,4)\) complex has a nonzero mod-two quotient; the determinant-two minor bounds its order by two, proving it is \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

**3. Adding rook cells cannot create new first homology.** We use a standard simplicial attachment lemma: if \(K'\) is obtained from \(K\) by adding one vertex \(x\) with a nonempty connected link in \(K'\), then the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for \(K'=K\cup(x*\operatorname{lk}(x))\) gives a surjection \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\twoheadrightarrow H_1(K';\mathbb Z)\), provided \(K\) is connected. The link need only have a connected one-skeleton.

To apply this within the *final* rook neighborhood complex, start with the cells of the \(4\)-by-\(4\) northwest rectangle when \(m,n\ge4\). Add the remaining rows, one cell at a time in each of the first four columns, then add the remaining columns, one cell at a time over all rows. At every step, use the subcomplex **induced by the cells already included**, with simplex membership checked against the full final rook graph. Its one-skeleton remains complete. The link of each new cell \(x\) has all earlier cells as vertices and has a connected one-skeleton, as follows.

For a new-row cell \(x=(r,c)\) with \(r\ge4\), \(c<4\), the earlier cells lie in columns \(0,1,2,3\). Each earlier \(u\) attaches in the link to a cell in the base rectangle: if \(u=(i,j)\), \(i\ne r\), \(j\ne c\), use the common neighbor \((r,j)\) and a base cell \((k,j)\) with \(k\ne i,r\); if \(j=c\), use neighbor \((k,c)\) and base cell \((k,d)\) where \(k\ne i,r\), \(d\ne c\); if \(i=r\), use neighbor \((r,d)\) and base cell \((k,d)\) with \(d\notin\{c,j\}\). The link graph induced on the base rectangle is connected: the base cells in column \(c\) connect pairwise via centers \((i,c)\) in unused base rows, and any base cell \((i,j)\) with \(j\ne c\) connects to a column-\(c\) base cell \((k,c)\), \(k\ne i\), using center \((i,c)\).

For a new-column cell \(x=(r,c)\) with \(c\ge4\), every earlier cell is in a column less than \(c\). The link on the base rectangle is connected: cells in each base row \(i\ne r\) connect via center \((i,c)\); those in row \(r\), if present in the base, connect to cells of other rows via centers \((r,d)\) in the first four columns; cells in distinct rows outside row \(r\) connect along base columns via a center \((r,d)\) whenever needed. An earlier cell \(u=(i,j)\) with \(i\ne r\) and \(j<4\) connects to a base cell \((k,j)\) using center \((r,j)\), choosing a base row \(k\ne i,r\). A cell \(u=(r,j)\) connects to base cell \((k,d)\) using center \((r,d)\) for a base column \(d\ne j\) and base row \(k\ne r\). An earlier cell \((i,j)\) with \(i\ne r\), \(j\ge4\) connects through center \((i,c)\) to \((i,d)\) in an earlier base column \(d<4\); the latter has already been connected to the base rectangle. All such witnesses are allowed even if their centers have not yet been included, because the complex is induced from the full final neighborhood complex. Thus every link is connected.

For \(m=3\), start instead with the first four columns of the \(3\)-by-\(n\) graph and add new columns in the same order. The preceding new-column proof still works: it only needs three base rows, four base columns, and no extra rows. Interchanging coordinates treats \(n=3\).

**4. Preserving the lower bound under extension.** For \(m,n\ge4\), the original \((4,4)\) complex has exactly the same **two-skeleton** as the induced complex on those sixteen cells in the larger rook graph. Indeed, any triple with a common external witness must all lie in an old row or old column, and the fourth old cell of that row or column already supplies a witness; mixed triangles cannot acquire external witnesses. Thus the first-homology group of this initial subcomplex remains \(\mathbb Z/2\). The connected-link attachment lemma therefore makes every larger first-homology group a quotient of \(\mathbb Z/2\); the global mod-two cocycle \(\epsilon\), evaluated on the same corner triangle loop, prevents its vanishing. This proves the asserted torsion formula.

For \(m=3\), the \((3,4)\) subcomplex likewise preserves its two-skeleton inside all larger boards, because a triple of old cells having a new external witness must occupy an old row, whose fourth old cell supplies an old witness. The link lemma makes \(H_1\) a quotient of \(\mathbb Z\). The cocycle \(\omega\) defined above is still closed on every triangle of the larger \((3,n)\) complex, and evaluates to \(-6\) on the same full-column cycle; consequently the quotient remains infinite and must be \(\mathbb Z\). Transposition handles the other dimension. Together with the \((3,3)\) base this proves every case.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_rook_h1.py` from the directory containing `base_certificates.json`. The program recreates the three base complexes from the coordinate rules, validates all certificate triangles and cycle-basis rows, and confirms the exact integer determinants \(-1,+1,+2\) for minors of sizes \(26,54,105\). It independently checks the integral row and column cocycle identities for all triangles in the finite bases, checks the mod-two cocycle and its nonzero loop pairing in the four-by-four case, and tests every inductive link for boards from \(3\)-by-\(4\) through \(3\)-by-\(8\) and all pairs between \(4\) and \(7\). The script ends with `CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_OK`. The all-size assertion rests on the preceding general attachment and cocycle arguments, not on finite experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's general description of fundamental groups of neighborhood complexes [1] is the principal historical context, but its theorem identifies those groups with a graph-theoretic invariant without calculating the displayed homology groups for rook graphs. The previously published exact \((4,4)\) open-neighborhood computation [2] establishes only the two-torsion base case. The all-parameter calculation for higher-degree rook-neighborhood homology [3] explicitly leaves degrees one and two untreated, and its higher-degree result does not determine the attachment-induced first-homology map used here. The two-row rook-neighborhood classification [4] concerns a different dimensional boundary and does not imply the three-row transition. Closed-neighborhood and Čech results must not be substituted for this open-neighborhood calculation. The new assertion is the full rectangular first-homology classification, including its sharp three-row versus four-row boundary and all-size torsion persistence.

## Limitations
This theorem is about first integral homology, not a full homotopy classification, fundamental-group isomorphism type, higher-degree homology, or equivariant representations. It makes no claim about one-row or two-row boards. The three finite determinants are fully replayable; the infinitude assertion depends on the explicit combinatorial proof that the links remain connected at every extension step. Searches of indexed results and accessible primary literature cannot exclude obscure or unindexed equivalent work; independent external scientific audit has not been performed.
