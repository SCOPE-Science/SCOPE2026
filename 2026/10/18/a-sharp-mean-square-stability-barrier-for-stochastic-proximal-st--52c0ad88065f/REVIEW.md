# Scientific review: a sharp mean-square stability barrier for stochastic proximal steps

**Correctness — PASS.** The derivation follows the exact estimator and shifted client-proximal subproblem in Algorithm 1 of arXiv:2405.20127v1. It reduces the sampled iteration to the two explicitly printed \(3\times3\) state matrices and lifts their independent-sample covariance update to a cone-preserving operator on six symmetric coordinates. The closed determinant identity has one positive threshold root, a positive-definite Lyapunov certificate proves mean-square stability on one side, and a strict determinant sign change plus Perron--Frobenius continuity proves instability on the other. Positive two-step covariance and observability determinants transfer the operator-level instability to the expected squared primal error from a compatible initialization. The direct recurrence, certificates, polynomial identities and rational examples were reproduced by the included checker. The reliance on the standard finite-dimensional Perron--Frobenius theorem and exact arithmetic, rather than on experimental sampling, is explicit.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk.** The source paper proves bounds under a uniform gradient-variance hypothesis and sufficient small steps, not the exact second-moment threshold of this heterogeneous scalar example. The closest inspected public research record establishes only first-moment SPAM stability for two quadratic clients, explicitly excluding mean-square claims. The new result provides a separate second-moment operator, a sharp quintic boundary and an explicit interval where first moments converge but second moments diverge. Earlier stochastic proximal momentum papers examine direct heavy-ball inertia rather than the same-sample SPAM estimator. Searches did not identify a published covariance classification subsuming this one. An undiscovered algebraically equivalent specialized example remains possible; priority is not independently established.

**Value — PASS.** A flat client alternating with a curved convex client, equal probabilities, and half refresh form a natural minimal test of whether corrected stochastic gradient memory retains proximal stability. The exact stepsize frontier is not merely a finite numerical example: it classifies the entire positive stepsize half-line for this algorithm and supplies a compatible initialization with exponential expected squared error despite a stable signed mean. It clarifies which notion of convergence is being controlled when momentum variance reduction is combined with exact local proximal steps. The calculation can serve as a precise regression benchmark for analyses that extend past the bounded-gradient-variance regime. No general multi-client or multidimensional guarantee follows.
