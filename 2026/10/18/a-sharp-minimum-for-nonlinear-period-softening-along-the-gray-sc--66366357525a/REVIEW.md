# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is about one precisely normalized physical-time period residual, not a raw period trend along a particular feed/decay path. The positive-equilibrium Hopf conditions give the exact full parameterization \(0<t<1\), nonzero frequency \(\omega^2=k^2-F^2\), and fixed amplitude normalization as the first Fourier harmonic of the autocatalyst concentration. An exact time-preserving polynomial coordinate reduction yields the cubic complex resonant coefficient. The included SymPy program constructs the quadratic homological transformation and verifies both real and imaginary cubic coefficients algebraically modulo \(\omega^2=k^2-F^2\). The imaginary coefficient is everywhere strictly positive; the radial coefficient vanishes exactly at the known generalized-Hopf point. Standard smoothly parameterized Hopf normal-form and angular-averaging estimates yield \(T\omega_{\mathrm{lin}}/(2\pi)-1=C(t)a^2+o(a^2)\) for *all* small-cycle sequences approaching each fixed Hopf point, with the comparator evaluated at the actual parameters. A derivative sign proof gives the unique sharp global minimizer of the explicit coefficient. The included numerical shooting checker independently corroborates two sample regimes but is not used as an infinite proof. No claim of global periodic-orbit classification is made.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The closest directly inspected work is J. Delgado, L. I. Hernández-Martínez and J. Pérez-López, *Global bifurcation map of the homogeneous states in the Gray–Scott model*, arXiv:1702.03353v1 (2017), especially its Section 7 and appendix. It gives the same two-parameter ODE, rigorous Hopf/Bautin bifurcations, and the real first Lyapunov coefficient. Its printed angular normal form is normalized to \(\dot\phi=1\), which cannot imply a coefficient for *physical-time* period relative to the linear focus at the same parameters. The source does not state the present angular cubic coefficient or unique minimizer. P. Gray and S. K. Scott, *Chemical Engineering Science* 39 (1984), DOI:10.1016/0009-2509(84)87017-7, describe physical oscillation periods along residence-time changes; their indexed abstract does not imply the path-independent first-harmonic coefficient or sharp minimum. Complete 1984 article text was unavailable and its potential treatment of frequency-amplitude dependence remains a residual priority risk. Related earlier cubic-autocatalysis studies use different kinetic detail. Bibliographic searches cannot prove absolute priority, and no unpublished result is claimed excluded.

## Value — PASS
The imaginary cubic resonance is a natural invariant of the physical oscillator that is erased by the time rescaling commonly used in orbital bifurcation classifications. Its positive sign holds on the *entire* nondegenerate Hopf arc and continues through the generalized-Hopf stability-change point, distinguishing period softening from radial criticality. More importantly, the universal sharp minimum gives an exact optimum of a physically measurable normalized cycle period response, with a unique algebraic optimizer and independently checkable global proof. It can guide sensitivity comparisons of chemical cycles without claiming a general theorem for other models. The contribution is not a new statement of the known Hopf or Bautin threshold.
