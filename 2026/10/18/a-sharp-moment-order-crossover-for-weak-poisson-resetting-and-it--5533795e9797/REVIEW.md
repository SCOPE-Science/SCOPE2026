# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Poisson and Gaussian stationary laws are each represented by a conditionally Gaussian stochastic convolution whose variance is a positive exponential functional. A unit-interval decomposition proves the exact necessary and sufficient moment-existence criteria, including their equality boundaries. Finite even moments obey generator recursions. Uniform signed binomial remainder and logarithmic expansion yield the coefficient \(2\lambda/[9(\lambda-\gamma)]\) in the relative-error crossover; strictly convex critical-order equations and the elementary positive function \(z^2-2z+2(1-e^{-z})\) give the two separate moment-existence limits. Independent rational and numerical finite checks support, but do not replace, the infinite-order arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 2026 diffusion-approximation article establishes the weak-reset model and its Gaussian limiting description; the 2022 proportional-reset article gives moment recursions for a model with constant, not linearly proportional, inter-reset drift. Related multiplicative-process work covers general heavy-tail exponents. Neither the motivating work nor the inspected comparators explicitly states the growing-order \(s^{-2/3}\) Poisson-versus-Gaussian stationary moment crossover together with the separated \(s^{-1}\) existence frontiers. This narrower claim excludes generator formulas, diffusion approximation construction, and generic power-tail facts from originality. The principal residual risk is implication by an unlocated general nonuniform moment-approximation theorem; the 2024 rescaling paper's complete text was unavailable and its abstract alone cannot exclude implicit coverage.

## Value — PASS

Second-order diffusion approximations are frequently used when discrete catastrophes are weak and frequent. The result establishes a sharp, motivated limit to this practice: stationary variance agrees exactly whenever finite and each fixed moment agrees asymptotically, but relative moment accuracy is lost at order \(s^{-2/3}\), strictly below the degree \(s^{-1}\) where moment existence diverges. The two scales answer different statistical-physics questions about rare large fluctuations. The constants, boundary conditions, and noncommuting large-order limit are quantified.
