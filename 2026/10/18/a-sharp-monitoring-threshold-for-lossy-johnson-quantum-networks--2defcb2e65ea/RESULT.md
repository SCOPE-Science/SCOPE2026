# A sharp monitoring threshold for lossy Johnson quantum networks

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\), \(1\le k<n\), and \(1\le r\le n-k\). The vertices of the Johnson graph \(J(n,k)\) are all \(k\)-subsets of \([n]\); two vertices are adjacent when they differ by one element. Fix a set \(R\subset[n]\) of \(r\) monitored coordinates, and irreversibly absorb every vertex \(T\) with \(T\cap R\ne\varnothing\). Then a walker initially at an unmonitored vertex has eventual absorption probability
\[
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{abs}}=\min\left(1,\frac{k}{n-r-k+1}\right)}.
\]
For an initially monitored vertex, the probability is one. The entire nondecaying subspace has dimension
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal D=\max\left\{\binom{n-r}{k}-\binom{n-r}{k-1},\,0\right\}}.
\]
Consequently, **all initial states are eventually absorbed if and only if**
\[
\boxed{r\ge\max\{1,n-2k+1\}}.
\]
In the nontrapping regime, this guarantee is uniform in the choice of any fixed strictly positive detector rates, though no uniform finite-time bound is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope

Choose \(J>0\) and consider the adjacency Hamiltonian \(J A\) on \(\mathbb C^{\binom nk}\). Write \(F\) for the monitored configurations that intersect \(R\), and \(C\) for the remaining configurations. Choose independently arbitrary \(\gamma_T>0\) for every \(T\in F\), set \(\Gamma=\sum_{T\in F}\gamma_T|T\rangle\langle T|\), and let \(V_F\) be any Hermitian matrix supported on \(F\times F\). The conditional no-jump evolution is generated by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=J A+V_F-\frac i2\Gamma,
\qquad \psi(t)=\exp(-itH_{\mathrm{eff}})\psi(0).
\]
The cumulative absorption probability is \(1-\|\psi(t)\|^2\) for a normalized input, and its infinite-time limit exists. No onsite perturbation on \(C\), extra cross-coupling, dephasing, or zero absorption rate is allowed. When \(r>n-k\), every configuration is monitored, so certain absorption is immediate; the nontrivial theorem is stated for \(1\le r\le n-k\).

## Proof

**1. Reduction to Boolean inclusion.** Put \(m=n-r\) and identify \(C\) with the \(k\)-subsets of an \(m\)-set. Define the down-incidence matrix \(D:\mathbb C^{\binom mk}\to\mathbb C^{\binom m{k-1}}\) by
\[
(Df)(S)=\sum_{T\supset S,\ |T|=k}f(T),\qquad |S|=k-1.
\]
A monitored configuration with precisely one coordinate in \(R\) has the form \(\{a\}\cup S\), where \(a\in R\) and \(S\) is an unmonitored \((k-1)\)-subset. Its Hamiltonian coupling from \(C\) is exactly \(J(Df)(S)\). A monitored configuration containing two or more elements of \(R\) has no edge directly to \(C\). Hence the entire cross-boundary coupling vanishes if and only if \(Df=0\).

The adjacency matrix within \(C\) obeys the **exact matrix identity**
\[
A_C=D^*D-kI.
\]
Indeed, two different \(k\)-subsets share exactly one \((k-1)\)-subset when adjacent and none otherwise; the diagonal of \(D^*D\) equals \(k\). Consequently, every vector in \(\ker D\) is an eigenvector of \(A_C\) of eigenvalue \(-k\). Its extension by zero to \(F\) is an undamped eigenvector of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with energy \(-Jk\), independently of \(V_F\) and \(\Gamma\).

**2. Exhaustiveness and actual decay.** For an eigenvector \(z\) of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\), taking imaginary parts of its quadratic form gives
\[
\operatorname{Im}\lambda\,\|z\|^2=-\tfrac12\sum_{T\in F}\gamma_T|z_T|^2.
\]
Thus every eigenvalue lies in the lower half-plane; an eigenvalue on the real axis forces \(z_F=0\), and the boundary equation then gives \(Dz_C=0\). Hence all real-energy modes are exactly \(\mathcal D=\ker D\) embedded in \(C\). This subspace is reducing for both \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) and its adjoint: on it each acts as the same real scalar \(-Jk\). The finite-dimensional complementary block has only strictly decaying eigenvalues, so its matrix exponential tends to zero (also for nontrivial Jordan blocks). Therefore, for the orthogonal projector \(P_\mathcal D\),
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\psi(t)\|^2=\|P_\mathcal D\psi(0)\|^2.
\]
This proves both the infinite-time interpretation and independence from every strictly positive monitored rate and all detector-side Hermitian dynamics.

**3. Exact incidence rank.** On subset-rank \(\ell\), let \(U_\ell\) add one element and \(D_\ell=U_{\ell-1}^*\) remove one. Counting add-remove and remove-add paths gives
\[
D_{\ell+1}U_\ell-U_{\ell-1}D_\ell=(m-2\ell)I.
\]
If \(2\ell<m\), the inner-product identity \(\|U_\ell f\|^2=\|D_\ell f\|^2+(m-2\ell)\|f\|^2\) makes \(U_\ell\) injective. Therefore \(D_k=U_{k-1}^*\) is surjective when \(k\le(m+1)/2\). When \(k>(m+1)/2\), complementing every subset identifies \(D_k\) with \(U_{m-k}\), which is injective because \(m-k<m/2\). These overlapping cases give the full-rank identity
\[
\operatorname{rank}D=\min\left\{\binom mk,\binom m{k-1}\right\}.
\]
The claimed dark dimension follows by rank-nullity. This independently reconstructs the particular subset-incidence rank theorem needed here rather than treating a general literature theorem as an unverified black box.

**4. All localized initial states and threshold.** Permutations of the unmonitored \(m\) coordinates preserve \(\ker D\) and act transitively on the vertices of \(C\). Thus every diagonal entry of its orthogonal projector has the same value, namely \(\dim\mathcal D/\binom mk\) by taking the trace. Each unmonitored basis initial state therefore has absorption probability
\[
1-\frac{\dim\mathcal D}{\binom mk}=
\min\left\{1,\frac{\binom m{k-1}}{\binom mk}\right\}
=\min\left\{1,\frac{k}{m-k+1}\right\}.
\]
A basis vector in \(F\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal D\), so it is completely absorbed. Moreover, the normalized uniform vector on \(C\) lies in \(\operatorname{ran}D^*\), since \(D^*\) takes the constant vector on \((k-1)\)-subsets to \(k\) times the constant vector on \(k\)-subsets. Hence this coherent input also absorbs completely. Finally, \(\mathcal D=\{0\}\) holds precisely when \(m\le2k-1\), or \(r\ge n-2k+1\), with \(r\ge1\) already assumed. If \(\mathcal D\ne\{0\}\), any normalized dark vector is a counterexample to absorption for every input. This proves necessity and sufficiency of the universal-monitoring threshold.

## Verification

Run `python3 check.py` with Python 3, NumPy, and SciPy. The script performs 443 finite checks, comprising all valid parameters \(3\le n\le8\), exact expected incidence ranks, dark-subspace invariance, vertex-projector diagonals, eigenvalue damping with randomly unequal positive sink rates, representative direct long-time propagators and coherent inputs, and additional arbitrary Hermitian perturbations on the monitored sector. The maximum observed numerical difference in the directly compared quantities was approximately \(2.23\times10^{-14}\). These finite checks are supporting evidence only; the preceding proof covers every stated integer parameter and arbitrary positive rates.

For example, with \(n=7\), \(k=2\), moving from \(r=1\) to \(r=4\) monitored coordinates gives absorption probabilities \(2/5,\ 1/2,\ 2/3,\ 1\) from any unmonitored basis configuration. The threshold occurs at exactly \(r=4\).

## Relationship to prior work

The general quantum-network distinction between bright and dark states is established by Le, Donati, Severini, and Caruso (2017); their complete-graph example includes the \(k=1\) boundary case. The rank of subset-incidence matrices is classical (Gottlieb, 1966). Spatial-search analyses of Johnson graphs by Tanaka, Sabri, and Portugal (2021), and Lugão and collaborators (2022), concern *Hermitian marked-vertex search*, rather than absorption by an entire coordinate-defined face. Lewis, Banchi, and Bose (2024) map fixed-excitation all-to-all spin networks to Johnson graphs and study local-field marking; that setting supplies a physical motivation for local-coordinate monitoring but not the present irreversible dark-space classification. The contribution here is the exact, all-parameter connection between many-body coordinate monitoring, an incidence-kernel dark eigenspace, universal absorption thresholds, and rate-independent individual absorption probabilities. The general dark-subspace idea and the incidence-rank theorem are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The asserted probabilities are infinite-time no-jump absorption probabilities, not finite-time mixing, detector-click timing distributions, or estimates of experimental readout precision. The coherent Hamiltonian has exactly uniform real hopping \(J>0\) on every Johnson edge, and the absorber strength is strictly positive at every monitored configuration. Arbitrary Hermitian detector-side perturbations are allowed but arbitrary perturbations in the unmonitored region are not. This is not a statement about a generic Johnson-graph unitary search oracle, projective detection after discrete times, nonlinear many-body interactions, or dephasing throughout the graph. A broader representation-theoretic treatment might contain an implicit equivalent statement; separate independent assessment of originality remains appropriate.
