# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

I checked every equilibrium without needing to assume its symmetry or invoke general uniqueness. If the total outer-route flows are x and y, then the cost difference P1−P2 for humans is (p+q)(x−y), and for altruists it is twice this, since the intercepts cancel. At x>y nobody uses P1 (P2 is strictly cheaper for both types), contrary to x>0; x<y is symmetric. Hence x=y=(1−z)/2. At any such split the human shortcut-minus-outer difference is −H+Lz, and the altruist difference is K+2Lz>0. Thus z is all human, 0≤z≤1−α. For positive human mass, z>h excludes using the shortcut and contradicts z>0; z<h and z<1−α excludes human use of the outer routes and contradicts z<1−α. These conditions force z=min{h,1−α}, with the same formula when humans are absent. The stated symmetric type splits realize this z including equality and both penetration endpoints. For arbitrary feasible x,y,z the social cost is S0+Kz+Lz²+(p+q)(x−y)²/2, by expanding the five edge costs. Hence z=0, x=y is the unique aggregate optimum, while there is positive loss for any α<1. K,L>0 imply strictly decreasing loss after the plateau end, not at the end itself. The family has H=1, K=n−3/2, L=n+3/2 and exact gap (2n−1)/(2n+3); the plateau end tends to one and the absolute gap tends to one. S0=2n+4 in that family, so the relative gap tends to zero; the frozen result asserts only an absolute gap. Yi–Wei's Assumption 1 and Theorem 2 also apply to these affine delays. The included rational script checks the announced examples and construction, not uniqueness; inspected, not executed.

## originality

PASS

Prior work establishes the simultaneous mixed-routing model, aggregate uniqueness, and qualitative and exact plateau phenomena in other networks/games. The checked distinct claim is the whole penetration-response law on the open positive-affine five-edge Braess regime together with its explicit near-full penetration, nonvanishing absolute-loss family.

## value

PASS

A cutoff for first meaningful adoption is a natural question on the canonical non-series-parallel network. Here strict affine inequalities give a robust plateau, exact subsequent gain and a quantified family where the plateau can cover almost all penetrations with an unchanged order-one absolute gap. This distinguishes a policy-relevant structural obstruction from a numerical threshold for one choice of costs. It does not show persistent proportional inefficiency or make a policy prediction for heterogeneous transportation networks.
