In Bayesian last-success stopping with a \(\operatorname{Beta}(A,B)\) posterior and \(k\) future trials, the optimal acceptance boundary is unique and is exactly \(A=(B+k-1)/k\) if and only if \(B\geq k(k-1)+1\) (or \(k=1\)); below this sharp failure-shape threshold, adaptive waiting moves the boundary strictly downward. The result recovers a rigorously optimal first-future-success policy in an explicit all-horizon region even though unrestricted Bayesian posterior paths need not satisfy classical monotone stopping assumptions.
