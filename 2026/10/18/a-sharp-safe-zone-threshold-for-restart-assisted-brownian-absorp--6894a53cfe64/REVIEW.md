# Scientific assessment: sharp safe-zone threshold for restart-assisted Brownian absorption

## Correctness — PASS
The claim has explicit quantifiers: all \(L>0\), all \(0\le a\le L\), and all \(r\ge0\). The killed Brownian resolvent has derivative jump \(-2\), which fixes the diffusion normalization. Safe resets have probability \(rS_r<1\), so the renewal equation proves the exact mean rather than relying on simulations. The global no-benefit branch follows from the all-coefficient hyperbolic inequality; its equality case is strict for every positive rate. The beneficial branch follows from the negative quadratic perturbation at zero rate; for interior cutoff parameters the mean diverges at high rate and therefore attains a finite positive minimum. The near-threshold unique global optimizer follows from strict critical non-improvement, uniform tail growth, and the analytic implicit-function theorem. Independent discrete backward-equation computations agree to finite-difference precision. No claim is made for modified stochastic dynamics.

## Originality — PASS (literature comparison, not priority certification)
The most direct source is Grange, arXiv:2111.15241, whose full available preprint was inspected at the model definition and renewal equations: its geometric shortest-return region is fixed at \(a=L/2\). It does not establish the variable-feedback safe-zone threshold \(1-1/\sqrt6\) or the near-critical optimal-rate and optimal-gain coefficients. Pal–Prasad, arXiv:1812.03009, was inspected at the backward equation and conclusions: its resetting events always restart at a chosen point and are not conditionally absorbing. Chen–Huang, arXiv:2109.11101, treats critical *starting positions* in usual bounded-domain resetting rather than the reset-triggered completion rule; only its abstract was accessible. Bressloff, arXiv:2205.13989, studies an occupation-time absorption state, not a spatially conditional alarm response. Semantic published-research searches and published prior-claim records yielded no implication-covering statement. The possibility of an implicit equivalent in broader resetting theory remains a priority risk; absence of search hits is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The feedback cutoff has a natural physical interpretation: an alarm initiates another search only while sufficiently close to the origin and otherwise completes the attempt. Its optimality is not settled by the existence of a renewal formula. The derived sharp global threshold distinguishes a region where no alarm rate can ever help from one where a positive rate does help, while the critical scalings quantify how rapidly the optimal policy and attainable advantage emerge. The standard shortest-route winding policy is included as a geometrically distinguished interior point. This is a complete phase boundary for a motivated one-parameter control family, not an arbitrarily chosen numerical instance.

## Closest literature and limitations
Grange (2021), Pal–Prasad (2018), Chen–Huang (2021), and Bressloff (2022) provide the relevant physical context. The new result is restricted to standard diffusion, symmetric interval geometry, Poisson alarms, instantaneous returns, and the stated conditional absorption rule; no global uniqueness theorem is asserted far from criticality. Full-text access for one neighboring bounded-domain source was unavailable. Mathematical priority and correctness have not been independently evaluated.
