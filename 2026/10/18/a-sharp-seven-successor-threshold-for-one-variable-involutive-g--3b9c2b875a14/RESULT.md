# A sharp seven-successor threshold for one-variable involutive Gödel modal logic

## Finding

Let \(S_m\) be the finite crisp rooted Kripke star consisting of a root \(r\) with exactly \(m\geq1\) distinct terminal successors and no other accessibility arrows. Work in the one-variable language of Gödel modal logic with involutive negation, with connectives \(\wedge,\to,\sim,\Box,\Diamond\). Propositional values lie in \([0,1]\), conjunction is minimum, \(\sim a=1-a\), and implication is \(a\to_G b=1\) if \(a\leq b\), and \(b\) otherwise. On this crisp frame, \(\Box\) and \(\Diamond\) are successorwise minimum and maximum; at a terminal world they have values \(1\) and \(0\).

**Uniform pruning theorem.** For every \(m\geq1\), every valuation of the sole variable \(p\) on \(S_m\), and every root value, there is a subset of at most seven leaves such that deleting all other leaves preserves the truth degree at \(r\) of **every** one-variable formula simultaneously (with unrestricted nesting and no formula-size bound). The number seven is optimal for simultaneous exact root-degree preservation.

**Sharp frame-validity hierarchy.** Let \(L_m\) be the collection of one-variable formulas that attain degree \(1\) at every world under every valuation on \(S_m\). Then
\[
L_1\supsetneq L_2\supsetneq L_3\supsetneq L_4\supsetneq L_5\supsetneq L_6\supsetneq L_7=L_8=L_9=\cdots.
\]
Explicit formulas witnessing all six strict inclusions are given below.

## Assumptions and scope

The accessibility relation is crisp and consists **only** of arrows from the root to the terminal leaves. Leaves are nonreflexive and have no successors; there are no loops at the root. There is exactly one propositional variable, but arbitrarily many occurrences, and the whole modal language, not just formulas of depth one, is covered. The theorem uses ordinary \([0,1]\)-valued (standard) semantics, not alternative rounded semantics. It does not claim a seven-leaf bound for arbitrary frames, fuzzy accessibility weights, multiple propositional variables, or infinite stars whose infima may not be attained.

Use the derived formulas \(\mathbf1=(p\to p)\), \(\mathbf0=\sim\mathbf1\), \(\neg\alpha=(\alpha\to\mathbf0)\), and \(\Delta\alpha=((\sim\alpha)\to\mathbf0)\). Thus \(\Delta\alpha\) has degree \(1\) exactly when \(\alpha\) has degree \(1\), otherwise \(0\).

## Proof

**1. Normal form at terminal worlds.** At every leaf, all modal subformulas are the constants \(\Box\psi=1\) and \(\Diamond\psi=0\). Consequently each complete formula gives, as a function of the leaf's atomic degree \(x\), a unary propositional term generated by \(0,1,x,1-x\) under minimum, Gödel implication, and involution.

Define the five strata
\[
\{0\},\quad (0,\tfrac12),\quad\{\tfrac12\},\quad(\tfrac12,1),\quad\{1\}.
\]
On either open interval the four quantities \(0,x,1-x,1\) have a fixed strict order (the order of the two nonconstant quantities reverses at one half). The three term operations only choose among existing quantities pointwise, and their choices depend on comparisons of their inputs. By structural induction, every unary leaf term, restricted to either open interval, agrees identically with one of \(0,x,1-x,1\). At the three singleton strata it has a single fixed value. This is a finite, syntactically uniform normal form, not a restriction on formula depth.

**2. Uniform seven-point extremum preservation.** From the finite nonempty multiset of leaf degrees, retain each of \(0,\tfrac12,1\) that is present, and in each of \((0,\tfrac12)\) and \((\tfrac12,1)\) retain its smallest and largest present degree, suppressing duplicates. At most \(3+2+2=7\) degrees remain. For **every** leaf term, the minimum and maximum over all original leaf degrees are attained among the retained degrees: on the open intervals the term is constant, increasing as \(x\), or decreasing as \(1-x\); singleton strata are preserved exactly. Hence, for every subformula \(\psi\), the values of \(\Box\psi\) and \(\Diamond\psi\) at the root are unchanged by deletion. Induction on the entire formula, keeping the root's atomic degree fixed, proves that every formula's root degree is unchanged. Modal nesting creates no exception because a leaf evaluates its own modal subformulas to constants.

**3. Seven is necessary.** Define the following unary crisp indicator formulas (each has value \(1\) exactly on the named stratum, otherwise \(0\)):
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_0&=\neg p, & e_{1/2}&=\Delta((p\to\sim p)\wedge(\sim p\to p)), & e_1&=\Delta p,\\
e_{<}&=\sim\Delta(\sim p\to p)\wedge\sim\neg p,
& e_{>}&=\sim\Delta(p\to\sim p)\wedge\sim\Delta p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The indicator \(e_<\) recognizes \((0,\tfrac12)\), while \(e_>\) recognizes \((\tfrac12,1)\). Consider seven leaves whose atomic degrees are \(0,\tfrac15,\tfrac25,\tfrac12,\tfrac35,\tfrac45,1\). Each of the seven leaves is a unique extremum of at least one of the following modal terms evaluated at the root:
\[
\Diamond e_0,\quad\Diamond e_{1/2},\quad\Diamond e_1,\quad
\Diamond(e_<\wedge\sim p),\quad\Diamond(e_<\wedge p),\quad
\Diamond(e_>\wedge\sim p),\quad\Diamond(e_>\wedge p).
\]
The first three detect the respective singleton degrees; the last four detect, respectively, the minimum and maximum within the two open intervals. Deleting any one of these seven leaves changes at least one displayed root degree. Thus no choice of six leaves always preserves all formula degrees simultaneously.

**4. Formulas separating every consecutive pair of frame logics.** For either open-interval indicator \(e\in\{e_<,e_>\}\), define the crisp root test
\[
\operatorname{Spread}(e)=
\sim\Delta\!\left(
\Diamond(e\wedge p)\to\sim\Diamond(e\wedge\sim p)
\right).
\]
Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the maximum and minimum atomic degrees, respectively, of leaves satisfying \(e\), when such leaves exist. Then \(\Diamond(e\wedge p)=A\) and \(\sim\Diamond(e\wedge\sim p)=B\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Spread}(e)=1\) precisely if there are at least two **distinct** degrees within that open interval. If the interval is empty the test is \(0\) by direct evaluation. These tests take only degrees \(0,1\).

Order seven crisp requirements as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_1&=\Diamond e_0,& T_2&=\Diamond e_{1/2},& T_3&=\Diamond e_1,\\
T_4&=\Diamond e_<,& T_5&=\operatorname{Spread}(e_<),&
T_6&=\Diamond e_>,& T_7&=\operatorname{Spread}(e_>).
\end{aligned}
\]
For each \(k=2,3,4,5,6,7\) set \(\Theta_k=\sim(T_1\wedge\cdots\wedge T_k)\). The first \(k\) requirements need at least \(k\) leaves, because the singleton strata and the two open strata are disjoint and each spread requirement needs a second, distinct degree in its stratum. Hence \(\Theta_k\) is degree \(1\) at the root of every \(S_{k-1}\) valuation. It is also degree \(1\) at every terminal world, since there \(T_1=0\). Thus \(\Theta_k\in L_{k-1}\).

In \(S_k\), assign the first \(k\) degrees from the ordered list \(0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac15,\tfrac25,\tfrac35,\tfrac45\) to the leaves. All \(T_1,\dots,T_k\) become \(1\) at the root, so \(\Theta_k=0\) there. Hence \(\Theta_k\notin L_k\), establishing all six strict inclusions.

Finally, \(L_{m+1}\subseteq L_m\) for all \(m\geq1\): a countervaluation on \(S_m\) extends to \(S_{m+1}\) by duplicating one leaf's atomic degree; all old formula values remain unchanged. For \(m\geq7\), a countervaluation at the root of \(S_m\) compresses to at most seven leaves by Step 2; duplicate retained degrees to make exactly seven leaves. A countervaluation at a terminal world can be realized at a terminal world of \(S_7\), as terminal evaluations depend only on that world's degree. Thus \(L_7\subseteq L_m\), proving stabilization.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library program `check.py` implements the primitive algebraic operations and evaluates formulas directly on rooted stars, independently of the mathematical proof. It tests the five indicator formulas on 101 evenly spaced degrees, the six sharp separating families on 1,715 small-star multisets, 22,500 random nested-formula root comparisons before and after seven-representative pruning, and the indispensability of each canonical seven-leaf witness. It prints `ALL TESTS PASSED` on success. Run with `python3 check.py` next to this document. Tests use exact integer degrees scaled by 100; they are finite corroboration, not an assertion that discretization proves the continuum theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Bílková, Ferguson and Kozhemiachenko (2025) introduce and analyze the standard involutive Gödel modal semantics with expressible halfway-point tests, finitary alternative models, and tableaux. The present result uses their propositional and modal clauses but gives a **formula-independent exact finite-star pruning bound** and a **sharp seven-stage one-variable frame-validity hierarchy**, neither part of the cited article's results. Their earlier 2024 paper also studies the same logical signature, so the language and semantics are not claimed as new.

Ferrari, Fiorentini and Rodriguez (2025) study witnessed **non-involutive** Gödel modal models. Without \(\sim\), a three-leaf simultaneous reduction was previously derived for the one-variable star family; it does not imply the seven-leaf bound in the stronger involutive language. In the present language \(\sim\) makes the midpoint \(\tfrac12\) and the two surrounding open intervals separately observable, and the lower-bound formulas genuinely require seven witnesses. Generic modal finite-model properties and formula-dependent tableaux bounds do not entail this exact, formula-independent result.

## Limitations

Only finite nonreflexive rooted stars, crisp accessibility, a single propositional variable and ordinary standard semantics are asserted. The root-degree pruning statement does not say that the pruned model preserves interpretations at removed worlds. The sharp hierarchy concerns **validity** of one-variable formulas on the designated star frames, not logical equivalence of arbitrary pointed models. The novelty search does not exclude unindexed equivalent work; the 2024 related paper was identifiable bibliographically but its full text could not be inspected in the present source-access session. No independent external audit is claimed in this document.
