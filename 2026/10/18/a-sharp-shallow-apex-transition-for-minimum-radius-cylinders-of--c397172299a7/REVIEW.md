# Review of the shallow-apex cylinder threshold

**Correctness.** PASS. Projecting any covering cylinder along its axis preserves one horizontal width of the base, giving the global lower bound. Equality in the disk-width inequality forces the projected endpoints of a longest side to coincide and hence forces a horizontal longest-side axis. The unique projected diameter disk then gives the exact barycentric height inequality. Continuity of minimum enclosing disks on the compact axis-direction space makes the inequality strictly sharp beyond the cutoff. The proof covers unequal sides, ties, arbitrary interior projections and the flat endpoint, without using numerical extrapolation. An exact rational-coordinate checker independently confirms the displayed sample thresholds.

**Originality.** PASS on the inspected evidence, with residual literature risk acknowledged. Sections 2–3 of Brandenberg–Theobald (arXiv:math/0211344) provide the general minimum-cylinder/outer-radius formulation and contact reductions, not the all-height plateau threshold for an arbitrary base triangle and interior apex projection. Petitjean (arXiv:1008.5259, Sections 3–6) treats the general algebraic problem, regular tetrahedra and a five-vertex symmetric trigonal bipyramid. Its all-five-contact cutoff at \(h=1/\sqrt2\) matches the regular-base one-apex endpoint numerically, but its object, direction of threshold and circumscription quantifiers differ; his general algorithm alone does not state or directly yield this exact parameter classification without new geometric analysis. Maehara (2010) treats congruent embeddings of the fixed regular tetrahedron, not variable-height pyramids. An independently indexed minimum-width result for pyramids concerns the distance between parallel planes, not the least circular-cylinder radius: for a shallow pyramid these two functionals exhibit different behavior. Exact-title, synonym, parameter and implication searches did not reveal a direct assertion of the new threshold. The symmetric-bipyramid boundary is a genuine nearby known phenomenon, but not the claimed arbitrary-triangle classification. A general algorithm or uninspected paper might still conceal the formula; no claim of exhaustive novelty is made.

**Value.** PASS. This is a complete, sharp structural phase transition for a natural geometric invariant, not a numerical example or fixed-height special case. The precise barycentric threshold answers how far an arbitrary triangle's apex may be lifted without changing its optimal enclosing cylinder, including a full rigidity classification of optimal axes and the possibility that different longest-edge axes cease being optimal at different heights. It extracts a simple interpretable invariant from a generally nontrivial three-dimensional cylinder minimization problem.

**Scientific limits and closest literature.** The post-transition radius is not determined. The work does not classify exterior-apex projections or finite cylinder problems. The closest directly inspected primary sources are Brandenberg–Theobald (2002), Petitjean (2010/2011 preprint), and Maehara (2010); the 2000 Algorithmica paper was checked at the abstract level only, leaving a limited residual coverage risk.
