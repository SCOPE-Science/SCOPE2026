# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The weighted Paley–Wiener isometry supplies an exact half-line weighted-shift model. Direct iteration yields the full power norm, with unique stationary point; expansion of that point proves the coefficient and relative error. Nonatomic near-maximizer subspaces prove every finite-rank approximation distance is maximal. Spectra follow separately from the spectral-radius formula, multiplication operator theory, and infinite-multiplicity adjoint eigenvectors on each boundary fiber. The packaged finite checker supplies consistency tests but is not used as an infinite-dimensional proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The nearest full primary source, Elliott–Wynn (2009, arXiv:0910.0408v1), treats unweighted half-plane composition operators and asserts equality of norm, spectral radius, and essential norm; it does not treat multiplication by \(e^{-bz}\), which is essential to the collapse. Petkova (arXiv:1106.4769v3) establishes weighted-shift spectral theory subject to its stated bounded positive shift-ratio hypotheses, which exclude the super-Gaussian weight representing this operator in the interior quadrant. The 2004 Čučković–Zhao paper develops general weighted-composition criteria on disk Bergman spaces, but its full text could not be inspected because access was unavailable. Petersen (1975) considers weighted irrational rotation, and later Zen-space work concerns unweighted composition. Searches under exact operator formulas, weighted-shift equivalences, and spectral descriptions did not disclose the joint two-parameter spectral and sharp-power profile. A further plausible source is Matache (2019) on weighted composition and spectra on half-plane Hardy space. Its accepted manuscript was listed in an institutional repository, but downloading the full text failed; the published abstract could not settle the exact comparison. Chalendar–Partington (arXiv:1910.01475) was inspected and has a primarily disk-based setting, with unweighted half-plane results in §4.2. These are residual historical priority risks; failure of a search is not a proof of uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** This computes a complete natural two-parameter spectral boundary rather than one isolated norm: introducing both a rightward composition shift and a decaying analytic multiplier changes the spectral radius discontinuously from one to zero while preserving noncompactness and maximal finite-rank approximation distance. The Gaussian power-decay exponent, exact prefactor and full essential spectra distinguish the result from generic qualitative quasinilpotent weighted-shift examples. It gives a concrete calibration of how essential norm and essential spectral radius can diverge in analytic-function operator theory.

**Scope and risks.** All parameters, normalizations, and boundary cases are specified in `RESULT.md`. The result does not classify general weights; historical equivalence in differently formulated weighted-composition literature remains possible, especially in Matache's half-plane Hardy treatment, whose full text was unavailable. No external independent audit was performed.
