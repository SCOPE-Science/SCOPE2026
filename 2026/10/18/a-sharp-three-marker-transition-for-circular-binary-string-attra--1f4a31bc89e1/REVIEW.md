# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof forces both sites of a potential two-point attractor using three uniquely occurring gap/one-run factors, proves opposite endpoint bounds with two additional factors, and then checks *every* potentially missed factor by the explicit unmarked-arc decomposition. A three-point witness covers the complementary range. The included independent exact factor-mask checker enumerated all 220 triples with \(1\le a<b<c\le12\) and a direct-occurrence checker independently tested all such triples with \(c\le7\). Computation is additional evidence, not the proof of the infinite claim.

**Originality — PASS with bounded residual risk.** The 2021 foundational circular-attractor paper characterizes *consecutive* minimum attractor sites on primitive Sturmian conjugacy classes, and the 2023 algorithmic paper gives a general verification procedure. The compared statements and hypotheses do not contain or entail the explicit distant-site classification for all distinct three-gap words without new case analysis. Relevant accessible published literature was compared. Unindexed equivalent literature remains possible; no claim of absolute uniqueness is made.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The first aperiodic asymmetric triple-marker family has a complete, sharp two-versus-three circular-attractor phase transition and an exact count of all two-site witnesses. The transition depends only on the shortest and longest gaps, not the middle one. This exhibits a structural boundary not visible from the trivial alphabet-size lower bound.

The classification intentionally excludes ties in the gap lengths. The results concern *circular* factors, not ordinary linear string attractors. There has been no independent verification.
