A three-coordinate Gaussian prediction problem has a sharp two-sensor adaptive advantage: observing one bilinear factor first and routing the second reading by its realized magnitude achieves up to \(1+\sqrt{2/\pi}e^{-1/2}=1.483941449\ldots\) times the best fixed-pair variance reduction. The constant is uniquely attained when the linear and bilinear goals have equal fixed-pair value, and the optimal first reading has zero immediate benefit.
