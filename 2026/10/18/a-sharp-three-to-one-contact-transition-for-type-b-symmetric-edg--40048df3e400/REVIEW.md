# Review of the geometric claim

## Correctness — PASS

The proof establishes a polar with coordinate and edge absolute-value inequalities. In the nonnegative orthant it divides according to whether the maximal clique coordinate is at most or at least one-half. Strictly convex quadratic envelopes imply that the largest squared polar norm is exactly \(\max\{q,(p+q)/4\}\) and identify all equality configurations. The complete facet contacts follow because those equality configurations are polar vertices. Symmetry converts centered optimality to unrestricted inball optimality, while the span of active signed normals and strict slack of the remaining finitely many polar vertices prove the exact affine dimension of all centers. The outer sphere follows from the norm of all signed edge-root vertices. Exact rational computations cover bounded independent examples; no extrapolation from them is used.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

Ohsugi and Tsuchiya's original paper supplies this graph-dependent type-B polytope but primarily studies reflexivity, triangulation, and Ehrhart polynomials [1]. Their follow-up studies matching-related enumerative invariants [2]. The published broader literature on unconditional polytopes gives different objects or general theory; type-A symmetric edge polytopes live in a sum-zero hyperplane and are not equivalent to signed type-B root bodies. The inspected material does not imply the exact three-to-one Euclidean contact transition or center-dimension collapse. Potential older equivalent graph fractional-packing optimization results remain a residual novelty risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp threshold simultaneously changes the inradius formula, increases the number of tangent facets from \(2^q\) to exponentially many more, and destroys a full \(p\)-dimensional family of optimal inball centers. This is an all-parameter structural classification for a standard family of root polytopes, not a one-off parameter substitution. The polytope's two combinatorial vertex classes interact geometrically in a manner invisible to its basic Ehrhart data.

## Limitations

Only \(p,q\ge2\) and the canonical coordinate metric are covered. In the nonunique regime the affine span of the complete incenter locus is determined, not its entire face structure. The reproducible finite enumeration does not establish the theorem alone. Complete bibliographic uniqueness is unproved.
