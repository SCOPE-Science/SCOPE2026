# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact signed second-boundary matrix is reconstructed from the canonical seven-vertex matching complex. An integral 28-term chain has boundary exactly three times the canonical four-cycle, and a rational dual cochain has coboundary infinity norm at most one and boundary pairing exactly 28; this proves the sharp lower bound over real chains, not only integral chains. Mod-three column reduction proves the cycle is not a boundary. Exhaustive combinatorics and an independent counting formula verify that 630 of 945 four-cycles are one relabelling orbit of shortest nontrivial torsion cycles and that 315 have chords. All required certificates are included and executable.

**Originality — PASS with a stated literature risk.** Shareshian–Wachs (arXiv:math/0409054, Fig. 1.1 and Theorem 1.3) established the order-three homology and exhibited a filling of three times a four-cycle. Jonsson (arXiv:1203.5641, §4.1) proved generator and exact-sequence statements. Neither examined passage certifies an optimal norm against *all* two-chains. A separate published classification of seven-vertex matching-complex torsion graphs determines graph occurrence rather than the sharp filling area. The rational-cochain lower bound is a new logically independent conclusion. The remaining risk is unnoticed specialized work on minimal torsion fillings or an unindexed combinatorial reformulation.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The seven-point matching complex is the first classical matching complex exhibiting integral three-torsion. The least simplicial area of a multiple of its shortest torsion generator is a natural isoperimetric invariant: it quantifies the obstruction rather than only detecting torsion. The matching primal/dual certificates settle this exact invariant sharply, while the full 945-cycle dichotomy makes the statement independent of an arbitrarily chosen labelling or representative. This does not claim a general asymptotic theorem or arbitrary filling norm.
