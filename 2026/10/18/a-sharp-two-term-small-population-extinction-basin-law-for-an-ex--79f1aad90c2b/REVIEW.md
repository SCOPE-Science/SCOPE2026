# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof derives the ratio blow-up directly, verifies that the lower positive Lambert root is a hyperbolic saddle and the upper root a sink, and derives the unique stable graph coefficient \(\kappa=z_-/(\lambda_u-\lambda_z)<0\) from the invariant graph equation. The key subtlety is uniformity over the full physical square: a logarithmic high-ratio descent estimate bounds prey uniformly, so all very large initial predator-to-prey ratios enter the extinction strip without leaving the small-density regime. The two possible intersections of the graph with the top of the square then give the stated area integrals. Computations agree with both analytic branches. The double-root and non-small-square regimes are excluded.

**Originality (PASS, self-review).** The 2022 Blyuss–Kyrychko–Blyuss full text already derives the saddle/sink extinction directions via Lambert functions and reports qualitative bistability, but provides neither the curved basin graph coefficient nor a two-term initial-square extinction-area law. Its 2021 precursor treats delays and stochastic effects rather than this finite-density deterministic basin fraction. Searches of related ecological recurrence, stable-separatrix, and extinction calculations did not identify an implication of this two-term law. The remaining possibility of an unindexed specialized computation is an explicit literature-search risk; it is not claimed that absence from a search proves uniqueness.

**Value (PASS).** When both species begin scarce, the quantitative question is what share of comparable small initial densities leads to irreversible prey collapse versus predator extinction and prey recovery. The sharp leading sector fraction and strictly positive correction measure this decision boundary and distinguish two geometries depending on whether the limiting separator intersects the square's top edge. The uniform-in-ratio argument is substantive: standard local expansions at the blown-up saddle alone do not control the physical corner with arbitrary large predator/prey ratio.

**Closest literature and restrictions.** The original 2022 qualitative bistability theorem supplies the model and Lambert roots; the result here is not a claim to rederive those earlier conclusions. The law is asymptotic for two positive deterministic densities under strict simple-root assumptions. Numerical tests supplement but do not establish the infinite-time theorem.
