# A single covariance reversal and sharp perfect-correlation rate for root-cluster mutation boundaries

## Finding

In a uniformly random recursive tree, deletion of independently selected edges divides the tree into connected components. The root cluster has a random size; its *first-generation daughter count* is the number of deleted edges leaving that cluster. The two statistics compete at early times, yet their fluctuations ultimately track one another. The following theorem quantifies both regimes.

**Theorem.** Fix \(0<p<1\). For each integer \(n\ge2\), form a uniformly random recursive tree on the ordered vertex set \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), root it at \(1\), and retain each edge independently with probability \(p\). Let \(C_n\) be the size of the root component and \(B_n\) the number of deleted edges joining that component to the rest of the original tree. Define
\[
 \mu_n=\frac{\Gamma(n+p)}{\Gamma(n)\Gamma(1+p)},\qquad
 Q_n=\frac{\Gamma(n+2p)}{\Gamma(n)\Gamma(1+2p)},\qquad
 H_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac1{j+p},
\]
\[
 v_n=2Q_n-\mu_n-\mu_n^2,\qquad
 V_p=\frac2{\Gamma(1+2p)}-\frac1{\Gamma(1+p)^2}>0.
\]
Then \(\mathbb E C_n=\mu_n\), \(\operatorname{Var}(C_n)=v_n\), and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,B_n)=\frac{1-p}{p}v_n-(1-p)\mu_nH_n.}
\]
There is an integer \(N(p)\ge3\) such that this covariance is strictly negative for \(2\le n<N(p)\), is nonnegative at \(n=N(p)\), and is strictly positive for \(n>N(p)\). It can vanish at most once. In addition,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,B_n)\sim\frac{1-p}{p}V_p n^{2p},\qquad
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}n^p\bigl(1-\operatorname{Corr}(C_n,B_n)\bigr)=\frac1{2(1-p)\Gamma(1+p)V_p}.}
\]
In particular, the correlation tends to \(1\), even though the covariance at \(n=2\) equals \(-p(1-p)\).

## Assumptions and scope

A recursive tree is generated by choosing independently the parent of vertex \(j\) uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\) for every \(j\ge2\). Independently, every edge is retained with probability \(p\). The root component consists precisely of vertices whose entire ancestor path has retained edges. Every deleted edge with a parent in the root component starts a distinct first-generation daughter component; \(B_n\) counts these daughter *components*, **not** all vertices belonging to their descendant families. The theorem applies to each fixed \(p\in(0,1)\); endpoint cases are excluded because one variance degenerates. Correlation is well-defined for \(n\ge2\), when both statistics are nonconstant.

## Proof

**1. Markov growth and the compensating martingale.** Condition on the tree and edge states through vertex \(m\). The next vertex chooses a parent in the root component with probability \(C_m/m\). Its increment \((\Delta C,\Delta B)\) is \((1,0)\) with conditional probability \(pC_m/m\), \((0,1)\) with conditional probability \((1-p)C_m/m\), and \((0,0)\) otherwise. Thus
\[
\mathbb E(C_{m+1}\mid\mathcal F_m)=(1+p/m)C_m,
\qquad \mathbb E(B_{m+1}-B_m\mid\mathcal F_m)=(1-p)C_m/m.
\]
Write \(\alpha=(1-p)/p\) and \(D_n=B_n-\alpha(C_n-1)\). The process \((D_n)_{n\ge1}\) is a square-integrable mean-zero martingale with \(D_1=0\). The two nonzero increments of \(D\) are \(-\alpha\) and \(1\), respectively, so
\[
\mathbb E((D_{m+1}-D_m)^2\mid\mathcal F_m)=\alpha C_m/m.
\]
Summing the martingale quadratic variations and using the first-moment recurrence gives
\[
\mathbb E B_n=\alpha(\mu_n-1),\quad
\operatorname{Var}(D_n)=\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{\mu_m}{m}
=\frac{1-p}{p^2}(\mu_n-1).
\]
Here the last equality follows by telescoping \(\mu_{m+1}-\mu_m=p\mu_m/m\).

**2. Exact cross moment and root variance.** Set \(h_n=\mathbb E(C_nD_n)\). Since a retained arrival both increments \(C\) and decrements \(D\), while a deleted arrival increments only \(D\), multiplication of the increment rules gives
\[
h_{m+1}=(1+p/m)h_m-(1-p)\mu_m/m,\qquad h_1=0.
\]
The recurrence \(\mu_{m+1}=(1+p/m)\mu_m\) implies
\[
\frac{h_{m+1}}{\mu_{m+1}}=\frac{h_m}{\mu_m}-\frac{1-p}{m+p},
\quad\text{hence}\quad h_n=-(1-p)\mu_nH_n.
\]
The conditional increment of \(C^2\) yields
\[
\mathbb E C_{m+1}^{2}=(1+2p/m)\mathbb E C_m^{2}+p\mu_m/m.
\]
The solution with \(C_1=1\) is \(\mathbb E C_n^2=2Q_n-\mu_n\), checked directly by substitution. Therefore \(v_n=2Q_n-\mu_n-\mu_n^2\). Substituting \(B_n=\alpha(C_n-1)+D_n\) proves the boxed covariance identity.

**3. The unique reversal.** Define \(q_n=v_n/\mu_n^2=\operatorname{Var}(C_n/\mu_n)\) and \(b_n=\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,B_n)/\mu_n\). Because \(C_n/\mu_n\) is a martingale whose next-step conditional variance is positive with positive probability for every \(0<p<1\), the sequence \(q_n\) is strictly increasing, starting from \(q_1=0\). Elementary substitution in the moment recurrences yields the exact difference identity
\[
\boxed{b_{m+1}-b_m=\frac{(1-p)\mu_m}{m+p}\left(q_m-\frac{p}{m}\right).}
\]
The sequence \(p/m\) strictly decreases, so these increments start negative and can switch sign at most once, thereafter staying strictly positive. The initial covariance is \(-p(1-p)\) at \(n=2\). As established next, \(b_n\) diverges to \(+\infty\); consequently \(b_n\) itself crosses zero exactly once (possibly equaling zero at the crossing). Multiplication by \(\mu_n>0\) gives the asserted sign classification.

**4. Asymptotics and the correlation deficit.** Standard fixed-parameter gamma ratios imply \(\mu_n\sim n^p/\Gamma(1+p)\), \(Q_n\sim n^{2p}/\Gamma(1+2p)\), and \(H_n=\log n+O_p(1)\). Thus \(v_n\sim V_p n^{2p}\). Moreover \(V_p>0\): the nonconstant, nonnegative martingale \(C_n/\mu_n\) has a strictly positive variance already at \(n=2\), and its variance increases with \(n\) to \(\Gamma(1+p)^2V_p\) by the exact moment formula. Therefore the covariance has leading term \(\alpha V_pn^{2p}>0\), and \(b_n\sim\alpha\Gamma(1+p)V_p n^p\).

Finally, \(\operatorname{Var}(D_n)\sim(1-p)n^p/(p^2\Gamma(1+p))\), while \(\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,D_n)=-(1-p)\mu_nH_n=O_p(n^p\log n)\). Let
\[
u_n=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(C_n,D_n)}{\alpha v_n}=O_p(n^{-p}\log n),\quad
w_n=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(D_n)}{\alpha^2v_n}
\sim\frac{n^{-p}}{(1-p)\Gamma(1+p)V_p}.
\]
The exact correlation is \((1+u_n)/\sqrt{1+2u_n+w_n}\). As \(u_n^2=o(n^{-p})\) for fixed \(p>0\), Taylor expansion gives \(1-\operatorname{Corr}(C_n,B_n)=w_n/2+o(n^{-p})\), proving the sharp limit.

## Relationship to prior work

Baur and Bertoin (arXiv:1406.2238v1) describe the recursive-tree/Yule coupling, neutral mutations, and the fixed-parameter root-cluster scaling law. Baur (arXiv:1407.2508v1) studies the *sizes* of genealogically indexed percolation clusters in regimes where retention tends to one. Kürsten (arXiv:1512.05275v1) supplies independent formulas for the first two moments of the root-cluster size and for counts of **vertices** in first-generation daughter clusters. The statistic here instead counts daughter **components** (deleted boundary edges) and assesses their **finite-size cross covariance**, exact sign reversal, and sharp correlation-deficit constant. None of these three inspected texts explicitly states or immediately gives all those conclusions. A more general earlier multitype branching or urn theorem could nevertheless imply some portion of the asymptotics.

## Limitations

No uniformity in the retention parameter near \(0\) or \(1\) is established. The theorem concerns uniformly random recursive trees and independent bond retention, not arbitrary preferential-attachment models. The complete historical priority of the exact sign-reversal statement has not been established; independent scientific audit has not occurred.
