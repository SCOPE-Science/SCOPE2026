# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS
For every \(n\ge5\), each projective toric chart is reconstructed from the saturated lattice of basis differences. All basis ratios factor into allowed single swaps. Their bipartite-flow relations give rank-one determinantal rings, with one free factor precisely for bases containing one of the two parallel elements. Standard determinantal Jacobian and Segre-degree computations then prove the exact singular components, multiplicities, and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial support. The standalone test independently checks all exchange decompositions and four-cycle identities for \(4\le n\le9\), but is not used to infer the infinite theorem.

## Originality: PASS (with residual risk)
The closest primary sources are Feichtner–Sturmfels's matroid polytope and Bergman fan study and Lam–Postnikov's theory of alcoved matroid polytopes. Their relevant sections address polytope normal fans, bases, and triangulations rather than the full special-chart and singular-locus decomposition. Existing general determinantal-cone facts are used as tools, not claimed as novel. The fully uniform hypersimplex case has previously been described, but the mixed-dimensional singular locus after deleting a single parallel-pair basis is not implied by the hypersimplex's local models, which cannot determine new edges and their singular strata. Searches of the accessible mathematical index did not identify an equivalent family classification. Differently formulated or unindexed literature remains a risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The example is the minimal nonuniform rank-two matroid, obtained by making one pair parallel. It changes the singularity geometry from isolated points to a mixed-dimensional configuration with an exact count, complete local models, disjoint component geometry, and two different multiplicities. The statement is a natural structural classification of an infinite family rather than a numerically selected special case.

## Limits
The claim is about reduced support. No full singular subscheme or resolution is asserted. Literature search does not constitute proof of uniqueness, and the finite tests are checks, not the proof.
