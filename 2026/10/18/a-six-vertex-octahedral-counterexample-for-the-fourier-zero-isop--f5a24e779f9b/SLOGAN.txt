An elongated three-dimensional octahedron with only six vertices beats its equal-volume Euclidean ball in the distance to the first real Fourier zero. The integral-coordinate example \(\operatorname{conv}\{(\pm101,0,0),(0,\pm1,0),(0,0,\pm1)\}\) has a Fourier transform strictly positive for all axial frequencies when the transverse-frequency norm is at most \(\sqrt2\), giving a certified minimum-vertex polyhedral counterexample.
