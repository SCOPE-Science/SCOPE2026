# A sphere after two squares: strong discrete-Morse complexes of four-cycle bouquets

## Finding

For an integer \(p\geq 1\), let \(B_p\) be the one-dimensional simplicial complex obtained by identifying one vertex \(o\) in \(p\) distinct copies of the four-cycle \(C_4\), with no other identifications. Let \(\mathcal{SM}(B_p)\) denote the **full** strong discrete-Morse matching complex generated by the strong matchings induced by vertex filtrations, as defined by Knudson and Owens-White (2026). The distinction from the pure strong complex and the ordinary Morse matching complex is essential.

**Theorem.** For every integer \(p\geq2\) there is a simplicial collapse
\[
\mathcal{SM}(B_p)\searrow (C_6)^{*p},
\]
where each \(C_6\) on the right is a six-vertex simplicial cycle and \(*\) denotes simplicial join. Consequently
\[
\mathcal{SM}(B_p)\simeq S^{2p-1},\qquad
\widetilde H_k(\mathcal{SM}(B_p);\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&k=2p-1,\\0,&k\ne2p-1.\end{cases}
\]
For comparison, the one-petal boundary case satisfies \(\mathcal{SM}(B_1)\simeq\bigvee^5 S^1\). In particular, the two-petal complex is a three-sphere rather than a join of two copies of the one-petal strong-Morse complex.

## Assumptions and scope

The simplicial complex \(B_p\) has vertices \(o,a_i,b_i,c_i\), for \(1\leq i\leq p\), and its only one-dimensional facets are the four edges
\(\{o,a_i\},\{a_i,b_i\},\{b_i,c_i\},\{c_i,o\}\) for each \(i\). Its cycles are not filled by two-simplices. All filtrations used to generate the full strong matching complex arise from total orders on these \(3p+1\) vertices. The complex includes every submatching of every resulting strong matching; it is not restricted to maximal-dimensional facets.

An incidence atom \((v,vu)\) means the discrete-Morse pair of a vertex \(v\) with its incident edge \(\{v,u\}\). For a total ordering, this atom occurs exactly when \(u\) is the **unique neighbor of \(v\) preceding \(v\)**. Indeed, the descending link at a vertex of a one-dimensional complex is a discrete set of preceding neighbors. Its being a nonempty cone is equivalent to that set having exactly one member. This is the one-dimensional strong matching criterion from the descending-link construction; neither a generic acyclic matching nor an arbitrary incidence matching automatically satisfies it.

## Proof

Fix a petal \(o-a-b-c-o\). Excluding atoms rooted at \(o\), label its six possible incidence atoms
\[
A=(a,oa),\quad B=(a,ab),\quad C=(b,ab),\quad
D=(b,bc),\quad E=(c,oc),\quad F=(c,bc).
\]
Applying the unique-earlier-neighbor criterion to the twenty-four orders of \(o,a,b,c\), and taking downward closure, gives a one-dimensional simplicial complex \(L\). Its complete edge list is
\[
AC,\ CF,\ FB,\ BD,\ DE,\ EA.
\]
Thus \(L\cong C_6\), with no simplex of dimension greater than one. The list follows also by elementary order comparisons: the two alternative atoms rooted at the same vertex cannot coexist, while any listed edge is realized by an appropriate total order, and each unlisted pair is prohibited by the unique-earlier-neighbor condition.

Three subsets of local orders matter. If \(o\) precedes both \(a\) and \(c\), the induced local down-closure \(U\) has vertices \(A,C,D,E\) and edges \(AC,AE,DE\); hence \(U\) is the path \(C-A-E-D\), in particular **simplicially collapsible**. If \(a<o<c\), the local down-closure \(W_a\) consists of the three isolated vertices \(B,C,E\); if \(c<o<a\), the corresponding \(W_c\) has the three isolated vertices \(A,D,F\). These lists are exhaustive because they directly distinguish the position of \(o\) relative to its two local neighbors and the two possible positions of \(b\).

Write \(L_i,U_i,W_{i,a},W_{i,c}\) for these complexes on the atoms of petal \(i\), and put
\[
Z_p=L_1*\cdots*L_p\cong(C_6)^{*p}.
\]
Every face containing no atom rooted at \(o\) lies in \(Z_p\) by restricting the generating global order to each petal. Conversely, select independently any local orders realizing arbitrary faces in \(L_1,\ldots,L_p\). They share only the common vertex \(o\); all vertices locally before \(o\) can be placed before it globally, and all those locally after it can be placed afterward. Their local orderings therefore extend simultaneously to a global ordering, so every face of \(Z_p\) occurs in the full complex.

A global strong matching has at most one atom rooted at \(o\). Such an atom can only be \(h_{i,a}=(o,oa_i)\) or \(h_{i,c}=(o,oc_i)\). In the former case, precisely \(a_i\) precedes \(o\) among all \(2p\) neighbors of \(o\). Therefore its remaining atoms are exactly faces of
\[
T_{i,a}=W_{i,a}*\mathop{\Large *}_{j\ne i}U_j.
\]
Likewise \(T_{i,c}=W_{i,c}*\mathop{\Large *}_{j\ne i}U_j\). The converse is realized by interleaving local orders just as above, with all other petal endpoints after \(o\). Consequently there is an exact simplicial decomposition
\[
\mathcal{SM}(B_p)=Z_p\ \cup\
\bigcup_{i=1}^p\left((h_{i,a}*T_{i,a})\cup(h_{i,c}*T_{i,c})\right),
\]
where the base \(T_{i,u}\) of each cone lies in \(Z_p\) and the cones have pairwise distinct apex vertices.

When \(p\ge2\), every \(T_{i,u}\) contains at least one join factor \(U_j\), which is a four-vertex path. A join with a nonempty collapsible complex is collapsible: lift each elementary collapse in the collapsible factor across simplices of the other factor, then collapse the resulting cone. Thus every \(T_{i,u}\) is collapsible. More generally, if \(T\) simplicially collapses to a vertex, then its cone \(h*T\) simplicially collapses **relative to its base** \(T\): lift the free-face pairs in the collapse of \(T\) to faces containing \(h\), and finally remove the edge from \(h\) to the remaining vertex together with \(h\). In the displayed union, all faces outside \(Z_p\) have one uniquely specified apex, so these relative collapses cannot interfere. Collapsing every added cone yields
\(\mathcal{SM}(B_p)\searrow Z_p\).

Finally, \(L_i\) is a simplicial circle, and a join of \(p\) circles is a PL sphere of dimension \(2p-1\). This proves the theorem, including the asserted integral homology and the stronger simple-homotopy statement.

For \(p=1\), the decomposition has base \(L=C_6\) and two cone apices, each joined to three isolated base vertices. It is a connected graph with eight vertices and twelve edges, so its homotopy type is a wedge of \(12-8+1=5\) circles.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library program `verify_square_bouquet.py` separately enumerates filtration-induced strong matchings by dynamic programming over vertex orders, takes their exact downward closure, and compares it face by face with the displayed join-and-cone formula. For \(p=1,2,3\), it finds respectively 20, 296, and 3,732 nonempty simplices, with reduced homology predictions agreeing with exact mod-two boundary-rank computations. The local pieces \(L,U,W_a,W_c\) are likewise reconstructed by enumerating all 24 local orders.

The separate program `check_collapse.py` starts from the complete generated face sets, verifies each elementary free-face pair, and reduces the entire complexes for \(p=2\) and \(p=3\) to the precise target subcomplexes \((C_6)^{*2}\) and \((C_6)^{*3}\). It verifies 64 and 768 elementary collapses respectively. Both scripts and exact recorded outputs accompany this result. These finite tests are cross-checks, not substitutes for the all-parameter argument above.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens-White's paper *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (first public 2026-09-06, arXiv:2609.06844) defines the full complex and computes path, star, simplex, and simplex-boundary cases and related cycle invariants. In particular, their disjoint-union join property does **not** directly apply to petals that share one vertex. The present theorem gives a connected one-point-union formula, including an explicit relative simplicial collapse.

Donovan and Scoville, *Star clusters in the matching, Morse, and generalized complex of discrete Morse functions* (2023), classify the **ordinary edge matching complex** of Dutch windmill graphs, including four-cycle bouquets. Their matching complex is a different simplicial object and its homotopy result does not imply the strong-filtration result here. Earlier computations of ordinary or generalized discrete-Morse complexes similarly do not supply this strong version. The local one-dimensional descending-link criterion is grounded in Fernández's strong discrete Morse theory.

## Limitations

This statement concerns unfilled four-cycle bouquets with a single common vertex, the **full** strong complex, and integer \(p\ge2\). It is not a classification of arbitrary cactus graphs, arbitrary cycle lengths, the ordinary discrete-Morse complex, or the pure strong complex. The proof is complete under the stated definition, but originality is assessed against accessible sources and database comparisons; not every unindexed or unpublished work can be excluded. Independent scientific audit has not yet been performed.
