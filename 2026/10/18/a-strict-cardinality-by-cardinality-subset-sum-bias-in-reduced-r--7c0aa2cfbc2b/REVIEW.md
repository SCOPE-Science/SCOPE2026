# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof first shows that the unit group reduces with equal fibers to each nonzero residue class modulo any prime divisor. The resulting finite-root product has signed coefficient \((-1)^r B_{p,m}(r)\); Fourier inversion gives the stated exact counts. The coefficient is strictly positive for all allowable subset sizes, including both endpoints and the prime two. Uniformity modulo the original modulus would imply uniformity modulo the prime, so the nonzero difference excludes it. The standalone dynamic-programming checker independently verifies the finite range \(2\le N\le96\) but does not certify the infinite argument.

**Originality — PASS, with uncertainty.** Wagon and Wilf's 1994 main theorem counts fixed-cardinality subsets of an entire initial interval; its prime-modulus instance implies the new nonuniformity corollary for prime moduli, but its written scope does not include the reduced-residue system at composite moduli or the signed bias formula. Gaitanas's 2023 Theorem 1.1 concerns *all nonempty subset sizes combined*; the new statement separates every cardinality and gives exact prime-projected multiplicities. Primary text and targeted literature comparisons did not uncover stronger coverage. It remains possible that general elementary-symmetric Fourier literature implies the formula implicitly, and independent priority review is required.

**Value — PASS.** Exact failure of equidistribution for every subset size is a natural completion of the fixed-cardinality subset-sum question for the canonical unit set. The uniform positive bias coefficient gives more than a yes/no answer and explains alternation across cardinalities. In particular, at \(N=21,r=6\) the total \(924\) is divisible by \(21\), yet uniformity fails because the counts modulo \(3\) are \(402,261,261\). The all-size pooled theorem of Gaitanas shows why the distinction matters.

**Closest literature and limits.** S. Wagon and H. Wilf, *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* 1 (1994), #R3, DOI 10.37236/1183, original fixed-size interval setting; K. Gaitanas, arXiv:2304.14141 (2023), pooled nonempty unit subsets; Y. O. Hamidoune, *Bulletin of the LMS* 26 (1994), 557–563, DOI 10.1112/blms/26.6.557 (abstract-only comparison). The result determines prime-projected distributions, not the complete residue distribution modulo composite \(N\). Full-text coverage of every possible general character formula is not claimed.
