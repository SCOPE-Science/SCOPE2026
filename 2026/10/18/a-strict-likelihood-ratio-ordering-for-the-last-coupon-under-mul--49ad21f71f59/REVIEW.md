# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim follows from the exact multinomial coefficient identity, the two-by-two total positivity of Toeplitz kernels generated by log-concave sequences, and a strict minor at the minimal supported coefficient. The proof treats all positive weights, every integer quota and the boundary with zero null-coupon weight. Exact rational checks compare independent full-sequence enumeration against the generating-function law. Infinite quantifiers are established analytically rather than inferred from finite checks.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
The inspected full texts are Myers and Wilf (2003), especially Section 3 on uniform multiple-quota completion distributions, and Anceaume, Busnel and Sericola (2014), especially Section 2 on arbitrary-weight unlabelled completion distributions with null coupons. Neither inspected work identifies a strict conditional likelihood-ratio ordering for the last coupon label. The existing unlabelled completion laws and standard log-concavity theory are acknowledged; they do not by themselves supply the claimed strict conditional ordering. Searches under terms involving last-type identity, reverse-hazard order, discrete completion times, and quotas found no decisive stronger published coverage. Residual risk: an equivalent reliability or total-positivity reformulation may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete finite-sample pairwise likelihood-ratio order is a structural law for terminal-type inference. It yields strict stochastic ordering of completion times conditioned on the identity of the last quota to finish, an exact no-information boundary at the shortest possible completion, and exponential strengthening of rarity evidence at long completion times. It applies to the entire finite-weight family, all fixed common quotas, and optional null draws. This is more informative than a moment, a one-parameter specialization, or a recomputation of an unlabeled completion distribution.

## Limits
No independent validation; no claim of an extension to unequal quotas, adaptive draws, or a growing-number-of-types asymptotic. Full equivalence searches cannot establish absolute novelty.
