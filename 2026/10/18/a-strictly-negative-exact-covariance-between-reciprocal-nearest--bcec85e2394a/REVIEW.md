# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

For each configuration, reciprocal 1-nearest-neighbor pairs correspond to cyclic gap minima; isolated geometric-graph vertices correspond to two successive gaps greater than the radius. Conditional on the number of large gaps, uniform gap ranks are independent of the magnitudes. The two-adjacent-high conditioning calculation gives the exact rational conditional covariance. The Dirichlet-simplex survival formula and inclusion-exclusion give the stated closed form. The strict sign follows from positive simplex volume, and the rare-isolate constant from three explicit asymptotic terms. The attached standalone verification code checked 46,200 cyclic permutations and 45,131 assertions; the proof, not the finite experiment, establishes universal quantifiers.

## Originality: PASS, with acknowledged residual risk

Full-text inspection of Decreusefond–Ferraz arXiv:1008.5140v1 found Poisson random geometric graph components, not the reciprocal-nearest-neighbor versus radius-isolate mixed moment. Full-text inspection of Ma–Chow arXiv:1203.6264v1 found cyclic permutation-cycle peaks and valleys, not the joint threshold-marked circular-gap statistic. Pickard (1982) treats mutual nearest-neighbor phenomena, but only bibliographic and abstract material could be obtained; its full-text implication remains unassessed. No identified prior statement yields the finite covariance polynomial, the universal conditional identity, or the critical nonzero mixed-moment limit. Absence of an identified source is not a proof of historical priority.

## Value: PASS

The two statistics quantify a natural tension between fine-scale reciprocal attachments and hard-radius disconnection in a one-dimensional spatial network. The covariance is uniformly negative for all nontrivial radii and its negative magnitude remains order one when isolated vertices become rare. This is not read off from the separate reciprocal-pair and isolated-vertex means; the exact conditional law reveals the source of the dependence. Its scope is a motivated complete finite-size dependence classification, not an arbitrary numerical case.

## Limitations and historical comparisons

The result applies only to iid uniform circle samples, fixed n>=5, and hard connection thresholds. General theorems on marked local minima or adjacent exceedances could conceivably entail the displayed identity even if titles differ. The Pickard 1982 full text was unavailable; interpretation of its abstract is necessarily limited. No independent validation is claimed.
