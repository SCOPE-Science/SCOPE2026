# Scientific review: three-season orientation law

## Correctness — PASS

For strictly positive phase parameters, each product of three stage matrices has a positive Perron root and a common negative determinant. Direct multiplication yields the exact trace difference \(b\Delta\), where \(\Delta\) is the oriented area determinant of the three survival-rate pairs. Since the positive eigenvalue is strictly increasing in trace for fixed negative determinant, this proves the stated necessary-and-sufficient ranking and equality criteria. For each cyclic order, the unit-eigenvalue test is the strictly decreasing cubic \(1-a_1a_2a_3-bC-b^3s_1s_2s_3\). It has exactly one positive root. Its two critical roots differ exactly when \(\Delta\ne0\), yielding the claimed nonempty interval in which the order reverses the growth verdict. The two-phase impossibility follows from invariance of the spectrum of a two-factor product under reversal. All rational witness signs were verified independently using exact matrix arithmetic. The symbolic and fraction checkers were run from the enclosed files.

## Originality — PASS with stated residual risk

Leslie's foundational work allows time-varying population matrices, but the inspected historical pages do not supply the three-phase geometric criterion. Cushing and Ackleh provide general reproduction-number theory for periodic matrix models, explicitly computing period-two Leslie cases; accessible indexed excerpts of their full text do not disclose an equivalent period-three survival-orientation result. Caswell and Shyu (2012), represented by Caswell's open 2019 chapter adapting that research, provide a general ordered-product sensitivity theory, not the exact two-class determinant criterion or the iff existence of an open fecundity interval with opposite invasion verdicts. Searches for matrix-triple trace identities and periodic vital-rate scheduling were also performed. An earlier exact result expressed as a commutator, polygonal orientation, or special stage-model identity remains possible; the full publisher copy of the Cushing–Ackleh study could not be independently inspected here. The conclusion is therefore a reasoned best-knowledge novelty judgment, not a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives a natural and complete structural answer to whether only the *ordering* of a fixed collection of stage-specific environments can determine population growth versus decline. The oriented-area law distinguishes all possible three-phase schedules, gives the sharp minimal number of phases, and proves a nonempty reversal interval for **every** noncollinear demographic triple rather than supplying a single selected numerical example. This matters for periodic life-history and intervention scheduling when the fertility level is a controlled common parameter. It is a deterministic linear low-density statement, not a guaranteed real-world extinction or persistence result.
