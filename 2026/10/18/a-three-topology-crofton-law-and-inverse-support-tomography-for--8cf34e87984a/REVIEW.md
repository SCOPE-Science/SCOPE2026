# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The meridian-fiber projection reduces plane intersection to one strictly monotone cosine interval because the revolution radius exceeds the radial semiextent. Its circle preimage has three possible generic topologies. Haar offset integration gives all three exact probabilities. For smooth strictly positively curved meridians the derivative of the annulus integral determines the monotone threshold angle and hence every meridian support value. The included checker corroborates the proof with 797 topology comparisons, 450,000 random planes, and 15 derivative-reconstruction tests. Its finite computations do not establish the theorem independently.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
The accessible full Sym (2009) and Moroni (2017) accounts explain algebraic planar sections of circular torus surfaces; they do not establish a Crofton three-type probability law for arbitrary convex meridians, or meridian reconstruction from probabilities as the revolution radius varies. Published published-finding corpus checks for equivalent terminology, stronger coverage, and parameter variants did not reveal a dominating result. An earlier convex-cylinder cap-contact width-distribution theorem does not imply recovery of a toroidal meridian support function. The Kim and Kim (2000) manuscript could be reviewed only in partial landing-page material; other historical stereology could contain a differently worded precedent. These are residual priority risks, not claimed settled.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant-plane law classifies a natural random topological experiment on nonconvex solids and unifies smooth and nonsmooth coordinate-symmetric convex meridians. The all-radii reconstruction shows that coarse topological frequencies nevertheless identify a smooth positively curved cross-sectional shape, a meaningful inverse-geometric result beyond circular-torus examples. A single-radius sample does not identify the meridian.
