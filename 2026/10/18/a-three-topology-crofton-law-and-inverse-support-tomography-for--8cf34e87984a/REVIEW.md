# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

At each azimuth the meridian intersection is convex; boundary support levels f_plus/minus(v)=R sin(theta)v plus/minus h_L(sin(theta)v,cos(theta)) increase strictly because R>a. The feasible cosine interval lifts to a full circle, one arc, or two arcs; these correspond to the three section topologies, except measure-zero tangencies. Haar normal density sin(theta) and offset length 2(R sin(theta)+H) give the stated A,C,Z integrals. For smooth positive-curvature L, H/sin(theta) is strictly decreasing from infinity to a and positive-part differentiation recovers theta_R and the support function. Independent scipy integration of circular meridian r=1,R=2 gives A=0.04338852250948179 and probabilities (0.0168774640204873,0.7442141312557108,0.2389084047238019), matching elementary closed form. Numerical checks support, but do not establish general convex-geometry proof. Non-smooth inverse reconstruction not claimed.

## originality

PASS

Primary geometric literature on toric section curves, including an inspected full Moroni exposition, concerns algebraic sections and special spiric/Villarceau curves; it does not give the Haar-weighted three-topology section distribution for arbitrary convex meridians or its support-function inverse. Resultary semantic query returned unrelated convex-support and torus results, not this law. Novelty is best of knowledge, not universal priority proof; older stereological Crofton formulae may encode an equivalent result.
