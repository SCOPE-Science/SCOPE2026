# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
For a uniformly random perfect matching of a complete graph, the inclusion probability of any three prescribed disjoint edges is exactly the reciprocal of three successive odd factors. The general proof enumerates disjoint cross-color edge pairs and triples using regularity; the only non-degree contribution at third order is the number of rainbow triangles. The ensuing mixed-cumulant formula, its universal covariance reduction and affine sign witness were reconstructed algebraically. The standalone exact enumerator checks six regular-color examples, including every matching of \(K_{12}\) in the circular-distance coloring and every matching of the affine \(K_8\) factorization. No asymptotic extrapolation is used.

## Originality — PASS
The inspected full text of Woolbright and Fu (1998) concerns existence of rainbow matchings and edge-switching, not moments of uniform matchings. Acan (2015) proves fixed-small-length chord-count Poisson limits; the general finite third mixed cumulant in arbitrary regular colorings is not implied. The closest separately indexed prescribed-difference matching census works in a different finite group and asks for matching realization multiplicities. A generic multivariate weighted-matching moment expansion could nevertheless encode this formula implicitly, so historical priority is not certified. The independent literature review remains unperformed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
There is a natural distinction between moments that see only degree regularity and the first moments that see the arrangement of edge colors. The exact triangle fingerprint draws that boundary at order three; the affine witness demonstrates an actual sign reversal inside one model without changing the corresponding lower moments. It is an exact, general finite-sample dependence classification useful when color profiles and rainbow structures are compared.

## Scope and outstanding risks
The claim requires a complete host graph and spanning regular color classes, and treats cumulants of three distinct colors only. Exhaustive finite checks do not establish the all-size identity, which instead follows from edge counting. No independent audit, external mathematical certification, or exhaustive historical-priority search is claimed.
