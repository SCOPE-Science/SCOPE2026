# A triangular Hilbert series for characteristic-two banana graph Jacobian schemes

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), let \(n\geq4\), and set \(R_n=k[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]\) with its standard grading. Write
\[
F_n=e_{n-1}(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j\ne i}x_j,
\qquad J_n=(F_n,\partial_{x_0}F_n,\ldots,\partial_{x_{n-1}}F_n).
\]
The **complete graded Hilbert series of the homogeneous singular scheme** is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_n/J_n}(t)=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n-3}\binom{r+2}{2}
\bigl(t^r+t^{2n-5-r}\bigr)}{(1-t)^{n-3}}.}
\]
In particular, its projective dimension is \(n-4\), its scheme-theoretic degree is \(2\binom n3\), and its reduced support is the union of the \(\binom n3\) coordinate projective \((n-4)\)-spaces obtained by setting three coordinates to zero. Each has generic scheme multiplicity two. For \(n=5\), the Hilbert series is
\[
\frac{1+3t+6t^2+6t^3+3t^4+t^5}{(1-t)^2},
\]
with eventual Hilbert polynomial \(20d-30\). The series and its lower-degree coefficients are stronger data than the known generic multiplicity alone.

## Assumptions and scope
The polynomial is the first Kirchhoff polynomial of the \(n\)-edge banana (two-vertex parallel-edge) graph. The singular *scheme* uses the ideal \(J_n\) containing both the equation and all partial derivatives. In characteristic two, the hypersurface equation must not be silently omitted when \(n\) is odd, because the Euler identity then gives no such implication. Only characteristic two and \(n\geq4\) are claimed. Degrees are for the associated projective homogeneous schemes.

## Proof
Fix the graded reverse lexicographic term order with \(x_0>\cdots>x_{n-1}\). For \(2\leq s\leq n\) and \(0\leq i\leq s-2\), put
\[
P_s=\prod_{j=s}^{n-1}x_j^2,\qquad
H_{s,i}=e_{s-2}(\{x_0,\ldots,x_{s-1}\}\setminus\{x_i\}),
\qquad G_{s,i}=P_sH_{s,i}.
\]
An elementary universal Gröbner-basis lemma states that the \(\binom n2\) polynomials \(G_{s,i}\) form a Gröbner basis of \(J_n\). Here is a proof with the Buchberger reductions made explicit.

First, the derivatives are \(H_{n,i}\); their sum is zero in characteristic two, so the derivatives with \(i\leq n-2\) generate all derivatives. The identity \(F_n=x_iH_{n,i}+\prod_{j\ne i}x_j\) shows that every squarefree product of all but one variable lies in \(J_n\). Splitting \(H_{s,i}\) according to whether it uses \(x_{s-1}\) gives
\[
H_{s,i}=\prod_{\substack{j<s-1\\j\ne i}}x_j+x_{s-1}H_{s-1,i}.
\]
After multiplication by \(x_{s-1}P_s\), its first term is a multiple of one of the preceding squarefree products. Descending induction therefore gives \(G_{s,i}\in J_n\). Conversely, \(F_n=(x_i+x_{n-1})G_{n,i}+G_{n-1,i}\) for \(i\leq n-3\), so the \(G_{s,i}\) generate \(J_n\).

Their leading monomials are
\[
\operatorname{LM}(G_{s,i})=\Bigl(\prod_{\substack{0\leq j\leq s-2\\j\ne i}}x_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j=s}^{n-1}x_j^2\Bigr).
\]
For checking Buchberger's criterion, extend the notation by defining \(G_{s,s-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}G_{s,i}\) when it occurs *only on the right side* at a lower level, and put \(G_{1,0}=0\). The characteristic-two elementary-symmetric identities give the following exact polynomial reductions:
\[
(x_i+x_{s-1})G_{s,i}+(x_j+x_{s-1})G_{s,j}
=G_{s-1,i}+G_{s-1,j}\quad(i<j\leq s-2),
\]
\[
(x_{s-1}+x_s)G_{s,i}+x_s^2G_{s+1,i}=G_{s-1,i},
\]
\[
x_iG_{s,i}+x_s^2G_{s+1,s-1}
=x_s\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}G_{s,j}
+x_{s-1}\sum_{\substack{0\leq j\leq s-2\\j\ne i}}G_{s,j}
+G_{s-1,i}.
\]
The first identity handles pairs at the same level; the second handles adjacent levels with matching indices; the third handles the adjacent case in which the higher-level index is the last permitted index. For adjacent levels with two different non-last indices, the leading monomial of \(G_{s+1,i}\) divides their least common multiple, and the previously handled pairs apply by Buchberger's chain criterion. For nonadjacent levels, an intermediate level has a leading monomial dividing the pair's least common multiple, reducing the level gap inductively. All terms on the displayed right sides have leading monomials strictly below the relevant S-pair least common multiple in graded reverse lexicographic order; thus these are standard reductions. This proves the claimed Gröbner basis for *every* \(n\), without relying on finite experiments.

Let \(M_n=\operatorname{in}(J_n)\). Its leading monomials yield the recursion
\[
M_n=V_{n-1}+x_{n-1}^2M_{n-1},
\]
where \(V_{n-1}\) is the ideal generated by all squarefree monomials of degree \(n-2\) in \(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-2}\). Since \(V_{n-1}\subset M_{n-1}\), splitting standard monomials by the exponent of \(x_{n-1}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_n/M_n}(t)=(1+t)\operatorname{Hilb}_{k[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-2}]/V_{n-1}}(t)
+\frac{t^2}{1-t}\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_{n-1}/M_{n-1}}(t).
\]
The squarefree-Veronese quotient has Hilbert series
\[
\frac{\sum_{r=0}^{n-3}(r+1)t^r}{(1-t)^{n-3}}.
\]
With \(h_n(t)=(1-t)^{n-3}\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_n/J_n}(t)\), the recursion becomes
\[
h_n(t)=(1+t)\sum_{r=0}^{n-3}(r+1)t^r+t^2h_{n-1}(t).
\]
The base case is \(h_4(t)=1+3t+3t^2+t^3\). Induction and the binomial identity \(\binom{r+2}{2}-\binom{r+1}{2}=r+1\) give the displayed triangular numerator. Evaluating at \(t=1\) yields \(2\sum_{r=0}^{n-3}\binom{r+2}{2}=2\binom n3\).

For completeness, with at least three zero coordinates, both \(F_n\) and every derivative vanish. With one zero, \(F_n\ne0\); with precisely two zeros, at least one derivative is nonzero; and with no zero coordinates, writing \(F_n=(\prod_jx_j)\sum_jx_j^{-1}\) excludes simultaneous vanishing of \(F_n\) and all derivatives. Thus the reduced support is exactly the stated union. At a generic three-zero stratum, the normal quadratic part is a unit times \(ab+ac+bc\). Its two independent characteristic-two partial derivatives impose \(a=b=c\), after which the equation gives \(a^2=0\). Therefore the generic transverse singular scheme has length two.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_hilbert.py` builds the exact characteristic-two polynomial and Jacobian ideal, recomputes a Gröbner basis over the prime field, and checks the proposed leading monomials and exact Hilbert numerator through \(4\leq n\leq11\). It also checks the displayed S-polynomial reduction identities symbolically for \(n\leq8\). The checks use exact integer and finite-field polynomial arithmetic, not floating point. The all-size conclusion rests on the algebraic proof above, not on the finite computational range.

## Relationship to prior work
Aluffi and Marcolli introduce and study the banana-graph hypersurfaces, principally their motivic and characteristic-class geometry. Patterson studies graph-hypersurface multiplicity and degeneracy loci and explicitly distinguishes those from scheme-theoretic identities. Xie's later general theorem establishes characteristic-dependent *generic* local rings of elementary-symmetric derivative and singular schemes, including the generic multiplicity-two phenomenon here; the generic thickness and reduced-support multiplicity are therefore **not new claims**. The increment proved here is the explicit complete *graded Hilbert series for all edge counts*, controlling every Hilbert coefficient and not merely the top-dimensional cycle. No inspected reference supplied or implied this full series; possible equivalent formulations in unlocated commutative-algebra literature remain an originality risk.

## Limitations
No assertion is made about a primary decomposition, global reducedness of other elementary-symmetric schemes, or detailed local structure at intersections of the coordinate components. The theorem is specific to characteristic two. The full Hilbert series does not on its own imply Cohen–Macaulayness or Gorensteinness, and neither property is claimed. Independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
