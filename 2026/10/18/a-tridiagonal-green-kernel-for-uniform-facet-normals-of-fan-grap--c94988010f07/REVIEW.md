# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is finite unit-edge fan graphs with at least three path vertices and a distinguished hub. The random vector consists of the hub-anchored integer extreme Lipschitz potentials, sampled uniformly over primal facets, not of arbitrary Lipschitz functions. The cited facet criterion implies precisely the signed words whose zero entries each meet a nonzero neighbor, with no opposite consecutive signs. Conditioning on the zero set makes signs of separate nonzero runs independent and symmetric; hence the full second moment reduces exactly to the count of words whose interval is nonzero. A one-sided completion count satisfies the cubic recurrence and factors this count into two boundary contributions. This proves the covariance formula for all dimensions. The strictly positive adjacent Green minors and explicit algebraic inverse prove tridiagonal precision; generating-function residue analysis proves the fixed-separation bulk limit. An independent graph-traversal enumeration and exact fraction-based inverse calculation passed for sizes two through nine. These finite checks support but do not replace the symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by bibliographic limitations

Higashitani, Jochemko and Michałek's published integer facet criterion, Braun and Bruegge's sparse-graph facet enumeration, and Mori, Mori and Ohsugi's suspension graph dominating-set characterization are prerequisite results, **not** new claims. The suspension characterization supplies the set of signed potentials but does not present the evaluated uniform second-moment matrix, its tridiagonal inverse, or the sharp algebraic decay of bulk correlations. The closest indexed fan-graph mathematical result calculates facet-distance spectra and optimal inball centers, not pairwise facet-normal covariance; the full record was inaccessible, so this remains a stated bibliographic risk. The relevant previously recorded cactus-graph covariance theorem is limited to cactus graphs and Euclidean-centered potentials; fan graphs with at least three path vertices have overlapping triangles and the present covariance has distinct hub-anchored precision structure. Full texts of four relevant arXiv/open-access papers were examined at their claims and applicable sections; direct targeted searches did not identify a covering theorem. Failure to locate one is not proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finding identifies an exact, nontrivial stochastic-geometric invariant of an infinite family of dual Banach unit balls. While existing results characterize which facets exist, extracting their full pairwise covariance is a distinct weighted enumeration problem. The factorization reveals an unexpected local inverse covariance for a graph with dense overlapping short cycles, and the bulk limit gives a sharp universal correlation length. This links facets of transportation balls with Green matrices and sparse second-order geometry, enabling exact linear regressions and efficient second-moment calculations without listing exponentially many facets.
