# Scientific review: Gaussian angular law and half-normal widths of star cosmological polytopes

## Correctness — PASS
The canonical source vertices are independently verified in Kühne and Monin, *Faces of Cosmological Polytopes*, Definition 1.1 and Remark 1.2. The orthogonal leaf-coordinate change gives the exact support maximum and its conditional integral without a geometric approximation. Exchangeability plus sign symmetry rigorously gives the two vertex-normal-cone orbits. The collision formula is the exact power sum of those probabilities, not a simulated or assumed uniform-cone law. Elementary Gaussian maximum tail estimates give the needed convergence of both leaf maxima in second mean, while the common hub coordinate survives and produces the half-normal limit. Orthogonal projection, chi-radius independence, and inverse-chi moment estimates justify passage from Gaussian widths to unit-sphere directional widths. Explicit boundary cases include the equilateral three-vertex case at one leaf and the origin-dependence distinction between support and width.

The accompanying standalone implementation directly reconstructs all vertices, checks exact support identities over 3,600 deterministic Gaussian samples across 20 dimensions, verifies intrinsic width under projection, and tests twelve CDF evaluations numerically. Those diagnostics supplement, rather than establish, the all-dimensional proof. A finite simulation cannot certify an extreme-value limit.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty
The geometric source literature constructs the polytope and characterizes its faces and triangulations. The inspected full text of Kühne–Monin discusses facial combinatorics, not Gaussian angle or width distributions. Published work giving the star insphere and graph-independent circumsphere concerns different metrics (facet contact and minimal enclosing distance) and has no implication to individual Gaussian normal-cone measures. A prior covariance formula for uniform random source vertices is not a Gaussian maximum or exposed-vertex distribution. The precise contribution is the two-orbit angular law, all-order collision moments, and an order-one half-normal limit for normalized directional widths in the canonical star family.

Searches of published mathematical records using the star, Gaussian support, normal-cone, width, angular moment, and exposed-vertex aliases located no theorem covering these claims. This is a qualified literature assessment, not a proof of absolute novelty. A preprint on toric triangulations was checked at abstract level; the full text was not needed for an implication comparison because its identified topic does not determine Euclidean Gaussian angles. Earlier physics-specific notes might use unanticipated equivalent terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The angular distribution gives exact geometric information invisible to the number of vertices, facets, inball, circumball, or uniform-vertex covariance. Although the first orbit contains twice as many vertices as the second, the orbits asymptotically receive equal total Gaussian exposure probability. Consequently each vertex from the smaller orbit has twice the asymptotic angular weight, and the collision probability is inflated by the exact factor \(9/8\) against a uniform distribution on the same vertex set. The common hub coordinate also changes the universal Gumbel picture for maxima: order-one fluctuations in Euclidean widths retain an explicit half-normal law. These features are structural for a natural infinite family of polytopes, not an arbitrary finite numerical slice.

## Closest sources and limits
Kühne and Monin, *Faces of Cosmological Polytopes*, arXiv:2209.08069v1 (first public 2022-09-16; DOI 10.4171/AIHPD/192), is the verified primary MSC2020 52B05 source. Relevant published mathematical records concern the exact inball of star cosmological polytopes and the exact circumball of connected cosmological polytopes; their full scientific statements were compared and found distinct. Juhnke-Kubitzke, Solus, and Venturello, arXiv:2303.05876v1, address triangulations and normalized volumes. The result assumes the standard Euclidean realization of stars, and does not extend to arbitrary weighted graphs. The width limit is asymptotic and independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
