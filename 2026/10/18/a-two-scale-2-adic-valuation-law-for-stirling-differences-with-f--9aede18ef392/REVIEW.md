# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Inclusion--exclusion separates the two parity classes with no hidden cancellation within either group. The odd group is controlled by an explicitly reproduced \(2^{23}\)-modular initial certificate and a squaring induction, followed by a uniform binomial remainder estimate in both the difference parameter and the exponent. The even group has a distinct explicit four-term leading coefficient equal to \(8128=2^6\cdot127\). The valuations are combined by the ultrametric rule, and the equality case is stated as a lower bound only. The arithmetic and the executable certificate were checked from their packaged representations.

## Originality

**PASS under checked literature, with priority risk.** The full primary theorem statements of Zhao--Zhao--Hong (arXiv:1407.8443v1, 2014) distinguish ordinary block counts, the exceptional Mersenne counts three and seven, and a special adjacent-doubling result; the general two-parameter fifteen-block case is identified as a gap. The present exact two-scale formula allows arbitrary \(a>b\), not merely the adjacent-doubling case. The 2014 earlier article's abstract excludes all Mersenne block counts from its general result. Davis's work treats partial Stirling functions and valuation bounds; Miska's accessible theorems concern valuations of individual Stirling numbers on residue classes. None of the checked statements contains the two-scale formula or its resonance lower bound. A stronger result hidden in the uninspected parts of another source could imply it; bibliographic discovery is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value

**PASS.** The first unresolved Mersenne block count is a natural test of the existing general theorem's exact excluded boundary. The two competing scales reveal a genuine transition between the odd-index analytic term and the even-index exponentially small term. The resonance detects cancellation of two leading contributions rather than a formal substitution; the statement supplies the full fixed-pair asymptotic and a sharp nonresonant valuation over an unrestricted family of parameters.

## Limitations and closest literature

Theorem covers \(n\ge10\); it leaves exact resonant valuations unresolved. This is a mathematically precise partial resolution of the specific general-parameter Mersenne gap, not a theorem for all Mersenne counts. Priority checks cannot exclude all unindexed general results. Relevant sources are arXiv:1407.8443v1; DOI:10.1016/j.jnt.2014.01.005; D. M. Davis (2014) on partial Stirling functions; and arXiv:1803.04533.
