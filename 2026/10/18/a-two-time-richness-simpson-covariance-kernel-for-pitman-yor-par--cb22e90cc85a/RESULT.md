# A two-time richness–Simpson covariance kernel for Pitman–Yor partitions

## Finding

Consider the exchangeable random partition of the positive integers generated by the two-parameter Chinese restaurant process with parameters \(0\le\alpha<1\) and \(\theta> -\alpha\). Let \(K_n\) be the number of occupied blocks after \(n\) customers. For \(m\ge2\), define the empirical Simpson concentration (pair-coincidence fraction)
\[
Q_m=\frac{2}{m(m-1)}\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}\mathbf 1\{i\text{ and }j\text{ are in the same block}\}.
\]
For every pair of integers \(n,m\ge2\), set
\[
b_n=\frac{(1-\alpha)(\theta+\alpha)}{(\theta+1)^2}
\prod_{j=2}^{n-1}\frac{\theta+j+\alpha}{\theta+j},
\]
where the product is empty when \(n=2\), and set
\[
r_{n,m}=\begin{cases}1,&m\le n,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{n(n-1)(\theta+m)(\theta+m+1)}{m(m-1)(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)},&m>n.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the full two-time covariance kernel has the factorization
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,Q_m)=-b_n r_{n,m}<0.}
\]
In particular, every earlier Simpson estimate has *exactly the same* covariance with later richness: if \(2\le m\le n\), the result does not depend on \(m\). For \(m>n\), the remaining dependence separates into a factor in \(n\) and a factor in \(m\).

For the Dirichlet-process boundary \(\alpha=0\), the prefix covariance is the size-independent constant
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,Q_m)=-\frac{\theta}{(\theta+1)^2}\qquad (2\le m\le n).
\]
For fixed \(0<\alpha<1\), its magnitude has the exact power-law constant
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{n^\alpha}
=\frac{(1-\alpha)(\theta+\alpha)}{(\theta+1)^2}
\frac{\Gamma(\theta+2)}{\Gamma(\theta+2+\alpha)}.
\]
The numerator above is strictly positive throughout the allowed parameter range.

## Assumptions and scope

The partition starts with one customer in one block. Conditional on the first \(t\) customers occupying \(k\) blocks with sizes \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\), the next customer joins block \(i\) with probability \((s_i-\alpha)/(\theta+t)\) and starts a new block with probability \((\theta+\alpha k)/(\theta+t)\). These probabilities define the usual exchangeable Pitman–Yor partition, including the Dirichlet-process case \(\alpha=0\). Here \(\theta> -\alpha\) implies \(\theta+t>0\) for all \(t\ge1\). All expectations and covariances are with respect to this single partition process. The statistic \(Q_m\) is a *finite-sample pair-frequency statistic*, not the unobserved population concentration.

## Proof

**1. A tagged coincidence.** Let \(H=\mathbf 1\{1,2\text{ belong to the same block}\}\). The customer-two seating rule gives
\[
p:=\mathbb EH=\frac{1-\alpha}{\theta+1},
\qquad 1-p=\frac{\theta+\alpha}{\theta+1}.
\]
The number of blocks at time two is exactly \(K_2=2-H\). At any later time \(t\ge2\), the predictive mean satisfies
\[
\mathbb E[K_{t+1}\mid\Pi_t]=\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\theta+t}\right)K_t+\frac{\theta}{\theta+t}.
\]
Consequently, with \(a_n=\prod_{j=2}^{n-1}(1+\alpha/(\theta+j))\),
\[
\mathbb E[K_n\mid H=1]-\mathbb E[K_n\mid H=0]=-a_n.
\]
For a Bernoulli variable, \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,H)=p(1-p)[\mathbb E(X\mid H=1)-\mathbb E(X\mid H=0)]\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,H)=-p(1-p)a_n=-b_n.\tag{1}
\]
This argument also covers \(n=2\), when \(a_2=1\).

**2. Exact prefix invariance.** The partition is exchangeable under permutations of its customer labels. If \(m\le n\), every pair indicator \(\mathbf1\{i\sim j\}\) with \(1\le i<j\le m\) has the same covariance with the symmetric statistic \(K_n\) as \(H\). Averaging these indicators in the definition of \(Q_m\) and using (1) yields
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K_n,Q_m)=-b_n\qquad(2\le m\le n).\tag{2}
\]
This is an identity of the entire cross-time kernel, not an asymptotic assertion.

**3. Conditional two-future-sample prediction.** For a partition \(\Pi_n\), let
\[
S_n=\sum_{B\in\Pi_n}|B|(|B|-1)=n(n-1)Q_n.
\]
The seating rule gives the conditional probability that a specific old customer shares a block with the *next* customer. Summing over all old customers yields
\[
\frac{S_n+(1-\alpha)n}{\theta+n}.
\]
For the next two customers to coincide, the first of them either joins an old block or initiates a fresh one. Substituting the two seating probabilities and expanding gives the conditional coincidence probability
\[
\frac{S_n+(1-\alpha)(2n+\theta)}{(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)}.\tag{3}
\]
Here, the dependence on the present number of blocks cancels *exactly*. Conditional on \(\Pi_n\), future customer labels are exchangeable: any future customer has the same old–future matching probabilities, and any two future customers have the same pair-coincidence probability (3). This property follows either from the exchangeable partition law or by sampling from its directing random frequencies.

Write \(h=m-n>0\). The expected number of ordered coinciding pairs among the first \(m\) customers, conditional on \(\Pi_n\), is therefore
\[
\mathbb E[S_m\mid\Pi_n]=S_n
+\frac{2h[S_n+(1-\alpha)n]}{\theta+n}
+\frac{h(h-1)[S_n+(1-\alpha)(2n+\theta)]}{(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)}.
\]
The coefficient of \(S_n\) on the right simplifies to
\[
1+\frac{2h}{\theta+n}+\frac{h(h-1)}{(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)}
=\frac{(\theta+m)(\theta+m+1)}{(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)}.
\]
Because \(\mathbb EQ_m=\mathbb EH=p\) for every \(m\ge2\), this yields the centered conditional-expectation identity
\[
\mathbb E[Q_m-p\mid\Pi_n]=
\frac{n(n-1)(\theta+m)(\theta+m+1)}{m(m-1)(\theta+n)(\theta+n+1)}(Q_n-p).\tag{4}
\]
Taking covariance with the \(\Pi_n\)-measurable variable \(K_n\) and then using (2) proves the \(m>n\) case.

**4. Sign and scaling.** Both \(p\) and \(1-p\) are strictly positive; every factor in the product defining \(b_n\) is positive, and every ratio \(r_{n,m}\) is positive, proving strict negativity for all allowed parameters and sizes. The finite product in \(b_n\) equals
\[
\prod_{j=2}^{n-1}\frac{\theta+j+\alpha}{\theta+j}=
\frac{\Gamma(\theta+n+\alpha)\Gamma(\theta+2)}{\Gamma(\theta+n)\Gamma(\theta+2+\alpha)}.
\]
The standard gamma-ratio asymptotic yields the stated limit for \(0<\alpha<1\); for \(\alpha=0\), the product is exactly one. This completes the proof.

The proof above establishes the all-parameter and all-size result independently of these finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Pitman and Yor developed the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet partition law and its full exchangeable partition probability function; the original paper's Appendix A.2 gives finite-sample block-size distribution formulas. Pitman's treatment of the associated Chinese restaurant process supplies the familiar predictive seating rule and growth of block counts. Both are essential starting points, but the material inspected does not present a closed two-time covariance with empirical Simpson concentration, the prefix-invariance law, or the factorization (4). The general sampling formula permits a finite summation of moments in principle; obtaining the present explicit signed cross-time kernel requires the additional cancellation and conditional projection in step 3.

Cerquetti studies the distributional asymptotics of *conditional* alpha-diversity, primarily the number of distinct species, given observed information. This differs from exact two-time covariance of the empirical pair-coincidence statistic with richness.

A published result on universal richness–Gini–Simpson covariance treats a *fixed iid categorical sampling law*, whose probabilities are not randomly mixed by a Pitman–Yor prior. Its same-sample sign is consistent with the opposite sign for Simpson concentration here, but it does not supply the two-time Pitman–Yor covariance: averaging conditional covariances over random frequencies also requires a distinct covariance between the conditional means. This new conditional mixing term cannot be dropped. A contemporary hierarchical Pitman–Yor treatment derives predictions for Gini–Simpson diversity; its conditional predictive-diversity formulas are related but do not state this joint sample-size kernel for richness and realized pair collisions.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to exchangeable two-parameter Pitman–Yor partitions in the stated parameter range and to finite-sample empirical Simpson concentration; it does not claim a general negative-dependence theorem for arbitrary exchangeable partitions or a central limit theorem. Conditional on the directing random frequencies, pairs of observations are iid, but unconditional covariances require the mixture term discussed above. Existing general exchangeable-partition probability formulas logically determine finite moments; an equivalent explicit covariance may exist under older notation for population genetics or species sampling. That residual bibliographic possibility does not affect the direct proof.
