# Review of A uniform imbalance clock and two-sided logarithmic selection delay near Leslie–Gower neutrality

## Correctness — PASS

The exact rational formula (5) for the squared-imbalance odds is reconstructed by subtracting the two competitive-map coordinates. Direct bounds on the reciprocal total mass then prove the all-time logarithmic error bound (1), not merely a local expansion at a fixed point. This bound remains negligible at the singular generations \(nsymp |\varepsilon|^{-1}\log(1/|\varepsilon|)\). Transforming back to composition establishes the positive-side sigmoid, the negative-side relaxation profile, and the two first-passage constants; the one-step overshoot is \(O(|\varepsilon|)\). The finite verification runs 60 exact rational multiplier/sign checks, 14,448 drift checks, and 16 crossover comparisons; the finite checks do not prove the limits.

## Originality — PASS (qualified historical priority)

Cushing, Levarge, Chitnis and Henson (2004) already introduce and globally classify this Leslie–Gower competition model; equilibrium states, the neutral line and final coexistence/exclusion are explicitly prior. Their full source was inspected and its Theorem 4 does not state the quantitative uniformly valid imbalance clock or logarithmic two-sided first-passage law. Mathematically comparable records concerning neutral coexistence and symmetry-breaking in **different dynamical systems** were found, but no claim was identified that implies the exact present result. The potentially relevant Chow–Hsieh (2012) complete article was unavailable; its absence is **not** evidence of noncoverage. A general perturbation theorem may also subsume the result. These are real priority risks, not scientific proof gaps.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact selection clock solves a motivated quantitative problem at the boundary between coexistence and exclusion: it gives how an initially vanishing compositional asymmetry amplifies through a long apparent coexistence plateau and how a small fixed asymmetry relaxes toward equal mixture on the opposite side. Explicit dependence on \(a,\gamma,d,\kappa\) and the uniform-in-time estimate control the full \(|\varepsilon|^{-1}\log(1/|\varepsilon|)\) regime, which neither simple local stability nor ultimate-attractor classification describes.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest identical-model work is Cushing et al. (2004), DOI `10.1080/10236190410001652739`, with qualitative classification rather than the finite-time formulas. Chow–Hsieh (2012), DOI `10.1080/10236198.2012.656618`, addresses a broader Beverton–Holt competition class, but full text could not be inspected. The claim assumes symmetric growth/cross-competition and fixed \(a>1\), and does not transfer automatically to asymmetric growth, noisy populations, or multiple species.
