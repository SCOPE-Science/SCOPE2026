# Scientific review of the type-\(D_n\) root Rips wedge

**Correctness — PASS.** At the inclusive threshold the inner-product-one graph is characterized exactly by shared signed coordinates. The classification of pairwise-intersecting two-subsets partitions its simplices into signed-coordinate stars and exceptional triangles. Each star is the join of \(n-1\) two-point sets, each nonempty pairwise intersection is a vertex, and the attaching loops of the extra triangles reduce to the 2-faces of an \(n\)-crosspolytope. The resulting CW homotopy decomposition proves the stated integral wedge theorem for all \(n\ge5\). Independent within-package finite enumeration for \(n=4,5,6,7\) confirms all predicted mod-two homology ranks. The general proof does not extrapolate the finite data.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** A known rank-four root configuration has the special value \(23\) in degree two, but this does not imply the uniform family for \(n\ge5\), where two distinct positive homology dimensions coexist. Green's half-cube complex [1] concerns a different vertex configuration. Goyal--Shukla--Singh's complete multipartite line-graph result [2, Theorem 3.9] concerns a strict supergraph of the root adjacency graph; that theorem's two-dimensional homology cannot directly determine the newly present high-degree sphere summands. Statement-level searches across published related mathematical records and primary-publication searches did not find a covering formula. Green's complete paper was not accessible for close reading; an equivalently indexed signed-graph result remains possible. Search failure is not an originality proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The type-\(D_n\) root set is canonical, and \(\sqrt2\) is its first nontrivial Euclidean Rips threshold. For every \(n\ge5\), the filtration already contains two genuinely different sphere dimensions; the formula explains their structural origins, while the high-degree summands distinguish this object from ordinary complete multipartite line-graph clique complexes. The result establishes an infinite-family integral homotopy type rather than an isolated finite table. No later-scale or equivariant statement is asserted.
