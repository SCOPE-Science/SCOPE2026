## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

Neither their marginal maximum distribution nor their exceedance asymptotics states the covariance reversal or the sharp double-logarithmic location. Darling's 1953 abstract describes transforms for sums of spacing functionals, a broader mechanism from which mixed distributions could potentially be extracted, but not an established implication of the uniqueness/sign theorem. Its full text was not inspected. Classical full joint spacing laws are stronger distributional starting points, yet extracting and classifying the sign threshold and its asymptotic scale requires the new stop-loss and rare-exponential arguments. Targeted mathematical literature and research-record searches did not reveal direct coverage. Absence from the retrieved results is not a priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The maximum spacing measures an extreme event, while the count of spacings over a variable level is the natural exceedance statistic. These may correlate negatively at low levels because a large extreme consumes total mass, but positively at high levels when the maximum itself guarantees exceedance. The theorem proves exactly one crossover for **every** sample size, an exact finite formula, and the unexpected \((\log\log m+\log\log\log m)/m\) crossover scale, much smaller than the typical \((\log m)/m\) maximum. These are motivated structural results, not an isolated numeric cutoff.
