# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The independent backward-equation construction yields exact first-adsorption transforms \(f_\kappa\) and \(g_\kappa\). Geometric renewal gives the absorption-time transforms. The exact sign factorization and strict monotonicity of \(t/\sinh t\) prove existence, uniqueness and boundaries without relying on numerical data. The finite-mean consequence is obtained by differentiating the transform at zero. Numerical checks of the backward equation, factorization and signs passed 12,118 tests; these are supporting checks, not proof of infinite parameter coverage.

**Originality — PASS, subject to a substantive source-implication risk.** Bressloff–Linn (arXiv:2602.02446v1, §IV, equations (4.4), (4.6), (4.15), Figures 9–10) already derived the full Laplace transforms and described MFPT comparisons. None of that is claimed as new. The proposition assessed here is the *sharp all-discount sign and crossing classification*, including exactly one switch, the analytic boundary \(r_c\), and universality across wall waiting distributions. The exact transforms logically make this derivable; an evaluator treating their full explicit transform as dominating every sign theorem may regard this as implicitly covered. The inspected primary text does not state the one-crossing law. That risk is explicitly recorded rather than inferred away from a search omission.

**Value — PASS.** The motivating study's choice between wall launch and reset launch was framed using mean times and numerical contours. A mean-optimal decision can be inverted when discounted early absorption is prioritized. The theorem gives the entire parameter-space answer, an exact switch frequency, and a distribution-free robustness guarantee, avoiding a routine single-parameter recomputation. The proof shows where the reversal arises structurally and why only one occurs.

**Closest literature and limits.** The primary source's general renewal transforms are unusually close and are the principal originality risk. The 2025 Bressloff Brownian renewal work addresses different bulk dynamics. Results do not assert first-order stochastic ordering, nor cover noisy or interacting particles. For unbounded-mean holding times the Laplace classification survives but the mean comparison is withheld.
