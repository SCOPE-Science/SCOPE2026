# Review of the first-collision modular Jordan theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The coefficientwise word-count recurrence yields the integral polynomial identity. At \(n=p\) the rank-one map \(L_{b_p}=L_x^p-L_x\) is nonzero, square-zero and annihilates \(L_x\). The separating minimal-polynomial factorization forces exactly one size-two zero block, while reduction of the integral characteristic polynomial and the prior nonmodular multiplicity theorem fix every multiplicity. The finite-field verifier independently checks the literal multiplication table for three primes; these computations do not replace the general proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to priority risk.** Brown's earlier left-regular-band random-walk analysis, Saliola's quiver treatment, and Brauner--Commins--Reiner's all-characteristic invariant-ring polynomial relation and characteristic-zero full-module decomposition are acknowledged. The full-module decomposition in their Theorem 4.11 requires \(|S_n|\) invertible and does not cover the case \(n=p=\operatorname{char}(k)\). The rank-one modular extension calculation addresses this gap. Searches under modular random-to-top, Jordan form and free left-regular-band aliases found no dominating published statement in inspected sources. An earlier equivalent shuffle-theoretic formulation remains possible.

## Value
**PASS.** A natural operator has an exact sharp change of semisimplicity at the first modular alphabet size, yet only one nonsimple Jordan block among \(\sum_{r=0}^p p!/(p-r)!\) basis vectors. The formula covers every prime and the full algebra, with a canonical, directly computable obstruction.

## Scope and remaining risks
The theorem does not classify alphabets larger than the characteristic, weighted generators, or other semigroups. The ordinary spectral multiplicities come from published work and are not asserted as new. Priority completeness and independent validation remain open.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
