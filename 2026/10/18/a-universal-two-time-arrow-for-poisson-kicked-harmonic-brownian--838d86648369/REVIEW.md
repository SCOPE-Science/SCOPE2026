# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stationary Poisson integral is well defined under the logarithmic moment. The bivariate Lévy measure has a nonempty oblique component on slope \(e^{-\kappa t}\), whereas the reversed measure is supported on the reciprocal slope plus the horizontal axis. By uniqueness of Lévy characteristics they cannot agree. For the finite-fourth-moment extension, the mixed cumulant identity \(K_{p,q}=e^{-q\kappa t}c_{p+q}\) and the cancellation of Gaussian pairings establish all signs and normalizations. Differentiation proves the unique optimal lags. Exact rational calculations passed 2,310 assertions, supporting but not replacing the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (same-model, priority risk retained)

The main closest source is Di Bello et al., arXiv:2309.13424, Section 5, which already introduces the model and exhibits numerical fourth-order time asymmetry for symmetric Gaussian kicks, but does not provide the signed all-lag formula, unique optimal lag, two-lag identification or general moment-free bivariate obstruction. Applebaum, arXiv:1411.2752, supplies general Lévy stochastic-integral machinery and stationary OU existence tools, not the stated quantitative active-bath classification. Ohga et al., arXiv:2303.13116, study general finite-state correlation-asymmetry bounds, not this exact law. A broader published equivalence theorem might imply the Lévy-support portion; the older Eliazar–Klafter shot-noise article could not be inspected in full. These are genuine outstanding priority risks, not independently cleared.

## Scientific value — PASS

The exact sign and peak lag resolve a measurement motivated explicitly by the original active-bath numerical figure. The full-law result separates invisible Gaussian covariance from genuine two-time irreversibility even when high moments cannot be used. The ratio at two observation lags gives an experimentally interpretable identification mechanism insensitive to unknown shot strength and thermal diffusivity at the ideal-theory level. These are not a mere restatement of the stationary kurtosis formula.

## Scope and limitations

Fourth-order estimates require finite \(\mathbb E|Y|^4\); estimation noise, nonlinear traps, entropy production, and transient initial laws are not treated. The claim of priority is provisional pending independent assessment of broader Lévy and shot-noise literature.
