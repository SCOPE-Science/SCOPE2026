# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The positive form and strict positivity for nonzero square-summable sequences make the multiplicative inequality well-defined. The exact resolvent-representation identity reduces the global constant to diagonal Green values. The free kernel and rank-one resolvent yield an explicitly factored rational function. Its quadratic derivative has a unique root in the admissible interval, giving the sharp constant and equality class. The complementary regime is controlled by the free lattice and a matching escape sequence. Localization uniqueness follows from eliminating the parameter between the stationary equations. Reproducing-kernel recurrence checks, symbolic expansions and finite-section comparisons are independently reproducible using `verify.py`.

## Originality
**PASS under the inspected evidence, with residual historical-priority uncertainty.** The closest published discrete source is Ilyin--Laptev--Zelik, arXiv:1407.0675v1, which identifies the sharp *homogeneous* one-dimensional constant and its Green function, but does not state an exceptional-bond phase transition or localized extremizer classification. The more general Ilyin--Zelik Green-function framework, arXiv:1303.0957v1, discusses manifolds, not the exact defective Jacobi matrix. Results on discrete Dirichlet interpolation for a half-line and higher-dimensional supercritical lattice Sobolev constants are logically different: they do not specialize to the present bi-infinite, single-bond multiplicative problem. Standard rank-one resolvent formulas alone do not supply the optimized sharp constant, global attainment classification or the nonmonotone concentration threshold. Some alternative weighted-Jacobi formulations may remain uninspected.

## Value
**PASS.** Sharp embedding constants are natural stability measures for lattice interpolation under a local conductivity defect. The result completely resolves a one-parameter model, identifies exactly when the optimal constant is attained, and exhibits the counterintuitive minimum localization length at a nonextremal defect strength. The quantitative onset and disconnection asymptotics distinguish the two singular perturbation regimes; the result is not simply a restatement of the unchanged homogeneous inequality.

## Limits
The model allows one and only one altered edge on a bi-infinite nearest-neighbor lattice. No multi-defect or higher-dimensional assertion is made. Historical priority remains open to further independent checking.
