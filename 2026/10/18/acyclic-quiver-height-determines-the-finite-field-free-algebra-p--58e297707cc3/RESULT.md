# Acyclic-quiver height determines the finite-field free-algebra potential

## Finding
Let \(Q\) be any finite nonempty acyclic directed multiquiver (parallel arrows are allowed), let \(q\) be a prime power, and let \(A=\mathbb F_qQ\) be its unital path algebra. Write \(h\) for the maximum number of vertices on an oriented path in \(Q\), including the trivial one-vertex paths. Let \(F_n\) be the relatively free unital associative \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra on \(n\) generators in the variety generated by \(A\), and put \(d_n=\dim_{\mathbb F_q} F_n\). Then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_n}=q^h,
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\log |F_n|}=q^h.
\]
In fact, for every integer \(n\ge 2\),
\[
q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)^{h-1}\le d_n \le C_Q n^{h-1}q^{hn},
\]
where \(C_Q=h|B|\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}|B|^r\) is an admissible explicit constant and \(B\) denotes the finite set of all paths in \(Q\). For \(h=1\) (no arrows), one has the exact formula \(d_n=q^n\) for every \(n\ge1\). Thus the potential is independent of the number of vertices not on a longest path, branching, and parallel-arrow multiplicities.

## Assumptions and scope
The signature consists of addition, \(\mathbb F_q\)-scalar multiplication, associative multiplication, and a distinguished unit. A polynomial function \(A^n\to A\) is the evaluation of a unital noncommutative polynomial in \(n\) variables with \(\mathbb F_q\)-coefficients. By the evaluation kernel description of identities, \(F_n\) is exactly the algebra of these polynomial functions; its dimension is finite. The finite directed graph may be disconnected and may have parallel arrows, but may not have directed cycles. The result concerns this specified linear-algebra variety, not the multiplicative-semigroup variety of \(A\).

## Proof
Choose the finite path basis \(B\), containing one length-zero idempotent \(e_v\) for each vertex \(v\). Write a generic input \(X_i\) as \(\sum_{p\in B}c_{i,p}p\), for \(1\le i\le n\). To bound \(d_n\), consider the coefficient of any fixed output path \(p\in B\) in the evaluation of an arbitrary word in the inputs. Expand every occurrence of each \(X_i\) in the path basis. Every surviving product is a concatenation of path factors, and the nontrivial path factors constitute a factorization of \(p\) into \(r\) nonempty paths, with \(0\le r\le h-1\). All other factors are vertex idempotents at one of the \(r+1\) successive vertices. There are finitely many choices of the output path and its nonempty-path factorization, independent of \(n\); after fixing such a choice, the nonempty factors are picked from at most \(n^r\) choices of input variables. The scalar diagonal factors belong to the vector space of all \(\mathbb F_q\)-valued functions of the \(n(r+1)\) diagonal-coordinate variables at those vertices, a space of dimension at most \(q^{n(r+1)}\). Hence, summing over path coefficients and factorizations, one obtains a constant \(C_Q\) with
\[
d_n\le C_Q\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}n^r q^{n(r+1)}\le C_Q h\,n^{h-1}q^{hn}.
\]
Absorbing the factor \(h\) into the constant gives the displayed upper bound. The same bound applies to arbitrary polynomial linear combinations because words span the polynomial-function algebra.

For the lower bound, fix a maximal oriented path \(v_1\xrightarrow{a_1}v_2\xrightarrow{a_2}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_{h-1}}v_h\), selecting individual arrows if parallel arrows occur. Restrict evaluations of \(X_1,\ldots,X_{n-1}\) to diagonal path-algebra elements, so that at vertex \(v_j\) their coordinate vector is \(\mathbf b_j\in\mathbb F_q^{n-1}\). Set the final input \(X_n=a_1+\cdots+a_{h-1}\). For \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb F_q^{n-1}\), define a noncommutative polynomial in the first \(n-1\) variables by
\[
\Delta_{\mathbf b}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-(X_i-b_i1)^{q-1}\bigr),
\]
with factors in increasing index order. On diagonal inputs its coefficient at vertex \(v_j\) is exactly the indicator of \(\mathbf b_j=\mathbf b\), since every nonzero field element has \((q-1)\)-st power one.

For each tuple \(\boldsymbol\beta=(\mathbf b_1,\ldots,\mathbf b_h)\) with \(\mathbf b_j\ne\mathbf b_{j+1}\), select \(i_j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) where the two neighboring vectors differ, and form
\[
T_{\boldsymbol\beta}=\Delta_{\mathbf b_1}[X_{i_1},X_n]\Delta_{\mathbf b_2}\cdots[X_{i_{h-1}},X_n]\Delta_{\mathbf b_h},
\]
where \([Y,Z]=YZ-ZY\). For the indicated restricted evaluation with diagonal vectors \(\mathbf c_1,\ldots,\mathbf c_h\), its coefficient along the fixed nontrivial path \(a_1\cdots a_{h-1}\) is zero except when \(\mathbf c_j=\mathbf b_j\) for every \(j\). In that case the coefficient is the product
\[
\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(b_{j,i_j}-b_{j+1,i_j}),
\]
which is nonzero in \(\mathbb F_q\). Consequently the distinct \(T_{\boldsymbol\beta}\) are linearly independent polynomial functions: their evaluations have disjoint distinguished nonzero coordinates. The number of admissible tuples is \(q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)^{h-1}\), giving the lower bound. For \(h=1\), the single polynomial \(\Delta_{\mathbf b}\) construction already gives \(q^{n-1}\) independent functions, and ordinary one-vertex scalar interpolation using all \(n\) variables gives \(d_n=q^n\). If there are multiple isolated vertices, the same scalar polynomials act coordinatewise and all variables on any one selected vertex can be chosen independently, so \(d_n=q^n\) still holds.

Taking \(n\)-th roots of the bounds yields \(\lim\sqrt[n]{d_n}=q^h\). Since \(|F_n|=q^{d_n}\), multiplying \(d_n\) by the fixed factor \(\log q\) leaves the root limit unchanged, proving the second identity.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library Python script \(\texttt{artifacts/verify.py}\) constructs finite binary path algebras, forms the vector space of all polynomial evaluation functions by closing the span of the unit and coordinate projections under right multiplication, and checks dimensions on six examples (isolated vertices, a single arrow, two parallel arrows, a directed three-chain, and a two-arrow fork). It also independently checks all \(36\) separator supports for a three-vertex chain with two diagonal inputs. Run with \(\texttt{python3 artifacts/verify.py}\). These finite tests supplement, but do not establish, the universal asymptotic proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Olshanskii (2026, arXiv:2606.04577) introduces the finite-algebra potential and establishes a maximal-potential criterion for prime rings/algebras. His criterion does not evaluate the nonprime acyclic path-algebra family or prove this height formula. Berele, Cerulli Irelli, De Loera Chávez, and Pascucci (2026) prove polynomial-identity equivalences between quiver path algebras and incidence algebras **over characteristic-zero fields**; this does not establish finite-field free-algebra dimensions. Gonçalves and Riva (2020) study graded polynomial identities of finite-field upper-triangular matrix algebras. This overlaps the chain case in objects, but their full text was not accessible for a decisive implication comparison and may contain information from which some special cases follow. Earlier discussions of free objects of triangular matrix multiplicative-semigroup varieties use a different algebraic signature and do not directly supply the present linear-variety theorem.

## Limitations
This result makes no assertion for quivers with directed cycles, infinite quivers, arbitrary quotient algebras of path algebras, or varieties of multiplicative semigroups. The upper estimate is not claimed to be an asymptotic equivalent, and the result does not determine a general exact finite-rank dimension formula. Although the closest accessible primary texts and targeted searches did not reveal a covering theorem, uninspected finite-field upper-triangular free-algebra literature remains a specific originality risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
