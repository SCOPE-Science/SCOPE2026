# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The relatively free algebra is the span of polynomial evaluation functions in the chosen unital associative \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra signature. Every surviving coefficient of a polynomial word follows a fixed directed path, with at most \(h-1\) nontrivial path factors. The finite factorization count and the \(q^{n(r+1)}\)-dimensional diagonal function space give the universal upper bound. Finite-field diagonal interpolation and commutator separators create \(q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)^{h-1}\) independent functions along a longest path. Taking roots proves both claimed limits. All fields, disconnected cases, and parallel arrows are included; directed cycles are excluded. The finite verification script passed the six small path-algebra cases and \(36\) separator tests; those checks do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS with recorded residual risk
The fully inspected Olshanskii paper defines potential and proves a prime/maximality characterization, not the path-height determination for nonprime acyclic path algebras. The fully inspected characteristic-zero polynomial-identity equivalence of Berele and coauthors does not imply the finite-field polynomial-function growth formula. Searches for aliases involving free spectra, triangular matrix varieties, and path algebras did not produce a covering published finding in the queried mathematical index. An earlier upper-triangular finite-field graded-identity paper overlaps the chain case; its accessible abstract does not establish the all-quiver theorem, but inaccessible detailed results remain a genuine possibility. Absence from searches does not prove novelty.

## Value — PASS
The motivating potential is a structural invariant of locally finite varieties. This exact result computes it for every finite acyclic path algebra over every finite field, giving an invariant determined by longest directed path rather than total algebra dimension, branching, or parallel-arrow multiplicities. The theorem includes infinitely many nonprime algebras, supplies reproducible quantitative bounds, and clarifies the information retained by the invariant. It is not merely a finite small-case computation.

## Independent review and limitations
The proof addresses the specified unital finite-field algebra variety; it does not calculate exact arbitrary-rank dimensions or classify cyclic quivers. Inaccessible older literature is explicitly identified in the accompanying structured report. Verification is same-model only.
