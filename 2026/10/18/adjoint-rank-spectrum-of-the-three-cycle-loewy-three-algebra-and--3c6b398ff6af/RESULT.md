# Adjoint-rank spectrum of the three-cycle Loewy-three algebra and the nine-dimensional Taft algebra

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, and let \(Q\) be the consistently oriented cycle on three vertices. Write \(J\) for the ideal generated by its arrows and \(A=kQ/J^3\). Thus \(A\) has dimension nine. For every \(a\in A\), put \(r(a)=\operatorname{rank}_k(b\mapsto ab-ba)\). The entire elementwise inner-derivation rank polynomial is

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_q(t):=\sum_{a\in A}t^{r(a)}={}&q+3q(q-1)t+q(q-1)^2(q+2)t^2\\
&+3q^4(q-1)t^3+q^4(q-1)(q^2+4q-2)t^4\\
&+3q^5(q-1)^2(q+1)t^5+q^7(q-1)(q-2)t^6.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q=2\) the ranks are exactly \(0,1,2,3,4,5\); for every \(q>2\) the ranks are exactly \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6\). In particular \(Z(A)=k\cdot1\), and all element-centralizer dimensions and multiplicities are \(9-r\) with coefficient \([t^r]F_q(t)\). The commuting probability is

\[
\Pr_{a,b\in A}(ab=ba)=\frac{F_q(q^{-1})}{q^9}
=\frac{5q^4+q^3-6q^2-q+2}{q^{10}}.
\]

When \(q\equiv1\pmod 3\), the same polynomial and probability apply to the nine-dimensional Taft Hopf algebra (as an associative algebra), since it is isomorphic to \(A\). The assertion concerns the ordinary algebra commutator, not the Hopf adjoint action.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertices are indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\). Denote the trivial path by \(e_i\), the arrow \(i\to i+1\) by \(u_i\), and its length-two successor path \(i\to i+2\) by \(v_i=u_i u_{i+1}\). The nine elements \(e_i,u_i,v_i\) are a basis of \(A\). Products are path concatenations when endpoints match and total path length is at most two; all products of total path length at least three vanish. No characteristic restrictions are imposed on this path-algebra assertion.

Write an arbitrary element uniquely as

\[
a=\sum_i d_i e_i+\sum_i b_i u_i+\sum_i c_i v_i
\quad(d_i,b_i,c_i\in k).
\]

For the Taft specialization choose a primitive third root \(\zeta\in k\). The Taft algebra generated by \(g,x\) with \(g^3=1\), \(x^3=0\), and \(gx=\zeta xg\) has dimension nine; Fourier idempotents of \(k[g]\) supply the three \(e_i\), multiplication by \(x\) supplies the cyclic arrows, and multiplication by \(x^2\) supplies their length-two concatenations. The restriction \(q\equiv1\pmod3\) ensures that these idempotents split over \(k\).

## Proof

**1. Commutator matrix and diagonal types.** In the basis listed above, direct path multiplication gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
[a,e_j]&=\sum_i b_i(\mathbf1_{i+1=j}-\mathbf1_{i=j})u_i
 +\sum_i c_i(\mathbf1_{i+2=j}-\mathbf1_{i=j})v_i,\\
[a,u_j]&=(d_j-d_{j+1})u_j+b_{j-1}v_{j-1}-b_{j+1}v_j,\\
[a,v_j]&=(d_j-d_{j+2})v_j.
\end{aligned}
\]

All indices are modulo three. The radical \(J\) has dimension six, its square has dimension three, and \([a,A]\subseteq J\).

If \(d_0,d_1,d_2\) are pairwise distinct, the map \(\operatorname{ad}_a\vert_J\) is block-triangular, with six nonzero diagonal entries \(d_i-d_{i+1}\) and \(d_i-d_{i+2}\). Hence \(r(a)=6\) for any \(b_i,c_i\). There are \(q(q-1)(q-2)q^6\) such elements, explaining the coefficient of \(t^6\).

**2. Exactly two equal diagonal values.** Fix \(d_0=d_1=\lambda\ne\mu=d_2\), or any cyclic rotation. The diagonal element \(D=\sum d_i e_i\) has two spectral blocks \(P=e_0+e_1\) and \(Q=e_2\). The corner \(PAP\) is four-dimensional, with two vertex idempotents and precisely two opposite-direction connecting paths, one of length one and one of length two. Their products are zero, so \(PAP\) is a radical-square-zero two-cycle algebra. The opposite corner \(QAQ\) has dimension one. The two cross-corners \(PAQ\) and \(QAP\) together have dimension four.

Every element reducing to \(D\) modulo \(J\) is conjugate by a unit of \(1+J\) to one whose off-diagonal spectral cross-blocks vanish: lift the pair of complementary spectral idempotents across the nilpotent ideal \(J\), then conjugate the lift to \(P,Q\). On the cross-corners the commutator has invertible semisimple part \(\lambda-\mu\), so has rank four. In \(PAP\), the residual element has constant diagonal \(\lambda\) and a radical part \(\rho\); its commutator rank is zero for \(\rho=0\) and one for \(\rho\ne0\). Therefore total rank is four or five.

The \(1+J\)-orbit of \(D\) has cardinality \(q^{6-2}=q^4\): its stabilizer is \(1+(J\cap C_A(D))\), and the latter vector space has dimension two (exactly the two paths between the equal-value vertices). This orbit is precisely the locus whose \(PAP\)-part is scalar, by the conjugacy of lifts of a split separable semisimple subalgebra. Thus among the \(q^6\) radical choices for this \(D\), exactly \(q^4\) have rank four, and the remaining \(q^6-q^4\) have rank five. There are \(3q(q-1)\) choices of two-equal diagonals.

**3. All diagonal values equal.** A scalar does not affect the adjoint map, so assume \(d_0=d_1=d_2=0\). Split the commutator matrix by \(A/J\), \(J/J^2\), and \(J^2\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&k\{e_i\}&k\{u_i\}\\\hline
k\{u_i\}&B&0\\
k\{v_i\}&C&K
\end{array}
\]

and the remaining three columns corresponding to \(v_i\) are zero. Here \(B\) and \(C\) are the weighted incidence maps of the first-step and second-step directed triangles, respectively, with weights \(b_i\) and \(c_i\). Their rows evaluate \(b_i(z_{i+1}-z_i)\) and \(c_i(z_{i+2}-z_i)\). The matrix \(K\) sends coordinates \(w_i\) to \(b_iw_{i+1}-b_{i+1}w_i\).

If \(b_0=b_1=b_2=0\), then \(B=K=0\). The rank of \(C\) is zero when all \(c_i=0\), one when precisely one \(c_i\) is nonzero, and two when at least two are nonzero. The corresponding counts among \(q^3\) choices are

\[
1,\quad3(q-1),\quad q^3-1-3(q-1)=(q-1)^2(q+2).
\]

If \(b\ne0\), then \(K\) always has rank two. This follows immediately by inspecting a nonzero \(b_j\): two rows of \(K\) are independent, while its determinant is identically zero. Let \(s=|\{i:b_i\ne0\}|\).

When \(s=1\), the incidence map \(B\) has rank one. Its kernel identifies the two endpoints of that one nonzero arrow. The only coordinate functional on the target \(J^2\) outside \(\operatorname{im}K\) is evaluation on the reverse length-two path between those same endpoints; hence \(C(\ker B)\subseteq\operatorname{im}K\). Therefore the block matrix has rank \(2+1=3\), regardless of \(c\). There are \(3(q-1)q^3\) radical choices.

When \(s=2\) or \(s=3\), the nonzero first-step edges connect the entire triangle; \(B\) has rank two and its kernel is the constant vector line. Every incidence map \(C\) kills constants, so the block matrix has rank \(2+2=4\), regardless of \(c\). The count is

\[
[3(q-1)^2+(q-1)^3]q^3=(q-1)^2(q+2)q^3.
\]

Multiplying these five all-equal counts by the \(q\) possible common values of \(d_i\), and adding the two-equal and all-distinct contributions above, gives exactly \(F_q(t)\). Substituting \(t=1\) yields \(F_q(1)=q^9\), independently confirming exhaustive case partitioning.

**4. Centralizers and commuting pairs.** Since \(\dim_k C_A(a)=9-r(a)\), the number of ordered commuting pairs is \(\sum_a q^{9-r(a)}\); division by \(q^{18}\) yields \(F_q(q^{-1})/q^9\). Algebraic simplification gives the displayed rational probability. All calculations work in characteristic two because they use only incidence ranks and differences of distinct diagonal field values.

## Verification

The accompanying self-contained `verify.py` constructs the multiplication table from the nine paths, derives all nine-by-nine commutator matrices by independent path concatenation, and performs finite-field Gaussian elimination. For each of the three diagonal-multiplicity types it exhaustively checks all \(q^6\) radical choices for \(q=2,3,4,5\) (the all-distinct type is absent for \(q=2\)). The \(q=4\) case uses an explicitly implemented four-element extension field, testing characteristic two with a primitive cube root of unity. The program prints `VERIFY_OK` only after each conditional histogram agrees with the proof. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the symbolic argument, which establishes every prime power.

## Relationship to prior work

Taft introduced finite-dimensional nonsemisimple Hopf algebras [1]; Chen, Van Oystaeyen and Zhang [2] give the Taft algebra's path-quiver and Loewy context while computing its representation Green ring. Shen [3] studies singularity categories of Nakayama algebras. Dutta, Basnet and Nath [4] establish general finite-ring commuting-probability relations and bounds. These inspected results do not state the present seven-coefficient elementwise adjoint-rank polynomial; a Green-ring product formula and a module-category equivalence do not by themselves determine centralizer dimensions of algebra elements. The truncated cyclic algebra with \(J^2=0\) is a proper quotient of the present algebra, but its centralizer ranks cannot recover the extra length-two commutators and the rank-five resonance stratum.

## Limitations

This is an exact result for the specified nine-dimensional cyclic truncated algebra, not an all-dimensional theorem for cyclic Nakayama or Taft algebras. The Taft identification requires a primitive cube root in the ground field; without that root, the path algebra still exists and the stated polynomial remains valid for it, but the Taft specialization is not asserted. The result concerns ordinary commutators, not Hopf adjoint actions, tensor-product multiplicities, or scheme-theoretic commutator fibers. No assertion of uniqueness in the literature is made.
