# Affine successor-hull equivalence and the exact depth-two spectrum of the one-variable modal parity fragment

## Finding
Fix any finite set of \(n\geq1\) propositional variables, and evaluate modal formulas using standard Kripke semantics on arbitrary frames \((W,R)\), with the Boolean connectives exclusive-or, truth constants, and negation and the modalities \(\Diamond\) and \(\Box\). At each finite modal depth \(d\), choose a finite basis of the vector space of depth-at-most-\(d\) formula truth functions over \(\mathbb F_2\), including the constant truth function, and let \(e_d(w)\) be its joint truth vector at a world \(w\).

**Affine successor-hull theorem.** For every \(d\geq0\) and any two pointed Kripke models, their worlds agree on **every** formula of modal depth at most \(d+1\) if and only if (i) their current propositional valuations agree and (ii) the affine hulls of their successor truth vectors \(\{e_d(v):wRv\}\) are equal, with the empty hull distinguished from every nonempty hull. This holds for infinite models, arbitrary accessibility relations, and all formulas of the stated bounded depth.

**Optimal successor compression.** If that affine hull has dimension \(r\), a world with at least one successor is depth-\(d+1\)-equivalent to a pointed model having exactly \(r+1\) immediate successors, and no depth-\(d+1\)-equivalent model can have fewer immediate successors. If the successor set is empty, the exact minimum is zero. The construction can be implemented recursively on a finite rooted tree with depth limited by the formula depth.

**Exact one-variable census.** For one propositional variable, there are exactly \(8\) depth-one pointed theories and exactly \(192\) depth-two pointed theories on arbitrary Kripke frames. At depth two, the \(8\) possible depth-one successor types have precisely \(96\) different affine hulls, including the empty hull; each may be combined with either valuation of the root variable. Their affine-hull dimension distribution, with the empty hull listed separately, is \((1,8,28,38,20,1)\) at dimensions \((\mathrm{empty},0,1,2,3,4)\). Five immediate successors are necessary and sufficient in the worst case for preserving all depth-two one-variable modal parity formulas; this bound is attained by a finite rooted tree. For \(n=1,2,3\) variables, the exact depth-one pointed-theory counts are respectively \(8,48,416\).

## Assumptions and scope
A Kripke model has a nonempty set \(W\), an unrestricted binary relation \(R\subseteq W\times W\), and a valuation of \(n\) variables. \(\Diamond\varphi\) holds at \(w\) precisely when some \(v\) with \(wRv\) satisfies \(\varphi\); \(\Box\varphi\) holds when all such \(v\) satisfy \(\varphi\), also when no successors exist. Exclusive-or denotes parity of two truth values. Equivalence at depth \(d\) means agreement on every formula of modal nesting depth at most \(d\), not unrestricted modal equivalence. An affine hull is the smallest affine subspace containing a nonempty set of binary vectors; by convention the hull of the empty set is an extra symbol \(\varnothing\). The rank/dimension of an affine hull is that of its difference space. No S5 reflexivity, transitivity, or symmetry is assumed.

## Proof
**1. Finite truth-function spaces.** Let \(V_d\) be the set of all \(\mathbb F_2\)-valued functions of pointed Kripke models defined by formulas of depth at most \(d\). Formula equivalence is pointwise over all pointed models. Because the fragment contains constants and is closed under exclusive-or, \(V_d\) is a vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). For \(d=0\), it is spanned by the truth constant and the \(n\) atomic truth functions, hence has dimension \(n+1\). Inductively, if \(V_d\) is finite, the set \(\{\Diamond f:f\in V_d\}\) is finite. The space \(V_{d+1}\) is spanned by the propositional atomic and constant functions and those modalized functions: formulas with several modal subformulas can only combine their truth values affinely. Indeed, \(\neg f=1\oplus f\) and \(\Box f=1\oplus\Diamond(1\oplus f)\). Thus \(V_{d+1}\) is finite-dimensional. Choose any basis \(\beta_d=(\top,\varphi_2,\ldots,\varphi_{m_d})\); write \(e_d(w)\in\mathbb F_2^{m_d}\) for simultaneous truth values. Its first coordinate is always one. Every formula of depth at most \(d\) is a linear functional on \(e_d(w)\), and every linear functional is represented by a formula formed from the basis via exclusive-or.

**2. Affine-hull test lemma.** For finite-dimensional binary affine space and sets \(S,T\) of vectors in the same affine hyperplane with first coordinate one, the following are equivalent: (a) their affine hulls agree, treating emptiness separately; (b) for every linear functional \(\ell\), \(\ell\) equals one at some point of \(S\) if and only if it equals one at some point of \(T\). Forward implication: a linear functional vanishes on \(S\) if and only if it vanishes on its affine hull, since affine combinations preserve its value when coefficients sum to one. Backward implication: if exactly one set is empty, the first-coordinate functional distinguishes them. If both are nonempty with different affine hulls, some point of one hull lies outside the other; affine linear algebra supplies an affine functional zero on the second hull and one at that point. Because the first coordinate is fixed at one, that affine functional is a linear functional on the full vector space. It vanishes on the second original set and is nonzero on the first hull, hence on at least one point of the first original set. This contradicts (b). The proof also covers nested proper affine hulls by interchanging the sides.

**3. Bounded-depth characterization.** At a world \(w\), each formula of depth at most \(d+1\) is an affine combination of atomic truth values and formulas \(\Diamond\varphi\) with \(\varphi\in V_d\). By Part 1, these modal tests run through the linear functionals \(\ell(e_d(v))\) over successor worlds \(v\). Part 2 identifies simultaneous equality of all such tests with equality of their affine successor hulls. If root propositional valuations differ, an atom separates the roots; if hulls differ, Part 2 produces a depth-\(d\) affine formula whose diamond separates them. The box operator adds no observations because of its duality to diamond and negation. This proves both directions for every \(d\), including infinite frames.

**4. Exact and sharp branching reduction.** Suppose the successor hull \(H\) is nonempty of affine dimension \(r\). Select \(r+1\) actual successor vectors that form an affine basis of \(H\); retain the corresponding distinct successors, and delete the other outgoing edges at the root. For retaining their depth-\(d\) truth vectors, use a separate copy of each selected successor's generated model to depth \(d\), so that the induced rooted model has exactly \(r+1\) immediate successors. By Part 3 it is depth-\(d+1\)-equivalent to the original. Conversely any model with the same depth-\(d+1\) theory has precisely the same nonempty affine successor hull by Part 3; fewer than \(r+1\) immediate successor worlds cannot span an affine subspace of dimension \(r\). For an empty hull, \(\Diamond\top\) forces zero successors. Iterating on copied children produces a finite, depth-bounded rooted tree; it preserves the root theory at the claimed depth. This says nothing about infinite-depth equivalence.

**5. The exact one-variable depth-two count.** At depth zero, the evaluation vector is \((1,p)\). An arbitrary depth-one world is determined by \(p\in\{0,1\}\) and by which of the two propositional successor values \(0\) and \(1\) occur. There are precisely four successor possibilities: no successor, only \(0\), only \(1\), and both. They are distinguished by \(\Diamond\top\), \(\Diamond\neg p\), and \(\Diamond p\). All are realized by elementary finite trees. Therefore the eight depth-one evaluation types have the distinct four-coordinate encodings
\[
  (p,\,u\lor v,\,u,\,v),\qquad p,u,v\in\{0,1\},
\]
where \(u\) says that some successor has \(p=0\) and \(v\) says that some successor has \(p=1\). These coordinates correspond to the depth-one formula basis \((p,\Diamond\top,\Diamond\neg p,\Diamond p)\), in addition to the constant coordinate one. Their binary encodings, with bits ordered as displayed from low to high, are exactly \(\{0,1,6,7,10,11,14,15\}\). Direct linear algebra shows the affine dimension of this eight-point set is four: the five vectors with codes \(0,1,6,10,14\) are affinely independent. (The exact list and independence are verified by the standalone checker.)

Every subset \(S\) of these eight types can appear as the set of immediate successor types of a new root: make a disjoint copy of a finite depth-one realizing tree for each chosen type, and give the new root one outgoing edge to each copy. The root's own truth value of \(p\) is independently chosen. By Part 3, the depth-two theory is thus determined precisely by the root truth value and the affine hull of \(S\). To count these hulls without any assumption of a particular model size, enumerate all \(2^8=256\) subsets \(S\), and construct the affine span as follows: if \(S=\varnothing\) output an empty symbol; otherwise take the least binary code \(s\in S\), compute the span of the binary differences \(\{x\oplus s:x\in S\}\), and output its translate by \(s\). Identify hulls by their exact sixteen-bit membership masks. The executable independent checker contains this exhaustive, finite specification and yields exactly \(96\) distinct masks, classified by affine dimension as \(1\) empty, \(8\) dimension-zero, \(28\) dimension-one, \(38\) dimension-two, \(20\) dimension-three, and \(1\) dimension-four. A separate enumeration by every affine linear existential test verifies that precisely the same \(96\) classes result. Each hull and both root valuations occur, giving \(2\cdot96=192\) distinct depth-two theories. The displayed affine-independent five successor types give a root requiring five successors, while Parts 3–4 show five always suffice. This is a finite exhaustive calculation with an explicit reconstructible algorithm, not an extrapolation from sampled larger frames.

**6. Depth-one counts for several variables.** The affine flats in \(\mathbb F_2^n\) comprise the empty set and, for each dimension \(r\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), \(2^{n-r}{n\brack r}_2\) translates of an \(r\)-dimensional vector subspace; \({n\brack r}_2\) is the binary Gaussian binomial coefficient. The root valuation may be any of \(2^n\) assignments independent of its successors. Hence the exact number is
\[
2^n\left(1+\sum_{r=0}^{n}2^{n-r}{n\brack r}_2\right).
\]
For \(n=1,2,3\), this evaluates to \(8,48,416\). A finite enumeration of all subsets of \(\mathbb F_2^n\) independently verifies these values.

## Verification
Run `python3 check.py` using Python 3.8 or later. The script computes the affine hulls from the exact list of all eight depth-one one-variable types; enumerates all 256 subsets and their sixteen-bit hull masks; independently compares the outcomes of all 32 affine existential tests on every subset; verifies the six dimension-class counts and the five-point independence witness; and directly evaluates 12,288 rooted instances from all 512 binary relations on three labelled worlds and all eight one-variable valuations. It checks that the affine-hull invariant and the full set of depth-two modalized affine tests coincide on those instances. It also enumerates the depth-one counts for one, two and three variables. Expected terminal output: `AFFINE_DEPTH_VERIFY_OK`. The unrestricted theorem is certified by Parts 1–4 of the proof; finite tests alone do not prove it.

## Relationship to prior work
Bezhanishvili, ten Cate, Ganguly, and Meier's *Modal Fragments* (2026, arXiv:2603.05055) introduces the simple-fragment framework and explicitly raises the problem of basis-sensitive bisimulation or model-comparison notions. The present result treats the particular affine Boolean basis and arbitrary one-relation \(\mathsf K\) frames at each finite modal depth. It is not a uniform solution for all Boolean clones. Krebs and Meier's *When Symmetry Yields NP-Hardness* (2025, arXiv:2512.17378) studies satisfiability of multi-agent affine modal logic on S5 and does not furnish this unrestricted-frame depth-two type census. An earlier single-agent S5 affine-hull classification gives a useful special-case comparison, but its equivalence-class accessibility collapses nesting and neither implies nor counts the 192 depth-two unrestricted-frame types.

## Limitations
All finite-depth assertions are relative to the fixed propositional vocabulary and affine Boolean basis. The successor reduction need not preserve arbitrary Boolean connectives, e.g. conjunction, or formulas with unbounded nesting depth. The numerical census \(192\) is only for one propositional variable and depth two on arbitrary Kripke frames. No claim of global bisimulation characterization for all Boolean clones, first-order equivalence, effective polynomial-time construction, or priority relative to every inaccessible historical source is made. The finite checker supplies a complete finite census and sampled model-semantic checks; the structural theorem rests on the displayed proof. A potential earlier equivalent finite-depth affine invariant under different terminology remains a literature risk.
