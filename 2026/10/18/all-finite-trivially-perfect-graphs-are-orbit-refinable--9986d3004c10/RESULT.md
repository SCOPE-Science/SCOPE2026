# All finite trivially perfect graphs are orbit-refinable

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite simple undirected trivially perfect graph, including disconnected graphs and the empty graph. Start ordinary one-dimensional counting colour refinement from the uniform vertex colouring; in each round, replace each colour by the ordered pair consisting of the old colour and the multiset of neighbour colours. **Then the stable colour classes are exactly the vertex orbits of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\).** Equivalently, every finite \((P_4,C_4)\)-free graph is *refinable* in the sense of Arvind--Köbler--Rattan--Verbitsky [1]. No auxiliary marked vertices are needed.

This statement concerns automorphism-orbit recovery *inside the graph*, not identification against every arbitrary nonisomorphic graph. For example, \(2K_3\) is trivially perfect, while \(C_6\) is not; both are six-vertex 2-regular graphs and are therefore indistinguishable from each other by uniform counting colour refinement. Thus the theorem does not claim all trivially perfect graphs are *amenable*.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs here are finite, simple, undirected and uncoloured. A graph is trivially perfect if every connected induced subgraph has a universal vertex; equivalently it has no induced four-vertex path or induced four-cycle [2,3]. A vertex is universal in a connected graph if it is adjacent to every other vertex. Empty graphs are handled vacuously. A colouring is equitable when vertices in one cell have the same number of neighbours in each cell. The stable output of counting colour refinement is the coarsest equitable partition refining the initial colouring. Automorphisms preserve every round, so their orbit partition always refines the stable colouring.

## Proof
We prove the stronger statement that the stable partition of the disjoint union of any finite family of trivially perfect graphs is the orbit partition, by induction on the greatest order \(m\) of a connected component. The family can equivalently be represented by its disjoint-union graph.

**Component-order signal.** In a trivially perfect connected component \(Q\) of order \(s\), at least one vertex \(u\) is universal and has degree \(s-1\). Every vertex \(v\in Q\) either is universal or is adjacent to such a vertex. Consequently
\[
  s=1+\max\{\deg_G(w):w\in N_G[v]\},
\]
where \(N_G[v]\) is the closed neighbourhood. A colour after two rounds determines the initial degrees of the vertex and all its neighbours, and therefore determines \(s\). Hence the stable partition separates vertices belonging to connected components of different orders.

The case \(m=0\) is empty. Assume the statement for every trivially perfect graph all of whose connected components have fewer than \(m\) vertices. Fix \(G\) with largest component order \(m\), and let \(P\) be its stable colour partition. The vertices lying in components of order smaller than \(m\) form a union of \(P\)-cells by the component-order signal. The restriction of an equitable partition to a union of its cells is equitable in the induced graph. By induction the restriction of \(P\) to those smaller components is finer than their automorphism-orbit partition. In particular, equal \(P\)-colours on those components imply a full-graph automorphism relating their vertices. Vertices in smaller components cannot share a \(P\)-colour with vertices in larger ones. It remains to handle the subgraph \(H\) formed by all components of order exactly \(m\).

Index the connected components of \(H\) by \(i\), and write \(H_i=K_{t_i}\vee R_i\), where \(U_i\), of size \(t_i\ge1\), is the set of *all* universal vertices of \(H_i\), and \(R_i=H_i-U_i\). Each \(R_i\) is a (possibly empty) disjoint union of trivially perfect connected components of order less than \(m\). Universals are true twins, so all of \(U_i\) have one stable colour, denoted \(r_i\). Within \(H\), the universal vertices have degree \(m-1\), while the remaining vertices have strictly smaller degree. Therefore \(U=\bigcup_i U_i\) and its complement \(R=\bigsqcup_iR_i\) are unions of \(P\)-cells.

The restriction \(P|_R\) is equitable on \(R\), hence it refines the coarsest equitable partition of \(R\). By the induction hypothesis for \(R\), this latter partition is the \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\)-orbit partition. Accordingly **equal \(P\)-colours within \(R\) imply membership in the same \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\)-orbit**, and every \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\)-orbit is a union of \(P|_R\)-cells.

Suppose \(r_i=r_j\). Equitability gives the same number of neighbours in \(U\) to any \(u\in U_i\) and \(u'\in U_j\); those numbers are \(t_i-1\) and \(t_j-1\), so \(t_i=t_j\). Moreover these two vertices have equal numbers of neighbours in every \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\)-orbit: each orbit is a union of stable cells. A universal vertex of \(H_i\) is adjacent to all of \(R_i\), and to no vertices of \(R_j\) for \(j\ne i\). Thus \(R_i\) and \(R_j\) have equal numbers of vertices in every orbit of \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\). Such an orbit is supported on connected components of one particular isomorphism type and one vertex role therein. Selecting one orbit of each connected-component type shows that \(R_i\) and \(R_j\) contain the same number of components of every type. Thus \(R_i\cong R_j\) and consequently \(H_i\cong H_j\).

If two vertices of \(U\) share their \(P\)-colour, they have the same root colour \(r_i\); the preceding paragraph gives an isomorphism between their parent components. It can be chosen to send the first universal vertex to the second and extended to an automorphism of \(G\), by exchanging the two parent components (or acting inside one component).

Finally suppose \(x\in R_i\) and \(y\in R_j\) share a stable \(P\)-colour. Equitability with respect to \(U\) shows that the multisets of neighbouring universal-vertex colours coincide. Each of those multisets consists of \(t_i\) copies of \(r_i\) or \(t_j\) copies of \(r_j\), respectively, so \(r_i=r_j\) and \(t_i=t_j\). As just shown, \(R_i\cong R_j\). Also, by induction applied to \(R\), the vertices \(x,y\) belong to the same \(\operatorname{Aut}(R)\)-orbit. They therefore occupy corresponding pointed isomorphism types of connected components of \(R\). Because \(R_i,R_j\) contain the same multiplicities of all component types, we can choose an isomorphism \(R_i\to R_j\) that takes \(x\) to \(y\), pair all remaining components type by type, and adjoin an arbitrary bijection \(U_i\to U_j\). This gives an isomorphism \(H_i\to H_j\) mapping \(x\) to \(y\), which extends to an automorphism of \(G\). Thus equal stable colours always imply membership in the same full-graph automorphism orbit. The reverse implication holds for all graphs by equivariance of refinement. The induction is complete. \(\square\)

## Verification
The proof is valid for every finite order and requires no exhaustive computation. As finite checks, `check_forest.py` constructs every canonical unlabelled rooted forest on one through eleven vertices, forms its ancestor-comparability graph, runs refinement, and tests pointed graph isomorphism for representatives within each colour class; its results are in `check_forest.out`. The tested rooted-forest counts are \(1,2,4,9,20,48,115,286,719,1842,4766\), and 39,884 vertex-pair orbit checks pass. The rooted-forest encoding is complete by recursive splitting into a lexicographically sorted multiset of recursively encoded rooted trees; each connected trivially perfect graph has such an ancestor-closure representation [2].

Separately `check_atlas.py` selects all \((P_4,C_4)\)-free graphs from NetworkX's graph atlas through seven vertices, uses an independent four-subset forbidden-graph predicate, and tests pointed graph isomorphism for each stable cell. Its counts \(1,2,4,9,20,48,115\) and 634 orbit checks agree. `check_nonamenable.py` verifies the graph-identification limitation \(2K_3\) versus \(C_6\). All scripts depend only on Python 3 and NetworkX 3.6 (or compatible versions). Run from their directory using `python check_forest.py`, `python check_atlas.py`, and `python check_nonamenable.py`; the corresponding `.out` files give exact observed output.

## Relationship to prior work
Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky [1] define the refinable property and prove the hierarchy from amenability through compactness to refinability. Their general hierarchy does not in itself establish refinability of all trivially perfect graphs. Rubio-Montiel [2] studies pseudo-Grundy colourings and the forbidden-subgraph characterization of trivially perfect graphs, not iterative counting colour refinement. Tinhofer [4] proves a Birkhoff property for **strong tree-cographs**, a specifically restricted subclass in which components at disjoint-union operations are pairwise nonisomorphic [5]; it does not directly cover disjoint unions of repeated complete components, such as \(2K_3\). The present argument handles all trivially perfect graphs, including repeated components. No exact theorem covering this all-graph-family claim was located in the inspected sources; a priority risk from earlier specialized results remains.

## Limitations
The claim is relative to **uniform-start, one-dimensional counting colour refinement** and concerns **orbit recovery inside a graph**. It is not an amenability theorem, not a claim about arbitrary cographs, not a complexity or iteration-depth bound, and not a certification of publication priority. The finite experiments do not establish the infinite theorem; its proof is the induction above. The full text of Tinhofer's earlier paper was not accessible for inspection, so its possible indirect implications remain a literature risk.
