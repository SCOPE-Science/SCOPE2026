# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The row-and-column occupancy equations are exact. The omitted-cell bounds prove the rigidity of the low-capacity optimum. When capacity is larger, all minimum sets are forced into either full-column arrangements or functions with minimum occupied-fibre sizes. The lower bounds, constructions, complete case split and the count of permutation-matrix intersections are proved. Direct exhaustive graph-neighborhood testing matches the theorem on ten labelled boards and 49 parameter cases; this is a check, not a substitute for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** The motivating 2026 Cartesian-product work proves a particular square-rook minimum cardinality, not a full classification and count of all optimum vertex subsets for arbitrary board dimensions and capacities. The foundational 2026 paper addresses general bounds and other graph families. Standard rook-graph domination-polynomial enumeration does not apply a per-selected-vertex capacity restriction. Known numerical optimum formulas and two-row distributions are explicitly excluded from the novelty assertion. Searches for equivalent row/column-load formulations and related published results did not reveal a theorem implying the new fibre-size enumeration. Some bibliographic coverage remains uncertain.

**Value — PASS.** The exact number and structure of minimum placements is intrinsic to capacity-constrained domination, and changes substantially at the transition between full-column solutions and one-per-column solutions. For instance, one parameter boundary admits exactly 18 optimal arrangements in two distinct structural families. The classification covers infinitely many board sizes and capacities and provides explicit, computable optimal-set multiplicities rather than a routine single numerical optimum.

**Limits.** The theorem concerns only rook graphs, counts only optimum sets, and has not received independent validation. The proof, not the finite tests, supports unbounded parameters. Bibliographic priority remains subject to further literature review.
