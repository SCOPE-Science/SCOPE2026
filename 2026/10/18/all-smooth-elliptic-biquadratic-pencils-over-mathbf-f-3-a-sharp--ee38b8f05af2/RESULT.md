# All-smooth elliptic biquadratic pencils over \(\mathbf F_3\): a sharp rational base-locus bound

## Finding
Let \(S=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbf F_3}\times\mathbb P^1_{\mathbf F_3}\). Among the two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_3\)-linear subspaces \(W\subset H^0(S,\mathcal O(2,2))\) whose four \(\mathbf F_3\)-rational projective members are **all geometrically smooth**, precisely \(599112\) occur. Their rational common-base-point count \(b(W)\) has the following exact distribution:

| \(b(W)\) | Pencils |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 115848 |
| 1 | 306432 |
| 2 | 153792 |
| 3 | 23040 |
| at least 4 | 0 |
| **Total** | **599112** |

Consequently the bound \(b(W)\le3\) is sharp. These are counts of linear pencils, not orbits under surface automorphisms. No assertion is made about pencils over other fields or a different bidegree.

## Assumptions and scope
Write a section as \(F(U,V;S,T)=\sum_{i,j=0}^2 c_{ij}a_i(U,V)a_j(S,T)\), where \((a_0,a_1,a_2)=(u^2,uv,v^2)\), with coefficients \(c_{ij}\in\mathbf F_3\). The section space has dimension nine; its nonzero sections modulo scalars number \(9841\). A pencil is a two-dimensional vector subspace, hence has exactly four rational projective members, represented by \(F,G,F+G,F+2G\) for any independent basis. “Smooth” means geometrically smooth over an algebraic closure, not merely free of rational singularities. The base count refers only to the \(16\) rational points of \(S\).

## Proof
**1. Exact smoothness.** Regard \(F\) as \(A(U,V)S^2+B(U,V)ST+C(U,V)T^2\). In characteristic three its branch polynomial is the homogeneous binary quartic \(\Delta=B^2-4AC=B^2-AC\). The curve is geometrically smooth if and only if \(\Delta\) is squarefree. Indeed, a squarefree degree-four branch divisor yields a finite separable double cover of \(\mathbb P^1\), smooth of genus one by Riemann–Hurwitz. Conversely a geometrically smooth divisor of bidegree \((2,2)\) has a finite separable degree-two projection whose branch divisor has four simple geometric points in odd characteristic. The program `pencil22.cpp` checks squarefreeness by the Euclidean algorithm, including roots at infinity. An independent program `groebner_check22.py` checks that the full Jacobian ideal is the unit ideal on all four standard affine charts. Both methods select exactly \(5184\) geometrically smooth projective sections among \(9841\).

**2. Enumerating every pencil.** Iterate all unordered pairs of distinct geometrically smooth projective sections \(\{F,G\}\). Retain the pair precisely if both remaining rational members, \(F+G\) and \(F+2G\), are geometrically smooth (scalar-normalize each member before looking it up). The finite enumeration finds \(3594672\) qualifying unordered generator pairs. Every pencil has four projective members and exactly \(\binom42=6\) unordered generator pairs, so there are \(3594672/6=599112\) pencils. For each pair compute the intersection of the two sixteen-bit rational zero masks; it is the common base locus of the entire pencil. The pair-count distribution, in order \(b=0,1,2,3\), is \(695088,1838592,922752,138240\); dividing by six gives the table above.

The second program `rref_pencils.cpp` enumerates all \(8069620\) two-dimensional subspaces in unique reduced-row-echelon form, independently using the smooth section identifiers generated by the Gröbner-basis test rather than the discriminant test. It reproduces exactly the four counts in the table and has no surviving pencil with at least four rational common points. Unique RREF enumeration proves that this calculation exhausts the Grassmannian without relying on pair-multiplicity bookkeeping.

**3. Geometric incidence and sharpness.** For each \(P\in S(\mathbf F_3)\), evaluation on \(W\) is a linear functional. If this functional is zero, all four projective members contain \(P\); otherwise exactly one does. Thus
\[
\sum_{[H]\in\mathbb P(W)(\mathbf F_3)}\#V(H)(\mathbf F_3)=16+3b(W).
\]
Every smooth member has genus one, so Hasse’s bound gives at most seven rational points for each member, and hence \(b(W)\le4\). Equality would force all four members to have seven rational points. The exhaustive enumeration contains no such pencil: this follows from the complete joint member-count records in `pencil22.out`, independently from the RREF distribution. Therefore \(b(W)\le3\).

A witness attaining equality is generated by
\[
F=U^2S^2+2UVS^2+2V^2S^2+UVT^2,\qquad
G=U^2ST+UVS^2+2V^2S^2+V^2T^2.
\]
The four members \(F,G,F+G,F+2G\) have respectively \(6,7,5,7\) rational points, and their common rational points are exactly
\[
([1:0],[0:1]),\quad([1:1],[1:2]),\quad([1:2],[1:1]).
\]
Direct projective-chart Gröbner-basis checks in `witness_check.py` verify that all four members are geometrically smooth. This establishes attainability independently of the aggregate counting argument.

## Verification
All counts are exact integer calculations over finite coefficient spaces. `groebner_check22.py` generates `groebner_smooth_codes.txt` using a full Jacobian ideal test. `pencil22.cpp` independently constructs smooth forms using the discriminant and enumerates pairs. `rref_pencils.cpp` enumerates RREF pencils from the independently generated smooth identifiers. `witness_check.py` validates the displayed example. The outputs `pencil22.out`, `rref_pencils.out`, and `witness.out` contain the complete tabulated results and example-point checks. Build C++ programs with `g++ -std=c++17 -O3`, run `python groebner_check22.py`, `./pencil22`, `./rref_pencils groebner_smooth_codes.txt`, then `python witness_check.py`. The projectivization and the sixfold pair count are explicitly treated in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (first public preprint 2022-03-14, arXiv:2203.07169), investigate smoothness of rational members of pencils and existence phenomena. Their general results motivate requiring every rational member smooth but do not give this exact bidegree-\((2,2)\) pencil count or the rational common-base-point distribution on the split quadric over \(\mathbf F_3\). Their earlier *A Bertini type theorem for pencils over finite fields* (arXiv:2004.03479) concerns Bertini-type existence for pencils, not the claimed complete small-field incidence distribution. The present enumeration is a separate finite exact classification; its novelty is not inferred merely from different terminology.

## Limitations
The numerical classification is field- and bidegree-specific. It counts literal pencils, not equivalence classes under automorphisms or abstract genus-one isomorphism. The upper bound for other finite fields is not addressed. Algebraic smoothness and completeness depend on the supplied exact computational implementations; the independent RREF enumeration and Jacobian tests reduce but do not eliminate implementation-risk considerations. Earlier unpublished or unindexed classifications might duplicate this distribution, and no independent external audit has been performed.
