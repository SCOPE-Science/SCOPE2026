# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof establishes an if-and-only-if classification of all nonempty color classes using a vertex outside the missing matching on each side. It then obtains an exact falling-factorial color-count polynomial, a sharp minimum by minimizing the odd-block counts, and the complete equality count. The direct graph-based checker enumerates all proper canonical color partitions for the stated small profiles and matches every predicted coefficient and optimizer count. The infinite-family result rests on the structural proof, not on this finite evidence.

**Originality — PASS (best knowledge).** The accessible primary full texts of Kwon–Park (arXiv:2401.11653), Caro–Petruševski–Škrekovski–Tuza (arXiv:2410.02336), and the 2025 odd-independence paper (arXiv:2509.20763) were consulted in the portions relevant to definitions, results and implications, and compared with the explicit claim. Further public scientific results on odd crowns and complete multipartite odd independence were read in full. The closest odd-crown work has a *spanning* deleted matching, while the complete multipartite work has no matching deletion; neither states the all-color polynomial for partial matching deletions. Broad existence/bound results do not entail the partition histogram or its exact optimal coefficients. Residual risk: an unindexed or differently named equivalent enumeration may exist.

**Value — PASS.** This is an exact natural coloring distribution for a dense, structured three-parameter family, not simply a restatement of a known minimum. The odd-block partition decomposition explains every color count and the optimal multiplicity. It identifies a particularly transparent, bidirectional edge-deletion sensitivity: one deleted edge lowers the invariant from four to three for even-even bipartitions but raises it from two to five for odd-odd bipartitions. The spanning-matching boundary is explicitly excluded rather than guessed.

No independent mathematical audit was performed. The computational experiments are finite corroboration; unindexed prior work remains a bibliographic risk.
