# Almost-complete cyclic phase retrieval for binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal B_5\) be the set of binary de Bruijn cycles of order five, identified only under cyclic rotation. Every \(B\in\mathcal B_5\) has \(32\) entries and contains each binary five-word exactly once cyclically; \(|\mathcal B_5|=2048\). Write \(x_j=2b_j-1\in\{-1,1\}\), with indices modulo \(32\), and define the unnormalized periodic autocorrelation
\[
R_B(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{31}x_jx_{j+t}\quad(0\le t\le31).
\]
**The complete cyclic phase-retrieval classification is as follows.** There are exactly \(526\) distinct autocorrelation functions. Their fiber-size distribution on the \(2048\) rotation classes is: \(30\) functions each from \(2\) cycles, \(495\) each from \(4\) cycles, and \(1\) from \(8\) cycles. Under reversal and bit complementation, the cycles form \(528\) orbits: \(32\) of size \(2\) and \(496\) of size \(4\). All but exactly **two** autocorrelation fibers consist of one such orbit. The two exceptional fibers consist, respectively, of two different size-two orbits (four cycles) and two different size-four orbits (eight cycles). Thus precisely two unordered pairs of inequivalent reversal/complement orbits are homometric.

Even the complete circular power spectrum cannot separate those two pairs. Conversely, among this family, the autocorrelations at lags \(1,\dots,13\) determine the entire autocorrelation function: there are \(526\) distinct truncated signatures through lag \(13\), but only \(521\) through lag \(12\). Since \(R_B(1)=\cdots=R_B(4)=0\) for all members, just the **nine** lags \(5,\dots,13\) suffice to recover the full power spectrum; lag \(13\) is necessary when observing an initial interval of lags.

Canonical representatives of the exceptional classes appear below. Each is rooted at its unique occurrence of `00000`; within each reversal/complement orbit, the lexicographically least such rooted word is displayed.

## Assumptions and scope

Only binary de Bruijn cycles of order exactly five are considered. Rotation is factored out from the beginning. Reversal and bit complementation are additional equivalences used to define the natural reconstruction ambiguity, not to reduce the initial \(2048\)-cycle population. Two cycles are called homometric if their full unnormalized periodic autocorrelations agree at every lag. All counts concern distinct cycles, not distinct rooted positions, unless explicitly described otherwise. All formulas and computations use exact integers; the theorem does not claim phase retrieval for arbitrary binary length-\(32\) words or other de Bruijn orders.

Let \(A_B=\{j:b_j=1\}\subset\mathbb Z/32\mathbb Z\). Each de Bruijn cycle is balanced, so \(|A_B|=16\). The cyclic interval vector \(c_B(t)=|A_B\cap(A_B-t)|\) obeys
\[
R_B(t)=32-4|A_B|+4c_B(t)=4c_B(t)-32.
\]
Thus homometry here is equivalent to equality of all cyclic difference multiplicities and, via the elementary discrete Wiener--Khinchin identity, to equality of the magnitudes squared of all \(32\) discrete Fourier coefficients. This also makes the result relevant to cyclic reconstruction from pair correlations.

## Proof

**Structural reductions.** Rotation preserves all \(R_B(t)\). Reversal exchanges the summation indices and preserves \(R_B(t)\), and complementing every bit negates all \(x_j\), again preserving \(R_B(t)\). Also \(R_B(t)=R_B(32-t)\), so \(R_B(1),\ldots,R_B(16)\) together with the fixed \(R_B(0)=32\) determine the full profile. For \(1\le t\le4\), each ordered binary pair \((b_j,b_{j+t})\) occurs eight times: these are the two designated coordinates within the \(32\) different five-bit factors. The four signed products therefore cancel and \(R_B(t)=0\).

**Complete cycle generation.** Consider the directed binary de Bruijn graph with vertices the \(16\) four-bit words and edges the \(32\) five-bit words. A five-bit edge \(e\in\{0,\ldots,31\}\) has source \(\lfloor e/2\rfloor\), target \(e\bmod16\), and label \(e\bmod2\). A binary order-five de Bruijn cycle is precisely an Eulerian circuit traversing each edge once. Every such circuit contains the edge `00000` exactly once, so anchoring that edge eliminates all rotational duplications. The first generator depth-first searches every unused-edge continuation after the anchored edge and accepts exactly the closed 32-edge tours. It returns \(2048\) different canonical cycles.

A second generator works in a different finite search space. At each four-bit vertex there are two incoming and two outgoing edges; an Euler circuit induces a bijection from incoming to outgoing edges, with exactly two possibilities. There are therefore \(2^{16}=65536\) full local-pairing assignments. Each induces a permutation of the \(32\) directed edges. It describes one Eulerian circuit exactly if this permutation consists of one cycle of length \(32\). The second generator examines every assignment, selecting exactly the same \(2048\) canonical cycles. The converse is exact: the incoming-to-outgoing successor rule of any anchored Eulerian circuit is a unique pairing assignment. Thus neither generator omits a compliant cycle.

**Exact autocorrelation classification.** The checker independently computes each \(16\)-entry signature by (i) direct signed-product sums, (ii) the ones-set difference-intersection formula \(R_B(t)=4c_B(t)-32\), and (iii) popcounts of cyclically shifted bitmasks. All three methods agree on every lag for each of the \(2048\) cycles. Grouping equal vectors gives \(526\) groups with sizes \(30\) groups of size \(2\), \(495\) of size \(4\), and one of size \(8\); the checksum \(30\cdot2+495\cdot4+8=2048\) holds.

For every canonical cycle, apply reversal and complementation, re-root at the unique five-zero factor, and list the resulting canonical words. This partitions the \(2048\) cycles into \(32\) orbits of size \(2\) and \(496\) of size \(4\). Sorting the orbit representatives within each autocorrelation group yields exactly \(30\) size-two groups containing one orbit, \(494\) size-four groups containing one orbit, one size-four group containing two size-two orbits, and one size-eight group containing two size-four orbits. Those last two groups have exactly the representatives displayed above. This establishes the complete two-pair exception statement, not just the existence of examples.

**Sharp lag cutoff.** Independently partitioning by prefixes of the autocorrelation vector gives \(521\) classes through lag \(12\) and \(526\) through lag \(13\), with no additional distinctions through lag \(16\). For a concrete strictness witness, the cycles `00000111010111110010100110110001` and `00000110010011100010110111110101` share all lags \(1\) through \(12\), but have \(R(13)=4\) and \(R(13)=0\), respectively. Consequently lag \(13\) is necessary for an initial consecutive-lag prefix to recover the full vector.

Each finite count in the preceding two paragraphs is an exhaustively proved count: the graph arguments prove domain completeness, all loop bounds are explicit, and the executable verifier rebuilds the domain and checks every indicated equality without relying on the supplied tables as inputs.

## Relationship to prior work

Spinsante, Andrenacci, and Gambi (2011) study the autocorrelation and cross-correlation properties of de Bruijn spreading sequences, including exhaustive order-five statistics, mean profiles, and peak-sidelobe subfamilies. Their full article discusses the same \(2048\) rotation classes and the importance of correlation variability. Their published tables of peaks and aggregate statistics do not classify equality of the complete autocorrelation functions, the two nontrivial homometric pairs, or the short-lag reconstruction threshold.

Mandereau, Ghisi, Amiot, Andreatta, and Agon (2011) formulate cyclic homometry and Patterson functions in a general group-theoretic setting. Their preprint treats interval vectors, inversion, transposition, and quotient transfers, but it does not identify the de Bruijn-restricted \(32\)-point family or provide its \(526\)-fiber census. Rosenblatt and Seymour (1982) give a general structural framework for homometric finite sets; the publicly accessible abstract supplies no specialization to this exact binary de Bruijn family. These general formulations explain the invariant but do not themselves imply the numerical finite classification.

A scalar autocorrelation statistic such as total sidelobe energy or largest sidelobe does not determine the full lag profile and cannot by itself settle which cycles are homometric. The present classification concerns the *entire vector* and its reconstruction fibers, not a new name for scalar performance data.

## Limitations

The result is an exact finite theorem for order five, not a general rigidity theorem for every order. The two exceptional pairs prove that cyclic autocorrelation is not a universally injective invariant even after quotienting by rotation, reversal, and complementation. Nothing here characterizes phase-retrieval fibers of arbitrary balanced \(32\)-bit words. All verification is finite and reproducible, but no independent scientific audit is asserted. Although the inspected primary material and searches revealed no previous order-five homometry classification, older unindexed data, unpublished enumerations, and the inaccessible full text of some general homometry treatments remain historical-priority risks.
