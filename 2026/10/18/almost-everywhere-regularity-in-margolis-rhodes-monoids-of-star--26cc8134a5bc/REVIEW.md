# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The defining inverse-simplex condition forces any repeated fibre to be an edge containing the star centre. Partitioning by whether this fibre lands at the centre or a leaf produces an exhaustive count. Injective maps are classified by the induced-image edge condition. A section criterion makes regularity equivalent to continuous selection of one representative per fibre; it proves every singular map regular and isolates the unique family of nonregular injections. The idempotent fixed-point requirement and labelled-rank counts are exhaustively reconciled. The sharp asymptotic follows from the ratio of consecutive rook-deficiency weights and an exact expectation identity, without requiring finite extrapolation. The bundled program exhaustively checks stars with one through five leaves, including regularity via independent section enumeration.

## Originality: PASS (self-review)

Margolis–Rhodes (arXiv:2609.39370) establishes the general graph-monoid framework and that a star monoid is not regular as a whole, but its inspected sections develop explicit path/cycle enumerations and do not supply the exact individual-regularity classification, star count, rank distribution or square-root asymptotic here. Their earlier arXiv:2005.02606 provides the general continuity definition rather than the star census. Targeted public searches and published-finding corpus queries for star monoids, partial continuous star maps, regular-element counts, rank profiles and rook-number asymptotics identified no directly covering finding. The general decomposition strongly motivates the calculation; prior implicit specialization or less-indexed coverage remains a material bibliographic risk.

## Value: PASS

This gives a complete, parameter-uniform enumeration and regularity stratification for the basic nonpath tree family. More importantly, it exhibits an exact sharp contrast between the *global* failure of regularity and *typical-element* regularity: both the nonregular and injective fractions decay like the same inverse square root, while the intersection of regular and singular elements becomes overwhelming. This is a natural statistic for the newly studied graph monoids, not an arbitrary finite example.

## Scope and residual risks

Results are specific to labelled stars, not general graphs. Published definitions and general criteria are credited, with no novelty claim for them. Searches do not establish uniqueness; the 2023 complexity-examples paper was located at abstract/metadata level only, though its described emphasis is complexity rather than star enumeration. The sole proof of the infinite result is mathematical; finite checks serve as independent computational corroboration of small cases.
