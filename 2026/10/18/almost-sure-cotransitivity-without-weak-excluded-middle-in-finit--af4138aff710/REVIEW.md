# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain is the up-set Heyting algebra of a rooted star with \(m\geq1\) maximal leaves. The critical lemma computes negated biconditional solely from maximal traces, taking explicit care of the two distinct elements with full maximal trace. The cotransitivity failure classification is an if-and-only-if statement and partitions complementary trace pairs into ordinary and endpoint cases without double counting. The count is \((2^m-2)(2^m+3)\), not merely a lower bound. Separate direct computations establish the WEM count. The numerical verifier reconstructs implication by the residuation universal property and exhaustively checks the values for \(m\leq7\), with \(2{,}463{,}045\) triples. The infinite-family conclusion is proved by the closed formula; enumeration alone would be insufficient.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Kocsis (arXiv:2209.03920), Proposition 3.7 and Theorem 4.8, provides the qualitative equivalence of global cotransitivity and weak excluded middle, not their two frequencies. Bumpus–Kocsis (arXiv:2110.11515), Section 3 and Theorem 3.15, studies satisfiability gaps and uses the identical Boolean-plus-top family for material implication, not cotransitivity. Therefore using the same family is not presented as original. An accessible pre-existing general WEM/DNE spectrum already implies the WEM subcount on this frame, and that known part is not presented as independent novelty. The new statement is the exact *cotransitivity* triple count and its quantitative contrast with the WEM behavior. Subject searches for apartness/cotransitivity degrees and semantic research-record searches did not identify covering work. Risks remain from unindexed alternative terminology and unpublished work; search absence alone is not novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural question arising from the classification of definable apartness is whether approximate cotransitivity quantitatively indicates approximate weak excluded middle. The theorem provides an exact negative answer, in which the former probability tends to one and the latter tends to zero. This distinguishes a qualitative equational equivalence from probabilistic stability, gives a sharp rational benchmark on an infinite family, and makes explicit why rare complementary pairs rather than general WEM failures drive cotransitivity. It is not claimed as a classification of all Heyting algebras.
