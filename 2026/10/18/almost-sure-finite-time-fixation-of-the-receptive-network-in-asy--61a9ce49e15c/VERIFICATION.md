# Verification and scientific limits

The key two-coordinate identity was expanded exactly: a receptive compromise decreases the squared-opinion sum by \(2\mu(1-\mu)(x_i-x_j)^2\), which is nonnegative under the specified parameter interval. For each cardinality, the smallest-subset sum is monotone because every updated subset sum is a convex combination of two previous subset sums. These two invariants imply convergence of every labeled opinion, without reliance on an external convergence assertion.

The conditional selection argument is an exact probability estimate: the probability of avoiding a prescribed edge on the first \(k\) receptive opportunities is at most \((1-q)^k\). The proof does not assume that receptive opportunities are independent of one another.

Edge fixation has been checked in both exhaustive cases of equal and unequal limit opinions. For equal limits, the first receptive meeting after all later opinion gaps become smaller than \(\gamma\) raises confidence to at least \(\gamma\) and makes later edge receptivity permanent. For unequal limits, a late successful meeting would contradict the vanishing increment of a convergent opinion.

No simulation, finite enumeration or source snippet is offered as proof of almost-sure graph fixation. The theorem has no fixation-time tail bound. The boundary \(\delta=1\) is covered for graph fixation but deliberately excluded from the confidence-limit correspondence. Independent verification and publication-priority audit are not performed.
