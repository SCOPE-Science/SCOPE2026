# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The universal inequality is derived directly from the antichain-filter subcubes and a rank partition into exactly \(h\) antichains. Equality forces one nonzero coefficient in each rank, all with equal amplitude; the antichain inequality makes the support totally ordered; and the exact suffix-sum formula forces alternating signs. These are all finite combinatorial identities with no unproved extension from numerical evidence. For a singleton or antichain (\(h=1\)), the directions reduce to the coordinate axes. The signed-chain support formula is exact in arbitrary dimension, and the published enriched-chain facet description gives the contact ratio. The standalone exact-integer script checked 407 naturally labeled relation systems (covering all isomorphism types on at most five elements), with 89,794 ternary-direction comparisons, but it is corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Prior enriched-chain results already establish the radius \(1/\sqrt h\) and all \(2^hL\) signed-chain facet contacts **for the enriched chain polytope**. Combined with the elementary inclusion into the order-polytope difference body, they account for the lower bound and, with an explicit alternating construction, the radius value. This numerical portion is not claimed as original. The compared publications do not give the precise *difference-body* normal classification, the suffix-walk support law, or the exact loss of all nonalternating contacts. Those statements do not follow merely from the enriched body's tangencies, because tangency need not persist on passing to a larger body. Full-text sections on order-polytope triangulations, enriched-chain facet representations, and a recent edge-classification paper were examined. An earlier result using other language could still exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The difference body and the enriched chain polytope are canonical symmetric convex bodies associated with the same poset. Their radii agree, yet the new theorem identifies a rigid and exponentially smaller set of optimal support normals and provides a dimension-independent factor-two separation for all other signed longest-chain normals. The precise contact geometry is a natural, complete equality classification rather than an arbitrary invariant or a simple repetition of the radius. It distinguishes objects that a shared scalar radius cannot distinguish.

## Boundaries
The metric is the standard Euclidean one, and the result classifies normals of a centered maximal ball, not the full set of centers. General affine changes need not preserve the formula. Independently conducted mathematical validation is still absent.
