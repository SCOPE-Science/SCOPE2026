# An angular graph criterion for full-unitary rotation continuity of diagonal Fock operators

## Finding

Fix an integer \(d\ge2\) and \(t>0\). On the Gaussian Bargmann–Fock space \(F_t^2(\mathbb C^d)\), let \(\{e_\alpha:\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^d\}\) be the normalized monomials, and let \(D_a e_\alpha=a_\alpha e_\alpha\) for an arbitrary bounded complex array \(a\). Let \(\Gamma(V)f(z)=f(V^{-1}z)\), for \(V\in U(d)\), so that \(\Gamma\) is a unitary representation (switching the inverse is immaterial to the continuity criterion).

For every pair \(i\ne j\) and every multiindex \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha_j\ge1\), join \(\alpha\) to \(\beta=\alpha+e_i-e_j\) by an undirected edge of length

\[
w_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\alpha_i+1)\alpha_j}}.
\]

For each total degree \(m\), take shortest-path distance within the finite connected graph on \(\{\alpha:|\alpha|=m\}\). Write this distance as \(d_{\mathrm{ang}}\), assigning infinite distance across different degrees.

**Theorem.** The following conditions are equivalent.

1. \(\lim_{V\to I}\|\Gamma(V)D_a\Gamma(V)^*-D_a\|=0\).
2. \(\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\sup\{|a_\alpha-a_\beta|:|\alpha|=|\beta|,\ d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\alpha,\beta)<\delta\}=0\).
3. The array \(a\) is a uniform limit of bounded arrays \(b\) satisfying the finite weighted edge bound
\[
L(b):=\sup_{\substack{i\ne j,\ \alpha_j\ge1}}
\sqrt{(\alpha_i+1)\alpha_j}\,
|b_{\alpha+e_i-e_j}-b_\alpha|<\infty.
\]

This characterizes full-unitary rotational norm continuity *within the complete class of monomial-diagonal bounded operators*. It does not characterize arbitrary, off-diagonal operators.

In dimension \(d=2\), writing \(\alpha=(k,m-k)\) and \(\beta=(\ell,m-\ell)\), the metric is explicitly
\[
d_m(k,\ell)=
\sum_{r=\min(k,\ell)}^{\max(k,\ell)-1}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{(r+1)(m-r)}}.
\]
For \(m\ge1\), set \(\theta_{m,k}=2\arcsin\sqrt{k/m}\). Then
\[
d_m(k,\ell)\le|\theta_{m,k}-\theta_{m,\ell}|
\le\pi\,d_m(k,\ell).
\]

**Separation from the Toeplitz algebra.** For \(d=2\), the bounded-symbol Toeplitz algebra \(\mathcal T(F_t^2(\mathbb C^2))\) and the norm-continuous full-rotation algebra are incomparable even on the monomial-diagonal class. Specifically, the bounded diagonal
\[
a_{k,\ell}=
\begin{cases}
\exp\!\bigl(ik/\sqrt{k+\ell}\bigr),&k+\ell\ge1,\\
1,&k=\ell=0
\end{cases}
\]
is in the Toeplitz algebra but is not full-rotation continuous. In the other direction \(a_{k,\ell}=(-1)^{k+\ell}\) defines a full-rotation-invariant diagonal outside the Toeplitz algebra.

## Assumptions and scope

The Fock-space inner product is defined by the Gaussian measure
\[
(\pi t)^{-d}\exp(-|z|^2/t)\,dV(z).
\]
The normalized monomials are
\[
e_\alpha(z)=\frac{z^\alpha}{\sqrt{t^{|\alpha|}\alpha!}},
\qquad
\alpha!=\prod_{i=1}^d\alpha_i!.
\]
Every array considered as a diagonal is bounded in supremum norm. A graph edge has positive length; each fixed-degree graph is finite and connected. Uniform continuity is global over the *disjoint union* of these graphs, so its modulus is uniform in total degree. The parameter \(t>0\) cancels from the normalization of the rotation Lie algebra.

The Toeplitz algebra refers to the norm-closed algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with bounded measurable symbols on the same Gaussian Fock space. Its monomial-diagonal membership test, used only in the separation examples, is Theorem 3.2 of Fulsche (2026), based on earlier quasi-radial Toeplitz work.

## Proof

**Step 1: the exact coefficient of an infinitesimal coordinate rotation.**
Let \(E_{ij}\) be the elementary \(d\times d\) matrix and define on holomorphic polynomials \(J_{ij}=z_i\partial_{z_j}\). An exact basis calculation, including the normalization, gives
\[
J_{ij}e_\alpha
=\sqrt{(\alpha_i+1)\alpha_j}\,e_{\alpha+e_i-e_j}
\quad(\alpha_j\ge1),
\]
and zero otherwise. Thus for a bounded diagonal \(D_b\),
\[
[J_{ij},D_b]e_\alpha
=\sqrt{(\alpha_i+1)\alpha_j}
(b_\alpha-b_{\alpha+e_i-e_j})e_{\alpha+e_i-e_j}.
\]
The output map is injective on the indices for each fixed ordered pair; therefore its operator norm, whenever finite, is exactly the supremum of the displayed coefficient moduli.

**Step 2: bounded graph slopes imply norm continuity.**
For a skew-Hermitian matrix \(X\), the differential \(d\Gamma(X)\) is a real-linear combination (up to an irrelevant sign) of \(J_{ij}\) and of the diagonal number operators \(J_{ii}\). Its diagonal terms commute with every \(D_b\). If \(L(b)<\infty\), the off-diagonal commutators are bounded, with
\[
\|[d\Gamma(X),D_b]\|
\le C_d L(b)\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\]
for a constant depending only on \(d\). Each homogeneous-degree subspace is finite-dimensional and is invariant under the rotations and \(D_b\). The ordinary finite-dimensional Duhamel formula on every block, with this bound independent of degree, gives
\[
\|\Gamma(e^X)D_b\Gamma(e^X)^*-D_b\|
\le C_d L(b)\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
For \(V\) near \(I\), a skew-Hermitian logarithm \(X\to0\) exists; hence \(D_b\) has norm-continuous full-unitary orbit. The set of such operators is norm closed under uniform limits, proving (3) implies (1).

**Step 3: norm-continuity forces approximation by bounded graph slopes.**
Suppose (1). The \(U(d)\)-conjugation orbit map \(g\mapsto\Gamma(g)D_a\Gamma(g)^*\) is norm continuous on the compact group. Let \(\phi_\varepsilon\) be smooth nonnegative approximate-identity kernels of integral \(1\), and form the Bochner integral
\[
A_\varepsilon=\int_{U(d)}
\phi_\varepsilon(g)\Gamma(g)D_a\Gamma(g)^*\,dg.
\]
Then \(\|A_\varepsilon-D_a\|\to0\), and the conjugation orbit of \(A_\varepsilon\) is smooth in operator norm (differentiate the smooth left translates of the kernel in the Banach-valued integral). Now average \(A_\varepsilon\) over the diagonal torus:
\[
B_\varepsilon
=\int_{\mathbb T^d}
\Gamma(h)A_\varepsilon\Gamma(h)^*\,dh.
\]
The torus expectation has norm \(1\), fixes \(D_a\), and kills all off-diagonal monomial matrix entries. Hence \(B_\varepsilon=D_{b^{(\varepsilon)}}\) is diagonal and
\(\|b^{(\varepsilon)}-a\|_\infty
=\|B_\varepsilon-D_a\|\to0\).
Moreover, \(B_\varepsilon\) has a smooth norm orbit: after conjugating the integrand by \(\Gamma(k)\), move \(\Gamma(h)\) to the outside and observe that the inner group argument is \(h^{-1}kh\), whose infinitesimal derivatives are uniformly bounded as \(h\) ranges over the compact torus. The norm derivative of the orbit in every skew-Hermitian direction is therefore a bounded operator.

In particular, for \(X_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{ji}\), the derivative \([d\Gamma(X_{ij}),B_\varepsilon]\) extends boundedly to the whole space. Its matrix entry from \(e_\alpha\) to \(e_{\alpha+e_i-e_j}\) has modulus
\[
\sqrt{(\alpha_i+1)\alpha_j}\,
|b^{(\varepsilon)}_{\alpha+e_i-e_j}-b^{(\varepsilon)}_\alpha|.
\]
The contribution from \(J_{ji}\) goes to the opposite neighboring basis vector and does not cancel this entry. Each entry is bounded by the norm derivative, uniformly in \(\alpha\). As \(d\) is fixed and only finitely many \(i,j\) occur, \(L(b^{(\varepsilon)})<\infty\). This establishes (1) implies (3).

**Step 4: the graph-geometric equivalence.**
If \(L(b)<\infty\), the difference between \(b\) at two vertices of the same degree is bounded by \(L(b)\) times the total edge length along every connecting path, hence by \(L(b)d_{\mathrm{ang}}\). Uniform limits are uniformly continuous, proving (3) implies (2).

Conversely, assume (2). For any bounded *real-valued* uniformly continuous array \(u\) on the graph, define on each connected degree component
\[
u_L(x)=\inf_{y\text{ in the same degree}}
\bigl(u(y)+L\,d_{\mathrm{ang}}(x,y)\bigr).
\]
By the triangle inequality, \(u_L\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, with \(\inf u\le u_L\le u\).
Given \(\eta>0\), choose \(\delta>0\) such that \(d_{\mathrm{ang}}(x,y)<\delta\) implies \(|u(x)-u(y)|<\eta\). If \(L\delta\ge2\|u\|_\infty\), then terms with distance at least \(\delta\) cannot decrease \(u(x)\); terms at smaller distance decrease it by at most \(\eta\). Consequently \(\|u_L-u\|_\infty\le\eta\), *uniformly over all degrees*. Apply this construction separately to the real and imaginary parts of \(a\); the resulting complex array is graph-Lipschitz and uniformly close to \(a\). This establishes (2) implies (3), and completes the equivalence.

**Step 5: the two-coordinate angular formula.**
For fixed \(m\ge1\), the graph is the path \(0,1,\ldots,m\), and its consecutive edge length is \(((k+1)(m-k))^{-1/2}\). The function
\[
\theta_m(x)=2\arcsin\sqrt{x/m}
\quad (0\le x\le m)
\]
satisfies \(\theta_m'(x)=[x(m-x)]^{-1/2}\) for \(0<x<m\). For \(x=k+s\) with \(0<s<1\),
\[
x(m-x)\le(k+1)(m-k)
\]
and, also,
\[
x(m-x)\ge(k+1)(m-k)\,s(1-s).
\]
Integration over \(s\in(0,1)\) gives
\[
\frac1{\sqrt{(k+1)(m-k)}}
\le\theta_m(k+1)-\theta_m(k)
\le\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{(k+1)(m-k)}}.
\]
Add over the unique path from \(k\) to \(\ell\), giving the asserted metric comparison.

**Step 6: explicit separation.**
On the positive quadrant, let
\[
F(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
x^2/\sqrt{x^2+y^2},&(x,y)\ne(0,0),\\
0,&(x,y)=(0,0).
\end{cases}
\]
It is globally Lipschitz in the ordinary Euclidean metric: away from the origin its partial derivatives are
\[
\partial_xF=\frac{x(x^2+2y^2)}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}},
\qquad
\partial_yF=-\frac{x^2y}{(x^2+y^2)^{3/2}},
\]
which are bounded, and continuity covers the origin. Thus the array \(a_{k,\ell}=\exp(iF(\sqrt k,\sqrt\ell))\) is uniformly continuous for the square-root metric
\[
\rho_2((k,\ell),(k',\ell'))=
|\sqrt k-\sqrt{k'}|+|\sqrt\ell-\sqrt{\ell'}|.
\]
By Fulsche's Theorem 3.2 (diagonal specialization), this operator lies in the bounded-symbol Toeplitz algebra. But take even \(m\), \(k=m/2\) and \(h_m=\lfloor\pi\sqrt m\rfloor\). The graph path from \((k,m-k)\) to \((k+h_m,m-k-h_m)\) has length \(O(m^{-1/2})\), whereas the coefficient ratio is \(\exp(ih_m/\sqrt m)\to-1\). Theorem condition (2) fails, so this Toeplitz diagonal is not full-rotation continuous.

For the converse direction, \(c_{k,\ell}=(-1)^{k+\ell}\) depends only on total degree. Each homogeneous-degree subspace is invariant under the full \(U(2)\) representation, so \(D_c\) commutes with every rotation and is fully rotation-invariant. Nevertheless
\[
\rho_2((m,0),(m+1,0))
=\sqrt{m+1}-\sqrt m\longrightarrow0,
\]
while \(|c_{m,0}-c_{m+1,0}|=2\), violating Fulsche's diagonal Toeplitz criterion. It therefore lies outside the Toeplitz algebra.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` checks the normalized infinitesimal transfer weights against finite-degree basis coefficients, verifies the angular metric inequalities for a range of finite degrees, and evaluates the separating sequences along explicitly chosen increasing degrees. The infinite-dimensional equivalence is proved analytically in Steps 1–6; finite checks are not a proof of the global statement. The assumptions \(d\ge2\), \(t>0\), bounded diagonal arrays, and full \(U(d)\) norm continuity are essential to the stated domain. Independent validation has not been carried out.

## Relationship to prior work

R. Fulsche, *Rotationally-Continuous Operators on the Fock Space* (first publicly posted 11 November 2025, arXiv:2511.08353; 2026 journal version), proves the one-variable rotation/band-dominated correspondence, and in Theorems 3.1–3.2 handles *separate coordinate torus rotations* and the multivariable Toeplitz intersection. Section 3.1, Problem 1, explicitly distinguishes and poses the *full noncommutative unitary group* matrix-characterization problem. The present result gives a complete answer for monomial-diagonal bounded operators, not for its full off-diagonal scope. In particular the earlier torus characterization imposes no restriction on diagonal arrays, whereas the weighted angular metric is nontrivial. The earlier multivariable square-root metric concerns Toeplitz membership rather than norm continuity of the full-unitary orbit.

V. Dewage and G. Ólafsson, *Toeplitz Operators on the Fock Space with Quasi-Radial Symbols* (2022), develop quasi-radial Toeplitz-algebra descriptions via square-root metrics. That result supports the distinction between Toeplitz regularity and the new full-group angular regularity; it does not supply this graph criterion. No published equivalent diagonal full-unitary graph test was found in the checked literature, but this is not a proof of global novelty.

## Limitations

The classification applies only to operators diagonal in the normalized monomial basis, not arbitrary operators with nonzero off-diagonal matrix entries; hence it is a partial solution to the full-unitary matrix problem. The Toeplitz comparison relies explicitly on the published square-root metric characterization and does not classify the entire intersection for arbitrary matrices. No quantitative optimal modulus of continuity for arbitrary input arrays is asserted. General compact-Lie-group smooth-vector techniques are classical; an unlocated earlier formulation of the resulting graph metric remains a bibliographic risk.
