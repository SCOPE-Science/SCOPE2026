# Scientific assessment: facet-stratified random simplex second moments

## Correctness — PASS
The complete analytic argument uses Dirichlet coordinate moments, a precise squarefree coefficient identity for independent but differently distributed columns, and an evaluated rank-two determinant. The coefficient of the repeated-index term vanishes, and the remaining coefficient gives the exact moment in every dimension. The affine determinant normalization, signed first moment, interior-sampling denominator, and open-neighborhood sign argument are explicit. Exact derangement enumeration for dimensions \(2\) through \(7\) agrees with the universal result. The proof, not enumeration, certifies infinitely many dimensions.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest directly matched primary work is Reitzner–Schütt–Werner, Section 3.3: it introduces general random-boundary-simplex moments and facet-conditioned constants without evaluating this distinct-facet squared-volume constant. Rademacher's covariance expression is restricted to identically distributed interior samples, and Reichenwallner's boundary model draws identically from the whole boundary rather than one sample per distinct facet. Turchi–Wespi's smooth-boundary fluctuation theorems do not cover this facet setup. Mathematical-record searches for the proposed formulas and signed derangement identity identified related but non-implicative geometric results. The general mixed-discriminant trick is pre-existing; novelty concerns evaluating the concrete family, its exact interior ratio and sign/combinatorial consequences. An older formulation in stochastic-matrix or geometric-probability language remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a shape-independent, all-dimension second-volume-moment law for a geometrically natural, precisely stratified boundary sampling model. The exact boundary/interior second-moment amplification approaches \(e^2\), giving a benchmark for comparing natural sampling designs in stochastic convex geometry. The determinant-sign threshold and an unusually simple signed derangement-pair sum are structural consequences, not an arbitrary small calculation. The theorem does not claim a first unsigned moment or an entire probability distribution.
