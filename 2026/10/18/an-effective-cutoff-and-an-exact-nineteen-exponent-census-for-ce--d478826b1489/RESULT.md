# An effective cutoff and an exact nineteen-exponent census for centered powers

## Finding
For any integers \(k\ge2\), \(m\ge2\), and \(u,v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), consider
\[
(2m+1)^k=u m^k+v(m+1)^k.\tag{1}
\]
Every solution satisfies
\[
m\le M_k:=\max\!\left(k,\left\lfloor\frac{2^k-2}{k}\right\rfloor\right).\tag{2}
\]
Moreover the complete solution set for \(2\le k\le20\), in the order \((k,m,u,v)\), consists of
\[
(2,2,4,1),\ (4,2,34,1),\ (4,3,17,4),\ (11,2,1093,263),\ (13,2,51118,503).\tag{3}
\]
The cutoff holds for **all** exponents, while (3) is a finite, proved classification only through exponent \(20\).

## Assumptions and scope
All variables are ordinary integers, powers are exact, and \(u,v\) may be zero. The lower bound \(m\ge2\) is essential to the application involving three consecutive integers \((2m,2m+1,2m+2)\). No primality or coprimality hypothesis is imposed on \(m\). The claim concerns the exponential Diophantine equation (1), not all numerical semigroups or all exponents at once.

## Proof
Write \(X=m^k\), \(Y=(m+1)^k\), \(Z=(2m+1)^k\). Since \(Y>0\), (1) and \(u\ge0\) give
\[
0\le v\le \frac{Z}{Y}=\left(\frac{2m+1}{m+1}\right)^k<2^k.\tag{4}
\]
Reducing (1) modulo \(m\), since \(k\ge2\), yields \(v\equiv1\pmod m\), so \(v=1+jm\) for an integer \(j\ge0\). Reducing modulo \(m^2\), the binomial theorem yields
\[
Z\equiv1+2km,\qquad Y\equiv1+km,\qquad uX\equiv0\pmod{m^2}.
\]
Therefore \((1+jm)(1+km)\equiv1+2km\pmod{m^2}\), whence \(j\equiv k\pmod m\). If \(m\le k\), (2) is immediate. Otherwise \(m>k\), so the least nonnegative integer \(j\equiv k\pmod m\) equals \(k\), and in particular \(j\ge k\). By (4), \(1+km\le v\le2^k-1\); thus \(m\le(2^k-2)/k\), proving (2).

For each fixed admissible \(k,m\), since \(\gcd(m^k,(m+1)^k)=1\), (1) forces
\[
v\equiv Z\,Y^{-1}\pmod X.
\]
The bound \(v<2^k\le m^k=X\) selects **exactly one possible** nonnegative \(v\), the least residue. It is a solution exactly when \(Z-vY\) is a nonnegative multiple of \(X\), whereupon \(u=(Z-vY)/X\) is uniquely determined. Equation (2) makes this an exhaustive algorithm with only \(M_k-1\) candidates \(m\) for each fixed \(k\).

The attached self-contained `CHECKER.py` exhausts these domains at every \(k=2,3,\ldots,20\), in exact integer arithmetic and via two independent enumerators: (i) the least-residue modular method just proved, and (ii) direct enumeration of \(j\equiv k\pmod m\) in \(v=1+jm\), restricted by \(v\le2^k-1\). It asserts identity of both complete solution lists and verifies every proposed solution in the original equation. Both methods return precisely (3). This finite exhaustive computation, combined with (2), proves the classification; sampling without (2) would not.

## Verification
Run `python3 CHECKER.py` using Python 3.8 or later. It performs only exact integer computations, checks that the two independent algorithms agree at each exponent, and prints the domain sizes and the exact five solutions. Its largest domain is \(m=2,\ldots,52428\) at \(k=20\). The checksum of the embedded checker is recorded in the private delivery envelope. The human proof of (2) uses no external computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Fel's archival paper (first released on 2016-08-31) studies the numerical semigroups generated by powers of three consecutive integers. It proves classifications for exponents \(2,3,4\) and reports computational examples at exponents \(11\) and \(13\), in the odd-center range \(2\le m\le50\). The present equation is exactly Fel's center-generator membership equation after the common factor in the two outer powers is removed. The five identities themselves include *previously exhibited* examples: novelty is not asserted for them individually. The contribution is an **all-\(m\)** bound for each exponent and an exhaustive proof for every \(2\le k\le20\), upgrading those restricted finite observations. The earlier paper of Lepilov, O'Rourke, and Swanson derives Frobenius formulas only for squares and cubes and therefore does not provide a general-exponent cutoff.

## Limitations
The number of solutions for \(k>20\) is not determined. The cutoff is sufficient, not claimed optimal. The result does not classify all symmetric numerical semigroups generated by three consecutive powers, because that would require separate treatment of possibly redundant generators and additional structure. A later, differently formulated equivalence to the cutoff or a stronger theorem might exist despite the targeted literature searches; originality is not guaranteed by a search alone. Independent verification has not been performed.
