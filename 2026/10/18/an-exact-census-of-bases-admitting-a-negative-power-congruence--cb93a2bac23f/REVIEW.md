# Review of the negative-power base census

## Correctness — PASS
The odd prime-power unit group is cyclic, permitting an exact count of elements in each two-adic multiplicative-order stratum. The exponent congruences are simultaneously soluble precisely when those valuations agree, and half the least common multiple of the orders supplies a witness. For moduli divisible by four, the odd-power automorphism of the finite two-group forces the two-adic component to be exactly \(-1\); the remaining local order valuations must all be one. The CRT then counts independent components. Direct orbit enumeration for moduli \(3\) through \(400\) agrees. The finite computations are not used as proof for unbounded moduli.

## Originality — PASS within evidence
Klaška (2023), Theorems 9–10, proves the local rank criteria for fixed base and odd composite modulus, and is the closest primary source. Its complete article does not give the modulus-by-modulus number of bases satisfying a negative-power congruence or the geometric stratification. Focused searches for an exact total and its equivalent cyclic-subgroup interpretation, including mathematical-record searches, did not identify the formula. This does not prove global novelty; the standard cyclic-group count makes independent derivation plausible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The number of bases with a defined negative rank is a natural quantitative invariant complementary to the existence question studied in the cited paper. The exact count is not a product of local counts: global exponents force synchronization of all two-adic order valuations, producing a geometric rather than multiplicative law. A modulus divisible by four instead permits one two-primary base and forces odd exponents. These distinctions make the census useful when comparing local solvability frequencies and studying further negative-rank statistics, although the proof is elementary.

## Limitations and risk
The formula is an exact census, not a theorem about prime distribution or a new local rank criterion. An unindexed statement of the count or recognition as a routine group-theoretic corollary remains possible. Independent mathematical audit has not occurred.
