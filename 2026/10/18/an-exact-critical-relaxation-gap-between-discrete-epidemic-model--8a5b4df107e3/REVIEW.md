# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The probability-cube dynamics are explicit and independently reconstructed. The proof uses the exact upper bound by the critical linear matrix and a strict two-update Perron-mass decrease to establish global extinction for every nonzero initial state; it does not infer global convergence merely from local linearization. The undirected connected graph gives one neutral Perron mode and strictly contracting transverse modes for every recovery probability strictly between zero and one. Finite-product expansion produces two explicit quadratic forms, whose Perron projections differ by exactly \(\delta^2\sum_i w_iv_i^2\). After proving transverse alignment, an inverse-amplitude telescoping argument gives the infinite-time limits. The boundary case \(\delta=1\) is excluded and separately demonstrated by an exact two-vertex orbit. The standalone recurrence checks reproduce the predicted formulas on three graph topologies, but these checks are ancillary to the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Dung and Phuc, arXiv:2607.14048, derive a community-based collection of spectral thresholds for the WIR map but do not quantify convergence at equality. Granell et al., DOI:10.1002/andp.202400078, explicitly give both WIR and WOR and note their common threshold and distinct stationary behavior; those facts are not asserted as new. Chakrabarti et al., DOI:10.1145/1284680.1284681, provide the earlier spectral WIR framework. Arenas et al., DOI:10.1016/j.chaos.2022.112921, calculate the WIR Hessian and prove a transcritical bifurcation but neither equality-case rates nor the pairwise WIR/WOR comparison. The prior Hessian calculation is explicitly credited, not claimed as original. The present claim is an explicit, global, arbitrary-connected-graph equality-case asymptotic comparison, rather than these threshold or endemic-state results. Prasse–Van Mieghem examine a continuous-time NIMFA model, so their asymptotic statements do not directly cover the two discrete time recurrences. Relevant semantic mathematical-record searches and available historical claim comparisons did not identify the exact theorem, but a negative search does not establish novelty. Wang, Wang and Shen, DOI:10.1016/j.aml.2019.03.011, is a plausible discrete SIS predecessor whose full text was not available. Broader parabolic-map theory is also potentially applicable and independent priority adjudication remains necessary.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The 2024 review identifies WIR and WOR as naturally distinct infection-update mechanisms with an identical spectral threshold. The theorem supplies the missing quantitative invariant at that threshold: a graph-dependent, initial-condition-independent critical amplitude for each mechanism, a graph-uniform strict separation, and a closed regular-degree law. The singular two-vertex recovery endpoint shows that the quantitative distinction cannot be removed by declaring the mechanisms equivalent at criticality. Such a rate is directly relevant to interpreting finite-horizon prevalence near a threshold, without claiming empirical accuracy of independence closure. This is more than a generic stability corollary: it states the full network-dependent amplitudes and exactly isolates the reinfection correction. Scope is confined to finite undirected connected simple graphs and deterministic closure, not actual stochastic epidemic sample paths.
