# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For all integer exponents \(k\ge1\) and all prime pairs in the stated strip, the proof derives an exact representation condition for the abundance, then shows that the two short terminal residues necessary for \(a\ge q\) cannot coexist when \(p>(p-M)^2\). This is the critical nonstandard step and uses only the explicit interval for \(h(1+d)\) modulo \(p\). The remaining \(0<a<q\) case gives the necessary-and-sufficient residue and open-interval conditions, followed by an explicit abundance bound and a proper-divisor primitivity proof. The packaged checker passes direct arithmetic tests and reproduces Iannucci's first ten published counts; these tests are corroborative rather than the infinite justification.

## Originality — PASS, with stated risk
The 2015 primary paper gives a finite search criterion and conjectures the one-third-scale abundance inequality, but does not classify all larger primes in the near-Mersenne square-root strip. Melfi's 2014 result is a different sufficient construction far from this strip. The closest published mathematical-results entry establishes the broader yet weaker bound \(a<3M^2/4+M\), which does not imply the residue classification or the one-third bound here. Targeted searches for an exact classification, an equivalent modular expression, and stronger coverage did not reveal a dominating result. Pajunen's older structural characterization was not fully inspected; a concealed equivalent remains a residual risk, not proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The theorem settles a mathematically motivated nonempty-width region of Iannucci's explicit abundance conjecture for every exponent and gives a complete prime-in-interval/residue-class characterization for that region. The result rules out the difficult regime \(a\ge q\) by a reusable short-residue obstruction. It advances both the conjectured inequality and the exact distribution of eligible prime cofactors, beyond a bounded census or routine substitution. It neither solves the full conjecture nor asserts infinitude.
