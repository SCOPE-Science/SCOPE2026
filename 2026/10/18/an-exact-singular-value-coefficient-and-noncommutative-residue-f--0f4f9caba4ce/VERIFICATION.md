# Verification scope

The starting discrete matrix identity is a direct subtraction of the Cesàro and forward-shift matrices; both operators are bounded on \(\ell_2\). The finite strict triangular block reduces exactly, after removing its zero row and column, to the inclusive ones matrix. Its reciprocal singular values are the square roots of a discrete first-difference Jacobi matrix; direct diagonalization gives the sine formula recorded in the proof.

The proof's infinite-dimensional step is controlled by the explicit dyadic \(S_p\) estimate with \(1/2<p<1\). The non-diagonal part between geometric blocks has finite rank at fixed cutoffs, and the tails are estimated uniformly before removal. The normalized counting limit and gamma coefficient therefore follow analytically. Numerically, the constant is \(1.393203929685676859\ldots\), with square root \(1.180340599016096226\ldots\). Finite truncation checks are diagnostics and not a proof of an infinite limit.

The optimal approximation-number identity is the Hilbert-space singular-value characterization. The Dixmier-trace consequence uses existence of the ordinary logarithmic singular-value average; no additional generalized-limit choice affects its value. Research and public-literature comparisons do not certify universal novelty. Independent audit and external validation have not been performed.
