# Scientific review: circular finite Fock sampling

## Correctness — PASS

The proof controls arbitrary angular configurations by the fixed diagonal of the Gram operator. Fourier orthogonality attains the Rayleigh and determinant bounds for regular polygons; Newton identities and equality in Rayleigh/Hadamard force angular regularity. The factorial endpoint balance gives the unique E/minimum-condition radius, the strictly increasing truncated-Poisson mean gives the unique D/determinant radius, and a strict geometric-mean pairing separates them for every degree at least three. Stirling's asymptotics give the stated exponential squared-conditioning loss. The numerical checker completed 341 independent finite tests, maximum measured regular-spectrum error 3.55e-15; those do not serve as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)

The principal prior Fock sampling framework is Gröchenig–Ortega-Cerdà, arXiv:2109.11825 (full available text inspected). Karvonen–Särkkä–Tanaka, arXiv:1912.07316 (accessible text inspected) develops kernel Fekete interpolation, without this exact circular Fock E/D comparison. An indexed result on concentric Szegő orbit frames concerns a different finite Blaschke kernel and was available only as a summary. Older 1993 and 1994 circular coherent-state papers were available as abstracts only; mathematical equivalence from those unread full texts is a remaining risk. Searches of indexed mathematical results and accessible prior claims revealed no direct stronger covering result. The Fourier orthogonality, Hadamard and Newton tools are acknowledged classical; their optimized all-degree combined consequence is the claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finite truncated-Gaussian Fock space gives a natural exactly solvable sampling-design model. The same regular angular placement is forced by both objectives, but their optimal radii differ sharply, causing an exponentially increasing squared-conditioning cost at the volume-optimal design. This quantifies an intrinsic, practically relevant tradeoff between determinant-driven and worst-case reconstruction-stability criteria with full optimizer classification.

## Limits and closest literature

The theorem is not global over unrestricted planar point sets, multiple circles or free weights; it applies to finite-section-kernel normalization rather than full-kernel normalization. Independent auditing has not been performed. Older coherent-state circle articles could contain unindexed related optimization.
