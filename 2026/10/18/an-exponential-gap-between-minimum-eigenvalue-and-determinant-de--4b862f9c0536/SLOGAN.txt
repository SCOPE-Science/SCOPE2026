Among all square Gaussian-Fock polynomial sampling designs on one circle, a regular polygon uniquely optimizes both the least frame eigenvalue and condition number at \(\pi\rho^2=(m!)^{1/m}\), but it maximizes the normalized Gram determinant at a strictly larger radius determined by a truncated-Poisson mean equation. For every degree \(m\ge3\), the determinant-optimal design incurs an exponentially increasing relative squared-conditioning penalty, asymptotic to a specified algebraic prefactor times \(\exp((1/2-1/e)m)\).
