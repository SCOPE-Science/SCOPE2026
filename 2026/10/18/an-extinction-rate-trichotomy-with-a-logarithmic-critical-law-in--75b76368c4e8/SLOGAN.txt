In a weak-Allee predator–prey equation, a nonempty open set of strictly positive populations goes extinct with three sharply different rates: both species decay as \(t^{-1}\) below \(\alpha=\gamma/e\), prey has the critical \((t\log t)^{-1}\) law at equality, and above equality it decays as \(t^{-\alpha e/\gamma}\). An exact example has both an extinction basin and an attracting positive coexistence state.
