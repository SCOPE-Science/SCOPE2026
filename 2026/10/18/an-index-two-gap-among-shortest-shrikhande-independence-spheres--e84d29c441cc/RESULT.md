# An index-two gap among shortest Shrikhande independence spheres

## Finding

Let \(S\) be the Shrikhande graph in its Cayley realization on \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), with connection set
\[
\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\},
\]
and let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(S)\) be its ordinary independence complex. For each vertex set \(W\), write \(K[W]\) for the full induced subcomplex. Let \(L\subseteq\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) be the subgroup generated by all fundamental cycles of induced six-vertex two-spheres.

**Theorem.** The sharp size of an induced vertex support for nonzero second homology is six. Precisely \(32\) six-vertex restrictions have nonzero second homology, each is the octahedral sphere \((S^0)^{*3}\), and they form one orbit under a group of \(192\) graph automorphisms. Every one of their ambient classes is primitive, but together they generate a proper index-two subgroup:
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{23},\qquad
[\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z):L]=2.}
\]
A homology class outside \(L\) cannot be represented by a cycle on at most seven vertices of any induced subcomplex. This bound is sharp: the induced eight-vertex complex
\[
K[\{0,1,2,4,7,9,10,11\}]
\]
collapses onto a triangulated two-sphere whose fundamental cycle represents the missing coset. Adjoining that one class to the \(32\) octahedral classes generates \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) **integrally**.

## Assumptions and scope

Coordinates \((a,b)\), where \(0\leq a,b\leq3\), are encoded as \(4a+b\); all coordinate arithmetic in the graph is modulo four. Independence means absence of a graph edge, and a face is oriented by increasing numerical vertex order. Homology is reduced integral simplicial homology unless a coefficient field is displayed. The theorem is restricted to the given finite graph, its induced subcomplexes, and second homology. An ``induced-support class'' is the image of a cycle supported inside \(K[W]\), not merely the abstract existence of a nonzero homology group in an unrelated complex.

## Proof

**1. Absolute integral homology.** Enumerating exactly the independent sets on the sixteen labeled vertices gives the nonempty face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)=(16,72,96,16).
\]
Let \(\partial_2:C_2\to C_1\) and \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\) be the signed simplicial boundaries, of sizes \(72\times96\) and \(96\times16\). Their ranks over \(\mathbb Q\) are \(57\) and \(16\), respectively. All sixteen nonzero Smith invariant factors of \(\partial_3\) equal one. Consequently its image is primitive in the integral triangle chain group and
\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)
 =\ker(\partial_2)/\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)
 \cong\mathbb Z^{96-57-16}=\mathbb Z^{23}.
\]

**2. Why six vertices are the first possible support.** If a finite flag complex has nonzero second homology over a field, choose a vertex-minimal induced subcomplex \(X\) carrying such homology. For any vertex \(v\) of \(X\), the decomposition into the deletion and the vertex star, together with the long exact sequence, injects the nonzero second homology into the first homology of the flag vertex link. A flag complex with nonzero first homology requires at least four vertices. Thus every vertex of \(X\) has at least four neighbors in its one-skeleton. Five vertices would force \(X\) to be a simplex, a contradiction. If there are exactly six vertices, each has at most one nonneighbor; a vertex with no nonneighbor would make the complex a cone. Hence the nonadjacency graph is precisely three disjoint edges, and \(X=(S^0)^{*3}\cong S^2\). The argument applies in particular over \(\mathbb F_2\) and establishes the six-vertex classification.

For the given Cayley graph, checking all \(\binom{16}{6}=8008\) vertex sets finds exactly \(32\) whose induced graph is three disjoint edges. For the corresponding ordered edge pairs \((a_i,b_i)\), where \(a_i<b_i\), form the integral octahedral cycle as the oriented simplicial join of the three zero-cycles \([a_i]-[b_i]\), with the usual reordering signs. Collect these \(32\) cycles as columns of a \(96\times32\) matrix \(Z\). Directly, \(\partial_2 Z=0\).

There are twelve invertible linear transformations of \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\) preserving the displayed connection set. Combining them with all sixteen translations yields \(192\) distinct graph automorphisms. They carry one six-vertex octahedral support to precisely all \(32\) supports, proving one orbit.

**3. Exact lattice index.** The integer matrix
\[
M=[\partial_3\mid Z]
\]
has rank \(39\) and Smith nonzero invariant factors
\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{38\ \mathrm{factors}},\ 2.
\]
Since \(\ker(\partial_2)\) is a primitive lattice in \(C_2\), and \(M\) generates a subgroup of equal rank in that kernel, the finite quotient of the kernel by the columns of \(M\) has order two. It is exactly \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L\). Moreover, adjoining any one octahedral column to \(\partial_3\) gives seventeen unit Smith factors. Such a class is primitive; transitivity proves this for all \(32\) classes. Their rational span is nevertheless the full rank-\(23\) homology.

**4. The obstruction persists through seven vertices.** For each of the \(\binom{16}{7}=11440\) induced seven-vertex subcomplexes, an exhaustive binary boundary calculation gives the following complete classification:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_2(K[W];\mathbb F_2)&0&1\\\hline
\#\{W:|W|=7\}&11120&320
\end{array}
\]
The \(320\) positive cases are *exactly* the supersets of one of the \(32\) octahedral supports, each with a unique such support. Every octahedral class survives mod two in the ambient complex (the boundary matrix augmented by one octahedral cycle has binary rank \(17\), rather than \(16\)). Therefore every induced seven-vertex mod-two homology image is contained in the reduction of \(L\). The same holds for smaller supports by the six-vertex argument. Because the ambient integral homology is free and its quotient by \(L\) is \(\mathbb Z/2\), membership in \(L\) is detected by reduction modulo two. Thus no class outside \(L\) has a representative on seven or fewer induced vertices.

**5. Eight vertices suffice.** Put
\[
W=\{0,1,2,4,7,9,10,11\}.
\]
The induced subcomplex has face vector \((8,19,14,1)\). Its unique tetrahedron is \((0,2,9,11)\). Collapse this tetrahedron across its free triangle \((0,2,9)\); then collapse the remaining triangle \((2,9,11)\) across its free edge \((2,9)\). The remaining complex has vector \((8,18,12)\), is connected, and has a circular link at every vertex, so it is the two-sphere. Its twelve oriented triangles, in order, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&t_1=(0,2,10),\quad t_2=(0,2,11),\quad t_3=(0,7,9),\quad t_4=(0,7,10),\\
&t_5=(0,9,11),\quad t_6=(1,4,10),\quad t_7=(1,4,11),\quad t_8=(1,7,9),\\
&t_9=(1,7,10),\quad t_{10}=(1,9,11),\quad t_{11}=(2,4,10),\quad t_{12}=(2,4,11).
\end{aligned}
\]
The fundamental cycle is
\[
z=\sum_{i=1}^{12}(-1)^{i-1}[t_i].
\]
The exact boundary computation gives \(\partial_2z=0\). The enlarged matrix \([\partial_3\mid Z\mid z]\) has exactly \(39\) unit Smith invariant factors. Therefore the octahedral classes together with \([z]\) generate the full integral homology, completing both the sharp eight-vertex assertion and the lattice classification.

## Relationship to prior work

The graph is the exceptional strongly regular graph sharing parameters \((16,6,2,2)\) with the four-by-four rook graph. Its ambient independence-complex homotopy type \(\bigvee^{23}S^2\) and its full symmetry character have already been determined in publicly available mathematical records. Those results, as well as the established chessboard-complex literature, are not claimed here. In particular, the known abstract wedge homotopy type does not imply integral generation by shortest induced cycles; a rank-\(23\) rational span may be an index-two subgroup, as the exact computation demonstrates. The present theorem determines the smallest carriers, their single group orbit, the integral index, and the sharp extra-support requirement. Barmak's star-cluster results and Shareshian--Wachs's chessboard homology theorems give general or different-graph context, rather than an implication of this specific sublattice calculation.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite computation for this one graph, not a family theorem for strongly regular graphs. The eight-vertex witness is not claimed unique, and no complete census of all larger minimal cycles is asserted. The Smith certificates and exhaustive seven-vertex census are reproducible exact computations, but scientific priority cannot be guaranteed from index searches: equivalent results in literature outside accessible full text remain possible. In particular, the relevant 2026 graph monograph was available through its public chapter summaries, not its complete text. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
