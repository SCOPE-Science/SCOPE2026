# An optimal 72-cell Morse model for the five-cube scale-four Čech nerve

## Finding

Let \(Q_5\) be the binary five-dimensional cube with Hamming metric and let \(X=\mathcal N(Q_5,4)\) be its intrinsic graph Čech nerve at scale four. This nerve has 1,910,200 nonempty simplices, yet admits an **acyclic discrete Morse matching** with exactly one critical vertex, eleven critical 4-simplices, and sixty critical 6-simplices. The matching has 955,064 pairs and is specified by a finite deterministic rule below. Accordingly \(X\) is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with precisely 72 cells. Its cell count is minimal among all CW models with these integral homology groups.

There is a further homotopy-theoretic consequence beyond the preexisting Betti-number computation. There exists an integer \(t\) with \(0\le t\le 11\) such that

\[
X\simeq \bigvee^{t}\Sigma^2\mathbb{CP}^{2}\ \vee\ \bigvee^{11-t}S^{4}\ \vee\ \bigvee^{60-t}S^{6}.
\]

In this formula \(t\) is exactly the rank over \(\mathbb F_2\) of the Steenrod square \(Sq^2:H^4(X;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^6(X;\mathbb F_2)\). **The value of \(t\) is not determined here.** In particular, the model does not establish that \(X\) is a wedge of spheres.

## Assumptions and scope

Represent each cube vertex by an integer from zero to 31, the five binary coordinates of that integer. For each center \(c\), define the closed radius-two Hamming ball

\[
B(c)=\{v\in\{0,\ldots,31\}:\operatorname{popcount}(c\oplus v)\le 2\}.
\]

Each ball has 16 vertices. The simplices of \(X\) are precisely all nonempty subsets of at least one \(B(c)\), together with the empty simplex in the usual abstract-simplicial-complex convention. This is the scale-four identification in Adams–Shukla–Singh, Lemma 2.2. A face \(\sigma\) is encoded by the unsigned 32-bit word whose set-bit positions are its vertices.

The claim concerns **only** the particular cube dimension five and Čech scale four. All computations use integer bit operations and finite exhaustive enumeration; there is no floating-point geometry.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive model and explicit matching.** Enumerate every nonempty submask of each of the 32 ball bitmasks and deduplicate the resulting integer masks. Sort the distinct face masks in increasing integer order. Initially mark every face as unmatched. For vertex labels \(v=0,1,\ldots,31\) in order, scan the sorted faces \(\sigma\) in increasing mask order. Whenever both \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) are still unmatched faces, and \(v\notin\sigma\), match this pair and mark both unavailable for every subsequent step. This is a well-defined face-poset matching: every pairing is an immediate inclusion, and no face belongs to two pairs. The algorithm's exact pseudocode and all finite checks are supplied in `verify.cpp`.

Exhaustive execution reports 1,910,200 distinct nonempty faces, 955,064 matched pairs, and just 72 unmatched faces: one in dimension zero, 11 in dimension four, and 60 in dimension six. The dimension-wise face vector is printed by the verifier and independently regenerated by `fvector.py`.

**2. Exhaustive acyclicity.** For every nonempty codimension-one inclusion \(\tau\subset\sigma\), direct its cover edge from \(\tau\) to \(\sigma\) if the two faces are a matched pair, and otherwise from \(\sigma\) to \(\tau\). This produces a directed graph on the 1,910,200 nonempty faces with exactly 15,768,064 cover edges. The verifier constructs all these edges implicitly, computes all indegrees, and performs Kahn's topological-sort deletion, visiting all 1,910,200 nodes. Therefore the directed graph has no cycle. This is the standard finite acyclicity criterion for a discrete Morse matching, **not** an inference from Betti-number agreement. Forman's discrete Morse theorem yields a CW complex with exactly the enumerated critical cells.

**3. Integral homology and minimality.** The CW model from Step 2 has no cells in adjacent positive degrees: its cellular differentials are all zero. Consequently its reduced integral homology is free of ranks 11 in degree four and 60 in degree six, vanishing elsewhere. Independently, Adams–Shukla–Singh, Table 1, already reports

\[
\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{11},\qquad
\widetilde H_6(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{60},
\]

with all other reduced integral homology zero. This published computation is a consistency check rather than an essential premise of the new proof. Any finite CW complex homotopy equivalent to \(X\) therefore requires at least one 0-cell, at least 11 4-cells and at least 60 6-cells, by cellular chain-group rank bounds. The 72-cell discrete Morse model attains all three lower bounds simultaneously. **These homology ranks are previously published; the explicit optimal Morse matching is the finding.**

**4. Classification of remaining attaching maps.** The CW model has one 0-cell and no cells of dimensions 1, 2, 3 or 5. Hence its four-skeleton is \(\bigvee^{11}S^4\), and sixty six-cells attach through a map \(\bigvee^{60}S^5\to\bigvee^{11}S^4\). The Hilton–Milnor dimension range gives

\[
\pi_5(\bigvee^{11}S^4)\cong\bigoplus^{11}\pi_5(S^4)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^{11},
\]

since the first Whitehead products have dimension seven. Thus the attaching data constitute an \(11\times60\) matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\). Homotopy self-equivalences of the source and target sphere wedges realize the necessary elementary row and column changes because integral unimodular changes surject onto invertible mod-two changes. Reducing the matrix to rank-normal form yields \(t\) disjoint cofibers of the double suspension of the Hopf map, \(\Sigma^2\mathbb{CP}^2\), and the unpaired 4- and 6-spheres. On each suspended complex-projective-plane summand, \(Sq^2\) takes its degree-four generator nontrivially to degree six; it vanishes on sphere summands. Hence the stated rank description of \(t\).

## Verification

Compile and execute the packaged `verify.cpp` using a C++17 compiler, for example `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify && ./verify`. It prints the face and critical-cell counts, the count of all Hasse cover edges, the complete Kahn traversal, and a final `VERIFIED` marker only after assertions pass. Run `python fvector.py` separately to regenerate face counts and the reduced Euler characteristic by an independent implementation. Expected exact outputs are stored in `verify_cpp_output.txt` and `verify_python_output.txt`. The direct enumeration and Kahn verification are feasible on a workstation; no stored giant face list is needed in the package beyond the deterministic verifier itself.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams–Shukla–Singh (first public December 12, 2022) define these hypercube Čech nerves and explicitly publish the **same** integral Betti ranks \(11\) and \(60\) for the five-cube scale-four case. Their Table 1 and Section 7 distinguish that information from an actual homotopy classification and pose open wedge-of-spheres and collapsibility questions. The present result does not rebrand their homology calculation: its contribution is a complete **acyclic perfect Morse matching**, an optimal 72-cell CW model, and the consequent precise 2-primary attaching-map classification.

Biswas (first public September 26, 2026) recently investigates Čech complexes of finite subsets of hypercubes and proposes higher-radius structural conjectures. Only the verified abstract of that recent paper was accessible for comparison; it does not state this complete face-poset matching. Its full text remains an originality-access risk. Closest independently indexed records on four-cube scale-three and six-cube scale-three Čech homotopy concern different complexes and do not imply the five-cube scale-four matching.

## Limitations

The rank \(t\) of \(Sq^2\) has not been computed and may be positive. No actual wedge-of-spheres homotopy type, simple-homotopy classification, \(7\)-collapsibility, infinite-parameter extension, or equivariant strengthening is asserted. A discrete Morse CW reduction does **not** by itself establish the stronger elementary-collapse or collapsibility-number conjectures in the cited literature. The exhaustive finite certificate proves the matching and model only at the specified parameters; its output is not an infinite-family theorem. One relevant 2026 primary paper could not be inspected in full.
