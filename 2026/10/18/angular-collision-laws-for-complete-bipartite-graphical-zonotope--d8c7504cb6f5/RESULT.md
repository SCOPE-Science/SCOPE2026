# Angular collision laws for complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes

## Finding

Let \(p,q\ge1\), let \(n=p+q\), and let \(Z_{p,q}\) be the standard centered graphical zonotope of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{p,q}\), in its intrinsic Euclidean hyperplane. Choose two independent uniform spherical objective directions and maximize their associated linear functionals over \(Z_{p,q}\). Denote by \(C_{p,q}\) the probability that these two directions select the same vertex. The maximizer is almost surely unique.

Define the *formal* series \(F(t)=\sum_{j\ge1}j!t^j\). Then the exact collision probability is
\[
 C_{p,q}=\frac{p!q!}{(p+q)!^2}[x^py^q]\frac{F(x)F(y)(2+F(x)+F(y))}{1-F(x)F(y)}.
\]
All coefficients of the rational expression in formal series are finite, even though \(F\) has zero analytic radius of convergence.

For fixed \(q\ge1\), set \(H_q=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom qj^{-1}\). As \(p\) increases,
\[
 C_{p,q}=\left(\frac{p!q!}{(p+q)!}\right)^2\left(H_q+O_q(p^{-1})\right),\qquad
 \lim_{p\to\infty}p^{2q}C_{p,q}=(q!)^2H_q.
\]
In particular, the order-two effective number \(1/C_{p,q}\) of vertices seen by random linear objectives grows polynomially like \(p^{2q}/((q!)^2H_q)\), despite the actual number of vertices being at least \((q+1)^p\).

The same proof gives the complete normal-cone angle spectrum. Represent an acyclic orientation by alternating maximal nonempty blocks of the two vertex classes, with ordered block sizes \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_\ell\). The normalized solid angle of the corresponding vertex is exactly
\[
 \alpha=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^k a_i!\,\prod_{j=1}^\ell b_j!}{(p+q)!}.
\]
For a specified alternating block-size pattern, there are exactly \(p!q!/(\prod a_i!\prod b_j!)\) distinct vertices. Consequently the above formulas describe every cone angle and its multiplicity, without an uncomputed integral.

For completeness, the largest angle is \(p!q!/(p+q)!\), attained only at the two all-one-class-before-the-other orientations; this maximum is **already implied** by established optimal-orientation theory and is not asserted as novel. For \(p\ge q\), the smallest angle and its multiplicity are as follows. If \(p=q\), the minimum is \(1/(2p)!\), attained at exactly \(2(p!)^2\) vertices. If \(p>q\), write \(p=(q+1)s+r\), where \(s\ge1\) and \(0\le r<q+1\), and define \(L=(s!)^{q+1-r}((s+1)!)^r\). Then
\[
 \alpha_{\min}=\frac{L}{(p+q)!},\qquad
 N_{\min}=\frac{q!p!}{L}\binom{q+1}{r}.
\]
The expressions for \(q>p\) follow by interchanging the two classes.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient vertex-coordinate space is \(\mathbb R^n\), with standard orthonormal basis \(e_v\). Its zero-sum hyperplane is \(H=\{z:\sum_vz_v=0\}\), equipped with the induced Euclidean inner product. If \(A,B\) denote the two nonempty bipartition classes, then
\[
 Z_{p,q}=\sum_{a\in A,b\in B}\left[-\frac{e_a-e_b}{2},\frac{e_a-e_b}{2}\right]\subset H.
\]
It is full dimensional in \(H\), since \(K_{p,q}\) is connected. The word *angle* means the probability that an isotropic Gaussian vector in \(H\) lies in a vertex's outer normal cone; equivalently, the cone's spherical measure divided by the total sphere measure. The coefficient operator refers exclusively to formal power series. The asymptotic statement fixes \(q\) while \(p\to\infty\); it does not assert uniformity in a growing \(q\).

## Proof

**Normal cones and ordered blocks.** For a generic objective \(u\in H\), maximizing over each defining segment chooses the sign of \(\langle u,e_a-e_b\rangle=u_a-u_b\). Thus the maximizer is indexed by the acyclic orientation obtained by comparing the \(n\) vertex coordinates. Distinct acyclic orientations give distinct exposed vertices (the standard graphical-zonotope/graphic-arrangement correspondence). An isotropic Gaussian in \(H\) can be generated by subtracting the mean from \(n\) independent standard Gaussians; all pairwise comparisons are unchanged. The \(n!\) strict coordinate orders therefore have equal probability \(1/n!\).

Fix an orientation of \(K_{p,q}\). In any compatible total order, group consecutive elements of the same bipartition class into maximal blocks. The class labels alternate. Because every cross-class pair is an edge, an element of one block must precede every element of any subsequent block. Indeed, neighboring unlike blocks are entirely comparable in the corresponding direction, and transitive closure orders all nonadjacent blocks. Within a block there are no edges, so its elements can be rearranged freely. Conversely, changing the order of unlike blocks changes at least one cross-edge orientation. Therefore the orientation is uniquely represented by its sequence of labeled blocks, and its compatible orders are precisely its \(\prod a_i!\prod b_j!\) within-block permutations. Dividing by \(n!\) proves the angle formula. Choosing labeled elements for blocks gives the claimed multiplicity of each ordered block-size pattern.

**Collision series.** Almost-sure uniqueness and independence give \(C_{p,q}=\sum_v\alpha_v^2\). For a fixed block pattern, multiplying its number of vertices by the square of its angle leaves
\[
 \frac{p!q!}{n!^2}\prod_i a_i!\prod_j b_j!.
\]
Weighting every nonempty block of the first class by \(F(x)\) and of the second by \(F(y)\), summing over alternating block strings gives two families beginning and ending in different classes, plus two families beginning and ending in the same class:
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}2(F(x)F(y))^k
 +\sum_{k\ge1}F(x)^{k+1}F(y)^k
 +\sum_{k\ge1}F(x)^kF(y)^{k+1}
 =\frac{F(x)F(y)(2+F(x)+F(y))}{1-F(x)F(y)}.
\]
Taking the indicated coefficient yields the exact collision identity. This is a formal identity: no convergence of \(F\) is assumed.

**Long-side asymptotics.** For \(k\ge1\) and integer \(p\ge k\), put
\[
 S_k(p)=\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_k=p\atop a_i\ge1}\frac{\prod_i a_i!}{p!}.
\]
We have \(S_1(p)=1\) and
\[
 S_2(p)=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\binom pa^{-1}\le\frac{4(p-2)}{p(p-1)}\quad(p\ge4).
\]
The bound follows from the two endpoint terms \(1/p\) and from \(\binom pa\ge\binom p2\) for the remaining \(p-3\) terms. Also \(S_2(m)\le2/3\) for every \(m\ge2\), by this bound and direct inspection of \(m=2,3\). For each composition into \(k\) positive blocks, merging one of its \(k-1\) adjacent pairs creates a composition into \(k-1\) blocks; reversing the merge has total relative factorial weight \(S_2(m)\), where \(m\) is the merged block size. Counting every \(k\)-block composition by each of its \(k-1\) possible merges gives
\[
 (k-1)S_k(p)\le(k-1)\frac23 S_{k-1}(p).
\]
Thus \(S_k(p)\le S_2(p)=O(p^{-1})\) for every \(k\ge2\), uniformly in \(k\).

For fixed \(q\), every alternating block pattern has at most \(q+1\) first-class blocks; there are finitely many second-class ordered compositions. Contributions with at least two first-class blocks are therefore \(O_q(p!q!/p)\) to the coefficient in the collision formula. A pattern with exactly one first-class block has either one second-class block on one side (two choices), or two second-class blocks, one on either side. These contribute
\[
 p!\left(2q!+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}j!(q-j)!\right)=p!q!H_q.
\]
Therefore the weighted coefficient equals \(p!q!(H_q+O_q(p^{-1}))\). Substitution proves the asymptotic formula; since \(p!/(p+q)!=p^{-q}(1+O_q(p^{-1}))\), the stated limit follows.

**Exponential vertex count.** Fix a labeled order on the \(q\) vertices of the second class. For each of the \(p\) first-class vertices, independently select one of the \(q+1\) gaps before, between, or after these ordered vertices. Every assignment defines an acyclic orientation and two assignments disagree on an incident edge, so the \((q+1)^p\) assignments yield distinct vertices. Together with the collision asymptotics this proves the exponential-versus-polynomial contrast.

**Sharp minimum-angle classification.** It suffices to assume \(p\ge q\). At most \(q+1\) first-class blocks can occur. Their factorial product is minimized, permitting empty slots, by balancing \(p\) integers across \(q+1\) slots: the smoothing inequality \(a!b!>(a-1)!(b+1)!\) holds whenever \(a\ge b+2\). Every second-class block contributes a factorial of at least one. If \(p>q\), equality requires all \(q+1\) first-class slots positive and balanced, all \(q\) second-class blocks singleton, and the alternating word to start and end in the first class. The \(r\) enlarged slots may be chosen in \(\binom{q+1}{r}\) ways. Assigning labeled vertices to ordered blocks gives \(p!q!/L\) orientations per choice, proving both formulas. If \(p=q\), the minimum factorial product is one; all \(2p\) runs are singletons and exactly two alternating class orders are possible, each yielding \((p!)^2\) labeled orientations. Finally, \(\prod a_i!\le p!\), \(\prod b_j!\le q!\), with simultaneous equality only at the two two-block orientations, giving the stated (previously known in broader form) maximum.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library checker `angular_collision_checker.py` enumerates labeled total orders and their cross-class orientation signatures for 15 parameter pairs with \(p,q\le4\) and \(p+q\le7\), representing 2,818 orientation signatures in total. It compares the sum of squared signature multiplicities with both the binary-word factorial-run census and the formal-series convolution. It also checks the complete extreme-angle formulas and their multiplicities. In particular, it verifies \(C_{2,2}=7/72\). For each \(q\in\{1,2,3\}\), it evaluates the independent formal coefficient formula at \(p=30,60,120\) and confirms approach from below toward the predicted fixed-\(q\) limit. These checks are finite corroboration; the universal statements are proved above. Run with `python angular_collision_checker.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Padrol, Pilaud, and Poullot provide graphical-zonotope and normal-fan foundations and deformation theory; they do not calculate the two-objective collision probability or its fixed-part asymptotics. Stinson, Gleich, and Constantine study Gaussian random-objective vertex enumeration and normal-cone sampling probabilities for arbitrary zonotopes, but their inspected algorithmic and probability sections do not evaluate the complete-bipartite two-sample collision functional or the factorial-run asymptotic. Iriarte proves that the two bipartite orientations maximize the number of linear extensions for any connected bipartite graph and relates orientations to graphic-arrangement chambers; the maximal-angle statement here is explicitly **prior-covered** and serves only as context. Carballosa, Khera, and Reyes construct codes and enumerate acyclic orientations of complete multipartite graphs, while Cameron, Glass, Rekvényi, and Schumacher relate the count for complete bipartite graphs to poly-Bernoulli numbers. These count orientations rather than their quadratic angle weights. The exact squared-angle generating function and the polynomial effective-vertex asymptotic require the additional factorial-run convolution and uniform multi-run bound proved above. No assertion of globally proved bibliographic uniqueness is made.

## Limitations

All claims concern the standard Euclidean inner product on the zero-sum coordinate hyperplane, not arbitrary affine transformations or independently perturbed generators. The leading asymptotic holds for fixed \(q\); it does not cover simultaneous growth of the two bipartition sizes. The minimum- and maximum-angle classifications are supporting corollaries rather than separate claimed discoveries. The explicit angle formula combines standard orientation/linear-extension principles with a block decomposition; the central new object is the quadratic angular collision law and its sharp limit. The literature comparison is not a proof that no equivalent calculation exists under other terminology. No independent audit or validation has occurred.
