# Research assessment: arbitrary disturbance–diversity peaks

## Correctness — PASS

The homogeneous-reset succession chain has the stated unique stationary stage distribution, derived from the exponential disturbance-age representation and independently checked by exact rational generator balance. The explicit separated-rate construction produces more than \(0.48\) Shannon and Gini–Simpson stage diversity at each of \(n-1\) rate intensities and less than \(0.105\) Shannon / \(0.01\) Gini–Simpson diversity at each intervening intensity. Analyticity and continuity imply distinct strict maxima for all \(n\ge2\), not just for tested finite sizes. The three-stage Gini–Simpson derivative is a quartic over a positive denominator, and its discriminant, polynomial sign regions and a root-count argument give the complete threshold classification. The packaged independent balance solver and exact polynomial certificates executed successfully; they do not replace the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS, with stated risk

Johst and Huth (2005) already supplied the stochastic succession model, reported two-peaked diversity–disturbance relationships and explained the role of separated stage lifetimes; this prior contribution is not claimed as new. The inspected complete 2005 article does not give an unbounded family with at least \(n-1\) strict peaks simultaneously for Shannon and Gini–Simpson indices, or the necessary-and-sufficient three-stage Gini–Simpson ratio threshold \(r_*\). The full 2006 Solow–Smith succession paper concerns inverse inference for Markov chains, and published indexed comparisons found no equivalent quantitative shape theorem. Because older references were incompletely accessible and the 2005 mechanism naturally suggests extension, unnoticed prior coverage remains a genuine risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Peak multiplicity as disturbance intensity varies is the central structural question posed by the intermediate-disturbance hypothesis. The present theorem shows there is no global peak-count ceiling across succession-stage complexity, and establishes both the minimal stage number and the exact sharp three-stage rate boundary for Simpson diversity. Robust strict inequalities make the construction more than a knife-edge numerical example. It does not justify an empirical species-richness law: the modeled diversity is over landscape stages, with exponential progression and homogeneous Poisson resetting.
