# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. For reduced rational rotations, an exact power identity reduces spectral radius to the maximum of a Fourier-divisibility projection of the real logarithmic weight. For irrational rotations, finite Fourier geometric sums and uniform approximation prove a constant asymptotic growth rate. Analytic Fourier decay controls all rational approaches, and the explicit entire positive-coefficient construction realizes precisely every divisor-downward-closed denominator set. Positive coefficients give the claimed maximizer and a Gaussian tail yields the stated uniform error. Boundary cases (the zero potential, the empty ideal, zero rotation, arbitrary numerator, and infinite ideals) are included.

## Originality

PASS, subject to publication-priority risk. The fixed-angle radius formula itself is covered by classical results and is not presented as novel. The additional exact classification of parameter discontinuities and the inverse realization of **every** divisor-downward-closed rational-denominator set are not implications stated in the primary sources inspected. The strongest overlap is Zajkowski's variational principle, which describes each fixed operator but neither prescribes continuity sets in the rotation parameter nor constructs one analytic weight realizing an arbitrary such set. Macdonald's work addresses decomposability and power bounds at irrational rotations; Dobrick's theorem concerns peripheral spectral cyclicity. No directly equivalent claim was identified in semantic mathematical-record searches or the available same-topic results. Earlier alternative formulations in dynamical spectral theory remain possible.

## Value

PASS. The discontinuity locus of a natural positive-operator spectral invariant is classified without omission, and a sharp arithmetic restriction is proved both necessary and sufficient. The inverse theorem constructs every admissible pattern simultaneously, even with weights uniformly as close to the trivial constant as desired. The ability to produce a dense rational discontinuity locus while retaining all irrational continuity points and an explicit sharp decay law is a meaningful structural answer, not a fixed-parameter restatement.

## Closest literature and limitations

Zajkowski (Studia Math. 198, 2010) contains a general known variational principle. Macdonald (J. Operator Theory 35, 1996) studies weighted irrational-rotation power norms and decomposability. Dobrick (arXiv:2610.10093v1, 2026) treats positive-operator cyclicity and an unweighted irrational rotation. Priority uncertainty remains for older parameter-dependent spectral-radius results in ergodic optimization. The present conclusion applies only to positive analytic weights and scalar spectral radii, not arbitrary cocycles or full spectra.
