# Scientific review: arithmetic coalescence universality

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof checks the expansion modulo-one discontinuities explicitly: a shared expanding branch changes the signed separation by an integer after multiplication by the base, while a shared contraction divides separation by the base. The least integralizing denominator exponent is therefore an exactly absorbed skip-free walk. The cyclic ballot formula and quadratic generating function establish the first-meeting distribution for every integer base and every pair in scope. Conditioning below the drift threshold reverses the step bias. Reflection and a local central limit theorem prove the critical survival tail; an explicit expansion of the defective generating function proves the near-critical conditional Brownian hitting law. The attached exact-arithmetic two-trajectory verifier passed 511 comparisons for four different bases, including a composite base. These finite checks are corroborative and do not establish infinite statements on their own.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority uncertainty.** Homburg and Kalle (arXiv:2207.09987v1, Sections 1, 2.3 and 3 and Appendix A) supply the original affine maps, a Lyapunov-sign synchronization/intermittency/divergence theorem, a multivalued-grid collapse construction, and random-walk techniques. They do not state the complete *pairwise arithmetic* meeting classification, the exact finite-time meeting distribution, or the conditional Brownian first-passage scaling for diverging denominators. Goverse, Homburg and Lamb (2026) study smooth randomly translated circle maps rather than these discrete equal-base affine maps and do not establish base-adic coincidence laws. Yan and collaborators (arXiv:2308.09269) examine a related doubling/halving map but its studied quantities are one-point invariant density and autocorrelation, not common-noise pair meeting. Public indexed mathematical-record comparisons include a Gaussian crossover for expanding-only maps on finite grids, which is not a first-passage distribution for the present system. The ballot principle and Brownian first-passage law themselves are standard; the potential contribution is the exact model-specific arithmetic reduction and all-base critical universality. An equivalent historical deduction remains possible, especially given the elementary random-walk reduction. The later journal version of the originating paper was not independently compared throughout.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Exact meeting and asymptotic synchronization differ sharply. When contractions dominate, the source proves asymptotic synchronization, yet base-adic pairs have a strictly positive probability of *never meeting*; when expansion dominates, every base-adic pair nevertheless meets almost surely, and for a fixed reference point a countable dense set does so simultaneously. The complete depth-dependent survival law gives exact expected digital coalescence times and infinite-mean critical behavior. The near-critical inverse-Gaussian/Lévy crossover is uniform in the integer base and identifies a physically meaningful time scale for resolving initially very fine arithmetic separations. These assertions answer a natural two-point question not settled by one-point stationary or Lyapunov-sign results, although the first-passage computation is classical once the reduction is made.

## Limitations and closest literature

The proof assumes exact real arithmetic, a common random driving sequence, identical integer expansion and contraction bases, and independent branch choices. It does not address unequal bases, different noise streams, finite precision, or perturbations. Source-specific priority remains a risk because related work already develops lattice-grid collapse and random walks; independent review of the 2025 journal version and any earlier unindexed exact-meeting calculations is recommended.
