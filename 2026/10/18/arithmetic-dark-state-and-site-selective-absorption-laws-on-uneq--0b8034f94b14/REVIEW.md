# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result concerns the finite one-particle no-jump Hamiltonian, with all arm lengths at least one and positive uniform hopping and hub loss. The dissipativity identity identifies every nondecaying eigenvector with an adjacency eigenvector vanishing at the hub; this subspace reduces the nonnormal operator, so all complementary states decay. Removing the hub gives exact Dirichlet sine waves. The strictly monotone cosine dispersion ensures coincident energies occur exactly for equal reduced fractions. For each fraction, the hub matching condition is a single nonzero rank-one constraint on the participating arms, and its orthogonal projection gives the stated diagonal survival weight. Summing primitive frequencies yields the integer Ramanujan sum. Every term's nonnegativity and its vanishing condition establish the precise least-common-multiple criterion. The proof does not mistake finite enumeration for an infinite classification. The independent full-adjacency check verified 321 small configurations, 2904 vertex probabilities and dark-space dimensions with maximum floating discrepancy below \(2.1\times10^{-15}\). No external proof or validation is implied.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent priority audit

The closest full primary text is Yalouz–Pouthier arXiv:2210.17524v1, which assumes a *common* length across arms, uses rotational sector decomposition, and studies peripheral detuning and the absorption speed; its Section II and subsequent spectral/observables sections do not supply an unequal-length, rational site-resolved infinite-time survival formula. Their 2020 centrally trapped extended star paper treats a two-generation hub-and-peripheral-star topology rather than arbitrary chain arms. Le et al. arXiv:1707.07482v1 supplies the general dark-subspace concept and path sine eigenvectors but not the present number-theoretic classification. The equal-arm survival fraction is a known special case and is expressly not offered as new. Searches for equivalent spider, starlike, commensurate-arm, arithmetic trapping, and spectral projector descriptions in the relevant literature and published mathematical-result database did not locate a result that implies the full statement. The generic dark-subspace projector is a prerequisite, not an explicit resolution of the unequal-arm local site criterion. An equivalent specialized formula under unsearched terminology cannot be excluded.

## Value — PASS

Equal-length stars admit extensive symmetry reductions, but branch-length differences break them and create an exact arithmetic resonance problem. The result classifies whether the sink sees every eigenmode, counts inaccessible modes, and specifies **which individual injection sites** are still completely absorbed when the network globally has dark modes. The exact rational expression and sharp node-selection condition furnish a physically motivated geometric design rule without relying on heuristic relaxation-time plots. It is a natural complete classification of an infinite family, rather than an isolated small graph computation.

## Limitations and closest sources

The spectral projector statement is specific to a single absorbing hub, equal nearest-neighbor hopping, zero onsite energies, and finite path arms. It does not settle finite-time mixing, unequal couplings, disordered/dephased networks, or many-body master equations. Full primary literature inspected includes Yalouz–Pouthier arXiv:2210.17524v1, Le et al. arXiv:1707.07482v1, and the open full text associated with doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.101.012310.
