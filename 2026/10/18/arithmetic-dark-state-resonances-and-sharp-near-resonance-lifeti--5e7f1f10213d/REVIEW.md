# Review of the antipodal hypercube detector resonance theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed domain is all integers \(d\ge2\), positive \(J\) and \(\gamma\), and real shortcut strength \(\lambda\), with the specified Lindblad sink and antipodal initial state. Walsh decomposition gives an exactly invariant \((d+1)\)-state sector. Spectral collision analysis shows only isolated, disjoint odd-distance pairs. The bright-projector formula yields every exceptional absorption probability. At nonresonant strengths, a stable Lyapunov equation yields an exact finite mean and its positive detuning coefficient; binomial identities and the central-binomial estimate yield the large-dimensional coefficient. Reconstructed full-site propagators, exact rational sums, and finite-dimensional Lyapunov checks reproduce the stated identities; only the analytic arguments establish the all-size and limiting claims. The formula does not apply to alternate measurement protocols or unspecified initial states.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Mulherkar's 2026 cubelike-graph hitting result is for discrete-time Grover-coined dynamics, not the continuous coherent antipodal-link Hamiltonian with one absorbing sink. Thiel et al. 2019 derive a general bright/dark spectral framework and discuss ordinary hypercubes with stroboscopic detection; these principles are credited, rather than claimed. Liu et al. 2020 establish inverse-square mean detection-time divergence near sampling-phase collisions, and that generic phenomenon is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Varbanov et al. 2008 analyze Poisson-time measurement, a distinct continuous-time detection protocol, and prior works on quantum hypercube hitting/dark states are acknowledged. No inspected primary statement implies the exact parameter-dependent arithmetic spectrum, all-size exceptional probabilities, and explicit Lindblad-detuning coefficient for this particular family; direct derivability from general spectral results is a residual priority risk. The motivating 2026 manuscript was accessible only in abstract, and the 2008 primary manuscript was not accessible for complete inspection.

## Value — PASS

The resonant jump from certain detection to \(1/(d+1)\), the complete odd-integer classification, and the explicit inverse-square sensitivity coefficient quantify the fragility of quantum hitting to a physically natural coherent perfect-matching shortcut. The large-dimensional coefficient identifies the source of its dimension dependence. The object and observables are natural for engineered coherent quantum networks; the finding is not merely a numerical small-graph enumeration. Finite noise, static disorder, detuned onsite energies, and alternate monitoring can destroy the effect; no experimental feasibility or performance advantage is claimed.
