# Arithmetic inner Galois points on smooth ternary plane quartics

## Finding
Let \(C\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_3}\) be a geometrically smooth plane quartic. A point \(P\in C(\mathbf F_3)\) is an **arithmetic inner Galois point** when projection from \(P\) gives a degree-three Galois extension \(\mathbf F_3(C)/\mathbf F_3(\mathbf P^1)\), with the deck transformations defined over \(\mathbf F_3\). Among projective quartic equations with this property, there are exactly \(24\,570\) normalized homogeneous equations: \(24\,336\) have exactly one such rational point, and \(234\) have exactly four; no other positive number occurs.

Under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\), the one-point equations form eighteen orbits: ten of size \(1872\), five of size \(936\), and three of size \(312\). The four-point equations form a unique orbit of size \(234\), represented by
\[
 C_*:\quad X^4+YZ^3-Y^3Z=0.
\]
Its four arithmetic inner Galois points are the four rational points of \(X=0\); its projective automorphism group has order \(24\) and is isomorphic to \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\). The counts refer to equations up to nonzero scalar, rather than abstract curve isomorphism classes. The nineteen projective-orbit representatives, in a documented fixed monomial order, are included in `galois_output.json`.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is precisely \(\mathbf F_3\). Quartics are homogeneous, nonzero, identified under nonzero scalar, and required to be geometrically smooth (not merely nonsingular at the thirteen rational plane points). Arithmetic Galois means Galois over the unextended constant field, not just over its algebraic closure. The monomial ordering in the included catalogue and code is descending \(X\)-degree then descending \(Y\)-degree, with total degree four. A projective orbit is under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\); it is not asserted to classify unmarked abstract curves.

## Proof
**1. Elation normal form.** A smooth plane quartic is a nonhyperelliptic genus-three canonical curve; any automorphism of the curve is induced by a projective linear transformation. Let \(P=(1:0:0)\) be an arithmetic inner Galois point. A nontrivial deck transformation acts trivially on the lines through \(P\), so over \(\mathbf F_3\) its matrix, after projective rescaling, has form \(X\mapsto \lambda X+aY+bZ\), \(Y\mapsto Y\), \(Z\mapsto Z\). Its order is three, forcing \(\lambda=1\) and \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\). There are four order-three elation subgroups with center \(P\), indexed by \([a:b]\in\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_3)\). Conversely invariance under one such subgroup yields a degree-three Galois projection because the deck action is faithful and has size equal to the degree.

**2. Complete invariant normal form.** Fix the subgroup \(X\mapsto X+Y\). Its invariant ring is generated over \(\mathbf F_3[Y,Z]\) by \(W=X^3-XY^2\). Every invariant quartic through \(P\) therefore has the *unique* form \(F=WM(Y,Z)+H_4(Y,Z)\), with \(M\) linear and \(H_4\) binary quartic. Smoothness at \(P\) forces \(M\ne0\), and scalar normalization gives exactly four possibilities \(M=Y\) or \(M=Z+aY\) for \(a\in\mathbf F_3\).

For \(M=Y\), write \(H_4=\sum_{i=0}^4 c_iY^{4-i}Z^i\). Since \(F_X=-Y^3\), all singularities must lie on \(Y=0\). The restriction there is \(c_4 Z^4\), while \(P\) is smooth. Thus \(F\) is geometrically smooth exactly when \(c_4\ne0\), giving \(2\cdot3^4=162\) forms.

For \(M=V=Z+aY\), set \(U=Y\) and \(H_4=\sum_{i=0}^4c_i U^{4-i}V^i\). Here \(F_X=-U^2V\); any singular point must lie on \(U=0\) or \(V=0\). On \(V=0\), a singularity exists exactly when \(c_0=0\). On \(U=0\), there is a unique potential geometric singular point, and differentiating in \(U\) shows that it is singular exactly when \(c_3=0\). Again \(P\) is smooth. Therefore precisely \(c_0c_3\ne0\) characterizes geometric smoothness, yielding \(2^2\cdot 3^3=108\) forms for each \(a\). Altogether each elation subgroup fixes \(162+3\cdot108=486\) normalized smooth quartics.

**3. Exhaustive incidence enumeration.** The projective plane over \(\mathbf F_3\) has thirteen points. Each center supports four elation subgroups, for \(52\) pairs \((P,G)\). The program `verify_galois.py` enumerates all \(52\) by explicit invertible coordinate matrices and transforms all \(486\) canonical smooth quartics for each, without sampling. It projectively normalizes every image and records the center. The resulting distinct curve count is \(24\,570\), with center multiplicities \(24\,336\) and \(234\) for one and four centers respectively. The multiplicity-weighted sum is \(24\,336+4(234)=25\,272=52(486)\), also demonstrating no repeated elation subgroup at a fixed counted center.

**4. Projective orbits and exceptional group.** The two coordinate transpositions, one elementary shear, and one nontrivial diagonal scaling in `verify_galois.py` generate \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\). Their actions partition the entire set into eighteen orbits with one center and one orbit with four centers; the exact orbit sizes appear above. The exceptional representative is geometrically smooth because its three partial derivatives are \(X^3\), \(Z^3\), and \(-Y^3\), which have no common projective zero. Every determinant-one binary substitution of \((Y,Z)\) preserves \(YZ^3-Y^3Z\), giving an embedded copy of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\) in its projective stabilizer. A complete enumeration of projective transformations in `check_stabilizer.py` shows stabilizer order \(24\), so this subgroup is the whole group. Its action on the four rational centers factors through \(A_4\).

## Verification
Run, from one directory containing the supplied files, `python verify_galois.py`, `python check_geometric_smoothness.py`, and `python check_stabilizer.py` (Python with NumPy and SymPy). The first reproduces the full census, representative catalogue and orbit partition. The second independently checks Jacobian ideals of all nineteen projective representatives: on each of the three affine charts the quartic and its partial derivatives generate the unit ideal over \(\mathbf F_3\). Every remaining curve is projectively equivalent to a representative, so this checks geometric smoothness across the full census. The third exhausts the invertible ternary matrices and verifies the exceptional projective stabilizer. The included three result text files give the exact output. The algebraic normal-form proof, rather than finite tests alone, justifies complete enumeration of every eligible curve.

## Relationship to prior work
Fukasawa's *Complete Determination of the Number of Galois Points for a Smooth Plane Curve* gives an algebraically closed-field classification of the **number** of geometric inner/outer Galois points; it does not give this arithmetic \(\mathbf F_3\) count or nineteen rational projective-orbit types. Fukasawa–Miura–Takahashi, *Quasi-Galois Points, II: Arrangements* (2022), Theorem 3.4, proves the pattern \(0,1,4\) for relevant quasi-Galois points under the explicit hypothesis of an algebraically closed field of **characteristic zero**, so it cannot imply these characteristic-three descent-sensitive arithmetic counts. In particular the numerical pattern's agreement is not itself claimed as original. The novel assertion is the finite-field arithmetic and projective orbit classification with explicit exceptional stabilizer.

## Limitations
Arithmetic inner points may be fewer than geometric inner points; the result does not classify all geometric Galois points after extension of constants, all smooth quartics, or abstract genus-three curve isomorphism classes. Enumeration is exact only for the stated finite field. Publication priority is subject to the possibility of an earlier unindexed finite-field or Galois-point table; absence from indexed searches is not a proof of worldwide novelty. The supplied computational checks do not constitute independent third-party certification.
