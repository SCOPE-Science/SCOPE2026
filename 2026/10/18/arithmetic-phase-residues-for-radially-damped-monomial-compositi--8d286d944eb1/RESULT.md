# Arithmetic phase residues for radially damped monomial composition commutators

## Finding
For any integers \(m,n\ge2\), unimodular \(a,b\), and \(0<r<1\), consider on \(H^2(\mathbb D)\) the mixed-adjoint commutator
\[
K_r=C_{ra z^m}^{*}C_{rb z^n}-C_{rb z^n}C_{ra z^m}^{*}.
\]
Here \(C_\varphi f=f\circ\varphi\). Put \(d=\gcd(m,n)\), \(p=m/d\), \(q=n/d\), \(L=m+n\), and \(\xi=b^{m-1}\overline a^{\,n-1}\).

**Phase-sensitive trace mass.** Every \(K_r\) is trace class and
\[
\lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)\|K_r\|_{\mathcal S_1}
=\frac{d-1}{L}+\int_0^\infty e^{-2u}
  \left(\int_{H_\xi}|1-e^{-(L-2)u}z|\,d\mu_\xi(z)\right)du=:R(\xi),
\]
where \(H_\xi\) is the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by \(\xi\) on the unit circle and \(\mu_\xi\) is its normalized Haar probability. If \(\xi\) has finite order \(Q\), its inner integral is the average over the \(Q\) roots \(\xi^0,\ldots,\xi^{Q-1}\); if \(\xi\) has infinite order, it is the normalized circular integral. In particular,
\[
R(1)=\frac12+\frac{d-2}{L},\qquad
R(-1)=\frac12+\frac{d-1}{L},\qquad R(\xi)>R(1)\ \text{for every }\xi\ne1.
\]
The map \(\xi\mapsto R(\xi)\) is discontinuous at \(1\): along irrational-angle phases converging to \(1\), it has the constant value corresponding to full-circle averaging, strictly above \(R(1)\).

**Quadratic mass and operator topologies.** The exact Hilbert--Schmidt norm is
\[
\|K_r\|_{\mathcal S_2}^2
=\frac{r^{2(p+q)}}{1-r^{2(p+q)}}+\frac{r^4}{1-r^4}
 -2\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{r^{L+2}\xi}{1-r^{L+2}\xi}\right),
\]
so
\[
\lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)\|K_r\|_{\mathcal S_2}^{2}
=\frac{d}{2L}+\frac14-\frac{2}{L+2}\mathbf1_{\{\xi=1\}}.
\]
The operators converge strongly to \(K_1=[C_{az^m}^*,C_{bz^n}]\), and \(K_1=0\) if and only if \(d=1\) and \(\xi=1\). In every case this convergence fails in operator norm. At the exceptional zero-limit resonance (\(d=1,\xi=1\)), the **strictly positive** norm-defect limit is
\[
\lim_{r\uparrow1}\|K_r\|=
\left(1-\frac2L\right)\left(\frac2L\right)^{2/(L-2)}.
\]
For example, \(m=2,n=3\) gives trace residues \(3/10\) at \(\xi=1\) and \(1/2\) at \(\xi=-1\), although the commutator converges strongly to zero at \(\xi=1\).

## Assumptions and scope
All operators act on the usual unweighted complex Hardy Hilbert space with orthonormal monomials \(e_k(z)=z^k\), \(k\ge0\). The fixed integer exponents are at least two; the same radial factor \(r\in(0,1)\) multiplies both monomial symbols, and their phases \(a,b\) lie on the unit circle. The results are limits with exponents and phases fixed as \(r\uparrow1\); no uniformity as \(\xi\) varies is asserted. The term \(\mathbf1_{\{\xi=1\}}\) is one at the trivial phase and zero elsewhere. Schatten norms include zero singular values only vacuously.

## Proof
**1. Exact singular columns.** By composition on monomials, \(C_{ra z^m}e_k=(ra)^k e_{mk}\), and its adjoint sends \(e_{mk}\) to \((r\bar a)^k e_k\) while annihilating monomials with index not divisible by \(m\). Write \(d=\gcd(m,n)\). The commutator vanishes on all \(e_j\) except indices \(j=p\ell\), \(\ell\ge1\). Its nonzero candidate columns are scalar multiples of pairwise distinct basis vectors \(e_{q\ell}\), with coefficients
\[
w_\ell=\begin{cases}
 r^{(p+q)\ell}(b^p\bar a^{\,q})^\ell,& d\nmid\ell,\\
 r^{Lh}(b^m\bar a^{\,n})^h-r^{2h}(b\bar a)^h,&\ell=dh.
\end{cases}
\]
(The coefficient of \(e_0\) is zero.) Hence \(K_r^*K_r\) is diagonal and the complete nonzero singular-value multiset is the multiset \(\{|w_\ell|:\ell\ge1\}\), without a hidden multiplicity factor. Its exponential decay makes \(K_r\) trace class. The same formulas with \(r=1\) define \(K_1\).

**2. Trace series.** The \(d\nmid\ell\) terms sum to
\[
\frac{r^{p+q}}{1-r^{p+q}}-\frac{r^L}{1-r^L}.
\]
The other terms have absolute value \(r^{2h}|1-r^{(L-2)h}\xi^h|\). Therefore
\[
\|K_r\|_1=\frac{r^{p+q}}{1-r^{p+q}}-\frac{r^L}{1-r^L}
 +\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}r^{2h}|1-r^{(L-2)h}\xi^h|.
\]
The first difference, multiplied by \(1-r\), tends to \(1/(p+q)-1/L=(d-1)/L\).

**3. Phase-weighted Riemann limit.** Write \(t=-\log r\), so \(t/(1-r)\to1\). The remaining contribution is \(t\sum_{h\ge1}F(th,\xi^h)\), where \(F(u,z)=e^{-2u}|1-e^{-(L-2)u}z|\). For a fixed cutoff \(T\), approximate this continuous function uniformly on \([0,T]\times H_\xi\) by functions constant on finitely many \(u\)-intervals. On any such interval, the sequence \(\xi^h\) has Cesàro distribution \(\mu_\xi\): by periodicity if \(\xi\) has finite order, and by the geometric-sum/Weyl criterion if its angle is irrational. Thus the interval contributions converge to the corresponding Haar-averaged integrals. The absolute tail is at most \(2t\sum_{h>T/t}e^{-2th}\), uniformly vanishing as \(T\to\infty\). This proves the displayed formula for \(R(\xi)\).

When \(\xi=1\), the inner absolute value is \(1-e^{-(L-2)u}\), giving \(1/2-1/L\). When \(\xi=-1\), the two-point average of \(|1-v|\) and \(|1+v|\) equals \(1\) for \(0\le v\le1\), giving \(1/2\). For any nontrivial phase subgroup, \(|1-vz|>|1-v|\) whenever \(v>0\) and \(z\ne1\); Haar averaging therefore gives a strictly larger residue than at the trivial phase. Irrational-angle subgroups are the entire circle, making their residue independent of irrational angle and producing the stated discontinuity.

**4. Hilbert--Schmidt identity.** Square each \(w_\ell\), sum the two disjoint arithmetic progressions, and use the geometric-series identity. The \(r^{2Lh}\) terms cancel, leaving exactly the stated three-term expression. If \(\xi=1\), its final term contributes \(-2/(L+2)\) after multiplication by \(1-r\); if \(\xi\ne1\), the fraction remains bounded and contributes zero. The two positive geometric terms contribute \(1/[2(p+q)]=d/(2L)\) and \(1/4\).

**5. Strong but not norm convergence.** Each \(C_{ra z^m}\) is a contraction fixing the constants, so \(\|K_r\|\le2\). Coefficientwise convergence on every basis vector and uniform boundedness give strong convergence \(K_r\to K_1\). If \(d>1\), infinitely many \(d\nmid\ell\) columns of \(K_1\) have unit weight; if \(d=1\) but \(\xi\ne1\), infinitely many weights \(|\xi^h-1|\) stay bounded away from zero. Thus \(K_1\) is noncompact in either situation, while each \(K_r\) is compact, excluding norm convergence. In the remaining case \(d=1,\xi=1\), all weights of \(K_1\) vanish, and \(\|K_r\|=\max_{h\ge1}(r^{2h}-r^{Lh})\). With \(u=-h\log r\), this grid becomes dense in \([0,\infty)\) and its supremum tends to the maximum of \(e^{-2u}-e^{-Lu}\). Differentiating yields the displayed strictly positive value. This also proves the asserted exact zero-commutator classification.

## Verification
Reproduce with `python check.py` using Python 3, NumPy, and SciPy. A separately constructed finite-basis matrix checks the exact column formula for nontrivial phases and noncoprime exponent pairs. Separate numerical sums check the Hilbert--Schmidt identity, both phase-residue formulas via quadrature, and the resonant norm limit. The packaged script reports 1520 passing assertions. Finite checks do not replace the analytic proof of limits or equidistribution.

## Relationship to prior work
Jiang (2014), arXiv:1410.1168, analyzes mixed-adjoint commutator compactness for **automorphic** symbols, so its automorphism classification does not cover the non-automorphic power maps with \(m,n\ge2\). Clifford--Dabkowski--Wiggins (2021), DOI 10.1515/conop-2020-0108, Section 2.3 and later results, derive the gcd-based monomial actions and analyze **numerical ranges of individual products**. That gcd reduction is known and is not claimed as new here; this result instead quantifies the trace- and Hilbert--Schmidt-mass of the **difference of the products** as the symbols become inner, including the finite-cyclic/full-circle arithmetic phase split and its strong/operator-norm separation. Heller--Pons (2017) treats monomial composition operators on weighted Hardy spaces; full comparison was limited by inaccessible text.

## Limitations
There is no assertion that the monomial adjoint action, gcd reduction, geometric series, or Weyl equidistribution themselves are new. The novelty claim is the combined exact norm-residue and topology/phase classification for the stated radial mixed commutators. No uniform residue asymptotic holds or is claimed while phase varies with \(r\). Equivalent prior special cases may exist under Schatten-regularization terminology, especially in material not accessible in full. The result does not address nonradial approximations, general analytic symbols, or independent priority verification.
