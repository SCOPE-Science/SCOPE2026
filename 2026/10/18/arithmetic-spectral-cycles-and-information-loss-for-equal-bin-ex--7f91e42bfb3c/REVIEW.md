# Scientific review — arithmetic spectral cycles and information loss

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses exact residue counting for the interval partition, digit concatenation for all transition powers, Fourier-cycle factorization for the complete spectrum, and the exact two-mass entropy calculation. The independence equivalence, arithmetic periodicity and asymptotic coefficients follow for all integer parameters. An exact-rational checker verifies 7,100 direct preimage intersection cells and 44 symbolic characteristic polynomials for sampled bases and resolutions, plus root-cycle identities. Such finite checks are corroborative rather than a general proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Binder and Wissman (2010, DOI: 10.1063/1.3298244) establish the transfer-matrix framework and analyze selected equal-bin Bernoulli shifts, including the three-bin nontrivial spectrum and four-bin cutoff. Their full six-page primary text was inspected. Those known examples are not claimed anew. The contribution is the unrestricted integer-base and equal-bin arithmetic theorem, closed divisor-order spectrum, exact information law and nonconstant residue-dependent asymptotic prefactors. Statement-level searches uncovered no equivalent theorem, but a different Fourier, automata or cyclic-word formulation remains a concrete possibility. A loosely related digital-channel article was available only at abstract level; no full-text exclusion is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem resolves a natural resolution-dependence question left implicit by the original three-/four-bin measurements: it identifies the exact boundary between finite information cutoff and infinite-lag exponential information loss, the complete spectral multiplicities, and periodic modulations hidden beneath a universal exponential rate. The simultaneous classification of spectral and information phenomena gives more than a routine individual matrix computation.

## Limits and audit status

Only Lebesgue-distributed integer multiplication maps and standard aligned equal bins are covered. There are no claims about unequal bins, shifted bins, noninteger maps or multi-time independence. Prior-work comparison is substantial but not an exhaustive originality guarantee.
