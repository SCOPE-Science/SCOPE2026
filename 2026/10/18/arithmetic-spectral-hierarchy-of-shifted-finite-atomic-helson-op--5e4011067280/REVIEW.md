# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite positive atomic measure at \(R+s_j\) produces a trace-class operator \(H_R=T_R^*T_R\) and the \(m\)-dimensional zeta Gram matrix \(G_R=T_RT_R^*\), with exactly the same nonzero eigenvalues. The exponential-polynomial zero lemma proves the leading evaluation vectors are linearly independent, so the Gram determinants \(\Delta_k\) are strictly positive for every \(k\le m\). The compound-operator expansion of \(\bigwedge^kG_R\) is a convergent sum of positive rank-one terms. The unique minimal integer product \(k!\) yields the leading coefficient, and every other product is at least \((k-1)!(k+1)\). The summable \(\zeta(2)\) tail controls the complete remainder uniformly for large \(R\). Consecutive top-eigenvalue product ratios then yield the individual eigenvalue expansion with the stated error. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof. The exact rational checker passes 294 finite diagnostic assertions.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

Ma, Lu and Zu [1] establish moment-representation, domain, and trace-class results; their exact trace identity does not imply the separate \(k\)-th eigenvalue constants. Perfekt and Pushnitski [2] already give the zeta-kernel correspondence and finite-rank characterization: these steps are prior work and are not asserted as new. Their Sections 5 and 6 do not state translated-atom eigenvalue asymptotics. Chavan, Sahu and Sinha [4] treat measure-induced norm estimates, Schatten criteria and scattering, not this atomic hierarchy. Miheisi and Pushnitski [3] treat infinite-rank eigenvalue index \(n\to\infty\) for a fixed logarithmic symbol rather than fixed \(k\) and atomic translation \(R\to\infty\). Published searches under Helson, multiplicative Hankel, atomic moments, zeta Gram matrices, exterior products and finite-rank spectral asymptotics revealed no statement already implying the simultaneous determinant-ratio law. The residual possibility of an equivalent general theorem for exponentially separated rank-one sums remains; originality is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For a natural finite-atomic subfamily singled out by the recent general moment-form framework, the result describes the entire positive spectrum at once, not only boundedness, rank or trace. Its arithmetic rates \(k^{-2R}\), sharp determinant-ratio coefficients, exponentially small errors, eventual eigenvalue simplicity, and sharp approximation numbers give an explicit spectral hierarchy. The family has a motivated role as a tractable spectral model for positive Helson operators. The claim is not an arbitrary finite numerical table or a marginal adjustment to a constant; it provides a parameter-independent rate classification for every fixed number of atoms.
