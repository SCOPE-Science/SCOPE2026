# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Direct midpoint perturbations prove each necessity; saturated support and signed edge conditions prove sufficiency. A mixed component gives an integer span ratio, and the common lattice forces all nontight edges to be flat. The transfer recurrence has a proved state invariant, and the packaged exact checker confirms 36 direct-enumeration layers, independently of the recurrence. A separate numerical polytope computation agrees in five low dimensions; no finite experiment is treated as proof of the infinite statement.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The primary 2023 bounded-Lipschitz paper, especially its finite-space Section 6, obtains norming sets and shows that certain local tests or extension schemes fail as complete classifications. The 2022 predecessor offers a general LP treatment of finite Dudley norms. An existing qualitative finite-metric tight-edge face criterion subsumes individual extremality testing, but does not give the new integer-walk canonical model or the polynomial-time exact total census. Targeted searches did not find equivalent enumerative work. An unindexed equivalent remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** For an infinite family of finite-dimensional BL Banach balls, the theorem replaces combinatorially large polytope enumeration with a proved polynomial-time exact vertex count, while identifying a special arithmetic rigidity of extreme functions. This matters for understanding the test-function geometry behind finite Dudley dual optimization, without claiming that the dual optimization itself becomes polynomial time.

Independent mathematical validation was not performed. The claim is limited to real unit-spaced finite paths and the additive BL norm, and no elementary closed formula for the full counting sequence is asserted.
