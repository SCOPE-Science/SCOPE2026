# Associating tuples of finite spin factors recover Witt type at two generators

## Finding
Let \(q\) be an odd prime power, let \(m\ge2\), and let \((V,B)\) be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear space of dimension \(2m\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its Witt sign \(\epsilon\in\{+,-\}\) corresponds to indices \(m\) and \(m-1\), respectively. Equip \(J_B=\mathbb F_q1\oplus V\) with the Jordan multiplication
\[
(a,u)\circ(b,v)=(ab+B(u,v),av+bu).
\]
An ordered \(s\)-tuple \((x_1,\ldots,x_s)\), where \(s\ge2\), generates an associative unital Jordan subalgebra **if and only if** its imaginary parts span a space of dimension at most one **or** a totally isotropic subspace of \(B\). Equivalently every associator among the tuple entries vanishes.

Define the Gaussian coefficient and isotropic-plane counts by
\[
{n\brack r}_q=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{q^{n-j}-1}{q^{r-j}-1},\quad
I_r^+(m,q)={m\brack r}_q\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^{m-1-j}+1),\quad
I_r^-(m,q)={m-1\brack r}_q\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^{m-j}+1).
\]
These apply for \(0\le r\le m\) in the plus case, and \(0\le r\le m-1\) in the minus case; \(I_0^\epsilon=1\), and the counts vanish outside these ranges. Put \(w_+=m\), \(w_-=m-1\), \(L=(q^{2m}-1)/(q-1)\), and \(R_{s,r}=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^s-q^j)\). The **exact number** of such ordered tuples is
\[
\boxed{A_{m,s}^\epsilon(q)=q^s\left[\sum_{r=0}^{\min(s,w_\epsilon)}I_r^\epsilon(m,q)R_{s,r}+(L-I_1^\epsilon(m,q))(q^s-1)\right].}
\]
Already the \(s=2\) count distinguishes Witt type at fixed \((m,q)\):
\[
\boxed{A_{m,2}^+(q)-A_{m,2}^-(q)=2q^{m+1}(q^2-1)(q^{2m-2}-1)>0.}
\]
Every single element generates an associative algebra, so arity two is the **sharp first arity** that can distinguish the signs using this statistic.

Geometrically, over an algebraic closure the reduced associating locus on imaginary-part matrices \(Y\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2m\times s}\) is exactly the union of the rank-at-most-one determinantal locus \(D\) and the orthogonal nullcone \(Z=\{Y:B(Y_i,Y_j)=0\ \forall i,j\}\). The locus \(D\) is irreducible of dimension \(2m+s-1\). For \(2\le s<m\), \(Z\) is irreducible of dimension \(2ms-s(s+1)/2\); for \(s\ge m\), \(Z\) has the two established spinor components, each of dimension \(ms+m(m-1)/2\). Consequently the **reduced associating locus** has two components for \(2\le s<m\) and three for \(s\ge m\). The full tuple locus is its product with \(\mathbb A^s\), so add \(s\) to each dimension. The orthogonal-nullcone component theorem itself is prior literature, not a new claim.

## Assumptions and scope
The field has odd characteristic and arbitrary odd prime-power order. The form is nondegenerate and even-dimensional with \(m\ge2\). All tuple indices (including repeated indices) are tested. Associativity means associativity of the **unital subalgebra generated by the tuple**, not merely a single parenthesized product. Counting is for ordered tuples, not orbit classes. The geometric assertions concern **reduced support** over an algebraic closure; they do not imply reducedness or absence of embedded scheme components of the original associator ideal.

## Proof
**1. Associator criterion.** Direct substitution shows
\[
((a,u)\circ(b,v))\circ(c,w)-(a,u)\circ((b,v)\circ(c,w))=(0,B(u,v)w-B(v,w)u).
\]
If the span \(U\) of imaginary parts has dimension at most one, the right side vanishes. If \(B|_{U\times U}=0\), it also vanishes. Conversely, suppose \(\dim U\ge2\) and all associators vanish. Trilinearity implies \(B(u,v)w=B(v,w)u\) for all \(u,v,w\in U\). Fix independent \(u,w\). Their coefficients must vanish, so \(B(u,v)=B(v,w)=0\) for every \(v\in U\). Repeating with each vector in a basis of \(U\) forces \(B|_U=0\). Both the rank-one and the nullcone possibilities give an associative unital subalgebra, as \(\mathbb F_q1+U\) is multiplicatively closed (and in the null case \(U\circ U=0\)). Thus the pointwise condition is equivalent to subalgebra associativity.

**2. Count nullcone tuples.** An ordered imaginary-part tuple of span exactly \(r\) uniquely determines its \(r\)-dimensional span. For each totally isotropic \(r\)-plane, the number of surjections \(\mathbb F_q^s\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb F_q^r\) is \(R_{s,r}\). The classical orthogonal-polar-space incidence count gives \(I_r^\epsilon(m,q)\) as displayed (in particular \(I_1^+=(q^{m-1}+1)(q^m-1)/(q-1)\), \(I_1^-=(q^m+1)(q^{m-1}-1)/(q-1)\)). Hence \(\sum_r I_r^\epsilon R_{s,r}\) counts the Gram-zero tuples without overlap.

**3. Add anisotropic lines.** Rank-one tuples not included above span a nonisotropic projective line. There are exactly \(L-I_1^\epsilon\) such lines. For each, \(q^s-1\) nonzero coordinate tuples span it. The two branches are disjoint outside their isotropic rank-one overlap, so this accounts for every associating tuple, and free scalar coordinates multiply by \(q^s\).

**4. Sharp two-generator distinction.** For \(s=2\), the rank-zero and rank-one contributions combine into \(1+L(q^2-1)\). The only remaining contribution is \(I_2^\epsilon(q^2-1)(q^2-q)\). Simplifying the displayed product formulas gives
\[
I_2^+(m,q)-I_2^-(m,q)=\frac{2q^{m-2}(q^{2m-2}-1)}{q-1}.
\]
Multiplication by \(q^2(q^2-1)(q^2-q)\) proves the positive difference. For \(s=1\), any singly generated Jordan algebra is power-associative (and here lies in \(\mathbb F_q1+\mathbb F_q u\)), so no separation is possible.

**5. Reduced components.** The associator criterion holds after all algebraic field extensions. Thus its zero set is \(D\cup Z\); the Nullstellensatz yields equality of reduced supports. Rank-one matrices form an irreducible determinantal variety of dimension \(2m+s-1\); since anisotropic rank-one matrices exist, \(D\not\subset Z\). As \(m\ge2\) and \(s\ge2\), rank-two totally isotropic spans exist after algebraic closure, hence \(Z\not\subset D\). For \(s<m\), the rank-\(s\) stratum of \(Z\) fibers over \(\operatorname{OG}(s,2m)\) with full-row-rank coordinate matrices, yielding dimension \(s(2m-s)-s(s+1)/2+s^2=2ms-s(s+1)/2\), and a single irreducible closure. For \(s\ge m\), maximal-isotropic \(m\)-planes lie in two spinor families; each family has dimension \(m(m-1)/2\) and fiber dimension \(ms\), giving two irreducible closures. This is precisely the known orthogonal-nullcone component theorem of Hochster–Jeffries–Pandey–Singh, Theorem 7.13 (2023); only its incorporation into the **Jordan associating locus** is used here. All scalar coordinates are free.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_spin_factor.py` with ordinary Python 3. The checker independently evaluates all ordered binary associator equations over prime fields in dimensions four and six and compares with the closed isotropic-plane formula. Recorded imaginary-vector pair counts: \((m,q)=(2,3)\) yield \(705\) (plus) and \(321\) (minus); \((2,5)\) yield \(9505\) and \(3745\); \((3,3)\) yield \(27873\) and \(16353\). The complete Jordan tuple counts multiply these numbers by \(q^2\). Output was `VERIFY_OK`. Additional arithmetic consistency tests cover \(m=2,3,4\), \(q=3,5,7\), \(s=2,3,4\). Finite verification corroborates but does not prove the formulas for all \(q,m,s\), which follow from the counting argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Hamhalter–Turilova (arXiv:1111.7240, 2011) investigate associative Jordan operator subalgebras and their reconstruction properties, including real Hilbert-space spin factors; their positivity assumptions exclude the isotropic phenomena of finite fields. Hall–Rehren–Shpectorov (arXiv:1403.1898, 2014) treat Clifford-type Jordan algebras and structural generation, without these arithmetic tuple-count distributions. The entire rank-zero Gram locus and, in particular, its split two-spinor component theorem are **already covered** by Hochster–Jeffries–Pandey–Singh (arXiv:2210.09351, Theorem 7.13); the present proof relies on that established geometric result. Polynomial identities for algebras of symmetric bilinear forms (arXiv:2009.02530) give separate identity-theoretic context, not this Witt-sign pair-count comparison. The claimed contribution is the combined associator classification with the additional rank-one branch and exact finite-field Witt-sensitive tuple enumeration, including sharp arity two. Existing general Gaussian polar-space counts are not presented as new.

## Limitations
The statement does not treat characteristic two, degenerate forms, odd-dimensional forms, or other Jordan families. The geometry is set-theoretic/reduced, not an assertion about the defining ideal's scheme structure. The arithmetic formulas themselves are assembled from classical finite orthogonal geometry and may be obtainable as consequences of unlocated treatments of finite Jordan-algebra associativity; a literature search cannot certify priority. The computation tests only selected prime fields and does not independently certify the geometric component theorem.
