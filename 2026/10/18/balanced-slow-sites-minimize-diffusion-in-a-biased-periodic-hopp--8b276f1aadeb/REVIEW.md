# Scientific review: balanced slow-site transport

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes a finite period, strictly positive unequal clockwise and counterclockwise site-clock rates, positive two-value holding resistances, and an integer number of slow sites. Direct stationary flux gives the drift and entropy-production rate. A finite-state Poisson corrector produces the displacement variance as a sum of squares; inversion of a cyclic first-difference matrix yields the explicit positive pair kernel. Strict convexity of the cyclic exponential interaction proves the unique maximum at one block and the unique minimum at a rational mechanical pattern, up to rotation. The proof includes the all-size equality cases, not merely numerical enumeration. An independent exact-rational finite-generator checker passed 336 variance comparisons, 56 exhaustive small-size extrema families, and 56 bias-boundary tests.

## Originality — PASS with stated residual risks
Derrida (1983), Koza (1999), Zhang (2009), and Guérin–Dean (2015) give general transport calculations and constitute important prior coverage. The claim does not assert discovery of those formulas, the stationary current, or discrete convexity. Inspected primary text did not establish the specific all-period, fixed-binary-inventory diffusion optimization and uniqueness of its two extrema. A general transport formula allows computation of each arrangement but does not by itself state which arrangements globally optimize dispersion. An equivalent historical rearrangement treatment could still exist, especially in disordered-hopping literature. This qualification is not an independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
At identical mean drift and steady entropy dissipation, merely rearranging a fixed quantity of slow material sharply changes stochastic dispersion. The theorem provides an exact all-size solution to both extrema of this natural design problem, and identifies the minimizing balanced and maximizing clustered microstructures. The contrast between equivalent macroscopic current and inequivalent fluctuation response is useful beyond evaluation of one special lattice configuration.

## Limits
The strict extremal statement excludes zero bias and one-way motion, and is restricted to two site-clock resistance values with positive rates and a single noninteracting walker. The analytical theorem does not claim extension to edge-clock variations, heterogeneous directional biases, or infinite random disorder. Complete independent bibliographic and mathematical validation has not occurred.
