# Scientific review: beta disintegration and bulk covariance of bipartite Lipschitz dual balls

## Correctness
PASS. For every integer pair \(p,q\ge2\), conditioning on first-part extrema yields an exact \(r^{p-2}(2-r)^q\) density, which normalizes to a \(\operatorname{Beta}(p-1,q+1)\) law for the half-range. The unit Jacobian in the anchor and the remaining \(r^{p-2}\) powers follow from an explicit change of coordinates; the conditionally independent uniforms recover the original body. Independent beta and uniform moment identities give all three invariant covariance eigenvalues, and the determinant on the zero-sum Euclidean subspace gives the isotropic constant. Uniform Stirling bounds and the exact beta–gamma coupling give the asserted limits with their quantifiers and independence. The standalone exact-rational geometric integration program passed eight full-polytope volume and matrix-moment checks. Those computations corroborate, but do not establish, the arbitrary-dimensional theorem.

## Originality
PASS, subject to ordinary unindexed-literature uncertainty. The closest complete-bipartite primary source [1] evaluates facet and Ehrhart data, not continuous uniform interior moment tensors. The Lipschitz-free geometric study [2] treats volume products and Hanner geometry, not the three-sector covariance or the distributional limit. A published Hilbert-distance result for precisely the same dual ball optimizes quadratic forms over its extreme points, which does not imply their uniform-interior second moments. Earlier results on two-hub book metrics cover a two-element first group only, while the new exact formula includes arbitrary \(p\ge3\), and integer height-function range laws concern a discrete ensemble. Searches also compared covariance, isotropic constant, conditional-beta, Gamma–Gaussian and equivalent signed-chain formulations; no covering result was identified. The overlap comparison is about statements and mathematical implications rather than terminology, and absence from these searches is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Bulk covariance and the affine-invariant isotropic constant are intrinsic quantitative invariants of finite-dimensional Banach balls, distinct from boundary facets and volumes. The completely evaluated three-representation decomposition works for every pair of part sizes, is directly usable for exact random sampling and integrated quadratic observables, and reveals a uniform high-dimensional limit across arbitrarily imbalanced parts. The fixed-small-part Gamma–Gaussian transition is a genuinely distributional statement not given by counting vertices or a volume identity. In particular, it resolves a natural two-parameter question rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Residual limitations
The result concerns the real Lipschitz **dual** ball of unit-edge complete bipartite graphs only, not its primal transportation-cost ball or arbitrary edge weightings. The Gamma–Gaussian theorem assumes a fixed first part and growing second part. The finite checker uses numerical hull triangulation but exact integer/rational integration for all volumes and moments. An equivalent unindexed calculation or a general formula not recognized under another description remains possible. Independent scientific and bibliographic audits are outstanding.
