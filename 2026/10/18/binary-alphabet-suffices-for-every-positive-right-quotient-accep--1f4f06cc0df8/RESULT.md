# Binary alphabet suffices for every positive right-quotient accepting-state complexity of permutation languages

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma=\{a,b\}\) be an alphabet of exactly two letters. For a regular language \(L\), let \(\operatorname{asc}(L)\) be the minimum number of final states among complete deterministic finite automata recognizing \(L\). A permutation automaton is a complete deterministic automaton in which each letter permutes the states. For languages \(K,L\subseteq\Sigma^*\), define their right quotient by
\[
KL^{-1}=\{x\in\Sigma^*: \text{there exists }y\in L\text{ with }xy\in K\}.
\]
For every two nonnegative integers \(m,n\), the exact **fixed-binary-alphabet** spectrum of right-quotient accepting-state complexity for permutation languages is
\[
\left\{\operatorname{asc}(KL^{-1}):\ K,L\subseteq\Sigma^*\text{ accepted by permutation automata},\ \operatorname{asc}(K)=m,\ \operatorname{asc}(L)=n\right\}
=\begin{cases}\{0\},&m=0\text{ or }n=0,\\ \mathbb N_{>0},&m,n\ge1.\end{cases}
\]
The binary alphabet is **optimal** for the unbounded positive spectrum: over one letter, the corresponding spectrum for positive \(m,n\) is exactly \(\{1,\ldots,mn\}\), by Rauch and Holzer [1]. The new assertion is sufficiency of *two* letters, not the previously established unrestricted-alphabet spectrum.

More explicitly, for every \(m,n\ge1\) and every \(\alpha\ge m\), the proof supplies a fixed two-letter pair of minimal permutation automata whose accepting-state complexities are respectively \(m,n\) and whose quotient has accepting-state complexity \(\alpha\). For \(1\le\alpha<m\), the previously established unary witnesses [1] supply binary witnesses after duplicating their one letter.

## Assumptions and scope

All automata are deterministic, complete and finite. Accepting-state complexity is minimized over **all** complete deterministic automata, and equals the number of final states in the ordinary minimal DFA [1,2]. Initial states need not be final. Zero accepting-state complexity occurs precisely for the empty language. Permutation actions and group products use right-action convention: a word \(uv\) acts by first \(u\), then \(v\).

The new high-range construction is valid for all integers \(m,n\ge1\) and \(\alpha\ge m\), including \(m=n=\alpha=1\). Set \(k=\alpha+1\ge2\) and \(d=n+1\ge2\). Write \([k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), and use second coordinates in \(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\). Let \(\sigma=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ k)\) and \(\tau=(1\ 2)\).

## Proof

**Step 1. The binary witness pair.** Define \(A\) on the state set \([k]\), initial state \(1\), final set \(F_A=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and transitions
\[
p\cdot_A a=\sigma(p),\qquad p\cdot_A b=\tau(p).
\]
Define \(B\) on \([k]\times\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\), with initial state \((k,0)\), final set
\[
F_B=\{(k,j):0\le j\le d-2\},
\]
and transitions
\[
(p,j)\cdot_B a=(\sigma(p),j+1),\qquad(p,j)\cdot_B b=(\tau(p),j),
\]
where addition in the second coordinate is modulo \(d\). Both automata are permutation automata and \(|F_A|=m\), \(|F_B|=d-1=n\).

**Step 2. Full direct-product action using two generators.** The transformations induced by \(a\) and \(b\) on \(B\) are \((\sigma,1)\) and \((\tau,0)\) in \(S_k\times\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\). Under the right-action composition, \(\sigma\tau\) (first \(\sigma\), then \(\tau\)) fixes \(1\) and is a cycle of length \(k-1\) on \(\{2,\ldots,k\}\); for \(k=2\), it is the identity. Therefore
\[
a^k=(\operatorname{id},k),\qquad (ab)^{k-1}=(\operatorname{id},k-1).
\]
Because \(\gcd(k,k-1)=1\), these two transformations generate every pure counter shift \((\operatorname{id},j)\), including \((\operatorname{id},1)\), modulo arbitrary \(d\). Having all pure shifts, one obtains \((\sigma,0)=(\sigma,1)(\operatorname{id},-1)\) as well as \((\tau,0)\). Since \(\sigma,\tau\) generate the full symmetric group \(S_k\), the transformations generated by \(a,b\) are **exactly**
\[
S_k\times\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
\]
There is no restriction from requiring positive words: a submonoid generated by permutations on a finite set is a group, so inverses are positive powers. This argument works even when \(d\) is not coprime to \(k\): only the consecutive integers \(k\) and \(k-1\) must be coprime.

**Step 3. Minimality of the two source automata.** In \(A\), transitivity of \(S_k\) ensures all states are reachable. If \(p\ne q\), choose a symmetric-group element sending \(p\) into \(F_A\) and \(q\) to \(k\notin F_A\). Such an element is induced by a word, so the two states are distinguishable. Thus \(A\) is minimal and \(\operatorname{asc}(L(A))=m\).

The product-group action makes all \(kd\) states of \(B\) reachable from \((k,0)\). For two states \((p,i)\ne(q,j)\), if \(p\ne q\), choose a permutation sending \(p\) to \(k\) and \(q\) away from \(k\), together with a counter shift sending \(i\) to \(0\). The first state becomes final and the second remains nonfinal. If \(p=q\) but \(i\ne j\), send \(p\) to \(k\) and shift the counters so that \(j\) becomes \(d-1\); the other becomes a different, hence final, residue. Thus all distinct states are distinguishable, \(B\) is minimal and \(\operatorname{asc}(L(B))=d-1=n\).

**Step 4. Exact projected action of accepted suffixes.** Denote by \(\rho_w\in S_k\) the first-coordinate permutation induced by a word \(w\). From initial state \((k,0)\), a word belongs to \(L(B)\) exactly when \(\rho_w(k)=k\) and its counter increment is not \(d-1\). It follows that every accepted word induces an element of the point stabilizer \(H=\operatorname{Stab}_{S_k}(k)\). Conversely, each \(h\in H\) is realized jointly with counter increment \(0\), because Step 2 gives the full direct product. Its realizing word is accepted, since \(0\ne d-1\) for \(d\ge2\). Hence
\[
\{\rho_w:w\in L(B)\}=H.
\]

**Step 5. The quotient is minimal and has exactly \(\alpha\) final states.** By the right-quotient final-set construction, \(L(A)L(B)^{-1}\) is recognized on \(A\)'s transition graph with final set
\[
\widetilde F=\bigcup_{w\in L(B)}\rho_w^{-1}(F_A)=\bigcup_{h\in H}h^{-1}(F_A).
\]
Since \(H\) fixes \(k\), preserves \([k-1]\) and acts transitively on \([k-1]\), while \(\emptyset\ne F_A\subseteq[k-1]\), this union is precisely \([k-1]\). That quotient DFA is minimal: all its states are reachable under \(S_k\), and any two distinct states can be separated by a permutation sending exactly one to the unique nonfinal state \(k\). Its accepting-state complexity therefore equals \(k-1=\alpha\).

**Step 6. Complete spectrum and alphabet threshold.** For \(m,n\ge1\), the construction covers every \(\alpha\ge m\). The unary theorem of Rauch and Holzer [1, Corollary 32 of the 2022 preprint] supplies, for every \(1\le\alpha\le mn\), unary permutation-language witnesses. Replace the sole unary letter by two letters inducing the *same* permutation in each witness. This preserves both minimal automata and their right quotient (each binary word acts as the unary word of the same length). In particular, it covers all \(1\le\alpha<m\), since \(mn\ge m\). Thus every positive \(\alpha\) is attained on exactly two letters.

For positive \(m,n\), zero cannot occur: choose an accepted word \(y\in L\) and a final state \(f\) in an accessible permutation automaton for \(K\). The word \(y\) acts bijectively; the inverse image of \(f\) is reachable by some word \(x\). Thus \(xy\in K\), so \(x\in KL^{-1}\). A nonempty language cannot have accepting-state complexity zero. If \(m=0\) or \(n=0\), one input is empty and the quotient is empty, and any prescribed other input complexity can be obtained with a cyclic permutation automaton over the same binary alphabet (using both letters as the cycle). This proves both stated cases.

The published unary equality \(\{1,\ldots,mn\}\) [1] excludes unbounded positive spectra on a one-letter alphabet for fixed positive \(m,n\); hence two letters are the smallest possible alphabet for the full spectrum.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` directly builds the two-letter transition tables rather than relying on a symbolic permutation-package implementation. It exhaustively enumerates the generated finite transformation monoid, checks its cardinality against \(k!d\), independently minimizes both input automata by right-language partition refinement, projects every accepted transformation of \(B\), constructs the quotient final set directly, and minimizes that quotient.

For each \(k\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}\), each \(d\in\{2,3,4,5\}\), and every \(1\le m<k\), the 60 resulting triples passed; the largest enumerated group had 3600 elements. This verifies all these finite instances but is not a proof for unbounded parameters. The general proof is Steps 1–6 above. ## Relationship to prior work

Rauch and Holzer [1] proved the unary interval \(\{1,\ldots,mn\}\) and the standard right-quotient final-set formula. German [2] established the full unrestricted-alphabet spectrum but used **three letters** in its high-range witness: two letters to generate \(S_k\) and a third independent letter to advance the counter. The present theorem retains the stabilizer-saturation idea from [2]; its new point is a **two-generator direct-product realization** that compresses the independent counter letter into the symmetric-group cycle. The coprime exponents \(k\) and \(k-1\) prove that this compression works for every counter length \(d\).

German [3] studied **reversal**, not right quotient, and its binary constructions do not imply the present fixed-alphabet quotient statement. Other work on ordinary state complexity of permutation languages concerns a different numerical invariant. Thus the claim is specifically a sharp binary-alphabet refinement of [2], not a re-claim of its unrestricted spectrum, of the unary range, or of the stabilizer-final-set technique itself.

## Limitations

No assertion is made about minimizing the **number of states** in the witness automata, about binary syntactic monoid size, or about simultaneous quotients of several languages. The low-range proof invokes the established unary realization theorem [1]; the new argument is the explicit high-range binary construction. Searches cannot exclude an unindexed or differently formulated earlier binary-alphabet construction. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
