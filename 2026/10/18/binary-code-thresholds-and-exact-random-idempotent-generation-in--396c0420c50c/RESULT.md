# Binary-code thresholds and exact random idempotent generation in finite quiver algebras

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, let \(Q\) be any finite quiver with nonempty vertex set \(V\), and let \(A=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the arrow ideal. Write \(m_{ij}=\dim_k e_iJe_j\) for the number of arrows from \(i\) to \(j\) in the corner convention \(e_iJe_j\). Parallel arrows and loops are allowed. Every subalgebra generated below is understood to contain the identity and all scalars of \(k\).

For nonnegative integers \(t,s\), define the finite-field surjection count
\[
R_q(t,s)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(q^t-q^r),&t\ge s,\\0,&t<s,\end{cases}
\]
with \(R_q(t,0)=1\) for \(t\ge0\). For words \(c_i,c_j\in\{0,1\}^d\), let \(h(c_i,c_j)\) be their Hamming distance. Define \(E_d(Q,q)\) to be the number of ordered \(d\)-tuples of idempotents that generate \(A\), where \(d\ge1\).

**Theorem (exact conditional generation law).** If any \(m_{ii}>0\), then \(E_d(Q,q)=0\) for every \(d\). If every \(m_{ii}=0\), then
\[
\boxed{E_d(Q,q)=\sum_{c:V\hookrightarrow\{0,1\}^d}\ \prod_{i\ne j}q^{m_{ij}}R_q\bigl(h(c_i,c_j)-1,m_{ij}\bigr).}
\]
The sum is over all injective assignments of binary words to the vertices. Factors with \(m_{ij}=0\) equal one. A sum with no admissible assignments is zero.

The exact number of idempotents in \(A\), whether or not loops occur, is
\[
\boxed{T_Q(q)=\sum_{\epsilon:V\to\{0,1\}}q^{\sum_{i\ne j}m_{ij}\mathbf1_{\epsilon_i\ne\epsilon_j}}.}
\]
Consequently, the generation probability for \(d\) independently and uniformly selected idempotents is exactly \(E_d(Q,q)/T_Q(q)^d\).

**Theorem (sharp coding-theoretic threshold).** For a loopless quiver, the least *positive* number of idempotent algebra generators is the least \(d\ge1\) admitting an injection \(c:V\to\{0,1\}^d\) with
\[
 h(c_i,c_j)\ge m_{ij}+1\quad\text{whenever }m_{ij}>0.
\]
This minimal number is independent of \(q\), although the exact generation probabilities depend on \(q\). For a complete underlying graph with precisely \(M\ge1\) parallel arrows between each unordered pair, oriented in one direction per pair, the threshold equals the least \(d\) for which the classical binary-code bound \(A_2(d,M+1)\ge |V|\) holds, where \(A_2(d,\delta)\) is the largest size of a length-\(d\) binary code of minimum distance \(\delta\). For \(M=1\) and \(|V|\ge2\), the least number is exactly \(1+\lceil\log_2|V|\rceil\).

**Orientation-sensitive boundary.** On two vertices with two arrows, compare \(Q_{\parallel}\) (both arrows in the same direction) with \(Q_{\leftrightarrow}\) (one arrow in each direction). They have identical unoriented arrow multiplicities and identical idempotent totals \(T_{Q_{\parallel}}(q)=T_{Q_{\leftrightarrow}}(q)=2+2q^2\). But \(E_2(Q_{\parallel},q)=0\) whereas \(E_2(Q_{\leftrightarrow},q)=4q^4(1-q^{-1})^2\); in particular, at \(q=2\) the counts are zero and sixteen, respectively. Thus the idempotent-generation invariant detects oriented arrow-space splittings invisible to the total idempotent count.

## Assumptions and scope
All finite fields are covered, including characteristic two and extension fields. The vertex set is distinguished for the counting formula but no ordering of its elements is required. Quivers may contain oriented cycles: the quotient by \(J^2\) still has only length-zero and length-one paths. The theorem counts *embedded ordered tuples*, not automorphism orbits, and treats generation as unital \(k\)-algebra generation. The coding threshold is for loopless quivers only; any loop forces failure of all idempotent generation, because idempotents cannot have a loop component.

## Proof
Every element has unique form \(x=\sum_i\lambda_i e_i+\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\), with \(x_{ij}\in e_iJe_j\). Products of two radical elements vanish, and the radical corner of a product is \((xy)_{ij}=\lambda_i y_{ij}+x_{ij}\mu_j\). Thus an idempotent must satisfy \(\lambda_i^2=\lambda_i\), forcing \(\lambda_i\in\{0,1\}\), together with \((\lambda_i+\lambda_j-1)x_{ij}=0\). For \(i=j\), the coefficient \(2\lambda_i-1\) is always a unit, even in characteristic two, so every loop coordinate vanishes. For \(i\ne j\), a radical coordinate is freely selectable exactly when \(\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j\). Summing its \(q^{m_{ij}}\) choices over binary vertex assignments proves the formula for \(T_Q(q)\). If a loop exists, the space generated by all idempotents is contained in the subalgebra with zero loop coordinates, so the claimed zero result follows.

Assume henceforth that there are no loops. Fix a tuple \(x_1,\dots,x_d\) of idempotents, with binary diagonal word \(c_i=(\lambda_{1i},\dots,\lambda_{di})\) at vertex \(i\). The diagonal image of the algebra \(B=k\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle\) is all of \(k^V\) precisely when the words \(c_i\) are distinct: coincident evaluations cannot be separated, whereas polynomial interpolation on pairwise distinct points of \(k^d\) gives each coordinate idempotent. Hence injectivity is necessary.

Fix injective labels. For distinct \(i,j\), let \(U_{ij}:k^d\to e_iJe_j\) take the \(r\)-th coordinate vector to the radical component \((x_r)_{ij}\), and put \(\Delta_{ij}=(\lambda_{1i}-\lambda_{1j},\dots,\lambda_{di}-\lambda_{dj})\ne0\). **Block criterion:** \(B=A\) if and only if every \(U_{ij}|_{\ker\Delta_{ij}}\) is surjective.

For necessity, expand the \((i,j)\)-component of each noncommutative word in the generators. Since \(J^2=0\), each term contains at most one radical factor. The coefficient vector \(\alpha(w)\in k^d\) of the resulting linear combination of \((x_r)_{ij}\) satisfies the telescoping identity \(\Delta_{ij}\cdot\alpha(w)=w(c_i)-w(c_j)\). Linearity extends this to any polynomial. If a polynomial evaluates to an element of \(B\cap J\), the two diagonal evaluations vanish, so its \((i,j)\)-component lies in \(U_{ij}(\ker\Delta_{ij})\). Full generation forces surjectivity.

For sufficiency, because the diagonal image of \(B\) is all of \(k^V\), for each \(i\) choose \(b_i\in B\) with \(b_i\equiv e_i\pmod J\). For every \(v\in J\), one has \(b_i v b_j=e_i v e_j\), since \(J^2=0\). The \((i,j)\)-component of the commutator \([x_r,x_s]\) is \(\Delta_{ij,r}(x_s)_{ij}-\Delta_{ij,s}(x_r)_{ij}\). These vectors span exactly \(U_{ij}(\ker\Delta_{ij})\) as \(r,s\) vary, because \(\Delta_{ij}\ne0\). Sandwiching such commutators between \(b_i\) and \(b_j\) therefore puts the entire corner \(e_iJe_j\) in \(B\) whenever the block map is onto. All corners are onto by hypothesis, and there are no loop corners. Hence \(J\subset B\). Its diagonal image is already surjective, so \(B=A\).

For an idempotent tuple the map \(U_{ij}\) has zero columns at exactly those positions for which \(c_i\) and \(c_j\) agree. Write \(h=h(c_i,c_j)\). On the remaining \(h\) positions, \(\Delta_{ij}\) has nonzero entries (each is \(1\) or \(-1\)), and \(U_{ij}\) is a freely chosen linear map from \(k^h\) into \(k^{m_{ij}}\). Restriction to its \((h-1)\)-dimensional hyperplane \(\ker\Delta_{ij}\) is onto in exactly \(R_q(h-1,m_{ij})\) ways, while the missing one-dimensional value can be assigned in \(q^{m_{ij}}\) ways. Distinct arrow corners have independent free coordinates, and multiplying the counts over all corners and summing over the injective labels proves \(E_d(Q,q)\).

Positivity of \(R_q(h-1,m)\) for positive \(m\) is equivalent to \(h\ge m+1\). This proves the coding threshold and its independence from \(q\). In the complete \(M\)-arrow case this is exactly the classical binary-code minimum-distance condition. When \(M=1\), any such code is an independent set of the \(d\)-cube. A perfect matching of the cube bounds its size by \(2^{d-1}\), while the even-parity code attains this bound. Therefore \(A_2(d,2)=2^{d-1}\) and the formula for the least \(d\) follows. The two-vertex orientation calculation follows by taking Hamming distance two for the four injective assignments when \(d=2\). \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone program `check_idempotent_generation.py` constructs finite-field radical-square-zero multiplication independently, enumerates every idempotent in each tested algebra, and closes the \(k\)-linear span of each tuple under multiplication until stabilization. It compares these direct generated-algebra dimensions with the Hamming-surjection formula. Run `python3 check_idempotent_generation.py`; the final output should read `ALL_IDEMPOTENT_GENERATOR_CASES_PASSED 14`. It includes \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_4\), loops, parallel arrows, opposing arrows, and a three-vertex directed cycle. The separate full-rank factor is not used in the direct closure test. These are finite checks, not a proof for all finite fields or quivers; the preceding argument supplies the general proof.

Exact direct enumeration gives eight generating idempotent pairs among thirty-six ordered pairs for the two-vertex single-arrow algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\); sixteen among one hundred for opposing arrows; none for two parallel same-direction arrows; and 768 generating triples in the three-vertex directed path algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Hu and Xiao (available online January 11, 2021) characterize finite-dimensional algebras generated by idempotents through module-theoretic conditions, including absence of self-extensions of one-dimensional simple modules in the split setting. Their Theorem 2.11 answers whether *some* idempotents generate an algebra; it does not enumerate ordered generating tuples or identify optimal tuple sizes with binary error-correcting codes. That qualitative existence theorem is not claimed here.

Rowen and Segev (arXiv:1609.04899, first publicly posted September 16, 2016) describe associative algebras generated by two idempotents, including constraints from the central element \((a-b)^2\). Their Theorem 1.1 may recover qualitative two-generator special cases, but does not supply the all-\(d\), all-quiver finite-field formula or the coding reduction.

Sercombe and Shalev (arXiv:2009.01115) analyze random generation of finite associative algebras by unrestricted or nilpotent elements and related rank-conditioned sampling. Their displayed results do not count tuples sampled uniformly from the idempotents of a quiver algebra. An earlier exact *unrestricted-element* generation formula for radical-square-zero quiver algebras supplies a useful general block-surjectivity criterion; conditioning that criterion on idempotency yields part of the present proof. Its unconditional generating-tuple probability, however, does not determine the idempotent conditional distribution or its extremal binary-code threshold. The present claim is specifically that new conditional distribution and coding connection, not the pre-existing block criterion.

## Limitations
The enumeration assumes radical square zero and split vertex scalars; it does not address higher radical layers. The general coding criterion is exact but inherits unsolved extremal binary-code quantities for some complete multi-arrow graphs. The result concerns the probability of a *uniformly random idempotent* and should not be conflated with unrestricted-element random generation. The literature search and primary-source comparisons do not establish exhaustive priority: equivalent conditional enumerations or binary-code reductions in older work remain possible. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
