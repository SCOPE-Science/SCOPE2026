# Scientific review: idempotent generation of radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Correctness — PASS
The idempotent equation forces binary vertex labels and zeros in loop coordinates. In a fixed off-diagonal arrow corner, nonzero idempotent coordinates occur only at label-disagreement positions. The general block-surjectivity criterion follows directly from word expansion, a telescoping difference identity, and isolating commutators by lifts of diagonal coordinate vectors. Restricting to the disagreement positions yields exactly \(q^m R_q(h-1,m)\) valid coordinate assignments per corner. The proof handles arbitrary finite fields and all finite quivers, with a separate zero-loop obstruction. Fourteen fully enumerated examples agree with the formula; these computations are corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected coverage
Hu–Xiao establish the qualitative idempotent-generation criterion, not the conditional tuple distribution. Rowen–Segev provide the structural classification for two idempotents, which can constrain special cases but does not enumerate every ordered tuple in the family. Sercombe–Shalev investigate unrestricted/nilpotent random generation and related sampling. A previous exact unrestricted generation result on the same quiver family gives a block-rank criterion but neither the idempotent law nor the binary-code reduction as a stated invariant. The direct conditioning step is acknowledged, so novelty is limited to the exact conditioned census, sharp coding-theoretic generator threshold, and orientation-sensitive boundary. It remains possible that these have equivalent treatments in older algebra-generation or coding literature.

## Value — PASS
Idempotents are a canonical generating class for finite-dimensional algebras, and their optimal generation size is a natural invariant distinct from unrestricted rank. The theorem determines every finite-field probability, proves that the optimal idempotent-generator number is independent of the field size, and reduces an infinite class of algebra questions to sharp binary-code capacity. Even a two-vertex pair exhibits a measurable orientation sensitivity hidden from total idempotent counts. These are structural and quantitative gains, not just an isolated parameter computation.

## Limits
This concerns split radical-square-zero quiver algebras, unital algebra generation, and uniformly sampled idempotents. Some general binary-code thresholds are not expressed by elementary formulas. Bibliographic completeness and external proof verification remain open.
