# Binomial hypersurface charts and codimension-three singularities of cycle graphical-zonotope toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(C_n\) be the undirected cycle on vertices \(1,\ldots,n\), and let \(Z_n\subset M_\mathbb R=\{(u_i)\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i u_i=0\}\) be its graphical zonotope, the Minkowski sum of the segments \([0,e_i-e_{i+1}]\), with cyclic subscripts. Let \(X_n\) be the normal projective toric variety of the normal fan of \(Z_n\), over an algebraically closed field \(K\) of arbitrary characteristic.

The \(2^n-2\) torus-fixed points are indexed by the acyclic orientations of \(C_n\). If an orientation directs \(k\) of its \(n\) edges in the designated cyclic direction and \(n-k\) against it, its entire torus-invariant affine chart is canonically isomorphic, after naming the edge characters, to
\[
U_k=\operatorname{Spec}K[a_1,\ldots,a_k,b_1,\ldots,b_{n-k}]/(a_1\cdots a_k-b_1\cdots b_{n-k}),
\qquad 1\le k\le n-1.
\]
For \(k=1\) or \(k=n-1\), this chart is affine \((n-1)\)-space. For \(2\le k\le n-2\), its reduced singular locus is the union of the coordinate linear spaces obtained by setting any two distinct \(a\)-coordinates and any two distinct \(b\)-coordinates to zero. Each such space has dimension \(n-4\).

Consequently, \(X_3\) is smooth. For every \(n\ge4\), the reduced singular locus of \(X_n\) is nonempty and pure of dimension \(n-4\), hence **codimension three**. Exactly \(2^n-2n-2\) torus-fixed points are singular; among these, the number with Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity \(m\) is
\[
\sum_{\substack{2\le k\le n-2\\\min(k,n-k)=m}}\binom nk.
\]
Every \(X_n\) is normal, locally a hypersurface, Cohen--Macaulay, and Gorenstein. In particular \(X_4\) has exactly six isolated ordinary threefold nodes, while \(X_5\) has a pure one-dimensional singular locus containing exactly twenty singular torus-fixed points.

## Assumptions and scope
The lattice of one-parameter subgroups is \(N=\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\), with dual \(M=\{(v_i)\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum v_i=0\}\). The polygonal cycle has \(n\ge3\) distinct edges. A torus-fixed chart means the affine toric open set associated with the full-dimensional normal cone at one vertex of the polytope; not merely the completed local ring. Singular loci and dimensions are reduced geometric loci. All assertions hold in arbitrary characteristic.

## Proof
**Step 1: chambers and orientations.** The normal fan of a zonotope generated by vectors \(e_i-e_{i+1}\) is the fan of the hyperplane arrangement \(x_i=x_{i+1}\) in \(N_\mathbb R\). A full-dimensional chamber is specified by consistent strict inequalities around the cycle, i.e. an orientation other than the two directed cycles. Thus there are \(2^n-2\) chambers. Orient an edge from its smaller to its larger coordinate in the chamber. The corresponding normal cone \(\sigma\subset N_\mathbb R\) is described by \(x_{\mathrm{tail}}\le x_{\mathrm{head}}\) on each oriented edge. Its dual \(\sigma^\vee\subset M_\mathbb R\) is generated by the \(n\) oriented incidence vectors \(e_{\mathrm{head}}-e_{\mathrm{tail}}\).

**Step 2: integral semigroup presentation.** The oriented vertex-edge incidence matrix is totally unimodular. Given an integral vector in the nonnegative cone of its columns, the linear feasibility system for a nonnegative edge flow with that integral boundary has an integral feasible solution. Consequently its edge-incidence semigroup is saturated and equals \(\sigma^\vee\cap M\). Because the undirected graph has exactly one circuit, the lattice kernel of the incidence map \(\mathbb Z^n\to M\) is rank one generated by the primitive signed cycle vector. Exactly \(k\) entries have one sign and \(n-k\) the opposite sign. The toric ideal is therefore the height-one prime ideal generated by the single binomial \(\prod_i a_i-\prod_j b_j\); the asserted affine chart follows. For \(k=1\), eliminating the singleton variable gives a polynomial ring in \(n-1\) variables, and likewise for \(n-k=1\).

**Step 3: Jacobian and purity.** Assume \(k,n-k\ge2\). For \(F=\prod a_i-\prod b_j\), all derivatives in the \(a\)-block vanish precisely when at least two of its coordinates vanish. The same holds for the \(b\)-block. Hence the reduced singular locus in this entire affine chart is the union of \(\binom{k}{2}\binom{n-k}{2}\) distinct coordinate \((n-4)\)-planes, each obtained by setting two variables in each block to zero. These coordinate spaces are contained in \(V(F)\). The toric affine charts cover \(X_n\); purity of the reduced singular locus is local in the Zariski topology, so the global singular locus is pure of dimension \(n-4\) whenever it is nonempty. There is a nonempty chart with \(k=2\) for every \(n\ge4\). For \(n=3\), each admissible orientation has a singleton block and every chart is smooth.

**Step 4: torus-fixed points and multiplicities.** At the fixed point of \(U_k\), all edge characters vanish. Its affine hypersurface is smooth for \(k=1\) or \(n-k=1\), and singular otherwise. There are \(\binom nk\) orientations with a given count \(k\). Summing gives \(2^n-2-2n\) singular fixed points. At a singular fixed point, \(F\) has \(\mathfrak m\)-adic order \(\min(k,n-k)\) in the regular ambient local ring, hence this is its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity; the displayed distribution follows. For \(n=4\), the six charts with \(k=2\) have local equation \(a_1a_2-b_1b_2=0\), an ordinary threefold node isolated at the fixed point.

**Step 5: global singularity class.** Each chart is an integral normal toric affine variety and a hypersurface in affine space. Therefore all charts are local complete intersections and Gorenstein, so these properties hold globally. Normality also follows directly from the normal fan construction, and Cohen--Macaulayness from the local hypersurface presentation.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_cycle_zonotope.py` enumerates all \(2^n-2\) acyclic cycle orientations for \(3\le n\le8\); verifies the unique signed incidence circuit; checks unimodular determinants for every cycle-spanning tree; and independently counts smooth/singular fixed charts and their fixed-point multiplicity distribution. Its recorded output concludes `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite regression checks; the all-dimensional result is proved by the incidence-matrix and Jacobian arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Grujić's *Counting faces of graphical zonotopes* (arXiv:1604.06931, initially public 2016-04-23), notably Section 2 and Proposition 5.2, establishes the graphical-arrangement/chamber correspondence and the complete face-vector formula for the same cycle zonotope, including the rhombic dodecahedron in dimension three. The face-vector formula neither states the projective toric affine coordinate rings nor determines their Jacobian singularity strata.

Hallam and Machacek's *Gorenstein braid cones and crepant resolutions* (arXiv:2112.15308) studies the toric varieties of braid cones of posets and derives broad Gorenstein criteria; it provides a related framework in which some Gorenstein assertions are already implied. In particular, our Gorenstein corollary alone is not presented as a novel standalone theorem. The contribution here is the uniform exact binomial presentation of every affine chart for the *cycle graphical-zonotope* projective toric variety, together with the all-rank codimension-three singular-stratification and multiplicity census. Published work on the Laplacian of the cycle zonotope treats an unrelated graph invariant. Different graph families, such as complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes, do not imply this cycle-specific chart classification.

## Limitations
The finding does not determine the number or global incidence structure of the irreducible components of the singular locus for \(n\ge5\), nor does it compute their intersection cohomology. The chart coordinate identifications depend on orientation and edge naming; they are not global coordinate identifications. The broader literature may contain equivalent cycle-specific descriptions under the terminology of graphic-arrangement fans, order cones, or toric hypersurfaces. The comparisons made are not an exhaustive survey of unindexed literature. Independent validation has not been performed.
