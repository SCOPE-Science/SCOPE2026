# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The normal fan is the graphic-arrangement fan, so its maximal chambers correspond to noncyclic edge orientations. The oriented incidence matrix is totally unimodular and has one primitive cycle relation; this gives the saturated affine toric chart semigroup and the principal binomial defining ideal. The Jacobian in each of these charts is computed directly: at least two zeros in each factor are necessary and sufficient. Counting chambers yields the fixed-point totals, and the order of the local equation yields the multiplicities. Exact determinant and chamber-enumeration checks reproduced these identities for orders three through eight. Computations corroborate, but do not substitute for, the all-order proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Grujić (arXiv:1604.06931, Section 2 and Proposition 5.2) supplies the same polytopes' f-vectors and acyclic orientation correspondence, but not their toric chart equations or complete reduced singular-locus dimension. Hallam–Machacek (arXiv:2112.15308, Sections 1, 4, 6) supplies a broader theory of Gorenstein braid cones and covers aspects of Gorensteinness; the Gorenstein consequence is not claimed independently novel. Current indexed comparisons for 'graphic arrangement fan', 'cycle graphical zonotope toric singularities', 'binomial charts', and 'complete intersection' did not locate an equivalent singular-chart classification. The closest published cycle-zonotope Laplacian finding concerns a different invariant, and a graphical-zonotope toric finding for complete bipartite graphs concerns a different family. Exact equivalence in unindexed or differently formulated work is still possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem concerns the full singular geometry of a canonical family of projective toric varieties, not an arbitrary parameter slice. Uniform hypersurface charts are a strong local structural assertion: they show all these graph-arrangement toric varieties are Gorenstein and local complete intersections, locate the singular locus in global codimension three, and give multiplicity distributions over exponentially many distinguished fixed points. The phenomenon changes naturally from a smooth surface to six isolated threefold nodes and then positive-dimensional singularity strata.

## Limitations and risks
The singular-locus component count and global incidence for orders at least five are not determined. The existing Gorenstein theorem for braid cones may subsume a corollary, though not the exact equations and strata as inspected. Unindexed equivalent formulations may exist; independent audit is absent. The finite script is a consistency check rather than a proof of the infinite theorem.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
