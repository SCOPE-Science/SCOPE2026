# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The mean-square variance and Möbius identities give \(\Phi_{h_a}(z)=1-\rho(a,z)^2\), hence exactly \(\|h_a\|_G=1\). The simultaneous Lebesgue-point hypothesis is available for every complex BMO function because both \(f\) and \(|f|^2\) are integrable. A fixed-radius pseudohyperbolic disk around \(r\zeta\) lies in a uniformly controlled nontangential cone, so \(\Phi_f\) tends uniformly to zero there. Outside, the localized variance is at most \(1-s^2\). Combining those regions proves the full upper bound; testing at fixed interior points and at the moving center separately gives the two matching lower bounds. The same unit perturbations belong to all four stated Banach spaces. All limits and quantifiers have been checked; numerical illustrations are not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

The most pertinent paper is Dyakonov, arXiv:2609.33242v1, Section 2, whose closing paragraph proposes describing strongly extreme Garsia-unit-ball points; it does not contain the boundary-localized max law or their empty classification. Dyakonov, arXiv:2404.05565v2, Theorem 2.7 and Corollary 2.8 instead establish *ordinary* extreme points and so do not entail the new negative statement. The Hardy-space supremum-norm result arXiv:2301.11162 has different norm geometry. Literature and research-record searches using Garsia, BMO, BMOA, strong extremity, Poisson variance, Blaschke localization and max-norm aliases did not identify a theorem whose conclusions imply this one. A systematic index cannot guarantee historical priority, and literature on equivalent BMO renormings may contain overlooked comparisons.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A 2026 open direction asks for the strongly extreme geometry of the exact Garsia balls. The result closes the strongly extreme classification for four natural spaces, distinguishing it from the much richer known ordinary-extreme theory. The accompanying exact \(\ell_\infty\)-type asymptotic splitting identifies the structural reason and extends well beyond any single test function, providing a reusable mechanism for non-local-uniform convexity questions.
