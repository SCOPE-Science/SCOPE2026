# Self-review

**Claim examined:** The integral low-degree cohomology, Picard group, and Brauer vanishing for free sign quotients of smooth split-quadric complete intersections of every even dimension \(n\ge4\).

## Correctness — PASS
Integral weak Lefschetz is valid below the complex dimension of the smooth complete intersection, yielding the four required cohomology groups of the cover. The projective sign involution fixes the degree-two hyperplane generator. Periodic integral cohomology of \(\mathbb Z/2\), together with the zero odd rows, kills every degree-three Cartan–Leray term and gives the degree-two extension \(0\to\mathbb Z/2\to H^2\to\mathbb Z\to0\), which splits. Vanishing of sheaf cohomology on the cover descends by averaging; the exponential sequence proves the Brauer and Picard assertions. The holomorphic residue changes sign, so canonical two-torsion is nontrivial. This is a complete theoretical proof, not a deduction from finite sample computations.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Frassineti–Rizzo–Tufo–Verni [1] compute Brauer groups in the hyper-Kähler-cover setting; their group-cohomology techniques are openly used, but their stated theorems do not give this Calabi–Yau quadric family. Boissière–Camere–Sarti [2] already construct the identical family and address canonical torsion, so neither the family nor canonical two-torsion is claimed new. The complete degree-three integral vanishing and Brauer value are not stated in the two inspected primary works or in topical comparisons. An unindexed general theorem, informal note, or earlier calculation might nevertheless imply the result; priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Brauer group is a central and geometrically motivated invariant for Enriques-type quotients, not a chosen numerical slice. The conclusion is uniform in every even dimension at least four and exhibits a sharp contrast with dimension-two Enriques surfaces, whose Brauer group is nontrivial. It supplies the full integral degree-two and degree-three groups needed for obstruction-theoretic applications, while using classical methods honestly.

## Limitations and sources
The result requires a smooth free quotient, does not cover the Enriques surface case, and does not calculate middle cohomology. Sources: [1] arXiv:2605.05450v1 (6 May 2026), Sections 1–3; [2] arXiv:2409.09160v1 (13 September 2024), Section 6.1.5. No independent audit is asserted.
