# Canonical tensor-generation chambers and minimal obstruction multisections for toric scroll threefolds

## Finding

Let \(m\geq0\) and \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\). Put \(B=\mathbb F_m\), let \(S^2=-m\) be its negative section, and let \(F\) be a ruling fiber. On the smooth projective toric threefold
\[
Y_{m,a,b}=\mathbb P_B(\mathcal O_B\oplus\mathcal O_B(aS+bF)),
\]
use the Grothendieck quotient convention for \(\mathbb P\). Define \(\delta=b-ma\).

**Complete chamber theorem.** The canonical bundle \(\omega_Y\) is tensor-generating (its integer tensor powers classically generate \(D_{\mathrm{perf}}(Y)\)) if and only if
\[
(a-2)(b-m-2)>0,\qquad(a+2)(b+m+2)>0
\]
and, in addition,
\[
\begin{cases}
|\delta|\neq2,&m=0,\\
|\delta|\neq1,&m=1,\\
|\delta|>m-2,&m\geq2.
\end{cases}
\]
These conditions are necessary and sufficient for every integer triple in the stated range.

**Sharp obstruction-multisection theorem.** Suppose \(m\geq2\), put \(\kappa=m-2\), \(h=|\delta|\), and assume \(h\leq\kappa\). The ruled surface \(D=\pi^{-1}(S)\cong\mathbb F_h\) contains a smooth integral curve \(C\) with \(K_Y\cdot C=0\). The least positive degree \(p=C\cdot f\) of any such integral multisection over \(S\), where \(f\) is a fiber in \(D\), is
\[
p_{\min}=\begin{cases}1,&\kappa+h\ \text{even},\\2,&\kappa+h\ \text{odd}.\end{cases}
\]
A general smooth minimal-degree witness has genus \(0\) in the first case and genus \(m-3\) in the second. Thus non-invariant canonical-degree-zero curves can obstruct generation even when no torus-invariant curve has canonical degree zero.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The variety \(Y\) is smooth, projective and toric. The parameter \(m\) is any nonnegative integer; there is no positivity assumption on the twisting integers \(a,b\). The tensor-generation property is the derived-category one defined by Ito--Olander, not ampleness or global generation. Curves in the obstruction statement are **integral curves in the particular surface** \(D\), and their multisection degrees are measured against the ruling of \(D\) over \(S\); the theorem does not claim minimal degree among all curves in \(Y\).

## Proof

**1. Canonical divisor and two invariant sections.** Put \(L=aS+bF\) and \(\xi=c_1(\mathcal O_Y(1))\). Adjunction for the rank-two projective bundle gives
\[
K_Y=-2\xi+\pi^*(K_B+L),\qquad K_B=-2S-(m+2)F.
\]
The quotient sections associated with \(\mathcal O\) and \(L\) carry restrictions \(K_B+L\) and \(K_B-L\), respectively. On \(\mathbb F_m\), the pseudoeffective cone is generated by \(S,F\), and \(pS+qF\) is big precisely when \(p,q>0\). Consequently both section restrictions are big or anti-big precisely when
\[
(a-2)(b-m-2)>0,\qquad(a+2)(b+m+2)>0.
\]
The notation \(K_B\pm L\) means the two distinct section restrictions, not an equivalence under a choice of quotient convention.

**2. Restriction to the four invariant vertical surfaces.** Let \(C\subset B\) be any of the four torus-invariant base curves; write \(\ell=L\cdot C\), \(\kappa_C=K_B\cdot C\), \(h_C=|\ell|\). Then \(D_C=\pi^{-1}(C)\cong\mathbb F_{h_C}\). Denote its negative section by \(E\) and its ruling fiber by \(f\). Direct restriction of the displayed canonical divisor yields
\[
K_Y|_{D_C}\equiv-2E+(\kappa_C-h_C)f.
\]
This is never big, since its \(E\)-coefficient is negative. Its negative is big if and only if \(h_C>\kappa_C\), because the big cone of \(\mathbb F_{h_C}\) is \(\{rE+sf:r,s>0\}\). The values of \(\kappa_C\) are \(-2\) for each of the two invariant fibers, \(-m-2\) for the positive section \(S+mF\), and \(m-2\) for \(S\). Thus all vertical-surface tests are automatic when \(m\leq1\), while for \(m\geq2\) only \(D_S\) matters and it imposes \(|\delta|>m-2\).

**3. Invariant curves and sufficiency.** The toric orbit closures consist of \(Y\), its two sections and four vertical surfaces, and the invariant curves and fixed points. The anti-canonical divisor of a complete toric variety is big: the polytope of \(-K_Y=\sum D_\rho\) contains a neighborhood of the origin because the fan is complete. A torus-invariant ruling fiber has \(K_Y\)-degree \(-2\). A torus-invariant curve in a quotient section lying above a base curve \(C\) has degree \(\kappa_C+\ell\) or \(\kappa_C-\ell\). For the fibers and positive section, these nonzero-degree conditions follow from the strict section-bigness inequalities (which exclude \(a=\pm2\) and \(b=\pm(m+2)\)). Above the negative section they read \(m-2\pm\delta\neq0\); for \(m\leq1\), this is equivalent to the exceptional exclusions stated in the theorem, while for \(m\geq2\) it follows from \(|\delta|>m-2\). Torus-fixed points impose no restriction. Hence all orbit-closure restrictions are big or anti-big precisely in the claimed chambers. The toric orbit-closure criterion, itself following from the Ito--Olander derived positivity theorem, proves sufficiency; the same criterion proves necessity of the inequalities. Note that testing only invariant curves would **miss** the vertical-surface obstruction.

**4. Construction and sharpness of smooth obstruction curves.** For \(m\geq2\) with \(h=|\delta|\leq\kappa=m-2\), the restriction is
\[
K_Y|_D=-2E+(\kappa-h)f.
\]
For a multisection \(C\equiv pE+qf\) with \(p\geq1\), its canonical degree is \(K_Y\cdot C=p(\kappa+h)-2q\). For an irreducible curve not equal to the negative section \(E\), intersection nonnegativity with \(E\) gives \(q\geq ph\). The exceptional \(E\) itself is harmless to the lower-bound argument: its ruling degree is \(1\), and it has degree zero only when \(\kappa=h=0\). Therefore a zero-degree multisection requires \(2q=p(\kappa+h)\), and \(p\geq1\), forcing \(p\geq2\) when \(\kappa+h\) is odd.

If \(\kappa+h\) is even, \(q=(\kappa+h)/2\geq h\); the basepoint-free system \(|E+qf|\) has a smooth integral section. If \(\kappa+h\) is odd, take \(p=2\), \(q=\kappa+h\geq2h+1\); the basepoint-free linear system \(|2E+qf|\) contains a smooth integral bisection by Bertini. This achieves the least possible degree. Adjunction on \(D\), using \(K_D=-2E-(h+2)f\), yields genus \(0\) for the section and \(\kappa-1=m-3\) for the bisection. No exhaustive classification of all zero-degree curves in \(Y\) is claimed.

For a concrete family of non-invariant obstructions, take \(m\geq3\), \(a=3\), and \(b=3m+h\) with \(0\leq h<m-2\). Both displayed section-product inequalities hold; every torus-invariant curve has nonzero canonical degree, yet \(D_S\) supports the indicated degree-zero curve. In particular, \((m,a,b)=(3,3,9)\) has a rational bisection of ruling degree two; \((4,3,13)\) has an elliptic bisection of ruling degree two.

## Relationship to prior work

Ito--Olander (2025) establish the universal derived Nakai--Moishezon positivity criterion for tensor-generation, including applications to ruled surfaces. Ito--Zeng--Zeng (posted October 15, 2026, **after** the October 11 cutoff attached to the originating-source window) establish the general toric orbit-closure/affine-separation criterion and give sufficient projective-bundle conditions in Proposition 6.10, with the converse reserved for higher-rank trivial summands. Their Example 6.11 treats Hirzebruch bases in that sufficient-case regime; Example 6.12 supplies one rank-two projective-bundle example. These universal tests could in principle be specialized to recover the chamber statement, but the inspected text does not state the complete three-parameter classification or the sharp minimal-multisection degree/genus law. The contribution here is this complete explicit specialization and geometric obstruction theorem, not priority for the universal criteria or any of the authors' examples. The post-cutoff paper is used for comparison, not as the earliest originating public date.

## Limitations

The claimed novelty concerns the explicit full parameter classification and minimum-degree obstruction witnesses. These statements are consequences of classical projective-bundle intersection theory combined with known tensor-generation criteria, and an implicit earlier derivation remains possible. The numerical verifier cannot establish infinite completeness or absolute priority. Smoothness and irreducibility of general members use standard Bertini and ruled-surface geometry. The statement concerns the smooth split projective-bundle family over complex Hirzebruch surfaces, not arbitrary projective bundles or singular toric varieties.
