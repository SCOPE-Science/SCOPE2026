# Review of centered-strip perimeter tomography on parallelograms

**Correctness — PASS.** All quantifiers and geometric hypotheses are explicit: both bodies are full-dimensional origin-centered parallelograms, the centered strip has fixed half-width \(t>0\), and \(tB_2\) lies strictly inside each body. The pure and mixed chamber formulas follow by direct Euclidean polygon-edge accounting. Cross-multiplied trigonometric identities distinguish the two branch types on any open arc. Two pure neighborhoods then recover both edge directions and their side-line distances. The rational identity is supported by exact algebra; independent numerical clipping tests serve as finite verification only. The theorem does not rely on enumeration.

**Originality — PASS with residual historical priority risk.** Matthews (arXiv:2603.00002; Theorem 1.2 and its immediately ensuing paragraph) explicitly asks the perimeter version of an established slab-*area* tomography statement. The present conclusion is the full four-vertex symmetric case, not an area-theorem corollary. Searches also compared equivalent centered-strip descriptions, unlabeled widths, chords, and inverse reconstruction for parallelograms. The closest indexed results instead determine parallelograms from width statistics or handle tangent-chord measurements; no checked statement is strong enough to imply the new perimeter-slab theorem. The original Yaskin–Yaskina full journal text was not accessible via authorized routes, although its abstract and Matthews' 2026 discussion specify a volume rather than perimeter theorem. Earlier unindexed contributions remain a genuine risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The new result answers a natural explicit open geometric-tomography question for the smallest full-dimensional centrally symmetric polygon class, including all skew parallelograms and all side ratios. The non-antiperiodicity obstruction is a structural method designed for the genuinely different perimeter integrand, rather than a routine substitution in the existing area proof. It plausibly informs the next hexagon case, without claiming a general solution or quantitative stability.
