# Scientific review: information backflow under quantum history resetting

## Correctness (PASS)

The quantum history-resetting dynamics acts at every fixed time as an average of unitary conjugations generated by one randomly sampled effective evolution time. For an equatorial antipodal qubit pair, trace distance is exactly the modulus of the established Kummer coherence function. Eliminating the Volterra integral reproduces that Kummer differential equation, including its initial conditions and signs. The proposed strict distance increase has a wholly rational certificate: 80 hypergeometric terms are evaluated by exact `Fraction` arithmetic, and an explicitly bounded infinite-series tail is smaller than \(10^{-10}\). The certificate proves the full infinite-series inequality, rather than relying on a finite numerical observation. A positive trace-preserving intermediate channel would contract the trace distance, so the inequality proves non-divisibility. The result extends to a certified gap band by a pathwise accumulated-time bound and a seven-Lipschitz comparison; any finite-dimensional Hamiltonian with a Bohr frequency in the band inherits the witness.

## Originality (PASS; qualified)

The source by de Mauro, Kulkarni and Majumdar explicitly derives the evolution of all energy-basis coherences, including the Kummer function. That formula is prior art and not claimed as new. Its material on finite-spectrum relaxation treats algebraic long-time decay, not a strict *finite-time trace-distance revival* or the resulting failure of positive divisibility. The latter is a separate qualitative property: long-time decay does not imply that the modulus decreases at all intervening times. The certified lower bound is also strictly stronger than a plotted numerical suggestion.

Carollo and Wald treat time-dependent discrete-time resetting to a fixed state, and discuss distinguishability revivals under effective negative reset probabilities. The present history-dependent protocol has a strictly positive constant Poisson hazard and is not that model. Breuer, Laine and Piilo provide the general distinguishability criterion, not its application to this resetting mechanism. Published comparisons on random-telegraph dephasing, quantum first detection, and power-biased historical kernels concern distinct mechanisms, time regimes, and conclusions.

The strongest residual risk is that an unsurfaced application of a general hypergeometric, dephasing-channel, or preferential-relocation result already recognizes this backflow. Exact knowledge of the coherence function determines the answer *in principle*; novelty is claimed only for the proved operational consequence and certified open-parameter witness, not for the function itself. Literature search failure is not proof of absence.

## Value (PASS)

A nonlocal memory equation does not by itself show operational quantum information backflow: trace-distance increases are stronger, experimentally interpretable witnesses that rule out positive divisibility. This finding resolves that distinction for an exactly solvable positive-hazard reset model and supplies a nonzero certified lower bound, independent of the time scale \(r\). It also contrasts physically positive Poisson resetting to one's *own* past with the fixed-reference resets studied in earlier non-Markovianity work. A deterministic pathwise Lipschitz argument proves an explicit spectral-gap band and transfers the witness to any finite-dimensional Hamiltonian containing such an energy pair; the example is therefore not confined to one isolated Hamiltonian. It does not classify all frequency ratios or evaluate the full optimized backflow measure.

## Scope and independent validation

The theorem requires a uniform-history kernel, the stated spectral interval, and two specified observation times; it applies to any finite-dimensional Hamiltonian with an eligible energy pair. Exact rational arithmetic certifies the inequality; no finite simulation is treated as an infinite theorem. Broader novelty and physical realizability beyond the mathematical protocol remain separate questions.
