# Characteristic-dependent automorphism orbits in the four-dimensional null-filiform Zinbiel algebra

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, of characteristic \(p\), and let \(Z\) be the four-dimensional null-filiform Zinbiel algebra defined below. The number \(\omega_q(Z)\) of orbits of the natural action of the **\(k\)-linear** automorphism group \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(Z)\) on the **elements** of \(Z\) is

\[
\boxed{\omega_q(Z)=
\begin{cases}
2q+2+\gcd(3,q-1),&p=2,\\
2q+3+\gcd(4,q-1),&p=3,\\
4+\gcd(3,q-1)+\gcd(4,q-1),&p\ge5.
\end{cases}}
\]

This is a complete orbit classification, not merely a Burnside count. For characteristics two and three, genuine one-parameter families of orbits occur; for characteristics at least five, all orbit types are determined by finitely many power classes. In particular, the orbit count grows linearly with \(q\) in characteristics two and three, but is bounded by eleven in all characteristics at least five.

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) has exactly \(q=p^f\) elements, where \(p\) is prime and \(f\ge1\). The vector space \(Z\) has basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\), with product \(\circ\) defined by

\[
e_i\circ e_j=\binom{i+j-1}{j}e_{i+j}\quad(2\le i+j\le4),
\qquad e_i\circ e_j=0\quad(i+j>4).
\]

The binomial coefficients are integers mapped into \(k\); this definition makes sense in every characteristic. Its only possibly nonzero products are

\[
\begin{aligned}
e_1\circ e_1&=e_2,&e_1\circ e_2&=e_3,&e_2\circ e_1&=2e_3,\\
e_1\circ e_3&=e_4,&e_3\circ e_1&=3e_4,&e_2\circ e_2&=3e_4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The Zinbiel identity is \((x\circ y)\circ z=x\circ(y\circ z)+x\circ(z\circ y)\). The algebra is generated by \(e_1\), with \(e_{j+1}=e_1\circ e_j\). Orbits are under \(k\)-linear algebra automorphisms, not semilinear automorphisms, field automorphisms, or arbitrary invertible linear maps. No classification of all Zinbiel algebras, of subalgebras, or of automorphism group schemes is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Every automorphism is determined by its image of \(e_1\).** Let

\[
u=ae_1+be_2+ce_3+de_4.
\]

The algebra is generated by \(e_1\). The recursive images \(u_1=u\), \(u_{j+1}=u\circ u_j\) satisfy every defining product relation. One way to see this without dividing by factorials is induction on total degree using the Zinbiel identity, which yields the universal integral identity

\[
u_i\circ u_j=\binom{i+j-1}{j}u_{i+j}
\]

for every admissible pair, with \(u_i\circ u_j=0\) when \(i+j>4\). Therefore the substitution \(e_j\mapsto u_j\) is an endomorphism. Its matrix is triangular with diagonal \(a,a^2,a^3,a^4\), and is invertible exactly when \(a\ne0\). Conversely, every automorphism is obtained this way because \(e_1\) generates \(Z\) and the quotient \(Z/Z^2\) is one-dimensional. Thus \(|\operatorname{Aut}_k(Z)|=(q-1)q^3\).

Direct multiplication gives the characteristic-independent formulas

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(e_1)&=ae_1+be_2+ce_3+de_4,\\
\phi(e_2)&=a^2e_2+3abe_3+(4ac+3b^2)e_4,\\
\phi(e_3)&=a^3e_3+6a^2be_4,\\
\phi(e_4)&=a^4e_4,
\end{aligned}
\qquad a\in k^\times,\quad b,c,d\in k.
\]

**2. Reduction to three coordinates.** The zero vector is one orbit. Every vector with nonzero \(e_1\)-coefficient is the image of \(e_1\) under an automorphism, hence forms exactly one further orbit. On the invariant three-space \(W=\langle e_2,e_3,e_4\rangle\), write \(v=x e_2+y e_3+z e_4\). The transformation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
x'&=a^2x,\\
y'&=3abx+a^3y,\\
z'&=(4ac+3b^2)x+6a^2by+a^4z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Its three disjoint strata are \(x\ne0\), \(x=0,y\ne0\), and \(x=y=0,z\ne0\); their vanishing conditions are preserved. For \(m\ge1\), the number of cosets of \((k^\times)^m\) in \(k^\times\) is \(\gcd(m,q-1)\), since \(k^\times\) is cyclic.

**3. Characteristic at least five.** With \(x\ne0\), the nonzero coefficients \(3\) and \(4\) in (1) allow successive choices of \(b\) and \(c\) eliminating \(y'\) and \(z'\). The remaining coefficient \(x'=a^2x\) gives two square classes. When \(x=0,y\ne0\), \(6\) is invertible, so \(b\) eliminates \(z'\) and \(y'=a^3y\) gives \(\gcd(3,q-1)\) cube classes. Finally, nonzero multiples of \(e_4\) give \(\gcd(4,q-1)\) fourth-power classes. Including zero and the \(e_1\)-stratum yields the third case in the formula.

**4. Characteristic two.** Equation (1) becomes

\[
x'=a^2x,\qquad y'=abx+a^3y,\qquad z'=b^2x+a^4z.
\]

On \(x\ne0\), the quantity

\[
\delta=\frac{xz+y^2}{x^3}\in k
\]

is invariant: its numerator and denominator both scale by \(a^6\). Since a finite field is perfect, first choose the unique \(a\) making \(x'=1\), and then choose the unique \(b\) making \(y'=0\). The image is \(e_2+\delta e_4\). Therefore **every** \(\delta\in k\) gives exactly one orbit, for a total of \(q\) on this stratum.

For \(x=0,y\ne0\), the action is \((y,z)\mapsto(a^3y,a^4z)\). If \(z=0\), there are \(\gcd(3,q-1)\) cube classes. If \(z\ne0\), the invariant \(\eta=y^4/z^3\in k^\times\) is complete: selecting \(a=y/z\) sends the pair to \((\eta,\eta)\). There are \(q-1\) such orbits. Nonzero \(e_4\)-vectors constitute one orbit because the fourth-power map is bijective on \(k^\times\). With zero and the \(e_1\)-orbit this gives \(2+q+\gcd(3,q-1)+(q-1)+1=2q+2+\gcd(3,q-1)\).

**5. Characteristic three.** Equation (1) becomes

\[
x'=a^2x,\qquad y'=a^3y,\qquad z'=acx+a^4z.
\]

When \(x\ne0\), choose \(c\) to eliminate \(z\). If \(y=0\), there are exactly two square classes of \(x\). If \(y\ne0\), the invariant \(\theta=y^2/x^3\in k^\times\) is complete. Indeed, choosing \(a=y/x^2\) sends \((x,y)\) to \((\theta,\theta^2)\); this gives \(q-1\) further orbits. Thus the first stratum has \(q+1\) orbits.

For \(x=0,y\ne0\), the weights are again \((3,4)\). If \(z=0\), the cube map is bijective because \(q\) is a power of three, hence this case is one orbit. If \(z\ne0\), the same complete invariant \(\eta=y^4/z^3\) gives \(q-1\) orbits. Finally, the nonzero multiples of \(e_4\) yield \(\gcd(4,q-1)\) orbits. Summing the disjoint strata, zero, and the \(e_1\)-orbit gives \(2+(q+1)+q+\gcd(4,q-1)=2q+3+\gcd(4,q-1)\).

The three cases exhaust all primes and all elements, proving the formula. The normal forms in the proof also show that the listed invariants are **complete**, not just necessary.

## Verification

The included standalone program `verify_zinbiel.py` constructs the finite-field arithmetic, derives automorphism images directly from the displayed multiplication table (rather than substituting the expected orbit formula), checks their multiplication relations, forms the actual orbits of the invariant three-space, and compares the number with the theorem. The nonzero-first-coordinate stratum is handled by the proved transitivity argument.

The tested pairs \((p,q)\) are \((2,2),(3,3),(2,4),(5,5),(7,7),(2,8),(3,9),(11,11)\). Their exact total orbit counts are respectively \(7,11,13,9,9,19,25,7\). Running `python3 verify_zinbiel.py` yields `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite checks; the proof above, not their extrapolation, establishes the formula for every finite field.

## Relationship to prior work

Adashev, Omirov and Khudoyberdiyev (2007), Theorem 2.1 and their four-dimensional classification, describe the null-filiform algebra's multiplication and its isomorphism type over the complex numbers. That classification of **algebra structures** does not classify the action of the algebra's automorphism group on its **elements**, nor does it give finite-field or modular orbit formulas. Their multiplication formula supplies the natural object studied here; all orbit calculations are carried out independently in arbitrary characteristic.

Later work on local and two-local automorphisms of null-filiform Zinbiel algebras studies a related symmetry operation, but its available bibliographic material does not establish the exact finite-field element-orbit formulas. That source was not fully accessible and must not be treated as conclusively excluded. Work on central extensions and superalgebras is likewise about different classification objects.

## Limitations

The conclusion is limited to the particular four-dimensional algebra, finite fields, and ordinary \(k\)-linear algebra automorphisms. The orbit counts would change if field automorphisms were also allowed. The proof does not classify automorphism orbits for higher-dimensional null-filiform algebras, and does not calculate scheme-theoretic stabilizers or the geometry of an orbit-space quotient. Published or unindexed prior treatments of the exact modular element orbits, if they exist, could affect originality.
