# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every derivation has the unique block form from the Leibniz rule. On a rank-\(r\) idempotent generator action, the induced wedge-square map has eigenvalues \(0,1,2\), exposing the exact odd/even characteristic boundary. The central parameter equation splits along image and kernel; a central shear kills every admissible parameter, so both the orbit classification and full rank formula follow. Gaussian coefficients count every projection and every central parameter with no omitted cases. The two standalone finite-field checkers replay all stated finite tests. Boundary values \(r=0\) and \(r=n\) are included and consistent.

**Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk.** The precise free-Lie derivation framework appears in Benito–de-la-Concepción (2014) and Benito–Roldán-López (2019), but their examined statements stop short of the idempotent-orbit and rank census and assume characteristic zero. Burde–Moens (2011) studies invertible complex periodic derivations; Krutov–Lebedev (2017) studies modulo-two gradings of simple, not free nilpotent, Lie algebras. The closest published search results likewise address different Lie algebras or different invariants. An earlier equivalent grading-count formula could remain unindexed; bibliographic priority is not certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Idempotent derivations are a natural semisimple stratum of a derivation algebra, and in characteristic two encode \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\)-gradings. The complete all-dimension, all-field orbit decomposition displays a genuine characteristic jump (two versus \(n+1\) orbits), determines exact arithmetic orbit sizes and operator-rank multiplicities, and distinguishes nonconjugate objects with the same full rank. This is a natural complete classification rather than an arbitrary low-dimensional sample.

**Limits.** Only free two-step nilpotent Lie algebras are covered. Finite exhaustive tests are separate from the uniform symbolic proof. Other equivalent historical presentations under grading terminology remain possible.
