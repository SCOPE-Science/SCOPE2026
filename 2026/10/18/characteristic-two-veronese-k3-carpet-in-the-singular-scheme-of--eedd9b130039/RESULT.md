# Characteristic-two Veronese K3 carpet in the singular scheme of a symmetric permanent

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field, and let \(X\subset\mathbb P^5_k\) be the cubic hypersurface defined by the permanent of a generic symmetric three-by-three matrix:
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&d&e\\d&b&f\\e&f&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P=abc+af^2+be^2+cd^2+2def.
\]
Let \(\Sigma\) denote the intrinsic Jacobian singular scheme \(\operatorname{Proj}k[a,b,c,d,e,f]/(P,P_a,P_b,P_c,P_d,P_e,P_f)\).

**Characteristic two.** The singular scheme is the complete intersection
\[
\Sigma=V(bc+f^2,ac+e^2,ab+d^2)\subset\mathbb P^5_k.
\]
Its reduced scheme is the quadratic Veronese surface \(V=\nu_2(\mathbb P^2)\), and \(\Sigma\) is a multiplicity-two, locally square-zero thickening of \(V\). Its nilradical \(\mathcal L\) is an invertible \(\mathcal O_V\)-module, and under \(V\simeq\mathbb P^2\),
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-3)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O_\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(\Sigma\) is a **projective K3 carpet supported on the quadratic Veronese surface**: it is locally Cohen--Macaulay, has trivial dualizing sheaf, satisfies \(h^1(\mathcal O_\Sigma)=0\), and has degree \(8\). Its Hilbert polynomial is \(4m^2+2\), and its homogeneous coordinate-ring Hilbert series is \((1+t)^3/(1-t)^3\).

**Characteristic different from two.** The reduced singular scheme is the union of three smooth plane conics
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_a&=V(a,d,e,bc+f^2),\\
C_b&=V(b,d,f,ac+e^2),\\
C_c&=V(c,e,f,ab+d^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
They intersect pairwise at the three distinct diagonal coordinate points \(P_a,P_b,P_c\). The intrinsic singular scheme is generically reduced along each conic and possesses exactly **one embedded length-one associated-point contribution at each of the three diagonal coordinate points**. Its Hilbert polynomial is \(6m+3\). At the generic point of each conic the transverse singularity of \(X\) is a three-dimensional ordinary double point. In both characteristic regimes \(X\) is geometrically integral and normal.

## Assumptions and scope
These statements use the polynomial permanent in the ordinary symmetric coordinate ring, not a divided-power or apolar permanent. The base field is algebraically closed. All characteristics are included, divided into \(\operatorname{char}(k)=2\) and \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\). The scheme \(\Sigma\) is defined intrinsically by the first Fitting ideal of the cotangent module of the cubic hypersurface, equivalently by its hypersurface Jacobian equations. In characteristic three the defining cubic is retained explicitly rather than inferred solely from Euler's identity.

## Proof
Differentiating gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_a&=bc+f^2,&P_b&=ac+e^2,&P_c&=ab+d^2,\\
P_d&=2(cd+ef),&P_e&=2(be+df),&P_f&=2(af+de).
\end{aligned}
\]
At any geometric common zero of the partial derivatives at least one of \(a,b,c\) is nonzero: if all three vanished then the first three derivative equations force \(d=e=f=0\). Consequently the three affine opens \(a\ne0\), \(b\ne0\), \(c\ne0\) cover the entire projective singular scheme; symmetry allows calculations only on \(a=1\).

Suppose first that the characteristic is not two. The derivative equations \(P_b=P_c=P_f=0\) allow exact elimination
\[
c=-e^2,\qquad b=-d^2,\qquad f=-de.
\]
The other derivatives and the cubic then become
\[
P_a=2d^2e^2,\quad P_d=-4de^2,\quad P_e=-4d^2e,\quad P=-2d^2e^2.
\]
As \(2\) is invertible, the affine-chart Jacobian ring is exactly
\[
k[d,e]/(d^2e,de^2).
\]
Its radical is \((de)\); its embedded-primary decomposition is
\[
(d^2e,de^2)=(de)\cap(d,e)^3.
\]
The module between the reduced quotient and the original quotient is the one-dimensional vector space generated by the class of \(de\). Hence the two smooth axes meet with an extra length-one embedded contribution at the coordinate point. Applying the same calculation on the two other diagonal charts yields precisely the stated three conics, their pairwise intersections, and exactly three embedded points. The union of the reduced conics has Hilbert polynomial \(3(2m+1)-3=6m\), while the three embedded lengths add \(3\). At a generic point on any conic the quadratic normal part has full rank four: for example, at \((a,b,c,d,e,f)=(0,1,-1,0,0,1)\), the Hessian of \(P\) has rank four in every odd characteristic. The formal Morse lemma, applied transversely over the function field of the conic, gives a three-dimensional ordinary double point.

Now suppose the characteristic is two. The permanent coincides with the determinant of the symmetric matrix, and \(P_d=P_e=P_f=0\). Euler's identity for the cubic shows \(P\in(P_a,P_b,P_c)\). On \(a=1\), the first three partials give \(b=d^2\), \(c=e^2\), and
\[
f^2+d^2e^2=(f+de)^2=0.
\]
Writing \(u=f+de\), the chart is exactly \(\operatorname{Spec}k[d,e,u]/(u^2)\). The same holds on the other two diagonal charts. The reduced charts are the standard affine charts of the rank-one symmetric-matrix locus, hence the quadratic Veronese surface. Each chart has a principal square-zero nilradical, so the global nilradical is an invertible module \(\mathcal L\) on \(V\simeq\mathbb P^2\).

The three quadrics form a regular sequence: their common topological locus has dimension two inside \(\mathbb P^5\), hence their homogeneous ideal has height three. Thus the scheme is an unmixed complete intersection of type \((2,2,2)\). Its Hilbert series is \((1-t^2)^3/(1-t)^6=(1+t)^3/(1-t)^3\), and the Hilbert polynomial is \(4m^2+2\). Write \(\mathcal L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(s)\). The exact square-zero quotient sequence and \(\mathcal O_\Sigma(1)|_V=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\) give, for all large \(m\),
\[
4m^2+2=\chi(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(2m))+\chi(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(2m+s)).
\]
Comparing the linear coefficients forces \(s=-3\), and the constant terms agree. Consequently the displayed conormal exact sequence holds. Adjunction for a \((2,2,2)\) complete intersection in \(\mathbb P^5\) gives \(\omega_\Sigma\simeq\mathcal O_\Sigma\); the exact sequence and \(H^1(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(j))=0\) show \(H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal O_\Sigma)=0\). These establish the K3-carpet properties and degree eight.

Finally, \(P\) is irreducible in every characteristic: regarded as a polynomial linear in \(a\), its coefficient \(bc+f^2\) is an irreducible quadratic relatively prime to the constant term \(be^2+cd^2+2def\); for instance, on \(b=1,c=f=d=0,e=1\) the latter does not vanish. Thus \(X\) is integral. Its singular support has dimension one outside characteristic two and dimension two in characteristic two, whereas \(\dim X=4\). A hypersurface satisfies Serre's condition \(S_2\), and this singular-codimension estimate gives \(R_1\), proving normality.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone script `verify.py` symbolically differentiates the cubic and checks both affine elimination identities, the characteristic-two square-zero normal form, the complete-intersection Hilbert polynomial and its conormal twist. It enumerates all \(\mathbb F_q\)-rational projective singular-support points for \(q=2,3,5\), obtaining respectively \(7,9,15\), as predicted by the Veronese surface and the three-conic arrangement. Finally it checks the Hessian rank at a generic-conic witness modulo \(3,5,7\). The script prints `VERIFY_OK` when these checks pass. Finite-field enumeration corroborates, but does not replace, the algebraic proof for all algebraically closed fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Shafiei (2013) investigates the **apolar annihilator ideal** and cactus-rank aspects of the permanent of a generic symmetric matrix. That apolar ideal is different from the **Jacobian singular ideal** above, and the main treatments assume characteristic different from two. Alper--Bogart--Velasco (2015) study determinantal complexity and the singular codimension of the **generic nonsymmetric** permanent; those bounds do not determine these symmetric Jacobian schemes. Bangere--Mukherjee--Raychaudhury (2020, Theorem 3.4) prove nonexistence of K3 carpets on the second Veronese in their characteristic-zero setting. The present characteristic-two explicit Jacobian construction lies outside that hypothesis. Algebraically, the characteristic jump arises because the three off-diagonal derivatives acquire the factor two and vanish identically in characteristic two. The underlying symmetric determinantal rank-one locus is classical; neither its reduced description nor the general concept of a K3 carpet is claimed as new. The proposed contribution is the combined scheme-theoretic Jacobian calculation and its characteristic-dependent geometric interpretation.

## Limitations
The claim does not classify all K3 carpets or their deformations, and it does not assert an analogous characteristic-zero embedded carpet. Its novelty is subject to possible implicit coverage in older characteristic-two symmetric-determinantal literature; the literature comparison cannot guarantee uniqueness. The checker is a finite and symbolic consistency test, not a computer proof of literature novelty.
