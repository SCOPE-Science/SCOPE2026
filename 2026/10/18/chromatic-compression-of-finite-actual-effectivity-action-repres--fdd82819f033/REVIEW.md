# Scientific review: chromatic compression of finite actual-effectivity representations

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a sufficiency theorem for finite concurrent-game-representable neighbourhood frames with at least two agents. Every complete-profile intersection is a clique in the stated graph. Proper coloring is therefore injective within each intersection. Choosing the largest multiplicity coordinate yields a mixed-modulus signature whose every one-coordinate slice is surjective: the key congruence can be solved because the largest modulus covers a full residue system modulo every smaller modulus. The transition rule consequently stays within every player's chosen neighbourhood while reaching every member of that neighbourhood by a suitable opponent completion. No optimality claim is made. The packaged finite checker enumerates all 845 eligible two-agent frames on a three-outcome base and tests asymmetric and unbounded-gap constructions; finite checks are corroboration, not the infinite-parameter proof.

## Value — PASS

The theorem isolates the actual obstruction to reusing outcome colors: simultaneous co-occurrence in complete neighbourhood intersections. For two agents with star-shaped neighbourhoods on \(n+1\) outcomes, it guarantees two copies per neighbourhood for every \(n\), instead of the \(n+1\) copy allowance obtained from the earlier universal range formula. It is a structural improvement for natural sparse-intersection instances, not a parameter relabeling or a fixed small-instance enumeration.
