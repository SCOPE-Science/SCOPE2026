# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every unit is uniquely a diagonal unit times a radical unit because the arrow ideal squares to zero. The exact group-commutator identity \((1-s)x+(t-1)z\) forces the endpoints of every supported arrow to be separated by one of \(2r\) diagonal labels. Conversely, any proper \((q-1)^{2r}\)-coloring makes at least one coefficient invertible for each supported arrow, and independent coordinate assignment realizes the desired output. The derived subgroup, sharp width, image-size sum, loop boundary and \(q=2\) boundary follow. Direct unit multiplication and an independent small-field checker reproduce all seven tested images and a sharp \(K_5\) witness.

**Originality — PASS within examined coverage.** Iovanov–Sistko classify maximal subalgebras and quiver-algebra structure, not word images of unit groups. Nikolov–Segal establish general commutator-factor bounds without these exact support conditions; Bartholdi–Groth–Lysenok treat the Grigorchuk group. Earlier quiver-unit results give commuting-pair and order statistics; a published Kronecker-path calculation gives additive ring-element commutator fibers. None of the inspected statements implies the precise coloring criterion, the elementwise lengths and the complete image-size law without an additional argument. An equivalent semidirect-product formulation may remain undiscovered.

**Value — PASS.** Exact elementwise commutator length and the full word-image filtration are natural finite-group invariants. Their equality with chromatic thresholds is a structural bridge between group word maps and graph coloring. It produces arbitrarily large widths at any fixed field size \(q>2\), within a concrete metabelian family, and sharply distinguishes existence of a single commutator from generation by the full derived subgroup. This is a complete uniform classification, not a small numerical refinement.

No external mathematical validation has been conducted. The finding is restricted to radical-square-zero quiver algebras and does not count representing tuples.
