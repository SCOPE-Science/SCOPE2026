# Circular palindromic-factor complexity of binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal B_5\) be the set of binary de Bruijn cycles of order \(5\), modulo cyclic rotation. For \(B\in\mathcal B_5\), let \(\mathcal P(B)\) be the set of **distinct, nonempty palindromic circular factors** of lengths \(1\) through \(32\), and set \(P(B)=|\mathcal P(B)|\). Every factor is an ordinary contiguous binary word, allowed to cross the chosen end of the circle, and no factor uses more than one complete circuit.

The complete distribution of \(P\) over the \(2048\) cycles is:

| \(P(B)\) | Number of cycles | Orbits of size \(2\) | Orbits of size \(4\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(22\) | \(32\) | \(4\) | \(6\) |
| \(23\) | \(32\) | \(0\) | \(8\) |
| \(24\) | \(200\) | \(6\) | \(47\) |
| \(27\) | \(192\) | \(0\) | \(48\) |
| \(28\) | \(920\) | \(14\) | \(223\) |
| \(29\) | \(544\) | \(0\) | \(136\) |
| \(30\) | \(128\) | \(8\) | \(28\) |

Here the orbit counts refer to the action generated by reversal and bit complementation, after identifying rotations. There are \(32\) orbits of size \(2\) and \(496\) of size \(4\), hence \(528\) symmetry orbits in total. In particular, no cycle has exactly \(25\) or \(26\) distinct circular palindromic factors, and the sharp extrema \(22\) and \(30\) occur on \(32\) and \(128\) cycles.

The exact moments, when a rotation class is sampled uniformly, are
\[
\mathbb E[P]=\frac{1775}{64},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(P)=\frac{12511}{4096}.
\]

More strongly, the full vector of palindromic-factor counts by length
\[
\bigl(p_1(B),\ldots,p_{32}(B)\bigr),\qquad
p_j(B)=|\{u\in\mathcal P(B):|u|=j\}|,
\]
takes exactly \(51\) values. Their entire multiplicity table is provided in `profiles.csv`, with the seven-class totals and symmetry decomposition also in `spectrum.csv`.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is the labeled set \(\{0,1\}\), and a binary de Bruijn cycle of order \(5\) has circumference \(32\) and each five-bit word exactly once as a cyclic contiguous factor. Two cycles are identified only under cyclic rotation; reversal and bit complementation are not quotiented away when reporting the \(2048\)-cycle frequencies. They are used only to compute the additional symmetry-orbit columns. Palindromes are compared as exact strings, not by occurrence positions; multiple occurrences of the same palindrome count once.

All \(2^{\lceil j/2\rceil}\) binary palindromes of each length \(1\le j\le5\) occur in every cycle, since all length-five words occur. Thus each cycle has the fixed contribution
\[
p_1+p_2+p_3+p_4+p_5=2+2+4+4+8=20.
\]
The new distribution measures the additional distinct palindromes whose lengths exceed the defining de Bruijn order.

## Proof

**Exhaustion of cycles, first construction.** Form the order-four binary de Bruijn digraph with \(16\) vertices, labeled by four-bit words, and \(32\) directed edges, labeled by five-bit words. The edge \(e\in\{0,\ldots,31\}\) goes from vertex \(\lfloor e/2\rfloor\) to vertex \(e\bmod16\). Eulerian directed circuits in this graph correspond exactly to binary de Bruijn cycles of order five. Fixing the edge labeled `00000` removes rotational ambiguity, because that edge occurs exactly once. The depth-first enumerator `generate_euler` follows every unused outgoing edge and closes the tour after all \(32\) edges have been used, thus exhausts the possible tours with that fixed edge.

**Independent exhaustion.** At each of the \(16\) vertices there are two incoming and two outgoing edges. An Eulerian circuit determines a bijection from the pair of incoming edges to the pair of outgoing edges. There are exactly two such bijections at each vertex, hence \(2^{16}=65536\) global assignments. Every assignment defines a permutation of all \(32\) directed edges. It describes one Eulerian circuit if and only if this permutation consists of a single \(32\)-cycle. The separate routine `generate_pairings` checks this condition on every assignment. Conversely every Eulerian circuit determines one of the assignments and satisfies the one-cycle condition, so no cycle is omitted. Both generators independently yield the same set of \(2048\) normalized binary cycles.

**Independent palindrome enumeration.** For each generated cycle, `palindromes_direct` explicitly enumerates all \(32\) starting positions and all \(32\) admissible lengths, inserting a factor into a set exactly when the factor equals its reversal. Independently, `palindromes_centers` grows odd- and even-centered palindromes outward around all \(32\) circular centers, stopping at length \(32\). Every nonempty palindrome of length at most \(32\) has exactly one such center parity and is generated by outward matching, while every word generated in this way is palindromic. The two resulting **sets of strings**, not just their cardinalities, agree for every generated cycle.

**Aggregation.** Taking set cardinalities and tallying the \(2048\) cycles gives the seven rows above. Tallying instead the numbers of palindromes of each of the \(32\) lengths yields exactly \(51\) distinct vectors, tabulated without omission in `profiles.csv`. Summing the frequencies verifies \(2048\); exact rational moment calculations give the displayed mean and variance. Reversal and bit complementation preserve the cardinality and each length-specific count. Enumerating the orbits of their four possible combinations yields the additional orbit columns, whose weighted totals again equal \(2048\). This is a complete finite proof: exhaustive algorithms cover a mathematically specified finite set, and the reported outputs are independently cross-checked.

## Verification

Place `verify.py`, `spectrum.csv`, and `profiles.csv` in the same directory, and run `python3 verify.py` with a standard-library Python 3 installation. The script regenerates both cycle sets, checks the defining de Bruijn property independently, compares the two palindrome-set methods on each word, confirms the exact seven-class histogram and the \(51\) full vectors, checks all CSV rows, and recomputes the symmetry orbits and rational moments. Its successful run prints `VERIFY_OK` along with the cycle count, assignment count, profile count, moments, and orbit totals. The CSV files are human-readable exact count tables, not random samples.

Two illustrative cycles beginning with their unique `00000` factors are `00000100110100011101100101011111` and `00000100011010011101100101011111`. Both have longest circular palindrome of length \(7\), yet their numbers of distinct circular palindromes are respectively \(22\) and \(23\). Consequently, knowing only the longest palindrome does not determine the claimed invariant.

## Relationship to prior work

Anisiu, Anisiu, and Kása study palindrome-complexity functions of finite words and show how de Bruijn words can be used to construct all palindromes of a prescribed length. That is a generating device for palindromes, not the frequency distribution of the **distinct circular palindromes actually present in each de Bruijn cycle**. Their full available text does not report the seven-class spectrum or the \(51\) profiles here. Burris, Motta, and Shipman study the construction and existence of *unoriented* de Bruijn sequences, in which reversal pairs are covered rather than every directed length-order factor. Their Eulerian construction theory concerns a different finite family and does not yield the present counts. The standard count \(2048\) and the Eulerian characterization are used as established background, not claimed as new.

The comparison is at the level of logical implication: fixed short-length palindrome counts follow from the defining de Bruijn property; maximum palindrome length alone does not fix distinct palindrome counts, as the explicit pair above demonstrates; and counting reflection classes or unoriented covers does not enumerate palindrome sets of ordinary binary order-five de Bruijn cycles.

## Limitations

The exact classification is for binary **order five**. No formula for other orders or alphabet sizes is asserted. The computational proof relies on complete finite enumeration and fully specified executable procedures, not on an extrapolation from small samples. No independent external audit has been conducted. Historical originality remains conditional on the searched and inspected literature; an earlier unindexed census could exist. The circular invariant here is not the conventional palindromic defect of a particular linear rotation, and it should not be substituted for that invariant.
