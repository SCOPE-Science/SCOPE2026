# Column-reflection fixed loci of four-robust clique complexes of three-row grids

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(G_n=P_3\square P_n\), and let \(K_n=\operatorname{Cliq}_4(G_n)\) consist of the vertex sets \(W\) for which the induced graph \(G_n[W]\) has independence number at most three. Let \(\rho(i,j)=(i,n+1-j)\) reverse the columns. The geometric fixed-point space has the complete integral homotopy classification
\[
|K_n|^{\rho}\simeq
\begin{cases}
*,&n\text{ odd},\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{2n-4}S^2,&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, for even \(n\ge4\), \(\widetilde H_2(|K_n|^\rho;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2n-4}\), and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. For odd \(n\), all reduced integral homology vanishes. The fixed-point space is nonempty in both parities. Consequently the reduced Lefschetz number of \(\rho\) on \(|K_n|\) equals \(2n-4\) for even \(n\) and zero for odd \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(P_3\square P_n\) has three numbered rows and \(n\) numbered columns with horizontal/vertical nearest-neighbor edges. The four-robust clique complex uses the **independent-four-subset forbidden-face definition**, rather than the ordinary matching complex or the usual clique complex. A reflection-fixed geometric point has equal barycentric coordinates on the vertices in each \(\rho\)-orbit. Consequently the fixed space is canonically PL homeomorphic to an orbit complex \(Z_n\): its vertices are reflection orbits in \(V(G_n)\), and an orbit subset is a face precisely when the union of its vertex orbits belongs to \(K_n\). This homeomorphism requires no assumption that the ordinary homotopy type of \(K_n\) is known.

Because \(G_n\) is bipartite, König's theorem gives \(\alpha(G_n[W])=|W|-\nu(G_n[W])\), where \(\nu\) is the maximum matching size. If \(W\in K_n\), the bipartition also gives \(|W|\le 2\alpha(G_n[W])\le6\). These facts will be used repeatedly.

## Proof
**Odd column count.** Put \(n=2h+1\), \(h\ge1\). There are three singleton vertex orbits in the middle column: upper \(a\), middle \(c\), and lower \(b\). All remaining orbits comprise two vertices, one on either side of the middle column. We prove \(c\) is a cone vertex of \(Z_n\).

Let \(W\) be an invariant face not containing \(c\). Write \(r\) for the number of selected two-vertex orbits and \(f\in\{0,1,2\}\) for the number selected from \(\{a,b\}\). Then \(|W|=2r+f\le6\). If \(r=0\), adding \(c\) gives at most three vertices, hence a face. If \(r=1,f\le1\), the enlarged set has at most four vertices; when it has four, it contains the middle-column edge joining \(c\) to the selected member of \(\{a,b\}\). If \(r=1,f=2\), the four vertices of \(W\) form a face only if the paired orbit is the row-adjacent orbit next to the middle column in the upper or lower row. Indeed \(a,b\) are nonadjacent and have no other neighbors in the off-axis columns. That neighbor can be matched to the corresponding endpoint, and \(c\) can be matched to the other middle-column endpoint, giving a matching of size two on the enlarged five vertices. Thus it is a face.

If \(r=2,f=0\), the four vertices of \(W\) form a face exactly when the two chosen orbits are adjacent in one off-axis copy of the half-grid. This gives two disjoint edges, so adding \(c\) still leaves independence number at most three. If \(r=2,f=1\), the five vertices form a face only when the two chosen orbits are adjacent in the half-grid: otherwise there are no edges among their four off-axis vertices, and the sole selected middle-column vertex can participate in at most one matching edge, forcing matching number at most one and independence number at least four. When the two orbits are adjacent, take the matching edge on each side and the edge between \(c\) and the selected middle-column vertex; these three disjoint edges form a perfect matching after adding \(c\).

The case \(r=2,f=2\) cannot be a face: a perfect matching on its six vertices would have to match \(a\) and \(b\) to the upper and lower near-middle off-axis orbits, respectively. The two unused off-axis vertices would be in different rows, and cannot be adjacent, so a perfect matching is impossible. Likewise \(r=3,f=0\) cannot be a face: with no middle-column vertices there are two disconnected halves, each on three selected vertices, precluding a perfect matching. All remaining cases exceed six vertices. Hence \(W\cup\{c\}\) is a face whenever \(W\) is, proving the cone condition. Thus \(|K_{2h+1}|^\rho\cong|Z_{2h+1}|\) is contractible.

**Even column count.** Put \(n=2h\), \(h\ge2\). Every reflection orbit has two vertices. Identify its two-vertex orbit with a vertex of the folded rectangular grid \(H=P_3\square P_h\). Write \(M=\{m_1,m_2,m_3\}\) for the three vertices in the column adjacent to the reflection axis, and let \(L\) be the induced subgraph on \(U=V(H)\setminus M\). Thus \(L\cong P_3\square P_{h-1}\) is a connected graph with cycle rank \(\beta_1(L)=2(h-2)\). A selected orbit subset has cardinality at most three.

A two-orbit subset is a face of \(Z_{2h}\) if and only if it contains a vertex of \(M\), or its two vertices form an edge of \(L\). Indeed each orbit in \(M\) contains a horizontal edge crossing the reflection axis, and without such an orbit the two reflected copies are disjoint. A three-orbit subset is a face exactly when it contains a distinguished vertex \(m\in M\) such that its other two vertices form an edge of \(H\). To see this, the six original vertices form a face precisely when they admit a perfect matching, by König's theorem. Each half of the grid contains three selected vertices; their number of matching edges crossing the reflection axis is therefore odd. It is either one (a central crossing edge plus corresponding matching edges in the two halves) or three (all three selected orbits are in \(M\)). Both possibilities have exactly the stated orbit criterion. In the second case, after selecting an endpoint of the central three-vertex path \(H[M]\) as distinguished, the other two are adjacent.

Let \(D_3\) denote the discrete complex on the three vertices \(M\). The just-derived orbit description shows that \(Z_{2h}\) contains the simplicial join \(A=D_3*L\): it consists of \(L\), its three cones with apex in \(M\), and all their subfaces. Outside \(A\), the orbit complex has precisely three extra edges \(\{m_i,m_j\}\) and seven extra triangles: \(M\) itself, together with the six triangles \(\{m_i,m_j,u_i\}\) and \(\{m_i,m_j,u_j\}\) for the three unordered pairs \(\{i,j\}\subset\{1,2,3\}\), where \(u_i\in U\) is the unique neighbor of \(m_i\) in the preceding folded column.

Form \(B\) by attaching to \(A\), for each pair \(\{i,j\}\), the edge \(\{m_i,m_j\}\) together with the triangle \(\{m_i,m_j,u_i\}\). In \(B\), each new edge is a free face of its selected triangle, so these three elementary collapses yield \(B\searrow A\). The remaining four triangles of \(Z_{2h}\) attach along closed loops in \(B\). The join \(A=D_3*L\) is simply connected because \(L\) is connected (it is the union of three cones on \(L\)). Thus \(B\) is simply connected, every four attaching loop is null-homotopic, and \(|Z_{2h}|\simeq|A|\vee\bigvee^4S^2\).

Finally \(L\simeq\bigvee^{2h-4}S^1\), and the join of three discrete points with \(L\) is homotopy equivalent to the wedge of two suspensions of \(L\). Therefore \(A\simeq\bigvee^{4h-8}S^2\), and altogether \(|Z_{2h}|\simeq\bigvee^{4h-4}S^2=\bigvee^{2n-4}S^2\). The conclusion includes the boundary case \(h=2\), where \(L\) is a tree and \(A\) is contractible. This proves the theorem integrally.

## Verification
The independent script `verify_fixed.py` enumerates every vertex-orbit subset of total size at most six, tests the forbidden independent-four-subset condition against the original grid adjacency relation, constructs the predicted folded complex in the even cases, and checks the central cone criterion in the odd cases. It checks all \(38\) widths \(3\le n\le40\). In addition, it computes exact mod-two reduced simplicial homology of the fixed orbit complex at each width and compares it with the theorem. Every check passes. The script's finite checks are not the all-width proof; the proof is the orbit classification and homotopy decomposition above.

## Relationship to prior work
Filakovský, *The Topology of \(k\)-Robust Clique Complexes in Grid-like Graphs* (first posted February 11, 2026; arXiv:2602.11365v1) introduced these complexes, proved ordinary homotopy theorems for \(\(k=2,3\)\), and stated a higher-\(k\) conjecture. The present theorem concerns the actual geometric fixed-point spaces of the **four**-robust complexes, not the ordinary homotopy type or the truth of the general conjecture. Published results for three-robust complexes under grid reflections concern different simplicial complexes and do not imply this fixed-locus classification. The orbit-barycenter construction and the join/collapse principles are standard tools; their application to these specific four-robust fixed spaces is the asserted result.

## Limitations
No nonequivariant homotopy classification of the full four-robust complex \(K_n\) is claimed, and no formula for its complete action on individual homology groups is claimed. The result treats reflection reversing columns of **three-row grids** only; row reflection, half-turn, other grid sizes, and general square-sequence graphs are outside the theorem. Independent review has not been performed. An unindexed prior treatment of this particular fixed-locus problem could alter the originality assessment.
