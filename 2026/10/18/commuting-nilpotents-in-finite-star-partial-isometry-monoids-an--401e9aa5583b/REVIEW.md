# Review

## Correctness — PASS

A direct star-distance analysis partitions every nilpotent partial isometry into a leaf-only nilpotent or one of the single-hub arrows and two-arrow hub paths. The three mixed commutation tests are verified by arrow composition. The numbers of admissible source/terminal choices are \(c\), \(c\), and \(c^2-a_1\); the hub--hub pairs contribute \(3m^2-m\). The path-shape weight \(m!/\prod_j a_j!\) and the exponential-formula derivation are fully justified. Edge cases \(m=0,1\) are included. An independent metric-level enumeration for \(n\le6\) agrees with the closed formula. Risks: finite enumeration cannot prove a universal statement; the symbolic proof carries that burden.

## Originality — PASS, subject to normal literature limits

Fernandes--Paulista's complete open-access source provides the element decomposition, ranks, Green relations, and presentation, but not this commuting-nilpotent statistic or its cross-stratum compatibility rules. Laradji--Umar count single nilpotent maps in the symmetric inverse setting, not commuting pairs involving star-hub maps. Searches for the main claim, alternative wordings, partial-isometry commuting graphs, path partition statistics, and broader coverage found no equivalent published claim. The most similar retrieved records concerned different objects (such as nilpotent matrices or commuting graphs of finite groups), without implications for partial star isometries. Risks: the full text of the 2004 baseline paper was not accessible for detailed inspection, and no search can prove universal novelty; an external audit has not been performed.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete exact answer to a natural new statistic for a structurally studied inverse transformation monoid: the number of commuting nilpotent pairs that interact with the exceptional hub stratum. It provides both a structural commutation criterion and a closed correction to the leaf-only baseline, without attempting to solve the harder full symmetric-inverse-monoid commuting problem. The partition weights identify precisely the contributions of path sources, terminals and isolated vertices, making the boundary informative beyond a raw small-order census. The invariant is motivated by commutation in transformation monoids and is neither a parameter substitution nor a re-tabulation of known single-nilpotent counts. The work is limited to stars and to the hub correction.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
