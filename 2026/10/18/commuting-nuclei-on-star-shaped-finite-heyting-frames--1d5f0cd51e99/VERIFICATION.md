# Reproduction of finite checks

Use Python 3 with standard-library modules only. From the directory containing this document, run `python3 verify.py`.

The checker constructs the upset lattice of a rooted star independently from the combinatorial formulas. It generates nucleus maps pointwise from their subset parameter and checks extensivity, monotonicity via meet preservation, idempotence, and preservation of every binary meet for \(1\le n\le4\). For \(n=1,2\), it additionally enumerates every possible self-map on the complete upset lattice and verifies that the maps satisfying all nucleus axioms agree exactly with those generated by subsets. It computes commuting ordered pairs directly from the maps and compares them to the theorem's predicate and closed count.

Maximal commuting subsets are computed by an independent maximal-clique search on the full compatibility graph for \(1\le n\le4\). Direct enumeration of ordered pairwise-compatible tuples tests the closed expression for \(1\le n\le3\) and \(0\le k\le3\). Successful completion prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The checker does not enumerate arbitrary self-maps for \(n\ge3\), where their number grows very rapidly. Numerical or finite exhaustive checks do not certify the universal theorem; that relies on the direct mathematical proof in `RESULT.md`. No third-party data files or network access are required.
