# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement uses one explicit weighted shift family, a compact positive parameter set, and precisely specified WOT/SOT cluster notions. The coordinate formula is exact; the superlacunary scale separation is sufficient to exhaust all subsequential limits. The fixed-distance spike case yields \(D_{r,\alpha}\), while a distant next spike yields a common scalar diagonal. Explicit choices of subsequences realize all claimed limits, and bounded coordinatewise convergence is strong. A pair of diagonal evaluations isolates each clopen fiber. No unverified infinite extrapolation or computer-dependent lemma is used.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Ji–Yang (arXiv:2608.21553v2, Question 3 and Section 4) characterizes the bounded-below case and proves that one sparse-spike construction has a disconnected WOT cluster set by a single coefficient. It does not identify the full WOT/SOT cluster set for that example, classify its connected components, or realize arbitrary compact positive transversal profiles with unchanged scalar asymptotic numerical ranges. The published irreducible weighted-shift power-profile result concerns operator norm and essential norm sequences, not the operator-valued root-modulus cluster set. Ignatev's power-set result concerns local resolvent growth of quasinilpotent operators and does not imply this claim. Similar earlier work on logarithmic moving-average limit operators remains a specific priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The paper's explicit question about operator cluster-set structure is answered completely for a natural non-bounded-below family, not merely by another disconnectedness witness. All nonempty compact positive sets appear as transversal fibers; this supplies both interval and totally disconnected examples and shows that the full operator omega-limit geometry contains information invisible to **every** scalar asymptotic numerical range. The restriction to a constructed family is disclosed, and the result is not presented as a classification of all shifts.

## Closest literature and limitations
Closest source: Y. Ji and B. Yang, *Asymptotic Numerical Ranges and Invariant Subspaces of Operators*, arXiv:2608.21553 (2026), full relevant sections inspected. The source's Example 4.3 is special case antecedent, not claimed as original. The result is a concrete-class exact limit-set classification; general non-bounded-below operators are not addressed. No independent verification has been performed.
