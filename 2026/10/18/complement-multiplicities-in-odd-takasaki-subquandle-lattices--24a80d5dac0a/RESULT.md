# Complement multiplicities in odd Takasaki subquandle lattices

## Finding
Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of odd order, \(|A|\ge3\). Its Takasaki quandle \(T(A)\) has operation
\[
x\triangleright y=2y-x.
\]
Give its subquandle lattice a bottom element \(\varnothing\). For each nonempty proper subquandle \(U\), let \(\mathcal C_U(z)\) enumerate lattice complements \(V\) with weight \(z^{|V|}\); that is, \(U\cap V=\varnothing\) and the quandle generated by \(U\cup V\) is \(T(A)\).

Every nonempty subquandle is an affine coset \(a+H\) of a subgroup \(H\le A\). Fix \(U=a+H\). Then the complete complement-size polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mathcal C_{a+H}(z)=
\sum_{\substack{K\le A\\ A/(H+K)\ \mathrm{cyclic},\ [A:H+K]>1}}
[H+K:K] \,\varphi([A:H+K])\,z^{|K|}.}
\]
The formula is independent of \(a\). The cyclic-quotient condition is essential: the Euler-totient factor is applied only when \(A/(H+K)\) is cyclic and has order exceeding one.

For the odd cyclic group \(A=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), write the fixed coset as \(a+d\mathbb Z_n\), with \(1<d\mid n\). Its complete polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mathcal C_d(z)=
\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\\gcd(d,e)>1}}
\frac{e}{\gcd(d,e)}\,\varphi(\gcd(d,e))\,z^{n/e}.}
\]
Define \(\sigma(t)=\sum_{s\mid t}s\) and
\[
P_d(n)=\prod_{p\mid d}p^{v_p(n)}.
\]
Then the *number* of complements has the closed form
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \mathcal C_d(1)=(P_d(n)-1)\,\sigma\!\left(\frac n{P_d(n)}\right).}
\]
Hence the number depends only on which prime divisors of \(n\) divide \(d\), not their valuations inside \(d\). If \(n\) is an odd prime power, every nonempty proper subquandle has exactly \(n-1\) complements.

The full polynomial records genuinely more information: for \(A=C_3\times C_9\), the two order-three subgroups \(H_0=\{0\}\times3C_9\) and \(H_1=C_3\times\{0\}\) have respective complement polynomials
\[
\mathcal C_{H_0}(z)=18z^3+8z^9,
\qquad \mathcal C_{H_1}(z)=18z+24z^3+2z^9.
\]
Thus complements distinguish embeddings of equal-size subquandles.

## Assumptions and scope
The group \(A\) is finite, abelian, and of odd order at least three. We count **lattice** complements (disjoint meet, generated join), not set-theoretic complements nor complements in the underlying group subgroup lattice. \(U\) is nonempty and proper. The empty subquandle and the whole quandle have the obvious mutual complement, but those two boundary cases are not included in the formulas. A complement \(V\) of a nonempty proper \(U\) is necessarily nonempty and proper.

For \(A=\mathbb Z_n\), the divisor \(d\) is the index \([A:d\mathbb Z_n]\), so \(|U|=n/d\). All indices and orders in formulas are finite positive integers. Euler's function \(\varphi\) counts generators of finite cyclic groups; the divisor-sum function \(\sigma\) is used only on positive integers.

## Proof
**Coset classification.** Let \(S\) be a nonempty subquandle, and fix \(a\in S\). Right reflections \(R_y(x)=2y-x\) preserve \(S\) and, since \(A\) is odd and \(S\) finite, act bijectively on it. Their composites satisfy \(R_yR_z(x)=x+2(y-z)\) for \(y,z\in S\). Let \(H=\langle y-z:y,z\in S\rangle\). Because multiplication by two is an automorphism of the odd-order group \(A\), the subgroup generated by all \(2(y-z)\) is exactly \(H\). Therefore \(S\) is invariant under translations by \(H\), so \(a+H\subseteq S\). The reverse inclusion follows from the definition of \(H\). Conversely, every affine coset is closed under the Takasaki operation, hence is a subquandle.

**Complement criterion.** Take \(U=a+H\), \(V=b+K\), put \(M=H+K\), and \(t=b-a\). The affine cosets intersect exactly when \(t\in M\). Their join is the affine coset \(a+\langle H,K,t\rangle\) by the preceding coset classification. Thus they are lattice complements exactly when
\[
t\notin M,\qquad A=M+\langle t\rangle.
\]
Equivalently, \(A/M\) is a nontrivial cyclic group generated by \(t+M\). For fixed \(K\), each generator of \(A/M\) has \(|M|\) lifts \(t\in A\), and every \(K\)-coset accounts for exactly \(|K|\) lifts. Hence exactly
\[
[M:K]\varphi([A:M])
\]
choices of \(V=b+K\) are complements when the quotient is nontrivial cyclic, and none otherwise. Summing with \(|V|=|K|\) proves the first polynomial.

**Cyclic specialization.** Subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_n\) are uniquely \(e\mathbb Z_n\) for \(e\mid n\), with order \(n/e\). For \(H=d\mathbb Z_n\) and \(K=e\mathbb Z_n\), the sum \(M\) is \(g\mathbb Z_n\), where \(g=\gcd(d,e)\). The quotient has order \(g\) and is cyclic, while \([M:K]=e/g\). The required strict inequality \(g>1\) gives the second polynomial.

**Prime-support simplification.** The sum of coefficients of the second polynomial factors over prime powers. If \(p^\alpha\parallel n\) and \(p^\beta\parallel d\) with \(\beta\ge1\), the unrestricted local sum is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha}p^{j-\min(j,\beta)}\varphi(p^{\min(j,\beta)})
=\sum_{j=0}^{\beta}\varphi(p^j)+\sum_{j=\beta+1}^{\alpha}p^{j-\beta}\varphi(p^\beta)
=p^\alpha.
\]
For primes dividing \(n\) but not \(d\), the local sum is \(1+p+\cdots+p^\alpha\). Consequently the unrestricted product equals \(P_d(n)\sigma(n/P_d(n))\). The terms excluded by \(\gcd(d,e)=1\) force exponent zero at every prime dividing \(d\) and contribute \(\sigma(n/P_d(n))\). Subtraction gives \((P_d(n)-1)\sigma(n/P_d(n))\) and all claimed consequences.

The two displayed \(C_3\times C_9\) polynomials follow by applying the already proved subgroup formula to the ten subgroups of \(C_3\times C_9\), or by directly enumerating the cosets. Both approaches agree in the included finite checker.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify.py` using the Python standard library. It independently constructs subquandle closures and checks the general quotient count against direct lattice-complement tests for \(C_3\times C_3\), \(C_3\times C_5\), and \(C_3\times C_9\). For cyclic orders \(3,5,7,9,11,15,21,27,35,45\), it checks every divisor-index and complement size profile against direct join-and-meet enumeration, as well as the prime-support formula. For orders up to eleven, it enumerates every nonempty subset to verify the coset classification directly. The script returns `CHECKER_OK`.

All infinite-family conclusions are established by the proof above, not inferred from finite enumeration. These computations check boundary cases and the finite arithmetic but are not independent mathematical certification.

## Relationship to prior work
Amsberry, Bergquist, Horstkamp, Lee, and Yetter study quandle subobject lattices, establish structural conditions on strongly complemented subquandles, and discuss finite complementedness. Strong complementation there means the *set-theoretic* difference is itself a subquandle; it is not the present enumeration of all lattice complements. Their definitions and Theorem 3.7 were inspected in the available full text.

Saki and Kiani establish that odd Takasaki subquandles are cosets and study their lattice structure. Their 2020 article is a close precursor; the accessible abstract concerns coset classification, lattice homotopy, grading, and rigidity, and does not assert the complement multiplicity formulas above. Its full article was not accessible in this investigation. The distinct subrack-lattice work of Saki and Kiani establishes existence of complements in finite racks and describes coset phenomena, not the full size-refined enumeration checked here.

The present result turns those structural existence questions into exact complement-count invariants, including prime-support stability and sensitivity to the subgroup embedding. No checked source was found that implies the explicit polynomials or their closed cyclic totals, but this is a bounded literature comparison, not an exhaustive novelty certificate.

## Limitations
The odd-order hypothesis is essential for the full coset classification as proved here; no even-order classification is claimed. The formula concerns ordinary lattice complements and does not enumerate strongly complemented subquandles separately. The general finite-abelian formula is a finite subgroup-lattice sum, not a closed elementary expression for every group decomposition. A closely relevant 2020 article could only be inspected through its abstract, so an equivalent prior count in its inaccessible body remains a residual originality risk. No independent audit has been performed.
