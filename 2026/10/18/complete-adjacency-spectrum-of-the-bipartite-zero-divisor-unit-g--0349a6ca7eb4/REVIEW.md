# Scientific review: exact spectrum of the zero-divisor–unit graph

## Correctness
**PASS.** For each nonzero proper support \(S\), the incidence row is the complement of the product of coordinate nonmatch indicators. Coordinatewise multiplication by units makes the Gram matrix translation-invariant on the finite abelian unit group. Its characters form a complete orthogonal basis. The row Fourier transforms are computed explicitly and summed over all support rows; the trivial-character calculation includes both excluded support boundaries. The resulting eigenvalues are nonnegative with exactly the predicted full-support zero eigenspace. The block bipartite matrix gives the announced characteristic polynomial, with all multiplicities and the one-field boundary specified. Thirteen exact-integer characteristic-polynomial checks and direct modular-addition checks reproduced the formulas. None of the infinite-parameter conclusions depend on finite enumeration.

## Originality
**PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** The v1 full text of Mehry–Eisapoor Khasadan arXiv:2511.07854, Section 8, explicitly poses the spectrum of this graph on finite reduced rings as an open direction. Its other inspected sections have no adjacency eigenvalue formula. Masalkar et al. arXiv:2106.15977v2 study the different multiplicative zero-divisor graph, and Demiroğlu Karabulut–Espinosa arXiv:2605.05457v2 study a different additive Cayley graph where differences are invertible. No relevant indexed result or recoverable same-topic claim was found that implies the present full \(2^r\)-type Fourier-Gram formula or its incidence-nullity consequence. The original manuscript's later withdrawal/restructuring and possible independently known equivalent association-scheme formulas are residual literature risks, not evidence that a matching theorem exists.

## Value
**PASS.** The result solves the source's explicit spectral question for the entire natural class of finite reduced rings, not just two field factors or finitely many orders. The closed multiplicities reveal exact rank and nullity and handle arbitrary mixtures of finite-field orders, including characteristic two. This is a structurally motivated algebraic graph invariant rather than a sample computation or isolated numeric improvement.
