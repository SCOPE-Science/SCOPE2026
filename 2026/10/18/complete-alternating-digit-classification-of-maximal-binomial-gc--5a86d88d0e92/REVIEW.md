# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The base-\(p\) proof exhausts three disjoint digit-support cases. Mixed-parity support gives a zero-borrow witness unless the number has exactly the two claimed unit digits. Single-parity support gives a one-borrow witness. In the exceptional two-unit case, Kummer's theorem together with the bounded alternating digit sum excludes zero and one carry, and explicit selected indices attain exactly two carries. The finite exact-arithmetic verifier supports but does not replace these quantified arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The motivating 2026 paper establishes the sharp global maximum but not the complete equality locus for the first exponent \(m=p+1\); it treats least extremal rows for higher exponents separately. McTague's congruence criterion assumes a different relation between the restricted index modulus and the prime, and the relevant theorem statements in Wu address other parameters. The closest accessible prior result involving \(m=p^r-1\) and \(p=2\) only examines a bounded initial range and does not imply this full locus. Searches of mathematical records and primary literature returned no covering theorem. Residual risk is an implicit statement in broad binomial divisibility literature or unretrieved appendices.

## Value — PASS
The location of **every** maximum-valuation row is a natural exact invariant requested by the sharp-extrema framework. It is an infinite sparse two-digit classification in the first exponent family left outside the motivating paper's least-row theorem; the classification is not a restatement of the known upper bound or first witness. Alternating digit congruences impose a non-obvious obstruction to a single carry, potentially reusable in other modulus families.

## Limitations
No exact classification of non-extremal valuations is claimed. Priority assessment is best-knowledge, and external mathematical validation has not been performed.
