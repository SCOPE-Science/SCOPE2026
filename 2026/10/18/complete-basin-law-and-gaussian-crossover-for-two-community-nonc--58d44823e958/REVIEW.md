# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The original synchronous self-inclusive NCO spin rule is reproduced exactly. Every integer neighbor-field is in one of three disjoint classes (at most minus two, between minus one and one, or at least plus two). This proves the induced full-part overwrite/retention rule for each \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). Nine combinations of low, central and high part counts yield the four exhaustive outcomes within two rounds. Independence of binomial part counts proves exact basin probabilities; symmetry and Stirling's formula prove parity-dependent finite-size limits; a binomial triangular-array central limit theorem proves the critical Gaussian window. Exhaustive small-system and exact rational checks support, but do not replace, the proof.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual risk.** Liu–Achterberg–Kooij (2024) formulates the NCO update and approximates dynamics in low degree-correlation network ensembles rather than classifying complete bipartite basins exactly. Shao–Havlin–Stanley (2009) and ben-Avraham (2011) concern other network geometries. Fedrigo (2024) proves a complete bipartite outcome theorem for a different neighbor-threshold game without self-retaining central configurations; its outcome classification does not imply the present NCO four-part exact law, the frozen-state parity coefficient or the critical-window distribution. General binary majority period-two bounds are weaker. No covering claim was identified in the specific literature and semantic research-record searches. Residual risk: an equivalent calculation may appear in other threshold-automaton studies; one potentially adjacent student report was not available as full text.

**Value — PASS.** A complete bipartite graph is a natural, exactly solvable prototype of two internally exchangeable groups with dense cross-group ties and no intragroup links. The theorem contrasts the NCO model's possible coexistence of opinions with an unexpectedly nonvanishing asymptotic probability \(1/2\) of permanent alternation, even as each group's degree diverges. Its four-way full-state partition, exact probability law, parity-sensitive finite-size term and Gaussian bias transition answer a coherent finite-to-large-population question not given by the motivating mean-field approximation. They are not merely another isolated parameter example.

**Limits.** No extension is claimed to arbitrary two-community graphs, asynchronous dynamics, or other self-weights. The later threshold-protocol study and a potentially relevant uninspected document remain originality risks. Numerical verification is finite.
