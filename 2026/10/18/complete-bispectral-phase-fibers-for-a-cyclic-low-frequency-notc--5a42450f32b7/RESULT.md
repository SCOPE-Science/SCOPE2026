# Complete bispectral phase fibers for a cyclic low-frequency notch

## Finding

Let \(r\ge 3\) and \(N>2r\) be integers. Define the cyclic frequency mask
\[
S_{N,r}=\{-r,\ldots,-2,2,\ldots,r\}\pmod N.
\]
For any complex function on \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) whose Fourier transform is nonzero **exactly** on this mask, the pair consisting of its Fourier power spectrum and its complete third-order bispectrum has the following entire ambiguity group. Here \(\mathbb T\) is the unit circle, \(\mu_h\) is the finite group of \(h\)-th roots of unity, and the displayed group acts freely on the fixed-data fiber.

| Bandwidth | Cyclic order | Complete phase-ambiguity group |
|---|---|---|
| \(r=3\) | \(N=7,8\) | \(\mathbb T\) |
| \(r=3\) | \(N=9\) | \(\mathbb T^2\times\mu_3\) |
| \(r=3\) | \(N\ge10\) | \(\mathbb T^4\) |
| \(r=4\) | \(N=9,10\) | \(\mu_N\) |
| \(r=4\) | \(N=11\) | \(\mathbb T\) |
| \(r=4\) | \(N=12\) | \(\mathbb T^2\times\mu_6\) |
| \(r=4\) | \(N\ge13\) | \(\mathbb T^3\) |
| \(r=5\) | \(N=11,12,13\) | \(\mu_N\) |
| \(r=5\) | \(N=14\) | \(\mathbb T\times\mu_2\) |
| \(r=5\) | \(N=15\) | \(\mathbb T\times\mu_3\) |
| \(r=5\) | \(N\ge16\) | \(\mathbb T^2\) |
| \(r\ge6\) | \(2r<N\le3r\) | \(\mu_N\) |
| \(r\ge6\) | \(N>3r\) | \(\mathbb T\) |

**Sharp identification criterion.** The measurements identify the input uniquely up to cyclic translation if and only if either \(r=4\) and \(N\in\{9,10\}\), or \(r=5\) and \(N\in\{11,12,13\}\), or \(r\ge6\) and \(2r<N\le3r\). The absence of frequencies \(0,\pm1\) is therefore compatible with exact translation-only bispectral identification at sufficiently high occupied bandwidth; outside the indicated regimes there are continuously many inequivalent signals with identical measurements.

For \(r\ge6\), the phases can be parameterized explicitly by
\[
u(k)=z^k,\qquad k\in\{-r,\ldots,-2,2,\ldots,r\},
\]
where \(z\in\mathbb T\) is arbitrary when \(N>3r\), but must satisfy \(z^N=1\) when \(2r<N\le3r\). Thus the sharp transition is exactly at \(N=3r\).

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(G=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), with unnormalized Fourier transform and inverse
\[
F(k)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)e^{-2\pi i kx/N},\qquad
f(x)=\frac1N\sum_{k\in G}F(k)e^{2\pi i kx/N}.
\]
The measured power and bispectrum are
\[
P_f(k)=|F(k)|^2,\qquad
B_f(a,b)=F(a)F(b)\overline{F(a+b)}.
\]
All indexes are reduced modulo \(N\). The support hypothesis means \(F(k)\ne0\) for **every** \(k\in S_{N,r}\) and \(F(k)=0\) outside the mask, including its missing DC and first positive/negative harmonics. Coefficients may be arbitrary nonzero complex numbers. Both \(P_f\) and the entire array \(B_f\) are supplied; this theorem does not claim that \(B_f\) alone reveals all magnitudes when \(F(0)=0\). The signals are not assumed real or positive.

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction to an integer relation group.** For a second signal \(g\) with the same power spectrum, its Fourier transform is \(G(k)=u(k)F(k)\) on \(S_{N,r}\), for a unique phase map \(u:S_{N,r}\to\mathbb T\). Equality of all nonzero bispectral entries is exactly
\[
u(a)u(b)=u(a+b)\quad\text{if }a,b,a+b\in S_{N,r}\pmod N.
\]
The entries with a frequency outside the mask vanish for both signals. Conversely every such \(u\) yields a second signal with precisely the same measurements via Fourier inversion. Hence the fiber is the character group of the finitely presented abelian group
\[
\Lambda_{N,r}=\mathbb Z^{S_{N,r}}\big/
\langle e_a+e_b-e_c:a+b\equiv c\pmod N,\ a,b,c\in S_{N,r}\rangle.
\]
The character group is \(\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_{N,r},\mathbb T)\). The quotient presentation counts phases exactly, including finite torsion; the absence of any extra constraints follows from the preceding equivalence, not from a count of equations alone.

Use signed integer representatives \(-r,\ldots,-2,2,\ldots,r\), unique because \(N>2r\). Every valid relation has \(a+b-c\in\{0,\pm N\}\), since \(|a+b-c|\le3r<2N\). Call a relation with integer sum zero *unwrapped*.

**2. All larger bandwidths.** Suppose \(r\ge6\). In the unwrapped presentation let \(t=e_3-e_2\). The relations for the triples \((2,2,4)\), \((2,3,5)\), \((3,3,6)\) and \((2,4,6)\) imply
\[
e_4=2e_2,\quad e_5=e_2+e_3,\quad
2e_3=3e_2,\quad e_2=2t,\quad e_3=3t.
\]
Relations \((2,j,j+2)\) propagate \(e_k=kt\) to every positive \(2\le k\le r\). The negative-frequency analogs first yield an independent generator \(t'\) with \(e_{-k}=kt'\). But the unwrapped relations \((4,-2,2)\) and \((5,-3,2)\) give \(e_{-2}=-2t\) and \(e_{-3}=-3t\); hence \(t'=-t\). Therefore the entire unwrapped presentation is generated by \(t\) and satisfies
\[
e_k=kt\qquad(k\in S_{N,r}).
\]
It has no further torsion: mapping \(e_k\mapsto k\in\mathbb Z\) respects every unwrapped relation and sends \(t\) to \(1\). It is thus exactly \(\mathbb Z\).

A wrapped relation becomes \(\pm Nt=0\). If \(N>3r\), no such relation is possible and \(\Lambda_{N,r}\cong\mathbb Z\). If \(2r<N\le3r\), choose \(a,b,c\in\{2,\ldots,r\}\) with \(a+b+c=N\), which is possible because the triple sum of the integer interval \(\{2,\ldots,r\}\) is every integer from \(6\) to \(3r\). Then \(a+b\equiv-c\pmod N\), so \(e_a+e_b-e_{-c}=Nt=0\). Every other wrapped relation is also a multiple of \(Nt\); consequently \(\Lambda_{N,r}\cong\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). This proves the two infinite-family rows and their explicit phase parameterizations.

**3. The four-mode notch, \(r=3\).** There are no unwrapped triples on \(\{\pm2,\pm3\}\). When \(N\ge10\), there are no wrapped triples either, giving \(\mathbb Z^4\). For \(N=7\), direct enumeration of all allowed triples yields the equivalent relations
\[
e_{-3}=2e_2,\quad e_3=-2e_2,\quad e_{-2}=-e_2,
\]
with \(e_2\) free. For \(N=8\) they yield
\[
e_2=-2e_3,\quad e_{-2}=2e_3,\quad e_{-3}=-e_3,
\]
with \(e_3\) free. For \(N=9\), the only nontrivial equations reduce to \(e_{-3}=2e_3\) and \(3e_3=0\), while \(e_2\) and \(e_{-2}\) remain free. Thus the three asserted groups follow.

**4. The six-mode notch, \(r=4\).** All unwrapped relations are generated by
\[
e_4=2A,\quad e_{-2}=-A,\quad e_{-4}=-2A,\quad e_3=B,\quad e_{-3}=C,\quad A=e_2,
\]
so the unwrapped group is free on \(A,B,C\). For the only remaining wrapped orders, the following rows span the complete additional integer-relation lattice, with triples represented by their coefficient rows in the basis \((A,B,C)\):

| \(N\) | Generating wrapped-relation rows |
|---|---|
| 9 | \((3,1,0),(3,0,-1),(0,2,-1)\) |
| 10 | \((5,0,0),(2,2,0),(2,1,-1)\) |
| 11 | \((4,1,0),(4,0,-1)\) |
| 12 | \((6,0,0)\) |

For \(N=9\) the rows give \(B=-3A\), \(C=3A\), \(9A=0\); for \(N=10\), they give \(5A=0\), \(2(B+A)=0\), \(C=B+2A\), hence \(\mathbb Z/5\oplus\mathbb Z/2\cong\mathbb Z/10\). For \(N=11\), they give \(B=-4A\), \(C=4A\), with \(A\) free. For \(N=12\), they give \(6A=0\), with \(B,C\) free. No wrapped relations occur once \(N\ge13\). The finite table follows.

**5. The eight-mode notch, \(r=5\).** The unwrapped relations reduce exactly to two free generators \(A=e_2\), \(B=e_3\):
\[
e_4=2A,\qquad e_5=A+B,\qquad e_{-k}=-e_k\quad(2\le k\le5).
\]
Each wrapped relation is therefore equivalent to a sum of three integers in \(\{2,3,4,5\}\) equaling \(N\), possibly after replacing the relation by its negative. All additional relations are generated by the following rows in basis \((A,B)\):

| \(N\) | Generating wrapped-relation rows | Resulting group |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | \((4,1),(1,3)\) | \(\mathbb Z/11\) |
| 12 | \((3,2),(6,0)\) | \(\mathbb Z/12\) |
| 13 | \((2,3),(5,1)\) | \(\mathbb Z/13\) |
| 14 | \((4,2)\) | \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\) |
| 15 | \((3,3)\) | \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/3\) |

The first three integer matrices have respective determinant absolute values \(11,12,13\), and their entries have greatest common divisor \(1\), so their quotients are cyclic of those orders. The last two rows have entry gcd \(2\) and \(3\), respectively. No wrapped relation exists for \(N\ge16\), leaving \(\mathbb Z^2\). This completes the classification.

**6. Translation criterion.** A cyclic translation multiplies Fourier frequency \(k\) by \(e^{2\pi i kt/N}\) for some integer \(t\) (possibly with the opposite sign depending on translation convention). The translation subgroup has exactly \(N\) elements because the mask contains both frequencies \(2\) and \(3\), whose common gcd with \(N\) is one. Thus whenever the full ambiguity group is \(\mu_N\), the translation subgroup exhausts it. Every other row has a positive-dimensional torus factor, producing infinitely many inequivalent classes. This proves the sharp iff statement.

## Verification

A companion program `check.py` enumerates every valid bispectral triple for specified finite cases and computes the **integer** Smith normal form of the relation matrix. It checks the table for 76 distinct \((N,r)\) pairs and separately checks 68 sharp wrap/no-wrap witness configurations. Run it with Python 3 and SymPy 1.12 or later using `python check.py`.

This is a finite consistency and boundary check only. The proof above establishes the statements for every integer \(r\ge3\), \(N>2r\) by the explicit presentations and the no-wrap argument; the program does not claim an exhaustive experiment over all parameters.

## Relationship to prior work

Kakarala (2009) develops the third-order Fourier invariant on compact groups and establishes bispectrum uniqueness for nonsingular Fourier transforms and certain subgroup-closed nonzero blocks. Those sufficient conditions do not apply to this mask because it omits \(0\) and is not closed under addition. Pinilla, Mishra and Sadler (2021) study inversion for bandlimited signals under generic nonzero-coordinate assumptions; their stated recovery proof uses a nonzero zero-frequency coefficient. The present mask has three forced central zeros and requires an exact, nongeneric phase-fiber calculation. A separate published study, *Sharp asymmetric bispectral identifiability and minimal phase sampling*, treats the different masks \(\{-s,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,r\}\) with \(r>s\) and gives a related wrap threshold. Its stated mask includes the first harmonic and is therefore not a direct statement of the present three-frequency-notch classification, particularly its low-band torsion cases.

## Limitations

The theorem presumes exact Fourier magnitudes, the full noiseless bispectrum, and nonzero coefficients at **all** frequencies in the mask; erasures inside the mask, stability under noise, and recovery algorithms are not treated. The first public source date records the motivating 2009 bispectrum paper, not publication of this classification. Equivalent older descriptions through triple-correlation hypergraphs or integer relation lattices might exist; the literature comparison is not an exhaustive novelty proof. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
