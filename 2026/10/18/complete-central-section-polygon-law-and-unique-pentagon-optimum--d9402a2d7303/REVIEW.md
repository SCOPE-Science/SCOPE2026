# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. An exhaustive sign-pattern classification for the six prism vertices proves that almost every central section has three, four, or five vertices, and characterizes the triangular case by six linear inequalities. The six-sided normal cone has analytically evaluated spherical area. A separate edge-by-edge lune argument supplies the expected vertex number, which algebraically determines the remaining two probabilities. Explicit differentiation proves strict monotonicity of the pentagon maximization equation and hence global uniqueness. The executable checker independently counts crossings at every actual prism edge and tests the numerical law at five scale ratios. The infinite/asymptotic and uniqueness statements are mathematical conclusions, not deductions from finite trials.

## Originality
PASS under the examined evidence, with bibliographic uncertainty. The nearest general-volume publication is Ambrus (arXiv:2107.14778), whose main results characterize volume-critical cube sections, not the combinatorial probability distribution for equilateral triangular prisms. Closely relevant work by Konstantinova and Shchur (DOI:10.1016/j.eswa.2023.121195) computes angle densities from random sections with triangular-prism examples, but available abstracts do not establish the exact centroid-fixed Haar law or the unique optimal height. Its full text remained inaccessible, an explicitly retained residual risk. The related tri-directional Poisson line tessellation distribution (arXiv:2309.12771) concerns different random planar polygons, not sections of a fixed solid. Analogous exact central-section results for rectangular boxes have different polygon types and do not imply the new formulas.

## Mathematical value
PASS. In geometric stereology, the combinatorial type of a random section is a natural measurable observable. The finding gives the entire probability law for a continuous family of convex polyhedra and solves the genuine shape-design question of which aspect ratio makes pentagonal sections most likely. The exact height optimum and the joint classification of all three outcomes provide more than a sample calculation or a routine moment extension. Extension to arbitrary triangular prisms, offsets, and angle distributions is not asserted.
