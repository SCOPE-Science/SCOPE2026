# Complete closed-neighborhood shattering profiles of Hamming graphs

## Finding
For integers \(q\ge 2\) and \(d\ge1\), let \(H(d,q)\) have vertex set \(W=[q]^d\), with adjacency at Hamming distance one. Its **closed-neighborhood set system** is \(\mathcal B=\{B_1(c):c\in W\}\), where \(B_1(c)=\{x:\operatorname{dist}_H(x,c)\le1\}\). A set \(A\subseteq W\) is shattered if \(\{A\cap B:B\in\mathcal B\}=2^A\). Define the *shattering polynomial* \(S_{q,d}(t)=\sum_{A\text{ shattered}}t^{|A|}\).

**Theorem.** The polynomial has degree at most three for every \(q,d\). Writing \(N=q^d\), its constant coefficient is one; the singleton coefficient is zero if \(d=1\), and \(N\) otherwise. The two-set coefficient is
\[
s_2(q,d)=\begin{cases}
0,&d=1\text{ or }(d,q)=(2,2),\\
\frac{N}{2}\left[d(q-1)+\binom d2(q-1)^2\right],&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The three-set coefficient is
\[
s_3(q,d)=\begin{cases}
0,&d=1\text{ or }[d=2,\ q\le3],\\
q^2(q-1)^2(q-2),&d=2,\ q\ge4,\\
q^d\left[\binom{1+d(q-1)}3-d\binom q3\right],&d\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
There are no coefficients above degree three. In particular, the closed-neighborhood VC dimension is zero when \(d=1\); one when \((d,q)=(2,2)\); two when \((d,q)=(2,3)\); and three in all remaining cases \(d\ge2\). For \(d\ge3\), a three-set is shattered **if and only if** it lies in one radius-one Hamming ball but is not contained in any coordinate line; that containing ball is unique. For \(d=2\), the shattered three-sets, which exist exactly when \(q\ge4\), have two points in one coordinate line and a third on a perpendicular line through their unique common center, with the center distinct from all three points.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is any finite set of exactly \(q\) symbols; no field or group operation is required. The incidence is reflexive, i.e. radius **at most** one, rather than the open neighborhood of exact radius one. All shattered sets consist of distinct vertices. The polynomial counts unordered labeled-by-vertex shattered sets, not graph-automorphism orbits and not the number of possible traces on arbitrary fixed sets. The graph includes the degenerate one-coordinate complete graph.

## Proof
**Step 1: unique centers for noncollinear triples.** For distinct centers \(c,c'\), their closed one-balls intersect as follows. If the centers differ in one coordinate, the intersection is exactly their common coordinate line, of size \(q\). If they differ in two coordinates, the intersection consists of the two opposite corners of the corresponding coordinate rectangle. If they differ in at least three coordinates, the intersection is empty. Thus any triple lying in a one-ball but not all in one coordinate line has a **unique** covering center. (The two-corner intersection cannot contain a triple.)

**Step 2: four-set obstruction.** If a shattered four-set \(A\) existed, its full trace would give \(A\subseteq B_1(c)\) for some center \(c\). If \(A\) were collinear, every one-ball would meet its coordinate line in the entire line or at most one point, so a two-point trace on \(A\) would be impossible. Otherwise take a noncollinear three-subset \(T\subset A\). Step 1 says the only one-ball containing \(T\) is \(B_1(c)\), which also contains the fourth point. Hence the trace \(T\) cannot occur, another contradiction. All larger shattered sets would contain shattered four-subsets and are excluded.

**Step 3: three-set classification in \(d\ge3\).** Fix \(c\) and call each coordinate direction through \(c\) an *arm*, consisting of the \(q-1\) vertices differing from \(c\) in that coordinate. Noncollinear triples within \(B_1(c)\) have one of the following forms, after permuting coordinates and alphabet symbols: (A) the center itself and one vertex on each of two different arms; (B) one vertex on each of three different arms; or (C) two different vertices on one arm and a vertex on another. Form (C) requires \(q\ge3\). All eight traces exist in each case, as the following explicit constructions show. Write \(0\) for the center, use distinct nonzero letters \(a,a'\) in coordinate one, \(b\) in coordinate two, and (where needed) \(e\ne0\) in coordinate three.

For (A), take \(A=\{0,(a,0,0),(0,b,0)\}\). Centers \(0\), \((a,0,0)\), \((0,b,0)\), \((a,b,0)\), \((0,0,e)\), \((a,0,e)\), \((0,b,e)\), \((a,b,e)\) give respectively the full trace, the two pairs containing \(0\), the pair excluding \(0\), the singleton \(\{0\}\), the two other singletons, and the empty trace (the stated eight centers are in trace order full, two pairs, pair, singleton, singleton, singleton, empty). For (B), with \(A=\{(a,0,0),(0,b,0),(0,0,e)\}\), center \(0\) gives the full trace, each singleton is obtained by centering at that vertex, a two-point trace by centering at the coordinatewise union of its two vertices, and the empty trace by centering at \((a,b,e)\). For (C), with \(A=\{(a,0,0),(a',0,0),(0,b,0)\}\), center \(0\) gives the full trace; center \((a,0,0)\) gives the first two-point trace; centers \((a,b,0)\) and \((a',b,0)\) give the other pairs; centers \((a,0,e)\), \((a',0,e)\), and \((0,b,0)\) give the three singletons; and center \((0,0,e)\) gives the empty trace. Direct Hamming-distance checks establish these claims, including when more than three coordinates exist by holding the others fixed. Collinear triples cannot be shattered since no one-ball cuts exactly two points from a coordinate line. Step 1 ensures each noncollinear triple is counted at exactly one center. That center's ball has \(1+d(q-1)\) vertices, and its collinear triples are exactly \(d\binom q3\). Multiplying by the \(q^d\) centers proves the last case of \(s_3\).

**Step 4: three-sets in \(d=2\).** Types (A) and (C) are the only noncollinear triples. Type (A) cannot be shattered: every center whose one-ball contains the original center also contains at least one of the two other points, so the singleton consisting of the original center is impossible. For (C), take \(\{(a,0),(a',0),(0,b)\}\) with distinct nonzero \(a,a',b\). All eight traces can be realized if \(q\ge4\): the witnesses from type (C) above for the nonempty traces work already in two dimensions, using a second-coordinate letter \(b'\) distinct from \(0,b\) for the two singletons on the first arm, while the empty trace is obtained by a center \((a'',b')\) with \(a''\) distinct from \(0,a,a'\). Conversely, for an empty trace, the center must differ from \(a\) and \(a'\) in the first coordinate and from \(0\) in the second, but it must also differ from \(0\) in the first coordinate to avoid covering \((0,b)\). This demands a fourth first-coordinate symbol, so \(q\ge4\). Each such triple has a unique covering center. Choose that center in \(q^2\) ways, the arm carrying two points in two ways, the two distinct nonzero letters on that arm in \(\binom{q-1}{2}\) ways, and the third nonzero letter in \(q-1\) ways. The product is \(q^2(q-1)^2(q-2)\).

**Step 5: pairs and singletons.** For any pair to be shattered, one ball must contain both vertices, forcing Hamming distance at most two. If \(d\ge3\), every pair at distance one or two is shattered: two selected vertices have a common center; the two singleton traces are obtained at the vertices themselves when their distance is two, or at centers with one extra changed coordinate when their distance is one; an empty trace is obtained by changing two coordinates disjoint from the varying coordinate in the distance-one case, or one varying and one fresh coordinate in the distance-two case. If \(d=2,q\ge3\), the same assertion follows by using a third alphabet symbol to construct a center at distance two from both vertices; the singleton witnesses are immediate. If \(d=q=2\), every ball is a three-point complement in a four-point universe, so no pair has an empty trace. Count unordered pairs at distance one or two: each vertex has \(d(q-1)+\binom d2(q-1)^2\) such neighbors, proving \(s_2\). For \(d\ge2\), each singleton is shattered because its vertex itself is in a one-ball and some vertex at distance at least two excludes it; in \(d=1\), every ball is the full space, so no nonempty set is shattered. The empty set is always shattered.

Together these steps establish every coefficient, sharp boundary, and classification.

## Relationship to prior work
Benediktsson, Macpherson, and Adler (2020) prove sharp VC-dimension three for **open** Hamming-graph neighborhoods and note boundedness for closed neighborhoods. The present statement instead uses **closed** neighborhoods and gives the complete all-size shattering polynomial, with exact low-parameter exceptions. Coudert, Csikós, Ducoffe, and Viennot (2024) define graph VC dimension using closed neighborhoods and give a general computation method, but do not derive the Hamming-specific coefficients. Alon, Jin, and Sudakov (2024) determine breadth and Helly numbers for fixed-radius Hamming balls, which address irredundant intersections, not the count of all shattered vertex subsets. Housholder, Mangiapanello, and Senger (2025) consider open neighborhoods in induced subsets of Hamming graphs, a different set-system restriction. Known open-neighborhood upper bounds alone do not logically give the exact closed-neighborhood triple counts.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns radius-one closed neighborhoods of **full** finite Hamming graphs, not induced subgraphs, radius-two balls, open neighborhoods, graph neighborhood complexes, sample-compression schemes, or arbitrary first-order formulas. The classification is mathematical, not a claim of independent publication-priority certification. A statement under alternative set-system terminology could precede it; the checks described here did not identify one. Independent external mathematical review has not been performed.
