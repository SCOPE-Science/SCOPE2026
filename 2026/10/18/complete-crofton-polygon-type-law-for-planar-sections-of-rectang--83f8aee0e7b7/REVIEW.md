# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For any parallelotope, signed vertex heights \(\pm X\pm Y\pm Z\) split all intersecting plane offsets into intervals. Counting the twelve incident edge crossings for each interval proves the four offset-length identities \(\ell_3=4Z\), \(\ell_4=2(X+Y+Z)-8Z+2h\), \(\ell_5=4(Z-h)\), and \(\ell_6=2h\), with \(h=(Y+Z-X)_+\). This establishes the universal \(p_5=p_3-2p_6\) pointwise under angular averaging. For rectangular boxes, sphere integration evaluates the two remaining expectations by integrating linear forms over explicit spherical triangles with a co-normal boundary identity; all positive half-edge lengths are admitted. Analytically derived cube values and sum-to-one constraints were checked. A separate edge-crossing checker independently verified 125,000 generic directions and 875,000 interval classifications, plus numerical angular-mean checks; these are supplementary, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

Lonke, *On random sections of the cube*, arXiv:math/9812123v1, was inspected in full (HTML). Its Grassmannian model uses central linear sections, whereas the current theorem conditions an invariant measure on translated affine planes meeting a body. The equal-cube central hexagon probability is approximately \(0.35095931\); the current affine-plane Crofton probability is approximately \(0.04644769\), so the models are distinct. The full HTML of Marichal and Mossinghoff, *Slices, slabs, and sections of the unit hypercube*, arXiv:math/0607715v2, was inspected; it supplies fixed-section volume/spline formulas rather than the claimed four-type Crofton distribution. A publicly available result, *Exact Haar hexagon law and cubic rigidity for central sections of rectangular boxes* (2026), was read in full and explicitly excludes translated planes. OEIS A349605 concerns central cubes only. Scientific searches in related published research records found no covering four-type law, though nonmatches alone do not prove priority.

Chakerian and Logothetti, *Cube Slices, Pictorial Triangles, and Probability* (1991), is a specific unresolved historical-priority risk because its full article text was unavailable despite literature-access attempts; its bibliographic metadata and citations in later primary work were available. Its possible coverage is not discounted as if its full text had been read. Earlier general Crofton formulas, section splines, and spherical Green identities are used as prior principles rather than claimed as inventions. The scientific claim is the explicit complete distribution for arbitrary positive rectangular-box dimensions, together with the more general parallelotope probability identity.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural, finite geometric invariant of the standard motion-invariant sampling of planes is resolved for every rectangular-box aspect ratio: not only a mean section count but all four triangle/quad/pentagon/hexagon probabilities. The parameter dependence cannot be inferred by affine distortion of a Haar angle law, and the pointwise identity extends beyond the orthogonal subclass to every three-dimensional parallelotope. The result clarifies the distinction between central and translated-plane geometric sampling. These are substantive complete classifications, rather than a narrow numerical recomputation of a known cube value.

## Scope and unresolved limitations

The proof assumes a full-dimensional parallelotope and samples intersection planes by the invariant \(d\sigma\,dt\) law; no other plane selection distribution is covered. The formulas apply only to rectangular boxes. Historically unindexed literature and, especially, the inaccessible 1991 cube-section article could contain equivalent formulations. The finite checker provides computational replication, not independent mathematical certification.
