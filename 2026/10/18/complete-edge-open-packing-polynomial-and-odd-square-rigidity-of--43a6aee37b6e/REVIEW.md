# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** The proof derives a necessary-and-sufficient component decomposition directly from the third-edge exclusion defining an edge-open-packing set. Each selected component has at most one horizontal and one vertical edge, and distinct components use disjoint row and column labels. A bijective labeled count proves every polynomial coefficient; sharp occupancy bounds with explicit constructions prove the maximum and force all optimizer types. No step relies on finite experimentation as an infinite proof. The package's standalone enumerator checks thirteen boards and \(46{,}476\) feasible sets directly, and verifies the extremal coefficient identities for \(190\) larger parameter pairs.

**Originality: PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The definition and general star decomposition appear in Chelladurai–Kalimuthu–Soundararajan (2022) and are not presented as new. Primary full texts of that article, Brešar–Samadi (2024), and Brešar–Dravec–Hedžet–Samadi (2024) were compared by implication. The first treats standard graphs other than rook products; the second develops complexity, tree algorithms, and general bounds; the third treats Cartesian products through general lower bounds and separately analyzes *direct* graph products. None of the inspected theorems implies the full size distribution, the sharp odd-square threshold, or the optimizer counts for \(K_m\square K_n\). Targeted mathematical-record searches returned results about vertex open packing and other rook-graph invariants but no covering claim. Absence of an indexed result is not a proof of priority; unindexed or undiscovered equivalent literature remains a risk.

**Value: PASS.** The complete all-size enumerator is a natural quantitative refinement of edge-open-packing, a parameter motivated by injective edge coloring. The structural decomposition into three tile types is exact and yields a two-variable exponential generating function. The parity obstruction for odd square boards and the explicit numbers of all maximum packings are nontrivial extremal consequences for an infinite two-parameter family, not arbitrary isolated values.

**Closest literature and scope.** The 2022 foundational paper supplies the original definition and induced-star observation. The 2024 graph-product study supplies general inequalities but not these equality classifications. The theorem is restricted to Cartesian products of complete graphs; it makes no assertion about other products or arbitrary line graphs. Direct finite enumeration is a corroboration only.
