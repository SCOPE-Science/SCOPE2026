# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The coefficient identity follows from unique representations below \(ab\). Semigroup block thresholds identify precisely the cyclic member gaps; a reflection symmetry of insertion-time residues forces the triangular law. Telescoping integer floors produces the asserted exact frequencies and a square slope for all allowed coprime parameters. The case of a full-residue arc is handled separately. An independent cyclic-arc enumeration confirms the triangular lemma for \(2\le a\le27\), and independent coefficient computation agrees on 3,256 pairs. No finite experiment is treated as proof.

## Originality: PASS (best knowledge, residual risk)

The most pertinent published results are Camburu et al. (2015), Cafure–Cesaratto (2021, 2025), and Bachman (2026). Camburu et al. count only the maximal gaps and study selected instances; Cafure–Cesaratto (2025) compute all length multiplicities only at remainder \(\pm1\). Bachman determines gap sets, not the complete frequency function. The 2021 coefficient-word algorithm can recover frequencies by expansion, so the novelty claim is the **explicit all-residue triangular insertion law**, the **closed floor-sum histogram**, and the **twice-square slope**; not the ability to list coefficients. A hidden equivalent extraction of these formulas from existing word identities remains possible. Searches also compared additive-semigroup formulations and more general coefficient methods.

## Value: PASS

A 2025 paper singled out the problem of counting each binary-cyclotomic gap length and solved only two residue families. Extending the count to every coprime pair answers precisely that motivated gap, supplies a compact arithmetic procedure, and reveals a structural perfect-square slope independent of the larger parameter. Unlike a table recomputation, it is an exact infinite-family classification, with an independent proof.

## Scope and audit status

The result applies to binary inclusion-exclusion polynomials, not higher-generator analogues. Claimed priority remains subject to independent scholarly review and possible unindexed equivalent identities.
