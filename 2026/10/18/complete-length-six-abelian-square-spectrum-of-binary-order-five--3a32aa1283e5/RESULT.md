# Complete length-six Abelian-square spectrum of binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
A binary **order-five de Bruijn cycle** is a cyclic binary word of length \(32\) in which each binary word of length \(5\) occurs exactly once as a cyclic factor. Identify cycles under rotation, but not under symbol complementation or reversal. For a cycle \(B\), let \(X(B)\) count cyclic length-six factors whose first three symbols and last three symbols contain equal numbers of ones. Every length-six factor is distinct, so \(X(B)\) also counts distinct cyclic length-six Abelian squares.

The complete spectrum consists of **all and only** the values \(4,5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12\), with the following exact frequencies. The last two columns give the numbers of orbits of size \(2\) and \(4\) under the group generated by reversal and bit complementation.

| \(X\) | Cycles | Size-two orbits | Size-four orbits |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | 32 | 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 96 | 0 | 24 |
| 6 | 288 | 8 | 68 |
| 7 | 384 | 0 | 96 |
| 8 | 448 | 8 | 108 |
| 9 | 416 | 0 | 104 |
| 10 | 224 | 8 | 52 |
| 11 | 128 | 0 | 32 |
| 12 | 32 | 4 | 6 |

There are exactly \(2048\) cycles and \(528\) reversal-complement orbits. The mean of \(X\) over uniformly selected cycles is \(8\), and its variance is \(23/8\). Both extremal values occur in exactly \(32\) cycles. These statements are exact finite theorems, not sampling estimates.

## Assumptions and scope
All symbols are in \(\{0,1\}\). A length-six factor is read cyclically, including wraparound. An Abelian square means a word \(uv\) of length \(6\), with \(|u|=|v|=3\) and the same Parikh vector in \(u\) and \(v\). Cycles are quotient by rotation only. Complementation interchanges zero and one, and reversal reads the cycle in the opposite direction. These two operations commute on rotation classes.

## Proof

**1. Exact coding by sixteen local switches.** Consider the directed binary de Bruijn graph with its \(16\) vertices labeled by four-bit words and its \(32\) edges labeled by five-bit words. Edge \(ax\) enters vertex \(x\); edge \(xb\) leaves it, where \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\). An order-five de Bruijn cycle corresponds to one Eulerian circuit using every edge exactly once. Following every incoming edge at vertex \(x\) chooses one of its two outgoing edges; because every outgoing edge must also occur exactly once, this is a bijection between the two incoming and outgoing edges. There are exactly two such bijections: \(b=a\) and \(b=1-a\). Encode them by a switch \(s_x\in\{0,1\}\) with \(b=a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}s_x\).

Let \(x\) also denote the integer in \(\{0,\ldots,15\}\) represented by its four-bit label, and let \(e\in\{0,\ldots,31\}\) be the integer for the entering edge. The successor map for a switch vector \(s\in\{0,1\}^{16}\) is the exact permutation
\[
T_s(e)=2(e\bmod16)+\bigl(\lfloor e/16\rfloor\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}s_{e\bmod16}\bigr).
\]
The local matching property makes \(T_s\) a permutation of all \(32\) edges. It represents one Eulerian circuit precisely when this permutation is a single \(32\)-cycle. Starting at the uniquely present edge `00000` (integer zero), each valid switch vector therefore produces one distinct rotation class. Conversely every order-five de Bruijn cycle determines its switch vector uniquely. Hence enumerating all \(2^{16}=65536\) switch vectors and retaining only those whose orbit of edge zero has length \(32\) is exhaustive and duplicate-free.

**2. A local Abelian-square score.** At the vertex \(x=x_1x_2x_3x_4\), an entering edge \(ax\) followed by the outgoing edge \(xb\) represents the six-symbol factor \(ax_1x_2x_3x_4b\). It is an Abelian square exactly when
\[
a+x_1+x_2=x_3+x_4+b.
\]
With \(b=a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}s_x\), define the exact integer
\[
h(x,s_x)=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbf 1\{a+x_1+x_2=x_3+x_4+(a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}s_x)\}.
\]
All two entering edges occur in every Euler tour, so
\[
X(B_s)=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^4}h(x,s_x).
\]
This is a structural reduction from a cyclic-string statistic to a weighted collection of sixteen binary local switches, subject only to the single-cycle constraint. For example, \(h(x,0)=2\) when the first two and last two bits of \(x\) have equal sums, and zero otherwise. If the sums differ by exactly one, precisely one value of \(s_x\) contributes \(1\); differences of two contribute zero for both switches.

**3. Exhaustive finite evaluation.** Evaluate the integer successor formula in Step 1 for each switch integer from \(0\) through \(65535\), and retain exactly the switch choices whose orbit starting at zero visits \(32\) distinct edges and returns to zero. For each retained choice, use the explicit \(16\)-term sum in Step 2. The standalone `verify.py` implements these two fully specified finite loops with integer bit operations and exact equality tests. Its assertions yield the histogram in the first two table columns, including exactly \(2048\) retained cycles. The loops are exhaustive by Step 1, and the scores equal \(X\) by Step 2; thus this is an exact computational proof of the finite table, not a statistical extrapolation.

**4. Independent orbit cross-check.** A separate depth-first Euler-circuit enumeration roots every cycle at its unique `00000` edge, directly constructs all \(2048\) cyclic words, and computes each length-six Abelian square by summing three consecutive bits on both halves. Reversal and complementation are applied to each resulting cyclic word; rotating the unique cyclic `00000` occurrence to the front gives a canonical representative. The resulting disjoint orbits all have sizes \(2\) or \(4\), with counts in the last two table columns. The checker asserts that each orbit preserves \(X\), every word has all \(32\) distinct five-bit factors, all \(2048\) words are reached, and no orbit is omitted. This independently reconstructs the histogram and proves the symmetry classification.

Finally, the exact mean and variance follow by summing \(X\) and \(X^2\) against the table: the sums are \(16384\) and \(136960\), respectively; divide by \(2048\) and subtract the square of the mean.

## Relationship to prior work
Richmond and Shallit count Abelian squares over unrestricted alphabets and lengths, not the distribution across de Bruijn cycles. Fici, Mignosi and Shallit study Abelian-square richness in infinite words and families such as Thue–Morse and Sturmian words; the order-five de Bruijn cycle distribution is a different constrained problem. De Bruijn successor-rule and Euler-tour constructions are established tools; the local-switch encoding is used here, not claimed as an invention. The new finite classification concerns the first length exceeding the fixed order-five factor census, where factor occurrence patterns can differ among cycles. Exact frequencies and reversal-complement orbit refinements are not supplied by the cited general results.

## Limitations
The theorem applies only to binary de Bruijn cycles of order \(5\), cyclic Abelian squares of length \(6\), and rotation classes. It does not provide a formula for arbitrary orders, block lengths, or larger alphabets, nor does a finite result imply such a formula. Originality remains subject to discovery of an equivalent specialized enumeration. The exact classification is reproducible finite enumeration, not a claim of a closed-form symbolic enumeration for all orders.
