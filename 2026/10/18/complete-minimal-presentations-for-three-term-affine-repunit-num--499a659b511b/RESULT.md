# Complete minimal presentations for three-term affine repunit numerical semigroups

## Finding
Let \(a,b,d\) be integers with \(a\ge 2\), \(b\ge 2\), \(d\ge 1\), and \(\gcd(a,d)=1\). Put
\[
 A=a,\qquad B=ba+d,\qquad C=b^2a+(b+1)d,
 \qquad S=\langle A,B,C\rangle.
\]
For an arbitrary field \(K\), let \(I\) be the kernel of the monomial map \(K[X,Y,Z]\to K[t]\) with \(X\mapsto t^A\), \(Y\mapsto t^B\), and \(Z\mapsto t^C\). The following explicit binomials form an **inclusion-minimal generating set of \(I\)**, uniformly over all permitted parameters.

**Two-generated range.** If \(2\le a\le b+1\), set \(h=\lfloor(b+1)/a\rfloor\), \(s=b+1-ha\), and \(K_0=b^2-bs+hd\). Then
\[
 I=(Z-X^{K_0}Y^s,\;Y^a-X^B).
\]
Here \(S=\langle A,B\rangle\). Its intrinsic single Betti element is \(aB\) (the degree \(C\) of the first binomial belongs to the redundant three-variable description).

**Three-generated divisible range.** If \(a=q(b+1)\) with \(q\ge2\), then
\[
 I=(Y^{b+1}-X^bZ,\;Z^q-X^{b^2q+d}).
\]
The semigroup is a complete intersection with exactly two Betti elements \((b+1)B\) and \(qC\).

**Three-generated nondivisible range.** If \(a=q(b+1)+r\), \(q\ge1\), \(1\le r\le b\), set
\[
 M=b^2q+br+d,\qquad M'=b^2q+b(r-1)+d.
\]
Then
\[
 I=(Y^{b+1}-X^bZ,\;Y^rZ^q-X^M,\;Z^{q+1}-X^{M'}Y^{b+1-r}).
\]
Here the semigroup has three minimal generators, is **not** a complete intersection, and its three distinct Betti elements are
\[
 (b+1)B,\qquad rB+qC,\qquad (q+1)C.
\]
Thus the complete-intersection boundary in the three-generated range is precisely divisibility of \(a\) by \(b+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
All exponents above are nonnegative integers: in the two-generated range \(K_0\ge1\), and in the nondivisible range \(M,M'\ge1\). No primality assumption on the generators is made. A binomial presentation means a generating set for the defining toric ideal in the polynomial ring indicated; minimality is inclusion-minimality as an ideal generating set. The semigroup is numerical because \(\gcd(A,B)=\gcd(a,d)=1\). Betti elements mean degrees of generators in a minimal homogeneous binomial presentation using the **minimal** additive generators of \(S\). The formula in the two-generated range deliberately retains the redundant coordinate \(Z\), whereas its intrinsic minimal presentation uses just \(X,Y\).

## Proof
**1. Weight identities.** Direct expansion gives \((b+1)B=bA+C\). Write \(a=q(b+1)+r\) with \(0\le r\le b\). If \(q\ge1\), then
\[
 qC+rB=(b^2q+br+d)A.
\]
If \(r\ge1\), subtraction of the first relation yields
\[
 (q+1)C=(b+1-r)B+(b^2q+b(r-1)+d)A.
\]
All proposed relations are therefore in \(I\).

**2. The low range.** For \(a\le b+1\), divide \(b+1=ha+s\) with \(0\le s<a\). Substitution shows \(C=sB+(b^2-bs+hd)A\). Because \(0\le s\le b\), the exponent \(K_0=b(b-s)+hd\) is positive. Eliminate \(Z\) with this relation. Since \(\gcd(A,B)=1\), the kernel of \(K[X,Y]\to K[t]\) is generated by \(Y^a-X^B\): for two monomials of equal weighted degree their exponent difference is an integer multiple of \((B,-a)\), and division by this binomial joins them. Hence the two displayed binomials generate all of \(I\).

**3. Terminating reduction for the high ranges.** Assume \(a>b+1\). The first binomial allows replacement of every \(Y^{b+1}\) by \(X^bZ\). Assign \(T=y+(b+1)z\) to a monomial \(X^xY^yZ^z\). The first replacement preserves \(T\) and strictly decreases \(y\). In the divisible case, the second binomial replaces \(Z^q\) by a pure power of \(X\), decreasing \(T\) by \(a=q(b+1)\). Eventually every monomial reduces to
\[
 X^xY^yZ^z,\quad 0\le y\le b,\quad 0\le z<q.
\]
In the nondivisible case, replace \(Z^{q+1}\) using the third relation and replace \(Y^rZ^q\) using the second. Each such replacement decreases \(T\) by exactly \(a=q(b+1)+r\). Between these replacements, normalization of \(Y\) decreases \(y\) without increasing \(T\). Thus reduction terminates, and its terminal monomials have
\[
 0\le y\le b,\qquad 0\le z\le q,\qquad z=q\Longrightarrow y<r.
\]
In either range the terminal pairs \((y,z)\) correspond **bijectively** to integers \(T=y+(b+1)z\) with \(0\le T<a\).

**4. Uniqueness of terminal monomials.** If two terminal monomials have equal weighted degree, reducing modulo \(a\) yields \(dT\equiv dT'\pmod a\) because \(B\equiv d\) and \(C\equiv(b+1)d\pmod a\). Coprimality of \(a,d\) and \(0\le T,T'<a\) imply \(T=T'\). The quotient-remainder restrictions above then force the same \(y,z\), and equality of weighted degrees forces the same \(x\). Therefore distinct terminal monomials have distinct semigroup degrees. Every binomial of \(I\) reduces to zero modulo the displayed binomials, and decomposing an arbitrary polynomial in \(I\) by weighted degree gives generation of \(I\).

**5. Minimality and Betti degrees.** To see that each binomial is necessary, delete it and consider its left monomial. In the nondivisible case, \(Y^{b+1}\), \(Y^rZ^q\), and \(Z^{q+1}\), respectively, cannot undergo even one forward or backward application of the other two binomial relations: their exponent vectors have zero \(X\)-coordinate, and their \(Y,Z\) coordinates fall strictly below the competing left thresholds. Consequently their respective right monomials are not congruent using the remaining relations. In the divisible case the same argument applies to \(Y^{b+1}\) and \(Z^q\). In the low range \(Z\) and \(Y^a\) are similarly isolated after removal of their own relation. Thus no displayed generator can be omitted.

In a semigroup fiber, each necessary homogeneous relation has a distinct associated Betti degree; conversely the exhibited generating set supplies every Betti degree. The three degrees in the nondivisible case strictly increase: \(qC+rB-(b+1)B=(bq-b-1+r)B+qd>0\), and \((q+1)C-(qC+rB)=(b-r)B+d>0\). The two degrees in the divisible case also differ because \(qC>(b+1)B\) for \(q\ge2\). Finally, \(C\notin\langle A,B\rangle\) in the high range: the congruence \(C\equiv(b+1)d\pmod a\) requires at least \(b+1\) copies of \(B\) in any \(A,B\) representation, yet \(C=(b+1)B-bA<(b+1)B\). Since \(A<B<C\), the high-range generating set is minimal. This establishes both the intrinsic Betti lists and the complete-intersection dichotomy.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone script `verify.py` implements the three formulas, checks their homogeneous degrees directly, exhaustively groups all three-variable factorizations of weighted degree at most \(500\) and verifies equality of their reduced normal forms, and independently performs a finite breadth-first search in each relation's full-degree factorization fiber after omitting that relation. With \(2\le a\le23\), \(2\le b\le9\), \(1\le d\le12\), and \(\gcd(a,d)=1\), it checks **1,320** parameter settings and **2,294,527** factorization vectors; all tests passed. The exhaustive range is finite and is not the proof of the all-parameter statement; the terminating reduction and uniqueness argument above provides that proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu, Xin, Ye and Yin introduced and studied the broader family containing exactly these three generators; their 2023 preprints derive Apéry representatives, Frobenius numbers, and genus formulas (arXiv:2306.03459; arXiv:2306.10738). The displayed minimal defining binomials and parameter-dependent complete-intersection/Betti classification are **not stated** in their inspected results. Their Apéry normal forms are closely related to the terminating representatives in the proof, and this relationship is acknowledged.

There is also an equivalent generator parametrization \(\langle a,a+c,a+(b+1)c\rangle\) with \(c=(b-1)a+d\). Searches under this almost-arithmetic alias did not locate the three-regime explicit binomials. General almost-arithmetic-semigroup criteria of Maloo and Sengupta (2003) supply a framework for deciding complete intersections, but do not state the formulas in these affine-repunit parameters. The present classification proves all explicit relations and degrees rather than claiming a new general criterion for arbitrary triples.

Branco, Colaço and Ojeda (arXiv:2112.01106) and Colaço and Ojeda (arXiv:2312.06013) established toric presentations and minimal resolutions for a **different, restricted** generalized-repunit family whose first generator is a base-\(b\) repunit of prescribed length. It has overlapping examples (including some three-generated cases), so this finding does not claim originality in those special cases. Its parameter domain has **arbitrary** \(a,b,d\) satisfying the above coprimality restrictions and exhibits a complete-intersection branch absent from that restricted three-generator determinantal formulation. General theory of three-generated numerical semigroups provides algorithms for such ideals; the contribution asserted here is the explicit **uniform three-regime formula and boundary**, not the general existence of small presentations.

## Limitations
This result concerns exactly three terms and positive \(d\); it makes no claim about longer affine repunit sequences, negative \(d\), graded free resolutions beyond their first Betti degrees, Gröbner bases for arbitrary term orders, or computational complexity. Earlier works may implicitly enable specializations of general three-generator algorithms; searches cannot rule out every unindexed explicit formula. The mathematical proof and finite checks do not constitute an independent external audit.
