# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

The finite-dimensional spectral decomposition splits within-part zero-sum eigenvectors from the partwise-constant zero-sum subspace. The resulting kernel has positive diagonal entries and nonpositive off-diagonal entries for positive time. Exact row sums then give the endpoint operator norm. The universal coefficient follows from the sharp scalar maximum of \(t e^{-\lambda t}\); strictness for nonsingleton parts follows because both positive terms cannot attain their individual maxima at the same time. Pairwise log-derivative separation proves unique row crossings and no return to a smaller maximizing size. Both example graphs have the same order and distinct part-size set but different spectral multiplicities.

## Originality

A search comparing graph heat-semigroup derivatives, complete multipartite Laplacian spectra, row-total-variation speeds, and semigroup analyticity constants found no direct covering result among the inspected sources. Keller–Lenz establish general graph semigroups and the \(\ell^\infty\) heat equation; Oliveira–de Lima–Nikiforov develop blow-up Laplacian eigenvalue formulas. The exact endpoint profile, sharp graph-family equality criterion and multiplicity-blind inverse example were not identified as statements or immediate corollaries in those works. Spectral information alone does not resolve endpoint absolute row sums. A classical equivalent formulation remains a bibliographic risk.

## Value

The exact \(\ell^1/\ell^\infty\) derivative norm supplies a time-resolved stability invariant for a major inhomogeneous graph family, distinguishing a universal-vertex sharpness threshold and an ordered shift of worst-case heat-response location from high-degree to low-degree vertices. The explicit twenty-vertex example shows the loss of spectral multiplicity information in an entire nonlinear norm profile, a relevant boundary for inverse heat-profile methods. The theorem does not concern arbitrary graphs or normalized Laplacians.
