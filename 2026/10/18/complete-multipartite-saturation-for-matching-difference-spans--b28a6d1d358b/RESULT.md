# Complete multipartite saturation for matching-difference spans

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) be a finite simple complete multipartite graph with \(r\ge2\), all \(n_i\ge1\), and \(n=\sum_i n_i\). Work over a field of characteristic zero. Set
\[
\nu=\min\!\left\{\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor,\;n-\max_i n_i\right\}.
\]
For every integer \(0\le k\le\nu\), the degree-\(k\) matching-difference span of \(G\) coincides with that of the complete graph on the same vertex set:
\[
\mathcal P_k(G)=\mathcal P_k(K_n),\qquad
\dim\mathcal P_k(G)=\binom nk-\binom n{k-1},
\]
where \(\binom n{-1}=0\). For every \(k>\nu\), the span is zero. Consequently, the Dunkl matching subalgebra defined below has Hilbert series
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal D(G);t)=
\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}\left(\binom nk-\binom n{k-1}\right)t^k.}
\]
In particular, for every \(1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), this multipartite graph is \(k\)-saturated in the sense of Grinberg--Kirillov--Shapiro precisely when \(k\le n-\max_i n_i\). Thus, for complete multipartite graphs, \(k\)-saturation is equivalent to \(k\)-vertex-connectivity.

## Assumptions and scope

Take a vertex set \(V\) partitioned into nonempty parts \(V_1,\ldots,V_r\), of sizes \(n_1,\ldots,n_r\). An edge of \(G\) joins two vertices in different parts; an absent edge joins two vertices within a part. A \(k\)-matching is a set \(M\) of \(k\) pairwise vertex-disjoint edges. Given independent commuting indeterminates \(x_v\), define
\[
p_M(x)=\prod_{\{u,v\}\in M}(x_u-x_v),\qquad
\mathcal P_k(G)=\operatorname{span}\{p_M:M\text{ a }k\text{-matching of }G\}.
\]
An arbitrary orientation may be used for the factors; it changes only overall signs, not spans. The empty matching gives \(\mathcal P_0(G)=\mathbb Q\) after base extension, and there are no matching polynomials in degrees exceeding the matching number.

For clarity, \(\mathcal M(G)\) denotes the commutative quotient of the polynomial algebra on symbols \(u_e\) by every product of two symbols corresponding to edges sharing a vertex, including squares. Fix an orientation of the edges and let \(\theta_v\) be the signed sum of incident edge symbols. The algebra \(\mathcal D(G)\subseteq\mathcal M(G)\) is generated by these \(\theta_v\). The established matching-duality theorem identifies \(\dim\mathcal D(G)_k=\dim\mathcal P_k(G)\). All dimensions and algebra statements here are in characteristic zero.

## Proof

**1. Maximum matching size.** Put \(p=\max_i n_i\). Every matching has at most \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) edges. It also has at most \(n-p\) edges, because every matching edge uses at least one vertex outside a largest part unless it is wholly outside, in which case it uses two. Thus its size is at most \(\nu\).

Conversely let \(m=\nu\). If \(p\ge m\), select \(m\) vertices in a largest part and \(m\) outside it (the latter exist since \(n-p\ge m\)); pair them across the part boundary. If \(p<m\), choose any \(2m\) vertices. All part sizes in this induced \(2m\)-vertex multipartite graph are at most \(m-1\). Every complete multipartite graph on \(2m\) vertices whose parts each have size at most \(m\) has a perfect matching: take one vertex from each of the two largest parts; the remaining \(2m-2\) vertices have parts of size at most \(m-1\), and induction completes the matching. Consequently \(G\) has a matching of size \(m\), so its matching number is exactly \(\nu\).

**2. Replacing forbidden pairs.** Fix \(k\le\nu\). We prove that \(\mathcal P_k(K_n)\subseteq\mathcal P_k(G)\) by strong induction on the number of within-part edges in a \(k\)-matching \(M\) of \(K_n\). If there are none, the corresponding product already comes from a matching of \(G\).

Otherwise choose a within-part pair \(\{u,v\}\in M\), both vertices lying in a part \(A\). Let \(C=V\setminus A\). Since \(|A|\le p\) and \(k\le n-p\), we have \(|C|\ge k\).

If some \(w\in C\) is unused by \(M\), apply the exact linear identity
\[
(x_u-x_v)=(x_u-x_w)+(x_w-x_v).
\]
Each resulting product corresponds, up to sign, to a matching with strictly fewer within-part edges: the pair \(\{u,v\}\) is replaced by either \(\{u,w\}\) or \(\{w,v\}\), and all other pairs are unchanged. By induction both products lie in \(\mathcal P_k(G)\).

If no vertex of \(C\) is unused, the remaining \(k-1\) pairs cover all \(|C|\ge k\) outside vertices. Hence one of these pairs, say \(\{s,t\}\), has both endpoints in \(C\). Now apply the exact four-vertex identity
\[
(x_u-x_v)(x_s-x_t)
=(x_u-x_s)(x_v-x_t)-(x_u-x_t)(x_v-x_s).
\]
Each right-hand product consists of two genuine cross-part edges because \(u,v\in A\) and \(s,t\in C\). The other matching pairs are unaffected and remain disjoint. Therefore each right-hand term again has fewer within-part edges, and induction applies.

The induction terminates after finitely many reductions, proving the asserted inclusion. The reverse inclusion follows because \(G\) is a subgraph of \(K_n\). Thus \(\mathcal P_k(G)=\mathcal P_k(K_n)\) for every \(k\le\nu\). No matching exists for \(k>\nu\), as proved above, so the remaining homogeneous spans vanish.

**3. Dimensions and connectivity.** In characteristic zero the standard two-row Specht-polynomial realization gives
\[
\dim\mathcal P_k(K_n)=\dim S^{(n-k,k)}=
\binom nk-\binom n{k-1}\quad (0\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor).
\]
This is the complete-graph dimension result and matching-duality theorem established in the cited initiating paper, not a new premise invented here. Substitution into step 2 yields the displayed Hilbert series.

The vertex connectivity of a complete multipartite graph is exactly \(n-p\): deleting the vertices outside a largest part disconnects it (or reduces it to isolated vertices), while after deleting fewer than \(n-p\) vertices at least two parts remain, and their surviving vertices connect every other survivor. If \(k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) exceeds \(n-p\), no \(k\)-matching exists in \(G\), whereas \(\mathcal P_k(K_n)\neq0\). Therefore saturation occurs exactly for \(k\le n-p\), equivalent here to \(k\)-connectivity.

## Verification

A standalone integer-arithmetic verifier is provided as `verify.py`. It independently generates all complete multipartite profiles with between two and eight vertices, enumerates each graph's matchings, expands the matching-difference products in squarefree monomials, and computes their ranks by Gaussian elimination modulo the prime \(1000003\). It checks that all degrees through \(\nu\) attain the predicted full Specht rank and that every higher possible degree has no matchings. The verifier reports 58 graph profiles, 217 full-rank homogeneous cases, 20 zero-degree cases, and maximum tested rank 28. Since a nonzero modular minor also yields a nonzero integer minor, these are genuine finite corroborations of rational spanning. They are not an infinite proof; the two reduction identities and matching-size argument above establish the theorem for all part sizes.

For illustration, \(K_{4,4}\) has Hilbert series \(1+7t+20t^2+28t^3+14t^4\). An imbalanced example is \(K_{8,2,1}\), for which \(\nu=3\) and the series is \(1+10t+44t^2+110t^3\); all higher coefficients vanish.

## Relationship to prior work

Grinberg, Kirillov, and Shapiro introduce the matching algebra, its Dunkl subalgebra, the perfect pairing with matching-difference spans, the complete-graph two-row Specht dimensions, and the notion of \(k\)-saturation. Their Proposition 5.11 proves that \(k\)-linked graphs are \(k\)-saturated; Theorem 5.12 covers graphs whose complement is a matching; Corollary 5.13 applies this to complete multipartite graphs in which *every* part has size two. They also show that saturation implies \(k\)-connectivity. Those are credited results.

The result here treats **arbitrary positive multipartite part sizes**, including the complete bipartite family and all unbalanced multipartite families, with a direct two-case reduction for missing within-part edges. It proves the converse to the connectivity obstruction *for the entire complete multipartite class*, and computes the Dunkl Hilbert series in every degree. It is not an assertion that connectivity suffices for arbitrary graphs: the cited paper explicitly provides counterexamples beyond this class. The proof uses the previously known three- and four-vertex difference identities but adds the capacity pigeonhole argument \(|V\setminus A|\ge k\) to force an allowable reduction in every matching.

## Limitations

The polynomial-spanning proof uses integer identities and works over any coefficient field, but the stated dimension formula and its Hilbert-series consequence are asserted only in characteristic zero, where the complete-graph Specht dimension and matching duality are being invoked. The result concerns complete multipartite graphs with at least two nonempty parts; no formula for other graph classes is claimed. The similarity search and inspection of the initiating 2026 manuscript did not identify a covering statement, but unindexed or otherwise inaccessible earlier work may exist. No independent external audit has been performed.
