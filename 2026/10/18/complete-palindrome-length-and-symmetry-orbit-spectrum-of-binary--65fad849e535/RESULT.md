# Complete palindrome-length and symmetry-orbit spectrum of binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
A binary de Bruijn cycle of order five is a cyclic binary word of length \(32\) in which each of the \(32\) binary words of length five occurs exactly once as a circular factor. Cycles are identified under rotation, but not under reversal or binary complementation. For a cycle \(w\), define its **longest circular palindrome length** by
\[
\Lambda(w)=\max\{1\le \ell\le32:\text{some length-}\ell\text{ circular factor of }w\text{ equals its reversal}\}.
\]
Among the exactly \(2048\) order-five cycles, the complete distribution of \(\Lambda\), together with orbit counts under reversal and binary complementation, is:

| \(\Lambda\) | Cycles | Orbits of size two | Orbits of size four |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 200 | 14 | 43 |
| 8 | 192 | 4 | 46 |
| 9 | 856 | 6 | 211 |
| 10 | 128 | 0 | 32 |
| 11 | 392 | 6 | 95 |
| 12 | 32 | 0 | 8 |
| 13 | 200 | 2 | 49 |
| 15 | 48 | 0 | 12 |
| **Total** | **2048** | **32** | **496** |

No other value is attained. In particular, the minimum is \(7\), the maximum is \(15\), and no cycle has longest circular palindrome length \(14\) or at least \(16\). The action has \(528\) orbits in total and none of size one. Representative cycles with minimum and maximum, respectively, are `00000100011010011101100101011111` and `00000100011010011111001010111011`; their respective exhibited circular palindromes are `0001000` and `101001111100101`.

## Assumptions and scope
An \(\ell\)-letter circular factor is read using indices modulo \(32\), has exactly \(\ell\) symbols, and is considered only for \(1\le\ell\le32\). A palindrome is invariant under reversal, with no complement applied. The symmetry group on cycles is generated by reversal and interchange of zero and one, after quotienting by cyclic rotations. Thus orbit sizes divide four. Counts in the table concern rotation classes, not the \(32\) possible rooted cuts of each cycle. The theorem is an **exact finite classification for order five only**; it contains no claim of an all-order pattern.

## Proof
The following two finite enumerations are complete and yield identical word-to-value maps. The accompanying dependency-free `verify.py` implements both and asserts every stated count, so the exhaustive certificate can be replayed directly.

**1. Exhaustive local successor matchings.** Take the binary de Bruijn directed graph whose vertices are four-bit words and edges are five-bit words. An edge \(b_0b_1b_2b_3b_4\) goes from its first four bits to its last four bits. Each of the \(16\) vertices has exactly two incoming and two outgoing edges. In a directed Euler circuit the successor relation pairs the two incoming edges bijectively with the two outgoing edges at each vertex. Exactly two local pairings are possible: after an incoming edge with leading bit \(b\), use the outgoing edge whose trailing bit is either \(b\) or \(1-b\). Therefore all local successor systems are indexed uniquely by \(16\) independent binary switches, totaling \(2^{16}=65536\).

Each switch system defines a permutation of the \(32\) five-bit edges. It specifies an Euler circuit precisely when that permutation is one cycle of length \(32\). The verifier tests this condition exactly, beginning at the all-zero edge; thus it enumerates **every** order-five de Bruijn cycle and cannot include a non-Eulerian system. Every such cycle has a unique five-zero circular factor, so beginning at that factor gives a unique canonical representative under rotation. This removes all rotational duplicates.

**2. Independent Euler-tour enumeration.** A second routine starts with the all-zero edge fixed, then recursively chooses either unused outgoing edge of the current four-bit vertex until all \(32\) edges have been used, retaining precisely returns to the initial vertex. There is a one-to-one correspondence between retained depth-first search paths and Euler circuits canonically rooted at the all-zero edge. Each output is separately checked for \(32\) distinct circular five-letter factors. The two enumerations yield the same \(2048\) canonical strings, and the same longest-palindrome value for every string.

**3. Two independent palindrome computations.** For the switch construction, every odd and even circular center is expanded as long as the paired letters agree, with the length capped at \(32\). Every circular palindrome of allowed length has such a center, so the maximum obtained is exactly \(\Lambda\). For the Euler-tour construction, lengths \(32,31,\ldots,1\) are tested directly using all \(32\) starting positions in the doubled word, comparing each factor with its reversal. Equality of the complete string-to-value maps excludes mere agreement of aggregate histograms. Their common histogram is exactly the first two columns of the table.

**4. Symmetry refinement.** Both reversal and binary complementation preserve the de Bruijn condition and longest ordinary palindrome length. Canonicalize the four images \(w\), reverse\((w)\), complement\((w)\), and complement(reverse\((w)\)) by shifting the unique five-zero factor to the beginning. The verifier partitions the complete set of \(2048\) canonical cycles into these disjoint orbits and counts their sizes separately at each value of \(\Lambda\). This gives the remaining columns of the table; its \(32\) size-two and \(496\) size-four orbits account for all \(2048\) cycles exactly.

**5. Structural check on the lower endpoint.** Every order-five cycle has exactly one occurrence of `00000`. The symbols immediately before and after it must both be `1`, because another adjacent zero would create two distinct occurrences of the same five-zero factor. Hence every cycle contains the seven-letter circular palindrome `1000001`, independently proving \(\Lambda(w)\ge7\). The exhaustive certificate proves this bound sharp and establishes the remaining exact maxima, absences, and multiplicities.

The expected completion line begins with `VERIFY_OK` and includes `binary_order5_cycles=2048`, `local_switch_assignments=65536`, `independent_euler_and_switch_sets_identical=2048`, and `symmetry_orbits=528 size2=32 size4=496`. ## Relationship to prior work
Anisiu, Anisiu, and Kása (2010 public arXiv version) discuss de Bruijn words as devices for generating all palindromes of prescribed length, and study finite-word palindrome complexity. Their examined construction does not enumerate the longest circular palindromic factor over the full order-five de Bruijn class. Simpson (2014) studies the **number of distinct palindromes in arbitrary circular words** and gives a universal cardinality bound, not this maximum-length profile for a constrained universal-cycle family. Likewise, OEIS A247000 tabulates a maximum **number of distinct palindromes** across all cyclic binary words of each length, not the present longest-palindrome statistic or distribution. Standard Euler-tour/de Bruijn counting fixes the total \(2048\) but does not determine any of the palindrome-length bins or symmetry refinements. The separate existing BWT-run histogram for the same order-five de Bruijn class measures runs in a transformed last-column word, not palindromic factors of the original word; neither histogram implies the other.

## Limitations
The result is a fully exhaustive finite fact at order five and does not provide a general-order formula or asymptotic behavior. The proof relies on replayable exact enumeration, not a closed-form derivation of each frequency. Originality assessment is necessarily relative to accessible literature: Simpson's complete journal article could not be materially inspected, and an earlier unindexed enumeration remains possible. The theorem does not classify positions, counts, or contents of all shorter palindromic factors within each cycle.
