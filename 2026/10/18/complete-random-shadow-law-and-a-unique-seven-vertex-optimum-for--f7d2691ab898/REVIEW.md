# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The planar parallel-projection equivalence is invertible almost surely; the planar visible-edge-chain identity gives the count for each direction; dihedral symmetry orders precisely five threshold events; integrating their spherical tail probabilities yields the four exact formulas. The integral-ratio monotonicity argument proves that the seven-vertex probability has exactly one maximizer for all positive heights. An independent numerical checker reconstructs the true orthographic convex hull at each of 240000 sampled directions for six different aspect ratios and confirms both the combinatorial criterion and frequencies. Finite computation corroborates, rather than proves, the all-height theorem.

## Originality

**PASS relative to inspected literature and indexed comparisons.** McGuire's 2004 expectation identity constrains only the first moment of the silhouette count. Glisse and Lazard (2007) bound average silhouette size for surface-approximating meshes, and Black and Criado (2024) bound mean random-shadow vertices of fixed polytopes; neither supplies this probability mass function. Comparable exact shadow probabilities for pyramids over four-sided bases and for octahedral bipyramids concern distinct families, and their statements do not imply an exact five-sided-base law. Searches for pentagonal-bipyramid silhouettes and full seven-vertex shadow distributions yielded no exact prior statement. Historical priority nevertheless remains subject to future independent scrutiny, and not all older conference/book material could be read in full.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The complete four-atom law answers a natural orientation-statistics question about a classical seven-vertex Johnson solid and all its symmetric axial deformations. Its nontrivial unique-height optimum for the probability of displaying every vertex is additional structural information not contained in general mean-count formulas. The formulas are fully explicit and permit rigorous comparison of regular-faced and visibility-optimized forms. The result does not claim an all-polyhedra optimization or a general downstream theorem.

## Boundaries

All statements concern nondegenerate pentagonal bipyramids with matching opposite apex heights and uniformly random orthographic directions. Nongeneric directions and metric shadow statistics are not classified. Related catalog entries were available as short summaries where complete texts were inaccessible; this limits historical-priority confidence but does not introduce a known implication covering the specific theorem.
