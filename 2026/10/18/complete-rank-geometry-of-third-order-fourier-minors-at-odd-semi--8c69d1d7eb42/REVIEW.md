# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The determinant criterion follows by CRT factorization, exact cyclotomic linear independence, and a case split over the mod-prime residue multiplicities. The potentially delicate all-distinct case is handled by a singleton exponent argument, with the five-prime exception treated by opposite determinant signs. The rank-one test uses all \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors; the four overlap events yield the exact count formulas. Exact symbolic verification exhausts all minors at orders \(15\), \(21\), and \(33\); this is a check, not the proof of the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The closest primary 2024 paper (Caragea and Lee, arXiv:2409.09793v1, Theorem 1.1) handles principal minors only; it cannot imply the positive count of singular nonprincipal minors. The later Loukaki theorem handles selected equal-residue-profile submatrices under additional hypotheses and gives neither an all-pairs classification nor rank counts. Published related classifications for cyclic orders \(2p\), \(p^2\), and group \(\mathbb F_p^2\) do not imply the result for \(3p\). Searches of equivalent formulations and existing mathematical records found no matching all-minors classification. Bibliographic incompleteness, particularly older cyclic semiprime minor work, remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete third-minor rank census for the first odd semiprime family isolates the rank-one geometry that cannot occur in the analogous even-semiprime classification and gives exact degeneracy incidence for structured Fourier sampling. Principal-minor nonvanishing alone leaves this natural independent-row/column problem open, so the formula is a genuine classification of a motivated Fourier invariant rather than a parameter replacement.
