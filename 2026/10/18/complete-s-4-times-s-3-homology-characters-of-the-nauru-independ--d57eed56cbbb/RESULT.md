# Complete \(S_4\times S_3\)-homology characters of the Nauru independence complex

## Finding
Let \(N=GP(12,5)\) be the 24-vertex Nauru graph and \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(N)\) its ordinary simplicial independence complex. Identify \(N\) with the Cayley graph on \(S_4\) generated by \((1\,2),(1\,3),(1\,4)\). Its full graph automorphism group is \(G\cong S_4\times S_3\); for \((a,b)\in S_4\times S_3\), where \(S_3\) permutes \(2,3,4\) and fixes \(1\), the action on vertices is \(w\mapsto awb^{-1}\).

The reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_5(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4\), \(\widetilde H_6(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), and zero in all other degrees. The **complete rational equivariant refinement** is
\[
\widetilde H_5(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
(\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3})\otimes\operatorname{Perm}_{4},
\qquad
\widetilde H_6(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\mathbf1_{S_3},
\]
where \(\operatorname{Perm}_{4}\) is the four-point permutation representation of the \(S_4\) factor, inflated to \(G\). Equivalently, for every \((a,b)\in G\),
\[
\chi_5(a,b)=\operatorname{sgn}(a)\operatorname{sgn}(b)\#\operatorname{Fix}_{\{1,2,3,4\}}(a),
\qquad \chi_6(a,b)=\operatorname{sgn}(a).
\]
In particular the integral degree-six generator transforms by the sign of the \(S_4\) coordinate. Both positive-degree full-group invariant rational homology subspaces vanish, whereas the \(S_3\) factor fixes all of \(\widetilde H_6\) and no nonzero part of \(\widetilde H_5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(GP(12,5)\) has vertices \(u_i,v_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\) and undirected edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+5}\), indices modulo 12. Its 24 vertices are numbered \(u_0,\ldots,u_{11},v_0,\ldots,v_{11}\). Every independent vertex subset, including the empty subset in augmented chains, is a face of \(X\). The automorphism action on oriented simplicial chains is the usual signed permutation action. All homology representations above are natural ones induced by graph automorphisms. The result is graph-specific and no integral \(G\)-lattice identification in degree five is asserted.

## Proof
**1. Integral ranks from a finite acyclic matching.** Direct enumeration from the edge definition produces 35,219 independent faces, including the empty face. The numbers of nonempty faces in dimensions 0 through 11 are
\[
(24,240,1304,4212,8328,10036,7176,2964,752,156,24,2).
\]
On the augmented face poset use the vertex order
`(22,23,10,14,13,9,19,3,6,20,4,0,15,21,8,17,7,18,2,5,12,16,11,1)`.
At each vertex, simultaneously pair every still-unmatched face not containing that vertex with its one-vertex extension when both are still unmatched; remove those pairs from later stages. This constructs exactly 17,607 disjoint cover pairs and leaves four critical faces of dimension five and one of dimension six; the empty face is matched.

Orient every Hasse incidence downwards except matched incidences, which point upwards. The explicit directed graph has 35,219 nodes and 205,680 arrows. A complete topological sort visits all 35,219 nodes, proving acyclicity. For the integral Morse differential, orient every simplex by increasing vertex labels. A matched dimension-five lower face \(\sigma\) paired with an upper six-face \(\tau\) is eliminated by substituting
\[
\sigma\longmapsto-\sum_{\rho\subset\tau,\ |\rho|=6,\ \rho\ne\sigma}
\frac{[\tau:\rho]}{[\tau:\sigma]}\,\rho,
\]
where the incidences \([\tau:\rho]\in\{1,-1\}\) are computed exactly. Dimension-five faces paired down to dimension four project to zero. Recursive substitution along the acyclic matching produces the **zero** \(4\times1\) degree-six-to-five integral Morse matrix. All other reduced Morse differentials vanish for dimension reasons. Thus the only reduced integral homology groups are \(\mathbb Z^4\) in dimension five and \(\mathbb Z\) in dimension six, with no torsion. This is a finite exhaustive argument, not an extrapolation from sampling.

**2. The full symmetry group.** Enumerate the 24 permutations of four letters in lexicographic order and join \(w\) to \(w(1\,j)\) for \(j=2,3,4\). The explicit isomorphism from the numbered generalized Petersen graph to those lexicographic indices is
`(0,6,12,18,4,10,16,22,3,9,15,21,14,19,2,7,17,20,5,8,13,23,1,11)`.
Checking all 36 edges establishes this graph isomorphism. All 144 maps \(w\mapsto awb^{-1}\) are distinct graph automorphisms. Conversely, fix the identity vertex and distinguish its three neighbors individually. Iterated color refinement by each vertex's current color and the multiset of colors of its three neighbors gives 24 distinct vertex colors after five refinement rounds. Any automorphism fixing this four-vertex rooted neighborhood pointwise must therefore be the identity. There are at most \(24\cdot3!=144\) automorphisms. Hence this is the full automorphism group and the displayed direct product action is faithful.

**3. Signed Lefschetz character.** For \(g=(a,b)\), let \(L(g)=\sum_d(-1)^d\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_d(X;\mathbb Q))\). A \(g\)-invariant independent face must be the union of complete vertex cycles of \(g\). If it consists of \(k\) selected cycles, including \(k=0\) for the empty face, its signed contribution to the *reduced* chain Lefschetz number is \((-1)^{k-1}\). This follows because the sign of the simplex permutation is \((-1)^{\#\text{vertices}-k}\). Therefore \(L(g)\) is an exact finite alternating sum over the cycle unions that are independent in \(N\).

For the five cycle types \(\lambda\) of \(a\in S_4\), and three types \(\mu\) of \(b\in S_3\), the complete Lefschetz table is

| \(\lambda\backslash\mu\) | \(1^3\) | \(2,1\) | \(3\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(1^4\) | -3 | 5 | -3 |
| \(2,1,1\) | 1 | -3 | 1 |
| \(2,2\) | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| \(3,1\) | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| \(4\) | -1 | -1 | -1 |

The program evaluates all 144 group elements and independently recomputes the signed fixed-simplex sum for representatives of all 15 classes. By the integral Morse calculation, \(L=\chi_6-\chi_5\).

**4. Recovery of the two actual characters.** Since \(\widetilde H_6(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), the action on that group is a one-dimensional sign character. Every one-dimensional character of \(S_4\times S_3\) is one of \(\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}^{e}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3}^{f}\), for \(e,f\in\{0,1\}\). Exact class-table orthogonality gives
\[
\bigl\langle L,\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\mathbf1\bigr\rangle_G=1.
\]
Since \(\chi_5=\chi_6-L\) must be an actual character with nonnegative irreducible multiplicities, the multiplicity of \(\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\mathbf1\) in \(\chi_6\) must be one. Thus \(\chi_6=\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\mathbf1\). Subtracting the entire table and applying exact character orthogonality gives
\[
\chi_5=(\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3})
 + ((\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\otimes\operatorname{Std}_3)\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}_{S_3}).
\]
The sum in parentheses is precisely the sign twist of the four-point permutation module. This proves the asserted complete representations and the statements about invariant subspaces.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_nauru_symmetry.py` (Python 3.9 or later; standard library only) alongside this document. It independently constructs the generalized Petersen and Cayley graph definitions, verifies their 24-point isomorphism, enumerates all independent faces, checks the complete acyclic matching and its exact signed zero Morse boundary, verifies the order-144 full automorphism group via five rounds of color refinement, computes all signed Lefschetz traces, and calculates every multiplicity against the complete irreducible character tables of \(S_4\times S_3\). It also performs a direct signed-face trace check on a representative of every conjugacy class. Successful execution prints `NAURU_EQUIVARIANT_VERIFY_OK`. ## Relationship to prior work
Barmak's 2010 paper gives general star-cluster and suspension machinery for independence complexes of triangle-free graphs, not the 15-class character calculation here. Frucht, Graver and Watkins describe the automorphism groups of generalized Petersen graphs, not their independence-complex homology modules. A published exact same-object result, *The Nauru independence complex is \(\bigvee^4 S^5\vee S^6\)*, establishes the nonequivariant integral homotopy type. Its complete `RESULT.md` was inspected: it does not identify either natural automorphism representation. The present calculation starts from independently verified homology, then determines how **all** graph automorphisms act. Neither the ranks of the homology groups nor the group isomorphism imply the displayed two characters. Searches using Nauru, \(GP(12,5)\), arrangement-graph and star-transposition Cayley-graph equivalents did not identify a covering result; such a negative search is not proof of universal novelty.

## Limitations
This is a reproducible finite graph-specific calculation, not a structural theorem for all generalized Petersen or all permutation star graphs. The signed permutation-lattice structure of integral degree-five homology is not identified. The displayed rational degree-five representation need not give an integral permutation-module isomorphism. Equivalent unpublished or unindexed equivariant calculations remain possible, and independent scientific validation has not been performed.
