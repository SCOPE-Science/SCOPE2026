# Complete singular-stratum and Hilbert-series laws for odd-cycle dumbbell edge hypersurfaces

## Finding

Let \(a,b\ge 3\) be odd integers and \(\ell\ge1\). Join a cycle of length \(a\) to a vertex-disjoint cycle of length \(b\) by a simple path of \(\ell\) edges, with all interior path vertices new. Let \(G_{a,b,\ell}\) be this graph, \(E\) its edges, and \(k\) an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Its projective edge toric variety is
\[
X_{a,b,\ell}=\operatorname{Proj}k[x_e:e\in E]/I_G\subset\mathbb P^{a+b+\ell-1},
\]
where \(I_G\) is the kernel of the map \(x_{uv}\mapsto t_ut_v\).

Write \(c=(a+b)/2\). Then \(X_{a,b,\ell}\) is an integral hypersurface of degree \(c+\ell\). The reduced singular locus has **only projective coordinate linear spaces as irreducible components**, grouped by their codimension in \(X\):

If \(\ell=2r\) with \(r\ge1\), their numbers at codimensions \(1,2,3\), respectively, are
\[
 r^2,\qquad 2r\binom c2,\qquad \binom c2^2.
\]
There are precisely \((r+\binom c2)^2\) components. If \(\ell=2r+1\), \(r\ge0\), their respective numbers are
\[
 r(r+1),\qquad r\binom{c-1}{2}+(r+1)\binom{c+1}{2},\qquad
 \binom{c+1}{2}\binom{c-1}{2}.
\]
There are precisely \((r+\binom{c+1}{2})(r+1+\binom{c-1}{2})\) components. A component of codimension \(j\) in \(X\) is a projective linear space of codimension \(j+1\) in the ambient projective space.

The three families have, at their generic points, transverse analytic models, respectively,
\[
 u^2-v^2=0,\qquad u^2-vw=0,\qquad uv-wz=0.
\]
The first is a two-branch ordinary crossing; the second is a surface ordinary double point; the third is a threefold ordinary double point. The nonnormal locus is **exactly** the union of the codimension-one singular components. In particular \(X_{a,b,1}\) is normal and \(X_{a,b,\ell}\) is nonnormal for every \(\ell\ge2\).

There is also an exact *reduced singular-scheme* Hilbert series. Put \(m=a+b+\ell\) and define \(f_{s,q}(t)=1-s t^{q+s-1}+(s-1)t^{q+s}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}\bigl(k[x_e]/\sqrt{J(F)};t\bigr)=
\begin{cases}
 f_{c,r}(t)^2/(1-t)^m,&\ell=2r,\\[2mm]
 f_{c+1,r}(t)f_{c-1,r+1}(t)/(1-t)^m,&\ell=2r+1,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(\sqrt{J(F)}\) means the radical of the homogeneous Jacobian ideal of the defining hypersurface in \(k[x_e]\). In the first case \(r\ge1\); the second includes \(r=0\). The leading-dimensional singular degree is \(r^2\) for even \(\ell\ge2\), \(r(r+1)\) for odd \(\ell\ge3\), and \(\binom{c+1}{2}\) when \(\ell=1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All statements are over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero. The cycles are vertex-disjoint; there is exactly one connecting path, whose internal vertices have degree two. All edges, including path edges, are distinct. A graph edge variable has ordinary standard degree one. The singular locus means the scheme-theoretic Jacobian singular locus only when explicitly stated as such; its **reduced** structure is used in the component classification and Hilbert series. A generic transverse germ is taken at a general closed point of an irreducible component and is not a claim about its intersections with other components.

## Proof

**1. The graph toric equation.** Number consecutive edges on the first and second odd cycles by \(A_0,\ldots,A_{a-1}\) and \(B_0,\ldots,B_{b-1}\), beginning at each path endpoint. Number the path edges \(P_1,\ldots,P_\ell\). The integer incidence relation whose entries, in these orders, are
\[
 w_{A_i}=(-1)^i,\qquad w_{B_i}=(-1)^{\ell+1+i},\qquad
 w_{P_j}=2(-1)^j
\]
annihilates the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix. The graph has \(m=a+b+\ell\) edges and \(m-1\) vertices. Since it is connected and nonbipartite, its unsigned incidence matrix has rank \(m-1\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Its integral kernel therefore has rank one; the displayed primitive vector (some entries are \(1\)) generates it. Consequently its toric ideal is principal, generated by the irreducible binomial \(F=M_+-M_-\) from the positive and negative entries of \(w\). Both monomials have degree \(c+\ell\).

**2. Elementary critical-set lemma.** Suppose two degree-\(d\) monomials \(U,V\) have disjoint variables and \(F=U-V\). For a monomial with \(s\ge2\) variables of exponent one and \(q\ge0\) variables of exponent two, and no other variables, its entire gradient vanishes exactly when either (i) at least one squared variable vanishes, or (ii) at least two simple variables vanish. This follows directly by differentiating the monomial: a unique zero simple variable gives a nonzero derivative, whereas a zero squared variable kills every derivative in characteristic zero. The squarefree radical of the monomial gradient ideal is
\[
 Q\,V_{s,s-1},
\]
where \(Q\) is the product of all \(q\) squared variables (\(Q=1\) if \(q=0\)), and \(V_{s,s-1}\) is the squarefree monomial ideal generated by the \(s\) products of \(s-1\) distinct simple variables. Indeed its minimal coordinate primes are the single squared-variable primes and the simple-variable pair primes.

For \(F=U-V\), differentiation in disjoint sets gives \(J(F)=J(U)+J(V)\). In disjoint sets, the radical of this sum is the sum of the two squarefree monomial radicals. Thus its minimal coordinate primes are precisely all sums of one minimal prime from each side. Euler's identity places the entire gradient zero locus on \(F=0\). Its components have ambient coordinate codimensions \(2,3,4\), hence codimensions \(1,2,3\) in \(X\). None contains another.

**3. The parity count.** If \(\ell=2r\), each monomial has \(s=c\) simple cycle variables and \(q=r\) squared path variables. If \(\ell=2r+1\), the two respective pairs \((s,q)\) are \((c+1,r)\) and \((c-1,r+1)\). The minimal primes of each gradient radical number \(q\) (single squared variables) plus \(\binom s2\) (pairs of simple variables). Multiplying the two choices proves all three codimension counts, including \(\ell=1\) with zero divisorial components.

**4. Hilbert series.** For \(s\ge2\), the ideal \(V_{s,s-1}\) has \(s\) minimal generators of degree \(s-1\). Its pairwise least-common-multiple syzygies all have degree \(s\); exactly \(s-1\) of these are independent. Thus its Hilbert series as a graded module over \(s\) variables is \([s t^{s-1}-(s-1)t^s]/(1-t)^s\). Multiplication by \(Q\) is a graded shift by \(q\), while the \(q\) squared variables are otherwise free. The quotient by the critical radical has series \(f_{s,q}(t)/(1-t)^{s+q}\). The two variable groups are disjoint, so tensoring their quotients multiplies the series and gives the displayed formula. The leading projective degree follows from the maximal coordinate linear components (or by the pole at \(t=1\)).

**5. Generic local models and normality.** At the generic point of a minimal zero-coordinate component, every coordinate not prescribed zero is invertible. The binomial then has leading transverse equation equal to the product of the prescribed zero variables with their original multiplicities on the two sides, multiplied by units. Taking formal roots of units in characteristic zero reduces it exactly to one of \(u^2-v^2\), \(u^2-vw\), or \(uv-wz\). The gradient has no additional generic zero directions. Because \(X\) is an integral hypersurface, all its local rings satisfy Serre's condition \(S_2\). Hence its nonnormal locus is the union of its codimension-one singular components by the \(R_1+S_2\) criterion. These are precisely the pairs of opposite-parity connecting-path edges. The normality statement follows.

## Verification

The supplied standalone Python checker reconstructs the integer edge-incidence relation for seven nontrivial parameter triples; verifies the complete characteristic-zero Jacobian support pattern across **every** zero/nonzero coordinate pattern for those triples; checks the component counts; independently counts graded monomials of degrees zero through six in the reduced singular coordinate ring against the rational Hilbert-series formulas; and computes exact Hessian ranks \(2,3,4\) at sample generic points of each occurring component family. The tested parameter triples are \((a,b,\ell)=(3,3,1),(3,3,2),(3,3,3),(3,3,4),(3,5,2),(5,3,3),(5,5,2)\). The checker outputs `VERIFY_OK`. It is finite corroboration of the symbolic argument, not a proof of the full infinite family.

For the triangle-triangle graphs, the numbers of components at \(\ell=2,3,4\) are \(16,21,25\), respectively, with codimension profiles \((1,6,9)\), \((2,13,6)\), and \((4,12,9)\). The first case has reduced singular-locus Hilbert series \((1-3t^3+2t^4)^2/(1-t)^8\).

Run using a standard Python 3 interpreter: `python3 check.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

The normalization of graph edge rings and the odd-cycle normality criterion are established in Simis–Vasconcelos–Villarreal (1998) and Ohsugi–Hibi (1998). Matsui (2012) supplies a general presentation for the Ehrhart/normalization ring and explicitly studies the two-triangle, two-edge connecting-path graph, including a toric defining binomial and Ehrhart series (Example 1, pp. 621–622). These descriptions and the normality distinction are not claimed as new. The claimed contribution is the all-odd-cycle, all-path-length classification of the **reduced singular components by codimension**, the exact Hilbert series of the reduced Jacobian singular locus, the complete nonnormal support, and the three generic transverse local models. These are additional geometric calculations from the edge-ideal presentation, not a new presentation or normalization theorem.

## Limitations

The radical of the Jacobian ideal is determined, but the complete possibly nonreduced Jacobian scheme is not. No conductor ideal or full normalization map is computed here. The generic transverse models do not classify local germs at intersections of different singular components. Characteristic two is excluded; so are graphs with multiple connecting paths or with cycles sharing vertices. Any claim of literature novelty is subject to further specialist and bibliographic review, especially because the defining binomial is already implicit in classical work.
