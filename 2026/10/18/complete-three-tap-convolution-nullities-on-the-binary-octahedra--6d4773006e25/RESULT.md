# Complete three-tap convolution nullities on the binary octahedral group

## Finding
Let \(G=2O\) be the binary octahedral group of order \(48\), and let \(S\) range over its unordered three-element subsets. The complex convolution operator
\[
(T_S f)(x)=\sum_{s\in S}f(s^{-1}x),\qquad f:G\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
\]
has nullity determined **solely** by the unordered triple \(\tau(S)\) of the orders of the three differences \(s^{-1}t\), one for each unordered pair \(\{s,t\}\subset S\). The following table is complete; each signature is written in increasing order, and no other signature occurs.

The maximum nullity is \(32\), attained exactly by the \(64\) cosets of order-three subgroups (there are four such subgroups, each of index \(16\)).

A particularly distinct part concerns the normal binary tetrahedral subgroup \(H=2T\) of index two. Among the \(13,248\) supports **not** lying entirely inside a coset of \(H\), the nullities are exactly \(0,2,5,8\), with respective counts \(4,032,4,608,2,304,2,304\). In this mixed-coset case, nullity \(2\) occurs for signatures \((3,4,8)\) and \((4,6,8)\); nullity \(5\) for \((3,8,8)\) and \((6,8,8)\); nullity \(8\) for \((3,4,4)\) and \((4,4,6)\). All remaining mixed signatures are invertible.

## Assumptions and scope
Identify the quaternion algebra with \(\mathbb H=\mathbb R\langle1,i,j,k\rangle\). The group \(G\) consists of the following \(48\) unit quaternions:
\[
\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\;\cup\;
\left\{\tfrac12(\pm1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\right\}\;\cup\;
\left\{\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\pm e_r\pm e_s):0\le r<s\le3\right\},
\]
where \((e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)=(1,i,j,k)\), and every displayed sign is chosen independently. The three disjoint sets have cardinalities \(8,16,24\). Their union is a group under quaternion multiplication. The first \(24\) elements form \(H\). No self-adjointness of \(T_S\) is assumed: the support is not required to be inversion-invariant. All ranks and nullities are over \(\mathbb C\) (equivalently, over \(\mathbb Q\) for the displayed integer regular matrices).

## Proof
**Exact finite reduction.** A quaternion coordinate is encoded by an integer pair \((a,b)\), meaning \((a+b\sqrt2)/2\). Hamilton's multiplication formula gives a closed multiplication table on the displayed \(48\) elements; the verifier checks closure, identity, inverses, and all \(48^3\) associativity identities. Thus the construction requires no numerical comparison of algebraic numbers.

For \(S=\{s_0,s_1,s_2\}\), left-multiplying the support by \(s_0^{-1}\) gives \(\{1,a,b\}\) without changing the kernel dimension, since \(T_{uS}=L_uT_S\). Likewise, inner conjugation of the support conjugates the operator by the coordinate permutation \(f(x)\mapsto f(u^{-1}xu)\). There are exactly \(\binom{47}{2}=1,081\) unordered identity-normalized pairs \(\{a,b\}\).

The transformations generated by (i) renormalization at any of the three support elements and (ii) conjugation by each group element partition these \(1,081\) pairs into exactly \(28\) orbits. This is a complete finite calculation: the supplied program starts with all \(1,081\) pairs, repeatedly closes each orbit under both transformations, removes the visited pairs, and verifies that orbit sizes sum to \(1,081\). It does not assume that identical order signatures imply conjugacy.

For each orbit representative, form the exact \(48\)-by-\(48\) matrix
\[
M_{x,y}=\mathbf 1_{y=x}+\mathbf 1_{y=a^{-1}x}+\mathbf 1_{y=b^{-1}x}.
\]
Its rank is computed over the rationals by exact symbolic elimination. The program checks that each orbit has a constant order signature and aggregates the ranks into the nineteen signature rows displayed above. Since each three-point support occurs exactly three times among the \(48\) translates of normalized supports (one choice per distinguished support element), an orbit of \(r\) normalized pairs contributes exactly \(16r\) original supports. This proves the counting columns and exhausts all \(\binom{48}{3}\) cases. Exact modular elimination, separately at two primes, checks the stated rank for **every** normalized pair; those modular checks are corroboration, whereas the rational orbit-rank calculations establish the characteristic-zero theorem.

**Extremizers and cosets.** The unique signature with nullity \(32\) is \((3,3,3)\). Its exact orbit consists of the \(64\) translates of cyclic order-three subgroups. The order-three elements form four cyclic subgroups, each with sixteen cosets, giving the claimed characterization. Finally, after normalization, a support lies in one coset of \(H\) precisely when both \(a,b\in H\); separating these normalized cases yields \(4,048\) same-coset and \(13,248\) mixed-coset supports and their asserted nullity counts.

## Relationship to prior work
Dechant's 2012 preprint constructs the \(48\) binary-octahedral quaternionic spinors and identifies their \(F_4\) geometry. Arezoomand and Taeri's 2013 article proves the general Cayley-digraph Fourier block factorization; its Corollary 7 gives a representation-theoretic route to nullity, but not this finite support census.

A separate earlier classification for three-tap convolution on \(2T\) implies the same-coset portion by doubling nullities across the two left cosets of \(H\). It does **not** cover supports meeting both cosets of \(H\): the new mixed-coset cases have nullities \(2,5,8\), and the odd five-dimensional defects cannot result merely from doubling \(2T\) nullities. Related exact results on generalized dihedral and ternary Heisenberg groups concern different group families. The present statement is an exact order-signature and multiplicity classification for \(2O\), not a new general Fourier factorization.

## Limitations
The classification treats unweighted supports with exactly three distinct elements of the specified finite group. It does not classify complex weights, larger supports, eigenvalues away from zero, or other binary polyhedral groups. The finite exhaustive proof requires correct execution of the supplied exact checker; independent mathematical audit and publication-priority verification have not been performed. Published representation tables not found during the literature comparison could contain an equivalent re-expression.
