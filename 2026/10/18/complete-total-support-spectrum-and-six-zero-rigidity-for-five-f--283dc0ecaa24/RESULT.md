# Complete total-support spectrum and six-zero rigidity for five four-dimensional mutually unbiased bases

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_5\) be any complete system of five pairwise mutually unbiased orthonormal bases of \(\mathbb C^4\). For every nonzero vector \(\psi\), put
\[
S(\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{5}\big|\{b\in\mathcal B_j:\langle b,\psi\rangle\ne0\}\big|.
\]
The **complete attainable spectrum** is
\[
\boxed{\{S(\psi):\psi\ne0\}=\{14,17,18,19,20\}.}
\]
In particular, the seemingly intermediate values \(15\) and \(16\) do not occur, and the sharp lower bound is \(14\). There are exactly **forty projective minimizing rays**, each with basis-by-basis support multiset \(\{2,2,2,4,4\}\). In the canonical two-qubit Pauli realization they are exactly the four common eigenrays of each of the ten maximal commuting Pauli triples not among the five defining the bases. A further exact incidence statement is that, among the \(\binom{20}{4}=4845\) four-element subsets of the twenty measurement rays, exactly \(600\) are linearly dependent, and each is contained in the six-orthogonality set of exactly one of these forty minimizing rays.

## Assumptions and scope
A complete system here consists of five bases of four unit vectors each, satisfying \(|\langle b,c\rangle|^2=1/4\) whenever \(b,c\) belong to different bases. Supports count nonzero complex scalar products, not probabilities above a threshold. Vectors are unrestricted complex vectors, modulo nonzero scalar multiplication for the equality classification. The result applies to *every* complete five-base system: Brierley, Weigert and Bengtsson proved that such systems are all unitarily equivalent up to phase changes, basis permutations, vector permutations and (if necessary) complex conjugation. Those operations preserve support counts and projective-ray cardinalities.

For a concrete model, let \(I,X,Y,Z\) be the standard Pauli matrices. Set \(AB=A\otimes B\) and take the five commuting triples
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1&=\{ZI,IZ,ZZ\},&C_2&=\{XI,IX,XX\},\\
C_3&=\{XZ,ZY,YX\},&C_4&=\{YI,IY,YY\},\\
C_5&=\{YZ,ZX,XY\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their joint eigenbases constitute a complete system, as in the standard Pauli construction. The sign of an operator product is irrelevant when referring to its unsigned Pauli label. The fifteen unsigned nonidentity tensor-Pauli matrices form five disjoint triples above.

## Proof
**Projector construction.** Any maximal commuting triple \(C\) is generated by two independent commuting Hermitian Pauli matrices \(P,Q\). Its four rank-one spectral projectors are
\[
E_{s,t}(P,Q)=\frac{(I_4+sP)(I_4+tQ)}4,
\qquad s,t\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
There are exactly fifteen commuting triples: each of the fifteen nonidentity Pauli labels commutes with six other nonidentity labels, and the six neighbors pair under multiplication, giving three triples through any fixed label. Counting incidences gives \(15\cdot3/3=15\) triples. Five are the displayed \(C_j\), leaving ten. For any other triple, its three nonidentity labels necessarily belong to three distinct displayed \(C_j\), since two from one displayed triple would generate that entire triple. Thus each of its four eigenrays has two zero probabilities in each of three bases and none in the other two; its support multiset is \(\{2,2,2,4,4\}\). The four rays from each such triple are distinct, and no ray belongs to different triples, because the nonidentity Pauli operators for which a pure state is an eigenstate form exactly one maximal commuting triple. This produces forty minimizing candidates.

**An analytic lower-bound mechanism.** If a state has exactly two nonzero coefficients in a Pauli basis, it belongs to a two-dimensional eigenspace of one nonidentity operator \(P\) in that basis's triple. In another Pauli basis, conjugation by \(P\) permutes the four rank-one eigenprojectors as two disjoint transpositions: \(P\) commutes with exactly one and anticommutes with the other two nonidentity Pauli operators of that other triple. Because the state is a \(P\)-eigenvector, its probabilities in that other basis agree in these two-element pairs. Consequently every other basis has support either two or four, never three. If a second basis has support two, the state is also an eigenvector of a commuting Pauli \(Q\) from that basis. It is therefore one of the four joint eigenrays of \(P,Q\), and the product gives support two in a third basis and four in both remaining bases. A state supported on one vector in any basis has support four in every other basis, hence total \(17\). A state without any basis support one or two has total at least \(15\). It follows that total support below \(14\) is impossible and that the only support-\(14\) states are precisely the forty constructed above.

**The two missing support levels.** A separate complete finite incidence lemma strengthens the preceding minimum to the full spectrum. Label the twenty rank-one projectors in the five bases by \(E_1,\ldots,E_{20}\), and pick from each numerator \(4E_j\) any nonzero column \(v_j\in\mathbb Z[i]^4\); it spans the measurement ray. The following assertions hold:

1. All \(\binom{20}{3}=1140\) triples of the \(v_j\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb C\).
2. A four-element selection of the \(v_j\) is dependent **if and only if** its index set lies in the six-element set of measurement rays orthogonal to one of the forty previously constructed eigenrays. There are exactly \(40\binom64=600\) such selections out of \(4845\).

These are finite exact identities, not numerical rank thresholds. The accompanying `VERIFY.py` constructs each projector from the printed Pauli matrices, checks every triple using its four possible \(3\times3\) minors, checks each of the \(4845\) quadruples by its \(4\times4\) determinant, checks the complete list of forty six-zero sets, and verifies the equivalence in (2). Matrix entries and all determinant calculations are Gaussian integers; the bounded integer magnitudes are exactly representable in the checker's native complex arithmetic. The algorithm and complete finite domains are given in `VERIFY.py`; no untested symmetry reduction is used.

Suppose \(\psi\ne0\) has at least four zero measurements. Choose four of the corresponding \(v_j\). They must be dependent, since their common nonzero orthogonal vector is \(\psi\). By assertion (1) they span a three-dimensional subspace; by assertion (2) they belong to the orthogonality set of one of the forty specific eigenrays. The common orthogonal complement is one-dimensional, so \(\psi\) is that eigenray. Hence it has **exactly six** zeros. Therefore the only possible numbers of zero measurements are \(0,1,2,3,6\).

Every one of those counts occurs: the forty rays provide six zeros; each of the twenty basis rays has three zeros and none in other bases. For zero, one or two zeros, take a generic vector respectively outside all twenty measurement-orthogonality hyperplanes, in exactly one such hyperplane, or in the intersection of two of them but none of the remaining eighteen. These choices exist because finitely many proper complex linear subspaces cannot cover a nonzero complex linear subspace, and assertion (1) ensures that the two-hyperplane intersection is not contained in any third hyperplane. Subtracting the zero counts from twenty yields precisely \(\{14,17,18,19,20\}\).

**Extension to arbitrary complete systems.** The known uniqueness of complete mutually unbiased systems in complex dimension four identifies each such system with this Pauli model under transformations preserving orthogonality. Thus the theorem, including the number forty, holds without a Pauli-model restriction.

## Verification
Run `python3 VERIFY.py` using a standard Python 3 installation. It uses only the standard library. Its tests exhaust all fifteen commuting triples; the five pairwise unbiased spectral bases; all sixty associated stabilizer projectors; all \(1140\) measurement-ray triples; and all \(4845\) four-ray selections, including exactly \(600\) singular selections. There are no random samples, numerical tolerances, external datasets, external certificates or unstated dependencies. The analytic argument above establishes the inference from the finite incidence lemma to all complex vectors; enumerating a finite test family of vectors alone would not establish that inference.

## Relationship to prior work
Dongwei Li, *Parity-Sensitive Fourier Uncertainty and Zero-Set Rigidity on the Finite Parabola* (first posted 11 September 2026, arXiv:2609.16030), solves a complete-MUB total-support problem for **odd prime** dimensions \(p\ge5\), with value \(p^2-p+2\). It does not assert the characteristic-two, dimension-four spectrum or the classification of all attainable support totals. The direct preprint full text could not be accessed during the comparison; its accessible complete abstract specifies the odd-prime domain. Mandayam, Bandyopadhyay, Grassl and Wootters (arXiv:1302.3709), particularly Section 5.2 and Appendix C, prove three-basis collision-entropy sharpness using two-qubit Pauli eigenstates, thereby supplying antecedent constructions and part of the Pauli apparatus. That paper does not state the **five-basis** full support spectrum, the forbidden values \(15,16\), the \(600\) four-ray dependency incidence, or the complete projective equality classification in this total-support problem. Brierley, Weigert and Bengtsson (arXiv:0907.4097) prove uniqueness of complete five-base systems in dimension four; their theorem is used rather than re-proved here.

## Limitations
The exact finite certificate is for one canonical five-base Pauli system; passage to all complete five-base systems invokes the cited uniqueness theorem. The result does not address incomplete systems of bases, other dimensions, approximate zeros, or arbitrary families of twenty frames. The enumeration of dependent quadruples is finite and exhaustive, but it is not a general closed-form rank theorem in arbitrary dimension. Historical priority remains open to previously unindexed equivalent statements; the directly motivated 2026 preprint was compared at the abstract and domain level, not through a complete manuscript inspection. No claim of independent external validation is made.
