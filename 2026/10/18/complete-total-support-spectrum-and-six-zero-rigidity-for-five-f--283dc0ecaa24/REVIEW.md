# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For any complete five-base family in complex dimension four, the support spectrum is exactly \(\{14,17,18,19,20\}\); the minimizing projective family consists of forty non-basis Pauli-stabilizer rays. The proof uses published uniqueness of the dimension-four MUB configuration, exact Pauli rank-one projectors, and a fully exhaustive Gaussian-integer incidence lemma. The checker executed all \(1140\) triples and \(4845\) quadruples of the twenty measurement rays. Every dependent four-selection lies in one of forty orthogonality six-sets; \(600\) such selections exist. The analytic kernel argument handles all possible complex states, not only enumerated stabilizer vectors. Each possible support value has an existence argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Dongwei Li's September 2026 odd-prime result assumes \(p\ge5\) and does not cover the even-prime-power instance. The fully inspected earlier Pauli paper by Mandayam and collaborators supplies three-basis entropic equality and some extremizer constructions; these are credited. It does not give the five-basis support spectrum, the two forbidden levels or the complete rank-incidence theorem. The fully inspected low-dimensional MUB classification by Brierley and collaborators supplies uniqueness but no such support theorem. Exact research-index searches using dimension, support, stabilizer and four-selection aliases yielded no matching prior result. The 2026 motivating manuscript itself was inaccessible beyond the complete public abstract; absence of other equivalent historical formulations cannot be guaranteed.

## Value — PASS
The theorem settles a natural exact complementary-measurement support question in the first even prime-power dimension, determines every attainable value rather than only the minimum, identifies the sharp gaps, and classifies all minimum states and the complete four-ray dependency incidence. Its scope is exact and finite; no implication for other dimensions or approximate measurement sparsity is asserted.
