# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed law is restricted to uniform labeled trees, a fixed ordered pair of marked vertices, and a specified distance. Its central finite polynomial follows from the weighted Cayley identity after contraction of the prescribed marked path and isolation of the remaining singleton components. The factorization gives one compulsory uniform attachment and independent multinomial trials, with the spanning-path boundary explicitly separate. The Gaussian-process limit requires both the path length and the expected number of side incidences to diverge. An independent binomial central limit argument for the attachment total and a random-index empirical-process invariance principle for its uniformly allocated sites give the covariance kernel exactly. The finite verifier reconstructed 280,392 labeled trees and completed 487 exact checks. No computational sample is offered as proof of the asymptotic statement.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated priority risk

The full weighted forest enumeration is classical (Pitman, 1996), and the fixed-distance two-endpoint degree correlations are known (Białas and Oleś, 2010; Oleś, 2012). Those results are not claimed as independent advances. The present assessed statement concerns the complete conditional side-incidence process along all vertices of a marked path and its functional Gaussian interpolation as the path length grows from a vanishing proportion of the tree to an almost-spanning proportion. The inspected primary passages do not state this limit. A 2011 conditioned-Galton–Watson distance-profile source was retrieved but its whole text could not be inspected; possible indirect coverage via general spine or forest-process theorems remains a real risk. This is a self-review, not definitive historical-priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The side-incidence empirical profile is a canonical whole-path statistic for a random tree conditioned on marked-vertex separation. Its exact binomial–multinomial structure identifies what is random in branch number versus branch position. The continuous covariance interpolation between Brownian motion and Brownian bridge separates fluctuations in the total number of attachments from their placement along the geodesic; the phenomenon cannot be read from endpoint degree marginals alone. The scale and conditioning are natural, and the result is a complete limit classification for sequences with a diverging expected number of incidences.

## Closest literature and limitations

Pitman (1996) supplies the classical enumeration. Białas and Oleś (2010), as reproduced in Oleś (2012, Chapter 3), cover the fixed-distance two-point degree correlation. Devroye and Janson (2008/2011) address distance profiles of conditioned Galton–Watson trees; an accessible full text comparison remained incomplete. The statement concerns incident side edges, not attached component sizes or shapes, and does not claim unconditional convergence. Earlier equivalent spine-process results are not ruled out.
