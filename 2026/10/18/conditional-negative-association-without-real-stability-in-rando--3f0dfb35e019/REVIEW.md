# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The cactus cycle-rank identity gives an exact factorization into disjoint cycle blocks and bridges. Each cycle block is a Bernoulli product with a single all-ones penalty, whose covariance between arbitrary disjoint increasing observables has the displayed negative rank-one factorization. External tilts and positive-probability pinnings preserve that block form, and independent-block closure proves CNA+. The forest measure is the same construction with all-full cycles prohibited; all conditional laws are well defined on positive-probability events. The explicit complex zero of the cycle generating polynomial proves failure of real stability for every genuine cycle when the penalty is nontrivial. The stand-alone exact checker passed 33,908 rational assertions; the proof, not finite enumeration, supports universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Primary full text and statements of Park (arXiv:2608.08565v1, Sections 1, 2, 6) give conditional NA for connection indicators and only branch-separated observables on wired trees; they explicitly say this does not imply NA for arbitrary disjoint edge supports. Çiçeksiz–Ravichandran (arXiv:2610.00549v1, Introduction, Theorems 1.5 and 1.9, Sections 4–5) derives pairwise edge-correlation results, not full NA/CNA+ on cacti. Earlier series-parallel Rayleigh results provide pairwise negative correlation but not the all-disjoint-observables result. Searches of mathematical records and accessible prior findings did not locate the exact CNA+ and non-real-stable cactus theorem; missing full text of some older potential sources and implicit consequences are residual risks, so this is not an independent novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The question of whether negative pairwise edge dependence strengthens to full NA/CNA+ for random-cluster and forest measures is explicitly motivated in recent primary research. The result resolves an infinite family of heterogeneous finite graphical measures, controls nonlinear disjoint increasing observables and all positive-field conditional laws, and sharply separates that property from real stability.
