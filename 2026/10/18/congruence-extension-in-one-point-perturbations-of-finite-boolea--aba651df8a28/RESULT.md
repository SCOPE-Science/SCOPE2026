# Congruence extension in one-point perturbations of finite Boolean algebras

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(\Omega\) be an \(n\)-element set. Given a proper subset \(S\subsetneq\Omega\) and a nonempty subset \(T\subseteq\Omega\setminus S\), define a unary operation on the powerset Boolean algebra by
\[
f_{S,T}(X)=\begin{cases}S\cup T,&X=S,\\X,&X\ne S.\end{cases}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak B_{S,T}\) be the resulting Boolean algebra with one additional unary operation. It is an extensive, idempotent, one-exception perturbation of the identity.

**Classification.** The algebra \(\mathfrak B_{S,T}\) has the congruence extension property (CEP) **if and only if**
\[
S=\varnothing\qquad\text{or}\qquad |S|=n-1.
\]
Its congruence lattice is, under inclusion, the family of kernel supports
\[
\{\varnothing\}\ \cup\ \{J\subseteq\Omega:T\subseteq J\}.
\]
Thus it has exactly \(1+2^{n-|T|}\) congruences, independently of \(S\).

On a fixed labeled \(n\)-atom Boolean algebra there are exactly \(3^n-2^n\) such distinct perturbations, of which
\[
\boxed{2^n+n-1}
\]
have CEP and \(3^n-2^{n+1}-n+1\) fail CEP. Up to Boolean-algebra isomorphism there are \(n(n+1)/2\) operations in this family and exactly \(n+1\) CEP operations.

For instance, the labeled totals \((\text{CEP},\text{failure})\) for \(n=2,3,4,5\) are respectively \((5,0)\), \((10,9)\), \((19,46)\), and \((36,175)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The signature is the full Boolean-algebra signature together with the explicitly defined operation \(f_{S,T}\). A subalgebra must be closed under this extra operation. Its congruence extension property means that every congruence on every such subalgebra is the restriction of an ambient congruence. The universe \(\Omega\) is finite with \(n\ge2\); the properness of \(S\) and nonemptiness/disjointness of \(T\) ensure exactly one input is changed.

Here CEP concerns each individual expanded algebra, not the entire variety generated by it. No additivity or order-monotonicity is assumed. Every operation in the family is extensive and idempotent, but \(f_{\varnothing,T}\) is not normal in the sense of preserving the least element.

## Proof

**1. All ambient congruences.** Boolean congruences on \(\mathcal P(\Omega)\) are parametrized by \(J\subseteq\Omega\):
\[
X\equiv_J Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\mathbin\triangle Y\subseteq J.
\]
The identity congruence, \(J=\varnothing\), is always compatible with \(f_{S,T}\). Suppose a nonempty \(J\) is compatible. Pick \(a\in J\) and compare \(X=S\) with \(Y=S\mathbin\triangle\{a\}\ne S\). They are \(\equiv_J\)-equivalent, while
\[
f_{S,T}(X)\mathbin\triangle f_{S,T}(Y)=T\mathbin\triangle\{a\}.
\]
Compatibility forces \(T\mathbin\triangle\{a\}\subseteq J\), hence \(T\subseteq J\). Conversely, if \(T\subseteq J\), each exceptional application changes an input only on \(T\), so \(X\equiv_JY\) implies \(f_{S,T}(X)\equiv_Jf_{S,T}(Y)\). This proves the complete ambient congruence description and count.

**2. All expanded subalgebras.** Every Boolean subalgebra of the finite powerset algebra consists of unions of blocks from a partition \(\pi\) of \(\Omega\). Write \(B_\pi\) for this subalgebra. It is closed under \(f_{S,T}\) exactly when either (i) \(S\notin B_\pi\), or (ii) \(S,T\in B_\pi\). In case (i) the restriction of \(f_{S,T}\) is the identity. In case (ii) the same argument as in step 1, on the block-Boolean algebra, proves that its congruence-kernel supports are the zero support and all unions \(I\) of blocks with \(T\subseteq I\). Such congruences extend by choosing ambient support \(J=I\).

In case (i), all Boolean congruences of \(B_\pi\) are compatible with its identity unary operation. A congruence support \(I\) is a union of blocks of \(\pi\). If \(I\ne\varnothing\), it extends precisely when **no block outside \(I\) is entirely contained in \(T\)**. Indeed, by step 1 every possible nonidentity ambient support must contain \(T\); adding \(T\) to \(I\) collapses an extra complete partition block exactly when such a block lies in \(T\). When no such block exists, \(J=I\cup T\) extends the congruence. The identity congruence always extends by \(J=\varnothing\).

**3. Sufficiency of the stated criterion.** If \(S=\varnothing\), then \(S\in B_\pi\) for every Boolean subalgebra. Only case (ii) can be closed under \(f_{S,T}\), and step 2 gives extension for every congruence. If \(|S|=n-1\), let \(\Omega\setminus S=\{t\}\), so \(T=\{t\}\). In any partition \(\pi\) with \(S\notin B_\pi\), the block containing \(t\) must contain an element of \(S\); otherwise \(S\) would be a union of all remaining blocks. Therefore no block is contained in \(T\). Step 2 again gives extension.

**4. Necessity.** Suppose \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(|\Omega\setminus S|\ge2\). Choose \(s\in S\), \(t\in T\), and \(u\in\Omega\setminus(S\cup\{t\})\). Partition \(\Omega\) into the two blocks \(\{t\}\), \(\{s,u\}\), and singleton blocks for all other atoms. Since \(S\) separates \(s\) and \(u\), it is not a union of blocks, and the unary operation restricts to the identity on this subalgebra. The nonzero local congruence collapsing precisely the block \(\{s,u\}\) cannot extend: every nonzero ambient congruence contains \(T\), hence collapses the disjoint block \(\{t\}\) as well. This proves CEP failure.

**5. Enumeration.** Assign each atom to one of three states: in \(S\), in \(T\), or in neither. Excluding \(T=\varnothing\) leaves \(3^n-2^n\) distinct pairs. CEP pairs with \(S=\varnothing\) number \(2^n-1\); CEP pairs with \(S\) a coatom number \(n\) and do not overlap the first group for \(n\ge2\). Therefore the CEP count is \(2^n+n-1\). Permuting atoms identifies two pairs exactly when the ordered cardinalities \((|S|,|T|)\) agree; summing over all admissible positive sizes of \(T\) gives \(n(n+1)/2\) total orbits and \(n+1\) CEP orbits. All asserted formulas follow.

## Relationship to prior work

Gyenis, Molnár, and Öztürk (2026), Section 2, give a nonadditive CEP example on the three-atom Boolean algebra where one specified coatom is sent to the top and all other elements are fixed. That operation is one instance of the CEP branch classified here. Their paper is concerned chiefly with deduction theorems, varieties, and strong nonadditivity; its stated results do not give the general all-size classification, explicit ambient congruence lattice, or exact enumeration for arbitrary \(S,T\).

Gyenis and Molnár's earlier work on varieties without CEP gives structural families and failures of congruence extension at the variety level, rather than this all-size single-exception classification. A separate published census identifies eight elements as the first finite carrier size with a possible CEP failure and counts arbitrary unary expansions on that carrier; it is a finite-carrier result and does not imply the present all-size family theorem. For \(n=3\), the present family is a strict, structurally specified subset of the general census.

## Limitations

These formulas apply only to the precise one-point, extensive, idempotent unary operations defined above. They do not classify arbitrary Boolean frames, arbitrary unary perturbations, or the CEP of generated varieties. A previously unpublished equivalent characterization of this restricted family may exist under another formulation; this result makes no claim of absolute historical priority. The computational checker exhausts only the reported finite values of \(n\).
