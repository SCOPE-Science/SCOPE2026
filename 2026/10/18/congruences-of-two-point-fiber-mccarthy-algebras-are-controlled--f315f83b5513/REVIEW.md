# Review: congruences of two-point McCarthy fibers

**Correctness: PASS.** Restricting any congruence to the zero-fiber elements yields a join-semilattice congruence, since disjunction of two zero-fiber points computes their index join. The projection term onto the zero fiber rules out mixed-bit identifications between different index classes. Multiplication by arbitrary positive-fiber points forces every mixed-bit collapsed class to dominate the quotient; the greatest class, when present, can indeed be collapsed, and no other class can. A quotient homomorphism plus an explicit compatibility check proves the converse. The componentwise inclusion test gives the full congruence lattice. Independent finite exhaustive enumeration reproduces all predictions for six index semilattices, including nonchains.

**Originality: PASS with documented residual risk.** The nearest primary source (Bonzio and St. John, arXiv:2503.10816, 2025, journal 2026) defines the construction and verifies one top-fiber congruence, without classifying arbitrary congruences or showing the finite congruence-lattice product. The relevant earlier work by Vali, Sundarayya and Swamy (2010) treats ideal-induced congruences on all conditional algebras and does not derive a complete description of the two-point-fiber semilattice family. Claim-level mathematical-record comparisons  identified no equivalent or dominating statement. A historical general representation theorem might nonetheless imply this classification; full inspection of the 1990 foundational monograph/paper was not possible.

**Value: PASS.** The new object is not an arbitrary small algebra: it is the canonical two-point Boolean-fiber construction arising in the modern structural decomposition of the McCarthy variety. The result determines all quotients, the whole congruence order, and a sharp finite splitting law; it also identifies exactly why the optional splitting can disappear for infinite index semilattices. This distinguishes semilattice quotient information from Boolean-fiber collapse information and yields concrete nonchain counts and a closed chain formula.

The finite checks cannot settle uniqueness or priority beyond the inspected sources, and they are not a substitute for the all-size argument. The conclusion pertains only to the indicated construction, not the entire variety.
