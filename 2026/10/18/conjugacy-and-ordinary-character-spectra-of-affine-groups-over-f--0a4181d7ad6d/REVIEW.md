# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The affine conjugation formula fixes the multiplier and makes each valuation shell below \(v(1-u)\) into exactly one orbit, while the remaining ideal is one orbit. Ring ideal cardinalities give the class-size and centralizer counts. A generating additive character identifies the Pontryagin dual with the ring; the unit group acts transitively on each valuation shell. Clifford's little-group construction gives exactly the stated character degrees and a concrete induced-character realization. Subtracting two ideal character sums gives all character values. The two order-32 rings have visibly different abelianizations. Each implication is proved with hypotheses in `RESULT.md`; 14 finite tests directly enumerate classes, centralizers, degree orbits, and induced-character orbit sums.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Wan and Zhou, arXiv:2506.05652v1, work over finite fields and study different class-algebra stabilization phenomena. Existing treatments of \(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) affine groups and ordinary Clifford theory are acknowledged rather than claimed. Tanaka (2004) computes affine association-scheme characters for cyclic residue rings, not a classification of ordinary characters for all finite chain rings. The new assertion is the universal ring-independent ordinary conjugacy-class-size and character-degree distributions for all finite commutative chain rings plus a nonisomorphic equal-spectrum comparison. Comparison searches did not identify a result that logically subsumes this full statement. A more general published or thesis-level treatment may exist outside the inspected material, particularly the Dokchitser thesis cited in the cyclic-ring discussion.

## Value — PASS

The complete conjugacy and representation formulas classify natural invariants of every affine group on a finite commutative chain ring, without assuming equal characteristic. The uniform spectral distributions allow comparison across distinct ring geometries, and the explicit \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\) versus \(\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^3)\) example proves their failure to determine even the abelianization. This is a structural classification rather than a small parameter census.

## Remaining scientific limits

The full character tables need not agree for rings with the same \((q,n)\), and the statement does not cover general finite local rings. Finite checks are independent diagnostics, not general proofs. Bibliographic originality is not an external or independent certification.
