# Review of connected support-graph singularities

## Correctness

PASS. The argument uses all coordinate facets of the Birkhoff polytope and its exact face-support rank formula. The key lattice claim is an explicitly demonstrated integral exact sequence: missing-edge data extend to integer zero-margin matrices precisely when the imbalance of each connected support component vanishes. This proves the smooth-orbit criterion, free class-group formula, and the precise transverse rank-one matrix cone without extrapolating finite data. The packaged verifier exhausts the \(3\times3\) and \(4\times4\) facet intersections and checks higher-rank two-block arithmetic.

## Originality

PASS, subject to an unindexed-literature risk. Paffenholz (2013) supplies the face-graph and dimension results but not the toric singular-component classification. Lenz (2010) addresses the defining ideals and Gröbner bases, not the singular strata. Standard toric criteria alone do not specify the Birkhoff component count or transverse Segre dimensions. The known \(3\times3\) cubic case is excluded from the new theorem; the proposed classification is for all \(n\ge4\). Searches against available prior claims and published records showed no identical or stronger all-rank statement. A different formulation of transportation-polytope cone geometry could cover portions, so novelty is qualified rather than certified.

## Value

PASS. The theorem settles a natural exact geometric classification for the normal toric varieties of the doubly stochastic matrix polytopes: the complete singular support, the number and dimensions of its irreducible components, local divisor theory on every torus orbit, and generic normal slices of determinantal type. The component count grows centrally binomially, and the two transverse families at \(n=4\) have distinct multiplicities. This is more than a small numerical example or a generic toric criterion.

## Limits

The singular scheme structure at component crossings and resolutions are not determined. Generic normal slices do not determine all closed-point germs. Search-based overlap assessment has residual uncertainty. The finite checker is corroborative, not a proof of the all-rank assertion.
