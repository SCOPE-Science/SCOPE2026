# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The explicit three-element classification follows from the white-hub barrier at spoke vertices and the need for a second colored rim neighbor on the other side of a spoke. Constructive forcing verifies sufficiency. The reconfiguration adjacency follows from intersections of three-element sets; the hub cycle and one-step attachments prove connectivity. A pair-multiplicity table, obtained by substituting the explicit optimum families, supplies each vertex degree and the handshaking count. A separate exhaustive verifier tests the state classification and degree formulas for 30 distinct parameter choices, without treating finite checks as proof for every parameter.

## Originality

PASS with residual bibliographic uncertainty. Anderton et al. (arXiv:2310.18513) already give the zero-forcing number and discuss seed configurations; Geneson–Haas–Hogben (arXiv:2009.00220) establish the token-exchange framework and results for other graph classes. The related TAR literature (arXiv:2205.09668) concerns a different set family and adjacency rule. No inspected primary material gives the complete token-jumping exchange graph, degree distribution, edge formula or the connected hub-cycle description for generalized gears. Unindexed equivalent work remains a genuine risk.

## Value

PASS. Reconfigurability of optimum zero-forcing seeds is a natural operational question not decided by a minimum cardinality or propagation time. The result explicitly describes every optimum, its allowable local exchanges, a connected backbone and the complete degree distribution, showing precisely how all optimal starting configurations can be moved between without loss of feasibility.

## Limits and comparison

The scope is ordinary zero forcing on \(\operatorname{Gr}(m,r)\) for \(m\ge4\) and \(r\ge1\). The small three-spoke case is excluded; the diameter result is only a bound. Existing general theorems for forests cannot be applied because generalized gears have cycles. The classification of possible seed shapes is closely related to the propagation-time paper and is not claimed independently as an unrelated discovery.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
