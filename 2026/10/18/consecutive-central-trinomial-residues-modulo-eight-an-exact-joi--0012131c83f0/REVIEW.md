# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The two exact binary recurrences follow from the Laurent-polynomial constant-term identity; their modulo-eight simplification holds in each parity. The explicitly specified 24-state graph has a positive normalized invariant vector. Its verified annihilating polynomial bounds the full nontrivial spectrum by \(\sqrt2\), yielding the general-\(N\) discrepancy through dyadic blocks. Three exact counts and the reduced characteristic polynomial of the eighth power prove the sharpness identity for every positive exponent. The standalone checker verifies the finite matrix certificates, rather than replacing the proof of infinitude.

## Originality
PASS, with residual literature risk. Sun's 2010 paper develops central trinomial congruences; Rowland and Yassawi's 2013 paper gives the general automaton framework and a modulo-four example. Krattenthaler and Müller (2016), Theorem 17, already provide **the pointwise modulo-eight formula**, a stronger statement for individual terms, so that is expressly excluded from any originality claim. The asserted joint pair probabilities, quantitative \(\sqrt N\) discrepancy and sharpness are different asymptotic invariants, not present in the inspected theorem statements and sections. A general automatic-sequence cylinder-frequency result might cover the new calculation; this remains a risk.

## Value
PASS. Consecutive-term correlations are a natural refinement of single-term congruence classifications. The sixteen-way law quantifies a substantive failure of independence even though each marginal is uniform. The exact optimal discrepancy exponent gives an additional arithmetic limitation and is proved for infinitely many scales.

## Limitations and sources
The result is confined to consecutive pairs modulo eight; independent review and exhaustive coverage of all relevant literature have not occurred. Sources: Sun, arXiv:1008.3887; Rowland–Yassawi, arXiv:1310.8635, Section 4.2; Krattenthaler–Müller, arXiv:1608.05657, Section 8.4, Theorem 17. The last source is the closest and must be credited for pointwise congruences.
