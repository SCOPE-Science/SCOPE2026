# Coordinate and determinantal components of the first codimension-two restricted nullcone

## Finding

Let \(p\ge3\) be prime, \(n=p+2\), and \(k\) an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p\). On the restricted Lie algebra \(\mathfrak u_n(k)\) of strictly upper-triangular matrices, the restricted power is \(X^{[p]}=X^p\). Write \(a_i=X_{i,i+1}\) for \(1\le i\le p+1\) and \(b_i=X_{i,i+2}\) for \(1\le i\le p\).

The irreducible components of the reduced locus \(\{X\in\mathfrak u_n(k):X^p=0\}_{\mathrm{red}}\) are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{ij}&=V(a_i,a_j), & 1\le i<j\le p+1,\quad j-i\ge2,\\
Q_k&=V(a_k,a_{k+1}b_{k-1}+a_{k-1}b_k), & 2\le k\le p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The \(P_{ij}\) are coordinate subspaces, and the \(Q_k\) are products of the cone of \(2\times2\) matrices of rank at most one with an affine space. All components are geometrically irreducible and of codimension two. There are \(p(p-1)/2\) linear and \(p-1\) nonlinear components, for a total of \((p-1)(p+2)/2\).

The condition is automatic in sizes at most \(p\). At size \(p+1\) the locus is a union of coordinate hyperplanes, so the first size with codimension two is \(p+2\). The contribution here is the explicit linear and determinantal component decomposition at that size, not the numerical component count or the finite-field point count.

## Scope

The displayed equations describe the components after algebraic closure over any base field of characteristic \(p\). The entries at distance at least three above the diagonal are unrestricted. No assertion is made about reducedness of the ideal generated by the entries of \(X^p\).

## Proof

A term of an entry of \(X^p\) comes from a strictly increasing path of length \(p\). Its increments sum to at most \(p+1\), so they are all one or have just one increment of two. The only potentially nonzero entries are consequently
\[
(X^p)_{1,p+1}=\prod_{i=1}^p a_i,\qquad
(X^p)_{2,p+2}=\prod_{i=2}^{p+1} a_i,\qquad
(X^p)_{1,p+2}=\sum_{j=1}^p b_j\prod_{i\notin\{j,j+1\}}a_i,
\]
where the last product ranges over \(1\le i\le p+1\). The same path argument gives the asserted description at smaller sizes.

Two nonadjacent zeros among the \(a_i\) force all three entries to vanish and put the matrix on a \(P_{ij}\). If there is only one zero, the first two products force its index \(k\) to be interior. Only the \(j=k-1,k\) terms of the last expression can survive, and their sum is \(a_{k+1}b_{k-1}+a_{k-1}b_k\) times the product of the nonzero \(a_i\) outside \(\{k-1,k,k+1\}\). Thus this case lies on \(Q_k\) exactly when the last entry vanishes. The remaining possibility is just two adjacent zeros \(a_i=a_{i+1}=0\). The last entry then vanishes exactly when \(b_i=0\), and the point lies on \(Q_k\) for any interior index \(k\in\{i,i+1\}\). Conversely, when \(a_k=0\) the same factorization of the last entry holds regardless of further zeros, so every \(Q_k\) is contained in the locus. This proves equality of the reduced zero sets.

Each \(P_{ij}\) is linear. In \(a_k=0\) the four distinct coordinates in the remaining equation give a generic \(2\times2\) determinant after a sign change, so this is a geometrically irreducible hypersurface in the hyperplane. Its vanishing ideal has just the linear generator \(a_k\) in degree one up to scalar, showing it is distinct from the linear components and from the other \(Q_k\). The \(P_{ij}\) are distinct by their coordinate pairs. All these irreducible sets have codimension two, hence none properly contains another. The linear count is \(\binom{p+1}{2}-p=p(p-1)/2\); the nonlinear count is the number of interior indices.

## Relationship to prior work and finite checks

The characteristic-zero orbital-variety interpretation uses the nilpotent orbit closure of partition \((p,2)\); its tableau count gives the same numerical component count. The work of Melnikov, Boos and Reineke gives explicit context for square-zero upper-triangular varieties, not the component equations above for characteristic \(p\ge3\).

The finite-field count is already a specialization of the triangular Jordan-type enumerations of Yip and Fuchs–Kirillov. In particular, with \(n=p+2\) only the types \((n)\) and \((n-1,1)\) must be subtracted from \(q^{\binom n2}\). Fuchs–Kirillov, Proposition 3.1, gives \(A_n=q^{n-2}(q-1)A_{n-1}\) for the first type, and \(B_n=q^{n-2}((q-1)B_{n-1}+A_{n-1})\) for the second when \(n\ge4\). With \(A_3=q(q-1)^2\) and \(B_3=(q-1)(2q+1)\), subtraction gives
\[
q^{\binom{p+2}{2}}-A_{p+2}-B_{p+2}
=q^{\binom{p+2}{2}-p-2}\bigl(q^{p+2}-(q-1)^p(q^2+pq+1)\bigr).
\]
This known implication is useful as a count consistency check, not as an originality claim. The companion `verify_pnull.py` compares matrix-power vanishing with membership in the displayed union, exhaustively on the first two superdiagonals for \((p,q)=(3,3)\) and at the separate boundary \((2,2),(2,4)\), also checking the count. It additionally contains fixed-seed tests with unrestricted higher superdiagonals. The geometric result is proved above, not inferred from these finite checks.
