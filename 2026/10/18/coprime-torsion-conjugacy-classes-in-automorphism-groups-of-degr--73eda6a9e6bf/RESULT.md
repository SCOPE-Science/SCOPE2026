# Coprime torsion conjugacy classes in automorphism groups of degree-two truncated free associative algebras

## Finding
Let \(q\) be a prime power, let \(d\ge2\), and fix a positive integer \(\ell\mid(q-1)\). Set
\[
A_{d,q}=\mathbb F_q\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\rangle/(x_1,\ldots,x_d)^3,
\qquad G_{d,q}=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q}(A_{d,q}).
\]
This is the unital **noncommutative** free associative algebra truncated after words of length two. Put \(\Omega_\ell=\{\zeta\in\mathbb F_q^\times:\zeta^\ell=1\}\). For every family \(r=(r_\zeta)_{\zeta\in\Omega_\ell}\) of nonnegative integers summing to \(d\), define the quadratic-word resonance statistic
\[
H(r)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\Omega_\ell}r_\alpha r_\beta r_{\alpha\beta}.
\]
**Theorem.** The conjugacy classes of automorphisms \(f\in G_{d,q}\) satisfying \(f^\ell=1\) are in bijection with these multiplicity families. The class corresponding to \(r\) has exactly
\[
\boxed{C_q(r)=\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_q)|}{\prod_{\zeta\in\Omega_\ell}|\operatorname{GL}_{r_\zeta}(\mathbb F_q)|}\,q^{d^3-H(r)}}
\]
elements, where \(|\operatorname{GL}_0(\mathbb F_q)|=1\). Consequently the number of conjugacy classes contained in \(\{f:f^\ell=1\}\) is \(\binom{d+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\), and their total element count is
\[
\boxed{T_{d,q}(\ell)=\sum_{\substack{r_\zeta\ge0\\\sum r_\zeta=d}}C_q(r).}
\]
Every element in class \(r\) has exact order
\[
\operatorname{lcm}\{\operatorname{ord}(\zeta):r_\zeta>0\},
\]
with the convention that the order of the scalar \(1\) is one. This also gives the exact order-by-order distribution by restricting the sum. In particular, every such automorphism is conjugate to its diagonal linear part.

For odd \(q\) and \(\ell=2\), write \(a=r_1\), \(b=r_{-1}\), \(a+b=d\). Then \(H=a^3+3ab^2\) and the class size becomes
\[
\boxed{C_q(a,b)={d\brack a}_q q^{ab+3a^2b+b^3},}
\]
where \({d\brack a}_q\) is a Gaussian coefficient. For \(d=2\), the three involution-or-identity class sizes are \(1\), \(q(q+1)q^4\), and \(q^8\). Their sum is \(1+q^5(q+1)+q^8\); at \(q=3\), it is \(7534\).

## Assumptions and scope
The only characteristic hypothesis is \(\ell\mid(q-1)\), so \(\ell\) is prime to the characteristic and its distinct roots all belong to \(\mathbb F_q\). Nonprime extension fields are included. The symbols \(x_i\) are noncommuting, and the maximal ideal \(J\) satisfies \(J^3=0\). No claim is made about finite-order automorphisms of orders divisible by the field characteristic or with nonsplit linear eigenvalues. All conjugacy classes are taken inside the full automorphism group \(G_{d,q}\), not just its linear subgroup.

## Proof
Put \(V=J/J^2\), and identify \(J\), as a vector space, with \(V\oplus(V\otimes V)\). Multiplication of degree-one vectors is the unrestricted tensor product, and all products of total length three vanish. Since the algebra is freely generated by degree-one elements modulo words of length three, an algebra endomorphism fixing the identity is uniquely specified by arbitrary images of the generators in \(J\). It is invertible if and only if the degree-one component is an invertible map on \(V\). Hence every automorphism is specified by a unique \(g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\) and an arbitrary quadratic correction \(u\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,V\otimes V)\), and
\[
|G_{d,q}|=|\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_q)|q^{d^3}.
\]
Reparametrize the quadratic correction by \(m=ug^{-1}\). Then the automorphism group is the split semidirect product \(U\rtimes\operatorname{GL}(V)\) with additive \(U=\operatorname{Hom}(V,V\otimes V)\) and representation
\[
\rho(g)(m)=(g\otimes g)m g^{-1}.
\]
Explicitly \((m,g)(m',g')=(m+\rho(g)m',gg')\).

If \((m,g)^\ell=(0,I)\), its linear part satisfies \(g^\ell=I\). Because the polynomial \(t^\ell-1\) has distinct roots in \(\mathbb F_q\), \(g\) is diagonalizable with eigenvalues in \(\Omega_\ell\). Up to \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\)-conjugacy it is uniquely determined by eigenspace dimensions \(r_\zeta\). Conversely every such multiplicity family gives a linear automorphism \((0,g)\) of order dividing \(\ell\).

Fix such a \(g\). Let \(N_g=I+\rho(g)+\cdots+\rho(g)^{\ell-1}\). Direct semidirect-product multiplication gives
\[
(m,g)^\ell=(N_gm,I).
\]
The operator \(\rho(g)\) is diagonalizable and has \(\ell\)-th power one, so \(N_g\) acts by \(\ell\) on its fixed space and by zero on every other eigenspace. Since \(\ell\) is invertible in the field,
\[
\ker N_g=\operatorname{im}(I-\rho(g)),
\qquad \dim\ker N_g=d^3-\dim U^{\rho(g)}.
\]
Conjugation by \((v,I)\) changes the quadratic coordinate of \((0,g)\) by \((I-\rho(g))v\). Thus every \((m,g)\) with \((m,g)^\ell=1\) is conjugate to \((0,g)\), and all such quadratic coordinates form one orbit under \(U\). Since projection to \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\) preserves linear-part similarity under conjugation, different multiplicity families cannot merge.

To compute the orbit size, decompose \(V=\bigoplus_{\zeta\in\Omega_\ell}V_\zeta\). The subspace \(\operatorname{Hom}(V_\gamma,V_\alpha\otimes V_\beta)\subseteq U\) is acted on by \(\rho(g)\) with scalar \(\alpha\beta\gamma^{-1}\). Hence
\[
\dim U^{\rho(g)}=\sum_{\alpha\beta=\gamma}r_\alpha r_\beta r_\gamma=H(r),
\qquad |\ker N_g|=q^{d^3-H(r)}.
\]
The number of possible \(g\) having these eigenspace multiplicities is
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb F_q)|}{\prod_\zeta|\operatorname{GL}_{r_\zeta}(\mathbb F_q)|}.
\]
Each allows precisely \(q^{d^3-H(r)}\) quadratic corrections satisfying the torsion equation. Since all such elements lie in the same conjugacy class, their product is its cardinality, proving \(C_q(r)\). Counting weak compositions of \(d\) into \(\ell\) parts gives the conjugacy-class number. Conjugacy preserves element order, and a linear representative has order the least common multiple of its eigenvalue orders, proving the exact-order assertion.

For \(\ell=2\), the resonance triples have signs \((+,+,+)\), \((+,-,-)\), \((-,+,-)\), and \((-,-,+)\); hence \(H=a^3+3ab^2\). The identity \(|\operatorname{GL}_d|/(|\operatorname{GL}_a||\operatorname{GL}_b|)=q^{ab}{d\brack a}_q\) gives the displayed involution formula.

## Verification
A standalone dependency-free Python checker, `verify_torsion.py`, constructs the \(8\)-dimensional representation \(\rho(g)\) for \(d=2\) by explicit tensor-coordinate multiplication over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_7\). It independently enumerates all invertible matrices \(g\) satisfying \(g^\ell=I\), computes the kernel dimension of the norm operator \(N_g\) by modular row reduction, and compares the total weighted fiber counts and the class-by-class resonance formula. Six parameter pairs are checked, including \(\ell=2,3,4,6\). The script reports `ALL_TORSION_ENUMERATIONS_PASSED 6`. The general theorem is established by the preceding algebraic proof, not by these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Militaru's 2021 work expresses automorphism groups of arbitrary finite-dimensional associative algebras through their quantum symmetry semigroups and classifies group gradings by conjugation; that general abstract framework is fully acknowledged. The inspected article does not enumerate individual \(\ell\)-torsion conjugacy classes of the present truncated noncommutative free algebras.

A published classification of automorphisms of truncated coordinate-cross algebras gives a monomial-linear-part semidirect product. Those commutative algebras impose vanishing products between different generators, unlike the free noncommutative \(A_{d,q}\). Their automorphism-group structure therefore neither specializes to nor implies the displayed resonance counts. Another closely related tensor-fixed-space census for \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\) treats the representation \(g^T\otimes g^T\otimes g^{-1}\) over all invertible matrices; its fixed-space dimensions overlap the present \(d=2\) weight calculation, but it does not state the multi-eigenvalue multiplicity formula or full torsion-conjugacy counts in all dimensions. Elementary coprime-action conjugacy principles and semidirect-product counting are known background mechanisms, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
This theorem deliberately restricts to split, characteristic-coprime orders \(\ell\mid(q-1)\). The exact total count for a fixed \(\ell\) is a finite explicit sum, not a claimed closed product. The finite computations verify only the specified dimension-two prime-field cases. Literature searches and direct comparisons did not identify a dominating previously published formula for the full family, but a differently phrased consequence of the general theory of relatively free algebra automorphisms or coprime semidirect products could already contain the same count; this is an originality risk requiring independent expert assessment. Independent validation has not been performed.
