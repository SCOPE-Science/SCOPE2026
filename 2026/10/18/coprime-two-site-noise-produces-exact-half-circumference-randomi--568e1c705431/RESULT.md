# Coprime two-site noise produces exact half-circumference randomization in Rule 90

## Finding

**Theorem (exact randomization and arithmetic obstruction).** Let \(n\ge3\) and \(1\le d<n\). Consider the binary nearest-neighbor parity dynamics on the labelled cycle \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\):
\[
X_{t+1}=AX_t+\xi_t e_0+\eta_t e_d\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2^n,\qquad
(Ax)_j=x_{j-1}+x_{j+1}.
\]
Here all additions are modulo two, and the scalar variables \(\xi_t,\eta_t\) are independent fair bits, independent over time and of the initial configuration. Both selected sites receive **additive bit flips after the deterministic synchronous update**, not dilution of the parity rule. Fix an arbitrary deterministic initial configuration.

For every time \(t\ge0\), the law of \(X_t\) is uniform on an affine subspace of dimension
\[
r_t=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{span}\{A^ke_0,A^ke_d:0\le k<t\}.
\]
Let \(r_\infty\) denote the eventual dimension, reached by \(t=n\). The exact deficiency \(c(n,d)=n-r_\infty\) is
\[
c(n,d)=\begin{cases}
(\gcd(n,d)-1)/2,&n\text{ odd},\\[3pt]
h/2-\mathbf1_{d\not\equiv0\pmod h},&n\text{ even},\quad h=\gcd(n,2d).
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(X_t\) becomes **exactly uniform on all \(2^n\) configurations in finite time, regardless of its starting state, if and only if \(\gcd(n,d)=1\)**. In that case the exact first time is
\[
\boxed{t_{\mathrm{mix}}=\lceil n/2\rceil},\qquad
r_t=\min(2t,n)\quad\text{for every integer }t\ge0.
\]
With just one noisy site, complete finite-time randomization is impossible for all \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The system is finite, periodic and homogeneous. The parity operator \(A\) is the deterministic elementary Rule 90 map (not its stochastic dilution). At each synchronous step two fixed, distinct labelled sites receive fresh, independent, unbiased XOR flips. The result asserts **exact uniformity** rather than small total-variation error or asymptotic relaxation. The code uses least significant bit for site zero; indices in the proof are interpreted modulo \(n\).

## Proof

**1. A noise-history image gives the entire probability law.** Iteration yields
\[
X_t=A^tX_0+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}A^k(\xi'_{k}e_0+\eta'_{k}e_d),
\]
where the primed bits are a time-reordered independent fair family. A linear map sends uniform inputs over \(\mathbb F_2^{2t}\) to the uniform law on its image. Therefore the law is uniform on the coset \(A^tX_0+W_t\), with \(W_t\) the span displayed above. Cayley–Hamilton gives \(W_n=W_{n+1}=\cdots=W_\infty\).

**2. Krylov span of one noise site.** Put \(u_0=e_0\) and \(u_j=e_j+e_{-j}\) for \(1\le j\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). For \(j=1\), \(Au_1=u_2\) unless the terminal reflection is reached; the double contribution at zero cancels in characteristic two. For subsequent interior \(j\), \(Au_j=u_{j-1}+u_{j+1}\). Together with \(Ae_0=u_1\), triangular induction proves
\[
\operatorname{span}\{A^ke_0:0\le k<t\}
=\operatorname{span}\{u_0,\ldots,u_{t-1}\}
\quad(1\le t\le\lceil n/2\rceil),
\]
where at the odd terminal index the last vector is an ordinary reflection pair and at the even terminal index the antipodal vector is absent. Beyond this range the span does not increase. In particular its total dimension is \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) when \(n\) is odd and \(n/2\) when \(n\) is even. The same argument translated by \(d\) describes the span generated by \(e_d\).

**3. Duality turns the rank calculation into a dihedral-orbit census.** Use the nondegenerate standard scalar product over \(\mathbb F_2\). A vector \(v=(v_j)\) is orthogonal to the full span from site zero exactly when \(v_0=0\) and \(v_j=v_{-j}\) for every \(j\). Orthogonality to the full span from site \(d\) means \(v_d=0\) and \(v_j=v_{2d-j}\) for every \(j\). Thus
\[
W_\infty^\perp
=\{v:v_j=v_{-j}=v_{2d-j}\text{ for all }j,\ v_0=v_d=0\}.
\]
The two reflections compose to translation by \(2d\), so such vectors have period dividing \(h=\gcd(n,2d)\). They correspond to reflection-invariant binary functions on \(\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\) with zero values at residues \(0\) and \(d\).

If \(n\) is odd, \(h=\gcd(n,d)\) is odd. The inversion orbits consist of the single fixed residue zero and \((h-1)/2\) opposite pairs. Removing the zero orbit leaves \((h-1)/2\) independent bits. If \(n\) is even, \(h\) is even. The inversion orbits consist of fixed residues \(0,h/2\) and \((h-2)/2\) pairs, making \(h/2+1\) orbits. Requiring value zero at residue zero removes one. Since \(2d\equiv0\pmod h\), the residue \(d\) is either \(0\) or \(h/2\), and in the latter case removes one more orbit. Hence \(\dim W_\infty^\perp=c(n,d)\) as claimed.

**4. Complete randomization occurs precisely at coprime separation.** The preceding deficiency is zero for odd \(n\) exactly when \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). For even \(n\), the deficiency is zero exactly when \(h=2\) and \(d\) is odd, which is equivalent to \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). A single input has the smaller Krylov dimensions computed in Step 2, so it never fills \(\mathbb F_2^n\).

**5. Sharp finite-time threshold.** Suppose \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). Denote the truncated one-site spaces from Step 2 by \(U_0(t)\) and \(U_d(t)\). A vector lying in both is symmetric about both centers, hence invariant under translation by \(2d\). If \(n\) is odd, this makes the vector spatially constant. For \(t\le(n-1)/2\), every vector in \(U_0(t)\) misses at least one site, so their intersection is zero. At \(t=(n+1)/2\), both spaces are full reflection-fixed spaces and their intersection is the one-dimensional constant-vector space. Thus their sum has dimensions \(2t\) up to the preceding time and \(n\) at the threshold.

If \(n\) is even, coprimality makes \(d\) odd. Invariance under translation by \(2d\) then forces each common vector to be constant separately on the even and odd sites. For all \(t\le n/2\), the first truncated space has zero at its antipode \(n/2\), and the second has zero at its antipode \(d+n/2\). These two sites have opposite parity, so both constants vanish. Thus the two spaces have zero intersection, and their sum has dimension \(2t\) through \(t=n/2\). Finally, at any time fewer than \(\lceil n/2\rceil\), at most \(2t<n\) fair input bits are available, so full uniformity is impossible. This proves the exact first time and the asserted rank profile for all later times.

## Relationship to prior work

Martin, Odlyzko and Wolfram (1984) developed the deterministic algebraic dynamics and state-transition structure of Rule 90. Lettieri, Stevens and Thomas (2006) computed characteristic and minimal polynomials of the deterministic Rule 90 map and discussed periodic orbits. Neither of the inspected deterministic analyses states the two-site fair-additive-noise rank, the coprime spatial criterion, or exact first uniform time.

Mendonça (2015 preprint) examined a noisy exclusive-or probabilistic cellular automaton and associated nonequilibrium critical behavior. Its local rule retains the XOR output with probability \(p\) and otherwise writes zero; this is **not** the present two-site additive fair-bit perturbation. The present finite-volume result neither implies nor contradicts that model's infinite-system phase-transition analysis. Sundaram and Hadjicostis (2012/2013) give finite-field structural controllability criteria and generic index bounds for variable-weight multiagent systems. Their Theorem 5 assumes free self-loop weights and sufficiently large fields; it does not cover the fixed characteristic-two, zero-diagonal ring matrix here. Generic Kalman controllability and Krylov-space theory underlie the proof, and differently formulated controllability results remain a bibliographic risk.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes fresh independent unbiased binary flips at two **fixed** sites, synchronous deterministic Rule 90 evolution, and a finite ring. It makes no assertion of finite-time exact uniformity for biased or temporally correlated noise, for randomly moving noise sites, or for other automata. With noncoprime separation the zero-start law becomes uniform on a proper linear subspace, but we do not classify all stationary distributions for arbitrary starts. The historical-literature comparison is extensive but not exhaustive; independent external validation has not been performed.
