# Scientific review: cotangent screening of cyclic chord families

## Correctness — PASS
The source norm for the real free two-convex lattice is converted without symmetry restrictions into a supremum of nuclear norms, equivalently the concave trace-square-root functional over the entire edge-weight probability simplex. The unperturbed cycle has a globally optimal uniform weighting by averaging over rotations. Its mean-zero Laplacian is positive definite, so directional derivatives for each added rank-one chord are valid even though the full ambient Laplacian has a fixed constant nullspace. A necessary and sufficient supporting-hyperplane/KKT test gives every chord's screening inequality simultaneously. Fourier diagonalization and a telescoping finite sine sum evaluate the gradient ratio as the stated cotangent threshold; a positive directional derivative proves strict increase when any chord exceeds it. The short-arc potential proves pointwise strict growth when the amplitude exceeds the reciprocal length. Trigonometric asymptotics establish both limits. The standalone program reconstructed unrestricted simplex optimizations and passed 77 one- and two-chord configurations; that check is supplementary to the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk
Jardón-Sánchez et al., *Free Banach lattices under convexity conditions* (arXiv:2101.03510; Springer open-access text), provides the foundational lattice norm but not the added-chord spectral sensitivity. The accessible previous claim *Sharp spectral bounds and equality for graph-gradient two-summing norms* treats general bounds and the unperturbed edge-transitive graph; it does not include a new-chord all-or-none plateau theorem. Complete-bipartite and complete-tripartite calculations concern different edge sets; their thresholds cannot be substituted for a cycle's cotangent kernel. The full public research results *Cycle Riesz transforms as parity blocks of doubled cyclic Hilbert transforms* and *Exact endpoint norm and dyadic parity law for the cyclic Hilbert transform* share related finite trigonometric sums, but determine different operators and endpoint norms, with no implication to the free-lattice two-summing screening criterion. Semantic searches of mathematical records and published literature found no covering theorem. Absence of a retrieved match does not prove uniqueness: a more general Schatten concavity or optimal-design screening result could imply the threshold after nonobvious specialization.

## Value — PASS
The norm has a genuine structural plateau over a full product of amplitude intervals, no matter how many chords are added; beyond the exact coordinatewise boundary it strictly increases. This is not a small numerical refinement: it identifies an explicit and nontrivial stability domain for the absolutely two-summing norm of an infinite family of operators and demonstrates strict discrepancy between the free lattice's pointwise order and its norm. The distance dependence and opposite-vertex decay quantify how long-range perturbations become progressively more restrictive as dimension grows.

## Scope and limitations
The statement is limited to real Hilbert generators, two-convexity with constant one and the unweighted rim cycle, and determines only the norm-equality regime, not the post-threshold optimal value. Numerical tests do not prove the infinite-family theorem, and independent review is still needed to exclude literature using other terminology.
