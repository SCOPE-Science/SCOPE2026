# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The Bessel normalization follows from the spherical harmonic Fourier--Bessel identity with unnormalized surface measure and Fourier phase \(e^{-ix\cdot\omega}\). The common logarithmic pole of both moments is obtained from the fixed-order uniform Hankel expansion and the mean of \(|\cos t|^q\). Dirichlet integration by parts controls the zero-mean tails *uniformly* as \(q\downarrow q_c\). Consequently the adjacent-mode difference has a genuine conditional improper-integral limit. Its strict positivity uses the cited finite-cell theorem of Xu at exactly \(q=q_c>2\), not a formal subtraction of divergent integrals. The mean-value theorem then supplies both scaling laws and their constants. Risks: the nonstandard finite-cell positivity is a relied-upon published mathematical input; no independent proof of that input is provided. Numeric quadrature is a consistency check only.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** The close comparison is Xu, *Sharp radial--angular mixed norm Fourier extension and global stability on spheres*, arXiv:2610.19648, Theorems 1.2, 1.3, and 3.9: this establishes exact sharp finite-exponent modal coefficients and a finite-cell inequality, but not the endpoint limit of the optimal stability coefficient or the fractional \(1/d\) power law. The earlier mixed-norm Fourier--Bessel decomposition of Carneiro--Oliveira e Silva--Sousa (arXiv:1710.10365) supplies spectral context, not this critical asymptotic. The 2024 sharp ordinary \(L^4\) extension result of Carneiro--Negro--Oliveira e Silva (arXiv:2410.23106) concerns a different functional. Published record and technical-term searches found no equivalent endpoint asymptotic. These observations are evidence, not proof of priority. Risk: a closely related asymptotic could be implicit in unindexed discussions of renormalized Bessel moments, and the result critically builds on Xu's recent theorem.

**Value — PASS.** The physically natural strong-type boundary is precisely where the *individual* harmonic Bessel moments diverge; neither their exact finite-exponent values nor qualitative strict modal ordering conveys the rate at which the *sharp* stability constant vanishes. The fractional absolute exponent \(1/d\), positive finite coefficient, and distinct linear relative collapse quantify an otherwise singular endpoint and supply an endpoint-normalized observable that can be compared across dimensions. The result is not a renaming or an arbitrary finite truncation, and no analogous logarithmic pole is claimed for the ordinary \(L^4\) extension problem. Limits: fixed dimensions \(d\ge3\), no bounded critical endpoint, no closed form for the positive coefficient, no full dimension-two result.
