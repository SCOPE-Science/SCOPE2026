# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces every homogeneous block of the triangular analytic multiplier to a constant-coefficient tridiagonal Gram matrix, whose phase-gauged sine eigenvectors give the positive singular spectrum and rank. The trace identities and asymptotic constants follow from exact finite sums, endpoint Riemann estimates, harmonic sums and dominated convergence. In the near-critical window the rescaled positive singular squares converge termwise to \(\tau^2+(\pi ct/u)^2\); the summable \(t^{-p}\) majorant validates exchanging the limiting series and the degree Riemann sum only when \(p>1\). Exact \(p=2\) follows from a cosecant-squared sum. Finite-matrix computation independently checks algebra and constants but is not used to prove claims for all depths.

## Originality — PASS subject to historical-priority uncertainty

Rinkel–Seghier (arXiv:1302.6106v1) studies inverses of positive self-adjoint truncated Toeplitz operators on triangular Fourier regions; its main trace theorem assumes an essentially bounded inverse symbol. The current singular finite-section multiplier has a kernel even for generic coefficients, and its balanced squared symbol is zero on the torus. Thus that theorem does not imply the claimed pseudoinverse trace, threshold or \(N^{-1}\)-scale crossover. Widom (1989) gives abstract singular-value distribution theory, not this full finite-rank-deficient triangular spectrum at a vanishing symbol. Standard sine diagonalization is acknowledged as prior technique; no novelty is claimed for the general tridiagonal formula. Related research summaries about one-generator bidisk approximation and weighted Fock backward shifts concern different operators, and their full texts were not accessible in this comparison. Unindexed exact formulas remain a risk.

## Value — PASS

The mathematical question is well motivated by conditioning of triangular Toeplitz finite sections of a linear analytic multiplier: an arbitrarily small mismatch of coefficient moduli changes the asymptotic inverse-Schatten growth from a power higher than quadratic to quadratic. The exact squared inverse norm, full singular profile and critical-window scaling distinguish three exponent regimes and locate the singular perturbation scale, providing a concrete benchmark for pseudoinverse regularization on graded two-variable Hardy spaces rather than an arbitrary finite-matrix statistic.

## Scope and residual risks

The proof is limited to linear symbols, fixed exponents, total-degree truncations, and the stated fixed-parameter or \(N^{-1}\) imbalance limits. Possible equivalent historical tridiagonal calculations and unpublished finite-section singular-value formulas may affect priority; independent checking remains necessary.
