The final claim fixes the coordinate-segment convention, the intrinsic Euclidean metric and all quantifiers. For a selected edge forest, the exact determinant identity equates its Gram determinant to the product of component orders, by the matrix-tree theorem. The established intrinsic-volume expansion sums the square-root Gram minors. Multivariate Cayley enumeration counts every pair consisting of a tree and a marked forest exactly once after the ordered-component symmetry factor. This yields the finite formula for all integers \(n\ge r\ge3\). The standalone exact checker passed for all Prüfer trees of orders two through six, all their edge subsets and associated incidence Gram determinants, and every 7-vertex tree at the first new codimension. This checks 294,278 forest-subset observations; computation is not substituted for the infinite argument. No hidden analytic case remains for \(r\ge3\), and singleton components and empty Gram determinants are explicitly included.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limitations

Padrol–Pilaud–Poullot supplies the graphical zonotope and deformation framework, not the Cayley ensemble's Euclidean intrinsic-volume expectation. Schneider's random-zonotope expectation theorem assumes i.i.d. generator segments, whereas edges of a uniform labelled tree are dependent. Berestycki–Pitman's general Gibbs partition and Kolchin representations are explicitly **prior**, and directly explain the general form of the size distribution once the geometric block weights are independently derived; the submission does not claim a new general Kolchin theorem. Hackl–Panholzer–Wagner studies a different random-tree vertex-uncover process. Standard zonotope and Cayley identities alone do not state the derived all-codimension geometric asymptotic constant or the associated volume-tilted component behavior.

A previously obtained codimension-one surface-area expectation is excluded from this claim. The nonoverlapping assertion is the all-fixed-codimensions-at-least-two exact Euclidean formula, its \((\log n)^{r-1}\) hierarchy and the geometric size-spectrum realization. Searches for full statements, intrinsic-volume synonyms, square-root forest weights and logarithmic Gibbs analogues yielded no identified covering statement, but undetected equivalent and stronger Gibbs-assembly formulations remain a material bibliographic risk. Novelty is not inferred solely from unsuccessful searches.
