## correctness

PASS

The translated edge segments are generated by incidence columns in the Euclidean hyperplane. For a size-s component the edge Gram determinant is s (by the nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues, or the s maximal incidence minors of magnitude 1), so the forest contribution is the product of square roots. A fixed forest of component sizes s_i has n^(r−2)∏s_i spanning completions by contracting the components and using Prüfer weights. Multiplying internal Cayley counts, component weights and ordered label-partition counts, then dividing by r! and n^(n−2), gives (1), including the empty forest at r=n. Independently, in the coefficient sum with b_s=c/s+O(s^−2), take h=n/(2r). Terms with at least two sizes above h are O_r((log n)^(r−2)/n): fix the other sizes and sum the two O_r(1/n) factors over at most n choices. With exactly one large size, the small sizes are freely summed up to h and the remaining b_(n−S)=c/n+O_r((S+1)/n²), where S is their sum. Summing the error uses ∑_(s≤h) s b_s=O(n) and ∑_(s≤h) b_s=c log n+O(1). Summing over the r large positions gives the stated coefficient and Stirling gives (2). Restricting small coordinates to independent interior logarithmic intervals gives mass 1/r times their interval-length product; exhaustion gives one giant and the conditional iid-uniform limit. The n=5 check also follows from Prüfer degrees: the expected number of adjacent edge pairs is 18/5, leaving 12/5 disjoint pairs, with Gram square roots √3 and 2 respectively.
