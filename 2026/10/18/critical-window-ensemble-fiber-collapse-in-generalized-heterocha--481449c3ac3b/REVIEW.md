## Correctness — PASS
The exact geometry of every full vertical fiber image is an \(M\)-adic interval, including the wide-branch full-interval reset. This yields the reflected depth process with an explicitly holding zero boundary. A finite-time biased reflection argument and its likelihood-ratio conversion establish each probability, not merely expectations. A lattice local CLT and controlled geometric truncation yield the stated \(n^{-1/2}\) coefficient for each fixed drift in the \(n^{-1/2}\)-parameter window. Brownian invariance and fixed-bias Markov recurrence justify the complementary limits. The standard-library checker confirms finite exact-rational identities and several numerical scale comparisons. Computations are not presented as a proof of infinite-horizon limits.

## Originality — PASS
Primary papers [arXiv:2307.08119v1], [arXiv:2401.00836v1/v2], and [arXiv:2408.16618v1] establish the family, central Lyapunov regime, return-time tail methods, and sharp neutral correlation decay. Their variables are not the length of an entire vertical-fiber image; the 2024 correlation proof uses an absorbing rather than a reflecting boundary and establishes an \(n^{-3/2}\) estimate rather than the present \(n^{-1/2}\) finite-width critical window. The classic reflection principle and Brownian maximum law are explicitly credited. Relevant semantic searches were compared by statement and implication. Publication-priority risk persists from general near-critical reflected-queue literature; a negative search cannot certify absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS
Near the model's intrinsic zero-central-Lyapunov transition, the theorem gives a parameter-resolved law for a natural ensemble observable. In particular it reconciles mean-width collapse with almost-sure repeated re-expansion of every neutral fiber and supplies a nontrivial drift-dependent coefficient across the transition. This differentiates pathwise, ensemble, and correlation behavior, rather than recomputing a published table or a fixed-parameter example.

## Limits
The theorem is restricted to the original two-dimensional full-fiber observable and fixed drift \(\eta\) in a near-critical triangular parameter sequence. It does not control arbitrary subfiber distances or replace established mixing theorems. Independent priority and proof auditing remain outstanding.
