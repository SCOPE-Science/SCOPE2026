# Review of cycle-divisibility rank spectra

## Correctness

PASS. The map is uniquely represented by a block function and a family of within-block functions. Equivariance forces the latter to be constant on cycles; composition-idempotence forces a retract on retained cycles and image containment on omitted cycles. For each admissible retained set, the number of equivariant block maps is controlled by divisibility and phase choices. These independent choices give the exact rank polynomial. The minimum-rank and multiplicity formulas follow by a separate divisibility-poset argument. Direct complete-map enumeration in 15 finite cases verifies all claimed polynomial coefficients and extremal coefficients but is not substituted for the general proof.

## Originality

PASS relative to the checked literature, with explicit residual uncertainty. Dolinka–East (arXiv:1407.3312, Propositions 3.1–3.2) and Al-Aadhami (University of York thesis publicly deposited 2018-02-26, Theorem 4.3.4) enumerate idempotents without a prescribed commuting permutation. Konieczny (2003, Theorem 1) already establishes the cycle-divisibility rule for commuting transformations; no novelty is claimed for that rule alone. The inspected results do not state the full rank-enumerating convolution for their intersection or the exact least-image coefficient. The closest published database result concerns relative generator rank of wreath products, not this centralizer census. The possibility that the combined formula is known in older literature remains a limitation.

## Value

PASS. Idempotents and their centralizers are natural structural sets in finite semigroup theory. This formula refines the classical total idempotent census by symmetry type and by actual image cardinality, and the sharp minimum-rank multiplicity exposes a nontrivial divisibility obstruction: incomparable cycle lengths must survive simultaneously. It is a uniform answer for arbitrary block size and arbitrary block-cycle type, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Scope and limits

The block symmetry preserves within-block coordinates. No general centralizer classification for arbitrary wreath-product units is asserted. The standalone checker is exhaustive only for the listed finite cases. Older same-object results are acknowledged and not relabeled as novel, and originality is assessed only against checked sources.
