# Cycle-rank homology of total three-cut complexes of high-girth graphs

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected simple graph with \(N=|V|\ge6\) and no cycle of length three, four, or five. For a forest, interpret the girth as infinity. Write its cycle rank as \(b=|E|-N+1\). Let \(\Delta_3^t(G)\) denote the total three-cut complex, generated by the complements of independent three-subsets of \(V\). Define its Alexander dual
\[
\Gamma(G)=\{S\subseteq V:\alpha(G[S])\le2\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\Gamma(G)\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{b+1}{2}}S^3.}
\]
A wedge with zero summands means a point. In particular the **integral** reduced homology of the total cut complex is completely determined:
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3^t(G);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\binom{b+1}{2}},&j=N-6,\\
0,&j\ne N-6.
\end{cases}}
\]
If \(b>0\), the total cut complex is pure of dimension \(N-4\) but is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope

Graphs are finite, simple, and connected. The graph may be nonplanar, have vertices of arbitrary degree, or contain overlapping cycles of length at least six. The restriction on girth is essential to the stated face description: four- and five-cycles introduce additional low-dimensional simplices. The theorem does not assert a wedge-of-spheres decomposition for the total cut complex itself, nor an equivariant or canonical identification. The result holds for **every** graph satisfying these hypotheses, rather than just for selected incidence graphs or cycles.

The independence number \(\alpha(G[S])\) is the largest cardinality of an independent vertex subset of the induced graph on \(S\). A face \(F\) belongs to the total cut complex precisely if \(V\setminus F\) contains an independent triple. The displayed \(\Gamma(G)\) is therefore exactly the combinatorial Alexander dual of \(\Delta_3^t(G)\).

## Proof

**1. Description of the dual faces.** The empty face, every singleton, and every pair of vertices lie in \(\Gamma(G)\). A three-subset lies in \(\Gamma(G)\) exactly when it contains at least one graph edge. Since every induced subgraph on four vertices is triangle-free, it is bipartite; by the matching-cover equality for bipartite graphs, a four-subset has independence number at most two exactly when it contains a two-edge matching. Such a matching is unique: two distinct perfect matchings on the same four vertices would form a four-cycle.

No five-subset lies in \(\Gamma(G)\): a triangle-free five-vertex graph with independence number at most two has degree exactly two at each vertex (degree at least three yields three independent neighbors; degree at most one leaves an independent triple), hence is a five-cycle, contrary to the hypothesis. No six-subset belongs either, by the \(R(3,3)=6\) Ramsey theorem and triangle-freeness. Every larger set contains a six-subset, so the only nonempty simplices have one, two, three, or four vertices. Thus \(\Gamma(G)\) is at most three-dimensional.

**2. Trees give a contractible dual.** Suppose \(T\) is a tree, with leaf \(v\) and its neighbor \(u\). We have \(\operatorname{del}_v\Gamma(T)=\Gamma(T-v)\). The link \(\operatorname{lk}_v\Gamma(T)\) is a cone with apex \(u\). Indeed, if a face \(S\) of that link does not contain \(u\), the vertex \(v\) is isolated from \(S\), so \(S\) contains no independent pair and is a clique; hence \(S\cup\{u,v\}\) contains no independent triple, because \(uv\) is an edge and no two vertices of \(S\) are independent. The link is thus contractible. The decomposition into the deletion and the cone on this contractible link proves that \(\Gamma(T-v)\hookrightarrow\Gamma(T)\) is a homotopy equivalence. Repeated leaf removal reaches the one-edge tree, whose dual is a simplex; therefore \(\Gamma(T)\) is contractible.

**3. Adding an edge contributes an explicit number of three-cells.** Let \(H\) be a connected spanning subgraph of \(G\), and let \(e=ab\notin E(H)\) be an edge of \(G\). Since every subgraph of \(G\) has girth at least six, the face description in step 1 applies before and after adding \(e\). Put
\[
L=H[V\setminus\{a,b\}],\qquad
B=(N_H(a)\cup N_H(b))\setminus\{a,b\}.
\]
The two displayed neighborhoods are disjoint (a common neighbor would form a triangle with the new edge). The **only** simplices newly appearing in \(\Gamma(H+e)\setminus\Gamma(H)\) are the two-simplices
\[
\{a,b,w\}\quad(w\in V(L)\setminus B)
\]
and the three-simplices
\[
\{a,b,x,y\}\quad(xy\in E(L)).
\]
The second assertion uses the uniqueness of the two-edge matching on any four vertices: an older perfect matching would create a four-cycle with \(ab\).

Every component of \(L\) meets \(B\), since otherwise it would have no connection to \(a\) or \(b\) in the connected graph \(H\). For each \(w\in V(L)\setminus B\), choose a neighbor \(p(w)\) one step closer to \(B\) in \(L\). Match the newly appearing two-simplex \(\{a,b,w\}\) with the newly appearing three-simplex \(\{a,b,w,p(w)\}\). These are distinct pairs: the distance strictly decreases from \(w\) to \(p(w)\). In any alternating gradient path among the new simplices, the same distance strictly decreases whenever another new two-simplex is visited, so the matching is acyclic. Old simplices remain an untouched subcomplex. Consequently relative discrete Morse theory replaces the inclusion \(\Gamma(H)\subseteq\Gamma(H+e)\) by attachment of three-cells only, one for each unmatched new three-simplex.

The number of these cells is
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E(L)|-|V(L)\setminus B|
&=(|E(H)|-\deg_H(a)-\deg_H(b))\\
&\quad -(N-2-\deg_H(a)-\deg_H(b))\\
&=|E(H)|-N+2=b(H)+1,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(b(H)=|E(H)|-N+1\) because \(H\) is connected.

**4. Induction on cycle rank.** Choose a spanning tree \(T\) and insert the \(b\) remaining edges, one at a time, obtaining connected spanning subgraphs throughout. The dual of \(T\) is contractible by step 2. At the \(i\)-th insertion, the previous cycle rank is \(i-1\), so step 3 adds exactly \(i\) three-cells and no cells in lower dimensions. Inductively the old complex is a wedge of three-spheres (or a point). Every attaching map of a new three-cell is a map from \(S^2\) to such a wedge, and is null-homotopic because that wedge is two-connected. The new complex is therefore a wedge with \(i\) additional three-spheres. Summing \(1+2+\cdots+b\) proves the asserted homotopy equivalence.

**5. Integral duality and shellability.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the \(N\)-element vertex set gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3^t(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(\Gamma(G);\mathbb Z).
\]
A wedge of \(\binom{b+1}{2}\) three-spheres has free reduced integral cohomology of that rank only in degree three. Solving \(N-j-3=3\) yields \(j=N-6\), proving the formula, including integral torsion-freeness. Because every triangle-free graph on at least six vertices contains an independent triple, the total cut complex is nonempty; by definition its facets all have cardinality \(N-3\), so it is pure of dimension \(N-4\). When \(b>0\), the nonzero homology two degrees below the facet dimension contradicts the homology concentration of a pure shellable complex.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` reconstructs the bounded-independence dual from its candidate face description and, independently, directly tests \(\alpha(G[S])\le2\) on bounded-sized examples. For each tested graph, it chooses a spanning tree, adds chords one at a time, enumerates **all** newly appearing faces, constructs the distance-decreasing relative matching, checks distinct matching pairs and acyclicity, and computes reduced mod-two homology of the resulting full dual via exact binary elimination. It covers all 1,356 connected labeled graphs on six vertices with girth at least six, plus nine named cases: a six-vertex path, cycles of lengths six through nine, the three-arm theta graph with each path length three, bouquets of two and three six-cycles, and the Fano-plane incidence (Heawood) graph.

The verifier returns `VERIFY_OK named_cases=9 labeled_six_vertex_graphs=1356` with homology ranks consistent with \(\binom{b+1}{2}\), including the Heawood check \(b=8\) and rank \(36\). These are **finite corroborations**, not substitutes for the all-parameter proof of the matching or the integral conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced total cut complexes, proved contractibility for tree cases (Theorem 4.13), and determined the single-sphere homotopy type for cycles (Theorem 4.15). These are the \(b=0\) and \(b=1\) special cases of the integral homology conclusion, not new results here. Their work does not classify the dual for arbitrary high-girth graphs as a function of the cycle rank.

A subsequent publicly available computation determines the Heawood graph's total three-cut complex itself to be a wedge of 36 eight-spheres; that **stronger finite case** is acknowledged. Heawood has \(N=14\) and \(b=21-14+1=8\), so its known rank \(36\) agrees with the present uniform formula but is not reclaimed as original. Other previously published specific-graph computations cover objects with four- or five-cycles and lie outside the hypotheses.

Carnero Bravo's later paper studies the Alexander-dual bounded-independence complexes and proves a girth-based connectivity bound (Theorem 22, Corollary 23). Those statements give vanishing ranges, not the exact three-dimensional homotopy type or the cycle-rank multiplicity in the theorem above. Its explicit homotopy computations concern cycle powers, complete multipartite graphs, and selected products. The new proof supplies the missing edge-addition matching and all-parameter count.

## Limitations

No homotopy type is asserted for the original total cut complex except what follows from its homology and the previously known special cases. No assertion is made for disconnected graphs, graphs of girth at most five, or cut parameters other than three. Unindexed equivalent statements may exist; bibliographic searches, even together with full-text checks of the directly relevant theorems, cannot establish absolute priority. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
