# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The cyclic recurrence and Plücker reconstruction are inverse on the dense-edge locus. The boundary is controlled by finitely many irreducible two-cut complete intersections; transverse rank-three differential at their general points proves each is in the closure. The sparse submaximal ideal is radical by Deng–Kretschmer, so irreducibility yields primeness. Finite exact checks through order eight support, but do not substitute for, the all-order argument. Ordinary nodes are asserted only generically in characteristic zero.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Deng–Kretschmer establish radicality, Cohen–Macaulayness, degree and the graph connectivity criterion for determinant irreducibility. They pose the missing submaximal-primality statement for 2-connected graphs as Question 16, with computer checks only through size six. The present cycle-family all-order primality and Grassmannian/boundary argument were not implied by those statements. Boocher and Conca–Welker provide relevant antecedents but not an identified all-cycle proof. Residual risk: a broader or subsequent theorem might imply the cycle specialization.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result answers a motivated graph-determinantal primality question for infinitely many graph sizes and supplies a uniform geometric description, rather than merely recovering an enumerative invariant. It does not answer primality for every 2-connected graph.
