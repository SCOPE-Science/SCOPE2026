# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The rooted-forest count is the determinant of the nonroot complete-graph Laplacian minor. Deletion and reattachment of prescribed terminal vertices prove the exact finite conditional factorial moments; inversion yields the conditional mass function. The strict conditional mean sign and negative covariance are proved using a two-copy covariance identity. The sharp asymptotic uses a uniform Taylor estimate, exponentially small core tail, uniform Rayleigh moments and explicit covariance of the Rayleigh limit; the code checks small finite cases only, not the asymptotic theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

Pitman (2001) and Hansen–Jaworski (2006, 2010) provide the underlying cyclic-rooted-forest decomposition. Flajolet–Odlyzko (1990) treat marginals; those results are not claimed new. The assessed claim is the cyclic-core-conditioned terminal count law plus its strict negative regression and sharp mixed covariance coefficient. The read primary material did not state that theorem or a provably dominating statement. A direct derivation from known forest structure might exist in Mylläri (2002) or historical mapping-strata literature, not fully accessible. Prior-art search cannot certify uniqueness or independence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Cyclic points (order \(\sqrt n\)) and terminals (order \(n\)) are fundamental structural statistics of a functional graph. The occupancy identity gives every conditional probability. The exact sign and \(\sqrt n\) covariance constant show how a genuinely negative dependence survives while correlation tends to zero, beyond standard separate marginal limit theorems.

## Limitations

Uniform mappings only; no stochastic dominance between complete conditional laws is proved. Finite enumeration does not prove arbitrary-size or asymptotic statements. Historical priority needs independent investigation, especially the full 2002 forest-leaf paper.
