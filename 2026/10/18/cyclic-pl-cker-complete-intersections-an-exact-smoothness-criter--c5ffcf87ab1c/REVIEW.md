## Correctness — PASS
The scheme-theoretic pullback to rank-two frames is the cycle binomial-edge ideal, whose radicality and prime decomposition are established in the cited primary sources. The rank-two restriction removes the rank-one component. The remaining primes are all of dimension \(n-4\) on the Grassmannian, so the \(n\) Plücker hyperplanes cut a regular sequence. The direct Jacobian rank formula establishes the pointwise criterion in all characteristics; an exact Grassmannian chart gives the normal crossing model. The \(n=5,6\) descriptions are obtained directly from the surviving independent sets and their remaining coordinate runs. The embedded checker returned `VERIFY_OK`, but finite-field tests are not used as an all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Herzog–Hibi–Hreinsdóttir–Kahle–Rauh (arXiv:0909.4717) and Rauh (arXiv:1210.7960) already give the component decomposition, and its cyclic Lucas specialization is credited to their framework. Damadi–Rahmati (DOI:10.3390/math4020037) show that cycles have singular binomial-edge varieties, in their graph-level sense. The proposed incremental result is the all-point Grassmannian smoothness iff criterion, explicit transverse node chart and the geometric \(n=5,6\) realizations; those statements were not located in the inspected relevant material. A different formulation could exist elsewhere, and access to the full publisher page of Damadi–Rahmati was restricted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
These cyclic coordinate sections give a natural uniform sequence of Gorenstein varieties with trivial dualizing sheaf, including a nodal genus-one curve and a reducible K3-type surface. A test involving only neighboring columns determines their entire set-theoretic singular support in every characteristic and supplies a concrete geometric local model. The complete component indexing is prior work, not the value-bearing novelty by itself.

## Limitations and relevant sources
The theorem does not describe the nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, all higher-dimensional completed singular germs or deformation theory. The cited sources are arXiv:0909.4717, arXiv:1210.7960 and DOI:10.3390/math4020037. Earlier graph results must not be conflated with the all-point Grassmannian smoothness criterion. No independent validation is claimed.
