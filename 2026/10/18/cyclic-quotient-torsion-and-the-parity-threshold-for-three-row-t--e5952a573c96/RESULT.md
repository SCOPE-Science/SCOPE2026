# Cyclic quotient torsion and the parity threshold for three-row two-matching complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(X_n=M_2(K_{3,n})\) be the simplicial complex of subsets of the edges of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,n}\) meeting each graph vertex in at most two selected edges. Equivalently, \(X_n=\mathsf{CF}_3(K_3\square K_n)\) is the triangle-free vertex-set complex of the three-row rook graph. Let \(C_n\) act by cyclically shifting its \(n\) columns, and let \(Y_n=|X_n|/C_n\).

**Fixed-point and parity theorem.** A column permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\) has a fixed point on \(|X_n|\) if and only if it has a cycle of length one or two. Thus the column-shift \(C_n\)-action is free **if and only if \(n\) is odd**. Its quotient always has
\[
\pi_1(Y_n)\cong\begin{cases}C_n,&n\text{ odd},\\C_{n/2},&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]

**Complete odd-width integral classification.** For each odd \(n\ge5\), define
\[
B_n=\left(1-n+\binom n2\right)^3+n(2-n)^3+\binom n2,\qquad T_n=\frac{B_n+n-1}{n}.
\]
Then the integral homology of the orbit space is exactly
\[
H_j(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,\\
\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,&j=1,3,\\
\mathbb Z^{T_n},&j=5,\\
0,&j=2,4\text{ or }j>5.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the quotients have genuine torsion in two distinct degrees despite the covering complexes having free integral homology concentrated in dimension five. At \(n=5,7,9\), the top ranks are \(19,361,2101\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The column action does not permute the three row labels. A face is a \(3\times n\) zero-one matrix with row sums at most two and column sums at most two. The complex has dimension at most five. Only the fundamental group is asserted for even widths; no even-width integral homology classification is claimed. The quotient uses the geometric realization, not the combinatorial quotient on vertex orbits.

## Proof
**1. Connectivity and dimension.** Theorem 6.1 of Jojić–Panina–Vrećica–Živaljević gives \((\sum k_i-2)\)-connectivity when \(\sum l_j\ge\sum k_i+3-1\). Here the three row bounds are \(k_i=2\), and the \(n\) column bounds are \(l_j=2\), so the hypothesis \(2n\ge8\) holds for \(n\ge4\). Thus \(X_n\) is four-connected. Since a face has at most six cells, \(\dim X_n\le5\). In particular \(X_n\) is simply connected and its reduced integral homology is free and concentrated, if nonzero, in degree five.

**2. Exact fixed-point criterion.** A finite simplicial automorphism has a geometric fixed point precisely when it setwise preserves a nonempty face: one direction takes the barycenter of the preserved face, and the other takes the support of the invariant barycentric coordinates. For a permutation of columns alone, the orbit of a vertex \((i,j)\) has the length of the column cycle containing \(j\). If a nonempty face is invariant, it contains one such orbit, and the capacity two of row \(i\) forces the cycle length to be at most two. Conversely a cycle of length one gives a fixed vertex, and a cycle of length two gives a setwise preserved pair in any one row; both are faces. For a cyclic shift of even order, the half-turn has only two-cycles, hence fixes a point. For odd \(n\), every nonidentity shift has cycles of odd length at least three, so every nonidentity group element acts without fixed points. This proves the free-action threshold.

**3. Fundamental group, including even widths.** The finite simplicial action is discontinuous in Armstrong's sense. Armstrong's orbit-space theorem says that, for a simply connected polyhedron, \(\pi_1(|X_n|/C_n)\) is \(C_n\) modulo the subgroup generated by elements having fixed points. The fixed-point criterion shows this subgroup is trivial when \(n\) is odd and is generated by the unique half-turn when \(n\) is even. This gives the asserted two cases.

**4. Euler characteristic of the covering complex.** The reduced Euler computation is the signed count \(\sum_{F\in X_n}(-1)^{|F|}\), including the empty face. If one first enforces only the row bounds, the signed sum factors into \(\big(1-n+\binom n2\big)^3\). Exclude the events that a column contains all three selected cells. For one prescribed full column, the signed contribution from the remaining cells is \(-(2-n)^3\), yielding the inclusion-exclusion correction \(+n(2-n)^3\). For two prescribed full columns, the only possible face consists of those six cells and contributes \(+\binom n2\); three full columns are impossible. Therefore the signed sum is exactly \(B_n\). Because \(X_n\) is four-connected and five-dimensional, \(\widetilde H_5(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{B_n}\) and \(\chi(X_n)=1-B_n\).

**5. Integral quotient homology for odd widths.** For odd \(n\ge5\), the free action makes \(X_n\to Y_n\) a regular covering with deck group \(C_n\). As \(X_n\) is simply connected, the quotient has classifying map \(Y_n\to BC_n\) with four-connected homotopy fiber \(X_n\). The integral homology Serre (or Cartan–Leray) spectral sequence shows \(H_j(Y_n;\mathbb Z)\cong H_j(C_n;\mathbb Z)\) for \(j\le4\): namely \(\mathbb Z\) in degree zero, \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) in degrees one and three, and zero in degrees two and four. The quotient has a five-dimensional CW structure, so \(H_5(Y_n;\mathbb Z)=\ker(\partial_5)\) is a free abelian group. As the free cellular action has \(n\) cells in every nonempty orbit, \(\chi(Y_n)=\chi(X_n)/n=(1-B_n)/n\). Comparing with its already established lower homology ranks gives \(\operatorname{rank}H_5(Y_n)=(B_n+n-1)/n=T_n\). There are no groups above degree five. This proves the classification integrally.

## Verification
The supplied dependency-free script `verification/verify.py` independently generates every allowed face, checks the fixed-point obstruction for widths five and seven and half-turn witnesses for even widths four, six and eight, and recomputes the signed Euler count. For width five it constructs all quotient cellular boundary matrices with exact orientation signs and verifies that their integer compositions vanish. Matrix ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_7\) recover the predicted integral-homology consequences, including the universal coefficient theorem's extra mod-five classes in degrees two and four. Those finite checks do not prove the infinite-width result, which follows from the steps above.

## Relationship to prior work
The connectivity theorem of Jojić et al. is used as an established result, not claimed as new. Reiner and Roberts' Theorem 3.3 already describes the rational row-and-column symmetry representations of all multiple chessboard complexes abstractly; this work does not claim a new representation or a new nonequivariant sphere wedge. Singh studies other high-capacity matching-complex regimes. Ghosh–Selvaraja's 2026 clique-free framework motivates considering this rook-graph realization. The present assertion concerns the topology and **integral torsion of geometric cyclic orbit spaces**, including an exact elementwise fixed-point criterion and a sharp parity transition; none of the inspected results states this quotient classification. Armstrong's general orbit-space fundamental-group theorem and standard covering homology are explicitly acknowledged rather than presented as original techniques.

## Limitations
The result applies only to the three-row, capacity-two complete-bipartite family and the specified column-rotation action. For even widths, fixed points invalidate the free-cover homology computation; only their fundamental groups are classified. The conclusion neither classifies the full equivariant homotopy type nor the higher homotopy groups of the quotient. An older unindexed specialization or a general equivariant quotient result could subsume it; independent scientific audit is not available.
