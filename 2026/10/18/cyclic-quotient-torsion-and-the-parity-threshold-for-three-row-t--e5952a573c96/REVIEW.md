# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The capacity-two orbit criterion proves precisely which column permutations have geometric fixed points. The 2020 generalized-chessboard connectivity theorem applies for every \(n\ge4\), giving a four-connected complex of dimension five. The signed Euler count is an exact two-term inclusion-exclusion correction, and Armstrong's orbit-space theorem identifies the even and odd fundamental groups. For odd \(n\), the classifying-space spectral sequence and top-dimensional cellular freeness give the full integral homology. A degree-by-degree finite quotient cellular calculation at width five confirms the torsion pattern, including its universal-coefficient signatures.

## Originality — PASS, with reservations
The strongest predecessor, Reiner and Roberts (2000, Theorem 3.3), already describes the complete rational homology representation of the **covering multiple chessboard complex**. Jojić et al. (2020) provide the required connectivity. Neither theorem, nor the published small examples at widths four and five, gives the asserted integral homology of the geometric cyclic **quotient** or its free-action parity boundary. Generic orbit-space and covering theorems are acknowledged in the proof; consequently the result is an explicit, fairly short specialization of powerful established tools. The possibility of an unindexed earlier quotient calculation is substantial and remains open.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The cyclic quotient is canonical under column relabeling. Its integral torsion in both first and third homology cannot be read from the covering complex's Betti numbers or a rational character alone. The sharp odd/even freeness transition and the complete all-degree integral quotient homology give an infinite, naturally parameterized family of lens-space-like torsion examples and a reproducible benchmark for equivariant matching complexes. No general new covering theorem or broad implication is claimed.

## Scientific limitations
The even-width quotient is only classified at the level of its fundamental group. The result depends on existing chessboard connectivity and orbit-space theory; its originality is in the explicit combined quotient classification, not in either general technique. An independent comparison against the full historical literature remains warranted.
