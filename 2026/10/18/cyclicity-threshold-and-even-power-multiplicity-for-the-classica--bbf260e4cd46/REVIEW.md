# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The semigroup estimate and resolvent relation in the reference imply an exact operator-valued power moment formula. An annihilating dual functional for even powers has all odd moments of an exponentially decaying function equal to zero. The analytic hyperbolic-sine transform and uniqueness of the Fourier transform of an odd \(L^1\) extension force the function to vanish, so \(1\) is cyclic for \(C^2\) for \(p\ge2\); dense Hardy-space embeddings handle \(0<p<2\). For higher powers, gamma-product coefficient functionals are bounded on each \(H^p\), have exact Cesàro dual eigenvalues, and form Vandermonde-independent families of common eigenfunctionals after powering. This proves the lower multiplicity bound, while the even-power cyclicity proves its sharpness when the exponent is even.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Belli, Gül, Ross, and Siskakis (arXiv:2609.15911v1) prove cyclicity of the original operator and supply the semigroup premise. Their complete accessible paper does not establish cyclicity or multicyclicity of its powers. Gallardo-Gutiérrez, Partington, and Ross (arXiv:2307.06923) study invariant subspaces of the unpowered operator; the related real-variable paper (arXiv:2606.08600v1) has a different domain. Actual literature and mathematical-record searches found no statement that implies the complete Hardy-space power dichotomy and exact even-power multiplicity. The older Kriete–Trutt multiplication model could subsume an \(H^2\) special case, and unindexed equivalent results remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This answers the natural power-stability question raised by a new cyclicity theorem. The surprising coexistence of a cyclic square and noncyclic cube is established over the entire Hardy scale, including \(0<p<1\). The even-power theorem gives an exact minimal number of generators growing linearly, rather than just a negative cyclicity result. The exact odd-power multiplicities are not claimed.
