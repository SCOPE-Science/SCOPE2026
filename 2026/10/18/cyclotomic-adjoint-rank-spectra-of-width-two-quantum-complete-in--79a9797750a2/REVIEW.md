## Originality (O): PASS, residual risk
Bergh--Erdmann (arXiv:0709.3029v1) compute codimension-two Hochschild (co)homology and already explain degree-zero center information. Bergh--Oppermann (arXiv:0710.2606v1) establish representation-dimension results. Oppermann (Algebra & Number Theory 4 (2010), Theorem 3.4) gives a graded Hochschild basis. Those checked statements do not themselves determine the complete elementwise distribution of linear ranks of inner derivations or its finite-field commuting-pair polynomial. The earlier exponent-two result on commuting tuples covers \(n=2\), which is **not** part of the new claim. Existing exact commutator counts for Kronecker path algebras and adjoint ranks in selected Lie algebras do not imply the stated finite quantum-complete-intersection formula. Search and inspection cannot rule out equivalent unlocated calculations using Ore extensions or commutator-word maps, especially specialized computations of algebra commuting degrees. No absolute uniqueness claim is made.

## Value (V): PASS
The complete centralizer-size distribution is a natural finite-algebra invariant refining the center and the aggregate number of commuting pairs. The formula holds for every algebra length \(n\ge3\) and every finite field, detects twist resonance precisely through multiplicative order, and admits a particularly simple all-length nonresonant classification. The exact order-two versus nonresonant discrepancy already in dimension six is a motivated structural boundary, not an arbitrary numeric slice. These facts are potentially useful when distinguishing twisted algebras through the distribution of their internal commutator maps.
