# Cyclotomic automorphism resonances in the six-dimensional asymmetric quantum complete intersection

## Finding

For any field \(k\) and any \(\lambda\in k^\times\), let
\[
A_\lambda=k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^2,y^3,yx-\lambda xy).
\]
The complete group of \(k\)-algebra automorphisms is parametrized by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x&\longmapsto a x+cxy+d y^2+e xy^2,\\
 y&\longmapsto f x+g y+hxy+i y^2+jxy^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(a,g\in k^\times\), \(c,e,h,j\in k\) are unrestricted, and the **only** restrictions on the other three parameters are
\[
 (1+\lambda^2)d=0,\qquad
 (1+\lambda+\lambda^2)f=0,\qquad
 (\lambda-1)i=0.
\]
Every choice of parameters satisfying these conditions gives a distinct automorphism. Thus, writing
\[
\varepsilon_2=\mathbf1_{1+\lambda^2=0},\quad
\varepsilon_3=\mathbf1_{1+\lambda+\lambda^2=0},\quad
\varepsilon_1=\mathbf1_{\lambda=1},
\]
the reduced automorphism parameter variety is isomorphic, **as a variety, not necessarily as a group**, to
\[
 (\mathbb G_m)^2\times\mathbb A^{4+\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3}.
\]
For every finite field \(k=\mathbb F_Q\), this gives the exact group order
\[
\boxed{\lvert\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_Q}(A_\lambda)\rvert
=(Q-1)^2Q^{4+\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3}.}
\]
Away from the three specified resonance equations the reduced automorphism variety has dimension \(6\); a single resonance raises the dimension to \(7\). When \(\lambda=1\) in characteristic \(2\) or \(3\), two conditions resonate simultaneously and the dimension is \(8\). In particular, noncommutative parameters with primitive third- or fourth-root behavior admit distinct extra generator-mixing coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope

The algebra is unital and associative, with automorphisms required to fix \(k\) pointwise. The parameter \(\lambda\) is nonzero. The statement holds in all characteristics, including characteristic \(0\). A finite-field specialization requires \(Q\) to be a prime power and \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_Q^\times\). The reduced parameter variety refers to the reduced algebraic variety underlying the automorphism equations; no smoothness or reducedness assertion is made about the full automorphism **scheme**. The displayed product with affine space is a variety isomorphism, not a claimed direct-product law for automorphism composition.

The six monomials \(1,x,y,xy,y^2,xy^2\) form a basis. With \(\mathfrak m=(x,y)\), the quotient \(A_\lambda/\mathfrak m\cong k\) shows this is a local algebra and \(\mathfrak m^4=0\).

## Proof

First, moving each \(y\) past each \(x\) by \(yx=\lambda xy\) gives the six-element normal basis and the multiplication rule
\[
 (x^u y^v)(x^{u'}y^{v'})=\lambda^{vu'}x^{u+u'}y^{v+v'},
\]
with the product interpreted as zero if \(u+u'\ge2\) or \(v+v'\ge3\). Distinct basis monomials are independent by the standard reduction of this skew-truncated polynomial algebra, or directly by constructing its six-dimensional regular representation.

Any automorphism preserves the unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\); hence its images are
\[
 X=a x+b y+cxy+d y^2+e xy^2,\quad
 Y=f x+g y+hxy+i y^2+jxy^2.
\]
The coefficient of \(y^2\) in \(X^2\) is \(b^2\), which must vanish. Since \(k\) is a field, \(b=0\). The action on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is therefore triangular with diagonal coefficients \(a,g\), so any automorphism requires \(ag\ne0\).

With \(b=0\), direct multiplication in the basis gives all the potentially nonzero defining-relation residues:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 X^2&=a d(1+\lambda^2)xy^2,\\
 Y^3&=f g^2(1+\lambda+\lambda^2)xy^2,\\
 YX-\lambda XY&=(\lambda-1)
 [a i\lambda-d f(1+\lambda+\lambda^2)]xy^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two equations force \((1+\lambda^2)d=0\) and \((1+\lambda+\lambda^2)f=0\), because \(a,g\ne0\). Substituting the second identity into the third eliminates the \(df\) term; since \(a\lambda\ne0\), the third is then equivalent to \((\lambda-1)i=0\). This proves necessity **and** shows that those three independent conditions suffice for all defining relations. There are no further relation coefficients because every term of total degree at least four vanishes.

Conversely, given parameters satisfying the three equations and \(ag\ne0\), the universal property of the presentation gives a \(k\)-algebra endomorphism. Its action on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is invertible. Since the associated graded algebra is generated by this degree-one quotient, the induced graded endomorphism is surjective degree by degree. The radical filtration is finite, so the original endomorphism is surjective and hence bijective on the six-dimensional \(k\)-vector space. Thus it is an automorphism.

The free parameters are the four coordinates \(c,e,h,j\), two nonzero coordinates \(a,g\), and exactly one further free coordinate for each vanishing scalar among \(1+\lambda^2\), \(1+\lambda+\lambda^2\), and \(\lambda-1\). This gives the stated reduced parameter variety and its finite-field point count. The two simultaneous-resonance possibilities are precisely \(\lambda=1\) in characteristics \(2\) and \(3\): substitution into the scalar conditions verifies them, while any other simultaneous vanishing of the first two would force \(\lambda=0\), contrary to the hypotheses.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` implements multiplication of the six monomials directly over prime fields and \(\mathbb F_4\). With \(a=g=1\), it exhaustively visits every assignment of the seven other image coefficients, checks all three defining relations by independent multiplication, and verifies the count \(Q^{4+\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3}\). Rescaling the two generators by nonzero scalars accounts for the factor \((Q-1)^2\). The script also checks invertibility of sampled resulting six-by-six transformation matrices by finite-field elimination.

Tested cases include: \(\mathbb F_2\) at \(\lambda=1\): \(64\) automorphisms; \(\mathbb F_3\) at \(1\) and \(2\): \(2916\) and \(324\); \(\mathbb F_4\) at \(1\) and a primitive cubic root: \(36864\) and \(9216\); \(\mathbb F_5\) at \(1,2,4\): \(50000,50000,10000\). A primitive third-root parameter over \(\mathbb F_7\) yields \(605052\) automorphisms. These finite checks are corroboration, not the proof over arbitrary fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Bergh and Erdmann (2007) treat codimension-two quantum complete intersections with arbitrary truncation exponents, give the monomial basis and Frobenius/Hochschild framework, and mention scaling and Nakayama automorphisms. Their homology/cohomology results do not state this full six-dimensional automorphism normal form or the three scalar resonance conditions. Bergh and Oppermann (2007) establish bounds on representation dimension for quantum complete intersections; their theorems do not imply automorphism counts.

Duplij and Sinel’shchikov (2010, Proposition 1) recall that automorphisms of the **untruncated** generic complex quantum plane are diagonal scalings. The absence of nilpotency relations there excludes the additional nonlinear and root-resonant automorphisms appearing in the present finite-dimensional quotient.

The automorphism-group theory for commutative zero-dimensional monomial algebras in characteristic zero covers the **commutative** slice \(\lambda=1\) in that characteristic; this established special case is not asserted to be separately novel. Earlier treatments of the smallest four-dimensional quantum complete intersections concern relations \(x^2=y^2=0\) and therefore do not imply the cubic-truncation normal form or the primitive cubic/fourth-root strata. Likewise, truncated coordinate-cross algebras impose vanishing mixed products, rather than \(yx=\lambda xy\) with nonzero \(\lambda\). The present complete noncommutative resonance classification and characteristic-dependent finite-field order formula were not found in the inspected literature. This is a limited literature comparison, not an absolute claim of uniqueness.

## Limitations

No group-theoretic semidirect-product decomposition or full group scheme structure is claimed. In particular, the reduced variety classification does not establish scheme-theoretic reducedness. The formula concerns the fixed \((2,3)\) truncation, not arbitrary quantum complete intersections. Some specialized literature might contain an equivalent or stronger classification that was not located; independent checking remains outstanding.
