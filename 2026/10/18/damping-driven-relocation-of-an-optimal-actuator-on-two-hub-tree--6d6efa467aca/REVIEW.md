# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The objective is one half the diagonal of a positive-definite Laplacian resolvent by an exact semigroup integral. A leaf-versus-parent Sherman–Morrison argument strictly excludes every internal vertex. A three-by-three Schur complement reconstructs both remaining leaf-orbit diagonal entries. Their exact rational difference has strictly positive denominator and numerator \((m-2)\alpha^2+(m^2-4)\alpha-2\), which gives every case of the optimal placement classification and the unique critical root. Direct root rationalization gives the asymptotic law. The independent-inversion checks in the included script verify 406 symbolic and exact-matrix identities; this sampling is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 2013 Summers–Lygeros arXiv paper proves a general modular trace-of-Gramian placement theorem, not the location-switch threshold for a damped Laplacian on the specified two-hub tree. The 1997 Kirkland–Neumann–Shader article, inspected in open-access full text, covers Laplacian **group inverses** and the location of their extremal diagonal entries, not the positive-parameter shifted inverses used here. The zero-damping endpoint and general actuator-set modularity are therefore treated as known. The explicit family-level all-damping classification and asymptotic switching scale are not direct restatements of these results, and literature and research-record searches did not identify exact implication coverage. Specialized resolvent-centrality results or unindexed network-control examples could contain an equivalent threshold; absence of a search hit cannot establish absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

A choice among physically inequivalent actuator sites changes with uniform leakage, despite fixed edge weights and fixed input magnitude. The infinite tree family exhibits a sharp qualitative migration from many symmetry-equivalent terminal actuators to one unique central terminal actuator, including an exact small-branch exception and a dimension-dependent asymptotic scale. This answers a motivated control-placement question left unspecified by the general modularity theorem, rather than merely renaming a matrix identity.

## Limitations and follow-up verification

The actuator optimizes total impulse-response energy, not the rank or smallest eigenvalue of the Gramian, and may not control every state. The result is limited to this tree family and scalar uniform damping. Independent review of the proof and a broader specialist literature search remain appropriate.
