# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The order-theoretic proof reconstructs all predecessor sets by initial segments of the strict-successor-count ordering, an operation made unambiguous by the nested strict successor sets of interval orders. The dual nested-neighborhood property proves that the degree-pair cells are actual automorphism orbits. The rank-two counting formulas detect each pair multiplicity. A constructive terminal-suffix interval order realizes every staircase degree list, whose Ferrers-path count is Catalan. The row/column-margin fiber formula follows from the standard *unique* Fishburn-matrix encoding, with strictly positive margins; the two explicit four-point matrices show the first one-sided failure. Two different enumeration approaches checked all Fishburn matrices through nine elements and all topologically labeled interval orders through six. The infinity claim rests on proofs rather than these finite tests.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The primary published structural papers establish nested strict neighborhoods, indistinguishable elements and Fishburn-matrix encodings, but the inspected full texts do not present the integrated sharp rank-two identification of arbitrary finite interval orders plus a Catalan classification and a precise coefficient formula for every one-sided degree fiber. A contemporaneous paper on generating specialized classes of interval orders was read only at abstract level; its possible overlap with individual enumerative components is a specific residual risk, not a basis for asserting whole-document noncoverage. Accessible research-result records were also compared by implications, not title matching. This is same-model best-knowledge originality assessment and not an independent publication-priority certification.

## Value — PASS

The strict order on an interval family is a natural setting for comparing the information recovered by one-sided and two-sided local counts. The theorem gives an explicit isomorphism reconstruction and orbit partition for arbitrary sizes, a sharp finite-variable logical threshold, and a complete fiber formula quantifying exactly what the one-sided statistic loses, including the first collision. It is a complete invariant comparison for a classical infinite family, not a small parameter substitution. Parts of its proof invoke classical structural facts, so its significance lies in their combined quantitative information-theoretic and logical consequences, not in rediscovery of the Fishburn bijection.

## Limitations and closely related material

The theorem is relative to strict full-order relations and to finite interval orders. It says nothing about two-round directed Hasse-diagram signatures, about arbitrary posets with the same degree pair profiles, or about labeled-order counts. Explicit two-point examples witness the necessity of counting rank two only in this within-class setting. The references in RESULT.md distinguish inspected full texts from abstract-only material; residual priority and specialized-enumeration overlap are not excluded.
