# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete algebraic chain is complete for every odd prime and every positive block length. Lucas congruences yield digitwise factorization; the three-term Legendre recurrence excludes adjacent zero digits and endpoint zeroes. A carry analysis at the first changed digit yields the exact common-divisor test, and each complete base-prime block is counted without approximation. The polynomial differential equation and its involutive symmetry show that every prime congruent to three modulo four has a zero digit. Arbitrary-index density and the divergence of neighboring gcd values follow. A self-contained finite checker independently tests small cases but is not used as the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest primary texts are Sun, arXiv:1009.2486 (Delannoy sums), Sun, arXiv:1602.00574 (polynomial identities and supercongruences), Lengyel, *Integers* 21 (2021), A86 (special-index \(p\)-adic differences), and Rowland–Yassawi, arXiv:1310.8635 (general automatic congruences). The inspected theorems and targeted text searches do not state the exact count of indices at which an odd prime does not divide the gcd of consecutive Delannoy values, or density-one gcd divergence. General Lucas congruences are acknowledged antecedents: the claim is their prime-specific combination with a no-adjacent-roots recurrence, exact digit count, and a root at every prime congruent to three modulo four. OEIS A001850 and the accessible earlier Delannoy modulo-two period result concern different statistics. The residual risk is an unindexed equivalent derivation or stronger covering correlation theorem; this is not independent publication-level originality certification.

## Value — PASS

A natural classical lattice-path sequence displays a marked contrast to recurrence sequences whose consecutive terms have gcd one. The result gives an exact, all-length local formula and the global structural fact that neighboring gcd values exceed any prescribed bound outside a density-zero set. The infinitude of relevant primes and a genuinely computed natural-density exponent make the arithmetic phenomenon nontrivial; no arbitrary finite cutoff is selected.

## Scientific limitations

The local root counts are not explicitly solved for all primes, higher prime-adic valuations are not determined, and natural-density growth is not pointwise growth. Novelty still admits the stated literature risk; no independent audit or validation has been performed.
