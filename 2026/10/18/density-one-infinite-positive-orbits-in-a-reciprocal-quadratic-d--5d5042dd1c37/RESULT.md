# Density-one infinite positive orbits in a reciprocal quadratic divisibility family

## Finding
For each integer \(k\ge1\), define \(Q_k(T)=T^2-kT+1\). A reciprocal divisibility pair is a pair of integers \(x,y\ge2\) satisfying
\[
x\mid Q_k(y),\qquad y\mid Q_k(x).
\]
The following two explicit infinite constructions establish a global arithmetic result about the parameter \(k\).

**Theorem.** (i) For every integer \(k\ge10\) for which \(k+2\) is composite, there are infinitely many distinct reciprocal divisibility pairs of consecutive, strictly increasing, coprime positive integers. Write \(a\) for the least prime divisor of \(k+2\) and \(c=(k+2)/a\). Put
\[
H=(a-1)(c-1)-3,\quad u_0=a,\quad u_1=a(k-a)-1,\quad
u_{n+1}=H u_n-u_{n-1}+k\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Then \(H\ge2\) and every \((u_n,u_{n+1})\), \(n\ge0\), is such a pair. The exceptional parabolic case \(H=2\) occurs exactly when \(k=10\), and then
\[
u_n=5n^2+8n+2\quad(n\ge0).
\]
For every other parameter in (i), \(H\ge3\) and the sequence grows exponentially with positive rate determined by the larger root of \(X^2-HX+1\).

(ii) For every integer \(t\ge0\), the parameter \(k=41+21t\) also has infinitely many such reciprocal divisibility pairs. They are generated by
\[
u_0=7,\quad u_1=79+49t,\quad u_{n+1}=(5+4t)u_n-u_{n-1}+(41+21t)\quad(n\ge1).
\]
This includes infinitely many parameters for which \(k+2\) is prime (by Dirichlet's theorem on primes in the progression \(43\bmod21\)), so the compositeness hypothesis in (i) is sufficient but not necessary.

Consequently, the set of positive integer parameters \(k\) admitting infinitely many positive reciprocal divisibility pairs has natural density one. More quantitatively, if \(E(X)\) counts integers \(1\le k\le X\) not known to be solvable by (i) or (ii), then
\[
E(X)\le\pi(X+2)-\pi(X+2;21,1)+O(1)
=\left(\frac{11}{12}+o(1)\right)\frac{X}{\log X},
\]
where \(\pi(z;21,1)\) counts primes \(p\le z\) with \(p\equiv1\pmod{21}\). The bound on \(E(X)\) is an upper bound on uncovered parameters, **not** a classification of the remaining prime-shift parameters.

## Assumptions and scope
All divisibility relations are in \(\mathbb Z\). Consecutive pairs of the sequences are ordered positive integers with both coordinates at least two; no primality hypothesis is imposed on the coordinates. Parameters satisfy the exact ranges stated above. The density assertion concerns the set of parameters, not the density of solution pairs for a fixed parameter. The prime-counting asymptotic uses the standard prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions; the infinite sequences are unconditional elementary constructions.

## Proof
**1. A quadratic conic orbit lemma.** Fix integers \(k,d,a,b,H\) with \(k,d\ge1\), \(2\le a<b\), \(H\ge2\), and
\[
Q_k(a)=-db,\qquad b=Ha+d+k.
\]
Set \(u_{-1}=-d\), \(u_0=a\), \(u_1=b\) and extend by \(u_{n+1}=Hu_n-u_{n-1}+k\) for \(n\ge1\). Define
\[
F(X,Y)=X^2+Y^2-k(X+Y)+1-HXY.
\]
The two seed equations give \(F(-d,a)=0\). Direct expansion shows the Vieta transformation
\[
(X,Y)\longmapsto(Y,HY-X+k)
\]
preserves the equation \(F=0\): for fixed \(Y\), it exchanges the two roots in \(X\) of \(F(X,Y)=0\). Hence \(F(u_{n-1},u_n)=0\) for all \(n\ge0\). Rearranging yields the exact identity
\[
Q_k(u_n)=u_{n-1}(Hu_n-u_{n-1}+k)=u_{n-1}u_{n+1}\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Since \(u_1>u_0\) and \(H\ge2\), induction using
\[
u_{n+1}-u_n=(H-1)u_n-u_{n-1}+k\ge(u_n-u_{n-1})+k>0
\]
shows strict growth for nonnegative indices. For each \(n\ge0\), apply the displayed factorization at \(n\) and \(n+1\) to get
\[
u_{n+1}\mid Q_k(u_n),\qquad u_n\mid Q_k(u_{n+1}).
\]
Moreover, if \(g=\gcd(u_n,u_{n+1})\), then \(g\mid u_n\) and \(g\mid Q_k(u_n)\), but \(Q_k(u_n)\equiv1\pmod{u_n}\); thus \(g=1\). Strict growth gives infinitely many different pairs.

**2. Parameters with composite \(k+2\).** Let \(k\ge10\) and \(k+2=ac\) with \(a\) its least prime divisor, so \(2\le a\le c\). Put \(d=1\), \(b=a(k-a)-1\), and \(H=k-a-c=(a-1)(c-1)-3\). Then
\[
Q_k(a)=a^2-ka+1=-b,\qquad b=Ha+1+k.
\]
If \(a=2\), then \(c\ge6\), so \(H=c-4\ge2\), with equality only at \((a,c,k)=(2,6,10)\). If \(a\ge3\), the only case \(a=c=3\) gives \(k=7\) and is excluded, whence \(c\ge4\) and \(H\ge(3-1)(4-1)-3=3\). Since \(b=Ha+k+1>a\), the lemma applies. For \(k=10\), the recurrence is \(u_{n+1}=2u_n-u_{n-1}+10\) with \(u_0=2,u_1=15\), yielding \(u_n=5n^2+8n+2\) by second differences. If \(H\ge3\), the shift \(v_n=u_n+k/(H-2)\) satisfies \(v_{n+1}=Hv_n-v_{n-1}\), and its increasing positive initial terms imply \(v_n\sim C\lambda_H^n\) with \(C>0\) and \(\lambda_H=(H+\sqrt{H^2-4})/2>1\). This proves (i).

**3. A prime-shift progression.** For \(t\ge0\), take \(k=41+21t\), \(d=3\), \(a=7\), \(b=79+49t\), \(H=5+4t\). Then
\[
Q_k(7)=50-7k=-3(79+49t)=-db,
\]
and
\[
Ha+d+k=7(5+4t)+3+(41+21t)=79+49t=b.
\]
The lemma gives (ii). In particular \(k=41\) gives the initial solution \((7,79)\), with next orbit term \(429\). Since \(k+2=43+21t\), the prime progression theorem guarantees infinitely many values of \(t\) with prime \(k+2\). The converse to (i) is therefore false.

**4. Quantitative density.** The only parameters not covered by (i) lie either below \(10\) or have prime \(k+2\). The progression in (ii) covers every sufficiently large prime \(k+2\equiv1\pmod{21}\). Thus the uncovered count is at most \(\pi(X+2)-\pi(X+2;21,1)+O(1)\). The prime number theorem and its arithmetic-progression form, using \(\varphi(21)=12\), yield the asymptotic upper bound. In particular the exceptional set is \(o(X)\), proving density one.

## Verification
The provided standalone exact-integer program `verify.py` checks all seed identities, conic equations, strict growth, coprimality and the two divisibilities along thousands of explicitly constructed orbits. It covers every composite \(k+2\) with \(10\le k\le4000\), plus \(301\) progression parameters and multiple subsequent orbit terms. Its successful output is `VERIFY_OK composite_steps=103350 prime_progression_steps=7224 seeds=3445+301`. The finite tests are checks only; the preceding algebraic argument proves the universal claims, and the density asymptotic uses a standard prime-distribution theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Alekseyev and Khomovsky, *Congruence Jumping in Polynomial Divisibility Systems: Arithmetic Structure and Solution Sets* (publicly submitted 2026-09-30, arXiv:2610.00808, primary MSC 11D79), develop general reciprocal polynomial jumps and explicitly derive the classical fixed-conic Vieta mechanism for monic quadratic divisibility systems. Their Section 4 (especially the fixed-conic formulation) and Section 7.1 (historical quadratic chains) are the relevant antecedents for the orbit lemma; the lemma and recurrence technique are not claimed as new. W. H. Mills's 1954 work is an earlier antecedent of quadratic divisibility chains. The present theorem gives explicit *parameter-wide* arithmetic coverage of the negative-linear-coefficient family, a density-one result, a prime-shift congruence branch, and a sharp parabolic transition; these implications were not identified in the materially inspected source sections or accessible indexed comparisons. This absence is not proof of priority.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify every integer \(k\) or every pair in a fixed \(\mathcal S_k\). A prime value of \(k+2\) outside the displayed progression is not an obstruction. The formal parameter-density result does not imply natural density of solution pairs. The construction may be an implicit consequence of prior general conic arithmetic or of unindexed earlier work; the original 1954 paper's complete proof text was not materially examined. There is no independent audit of the result.
