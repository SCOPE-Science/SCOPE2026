# Depth-one expressive completeness for two epistemic team conditionals and the flatness boundary of a third

## Finding
For every finite propositional vocabulary \(N\) (including \(N=\varnothing\)), let \(V=\{0,1\}^N\) be its valuation set and let teams be arbitrary subsets \(T\subseteq V\). Enrich flat classical propositional team logic, with conjunction and lax splitting disjunction, separately by each of the three weak epistemic conditionals introduced by Barbero and Yang (2026): epistemic indicative \(\circ\!\to\), epistemic conditional \(\bullet\!\to\), and epistemic counterfactual \(\rightsquigarrow\).

**Theorem (expressive trichotomy and one-step normal form).** For each fixed \(N\), each of the first two enriched languages defines exactly the families \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal P(V)\) of teams satisfying
\[
\varnothing\in\mathcal P,\qquad (S,T\in\mathcal P\Rightarrow S\cup T\in\mathcal P).
\]
Every such family has, in either language, a defining formula with **conditional nesting depth at most one**, and every occurrence of the conditional has a flat classical antecedent and the flat classical consequent \(\bot\). For \(|N|\ge1\), this nesting-depth bound is sharp: some such properties are not definable at depth zero. In contrast, the language with only \(\rightsquigarrow\) defines **exactly the flat team properties** (properties of the form \(\{T:T\subseteq S\}\) for some \(S\subseteq V\)), even with unrestricted nesting depth.

Thus the two expressively complete extensions and the flat extension differ despite their shared preservation properties. The claim is about the precisely defined languages below, not first-order quantification, strong contradictory negation of arbitrary team formulas, or strict splitting disjunction.

## Assumptions and scope
Classical formulas \(\alpha\) are generated by propositional atoms, the constants \(\bot,\top\), Boolean conjunction, and negation/disjunction *within the classical sublanguage*. Their team semantics is flat: \(T\models\alpha\) if and only if each \(v\in T\) classically satisfies \(\alpha\). In particular, \(T\models\bot\) exactly when \(T=\varnothing\). For a chosen conditional \(\star\), the extended formulas are generated from these classical formulas by \(\land\), lax splitting disjunction \(\otimes\), and \(\star\). By definition, \(T\models A\otimes B\) if and only if there are \(T_1,T_2\subseteq T\) with \(T=T_1\cup T_2\), \(T_1\models A\), and \(T_2\models B\); overlap is allowed. Full contradictory negation of nonflat formulas is not a formation rule.

Write \(D_A(T)\) for \(\forall U\subseteq T\;(U\models A)\). The three published semantic clauses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
T\models A\circ\!\to B&\iff(D_A(T)\Rightarrow T\models B),\\
T\models A\bullet\!\to B&\iff(D_A(T)\Rightarrow D_B(T)),\\
T\models A\rightsquigarrow B&\iff\forall S\subseteq T\;(D_A(S)\Rightarrow S\models B).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are material truth conditions on predicates of teams; the arrows themselves are object-language connectives. Empty teams, empty vocabulary, and every finite family of teams are included.

For every \(S\subseteq V\) there is a classical formula \(\alpha_S\) whose classical satisfying valuations are precisely \(S\), so that \(T\models\alpha_S\iff T\subseteq S\). One may take the classical disjunction of valuation minterms, with the empty disjunction \(\bot\). All finite conjunctions or splitting disjunctions below are abbreviation conventions, not additional semantic primitives.

## Proof
**1. A universal-downward-test lemma.** If the satisfaction family of \(A\) contains \(\varnothing\) and is closed under unions, then
\[
D_A(T)\iff\forall v\in T\;\bigl(\{v\}\models A\bigr).
\]
The forward direction uses singleton subteams. Conversely, assuming every singleton of \(T\) satisfies \(A\), every nonempty subteam is a finite union of satisfying singletons, hence satisfies \(A\); the empty subteam satisfies \(A\) by hypothesis. Thus \(D_A\) is flat. The lemma uses finiteness of \(T\), which is guaranteed here.

**2. Union and empty-team preservation.** All classical formulas contain \(\varnothing\) and are union-closed, as are conjunction and lax splitting disjunction of such properties. For the latter, if \(T_i=X_i\cup Y_i\) witnesses \(T_i\models A\otimes B\) for \(i=1,2\), then \((X_1\cup X_2)\cup(Y_1\cup Y_2)\) witnesses the union by union closure of the two arguments. For either \(\circ\!\to\) or \(\bullet\!\to\), suppose \(T_1,T_2\) satisfy the conditional and write \(T=T_1\cup T_2\). If \(D_A(T)\) is false, both implications at \(T\) hold. If it is true, then \(D_A(T_1)\) and \(D_A(T_2)\) hold. In the indicative case \(T_i\models B\), hence \(T\models B\). In the epistemic-conditional case \(D_B(T_i)\) holds, and for any \(S\subseteq T\) we have \(S=(S\cap T_1)\cup(S\cap T_2)\), so union closure of \(B\) yields \(S\models B\); thus \(D_B(T)\). The empty team satisfies either conditional because both arguments already satisfy the empty team. Structural induction proves the necessary closure properties at arbitrary nesting depth.

**3. A depth-one formula for an exact nonempty team.** Fix \(v\in V\) and choose \(\star\in\{\circ\!\to,\bullet\!\to\}\). Define
\[
\epsilon_v^{\star}:=(\alpha_{V\setminus\{v\}}\;\star\;\bot).
\]
For any \(T\), \(D_{\alpha_{V\setminus\{v\}}}(T)\) holds exactly when \(v\notin T\), while both \(T\models\bot\) and \(D_\bot(T)\) hold exactly when \(T=\varnothing\). Therefore, for either conditional,
\[
T\models\epsilon_v^{\star}\iff(T=\varnothing\text{ or }v\in T).
\]
For every **nonempty** \(S\subseteq V\), put
\[
\theta_S^{\star}:=\alpha_S\land\bigwedge_{v\in S}\epsilon_v^{\star}.
\]
The flat conjunct requires \(T\subseteq S\); the remaining conjuncts either allow the empty team or require every \(v\in S\). Hence
\[
T\models\theta_S^{\star}\iff T\in\{\varnothing,S\}.
\]
Each conditional appears in depth one and has a classical antecedent and consequent.

**4. Normal form for every union-closed property.** Let \(\mathcal P\) contain the empty team and be union-closed. Define
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal P}^{\star}:=\bigotimes_{S\in\mathcal P\setminus\{\varnothing\}}\theta_S^{\star},
\]
interpreting an empty splitting disjunction as \(\bot\). Lax splitting semantics gives \(T\models\Phi_{\mathcal P}^{\star}\) precisely when \(T\) is the union of zero or more nonempty members of \(\mathcal P\), since each factor contributes either \(\varnothing\) or its selected \(S\). Every such union lies in \(\mathcal P\). Conversely, if nonempty \(T\in\mathcal P\), the factor indexed by \(S=T\) may contribute \(T\) and all other factors the empty team; if \(T=\varnothing\), all factors contribute the empty team. This proves sufficiency and the depth-one cutoff for both \(\star\) choices.

**5. Sharp depth and flat counterfactual boundary.** At conditional depth zero the only connectives outside classical formulas are \(\land,\otimes\); their resulting properties are flat, since intersection and lax splitting union of flat properties are flat. For \(|N|\ge1\), choose distinct valuations \(v,w\) and set \(S=\{v,w\}\). The family \(\{\varnothing,S\}\) is union-closed and contains the empty team, but is not flat because it excludes \(\{v\}\). It is defined by \(\theta_S^{\star}\) at depth one; the bound is sharp. When \(N\) is empty, \(|V|=1\) and every admissible union-closed empty-team property is already flat.

To analyze \(\rightsquigarrow\), suppose \(A,B\) are flat, corresponding to sets of valuations \(C,D\subseteq V\). For each team \(S\), \(D_A(S)\iff S\subseteq C\) and \(S\models B\iff S\subseteq D\). Consequently
\[
T\models A\rightsquigarrow B
\iff\neg\exists v\in T\;(v\in C\setminus D)
\iff T\subseteq(V\setminus C)\cup D.
\]
The first equivalence holds because a singleton \(\{v\}\) witnesses failure in one direction, whereas in the absence of such a singleton every \(S\subseteq T\cap C\) lies inside \(D\). The operation is therefore flat. Since conjunction and splitting disjunction preserve flatness, induction on arbitrary nested formulas shows every counterfactual-only formula is flat. Every flat property is definable classically by \(\alpha_S\); this completes the trichotomy. \(\square\)

## Verification
A standalone direct-semantics checker is embedded as `verify_conditionals.py`. Run with `python3 verify_conditionals.py` (standard Python 3 library only). It constructs the three arrow operations directly from their quantified team clauses, checks exact-team formulas and their lax-split normal forms for **every** union-closed empty-team property on abstract valuation sets of sizes 1, 2, 3, and 4, and tests the counterfactual's flat reduction on all pairs of flat predicates at these sizes. It also checks sharp nonflat examples for sizes 2 through 4. Recorded result: 2, 7, 61, and 2480 such families, respectively; 5100 conditional-family normal-form comparisons; 340 flat-counterfactual comparisons; final marker `VERIFY_OK`. These bounded computations are cross-checks only; the proof above establishes the theorem for every finite vocabulary.

## Relationship to prior work
Barbero and Yang, *Possible and impossible conditionals for team logics* (arXiv:2603.02136; first public 2026-03-02), Section 5 supplies the three distinct epistemic semantic clauses, including preservation facts. Its Section 6 explicitly raises the effect of adding such weak conditionals to classical team logic as a further question; it does **not** derive this expressive trichotomy or a conditional-depth-one complete normal form. The article's earlier impossibility result for conditionals satisfying stronger proof rules is consistent with this classification: the two operators here are weaker.

Yang, *Propositional union closed team logics* (arXiv:2008.10324), Section 3 proves that several **other** propositional languages, for example ones with nonempty splitting disjunction or inclusion atoms, express precisely all union-closed team properties containing the empty team. Yang and Väänänen, *Propositional Team Logics* (arXiv:1606.03984), also study a separate might modality and expressive classifications. Those results establish the importance and attainability of this class by other syntax, but **do not state** that the newly introduced 2026 epistemic indicative and epistemic conditional separately attain it with depth-one classical-antecedent tests, nor that the 2026 epistemic counterfactual collapses to classical expressivity. The present proof constructs the missing translations directly rather than assuming the earlier completeness results imply them.

## Limitations
The theorem is for **finite** propositional vocabularies and standard lax team splitting; it does not assert normal-form finiteness for infinite valuation spaces, complexity-optimal translation size, preservation under contradictory negation of arbitrary formulas, or first-order equivalence. The explicit construction has at most \(2^{|V|}-1\) factors, potentially doubly exponential in \(|N|\); no succinctness claim is made. Equivalent observations could appear in unindexed literature under different terminology; formal publication priority is not established by a finite literature search. Finite semantic tests do not certify the infinite family independently of the proof.
