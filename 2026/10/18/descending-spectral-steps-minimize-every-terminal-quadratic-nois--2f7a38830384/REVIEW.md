# Scientific review: descending spectral steps and terminal gradient-noise risk

## Correctness — PASS

The claim holds for all finite dimensions and horizons, positive-definite Hessians, positive predetermined step multisets, and independent centered gradients with common positive-semidefinite covariance. Independence yields the exact terminal covariance sum. Because the deterministic update factors commute, adjacent interchanges alter only their two directly injected noise terms. Expanding gives the exact exchange \(\alpha\beta(\beta-\alpha)T(HQ+QH)T\). For any positive-semidefinite spectral weight \(W\) commuting with \(H\), trace cyclicity reduces this to \(2\alpha\beta(\beta-\alpha)\operatorname{tr}(WHT^2Q)\), with the latter trace nonnegative by simultaneous diagonalization and positivity. Bubble sorting therefore proves global optimality; strictness is conditional on positive-definite noise/weight and nonzero suffix. Direct calculations verify the reciprocal-eigenvalue strictness claim and the correlated-noise counterexample to Loewner ordering. The supplied `check.py` executes 7,825 formula and risk checks plus 6,900 strict comparisons. These finite checks corroborate, rather than replace, the all-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliography risk

Targeted record searches tested aliases including stochastic polynomial/Richardson iteration, optimal step permutations, terminal covariance and Chebyshev root ordering. The closest numerical result on stagewise-safe Richardson iteration optimizes individually nonexpansive deterministic factors and does not imply the stochastic covariance exchange law. A record on random-reshuffled stochastic gradients compares sampling of component functions, not permutations of fixed positive step lengths under common additive noise. Published analyses of random-reshuffled component gradients concern component-index permutations, not prescribed step-length permutations.

The primary Zhou–Gu preprint establishes a Hanoi order motivated by deterministic high-frequency rebound; its accessible abstract does not describe a fixed iid gradient-noise model. A complete copy was not accessible, so potential overlap in the full text is an explicit risk, not a conclusion that all sections exclude the claim. Gutknecht–Röllin analyze Chebyshev implementation roundoff, whereas Fox–Parker analyze sampling distributions with step-dependent covariance selected to preserve a target invariant covariance. Lebedev–Finogenov is a historically important Chebyshev parameter-ordering source, but its full primary text was not reliably available for a decisive exclusion. No published exact terminal multiset-ordering theorem was identified in the material that could be inspected. This is evidence supporting originality, not an exhaustive bibliographic certificate.

## Value — PASS

The theorem resolves a natural and nontrivial design question left open by deterministic spectral ordering: for fixed steps and repeated noisy gradient calls, which permutation minimizes final accuracy loss? Its answer is simultaneously valid for every quadratic spectral risk and any spatial noise correlations, without imposing a stability bound on individual steps. The two-mode spectral-root example shows an unbounded \(\Theta(\kappa)\) relative objective-risk penalty for the deterministic high-frequency-first sequence, giving substantive motivation to distinguish terminal stochastic accuracy from transient deterministic stability. The indefinite correlated-noise example marks the precise danger in upgrading trace optimality to matrix Loewner optimality.

## Limitations and remaining risks

The analysis requires fixed in-advance steps, a quadratic with a fixed positive-definite Hessian, and independent zero-mean gradient errors of common covariance. It does not cover state-dependent or temporally correlated gradient noise, finite-precision roundoff, nonlinear objectives, data-dependent step adaptation, or a bound on maximum transient amplification. The motivating 2026 preprint and an older historical ordering source could not be fully inspected; these are substantive originality uncertainties. The proof does not assert strict ordering uniqueness for singular weights or zero-noise directions, nor Loewner optimality for all anisotropic covariances.
