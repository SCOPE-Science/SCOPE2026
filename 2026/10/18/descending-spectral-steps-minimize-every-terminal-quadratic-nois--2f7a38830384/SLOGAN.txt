For a positive-definite quadratic with independent gradient-oracle noise, sorting any prescribed positive steps from largest to smallest simultaneously minimizes every terminal quadratic risk weighted in the Hessian eigenbasis—even with correlated anisotropic noise and individually unstable steps. For reciprocal steps on a two-eigenvalue spectrum of condition number \(\kappa\), the reverse, high-frequency-first order can suffer asymptotically \(2\kappa\) times the expected terminal objective error, despite identical zero terminal deterministic bias.
