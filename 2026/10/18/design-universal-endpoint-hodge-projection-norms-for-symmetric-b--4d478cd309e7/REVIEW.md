# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. All quantifiers are conditional on an existing nontrivial symmetric \(2\)-design. The exact transfer-current formula follows by solving the Laplacian equations on zero-sum point and block spaces, using \(k^2-k+\lambda=\lambda v\). All off-diagonal signs follow from \(\lambda<k\), and the negative-edge counts follow from the point/block intersection axioms. Column sums and the diagonal resistance give the two exact \(\ell_1\) norms without any symmetry assumption on graph automorphisms. The \(\ell_\infty\) equality uses matrix symmetry. Exact rational grounded-Laplacian tests corroborate the proof for four structurally different designs. The scope deliberately excludes minimum projections over all complements.

## Originality

PASS under the completed comparisons, with residual bibliographic uncertainty. Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii, arXiv:2305.12582, develops invariant and minimal cycle projections for discrete tori and Hamming graphs, not an incidence-design transfer-current column law. Shramchenko–Shramchenko, arXiv:2312.03937, derives design matrix spectral results, not edge-space absolute projection norms. A previously recorded crown-graph cut/cycle result handles the special design with blocks complementary to singletons; it does not imply the general \(\lambda<k-1\) case or the projective-plane four-level current distribution. Schild–Rao–Srivastava, arXiv:1708.01632, supplies logarithmic-type general transfer-current bounds without this sharp dimension-free design-specific calculation. Related Heawood \(L_1\)-distortion, projective-plane homology, and design combinatorics evaluate different invariants. Unindexed or non-English electrical-network literature remains a possible equivalent-source risk; database absence alone is not relied on as novelty proof.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The output is a natural operator-norm invariant of the canonical Banach-space cut/cycle decomposition, determined exactly for an infinite incidence-geometry family without requiring automorphism transitivity. The theorem provides a complete sign-and-multiplicity classification of electrical responses; on projective planes it shows a four-level law, exact order-dependent constants, and the universally sharp, strict upper bounds \(5\) and \(6\), with projective planes approaching them. The universal degree-four switch in which complement has larger norm is structural information across all symmetric designs. The known crown special case is credited rather than presented as new.

## Limits

The theorem neither proves norm-minimality among all projections nor evaluates \(\ell_p\) norms for intermediate exponents. Existence of projective planes for every order is not assumed. Bibliographic coverage is not exhaustive and independent checking is pending.
