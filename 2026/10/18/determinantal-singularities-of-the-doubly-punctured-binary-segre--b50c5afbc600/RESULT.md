# Determinantal singularities of the doubly punctured binary Segre

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 3\), consider the complex projective monomial variety
\[
X_n=\overline{\bigl\{[z_S]_{\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq[n]}:\ z_S=\prod_{i\in S}t_i,\quad(t_1,\dots,t_n)\in(\mathbb C^*)^n\bigr\}}
\subset \mathbb P^{2^n-3}_{\mathbb C}.
\]
Equivalently this is the coordinate projection of the binary Segre variety \((\mathbb P^1)^n\) omitting the two opposite extreme monomials (the empty and full subsets). The projection from the original product is rational, not claimed to be a morphism everywhere.

**Theorem.** The variety \(X_n\) is normal and its singular locus consists of exactly \(2n\) reduced points as a set, namely the torus-fixed coordinate points \(p_S\) with \(|S|=1\) or \(|S|=n-1\). For every such point, not merely formally but on the entire standard affine coordinate chart,
\[
X_n\cap D_+(z_S)\ \simeq\ \{M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2,n-1}(\mathbb C):\operatorname{rank}M\le1\}.
\]
Thus the completed local ring at \(p_S\) is the completion at the zero matrix of the determinantal coordinate ring
\[
\mathbb C[a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1},b_1,\ldots,b_{n-1}]
/ (a_i b_j-a_j b_i:1\le i<j\le n-1).
\]
At all other closed points the local rings are regular. At each of the \(2n\) singular points,
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{X_n,p_S})\simeq\mathbb Z,
\qquad e(\mathcal O_{X_n,p_S})=n-1.
\]
The variety is Gorenstein, equivalently \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein, exactly when \(n=3\); for \(n\ge4\), its non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus is precisely these \(2n\) isolated points. Its embedded degree is
\[
\deg X_n=n!-2.
\]
For \(n=3\) one recovers the previously known toric intersection of two quadrics with six ordinary double points. The pointwise determinantal classification and its higher-dimensional \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein boundary, rather than this known base case or the monomial presentation, are the contribution evaluated here.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); the theorem concerns all \(n\ge3\), with the new uniform singularity determination of particular interest for \(n\ge4\). For each nonempty proper \(S\subset[n]\), let \(\mathbf1_S\in\mathbb Z^n\) be its indicator vector. The graded homogeneous coordinate ring is the subalgebra generated in degree one by \(T\prod_{i\in S}t_i\), with \(T\) an auxiliary homogenizing variable. The coordinate point \(p_S\) has coordinate \(z_S=1\) and all other coordinates zero. A local divisor-class group means the integral Weil-divisor class group of its local ring; \(e\) denotes the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity at its maximal ideal.

All claims concern the specified embedded toric variety, not arbitrary projections of Segre embeddings and not the Jacobian *scheme* with its possible nilpotent structure. The singular locus assertion describes its reduced support exactly.

## Proof

**1. Affine chart semigroups and coverage.** Every generator exponent \(\mathbf1_S\) is a vertex of its convex hull, since it is a zero-one cube vertex. The standard charts \(D_+(z_S)\), for all nonempty proper \(S\), cover \(X_n\) because they are the usual projective affine cover. Dehomogenizing the monomial coordinate ring at \(z_S\) gives the semigroup algebra
\[
\mathbb C[\mathbf1_U-\mathbf1_S:\ \varnothing\ne U\subsetneq[n]],
\]
where bracket notation denotes the \(\mathbb C\)-algebra spanned by Laurent monomials with the listed exponent differences. This is an exact equality of coordinate rings, not a set-theoretic parametrization.

**2. Every non-extreme chart is affine space.** Fix \(S\) with \(2\le |S|\le n-2\). Every difference \(\mathbf1_U-\mathbf1_S\) has nonnegative coefficient along \(e_i\) when \(i\notin S\) and nonnegative coefficient along \(-e_i\) when \(i\in S\). Conversely, each of these \(n\) signed basis vectors occurs among the differences: toggle exactly the corresponding membership bit. The toggled set remains nonempty and proper by the strict weight inequalities on \(S\). The chart semigroup is therefore the freely generated orthant, and \(X_n\cap D_+(z_S)\simeq\mathbb A^n\).

**3. Every extreme chart is a rank-one matrix cone.** By permutation symmetry take \(S=\{1\}\). In the chart \(z_{\{1\}}\ne0\), introduce the \(2(n-1)\) monomials
\[
a_j=\frac{z_{\{j\}}}{z_{\{1\}}}=\frac{t_j}{t_1},\qquad
b_j=\frac{z_{\{1,j\}}}{z_{\{1\}}}=t_j\qquad(2\le j\le n).
\]
Both displayed coordinates exist because \(n\ge3\). If \(1\in U\), then \(z_U/z_{\{1\}}=\prod_{j\in U\setminus\{1\}}b_j\). If \(1\notin U\), choose any \(j\in U\) and write \(z_U/z_{\{1\}}=a_j\prod_{k\in U\setminus\{j\}}b_k\). Thus the entire chart coordinate algebra is generated by precisely the entries of the matrix with rows \((a_j)\) and \((b_j)\).

These entries satisfy the minor relations \(a_i b_j-a_j b_i=0\). Conversely, the parametrization \(a_j=s t_j,\ b_j=t_j\) with independent \(s,t_j\) has dense image in the irreducible rank-at-most-one determinantal variety; the ideal of that variety is exactly its prime ideal of \(2\times2\) minors (the classical Segre ideal). Here \(s=t_1^{-1}\) ranges through a dense nonzero subset. Consequently the kernel of the chart map is the determinantal minor ideal itself, proving the asserted exact affine-scheme isomorphism. Complementation of subsets \(U\mapsto[n]\setminus U\) permutes the projective monomial coordinates and preserves the toric variety, so every \((n-1)\)-subset chart is of the same type.

**4. Normality and the complete singular support.** Affine space is normal. Every rank-at-most-one determinantal cone is normal: it is the affine cone over the projectively normal Segre embedding \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-2}\) by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\), equivalently the saturated semigroup algebra generated by the products of one row variable and one column variable. Hence the listed affine cover is normal, proving normality of \(X_n\).

A rank-one matrix is a smooth point of the rank-at-most-one determinantal variety: choose a nonzero entry and solve all remaining entries in terms of its row and column, yielding \(n\) independent local parameters. At the zero matrix all defining minors vanish to order at least two, so its Zariski tangent space has dimension \(2(n-1)>n\). Thus this chart has a unique singular point, the zero matrix corresponding to \(p_{\{1\}}\). Together with the smooth interior-weight charts and complementation, this proves the full \(2n\)-point singular-support classification.

**5. Integral local class group and canonical class.** Write \(m=n-1\). The cone just obtained is over \(Y=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\) polarized by \(H=\mathcal O_Y(1,1)\). Its vertex-local divisor-class group is \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)/\mathbb Z[H]\simeq\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z(1,1)\simeq\mathbb Z\). This can also be read integrally from the \(n+1\) primitive generators of the maximal cone in \(\mathbb Z^n\): \(-e_1\), the \(e_j\) for \(j\ge2\), and \(\sum_{j=1}^n e_j\); the first \(n\) already form a unimodular basis. Thus the toric divisor exact sequence has torsion-free cokernel of rank one, confirming the local group calculation without rationalizing.

The canonical bundle of \(Y\) is \(\mathcal O_Y(-2,-m)\). Its class modulo \((1,1)\) vanishes precisely when \(m=2\), equivalently \(n=3\). Since the local class group is torsion-free, \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartierness of the canonical divisor is equivalent to its being Cartier. The other points are regular and therefore Gorenstein. This proves both global canonical-property equivalences and the exact non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein locus.

The determinantal cone is standard graded and has degree equal to that of its Segre base: \(\deg(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{n-2},\mathcal O(1,1))=\binom{n-1}{1}=n-1\). Its vertex Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is therefore \(n-1\). More explicitly, its graded Hilbert series is \((1+(n-2)t)/(1-t)^n\), which has numerator value \(n-1\) at \(t=1\).

**6. Projective degree.** The exponent polytope is exactly
\[
P_n=\bigl\{x\in[0,1]^n:1\le\textstyle\sum_i x_i\le n-1\bigr\}.
\]
The inequalities cut off the two opposite corner simplices of the unit cube, each of Euclidean volume \(1/n!\), and introduce no other vertices: the clipping hyperplanes meet the cube only at the immediate binary neighbors of the removed endpoints. Thus \(\operatorname{vol}(P_n)=1-2/n!\). The toric degree is its normalized lattice volume, giving \(\deg X_n=n!-2\).

For \(n=3\), the six coordinates form three complement pairs. Their three complementary products are equal; two independent equations among these three products give a complete intersection of quadrics in \(\mathbb P^5\). Each of its six singular charts is \(\{ad-bc=0\}\subset\mathbb A^4\), an ordinary threefold node. This small case is documented in prior toric threefold literature and is not offered as an isolated new result.

## Verification

A standalone finite checker is supplied as `verify.py`, with recorded output `verification_output.txt` and optional dependency information in `DEPENDENCIES.txt`. Run `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing this file. It independently enumerates all monomial exponent differences on all \(238\) projective vertex charts for \(3\le n\le7\), totaling \(20{,}852\) membership checks. For each chart it constructs an explicit decomposition into the proposed generators and checks all rank-one quadratic exponent relations. It also checks the exact predicted smooth/singular chart counts. Optional convex-hull volume calculations numerically corroborate \(\deg X_n=n!-2\) for \(n=3,4,5\).

The enumerations do not prove statements for all \(n\); the general semigroup identities, the determinantal kernel theorem, and the canonical class argument above supply the infinite proof. The finite volume tests are numerical checks of the separate exact geometric volume argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Michałek--Oeding--Zwiernik, *Secant cumulants and toric geometry* (arXiv:1212.1515, first posted 7 December 2012), Definition 4.1 and Remark 4.2, already define the family of affine toric semigroup cones \(T_{a,b}\). Our projective variety is exactly \(\mathbb P(T_{1,n-1})\), so the monomial model is **not** a new construction. Their Proposition 4.7 gives quadratic generators for the full \(T_{a,b}\) class, and Example 4.4 explicitly records the six-vertex octahedron for \((a,b,n)=(1,2,3)\). Their detailed local-singularity classifications in Section 5 concern the different family \(T_{2,n}\) associated to secant cumulants, rather than the vertex charts of \(\mathbb P(T_{1,n-1})\). The contribution is the exact pointwise determinantal algebra for the latter family and the resulting class-group and canonical boundary for all dimensions.

Cheltsov--Shramov and collaborators' work on equivariant geometry of the Segre cubic and Burkhardt quartic describes the known six-nodal toric intersection of two quadrics (Proposition 6.1 in the cited edition). That covers the \(n=3\) base-case geometry, not the higher-dimensional determinant-cone classification. General results about rank-one determinantal ideals and affine toric canonical classes are used here as known tools, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

This theorem classifies the reduced singular support and the completed local rings of all singular points; it does not determine the global nilpotent or embedded structure of the projective Jacobian singular subscheme. It does not classify singularities of unrelated coordinate projections, other choices of deleted Segre monomials, or positive-characteristic deformations. General rank-one determinantal facts and the six-nodal threefold base case are established prior knowledge. The possibility of an unindexed earlier presentation of the uniform higher-dimensional local classification is a historical originality risk.
