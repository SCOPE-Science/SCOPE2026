# Scientific review: dihedral homology modules of ordinary wheel matching complexes

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns one precisely defined graph complex, its ordinary matching complex, with the dihedral action normalized on original rim vertices. The spoke filtration gives an equivariant relative direct sum of suspensions of \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{n-2})\); the path recursion yields one rational generator per spoke. The rim's only reduced rational homology is one-dimensional in the two stated congruence classes. In the \(n\equiv1\pmod3\) case, the path inclusion is a homotopy equivalence via two contractible-link attachments, making the connecting map surjective; in the \(n\equiv2\pmod3\) case, semisimplicity splits the short exact sequence. The two reflection signs are independently derived by a reduced Lefschetz calculation with simplex-orientation signs and a six-periodic path polynomial. The final induced-module trace formula is valid for every element of \(D_n\). Direct enumeration checks every automorphism for twelve rim lengths up to twenty, and separate small integer homology-rank computations agree with the theorem. This is an infinite proof with finite corroboration, not a generalization inferred from experiments.

## Originality — PASS within searched literature
The precise new claim is the **full rational \(D_n\)-module**, not the already established nonequivariant homotopy type. Boudouris–Kawakami–Kettinger, *Summer 2017 CURL Research*, Proposition 1.19, gives all wheel sphere multiplicities but not the action of polygon rotations or reflections on those classes. Adamaszek, arXiv:1106.6250, supplies classical path/cycle recursions and graph-independent cofibre methods, not a wheel character. Existing public dihedral work on cycle matching complexes applies to the rim \(B\) only; it does not compute the extra spoke-relative module, its stabilizer reflection sign, or its connecting map. Public work describing dihedral homology of **wheel discrete-Morse complexes** uses a different simplicial object (acyclic partial face matchings rather than disjoint graph edges), so it does not cover the assertion. Targeted topical and parameter searches returned no statement implying this induced-plus/minus-line formula. Residual risk: an equivariant wheel matching result might exist under alternative language or in incompletely indexed material. This review records a best-evidence research assessment, not an independent priority certification.

## Value — PASS
Wheel graphs are a canonical planar graph family, and their ordinary matching-complex wedge multiplicities have a published classification. Those integer ranks do not determine dihedral symmetry: the same top dimension \(9\) at rim lengths \(8\) and \(10\) masks reflection trace \(-1\) versus \(+1\), respectively. The new induced-module/one-line decomposition determines invariant classes, reflection eigenspaces and every rotation character for an infinite family, supplying a natural equivariant refinement rather than arbitrary parameter enumeration. The exclusion of the third congruence class follows structurally from its contractible spoke links; the main new mechanism appears exactly in the other two classes.

## Scope and source limitations
The proof describes rational representations only; integral module extensions and the third congruence class are not claimed. The most directly relevant whole-document comparison is the publicly available 2017 manuscript's wheel section, where non-equivariant coverage is explicit; searchable public research records provide additional object-level comparisons but not comprehensive proof of priority. The checker verifies signs and ranks in specified finite cases but is not used as an infinite certificate.
