# Dimension-independent optimal polyhedral generators for neutral toral products

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(q\ge2\) and \(m\ge2\), equip \(\mathbb T^{q+1}\) with normalized Haar probability and consider the toral endomorphism
\[
 T_{q,m}(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_q,y)
   =(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_q,my\bmod1).
\]
Let \(g(T_{q,m})\) be the minimum number of atoms of a finite **one-sided generating Borel partition** modulo Haar measure. Let \(g_{\mathrm{poly}}(T_{q,m})\) be the corresponding minimum when every atom is a finite union of polyhedra on a standard fundamental cube, so its torus boundary is piecewise affine. Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad g(T_{q,m})=g_{\mathrm{poly}}(T_{q,m})=m+1 \quad}
\]
**independently of the number \(q\) of fixed coordinates**. A concrete partition attaining the bound has only one special symbol in addition to the \(m\) expanding digits; the position of the expanding coordinate within disjoint test windows identifies which fixed coordinate that special symbol interrogates. For \(m=2\), this yields exactly three polyhedral atoms in every dimension \(q+1\), rather than the exponential-in-\(q\) alphabet of a previously available simultaneous-comparison construction.

## Assumptions and scope
All circle coordinates have canonical half-open representatives in \([0,1)\). A partition is one-sided generating modulo Haar measure if its forward itinerary separates a full-measure Borel subset, equivalently if the completed sigma-algebra generated by all nonnegative inverse iterates of its atoms is the full Borel sigma-algebra. We do not require Markov or independent atoms, pointwise generation on every orbit, or a generating Rokhlin partition.

Fix \(q,m\ge2\) and write
\[
 \delta=\frac1{m(2q+1)},\qquad
 a_i=(2i-1)\delta,\qquad b_i=2i\delta,
 \quad 1\le i\le q.
\]
The \(q\) pairwise disjoint intervals \(I_i=[a_i,b_i)\) lie strictly within the zero-digit strip \([0,1/m)\), and distinct intervals are separated by a gap of length \(\delta\). Define the special cell
\[
 P_*:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}
 \bigl\{(\boldsymbol\theta,y):a_i\le y<a_i+\delta\theta_i\bigr\}.
\]
Define \(P_0=\{0\le y<1/m\}\setminus P_*\), and for each \(1\le j\le m-1\) define \(P_j=\{j/m\le y<(j+1)/m\}\). This is a disjoint partition into exactly \(m+1\) nonempty Borel cells. The separating hyperplanes are \(y=a_i\), \(y=a_i+\delta\theta_i\), the base-\(m\) strip boundaries, and fundamental-cube seams. Thus the cells are finite unions of polyhedra and have Haar-null, piecewise affine boundaries. In particular, this is a smooth partition in the usual piecewise-smooth-boundary sense.

## Proof
**1. The construction has the claimed geometry and cardinality.** Since \(b_q=2q/[m(2q+1)]<1/m\), each test window belongs to the zero-digit strip. For every \(\theta_i\in[0,1)\),
\[
 a_i\le a_i+\delta\theta_i<b_i,
\]
so the special-cell pieces are disjoint. Every \(P_j\), for \(j\ge1\), is a nonempty strip; \(P_*\) has positive Haar measure, and \(P_0\) contains the final unused portion of the zero-digit strip. The defining inequalities are affine in the canonical coordinates; their complements are finite Boolean combinations of affine halfspaces. Hence every atom is a finite union of polyhedra in the cube.

**2. Recover the expanding coordinate from the forward name.** Let \(\omega_n\) be the partition symbol seen at \(T_{q,m}^n(\boldsymbol\theta,y)\). Merge the symbols \(*\) and \(0\) into the base-\(m\) digit \(0\), leaving \(1,\ldots,m-1\) unchanged. The resulting digits satisfy
\[
 d_n=\lfloor m y_n\rfloor,\qquad y_n=m^ny\bmod1.
\]
The recurrence \(y_{n+1}=my_n-d_n\) therefore gives, for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
 y=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{d_n}{m^{n+1}}+\frac{y_N}{m^N}.
\]
Because \(0\le y_N<1\), the forward name determines \(y=\sum_{n\ge0}d_n/m^{n+1}\) and hence all exact orbit values \(y_n\).

**3. Recover every fixed coordinate using only the one additional symbol.** There exists a Lebesgue-full set \(Y\subset[0,1)\) whose forward orbits under \(D_m:y\mapsto my\bmod1\) are uniformly distributed. For example, take the intersection of the Birkhoff full-measure sets for the countably many rational-endpoint intervals; the ergodicity of \(D_m\) follows from its independent base-\(m\) digits. Rational approximation of endpoints shows that for each \(y\in Y\), the empirical frequency of \(y_n\in[u,v)\) equals \(v-u\) **simultaneously for every real interval** \([u,v)\subseteq[0,1)\).

Let \(s_n=1\) when \(\omega_n=*\), and \(s_n=0\) otherwise. The already reconstructed \(y_n\) tell us in which, if any, test window \(I_i\) the expanding orbit is located. The special symbol occurs in \(I_i\) precisely when \(y_n\in[a_i,a_i+\delta\theta_i)\), and thus
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}s_n\,\mathbf1_{I_i}(y_n)
 =\delta\theta_i,
 \qquad 1\le i\le q.
\]
Every \(\theta_i\) is consequently recovered as \(\delta^{-1}\) times a limit computed solely from the one-sided name. Crucially the single full-measure set \(Y\) works for **every** fixed-coordinate vector \(\boldsymbol\theta\), because it ensures uniform frequencies at all thresholds. The names therefore separate the full Haar-measure set \([0,1)^q\times Y\). This proves \(g_{\mathrm{poly}}(T_{q,m})\le m+1\).

**4. The entropy obstruction gives a sharp lower bound even for arbitrary Borel atoms.** The Haar entropy is
\[
 h_{\mathrm{Haar}}(T_{q,m})=\log m,
\]
since \(D_m\) is the uniform one-sided \(m\)-symbol Bernoulli map and the fixed \(q\)-torus factor has entropy zero. For any generating partition into \(k\) cells, the finite-generator entropy identity and the alphabet bound give \(\log m\le\log k\); hence \(k\ge m\).

Suppose \(k=m\). Let \(H_N\) be the Shannon entropy of its length-\(N\) stationary label block. Then \(H_N\le N\log m\), while the stationary entropy-rate formula gives
\[
 \log m=h(T_{q,m},\mathcal P)
 =\inf_{N\ge1}\frac{H_N}{N}.
\]
Therefore equality \(H_N=N\log m\) holds for *every* \(N\). Every length-\(N\) label word consequently has probability \(m^{-N}\), so the forward-name process is the uniform i.i.d. \(m\)-symbol shift and is ergodic. But a one-sided generating partition makes the name map a measure-theoretic isomorphism. This would make \(T_{q,m}\) ergodic, which is false: for example, \(\mathbf1_{\{\theta_1<1/2\}}\) is a nonconstant invariant function. Thus \(k=m\) is impossible. Combining this with step 3 gives \(g(T_{q,m})=g_{\mathrm{poly}}(T_{q,m})=m+1\).

## Verification
The argument is exact and uses no finite extrapolation. Its critical points can be checked directly: the windows have width \(\delta\), are separated by gaps, and lie in the zero-digit strip; merging the two zero labels leaves the base-\(m\) digit; the special label restricted to window \(I_i\) records the interval \([a_i,a_i+\delta\theta_i)\); and every simultaneous frequency limit follows from one uniformly distributed orbit. The lower bound requires the entropy equality for **all** block lengths, not a single observed block or an appeal to the entropy inequality alone.

The optional standalone `verify.py` checks 2,520 exact-rational finite-name cases, positive witnesses for all labels and dimensions tested, and a finite prefix-frequency diagnostic. The program uses only Python's standard library. Execute `python verify.py` to reproduce those diagnostics. Its finite tests cannot certify almost-everywhere generation, entropy equality, or originality; those depend on the written proof and source comparisons.

## Relationship to prior work
Leuridan's *Linear toral endomorphisms, exactness, Bernoulliness and generators* (arXiv:2610.01642v1, first public 2026-10-01) provides the motivating distinction between exactness, one-sided Bernoulli codings, and smooth finite generators. Its formal nonexistence theorem assumes a **strictly contracting** eigenvalue; the present matrices have eigenvalues \(1\) and \(m\), so this theorem does not apply. The preprint's broader abstract formulation must not be substituted for its precise theorem.

The published result *Sharp \(m+1\) polyhedral generators for neutral–expanding toral products* proves the same sharp cardinality for **one** fixed torus coordinate, with arbitrary \(m\). It does not give a finite-polyhedral construction for \(q\ge2\). The published result *Smooth finite generators for neutral toral factors* proves the existence of smooth generators for **arbitrarily many** fixed coordinates, but uses up to \(2^{q+1}\) symbols, specializes to \(m=2\), and explicitly does not assert a minimal alphabet. The published result *Optimal \(m+1\)-cell polygonal generators for quasiperiodic–expanding toral maps* treats a single **moving irrational rotation** coordinate, not multiple fixed coordinates. These are substantial nearby results, but their stated constructions do not imply the present dimension-uniform **polyhedral** minimum. A measurable-space isomorphism can transfer an abstract \(m+1\)-symbol generator from one fixed coordinate to several; it does **not** preserve polyhedral boundaries. The distinguishing point here is the explicit finite-affine multi-coordinate construction with just one shared special symbol.

## Limitations
Generation is mod Haar null sets, not topological generation for all initial states. No Markov, Rokhlin, independence, continuous decoding, or finite-time recovery property is claimed. The matrices are diagonal with \(q\ge2\) identity directions and one positive integer expanding multiplier; results for neutral Jordan blocks, rotations of higher multiplicity, other invariant measures, and general partially hyperbolic integer matrices are not asserted. The theorem concerns the number of cells, not the minimal number of facets or connected components. Classical measurable-generator theorems and possible older geometric coding constructions represent residual bibliographic overlap risk; no search proves absolute novelty.
