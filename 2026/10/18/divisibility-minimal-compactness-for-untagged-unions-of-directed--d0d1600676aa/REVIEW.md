# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The cycle-homomorphism lemma follows from modular successor equations. The reverse implication attaches a cycle at every vertex and forces each root into its divisibility-minimal target, while all finite attachments are colorable by a single finite instance map. The forward implication uses finite connected hulls, finite-target stabilization by combining finitely many obstructions, a definable least-cycle assignment for each weak component, and finite (hence ZF-valid) assembly of homomorphisms obtained from the stated assumptions. No choice for arbitrary components or any infinite computational inference is used. The empty-instance case is covered by the empty map.

## Originality
PASS on inspected sources, with historical-priority risk. Li et al., arXiv:2609.33873v1, Def. 7 and Theorem 42, use unary tags and prove a different compactness formula for that expanded signature. Rorabaugh--Tardif--Wehlau, arXiv:1609.05221v5, §6, analyze individual cycles, and Long, arXiv:2605.19543v1, §3, identifies ordinary cycle homomorphism divisibility but not ZF compactness of arbitrary disconnected instances. A prior treatment of individual-cycle prime factorization does not, by itself, imply the missing union-vs-conjunction step; the proof supplies that step by pinning and canonical grouping. Searches of public mathematical research records and earlier related claims found no equivalent union formula. Older literature on coproducts and compactness might contain the result and cannot be ruled out solely by unsuccessful searches.

## Value
PASS. The 2026 treatment needs unary tags to organize its cyclic templates; the corresponding untagged finite-union construction is equally natural but has a different signature. The theorem supplies a uniform exact ZF classification for every finite length set, identifies redundant components by divisibility, and isolates the precise no-choice gadget and assembly principles. This is a meaningful model-theoretic reduction, not a numerical increment or restatement of cycle homomorphism divisibility. It does not assert independent new weak-choice degrees.

## Scope and limits
Only finite nonempty sets of lengths at least two and the untagged binary digraph language are handled. It remains possible that a general published template-coproduct theorem subsumes this result; no uniqueness certification or independent validation has been performed.
