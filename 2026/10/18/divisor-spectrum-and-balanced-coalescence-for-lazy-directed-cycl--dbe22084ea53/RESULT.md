# Divisor spectrum and balanced coalescence for lazy directed cycles

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge2\), a probability \(0<p<1\), and the irreducible aperiodic transition matrix on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\)
\[
P(x,x)=1-p,\qquad P(x,x+1)=p.
\]
A grand coupling is specified by independent, identically distributed random maps \(F_t\), each of the form \(F_t(x)=x+B_t(x)\pmod n\), where \(B_t(x)\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\mathbb P(B_t(x)=1)=p\) for every \(x\). No independence between the coordinates of a single mask is assumed. Let \(\mu\) be their common law, and let \(k(\mu)\) be the almost-sure eventual number of distinct images of the composed maps \(F_t\circ\cdots\circ F_1\).

Define the **common period subgroup** of the supported masks by
\[
H_\mu=\{h\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z:
 B(x+h)=B(x)\ \text{for every }x\text{ and every mask }B
 \text{ with }\mu(B)>0\}.
\]
Then, for every such grand coupling, almost surely
\[
\boxed{k(\mu)=|H_\mu|.}
\]
Moreover, every eventual coalescence class contains exactly \(n/|H_\mu|\) original states; although its membership may be random, **its cardinality is deterministic and all class cardinalities are equal**. As \(\mu\) ranges over every admissible grand coupling, the exact spectrum is
\[
\boxed{K(P)=\{k\in\mathbb N:k\mid n\}.}
\]
Each divisor is realized by an explicit random-mapping coupling.

## Assumptions and scope

The state space is the finite *directed* cycle with a holding probability. The probability \(p\) must be strictly between zero and one; the independent random maps are homogeneous in time, but the spatial mask coordinates may be arbitrarily dependent. Original states start simultaneously at every vertex. The claim concerns eventual coalescence of the forward iterated-map coupling, not arbitrary path couplings that are not generated by independent maps.

For a coupling satisfying these conditions, the support of \(\mu\) consists of finitely many binary masks. This is why the common-period condition in the theorem can be checked against the support rather than any uncountable exceptional set.

## Proof

**1. A two-path separation lemma.** Work first on a directed cycle of length \(m\ge2\), with any mask law whose one-coordinate Bernoulli marginals equal the same \(p\). Start two trajectories at distinct vertices, and consider their clockwise separation \(D_t\), initially in \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\). Until collision, lift this separation to integers and update it by
\[
D_{t+1}=D_t+B_{t+1}(Y_t)-B_{t+1}(X_t).
\]
If the update first reaches \(0\) or \(m\), the two trajectories have met modulo \(m\), and we stop the separation at that boundary. Each mask is independent of the past, so the two coordinate increments have the same conditional mean \(p\), and the stopped \(D_t\) is a bounded martingale in \([0,m]\).

By bounded-martingale convergence and integer-valuedness, \(D_t\) is eventually constant almost surely. On the event of no collision, its eventual value must be a nonzero \(d\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\). If there were a vertex \(x\) and a supported mask with \(B(x+d)\ne B(x)\), such discrepancies would have positive conditional probability at every time the first trajectory visits \(x\) while the separation is \(d\). The first trajectory alone is a finite irreducible Markov chain and visits every vertex infinitely often almost surely. Independence of successive masks and the conditional Borel–Cantelli lemma imply that discrepancies must recur, contradicting eventual constancy. Therefore noncollision has positive probability only if \(d\) is a common nonzero period of every supported mask.

In particular, **if the common period subgroup is trivial, any two trajectories collide almost surely**. Since there are finitely many pairs, all trajectories eventually coalesce almost surely.

**2. Reduction by common periods.** The set \(H_\mu\) is a subgroup: translation invariance of every supported mask is closed under sums and inverses. Write \(k=|H_\mu|\), \(q=n/k\), so \(H_\mu\) is the subgroup of multiples of \(q\) modulo \(n\). Every supported map is \(H_\mu\)-equivariant:
\[
F(x+h)=F(x)+h\qquad (h\in H_\mu).
\]
Consequently every map induces a random map on the quotient cycle \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), still given by holding or moving one step with marginal probability \(p\).

The quotient masks have **no nonzero common period**. Otherwise, a nonzero period in \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) would lift to a period of all the original masks not belonging to \(H_\mu\), contradicting its definition. If \(q\ge2\), the lemma therefore implies almost-sure joint coalescence on the quotient; for \(q=1\) this is immediate.

**3. Rank and class sizes.** There are \(q\) original \(H_\mu\)-orbits, each containing \(k\) states. For every finite composition of supported maps, equivariance implies that the \(k\) states in each original orbit are carried bijectively onto all \(k\) members of a target orbit. After the finite almost-sure time when all quotient trajectories coalesce, all original target orbits agree. Therefore the complete image contains exactly \(k\) states. Moreover, each of the \(q\) original orbits contributes exactly one original state to the preimage of each target state. Each eventual kernel/coalescence class thus has exactly \(q=n/k\) members. No further coalescence is possible, since the restriction of each subsequent supported map to the target orbit is a bijection. This proves both claims for an arbitrary grand coupling.

**4. Every divisor occurs.** Let \(k\mid n\) and \(q=n/k\). Independently for every time step, draw \(q\) independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) bits \((C_0,\ldots,C_{q-1})\), and repeat the resulting word periodically around the \(n\)-cycle: \(B(x)=C_{x\bmod q}\). Every bit pattern on the quotient has positive probability because \(0<p<1\). Hence the common period subgroup is exactly the multiples of \(q\), with order \(k\). The established formula gives \(k(\mu)=k\), with all \(k\) coalescence classes of cardinality \(q\). This constructs every divisor and completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone `checker.py` performs exact finite semigroup calculations, including all complementary two-mask couplings on cycles of sizes \(2\) through \(8\), and selected full-support quotient-period constructions. It verifies that the minimum transformation rank equals the order of the common mask-period subgroup in every tested case. Execute `python3 checker.py` with a standard Python 3 installation (no third-party packages). It reports 519 exact checks. These calculations are diagnostics, not the proof for arbitrary \(n\) or all coupling laws; that proof is given above.

## Relationship to prior work

Grimmett and Holmes' *Coalescence in Markov Chains* (arXiv:2510.13572, first publicly submitted 15 October 2025; published 2026) defines the grand-coupling spectrum \(K(P)\), raises its determination for particular \(P\) as Question 3.3, and asks about the determinism of coalescence-class sizes in Question 3.11. It supplies general bounds and examples but does not state the divisor spectrum or common-mask-period criterion for the directed lazy cycle. The present classification identifies the complete spectrum and a stronger fixed-cardinality property **for this specific transition family**, not for general irreducible chains.

Their earlier *Non-coupling from the past* (arXiv:1907.05605; 2021 chapter) introduced the coalescence-number framework. General facts about finite-state martingales, recurrence and random-mapping semigroups are used here as established tools. Prior work on synchronizing finite automata may share elements of the period-subgroup mechanism; this has not been ruled out exhaustively.

## Limitations

The theorem does not classify the actual random membership of coalescence classes. It does not cover bidirectional nearest-neighbor steps, spatially varying holding probabilities, arbitrary joint path couplings outside the independent-random-map setting, or infinite cycles. The available earlier chapter on non-coupling was not examined in full text; a remotely indexed or differently formulated finite-automata theorem could imply the present classification. Independent literature-priority verification is not claimed.
