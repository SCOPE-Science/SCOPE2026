# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The face criterion and neighborhood closure are checked at the level of sets. The Boolean model is justified by an explicit closure operator and is compatible with permutations and the canonical neighborhood duality map. Rank-selected Boolean shellability concentrates rational homology in degree \(r=n-2k\). Ribbon character expansion uses the ordinary augmented chain module and Pieri/Schur theory. The new twisted-character formula is proved through a bijection between invariant chains and weighted parity-orbit poset chains, including the orientation reversal and reduced empty-chain term. Independently implemented direct chain enumeration agrees in 90 exact instances; the ribbon expansions agree in 25 instances. Remaining uncertainty concerns bibliographic coverage, not a known missing proof step.

**Originality: PASS under the searched corpus, with a literature risk.** Kozlov already derives the nonequivariant Boolean model and wedge-of-spheres type and discusses the neighborhood involution; McNamara–van Willigenburg classify multiplicity-free ribbon functions; Nilakantan–Singh determine ordinary Kneser neighborhood homotopy in two families. None of these inspected treatments supplies the **mixed character of the canonical duality involution combined with every coordinate permutation**, which the weighted-even-cycle formula now computes. Related ordinary symmetric-group characters for small fixed subset ranks do not determine the duality twist. The fact that a new formula can be derived using earlier structural machinery is acknowledged; an unindexed explicit formula for the same mixed action would supersede novelty.

**Value: PASS.** For Kneser graph neighborhood complexes, the duality involution is intrinsic to the construction used in topological graph coloring. The joint character is a structural invariant not specified by the known homotopy ranks or by the coordinate-permutation representation alone. The explicit computation for all parameters and all mixed group elements—including the nontrivial \(-15\) trace at \((n,k)=(9,4)\)—makes the relation reproducible and reusable. The claim does not rely on arbitrary numerically chosen slices or on extending a previously recorded parameter table by one row.
