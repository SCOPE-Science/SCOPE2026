# Review: dyadic invariance of paperfolding cyclic complexity

**Correctness.** PASS. The even-decimation identity and alternating odd-position bits prove uniqueness of an alternating parity track at lengths at least eight. For lengths divisible by four, cyclic rotation preserves the alternating track's parity relation; lifting by interleaving constructs every doubled factor and is bijective on conjugacy classes. The proof treats boundary values and both starting-position parities. The standalone checker exhaustively verifies 20 nontrivial pairs and the exception at four, using Allouche's factor-count identity for finite-set completeness.

**Originality.** PASS within inspected evidence, with residual risk. Cassaigne–Fici–Sciortino–Zamboni, arXiv:1402.5843, Section 5.2 and Proposition 12, prove only the power-of-two family \(c_f(4\cdot2^j)=4\). Allouche (1992) proves ordinary factor complexity, not the new cyclic-class scale invariance. The new claim covers *every* length divisible by four from eight onward, and proves an explicit class bijection. Searches for factor conjugacy, dyadic necklaces, and doubling equivalence found no stronger recorded theorem, but an uncatalogued equivalent remains possible.

**Mathematical value.** PASS. The previously isolated dyadic power-of-two formula becomes a structural invariance law along every dyadic ray rooted at a multiple of four. It yields infinitely many new exact cyclic-complexity values (such as \(16\) on the ray from \(12\) and \(28\) on the ray from \(20\)), a constructive necklace bijection, and a sharp small-length exception. This is more than a fixed small enumeration or rephrasing of ordinary factor complexity.

**Limits.** This is not asserted for arbitrary generalized paperfolding sequences or lengths not divisible by four. Earlier published results and accessible mathematical-record comparisons do not establish absolute priority. The finite verifier has no bearing on independence of an external audit.
