# Eight all-smooth binary plane-filling quartic pencils and their Singer base schemes

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbf F_2\), and let \(V=k[X,Y,Z]_4\) be the vector space of homogeneous ternary quartics. A quartic is **plane-filling** when it vanishes at every rational point of \(\mathbf P^2(k)\). Define an **all-smooth plane-filling pencil** to be a two-dimensional \(k\)-linear subspace \(W\subseteq V\) for which every nonzero member cuts out a geometrically smooth plane curve and is plane-filling. Since \(k=\mathbf F_2\), such a pencil has three nonzero members.

**Theorem.** There are exactly \(8\) all-smooth plane-filling pencils. They form one orbit under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), whose order is \(168\); a pencil has stabilizer of order \(21\). The total number of smooth plane-filling quartics is \(24\), and each belongs to exactly one such pencil. Each pencil has a common cyclic projective subgroup of order \(7\), and the scheme-theoretic intersection of any two distinct members of a pencil is supported at exactly ten geometric points: the seven rational points, all of intersection multiplicity \(1\), and three Frobenius-conjugate \(\mathbf F_8\)-points, all of intersection multiplicity \(3\). The three latter points are the eigenpoints of the common cyclic subgroup.

One representative pencil is spanned by
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&X^3Y+X^3Z+X^2Y^2+XZ^3+Y^3Z+Y^2Z^2,\\
G={}&X^3Y+X^2YZ+X^2Z^2+XY^3+XY^2Z+XZ^3+Y^3Z+YZ^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Its third nonzero member is \(F+G\); all three are geometrically smooth.

## Assumptions and scope
Every coefficient, coordinate transformation and rational point is over \(k=\mathbf F_2\), unless an algebraic closure is expressly stated. Smoothness means **geometric** smoothness over \(\overline{k}\), not merely nonsingularity at rational points. Distinct pencils are distinct linear subspaces in fixed coordinates; projective orbit counts identify them under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\). The base scheme is defined by any two linearly independent quartics in a pencil; local intersection multiplicities are computed over \(\overline{k}\). The theorem does not classify pencils in other degrees or fields, nor curves up to arbitrary abstract isomorphism.

## Proof
**1. All plane-filling quartics.** For a matrix \(A\in M_3(k)\), with \(\mathbf X=(X,Y,Z)^t\), set
\[
F_A=\det(A\mathbf X,\mathbf X,\mathbf X^{[2]}),\qquad \mathbf X^{[2]}=(X^2,Y^2,Z^2)^t.
\]
The determinant is homogeneous of degree four and vanishes at each rational projective point, since \(\mathbf X^{[2]}=\mathbf X\) for a nonzero binary vector. The linear map \(A\mapsto F_A\) has kernel exactly \(kI_3\). Indeed, \(F_A=0\) forces the coefficient of each of the six monomials \(X^3Y,X^3Z,Y^3X,Y^3Z,Z^3X,Z^3Y\) to identify all off-diagonal entries as zero, and its three remaining independent mixed coefficients force the diagonal entries to agree; equivalently, direct elimination of the 15 coefficients gives this kernel. Hence its image has dimension eight. Evaluation of the 15 degree-four monomials at the seven rational projective points has rank seven, so its kernel has dimension eight. Therefore \(\{F_A\}\) is exactly the full space of \(256\) plane-filling quartics.

**2. Exact test for geometric smoothness.** For any quartic \(H\), consider
\[
\Phi_H:S_6\oplus S_7^{\oplus3}\longrightarrow S_{10},\qquad
(B,C,D,E)\longmapsto BH+CH_X+DH_Y+EH_Z.
\]
The target has dimension \(66\). The map is surjective if and only if the four forms \(H,H_X,H_Y,H_Z\) have no common projective zero over \(\overline{k}\). One implication is evaluation at a hypothetical common zero. Conversely, if the forms have no common projective zero, three general degree-four elements of their degree-four ideal component constitute a homogeneous regular sequence. The quotient has socle degree \(4+4+4-3=9\), so its degree-ten component vanishes. This proves the converse and the exact test for geometric smoothness. (The equation itself must be included in characteristic two; Euler's degree-four identity does not recover it from the derivatives.)

The first standalone verifier constructs the \(66\)-row matrix of \(\Phi_H\) for every one of the \(256\) plane-filling quartics and uses exact binary elimination. Exactly \(24\) have rank \(66\). As a separate exact check, for both matrices \(A\) representing a given \(F_A\), rank \(66\) is equivalent to \(A\) having neither a zero nor a nonzero binary eigenvector; equivalently its characteristic cubic is irreducible over \(k\). Thus \(48\) of the \(512\) matrices represent the \(24\) smooth quartics. The classifications of individual smooth plane-filling quartics by matrix type predate this result; only the pencil-level conclusions below are claimed as new.

**3. All pencils and one orbit.** Over \(k\), the nonzero forms of the pencil generated by \(F\) and \(G\) are exactly \(F,G,F+G\). Thus the exhaustive condition is that these three masks all belong to the 24-element smooth set. Exact enumeration gives the following eight unordered triples of degree-four monomial bitmasks (in the lexicographic degree-four monomial order recorded in the verifier):

```
6670 10994 12540
6754 11150 12780
6842 10348 13014
6876 10558 13282
6970 11100 12390
6998 10794 12668
7082 10726 12876
7116 10430 13170
```

Each of the 24 forms occurs in exactly one triple. Exhaustive enumeration of all \(168\) invertible binary three-by-three matrices and exact polynomial substitution sends a representative pencil onto all eight; its stabilizer has order \(21\). Every member curve has a stabilizer of order \(7\), and the three member stabilizers coincide. A second independent implementation enumerates irreducible binary matrices modulo scalars, forms pencils through binary matrix addition, and performs the group-orbit computation by matrix conjugation; it returns the same counts.

**4. The base scheme.** Fix a pencil \(W\), two distinct forms \(F,G\in W\), and their common subgroup \(S\simeq C_7\). The group \(S\) is a Singer subgroup: its action on the seven points of \(\mathbf P^2(k)\) is free and transitive. Over \(\overline{k}\), it has precisely three eigenpoints, a Frobenius orbit of degree three defined over \(\mathbf F_8\); every other geometric orbit has size seven. Let \(Q\) be an eigenpoint and let an order-seven representative act on its vector by a primitive seventh root \(\lambda\). Each quartic in \(W\) is invariant under \(S\), because its binary coefficients admit no nontrivial projective scalar; hence \(F(Q)=F(\lambda Q)=\lambda^4F(Q)\). Since \(\lambda^4\ne1\), we have \(F(Q)=0\). Similarly \(G(Q)=0\), so all three eigenpoints lie in the base.

Each of the seven rational points is a transverse intersection: \(F,G,F+G\) are smooth there, so the three gradient vectors \(\nabla F,\nabla G,\nabla F+\nabla G\) are all nonzero; over \(k\) the first two are therefore distinct, proving local intersection multiplicity one. Smooth plane quartics are geometrically irreducible (otherwise distinct components would intersect and create singularities), so \(F\) and \(G\) share no component. Bézout's theorem gives total intersection length \(4\cdot4=16\). Besides the seven rational points and the three eigenpoints, any further geometric base point would contribute an entire orbit of seven distinct points, exceeding the remaining length \(16-7-3=6\). Thus the support is exactly the ten claimed points. The three eigenpoints are Frobenius conjugates and consequently have equal local intersection multiplicities, collectively \(16-7=9\); each has multiplicity \(3\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fano_pencils.py` and `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_singer_matrix.cpp -o verify_singer_matrix && ./verify_singer_matrix` in a directory containing the two standalone files. Both programs use integer and bitwise arithmetic only. The first verifies the 256-form classification, the exact 66-rank geometric smoothness test, all eight pencils, the \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\) orbit and stabilizer, and the common order-seven subgroups. The second independently verifies 48 irreducible matrices, 24 curves modulo scalar matrices, eight pencils, and the orbit/stabilizer by conjugation. Their actual outputs are included in `OUTPUT_MAIN.txt` and `OUTPUT_MATRIX.txt`. The scheme-theoretic multiplicities follow from the proved group action, transversality and Bézout, not from a finite-field point-count heuristic.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (first public preprint March 14, 2022), ask for pencils whose every rational member is smooth, and establish existence for sufficiently large finite fields. Their results do not state this complete simultaneous plane-filling/smoothness classification over \(\mathbf F_2\). Asgarli and Ghioca, *Plane-filling curves of small degree over finite fields* (first public preprint July 6, 2023), investigate individual plane-filling curves and higher degrees, not these eight binary quartic pencils. Homma and Kim, *Nonsingular plane filling curves of minimum degree over a finite field and their automorphism groups* (2009 preprint, 2013 journal), already classify individual minimal-degree smooth plane-filling curves via matrices and give automorphism information. The 24-form classification is accordingly **not** claimed as original. The present new claim is the exact compatibility-of-members classification at the level of pencils and their nonreduced base intersections.

## Limitations
This is a finite, exact classification, not an existence theorem over arbitrary fields. The two independent programs share the underlying matrix criterion for smooth individual forms; the first, unlike the second, additionally checks geometric smoothness directly by an algebraic Macaulay-rank criterion. The later base-scheme argument is mathematical rather than experimentally enumerated. A complete publicly accessible full-text comparison with all appendices of Homma and Kim's 2009/2013 paper and Homma's 2019 paper was not obtainable; an implicit or unindexed treatment of the exact eight pencils remains a bibliographic priority risk. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
