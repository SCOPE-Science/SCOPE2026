# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The cubic degree and the exact count of \(21\) graph edges force any optimum to have a unique point and line in each colour class. The resulting \(5040\) mappings are exhausted with explicit incidence and pair-uniqueness tests, finding \(144\) nonincident mappings and precisely eight with all \(21\) pairs unique. A genuinely different \(135135\)-partition traversal obtains eight without assuming point-line pairing. The group order \(168\), transitive orbit of size eight, and stabiliser \(21\) are computed by enumerating all seven-point permutations preserving Fano blocks. Both programs replay the recorded output.

## Originality
**PASS with residual risk.** Buratti et al. already prove existence of harmonious seven-colourings for all Desarguesian projective planes (so the value seven is known), and Stinson provides an explicit order-seven nesting. Neither source's inspected material claims the complete count and single-orbit classification. Searches of the published mathematical-record catalogue and accessible research histories found no corresponding result; this does not prove uniqueness. The classical 1985 nesting-classification literature has not been compared in full, so a previously tabulated enumeration remains possible.

## Value
**PASS.** For the smallest projective-plane Banff design this counts and classifies *all* optimum assignments and shows no inequivalent solutions under collineations. It is a natural exact classification in the nested-Steiner-system programme, not a parameter artificially fixed to obtain a number. The known existence theorem supplies none of the number-eight or stabiliser-order-21 information.

## Scope and limits
The proof is finite, specific to the Fano-plane incidence graph, and uses exhaustive arithmetic over seven-point permutations. It does not generalise the enumeration to larger planes. External independent audit remains outstanding.
