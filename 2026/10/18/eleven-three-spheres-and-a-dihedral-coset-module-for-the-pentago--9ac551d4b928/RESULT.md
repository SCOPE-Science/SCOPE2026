# Eleven three-spheres and a dihedral coset module for the pentagonal-prism two-independence complex

## Finding
Let \(P=C_5\square K_2\) be the ten-vertex pentagonal-prism graph. The two-independence complex \(K=\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\) has integral homotopy type
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{11}S^3.
\]
The natural graph-automorphism group \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(P)\cong D_5\times C_2\), of order \(20\), acts on the only nonzero reduced rational homology as
\[
\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q[G/\langle ts\rangle]
\]
as a rational \(G\)-module. Here \(r\) rotates both pentagonal layers, \(s\) reflects both layers, and the central involution \(t\) exchanges the layers. The displayed coset module is the ten-dimensional permutation representation on left cosets of the order-two subgroup generated by \(ts\). In particular, the globally invariant rational third-homology subspace has dimension \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
Use the vertex set \(V(P)=\{(a,i):a\in\{0,1\},\ i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\}\), numbered \(5a+i\). Each \((a,i)\) is adjacent to \((a,i-1)\), \((a,i+1)\), and \((1-a,i)\). For any vertex subset \(S\), require every connected component of the *induced* graph \(P[S]\) to have at most two vertices. All its nonempty admissible subsets form the nonempty faces of \(K\); the empty face is implicit. Graphs are simple, and homotopy types refer to geometric realizations. The claim concerns this specified ten-vertex graph only; it makes no assertion for every circular ladder.

## Proof
**Exact finite simplicial model.** A connected graph on at least three vertices contains a (not necessarily induced) two-edge path on three distinct vertices. Hence a subset \(S\) is a face of \(K\) exactly when \(S\) contains no three vertices spanning two graph edges. The adjacency list above has fifteen edges and exactly thirty forbidden connected triples. The first accompanying verifier enumerates all \(2^{10}-1=1023\) nonempty subsets against these thirty triples. It obtains exactly \(230\) faces with dimension-by-dimension counts \((10,45,90,75,10)\). This is an exhaustive finite statement, not an inference from random sampling.

**Acyclic face-poset matching.** Encode every face by its ten-bit integer mask. The complete pairing certificate `morse_certificate.json` contains 109 pairs of masks \((F,F\cup\{v\})\). The pairs are generated by sequential toggling in the fixed vertex order \((3,7,6,8,9,1,0,2,4,5)\), restricted each time to the faces not previously paired. `verify_morse.py` reconstructs the face set from the graph, verifies each pair is a covering relation, checks that each face belongs to exactly one pair or the critical set, and independently regenerates the pairs from this order. It then reverses those matched arrows in the downward Hasse diagram and topologically sorts *all* \(230\) vertices and \(710\) covering arrows. The topological sort includes all vertices, proving that the matching is acyclic.

There remain exactly twelve critical nonempty faces: one vertex and eleven tetrahedra (three-dimensional simplices). By the discrete-Morse CW theorem, \(K\) is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell, no one- or two-cells, and eleven three-cells, and no cells in other positive dimensions. All attaching maps into the single zero-cell are constant, proving the asserted wedge of eleven three-spheres over the integers. In particular, reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{11}\) in degree three and zero otherwise.

**Equivariant Lefschetz character.** The two induced pentagonal cycles are the only five-cycles in \(P\), so every graph automorphism preserves or exchanges the two layers. Preservation forces one common dihedral action on the layers, and exchange adds the central involution; thus \(G\cong D_5\times C_2\). Each \(g\in G\) acts on simplicial chains by permuting vertex labels, with the ordinary orientation sign. Let
\[
L(g)=\sum_{d\ge0}(-1)^d\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_d(K;\mathbb Q)).
\]
The second verifier separately enumerates all faces by direct induced-component traversal, checks all twenty graph automorphisms, and computes \(L(g)\) from the signed fixed simplices in every degree. Since the just-proved homotopy type gives one-dimensional ordinary \(H_0\) and reduced rational homology only in degree three, \(L(g)=1-\chi_3(g)\). The complete class character is
\[
\chi_3(e)=11,\qquad \chi_3(tsr^k)=3\ (0\le k<5),\qquad \chi_3(g)=1\ \text{for the other fourteen elements}.
\]
To identify the module, consider the left action on the ten cosets of \(H=\langle ts\rangle\). Its permutation character takes value ten at the identity, two on the five conjugates of \(ts\), and zero elsewhere. Adding the trivial character therefore gives exactly \(\chi_3\). Character equality and semisimplicity over \(\mathbb Q\) yield \(\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q[G/H]\). Because the coset action is transitive, this representation has precisely two linearly independent \(G\)-fixed vectors.

## Verification
The pairing file is a complete finite certificate, not merely an experimental log. Running `python verify_morse.py morse_certificate.json` checks the 109 covering pairs, the complete 710-arrow reversed Hasse graph, acyclicity, and the twelve critical cells. Running `python verify_character.py` reconstructs the faces with a different criterion, confirms their mod-two Betti numbers \((0,0,0,11,0)\), and computes the full twenty-element rational character. It also constructs the ten cosets of \(\langle ts\rangle\) and directly compares the resulting permutation character. The recorded outputs are `morse_output.txt` and `character_output.txt`; the scripts derive their data from first principles. The matching certificate is small enough to be rechecked without external libraries.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs*, arXiv:2608.30601v2 (September 2026), prove that the **ordinary** independence complex of the pentagonal prism is homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\), and its perfect-matching complex is contractible. The present complex has the same graph vertices as ordinary independence, but allows induced two-vertex components and therefore is strictly larger; its eleven-dimensional third homology and equivariant coset-module decomposition do not follow from their results. Their perfect-matching complex instead has graph *edges* as vertices. Abdelmalek–Deshpande–Goyal–Roy–Singh, *Chordal graphs, higher independence and vertex decomposable complexes*, arXiv:2106.10863 (2021), establish broad higher-independence results for chordal families; the pentagonal prism has induced pentagonal cycles and is not chordal. Sawant, *On the Vertex Decomposability of \(r\)-Independence Complexes of Trees*, arXiv:2605.25150 (2026), concerns trees, not this cubic cyclic graph. The closest indexed homotopy classifications for higher independence of the Petersen graph use a different ten-vertex graph; they do not imply this computation.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-graph theorem, not a uniform classification of \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(C_n\square K_2)\). The rational representation is determined as a \(G\)-module; a canonical geometric basis and the integral \(\mathbb Z[G]\)-lattice decomposition are not asserted. Computational verification is exhaustive for the explicitly defined finite model; it makes no claims about any other graph. A targeted literature comparison found no covering theorem, but inaccessible or unindexed overlapping work may exist.
