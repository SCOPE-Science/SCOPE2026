# Equal-cycle symmetry fixed loci of complete directed-forest complexes

## Finding
Let \(k\ge2\) and \(d\ge2\) be integers, let \(m=kd\), and let \(X_m\) be the directed-forest complex of the complete bidirected graph on \([m]\). Its vertices are ordered arcs \(u\to v\), with \(u\ne v\); an arc set is a face when it has no directed cycles and every vertex has at most one incoming arc.

For a permutation \(g\) consisting of exactly \(k\) disjoint cycles of common length \(d\), the geometric fixed-point space satisfies
\[
\boxed{|X_m|^{\langle g\rangle}\simeq\bigvee^{(kd-1)^{k-1}}S^{k-2}.}
\]
More precisely, it has a **pure shellable triangulation** of dimension \(k-2\). Its only nonzero reduced integral homology is a free abelian group of rank \((kd-1)^{k-1}\) in degree \(k-2\).

For example, three disjoint transpositions on six vertices give a fixed-point space equivalent to \(25\) circles; four disjoint transpositions on eight vertices give \(343\) two-spheres. The case \(d=1\) recovers the familiar nonequivariant complete directed-forest theorem and is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
The arcs are distinct directed edges between distinct labeled vertices; opposite arcs are different vertices of the simplicial complex. A rooted forest has one parent for every non-root vertex, no parent for a root, and no directed cycles. The empty arc set is included as a face. The permutation acts by simultaneously relabeling both endpoints of each arc. Fixed points are taken in the **geometric realization**, not merely among the individually fixed simplicial vertices.

Write the cycles of \(g\) as \(C_1,\ldots,C_k\), each identified with \(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\) so that \(g(i,t)=(i,t+1)\). The group generated by \(g\) has order \(d\). There are no assertions for permutations with unequal cycle lengths, nor for the full strong Morse matching complex.

The complex \(X_m\) is the canonical vertex–edge-pair subcomplex identified in Theorem 9.3 of Knudson and Owens–White's 2026 study of strong discrete Morse matchings. That identification provides motivation and the verified recent topological setting; the present statement is directly about \(X_m\).

## Proof
**1. Geometric fixed points have a simplicial orbit model.** Given a simplicial permutation of any abstract finite simplicial complex, a point in a simplex is fixed exactly when its barycentric coordinates are constant on each vertex orbit under the permutation. The fixed subset of each invariant simplex is consequently a simplex whose vertices are normalized orbit sums of original vertices, and these simplices glue on their common faces. Therefore the geometric fixed-point space is naturally homeomorphic to the realization of the complex whose vertices are allowable full orbits of arc vertices and whose faces are collections of arc orbits whose union is a face of \(X_m\).

**2. Classify the allowable arc orbits.** Every arc from \((i,t)\) to \((j,t+a)\), for distinct cycle indices \(i,j\), generates exactly \(d\) arcs by applying \(g\), with the same phase difference \(a\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\). There are \(d\) phase choices per ordered pair of distinct cycles. An orbit of arcs with both endpoints in one cycle is not allowable: its union gives every vertex of that cycle an incoming arc and hence contains a directed cycle. For arcs between different cycles, two orbits with the same target cycle would give its vertices indegree at least two and are incompatible.

Consequently an allowable collection defines a parent map on cycle indices: each index either has no parent or has one distinct parent, with one of \(d\) possible phases attached to the parent choice. If the quotient parent map has a directed cycle, composing the phase translations around that cycle and iterating a finite number of times creates an actual directed cycle in the original arc union. Conversely, every directed cycle upstairs projects to a closed directed walk downstairs, so a quotient-directed cycle must occur. The allowable orbit collections are thus *exactly* the phase-colored rooted forests of the complete bidirected graph on \(k\) vertices.

**3. Shellability survives phase-color expansion.** Let \(D_k\) denote the ordinary complete directed-forest complex on \(k\) vertices. It is pure of dimension \(k-2\): any forest with more than one component can be enlarged by directing an edge from one component's root to a different root. Kozlov's shellability theorem for directed-tree complexes with a complete source, also presented in Jojić's treatment, applies to \(D_k\).

The fixed-point orbit complex is the *vertex expansion* \(D_k^{(d)}\): replace each directed-arc vertex of \(D_k\) by \(d\) clones and, in any face, select at most one clone for each original arc. For clarity, here is the shelling argument. Take a shelling \(F_1,\ldots,F_s\) of the pure base complex and enumerate the expanded facets first by the index of \(F_i\), then lexicographically by their choices of clones. If an earlier expanded facet comes from a strictly earlier base facet, the shelling property supplies an earlier base facet meeting the new base facet in a ridge containing their intersection; choose matching clone labels on the common base vertices to obtain the required earlier expanded ridge. If both expansions come from the same base facet, change the clone at the first lexicographically differing base vertex back to its value in the earlier facet. This gives the required earlier expanded ridge containing their intersection. Thus the expanded complex is pure shellable of dimension \(k-2\).

A nonempty pure shellable complex of dimension \(r\ge0\) has the homotopy type of a wedge of \(r\)-spheres (with the usual interpretation of a wedge of zero-spheres), so only the multiplicity remains to be determined.

**4. Count spheres with a signed forest determinant.** For each rooted forest \(F\) on the \(k\) quotient vertices, there are exactly \(d^{|E(F)|}\) independent phase assignments. Its alternating face sum, including the empty face, is
\[
Z_{k,d}=\sum_{F\text{ a rooted forest on }[k]}(-d)^{|E(F)|}.
\]
The directed matrix–forest determinant identity gives \(Z_{k,d}=\det(I-dL_k)\) where \(L_k=kI-J\) is the Laplacian of the complete undirected graph on \(k\) vertices; the orientation toward a root is uniquely determined by the underlying rooted forest. The eigenvalues of \(L_k\) are \(0\) once and \(k\) with multiplicity \(k-1\), so
\[
Z_{k,d}=(1-kd)^{k-1}.
\]
The reduced Euler characteristic of the fixed-point orbit complex is \(-Z_{k,d}\). Since all its reduced homology is free and concentrated in degree \(k-2\), its sphere multiplicity is
\[
(-1)^{k-2}[-(1-kd)^{k-1}]=(kd-1)^{k-1}.
\]
This proves the claimed integral homotopy classification for every \(k\ge2\), \(d\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens–White (2026) identify the complete directed-forest model inside the simplex strong discrete Morse matching complex and give its ordinary wedge rank, but not the geometric fixed-point homotopy types for equal-cycle permutations. Kozlov (1999) proves the underlying shellability for directed-tree complexes; Jojić (2013) revisits the complete-graph case. Moradi and Khosh-Ahang (2016) establish that simplicial vertex expansion preserves shellability. These results supply the separately cited inputs. Neither a shellability theorem alone nor an equivariant Euler-character calculation alone specifies the fixed subspace and its homotopy type: the orbit-face identification and the exact colored-forest count connect them.

A separate published result titled *Cycle-divisibility determinant characters of isolated rooted-forest complexes* treats the related **isolated-vertex subcomplex**, not this complete forest complex, and computes rational homology traces, not geometric fixed-point homotopy types. The present result makes no claim of newness for the weighted forest determinant itself. Targeted searches found no explicit equivalent or stronger fixed-space classification; this is not a proof that one cannot exist.

## Limitations
The theorem requires every cycle of the chosen permutation to have the same length. It concerns the canonical complete directed-forest subcomplex, not an equivariant deformation retraction of the full strong Morse complex; none is asserted. It is an integral homotopy-type statement but does not determine integral homology representation lattices or all subgroup quotient spaces. The novelty assessment remains contingent on the indexed and accessible literature; independent audit has not been performed.
