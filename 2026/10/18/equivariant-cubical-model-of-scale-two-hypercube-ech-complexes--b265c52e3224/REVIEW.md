# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof explicitly constructs an equivariant incidence-cone intermediary, retracts every center cone onto its base simplex through a compatible linear deformation, and removes only matched face pairs with unique generating centers. The uniqueness follows from the exact common-neighborhood intersection calculation for distinct cube vertices. The residual vertices, edges and triangles are identified bijectively with every cell in a prescribed triangulation of the \((n+1)\)-cube's complete two-skeleton. The parity-coordinate lift intertwines every affine cube symmetry, so the equivalence is equivariant in the strong fixed-point-preserving sense. The fixed-point consequences follow from reflected coordinate counts and the fact that a fixed point of a three-cycle cannot have three coordinates strictly between zero and one in a cubical two-skeleton. A separate geometric fixed-space computation through orbit-barycenter simplices confirms the two-component fixed homology for dimensions three through five. A standalone check confirms all 492 specified automorphism cases and exhaustive collapses for \(n\le5\); it is a diagnostic, not an infinite proof.

## Value — PASS

The higher-dimensional cubical two-skeleton is a natural, explicit, symmetry-preserving topological model for a natural infinite sequence of Čech nerves. It produces subgroup fixed-point topologies and a tractable integral cubical cell structure—information not encoded in earlier nonequivariant homotopy ranks or full rational homology characters. The uniform three-cycle fixed-point classification is a concrete nontrivial consequence, yielding two disjoint two-spheres for a five-dimensional base graph, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limits and sources

The theorem concerns finite graph-centered radius-one nerves and the full affine graph automorphism group, for integer \(n\ge1\), not other scales or Euclidean Čech complexes. Finite checks cover at most five dimensions. Source [1] Adams, Shukla, Singh, *Čech complexes of hypercube graphs*, arXiv:2212.05871, sections 2–4; source [2] Matsushita, *Closed neighborhood complexes of graphs*, arXiv:2511.12608.
