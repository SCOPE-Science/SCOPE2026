# Scientific review: three-leaf-star random adsorption

**Correctness — PASS.** Independent finite-state physical enumeration agrees exactly with the reduced count chain for 25 small bipartite substrates, and seeded simulations approach the proposed constant. More importantly, the stated chain rates follow by symmetry of surviving random priorities; on compact interior regions its uniformly convergent drift and bounded increments establish the fluid equations. Differentiating the exact invariant produces zero. The endgame is controlled by the fact that each remaining accepted star consumes at least one vertex from the originally small class, and a balanced start can leave only an arbitrarily small fraction after a sufficiently late interior stopping time. This proves the law in probability and expectation without extrapolating finite experiments.

**Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk.** The full accessible 2012 Bennett–Bohman hypergraph random-greedy paper treats regular hypergraphs, not unequal-density star-packings with two vertex-degree classes and a positive limiting unsaturated fraction. The 1994 de Oliveira–Tomé paper deals with inhomogeneous site adsorption on spatial bipartite lattices. The 1986 Hell–Kirkpatrick paper concerns existence and optimization of star factors; its full text could not be inspected, but its abstract makes no claim about randomized terminal distributions. Related path-of-length-two greedy adsorption has an algebraic jamming law; neither that result nor the cited regular-hypergraph theory implies the new essential-singularity formula. These are statement/assumption comparisons rather than inferences from an unsuccessful title search. Remaining risk: an unlocated specialized exact treatment of this same star process could exist.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact profile for a natural four-vertex adsorption object is not a parameter substitution in the three-vertex-path law: its approach to balance is infinitely flat, as opposed to a nontrivial power. The perfect-factor witness at fixed imbalance quantifies an extensive kinetic loss even where complete coverage is combinatorially possible. The distinction is directly relevant to irreversible packing versus static matching capacity.

Limitations: fixed limiting imbalance only; no central crossover fluctuation law, finite-size error rate, or quench-time distribution. A full bibliographic uniqueness guarantee and independent scientific audit are unavailable.
