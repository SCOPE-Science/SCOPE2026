# Review of the full partial-ASM Euclidean incenter classification

## Correctness

PASS. The exact prefix-sum inequality theorem supplies all containing halfspaces, with explicit Frobenius normal lengths. The bound from the first-row entries and the full first-row sum is sharp at the constant matrix. Equalities force the complete first row (and the first column in square shape), and perturbation witnesses make every other support inequality strict. This proves the affine dimensions and the universally tangent facets, not just feasibility of one ball. The accompanying decimal checker covers 1521 finite shapes as corroboration.

## Originality

PASS, qualified. Heuer–Striker (arXiv:2012.09901) determines the inequalities and irredundant facets but does not state Euclidean inradii, incenter affine hulls or universal tangencies. Heuer–Solhjem–Striker (arXiv:2403.02204) studies zero-pattern faces and order/flow equivalences, not the same ambient metric question. Indexed results on partial assignment and partial permutohedron geometry concern other bodies. There remains a possibility of an unindexed equivalent optimization or a stronger inaccessible result.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The theorem classifies an all-dimensional, two-parameter canonical geometric invariant, including the full optimal-center locus, its unexpectedly high affine dimension, a square-versus-rectangle change in forced equalities, and exact minimal and constant-center facet contacts. The result is not a statement about arbitrary affine models or metric-preserving projections.

## Limitations

The classification does not enumerate all vertices or face lattices of the incenter loci. The proof is analytical; finite checks only corroborate examples. Bibliographic coverage and independent mathematical validation remain incomplete.
