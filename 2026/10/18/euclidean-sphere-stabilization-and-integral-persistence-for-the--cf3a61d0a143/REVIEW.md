No finite experiment is extrapolated to other finite configurations.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The closest exact same-vertex result, *Complete graph-metric Vietoris–Rips filtration of the truncated octahedral graph*, explicitly uses shortest-path rather than Euclidean distance. Its graph-distance-three stage coalesces 24 Euclidean pairs at each of squared lengths 8, 10, and 12, so it does not give the newly determined earlier sphere threshold or its inclusion maps. The separate permutahedron/Kendall barcode also uses an intrinsic graph metric. Adamaszek's graph-power theorems treat graph powers, the Adamaszek–Adams classification treats subsets of a circle, and Saleh–Titz Mite–Witzel treat the five Platonic solids and explicitly name Archimedean solids as a potential extension. Those results do not imply this precise chordal-metric filtration. Searches under truncated octahedron, permutahedron, Euclidean Rips, shell thresholds, and alternate metric formulations found no direct covering statement; that absence alone does not prove novelty. Residual risk: an unindexed Euclidean polyhedral computation or stronger general result might already imply the claim.

The truncated octahedron is a natural vertex-transitive Archimedean polytope and three-dimensional permutahedron. In this example, the Euclidean metric resolves a single graph-distance-three shell into three distinct thresholds, and the exact topological stabilization occurs at the first threshold, not just at the known graph-metric endpoint.
