# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The arbitrary-parameter facet system is Theorem 2.3(c) of Bayer et al. Averaging every upper size-\(k\) inequality and combining with all lower-coordinate inequalities bounds any enclosed ball by \((k+\sqrt{k})r\le U_k-k\). The proposed constant center meets every facet inequality; equality forces a unique center, and distances identify every contact family. The phase-realization construction reduces to the strictly increasing transition numbers \(T_j\), then perturbs and scales its limiting parameters into positive integers. All boundary cases \(n=2\) and \(b_1=1\) were checked analytically. The standalone finite verifier corroborates the support and containment identities but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The 2022 original studies the two-parameter subfamily; the complete 2024 original and its 2025 journal version prove facets, vertices, polymatroid structure and graph/circuit diameter rather than this Euclidean inball/contact-phase classification. Searches for parking-polytope Euclidean inradii, symmetric polymatroid inballs, and equivalent Chebyshev-center terminology located no covering primary theorem. A previously known two-parameter radius formula does not imply the arbitrary contact-size realization statement. An uncatalogued general inball theorem for symmetric polymatroids could entail the radius formula, and remains a priority risk; no universal novelty certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural complete metric classification of an established polytope family, including the exact center and every facet contact. The strongest structural point is not the scalar finite minimum alone: every one of its \(n\) competitors is attained as a strictly unique phase by positive integral parameters, including intermediate cardinalities absent from the two-parameter geometry. It gives a reproducible obstruction to extrapolating endpoint-only behavior, and handles an unbounded sequence of dimensions without exhaustive enumeration.
