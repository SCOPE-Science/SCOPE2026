# Every interior neutralized-entropy gap in a minimal Cantor odometer

## Finding

For every pair of real numbers \(0<L<U<1\), there exist a compact Cantor set \(K\subset[0,1]\), a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism \(T:K\to K\) for the **ordinary Euclidean distance**, and an invariant probability measure \(\mu\) such that:

1. \(T\) is minimal, uniquely ergodic, and has ordinary topological entropy zero; \(\mu\) is its unique invariant probability measure.
2. Every iterate \(T^m\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\), has the **same** bi-Lipschitz constant, independent of \(m\).
3. For **every** \(r>0\), the neutralized topological entropies and the neutralized Pfister–Sullivan entropies of \(\mu\) satisfy

\[
\underline h_{\mathrm{top}}^{r}(T,K)
 =\underline h_{\mu}^{\mathrm{PS},r}(T,K)=rL,
\qquad
\overline h_{\mathrm{top}}^{r}(T,K)
 =\overline h_{\mu}^{\mathrm{PS},r}(T,K)=rU.
\]

The lower and upper integrated neutralized Brin–Katok entropies of \(\mu\) are also \(rL\) and \(rU\), respectively. Moreover, \(\underline{\dim}_{B}K=L\) and \(\overline{\dim}_{B}K=U\). All these systems, for every choice of \((L,U)\), are mutually topologically conjugate to the binary adding machine. Thus a strict upper/lower neutralized entropy gap persists even under minimality, unique ergodicity, and uniform-in-time bi-Lipschitz control; every interior gap profile is realized on a subset of the real line.

## Assumptions and scope

The metric throughout is the Euclidean distance inherited from \([0,1]\); in particular it is not selected by changing the neutralized scale after the system is constructed. The map is defined on a Cantor subset, not asserted to extend to an interval diffeomorphism.

For \(n\ge1\), define the forward Bowen metric \(d_n(x,y)=\max_{0\le j<n}|T^jx-T^jy|\). For a nonempty subset \(Z\subseteq K\), let \(s_n(Z,\varepsilon)\) denote the largest cardinality of a subset of \(Z\) whose distinct points have \(d_n\ge\varepsilon\). Set \(E_n(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{T^jx}\) and \(K_{n,F}=\{x:E_n(x)\in F\}\) for an open weak-star neighborhood \(F\) of \(\mu\). Following the definitions in Chen–Xie–Zhou [1], for \(r>0\),

\[
\underline h_\mu^{\mathrm{PS},r}
=\inf_{F\ni\mu}\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log s_n(K_{n,F},e^{-nr}),
\qquad
\overline h_\mu^{\mathrm{PS},r}
=\inf_{F\ni\mu}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log s_n(K_{n,F},e^{-nr}).
\]

The topological versions replace \(K_{n,F}\) with \(K\). Neutralized Brin–Katok quantities integrate the corresponding pointwise lower/upper limits of \(-n^{-1}\log\mu(B_n(x,e^{-nr}))\). Here all the latter limits have the same lower and upper values uniformly in the center, so there is no integration subtlety.

## Proof

**1. Constructing a uniform nonstationary Cantor set.** Put \(u=2^{-1/L}\) and \(v=2^{-1/U}\); then \(0<u<v<1/2\). Partition the positive integers into consecutive blocks of lengths \(b_j=2^{j^2}\), \(j=1,2,\ldots\). Choose \(c_k=u\) when \(k\) belongs to an odd-numbered block and \(c_k=v\) otherwise. Define \(a_0=1\), \(a_k=\prod_{j=1}^k c_j\), and

\[
\Phi(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-c_k)a_{k-1}\omega_k,
\qquad \omega=(\omega_1,\omega_2,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}.
\]

The weights telescope: \(\sum_{j>k}(1-c_j)a_{j-1}=a_k\). Thus the series converges uniformly and takes values in \([0,1]\). Let \(K=\Phi(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N})\). If \(\omega\ne\eta\) first differ in coordinate \(k\), their leading contributions have magnitude \((1-c_k)a_{k-1}\), while the whole remaining tail difference has magnitude at most \(a_k\). Therefore, writing \(\delta=1-2v>0\),

\[
\delta a_{k-1}\le |\Phi(\omega)-\Phi(\eta)|\le a_{k-1}. \tag{1}
\]

In particular \(\Phi\) is an injective continuous embedding and \(K\) is a Cantor set. Level-\(k\) cylinders are \(2^k\) disjoint pieces of Euclidean diameter at most \(a_k\); their points are pairwise separated across distinct cylinders by at least \(\delta a_{k-1}\). In the source product space define the ultrametric \(d_*(\omega,\eta)=a_{k-1}\) when the first discrepancy is \(k\), and zero on the diagonal. Inequality (1) says that \(\Phi\) is uniformly bi-Lipschitz from \(d_*\) to the Euclidean metric.

**2. Odometer dynamics.** View digit strings in least-significant-first order as the compact additive group \(\mathbb Z_2\) of binary integers. Set \(T=\Phi\circ(\omega\mapsto\omega+1)\circ\Phi^{-1}\), and let \(\mu\) be pushforward of Haar probability. Translation by any integer preserves congruence modulo \(2^k\) in both directions; consequently it preserves the index of the first differing bit. Thus \(d_*\) is invariant under every \(T^m\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\), and (1) yields uniformly in \(m,x,y\):

\[
\delta|x-y|\le |T^m x-T^m y|\le\delta^{-1}|x-y|. \tag{2}
\]

The subgroup generated by \(1\) is dense in \(\mathbb Z_2\), so the homeomorphism is minimal. To prove unique ergodicity without invoking an external classification, note that each residue class modulo \(2^k\) is visited by an orbit of length \(n\) with frequency differing from \(2^{-k}\) by at most \(1/n\), uniformly over its starting point. Every continuous function on \(\mathbb Z_2\) is uniformly approximated by functions constant on these classes. Hence ergodic averages converge uniformly to Haar integrals, and Haar is the only invariant probability. At any fixed scale the Bowen separated-set cardinalities are uniformly bounded in \(n\), by (2); therefore ordinary topological entropy is zero.

**3. Exact exponential-scale counting.** For \(t=e^{-nr}\), let \(k(n)\) be the least integer \(k\ge1\) with \(a_k<t\), and let \(j(n)\) be the least integer with \(a_j<t/\delta\) (defined for all sufficiently large \(n\)). If two points have the same first \(k(n)\) digits then, by (1) and invariance of \(d_*\), their Bowen distance is at most \(a_{k(n)}<t\). Hence at most one member of an \((n,t)\)-separated set lies in each such cylinder, proving \(s_n(K,t)\le2^{k(n)}\).

Conversely, choose one point in each cylinder of length \(j(n)\). For points in distinct cylinders, their first discrepancy \(\ell\le j(n)\) ensures, by (1), \(d_n\ge|x-y|\ge\delta a_{j(n)-1}\ge t\), so \(s_n(K,t)\ge2^{j(n)}\). Since \(c_i\le v<1\) and the thresholds \(t\) and \(t/\delta\) differ by a fixed factor, \(0\le k(n)-j(n)\le C\) for a constant \(C\) depending only on \(u,v\). Thus

\[
\log s_n(K,e^{-nr})=k(n)\log2+O(1), \tag{3}
\]

with the \(O(1)\) independent of \(n\) and \(r\) when \(r\) is fixed. Write \(S_k=-\log a_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\log(1/c_i)\). The defining inequalities for \(k(n)\) show \(S_{k(n)}=rn+O(1)\), because the increments of \(S_k\) lie in the fixed interval \([\log(1/v),\log(1/u)]\). Moreover,

\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{k\log2}{S_k}=L,
\qquad
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{k\log2}{S_k}=U. \tag{4}
\]

Indeed the ratios always lie between \(L\) and \(U\), and at the end of the \(j\)-th block the length \(2^{j^2}\) dominates all previous block lengths, so the ratio tends to \(L\) along odd endpoints and \(U\) along even endpoints. For any fixed \(r>0\), choosing an integer \(n\) nearest to \(S_k/r\) at each endpoint changes the cutoff index by at most a bounded number of levels; thus both limits in (4) are attained in the corresponding \(n\)-indexed subsequences. Equations (3)–(4) prove the topological entropy formulas.

The same cylinder argument at an arbitrary radius \(t\) gives \(\log N(K,t)=k(t)\log2+O(1)\), proving \(\underline{\dim}_B K=L\) and \(\overline{\dim}_B K=U\).

**4. Pfister–Sullivan localization and local measure.** Unique ergodicity from Step 2 yields uniform weak-star convergence \(E_n(x)\to\mu\). For **every** neighborhood \(F\ni\mu\), therefore, \(K_{n,F}=K\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). The PS separated-set counts eventually coincide exactly with the global counts, proving both PS identities without any interchange of liminf, limsup and infimum.

Each cylinder of length \(k(n)\) has Haar mass \(2^{-k(n)}\) and is contained in \(B_n(x,e^{-nr})\) whenever it contains \(x\). The ball, in turn, lies inside the length-\(j(n)\) cylinder through \(x\). Consequently, for every \(x\in K\),

\[
2^{-k(n)}\le\mu(B_n(x,e^{-nr}))\le2^{-j(n)}.
\]

Because the indices differ by at most \(C\), \(-\log\mu(B_n(x,e^{-nr}))=k(n)\log2+O(1)\) **uniformly** in \(x\). Integrating pointwise liminf/limsup proves the stated Brin–Katok identities. Finally, every \(T\) constructed above is conjugate through \(\Phi\) to translation by \(1\) on \(\mathbb Z_2\), so all examples are mutually conjugate. This finishes the proof.

## Verification

`verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic in the representative case \(u=1/9\), \(v=1/3\). It checks first-discrepancy preservation under odometer translations and the full finite-level Euclidean–ultrametric comparison for all pairs of \(7\)-bit strings and several iterates. It also checks the endpoint block-averaging trend for block indices through \(8\). Run `python verify.py` from the directory containing this document and the script. The tests are finite consistency checks; (1)–(4) prove the unbounded and all-real-parameter assertions.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen, Xie and Zhou [1] define neutralized Pfister–Sullivan entropy (Definition 2.7) and in their Example 2.17 exhibit different lower and upper PS entropies for a particular ergodic measure on a **nonminimal, non-uniquely-ergodic** compact invariant set. Their equation (2.7) already identifies topological neutralized entropy with scaled upper/lower box dimensions for isometries: that formula is **prior work**, not a claim here. The present contribution is the explicit **entire interior pair-realization under minimality, unique ergodicity, Euclidean embedding, and a uniform bi-Lipschitz constant for every iterate**, with both local and PS formulas verified at once. The smooth whole-manifold agreement in their Theorem 1.5 concerns a different setting.

Classical nonautonomous Moran theory [2] supplies general fractal-dimension machinery and anticipates how varying contraction levels can separate dimensions. No novelty is asserted for the underlying dimension estimates in isolation, Haar unique ergodicity of adding machines, or the general isometry identity. The new comparison is the combined dynamical realization and simultaneous sharp PS and Brin–Katok profiles on one minimal uniquely ergodic system. An implicit consequence in wider odometer/fractal entropy theory cannot be categorically excluded.

## Limitations

The result realizes exactly the parameter range \(0<L<U<1\); endpoints are not claimed. It is metric-dependent, as neutralized entropy itself is. No smooth ambient extension or claim concerning mixing systems is made. The verifier treats finite levels and a rational sample only; it is not an infinite proof. Scientific priority has not been independently established and may depend on whether the combination of established geometric and dynamical ingredients is regarded as already implicit in previous work.
