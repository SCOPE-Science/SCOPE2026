# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem defines a projected Haar density, not a ternary representation density. Its proof rigorously partitions the two \(3\)-adic indices into critical and regular residue classes, invokes the local inverse theorem for regular triangular indices, makes a Haar-preserving square-root substitution on the doubly critical branch, and evaluates two auxiliary integrals using independent valuation shells. The critical normalizations are \(T(a)=(9A^2-1)/8\) and \(9/64=(3/8)^2\). The \(Q\) recursion is \(Q(c)=4/9+Q(c/9)/9\) when \(9\mid c\). At equal product and shift valuations, the conditional cancellation factors \(1/2\), \(1/8\), and \(3/8\) yield the stated \(H\) coefficients. The standalone exact-arithmetic experiment passed 405 finite comparisons, but is not the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The August 27, 2026 paper of Bagchi and Zhou-Zheng establishes a \(\pmod9\) nonexistence obstruction and discusses local solvability, whereas this result gives exact Haar densities in every congruence and valuation branch. The March 2026 Bliznac Trebješanin paper treats global Pell solvability and the same congruence obstruction. The August 2025 Gurvich–Naumova paper treats square products without shifts, by parametric integer constructions rather than local value distributions. General quadratic-form local-density techniques are older; a routine direct implication has not been identified, but an implicit specialization remains a risk. Published mathematical-result searches and accessible historical claims disclosed no matching statement. Absence from search is not a proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The prime three is the precise modulus at which an unconditional obstruction for triangular-number Diophantine pairs is known. Quantifying the proportion of surviving index pairs at every \(3\)-power gives a complete local factor, reveals strong variation among unobstructed parameters, and distinguishes positive local density from existence of integral solutions (e.g. \(n=12\) gives \(1/6\)). The exact factors can inform local sieving and arithmetic heuristics. The claim neither purports to count global pairs nor substitutes for a local-global theorem.
