# Scientific review: absorbing first shell of a quantum hypercube

**Correctness — PASS.** The no-jump dissipativity identity isolates all undamped eigenvectors as Hermitian eigenvectors vanishing at every lossy neighbor. The target shell generates exactly the radial and standard shell components, except for one zero-energy radial eigenvector when the dimension is even. Exact Krawtchouk amplitudes give the removed state and a rank/constant-diagonal calculation supplies all vertex absorption probabilities. The proof addresses arbitrary positive unequal sink rates; the finite numerical program separately reproduces 557 comparisons with maximum error below \(3.5\times10^{-12}\). The exhaustive claim is analytical, not inferred from samples.

**Originality — PASS in self-review, with residual risks.** The closest checked works are Le et al., arXiv:1707.07482 (general graph dark subspaces and single-sink transfer), and Thiel et al., arXiv:1906.08112 and arXiv:1909.02114 (single-target stroboscopic detection, including a hypercube example and symmetry bounds). Strauch, arXiv:0808.3403, treats a different hypercube decoherence mechanism. None of their inspected results gives the whole first Hamming shell as independent continuous absorbers, the full dark-space dimension for all dimensions, or the exact parity-sensitive sitewise law for unequal positive absorption rates. A generalized multi-target controllability theorem may imply the result after a nontrivial hypercube computation; this remains a priority risk.

**Scientific value — PASS.** This is a natural complete detector-placement classification: the smallest complete neighborhood of one vertex is a graph cut, yet coherent trapping survives on alternating dimension parity. The closed formulas quantify location-dependent failure probabilities and furnish a rate-robust design diagnostic for sink-based single-excitation networks. The result is not a finite-time decay bound, a many-body theorem, or a disorder-robustness result.
