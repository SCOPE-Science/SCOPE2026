# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The orthogonal orbit/twin decomposition proves the cardinality obstructions. The quotient determinant evaluations separate residual eigenvalues for unequal parts; in the balanced case the exact combined eigenspace yields the additional sharp cardinality threshold. In the homogeneous case the full-part zero-sum eigenspaces give the stronger criterion. Direct coefficient extraction proves every generating polynomial and minimum count. The included executable passed 19,840 exact finite-field rank comparisons across two primes; the proof, not the finite test, establishes the infinite family.

**Originality — PASS, subject to independent verification.** Yuan et al. give a general maximum-multiplicity/PBH framework, not the one-defective-edge, all-placement polynomial. Monshizadeh et al. address strong structural controllability for all compatible matrices, not one fixed weighted Laplacian; their result supplies a zero-forcing comparison rather than this classification. Klee and Stamps handle Laplacian spectra of related bipartite graph families, including perfect-matching deletion, but do not give this one-exception actuator-set enumeration. Prior comparisons of uniform matching perturbations and two-clique bridge graphs concern different Laplacian eigenspace geometries and do not imply this single-edge placement criterion. Specialized unindexed work could still give an equivalent result.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem resolves every independent-actuator placement on a natural rank-one perturbation of a dense interconnection network, revealing a discontinuity of two required inputs for unequal part sizes and one for equal sizes, even when the graph support does not change. The full generating function supplies exact counts at every actuator budget and isolates the difference between exact controllability and structural sufficient conditions. No quantitative assertion is made about energy or conditioning near the singular weight.
