# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact hypotheses are real separable Hilbert input, scalar output, ReLU activation, signed measures with total-variation objective, Euclidean unit ball for joint bias/weight, and equality for every input. Restriction to the line generated by the slope is nonexpansive for total variation. In one dimension, signed positive- and negative-slope measures have bounded weighted first moments. The vanishing distributional second derivative forces their equality up to sign at every kink; the slope and intercept consequently determine their first two moments. The Euclidean triangle inequality gives the exact scalar lower bound. Its elementary convex minimization matches an explicit two- or three-atom measure. The atom-count statement follows from the same kink obstruction and strict inequality above the transition. Both zero-slope and threshold boundary cases are checked. Finite diagnostics confirm, but do not substitute for, these analytic steps.

## Originality — PASS (subject to bibliographic residual risk)

Yang–Shi's 2026 Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.4/Appendix D were materially inspected. Proposition 2.4 computes the homogeneous linear Schatten-one norm; the biased affine exact norm and its atom-count transition are not stated there. The older complete Barron representation treatment of E–Wojtowytsch, especially §§2 and 4.1, yields an equivalent BV-type function norm, not this sharp equality, although its signed-kink decomposition is closely related and acknowledged. The 2024 multi-output variation-space study gives representer theory, not an identified all-line exact affine norm formula. published-finding corpus and broad literature searches considered aliases, bias penalties, affine functions, ReLU total variation, and exact thresholds. No directly covering implication was identified. An undiscovered equivalent under different normalization remains possible; searches alone never prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The augmented-bias norm is intrinsic to regularization of infinite-width neural approximations; the source's linear norm is sharp only when the bias vanishes. A bias-sensitive exact formula is needed to quantify the cost of intercepts and their tradeoff with slopes. The threshold at \(1/\sqrt3\) marks a nontrivial transition between two and three optimally supported atoms, identifies the optimal constant-neuron contribution, and is dimension independent. This is a natural complete classification of a core affine subspace, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or isolated numerical bound. No downstream theorem is claimed.

## Closest literature and limitations

Sources: Yang–Shi (2026), arXiv:2606.01244, §§1–2/Appendix D; E–Wojtowytsch (2020/2021), arXiv:2006.05982, §§2,4.1; Shenouda–Parhi–Lee–Nowak (2024), JMLR 25; Savarese–Evron–Soudry–Srebro (2019), COLT 99. Different regularizers, unpenalized affine skips, compact input domains, and only equivalent (rather than exact) norms do not entail the stated equality. The analysis is exact for the stated signed-measure convention; bibliographic priority and generalization beyond that convention remain open.
