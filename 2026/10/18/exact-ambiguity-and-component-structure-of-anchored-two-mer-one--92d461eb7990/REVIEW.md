# Scientific review: anchored two-mer read deletion

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reconstructs consistent and inconsistent received read vectors from the physical overlapping pairs, then derives the full graph by unit transfers of positive run lengths. Stars-and-bars degree counting and the spectral walk formula for the off-diagonal alphabet transition matrix verify the graph and edge identities for all allowed integer parameters, including the exceptional lengths one and two. Complete direct readout enumeration cross-checks 270 small parameter/boundary cases. Finite checks do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence
The 2025 Hanania–Yaakobi equivalence with a derivative-domain zero-deletion channel is acknowledged and expressly not claimed as original. The relevant 2026 overlapping-mer read-channel papers establish coding bounds and run-based mechanisms, but the examined statements do not give the complete graph decomposition, endpoint-independent edge formula, or inverse-readout histogram. The closest indexed exact graph enumeration concerns two common descendants of ordinary one-symbol deletion, not the channel studied here. A directly relevant 2020 article could only be assessed from its abstract; the possibility of an earlier equivalent zero-deletion graph enumeration remains a genuine risk.

## Value — PASS
The result determines natural exact graph and inverse-channel invariants needed to assess confusability and decoding ambiguity of a single missing ordered two-symbol read. It classifies all connected components and solves the exact edge count across all finite alphabet sizes, lengths, and fixed boundary symbols, with a non-obvious boundary-independence threshold. It does not claim optimal code sizes or multiple-error performance.
