# Scientific review: angular excursion and reused Gaussian range sketches

**Correctness — PASS.** Rotating to the eigenbasis yields the exact one-column projection error \(\varepsilon/(\cos^2\phi+\varepsilon^2\sin^2\phi)^{1/2}\). The projective Gaussian angle is uniform modulo \(\pi\); continuity makes the eigenline image an interval of length \(\alpha\) modulo \(\pi\). Its distance law has one atom of mass \(\alpha/\pi\) and constant off-arc density \(2/\pi\), yielding the finite-parameter CDF and mean. The logarithmic integral expansion and the pointwise \(L^2\) identity are derived in the proof. The provided standalone numerical checker passed its independent projection, quadrature, angular coverage, and asymptotic comparisons. Computations are not presented as infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The nearest primary source, Kressner and Lam (arXiv:2302.12761, §§2.3.1–2.3.2), studies fixed Gaussian sketch reuse and includes a uniform-error bound, but assumes multiple sketch columns with oversampling for the uniform guarantee. Its discontinuity remark concerns a rank-losing deterministic example rather than this full-rank rotated family, exact positive-probability uniform catastrophe, and complete angular CDF. The basic HMT source (arXiv:0909.4061, §§9–10), the Gaussian range-approximation analysis (arXiv:2206.08793, §3), and the 2026 sharp randomized-SVD bound (arXiv:2605.19096, §3) concern fixed targets, pointwise error, or a minimax expectation rather than a continuously parameterized supremum. The 2026 bounds have sampling-rank assumptions excluding the present one-column configuration. Published mathematical-record searches and comparison with accessible related results did not reveal a covering theorem. Related historical formulations of arc coverage and parametric subspace approximations remain possible; absence of a search hit is not treated as proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The question is a direct limiting case of fixed-sketch parameter-dependent randomized approximation: whether pointwise and integrated accuracy imply reliable uniform accuracy when the dominant eigenline moves. The exact law identifies the sharp angular probability, a distributional atom, the logarithmic residual contribution, and the failure of uniform convergence on an analytic, uniformly full-rank matrix family. This is a meaningful distinction for uniform error guarantees; it is not merely a fixed-matrix pointwise bound or arbitrary numerical sample.

**Scientific limitations.** The theorem is restricted to a one-column, unoversampled real planar range finder and constant eigenvalues, with no claim about oversampled or high-dimensional algorithms. Numerical tests were finite. A stronger equivalent result in unlocated historical literature cannot be excluded. Independent verification is not claimed.
