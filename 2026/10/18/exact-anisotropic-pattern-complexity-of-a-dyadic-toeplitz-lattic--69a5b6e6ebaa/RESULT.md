# Exact anisotropic pattern complexity of a dyadic Toeplitz lattice

## Finding

Consider the binary two-dimensional Toeplitz subshift \(X\) generated by the dyadic semicocycle of Gómez and Petite, Example 5.9 and Section 5.1, with scale \(2^k\mathbb Z^2\) and prime parameter \(2\). Equivalently, its language is generated by the array \(c\) with
\[
c(i,j)=\nu_2(\gcd(|i|,|j|))\bmod 2\quad ((i,j)\ne(0,0)),
\]
where \(\nu_2(0)=\infty\), and either symbol may be assigned at the single point \((0,0)\). The convention \(\gcd(0,|j|)=|j|\) is used. Here the language is independent of the assignment at the exceptional point.

For integers \(n,m\ge 1\), put \(R_{n,m}=\{0,\ldots,2^n-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,2^m-1\}\), and let \(p(n,m)\) be the number of distinct \(R_{n,m}\)-patterns in \(X\). Then the following **exact formula** holds for every \(n,m\ge1\):
\[
p(n,m)=
\begin{cases}
5\,4^{n-1},&n=m,\\
2^{n+m+1}-4^{\min\{n,m\}},&n\ne m.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the normalized complexity is exactly
\[
\frac{p(n,m)}{|R_{n,m}|}=
\begin{cases}
5/4,&n=m,\\
2-2^{-|n-m|},&n\ne m.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the same zero-entropy minimal \(\mathbb Z^2\) system has **no shape-independent normalized complexity limit along rectangular Følner sequences**: square dyadic windows have limit \(5/4\), while windows of dimensions \(2^{2k}\times2^k\) have limit \(2\). More precisely, the complete set of subsequential normalized-complexity limits along dyadic rectangles with both side lengths tending to infinity is
\[
\{5/4\}\ \cup\ \{2-2^{-j}:j\in\mathbb N,\ j\ge1\}\ \cup\ \{2\}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The action is the \(\mathbb Z^2\) shift and its language consists of all translated finite patches of \(c\). This is precisely the single-discontinuity, parity-of-dyadic-valuation construction in the source, not an arbitrary two-dimensional period-doubling substitution. Every count is over **distinct binary configurations on a fixed labeled window**, not over equivalence classes under rotations, reflections or translations of a window. Both exponents are positive integers; no formula for arbitrary non-dyadic side lengths is asserted.

## Proof

**1. The language and the singular site.** For any finite rectangle \(R\) with side lengths at most \(2^K\), each integer translate of \(c\) is determined by the translating vector modulo \(2^K\), except possibly at the unique site at which both shifted coordinates vanish modulo \(2^K\). All other sites have a common minimum valuation strictly below \(K\) and hence an unambiguous symbol. At the exceptional site (if present), both symbols can occur: independently alter the translating vector by multiples of \(2^K\) so that the minimum valuation there is respectively \(K\) and \(K+1\), without changing any other symbol in \(R\). The original construction's almost-one-to-one odometer extension includes the two singular-fiber choices. Consequently the language can be counted from this valuation rule, including both exceptional symbols; this rules out any hidden assumption about finite-depth truncation.

**2. Four-way decimation.** In any \(2^n\times2^m\) pattern there is a unique pair of coordinate parities at which *both* shifted coordinates are even. Every other parity class has symbol zero. On the active parity subrectangle, divide both coordinates by \(2\); the valuation decreases by one, so its symbols form the bitwise complement of a valid \(2^{n-1}\times2^{m-1}\) pattern. Conversely any such smaller pattern and any of the four parity pairs are realized by an integer translate. If \(n,m\ge2\), the smaller window has at least \(2\times2\) sites and no pattern on it is identically one (three parity classes are always zero). Hence its complement contains a one, uniquely revealing the active parity pair. These four sets of larger patterns are therefore disjoint, and
\[
p(n,m)=4p(n-1,m-1)\qquad(n,m\ge2).
\]
At the \(2\times2\) base window there is exactly one jointly even site. The four possible locations of a single one, together with the all-zero pattern, give \(p(1,1)=5\).

**3. The one-short-side base.** Fix a window of height two and width \(2^k\). Exactly one row is potentially nonzero; the other is identically zero. Let \(W_k\) be the set of length-\(2^k\) binary words
\[
w_{t,a}(j)=\min\{\nu_2(a+j),t\}\bmod2,
\quad 0\le j<2^k,\quad t\in\mathbb N,\ t\ge1,\quad a\in\mathbb Z.
\]
These are precisely the possible active rows: the even shifted second coordinate may be prescribed any finite valuation \(t\ge1\), and possible limiting singular values introduce no additional finite words. The first two cases are
\[
W_1=\{00,01,10\},\qquad
W_2=\{0001,0010,0100,0101,1000,1010\}.
\]
Every word of length at least four in this family contains a one, since any four consecutive arguments include one having valuation exactly one. For \(k\ge3\), choosing the parity of \(a\) yields two disjoint classes: one parity of positions is identically zero, and the other consists either of all ones when \(t=1\), or of the bitwise complement of an arbitrary word from \(W_{k-1}\) when \(t\ge2\). The all-ones active sequence is not obtained from a complemented \(W_{k-1}\) because no word of \(W_{k-1}\) is all zero. Every word is nonzero, so the parity classes cannot overlap. It follows that
\[
|W_k|=2(|W_{k-1}|+1)\quad(k\ge3),
\qquad |W_2|=6,
\]
and hence \(|W_k|=2^{k+1}-2\) for \(k\ge2\). The active row in a height-two rectangle can be either row, and every such word is nonzero for \(k\ge2\). Therefore
\[
p(k,1)=p(1,k)=2|W_k|=2^{k+2}-4\quad(k\ge2).
\]

**4. Solution and Følner consequences.** For \(n=m\), apply the four-way recurrence \(n-1\) times to obtain \(5\,4^{n-1}\). When \(n>m\), apply it \(m-1\) times to reach the base \(p(n-m+1,1)\), giving
\[
4^{m-1}(2^{n-m+3}-4)=2^{n+m+1}-4^m.
\]
The case \(m>n\) follows by swapping coordinates. Division by the area gives the stated constants. Rectangles with both exponents tending to infinity are Følner in \(\mathbb Z^2\) because their boundary-to-area ratio tends to zero. The displayed normalized constants depend only on the integer difference \(|n-m|\). Every fixed difference produces its stated limit, unbounded differences produce \(2\), and these exhaust all subsequential limits because an integer difference either has a constant subsequence or tends to infinity on a subsequence.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python program `verify_patterns.py` enumerates all shifts modulo \(2^{\max\{n,m\}}\), with both choices at the unique possible singular site, and verifies all 25 pairs \(1\le n,m\le5\). It also enumerates all truncated valuation words through length \(64\), checks their exact cardinalities and the listed base languages, and tests the symbolic recurrence over larger parameter ranges. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-parameter combinatorial proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Gómez and Petite, arXiv:2610.06488v1 (2026), Section 5.1, Proposition 5.3, give the general upper bound \(|L_{F_k}(X)|\le2|F_k|\) on a scale's fundamental domains; their Example 5.9 specifies the dyadic \(\mathbb Z^2\) system with maximal normalizer. The upper bound by itself neither specifies the sharp square-window coefficient \(5/4\) nor covers non-square dyadic rectangles (which need not be fundamental domains of the chosen scale). The present exact count and its Følner-shape dependence are the assessed additions. The paper's automorphism and normalizer classifications are used only as background and are not claimed anew.

The earlier Cabezas–Petite (2024) paper treats different substitutive constructions and their symmetry groups, not this fixed binary valuation array's anisotropic rectangular language. Lee–Flom–Ben-Abraham (2016), *Multidimensional period doubling structures*, instead has the two-dimensional formula \(a_2(i,j)=a_1(i+j)\), so its configuration is not this coordinatewise minimum-valuation array. Sell (2020) determines one-dimensional simple Toeplitz word complexities, not this two-dimensional dyadic-rectangle formula. Classical decimation and block-counting methods are not claimed as innovations.

## Limitations

No claim is made about non-dyadic rectangles, the complete classification of all minimal Toeplitz systems, or the novelty of general one-dimensional period-doubling complexity. The equality follows for the precisely specified construction and its language; other substitutions sometimes called multidimensional period doubling can have different pattern languages. Scientific originality is conditional on the scope of the accessible primary literature and remains subject to independent priority assessment.
