# Scientific review of the equilateral-triangle separation-body area profile

## Correctness

PASS. Schneider's planar normalization converts the exact definition to perimeter excess \(q=\pi\delta\). For the three side chambers and three corner chambers, the convex-hull visible-edge paths yield the two claimed ellipse families with foci at the opposite side's endpoints. On an edge-extension ray the transition distance uniquely solves \(t+\sqrt{t^2+2t+4}-2=q\), giving \(t=q(q+4)/(2(q+3))\). The two oriented ellipse Green integrals sum to the exact area, and the endpoint and scaling follow continuously. An independent numerical hull-perimeter and polar-area checker tests six positive parameters. Numerical evidence is supplemental, not the proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to ordinary bibliographic risk. Closest literature: Schneider (arXiv:1910.12670) establishes the polygonal ellipse-arc principle and the perimeter normalization but no equilateral-triangle area profile; Böröczky–Schneider (arXiv:0901.3343) defines the mean-width level set and proves stochastic inequalities rather than the area; *Exact area profile of the square separation body* (published 2026-10-04) treats the different, four-sided centrally symmetric case. A floating-body profile for every triangle is not a stronger claim: the cap-volume interior construction differs. Research-record searches for the direct triangular area, aliases, all-parameter ellipse arc formulas, and more general coverage found no equivalent. A possible unindexed historic calculation is the residual risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The triangle is the simplest noncentrally symmetric polygon and the basic simplex in two dimensions. Schneider's separation bodies are used in volume estimates for Poisson circumscribed polytopes. A closed area profile valid at every parameter, rather than only an arc-existence observation or first asymptotic exponent, provides an exact benchmark. The six-arc transition law and cancellation of the first analytic correction require an explicit global geometric calculation. This result makes no claim of a general arbitrary-polygon theorem or a new random-expectation formula.
