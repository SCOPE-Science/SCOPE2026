Three independent uniform points, one on each side of any Euclidean triangle, produce a random inscribed triangle whose probability of containing a fixed point is exactly \(\sum_{i=1}^3 t_i(1-t_i)\log((1-t_i)/t_i)\), where \((t_1,t_2,t_3)\) are the host-point's barycentric coordinates and endpoint values are continuous. This affine-universal inclusion depth is strictly Schur-concave, has the unique maximum \(2\log2/3\) at the centroid, and vanishes toward vertices at the binary-entropy rate.
