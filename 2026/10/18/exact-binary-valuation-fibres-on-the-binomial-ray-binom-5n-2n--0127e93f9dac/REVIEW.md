# Scientific assessment: exact binary valuation fibres on a binomial ray

**Correctness — PASS.** Kummer's theorem equates the two-adic valuation of \(\binom{5n}{2n}\) with the number of binary carries in \(2n+3n\). The proof keeps separate the carry used to generate digits of \(3n\) from the carry whose count gives the binomial valuation; it explicitly covers every state and the trailing-zero flush. The resulting eight-state transfer formula is valid for every nonnegative length and counts every index once. Exact symbolic elimination proves the stated formulas for valuations zero and one. The actual accompanying checker reproduces the symbolic equalities and independently checks complete histograms through \(L=15\). No finite computation is substituted for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Rowland's arXiv:1001.1783 (2010) distributes values across a single fixed row of Pascal's triangle, whereas the current problem selects the one-dimensional arithmetic ray \(\binom{5n}{2n}\) and varies its index. The later row-distribution analysis by Spiegelhofer–Wallner (arXiv:1710.10884) has the same mismatch. OEIS A130137 already records the quartic-recurrence sequence that matches the zero-valuation layer after shifting; this precedent is explicitly credited. OEIS A130136's finer counts concern occurrences of `0110` inside binary words, not the ray's valuation fibres. An earlier exact dyadic valuation distribution on the ray \(\binom{3n}{n}\) is a particularly close comparison: it uses a four-state carry system and gives identically zero valuation-one counts. Its proof technique motivates caution, but its equation specializes to a different arithmetic ray; in the present case the digits of \(3n\) are themselves computed with a carry and the valuation-one layer is nonempty with a squared quartic denominator. This is not a notational substitution, although the general transfer-matrix method is shared. The new statements are the full specific ray transfer and the closed valuation-one count. Targeted queries for the formula and aliases in published mathematical research records, OEIS, and literature did not identify an equivalent. The principal residual risk is a stronger generic result on digit-carry transducers or rational-slope binomial coefficients that could imply this specialization without naming it; absence from search results does not establish absolute priority.

**Value — PASS.** The valuation distribution of \(\binom{5n}{2n}\) is a natural infinite family of divisibility statistics: valuation zero measures odd terms, valuation one measures terms divisible by two but not four, and the matrix answers every higher-layer counting problem exactly. Simultaneously tracking multiplication and addition carries creates a nontrivial binary-digital interaction beyond the familiar central-binomial popcount identity. The valuation-one layer exhibits a squared rational denominator, unlike the parity layer, and supplies a new exact structural description of the first nonzero divisibility shell, as well as a reproducible method for all deeper shells. No general theorem or publication-scale contribution is asserted.

**Scope and limitations.** The formula is restricted to the prime two and slope \((5,2)\). Its original value may be reduced if an uninspected general carry-enumeration result specializes to this matrix; such a source would require a substantive implication comparison. The symbolic computation is independently reproducible, but independent mathematical audit has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
