# Scientific review: finite-chain strict implication and its box reduct

## Correctness — PASS

For a finite totally ordered Heyting base, the four displayed weak-Heyting axioms force every off-diagonal binary value to equal the minimum of adjacent-arrow parameters; conversely arbitrary parameters produce operations satisfying the axioms. This gives an explicit, injective and surjective normal form. The box is the suffix-minimum sequence of those parameters, and backward local equations give mutually independent fiber factors. Stars-and-bars counts boxes, and plateau values determine uniquely reconstructible ones. Edge boundary \(n=1\) and bottom/top values have been treated. Full-table brute force on the three-element chain and parameter enumeration through \(n=5\) corroborate all expressions.

## Originality — PASS (residual risk recorded)

The 2021 Heyting–Lewis article supplies the axioms and qualitative irreducibility of strict arrow to unary box, but not the quantitative fiber invariant under inspection of its axioms, representation and semantic sections. The broad 2015 enumeration algorithm for weak Heyting algebras on finite distributive lattices dominates the problem of generating examples and may already cover the elementary total enumeration, which is acknowledged rather than claimed as new. Its accessible abstract does not identify any box-fiber product or singleton-fiber theorem; full-text inspection was not possible. Celani's n-linear study describes varieties rather than the specific finite reduct fibers. Published search and published-finding corpus comparisons did not locate a matching fiber classification; searches are not a uniqueness proof. An equivalent earlier enumeration may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Unary box and binary constructive strict implication differ in expressive strength; their precise information-loss boundary is therefore a motivated invariant, rather than an arbitrary finite table. The result gives the entire inverse-fiber function, a sharp description of all uniquely recoverable box reducts, and maximal ambiguity, all uniformly in chain size. It is not just a recomputation of previously countable total expansions or a statement about finite computational feasibility.

The theorem does not establish syntactic definability and is limited to chain Heyting reducts with the four weak-Heyting identities. Independent verification and originality assessment remain outstanding.
