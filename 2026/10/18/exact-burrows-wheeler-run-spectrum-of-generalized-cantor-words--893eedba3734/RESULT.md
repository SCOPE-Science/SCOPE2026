# Exact Burrows–Wheeler run spectrum of generalized Cantor words

## Finding

Fix an integer base \(b\ge3\). On the ordered binary alphabet \(0<1\), consider the uniform morphism
\[
\sigma_b(1)=1\,0^{b-2}\,1,\qquad \sigma_b(0)=0^b,
\]
and its level-\(k\) word \(C_{b,k}=\sigma_b^k(1)\) of length \(b^k\). Let \(\operatorname{BWT}(w)\) be the last column obtained by lexicographically sorting all *cyclic rotations* of \(w\), without a sentinel. Let \(r(w)\) count maximal constant-letter runs in \(w\).

**Theorem.** For every integer \(b\ge3\) and \(k\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{r(\operatorname{BWT}(C_{b,k}))=4k.}
\]
Moreover, the **ordered lengths of the maximal one-runs** in this BWT are
\[
1,\ (2^1-1),1,\ (2^2-1),1,\ldots,\ (2^{k-1}-1),1,\ 1.
\]
Thus the entire one-run spectrum is independent of the base \(b\), despite the word length varying as \(b^k\). The original word has \(2^{k+1}-1\) runs; its BWT run ratio is exactly \(4k/(2^{k+1}-1)\), which tends to zero with \(k\).

A stronger assertion describes the rows of the BWT that start with zero. Set \(h_j=(b-2)b^j\) for \(0\le j<k\). Partition all zero-starting rotations according to the number \(h\) of leading zeros before their first one, ordering these groups by descending \(h\). The BWT characters in the group for \(h=h_j\), in order, are exactly
\[
1^{2^{k-j-1}-1}\,0^{2^{k-j-1}-1}\,1.
\]
Every other group has only zeros. The BWT characters of all one-starting rotations form one final group containing exactly one one; for \(k\ge2\) this final group begins and ends with zero.

## Assumptions and scope

Only integer \(b\ge3\), integer \(k\ge2\), cyclic rotations, and the alphabet order \(0<1\) are included. No end-of-string marker, bijective BWT, or suffix-order BWT is used. For completeness, \(C_{b,0}=1\) gives one BWT run; level \(k=1\) has two runs when \(b=3\) and four runs for \(b\ge4\), so the theorem's lower bound on \(k\) is necessary.

A maximal one-run means a maximal contiguous block of ones in the transformed word. Its *ordered* spectrum records these block lengths from left to right, rather than only their multiset. The theorem concerns the whole finite sequence of iterates, not a finite prefix experiment.

## Proof

**1. Circular gap formula.** The word \(C_{b,k}\) begins and ends in one, has \(2^k\) ones, and is generated by
\[
C_{b,k}=C_{b,k-1}\,0^{(b-2)b^{k-1}}\,C_{b,k-1}.
\]
Index the circular zero gaps *after successive ones* by \(g_1,\dots,g_{2^k}\); the last gap goes from the last one back to the first. Induction on \(k\) proves
\[
g_i=(b-2)b^{\nu_2(i)}\quad(1\le i<2^k),\qquad g_{2^k}=0,
\]
where \(\nu_2(i)\) is the exponent of two dividing positive integer \(i\). In particular, \(h_{k-1}\) is the unique largest circular zero gap, the zero gap is unique, and the circular word is primitive: a proper cyclic power would repeat every gap and hence repeat the unique maximum.

**2. Leading-zero groups.** Any rotation beginning in a zero gap has a unique leading prefix \(0^h1\) with \(1\le h\le h_{k-1}\). Rotations with larger \(h\) are lexicographically earlier, since \(0<1\). For a fixed \(h\), exactly one such rotation occurs for every circular zero gap \(g_i\ge h\). Its BWT predecessor is one if and only if \(g_i=h\); otherwise the predecessor is zero. Consequently, the group contains only zeros unless \(h=h_j\) for some \(j\).

**3. Exact order inside a nonzero group.** Fix \(h=h_j\). Eligible indices \(i\) are the multiples of \(2^j\) in \(1,\dots,2^k-1\). Once their common initial prefix \(0^{h_j}1\) is removed, they are compared by the sequence of *subsequent* circular zero gaps; a longer first differing gap produces the lexicographically smaller binary rotation.

For every eligible \(i\) and every \(1\le t<2^j\), the subsequent gap equals \(g_{i+t}=(b-2)b^{\nu_2(t)}\), independent of \(i\). At the first possibly differing offset \(t=2^j\), there are exactly three cases:

- If \(\nu_2(i)=j\) and \(i\ne2^k-2^j\), then \(g_{i+2^j}\ge h_{j+1}>h_j\), so this rotation is in the first class.
- If \(\nu_2(i)>j\), then \(g_{i+2^j}=h_j\), giving the middle class.
- If \(i=2^k-2^j\), then \(g_{i+2^j}=g_{2^k}=0\), giving the last class.

These classes are strictly ordered in the displayed order. There are \(2^{k-j-1}-1\) indices in the first class, \(2^{k-j-1}-1\) in the middle, and exactly one in the last. The BWT predecessor is one in the first and last classes (because their current gap equals \(h_j\)), and zero in the middle. Thus the group is precisely
\[
1^{2^{k-j-1}-1}0^{2^{k-j-1}-1}1.
\]
For \(j=k-1\), both exponents are zero, giving the singleton \(1\). This proves the stronger group statement without assuming any unverified order among rotations within the three classes.

**4. The one-starting rotations.** They occupy a final block because \(0<1\). A one-starting rotation has BWT predecessor one exactly when its immediately preceding circular gap has length zero, which occurs once, at the first one. Thus this block has one one. For \(k\ge2\), its first rotation is the one followed by the unique longest zero gap and its predecessor is zero. Its last rotation is the unique one beginning with \(11\) (the zero-length circular gap) and its predecessor is also zero. Hence the final block starts and ends with zero.

**5. Count runs.** Each pair of distinct eligible gap lengths \(h_j<h_{j+1}\) has at least one intervening integer \(h\), and the corresponding leading-zero group exists and consists entirely of zeros. The group for \(h_{k-1}\) contributes one one-run. Each of the remaining \(k-1\) special groups contributes two one-runs, because its interior zero block has positive length; adjacent special groups are separated by nonempty all-zero groups. The final one-starting block adds one further one-run. Therefore the transformed word has \(1+2(k-1)+1=2k\) maximal one-runs. It starts with one (from the unique longest-gap group) and ends with zero (from the final one-starting block), so every one-run is followed by one zero-run and \(r=4k\). The ordered one-run length statement follows from the same group decomposition.

Finally, all \(2^k\) ones of the *linear* word \(C_{b,k}\) are separated by nonempty zero blocks and occur at both endpoints, giving exactly \(2^{k+1}-1\) runs before transformation. This proves the run-ratio identity.

## Relationship to prior work

Gil and Scott (2012, arXiv:1201.3077) describe the classical cyclic BWT and study a different, bijective string-sorting transform. Their results do not give Cantor-word BWT spectra. Brlek, Frosini, Mancini, Pergola, and Rinaldi (2019) provide exact transforms for Thue–Morse, generalized Fibonacci, and morphism-composition families; their cases do not contain the present nonprimitive Cantor substitution.

Frosini, Mancini, Rinaldi, Romana, and Sciortino (2022, arXiv:2202.02609, Theorems 4.1–4.2) treat a larger class of growing nonprimitive binary morphisms, including the family here, and establish the asymptotic estimate \(r(\operatorname{BWT}(C_{b,k}))=\Theta(k)\). That stronger **domain** coverage is explicitly acknowledged. Their bounds do **not** give the exact coefficient \(4\), the base-independent ordered one-run lengths, or the three-class lexicographic decomposition established above. This is the precise incremental content of the theorem, rather than a claim to a new logarithmic growth order.

## Limitations

The theorem uses classical cyclic BWT without a sentinel and does not assert the same run count for sentinel-terminated, suffix-ordered, extended, or bijective variants. It establishes the complete ordered *one-run* spectrum but not closed formulas for every individual zero-run length. The literature comparison is based on inspected accessible sources and searches; coverage by a differently described unindexed result cannot be ruled out. No results are asserted for other transform conventions.
