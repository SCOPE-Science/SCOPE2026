# Exact Cartier and Frobenius strata of binary smooth plane quintics

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal Q\) be the set of nonzero homogeneous degree-five polynomials \(F\in\mathbf F_2[X,Y,Z]\) for which \(C_F\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) is geometrically smooth. Write \(f(C_F)\) for the \(2\)-rank of its genus-six Jacobian and \(a(C_F)=\dim_{\overline{\mathbf F}_2}\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha_2,\operatorname{Jac}(C_F)[2])\) for its \(a\)-number. The complete equation-weighted joint distribution is:

| \(f\) | \(a\) | Number of smooth equations | \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\) orbits |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 3 | 10,752 | 71 |
| 1 | 1 | 10,752 | 68 |
| 1 | 3 | 18,816 | 121 |
| 2 | 1 | 18,816 | 124 |
| 2 | 3 | 28,224 | 175 |
| 3 | 1 | 38,976 | 242 |
| 3 | 3 | 28,224 | 176 |
| 4 | 1 | 76,608 | 464 |
| 5 | 1 | 155,904 | 948 |
| 6 | 0 | 301,056 | 1,815 |
| **Total** | | **688,128** | **4,204** |

In particular, **only \(a=0,1,3\) occur**; their equation-weighted proportions among smooth plane quintics are respectively \(7/16,7/16,1/8\).

The joint distribution contains genuinely more information than the zeta function. For example, the following two curves over \(\mathbf F_2\) are geometrically smooth:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_A:\quad &Z^5+Y^3Z^2+Y^5+XY^2Z^2+XY^4+X^2Z^3+X^2YZ^2+X^2Y^2Z+X^5=0,\\
C_B:\quad &Z^5+YZ^4+Y^3Z^2+Y^4Z+Y^5+XY^3Z+XY^4+X^2Z^3+X^2YZ^2+X^2Y^2Z+X^5=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both have \(f=3\) and exactly the same point counts \((N_1,\ldots,N_6)=(0,2,6,14,60,74)\), giving the same complete Weil numerator
\[
L(T)=1-3T+3T^2-T^3+6T^5-16T^6+12T^7-8T^9+48T^{10}-96T^{11}+64T^{12}.
\]
Nevertheless, \(a(C_A)=3\) and \(a(C_B)=1\). Thus their Jacobians are \(\mathbf F_2\)-isogenous but have different \(a\)-numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
All coefficients and rational projective transformations are over \(\mathbf F_2\); geometric smoothness and \(a\)-number are over its algebraic closure. Each nonzero quintic equation is counted once, not up to isomorphism. Since \(\mathbf F_2^\times\) is trivial, there is no nontrivial scalar identification. The complete coefficient space contains \(2^{21}-1=2,097,151\) nonzero forms, and a smooth plane quintic has genus \(\binom{4}{2}=6\). The orbit counts refer only to coordinate changes over \(\mathbf F_2\), not to abstract curve isomorphism classes.

## Proof
**Exact geometric smoothness test.** Write \(S_d=\mathbf F_2[X,Y,Z]_d\). For any ternary quintic \(F\), Euler's identity gives \(F=XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z\). Thus a projective point is singular on \(F=0\) if and only if all three quartic partial derivatives vanish there. Their homogeneous ideal is base-point-free if and only if the three partial derivatives form a regular sequence. In the latter case its quotient has Hilbert series \((1+t+t^2+t^3)^3\), with socle degree \(9\); consequently the degree-ten piece vanishes. Conversely, vanishing of that degree-ten piece forces the projective zero locus of the homogeneous ideal to be empty. Therefore \(C_F\) is geometrically smooth exactly when the explicit linear map
\[
S_6^{\oplus3}\longrightarrow S_{10},\qquad (A,B,C)\longmapsto AF_X+BF_Y+CF_Z
\]
has rank \(\dim S_{10}=66\). This tests geometric, not just rational, smoothness using finite binary linear algebra.

**Cartier matrix and stable Frobenius.** Index a basis of regular differentials by the six triples \(I=(i_1,i_2,i_3)\) of strictly positive integers summing to \(5\); the corresponding adjoint form has exponents \((i_1-1,i_2-1,i_3-1)\). In characteristic two the coefficient-extraction formula for the Cartier/Hasse–Witt matrix is
\[
H_{I,J}=[X^{2i_1-j_1}Y^{2i_2-j_2}Z^{2i_3-j_3}]F,
\]
where a coefficient is zero whenever an exponent is negative. This is the standard plane-curve Cartier formula obtained by applying Cartier to the Poincaré-residue basis. Because the ground field is \(\mathbf F_2\), Frobenius acts trivially on coefficients; the geometric \(a\)-number equals \(6-\operatorname{rank}H\), and the \(2\)-rank is the stable rank \(\operatorname{rank}(H^6)\). Stabilization by exponent six holds for every linear operator on a six-dimensional vector space.

**Exhaustive finite calculation.** `code/enumerate_direct.cpp` visits every nonzero 21-bit coefficient mask in a fixed degree-five monomial order. For each, it constructs the 84-by-66 multiplication matrix just described, tests rank 66, constructs the six-by-six matrix \(H\), and tallies \((\operatorname{rank}H^6,6-\operatorname{rank}H)\). Its exact output is `data/direct_spectrum.txt` and its totals agree entry-by-entry with the table above. Independently, `code/enumerate_orbits.cpp` enumerates the orbits generated by a coordinate transposition, coordinate cycle, and shear, which generate the order-168 group \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\). It tests one representative per orbit, multiplies by the actual orbit length, and obtains the same ten joint counts and the 4,204 smooth orbits. The complete representatives with orbit weights and invariants are in `data/representatives.csv`; the complete orbit output is `data/orbit_spectrum.txt`. As an external consistency check, the weighted quotient count is \(688128/168=4096\), matching the weighted count recorded by the earlier genus-six census; the 4,204 projective-orbit count also agrees with its plane-quintic classification. No statistical sampling occurs.

**Same-zeta, different-\(a\) witnesses.** `code/find_zeta_pair.cpp` evaluates each smooth orbit representative on all points of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_{2^n})\) for \(1\le n\le6\) using explicit irreducible binary extension-field polynomials. It locates the displayed pair with distinct \(a\)-numbers but identical six point counts (`data/zeta_pairs.txt`). Independently, `code/verify_zeta_pair.py`, requiring only the Python standard library, checks that each displayed polynomial and its two affine partial derivatives generate the unit ideal on each of the three affine charts using an independently implemented Buchberger algorithm. It recomputes both Hasse–Witt matrices and all twelve extension-field point counts. Its output is `data/pair_verification.txt`. For a genus-six curve the first six power sums determine the first six Weil coefficients via Newton's identities, and the functional equation determines the remaining six. This proves equality of the displayed degree-twelve Weil polynomials. Over finite fields, equality of Weil polynomials implies that the Jacobians are isogenous.

## Verification
With a C++17 compiler and Python 3, compile the three sources in `code/` (for example using `c++ -O2 -std=c++17`) and run the first executable in a working directory; it creates `representatives.csv`. Running the independent direct enumerator reproduces the entire joint table without using that file. The point-count collision executable reads `representatives.csv` from the current directory, which can also be copied from `data/representatives.csv`. Run `python3 code/verify_zeta_pair.py` to verify the two explicit witnesses without external Python packages. Each supplied source and the exact saved outputs are included in the package; numerical agreement is not a substitute for the foregoing algebraic reductions, which establish the interpretations of every computed rank.

## Relationship to prior work
Huang, Kedlaya and Lau's 2024 genus-six census enumerates all smooth binary plane quintics among the Brill–Noether strata (see Section 3.3) and provides a broad classification by zeta functions. The existence of the underlying complete equation census is *not* claimed here. The present joint \(2\)-rank/\(a\)-number distribution, forbidden \(a\)-number strata, and explicit equal-zeta/different-\(a\) witnesses refine arithmetic information beyond the reported zeta classification. The Cartier coefficient-extraction method originates in Stöhr and Voloch and is *not* new. Related Cartier-rank bounds such as Zhou's 2018 results do not provide this complete genus-six binary plane-quintic enumeration. A pre-existing published table or unpublished postprocessing of the available census data may nevertheless reproduce the same exact counts.

## Limitations
The proof is computer-assisted, although finite and exhaustive with two differently structured implementations and a separate Gröbner witness check. The equation-weighted distribution is **not** a distribution on abstract curve isomorphism classes or an assertion for any field other than \(\mathbf F_2\). The witnesses prove same-zeta/different-\(a\) existence, not any assertion about broader moduli loci. Priority is based on the accessible inspected literature, not on an exhaustive worldwide bibliographic guarantee. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
