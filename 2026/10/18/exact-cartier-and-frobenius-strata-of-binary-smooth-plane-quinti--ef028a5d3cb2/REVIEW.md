# Scientific review: Cartier and Frobenius strata of binary smooth plane quintics

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact algebraic reduction identifies geometric smoothness with surjectivity of a degree-ten Macaulay multiplication map built from three quartic derivatives. The standard Cartier extraction formula gives \(a=6-\operatorname{rank}H\) and \(2\)-rank \(\operatorname{rank}(H^6)\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). Two independently structured exhaustive programs agree on all ten population counts and the smooth total; the resulting 4,204 projective orbits and the quotient weight \(688128/168=4096\) also agree with the external genus-six plane-quintic census; an independently written Buchberger and extension-field program verifies both explicit same-zeta witnesses. Risk: the finite enumeration relies on correct software implementations, though the independent algorithms and exact algebraic reductions substantially mitigate that risk.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk.** The full text of Huang–Kedlaya–Lau (arXiv:2402.00716, initially 2024-02-01), especially Section 3.3 and the zeta-function discussion, supplies the underlying complete genus-six equation census but not this joint \(2\)-rank/\(a\)-number table or the equal-zeta/different-\(a\) witnesses. Stöhr–Voloch's Cartier formula is explicitly treated as prior mathematical machinery. Zhou's Cartier-rank bounds are not a binary plane-quintic census. An accessible prior finite count for a special seven-rational-point quintic subfamily does not imply a joint count for all 688,128 smooth equations. Searches of mathematical research records did not identify a stronger covering statement. Risk: a prior unindexed or unpublished calculation from the public census data could contain the same distribution.

**Value — PASS.** The classification solves the natural exact arithmetic stratification of the entire smooth plane-quintic locus over the smallest field, rules out four potential \(a\)-number values, gives full coupling with \(2\)-rank, and exhibits two isogenous Jacobians with different \(a\)-numbers through concrete geometrically smooth equations. This is beyond a routine recomputation of the known number of genus-six curves or their zeta functions, and can guide investigations of Cartier loci and Newton/Ekedahl–Oort refinements.

No curve-isomorphism-class distribution is claimed. The explicit equation-weighted versus curve-weighted distinction is essential; primary source data may enable further refinements. The review addresses this claim and its included verification artifacts, not the quality of any earlier results.
