# Scientific assessment: finite Lorentz centering constants

## Correctness

**PASS.** Permutation averaging proves that unrestricted projection minimization reduces to centering. A signed-indicator convex decomposition is exact for the real weighted \(\ell_1\)-rearrangement norm. Three-level sorting gives the explicit finite maximum, and two universal probes force total weight \(W_n=2w_1\) in any contractive case. Further strictly positive witnesses rule out all dimensions at least four. Dimension three is determined exhaustively by its signed-support types. Exact-rational finite tests support but do not replace these proofs.

## Originality

**PASS, with bibliographic qualification.** Searches of published mathematical findings and literature distinguished the real endpoint \(p=1\) from the \(1<p<\infty\) Lorentz theorem of Randrianantoanina (1997), the complex/disjoint-support theorem of 1996, and broader existence-set and operator-norm literature. Neither a general equivalence of existence sets with norm-one projections nor a positive-operator norm formula implies the explicit signed centering threshold without additional computation. A 2009 source on \(d(w,1)\) complementability and a 1999 operator-norm source could not be exhaustively inspected, leaving a real risk of an unindexed equivalent claim.

## Value

**PASS.** The result is a complete classification of norm-one complementability for the natural permutation-invariant zero-sum hyperplane in the entire family of finite real \(d(w,1)\) spaces with strictly positive weights. The exact three-dimensional phase boundary and sharp dimensional obstruction are structural information about the endpoint omitted from the smooth-exponent result, not a parameter substitution or isolated norm evaluation. The finite formula also computes every relative projection constant in the family.

## Limitations

The theorem covers only the specified real finite hyperplane, no arbitrary hyperplanes, complex spaces or infinite sequences. Absolute bibliographic novelty is not independently verified. The no-contractivity statement is strict for each positive weight vector; no uniform gap over all vectors is asserted.
