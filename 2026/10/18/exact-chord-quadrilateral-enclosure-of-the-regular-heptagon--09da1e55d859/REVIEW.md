# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every allowed chord normal is one of fourteen directions, and all supporting offsets are explicitly either the circumradius or the inradius. Tightening reduces the problem to finitely many four-normal intersections or triangles. The derivation of the support-line area identity is valid whenever all four constraints are active; redundant cases are excluded by the sharp inball-containing-triangle bound. The rational interval checker verifies all 511 positively spanning normal quadruples: seven minimizers and a strict area gap exceeding 1/40 for every competing formal four-line expression. It verifies the area-ratio enclosure as well. The proof's infinite quantifiers are reduced to this exhaustive finite classification rather than inferred from sample tests.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk

Vígh's full note reports the heptagon's approximately 1.2588 ratio from numerical investigation but does not give the exact trigonometric value, exhaustive supporting-normal proof, or the seven equality quadrilaterals. The 2021 pentagon enclosure theorem concerns a different input shape and its heptagon passage concerns six-sided enclosures. Fodor–Grundbacher's global quadrilateral estimate is weaker and unrestricted. Prior work on enclosing parallelograms does not determine the minimum over nonparallel quadrilaterals. Closest overlap identified in related mathematical records concerns a regular *nonagon* chord-quadrilateral calculation; it does not entail the heptagon optimum. Equivalent older regular-polygon formulas cannot be entirely excluded by searchable literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The heptagon is the smallest odd polygon beyond the known pentagon case of the newly proposed chord conjecture. An exact first nontrivial odd-order benchmark and classification of all optimal supporting configurations give a reproducible endpoint test for this geometric conjecture. The theorem certifies both a sharp algebraic-trigonometric ratio and a discrete separation between its minimizers and all other feasible normal patterns; the latter identifies the mechanism of rigidity inside the finite direction model. This is not claimed as a proof for arbitrary heptagons or unrestricted enclosing quadrilaterals.
