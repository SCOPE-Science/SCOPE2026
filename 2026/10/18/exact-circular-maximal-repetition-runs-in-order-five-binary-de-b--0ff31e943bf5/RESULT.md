# Exact circular maximal-repetition runs in order-five binary de Bruijn cycles

## Finding

Let \(B\) range over binary de Bruijn cycles of order five, considered modulo cyclic rotation only. A **circular run** is a triple \((i,L,p)\) consisting of a starting position \(i\), a factor length \(L\), and the *least period* \(p\) of that circular factor, such that \(L\ge 2p\) and the periodicity cannot be extended by one symbol to either side. Positions are modulo the circumference, and only factors of length at most one circumference are considered. Write \(r_p(B)\) for the number of these runs with least period \(p\).

Every such cycle satisfies \(r_1(B)=8\), \(r_2(B)=2\), and \(r_p(B)=0\) for every \(p\ge5\). The **complete joint spectrum** of the remaining two counts, over all \(2048\) rotation classes, is:

| \((r_3,r_4)\) | Number of cycles | Symmetry orbits of size 2 | Symmetry orbits of size 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \((0,2)\) | 16 | 4 | 2 |
| \((1,1)\) | 224 | 0 | 56 |
| \((1,2)\) | 128 | 0 | 32 |
| \((2,0)\) | 768 | 20 | 182 |
| \((2,1)\) | 608 | 4 | 150 |
| \((2,2)\) | 304 | 4 | 74 |
| **Total** | **2048** | **32** | **496** |

The symmetry group is generated by reversal and bit complementation, modulo rotations. Consequently, there are \(528\) symmetry orbits. The total number of circular runs \(R(B)=r_1(B)+r_2(B)+r_3(B)+r_4(B)\) has exactly the distribution

\[
\#\{B:R(B)=12\}=1008,\qquad
\#\{B:R(B)=13\}=736,\qquad
\#\{B:R(B)=14\}=304.
\]

In particular, exactly \(12,13,14\) circular runs are possible; the exact uniform-cycle mean is \(405/32\), and the variance is \(535/1024\). The pair of period-two run lengths is always either \((4,6)\), in exactly \(1536\) cycles, or \((5,5)\), in exactly \(512\) cycles. These period and length profiles are computed for every cycle in `profiles.csv`.

## Assumptions and scope

A binary de Bruijn cycle of order \(n\) is a circle of length \(2^n\) in which every binary word of length \(n\) occurs at exactly one circular starting position. For a circular factor \(F=B_i B_{i+1}\cdots B_{i+L-1}\), an integer \(p\) is a period if \(B_{i+t}=B_{i+t+p}\) for each \(0\le t<L-p\). Its *least* period is the smallest positive such integer. For \((i,L,p)\) to be a circular run, require \(2p\le L\le 2^n\), least period \(p\), and both strict inequalities

\[
B_{i-1}\ne B_{i+p-1},\qquad B_{i+L}\ne B_{i+L-p}.
\]

All indices are cyclic. Runs are counted by their starting positions and maximal intervals, not merely by their distinct underlying strings. The integer \(p=2^n\), which is the trivial period of the infinitely repeated circle, is excluded automatically by \(2p\le L\le 2^n\). For order five, every nontrivial run has length at most eight, so the full-circumference boundary never arises. Equality of cycles means equality after a cyclic rotation; reversal and complementation are *not* identified in the count \(2048\).

## Proof

**Period restriction, valid at every order.** In any order-\(n\) de Bruijn cycle, a circular factor of least period \(p<2^n\) cannot have length at least \(p+n\): its two length-\(n\) windows starting \(p\) positions apart would agree, contradicting unique occurrence. Thus \(L\le p+n-1\). A run must also have \(L\ge2p\), hence \(p\le n-1\). For order five, \(p\le4\) and \(L\le p+4\le8\). This turns the circular problem into a finite set of local tests with no truncation ambiguity.

**Unary runs, valid at every binary order \(n\ge3\).** For \(2\le L\le n-2\), a maximal constant-letter run of length \(L\) is exactly an occurrence of either \(10^L1\) or \(01^L0\). Every prescribed word of length \(L+2\le n\) occurs \(2^{n-L-2}\) times; the total count of these runs is \(2^{n-L-1}\). There is no maximal unary run of length \(n-1\), because both occurrences of each constant word of length \(n-1\) are contained in the unique corresponding constant word of length \(n\). The two constant length-\(n\) words each give one maximal run, and no longer constant run exists. Summing yields \(2^{n-2}\) period-one runs. For \(n=5\), there are respectively \(4,2,0,2\) unary runs of lengths \(2,3,4,5\), hence eight.

**Period two at order five.** Every period-two run has length \(4\), \(5\), or \(6\), by the restriction above, and is alternating. The length-four alternating windows `0101` and `1010` each occur twice, giving four occurrences in total. The length-five alternating windows `01010` and `10101` each occur once, giving two. Every alternating window lies in exactly one maximal period-two run. A run of length \(L\) contributes \(L-3\) length-four windows and \(L-4\) length-five windows. Subtracting these totals shows that there are exactly \(4-2=2\) period-two runs. Their lengths sum to \(10\), so they are \((4,6)\) or \((5,5)\).

**Complete enumeration of periods three and four.** The order-four binary de Bruijn directed graph has \(16\) vertices (four-bit words) and \(32\) directed edges (five-bit words). Each vertex has two incoming and two outgoing edges. Fixing the distinguished edge `00000`, depth-first enumeration of edge-simple Eulerian circuits gives every order-five cycle exactly once after rotation. Independently, each vertex has two possible bijections from its incoming to outgoing edges; there are exactly \(2^{16}=65536\) complete pairing assignments. Such an assignment represents an Eulerian circuit exactly when its induced permutation of the \(32\) edges consists of a single cycle. Both complete constructions yield precisely the same \(2048\) rotation classes.

For each circle, the program `verify.py` determines maximal periodic intervals by extending every initial square of least period at most four. Independently, it enumerates all circular intervals of lengths two through eight, identifies their least periods directly, and checks both extension inequalities. The two sets of triples agree for every one of the \(2048\) cycles. The six-row table follows by integer counting of these exhaustive sets. The same data establish the length-two pattern counts and all symmetry orbits: each orbit is the set of distinct rotation classes obtained by reversal and bit complementation. The CSV lists every canonical cycle and its verified profile; the self-contained checker reproduces and compares every row.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `profiles.csv`. It requires only the Python standard library. It performs two independently complete graph enumerations, tests all \(65536\) vertex-pairing assignments, validates every five-bit window, compares two independent run-detection algorithms on all \(2048\) cycles, recomputes the complete joint profile distribution, checks the orbit multiplicities, evaluates the mean and variance in exact rational arithmetic, and compares all \(2048\) rows against `profiles.csv`.

An observed successful execution of the packaged sources reported `VERIFY_OK` for both graph generators, all run methods, the six joint cells, moments, the period-two length alternatives, and the \(528\) symmetry orbits. The mathematics proves an exact finite census; no statistical sampling, unbounded induction from finite trials, or conjectured higher-order spectrum is involved.

## Relationship to prior work

Crochemore, Iliopoulos, Kubica, Radoszewski, Rytter, and Walen develop general extremal bounds for highly periodic maximal runs in arbitrary linear strings. Bannai, I, Inenaga, Nakashima, Takeda, and Tsuruta establish general linear-string run bounds and give a Lyndon-word characterization. Neither source supplies exact period-by-period counts or a six-cell frequency law in the rotation classes of the order-five de Bruijn family.

Ruskey, Sawada, and Williams construct fixed-weight de Bruijn-type cycles; that construction result does not determine the present all-cycle run spectrum. Known de Bruijn uniform-factor counts (including the usual constant-symbol run or R-2 property) already imply the unary part; **that part is background structure, not claimed as a new independent discovery**. The new finite classification is the simultaneous distribution of maximal periodic runs of least periods three and four and the full associated cycle/orbit census. Earlier results on runs of identical symbols, Abelian squares, ordinary squares in other recursive words, or BWT run counts do not by themselves imply these frequencies.

## Limitations

The full six-cell spectrum and orbit counts apply only to binary order-five de Bruijn cycles. The bound \(p\le n-1\) and the unary-run count \(2^{n-2}\) hold for all binary orders \(n\ge3\); the exact counts at higher nontrivial least periods for larger orders are not determined here. Historical originality is supported by inspected relevant sources and searchable records, not by a proof that no unindexed enumeration exists. The provided checking code is reproducible, but no independent external scientific audit has been performed.
