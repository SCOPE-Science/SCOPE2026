# Review of the height-four regular Esakia classification

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the published finite regularity criterion of Grilletti and Quadrellaro (Theorem 4.7) as a premise and derives an explicit, reversible immediate-successor normal form for exactly three maxima and height four. It distinguishes *covers* from sets of reachable maxima; this distinction is essential to the classification. The root is excluded exactly when one selected depth-two vertex covers every minimal vertex of the upper poset. Every other construction has a unique least element and the required regular cover sets. Marked enumeration gives \(16718\), and the orbit counts \(16718,1070,1070,1070,68,68\) give \(3344\). A standalone exhaustive program reconstructs every configuration and independently checks the orbit count. No computational timeout is interpreted as a mathematical result.

## Originality — PASS
Primary full text inspected: Grilletti–Quadrellaro (arXiv:2303.11246; doi:10.1007/s00012-023-00833-5), specifically the regularity criterion in Section 4, the three-maximal infinite families in Section 5, the logic applications in Section 6 and the explicit unanswered classification question in Section 7. Those results supply the prerequisite but do not enumerate the posets constrained simultaneously by height four and three maxima, or identify their antichain orbit normal form. Search comparisons for regular Heyting algebra enumeration, regular Esakia posets, three maximal elements, height four, \(3344\), and \(16718\) found no stronger same-object count. Nearest other results about weak-order posets, ordinal Kripke frames and general finite Heyting algebra homogeneity address different objects and invariants. Residual risk: unindexed earlier equivalent enumeration or a classification in a different language; this remains an evidentiary risk, not a claim of independently certified uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The original paper specifically asks for a classification of finite regular posets by depth and number of maximal points. The theorem resolves a natural bounded instance *completely*: three maximal worlds correspond to a fixed eight-element Boolean core, and height four is the first height with two interacting nonmaximal levels above the root. The \(S_3\)-equivariant antichain normal form provides more than a bare integer and gives explicit finite model representatives for regularly generated, subdirectly irreducible intuitionistic/DNA-algebra semantics. It is not claimed to determine logical inequivalence or varieties.
