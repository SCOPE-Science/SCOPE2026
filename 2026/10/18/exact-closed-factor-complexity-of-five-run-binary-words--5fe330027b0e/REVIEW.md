# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete case division by factor-run number (one through five) is sufficient because the ambient word has five runs. Every case classifies all admissible proper borders, and the three-run double count is removed only when the two interior one-run lengths coincide. The elementary minimum identity gives the exact count, and the two-case envelope argument proves the sharp asymptotic claim. A separate direct factor-and-border enumeration passed 9,592 parameter cases, plus ten explicit sharp-family checks. These finite checks support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the stated residual risk

The closest mathematical sources are Badkobeh–Fici–Lipták (arXiv:1305.6395, Section 5) and Parshina–Puzynina (arXiv:2111.00863, finite-word bounds and Proposition 8). The former gives a five-run example proving a lower bound but not a uniform exact formula or restricted sharp constant; the latter establishes the unrestricted \(n^2/6\) asymptotic, which does not imply the present five-run \(n^2/8\). Published research-record searches and published comparison records did not produce an equivalent formula. An unindexed bounded-run computation or result derivable from general return-word machinery may exist; failure to find one does not prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The number of distinct closed factors measures complete first returns and is a natural word-complexity invariant. The result treats every positive five-run length vector, isolates a nonobvious resonance at equality of the two one-runs, and gives a sharp structural asymptotic coefficient within a natural bounded-run class. This does not assert a new unrestricted maximum or a new general algorithm.
