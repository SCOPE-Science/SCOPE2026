# Scientific assessment of the coefficient-shape theorem

## Correctness

The polynomial is used as a counting input and is rederived for completeness. Its coefficients through degree seven verify the finite determinant and mode ranges. A negative determinant at degree five for orders 13–21 and at degree four for orders at least 22 proves failure of ordinary log-concavity. A strict dip in degree five at order 14 is the only failure of unimodality: for orders at least 15 the low coefficients increase through degree seven and are followed by a binomial tail. Finite formula evaluations corroborate but do not replace these sign arguments.

## Scope of the distinct contribution

The same-family Resultary result *Dissociation polynomials and maximal-set purity of KG(n,2)* gives an equivalent all-set polynomial as well as the maximal-set theorem. Neither enumeration is presented as a distinct contribution here, and the earlier four-label localization is credited to Brešar and Dravec. The surviving claim is the exact log-concavity and unimodality classification, not a claim of precedence over uninspected sources.

## Value

The two sharp thresholds disclose different behavior in a canonical graph family: log-concavity fails permanently beyond a cutoff, while unimodality fails at just one order. This is not information supplied by the dissociation number, by a generic graph bound, or by the mere presence of a unimodal binomial tail.
