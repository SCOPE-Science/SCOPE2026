# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof establishes a general, quantified necessary-and-sufficient criterion for the equality of two lower-approximation compositions. Equality forces their common image to be a union of components of the join of the two equivalence relations; each nonempty image block is detected by containment of a two-step expanded granule. For the alternating cyclic pair partitions those granules are exactly all four-consecutive-point windows. The automaton trace counts every admissible labelled cyclic word once, and Perron--Frobenius theory establishes the exponential asymptotic. Direct finite enumeration separately checks both partition compositions, the general criterion, and the transfer formula. Computational evidence is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest inspected primary sources are Gopaulsingh, *Double Successive Rough Set Approximations* (arXiv:1612.03814), which proves representation and reconstruction results for composed rough approximations, and Howlader--Banerjee, *Kripke Contexts, Double Boolean Algebras with Operators and Corresponding Modal Systems* (arXiv:2204.04671), which develops algebraic and logical semantics of approximation operators. Neither inspected contribution implies an exact cyclic valuation-agreement rate or the general join-component criterion without additional analysis. Dedicated research-record and literature searches for both compositions, commuting S5 modalities, cyclic partition overlaps and forbidden binary windows produced no recognized covering result. The run-avoidance transfer matrix is classical and is not claimed as a newly discovered enumerator. An alternate published formula under equivalent language remains a residual risk; no priority guarantee is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cyclic alternating pair-partitions form a canonical minimal connected overlap geometry for two regular equivalence granulations. The result quantifies precise order sensitivity for double lower rough approximations: universal agreement in the four-point case changes into exponentially vanishing agreement for long cycles. The structural characterization extends to any two finite equivalence partitions and makes it possible to decide exactly which subsets commute. These conclusions address a motivated quantitative version of the multigranular order-dependence problem and are more than a numerical table or a routine normalization check.

## Scope and unresolved risks

The cyclic expression is specialized, and no theorem is asserted for randomly sampled partitions or arbitrary rough-set operators. Equivalent older results could use multimodal logic terminology, and further independent literature examination may be needed. Verification over finite examples does not replace the general proof.
