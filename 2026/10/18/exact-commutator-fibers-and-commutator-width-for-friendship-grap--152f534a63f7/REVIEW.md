# Scientific review: friendship-graph commutator fibers

## Correctness — PASS
The identity \([g,h]_{uv}=x_uy_v-y_ux_v\) is checked from the associative group multiplication, without dividing by two. Every prescribed target is counted via a disjoint partition into zero and nonzero hub coordinate pairs. The determinant zero/nonzero fibers and the \(q\)-, \(q^2\)-, or zero-solution linear equations on each triangle give the complete closed formula. The explicit image criterion and two-summand construction prove sharp width, not just finite feasibility. All seven enumerated field/size cases check all central targets and an independent conjugacy-derived zero-fiber count. The general proof, not the computations, covers all prime-power fields and all positive numbers of triangles.

## Originality — PASS within examined coverage
The closest inspected primary source is Rossmann's arXiv:2107.05564v1, which contains the graphical-group construction and conjugacy-class counts. The zero commutator aggregate is already inferable and is explicitly excluded from the new part of the result. The checked sections do not give the nonzero prescribed-target fibers, the whole commutator image, or the sharp width transition for triangle bouquets. Naik's arXiv:1805.00091v1 treats commutator-word distributions in other finite-group families; Cocke–Ho arXiv:1807.07111v1 addresses general word maps. Scientifically relevant published finite-group results for two-generated class-two groups and Kronecker path algebras concern different constructions and do not imply this family-specific full fiber law. Searches also compared the equivalents 'graphical p-groups', 'class-two graph quotients', 'friendship/windmill graphs', and 'projected decomposable bivectors'. A residual risk remains from older unexamined class-two commutator-width literature, including Sinha's work on class-two nilpotent linear groups, and possible equivalent formulations not retrieved. Priority is therefore not represented as independently established.

## Value — PASS
The commutator word map is a natural group invariant, and this theorem supplies the complete nonzero target distribution, an exact support obstruction to being a commutator, and the surprising sharp width jump from a triangle to any bouquet of at least two triangles. Its statement applies uniformly to infinitely many graph sizes and all finite fields, and goes substantially beyond the known commuting probability at the identity. The image count measures precisely how far the commutator word map is from surjective even though the derived subgroup is generated by individual commutators.
