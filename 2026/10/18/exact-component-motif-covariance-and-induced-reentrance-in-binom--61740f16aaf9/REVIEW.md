# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** For each candidate component vertex set, conditioning on its being an \(H\)-component removes all crossing edges, fixes the number of internal connected \(F\)-motifs, and leaves an independent graph of order \(n-h\) outside. Covariance linearity yields an exact identity. Differentiating the binomial probability factor gives the complete sign classification. Finite exhaustive tests independently check 660 rational identities. The proof, not those tests, establishes all sizes and probabilities.

## Originality

**PASS on investigated evidence, with residual priority risk.** Ráth's component-size change-of-measure formula (arXiv:1707.05169, Proposition 1.1 and Lemma 2.1) does not provide the fixed-shape component–motif sign classification. Sah and Sawhney's full open-access subgraph-count treatment (arXiv:2006.11369) focuses on local limit theorems and graph factors rather than this mixed finite-size covariance. Ellis and Ferry's subgraph-overlay work (2010) studies variance of ordinary subgraph counts; material accessible during the comparison did not settle this mixed component statistic. A general uninspected identity could imply the result, particularly in older random-graph enumeration literature. No claim of proven worldwide priority is made.

## Value

**PASS.** The result supplies a natural complete covariance phase diagram for connected local motifs versus global component types. Requiring the motif to be induced produces two sign changes while ordinary subgraph counting admits at most one. The three-vertex-path example is the smallest reentrant instance, with two sharply separated large-order transition scales. These are structural dependence phenomena, not only recomputed marginal moments.

## Independent assessment

The finite verifier does not replace the analytical proof. The literature search cannot establish uniqueness or replace independent priority evaluation. The edge-independent \(G(n,p)\) assumption and connected-motif hypothesis are essential.
