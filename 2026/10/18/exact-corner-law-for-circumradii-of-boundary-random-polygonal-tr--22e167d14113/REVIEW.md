# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any fixed genuine convex polygon, the diameter bound for a triangle of circumradius at most \(t\) confines every sufficiently small noncollinear triple to two sides incident at a common vertex. The exact, scale-homogeneous three-dimensional corner integral reduces by Tonelli's theorem to a squared elementary one-dimensional integral. Six labeling choices yield the coefficient in the theorem. Strict convexity of the exterior-angle score and Jensen's inequality prove the sharp equiangular minimum. Boundary cases and the convention for collinear sampled triples are stated explicitly. A standalone script checks 5 angular integrals, the regular-polygon reduction and Jensen tests, and direct boundary samples; none of its finite tests is relied upon as proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk

Kórus (2020) samples points on a circle or random side-length triples; Labelle (2023) samples side-length triples under a perimeter constraint. These are not three arclength-uniform points on the boundary of an arbitrary fixed polygon. The closest related boundary-random-polytope survey addresses other observables. Indexed work on three interior-uniform points from a smooth planar body has a fourth-order small-radius tail, whereas the present result is a third-order exact law supported on polygon corners. A two-point boundary distance result does not imply the three-point circumradius law. Searches for the claim's equivalent formulations, angle coefficient, distribution and sharp equality class did not locate an exact covering theorem. An inaccessible or unindexed equivalent derivation is a residual originality risk, not a claim of global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This gives a complete and *exact* local probability law for all convex Euclidean polygons, with a universal cubic exponent, an explicit angle/perimeter fingerprint and a sharp, nontrivial equiangular optimizer. The positivity interval and exactness distinguish it from a leading-order numerical fit. The observation supplies a benchmark for stochastic convex geometry at nonsmooth boundaries and distinguishes polygon-boundary sampling from smooth-body interior sampling without assuming a downstream theorem.
