# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For a generic normal, the three positive vertex heights have sorted magnitudes \(A>B>C\). An offset plane cuts four octahedral edges for \(B<|t|<A\), and six for \(|t|<B\); conditioning invariant-plane measure therefore yields \(\Pr(N=6)=\mathbb EB/\mathbb EA\). The exact identity \(B=\sum_{i<j}\max(x_i,x_j)-2A\) and the pairwise spherical maximum integrals yield \(\mathbb EB=S/2-2\mathbb EA\). The polyhedral Steiner/mean-width formula, with four edges in each of three length and exterior-angle classes, yields \(\mathbb EA=T/\pi\), proving the closed law. In the axial case, direct differentiation gives \(T'=S'\phi\) and \((T/S)'=(\sqrt2S'/S^2)(\phi-\psi)\), whose sign changes once at aspect ratio one. Exact limits close both boundaries. These steps prove the infinite continuum assertions; numerical tests alone would not.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest existing regular-cross-polytope affine-Crofton face-vector formula applies to equal axes, and therefore the equal-axis number is expressly excluded from novelty. The full triaxial **noncentral affine-section** probability is distinct from the previously published all-triaxial and axial **orthogonal shadow** probabilities [2,3]; projection normals are Haar uniform without a random offset, whereas affine section normals are width-weighted. The 2026 slicing study [1] discusses slice combinatorics and equivalence but does not evaluate this continuous geometric probability. Comparison covered parameter substitutions, dualities, equivalent order-statistic formulations, and stronger results. Residual risk: older or unindexed Crofton integral-geometry literature may contain an equivalent specialization. Absence from searches is not proof of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact distribution across two independent aspect ratios is a complete, geometrically natural probability law, not a single unmotivated numerical example. Its strict axial optimizer and the distinct flat/tall degeneration limits explain how the incidence geometry of a real convex body alters randomly oriented and translated cuts. It supplies a benchmark for distinguishing affine Crofton sections from Haar projections. It does not claim a triaxial global optimizer or a general-purpose probabilistic inequality.
