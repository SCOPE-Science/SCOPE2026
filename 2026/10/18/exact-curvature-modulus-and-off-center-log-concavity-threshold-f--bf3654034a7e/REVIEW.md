# Scientific review: symmetric triple-Gaussian curvature

## Correctness — PASS

The statement covers every strictly positive separation and variance, every central weight in \((0,1)\), and all real observations. Normalization follows immediately from the three Gaussian components. Log-density differentiation gives the rational function \(R_c\), and two exact factorizations prove its global maximum for both parameter branches, including the shared equality point. The resulting piecewise curvature modulus yields all four parameter regimes; the cases \(s=\sqrt3\), \(s=2\), and limiting central weights were explicitly checked. For strict unimodality, the global derivative argument is independent of finite numerical sampling. The special curvature deficit \(-1/1023\) is exact.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

The closest direct literature covers (i) the sharp **two-component** normal-mixture threshold (Cule, Samworth, Stewart, 2010), (ii) general mixture Hessian formulas without sign classification (Lau, Liu, Pock, 2023), and (iii) nonsharp general Hessian bounds and local conditional sampling results (Cheng, Wang, Zhang, Zhu, 2023). Their stated claims and supporting material do not determine the three-component best curvature modulus, the \(\sqrt3\) off-center bifurcation, or the sharp impossibility at separation \(2\). A universal Hessian identity is not itself the solved maximum over observations. An independent exhaustive priority review has not occurred; equivalent earlier formulations remain a risk.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete shape classification for a natural first nontrivial extension of the binary Gaussian-location mixture, with an exact quantitative curvature modulus. The phase changes at two intrinsic geometric separations and demonstrates that strictly unimodal densities arbitrarily close to a Gaussian in total variation may still have a nonconvex negative log-density in remote tails. The sharp modulus is the directly relevant global curvature parameter for analyses predicated on strong log-concavity, though no sampling convergence theorem is claimed.

## Limits

The result is restricted to three symmetric Gaussian locations sharing one variance. The proof is analytic; the included finite checks are reproducibility aids. Residual risk concerns prior results under alternate terminology, not a known covering statement.
