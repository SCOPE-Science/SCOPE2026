# Review of exact cyclic palindromic-deletion spectrum

## Correctness — PASS
For each order-five binary de Bruijn cycle, each directed length-five edge determines one vertex-pairing successor. Conversely an assignment of one of two bijections at each of the 16 order-four vertices produces a de Bruijn cycle exactly when the edge-successor permutation consists of a single 32-cycle. Hence all \(2^{16}\) assignments constitute a complete enumeration. An independent Eulerian depth-first generator returns the identical 2048 rooted cycles. The interval dynamic program proves longest-palindromic-subsequence length by induction, and an independent LCS-versus-reversal implementation agrees on every one of 65536 rooted rotations. The five multiplicities sum to 2048. The attached independent code paths check the complete CSV, two exact moments, and all symmetry orbits. Boundary cuts and exact one-circumference normalization are explicit. This is an exhaustive finite proof; no all-order extrapolation is made.

## Originality — PASS against available evidence
The inspected full text of Chowdhury et al. (arXiv:1110.5296, 2011) gives general algorithms for common palindromic subsequences, not a population census of de Bruijn cycles. The inspected OEIS A318916 entry tabulates the *unrestricted* minimum over binary circles (24 at length 32) but does not imply the de Bruijn-constrained five-value spectrum. Brodal et al. (ESA 2024) treats the general LPS/LCS reduction, with no de Bruijn-distribution claim in its inspected abstract; its complete proof text was not examined in this review. The nearby historical circular-de-Bruijn palindrome claim counts **contiguous factors**, not scattered subsequences, a separate historical four-run formula counts **distinct subsequences**, and a historical de Bruijn-circle palindromic-length classification minimizes the **number of contiguous palindromic partition blocks** rather than the number of deletions to one scattered palindrome. None is the present cyclic longest scattered-subsequence statistic. Mathematical-record searches for exact circular LPS tables, deletion distance, universal cycles, and rotational variants returned different subjects; a negative search is not proof of historical priority. Unindexed or supplementary numerical enumerations remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is the minimum number of deletions needed to make any single cut of a universal binary circle into a palindrome. This is a natural nonlocal sequence statistic, motivated independently by the circular LPS extremal problem OEIS A318916. Restricting to order-five de Bruijn cycles raises the tabulated unrestricted minimum from 24 to the *proved* constrained minimum 25, and yields the complete five-value distribution, exact moments, 528 reversal/complement symmetry orbits, and full cycle-by-cycle data. No pre-existing generic LPS algorithm by itself yields these population frequencies. The result is a natural complete finite classification rather than a trivial renaming or an arbitrary isolated example.
