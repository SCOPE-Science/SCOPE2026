# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The stated \(C^1\), fixed-order, self-inclusive complete-mean model has an exact sweep Jacobian \(M=-c(I+cL)^{-1}U\), derived by separately accounting for previously updated and not-yet-updated coordinates. The determinant lemma and an explicitly solved triangular recurrence yield the characteristic polynomial, including its removable singularity at \(z=1\). A strict convex-combination modulus argument proves every root is internal when \(0<c<1\); at \(c=1\) the polynomial is the cyclotomic-root sum and Cayley–Hamilton gives exact linear order; at \(c>1\) the determinant is an instability obstruction. The threshold, endpoint, and logistic specialization all follow analytically. Exact symbolic calculations independently reproduce the matrices and characteristic polynomials in dimensions one through eight. The boundary assertion is *linear only*; its nonlinear stability is not inferred.

## Originality — PASS, with identifiable bibliographic risk
The full 1998 Abramson–Zanette primary article introduces complete-mean coupling but chooses random update order; the fixed deterministic repeated order changes the spectral question. Full inspection of Section 4 of Atmanspacher–Filk–Scheingraber (2004/2005) found general Jacobian products and heuristic randomized-update conditions, not an exact cyclic complete-mean polynomial. Jiang–Antillón–Escalona (1999 and 2000) explicitly study sequential global coupling, but the available abstracts describe numerical dynamics and were not sufficient for a full negative-coverage conclusion. The 2019 Deshmukh–Matte–Gade sequential-sublattice study concerns nearest-neighbor update blocks; its finite-mode resonances are related but do not directly imply this all-\(N\) complete-graph classification. Semantic searches of recorded research findings found related lattice and Gauss–Seidel results but no statement-level equivalent. **Remaining risk:** an unavailable sequential-coupling article may already imply a special case, or a classical weighted iteration theorem may encode the algebra in another terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The object is the standard full-mean coupling model, and its deterministic site-update rule is a natural alternative to randomized asynchronous sampling. The theorem supplies a complete sharp *finite population* stability threshold for every negative-slope scalar fixed point, a universal finite-order spectral resonance exactly on the boundary, and an explicit difference from simultaneous updates. For the fully chaotic logistic parameter it identifies the minimum population size that permits local stabilization. The exact degree-dependent spectrum, rather than a small numerical improvement, explains why update order and network size qualitatively change local dynamics.

## Scientific limitations
The conclusion concerns the homogeneous fixed point, full self-inclusive mean, and one deterministic sweep permutation. It does not establish nonlinear dynamics at the resonance, global basins, random-order stability, or extensions to partial coupling. Two especially close sequential-update primary articles were unavailable in full; independent verification of originality is therefore still warranted.
