# Scientific review: heterogeneous-star Dudley dual norm

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns the sum-normalized real bounded-Lipschitz dual norm on a finite vertex metric star with arbitrary positive radii and arbitrary signed atomic masses. Star geometry makes all pairwise Lipschitz restrictions equivalent to independent hub-to-leaf restrictions. Optimizing each signed leaf over its permitted interval reduces the problem to two nonnegative cap variables. The exact fractional objective is linear-fractional between finitely many arm-length knots and is monotone on each one-variable knot interval; its two infinite-tail limits are bounded by the already included constant-potential value. That proves the finite grid formula. The stated exact sorting algorithm follows from the equivalent monotone piecewise-linear zero test, with all switch values obtained from tail masses. The bound includes degenerate zero masses, tied radii, nonzero total mass, and singleton stars. Rational and numerical cross-checks are separately recorded in VERIFICATION.md. Their finite coverage is not a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by residual bibliographic risk)

The strongest directly applicable published prior result inspected is Hille–Theewis, arXiv:2206.12234, Section 6: it gives an LP on an arbitrary finite metric, so it computes any individual weighted-star instance, but it does not supply a specialized closed form, arm-length knot reduction, explicit attaining class, or exact near-linear sorting/root algorithm. Hille–Theewis, arXiv:2305.08500, provides general norming/extremal sets and specifically motivates practical computation, without an evaluated weighted-star dual expression. The published *Exact Dudley norms and mass-imbalance thresholds on equiradial stars* covers the equal-arm, zero-total-mass specialization, not arbitrary heterogeneous radii and signed total mass. The independently distinct maximum-normalized Fortet–Mourier norm must not be confused with the sum-normalized norm here. published-finding corpus and other literature were searched for weighted stars, signed-mass norms, aliases including the Dudley and flat norms, and equivalent piecewise-linear support optimization; no inspected stronger formula dominates the present assertion. Full bibliographic completeness cannot be guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The bounded-Lipschitz dual norm is a standard natural distance on atomic signed measures, including problems where total mass changes. Unequal arms are the generic parameter regime for metric stars and prevent collapse to the known equal-arm two-branch formula. The new formula gives exact values, extremizing test functions, a sharp finite candidate set, and an exact \(O(m\log m)\) arithmetic algorithm instead of a general-purpose finite LP or enumeration of potentially many extreme points. The structural reduction into independent positive- and negative-arm caps explains this efficiency. This is a meaningful classification and computational advance on a natural infinite family, not merely a numerical lookup or one-off evaluation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Relevant primary texts: S. C. Hille and E. S. Theewis, arXiv:2206.12234v2, especially Section 6, Lemma 6.1 and (39); and arXiv:2305.08500v1, especially Sections 1, 5 and 6. The published equal-arm result was checked in its full RESULT.md and is a strictly narrower geometry and measure class. The 2012 Sriperumbudur et al. empirical IPM paper could be assessed only through its abstract and the account in Hille–Theewis; its full text was not retrieved, and it remains a bibliographic risk, though the described technique is general LP rather than this explicit weighted-star root method. Search limitations also include possible other names for a general flat metric or a prior algebraic reduction buried within a general optimization treatment. The theorem is finite, real, and vertex-supported; it does not cover arbitrary trees, continuum arms, or bit-complexity of exact rational arithmetic.
