# Review of the dyadic-residue block-Walsh embedding theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof expands the precisely normalized sampled Gram matrix and applies Walsh orthogonality within each complete dyadic row block. The resulting exact identity establishes all support-class rank bounds, the necessary-and-sufficient all-sample isometry criterion, and the two-plane binomial law. The finite checker exhaustively verifies direct integer Gram entries and support counts for small dimensions; the infinite statement rests on the explicit character identity, not on enumeration.

**Originality — PASS (within the inspected literature).** The motivating 2022 construction and its 2023 expanded follow-up provide concentration-based embedding guarantees and leverage-score estimates, not an exact dyadic-residue decomposition for every coordinate support or a binomial finite-sample rank-loss distribution. The 2023 Balabanov–Beaupère–Grigori–Lederer locally formed block sketch is a different random matrix; a two-character cyclic-frame erasure theorem concerns a different character group and deterministic deletion masks. Relevant published mathematical records and prior related work were compared by their stated implications, including Fourier-frame spectral classifications. The residual uncertainty is an unindexed equivalent formulation in broader finite-group sampling literature.

**Value — PASS.** The result identifies a concrete sampling-design boundary that uniform block Frobenius leverage cannot detect: any coordinate support using distinct low-bit classes is perfectly isometric after a single complete block, while a pair sharing one class can lose rank with an explicit probability independent of the block's row count. It classifies all such coordinate supports and counts them, and its distribution quantifies the number of independent blocks required to suppress this failure. These are direct structural facts about a published randomized numerical linear algebra construction, not a change of notation or an arbitrary isolated numerical example.

No theorem about arbitrary subspaces, random row permutations, or without-replacement sampling follows from the claim. A related folklore finite-group Fourier aliasing formulation may exist and should be considered in any subsequent independent assessment.
