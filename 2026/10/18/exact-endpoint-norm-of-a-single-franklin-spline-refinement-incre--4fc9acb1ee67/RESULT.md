# Exact endpoint norm of a single Franklin-spline refinement increment

## Finding

Let \(S\subset L^2(\mathbb R)\) be the closed space of continuous, piecewise-affine functions with knots at every integer, with orthogonal projector \(P\). For \(0<t<1\), let \(S_t\) be the corresponding space after inserting the additional knot \(t\), and let \(P_t\) be its orthogonal projector. Define the refinement increment \(Q_t=P_t-P\). Both projectors and the increment admit bounded extensions to the endpoint spaces.

Put \(u=t(1-t)\), \(s=\sqrt3\), and
\[
R(u)=\frac{(96s-144)u^3+(84s-96)u^2+(135-57s)u+12(s-1)}{(48s-80)u^3+(60-28s)u^2+(6+4s)u+6(s-1)}.
\]
Then the sharp endpoint norms satisfy
\[
\boxed{\|Q_t\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}=\|Q_t\|_{L^1\to L^1}=R(t(1-t)).}
\]
The function \(R\) is strictly increasing on \([0,1/4]\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{2<\|Q_t\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}\le\frac{27}{26}+\frac{45\sqrt3}{52}=2.537351660396\ldots.}
\]
Equality in the upper bound holds exactly at \(t=1/2\); the norm tends to \(2\) as \(t\downarrow0\) or \(t\uparrow1\), with neither endpoint included. More generally, \(\|Q_t\|\) strictly increases as the insertion moves from either old endpoint toward the cell midpoint.

## Assumptions and scope

All inner products and integrals use ordinary Lebesgue measure on the full real line. The spline spaces are the closed subspaces of \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) generated by the integer translates of the cardinal hat \(N_j(x)=\max\{1-|x-j|,0\}\); the refined space adds one continuous tent with apex at the inserted knot. The theorem concerns the **increment** \(P_t-P\), not the norm of either whole orthoprojector. Its statement holds for every real \(t\) strictly between \(0\) and \(1\). The result is independent of which unit cell is refined by translation invariance, and of common positive rescaling of the grid.

## Proof

**Step 1: The one-dimensional complement.** Write \(h_t(x)=x/t\) for \(0\le x\le t\), \(h_t(x)=(1-x)/(1-t)\) for \(t\le x\le1\), and \(h_t(x)=0\) elsewhere. The refined space is \(S_t=S+\operatorname{span}\{h_t\}\). Define \(g_t=h_t-Ph_t\); then \(g_t\perp S\), \(g_t\ne0\), and \(S_t=S\oplus\operatorname{span}\{g_t\}\), so
\[
Q_tf=\frac{\langle f,g_t\rangle}{\|g_t\|_2^2}\,g_t.\tag{1}
\]

**Step 2: Invert the unperturbed infinite Gram matrix.** The cardinal hats obey
\[
\langle N_j,N_k\rangle=\begin{cases}2/3,&j=k,\\1/6,&|j-k|=1,\\0,&|j-k|>1.\end{cases}
\]
Put \(q=2-\sqrt3\). The bounded inverse Gram matrix is
\[
(G^{-1})_{jk}=\sqrt3(-q)^{|j-k|};\tag{2}
\]
this follows by checking \(q^2-4q+1=0\) and the diagonal normalization. The right side is exponentially summable, so inversion is valid on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\). Since
\[
\langle h_t,N_0\rangle=(2-t)/6,\qquad\langle h_t,N_1\rangle=(1+t)/6,
\]
with all other hat inner products zero, the nodal values \(c_j=(Ph_t)(j)\) satisfy
\[
c_0=\frac{1-(s-1)t}{2},\qquad
c_1=\frac{2-s+(s-1)t}{2},\qquad
c_j=\begin{cases}c_0(-q)^{-j},&j\le0,\\c_1(-q)^{j-1},&j\ge1.\end{cases}\tag{3}
\]
In particular, \(c_0,c_1>0\) and the tails alternate sign, decaying geometrically.

**Step 3: Exact norms of the new orthogonal spline.** At all old integer knots \(g_t(j)=-c_j\). At the newly inserted knot,
\[
d=g_t(t)=1-(1-t)c_0-tc_1=\frac12+(s-1)t(1-t).\tag{4}
\]
Direct subtraction shows \(d>c_0\) and \(d>c_1\) for \(0<t<1\). Every segment of \(g_t\) is affine, and \(|c_j|\le\max(c_0,c_1)\), hence
\[
\|g_t\|_\infty=d.\tag{5}
\]
By orthogonality and \(\|h_t\|_2^2=1/3\),
\[
E(t):=\|g_t\|_2^2=\frac13-\frac{(2-t)c_0+(1+t)c_1}{6}
=\frac{s+2(s-1)t(1-t)}{12}>0.\tag{6}
\]
For a line segment of length \(\ell\) whose endpoint values have opposite signs and absolute magnitudes \(a,b>0\), direct integration of its absolute value gives \(\ell(a^2+b^2)/(2(a+b))\). Apply this to all unit cells outside \([0,1]\), using the geometric values (3):
\[
\int_{\mathbb R\setminus[0,1]}|g_t(x)|\,dx
=\frac{(c_0+c_1)(1+q^2)}{2(1-q^2)}=\frac{s-1}{2}.\tag{7}
\]
The two pieces inside \([0,1]\) have endpoint pairs \((-c_0,d)\) and \((d,-c_1)\). It follows that
\[
B(t):=\|g_t\|_1=\frac{s-1}{2}
+\frac{t(c_0^2+d^2)}{2(c_0+d)}
+\frac{(1-t)(c_1^2+d^2)}{2(c_1+d)}.\tag{8}
\]
This convergent expression includes both infinite tails; no truncation is used in the theorem.

**Step 4: Sharp endpoint norm.** Equation (1), \(g_t\in L^1\cap L^\infty\), and the sign-function witness \(f=\operatorname{sgn}(g_t)\) show
\[
\|Q_t\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}=\frac{d B(t)}{E(t)}.\tag{9}
\]
The transposed rank-one kernel is identical, proving equality with the \(L^1\to L^1\) norm. Algebraic simplification of (3)–(9), replacing \(t(1-t)\) by \(u\), gives precisely the stated rational function \(R(u)\).

**Step 5: Strict monotonicity and extremizers.** Define
\[
H(u)=(-40+24s)u^3+(30-14s)u^2+(3+2s)u+3(s-1),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(u)={}&(1760s-3040)u^4+(3216-1840s)u^3\\
&+(634s-1062)u^2+(636-356s)u+(94s-159).
\end{aligned}
\]
Differentiating the rational expression yields the exact identity
\[
R'(u)=\frac{3J(u)}{H(u)^2}.\tag{10}
\]
All coefficients of \(H\) and \(J\) are strictly positive for \(s=\sqrt3\) (for example, the elementary rational bounds \(1.732<s<1.733\) certify each sign). Thus \(R'(u)>0\) throughout \([0,1/4]\). Finally,
\[
R(0)=2,\qquad R(1/4)=\frac{27}{26}+\frac{45s}{52}.
\]
Since \(u=t(1-t)\) is symmetric and uniquely maximized at \(t=1/2\), this proves all asserted equalities, strict bounds, and uniqueness conditions.

## Relationship to prior work

Ciesielski and Kamont (2004) derive Gram-inverse formulas and sharp Lebesgue bounds for **whole** Franklin spline orthogonal projections along specified grids. Their formulas (2.2) and (2.11)–(2.12) concern kernels of \(P\), not the rank-one kernel of \(P_t-P\). Passenbrunner (2011) determines a sharp **periodic whole-projector** Lebesgue constant for a partially uniform knot family, likewise not the present continuously parameterized increment. Passenbrunner (2012) supplies general geometric-decay estimates for spline Gram inverses, not an exact one-cell increment norm. Bechler's 2003 Strömberg-wavelet article is a plausible additional priority source: its abstract and the 2004 description concern whole partial-sum projection constants; the complete primary text was not available for comparison. The present claim is therefore specifically restricted to the **sharp single-insertion increment and its strict midpoint extremality**, not general Franklin bounds, Gram inversion, or the existence of wavelet refinements.

## Limitations

The theorem is for first-degree continuous splines on the full real line, one additional knot inside one previously unit-length cell, and the two endpoint operator norms. It does not determine the norm of the refined whole projection \(P_t\), prove corresponding facts for nonuniform underlying meshes, or address higher-degree splines. A historical-priority risk remains from literature inaccessible in full, especially the Strömberg-wavelet article. Reproducibility tests do not constitute an independent mathematical or bibliographic audit.
