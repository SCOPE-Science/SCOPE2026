# Exact endpoint norms and extremal scale selections for finite dyadic Haar projections

## Finding

For each integer \(d\ge1\), let \(\mathcal F_m\) be the dyadic sigma-algebra on \([0,1]\) generated by the \(2^m\) intervals of length \(2^{-m}\), let \(E_m\) denote conditional expectation onto \(\mathcal F_m\), and set \(D_m=E_m-E_{m-1}\) for \(1\le m\le d\). For every subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\), put \(P_S=\sum_{m\in S}D_m\), acting on real or complex \(L^1(0,1)\). Then

\[
\boxed{\max_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}\|P_S\|_{L^1\to L^1}
=\frac d3+\frac79+\frac{(-1)^d}{9\,2^{d-1}}.}
\]

The sharp growth rate is \(\frac13\log_2(2^d)\), with the displayed exact boundary correction at every finite depth. The maximizing selectors are completely classified: for \(d=1\) the only choice is \(S=\{1\}\), for \(d=2\) it is \(S=\{1,2\}\), and for \(d\ge3\) there are exactly two. In the notation \(b_m=\mathbf 1_{\{m\in S\}}\), both have \(b_1=b_d=1\); one takes \(b_2=0\) and alternates through \(b_{d-1}\), while the other takes \(b_2=1\) and alternates through \(b_{d-1}\). The terminal digit \(b_d=1\) is imposed after this alternation.

Here only **scale-homogeneous** Haar spectral projections are optimized: at any chosen depth, every Haar function of that scale is included. No claim is made about projections onto arbitrary subsets of individual Haar functions, or about the norm of an arbitrary martingale transform.

## Assumptions and scope

Use Lebesgue probability measure on \([0,1]\). Each \(E_m\) is the usual dyadic conditional expectation, with \(E_0f=\int_0^1f\). The operator \(P_S\) is finite rank, bounded on \(L^1\), and annihilates constants. Its finite-dimensional restriction acts on the \(2^d\) depth-\(d\) atoms with normalized counting measure. The theorem holds for every integer \(d\ge1\) and every subset of the \(d\) positive Haar levels, over either scalar field.

The source motivating the scale-selection question is Seeger and Ullrich, *Lower bounds for Haar projections: deterministic examples*, arXiv:1511.01470v1 (first public version dated November 4, 2015; primary MSC \(46E35\)). Its Sobolev–Triebel–Lizorkin setting is not the endpoint problem solved here.

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction to one column.** Since \(P_S=P_SE_d\) and \(E_d\) is an \(L^1\) contraction, its norm equals that of the induced matrix on the depth-\(d\) atoms. Every column of that matrix is a rearrangement of every other column by a measure-preserving automorphism of the dyadic tree that fixes each Haar level. Thus its \(\ell^1\) norm is the absolute sum of any column.

Fix the first depth-\(d\) atom as the input basis vector. Write \(b_m=\mathbf1_{\{m\in S\}}\) and \(A_\ell=\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}b_m2^{m-1}\), with \(A_0=0\). There are \(2^{d-\ell-1}\) output atoms sharing exactly the first \(\ell\) binary digits with the input atom, for each \(0\le\ell<d\), and one output atom sharing all \(d\) digits. On a shell of common-prefix length \(\ell\), the matrix entry is \(2^{-d}(A_\ell-b_{\ell+1}2^\ell)\); at the input atom it is \(2^{-d}A_d\). Hence

\[
\|P_S\|_{1\to1}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\frac{|A_\ell-b_{\ell+1}2^\ell|}{2^{\ell+1}}+\frac{A_d}{2^d}.
\tag{1}
\]

Since \(0\le A_\ell<2^\ell\), define \(t_0=0\) and \(t_m=A_m/2^m=(t_{m-1}+b_m)/2\). Formula (1) becomes the exact finite-control identity

\[
\|P_S\|_{1\to1}=\frac12\sum_{m=1}^{d}|b_m-t_{m-1}|+t_d.
\tag{2}
\]

**2. Solving the finite Bellman equation.** For \(k\ge0\) and \(0\le t\le1\), let \(V_k(t)\) be the maximum of the objective \(\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{k}|b_j-t_{j-1}|+t_k\), starting from \(t_0=t\) and choosing the \(k\) binary digits freely. Directly, \(V_0(t)=t\) and

\[
V_k(t)=\max\left\{\frac t2+V_{k-1}(t/2),\quad
\frac{1-t}{2}+V_{k-1}((1+t)/2)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The base case is \(V_1(t)=\max\{t,1\}=1\) on \([0,1]\). Suppose inductively that \(V_{k-1}(t)=a_{k-1}+c_{k-1}|t-1/2|\), where \(0\le c_{k-1}<1\). Substitution into (3) gives the affine alternatives

\[
a_{k-1}+\frac{c_{k-1}}2+\frac{1-c_{k-1}}2t,
\qquad
 a_{k-1}+\frac12-\frac{1-c_{k-1}}2t.
\]

They cross exactly at \(t=1/2\), and therefore

\[
V_k(t)=a_k+c_k|t-1/2|,\qquad
 a_k=a_{k-1}+\frac{1+c_{k-1}}4,\quad
 c_k=\frac{1-c_{k-1}}2.
\tag{4}
\]

Starting with \(a_1=1\) and \(c_1=0\), one obtains for every \(k\ge1\)

\[
c_k=\frac13-\frac13(-1/2)^{k-1},\qquad
 a_k=\frac{k}{3}+\frac{11}{18}+\frac1{18}(-1/2)^{k-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus (2) and (5) give

\[
\max_S\|P_S\|_{1\to1}=V_d(0)=a_d+c_d/2
=\frac d3+\frac79+\frac{(-1)^d}{9\,2^{d-1}}.
\]

**3. Equality and all maximizers.** For \(k\ge2\), the affine alternatives in (3) are strictly ordered except at \(t=1/2\), because \(c_{k-1}<1\): the next digit must be \(1\) for \(t<1/2\), must be \(0\) for \(t>1/2\), and either digit is optimal at equality. For the last step \(k=1\), digit \(1\) is strictly optimal whenever \(t<1\). Starting from \(t_0=0\), for \(d\ge2\) the first digit is forced to \(1\), making \(t_1=1/2\). If \(d\ge3\), the second digit is the one possible tie; thereafter \(t_m\neq1/2\) and the preceding strict rule forces alternating digits until the terminal forced \(1\). Both listed selectors are therefore optimal and no others are. The cases \(d=1,2\) follow directly.

## Verification

The direct finite-matrix formula (1) and the Bellman formula (4) were checked in exact rational arithmetic with the standalone `checker.py`. All \(126\) selectors through depth \(6\) were tested against every column of the actual conditional-expectation matrix; all \(65{,}534\) selectors through depth \(15\) were enumerated to confirm the closed maximum and the complete maximizer classification. The checker requires only the Python standard library and can be run with `python checker.py`.

These finite checks are consistency tests, not the infinite-depth justification; equations (1)–(5) prove the theorem for every \(d\).

## Relationship to prior work

Seeger and Ullrich study Haar projection numbers in Sobolev and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and construct scale selections with growth in smoothness norms, not the sharp finite-depth \(L^1\) endpoint norm or all optimizers [1]. Kadets studies monotone bounded completeness of the Haar basis in \(L^1\), not scale-selection projection constants [2]. Osękowski proves sharp restricted weak-type inequalities for Haar projections [3] and sharp distribution-function bounds for signed Haar transforms [5], neither of which specifies this finite strong \(L^1\) optimization. Astashkin, Huang, Pliev, Sukochev, and Zanin single out an infinite alternating-scale martingale transform for sharp distributional comparisons with the Calderón operator [4]; its theorem does not identify the depth-by-depth exact maximizing selectors or the rational boundary term here. The occurrence of alternating scales in [4] is important prior structural context, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The theorem covers the classical uniform binary dyadic filtration and homogeneous selection of full levels only. It does not settle weighted or irregular trees, general individual-Haar selection, arbitrary predictable martingale multipliers, or \(L^p\) constants away from \(p=1\). Priority against unindexed historical endpoint calculations cannot be guaranteed; the related 2014 Haar weak-type paper has been inspected in full, but unindexed earlier endpoint calculations remain possible. No independent verification has been performed.
