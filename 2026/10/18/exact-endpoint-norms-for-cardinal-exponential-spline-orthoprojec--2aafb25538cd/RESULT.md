# Exact endpoint norms for cardinal exponential-spline orthoprojectors

## Finding
For each tension parameter \(\theta>0\), let \(P_\theta\) be the \(L^2(\mathbb R)\)-orthogonal projector onto the closed shift-invariant space generated by the compactly supported cardinal function
\[
\phi_\theta(x)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sinh(\theta(1-|x|))}{\sinh\theta},&|x|\le 1,\\
0,&|x|>1.
\end{cases}
\]
The orthogonal projector extends boundedly to \(L^1(\mathbb R)\) and \(L^\infty(\mathbb R)\), and its **exact and equal sharp endpoint norms** are
\[
\boxed{\|P_\theta\|_{L^1\to L^1}=\|P_\theta\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}
=\frac{2\big[(1+q)(1+\cosh\theta)-2\sqrt{1+2q\cosh\theta+q^2}\big]}
{\Delta(1-q)\theta\sinh\theta}},
\]
where, with dependence on \(\theta\) understood,
\[
A=\frac{\sinh\theta\cosh\theta-\theta}{\theta\sinh^2\theta},\qquad
B=\frac{\theta\cosh\theta-\sinh\theta}{2\theta\sinh^2\theta},\qquad
\Delta=\sqrt{A^2-4B^2},\qquad q=\frac{2B}{A+\Delta}.
\]
Here \(0<q<1\), \(\Delta>0\). The pointwise Lebesgue function \(x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb R}|P_\theta(x,y)|\,dy\) is strictly convex on every open knot interval and reaches its global maximum **exactly at the integer knots**.

The tension response is not constant or monotone on the positive half-line:
\[
\|P_\theta\|_{\infty\to\infty}=2+\frac{\theta^2}{120}+O(\theta^4)\quad(\theta\downarrow0),
\qquad
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\|P_\theta\|_{\infty\to\infty}=2.
\]
Consequently, it has a global maximum strictly above \(2\) at some finite positive tension. No uniqueness or exact value for the optimal tension is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
The underlying measure is Lebesgue measure on the whole real line. On each interval between consecutive integer knots, a function in the spline space satisfies the homogeneous equation \(f''=\theta^2 f\); the continuous compactly supported cardinal functions \(\phi_\theta(\cdot-j)\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), form its stable shift basis. This statement concerns an **orthogonal least-squares projector**, not the cardinal interpolation projector and not the sharp mesh-independent worst-case constant over all knot sequences. For knot separation \(h>0\) and differential equation \(f''=\alpha^2 f\), the sole dimensionless parameter is \(\theta=|\alpha|h\), by unitary dilation. The limiting case \(\theta=0\) is the ordinary uniform piecewise-linear spline projector, of endpoint norm \(2\).

## Proof
**1. Stable Gram inverse.** Put \(\phi_j=\phi_\theta(\cdot-j)\). Compact support makes the Gram matrix \(G=(\langle\phi_i,\phi_j\rangle)\) tridiagonal Toeplitz, with diagonal \(A=2\int_0^1\phi_\theta(u)^2\,du\) and adjacent diagonal \(B=\int_0^1\phi_\theta(u)\phi_\theta(1-u)\,du\). Elementary hyperbolic integration gives the displayed formulas. Positivity and the identity
\[
A-2B=\int_0^1\big[\phi_\theta(u)-\phi_\theta(1-u)\big]^2du>0
\]
show that \(A>2B>0\); thus the symbol \(A+2B\cos\omega\) is uniformly positive and \(\{\phi_j\}\) is a Riesz basis for its closed span. Solving the constant-coefficient recurrence yields the exact inverse
\[
(G^{-1})_{ij}=\Delta^{-1}(-q)^{|i-j|},\qquad q+q^{-1}=A/B.
\]
In particular the inverse coefficients decay geometrically.

**2. All maximizers are knots.** The projection has a real, symmetric, continuous kernel
\[
K_\theta(x,y)=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb Z}\phi_i(x)(G^{-1})_{ij}\phi_j(y),
\]
and the geometric inverse gives uniformly bounded absolute row integrals. For fixed \(y\), on an open interval between successive knots, \(x\mapsto K_\theta(x,y)\) solves \(K''=\theta^2 K\). Its absolute value is convex, in the distribution sense: away from zeros it satisfies \((|K|)''=\theta^2|K|\), while at any simple zero its derivative has a nonnegative jump. Integrating in \(y\), the Lebesgue function \(\mathcal L(x)=\int|K_\theta(x,y)|dy\) satisfies \(\mathcal L''\ge\theta^2\mathcal L>0\) distributionally within each cell. Thus it is strictly convex there. Translation invariance gives equal values at every knot, so they, and only they, maximize \(\mathcal L\). The integral-kernel formula gives the sharp \(L^\infty\) norm; symmetry gives the same \(L^1\) norm.

**3. Evaluate the nodal row exactly.** Since \(\phi_i(0)=\mathbf1_{i=0}\), for \(j\ge0\) and \(u\in[0,1]\),
\[
K_\theta(0,j+u)=\Delta^{-1}(-q)^j
\big[\phi_\theta(u)-q\phi_\theta(1-u)\big].
\]
Reflection symmetry covers negative \(y\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal L(0)=\frac{2J}{\Delta(1-q)},\qquad
J=\int_0^1\left|\frac{\sinh(\theta(1-u))-q\sinh(\theta u)}{\sinh\theta}\right|du.
\]
The integrand changes sign exactly once because the first hyperbolic sine decreases and the second increases. At its root \(u_*\),
\[
\tanh(\theta u_*)=\frac{\sinh\theta}{\cosh\theta+q},\qquad
\cosh(\theta(1-u_*))+q\cosh(\theta u_*)=\sqrt{1+2q\cosh\theta+q^2}.
\]
Splitting the integral at \(u_*\) gives
\[
J=\frac{(1+q)(1+\cosh\theta)-2\sqrt{1+2q\cosh\theta+q^2}}{\theta\sinh\theta},
\]
proving the claimed sharp norm.

**4. Boundary tension and a genuine interior bump.** With \(t=\theta^2\), direct expansion of the Gram integrals gives
\[
A=\frac23-\frac4{45}t+O(t^2),\qquad
B=\frac16-\frac7{180}t+O(t^2),\qquad
\Delta=\frac1{\sqrt3}\left(1-\frac{t}{10}\right)+O(t^2),
\]
\[
q=r-\frac{r}{5\sqrt3}t+O(t^2),\qquad r=2-\sqrt3.
\]
To check the absolute-integral coefficient without differentiating a moving breakpoint incorrectly, use
\[
\phi_\theta(u)=(1-u)+t\frac{-2u+3u^2-u^3}{6}+O(t^2)
\]
uniformly for \(0\le u\le1\). Writing \(\kappa=-r/(5\sqrt3)\), the integrand before taking its absolute value is
\[
1-(1+r)u+t\left[\frac{(-2+r)u+3u^2-(1+r)u^3}{6}-\kappa u\right]+O(t^2).
\]
Its unique zero is at \(v=(1+r)^{-1}+O(t)\). For an antiderivative \(F(w)=[(-2+r)w^2/2+w^3-(1+r)w^4/4]/6-\kappa w^2/2\), the coefficient of \(t\) in \(J\) is \(2F((1+r)^{-1})-F(1)\), because the endpoint shift multiplies the vanishing leading integrand. Substitution into \(2J/[\Delta(1-q)]\) gives exactly \(2+t/120+O(t^2)\).

As \(\theta\to\infty\), the explicit Gram integrals give \(A\sim\theta^{-1}\), \(B\sim e^{-\theta}\), hence \(q\to0\) and \(\Delta\sim\theta^{-1}\). Moreover, by \(\big||a-qb|-a\big|\le qb\) with nonnegative \(a,b\),
\[
J=\frac{\tanh(\theta/2)}{\theta}+O\left(\frac{q}{\theta}\right)\sim\theta^{-1}.
\]
Thus the exact norm tends to \(2\). The positive quadratic correction near zero and the equal endpoint limits force an attained interior maximum greater than \(2\), without any assumption about its uniqueness.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library script `check.py` independently constructs finite tridiagonal Gram inverses, integrates their kernel rows exactly interval by interval, and compares their norm limits with the closed formula. It also checks an independently quadrature-evaluated kernel row and the small-tension expansion at several positive parameter values. The script reports 34 passing consistency assertions. These finite calculations check signs, constants, and normalization; they do not replace the analytical proof on \(\mathbb R\).

## Relationship to prior work
Kayumov's 2004 work on second-order exponential \(L\)-splines determines the sharp **uniform-over-all-meshes** bound \(3\), not the parameter-specific full-line cardinal norm claimed here. Unser and Blu's 2005 cardinal exponential-spline theory provides Riesz-basis and filter representations for orthogonal approximation, including a general projection formula; no exact endpoint operator-norm curve, knot-maximizer rigidity, or finite-tension amplification law was located in the accessible material. The later Chebyshev-spline projection results establish general \(L^\infty\) boundedness, not this sharp tension profile. The zero-tension polynomial spline limit is classical, and no priority is claimed for the tridiagonal Toeplitz inverse or the general projection representation.

## Limitations
The theorem covers the entire real line and uniform knots, not finite intervals with boundary conditions, irregular meshes, other exponential exponents, or the general Chebyshev class. It proves neither a closed form nor uniqueness of the interior maximizing tension. Bibliographic search cannot establish uniqueness; a plausible complete 2004 original thesis and one 2005 paper were not obtainable in full through the accessible retrieval routes, so equivalent historical coverage remains a material residual priority risk.
