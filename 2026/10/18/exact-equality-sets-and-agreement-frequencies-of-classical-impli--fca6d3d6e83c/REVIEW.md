# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof separates necessity and sufficiency. Each formula has a coordinate that, when true, forces the formula true; an equation must therefore hold on an all-true codimension-two face. Conversely, the explicit recursive implication-only translation represents every function forced true by a chosen coordinate, and the four-case construction represents precisely the requested equality set. Counting free assignments over the face gives the complete dyadic interval \([1/4,1]\). The one-variable boundary and the four groupoid conjugates were checked. Exhaustive truth-table closure independently agreed with the set-theoretic characterization for one through three variables. Finite code checks are corroboration, not the infinite-arity proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Cardó (2026), Section 4 Table 1, explicitly labels the spectra of the four relevant two-point operations unknown. Fournier et al. (2012) already provide the stronger **individual-function** description of classical implication-only terms, so that description is credited and not treated as new. Their work studies random formula trees, not the exact equality sets of *pairs* of formulas or the equational probability spectrum. The missing step is the two-coordinate face characterization and its quantitative consequences; the older function classification is an ingredient rather than an explicit statement of the exact spectrum. The 2026 companion paper instead classifies minimal spectra. Published-source and semantic research-record searches found no equivalent exact solution-set or spectrum theorem. Because the solution follows fairly directly from a known clone description, the possibility of an unlocated older observation is a material priority risk.

## Value — PASS

This resolves the exact spectrum left unknown in a recently published full table of all two-element binary algebras. It identifies a sharp, arity-independent \(1/4\) barrier and proves there are no other restrictions above it, strengthened to a complete classification of possible equation-solution sets. The object is the smallest nontrivial classical implicational fragment and the invariant directly measures agreement of two formulas; neither the cutoff nor the complete spectrum was provided by the motivating table. No large downstream theorem is claimed.

## Limitations and sources

The claim applies only to classical, uniform Boolean assignments and equations between positive-letter implication-only formulas. It is distinct from the probability distribution over random formula trees analyzed in Fournier et al. Only small arities were computationally enumerated, and independent assessment remains outstanding. Primary sources are arXiv:2604.08446v1 (Table 1), DOI:10.1002/rsa.20379 (author manuscript and published citation), and arXiv:2603.19487.
