# Exact equational probability spectra of finite Latin Alexander quandles

## Finding
Let \(p\) be a prime, let \(V\) be a nonzero \(n\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_p\), and let \(T\) be a linear automorphism such that \(I-T\) is also invertible. In the finite Latin Alexander quandle \(Q(V,T)\), take the *binary-operation signature alone*:
\[
x*y=T(x)+(I-T)(y).
\]
For each equation between terms in that operation, choose all distinct variables independently and uniformly from \(V\). Its satisfaction probability belongs to the exact spectrum
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{PSpec}(Q(V,T))=\{p^{-\operatorname{rank}g(T)}:g\in\mathbb F_p[X]\}.}
\]
Every displayed probability is attained by an equation involving only two variables. No constants, division operations, external addition, or scalar maps are included in the term signature.

Write the elementary-divisor decomposition of the \(\mathbb F_p[X]\)-module \(V\) under \(T\) as
\[
V\cong\bigoplus_{f}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r_f}\mathbb F_p[X]/(f^{\lambda_{f,j}}),
\]
where the \(f\) are distinct monic irreducible polynomials, \(\lambda_{f,j}\ge1\), and \(e_f=\max_j\lambda_{f,j}\). Then the *entire spectrum*, without enumerating term equations, is
\[
\boxed{\left\{p^{-\sum_f\deg(f)\sum_j\max(\lambda_{f,j}-a_f,0)}:\ 0\le a_f\le e_f\text{ integers}\right\}.}
\]
The spectrum is minimal, namely \(\{1,p^{-n}\}\), if and only if the minimal polynomial of \(T\) is irreducible. This includes direct sums of multiple copies of one simple module; irreducibility of the *representation* is not required. If \(p\ge3\), at each fixed order \(p^n\) **every number of spectrum levels from two through \(n+1\) occurs** within this family.

## Assumptions and scope
The two automorphisms \(T\) and \(I-T\) are both required: they make the affine operation a quasigroup (Latin) and allow inverses of coefficient transformations in the finite term clone. The binary operation is idempotent and medial. All ranks and module decompositions are over the prime field \(\mathbb F_p\). The domain of the probability is the full Cartesian power of the universe indexed by the distinct variables in each equation. Repeated appearances of a symbol remain the same variable. Terms use only the basic binary multiplication \(*\); an affine expression obtained in a proof is not itself assumed to be a term unless explicitly constructed via the term-closure argument below.

## Proof
**1. All binary term coefficients occur.** Put \(R=\mathbb F_p[T]\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_p}(V)\). Induction on syntax shows that any term \(u(x,y)\) has the form
\[
u(x,y)=A_u x+(I-A_u)y,\qquad A_u\in R,
\]
with coefficients \(I\) and \(0\) for the variable terms \(x\) and \(y\). If \(u\) and \(v\) have coefficients \(A\) and \(B\), then \(u*v\) has coefficient \(TA+(I-T)B\). Let \(S\subseteq R\) consist of coefficients actually attained. It is finite, contains \(0,I\), and is closed under this affine binary operation.

The maps \(S\to S\), \(A\mapsto TA\), and \(A\mapsto(I-T)A\) are well defined by composing a term with the other variable. They are injective because \(T\) and \(I-T\) are invertible. Since \(S\) is finite, these maps are surjective. Hence, for \(A,B\in S\), choose \(C,D\in S\) satisfying \(TC=A\) and \((I-T)D=B\). Then \(C*D\) has coefficient \(A+B\), proving \(S\) is closed under ordinary addition. A finite nonempty additive subsemigroup containing zero is a subgroup (repeated addition produces additive inverses). The subgroup \(S\) contains \(I\) and is stable under multiplication by \(T\) via \(A\mapsto TA\), so it contains every polynomial in \(T\) with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_p\). Thus \(S=R\).

In particular, for each \(g\in\mathbb F_p[X]\), there is a binary term \(u_g(x,y)\) with
\[
u_g(x,y)=g(T)x+(I-g(T))y.
\]
Its equation \(u_g(x,y)=y\) holds precisely when \(g(T)(x-y)=0\), hence with probability \(p^{-\operatorname{rank}g(T)}\). This proves all proposed probability values occur using two variables.

**2. No other probabilities occur.** For a term \(u\) in distinct variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\), repeated induction gives
\[
u(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_i x_i,\qquad A_i\in R,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_i=I.
\]
For any two such terms, their difference is a homogeneous linear map
\[
F:V^k\longrightarrow V,\quad F(\mathbf x)=\sum_i B_i x_i,\qquad B_i\in R,\quad \sum_iB_i=0.
\]
Let \(m_T\) be the minimal polynomial of \(T\). Evaluation identifies \(R\) with the principal ideal ring \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(m_T)\). The ideal generated by \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\) therefore equals \((g(T))\) for some polynomial \(g\). Bézout identities within this quotient show
\[
\operatorname{im}F=\sum_iB_iV=g(T)V.
\]
Indeed, the inclusion left-to-right follows from \(B_i\in(g(T))\), and right-to-left from expressing \(g(T)\) as a linear combination of the \(B_i\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}F=\operatorname{rank}g(T)\). Every fiber of \(F\) above zero has size \(p^{nk-\operatorname{rank}F}\), so the equation has probability \(p^{-\operatorname{rank}g(T)}\). Together with step 1, this proves the exact spectrum and binary witness assertion.

**3. Rational canonical formula.** The structure theorem for finitely generated modules over \(\mathbb F_p[X]\) gives the displayed decomposition. On a summand \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(f^\lambda)\), multiplication by \(f^a\) has image dimension \(\deg(f)\max(\lambda-a,0)\). For each polynomial \(g\), use the truncated \(f\)-adic valuations \(a_f=\min(v_f(g),e_f)\). Factors coprime to \(f\) are invertible on its summands, so ranks add exactly as displayed. Conversely every tuple \((a_f)_f\) occurs for \(g=\prod_f f^{a_f}\). This proves the second boxed formula.

**4. Minimality and level counts.** If \(m_T\) is irreducible, then \(R\) is a field; every nonzero \(g(T)\) is invertible, so the only ranks are \(0,n\). Conversely, if \(m_T\) is reducible (including a repeated irreducible factor), its quotient ring \(R\) has a nonzero nonunit: choose the image of one proper nonconstant irreducible factor \(f\) of \(m_T\). Its action on the faithful \(R\)-module \(V\) is nonzero and noninvertible, giving \(0<\operatorname{rank}f(T)<n\), an additional probability. Faithfulness follows from the definition of \(m_T\). This establishes the equivalence.

Any spectrum is a subset of \(\{1,p^{-1},\ldots,p^{-n}\}\) containing both endpoints; hence it has at most \(n+1\) levels. For \(p\ge3\), fix \(a\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}\). For each \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), choose \(T=J_k(a)\oplus aI_{n-k}\), with \(J_k(a)\) the single \(k\)-dimensional Jordan block. The polynomial \(m_T=(X-a)^k\). The ranks of \((T-aI)^j\) for \(0\le j\le k\) are \(n\) at \(j=0\) and \(k-j\) for \(1\le j\le k\), giving exactly \(k+1\) distinct probabilities. Thus every spectrum cardinality \(2,3,\ldots,n+1\) is realized at the fixed universe size \(p^n\).

## Verification
The standalone script `verify.py` uses exact modular matrix arithmetic. It independently generates the coefficient ring by polynomial evaluation, grows the binary-term coefficient closure until stable, confirms the two sets agree, checks the rank-based probability for every coefficient by exhaustive kernel enumeration, and compares exact sampled ternary-term syntax against complete solution counts. Six test configurations pass: scalar and nondiagonalizable two-dimensional cases over \(\mathbb F_3\); an irreducible quadratic over \(\mathbb F_2\); two distinct scalar eigenvalues over \(\mathbb F_5\); a mixed Jordan decomposition over \(\mathbb F_3\); and a squared irreducible quadratic over \(\mathbb F_2\). The checked rank sets are respectively \(\{0,2\},\{0,1,2\},\{0,2\},\{0,1,2\},\{0,1,3\},\{0,2,4\}\). These finite checks test the implementation and special cases; the proof above establishes the universal quantifiers.

Two Latin Alexander quandles on the nine-element additive group \(\mathbb F_3^2\) illustrate the invariant: \(T=2I\) gives spectrum \(\{1,1/9\}\), whereas a single length-two Jordan block with eigenvalue \(2\) gives \(\{1,1/3,1/9\}\).

## Relationship to prior work
Cardó (2026) introduced and studied equational probability spectra, identifying groupoid minimality as strongly constrained by quasigroup structure, and established general product/derived-operation inclusions. These results imply some prime-order or scalar special cases but do not give the all-term rank formula, rational-canonical stratification, or the minimal-polynomial criterion for finite Latin Alexander quandles. Ta (2026) establishes the module-theoretic framework for medial Latin quandles and the corresponding free objects; it does not state exact equational satisfaction probabilities. The present proof uses the affine representation and adds an exact term-coefficient reachability argument and rational-canonical enumeration. The known classification of finite Alexander quandles by Nelson concerns quandle isomorphism rather than equation-probability spectra. Public mathematical-record and literature searches using both 'probabilistic' and 'equational' terminology found no covering result; this is not a claim that absence of search hits proves priority.

## Limitations
This is a statement about nonempty elementary abelian \(p\)-groups and their linear Latin Alexander operations in the *one-binary-operation* signature. It does not automatically extend to arbitrary finite abelian groups with \(p\)-power torsion, non-Latin Alexander quandles, racks equipped with inversion/division, or terms with constants. The resulting spectrum need not determine the isomorphism class of \(T\); it records attainable ranks, not their multiplicities among polynomials. The integer cardinality statement requires \(p\ge3\). External literature may include equivalent formulations under clone theory or finite affine quasigroup terminology; independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
