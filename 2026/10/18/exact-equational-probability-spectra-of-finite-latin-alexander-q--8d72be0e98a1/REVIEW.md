# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof requires precisely that both \(T\) and \(I-T\) act invertibly. Finiteness converts the two coefficient-injection maps of the term clone to surjections; their inverses construct addition of attainable coefficients. Since the closure contains the identity, the entire ring \(\mathbb F_p[T]\) appears in binary terms. For arbitrary equations, all differences are homogeneous maps whose images are generated by a principal ideal in \(\mathbb F_p[X]/(m_T)\), so uniform solution probabilities are exactly powers of \(p\) indexed by polynomial ranks. Rational canonical form proves the explicit enumerator, and a nonzero nonunit detects failure of the minimal-spectrum property. The sharp spectral-cardinality range follows from a single Jordan block plus scalar summands. Six finite-field configurations and sampled higher-variable equations passed independent exhaustive testing; no finite test is presented as proof of the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic uncertainty
Cardó, *On semigroups and groupoids with minimal probabilistic spectrum* (arXiv:2603.19487, particularly Proposition 1 and Theorem 4), supplies the probability framework, groupoid/quasigroup necessary condition and derived/group product facts, but not these full-rank spectrum statements. Ta, *On medial Latin quandles and affine modules* (doi:10.4153/S0008439526102306, particularly sections 4–6), develops module equivalences rather than probability enumeration. The affine-module equivalence explains the linear setting but does not by itself establish which linear combinations appear as binary terms in the unexpanded signature or compute all solution probabilities. Public literature and mathematical-result searches checked semantically equivalent 'equational probability', 'term clone' and 'medial quasigroup' formulations. Specific existing finite-field quandle-algebra idempotent and operator-envelope classifications concern different invariants and do not imply these equation spectra. A closely related but unindexed clone-theoretic result remains possible.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete exact distribution of *attainable probabilities* of arbitrary algebraic equations over an infinite family of finite quasigroups, including nonsemisimple defining transformations. It resolves the precise minimal-spectrum criterion and shows that all intermediate numbers of spectrum levels occur at a fixed universe order for odd prime fields. The two nine-element examples show that nilpotent Jordan structure is detected by this invariant despite the same additive group and universe size. It directly develops an open structural direction motivated by recent work on minimal probabilistic spectra, beyond mere known prime-field scalar examples.

## Remaining scientific limits
The claim does not determine how many syntactically distinct equations produce a fixed probability. Searches cannot establish absolute novelty; specialized pre-2026 finite quasigroup term-clone literature may contain equivalent results. Independent mathematical verification is pending.
