# Exact Euler-form radical for every faithful finite abelian planar quotient

## Finding
Let \(H\) be a nontrivial finite abelian group with \(N=|H|\), generated by two character weights \(a,b\in H\). The induced action of \(G=\widehat H\) on \(\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}\) has trivial generic stabilizer. Denote its Euler quadratic form on real character-multiplicity vectors by \(Q\). The form is the real polynomial determined by the representation-theoretic formula of Bachhuber--Gholampour, Theorem 4.6; no assertion about realization of arbitrary real vectors as Hilbert polynomials is made.

Write \(c=a+b\), \(d_a=N/\operatorname{ord}(a)\), \(d_b=N/\operatorname{ord}(b)\), and \(d_c=N/\operatorname{ord}(c)\), where the zero element has order one. Let \(\varepsilon_a\) and \(\varepsilon_b\) indicate whether \(d_a\) and \(d_b\) are even. Then the real nullity of the polar form is exactly
\[
\boxed{\dim\ker Q=d_a+d_b-1-\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_b+\begin{cases}d_c,&2\mid\operatorname{ord}(c),\\0,&2\nmid\operatorname{ord}(c).\end{cases}}
\]
In particular, \(Q\) is nondegenerate on the quotient by the constant-multiplicity line exactly when \(H\) is cyclic, both \(a\) and \(b\) are generators, and \(\operatorname{ord}(a+b)\) is odd. Thus every faithful noncyclic abelian action has a radical strictly larger than the constant line.

For example, the natural action of \(\mu_2\times\mu_2\) with coordinate characters generating distinct factors has nullity three; for \(\mu_3\times\mu_3\) the analogous nullity is five. The scalar \(\mu_4\) action with both weights one has nullity three, while the \(\mu_6\) action of weights one and minus one has nullity one.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). A *faithful* planar abelian action means that the two coordinate characters \(a,b\) generate the finite group \(H=\operatorname{Irr}(G)\). This includes cyclic actions with nonprimitive individual weights, and abelian groups requiring two generators. \(Q\) is understood as a quadratic form on the entire real vector space \(\mathbb R^H\), whose coordinates are indexed by characters of \(G\). The phrase \(\ker Q\) means the radical of its symmetric polar bilinear form, not the quadratic null cone \(\{x:Q(x)=0\}\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (signed translation formula).** The coefficients in Bachhuber--Gholampour Theorem 4.6 imply, without assuming that the three characters \(a,b,c\) are distinct,
\[
Q(x)=\frac12\sum_{h\in H}\left((x_h-x_{h+a})^2+(x_h-x_{h+b})^2-(x_h-x_{h+c})^2\right).
\]
The factor \(1/2\) is independent of \(h\), and is immaterial to the radical. Terms with a zero character vanish automatically.

**Step 2 (Fourier factorization).** The translations on \(H\) are simultaneously diagonalized by the orthogonal character basis indexed by \(g\in\widehat H\). Up to a fixed nonzero scalar, the associated real eigenvalue is
\[
\lambda_g=1-\operatorname{Re}g(a)-\operatorname{Re}g(b)+\operatorname{Re}g(a+b).
\]
Choose arguments \(g(a)=e^{i\alpha}\), \(g(b)=e^{i\beta}\). The trigonometric identity
\[
1-\cos\alpha-\cos\beta+\cos(\alpha+\beta)
=-4\sin(\alpha/2)\sin(\beta/2)\cos((\alpha+\beta)/2)
\]
shows that an eigenvalue vanishes **if and only if**
\[
g(a)=1\quad\text{or}\quad g(b)=1\quad\text{or}\quad g(c)=-1.
\]
This condition is independent of the chosen arguments. Although complex characters occur in conjugate pairs, the complexified real symmetric matrix has the same nullity as its real matrix, so each zero Fourier mode contributes one to the real nullity.

**Step 3 (exact union cardinality).** Put \(A=\{g:g(a)=1\}\), \(B=\{g:g(b)=1\}\), and \(C=\{g:g(c)=-1\}\). By finite abelian character duality,
\[
|A|=d_a,\qquad |B|=d_b,\qquad |A\cap B|=1.
\]
Indeed the pair \(a,b\) generates \(H\), hence a character vanishing on both is trivial. The evaluation map on \(c\) has image the full group of \(\operatorname{ord}(c)\)-th roots of unity. Therefore \(C\) is empty when \(\operatorname{ord}(c)\) is odd, and otherwise has \(d_c\) elements.

Restriction of evaluation on \(b\) to \(A\) is injective: its kernel is \(A\cap B=\{1\}\). Hence \(A\) is cyclic, with the values \(g(b)\), for \(g\in A\), running once through the \(d_a\)-th roots of unity. Because \(g(c)=g(b)\) on \(A\), precisely one element of \(A\cap C\) exists if \(d_a\) is even, and none if \(d_a\) is odd. Thus \(|A\cap C|=\varepsilon_a\). Similarly \(|B\cap C|=\varepsilon_b\). No triple intersection is possible since the unique element of \(A\cap B\) sends \(c\) to one. Inclusion-exclusion gives the boxed formula.

**Step 4 (sharp nondegeneracy criterion).** Constant vectors are always in the radical, so nullity one means \(A\cup B\cup C=\{1\}\). This is equivalent to \(A=B=\{1\}\) and \(C=\varnothing\), namely \(d_a=d_b=1\) and \(\operatorname{ord}(c)\) odd. If either \(a\) or \(b\) generates \(H\), then \(H\) is cyclic. Conversely, if \(H\) is cyclic and both are generators with \(c\) of odd order, these conditions hold. For a noncyclic group neither generator is individual, so \(A\) and \(B\) already provide nonconstant null modes.

## Verification
A standalone reproducible Python checker is supplied as `verify.py`. It constructs the finite character group explicitly, enumerates the three exact modular vanishing criteria, and compares their union size with the closed formula. It checks 31,925 faithful cyclic and rank-two abelian action cases. It also constructs the integral Hessian \(2I-P_a-P_{-a}-P_b-P_{-b}+P_c+P_{-c}\) and computes its exact rational rank independently in 30 small cases. The run reported `VERIFY_OK faithful_action_profiles=31925 exact_matrix_rank_checks=30`. These finite tests corroborate rather than prove the universal result, which is established by the character-duality proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bachhuber and Gholampour, *G-Equivariant Hilbert Schemes and Quadratic Forms*, arXiv:2610.12736v1 (first public October 8, 2026), derive and classify the coefficient patterns of the Euler forms (Theorem 4.6 and Sections 6--7); the inspected source does not state this complete rank formula. Earlier integral-cokernel computations for scalar cyclic quotients, for the weights \((1,2)\), and for cyclic weights \((1,t)\) with \(t\) a unit determine the radical in these specializations, but do not imply the formula for arbitrary faithful two-generator groups or nonunit cyclic weights. A further prime-cyclic positivity classification concerns eigenvalue signs, not the new general abelian radical. General McKay-quiver theory provides context rather than the stated degeneracy count.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the real radical, **not** the integral Smith normal form, the full signature, the existence of Hilbert subschemes with arbitrary real multiplicity vectors, or geometric singularities of a moduli scheme. In particular no conclusion about representability of an arbitrary null vector is asserted. The Fourier criterion is elementary once the nontrivial Euler-form expression is given; an equivalent argument may appear implicitly in older literature. Independent historical-priority certification is not claimed.
