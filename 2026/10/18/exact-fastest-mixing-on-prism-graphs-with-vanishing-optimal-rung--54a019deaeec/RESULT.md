# Exact fastest mixing on prism graphs with vanishing optimal rung weights

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(G_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the prism graph with two \(n\)-cycle rims and \(n\) rungs. Set
\[
\delta_n=2\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}\right),\qquad
\Delta_n=2\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{n}\right),\qquad
H_n=\Delta_n+2\delta_n.
\]
Among all real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, row-stochastic \(2n\)-by-\(2n\) matrices whose nonzero off-diagonal entries occur only on edges of \(G_n\), the minimum second-largest eigenvalue modulus is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mu_n^*=\frac{\Delta_n}{H_n}}.
\]
It is attained by assigning transition probability \(a_n=2/H_n\) to every rim edge and \(b_n=\delta_n/H_n\) to every rung, with holding probability
\[
\boxed{h_n=\frac{\Delta_n+\delta_n-4}{H_n}\ge0}
\]
at every vertex. This pair \((a_n,b_n)\) is the unique optimum among matrices invariant under the natural rim rotations, reflections and rim interchange. No uniqueness assertion is made among non-invariant matrices.

The optimal rung probability vanishes quadratically while rim transitions remain order one:
\[
a_n=\tfrac12+O(n^{-2}),\quad b_n=\frac{\pi^2}{n^2}+O(n^{-4}),\quad
\mu_n^*=1-\frac{2\pi^2}{n^2}+O(n^{-4}).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are pairs \((j,\varepsilon)\), with \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Edges are \((j,\varepsilon)\sim(j\pm1,\varepsilon)\) and \((j,0)\sim(j,1)\). In the triangular-prism case \(n=3\), the cycle is a triangle and the two rim neighbors are distinct. Loops represent holding probabilities; zero weights on permitted edges are allowed. The objective is the spectral norm \(\mu(P)=\|P-(2n)^{-1}{\bf1}{\bf1}^{\mathsf T}\|_2\). It is not the nonreversible-chain problem or a continuous-time gap problem.

## Proof
**Symmetrization.** The stated transition-matrix feasible set is convex. The objective is a convex norm. Averaging any feasible \(P\) over the subgroup generated by common rotations and reflections of the rims and by interchange of rims cannot increase \(\mu(P)\). The subgroup has exactly two edge orbits (rims and rungs), so the global minimum equals the minimum over two edge weights \(a,b\ge0\), \(2a+b\le1\). Such a matrix has constant diagonal \(1-2a-b\) and admits the factorization
\[
P=I-a(L_{C_n}\otimes I_2)-b(I_n\otimes L_{K_2}),
\]
where \(L\) is the unweighted combinatorial Laplacian.

**Exact spectrum.** Fourier modes of \(C_n\) and symmetric/antisymmetric modes of \(K_2\) give, for \(0\le k<n\),
\[
\lambda_{k,+}=1-a d_k,\qquad \lambda_{k,-}=1-a d_k-2b,\qquad
d_k=2\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right).
\]
The \((k,+)=(0,+)\) mode is constant and excluded from the SLEM. Every nonzero cycle eigenvalue lies in \([\delta_n,\Delta_n]\), and the antisymmetric branch runs over \([0,\Delta_n]\).

**Global lower bound.** For any \(a,b\) and any corresponding spectral radius bound \(t\), the three spectral constraints
\[
X=1-\delta_n a\le t,\quad
Y=1-2b\le t,\quad
Z=-1+\Delta_n a+2b\le t
\]
are necessary. They admit the exact positive-weight identity
\[
\frac{\Delta_n X+\delta_n Y+\delta_n Z}{H_n}
=\frac{\Delta_n}{H_n}.
\]
Consequently \(t\ge\Delta_n/H_n\), independently of \(a,b\). Equality forces \(X=Y=Z=t\) because all three weights are positive. These equations uniquely give \(a=2/H_n\), \(b=\delta_n/H_n\). In particular, an invariant optimizer is unique.

**Feasibility.** For even \(n\), \(\Delta_n=4\), so \(\Delta_n+\delta_n-4=\delta_n>0\). For odd \(n\), with \(c=\cos(\pi/n)\in(0,1)\),
\[
\Delta_n=2(1+c),\quad \delta_n=4(1-c^2),\quad
\Delta_n+\delta_n-4=2(1-c)(1+2c)>0.
\]
Thus \(a,b>0\) and \(2a+b\le1\). At the candidate weights, the first nonconstant symmetric Fourier mode and the zero-frequency antisymmetric mode equal \(+\Delta_n/H_n\), while the highest-frequency antisymmetric mode equals \(-\Delta_n/H_n\). For intermediate frequencies, each eigenvalue is affine in \(d_k\) and therefore lies between its branch's endpoint values. In the symmetric branch the remaining endpoint \(1-2\Delta_n/H_n\) has absolute value at most \(\Delta_n/H_n\), since \(0<\delta_n\le\Delta_n\). Every nonconstant eigenvalue therefore has modulus at most \(\Delta_n/H_n\), attaining the lower bound. Symmetrization makes this the globally optimal SLEM.

**Asymptotics.** The lazy equal-edge benchmark assigns \(a=b=1/4\), with holding probability \(1/4\). Its nonconstant symmetric first Fourier mode is \(1-\delta_n/4\), and for all sufficiently large \(n\) it controls the SLEM, giving spectral gap \(\pi^2 n^{-2}+O(n^{-4})\). Hence the optimized prism has asymptotically twice that baseline spectral gap. The elementary expansions \(\delta_n=4\pi^2 n^{-2}+O(n^{-4})\) and \(\Delta_n=4+O(n^{-2})\) give the displayed expressions for the probabilities and the optimal modulus.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_prism.py` constructs the full \(2n\)-vertex transition matrix. Using rational arithmetic, it checks the exact characteristic polynomial against the product-spectrum factorization for \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\). With NumPy and SciPy installed, it also checks the full matrix spectrum and independently solves the two-edge-orbit linear program for each integer \(3\le n\le60\). On the supplied run, all three exact cases and all 58 numerical cases passed. These finitely many tests support, but do not replace, the all-\(n\) analytical proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Boyd, Diaconis and Xiao introduced the convex fastest-mixing formulation in 2004. Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao's 2007 preprint proves symmetry reduction and gives the Cartesian-product Laplacian spectrum, including examples involving toroidal meshes and hypercubes. Those ingredients are used here. Jafarizadeh (2010) solves fastest mixing for cyclic and semi-cyclic multipartite networks, but its two-vertex-group semi-cycle consists of alternating complete bipartite and matching seams rather than the prism rung/rim geometry (apart from an exceptional four-column cube case). Their explicit edge-transitive formula does not determine the two distinct optimal transition weights for \(C_n\square K_2\) when the graph has two edge orbits. The present claim is the complete two-weight optimization on this natural prism family, including a matched three-mode lower bound and the vanishing-rung law. It is not a claim that symmetry reduction or Fourier diagonalization is new.

## Limitations
The result concerns only symmetric stochastic matrices and uniform stationarity; it does not establish the fastest nonreversible or nonuniform-stationary chain. It establishes global optimal **value**, not global uniqueness outside the invariant class. The literature comparison has not ruled out unindexed equivalent prism-specific calculations or a stronger general Cartesian-product result not encountered in the inspected texts.
