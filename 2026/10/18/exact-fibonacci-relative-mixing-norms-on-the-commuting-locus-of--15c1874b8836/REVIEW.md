# Research review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim concerns the explicitly defined invariant probability measure supported on the quaternionic commuting locus, not ambient product Haar measure. Verjovsky's restriction of each word-map lift to a maximal torus gives the factor relation with the Fibonacci toral automorphism. Fourier orthogonality reduces the sharp conditional-correlation norm to a lattice minimum. Writing the Fibonacci matrix as the square of a determinant-minus-one integer matrix converts the minimum into two symmetric iterates; a triangle-inequality lower bound and explicit handling of exceptional time indices prove the exact exponent. Equality is realized by symmetric cosine characters, and Fourier density proves relative mixing for all square-integrable observables. The accompanying independent integer-arithmetic replay checks finite instances but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS on the inspected evidence, with a specific residual bibliographic risk.** Verjovsky (arXiv:2608.08252) supplies the quaternionic Nielsen lift, commuting-locus fibration and invariant maximal-torus dynamics, but not an analytic conditional-correlation operator norm. Slipantschuk, Bandtlow and Just (arXiv:1605.02883) calculate transfer spectral data in anisotropically weighted analytic spaces for a particular torus deformation; they do not provide the conditioned probability on the quaternionic commuting locus or the exact symmetric-weight Fibonacci minimization. Classical toral Fourier mixing explains the mechanism but does not by itself specify the optimal finite-time norm and its minimizing frequencies. It remains possible that this precise arithmetic optimization is implicit in another Fourier-dynamics paper.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Relative mixing is a natural nontrivial quantitative property when the relevant invariant measure is not ergodic and ordinary mixing fails. The result computes the exact operator norm at every time, not just its asymptotic exponential scale, and classifies the attaining Fourier modes. The Fibonacci exponents are the precise bridge between the hyperbolic matrix's arithmetic and observable correlations; the result applies uniformly across otherwise different quaternionic word representatives of the same toral matrix.

## Scope and limits
The canonical measure is singular relative to ambient six-dimensional Haar volume. There is no claim about ambient ergodicity or mixing, general exponent matrices, or different analytic function norms. Earlier work is used for known torus geometry and qualitative Fourier theory; priority beyond the sources actually inspected is not independently certified.
