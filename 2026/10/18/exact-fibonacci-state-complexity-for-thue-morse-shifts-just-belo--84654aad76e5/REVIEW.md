# Scientific assessment — Mersenne-shift Thue–Morse state complexity

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof first identifies the exact continuation equivalence for every binary input prefix by splitting the addition into high and low binary blocks. It then derives the precise offset set for a shift of the form \(2^k-1\), counts the two parity classes separately, and proves their disjointness using the absence of three consecutive equal Thue–Morse bits. The lower as well as the upper bound follows because each distinct fingerprint is a distinguishable residual. The auxiliary Fibonacci profile count is proved by carry-run parity and constructive realizability. Direct finite checks through \(k=11\), plus independent DFAO minimization through \(k=7\), passed. The argument, not these finite observations, establishes every \(k\ge1\).

**Originality — PASS within examined evidence.** Moradi–Rampersad–Shallit (arXiv:2512.10017) leave the general exact-shift question open and prove only general upper and sparse lower bounds. OEIS A382296 lists initial exact values but gives an infinite formula for power-of-two shifts only; Biswas proves that power-of-two formula in a full inspected source manuscript. The present all-\(k\) result instead treats \(2^k-1\), whose carry-offset family is distinct. The 2021 translation-and-multiplication theorem of Charlier–Cisternino–Massuir is restricted to \(m\mathcal T+r\) with \(0\le r<m\); this does not cover arbitrary nonzero translates of \(\mathcal T\). A related successor-to-powers shift classification concerns different parameters and does not imply the Mersenne formula without additional work. Searching by equivalent descriptions, Fibonacci expression, shift parameters, and state counts found no covering record. The complete linked finite OEIS table and older unindexed literature were not exhaustively read; historical priority remains a risk.

**Value — PASS.** The exact number of minimal automaton states is a natural invariant of shifts of a classical automatic sequence. This theorem resolves the entire infinite family of Mersenne translations, not an arbitrary isolated computation, within an explicitly identified general open problem. Its structural recurrence \(N(2^k-1)=N(2^{k-1}-1)+2F_{k+2}\) separates fingerprints into a Mersenne predecessor profile and a dyadic profile, providing a reusable mechanism. The constant deficit of six relative to the neighboring successor-power Fibonacci count is a meaningful boundary phenomenon, although no formula is asserted for arbitrary shifts.

**Closest literature and limitations.** See Moradi–Rampersad–Shallit (2025), OEIS A382296 (2025), Biswas (2026), and Charlier–Cisternino–Massuir (2021). The present result is confined to binary MSD-first DFAOs for \(2^k-1\) and relies on a mathematical proof; finite program outputs are corroboration only. The 2021 paper was compared using its accessible published abstract rather than its entire PDF. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
