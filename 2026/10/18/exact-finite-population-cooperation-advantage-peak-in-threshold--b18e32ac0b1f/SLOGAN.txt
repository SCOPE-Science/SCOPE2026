In a finite threshold collective-action game with \(Z\) players, group size \(N\), and required coalition size \(M\), the selective advantage of cooperation peaks at exactly \(\lceil MZ/N-1\rceil\) cooperators, or ties at two adjacent integers when \(N\mid MZ\). An exact hypergeometric difference factorization proves a sharp benefit threshold and shows that the cooperation-favored compositions form one contiguous interval, with two sign reversals attainable.
