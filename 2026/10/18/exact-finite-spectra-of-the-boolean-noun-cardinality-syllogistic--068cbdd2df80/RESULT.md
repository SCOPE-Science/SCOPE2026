# Exact finite spectra of the Boolean-noun cardinality syllogistic

## Finding

Let \(L_{\vee,\neg}\) have the four atomic sentence forms of the syllogistic with cardinality comparisons, but allow **finite unions and complements within nouns**, not Boolean connectives between sentences. Theories may be infinite sets of such atomic sentences, interpreted on finite **nonempty** universes. Then

\[
\bigl\{\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma):\Gamma\text{ is an }L_{\vee,\neg}\text{-theory}\bigr\}
=\{\varnothing\}\cup
\bigl\{S\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}:S\ne\varnothing\text{ and }S+S\subseteq S\bigr\}.
\]

Thus **the finite spectra are exactly the empty set and the nonempty additive subsemigroups of the positive integers**. Every such spectrum has a **finite** theory presentation. More precisely, if \(S=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_t\rangle_+\) with positive integer generators, then a finite presentation can use at most \(\max\{1,\lceil\log_2(g_1+\cdots+g_t)\rceil\}\) raw noun variables. An explicit algorithm is given in the proof.

## Assumptions and scope

A model consists of a nonempty finite domain \(M\) and an interpretation of raw noun variables by subsets of \(M\). A noun \(\bar\alpha\) denotes \(M\setminus I(\alpha)\) and \(\alpha\vee\beta\) denotes \(I(\alpha)\cup I(\beta)\). In particular \(\alpha\wedge\beta:=\overline{\bar\alpha\vee\bar\beta}\) denotes intersection. Nouns can be arbitrary finite terms in these operations. The only sentences are \(\forall(\alpha,\beta)\), \(\exists(\alpha,\beta)\), \(\exists^{\ge}(\alpha,\beta)\), and \(\exists^{>}(\alpha,\beta)\), with semantics respectively

\[
I(\alpha)\subseteq I(\beta),\quad
I(\alpha)\cap I(\beta)\ne\varnothing,\quad
|I(\alpha)|\ge|I(\beta)|,\quad
|I(\alpha)|>|I(\beta)|.
\]

There is **no sentence-level disjunction or negation** in this language. For any theory \(\Gamma\), its spectrum is \(\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma)=\{|M|:M\models\Gamma,\ 0<|M|<\infty\}\). For positive integers \(g_1,\ldots,g_t\), \(\langle g_1,\ldots,g_t\rangle_+\) denotes their nonzero nonnegative-integer combinations.

## Proof

**1. Necessity: every nonempty spectrum is closed under addition.** Given two disjoint finite models \((M_1,I_1)\), \((M_2,I_2)\) of the same theory, interpret each raw variable on their disjoint union componentwise. For **every** Boolean noun term \(\alpha\), structural induction gives

\[
I(\alpha)=I_1(\alpha)\mathbin{\dot\cup}I_2(\alpha).
\]

The induction includes complementation because the complement of a componentwise union, relative to the whole disjoint union, is the componentwise union of complements. Hence subset inclusion persists; a nonempty intersection remains nonempty; and the two cardinality comparisons survive by addition of the corresponding inequalities (including the strict case). Every atomic sentence, and therefore the whole theory, remains true. Thus if \(m,n\in\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma)\), then \(m+n\in\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma)\). The empty spectrum is possible.

**2. Every nonempty additive subsemigroup is finitely generated.** For \(S\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}\) nonempty and closed under addition, let \(a=\min S\). For each residue class modulo \(a\) attained by \(S\), let \(b_r\) be the least member of \(S\) with that residue. For any \(n\in S\) with residue \(r\), minimality implies \(n=b_r+ka\) for some integer \(k\ge0\). Conversely every such sum is in \(S\). Therefore \(S\) is generated by the finite family consisting of \(a\) and the \(b_r\). No assumption that the generators are coprime is required.

**3. Sufficiency: encode an arbitrary finitely generated semigroup.** Fix \(g_1,\ldots,g_t\ge1\), let \(h=g_1+\cdots+g_t\), and take \(k=\max\{1,\lceil\log_2h\rceil\}\) raw variables \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\). Their \(2^k\) Boolean atoms

\[
A_\sigma=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{k}B_{j,\sigma_j},\qquad
B_{j,1}=q_j,\quad B_{j,0}=\bar q_j,\quad
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^k
\]

are expressible noun terms and partition every model's universe. Select \(h\) distinct atoms, divided into \(t\) blocks \(C_i=(A_{i,1},\ldots,A_{i,g_i})\). For each unused atom \(A\), add the sentence \(\forall(A,\bar A)\), which asserts that \(A\) is empty. For each \(i\) and \(2\le j\le g_i\), add the two sentences

\[
\exists^{\ge}(A_{i,1},A_{i,j}),\qquad
\exists^{\ge}(A_{i,j},A_{i,1}).
\]

These assert exactly that the \(g_i\) atom cells in block \(C_i\) all have a common size \(u_i\ge0\). As the atoms partition the universe, every model of the resulting finite theory \(\Gamma_G\) has size

\[
|M|=g_1u_1+\cdots+g_tu_t>0.
\]

Conversely, given any choice of nonnegative \(u_i\) with this positive sum, partition a set of that size into the designated atom cells of sizes \(u_i\) and leave the unused atoms empty. Interpreting each \(q_j\) by the union of those cells whose label has bit \(j\) equal to one makes every sentence true. Therefore \(\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma_G)=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_t\rangle_+\). The number of constraints is finite, at most \(2\sum_i(g_i-1)+(2^k-h)\). The empty spectrum is obtained from the contradictory theory \(\{\forall(p,\bar p),\exists(p,p)\}\).

Together, these steps establish the claimed equality and the finite-theory presentation for every possible spectrum.

It also evaluates all four atomic sentence types for every pair of Boolean noun truth masks (two raw variables) on pairs of universes of sizes at most two, checking componentwise disjoint-union preservation. Execute \(\texttt{python3 semigroup\_spectra\_verify.py}\). It prints \(\texttt{VERIFY\_OK}\) after passing all assertions. These computations test finite instances only; the three mathematical proof steps establish the theorem for all finite cardinalities and all (including infinite) theories.

For example, the generators \(3,5\) yield precisely \(\{3,5,6,8,9,10,11,12,\ldots\}\), which is not an eventual tail or even tail. The checker confirms membership through size twelve.

## Relationship to prior work

Jiang, *Finite Spectra of Syllogistic Logic with Cardinality Comparisons* (arXiv:2609.24902v1, 21 September 2026), Theorem 3.16, classifies the **base** language spectra as tails and even tails. Its Theorem 4.5 concerns Boolean connectives **between sentences**, not Boolean operations **inside nouns**. Section 5.1 introduces noun-level union/intersection and constructs the single example of divisibility by three. The present statement gives a complete spectrum characterization for that expressly introduced noun-level extension, including every mixed-generator additive semigroup and a finite defining theory.

Jiang's Lemma 3.8 already proves additive closure for the base language; Step 1 extends that argument to Boolean noun terms. Neither that necessary condition nor the three-divisibility construction entails Step 3 for arbitrary finitely generated semigroups. The earlier syllogistic language of Moss (2016) supplies background syntax, not a classification of the Boolean-noun extension. General first-order spectrum theorems concern a much richer language and do not establish **which** spectra this restricted syllogistic admits.

## Limitations

The universe is required to be finite and nonempty. The claim does not classify spectra of a language with sentence-level disjunction, nor spectra over infinite cardinalities. The stated variable bound is a constructive upper bound, not an optimality assertion. The general mathematical proof is not replaced by the bounded program. The originality comparison covers the full accessible motivating manuscript, specific related sources and targeted literature/database searches, but cannot rule out unindexed earlier results or an equivalent formulation in incompletely accessible historical work. No independent audit has been performed.
