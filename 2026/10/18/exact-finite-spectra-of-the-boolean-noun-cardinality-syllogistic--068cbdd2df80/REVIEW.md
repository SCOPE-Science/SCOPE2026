# Scientific review: Boolean-noun cardinality syllogistic spectra

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is proved by (i) disjoint-union preservation of every Boolean noun term and all four permitted sentence types, (ii) a residue-class proof that all positive-integer additive subsemigroups are finitely generated, and (iii) explicit Boolean-atom partitions realizing arbitrary generators. The empty-spectrum boundary, nonempty-universe quantifier, equality as paired comparisons, and finite variable bound are included. The checker independently confirmed 206,459 finite cell vectors and 409,600 atomic disjoint-union implications; these experiments are supporting diagnostics and are not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with remaining search risk.** Jiang's arXiv:2609.24902v1 gives base-language tails/even tails (Theorem 3.16), sentence-level Boolean-extension spectra (Theorem 4.5), and the example of divisibility by three after noun-level union (Section 5.1). This result supplies a complete characterization for the noun-level extension using arbitrary semigroup generators, which is not a corollary of the specific three-fold construction. Relevant semantic and numerical-semigroup searches did not identify a prior classification. Earlier or unindexed work on Boolean set nouns remains a possible source of equivalence.

**Value — PASS.** The result answers the natural classification problem suggested by the paper's precise noun-level extension. It identifies the whole family of attainable numerical spectra, produces finite constructive theories from arbitrary generators, and separates noun-level Boolean operations from sentence-level Boolean connectives. The result is not simply another divisibility example.

Closest literature: Jiang (2026), Theorems 3.16 and 4.5 and Section 5.1; Moss (2016), base syntax; Moss–Topal (2020), infinite domains. Limits: only finite nonempty domains and atomic-sentence theories with Boolean noun terms; the generator-based variable bound is not claimed optimal. Additional inaccessible or unindexed sources may affect novelty.
