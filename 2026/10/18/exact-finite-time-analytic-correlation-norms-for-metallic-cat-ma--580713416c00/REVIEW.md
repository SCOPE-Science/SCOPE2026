# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reduces the Fourier correlation bilinear form to the supremum of a weighted permutation on the nonzero lattice. Since the map is symmetric unimodular, the input-output Fourier map is \(k\mapsto A_s^nk\) with no lost frequencies. The unimodular half-iterate change \(v=M_s^nk\) is legitimate for every integer \(n\ge1\). The three exhaustive cases \(xy>0\), \(xy<0\), and \(xy=0\) prove the precise minimum and the four equality indices. The norm's equality cases follow from equality in Cauchy--Schwarz. The verifier's 15,456 exact tests independently check low finite parameters but are not presented as proof for all parameters.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with historical priority uncertainty
Chiem (arXiv:2502.18628v1, full available preprint) studies a different cat-driven Schrödinger-cocycle Lyapunov problem. Axenides--Floratos--Nicolis (arXiv:2401.08521v1, correlation sections read) derive smooth correlation/entropy-time bounds, not the all-time weighted analytic operator norm. Shchetka (arXiv:2610.19019v1, analytic Hilbert definition, Theorem 3.1 and §4.5 examined) proves the sharp asymptotic exponent, including the \(s=2\) linear map, but does not compute the exact finite-time norm or all optimizing Fourier modes. Exact frequency minimization is a distinct missing implication, not a restatement of the known escape-rate exponent. The older Brini--Siboni torus correlation-decay paper was identified but not fully accessible, so the same statement could have prior art. No independent novelty certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The four-mode result determines a natural exact finite-time analytic mixing benchmark for every metallic cat map, not merely the asymptotic escape exponent. Its exact normalization resolves the worst analytic frequency pair at every iterate and gives sharp constants without hidden dimension- or time-dependent losses. The whole integer family and complete equality classification make it a structural lattice-optimality result rather than a single numerical test or an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limits
These are exact two-observable bilinear operator norms in the specified isotropic \(\ell^1\)-weighted analytic space. They do not imply an optimum for a single observable paired with itself, a nonlinear perturbation, or a distinct analytic norm. The bibliographic priority caveat remains. Same-model calculations are not independent validation.
