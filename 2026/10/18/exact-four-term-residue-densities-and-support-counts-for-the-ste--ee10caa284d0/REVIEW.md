# Review

## Correctness
PASS. Conditional on a fixed trailing-binary-run valuation, the Stern pair is the unimodular image of a uniformly distributed primitive pair, and the geometric tail is uniformly negligible. The even/odd four-term identities give both congruence branches exactly. Their intersection is a single attainable quadruple for each primitive initial pair; the local prime-power support sum is a finite valuation partition. Exact arithmetic checks verify finite boundary cases and mass normalization. Infinite scope follows analytically from published pair equidistribution and elementary congruences.

## Originality
PASS, with stated uncertainty. Reznick's 2006 Theorem 4.7 and Deshouillers–Schinzel's 2019 Theorem 1.1 provide uniform primitive pair distribution, not the explicit four-term conditional mass or complete support enumeration. Existing exact three-term laws are shorter marginals, which do not determine the four-term coupling. Relevant textual, mathematical-results, and own-claim checks uncovered no identical four-coordinate formula or multiplicative support count. The broad dyadic identities in Reznick supply useful machinery but not this full arithmetic classification. A prior unindexed derivation remains possible.

## Value
PASS. The result gives the first nontrivial two-extra-coordinate joint law beyond primitive pairs, including arithmetic conditions for positivity and the exact number of admissible four-term residue patterns for every modulus. The branch-intersection correction and CRT-local census explain why longer Stern blocks have constrained residue support. The count is a natural invariant of modular block complexity rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.
