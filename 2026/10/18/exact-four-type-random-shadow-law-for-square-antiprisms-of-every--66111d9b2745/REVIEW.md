# Scientific review: square-antiprism random-shadow law

## Correctness (PASS)
The theorem states the exact vertex coordinates and all positive heights; it excludes only the explicitly measure-zero nongeneric directions. The ten supporting facet planes and sixteen adjacencies give a signed-horizon count of \(6+A+C-|B-D|\). For the entire fundamental wedge, its four thresholds appear in one of exactly two stated orders. Their spherical integrations give all four probabilities and strict positivity. The independent planar-convex-hull checker passed 72,000 cases across four heights, spherical quadrature and dihedral-expectation cross-checks; only the algebraic geometric argument supports the infinite quantifier.

## Originality (PASS relative to checked sources)
McGuire's 2004 article supplies the first-moment dihedral formula, but an expectation does not give four outcome probabilities. The full 2007 Glisse–Lazard text proves asymptotic expectations for broader surface-approximation families and contains no fixed square-antiprism distribution. The related exact X-ray-number theorem for the uniform-antiprism family addresses penetrating line directions, not random projected polygon types; its complete RESULT text was compared. Similar random projection and central-section claims for other polytopes do not imply this all-height distribution. Searches included the polar square-trapezohedron reformulation. A historical special-case table or unindexed source could still coincide; novelty is supported only relative to the material checked.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The law completely classifies a natural, symmetric eight-vertex space polytope under isotropic viewing for an entire nontrivial deformation family, rather than merely computing one mean or one special pose. It reveals four positive combinatorial classes, closed arcsine weights and a height-independent angular transition; the regular-faced benchmark provides a concrete stereological prediction. This full natural classification is not a renaming of an existing first-moment formula.

## Scope and limits
The result does not cover unequal ring radii, arbitrary twist, perspective views or correlations with projected area. Independent mathematical validation and definitive historical priority remain open.
