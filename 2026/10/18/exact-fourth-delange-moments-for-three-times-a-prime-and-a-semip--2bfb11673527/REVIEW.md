# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Inclusion–exclusion gives the exact sawtooth identity, and coupling on the \(p\)-grid gives the stated CDF error. The density estimate follows from a direct piecewise change of variables. The explicit fourth-moment calculation is obtained by interval integration and a degree-six polynomial identity. All fourteen determining interpolation nodes were checked exactly from the executable bundle; 123 separate prime instances provide independent cross-checks. There is no extrapolation from finite samples without a degree bound. Boundary conventions, prime distinctness, and normalization are explicit.

## Originality — PASS, with specified risk
Brown's October 2026 paper provides the general reduction to Franel integrals and an unevaluated fourfold Möbius sum, whereas the present result evaluates the \(3p\) family explicitly and supplies a quantified limit law for \(qp\) at fixed \(q\). McIntosh's full 1996 article gives general order-four Franel integral tools and explicit special cases but not this exact semiprime family. Searches of published mathematical findings and the accessible prior corpus disclosed no claim implying either new assertion. The full Greaves–Hall–Huxley–Wilson article was not accessible, leaving a material risk of implicit prior derivation; absence of a matching search result is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The motivating paper identifies evaluation of the fourth Delange integral over odd squarefree composite moduli as an open calculation. The first such natural family, \(3p\), now has an exact arithmetic two-branch expression. The independent limiting-law result adds a uniform quantitative explanation of the common moment limit across all fixed prime factors, not just an isolated number. This is a structural, reproducible special-family resolution rather than a table entry or a restatement of Delange's already known second moment.

## Remaining limits
No general formula for arbitrary squarefree moduli is obtained. The statistical estimate assumes fixed \(q\) and its precision deteriorates when \(q\) grows with \(p\). Historical multiple-Franel-integral coverage has not been ruled out completely.
