# Exact fourth Schatten phase profile and sharp interference of fourth-order nilpotents

## Finding
Let \(T\) be a complex Hilbert–Schmidt operator on a complex Hilbert space, with \(T^4=0\). For \(0\le t\le1\), put \(s=1-t\) and
\[
R_{t,\theta}(T)=t e^{i\theta}T+s e^{-i\theta}T^*,\quad
\gamma(T)=\operatorname{Tr}(T^3T^*),\quad
B_t(T)=(t^4+s^4)\|T\|_4^4+4t^2s^2\|T^2\|_2^2.
\]
For every real \(\theta\), the full fourth-power angular profile is exactly
\[
\boxed{\ \|R_{t,\theta}(T)\|_4^4
=B_t(T)+4ts(t^2+s^2)\operatorname{Re}(e^{2i\theta}\gamma(T)).\ }
\]
Consequently the weighted fourth Schatten numerical radius defined by \(w_{t,4}(T)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb R}\|R_{t,\theta}(T)\|_4\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{\ w_{t,4}(T)^4=B_t(T)+4ts(t^2+s^2)|\gamma(T)|.\ }
\]
For \(0<t<1\), the fourth-power profile is independent of phase if and only if \(\gamma(T)=0\), and its exact maximum-minus-minimum oscillation obeys the sharp estimate
\[
\boxed{\ 0\le \operatorname{osc}_{\theta}\|R_{t,\theta}(T)\|_4^4
=8ts(t^2+s^2)|\gamma(T)|
\le\frac12ts(t^2+s^2)\|T\|_2^4.\ }
\]
For \(0<t<1\) and every nonzero \(T\), equality in the last inequality holds if and only if, up to unitary similarity and an orthogonal zero summand, \(T\) is the four-dimensional operator
\[
T e_1=0,\quad T e_2=a e_1,\quad T e_3=b e_2,\quad T e_4=c e_3+d e_1,
\qquad |a|=|b|=|c|=|d|=\|T\|_2/2.
\]
In particular every operator satisfying \(T^3=0\) has a phase-independent fourth-power profile, whereas fourth-order nilpotency already permits the optimal nonzero oscillation; at \(t=1/2\) the sharp oscillation coefficient is \(1/16\) times \(\|T\|_2^4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The Hilbert space is arbitrary (finite- or infinite-dimensional); separability is unnecessary after restriction to the separable range generated by the Hilbert–Schmidt operator. Norms \(\|\cdot\|_p\) are Schatten norms and \(\operatorname{Tr}\) is the standard trace. The operator need not admit any circle covariance or weighted-shift representation. Products under the trace are trace-class because \(T\in\mathcal S_2\). The statements include the endpoint weights. At \(t=0\) or \(t=1\) the oscillation and its upper bound are both zero for every eligible operator, so the nonzero sharpness classification is asserted only for \(0<t<1\).

## Proof
**1. Exact trace expansion.** Set \(A=T^*T\), \(B=TT^*\), and \(z=e^{i\theta}\). Then
\[
R_{t,\theta}(T)^*R_{t,\theta}(T)
=t^2A+s^2B+ts(z^2T^2+\overline z^{\,2}T^{*2}).
\]
Squaring and tracing yields a constant part and terms proportional to \(z^2\), \(\overline z^{\,2}\), \(z^4\), and \(\overline z^{\,4}\). Nilpotency gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(T^4)=0\) and removes both fourth-harmonic terms. The cyclic trace identities
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(B^2)=\|T\|_4^4,\quad
\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\operatorname{Tr}(T^{*2}T^2)=\|T^2\|_2^2,
\]
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(AT^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(BT^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(T^3T^*)=\gamma(T)
\]
leave exactly the displayed expression \(B_t(T)+4ts(t^2+s^2)\operatorname{Re}(z^2\gamma(T))\). Maximizing/minimizing the real part over \(\theta\) gives the exact radius and oscillation formulas. For \(T^3=0\), \(\gamma(T)=0\).

**2. A sharp quartic interference inequality.** Put \(K_j=\ker T^j\), with \(K_0=\{0\}\) and \(K_4=H\), and set \(E_j=K_j\ominus K_{j-1}\) for \(1\le j\le4\). The decomposition \(H=E_1\oplus E_2\oplus E_3\oplus E_4\) is orthogonal; since \(T K_j\subseteq K_{j-1}\), its matrix has nonzero blocks only strictly above the diagonal. Write
\[
A_{12}=T|_{E_2\to E_1},\quad A_{23}=T|_{E_3\to E_2},\quad A_{34}=T|_{E_4\to E_3},\quad A_{14}=T|_{E_4\to E_1}.
\]
The only nonzero block of \(T^3\) is the \((1,4)\) block \(A_{12}A_{23}A_{34}\), so
\[
\gamma(T)=\langle A_{12}A_{23}A_{34},A_{14}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz, the Hilbert–Schmidt product bound, and arithmetic–geometric mean give
\[
|\gamma(T)|\le\prod_{(i,j)\in\{(1,2),(2,3),(3,4),(1,4)\}}\|A_{ij}\|_2
\le\left(\frac{\sum \|A_{ij}\|_2^2}{4}\right)^2
\le\frac1{16}\|T\|_2^4.
\]
The final inequality includes any possible unused blocks \((1,3)\) and \((2,4)\) in \(\|T\|_2^2\); thus it is valid in arbitrary dimension.

**3. Complete equality structure.** Suppose \(T\ne0\) attains equality in \(|\gamma(T)|\le\|T\|_2^4/16\). Equality in the last two inequalities forces the blocks \(A_{13},A_{24}\) to vanish and the four displayed Hilbert–Schmidt block norms to be the same positive number \(r=\|T\|_2/2\). Equality in the product bound \(\|A_{12}A_{23}A_{34}\|_2\le\|A_{12}\|_2\|A_{23}\|_2\|A_{34}\|_2\) forces all three maps to be rank-one and their nonzero singular vectors to match consecutively: \(\|S\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|S\|_2\) for a nonzero compact \(S\) exactly when it has rank one. Equality in the final Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz bound makes \(A_{14}\) a scalar multiple of their rank-one product. Consequently the four blocks occupy four aligned one-dimensional orthogonal subspaces and vanish on their orthogonal complement. In orthonormal vectors chosen from these four subspaces the operator has exactly the displayed path-and-shortcut form, with all four moduli \(r\). Conversely any such operator has \(\|T\|_2^2=4r^2\) and \(|\gamma(T)|=r^4=\|T\|_2^4/16\). Phases are unrestricted and need not be removed simultaneously by diagonal unitary conjugation.

## Verification
Run `python CHECKER.py` with Python 3 and NumPy installed. The script checks the exact trace identity against explicit matrix products for a deterministic collection of complex 4-layer nilpotents, compares the phase extrema and sharp ratio, and tests the boundary cases. It prints `FOURTH_NILPOTENT_PHASE_PROFILE_OK` on success. These finite checks corroborate but do not prove the all-dimensional inequalities or the equality classification; those follow from the preceding trace and filtration arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Pal and Chandok [1] introduced the motivating 2026 set of weighted Schatten radius bounds via generalized Aluthge transformations, including nilpotent matrix examples. Their complete available paper develops estimates and does not contain the exact fourth-order-nilpotent phase profile or sharp interference classification proved here. Sahoo [2] and Kittaneh, Sahoo and Stanković [3] provide relevant earlier operator-inequality context; the accessible bibliographic records concern general bounds rather than this classification. The full paper of [2] was not retrieved and remains a publication-priority risk.

A previously published circle-covariant Schatten moment theorem [4] yields phase independence for graded operators. Its hypothesis excludes generic fourth-order nilpotents; the explicit path-plus-shortcut equality example here has nonconstant angular profile and hence cannot be circle-covariant. That earlier theorem therefore cannot imply this noncovariant sharp oscillation law, though its graded, phase-independent special cases are prior-covered. The new contribution is the exact profile, interference trace, and sharp rigidity for the entire fourth-order nilpotent class, not the definitions of Schatten numerical radii or the elementary phase invariance in graded cases.

## Limitations
The formula is for the fourth Schatten power and operators with \(T^4=0\). Neither a corresponding formula for other Schatten exponents nor a universal sharp oscillation law for higher nilpotency indices is asserted. The numerical checker examines only finite matrices. Literature/database searches cannot conclusively establish publication priority; an equivalent result in older noncommutative trace-moment or operator-ideal literature may exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
