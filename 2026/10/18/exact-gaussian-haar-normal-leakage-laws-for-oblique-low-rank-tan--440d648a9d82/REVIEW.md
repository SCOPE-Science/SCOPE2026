# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The discrepancy is exactly the oblique projection of the normal component. Its three terms lie in orthogonal matrix blocks, so the squared Frobenius norm is their exact sum. The Gaussian one-sided coefficient follows from independent Gaussian blocks and the classical inverse-Wishart inverse trace; the Haar one follows from the idempotence identity \(AA^{\mathsf T}=W^{-1}-I\) and Gaussian-frame representation. Rotational invariance and independence of sketches give the exact two-sided expectation and divergence for every fixed nonzero normal component. Top singular vectors attain the realized operator norm. The standalone CHECK.py program exercises the finite identities and finite-sample predictions; simulation is not substituted for proofs of integrability or infinite-dimensional families.

## Originality (PASS; non-exhaustive bibliographic review)
Carrel–Grigori (arXiv:2607.03402v1, Section 4) supplies the oblique low-rank tangent projector and describes qualitative normal-direction leakage, but its inspected results do not provide the exact Gaussian–Haar expectation, integrability threshold, or realized sharp norm. Tropp et al. (arXiv:1609.00048v2, Appendix A) proves the familiar one-sided Gaussian pseudoinverse ingredient, not the full two-sided oblique tangent claim. Lacotte et al. (arXiv:2002.00864v5) compares sketches for iterative Hessian sketch, an analytically distinct object. Niu et al. (arXiv:2605.24955, Sections 1–2) studies random-sampling oblique regression/CUR rather than independent Gaussian or Haar tangent sketches. Published semantic research search and accessible earlier claims showed no implication covering this exact theorem. Risk remains from unindexed equivalent results, especially inverse-matrix-beta and oblique-projector literature; a failed search is not proof of uniqueness.

## Value (PASS)
For a newly studied low-rank tangent projector, the squared mean normal-residual leakage is a natural stability quantity. The theorem establishes a sharp finite versus infinite expectation boundary and a finite-population Haar gain; the two-by-two square-sketch example shows that merely using all sketch rows cannot regularize Gaussian least squares, whereas Haar orthogonalization can eliminate leakage. This informs sketch design and interpretation of oblique projector error bounds, rather than only providing an arbitrary coefficient computation. It does not establish nonlinear trajectory guarantees or a runtime advantage.
