The necessary-and-sufficient fourth-order Hurwitz inequalities are given with all sign assumptions; the folded-Cauchy ratio is derived from independent Gaussian magnitudes; the trivariate orthant identity is proved from angular symmetry; the substitution to a compact one-dimensional integral is exact. The other four probabilities use an explicit published index relation, not a simulated estimate. Endpoint and zero-probability degeneracies are specified. The numerical diagnostic is auxiliary.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a natural, exactly specified stability invariant for Gaussian fourth-order linear differential equations. It replaces an approximate tabular quantity that determines *all* index probabilities by an elementary one-dimensional integral, providing a reproducible analytic benchmark and a dimensional reduction from the original five-dimensional probability. The result does not purport to solve general degree or asymptotic probability questions.
