# Exact gcd spectra of proposition-free loop dynamic logic on finite directed cycles

## Finding
Consider the directed cycle \(C_n\) with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), for every integer \(n\ge1\). A program letter \(a\) advances by one and its converse \(a^{-1}\) retreats by one. The language is proposition-free propositional dynamic logic with Booleans, diamonds \(\langle\pi\rangle\varphi\), and the loop predicate \(\operatorname{loop}(\pi)\), allowing program union, concatenation, Kleene star, and tests \(\varphi?\). No atomic propositions, nominals, or distinguished vertices are permitted.

For a formula \(\varphi\), let its **cycle spectrum** be \(S_\varphi=\{n\ge1:C_n\models\varphi\}\), where satisfaction at one, hence every, vertex is meant. Then the following conditions on \(S\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}\) are equivalent.

1. \(S=S_\varphi\) for some formula of the specified language.
2. \(S\) lies in the Boolean algebra generated by all divisibility sets \(D_d=\{n\ge1:d\mid n\}\) for \(d\ge1\) and all finite subsets of \(\mathbb N_{>0}\).
3. There exist an integer \(M\ge1\), a finite exceptional set \(F\subset\mathbb N_{>0}\), and a subset \(E\) of the divisors of \(M\), such that whenever \(n\notin F\), membership \(n\in S\) holds exactly when \(\gcd(n,M)\in E\).

These descriptions are effective: a formula yields a finite Boolean combination of divisibility and singleton predicates, so equivalence and validity over the entire cycle family are decidable. This algebra is a strict subalgebra of the ultimately periodic subsets of positive integers: the set \(\{n\ge1:n\equiv1\pmod4\}\) is ultimately periodic but is not definable.

## Assumptions and scope
The underlying models are **exactly** the finite nonempty directed cycles \(C_n\), with their single deterministic invertible successor and its converse, and without valuations for proposition letters. There is no restriction on program star, formula negation, modal nesting, or tests. A program denotes a binary relation in the standard PDL manner; \(\operatorname{loop}(\pi)\) holds at a vertex precisely when its diagonal pair lies in the program relation. The assertion is about the truth of an individual formula over this fixed family, not the finite satisfiability of loop dynamic logic over arbitrary graphs or arbitrary propositional valuations.

## Proof
**1. Symmetry and test elimination at a fixed length.** Every translation \(i\mapsto i+j\pmod n\) is a model automorphism. By structural induction on the mutual formula/program syntax, every formula has constant truth value on each \(C_n\). Consequently a test \(\psi?\) on \(C_n\) is either the identity program or the empty program. Fix any finite formula/program expression. Once the finitely many spectra of its proper test subformulas are specified, the program becomes an ordinary regular expression over \(\{a,a^{-1}\}\), with some tests replaced by epsilon and the others by the empty regular language. We induct on formula complexity and partition lengths by these finitely many lower-complexity test spectra.

**2. The arithmetic lemma.** For a regular language \(L\subseteq\{a,a^{-1}\}^{*}\), define its signed-displacement set \(W(L)=\{|w|_a-|w|_{a^{-1}}:w\in L\}\subseteq\mathbb Z\). By the effective semilinearity of Parikh images, \(W(L)\) is an effectively computable finite union of linear sets
\[
P=\Big\{b+\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_j c_j:t_j\in\mathbb N\Big\},
\]
with integer \(b,c_j\) (and \(r=0\) allowed). A run of \(w\) returns to its starting point in \(C_n\) exactly when \(n\mid(|w|_a-|w|_{a^{-1}})\). Therefore \(\operatorname{loop}(L)\) holds on \(C_n\) precisely when \(n\mid x\) for some \(x\in W(L)\).

For one component \(P\), this last condition is equivalent to
\[
\gcd(n,c_1,\ldots,c_r)\mid b.
\]
Necessity is immediate. Conversely, Bézout supplies integers \(z,z_j\) with \(b=zn+\sum_j z_jc_j\). Replacing every \(-z_j\) by its nonnegative residue modulo \(n\) gives \(t_j\ge0\) and \(b+\sum_jt_jc_j\equiv0\pmod n\). This works also for negative or zero \(c_j\). When some \(c_j\ne0\), put \(g=\gcd(|c_1|,\ldots,|c_r|)>0\). Then the condition is \(\gcd(n,g)\mid b\), depending only on \(\gcd(n,g)\). When all \(c_j=0\), including \(r=0\), the condition is \(n\mid b\): it is always true if \(b=0\), and otherwise holds for only finitely many \(n\). Finite unions of these conditions lie in the algebra in condition 2.

**3. Modal closure including nested tests.** Let \(\mathcal A\) be the Boolean algebra in condition 2. By induction, all proper test subformulas have spectra in \(\mathcal A\). Partition positive integers into finitely many cells of \(\mathcal A\) on which their truth values are fixed. On each cell, every program expression reduces to a regular language as in step 1. Hence its loop predicate has spectrum in \(\mathcal A\) by step 2. For a diamond \(\langle\pi\rangle\psi\), the target \(\psi\) is either true everywhere or false everywhere, and the reduced regular language is either empty or nonempty independently of the cycle length within the cell. Thus its spectrum lies in \(\mathcal A\). Boolean formula operations preserve \(\mathcal A\), finishing the induction and proving 1 implies 2.

**4. Converse expressibility and the gcd normal form.** For every prime \(p\) and \(e\ge1\), the loop condition
\[
\operatorname{loop}\big((a^{p^e})^*;a^{p^{e-1}}\big)
\]
holds on \(C_n\) if and only if \(\gcd(n,p^e)\mid p^{e-1}\), equivalently \(p^e\nmid n\). Its negation defines \(D_{p^e}\), and finite conjunctions define all \(D_d\). Here a displayed power of \(a\) is the indicated finite program concatenation, not a new program operator. For \(t\ge1\), the formula
\[
\operatorname{loop}(a^t)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{\substack{d\mid t\\1\le d<t}}\neg\operatorname{loop}(a^d)
\]
defines precisely the singleton length \(\{t\}\), including \(t=1\), where the conjunction is empty. Consequently all finite sets and Boolean combinations in condition 2 are expressible. For 2 equivalent to 3, note that any finite Boolean combination of divisibility predicates \(D_d\) depends only on \(\gcd(n,M)\), with \(M\) the least common multiple of its finitely many indices. Conversely each equality \(\gcd(n,M)=d\), for \(d\mid M\), is a finite Boolean combination of divisibility tests: require \(d\mid n\) and forbid \(dp\mid n\) for every prime \(p\) with \(dp\mid M\). Finite exceptions are handled by singleton formulas.

**5. Effectivity and strictness.** The mutual structural induction is constructive; regular expressions admit effective semilinear displacement descriptions, and the arithmetic lemma converts them to finite gcd/exception descriptions. Boolean operations on such descriptions are effective. To test whether a representation is empty, enumerate the finitely many exceptional lengths and the nonempty gcd cells: the cell for \(d\mid M\) always contains \(n=d(1+kM)\) for infinitely many \(k\). Hence one can decide emptiness, implication and equivalence of spectra.

The arithmetic progression \(n\equiv1\pmod4\) is not in \(\mathcal A\). Were its membership determined outside a finite set by \(\gcd(n,M)\), there would be arbitrarily large numbers coprime to \(M\) in both congruence classes \(1\) and \(3\) modulo \(4\). Indeed, require either odd class modulo \(4\), and require nonzero residues for each odd prime factor of \(M\); the Chinese remainder theorem gives infinite solutions. Both gcd values equal \(1\), a contradiction. Every set of condition 3 is ultimately periodic (outside the exceptions, changing \(n\) by \(M\) preserves gcd), so the inclusion in the ultimately periodic sets is strict.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `loop_cycle_verify.py` independently compares full world-by-world relational semantics with a translation-invariant displacement evaluator on randomly constructed proposition-free nested formulas using both program directions, tests, union, concatenation, star, diamonds and loops. It passed \(5{,}280\) comparisons (\(240\) formulas on each \(C_n\), \(1\le n\le22\)). It also checked \(2{,}210\) arithmetic examples of prime-power divisibility and exact singleton recognition on cycle lengths up to \(170\). Output: `CYCLE_LOOP_VERIFY_OK formula_semantics=5280 arithmetic=2210`. The finite computations are consistency checks; the proof above is unrestricted.

## Relationship to prior work
Bednarczyk and Swoboda (2026) analyze finite satisfiability for powerful loop PDL fragments over arbitrary relational models, including converse, whereas this theorem concerns the exact arithmetical spectra of **formulas with no proposition letters** on one restricted but natural infinite family of models. Their definitions of looping programs motivate the setting, but their stated satisfiability bounds neither entail nor provide this gcd classification. Their earlier workshop manuscript treats restricted loop finite satisfiability, not cycle-length spectra. The constructive semilinearity used in the proof is a classical input (Parikh's theorem, as expounded by Esparza, Ganty, Kiefer, and Luttenberger); it alone gives only the upper-bound component, not the expressibility converse, closure under arbitrary nested tests, or the arithmetic nondefinability result. Standard dynamic-logic expositions discuss loop definability over general frames without addressing this exact family-specific spectrum. Earlier work on first-order spectra with successor operates in a different syntax and model class. A possibility that equivalent arithmetic normal forms occur in older algebraic automata literature remains.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to **unlabeled single-generator permutation cycles** with the designated converse. It does not apply to colored cycles with freely varying proposition valuations, nondeterministic graphs, arbitrary finite permutations with multiple orbits, or program intersection. No polynomial complexity bound for the constructive normalization is claimed; effective computability is sufficient. The verification program only exercises finite examples. Mathematical originality is supported by targeted comparisons rather than exhaustive coverage of historic literature. Independent validation has not been performed.
