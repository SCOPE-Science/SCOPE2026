# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The frozen path either advances deterministically until its first opposite arrow or alternates forever on an adjacent two-cycle. This gives all disjoint contributions to each signed displacement and the zero atom. Summing an independent geometric block time yields the normalized two-sided geometric law. Direct generating-function differentiation gives the block moments. The regenerative strong law and central limit theorem apply because block lengths have geometric moments, and the exact covariance term handles time-displacement dependence. A symbolic checker reconstructs the stated rational variance, verifies derivative signs/limits, and exhaustively enumerates finite frozen-arrow paths with exact rational arithmetic. Boundary conditions \(p=1\), \(p\downarrow0\), \(q=1/2\), and both signs of drift were checked. Only annealed CLT is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The full-text comparison against Martin and Nagel, arXiv:2610.11919v1, includes their model, equations (1.4),(1.7), Theorems 2.1/2.4, and proof sections 3.1–3.6: general regenerative identities, the local biased endpoint effect and local speed maximum are **not new**. Their inspected results do not give a two-sided geometric law, the exact rational all-refresh-rate coefficients for deterministic arrows, or the resulting global contrast. Avena–den Hollander's cooling regime uses deterministic diverging resampling intervals. Research-record searches of arrow fields, traps, two-cycle displacement, regeneration, and diffusivity did not return an equivalent complete claim. There remains an unexcluded risk from general renewal-reward and arrow-model literature and independent priority assessment is absent. Searches alone are not uniqueness proofs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exactly solvable singular, non-elliptic boundary of a recently investigated dynamically refreshed environment. The full block law gives complete finite-regeneration displacement statistics and globally resolves the refresh-dependence of speed and variance, contrasting monotone transport and interior fluctuation amplification in the biased case. It also supplies an unbiased global comparison, stronger than the earlier local near-full-refresh theorems. The conclusion is neither a restatement of the general LLN/CLT nor a tiny coefficient improvement.

## Limitations
Only global geometric refresh, one-dimensional iid deterministic arrows, and annealed limits are covered. The proof does not show a unique interior maximum, a quenched limit or monotonicity for general site laws. Potential stronger implicit coverage in older specialized literature remains a priority risk.
