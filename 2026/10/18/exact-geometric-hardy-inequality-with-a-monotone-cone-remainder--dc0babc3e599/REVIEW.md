# Exact geometric Hardy inequality with a monotone-cone remainder — review

## Correctness (PASS)

A nonnegative decreasing sequence decomposes into its limit and summable nonnegative successive drops. With a geometric random index, memorylessness yields exact pairwise moments of cutoff indicators and truncated index functions. Subtracting the two quadratic expansions gives the specified positive remainder for all infinite sequences. Since geometric weights sum to one, the \(\ell^p\)-to-\(\ell^2\) inclusion for \(p\ge2\) establishes the common sharp constant, attained only by nonzero constant sequences. The standard unilateral-shift resolvent supplies the unrestricted norm. The accompanying program tests exact rational identities, including full geometric tails; no inference from finite testing to infinite proof is made.

## Originality (PASS; bibliographic qualifications)

Kalybay–Persson–Temirkhanova (2015), doi:10.1186/s13660-015-0843-9, was read in full HTML. Their Theorems 2.1 and 5.2 give boundedness characterizations and two-sided **comparability**, not an exact formula or drop remainder; their exponent condition excludes the equal-exponent endpoint. Gao (2014), arXiv:1401.7156v1, was also read in full HTML. Its Theorem 1.1 uses normalized partial averages with the *same weights* and exponent on both sides, and gives upper/lower bounds rather than the present cross-exponent geometric norm. Bennett–Grosse-Erdmann (2006) is a highly relevant earlier treatment; its abstract and the accurate best-constant bounds quoted by Gao were inspected, but not the full paper. Equivalent older formulas cannot be ruled out; neither absence of search hits nor category separation is treated as a proof of novelty. The existing published database comparisons concern other operator invariants, such as absolute-Cesàro self-improvement.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The model is a natural discrete Hardy cumulative operator on the cone of monotone functions with a canonical one-parameter geometric measure. The closed-form sharp norm for every \(p\ge2\), its complete equality classification, and the **exact positive quadratic stability kernel for every drop** yield a structural and quantitatively sharp statement, as opposed to an arbitrary finite norm computation. The exact strict gap to the unrestricted Hilbert norm shows that monotonicity alters the optimum in a way not apparent from ordinary boundedness criteria. The result makes no claim about arbitrary weights or kernels.

## Limitations and nearest literature

The relevant Bennett–Grosse-Erdmann (2006) full text was not accessible, and older generalized Hardy characterization theory may contain an indirectly covering exact theorem. Andersen (1991) was inspected only through bibliographic scientific summaries. No conclusions about whole-document noncoverage are drawn from these partial sources. Independent scrutiny of prior literature remains desirable. The proof is restricted to the real, nonnegative, nonincreasing cone, geometric input/output weights and exponents \(2\le p\le\infty\).
