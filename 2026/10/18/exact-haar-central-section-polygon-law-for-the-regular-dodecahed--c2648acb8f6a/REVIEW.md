The exact dodecahedral side-count correspondence and full Haar probability law were not found in inspected primary literature. (2024) establish general combinatorial section-enumeration tools without evaluated dodecahedron Haar probabilities. The complete published icosahedron result concerns sections of a different solid; polarity yields a **projection** law, not the dodecahedron-section law. A pre-existing result in historical stereology remains possible. MathPages' decimal area fractions differ from those obtained from the exact spherical-angle certificate, and no priority claim is based on that numerical discrepancy.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result determines a complete, scale-invariant, three-atom distribution for one of the five natural Platonic-body section models. The normal-space chamber table alone is insufficient to identify planar section types; the edge-incidence proof completes that geometric mapping and gives three closed elementary probabilities. The object, invariant, and need for exact type frequencies are motivated by stereological random-section questions, and the classification covers every generic normal rather than an arbitrarily selected sample.

## Source comparison and limitations

The closest published comparison is the full central icosahedron type law, which yields ten- and twelve-gons under a six-circle tessellation. Related arXiv:2006.09492, arXiv:2108.08917, and arXiv:2412.12419 treat other objects, quantities or general algorithms. The earlier MathPages ten-circle partition, with numerically tabulated regional sizes, is directly acknowledged. The present result is only for a regular, centrally cut dodecahedron and generic normals. An equivalent unindexed work and uncertainty about the older decimal regional-area calculation remain bibliographic risks.
