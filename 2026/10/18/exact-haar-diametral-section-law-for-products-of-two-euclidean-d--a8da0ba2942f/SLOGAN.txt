A Haar-random central two-plane section of \(B_2^2\times hB_2^2\) retains the entire ambient Euclidean diameter with exact probability \(\pi h/[2(1+h^2)]\), maximized uniquely at \(h=1\) with value \(\pi/4\). A four-dimensional product of two disks thus has a sharp jump in the full-diameter preservation probability from zero for random lines to this nontrivial probability for random two-planes, then to certainty for every three-plane.
