# Review of the central-section polygon law

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted distribution follows from the exact 24-vertex/36-edge description of the body. The five normal sign chambers in the signed-permutation fundamental sector are exhaustive. The polygon side counts are exact finite edge-crossing counts; the ten-sided chamber is a spherical triangle with solid-angle denominator \(11+3\sqrt{14}+4\sqrt6+2\sqrt{21}\). Every edge has endpoint cosine \(4/5\), giving the independently derived mean \(36\arccos(4/5)/\pi\); this and normalization fix the remaining two masses. The standalone checker verifies the finite classification and independently compares 605 generic sections reconstructed from all 14 facet inequalities. The continuum formula is an analytic consequence of these facts, not of Monte Carlo.

## Originality — PASS with historical priority risk
Recent full-text work by Cesaroni and Novaga (arXiv:2609.02384v1) concerns an isoperimetric optimum of the same regular truncated octahedron, not random central-section sidedness. De Loera, Lopez-Campos and Torres (arXiv:2412.12419v1) study allowable side counts of polytope sections but do not evaluate this spherical probability law. Similar published exact laws for the icosahedron and rectangular boxes treat different bodies. The closest previously documented same-body statistic is planar projection area, which is not the central-section polygon law. The abstract of Birkemeyer et al. (arXiv:2609.16195v1) was accessible but its full text was not, creating an explicit unresolved priority risk. Exhaustive historical uniqueness is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete Haar side-count law is a natural stereological invariant for the BCC Voronoi cell, the distinguished space-filling regular truncated octahedron. The transition from 6 to 8 to 10 sides, exact decagonal solid angle, and independent edge-angle expectation provide structural information not supplied by the canonical surface-area or projection-area formulas. This is an exact self-contained classification for one geometrically motivated solid, without a claim about all parallelohedra.

## Limitations
Only centered generic Haar-random planes and an undistorted regular truncated octahedron are covered. Noncentral slicing, nonuniform directions, and affine deformations remain unaddressed. Related literature may contain equivalent formulas under alternative names; no independent validation has been performed.
