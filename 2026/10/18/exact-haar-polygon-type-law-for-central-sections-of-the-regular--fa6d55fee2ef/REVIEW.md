# Scientific review: truncated-tetrahedron central section law

## Correctness

**PASS.** The object is the twelve-vertex regular truncated tetrahedron with explicit integer coordinates. Its eighteen edges are obtained by an exact squared-distance test and independently described as twelve truncation-face edges plus six original-edge remainders. For a normal outside twelve exceptional great circles, the section polygon's vertex count is the number of sign-changing edges. No section vertex is lost to degeneracy on this full-measure set. The complete great-circle arrangement has 132 distinct crossing points, 264 arcs, and 134 convex spherical chambers. A deterministic integer sign-certificate enumerates all chambers and identifies their corners. The spherical-excess formula then gives six explicit chamber areas; their weighted total is exactly \(4\pi\), with the displayed three section-count probabilities. Independent edge-crossing expectation equals \(18\arccos(7/11)/\pi\), exactly matching the distribution's mean. The finite checker has a mathematical completeness argument, not a statistical inference.

## Originality

**PASS under currently inspected evidence, with residual historical risk.** The first motivating source, Birkemeyer–Brose–Brandenburg–Prün (2026, arXiv:2609.16195), develops combinatorial reconstruction and inequalities from central section vertex counts; its inspected theorems and examples do not give Haar measure for truncated tetrahedron sections. De Loera–Lopez-Campos–Torres (2024, arXiv:2412.12419), studies maximum attainable counts, section-vertex sequences, and cubes, not the three exact Haar probabilities or the spherical normal-cell census. Earlier exact probability laws for different three-dimensional solids are not transferable without an explicit transformation preserving both edge-sign arrangements and uniform spherical area; the present solid is not such an already covered case. Targeted literature searches located no equivalent distribution or stronger theorem that implies this claim. The overlap comparison is by statements and implications rather than titles. Historical priority against inaccessible or unindexed older tables cannot be guaranteed and remains a declared risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Exact random central-section combinatorics of a major Archimedean solid is a natural classification problem motivated by research on section-vertex functions. The distribution has three nontrivial support values and the calculation reveals six spherical normal-cell classes, including unexpected eight-sided orientation cells; the full event probabilities and exact mean are geometric invariants of the chosen Euclidean uniform-direction measure. The exact six-class finite answer gives a reproducible reference for future polyhedral integral-geometry calculations, not merely a small numerical improvement or a parameter substitution.

## Scope and limits

The theorem covers one regular solid and Haar-distributed *central* normals; it does not classify arbitrary offset sections or other Archimedean solids. The included integer proof certificate checks the finite combinatorial identities, while standard spherical area and probabilistic lemmas provide the analytic bridge to normalized probabilities. The computed results and originality comparison have received no independent scientific audit.
