# Scientific review: higher Witt commuting tuples

**Correctness — PASS.** The full proof partitions nilpotent derivations by coefficient valuation, isolates and counts unit-coefficient nilpotent lines by an explicit reciprocal-integrability condition, and classifies low-valuation centralizers as uniquely determined abelian spaces. The resulting tuple strata partition the complete set, and their algebraic parameter spaces prove the dimension statement. The standalone code exhaustively checks direct derivation matrices in characteristic five and two characteristic-three boundary fields; only the proof establishes unrestricted arities.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risks.** The closest primary source is Yao and Chang, *Nilpotent Commuting Varieties of the Witt Algebra* (arXiv:1301.5667v1), especially its Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 3.6. It supplies the centralizer classification and analyzes **pairs**, with geometric dimension \(p\). The new final claim concerns exact finite-field point counts of ordered pairwise commuting tuples for every \(m\ge3\), plus the higher-arity dimension \(m(p-1)/2+1\). The pair case is explicitly treated as prior, not a newly discovered special case. Evans–Fuchs (arXiv:math/0111271v1) establishes restricted-Witt algebra context but does not cover higher tuples; Premet (arXiv:1405.2426) analyzes regular derivations rather than simultaneous commuting tuples. An equivalent older higher-arity enumerator remains possible under different terminology.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Higher nilpotent commuting varieties are canonical in modular Lie representation theory and support-variety geometry. The exact all-arity point counts and sharp change from the pair dimension to the higher-tuple dimension are complete, natural structural invariants, not arbitrary parameter samples or only isolated finite computations. Distinct nilpotent-centralizer strata explain the leading asymptotics.

The proof is restricted to the rank-one Witt algebra in characteristic at least five. A full classification of irreducible components and an independent mathematical audit are outside the claim.
