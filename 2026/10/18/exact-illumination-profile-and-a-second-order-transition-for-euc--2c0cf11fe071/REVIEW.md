# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The volume law is derived from the complete feasible-center lens, not inferred from a drawing. The argument explicitly controls the two arcs of the center lens, identifies the extremal radius-\(R\) disks, and computes the Green-area contribution of their tangential boundary arcs. The global endpoints, strict inclusion and volume monotonicity, nonexistence of a feasible containing disk outside the maximal radius, and equivalence between disk radius at most \(R\) and \(R\)-ball convexity are checked. The \(\delta^{4/3}\) coefficient is obtained by exact symbolic Puiseux inversion, and the standalone checker reproduces its algebra. Scope: fixed \(0<r<R\) and unweighted planar area.

## Originality

PASS under the stated best-knowledge search standard, with residual historical-priority risk. Oliva–Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2607.13714v1, publicly posted 2026-07-15) introduce the relevant illumination operation and establish general convexity and leading small-\(\delta\) asymptotics. They include qualitative examples that \(R\)-ball convexity may fail, but the examined results do not give a complete radial area profile, a sharp illumination-volume threshold, or a sign-changing second coefficient. Schütt–Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2504.15488v1) treat related floating, not illuminated, bodies. Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2512.19836v1) study relative surface areas. The closest geometric second-order literature concerns ordinary geodesic/hyperbolic illumination, a different hull operation. The checked existing records do not imply the combined new conclusion. A historically older circle-geometry evaluation is still possible; no independent claim of priority is made.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The original construction is a recently introduced geometric quantity with first-order asymptotics; the new full-range elementary benchmark determines the exact parameter at which ordinary convexity ceases to imply \(R\)-ball convexity. The nontrivial second correction has an exact critical radius ratio and reverses sign, identifying a sharp local geometric regime that the leading relative-affine-area term cannot detect. The fully nonlinear profile and its boundary mechanism offer a reproducible test case for extension to smooth noncircular ball-convex bodies.

## Limitations and closest literature

Only round planar disks and fixed radii are covered, with no uniform expansion near degenerating radius ratios; the coefficient of the subsequent correction at the critical ratio remains unknown.
