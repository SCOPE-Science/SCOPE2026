# Scientific review: spherical-sector John ellipsoid

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed ellipsoid is feasible in the cone because \(c^2=a^2+\cot^2\theta\,b^2\) in both regimes. Maximizing a single quadratic on \([-1,1]\) proves spherical containment for every \(d\ge2\) and every aperture, with the two branches meeting exactly where its maximizer leaves the axial tip. Explicit finite John contact decompositions prove global maximality among all ellipsoids, not merely axial ones: positive weights produce zero first moment and identity second moment. The volume and second-derivative jump follow by substitution and differentiation. The standalone verifier passed exact rational identities and ten independent numerical three-parameter comparisons; numerical sampling is corroborative, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated historical risks

Güler–Gürtuna, arXiv:0709.0707 (2007), was inspected in full relevant sections including its introduction, §3, §7, and §8. Its exact bodies are ball/ellipsoid slabs between parallel planes and flat truncated second-order cones, not the intersection of a ball and a pointed cone. Its cone results concern covering ellipsoids, and its inscribed results concern slabs. Ivanov–Lángi–Naszódi–Sagmeister, arXiv:2507.06947 (2025), was read in its introduction, contact conditions, and cone-related discussion; it establishes general necessary conditions and examples, not the displayed all-dimensional explicit sector phase transition. Searches of published mathematical records found a John ellipsoid for a flat Khachiyan half-cone and a capped hypercube, but these body families and contact conditions do not imply the present sharp spherical contact switch. Comparisons were statement/implication based rather than title based. The closest flat-half-cone result was available by index summary only; an inaccessible full text could conceal an unexpected reduction, and a broader historical-priority search remains valuable.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The intersection of the Euclidean ball and an infinite circular cone is a natural model with both curved and conical boundary pieces; its maximal contained ellipsoid is an intrinsic affine-geometric invariant. The theorem solves this optimization exactly in every dimension, locates a previously unaccounted boundary-contact bifurcation, supplies explicit positive John certificates, and determines a dimension-sharp second-derivative jump. This is a structural classification rather than a perturbative or selected-numerical example. It does not solve general cones or non-Euclidean balls.
