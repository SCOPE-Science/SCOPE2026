# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The parameter domain is explicit: each integer \(n\ge2\) and real \(0\le s<1\). The positive algebraic semiaxes satisfy the two governing identities; completing the square proves inclusion in **both** balls. The claimed contact orbits follow from the exact equality case. At \(2n\) genuine common supporting points, a centered regular-simplex frame has weighted mean zero and covariance identity. Hence every arbitrary competitor ellipsoid has transformed trace at most \(n\), determinant at most one, and equality only for the unit ball at the origin. This establishes unrestricted uniqueness rather than merely optimality among centered ellipsoids of revolution. The thin-lens limit follows from separately proved semiaxis and integral asymptotics. The standalone numerical diagnostic checks these identities but is not used to infer universal truth from a grid.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
Finch's arXiv:1301.5515v1, §1, analyzes the same symmetric lens and its intrinsic measures, not maximal inscribed ellipsoids. The previously published all-dimensional mean-width result for the same lens supplies the support function and an intrinsic-volume formula, not an explicit John ellipsoid. Güler and Gürtuna, DOI:10.1080/10556788.2011.626037, §6, solve the John problem for spherical slabs, which have flat caps and are not affinely equivalent to two-ball intersections. Ivanov et al., arXiv:2507.06947v2, supply general ellipsoid-of-revolution and contact conditions, not this lens formula. Statement-level comparisons against related published mathematical records and the inspected primary sources did not identify stronger coverage. Unindexed historical geometry or optimization literature might nevertheless contain an equivalent formula; the search cannot certify absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
The intersection of two equal balls is a canonical body of ball-convex geometry, long studied through its volume and mean width. The exact maximum-volume ellipsoid is a natural previously undetermined geometric invariant, with a global (not symmetry-restricted) optimum, a rigid contact-latitude law independent of separation, and a positive closed thin-body volume-fraction limit in every dimension. These structural conclusions are mathematically motivated, and go beyond textbook use of the existence of John's ellipsoid or recomputation of a familiar table entry. No claim of broader results for unequal balls or arbitrary ball intersections is made.
