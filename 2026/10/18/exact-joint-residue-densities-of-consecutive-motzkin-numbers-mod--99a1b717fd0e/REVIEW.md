# Self-review

## Correctness: PASS

The exact matrix follows from Theorem 11 of Krattenthaler and Müller (arxiv:1608.05657), a proved 16-state primitive Markov chain for the independent high binary digits, and an exhaustive disjoint partition into 26 ordinary low words and exceptional prefixes classified by the first zero bit and its successor. Four-step invariance makes the exceptional geometric tail exactly summable. Every asserted zero transition is absent in every prefix case. Aligned-dyadic convergence, uniform in the remaining fixed upper digits, yields ordinary natural densities for initial intervals. The bundled verifier replays the 64 exact fractions, graph calculations, modular prefix invariance and 4096 exact recurrence coefficients. Finite testing is not used as proof of the infinite limit.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risk

Rowland–Yassawi (arxiv:1310.8635, Sections 3.2–3.3) presents a modular automaton and one-coordinate distributions for smaller moduli. Krattenthaler–Müller (arxiv:1608.05657, Theorem 11) and Wang–Xin (DOI 10.37236/7092, Theorem 3) provide pointwise modulo-eight congruences, not the evaluated joint limiting two-coordinate measure. Searches for equivalent transition tables, automatic-correlation reductions, indexed exact databases and broader coverage found no explicit 8-by-8 joint law or five forbidden ordered pairs. General automaton techniques can in principle calculate correlations, so an unindexed table or stronger earlier theorem remains a risk, not disproved by searches.

## Value: PASS

All 64 limiting neighboring-residue probabilities are natural arithmetic invariants of the well-studied Motzkin sequence. Their exact enumeration proves 44 positive ordered pairs, five otherwise surprising impossible transitions, and a strong distinction between marginal and adjacent distributions: residue 4 has positive one-point frequency 1/6 but the pair (4,4) never occurs. The pointwise prior formulas do not by themselves present this complete correlation information. The scope is limited to neighboring terms modulo eight and has no discrepancy rate.
