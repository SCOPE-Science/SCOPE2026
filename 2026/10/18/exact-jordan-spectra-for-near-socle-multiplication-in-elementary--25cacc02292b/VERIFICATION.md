# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The characteristic-two group algebra is the square-free exterior algebra. For homogeneous u in degree n-3 the only nonzero graded maps under multiplication start at degrees 0,1,2,3, the outer maps have rank one for u nonzero, and the middle maps are dual under perfect wedge pairing, each of contraction rank r of the dual alternating 3-form. Therefore rank L_u=2+2r and square-zero Jordan type is fixed by r. A nonzero alternating three-form has full contraction rank 3 or at least 5: rank 4 is impossible by identification of Lambda^3 on 4-space with a vector contraction of volume; explicit full-support forms in dimensions 3,5,6,7 plus direct sums realize every permitted larger rank. Subspace-lattice Möbius inversion counts forms with zero radical and yields N_{n,q}(r)=[n choose r]_q F_r(q). A fresh exhaustive independent 2^20 enumeration for n=6, q=2 gave multiplication ranks 0,8,12,14 in counts 1,1395,54684,992496. The all-n,q theorem rests on the exterior pairing and inversion, not this census.

## originality

PASS

Prior Jordan-type representation papers study generic or constant-type module behavior, whereas this theorem enumerates every coefficient of one particular graded regular-module operator. Classification of alternating trilinear forms by Cohen–Helminck through dimension seven and Hora–Pudlak in dimension eight can determine bounded cases, but neither cited result supplies the direct all-dimensional Gaussian radical-kernel census as an explicit consequence in material accessed. Counting solely by radical rank avoids full GL(V)-orbit classification. Risk of older general radical-kernel inversion formula being recognized as mathematically equivalent remains disclosed.

## value

PASS

The near-socle component of the regular group algebra is a naturally singled-out graded slice: its multiplication operators have a robust structural rank defect. Exact possible Jordan partitions, the forbidden multiplication rank ten, and full all-field multiplicities form a natural complete classification useful for modular representation examples and finite alternating-form enumeration.
