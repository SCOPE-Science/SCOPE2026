For every Euclidean ball cut by one halfspace, the largest contained centrally symmetric convex body is a unique reflected-intersection core with an exact all-dimensional volume integral. Its symmetry center stays at the original ball center until the sharp cut threshold \(a=-\sqrt{1-2^{-2/(n-1)}}\), then moves into the cap; in three dimensions the optimal symmetry fraction of a hemisphere is exactly \(2/\sqrt7\).
