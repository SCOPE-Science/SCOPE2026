# Exact least periods and value densities of all bounded equally shifted Fibonacci gcd sequences

## Finding
Let \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\), and fix any integer \(a\) with \(|a|\ge2\). Define
\[
G_a(n)=\gcd(F_n+a,F_{n+1}+a),\quad n\ge1,\qquad N=a^4-1.
\]
The gcd is taken nonnegative; under these hypotheses it is positive. For any integer \(d\ge2\), write \(z(d)\) for the least positive index with \(d\mid F_{z(d)}\). Let \(u_d\) be the residue \(F_{z(d)+1}\bmod d\), and let \(h_d\) be its multiplicative order modulo \(d\). This order exists and divides \(4\). Call \(d\) **eligible** for \(a\) when \(-a\bmod d\) lies in the cyclic group generated by \(u_d\). For each eligible \(d\), let \(j_d\) be the unique integer \(0\le j_d<h_d\) satisfying \(u_d^{j_d}\equiv-a\pmod d\), and set
\[
L_d=z(d)h_d,\qquad c_d\equiv1+j_dz(d)\pmod{L_d}.
\]

**Exact divisor-event classification.** For every divisor \(d\mid N\) with \(d\ge2\), and every \(n\ge1\),
\[
d\mid G_a(n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
n\equiv c_d\pmod{L_d},&\text{if }d\text{ is eligible};\\
\text{false},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
A nonempty divisor event has **least** positive period \(L_d\), not just some sufficient period. For \(d=1\) define \(L_1=1\), \(c_1=0\), and declare it eligible.

**Exact least period.** The least positive period of the entire integer-valued sequence \(G_a(n)\), starting at \(n=1\), is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle P(a)=\operatorname{lcm}_{\substack{d\mid N\\ d\ \text{eligible}}}L_d.}
\]
Thus the least period is computable from the divisors of \(a^4-1\), Fibonacci ranks, and discrete logarithms of order at most \(4\); no search through candidate periods of the gcd sequence is needed. It divides the Pisano period modulo \(N\).

**Exact value-frequency law.** For each positive divisor \(q\mid N\), the natural density of \(\{n\ge1:G_a(n)=q\}\) exists and equals
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\delta_a(q)=\sum_{\substack{q\mid d\mid N\\d\ \text{eligible}}}
\frac{\mu(d/q)}{L_d}.}
\]
Here \(\mu\) is the Möbius function. Every \(\delta_a(q)\) is a nonnegative rational number, they sum to \(1\), and a given divisor \(q\) occurs as an exact gcd value if and only if \(\delta_a(q)>0\). Thus the formula also determines the exact finite value spectrum and the number of distinct values for each fixed \(a\).

## Assumptions and scope
The index domain is \(n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\); the shift is any fixed integer \(a\) with \(|a|\ge2\). The excluded shifts \(-1,0,1\) are not asserted to satisfy the theorem. The integer \(N=a^4-1\) is positive. Every modular order and rank in the finding is a finite positive integer. A congruence modulo \(1\) is interpreted as holding for all integers. Natural density refers to the limit of counts among \(1\le n\le X\) divided by \(X\) as \(X\to\infty\).

## Proof
**1. Uniform bound for all gcd values.** The Fibonacci identity
\[
F_{n+1}^2-F_nF_{n+1}-F_n^2=(-1)^n
\]
shows, upon reducing both successive Fibonacci values modulo \(G_a(n)\), that \(-a^2\equiv(-1)^n\pmod{G_a(n)}\). Consequently,
\[
G_a(n)\mid a^2+(-1)^n\mid(a^2-1)(a^2+1)=N.
\]
This known divisibility bound, proved here for completeness, makes the set of relevant divisors finite.

**2. Ranks and scalar Fibonacci matrices.** Let
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q^m=\begin{pmatrix}F_{m+1}&F_m\\F_m&F_{m-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad(m\ge1).
\]
Since \(Q\) is invertible modulo every \(d\), some positive power of \(Q\) is the identity modulo \(d\); thus \(z(d)\) exists. The standard strong divisibility property \(\gcd(F_r,F_s)=F_{\gcd(r,s)}\), extended to the zero index, implies
\[
d\mid F_m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z(d)\mid m\qquad(m\ge0).
\]
Because \(F_{z(d)}\equiv0\pmod d\), the recurrence gives \(F_{z(d)-1}\equiv F_{z(d)+1}\pmod d\), so
\[
Q^{z(d)}\equiv u_d I\pmod d.
\]
Taking determinants yields \(u_d^2\equiv(-1)^{z(d)}\pmod d\). Therefore \(u_d\) is a unit and \(u_d^4\equiv1\pmod d\); its order \(h_d\) divides \(4\). Repeated scalar multiplication gives
\[
F_{1+jz(d)}\equiv u_d^j\pmod d\qquad(j\ge0).
\]
These rank and matrix facts are standard and are also used in the originating literature; they are not separately claimed as novel.

**3. Exact single-residue divisor events.** The Fibonacci recurrence gives \(F_{n+1}-F_n=F_{n-1}\). For \(n\ge1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\mid G_a(n)&\Longleftrightarrow d\mid F_{n-1}\ \text{and}\ d\mid F_n+a\\
&\Longleftrightarrow n-1=jz(d)\ \text{for an integer }j\ge0,\quad u_d^j\equiv-a\pmod d.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(-a\notin\langle u_d\rangle\), no such index exists. Otherwise \(j\equiv j_d\pmod{h_d}\), yielding precisely the single residue class \(n\equiv1+j_dz(d)\pmod{z(d)h_d}\). A nonempty single residue class modulo a positive integer \(L\) has least period exactly \(L\): every translation preserving it is divisible by \(L\). This proves the first assertion, including sharpness.

**4. Least period of the full gcd.** Define \(P(a)\) by the stated least common multiple, with the divisor \(1\) included. Every divisor predicate \(d\mid G_a(n)\) is invariant under adding \(P(a)\). Since \(G_a(n)\mid N\), the elementary identity \(m=\sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)\) recovers the actual gcd from its divisor predicates:
\[
G_a(n)=\sum_{\substack{d\mid N\\ d\mid G_a(n)}}\varphi(d).
\]
Hence \(P(a)\) is a period. Conversely, let \(T\ge1\) be any period of \(G_a(n)\). Then every divisor predicate is unchanged by translation by \(T\), and the exact single-residue property forces \(L_d\mid T\) for each eligible \(d\mid N\). Thus \(P(a)\mid T\), proving minimality. Since \(G_a(n)\) depends only on the consecutive Fibonacci residues modulo \(N\), the Pisano period \(\pi(N)\) is a period, and so \(P(a)\mid\pi(N)\).

**5. Exact distributions.** For every \(q\mid N\), the finite Möbius identity
\[
\mathbf1_{\{G_a(n)=q\}}=
\sum_{\substack{q\mid d\mid N\\d\mid G_a(n)}}\mu(d/q)
\]
follows by writing \(G_a(n)=qk\) when \(q\) divides the gcd and using \(\sum_{r\mid k}\mu(r)=\mathbf1_{\{k=1\}}\); when \(q\nmid G_a(n)\), both sides vanish. By part 3, each eligible condition \(d\mid G_a(n)\) is one arithmetic progression with natural density \(1/L_d\), while ineligible conditions have density \(0\). Summing finitely many such densities proves the formula for \(\delta_a(q)\). Nonnegativity, total mass \(1\), and the positivity criterion follow equally from the periodic sequence of least period \(P(a)\), since \(\delta_a(q)\) is the number of occurrences of \(q\) in one complete period divided by \(P(a)\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone file `verify.py` uses Python's standard library and independently constructs each local event, checks the claimed residue condition against successive modular Fibonacci values, reconstructs every gcd by the totient divisor sum, excludes all smaller divisors of the proposed period as periods, and compares the Möbius rational density with exact frequencies from a complete period. The checks succeed for **all 22 integer shifts with \(2\le|a|\le12\)**. They are finite illustrations only: the infinite results are established by the preceding proof.

For example, \(a=3\) gives \(N=80\), \(P(3)=60\), and over each period the respective counts for gcd values \(1,2,4,5,8,10\) are \(38,9,5,2,5,1\). Also \(P(9)=60\), whereas \(P(-9)=120\), illustrating that changing the sign of the shift can alter the exact least period. To reproduce, run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing this file and its accompanying script; success ends with `VERIFY_OK: 22 signed shifts, complete least-period and density replay`.

## Relationship to prior work
Spilker's 2016 analysis established the boundedness, divisor restriction, and existence of a period modulo \(a^4-1\), among other properties. Rahn and Kreh's 2018 paper calculated the cases \(a=\pm3\), analyzed ranks and the scalar residue \(F_{z(d)+1}\), and explicitly posed the **general smallest-period problem** for all \(|a|\ge2\) as Problem 13 (page 11). Their Theorem 3 supplies necessary restrictions on which divisors occur as **exact gcd values**; it does not state the single-progression characterization of every divisor predicate, a formula for the least overall period, or the Möbius density distribution given here. Chen and Pan (2020) prove sufficient periods for general shifted Horadam gcd sequences and expressly say their method need not determine the smallest period (Section 2, page 5). The 2022 papers of Hieu–Spilker–Thang and Do–Thang extend boundedness/periodicity to more general shifted Fibonacci configurations, but expressly distinguish a sufficient Fibonacci-modulus period from the smallest period and do not provide this all-parameter least-period formula. Taşdemir and Ertaş (2025), Theorems 3.1–3.2, give explicit residue-by-residue gcd values only for the special shifts \(a=\pm4\); those are consistent special instances and do not imply the universal local minimal-period or distribution law. Known scalar-matrix and strong-divisibility lemmas are credited, not claimed as new. The contribution is their synthesis into sharp local periods and a global minimality proof, together with the exact arithmetic distribution of gcd values.

## Limitations
The result applies to equal shifts and consecutive Fibonacci terms, with \(|a|\ge2\); it does not address differing shifts, general gap lengths, or excluded shifts. Although all formulas are finite and effective, obtaining the factorization of \(a^4-1\) and all associated ranks may be costly for large \(|a|\); no polynomial-time claim or asymptotic law in \(|a|\) is made. Equivalent or stronger unpublished/unindexed results cannot be excluded. Spilker's 2016 chapter was available in accessible metadata and in detailed restatement by later papers, but not in full text in the consulted sources; this is a specific residual literature-access limitation, not evidence of a contradictory theorem. An independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
