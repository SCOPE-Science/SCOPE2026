# Exact local KL separation of Mallows and Plackett–Luce ranking laws

## Finding

For the uniform-reference Kendall Mallows family and the item-weight Plackett–Luce family on the same \(n\) labeled alternatives, the **best-possible relative-entropy cost of replacing one family with the other near uniformity** has an explicit harmonic-number coefficient for every \(n\ge2\).

\[
\inf_{\theta:\,\sum_i\theta_i=0} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(M_{n,\varepsilon}\Vert L_{n,\theta})
= C_n\varepsilon^2+O_n(|\varepsilon|^3),\qquad
C_n=\frac{n(n-1)(2n+5)}{144}-\frac{n(n^2-1)(n-1)}{96(n-H_n)},
\quad H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j.
\]

Here the asymptotic is at fixed \(n\) as \(\varepsilon\to0\). The coefficient is zero exactly when \(n=2\), is positive for every \(n\ge3\), and obeys
\[
C_3=\frac5{168},\qquad C_n\sim\frac{n^3}{288}\quad(n\to\infty).
\]
The unique best-fitting centered Plackett–Luce log-weights satisfy
\[
\theta_i^*(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\,\frac{n-1}{2(n-H_n)}\left(i-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)+O_n(\varepsilon^2).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Fix \(n\ge2\). A permutation \(\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n)\) lists the items from first to last. Its inversion count is \(I(\pi)=\#\{(a,b):a<b,\ \pi_a>\pi_b\}\). Define
\[
M_{n,\varepsilon}(\pi)=\frac{e^{\varepsilon I(\pi)}}{\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}e^{\varepsilon I(\sigma)}},\qquad
L_{n,\theta}(\pi)=\prod_{t=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\theta_{\pi_t}}}{\sum_{j=t}^{n}e^{\theta_{\pi_j}}},\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_i=0.
\]
\(\varepsilon\in\mathbb R\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R^n\). Divergence is in natural-log units and is directed from Mallows to Plackett–Luce. The expansion's constants may depend on fixed \(n\); the growing-\(n\) statement concerns its coefficient, not a simultaneous uniform expansion.

## Proof

**Step 1: local information projection.** Under the uniform law \(U\) on \(S_n\), the Mallows score at \(\varepsilon=0\) is \(Z=I-\mathbb E_U I\). Write \(R_i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) for the rank of item \(i\). The score of \(\log L\) at \(\theta=0\) is
\[
S_i=1-\sum_{t=1}^{R_i}\frac1{n-t+1}=1-H_n+H_{n-R_i}.
\]
It has zero mean and \(\sum_iS_i=0\). Every positive-target ranking law has a unique interior Plackett–Luce information projection modulo common shifts: the objective is coercive and strictly convex on \(\sum\theta_i=0\), since every ordering has positive target probability. The Hessian at the uniform target is positive definite on this subspace. Therefore the minimizer is smooth near \(\varepsilon=0\). Writing \(G=\mathbb E_U[SS^\top]\) and \(b=\mathbb E_U[ZS]\), the standard second-order expansion of two normalized finite exponential families, or a direct Taylor expansion of their log probabilities, yields
\[
\inf_\theta D_{\rm KL}(M_{n,\varepsilon}\Vert L_{n,\theta})
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\left(\mathbb E_UZ^2-b^\top G^{+}b\right)+O_n(|\varepsilon|^3),
\quad \theta^*(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon G^{+}b+O_n(\varepsilon^2),
\]
where \(G^+\) is the inverse on the centered subspace.

**Step 2: score covariance.** A random uniform ranking may be generated by sequential uniform selection among remaining items. The selection-stage score increments are martingale differences. At a stage with \(m\) items remaining, the probability that item \(i\) is still present equals \(m/n\), and its conditional score variance given presence equals \(m^{-1}(1-m^{-1})\). Summing from \(m=n\) down to \(m=2\) gives
\[
G_{ii}=\frac1n\sum_{m=2}^{n}(1-m^{-1})=\frac{n-H_n}{n}.
\]
Exchangeability and \(\sum_i S_i=0\) imply \(G_{ij}=-G_{ii}/(n-1)\) for \(i\ne j\). Thus \(G\) has the single nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda=(n-H_n)/(n-1)\), with multiplicity \(n-1\).

**Step 3: cross-covariance.** Conditioning on \(R_i=r\), every pair not involving label \(i\) remains unbiased, whereas the \(i-1\) smaller and \(n-i\) larger labels contribute respectively \((n-r)/(n-1)\) and \((r-1)/(n-1)\) per pair. Hence
\[
\mathbb E_U[Z\mid R_i=r]=\frac{(i-(n+1)/2)(n+1-2r)}{n-1}.
\]
The elementary harmonic identities \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_k=nH_{n-1}-(n-1)\) and \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}kH_k=\frac{n(n-1)}2H_{n-1}-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}4\) yield
\[
\mathbb E_U[(n+1-2R_i)S_i]=\frac{n-1}{2},\qquad
b_i=\frac12\left(i-\frac{n+1}{2}\right).
\]
Thus \(\|b\|^2=n(n^2-1)/48\) and
\[
b^\top G^{+}b=\frac{n(n^2-1)(n-1)}{48(n-H_n)}.
\]

**Step 4: inversion variance and strictness.** Counting covariances of inversion indicators for pairs sharing a label gives the classical uniform-permutation identity
\[
\operatorname{Var}_U(I)=\frac{n(n-1)(2n+5)}{72}.
\]
Substitution into Step 1 proves the coefficient and parameter expansion. For \(n=2\) the two families coincide after choosing an appropriate log-rate difference. For \(n\ge3\), if the quadratic residual vanished then the inversion score would be a linear combination \(\sum_i a_i S_i\) on every permutation. Swapping labels \(u<v\) occupying consecutive positions \(r,r+1\) changes \(I\) by exactly one, but changes \(\sum_i a_iS_i\) by \((a_u-a_v)/(n-r)\) up to a fixed sign. Since the same two labels can be placed at either of two distinct consecutive ranks when \(n\ge3\), no fixed coefficients can make these changes both equal one. Therefore the residual, and hence \(C_n\), is strictly positive. Finally \(H_n=O(\log n)\) gives \(C_n\sim n^3/288\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing this result. The checker uses only the Python standard library and independently enumerates permutations for \(2\le n\le6\), confirming score means, the covariance eigenvalue, cross-covariances, inversion variance, and the formula for \(C_n\) in rational arithmetic. It does not establish the infinite-\(n\) or infinitesimal-\(\varepsilon\) assertions, which are proven above. Direct numerical KL minimization near the uniform point was separately compared against the predicted curvature at \(n=3,4,5\); those finite checks are supplementary, not the analytical proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The Kendall Mallows and Plackett–Luce ranking families are classical. A 2018 comparative study by El Mesaoudi-Paul, Hüllermeier and Busa-Fekete uses KL divergence empirically when comparing ranking models, without the all-size analytic local projection curvature derived here. Turvey's October 2026 work asks which permutation laws arise by ranking independent random variables; its publicly accessible abstract reports an obstruction for Mallows laws at four or more items. Since Plackett–Luce ranks independent exponential clocks, that obstruction supplies qualitative context but neither determines the best KL approximation nor the first nontrivial \(n=3\) coefficient. The present result calculates exactly the Fisher-orthogonal curvature of this particular pair of model families, without claiming the information-projection method itself is new.

## Limitations

This is a local, one-directional KL approximation near \(\varepsilon=0\). No uniform-in-\(n\) remainder bound, large-deviation estimate, finite-separation global KL minimizer, or claim about all independent-variable ranking laws is made. The motivating 2026 preprint's full text could not be inspected through the available public retrieval channel; its abstract establishes the qualitative comparison but leaves some residual prior-coverage uncertainty. Older literature on statistical ranking-model projection may contain an equivalent coefficient, and independent verification remains outstanding.
