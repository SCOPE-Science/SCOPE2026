# Exact log-concavity phase transition for half-discount Pitman–Yor block counts

## Finding
Let \(K_n\) be the number of distinct blocks among \(n\) observations from the two-parameter Ewens–Pitman sampling model with discount \(\alpha=1/2\) and strength \(\theta>-1/2\). For every integer \(n\ge3\), its probability mass function \(p_{n,k}=\Pr(K_n=k)\), on \(1\le k\le n\), is ordinarily log-concave if and only if
\[
\boxed{\theta\ge-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}.}
\]
Equivalently, all inequalities \(p_{n,k}^2\ge p_{n,k-1}p_{n,k+1}\), \(2\le k\le n-1\), hold exactly on this strength interval. Above the boundary all are strict. At the boundary equality occurs at \(k=2\) and nowhere else. For \(n\in\{1,2\}\), log-concavity is automatic because there are no interior triples.

For each fixed negative \(-1/2<\theta<0\), the admissible sample sizes \(n\ge3\) are exactly those with \(n\le 1-1/(2\theta)\). Thus a fixed negative strength causes a sharp, finite loss of log-concavity as the sample size grows. Every nonnegative strength gives log-concavity for all \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope
The exchangeable partition is generated by the usual Chinese-restaurant rule: after \(m\) customers occupying \(k\) blocks, the next customer creates a block with conditional probability \((\theta+k/2)/(\theta+m)\) and joins an existing block otherwise. The assumption \(\theta>-1/2\) makes these probabilities admissible and ensures that each \(p_{n,k}\) in the stated support is strictly positive. Ordinary log-concavity concerns the block-count probability masses, not log-concavity in a model parameter and not real-rootedness of the probability-generating polynomial.

## Proof
**1. Block-count coefficients.** Define \(S_{n,k}\) by \(S_{1,1}=1\), zero outside \(1\le k\le n\), and
\[
S_{n+1,k}=(n-k/2)S_{n,k}+S_{n,k-1}.
\]
The Chinese-restaurant rule, or the exchangeable partition probability formula, gives
\[
p_{n,k}=\frac{S_{n,k}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(\theta+i/2)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(\theta+j)}.
\]
An empty product is one. The exponential generating function for fixed \(k\) is
\[
\sum_{n\ge k}\frac{S_{n,k}}{n!}z^n=\frac{2^k}{k!}(1-\sqrt{1-z})^k.
\]
For clarity, this follows from the exponential formula for set partitions with block weight \((1/2)_{m-1}\); the block series is \(2(1-\sqrt{1-z})\).

Put \(u=1-\sqrt{1-z}\), so \(z=u(2-u)\). Lagrange inversion gives
\[
[z^n]u^k=\frac{k}{n}\,2^{k-2n}\binom{2n-k-1}{n-k},
\]
which proves the closed form
\[
S_{n,k}=\frac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)!}\,2^{2k-2n}\binom{2n-k-1}{n-k}.
\]
These identities hold for every integer \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le n\), not merely on a checked range.

**2. Neighboring-ratio curvature.** Because all masses are positive,
\[
R_{n,k}:=\frac{p_{n,k+1}}{p_{n,k}}=\frac{2(n-k)(k+2\theta)}{k(2n-k-1)},\qquad 1\le k<n.
\]
For \(2\le k\le n-1\), direct expansion and cancellation yield
\[
R_{n,k-1}-R_{n,k}=\frac{2H_{n,k}(\theta)}{k(k-1)(2n-k)(2n-k-1)},
\]
where
\[
H_{n,k}(\theta)=k(k-1)(n-1)+2\theta\bigl(k^2-2kn-k+2n^2\bigr).
\]
The coefficient of \(2\theta\) is strictly positive, since
\[
k^2-2kn-k+2n^2=(n-k)(2n-k)+k(n-1)>0.
\]
Hence each log-concavity inequality is monotone in \(\theta\). At \(\theta_*=-1/[2(n-1)]\), one obtains the exact factorization
\[
H_{n,k}(\theta_*)=\frac{n(k-2)(kn-2k+n)}{n-1}\ge0.
\]
The final factor is \(k(n-2)+n>0\) for all stated indices, so equality holds precisely when \(k=2\). It follows that every \(\theta\ge\theta_*\) gives log-concavity, with the stated strictness.

**3. Sharp necessity.** At the first interior index,
\[
H_{n,2}(\theta)=2(n-1)\bigl(1+2(n-1)\theta\bigr).
\]
For \(\theta<\theta_*\), this is negative, making \(R_{n,1}<R_{n,2}\), equivalently \(p_{n,2}^2<p_{n,1}p_{n,3}\). The condition is therefore necessary. The sample-size formulation is elementary rearrangement of the same bound for \(\theta<0\). This completes the classification.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library program `check.py` constructs the probability masses in two independent ways: by the block-creation Markov recurrence and by the closed-form coefficients. It performs exact rational checks of their normalization, ratio formula, curvature polynomial, and threshold over all \(1\le n\le42\), including boundary and nearby strengths. Its output reports 19,764 checks. This finite exercise corroborates identities; the preceding algebra proves the statement for all admissible \(n\) and \(\theta\).

For a concrete failure, \(n=4\), \(\theta=-1/4\) gives
\[
(p_{4,1},p_{4,2},p_{4,3},p_{4,4})=(40,20,12,5)/77,
\]
and \(p_{4,2}^2-p_{4,1}p_{4,3}=-80/5929<0\). At the sharp boundary \(\theta=-1/6\) for \(n=4\), the masses are \((81,54,36,16)/187\), with equality at \(k=2\).

## Relationship to prior work
The Ewens–Pitman sampling formula and its block-count asymptotics are classical; Dolera and Favaro [1] provide a compound-Poisson perspective and a 2021 public source for this family. Han and Seo [2, Theorem 4.2] prove ordinary log-concavity of the signless Bessel number coefficients \(a(n,k)\). The exact coefficient identity above shows \(S_{n,k}=2^{-(n-k)}a(n,k)\), so that theorem already covers log-concavity of the unweighted \(S_{n,k}\) sequence. It does not cover the *weighted probability sequence*: the additional block-creation factor \(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(\theta+i/2)\) changes its curvature, and for the admissible negative strength in the displayed example it destroys log-concavity. The threshold and its necessity therefore do not follow from the established coefficient theorem alone. Zhao [3] studies log-concavity in other generalized Stirling arrays, without giving this weighted partition-count phase boundary. Cereda, Corradi, and Viscardi [4] use the same two-parameter partition sampling formula for statistical inference rather than deriving this shape classification.

## Limitations
The claim is only for the discount \(\alpha=1/2\), all strengths \(\theta>-1/2\), and ordinary log-concavity of the finite sample block-count law. It does not classify other discounts, stronger total-positivity properties, or multimodality. No independent audit has been performed. An unpublished or unindexed equivalent statement cannot be ruled out by literature searches alone.
