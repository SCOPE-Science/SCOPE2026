# Review of the exact logarithmic blow-up clock

## Correctness

PASS. The positive energy has derivative \(-\eta x^n v^2\); the exact projective equation is \(\dot z=-x^n[(n+1)z^2+\eta z+1]\). The positive-discriminant obstruction and the absence of invariant directions give the sharp winding transition. Normalizing the projective quadrature at \(z=+\infty\) establishes the half-return velocity ratio; an integrable angular quadrature gives the actual physical flight duration. Quasi-homogeneous scaling plus summable reverse-time flights establishes both finite escape and the exact logarithmic profile period. The n=2, unit-damping period and multiplier agree with published numerical values to more than ten digits. Finite numerical checks are corroboration, not the basis for the universal proof.

## Originality

PASS under accessible evidence, with material residual risk. The 2026 Matsue paper supplies the general profile correspondence and its numerical example; the 2018 Matsue paper supplies qualitative oscillatory blow-up and an integral Poincaré return formula at unit damping. Neither inspected document states the all-parameter closed logarithmic-period law and its sharp singular limit. The earlier source already implies a return integral at its fixed parameter, which is not claimed separately. The Dumortier–Herssens near-infinity article (1999) and earlier classifications could contain a stronger implicit quantitative comparison; complete full-text inspection was unavailable. No independent priority certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The exact clock replaces a numerical profile period of a recently studied model by a closed form for all even degrees and a physically natural variable damping coefficient. It also identifies the precise loss of oscillation and the essential-singularity growth of consecutive physical flight-time ratios. The output is a motivated structural classification of a standard quasi-homogeneous oscillator, not a one-off numerical invariant.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem is for \(n\in\{2,4,6,\ldots\}\), \(0<\eta<2\sqrt{n+1}\) on the oscillatory side, and exactly homogeneous polynomial dynamics. The critical equality is classified only by the absence of winding; no critical asymptotic relaxation rate or robustness under perturbations is proved. Qualitative near-infinity classifications and the general profile correspondence remain credited to prior literature.
