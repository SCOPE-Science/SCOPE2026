In the balanced-zealot two-option cross-inhibition model, an exact quartic root determines when the stable consensus favoring the lower-quality option disappears: below the threshold there are two attractors and one saddle, and above it only the higher-quality attractor remains. The threshold strictly decreases with quality ratio \(q>1\), approaching \(1/5\) at equal qualities and decaying as \(1/(4q^2)\) for large quality asymmetry.
