# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** In the unrestricted de Bruijn graph, a source vertex with first equal pair at zero-based index \(t\) cannot reach a required-edge surplus in fewer than \(t\) shifts. Integral flow decomposition gives a lower bound obtained by summing those costs. The map from each deficit's first equal pair to a surplus's last equal pair is explicitly invertible and realizes every bound simultaneously via paths that themselves avoid `000` and `111`; strong connectivity gives an Eulerian host. Run-composition enumeration proves the Lucas expression. The complete verifier independently checks constructive hosts at orders 3 through 18, flow-matching minima at 3 through 11, and shorter-host nonexistence by brute force at 3 through 5. None of these finite checks is used as the infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS against accessible comparisons, subject to residual risk.** The arXiv works of Moreno–Matamala (0711.1695) and Leitner–Godbole (0808.1309) study forbidden-language Eulerian universal-cycle construction, rather than unrestricted shortest cyclic covering with repeated required windows. The complete accessible 2011 Compeau–Pevzner–Tesler article gives general multigraph/Eulerian tools but not the two-forbidden-triple Lucas optimum. A 2022 run-length-limited de Bruijn paper studies maximal-length sequences with distinct windows, a different quantifier and optimization objective. Closest individual-forbidden-triple results have different required languages; deleting their forbidden edges does not determine the new optimum. Abstract-only source access and potential unindexed equivalent formulations remain risks.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The requirement to cover every linearly allowed window, including words whose periodic repetition violates the run bound, is natural in constrained periodic testing and is not solved by ordinary universal-cycle existence. The precise optimum holds at all orders, with a constructive forbidden-free optimum despite allowing arbitrary cyclic hosts. The structural first/last-double-pair matching is a nontrivial explanation of why the lower bound is globally attainable, and the relative overhead converges to \((3-\sqrt5)/2\). It does not purport to classify all optimal hosts.
