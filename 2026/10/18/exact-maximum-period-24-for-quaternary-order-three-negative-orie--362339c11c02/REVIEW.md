# Scientific review — exact quaternary negative-orientable order-three period

**Correctness: PASS.** Negative reversal has precisely eight fixed triples and \(28\) nonfixed pair classes. The quotient of ordered-pair vertices has six odd-degree vertices, forcing at least three omitted classes in any balanced cyclic edge set. Its only two possible three-edge omission matchings violate an orientation-independent signed divergence identity, with respective values \(-2\) and \(+2\). Therefore the upper bound is \(24\). A complete cyclic-window check verifies the explicit \(24\)-symbol word. The complete argument, definitions, table, and reproducible standalone checker appear in the result package; a finite computer check is used only to enumerate a small *exhaustively specified* table, not to extrapolate an infinite statement.

The former explicitly records a remaining gap for order exceeding two. A newer paper, arXiv:2603.25286, solves a different involution problem (negation without reversal) and is not covering. The possibility of previously unindexed equivalent constructions and bounds remains a residual originality risk.

**Value: PASS.** An exact extremum for a natural negative-reversal constraint fills an explicit numerical gap in contemporary position-location sequence research. The improvement is not assessed merely by its one-unit upper-bound change: both an improved feasible sequence and a new impossibility mechanism are supplied, determining the precise extremal value.
