# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The greedy embedding criterion proves occurrence of a nonempty word in a permutation power exactly when the weak-descent count is at most one less than the power. Endpoint deletion forces the absent count to equal the power; interior deletion changes the descent count by exactly the stated triple statistic. This gives a necessary and sufficient characterization with all boundary cases. A nine-state weighted transition identity yields the rational enumerator; its nine polynomial vector identities and numerator identity are exact and checked with integer polynomial arithmetic in `check.py`. Independent definition-level enumeration agrees for powers one through three, and all ternary symbol orders at powers one and two. The finite enumeration is supporting evidence rather than a proof of the infinite case.

## Originality — PASS within examined sources
The closest direct precedent, Kosche et al. (*Absent Subsequences in Words*, arXiv:2108.13968, Section 3, Proposition 3.6), already studies powers of a repeated ordered permutation and proves exact *shortest*-absent-subsequence counts plus an exponential family of minimal absent subsequences. Those known contributions are credited. A separate exact result for the binary alternating-power minimal-absent-subsequence family also exists and covers the two-letter case; this work claims originality only for the ternary all-MAS formula and its structural specialization, not for that binary case. It does not supply the full ternary MAS rational generating function or the stated two-step comparison enumeration in the inspected paper. Manea et al. (arXiv:2504.21471) provide general optimal MAS enumeration algorithms for individual input strings, not this infinite-family generating function. Li et al. (arXiv:2601.06493) enumerate deletion-ball sizes of periodic words, but deletion-ball sizes alone do not determine membership-minimal absent words. Semantic research-record searches for minimal absent subsequences, ternary periodic permutations, rational generating functions, and the first counts did not expose a directly covering result. Unindexed equivalent work remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
Repeated permutations are the extremal periodic inputs in the deletion-subsequence literature and are studied explicitly for absent subsequences. The complete ternary MAS count resolves the smallest nonbinary periodic case beyond the previously known shortest-absent counts and exponential qualitative bounds. The exact local test, full length-refined matrix formula and closed recurrence enable comparisons and reproducible benchmarks without running an output-sized enumerator for each repetition count. This is more than a table recomputation or arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations
The rational scalar formula is ternary; no general scalar simplification is asserted. Some prior generic algorithms enumerate MAS for individual words; no algorithmic complexity improvement is claimed. The evidence is a self-review only, and a separately performed independent audit would remain necessary.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
