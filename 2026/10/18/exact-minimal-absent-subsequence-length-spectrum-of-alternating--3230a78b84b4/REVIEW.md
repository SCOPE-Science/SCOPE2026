# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The greedy embedding lemma \(P(u)=m+a+E(u)\) follows from the exact one-step/two-step movements on an alternating binary word. All single-letter deletions are classified, so minimal absence is equivalent to the two endpoint cases and the stated run-length restrictions; there are no omitted deletion positions. The four disjoint run-composition cases and two Pascal reductions establish the theorem for all positive integer parameters. The generating function follows from the binomial series. Direct independent finite subsequence enumeration agrees for all \(k\) from 1 through 12; those tests are corroboration rather than an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Kosche–Koß–Manea–Siemer, arXiv:2108.13968, Section 3, introduce minimal absent scattered subsequences and study repeated-permutation words, but their polynomial-vs-exponential proof explicitly assumes an even alphabet with at least four letters. It does not give the exact binary length-by-length distribution. Adamson et al., arXiv:2407.18599, Theorem 2, count arbitrary present/absent subsequences of fixed length rather than the subsequence-minimal absent subset; their theorem does not yield this binomial boundary. Manea–Ringleb–Siemer–Winkler, arXiv:2504.21471, give general enumeration/longest-MAS algorithms but no exact family spectrum in the inspected portions. Searches for exact parameters, binomial profile, sequence and repeated binary permutation produced no equivalent published claim. Related unpublished results and an unindexed equivalent remain possible; neither absent-search results nor this review establish priority conclusively.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The repeated ascending binary permutation is a standard shortest \(k\)-universal word and a natural counterpart of the primary paper's canonical repeated-permutation example. The result gives the complete all-length minimal obstruction profile, sharp longest obstruction and a provable binary exponential-versus-linear separation. This exact infinite-family classification is not merely a restatement of an SAS count or an enumeration algorithm.

**Scope and risks.** The result does not extend to other alphabets, general binary words, or contiguous absent factors. The proof is self-contained; the independent audit is still outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
