# Scientific review — clique-prism projection constants

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for every integer \(m\ge3\), real unweighted \(\ell_1\) edge space, and infima over all projections. The proof checks the graph-incidence decomposition, the explicit projector and intertwiner, the one-dimensional equivariant Hom spaces, the two signed column-orbit formulas, and global sharpness via supporting affine minorants. The sign ranges at both crossings were checked: \(-2<t_m<m\) and \(0<s_m<2\). A standard-library rational program corroborated 868 exact assertions, but the general statement rests on the full algebraic proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic limits

The closest primary treatment is Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii, arXiv:2305.12582 (2023), particularly Sections 2.4–2.7 on tori and homogeneous Hamming graphs. The current graphs \(K_m\square K_2\) have unequal clique factors and two edge orbits. The paper's projection methodology does not itself state or imply the two exact formulas without additional symmetry decomposition and minimization. Their arXiv:1807.03814 (2018) addresses finite metric free-space structure and cycle projections in other graph families. Dedicated mathematical statement/parameter/alias searches identified no covering published record. A differently worded or unindexed source could still cover the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS

These are exact natural relative projection constants for a whole infinite family of finite-dimensional Banach spaces, not an isolated parameter or an arbitrary table slice. The proof identifies the sharp optimum in two different projection problems and reveals a nontrivial orthogonal-optimality transition at \(m=4\), including explicit optimal operators. The exact constants give benchmarks for group-averaged Banach-space projections and graph transportation quotients.

## Limitations

Results are confined to real, unweighted \(\ell_1\) edge geometry of the stated family. Unindexed bibliographic equivalents remain possible; no independent audit has been conducted. Finite exact computations do not replace the infinite-family proof.
