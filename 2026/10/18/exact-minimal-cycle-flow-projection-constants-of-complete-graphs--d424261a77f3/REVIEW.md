# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The complete-graph incidence identity \(BB^{\mathsf T}=nI-J\) proves the proposed operator is a cycle projection. A direct exact column calculation gives norm \(3-6/n\). The nontrivial optimality argument averages every projection over signed vertex permutations and classifies the resulting equivariant map through the stabilizer of one oriented edge. The proof handles \(n=3\), all larger finite integers, correct signs, dimension, and real scalars. The packaged exact checker confirms 59,831 rational equalities for sizes 3–12. These finite checks do not replace the full-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii, arXiv:1807.03814v1, Sections 1.1, 1.3, 4.4 and 6, provide the edge-flow quotient and symmetrization machinery; Section 2 specifically notes \(K_n\)'s large cycle space without evaluating the minimum projection norm. Novotný, arXiv:1905.13188v1, studies retractional Schauder bases and cycles, not the complete-graph circulation projection. Kobos, arXiv:1411.6214v1, treats codimension-one hyperplanes in \(\ell_1\) and general Banach spaces, whereas the complete-graph circulation space has codimension \(n-1\). Existing general symmetrization does not already imply this exact constant without the explicit stabilizer and column evaluation. Research-database and literature searches examined graphical cycle projections, signed-edge Hodge projections, and equivalent quotient formulations. Residual risk: a classical complete-graph Hodge projection calculation may be published under a different name.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The explicit formula gives a full natural family of intrinsically defined relative Banach-space projection constants, with graph cycle dimension \(\binom{n-1}{2}\), a sharp transition from norm-one complementability at \(n=3\) to noncontractivity thereafter, and asymptotic constant \(3\). This addresses the quantification of cycle-space complementability highlighted in the graph Lipschitz-free literature; a large-rank sequence cannot be reduced to the known hyperplane formula. No stronger downstream application is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature
Only complete unweighted graphs, real signed edge flows and the \(\ell_1\) edge norm are covered. Other norm-minimizing projections need not be unique. The relevant older sources and their exact distinct statements are identified above; indexing completeness is not claimed.
