## Correctness — PASS
The pair-weight lower bound is sharp and forces each minimum labeling to use a maximum independent set of original cycle vertices as its zero-core set. The five exact weight-two pair types, the independent zero-leaf constraints, and the color-deficiency rule together give a bijective classification without omitted cases. Distinct maximum independent sets cannot generate the same function. The two alternating cases for even cycles and the \(n\) independent sets for odd cycles lead to the cyclic trace and the path transfer product respectively; the two-state symmetric reduction proves the closed formulas. An independently programmed all-labeling enumeration for \(n=3,4,5,6\) agrees with the theorem. Finitary calculations corroborate but are not the infinite proof.

A complete priority guarantee is not possible; an unindexed table or independent same-formula calculation is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
When the optimal weight is already known, its number of realizations measures the flexibility of minimum guard assignments, a natural combinatorial object not recovered from the scalar optimum. For a ubiquitous cycle-with-private-leaves family, the result gives *every* optimum, a nontrivial three-state transfer construction, and two qualitatively different even/odd counting regimes (including a second-order recurrence and a closed algebraic expression). This exact finite-choice structure can be reused to study optimal-label sampling and multiplicity under graph modifications. The claim does not require a conjecture that these numerical counts alone imply a downstream theorem.
