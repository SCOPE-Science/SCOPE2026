# Exact negative skyline–hypervolume covariance in a random square

## Finding

Let \(n\geq 1\) independent points be uniformly distributed on \([0,1]^2\), and let \(M_n\) be their number of coordinatewise Pareto maxima (skyline points). Let \(A_n\) be the area of the union of their origin-anchored rectangles, \(\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}([0,X_j]\times[0,Y_j])\). Thus \(A_n\) is the dominated hypervolume with reference point the origin. Define \(H_m=\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/j\) and \(H_m^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/j^2\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M_n,A_n)
=-\frac{H_{n+1}-H_{n+1}^{(2)}-1+H_{n+1}/(n+1)}{n+1}.
\]

This covariance is zero at \(n=1\) and strictly negative for every \(n\geq2\). Moreover,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(M_n,A_n)\sim-\frac{\log n}{n},\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\operatorname{Cov}(M_n,A_n)}{\mathbb E[1-A_n]}=1,
\quad\mathbb E[1-A_n]=\frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1}.
\]

There is also an exact position-resolved statement. Sort the points in decreasing order of their first coordinate and let \(I_i\) indicate that the second coordinate at position \(i\) is a new strict upper record. Then \(M_n=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i\), and for each \(1\leq i\leq n\),

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,A_n)=-\frac{i-H_i}{(n+1)i(i+1)}.
\]

The record at position one has covariance zero; every later record indicator has strictly negative covariance with the dominated area.

## Assumptions and scope

The sample size \(n\) is a positive integer. Points are independently sampled from the continuous uniform distribution on the closed unit square; all ties have probability zero. Maxima mean points with no other sample point at least as large in both coordinates. The definition of \(A_n\) includes the entire union of rectangles, not merely boxes incident to maxima. Results concern exact moments, not conditional independence or full stochastic ordering.

The formulas also apply to independent draws from any two-dimensional product distribution with continuous marginal distribution functions, provided the hypervolume statistic is interpreted as the product-population probability content of the coordinatewise lower set generated by the sample. Applying the two marginal cumulative distribution functions reduces this case to the uniform square almost surely. For general marginals, the formulas do not automatically describe ordinary unweighted Euclidean area in the original coordinates.

## Proof

**1. A record and spacing representation.** Order the first coordinates decreasingly as \(U_1>\cdots>U_n\), put \(U_{n+1}=0\), and let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\) be the correspondingly reordered second coordinates. They remain independent uniform variables and are independent of the first-coordinate order statistics. Put \(S_k=U_k-U_{k+1}\), \(Z_k=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}Y_j\), and \(I_i=\mathbf1\{Y_i>\max_{j<i}Y_j\}\), with \(I_1=1\). The vertical cross-section of the dominated union over horizontal width \(S_k\) has height \(Z_k\), hence

\[
A_n=\sum_{k=1}^nS_kZ_k,
\qquad \mathbb E[S_k]=\frac1{n+1},
\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(I_i,A_n)=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^n\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,Z_k).
\]

The ordering equivalence gives \(M_n=\sum_{i=1}^nI_i\). All \(n+1\) spacings, including the unused gap above \(U_1\), have the symmetric Dirichlet law; only their stated mean and independence from the second coordinates are needed.

**2. A local mixed-record identity.** For \(k\geq i\), \(I_i\) depends only on the relative ranks of the first \(k\) second coordinates, which are independent of their maximum \(Z_k\). Thus \(\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,Z_k)=0\). For \(k<i\), the record event has probability \(\mathbb P(I_i=1)=1/i\). Given that event, \(Y_i\) is distributed as the maximum of \(i\) independent uniforms and has mean \(i/(i+1)\). Conditional on its value \(y\), the preceding \(i-1\) coordinates are independent uniform variables on \((0,y)\). The maximum of the first \(k\) has conditional mean \(ky/(k+1)\), so

\[
\mathbb E[I_iZ_k]=\frac{k}{(i+1)(k+1)},\quad
\mathbb E[I_i]=\frac1i,\quad
\mathbb E[Z_k]=\frac{k}{k+1},\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,Z_k)=-\frac{k}{i(i+1)(k+1)}.
\]

Since \(\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}k/(k+1)=i-H_i\), the position-resolved covariance follows, including \(i=1\).

**3. Exact total covariance and its sign.** Summing the position identities gives

\[
-\operatorname{Cov}(M_n,A_n)
=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{i=2}^n\frac{i-H_i}{i(i+1)}
=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{k+1}\left(\frac1{k+1}-\frac1{n+1}\right).
\]

For \(n\geq2\), every term in the latter sum is strictly positive. Expanding \(k/(k+1)=1-1/(k+1)\) and collecting harmonic sums yields exactly

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{k+1}\left(\frac1{k+1}-\frac1{n+1}\right)
=H_{n+1}-H_{n+1}^{(2)}-1+\frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1}.
\]

For \(n=1\) the sum is empty, consistent with \(M_1=1\) deterministically.

**4. Means and limits.** Since \(\mathbb E[Z_k]=k/(k+1)\), the spacing representation gives

\[
\mathbb E[A_n]=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{k}{k+1}
=1-\frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1}.
\]

The standard harmonic estimates \(H_m=\log m+\gamma+O(1/m)\) and \(H_m^{(2)}=\pi^2/6+O(1/m)\) then prove \(\operatorname{Cov}(M_n,A_n)\sim-(\log n)/n\) and the stated ratio limit. The probability-integral transform proves the product-population-content extension under continuous independent marginals.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone file `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It exhaustively enumerates all second-coordinate rank permutations through nine points and computes exact rational mixed expectations after integrating over the first-coordinate spacings and second-coordinate order statistics. It independently matches the local and total covariance formulas and the dominated-area mean. Further exact rational identities were checked through \(n=150\), and six explicitly bounded large-sample numerical scaling comparisons were made. Its successful run reported 373 exact rational finite checks and six numerical comparisons. These computations corroborate the analytic argument; finite enumeration does not establish the unrestricted theorem.

Run with `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing this file.

## Relationship to prior work

The distribution of the number of Pareto maxima in random planar samples, including the uniform square, is classical. Bai, Hwang, Liang, and Tsai (2001) establish limit theorems for this count, including planar sampling models. Hwang, Tsai, and Chen (2011) study count thresholds for higher-dimensional \(k\)-dominant skylines. Barbour and Xia (2001) study the number of two-dimensional maxima. These are related but distinguishable from the **joint dependence on dominated hypervolume of the same point sample**.

The distinct statement established here is the finite-sample positionwise negative covariance and its exact harmonic sum, including its comparison to the expected uncovered square area. The cited literature supplies background on marginal maximal-point counts; no claim is made that standard record or spacing facts themselves are novel.

## Limitations

This is a two-dimensional iid product-marginal result. It does not prove an analogous sign for dependent coordinates, higher-dimensional ordinary volume, or conditional stochastic ordering. The product-population-content interpretation, rather than raw area, is necessary for the distribution-free extension. Specialized earlier joint record-process formulas or uninspected portions of older literature may imply the stated mixed moments; independent historical-priority assessment remains necessary. The correctness evidence consists of the analytic proof and supporting finite checks, not an independent external validation.
