# Exact one-generator size spectrum for the modal complex algebra of the natural-number chain

## Finding
Let \(W=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\), equipped with reflexive order \(\le\), and let \(\mathfrak A=\mathcal P(W)\) carry its ordinary Boolean operations and
\[
\Diamond U=\{i\in W:\text{some }j\ge i\text{ belongs to }U\}.
\]
For \(X\subseteq W\), write \(\langle X\rangle\) for the Boolean-modal subalgebra generated by \(X\). Define the switching set
\[
S(X)=\{i\in W:\mathbf1_X(i)\ne\mathbf1_X(i+1)\},\qquad s(X)=|S(X)|.
\]
The exact classification, with no regularity or computability assumption on \(X\), is
\[
|\langle X\rangle|=\begin{cases}2^{s(X)+1},&s(X)<\infty,\\4,&s(X)=\infty.\end{cases}
\]
When \(s(X)<\infty\), the atoms of \(\langle X\rangle\), viewed as a Boolean algebra, are exactly the \(s(X)+1\) nonempty maximal consecutive intervals on which \(\mathbf1_X\) is constant. Therefore every power of two \(2^m\), \(m\ge1\), is realized as the cardinality of a one-generated subalgebra of this single locally finite modal algebra. By contrast every infinitely switching generator gives the four-element algebra.

## Assumptions and scope
The modal accessibility relation is specifically \(iRj\iff i\le j\), including loops. All subsets of \(W\), including noncomputable sets, are admitted as generators. The language has Boolean complement, union, intersection, the two Boolean constants, and the unary existential modal operator \(\Diamond\); other Boolean/modal operations are term-definable. A switching point is an index \(i\) at which membership changes between consecutive integers \(i\) and \(i+1\). An empty switching set includes \(X=\varnothing\) and \(X=W\).

## Proof
**Infinite switching.** If \(S(X)\) is infinite, both \(X\) and \(W\setminus X\) are unbounded in \(W\): if either were bounded, membership would be eventually constant. Hence
\[
\Diamond X=\Diamond(W\setminus X)=W.
\]
The four Boolean sets \(\varnothing,W,X,W\setminus X\) form a subalgebra under \(\Diamond\), because \(\Diamond\varnothing=\varnothing\), \(\Diamond W=W\), and the preceding equalities hold. They are all distinct (both colors occur), and the Boolean algebra generated by \(X\) contains all four. The generated modal algebra therefore has size four.

**Finitely many switches: upper bound.** Enumerate the switching set as \(t_1<\cdots<t_s\) when \(s>0\). Partition \(W\) into its \(s+1\) maximal constant runs:
\[
R_1=[0,t_1],\quad R_j=[t_{j-1}+1,t_j]\ (2\le j\le s),\quad R_{s+1}=[t_s+1,\infty).
\]
For \(s=0\), the sole run is \(R_1=W\). Let \(\mathcal B\) be all unions of these runs. It contains \(X\) and is Boolean closed. If a union is unbounded, it includes \(R_{s+1}\), and its diamond is \(W\). If it is nonempty and bounded, its rightmost point is the right endpoint of some finite run \(R_j\), and its diamond is the prefix \(P_j=R_1\cup\cdots\cup R_j\), also a union of runs. Its empty union has empty diamond. Thus \(\mathcal B\) is a modal subalgebra and \(\langle X\rangle\subseteq\mathcal B\). In particular \(|\langle X\rangle|\le 2^{s+1}\).

**Finitely many switches: lower bound.** For \(s=0\), the generator is \(\varnothing\) or \(W\), and the generated algebra has precisely two elements. Suppose \(s\ge1\). Let \(T\) be the one of \(X\), \(W\setminus X\) which *does not contain* the unbounded last run \(R_{s+1}\). Then \(T\) is nonempty and bounded, its rightmost included run is \(R_s\), and
\[
P_s=\Diamond T\in\langle X\rangle.
\]
Induct downward. Given \(P_j\in\langle X\rangle\) for \(2\le j\le s\), select \(C_j\in\{X,W\setminus X\}\) opposite in color to the constant color of \(R_j\), and put \(V_j=P_j\cap C_j\). Because consecutive runs alternate, \(V_j\) contains all of \(R_{j-1}\), contains none of \(R_j\), and has no points greater than \(t_{j-1}\). Consequently its largest point is exactly \(t_{j-1}\), whence
\[
P_{j-1}=\Diamond V_j\in\langle X\rangle.
\]
The prefix sets \(P_1,\ldots,P_s\), together with \(P_0=\varnothing\) and \(P_{s+1}=W\), generate every run by \(R_j=P_j\setminus P_{j-1}\). Therefore \(\mathcal B\subseteq\langle X\rangle\). Equality follows and \(|\langle X\rangle|=2^{s+1}\).

**Full size spectrum.** For each \(m\ge1\), choose any ultimately constant binary sequence with exactly \(m-1\) changes (for example, membership alternates on the first \(m\) singleton positions and then remains constant). The preceding construction yields exactly \(2^m\) elements; no other finite cardinalities occur because all generated algebras are finite Boolean algebras.

## Verification
A standalone executable checker `checker.py` independently builds, for each bit pattern on a finite prefix followed by a constant tail, the full modal closure by complement, meet, and \(\Diamond\). The tail is represented by a final stable state, so the checker examines actual ultimately constant subsets of \(W\), not merely arbitrary finite-chain approximations. It compares the resulting cardinality and Boolean atom count with the theorem for every prefix pattern of length at most eight. The proof above covers every infinite or finite switching set, independently of these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Agnew (2026), Theorem 3.16, classifies local finiteness for transitive complex modal algebras. It explicitly treats the standard natural-number order as locally finite, but its result does not specify the exact one-generator cardinality or the infinite-switching collapse. Shapirovsky (2019), Example 1 and Theorem 1, proves local finiteness of the complex modal algebra of \((\omega,\le)\) and its finite direct powers through tuned refinements; the inspected manuscript likewise does not give this cardinality formula or identify its atoms. The classification here is a quantitative refinement for the one-dimensional reflexive chain, not a new proof of either general local-finiteness theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem is confined to the full Boolean complex algebra of \((\mathbb N,\le)\), and counts one-generated subalgebras only; it does not classify several generators, strict successor accessibility, reverse order, or products of chains. The mathematical argument is complete, but priority may be affected by older, differently phrased work on tuned partitions or finite modal algebras. Finite code tests do not certify an arbitrary infinite subset.
