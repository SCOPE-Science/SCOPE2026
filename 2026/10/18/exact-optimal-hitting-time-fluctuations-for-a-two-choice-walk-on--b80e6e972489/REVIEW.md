# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For each cycle the mean-optimal distance rule follows from the Bellman equation and strict increase of the explicit expected hitting-time function; at the antipode the correct parity-dependent reflecting/holding condition applies. First- and second-moment recurrences yield the stated expressions. The exact probability-generating function and its uniform analytic remainder prove Gaussian convergence and the uniform variance bound. The checker independently verifies small finite chains using rational arithmetic, checks proposal-level Bellman optimality and tests transforms. No finite experiment is substituted for the uniform-limit proof.

**Originality — PASS (inspection-qualified).** The original two-choice model and order-sharp torus hitting-time bounds are due to Georgakopoulos, Haslegrave, Sauerwald and Sylvester, arXiv:1911.05170, whose full openly accessible version was checked for cycle results, exact formulas and limiting laws. It does not state the present explicit moments, cycle parity corrections or Gaussian law. The related but distinct free-choice-probability model in Haslegrave, Sauerwald and Sylvester, arXiv:2006.02475, does not imply these statements. The first paper already uses a three-quarters directional preference elsewhere; that elementary bias is not claimed new. A possible equivalent treatment in classical biased birth-death hitting-time literature remains a priority risk. Searches and source comparisons therefore support but do not conclusively certify originality.

**Value — PASS.** The prescribed policy is naturally mean-optimal for a stochastic control problem, and the result supplies quantitative hitting-time uncertainty and a uniform central limit theorem beyond existing order-of-magnitude hitting bounds. The even/odd antipode corrections isolate a genuine geometric boundary effect; the variance coefficient six gives a distributional prediction for long target distances.

**Scientific limitations.** Independent audit, complete historical priority survey and extensions to other control objectives have not been carried out.
