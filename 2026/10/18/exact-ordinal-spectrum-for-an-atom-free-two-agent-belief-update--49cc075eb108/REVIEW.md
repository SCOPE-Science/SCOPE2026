# Scientific review: exact ordinal spectrum for an atom-free two-agent belief update

## Correctness — PASS

The announced result uses exactly the target-restricting believed-announcement update. For a fixed infinite carrier bound, an ordinal longer than the successor cardinal cannot consist entirely of strict deletions because each strict stage must remove a distinct labelled arrow. The constructive converse is an explicit well-founded decreasing-sequence tree. The proof verifies disjointness of each agent's source blocks and the \(K45\) axioms, then uses a transfinite induction on the exact surviving target sets. The key identity is that, for every *current* arrow target \(y\), \(\chi(y)\) is true exactly when the stage is below its ordinal rank. This makes the first fixed stage exactly the root rank, rather than merely bounding it. Cardinality of finite decreasing sequences supplies the stated carrier budget. Bisimulation of one-agent atom-free targets and the propositional depth-zero case establish the sharp syntax boundary. The actual finite checker passed heights 0–8, confirming all claimed finite block identities and first fixed stages. The induction, not finite experiments, supports infinite ordinals. ZFC, nonserial \(K45\), and target restriction are essential declared assumptions.

## Originality — PASS, with stated risk

The closest preprint is Yamada, *Eventual and Strong Eventual Notions in Public Announcements* (arXiv:2609.24006v1, 2026). Its Definition 5 is the same arrow-target update and Definition 8 introduces transfinite iteration. Its Proposition 3 gives termination by a cardinal bound, and Lemma 6 restricts one-agent convergence. Most importantly, Lemma 7 already constructs the *same style of alternating-parity rank tree* with rank \(\omega\), using a valuation marker and a more nested formula to prove nonimplications among eventual-self-refutation properties. The geometric block method and the \(\omega\)-example are therefore prior art, not new ingredients. The inspected article does **not** claim the all-ordinal exact spectrum for the one fixed atom-free depth-one formula \(\Diamond_a\top\wedge\Diamond_b\top\), nor the accompanying expression-resource threshold. A finite-state prior result bounds target removals without constructing arbitrary ordinals. Searches for aliases under closure rank, transfinite announcement iteration, fixed-point closure ordinal, and two-agent serial-successor intersection did not find a statement implying this combined result. Search failure alone does not certify originality. An undiscovered equivalent theorem about deflationary modal fixed points or selection of such tree encodings remains a material residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The question is natural in a newly developed transfinite believed-announcement semantics: which transfinite stages are genuinely needed, if announcement complexity is kept fixed? The answer is a complete closure-ordinal spectrum at every infinite cardinal. Its strongest content is that **no proposition letters and only one modal nesting level** suffice for arbitrary closure ordinals with two \(K45\) agents, while no atom-free one-agent or depth-zero formula can have even two strict stages. This separates the source's more complicated parity-labelled construction from the resources actually necessary for arbitrary stabilization ranks. The construction gives exact realizations and a sharp cardinal ceiling rather than examples at selected finite or \(\omega\) stages. Its value does not rely on a downstream theorem or a novel general-purpose ordinal rank principle.

## Literature comparison and limits

Primary source: E. Yamada, arXiv:2609.24006v1 (submitted September 21, 2026), especially Definitions 5 and 8, Proposition 3, Lemmas 6–7, main theorems, and conclusion. The article's Lemma 7 directly anticipates the alternating-block technique, and this attribution is essential. Klein and Rendsvig (2017), arXiv:1709.00359, discusses recurrence of dynamic epistemic updates at a broader level; only the abstract was inspected here and no full-text exclusion is claimed. A prior finite-state research result gives sharp \(|W|\)-stage termination for arbitrary formulas; a prior S5 result concerns world-deleting truthful announcements, not the arrow-only \(K45\) transfinite ordinal problem. Older equivalent formulations and extensions to serial/knowledge frames remain open research risks. No independent audit was commissioned or performed.
