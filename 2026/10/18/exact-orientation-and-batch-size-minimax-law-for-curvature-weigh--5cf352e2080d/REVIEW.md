# Scientific review: orientation-sensitive curvature-weighted Rademacher probes

## Correctness — PASS
The exact three-point curvature of the stated quadratic is \(z^{\mathsf T}H z\ge2\), so the permitted power-one preconditioner does not clip. Both Rademacher projective directions induce rank-one orthogonal projectors in the Hessian metric. Their arithmetic mean has eigenvalues \((1\pm\delta_\theta)/2\). Independence supplies the exact batch second-moment polynomial, whose largest Hessian-metric eigenvalue is the claimed worst-start risk. Averaging its two eigenmode values proves the sharp lower bound \(1/(n+1)\), with equality and uniqueness at the stated rotation and step. The optimum's conditional error identity propagates exactly through independent batches. The axis-aligned limit follows by bounding any improving step and tracking the small eigenvalue. ## Originality — PASS, with stated risks
The September 29, 2026 primary source arXiv:2609.38095v1 already defines the three-point curvature-weighted update and explicitly points out in Appendix A.2 that rotation matters with sign probes. Neither its relevant mathematical sections nor its rotated paraboloid experiments give the present exact Hessian-energy risk spectrum for every angle and batch size, the sharp jointly optimal step and orientation, or the exact at-optimum all-horizon mean-square law. The classical normalized-direction line-search viewpoint makes the eigen-aligned special case familiar and is not claimed as a new general algorithm. Published related records include Random Pursuit (Stich, Müller, and Gärtner, 2013) and stochastic subspace descent (Kozak et al., 2020/2021), which address different random direction laws or general rates; the accessible source materials do not establish the complete claimed orientation characterization. Semantic research-record searches did not reveal a dominating statement. The older line-search sources could not all be inspected at full-text depth, and an exact equivalent in other literature cannot be ruled out.

## Value — PASS
The result solves a natural complete design question left quantitative by a recent numerical method: for fixed anisotropic eigenvalues and a finite number of sign probes, which orientation and positive step minimize the worst conditional expected objective error? It supplies a sharp all-angle formula, a unique global solution, exact finite-horizon decay at that solution, and an explicit high-condition-number obstruction for an axis-aligned problem. This explains why Hessian condition number alone cannot predict curvature-weighted sign-probe performance. The theorem deliberately treats the power-one (rather than experimentally preferred smaller-power) variant on a centered planar quadratic, with exact independent noise-free probes; it does not imply guarantees for realistic networks or noisy losses.
