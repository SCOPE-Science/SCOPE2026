# Exact \(p\)-rank and \(a\)-number strata in a \(\mathrm{PGL}(2,7)\)-invariant octic pencil

## Finding
Let \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_7\). For every \(\lambda\in k\setminus\{1,-1\}\), let \(C_\lambda\subset\mathbf P^2_k\) be the smooth plane octic
\[
Y^8-X^7Z-XZ^7-\lambda(Y^2-2XZ)^4=0.
\]
Its genus is \(21\). Write \(f(C_\lambda)\) for the \(7\)-rank of its Jacobian, and \(a(C_\lambda)=\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha_7,\operatorname{Jac}(C_\lambda))\) for its \(a\)-number. Their **complete joint stratification** is
\[
(f(C_\lambda),a(C_\lambda))=
\begin{cases}
(0,21),&\lambda=0,\\
(14,7),&\lambda^2=2,\\
(21,0),&\lambda\ne0,\ \lambda^2\ne2.
\end{cases}
\]
The two intermediate parameters are \(\lambda=3\) and \(\lambda=4\) in \(\mathbf F_7\); they are smooth members distinct from the excluded singular parameters. In particular, the smooth pencil has two nonordinary members with \(a\)-number \(7\), besides the superspecial Hermitian member at \(\lambda=0\).

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is geometric over the algebraic closure of characteristic \(7\). The pencil, its smoothness for \(\lambda\notin\{1,-1\}\), and its \(\mathrm{PGL}(2,7)\) action are as in Borges--Korchmáros--Speziali, Section 4.8, Lemma 4.23 and Theorem 4.25. The arithmetic genus of a smooth plane octic is \((8-1)(8-2)/2=21\). The curve at \(\lambda=0\) is the degree-eight Hermitian curve. No claim is made for the singular fibers \(\lambda=\pm1\), for other characteristics, or for a complete Newton polygon or Ekedahl--Oort type.

For a smooth curve \(C\), \(a(C)=g-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)\), where \(\mathcal C\) is the Cartier operator on regular differentials. Its \(p\)-rank \(f(C)\) is the stable rank of the Frobenius action on first structure-sheaf cohomology, not automatically the rank of a single Cartier matrix.

## Proof
**1. The genus-three quotient and \(p\)-rank.** In the affine chart \(Z=1\), put \(x=X/Z\) and \(y=Y/Z\). The translations indexed by \(b\in\mathbf F_7\) act as
\[
(x,y)\mapsto(x+by+b^2/2,y+b).
\]
They form a subgroup \(\Sigma\) of order \(7\), and the invariants \(\xi=y^2-2x\), \(\eta=y^7-y\) satisfy, by direct substitution in the defining equation,
\[
\eta^2=\xi^7+\xi-2\lambda\xi^4.
\]
The invariant function field is generated by \(\xi,\eta\) and defines a smooth genus-three hyperelliptic quotient \(B_\lambda=C_\lambda/\Sigma\). The polynomial on the right is squarefree precisely for the admissible parameters: in characteristic \(7\), its derivative is \(1-8\lambda\xi^3=1-\lambda\xi^3\), and the repeated-root calculation reduces to \(\lambda=\pm1\). In particular, \(B_\lambda\) is smooth throughout the stated domain.

For \(h(\xi)=\xi^7-2\lambda\xi^4+\xi\), the standard genus-three hyperelliptic Cartier-coefficient formula takes the entries from \(h(\xi)^3\) at degrees \(7i-j\), \(1\le i,j\le3\). Its coefficient matrix (before entrywise seventh roots) is
\[
Q_\lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,-\lambda^3+2\lambda,\lambda).
\]
Because this matrix is diagonal, its semilinear iterations have stable rank equal to its number of nonzero diagonal entries. Thus
\[
f(B_\lambda)=0\ (\lambda=0),\qquad
f(B_\lambda)=2\ (\lambda^2=2),\qquad
f(B_\lambda)=3\ (\text{otherwise}).
\]
Every nonidentity element of \(\Sigma\) fixes exactly one point of \(C_\lambda\), the point \([1:0:0]\), and that point is totally ramified. The Deuring--Shafarevich formula therefore gives
\[
f(C_\lambda)-1=7\bigl(f(B_\lambda)-1\bigr)+(7-1),
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad f(C_\lambda)=7f(B_\lambda).
\]
This proves the three asserted \(p\)-ranks. The sign of the middle term in the displayed affine quotient equation is fixed here by the explicit substitution; replacing \(\lambda\) by \(-\lambda\) leaves the exceptional square condition unchanged.

**2. The full plane Cartier rank.** Index the \(21\) adjoint monomials of degree five by positive triples
\[
\mathcal I=\{\alpha=(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)\in\mathbf Z_{>0}^3:\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=8\}.
\]
Let \(F_\lambda\) be the homogeneous degree-eight defining polynomial. Define a \(21\times21\) matrix \(A_\lambda\) whose \((\alpha,\beta)\) entry is the coefficient of
\[
X^{7\alpha_1-\beta_1}Y^{7\alpha_2-\beta_2}Z^{7\alpha_3-\beta_3}
\]
in \(F_\lambda^6\), interpreted as zero if an exponent is negative. The plane Cartier formula identifies the matrix of the Cartier operator with an entrywise seventh-root of this coefficient matrix, up to transpose and basis conventions. Therefore the two matrices have the same rank over \(k\).

An exact expansion in \(\mathbf F_7[\lambda][X,Y,Z]\) gives, after simultaneous reordering of rows and columns, four scalar blocks, four \(2\times2\) blocks, and three \(3\times3\) blocks. Every scalar block has determinant \(\lambda\). Each block of size \(d=2\) or \(d=3\) has determinant
\[
-\lambda^d(\lambda^2-2).
\]
The corresponding seven nontrivial blocks each have corank **exactly one** when \(\lambda^2=2\): a nonzero \((d-1)\)-minor is present in each case. All entries vanish when \(\lambda=0\). These statements are exact polynomial identities; the complete coefficient expansion and the minors are independently reproducible with the accompanying standard-library script `verify.py`.

It follows that
\[
\det A_\lambda=-\lambda^{21}(\lambda^2-2)^7,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(A_\lambda)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\lambda=0,\\
14,&\lambda^2=2,\\
21,&\lambda\ne0,\ \lambda^2\ne2.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently \(a(C_\lambda)=21-\operatorname{rank}(A_\lambda)\), proving the asserted \(a\)-numbers. Combining this with Step 1 finishes the joint classification.

## Verification
With Python 3, run `python verify.py` from the directory containing that standalone file. No third-party packages, data files, or external services are required. The code constructs the seven-term homogeneous polynomial in \(\mathbf F_7[\lambda][X,Y,Z]\), multiplies it symbolically to its sixth power, extracts the entire \(21\times21\) coefficient matrix, and checks the eleven block determinants as polynomial identities. It also checks that each nontrivial block loses exactly one rank at both roots of \(\lambda^2-2\), verifies the zero matrix at \(\lambda=0\), and independently recomputes the genus-three quotient coefficients from the cubic power of its hyperelliptic equation. Finite specializations are additional checks, not substitutes for the symbolic identities.

## Relationship to prior work
Borges, Korchmáros and Speziali introduce this invariant pencil and prove smoothness and automorphism results (Section 4.8); their Theorem 8.1 proves that all but finitely many parameters yield ordinary curves. Their proof uses the additive quotient and Miller's work on the Hasse--Witt matrix of the *quotient hyperelliptic curve*. These results provide the ingredients to extract the \(p\)-rank cases, but do not determine the plane octic's Cartier rank, its exceptional \(a\)-number \(7\), or the complete joint stratification. The standard plane Cartier-coefficient method of Stöhr--Voloch and its presentation in later computational literature gives a way to make this calculation, not the evaluated family-specific conclusion. Montanucci--Speziali compute Fermat and Hurwitz curve \(a\)-numbers, including the Hermitian parameter \(\lambda=0\), but not the present nonzero-parameter octic pencil. Booher--Cais analyze \(a\)-numbers of Artin--Schreier covers; their general bounds and special exact formulas do not specify this pencil's two nonordinary members, whose quotient curves are nonordinary. Related standard matrix conventions are discussed by Achter and Howe.

## Limitations
This is a complete classification of the two specified invariants in this characteristic-seven pencil, not a claim of a new general formula for all prime powers or a full description of the Jacobians. The exceptional values \(\lambda=3,4\) are parameter values, without an asserted classification of abstract isomorphism classes. The core algebraic computation is reproducible but has not undergone an independent mathematical audit. A historical hyperelliptic-matrix paper of Miller was not available in full in the consulted open sources; the originating paper's use of it was compared, but its uninspected details remain a bibliographic risk. Unindexed prior parameter calculations also remain possible.
