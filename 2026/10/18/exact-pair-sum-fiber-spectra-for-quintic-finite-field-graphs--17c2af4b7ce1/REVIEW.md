# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The ordered-pair map on the fifth-power graph reduces, for each nonzero first-coordinate sum, to two coupled quadratic discriminants. The nontrivial character identity is proved by counting factorizations of a hyperbola, with all special cases at square parameters zero and one handled explicitly. The five fiber multiplicities exhaust the \(q\) possible second-coordinate sums. Their exact second moment proves the additive-energy formula, and additive-character orthogonality gives the normalized constant-input Fourier fourth moment. The finite checker separately verifies the histogram across prime and quadratic extension fields. No finite test is treated as a proof for all fields.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

Koh–Shen (arXiv:1003.4240, Theorem 1.1 and Section 3) prove exponent-range boundedness for general plane algebraic curves, not the exact quintic fiber census. González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva (arXiv:2405.16647v2, Theorems 1.1–1.6) concern paraboloids, a parabola and cones, not the fifth-power graph. An adjacent-degree published quartic-graph result concerns constant-input energy for a different collision polynomial. Prior examinations of related cubic-graph and quartic-graph claims do not mathematically imply the quintic distribution. A classical identity or table for quintic monomial sums could still supply equivalent coverage; the result makes no claim of independently certified priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The fifth-power graph is a natural nonlinear finite-field Fourier-support curve. The result gives the complete distribution of its two-sum representation numbers for every extension field in the stated characteristics, distinguishes residue-sensitive fiber patterns, and proves that the aggregate fourth moment cancels one of the two character parameters. This goes beyond an arbitrary isolated energy count: it determines all convolution multiplicities and their exact fourth-moment consequence, including two extension-field test cases. It does not claim a sharp inequality for unrestricted input weights.
