# Exact parallel and sequential common-knowledge depth distributions with binary information cells

## Finding
Consider two named agents whose finite \(\mathsf{S5}\) information partitions have cells of size at most two. For the induced event operators, the **uniform parallel depth** is the least \(d\) such that \(E^d(P)=C(P)\) for every event \(P\), where \(E(P)=K_R(P)\cap K_S(P)\). The **uniform sequential depth** is the analogous least \(d\) with \(T^d(P)=C(P)\), where \(T=K_R\circ K_S\). Both are exactly enumerable, for every finite number \(n\) of labeled worlds and every nonnegative cutoff \(d\), by

\[
\#\{(R,S):\delta_E\le d\}=
 n![z^n]\exp\!\left(z+\sum_{r=2}^{d+1}z^r+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z^{2j}}{2j}\right),
\]
\[
\#\{(R,S):\delta_T\le d\}=
 n![z^n]\exp\!\left(z+\sum_{r=2}^{2d}z^r+\sum_{j=1}^{2d}\frac{z^{2j}}{2j}\right).
\]
Empty sums are zero. Counts of **exact** depth \(d\) are differences of consecutive cumulative counts. For \(n\ge3\), precisely \(n!\) labeled frames reach the largest parallel depth \(n-1\); they are the alternately colored spanning paths. The largest sequential depth in this class is \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) for \(n\ge2\). If every information cell has **exactly** two worlds, put \(n=2m\) and discard both path sums and the singleton term in the displayed exponentials: the resulting two cycle-only exponential formulas give the complete distributions. In that restricted case the largest respective depths are \(m\) and \(\lceil m/2\rceil\), and exactly \((2m-1)!\) labeled frames attain maximal parallel depth.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(W=\{1,\dots,n\}\), \(n\ge1\). Relations \(R,S\) are equivalence relations on \(W\), with all equivalence classes of size at most two; the agents are distinguished and the worlds labeled. For any \(P\subseteq W\), define \(K_R(P)=\{w:[w]_R\subseteq P\}\), and likewise \(K_S\). The common-knowledge event \(C(P)\) consists of those \(w\) whose entire connected component in the undirected graph \(G\) of distinct-world \(R\)- or \(S\)-links lies in \(P\). Iteration counts begin at zero: \(E^0=T^0\) is the identity on events. Uniform depth quantifies over **all** events, not over one valuation or one world. This is finite-world standard two-agent \(\mathsf{S5}\) semantics; no assertion is made about arbitrary unbounded-size information cells or infinite worlds.

## Proof
**1. Component decomposition.** Each \(R\)-class and \(S\)-class has at most two vertices. Draw nontrivial \(R\)-pairs as red edges and nontrivial \(S\)-pairs as blue edges, retaining both colors if the two pairs coincide. At each world at most one edge of each color is incident. Each connected component is therefore an isolated point, an alternating simple path on \(r\ge2\) vertices, or an alternating cycle on \(2j\ge2\) vertices, interpreting the coincident two-colored pair as the cycle with \(j=1\). This list is exhaustive and disjoint.

**2. Semantic depth per component.** For an event \(P\), membership of \(w\) in \(E^d(P)\) means every vertex of graph distance at most \(d\) from \(w\) lies in \(P\): one \(E\) applies one edge of either color (including reflexive steps). Therefore the smallest \(d\) working for all \(w,P\) is the maximum component diameter. The parallel depths of a path on \(r\) vertices and of a \(2j\)-cycle are respectively \(r-1\) and \(j\); an isolated point has depth zero.

For \(T=K_R\circ K_S\), one iteration tests all endpoints of an \(R\)-step followed by an \(S\)-step; either step may be reflexive. On an alternating path, moving \(\ell\) edges in the direction whose initial edge is red needs \(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\) iterations, and in the direction whose initial edge is blue needs \(\lceil(\ell+1)/2\rceil\). Thus the maximum over ordered vertex pairs on a path of \(r\ge2\) vertices is \(\lceil r/2\rceil\). On an alternating \(2j\)-cycle, the two routes to a vertex have complementary lengths; choosing the shorter appropriately colored route shows all vertices are accessible in \(\lceil j/2\rceil\) iterations, and an antipodal vertex (or a near-antipodal one when necessary) witnesses optimality. A double-colored two-vertex component has sequential depth one. An isolated point has depth zero. Finally, a relation-power computation holds for all events exactly when its reachable sets have filled their components: otherwise taking the complement of one missing vertex separates the approximant from \(C(P)\). These are therefore the uniform semantic depths, not merely graph proxies.

**3. Labeled component counts.** On a specified set of \(r\ge2\) vertices, a colored path is determined by a linear ordering up to reversal and one of two initial edge colors: \(r!/2\) times two, hence \(r!\) such paths. For a cycle on specified \(2j\) vertices, choose its red perfect matching, in \((2j-1)!!\) ways. After red pairs are fixed, the number of blue matchings giving one connected alternating component is \(2^{j-1}(j-1)!\); this also holds for \(j=1\), the doubled edge. The product is \((2j-1)!\). Thus the exponential generating function of allowed components is the exponential of one singleton term \(z\), one \(z^r\) for each permitted path size, and one \(z^{2j}/(2j)\) for each permitted cycle size. Using the depth cutoffs in step 2 yields both displayed coefficient formulas for every \(n,d\).

**4. Extremes and exactly binary cells.** For \(n\ge2\), a spanning alternating path reaches parallel depth \(n-1\) and sequential depth \(\lceil n/2\rceil\). No component has more than \(n\) vertices, so these are the maxima. For \(n\ge3\), parallel depth \(n-1\) forces a spanning path, and its count is \(n!\). For \(n=2\), three nontrivial edge-color patterns have depth one (the exceptional boundary). When every class has size two, the two agents each induce perfect matchings, hence no isolated points or paths occur; necessarily \(n=2m\). The largest alternating cycle has \(2m\) vertices, with depths \(m\) and \(\lceil m/2\rceil\). A single such component can be formed in \((2m-1)!\) labeled ways. In this subfamily the cycle-only exponential generating functions enumerate all cases.

## Verification
The companion executable `verify.py` uses only Python's standard library. It enumerates all involution pairs on labeled world sets of sizes \(1\) through \(7\), obtains **both** depth distributions by explicit relation composition and graph-component reachability, and independently evaluates the generating-function coefficients exactly with rational arithmetic. It additionally enumerates all pairs of perfect matchings through eight worlds, and all unrestricted equivalence-relation pairs of three through six worlds to test the spanning-path extremal statement. A separate exhaustive truth-set calculation on every event and every involution pair through four worlds recomputes both iterated knowledge operations literally; all checks return `VERIFY_OK`.

For \(n=4\), the parallel depth distribution \((\delta=0,1,2,3)\) is \((1,45,30,24)\); the sequential distribution is \((1,51,48)\). For \(n=6\), the parallel distribution is \((1,855,1920,1560,720,720)\), and the sequential distribution is \((1,1215,3120,1440)\). These are exhaustive finite checks of the formulas, not substitutes for their all-\(n\) proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The standard information-partition interpretation of common knowledge and the fixed-point/hierarchy equivalence are classical (Aumann and Barwise; see the Stanford Encyclopedia treatment). Humml and Schröder (2022/2023) provide a contemporary epistemic-logic motivation with finite models and generalized groups, not these two exact finite depth histograms. Lugo's work (2009) studies cycle and path decompositions of pairs of involutions, including fixed-point-free pairs, and supplies known combinatorial background; neither the path/cycle species itself nor the cycle enumeration is claimed new. The present single result concerns their **joint use to measure two distinct, precisely defined common-knowledge approximation procedures**, including exact semantic-depth cutoffs and all labeled finite-frame distributions. Published database and literature searches did not reveal this joint statement; alternate or unindexed presentations remain possible.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes two named agents and each information class has at most two worlds. The equality of finite approximants is uniform over events; it does not measure the depth of a particular formula at a particular state. There is no asymptotic claim and no assertion of originality for the classical graph decomposition, involution enumeration, or common-knowledge semantics. The literature comparison was limited by incomplete access to the complete text of Lugo's original preprint and the longer arXiv version of Humml–Schröder; older equivalent formulations of the combined iteration formulas cannot be ruled out. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
