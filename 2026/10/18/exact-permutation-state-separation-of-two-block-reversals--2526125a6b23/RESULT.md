# Exact permutation-state separation of two-block reversals

## Finding

Fix distinct letters \(a,b\) and positive integers \(r,s\). Let \(D(r,s)\) be the least number of states of a complete deterministic permutation automaton which accepts exactly one of the words \(a^r b^s\) and \(b^s a^r\) (with other inputs unrestricted). Define \(E_d=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,d)\) and the following two conditions:

\[
P_3(r,s):\quad 6\nmid r,\quad 6\nmid s,\quad (2\nmid r\ \text{or}\ 2\nmid s),
\]
\[
P_4(r,s):\quad 12\nmid r,\quad 12\nmid s,\quad \neg(6\mid r\ \text{and}\ 6\mid s).
\]

**Theorem.** For all positive \(r,s\), the exact separation size is

\[
D(r,s)=\begin{cases}
3,&P_3(r,s),\\
4,&\neg P_3(r,s)\text{ and }P_4(r,s),\\
\min\{d\ge5:E_d\nmid r\text{ and }E_d\nmid s\},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, if \(r=s=E_N\) for an integer \(N\ge4\), then \(D(r,s)\) equals the least prime power strictly greater than \(N\). The minimum always exists, and the result specifies the *least* possible state count, not merely a separating construction.

## Assumptions and scope

A complete deterministic permutation automaton has a finite nonempty state set \(Q\), an initial state \(q_0\), an accepting subset \(F\subseteq Q\), and, for each input letter, a bijection \(Q\to Q\). The state count is \(|Q|\); unreachable states are permitted in the definition of the minimum, but cannot improve it. The alphabet is precisely \(\{a,b\}\), with no restrictions on \(F\). Both exponents are strictly positive, and the two compared words have the same length. Composition of permutations is functional composition, and noncommutation does not depend on the choice of convention.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to commuting powers.** For a fixed \(d\), a permutation automaton can assign arbitrary elements \(x,y\in S_d\) to \(a,b\), respectively. The word actions are the two products \(x^r y^s\) and \(y^s x^r\). If these transformations coincide, no initial/accepting states can separate the words. If they differ, select a point \(q_0\) at which they differ and let \(F\) contain one, but not the other, of the two endpoints. Consequently a \(d\)-state separating automaton exists if and only if some \(r\)-th and \(s\)-th powers in \(S_d\) fail to commute.

For a positive integer \(m\), let \(H_m(d)=\langle g^m:g\in S_d\rangle\), the subgroup generated by all \(m\)-th powers. It is normal, because conjugating \(g^m\) gives another \(m\)-th power. Two families of elements commute pairwise precisely when their generated subgroups commute elementwise. Thus \(d\) states suffice exactly when \([H_r(d),H_s(d)]\ne1\).

**2. Degrees one through three.** The groups \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) are abelian, so one or two states never suffice. In \(S_3\), a transposition remains a transposition under every odd power, and all transpositions generate \(S_3\). When \(m\) is even but not divisible by \(3\), transposition powers vanish and powers of the two nontrivial \(3\)-cycles generate \(A_3\). When \(6\mid m\), every power is the identity. Hence

\[
H_m(3)=\begin{cases}S_3,&2\nmid m,\\ A_3,&2\mid m,\ 3\nmid m,\\1,&6\mid m.\end{cases}
\]

Since \(A_3\) is abelian, but neither \(S_3\) with itself nor \(S_3\) with \(A_3\) commutes elementwise, the precise three-state criterion is \(P_3(r,s)\).

**3. Degree four.** An element of \(S_4\) has order dividing \(12\); possible nonidentity cycle types have orders \(2,3,4\). Odd powers of transpositions generate \(S_4\). When \(m\) is even with \(3\nmid m\), all powers are even, and the powers of \(3\)-cycles generate \(A_4\), so \(H_m(4)=A_4\). When \(6\mid m\) and \(12\nmid m\), the only nonidentity powers come from \(4\)-cycles: each such \(m\)-th power is a double transposition, and the three double transpositions generate the Klein four group \(V_4\). Finally \(12\mid m\) makes all powers trivial. Thus

\[
H_m(4)=\begin{cases}
S_4,&2\nmid m,\\
A_4,&2\mid m,\ 3\nmid m,\\
V_4,&6\mid m,\ 12\nmid m,\\
1,&12\mid m.
\end{cases}
\]

The subgroup \(V_4\) is abelian. In contrast, a \(3\)-cycle conjugates the three nonidentity elements of \(V_4\) nontrivially, so \(A_4\) does not centralize \(V_4\); similarly neither \(A_4\) nor \(S_4\) is abelian. Thus any two nontrivial displayed subgroups fail to commute elementwise, except the pair \((V_4,V_4)\). This is exactly the condition \(P_4(r,s)\). (If \(P_3\) already holds, the minimum was three.)

**4. All degrees at least five.** The exponent of \(S_d\), the least positive number divisible by every element order, is \(E_d=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,d)\): every permutation decomposes into cycles of lengths at most \(d\), while a cycle of every length from \(1\) to \(d\) exists.

If \(E_d\mid m\), every \(m\)-th power is trivial and \(H_m(d)=1\). Otherwise some cycle length \(k\le d\) does not divide \(m\), so \(H_m(d)\ne1\). If \(m\) is odd, all transpositions are \(m\)-th powers, hence \(H_m(d)=S_d\). If \(m\) is even, the sign of every \(m\)-th power is \(+1\), so \(H_m(d)\le A_d\). It is a nontrivial normal subgroup of \(A_d\), and simplicity of \(A_d\) for \(d\ge5\) yields \(H_m(d)=A_d\). Therefore

\[
H_m(d)=\begin{cases}
1,&E_d\mid m,\\
S_d,&E_d\nmid m\text{ and }2\nmid m,\\
A_d,&E_d\nmid m\text{ and }2\mid m,
\end{cases}\qquad d\ge5.
\]

Since \(A_d\) is nonabelian, any two nontrivial groups in this list fail to centralize each other: they both contain \(A_d\). Hence, for \(d\ge5\), separation is possible exactly when \(E_d\nmid r\) and \(E_d\nmid s\). The three preceding degree analyses prove the formula. As \(E_d\ge d\), the last minimum exists for finite fixed positive exponents.

**5. Prime-power threshold.** For \(N\ge4\), set \(r=s=E_N\). Both small-degree conditions fail since \(12\mid E_N\). For any \(d\ge5\), \(E_d\nmid E_N\) holds if and only if \(E_d>E_N\), and the least \(d\) with \(E_d>E_N\) is the least prime power strictly greater than \(N\). Indeed, \(E_d/E_{d-1}>1\) holds exactly when \(d\) is a prime power, by the prime-power valuations of a least common multiple. This proves the corollary.

## Relationship to prior work

Xu and Regan (2026) study worst-case permutation-automaton word separation and relate it to positive identities of symmetric groups. Their new asymptotic lower bound and permutation-order covering construction motivate looking for natural, exactly solvable equal-length pairs. The theorem here concerns only pairs of two monotone runs and supplies an all-exponents exact *pairwise* threshold rather than their worst-case asymptotic bound.

Bulatov, Karpova, Shur, and Startsev (2016/2017) investigate positive group identities for automaton separation. Xu and Regan explicitly report that Karpova and Shur (2021, Theorems 18 and 21) characterized identities of the more constrained form \( (ab)^u(ba)^v=(ba)^v(ab)^u \) for \(S_d\) with \(d\ge5\). There the two substituted group elements are conjugate (the products \(xy\) and \(yx\)), whereas the present claim tests *independently chosen* letter permutations and includes degrees three and four. That earlier theorem therefore does not automatically supply this arbitrary-power classification, although a more general statement within the same paper remains possible. Karpova and Shur (2021) also examine finite low-degree catalogs. Its accessible abstract and beginning of introduction establish significant thematic proximity; a complete comparison to its inaccessible main text is **not** claimed. None of the primary passages actually examined gave the displayed three-/four-state exceptional formula and all-degree rule. Its possible prior coverage remains a material originality caveat.

## Limitations

The formula concerns complete deterministic *permutation* automata, not unrestricted automata, and only the swapped two-run word pair. It is not a new general theorem about normal subgroups of symmetric groups, the exponent \(E_d\), or the equivalence of word identities and automaton separation: these are established tools used in a new exact pairwise classification. Similarity searches and limited source access do not prove priority. The full body of the 2021 Karpova--Shur paper was not available during the comparison, so an equivalent classification cannot be categorically excluded. Independent scientific assessment remains outstanding.
