# Review of the radial Rodrigues limiting law

## Correctness
**PASS.** For the canonical polynomial \(P(z)=z^d-1\), finite derivatives have radial real-root confinement by the explicit \(z^rQ(z^d)\) differentiation recurrence and Rolle's theorem. The fixed-ratio existence and algebraic Cauchy-transform identity follow from the cited Hägg–Shapiro theorem and selected saddle. The inverse polynomial has exactly one nonzero critical value \(T\), its potential pole at \(t=1\) lies on the physical branch precisely for \(\alpha<1\), and Stieltjes inversion gives a positive interior density. Taylor/Puiseux expansions at \(t=0\) and \(t=T\) give the three sharp displayed edge laws. Lagrange inversion supplies all moments. Exact finite coefficient computations and numerical branch evaluations corroborate the algebra. They are supporting tests, not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality
**PASS with documented residual risk.** The recent general convergence theorem gives implicit existence and transform equations for all polynomial descendants. Its Proposition 3.4 already gives the isolated root-atom mass and its threshold, so these two facts alone are not new. What is not supplied there is the complete continuous support, rational Fuss–Catalan generating function, precise pole/branch collision mechanism, or three sharp endpoint density laws for the repeated-critical-point family. The earlier Bøgvad–Hägg–Shapiro treatment requires a simple critical set, violated here for \(d\ge3\). The checked classical star-polynomial paper studies a different type-I Angelesco family and its distinct endpoint exponents. Claim- and structure-directed database and literature queries revealed no equivalent special-family phase diagram or general prior theorem immediately yielding all its quantified conclusions. The broad 2026 exponential-profile papers give general coefficient-transform methods and a catalogue of classical real-rooted examples, but the inspected parts do not state this regular-polygon ratio family or its full pole/edge phase. The Martínez-Finkelshtein–Rakhmanov flow theorem gives Cauchy-transform convergence near infinity, not these evaluated physical densities. A possible connection to generalized Angelesco/type-II or free-probability laws remains a historical-priority risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The regularly spaced-root polynomial is a canonical, non-generic test object for the recent full-shadow fixed-ratio theorem, and its repeated critical point is excluded from the earlier convergence framework. The exact radial radius, every moment, atom disappearance at \(\alpha=1\), and sharp conversion of a soft square-root edge to an inverse-square-root edge characterize a natural one-parameter family rather than an arbitrary finite slice. These quantitative phase laws are not supplied as tabulated answers by the motivating paper.

## Closest literature and limitations
Hägg–Shapiro (2026, arXiv:2610.09602v1) provides the general analytic foundation. Bøgvad–Hägg–Shapiro (2023, DOI:10.1007/s00365-023-09657-x) is the earlier simple-critical general theory. Leurs–Van Assche (2019, DOI:10.1007/s00365-019-09457-2) concerns type-I Jacobi–Angelesco polynomials on a star, with different zero-density equations. Jalowy–Kabluchko–Marynych (2026, DOI:10.1007/s00365-026-09778-z and DOI:10.1007/s00365-026-09775-2) provide general real-rooted methods and classical examples; Martínez-Finkelshtein–Rakhmanov (2024, arXiv:2408.13851) studies general derivative flow. The proof does not establish a finite-power error rate, endpoint-uniform parameter asymptotics, or exhaustive historical novelty.
