# Scientific assessment — cycle/record positional covariance

**Correctness: PASS.** Conditioning on a uniformly revealed one-line prefix decomposes disjoint cycles into already closed components and a uniform permutation of the remaining open path components. Counting specified closed cycles and their compatibility with an upper record yields the exact kernel. A binomial-unimodality tail estimate proves the stated two-term covariance asymptotic. Exact rational exhaustive checks through nine letters and a separate harmonic-identity check through 200 letters corroborate, but do not replace, the unrestricted argument.

**Originality: PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The exact same-permutation positional cycle/record covariance is distinct from Foata equidistribution and marginal cycle-count asymptotics. The inspected primary material on cycle counts (Maples–Nikeghbali–Zeindler), records/record equidistribution (Mahmoud), Euler–Stirling joint statistics (Jin), and record-maxima classes (Baril–Petrossian) does not state this particular pointwise mixed moment; the related Dubach cycle-type-conditioned record results involve different observables. Hamaker–Rhoades (2025), full text, provides general moment formulas for partial permutations conditional on conjugacy class; its theorem does not state the present evaluated cycle/record-position covariance and its §6.2 poses more general joint-statistic questions. Public mathematical research-index comparisons located a cycle-versus-inversion-indicator covariance, and related permutation record kernels, but not this one. A broader uninspected joint generating function could imply the formula, so priority is not certified.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** Disjoint-cycle count and strict record count are classical, naturally paired Stirling statistics; marginal equidistribution says nothing about their same-permutation dependence. The kernel shows that each nondeterministic record is positively correlated with the cycle count, while asymptotically almost all the covariance is localized at the last position. These are exact, structural joint-distribution facts rather than arbitrary small-table values.
