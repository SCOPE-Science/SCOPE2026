# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The graph geodesic inequalities reduce exactly to an anchored cube-slab polytope under a unimodular difference map. A Fourier identity for the second-moment density of uniform increments gives the evaluated variance formula; exchangeability yields every covariance entry. An even-unimodal convolution argument proves strictly negative increment covariance for every nonredundant closing edge, not just tested rational cases. The matrix inversion is an exact rank-one calculation. For strictly positive limiting slab fractions, the conditioned Donsker theorem, uniform integrability, and Gaussian endpoint regression give the Brownian limit and terminal-stiffness scaling. The independent finite polytope integrals reproduced the formulas in 18 rational configurations. Risk: asymptotic thin-slab functional convergence is deliberately not claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk

The previously known weighted-cycle volume calculation supplies the volume-normalization formula; volume is excluded from the novelty assertion. Earlier cycle Lipschitz-free work concerns Schauder bases (Novotný, arXiv:1905.13188), while Alexander et al. (arXiv:1911.10642) address general geometry and volume products. De Bruyne et al. (arXiv:2104.06145) develop sampling of fixed-endpoint bridges; that source does not give the all-dimensional weighted-cycle precision matrix or its strict edge-redundancy criterion. Searches also compared numerical index, Hilbert distortion, Brownian conditioning and cube-section aliases. The closest records did not imply the combined theorem. Remaining risk: a differently indexed exact moment formula might exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The covariance precision directly detects whether the cycle-closing edge is essential, via a strictly positive terminal spring, even though the uniformly sampled coordinate law is non-Gaussian. It gives all pairwise second moments, determinant and a banded inverse in arbitrarily large dimension using only three one-dimensional spline integrals. The sharp \(\sqrt d\) crossover connects bulk metric-polytope geometry to a non-Gaussian mixture of Brownian bridge and truncated endpoint, providing natural geometric scaling data beyond counts or volume alone.
