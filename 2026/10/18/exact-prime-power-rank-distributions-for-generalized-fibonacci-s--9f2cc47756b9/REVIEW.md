# Scientific review: prime-power generalized Fibonacci ranks

## Correctness

PASS. The claim distinguishes the unit-discriminant case from the ramified classes. The identity \(U_{ab}=U_a(U_b\text{-trace counterpart})\) is used in its explicit form \(U_{ab}(s)=U_a(s)U_b(V_a(s))\). In the unit-discriminant case, first vanishing is exactly the order of the square of a characteristic root in a cyclic split or norm-one group. The two-to-one trace quotient, square-map kernel and exclusion of the two discriminant-zero residues produce the claimed totient counts. For ramified classes at primes at least five, Taylor expansion at the double root makes each additional prime-power index add exactly one valuation. At three, the first factor can have a higher valuation; the remaining factors each contribute one. The counts by valuation of \(s^2-1\) establish the exceptional tower. All assumptions and boundary cases \(k=1\) and \(d=1\) are explicitly covered. The attached checker independently confirms all distributions over 4,815 parameters in 25 rings; finite checks are not offered as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to residual literature risk. Park (arXiv:1407.8086v1) obtains the prime-level rank through eigenvalue orders and explicitly asks for the dependence of prime-power rank on the recurrence parameter. Gassert (arXiv:1209.4396) studies finite-field first-kind Chebyshev dynamics, not complete second-kind first-zero multiplicities. Panraksa and Tangboonduangjit (arXiv:2605.04417) extend first-kind fixed-point and orbit-period counts to prime-power rings and use closely related cyclic-group structures, but their fixed-point equation is \(T_n(x)=x\), whereas the present equation is \(U_{n-1}(x/2)=0\); their formulas do not supply the stated valuations and multiplicities for discriminant-zero classes. Related published research records on Lucas period equality and prime races likewise do not imply a parameter-by-parameter exact first-zero census. An inaccessible or unindexed stronger polynomial-root theorem could nevertheless subsume it, so independent originality confirmation remains warranted.

## Value

PASS. The result answers a question expressly raised in Park's 2014 paper, determines the full rank spectrum and multiplicity for every odd prime power, and explains the genuinely different prime-three ramification pattern rather than a mere table of small cases. The invariant has an established number-theoretic meaning and the theorem gives an infinite uniform formula.

## Limits and closest literature

The result does not cover characteristic two or a general second Lucas parameter. The predecessor's finite-field order proof and subsequent first-kind Chebyshev dynamical methods are acknowledged. Only selected relevant parts of available full texts were materially inspected. Unindexed equivalences remain a residual risk. No external mathematical validation or independent audit has been performed.
