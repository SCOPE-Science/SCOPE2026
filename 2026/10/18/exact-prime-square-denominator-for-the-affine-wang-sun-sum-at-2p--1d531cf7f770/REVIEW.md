# Scientific review: exact prime-square Wang–Sun denominator

## Correctness
**PASS.** The precise quantification is over every prime \(p\ge5\). The divisor reduction for \(n=2p^2\) has only two nonzero blocks, and the lower block makes an integral correction. The odd-root Newton sum is an integer, primitive \(p^2\)-root contributions have valuation greater than \(-2p\), and the primitive \(p\)-root expansion has precisely one term with valuation \(-2p\). The complementary terms are bounded strictly above it by an explicit factorial valuation case split. The cyclotomic norm argument rules out every other denominator prime. Exact rational computations for six primes supplement but do not replace the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to the identified prior-art risk.** Agustín-Aquino's October 2, 2026 preprint [1], Sections 5–8, determines the general affine reduction and the first-prime layer \(n=2p\). It does not state the exact reduced denominator for the prime-square layer \(n=2p^2\). Its closed formula is applicable to individual inputs, but the cancellation control needed for a uniform simple denominator exponent is additional. The 2022 precursor [2] presents only initial values. Available scientific-result searches and comparisons with existing related findings disclosed a nearby formula at \(n=4p\), which does not imply this assertion at \(2p^2\), and did not locate this prime-square law. Earlier unindexed equivalent calculations or stronger unstated consequences of a general formula remain the main priority risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The original problem's exact rational values have unexpectedly large prime-power denominators. The sharp prime-square law explains the growth and shows that the old first-prime pattern of exponent \(1\) changes to an exponent depending on \(p\), a structural property relevant to understanding further prime-power indices. The value lies in the full exact natural parameter family rather than a handful of table entries.

## Limits
No claim is made for \(p=2,3\), for \(2p^k\) when \(k\ge3\), or for composite mixed-prime indices. No asymptotic numerator law, optimal general prime-power extension, or independent external verification is asserted. Published closed formulas are related and remain a possible originality risk.
