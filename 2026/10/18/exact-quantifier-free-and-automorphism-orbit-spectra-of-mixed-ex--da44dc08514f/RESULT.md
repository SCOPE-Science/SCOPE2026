# Exact quantifier-free and automorphism-orbit spectra of mixed-exponent rank-two \(p\)-groups

## Finding

Let \(p\) be any prime, \(k\ge1\), and \(A_p=C_{p^2}\oplus C_p\), viewed in the pure group signature \((+,-,0)\). Let \(T_{p,k}\) count complete parameter-free quantifier-free types of ordered \(k\)-tuples that are realized in \(A_p\). Let \(O_{p,k}\) count orbits of ordered \(k\)-tuples under \(\operatorname{Aut}(A_p)\). Define
\[
u_k=\frac{p^k-1}{p-1},\qquad v_k=\begin{cases}0&k=1,\\\displaystyle\frac{(p^k-1)(p^k-p)}{(p^2-1)(p^2-p)}&k\ge2,\end{cases}
\]
and \(w_1=0\), \(w_k=p^{k-2}u_ku_{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\). Here \(v_k\) is the Gaussian coefficient \(\binom{k}{2}_p\). Then
\[
\boxed{T_{p,k}=1+(1+p^{k-1})u_k+v_k+w_k},\qquad
\boxed{O_{p,k}=T_{p,k}+u_k+p\,v_k}.
\]
Thus exactly \(2^{T_{p,k}}\) parameter-free quantifier-free \(k\)-ary relations are definable, versus \(2^{O_{p,k}}\) automorphism-invariant relations, and the fraction of invariant relations that are quantifier-free definable is \(2^{-u_k-pv_k}\). In particular, at fixed \(p\), the exponent \(u_k+pv_k\) grows as \(\Theta_p(p^{2k})\) as \(k\to\infty\).

The realized quantifier-free types, stratified by the isomorphism type of the subgroup generated by their tuples, have counts
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
H&1&C_p&C_{p^2}&C_p\oplus C_p&A_p\\\hline
\text{types}&1&u_k&p^{k-1}u_k&v_k&w_k\\
\text{automorphism orbits per type}&1&2&1&p+1&1.
\end{array}
\]
The smallest finite abelian group admitting an automorphism-invariant *unary* relation that is not parameter-free quantifier-free definable is \(C_4\oplus C_2\), of order eight.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are finite. The vocabulary contains the group operations, inverse, and the identity, but **no additional constants naming nonidentity elements and no parameters**. Tuple variables are ordered and repetitions are allowed. Two tuples have the same complete quantifier-free type when they satisfy exactly the same equalities between group terms, and consequently the same Boolean combinations of those equalities. An automorphism-invariant relation is simply a union of complete automorphism orbits. No claim is made for first-order formulas permitting quantifiers, arbitrary nonabelian groups, or products involving additional primary factors.

## Proof

**Kernel classification of quantifier-free types.** Put \(R=\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\). Each ordered tuple \(\boldsymbol a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in A_p^k\) defines an \(R\)-module homomorphism
\[
f_{\boldsymbol a}:R^k\longrightarrow A_p,\qquad (r_1,\ldots,r_k)\longmapsto \sum_i r_i a_i.
\]
Every group term is an integer linear combination of the variables; since \(A_p\) has exponent \(p^2\), every equality between terms is equivalent to \(f_{\boldsymbol a}(r)=0\) for some \(r\in R^k\). Thus the complete quantifier-free type of \(\boldsymbol a\) is exactly the kernel \(K_{\boldsymbol a}=\ker f_{\boldsymbol a}\). The quotient \(R^k/K_{\boldsymbol a}\) identifies canonically with the subgroup \(H=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_k\rangle\) of \(A_p\). Conversely any submodule \(K\) with \(R^k/K\) isomorphic to a subgroup of \(A_p\) occurs as a tuple kernel. Exactly five isomorphism types can arise: \(1,C_p,C_{p^2},C_p\oplus C_p,A_p\).

If \(H\) is one such type, the number of kernels with quotient isomorphic to \(H\) equals the number of epimorphisms \(R^k\twoheadrightarrow H\) divided by \(|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|\). This is because postcomposing an epimorphism by an automorphism preserves its kernel and acts freely and transitively on the epimorphisms having that kernel.

**Epimorphism enumeration.** For a finite abelian \(p\)-group \(H\) of minimal generator rank \(r\), Nakayama's generator criterion reduces surjectivity to spanning the \(r\)-dimensional vector space \(H/pH\) over \(\mathbb F_p\). Consequently the number of ordered generating \(k\)-tuples of \(H\), equivalently of epimorphisms \(R^k\twoheadrightarrow H\), is
\[
|pH|^k\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(p^k-p^i).
\]
For \(H=C_p\), division by \(|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|=p-1\) gives \(u_k\). For \(H=C_{p^2}\), the count is \(p^k(p^k-1)/[p(p-1)]=p^{k-1}u_k\). For \(H=C_p\oplus C_p\), division by \(|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)|=(p^2-1)(p^2-p)\) yields \(v_k\) (and no surjection exists for \(k=1\)). For \(H=A_p\), surjectivity gives \(p^k(p^k-1)(p^k-p)\) epimorphisms. Each automorphism has the form
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto\big(a x+p b y\pmod{p^2},\ c(x\bmod p)+d y\pmod p\big),
\]
where \(a\in(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)^\times\), \(b,c\in\mathbb F_p\), and \(d\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). Its total number is \(p^3(p-1)^2\), giving \(w_k=p^{k-2}u_ku_{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\). One trivial kernel quotient accounts for the remaining type. Summation proves the formula for \(T_{p,k}\).

**Automorphism-orbit splitting.** A kernel \(K\) identifies its quotient with a particular subgroup type \(H\le A_p\), but the embedding of that quotient into \(A_p\) can fall into more than one global automorphism orbit. More precisely, orbit classes of tuples with kernel \(K\) coincide with \(\operatorname{Aut}(A_p)\)-orbits on embeddings \(R^k/K\hookrightarrow A_p\).

For trivial \(H\) there is one embedding. Any element of order \(p^2\) lies in one automorphism orbit because the parameter \(a\) moves its first coordinate among all units and \(c\) can adjust the second coordinate. Hence \(H=C_{p^2}\) has one embedding orbit. A generating tuple of all \(A_p\) identifies an automorphism of \(A_p\), so the full-group quotient also has one embedding orbit.

The characteristic subgroup \(pA_p\cong C_p\) is a distinguished one-dimensional subspace of \(A_p[p]\cong\mathbb F_p^2\). On coordinates \((p t,y)\) the automorphism formula induces precisely the invertible upper-triangular matrices \(\begin{pmatrix}\bar a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}\). Thus nonzero order-\(p\) elements form exactly two global orbits: in \(pA_p\), or outside it. For \(H=C_p\oplus C_p\), every embedding is an isomorphism \(H\to A_p[p]\). Quotienting all \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\) identifications by the upper-triangular subgroup yields index \(p+1\), equivalently one orbit for each line of \(H\) mapping onto the characteristic line \(pA_p\). These yield splitting multiplicities \(1,2,1,p+1,1\), so
\[
O_{p,k}=1+2u_k+p^{k-1}u_k+(p+1)v_k+w_k=T_{p,k}+u_k+p v_k.
\]
Every quantifier-free formula selects a union of the finitely many complete quantifier-free types: distinct kernels can be separated using the finitely many coefficient equations in \(R^k\), and a finite disjunction defines any union. Similarly, unions of automorphism orbits are exactly the invariant relations. Raising two to the respective type and orbit counts establishes both relation counts and their ratio.

**Smallest unary obstruction.** For \(k=1\) the formulas give \(T_{p,1}=3\) and \(O_{p,1}=4\). The characteristic subgroup \(pA_p\), viewed as a unary subset, is automorphism-invariant but not quantifier-free definable: both its nonzero elements and the order-\(p\) elements outside it satisfy the same unary coefficient equations. For \(p=2\), this gives an order-eight example. Every finite abelian group of order strictly below eight is either cyclic or \(C_2\oplus C_2\). In a cyclic group automorphism orbits of elements are classified by their orders, which are also their unary quantifier-free types; in \(C_2\oplus C_2\), all nonzero elements are in a single automorphism orbit and have the same unary quantifier-free type. Thus none of those smaller abelian groups has such an obstruction.

## Relationship to prior work

Campercholi, Tellechea, and Ventura (2023) prove a general semantic characterization of quantifier-free definability by preservation under subisomorphisms and give decision algorithms, including tests involving finite products with \(C_2\) and \(C_4\). The present result uses that natural setting but provides **explicit all-arity cardinalities** for both its quantifier-free and global-automorphism partitions on the mixed-exponent group \(C_{p^2}\oplus C_p\), including the sharp first unary obstruction. Mader (2012), Theorem 3.2, gives general automorphism descriptions of finite abelian \(p\)-groups that imply the matrix form above, but does not explicitly count these two logical tuple partitions. Classical finite-vector-space Gaussian counting gives one term \(v_k\) in the formula, not the complete result. The characterizations, automorphism matrix technology, and Gaussian coefficients themselves are not asserted to be new.

## Limitations

This is an exact theorem within one natural infinite family, not a classification for arbitrary finite abelian groups or arbitrary finite algebras. No independent external audit or publication-priority certification is claimed. Equivalent all-arity conclusions might appear in earlier algebraic homogeneity, embedding orbit, or subgroup-counting literature under different terminology; accessible comparisons did not establish such coverage. A relevant older treatise on subgroup orbits was inaccessible in full and remains a residual literature risk. The finite checker is corroboration only.
