For every \(r\ge2\), counting base-four digits equal to \(3\) modulo \(r\) requires exactly \(r\) states in base four, \(2r^2\) in binary read most-significant-digit first, and \(3r\) in binary read least-significant-digit first. The newly studied nonregular-Abelian-complexity example at \(r=3\) therefore has exact state costs \(3\), \(18\), and \(9\), exposing a sharp quadratic reading-order gap in a natural digit-count family.
