# Scientific review: the angular width law of kites

## Correctness — PASS
For an arbitrary kite normalized to \(Q_{a,b}\), the vertex-support calculation gives three exact trigonometric branches. Their sublevel angular lengths are evaluated on the correct domains, proving the all-parameter CDF. The middle derivative has a zero strictly inside its branch if and only if \(b>1\) and \(ab<1\), and its second derivative is negative. The coefficient of the ensuing density singularity follows from the quadratic Taylor expansion and two inverse branches; a half coefficient at a transition boundary and additive coincident critical levels are treated explicitly. A standalone checker independently uses all four vertex projections and tests the CDF and geometric identities. Finite checks support but do not establish the general proof.

## Originality — PASS
Akiyama and Kamae, arXiv:2201.06736 (2022), study the same random width observable but derive primarily scalar first/second moment and extremal-deviation results; their Section 7 motivates, but does not classify, the all-parameter kite distribution. Martirosyan, doi:10.3103/S1068362323060055 (2023), treats conditional random chord-length distributions of general quadrilaterals, not distributions obtained by randomizing projection direction. Targeted mathematical-record searches additionally found triangle, parallelogram, cube and octahedron results but no equivalent two-parameter kite law or sharp three-maximum bifurcation. A general support-function decomposition is prior background and is credited. Full accessible primary texts were read; the absence of unindexed equivalent work cannot be guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The published deviation-rate work singles out asymmetric kites in the unsettled quadrilateral setting. Moving beyond its two moments, the present exact full-law classification establishes how many angular maximum branches a kite contributes and how their probability-density singularity changes sharply under two independent geometric parameters. This is a natural, reproducible classification across an entire shape family, not a small numerical improvement. It does not claim to determine the deviation-rate minimizing kite or extend to arbitrary quadrilaterals.

## Limits and review status
The angle distribution is uniform on unoriented lines; chord distributions use a different probability model. Critical-value coincidences require summed local density coefficients. The review does not constitute independent mathematical validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
