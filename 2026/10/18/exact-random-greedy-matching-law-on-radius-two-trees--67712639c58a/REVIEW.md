# Scientific review: edge-order greedy matchings on radius-two trees

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem concerns finite simple trees with a specified radius-two root, every positive branch count and every zero-leaf boundary. Every output either accepts no central edge and takes one outer edge from each branch, or accepts exactly one central edge and takes the earliest outer edge in each other nonempty branch. Eligibility of a central edge is determined solely by its own branch, and the earliest eligible center is precisely the one accepted. Conditioning on its arrival time and multiplying the independent no-earlier-eligibility probabilities proves the polynomial-integral formula. Permutation symmetry of the distinct outer-edge labels gives the complete law rather than merely aggregate masses. The no-center event gives the strict total-variation obstruction for two or more fully positive branches. The double-star edge-counting check is a boundary specialization already covered by the earlier cograph probability law, and is not part of the novelty claim. A separately implemented rational first-edge recursion checked 452 profiles with 5,163 output states; direct exhaustive permutation enumeration checked 189,821 orders; an additional 21,840 inequality checks and 289 double-star comparisons agreed. These are checks of the infinite proof, not substitutes for it.

**Originality — PASS, bounded uncertainty.** Fairfax-Ball's complete October 1, 2026 primary manuscript, *Greedy Uniformity on Trees* (arXiv:2610.02276v1), was read: its process inspects vertices and selects maximal independent sets of trees, whereas the present result orders edges and samples maximal matchings. The equivalent maximal-independent-set model is on the line graph, which is generally not a tree. A previous exact cotree recursion for random-greedy independent sets on all cographs already covers the double-star specialization, and that boundary result is expressly excluded from our novelty claim. The earlier sufficient-uniformity work of Kryven–Versendaal–de Vries (arXiv:2608.07239v1) does not in the inspected scholarly description establish this complete tree-family matching law. The 2024 local-limit study of greedy MIS and the ITCS 2025 random-greedy-matching work concern size/density/query questions rather than each terminal matching's probability. Closest inspected published graph-class results address threshold graphs, bipartite chain graphs, and the arithmetic average of maximal-matching sizes in cyclic graphs, not this claimed distribution. The direct first-edge dynamic recurrence and symmetry are standard; the contribution is the explicit family-wide integral and sharp uniformity obstruction/exception. Search under dimer parking and random sequential adsorption terminology found no decisive equivalent. Unindexed older RSA material remains a genuine priority risk; searches alone never certify novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem gives the entire labeled probability law for an infinite natural family of trees, replacing factorially many edge orders with exact univariate polynomial integration and transparent branch parameters. Even where every maximal matching has the same size, it proves that sampling is quantitatively biased. The all-positive branch subfamily exposes nonuniform sampling even when every maximal matching has the same size; this is a substantive distributional effect invisible to cardinality-only theorems. This is not an arbitrary tiny computation or a routine re-expression of one finite instance.

The principal scientific limitation is that the formulas apply to radius-two trees only; no uniformity classification is claimed for arbitrary trees or for all mixed zero/nonzero branch counts. A residual literature risk is a result indexed under historical dimer random-sequential-adsorption terminology. Mathematical derivations and finite checking have received this review; external scholarly verification remains outstanding.
