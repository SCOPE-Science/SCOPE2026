# Exact random invariable-generation laws and a Gaussian waiting-time transition for cyclic Frobenius groups

## Finding

Let \(p\) be a prime, \(V\ne0\) a finite elementary abelian \(p\)-group, and \(H\cong C_m\) a cyclic group of order \(m>1\) acting on \(V\) without nonzero fixed vectors for any nonidentity element. Let \(G=V\rtimes H\) and \(N=|V|\).

Write the semisimple \(\mathbb F_pH\)-module as
\[
V\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^s U_i^{e_i},\quad d_i=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}U_i,
\]
where the \(U_i\) are pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible modules and each \(e_i\ge1\). For integers \(j,\ell\ge0\), set
\[
F_V(j)=\prod_{i=1}^s\prod_{a=0}^{e_i-1}\bigl(p^{d_i j}-p^{d_i a}\bigr),\qquad
T_m(\ell)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)(d-1)^\ell.
\]
The empty product is one and \(0^0=1\). If \(j<\max_i e_i\), then \(F_V(j)=0\).

**Exact tuple theorem.** For every integer \(k\ge0\), the number of ordered \(k\)-tuples \((g_1,\ldots,g_k)\in G^k\) that invariably generate \(G\) is
\[
\boxed{I_G(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}F_V(j)N^{k-j}T_m(k-j).}
\]
Thus the exact probability of invariable generation by \(k\) independent uniformly sampled elements is \(I_G(k)/(mN)^k\).

**Gaussian transition and second-order waiting-time theorem.** Fix a prime \(p\), an integer \(m>1\) dividing \(p-1\), and a scalar \(\zeta\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) of order \(m\). For \(n\ge1\), let \(G_n=\mathbb F_p^n\rtimes_\zeta C_m\), and let \(\tau_n\) be the smallest number of independent uniform samples that invariably generate \(G_n\). Then
\[
\boxed{\frac{\tau_n-mn}{\sqrt{m(m-1)n}}\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,1).}
\]
Equivalently, for each real \(t\), with \(k_n=\lfloor mn+t\sqrt{m(m-1)n}\rfloor\),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{I_{G_n}(k_n)}{|G_n|^{k_n}}=\Phi(t),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function. Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\mathbb E\tau_n-mn\bigr)
=m\sum_{j=1}^\infty\frac1{p^j-1}.}
\]
There is an explicit finite-\(n\) bound. If \(\mathcal P(m)\) is the set of primes dividing \(m\), then
\[
0\le \mathbb E\tau_n-mn-m\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1{p^j-1}
\le\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P(m)}\frac{\ell^{-n}}{1-\ell^{-1}}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

An ordered tuple invariably generates a group if every tuple formed by independently conjugating its entries generates the group. Repeated entries are allowed. Sampling is uniform on elements of the group (equivalently, on conjugacy classes weighted by class size), **not** uniform on the set of conjugacy classes. The fixed-point-free hypothesis is imposed on every nonidentity element of \(H\), not merely on one generator.

The exact formula covers all such finite cyclic-complement Frobenius groups, including reducible modules, repeated irreducible summands, all permitted characteristics and all nonnegative tuple lengths. The Gaussian and mean theorems are specifically for a fixed prime \(p\), fixed divisor \(m>1\) of \(p-1\), and growing multiplicity \(n\) of a single faithful one-dimensional scalar module. No claims are made for varying \(m\), noncyclic complements, or arbitrary nonsemisimple kernels.

## Proof

**1. Conjugacy-class reduction.** Since the complement acts fixed-point-freely, for each \(h\ne1\) the map \(1-h:V\to V\) is invertible. Consequently every group element with quotient component \(h\ne1\) is conjugate by some element of \(V\) to \(h\). Conjugating by an element of \(V\) fixes \(V\) pointwise, as \(V\) is abelian. Pure kernel elements have conjugates lying in their respective \(H\)-orbits, and multiplying a vector by \(H\) does not change the \(\mathbb F_pH\)-submodule generated by any list of such vectors.

Given \(k\) elements, choose the \(j\) entries whose quotient components equal \(1\). If the images of the other \(k-j\) entries do not generate \(H\), then no conjugated tuple can generate \(G\). Even when they do generate \(H\), all those nonidentity-quotient entries can independently be conjugated to their representatives in \(H\). If the pure kernel entries generate only a proper \(H\)-submodule, this choice gives a proper subgroup. Conversely, if those kernel entries span \(V\) as an \(\mathbb F_pH\)-module and the quotient images generate \(H\), then every conjugated tuple generates \(H\) modulo \(V\) and contains a kernel subset spanning \(V\) as a module. Therefore it generates all of \(G\). These two requirements are exactly equivalent to invariable generation.

**2. Kernel and quotient counts.** Maschke's theorem applies because \(p\nmid m\). The group algebra of a cyclic group is commutative and semisimple; the endomorphism division algebra of \(U_i\) is \(\mathbb F_{p^{d_i}}\). Thus \(j\) selected kernel vectors span \(U_i^{e_i}\) as an \(H\)-module exactly when the \(e_i\times j\) matrix of their \(i\)-th isotypic coordinates over \(\mathbb F_{p^{d_i}}\) has full row rank. The number of such matrices is \(\prod_{a=0}^{e_i-1}(p^{d_i j}-p^{d_i a})\). Choices between distinct isotypic components are independent, giving \(F_V(j)\).

A tuple of \(\ell\) nonidentity elements in \(C_m\) generates it precisely when it is not contained in any proper subgroup. Counting all nonidentity entries from the subgroup of order \(d\), which has \(d-1\) choices, and applying divisor-lattice Möbius inversion gives \(T_m(\ell)\). Given a nonidentity quotient component there are \(N\) arbitrary kernel coordinates. Choosing the positions of the \(j\) pure kernel entries gives the binomial factor. Multiplying and summing proves the exact tuple theorem.

**3. Coupled stopping times in the scalar family.** Sample uniformly from \(G_n\) repeatedly. Define \(\sigma_n\) to be the first time the pure-kernel samples span \(\mathbb F_p^n\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and \(\eta\) the first time the images of *all* sampled elements generate \(C_m\). By step 1, the invariable-generation time obeys the **pathwise equality**
\[
\tau_n=\max(\sigma_n,\eta).
\]
At current pure-kernel rank \(r\), the next sample increases the rank precisely when it lies in \(V\) (probability \(1/m\)) and outside the current span (conditional probability \(1-p^{r-n}\)). Successive rank-increment waiting times are independent geometric random variables with parameters \(m^{-1}(1-p^{r-n})\), for \(0\le r<n\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E\sigma_n=m\sum_{j=1}^n(1-p^{-j})^{-1}
=mn+m\sum_{j=1}^n(p^j-1)^{-1}.
\]
The variance of a geometric random variable of success probability \(a\) on \(\{1,2,\ldots\}\) is \((1-a)/a^2\). In this case the geometric parameters are bounded away from zero uniformly in \(r,n\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\sigma_n)=m(m-1)n+O_{p,m}(1),
\]
with a uniformly bounded third absolute centered moment per rank increment. Lyapunov's central limit theorem now gives
\[
\frac{\sigma_n-mn}{\sqrt{m(m-1)n}}\Longrightarrow N(0,1),
\]
since \(\mathbb E\sigma_n-mn\) stays bounded.

**4. Quotient generation has exponentially small effect.** For each prime \(\ell\mid m\), the cyclic group \(C_m\) has one maximal subgroup of index \(\ell\). Since quotient components are independent uniform elements, the probability that the first \(t\) fail to generate \(C_m\) is at most \(\sum_{\ell\mid m,\ \ell\text{ prime}}\ell^{-t}\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E(\eta-n)_+
=\sum_{t=n}^{\infty}\Pr(\eta>t)
\le\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P(m)}\frac{\ell^{-n}}{1-\ell^{-1}}.
\]
As \(\sigma_n\ge n\) deterministically, \(0\le\tau_n-\sigma_n\le(\eta-n)_+\). The expectation bound follows, and \(\tau_n-\sigma_n\to0\) in probability, giving the asserted Gaussian limit by Slutsky's theorem. Lastly, \(\sum_{j>n}(p^j-1)^{-1}=O_p(p^{-n})\), so replacing the finite mean correction by its infinite sum gives the stated second-order constant. This completes all parts of the proof.

## Verification

The standalone Python checker constructs finite scalar semidirect products directly. It independently enumerates all subgroups by closure, identifies maximal proper subgroups, enumerates conjugacy classes by the group law, and tests every ordered tuple against the exact maximal-subgroup obstruction to invariable generation. These brute-force counts match the closed formula in nine complete configurations. Among them: \(G=\mathbb F_3^2\rtimes C_2\) has zero invariably generating ordered pairs and exactly \(1296\) invariably generating ordered triples out of \(5832\), while \(\mathbb F_5\rtimes C_4\) has \(80\) successful ordered pairs out of \(400\). Run `python check_invariable.py` using Python's standard library. The checker implements the finite enumerations only; the asymptotic assertions are established analytically above.

## Relationship to prior work

Garzoni's 2020 preprint on the invariably generating graph studies connected components and random pairs of conjugacy classes for a different family, rather than the all-length tuple distribution and growing scalar multiplicity addressed here. Detomi and Lucchini (2014) already give a broader *rank-style existence criterion* for lifting invariable generators across elementary-abelian module extensions (their Proposition 8). Accordingly, the rank-based necessary-and-sufficient criterion in step 1 is **not asserted to be new**; the claimed contribution is its exact all-length probabilistic census throughout the fixed-point-free cyclic-complement family and, more specifically, the Gaussian critical window and sharp bounded second-order mean correction at fixed \(p,m\) and growing scalar multiplicity.

Kowalski and Zywina (2010) calculate Chebotarev invariants for the affine groups \(\mathbb F_q\rtimes\mathbb F_q^\times\), a significant previously covered special case. Their Proposition 4.1 concerns a varying complement of size \(q-1\), not the fixed-complement, growing multiplicity regime of the present limit theorem. A 2026 published result gives an exact complete-bipartite invariable generating **two-class graph** for elementary-abelian cyclic Frobenius groups, including the multiplicity-free criterion; this known graph structure does not count arbitrary uniform tuples or imply the Gaussian waiting-time profile. General Möbius inclusion–exclusion for any finite group also already exists, but is not the explicit product formula derived here.

## Limitations

The exact formula is restricted to a fixed-point-free cyclic complement and an elementary abelian kernel; the Gaussian and waiting-time limit further require fixed \(p,m\) and a faithful scalar action. Invariable generation is understood under **element-uniform**, not class-uniform, sampling. Finite tests cannot certify infinite-dimensional asymptotics; these use the independent geometric-waiting-time and quotient-union-bound arguments. General semidirect-product invariable-generation criteria and the affine \(\mathbb F_q\rtimes\mathbb F_q^\times\) mean-invariant calculation are pre-existing results, not new claims. A formula with equivalent fixed-complement asymptotics might exist under probabilistic-generation or coupon-collector terminology not discovered in the searched literature; independent priority evaluation remains to be done.
