# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The sole finding is an exact probability law for uniformly random vertex-order FirstFit on \(K_{n,n}\) minus a perfect matching, for every integer \(n\ge3\). Conditioning on the first vertex partitions orders into: the first deleted-matching partner arrives late (two colors), arrives early after at least two other opposite-side vertices (three colors), or arrives early after at most one (one color plus a smaller uniform crown process). The probabilities of those events are derived without independence assumptions. The recursion is solved for all tail probabilities, and the factorial extremal law and asymptotic mean follow by exact algebra. Separate direct and state-summed enumeration tests agree through \(n=7\). The finite checks support rather than replace the proof. The disconnected \(n=2\) boundary is handled explicitly.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The most directly relevant primary source is Ye et al. (2026), arXiv:2608.29603v1, Section 3.1, Theorem 3. Its full HTML states only \(\mathbb E[C_n]\le2.5+O(1/n)\), not the distribution or the exact asymptotic mean. The current claim is strictly stronger: the older upper bound cannot imply the exact tail or the convergence to \(2\). The 2024 random-order FirstFit paper is limited to forests; adjacent published exact random-greedy results concern maximal independent sets, not FirstFit vertex coloring. Focused searches located no equivalent probability law. Absence from index/search is not proof of absolute priority; unindexed earlier random-order/Grundy work remains the chief residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Crown graphs are the standard example of arbitrarily poor adversarial FirstFit coloring. The recently proved constant mean in random order motivates quantifying how rare the bad orderings really are. The new exact law not only determines every color-count probability and the worst-case event but sharpens the available bound from \(2.5+O(1/n)\) to \(2+1/n+3/(4n^2)+O(n^{-3})\). A regenerative decomposition explains why additional colors have rapidly decreasing probabilities, rather than merely producing a tabulation.

**Scientific limits.** This is not an exact law for arbitrary bipartite graphs, for the blow-up examples, or for other arrival/algorithm models. The proof, source inspection and reproducible checks are available in the accompanying package. Unindexed equivalent literature cannot be ruled out, and no independent audit is claimed.
