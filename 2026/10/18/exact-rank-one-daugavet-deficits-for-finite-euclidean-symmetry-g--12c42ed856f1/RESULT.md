# Exact rank-one Daugavet deficits for finite Euclidean symmetry groups

## Finding

For a finite subgroup \(G\) of the real orthogonal group \(O(d)\), \(d\ge2\), and \(t>0\), define the *rank-one group Daugavet profile*
\[
\mathfrak D_G(t)=\min_{\substack{T:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d\\\operatorname{rank}T=1,\ \|T\|=t}}\ \max_{g\in G}\|I+gT\|.
\]
All norms are Euclidean operator norms. Put
\[
\Phi_t(c)=\left(\frac{2+t^2+2tc+t\sqrt{t^2+4+4tc}}2\right)^{1/2},\qquad -1\le c\le1.
\]

**Theorem (finite-symmetry Daugavet profile).**

1. If \(H_d\le O(d)\) is the full group of signed coordinate permutations (the type-\(B_d\) reflection group), then for every integer \(d\ge2\) and every \(t>0\),
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak D_{H_d}(t)=\Phi_t(d^{-1/2}).}
\]
The minimizing rank-one operators \(T=t\,u\otimes v\), where \(\|u\|=\|v\|=1\) and \((u\otimes v)x=\langle x,v\rangle u\), are **exactly** those for which one of \(u,v\) has one nonzero coordinate and the other has all \(d\) coordinates of absolute value \(d^{-1/2}\). The two roles may be exchanged; arbitrary coordinate signs and positions are allowed.

2. In the Euclidean plane, let \(C_N\) be the rotation group of order \(N\ge1\), and \(D_N\) the dihedral isometry group of order \(2N\), generated by \(C_N\) and a reflection. For all \(t>0\),
\[
\boxed{\mathfrak D_{C_N}(t)=\mathfrak D_{D_N}(t)=\Phi_t\!\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{N}\right).}
\]
Consequently, among **all finite subgroups** \(G\le O(2)\) with \(|G|\le M\), the optimal profile is
\[
\boxed{\max_{|G|\le M}\mathfrak D_G(t)=\Phi_t\!\left(\cos\frac\pi M\right),}
\]
attained precisely by rotation groups of order \(M\) (up to conjugacy). For fixed \(t>0\),
\[
(1+t)-\mathfrak D_{C_M}(t)=\frac{\pi^2t}{2(t+2)M^2}+O_t(M^{-4})\quad(M\to\infty).
\]

In particular, for norm-one rank-one perturbations, \(\mathfrak D_{H_d}(1)\to(1+\sqrt5)/2\) as \(d\to\infty\). Thus the sharp worst-case deficit from the ideal Daugavet equality \(\|I+gT\|=1+t\) approaches \((3-\sqrt5)/2\) for these growing finite reflection groups, whereas optimally sized planar finite groups approach equality at the sharp quadratic rate displayed above.

## Assumptions and scope

The scalar field is \(\mathbb R\). The parameter \(t\) is strictly positive, \(d\ge2\), \(N,M\ge1\) are integers. The group acts on the **output** of \(T\): the quantity is \(\|I+g\circ T\|\), not \(\|I+T\circ g\|\) for a fixed \(g\) (their maxima over an orthogonal group agree, but the convention matters). Rank-one means rank *exactly one*. The theorem concerns only the specified groups and finite planar subgroups, not arbitrary finite orthogonal groups in higher dimension or arbitrary-rank perturbations.

## Proof

**1. Rank-one singular-value formula.** Let \(u,v\in\mathbb R^d\) be unit vectors, \(t>0\), and set \(c=\langle u,v\rangle\). For \(A=I+t\,u\otimes v\), the positive matrix \(A^*A\) equals
\[
I+t(u\otimes v+v\otimes u)+t^2v\otimes v.
\]
On the at-most-two-dimensional span of \(u,v\), its two relevant eigenvalues (including the unit eigenvalue when the span is one-dimensional) have trace \(2+t^2+2tc\) and product \((1+tc)^2\). The discriminant is \(t^2(t^2+4+4tc)\). The larger eigenvalue is therefore
\[
\lambda_+(t,c)=\frac{2+t^2+2tc+t\sqrt{t^2+4+4tc}}2.
\]
It is at least one, so extra eigenvalues equal to one do not change the norm. This remains valid at the collinear endpoints \(c=\pm1\), as follows directly from \(I\pm t\,v\otimes v\). Therefore \(\|I+t\,u\otimes v\|=\Phi_t(c)\). Since
\[
\frac{\partial\lambda_+}{\partial c}=t+\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{t^2+4+4tc}}>0
\]
for \(-1<c<1\), continuity gives strict monotonicity of \(\Phi_t\) throughout \([-1,1]\). (At the exceptional square-root endpoint, strictness still follows from the interior.)

Every rank-one \(T\) of norm \(t\) has a representation \(t\,u\otimes v\) with unit \(u,v\). Thus for any finite \(G\),
\[
\mathfrak D_G(t)=\Phi_t(\kappa_G),\qquad
\kappa_G:=\min_{u,v\in S^{d-1}}\max_{g\in G}\langle gu,v\rangle.
\]
Compactness of the two spheres and finiteness of \(G\) give genuine minima.

**2. Signed-permutation minimax and its equality cases.** For a unit vector \(u\), let \(a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_d\ge0\) be the decreasing rearrangement of its coordinate magnitudes; similarly \(b\) for \(v\). Independent sign choices, followed by the rearrangement inequality, show
\[
\max_{g\in H_d}\langle gu,v\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_i b_i.
\]
Set \(a_{d+1}=b_{d+1}=0\) and \(p_k=(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)\), with \(k\) ones. Then
\[
a=\sum_{k=1}^d(a_k-a_{k+1})p_k,
\qquad b=\sum_{\ell=1}^d(b_\ell-b_{\ell+1})p_\ell.
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative and
\[
\langle p_k,p_\ell\rangle=\min(k,\ell)\ge\sqrt{k\ell/d},
\]
because \(\max(k,\ell)\le d\min(k,\ell)\). Consequently
\[
\sum_i a_i b_i\ge\frac1{\sqrt d}
\left(\sum_k(a_k-a_{k+1})\sqrt k\right)
\left(\sum_\ell(b_\ell-b_{\ell+1})\sqrt\ell\right)
\ge\frac{\|a\|_2\|b\|_2}{\sqrt d}=\frac1{\sqrt d}.
\]
The second inequality is the triangle inequality for the displayed positive prefix decompositions. Equality is realized by \(a=(1,0,\ldots,0)\) and \(b=(d^{-1/2},\ldots,d^{-1/2})\), so \(\kappa_{H_d}=d^{-1/2}\).

For completeness, equality is rigid. In Euclidean space, equality in the triangle inequality for a positive sum of nonzero vectors forces all summands to point in the same direction. Distinct prefix vectors \(p_k\) are not collinear. Hence each of \(a,b\) must be a single normalized prefix vector. Equality in \(\min(k,\ell)\ge\sqrt{k\ell/d}\) then forces \(\{k,\ell\}=\{1,d\}\). This yields exactly the sparse/uniform minimizers stated. Strict monotonicity of \(\Phi_t\) transports the equality cases to all \(t>0\).

**3. Planar rotations and reflections.** Identify the unit circle with angles modulo \(2\pi\). The orbit of any vector under \(C_N\) consists of \(N\) equally spaced unit directions. For two unit vectors \(u,v\), the largest inner product over this orbit is the cosine of the smallest circular angular separation from \(v\) to a point of the orbit. That separation is at most \(\pi/N\). By choosing \(v\) exactly halfway between adjacent orbit directions one attains that maximum separation, so
\[
\kappa_{C_N}=\cos(\pi/N).
\]
Since \(D_N\) contains \(C_N\), its minimax constant is at least this value. Choose \(u\) on a reflection axis of \(D_N\). That reflection fixes \(u\), hence the entire \(D_N\)-orbit of \(u\) coincides with its \(C_N\)-orbit. The halfway choice of \(v\) proves the reverse inequality. Thus \(\kappa_{D_N}=\kappa_{C_N}\).

Every finite subgroup of \(O(2)\) is either a cyclic rotation group or a dihedral group: the orientation-preserving subgroup is a finite subgroup of the circle (hence cyclic), and, when present, any reflection produces exactly one additional coset. A cyclic group of cardinality \(q\le M\) has constant \(\cos(\pi/q)\). A dihedral group of cardinality \(2q\le M\) has constant \(\cos(\pi/q)\). Since \(\cos(\pi/q)\) strictly increases with positive integral \(q\), the largest achievable constant at cardinality at most \(M\) is \(\cos(\pi/M)\), uniquely at the rotation group of order \(M\). Apply \(\Phi_t\) to obtain the asserted optimality.

**4. Rates.** Direct substitution gives \(\Phi_t(1)=1+t\). Differentiating \(\Phi_t\) at \(c=1\) gives \(\Phi_t'(1)=t/(t+2)\). Since \(\cos(\pi/M)=1-\pi^2/(2M^2)+O(M^{-4})\), Taylor expansion proves the displayed sharp quadratic deficit. Finally \(d^{-1/2}\to0\); at \(t=1\),
\[
\Phi_1(0)=\sqrt{(3+\sqrt5)/2}=(1+\sqrt5)/2,
\]
which proves the golden-ratio limit.

## Verification

The singular-value calculation is an exact two-eigenvalue calculation, including collinear endpoints. The prefix-vector proof is a dimension-uniform analytic inequality with explicit equality cases, not an extrapolation from finite-dimensional tests. The group classification in the plane treats \(C_1,D_1\) as well as all higher orders. Useful independent spot checks from the closed formula: at \(t=1\), \(\mathfrak D_{H_2}(1)=\Phi_1(1/\sqrt2)\approx1.898966326\), \(\mathfrak D_{H_4}(1)=\Phi_1(1/2)\approx1.822875656\), and \(\mathfrak D_{C_2}(1)=\Phi_1(0)\approx1.618033989\). They serve only as arithmetic diagnostics. To reproduce any value, evaluate the displayed elementary function \(\Phi_t\) at the indicated group minimax constant.

## Relationship to prior work

Dantas, del Río and Raunig (2026) introduce the group Daugavet equation \(\sup_{g\in G}\|I+gT\|=1+\|T\|\), give qualitative orbit-transitivity criteria (their Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.6), and obtain a different quantitative estimate under an actual group Daugavet hypothesis (Proposition 5.10). Their statements imply that a finite group cannot act transitively on a nontrivial Euclidean sphere, but do not give the sharp worst rank-one norm for the nontransitive finite groups here. The present exact scale-dependent minimax calculation, rigidity of all type-B extremizers, finite planar group-size optimality, and sharp quadratic rate are distinct conclusions. The rearrangement step uses the classical rearrangement inequality; that inequality itself is not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to real Euclidean spaces, norm-constrained rank-one operators, and the specified group classes. It does not establish a new general criterion for the group Daugavet property, which is already known; nor does it determine the analogous minimax for all finite subgroups of higher-dimensional orthogonal groups. It is an analytic theorem with no independent review. A differently worded earlier finite-orbit covering or rank-one norm profile could overlap; the supplied literature comparison cannot prove publication priority exhaustively.
