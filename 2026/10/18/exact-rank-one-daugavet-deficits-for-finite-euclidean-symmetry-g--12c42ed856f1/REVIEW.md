# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every real norm-\(t\) rank-one operator is \(t\,u\otimes v\) for unit vectors. The exact top singular value of \(I+t\,u\otimes v\) depends strictly increasingly on \(\langle u,v\rangle\). The signed-permutation orbit maximum is the dot product of decreasing absolute-coordinate sequences, and an explicit positive-prefix decomposition gives a sharp \(d^{-1/2}\) lower bound. Equality analysis forces a singleton support paired with a uniform-magnitude vector. For rotations, nearest-orbit separation is exactly at most \(\pi/N\); reflections can be neutralized by an axis-fixed source vector. These steps establish all asserted minimax formulas, planar cardinality optimality, and the derivative-based rate. The endpoint cases are included. No finite sample is used as a proof of the dimension-uniform inequalities.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication priority

Dantas, del Río and Raunig (arXiv:2606.20429v1, first public 18 June 2026), especially Definition 2.3, Corollary 3.6 and Proposition 5.10, give the group-Daugavet definition, a qualitative exact-property criterion in finite-dimensional strictly convex spaces, and a conditional inequality. Full-text comparison does not yield the sharp fixed-norm finite-group minimax, the type-B equality classification, or size-optimal planar rate. Related published finite-group Fourier-norm and rank-one Daugavet records studied distinct operators/quantifiers. An earlier equivalent spherical-orbit-support or Weyl-chamber formulation remains a priority risk; semantic nonmatching is not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem makes a recent qualitative group-Daugavet notion quantitatively sharp in two canonical finite-symmetry families. The rigid optimizers, full perturbation-size dependence, finite-group design optimality in the plane and sharp quadratic approach to equality are structural invariants motivated by orbit approximation, rather than a selected numerical example. Their distinct high-dimensional behavior supplies a concrete boundary to finite-symmetry Daugavet approximation.

## Limitations

Real Euclidean spaces, rank-one operators, full signed-permutation groups and planar finite subgroups only. No claims about all higher-dimensional finite groups, arbitrary-rank operators, or general Banach norms. Publication-priority risk persists for possible alternate formulations; no independent mathematical audit was conducted.
