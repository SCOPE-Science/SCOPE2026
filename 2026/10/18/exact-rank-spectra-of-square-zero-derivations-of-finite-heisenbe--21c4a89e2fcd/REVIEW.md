# Scientific review: square-zero Heisenberg derivation ranks

## Correctness

**PASS.** The derivation identity gives precisely the block constraints \(A^{\mathsf T}J+JA=\lambda J\), and square zero forces \(\lambda=0\), \(A^2=0\), and \(fA=0\). The correspondence between square-zero symplectic operators of rank \(r\) and pairs (isotropic image space, nondegenerate symmetric form) is bijective in every characteristic. An explicit count of compatible central functionals yields both possible ranks. The nonsingular symmetric form counts use the correct characteristic-two isometry-group split, which simplifies to the same total product formula as in odd characteristic. Direct small-field enumeration verifies the formulas, but does not replace the proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** Lebedev (arXiv:math/0601536, §§1.5 and 2) provides the characteristic-two symmetric-form types, not a rank distribution of square-zero Heisenberg derivations. Brouwer–Gow–Sheekey (DOI:10.37236/3810, §2.2) counts all nilpotent symplectic Lie algebra elements by total, not square-zero elements by rank, and does not incorporate central-functional choices that can shift full derivation rank. Related publicly stated Heisenberg endomorphism-rank and automorphism results concern different morphism conditions; their statements do not entail this distribution. An older modular Lie derivation enumeration or a symplectic nilpotent-orbit calculation may contain an equivalent formula under another presentation; this remains a concrete bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The theorem determines the entire rank statistic on a natural finite algebraic subset of the derivation Lie algebra for every Heisenberg dimension and every finite field. The exact rank cutoff and large-field full-allowed-rank concentration quantify a structural feature not visible in the total nilpotent symplectic count. The even-characteristic term requires a genuine alternating/nonalternating split in even symmetric-form dimension; this is not a cosmetic substitution. It is an arithmetic classification, not a conjectural extension or a small isolated computation.

## Scope and limitations

The result counts individual derivations, not conjugacy classes, and assumes a nondegenerate Heisenberg commutator form. The published primary materials establish important ingredients but do not establish historical uniqueness. The symbolic argument is general; numerical checks are finite.
