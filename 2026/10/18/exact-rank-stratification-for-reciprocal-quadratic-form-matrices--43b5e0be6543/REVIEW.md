# Review of the exact quadratic-form rank stratification

**Correctness — PASS.** The coefficient-matrix derivation has two independent boundary pivots. Each other potential pivot corresponds to a unique exponent, whose interpolation coefficient is an explicit trace of conjugate roots. The precise root-ratio congruence count covers odd orders, even orders, and the exceptional orders two and four. Integer divisibility follows from Smith normal form. A self-contained exact finite-field verifier independently compares all 724 eligible parameter pairs for seven primes. The finite comparisons support but do not prove the universal statement.

**Originality — PASS with qualified risk.** Zhang--Yang's full-size result gives a rank upper bound under nonsquare constant coefficient, not an exact rank for anisotropic discriminants. She--Sun's and Zhu--Ren's related determinant formulas concern proper submatrices and specified cases. The order-based formula for all full-matrix rank strata was not identified in the inspected full texts, published mathematical records, or semantic mathematical searches. An implicit consequence of general interpolation theory remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The rank is the quantity directly controlling prime-power divisibility of a natural determinant under current study. The theorem determines its exact dependence on the order of a norm-one root ratio and yields additional powers, for instance a fourth power of \(19\) for parameters \((1,10)\) at \(p=19\), without claiming an exact determinant valuation.

**Limitations.** Split discriminants are excluded. The p-adic determinant exponent is a guaranteed lower bound only; it may not be sharp. The result has not received independent validation.
