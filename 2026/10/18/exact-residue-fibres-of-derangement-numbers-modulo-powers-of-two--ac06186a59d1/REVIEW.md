# Scientific comparison and review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement quantifies over all integer exponents \(k\ge3\) and all residues modulo \(2^k\). The proof constructs the standard \(2\)-adic interpolation \(F\), proves uniform convergence of its derivative and an exact first-order congruence with second-order integral remainder, and determines the derivative valuations on both parity classes. These imply exact local distance formulas on one odd and two even branches. Their normalized maps are isometries of \(\mathbb Z_2\), so finite-quotient injectivity forces surjectivity; the index fibres then have multiplicities one or four. The finite checker verifies the complete distribution for ten exponents, but the unrestricted quantifiers follow from the analytic argument, not extrapolation. Boundary cases \(k=1,2\) are stated separately and correctly.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual risk

The source comparison distinguishes the full all-exponents residue-fibre law from antecedents. Barsky (1981, Proposition 5) establishes analytic interpolation, not a fibre census. Miska (2015, Section 4.1.1) determines minimal periods modulo integers and Section 4.1.3 discusses valuations; these known results are credited. O'Desky and Richman (2020, Theorems 4.3 and 4.5) establish the relevant locally analytic falling-factorial interpolation and metric-map property but not the needed parity-dependent equality in the metric or the resulting image and fibres. Ulas (2023, Lemma 2.1) determines all residues modulo eight, so the \(k=3\) base profile is explicitly not claimed as new; Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.3 give valuations and three low binary digits of normalized terms, not unrestricted exact target multiplicities modulo \(2^k\). The OEIS derangement entry lists elementary congruences but no all-exponents density law. Literature and mathematical-result searches using derangement, subfactorial, rencontres, prime-power fibres, and equivalent \(2\)-adic image formulations did not locate a dominating statement. Absence of a search hit is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

For a classical integer counting sequence with established but incomplete modular arithmetic, the image of the map modulo every power of two and the exact pushforward distribution form a natural arithmetic invariant. The result gives a complete uniform infinite family (not a chosen finite slice) and a structural explanation: one full isometric odd-index branch and two valuation-shifted even branches produce the different multiplicities. The formula makes precise the nonuniform distribution of attainable residues and reveals a stable image fraction of five eighths, beyond mere periodicity or the first three binary digits.

## Limitations and comparison risk

The result does not address odd prime powers or generalized derangements. A broader unpublished or unindexed \(2\)-adic fibre theorem could imply the statement; independent originality validation has not been performed. No new claim is made for the published modulo-eight base case, basic periodicity, established valuation identities, or interpolation itself. The supplied executable tests are reproducible finite checks, not an independent proof or an external audit.
