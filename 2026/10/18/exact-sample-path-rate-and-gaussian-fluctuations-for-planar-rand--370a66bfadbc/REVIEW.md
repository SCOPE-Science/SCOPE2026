# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The projection factorization, Poisson angular density, stationary one-dependence, logarithmic moment bounds, and conditional-covariance computation together establish the stated almost-sure drift and central limit theorem. The nonstandard logarithm Fourier identities and explicit dilogarithm reduction are reproduced by independent numerical angular quadratures and the exact characteristic polynomial check. Finite simulations do not establish the infinite-horizon conclusions.

## Originality: PASS
The exact mean-square rate for equivalent planar Gaussian sketch-and-project is known; this result expressly does not claim it as new. A second-moment Perron root does not determine the sample-path logarithmic exponent, and general exact random pursuit and stable residual line-search results do not state this distributional formula. Search by projection-product, Gaussian directions, Lyapunov exponents, and log-cosine fluctuation aliases found no covering result. Potential unindexed equivalent formulas remain a bibliographic risk.

## Value: PASS
For iterative refinement with independent uninformative correction directions, the exact line-search method still converges pathwise with a computable stochastic fluctuation law. The discrepancy between expected-square and typical-square rates is a meaningful distinction for interpreting randomized solver trajectories. The explicit central-limit variance adds a parameter-resolved uncertainty characterization not supplied by one-step expected-error bounds.
