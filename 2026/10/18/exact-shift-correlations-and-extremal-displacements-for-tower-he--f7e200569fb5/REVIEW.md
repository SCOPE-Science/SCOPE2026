# Scientific comparison and limitations

## Correctness — PASS

The recursive characterization of tower height is used to identify the least forbidden exponent. A complete one-prime valuation case split gives the joint forbidden probability. Finite-prime CRT and a summable prime-power tail establish actual natural densities, rather than formal products. Nonnegativity and exact zero cases of the simultaneous-forbidden probability classify every minimizing shift, and product convergence proves strict nonattainment of the supremum. All claims include fixed-level and fixed-shift quantifiers. An executable finite verifier complements the infinite proof. Its finite observations are not substituted for that proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature

De Koninck and Verreault (arXiv:2308.09149) prove one-point tower-height densities but no two-point displacement spectrum. Srichan, Rungtanapirom, and Wananiyakul (DOI:10.1051/ita/2026022) prove the general adjacent \(M\)-free density: its implication for the displacement-one specialization is acknowledged explicitly as covered. Their theorem does not state the arbitrary-shift correlation factors or all extremizers. Mirsky's arbitrary-shift squarefree/power-free formula applies to consecutive forbidden exponents, whereas the tower-height forbidden set has gaps for \(k\ge2\). Devlin and Gnang (arXiv:1204.5251) address presence of primes within a tower, not shifted height correlations. Equivalent unpublished or unindexed generalizations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The displacement response of a natural multiplicative level set is not determined by its one-point density. The result gives exact two-point arithmetic statistics at every shift, a complete and sharp classification of the displacements minimizing simultaneous low height, a natural density for those extremizers, and a strict unattained upper envelope. The covariance changes sign across natural classes of shifts, revealing nonmixing structure. This is a motivated infinite classification extending the cited tower-height density problem; its squarefree boundary case is not claimed as novel.

## Limitations

No uniform effective asymptotic in the shift or level is asserted, and the covariance sign is not classified at every shift. All source comparisons carry a residual risk of unindexed equivalent results. Computational tests supplement the proof only.
