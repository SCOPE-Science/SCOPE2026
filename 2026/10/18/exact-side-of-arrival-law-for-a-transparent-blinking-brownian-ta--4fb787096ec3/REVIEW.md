# Scientific review — directional capture at a blinking target

**Correctness — PASS.** The event is defined as the side of the first *active* arrival at the origin. The stopped two-state backward equations follow from the Brownian generator, independent gate jumps and particle-only Poisson resets. The active state has distinct one-sided Dirichlet data and the inactive state has continuous value and derivative; both are essential. The exact bounded solution yields the two stated conditional formulas and their stationary average. Differentiating an explicit single-variable reparametrization establishes strict resetting monotonicity and strict switching-speed monotonicity at fixed inactive fraction, with the boundary layer obtained from exact limits. Eventual capture almost surely follows from a uniform positive chance per reset epoch, ensuring identification of the backward solution with the hitting probability. Numerical residuals and independent bridge simulations support, but do not replace, these arguments.

**Originality — PASS (same-model, with residual risks).** The closest article, Żbik et al., arXiv:2512.09739, investigates precisely this transparent model and presents side-of-arrival frequencies numerically (Fig. 5), while its analytical results concern time-to-detection laws. The proposed novelty is specifically the side-resolved closed probability, its global monotonicities, and the singular fast-gating and high-reset distance limits. Bressloff, arXiv:2009.02739, and Mercado-Vásquez–Boyer, arXiv:2107.02148, use a reflective inactive half-line target, so their boundary problems lack capture from both sides. Bressloff, arXiv:2601.01588, considers resetting with reflecting gated boundaries and, separately, distinct-endpoint splitting; that is not approach-side marking at one transparent interface. Search of additional literature and mathematical-finding records revealed no clear equivalent. Residual risk: broader Markov-modulated diffusion boundary theory or an independently derived splitting formula could imply the result; the source's full time-transform formulas were not proved logically insufficient to reconstruct other observables.

**Value — PASS.** The source itself motivates the unresolved directional capture statistic through simulations; an exact formula specifies this natural observable without timestep bias, converts reset and gate-speed trends into theorems, and exposes a genuine singularity in the fast-gating interpretation of a path-direction marking. The high-reset distance layer quantifies when the opposite-side signal remains observable. This is a self-contained structural finding rather than an isolated numerical increment.

**Scientific limits.** Results apply only to one-dimensional Brownian motion, a stationary or specified initial gate, positive Markov switch rates, particle-only Poisson resetting, and transparent inactivity. There is no claim for other transport laws or finite-time joint first-passage statistics. The authorship and scientific priority of this derived statement have not been independently assessed.
