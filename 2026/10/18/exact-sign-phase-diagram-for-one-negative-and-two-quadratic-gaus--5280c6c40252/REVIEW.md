# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
Conditional bivariate normal moments reduce the problem to a fourth-order Wick identity and two gamma-recursion moment ratios. Substitution and expansion give the stated correlation polynomial exactly; no finite computation is used as an infinite proof. The strict equicorrelation sign classification follows from positivity for negative correlations and monotonicity in the negative exponent for positive correlations. The included exact rational checker independently verifies the polynomial reduction and representative PSD triples.

## Originality (O): PASS on inspected evidence
Russell and Sun (arXiv:2205.10231) settle the two-dimensional opposite inequality. Ogasawara's structurally constrained multivariate comparison (author-supplied 2024 preprint) proves regimes but does not present the exact full-correlation necessary-and-sufficient sign locus; its scraped formulas are partly inaccessible, so this remains a residual risk. Lan, Ouimet and Sun (arXiv:2505.09976, Theorems 1 and 2) prove a covariance-corrected lower bound and a convex-function upper bound, but neither implies a precise sign classification against the product of separate moments. Searches in the published-finding corpus record system and targeted mathematical-literature searches revealed no matching claim. Search absence is not proof of originality.

## Value (V): PASS
The dimension-two mixed-sign Gaussian product inequality is uniform in the correlation; adding a second positive quadratic factor is the first natural setting in which this uniform direction fails. Rather than an isolated negative example, the exact correlation-domain classification and sharp exponent transition specify when either inequality and equality hold. This is a natural complete distributional-moment classification in a motivated problem, not a numerical parameter tweak.

## Limits
Only one negative exponent in \((-1,0)\) and two quadratic positive factors are covered. Independent verification remains outstanding; later unindexed equivalent formulae remain an originality risk.
