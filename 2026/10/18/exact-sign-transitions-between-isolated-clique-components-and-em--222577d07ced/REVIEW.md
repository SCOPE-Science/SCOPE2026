# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Conditioning on an isolated complete component fixes precisely the interior and boundary edges of its vertex set, leaving all complementary edges independent. The conditional clique expectation splits into a deterministic within-component term and an independent complementary term. Subtracting the ordinary clique expectation and summing over component candidates yields the exact covariance. The strict threshold signs, scaling exponent and seven-vertex reversal follow algebraically. An independent enumeration of all graphs through six vertices and exact threshold inequalities provided 52,475 checks. Enumeration is not relied upon as a proof for arbitrary size.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Comparison with the actual complete HTML texts of Janson–Knuth–Łuczak–Pittel (1993) and Bhamidi–Budhiraja–Wang (2012) found component-counting and critical-regime structures, but not the stated isolated-clique/component-vs-unrestricted-clique covariance or its finite-size sign diagram. The complete HTML of the globally clique-component-conditioned model of Gösgens et al. (2024) describes a distinct conditional ensemble. Abstract-level comparisons with Barbour et al. (1990) concern maximal rather than isolated cliques. No directly encompassing published formula was identified in the examined sources, but general graph-count overlay polynomials or other joint generating functions may imply this elementary conditioning identity. This is a best-knowledge literature assessment, not a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The count of fully connected isolated communities and the count of complete motifs are two natural but inequivalent network observables. The formula classifies their correlation signs for every pair of clique orders and every independent-edge density, identifies a distinct scale \(n^{-2/s}\) for each motif order, and exhibits a finite-size reversal showing that the eventual threshold hierarchy is not a universal finite-n monotonicity. It is a complete natural dependence classification, not merely an isolated numerical example.

## Scientific limitations
No heterogeneous-edge or arbitrary-motif claim is made. The conditioning identity may be derivable from classical general counting methods. Independent verification and historical-priority assessment remain outstanding.
