# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The argument writes each Kneser-vertex closed-neighborhood sum as \(T_{ij}=W-s_i-s_j+2f_{ij}\), then sums over a fixed ground-set element. This forbids weight \(2\), forces an extremal signed incidence sum \(s_i=2\) at weight \(3\), and thereby forces odd ground-set order and the odd extremal partition. When all incidence sums are at most \(1\), the explicit slack count gives a contradiction. The two stated constructions meet the exact label, neighborhood-sum, and negative-vertex defender requirements in all claimed orders. The odd characterization explicitly retains the internal negative-edge defender clause, which is not implied solely by local label sums. Finite checks agree with the constructions and initial enumerations, but the proof is independent of them.

## Originality — PASS
Zec and Grbić (2022/2023), Theorem 3, establish only an interval of length one for odd orders and length three for even orders: lower bound \(2\), upper bounds \(3\) and \(5\) respectively. Their exact cases \(n=9,10,11\), and their odd-order cycle-based witnesses, are acknowledged as prior. The new lower-bound rigidity establishes exact values for all sufficiently large orders, the even construction sharpens the known upper bound, and the odd structural classification is not implied by the cited bound or isolated computations. Searches of public mathematical records and related signed-Roman terminology found no decisive stronger covering theorem. The possibility of equivalent work outside the inspected records remains.

## Value — PASS
The theorem settles a concrete published quantitative problem over an infinite canonical Kneser-graph family, reducing both parity intervals to exact answers. It also describes all minimum odd-order functions, identifying why the parity obstruction appears and furnishing natural first optimizer counts. The incidence-slack argument is structural rather than a numerical recomputation or routine special case.
