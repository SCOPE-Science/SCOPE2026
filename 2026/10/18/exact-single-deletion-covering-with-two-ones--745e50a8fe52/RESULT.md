# Exact single-deletion covering with two ones

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), consider a binary code \(C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\), with every word containing exactly two occurrences of \(1\). Write \(D_1(x)\) for the **set of distinct** length-\(n-1\) words obtainable by deleting exactly one coordinate of \(x\). Require
\[
\bigcup_{x\in C}D_1(x)=\{y\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}:\operatorname{wt}(y)\in\{1,2\}\}.
\]
Then the exact minimum is
\[
\boxed{K_2(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n(n-1)}6\right\rfloor.}
\]
The target space is the **attainable constant-composition deletion universe**, not every binary word of length \(n-1\). For \(n\ge4\), one optimum consists of the words whose two \(1\)-positions \(0\le i<j<n\) obey \(i+j\equiv0\pmod 3\). For \(n=3\), the sole word with support \(\{0,2\}\) is optimal.

## Assumptions and scope

Positions are zero-based, words are linear rather than circular, and one deletion is exact; deleting either a zero or a one is allowed. Repeated deletion outputs are counted once. The alphabet is binary and the transmitted words have weight exactly two. Only \(n\ge3\) is asserted. This is a covering problem, not an error-correction or unique-decoding assertion.

## Proof

**Upper bound.** Represent the weight-two input by its positions \((i,j)\), with \(0\le i<j<n\). A received weight-two word with ones at \((a,b)\), \(0\le a<b\le n-2\), has exactly the following three weight-two supersequences of length \(n\):
\[
(a,b),\qquad(a,b+1),\qquad(a+1,b+1).
\]
They arise by inserting a zero after, between, or before the two ones; their coordinate sums form three consecutive integers. Exactly one satisfies \(i+j\equiv0\pmod3\).

A received weight-one word with its one at \(t\) is generated by deleting the other one of a selected word. For \(n\ge4\), if \(t=0\) use the three candidate supports \((0,1),(0,2),(0,3)\); if \(t=n-2\) use \((n-4,n-1),(n-3,n-1),(n-2,n-1)\). For \(1\le t\le n-3\), use \((t-1,t+1),(t,t+1),(t,t+2)\). In every case the three coordinate sums are consecutive, so precisely one candidate belongs to the residue-zero code. Its deletion output contains the prescribed weight-one word. For \(n=3\), the input `101` deletes to `11`, `10`, and `01`.

To count the residue-zero supports, distribute the positions into their three residue classes modulo three. Directly counting pairs gives the following values for \(n=3k,3k+1,3k+2\), respectively:
\[
\frac{3k^2-k}{2},\qquad\frac{3k^2+k}{2},\qquad\frac{3k^2+3k}{2}.
\]
Each is \(\lfloor n(n-1)/6\rfloor\), establishing the upper bound.

**Lower bound: a rational weighted certificate valid for every length.** Put \(d=n-2\) and \(v=-1/2\). Represent a pair \(0\le i<j<n\) by the triple
\[
(i,\ j-i-1,\ n-1-j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3
\]
of coordinate sum \(d\). A received weight-two word is similarly represented by a triple of sum \(d-1\). Its three possible inputs correspond exactly to incrementing one of its three coordinates. Thus any covering code hits all the triples of sum \(d-1\) through the three possible coordinate increments.

For a triple \(u=(a,b,c)\) of nonnegative integers with \(a+b+c=d-1\), define \(p(s)=\max\{v^s,0\}\) and
\[
q_0(u)=\frac13+\frac{v^a+v^b+v^c}{6},\quad
r(u)=\frac1{12}\sum_{h:\,u_h=0}\ \sum_{j\ne h}p(u_j),\quad
q(u)=q_0(u)-r(u).
\]
All these expressions are rational. The weights \(q(u)\) are nonnegative. Indeed, if all coordinates are positive then \(q=q_0\ge1/12\); if exactly one is zero then \(q_0\ge1/3\), while \(r\le1/24\); if exactly two are zero then \(q_0\ge7/12\), while \(r\le5/24\); if all are zero (only \(d=1\)), then \(q=1/3\).

For every input triple \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\) of sum \(d\), we claim
\[
\sum_{h:x_h>0}q(x-e_h)\le1.
\]
If all three coordinates are positive, the sum of the \(q_0\)-terms equals exactly \(1\), since \(v^{s-1}+2v^s=0\) for \(s\ge1\); subtracting the nonnegative \(r\)-terms cannot increase it. If exactly one coordinate is zero, write the other two as \(b,c\ge1\). The two \(q_0\)-terms sum to
\[
1+\frac{v^{b-1}+v^{c-1}}{12}.
\]
Their two correction terms jointly contribute at least \((p(b-1)+p(c-1))/12\), so the corrected sum is at most one. If two coordinates vanish, there is only one descendant of weight two, whose weight is at most \(q_0\le5/6\). Hence the claim holds in every case.

Let \(N=\binom n2=\binom{d+2}{2}\). Summing the uncorrected weights over all triples of sum \(d-1\) and using the finite geometric-derivative identity gives
\[
\sum_u q_0(u)=\frac N3-\frac29(1-v^{d+1}).
\]
The total correction, including multiplicity at faces and corners, is exactly
\[
\sum_u r(u)=\frac12\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}p(s)<\frac23.
\]
As \(d\ge1\), the deficit in the first equation is at most \(5/18\). Consequently
\[
\sum_uq(u)>\frac N3-\frac{17}{18}>\frac N3-1.
\]
Every selected input covers output triples of total weight at most one, while every output triple must be covered. Summing their inequalities yields \(\lvert C\rvert\ge\sum_uq(u)\). Because the left side is an integer, this implies \(\lvert C\rvert\ge\lfloor N/3\rfloor\), as required. The proof uses only the weight-two outputs for this lower bound; the weight-one outputs were checked separately in the upper construction.

## Relationship to prior work

Deletion-covering codes were introduced systematically by Lenz, Rashtchian, Siegel, and Yaakobi, who proved general bounds and used weighted deletion-ball inequalities. Xie, Sun, and Ge developed covering-code bounds and constructions, including a large-alphabet hypergraph approach. Their statements concern the unrestricted received-word space, rather than the exact minimum for the weight-two transmitted class and its attainable one-deletion outputs. The present result combines a three-residue construction with a specialized, explicit all-length fractional-dual certificate. The general linear-programming lower-bound principle is not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to exactly two ones, binary words, a single deletion and the feasible received weights one or two. It makes no assertion for unrestricted deletion covering, other fixed weights, more deletions, insertion covering, or unique decodability. The literature comparison is based on inspected relevant texts and searches; an equivalent priority result in unindexed fixed-composition literature cannot be ruled out. No independent validation has been performed.
