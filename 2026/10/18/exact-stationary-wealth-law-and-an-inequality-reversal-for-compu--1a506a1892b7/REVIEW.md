# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The source's infinite-population transition law is reproduced as a discrete-time single-server Poisson queue. Negative drift for positive wealth proves positive recurrence at fixed arrival rate. Stationary generating-function algebra yields the unique admissible conserved-mean arrival rate; exact derivatives yield mean and variance; the survival-series Gini identity and the exact inequality \((2-\sqrt2)^2>1/3\) establish the sign of the benchmark comparison without numeric experiments. The extracted standalone checker supplies an independently executable high-precision finite-term calculation and analytic truncation enclosure. The infinite-state argument is in the proof, not inferred from code.

**Originality — PASS, subject to stated prior-art risk.** The motivating full text derives the mean-field recurrence and reports numerical inequality statistics, but does not provide the exact closure or the like-for-like standard discrete Gini comparison. Classical M/D/1 formulas explicitly cover the fixed-rate queue, and they are not claimed as novel. Sakagawa studies other exchange mechanisms. Searches for the precise compulsory parallel unit-payment process combined with queueing and geometric-Gini comparisons did not reveal a matching published result; negative searches are not proof of novelty. An undiscovered application of M/D/1 results to the same economic model could cover this result.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The paper's central outcome is a stationary inequality comparison. An exact mean-preserving stationary distribution and variance, coupled with a rigorous reversal of the benchmark Gini ordering, changes the interpretation of this specific mechanism rather than merely checking an isolated coefficient. The positive-wealth fraction and poverty fraction are exact for all positive conserved means. This does not establish empirical welfare or a comparison under all possible baselines.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Liuh, arXiv:2511.08202v1, Sections 2–5, provides the exact model and master equation. Nakagawa (2005) covers the generic unit-service Poisson queue stationary law; the model-specific mean closure and Gini comparison require additional work. Sakagawa, arXiv:2501.02490v1, concerns different microscopic exchanges. The theorem applies only to fixed points of the Poisson mean-field map; it does not prove finite-agent stationary limits or global nonlinear convergence. The 2005 journal full text was not accessible in this session; its classical result was checked against accessible queueing formulas and bibliographic information.
