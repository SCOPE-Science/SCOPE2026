# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The lower bound is proved from the behaviour of a single colour class: two adjacent occupied columns force an alternating entire row-parity pattern, contradicting odd cyclic closure. The resulting maximum class size gives the sharp five- or six-colour obstruction. Bipartite even prisms require only two colours; two explicit five-colour square-colouring base blocks with period-four insertion give all odd orders at least five. The 13 possible three-column local configurations were inspected and checked by an independent verifier. The exact domain includes the triangular-prism exception; finite computation is corroboration, not proof of the infinite claim.

## Originality — PASS (subject to remaining source-access uncertainty)
The compared graph-product bound in Caro et al. (arXiv:2410.02336, Theorem 4.2) is submultiplicative, not an exact formula for \(C_n\square K_2\). Caro et al. (arXiv:2509.20763, Proposition 1) supply the general odd independence–chromatic inequality; their companion grid paper (arXiv:2510.01897) does not present the exact prism determination. Pradhan et al. (arXiv:2610.01441, 2026-10-01) discuss complexity and special algorithms without identifying this exact family in the accessible abstract. No direct equivalent theorem emerged from searches for square-colouring and strong odd prism aliases. An unlocated prior distance-two formula or an inaccessible result in the focal preprint could affect priority and remains a material literature risk.

## Value — PASS
The result determines a natural graph invariant on an entire cubic Cartesian-product family, including an unexpected dichotomy between trivial bipartite two-colourings and optimal nonbipartite five-colourings, and the small triangular exception. It supplies a uniform constant-width colouring construction and explains the lower obstruction structurally. This exact infinite classification is more informative than general product upper bounds and can benchmark strong odd colouring algorithms on bounded-width cyclic graphs.

## Limitations
The result does not classify Möbius ladders or general cubic graphs. Formal independent validation and definitive publication priority remain outstanding; a complete full-text inspection of the October 2026 complexity preprint could not be completed because its full text was inaccessible.
