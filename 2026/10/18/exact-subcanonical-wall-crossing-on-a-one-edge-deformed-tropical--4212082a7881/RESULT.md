# Exact subcanonical wall crossing on a one-edge-deformed tropical tetrahedron

## Finding

Let \(\Gamma_\lambda\) be the compact, unweighted-vertex metric graph with combinatorial graph \(K_4\) and vertices \(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\). Give the edge \(v_0v_1\) length \(\lambda>0\), and give each of the other five edges length \(1\). Its genus is \(3\) and its canonical divisor is \(K=v_0+v_1+v_2+v_3\). Call \(p\in\Gamma_\lambda\) **subcanonical** when \(K\sim4p\) under metric-graph linear equivalence by continuous piecewise-affine functions with integer slopes.

The **complete subcanonical locus** is

\[
\{p\in\Gamma_\lambda:K\sim4p\}=
\begin{cases}
\{v_2,v_3\},&0<\lambda<1,\\
\{v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\},&\lambda=1,\\
\{v_2,v_3,a_\lambda,b_\lambda\},&\lambda>1,
\end{cases}
\]

where \(a_\lambda,b_\lambda\) are the two interior points of \(v_0v_1\), measured from \(v_0\), with respective coordinates

\[
t(a_\lambda)=\frac{\lambda-1}{4},\qquad
 t(b_\lambda)=\frac{3\lambda+1}{4}.
\]

In particular, shortening one edge from the equilateral tetrahedron removes two subcanonical points, while lengthening it replaces the two endpoints of that edge with two interior subcanonical points. The statement holds for **every real** \(\lambda>0\), including irrational values; the sampled computations cited below are not used to infer this universal quantifier.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs here are compact metric graphs with zero vertex-genus weights (no added genus at any vertex). A divisor is a finite integral formal sum of graph points. For a continuous piecewise-affine function with integral slopes, its principal divisor is the sum of outgoing slopes at each point. Linear equivalence is the relation generated by these principal divisors. A point in the interior of an edge has valence \(2\), and each original vertex has valence \(3\), so \(K=\sum_i v_i\). The condition \(K\sim4p\) is stricter than being an arbitrary tropical canonical Weierstrass point; no classification of the entire canonical Weierstrass locus is asserted.

Use the global orientation \(i\to j\) when \(i<j\). If \(p\) is in the interior of \(v_iv_j\), let \(t\) be its distance from \(v_i\), with \(0<t<\ell(v_iv_j)\). Slopes in the proof are measured in this orientation, with the edge carrying \(p\) split at \(p\).

## Proof

**1. Two permanent subcanonical vertices.** Fix \(i\in\{2,3\}\). Assign a piecewise-affine function the value \(0\) at \(v_i\) and the value \(1\) at the other three vertices, extending affinely across every edge. Each of the three edges incident to \(v_i\) has length \(1\). The resulting outgoing-slope sum is \(3\) at \(v_i\) and \(-1\) at each other vertex, with no interior divisor. Thus the function has divisor \(4v_i-K\), proving \(K\sim4v_i\) for all \(\lambda>0\).

**2. Reduction to an integral network problem.** By step 1, a graph point \(p\) is subcanonical if and only if \(4p-4v_2\) is a principal divisor. There is a unique continuous piecewise-affine **real-slope** function with divisor \(4v_2-4p\), up to addition of a constant: on the finite graph obtained by inserting \(p\) as a vertex, solve the weighted graph-Laplacian equations with conductances equal to reciprocal edge lengths; uniqueness follows because the graph is connected and the kernel of its Laplacian consists of constants. A piecewise-affine function with divisor supported only at \(p\) and the original vertices cannot introduce any additional slope change inside an open edge. Therefore \(4p-4v_2\) is principal over the metric graph precisely when **all slopes of that unique real solution are integers**. The sign chosen for the Laplacian right-hand side only reverses all slopes and does not change integrality.

**3. Exclude interiors of the five unit edges.** For \(p\) on any one of the five unit edges, write \(0<t<1\) as above. Solving the four independent node equations after grounding \(v_2\) yields the following particular oriented slopes (one per case):

| Edge containing \(p\) | Unsplit edge | Slope of the real solution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(v_0v_2\) | \(v_2v_3\) | \(1-t\) |
| \(v_1v_2\) | \(v_2v_3\) | \(1-t\) |
| \(v_0v_3\) | \(v_2v_3\) | \(1+t\) |
| \(v_1v_3\) | \(v_2v_3\) | \(1+t\) |
| \(v_2v_3\) | \(v_0v_2\) | \(-t\) |

All five displayed slopes are nonintegers when \(0<t<1\). Hence no interior point of a unit edge is subcanonical. These identities are independent of \(\lambda\); they can also be recovered directly by eliminating the node potentials from the conductivity matrix described in step 2.

**4. Solve along the variable edge.** Let \(p\) lie in the open edge \(v_0v_1\), distance \(t\) from \(v_0\). Solving the same weighted Laplacian, and writing

\[
k=\frac{4t-3\lambda-1}{2(\lambda+1)},
\]

gives the following complete slope vector, oriented from lower to higher numbered vertices and with the variable edge split as \(v_0\to p\to v_1\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
\text{segment}&02&03&12&13&23&0p&p1\\\hline
\text{slope}&k-1&k&-k-2&-k-1&1&1-2k&-3-2k
\end{array}.
\]

For instance, the potential differences on the intact unit edges give \(U_2-U_0=k-1\), \(U_3-U_0=k\), \(U_2-U_1=-k-2\), \(U_3-U_1=-k-1\), and \(U_3-U_2=1\); hence \(U_1-U_0=2k+1\). Integrating the two displayed slopes along the split edge gives the compatibility equation

\[
(1-2k)t+(-3-2k)(\lambda-t)=2k+1,
\]

which is equivalent to the displayed expression for \(k\). The outgoing-slope balances at the five nodes match the divisor \(4v_2-4p\); these relations therefore give the unique real solution. Since one intact edge has slope exactly \(k\) and every other slope is an integer affine function of \(k\), integrality is **equivalent** to \(k\in\mathbb Z\).

As \(0<t<\lambda\),

\[
-\frac{3\lambda+1}{2(\lambda+1)}<k<\frac{\lambda-1}{2(\lambda+1)}.
\]

The open interval is contained in \((-3/2,1/2)\), so the only possible integers are \(-1\) and \(0\). Solving for \(t\) gives

\[
k=-1\iff t=\frac{\lambda-1}{4},\qquad
k=0\iff t=\frac{3\lambda+1}{4}.
\]

Both coordinates lie strictly between \(0\) and \(\lambda\) if and only if \(\lambda>1\). They give distinct interior points. There are no interior solutions when \(0<\lambda\le1\).

**5. Check the remaining vertices.** At \(p=v_0\), take the limit \(t\to0\) in the unique real solution along the variable edge: its intact-edge slope is

\[
k_0=-\frac{3\lambda+1}{2(\lambda+1)}\in(-3/2,-1/2).
\]

It is an integer only if \(k_0=-1\), which is equivalent to \(\lambda=1\). By the symmetry exchanging \(v_0\) and \(v_1\), the same holds for \(v_1\). Together with steps 1, 3, and 4, this exhausts every graph point and completes the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Amini, Gierczak, and Richman explicitly state that the canonical Weierstrass locus of the equilateral metric \(K_4\) consists of its four vertices, each of weight two (2023 preprint, Example 3.5). The present result recovers precisely that symmetric case and classifies the stricter condition \(K\sim4p\) throughout its simplest edge-length deformation.

Amini and Kawaguchi (2026) establish for non-hyperelliptic tropical genus-three curves that the canonical gap sequence \((1,2,5)\) is equivalent to \(K\sim4p\), and investigate maximal Weierstrass components and weights. Their general arguments do not specify the two threshold-dependent interior coordinates or the complete subcanonical locus in this one-edge-deformed family. In particular, their bounds on **maximal Weierstrass points** are not counts of subcanonical points and are not contradicted here.

Chan and Jiradilok (2015) analyze the seven effective tropical theta-characteristic classes and specialization of algebraic bitangents on general metric \(K_4\) graphs. A general description of effective theta classes, represented by degree-two divisors, does not by itself identify the degree-one points \(p\) whose doubled divisor satisfies \(2(2p)\sim K\); the particular exact locus and wall crossing proved here require the integer-slope calculation above.

## Limitations

The statement concerns the full continuum of edge lengths \(\lambda>0\), but only one varying edge and only subcanonical points. It does not determine the entire tropical canonical Weierstrass locus, Weierstrass weights, algebraic lifts, or the case of six independently variable edge lengths. No claim is made that the four located points exhaust other, weaker notions of Weierstrass points, or that the result follows from the finite test grid alone.
