# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact alphabet, palindrome convention, and subsequence interpretation are fixed. Barker et al.'s explicitly stated palindrome-index formula (Proposition 15) reduces the problem to the half-word's greedy arch count and remainder support. Counting minimal completion blocks and incomplete remainders gives the displayed rational bivariate series. The sharp minimum lengths and their multiplicities follow from forcing every arch to be a permutation and, in the odd case, using the unique missing center. Classical renewal theorems supply the fixed-alphabet normal law with the correct factor-two variance adjustment. Direct independent-subsequence and exact-coefficient tests corroborate the proof; no finite test is presented as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The central symmetry lemma was already proved by Barker et al. (2020), and is excluded from the claimed contribution. The nearest inspected works concern that index lemma, regular-language counting (Adamson et al., 2023), and arch-factorization algorithms (Schnoebelen and Veron, 2023). No inspected primary statement or research-record result gives the full all-alphabet palindromic length-index bivariate series, exact minimum-length counts or random-palindrome CLT. These are compared by implications rather than titles. An unindexed derivation or a result in unavailable literature is possible, and the enumeration follows from combining known ingredients.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Palindromes form a canonical nonregular symmetry-constrained language. Determining their full subsequence-universality distribution at every length and for every finite alphabet is a natural complete enumeration, while the sharp extreme multiplicities and limiting fluctuation law characterize both the extremal and typical regimes. These facts can be used as concrete benchmarks for symmetric constrained-subsequence problems, beyond the previously available index test for individual palindromes. The value is in this complete profile, not in claiming a new symmetry lemma.

**Closest literature and limits.** Barker et al., arXiv:2003.04629, Proposition 15, is a decisive prior source for the palindrome-index reduction, so no priority is claimed for that reduction. Adamson et al., arXiv:2311.10658, works with regular languages; the full palindrome language is nonregular for alphabets of size at least two. Schnoebelen and Veron, arXiv:2304.11932, investigate computational arch representations rather than the displayed enumerator. The conclusions are restricted to fixed alphabet size in the probability limit, and no claim of literature exhaustiveness is made.
