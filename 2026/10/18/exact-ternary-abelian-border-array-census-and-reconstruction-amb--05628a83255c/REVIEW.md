The restricted-growth enumeration is bijective with words modulo global alphabet relabeling. Every candidate border length is checked exactly; the four-bit encoding cannot collide for lengths at most sixteen. Its ternary bound is not an exact count, and its results do not specify the complete length-sixteen inverse-fiber distribution. The other relevant published works concern binary words or individual border statistics, not equivalence classes of complete ternary all-prefix arrays. Searches of relevant mathematical records and exact numerical formulations produced no covering statement. Unindexed equivalent tables or stronger work not discovered in the search remain residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The source article explicitly identifies larger-alphabet array inference and enumeration as difficult beyond the binary case. The census supplies sixteen exact initial terms of the central, naturally defined ternary enumeration problem, rather than one arbitrary example. The full length-sixteen histogram quantifies how the \(7{,}174{,}454\) canonical words collapse onto \(1{,}151{,}825\) arrays and identifies \(468{,}549\) singleton reconstruction classes; those finer data cannot be inferred from the source's upper bound. The separate all-length extremal-fiber conclusion gives an exact sharp worst-case reconstruction-ambiguity guarantee. These are useful benchmarks for evaluating ternary string-inference conjectures. They do not resolve the unrestricted enumeration problem.
