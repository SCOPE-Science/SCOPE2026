# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact inverse branches \(\phi_i(u)=c_i+\varepsilon_i\ell_i(u-1/2)\) induce \(Pg=\theta g\) and \(Ph=Ah+Bg\), with \(g=u-1/2\) and \(h=g^2-1/12\). Iterating the triangular two-dimensional polynomial action, applying Perron duality twice and using \(\int gh=0\) and \(\int g^2=1/12\) proves every strictly positive two-lag moment without exceptional denominators. The \(A>0\) boundary establishes necessity for gap-reversal equality, including degenerate \(\theta=0\) and \(B=0\) cases. Exact independent rational cylinder integration confirms 49 representative comparisons, but finitely many tests are not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority not independently certified)
The 2006 Lawrance–Papamarkou proceedings gives linear and quadratic *two-time* ACFs on exactly the same maps; the 2014 Papamarkou–Lawrance paper further evaluates quadratic ACFs and discusses mixed *two-time* moments. Thomine's 2010 full transfer-operator treatment addresses spectral gaps and mixing, not an evaluated three-time cubic tensor. The new claim concerns two distinct positive gaps, a complete reversal criterion, and an explicit pair of maps sharing both existing autocorrelation series while exhibiting differing cubic time correlations. Comparisons with published research records and accessible historical claims found no encompassing evaluated theorem; failure to find one is not proof of global priority. Mathematical ease of deriving the formula from earlier transfer methods and possible earlier statistics-literature formulations remain substantive risks.

## Value — PASS
The prior pair-correlation statistics cannot distinguish the two explicit four-branch systems: both have identical linear and quadratic ACFs at all lags. The three-time tensor separates them, yielding a natural, fully evaluated dynamical information invariant instead of an arbitrary finite parameter slice. The exact time-gap-reversal classification and a sharp extremal skew-tent parameter quantify detectable nonlinear temporal directionality. No claim is made that third-order symmetry entails a reversible process.

## Closest literature and scope limitations
Lawrance–Papamarkou (NOLTA 2006, equations (1)–(3)) and Papamarkou–Lawrance (2014, DOI 10.1142/S0218127414500710) supply the existing linear/quadratic ACF foundation. The theorem only addresses the stationary centered-coordinate third moments of full-branch affine interval maps; it does not classify full time reversibility, arbitrary observables, or nonlinear/non-covering branches. A broader existing polynomial-transfer result may imply it and remains a priority risk.
