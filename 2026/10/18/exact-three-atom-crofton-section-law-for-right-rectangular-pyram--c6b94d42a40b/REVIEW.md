# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For arbitrary \(a,b,h>0\), the plane law is the standard invariant Crofton law and has finite total mass \(4Z\) over intersecting oriented planes. The proof classifies which separations of the five pyramid vertices produce five crossed edges, evaluates two full positive-octant spherical integrals, obtains the pentagonal mass, and determines the two remaining masses from the edge-count mean and normalization. Quantifiers, degenerate planes, and scale factors are addressed explicitly. The independent code checks both analytic integral identities with numerical quadrature and compares all three probabilities with 825,000 width-weighted plane samples. Simulations alone would not prove the formulas; the analytical steps do.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence
The accessible complete Socha (1988) and Feldman–Klain (2008) papers treat section angles and orthogonal projections, respectively. A closely matched rectangular-pyramid shadow result concerns a different probability experiment. No accessible inspected text establishes the same shape-dependent three-class Crofton section law. Voss (1982) does describe exactly this *type* of stereological frequency calculation for unspecified polyhedron families, but its full text is inaccessible. There is genuine residual priority uncertainty as to whether a square or rectangle pyramid was included; the inaccessible work is **not** represented as non-covering. This is a best-knowledge comparison rather than definitive uniqueness certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full natural side-count law for all right rectangular pyramids is a geometrically motivated two-parameter classification of random physical sections. The explicit pentagon measure requires a new positive-part chamber calculation; an edge-crossing identity alone supplies only the mean. All three atoms remain positive, contrasting with degenerating limiting geometries. The result has potential stereological use where section shapes, not shadows, are observed.

## Limitations and closest literature
Voss (1982), *Frequencies of n-polygons in planar sections of polyhedrons*, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2818.1982.tb00443.x, remains uninspected beyond its publisher abstract, and old numerical tables may overlap. Socha (1988) addresses angular distributions and Feldman–Klain (2008) addresses projections; neither is being cited as the source of the derived arctangent law. The theorem is for right rectangular pyramids, not oblique pyramids or other random-plane schemes. Scientific calculations have not received independent mathematical audit.
