# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof treats all nonnegative exponent pairs, establishes necessity by placing the largest divisor in one class, and reduces the full parameter region by an exact multiplicative inequality. Sufficiency uses exactly thirteen explicitly enumerated, disjoint, equal-sum weighted tile assignments. The standard-library verifier checks every exact tile equality and 256 further complete small parameter cases. Translation multiplies each weight by the same integer; a Cartesian product of one-dimensional length decompositions covers the entire exponent rectangle without overlap. No finite-range extrapolation is used in the universal step.

## Originality — PASS, within inspected evidence
Mahanta–Saikia–Yaqubi, arXiv:2008.11096v1, Theorem 3.3, proves the \(b=1\) part. Jokar, arXiv:2207.09053v1, Proposition 2.7 and Section 5, discusses a distinct exponent-progression closure and some individual examples, not the exact all-\(b\) criterion. Searches against mathematical-result records and indexed primary literature found no stronger theorem applying to the complete family. Previously known boundary cases are expressly attributed. Unindexed overlapping work remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
This closes an arithmetically natural two-parameter extension of a published three-layered classification, showing that the simple necessary abundance inequality becomes sufficient for the full exponent family. The proof's finite tile decomposition is a constructive structural mechanism rather than a census of isolated cases. The sharp new boundary \((a,b)=(2,2)\) is not implied by the special \(b=1\) result.

## Scientific limits
Other fixed odd primes and higher exponents of five are not classified, and the number of partitions is not counted. Independent review may reveal additional relevant literature.
