# Scientific review: cuboctahedral spherical coverage law

## Correctness — PASS
Direct signed-coordinate enumeration gives 24 nearest-neighbor pairs, eight equilateral face triples, no four-cliques and a gap to all other pairs. Thus the cap union is exhausted by one-, two- and three-fold areas until the sharp covering radius. Spherical Gauss–Bonnet derives the lens and curvilinear-triangle terms; explicit Voronoi inequalities prove the endpoint. Tangent-plane blow-ups at the eight face centers and six axial farthest directions give the quadratic triple onset and the quadratic/cubic endpoint coefficients. Independent one-dimensional numerical quadratures, exact incidence checks, quasirandom-sphere integration, and high-precision boundary tests support the derivation. They are finite corroboration, not proofs of infinite assertions.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected statements
Fowler, Tarnai and Kabai (2005) explicitly study the **same cuboctahedral twelve sites** at their 30-degree nonoverlap packing radius. This is a critical prior premise, not a newly claimed packing discovery. Their analyzed problem does not give the present all-radius cap-union law; in particular it does not evaluate the two overlapping regimes or the endpoint tail. Fowler, Tarnai and Gáspár (2002) optimize six antipodal pairs and select a different icosahedral configuration. Kimberling (2011) gives a general spherical nearest-site construction and nearest-vertex means for the five Platonic solids, not this Archimedean full CDF. Koay (2012) addresses spherical Voronoi computation. A published simplex-centered cap-triple note treats a different Gram geometry and dimension; a Euclidean cuboctahedral Vietoris–Rips record treats topology rather than coverage. The detailed implication comparisons and inspected scopes appear in `AUDIT.json`. Unindexed old covering formulas remain a historical-priority uncertainty, and the absence of a result from a search is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The twelve cuboctahedral sites are the natural face-centered-cubic kissing shell. Their fixed spherical covering radius alone misses the distribution of near and far directions, a natural quantization/coverage question. The explicit CDF describes that entire invariant, with two sharp overlap thresholds, a genuinely quadratic triple-onset coefficient and a two-term tail at six worst-covered directions. Neither a general probabilistic identity nor a single numerical packing value supplies these facts without the evaluated geometric argument.

## Limits
The law concerns uniform spherical area and twelve fixed cuboctahedral directions, not optimal arrangements of twelve sites. No independent audit or exhaustive historical priority certification is claimed.
