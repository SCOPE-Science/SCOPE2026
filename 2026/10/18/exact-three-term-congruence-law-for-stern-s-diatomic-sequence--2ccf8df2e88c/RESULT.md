# Exact three-term congruence law for Stern's diatomic sequence

## Finding
Let \(s(0)=0\), \(s(1)=1\), \(s(2n)=s(n)\) for \(n\ge1\), and \(s(2n+1)=s(n)+s(n+1)\) for \(n\ge0\). Fix any integer modulus \(m\ge2\) and residue classes \(a,b,c\pmod m\). The natural density
\[
\mu_m(a,b,c)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\#\{0\le n<N:(s(n),s(n+1),s(n+2))\equiv(a,b,c)\pmod m\}
\]
exists and has the following exact closed form. Define
\[
J_2(m)=m^2\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-2}),\quad d=\gcd(2b,m),\quad q=m/d,\quad \Delta\equiv a+c-b\pmod m.
\]
If \(\gcd(a,b,m)>1\), or if the congruence \(d\mid\Delta\) fails, then \(\mu_m(a,b,c)=0\). In every other case, when \(q>1\), let \(r\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) be the unique solution of
\[
\frac{2b}{d}r\equiv\frac{\Delta}{d}\pmod q;
\]
when \(q=1\), set \(r=0\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mu_m(a,b,c)=\frac1{J_2(m)}\frac{2^{-(r+1)}}{1-2^{-q}}.}
\]
The resulting conditional distribution of the third residue, given any admissible first two residues, is a cyclically ordered truncated geometric distribution. For example, modulo \(3\), if \(3\nmid b\), the three third-coordinate values corresponding to \(r=0,1,2\) have respective conditional probabilities \(4/7,2/7,1/7\); if \(3\mid b\), the third residue is deterministically \(-a\).

## Assumptions and scope
All densities are natural densities among nonnegative integer indices. Residues may be represented by any integers: both the divisibility test and the residue \(r\) are independent of the choice of representatives. When \(q=1\), the congruence on \(r\) has a single class, and the displayed probability simplifies to \(1/J_2(m)\). The theorem covers every integer modulus, including even and non-squarefree moduli. It gives limiting frequencies, not finite-prefix exact counts or uniform error exponents as \(m\) varies.

## Proof
**Step 1: an identity coupling three terms to the index.** For every \(n\ge0\),
\[
s(n+2)=\bigl(2\nu_2(n+1)+1\bigr)s(n+1)-s(n).
\tag{1}
\]
For even \(n=2u\), \(\nu_2(n+1)=0\), and the equation follows immediately by inserting \(s(2u)=s(u)\), \(s(2u+1)=s(u)+s(u+1)\), and \(s(2u+2)=s(u+1)\). For odd \(n=2u+1\), the desired equation, after substitution of the Stern recurrence, reduces to (1) at \(u\), since \(\nu_2(2u+2)=1+\nu_2(u+1)\). The base case \(n=0\) is immediate, so strong induction proves (1).

**Step 2: the finite primitive-pair Markov chain.** Let
\[
S_m=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^2:\gcd(x,y,m)=1\}.
\]
This set has cardinality \(J_2(m)\), by the Chinese remainder theorem. Reading the binary digits of \(n\ge1\) after its initial leading \(1\), the pair \((s(n),s(n+1))\pmod m\) evolves by the digit maps
\[
L(x,y)=(x,x+y)\quad\text{for digit }0,\qquad
R(x,y)=(x+y,y)\quad\text{for digit }1.
\tag{2}
\]
Both maps permute \(S_m\). The directed graph generated by these two maps is strongly connected: every primitive residue pair has a coprime positive integer lift, hence appears as a consecutive Stern pair in the Calkin--Wilf enumeration; and each digit map has finite order as a permutation, making every directed path reversible by positive iterates. Alternatively, these statements are Lemmas 4.1 and 4.3 and the surrounding argument in Reznick's paper [1]. The state \((1,0)\) is fixed by \(R\), so the graph is aperiodic. Giving each digit probability \(1/2\) produces a finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain. Both maps are permutations, hence the transition matrix is doubly stochastic. Its distribution therefore converges to the **uniform** law on \(S_m\) from *every* starting state, uniformly because \(S_m\) is finite.

**Step 3: independence of the terminal run.** An integer \(n\) has \(\nu_2(n+1)=t\) precisely when its binary expansion ends in a \(0\) followed by \(t\) ones (apart from the exceptional all-ones words). Within a binary cylinder of length \(2^j\), i.e., an aligned interval of indices with a fixed binary prefix and \(j\) unrestricted lower bits, for each \(0\le t<j\) exactly the fraction \(2^{-(t+1)}\) of indices end in \(0\,1^t\). Conditional on this suffix, there are \(j-t-1\) free preceding bits. By Step 2 their image state tends uniformly to \(1/J_2(m)\) on \(S_m\) as \(j\to\infty\), and applying the fixed permutation \(R^tL\) preserves this distribution. The exceptional fraction for which the lower \(j\) digits are all ones is at most \(2^{-j}\). First bound the contribution of \(t\ge T\) by \(2^{-T}\), uniformly in the fixed prefix, then send \(j\to\infty\), then \(T\to\infty\). This proves the joint limit
\[
\lim_{j\to\infty}\Pr\bigl((s(n),s(n+1))\equiv(a,b)\pmod m,\ \nu_2(n+1)=t\bigr)
=\frac{2^{-(t+1)}}{J_2(m)}
\tag{3}
\]
for every \((a,b)\in S_m\), uniformly across aligned cylinders whose length tends to infinity. States outside \(S_m\) never occur.

To obtain an ordinary natural density rather than only a dyadic-row limit, decompose the interval \([0,N)\) into aligned dyadic cylinders corresponding to the binary expansion of \(N\). For any fixed cutoff \(j_0\), the union of cylinders of length less than \(2^{j_0}\) has fewer than \(2^{j_0}\) indices. All other cylinders have the limiting law (3) with uniformly arbitrarily small error as \(j_0\to\infty\). Divide by \(N\), send \(N\to\infty\), and then \(j_0\to\infty\). This establishes the joint natural-density law stated in (3).

**Step 4: sum the valuation classes.** By (1), a given primitive pair \((a,b)\) and terminal-run length \(t\) yield
\[
c\equiv(2t+1)b-a\pmod m,
\quad\text{i.e.}\quad 2bt\equiv a+c-b\pmod m.
\]
This congruence has solutions if and only if \(d\mid\Delta\). If it has solutions, they form the single class \(t\equiv r\pmod q\). Multiplying the joint mass in (3) by the appropriate indicator and summing gives
\[
\mu_m(a,b,c)=\frac1{J_2(m)}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-(r+jq+1)}
=\frac1{J_2(m)}\frac{2^{-(r+1)}}{1-2^{-q}}.
\]
The formula is zero in the other cases. This proves all asserted limits and classifications.

## Verification
The companion file `verify.py` implements the Stern recurrence independently, checks (1) for the first \(200000\) indices, checks all exactly normalized rational probability tables for \(2\le m\le12\), verifies pair marginal masses, and directly counts every triple in a full binary row of \(65536\) indices for moduli \(3,4,6,7\). It also verifies finite-state reachability for small moduli. These finite tests support implementation and boundary handling; the infinite statement follows solely from the proof above and the standard finite-Markov-chain convergence theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Reznick [1] establishes equidistribution of *consecutive pairs* modulo every modulus, and derives explicit one-coordinate marginal densities. Equations (2) and the uniform pair limit are therefore **prior work**, not new here. The present result conditions on the terminal binary run (which is not a fixed function of the residue pair), proves the needed joint independence using free preceding digits, and then resolves the resulting infinite geometric mixture into a closed formula for every *three-term* residue specification. Stanley [2, Chapter 6] explicitly proposes congruence conditions on \(k\) consecutive Stern terms as a further question; the present theorem answers its first non-pair case, \(k=3\), not the general case. Northshield [3] reproves and extends pair-based modular distributions for other sequences, but does not give this three-coordinate Stern law. The inspected source texts contain no equivalent all-modulus triple formula.

## Limitations
The Markov-chain mixing rate depends on the fixed modulus, and no uniform-in-modulus quantitative discrepancy bound is claimed. This does not classify windows of \(k\ge4\) terms. Searches cannot establish absolute publication priority: an unindexed earlier formula, or an uninspected appendix in related digital-distribution literature, may exist.
