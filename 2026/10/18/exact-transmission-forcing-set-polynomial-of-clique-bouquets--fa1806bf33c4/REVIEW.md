# Review

## Correctness — PASS

Every initially successful seed set must leave at most one unfilled vertex in each blade. The proof exhaustively considers the number of incomplete blades, whether the hub starts filled, the exact first-round contribution to the hub, and the possible second-round contribution from the hub. This yields the full polynomial with sharp weak inequalities; summing binomial choices proves all coefficients. In particular the intermediate threshold \(T\) is a minimum of two necessary-and-sufficient round constraints. Independent exact-rational enumerations on seven graph sizes and \(337\) parameter/graph pairs confirmed the entire coefficient sequence, including boundary equality. These finite checks are corroborating evidence, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest primary source is Berliner et al., *Transmission Zero Forcing* (arXiv:2606.22246v1), whose general framework and degree bounds are used, not claimed as new. Its graph-family calculations do not give the two-parameter clique-bouquet forcing-set enumerator. Published transmission forcing on complete multipartite graphs concerns a different graph family, so it cannot cover this formula by substitution. The published ordinary zero-forcing polynomial for friendship graphs recovers the low-threshold specialization with \(m=2\); this known part is explicitly excluded from the claim of novelty. Comparisons of the assertions and actual indexed records found no result covering the full polynomial and its \(\alpha^2\) intermediate threshold. Some relevant indexed records were available only as abstracts or short summaries, and unindexed equivalent work remains a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The object is a natural infinite family of clique-sum block graphs and the invariant is the full distribution of successful forcing sets, not merely a numerical bound. The theorem completely resolves all admissible \(\alpha,\beta,k,m\) and reveals a two-round accumulation threshold, an unbounded discontinuous jump in the optimum, and explicit failure of upward closure for the successful-set family. These are substantive structural features not supplied by the existing general degree bound.

## Closest literature and limitations

The primary 2026 transmission-forcing paper provides the forcing rule and general bounds. The published complete-multipartite transmission-forcing work and the ordinary friendship-graph polynomial cover other graph structures or only a low-threshold specialization. The finding is not a global treatment of block graphs, unequal clique sizes or asynchronous forcing. The full text of every potentially related published record was not inspected; mathematical priority is therefore subject to the stated residual risk.
