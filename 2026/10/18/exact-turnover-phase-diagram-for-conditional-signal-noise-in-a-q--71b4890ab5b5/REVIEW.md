# Scientific review: conditional signal-noise turnover classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim concerns the stationary Gaussian linear-noise approximation at a stable positive equilibrium, rather than the finite-population extinction-prone chain. Starting from the source's four reaction channels, the stated stable Jacobian and noise covariance lead to three Lyapunov equations. Their explicit solution has positive denominators under the assumptions. Gaussian conditioning gives the exact rational variance formula; one derivative factorization proves all qualitative regimes and the unique interior optimum. The symbolic checker verifies the matrix equation and Schur complement; the rational and high-precision checks independently confirm the numerical benchmark. The infinite parameter classification is analytical, not inferred from finite tests.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2026 primary source states the noise approximation and reports conditional variances at three turnover rates, but the inspected material does not state a closed-form all-rate conditional-variance law, an exact feedback-gain sign boundary, or an explicit unique-maximizer criterion and value. Broader linear-noise reductions (Thomas–Straube–Grima) and feedback-noise design analysis (Oyarzún–Lugagne–Stan) concern general methods or different reaction networks. A general pre-existing two-coordinate Gaussian covariance theorem might algebraically imply the formula; that remains the principal residual originality risk. Searches alone do not prove priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The motivating model was explicitly used to compare signal-noise behavior across turnover speeds, but three sample rates cannot identify a unique maximum or determine when it disappears. The theorem gives a sharp, all-parameter decision rule for persistent above-Poisson versus below-Poisson conditional fluctuations and an exact optimal timescale when the peak exists. It helps distinguish signal-turnover effects from changes in deterministic equilibria and provides a reproducible quantitative benchmark. This is a model-specific sharp classification, not a proposed general theorem about all biochemical systems.
