# Review of the finite-field spin-factor two-idempotent theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The primitive-idempotent equations reduce the ordered factorization fiber over \(w\) exactly to the quadratic level set \(\{z\in w^\perp:Q(z)=1/4-Q(w)\}\). The proof splits the zero, anisotropic and nonzero isotropic cases. The last requires quotienting by the one-dimensional radical and recording the determinant sign, both proved explicitly. A Gauss-sum computation supplies all nondegenerate value counts, and positivity yields the dimension threshold. The standalone checker independently enumerated all pairs and all product fibers in twenty diagonal-form configurations, including extension fields and both discriminant classes. Those calculations are finite checks, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge comparison

Gogić–Tomašević, arXiv:2609.23581v1, §3 and Lemma 3.1, already give the product-graph relation over quadratically closed fields. The present claim **does not** repackage that relation as a new finding; it establishes exact multiplicities of primitive-idempotent pairs for all finite fields of odd order and pinpoints a sharp ternary discriminant obstruction which fails over quadratically closed fields. Their later higher-word-length analysis does not imply the complete two-factor finite-field fiber table. Rowen–Segev, arXiv:1609.04899, Theorem 1.2, treats the algebra **generated** by two idempotents, not the exact image or fiber counts of their Jordan product. The closest published database records returned concern idempotent–nilpotent associative matrix factorizations and other distinct tensor/finite-field enumerations, not these spin-factor fibers. A related orthogonal association-scheme intersection formula may exist unindexed; absent such a covering implication, the result passes originality subject to residual bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS

The question is motivated by a real hypothesis gap in a recent structural spin-factor idempotent-word theorem. Its answer describes every factorization multiplicity for an arbitrary nondegenerate form, all dimensions at least three and every odd finite field. The precise missing locus in the minimal dimension and its quadratic-character switch are structural, not a small arbitrary numeric example. This is a complete enumerative answer that is useful for distinguishing solvable Jordan products over non-quadratically-closed fields.

## Limitations

The result excludes characteristic two, degenerate forms, trivial idempotent factors, and arbitrary word lengths. Standard quadratic-form intersection numbers may imply the enumerative formula under alternate nomenclature, so absence in searched papers/databases is not proof of global first publication. Verification is internal and not independent refereeing.
