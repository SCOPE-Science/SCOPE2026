For binary length-\(n\) words containing exactly two ones, the largest code allowing at most \(\ell\ge2\) candidate codewords after one deletion is exactly \(n-1+\lfloor (n-1)\min(\ell-1,n-2)/2\rfloor\), except that \((n,\ell)=(3,2)\) gives two. The extremizers come from bounded-degree graphs on the \(n-1\) one-symbol deletion outputs, with a nonconsecutive matching resolving the two-list case.
