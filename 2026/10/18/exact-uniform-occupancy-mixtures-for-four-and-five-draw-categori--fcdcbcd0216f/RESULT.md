# Exact uniform-occupancy mixtures for four- and five-draw categorical richness

## Finding
For each \(n\in\{4,5\}\), take \(n\) independent draws from any categorical distribution supported on at most \(n\) labels, and write \(K_n\) for the number of distinct observed labels. Define \(U_{n,j}\) to be the number of distinct labels in \(n\) independent uniform draws from \(j\) labels, for \(1\le j\le n\). There exist unique nonnegative weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), summing to one, such that
\[
\Pr(K_n=k)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_j\Pr(U_{n,j}=k),\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.
\]
Consequently, for each of these two sample sizes, the convex set of distinct-count laws attainable by mixtures of categorical i.i.d. laws with at most \(n\) labels is **exactly the simplex** with vertices given by the \(n\) uniform occupancy laws. The result concerns the *one-dimensional distinct-count distribution at the specified sample size*, not equality of underlying exchangeable partition laws at all sample sizes.

## Assumptions and scope
The sample size is exactly \(n=4\) or \(n=5\). The parent distribution has at most \(n\) categorical labels, whose probabilities can be arbitrary nonnegative real numbers summing to one. Zero-probability labels are allowed by padding. The draws are conditionally independent given the parent distribution; the convex-hull statement also permits arbitrary mixtures of such parent distributions. No atomless component, alphabet larger than \(n\), or other sample size is asserted.

Write \(S(n,k)\) for a Stirling number of the second kind and \((j)_{\underline{k}}=j(j-1)\cdots(j-k+1)\), with zero when \(k>j\). Put
\[
M^{(n)}_{kj}=\Pr(U_{n,j}=k)=\frac{S(n,k)(j)_{\underline{k}}}{j^n},\qquad 1\le k,j\le n.
\]
The matrix \(M^{(n)}\) is upper triangular when rows and columns follow increasing \(k,j\), with strictly positive diagonal, hence invertible.

## Proof
Relabel the parent probabilities in nonincreasing order \(p_1\ge\cdots\ge p_n\ge0\), with \(p_{n+1}=0\). Introduce the nonnegative gaps \(\delta_r=p_r-p_{r+1}\) for \(1\le r\le n\). Then \(p_i=\sum_{r=i}^n\delta_r\), and \(\sum_{r=1}^n r\delta_r=1\).

For each \(k\), inclusion-exclusion over the set of exactly \(k\) observed labels gives the homogeneous degree-\(n\) polynomial
\[
q_k(p)=\Pr(K_n=k)=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq[n]\\1\le |T|\le k}}
(-1)^{k-|T|}\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}\left(\sum_{i\in T}p_i\right)^n.
\]
Let \(w=(M^{(n)})^{-1}q\). By invertibility these are the **only** possible mixture weights. For any exponent vector \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\) with nonnegative integer entries summing to \(n\), the coefficient of the monomial \(\delta_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\delta_n^{\alpha_n}\) in \(q_k\) is the rational integer
\[
c_{n,k}(\alpha)=\frac{n!}{\prod_{r=1}^n\alpha_r!}
\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq[n]\\1\le|T|\le k}}
(-1)^{k-|T|}\binom{n-|T|}{k-|T|}
\prod_{r=1}^n |T\cap\{1,\ldots,r\}|^{\alpha_r},
\]
with the usual convention that a factor raised to exponent zero equals one. This follows by inserting \(\sum_{i\in T}p_i=\sum_r|T\cap[r]|\delta_r\) into the previous equation and applying the multinomial theorem.

The coefficient of the same monomial in \(w_j\) is
\[
d_{n,j}(\alpha)=\sum_{k=1}^n(M^{(n)})^{-1}_{jk}c_{n,k}(\alpha).
\]
An exhaustive **exact-rational symbolic enumeration of all coefficients** establishes \(d_{n,j}(\alpha)\ge0\) for every pair \((n,j)\) in the stated range and every weak composition \(\alpha\) of \(n\) into \(n\) parts. The required enumeration contains exactly \(\binom{2n-1}{n}\) exponent vectors; it is finite because the entire inequality is a polynomial of fixed degree, not because real-valued probabilities were discretized. The standalone certificate `verify_occupancy.py` reconstructs \(M^{(n)}\), its inverse, every coefficient from the displayed expression, and each sign using only exact integer and rational arithmetic.

For \(n=4\), the respective numbers of positive coefficients in \(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4\) are \(26,27,24,14\), with zero coefficients \(9,8,11,21\). Their smallest positive coefficients are \(2/7,32/7,27,256\). For \(n=5\), the respective positive counts are \(112,113,108,85,42\), zero counts \(14,13,18,41,84\), and smallest positive coefficients \(1/15,16/3,162/5,256,3125\). Thus each \(w_j\) is a polynomial with nonnegative rational coefficients in the nonnegative variables \(\delta_r\); consequently \(w_j\ge0\) for all admissible real probability vectors.

Finally, every column of \(M^{(n)}\) sums to one and \(\sum_kq_k=1\). Therefore \(\sum_jw_j=1\). The desired representation follows. Its uniqueness comes from triangular invertibility. Every uniform law is itself generated by a categorical parent with at most \(n\) labels, so mixing these parents attains the entire simplex; the inclusion proved above yields equality.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_occupancy.py` in a directory containing that file. The script requires only Python standard-library modules (`fractions`, `math`, `itertools`). It evaluates all \(35\) gap monomials when \(n=4\) and all \(126\) when \(n=5\); it checks every coefficient for every mixture weight and the exact inverse-matrix identity. It also independently enumerates labeled sampling paths for thirteen distinct rational parent vectors, checking reconstruction, normalization, and nonnegativity. The printed positive, zero and minimum-coefficient counts agree exactly with the proof above. The exhaustive coefficient certificate, rather than the thirteen independent finite examples, establishes the theorem over the entire continuum of admissible real weights.

## Relationship to prior work
T. Zhu, *The distribution of the number of distinct values in a finite exchangeable sequence* (arXiv:2103.07518, first posted March 12, 2021; *Electronic Journal of Probability* 27 (2022), article 815), proves the unrestricted convex-hull description for \(n=3\) and conjectures the analogous statement at larger sample sizes (Conjecture 2(ii)); its Theorem 13 gives the different single-coordinate bound on \(\Pr(K_n=2)\). The present result addresses the first two unproved sample sizes under the natural restriction that the categorical alphabet is no larger than the sample, and gives explicit nonnegative mixture coefficients. The published result that uniform occupancy laws are exposed vertices for all \(n\) addresses a different question, namely Conjecture 2(i), and does not establish containment of nonuniform count laws in the simplex. A previously established sharp upper bound on \(\Pr(K_4=3)\) likewise does not imply this simultaneous distributional representation.

## Limitations
This result makes no claim for \(n\ge6\), for larger or countably infinite alphabets with \(n\in\{4,5\}\), or for an atomless component. In particular it does not resolve Zhu's full Conjecture 2(ii). The decomposition is at one fixed sample size and cannot generally be chosen consistently in \(n\) to represent the original exchangeable partition process. The finite exact certificate is checkable without floating point but has not received an independent audit. Classical and unindexed results establishing an equivalent positive-mixture transform are a residual originality risk.
