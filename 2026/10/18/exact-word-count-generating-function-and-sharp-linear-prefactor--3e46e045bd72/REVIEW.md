# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The original canonical coding is the Dyck language by the published Theorem A. A typed admissible word over each coarse sequence is counted exactly by the \(M\)-th power of the running record of unpaired closing brackets. A biased simple random walk supplies a defective first-passage generating function. Strong Markov decomposition gives the exact algebraic word enumerator, while a verified double pole at \(1\) and a holomorphic remainder on a larger disk establish the two sharp coefficients and exponentially small normalized remainder. The test script independently compares direct language counts, weighted walk maxima and exact formal-series coefficients for \(M=2,3,4\). It does not prove the analytic theorem by enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on remaining historical-priority risk
Takahasi–Yamamoto [1] already proved the coding theorem and the known entropy, and Krieger/Inoue [4] studied zeta functions; neither is new here. Takahasi [3] counts periodic orbits, not all admissible finite words. Previously recorded work on neutral-periodic fibers and their walk statistics counts different quantities. The contribution is the explicit all-length distinct word generating function and its sharp linear prefactor, not known symbolic representations. Semantic and literature searches did not identify equivalent formulas. A historical Dyck-language enumeration outside inspected sources could supersede priority; this remains a material risk, not a mathematical refutation.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Finite-word complexity is a natural entropy-convergence diagnostic for the same heterochaotic shifts with two known maximal-entropy measures. The linear factor in \(A_{n,M}\) creates a quantitative \(\log n/n\) correction that cannot be recovered from the published leading entropy alone. The result covers an entire infinite alphabet-size family and all interior geometric parameters rather than only small finite word tables.
