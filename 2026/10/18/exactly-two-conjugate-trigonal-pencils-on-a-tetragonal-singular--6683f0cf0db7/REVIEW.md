# Review

## Correctness
The construction is based on a squarefree genus-two quintic and two specified ordinary nonsplit nodes. The denominator \(x(x-1)\) takes value \(1\) at both branches of each node, and the two explicit functions have three simple poles away from the nodes; they define genuine degree-three morphisms after quadratic extension. The two translated theta curves in the normalization's Jacobian have intersection number \(2\), and the two functions give distinct reduced intersection points. The unique hyperelliptic pencil mismatches both nodes, excluding degree-two or defective degree-three pencils. Exact finite-field tests execute successfully, while the geometric completeness argument is proved symbolically.

## Originality
The originating paper gives the curve and its \(\mathbb F_3\)-gonality \(4\), but not its geometric trigonal pencils or their splitting field. Generic genus-four geometry explains a possible bound of two pencils, not that this specified nodal curve realizes the bound with a single quadratic Galois orbit. Targeted searches for the exact equation, the two nodes, base-field changes, and equivalent genus-four quadric descriptions did not identify the exact arithmetic classification. Search incompleteness and potential unpublished overlap remain risks.

## Mathematical value
This resolves a natural arithmetic-versus-geometric distinction for the precise tetragonal example used to exhibit the boundary in the recent singular-curve construction. The complete two-point Galois structure, not merely an upper bound, identifies the field on which every minimal trigonal pencil appears. The examples give actual degree-three morphisms, beyond existence of torsion-free series. It is a focused result about one motivating example, not a general theorem on all singular curves.
