# Exceptional preferences bound the stable-marriage lattice dimension

## Finding
Consider a complete, strict, balanced stable-marriage instance with \(n\ge1\) men and \(n\) women. Suppose a specified set \(E\) of \(k\) women, \(0\le k\le n\), may have arbitrary preference orders, while all women outside \(E\) have one identical strict master order of the men. Every man's preference list is unrestricted. Write \(\mathcal S\) for its stable matchings, ordered by the usual women-dominance relation: \(M\preceq N\) when each woman weakly prefers her partner in \(N\) to her partner in \(M\).

**Theorem.** Recording the partners of the \(k\) exceptional women identifies every stable matching uniquely and gives an order embedding of \(\mathcal S\) into a product of \(k\) chains. The image is closed under componentwise minimum and maximum (when rankings are oriented consistently). In particular
\[
|\mathcal S|\le (n)_k=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!},\qquad
\dim(\mathcal S)\le k\quad(k\ge1),
\]
where \(\dim\) means order dimension. For \(k=0\) the stable matching is unique. For each \(n\ge1\), the cardinality bound \(|\mathcal S|\le n\) at \(k=1\) is attained; for every \(k\ge1\), there is an instance with exactly \(k\) exceptional women whose stable-matching lattice is the Boolean lattice on \(k\) generators, so the order-dimension bound is attained.

Every stable matching can be enumerated without duplicates by considering at most \((n)_k\) injective assignments to the exceptional women, completing the remaining matching greedily, and testing stability. This requires \(O((n)_k n^2)\) comparison operations with direct rank tables; it is polynomial for any fixed \(k\) but is not asserted to be fixed-parameter tractable.

## Assumptions and scope
All \(n^2\) pairs are acceptable, preferences are complete and strict, and stable means absence of a pair whose members strictly prefer each other to their assigned partners. Both sides have size \(n\); every stable matching is perfect. There are no ties, quotas, couples, or outside options. The exceptional women are a specified set; some may coincidentally rank men exactly as the common master list. Order dimension means the least number of linear extensions whose intersection is the given finite partial order. A one-element partial order has dimension one under this convention.

## Proof

**1. Master-order uniqueness lemma.** If every woman in a strict complete balanced market shares a ranking \(m_1\succ m_2\succ\cdots\succ m_r\) of its \(r\) men, a stable matching is forced: man \(m_1\) receives his first-choice woman, then \(m_2\) receives his favorite remaining woman, and so on. Indeed, if \(m_1\) did not receive his first choice, that woman would prefer him to her assigned man and they would block. Remove the pair and induct. The greedy result itself is stable: a man \(m_i\) was free to choose any woman still unmatched when his turn came; any woman already allocated prefers her earlier man to him. Thus the stable matching is unique.

**2. Injection and counting.** Fix any injective assignment of \(k\) distinct men to the exceptional women. If a stable matching realizes this assignment, delete those women and their assigned men. Its restriction is stable in the residual market: any residual blocking pair would also block the original matching. All residual women share the restriction of the original master order, so Step 1 forces the residual matching uniquely. There are at most \((n)_k\) assignments of distinct men to the exceptional women, and therefore at most that many stable matchings. The same construction enumerates every possibility: for each exceptional assignment, greedily complete the residual market and test all original man–woman pairs for blocking. The greedy step needs at most quadratic comparisons, as does the stability test. When \(k=0\), Step 1 itself proves uniqueness.

**3. Order embedding.** Assign to a stable matching \(M\) the ordered tuple of the \(k\) exceptional women's partners, each coordinate arranged in that woman's strict preference order. The map is injective by Step 2. The classical strict stable-marriage lattice identity says that the womenwise better partners of any two stable matchings form another stable matching, their join in women-dominance order; the womenwise worse partners form their meet. (This identity is also proved by the no-blocking argument: if two women both selected the same man as their preferred partner from two stable matchings, his being matched to those women in opposite matchings would contradict stability of the matching where he receives his less preferred of the two women. After bijectivity, any pair blocking the womenwise-better matching would block whichever original matching gave that woman her selected partner; analogously the meet is the other lattice operation.)

Suppose exceptional women all weakly prefer their partners in stable matching \(N\) to their partners in stable matching \(M\). Their partners in the womenwise join \(J=M\vee N\) are therefore exactly their partners in \(N\). By Step 2, \(J=N\). Hence all women weakly prefer \(N\) to \(M\). The converse is immediate. Consequently comparison of exceptional coordinates is **equivalent** to womenwise order, not only necessary. Since join and meet choose coordinatewise the better or worse partner, the image is a sublattice of a product of \(k\) chains. A product of \(k\) finite chains has order dimension at most \(k\): take \(k\) lexicographic orders, each giving a different coordinate first and breaking ties by a fixed ordering of other coordinates. Restrict these linear extensions to the image to obtain \(\dim(\mathcal S)\le k\) for \(k\ge1\).

**4. Sharp cardinality with one exceptional woman.** Label men \(m_0,\ldots,m_{n-1}\) and women \(w_0,\ldots,w_{n-1}\). Woman \(w_0\) ranks \(m_{n-1}\succ\cdots\succ m_0\), and all other women rank \(m_0\succ\cdots\succ m_{n-1}\). Man \(m_0\) ranks women by increasing index. For \(i\ge1\), man \(m_i\) ranks \(w_1,\ldots,w_i\), then \(w_0\), then \(w_{i+1},\ldots,w_{n-1}\), in the displayed order. For each \(t\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), define matching \(M_t\) by \(m_t\leftrightarrow w_0\), \(m_i\leftrightarrow w_{i+1}\) for \(i<t\), and \(m_i\leftrightarrow w_i\) for \(i>t\). It is a perfect matching. The only men preferred by \(w_0\) to \(m_t\) have indices \(i>t\), but each such man prefers his allocated \(w_i\) to \(w_0\). Each ordinary woman always prefers a smaller-index man, and checking the three regions \(i<t\), \(i=t\), \(i>t\) shows that any smaller-index man she prefers is already paired with a woman that he ranks higher than her; any potential blocking pair is excluded. Hence all \(M_t\) are stable. They are distinct, giving at least \(n\) stable matchings, exactly the Step-2 upper bound. The claim includes the vacuous \(n=1\) case.

**5. Sharp dimension with \(k\) exceptional women.** Take \(n=2k\) and organize each side into \(k\) labeled two-person blocks. In block \(i\), men are \(a_i,b_i\), ordinary woman is \(y_i\), and exceptional woman is \(x_i\). All \(y_i\) rank men by the same master order \(a_1\succ b_1\succ\cdots\succ a_k\succ b_k\). Each \(x_i\) ranks \(b_i\succ a_i\) above every man outside the block. Each \(a_i\) ranks \(x_i\succ y_i\) above every outside woman, and each \(b_i\) ranks \(y_i\succ x_i\) above every outside woman (the remaining orders can be arbitrary). In every stable matching each block is matched internally: induct on block number. Men of the earliest unprocessed block rank its women above all unprocessed external women, and its ordinary woman ranks its men above every later-block man; its exceptional woman does likewise, so a cross-block matching creates a blocking pair. Earlier blocks are already internally matched and cannot provide partners. Thus all blocks match internally. Each block has exactly two stable configurations, \((a_i,x_i),(b_i,y_i)\) and \((a_i,y_i),(b_i,x_i)\), and all combinations are stable: cross-block pairs cannot block because every man prefers both women of his own block to outside women. Therefore the lattice is the direct product of \(k\) two-element chains, \(B_k\). Its dimension is \(k\): for \(k\ge3\), the \(k\) incomparable pairs of singleton \(\{i\}\) and its complement \([k]\setminus\{i\}\) cannot have two reversed in the same linear extension, since \(\{i\}\subseteq[k]\setminus\{j\}\) whenever \(i\ne j\); for \(k=2\) the diamond has dimension two, and for \(k=1\) the chain has dimension one. Together with Step 3 this is sharp for every \(k\ge1\).

## Verification
The supplied `verify.py` implements exact exhaustive stability tests independently of the greedy completion routine. For one exceptional woman and the master list fixed, it checks all \(8\) two-by-two and \(1296\) three-by-three instances, comparing the complete stable set with projection-and-greedy candidates and comparing exceptional-coordinate dominance to all-women dominance. It exhaustively checks the proposed \(n\)-matching construction for \(1\le n\le6\) and the Boolean-lattice construction for \(1\le k\le3\), including the exact sets of stable matchings. Run `python3 verify.py` and compare stdout to `certificate.json`. The unrestricted theorem rests on the proof above, **not** on finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Irving, Manlove, and Scott (2008) analyzed master preference lists and give uniqueness in the fully shared strict-preference setting; Step 1 is a special standard case of their setting, not presented as new. The distributive lattice of stable matchings and operations of selecting each woman's better/worse partner are classical, reflected in Irving and Leather's 1986 counting framework. Meeks and Rastegari (2017) study stable matching under similarity types and exceptions to candidate rankings, a different parameterization from \(k\) fully exceptional agents. The contribution assessed here is the combined sharp structural bound, the faithful \(k\)-coordinate lattice representation, and the explicit cardinality/dimension sharpness constructions. Earlier equivalent formulations under common priorities or rotation-poset dimension remain an originality risk.

## Limitations
This result is not a bound for markets with ties, incomplete lists, unequal sides, unmatched agents, or only approximately shared preferences. The factorial upper bound is not claimed optimal for every pair \((n,k)\) with \(k>1\). The enumeration complexity is an XP-style polynomial for fixed \(k\), not a fixed-parameter tractability result. Scientific originality is based on accessible-source comparisons, not a proof that no equivalent older theorem exists.
