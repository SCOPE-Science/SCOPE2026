# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Fixing the partners of exceptional women leaves a unique stable residual instance, by the common-master greedy lemma. This makes the partner projection injective. The womenwise join/meet theorem upgrades the projection to a sublattice embedding and implies the dimension bound. Explicit stable matching families attain the \(k=1\) cardinality bound and all positive dimension bounds. Finite exhaustive exact checks confirm the small instances and the constructions; infinite assertions are proved symbolically, not inferred from tests. Residual correctness risk: the assumptions of strictness, completeness and global block freedom are essential, and the result does not generalize without them.

## Originality — PASS
The 2008 master-list source proves the underlying shared-order uniqueness and is credited as background, while standard stable marriage work already establishes distributive lattice operations. The assessed claim is stronger: one arbitrary whole-list exception parameter gives an injective exceptional-partner representation, an order embedding of the full stable lattice into a product of \(k\) chains, a cardinality bound with sharp one-exception cases, and Boolean-lattice sharp dimension examples. The inspected 2017 similar-agent source parameterizes candidate-level exceptions and hard matching problems, not this theorem. Compared close local published stable-marriage censuses by implication, not wording. Remaining risk: earlier rotation-poset or common-priority parameterization may already imply this statement; full bibliographic completeness is not guaranteed.

## Value — PASS
Common preference priorities model school and hospital admissions, where a master order may be disrupted by a small number of agents. Counting feasible stable outcomes is #P-complete in general, but the finding gives a precise polynomial-output-size regime for any fixed number of fully exceptional agents, an exact low-dimensional lattice coordinate system, a constructive enumeration method, and sharp extremal witnesses. The dimension bound adds structural content beyond the elementary counting estimate. No claim of FPT complexity or optimal cardinality for all \(k>1\) is made.

## Source comparison and limitations
Irving–Manlove–Scott (2008) handles the exact shared master-list setting; Irving–Leather (1986) establishes complexity and lattice framework; Meeks–Rastegari (2017) concerns similarity types and candidate exceptions; Alkan (2001) gives related lattice structure. None of the inspected material asserts the combined \(k\)-coordinate theorem. Possible equivalent earlier results in stable matching lattice/rotation literature remain a bibliographic risk. The theorem assumes complete strict preferences and one common master list outside a specified set of whole-list exceptions.
