# Reproducibility and verification

The file `verify_klein.py` is standalone Python 3 with only standard-library imports. Executing `python3 verify_klein.py` terminates with `VERIFY_OK` after directly enumerating projective-torus points over \(\mathbb F_5\), \(\mathbb F_{11}\), and \(\mathbb F_7\) for the cubic instances with \(s=4,5,6\). It checks the complete Jacobian and polynomial equation, counts \(1,1,3\) points, and verifies Hessian ranks \(2,3,4\). These fields contain all geometric singular points for those chosen cases; this is not asserted for arbitrary finite fields.

For \((s,d,p)=(4,3,5)\), on the affine chart \(x_0=1\) a reduced lexicographic Gröbner basis of the ideal generated by the local polynomial and all affine first derivatives (writing the other coordinates as \(z_0,z_1,z_2\)) is
\[
z_0-2z_2^2,\quad z_1+2z_2^3,\quad z_2^5-2.
\]
In characteristic five the final polynomial is \((z_2-2)^5\), so the local algebra has length five. For \((s,d,p)=(5,3,11)\), the corresponding basis in variables \(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3\) is
\[
z_0-5z_3^2,\quad z_1-3z_3^9,\quad z_2-4z_3^6,\quad z_3^{11}-4,
\]
with final polynomial \((z_3-4)^{11}\) modulo eleven. Both bases can be checked by reducing the polynomial and derivative generators and verifying all S-polynomial remainders vanish. These computations test the particular local thicknesses, not the all-parameter classification.

The universal proof is contained in `RESULT.md`: the integral character-lattice cokernel is cyclic of order \(|Q_{s,d}|\), the projective Jacobian scheme is the fiber of its torus isogeny, and the logarithmic Hessian rank is derived without finite extrapolation. No assertions or local computations are supplied for characteristics dividing \(d\). Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
