# Exceptional star strata in hollow-symmetric determinant hypersurfaces

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge5\), the determinant hypersurface of the generic **hollow symmetric** matrix has a reduced singular locus with exactly \(n+1\) irreducible components. Unlike the unconstrained symmetric determinant, it has \(n\) additional linear components consisting of matrices with an identically zero row and column. These components are smaller-dimensional than the corank-two component, and the intersections with that component have a uniform irreducible determinantal description.

Write \(N=\binom n2\). The precise classification is
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}=Y_n\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}L_i,
\quad Y_n=V(I_{n-1}(A_n)),
\quad L_i=V(a_{ij}:j\ne i).
\]
Here \(Y_n\) is irreducible of projective codimension \(3\), degree \(\binom{n+1}{3}\), and dimension \(N-4\). Each \(L_i\) is a projective linear space of dimension \(\binom{n-1}{2}-1\) and codimension \(n-1\), hence contributes a distinct irreducible component. At generic points of \(Y_n\) and \(L_i\), respectively, the transverse hypersurface germs are an ordinary surface double point and a nondegenerate quadratic cone in \(n-1\) variables. In particular all generic transverse multiplicities are \(2\).

There is also a scheme-theoretic intersection formula:
\[
Y_n\cap L_i\ \cong\ V(\det A_{[n]\setminus\{i\}})\subset L_i
\]
where the right side is an irreducible hypersurface of degree \(n-1\). For distinct \(i,j\),
\[
L_i\cap L_j\cong\mathbb P^{\binom{n-2}{2}-1}\subset Y_n.
\]
Consequently, the reduced singular locus is connected and non-equidimensional. The whole hypersurface \(X_n\) is geometrically integral and normal.

The formulas for the irreducibility and degree of the corank-two variety \(Y_n\), taken alone, are established by existing Gram-determinantal theory; the claimed contribution is the complete singular-support decomposition, its excess linear strata, their incidence and generic transverse models.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(0\), with an integer \(n\ge5\). Let \(R=k[a_{ij}:1\le i<j\le n]\) and \(A_n=(a_{ij})\), where \(a_{ii}=0\), \(a_{ji}=a_{ij}\) for \(j>i\). All projective loci lie in \(\mathbb P(R_1^*)=\mathbb P^{N-1}\). Let \(I_{n-1}(A_n)\) be the ideal generated by all \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) minors (not only principal minors). Singular loci are interpreted via the ordinary projective Jacobian criterion. Assertions about the singular locus concern its **reduced support**; the displayed intersection \(Y_n\cap L_i\) is instead the scheme-theoretic intersection of the indicated determinantal and linear subschemes.

## Proof

**1. Exact Jacobian support.** Differentiating in a symmetric off-diagonal coordinate gives \(\partial\det(A_n)/\partial a_{ij}=2\operatorname{adj}(A_n)_{ij}\) for \(i<j\). If \(\operatorname{rank}(A_n)\le n-2\), every adjugate entry vanishes. If the rank equals \(n-1\), the symmetric adjugate is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(vv^{\mathsf T}\), where \(v\) spans its one-dimensional kernel. All off-diagonal derivatives vanish precisely when \(v_iv_j=0\) for every distinct pair, hence precisely when \(v\) is supported in one coordinate. In that event \(A_nv=0\) says the corresponding whole row and column are zero. Thus the displayed set-theoretic decomposition holds, including all singular points and no nonsingular points. Since the determinant is homogeneous, this is also the projective Jacobian locus in characteristic zero.

**2. Corank-two component.** Put \(r=n-2\ge3\). Every symmetric matrix of rank at most \(r\) is a Gram matrix of \(n\) vectors in \(k^r\); the zero diagonal means each of these vectors is isotropic for the standard nondegenerate quadratic form. The affine isotropic cone \(Q_r=\{v\in k^r:v\cdot v=0\}\) is irreducible of dimension \(r-1\) because \(r\ge3\). Hence \(Q_r^n\) is irreducible, and its Gram image is precisely the affine cone over \(Y_n\), so \(Y_n\) is irreducible. A general isotropic frame spans \(k^r\), and two such full-rank frames have the same Gram matrix precisely when related by the orthogonal group of dimension \(\binom r2\). Therefore the affine image dimension is \(n(r-1)-\binom r2=N-3\), giving projective dimension \(N-4\) and codimension \(3\). The degree \(\binom{n+1}{3}\) follows from the classical degree formula for the symmetric corank-two determinantal variety, or from the product formula in El Maazouz–Sturmfels–Sverrisdóttir, Corollary 2.6, specialized to one-by-one zero diagonal blocks. This degree and the underlying primeness are acknowledged prior results.

**3. Distinct star components and normality.** Every \(L_i\) is irreducible, of the asserted dimension. It is not contained in \(Y_n\): its complementary \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) hollow block can be chosen as the adjacency matrix of a complete graph with off-diagonal entries \(1\), which has determinant \((-1)^{n-2}(n-2)\ne0\). Conversely \(Y_n\) has codimension \(3<n-1\) and is not any \(L_i\). The determinant is nonzero since the same all-ones-off-diagonal matrix of size \(n\) has determinant \((-1)^{n-1}(n-1)\ne0\). Because the singular locus just computed has ambient codimension at least \(3\), the determinant cannot have distinct positive-degree factors (their intersection would be singular in ambient codimension at most \(2\)), nor can it have a repeated factor (which would produce a codimension-one singular set). Therefore \(X_n\) is reduced and irreducible. Its singular locus has codimension at least \(2\) within \(X_n\), and the hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay, so Serre's \(R_1+S_2\) criterion gives normality.

**4. Scheme-theoretic intersections.** Modulo the linear ideal defining \(L_i\), all \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) minors of \(A_n\) vanish except possibly the principal minor omitting row and column \(i\), which equals \(\det A_{[n]\setminus\{i\}}\). Thus \(I_{n-1}(A_n)+I(L_i)\) cuts out exactly the stated hypersurface as a scheme. Its defining determinant is irreducible for matrix size \(n-1\ge4\), by the same irreducible Gram parametrization with rank \(n-2\ge3\) (or the one-block case of the published prime-ideal theorem). For \(i\ne j\), imposing both zero stars leaves precisely \(\binom{n-2}{2}\) free off-diagonal coordinates, proving the linear intersection formula. Two zero rows force rank at most \(n-2\), so \(L_i\cap L_j\subseteq Y_n\). Every \(L_i\) meets \(Y_n\) in the nonempty determinant hypersurface above. Hence their union is connected.

**5. Generic transverse germs.** At a general point of \(L_i\setminus Y_n\), write \(A_n=\begin{pmatrix}0&w^{\mathsf T}\\w&B\end{pmatrix}\), where the \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) hollow block \(B\) is invertible. The Schur complement identity is the **exact** local equality \(\det(A_n)=-\det(B)\,w^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}w\). The \(n-1\) coordinates of \(w\) are normal coordinates to \(L_i\), and the quadratic form is nondegenerate. This gives the claimed transverse ordinary quadratic hypersurface.

At a rank-\(n-2\) point of \(Y_n\), block elimination around an invertible \((n-2)\times(n-2)\) minor expresses the determinant as a unit times the determinant of a symmetric \(2\times2\) residual block. Its three entries furnish independent normal parameters whenever the restriction from hollow symmetric perturbations to quadratic forms on the two-dimensional kernel is surjective. This condition holds on a nonempty open part of \(Y_n\): take a rank-\(n-2\) hollow witness whose kernel is \(W=\langle e_1-e_2,e_3-e_4\rangle\). For \(n=5\), take the matrix with edges between \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\) and all four edges from vertex \(5\) to \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) equal to \(1\); its rank is \(3\). For \(n\ge6\), use the complete-bipartite adjacency matrix of \(K_{2,2}\) on the first four vertices (rank \(2\)) and direct-sum with the invertible all-ones-off-diagonal matrix of size \(n-4\). Perturbations in entries \(a_{12},a_{34},a_{13}\) give independent quadratic forms on \(W\). Thus the generic transverse germ is \(uv-w^2=0\), the ordinary \(A_1\) surface singularity. Each generic transverse hypersurface has multiplicity \(2\).

## Verification

Run the standalone file `CHECK_HOLLOW.py` using a standard Python 3 interpreter. It independently expands the hollow determinant by its permutation definition and all ten partial derivatives for \(n=5\), exhaustively evaluates all \(3^{10}=59049\) matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\), computes modular ranks, and checks that the vanishing Jacobian equals the corank-two-or-zero-star condition. The result is \(4761\) singular affine matrices, of which \(2340\) have rank \(4\) and a zero star; all remaining singular matrices have rank at most \(3\). The checker also reconstructs the rank-\(n-2\) and kernel witnesses and the three independent transverse directions for \(5\le n\le12\), returning `VERIFY_OK`.

The finite-field enumeration and finite witnesses are consistency checks, **not** proofs of all-\(n\) statements in characteristic zero; those follow from the symbolic algebraic arguments above. No claimed verification relies on code from outside this package.

## Relationship to prior work

Kerner–Vinnikov (2010) develops a general local theory of determinantal representations and their singularities, without this hollow-family global stratification. Huang (2013) treats generic determinant multilinear forms rather than the fixed zero-diagonal determinant hypersurface. El Maazouz–Sturmfels–Sverrisdóttir (first public preprint 2024) studies exactly the relevant zero-block symmetric rank varieties: its Theorem 2.5 and Corollary 2.6 **already imply** the irreducibility, codimension and degree of \(Y_n\), and Example 2.10 analyzes singular components in a different two-by-two zero-block hypersurface. Those ingredients are not presented as novel here. The proposed distinct statement is the exact \(n+1\)-component Jacobian support of the one-by-one zero-block determinant, its \(n\) star components, all their determinantal and linear intersections, and the two transverse models. Xie (2026) addresses off-diagonal Gram-map fibers and rational-singularity thresholds, not the projective determinant hypersurface. Existing general theorems plus standard adjugate identities make the result accessible, and equivalent implicit coverage remains a residual novelty risk.

## Limitations

Only characteristic zero is asserted. The theorem describes the **reduced** singular support, not the potentially nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme or embedded associated primes. Generic transverse analytic models do not classify every nongeneric completed local germ, including points where singular components meet. The degree of \(Y_n\), its irreducibility and the elementary matrix identities are existing tools, not separately new claims. The smaller case \(n=4\) is excluded because its rank-at-most-two hollow determinantal locus is reducible. Independence and external validation are not asserted.
