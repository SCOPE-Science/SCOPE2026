# Exchangeable edge conductances restore uniform last-discovery on a cycle

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge 3\). On the cycle with vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\), let \(C_i>0\) be the conductance of the edge joining \(i\) to \(i+1\), with indices modulo \(n\). After sampling the conductance vector once, run a nearest-neighbor walk from vertex \(0\) whose probability of traversing a neighboring edge is proportional to its conductance. Let \(L\) be the last *new* vertex discovered by the walk. Assume only that the joint law of the \(n\) positive finite conductances is exchangeable under all permutations of their edge indices; dependence and nonidentical-looking sample realizations are allowed.

**Theorem (conditional universality).** Write \(\mathcal M\) for the unordered multiset of sampled conductances, including multiplicities. Then, for every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\),
\[
\Pr(L=k\mid\mathcal M)=\frac1{n-1}\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
In particular, \(L\) is uniform on all non-starting vertices and independent of *every* permutation-invariant statistic of the edge environment. There are no moment, boundedness, independence, or ellipticity assumptions beyond almost-sure strict positivity and finiteness.

The phenomenon is genuinely annealed: with fixed nonuniform conductances the conditional probabilities need not be equal. For example, if all edges have conductance \(1\) except the edge \((n-1,0)\), which has conductance \(w>0\), then the quenched probabilities are \(w/(1+(n-2)w)\) for \(1\le k\le n-2\), and \(1/(1+(n-2)w)\) for \(k=n-1\). This deterministic calculation is an illustration, not an independently claimed priority result.

## Assumptions and scope

The environment is sampled once and frozen; the resulting walk is a finite, irreducible, reversible Markov chain. Conditional on the environment, the transition probability from vertex \(i\) to \(i+1\) is \(C_i/(C_{i-1}+C_i)\), and the reverse-neighbor probability is \(C_{i-1}/(C_{i-1}+C_i)\). Every vertex is discovered in finite time almost surely. Exchangeability means invariance of the full joint conductance law under the entire symmetric group on edge indices, not merely under rotations or reflections. Both continuous and discrete environments, including arbitrary exchangeable dependence, are covered. Conditioning on \(\mathcal M\) is in the regular-conditional-expectation sense.

## Proof

Set the edge resistance \(r_i=C_i^{-1}\). Its joint law is also exchangeable. Fix \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) and remove vertex \(k\). The remaining vertices form the path from \(k+1\) clockwise to \(k-1\). The resistances along its \(n-2\) edges, and the initial position of vertex \(0\), determine
\[
R_k=\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}r_{(k+j)\bmod n}>0,
\qquad A_k=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}r_j,
\qquad 0\le A_k\le R_k.
\]
The quantity \(A_k\) is the total resistance along this path from endpoint \(k+1\) to the starting vertex \(0\), and includes precisely \(n-k-1\) of its \(n-2\) edges.

For a nearest-neighbor conductance walk restricted to a path, the harmonic hitting function is linear in accumulated resistance. Therefore, before ever visiting \(k\), the walk reaches endpoint \(k+1\) before endpoint \(k-1\) with probability \(1-A_k/R_k\); the opposite endpoint is reached first with probability \(A_k/R_k\). Starting at endpoint \(k+1\), the probability of reaching endpoint \(k-1\) before visiting \(k\) is \(r_k/(R_k+r_k)\), by the same resistance-coordinate gambler's-ruin formula on the path through \(k\). Symmetrically, starting at \(k-1\), the corresponding probability of first reaching \(k+1\) before \(k\) is \(r_{k-1}/(R_k+r_{k-1})\). These two disjoint possibilities characterize precisely the event that every other vertex is discovered before \(k\). Strong Markov at the first endpoint gives the exact fixed-environment identity
\[
q_k(r):=\Pr_r(L=k)
=\left(1-\frac{A_k}{R_k}\right)\frac{r_k}{R_k+r_k}
+\frac{A_k}{R_k}\frac{r_{k-1}}{R_k+r_{k-1}}.\tag{1}
\]
This is used as a proof identity; weighted-cycle last-coverage probabilities were studied earlier by Mosbah and Saheb, and no priority claim is made for the fixed-environment computation alone.

Let \(\mathscr F\) be the sigma-field generated by the unordered multiset of all \(n\) edge resistances. The conditional law given \(\mathscr F\) is invariant under all edge-index permutations: exchangeability persists under conditioning on a permutation-invariant statistic. Conditional on \(\mathscr F\), \(R_k,r_{k-1},r_k\), the \(n-2\) resistances in \(R_k\) remain exchangeable. Since the \(n-2\) shares sum to one,
\[
\mathbb E\left[\left.\frac{A_k}{R_k}\,\right|\mathscr F,R_k,r_{k-1},r_k\right]
=\frac{n-k-1}{n-2}.\tag{2}
\]
Likewise, given \(\mathscr F\), the \(n-1\) entries consisting of \(r_k\) and the \(n-2\) entries making up \(R_k\) are exchangeable. Their positive normalized shares sum to one. Hence
\[
\mathbb E\left[\left.\frac{r_k}{R_k+r_k}\,\right|\mathscr F\right]
=\frac1{n-1}.
\]
The same argument for the \(n-1\) distinct entries \(r_{k-1}\) and those making up \(R_k\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\left[\left.\frac{r_{k-1}}{R_k+r_{k-1}}\,\right|\mathscr F\right]
=\frac1{n-1}.\tag{3}
\]
Taking conditional expectations of (1), using (2) and then (3), yields
\[
\Pr(L=k\mid\mathscr F)=
\left(1-\frac{n-k-1}{n-2}\right)\frac1{n-1}
+\frac{n-k-1}{n-2}\frac1{n-1}
=\frac1{n-1}.
\]
All conditional variables are bounded between zero and one; no moment assumption is invoked. The one-defect example follows by substitution into (1). This proves the stated joint-distribution claim.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone program `verify_exchangeable_cycle.py` uses only the Python standard library and exact rational arithmetic. It independently solves finite-state absorbing Markov equations for small weighted cycles, compares these probabilities with (1), exhaustively averages (1) over all resistance permutations for sizes three through six, checks multisets with repeated resistances, and evaluates the one-defect example. Running `python verify_exchangeable_cycle.py` produced `PASS 187 exact rational consistency assertions; no infinite proof inferred`. The general result is proved above, not extrapolated from these checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Idnani and Salem, arXiv:2610.15115 (public October 10, 2026), derive last-coverage probabilities on cycles for spatially homogeneous directional bias and deterministic time-varying bias; their §6.2 flags random and position-dependent transition probabilities as a further question. Their unbiased uniformity result and the older uniform cycle result of Lovász and Winkler do not assert uniformity for a frozen heterogeneous reversible random environment conditioned on its unordered edge weights.

Mosbah and Saheb (2000) explicitly study *deterministic nonuniform* random walks on weighted cycles and report a formula for last coverage at a vertex. Their fixed-environment coverage substantially overlaps the intermediate computation (1). The theorem stated here instead concerns the conditional **annealed invariance under all exchangeable edge laws**, including arbitrarily dependent weights; it is not an unconditional formula for a deterministic weighted cycle. The full text of the 2000 paper was not accessible in the available sources, so whether it contains an equivalent exchangeable averaging result remains a priority risk.

Buchanan, Horn, and Rombach consider uniform last-visit behavior on directed graphs in the simple graph-walk framework; their identified results do not state the random-conductance conditional uniformity theorem. A comparison of accessible previously recorded mathematical claims and targeted mathematical research search results identified no exact coverage, although database absence does not establish priority.

## Limitations

Uniformity is an *environment-averaged* conclusion. It can fail after conditioning on the ordered conductance vector, as the explicit defect illustrates. Edge exchangeability is stronger than cyclic stationarity; no assertion is made under rotation-invariant environments alone, or for nonreversible site-bias random environments. Nothing is proved here about the cover time, its coupling to the last vertex, or multiwalker coverage. Scientific originality has only a qualified literature assessment; the closely related 2000 full text remains unavailable, and no independent audit or priority certification has been performed.
