# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed finite-poset nucleus normal form follows from the established Artinian-poset subset/nucleus theorem, and the explicit top and bottom action formulas follow from upper cones. Comparing the two compositions gives exactly the stated inclusion conditions. The matching argument proves acyclicity of every commuting incidence graph. A spanning-tree equality case forces all nontrivial lower subsets to have a common top neighbor and then an extreme Boolean subset; this identifies exactly the two maximizing families. The equal/strict-comparable/incomparable partition of ordered lower-subset pairs gives the count and the stated sharp balanced asymptotic. The standalone checker separately tests direct operator commutation and graph cliques in finite cases, and enumerates fixed-point families for the smallest case. No infinite proof is inferred from computation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty
Lindenhovius and Ochs give the subset-to-nucleus correspondence, not the present commuting relation. Escardó treats composition and joins in general, and Hemelaer counts topologies; none of the inspected relevant portions gives the forest-bound extremal theorem, its equality cases, or the ordered-pair law. Earlier one-root results treat a different domain and do not imply the two-sided incomparability argument needed here. Actual semantic and literature searches found no directly covering result. A synonymous older classification may exist and is not ruled out.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Determining the largest sets of closure modalities that commute under composition is a motivated, natural invariant, not a generic enumeration of all maps. The sharp upper bound, exactly two extremizers and two-parameter commuting probability law together supply structural and quantitative content, including a balanced exponential rarity regime. This does not rely on claiming that the already-known nucleus parametrization is novel.

## Sources and limitations
Ochs (arXiv:2107.08501, full text); Lindenhovius (arXiv:1405.4408, Theorem C.4 and corresponding appendices); Hemelaer (arXiv:1811.10039, the relevant counting sections); Escardó (Joins in the frame of nuclei, §§1,3). The theorem assumes complete two-level frames with at least two elements per level and does not classify all inclusion-maximal commuting families. An independent assessment has not been performed.
