# Extremal minimal supersequences of unequal opposite binary blocks

## Finding
Let \(a,b,c,d\ge 2\) be integers, and let \(A=0^a1^b\) and \(B=1^c0^d\). A *minimal common supersequence* (MCS) contains both inputs as scattered subsequences and ceases to contain at least one input after deletion of any one symbol. The maximum length of an MCS of \(A,B\) is exactly
\[
L=a+b+c+d-1.
\]
The number of distinct MCSs of this maximum length is exactly
\[
\boxed{2\binom{a+c-2}{a-1}\binom{b+d-2}{b-1}}.
\]
All maximizers fall into two disjoint explicitly described families. The **shared-one family** consists of \(X1Y\), where \(X\) contains exactly \(a\) zeros and \(c-1\) ones and ends in zero, whereas \(Y\) contains exactly \(d\) zeros and \(b-1\) ones and begins in zero. The **shared-zero family** consists of \(U0V\), where \(U\) contains exactly \(a-1\) zeros and \(c\) ones and ends in one, whereas \(V\) contains exactly \(d-1\) zeros and \(b\) ones and begins in one.

The product separates the two cross-run parameter pairs \( (a,c) \) and \( (b,d) \), and it is invariant under separately exchanging the two members of either pair. This is an extremal count, not a formula for the full spectrum of MCS lengths.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is the ordered two-letter set \(\{0,1\}\). Inputs and outputs are finite, without cyclic identification. An embedding of a word \(T\) into \(W\) is a strictly increasing position map whose selected letters spell \(T\). A position is *essential for* \(T\) if deleting it destroys every embedding of \(T\). The set \(E_T\) of essential positions is precisely the intersection of the position sets of all embeddings of \(T\). Thus \(|E_T|\le |T|\), with equality if and only if \(T\) has a unique embedding into \(W\). Minimality of a common supersequence means \(E_A\cup E_B\) is the entire set of positions. As the common-supersequence property is closed upward under taking supersequences, checking single deletions suffices for full subsequence-minimality.

All four exponents are at least two; the restriction is material. For instance, \(A=01\), \(B=100\) have three maximum-length MCSs of length four, whereas the displayed product with singleton runs would give only two.

## Proof
**1. Sharp upper bound.** Write \(m=|A|\), \(n=|B|\), and let \(W\) be any MCS. Essential positions of the two inputs cover \(W\), so \(|W|\le m+n\). Suppose equality. Necessarily \(|E_A|=m\), \(|E_B|=n\), these two sets are disjoint, and both inputs have unique embeddings whose images partition \(W\). Color each position by the input that owns it. Both inputs contain both symbols, so there are two differently colored positions carrying the same symbol. Choose such a pair \(i<j\) with minimum separation and write \(x=W[i]=W[j]\). Interchange the colors if necessary so that \(i\) belongs to the embedding of \(A\) and \(j\) to that of \(B\). Uniqueness of the \(A\)-embedding forces an \(A\)-colored position in \((i,j)\): otherwise \(i\) could be replaced by \(j\). Likewise uniqueness of the \(B\)-embedding forces a \(B\)-colored position in \((i,j)\): otherwise \(j\) could be replaced by \(i\). Neither interior position can contain \(x\), by minimality of \(j-i\). Over a binary alphabet they therefore both contain the opposite letter, giving a closer pair of differently colored equal letters. Contradiction. Hence \(|W|\le m+n-1\).

**2. The near-upper-bound rigidity lemma.** Suppose now that \(|W|=m+n-1\). Since \(|E_A|+|E_B|\ge m+n-1\), either both sets have maximal size \(m,n\), or one has exactly one fewer essential position and the other has its full size. We rule out the latter using the assumption that every constant run of both inputs has length at least two.

Assume, by symmetry, \(|E_A|=m-1\), \(|E_B|=n\). Then \(E_A\) and \(E_B\) are disjoint and partition \(W\). Every embedding of \(A\) uses all \(m-1\) fixed positions in \(E_A\) and exactly one additional position in \(E_B\). Because \(A\) does not have a unique embedding, choose distinct additional positions \(p<q\) from two embeddings. Write the ordered letters on \(E_A\) as \(e_1\cdots e_{m-1}\). Inserting the letter at \(p\) at some gap \(j\) among these \(m-1\) letters, or that at \(q\) at gap \(k\), must produce the same input \(A\). If \(j<k\), comparison of the two resulting words forces both inserted letters and \(e_j,\ldots,e_{k-1}\) to be identical; using both \(p,q\) while omitting position \(e_j\) still embeds \(A\). The supposedly essential position \(e_j\) could therefore be deleted, a contradiction. Thus the two insertions use the same gap \(j=k\), and the inserted letters are equal to \(A[j]\). Since every run of \(A\) has length at least two, its \(j\)-th letter has an equal neighboring letter. Replace that neighboring fixed position by the two ordered positions \(p,q\), assigning them to the two neighboring equal letters. This gives an embedding after deletion of another essential position, again a contradiction. The symmetric case, in which \(B\) has one fewer essential position, is identical. It follows that both inputs have unique embeddings. Their images cover \(W\) and meet in exactly one position.

**3. Locating the single shared letter.** In the unique embedding of \(A=0^a1^b\), its zeros are necessarily the first \(a\) zeros of \(W\), and its ones the last \(b\) ones of \(W\): these are the corresponding left- and right-greedy choices. Similarly, the ones of \(B\) are the first \(c\) ones and its zeros the last \(d\) zeros. The single common position is either a one or a zero. If it is a one, there are exactly \(b+c-1\) ones altogether, and the common one is simultaneously the \(c\)-th one of \(B\) and the first one of \(A\). All \(a\) zeros of \(A\) precede this one; all \(d\) zeros of \(B\) follow it. Hence \(W=X1Y\) with the prescribed symbol counts. If \(X\) ended in one, \(A\) could use that last one in place of its shared first one and drop its last embedded one, contradicting uniqueness. If \(Y\) began in one, \(B\) could move its last embedded one to the start of \(Y\), again contradicting uniqueness. Thus \(X\) ends in zero and \(Y\) begins in zero.

If the shared position is a zero, it must be the last zero embedded by \(A\) and the first zero embedded by \(B\). The same greedy-embedding argument, with symbols and boundary directions exchanged, gives exactly \(W=U0V\) with the displayed counts and the forced endpoint ones.

**4. Sufficiency and enumeration.** Conversely, any constructed \(X1Y\) has a unique embedding of \(A\), which uses all \(a\) zeros of \(X\) and then all \(b\) ones after the final zero of \(X\). It also has a unique embedding of \(B\), which uses all \(c\) ones before the initial zero of \(Y\) and then all \(d\) zeros of \(Y\). These embeddings cover all positions, so every position is essential for at least one input. Thus the word is an MCS. The analogous argument applies to \(U0V\). The types are disjoint because their unique embedding intersection carries different symbols.

For type \(X1Y\), choose the first \(a+c-2\) letters of \(X\) from \(a-1\) zeros and \(c-1\) ones; choose the final \(b+d-2\) letters of \(Y\) from \(b-1\) ones and \(d-1\) zeros. This gives \(\binom{a+c-2}{a-1}\binom{b+d-2}{b-1}\) distinct words. For type \(U0V\), the two analogous choices yield the same product by binomial symmetry. Both types attain length \(m+n-1\), establishing the bound and the formula.

## Verification
The standalone Python checker `verify.py` independently tests subsequence membership and every one-position deletion for each binary candidate at length \(a+b+c+d-1\), on all parameter quadruples \(2\le a,b,c,d\le4\) with \(a+b+c+d\le15\). It compares the *entire set* of extremizers, not merely their count, against two independently generated closed-form families. It checks the proposed numerical product, excludes MCSs at length \(a+b+c+d\) on all \(2\le a,b,c,d\le3\), and verifies a singleton-run counterexample. Reproduction: `python3 verify.py`. The recorded output is in `verification_output.txt`. Finite checks support but do not replace the proof for arbitrary parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Sopp, Liyanage, Gong and Zhu (arXiv:2603.22591, first posted 2026-03-23, MSC 68W32) give a general polynomial-delay enumeration algorithm for minimal common supersequences, rather than this closed four-parameter extremal classification. The manuscript's definitions, essential-position criterion and enumeration sections were inspected. Fraser, Irving and Middendorf's 1996 work introduced algorithms for finding *longest* minimal common supersequences; its companion thesis chapter gives a general dynamic program but no inspected closed run-family formula. An earlier exact all-length spectrum for the equal-parameter pair \(0^m1^m\), \(1^m0^m\) includes the diagonal special case \(a=b=c=d=m\) of this count. It does **not** determine the asymmetric four-variable statement, its two complete word families, or the independent pair-swap symmetries. General algorithms do not by themselves constitute this uniform closed-form classification.

## Limitations
This proof does not enumerate shorter MCSs, and it excludes singleton runs: the formula is false without a nondegeneracy condition. It does not prove any result for more than two input strings or alphabets larger than two. The original 1996 journal article and a 1995 binary-sequence paper by Middendorf could not be inspected in full; an equivalent unindexed theorem there is a residual originality risk. Independent external scientific review has not been performed.
