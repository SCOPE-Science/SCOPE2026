At twice every substitution length, the complete maximum-one-count factor class of a generalized Cantor word has exactly \(2q^k\) distinct words for \(q=3\), but only \(q^k+1\) for each \(q\ge4\). These classes asymptotically comprise one-half versus one-third of all distinct factors at those lengths; an explicit five-family decomposition proves the transition.
