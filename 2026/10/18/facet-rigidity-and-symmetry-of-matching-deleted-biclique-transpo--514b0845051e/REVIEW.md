# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(p,q\ge3\) and \(0\le k\le\min\{p,q\}\), the graph is connected, and the Lipschitz-free unit ball is exactly the convex hull of its signed edge differences because shortest-path molecules telescope. A polar vertex is characterized by the rank \(|V|-1\) of tight incidence rows, or equivalent tight-edge spanning connectivity. In any connected bipartite graph the parity of tight spanning-tree differences forces every original edge to have absolute potential difference one. A removed matching edge is either gap one (reducing to the published complete-bipartite two-family classification) or gap three (forcing all other vertex heights and generating exactly two signed exceptional facets). These cases are exhaustive and mutually disjoint except for the two explicitly shared baseline functions, yielding \(2^p+2^q-2+2k\). The automorphism group preserves or exchanges the unique bipartition, and its action on a binary family reduces exactly to complement-involution pairs \((j,\ell)\); Burnside gives the orbit formula and binomial orbit sizes, with the separate exception orbit. Separate exact computational checks: 72 spanning-tree enumeration cases, slack-permitting incidence-rank enumeration for small graphs, and 52 symmetry-orbit computations matching every orbit size; the script is embedded and can be replayed. No finite check is used to prove the infinite result.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk
The 2018 Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek Proposition 3.4 already contains the baseline complete-bipartite formula and classifies its two potential families; those are not claimed as new. The 2019 D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek Lemma 4.5 contains bipartite full-edge tightness; Chen–Davis–Korchevskaia 2021 gives general facet-subgraph and cut-set constraints. The published 2026 crown-inradius result **materially covers the full-matching balanced specialization**, including its gap-three patterns: the crown count is used solely as an illustrative covered specialization. The present distinct assertion is a complete rectangular-and-partial-matching deletion rigidity theorem, simultaneous exact count, and full orbit representatives and multiplicities for all \(p,q,k\); no inspected source contains or implies those explicit general formulas without substantive additional analysis. Braun–Bruegge's 2023 tables may cover isolated small numerical instances but not this complete family. published-finding corpus semantic searches for formulas, matching-deleted bicliques, crown variants, and orbit equivalents did not identify exact general coverage. Similar work outside indexed or accessible literature remains possible; absence of a match is not proof of originality.

## Value — PASS
Matching deletion is a canonical controlled perturbation of a complete bipartite graph metric, producing diameter-three pairs while preserving dense connectivity. Its facets could a priori respond nonlinearly to interacting deletions; instead **exactly two signed new facets arise per removed pair**, and their potential vectors admit a forced global normal form. The all-parameter automorphism orbit partition—including class counts, representatives and exact sizes—distinguishes label-equivalent facets and gives a nontrivial symmetry invariant of the associated finite-dimensional Banach norms. This is a natural infinite-family structural classification and perturbation-stability result, not a raw table recomputation or arbitrary fixed-size slice. It does not assert consequences for unrelated graph families or new graph-metric inradius results.
