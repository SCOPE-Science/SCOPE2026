# Factorial deterministic state complexity of odd-prime roots of permutation languages

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\) and every odd prime \(p\) with \(p\le n\), the maximal state complexity of the power-root language
\[
\operatorname{Root}_p(L)=\{w\in\Sigma^*:w^p\in L\}
\]
among languages whose **minimal complete** deterministic automaton has \(n\) states and every input letter acts as a permutation is exactly \(n!\). Two input letters attain the bound, whereas one letter cannot. The theorem concerns deterministic state complexity of the root, not nondeterministic complexity or the minimal size of its original automaton.

## Assumptions and scope
All automata have finite nonempty input alphabets, no epsilon-transitions, total deterministic transitions, and the rejecting state, when present, counts. A permutation automaton has bijective letter maps on its state set. Let \(S_n\) act on \(Q=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\) as functions, with \(gh=g\circ h\). If a word \(w\) induces \(g_w\), then \(g_{wx}=g_xg_w\). A prime here means a positive rational prime. Parameters: \(n\ge3\), odd prime \(3\le p\le n\). These restrictions are essential to the witness proof; the claim makes no assertion for exponents beyond them.

## Proof
**1. Universal upper bound.** Let \(A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)\) be any minimal complete \(n\)-state permutation DFA, and let \(G\le S_Q\) be its transition group. Inverses of letter permutations are positive powers, so the word transition semigroup equals the group generated by the letters. The power-root language is recognized by the complete DFA with states \(G\), initial state the identity, letter action \(g\mapsto g_xg\), and accepting group elements \(g\) satisfying \(g^p(q_0)\in F\). Thus its minimal state count is at most \(|G|\le n!\).

**2. Binary witness and minimality of the input automaton.** On \(Q\), take letters inducing the \(n\)-cycle \(\sigma=(0\ 1\ \cdots\ n-1)\) and transposition \(\tau=(0\ 1)\), initial state \(0\), and unique accepting state \(0\). These permutations generate \(S_n\): conjugating \(\tau\) by powers of the cycle gives adjacent transpositions around the cycle, which generate the full symmetric group. Hence all \(n\) states are reachable. For distinct states \(i,j\), a permutation sending \(i\) to \(0\) and \(j\) away from \(0\) is word-realizable, so the original automaton is minimal with exactly \(n\) states.

**3. Prime-cycle acceptance criterion.** Put \(B_p=\{g\in S_n:g^p(0)=0\}\). The length of the cycle of \(g\) containing \(0\) divides the prime \(p\) exactly when it is either \(1\) or \(p\). Therefore \(B_p\) is precisely the collection of permutations fixing \(0\) or having a \(p\)-cycle through \(0\). We prove the following nonstandard lemma with all quantifiers explicit:

**Lemma (trivial right-translation stabilizer).** For every \(n\ge p\ge3\) with \(p\) prime, and every nonidentity \(h\in S_n\), there is \(g\in B_p\) such that \(gh\notin B_p\).

*Proof.* If \(h(0)=0\), choose \(a\in Q\setminus\{0\}\) moved by \(h\) and put \(b=h(a)\). The three points \(0,a,b\) are distinct. Choose any additional \(p-3\) distinct points different from these (possible since \(n\ge p\)), and let \(g\) be the \(p\)-cycle \((0\ a\ c_1\ \cdots\ c_{p-3}\ b)\), fixing all other points. Then \(g\in B_p\); however, \((gh)(0)=g(0)=a\) and \((gh)(a)=g(h(a))=g(b)=0\). Thus \(0\) lies in a \(2\)-cycle of \(gh\), not in a cycle of length \(1\) or the odd prime \(p\). Hence \(gh\notin B_p\).

Now suppose \(h(0)\ne0\). If \(h\notin B_p\), choose \(g=e\) and finish. Otherwise the \(0\)-cycle of \(h\) has length \(p\). Put \(a=h(0)\), \(b=h^{-1}(0)\). Since \(p\ge3\), the three points \(0,a,b\) are distinct. Take \(g=(a\ b)\), which fixes \(0\) and therefore lies in \(B_p\). Then \((gh)(0)=g(a)=b\) and \((gh)(b)=g(0)=0\). Again \(0\) lies in a \(2\)-cycle of \(gh\), proving \(gh\notin B_p\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

**4. All group states are distinguishable.** The subset \(B_p\) has trivial right-translation stabilizer, by the lemma: only \(h=e\) satisfies \(B_p h=B_p\). In the power-root group automaton, every \(g\in S_n\) is reachable. For distinct group states \(u,v\), set \(h=u^{-1}v\ne e\). Since \(B_p h\ne B_p\), there exists \(x\in S_n\) for which the membership indicators of \(x\) and \(xh\) in \(B_p\) differ. There is a continuation word realizing \(x u^{-1}\); after reading it, the two states become \(x\) and \(x u^{-1}v=xh\). Exactly one accepts. Every pair of the \(n!\) states is therefore Myhill--Nerode distinguishable, proving the lower bound.

**5. Minimum alphabet size.** Over a unary alphabet the transition group is generated by one permutation, so its size is the order of a single element of \(S_n\). For \(n\ge3\) this cyclic subgroup is proper in the noncyclic group \(S_n\), hence has order strictly less than \(n!\). By Step 1, no unary witness attains the factorial value, while the binary construction does. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Caron, Hamel-De le Court, Luque, and Patrou's work on state-complexity modifiers studies the unrestricted square-root operation and its general transformation-monoid upper bounds, rather than the odd-prime-root sharp factorial bound for minimal permutation automata. Caron, Hamel-De le Court, and Luque's broader study of roots and Boolean operations supplies a potentially relevant general framework; its full text was not available during this comparison, so it remains an explicitly unresolved originality risk. Guyot's subword-closed-language treatment is a different input-language class. Onishchenko's May 2026 result determines the **nondeterministic** complexity of the square-root operation for general NFAs; it neither asserts this deterministic permutation-restricted bound nor the odd-prime witness classification. Work on concatenation squares of permutation automata concerns \(L^2\), not power roots. A known square-root bound for permutation languages does not itself imply the odd-prime case: the lemma above needs to rule out nontrivial stabilizers for a different accepting set with \(p\)-cycles.

## Limitations
Only odd prime exponents satisfying \(p\le n\) are covered. Composite exponents, large primes, bounds for other subclasses, optimal witness word lengths, and nondeterministic root complexity are not determined here. No independent mathematical audit has been performed. A potentially broader earlier treatment of root operations was identified from bibliographic evidence, but its full proof could not be compared; originality is therefore conditional on the examined accessible literature, not a claim of exhaustive priority.
