## originality

PASS

Translating rows to independent nonidentical truncated Poisson variables is mathematically natural. The known finite enumeration implies the basic distribution, but a closed finite count, without an asymptotic probability theorem, is not itself the stated factorial-scale total-variation theorem; undiscovered probabilistic restatements remain possible.

There is no argument that novelty follows from nonmatching words: a published stronger theorem for the same random complete-implication model would cover it. The inspected structural and probability inputs together are not already a literature claim of this concrete conclusion.

The old numerical finite enumeration is known-table coverage of ingredients, not an explicit database containing distributional limits; incomplete source access leaves a specific risk.

The final theorem is derivable from known enumerative data plus standard asymptotic probability machinery, but its nontrivial asymptotic comparison is not stated or automatically given by the prior count theorem; the novelty is a scoped asymptotic refinement rather than a claim of new finite algebra enumeration.
