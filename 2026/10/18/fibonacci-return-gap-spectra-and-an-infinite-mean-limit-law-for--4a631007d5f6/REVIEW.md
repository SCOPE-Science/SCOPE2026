# Research review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every finite-window gap belongs either to a binary-length shell boundary or to an inherited regular-language bridge. The ordered recurrence and Fibonacci tally recover exact multiplicities, including the duplicate bridge length three. The weak-limit mass sums to one, the geometric-tail identity is exact, and the series for the expected gap diverges. Independently, an enumerator built directly from binary zero-run checks reproduces every histogram and endpoint sum for 18 complete scales, but finite tests are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS relative to the inspected record, subject to residual risk.** OEIS A060142 is the full support, and OEIS A219609 already lists **the exact gap sequence** via odd elements; the prior data are acknowledged and not claimed to be new. Inspecting A060142, A219608 and A219609, including the latter's defining Haskell construction and table reference, yields no all-cutoff Fibonacci histograms or limiting law. Relevant automatic-sequence literature gives the underlying automaton and general gap constraints, not the quantitative theorem. The closest self-generating-set paper concerns a different closure rule. An uncatalogued equivalent explicit distribution remains possible.

## Value

**PASS.** Exact full-spectrum return-time multiplicities for a classical sparse automatic word are motivated by its known uneven zero runs and return statistics, not by a selected numerical slice. The explicit nondegenerate probability law and its infinite expectation quantify how common short returns coexist with arbitrarily rare, exponentially large gaps. This sharpens a previously tabulated gap sequence with uniform, mathematically explanatory all-scale results.

## Residual risks and scope

The theorem addresses only dyadic index intervals; empirical gap frequencies are normalized by selected-index pairs, not integer positions. Database indexing cannot rule out an earlier unpublished or unindexed equivalent histogram. Existing OEIS tables already determine finite initial gap values and must not be mistaken for a novelty claim.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
