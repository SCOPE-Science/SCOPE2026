# Fifteen conifold surface strata in the triangular-prism edge toric fivefold

## Finding
Let \(G=C_3\square K_2\), with vertices \(0,1,2,3,4,5\), be the triangular-prism graph. Its nine edges are numbered in this order:
\[
(01),(12),(02),(34),(45),(35),(03),(14),(25).
\]
Over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of arbitrary characteristic, define the projective edge toric variety
\[
X_G=\overline{\{[t_0t_1:t_1t_2:t_0t_2:t_3t_4:t_4t_5:t_3t_5:t_0t_3:t_1t_4:t_2t_5]\}}\subset\mathbb P^8_k,
\]
where the closure is taken over the algebraic torus. Then \(X_G\) is normal and five-dimensional. Its reduced singular locus has exactly fifteen irreducible components, twelve isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^2\) and three to \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). Consequently
\[
\deg\operatorname{Sing}(X_G)_{\mathrm{red}}=12+3\cdot2=18.
\]
All fifteen are two-dimensional torus-orbit closures. The completed local transverse ring at the generic point of each is
\[
K[[u,v,w,z]]/(uv-wz),
\]
where \(K\) is the residue field at that generic point. The non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus of \(X_G\) is precisely its singular support.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is algebraically closed, without characteristic restriction. The *edge polytope* \(P_G\) is the convex hull of the nine vectors \(e_i+e_j\) indexed by edges of \(G\), regarded in the rank-five integral affine lattice \(\sum x_i=2\). The edge-point differences span that complete lattice, as verified by the gcd of all maximal minors. Faces are represented by subsets of the indexed nine vertices of \(P_G\); orbit closures and normal cones refer to the ordinary projective normal toric variety \(X_G\), not to a possibly nonnormal monomial image. A generic transverse completion means the completion at the prime of the dense torus orbit of an irreducible surface component, after removing its two torus directions.

## Proof
**Normality and dimension.** The graph contains two disjoint odd triangles, and three edges join the triangles. Thus it satisfies the odd-cycle condition of Ohsugi--Hibi: every pair of vertex-disjoint odd cycles has an edge joining their vertices. No further pair of disjoint odd cycles exists on six vertices. The normality criterion for edge rings therefore gives normality of the graph's edge semigroup. The nine exponent points affinely span a rank-five lattice, so the projective variety has dimension five.

**Exact face and cone computation.** Delete the sixth coordinate from the edge vectors, giving nine integral points in \(\mathbb Z^5\). Every facet arises from five affinely independent points; enumerate every five-element choice, construct its primitive affine normal by signed maximal minors, and retain exactly the supporting hyperplanes with all nine points on one side. This produces twelve facets, with their sets of incident indexed vertices:

```
(0,1,2,3,6,7) (0,1,2,4,7,8) (0,1,2,5,6,8)
(0,1,3,5,6,7) (0,1,4,5,7,8) (0,2,3,4,6,7)
(0,2,4,5,6,8) (0,3,4,5,6,7) (1,2,3,4,7,8)
(1,2,3,5,6,8) (1,3,4,5,7,8) (2,3,4,5,6,8)
```

All faces are the nonempty intersections of subsets of these facets. The exact dimensional face counts, from dimensions zero through five, are
\[
(9,30,47,36,12,1).
\]
For each face \(Q\) of dimension \(d\), its normal cone has rank \(5-d\) and is generated by the primitive inward normals of the facets containing \(Q\). Such an affine toric chart is smooth along the corresponding orbit precisely when the cone is generated by a subset of a lattice basis. The test first compares the number of rays with \(5-d\) and, in the simplicial case, tests saturation by the gcd of all maximal integral minors. Exhaustive exact enumeration finds nonsmooth orbit counts \(9,24,15,0,0,0\) in dimensions zero through five, respectively. Every nonsmooth cone is nonsimplicial; all simplicial cones are saturated and smooth.

**Identification of the maximal singular strata.** The inclusion-maximal nonsmooth faces all have dimension two. Their complete vertex-index list consists of the following twelve triangles:

```
(0,1,7) (0,2,6) (0,4,7) (0,5,6)
(1,2,8) (1,3,7) (1,5,8) (2,3,6)
(2,4,8) (3,4,7) (3,5,6) (4,5,8)
```

and three quadrilaterals:

```
(0,3,6,7) (1,4,7,8) (2,5,6,8)
```

For the twelve triangles, the two primitive edge differences have lattice index one in their affine span. Their orbit closures are therefore projective planes with degree one. For each quadrilateral, opposite vertices add to the same vector, and the two independent edge differences form a saturated basis of the face lattice. Each is a unit lattice parallelogram whose toric orbit closure is the Segre quadric \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), of degree two. The toric orbit--face correspondence shows that all other nonsmooth orbits lie in the closures of these fifteen two-dimensional orbits, giving the complete reduced support and its degree.

**The transverse ordinary-node calculation.** Each of the fifteen dimension-two faces is contained in four facets. Their four primitive normal rays have rank three, and their unique primitive integer dependence has two coefficients \(+1\) and two coefficients \(-1\). Each triple of the four rays generates a saturated rank-three sublattice, by maximal-minor gcd one. Thus, in the quotient normal lattice, the four rays form the standard primitive conifold configuration. Its affine toric threefold is \(\operatorname{Spec}K[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\). Completing at the orbit generic point produces the asserted transverse node. Since precisely the nonsimplicial normal cones are nonsmooth, the toric \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality criterion shows that the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus agrees with the reduced singular support.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi and Hibi's normality criterion for finite-graph edge rings supplies the structural normality input and their later paper classifies *globally* simple/smooth graph edge polytopes. Tran and Ziegler's general edge-polytope study concerns extremal face numbers. None of the three inspected sources states the fifteen-surface singular-stratum list, its split into planes and Segre quadrics, its degree eighteen, or the fifteen generic conifold structures for this particular prism. The computation specializes standard toric methods to a single naturally occurring graph; the general methods and normality criterion are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The singular locus is described only as a *reduced support*. The result does not determine the full nonreduced Jacobian or Fitting singular scheme, multiplicities at intersections, completed local rings at smaller torus orbits, a resolution, or cohomological invariants. The claimed generic transverse analytic model does not assert every point of each surface has a product-neighborhood with a node. No claim of exhaustive coverage of otherwise unidentified equivalent literature is made. Independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
