# Fifteen singular components on the wheel-edge toric fivefold

## Finding
Let \(G=K_1*C_5\) be the wheel with vertex set \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), hub \(0\), and rim cycle \(1-2-3-4-5-1\). Form the lattice edge polytope
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i+e_j:\{i,j\}\in E(G)\}
\subset\{x\in\mathbb R^6:\textstyle\sum_i x_i=2\},
\]
and let \(X_P\) be the normal projective toric variety of its normal fan. Its **entire reduced singular locus** consists of exactly fifteen irreducible torus-orbit closures: ten copies of \(\mathbb P^2\) and five copies of \(\mathbb P^1\). The ten planes have generic transverse ordinary-threefold-node equation \(ab-cd=0\), and the five lines have generic transverse rank-one \(2\times3\) determinantal-cone equation (all \(2\times2\) minors). The support is non-equidimensional; the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus has the same underlying support.

For precise indexing, enumerate the ten vertices of \(P\) by
\[
v_0=\rho(01),\ldots,v_4=\rho(05),\quad
v_5=\rho(12),v_6=\rho(23),v_7=\rho(34),v_8=\rho(45),v_9=\rho(51),
\quad\rho(ij)=e_i+e_j.
\]
Write \(V(S)=\overline{O_{F_S}}\) for the normal-fan orbit closure of the face \(F_S=\operatorname{conv}\{v_i:i\in S\}\). The five line components have \(S\) equal to
\[
\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,4\}.
\]
The ten plane components have \(S\) equal to
\[
\begin{gathered}
\{0,5,9\},\{5,6,9\},\{1,5,6\},\{2,6,7\},\{5,6,7\},\\
\{4,8,9\},\{5,8,9\},\{7,8,9\},\{3,7,8\},\{6,7,8\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The normal fan has twelve rays and 153 cones. The numbers of faces of dimensions zero through five are \((10,35,55,40,12,1)\); singular torus-orbit counts in dimensions zero, one, and two are \((10,25,10)\), and all orbits of higher dimension are smooth. These statements hold over every algebraically closed field, in any characteristic.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the intrinsic character lattice generated by vertex differences in the sum-two hyperplane, and its full dual lattice for normal-cone rays. Dropping the sixth coordinate identifies this character lattice with \(\mathbb Z^5\); the Smith normal form of the nine differences from \(v_0\) has five unit invariant factors. The toric variety is **the normal-fan variety**, irrespective of which homogeneous edge-ring embedding is considered. The singular locus is reduced set-theoretic support, not the scheme defined by a Jacobian ideal.

## Proof
In dropped coordinates \(y=(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\), the polytope has the following twelve complete facet inequalities \(n_i\cdot y\ge c_i\); each \(n_i\) is primitive in the actual dual lattice. The right column gives the indices of vertices on the facet.

| \(i\) | \(n_i\) | \(c_i\) | vertex indices |
|---:|---|---:|---|
|0|(-1,0,0,0,0)|-1|01234|
|1|(-1,-1,-1,-1,-1)|-2|0123567|
|2|(0,0,0,0,1)|0|0124569|
|3|(0,0,0,1,0)|0|0134589|
|4|(0,0,1,0,0)|0|0234789|
|5|(0,-1,0,-1,0)|-1|025679|
|6|(0,-1,0,0,-1)|-1|035789|
|7|(0,1,0,0,0)|0|1234678|
|8|(0,0,-1,0,-1)|-1|135678|
|9|(1,1,0,1,1)|1|145689|
|10|(1,1,1,0,1)|1|246789|
|11|(1,0,0,0,0)|0|56789|

The table is exhaustive: a facet of a five-polytope contains at least five affinely independent vertices. Loop over the \(\binom{10}{5}=252\) five-subsets, solve the one-dimensional rational annihilator of each rank-four difference matrix, normalize to a primitive integer vector, orient its inequality toward all ten points, and retain support sets with affine dimension four. Exactly the twelve table entries survive. Intersecting their vertex subsets gives exactly 153 nonempty faces, with the stated face vector. The included `verify.py` implements these exact operations (no floating-point rank tests).

For a face \(F\) of dimension \(d\), its normal cone has rank \(5-d\), and its primitive rays are exactly the inward facet normals active on \(F\). If these rays number more than \(5-d\), the cone is nonsimplicial and singular. If their number equals \(5-d\), Smith normal form decides whether they generate their saturated lattice (smoothness). Executing this test on **every face** yields ten singular vertices, twenty-five singular edges, ten singular triangles, and no others. Each simplicial cone has Smith index one, so no simplicial singularity was omitted. Taking inclusion-maximal singular faces in the face lattice yields precisely the fifteen index sets above. Torus-orbit closure reverses face inclusion, proving that the corresponding fifteen orbit closures are exactly the irreducible components of the reduced singular support.

For each of the ten triangle faces, the two difference vectors of its three lattice vertices span a saturated rank-two sublattice (Smith index one). Hence its orbit closure is the toric variety of a unimodular triangle, namely \(\mathbb P^2\). The five edge faces are primitive segments, hence have orbit closures \(\mathbb P^1\).

For each maximal singular triangle face, its four primitive normal rays span a saturated rank-three lattice, and their unique primitive dependence has two coefficients \(+1\) and two \(-1\). Up to unimodular coordinates, this is the fan cone defining \(k[a,b,c,d]/(ab-cd)\); a torus factor accounts for the two-dimensional orbit. At each maximal singular edge, the five primitive normal rays span a saturated rank-four lattice and their unique primitive dependence has two coefficients of one sign and three of the other, all of magnitude one. The affine transverse model is consequently the \(2\times3\) rank-one determinantal cone. These models are independent of characteristic because all lattice relation coefficients and saturation indices equal one. Since every simplicial cone was shown unimodular, the singular support agrees set-theoretically with the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial support.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` using Python 3 with SymPy installed. This standalone file reconstructs graph-edge vertices, obtains all facets from every five-vertex subset by exact rational nullspaces, reconstructs every face by intersections, computes exact ranks and Smith indices for all face cones, and verifies both families of maximal singular faces and the transverse ray relations. Its recorded output is

```
VERIFY_OK {'facets': 12, 'faces': 153, 'f_vector': (10, 35, 55, 40, 12, 1), 'singular_orbits': {0: 10, 1: 25, 2: 10}, 'maximal_singular_faces': {1: 5, 2: 10}}
```

The proof is finite **and exhaustive**, because all facets and face cones are enumerated by certified integer/rational operations. No assertion about uncomputed infinite families is inferred from small-case examples.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi--Hibi (2008), *Simple polytopes arising from finite graphs*, classifies when edge polytopes are simple and smooth and provides criteria for edges. Tran--Ziegler (2013/2014), *Extremal edge polytopes*, develops edge-face criteria, face-number results, and extremal constructions. The present result concerns a particular non-simple five-polytope and supplies all maximal singular normal-fan faces and their saturated local toric models. Neither cited source gives this fifteen-component singular support or its two transverse types. Other graph polytopes made from signed roots or graphical zonotopes are different polytopes and do not imply this classification. The prior general facet and edge criteria are compatible with, but do not subsume, the explicit normal-cone classification here.

## Limitations
No nonreduced singular/Jacobian scheme, special analytic germs where orbit closures intersect, singular-support Hilbert polynomial, or primary decomposition is claimed. The result uses the **normal fan of the ordinary unsigned edge polytope**, not the symmetric edge polytope or graphical zonotope of the same graph. A full human literature review could identify an implicit equivalent classification; absence from targeted searches alone is not a uniqueness certificate. Independent audit is not claimed.
