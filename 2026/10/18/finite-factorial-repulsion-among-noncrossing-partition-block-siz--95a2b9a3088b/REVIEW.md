# Review: Finite factorial repulsion among noncrossing partition block sizes

## Correctness

PASS. The recursive noncrossing decomposition and Lagrange inversion give the exact all-order moment kernel. The residual-weak-composition factorization is reconstructed, including the boundary \(m\ge N+1\). The critical binomial ratio is decreasing in each positive marked size; its maximal value has nonnegative denominator difference \(4N(N-1)(N-2)+(4N^2-7N+1)h+(3N-2)h^2+h^3\) for \(N\ge2\), \(h\ge0\). The equality case \((N,m,r,s)=(2,3,1,1)\) does not occur at the initial stage for \(n\ge3\). Telescoping proves the strict all-order statement. The finite verifier independently checks 83,058 exact assertions, and the three-element counterexample is evaluated directly. Computation is not an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS on inspected evidence, subject to historical priority risk. Kargin, arXiv:1907.00632v1, Section 2.2, proves the marked generating function, exact first and pair mixed moments, and an asymptotically negative pairwise covariance. None of these is claimed as new. The assessed statement concerns strict finite inequalities for arbitrarily high mixed falling-factorial orders, and a precise counterexample to ordinary negative association. Searches for all-order block-size factorial negative dependence, conditioned weak-composition versions, Narayana/Lorentzian dominators, and the closest mathematical records did not identify coverage. The exact prior enumerator could admit an equivalent unstated corollary; this remains an explicit risk. Mutafchiev–Savov studies a different, unrestricted partition model; only its abstract was inspected.

## Value

PASS. Simultaneous block-size multiplicities and factorial moments are natural observables in noncrossing random partitions. The theorem upgrades fixed-size asymptotic pairwise behavior to every finite size and all mixed orders, including large blocks, while locating the precise boundary of the dependence conclusion. Positive covariance of increasing presence indicators at size three demonstrates why a stronger negative-association assertion would be wrong.

## Limitations

No arbitrary-increasing-function negative association, independent priority verification, or non-classical Coxeter-type extension is claimed. The supporting exact coefficient mechanism is preexisting.
