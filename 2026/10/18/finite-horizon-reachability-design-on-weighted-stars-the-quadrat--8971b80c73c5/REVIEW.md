# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is quantified for every integer number of leaves at least two, every fixed positive total conductance, and every sufficiently small positive continuous-time horizon. The star Krylov determinant is derived by a polynomial-recursion/Vandermonde identity; the companion representation proves joint analyticity even on the singular simplex boundary. The universal quadratic Gramian coefficient follows from an exponential alternant expansion and three explicit Jacobi-ensemble moments derived with shifted Legendre orthogonal polynomials. Strict concavity and the implicit-function theorem give the unique global ordered optimum, and two Hessian identities yield the explicit second-order drift. The packaged standard-library exact rational program reproduces all 39 stated finite identities in 23 finite examples. Its finite scope is distinguished from the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS, with remaining literature risk.** Lou–Hong's star controllability calculation provides a Vandermonde factor, and classical Laguerre electrostatics provides the leading allocation; neither is represented as new. An existing discrete-time shortest-horizon weighted-star Gramian optimum has exactly the Laguerre weights, but the continuous-time Gramian is different: it has the proved nonzero quadratic change for positive time. Becker–Pequito–Pappas–Preciado study graph edge tuning under minimum-eigenvalue and trace-inverse constraints using optimization relaxations, rather than the exact time-dependent determinant optimum. Trélat–Zuazua give the general small-time determinant through the linear trace term, with quadratic terms in the remainder; their inspected Corollary 2 does not imply the explicit quadratic coefficient or the star-design drift. Extensive literal and semantic searches have not identified an exact prior for the final conclusion. Remaining risk: a different field may have already written the same design perturbation in equivalent notation.

**Value — PASS.** The determinant is the squared reachable-ellipsoid volume under a scalar actuation energy limit, so finite-horizon conductance allocation is an intrinsic control-network design question. The theorem identifies the first nonzero correction to the classical limiting design, shows how each edge must be redistributed, and proves global uniqueness rather than merely stationarity. At two leaves the coefficient is explicitly nonzero. The effect is not a cosmetic restatement of the known Laguerre extremum and contrasts with the exact horizon-independence of the shortest-step discrete analogue.

**Limits and evidence.** No largest admissible horizon, higher-order coefficients, other trees, heterogeneous prices, or independent expert validation are claimed. Relevant sources were compared at the level of actual objective, normalization, scope, and statement implication, including primary-paper material and accessible historical claims.
