# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Dyadic binary coding converts each threshold observation into an exactly specified sliding-block factor of independent fair bits. The probability of a constant observed word is an integer count divided by a power of two, with matching constructive lower and disjoint-block upper exponential bounds. Assuming finite Markov order would force the integer counts to have an eventually constant rational ratio; infinite divisibility forces that ratio to be an integer, excluded by the strict growth bounds. The half threshold gives independent digits. The included integer-code checks test finite cases independently and are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS on the comparison performed, with historical-priority risk. Alcaraz Barrera's 2015 doubling-map paper treats hole survivors and intrinsic ergodicity, not the stationary Markov order of every dyadic threshold observation. Boyle–Petersen's general hidden Markov results give algebraic criteria in principle but not this explicit all-cutoff classification. Related prior work on a logistic-map quarter threshold identifies a specific different observed process; it does not imply the dyadic-cutoff classification. No directly equivalent theorem was identified in searches of mathematical research records and accessible previous claims. Earlier unindexed or differently phrased symbolic-factor theorems remain possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The finite-versus-infinite-memory boundary for a natural family of interval sensors is a structural classification rather than a numerical special case. It separates finite hidden-state realizability from finite Markov order for every non-half dyadic resolution and gives explicit uniform exponential bounds on long constant-symbol observations. The result has natural relevance to symbolic observation, coarse-graining, and information retention under an expanding dynamical map. It does not purport to classify nondyadic observations.

## Scientific limits
The formula is exact only under Lebesgue stationary initialization with a half-open threshold sensor and dyadic cutoffs. No claim of exact per-cutoff persistence exponent or of absolute historical priority is made. Human evaluation of originality remains advisable.
