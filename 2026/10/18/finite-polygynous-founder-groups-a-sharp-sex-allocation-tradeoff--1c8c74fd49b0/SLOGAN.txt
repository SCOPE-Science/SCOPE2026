For a founder group with \(n\ge3\) independently assigned sexes and male capacity \(h\ge2\), the unique female-birth probability maximizing expected mating females exceeds \(1/2\), whereas the probability of any mating is uniquely maximized at \(1/2\); at fixed capacity the output optimum has a sharp binomial-quantile and normal-correction law. When capacity satisfies \(h\ge n-1\), the output-optimal expected number of males grows as \(\log n\) rather than a fixed reserve, and mating failure is asymptotic to \(1/n\).
