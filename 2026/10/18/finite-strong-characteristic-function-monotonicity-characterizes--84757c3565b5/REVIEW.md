# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The all-tilts condition makes the generating polynomial's modulus nonincreasing on **every** upper semicircle. A nonreal zero would force vanishing on a circular arc, impossible for a nonzero polynomial. Positivity on the nonnegative real axis then forces all zeros negative, and normalized linear factorization proves the independent-Bernoulli representation. The converse follows from strict decrease of each tilted Bernoulli-factor modulus. The full quadratic boundary and the example follow by exact algebra and an explicit complex root. The illustrative checker was executed successfully. The claim is restricted to finite support.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected evidence. Aamand et al.'s definitions, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2 prove the Bernoulli sufficiency direction, but not the finite-support necessity. Tang and Tang's Section 4 reports the earlier real-rooted-polynomial characterization, but never compares that characterization with strong monotonicity under all tilts. Searches for the condition and its Fourier/polynomial aliases, together with comparisons against accessible archived claims, found no equivalent necessity theorem. The argument is short, and similar facts could be implicit in classical polynomial literature; this remains a genuine limitation of the originality assessment.

## Value
PASS. The condition is a hypothesis for sharp tilted point-probability estimates, so identifying **all** finitely supported laws satisfying it closes a natural classification gap and reveals that this ostensibly broad assumption yields no additional bounded-support laws beyond Poisson-binomial ones. The exact three-atom inequality shows concretely why mere monotonicity is insufficient. No improvement to the constants in the local limit theorem is claimed.
