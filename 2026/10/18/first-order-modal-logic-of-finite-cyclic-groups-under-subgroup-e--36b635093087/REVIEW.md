# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact statement concerns finite cyclic groups, subgroup inclusion up to isomorphism, and first-order group **sentence** substitutions. Baur–Monk reduces each sentence to finite pp-subgroup index inequalities. Integer Smith normal form proves the needed local exponent shape; every such index is either eventually decided solely by finitely many capped prime valuations or becomes true above a finite cardinality bound. The explicit finite quotient records the finitely many exceptional small orders and is verified to satisfy both directions of bounded-morphism accessibility. Its target is finite, directed and antisymmetric. The Boolean-cube quotient is surjective, has an explicit lifting witness, and admits definable pullback of every cube valuation. Independent finite computations exercise both morphism constructions and finite-frame modal axioms. The infinite argument depends on the stated published Baur–Monk and finite-Boolean Grz.2 theorems.

## Originality — PASS, subject to documented search limitations
The full-text 2023 primary source proves S4.2 for all abelian groups, not Grz.2 for finite cyclic groups. Bova–Martin establish the nonmodal logical elimination, and the later Wołoszyn manuscript the non-group-theoretic Boolean-cube completeness. Their theorems separately do not state the restricted group classification, and the critical new deduction is a uniform finite **directed p-morphic quotient** for arbitrary first-order cyclic-group sentences, not the already-known control statements. Searches for finite cyclic structure modal logic, Grz.2, divisibility-frame p-morphisms, and subgroup embeddings found no directly equivalent claim. Bibliographic risks: a cited 2018 thesis was not obtained in full, and equivalent unindexed model-theoretic formulations may exist. Lack of a hit is not proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The result determines a complete structure modal logic for a natural, infinite algebraic subclass that behaves strictly differently from its parent class. It identifies the logical mechanism for this strengthening (finite-state behavior of first-order descriptions) and separates it sharply from unrestricted frame semantics. The structural quotient lemma is reusable beyond the exact modal classification and identifies an explicit boundary of the known abelian-group result.

## Scope and limitations
This finding is not an unconditional frame logic statement and does not extend to all finite abelian groups. The cited later modal characterization is a necessary input for the exact upper bound. Independently checked finite examples cannot replace the infinite proof. Full bibliographic uniqueness is not claimed.
