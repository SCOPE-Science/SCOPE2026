# Review of dyadic cototient-height maximizer fibers

**Correctness — PASS.** For all integers \(1\le n\le2^{19}\), two exact sieve methods compute the same Euler totient values. The well-founded recurrence \(C(n)=1+C(n-\varphi(n))\), with strictly smaller positive arguments, then computes the exact height of every integer in the finite domain. The complete list of maximizing arguments in each of 19 disjoint dyadic intervals is reproduced and checked against `MAXIMIZERS.json`. The joint table includes 123 maximizers, 44 at scale 18, and precisely one at scale 19: 462210. This is an exhaustive finite classification, not an infinite inference.

**Originality — PASS for the repaired, narrower contribution, with residual risks.** OEIS A098197, dating to 2004, gives first-hit positions at each cototient trajectory length; its entries already imply the first three-unit maximum jump at \(k=19\), \(M_{18}=30\), \(M_{19}=33\), and the least argument 462210 reaching 33. None of those numbers is asserted as new. The full sets of *all* maximizing arguments for each \(k\le19\), and hence their cardinality profile, are not implied by the first-hit table. OEIS A071575 and Ellis et al.'s Theorem 4.8 describe the height function and attainable-height intervals, not the complete argmax fibers. An unindexed complete census could cover the latter and remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS, confined to finite extremal arithmetic dynamics.** The cutoff is determined by the previously known first anomalous three-unit jump, not chosen arbitrarily after experimentation. A complete classification of extremizers at each earlier scale, particularly the collapse from 44 tied maxima to a single exceptional maximizer, is a natural exact benchmark for the published dyadic height stratification. It supplies a specific multiplicity and membership fact unavailable from first-hit values alone; there is no claim to a general growth theorem.

**References and limits.** OEIS A098197 and its indices 1–40, OEIS A071575, and Ellis–Shi–Thanatipanonda–Tu, *Two Games on Arithmetic Functions: SALIQUANT and NONTOTIENT* (2023), arXiv:2309.01231, Section 4. The certificate covers \(n\le524288\) only. Exact agreement of independently implemented recurrences supports numerical correctness; publication priority requires separate literature comparison.
