# First two ambiguity layers of directed colour refinement on finite tournaments

## Finding
For uncoloured, finite nonempty tournaments in the single-arc language, directed colour refinement (one-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman refinement) distinguishes every pair of nonisomorphic tournaments of cardinality at most five. At order six the \(56\) isomorphism types yield exactly \(55\) counting-two-variable, \(C^2\), classes (one class of size two, all others singletons). At order seven the \(456\) isomorphism types yield exactly \(451\) classes (three doubletons, one tripleton, and the remaining \(447\) singleton classes).

Here are *all* the nonsingleton classes, represented by the canonical integer codes defined below:

| Vertices | Nontrivial class of canonical codes | Sorted outdegrees | Numbers of transitive four-vertex subtournaments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(6\) | `9378`, `9381` | \(2,2,2,3,3,3\) | \(0,3\) |
| \(7\) | `9378`, `9381` | \(0,3,3,3,4,4,4\) | \(12,15\) |
| \(7\) | `559362`, `559365` | \(2,2,2,3,4,4,4\) | \(6,9\) |
| \(7\) | `594448`, `594468` | \(2,2,2,3,3,3,6\) | \(12,15\) |
| \(7\) | `232512`, `232706`, `238754` | \(3,3,3,3,3,3,3\) | \(7,3,0\) |

In particular, six is the smallest cardinality at which two distinct tournament isomorphism types agree in \(C^2\). The order-seven tripleton consists of exactly the three regular tournaments of that order.

## Assumptions and scope
A **tournament** is a finite, nonempty, irreflexive structure \(T=(V,R)\), where exactly one of \(R(u,v)\) and \(R(v,u)\) holds for every two distinct vertices. Structures are uncoloured and have no named constants. Equivalent means agreeing on every sentence of two-variable first-order logic with counting quantifiers, \(C^2\). The usual directed version of colour refinement starts with all vertices the same colour, and repeatedly recolours a vertex by its old colour and the numbers of out- and in-neighbours in each old colour. Two tournaments are indistinguishable when the stable globally synchronized colour histograms agree. The equivalence between this test and \(C^2\) is standard for finite binary relational structures (see Kiefer, Chapter 6).

In the *canonical integer code* of an \(n\)-vertex tournament, write the upper triangular part of its zero-diagonal adjacency matrix in row order as a binary integer, where a bit \(1\) means the arc goes from the smaller index to the larger. Sort the vertices by **nondecreasing outdegree**; among vertices with equal outdegree, try every permutation and retain the numerically smallest integer. Thus all codes in the table are within their separately specified order. The included verifier implements these conventions without outside libraries.

## Proof
**Complete generation of isomorphism classes.** An isomorphism preserves outdegrees. Consequently any isomorphism between degree-sorted representations permutes only vertices with equal outdegree. Taking the minimum upper-triangle integer over *all* such permutations is therefore a complete, collision-free canonical isomorphism invariant. The one-vertex tournament initializes the census. Every \(n\)-vertex tournament is obtained by deleting a vertex and then adding it back to an \((n-1)\)-vertex tournament, orienting independently its incident arcs to all old vertices. Therefore, taking every previous canonical representative and every possible incident-arc mask and canonicalizing the extension enumerates all and only the new isomorphism classes. The inductively exhaustive enumerator returns \(1,1,2,4,12,56,456\) at orders \(1\) through \(7\); the last six numbers also match the independent tournament catalogue of McKay.

**Exact directed refinement.** For each fixed order, initialize every vertex of every canonical representative with the same global colour. Replace the old colour of each vertex by the tuple consisting of (i) that old colour, (ii) counts of outgoing arcs to vertices of *each* old colour, and (iii) counts of incoming arcs from vertices of *each* old colour; map distinct tuples to one single new colour palette shared across **all** representatives. Repeat until the number of distinct colours no longer increases. A refinement cannot merge colours because the old colour is part of the key; hence this termination test proves stability. Equality of the resulting vertex-colour multisets is exactly directed colour-refinement equivalence, hence \(C^2\)-equivalence. Exhaustive finite evaluation, reproduced by `verify_tournaments.py`, yields precisely the nonsingleton code sets in the table and no others. The code checks every extension, every degree-preserving permutation needed for canonicalization, and every vertex and refinement round; it is an exhaustive bounded certificate, not an inference from sampling.

**Explicit order-six obstruction.** Take two triples \(A=\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}\) and \(B=\{b_0,b_1,b_2\}\), subscripts modulo three. Direct \(a_i\to a_{i+1}\) cyclically. Between the triples direct \(a_i\to b_i\), and direct \(b_j\to a_i\) for \(j\ne i\). In one tournament direct \(b_i\to b_{i+1}\) cyclically; in the other, reverse those three arcs. Every vertex of \(A\) has two outgoing arcs (one to its own triple and one to \(B\)), and every vertex of \(B\) has three (one inside \(B\), two to \(A\)). Therefore the initial outdegree partition \(A\sqcup B\) is equitable for both orientations; directed refinement never splits either triple, and both tournaments have exactly the same two colour histograms. They are not isomorphic because they contain respectively \(3\) and \(0\) transitive four-vertex subtournaments. Canonicalization gives `9381` and `9378` respectively.

The exhaustive computation proves there are no other order-six collisions and that at order seven exactly the displayed four classes remain. In the order-seven tripleton every vertex has outdegree three; all three tournaments are regular, so refinement leaves one cell, while their different four-subtournament counts prove nonisomorphism. Different cardinalities are always distinguished by the counting quantifiers themselves.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_tournaments.py` with Python 3 and only its standard library. It prints the complete isomorphism and \(C^2\)-class counts for orders \(1\) through \(7\) and the exact nonsingleton code sets; its assertions cover every enumerated object, the full ambiguity classification, the explicit pair, and nonisomorphism via transitive quadruples. To avoid reliance on an external catalogue, the verifier constructs representatives recursively from the one-vertex base and checks that each code is canonical. The standard tournament totals provide an independent published consistency comparison, not the logical-classification proof. In the executable, synchronous refinement is performed jointly across each order's full representative family rather than by independently normalized per-graph palettes.

## Relationship to prior work
Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky (2015) provide the general colour-refinement and counting-logic framework for undirected graphs; they do not tabulate the directed tournament strata. Kiefer's later (2020) Chapter 6 establishes a far stronger general *individual identification* criterion for vertex/edge-coloured partially oriented graphs, including tournaments. In particular, it already implies that different regular tournaments cannot be individually identified, and its proof contains mechanisms for two cyclic classes. Thus the mere existence of an ambiguous six-vertex tournament or of three regular seven-vertex tournaments is **not** claimed as a new general phenomenon. The present bounded contribution is the simultaneous complete \(C^2\)-equivalence census for *all* tournament isomorphism types through seven vertices, including the exact \(55\)/\(451\) spectrum and the four complete seven-vertex collision classes. McKay's exhaustive tournament catalogue provides the \(56\)/\(456\) isomorphism counts but does not tabulate \(C^2\)-equivalence classes. Broader tournament isomorphism results of Grohe and Neuen consider higher-dimensional refinement, not the exact one-dimensional small-order partition.

## Limitations
This is a complete classification *only through order seven*, not a formula for arbitrary tournament cardinality. Its exhaustiveness rests on the stated finite canonical-extension algorithm and its actual executable verification; the explicit pair and the regularity observation alone cannot establish the full census. No claim of independent review or formal proof-assistant certification is made. Despite inspection of directly relevant literature, an earlier unlocated exact logical-equivalence tabulation is a residual priority risk. General individual-identification criteria already imply parts of the existence statements; originality is specifically limited to the complete small-order equivalence strata and exact counts.
