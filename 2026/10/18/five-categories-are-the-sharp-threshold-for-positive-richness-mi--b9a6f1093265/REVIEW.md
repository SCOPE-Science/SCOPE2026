# Review of the five-category richness–missing-mass threshold

## Correctness

**PASS.** The covariance identity follows from two explicit expectations: \(\mathbb EM_2=1-2s_2+s_3\) and \(\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{X_1=X_2\}}M_2]=s_2-s_3\). The support exclusion reduces, at fixed \(s_2\), to a constrained maximization of \(s_3\). Lagrange multiplier and second-order tangent-space arguments force every nonuniform maximizing active support to have one large mass and equal smaller masses. Each support size at most four then has a strictly negative factorization. A five-atom exact witness has covariance \(1/1600\), and continuity supplies larger supports. Exact rational checks complement but do not replace the universal proof. The one-atom boundary is zero.

## Originality

**PASS on inspected primary material, with historical-priority risk.** Chebunin and Zuyev (arXiv:1906.10949, full published open-access text examined) give joint occupancy/missing-mass limit theorems and Poissonized covariance, not a finite two-draw support threshold. Lee and Böhme (arXiv:2402.05835; ICLR 2025 Appendix D.3, inspected) do derive general covariance components between frequency counts and missing mass: the raw covariance identity is therefore explicitly treated as known-in-principle, not as the novelty. The new proposition is the *sharp five-atom sign possibility boundary*, including full exclusion of one through four atoms. Inspected mathematical records on successive discoveries, richness–frequency count covariance and richness–Gini–Simpson covariance have different statistics and do not imply the threshold. The residual risk is an older equivalent constrained power-sum inequality or support-bound theorem.

## Value

**PASS.** In a fixed-size sample, richness is normally expected to be anticorrelated with unseen mass. The theorem identifies the precise least alphabet size at which heterogeneity reverses that sign and demonstrates why an independent Poissonized approximation can conceal the effect. The minimal-support cutoff is a natural complete classification at the first nonconstant richness horizon, rather than a chosen grid point or arbitrary narrow numerical example.

## Scope and remaining limitations

The result is restricted to two observations and strictly positive finite-support laws; it neither describes higher sample sizes nor all possible sign regions and extrema when support is five or more. It uses standard moment and constrained-optimization methods. No claim of a new general covariance formula is made. Independent literature-priority and mathematical review have not been performed.
