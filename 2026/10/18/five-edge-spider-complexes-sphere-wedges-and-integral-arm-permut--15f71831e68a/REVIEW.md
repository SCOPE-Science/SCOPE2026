# Review of the five-edge spider theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact vertex/face definitions reduce admissibility to avoidance of induced three-vertex paths. The two essential finite complexes are proved by explicit free-face collapses: the four-vertex path complex collapses onto the three-vertex path circle (three pairs), and the five-vertex path complex collapses to a point (eleven pairs). The center-link decomposition is obtained by partitioning faces according to whether one center-neighbor is selected. Each inclusion of an attaching sphere into the join is a homotopy equivalence, yielding the wedge by consecutive cone attachments and a final suspension. Over the integers, the relative long exact sequence gives the augmentation lattice with signs controlled by even-degree join permutations. Complete finite checks for two and three arms agree with the homology and all signed symmetry characters. Residual risk: the homotopy-pushout proof must be interpreted for geometric realizations; no claim of a global simplicial collapse is made.

**Originality — PASS.** The closest papers [1]–[3] were inspected in accessible full text. Sawant establishes a general vertex-decomposition theorem for trees, not explicit homotopy multiplicities or the equivariant integral lattice; Deshpande–Singh explicitly treat a list of distinct tree/graph families, and their path result covers the degenerate two-arm case but not the branching family; Ghosh–Selvaraja establish shellability criteria, not the lattice. Kumar–Sarkar study algebraic regularity. Exact, alias, stronger-coverage, and implication searches of prior mathematical records and accessible historical claims did not identify a theorem covering the five-edge branching spiders. Risk: specialized unindexed three-path-free spider results could still exist.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural infinite family of subdivided stars whose two-independence complexes have explicitly increasing sphere multiplicities, refining the qualitative vertex-decomposability theorem for trees. The exact integer automorphism module distinguishes different arm symmetries and cannot be deduced from shellability or ordinary Betti numbers. The result gives an explicit structural center-link calculation, not a renaming of a known graph type or a finite observation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
