# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a finite periodic union, doubling is bijective on the union and no two predecessors are antipodal. A selector for a containing flower must realize each required predecessor in its completed graph. If a boundary occurs at a sampled image, shift its jump a small amount to keep the required trace; only one of the two traces is required there. In numerical coordinates the two branches on [0,1) have labels 0 and 1; they exchange at the circular seam. The exact minimum is thus the number of adjacent numerical label changes plus one if the first and last labels agree. The upper bound is realized by putting one jump in each forced gap. For a reduced slope k/n<1/2, mechanical phases j/n are strictly lexicographically ordered by first differing floor partial sums; the preceding digit is one exactly for j<k. The no-11 property places lower-half points of the orbit below 1/3 and upper-half points below 2/3, with equality only for the excluded alternating orbit. In the lower half the original and reflected points therefore form two disjoint consecutive groups with labels 1^k 0^(n−2k) and 1^k. The upper half reverses and complements this sequence, yielding six nonempty blocks, five internal jumps and no seam jump. Complementation exchanges slopes k/n and 1−k/n up to cyclic phase; the singleton zero and period-two alternating orbit each need one petal. Independent exact-fraction tests on all 138 reduced slopes below one-half with denominators 3–30 matched the label string and minimum. To replay, encode each rotation by its base-two numerator over 2^n−1, adjoin negatives modulo that denominator, map each point to its double and read the unique predecessor bits in sorted image order. These are bounded diagnostics; the formula and minimum are proved by the selector and ordering arguments. The frozen verify.py was inspected, not run.

## originality

PASS

The one-orbit flower criterion and mechanical coding are prior inputs, not new contributions. Brown's exact one-orbit criterion uses the antipodal translate of a periodic orbit, not its circle reflection. His two-orbit three-flower example has unequal periods and concerns containment. The checked BWT source gives the equivalent predecessor-label formalism for sorted rotations, but its inspected standard-word results do not evaluate the merged rotations of a lower mechanical word and its complement. The supported distinct claim is the exact five-petal minimum and six-block label word for this rational reflection pair, not general two-orbit theory or historical priority.

## value

PASS

The petal minimum quantifies how many inverse-branch switches are needed to retain two canonical periodic supports simultaneously. A reflection pair is a natural symmetric test of whether the one-petal property survives a union. The uniform answer of five, though each individual component fits in one, gives a concrete lower as well as upper bound from an explicit combinatorial mechanism. The conclusion covers all admissible slopes and identifies the degenerate symmetric cases rather than reporting arbitrary finite counts.
