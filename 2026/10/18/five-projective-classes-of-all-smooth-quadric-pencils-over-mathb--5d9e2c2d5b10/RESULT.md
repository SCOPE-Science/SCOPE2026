# Five projective classes of all-smooth quadric pencils over \(\mathbf F_2\)
## Finding
Let \(V=\mathbf F_2[X,Y,Z,W]_2\), and call a two-dimensional vector subspace \(L\subset V\) an *all-smooth pencil* when each of its three nonzero members cuts out a geometrically smooth quadric surface in \(\mathbf P^3\). There are exactly **14,336** all-smooth pencils. Under \(\mathrm{PGL}_4(\mathbf F_2)\) there are exactly **five** orbits. The following representatives are given by two binary integer masks encoding \(f,g\), as specified below.

| Representatives \((f,g)\) | Orbit cardinality | Rational base points | Geometrically smooth base? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| (192, 352) | 560 | 6 | No |
| (192, 353) | 3,360 | 6 | No |
| (192, 357) | 5,040 | 2 | Yes |
| (192, 358) | 5,040 | 4 | Yes |
| (198, 367) | 336 | 0 | No |

Thus precisely **10,080** of these pencils have a geometrically smooth base curve, always a genus-one complete intersection of two quadrics. In that subfamily there are precisely two projective equivalence classes, with respectively two and four rational base points. The remaining **4,256** have singular base curves. This theorem concerns embeddings and pencils under linear projective equivalence, not abstract isomorphism classes of curves.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is exactly \(k=\mathbf F_2\). The space \(V\) consists of all homogeneous quadratics in four variables with coefficients in \(k\), not just quadrics with a rational point. Geometric smoothness means smooth over the algebraic closure. A pencil is a *subspace*, with no ordering or choice of generators, so replacing \((f,g)\) by any other basis does not change it. The base curve is the scheme-theoretic intersection \(C=V(f,g)\subset\mathbf P^3\). Its rational base-point count is \(\# C(k)\), irrespective of singularity.

For reproducibility encode a quadratic by a ten-bit integer using monomial order
\[
(X^2,Y^2,Z^2,W^2,XY,XZ,XW,YZ,YW,ZW)
\]
from least to most significant bit. For instance mask \(192\) represents \(XW+YZ\). The five representative pairs in the table, together with this convention, specify the objects exactly.

## Proof
**1. Smoothness criterion.** Write \(q=\sum_{i=0}^3 d_i X_i^2+\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}X_iX_j\). Its gradient has coefficient matrix the alternating polar form \(B_q\) with upper-triangular entries \(a_{ij}\). In characteristic two the polar form is nondegenerate exactly when its Pfaffian is nonzero:
\[
 a_{01}a_{23}+a_{02}a_{13}+a_{03}a_{12}=1.
\]
Then there are no projective zeros of the gradient. Conversely if \(B_q\) is degenerate, its radical has dimension at least two; the restriction of \(q\) to this radical is a square of a linear form over the algebraic closure, hence has a nonzero zero. Thus \(q=0\) is geometrically singular. There are exactly 28 polar coefficient vectors with Pfaffian one (checking the 64 six-bit vectors) and 16 independent choices of square coefficients, giving 448 smooth quadratic forms.

**2. Complete count.** A pencil has three smooth nonzero members \(f,g,f+g\). The computation enumerates every unordered triple \(\{f,g,f+g\}\) from the 448 smooth forms and checks the Pfaffian criterion for all three. There are exactly 56 two-dimensional subspaces of the six-dimensional polar-coefficient space whose three nonzero vectors have Pfaffian one. For each such polar pencil, the four square coefficients of \(f\) and of \(g\) can be chosen freely, yielding \(2^8=256\) distinct lifts. Therefore the number of smooth pencils is
\[
56\cdot256=14,336.
\]
This factorization also independently checks the enumerated number of distinct triples.

**3. Five projective orbits.** Over \(\mathbf F_2\), scalar matrices are trivial and \(\#\mathrm{PGL}_4(k)=\#\mathrm{GL}_4(k)=20,160\). The attached exhaustive orbit verifier applies the four coordinate transformations consisting of adjacent swaps \((X,Y)\), \((Y,Z)\), \((Z,W)\) and the substitution \(X\mapsto X+Y\). These generate all elementary transvections and hence \(\mathrm{GL}_4(k)\). Every transformation is implemented by quadratic substitution on the ten-bit basis. Starting from the smallest unseen pencil, breadth-first closure gives exactly five disjoint orbits, of sizes \(560,3360,5040,5040,336\). They sum to \(14,336\), establishing exhaustiveness. Direct evaluation of each representative's two equations at all 15 points of \(\mathbf P^3(k)\) gives the rational base-point counts shown in the table; these counts are orbit invariants.

**4. Smoothness of base loci.** For representative pairs \((192,357)\) and \((192,358)\), the attached independent Jacobian/Groebner calculation checks the ideal generated by the two dehomogenized quadrics and all three \(2\times2\) minors of their \(2\times3\) Jacobian on each of the four standard affine charts. Exact Buchberger reduction over \(\mathbf F_2\) yields the unit ideal on *every* chart. Thus the base intersections are geometrically smooth. Every smooth complete intersection of two quadrics in \(\mathbf P^3\) is connected and has canonical bundle \(\mathcal O_C\) by adjunction, hence is geometrically integral of genus one.

For the other three representatives the base has zero or six rational points. Were it geometrically smooth it would be a genus-one complete intersection, satisfying the Hasse bound
\[
3-2\sqrt2\leq\#C(\mathbf F_2)\leq3+2\sqrt2,
\]
which forces the integer point count between one and five. Therefore the three remaining base schemes cannot be geometrically smooth. This distinguishes all five classes as claimed.

## Verification
The self-contained scripts `verify_census.py` and `verify_base_smoothness.py` use only Python's standard library. Running `python verify_census.py` produces `CENSUS_VERIFY_OK`, identifies 448 smooth forms, 14,336 all-smooth pencils, 56 polar pencils and the full five-orbit table. Running `python verify_base_smoothness.py` (from the same directory, so the first module can be imported) produces `BASE_SMOOTHNESS_VERIFY_OK`: both prospective genus-one representatives have unit singularity ideal on all four affine charts. No finite-extension sampling is used as proof of geometric smoothness: the Pfaffian and unit-ideal calculations are exact algebraic criteria. The source code and full monomial conventions are included with this result.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields*, arXiv:2203.07169v1 (2022), Section 3, prove existence of all-smooth pencils of quadric surfaces over arbitrary finite fields, including characteristic two (Proposition 3.5). Their paper does **not** provide this exhaustive binary classification, the five projective orbit sizes, or the base-locus dichotomy. Existence is explicitly **not** claimed as a new result. Boralevi and Mezzetti, *Pencils of singular quadrics of constant rank and their orbits*, arXiv:2206.00503v1 (2022), analyze entirely singular constant-rank pencils over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field; their orbit results do not imply the five binary all-smooth classes. The present statement is about all smooth **rational members**, not about smoothness of every geometric member of the pencil, nor about abstract classification of elliptic curves.

## Limitations
The counts are an exact, exhaustive small-field classification only for \(\mathbf F_2\); they provide no formula for general \(\mathbf F_{2^m}\). Smoothness of the three rational quadric members does not force smoothness of the base: in three orbits the base is singular. The two base-smooth orbits are not asserted to give two distinct abstract isomorphism classes of curves. The finite computation and its proofs are reproducible, but no external independent audit or exhaustive literature-completeness certification has been performed. Unindexed equivalent finite-geometry enumerations remain a bibliographic risk.
