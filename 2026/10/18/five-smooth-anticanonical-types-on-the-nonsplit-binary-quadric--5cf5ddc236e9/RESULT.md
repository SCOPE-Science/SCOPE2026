# Five smooth anticanonical types on the nonsplit binary quadric

## Finding
Fix the nonsplit smooth quadric surface
\[
Q=V(x_0x_1+x_2^2+x_2x_3+x_3^2)\subset\mathbf P^3_{\mathbf F_2}.
\]
Its anticanonical linear system \(|-K_Q|=|\mathcal O_Q(2)|\) has exactly \(511\) nonzero rational sections (scalars are trivial over \(\mathbf F_2\)). Exactly **240** have geometrically smooth zero curves. These are smooth genus-one curves of degree four, and under the full \(\mathbf F_2\)-projective automorphism group of \(Q\), they form exactly **five** distinct orbits. Their rational point counts and orbit sizes are

| \(\#C(\mathbf F_2)\) | Orbit sizes | Sections |
| ---: | :--- | ---: |
| 1 | 60 | 60 |
| 2 | 30, 60 | 90 |
| 3 | 60 | 60 |
| 4 | 30 | 30 |
| 5 | none | 0 |
| **Total** | **five orbits** | **240** |

Consequently, a smooth anticanonical curve on the nonsplit quadric has **at most four** rational points, although the Hasse bound for genus one over \(\mathbf F_2\) permits five. In fact, **every anticanonical section through all five rational points is reducible**, a union of two rational plane sections, and is geometrically singular. This last exclusion has a separate incidence-geometric proof.

## Assumptions and scope
The only ground field is \(\mathbf F_2\). Every divisor is the scheme-theoretic zero locus on the fixed geometrically smooth nonsplit quadric \(Q\). Distinct sections mean distinct elements of \(H^0(Q,\mathcal O_Q(2))\) up to nonzero scalar; this gives exactly one equation per divisor over the ground field. Two sections are projectively equivalent only when related by \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(Q)\); abstract elliptic-curve isomorphism is **not** claimed.

The embedding of \(Q\) into projective three-space has hyperplane class \(H\), with \(H^2=2\) and \(-K_Q=2H\). Therefore a smooth divisor in \(|2H|\) has degree \((2H)H=4\) and, by adjunction, canonical divisor \(K_C=(K_Q+2H)|_C=0\). A smooth connected complete intersection of two quadrics is geometrically integral of genus one. The quadric has the five rational points
\[
[1:0:0:0],\quad[0:1:0:0],\quad[1:1:1:0],\quad[1:1:0:1],\quad[1:1:1:1].
\]

## Proof
**1. Section count.** Restriction of quadrics gives
\[
H^0(\mathbf P^3,\mathcal O(2))/\langle Q\rangle\cong H^0(Q,\mathcal O_Q(2)),
\]
a nine-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbf F_2\), so its projectivization contains \(2^9-1=511\) rational sections. Quadratic coefficient masks use the ten monomials of degree two, taken in lexicographic exponent-vector order; canonical representatives are obtained by clearing the highest set bit of the coefficient mask of \(Q\) modulo that mask.

**2. Full symmetry.** The five displayed rational points constitute a projective frame: no four are coplanar. Every automorphism of \(Q\) permutes them faithfully, hence injects into \(S_5\). For each of the \(120\) point permutations, the unique projectivity carrying the frame to the permuted frame preserves the defining quadratic equation. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(Q)\cong S_5,\qquad |\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(Q)|=120.
\]
The included orbit verifier constructs all these transformations as binary matrices, checks preservation of \(Q\), acts on all \(511\) sections, and obtains \(17\) orbits. The orbit sizes are five of size \(60\), four of size \(30\), three of size \(10\), two of size \(15\), and one each of sizes \(20\), \(5\), and \(6\); their weighted sum is \(511\).

**3. Exact geometric smoothness.** For a second quadric \(R\), put \(C_R=V(Q,R)\). The Jacobian criterion says that \(C_R\) is singular over an algebraic closure exactly when the homogeneous ideal generated by \(Q\), \(R\), and the six \(2\times2\) minors of the two-row gradient matrix vanishes at some nonzero point. On each of the four standard affine charts, a reduced Gröbner basis is \(\{1\}\) precisely when no such singular point exists there. These are polynomial ideal computations over \(\mathbf F_2\), not finite-field point sampling. The orbit verifier checks a representative from each of the \(17\) section orbits; exactly five representatives pass. They have coefficient masks \(76,79,82,83,97\) and orbit sizes and rational-point counts respectively
\[
(60,3),\ (30,2),\ (60,2),\ (30,4),\ (60,1).
\]
No other orbit is smooth. Hence there are precisely \(240\) smooth anticanonical sections.

For clarity, if the coordinates are \((x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)\), the five representatives are given by \(Q=0\) together with, respectively,
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{76}&=x_2^2+x_1x_3+x_0x_3,\\
R_{79}&=x_3^2+x_2x_3+x_2^2+x_1x_3+x_0x_3,\\
R_{82}&=x_2x_3+x_1x_2+x_0x_3,\\
R_{83}&=x_3^2+x_2x_3+x_1x_2+x_0x_3,\\
R_{97}&=x_3^2+x_1^2+x_0x_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The orbit sizes sum to \(240\). Since rational point count is invariant under projectivities of \(Q\), the above orbit representatives yield the stated distribution.

**4. Independent five-point obstruction.** Evaluation on the five rational points has rank five on the nine-dimensional section space. Therefore exactly \(2^{9-5}-1=15\) nonzero anticanonical sections vanish at all five points. The five points form an elliptic ovoid: every rational hyperplane cuts \(Q\) either in a tangent section with one rational point or a nonsingular plane conic with three rational points. The ten triples of rational points determine ten distinct secant planes. There are exactly \(\frac12\binom{5}{3}\binom{3}{1}=15\) unordered pairs of secant planes whose two triples cover all five points (their triples overlap in one point). Each pair gives a distinct reducible anticanonical section through the five points, as a product of its two plane equations restricted to \(Q\). Their number exhausts all \(15\) sections through five points. Such a union of two nonempty plane-section conics is geometrically singular at their common rational point. Thus none of the sections with five rational points is smooth. Finally, the Hasse inequality for smooth genus-one curves gives at least one rational point over \(\mathbf F_2\), and the classification attains all remaining counts \(1,2,3,4\).

## Verification
`verify_orbits.py` (Python 3 and SymPy) enumerates the quotient section space, the entire \(120\)-element automorphism group, its \(17\) coefficient orbits, the four-chart Gröbner singularity test on every orbit representative, and the smooth-orbit rational counts. It reproduces exactly five smooth orbits and \(240\) smooth sections.

`verify_direct.py` uses an independently coded monomial order and direct Gröbner computations with lexicographic order on **all 511** sections; it does not use orbit multiplication. It independently returns the full rational-point histogram \(\{1:60,2:90,3:60,4:30\}\) and a total of \(240\). Both programs have been executed from the included exact source files; the output data are in `EXPECTED.json`. See `REPRODUCE.txt` for commands and dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca (arXiv:2203.07169, first public 2022-03-14) construct pencils of quadrics over finite fields with all rational members smooth, including an even-characteristic construction in their Proposition 3.5. Their existence result neither counts smooth anticanonical sections on a fixed nonsplit \(\mathbf F_2\)-quadric nor gives the five embedded equivalence types or the five-point obstruction. Fevola, Mandelshtam and Sturmfels (arXiv:2009.04334) classify and stratify regular pencils of symmetric matrices over characteristic-zero ground fields. Boralevi and Mezzetti (arXiv:2206.00503) treat constant-rank singular quadric pencils, explicitly in characteristic zero. Fisher (arXiv:math/0610403) develops general genus-one models and invariants, explicitly not in characteristic two in the relevant presentation. These results provide geometric context but do not entail the stated characteristic-two nonsplit-quadric classification. Existing counts for pencils whose **every** rational member is smooth have different hypotheses and do not cover the fixed-quadric anticanonical system considered here.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification of sections on one fixed nonsplit quadric and equivalence under its rational automorphism group, not an abstract isomorphism classification of genus-one curves, not a comparison with the split quadric, and not a result for larger fields. An unindexed earlier finite-geometric or genus-one-model enumeration could coincide with the table. Computer-aided exhaustive orbit enumeration establishes the exact weighted counts; the five-point obstruction additionally has a separate geometric argument. No external independent validation has been performed.
