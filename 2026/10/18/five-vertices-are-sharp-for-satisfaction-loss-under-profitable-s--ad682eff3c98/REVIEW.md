# Self-review: satisfaction loss in profitable two-type swaps

## Correctness — PASS
A connected graph has positive degrees; the two movers' post-exchange utilities are evaluated at their destination vertices. The exact mover inequalities cross multiply positive degrees, while the independent satisfaction headcount tests whether twice each same-type neighbor count is at least the vertex degree. The six-edge witness yields utilities \(0,1/3,1/2,0,2/3\) and \(1,1/3,0,1/3,1/3\), so both movers strictly benefit and the satisfied headcount falls \(2\) to \(1\). All connected graph edge masks up to five vertices, all permitted type assignments, and all opposite-type swaps are covered by the finite exact checker. Only two of the 21 connected graph classes at five vertices allow a harmful profitable exchange; none at four. The checker was executed successfully and the count was independently cross-checked against a separate graph-atlas enumeration. This is an exact finite classification, not a claim of general behavior on larger graphs.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk
The 2019 Agarwal–Elkind–Gan–Voudouris swap-game definition matches the strict untruncated utility comparison, but its inspected existence, welfare and stability results do not imply a change of sign in a *binary* satisfaction headcount after every privately improving swap. The 2018 Chauhan–Lenzner–Molitor and 2019 Echzell et al. analyses use threshold-truncated incentives, which differ exactly for an already satisfied mover who still has an incentive to improve raw utility. Bilò et al. (2022) investigate potential and zero-utility counts but not the stated exact half-threshold criterion; Zhang (2011) motivates spatial segregation with a different preference profile. The finite sharp cutoff, six-edge witness, and enumeration of two admissible five-node topology classes were not identified in these inspected sources, relevant mathematical-index matches, or accessible claims from earlier research. The result could nevertheless be implicit in an unexamined paper, appendix, or alternative notion of segregation welfare; independent bibliographic comparison is outstanding.

## Value — PASS
The social metric is the common, interpretable count of agents whose local neighborhood comprises at least half their own type. Two individually improving location exchanges need not increase that headcount, **even when their continuous total utility improves**. The smallest-order obstruction and complete two-topology boundary are meaningful graph-structural information about private exchange versus a natural coarse welfare measure; they are not arbitrary relabelings or a raw numerical curiosity. The theorem deliberately separates mover incentives from external satisfaction evaluation. Its scope excludes longer-run welfare, different tolerance rules, vacancies, and graphs above order five.
