The universal bound of at most \(n\) substrings occurring more often than their own lengths is asymptotically sharp even for **binary** texts: for every \(n\ge64\), an explicit length-\(n\) binary text has more than \(n-3\sqrt{n\log_2 n}\) such factors. Periodic de Bruijn cycles preserve all cyclic phases after a logarithmic distinguishing length, eliminating the growing-alphabet requirement in the earlier sharpness example.
