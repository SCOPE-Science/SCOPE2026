**Correctness — PASS.** The endpoint support and the hypergeometric indicator-moment formulas give the stated mean and variance. Any Poisson–binomial distribution having both support endpoints \(1\) and \(M\) must contain one sure success and exactly \(M-1\) nondegenerate summands, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives a sharp variance ceiling. Substitution yields a strictly positive gap in both the balanced and unbalanced cases. The remaining minority-at-most-one cases have explicit representations. Exact enumeration tests provide independent finite checks, not an infinite proof.

Oh–Jang's run-length distribution addresses another model. Published work on dependence signs of fixed-composition transition indicators does not imply that the *sum's distribution* is or is not a sum of other independent Bernoullis. No matching classification was found in targeted record or literature searches; an old equivalent in classical runs literature remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The conditional runs count is an established distribution-free testing statistic. This is a complete two-parameter classification of a natural independence-representation property, with a strictly quantified obstruction that excludes heterogeneous Bernoulli surrogates of any permitted length. The boundary \(\min(a,b)=1\) and the strictly positive gap at all other interior compositions illuminate why knowing the individual transition indicators are dependent is insufficient for determining the count's representability.
