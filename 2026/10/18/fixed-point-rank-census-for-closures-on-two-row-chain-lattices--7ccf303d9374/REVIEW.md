# Scientific review: fixed-point-rank census for closures on two-row chain lattices

## Correctness — PASS
The exact statement concerns all \(n\ge1\) and all extensive, monotone, idempotent endomaps of \(C_2\times C_n\). The proof first establishes the fixed-point-set/meet-closed-set equivalence on a finite lattice, then proves that the lower-row subset \(A\) and upper-row subset \(B\) are admissible exactly when \(n-1\in B\) and, for nonempty \(A\) with \(m=\max A\), \(B\cap[0,m]\subseteq A\). Splitting by the maximum lower fixed point yields \(t(1+t)^{n-1}+t^2\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(1+t+t^2)^m(1+t)^{n-1-m}\), which simplifies algebraically to the stated polynomial. A separate direct enumeration of all extensive maps and tests of idempotency and monotonicity through \(n=5\) matches the meet-closed-subset histograms; raw meet-subset tests reach \(n=8\). The correspondence to saturated transfer systems uses a specifically identified existing theorem and a direct order-reversing involution. The Gaussian law follows by an explicit exponentially small total-variation comparison. Finite computations confirm small instances but are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Glück, *Isolated Suborders and their Application to Counting Closure Operators* (arXiv:2302.13081), provides the general closure-system framework and only special-order enumerations for chains and diamonds in Sections 7.1–7.2; the rank polynomial on product ladders is absent from the inspected result statements. Hafeez et al., *Saturated and linear isometric transfer systems for cyclic groups of order \(p^m q^n\)* (arXiv:2109.08210), Theorems 1.1/4.7, already enumerate the **total** saturated transfer systems on all rectangular grids. Bao et al., *Transfer systems for rank two elementary Abelian groups: characteristic functions and matchstick games* (arXiv:2310.13835), Theorem 2.8/Remark 2.9, establish the correspondence to interior operators, and Remark 3.15 discusses broader product-lattice enumeration. Neither inspected source gives the cardinality distribution of fixed elements of the characteristic operator. OEIS A083313 records the unrefined integer sequence; no exact table for the rank polynomial was found. Searches under equivalent terms including closure systems, Moore families, rectangular subgroup lattices and characteristic-map fixed-point spectra did not identify a stronger or identical published claim. The result is NOT presented as a novel total enumeration or a novel saturated-transfer correspondence. An equivalent earlier rank enumeration may exist under less accessible terminology, and independent novelty review remains necessary.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The fixed-point cardinality is a natural structural parameter: it counts the closed lattice elements, or equivalently the subgroups unchanged by Hill's characteristic interior map—precisely the subgroups which receive no non-identity transfer from a proper subgroup. The complete rank polynomial resolves every value of this invariant for a full, variable-width family, whereas the prior total count determines only the specialization \(t=1\). It also yields an exact coefficient distribution, proves every permitted rank occurs, and supplies a Gaussian limiting law. The lattice family is independently motivated by cyclic subgroup grids and earlier saturated-transfer enumeration, not selected as an arbitrary numerical slice. There is no claim to a general algorithm for all products, nor to results for non-saturated transfer structures.

## Boundaries and follow-up risk
The claim is restricted to \(C_2\times C_n\), and the equivalence uses saturated (not arbitrary) transfer systems on cyclic subgroup lattices. The infinite-dimensional statement is a mathematical proof, with exhaustive verification restricted to small \(n\). Full texts of the most directly relevant works were inspected, but a different publication or unpublished note could contain an equivalent refinement; that is the principal originality risk. No independent external validation has been undertaken.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
