# Scientific review: four-cut homotopy of graphs without short cycles

## Correctness — PASS
The proof precisely defines the ordinary cut complex and its combinatorial Alexander dual. The girth restriction excludes any five-element dual face, so the dual has a complete two-skeleton and tetrahedra exactly on connected induced four-vertex trees. Each tetrahedron has an explicitly selected triangular free face, by excluding alternative common neighbors or shortcuts via cycles of lengths four or five. These independent elementary collapses yield a two-complex with calculable free integral second homology and trivial fundamental group. Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives the original cut-complex homology; its complete lower skeleton and the Hurewicz–Whitehead argument establish the claimed wedge type in the stated ranges. ## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675), Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2 already give the all-high-girth Euler characteristic and reduced integral homology limited to two top degrees. Their later tree and cycle classifications are special cases of the new conclusion. These known ingredients and cases are excluded from the novelty claim. The additional decisive fact is the vanishing of the lower possible homology group for **all** finite graphs of girth at least six, obtained by the free-face collapse on the dual, followed by a complete homotopy classification for all orders at least eight. *Topology of Cut Complexes II* (arXiv:2407.08158) proves an ordinary four-cut wedge theorem for rectangular grids, but grids contain four-cycles; its scope neither implies nor is implied by this high-girth theorem. Searches of mathematical literature and published-result records did not identify a stronger theorem implying this particular classification. An unindexed equivalent remains possible; these checks do not establish uniqueness or independence.

## Value — PASS
The new result gives a full classification across a natural infinite graph class with arbitrary connectedness, degree distribution, and cycle rank. Its rank depends only on degree moments and degree correlations across edges, and it shows that any two cubic high-girth graphs on the same number of vertices have isomorphic nonequivariant four-cut homotopy types. The comparison with the previously computed Heawood value and the newly covered Möbius–Kantor value illustrates a structural theorem that is not a small isolated computation.

## Limitations
The original complex's homotopy type for seven vertices is not asserted; homology there is fully determined. The conclusion is nonequivariant, does not establish shellability, does not claim sharpness of girth six, and has not received independent external validation. The documented literature inspection cannot rule out unindexed equivalent work.
