# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved for each integer \(n\ge5\) by an exact degree identity for how a two-subset meets a selected four-edge graph. All three-edge subgraph types are excluded as ordinary forts, and four-edge structures are fully classified by their maximum degrees. This includes the six-vertex exceptional claw-plus-edge configuration. Exact independent enumeration through \(n=10\) and separate forcing simulations through \(n=8\) reproduce the classifications. The proof, not finite search, establishes the unbounded parameter range.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit access risk

The 2019 Kneser paper proves **minimum successful skew forcing** rather than maximal failed-set multiplicities. The 2022 failed-skew paper characterizes the case of failed-skew number one in general graphs, not this family. The 2024 paper proves the **ordinary scalar** failed forcing value and is therefore credited; its accessible abstract does not state minimum-fort classifications or any skew-failure census. Exact and equivalent-formulation searches across accessible literature and research-record results did not reveal a covering enumeration. The 2024 article's full text was inaccessible and may contain more than the abstract discloses, so independent bibliographic priority checking is still needed.

## Value — PASS

The result gives a complete, natural optimal-configuration census in a canonical symmetric graph family, and isolates an exact distinction between ordinary and skew propagation even though their largest failed seed sizes coincide. The four-edge-star versus four-cycle versus four-matching characterization and exceptional \(n=6\) structure cannot be recovered from the known scalar forcing numbers; in particular, the distinct counts grow with \(n\). This is a structural enumeration, not merely a named substitution or a known-table recomputation.
