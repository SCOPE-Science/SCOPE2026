# Four Hilbert-series strata for two quadratic relations in a five-generator exterior algebra over finite fields

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, \(\dim_k V=5\), \(E=\bigwedge V\), and \(W\le\bigwedge^2V\) any two-dimensional subspace. Let \(H_W(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}\dim_k(E/(W))_j t^j\). Write \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{r}_q\) for the number of \(r\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^n\), with \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{1}_q=(q^n-1)/(q-1)\). There are exactly four possible Hilbert series, with the following respective numbers of subspaces \(W\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
H_W(t)&\#\{W\}\\\hline
1+5t+8t^2+5t^3+t^4&C_3\\
1+5t+8t^2+4t^3&C_4\\
1+5t+8t^2+2t^3&C_{5,1}\\
1+5t+8t^2+t^3&C_{5,0}
\end{array}
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_3&=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{3}_q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{2}_q,\\
C_4&=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{4}_q\left(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{6}{2}_q-\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{4}{3}_q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{2}_q\right),\\
C_{5,1}&=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{2}_q\left(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{9}{1}_q-\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{1}_q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{1}_q+q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{1}_q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{2}{1}_q\right),\\
C_{5,0}&=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{10}{2}_q-C_3-C_4-C_{5,1}
=q(q-1)C_{5,1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Every field order \(q\) is a prime power, with no restriction on characteristic. The ideal \((W)\) is generated by the two-dimensional *linear subspace of homogeneous quadrics*, not a chosen ordered basis. Define \(S(W)\) as the smallest subspace \(U\le V\) for which \(W\subseteq\bigwedge^2U\); equivalently it is spanned by contractions \(\iota_\lambda\omega\), for \(\lambda\in V^*\) and \(\omega\in W\). Let \(s=\dim S(W)\). Then \(s=3,4,5\). The rows correspond respectively to \(s=3\), \(s=4\), \(s=5\) with exactly one decomposable point in \(\mathbb P(W)\), and \(s=5\) with none. 'Decomposable' means a nonzero wedge \(u\wedge v\); over every field this is precisely rank two as an alternating bivector.

## Proof
First \(\dim E_j=\binom5j\). The quotient has degree-zero and degree-one dimensions \(1,5\), degree-two dimension \(10-2=8\), and degree-five dimension zero because any nonzero element of \(W\) wedges nontrivially with some element of \(\bigwedge^3V\). Its remaining dimensions are computed from wedge-product images.

If \(s=3\), write \(V=U\oplus T\) with \(\dim U=3\), \(\dim T=2\). The two-dimensional space \(W\subset\bigwedge^2U\) satisfies \(W\wedge U=\bigwedge^3U\), so \(\dim(W\wedge V)=1+2\cdot2=5\). The degree-four ideal is \((\bigwedge^3U\wedge T)\oplus(W\wedge\bigwedge^2T)\), of dimension \(2+2=4\). Hence the first Hilbert series.

If \(s\ge4\), the wedge map \(W\otimes\bigwedge^2V\longrightarrow\bigwedge^4V\) is surjective. Otherwise, by the perfect wedge pairing \(\bigwedge^4V\times V\to\bigwedge^5V\), some \(0\ne v\in V\) annihilates \(W\wedge\bigwedge^2V\); equivalently \(v\wedge W=0\). This forces \(W\subset v\wedge V\), and consequently \(\dim S(W)\le3\), a contradiction. Thus degree four vanishes for \(s=4,5\).

If \(s=4\), choose the minimal \(U\le V\) of dimension four and a complement \(ke\). The map \(W\otimes U\to\bigwedge^3U\) is surjective: failure, using the perfect pairing \(\bigwedge^3U\times U\to\bigwedge^4U\), implies \(v\wedge W=0\) for some nonzero \(v\in U\), giving support at most three. Its image is all four dimensions of \(\bigwedge^3U\), and \(W\wedge e\) adds two dimensions. Thus degree three has dimension \(10-6=4\).

If \(s=5\), \(W\) contains a rank-four bivector \(\alpha\): otherwise every nonzero member has rank two, and the supports of two independent decomposable members span at most four dimensions, contradicting \(s=5\). Choose its support \(U\) of dimension four, complement \(ke\), and express a second basis element as \(\beta+e\wedge x\), with \(\beta\in\bigwedge^2U\) and \(0\ne x\in U\). For rank-four \(\alpha\), the map \(U\to\bigwedge^3U\), \(u\mapsto\alpha\wedge u\), is an isomorphism. The kernel of \(W\otimes V\to\bigwedge^3V\) is therefore identified with the kernel of
\[
U\oplus k^2\longrightarrow\bigwedge^2U,\qquad(v,a,b)\longmapsto x\wedge v+a\alpha+b\beta.
\]
Here \(x\wedge U\) has dimension three and \(\alpha\notin x\wedge U\). The map thus has rank four or five; the degree-three quotient has dimension respectively two or one. It has rank four precisely when \(\beta\in x\wedge U+k\alpha\), equivalently when \(W\) has a nonzero decomposable element, because then some \(\beta-c\alpha\) is \(x\wedge(u+e)\), up to a harmless choice of signs. There can be at most one projective decomposable member: two independent such members would span \(W\), forcing \(\dim S(W)\le4\). This proves the four-series classification.

For the enumeration, each \(W\) has a unique minimal support. Thus \(s=3\) contributes \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{3}_q\) choices of support and \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{2}_q\) choices of \(W\subset\bigwedge^2U\). Similarly \(s=4\) contributes \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{4}_q\) times the number of two-subspaces of the six-dimensional \(\bigwedge^2U\), minus those with support three, giving \(C_4\).

To count \(s=5\) with one decomposable point, first fix that unique point \(\langle\omega\rangle\). There are \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{5}{2}_q\) choices since decomposable points correspond to two-planes in \(V\). With \(P=S(\omega)\), the spaces \(W\supset\langle\omega\rangle\) are projective points of \(\bigwedge^2V/\langle\omega\rangle\). Given a \(j\)-dimensional subspace \(T\) of \(V/P\), the number with support contained in \(P+T\) is \(F_j=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{\binom{j+2}{2}-1}{1}_q\). Möbius inversion in the three-dimensional subspace lattice \(V/P\) gives exactly \(F_3-\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{1}_qF_2+q\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{3}{1}_qF_1\) spaces of full support five. There is no overcount because each has exactly one decomposable point. The fourth count is the complement among all \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{10}{2}_q\) two-subspaces; straightforward expansion of these Gaussian coefficients gives \(C_{5,0}=q(q-1)C_{5,1}\). In particular all four types occur for every \(q\ge2\).

## Verification
For \(q=2\), independent exhaustive reduced-row-echelon enumeration of all \(174251\) two-subspaces of \(\bigwedge^2\mathbb F_2^5\), computing the actual wedge multiplication ranks and supports, returned counts \(1085,16926,52080,104160\) in the order above. The same identities were checked numerically for \(q=3,4\), with totals respectively \(72636421\) and \(6108368805\). Independently sampled wedge-rank tests over \(\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\) agreed with the classifications. These finite tests are corroboration, not a substitute for the preceding proof. The included `verify.py` reproduces the exhaustive binary test and formula calculations.

## Relationship to prior work
Moreno-Socías and Snellman (2000 preprint, 2002 publication) examine generic ideals in exterior algebras; their generic setting does not give all four strata or finite-field frequencies. Earlier work of Fröberg and Löfwall identifies compulsory linear syzygies for two quadratic generators but does not enumerate all pencils over every field. Shaw, Maks and Gordon (2005) *already classify the entire binary projective-line incidence problem*, so the \(q=2\) numbers are an independently obtained restatement of **known** counts, not new. The claimed contribution here is the uniform all-prime-power Hilbert-series stratification and its four exact counting formulas, including the ratio \(q(q-1)\). Published binary line-orbit classifications do not themselves imply the uniform positive-characteristic counting theorem for arbitrary \(q\).

## Limitations
These formulas count all two-dimensional relation subspaces in the specified five-generator exterior algebra. They do not classify all orbit types, quotient isomorphism classes, Betti tables, or arbitrary numbers of variables or relations. The complete binary line incidence distribution is already in prior literature. More general finite-geometric orbit-count papers may subsume parts of the uniform formula; this residual literature risk is explicitly acknowledged. Independent audit has not been performed.
