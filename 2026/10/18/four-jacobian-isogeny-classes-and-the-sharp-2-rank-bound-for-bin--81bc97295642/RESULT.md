# Four Jacobian isogeny classes and the sharp 2-rank bound for binary filling biquartic-cubic curves

## Finding

Let \(C\) be a geometrically smooth curve of bidegree \((4,3)\) in \(\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\) that contains all nine rational points of the product. Then \(C\) has genus \(6\), and its Jacobian has \(2\)-rank at least \(5\). Exactly \(36\) such bihomogeneous equations have \(2\)-rank \(5\) and \(a\)-number \(1\), and the other \(30\) have \(2\)-rank \(6\) and \(a\)-number \(0\). The \(36\) nonordinary equations form one orbit under the factor-preserving action of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)^2\). Their Jacobians are divided into exactly four \(\mathbf F_2\)-isogeny classes, specified by the four distinct degree-twelve Weil numerators below. All of these counts concern **embedded equations**, not asserted classes of abstract curves.

For a smooth curve \(C\), put \(N_n(C)=\#C(\mathbf F_{2^n})\) and
\[
Z_C(T)=\exp\biggl(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{N_n(C)T^n}{n}\biggr)=\frac{L_C(T)}{(1-T)(1-2T)},\qquad L_C(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{12}c_jT^j.
\]
Each row of the table is one ambient orbit; the mask is an unambiguous compact equation encoding defined in the next section. The coefficient lists run from \(c_0\) to \(c_{12}\).

| Equation mask | Orbit size | \(N_1,\ldots,N_6\) | Coefficients of \(L_C\) | \(2\)-rank | \(a\)-number |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| `396246` | 6 | \((9,9,9,9,69,51)\) | \((1,6,20,48,90,144,211,288,360,384,320,192,64)\) | 6 | 0 |
| `399606` | 18 | \((9,9,9,21,39,39)\) | \((1,6,20,48,93,156,233,312,372,384,320,192,64)\) | 6 | 0 |
| `404886` | 36 | \((9,9,12,17,24,78)\) | \((1,6,20,49,98,167,250,334,392,392,320,192,64)\) | 5 | 1 |
| `420790` | 6 | \((9,11,9,23,9,53)\) | \((1,6,21,54,114,204,313,408,456,432,336,192,64)\) | 6 | 0 |

In particular, the minimum \(2\)-rank is **exactly five**; it is not zero. By the finite-field isogeny theorem for abelian varieties, the four distinct Weil numerators give precisely four \(\mathbf F_2\)-isogeny classes of the Jacobians represented here.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over the field \(\mathbf F_2\). Let
\[
 m_{a,b}=X_0^{4-a}X_1^aY_0^{3-b}Y_1^b
 \quad(0\le a\le4,\ 0\le b\le3),
\qquad F=\sum_{a=0}^4\sum_{b=0}^3 \epsilon_{a,b}m_{a,b},\qquad \epsilon_{a,b}\in\mathbf F_2.
\]
Encode each equation by the integer
\[
\operatorname{mask}(F)=\sum_{a=0}^4\sum_{b=0}^3\epsilon_{a,b}2^{4a+b}.
\]
Thus, for example, `404886` represents the polynomial whose nonzero \((a,b)\) are
\[
 \{(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,3),(2,0),(2,2),(2,3),(3,1),(4,1),(4,2)\}.
\]
All curves must contain each of the nine rational points, and must be smooth over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\), not merely nonsingular at rational points. Since \(4,3>0\), any geometrically smooth divisor of this bidegree is geometrically integral (distinct geometric components on the product would intersect). Its arithmetic genus is \((4-1)(3-1)=6\). The ambient equivalence group is factor-preserving: swapping the factors changes bidegree and is not included.

## Proof

**Exhaustive equation space and geometric smoothness.** The vector space of bidegree-\((4,3)\) forms has dimension \(20\). Evaluation at the nine rational points gives nine independent linear conditions over \(\mathbf F_2\): row reduction of the \(9\times20\) evaluation matrix has rank \(9\). Its kernel has dimension \(11\), hence precisely \(2^{11}-1=2047\) nonzero equations. Over \(\mathbf F_2\), no two distinct nonzero forms differ by a nontrivial scalar.

For every equation, check the four possible affine charts \(X_i=Y_j=1\), \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\). On each chart form the ideal generated by the defining polynomial and all four homogeneous partial derivatives. By the geometric Jacobian criterion and the Nullstellensatz, the curve has no singular geometric point in that chart if and only if this ideal is the unit ideal over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\). An exact Gröbner basis over \(\mathbf F_2\) certifies the unit ideal by returning \(\{1\}\). A first derivative check at the nine rational points leaves \(364\) candidates, and these four-chart certificates leave exactly \(66\) geometrically smooth forms. No bounded extension-field sampling is used for this smoothness conclusion.

**Symmetry and representatives.** Every \(2\times2\) invertible matrix over \(\mathbf F_2\) is a distinct element of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\), giving \(6^2=36\) factor-preserving transformations. Apply all \(36\) substitutions to each of the \(66\) smooth masks and partition into orbits. The four minimum masks and orbit sizes are exactly those in the table: \((396246,6)\), \((399606,18)\), \((404886,36)\), \((420790,6)\). As the orbits partition all \(66\) forms, calculations on the four representatives suffice for every smooth curve in the stated class.

**Exact zeta functions.** For each representative, evaluate its bihomogeneous form over all \((2^n+1)^2\) points of \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_{2^n})^2\), for every \(1\le n\le6\). The program constructs the six finite fields using the irreducible binary polynomials with coefficient bit masks `0b11`, `0b111`, `0b1011`, `0b10011`, `0b100101`, and `0b1000011`, respectively (each is checked to be irreducible in the indicated degree). It then obtains the six point counts displayed in the table. Define \(c_0=1\). The identity for \(Z_C(T)\) above implies, successively,
\[
 n c_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \bigl(N_k-(2^k+1)\bigr)c_{n-k},\qquad 1\le n\le6.
\]
For a smooth genus-six curve over \(\mathbf F_2\), the Weil functional equation imposes
\[
 c_{12-j}=2^{6-j}c_j,\qquad0\le j\le6.
\]
These two identities determine every \(c_j\) and give the four displayed vectors. Because the vectors differ, no two row-types have \(\mathbf F_2\)-isogenous Jacobians, while members of the same row are related by rational ambient automorphisms and therefore have isomorphic Jacobians.

**Cartier and the exact \(2\)-rank.** On the affine chart \(X_0=Y_0=1\), write \(f(x,y)=F(1,x;1,y)\). Adjunction gives a basis of holomorphic differentials indexed by \(\{x^iy^j:0\le i\le2,\ 0\le j\le1\}\). In the exact sequence \(0\to\mathcal O_Q(-4,-3)\to\mathcal O_Q\to\mathcal O_C\to0\), Frobenius on \(H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)\) is computed by multiplication by \(F^{p-1}\) after Frobenius pullback on \(H^2(Q,\mathcal O_Q(-4,-3))\); the six-element Čech basis gives the following coefficient formula. The Cartier-matrix entry (up to the choice of transpose convention) for source index \((i,j)\) and target index \((k,\ell)\) is the coefficient of
\[
 x^{2i+1-k}y^{2j+1-\ell}\quad\text{in }f(x,y)
\]
(the exponent \(p-1=1\) in characteristic two). In that ordered six-element basis, the four row-bit matrices have rows, respectively,
\[
(6,11,44,52,4,8),\quad(7,11,50,52,36,8),\quad(6,9,41,28,20,8),\quad(7,9,61,22,20,40).
\]
The exact ranks of these matrices are \((6,6,5,6)\), and the ranks of their sixth powers are likewise \((6,6,5,6)\). Over \(\mathbf F_2\), the Frobenius twist of the matrices does not change their coefficients. The \(2\)-rank is the stable rank of Cartier (equivalently, its sixth iterate in dimension six), and the \(a\)-number is six minus the rank of the first Cartier matrix. This establishes the sharp \(2\)-rank bound, exact nonordinary orbit size, and the \(a\)-number dichotomy. Independently, the degrees of the four Weil numerators modulo \(2\) are \((6,6,5,6)\), consistent with the stable ranks.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. The self-contained program constructs the \(9\times20\) evaluation matrix, enumerates all \(2047\) nonzero forms, checks all four affine-chart singularity ideals, constructs the full action of the \(36\) rational ambient transformations, and evaluates \(N_1,\ldots,N_6\) for each orbit representative using finite-field multiplication. It then computes Weil coefficients, Cartier matrices, their first and sixth ranks, and checks them against explicit expected arrays. Its successful output terminates with `PASS: 2047 candidate equations, 364 rationally nonsingular, 66 geometrically smooth; 4 distinct zeta functions`.

## Relationship to prior work

Homma and Kim, *Filling curves for \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\)* (first publicly posted 22 March 2022), prove that bidegree \((4,3)\) is minimal for a smooth filling curve over \(\mathbf F_2\), and supply an existence example (Theorem 4.1). The present statement does **not** claim originality for that degree threshold or the existence of filling curves. The counts of \(66\) smooth equations and four ambient orbits are used as a baseline, rather than presented as the mathematical innovation. The specific new claim is the complete zeta-polynomial/isogeny stratification, its sharp \(2\)-rank floor, and the unique nonordinary orbit.

Ahammed, Campbell and Moon, *Minimal algebraic space filling curves on the product of projective lines* (2025), analyze smooth minimal filling curves, especially odd-characteristic examples. Their Section 4 enumerates examples over \(\mathbf F_3\) and \(\mathbf F_5\). Neither inspected article states these genus-six Weil numerators or this \(\mathbf F_2\) Cartier-rank classification. This is an evidence-based comparison, not a proof of global priority.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to geometrically smooth filling curves of bidegree \((4,3)\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). It does not classify all genus-six curves or establish abstract isomorphism classes beyond the exhibited rational ambient classes. The four Weil polynomials classify only Jacobian \(\mathbf F_2\)-isogeny classes. Point counts for \(n\le6\) determine the full zeta functions only because smoothness, genus six and the Weil functional equation are proved. An unindexed equivalent arithmetic classification may exist. The exhaustive computation assumes the standard soundness of exact binary arithmetic and Gröbner algorithms as implemented in SymPy; the complete standalone code is supplied for inspection and replay.
