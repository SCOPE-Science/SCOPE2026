# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The intersection-of-balls lemma is proved by Hamming coordinate counting. It excludes five-scales and forces the four-scale case split. Each surviving geometric type has an explicit unique attribute witness, and counting corrects the double-counting of two-dimensional rectangles. Exact independent enumeration and a five-scale exclusion checker support the proof for small parameters. The argument covers all \(q\ge3,d\ge2\).

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Koyda–Stumme (arXiv:2104.07159) studies Boolean suborders and contranominal subcontexts in general; Dürrschnabel–Koyda–Stumme (arXiv:2106.10978) relates their detection to induced matching and algorithms. Their inspected manuscripts and Hamming/distance searches contain no classification of four-scale objects in nonbinary radius-one Hamming contexts and no two-line-plus-parity counting formula. The binary special case is not separately claimed. An equivalent specialized enumeration under different language remains a material priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Maximal contranominal scale size controls Boolean suborders and concept-lattice complexity, while generic detection is computationally difficult. A sharp dimension ceiling, full geometric classification and explicit all-parameter count give structural information not available from a generic enumerator. Nonbinary alphabets exhibit a new two-line family with differing column choices, so the count is not an immediate binary specialization.

**Limits.** Only radius-one Hamming contexts are covered. Finite experiments are not an infinite proof. Publication priority has not been independently certified.
