# Review of the four-rank parabolic orbit census

## Correctness (C): PASS

The invariant \((u,v,t,w)\) is complete under upper-block-triangular conjugacy. The base-change orbit of adjacent blocks \((A,B)\) is determined by ranks and the intersection \(\ker A\cap\operatorname{im}B\). Unipotent conjugations of the long block are precisely maps factoring through \(B\) plus maps into \(\operatorname{im}A\); the resulting quotient is the Hom-space on \(\ker B\) to \(X/\operatorname{im}A\). Its residual stabilizer acts as independent general linear groups. Gaussian intersection counting and constant-fiber linear algebra give the exact orbit cardinality. Direct enumeration and complete finite parabolic-orbit reconstruction in stated small cases agree. The finite tests supplement, not replace, the proof.

## Originality (O): PASS within inspected coverage

Goodwin–Hille–Röhrle (arXiv:0711.3711, full HTML inspected) already establish finiteness and representational classifications of related parabolic orbits. No priority is claimed for orbit finiteness. Fuchs–Kirillov (arXiv:2204.00066, complete HTML inspected), Karp–Thomas (arXiv:2207.12590), and Bardestani–Mallahi-Karai–Ram–Salmasian (arXiv:2503.15440v4, PDF text studied) enumerate or analyze global Jordan-type marginals. Those marginals do not determine the four-rank parabolic-orbit partition or cardinality of each such orbit. The assessed contribution is the latter refined quantitative classification, together with the balanced-level orbit count. Targeted searches and comparison with relevant published mathematical records did not identify an exact duplicate; residual possibility of a classical implicit equivalent remains. Independent historical novelty verification has not occurred.

## Value (V): PASS

The nilradical of a parabolic subgroup is a canonical finite-field algebraic object. Each four-rank stratum is a full group orbit with an explicit integer cardinality, providing a complete natural invariant across all three-block dimensions and all finite fields, substantially finer than Jordan-type marginals. The field-independent orbit count and closed even/odd balanced formulas are natural finite representation invariants; conditional independence of the two remaining ranks makes the count practically reusable. The result does not depend on arbitrary small-parameter selection.

## Boundaries and risks

General parabolic orbit finiteness and univariate nilpotent Jordan counts are known. The closed classification is restricted to precisely three full incidence levels. Higher flags and arbitrary height-three incidence posets are outside scope. The closest unresolved priority risk is an equivalent three-block quiver-theoretic orbit-size formula in literature not retrieved by the comparisons.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
