# Fourier classification and modular resonance of derivations in odd Takasaki quandle algebras

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of odd order \(N>1\), and let \(K\) be a field whose characteristic does not divide \(N\). The nonassociative Takasaki quandle algebra \(Q_K(A)\) has basis \(\{e_a:a\in A\}\) and multiplication \(e_a e_b=e_{2b-a}\). A derivation is a \(K\)-linear map satisfying \(D(uv)=D(u)v+uD(v)\) for every \(u,v\in Q_K(A)\).

**Classification theorem.** After extending to a field containing all character values of \(A\), set \(f_\chi=\sum_{a\in A}\chi(a)e_a\) for \(\chi\in\widehat A\). Every derivation, and only a derivation, is determined by scalars \(\lambda_\chi\) such that

\[
D f_\chi=\lambda_\chi f_\chi,\qquad
\lambda_0=0,\quad \lambda_{-\chi}=-\lambda_\chi,\quad
\lambda_{2\chi}=2\lambda_\chi.
\]

The derivation Lie algebra is therefore abelian. Over a nonsplitting field \(K\), the same classification holds with the necessary Galois descent condition, and its dimension is the geometric dimension of the displayed linear system.

Write \(r_d=\operatorname{ord}_d(2)\) for each character order \(d>1\), and define \(L_d=r_d\) if \(-1\) belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by \(2\) modulo \(d\), and \(L_d=2r_d\) otherwise. In odd coefficient characteristic \(p\), define a resonance indicator \(I_d\) as follows:

\[
I_d=\begin{cases}
1,&-1\in\langle2\rangle\pmod d\ \text{and}\ 2^{r_d/2}\equiv-1\pmod p,\\
1,&-1\notin\langle2\rangle\pmod d\ \text{and}\ 2^{r_d}\equiv1\pmod p,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

If \(A=C_n\) is cyclic of odd order, then

\[
\boxed{\dim_K\operatorname{Der}Q_K(C_n)=
\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d>1}}\frac{\varphi(d)}{L_d}I_d.}
\]

For general odd-order abelian \(A\), replace the multiplicity \(\varphi(d)/L_d\) by \(m_d/L_d\), where \(m_d=\#\{\chi\in\widehat A:\operatorname{ord}(\chi)=d\}\). Equivalently, count resonant orbits of \(\langle-1,2\rangle\) on \(\widehat A\setminus\{0\}\). In characteristic zero or two, the dimension is zero. Over any finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic coprime to \(N\), the number of derivations is exactly \(q^{\dim}\).

**Infinite modular family.** For every prime \(\ell\equiv3\pmod8\), with \(\ell>3\), and every field \(K\) of characteristic three, the dimension is \((\ell-1)/\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)\). There is a concrete nonzero derivation

\[
\boxed{D(e_a)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times}
\left(\frac t\ell\right)e_{a+t},}
\]

where the Legendre symbol is read as \(0,1,-1\in K\). For example, \(\dim\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb F_3}(\mathbb F_3[R_{11}])=1\), and for \(\ell=43\) it is \(3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The group \(A\) is written additively and is finite of odd order. The product is the right Takasaki operation \(a\triangleright b=2b-a\); using the opposite convention changes the algebra and requires a separate analysis. The characteristic of \(K\) must not divide \(|A|\), so that finite-group Fourier inversion and character orthogonality hold after scalar extension. A character of order \(d\) has \(d>1\) odd, ensuring that multiplication by two is invertible on its orbit. The dimension formula uses integers \(r_d,L_d,m_d\) and congruences interpreted in the coefficient characteristic; \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient.

## Proof

**1. Fourier multiplication.** Extend scalars to a splitting field \(E\) of the finite abelian group \(A\). The \(f_\chi\) form a basis because \(\operatorname{char}E\nmid N\). Set \(c=2b-a\) in the product of two Fourier vectors. Character orthogonality gives

\[
f_\chi f_\psi=\sum_{a,b\in A}\chi(a)\psi(b)e_{2b-a}
=\begin{cases}Nf_{-\chi},&\psi=-2\chi,\\0,&\psi\ne-2\chi.\end{cases}
\]

The multiplicative condition \(\psi=-2\chi\) is in additive character notation (meaning \(\psi=\chi^{-2}\)).

**2. Every derivation is Fourier-diagonal.** Let \(D\) be an arbitrary derivation and write \(Df_\chi=\sum_\eta d_{\eta\chi}f_\eta\). Differentiate the nonzero product \(f_\chi f_{-2\chi}=Nf_{-\chi}\). In \((Df_\chi)f_{-2\chi}\), the multiplication rule allows only \(\eta=\chi\), since doubling is a bijection of \(\widehat A\). In \(f_\chi(Df_{-2\chi})\), only \(\eta=-2\chi\) survives. Consequently

\[
Df_{-\chi}=(d_{\chi\chi}+d_{-2\chi,-2\chi})f_{-\chi}.
\]

Inversion permutes the character group, so \(D\) is diagonal. Substituting its diagonal form into this equation yields \(\lambda_{-\chi}=\lambda_\chi+\lambda_{-2\chi}\). Replacing \(\chi\) by \(-\chi\) and subtracting implies \(\lambda_{-2\chi}=-\lambda_{2\chi}\), hence \(\lambda_{-\chi}=-\lambda_\chi\) for every \(\chi\). Then \(\lambda_{2\chi}=2\lambda_\chi\), and the zero character has coefficient zero. Conversely, these relations make the Leibniz identity hold on every nonzero product, while every zero product has zero Leibniz right-hand side by the same sparse Fourier multiplication rule. Thus they are necessary and sufficient.

**3. Arithmetic orbit count.** On an orbit of the two commuting permutations \(\chi\mapsto2\chi\) and \(\chi\mapsto-\chi\), the prescribed eigenvalue evolves by multiplication by \(2\) and \(-1\), respectively. For a character of exact order \(d\), its doubling period is \(r_d\). If \(-1\in\langle2\rangle\pmod d\), this sign operation equals the \(r_d/2\)-fold doubling operation, and consistency is exactly \(2^{r_d/2}=-1\) in \(K\). Otherwise the sign operation doubles the orbit length and consistency is exactly \(2^{r_d}=1\) in \(K\). When consistent, each orbit contributes one scalar parameter; otherwise it contributes none. Exactly \(m_d/L_d\) such orbits have character order \(d\). In characteristic two, \(\lambda_{2\chi}=0\) for all \(\chi\), so all parameters vanish. In characteristic zero, no positive power of the integer two equals \(1\) or \(-1\) in \(K\), so all parameters vanish. Since the full derivation equations are homogeneous linear equations defined over \(K\), extension to \(E\) preserves their rank and the resulting dimension. Scalar descent also proves all derivations commute: after base change, they are simultaneously diagonal.

**4. Legendre-symbol family.** For \(\ell\equiv3\pmod8\), the quadratic character modulo \(\ell\) satisfies \((\frac{-1}{\ell})=(\frac2\ell)=-1\). The order \(r=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)\) is even, with \(r/2\) odd and \(2^{r/2}=-1\pmod\ell\); hence \(2^{r/2}=-1\) also in characteristic three. There are \((\ell-1)/r\) doubling orbits. For the explicit formula, let \(c_0=0\) and \(c_t=(\frac t\ell)\) otherwise. Expanding the Leibniz rule at \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\) shows that it is equivalent to \(c_t=c_{-t}+c_{t/2}\) for every \(t\). The character identities give \(c_{-t}+c_{t/2}=-2c_t=c_t\) in characteristic three. The resulting linear map is nonzero and therefore a genuine derivation.

## Verification

The bundled standalone `verify.py` constructs every coefficient equation for an arbitrary matrix of a linear endomorphism of \(K[R_n]\), computes its rank by exact modular row elimination, and compares the resulting nullity with the stated arithmetic formula. It checks eight index-and-characteristic pairs: \((3,2),(3,5),(5,2),(5,3),(7,3),(9,2),(11,3),(13,5)\). It separately checks the Legendre-symbol Leibniz coefficient identity for \(\ell=11,19,43,59\). All checks passed. These finite checks test the formulas but are not an argument for the universal quantifiers, which are proved above.

To reproduce: run `python3 verify.py` in a standard Python 3 interpreter. No nonstandard packages, external files, random inputs or network access are required.

## Relationship to prior work

Elhamdadi, Makhlouf, Silvestrov and Zappalà (2021), *The derivation problem for quandle algebras* (arXiv:2106.08289), prove a classification for odd dihedral quandles over characteristic-zero fields and provide selected positive-characteristic low-dimensional computations. The zero-derivation case here agrees with that theorem; the all-rank, modular-characteristic dimension and Legendre family are not among its stated conclusions.

Elhamdadi, Ta and Virgin (2026), *Fourier Analysis and Idempotents in Quandle Algebras* (arXiv:2609.03799), develop a character approach to the multiplication and idempotents of Alexander and Takasaki quandle algebras. Their Fourier machinery is a foundation for this direction, but an idempotent classification is not itself a derivation classification. A prior exact finite-field idempotent-orbit calculation for odd dihedral quandle algebras contains nonlinear Fourier constraints whose formal tangent equations resemble the additive relations above. The present proof separately establishes that every derivation must be Fourier diagonal and counts the complete space for all odd-order finite abelian groups, with modular resonance invisible in ordinary point counts. This distinction avoids treating the earlier idempotent enumeration as a derivation theorem.

Sangare and Ta (2026), *Idempotents, automorphism groups, and commutator widths of quandle algebras* (arXiv:2608.01550), compute automorphism information, notably for integral odd dihedral quandle rings, not the full modular derivation dimension. The available summary of Zhang (2026), *Group-ring methods for Alexander quandle rings* (arXiv:2610.08204), concerns group-ring structures and idempotents rather than a proved derivation dimension formula; that comparison is abstract-only.

## Limitations

The classification excludes characteristics dividing \(|A|\), even-order groups, nonabelian Takasaki analogues, and other Alexander-quandle multiplications. The explicit dimension formula does not assert a canonical eigenbasis over a nonsplitting coefficient field; the number of eigenvalue parameters descends by a linear rank argument. The claimed mathematical novelty is subject to independent expert literature review, particularly historical results stated as infinitesimal idempotent geometry or automorphism-scheme tangent spaces. The finite computations support but cannot replace the general proof.
