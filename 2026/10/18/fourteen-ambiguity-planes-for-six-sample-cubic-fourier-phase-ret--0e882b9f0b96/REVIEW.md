# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement reduces every inequivalent pair to a nonzero traceless Hermitian kernel matrix of rank two. Exact Fourier linear algebra gives a five-real-dimensional kernel, and the rank-two equations are solved by the displayed exhaustive cases. The fourteen representatives have rank two and pairwise different range planes; an exact symbolic check of all 91 projector comparisons verifies the finite algebraic certificate. Conversely, each range plane has an open dense set of two-to-one ambiguities by the explicit eigenvector formula. The six-point obstruction and almost-everywhere uniqueness therefore follow without an extrapolation from finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS, best knowledge

Jaming and Rathmair, *Uniqueness of phase retrieval from three measurements*, arXiv:2205.08753v1 (18 May 2022), section 3, Proposition 3.2, prove global recovery from seven value-and-derivative sample pairs in dimension four. Their Question 3.5 asks when the analogous problem has almost-everywhere uniqueness at smaller sample numbers. Neither Proposition 3.2 nor their undersampling Proposition 3.4 supplies the six-sample cubic generic recovery and fourteen ambiguity planes. Direct semantic research-index and literature searches did not identify this classification. The basic Hermitian lifting method is known and is not claimed as novel.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result directly resolves one natural finite-dimensional instance of an explicit open generic-recovery question and completely describes the exceptional coefficient geometry, not merely a numerical injectivity threshold. The exact algebraic classification distinguishes fourteen genuine ambiguity directions; these may matter for analyzing ill-conditioned inverse problems or exception-aware sensing. A full classification at the first cubic subcritical grid is more informative than an isolated counterexample.
