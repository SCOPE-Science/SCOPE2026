# Review of the fractal census of extremal prodigious runs

## Correctness — PASS

The classification of possible extremal starts is explicitly attributed to the full-text-inspected original theorem. The remaining necessity and sufficiency use the identity for digit products at the offsets within an extremal run and elementary divisibility by each digit below the base. Every prime-power factor of the modulus is treated, including the factor sharing primes with the base. A finite Fourier expansion yields the exact residue count and uniform exponential bound. Fixed-prefix asymptotics prove the Cantor measure limit; summing shells and comparing two explicit threshold sequences proves the different positive accumulation constants. This is a complete argument for each fixed integer base at least three. Exact independent enumeration in verify.py checks 80 shells and the actual prodigious condition over finite ranges; that computation does not substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected sources

The 2022 paper by Gohn et al. proves the maximal length and localizes candidates but does not enumerate all maximum-length starts or obtain their shell asymptotic, Cantor distribution, or oscillating normalized cumulative count. Banks--Shparlinski's general digit-restriction arithmetic results cover a pre-existing ingredient and are not claimed as original. He--Sanna study the all-digits-product (Zuckerman) object, not maximum-run starts in the nonzero-digit-product setting. A sequence database lists base-ten prodigious integers but gives no corresponding asymptotic. Targeted mathematical-result searches did not reveal an equivalent result; a negative search is not proof of global priority. Residual risk: an unindexed direct corollary in broader digital congruence work or an unpublished maximal-run counting theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Maximum attainable consecutive prodigious runs are singled out in the originating research, but their frequency and locations are left quantitatively unresolved. This result gives an exact, usable digital and congruence description of *every* extremal run, a sharp census for each length with all exceptional noncoprime factors handled, and a Cantor-limit mechanism proving that no global power-law constant exists. Those consequences are structural and answer the natural abundance and distribution questions, not merely a test of isolated examples. General equidistribution by itself does not supply the maximal-run characterization or global oscillation.
