# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The twelve-vertex LCF graph definition and all \(4096\) possible vertex subsets are checked exactly. The independent-face poset has \(195\) nonempty elements. The deterministic greedy elementary-collapse matching has \(95\) disjoint matched pairs and critical dimensions \(0,2,2,2,2\). Its reversed-matched Hasse digraph was fully topologically sorted, proving acyclicity and the claimed ordinary sphere wedge. Forty-eight adjacency-preserving automorphisms were enumerated exhaustively; their signed fixed-face counts yield character sums \(48\) and squared sums \(96\), with central trace \(-2\). These numbers imply one invariant line and a three-dimensional absolutely irreducible complement. Boundary ranks were also checked over two finite fields, but field computations are not substituted for the integral Morse proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic uncertainty
Barmak's 2010 full text gives a suspension theorem for triangle-free graphs but not the Franklin multiplicity or automorphism representation. The closest indexed graph-specific published result concerns the ordinary Franklin matching complex, a distinct complex with graph edges as simplex vertices. Searches included Franklin, six-crossed-prism, Knödel, and symmetry-module formulations, and accessible own claims were compared for overlap. The record supports a distinct new theorem under the inspected sources, not an assertion of exhaustive literature priority. A possible inaccessible or unindexed exact precedent remains the principal risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Franklin graph is a canonical 12-vertex cubic bipartite graph associated with the Klein-bottle coloring problem. Determining both its entire independence-complex homotopy type and its full automorphism representation resolves natural invariants not implied by the graph's ordinary parameters or general suspension theorems. The one-dimensional invariant class and the central sign on the irreducible three-dimensional part give an interpretable structural symmetry distinction beyond Betti numbers.

## Limitations
The result treats one named graph, gives a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence and a rational group-module decomposition, and does not classify integral group modules or assert priority over inaccessible literature.
