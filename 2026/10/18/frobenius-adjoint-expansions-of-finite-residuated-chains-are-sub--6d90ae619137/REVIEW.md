# Review of finite-chain Frobenius-adjoint classification

## Correctness

**PASS.** The all-finite-chain bijection is established in both directions from F1–F5, not inferred from enumeration. The necessity proof derives the common fixed-point image and closure under fusion and residual; the converse verifies adjunction and all three Frobenius laws for subalgebra roundings. The Łukasiewicz specialization is derived with truncated sum, subtraction, and the minimal-positive-element argument. A standalone exhaustive checker corroborates the normal form for four distinct finite residuated-chain families and the divisor count for standard Łukasiewicz chains.

## Originality

**PASS with documented historical-priority risk.** Wang–Shi–Wang (2026) define Frobenius-adjoint residuated algebras and prove necessary fixed-point properties. The closest accessible published classification identifies Gödel expansions and the known \(2^{n-2}\) count. That Gödel-only result does not assert arbitrary finite non-idempotent residuated-chain sufficiency or the Łukasiewicz expansion/divisor-lattice correspondence. Teheux (2007) studies MV-subalgebra lattices; that older arithmetic ingredient is credited and not claimed as new. The combined classification, its constructive converse, and its application to the new Frobenius-adjoint semantics were not located by targeted searches. The literature search does not establish absolute priority; the possibility of an equivalent older categorical or subalgebra formulation remains.

## Value

**PASS.** This is a complete natural classification of operator expansions in a recently introduced logical algebraic semantics, rather than an isolated instance count. It gives every admissible pair on all finite residuated chains by a canonical construction. On the standard \(n\)-valued Łukasiewicz chain the number and order of expansions become the exact divisor lattice of \(n-1\), contrasting sharply with the established exponential Gödel count. This determines a natural representation and size invariant of the finite models and makes systematic finite-model construction possible.

## Limits

The theorem applies to finite, totally ordered, bounded integral commutative residuated lattices only. It does not classify arbitrary finite posets, all infinite chains, or monadic expansions under separate axioms. Sources on general adjoint maps and MV-subalgebras are relevant prior ingredients, and no independent audit has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
