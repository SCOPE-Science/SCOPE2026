# Frobenius and genus formulas for primitive triples of consecutive tetrahedral numbers

## Finding

Let \(T_n=\binom{n+2}{3}\) be the \(n\)-th tetrahedral number. For every integer \(n\ge2\), put
\[
h_n=\gcd(T_n,T_{n+1},T_{n+2}),\qquad
S_n=\langle T_n/h_n,T_{n+1}/h_n,T_{n+2}/h_n\rangle.
\]
Every \(S_n\) is a **symmetric numerical semigroup**. Writing \(F_n\) for its Frobenius number and \(g_n\) for its genus, the following six-case formula is exact:
\[
F_n=d(uv-u-v)+(d-1)z,\qquad g_n=(F_n+1)/2.
\]
The four integers \((d,u,v,z)\) are given by this table; all divisions are exact in the stated residue class.

| \(n\bmod 6\) | \(d\) | \(u\) | \(v\) | \(z\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(n+1\) | \(n/3\) | \((n+3)/3\) | \((n+3)(n+4)/3\) |
| \(1\) | \((n+1)/2\) | \(n\) | \(n+3\) | \((n+3)(n+4)/2\) |
| \(2\), \(n\ge8\) | \((n+1)/3\) | \(n\) | \(n+3\) | \((n+3)(n+4)/3\) |
| \(3\) | \((n+1)/2\) | \(n/3\) | \((n+3)/3\) | \((n+3)(n+4)/6\) |
| \(4\) | \(n+1\) | \(n\) | \(n+3\) | \((n+3)(n+4)\) |
| \(5\) | \((n+3)/2\) | \((n+1)/3\) | \((n+4)/3\) | \(n(n+1)/6\) |

The boundary case \(n=2\) uses \((d,u,v,z)=(5,1,2,2)\), hence \(S_2=\langle2,5\rangle\), \(F_2=3\), and \(g_2=2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The variables \(n\) are integers \(n\ge2\); semigroups contain \(0\), and the genus is the finite number of nonnegative integers absent from the semigroup. A numerical semigroup is symmetric if for each integer \(x\), exactly one of \(x\) and \(F-x\) belongs to it. The primitive normalization by the *common* gcd of the three original tetrahedral numbers is essential. Without it, the three terms need not have gcd one, so a Frobenius number need not exist. The four-term semigroup generated by consecutive unnormalized tetrahedral numbers is a different object.

## Proof

**1. Arithmetic reduction.** Put \(U=n(n+1)\), \(V=(n+1)(n+3)\), and \(W=(n+3)(n+4)\). The three tetrahedral numbers are the same positive rational multiple \((n+2)/6\) of \((U,V,W)\). Consequently, after primitive normalization,
\[
S_n=\langle U/c,V/c,W/c\rangle,\qquad c=\gcd(U,V,W).
\]
The gcd identities \(\gcd(U,V)=(n+1)\gcd(n,3)\) and \(\gcd(V,W)=(n+3)\gcd(n+1,3)\), followed by the six residue checks, give
\[
c=\begin{cases}2,&n\equiv1\pmod6,\\6,&n\equiv3,5\pmod6,\\3,&n\equiv0,2\pmod6,\\1,&n\equiv4\pmod6.\end{cases}
\]
For all \(n\ge3\), use the first two normalized generators as \(du,dv\), leaving \(z\) as the third, except when \(n\equiv5\pmod6\), where the last two are \(du,dv\) and the first is \(z\). Elementary gcd calculations yield the displayed table and establish
\[
\gcd(u,v)=\gcd(d,z)=1,\qquad d\ge2.
\]

**2. The decisive gluing.** In every case \(z\in\langle u,v\rangle\), as witnessed by nonnegative integer coefficients. For residues \(0\), \(2\), and \(4\), respectively,
\[
z=(n+4)v,\qquad z=((n+4)/3)v,\qquad z=(n+4)v.
\]
For residue \(1\), \(z=((n+3)/2)u+2v\). For residue \(3\), write \(m=n/3\); then \(m\) is odd, \(u=m\), \(v=m+1\), and
\[
z=\frac{3(m+1)}2u+2v.
\]
For residue \(5\), write \(k=(n+1)/6\); then \(u=2k\), \(v=2k+1\), and
\[
z=(2k-1)u+kv.
\]
Therefore, for every \(n\ge3\),
\[
S_n=d\langle u,v\rangle+z\mathbb N_0.
\]
For \(n=2\), the normalized generators are \((2,5,10)\); choose \((d,u,v,z)=(5,1,2,2)\), which has the same structure.

**3. Frobenius formula and symmetry.** Let \(Q=\langle u,v\rangle\). Since \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), Sylvester's formula gives \(F(Q)=uv-u-v\), including \(F(Q)=-1\) if one generator is \(1\). Since \(\gcd(d,z)=1\), each integer \(x\) has a unique \(j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\) satisfying \(x\equiv jz\pmod d\). Because \(z\in Q\),
\[
x\in S_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (x-jz)/d\in Q.
\]
The largest gap in residue class \(jz\bmod d\) is thus \(dF(Q)+jz\), and the largest across these classes is \(F_n=dF(Q)+(d-1)z\). Moreover, for each gap \(x\), its complementary residue class has parameter \(d-1-j\) and quotient \(F(Q)-(x-jz)/d\). The two-generator semigroup \(Q\) is symmetric, so this quotient belongs to \(Q\); hence \(F_n-x\in S_n\). The same argument applies if the quotient is negative, because then its complementary quotient exceeds \(F(Q)\) and belongs to \(Q\). Thus \(S_n\) is symmetric and \(g_n=(F_n+1)/2\). This completes all cases without a finite-search assumption.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` checks the normalization, gluing representations and Frobenius formulas for all \(2\le n\le300\). For every \(2\le n\le160\), it independently computes the Apéry set by shortest-path search and compares Frobenius number and genus. Running `python3 verify.py` produces `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests check arithmetic and implementation; the universal statement follows from the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

OEIS A069761 (first contributed April 9, 2002) tabulates the Frobenius number for **four consecutive, unnormalized** tetrahedral numbers, not the primitive three-term window. Robles-Pérez and Rosales (2017), especially Section 3, Propositions 10–12, establish the four-term formula and examine gcds of three-term prefixes as an intermediate tool. Their displayed identities imply telescopic behavior for some normalized three-term windows, but they do not state this all-residue three-term Frobenius/genus classification; their four-term Frobenius invariant does not determine it. For example, at \(n=2\), the primitive three-term Frobenius number is \(3\), whereas the published four-term Frobenius number is \(41\). The work uses the classical Sylvester and Johnson/Brauer–Shockley gluing formulas; it does not claim those formulas as new. Fel (2016) studies three consecutive integer **powers**, a different generating family.

## Limitations

This is a result specifically about primitive triples of *ordinary* tetrahedral numbers, not unnormalized triples or arbitrary polyhedral-number families. The construction is a comparatively short application of known gluing theory, and several residue cases are close consequences of relations already present in the 2017 four-term paper. Although the complete normalized three-term classification was not located in the inspected material, an implicit derivation from that paper or from another unlocated source remains a substantive originality risk. No independent mathematical or priority audit has been performed.
