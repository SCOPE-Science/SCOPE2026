# Frobenius-degree and orbit laws for pairs of idempotents in finite matrix rings

## Finding

Let \(q=p^e\) with \(p\) prime, and choose an ordered pair \((E,F)\) from the set of all idempotents of \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\). In this note **ring generation** means the smallest subring containing the identity and \(E,F\), with only prime-field scalar multiplication automatic. This differs from **algebra generation**, in which all \(\mathbb F_q\)-scalar matrices are supplied in advance.

For every positive integer \(d\), put
\[
T_d=\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(r)p^{d/r},\qquad
S_d=T_d-2\mathbf 1_{d=1}.
\]
Thus \(S_d\) counts elements of exact degree \(d\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) other than zero and one. For each divisor \(d\mid e\), the number of ordered idempotent pairs that generate the full \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra and generate, as an ordinary unital ring, a copy of \(M_2(\mathbb F_{p^d})\) is
\[
\boxed{G_d=q(q+1)(q-1)S_d.}
\]
In particular, the number generating the **entire ring without supplied field scalars** is
\[
\boxed{G_e=q(q+1)(q-1)S_e,}
\]
and, because the total idempotent count is \(q(q+1)+2\), the probability for independent uniformly random idempotents is
\[
\boxed{\frac{q(q+1)(q-1)S_e}{[q(q+1)+2]^2}.}
\]
The nondegenerate ordered rank-one idempotent pairs are classified under simultaneous conjugation by \(a=\operatorname{tr}(EF)\in\mathbb F_q\setminus\{0,1\}\): there is exactly one free projective conjugacy orbit per value of \(a\), each of size \(q(q^2-1)\). All formulas include \(p=2\). In particular \(G_e=0\) for \(q=2\).

## Assumptions and scope

The matrices are \(2\)-by-\(2\) over an arbitrary finite field \(\mathbb F_q\). An idempotent means \(E^2=E\). "A copy" above means a subring conjugate inside \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) to the standard scalar-entry subring over the unique subfield \(\mathbb F_{p^d}\). The count \(G_d\) covers pairs generating the full ambient algebra over \(\mathbb F_q\); it does not attempt to classify every subring arising from degenerate pairs. Distinct choices \((E,F)\) are counted as ordered pairs.

## Proof

**Step 1: parameterize idempotents.** An idempotent over a field has eigenvalues in \(\{0,1\}\). In dimension two it therefore has rank zero, one or two. The only rank-zero and rank-two idempotents are zero and the identity. A rank-one idempotent is uniquely determined by an ordered pair of complementary lines (image and kernel): there are \(q+1\) possible images and \(q\) complementary kernels. Thus the total number of idempotents is \(q(q+1)+2\). A pair generating the full matrix algebra must consist of rank-one idempotents, because including zero or the identity leaves a commutative singly generated algebra.

**Step 2: the scalar invariant and the nondegenerate orbit.** Fix a rank-one idempotent \(E\) and simultaneously conjugate it to
\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every other rank-one idempotent has a unique representation
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&1-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad bc=a(1-a).
\]
Indeed, trace one and determinant zero are equivalent to being rank-one idempotent by the Cayley–Hamilton identity, including in characteristic two. Also \(a=\operatorname{tr}(EF)\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
(E-F)^2=(1-a)I.
\]
When \(a\notin\{0,1\}\), both \(b\) and \(c\) are nonzero. For each of the \(q-2\) such values of \(a\), there are \(q-1\) choices of \(b\), with \(c=a(1-a)/b\). Diagonal conjugation fixing \(E\) acts transitively on these choices and has trivial stabilizer modulo scalar matrices. Hence each \(a\) gives exactly one free \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\)-orbit and its size is \(q(q+1)(q-1)=q(q^2-1)\).

If \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the equation \(bc=0\) makes \(F\) triangular in the chosen coordinates. Since \(E\) is diagonal, the two idempotents preserve a common proper line. Every ring polynomial in them preserves that line, so the generated ring is a proper subring and cannot equal \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\). Thus exactly the nondegenerate pairs generate the full algebra when coefficient scalars are supplied.

**Step 3: determine the unital generated ring without field scalars.** For a nondegenerate pair put \(k=\mathbb F_p(a)\). The identity above shows \(aI=I-(E-F)^2\) is in the unital ring \(R=\langle E,F\rangle_{\rm ring}\). Therefore \(kI\subseteq R\) (the finite integral subring generated by \(aI\) is the field \(kI\)). The following four matrix positions belong to \(R\):
\[
E_{11}=E,\quad E_{22}=I-E,\quad
bE_{12}=EF(I-E),\quad cE_{21}=(I-E)FE.
\]
As \(bc=a(1-a)\in k^\times\), these four generate the unital \(k\)-algebra
\[
B=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&by\\cz&w\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z,w\in k\right\}.
\]
It is a subring: the diagonal cross-products introduce only the coefficient \(bc\in k\). It contains \(E,F\), so \(R\subseteq B\); conversely the four displayed positions and \(kI\) show \(B\subseteq R\). Thus \(R=B\). Conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(b,1)\) identifies \(B\) with \(M_2(k)\), since \(ck=b^{-1}k\). If \([k:\mathbb F_p]=d\), then \(|R|=p^{4d}\). In particular \(R=M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) exactly when \(d=e\), because the unique subfield of order \(p^e\) is the whole \(\mathbb F_q\).

**Step 4: count Frobenius degrees.** The elements in \(\mathbb F_q\) of exact degree \(d\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), for \(d\mid e\), are counted by the standard Möbius-inversion formula \(T_d=\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(r)p^{d/r}\). Excluding \(a=0,1\) removes two elements of exact degree one and none for \(d>1\), giving \(S_d\). For every such \(a\), Step 2 gives \(q(q+1)(q-1)\) ordered pairs. Multiplication yields \(G_d\) and the probability displayed above. No asymptotic or numerical approximation is used in this proof.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` constructs finite fields as polynomial quotients and enumerates idempotent pairs by direct \(2\)-by-\(2\) multiplication. It forms the ordinary unital prime-field subring by linear closure under multiplication by the two generators and independently compares its dimension with the theorem. All nondegenerate pairs were enumerated for each tested field; direct subring-closure verification was exhaustive for all six listed fields. It reported `VERIFY_OK`. Counts of pairs generating the whole unital ring were \(0,24,120,360,3024,4320\) for \(q=2,3,4,5,8,9\), respectively. The proof, not the finite tests, covers all finite fields.

## Relationship to prior work

The two-idempotent structure theorem of Rowen and Segev (2016, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 2.7) establishes the central element \((E-F)^2\), the matrix-ring structure in nondegenerate cases, and the characteristic-two two-generator exception. These foundational structural facts are not claimed as new. Kravchenko, Mazur and Petrenko (2014) study unrestricted finite-matrix-algebra generating tuples; their count does not impose idempotency or resolve the orbit-by-trace arithmetic distribution above. Jia and Xiao (2026) connect finite-dimensional algebras generated by idempotents to zero-Jordan-product-determined algebras but do not count constrained idempotent generating pairs. The contribution here is an exact field-of-definition spectrum, orbit-size law, and scalar-free generating probability for idempotent pairs.

## Limitations

The statement concerns \(2\)-by-\(2\) full matrix rings over finite fields and nondegenerate idempotent pairs. It does not classify subrings generated by triangular/degenerate pairs or higher matrix dimensions. The counting theorem is established by direct proof and corroborated in small fields; no independent expert review has been conducted. Previously unindexed enumerations of idempotent pairs could still overlap the explicit counting result.
