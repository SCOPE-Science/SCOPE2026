# Review of the idempotent generating-pair theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces every nontrivial idempotent pair to two rank-one idempotents. With the first equal to a diagonal rank-one projector, the second has the form described in RESULT.md with determinant zero. The central identity \((E-F)^2=(1-a)I\) and the two off-diagonal corners give an explicit description of the ordinary unital ring as a conjugate of \(M_2(\mathbb F_p(a))\). Both inclusions are proved. Möbius inversion counts exact Frobenius degrees, while line-complement counting supplies the orbit factor. Boundary values \(q=2\), \(a=0,1\), and characteristic two are explicitly handled. Finite-field direct subring generation confirms the predicted counts for six fields; all ordered rank-one idempotent pairs were checked by prime-field ring-closure enumeration in each of the six fields.

## Originality — PASS (self-review)

Rowen–Segev's 2016 Theorem 1.1 already proves the underlying two-idempotent structure, and their Remark 2.7 already notes the binary exception; neither is represented here as novel. Kravchenko–Mazur–Petrenko's earlier work counts unrestricted algebra-generator tuples, not the constrained idempotent sample with scalar-free ring generation. Jia–Xiao 2026 proves a characterization of algebras generated by idempotents rather than enumerating constrained generating pairs. Comparisons of these precise statements with the new orbit-parametrized Frobenius-degree and probability formulas found no direct implication giving the presented exact count without additional new counting. Equivalent formulations in other unindexed literature remain possible.

## Value — PASS

The natural question is quantitative: among idempotents, which ordered pairs generate the full finite matrix ring when coefficient scalars are *not* supplied? The answer separates field-relative generation from genuine ring generation and supplies an exact probability, exact subfield-conjugacy spectrum, and one-orbit-per-trace law. This is a complete, field-uniform enumerative result for a canonical small matrix-ring family, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The lower-field strata remain significant when the field extension degree is composite.

## Limitations and remaining risks

No assertion is made about degenerate-pair subring types or higher-dimensional matrices. The 2015 publisher edition of *Generators of maximal orders* was not available as full text from its publisher; substantive Sections 2–3 and its 2014 open preprint metadata were accessible in indexed excerpts. While the source concerns unrestricted tuples and contains no identified idempotent-constrained count, implicit overlap cannot be excluded categorically. Independent review remains outstanding.
