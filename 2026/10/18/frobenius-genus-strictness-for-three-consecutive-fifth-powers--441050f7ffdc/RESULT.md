# Frobenius–genus strictness for three consecutive fifth powers

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let
\[
S_n=\langle(n-1)^5,n^5,(n+1)^5\rangle.
\]
If \(F_n\) is the Frobenius number and \(g_n\) the genus, then
\[
2g_n=F_n+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n=3.
\]
More precisely, \(S_3=\langle32,243\rangle\) has \(F_3=7501\) and \(g_3=3751\); for every \(n\ge4\) one has \(2g_n>F_n+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
Generated semigroups use nonnegative integer coefficients and contain zero. The two smaller generators are coprime, so the semigroup is cofinite. The genus counts the nonnegative gaps. On \([0,F_n]\), reflection about \(F_n/2\) sends every member to a gap, since no two members can sum to \(F_n\). Thus \(2g_n\ge F_n+1\), with equality precisely for a symmetric semigroup (exactly one of \(x,F_n-x\) belongs to it for all integers \(0\le x\le F_n\)).

## Proof
We use the classical three-generator symmetry theorem of Herzog, in the form of the complete-intersection structure in Watanabe [2, Proposition 3]: **if a symmetric numerical semigroup is minimally generated by three positive integers**, then, after reordering those generators, they are \(du,dv,w\), where \(d>1\), \(\gcd(u,v)=\gcd(d,w)=1\), and \(w\in\langle u,v\rangle\). In particular, among three minimal generators at least one pair has a nontrivial gcd. The hypothesis of minimal generation matters; we check it before using the theorem. We also use the two-generator Frobenius and symmetry formulas for coprime generators.

**The exception.** For \(n=3\), the largest generator \(1024\) is a multiple of \(32\), so \(S_3=\langle32,243\rangle\) is symmetric and \(F_3=32\cdot243-32-243=7501\), \(g_3=(F_3+1)/2=3751\).

**Odd indices \(n=2m+1\), \(m\ge2\).** Put \(u=m^5\), \(v=(m+1)^5\), and \(w=(2m+1)^5\). The generators in increasing order are \(32u,w,32v\). The smallest is not redundant, and the middle one is odd, so it cannot be generated by the two even outer generators. If \(32v=A(32u)+Cw\), reduction modulo \(32\) forces \(C=32B\) since \(w\) is coprime to \(32\). Then \(v=Au+Bw\), which forces \(B=0\) since \(w>v\); but \(u>1\) and \(\gcd(u,v)=1\). The largest is therefore not redundant either. The only pair of generators with a nontrivial gcd is the outer pair, and its gcd is exactly \(32\). Consequently symmetry would force
\[
(2m+1)^5=A m^5+B(m+1)^5,\qquad A,B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{1}
\]
The ratio \((2m+1)/(m+1)<2\) implies \(B\le31\). Modulo \(m\), (1) gives \(B\equiv1\pmod m\), so \(B=1+jm\) with \(j\ge0\) and \(jm\le30\). Modulo \(m^2\), the left side of (1) is \(1+10m\) and the right side is \(1+(j+5)m\), so \(j\equiv5\pmod m\). For \(m\ge7\), this is impossible because \(0\le j\le4\).

For \(2\le m\le6\), the exact upper bound \(B\le\lfloor(2m+1)^5/(m+1)^5\rfloor\) and the two congruences leave the following cases. Each remainder is that of \((2m+1)^5-B(m+1)^5\) modulo \(m^5\), in the listed order.

| \(m\) | Possible \(B\) | Remainders |
|---:|:---|:---|
| 2 | 3, 7, 11 | 28, 16, 4 |
| 3 | 7, 16 | 162, 180 |
| 4 | 5 | 416 |
| 5 | 1 | 150 |
| 6 | none | — |

Thus (1) is impossible for every \(m\ge2\), excluding symmetry for all odd \(n\ge5\).

**Even indices \(n\ge4\).** The generators are pairwise coprime, since \(n-1,n+1\) are odd and differ by two. The smallest is not redundant; the middle generator is below the largest and not divisible by the smallest. Redundancy of the largest would require
\[
(n+1)^5=A(n-1)^5+Bn^5,\qquad A,B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{2}
\]
Modulo \(n\) this gives \(A\equiv-1\pmod n\), so \(A\ge n-1\). On the other hand \(A\le((n+1)/(n-1))^5\). For \(n\ge8\), the latter is at most \((9/7)^5<7\le n-1\), a contradiction. At \(n=6\) only \(A=5\) satisfies the bound and congruence; the remainder in (2) modulo \(6^5\) is \(1182\). At \(n=4\) the candidates \(A=3,7,11\) give remainders \(348,400,452\) modulo \(4^5\). None vanishes. The three generators are therefore minimal and pairwise coprime, so the symmetry theorem rules out symmetry. This proves strictness for the even indices as well.

## Verification
The accompanying Python 3 program `verify.py` checks the finite candidate exclusions, expanded finite ranges of the two membership equations, and exact Frobenius/genus computations for \(3\le n\le11\) via Apéry sets. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK`. The congruence bounds in the proof, not the finite parameter sweeps, handle all indices.

## Relationship to prior work
Fel [1, §1.1] classifies symmetry at exponents two, three, and four, with the quartic exceptions \(n=3,5,7\). The higher-power discussion [1, §5] includes a finite scan of odd indices and records higher-power exceptions at exponents eleven and thirteen; it does not prove the all-index fifth-power classification. The contribution here is the all-index exclusion at exponent five, including even indices, by the explicit bounds and congruences above. The three-generator structural criterion is classical, not a contribution of this calculation.

## Limitations
Beyond \(n=3\), this result determines symmetry or strictness, not either invariant separately or the size of the strict gap. It does not address other exponents. The literature comparison does not certify historical priority.
