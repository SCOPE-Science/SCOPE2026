# Review of the fifth-power Frobenius–genus classification

## Correctness — PASS
The statement is equivalent to symmetry of a three-generated numerical semigroup. Its critical premise is the classical three-generated symmetry/gluing criterion, quoted explicitly from the freely accessible complete text of Fel [1, Section 1, Lemma 1 and formula (1.4)]. For odd indices the reduced-generator equation is necessary and sufficient, and the coefficient bound, congruences modulo \(m\) and \(m^2\), and five small values prove complete exclusion. Even indices have pairwise-coprime generators; two generators cannot be redundant, and the third is ruled out by a sharp modular lower bound and both small cases. The exact two-generator formulas certify \(n=3\). The independent code uses integer residues and exact Apéry sets; none of its finite ranges replaces the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty
Fel's 2016 paper [1] explicitly analyzes the very same semigroup family for exponents two through four and flags \(k\ge5\) for further analysis; its Proposition 3 has exceptions \(n=3,5,7\) in the fourth-power case. Fel §5 already gives bounded numerical evidence against fifth-power odd-index exceptions, but not the all-index fifth-power rigidity theorem. Published research-record searches, including aliases 'symmetric', 'Gorenstein', 'Frobenius genus equality', and 'consecutive fifth powers', returned no equivalent or dominating statement in the results inspected. A later unindexed general theorem may subsume this specific case; neither a failed search nor an inaccessible source certifies uniqueness. Background [2] treats general Frobenius/genus computation without this sequence of generators.

## Value — PASS
The object is the first unresolved power after Fel's explicit cubic and quartic symmetry classifications, and the exponent-five result marks a structural boundary: quartic symmetric examples at \(n=5,7\) vanish. Proving a complete natural family classification, equivalent to the strict Frobenius/genus inequality at all remaining indices, answers a mathematically motivated question rather than tabulating isolated cases.

## Limitations and closely related sources
No formulas for the individual large-index Frobenius numbers or genera are proved; the claim is only strictness. The general gluing classification is cited rather than reproved. Relevant comparisons are Fel (2016), Sections 1 and the appended low-power symmetry arguments, and Rosales–Branco (2011), on Frobenius and genus methodology.
