# Frobenius-orbit census of idempotents in finite dihedral quandle algebras

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 3\) be odd and let \(q\) be a prime power with \(\gcd(n,q)=1\). The algebra \(\mathbb F_q[R_n]\) is the finite-dimensional, generally nonassociative algebra with basis \(\{e_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\) and products \(e_ie_j=e_{2j-i}\). The following arithmetic orbit formula enumerates **all** its idempotents, including those of augmentation zero.

Put \(s=\operatorname{ord}_n(q)\). Let \(\Gamma=\langle -1,2\rangle\) and \(\Delta=\langle -1,2,q\rangle\), acting on the nonzero residues modulo \(n\). For each \(\Delta\)-orbit \(D\), choose \(k\in D\), put \(d=n/\gcd(n,k)\) and \(t=\operatorname{ord}_d(2)\), and set
\[
M_D=\begin{cases}2^{t/2}+1,&2^{t/2}\equiv-1\pmod d,\\2^t-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
(The first case requires even \(t\).) Let \(a\ge1\) be minimal with \(q^ak\in\Gamma k\). Choose \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) and \(0\le b<t\) such that \(q^ak\equiv\varepsilon2^bk\pmod n\). Define
\[
g_D=\gcd\bigl(M_D,q^s-1,\,|q^a-\varepsilon2^b|\bigr).
\]
Then \(g_D\) is independent of the allowed choices, and the exact number of idempotents is
\[
\boxed{\#\{x\in\mathbb F_q[R_n]:x^2=x\}=2\prod_{D\in((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\setminus\{0\})/\Delta}(1+g_D).}
\]
Each factor corresponds to either the zero Fourier orbit or one of \(g_D\) nonzero Fourier-orbit assignments. Thus the proof classifies the idempotents individually, not only their total.

If \(n\mid q-1\), then \(s=1\) and \(\Delta=\Gamma\); this reduces to
\[
\boxed{2\prod_{D\in((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\setminus\{0\})/\Gamma}\bigl(1+\gcd(M_D,q-1)\bigr).}
\]
For \(n=11\), the split-field count is \(24\) over \(\mathbb F_{23}\) and \(68\) over \(\mathbb F_{67}\). The nonsplit counts are \(68\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(24\) over \(\mathbb F_3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The quandle multiplication is \(i\triangleright j=2j-i\) modulo \(n\). A general algebra element is \(x=\sum_i c_ie_i\) with \(c_i\in\mathbb F_q\). Its augmentation is \(\sum_i c_i\). No associativity, unit or invertibility is assumed. The condition \(\gcd(n,q)=1\) means the field characteristic does not divide \(n\); this is essential for the discrete Fourier transform. The formula applies to composite odd \(n\) and to fields of even or odd characteristic.

For all \(d\) occurring above, \(d\ge3\) is odd, and \(2\) is invertible modulo \(d\). In the definition of \(M_D\), take the first branch exactly if \(-1\) belongs to the cyclic group generated by \(2\) modulo \(d\). The integer \(s\) exists because \(q\) is prime to \(n\). A gcd containing zero is interpreted in the usual way, so that \(\gcd(m,0)=m\) for positive \(m\).

## Proof

**Fourier reduction.** Let \(E=\mathbb F_{q^s}\), and fix a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\in E\). For an element \(x=\sum_i c_ie_i\), define \(A_k=\sum_i c_i\zeta^{ki}\), with indices modulo \(n\). Since \(n\) is invertible, the inverse Fourier transform is \(c_i=n^{-1}\sum_k A_k\zeta^{-ki}\). Directly expanding the quandle product gives
\[
x^2=x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_k=A_{-k}A_{2k}\quad\text{for every }k.
\]
The coefficient vector lies in \(\mathbb F_q^n\) exactly when its Fourier transform satisfies \(A_{qk}=A_k^q\) for every \(k\). Indeed, this follows by raising the transform and inverse transform to the \(q\)-th power. The zero-frequency equation is \(A_0=A_0^2\), so independently \(A_0\in\{0,1\}\).

**One \(\Gamma\)-orbit.** Because multiplication by \(-1\) and by \(2\) permutes the nonzero indices, the support of a solution \(A_k=A_{-k}A_{2k}\) is a union of whole \(\Gamma\)-orbits. On any active orbit, compare the equations for \(k\) and \(-k\), obtaining
\[
A_kA_{-k}=(A_kA_{-k})(A_{2k}A_{-2k}).
\]
Cancellation, followed by permuting indices under doubling, implies \(A_{-k}=A_k^{-1}\) and \(A_{2k}=A_k^2\). Thus all coordinates on an active \(\Gamma\)-orbit are powers \(u^{\varepsilon2^j}\) of its chosen seed \(u=A_k\ne0\). If \(-k\) lies in the doubling orbit, then \(2^{t/2}k=-k\) and the consistency equation is \(u^{2^{t/2}+1}=1\). Otherwise the two doubling orbits are disjoint and the consistency equation is \(u^{2^t-1}=1\). These are exactly the two definitions of \(M_D\). Conversely, each corresponding root \(u\) produces a unique solution on this orbit.

**Frobenius descent.** Multiplication by \(q\) permutes the \(\Gamma\)-orbits and commutes with \(\Gamma\). They form cycles; each is precisely one \(\Delta\)-orbit \(D\). Starting at \(k\), successive \(q\)-translates of its \(\Gamma\)-orbit are distinct until the first return after \(a\) steps, when \(q^ak=\varepsilon2^bk\). A nonzero assignment to the first orbit propagates uniquely to all others by \(A_{qk}=A_k^q\). It closes consistently precisely when
\[
u^{q^a}=u^{\varepsilon2^b},\qquad u^{M_D}=1,\qquad u^{q^s-1}=1.
\]
The final condition enforces membership in \(E^\times\). Since \(E^\times\) is cyclic of order \(q^s-1\), exactly \(g_D\) nonzero seeds satisfy these conditions. The only other assignment on \(D\) is the identically zero one. The construction is independent of the representation of \(q^ak\) because any two allowed exponents differ by a multiple of \(M_D\), as follows from the explicit orbit relations established above. Different \(\Delta\)-orbits are independent.

Multiplying the \(1+g_D\) choices and the two independent values of \(A_0\), then applying inverse Fourier transform, proves the formula and its bijective parametrization. In the split case \(s=a=1\), choose \(\varepsilon=1\) and \(b=0\) to obtain the stated reduction.

## Verification

The included standalone `verify.py` computes the \(\Gamma\)- and \(\Delta\)-orbits and arithmetic factors, directly enumerates all coefficient vectors for ten small nonsplit prime-field instances, and tests \(x^2=x\) using the original multiplication table. It also exhaustively counts the split examples \((n,q)=(3,7),(5,11)\), checks the four-element field for \(n=3\), and reconstructs every predicted idempotent by inverse Fourier transform in nine split prime-field cases up to \(n=17\). It reports `ALL_QUANDLE_IDEMPOTENT_CHECKS_PASSED`. These finite tests are independent consistency checks, not a proof for arbitrary \(n,q\).

## Relationship to prior work

Elhamdadi, Nunez, Singh and Swain (2022) study idempotents, free products and quandle coverings. Earlier integral dihedral-quandle idempotent calculations and small-field cases establish special information, not the general finite-field arithmetic census asserted here. Elhamdadi, Ta and Virgin (2026) give the Fourier recurrence \(A_k=A_{-k}A_{2k}\) for complex quandle algebras; this Fourier method and recurrence are explicitly **prior art**. The contribution here is the complete multiplicative-orbit solution over arbitrary nonmodular finite fields, including Frobenius descent, precise orbit factors and arithmetic jumps. The examples for \(R_3\) and \(R_5\) overlap published small-case calculations and are verification rather than novelty claims.

## Limitations

The formula does not apply when the characteristic divides \(n\); modular Fourier inversion fails there, and modular idempotents form a different problem. The theorem counts labelled algebra elements, not orbits under algebra automorphisms. It gives no count of quasigroup/quandle homomorphisms, and it does not establish historical priority against all unindexed literature. The Fourier recurrence is already known; priority is restricted to the finite-field arithmetic enumeration and descent described above. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
