# Frobenius-orbit generation law for finite-field incidence rings

## Finding

Let \(P\) be a nonempty finite partially ordered set with \(n\) elements, \(c\) cover relations, and \(\rho=|\{(i,j):i\le_P j\}|\). Let \(q=p^e\), with \(p\) prime and \(e\ge1\), and write \(A=I(P,\mathbb F_q)\) for the incidence algebra, regarded only as a **unital ring** (equivalently, as an \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra). For any integer \(k\ge1\), put

\[
N_e(k)=\frac1e\sum_{d\mid e}\mu(d)p^{ek/d}.
\]

The number of ordered \(k\)-tuples of elements of \(A\) that generate the entire unital ring *without adjoining scalar matrices from \(\mathbb F_q\)* is exactly

\[
\boxed{G_{P,q}(k)=e^n (N_e(k))_{\underline n}\,(q^k-q)^c\,q^{k(\rho-n-c)},}
\]

where \((N)_{\underline n}=N(N-1)\cdots(N-n+1)\), interpreted as zero for \(N<n\). The factor \(0^0\), which can occur when \(c=0\), means \(1\). In particular the exact probability for independent uniform elements is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P_{\mathrm{ring}}(P,q,k)=\frac{e^n(N_e(k))_{\underline n}}{q^{kn}}\left(1-q^{1-k}\right)^c.}
\]

The least **positive** number of unital-ring generators equals the least \(k\ge1\) for which \(N_e(k)\ge n\) and either \(c=0\) or \(k\ge2\). In comparison, when the entire scalar field is supplied in advance, the known probability is \((q^k)_{\underline n}q^{-kn}(1-q^{1-k})^c\). When the scalar-assisted probability is nonzero, the exact ratio of unassisted to assisted generation is the **poset-independent Galois penalty** \(e^n(N_e(k))_{\underline n}/(q^k)_{\underline n}\). Thus scalar-field generation and prime-field ring generation are genuinely different for nonprime fields. For the two-element chain over \(\mathbb F_4\), exactly \(1440\) of the \(4096\) ordered pairs generate as a unital ring, whereas \(2880\) generate if all \(\mathbb F_4\) scalars are supplied.

## Assumptions and scope

Every incidence-algebra element is a matrix \(M=(m_{ij})\) with entries in \(\mathbb F_q\), with \(m_{ij}=0\) unless \(i\le_P j\). Matrix multiplication is used. The unital subring generated by \(M_1,\ldots,M_k\) is the image of the free **unital** associative algebra \(\mathbb F_p\langle X_1,\ldots,X_k\rangle\); it is not, by definition, closed under multiplication by arbitrary elements of \(\mathbb F_q\). The result holds without restrictions on the shape, connectedness, width, or height of \(P\), and includes the discrete poset and the one-element poset.

For each vertex \(i\), its **diagonal label** is \(a_i=((M_1)_{ii},\ldots,(M_k)_{ii})\in\mathbb F_q^k\). For a cover \(i\lessdot j\), its **cover label** is \(w_{ij}=((M_1)_{ij},\ldots,(M_k)_{ij})\in\mathbb F_q^k\). Frobenius acts on \(\mathbb F_q^k\) coordinatewise by \(a\mapsto a^p\). A *full orbit* has exactly \(e\) distinct points.

## Proof

**Step 1: diagonal generation.** Let \(J\) be the ideal of strictly upper-triangular incidence matrices. The quotient \(A/J\) is \(\mathbb F_q^n\). The diagonal image of the generated unital subring is the image of the evaluation map

\[
\mathbb F_p[X_1,\ldots,X_k]\longrightarrow \mathbb F_q^n,
\qquad f\longmapsto (f(a_1),\ldots,f(a_n)).
\]

The evaluation onto the \(i\)-th coordinate is onto \(\mathbb F_q\) precisely when its tuple \(a_i\) generates \(\mathbb F_q\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), equivalently when \(a_i\) has a full Frobenius orbit of length \(e\). Two such evaluations have equal kernels precisely when the labels belong to the same Frobenius orbit: field isomorphisms over \(\mathbb F_p\) are Frobenius powers. These kernels are maximal; the Chinese remainder theorem therefore makes the full evaluation onto \(\mathbb F_q^n\) exactly when all \(n\) labels lie in **distinct full Frobenius orbits**. Möbius inversion counts the points of orbit size \(e\) as \(\sum_{d\mid e}\mu(d)p^{ek/d}\). Thus there are \(N_e(k)\) full orbits, with \(e\) points each, and exactly \(e^n(N_e(k))_{\underline n}\) admissible ordered diagonal-label lists. For \(e=1\) this specializes to pairwise distinct labels in \(\mathbb F_q^k\).

**Step 2: the two-by-two cover lemma.** For every cover \(i\lessdot j\), the restriction to the \((i,i),(i,j),(j,j)\) entries is an algebra homomorphism \(\pi_{ij}:A\to T_2(\mathbb F_q)\); the cover hypothesis removes intermediate product terms. Let \(B_{ij}\) be the unital \(\mathbb F_p\)-subalgebra generated by these restrictions. Under Step 1 its diagonal map is onto \(\mathbb F_q^2\). Its kernel lies in the one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-space \(\mathbb F_q E_{12}\). If this kernel is nonzero, multiplication by lifts of arbitrary left diagonal scalars makes it all of \(\mathbb F_q E_{12}\), whence \(B_{ij}=T_2(\mathbb F_q)\).

If the kernel vanishes, the diagonal map identifies \(B_{ij}\) with the commutative ring \(\mathbb F_q^2\). The unique lifted idempotent with diagonal \((1,0)\) then has the form \(E=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) for some \(t\in\mathbb F_q\). Every matrix \(H=\begin{pmatrix}a&w\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\in B_{ij}\) commutes with \(E\), forcing \(w=t(a-b)\). Conversely, if the generator cover coordinates satisfy \(w_{ij}=t(a_i-a_j)\) for a common \(t\in\mathbb F_q\), every generator lies in the conjugated diagonal subring \(\{\begin{pmatrix}a&t(a-b)\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\}\), so \(B_{ij}\) is proper. Therefore the cover projection is surjective **if and only if** \(w_{ij}\) is **not** a scalar multiple of \(a_i-a_j\). Because the diagonal labels belong to distinct Frobenius orbits, \(a_i-a_j\ne0\), and there are exactly \(q^k-q\) valid cover vectors.

**Step 3: local-to-global generation.** Let \(B\subseteq A\) be the generated subring. Suppose its diagonal image is all of \(\mathbb F_q^n\) and every cover projection is onto \(T_2(\mathbb F_q)\). The nilpotent ideal \(K=B\cap J\) is the Jacobson radical of \(B\), since \(B/K\cong\mathbb F_q^n\) is semisimple. The quotient is separable over the perfect prime field \(\mathbb F_p\). The standard Wedderburn–Malcev splitting and conjugacy theorem provides a subalgebra \(C\subseteq B\) mapping isomorphically onto \(\mathbb F_q^n\) and an invertible element \(u\in1+J\) such that \(uCu^{-1}\) is the standard diagonal algebra \(D\). Conjugation by \(u\) preserves the fact that each cover projection is surjective, because it induces conjugation by an invertible upper-unitriangular two-by-two matrix on each cover. Hence \(uBu^{-1}\) contains \(D\) and has full cover projections. Cutting with the diagonal idempotents at vertices \(i,j\) shows that it contains every \(\mathbb F_q E_{ij}\) for \(i\lessdot j\). The product of the matrix units along a saturated chain is \(E_{ij}\); thus it contains all permitted \(E_{ij}\) and equals \(A\). Conjugating back, \(B=A\). Necessity of the diagonal and cover conditions follows from the corresponding quotient homomorphisms. This establishes the exact generator criterion.

**Step 4: counting and the minimum.** The \(c\) cover-coordinate vectors are independently chosen, each in \(q^k-q\) ways after the diagonal labels. The \(\rho-n-c\) remaining comparable off-diagonal positions are unrestricted, giving \(q^{k(\rho-n-c)}\) possibilities. Multiplying with Step 1 yields \(G_{P,q}(k)\); dividing by \(|A|^k=q^{k\rho}\) gives the probability. The count is positive exactly under the claimed two numerical conditions. If \(c>0\), then \(k=1\) cannot generate a noncommutative incidence algebra, reflected by \(q^k-q=0\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verifier.py`. The standalone script explicitly constructs finite prime-field-generated subrings as additive spans closed under multiplication, independently of Frobenius-orbit tests, and exhaustively compares the resulting generation counts to the formula for five chain cases \((q,n,k)=(2,2,2),(3,2,2),(4,2,1),(4,2,2),(2,3,2)\), a three-vertex fork over \(\mathbb F_2\) with \(k=2\), and the two-vertex discrete poset over \(\mathbb F_4\) with \(k=2\). The seven pairs \((\text{direct count},\text{formula})\) are \((24,24),(432,432),(0,0),(1440,1440),(384,384),(96,96),(120,120)\), respectively. These finite cases are corroboration, not a proof of the unrestricted claim. The symbolic proof is Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work

Kolegov [1, Section 3, Lemmas 3–4; Section 4, Theorem 7] counts tuples that **together with every scalar matrix from the coefficient ring** generate the incidence ring. With coefficient field \(\mathbb F_q\), its diagonal requirement is pairwise distinct \(\mathbb F_q\)-labels and its cover factor is \(q^k-q\). The present ring-generation event does **not** adjoin that scalar field; it requires separation of Frobenius orbits and yields a different count when \(e>1\). The distinction is genuine already in the explicit \(\mathbb F_4\), two-vertex example.

First, Reichstein and Salazar [2, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 3.2, Lemma 3.3] determine the number of Frobenius orbits required for generators of products of finite field extensions, establishing the **diagonal quotient ingredient**, which is not claimed as new here. Their statement does not count generators of incidence rings with nonzero radical or give the independent cover correction. Earlier generator criteria of Longstaff–Rosenthal [3] and Kolegov [4] establish foundational structural results with specified coefficient algebra; those results are credited, not reproduced as novel findings. The new theorem is the exact prime-field **ring** generation law for arbitrary incidence posets, including the justification that the radical test still factorizes after imposing the Frobenius condition.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns uniform independent tuples and finite incidence algebras over fields, not arbitrary finite coefficient rings or non-unital generation. The distinction disappears for prime coefficient fields (\(e=1\)), in which case the counting formula is already contained in [1]. The exact diagonal-orbit formula is previously established in [2]. This claim does not assert that the individual diagonal or cover lemmas are new. A related 2023 generator-classification paper [4] was not available in full text for the present comparison, so an implicit reformulation of the combined result there remains a bibliographic risk.
