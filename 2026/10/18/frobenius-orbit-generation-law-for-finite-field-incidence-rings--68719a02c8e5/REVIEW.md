# Review of the prime-field incidence-ring generation law

## Correctness — PASS

The statement counts unital subring generation over the prime field rather than generation after adjoining scalar matrices. Step 1 proves the exact diagonal criterion via the Chinese remainder theorem, full Frobenius orbits, and Möbius inversion. Step 2 directly classifies subalgebras of the two-by-two triangular ring that surject onto both diagonal fields: a proper such subalgebra has trivial off-diagonal kernel and is a conjugated diagonal algebra; otherwise it is the entire triangular algebra. Step 3 uses the standard Wedderburn–Malcev theorem under its verified hypotheses (finite-dimensional algebras over a perfect field, separable semisimple quotient) and the cover-matrix-unit products to establish that the local tests are jointly sufficient. Step 4 counts independent coordinates. The proof includes the one-vertex and discrete-poset cases, the zero-factor convention, nonprime fields, and the restriction to positive tuple lengths. The verifier independently enumerates seven small incidence-ring cases and matches every count; it does not replace Steps 1–4. No proof of the Wedderburn–Malcev theorem itself is supplied, since it is a standard cited structure theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with specified limits

The 2025 primary source by Kolegov counts only generation after the entire coefficient ring of scalars is adjoined; the definition in its Section 2, the main Theorem 7 and the field-specialized Lemma 4 preserve that assumption. Its formula for a field of cardinality \(q\) requires only distinct diagonal labels, not distinct Frobenius orbits, so it does not imply the stated nonprime-field ring-generation probability without another substantive argument. First–Reichstein–Salazar's 2017 paper proves the Frobenius-orbit generator condition for étale field products, exactly the quotient phenomenon acknowledged here, but does not handle nontrivial incidence radicals. The present contribution combines these ingredients by a separately proved ring-level cover criterion and local-to-global lifting argument and derives the complete incidence-poset probability. The \(\mathbb F_4\) chain produces different numerical probabilities, ruling out mere restatement. Relevant published database and semantic searches for ring generation without scalars and Galois-label criteria did not identify a covering result. This does not certify global uniqueness: the full 2023 related paper was not read and an implicit older ring-generation formula remains possible.

## Value — PASS

Exact generator probabilities for structured finite rings are natural invariants: existing work studies random generation with scalars, while actual ring generation from arbitrary matrices is a different experiment and can be half as frequent already in the smallest nonprime-field triangular case. The result supplies a complete formula and a sharp existence threshold simultaneously for every finite poset, with explicit dependence on its cover count and its coefficient-field extension degree. It separates a Galois obstruction from a radical obstruction and gives a reproducible method for comparing both notions of generation. Neither the scalar-assisted formula alone nor the field-product generator theorem alone supplies this count.
