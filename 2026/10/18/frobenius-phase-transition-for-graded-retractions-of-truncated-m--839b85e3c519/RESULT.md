# Frobenius phase transition for graded retractions of truncated monomial complete intersections

## Finding
Let \(q=p^f\) be a prime power, and let \(n,d\ge 2\). Set
\[
A_{n,d}(q)=\mathbb F_q[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(X_1^d,\ldots,X_n^d)
\]
with its standard nonnegative grading. All endomorphisms below are unital, \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear and grading-preserving. For such a map \(F\), define \(r(F)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q} F(A_1)\). Its algebra image always has dimension \(d^{r(F)}\) over \(\mathbb F_q\).

**Structural dichotomy.** If \(d\) is a positive power of \(p\), the full graded endomorphism monoid is \(M_n(\mathbb F_q)\), through restriction to degree one. Otherwise, it is exactly the monoid of column-monomial \(n\times n\) matrices: each column has at most one nonzero entry. The map between matrices and algebra endomorphisms preserves composition.

Write \({n\brack r}_q=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^{n-i}-1)/(q^{r-i}-1)\). The complete graded endomorphism rank polynomial \(E_{n,d,q}(z)=\sum_F z^{r(F)}\) is
\[
E_{n,d,q}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^n {n\brack r}_q\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^n-q^j)z^r,&d\text{ is a power of }p,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom rj\bigl(1+(r-j)(q-1)\bigr)^nz^r,&d\text{ is not a power of }p.
\end{cases}
\]

The complete graded retraction rank polynomial \(R_{n,d,q}(z)=\sum_{F^2=F}z^{r(F)}\) is
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d,q}(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^n {n\brack r}_q q^{r(n-r)}z^r,& d\text{ is a power of }p,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr\bigl(1+r(q-1)\bigr)^{n-r}z^r,&d\text{ is not a power of }p.
\end{cases}}
\]
Thus the retraction-image dimensions are exactly \(1,d,d^2,\ldots,d^n\). In the Frobenius-power case, each degree-one \(r\)-plane is the degree-one part of exactly one image subalgebra, and that image supports \(q^{r(n-r)}\) retractions. In the non-power case, the retraction images are exactly the coordinate subalgebras on \(r\) variables; each such image supports \((1+r(q-1))^{n-r}\) retractions. In both cases every image is isomorphic as a graded algebra to \(A_{r,d}(q)\), with \(A_{0,d}(q)=\mathbb F_q\).

For example, \(n=3\), \(q=3\) gives \(R_{3,2,3}(z)=1+27z+15z^2+z^3\) (44 retractions), but \(R_{3,3,3}(z)=1+117z+117z^2+z^3\) (236 retractions). For \(n=3\), \(q=2\), the corresponding \(d=2\) and \(d=3\) totals are 58 and 23. This is a sharp change with the truncation exponent, not a change in the underlying degree-one vector-space dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is exactly \(\mathbb F_q\) with characteristic \(p\), the common truncation exponent is \(d\ge2\), and \(n\ge2\). Only unital coefficient-field-fixing endomorphisms preserving the total-degree grading are counted. The power condition means \(d=p^s\) for an integer \(s\ge1\); it is not the condition that \(d\) merely be divisible by \(p\). Composition, not multiplication inside the algebra, defines idempotence. Algebra-image dimension includes the unit.

## Proof
Every graded unital endomorphism is determined uniquely by the images of the degree-one generators: \(F(X_j)=\sum_i m_{ij}X_i\). The universal property of the quotient shows that a matrix \(M=(m_{ij})\) defines such a map if and only if \((\sum_i m_{ij}X_i)^d=0\) in the quotient for every \(j\). All other defining relations are automatically preserved because the quotient is commutative.

If \(d=p^s\), the Frobenius identity gives \((\sum_i m_{ij}X_i)^d=\sum_i m_{ij}^d X_i^d=0\), so no restrictions on \(M\) remain. If \(d\) is not a power of \(p\), write \(d=p^s u\) with \(p\nmid u\) and \(u>1\). The coefficient of \(T^{p^s}\) in \((1+T)^d\) is \(u\not\equiv0\pmod p\). If the same column has nonzero coefficients at two distinct variables \(X_i,X_k\), the coefficient of \(X_i^{p^s}X_k^{d-p^s}\) in its \(d\)-th power is therefore nonzero. These exponents are both strictly less than \(d\), so the monomial survives in the quotient: a contradiction. Conversely, a column with at most one nonzero entry has \(d\)-th power zero. This proves both endomorphism-monoid descriptions, and their compatibility with composition follows from restriction to degree one.

For a column-monomial matrix, its column-span is the coordinate subspace on the rows that occur with nonzero entries. The corresponding algebra image is generated by those same coordinate variables, giving algebra dimension \(d^r\). If \(d=p^s\), every invertible linear change of the generators extends to a graded algebra automorphism by the Frobenius identity; its inverse also extends. Given an arbitrary rank-\(r\) linear map, change degree-one coordinates so that its image is the first \(r\) coordinate directions. The algebra image is the corresponding truncated polynomial subalgebra, again of dimension \(d^r\).

In the Frobenius-power case, count matrices of degree-one rank \(r\) by first choosing their image \(r\)-plane in \({n\brack r}_q\) ways and then choosing a surjection from an \(n\)-space to that \(r\)-plane in \(\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^n-q^j)\) ways. In the other case, choose the set of \(r\) occupied coordinate rows in \(\binom nr\) ways. Every column is either zero or has one nonzero weighted entry in one of those rows. Requiring every chosen row to occur and applying inclusion-exclusion gives \(\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom rj(1+(r-j)(q-1))^n\) choices. These are exactly the coefficients of \(E_{n,d,q}\).

For retractions in the Frobenius-power regime, \(F^2=F\) is equivalent to \(M^2=M\). A rank-\(r\) idempotent with specified image \(W\) is the projection onto \(W\) along a complementary \((n-r)\)-plane. Such complements are graphs of linear maps from one fixed complement to \(W\), so there are exactly \(q^{r(n-r)}\) projections for each \(W\). In the column-monomial regime, if column \(j\) is nonzero and equals \(aX_i\), idempotence forces the column indexed by \(i\) to equal \(X_i\). Hence every occupied coordinate row is a fixed root of coefficient \(1\). After choosing \(r\) root indices, each of the other \(n-r\) columns independently either vanishes or maps with a nonzero scalar into one of those \(r\) roots, giving \((1+r(q-1))^{n-r}\) possibilities. These possibilities are exhaustive and disjoint. This proves \(R_{n,d,q}\), the image classification, and every specified-image multiplicity.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone script `verify_graded_retractions.py` implements exact arithmetic for \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_5\). It constructs the truncated polynomial quotient multiplication, tests the generator \(d\)-th-power relations directly for every possible degree-one column, exhaustively constructs all valid degree-one matrices in 18 test cases, and separately tests matrix idempotence, rank, and both complete polynomials. The returned line is `ALL_EXACT_CHECKS_PASSED 18`. These finite checks test the formulas but are not substituted for the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The squarefree-monomial basis and the complete-intersection family itself are standard. Phuong and Tran, arXiv:2211.13548v1 (November 24, 2022), examine the same quadratic member and broader monomial complete intersections, but study multiplication-map ranks and the strong Lefschetz property, not endomorphism-monoid retractions. Díaz, Liendo, Manzano-Flores, and Regeta, arXiv:2408.02197v1 (August 5, 2024), investigate full automorphism groups of zero-dimensional monomial algebras in characteristic zero; their Example 4.25 handles the two-generator quadratic member and its non-graded automorphisms. The invertible characteristic-zero monomial behavior is therefore not claimed as a novelty. The characteristic-two squarefree specialization also has an earlier, stronger nongraded retraction classification, so its retract isomorphism types and rank support are not asserted to be new. The new claim is the complete graded all-exponent phase criterion and rank-refined census across both regimes, rather than either previously understood specialization. The result here concerns all grading-preserving endomorphisms, their noninvertible retractions, precise image fibers, and the finite-characteristic power-of-\(p\) transition.

## Limitations
No claim is made about arbitrary nongraded endomorphisms, unequal generator exponents, noncommutative truncations, or generic coefficient rings. No assertion is made that the familiar Gaussian-binomial or monomial-matrix ingredients are new. An equivalent reformulation could exist in older work on graded endomorphism semigroups; the cited literature comparisons and database search do not constitute a proof of absolute priority. Independent external mathematical audit has not been performed.
