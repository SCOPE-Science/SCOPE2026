# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The degree-one matrix determines a unique graded endomorphism exactly when each column's linear form has d-th power zero. Frobenius gives no restrictions for d=p^s. For d=p^s u with p not dividing u and u>1, two nonzero coefficients give the nonzero coefficient binom(d,p^s) times their respective powers on a surviving two-variable monomial. This proves the monoid dichotomy, not just a claim about invertible matrices. Each image is generated by its degree-one image: a coordinate subspace in the monomial regime, and equivalent to one by an invertible linear substitution in the Frobenius regime. Thus image dimension is d^r including the ranks 0 and n. For E, count surjections onto a fixed r-plane, or use inclusion-exclusion on occupied rows. For R, idempotents with a fixed image plane correspond to complements, which are graphs of maps from a fixed complement into that plane; in the monomial case idempotence fixes each occupied column index with coefficient one, and every other column is zero or a weighted choice of an occupied row. This reconstructs every coefficient and the image fibers.

## originality

PASS

Prior work already covers the isomorphism types of graded retract images in this family, a stronger ungraded classification at d=2 in characteristic two, and adjacent-exponent invertible analogues. The supported distinction is the all-exponent graded endomorphism and retraction census across both regimes, including their noninvertibles and the exact image fibers; it is not the qualitative classification or the standard matrix counts separately.

## value

PASS

For a natural family of modular complete intersections the labeled census distinguishes two genuinely different regimes for noninvertible maps while degree one has the same dimension. It also tells exactly how many retractions realize any given embedded image. Those fiber multiplicities and the common all-exponent framework answer questions not settled by knowing only image isomorphism types, invertible symmetries, or the matrix enumeration in one regime.
