# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The idempotent equation is exactly \(x=Mx^{[2]}\) with the stated index convention; a normal basis converts it into a cyclic linear system with initial vector in \(\ker(M^f-I)\). The Fitting decomposition gives the precise geometric count and least splitting field, and Frobenius orbit counting yields the zeta product. Every essential quantifier is explicit. A standalone checker agrees with the rank formula on all 530 binary matrices in dimensions one through three over three small fields and on the complete five-cycle/Petersen examples. These experiments are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty. Giacomelli's arXiv:2605.12672v1 defines the graph algebras and discusses the named examples but does not supply their extension-field idempotent zeta functions in the relevant full-text sections inspected. Narkuziyev's arXiv:2005.05793 and arXiv:1803.11415 address permutation-defined subfamilies and some idempotents, not the matrix-rank transfer theorem for all binary structures. Earlier explicit cubelike Cayley evolution-algebra formulas cover the special binary matrix identities \(M^2=0\) or \(M^2=I\); complete-graph formulas handle a second special family. The present formula indeed subsumes their characteristic-two point counts, but neither prior statement implies the arbitrary-matrix normal-basis law, exact splitting period, and zeta function. Equivalent linearized-polynomial or finite-dynamical-system formulations could be unindexed; a later survey of idempotents was available only by abstract and remains a comparison risk.

## Value
PASS. Idempotents are a natural structural invariant of evolution algebras. This result gives a complete field-extension arithmetic distribution for every binary graph, the precise first field where all geometric idempotents appear, and an explicitly computable rational zeta function; neither finite enumeration nor an arbitrary slice is needed. Distinct graphs supply genuinely different Frobenius-period profiles, illustrated by the five-cycle and Petersen examples from the motivating paper.
