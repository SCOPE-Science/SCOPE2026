# Frobenius-visible coefficients determine automorphisms of equal-power truncated polynomial algebras

## Finding
For a prime power \(q=p^f\), integers \(r\ge2\) and \(d\ge2\), consider the local commutative algebra
\[
A_{r,d}=\mathbb F_q[x_1,\ldots,x_r]/(x_1^d,\ldots,x_r^d).
\]
Write \(d=mp^s\), where \(s\ge0\) and \(p\nmid m\). Its group of \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra automorphisms has the exact order
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Aut}(A_{r,d})|=
\begin{cases}
|\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_q)|\,q^{r(d^r-1-r)},&m=1,\\[2pt]
r!(q-1)^r q^{r(d^r-1-m^{r-1})},&m\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
Here and below the box is merely typographical; all counts refer to actual algebra automorphisms, not polynomial maps modulo pointwise equivalence. The leading-coefficient homomorphism on the cotangent space \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) has image the full general linear group when \(m=1\), and exactly the monomial matrix group \((\mathbb F_q^\times)^r\rtimes S_r\) when \(m\ge2\).

More precisely, in the second case fix a permutation \(\sigma\) and nonzero coefficients \(a_i\). The possible images \(f_i\) of the generators are exactly the polynomials whose linear terms are \(a_i x_{\sigma(i)}\) and for which the coefficient of every degree-at-least-two monomial \(x^\alpha\) vanishes whenever
\[
\alpha_{\sigma(i)}=0\quad\text{and}\quad0\le\alpha_j<m\ \text{for every }j.
\]
Every other higher coefficient is independent and arbitrary. Thus the \(p\)-free factor \(m\), rather than just the predicate \(p\mid d\), controls the nonlinear automorphism fibers.

As reduced varieties over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_q\), the automorphism parameter spaces are respectively
\[
\operatorname{GL}_r\times\mathbb A^{r(d^r-1-r)}\quad(m=1),
\]
\[
\coprod_{\sigma\in S_r}(\mathbb G_m)^r\times
\mathbb A^{r(d^r-1-m^{r-1})}\quad(m\ge2).
\]
In particular, the number of geometric connected components is one for a pure \(p\)-power exponent and \(r!\) otherwise; the reduced dimensions are \(r(d^r-1)\) and \(r(d^r-m^{r-1})\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The field in the counting statements is \(\mathbb F_q\), with characteristic \(p>0\). The normal forms hold over any field of characteristic \(p\), but the point-count statements require \(q\) to be a prime power. The geometric assertions refer to the reduced underlying automorphism parameter variety, **not** to possible nonreduced group-scheme structure. Only equal exponents \(d\) in each variable are covered, and every automorphism fixes the base field elementwise.

## Proof
**1. Invertible cotangent action is enough.** The residue field of \(A_{r,d}\) is \(\mathbb F_q\), and its unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) is generated by the coordinate classes. A proposed tuple \((f_1,\ldots,f_r)\subseteq\mathfrak m\) defines an endomorphism precisely when every \(f_i^d=0\). If the induced matrix on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is invertible, the \(f_i\) generate \(\mathfrak m\) by Nakayama's lemma. The map is then surjective and, as an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space, is bijective. The converse is immediate.

**2. A prime-to-characteristic rigidity lemma.** Let \(R=B[t]/(t^m)\), where \(B\) is a finite-dimensional commutative local \(k\)-algebra and \(m\ge2\) is invertible in \(k\). Suppose \(g=b+t u\), where \(b\in\operatorname{rad}(B)\) and \(u\) has invertible constant coefficient \(u_0\in B\). The coefficient of \(t^{m-1}\) in \(g^m\) is
\[
b\bigl(m u_0^{m-1}+b C\bigr)
\]
for a suitable \(C\in B\): contributions with exactly one \(b\) have coefficient \(m b u_0^{m-1}\), and every remaining contribution contains \(b^2\). The parenthesized factor is a unit, because \(b\) is nilpotent. Therefore \(g^m=0\) forces \(b=0\). Conversely, \(t\mid g\) implies \(g^m=0\). Thus an \(m\)-nilpotent element whose linear term is a nonzero multiple of \(t\) and has no other linear term is divisible by \(t\).

**3. The pure \(p\)-power case.** If \(m=1\), then \(d=p^s\). For any \(f\in\mathfrak m\), the Frobenius identity gives \(f^d=\sum_{\alpha\ne0}c_\alpha^d x^{d\alpha}=0\), since each positive multi-index has a coordinate of exponent at least \(d\) after taking the power. Every invertible cotangent matrix therefore extends, and every one of the \(r(d^r-1-r)\) higher coefficients is unrestricted. This gives the first normal form, order formula, and reduced parameter-space description.

**4. Cotangent rigidity when \(m\ge2\).** Let \(f\in\mathfrak m\) have linear part \(\ell=\sum_j a_jx_j\) and satisfy \(f^d=0\). The homogeneous degree-\(d\) part of \(f^d\) equals \(\ell^d\). If two coefficients \(a_i,a_j\) are nonzero, then the admissible monomial \(x_i^{d-p^s}x_j^{p^s}\) has coefficient
\[
\binom d{p^s}a_i^{d-p^s}a_j^{p^s}\ne0.
\]
Indeed \(0<p^s<d\), and the binomial coefficient is congruent to \(m\) modulo \(p\) by the elementary binomial/Frobenius expansion (equivalently Lucas's congruence). No other term contributes to this coefficient. This contradicts \(f^d=0\). Consequently every nonzero cotangent row has a single nonzero entry; invertibility makes the matrix monomial. Conversely, every monomial matrix is realized by linear substitutions.

**5. Exactly which nonlinear coefficients survive.** Fix the nonzero linear term of \(f\) to be \(a x_j\). Let \(y_i=x_i^{p^s}\). In characteristic \(p\), the subalgebra generated by the \(y_i\) is canonically isomorphic to \(k[y_1,\ldots,y_r]/(y_i^m)\). Taking the \(p^s\)-th power of \(f\) retains precisely the monomials \(c_\alpha^{p^s}y^\alpha\) for which all \(\alpha_i<m\); every other monomial dies. The surviving polynomial has unique nonzero linear term \(a^{p^s}y_j\). Since
\[
f^d=\bigl(f^{p^s}\bigr)^m,
\]
the lemma in step 2 says that this power vanishes if and only if \(f^{p^s}\) is divisible by \(y_j\). Field Frobenius is injective, so this is equivalent to the claimed vanishing of coefficients with \(\alpha_j=0\) and all \(\alpha_i<m\). There are exactly \(m^{r-1}-r\) forbidden higher monomials: from the \(m^{r-1}\) monomials with \(\alpha_j=0\) and all exponents below \(m\), remove the constant and the \(r-1\) other linear monomials. Starting with \(d^r-1-r\) higher monomials leaves
\[
d^r-1-m^{r-1}
\]
free coefficients per generator. For fixed permutation and \(r\) nonzero leading coefficients, these are independent across the generator images. Multiplication by \(r!(q-1)^r\) proves the second formula.

**6. Reduced geometry.** The normal-form restrictions describe the underlying sets of automorphisms over every algebraically closed extension. In the pure-power case the set is an open \(\operatorname{GL}_r\) linear-parameter locus times affine higher-coefficient space. In the other case the monomial cotangent locus splits into disjoint permutation charts, each with a torus of leading coefficients and an affine space of independent allowed higher coefficients. Taking the reduced induced structure gives the displayed geometric isomorphisms. Connectedness and dimension follow immediately. We make no inference about nilpotents in the unreduced parameter scheme.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` independently represents the truncated polynomial basis by exponent tuples, multiplies polynomials with exact modular arithmetic, and checks each candidate \(d\)-th power. For \((r,d,q)=(2,2,2),(2,2,3),(2,3,2),(2,3,3),(3,2,2)\), it exhausts **every possible image of each generator** and combines admissible rows over all invertible cotangent matrices. The exact automorphism counts are respectively \(24,72,2048,25509168,688128\). Additionally, deterministic sparse substitutions check direct power multiplication against the coefficient test at \((r,d,p)=(3,6,3),(2,6,3),(3,4,2),(2,6,2)\). The saved replay prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are consistency evidence, not the proof of the universal assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Satya Deo and Jitendra Kumar Maitra, *Automorphism groups of truncated polynomial algebras* (1997), study **graded** automorphisms over characteristic-zero fields, including a wreath-product theorem for tensor products of truncated one-generator polynomial algebras. It provides a strong antecedent for the monomial leading action in characteristic zero, but it does not provide ungraded positive-characteristic automorphism fibers or the present \(p\)-free-part enumeration. Its seven-page text was inspected.

Díaz, Liendo, Manzano-Flores and Regeta (arXiv:2408.02197, 2024) give a broad characteristic-zero theory for all zero-dimensional monomial algebras. The characteristic-zero specialization is therefore **not** claimed to be independently novel. Their positive-characteristic counterpart is not provided by the inspected abstract. The complete paper was not available in the research access path, a residual literature risk explicitly retained here.

Angeltveit, Gerhardt, Hill and Lindenstrauss (arXiv:1206.0247, first submitted 1 June 2012) work with the same family of multi-truncated commutative polynomial rings and analyze their algebraic \(K\)-theory via Witt vectors, not their automorphism enumeration; the introduction and stated theorems were inspected. A published study of truncated **coordinate-cross** algebras instead imposes all cross products \(x_ix_j=0\) for distinct variables, which is not the present tensor-product algebra and does not imply its Frobenius-visible coefficient conditions. Novelty is asserted only for the stated modular normal forms, complete counts, and component transitions, not for the standard finite-local Nakayama lemma or characteristic-zero graded wreath-product results.

## Limitations
The algebra must have equal truncation exponent on every variable. The geometric statements concern reduced automorphism varieties and do not establish smoothness or reducedness of the full automorphism group scheme. No analogous exact formula is claimed for unequal exponents or other monomial quotients. An older specialized positive-characteristic automorphism classification could imply these results; a search of records and the identified direct full text did not locate one. Independent auditing has not been performed.
