# Frozen and mobile minimum Roman functions of the Desargues graph

## Finding
Let \(D=P(10,3)\) be the Desargues graph. Its minimum Roman dominating functions comprise exactly \(340\) assignments of weight \(12\). Define the graph \(\mathcal R_R(D)\) on these assignments using the Roman-function changes \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) defined below. Then
\[
\mathcal R_R(D)\cong5H_{48}\sqcup100K_1,
\]
where \(H_{48}\) is a connected \(48\)-vertex, \(108\)-edge graph with radius \(6\), diameter \(8\), degree multiset \(4^{42}8^6\), and eccentricity multiset \(6^6 7^{36}8^6\). The graph has exactly \(540\) edges: \(180\) of type \(P_1\), none of type \(P_2\), and \(360\) of type \(P_3\). Its five nontrivial components are mutually isomorphic, cyclically permuted by a rotation of \(D\). Among its \(340\) configurations, \(100\) cannot move at all.

The three possible label-count profiles \((\#1,\#2)\) are \((0,6)\), \((2,5)\), \((4,4)\), occurring \(130,180,30\) times, respectively. Each mobile component contains \(18,24,6\) assignments of these respective profiles; the isolated assignments have profiles \((0,6)\) forty times and \((2,5)\) sixty times. In each mobile component, the six \((0,6)\) vertices of degree \(8\) have eccentricity \(6\); the six \((4,4)\) vertices have eccentricity \(8\); the other \(36\) have eccentricity \(7\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(D\) are \(u_i,v_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z\). Its edges are \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), and \(v_iv_{i+3}\) for every \(i\). A Roman dominating function (RDF) is a labeling \(f:V(D)\to\{0,1,2\}\) in which every vertex labeled zero has a neighbor labeled two. Its weight is \(\sum_{x\in V(D)}f(x)\), and \(\gamma_R(D)\) is the minimum weight. A vertex of \(\mathcal R_R(D)\) is an RDF of weight \(\gamma_R(D)\).

Two *distinct minimum* RDFs are adjacent when one can be obtained from the other by exactly one of the following elementary operations (in either direction), with the resulting labeling still an RDF of minimum weight: \(P_1\), interchange a \(2\) and a \(0\) on two arbitrary vertices; \(P_2\), interchange a \(2\) and a \(1\) on two arbitrary vertices; \(P_3\), replace labels \(1,1\) at the two ends of a graph edge by labels \(2,0\) (or reverse the replacement). These are the operations of the Roman-function adjacency framework of Samodivkin (2017), excluding his triangle-dependent \(P_4\) because \(D\) is bipartite. Every actually available \(P_1\) change in the present graph has adjacent underlying vertices, so restricting \(P_1\) to adjacent vertices would leave the theorem unchanged. Both the underlying graph and the permitted transformations are essential to the scope.

## Proof
**Exact minimum-function encoding.** For \(T\subseteq V(D)\), let \(N[T]\) denote its closed neighborhood. In a minimum RDF, the set \(T=f^{-1}(2)\) determines every other label: each vertex outside \(N[T]\) must be labeled \(1\), and each vertex of \(N[T]\setminus T\) must be labeled \(0\). Indeed, the first conclusion follows from the RDF condition; any \(1\) on the second set could be lowered to \(0\) while preserving Roman domination, contradicting minimum weight. Conversely the indicated labeling is Roman dominating. Consequently, its minimum possible weight at fixed \(T\) is
\[
w(T)=2|T|+|V(D)\setminus N[T]|,
\quad \gamma_R(D)=\min_{T\subseteq V(D)}w(T).
\]
This encoding establishes an exhaustive finite proof procedure: there are exactly \(2^{20}=1,048,576\) subsets \(T\), each represented by a twenty-bit word. `checks/verify_cyclic.py` computes \(N[T]\) recursively by removing the lowest set bit, evaluates \(w(T)\) for *every* word, and retains *every* equality case. It yields minimum \(12\) and exactly \(340\) equality cases. The independent `checks/verify_incidence.py` constructs the same graph as incidence between the two- and three-element subsets of a five-element set, verifies an explicit twenty-vertex graph isomorphism to the \(u/v\) model, and enumerates independently all sets \(T\) of size at most \(6\). This second enumeration is exhaustive for minima because any \(T\) with at least \(7\) vertices incurs weight at least \(14\), whereas weight \(12\) is attained. It yields the same minimum and all \(340\) configurations.

**Exact reconfiguration relation.** Encode an RDF by its ordered length-twenty string of labels in the order \(u_0,\ldots,u_9,v_0,\ldots,v_9\). A pair of distinct encoded optimum functions is an edge exactly when it satisfies one of the three changes above. The first program generates all such moves by scanning all ordered vertex pairs at every optimum function, retaining only endpoints in the enumerated set. The second program independently checks all unordered pairs of the \(340\) optimum functions, classifies their two-coordinate differences according to \(P_1,P_2,P_3\), and independently identifies all valid transitions. The complete canonical sorted output is included as `data/states_edges.json` with each edge annotated by move type; each independent program reconstructs and exactly checks the same certificate. The resulting \(540\) distinct unordered edges have type-counts \(180,0,360\), respectively. Thus the graph under discussion, and no larger graph with an unstated transformation rule, has been exhaustively reconstructed.

**Components, distances, and symmetry.** Exhaustive breadth-first searches on that complete finite adjacency graph give \(100\) singleton components and exactly five components with \(48\) vertices each. Every mobile component has \(108\) edges, degree multiset \(4^{42}8^6\), and eccentricity multiset \(6^6 7^{36}8^6\); in particular its radius is \(6\) and its diameter is \(8\). The independent pairwise implementation reaches the same component and distance census. For every \(i\), simultaneously sending \(u_i\mapsto u_{i+1}\) and \(v_i\mapsto v_{i+1}\) is an automorphism of \(D\) and therefore induces one of \(\mathcal R_R(D)\). The first verifier checks its induced permutation of the five mobile components is one five-cycle (not merely that their sizes agree), certifying their mutual isomorphism. Its classification of all profile, degree and eccentricity counts yields the remaining stated assertions. Every finite search runs through its full stated range and terminates with checked assertions; a timeout or nonmatching log would not establish the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhang, Shao and Xu (2014), Theorem 3, give \(\gamma_R(P(10,3))=12\) as a consequence of a result covering generalized Petersen graphs \(P(n,3)\). Thus the weight \(12\) is *not* claimed as new. Samodivkin (2017), Section 4, introduces Roman-function reconfiguration moves and investigates families such as paths and cycles. Neither inspected article supplies the complete count of optimum functions or any edge/component/radius/diameter classification for the Desargues graph under this move relation. The novel claim is precisely the fully typed optimum-configuration graph and its decomposition, distances, and symmetry.

## Limitations
The classification is finite, applies only to \(D=P(10,3)\) and the specified \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) moves, and does not assert a formula for other generalized Petersen graphs or other Roman-reconfiguration rules. The five copies of \(H_{48}\) are defined by their isomorphic induced components and their exact certified state-edge descriptions; no claim of a shorter standard named-graph representation is made. Exhaustive computation here is a finite proof, not evidence of a general pattern. A mathematical originality search cannot rule out all unindexed or otherwise inaccessible equivalent prior classifications. Independent external scientific audit has not been performed.
