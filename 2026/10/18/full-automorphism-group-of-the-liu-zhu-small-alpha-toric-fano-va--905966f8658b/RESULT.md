# Full automorphism group of the Liu–Zhu small-alpha toric Fano varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 2\), put \(\ell=2n-1\) and
\[
N_n=\{(d_0,\ldots,d_n)\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}:\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i=0\}.
\]
Let \(Q_n\subset N_n\otimes\mathbb R\) be the rational lattice polytope
\[
Q_n=\left\{d:\textstyle\sum_i d_i=0,\quad -\ell\le d_i\le 2\ \ (0\le i\le n)\right\},
\]
and let \(X_n\) be the complete complex toric variety of the fan consisting of cones over the faces of \(Q_n\). These are the small-alpha toric Fano varieties introduced by Liu and Zhu.

**Theorem.** The full algebraic automorphism group is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(X_n)\cong T_{N_n}\rtimes\mathfrak S_{n+1}},
\]
where \(T_{N_n}=N_n\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C^*\cong(\mathbb C^*)^n\) and \(\mathfrak S_{n+1}\) permutes the \(n+1\) coordinates of \(N_n\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}^{0}(X_n)=T_{N_n},\qquad
\pi_0\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(X_n)\cong\mathfrak S_{n+1}.}
\]
The fan has **no Demazure roots**; consequently no nonzero normalized additive-group action occurs, the automorphism group has dimension \(n\), and it has exactly \((n+1)!\) connected components. This includes \(n=2\), where the two types of maximal cones are distinguished by multiplicities \(3\) and \(8\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\); \(n\) is any integer at least two. The group \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(X_n)\) comprises *all* algebraic automorphisms of the abstract complete normal toric variety, not only torus-equivariant automorphisms. The torus action is effective and has cocharacter lattice \(N_n\). The fan is the face fan, not the normal fan, of \(Q_n\).

The vertices of \(Q_n\) are the \(n(n+1)\) primitive vectors \(v_{p,q}\), indexed by ordered pairs \(p\ne q\) in \(\{0,\ldots,n\}\), given by
\[
(v_{p,q})_p=-(2n-1),\quad (v_{p,q})_q=1,\quad
(v_{p,q})_i=2\quad(i\notin\{p,q\}).
\]
Indeed, a vertex of this hyperplane section of a box has all but at most one coordinate at a box endpoint; the only feasible endpoint pattern has one lower endpoint, \(n-1\) upper endpoints, and remaining coordinate \(1\). The displayed vectors have a coordinate \(1\), hence are primitive in \(N_n\). The origin lies strictly inside \(Q_n\).

## Proof
**Step 1: no Demazure roots.** The character lattice is \(M_n=\mathbb Z^{n+1}/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). For a character represented by \(a=(a_0,\ldots,a_n)\), write \(S=\sum_i a_i\). Its pairing with a primitive ray generator is
\[
f_{p,q}=\langle a,v_{p,q}\rangle=2S-(2n+1)a_p-a_q.
\]
By symmetry of the ordered pairs, the sum of all primitive rays is zero:
\[
\sum_{p\ne q}v_{p,q}=0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad\sum_{p\ne q}f_{p,q}=0.
\]
A Demazure root would be a character \(a\) with one pairing equal to \(-1\) and all the other pairings nonnegative integers. The displayed sum would force exactly one other pairing to equal \(1\) and every remaining pairing to vanish. Thus such a character would vanish on all but two of the \(n(n+1)\) rays.

When \(n\ge3\), group the pairings in rows indexed by \(p\). At most two rows can contain a nonzero pairing, so there exist distinct indices \(p,r\) for which \(f_{p,q}=0\) for all \(q\ne p\) and \(f_{r,q}=0\) for all \(q\ne r\). The first row equation says that \(a_q\) is independent of \(q\ne p\); the second says that \(a_q\) is independent of \(q\ne r\). The two complements intersect, and their union is the full index set. Hence all \(a_i\) are equal and all \(f_{p,q}\) are zero, a contradiction.

When \(n=2\), the polygon \(Q_2\) contains the origin in its interior and has six distinct ray directions. A line through the origin can contain at most two of its vertices. Consequently any four ray generators span \(N_2\otimes\mathbb Q\); a nonzero functional cannot vanish on four of the six rays. This again contradicts the necessary two-nonzero-pairing condition. Therefore the Demazure root set is empty for every \(n\ge2\).

For any complete complex toric variety, its connected automorphism group is generated by its dense torus and the root subgroups associated with the Demazure roots. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X_n)=T_{N_n}.
\]
This invocation is valid for the nonsimplicial fans that occur in higher dimensions.

**Step 2: the lattice automorphism group of the fan.** An automorphism of the fan permutes its primitive rays, so it preserves their convex hull \(Q_n\) and permutes the facets of \(Q_n\). Each coordinate lower face \(F_i^-=\{d_i=-\ell\}\) contains \(n\) vertices (the vectors \(v_{i,q}\)); each upper face \(F_i^+=\{d_i=2\}\) contains \(n(n-1)\) vertices (the vectors \(v_{p,q}\) with \(p,q\ne i\)). These are precisely all \(2(n+1)\) facets: each defining equality has a relative-interior point, and the box section is full dimensional.

For \(n\ge3\), the two facet types have different vertex counts, so every fan automorphism permutes the \(n+1\) lower facets among themselves. For \(n=2\), both types have two vertices, but in the lattice coordinates \((d_0,d_1)\) the two rays of \(F_2^-\) are \((1,2),(2,1)\) and have determinant of absolute value \(3\), whereas the rays of \(F_2^+\) are \((-3,1),(1,-3)\) and have determinant of absolute value \(8\). The same holds by coordinate symmetry for the other facets. A lattice automorphism preserves this cone index, so lower facets cannot be exchanged with upper facets when \(n=2\) either.

Let \(A\in\operatorname{GL}(N_n)\) preserve the fan. We have proved that \(A\) permutes the lower facets. Suppose it sends \(F_i^-\) to \(F_{\sigma(i)}^-\). Because \(A(Q_n)=Q_n\), the linear forms \(d_{\sigma(i)}\circ A\) and \(d_i\) define the same affine supporting hyperplane with the same nonzero constant \(-\ell\). They therefore coincide. This holds for every \(i\), so \(A\) is exactly the coordinate permutation \(\sigma\). Conversely, every coordinate permutation preserves the lattice and the box section. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(N_n,\Sigma_n)=\mathfrak S_{n+1}.
\]

**Step 3: all automorphisms.** Since \(\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X_n)=T_{N_n}\), this torus is normal in the full automorphism group. Therefore every automorphism normalizes it. Any automorphism normalizing the dense torus acts on that torus as a translation followed by a torus-group automorphism; extension across the toric boundary is equivalent to preservation of the fan. This gives the split exact sequence
\[
1\longrightarrow T_{N_n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X_n)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(N_n,\Sigma_n)\longrightarrow1,
\]
with splitting given by toric coordinate permutations. Combining the preceding steps proves the theorem.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 script `artifacts/verify.py` reconstructs the primitive ray list, checks that all ray sums vanish, checks the vertex counts of the two facet types, and performs exact modular-linear-algebra rank tests after deleting every pair of rays for \(2\le n\le8\). The rank test gives an additional finite certificate that no nonzero linear functional can have support on only two of the rays. For the exceptional surface case it also checks the determinant indices \(3\) and \(8\). Running the script prints `VERIFY_OK`.

The proof of root-freeness, the facet classification, and the automorphism group is uniform for *all* integers \(n\ge2\). The finite computations corroborate but do not establish that universal assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Liu and Zhu introduce these K-polystable toric \(\mathbb Q\)-Fano varieties and establish their polar-polytope construction and \(\alpha\)-invariant. A published computation of their two types of *local affine divisor class groups* distinguishes the fixed-point singularities, but it neither determines the full automorphism group nor excludes Demazure roots. The latter distinction requires the global primitive-ray vanishing argument in this result.

The general toric automorphism theorem, developed by Demazure and treated for complete toric varieties by Sancho, Moreno and Sancho, expresses the connected group using roots and the finite quotient using fan symmetries. Kaloghiros and Petracci provide a sufficient root-free criterion in their Proposition 2.8. Those general results are tools rather than evaluations for this specific infinite family. This theorem establishes both missing family-specific parts: empty root set and the *full* hidden coordinate-permutation group, not merely the cyclic symmetry evident in an original presentation.

## Limitations
The result is about algebraic automorphisms over \(\mathbb C\); it does not classify automorphisms after reduction to arbitrary positive characteristic or nonalgebraic homeomorphisms. It also makes no claim about deformations, K-moduli components, or singularity resolutions. The original Liu–Zhu manuscript was identifiable and its construction confirmed through an accessible published full mathematical account, but the originating paper's full text was not obtainable for complete priority comparison in this investigation. An unindexed equivalent computation remains possible. No independent priority certification is asserted.
