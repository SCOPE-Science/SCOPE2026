# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. A dominating set in the clique prism either meets both cliques or is one complete layer. For sets meeting both cliques, every selected vertex has its layer's number of omissions plus, possibly, one omitted matching neighbour. This gives the two exact inequalities in the theorem. A unique four-type column encoding supplies the multinomial polynomial without double counting. The lower bound for the selected set's size and the equality rigidity establish all minimizer cases and their multiplicities. The proof checks \(k=0\), \(k=n\), and the \(n=2\) exception separately. Independent direct-neighbourhood enumeration agrees for all tested orders 2 through 8 and all capacities.

## Originality

PASS, provisionally. Tepeh's initial 2026 preprint (arXiv:2606.22428), Section 4.1, gives a special square-rook minimum result and general bounds; it has no matching two-row all-cardinality polynomial in the inspected full text. The earlier recorded *Exact k-limited domination of rectangular rook graphs* gives the numerical minimum for all rectangles and a minimizer classification when the capacity is below both dimensions. It thus covers the present minimum-size numerical corollary and low-capacity special cases; neither of those is asserted original. It does not state the complete polynomial or cover the minimizer count in the nontrivial two-row range \(2\le k<n\). Božović et al.'s foundational full article concerns general bounds and graph families rather than this complete enumerator. Searches under limited domination, \((0,k)\)-pitchfork domination, clique prism and two-row rook graph found no stronger covering all-set result. Residual risks include unindexed enumerators and unread later full-text revisions.

## Value

PASS. The graph family is the smallest nontrivial family of clique-by-clique Cartesian products beyond a complete graph, and it is directly motivated by recent complexity and graph-product studies of capacity-constrained domination. A complete labelled-set classification and degree-by-degree generating polynomial give strictly finer information than the already known optimal value, particularly exact minimizer multiplicities across the capacity transition \(2k=n\). The new statement is an unrestricted infinite-family counting theorem, not a tiny computational slice or a recalculation of the known numerical parameter.

## Limitations

Literature priority remains provisional because not every revision and every synonym-indexed paper was materially accessible. Finite verification is supplementary to, not a substitute for, the full proof. No claim about arbitrary rook graphs is made.
