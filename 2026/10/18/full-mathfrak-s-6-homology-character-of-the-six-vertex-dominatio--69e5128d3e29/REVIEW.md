# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The face predicate is equivalent to absence of a dominating vertex pair. The finite package enumerates all \(4503\) faces, builds the signed invariant chain complexes for every conjugacy class, confirms their boundary squares vanish, checks ranks over two distinct primes, reconstructs characters by cyclic averaging, and verifies all eleven signed Lefschetz identities and the Specht orthogonality relations. The inference from modular invariants to rational invariants is justified by the previously established torsion-free integral homology and localization at primes not dividing the cyclic group order. The prior integral sphere-wedge theorem has been explicitly identified as an input rather than reproved.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected evidence. González and Hoekstra-Mendoza supply the original graph-complex context without a homology representation; the published \(D_{6,3}\) wedge classification supplies dimensions and freeness, not vertex-relabeling characters. The related stable Specht theorem treats \(D_{n,n-2}\), and the adjacent seven-vertex computation treats \(D_{7,4}\) nonequivariantly. Targeted mathematical database searches for the eleven-class graded characters, the two Specht decompositions, and invariant quotients found no dominating result. The principal residual risk is an unindexed equivalent equivariant computation.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The complete graded representation distinguishes nine different non-linear irreducible symmetry types in the first singled-out mixed-dimensional example of bounded-domination graph complexes. It gives information inaccessible from wedge multiplicities, including the absence of both trivial and sign summands and rational acyclicity of the two natural global quotients. The result is a motivated classification of an existing mathematically distinguished object, not a generic negative test.

## Scope and limitations
The result is finite and rational. It neither recomputes the known nonequivariant homotopy type as a novelty nor asserts an integral group-ring module classification or a uniform all-parameter formula. The exactness of the finite invariant-rank step relies on the cited integral freeness theorem; no independent audit of this new character result has occurred.
