# Full \(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_3)\) monodromy in a nowhere-ordinary genus-two pencil

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(7\), and set \(B=\mathbb A^1_k\setminus\{t:t^4=1\}\). For every geometric parameter \(t\in B\), let \(C_t\) be the smooth projective completion of
\[y^2=x^5+x+t.\]
This family has genus two and is **nowhere ordinary**. Its Jacobian has \(7\)-rank \(1\) and \(a\)-number \(1\) when \(t\ne0\), and is superspecial at the smooth fiber \(t=0\). The geometric monodromy on the rank-four étale \(3\)-torsion local system \(\operatorname{Jac}(C/B)[3]\) is the **full** group
\[\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_3),\qquad |\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_3)|=51840.\]
The same image occurs over \(B\setminus\{0\}\), where every fiber has \(7\)-rank exactly one. In particular, this is a nonisotrivial pencil whose entire smooth locus is nonordinary but whose prime-to-characteristic monodromy is maximal at level three.

## Assumptions and scope
All geometric assertions are over \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_7\), and all fundamental groups and monodromy groups are geometric. The symplectic pairing is the Weil pairing of the principally polarized genus-two Jacobian, identified with \(\mathbb F_3\). The statement concerns level-three torsion; no claim about the full \(3\)-adic monodromy is made. The smooth base excludes the four distinct roots of \(t^4=1\), but does not exclude \(t=0\).

## Proof
**1. Smoothness and the Frobenius stratum.** For the polynomial \(f=x^5+x+t\), the discriminant over \(\mathbb Z\) is \(256+3125t^4\), which reduces modulo seven to \(3(t^4-1)\). Therefore the specified fibers have squarefree degree-five branch polynomial and smooth projective genus two. The degree-five hyperelliptic Cartier–Manin coefficient convention gives \(M_{ij}=[x^{7i-j}]f^{3}\), for \(i,j\in\{1,2\}\); direct expansion yields
\[M=\begin{pmatrix}6t&3t^2\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{in characteristic }7.\]
Its determinant always vanishes. When \(t\ne0\) its rank is one, and the semilinear iterate has nonzero upper-left entry \((6t)(6t)^7\), so the stable Hasse–Witt rank, or \(7\)-rank, is one. The Cartier-kernel dimension (the \(a\)-number) is one. At \(t=0\), the matrix vanishes, giving \(a=2\), which for a principally polarized abelian surface is the superspecial case. This is a smooth fiber because its discriminant is \(3\). The Newton slopes when \(t\ne0\) are \(0,\frac12,\frac12,1\); at \(t=0\) they are all \(\frac12\). The smooth variation with different \(7\)-ranks also proves that the family is nonisotrivial, by separatedness of the moduli of smooth curves.

**2. Four simple degenerations and an \(A_4\) vanishing-cycle basis.** In the total polynomial map \((x,y)\mapsto y^2-x^5-x\), critical points obey \(y=0\) and \(5x^4+1=0\); these are four distinct nonzero points. Their critical values are distinct, and each fiber has exactly one ordinary double point. The degree-five polynomial gives, in characteristic zero, a morsification of the plane curve singularity \(y^2=x^5\), of type \(A_4\). A distinguished system of four vanishing cycles \(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_4\) is a basis of \(H_1(C_t,\mathbb Z)\) with alternating pairing \(\langle\delta_i,\delta_{i+1}\rangle=1\), \(\langle\delta_{i+1},\delta_i\rangle=-1\), and all other pairings zero. Its determinant is one. Picard–Lefschetz monodromy about the four simple critical values consists of the four transvections \(T_i(v)=v+\langle v,\delta_i\rangle\delta_i\). Changing signs or orientations only replaces a generator with its inverse or conjugate and does not change the generated group.

**3. Exact level-three group.** Reduce the basis and its pairing modulo three. Write \(\Omega\) for the four-by-four alternating matrix whose only nonzero entries are \(\Omega_{i,i+1}=1\), \(\Omega_{i+1,i}=-1\). The transvection matrices are \(T_i=I+e_i(\Omega e_i)^\mathsf T\). Starting at the identity and repeatedly multiplying on the right by these four matrices enumerates exactly \(51840\) distinct four-by-four matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\). The accompanying standalone checker implements this closure algorithm, checks symplectic preservation, and independently evaluates the order formula \(3^4(3^2-1)(3^4-1)=51840\). Thus the image in characteristic zero is exactly \(\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_3)\), rather than merely a finite-index subgroup in an integral symplectic group.

**4. Transport to characteristic seven.** Work with the integral family over the local ring \(\mathbb Z_{(7)}\) after inverting the discriminant \(256+3125t^4\). Its four discriminant points remain distinct and simple modulo seven, and together with the point at infinity form a relative disjoint marked divisor on the projective parameter line. The smooth relative Jacobian has a finite étale full symplectic level-three frame torsor over the complement. Every possible geometric monodromy element lies in \(\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_3)\), whose order \(51840\) is **prime to seven**. Its geometric Galois cover is therefore tame at the boundary. The prime-to-seven tame specialization theorem for a pointed projective line with distinct sections identifies its geometric finite prime-to-seven monodromy quotients between characteristic zero and characteristic seven. Hence the already-surjective level-three representation remains surjective over \(B\). Finally, removing the additional smooth point \(t=0\) induces a surjection on the geometric étale fundamental groups, so the level-three image over \(B\setminus\{0\}\) is still full.

## Relationship to prior work
Hui June Zhu, *Construction of generically ordinary families of hyperelliptic curves* (arXiv:2606.31783v1, June 30, 2026), proves generic ordinarity for \(p>P^+(d)\) and records \(P^+(5)=7\), discusses large (finite-index) monodromy for Morse-polynomial families, and gives a general formula for the Hasse–Witt coefficient matrix. These general tools underlie the present calculation, but Zhu does not assert the exact level-three monodromy in the exceptional characteristic-seven fiber family, nor the complete jump at the smooth superspecial parameter. Achter and Pries determine monodromy over **entire** moduli strata of curves or hyperelliptic curves, not the image of this specific one-parameter pencil. Brendle and Margalit's integral Burau representation describes the relevant braid-group symplectic action, but does not by itself identify the characteristic-seven geometric family or establish preservation of its exact level-three image across specialization. This theorem gives an explicit small-prime instance in which generic ordinarity fails while level-three geometric monodromy is maximal.

## Limitations
This is an exact assertion for characteristic seven, genus two and level three; it does not determine full \(\ell\)-adic images, the full geometric endomorphism ring, or all isomorphism classes of fibers. It relies on standard Picard–Lefschetz theory for the \(A_4\) morsification and on the prime-to-characteristic tame specialization theorem. The matrix formula itself is standard; the distinction from the closest sources is the exact image for this nonordinary pencil. General braid-image theorems may offer alternative proofs or imply portions of the statement, and an earlier equivalent explicit application cannot be excluded without an independent literature audit.
