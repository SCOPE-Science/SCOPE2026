# Full monodromy, discriminant and exceptional fibers of the dihedral Seshadri cover

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge5\), consider the finite morphism \(\phi:Y_n\to B\) constructed below. It has degree \(2n\) and connected Galois closure \(S\to B\) with group \(D_n\times D_n\), of order \(4n^2\). Its reduced branch locus consists of the six fibers \(u=2,-2,\infty\) and \(v=2,-2,\infty\).

On a geometric fiber of degree \(2n\), an inertia generator around any one of the four finite branch lines acts as \(n\) disjoint transpositions; around either line at infinity it acts as two \(n\)-cycles. The discriminant (branch divisor counted with different/discriminant multiplicities) is
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(\phi)=n\sum_{\epsilon=\pm2}\bigl([u=\epsilon]+[v=\epsilon]\bigr)+2(n-1)\bigl([u=\infty]+[v=\infty]\bigr).
\]
All six divisorial components of ramification in \(Y_n\) are irreducible with normalization \(\mathbb P^1\). Their maps to the corresponding branch lines have degree \(n\) (finite lines, ramification index \(2\)) or degree \(2\) (infinity lines, ramification index \(n\)).

The nine pairwise line crossings have the following complete reduced fiber description:

* At each of the four points \((u,v)\in\{2,-2\}^2\), the fiber consists of \((n+1)/2\) geometric points: exactly one is an \(A_1\) singularity of \(Y_n\), and the remaining \((n-1)/2\) points are smooth.
* At each of the four points with one coordinate in \(\{2,-2\}\) and the other \(\infty\), the fiber is a single smooth geometric point.
* Over \((\infty,\infty)\), there are exactly two geometric points, both singular, of cyclic quotient types \(\frac1n(1,2)\) and \(\frac1n(1,n-2)\).

The known six singularities of \(Y_n\) occur precisely at these stated crossing fibers.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Fix an odd integer \(n\ge5\) and a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\). Let \(S=\mathbb P^1_x\times\mathbb P^1_z\), and let \(G_n\cong D_n\) be the order-\(2n\) group generated by
\[
r(x,z)=(\zeta x,\zeta^2z),\qquad s(x,z)=(x^{-1},z^{-1}).
\]
The two rational expressions \(u=x^n+x^{-n}\) and \(v=z^n+z^{-n}\) extend to maps to \(\mathbb P^1\) and descend to a finite morphism \(\phi:Y_n=S/G_n\to B=\mathbb P^1_u\times\mathbb P^1_v\). This is exactly the normalized group action and pair of invariant morphisms used in Malara--Merta--Szpond--Zieliński, Section 6. The geometric fiber descriptions count reduced geometric points, not scheme-theoretic lengths. The discriminant is the divisor of the discriminant of the tame finite function-field extension, not merely its reduced support.

## Proof
**1. The Galois closure.** The map on one factor is
\[
f_n:\mathbb P^1\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1,\qquad [X:Y]\longmapsto[X^{2n}+Y^{2n}:X^nY^n].
\]
It is a degree-\(2n\) Galois map, with deck group generated by multiplication by \(\zeta\) and inversion, hence isomorphic to \(D_n\). Its branch values are \(2,-2,\infty\). At either finite branch value there are \(n\) points of index \(2\), and over infinity there are two points of index \(n\). This follows directly from \(f_n(x)-2=(x^n-1)^2/x^n\), \(f_n(x)+2=(x^n+1)^2/x^n\), and the local orders at \(0\) and \(\infty\).

Thus \(\pi=f_n\times f_n:S\to B\) is Galois with group \(H=D_n\times D_n\) and degree \(4n^2\). Write \(r_1,s_1\) and \(r_2,s_2\) for the standard generators of its two factors. Because \(n\) is odd, the map \(\alpha:D_n\to D_n\) sending \(r_1\mapsto r_2^2\), \(s_1\mapsto s_2\) is an isomorphism. The subgroup producing \(Y_n\) is its graph \(G_n=\{(g,\alpha(g)):g\in D_n\}\). Consequently \([\mathbb C(Y_n):\mathbb C(B)]=|H|/|G_n|=2n\).

The core of the graph in \(H\) is trivial: if \((g,\alpha(g))\) belongs to every conjugate, conjugation by \((h,1)\) forces \(g\in Z(D_n)\); because \(n\) is odd, \(Z(D_n)=1\). Hence \(S\to B\) is the smallest normal/Galois extension containing \(\mathbb C(Y_n)\). The faithful degree-\(2n\) permutation action on cosets of \(G_n\) is the full monodromy group \(H\), not merely an abstract group that surjects onto it.

**2. Exact branch cycles and discriminant.** Identify the coset set \(H/G_n\) with the \(2n\) elements of \(D_n\). Under this identification \((a,b)\in H\) acts by
\[
t\longmapsto b\,t\,\alpha(a)^{-1}.
\]
Left or right multiplication by a reflection of \(D_n\) is fixed-point free of order \(2\), yielding \(n\) transpositions. Left or right multiplication by a rotation of order \(n\) has exactly two orbits of length \(n\). Each of the six coordinate branch lines gives precisely one of these types. The map \(S\to Y_n\) is étale in codimension one: the only nonidentity stabilizers of \(G_n\) fix isolated points, namely the four corners for rotations and finite torus points for reflections. The six lines are therefore the entire reduced branch locus of \(\phi\). Over a general point of a finite branch line there are \(n\) ramification points of index \(2\); over a general point of an infinity line there are two ramification points of index \(n\). In characteristic zero, the discriminant order of such a separable finite extension at a generic branch divisor is \(\sum(e_i-1)f_i\), giving the asserted coefficients \(n\) and \(2(n-1)\).

**3. Irreducible ramification curves.** For \(u=2\) and \(u=-2\), the inverse image in \(S\) is a disjoint union of \(n\) vertical \(\mathbb P^1\)'s, permuted transitively by \(G_n\); a component has a reflection as its setwise stabilizer, so its image in \(Y_n\) is irreducible with normalization \(\mathbb P^1/\langle s\rangle\cong\mathbb P^1\). The map to the other target coordinate has degree \(n\). Above \(u=\infty\), the two vertical curves \(x=0,\infty\) are exchanged by the reflections; the setwise stabilizer of either is the rotation group of order \(n\), hence the image again has rational normalization and maps with degree \(2\) to the other target coordinate. The three horizontal cases are identical (multiplication by \(2\) is invertible modulo odd \(n\)). Their ramification indices are the branch-cycle orders computed above.

**4. Every crossing fiber.** A geometric fiber over an intersection of two branch lines is naturally the set of orbits of the two commuting coordinate inertia subgroups on \(H/G_n\cong D_n\). For two finite lines the involutions act by left and right multiplication by reflections. After conjugating the reflections, write the actions \(t\mapsto st\) and \(t\mapsto ts\). Their orbits have length \(2\) precisely on the centralizer \(C_{D_n}(s)=\{1,s\}\), which forms a single orbit of size \(2\); the remaining \(2n-2\) elements form \((n-1)/2\) four-element orbits. At the size-two orbit, the stabilizer in the surface quotient is a simultaneous reflection acting as \((-1,-1)\) in regular local parameters, so the completed local invariant ring is
\[
\mathbb C[[a,b]]^{\langle(a,b)\mapsto(-a,-b)\rangle}\cong\mathbb C[[A,B,C]]/(AB-C^2).
\]
The other surface points are smooth because their stabilizers are trivial. The argument does not depend on which signs \(\pm2\) occur.

At a finite-infinity crossing, a coordinate reflection and an independent coordinate rotation act transitively on the \(2n\) cosets: the rotation moves within the two rotation cosets of \(D_n\), and the reflection exchanges them. Thus there is a single geometric point. It is smooth because the only points of \(S\) above such a crossing have exactly one coordinate at \(0\) or \(\infty\), and no nontrivial element of the diagonal \(G_n\) fixes one of these points.

Over \((\infty,\infty)\), the two coordinate rotations act on \(D_n\) with exactly two orbits, of size \(n\), consisting respectively of rotations and reflections. They correspond to the two orbits of corners of \(S\), whose stabilizers are cyclic of order \(n\). Their tangent representations have weights \((1,2)\) and \((1,-2)\) modulo \(n\), giving the stated cyclic quotient types. Malara--Merta--Szpond--Zieliński independently identify these and the four \(A_1\) points as the complete singular locus of \(Y_n\). This completes the classification.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free `verify_dihedral_cover.py` implements the finite group \(D_n\), checks the automorphism \(r\mapsto r^2\), computes inertia cycle partitions and the double-inertia crossing orbits, and checks the faithful \(D_n\times D_n\) permutation image. Running `python verify_dihedral_cover.py` prints `VERIFY_OK` for \(n=5,7,9,11,13\). The all-odd-\(n\) theorem follows from the proofs above; these finitely many tests are not a proof for arbitrary \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Malara--Merta--Szpond--Zieliński (arXiv:2610.01783v1, 2026-10-01), especially Section 6, introduce exactly \(G_n\), its quotient \(Y_n\), both invariant maps, its six singularities, and the first-coordinate ruling. Their results give the geometric starting point and some local singularity information. Their text does not provide the Galois closure or full \(2n\)-sheet monodromy of the combined finite morphism, the six-branch discriminant, or the point-by-point fibers of the nine branch-line intersections.

Laface--Ugaglia (arXiv:2609.26521v2, Section 2) analyze the preceding \(n=5\) dihedral quotient and first-coordinate ruling; they do not give the combined two-coordinate covering classification. The abstract dihedral covering and inertia methods used here are standard; originality is claimed only for their explicit full-cover and crossing-stratum determination in this geometrically distinguished surface family, not for the methods.

## Limitations
The covering is considered over \(\mathbb C\) for odd \(n\ge5\) only; it is not asserted that the same subgroup description holds for even \(n\). The result does not address Seshadri constants beyond the source paper, the minimal resolution of individual branch intersections, integral homology, or new birational invariants. There is residual priority risk that a general published result about graph-subgroup quotients of product dihedral covers already implies the classification, even if no equivalent instance was found in the checked literature. The curve statements concern normalizations and generic ramification, not full singular scheme structures at branch crossings.
