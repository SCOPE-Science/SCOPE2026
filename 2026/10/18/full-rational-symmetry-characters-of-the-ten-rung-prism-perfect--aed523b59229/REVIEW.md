# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** All 125 perfect matchings and all 60,852 faces of the ten-rung prism perfect-matching complex are generated exactly, with the conflict subcomplex independently enumerated (59,050 faces). The 30,069 and 29,283 elementary collapse pairs are checked at selection; exact rational boundary reduction gives the two required rational Betti dimensions for the full complex and the one degree of the subcomplex. All 40 dihedral-and-swap symmetries are verified, and signed simplicial Lefschetz calculations recover the stated representation characters. A second direct sign algorithm agrees on nine nonidentity symmetries. The only mathematical assumptions are the stated graph and standard simplicial homology and Lefschetz definitions.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The closest directly relevant publication, Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2, Section 5.2, gives the underlying nonequivariant wedge types, but no homology action of the full prism automorphism group. The claim adds 16 class values and explicit character decompositions in the first mixed-degree case. Published and indexed searches using circular ladder, prism, tesseract, Möbius ladder, perfect-matching, equivariant, layer interchange, rotation/reflection and dihedral aliases did not identify the same complete character. A closely related published-finding corpus record classifies the *independence* complex representation, not the perfect-matching complex. A surviving risk is an unindexed representation calculation or a deduction implicit in unpublished work.

**Value — PASS.** At ten rungs the nonequivariant homology first occupies two different positive degrees. Both are rank two yet carry different one-dimensional symmetry factors; rotations, reflections, and layer interchange separate them. This is a complete representation-level classification of a natural, highly symmetric finite topological complex rather than an isolated face count or routine Euler-characteristic computation. It makes no universal claim for all prism widths or integral coefficient modules.
