# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The argument independently derives the proper-subalgebra fibers from bracket closure, the exact spanning criterion from nondegeneracy, and all multiplicities by symplectic subspace incidence. The decisive isotropic-complement counts are \(q^3\) for Lagrangian planes (symmetric two-by-two forms) and \(q(q^2-1)\) for nondegenerate planes (a fixed determinant fiber of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\)); both proofs work in characteristic two. Direct bracket-generated adjacency was exhaustively checked for all proper subalgebras over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), with exactly the five claimed degree-multiplicity pairs.

## Originality — PASS
The first relevant source, arXiv:2605.09583, classifies comaximal graphs only through Lie-algebra dimension three, including the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra. The full arXiv:2608.16575 follow-up classifies selected dimension-four cases (Section 4) and establishes a generic Frattini blow-up relation (Theorem 6.1). That theorem does not specify the symplectic-plane fiber multiplicities or degree distribution in dimension five. Targeted mathematical-database searches for Heisenberg generating/comaximal graphs, valencies, isotropic complements, and five-dimensional degree spectra identified only distinct invariants such as subalgebra-commutativity degree. The claim does not assert novelty for the underlying graph definition or Frattini reduction. Residual risks include unindexed exact enumerations and alternative finite-polar-space graph formulations.

## Value — PASS
The first genuinely symplectic-rank-two Heisenberg case separates its two-dimensional projected subalgebras into two different degree classes, whereas the rank-one Heisenberg case has a regular nonisolated part. The result supplies a complete, field-uniform graph invariant and exact edge, clique, chromatic, and diameter consequences; these require the nontrivial Lagrangian-complement and nondegenerate-complement enumeration.
