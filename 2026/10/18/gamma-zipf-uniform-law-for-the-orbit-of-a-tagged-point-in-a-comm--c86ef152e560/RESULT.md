# Gamma–Zipf–uniform law for the orbit of a tagged point in a commuting permutation pair

## Finding
For each fixed \(\theta>0\) and \(n\ge1\), sample an ordered commuting pair \((\sigma,\tau)\) in \(S_n^2\) with weight proportional to \(\theta^{K(\sigma,\tau)}\), where \(K\) is the number of joint orbits. On the orbit of the tagged label \(1\), let \(J_n\) be the orbit cardinality, \(A_n\) the length of its \(\sigma\)-cycle, \(D_n=J_n/A_n\), and \(B_n\in\{0,\ldots,A_n-1\}\) the unique residue satisfying \(\tau^{D_n}(1)=\sigma^{B_n}(1)\). Let
\[
Z_m(\theta)=[z^m]\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}(1-z^r)^{-\theta},\qquad Z_0(\theta)=1.
\]
For all integers \(a,d\ge1\), \(ad\le n\), and \(0\le b<a\), the **exact joint law** is
\[
\Pr(A_n=a,D_n=d,B_n=b)=\frac{\theta}{n}\frac{Z_{n-ad}(\theta)}{Z_n(\theta)}.
\]
Put \(\beta_\theta=\pi\sqrt{\theta/6}\). As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\left(J_n/\sqrt n,D_n,B_n/A_n\right)\ \Longrightarrow\ (X_\theta,D,U),
\]
where **the three limits are mutually independent**; \(X_\theta\) has gamma density \(\beta_\theta^2 x e^{-\beta_\theta x}\) for \(x>0\), \(\Pr(D=d)=6/(\pi^2d^2)\) for \(d=1,2,\ldots\), and \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\). In particular, the discrete height and normalized twist laws are universal with respect to \(\theta\).

## Assumptions and scope
The integer \(n\) tends to infinity while \(\theta\in(0,\infty)\) remains fixed. The permutations act on the labeled set \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\). A joint orbit means an orbit of the group generated by both commuting permutations. The tag is the fixed label \(1\), whose orbit has the same law as that of an independently uniform tagged vertex by simultaneous-conjugation invariance. The count \(D_n\) is the number of distinct \(\sigma\)-cycles within that joint orbit; \(B_n\) is a twist relative to the given ordered generators, not an invariant under interchanging the two generators. Every limit is weak convergence on \((0,\infty)\times\mathbb N\times[0,1]\) with the discrete topology on \(\mathbb N\).

## Proof
**1. Connected-component enumeration.** Any transitive action of two commuting permutations on \(j\) points factors through a transitive action of \(\mathbb Z^2\), hence through translation on \(\mathbb Z^2/H\) for a subgroup \(H\) of index \(j\). Every such subgroup is described uniquely by \(a,d\ge1\) with \(ad=j\) and \(b\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\), through
\[
H=\langle(a,0),(-b,d)\rangle.
\]
Translation by the first basis vector has cycles of length \(a\) and their number is \(d\); translation by the second generator satisfies its \(d\)-th power equal to the \(b\)-th power of the first. For a fixed subgroup the number of transitive labeled commuting pairs is \(j!/j=(j-1)!\): there are \(j!\) labelings of the cosets, modulo the \(j\) translations centralizing the transitive action.

**2. Exact tagged-orbit probability.** The exponential formula and the preceding subgroup count show that the total pair weight divided by \(n!\) is
\[
Z_n(\theta)=[z^n]\exp\left(\theta\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\sigma_1(j)}jz^j\right)=[z^n]\prod_{r\ge1}(1-z^r)^{-\theta},
\]
where \(\sigma_1(j)=\sum_{e\mid j}e\). If the tagged component has prescribed \((a,d,b)\) with \(ad=j\), choose its other \(j-1\) labels, give that component one of its \((j-1)!\) connected actions, and put an arbitrary weighted commuting pair on the remaining labels. The resulting normalized probability is exactly \(\theta Z_{n-j}(\theta)/(n Z_n(\theta))\). The formula also proves that, conditional on \((A_n,D_n)=(a,d)\), the twist \(B_n\) is discrete uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\).

**3. A coefficient ratio.** Set \(\beta=\beta_\theta\). The classical eta-product estimate as \(t\downarrow0\) is
\[
\prod_{r\ge1}(1-e^{-rt})^{-\theta}=\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\theta/2}\exp\left(\frac{\beta^2}{t}+O_\theta(t)\right).
\]
A one-saddle Cauchy extraction gives the fully normalized coefficient asymptotic
\[
Z_n(\theta)\sim\frac{\beta^{(\theta+1)/2}}{2\sqrt\pi(2\pi)^{\theta/2}}\,n^{-(\theta+3)/4}\exp(2\beta\sqrt n).
\]
For completeness, at the contour radius \(e^{-t}\), \(t=\beta/\sqrt n\), the quadratic term of the log-integrand has magnitude \(\theta\sum_{j\ge1}j\sigma_1(j)e^{-jt}\sim2\beta^2/t^3\), so the Gaussian integral contributes \(t^{3/2}/(2\sqrt\pi\beta)\). The first displayed estimate supplies its prefactor. A standard minor-arc bound follows already from retaining only the single-harmonic terms of the logarithmic Euler product:
\[
\log\frac{F_\theta(e^{-t})}{|F_\theta(e^{-t+i\varphi})|}\ge\theta\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-mt}(1-\cos(m\varphi))\ \gg_\theta\ \min\{\varphi^2/t^3,1/t\},
\]
for \(|\varphi|\le\pi\); together with a major arc of width \(t^{3/2}\log(1/t)\), this makes the Gaussian calculation rigorous (Taylor's relative remainder tends to zero). The asymptotic implies, uniformly whenever \(0\le j\le M\sqrt n\) for fixed \(M\),
\[
\frac{Z_{n-j}(\theta)}{Z_n(\theta)}=\exp(-\beta j/\sqrt n)+o(1).
\]

**4. Fixed-height joint local limits.** Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(0<x<y<\infty\). Sum the exact law over all \(a\) with \(ad/\sqrt n\in[x,y]\) and all \(b\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}\). Since the mesh in \(u=ad/\sqrt n\) is \(d/\sqrt n\), the uniform ratio estimate and a Riemann sum yield
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{D_n=d,\ x\le J_n/\sqrt n\le y\}=\frac\theta{d^2}\int_x^y u e^{-\beta u}\,du.
\]
The total mass of these limiting probabilities across all \(d\ge1\) and \(u>0\) equals
\[
\frac\theta{\beta^2}\sum_{d\ge1}\frac1{d^2}=\frac\theta{\theta\pi^2/6}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}6=1.
\]
Therefore no probability escapes to \(d=\infty\), \(u=0\), or \(u=\infty\), and the pair \((J_n/\sqrt n,D_n)\) converges with product limit: gamma shape two, rate \(\beta\), times Zipf probability \(1/(\zeta(2)d^2)\).

**5. Twist independence.** Conditional on \(A_n=a,D_n=d\), \(B_n/a\) is the discrete-uniform grid of mesh \(1/a\). For each fixed \(d\) and each compact range \(0<x\le J_n/\sqrt n\le y\), one has \(a\ge x\sqrt n/d\to\infty\); thus this grid converges uniformly to Lebesgue measure. Apply the preceding joint local limits to products of bounded continuous test functions, and then discard the complementary small-probability region by tightness from Step 4. This proves mutual independence of \(X_\theta,D,U\) and completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying Python 3 standard-library program, `verification/commuting_tagged_verify.py`, performs exhaustive enumeration of commuting permutation pairs through \(n=5\) for two exact choices of \(\theta\), determines the tagged orbital parameters including twist from the actual permutations, and checks every attained exact probability against the formula. It independently checks the generating-function coefficient recurrence with exact rational arithmetic and performs finite numerical coefficient-ratio diagnostics. Executed successfully: 162 exhaustive pointed-orbit assertions, 175 exact coefficient assertions, and 8 numerical asymptotic diagnostics. These finite tests do not prove the infinite weak limit; Steps 3–5 do.

## Relationship to prior work
Abdesselam and Starr (arXiv:2505.11469, §§1.1–1.3) establish a central limit theorem for the **number** of joint orbits for this Ewens-type commuting-tuple model; they describe the torus dimensions/twist but do not give their tagged-component asymptotic joint law. Abdesselam, Brunialti, Doan and Velie (arXiv:2309.09407) give a bijective enumeration for commuting tuples, from which the finite pointed-component formula is an elementary extraction, rather than an independently claimed new combinatorial technique. The distinct addition here is a shape-two Gamma size limit, a universal inverse-square number-of-cycles law and independent continuum twist in one joint theorem. Ercolani and Ueltschi (arXiv:1102.4796) study asymptotic cycle-length statistics for non-uniform random permutations, but their smooth power-like cycle-weight assumptions do not directly apply to the arithmetic weights \(\theta\sigma_1(j)\), and do not classify the two-generator torus dimensions or twist. Independent priority checking is still necessary.

## Limitations
The result fixes the positive parameter \(\theta\) as \(n\to\infty\) and treats commuting **pairs**, not three or more generators; no convergence rate or uniformity in moving \(\theta\) is claimed. The exact finite identity follows from known torus enumeration, so it is not separately claimed as unprecedented. The asymptotic law is self-reviewed rather than independently audited, and earlier general component-size limit theorems could conceivably imply its gamma marginal. The full three-factor limit has not been independently checked for priority.
