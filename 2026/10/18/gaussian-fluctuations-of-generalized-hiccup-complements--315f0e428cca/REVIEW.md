# Mathematical review

**Correctness (PASS).** The theorem concerns every fixed pair of integer parameters \(k\ge2\), \(x\ge3\). The antecedent complement-word identity is explicitly attributed to Cloitre's Proposition 4.3. The proof derives the exact tree identities \(L_h=(k^{h+1}-1)/(k-1)\), \(Z(W_h)=k^h\), and \(\Delta(W_h)=1\), and the prefix bound by induction. A uniform tree position couples, apart from probability \(O(k^{-r})\), to independent uniform child choices up to height \(r=\lfloor h^{1/3}\rfloor\). The remainder is \(O(r)=o(\sqrt h)\). The tiling of a prefix of the infinite word discards a vanishing proportion \(O(k^m/N+1/L_m+H/N)\) of positions, and every retained block has a uniformly controlled drift offset. Together these prove weak convergence for the stated normalization by the classical central limit theorem. The enclosed finite checker independently confirms small tree identities and direct hiccup/complement equivalence; finite checks are not used to assert convergence.

**Originality (PASS; limited by discoverability).** Targeted research-index searches for the exact paper, morphic hiccup sequences, meta-Fibonacci complements, and Gaussian limit laws found no matching result. The closest examined sources are Cloitre [1] (full text: exact morphic/tree representation and recurrences), Fokkink--Joshi [2] (full article: morphic/Beatty/continued-fraction aspects), Deugau--Ruskey [3] (full twelve-page study: tree recurrences and generating functions), Ruskey--Deugau [5] (36-page study: Theorem 25 has an exact inverse-position base-\(k\) digit-sum identity for the special meta-Fibonacci family, and Theorem 28 a first-order density result), and Erickson et al. [4] (full available author manuscript: Conolly-like frequency and asymptotic density). Their explicit special-family digit-sum identity may yield the \(x=k+1\) specialization by an additional inversion and classical digit-sum limit, but none states or proves the general random-index Gaussian limit with arbitrary \(x\), or the corresponding drift/variance. The structural source is stronger for exact deterministic specification, but does not contain the new distributional assertion or its nontrivial multiscale proof. The main residual risk is an unindexed probabilistic limit under another name or an unexamined specialized treatment of uniform tree positions.

**Value (PASS).** The density \((k-1)/k\) and exact complement word leave a natural quantitative question about the size and typical behavior of the remaining discrepancy. The theorem provides a second-order Gaussian limit, an explicit arithmetic drift dependent on the initial value, and a universal variance coefficient across all fixed \(x\) with a given \(k\). This directly characterizes typical fluctuations for the family recently linked to nested recurrences and complete tree labels; it is not a numerical slice or a routine algebraic restatement. Future work could examine local limit rates, extreme values, or joint laws, but those are not required for this theorem.

**Limitations and residual risks.** The statement is a weak empirical distribution limit for fixed parameters, not a pointwise or functional central limit theorem; no convergence rate is claimed. The literature comparison cannot exclude unindexed earlier work. There has been no independent proof review.
