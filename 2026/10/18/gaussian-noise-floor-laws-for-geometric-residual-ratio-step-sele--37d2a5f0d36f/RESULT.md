# Gaussian noise-floor laws for geometric residual-ratio step selection

## Finding
Consider the geometric sliding-window residual-norm statistic used to select a step of the form \(\alpha=1+\rho\) in adaptive gradient descent [1]. With independent additive Gaussian errors in the *measured* residual vectors, this statistic has a sharp polynomial-tail and moment classification. For residual-vector dimension \(d\ge1\) and fixed window length \(w\ge1\), its \(q\)-th positive moment exists exactly when \(q<dw\). At a vanishing true residual, the limiting statistic is a transformed \(F\)-ratio, exceeds one with probability one half, and has squared expectation strictly greater than one (possibly infinite). Thus the associated one-step multiplier in a unit-curvature gradient mode is not mean-square contractive at the Gaussian observation-noise floor, regardless of the fixed window size. A full-history geometric window instead tends almost surely to the critical value one, producing a step tending to two.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix integers \(d,w\ge1\), a scalar \(\sigma>0\), a deterministic sequence \(r_k\in\mathbb R^d\) with \(r_k\to0\), and independent \(Z_k\sim N_d(0,I_d)\). The measured residual is \(Y_k=r_k+\sigma Z_k\). At each \(k\ge w\), define
\[
Q_{k,w}=\left(\frac{\|Y_k\|_2}{\|Y_{k-w}\|_2}\right)^{1/w},\qquad \alpha_{k+1}=1+Q_{k,w}.
\]
The two norm values are independent, and their denominators are nonzero almost surely. This is a two-oracle diagnostic model: measurement noise affects the step-selection statistic, while any subsequent gradient direction in the one-step implication is evaluated exactly. The sequence \(r_k\) is prescribed, not assumed to be generated by the noisy feedback process. The original noiseless statistic telescopes to the displayed formula [1, Eq. (3)].

**Finite-\(k\) sharp boundary and tail.** For every fixed \(k\ge w\) and every \(q>0\),
\[
\mathbb E Q_{k,w}^{q}<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q<dw.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{dw}\mathbb P(Q_{k,w}>t)
=\frac{e^{-\|r_{k-w}\|_2^2/(2\sigma^2)}\,\mathbb E\|r_k+\sigma Z\|_2^d}
{2^{d/2}\sigma^d\Gamma(d/2+1)}\in(0,\infty),
\]
where \(Z\sim N_d(0,I_d)\). In particular, the second moment of the proposed step is infinite whenever \(dw\le2\), even when each measurement noise itself has moments of all orders.

**Fixed-window noise-floor law.** For each fixed \(w\), as \(k\to\infty\),
\[
Q_{k,w}\ \Rightarrow\ Q_{\infty,w}=
\left(\frac{X}{V}\right)^{1/(2w)},\qquad
X,V\ \text{independent }\chi^2_d.
\]
Writing \(a=d/2\), the cumulative distribution and the complete positive-moment spectrum are
\[
\mathbb P(Q_{\infty,w}\le t)=I_{\,t^{2w}/(1+t^{2w})}(a,a)
\quad(t>0),
\]
\[
\mathbb E Q_{\infty,w}^{q}
=\frac{\Gamma(a+q/(2w))\Gamma(a-q/(2w))}{\Gamma(a)^2}
\quad (0<q<dw),
\]
with infinite moments for \(q\ge dw\). Here \(I_z\) is the regularized incomplete beta function. The exact asymptotic tail constant is
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{dw}\mathbb P(Q_{\infty,w}>t)
=\frac{\Gamma(d)}{\Gamma(d/2+1)\Gamma(d/2)}.
\]
In dimension one, \(\mathbb P(Q_{\infty,w}\le t)=(2/\pi)\arctan(t^w)\). In all dimensions,
\[
\mathbb P(Q_{\infty,w}>1)=\frac12,
\qquad \mathbb E Q_{\infty,w}^2>1
\]
where the second expectation may be infinite.

**Full-history window.** For \(w=k\),
\[
Q_{k,k}=\left(\frac{\|Y_k\|_2}{\|Y_0\|_2}\right)^{1/k}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad \alpha_{k+1}\longrightarrow2.
\]
For a normalized quadratic with a unit Hessian eigenvalue, a single exact-gradient update using \(\alpha_{k+1}=1+Q_{k,w}\) multiplies the error component in that eigenvector by \(-Q_{k,w}\). Consequently the fixed-window noise-floor one-step probability of amplification tends to \(1/2\), and the mean-square gain tends to the value above when the moment exists; it is infinite at every sufficiently defined step when \(dw\le2\). This is **not** an assertion of almost-sure divergence or of an entire adaptive optimization trajectory.

## Proof
For fixed \(k,w\), put \(U=\|Y_k\|_2\) and \(V=\|Y_{k-w}\|_2\). They are independent positive random variables. The smooth strictly positive Gaussian density of \(Y_{k-w}\) at zero gives the small-ball expansion, for \(s\downarrow0\),
\[
\mathbb P(V<s)
=\frac{e^{-\|r_{k-w}\|_2^2/(2\sigma^2)}}{2^{d/2}\sigma^d\Gamma(d/2+1)}s^d+o(s^d).
\]
Integration of the density near zero proves \(\mathbb E[V^{-p}]<\infty\) exactly for \(p<d\); its critical moment diverges logarithmically. All positive moments of \(U\) are finite and positive. Independence in
\(\mathbb E[Q_{k,w}^q]=\mathbb E[U^{q/w}]\mathbb E[V^{-q/w}]\)
therefore proves the moment threshold.

For the tail constant, condition on \(U\):
\(\mathbb P(Q_{k,w}>t)=\mathbb E[\mathbb P(V<U/t^w\mid U)]\).
The Gaussian density is globally bounded. Hence the small-ball probability divided by the radius to the power \(d\) is uniformly bounded, including for large radius; dominated convergence applies with integrable dominating function proportional to \(U^d\). This yields the stated sharp constant.

As the deterministic means \(r_k\) and \(r_{k-w}\) vanish, the independent Gaussian norm pair converges jointly to two independent central chi norms. The continuous mapping theorem yields the limiting ratio. If \(X,V\) are independent \(\chi^2_d\) variables, \(X/(X+V)\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,a)\). The CDF follows by \(X/V\le t^{2w}\). Independent chi-square positive and negative moment integrals give the gamma formula and its exact domain. The limiting tail constant follows either from the small-ball coefficient with zero means or the beta density. For each \(0<q<dw\), choose \(q'\) strictly between \(q\) and \(dw\). Because the means remain bounded and the Gaussian small-ball bounds hold uniformly in their centers, the moments \(\mathbb E Q_{k,w}^{q'}\) are uniformly bounded; hence the \(q\)-th moments also converge to the stated limiting value. Symmetry of \(X,V\) implies \(Q_{\infty,w}\) and its reciprocal have the same distribution; hence the probability above one is one half. The strict inequality \(\mathbb E Q_{\infty,w}^2>1\) follows from the strict pointwise arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for \(Q^2+Q^{-2}\) under this nondegenerate reciprocal symmetry; if either expectation diverges the inequality remains valid.

For the full-history statement, boundedness of \(r_k\) and Gaussian small-ball and large-deviation bounds imply, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), that the events \(\|Y_k\|_2<e^{-\varepsilon k}\) and \(\|Y_k\|_2>e^{\varepsilon k}\) have summable probabilities. Borel–Cantelli gives \(k^{-1}\log\|Y_k\|_2\to0\) almost surely. Since \(\|Y_0\|_2\in(0,\infty)\) almost surely, \(\log Q_{k,k}\to0\), proving the assertion. Finally a unit-curvature exact-gradient component \(e\) is updated to \((1-\alpha)e=-Qe\). No multistep conclusion follows from this one-step identity without a separate analysis of feedback dependence.

## Relationship to prior work
Hu, Pollock, Xue, and Zhu [1], first released February 14, 2026, introduce the geometric residual-ratio feedback and its \(1+\rho\) gradient step. Their analysis treats exact residual information; this finding addresses an independent Gaussian **measurement-noise** extension rather than disputing the exact-information convergence claims. The chi-square ratio, beta transformation, and dimension-one Cauchy law are classical probability identities and are not individually claimed as new. The new statement under assessment is their sharp finite-step and noise-floor *joint consequence* for this particular adaptive residual-ratio feedback, including the \(dw\) moment boundary, one-step mean-square instability, and fixed-window/full-history contrast. Heavy-tailed noise convergence studies [2] analyze different stochastic-gradient dynamics; they do not by themselves give this feedback-statistic classification.

## Limitations
This is an exact diagnostic result under independent isotropic Gaussian **observation** errors and prescribed vanishing deterministic residuals. It does not analyze correlated noise, spatially anisotropic covariance, noisy gradient-direction updates, clipping, finite-precision overflow, or the long-run dynamics of the feedback-controlled optimization process. A moment of the step statistic does not equal a moment of the full optimizer. For the full-history case, convergence of the chosen step toward two only reaches the deterministic stability boundary; it does not prove divergence. Prior analytical results on ratios of Gaussian norms may imply distributional subformulas, so the claimed distinction is restricted to their consequences for the concrete 2026 parameter rule.
