# Gaussian reduction threshold for random binary formal contexts

## Finding

Let \(r\ge 2\) and \(m\ge1\), and construct a formal context with \(r\) labelled objects and \(m\) labelled attributes by choosing all incidences independently with probability \(p\in(0,1)\). An object is **individually reducible** if its intent is the intersection of the intents of some subset of *other* objects, where the empty intersection is the full attribute set. Equivalently, deleting that object alone does not change the concept lattice up to isomorphism.

For a fixed object, write \(Q_{r,m}(p)\) for its reducibility probability. Then the following two exact, equal formulas hold:

\[
Q_{r,m}(p)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom ms p^s(1-p)^{m-s}
 \sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}k(p^s)^k(1-p^s)^{r-1-k}(1-p^k)^{m-s},
\]

\[
Q_{r,m}(p)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom ms p^s(1-p)^{m-s}
 \sum_{j=0}^{m-s}(-1)^j\binom{m-s}j\bigl(1-p^s(1-p^j)\bigr)^{r-1}.
\]

Powers with exponent zero are one, including the term where the base is zero; this correctly counts the empty intersection when the target object has all attributes.

Fix \(p\in(0,1)\), put \(c_p=-p\log p\) and \(\sigma_p=(-\log p)\sqrt{p(1-p)}\), and suppose the integer sequence \(r_m\ge2\) satisfies

\[
\frac{\log(r_m-1)-c_pm}{\sigma_p\sqrt m}\longrightarrow a\in\mathbb R.
\]

Then, with \(\Phi\) the standard normal distribution function,

\[
\boxed{\lim_{m\to\infty}Q_{r_m,m}(p)=\Phi(a).}
\]

More coarsely, if \(\log(r_m-1)/m\) converges to a value strictly below \(c_p\), the limit is zero; if it converges to a value strictly above \(c_p\), the limit is one. In the uniform case \(p=1/2\), the critical scaling is \(r_m=1+\lfloor 2^{m/2+a\sqrt m/2}\rfloor\), giving limit \(\Phi(a)\). At this scaling the probability that the specified object has an identical row among the others tends to zero. Thus the transition is overwhelmingly about genuine intersection-reduction rather than duplicate-row clarification.

The expected proportion of individually reducible labelled objects equals \(Q_{r,m}(p)\) exactly. In the zero- and one-limit regimes this proportion also converges in probability to zero and one, respectively. No assertion is made about simultaneous deletion of all individually reducible objects.

## Assumptions and scope

All objects and attributes are labelled. A row's intent is the subset of columns in which that row has value one. The model is the fixed-dimension i.i.d. Bernoulli incidence model, not a row-sum-preserving or Dirichlet null model. The parameter \(p\) stays fixed strictly between zero and one in the limiting theorem. The number of rows may grow exponentially with the number of attributes. The logarithm is natural except where base two is explicitly displayed.

## Proof

**1. Reduction criterion.** Let the target intent be \(B\). The family of intents generated by arbitrary intersections of object intents, including the empty intersection, describes the intent side of the concept lattice. Removing the target preserves that family if and only if \(B\) is an intersection of some remaining intents: sufficiency follows by substitution into every meet, and necessity by expressing \(B\) in the generated family after removal.

**2. Conditional witness criterion.** Condition on \(|B|=s\). Call another row *eligible* if it has all \(s\) attributes in \(B\). The number \(K\) of eligible rows is binomial with parameters \(r-1\) and \(p^s\). An intersection of other rows equals \(B\) exactly when every attribute outside \(B\) is absent from at least one eligible row. Indeed, any representation uses only eligible rows, and intersecting all eligible rows works whenever a representation exists. If \(s=m\), the empty intersection supplies the representation even when \(K=0\).

Conditional on the eligible-row identities, entries of those rows outside \(B\) are independent Bernoulli variables of parameter \(p\). Consequently the witness probability, given \(K=k\), is \((1-p^k)^{m-s}\). Averaging over \(K\) and \(s\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,p)\) proves the first formula, including the specified exponent-zero convention.

For the second formula, fix a set of \(j\) attributes outside \(B\). The probability that no eligible row is absent from any one of those \(j\) attributes is \([1-p^s(1-p^j)]^{r-1}\). Inclusion–exclusion over the \(m-s\) missing attributes gives the second inner sum.

**3. Gaussian critical window.** Put \(S_m=|B|\) and \(Z_m=(S_m-mp)/\sqrt{mp(1-p)}\). The central limit theorem gives \(Z_m\Rightarrow N(0,1)\). The conditional mean of eligible rows is \(\mu_m=(r_m-1)p^{S_m}\). Under the stated scaling,

\[
\log\mu_m=\sigma_p\sqrt m\,(a-Z_m)+o(\sqrt m).
\]

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Uniformly on \(Z_m\le a-\varepsilon\), the mean \(\mu_m\) grows at least as \(\exp(\sigma_p\varepsilon\sqrt m/2)\) eventually. A binomial Chernoff bound gives \(\Pr(K<\mu_m/2\mid S_m)\le\exp(-\mu_m/8)\). Otherwise a union bound shows that some attribute outside \(B\) has no zero witness with probability at most \(m p^{\mu_m/2}\), which tends to zero. Uniformly on \(Z_m\ge a+\varepsilon\), the mean is at most \(\exp(-\sigma_p\varepsilon\sqrt m/2)\); except for \(S_m=m\), reducibility implies \(K\ge1\), whose conditional probability is at most \(\mu_m\). The exceptional event \(S_m=m\) has probability \(p^m\). Sandwiching between the central-limit probabilities of \(Z_m\le a\pm\varepsilon\) and then letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the Gaussian limit. The coarse zero/one regimes use the same bounds together with the law of large numbers.

**4. Clarification is asymptotically absent.** At \(p=1/2\) in the displayed critical window, for any specified row each other row matches it with probability \(2^{-m}\). A union bound gives at most \((r_m-1)2^{-m}=\exp(-m\log 2/2+O(\sqrt m))\), which tends to zero. The probability of an all-one target, the only possible empty-intersection case, also tends to zero. Hence, for every finite \(a\), an asymptotically positive fraction \(\Phi(a)\) of row instances are reducible by nonempty intersections of distinct, nonidentical rows.

**5. Expected proportion.** Exchangeability gives the exact expected proportion. If its mean tends to zero, Markov's inequality gives convergence in probability to zero; apply the same argument to the complementary fraction in the one-limit regime.

## Verification

The standalone program `verify_reduction.py` compares the exact conditional-binomial formula and the independent inclusion–exclusion formula against direct enumeration of every binary incidence matrix on \(r\) rows and \(m\) columns for 33 triples with \(r\in\{2,3,4\}\), \(m\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), \(rm\le12\), and \(p\in\{1/3,1/2,2/3\}\). Every calculation is performed in exact rational arithmetic. For example, \(Q_{3,4}(1/2)=831/4096\). The program separately checks the one-column boundary \(Q_{r,1}(1/2)=1-2^{-r}\) for \(r=2,3,4\). Run with `python3 verify_reduction.py`; expected final line: `VERIFY_OK`.

The asymptotic result is proved analytically; the finite exhaustive checks are not a substitute for the limit argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Random incidence models for formal contexts were studied by Felde, Hanika and Stumme (2020), and Sakurai (arXiv:2101.11023, first posted 2021) calculated the expected number of formal concepts, not object-reduction probabilities. Standard object reduction by intersections preserves the concept lattice; Pattison and Nataraja (2023) study algorithms for context standardisation and reduction, not a Bernoulli phase transition.

Most importantly, Jacobovic and Zuk (arXiv:2112.15534, first posted 2021), especially their Bernoulli formula (26) and Theorem 1, determine the probability that a random row has **no dominating row** and the first-order location \(c_p=-p\log p\) of its threshold. That is a genuinely stronger antecedent than title matching suggests. It does *not* give the exact object-reduction law: a dominating row need not provide zero witnesses for every missing attribute, and the finite probabilities differ. Nor does their stated Bernoulli theorem give the Gaussian critical profile for intersection-reducibility. The new work proves that in the critical window the distinction between having an eligible upper row and being generated by intersections disappears in probability, while identifying its exact finite correction. The location of the transition is credited to the prior dominance phenomenon rather than claimed anew.

## Limitations

This theorem does not determine the distribution of the number of simultaneously removable rows or the size of a fully reduced context. It concerns a fixed object's reducibility before any deletions; removing every row satisfying this property at once may change the lattice. Parameters varying with \(m\), dependent incidence models, and fluctuations of the empirical reducible proportion in the nondegenerate Gaussian window are not covered. An equivalent formulation might exist in specialised random-set-system literature; the bibliography and similarity searches did not establish uniqueness.
