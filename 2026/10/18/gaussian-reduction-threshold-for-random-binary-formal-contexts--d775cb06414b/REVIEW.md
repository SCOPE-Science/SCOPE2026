# Review of the reduction probability and Gaussian window

## Correctness — PASS

Conditional on a row's number of present attributes, the eligible covering rows are binomial, and every absent attribute must have a zero witness. This proves the exact probability in two independently derived forms. The critical-window proof uses a quantitative split according to the central-limit fluctuation of the row sum; Chernoff concentration and a union bound give conditional probability tending to one or zero away from the split. The full-row/empty-intersection boundary is explicitly included. All 33 small exact-rational tests passed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty

Jacobovic and Zuk already identify the same first-order exponential threshold for dominance of Bernoulli vectors and have an exact finite formula for that *different* event. The present exact law concerns a row being generated by intersections of other rows, and the displayed Gaussian profile requires demonstrating that the extra witness-coverage condition becomes automatic at the critical scale. The statements are not interchangeable at finite size. Sakurai treats expected concept counts; Felde, Hanika and Stumme treat random contexts; Pattison and Nataraja treat context standardisation algorithms. A hidden equivalent formulation in random set-system theory remains possible, and not every related primary article could be read in full.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Individual object reducibility is a canonical data-reduction invariant because deleting such an object preserves the entire concept lattice. The theorem determines its exact Bernoulli probability for arbitrary finite context dimensions and the Gaussian transition when object count is exponentially large relative to attribute count. At uniform incidence the phenomenon is distinct from duplicate clarification with probability tending to one. It does not assume that all individually reducible objects can be removed simultaneously.

Closest related literature: Jacobovic–Zuk (arXiv:2112.15534), Sakurai (arXiv:2101.11023), Felde–Hanika–Stumme (DOI: 10.3390/info11030135), and Pattison–Nataraja (DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-35949-1_2). Residual risks are incomplete coverage of unindexed finite-set-system literature and partial inaccessibility of certain primary full texts.
