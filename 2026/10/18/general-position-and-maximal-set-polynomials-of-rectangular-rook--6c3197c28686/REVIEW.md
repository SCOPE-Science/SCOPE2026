# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof converts geodesic triples into bipartite three-edge paths, classifies path-free components as stars, establishes the labeled exponential formula, and proves necessary and sufficient conditions for inclusion-maximal forests. Inversion, restricted set partitions, and constructive spanning-star forests yield the full maximal polynomial and its support. A separate direct-geodesic exhaustive program tested 370,512 vertex subsets on ten boards; symbolic coefficient checks covered 42 additional boards. This is bounded computation supporting a general proof, not certification by exhaustive examples.

**Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The scalar minimum for Cartesian products of complete graphs (Di Stefano et al., 2023) and the known scalar maximum are prior results, not claimed as new. Iršič et al. (2024) define the general-position polynomial and establish its general framework; their inspected results do not imply the complete two-parameter rook-graph polynomial or the maximal-set distribution. Searches compared both the ordinary graph wording and the P4-free bipartite star-forest representation. Equivalent tabulations in unindexed combinatorics literature remain possible; independent priority review is required.

**Value — PASS.** The exact distribution of all GP sets and of every inclusion-maximal GP set answers a motivated two-parameter enumeration problem. The complete support classification produces unbounded gaps in maximal sizes not resolved by knowing only the lower and upper GP numbers.

**Scope and limits.** The statement is confined to two-factor complete Cartesian products with factor orders at least two. Its code independently checks finite cases but cannot replace the proof. Independent scientific validation remains outstanding.
