# Review of the rectangular rook general-position polynomial

**Correctness — PASS.** The forbidden rook geodesic is exactly a three-edge path in the bipartite edge model. The no-path condition is equivalent to a spanning star forest. A two-sort component decomposition proves the EGF, and a disjoint-heavy-column decomposition proves the finite coefficient sum. Forest component bounds and a complete boundary classification prove the leading coefficient. Literal finite geodesic tests agree with two independent enumerators; finite checks do not constitute an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The known general-position number for complete-factor Cartesian products is fully credited. An earlier broad general-position structural theorem may imply the set-family correspondence after specializing, but the inspected works do not present the complete bivariate rook polynomial or all maximal-set counts. The 2024 general-position polynomial work addresses other products; 2026 complete multipartite polynomials have a different graph operation. No matching formula was found under searched star-forest and metric-independence synonyms. Absolute bibliographic priority is unresolved.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem counts all sizes of an extremal-geodesic invariant in a natural rectangular family, exposes the structural bipartite forest model, and adds exact multiplicities to a previously known maximum-size value. The bivariate EGF supports coefficient extraction throughout the infinite family rather than a collection of ad hoc evaluations.
