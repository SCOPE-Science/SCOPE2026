# Generating rank, valuation census, and Gaussian completeness window for two-level bi-intuitionistic models

## Finding

Fix positive integers \(m,n\) and let \(P_{m,n}=L\sqcup U\) be the height-two poset with \(|L|=m\), \(|U|=n\), and every lower world strictly below every upper world. On its persistent propositional valuations, use ordinary bi-intuitionistic conjunction, disjunction, implication, co-implication, and constants. Write \(\mathcal A(V)\) for the algebra of truth sets definable from \(k\) ordered propositional letters with valuation tuple \(V=(X_1,\ldots,X_k)\), with \(k\ge0\).

**Exact subalgebra.** Partition the variable indices into \(I=\{i:X_i\cap L=\varnothing\}\) and \(J=\{1,\ldots,k\}\setminus I\). Let \(a\) be the number of distinct binary \(I\)-signatures realized by worlds in \(U\), and let \(b\) be the number of distinct binary \(J\)-signatures realized by worlds in \(L\). Empty signatures count as one possible signature, so \(a,b\ge1\). Then
\[
|\mathcal A(V)|=
\begin{cases}
2,&X_i\in\{\varnothing,P_{m,n}\}\text{ for every }i,\\
2^a+2^b-1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Every definable truth set is an arbitrary union of signature classes inside one of the two Boolean intervals separated by the definable junction \(U\) (unless only the two constant truth sets are present).

**Sharp rank and exact number of generating valuations.** The least number of proposition letters for which every upset is definable is
\[
\rho(m,n)=\max\{1,\lceil\log_2 m\rceil+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\}.
\]
Let \((N)_{\underline s}=N(N-1)\cdots(N-s+1)\) for integers \(s\ge0\), with \((N)_{\underline0}=1\) and value zero if \(N<s\). The number of ordered \(k\)-tuples of persistent valuations that generate the full algebra is
\[
G_{m,n}(k)=
\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{k}{r}(2^r)_{\underline n}
\sum_{j=0}^{k-r}(-1)^j\binom{k-r}{j}
(2^{k-r-j})_{\underline m}
-\mathbf 1_{\{m=n=1\}}2^k.
\]
Here the indicator subtracts the all-constant valuations in the unique three-element case. Both the rank and the counting formula hold for \(k=0\).

**Gaussian window for uniformly random valuations.** For each \(t\ge2\), independently sample \(k_t\) persistent valuations uniformly from the \(2^{t+1}-1\) upsets of \(P_{t,t}\). Set \(h_t=\log_2 t\), and suppose \(k_t\) is a sequence of nonnegative integers satisfying \((k_t-4h_t)/\sqrt{h_t}\to c\) for a real \(c\). Then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\Pr\bigl(\mathcal A(V)=\operatorname{Up}(P_{t,t})\bigr)
=\max\{0,2\Phi(c/2)-1\},
\]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Thus random completeness has a \(4\log_2 t\) threshold with a square-root-logarithmic Gaussian window, whereas the optimal deterministic rank is \(2\lceil\log_2 t\rceil\).

## Assumptions and scope

Every poset \(P_{m,n}\) is **finite**, \(m,n\ge1\), and contains exactly two nonempty antichain levels with *all* cross-level comparisons. Its upset algebra has underlying set
\[
\operatorname{Up}(P_{m,n})=\{T:T\subseteq U\}\ \cup\ \{U\cup B:B\subseteq L\},
\]
with exactly \(2^n+2^m-1\) members. For arbitrary upsets \(A,B\), implication and co-implication have truth sets
\[
A\Rightarrow B=P_{m,n}\setminus\downarrow(A\setminus B),\qquad
A\mathbin{\backslash}B=\uparrow(A\setminus B).
\]
The second notation denotes *logical co-implication*, not relative set difference. The constants are \(\varnothing,P_{m,n}\); the other connectives are intersection and union. Formula truth sets are precisely the double-Heyting subalgebra generated by the chosen variable upsets.

For \(i\in I\), persistence forces \(X_i\subseteq U\). For \(j\in J\), persistence forces \(X_j=U\cup B_j\) with \(\varnothing\ne B_j\subseteq L\). The upper signature of \(u\in U\) is \((\mathbf1_{u\in X_i})_{i\in I}\), while the lower signature of \(\ell\in L\) is \((\mathbf1_{\ell\in X_j})_{j\in J}\). No restriction is placed on the truth of arbitrary nested formulas.

## Proof

**1. Signature upper bound.** Every atomic valuation is constant on each upper signature class and each lower signature class. Assume a truth set \(A\) has this property. Set operations preserve it. For any two signature-respecting sets \(A,B\), the difference \(D=A\setminus B\) is signature-respecting. A top world \(u\) belongs to \(\downarrow D\) precisely when \(u\in D\), because it has no proper successors; a lower world \(\ell\) belongs to \(\downarrow D\) precisely when either \(\ell\in D\) or \(D\cap U\ne\varnothing\). Dually, \(\ell\in\uparrow D\) precisely when \(\ell\in D\), while \(u\in\uparrow D\) precisely when \(u\in D\) or \(D\cap L\ne\varnothing\). Thus implication and co-implication preserve signatures. Every definable upset is consequently a union of whole classes on \(U\), or all of \(U\) together with whole classes on \(L\). There are at most \(2^a+2^b-1\) such upsets.

**2. Definability of the junction.** Suppose some variable \(X\) is neither \(\varnothing\) nor \(P_{m,n}\). If \(X\subseteq U\) is nonempty, then \(X\cup(X\Rightarrow\varnothing)=U\); this remains true for \(X=U\), where the implication part is empty. Otherwise \(X=U\cup B\) with \(\varnothing\subsetneq B\subsetneq L\), and
\[
X\cap(P_{m,n}\mathbin{\backslash}X)=U.
\]
Indeed \(P_{m,n}\setminus X=L\setminus B\) is nonempty, whose upward closure is \(U\cup(L\setminus B)\). In every nonconstant case, therefore, \(U\) itself has a defining formula. In the all-constant case every term has value \(\varnothing\) or \(P_{m,n}\), giving exactly two truth sets.

**3. Attainment of every signature class.** With \(U\) definable, the downwards Boolean interval \([\varnothing,U]\) is closed under \(\cap,\cup\), with relative complements of the top-observation generators given by
\[
U\cap(X_i\Rightarrow\varnothing)=U\setminus X_i\quad(i\in I).
\]
Hence ordinary finite Boolean combinations of those generators define each of the \(a\) nonempty top signature classes and all their unions. Likewise the upwards Boolean interval \([U,P_{m,n}]\) is closed under \(\cap,\cup\), and its relative complements of the lower-observation generators are
\[
U\cup(P_{m,n}\mathbin{\backslash}X_j)=U\cup(L\setminus(X_j\cap L))\quad(j\in J).
\]
Boolean combinations define every union of the \(b\) lower signature classes, adjoined to \(U\). The two intervals intersect only at \(U\). This attains the upper bound, proving the exact subalgebra formula.

**4. Rank and enumeration.** Full generation requires and, apart from the all-constant case, is equivalent to injectivity of both signature maps: \(a=n\) and \(b=m\). If \(r=|I|\) and \(q=|J|=k-r\), pigeonhole gives \(2^r\ge n\) and \(2^q\ge m\). Therefore \(k\ge\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+\lceil\log_2 m\rceil\). For \(m,n\ne(1,1)\) this bound is attainable: choose independent coordinates realizing distinct binary codes on each level and, on the lower level, include the all-ones code so every \(j\in J\) has nonempty lower trace. Since at least one level has two distinct codes, the valuation is nonconstant. If \(m=n=1\), no constants alone distinguish \(U\) from the two extrema, but one proposition valued \(U\) does. The rank follows.

For exact enumeration, choose the \(r\) indices in \(I\) in \(\binom{k}{r}\) ways. Assign distinct \(r\)-bit signatures to the \(n\) labeled top worlds in \((2^r)_{\underline n}\) ways. The \(m\) labeled lower worlds must receive distinct \(q\)-bit signatures and each of the \(q\) coordinates must be one somewhere (otherwise its variable would belong to \(I\)). Inclusion-exclusion over forced-zero coordinates counts these assignments by
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{q}(-1)^j\binom qj(2^{q-j})_{\underline m}.
\]
Multiplying and summing over \(r\) proves the displayed formula, except that if \(m=n=1\), all \(2^k\) tuples valued only at the extrema have injective empty-level signatures but generate just two elements. Subtract exactly those tuples.

**5. Gaussian critical window.** Write \(R_t=|I|\). A uniformly random upset has empty lower trace with probability \(p_t=2^t/(2^{t+1}-1)=1/2+O(2^{-t})\), so \(R_t\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k_t,p_t)\). Conditional on \(R_t=r\), the binary signatures of the \(t\) labeled upper worlds are independent uniform samples from \(2^r\) words. Their injectivity probability is
\[
\beta_t(r)=\frac{(2^r)_{\underline t}}{(2^r)^t}.
\]
The bottom signatures have independent uniform binary coordinates *conditioned on every one of the \(k_t-r\) coordinate columns being nonzero*. Dropping these column-conditioning events changes probabilities by at most \((k_t-r)2^{-t}\) under the unconditioned law; uniformly for \(r\), this is \(O(k_t2^{-t})=o(1)\). Therefore the generation probability differs by \(o(1)\) from
\[
\mathbb E[\beta_t(R_t)\beta_t(k_t-R_t)].
\]
The exceptional constant-valuation issue is absent once \(t\ge2\) and both signatures are injective.

For all integers \(s\ge0\), the birthday estimates give
\[
1-\binom t2 2^{-s}\ \le\ \beta_t(s)\ \le\
\exp\bigl(-t(t-1)/2^{s+1}\bigr).
\]
Choose any \(w_t\to\infty\) with \(w_t=o(\sqrt{\log_2 t})\), for example \(w_t=(\log_2 t)^{1/4}\). Then \(\beta_t(s)\to1\) uniformly for \(s\ge2\log_2 t+w_t\), and tends to zero uniformly for \(s\le2\log_2 t-w_t\). The binomial mass in either transition strip is \(o(1)\) by the binomial local bound, since \(k_t=\Theta(\log_2 t)\). Hence the expectation differs by \(o(1)\) from the probability that simultaneously \(R_t>2\log_2 t\) and \(k_t-R_t>2\log_2 t\). With \(h_t=\log_2 t\), the central limit theorem gives
\[
\frac{R_t-k_t/2}{\sqrt{h_t}}\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,1),
\]
so the two inequalities define the limiting interval \((-c/2,c/2)\). Its Gaussian mass is \(\max\{0,2\Phi(c/2)-1\}\). This proves the critical-window assertion.

## Verification

The standalone file `CHECKER.py` independently builds the full upset lattice of each tested poset, computes both residuals from upward and downward closures, and closes the generated algebra under all six operations. It compares that direct semantics with the claimed signature formula, and compares a direct census of full generators against the inclusion-exclusion formula. Execute `python3 CHECKER.py` in the directory containing this file and the checker. Exhaustive runs for all \(1\le m,n\le3\), \(0\le k\le3\) yielded **8,936 valuation tuples**, **36 census checks**, and zero mismatches; the output is `BI_HEIGHT_VERIFY_OK semantic=8936 counts=36`. These are finite tests, not a proof of the unbounded rank, enumeration, or Gaussian limit.

## Relationship to prior work

Badia, Kowalski, and Olkhovikov (2026), Proposition 18, identifies the definable-upset algebra of a bi-intuitionistic model as the double-Heyting subalgebra generated by its propositional valuations. Their principal theorems characterize definable classes of models and frames through closure properties. The present theorem computes that algebra explicitly on complete height-two frames, enumerates all complete labeled valuations, and derives a probabilistic critical window; these quantitative results are not stated in the inspected sections of that source. Taylor (2017) gives general double-Heyting algebra background and Tasiou (2025) studies profinite bi-Heyting algebras. Those broader frameworks do not, in the material inspected, supply this exact levelwise code census or its birthday-window consequence.

## Limitations

The result applies only to finite **complete height-two** posets, not arbitrary height-two graphs or multi-level posets. The Gaussian assertion uses independent *uniform* sampling from all persistent valuations, and balanced level sizes tending to infinity. The count is for ordered, labeled valuations, not for isomorphism classes or formula lengths. Historical priority remains subject to possible equivalent presentations in older double-Heyting or finite-frame generation literature; the full 2016 directed-bisimulation article and the entire 2017 thesis were not inspected.
