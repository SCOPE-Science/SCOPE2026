# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The derivative magnitude trigonometric polynomial has frequencies \([-(d-1),d-1]\), hence its \(2d-1\) equispaced samples recover its complete continuous power spectrum. Every candidate derivative is therefore among a finite zero-flip family. For each nonempty flip, the derivative constant term has different modulus on a full-measure root locus. At \(d\ge3\), Fourier moments zero and one of the polynomial intensities have no aliases and give the exact constant/first-autocorrelation equations. Solving the latter for the competing integration constant, the former becomes a nontrivial real quadratic with nonzero radial coefficient, admitting at most a circle. The Vieta local coordinate argument makes the excluded derivative-root conditions null, and local analytic root labeling makes the finite circle union measurable. Fubini proves measure-zero ambiguity. The script provides finite exact-arithmetic witnesses but is not the basis of the all-degree proof.

## Originality — PASS, best knowledge

Jaming–Rathmair (arXiv:2205.08753v1, §3.1) establish universal uniqueness at \(2N-1\) sample sites and directly ask in Question 3.5 for almost-everywhere sampling thresholds. This theorem proves one nontrivial smaller-grid case \(M=2N-3\), \(N\ge4\), using only three polynomial moments besides the derivative spectrum. Their nongeneric counterexamples for \(2N-2\) sites do not imply or contradict this. Király–Ehler (arXiv:1402.4053v1) concern generic measurement ensembles, not the fixed derivative Fourier mask. Direct phase-retrieval, reciprocal-zero, and spectral-factor overlaps were searched. An older equivalent under coded-diffraction terminology remains a bibliographic risk, so independent literature review would still be valuable.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The problem is the explicit generic-sampling question asked by the motivating primary paper. The new statement works at all polynomial degrees at least three, improves the known sufficient site count by two for generic signals, supplies a finite spectral-factor recovery algorithm, and sharply limits the exceptional integration constants for each generic derivative. This is structural information about a deterministic mask rather than a routine finite example.

## Boundaries and remaining uncertainty

This does not prove the smallest generic site count or universal uniqueness. The circle cover is not a complete description of actual ambiguous fibers, because additional polynomial-intensity moments can exclude some circle points. Exceptional derivative factors with reciprocal or unit-modulus roots have not been classified, and computational witnesses cannot establish an infinite theorem.
