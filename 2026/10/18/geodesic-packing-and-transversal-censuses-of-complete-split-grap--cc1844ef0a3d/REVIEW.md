# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The diameter-two path classification is exhaustive: the only maximal paths are clique edges and independent–clique–independent triples. Independent labelled-block choices yield the precise factorial factors, and the same path list forces the full list of inclusion-minimal hitting sets. The fixed-three-path-count optimization proves the packing number and parity-sensitive top coefficient; elementary cardinality comparison proves the exact dual-equality threshold. The standalone checker independently validates the definition for 22 finite graph profiles and tests all claimed algebraic bounds across 5,000 larger parameter pairs. Scope: \(r\ge1\), \(s\ge2\). The argument does not claim any result for other split graphs.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not certified priority).** Manuel–Brešar–Klavžar (arXiv:2210.15325) introduced geodesic packing and studied complete graphs, balanced complete bipartite graphs, trees, Cartesian rook graphs and diagonal grids, but not the exact paired census of complete split graphs. Peterin–Semanišin, *Geodesic transversal problem for join and lexicographic product of graphs*, Theorem 3.5, covers the *scalar* transversal number as an implication of its general join theorem; this is explicitly treated as prior art. That theorem does not enumerate all minimal transversals or any vertex-disjoint maximal-path packings. Search comparisons included split-graph and join aliases, stronger general join coverage, complete-split numerical formula queries, and nearby polynomial/packing database results; no covering statement was found. Residual risk: unindexed exact computations or other literature could overlap the enumeration.

**Value — PASS.** The known covering/packing duality motivates calculating *both complete solution families* on a natural non-tree chordal class with two different maximal-geodesic lengths. The full polynomials give numbers of all feasible packings, all minimal blocking sets, and all optimum configurations. In particular they reveal a sharp structural transition from an inequality to equality of the dual extrema at twice as many independent as clique vertices, together with parity-sensitive families of optimal witnesses. These conclusions are not mere restatements of the previously published scalar join-transversal formula.

Exact source coverage was limited to identified primary articles/preprints and available search indices; negative search results are not a novelty theorem. Computation verifies finite parameters only; the general statements depend on the full proof.
