# Scientific review — geometric strong-reset boundary layer and universal throughput

## Correctness — PASS

For the specified finite continuous-time open exclusion chain, the block generator splits states with available local returns from states without them. Every fast state enters the no-return set in exactly one reset, so its eliminated resolvent is analytic in reciprocal resetting rate. The limiting effective chain has one recurrent class: a particle at the injection site only. At first order there is exactly one occupied fast state, and the rare injection-after-hop creates a single additional particle. The reduced chain on those additional-particle positions has forward motion at rate one, a transfer to the second site at rate one half after the initial particle hops, and extraction at the end. Solving its flux balances yields the explicit geometric coefficients. Direct stationary-current and covariance identities give their claimed first-order laws, including the two-site exception. The proof addresses quantifiers, rates, all fixed lengths, normalization, and source configurations. Reproducibility code passed 1,734 exact-rational assertions. Finite calculations corroborate the proof, not the universal claim by themselves.

## Value — PASS

Local resetting attempts are blocked when the destination is occupied, so the strong-reset regime has nontrivial boundary feedback rather than trivial instantaneous collapse. The exact geometric profile describes how rare additional-particle mass is distributed, the current law isolates a striking length-two exception, and the order-\(r^{-1}\) covariance demonstrates that independent-site mean-field closure misses a leading boundary effect. These quantities are physically interpretable and directly checkable with finite-size stochastic simulations. The exponential growth of the prefactor with length also makes the order of limits a substantive issue, not an arbitrary parameter slice.
