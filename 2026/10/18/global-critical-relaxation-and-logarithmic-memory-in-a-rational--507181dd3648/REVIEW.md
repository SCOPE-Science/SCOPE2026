The global contraction of the first-coordinate magnitude and the stable linear filter exclude a nonzero terminal magnitude for every real initial state. There is no claim of a uniform transient bound.

Leonel, Ferreira and Oliveira (2026) demonstrate the generic local \(n^{-1/2}\) exponent: that implication is acknowledged and excluded from the claimed novelty. The older cited technical report was not accessible for a full comparison, and no absolute priority guarantee is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The boundary of global attraction is where linear asymptotic stability breaks down, and the exact global recovery rate is not implied by the earlier strict-parameter stability results. The theorem distinguishes exceptional exponential and generic critical power-law convergence, exhibits an unexpected stable-parameter-independent physical-coordinate amplitude, and computes the previously undetermined logarithmic memory term in the original iteration clock, beyond the standard critical exponent. These quantitative invariants remain useful even though the model is low-dimensional and explicit.
