# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The thermal number generating function is independently derivable from centered Gaussian covariance overlaps. The conditional subtraction parity is its derivative ratio at \(-1\) and \(1\), with positive normalizer. The exact all-order sign follows by a positive-coefficient expansion and the zero of \(\cosh(2r)-2\nu-1\). The two numerator square-root singularities give separate contributions; their analytic factors and the unique denominator singularity produce the stated unit prefactors. Distinct branch locations yield uniform coefficient transfer near the thermal threshold even when their moduli coincide. Differentiating their log-radius ratio gives the \(1/m\) hyperbolic crossover. Numerical Fock-space and recurrence checks agree; finite checks do not prove the limit.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on available-source comparison

The 2010 paper arXiv:1008.5256v1 contains arbitrary fixed-order Wigner formulas and the single-subtraction thermal negativity threshold. The 2011 arXiv:1112.0854v1 contains the finite-order Legendre/generating-function formalism. Those exact source formulas are explicitly credited, as is the later Gaussian-state nonclassicality study arXiv:2204.06358. The assessed mathematical addition is the sharp \(m\to\infty\) two-branch parity asymptotic, with exact coefficients and a simultaneously varying thermal population \(\nu=\sinh^2r+s/m\). The original fixed-order expressions do not explicitly state that boundary layer. Targeted searches did not find an identical limit; this is not a proof of publication priority. A later broad thesis covering related finite-order photon statistics was inaccessible in full and remains a concrete residual comparison risk.

## Value — PASS

The result separates permanent odd-subtraction origin negativity from exponentially vanishing parity contrast and specifies the sharp thermal calibration scale \(1/m\) where the sign transition occurs. The sign threshold alone is available from prior finite-order formulas, but the large-order thermal sensitivity and explicit hyperbolic scaling function provide a distinct, motivated asymptotic measurement benchmark. No integrated-Wigner-negativity, finite-sample, or experimental-cost claim is made.

## Limitations and remaining risks

One centered mode, ideal subtraction, positive squeezing, strictly positive thermal population for exponential asymptotics, and \(v<1/2\) are required. The pure squeezed-vacuum endpoint is exceptional. Published fixed-order formulas can be used to derive these limits with additional asymptotic work; a previously unnoticed derivation or general uniform transfer theorem specialized to the same observable may limit priority. Independent publication-priority verification remains necessary.
