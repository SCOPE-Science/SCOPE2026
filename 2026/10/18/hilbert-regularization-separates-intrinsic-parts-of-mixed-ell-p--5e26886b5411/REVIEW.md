# Review of Hilbert regularization in intrinsic norm-cone geometry

## Correctness
**PASS.** The classification follows from two independently justified components: (i) a uniform Hessian comparison proving \(\Delta_p\ge c_{p,n}\Delta_2\) below exponent two and \(\Delta_p\le C_{p,n}\Delta_2\) above it; (ii) exact small-angle defect orders \(|t|^p\) and \(t^2\). The convexity proof treats coordinate hyperplanes by continuity and integrability, and lines through the origin by positivity and homogeneity. The endpoint metric bound uses an actual limit of a ratio of defects and the initiating paper's exact metric formula. A deterministic checker passed its finite tests, which are not substituted for the analytic proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** Acosta-Portilla's full manuscript (arXiv:2609.28922v1, pp. 1–25) proves the general comparability criterion and treats Euclidean versus \(\ell_4\) as a single negative example; it does not classify additive \(\ell_p\)/Euclidean mixtures or the logarithmic regularization boundary. Two closest published related results, identifiers `9351eabec91a` and `6f2b4c5fbe28`, were read in full. Their polyhedral and Hilbert-only conclusions neither entail nor subsume the non-Hilbert classification here. Searches for equivalent curvature and triangular-defect formulations found no covering result; unindexed literature is a remaining risk, not a proof of global priority.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The source introduced a component structure on the cone of equivalent norms, with very few classified families beyond Hilbertian and polyhedral instances. The theorem completely classifies a natural continuously parameterized family of smooth, strictly convex renormings, discovers an asymmetric exponent-two smoothing boundary, and quantifies a logarithmic blow-up to an infinite-distance endpoint despite arbitrarily small ordinary norm distortion. These are structural features of the new intrinsic geometry, not a mere recomputation of the source's two-dimensional example.

## Scope and limitations
The result is real, finite-dimensional and additive; no infinite-dimensional or complex statement is asserted. Exact bounded corrections to the logarithmic metric asymptotic are not computed. Priority among inaccessible or unindexed sources remains uncertain.
