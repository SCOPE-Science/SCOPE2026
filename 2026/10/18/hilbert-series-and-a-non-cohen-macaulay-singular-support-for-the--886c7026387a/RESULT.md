# Hilbert series and a non-Cohen–Macaulay singular support for the four-by-four Birkhoff toric variety

## Finding
Let \(B_4\) be the polytope of nonnegative four-by-four real matrices with each row and column sum equal to one, and let \(X=X_{B_4}\subset\mathbb P^{23}_k\) be its polarized normal projective toric variety over any algebraically closed field \(k\). Give \(Y=\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}\) the reduced induced projective scheme structure. Then its standard homogeneous coordinate ring \(R_Y\) has, for every integer \(d\ge1\),
\[
\dim_k(R_Y)_d=2d^4+12d^3+48d^2-72d+34,
\qquad \dim_k(R_Y)_0=1.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_Y}(t)=
\frac{1+19t+100t^2-84t^3-21t^4+33t^5}{(1-t)^5}.
\]
In particular \(\deg Y=48\), and \(R_Y\) is not Cohen–Macaulay (so \(Y\) is not arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay). The polynomial starts with values \(24,210,736,1794\) in degrees one through four.

## Assumptions and scope
The lattice of \(B_4\) is the affine lattice of integral row/column-sum-one matrices. The polarized toric homogeneous ring is the degree-graded semigroup algebra whose degree-\(d\) basis is indexed by the nonnegative integral four-by-four matrices whose row and column sums all equal \(d\). This follows from the integer decomposition property: an integral \(d\)-regular bipartite multigraph decomposes into \(d\) perfect matchings. The homogeneous ideal of a reduced union of torus-orbit closures is the intersection of the corresponding torus-invariant face ideals.

## Proof
**1. Singular faces.** A nonempty face \(F\) of \(B_4\) corresponds to the positive-support bipartite graph \(G\) of any point in its relative interior. Each edge of \(G\) belongs to a perfect matching. Let \(e\) be its edge count and \(c\) its number of connected components. Every component has equally many row and column vertices, since the equal positive margins balance there. The rank of the bipartite incidence matrix is \(8-c\), so \(\dim F=e-8+c\). The ambient polytope has dimension nine, and the normal cone has dimension \(17-e-c\). Its extremal rays are the coordinate facet normals for the \(16-e\) missing edges.

If \(c\ge2\), this cone has \(c-1\) more rays than its dimension, hence is nonsimplicial and singular. If \(c=1\), the rays number its dimension and form an integral lattice basis: prescribe arbitrary integral values on the missing edges and solve the row- and column-sum-zero equations on the connected graph \(G\). The necessary total balance is automatic, and an integral solution exists by a spanning-tree elimination (or total unimodularity of bipartite incidence). Thus these primitive coordinate rays form a lattice basis and the orbit is smooth. Therefore the singular torus orbits are exactly those whose support graph is disconnected.

**2. Hilbert functions as disconnected-support counts.** For an integer \(d\ge1\), the support of a nonnegative integral four-by-four matrix with all margins \(d\) is an elementary balanced bipartite graph: every positive edge occurs in a perfect matching of the associated \(d\)-regular bipartite multigraph. The monomial in \((R_X)_d\) corresponding to this matrix survives in the reduced union of singular face orbit-closures if and only if its support is disconnected. In particular, write \(T_{m,d}\) for the number of \(m\)-by-\(m\) nonnegative integral matrices with every margin \(d\), and \(C_{m,d}\) for the corresponding count having *connected* positive-support graph. For \(d\ge1\), \(C_{1,d}=1\), and \(C_{2,d}=d-1\): the two-by-two matrix has entries \(a,d-a,d-a,a\), and connectedness requires \(1\le a\le d-1\).

For three-by-three matrices the toric ideal of \(B_3\) is generated by one cubic, giving
\[
T_{3,d}=\binom{d+5}{5}-\binom{d+2}{5}
=\frac{(d+1)(d+2)(d^2+3d+4)}8.
\]
The disconnected cases consist of nine choices of a balanced one-by-one block paired with a *connected* two-by-two block, or six permutation matrices consisting of three one-by-one blocks. Consequently,
\[
C_{3,d}=T_{3,d}-9(d-1)-6=T_{3,d}-9d+3.
\]

Every disconnected four-by-four support has a unique decomposition into its connected balanced blocks. Partitioning the four labeled row vertices and four labeled column vertices, then pairing equal-size blocks, gives four mutually exclusive block-size types: \((3,1)\) with \(16\) choices, \((2,2)\) with \(18\) choices, \((2,1,1)\) with \(72\) choices, and \((1,1,1,1)\) with \(24\) choices. It follows exactly (not merely asymptotically) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dim_k(R_Y)_d
&=16C_{3,d}+18C_{2,d}^2+72C_{2,d}+24\\
&=16T_{3,d}+18d^2-108d+18\\
&=2d^4+12d^3+48d^2-72d+34.
\end{aligned}
\]
The degree-zero component is \(k\), not the formal specialization of the positive-degree polynomial: the only degree-zero monomial is the common unit.

**3. Rational series, degree, and Cohen–Macaulay obstruction.** Multiplication of the resulting generating function by \((1-t)^5\) yields numerator \(1+19t+100t^2-84t^3-21t^4+33t^5\). Since the maximal singular face is a four-dimensional \(B_3\) block face, \(\dim Y=4\) and \(\dim R_Y=5\). The leading coefficient \(2\) of its degree-four Hilbert polynomial gives degree \(4!\cdot2=48\). Were \(R_Y\) Cohen–Macaulay, a linear system of five parameters over an infinite extension of \(k\) would be regular, and the Hilbert numerator would be the Hilbert series of its finite-dimensional graded quotient. Such a series cannot have negative coefficients; the coefficients \(-84\) and \(-21\) give a contradiction. This argument is characteristic-independent.

## Verification
A stand-alone standard-library checker enumerates all four-by-four equal-margin nonnegative integer matrices for degrees \(1,2,3,4\), independently tests support connectivity by graph traversal, and compares the resulting counts with the closed formula. The measured pairs (all matrices, disconnected-support matrices) are \((24,24)\), \((282,210)\), \((2008,736)\), and \((10147,1794)\), respectively. It also verifies the polynomial-to-Hilbert-numerator conversion by finite differences. Finite enumeration checks only these four degrees; the block decomposition and three-by-three cubic Hilbert formula prove all degrees.

## Relationship to prior work
The explicit cubic equation of the three-by-three Birkhoff toric hypersurface is standard and appears in Haase–Paffenholz, *Quadratic Gröbner Bases for Smooth 3×3 Transportation Polytopes* (2006), Section 1.3. Paffenholz, *Faces of Birkhoff Polytopes* (2013), develops the elementary bipartite face-graph description and studies types and products of faces, but does not state the present Hilbert series of the reduced singular support. Ahmed, *Magic graphs and the faces of the Birkhoff polytope* (2004), treats magic graph labelings and face enumerations. The novelty asserted here is the precise singular-support Hilbert series and its arithmetic Cohen–Macaulay obstruction; none of the cited face-lattice or ambient Ehrhart counts alone state this invariant. Its underlying singular-orbit description is not asserted to be new.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the *reduced* singular locus in the specified projective embedding, not the nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme or its conductor. The degree-zero correction matters and must not be dropped. The graph-labeling literature may contain an equivalent implicit inclusion-exclusion formula; no uniqueness claim is made. The result makes no statement that \(Y\) fails local Cohen–Macaulayness; it establishes failure of the homogeneous coordinate ring to be Cohen–Macaulay.
