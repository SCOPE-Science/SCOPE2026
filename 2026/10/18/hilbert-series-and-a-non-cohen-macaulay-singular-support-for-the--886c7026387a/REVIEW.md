# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof independently characterizes singular torus orbits by connectedness of the perfect-matching support graph, using a facet-ray count for disconnected supports and an integral spanning-tree solution for connected supports. The homogeneous ring has the Birkhoff semigroup monomial basis by decomposition of regular bipartite multigraphs into perfect matchings. Unique decomposition into connected positive-support blocks yields the four explicit coefficients \(16,18,72,24\). The three-by-three Hilbert function follows from the classical cubic hypersurface equation, and exact symbolic expansion gives the stated polynomial and Hilbert series. Independently enumerated degree-one through degree-four tables corroborate the formula; all-degree correctness rests on the proof, not the finite experiment. The negative Hilbert numerator violates a necessary condition for arithmetic Cohen–Macaulayness.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest known antecedents give the three-by-three cubic toric ring (Haase–Paffenholz, 2006), the face-graph product structure (Paffenholz, 2013), and magic-graph Ehrhart methods (Ahmed, 2004). These do not explicitly compute the reduced singular-support Hilbert series for the four-by-four polarized Birkhoff toric variety. Exact mathematical-record searches for its formula, disconnected-support counts and non-Cohen–Macaulay consequence identified no matching published result. The face decomposition itself is classical in spirit and is not claimed new. An implicit equivalent specialization of a more general graph-labeling enumeration remains a residual risk; the 2004 Ahmed paper was available here only at abstract level.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete Hilbert function and series of a naturally defined reduced singular scheme determine its degree and exhibit an arithmetic Cohen–Macaulay defect that is not determined by ambient Birkhoff Ehrhart formulas, component counts, or individual component geometry. The exact count comes from a nontrivial two-colored connected-block decomposition, and the degree-zero correction is essential. This is a structural invariant of the singular locus, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Scope and limitations
The induced reduced singular support is treated, not the Jacobian singular scheme. No assertion of local non-Cohen–Macaulayness is made. An unidentified covering general formula cannot be ruled out solely by database search; a fuller reading of relevant magic-labeling literature remains useful.
