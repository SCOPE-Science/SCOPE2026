# Homotopy classification of four-cut complexes at the pentagonal girth boundary

## Finding
Let \(G\) be **any finite simple undirected graph on \(n\ge 9\) vertices** with girth at least five (forests have infinite girth). Define its ordinary four-cut complex \(\Delta_4(G)\) to have facets \(V(G)\setminus U\), where \(U\) ranges over four-vertex subsets whose induced graph \(G[U]\) is disconnected. Write \(p_5(G)\) for the number of unoriented five-cycles, each counted once. Then

\[
\Delta_4(G)\simeq\bigvee^{B(G)}S^{n-5},\qquad
B(G)=\binom{n-1}{3}-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\binom{\deg(v)}3-\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(\deg(u)-1)(\deg(v)-1)+p_5(G).
\]

In particular the full reduced integral homology is free of rank \(B(G)\) in degree \(n-5\) and vanishes in every other degree, with a zero-fold wedge interpreted as a point. No connectedness, regularity, planarity, or disjointness of five-cycles is assumed. The earlier known Euler-characteristic correction by five-cycles is **not** newly claimed; the new statement is its upgrade to a uniform *integral homotopy classification*. For example, the dodecahedral graph \(G(10,2)\) has \(n=20\), thirty edges, twelve five-cycles and degree three everywhere, hence \(\Delta_4(G(10,2))\simeq\bigvee^{841}S^{15}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The girth of a graph is the length of its shortest cycle; the condition means no triangles and no four-cycles, and five-cycles may overlap in edges or vertices. A four-cut complex is the **ordinary** cut complex determined by *disconnected* induced four-subsets, not the total cut complex determined only by independent sets. All complexes are finite abstract simplicial complexes on the common ambient vertex set \(V(G)\), and all homology/cohomology is reduced and integral unless explicitly noted. A five-cycle is automatically induced under the girth condition. The lower bound \(n\ge9\) is used in the homotopy upgrade; no smaller-order classification is asserted.

## Proof
**Step 1: the complete Alexander dual.** Put \(K=\Delta_4(G)^*=\{W\subseteq V(G):V(G)\setminus W\notin\Delta_4(G)\}\). By the cut definition, \(W\in K\) precisely when *every* four-subset of \(W\) induces a connected graph. Hence all subsets of at most three vertices belong to \(K\); the tetrahedra (four-element faces) are exactly the connected induced four-sets. As \(G\) has no triangles or squares, every such induced four-set is either a four-vertex path \(P_4\) or a claw \(K_{1,3}\).

A five-vertex set lies in \(K\) exactly when all its four-vertex deletions are connected. This forces its induced graph to be connected and to have no cut vertex. A connected graph on five vertices of girth at least five is either a tree or a cycle of length five; every five-vertex tree has a cut vertex, so precisely the five-cycles give four-dimensional faces of \(K\). There are **no six-vertex faces**: if a six-set had all its four-subsets connected, every induced five-vertex subset would be a five-cycle. Fixing a vertex \(u\) and varying the omitted vertex \(v\ne u\), the equality \(\deg_{G[S]}(u)-\mathbf{1}_{uv\in E}=2\) would hold for all five choices of \(v\); this would force every other vertex either to be adjacent to \(u\) or all to be nonadjacent, contradicting the five-cycle condition. Thus no larger faces exist. In summary, \(K\) contains the full two-skeleton of the \((n-1)\)-simplex, the tetrahedra on connected four-sets, and the four-simplices on five-cycles, with nothing higher.

**Step 2: free-face collapse of each pentagon.** Every induced four-vertex path \(a-b-c-d\) belongs to at most one five-cycle. Indeed, another completion would require a vertex adjacent to both \(a\) and \(d\), and two distinct such completions would create a four-cycle. Thus every four-simplex supported on a five-cycle has a tetrahedral facet contained in no other four-simplex. Collapse each five-cycle four-simplex together with one of its tetrahedral facets.

The four remaining tetrahedral facets of each removed five-cycle can now be collapsed successively along triangular faces consisting of one graph edge and one graph-isolated vertex. For a path \(a-b-c-d\), the triangle \(\{a,b,d\}\) is contained in exactly the original path tetrahedron and, *if it exists*, the tetrahedron obtained by replacing \(c\) with a common neighbor of \(a\) and \(d\); the alternative of another vertex adjacent to both \(b\) and \(d\) creates a forbidden four-cycle. The second tetrahedron exists exactly when the path belongs to a five-cycle, and is the adjacent path tetrahedron around that same cycle. Hence these sharing relations form an isolated five-cycle of tetrahedra for each pentagon. After one tetrahedron is removed with the four-simplex, the adjacent tetrahedron has a free triangle; removing it gives the next free triangle, and so on through all four remaining tetrahedra. The triangular faces used here are not present in tetrahedra outside that pentagon, even when two distinct five-cycles share edges.

Every remaining path tetrahedron lies in no five-cycle. For \(a-b-c-d\), the triangle \(\{a,b,d\}\) then belongs to no other tetrahedron by the preceding extension analysis. Collapse this tetrahedron along that free triangle. Finally, each claw tetrahedron collapses along the triangle on its three leaves. That triple is independent and hence cannot belong to a path tetrahedron; two different claw centers on those leaves would give a forbidden four-cycle. Therefore this triangular face is also free. We have exhibited only elementary simplicial collapses, eliminating every face of dimension above two. The residual complex \(K'\) has the same homotopy type as \(K\), is two-dimensional, and retains the same fundamental group.

**Step 3: topology and rank of the dual.** Before collapse, \(K\) contains the complete two-skeleton of a simplex, which is simply connected; attaching cells of dimension at least three leaves fundamental groups unchanged, so \(K\) and \(K'\) are simply connected. A connected simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres: its integral second homology is free, spherical classes represent a basis by Hurewicz, and the resulting wedge-to-complex map is a homology equivalence and hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead.

The full two-skeleton on \(n\) vertices has \(\binom{n-1}{3}\) independent two-dimensional homology classes. Let \(c_4(G)\) be the number of connected four-vertex induced sets. In the elementary collapses, precisely \(c_4(G)-p_5(G)\) triangular faces are removed: each five-cycle first removes one tetrahedron together with a four-simplex, and every other tetrahedron is removed together with a distinct triangle. Thus
\[
K\simeq K'\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{3}-c_4(G)+p_5(G)}S^2.
\]
Every connected induced four-set is either a claw, counted once by \(\sum_v\binom{\deg(v)}3\), or a path, counted once by its central edge and one additional neighbor at each end, namely \(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(\deg(u)-1)(\deg(v)-1)\). Absence of short cycles ensures all these choices give distinct induced four-vertex trees. This gives the displayed formula for \(B(G)\) and, since it counts two-spheres after actual collapses, proves \(B(G)\ge0\).

**Step 4: integral duality and the original homotopy type.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_4(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{n-i-3}(K;\mathbb Z).
\]
Since \(K\) is a wedge of two-spheres, \(\Delta_4(G)\) has free reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z^{B(G)}\) only in degree \(n-5\). No six-vertex set lies in \(K\), so every vertex subset of size at most \(n-6\) is a face of \(\Delta_4(G)\). It therefore contains the full \((n-7)\)-skeleton of the simplex. For \(n\ge9\) this includes the full two-skeleton and gives simple connectivity. Integral homology vanishing below \(n-5\), together with induction through the Hurewicz theorem, gives \((n-6)\)-connectivity. Choosing spherical representatives of a basis in degree \(n-5\) yields a map from \(\bigvee^{B(G)}S^{n-5}\) inducing an isomorphism on all integral homology. Whitehead's theorem gives the asserted homotopy equivalence. The same argument makes the zero-rank case contractible. This is a nonequivariant homotopy statement.

## Verification
The standard-library program `checks/check_girth5_cut.py` constructs cycles, the Petersen and dodecahedral generalized Petersen graphs, the Heawood graph, a disconnected example, and two graphs with pentagons sharing one or two edges. For each it enumerates every connected four-set, every five-set with all connected four-subsets, and every candidate six-set; identifies five-faces with induced five-cycles; verifies the degree formula; and checks two independent structures: the mod-two ranks of the actual tetrahedral/five-cell boundary maps and a sequence of legal elementary simplicial collapses leaving no tetrahedra. `checks/check_output.txt` records its execution. For the Petersen graph it obtains \(c_4=70\), \(p_5=12\), and \(B=26\); for the dodecahedral graph, \(c_4=140\), \(p_5=12\), and \(B=841\). These finite checks are corroboration, not a proof for arbitrary graphs. The uniform homotopy proof is Steps 1–4 above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer and collaborators introduced ordinary cut complexes and proved the girth-at-least-\(k+2\) skeleton criterion (arXiv:2304.13675, Proposition 6.2), as well as exact forest and cycle special cases. Their sequel (arXiv:2407.08158, Proposition 2.18) **already obtains the face-lattice description and five-cycle correction to the reduced Euler characteristic** for the girth-five four-cut case, but does not assert that all lower integral homology vanishes or prove the universal sphere-wedge homotopy type. The present result upgrades that previously known Euler formula via an explicit dual collapse.

Previously published general high-girth homotopy classifications require girth at least six when the cut size is four, and thus do not cover five-cycles. A distinct three-cut classification for triangle-free graphs with edge-disjoint four-cycles handles a different cut size and imposes an edge-disjointness condition; it does not imply the four-cut theorem here, which permits pentagons sharing edges. The Petersen four-cut sphere-wedge value 26 and cycle/forest cases were already known and are **not** claimed as new. The complete girth-five generality, with the integral homotopy upgrade and dodecahedral instance, is the proposed contribution.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to \(n\ge9\), simple undirected graphs of girth at least five, and the ordinary four-cut complex. It asserts neither shellability nor an equivariant homotopy decomposition. It does not extend to graphs with triangles or squares or classify any smaller boundary sizes. The five-cycle Euler correction, the Petersen value, and several cycle/forest subcases are prior results. The available literature comparison cannot eliminate an unindexed equivalent homotopy theorem; no independent mathematical validation is claimed.
