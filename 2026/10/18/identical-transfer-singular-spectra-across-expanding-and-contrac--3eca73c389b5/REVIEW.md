# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The defining Fourier action of the Haar-adjoint transfer operator is obtained directly from toral character orthogonality. Commuting unimodularity proves equality of the entire dual image lattices for all iterates. The explicit two-dimensional matrices satisfy the integer identities \(R^2=2I\) and \(U=I+R\), and elementary diagonalization gives different eigenvalue expansion classes. The resulting singular multisets, norms, and Schatten criteria follow by orthogonal Fourier decomposition, shortest-vector bounds, and dominated convergence. An exact-integer checker corroborates 51,623 finite lattice-membership instances; finite tests are not a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS at the level of currently available evidence, with residual risk.** A search of related result records, published mathematical statements and primary literature found no result implying this joint cross-space singular-spectrum indistinguishability and exact two-parity profile. Leuridan (arXiv:2610.01642v1) distinguishes expanding and nonexpanding toral dynamics using Bernoulli and generator properties; its abstract was read, but the full preprint remained inaccessible. Wormell (arXiv:1705.04431v4) and Bandtlow–Slipantschuk (arXiv:2004.03534v1) were read in their HTML presentations and concern approximation schemes and other spectral data, rather than the sharp nonzero singular multiset of all iterates for these commuting toral families. Standard Fourier and lattice identities are prior mathematics, and potential equivalent algebraic formulations elsewhere remain a risk. A negative search is not a mathematical uniqueness proof.

## Value
**PASS.** The issue is whether detailed quantitative smoothing spectra distinguish dramatically different invariant dynamical geometries. The result supplies a direct structural obstruction, not only matching norms: all positive singular values, with multiplicities, match at every time, while derivative expansion classes change and Lyapunov gaps become arbitrarily large. The exact two-phase Schatten limits additionally separate maximal smoothing from trace-ideal behavior. This is a useful boundary for spectral inference from regularized transfer operators, rather than an arbitrary finite calculation.

## Boundaries
The statement is for transfer maps from a fixed Fourier-weighted Hilbert space to \(L^2\), not spectral equivalence of self-maps on one space. It does not provide dynamical conjugacies or compare entropy. Full-text access to one closely related preprint was unavailable and specialist searches cannot rule out every older equivalent theorem. Independent review is still required.
