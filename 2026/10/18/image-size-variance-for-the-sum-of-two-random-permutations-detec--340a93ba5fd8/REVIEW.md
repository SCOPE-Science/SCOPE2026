# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The random sum is relabeled exactly to \(u+\sigma(u)\) with \(\sigma\) a single uniform random permutation. Avoidance of one output is an ordinary derangement event. Simultaneous avoidance of two distinct labels corresponds to a bipartite board that is the disjoint union of alternating even cycles indexed by cosets of the subgroup generated by their difference. Each cycle has the stated standard cycle-matching polynomial; inclusion-exclusion over nonattacking forbidden positions gives the exact pair-missing probability. Summing covariances of absent-output indicators yields the variance. All hypotheses, edge cases \(N\ge2\), coefficient bounds, and factorial normalizations are explicit. For the two order-four groups, exhaustive independent enumeration agrees with the exact rational formulas. Risks: general computation depends on exact coefficient extraction; bounded enumeration alone does not certify the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest directly motivating full text is Bhaumik–Guo–Guo–Jha, arXiv:2609.35421 (first submitted 2026-09-28), which studies this random mapping through classical/quantum distinguisher bounds rather than exact global image-size covariance. Donovan–Johnson–Wanless, DOI:10.1002/jcd.21418, is a substantial Latin-square cycle/permanent antecedent: its publicly accessible abstract describes two-symbol cycles and coefficients supported on two symbols, but does not explicitly state this two-symbol avoidance count, variance or group-order-four separation. Its full text was unavailable and must not be treated as excluded. The standard inclusion-exclusion/matching-counting machinery is prior work, not claimed as novel. A broader historical Latin-square/permanent theorem could imply the new formula; independent priority verification remains necessary. The mathematical comparison is between the exact joint absence law plus its variance consequence and the cited statements, not between document titles.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The arithmetic of the underlying group affects the exact variance of the image size of the sum of two independent uniform permutations, although its expected image size is universal. The general finite formula makes the entire element-order profile computationally relevant; the smallest nonisomorphic abelian groups provide an exact, sharply contrasting example with identical mean and different full support. This addresses a natural image/occupancy statistic of the recently studied permutation-sum random map and goes beyond a single arbitrary finite census. No extension to arbitrary numbers of summands, statistical estimation, or group identification is claimed.
