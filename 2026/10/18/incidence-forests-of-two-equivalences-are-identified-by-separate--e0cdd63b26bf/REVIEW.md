# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem assumes finite nonempty sets with two genuine equivalence relations. An element corresponds bijectively to a side-labelled incidence edge. The induction establishing that each round colour determines the two truncated alternating unfoldings is explicit and uses precisely one removal of the marked element from each classwise multiset. A forest has no repeated incidence edge along a nonbacktracking alternating walk, while a cyclic incidence component yields arbitrarily deep unfoldings, giving the all-size separation. Edge-rooted component recovery and multiplicity division then establish isomorphism. Global exact-code enumeration through eight elements independently tests the combinatorial criterion, not its infinite generalization. The claim is only relative to two-equivalence interpretations; that restriction is essential.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest direct conceptual prior result is forest amenability in Arvind–Köbler–Rattan–Verbitsky, *Graph Isomorphism, Color Refinement, and Compactness*, arXiv:1502.01255 (2015). Ordinary vertex-refinement on a bipartite incidence forest is not the named two-equivalence edge-refinement used here; the latter requires an additional explicit reconstruction lemma. Kieroński–Otto (LICS 2005/JSL 2012) and Pratt-Hartmann (2015) study satisfiability under equivalence constraints, not isomorphism-identifiability of incidence forests. Esperet–Gimbel–King (2009) concerns coverings of line graphs by equivalence subgraphs. Searches for aliases involving incidence multigraphs, partition pairs, two-equivalence structures, forest recognition, unfolding, and counting logic did not locate exact coverage. This does not prove global priority: a specialized edge-refinement or relational-universal-cover theorem could subsume the result. One potentially relevant original proof source was available only at abstract level.

## Value — PASS
This is an all-size identification theorem for a natural model-theoretic class defined by two equivalence relations, whose class intersections form an acyclic bipartite multigraph. The result establishes a nontrivial transfer from tree-like incidence geometry to counting logic on an edge universe with clique relations. It yields a concrete bounded-rank invariant and a complete orbit partition. The statement does not simply restate ordinary forest amenability: the underlying two-relational Gaifman graph has large cliques, and the proof recovers incidence branches from weaker neighbour-count data.

## Limitations
The theorem identifies structures only against models in which both named relations are equivalences. No optimality of the rank bound or classification of cyclic incidence structures is claimed. The small-order computation is corroboration only. Specialist review of earlier line-graph refinement and relational cover results is still warranted.
