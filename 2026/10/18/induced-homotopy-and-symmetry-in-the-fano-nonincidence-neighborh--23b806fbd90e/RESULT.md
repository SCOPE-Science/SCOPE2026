# Induced homotopy and symmetry in the Fano nonincidence neighborhood complex

## Finding
Let \(\Pi=\operatorname{PG}(2,2)\) be the Fano projective plane, with seven points and seven lines. Let \(G_{\mathrm{non}}\) be its bipartite **nonincidence** graph: a point is joined to a line precisely when the point does not lie on the line. Let \(X=\mathcal N(G_{\mathrm{non}})\) be the ordinary *open* Lovász neighborhood complex. Its two connected components, indexed by points and lines, are isomorphic. Write \(K\) for the point-side component, on the seven Fano points \(P\).

For every nonempty \(W\subseteq P\), its induced subcomplex \(K[W]\) has the following **complete integral homotopy type**:

\[
K[W]\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^1,& |W|=3\text{ and }W\text{ is a Fano line},\\
S^2,& |W|=5,\\
\bigvee^{3} S^2,& |W|=6,\\
\bigvee^{6} S^2,& |W|=7,\\
\mathrm{point},&\text{in all remaining cases}.
\end{cases}
\]

For **every** inclusion of nonempty subsets \(W\subseteq W'\subseteq P\), the induced integral homomorphism \(\widetilde H_2(K[W];\mathbb Z)\to\widetilde H_2(K[W'];\mathbb Z)\) is injective. In particular, the minimum number of vertices carrying a nonzero class in the ambient \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) is **five**. Exactly the \(\binom75=21\) five-point subsets attain this minimum; each supplies a rank-one subgroup, and their images span the full six-dimensional rational second homology.

The natural collineation group \(\Gamma=\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)\), of order \(168\), acts on \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) with character
\[
\chi=(6,-2,0,2,-1,-1)
\]
on classes ordered by element orders \((1,2,3,4,7_A,7_B)\). Over \(\mathbb C\) this is the sum of the two conjugate three-dimensional irreducibles. Thus the six-dimensional rational module is irreducible, and the twenty-one minimal-support lines generate it as a collineation-invariant rational space.

## Assumptions and scope
Represent the points of \(\Pi\) by the seven nonzero binary vectors \(P=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\), written as integers \(1,\ldots,7\). The seven lines are the triples \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) for distinct nonzero vectors \(a,b\). Their complementary four-point sets are called *caps*. The point-side neighborhood complex \(K\) consists of subsets of the points that avoid at least one Fano line, or equivalently the union of the seven full tetrahedra supported on the caps. Here an empty subset is allowed in the simplicial complex in the usual way, but the displayed homotopy classification concerns nonempty \(W\).

For \(W\subseteq P\), \(K[W]\) denotes the full *induced* subcomplex. All homotopy equivalences are ordinary integral homotopy equivalences. The homological inclusion statements are with integral coefficients and do not assert that their image lattices are saturated. The character statement uses rational coefficients and the incidence-preserving collineation group; it does not classify the action of point-line dualities on both components jointly.

## Proof
**1. Face structure.** Two Fano points lie together on precisely one line. Any two points have exactly two Fano lines avoiding them, so every pair is an edge of \(K\). A triple has a common line avoiding it precisely when it is *not* a Fano line: for a noncollinear triple there is exactly one avoiding line, and for a collinear triple there is none. Hence the triangles are exactly the \(28\) noncollinear triples. The seven three-simplices are precisely the seven four-point caps. Every triangle belongs to exactly one cap; two caps intersect in an edge. Thus the complete face vector is \((7,21,28,7)\), in dimensions zero to three.

**2. Fundamental group and collapse.** Fix \(W\) with \(|W|\ge4\). Since the one-skeleton is a complete graph, its fundamental group is normally generated by triangular loops. An allowed noncollinear triangle is filled already. If a triangle \(\{a,b,c\}\subseteq W\) is a forbidden Fano line, choose any fourth point \(q\in W\setminus\{a,b,c\}\). The three triangles \(\{q,a,b\}\), \(\{q,b,c\}\), \(\{q,c,a\}\) are noncollinear and form a cone filling the boundary of the missing triangle. Therefore \(\pi_1(K[W])=0\).

Every tetrahedron of \(K[W]\) has a triangular face belonging to no other tetrahedron. Choose one such face per tetrahedron and collapse these disjoint free-face pairs, leaving a two-dimensional, simply connected finite simplicial complex. For a simply connected two-dimensional finite CW complex, the second homology is free abelian, and the Hurewicz and homological Whitehead theorems identify its homotopy type with a wedge of two-spheres, of multiplicity its reduced Euler characteristic. The same argument with zero multiplicity gives a contractible complex.

**3. Exact ranks for every vertex subset.** Set \(s=|W|\), let \(L(W)\) be the number of Fano lines contained in \(W\), and let \(T(W)\) be the number of Fano lines containing its complement \(P\setminus W\). Directly from the face structure,
\[
\widetilde\chi(K[W])=s-\binom s2+\binom s3-L(W)-T(W)-1.
\]
For four points there are two cases: \((L,T)=(0,1)\) for a cap and \((1,0)\) otherwise, both giving zero. For five, six and seven points the pairs \((L,T)\) are respectively \((2,1)\), \((4,3)\), and \((7,7)\). The reduced Euler characteristics are therefore \(1,3,6\), respectively. For one or two points, the induced subcomplex is a point or an edge. For three points it is a filled triangle unless those three points are a Fano line, in which case it is a triangle boundary \(S^1\). This establishes the complete homotopy classification.

**4. Injectivity of all degree-two inclusion maps.** Let \(W\subseteq W'\), and suppose an integral two-cycle \(z\) supported in \(K[W]\) becomes a boundary in \(K[W']\): write \(z=\partial c\) for an integral three-chain \(c\). If a tetrahedron \(\sigma\) occurring in \(c\) has any vertex outside \(W\), take a triangular face of \(\sigma\) containing that vertex. This triangle belongs to no other tetrahedron of \(K\), while its coefficient in \(z\) is zero. Its coefficient in \(\partial c\) is therefore \(\pm\) the coefficient of \(\sigma\), forcing the latter to vanish. Consequently \(c\) is supported inside \(W\) and \(z\) was already a boundary in \(K[W]\). This proves injection integrally, including the case \(W'=P\).

By Step 3, there is no positive second homology on fewer than five vertices, while every one of the twenty-one five-point subsets has rank one. All twenty-one images in ambient homology are nonzero by Step 4.

**5. Group action and span.** Each invertible binary three-by-three matrix permutes the seven points, preserves lines and caps, and acts simplicially on \(K\). Its signed trace on an invariant oriented simplex is the sign of the induced vertex permutation. The alternating signed face trace is the Lefschetz number. Since \(K\) is connected and its only positive rational homology is in degree two, \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2)=L(g)-1\). Enumerating the \(168\) invertible binary matrices gives the values \(6,-2,0,2,-1\) for element orders \(1,2,3,4,7\), on respectively \(1,21,56,42,48\) elements; the two order-seven classes both have trace \(-1\). The standalone verifier constructs all matrices and all signed invariant-face traces directly.

The standard character table of \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) identifies this character as the sum of its two Galois-conjugate three-dimensional complex irreducibles, which together form one irreducible rational representation. The collineation group is transitive on unordered pairs of distinct Fano points, hence transitive on their twenty-one five-point complements. The orbit of any nonzero class carried by one five-point complement lies in the sum of these twenty-one inclusion images. That sum is a nonzero invariant rational subspace and must, by rational irreducibility, be all of \(H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\).

## Relationship to prior work
Lovász's open-neighborhood construction and the general fundamental-group framework of Matsushita supply the established graph-topological setting. The published ternary-plane nonincidence-neighborhood calculation treats \(\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\), not this binary plane, and determines a different mixed-dimensional complex; its explicit statement and proof do not classify induced restrictions here. Existing work on the Fano matroid independence complex computes a thirteen-dimensional top-homology representation: that complex is the two-skeleton of the present \(K\), but lacks the seven tetrahedra that change both the homotopy type and the images of all induced-subcomplex inclusion maps. A separate Heawood *incidence-graph independence* complex carries the same six-dimensional rational character, but it is a different fourteen-vertex simplicial complex with homology in degree three; equality of characters does not imply the support classification or integral inclusion theorem proved here. All existing ranks and character identifications are credited as comparisons, not represented as newly discovered general tools.

## Limitations
This is an exact theorem for the seven-point Fano plane and its fourteen-vertex point-line nonincidence graph, not an asserted formula for higher projective-plane orders. It does not describe the full point-line-duality action on the disconnected total neighborhood complex. The twenty-one rank-one image subgroups are proved to span over \(\mathbb Q\), not necessarily as a saturated integral lattice. No independent external scientific audit has been carried out; a differently indexed equivalent classification could exist.
