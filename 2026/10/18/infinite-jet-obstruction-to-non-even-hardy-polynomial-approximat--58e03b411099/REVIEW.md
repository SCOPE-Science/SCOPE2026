# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns the complete \(H^p\) objective, not a minimizer or minimum error. Both rational functions are finite Blaschke products and agree through Taylor order \(M-1\). For \(|\lambda|<1/2\), the generalized-binomial expansions converge absolutely on the boundary, so coefficient integration is valid. The diagonal terms are independent of the inner function, while unequal-index terms need only the inner Taylor coefficients up to the smaller index. Thus all probe coefficients through \(2M\) agree; only the index pairs \((M,M+1)\) and its reversal access the differing \(M\)-th inner coefficient at the next order, giving \(-2(1-a^2)\beta_M\beta_{M+1}\ne0\). The standalone rational coefficient checker reproduced this identity in 72 distinct rational parameter triples. No finite enumeration is used to justify the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge, residual risk retained)
The closest primary full-text sources concern general nonlinear \(H^p\) optimal polynomial approximants (Centner, Cheng, Felder, arXiv:2305.16068) and subsequent structural properties (Cheng, Felder, arXiv:2310.16010). Neither inspected statement compares distinct inner functions with arbitrarily long matching initial jets or gives the first exact non-even-exponent sensitivity coefficient for the full objective. Additional prior work on \(L^p\) approximants (arXiv:2112.14002) and Hilbert-space approximation (arXiv:2102.01725) does not imply the non-even-\(p\) obstruction. A separate positive finite-jet theorem at even exponents has been considered for overlap, and is not claimed here. An old equivalent binomial-moment argument could exist under different indexing; the present comparisons do not constitute proof of global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Non-Hilbert optimal polynomial approximation depends on a nonlinear power of the boundary modulus. A sharp quantitative distinction between even exponents (terminating moment polynomials) and non-even exponents explains exactly why a finite-Taylor-data reduction for the *whole objective* cannot persist. Explicit high-order, finite-dimensional inner witnesses show that the failure is intrinsic even without singular or infinite Blaschke functions. The delayed onset at probe order \(2M+1\) gives a concrete sensitivity scale beyond a qualitative counterexample.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem says nothing about whether the minimizer or its minimum value might nevertheless be finitely determined. One inaccessible or uncatalogued equivalent formulation remains possible. A source-search absence is not itself evidence of mathematical originality.
