# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an all-dimensional, all-polytope nonseparability statement. A vertex-only triangulation and the correctly normalized simplex section formula yield an exact polynomial spline with coefficients attached to projected vertices. Each coefficient is a nonzero rational function because all simplex terms have one sign at a direction exposing its vertex. Any exposed edge provides an arbitrarily near pair of top projection heights; one moves inside a fixed near-support strip while all others are excluded. Distributional differentiation produces nonzero, distinct atoms and establishes linear independence with no conditions on hypothetical separated factors. The exact-rational checker confirms concrete square and tetrahedron identities, not universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS (with residual bibliographic risk)

Fish–Ryabogin–Sun–Yaskin (arXiv:2610.10229) defines near-support finite separability and proves a smooth even-dimensional rigidity statement. Its theorem does not cover polytopes. Lasserre (arXiv:1412.4617) proves the needed simplex slice formula. Shapiro (arXiv:2605.15917) proves polytope projection densities are splines and their highest derivative is supported at projected vertices; neither fixed-direction source supplies the general noncancellation-and-infinite-rank deduction. Semantic research-record and literature searches found no direct equivalent. Because the proof uses classical spline machinery, an older unindexed statement remains a real priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Near-support separability is a geometric-tomography regularity criterion, with an unresolved smooth odd-dimensional extension. Proving that *no* polytope in any dimension at least two has this property is a natural complete negative classification for an important nonsmooth class. The stronger conclusion isolates infinite functional rank in every edge normal neighborhood, on one common arbitrarily thin boundary interval. It does not characterize general nonsmooth bodies or imply numerical approximation lower bounds.

## Checked prior literature and limitations

The three source papers above were examined in their directly relevant mathematical sections. The closest research-record hit concerned derivative jumps in a special truncated-simplex construction rather than finite rank for arbitrary polytopes; accessible historical claims also showed no overlap. The result is conditional only on standard Euclidean polytope definitions and its stated hypotheses; priority cannot be independently guaranteed.
