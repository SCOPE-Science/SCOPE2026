# Scientific review: biased lattice heat-gradient transition theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The bounded-generator exponential is the convolution semigroup of a difference of independent Poisson counts. Its sharp spatial-difference norm is the total variation of two consecutive translates of the Skellam kernel. Strict modified-Bessel Turán inequalities establish log-concavity and the exact reduction to twice the maximal probability. A Bessel-ratio Riccati equation and first-contact comparison prove strict monotonicity in time, while the Turán inequalities prove strict monotonicity in the index. These facts imply precisely one threshold per consecutive modal pair and strictly positive one-sided derivative jumps. The Fourier inversion argument isolates the Gaussian saddle, controls its complement exponentially and obtains the stated constant-order shift in threshold locations. The proof does not extrapolate finite experiments. Numerical checks are supplemental.

**Originality — PASS within examined evidence.** This result is expressed both as a sharp \(\ell^\infty\)-to-edge-Lipschitz norm and as a time-dependent modal phase classification of a Skellam law. Balachandran–Viles–Kolaczyk (arXiv:1311.1450v1) treat Bessel ratios, Skellam mass bounds and tails; the checked introduction, main-results sections and application material do not give the infinite exact phase sequence, temporal cusp formula or shifted threshold expansion. Koopman–Lit–Lucas (2014 preprint) treat statistical Skellam models, not a sharp semigroup-gradient operator norm. Related existing calculations for the symmetric lattice *time derivative* of heat kernels and finite-tree heat smoothing concern different operators or domains and do not imply the asymmetric spatial-difference norm cusps. Searches for Skellam mode thresholds and heat-gradient constants did not find a decisive stronger result. The nonexistence of matching search results does not establish absolute historical priority. An older Skellam-mode classification, modified-Bessel ratio analysis or birth–death transition-kernel result could conceivably imply some or all components; independent historical checking is outstanding.

**Value — PASS.** Sharp smoothing of discontinuous inputs is a natural endpoint invariant for Markov heat semigroups. The exact norm formula alone identifies the worst-case sign inputs, but the strict infinite succession of mode changes under arbitrarily small fixed drift, the contrast with analytic symmetric flow, explicit derivative jumps and the order-one asymptotic shift in crossing times give a substantive structural distinction. These assertions quantify the transition from probabilistic modal migration to nonsmoothness of an operator norm, not an arbitrary finite table or routine numerical refinement.

**Limits.** The proof is for positive constant nearest-neighbor rates on the entire integer lattice. The asymptotic in the transition index assumes fixed unequal rates. Full historical literature coverage, independent peer review and formal certification are not asserted. Finite assertions cannot verify the infinite threshold sequence by themselves.
