# Review of the initial non-repetitive complexity theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** From the odd-part definition, the exact identities \(f_{2j}=j\bmod2\) and \(f_{2j+1}=f_j\) hold for every nonnegative integer. No length-four factor is alternating, and this distinguishes even and odd starts of every length-at-least-eight factor. For lengths at least sixteen, the position-parity separation and exact even/odd decimation establish \(R(n)=2R(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)\) and double the earlier matching position. The 24 pairwise distinct length-eight factors and length-fifteen match at starts 8 and 24 form a finite base for all lengths 8–15; the seven shorter base cases are explicitly tabulated. Induction then proves the all-length statement. A standalone exact-window verifier checked all lengths through 320 and seventeen larger transition lengths through 8191, with `VERIFY_OK`. The tests corroborate but do not replace the induction.

**Originality — PASS subject to identified literature-access risk.** Nicholson–Rampersad (arXiv:1507.08206) explicitly handles Thue–Morse, Fibonacci, and Tribonacci, not the regular paperfolding word. Allouche (1992) counts *all distinct factors*; this does not specify the earliest duplicate in a prefix scan. Burns (arXiv:2210.14719, Theorem 3.4) determines the *last first-occurrence position* of a length-\(n\) factor, not the first duplicate. OEIS A337120 records the aggregate factor count rather than the first collision. The Allouche–Bousquet-Mélou 1994 paper constructs canonical representative positions of factors, which is close but is not shown by its abstract to determine the first repeated pair: the full paper was inaccessible during this comparison. The exact claimed law was not found in available historical claims or targeted scientific searches, but unindexed equivalence remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is the complete exact prefix-collision law for a classical automatic word at **every positive length**, together with its unique earlier matching position. It explains a structurally sharp oscillation: \(R(n)/n\) has lower and upper limit \(3/2\) and \(3\), and relative to the established total factor count \(p_f(n)=4n\) the first non-repeating scan asymptotically covers between \(3/8\) and \(3/4\) of all factor types. The parity-and-decimation argument is specific to the paperfolding construction and is not a parameter change or numerical increment to another word's formula. These first-collision statistics are not implied by ordinary factor counts, abelian complexity, or appearance-function endpoints.

**Closest literature and limits.** Nicholson–Rampersad (2015), Allouche (1992), Allouche–Bousquet-Mélou (1994, abstract only), and Burns (2022) are the material comparisons. The theorem does not extend to arbitrary folding instructions and does not enumerate all factor-return times. No independent review or validation was carried out.
