# Review of integer-order rank rigidity for fractional Volterra self-commutators

## Correctness — PASS

For all real \(\alpha>1/2\), a direct kernel integral gives the exact Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the Riemann–Liouville integral. Reflection swaps left and right integration, hence the trace-class self-commutator has an eigenvalue pairing \(\lambda\leftrightarrow-\lambda\). At integer \(m\), the difference of the two composite integral operators has vanishing weak derivative of order \(2m\); its range is the polynomial space of dimension \(2m\). Injectivity on this polynomial space follows from two-sided vanishing endpoint jets and a polynomial-degree contradiction, giving exact rank \(2m\). For noninteger order, every one of the infinitely many fractional-power coefficients is nonzero, and a Vandermonde argument makes any finitely many distinct kernel columns linearly independent. Continuity of the kernel passes this to infinite operator rank. The compact spectral theorem and reflection then establish infinite inertia on each side. A standalone program passed 948 finite cross-checks; the infinite proof does not depend on those tests.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk

The nearest fully inspected article by Khadkhuu–Tsedenbayar (2022, DOI:10.5564/jasea.v3i1.2455) treats norms for the first and second powers; these special cases are expressly prior work. Ransford–Tsedenbayar (2024, arXiv:2402.09287) establishes finite-rank real or imaginary parts of each integer Volterra power; by the commutator identity that work already implies the integer **upper** rank bound, so that bound is not claimed as new. Lyubich–Tsedenbayar (2010, DOI:10.4064/sm199-2-3), fully inspected, treats polynomial Volterra singular values, not this fractional self-commutator rank dichotomy. The 2012 ter Elst–Sauter–Zemánek article (DOI:10.1007/s00013-012-0444-5) concerns the Volterra-generated algebra and commutant; accessible abstract and metadata do not describe the dichotomy, but inaccessible full text remains a genuine overlap risk. Searches for finite-rank fractional Volterra self-commutators and direct scientific implication comparisons found no theorem covering finite-rank **if and only if integer** plus exact sign inertia. Search absence by itself does not establish priority; the positive assessment is limited to accessible scientific evidence.

## Value — PASS

Classifying exactly when an entire natural analytic semigroup of compact, Hilbert–Schmidt operators has a finite-rank self-commutator is a structural problem in nonnormal operator theory. The rank switches discontinuously between \(2m\) and infinity at every positive integer, while the self-commutator remains trace class and has perfectly paired positive and negative spectra. This is a natural complete classification with a clear conceptual distinction between polynomial and fractional kernels, rather than a small numerical improvement or a rephrasing of singular-value formulas.

## Limitations and comparison boundaries

No claim is made below or at the Hilbert–Schmidt threshold \(\alpha=1/2\), nor about individual nonzero eigenvalue magnitudes. First- and second-power results and the general integer upper bound have direct published antecedents. Unavailable historical full text and unindexed older equivalences are residual originality risks. The review is internal and has not been independently audited.
