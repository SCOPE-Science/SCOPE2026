# Scientific review: integral cohomology and Chow rings of a smooth affine quotient family

## Correctness — PASS
The quotient has two explicit gauges, \(x\ne0\) and \(u_1\ne0\), giving an affine-space open stratum and a smooth torus-cylinder boundary divisor. The second chart shows the boundary's normal line is trivial. The integral Gysin exact sequence then computes all integral cohomology groups with no torsion. The Gysin self-intersection formula forces every product of positive-degree classes to vanish. Localization of Chow groups and the nonzero integral divisor class give the stated integral Chow ring. Scissor additivity and finite-field point counts follow from the same algebraic stratification. The finite checks in `verify.py` independently test the equations and point counts, not the general Gysin argument. No claim of full homotopy type or a stronger motivic isomorphism is made.

## Originality — PASS, with literature-access risk
Nikitina's first public preprint (arXiv:2610.05344, 2026-10-04) introduces the smooth quotient family and proves its generic flexibility, nonflexibility, and the existence of an invariant divisor. The accessible construction/proof sections give invariant equations but do not state its two-stratum affine-space/torus structure, full integral cohomology ring, integral Chow ring, or uniform counting polynomial. Kaliman–Zaidenberg's affine-modification work (arXiv:math/9801076) is a broader topological method, but its inspected abstract does not determine these invariants for this later quotient. Semantic database searches for the defining equation, divisor, Betti numbers, Chow ring, affine complement, and equivalent affine-modification descriptions revealed no same-object or dominating theorem; unrelated Schubert-complement and toric Betti-number results were distinguished by object and assumptions. Literature-search absence is not uniqueness proof. Last pages of the originating PDF and full text of the older affine-modification paper were not inspected, leaving a finite residual coverage risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The computation gives explicit integral ring-level topological invariants attached to the motivating family of smooth but nonflexible generically flexible affine varieties. The result identifies a simply connected family with \(\binom n{j}\) torus-derived integral cohomology classes in degree \(j+2\), all mutually square-zero, although its Chow ring is concentrated in codimensions zero and one. The affine/torus stratification also gives uniform motivic classes and counts over finite fields. It distinguishes global topology from flexibility behavior and determines natural full invariants for every member, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice or finite numerical experiment.

## Limitations and prior literature
The result does not classify higher homotopy groups, determine an integral motive, or settle the automorphism group.
