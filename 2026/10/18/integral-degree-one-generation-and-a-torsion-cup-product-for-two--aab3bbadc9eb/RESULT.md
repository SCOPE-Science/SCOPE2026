# Integral degree-one generation and a torsion cup product for two unordered particles on \(K_6\)

## Finding
Let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|K_6|)\), where \(|K_6|\) is the topological realization of the simple complete graph on six vertices. The entire integral cohomology ring \(H^*(X;\mathbb Z)\) is generated by its degree-one classes. Specifically,
\[
H^1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{10},\qquad
H^2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\]
all higher cohomology vanishes, and the natural cup-product map
\[
\bigwedge{}^2 H^1(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H^2(X;\mathbb Z)
\]
is surjective **integrally**, including onto the order-two summand.

Number the graph vertices \(0,1,\ldots,5\), with oriented graph edge \((i,j)\) whenever \(i<j\). Let \(\alpha_{ij}\) for \(1\le i<j\le5\) denote the degree-one class that counts the total signed traversal of edge \((i,j)\) by either of the two particles. These ten classes form an integral basis. A specific nonzero order-two class is
\[
\tau=\alpha_{13}\smile\alpha_{24}-\alpha_{13}\smile\alpha_{45}
+\alpha_{15}\smile\alpha_{23}+\alpha_{15}\smile\alpha_{24}
-\alpha_{23}\smile\alpha_{45},\quad 2\tau=0\ne\tau.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(\operatorname{UConf}_2(|K_6|)\) consists of unordered two-element subsets of the *continuous* graph with distinct points. Collisions are prohibited everywhere, including graph vertices. It is neither the ordered two-point space nor a space with collision-permitting sinks. The integral cohomology is ordinary singular cohomology. No rationalization or localization is used.

The standard equivariant deleted-product deformation retraction identifies this space with the unordered cubical complex \(D\) of pairs of cells with disjoint closed supports. Its zero-cells are unordered distinct vertex pairs, its oriented one-cells are \((e,v)\) with graph edge \(e=(i,j)\), \(i<j\), and stationary vertex \(v\notin e\); its oriented squares are unordered pairs \((e,f)\) of vertex-disjoint edges, ordered lexicographically \(e<f\). Hence the integral chain dimensions in degrees zero, one, two are respectively \(15,60,45\).

## Proof
**Cellular coboundaries.** Orient a one-cell \((e,v)\) from the moving particle at the smaller endpoint of \(e\) to its larger endpoint. For an oriented square \(((u,v),(w,z))\) with \((u,v)<(w,z)\), the degree-two chain boundary is
\[
\partial[(u,v),(w,z)] =[(w,z),v]-[(w,z),u]-[(u,v),z]+[(u,v),w].
\]
Let \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) be the resulting integer cellular boundaries and set \(M=B_2^{\mathsf T}\), a \(45\)-by-\(60\) integer matrix. Exact certificates establish \(B_1B_2=0\), \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M=36\), \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(M\bmod2)=35\), a \(35\)-square minor of \(M\) of determinant \(1\), and a \(36\)-square minor of determinant \(2\). Indeed the \(36\)-minor gives rank at least \(36\), while the \(24\) linearly independent cocycles below give rank at most \(36\). The mod-two rank forces all maximal minors even, the exhibited determinant-two minor forces their gcd to be exactly two, and the determinant-one \(35\)-minor forces all previous determinantal divisors to be one. Thus
\[
\operatorname{coker}(M)=H^2(D;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The one-skeleton is connected, so \(\operatorname{rank}B_1=14\). Hence the first rational Betti number is \(60-14-36=10\). The universal coefficient theorem also gives \(H_1(D;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{10}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), and the kernel of \(B_2\) is free of rank nine.

**Integral first-cohomology basis.** For every non-tree edge \((i,j)\), \(1\le i<j\le5\), define the one-cochain \(a_{ij}\) to be \(1\) on \(((i,j),v)\) for all four stationary vertices \(v\notin\{i,j\}\), and zero on all other one-cells. The displayed square-boundary formula gives \(Ma_{ij}=0\). Combine these ten cochains with the fourteen independent vertex coboundaries (omit the lexicographically first configuration vertex) in a \(60\)-by-\(24\) matrix \(L\). Its \(24\)-square determinant certificate equals \(1\). Hence the column lattice of \(L\) is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^{60}\). Because \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\ker M=24\) and \(\ker M\) is itself primitive, \(\operatorname{im}L=\ker M\) as **integer lattices**. Quotienting by the fourteen coboundaries proves that the ten classes \(\alpha_{ij}=[a_{ij}]\) form an integral basis of \(H^1\).

**Cup-product surjectivity.** On a square \((e,f)\) with \(e<f\), the total-motion classes satisfy the product formula
\[
(a_{ij}\smile a_{kl})(e,f)
=a_{ij}(e)a_{kl}(f)-a_{ij}(f)a_{kl}(e).
\]
This is the ordinary product-cubical cup rule pulled back from the symmetric square of the graph: first sum the two one-dimensional motion cochains, then expand their product. If \(e,f\) are disjoint non-tree edges, \(\alpha_e\smile\alpha_f\) is exactly the elementary square cochain supported at \((e,f)\). There are fifteen such squares. In lexicographic square order they occupy positions \(30,31,\ldots,44\) (indices here and in the verifier start at zero). On the other thirty rows, a selected \(30\)-by-\(30\) minor of the coboundary matrix \(M\) has determinant \(1\). Therefore every integral degree-two cochain differs by an integral coboundary from an integer combination of those fifteen cup products. In other words, the cup-product map is surjective onto *all* of \(H^2\), not only onto its free quotient.

**A detected torsion product.** Let \(x\) be the integer one-cochain supported on the one-cell indices
\[
4,8,9,16,17,18,25,28,33,34,40,41,46,48,49,50
\]
and let \(q\) be the integer one-cochain supported on indices
\[
4,8,9,16,17,24,25,26,28,36,37,38,40,41.
\]
The exact matrix identity \(M(x-2q)=2t\) holds, where \(t\) is the above five-product representative of \(\tau\) (its square coordinates are \(t_{33}=1,t_{35}=-1,t_{39}=1,t_{40}=1,t_{42}=-1\), all others zero). Thus \(2\tau=0\). However \(Mx\) is even and the reduction of \(x\) modulo two does **not** belong to the \(24\)-dimensional span of \(L\) modulo two: appending it raises rank from \(24\) to \(25\). If \(t\) were an integral coboundary, \(x\) modulo two would come from an integral cocycle, a contradiction to \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}M=\operatorname{im}L\). Hence \(\tau\ne0\) and has order exactly two.

There are no cells above dimension two. The asserted degree-one generation of the full ring follows.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett–Farber (2009) develops the ordered two-particle intersection theory and describes rational cup products mainly for planar graphs. Farber–Hanbury (2010) proves that complete graphs are mature and computes ordered two-point homology ranks; it does not determine this unordered **integral** cup map. Ko–Park (2011) gives general first-homology formulas for graph braid groups, but not the present multiplicative statement. González–González (2022 preprint, 2024 journal) computes the complete cohomology algebra of **ordered** pairs on complete graphs over arbitrary coefficient rings. That is a particularly close comparison, but the integral ring of the unordered quotient is not determined by the ordered ring: two-primary torsion is invisible in the rational transfer argument, and the present torsion class pulls back to zero. Ramos–Yun (2026) concerns rational homology representations of graph configuration families; only its publicly accessible abstract was used for the comparison.

The exact literature and indexed-results searches did not reveal an equivalent computation for unordered \(K_6\), but that absence is not a proof of uniqueness. The standalone lattice calculations, not a bibliographic inference, establish the mathematical statement.

## Limitations
The theorem is confined to two unordered collision-free points on \(K_6\); it does not classify complete graphs of other orders, give all multiplication relations among the ten generators, or determine any additional group action. The order-two class is exhibited as a *linear combination* of cup products; no assertion is made that a single elementary product is torsion. Exact finite certificates prove this finite case, not an infinite-family claim. Bibliographic completeness, especially in unindexed or inaccessible literature, remains uncertain. Independent validation has not been performed.
