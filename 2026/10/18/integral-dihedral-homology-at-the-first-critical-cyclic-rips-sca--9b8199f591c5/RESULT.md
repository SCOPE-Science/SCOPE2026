# Integral dihedral homology at the first critical cyclic Rips scale

## Finding

For each integer \(r\ge 1\), put \(n=3r\), and give \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) its cyclic graph metric. Let
\[
K_r=\mathrm{VR}_{\le}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z;r)=\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^r).
\]
Here \(C_n^r\) joins distinct vertices at cyclic graph distance at most \(r\). Write \(D_n=\langle\rho,\tau\rangle\), where \(\rho(i)=i+1\) and \(\tau(i)=-i\), both modulo \(n\). Let \(E_r=\mathbb Z^{\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z}\) be the permutation lattice with induced actions \(\rho(e_i)=e_{i+1}\), \(\tau(e_i)=e_{-i}\), and let \(I_r=\ker(E_r\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\). Let \(\varepsilon\) be the rank-one integral character with \(\varepsilon(\rho)=1\), \(\varepsilon(\tau)=-1\).

**Theorem.** As integral \(D_n\)-modules,
\[
\widetilde H_2(K_r;\mathbb Z)\cong\varepsilon\otimes_{\mathbb Z}I_r,
\qquad \widetilde H_q(K_r;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(q\ne2).
\]
This includes \(r=1\), when the module is zero. Equivalently, for every integer \(j\), the character in degree two is
\[
\chi_r(\rho^j)=\begin{cases}r-1,&r\mid j,\\-1,&r\nmid j,\end{cases}
\qquad
\chi_r(\rho^j\tau)=1-\#\{i\pmod r:2i\equiv j\pmod r\}.
\]
In particular, all reflection traces vanish for odd \(r\). For even \(r\), the reflection trace is \(-1\) for even \(j\) and \(+1\) for odd \(j\). The reduced rational homology of the full dihedral quotient \(|K_r|/D_n\) vanishes in every degree.

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes are finite abstract simplicial complexes, with the empty face included in reduced homology. The scale inequality is inclusive. Cyclic graph distance, not Euclidean chord distance, defines the filtration parameter. All homology and group-ring statements are integral unless \(\mathbb Q\) is explicitly displayed. The action is the geometric action of the specified rotations and reflections on vertices. There is no assertion about other distance scales or irregularly sampled circles.

## Proof

**1. Two families of facets.** For \(i\pmod n\), put
\[
W_i=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+r\}\pmod n,
\]
and for \(i\pmod r\), put \(T_i=\{i,i+r,i+2r\}\pmod n\). Every clique of \(C_n^r\) is contained either in one \(W_i\) or in one \(T_i\). Indeed, translate one of its vertices to zero. Every other vertex then lies in the forward interval from zero to \(r\), or the backward interval from zero to \(-r\). Let \(a,b\in[0,r]\) be the largest respective distances used on these two sides (an absent side gives an immediate containing window). The two extremal vertices are adjacent only if \(a+b\le r\) or \(a+b\ge2r\). In the first case the clique lies in an interval of length at most \(r\). In the second case \(a=b=r\); any further vertex strictly between either endpoint and zero is nonadjacent to the opposite endpoint. Hence the clique is the equilateral triple. Every listed \(W_i\) and \(T_i\) is itself a clique. The case \(r=1\) follows directly as well.

Let \(A_r\subseteq K_r\) consist of all faces contained in at least one \(W_i\). Thus \(K_r\) is obtained from \(A_r\) by attaching exactly \(r\) triangular faces \(T_i\), with all their proper faces already in \(A_r\).

**2. The window complex is a circle.** For \(r=1\), \(A_1=C_3\). Suppose \(r\ge2\). Each edge \(\{i,i+r\}\) of cyclic distance exactly \(r\) is contained in exactly one window, namely \(W_i\). Any face of \(A_r\) containing that edge belongs to no other window, and it has no second distance-\(r\) edge: three pairwise distance-\(r\) vertices would form one of the excluded \(T_i\). For each \(i\), pair all such faces not containing \(i+1\) with the face obtained by adding \(i+1\). These are disjoint cone matchings on the families of faces containing a distance-\(r\) edge. Each is acyclic, and no alternating cycle can cross from one family to another. Their unpaired faces form \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^{r-1})\). By the standard collapse criterion, \(A_r\) simplicially collapses to that subcomplex.

For every \(1\le k\le r-1\), the cycle \(C_n\) has girth \(n=3r\ge3k+1\). Proposition 2.2 of Adamaszek (2011) then gives successive collapses \(\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^k)\searrow\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^{k-1})\), ending at \(C_n\). Consequently the inclusion \(C_n\hookrightarrow A_r\) is a homotopy equivalence for every \(r\ge1\). In particular \(H_1(A_r;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), generated by the oriented original cycle, \(H_q(A_r;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(q\ge2\), and the dihedral action on \(H_1(A_r;\mathbb Z)\) is trivial on rotations and multiplication by \(-1\) on reflections. The inclusion is equivariant even though no equivariant collapse sequence is required.

**3. The integral relative boundary.** Since exactly the interiors of the triangles \(T_i\) lie outside \(A_r\), the relative chain complex has
\[
H_2(K_r,A_r;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathbb Z[T_i],
\]
with other relative homology groups zero. Give \(T_i\) the orientation \([i,i+r,i+2r]\), in cyclic positive order. Its boundary consists of three forward distance-\(r\) edges. Each such oriented edge is homologous in its window to the positively directed path of \(r\) original cycle edges connecting its endpoints. Therefore the connecting homomorphism
\[
\partial:H_2(K_r,A_r;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(A_r;\mathbb Z)
\]
sends **each** \([T_i]\) to the same positive generator \([C_n]\). It is the integral summation map, not merely a rational or mod-two map.

The long exact sequence of the pair gives
\[
0\longrightarrow\widetilde H_2(K_r;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^r\xrightarrow{(1,\ldots,1)}\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow\widetilde H_1(K_r;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde H_2(K_r;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank \(r-1\), and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. The boundary surjectivity is also consistent with the previously known nonequivariant wedge of \(r-1\) two-spheres (Adamaszek, Corollary 6.7); that homotopy classification is not the new assertion.

**4. The dihedral module.** The rotation \(\rho\) permutes the oriented relative triangles by \(\rho[T_i]=[T_{i+1}]\), with indices modulo \(r\). At the wraparound, the three vertices undergo an orientation-preserving three-cycle. The reflection \(\tau\) sends \([T_i]\) to \(-[T_{-i}]\). It follows that the relative lattice is exactly \(\varepsilon\otimes E_r\), while \(H_1(A_r;\mathbb Z)\cong\varepsilon\). The connecting homomorphism is the equivariant signed augmentation. Its kernel is precisely \(\varepsilon\otimes I_r\), proving the integral claim, including all reflection signs.

**5. Character and quotient.** The rotation \(\rho^j\) fixes every index of \(\mathbb Z/r\) when \(r\mid j\) and none otherwise. The index permutation induced by \(\rho^j\tau\) fixes precisely the solutions of \(2i\equiv j\pmod r\), and the tensor factor \(\varepsilon\) reverses its trace. Subtracting the augmentation line yields the displayed character. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the only rotation-invariant vector in the permutation module is a constant vector, which lies in \(I_r\otimes\mathbb Q\) only when it is zero. Therefore the reduced rational homology has no invariant vectors even under the rotations. Rational transfer (after equivariant subdivision) identifies the quotient's rational homology with invariants, giving the last assertion.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone programs use only the Python standard library. Run `python verify_structure.py` and `python verify_homology.py` from a directory containing both scripts. The first program independently enumerates maximal cliques for \(1\le r\le12\), verifies all window/equilateral facets, and replays an explicit elementary-collapse sequence \(A_r\searrow\operatorname{Cl}(C_n^{r-1})\) for \(2\le r\le6\). The second builds the simplicial boundary operators, computes exact mod-two ranks for \(1\le r\le6\), and checks signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz traces for every rotation and reflection in those cases. Both programs terminate with an `OK` marker on success. The finite checks are not substitutes for the all-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek's *Clique complexes and graph powers* (arXiv:1104.0433, Corollary 6.7) already determines the nonequivariant homotopy type \(K_r\simeq\bigvee^{r-1}S^2\), including the critical equality of radius and one-third of the cycle length. Adamaszek, Adams, Frick, Peterson and Previte-Johnson's *Nerve complexes of circular arcs* (arXiv:1410.4336, Section 7) computes dihedral homology actions for the **nerve** complexes \(N(n,k)\), and Section 8 relates their **clique** counterparts by a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence; Example 8.3 explicitly discusses the \(r=3\) wedge of two spheres. A homotopy equivalence not compatible with the specified dihedral actions does not by itself identify their integral homology modules. The present theorem directly computes the action on the clique complexes at the critical family \(n=3r\), with a signed integral relative-chain model. Adamaszek and Adams (arXiv:1503.03669) further develop the general nonequivariant circle Rips classification. These known homotopy types and related nerve-module results are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The integral theorem is proved only at the first critical scale \(n=3r\) for equally spaced samples in the cyclic metric. It does not determine the homology representation at later critical winding fractions, the integral homology of the nonfree symmetry quotient, or equivariant homotopy type. An equivalent formulation in unindexed literature is possible. No independent external audit of this result is asserted.
