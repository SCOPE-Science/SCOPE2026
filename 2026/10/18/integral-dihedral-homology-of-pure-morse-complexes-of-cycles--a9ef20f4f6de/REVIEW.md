# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For each integer \(n\ge5\), the simple-cycle incidence complex has exactly \(n^2\) top facets, characterized by rooted spanning paths. Chari–Joswig, Theorem 4.1, supplies the torsion-free homology concentration in degrees two and \(n-2\). The formal boundaries of the even and odd \(n\)-vertex incidence sets are actual integral top cycles; disjoint facet supports and the rank-two theorem prove they are an integral basis. Group-generator permutation signs give the full top module. The rotation generator has no invariant nonempty face, implying reduced Lefschetz number \(-1\), and the reflection fixed-point set is two disjoint simplices, implying reduced Lefschetz number \(1\); these force the rank-one degree-two action to be trivial. The argument covers both parities and uses reduced, not ordinary, Lefschetz normalization. Independent exact enumeration of all dihedral elements for \(5\le n\le11\) and augmented-chain computations over two prime fields for \(5\le n\le9\) corroborate the proof. Finite verification does not establish the universal claim by itself.

## Originality — PASS, with specified limitations

The closest exact work is Chari–Joswig, *Discrete Morse Complexes*, Theorem 4.1: the underlying wedge of spheres and its Betti numbers are already known. Neither those numerical invariants nor the bare wedge homotopy type determines the natural dihedral action. Lin–Scoville's theorem concerns automorphism groups of full Morse complexes rather than integral representations on pure-complex homology. The 2008 study by Ayala and coauthors concerns structural spanning-tree decompositions; comparison used its abstract only, not its full text. Searches under pure Morse, rooted-forest, cycle, dihedral, homology-action and integral representation formulations did not identify a stronger covering theorem, but unindexed and inaccessible equivalent literature remains possible. The new claim is the integral character-and-lattice identification, not a new nonequivariant homotopy equivalence.

## Value — PASS

The dihedral group is the canonical symmetry group of a cycle, and a top-homology rank of two cannot determine its integral \(D_n\)-module structure. The theorem supplies an exact integral permutation lattice and the parity-dependent rotation twist for an unbounded natural family, identifies an invariant degree-two class, and gives a rational quotient-homology criterion. Unlike separate rational character formulas, the integral lattice identifies which two-dimensional summand is realized before rational splitting. It is a substantive equivariant refinement rather than a restatement of sphere counts.

## Limitations and relevant sources

The scope excludes the full (non-pure) Morse complex, integral quotient torsion and equivariant homotopy classification. The 2008 pure Morse paper was accessible only as an abstract, leaving bounded bibliographic uncertainty.
