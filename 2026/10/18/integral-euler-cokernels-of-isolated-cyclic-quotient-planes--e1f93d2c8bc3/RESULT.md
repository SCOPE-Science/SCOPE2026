# Integral Euler cokernels of isolated cyclic quotient planes

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq 3\) and every \(a\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) with \(\gcd(a,n)=1\), consider the complex quotient stack
\[
\mathcal X_{n,a}=[\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}/\mu_n],\qquad
\zeta\cdot(x,y)=(\zeta x,\zeta^a y).
\]
Both coordinate characters are faithful; the coarse quotient has an isolated cyclic quotient singularity at the origin. Let \(H_{n,a}\) denote the **integral Hessian**, equivalently the polar matrix, of the Euler quadratic form of Bachhuber--Gholampour on the character multiplicity lattice \(\mathbb Z^n\). Put \(g=\gcd(n,a+1)\) and \(m=n/g\). Then the complete integral cokernel is
\[
\operatorname{coker}_{\mathbb Z}(H_{n,a})\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,&m\text{ odd},\quad g=1,\\
\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{g-2}\oplus\mathbb Z/(2n)\mathbb Z,&m\text{ odd},\quad g\geq2,\\
\mathbb Z^{g+1}\oplus\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z,&m\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the radical has rank \(1\) if \(m\) is odd and rank \(g+1\) if \(m\) is even. The torsion order equals \(n2^{g-1}\) in the first regime and \(n/2\) in the second. The exceptional regime necessarily has even \(n\) and even \(g\). The formula includes the exactly crepant action \(a=n-1\) and arbitrary isolated cyclic quotient plane actions with both weights units modulo \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope

The coefficient lattice has ordered basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}\), with indices modulo \(n\); the shift \(S\) sends \(e_i\) to \(e_{i+1}\). We use the Hessian convention \((H_{n,a})_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j Q_{n,a}\), not the half-Hessian convention. The source's coefficient formula gives the following integral quadratic polynomial for every integral vector \(v=(v_j)\):
\[
Q_{n,a}(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
\bigl(v_j^2-v_jv_{j+1}-v_jv_{j+a}+v_jv_{j+a+1}\bigr).
\]
This formula incorporates coincident characters by combining their terms before taking derivatives. Its Hessian is
\[
H_{n,a}=2I-S-S^{-1}-S^a-S^{-a}+S^{a+1}+S^{-(a+1)}.
\]
No assertion is made about unequal weights with \(\gcd(a,n)>1\), noncyclic groups, or automorphisms of the resulting abelian group beyond its displayed invariant factors.

## Proof

**1. Integer cyclic factorization and a basic quotient.** Work in the free abelian group \(R=\mathbb Z[t]/(t^n-1)\). Set \(b=a+1\), \(A=(t-1)(t^a-1)\), and \(B=t^b+1\). The displayed Hessian is multiplication by the Laurent polynomial
\[
t^{-b}(t-1)(t^a-1)(t^b+1).
\]
Since multiplication by \(t\) is unimodular in \(R\), its cokernel is \(R/(ABR)\). The map \(t^a-1:R\to R\) has image precisely the augmentation ideal \(I=(t-1)R\): because \(\gcd(a,n)=1\), differences along steps of length \(a\) connect all \(n\) cyclic positions and generate every coordinate difference. Hence \(AR=(t-1)I=(t-1)^2R\). The usual cyclic-difference calculation gives
\[
\ker A=\mathbb Z N,\quad N=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1},\qquad
\operatorname{coker}A\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
For clarity, the last equality follows from \(R/I\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(I/(t-1)I\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\): the latter is generated by the class of \(t-1\), with relation \(n(t-1)=0\) modulo \((t-1)I\). From \(ABR\subseteq AR\) there is an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow R/(BR+\mathbb ZN)
\xrightarrow{[u]\mapsto[Au]}
R/(ABR)\longrightarrow R/(AR)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1}
\]
All these are exact sequences of finitely generated abelian groups.

**2. Odd cycle length: the full torsion structure.** Multiplication by \(B=1+t^b\) acts separately on the \(g=\gcd(n,b)\) cycles of the permutation \(j\mapsto j+b\) of length \(m=n/g\). If \(m\) is odd, the cokernel on each cycle is \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\): successive equations \(u_j+u_{j+b}=0\) lead to the last relation \(2u_j=0\). Thus \(R/BR\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^g\). The norm element \(N\) represents the all-ones vector in this quotient, since each cycle contains \(m\) odd entries. Therefore
\[
R/(BR+\mathbb ZN)\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{g-1}.
\]
Sequence (1) implies that the torsion \(T\) of \(\operatorname{coker}H_{n,a}\) is an extension of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) by \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{g-1}\). It has order \(n2^{g-1}\), and its free rank is one.

When \(n\) is odd the extension splits, since multiplication by \(n\) is invertible on the elementary two-group. For \(g>1\) this gives the displayed invariant factors after combining the coprime \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and one \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). When \(g=1\), the answer is \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\).

For even \(n\), the unit \(a\) is odd. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the matrix is multiplication by \((t+1)(t^a+1)(t^b+1)\) on \(\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^n+1)\). Because \(m\) is odd, the greatest common divisor of \(t^n+1\) and this product is precisely \(t^g+1\), **including multiplicities**: the latter already contains the full \(2\)-power multiplicity of the root \(t=1\) in \(t^n+1\), while \(t^a+1\) has no other common root with \(t^n+1\). Consequently
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\ker(H_{n,a}\bmod2)=g.
\]
There are exactly \(g-1\) nontrivial even Smith factors after subtracting the free rank one. A split extension would have \(g\) such factors. Hence it is nonsplit. Every extension of one cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) by an elementary two-group is, up to automorphism of the elementary group, either split or has one nonzero extension coordinate. The nonsplit form is therefore \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{g-2}\oplus\mathbb Z/(2n)\mathbb Z\). This completes the odd-\(m\) regime.

**3. Even cycle length: cyclic torsion.** Suppose \(m\) is even. Since \(\gcd(a,n)=1\), necessarily \(n\), \(a+1\), and \(g\) are even (and \(a\) is odd). On each length-\(m\) cycle the cokernel of \(1+t^b\) is freely generated by the alternating-sign class; hence \(R/BR\cong\mathbb Z^g\). The norm element \(N\) maps to zero because the alternating sum of an even number of ones is zero. Consequently sequence (1) becomes
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^g\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}H_{n,a}
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its finite torsion embeds into \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), so it is **cyclic**, and the free rank is \(g+1\). It remains to determine the torsion order.

**4. Integral kernel and the torsion order in the even regime.** Over \(\mathbb C\), the Fourier eigenvalues are obtained by substituting \(n\)-th roots of unity into the factorization of Step 1. Because \(\gcd(a,n)=1\), their zeros are precisely \(t=1\) and the \(g\) roots of \(t^g=-1\). Thus the nullity is \(g+1\). All integer kernel vectors \(w\) have the form
\[
w_{r+kg}=\begin{cases}u_r,&k\text{ even},\\v_r,&k\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad 0\le r<g,\quad 0\le k<m,
\]
where the integers \(u_r+v_r\) are independent of \(r\). A \(\mathbb Z\)-basis consists of the vector \(B_0\) that is one at all even \(k\) and zero at odd \(k\), together with the \(g\) vectors \(A_r\) that alternate \(+1,-1\) in residue class \(r\) and vanish elsewhere. Their Gram matrix has \(\langle B_0,B_0\rangle=n/2\), \(\langle B_0,A_r\rangle=m/2\), and \(\langle A_r,A_s\rangle=m\delta_{rs}\). Its determinant is
\[
\det\operatorname{Gram}(\ker_{\mathbb Z}H_{n,a})=nm^g/4.
\tag{2}
\]
For a symmetric integral matrix with a primitive integral kernel, the order of the torsion of its cokernel is the absolute product of nonzero eigenvalues divided by the Gram determinant of an integer basis of the kernel. This follows by comparing the covolume of its image in the saturated orthogonal lattice to the eigenvalue determinant of the induced map on the real orthogonal complement.

Write \(K=\{\zeta:\zeta^g=-1\}\), so \(|K|=g\). Among the \(n\)-th roots of unity, the nonzero-eigenvalue factors of \((t-1)(t^a-1)\) have absolute product \(n^2/4\): on all nontrivial roots the two products have absolute value \(n\) apiece, while on \(K\) each product has absolute value two. For the second product, use \(\zeta^a=-\zeta^{-1}\) on \(K\) and \(g\) even. Since \(b/g\) is coprime to \(m\), the product of \(|1+\zeta^b|\) over roots outside \(K\) is \(m^g\); omitting \(\zeta=1\) divides this by two. Thus the absolute product of all nonzero eigenvalues is
\[
\frac{n^2}{4}\cdot\frac{m^g}{2}=\frac{n^2m^g}{8}.
\tag{3}
\]
Dividing (3) by (2) yields \(n/2\). Since the torsion is cyclic by Step 3, it is exactly \(\mathbb Z/(n/2)\mathbb Z\), as claimed.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` constructs \(H_{n,a}\) directly from its seven shift terms and calculates its **integer Smith normal form**, using exact arithmetic in SymPy. The program checked all admissible pairs with \(3\le n\le22\), plus six additional pairs through \(n=36\), totaling 154 cases. In particular, it confirmed
\[
\begin{aligned}
(n,a)=(9,2)&:\quad \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/18,\\
(10,3)&:\quad\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/20,\\
(12,5)&:\quad\mathbb Z^7\oplus\mathbb Z/6,\\
(15,4)&:\quad\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^3\oplus\mathbb Z/30.
\end{aligned}
\]
These finite checks verify the implementation and examples but are **not** offered as proof for all parameters. Steps 1--4 supply the infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Bachhuber and Gholampour, *G-Equivariant Hilbert Schemes and Quadratic Forms* (arXiv:2610.12736v1, first public date October 8, 2026), Theorem 4.6 and Sections 6--7, give the coefficient formula and classification of planar abelian quotient-stack Euler forms. Their full-text statement concerns the quadratic form, not its integral cokernel; the factorization and complete all-unit-weight Smith classification here are additional conclusions.

Noferini and Williams, *Smith forms of matrices in Companion Rings, with group theoretic and topological applications* (Linear Algebra and its Applications 708 (2025), 372--404, DOI:10.1016/j.laa.2024.12.003), develop general circulant-matrix techniques and analyze several other specific families. Their general toolkit is relevant, but their inspected introduction, principal Smith-form theorems, and application families do not give this cyclic Euler-polar family or its gcd/parity formula. Known special-weight calculations do not imply an all-unit-weight classification without analyzing the interaction of the three factors of the Hessian.

## Limitations

The classification assumes \(\gcd(a,n)=1\), so that the plane action is isolated on both coordinate axes. It does not classify actions with one nonunit weight or general abelian groups. The formula is for the stated Hessian normalization, not a half-Hessian or arbitrary congruent scaling. This is a computation of an integral Euler-form invariant; no new existence, smoothness, or dimension result about the Hilbert schemes themselves is claimed. An equivalent pre-existing computation in unindexed circulant-group literature cannot be completely excluded.
