# Mathematical review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The claim concerns the exact reduced integral homology of \(\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{KG}(7,3))\). The complete face enumeration, dual deterministic acyclic Morse matchings, exact signed integral differentials, and full integer unimodular reductions provide a certificate for every asserted degree. There are \(1,278,686\) augmented faces; the forward and reverse Morse reductions yield identical homology ranks and only unit nonzero Smith invariant factors. The executable reconstructs the data and checks acyclicity and \(\partial^2=0\), not merely displayed numerical ranks. This establishes torsion-freeness without sampling any prime.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual priority risk
Feng and Wang (2024, arXiv:2404.10566) explicitly study the same thirty-five-vertex intersecting triple complex and report its known field Betti ranks; those ranks are **not new** here. Feng (2026, arXiv:2610.17394) proves the corresponding family-wide rational ranks, but explicitly raises integral torsion-freeness as an open question (Question 19). Neither source supplies or implies all-prime integral torsion-freeness at this finite parameter. Targeted mathematical-record searches for Kneser, odd-graph, and metric aliases identified no exact integral Smith calculation. Stable Kneser/Schrijver complexes, Johnson graph metric Rips complexes at other scales, and two-subset Kneser Čech complexes have different face criteria. Priority remains subject to an unindexed-source risk; search absence alone is not taken as a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The finding settles the first genuinely nontrivial \(k=1\) instance of a recently stated, natural integral torsion question about three-subset Kneser independence complexes. Exact integral primitivity is not a routine restatement of the already known field ranks: odd torsion could survive unchanged rational and mod-two homology. The result supplies a reproducible finite anchor and clarifies the all-prime obstruction at the smallest positive parameter. No unsupported wider-family theorem or homotopy wedge classification is claimed.
