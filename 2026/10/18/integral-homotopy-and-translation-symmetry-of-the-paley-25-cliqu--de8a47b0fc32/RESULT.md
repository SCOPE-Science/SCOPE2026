# Integral homotopy and translation symmetry of the Paley-25 clique complex

## Finding
Let \(P_{25}\) be the Paley graph on \(F=\mathbb F_5[t]/(t^2-2)\), with vertices adjacent if their difference is a nonzero square. Let \(K=\operatorname{Cl}(P_{25})\), let \(T=(F,+)\) act by translations, and let \(Y=|K|/T\) be the geometric orbit space. Then
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{64}S^2.
\]
For each one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_5\)-subspace \(U\le T\), define the four-dimensional rational representation \(V_U\) as the sum of the four distinct nontrivial complex characters of \(T/U\), regarded as a rational representation. Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of the three one-dimensional subspaces whose nonzero vectors are squares in \(F\), and let \(\mathcal N\) be the other three. Then
\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb Q^{\oplus4}\oplus
\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal S}V_U^{\oplus3}\oplus
\bigoplus_{U\in\mathcal N}V_U^{\oplus2}
\]
as rational \(T\)-modules. In particular, the trace on \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) equals \(64\) at zero, \(4\) for a nonzero square translation, and \(-1\) for a nonsquare translation. The orbit space is simply connected and satisfies
\[
\pi_1(Y)=0,\qquad
\widetilde H_j(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Q^4,&j=2,\\0,&j\ne2.\end{cases}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The finite field is represented by pairs \((a,b)\in\mathbb F_5^2\) meaning \(a+bt\), where \(t^2=2\). The polynomial is irreducible because \(2\) is not a square in \(\mathbb F_5\). There are \(12\) nonzero squares, partitioned into three \(\mathbb F_5\)-lines generated by \(1\), \(1+t\), and \(4+t\). The first statement is ordinary integral homotopy, not equivariant homotopy equivalence. The group action statement concerns rational homology, not an integral lattice decomposition. The orbit-space homology statement is rational only.

## Proof
**1. Complete combinatorial model.** In the basis \(1,t\), field addition is coordinatewise modulo \(5\) and multiplication is
\[
(a,b)(c,d)=(ac+2bd,ad+bc)\pmod 5.
\]
The nonzero squares, encoded by \(a+5b\), are
\[
\{1,2,3,4,6,9,12,13,17,18,21,24\}.
\]
Consequently the graph is the union of the three families of parallel \(5\)-point affine lines in the directions \(\langle1\rangle\), \(\langle1+t\rangle\), and \(\langle4+t\rangle\). Clique enumeration yields the exact numbers of nonempty simplices in dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4\):
\[
(25,150,250,75,15).
\]
There are no faces of higher dimension. The certificate `morse_certificate.json` lists every matched incident pair and every critical simplex, using vertices \(a+5b\in\{0,\ldots,24\}\).

**2. Complete discrete-Morse certificate.** In the independently executable `verify_p25.py`, the graph and all \(515\) nonempty simplices are reconstructed from finite-field arithmetic without trusting the certificate. The verifier proves that the \(225\) listed matching pairs are distinct codimension-one incidences, the remaining \(65\) simplices are exactly the listed critical simplices, and the matching covers every simplex exactly once either as a pair member or as a critical simplex. It orients all \(1425\) face-poset cover relations downward, reversing just the \(225\) matched relations. A full topological sort visits all \(515\) faces, proving that the directed graph is acyclic. There is exactly one critical vertex and exactly \(64\) critical two-simplices; there are no critical cells of dimensions \(1\), \(3\), or \(4\). Forman's discrete-Morse theorem therefore gives a CW model with one point and \(64\) two-cells attached to that point, which is \(\bigvee^{64}S^2\). In particular \(K\) is simply connected and its integral homology is free of rank \(64\) in degree \(2\), with no other reduced integral homology.

**3. Translation trace and rational representation.** Every nonzero \(v\in F\) has additive order \(5\). An invariant nonempty simplex under translation by \(v\) must be a union of full \(5\)-cycles, hence is a five-vertex simplex, since \(K\) has no larger faces. Such a five-set is a clique exactly when \(v\) is a square: it is one of the five cosets of \(\langle v\rangle\), each a four-dimensional simplex. Its vertex permutation is a \(5\)-cycle, whose orientation sign is \(+1\), so it contributes \(+1\) to the alternating simplicial trace. Thus the Lefschetz number is \(5\) for the twelve nonzero square translations and \(0\) for the twelve nonsquare translations. As \(H_0\) is trivial of dimension one and only \(\widetilde H_2\) survives, the traces on \(H_2\) are \(4\) and \(-1\), respectively; at the identity the trace is \(64\).

There are six rational nontrivial irreducibles \(V_U\) of \(T\), each of dimension \(4\). Their character is \(4\) on translations in \(U\) and \(-1\) off \(U\). The average of the calculated character gives the trivial multiplicity
\[
(64+12\cdot4-12)/25=4.
\]
Writing \(m_U\) for the multiplicity of \(V_U\), the identity value gives \(\sum_U m_U=15\), while a nonzero \(v\in U\) gives \(\chi(v)=4-15+5m_U=-11+5m_U\). Hence \(m_U=3\) for a square direction and \(m_U=2\) otherwise, proving the displayed rational decomposition.

**4. Orbit-space fundamental group and rational homology.** Translation by \(1\) fixes the barycenter of the simplex \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\); translation by \(1+t\) fixes the barycenter of its own five-element additive orbit, also a simplex. These two linearly independent translations generate \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_5\). The orbit-space theorem of Armstrong applies to the simply connected finite simplicial complex \(K\): \(\pi_1(Y)\) is \(T\) divided by the normal subgroup generated by elements with geometric fixed points. Therefore \(\pi_1(Y)=0\). For a finite simplicial group action, the rational homology of the geometric orbit space is the invariant rational homology of the original complex, as seen after barycentric subdivision and averaging. The trivial multiplicity is \(4\); no other reduced rational homology occurs. This proves the orbit-space formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Cioabă and Mim (arXiv:2606.27328, first public June 25, 2026), Theorem 3.12, determine homology over arbitrary fields of clique complexes of Latin-square graphs of order at least five: the second Betti number is \((n-1)^3-I\), where \(I\) counts intercalates. The Paley-25 graph is the Latin-square graph from the additive square of order five; its cyclic Latin square has no intercalates, giving the already-known rank \(64\). That homology calculation is not claimed as new. The new results are an explicit optimal acyclic Morse matching giving the **integral homotopy type**, the complete rational translation character and decomposition, and the simply connected translation quotient with its rational homology. Their paper states no fundamental-group or homotopy-type classification of this case. Cioabă, Guo, Ji and Mim (arXiv:2508.05871) analyze strongly regular clique-complex Laplacians and first cohomology, including Paley-25, but do not give the asserted integral homotopy or translation module. The orbit-space fundamental-group theorem is due to Armstrong and is used, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
This is a complete result for the canonical graph on \(25\) vertices only: no homotopy classification of all Latin-square or Paley graphs is asserted. The exhibited Morse matching is not equivariant. The rational representation does not determine the integral homology lattice under translations. The integral higher homology of the geometric orbit space is not determined; the orbit-space calculation is a simple-connectivity and rational-homology statement. Originality remains conditional on the available indexed and inspected primary literature; a previously unindexed equivalent computation may exist.
