# Integral homotopy of the bidirected triangular-prism multipath complex

## Finding
Let \(P=C_3\square K_2\) be the triangular-prism graph with each undirected edge replaced by both opposing directed arcs. Its multipath complex \(\mathcal X(\overleftrightarrow P)\), consisting of arc sets inducing vertex-disjoint directed paths, has the integral homotopy type
\[
\mathcal X(\overleftrightarrow P)\simeq
\bigvee^{5}S^{2}\vee\bigvee^{22}S^{3}\vee\bigvee^{6}S^{4}.
\]
In particular, its nonzero reduced integral homology is free of ranks \(5,22,6\) in degrees \(2,3,4\), respectively. The assertion determines the actual homotopy type, not only the homology ranks.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). The undirected edges are \(01,02,12,34,35,45,03,14,25\), each assigned both orientations. The eighteen directed arcs are ordered lexicographically. A simplex is a subset of arcs such that every vertex has at most one selected outgoing arc and at most one selected incoming arc, and the resulting selected digraph has no directed cycle. Empty and isolated path components are permitted. Simplices are oriented by increasing arc index; all integral chains and cup products use these orientations. The graph is the unique connected nonbipartite cubic graph on six vertices, up to isomorphism; the result is for this graph alone.

## Proof
**Face enumeration and acyclic reduction.** The complete set of simplices can be enumerated by testing all \(2^{18}\) arc subsets using the indegree, outdegree and directed-cycle criteria. An independent recursive enumeration inserting arcs in increasing index gives the same \(687\) simplices, including the empty simplex, with counts by cardinality \((1,18,108,260,240,60)\). On the nonempty faces, perform an acyclic partial matching stage by stage for the eighteen arcs in their order. At stage \(i\), pair each still-unmatched face not containing arc \(i\) with its still-unmatched union with arc \(i\), whenever both exist. Exactly \(326\) disjoint face pairs result. Orient each matched codimension-one Hasse edge upward and every other Hasse edge downward. Topological sorting exhausts all \(686\) nonempty faces and \(2256\) incidences; hence there is no directed cycle. The unmatched critical cells number \(1,0,5,22,6\) in dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4\), respectively. By discrete Morse theory the simplicial complex has a homotopy-equivalent CW model with precisely those cells.

**Integral Morse boundary.** If a lower simplex \(a\) is matched to upper simplex \(b\) with incidence \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), recursively project its cellular generator to critical generators through the exact relation \(P(\partial b)=0\), taking matched upper simplices to zero. Acyclicity makes the recursion terminate. Computing projected boundaries of every critical three-cell and four-cell over the integers gives zero. The implementation additionally checks the projected boundaries of every nonempty positive-dimensional face vanish, consistent with a chain projection to the zero-differential critical complex. Hence the Morse complex has differential zero, proving the stated free integral homology.

**Attaching maps in dimension three.** With only one zero-cell and no one-cells, the two-skeleton of the Morse model is \(\bigvee^5 S^2\). Each three-cell attaches by an element of \(\pi_2(\bigvee^5S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^5\), detected completely by the integral degree vector, namely its three-to-two Morse differential. The differential is zero. Thus the three-skeleton is \(\bigvee^5S^2\vee\bigvee^{22}S^3\).

**Attaching maps in dimension four.** The Hilton--Milnor decomposition in this dimension identifies \(\pi_3(\bigvee^5S^2\vee\bigvee^{22}S^3)\) as a torsion-free abelian group whose generators comprise five Hopf maps on the two-sphere summands, ten pairwise Whitehead products of distinct two-sphere inclusions, and twenty-two degree-one maps to the three-spheres. The integral four-to-three Morse differential vanishes, ruling out all three-sphere components. The remaining fifteen Hopf and Whitehead coefficients are detected by the integral products of a basis of five degree-two cohomology classes evaluated on the integral four-cell generators. This standard cup/attaching formula uses diagonal products for Hopf invariants and off-diagonal products for Whitehead coefficients.

For the actual complex, take five integral cocycles given by the dual coordinate functionals of the five critical two-cells, pulled back by the integer Morse projection. Their cocycle property is checked on all three-simplices, and their cohomology independence is confirmed by the boundary rank calculation. An exact rational sparse reduction of the original four-to-three simplicial boundary yields six independent four-cycles; every cycle boundary is checked to vanish exactly. Every one of the fifteen degree-two Alexander--Whitney products evaluates to zero on each of those six cycles. Thus all cup products are zero over the rationals, hence also integrally because integral fourth cohomology is free and the six cycles span it over the rationals. The Morse model has no four-to-three boundary; its six four-cell classes form an integral fourth-homology basis. Consequently all fifteen attaching coefficients of each four-cell vanish. Every four-cell is therefore attached nullhomotopically. This proves the claimed wedge equivalence.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_prism.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages. It exhaustively enumerates all faces using two algorithms; checks all \(2256\) Hasse incidences and the resulting acyclic matching; carries out integral recursive Morse projection and zero-boundary checks; verifies five degree-two integral cocycles; produces six exact rational four-cycles; checks all \(15\times6\) cup-product evaluations; and independently computes original simplicial boundary ranks over fields of orders \(2\) and \(101\). Its final marker is `VERIFIED_PRISM_MULTIPATH_WEDGE`.

The independent finite-field boundary ranks in dimensions \(1,2,3,4\) are \((17,91,164,54)\). Field ranks are checks, not the proof of torsion-freeness. The latter follows from the integer Morse matching and zero differentials; the homotopy statement additionally requires the integral attaching-map argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi, Collari, Di Trani and Smith introduced this multipath complex framework and classified several directed paths, polygons, oriented grids and other families [1]. Their computations for oriented grids and certain complete bipartite digraphs do not supply the bidirected triangular-prism result. Caputi, Collari and Smith studied multipath complexes of bidirectional paths and polygons [2], rather than this trivalent prism. The alternative description as a cycle-free directed path-system complex is equivalent by the stated indegree, outdegree and acyclicity conditions; unlike an ordinary matching complex or a discrete-Morse matching complex, its simplices may include consecutive directed arcs. The previously studied six-vertex triangular-prism discrete-Morse matching complex therefore computes a different object. No claim is made that a search exhausts unpublished or unindexed equivalent work.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns exactly the fully bidirected triangular-prism graph; it does not classify all prism lengths, orientations, or other cubic graphs. Full homotopy equivalence is established by a finite exact combinatorial certificate together with standard nontrivial facts from discrete Morse theory and low-dimensional homotopy theory. The computation is independently reproducible with the included standard-library script, but it has not received an independent mathematical audit. Unindexed equivalent calculations remain a priority risk.
