# Integral homotopy of the scale-two Čech nerve of \(J(6,3)\)

## Finding

Write \(G=J(6,3)\) for the Johnson graph whose twenty vertices are the three-element subsets of \([6]=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), with an edge between distinct triples precisely when their intersection has size two. Let \(K=\mathcal N(G,2)\) denote the intrinsic graph Čech complex at scale two, meaning the nerve of closed metric balls of radius one centered at graph vertices. Then there is an **integral homotopy equivalence**

\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{11}S^3\vee\bigvee^{30}S^4.
\]

In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{11}\) and \(\widetilde H_4(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{30}\), with every other reduced integral homology group zero. The complex is pure of simplicial dimension nine, so this is a mixed-dimensional homotopy classification rather than a consequence of purity or of rational Betti numbers alone.

## Assumptions and scope

All sets and graph vertices are finite and labeled. The closed radius-one neighborhood of a triple \(A\) is

\[
N[A]=\{B\in\tbinom{[6]}3:|A\cap B|\ge2\}.
\]

There are exactly ten vertices in each such neighborhood: \(A\) itself and the nine triples obtained by exchanging one element. For even Čech scale two, the graph-ball nerve is exactly the simplicial complex generated by these twenty ten-vertex sets, by the graph Čech description in [1, Lemma 2.2]. Every ten-set is distinct, and no one contains another, so these are exactly its maximal simplices. We use integral cellular chains with increasing labeled simplicial-vertex order and boundary \(\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_d]=\sum_{i=0}^d(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_i,\ldots,v_d]\). The assertion is specific to \(J(6,3)\) at scale two.

## Proof

**Complete face set.** Order all twenty triples lexicographically, encoding every nonempty face by a twenty-bit integer. Enumerate every nonempty submask of every closed neighborhood \(N[A]\) and deduplicate. This yields exactly \(16{,}140\) nonempty faces, with face vector by dimensions zero through nine

\[
(f_0,\ldots,f_9)=(20,180,960,2850,4500,4110,2400,900,200,20).
\]

An independent exhaustive common-center test checks membership for every one of the \(2^{20}-1\) possible nonempty vertex subsets: a set \(\sigma\) is a simplex if and only if \(\bigcap_{v\in\sigma}\{c:v\in N[c]\}\ne\varnothing\). The two descriptions agree on all possibilities.

**Explicit matching.** Initialize the set of unmatched faces to the complete nonempty face set. For each lexicographic vertex index \(j=0,1,\ldots,19\), simultaneously pair all currently unmatched faces \(\sigma\) not containing \(j\) for which \(\sigma\cup\{j\}\) also remains unmatched, and remove the paired faces before moving to \(j+1\). The numbers of pairs at successive stages are

\[
4390,1487,688,68,659,314,35,287,33,26,18,8,2,7,1,4,6,1,7,8.
\]

They total \(8049\), leaving precisely one unmatched dimension-zero face, eleven unmatched dimension-three faces and thirty unmatched dimension-four faces. Pairing is along a codimension-one cover, so it defines a partial matching on the face poset.

**Acyclicity certificate.** Direct every matched face-poset covering upward and every remaining covering downward. The standalone checker enumerates all \(87{,}800\) covering incidences between nonempty faces, constructs their directed graph and exhaustively topologically sorts it. All \(16{,}140\) faces are removed by the sort. Therefore the matching has no closed gradient path and is acyclic. This fully finite enumeration is a proof for this specific complex, not a sample extrapolation.

**Integral Morse differential.** Apply Forman's cellular Morse reduction with the signed incidence coefficients above. For every matched lower three-face \(\sigma\) paired with an upper four-face \(\tau\), the boundary incidence is \(\epsilon=\pm1\); cancel the pair by rewriting \(\sigma=-\epsilon\sum_{\rho\ne\sigma}[\rho:\tau]\rho\). Recursively expand these relations, omitting all matched upper three-faces, until the eleven critical three-faces give the integral basis. Acyclicity ensures termination, and the checker performs the recurrence over exact signed integers (no modular or floating-point arithmetic). For each of the thirty critical four-faces, the reduced boundary is the zero vector in \(\mathbb Z^{11}\). Hence the complete integer Morse differential \(C_4^{\mathrm M}\to C_3^{\mathrm M}\) is exactly zero.

**Homotopy conclusion.** The associated discrete-Morse CW complex has one zero-cell, eleven three-cells and thirty four-cells and no cells in dimensions one or two. Its three-skeleton is \(\bigvee^{11}S^3\). Each four-cell is attached along an element of \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{11}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{11}\), where the Hurewicz identification records precisely its integer cellular boundary column. Since all thirty columns vanish, every attaching map is nullhomotopic. Therefore the CW complex, and hence \(K\), is homotopy equivalent to the stated wedge. The Euler check \(\widetilde\chi(K)=30-11=19\) agrees with the face vector.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Shukla and Singh [1] define graph Čech nerves in the required metric normalization and prove important homotopy calculations for hypercube graphs; their theorems do not determine the scale-two Čech complex of the non-hypercube Johnson graph \(J(6,3)\). Shuldiner and Oldford [2] analyze Johnson-graph cliques, which do not constitute the closed-neighborhood nerve studied here. Goyal, Shukla and Singh [3] treat flag complexes of line graphs, also a different construction. Previte's broader \(D\)-neighborhood language [4] includes the closed-neighborhood construction, but the inspected available material did not give this Johnson-graph classification.

A separately published scale-one Johnson-graph Vietoris–Rips result [5] gives \(\operatorname{VR}(J(6,3);1)\simeq\bigvee^{19}S^2\); the present complex is instead the graph **Čech** nerve at scale **two**, and its reduced homology is in degrees three and four. This rules out a superficial same-graph or same-parameter identification. Related exact Čech calculations for Kneser graphs, hypercubes and two-subset triangular Johnson graphs concern different graph families or constructions and do not imply the displayed homotopy equivalence. No equivalent published homotopy classification was identified in the specifically inspected literature and claim searches.

## Limitations

The result does not determine the action of the Johnson-graph automorphism group on integral homology, an equivariant splitting, the Čech inclusion maps at other scales, or the full persistent barcode. The exact computational certificate addresses the fixed twenty-vertex graph, not an all-parameter theorem for general Johnson graphs. The 2014 neighborhood-complex thesis was inspected only in accessible parts, and unidentified or unindexed equivalent publications remain a residual originality risk. The displayed wedge decomposition is integral but has not been independently audited.
