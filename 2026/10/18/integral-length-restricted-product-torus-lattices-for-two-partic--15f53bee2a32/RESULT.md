# Integral length-restricted product-torus lattices for two particles on the complete tripartite graph

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{3,3,3}\) have vertex partition \(\{0,1,2\}\sqcup\{3,4,5\}\sqcup\{6,7,8\}\), all edges between different parts, and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\) be the space of two unordered distinct points on the geometric graph. Then
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{81}.
\]
Every unordered pair of vertex-disjoint, unoriented simple cycles of lengths \(p\le q\) determines an embedded product torus in \(X\). Write \(L_{p,q}\) for the subgroup generated by all such torus fundamental classes. The exhaustive integral classification is:

| Cycle lengths \((p,q)\) | Number of tori | Rank of \(L_{p,q}\) | Integral quotient \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_{p,q}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \((3,3)\) | 108 | 81 | zero |
| \((3,4)\) | 405 | 81 | \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) |
| \((3,5)\) | 648 | 81 | zero |
| \((3,6)\) | 432 | 81 | \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) |
| \((4,4)\) | 243 | 60 | \(\mathbb Z^{21}\) |
| \((4,5)\) | 324 | 81 | \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) |

In particular the 108 shortest product tori, corresponding to two disjoint triangles, generate \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) *integrally*: an explicitly indexed selection of 81 of them is a basis. The 432 tori of length type \((3,6)\), in contrast, span rationally but miss a **cyclic order-four** integral quotient, even though each individual torus has a primitive class.

## Assumptions and scope
Simple cycles mean cyclic sequences of distinct graph vertices with at least three edges; each unoriented cycle is counted once, up to cyclic rotation and reversal. For a cycle pair, disjointness is disjointness of their *vertex sets*. Each cycle is given an orientation solely to specify the sign of its torus fundamental class; changing either orientation does not change the generated lattice. The graph is the *geometric realization* of the stated simple abstract graph; graph edges have no additional subdivisions. Homology is ordinary singular homology over the integers. No statement is made about products of cycles sharing vertices or about higher-particle configuration spaces.

## Proof
**Cellular model.** The standard equivariant deleted-product retraction for two particles [1, 3] identifies \(X\) up to homotopy with the quotient of the ordered deleted cubical product of \(G\) by particle exchange. It has one zero-cell for each unordered pair of distinct graph vertices, one one-cell \((e,v)\) for every graph edge \(e\) and vertex \(v\notin e\), and one two-cell \(\{e,f\}\) for every pair of vertex-disjoint edges. Direct enumeration gives the chain ranks
\[
\operatorname{rank}C_0=36,\qquad \operatorname{rank}C_1=189,\qquad \operatorname{rank}C_2=216.
\]
Orient each graph edge \((a,b)\) from the smaller to the larger vertex, and orient a square by the product \(e\times f\), with lexicographically \(e<f\). For \(e=(a,b)<f=(c,d)\), the boundary is
\[
\partial_2(e,f)=(e,c)-(e,d)-(f,a)+(f,b).
\]
Here \((e,v)\) is the oriented one-cell obtained by moving the edge particle from its lower to its higher endpoint while the other particle stays at \(v\). The program constructs these boundaries and checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) for every square.

**All geometric tori.** List every unoriented simple cycle by taking its minimum-labelled vertex as the first vertex and choosing the orientation for which the second vertex is smaller than the last. There are exactly 6705 simple cycles. For each unordered disjoint cycle pair, multiply its two integral signed one-cycles. Each oriented square \(e\times f\) receives coefficient \(\pm1\) according to the edge orientation and whether the ordered pair is flipped to \(e<f\). Direct expansion verifies that every resulting integer two-chain is a two-cycle. All disjoint pairs (2160 in total) fall into exactly the six displayed length types.

**Sharp integral rank and a basis.** Sparse Gaussian elimination on the 216 columns of \(\partial_2\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) yields rank 135, hence integer rank at least 135. The standalone `basis_indices.json` contains 81 *distinct* indices into the lexicographically sorted list of the 108 triangle-pair cycles and 81 *distinct* indices into the lexicographically sorted 216 square cells. With these selections, the 81-by-81 integer coefficient minor is exactly \(+1\), checked using determinant computation over \(\mathbb Z\). Thus \(\ker(\partial_2\otimes\mathbb Q)\) has dimension at least 81, so the boundary rank is at most 135. The two bounds prove that its rank is *exactly* 135.

For integrality, projection of the rational kernel onto the 81 chosen square coordinates is injective because the 81 displayed cycles already form a rational basis. The unimodular minor maps the integer span of these cycles *onto* \(\mathbb Z^{81}\) under the projection. Every integer cycle projects to integer coordinates, and subtracting the corresponding integer combination of the selected cycles leaves a rational-kernel vector with zero projection; injectivity makes it zero. Therefore the chosen 81 triangle-pair tori are an integral basis of the complete kernel. Since there are no three-cells, \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\), establishing the first assertion with no torsion or index ambiguity.

**All restricted lattices.** Express every one of the 2160 integer torus cycles in this integral basis, by multiplying its 81 selected square coordinates by the inverse unimodular minor. For each length type, form the resulting 81-by-\(N\) integer matrix, compute its Hermite column basis, and then the Smith normal form of that basis when the rank is 81. Its free rank, and its nonunit Smith factors, are exactly those in the table. In particular type \((3,6)\) has a single nonunit factor \(4\), not two factors of \(2\). For \((4,4)\), the column rank is 60 and all 60 nonzero Smith factors are units, so the quotient is free of rank 21. Every individual torus class is primitive because its signed chain has a coefficient \(\pm1\) in some square-cell coordinate, and the kernel is a subgroup of the free chain group.

All steps are finite, exact, and exhaustively reproduced by the supplied program. This proof uses modular arithmetic only for a rank *lower bound* and an independent integral unimodular certificate for the complementary bound and saturation.

## Verification
Run `python verify_k333.py` with `basis_indices.json` beside the script in an environment with `sympy==1.14.0`; Python 3.11 or later is supported. The verifier constructs the graph, the complete cubical boundaries, all simple cycles and disjoint pairs, checks their chain boundaries, verifies the exact 81-by-81 determinant and the rank bound over \(\mathbb F_3\), then obtains every length-specific rank and Smith quotient over the integers. The final line is `VERIFY_OK_K333_INTEGRAL_LENGTH_RESTRICTED_TORUS_LATTICES`. Neither an external input table nor a precomputed homology group is assumed. The certificate file pins only transparent zero-based combinatorial row and column selections, not claimed answers.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett–Farber [1] give deleted-product and cycle-intersection methods, including explicit generators under *planarity* assumptions, while Farber–Hanbury [2] study mature nonplanar graphs and abstract ordered-configuration Betti numbers. Ko–Park [3] supply graph-braid and cubical-model context. Ramos–Yun [5] compute rational stable homology representations for the distinct family \(K_{n,n,1}\), not \(K_{3,3,3}\), and do not determine the present integral cycle-length lattices. An–Knudsen [4] prove toric/theta-generation theorems for *planar* graph braid groups and explicitly discuss the nonplanar extension as an open issue. The graph \(K_{3,3,3}\) contains \(K_{3,3}\) and is not planar. The present exact statement concerns the **unordered** two-particle space and the **six natural length-restricted integral torus lattices**; neither a planar theorem nor an abstract rational rank formula specifies the cyclic order-four gap. It does not claim to originate the cubical model, the general torus construction, or the overall idea of spanning by geometric cycles.

## Limitations
The classification is for this one naturally symmetric nonplanar graph, not an arbitrary complete multipartite graph. The table addresses geometric product tori only; it does not classify all minimum-cell nonzero two-cycles, cup products, or first homology. Published literature searched and inspected does not prove global originality: less-indexed equivalent integral computations or a more general theorem with this as an immediate corollary remain possible. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
