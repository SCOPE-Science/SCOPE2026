# Integral mixed-dimensional homotopy of the Paley thirteen-vertex three-cut complex

## Finding
Let \(P_{13}\) be the quadratic-residue Paley graph on \(\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z\), and let \(\Delta_3(P_{13})\) be the **ordinary** three-cut complex generated by the complements of disconnected induced triples. Then the following is an integral homotopy equivalence:
\[
\Delta_3(P_{13})\simeq S^7\vee S^7\vee S^8.
\]
Consequently, its nonzero reduced integral homology groups are precisely \(\widetilde H_7\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and \(\widetilde H_8\cong\mathbb Z\). Although the complex is pure nine-dimensional, it is not shellable. This asserts a homotopy type, not merely homology over a field.

## Assumptions and scope
Take the vertex set \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,12\}\), and join distinct vertices \(i,j\) exactly when the residue \(j-i\pmod {13}\) belongs to \(\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}\). Thus the graph is the strongly regular graph with parameters \((13,6,2,3)\). The word *disconnected* refers to the induced graph on exactly three vertices: a triple is disconnected precisely when it spans at most one edge. A simplex \(\sigma\subseteq V\) belongs to \(K=\Delta_3(P_{13})\) if and only if its complement contains at least one such disconnected triple. In particular, the complex is distinct from the total three-cut complex based only on independent triples, and distinct from the ordinary open-neighborhood complex of this graph.

There are exactly \(143\) disconnected triples, hence \(143\) facets of cardinality \(10\). The face counts, ordered by cardinalities \(1,2,\ldots,10\), are
\[
(13,78,286,715,1287,1716,1716,1287,637,143).
\]
These counts include every nonempty face exactly once; the empty face is included as usual in the simplicial complex but not in the discrete-Morse face poset. The complex is pure of dimension \(9\).

## Proof
Use the vertex order
\[
(8,12,9,1,2,11,0,3,5,7,6,10,4).
\]
At each successive pivot vertex \(v\), match every *remaining* nonempty simplex \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both remain. Remove all those matched pairs before advancing to the next pivot. This gives exactly \(3,936\) distinct elementary face/coface pairs among the \(7,878\) nonempty faces.

This matching is acyclic: direct construction of every one of the \(50,076\) nonempty face-poset covering incidences, with matched incidences oriented upward and all other incidences downward, admits a topological ordering through all \(7,878\) faces. This is an exhaustive acyclicity certificate, not a sample of gradient paths; the full deterministic verifier is included. The unmatched simplices are one vertex (bit mask `256`), three seven-dimensional simplices (bit masks `4671`, `6711`, `7695`), and two eight-dimensional simplices (bit masks `7389`, `7739`). Bit \(i\) always represents vertex \(i\); all orientations use increasing vertex order.

The resulting Morse CW complex has one zero-cell, three seven-cells and two eight-cells, and no other cells. To calculate its integral cellular boundary, recursively reduce signed seven-dimensional faces along the matched pairs between seven- and eight-cells. For a matched pair \((\sigma,\eta)\) with \(\dim\sigma=7\), \(\dim\eta=8\), the oriented incidence number \([\eta:\sigma]\in\{+1,-1\}\) yields the exact substitution
\[
\rho(\sigma)=-[\eta:\sigma]\sum_{\tau\subset\eta,\,\dim\tau=7,\,\tau\ne\sigma}[\eta:\tau]\rho(\tau).
\]
Each critical seven-cell reduces to its basis vector, and each seven-cell paired with a six-cell reduces to zero. The verified acyclicity guarantees termination of these substitutions. With the critical seven-cells and eight-cells in the increasing bit-mask orders above, the integer Morse boundary \(C_8\to C_7\) has *two identical columns*:
\[
\partial_8=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular its rank is \(1\), its nonzero Smith invariant is \(1\), and it has no torsion in its cokernel. More strongly, because the seven-skeleton is a wedge of three seven-spheres and \(\pi_7(\bigvee^3 S^7)\cong\mathbb Z^3\), the degree vector gives each entire attaching map. An integral change of seven-cell basis sends the primitive vector \((-1,-1,1)\) to \((1,0,0)\). The first eight-cell cancels that seven-sphere, and the second eight-cell attaches trivially after the cancellation. This produces \(S^7\vee S^7\vee S^8\) up to homotopy, integrally.

As the original complex is pure of dimension \(9\) but has nonzero reduced homology in dimensions \(7\) and \(8\), it cannot be shellable: a pure shellable complex can have reduced homology only in its top dimension.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_paley13.py` from this package; it requires only the Python standard library. It reconstructs the graph, verifies its strong-regularity parameters, checks all disconnected triples and simplices, builds the stated matching, topologically sorts the entire oriented Hasse diagram, and performs the signed integral Morse differential reduction. Its deterministic reference output is in `expected_output.txt`.

An independently written Alexander-dual check, `python3 verify_alexander_dual.py`, verifies that the dual complex has face counts by cardinality \((1,13,78,143,78)\), including the empty face. The ranks of its positive simplicial boundary maps are \((12,66,76)\) over each of the primes \(2,3,5,7,11,101\), giving reduced Betti numbers \(1\) in degree \(2\) and \(2\) in degree \(3\). Combinatorial Alexander duality independently matches the asserted ranks of \(K\) in degrees \(8\) and \(7\). This modular check is corroboration, **not** a proof of absence of integral torsion or a replacement for the integral Morse attaching-degree calculation. The deterministic output is `expected_dual_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work
The ordinary cut-complex construction and the distinction between ordinary and total cut complexes are defined in the primary works `arXiv:2209.13503` and `arXiv:2304.13675`. The latter studies chordality, graph operations, complete multipartite graphs, cycles, clique prisms and general face-lattice criteria. Its Theorem 6.1 assumes a complete codimension-one skeleton; that hypothesis fails here: among the \(\binom{13}{9}=715\) possible eight-dimensional faces, only \(637\) occur. Its general statements therefore do not supply this homotopy equivalence.

`arXiv:2407.08158` treats disjoint unions, squared paths and grids. `arXiv:2406.01979` and `arXiv:2603.23155` establish shellability results for squared cycles and powers of cycles. The Paley graph \(P_{13}\) is not one of those cycle powers: it has parameters \((13,6,2,3)\) and the complex here has homology below the pure top dimension, unlike the covered shellable cases. Published descriptions of the open-neighborhood complex of \(P_{13}\) involve a different face condition and do not determine the ordinary three-cut homotopy type.

## Limitations
This is a single, canonical finite-graph classification. It asserts neither a formula for all Paley graphs nor an equivariant homotopy splitting under their automorphism groups. It is not an independence-complex or total-cut-complex statement. The principal finite certificates are fully reproducible from the included scripts, but verification and the literature comparison have not been independently audited. An unindexed equivalent prior computation remains possible.
