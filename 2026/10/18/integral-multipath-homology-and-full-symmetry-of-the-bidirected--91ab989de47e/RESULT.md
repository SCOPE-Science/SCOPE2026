# Integral multipath homology and full symmetry of the bidirected triangular prism

## Finding

The multipath complex \(X(D)\) of the **bidirected triangular-prism graph** \(D=\overleftrightarrow{C_3\square K_2}\) has torsion-free reduced integral homology
\[
\widetilde H_k(X(D);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^5,& k=2,\\
\mathbb Z^{22},& k=3,\\
\mathbb Z^6,& k=4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The natural action of \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(C_3\square K_2)\cong D_3\times C_2\) has complete rational characters as follows. The column headed \(k\) gives the trace on \(\widetilde H_k(X(D);\mathbb Q)\).

| Symmetry | \(k=2\) | \(k=3\) | \(k=4\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Identity \(1\) | 5 | 22 | 6 |
| Rotation \(r\) | 5 | 4 | 0 |
| Reflection \(s\) | -1 | 0 | 0 |
| Layer swap \(t\) | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| \(rt\) | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| \(st\) | -1 | 0 | -2 |

The three class representatives with reflections each represent three group elements in the corresponding class, while \(r\) and \(rt\) each represent two elements. Averaging the characters gives dimensions \(1,3,0\) of the subspaces fixed by all automorphisms in degrees \(2,3,4\).

Writing \(\mathbf1\) for the trivial rational representation of \(D_3\), \(\varepsilon\) for its reflection-sign representation and \(\rho\) for its standard two-dimensional representation, with superscripts \(+\) and \(-\) denoting the layer-swap eigenspaces, the representations decompose as
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde H_2&\cong(\mathbf1\oplus2\varepsilon)^+\oplus(\mathbf1\oplus\varepsilon)^-,\\
\widetilde H_3&\cong(3\mathbf1\oplus3\varepsilon\oplus3\rho)^+\oplus(2\mathbf1\oplus2\varepsilon\oplus3\rho)^-,\\
\widetilde H_4&\cong(\varepsilon\oplus\rho)^+\oplus(\mathbf1\oplus\rho)^-.
\end{aligned}
\]
All representations in these equations are over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Assumptions and scope

Label the vertices of the undirected triangular prism by \(0,1,2\) and \(3,4,5\). Each triple induces a triangle; the additional edges are \(\{0,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,5\}\). The directed graph \(D\) has all eighteen arcs obtained by taking both orientations of every one of the nine undirected edges. Its multipath complex has the arcs as simplicial vertices. A set of arcs is a simplex precisely when every original graph vertex has indegree at most one and outdegree at most one, and the resulting directed subgraph contains no directed cycle. Isolated original graph vertices are allowed.

The group \(G\) is generated by \(r:i\mapsto i+1\pmod3\) within each layer, \(s:i\mapsto-i\pmod3\) within each layer, and \(t\), which interchanges the corresponding vertices of the two layers. All homology groups are **reduced** unless explicitly stated otherwise. Traces refer to the orientation of simplices obtained by ordering arcs lexicographically by their ordered pairs of endpoints.

## Proof

**Exhaustive domain.** Order the eighteen directed arcs lexicographically and encode any arc set as an eighteen-bit integer. Exhaustively examine every mask from zero through \(2^{18}-1\). Check the incoming and outgoing degrees and then follow the unique successor from each vertex to exclude directed cycles. The resulting complex has \(686\) nonempty simplices and face counts, in dimensions zero through four, of \((18,108,260,240,60)\). No simplex occurs in a higher dimension. In particular, the reduced Euler characteristic is \(-11\).

**Integral discrete-Morse reduction.** Label the arcs from zero to seventeen in the fixed lexicographic order, and apply sequential vertex toggles in the explicit order

`(13,12,4,9,5,7,16,6,10,15,1,11,2,14,17,8,0,3)`.

At each toggle index, pair every remaining face not containing that arc with its union with that arc, if both remain. The empty face is excluded from matching. Exactly \(326\) pairs are produced, leaving one critical vertex, five critical two-cells, twenty-two critical three-cells, and six critical four-cells. The directed Hasse graph with matched covers reversed has \(686\) nodes and \(2256\) cover relations. The standalone checker topologically sorts all \(686\) nodes, thereby verifying acyclicity rather than merely relying on a greedy heuristic.

To calculate the integral Morse boundary, write \([h:f]\in\{\pm1\}\) for the coefficient of an oriented facet \(f\) in the simplicial boundary of a simplex \(h\). For a lower cell \(f\) paired with \(h\), define its projection recursively by
\[
p(f)=-[h:f]\sum_{u\subset h,\,u\ne f,\,|u|=|f|}[h:u]p(u).
\]
For a critical cell \(c\), set \(p(c)=c\), and for an upper matched cell set \(p(c)=0\). Acyclicity makes these signed recursions finite. For every critical cell \(c\) in degrees three and four, evaluate \(p(\partial c)\). Both complete Morse matrices are **zero over the integers**, of sizes \(5\times22\) and \(22\times6\), respectively. There are no critical one-cells, so all other Morse differentials vanish. The integral Morse complex therefore gives precisely the stated free reduced homology groups, with no hidden torsion.

**The complete group character.** For each critical generator \(c\), construct an actual integral simplicial cycle using the matching homotopy \(H\), defined recursively on paired lower cells by
\[
H(f)=[h:f]h-[h:f]\sum_{u\subset h,\,u\ne f,\,|u|=|f|}[h:u]H(u),
\]
and zero on the remaining cells. The cycle is \(\ell(c)=c-H(\partial c)\). The verifier checks \(\partial\ell(c)=0\) and \(p(\ell(c))=c\) for all \(33\) positive-dimensional critical generators. For each graph automorphism \(g\), apply the induced signed arc permutation to \(\ell(c)\), project the image with \(p\), and read off the exact integer matrix of \(g\) on the Morse homology basis. Its traces give the table above. All rotation, reflection and layer-exchange group relations are checked on the resulting matrices. Additionally, for all twelve group elements, the alternating homology trace equals the alternating trace on the original simplicial chains, an independent Lefschetz consistency check.

The irreducible rational decompositions follow by taking inner products of the character with the three irreducible characters of \(D_3\), separately in the \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces of \(t\).

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_prism_morse.py` implements the exact face enumeration, the complete matching, all \(2256\) oriented cover checks, the signed integral Morse matrices, explicit cycle lifts and the full group action. It prints `exact_chain_symmetry_verification: PASS` only after verifying every required equality.

The separately implemented `verify_prism_independent.py` generates the valid arc sets recursively, avoiding the first program's all-mask method. It calculates mod-two simplicial boundary ranks \((17,91,164,54)\), giving unreduced Betti numbers \((1,0,5,22,6)\), and checks the signed Lefschetz identity for all twelve automorphisms. This finite-field calculation is a cross-check, **not** the basis for the claim of integral torsion-freeness. Expected terminal outputs are bundled as `verify_prism_morse_output.txt` and `verify_prism_independent_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Caputi, Collari, and Smith (2026, arXiv:2601.05670v1) calculate homology and connectivity for bidirected paths and polygons and study specific orientation-changing operations; the triangular prism is neither a path nor a polygon and is not obtained from their displayed suspension operation in a way yielding the present computation. Their main formulas do not imply either the three-degree integral Betti profile or the full \(D_3\times C_2\) character computed here.

Caputi, Collari, Di Trani, and Smith (2022, arXiv:2208.04656v1) study multipath complexes of oriented paths, polygons, selected small oriented grids and transitive tournaments. The bidirected prism contains triangles and is not one of their listed grid families. Its directed cycle structure also prevents direct use of the acyclic-digraph matching-complex comparison. The general dynamical-module decomposition does not provide the present explicit numerical answer without a new calculation.

Related calculations for ordinary discrete-Morse matching complexes of a triangular prism concern **a different simplicial complex**: those vertices are Hasse-incidence pairs of the undirected graph, rather than the eighteen directed arcs used here. Likewise, results about total cut, Čech or Vietoris–Rips complexes of prisms do not imply this theorem despite sharing the underlying graph. Published-record comparisons of those objects and targeted searches for the exact arc-domain and homology data found no equivalent or stronger statement that entails this result.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns only the bidirected triangular prism, not all prisms. It computes integral homology and the **rational** group action but does not classify integral representation lattices. The zero Morse differentials do not, by themselves, prove a wedge-of-spheres homotopy type in this mixed-dimensional setting; no such homotopy classification is asserted. The literature checks cover identified accessible sources and indexed mathematical records, not every possible manuscript or database.
