# Integral path homology of carry-free face-step circulants

## Finding

For every integer \(d\ge1\), every simplicial complex \(L\) on the labeled vertex set \([d]=\{1,\ldots,d\}\) containing every singleton, every integer \(q\ge3\), and every integer \(n>2\max S(L,q)\), set
\[
S(L,q)=\left\{\sum_{i\in F}q^{i-1}:\emptyset\ne F\in L\right\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n-1\}.
\]
Let \(D(L,q,n)\) be the directed Cayley graph of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) with arcs \(a\to a+s\) for \(s\in S(L,q)\). For ordinary integral Grigor'yan–Lin–Muranov–Yau (GLMY) path homology,
\[
\boxed{H_k^{\mathrm{path}}(D(L,q,n);\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge^k\mathbb Z^d\quad(k\ge0).}
\]
Thus every integral group is free, its rank is \(\binom dk\) for \(0\le k\le d\), and it vanishes above degree \(d\). The result holds regardless of which higher faces belong to \(L\). In particular, a directed graph whose shortcut generators encode the edges or higher simplices of an arbitrary complex has the same integral path Betti numbers as the generator set consisting only of the \(d\) singleton steps.

## Assumptions and scope

An abstract simplicial complex is downward closed under subsets. All \(d\) vertices occur as singleton faces, and the empty face is excluded from the connection set. Writing \(\mathbf1_F\in\{0,1\}^d\) for the incidence vector of a face, each generator \(s_F\) is its base-\(q\) encoding. The condition \(q\ge3\) prevents carries when adding two generators; the condition \(n>2\max S\) prevents modular wraparound in all sums of at most two generators (including the empty sum zero). The directed graph is not symmetrized. All statements use the regular integral GLMY path-chain complex \(\Omega_*(D;\mathbb Z)\), whose chains are linear combinations of allowed directed paths with allowed boundaries. An isomorphism of homology groups, rather than a specified canonical basis or a statement about directed-flag homology, is claimed.

## Proof

**1. Two-sum marked relations.** Let \(T_L=\{\mathbf1_F:\emptyset\ne F\in L\}\subset\mathbb Z^d\). Since two incidence vectors add with coordinate digits in \(\{0,1,2\}\), base-\(q\) uniqueness gives, whenever \(F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4\) are faces or empty,
\[
s_{F_1}+s_{F_2}=s_{F_3}+s_{F_4}\iff
\mathbf1_{F_1}+\mathbf1_{F_2}=\mathbf1_{F_3}+\mathbf1_{F_4},
\]
where \(s_{\emptyset}=0\). The same holds modulo \(n\) for these sums. Let \(Q_S\) be the abelian group freely generated by symbols \(E_F\), for nonempty faces, modulo all length-at-most-two additive relations among the corresponding generators. For every nonsingleton face and every \(i\in F\), downward closure implies \(F\setminus\{i\}\in L\), and the legal merge relation yields \(E_F=E_{F\setminus\{i\}}+E_{\{i\}}\). Inductively, \(E_F=\sum_{i\in F}E_{\{i\}}\). Conversely all imposed short relations preserve these incidence-vector sums by the carry-free equivalence. It follows that
\[
Q_S\cong\mathbb Z^d,\qquad E_F\longmapsto\mathbf1_F.
\]
The marked two-step cover of \(D(L,q,n)\) is therefore \(\widetilde D_L=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z^d,T_L)\), with deck group
\[
N=\ker\left(\mathbb Z^d\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,\quad
(a_i)_{i=1}^d\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^d a_iq^{i-1}\bmod n\right).
\]
The map is surjective because the first singleton step is \(1\), so \(N\) is free abelian of rank \(d\).

**2. Integral acyclicity of every downward-closed marked lift.** Fix a coordinate \(i\) and integer threshold \(r\). The map \(F_{i,r}(a)_i=\max(a_i,r)\) and \(F_{i,r}(a)_j=a_j\) for \(j\ne i\) is a digraph map \(\widetilde D_L\to\widetilde D_L\). Indeed, every allowed step \(\mathbf1_F\) becomes either the same step, \(\mathbf1_{F\setminus\{i\}}\), or zero. These are allowed steps or a stationary vertex because \(L\) is downward closed. For each \(a\), the vertices \(F_{i,r}(a)\) and \(F_{i,r+1}(a)\) coincide or are joined by the allowed arrow \(+\mathbf e_i\), so consecutive clamps are connected by a one-step digraph homotopy. Their induced integral path-chain maps are chain homotopic.

Let \(z\) be any finite-support reduced integral path cycle of \(\widetilde D_L\). For coordinate \(i\), choose a threshold below the smallest \(i\)-coordinate of every vertex occurring in \(z\), and a later threshold above its largest such coordinate. The initial clamp fixes \(z\); the later clamp makes coordinate \(i\) constant on the image. Repeat this independently over all \(d\) coordinates, using only integral chain homotopies. The eventual image is supported on one vertex, hence is zero in positive reduced degrees and in reduced degree zero. Thus
\[
\widetilde H_k^{\mathrm{path}}(\widetilde D_L;\mathbb Z)=0\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
This is an actual proof for every finite-support cycle, not an extrapolation from finite tests.

**3. Integral descent.** Let \(A_m\) be the free abelian group on allowed length-\(m\) paths starting at a fixed vertex, and let \(W_m\) be the integer kernel of the boundary map onto the quotient spanned by forbidden internal faces. Because the permitted/forbidden patterns depend only on relations between sums of at most two steps, the same subgroup \(W_m\) applies to the cover and quotient. Every \(W_m\) is free abelian (a subgroup of the finite-rank free group \(A_m\)). The translation action yields
\[
\Omega_m(\widetilde D_L;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z^d]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}W_m.
\]
In particular each \(\Omega_m\) is a free \(\mathbb Z[N]\)-module. Integral acyclicity from Step 2 makes the augmented complex a free \(\mathbb Z[N]\)-resolution of the trivial module \(\mathbb Z\). Taking \(N\)-coinvariants, the two-sum compatibility from Step 1 identifies the resulting complex with \(\Omega_*(D(L,q,n);\mathbb Z)\). Hence
\[
H_k^{\mathrm{path}}(D(L,q,n);\mathbb Z)
\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathbb Z[N]}_k(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)
\cong H_k(N;\mathbb Z)
\cong\bigwedge^k\mathbb Z^d.
\]
The final isomorphism follows from the integral Koszul resolution for a free abelian group of rank \(d\). This also excludes torsion in every degree.

**4. An explicit nonzero top class.** For \(s_i=q^{i-1}\), sum all translates, over the cyclic vertices and all permutations, of the oriented length-\(d\) step path \((s_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,s_{\sigma(d)})\) with coefficient \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\). Adjacent swaps cancel illegal internal faces, while the first and last face contributions cancel after summing translations. This gives an integral \(d\)-cycle \(z\). Extend \(\mathbf1_F\) additively to all sums of two steps and take the determinant of the \(d\) increment vectors as a \(d\)-cochain. Multilinearity gives a zero coboundary, including on faces containing legal shortcut merges. It evaluates on \(z\) to \(n\,d!\ne0\), independently witnessing the nonzero top-degree class. This pairing alone does not establish the full homology ranks; Steps 1–3 do.

## Verification

The accompanying dependency-free script `verify_face_downsets.py` checks three nontrivial downward-closed examples: a three-vertex path complex (steps \(\{1,3,4,9,12\}\), modulus \(25\)), the full simplex on three vertices (steps \(\{1,3,4,9,10,12,13\}\), modulus \(27\)), and a four-vertex path complex (steps \(\{1,3,4,9,12,27,36\}\), modulus \(73\)). For each it exhaustively checks all short additive equivalences, every relevant local coordinate-clamp outcome on a finite box, the full signed boundary of the explicitly constructed top cycle, and the determinant-cocycle identity for every \((d+1)\)-step word. It obtains top-cycle pairings \(150\), \(162\), and \(1752\), respectively. Separate modular Fourier block computations on the first two examples give trivial-translation Betti vectors \((3,3,1)\) in positive degrees \(1\) through \(3\) and zero on the sampled nontrivial blocks. The verifier's finite checks do not replace the universal integral proof or certify exact homology ranks by themselves.

## Relationship to prior work

The foundational covering mechanism of Di–Ivanov–Mukoseev–Zhang and the explicit two-sum lattice construction of Tang–Yau, especially their Proposition 5.2, are prior work and are used as context for Step 3. Tang–Yau's Example 4.13 already covers the isolated-singletons choice of \(L\), and their Theorem 6.2 treats rectangular families with no legal sum of two generators. These known cases are not claimed as new. The new mathematical content is the coordinatewise acyclicity theorem for **all downward-closed incidence-step configurations**, and its uniform **integral** descent for every simplicial complex \(L\), including families with both legal shortcut merges and colliding pair sums. For example, \(1+3=4\), \(3+9=12\), and \(1+12=4+9\) for the three-vertex path case; the no-shortcut and unique-pair-sum theorems cannot be substituted directly. The general acyclic-cover criterion is stronger in its scope of applicability but does not establish this arbitrary-downset acyclicity hypothesis by itself.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns integral GLMY path homology, not homotopy type of a topological realization, not arbitrary non-downward-closed connection sets, and not short moduli where two-step wraparound occurs. It does not assert canonical naturality of the chosen integral exterior-group isomorphism. Its originality has not been independently adjudicated. Published general covering theorems substantially overlap the proof mechanism; an equivalent arbitrary-downset corollary may exist outside the checked sources. The statement is a specialization after a separately proved lift-acyclicity lemma and not a new proof of the general covering theorem.
