# Integral relations among 18 disjoint-square tori in \(\operatorname{UConf}_2(K_{4,4})\)

## Finding

For the complete bipartite graph \(G=K_{4,4}\), with left vertices \(L=\{0,1,2,3\}\) and right vertices \(R=\{4,5,6,7\}\), the unordered collision-free two-point configuration space \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\) has eighteen natural embedded product tori, one for each pair of vertex-disjoint simple cycles. Their integral classes generate all of \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{12}\), and **the six relations displayed below form the entire primitive relation lattice**, not merely a rational relation space.

For \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\) set
\[
L_0=(0,1),\quad L_1=(0,2),\quad L_2=(0,3).
\]
For \(j\in\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) set
\[
(R_0,R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4,R_5)=((4,5),(4,6),(4,7),(5,6),(5,7),(6,7)).
\]
Write \(t_{ij}\) for the product of the oriented four-cycles on \(L_i\cup R_j\) and on their respective complementary vertex pairs. Their orientations are specified in the next section.

The following are the complete six independent integral relations:
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_i&=t_{i0}-t_{i1}+t_{i2}+t_{i3}-t_{i4}+t_{i5},\quad i=0,1,2,\\
M_1&=(t_{00}-t_{01}+t_{02})-(t_{10}-t_{11}+t_{12})+(t_{20}-t_{21}+t_{22}),\\
M_2&=(-t_{01}+t_{04})+(t_{11}-t_{14})+(-t_{21}+t_{24}),\\
M_3&=(-t_{00}+t_{05})+(t_{10}-t_{15})+(-t_{20}+t_{25}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z^{18}/\langle R_0,R_1,R_2,M_1,M_2,M_3\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^{12}.
\]
One integral torus basis consists of the classes with zero-based indices \(0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,12,13\) under the convention \(t_{ij}\leftrightarrow 6i+j\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite simplicial graphs viewed as one-dimensional CW complexes; particles are **unordered** and collisions are forbidden at every point, including vertices. With the original simple graph \(K_{4,4}\), the two-particle Abrams cubical model is a deformation retract: cycles have length four and paths between distinct essential vertices have length one, satisfying the two-particle subdivision bounds. It has \(28\) vertex-cells, \(96\) edge-cells and \(72\) square-cells. No cell lies in dimension three, so second homology is literally the integer kernel of the second cellular boundary.

Orient every graph edge \((a,u)\) from its left endpoint \(a\in L\) to its right endpoint \(u\in R\). For two ordered left vertices \(a<b\) and right vertices \(u<v\), orient the corresponding square-cycle by the integer edge chain
\[
 c(a,b;u,v)=[a,u]-[b,u]+[b,v]-[a,v].
\]
For disjoint cycles, define the product-torus two-chain by the shuffle-antisymmetric cubical exterior product \(c\wedge c'\): an oriented disjoint edge pair \(e_p\wedge e_q\) uses the coefficient of \(e_p\otimes e_q-e_q\otimes e_p\), with graph-edge indices increasing in the cubical basis. This is the normalization used in the executable certificate. Swapping the two **cycles** reverses orientation, while swapping the two **particles** has already been quotiented out.

A simple cycle in this bipartite graph has at least four vertices. Two vertex-disjoint cycles therefore use all eight vertices and must each be a four-cycle. The partition of the four left vertices into two pairs has three choices, and for each such partition there are six choices assigning a right pair to the first left pair. Hence there are precisely \(18\) unoriented product-torus carriers.

## Proof

First construct the oriented cubical complex. Its \(72\) square-cells correspond to unordered pairs of graph edges with disjoint endpoint sets. Its \(96\) one-cells correspond to a stationary vertex and a nonincident moving edge. If graph edges are \(e=(a,b)\) and \(f=(c,d)\), both oriented left to right, the square boundary is
\[
\partial(e\wedge f)= (b,f)-(a,f)-(d,e)+(c,e)
\]
when the two edge indices are increasing. This yields a \(96\)-by-\(72\) integer matrix \(B\).

Next construct the \(72\)-by-\(18\) integer matrix \(T\) whose columns are the eighteen geometrically oriented product-torus cycles. Directly from the four-cycle boundary, or by the exact matrix multiplication in the verifier,
\[
 BT=0.
\]
The modular elimination certificate produces \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}(B)=60\). Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}_\mathbb Q(B)\ge60\), and \(\operatorname{rank}_\mathbb Q\ker B\le12\).

The twelve torus columns indexed by
\[
(0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,12,13)
\]
have a \(12\)-by-\(12\) coefficient submatrix on square-cell rows
\[
(0,1,3,4,9,10,12,13,18,21,36,37)
\]
with **exact integer determinant \(+1\)**, computed by fraction-free elimination. Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}_\mathbb Q(T)\ge12\), forcing \(\operatorname{rank}_\mathbb Q(B)=60\) and \(\ker_\mathbb Z(B)\cong\mathbb Z^{12}\). The unit minor also makes the sublattice generated by those twelve torus columns a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z^{72}\). Since \(\ker_\mathbb Z(B)\) is rank twelve and contains that saturated sublattice, they are equal. This proves integral generation, not just rational spanning.

Let \(S\) be the \(18\)-by-\(6\) integer relation-coefficient matrix with columns \((R_0,R_1,R_2,M_1,M_2,M_3)\). Exact integer multiplication gives \(TS=0\). Its six-by-six submatrix on rows
\[
(5,11,14,15,16,17)
\]
has determinant \(-1\). Thus its six columns generate a primitive rank-six sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^{18}\). Since \(T\) has rank twelve, its integral kernel also has rank six and is primitive, so the two lattices coincide. This is the complete integral relation presentation.

## Relationship to prior work

Barnett–Farber (2009) develop intersection theory for **ordered** two-point graph configurations, and Farber–Hanbury (2010) compute complete-bipartite ordered configuration homology using maturity. Barnett's thesis (2009 manuscript; publicly deposited 2010), Chapter 5 Section 5.2, treats ordered two-particle complete-bipartite graph homology and exhibits four-cycle tensor relations. Those results supply strong prior context and imply substantial ordered homological information; their abstract ranks are not claimed as new here. The specific result here is a **complete primitive integral presentation for the eighteen unordered disjoint-square product-torus generators**. A simple inference from ordered ranks or rational representations alone does not settle integral saturation, nor does it exhibit these six relations.

The ordered calculation is the closest risk of equivalent reformulation: an explicit integral passage to coinvariants might conceivably yield the same presentation, but the inspected sources do not state this unordered relation lattice or these determinant certificates. General planar second-homology generation results do not directly cover the nonplanar graph \(K_{4,4}\).

## Limitations

The theorem is for the graph \(K_{4,4}\) and unordered **collision-free** two-point configurations only. It does not assert a uniform presentation for all complete bipartite graphs, a homotopy equivalence, a minimal word-length characterization of relations, or any result for vertex-sink configuration spaces. The evidence establishes a complete finite integer chain presentation; it does not certify novelty against inaccessible or unindexed literature.
