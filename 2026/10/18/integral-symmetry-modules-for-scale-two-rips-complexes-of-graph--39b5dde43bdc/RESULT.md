# Integral symmetry modules for scale-two Rips complexes of graph subdivisions

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite connected simple graph with at least one edge. Write \(S(G)\) for the graph obtained by subdividing every edge exactly once, with the shortest-path metric on its *vertices*, and put
\[
 X=\operatorname{VR}_{\leq2}(S(G)),\qquad B=\operatorname{Cl}(G),\qquad \Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(G).
\]
Let \(\mathcal T(G)\) be the set of unordered triangles of \(G\). The oriented-triangle lattice \(\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{or}}[\mathcal T(G)]\) is generated by \([a,b,c]\) for ordered triples spanning a triangle, with alternating relations; \(g[a,b,c]=[ga,gb,gc]\) for \(g\in\Gamma\). There are natural integral \(\Gamma\)-module isomorphisms
\[
 H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(B;\mathbb Z)\oplus\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{or}}[\mathcal T(G)],
 \qquad H_j(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_j(B;\mathbb Z)\quad(j\ne2),
\]
where the first summand is induced by the inclusion \(B\hookrightarrow X\), and the second by *explicit canonical oriented octahedral cycles* supported on six vertices of \(X\) per triangle. These isomorphisms commute with all graph isomorphisms. Thus the degree-two rational character difference for every automorphism \(g\) is
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl(g\mid H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\bigr)
 -\operatorname{tr}\bigl(g\mid H_2(B;\mathbb Q)\bigr)
 =\sum_{\substack{\tau\in\mathcal T(G)\\g\tau=\tau}}
   \operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_\tau).
\]
The scale-one-to-two Rips map on first integral homology is, under the canonical subdivision identification, the natural quotient
\[
 Z_1(G;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow Z_1(G;\mathbb Z)/\langle\partial[a,b,c]:\{a,b,c\}\in\mathcal T(G)\rangle
 \cong H_1(B;\mathbb Z),
\]
with the action of \(\Gamma\) respected. This is a statement about an integral *persistent map*, not just the two homology ranks.

In particular, for \(G=K_n\), \(n\ge3\), with its natural \(S_n\)-action,
\[
 H_2\bigl(\operatorname{VR}_{\leq2}(S(K_n));\mathbb Z\bigr)
 \cong\bigwedge{}^3\mathbb Z^n,
\]
where \(S_n\) permutes the standard basis. Although its rank is \(\binom n3\), it has **no nonzero \(S_n\)-invariant integral or rational class**: a triangle-preserving transposition negates its oriented generator. Thus the ordinary sphere count alone does not determine the symmetry action.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and connected and contain an edge; isolated components can be treated componentwise, but are not required in the claim. The Vietoris–Rips convention is inclusive graph distance \(\leq2\), on the finite vertex set rather than the continuous metric realization of a graph. An original vertex is denoted \(v\), and the midpoint vertex introduced on an original edge \(\{u,v\}\) is denoted \(e_{uv}\). All homology is unreduced singular/simplicial homology with integer coefficients unless the character formula explicitly tensors with \(\mathbb Q\). The module isomorphism is natural with respect to graph **isomorphisms**, not arbitrary graph homomorphisms; no claim of equivariant space-level wedge decomposition is made.

## Proof
**1. Graph-square identification and relative cells.** Distinct original vertices in \(S(G)\) are at distance at most two precisely when joined in \(G\). An original vertex \(v\) and an edge-vertex \(e_{uw}\) are within two precisely when \(v\) is an endpoint of \(uw\). Two edge-vertices are within two precisely when their edges share an endpoint. Thus \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(T(G))\), where \(T(G)\) is the usual total graph on \(V(G)\sqcup E(G)\). Every clique in \(T(G)\) is of one of these types: original-vertex clique; a subset of an edge-star possibly including its common original vertex; the triple \(\{u,v,e_{uv}\}\); or the three edge-vertices corresponding to an original triangle. The last triples, call them \(q_\tau\), are maximal 2-faces.

Let \(A\subseteq X\) result by deleting the interiors of every \(q_\tau\), keeping their boundaries. All missing faces are exactly those maximal 2-simplices, so \(C_j(X,A;\mathbb Z)\) vanishes except in degree two, where it is the oriented free triangle lattice with generators \(q_\tau\). This identification is \(\Gamma\)-equivariant, with the same orientation sign that permutes the vertices of \(q_\tau\).

**2. The core inclusion is a homology equivalence.** There is a homotopy equivalence \(A\simeq B\) induced by the inclusion \(B\hookrightarrow A\). An elementary direct argument deletes edge-vertices \(e_{uv}\) one after another. At each step, the link of a remaining \(e_{uv}\) is a pair of simplices (stars centered at \(u\) and \(v\), supported on not-yet-deleted edge-vertices) connected by the edge \(\{u,v\}\). That link is contractible; vertex deletion with contractible link is a homotopy equivalence. At the end the remaining induced subcomplex is exactly \(B\). Although the deletion order is not required to be invariant, the *inclusion map* \(B\hookrightarrow A\) is invariant, and its homology inverse is automatically \(\Gamma\)-equivariant. The same fact, with a simplicial-collapse proof, appears in Adamaszek's 2012 Theorem 5.1 proof; we do not claim the nonequivariant homotopy reduction as new.

**3. Canonical integral cycles.** Fix any oriented triangle \([a,b,c]\) of \(G\). Its three opposite, nonincident pairs in \(T(G)\) are
\[
 (a,e_{bc}),\quad(b,e_{ac}),\quad(c,e_{ab}).
\]
The six vertices induce the boundary of a three-dimensional cross-polytope: every pair is adjacent except each displayed opposite pair. The join product of reduced zero-chains
\[
 z_{abc}=([a]-[e_{bc}])*([b]-[e_{ac}])*([c]-[e_{ab}])
\]
is therefore an actual integral 2-cycle in \(X\) (with the join orientation), since the simplicial boundary of each factor vanishes in the augmented complex. Its coefficient on the exceptional edge-only oriented triangle \([e_{bc},e_{ac},e_{ab}]\) is \(-1\) with this ordered orientation, hence a unit. It is zero on the exceptional \(q_\sigma\) for every \(\sigma\ne\{a,b,c\}\). Exchanging \(a,b,c\) permutes join factors with the sign of the permutation, so \(z_{abc}\) satisfies alternating relations exactly; also \(g z_{abc}=z_{ga,gb,gc}\). Consequently these cycles define a canonical \(\Gamma\)-linear section of the projection
\[
 H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(X,A;\mathbb Z)
 \cong\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{or}}[\mathcal T(G)]
\]
(after choosing the consistent global sign convention between oriented exceptional faces and \(z\)). In the long exact sequence of \((X,A)\), this forces the connecting homomorphism into \(H_1(A)\) to vanish, and splits the resulting short exact sequence over \(\mathbb Z\), **equivariantly**. For \(j\ne2\), the relative long exact sequence gives the natural inclusion-induced homology isomorphisms. Composing the \(\Gamma\)-equivariant inclusion-induced equivalence \(H_*(B)\to H_*(A)\) proves the first assertions.

**4. Character and persistent map.** An oriented-triangle basis vector contributes to the trace of \(g\) precisely when its underlying unordered triangle is preserved; the contribution is the permutation sign on its vertices. This proves the character identity. At scale one, \(\operatorname{VR}_{\leq1}(S(G))=S(G)\), naturally homeomorphic to the graph \(G\) by edge subdivision. Inside \(X\), the two-edge subdivided path \(u,e_{uv},v\) is homotopic relative endpoints to the old edge \(u,v\) through the filled 2-simplex \(\{u,e_{uv},v\}\). Hence the persistence homomorphism is exactly the map \(H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(B;\mathbb Z)\), quotienting graph cycles by boundaries of its flag 2-simplices, namely oriented graph triangles. This is natural under automorphisms, even if the quotient has torsion.

**5. Complete-graph representation.** If \(G=K_n\), then \(B\) is a simplex and \(H_2(B)=0\). Every three-subset is a triangle. The alternating free lattice on oriented three-subsets of \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) is, by definition, the third exterior power of the natural permutation lattice. The absence of invariants follows from transitivity on three-subsets together with a transposition exchanging two vertices within a fixed triple; invariance would force its coefficient to be its negative, hence zero over \(\mathbb Z\) or \(\mathbb Q\). This deduction holds for every \(n\ge3\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_total_graph.py` constructs all connected labeled graphs on two to five vertices and 85 additional connected random six-vertex graphs (856 instances altogether); checks the distance-two graph, the classification and links of core faces, actual integral octahedral cycle boundaries and independent exceptional coordinates, and mod-two homology ranks. It visits 46,943 total-complex faces in the full at-most-five-vertex census. The separate `verify_equivariant.py` verifies the signed character independently by exact simplicial Lefschetz chain traces for all 6, 24, 120 and 720 automorphisms of \(K_3,K_4,K_5,K_6\), respectively. Recorded program output is included. Neither finite computation substitutes for the proof for arbitrary \(G\) or \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers* (first publicly posted April 3, 2011; journal publication 2012), Theorem 5.1, **already proves** the unrestricted nonequivariant homotopy formula \(\operatorname{Cl}(T(G))\simeq\operatorname{Cl}(G)\vee\bigvee^{t(G)}S^2\), via a core collapse and exceptional triangles. The present statement does **not** claim this homotopy type as new. The additional assertion is the **canonical, integral, automorphism-equivariant decomposition** determined by explicit octahedral cycles, its signed character (rather than an unsigned permutation module), and the equivariant persistence morphism. The 2011/2012 theorem does not state the group action, a canonical integral cycle splitting, or an equivariant persistence representation. Merely knowing the nonequivariant homotopy type cannot determine the group representation, though the known relative-cell proof makes the new refinement natural to investigate.

Galetto, Montaño and Wellner's June 18, 2026 study of group actions on Vietoris–Rips homology of hypercubes motivates investigating *integral symmetry representations* of graph-metric Rips complexes. Its object is the Hamming cube rather than a graph subdivision. A separately published calculation of the nonequivariant full Rips filtration of \(S(K_n)\) supplies the rank \(\binom n3\) but does not supply the integral \(S_n\)-representation. The new signed module distinguishes automorphisms with the same effect on sphere multiplicities, and gives a uniform all-graph statement that is not a consequence of the older scalar Betti counts alone.

## Limitations
The group action is the natural \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-action; no statement is made about all automorphisms of the total graph that need not preserve the original/edge vertex types. The result gives homology modules and the scale-one-to-two map, **not** an equivariant homeomorphism or space-level wedge splitting. Complete classification of later Rips scales is not claimed. The underlying nonequivariant theorem was known since 2011; the novelty and value here rest solely on the symmetry-sensitive integral refinement. Earlier unindexed equivariant formulations or an explicit derivation in related work remain bibliographic risks. The finite verification samples are neither a proof of the infinite result nor an originality certificate.
