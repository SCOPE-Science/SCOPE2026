# Integral torsion and the mod-two cup square in an antipodal Möbius-ladder independence quotient

## Finding
Let \(M_{12}\) be the graph with vertices \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\) and edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and \(\{i,i+6\}\), with indices modulo twelve. Put \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(M_{12})\). The antipodal automorphism \(a(i)=i+6\) induces an involution on \(|X|\), and let \(Y=|X|/\langle a\rangle\).

**Theorem.** The action of \(\langle a\rangle\) is free, the quotient has \(\pi_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), and its complete integral homology is
\[
H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&j=0,2,\\
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&j=1,\\
0,&j\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
The complete mod-two cohomology ring is
\[
H^*(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong
\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x^3,xy,y^2),\qquad |x|=1,\ |y|=2,
\]
where \(x\) is the nonzero class in \(H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\), \(y\) completes \(x^2\) to a basis of \(H^2(Y;\mathbb F_2)\), and **\(x^2\ne0\)**. In particular the quotient has integral two-torsion although the covering independence complex has free integral homology.

## Assumptions and scope
The independence complex \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) consists of all subsets of vertices of \(G\) containing no adjacent pair. Simplicial complexes and their geometric realizations are distinguished; the quotient is a finite regular CW complex, not asserted to be an ordinary simplicial complex with the same orbit vertices. The term \(\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x^3,xy,y^2)\) is graded-commutative with the displayed degrees. The theorem is an exact classification for the twelve-vertex graph; no corresponding statement is claimed for every ladder width.

## Proof
**1. A free regular CW quotient.** Every antipodal vertex pair \(\{i,i+6\}\) is an edge of \(M_{12}\); hence no nonempty independent simplex contains both vertices of such a pair. If \(\sigma\) is a nonempty face, then \(\sigma\cap a(\sigma)=\varnothing\), and its orbit consists of two distinct faces. Thus no point of \(|X|\) is fixed by \(a\). More strongly, each simplex embeds in the quotient together with its entire boundary, and different faces of the same simplex have distinct orbits. Therefore the face orbits form a regular CW structure on \(Y\), with each orbit cell bounded by the ordinary faces of its representative simplex. Exhaustive independent-set enumeration gives the covering face-vector \((12,48,76,48,12)\) in degrees zero through four and the orbit-cell vector \((6,24,38,24,6)\), with \(98\) total cells and Euler characteristic \(2\). All these finite statements are checked from the adjacency rule, not accepted from a stored count.

**2. The covering complex is simply connected.** The \(1\)-skeleton of \(X\) is connected. The ordinary edge-path presentation of its fundamental group starts with one generator for every edge outside a spanning tree, and has triangular relations for every \(2\)-simplex. The included `CERTIFICATE.json` specifies an eleven-edge spanning tree and a sequence of \(37\) triangles. Each successive triangle has exactly one edge not previously generated by the tree and earlier triangles, and every one of the \(48\) edges is obtained after the final step. Accordingly all \(37\) edge-path generators die, proving \(\pi_1(X)=0\). The verifier checks the complete edge and triangle lists, the spanning-tree property, and every elimination step. Because the antipodal involution acts freely and \(X\) is simply connected, \(X\to Y\) is the universal two-sheeted covering, so \(\pi_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Consequently \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

**3. Eliminate all higher orbit cells.** Each row in the `collapse_pairs` array of `CERTIFICATE.json` names a face-orbit cell and a unique codimension-one coface-orbit cell. The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs all \(98\) orbit cells and all incidence relations; at every successive step it checks that the lower cell has exactly one remaining coface and that the pair lifts to a pair of antipodal elementary simplicial collapses. There are exactly \(32\) valid elementary collapses, leaving a two-dimensional regular CW subcomplex with cell vector \((6,16,12)\). Thus \(H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(j\ge3\), and \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\), as a subgroup of a free cellular chain group in degree two, is torsion-free. Since \(\chi(Y)=2\), \(H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), and \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\) is finite, its free rank is one. Therefore \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), finishing the integral homology classification.

**4. The nonzero cup square.** The universal coefficient theorem gives \(H^1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). The coefficient exact sequence \(0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb F_2\to0\) has a connecting Bockstein \(\delta:H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\to H^2(Y;\mathbb Z)\). Since \(H^1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2\), it sends the unique nonzero class \(x\) to the nonzero order-two class. Reduction modulo two maps that order-two class injectively into \(H^2(Y;\mathbb F_2)\): the only element divisible by two in \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) lies in the free factor. The mod-two reduction of the integral Bockstein is \(\mathrm{Sq}^1\), and \(\mathrm{Sq}^1(x)=x\smile x\) in degree one. Hence \(x^2\ne0\). By the universal coefficient theorem \(H^2(Y;\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2^2\); take another generator \(y\) complementary to \(x^2\). Since the quotient has no cohomology beyond degree two, all products in degree greater than two vanish. These facts give precisely the displayed ring.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify.py` reads the included `CERTIFICATE.json`, builds the twelve-vertex graph, exhaustively enumerates all \(197\) independent faces including the empty face, and checks the free involution and exact \(98\)-cell quotient. It replays the eleven-edge spanning tree and \(37\) triangular relations certifying simple connectivity, then all \(32\) cellular collapse pairs. As an independent check of the orbit boundary model, binary Gaussian elimination yields mod-two Betti numbers \((1,1,2,0,0)\), as predicted by the integral classification and universal coefficient theorem. Running `python verify.py` in the same directory as `CERTIFICATE.json` produces the included `OUTPUT.txt`, ending with `VERIFY_OK`. Finite certificates establish these exact finite statements, while the Bockstein computation proves the ring statement; no finite observation is extrapolated to an infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, *The Homotopy Types of the Independence and Perfect Matching Complexes of Möbius and Circular Ladder Graphs* (arXiv:2608.30601v1, first public 2026-08-31), classify the ordinary independence complexes of Möbius ladders. Their Theorem 12 gives \(X\simeq\bigvee^3S^2\) for this graph, but does not determine any antipodal orbit-space fundamental group, integral homology or cup products. The published *Dihedral rational homology representations of Möbius-ladder independence complexes* gives the full rational dihedral character; it therefore constrains rational quotient Betti numbers, but neither establishes integral two-torsion nor identifies the nonzero mod-two cup square. Our result does not claim novelty for their nonequivariant homotopy type or rational character. The matching-complex results for this graph family concern a different simplicial object. The new invariant here is the full integral quotient homology and mod-two multiplicative cohomology, not an isolated recalculation of a known sphere count.

## Limitations
This is a finite, specific-graph result, not an infinite-family orbit-space classification. The mod-two cohomology ring happens to agree with that of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\vee S^2\), but a homotopy equivalence to that wedge is **not** claimed or proved: a cohomology ring does not determine the full homotopy type. The integral homology is fully established, but possible higher homotopy invariants are not computed. Specialized unindexed results might give equivalent integral orbit-space information; independent scientific validation has not been performed.
