# Scientific review: irrational sweep-phase law for two-node Verlet SDC

## Correctness — PASS

The stated two-node quadrature, strictly lower-triangular velocity-Verlet position matrix, and stage recurrence specialize the published equations to the scalar harmonic oscillator with constant stage initialization. Direct symbolic integration supplies the displayed matrices. The collocation limit is the two-stage Gauss method, with exact unit determinant. Subtracting its stages from the finite-sweep stages yields an exact linear error recurrence. Its two-node compression is the explicit real matrix \(H\), and a fixed-sweep determinant expansion gives the precise coefficient \((-1)^kqH^kc\) at order \(z^{k+1}\). The corresponding quadratic Schur conditions have two automatically positive factors for small positive \(z\), so the determinant coefficient controls strict stability versus instability. The complex eigenvalues of \(H\) give an irrational phase: \(2\cos(2\theta)=\sqrt3-1\), whose conjugate \(-\sqrt3-1\) cannot be a root-of-unity trace. Irrational-rotation equidistribution proves both half-density assertions. These are analytic arguments for all positive integers, not extrapolations from tests.

The standalone `verify.py` passed exact symbolic reconstructions of the quadrature and preconditioner, the Gauss collocation map and its determinant, six independent symbolic leading-coefficient checks, and nine independent high-precision forward-substitution comparisons with the claimed leading signs and scales. Reproduction needs Python, SymPy, and mpmath. Potential misinterpretation of sweep numbering is avoided by specifying exactly \(k\) applications to constant initial stages.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk

The closest primary source is Akramov, Götschel, Minion, Ruprecht, and Speck, *Spectral deferred correction methods for second-order problems* (arXiv:2310.08352, first public 12 October 2023; DOI: 10.1137/23M1592596). Its detailed SDC sweep and oscillator-stability derivation, Section 3 and Table 1 were inspected in accessible full HTML. It reports a nonmonotone pattern for small sweep counts and states that the observed apparent odd/even behavior was unexplained. Its convergence and consistency theorems do not establish the present all-sweep-count local determinant coefficient or natural-density classification. The preprint and final open-access journal text agree on the relevant formulas and stability table.

A distinct prior first-order Gauss–Lobatto, implicit-Euler-preconditioned SDC computation obtains a half-density irrational-phase law for a stiff scalar negative-real-axis limit. That mathematical result does not imply this second-order Gauss–Legendre velocity-Verlet oscillator law: its underlying stage preconditioner, limiting regime and stability diagnostic are different. Classical Gaussian collocation symplecticity, Schur stability conditions, and irrational rotation theory are recognized as standard tools, not claimed as new results. Targeted searches for equivalent statements and broader coverage identified no result containing the exact new theorem. Related Boris-SDC literature was available only through abstracts/background summaries, so unrecognized overlap in specialized older corrections or more recent unindexed work remains possible. Self-review is not an independent publication-priority certification.

## Value — PASS

A directly motivated unresolved issue in the primary paper is why changing the number of second-order SDC sweeps unpredictably switches harmonic-oscillator stability. The result provides an explicit, structurally interpretable two-node compression and a sharp all-count classification. The main conclusion rules out a simplistic permanent odd/even rule: despite the first-four pattern, each sign occurs for precisely half of sweep counts in natural density. This matters for interpreting long-time use of high-order correction sweeps: taking more sweeps at a fixed small step does not monotonically improve strict Schur stability, although the converged collocation map is neutrally stable. This is a new finite-method phenomenon, not a normalization artifact or an arbitrary numerical slice.

The theorem is restricted to two interior Gauss–Legendre nodes, exact arithmetic, constant initial nodal positions, an undamped scalar oscillator, and the fixed-\(k\) small-\(z\) regime. It gives no uniform step threshold over all counts and no theorem for different nodes, damping or nonlinear problems. These limitations do not undermine the relevance of the exact density to the motivating numerical observation.
