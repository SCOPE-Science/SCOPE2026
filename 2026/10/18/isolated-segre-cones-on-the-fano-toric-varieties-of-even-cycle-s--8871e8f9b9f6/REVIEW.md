# Review of the even-cycle symmetric-edge toric singularity classification

## Correctness — PASS

The proof works for every integer \(r\ge2\) and over every algebraically closed field. It explicitly fixes the root lattice and the face fan, writes down the polar slab cube, proves that for \(n=2r\) the polar has exactly \(\binom{2r}{r}\) vertices, and identifies each dual facet's \(2r\) primitive generators. Every \((2r-1)\)-element deletion is a unimodular basis; hence every proper cone is smooth. The exact dual semigroup is the lattice of balanced nonnegative exponent vectors, generated by cross-block pairings. Elementary pairing exchanges supply the minor relations, giving the entire rank-one \(r\times r\) determinantal affine chart. Segre intersection theory proves the multiplicity. The finite arithmetic checker confirms the facet and lattice assertions through \(n=10\) but is not used to infer the infinite theorem. The chart dimension, projective multiplicity, and lattice normalization have been separately checked for consistency.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Higashitani (arXiv:1103.2202, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3) already establishes the symmetric-graph smoothness versus even-cycle criterion, and Ohsugi–Shibata (arXiv:1109.0791) recall known odd-cycle smoothness. Those implications are clearly excluded from the originality claim. Matsui et al. (arXiv:1003.5444) introduce the symmetric-edge polytope and study its Gorenstein/Ehrhart geometry. The inspected primary treatments do not specify the **number and complete local analytic/algebraic types** of even-cycle singularities. Equivalent descriptions as homogeneous-cycle cones, non-simplicial Fano facets, Segre toric singularities and determinantal rank-one charts were compared, including potential implication from Higashitani's broad smoothness theorem; that theorem does not supply the local ring. Searches and source reading do not rule out all unindexed or differently formulated earlier work, so this residual originality risk remains. Neither the parity criterion nor the existence of nonsimplicial even-cycle facets is claimed as a new result in isolation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The symmetric-edge polytope of a simple cycle is a canonical reflexive Fano example with a sharp smoothness parity boundary. Knowing only that an even cycle is singular does not determine the dimension, number, incidence or local types of its singularities. The present complete all-parameter calculation resolves the precise geometric structure: all singularities are isolated, all occur at torus-fixed points, and all have a uniform explicit rank-one determinantal model with growing, exact multiplicity \(\binom{2r-2}{r-1}\). The family links toric graph constructions to Segre singularities and local factoriality/Gorenstein behavior. This is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary isolated small-case invariant. The claim does not extend to other graphs, nor to the toric variety of the *normal* fan.

## Closest literature and limitations

- T. Matsui et al., *Roots of Ehrhart polynomials arising from graphs*, arXiv:1003.5444 (2010): polytope definition, reflexivity, Ehrhart results, not the local singularity classification.
- A. Higashitani, *Smooth Fano polytopes arising from finite directed graphs*, arXiv:1103.2202 (2011): stronger published global smoothness criterion; nonlocal implication explicitly credited.
- H. Ohsugi and K. Shibata, *Smooth Fano polytopes whose Ehrhart polynomial has a root with large real part*, arXiv:1109.0791 (2011): previously known odd-cycle smoothness and Ehrhart roots.
