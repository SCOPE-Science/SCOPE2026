# Isometric moduli and symmetry collapse for positive cubical hyperplanes

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge3\). For a vector of strictly positive real weights \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), let
\[
H_a=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i=0\right\},
\qquad \|x\|_{H_a}=\max_{1\le i\le n}|x_i|.
\]
Put \(S_a=\sum_i a_i\) and call \(a\) **balanced** when \(2\max_i a_i<S_a\). The following is a complete real-linear isometry classification.

**Classification theorem.** If \(a\) is not balanced, then \(H_a\) is isometric to \(\ell_\infty^{n-1}(\mathbb R)\). If \(a\) is balanced, its unit ball has exactly \(2n\) facets, and \(H_a\) is not isometric to \(\ell_\infty^{n-1}(\mathbb R)\). For two balanced positive vectors \(a,b\),
\[
H_a\cong_{\rm iso} H_b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\left\{\frac{a_i}{S_a}:1\le i\le n\right\}_{\rm multi}
=
\left\{\frac{b_i}{S_b}:1\le i\le n\right\}_{\rm multi}.
\]
Every such surjective linear isometry is the restriction of a **unique** ambient global-sign coordinate permutation matching these normalized weights. In particular, if the distinct entries of \(a\) have multiplicities \(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), then in the balanced regime
\[
\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathbb R}(H_a)
\cong C_2\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}S_{m_j},
\qquad |\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathbb R}(H_a)|=2\prod_{j=1}^r m_j!.
\]
In the nonbalanced regime its isometry group is the hyperoctahedral group of dimension \(n-1\), of order \(2^{n-1}(n-1)!\). Thus there is one cubical isometry type and, separately, a continuous family of inequivalent balanced types for every \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

All spaces and isometries are **real**. The weights are strictly positive, with no assumption of integrality or pairwise distinctness; their common positive scale is irrelevant. An isometry means a surjective linear norm-preserving map, not an affine or nonlinear metric-space isometry. All claims hold in every finite dimension \(n\ge3\). Vectors with a zero weight are outside this theorem. The distinction at equality \(2\max a_i=S_a\) belongs to the nonbalanced (cubical) case.

## Proof

**1. The dominant case.** If \(2a_k\ge S_a\), then for every \(x\in H_a\),
\[
|x_k|=\frac{|\sum_{i\ne k}a_i x_i|}{a_k}
\le\frac{S_a-a_k}{a_k}\max_{i\ne k}|x_i|
\le\max_{i\ne k}|x_i|.
\]
Given arbitrary other coordinates, the equation uniquely determines \(x_k\) and this bound holds. Dropping coordinate \(k\) therefore is an onto linear isometry from \(H_a\) to \(\ell_\infty^{n-1}\). At most one coordinate can be dominant because all weights are positive. The cube has \(2(n-1)\) facets and the stated hyperoctahedral isometry group.

**2. Every coordinate inequality is a genuine facet in the balanced case.** Let \(B_a=\{x\in H_a:\max_i|x_i|\le1\}\). For each \(i\), define
\[
z^{(i)}_i=1,\qquad
z^{(i)}_j=-\frac{a_i}{S_a-a_i}\quad(j\ne i).
\]
The defining weighted sum vanishes and strict balance gives \(|z^{(i)}_j|<1\) for all \(j\ne i\). Hence \(B_a\cap\{x_i=1\}\) contains a relative interior point of the hyperplane \(H_a\cap\{x_i=1\}\) and is a facet. Central symmetry gives the opposite facet. Since the \(2n\) displayed coordinate inequalities already define \(B_a\), no other facets exist. The coordinate restrictions \(f_i^a(x)=x_i\), regarded as elements of \(H_a^*\), are distinct up to signs for \(n\ge3\). Indeed, no nonzero ambient vector supported on two coordinates is proportional to the all-positive vector \(a\). By polar duality, the dual unit ball has exactly the \(2n\) extreme points \(\{\pm f_i^a\}_{i=1}^n\).

**3. The unique circuit recovers every weight.** The functionals \(f_i^a\) span \(H_a^*\), whose dimension is \(n-1\), and satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n a_i f_i^a=0.
\]
Their space of linear relations is therefore one-dimensional, generated by the strictly positive coefficient vector \(a\). If \(U:H_a\to H_b\) is a surjective real-linear isometry between balanced spaces, its dual sends the extreme points of the target dual unit ball bijectively onto those of the source. Consequently, for some permutation \(\pi\in S_n\) and signs \(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
f_i^b\circ U=\varepsilon_i f_{\pi(i)}^a.
\]
Applying this identity to the relation \(\sum_i b_i f_i^b=0\), uniqueness of the source relation yields a nonzero real scalar \(c\) with
\[
b_i\varepsilon_i=c\,a_{\pi(i)}\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
All \(a_i,b_i\) are positive, so the signs \(\varepsilon_i\) agree. Taking absolute values and summing gives \(|c|=S_b/S_a\), proving equality of normalized multisets. Moreover \((Ux)_i=\varepsilon_i x_{\pi(i)}\), so the isometry is an ambient global-sign coordinate permutation. Distinct coordinate restrictions show its ambient extension is unique.

**4. Converse, mutual exclusion, and groups.** If \(b_i/S_b=a_{\pi(i)}/S_a\), the ambient permutation \((Ux)_i=x_{\pi(i)}\), or its negative, takes \(H_a\) onto \(H_b\) isometrically. Balanced spaces have \(2n\) facets, whereas nonbalanced ones have \(2(n-1)\), so the regimes cannot be isometric. For \(a=b\), the allowed permutations are exactly the coordinate permutations preserving each weight level, and the common sign is independent of them; this gives the displayed direct-product group and order. In the nonbalanced regime, transfer the standard signed-permutation isometry group through the coordinate-deletion isometry in Step 1. This completes the classification.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 checker `checker.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. It inspects all \(441\) nondecreasing integer weight vectors with \(3\le n\le6\) and entries from \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\): \(417\) balanced and \(24\) dominant. It confirms \(4374\) positive/negative relative-interior facet witnesses, checks the defining dual circuit, tests signed-permutation stabilizers on balanced samples through dimension five, and tests elimination in dominant cases. Every assertion passed. Run `python3 checker.py` using only the Python standard library. These finite checks are independent consistency tests; the proof above, not enumeration, establishes the theorem for all positive real weights and all \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work

Sohel, Sain and Paul, *On subspaces of \(\ell_\infty\) and extreme contraction in \(L(X,\ell_\infty^n)\)*, arXiv:2208.10184v1 (August 22, 2022), study minimal finite sup-norm embeddings, facet counts (Theorem 2.8) and extreme contractions. In particular, facet counting distinguishes the cubical and noncubical regimes. The present classification goes beyond facet counts: it recovers the complete normalized coefficient multiset from the abstract Banach-space norm, identifies **every** isometry between balanced hyperplanes, and determines the full symmetry groups, including their discontinuous change at the dominance boundary.

Sain, Sohel and Paul, *Extreme contractions on finite-dimensional Banach spaces*, arXiv:2407.05545v1 (July 8, 2024), characterize extreme contractions in finite-dimensional polyhedral operator spaces, but do not give this coefficient-recovery classification of weighted sup-norm hyperplanes. Standard invariance of facets and polar duality are the starting ingredients here; the positive one-circuit rigidity is the decisive special feature.

## Limitations

This is a classification of surjective **linear** isometries for strictly positive weights only. It does not classify arbitrary embeddings, nonlinear sphere isometries, vectors containing zero coefficients, or the Banach–Mazur distance between different balanced types. The literature comparison found no explicit statement equivalent to the classification; a general older theorem on uniqueness of minimal finite cubical representations might subsume it, which remains the principal bibliographic risk. The checker provides no general-purpose formal proof and does not establish novelty.
