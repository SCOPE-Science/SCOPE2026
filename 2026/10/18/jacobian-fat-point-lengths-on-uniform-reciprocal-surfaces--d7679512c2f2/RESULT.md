# Jacobian fat-point lengths on uniform reciprocal surfaces

## Finding
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. Choose \(n\ge4\) linear forms \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n\) on a three-dimensional vector space, with every triple linearly independent. Define the projective reciprocal surface
\[
X_n=\overline{\{[1/\ell_1(p):\cdots:1/\ell_n(p)]:p\in\mathbb P^2,\ \ell_1(p)\cdots\ell_n(p)\ne0\}}\ \subset\mathbb P^{n-1}.
\]
For the intrinsic Jacobian singular scheme \(\Sigma=V(\operatorname{Fitt}_2\Omega_{X_n/k})\), write \(P_i\) for the coordinate point with its \(i\)-th coordinate nonzero, \(r=n-2\), and \(\mathfrak m\) for the homogeneous maximal ideal of the \(r\)-th Veronese cone \(A_r=k[u^r,u^{r-1}v,\ldots,v^r]\). Then \(\Sigma\) has support exactly \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\), and there are local completed-ring isomorphisms
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,P_i}\cong\widehat{(A_r)_{\mathfrak m}},\qquad
(\operatorname{Fitt}_2\Omega_{\mathcal O_{X_n,P_i}/k})\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,P_i}=\mathfrak m^{r-1}\widehat{(A_r)_{\mathfrak m}}.
\]
The second expression denotes extension of the intrinsic Fitting ideal to the completion; no claim is made about ordinary (noncontinuous) differentials of the completed ring. Consequently every local singular subscheme has length
\[
\operatorname{length}(\mathcal O_{\Sigma,P_i})=
\sum_{d=0}^{r-2}(rd+1)=\frac{(r-1)(r(r-2)+2)}2,
\]
and the zero-dimensional projective scheme has degree
\[
\boxed{\deg\Sigma=\frac{n(n-3)((n-2)(n-4)+2)}2.}
\]
For \(n=4,5,6,7\), respectively, the local lengths are \(1,5,15,34\), and the total degrees are \(4,25,90,238\).

## Assumptions and scope
Every triple of forms is independent; equivalently the underlying rank-three representable matroid is uniform. The field is algebraically closed and has characteristic zero; this is needed for irreducibility of symmetric-power representations in the Jacobian-ideal argument. The intrinsic singular scheme is defined by the second Fitting ideal of Kähler differentials of a pure two-dimensional variety, not by an arbitrary choice of a generating set or an ambient thickening. The result describes local scheme structure and total length; it does not assert global presentation formulas for the Fitting ideal in all projective charts.

## Proof
**Step 1: the exact local analytic type.** Fix \(i\). After a linear change of source coordinates, take \(\ell_i=s\) and write \(\ell_j=L_j(u,v)+c_js\) for \(j\ne i\). Every two \(L_j\) are linearly independent, as otherwise \(s,\ell_j,\ell_h\) would be dependent. In the target chart with the \(i\)-th coordinate equal to one, set \(z_j=Y_j/Y_i=s/(L_j+c_js)\). The rational coordinate substitution
\[
w_j=\frac{z_j}{1-c_jz_j},\qquad z_j=\frac{w_j}{1+c_jw_j}
\]
is an isomorphism of completed ambient local rings at the origin and transforms the reciprocal parametrization into \(w_j=s/L_j(u,v)\). On the dense open set where all \(L_j\) are nonzero, write \(\tau=s/\prod_{h\ne i}L_h(u,v)\), so \(w_j=\tau F_j(u,v)\) where
\[
F_j(u,v)=\prod_{\substack{h\ne i\\h\ne j}}L_h(u,v),\qquad \deg F_j=n-2=r.
\]
The \(r+1\) binary degree-\(r\) forms \(F_j\) are linearly independent: evaluation at the unique zero of \(L_j\) kills all \(F_h\) with \(h\ne j\) and does not kill \(F_j\). They are therefore a basis of \(H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(r))\). After an invertible linear change of target coordinates, the parametrized image is the standard affine cone over the degree-\(r\) rational normal curve, whose ring is \(A_r\). Since the chart image is the closure of this dense parametrized locus, its local completion equals the vertex completion of \(A_r\); there is no additional branch or component.

**Step 2: the vertex Fitting ideal.** Write \(A_r=k[x_0,\ldots,x_r]/I\), with \(x_j=u^{r-j}v^j\) and \(I\) generated by the \(2\times2\) minors of the Hankel matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}x_0&x_1&\cdots&x_{r-1}\\x_1&x_2&\cdots&x_r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is the standard determinantal presentation of a rational normal curve cone. The natural presentation of \(\Omega_{A_r/k}\) has \(r+1\) generators and Jacobian rows given by those quadrics. Thus \(\operatorname{Fitt}_2\Omega_{A_r/k}\) is generated by \((r-1)\)-minors of that Jacobian. All its entries are linear, proving inclusion in \(\mathfrak m^{r-1}\). Conversely, the \(r-1\) quadrics \(q_j=x_0x_{j+1}-x_1x_j\) for \(1\le j\le r-1\) give, on differentiation with respect to \(x_2,\ldots,x_r\), a triangular Jacobian submatrix with diagonal entries \(x_0\). Its determinant is \(x_0^{r-1}\), hence the Fitting ideal has a nonzero degree-\((r-1)\) element. It is stable under the natural \(\operatorname{SL}_2(k)\)-action. The degree-\((r-1)\) piece of \(A_r\) is the irreducible characteristic-zero module \(\operatorname{Sym}^{r(r-1)}(k^2)\); therefore the invariant subspace of the Fitting ideal in that degree is the entire piece. Since \(A_r\) is generated in degree one, its entire ideal \(\mathfrak m^{r-1}\) follows. Equality holds before and after completion.

**Step 3: support and length.** The established singular-support theorem for reciprocal planes (Sanyal–Sturmfels–Vinzant, Corollary 26 and Example 38) specializes to the \(n\) coordinate points for a uniform rank-three matroid. Independently, every point on the dense coordinate torus is smooth by the inverse torus automorphism, and the boundary classification rules out additional singular strata. On a degree-\(r\) Veronese cone, \(\dim_k(A_r)_d=rd+1\). Modulo \(\mathfrak m^{r-1}\), exactly the degrees \(0\le d\le r-2\) remain, yielding the displayed finite sum and global degree.

## Verification
A standalone script named `verify.py` verifies, by exact rational symbolic arithmetic, that the requisite Hankel Jacobian minors span the full expected \(\operatorname{SL}_2\)-module for \(r=2,3,4\). It independently checks a nonzero determinant of the binary-form basis for those degrees and evaluates the length formula for the first thirty degrees. To execute: `python verify.py` (requires SymPy). Its expected final line starts `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are corroboration only: the all-\(n\) and characteristic-zero conclusions rest on the explicit coordinate transformation, invariant-theoretic argument, and graded-ring calculation above.

## Relationship to prior work
Proudfoot–Speyer established the circuit ideal, broken-circuit degeneration, Cohen–Macaulayness, and the matroid-indexed strata of reciprocal linear spaces. Sanyal–Sturmfels–Vinzant, Theorem 24, proved the scheme-theoretic tangent-cone formula and, in Corollary 26 and Example 38, identified the reduced singular support for the generic rank-three case. These facts are used and **not claimed as new**. The present conclusion distinguishes the complete local ring from its tangent cone through an explicit coordinate transformation, then determines the intrinsic, potentially nonreduced Jacobian subscheme and its lengths. Kummer–Vinzant's determinantal Chow-form work is different from this local intrinsic Fitting calculation. No claim of novelty is made for the classical Veronese presentation itself.

## Limitations
The formula is proved only in characteristic zero. Known reduced singular-support classifications and tangent-cone statements are prior work, rather than new conclusions. The originality comparison is necessarily nonexhaustive; a general determination of Jacobian ideals of Veronese cones could imply the local calculation, and literature under different terminology may cover it. The existence of a computer check is neither an independent audit nor proof of the infinite-dimensional representation step.
