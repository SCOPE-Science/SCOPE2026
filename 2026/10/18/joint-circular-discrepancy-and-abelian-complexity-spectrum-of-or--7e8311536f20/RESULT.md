# Joint circular-discrepancy and Abelian-complexity spectrum of order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal B_5\) consist of the binary de Bruijn cycles of order five, identified under cyclic rotation but **not** under bit complementation or reversal. Thus each cycle is a length-\(32\) binary circle containing every length-five binary string exactly once.

For a cycle \(B\), let \(D(B)\) be its **circular discrepancy**: the maximum of \(\bigl||u|_1-|u|_0\bigr|\) over circular factors \(u\) of lengths from \(1\) to \(32\), inclusive. Let \(\rho_B(\ell)\) be the number of distinct Parikh vectors (equivalently, distinct numbers of ones) among the \(32\) length-\(\ell\) circular factors, and let \(P(B)=\max_{1\le\ell\le32}\rho_B(\ell)\) be the **peak circular Abelian complexity**.

**Theorem (complete joint finite spectrum).** There are exactly \(2048\) cycles in \(\mathcal B_5\). For every pair \((d,p)\), the number of cycles satisfying \(\bigl(D(B),P(B)\bigr)=(d,p)\) is zero except in the following eight cases:

| Discrepancy \(d\) | Abelian peak \(p\) | Cycles | Symmetry orbits of size two | Symmetry orbits of size four |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 | 6 | 224 | 8 | 52 |
| 6 | 6 | 304 | 0 | 76 |
| 6 | 7 | 384 | 8 | 92 |
| 7 | 6 | 48 | 0 | 12 |
| 7 | 7 | 640 | 0 | 160 |
| 7 | 8 | 224 | 12 | 50 |
| 8 | 8 | 192 | 0 | 48 |
| 8 | 9 | 32 | 4 | 6 |
| **Total** | | **2048** | **32** | **496** |

The symmetry group is generated by reversal and bitwise complementation, acting on rotation classes. In particular, exactly \(224\) order-five cycles attain the smallest possible discrepancy \(5\), while exactly \(224\) have discrepancy \(8\). Peak Abelian complexity alone does not determine discrepancy: for example, \(P(B)=6\) occurs with \(D(B)\) equal to \(5\), \(6\), or \(7\). There are exactly \(528\) reversal/complementation orbits. Every displayed pair occurs, and no other pair does.

The discrepancy marginal is \(5:224,\;6:688,\;7:912,\;8:224\). The Abelian-peak marginal is \(6:576,\;7:1024,\;8:416,\;9:32\). For a uniformly chosen rotation class, \(\mathbb E[D]=839/128\) and \(\mathbb E[P]=445/64\).

## Assumptions and scope

Circular factors begin at any of the \(32\) positions and traverse at most one full circumference. Repeated circular occurrences are allowed when forming the set of Parikh vectors, but \(\rho_B(\ell)\) counts *distinct* vectors. The empty word is not considered. Cycles are rooted for enumeration at their unique occurrence of \(00000\) and then identified under rotation only; reversal/complementation are used solely to tabulate optional symmetry orbits, **not** to divide the cycle count. The claim is exact only at binary order five. No higher-order enumeration or asymptotic distribution is asserted.

## Proof

**Reduction 1: cyclic discrepancy.** Give bit \(1\) increment \(+1\) and bit \(0\) increment \(-1\). Every de Bruijn cycle has \(16\) of each bit, so its total increment is zero. Let \(S_t\), for \(0\le t\le32\), be its prefix sums, with \(S_0=S_{32}=0\). An interval increment is a difference of two prefix sums. If the extreme prefix-sum indices appear in the wrong order, the complementary circular interval has the opposite increment. Consequently
\[
D(B)=\max_{0\le t\le32}S_t-\min_{0\le t\le32}S_t.
\]
This permits a linear-time calculation independently checkable by evaluating all circular intervals.

**Reduction 2: circular Abelian complexity.** At a fixed length \(\ell\), the number of ones in consecutive circular windows changes by one of \(-1,0,+1\). Since the sequence of window counts is cyclic, every integer between its minimum \(m_\ell\) and maximum \(M_\ell\) occurs. Thus
\[
\rho_B(\ell)=M_\ell-m_\ell+1,
\quad
P(B)=1+\max_{1\le\ell\le32}(M_\ell-m_\ell).
\]
At length \(32\), every circular factor contains all \(16\) ones, so \(\rho_B(32)=1\); the verifier may therefore omit this length without changing the peak. In particular \(P(B)\ge6\), since all length-five binary words occur. The prefix-sum range also gives \(P(B)\le D(B)+1\). These bounds are consistency checks, not the source of the eight-cell enumeration.

**Reduction 3: exhaustive finite domain.** Construct the binary de Bruijn directed graph on the sixteen length-four vertices, with an edge for each length-five word. Each vertex has two incoming edges and two outgoing edges. A directed Euler circuit induces a bijection from its two incoming edges to its two outgoing edges at every vertex; there are exactly two such pairings per vertex. Conversely, a choice of the \(16\) pairings defines a permutation of the \(32\) edges, and it corresponds to an Euler circuit precisely when that permutation has one cycle. Hence the exhaustive finite domain comprises exactly \(2^{16}=65536\) pairing assignments. Fix the unique edge \(00000\) as the starting edge to remove all cyclic-rotation multiplicities.

The standalone program `verify.py` independently enumerates the Euler tours a second way, by depth-first search on unused edges beginning with \(00000\). Both methods produce precisely the same \(2048\) rooted cycles; an explicit factor-uniqueness check confirms that every output is an order-five de Bruijn cycle. This agreement is an additional verification rather than an unproved completeness assumption: the pairing argument covers every possible Euler circuit, and the depth-first search branches over every legal next edge.

**Reduction 4: exact classification and symmetry.** On each of the \(2048\) cycles, the program evaluates the prefix-sum formula and separately enumerates all circular intervals to check \(D\). It computes \(P\) once by materializing the sets of window-one-counts at each length and independently by sliding-window minima and maxima. All pairs of outputs agree cycle by cycle. Incrementing one counter for each output pair yields the exact eight counts displayed above; the counters add to \(2048\). Reversing a cycle or complementing its bits preserves both invariants. Applying these two operations and canonicalizing cyclic rotations partitions the complete set into \(528\) orbits and gives the size-two and size-four columns. For each row, twice the number of size-two orbits plus four times the number of size-four orbits equals the cycle count. `spectrum.csv` contains each row and a concrete cycle witness.

The algorithms are deterministic and entirely finite; there are no random samples, cutoff heuristics, approximate arithmetic operations or unexamined infinite cases.

## Verification

With Python 3 and the two files `verify.py` and `spectrum.csv` in the same directory, execute `python3 verify.py`. The verifier uses only the Python standard library. It examines all \(65536\) local-pairing assignments, independently reconstructs the same \(2048\) cycles by Eulerian backtracking, computes two versions of each invariant for every cycle, checks the eight-cell distribution, and verifies the orbit decomposition. The program also independently checks the number of cycles at orders one through five. All asserted finite counts and cell exclusions are therefore backed by an explicit replayable exhaustive proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Richomme, Saari, and Zamboni introduced and developed the Abelian-complexity framework for infinite words and minimal subshifts [1]; their work provides the Parikh-vector viewpoint but no order-five de Bruijn population distribution. Gabrić and Sawada studied discrepancy in selected de Bruijn constructions and feedback-register families [2], and explicitly identified distribution across all de Bruijn sequences as a direction for future investigation. Their construction-specific tables do not imply an eight-cell exhaustive census. Álvarez, Becher, Mereb, Pajor, and Soto established that the minimum possible discrepancy at every binary order is the order itself [3]; this implies the minimum value \(5\) here but neither its multiplicity nor the joint Abelian-peak classification. The curated de Bruijn properties and construction tables [4] likewise give selected construction values, not the joint population frequency or symmetry-orbit breakdown.

## Limitations

The theorem is a finite classification for the \(2048\) binary order-five rotation classes. It supplies neither a general-order joint distribution nor a new universal minimum-discrepancy construction. The finite proof depends on correctly executing the transparent exhaustive program; two independent enumeration methods and two implementations of each invariant substantially reduce, but do not eliminate, ordinary software-verification risk. No priority claim beyond the inspected literature is asserted. Earlier unindexed numerical tables or supplemental data could contain overlapping values; a separate independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
