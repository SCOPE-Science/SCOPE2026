# Joint discrepancy and reverse-complement symmetry spectrum for binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
A binary order-five de Bruijn cycle is a cyclic binary word of length \(32\) containing every binary block of length \(5\) exactly once. Cycles are identified under rotation, but not under complement or reversal. For such a cycle \(w\), define the **cyclic discrepancy**
\[
D(w)=\max_{u\text{ a cyclic contiguous factor of }w}\bigl|\#_1(u)-\#_0(u)\bigr|.
\]
Let \(c\) complement all bits and \(r\) reverse their order, each acting on rotation classes. A cycle is **CR-fixed** if it equals \(cr(w)\) up to rotation. The complete joint distribution is

| \(D(w)\) | All cycles | CR-fixed cycles | Orbits of size \(2\) under \(\langle c,r\rangle\) | Orbits of size \(4\) |
|:--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| \(5\) | 224 | 16 | 8 | 52 |
| \(6\) | 688 | 16 | 8 | 168 |
| \(7\) | 912 | 24 | 12 | 222 |
| \(8\) | 224 | 8 | 4 | 54 |
| Total | 2048 | 64 | 32 | 496 |

No other discrepancies occur. Thus the 64 CR-fixed cycles split into discrepancy multiplicities \(16,16,24,8\), and the complete quotient under complement and reversal contains \(528\) orbits. The two-element orbits consist exactly of the CR-fixed cycles paired by complement (equivalently by reversal).

The unrestricted marginal distribution and the minimum-discrepancy orbit counts had previously been calculated; the contribution here is the **full joint CR-symmetry and discrepancy distribution**, including all nonminimal discrepancy classes.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\), the de Bruijn order is exactly \(5\), the cyclic length is exactly \(32\), and every rotation class is counted once. The full symmetry group is generated by bitwise complement \(c\) and reversal \(r\); rotations are already identified. Discrepancy is the maximum absolute imbalance over all circular factors, including the empty factor and full period. The theorem does not concern larger orders, linear representatives, or alternative discrepancy norms.

## Proof
**1. Complete, nonredundant finite state-space encoding.** Every cyclic de Bruijn word \(w\) defines a unique successor permutation on the 32 binary five-bit states. Represent a state as \((x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\); its successor must be \((x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,f(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4))\). For each fixed four-bit tail \(t\), the distinct states \((0,t)\) and \((1,t)\) must have different successors: otherwise the state-successor map would not be a permutation. Consequently
\[
f(x_0,t)=x_0\oplus g(t)
\]
for a uniquely defined Boolean function \(g:\{0,1\}^4\to\{0,1\}\), where \(\oplus\) is addition modulo \(2\). Exactly \(2^{16}=65536\) functions \(g\) exist.

For each \(g\), start at the state \(00000\), advance by the prescribed successor rule, and accept if and only if all 32 states are visited exactly once and the 32nd successor returns to \(00000\). Every accepted register yields a de Bruijn cycle, since successive visited states are precisely the length-five cyclic blocks. Conversely, every de Bruijn cycle passes this test, and the unique occurrence of state \(00000\) fixes a unique rotation. No two different \(g\) can yield the same cycle, because all 32 successor values occur and recover \(g\). This proves that the exhaustive loop in `verify_fsr.py` is a mathematically complete and nonduplicating enumeration of the desired objects.

**2. Discrepancy computation.** Every such cycle has 16 zeros and 16 ones (because exactly half of the 32 distinct five-bit blocks start in each symbol). For its uniquely anchored linear representative, set \(p_0=0\) and \(p_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(2w_i-1)\) for \(1\le j\le32\). In particular \(p_{32}=0\). Every nonwrapping factor has imbalance \(p_j-p_i\). Any wrapping factor has imbalance minus that of its complementary nonwrapping factor, because total imbalance is zero. Therefore
\[
D(w)=\max_{0\le j\le32}p_j-\min_{0\le j\le32}p_j.
\]
The exhaustive first checker applies precisely this integer formula to each accepted register.

**3. Symmetry and orbit counts.** The operators \(c\) and \(r\) commute, square to the identity, and preserve \(D\), so each \(D\)-class is a union of orbits of size \(1\), \(2\), or \(4\). To compare rotation classes, the checker computes the lexicographically least rotation of each of \(w,c(w),r(w),cr(w)\). It records the number of distinct canonical words in this set. The complete exact enumeration gives the histogram and the orbit sizes in the table. Separately it checks that \(w\) is CR-fixed precisely when its canonical form equals that of \(cr(w)\), and finds 64 such cycles.

For additional independent coverage of the critical CR-fixed cells, the classical CR characterization (Etzion--Lempel, quoted as Lemma 1.1 by Chang--Wang) says that a cyclic binary word is CR-fixed exactly when some rotation has the form \(X\,cr(X)\), with \(X\) a half-period word. The checker `verify_halfwords.py` enumerates all \(2^{16}\) possible halves \(X\), constructs the 32-bit anti-palindromic word, verifies directly that all 32 length-five cyclic factors are distinct, and deduplicates rotations. It finds 128 qualifying halves, two per each of 64 rotation classes, and independently obtains \(16,16,24,8\) for discrepancies \(5,6,7,8\). It also checks direct witness cycles for each discrepancy.

The first checker finds that complement and reversal have no individually fixed rotation classes. Thus a cycle has a two-element orbit under \(\langle c,r\rangle\) if and only if it is CR-fixed; all others have four-element orbits. Dividing the CR-fixed counts by 2 and the remaining counts by 4 yields all eight orbit counts in the table. Both enumerations use exact finite integer operations, with no heuristic pruning, sampling, floating-point decisions, or extrapolation.

## Verification
Run the two standalone standard-library-only Python programs from the same directory:

```
python3 verify_fsr.py
python3 verify_halfwords.py
```

The first program prints `FSM ALL 2048 [(5, 224), (6, 688), (7, 912), (8, 224)]`, `FSM CR 64 [(5, 16), (6, 16), (7, 24), (8, 8)]`, the eight predicted orbit multiplicities, and `FSR_OK`. The second prints `halfwords valid 128 unique rotations 64 hist {5: 16, 6: 16, 7: 24, 8: 8}` and `HALFWORDS_OK`. The independent second method enumerates only the CR-fixed subset and therefore corroborates its four cells; the first method proves the exhaustive all-cycle and orbit totals.

One explicit representative for each CR-fixed discrepancy is:

Each row can be verified directly by the second program's full cyclic factor test. Finite exhaustive computation is the exact proof here because the target domain contains finitely many explicitly exhausted objects; no claim is made about an infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
Chang and Wang (arXiv:2408.01794, initially public 2024-08-03) construct and characterize CR de Bruijn sequences for odd orders. Lemma 1.1 gives the half-word normal form used here, while Theorem 3.6 proves a divisibility property for CR counts. Their inspected text studies construction and linear complexity, not the joint distribution of circular symbol-balance discrepancy and complement/reversal orbit sizes.

Gabric and Sawada (arXiv:2005.01638, first released 2020-05-04) define and investigate circular discrepancy of binary de Bruijn sequences, mainly through constructions and growth bounds. Álvarez et al. (arXiv:2407.17367, first released 2024-07-24) give constructions attaining optimal global minimum discrepancy; these statements do not enumerate all CR-fixed order-five cycles by discrepancy.

The unrestricted order-five discrepancy counts \(224,688,912,224\), together with the decomposition of the 224 minimum-discrepancy cycles into 8 size-two and 52 size-four symmetry orbits, were previously recorded. Those previously known data are **not** claimed as new. They do not imply the three new nonminimum CR-frequency cells \(16,24,8\) or their size-two/size-four orbit decomposition at discrepancies \(6,7,8\); this complete interaction between two independently motivated invariants is the present classification.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification at order five, not an asymptotic, algorithmic, or larger-order theorem. Computing one such cross-classification at a fixed order does not determine whether similar distributions stabilize or exhibit periodicity at higher orders. Although primary sources and research records were compared, priority against unpublished or unindexed equivalent enumerations cannot be guaranteed. The numerical result has two exhaustive implementations, but no separate independent scholarly audit has been conducted.
