# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every abelian-image group homomorphism factors through the elementary abelian quotient and is uniquely a pair \((A,\ell)\) with totally isotropic \(\operatorname{im}A\). A direct count of surjections restricting to the identity on a prescribed subspace, followed by exact Gaussian Möbius inversion, proves the fixed-space census. The image-size dichotomy uses the explicit criterion \(\ell|_{\ker A}=0\), and the fixed-point group is the graph of \(\ell\) above \(\ker(I-A)\). The proof covers all odd primes and all \(n\ge1\); standard-library exhaustive tests independently verify smaller cases.

**Originality — PASS within checked literature.** Kumar--Pradhan (2019, arXiv:1908.00331, Theorems 5.2--5.3) already classifies abelian-image maps and counts their **unrefined** isotropic ranks; neither that result nor its total-number formula resolves how many maps have each fixed-point subgroup size. Here the claimed refinement is the joint \((r,s,k)\)-census. Koch--Stordy--Truman (2020, arXiv:2006.13196) supplies motivation via the Yang--Baxter equation, without the present enumeration. Nejabati Zenouz (2018, arXiv:1804.01360) classifies related small-order brace objects, not the all-rank labelled joint census. The closest published database match concerns fixed-point-free automorphisms of a Heisenberg Lie algebra, a distinct nonabelian object and class of maps. Searches cannot establish historical uniqueness; a hidden equivalent formulation in older symplectic or brace literature remains possible. The 2019 unrefined enumerations are explicitly excluded from novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Fixed-point-free abelian endomorphisms are natural inputs to the established Yang--Baxter/brace construction; an exact uniform joint census gives both their cardinalities and image-size distribution. The distinction between fixed-space size and isotropic image rank is not supplied by the existing unrefined formula. Subspace-lattice inversion provides a motivated structural refinement in an infinite family, with a closed fixed-point-free count and reproducible small-field examples.

**Scientific limits.** Odd-prime exponent-\(p\) groups only; no brace-isomorphism classification, no historical uniqueness claim, no inference from finite tests to the infinite theorem. The original publications and a closely related published mathematical abstract were compared at the level indicated in the audit.
