## Correctness — PASS

The central identity was derived directly from feasible potential slices, not from a guessed polynomial fit. The regular first-class vertices force all second-class coordinates into a common interval of length \(L=2-R\). The exceptional vertex contributes the exact section integral \(F_{r,k}(L,h)\); a binomial expansion of its deficit and the explicitly proved beta moments of the range measure yield the formula for every integer \(p\ge2\), \(q\ge3\), \(2\le k\le q-1\) and every real \(h\in[1,3]\). The \(p=2\) point-mass boundary case is included in the moment identity, not inferred by continuity. Endpoint normalization, monotonicity, strict superadditivity of the discrete deletion increments, and the positive leading coefficient at \(h=3\) all follow algebraically.

The standalone lattice-point checker recovered volume from the degree-\(d\) Ehrhart leading coefficient for eight independently defined difference-constraint polytopes. A finite check is not a proof of the continuum theorem; the all-parameter slice argument supplies that proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge comparison)

The exact one-edge deformation is an acknowledged special case, not a newly claimed theorem. For \(k\ge2\), applying the one-edge formula independently would incorrectly retain exponent \(p+q-1\) and miss the nonlinear dependence on \(q-k+1\). The full multi-edge section integral is needed.

The inspected primary material concerns graph transportation-cost quotient structure (Ostrovska–Ostrovskii), extremal Lipschitz-free volume products including equilateral examples (Alexander et al.), symmetric-edge-polytope Ehrhart/facet invariants of the unweighted complete bipartite graph (Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek), and graph-root-polytope connections (D'Alì–Delucchi–Michałek). An unindexed or differently named equivalent, or relevant material not reached by the search, remains possible.

## Value — PASS

This class is a natural perturbation of the finite metric underlying a standard family of Lipschitz-free Banach spaces. The exact formula is not merely a numerical extra case: it quantifies nonadditive effects of simultaneously relaxing metric inequalities and produces a sharp, variable differentiability order at geometric redundancy. For any \(k\ge2\), the exponent drops from the known single-edge value to \(p+q-k\), while the deletion increments increase strictly. The result supplies a reproducible structural law for an infinite discrete/continuous parameter family, not a table entry.
