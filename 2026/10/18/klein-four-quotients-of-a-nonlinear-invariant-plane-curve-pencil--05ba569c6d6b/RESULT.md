# Klein-four quotients of a nonlinear-invariant plane-curve pencil

## Finding
Let \(q=p^e\) be an odd prime power, let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), and take \(\lambda\in k\setminus\{1,-1\}\). Let \(X_{q,\lambda}\) be the smooth normalization of the irreducible plane curve with affine equation
\[
(x+y)^{q+1}-2\bigl((xy)^q+xy\bigr)-\lambda(x-y)^{q+1}=0.
\]
The commuting involutions \(\Phi(x,y)=(y,x)\) and \(\iota(x,y)=(x^{-1},y^{-1})\) generate a Klein-four group \(V\). Set \(h=(q-1)/2\), \(m=h+1\), and define \(T_0(z)=2\), \(T_1(z)=z\), \(T_{r+1}(z)=zT_r(z)-T_{r-1}(z)\). Then the quotient \(Z_{q,\lambda}=X_{q,\lambda}/V\) has a birational affine model
\[
2T_h\!\left(\frac{w^2}{t}-2\right)=t^m-\lambda(t-4)^m,\qquad t\ne0,
\]
whose function field is generated by the displayed \(t,w\), and its genus is exactly
\[
g(Z_{q,\lambda})=\frac{(q-1)^2}{4}=h^2.
\]
The displayed rational equation means the smooth projective normalization of its irreducible closure. For \(q=3\), the model is the smooth genus-one cubic
\[
w^2=t\bigl[(\lambda-1)t^2+\lambda t+(\lambda-1)\bigr],
\]
with modular invariant
\[
j(\lambda)=-\frac{\lambda^6}{(\lambda-1)^4(\lambda+1)}\quad\text{in characteristic }3.
\]
It is supersingular exactly at \(\lambda=0\), and ordinary for all other permitted parameters. The extension of this degree-six separable rational map from the parameter line to the \(j\)-line has exactly two branch values, \(0\) and \(\infty\). Over \(0\) its ramification partition is \((6)\); over \(\infty\) it is \((4,1,1)\). These assertions concern the quotient and its modular map; they do not assert that excluded members of the original pencil are smooth.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves and quotient genera are geometric, over the algebraic closure \(k\). The exponent \(q\) must be an odd prime power and \(\lambda\) must avoid the two reducible parameters \(\pm1\). Inversion is an automorphism of the function field and need not be everywhere defined on the original singular plane embedding. The two involutions have different nontrivial actions; their generated group has order four because one exchanges the coordinate functions and the other inverts them. The relevant primary classification and smoothness of the original normalization are from Borges–Korchmáros–Speziali, while the invariant field, explicit second quotient and its genus are computed below.

## Proof
**First quotient and smoothness.** Put \(a=x+y\) and \(b=xy\). The fixed field of the swap is \(k(a,b)\): the original function field is generated over it by the two roots of \(U^2-aU+b\), and the swap acts nontrivially. Its plane equation is
\[
D(a,b)=a^{q+1}-2(b^q+b)-\lambda(a^2-4b)^m=0.
\]
This is a smooth plane curve of degree \(q+1\). Indeed, at an affine singular point, its \(b\)-derivative would give \(\lambda(a^2-4b)^h=1\). The \(a\)-derivative would then give \(a^q=a\). Substituting the first identity into \(D=0\) forces \(b^q=b\). Hence \(a,b\in\mathbb F_q\) and \(a^2-4b\ne0\); its \(h\)-th power lies in \(\{\pm1\}\), contradicting \(\lambda\notin\{\pm1\}\). In projective coordinates \([A:B:Z]\), the only point at infinity is \([0:1:0]\), where the \(Z\)-derivative equals \(-2\). Thus \(D\) is smooth and irreducible, with genus \(q(q-1)/2\).

**Second quotient.** Inversion induces \((a,b)\mapsto(a/b,1/b)\). Define invariant rational functions
\[
t=\frac{a^2}{b},\qquad w=\frac{a(b+1)}b.
\]
The identity \(w^2=t(b+2+b^{-1})\) gives \(b+b^{-1}=w^2/t-2\). Dividing the equation for \(D\) by \(b^m\), and using \(T_h(b+b^{-1})=b^h+b^{-h}\), gives the announced model. Conversely, \(b\) satisfies
\[
b^2-\left(\frac{w^2}{t}-2\right)b+1=0,
\]
and \(a=wb/(b+1)\). Hence \([k(a,b):k(t,w)]\le2\), and because inversion is nontrivial and fixes \(t,w\), equality holds and \(k(t,w)=k(a,b)^{\langle\iota\rangle}\). This also identifies the function field of \(X/V\), not merely an equation satisfied by invariant functions. The equation is generically irreducible: on \(D\), the only poles of \(t\) are at \((a,b)=(0,0)\) and the unique infinite point, each of order \(q-1\), as follows from the local expansions \(b\sim(1-\lambda)a^{q+1}/2\) at the first point and \(Z\sim(1-\lambda)A^{q+1}/2\) on the chart \(B=1\) at the second. Thus \(\deg(D\to\mathbb P^1_t)=2(q-1)\), and \(\deg(Z\to\mathbb P^1_t)=q-1=2h\), the degree in \(w\) of the displayed equation.

**Fixed points and genus.** In affine coordinates with \(b\ne0\), a fixed point of inversion must satisfy \(b^2=1\) and \(a=a/b\). For \(b=-1\), this forces \(a=0\), which lies on \(D\) only if \(\lambda=1\), excluded. For \(b=1\), fixed points are roots of
\[
f(a)=a^{q+1}-4-\lambda(a^2-4)^m.
\]
There are exactly \(q+1\) distinct roots: if \(f=f'=0\) and \(a\ne0\), one derives \(a^{q-1}=1\) and \(\lambda(a^2-4)^h=1\), which again forces \(\lambda=\pm1\), since \(a\in\mathbb F_q\). If \(a=0\), the equation \(f(0)=0\) itself forces \(\lambda=\pm1\). Inversion exchanges the single point \((a,b)=(0,0)\) with the single point at infinity, so there are no other fixed points. The quotient of the smooth curve \(D\) by this tame involution consequently has exactly \(q+1\) simple branch points. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
q(q-1)-2=2(2g(Z)-2)+(q+1),
\]
which solves to the claimed square genus.

**The characteristic-three modular map.** For \(q=3\), the invariant relation simplifies in characteristic three to the displayed cubic. Its right-hand side is a squarefree cubic exactly when \(\lambda\ne\pm1\): it has leading and constant coefficient \(\lambda-1\) and its quadratic factor has discriminant \(\lambda^2-4(\lambda-1)^2=-(\lambda+1)\). Writing \(r=\lambda/(\lambda-1)\), scaling the quadratic twist gives \(y^2=t(t^2+rt+1)\). Standard integral Weierstrass invariants reduced modulo three give \(c_4=r^2\), \(\Delta=r^2-1\), and \(j=r^6/(r^2-1)\), yielding the formula in \(\lambda\). The characteristic-three Hasse invariant of this cubic is \(r\), proving supersingularity exactly at \(\lambda=0\). The rational \(j\)-map has degree six and is separable because
\[
\frac{j'(\lambda)}{j(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^2-1}.
\]
Its unique finite zero at \(\lambda=0\) has multiplicity six, and its poles at \(\lambda=1,-1,\infty\) have orders four, one and one. The derivative has no other zero away from zeros or poles. Thus the only branch values are \(0\) and \(\infty\), with the specified partitions. The wild ramification above \(0\) is accounted for: the derivative vanishes to order seven there, whereas the tame pole at \(\lambda=1\) contributes different exponent three, summing to the required ten for a separable degree-six map of projective lines.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact symbolic identities to check the invariant-model substitution for \(q=3,5,7,9,11,13\), the elliptic equation in characteristic three, the \(j\)-invariant identity and logarithmic derivative modulo three, and fixed-point polynomial squarefreeness for all permitted prime-field values in several test characteristics. It prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite checks reinforce, but do not replace, the parameter-uniform function-field, fixed-point and Riemann–Hurwitz arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Borges, Korchmáros and Speziali introduce the singular plane pencil in Lemma 5.2 and establish its automorphisms and genus. Their Proposition 8.3 identifies the first quotient by coordinate interchange, Proposition 8.4 proves that first double covering is unramified, and Proposition 8.5 gives the original curve's genus. Those results are prior work, not asserted here as discoveries. The new assertion is the explicit **second** quotient by inversion, its square genus for all odd prime powers, and the precise elliptic modular and ramification strata at the smallest odd exponent. The source manuscript's discussion of the Klein-four action does not give these computations. General treatments of genus-five bielliptic curves over characteristic zero concern a different ground field and do not imply the characteristic-three formula. A projectively equivalent presentation or later full journal version could contain overlapping results not detected by the inspected public literature.

## Limitations
This finding does not determine automorphism groups of the quotient curves, their full Jacobian decompositions, or behavior at excluded parameters. It does not provide a complete classification of all covers with these ramification partitions or prove that the modular rational map is new up to all changes of parameter. The model is a birational affine equation; for \(q>3\) it may have singular affine points and must be normalized. The elliptic formula applies only to characteristic three. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
