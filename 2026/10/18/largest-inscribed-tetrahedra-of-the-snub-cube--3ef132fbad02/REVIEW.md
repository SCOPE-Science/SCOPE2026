# Scientific review: largest inscribed tetrahedra of the snub cube

## Correctness — PASS

The certificate constructs the complete \(24\)-vertex snub-cube set from the tribonacci minimal polynomial, enumerates all \(10626\) possible distinct four-vertex subsets, and verifies the sharp algebraic determinant bound by exact rational interval signs. It counts exactly \(24\) vertex extremizers and establishes a combinatorial incidence invariant: each such extremizer belongs to one and only one of eight three-apex-base families. A separate exact facet-normal test certifies that the movable apices range over genuine triangular facets. Multiaffinity of the oriented determinant, together with the unique-special-base incidence property, proves exhaustion even when arbitrary tetrahedron vertices vary continuously throughout the solid. Exact normalization and chirality symmetry were independently checked. The finite enumeration is complete, not a numerical sample.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

The snub cube is a classical uniform solid, but its known total volume, circumradius, Tammes-packing significance and angular metric data are not the same as the largest contained tetrahedron. Longuet-Higgins's full available primary paper concerns spherical-cap placement and growth on a sphere, not the four-point volume optimum in the resulting polyhedron. Horváth–Lángi's full accessible paper optimizes polygons and polyhedra with fixed vertex counts on a circumsphere; that distinct feasible class and objective do not deliver the claimed internal snub-cube formula. The standard MathWorld polyhedron entry gives total-solid metrics, not the tetrahedron optimum or the eight triangular-face equality components. Related exact computations for a regular icosahedron or cube–octahedron intersections apply to geometrically different convex bodies and do not imply a numerical snub-cube statement. A known homotopy calculation for the independence complex of the same snub cubical graph is metric-independent and cannot imply this four-point Euclidean volume invariant. No coincident result was identified among the evaluated related mathematical claims. Historical or unindexed classifications remain a limited risk, not positive evidence of coverage.

## Value — PASS

The largest contained simplex is a natural sharp invariant measuring how a well-studied Archimedean body supports maximal four-point volume. The snub cube is among the two chiral Archimedean solids and has a canonical tribonacci metric. In addition to the exact constant, the answer exhibits and completely classifies eight two-dimensional faces of equality configurations, an unexpected structural degeneracy invisible from the containing body's known volume or from isolated maximizing four-vertex sets. The result supplies a precise benchmark for comparisons with other symmetric and chiral polyhedra; no broader theorem or independent validation is claimed.
