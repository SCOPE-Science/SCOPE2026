## Correctness — PASS

The two alternating parts yield independent uniform coupon streams. The first part already contains a visited starting vertex, producing completion-count distributions \(C_a-1\) and \(C_b\); the parity of visits makes the last-discovery comparison \(\Pr(C_b\geq C_a)\). Inclusion-exclusion and geometric-stage generating functions yield the exact rational formula. Monotone geometric couplings give the strict finite majority boundary. The classical coupon-collector Gumbel limit and independent-Gumbel logistic difference give the sharp \(n/\log n\) window. Fourier inversion, a uniform integrable geometric characteristic-function bound and Plancherel prove \(n\Pr(C_n=C_n')\to 1/4\), hence the exact \(1/(8n)\) total-variation asymptotic. The standalone exact checker passed 314 independent rational assertions across the \(1\leq a,b\leq10\) grid and four small examples. Those experiments do not replace infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

Buchanan, Horn and Rombach (arXiv:2209.08079, 2022) classify exactly uniform last-visit behavior, without quantitative complete-bipartite formulas, window or asymptotic. Ohwa and Shirai (2008, DOI 10.2206/kyushujm.62.281) derive a generic Markov-chain joint cover-time/terminal-site formula, which *can in principle be specialized* to give finite probabilities, and is not being claimed as new. The sharp all-size majority boundary, exact bipartite logistic scaling and leading total-variation constant are not stated in either inspected full text. Lovász and Winkler (1993, DOI 10.1002/jgt.3190170505) are credited for the qualitative classification; publisher full text was inaccessible, so a residual equivalence risk persists. Standard coupon-collector and Gumbel facts are not claimed new. Related published records were compared at the level of assertions, not title keywords alone. An unlocated earlier quantitative specialization remains a historical-priority risk; no independent priority certificate is claimed.

## Value — PASS

The final discovered vertex is a natural random-walk cover-tour statistic. Its opposite-part bias has a counterintuitive strict boundary even on equal parts. The logistic \(n/\log n\) scale resolves which part wins under near-balance; the sharp \(1/(8n)\) total-variation law quantifies how close the full last-vertex distribution gets to uniformity although exact uniformity fails. These are graph-specific finite and scaling facts beyond the generic cover-time formulas and explain the parity advantage.

## Limits and closest related literature

The 1993 paper was accessible only in abstract/metadata and remains a specific priority risk. General Markov-chain formula specialization and classical Gumbel results are explicitly credited.
