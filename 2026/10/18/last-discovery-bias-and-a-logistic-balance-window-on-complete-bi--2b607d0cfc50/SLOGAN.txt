For a simple random walk on the complete bipartite graph \(K_{a,b}\) started in the first part, the last newly visited vertex lies in the opposite part with probability strictly above \(1/2\) exactly when \(b\geq a\). Its exact probability is a rational coupon-collector comparison, has a logistic crossover when \(b-a\) is of order \(a/\log a\), and on balanced graphs the last-vertex law approaches uniformity in total variation at the sharp rate \(1/(8a)\).
