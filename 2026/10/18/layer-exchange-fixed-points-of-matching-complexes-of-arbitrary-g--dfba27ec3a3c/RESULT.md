# Layer-exchange fixed points of matching complexes of arbitrary graph prisms

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite simple graph with \(n=|V|\ge1\). Put \(P(G)=G\square K_2\), and let \(\tau\) interchange its two graph layers. Let \(\mathcal M(H)\) denote the ordinary matching complex of a graph \(H\), and let \(\mathcal M_p(H)\) denote the subcomplex generated by its perfect matchings. Let \(W(G)\) be obtained from \(G\) by adjoining one private degree-one leaf to each vertex of \(G\). Finally, let \(p(G)\) be the number of perfect matchings of \(G\).

The two geometric fixed-point loci in the original edge-simplex coincide, and there is a canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
|\mathcal M(P(G))|^{\langle\tau\rangle}
=|\mathcal M_p(P(G))|^{\langle\tau\rangle}
\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}|\mathcal M(W(G))|.
\]
Their integral homotopy type is completely determined by the perfect-matching count:
\[
|\mathcal M(P(G))|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\simeq
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{pt},&p(G)=0,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{p(G)}S^{k-1},&n=2k\text{ and }p(G)>0.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(k=1\), a wedge of \(p(G)\) copies of \(S^0\) is understood as \(p(G)+1\) points. All nonzero reduced integral homology is free abelian and concentrated in degree \(k-1\), with rank \(p(G)\). In particular, for \(G=K_{2k}\) this rank is \((2k-1)!!\), and for \(G=K_{k,k}\) it is \(k!\). If \(n\) is odd the fixed locus is contractible.

## Assumptions and scope
A matching is a set of graph edges with pairwise disjoint endpoints. The simplicial vertices of \(\mathcal M(H)\) are the edges of \(H\); a simplex is any matching, including the empty face in the abstract complex. In \(\mathcal M_p(H)\), the facets are the graph's perfect matchings and every subset of such a matching is a face. Every prism \(P(G)\) admits a perfect matching consisting of its vertical rungs, even if \(G\) itself admits none. All fixed points are geometric fixed points of the induced simplicial involution, not simply simplices pointwise fixed at their vertices. The theorem allows disconnected graphs and isolated vertices. The exponent \(k-1\) applies only when the perfect-matching count is positive, which forces \(n=2k\).

## Proof
**1. A canonical geometric fixed-point triangulation.** The involution \(\tau\) fixes each vertical rung \(r_v=\{(v,0),(v,1)\}\), and swaps the two horizontal graph edges \(e^0=\{(u,0),(v,0)\}\) and \(e^1=\{(u,1),(v,1)\}\), for each \(e=uv\in E\). Every point of the realization of a finite simplicial complex has unique barycentric coordinates; it is fixed by \(\tau\) precisely when those coordinates are constant on every orbit of simplicial vertices. Hence the fixed locus is canonically PL homeomorphic to the complex whose vertices are the permitted vertex-orbits and whose simplices are orbit collections whose union is an original simplex. Here the orbit \(\{e^0,e^1\}\) is permitted because the two layer edges are disjoint; rung orbits are permitted as well. Such a union is a matching if and only if no two chosen original graph edges share an endpoint and no chosen rung shares a vertex with a chosen original graph edge. The orbit complex is exactly \(\mathcal M(W(G))\), with a rung standing for the private leaf edge at that vertex and a two-layer orbit standing for an original edge. This proves the stated PL homeomorphism.

Moreover, every invariant matching can be extended to a perfect matching of \(P(G)\): for every vertex \(v\) not covered by a chosen two-layer horizontal-edge orbit and not already covered by a chosen rung, add rung \(r_v\). These additions are mutually disjoint and cover all remaining prism vertices. Thus every invariant simplex of \(\mathcal M(P(G))\) is already a face of \(\mathcal M_p(P(G))\). Conversely, the latter is a subcomplex of the former, so the two geometric fixed sets are equal.

**2. A uniform acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** Fix any linear ordering of \(V\). On the *augmented* face poset of \(\mathcal M(W(G))\), including the empty face, define a matching recursively. Given a residual induced subgraph on \(U\subseteq V\), choose its smallest vertex \(v\). Partition residual matching faces into \(Z\), those containing no original graph edge incident with \(v\), and \(B_w\), those containing the unique original edge \(vw\), one block for each neighbor \(w\) of \(v\) in \(U\). All faces of \(Z\) pair by toggling the private leaf edge at \(v\). For a face in \(B_w\), delete the already selected edge \(vw\), identify the remaining matching with a matching in \(W(G[U\setminus\{v,w\}])\), and recurse inside that block. If \(U\) is empty, leave its one empty face unpaired. If \(v\) is isolated in \(G[U]\), the full residual poset is \(Z\) and gets paired, leaving no critical faces.

This matching is well defined: a matching contains at most one original edge incident with \(v\), and in \(Z\) toggling the pendant edge is always legal. It is acyclic with the standard orientation (matched face-poset covers upward, all other covers downward). Within \(Z\), upward steps can only insert the pendant edge at \(v\); that edge cannot subsequently be deleted by a downward unmatched cover, and all other downward steps lower the number of other selected edges. Hence there is no directed cycle inside \(Z\). Each \(B_w\) is acyclic by induction. A Hasse cover crossing from \(B_w\) to \(Z\) removes the selected original edge \(vw\) and is unmatched, hence oriented from \(B_w\) into \(Z\); no cover runs from \(Z\) into a block. No cover changes \(B_w\) into a different \(B_{w'}\) directly. Consequently no directed cycle can cross blocks. This proves global acyclicity by induction on \(|U|\).

The unmatched faces are exactly the original-edge perfect matchings of \(G\): a residual branch survives only by selecting an original edge at every pivot and eventually exhausting all original vertices. Every unmatched face contains \(n/2\) original graph edges and no pendant edge. Thus there are \(p(G)\) unmatched faces, all of dimension \(k-1\) when \(n=2k\); otherwise there are none. The augmented empty face is always paired with the private pendant edge at the least vertex of \(G\), because \(n\ge1\). Delete that augmented matching pair and declare its one-edge vertex a critical zero-cell in the ordinary nonempty-face poset. The matching on the remaining nonempty faces is still acyclic, with one critical vertex and exactly \(p(G)\) other critical cells, all in dimension \(k-1\).

**3. Integral homotopy conclusion.** Forman's discrete-Morse theorem provides a CW model with one zero-cell and the listed other cells. If \(p(G)=0\), there is exactly one cell, hence the realization is contractible. If \(p(G)>0\), the only positive-dimensional cells have dimension \(k-1\); their attaching maps are necessarily trivial because the \((k-2)\)-skeleton is a point (for \(k=2\), these are loops attached to a point). When \(k=1\), there are \(p(G)+1\) zero-cells. In each case the asserted wedge-of-spheres integral homotopy type follows, including the \(S^0\) convention. The perfect-matching counts for complete and complete bipartite graphs are the elementary numbers of ways to pair their vertices, respectively to choose a bijection between the two parts.

## Verification
The included standalone `prism_fixed_point_verifier.py` requires only Python 3. It enumerates **all 76 simple labeled graphs on at most four vertices**, builds the entire augmented matching face poset of \(W(G)\), directly verifies every involution-orbit face against invariant matchings of the prism, reconstructs the recursive Morse pairs, checks that unmatched faces are exactly the perfect matchings of the original graph, and checks global directed acyclicity by a complete topological sort of every Hasse cover. These tests inspected 3,822 face-poset covers across the exhaustive small cases. The same verifier also checks six larger canonical graphs, including \(K_6\), \(K_{3,3}\), and \(K_7\), on their full face posets. Its recorded output ends with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are independent corroboration; the all-graph result follows from the proof above, not from finite extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak (arXiv:2608.30601, initially posted August 31, 2026; updated with circular ladders September 14) determine nonequivariant homotopy types of the independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius and circular ladders. In particular they classify \(\mathcal M_p(C_n\square K_2)\), but their main theorems and Section 5.2 (Theorem 25) do not classify its geometric layer-exchange fixed locus and do not assert a theorem for \(G\square K_2\) with arbitrary \(G\). The known fixed-point classification for circular ladders, when \(G=C_n\), is a **special case** of the present formula (two perfect matchings when \(n\) is even, none when \(n\) is odd); that special case is acknowledged and not claimed as new. The current contribution is the uniform all-graph PL fixed-orbit model, the equivalence of fixed loci for ordinary and perfect matching complexes, and a global discrete-Morse theorem calculating the fixed-locus homotopy from \(p(G)\) without restrictions on \(G\).

Work on higher-capacity two-matching complexes of whiskered graphs concerns a different degree constraint, and standard matching-complex calculations for particular ladders and grids compute the homology of the full complex rather than the geometric fixed-point homotopy type. Ordinary nonequivariant sphere-wedge statements do not imply the present result because fixed-point spaces cannot generally be recovered from homotopy types or rational character traces alone.

## Limitations
The theorem determines only the layer-swap fixed locus of the matching complex of a prism, not the nonequivariant homotopy type of the entire prism matching complex, other automorphism fixed loci, or the full equivariant homotopy type. The perfect-matching count \(p(G)\) can be difficult to compute for arbitrary graphs; this is an exact structural reduction rather than a polynomial-time algorithm for counting. No independent scientific audit has been performed. An unindexed prior general whiskered-matching theorem remains a bibliographic novelty risk; targeted searches and the primary sources listed below did not reveal a dominating result.
