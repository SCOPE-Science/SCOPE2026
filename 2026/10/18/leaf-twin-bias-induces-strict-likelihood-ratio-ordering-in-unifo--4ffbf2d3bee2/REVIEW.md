# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The two-leaf contraction also gives an exact cherry-Palm construction and its occupancy distribution. Conditional on a degree sequence, pruning two prescribed leaves with a common neighbor yields the exact falling-factorial share of centers; conditioned on the number of distinct Prüfer symbols, merging two positions yields the Stirling ratio. Induction using real-rootedness of the Stirling polynomials and Newton's inequalities proves strict monotonicity at every endpoint. The likelihood-ratio result follows from elementary size-biasing. Exhaustive enumeration of all 280,388 labeled trees of orders 4–8 and 4,850 finite strict-ratio checks corroborated the formulas. The proof, not those finite checks, establishes all orders.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Full relevant material from Rényi (1959), Erdős–Rényi (1963), Pitman (1997), and the complete accessible section on cherries from the 2020 symmetry paper was compared. The 1959 source enumerates leaf counts; the 1963 paper defines and counts cherries unconditionally; the 1997 forest survey gives degree-profile machinery; the 2020 paper uses a narrower center-degree restriction. None of the examined passages implies the strict conditional regression or the exact likelihood-ratio statement as a stated result. An implicit equivalent might be extractable from a more general historical mixed-statistic enumerator, particularly uninspected older work on tree automorphism orbits. Novelty is subject to independent verification.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Leaf twins are local witnesses of graph automorphisms, while leaf count measures global tree branching. The theorem supplies a complete finite-size conditional dependence law and a strict distributional order, not merely the already-known first moment of cherries. The likelihood-ratio conclusion applies to every increasing leaf-count statistic and precisely states how sampling trees by local symmetries changes global topology.
