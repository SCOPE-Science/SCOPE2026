# Scientific review: arithmetic resonance in regular-polygon excluded areas

## Correctness
**PASS.** The scope is all integers \(n,m\ge3\), all positive circumradii, and all real angles. The derivation starts with the exact planar mixed-area support sum, identifies the full residue multiset using coprimality after reduction by \(\gcd(n,m)\), and evaluates the centered finite cosine progression. These steps include the orientation phase and handle coincident edge normals by continuity of the support function. Uniform rotation folds onto a uniform interval and gives the CDF and moment integrals. Exact fixed-area radii and uniform Taylor estimates justify both limits and the universal limiting law. Independent finite convex-hull calculations passed 1,376 checks; this is corroboration rather than proof of universal quantifiers. The theorem does not assert numerical RSA jamming limits.

## Originality
**PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** The most relevant inspected 2026 binary regular-polygon RSA source provides analytic/numerical excluded areas for aligned shapes and a finite table; it does not evaluate the full relative-angle profile. The inspected 2019 relative-angle excluded-area study gives analytic checks for equilateral triangles and squares and plots selected same-species regular polygons; these are special cases, not an all-\(n,m\) identity. The new claim combines an exact \(\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)\)-periodic profile for arbitrary mixtures with its complete Haar-orientation law and the sharp equal-area two-sequence asymptotic contrast. The general planar support-sum formula is acknowledged as standard and can be algorithmically specialized, but it does not itself state the arithmetic folding or sharp scaling. Indexed related geometric findings concern rotational intersections and Minkowski averages of other shapes, not this pair-excluded-area result. An equivalent uncatalogued classical evaluation remains possible.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Pair excluded area determines the geometric exclusion region for polygon mixtures and is explicitly used in recent binary random sequential adsorption studies. In the physically relevant equal-area normalization, the theorem explains an arithmetic effect not visible from side counts separately: equal orders give \(k^{-2}\) angular variation, whereas consecutive coprime orders give \(k^{-4}\). Exact extrema, full orientation distribution, and sharp variance scaling make this a complete geometric answer to the arbitrary-relative-orientation extension of the aligned-mixture calculation. The claim does not imply any law for many-body saturation, and that boundary is kept explicit.
