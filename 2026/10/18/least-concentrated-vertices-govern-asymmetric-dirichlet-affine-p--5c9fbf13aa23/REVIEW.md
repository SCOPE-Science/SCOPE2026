# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Gram matrix is explicitly inverted, reproducing the affine polynomial kernel. Strict vertex localization comes from the triangle inequality with opposite kernel signs on a set of positive Dirichlet measure. The beta integration gives the exact norm; the gamma-splitting coupling is a conditional mean-preserving spread that strictly orders vertex norms. The concentration-budget classification and fixed-proportion Gaussian asymptotic follow with stated boundary and uniform-integrability arguments. The 241 passing finite tests independently evaluate several algebraic and numerical consequences, without standing in for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS within examined evidence.** The closely relevant Griffiths–Spanò degree-one kernel is explicitly treated as prior. Its examined source sections give the kernel and beta marginalization, but not the asymmetric sharp norm, strict smallest-parameter vertex classification, or concentration-budget minimax. The public uniform-simplex affine projection result covers the special balanced case only; the previously studied symmetric-Dirichlet family also covers equal shapes only. The later beta convex-transform order result addresses a different comparison and does not supply the stated norm optimization. Targeted scientific-record searches yielded no equivalent or stronger asymmetric-projector result. Priority remains provisional because some other plausible full texts could not be obtained.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem resolves a full, natural parameter-allocation question for a widely used weighted least-squares projector: it gives an exact stability cost for heterogeneous concentration, identifies all worst-case vertices, proves unique balanced minimax design, and computes the nonattained sharp boundary cost and large-concentration Gaussian law. These are not routine substitutions of the symmetric formula: the decisive assertion is strict ordering across distinct Dirichlet measures, requiring a coupled convex-order argument.

The 2013 Dirichlet-polynomial-kernel article was examined in its relevant full-text sections, and the published uniform-simplex mathematical result was read in full. The 2021 beta-order full text was inspected for its main definitions, theorems, and applications. An earlier multivariate orthogonal-polynomial source and some approximation references were accessible only at abstract level; no full-document exclusion is inferred from them.
