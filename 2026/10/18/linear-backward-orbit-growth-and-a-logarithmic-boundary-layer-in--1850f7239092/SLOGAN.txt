Just above the loss-of-invertibility threshold, a unit-weight Huygens star has exactly \(2n+1\) distinct \(n\)-step ancestors of its all-opposition sink, with every ancestor forced onto one diagonal circle. For each other point of that diagonal, the number of ancestors at depth \(n\) eventually stabilizes to a finite value, and this stabilized count grows as \(2\log(1/|t|)/\log(1/(c-1))+O_c(1)\) when the target approaches the sink.
