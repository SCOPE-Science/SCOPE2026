# Literal-symmetry classification of two-variable convex team meanings

## Finding

Fix exactly two propositional atoms and let \(V=\{0,1\}^{2}\) be their four valuations. A **team** is any subset of \(V\), including the empty team. A **convex meaning** is a family \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal P(V)\) such that
\[
 s,t\in\mathcal C,\quad s\subseteq u\subseteq t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\in\mathcal C.
\]
Let \(G\) act on valuations by independently complementing either Boolean coordinate and exchanging the two coordinates; \(G\cong C_2\wr S_2\cong D_8\). Its induced action on teams and meanings corresponds to simultaneous renaming and literal complementation of the propositional atoms.

**Theorem.** The \(3{,}938\) convex meanings on two atoms split into exactly **\(700\)** \(G\)-orbits. Their complete orbit-size distribution is:

| Size of a meaning-orbit | Number of orbits | Meanings covered |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(1\) | \(26\) | \(26\) |
| \(2\) | \(54\) | \(108\) |
| \(4\) | \(289\) | \(1{,}156\) |
| \(8\) | \(331\) | \(2{,}648\) |
| **All** | **\(700\)** | **\(3{,}938\)** |

If all permutations of the **four valuations** are allowed instead, there are exactly \(349\) orbits under \(S_4\). Thus semantic reindexing of valuations identifies more meanings than the substitutions generated by renaming and negating individual propositional atoms. This distinction first becomes possible with two atoms, since for a single atom the literal symmetry group is already the full permutation group of its two valuations.

By the expressive-completeness theorem of Anttila and Knudstorp, these are also exact counts of semantic formula-equivalence classes, respectively modulo literal symmetry, in any of their three expressively complete propositional convex team logics.

## Assumptions and scope

The universe of valuations is exactly \(V=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}\); the base set of teams has \(16\) elements. Meanings may be empty and may contain the empty team. Convexity refers to **inclusion** of teams, not geometric convexity of the square of valuations. No assumption of downward closure, union closure, nonemptiness, or probabilistic structure is imposed. Formula equivalence means equality of truth sets over all teams on these two atoms. Only signed-coordinate permutations comprise \(G\); arbitrary permutations of four valuations are considered separately and are *not* asserted to be literal substitutions.

## Proof

**1. From formulas to finite order-convex sets.** Theorem 2.19 of Anttila and Knudstorp (arXiv:2503.21850) proves that three propositional systems each define exactly all convex team properties on any fixed finite set of variables. On two atoms, the ordered set of teams is the Boolean lattice \(B_4=(\mathcal P(V),\subseteq)\). It therefore suffices to count orbits of order-convex subsets of \(B_4\). Substituting a literal \(\neg p\) for an atom \(p\) swaps its two valuation labels and transports teams componentwise; exchanging atoms permutes the coordinates. The resulting eight transformations are exactly the signed-coordinate group \(G\).

**2. A finite quotient-poset lemma.** Let a finite group \(H\) act by automorphisms on a finite poset \(P\). Give the set \(P/H\) of point-orbits the order \([a]\le[b]\) iff some representatives satisfy \(a'\le b'\). This is a partial order: alignment of representatives proves transitivity; if \([a]\le[b]\le[a]\), cyclically applying the finite-order group product to the resulting comparisons forces equality and hence equality of orbits (for \(B_4\), cardinality grading also immediately proves antisymmetry). The inverse-image map is a bijection between order-convex subsets of \(P/H\) and \(H\)-invariant order-convex subsets of \(P\). Indeed, a chain \([a]\le[b]\le[c]\) can be lifted, by aligning the two appearances of \([b]\), to \(a'\le b'\le c'\). Conversely any interval in \(P\) maps to an interval in its orbit quotient. Apply this to the cyclic subgroup generated by each \(g\in G\).

**3. Exact fixed-point counts.** Encode each valuation by one of \(0,1,2,3\), each team as a four-bit mask \(0,\ldots,15\), and a family of teams as a sixteen-bit mask \(0,\ldots,65535\). The completely specified interval predicate used by `check_orbits.py` is
\[
 \operatorname{conv}(F)\iff
 \forall a,b\in B_4\;\bigl[(a,b\in F\land a\subseteq b)
 \Rightarrow (\forall z\in B_4\;(a\subseteq z\subseteq b\Rightarrow z\in F))\bigr].
\]
There are exactly \(3{,}938\) passing masks. For each cycle type of \(g\), count convex subsets of the quotient poset \(B_4/\langle g\rangle\), using the same explicit finite interval predicate. The complete finite case split is:

| Cycle type on the four valuations | Multiplicity in \(G\) | Quotient-poset size | Fixed convex meanings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(1^4\) | \(1\) | \(16\) | \(3{,}938\) |
| \(1^2 2\) | \(2\) | \(12\) | \(526\) |
| \(2^2\) | \(3\) | \(10\) | \(186\) |
| \(4\) | \(2\) | \(6\) | \(26\) |

All bounds on the search variables and all inclusion predicates appear in the standalone checker. Burnside's lemma now gives
\[
 |\mathcal C/G|=\frac{3938+2\cdot526+3\cdot186+2\cdot26}{8}
 =\boxed{700}.
\]
Canonical orbit enumeration across all \(3{,}938\) convex masks, with each transformed family calculated directly from the eight permutations, gives orbit-size frequencies \((26,54,289,331)\) for sizes \((1,2,4,8)\). The identity \(26+2(54)+4(289)+8(331)=3{,}938\) checks their exhaustive coverage.

For completeness, under \(S_4\) the five permutation-cycle types have fixed counts \(3938,526,186,71,26\), respectively for \(1^4,1^2 2,2^2,1\,3,4\), and have multiplicities \(1,6,3,8,6\). Thus
\[
 |\mathcal C/S_4|=\frac{3938+6\cdot526+3\cdot186+8\cdot71+6\cdot26}{24}
 =\boxed{349}.
\]
The \(S_4\)-action is an additional semantic comparison, not an identification of all its permutations with literal substitutions.

**4. Independent finite count.** A nonempty order-convex subset \(F\) of any finite poset is uniquely determined by its antichains \(M=\min(F)\) and \(U=\max(F)\). These are exactly the pairs of nonempty antichains such that every element of \(M\) lies below some element of \(U\), and conversely every element of \(U\) lies above some element of \(M\). Indeed
\[
 F=\uparrow M\cap\downarrow U.
\]
The checker `check_antichains.py` independently enumerates all \(168\) antichains of \(B_4\), tests all their ordered pairs for these two coverage conditions, and adds the empty convex subset. It obtains exactly \(3{,}937\) compatible nonempty minimum/maximum pairs and, including the empty family, \(3{,}938\) convex meanings. Transporting these pairs directly under the eight transformations (without the interval-mask algorithm) reproduces the fixed-point counts and complete orbit-size distribution above. Thus both finite enumeration routes have independently specified, exhaustive finite domains.

## Verification

Run with Python 3 and the standard library, from the directory containing the two enclosed standalone programs:

```sh
python3 check_orbits.py
python3 check_antichains.py
```

Expected terminal lines:

```text
ORBIT_CERT_OK convex=3938 D8_orbits=700 orbit_sizes={1:26,2:54,4:289,8:331} fixed_types={1^4:3938,1^2_2:526,2^2:186,4:26} S4_orbits=349
ANTICHAIN_CERT_OK antichains=168 valid_pairs=3938 fixed_sorted=[26,26,186,186,186,526,526,3938] orbits=700 sizes={1:26,2:54,4:289,8:331}
```

The scripts use exhaustive loops over a fully specified sixteen-element team poset and are finite certificates for the stated two-variable census. They are not intended to prove analogous formulas for arbitrary numbers of propositional variables.

## Relationship to prior work

Anttila and Knudstorp (2025, Theorem 2.19) establish expressive completeness for *all* convex properties, supplying the logically motivated class being counted. Their theorem does not supply these signed-literal orbit counts. OEIS A369313 already tabulates the **labeled** number \(3{,}938\) of convex subsets of the four-dimensional Boolean lattice; this known number is used as a corroborating boundary, not claimed as new. Elder and collaborators (2023) study toggle/rowmotion dynamics and enumeration of interval-closed sets, but those orbits are under rowmotion rather than the literal-renaming action analyzed here. The present result isolates the full \(G\)-orbit profile and the strict \(G\) versus \(S_4\) quotient distinction for the first multi-atom team vocabulary.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to exactly two Boolean propositional atoms and semantic equivalence, without formula-size bounds or a general closed form for larger vocabularies. The \(S_4\) comparison does not imply that every arbitrary four-valuation permutation is induced by a literal substitution. The independent antichain and mask enumerations prove a finite census only; priority against unindexed symmetry-enumeration literature remains uncertain.
