# Scientific review — literal symmetries of two-atom convex team meanings

## Correctness — PASS

A team is a subset of the four Boolean valuations and a meaning an inclusion-convex subset of the sixteen-element team lattice. Each literal substitution induces a signed-coordinate permutation. Invariant convex families correspond to convex sets in the finite quotient poset, and the independent min/max-antichain characterization gives a second full enumeration. The exhaustive interval-mask program verifies all 65,536 candidate families and all eight signed permutations, producing 3,938 meanings, 700 orbits, orbit sizes (26,54,289,331), and 349 orbits under the full 24-element valuation permutation group. The independent 168-antichain checker reproduces the labeled count, eight fixed counts, and signed orbit histogram. These finite certificates apply to the exact two-atom domain, with no unproved extrapolation to more atoms.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by historical-priority risk)

Anttila and Knudstorp (arXiv:2503.21850, Theorem 2.19) establish expressive completeness for convex propositional team properties, rather than these symmetry-quotient cardinalities. OEIS A369313 already records the 3,938 labeled interval-convex families; that count is not claimed as new. Elder et al. (arXiv:2307.08520) study interval-closed sets and toggle/rowmotion orbits, not atom-renaming and literal-complementation orbits. Targeted published-result searches and semantic-record comparisons did not reveal the exact 700/349 pair or the complete orbit histogram. The classification could nevertheless be implicit in an older unindexed symmetry table; lack of a match is not a proof of unique historical priority.

## Value — PASS

Quotienting formulas by their permitted literal symmetries is a natural semantic normalization in a recently established expressively complete convex team logic. Two atoms are the first vocabulary on which the signed-literal group is strictly smaller than the unrestricted valuation-permutation group. Exact orbit counts, stabilizer frequencies and a contrasting full-permutation quotient identify the first nontrivial structural difference, while the complete checkers make the invariant independently reproducible.

## Closest literature and limits

A. Anttila and S. B. Knudstorp, *Convex Team Logics*, arXiv:2503.21850 (first posted 2025-03-27), Theorem 2.19, supplies logical expressive completeness. OEIS A369313 supplies the previously known 3,938 labeled count. Elder et al., *Toggling, rowmotion, and homomesy on interval-closed sets*, arXiv:2307.08520, addresses a different group action. Scope is exactly two Boolean atoms, not unbounded-variable complexity or syntactic formula-length optimization. Independent review remains necessary for publication-grade priority claims.
