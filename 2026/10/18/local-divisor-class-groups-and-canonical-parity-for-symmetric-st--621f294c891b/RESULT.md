# Local divisor class groups and canonical parity for symmetric-stochastic toric varieties

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(S_n\) be the convex polytope of symmetric \(n\times n\) nonnegative real matrices with each row sum one, and let \(Y_n\) be the normal projective toric variety of its normal fan. We use the lattice of integral symmetric matrices with zero row sums as the ambient difference lattice.

A vertex \(v\) is a disjoint union of singleton diagonal loops, edges on paired vertices, and undirected odd cycles of length at least three, with weights respectively \(1\), \(1\), and \(1/2\). Write \(t(v)\) for its number of pairs and \(c(v)\) for its number of odd cycles. Its normal-fan affine chart \(U_v\) has
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_v)\cong\mathbb Z^{t(v)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{c(v)}.
\]
The canonical divisor is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier; its Cartier index at the fixed point is \(2\) precisely if \(n\) is odd and \(c(v)>0\), and is \(1\) otherwise. In particular, \(Y_n\) is Gorenstein for even \(n\) and has global \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein index exactly \(2\) for odd \(n\). At a torus-fixed point, \(Y_n\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial exactly if \(t(v)=0\), and smooth exactly if \(t(v)=c(v)=0\). The sole smooth torus-fixed point is the identity matrix.

With formal variables \(z,u,q\), the joint distribution of the two defects is
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{n!}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{Vert}(S_n)}u^{t(v)}q^{c(v)}
=\exp\left(z+\frac{uz^2}{2}+\frac q2\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}\right)
=\exp\left((1-q/2)z+\frac{uz^2}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{q/4}.
\]
The equalities are identities of formal power series in \(z\) with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q[u,q]\).

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is algebraically closed, of arbitrary characteristic. The matrix size satisfies \(n\ge3\). No characteristic restriction is needed because the group computations are lattice-theoretic; in characteristic two, no assertion concerning a reduced order-two quotient group scheme is used.

Write \(d=\binom n2\) and use off-diagonal variables \(y_{ij}=x_{ij}=x_{ji}\) for \(i<j\). Diagonal variables satisfy \(x_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\ne i}y_{ij}\). Thus the affine difference lattice is \(\mathbb Z^d\), and
\[
S_n\cong\left\{y:\ y_{ij}\ge0,\quad 1-\sum_{j\ne i}y_{ij}\ge0\quad(1\le i\le n)\right\}.
\]
The \(d+n\) inequalities displayed are distinct genuine facets when \(n\ge3\); each has a relative-interior point with all other inequalities strict. Their primitive inward normals are the coordinate forms \(e_{ij}\) and the negative unsigned star forms \(-\sum_{j\ne i}e_{ij}\).

The variety \(Y_n\) is the toric variety of the full normal fan, not the affine cone over the homogeneous Ehrhart semigroup ring of \(S_n\). Its dimension is \(d\). The local group refers to Weil divisors modulo principal divisors on the entire torus-invariant affine chart \(U_v\).

## Proof

**1. Vertex support.** The extreme-point classification of symmetric stochastic matrices (Katz, and Brualdi--Cao) says that the graph of positive off-diagonal entries is a disjoint union of isolated edges with weight \(1\) and odd cycles of length at least three with weight \(1/2\). Each remaining vertex has its sole positive diagonal entry equal to \(1\). In particular, every non-loop vertex has zero diagonal, and the positive off-diagonal edges are exactly the edges of its pair or odd-cycle component. Conversely every such support decomposition determines a vertex.

**2. Normal-cone rays and the local class group.** The normal cone at a vertex is generated by the primitive inward facet normals corresponding to vanishing entries. The toric exact sequence for its full-dimensional affine cone is
\[
0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\;A_v\;}\mathbb Z^{r(v)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(U_v)\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(M\cong\mathbb Z^d\) and the rows of \(A_v\) are its primitive ray generators. Every vanishing off-diagonal entry contributes a unit row \(e_{ij}\). Eliminating these unit rows integrally leaves a block-diagonal matrix from the zero-diagonal vertices against positive off-diagonal edges. Its blocks are the negative unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrices of the positive-support connected components. Singleton loops contribute no rows or columns. A pair contributes the \(2\times1\) block \((-1,-1)^T\), whose cokernel is \(\mathbb Z\). Each odd \(\ell\)-cycle contributes the \(\ell\times\ell\) unsigned cycle-incidence matrix; row/column integer operations reduce it to Smith factors \(1\) repeated \(\ell-1\) times and \(2\) once. These Smith reductions are immediate by eliminating \(\ell-1\) successive edges and observing that the remaining row relation is twice the last edge for odd \(\ell\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(U_v)\cong\mathbb Z^{t(v)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{c(v)}.
\]
For a normal affine toric variety, \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality holds if and only if this class group has rank zero. The torsion factors obstruct smoothness; hence smoothness holds exactly when both component counts vanish, giving the identity-matrix vertex.

**3. Canonical index.** The toric canonical divisor is \(-\sum_{\rho}D_\rho\). It is Cartier at the torus-fixed point exactly when there is an integral covector \(m\in M\) with pairing \(\langle m,a_\rho\rangle=1\) for every primitive ray \(a_\rho\) of the normal cone; rational solvability gives \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartierness. Every off-support off-diagonal edge requires \(m_{ij}=1\). For a pair \(\{i,j\}\), the two star equations are \(-\sum_{k\ne i}m_{ik}=1\) and \(-\sum_{k\ne j}m_{jk}=1\). There are \(n-2\) off-support edges at each endpoint, forcing \(m_{ij}=-(n-1)\). For an odd support cycle, there are \(n-3\) off-support edges at each cycle vertex; the two incident cycle variables have sum \(-(n-2)\). The unique rational solution on an odd cycle assigns every cycle edge \(-(n-2)/2\). This is integral when \(n\) is even and half-integral when \(n\) is odd. Thus the local Cartier index is \(2\) precisely when \(n\) is odd and there exists an odd-cycle component, and is \(1\) in all other cases. Rational solutions always exist. The full normal fan is covered by these maximal cones, so the global \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein index is the least common multiple of the local indices. For every odd \(n\ge3\) the support containing one odd \(n\)-cycle exists, so the global index is exactly \(2\); it is \(1\) for even \(n\).

**4. Complete enumeration.** Apply the labeled exponential formula to set partitions into permitted components. A singleton loop contributes \(z\); a paired edge contributes \(uz^2/2\); and a cycle of odd length \(\ell\ge3\) has \((\ell-1)!/2\) possible undirected cyclic orders and contributes \(qz^\ell/(2\ell)\). Exponentiating their sum gives the first displayed series. The identity \(\sum_{j\ge1}z^{2j+1}/(2j+1)=\tfrac12\log((1+z)/(1-z))-z\) yields the closed form. This proves the bivariate formula for every \(n\ge0\), with the empty decomposition convention at \(n=0\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify_symmetric_stochastic.py` enumerates all loop/pair/odd-cycle support decompositions for \(3\le n\le6\). For each, it constructs the actual active-facet ray matrix in the full \(\binom n2\)-coordinate lattice, checks its integral Smith normal form, and exactly solves the toric canonical equations. Separately, for \(n=3,4\), an exact basic-feasible-solution enumeration using all independent facet subsets recovers precisely the proposed vertices. The complete fixed-point counts are \(5,14,58,238\) in dimensions indexed by \(n=3,4,5,6\), and the independent check returns `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests support reproducibility but do not substitute for the all-parameter proof above. Running requires Python 3 and SymPy.

## Relationship to prior work

Katz (1970) determined extreme symmetric-stochastic matrices. Brualdi and Cao (arXiv:1712.03129, 2017) give an accessible modern classification in terms of the same permitted loop/pair/odd-cycle components; those established vertex types are not new here. Davis (arXiv:1409.2742, 2014; *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics*, 2015) treats Ehrhart series, facets and Gorenstein properties of the symmetric-stochastic polytope in an Ehrhart setting; the global even-parity Gorenstein observation should not be represented as independent of that work. Our statement concerns the normal-fan toric variety's *local affine Weil divisor class groups*, the detailed local Cartier indices and their joint support-type distribution, rather than the Ehrhart ring's Hilbert-series classification. The inspected modern texts do not state or directly subsume the complete local formulas.

## Limitations

The result determines local class groups and canonical indices at torus-fixed charts, not the scheme structure or irreducible components of the entire singular locus, nor analytic types at arbitrary lower-dimensional orbits. The claim is over arbitrary algebraically closed fields but does not identify a characteristic-independent finite quotient representation at a torsion point. An older equivalent result under alternative nomenclature is possible; Katz's original full text was not accessible, although its vertex characterization was recovered from later full text. Scientific independence beyond the stated literature comparisons has not been externally established.
