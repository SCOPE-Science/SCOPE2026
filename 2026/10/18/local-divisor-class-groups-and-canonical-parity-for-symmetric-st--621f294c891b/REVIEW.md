# Review: local divisor class groups of symmetric-stochastic toric varieties

## Correctness — PASS

The matrix coordinates give every primitive facet normal in the integral off-diagonal lattice. At any vertex, eliminating active unit rows leaves one unsigned incidence block per nontrivial support component: each pair contributes one free generator, each odd cycle contributes one Smith invariant \(2\). These blocks prove the claimed affine toric class group. Rational canonical Cartier equations have unique solutions \(-(n-1)\) on positive pairs and \(-(n-2)/2\) on positive odd cycles, proving the stated indices. The exponential formula for labeled loops, pairs and undirected odd cycles establishes the complete generating series. An exact checker recomputes the relevant Smith forms and canonical pairings for every support decomposition through size six, and independently enumerates the feasible vertices through size four.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risks

Katz's vertex characterization and the later Brualdi--Cao classification are explicitly prior; they do not themselves give local toric divisor groups. Davis examines the same polytope's facet and Ehrhart-Gorenstein properties, so the even-parity global Gorenstein context is not claimed independently. The result instead supplies all vertex-indexed local class groups, canonical Cartier indices, and their joint EGF. Available literature and equivalent-term mathematical searches found no theorem implying all three. Katz's full text was inaccessible, and unindexed or differently worded earlier local-classification results remain genuine risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The polytope is a natural fractional one-factor configuration that includes abundant half-integral vertices. The theorem separates non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial free-class defects caused by paired components from finite order-two class defects caused by odd cycles; canonical Cartier parity depends on the ambient matrix size. This simultaneous local classification and type census adds structural information unavailable from vertex counting, global normality, or the Ehrhart series alone.

## Limits and nearest literature

The nearest treatments are Katz (1970), Brualdi--Cao (2017/2018), and Davis (2014/2015). The proof is an all-size argument; finite checks are only corroboration. Complete singular-locus components, nonfixed orbit charts, and the affine Ehrhart semigroup ring are outside the claim.
