# Locating-dominating coalition numbers of complete multipartite graphs

## Finding
For every finite simple connected complete multipartite graph \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) with \(r\ge2\), \(n_i\ge1\), and \(N=\sum_i n_i\ge3\), define
\[s=|\{i:n_i=1\}|,\qquad t=|\{i:n_i=2\}|.\]
Its locating-dominating coalition number is
\[
C_L(G)=\begin{cases}
4,&\max_i n_i\le2,\quad s\le2,\quad t+\mathbf 1_{\{s=2\}}\ge2,\\
3,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular every complete multipartite graph of order at least three has a locating-dominating coalition partition, and none has more than four classes. Four classes occur precisely when the auxiliary constraint graph defined below is a matching with at least two edges (possibly with isolated vertices).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and labelled, although the numerical result depends only on part sizes. A set \(D\subseteq V(G)\) is locating-dominating (LD) when each vertex outside \(D\) has a nonempty set of neighbours in \(D\), with these neighbourhood traces pairwise distinct. A locating-dominating coalition partition is a partition of \(V(G)\) into nonempty blocks, none individually LD, such that each block has a distinct partner block whose union with it is LD. Write \(C_L(G)\) for the largest number of blocks. The two-vertex complete graph \(K_2\), which has no such partition, is excluded.

Define \(H(G)\) on \(V(G)\) as follows: for every multipartite class of order at least two, include all edges inside that class; also include all edges among vertices belonging to singleton classes. There are no other edges in \(H(G)\). Thus \(H(G)\) is a disjoint union of cliques, with the clique on singleton classes permitted to be empty or a single vertex. A matching with isolated vertices means every nontrivial component is exactly \(K_2\).

## Proof
**1. Reduction to vertex covers.** Write \(U_i\) for the multipartite classes, and let \(D\subseteq V(G)\). Two omitted vertices in the same class have identical traces in \(D\); so do two omitted vertices from distinct singleton classes, since each of their traces is exactly \(D\). Consequently, an LD set must meet every edge of \(H(G)\), that is, be a vertex cover of \(H(G)\).

Conversely assume \(G\) is not a star \(K_{1,m}\), \(m\ge2\), and \(D\) is a vertex cover of \(H(G)\). There are three possibilities: (i) there are at least two non-singleton classes; (ii) exactly one such class and at least two singleton classes; (iii) every class is singleton and there are at least three classes. In case (i), \(D\) meets each of at least two non-singleton classes; in case (ii), it meets the non-singleton class and at least one singleton class; and in case (iii), it meets at least two singleton classes. Hence \(D\) meets at least two distinct multipartite classes. Every outside vertex therefore has a nonempty trace. Any two outside vertices from distinct non-singleton classes have traces \(D\setminus(D\cap U_i)\) and \(D\setminus(D\cap U_j)\), distinct because both selected intersections are nonempty and disjoint. An outside vertex from a singleton class has trace \(D\), which differs from the trace of every outside vertex in a non-singleton class. At most one singleton-class vertex is omitted. Thus all outside traces are distinct, and \(D\) is LD.

Accordingly, outside the star exception, \(D\) is LD **if and only if** it is a vertex cover of \(H(G)\).

**2. The universal bound of four.** Fix any coalition partition \(\Pi=\{B_1,\ldots,B_k\}\) for a non-star graph. Form a graph with loops \(J\) on the block indices: every edge \(uv\) of \(H(G)\) gives the edge between the blocks containing \(u\) and \(v\), a loop if these blocks coincide. Duplicates may be discarded. By step 1, a union of one or two blocks is LD exactly when its block-index set is a vertex cover of \(J\). Thus no singleton index is a vertex cover, and every index belongs to some vertex cover of size two.

We claim such a looped graph has at most four vertices. If it has two disjoint edges, with a loop regarded as an edge with one endpoint, then every cover of size two must choose a vertex in each edge; consequently every index lies among the at most four endpoints of those two edges. If no two edges are disjoint, and no one-vertex cover exists, the graph has no loop (a loop would otherwise force a singleton cover), and its nonempty edges form a triangle: indeed, any pairwise-intersecting family of ordinary graph edges is either a star or contained in a triangle. Since a star has a one-vertex cover, only the triangle is possible, and no fourth vertex can occur in a size-two cover. This proves \(k\le4\).

**3. Four-block necessity.** Suppose \(k=4\). The graph \(H(G)\) cannot have a clique of order three. If three vertices of such a clique occupy at least three different blocks, their pair edges produce a triangle in \(J\), and a cover of size two containing the fourth index is impossible. If any two clique vertices occupy the same block, their edge produces a loop at index \(j\). Every cover of size two must then contain \(j\). Because \(\{j\}\) is not a cover, some other edge has neither endpoint \(j\); any size-two cover must use \(j\) and one of the at most two endpoints of that other edge. Therefore at most three indices can participate, again a contradiction. Hence every component of \(H(G)\) has at most two vertices. Moreover, \(H(G)\) must have at least two edges: with zero edges every singleton index is a vertex cover, and with one edge one of its endpoint indices is a singleton vertex cover (including the loop case). Thus \(H(G)\) is a matching with at least two edges, plus any isolated vertices.

**4. Four-block sufficiency.** Conversely suppose \(H(G)\) is a matching containing at least two edges. Split its edges into two nonempty groups. For the first group assign one endpoint of every edge to block \(B_1\) and its other endpoint to \(B_2\); for the second group assign one endpoint to \(B_3\) and its other endpoint to \(B_4\). Assign any isolated vertices to any of these four blocks. Each block omits both ends of an edge from the opposite group, so none is a vertex cover. Each union \(B_i\cup B_j\), with \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and \(j\in\{3,4\}\), contains one endpoint of every edge of \(H(G)\), hence is an LD set. Therefore \(\Pi\) is a four-block coalition partition.

**5. Existence of three blocks and the star exception.** Choose two vertices \(u,v\) in a multipartite class of size at least two; when all classes are singleton, choose any two vertices. The three blocks \(\{u\}\), \(\{v\}\), and \(V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}\) are all non-LD: the last has the two omitted vertices with identical traces, and the singletons cannot distinguish these twin vertices or all other vertices. Each singleton forms a coalition with the last block, because removing just one vertex from any connected graph gives an LD set. Thus \(C_L(G)\ge3\). The same construction works in stars. For a star \(K_{1,m}\), \(m\ge2\), every LD set contains at least \(m\) vertices: two omitted leaves are indistinguishable, and if the center and one leaf are both omitted the latter has no selected neighbour. Hence \(\gamma_L(K_{1,m})=m\), since all leaves form an LD set. A coalition partition with \(k\) blocks contains two whose union has at least \(m\) vertices, so \(m+1\ge m+(k-2)\), yielding \(k\le3\). Therefore stars also have exactly three blocks.

Finally, from the definition of \(H(G)\), its nontrivial cliques have orders \(n_i\) for \(n_i\ge2\), together with a clique of order \(s\) on the singleton-class vertices when \(s\ge2\). It is a matching with at least two edges precisely when \(\max_i n_i\le2\), \(s\le2\), and \(t+\mathbf1_{\{s=2\}}\ge2\). Combining the preceding steps gives the stated exact classification. \(\square\)

## Verification
A standalone verifier `verify.py` constructs each complete multipartite graph from its part-size vector, exhaustively checks all vertex subsets for the LD property, independently tests the vertex-cover criterion outside the star exception, enumerates every unlabeled set partition into nonempty blocks, and computes the maximum number of coalition blocks. It compares these data with the formula for all \(86\) integer part-size profiles of orders \(3\) through \(9\). All checks pass. It separately checks the cover-support lemma for each of the \(1,024\) loopless simple graphs on five indices. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.10 or later. These finite tests corroborate but do not substitute for the proof for arbitrary orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Chellali, Dobrynin, Foucaud, Golmohammadi and Valenzuela-Tripodoro (2026) introduced locating-dominating coalitions and provided general existence and upper-bound results, including \(C_L(G)\le |V(G)|-\gamma_L(G)+2\), as well as classifications of graphs with maximal possible coalition number and several paths and cycles. In particular some small complete multipartite examples are already covered by their general results, and those instances are not claimed individually as new. The present result gives a uniform constant upper bound and an exact equality characterization throughout the infinite class of complete multipartite graphs; neither the cited general inequality nor its small-order equality characterization supplies that statement.

Other recent work on self- and solid-location domination in complete multipartite graphs treats different locating-code conditions, not coalition partitions. The reduction through a vertex-cover quotient is specific to the coalition condition and yields the four-block obstruction directly.

## Limitations
The statement covers connected complete multipartite graphs with at least three vertices, not arbitrary graphs or disconnected complete multipartite conventions. The two-vertex complete graph has no such partition. The result has been checked mathematically and by finite computation here, but no independent mathematical audit is claimed. Bibliographic priority is not established conclusively: the full 23-page original 2026 manuscript was inspected (including the general bound, paths, cycles, small-order classifications, computational complexity and open problems), and no complete multipartite formula was found there. The cited small-order results already cover some special examples but do not imply the new uniform theorem. Unindexed papers or differently phrased equivalent results might remain undiscovered.
