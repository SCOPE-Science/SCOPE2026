# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. The Cartesian-to-polar identities and the exact change of physical time are checked symbolically and by independent high-precision diagnostic calculations. The integral \(I\) is a strictly increasing bijection, the periodic profile is smooth and positive, and the \(O(t^{-1})\) term follows from an exact identity and bounded periodic derivatives. The logarithmic occupation law follows by the change of variables \(u=\log t\) and periodic Cesàro averaging. The argument includes all \(a>0\), every real \(b\), and all nonzero initial points; no finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual literature risk. The full Liu–Fitzsimmons primary article, especially Sections III-A and IV-D, supplies the model, its polar system, global attraction, and Lyapunov consequences, but not the physical-time amplitude profile or universal logarithmic occupation theorem. The physically related, fully read homogeneous star-node result treats \(\lambda>0\) attracting finite-radius cycles; no exact time-decay result at \(\lambda=0\) follows from its stated theorem. The fully read cubic Liénard result has a constant rescaled radial limit and a different phase mechanism. Multiple semantic database queries returned no covering theorem. Standard solvability of homogeneous polar flows is not itself claimed new; an unindexed classical homogeneous-focus computation might nevertheless cover this exact formula.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Physical-time convergence is not determined by the mere existence of a global Lyapunov function. The theorem identifies an unusual qualitative obstruction to a sharp constant radial prefactor in the canonical integer-coefficient nonanalytic Lyapunov counterexample. It quantitatively characterizes the entire oscillatory prefactor and the robust logarithmic sampling distribution, rather than only bounding decay above and below. The sharp constancy threshold \(b=0\) explains which member of the motivated family has ordinary asymptotic normalization.
