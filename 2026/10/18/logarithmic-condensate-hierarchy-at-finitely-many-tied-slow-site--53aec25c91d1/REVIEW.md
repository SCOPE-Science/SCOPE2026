# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite invariant weights follow from a direct balance identity on the directed ring. Counting ordinary-site allocations reduces their total to a geometrically tilted negative-binomial law. The exact slow partition is a fixed-column Stirling coefficient, giving its asymptotic normalization. Harmonic box sums establish the full joint log-coordinate limit, and an explicit two-macroscopic-variable Riemann sum establishes the rare-sharing coefficient, with three-or-more-macroscopic corrections one logarithmic order smaller. A central limit argument transfers Gaussian ordinary-site fluctuations and establishes independence using uniform conditional convergence. Exact-rational finite-state flux checks and exact Stirling identities passed. Scope is fixed slow-site multiplicity and strictly supercritical density.

## Originality — PASS, with an explicit equivalent-object risk
Angel–Evans–Mukamel (2004) establishes the one-defect stationary method; Godrèche–Luck (2012), Appendix A, covers the two-site canonical profile (including the underlying critical finite probability weight), but not the \(m\ge3\) joint critical logarithmic hierarchy and its coupled Gaussian fluid and rare two-macro coefficient. Schwarzkopf–Evans–Mukamel (2008) concerns a different nonmonotone multiple-condensation mechanism; Mailler–Mörters–Ueltschi (2015) treats random weak disorder without finitely many exact ties. The exact conditional law is equivalent to uniformly labeled cycle lengths of a random permutation conditioned to have exactly \(m\) cycles, by the classical Cauchy formula. That equivalence was specifically considered: the inspected classical identity and general cycle-count results do not state the joint asymptotic and correction in the present form, but an unindexed specialized conditional-cycle theorem remains a material risk. This finding does not claim novelty of the Stirling formula or finite product measure.
