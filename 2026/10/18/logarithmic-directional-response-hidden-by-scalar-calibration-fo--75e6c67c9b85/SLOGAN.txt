For a noisy real \(1\times2\) polar-normalized update, increasing Gaussian covariance anisotropy makes the weaker-noise coordinate's local response eventually exceed the other by a factor \(\log(4r)-1+o(1)\). Simultaneously the best noise-weighted scalar response fit has relative error \(\sim(\log r)^2/r^2\to0\), making a quantitatively small scalar diagnostic compatible with arbitrarily large directional gain disparity.
