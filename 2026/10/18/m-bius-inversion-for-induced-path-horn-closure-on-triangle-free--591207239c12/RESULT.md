# Möbius inversion for induced-path Horn closure on triangle-free line graphs

## Finding

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple triangle-free graph; isolated vertices are allowed. Let \(L(G)\) be its line graph, whose vertices are the edges of \(G\). Consider the closure \(c\) generated by the binary-premise Horn implications \(\{f,g\}\to e\) whenever \(f,e,g\), in this order, form an induced three-vertex path in \(L(G)\). Write \(\mathcal L(G)\) for the lattice of closed edge subsets, ordered by inclusion.

**Theorem.** For every \(S\subseteq E\), let \(V(S)\) be the set of endpoints of its edges. Then
\[
c(S)=E(G[V(S)]).
\]
In particular, forward chaining reaches its final fixed point after one simultaneous application of the implications. A set \(A\subseteq E\) is closed exactly when \(A=E(G[V(A)])\).

For any interval \(A\subseteq B\) in \(\mathcal L(G)\), put
\[
R=V(B)\setminus V(A),\qquad W=R\setminus N_G(V(A)),
\]
where \(N_G(U)\) is the set of vertices outside \(U\) adjacent to at least one member of \(U\). Define the independence polynomial of an induced graph by
\[
I_{G[W]}(t)=\sum_{J\subseteq W\, :\, J\text{ independent in }G[W]}t^{|J|},\qquad I_{G[\varnothing]}(t)=1.
\]
Then the Möbius function of this Horn-model lattice is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mu_{\mathcal L(G)}(A,B)=(-1)^{|R|}I_{G[W]}(-1).}
\]
This formula handles the empty graph, empty interval, disconnected graphs, and intervals with arbitrary nonempty lower endpoints without exceptions.

**Forest rigidity and cyclic amplification.** If \(G\) is a forest, every interval has Möbius number in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). For the cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge4\), its full interval satisfies
\[
\mu(\varnothing,E(C_n))=(-1)^n\,2\cos(n\pi/3).
\]
Hence a disjoint union of \(k\ge1\) copies of \(C_6\) has bottom-to-top Möbius number \(2^k\). The triangle-free hypothesis is material for the closure identity: on a triangle, two selected edges activate all three endpoints, but the line graph has no induced three-vertex path and the third edge remains unforced.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and triangle-free unless a counterexample is explicitly identified. An induced three-vertex path \(f,e,g\) in a line graph means that \(f\) and \(e\) share a vertex of \(G\), \(e\) and \(g\) share a vertex, while \(f\) and \(g\) are disjoint edges of \(G\). Its middle line-graph vertex \(e\) is the consequent. A Horn premise is a set of two edge-vertices, not an ordered pair. The empty edge set is closed. The lattice meet is set intersection, and the join is the closure of union. The Möbius function is characterized by \(\mu(A,A)=1\) and \(\sum_{A\subseteq C\subseteq B}\mu(A,C)=0\) whenever \(A\subsetneq B\).

## Proof

**1. Exactly one-step induced-edge closure.** Every Horn rule \(\{f,g\}\to e\) has both endpoints of \(e\) incident to one of the selected premise edges, because \(f\) and \(g\) are disjoint and each meets \(e\). Thus each consequence of an initial set \(S\) belongs to \(E(G[V(S)])\), and this family itself is closed under every rule.

Conversely, take an edge \(e=uv\in E(G[V(S)])\setminus S\). Some \(f\in S\) meets \(u\), and some \(g\in S\) meets \(v\). Since \(e\notin S\), write \(f=ux\) with \(x\ne v\) and \(g=vy\) with \(y\ne u\). The equality \(x=y\) would complete a triangle with \(e\), so \(x\ne y\). The edges \(f\) and \(g\) are disjoint, and \(f,e,g\) is an induced three-vertex path in \(L(G)\). Accordingly \(\{f,g\}\to e\) fires directly from \(S\). This proves the claimed closure equality and single-round saturation.

**2. A Boolean-fiber Möbius identity.** Let \(c\) be any closure operator on a finite set, and let \(A,B\) be closed with \(A\subseteq B\). Then
\[
\mu(A,B)=\sum_{S\subseteq B\setminus A\, :\,c(A\cup S)=B}(-1)^{|S|}.
\]
To prove it, let the right side be \(m(A,B)\). Since \(B\) is closed, every \(S\subseteq B\setminus A\) has \(c(A\cup S)\subseteq B\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{A\subseteq C\subseteq B}m(A,C)
=\sum_{S\subseteq B\setminus A}(-1)^{|S|}
=\begin{cases}1,&A=B,\\0,&A\subsetneq B.\end{cases}
\]
By the defining Möbius recurrence, \(m=\mu\).

**3. Convert generation into edge covering.** Fix closed \(A\subseteq B\), let \(U=V(A)\) and \(R=V(B)\setminus U\). Any \(S\subseteq B\setminus A\) satisfies \(c(A\cup S)=B\) if and only if each vertex of \(R\) is incident to an edge of \(S\). Indeed, \(V(A\cup S)=U\cup V(S)\) and step 1 says the closure is the induced-edge set on precisely those active vertices. Since a closed edge set has no isolated vertices in its active vertex set, equality of closures and equality of active vertex sets are equivalent. All edges of \(B\setminus A\) have at least one endpoint in \(R\).

Use inclusion-exclusion over uncovered vertices. Let \(\delta_{B\setminus A}(J)\) consist of edges of \(B\setminus A\) incident with \(J\subseteq R\). By step 2,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(A,B)
&=\sum_{S\subseteq B\setminus A}(-1)^{|S|}
  \prod_{v\in R}\bigl(1-\mathbf1_{\{v\text{ not incident with an edge of }S\}}\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{J\subseteq R}(-1)^{|J|}
  \sum_{S\subseteq(B\setminus A)\setminus\delta_{B\setminus A}(J)}(-1)^{|S|}\\
&=\sum_{J\subseteq R\, :\,\delta_{B\setminus A}(J)=B\setminus A}(-1)^{|J|}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last condition says precisely that \(J\) is a vertex cover of all new edges.

**4. Reduce the vertex covers to an independence polynomial.** Put \(K=R\cap N_G(U)\) and \(W=R\setminus K\). Every edge between \(U\) and \(R\) forces its endpoint in \(K\) into the vertex cover \(J\), so \(K\subseteq J\). Once all vertices in \(K\) are selected, every edge touching \(K\) is covered. The remaining constraints are exactly the edges of \(G[W]\); there are no edges of \(B\setminus A\) entirely in \(U\), because \(A=E(G[U])\). Thus eligible sets have the unique form \(J=K\cup T\) where \(T\subseteq W\) is a vertex cover of \(G[W]\). Complementing \(T\) inside \(W\) gives an independent set \(Q=W\setminus T\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(A,B)
&=(-1)^{|K|}\sum_{T\text{ vertex cover of }G[W]}(-1)^{|T|}\\
&=(-1)^{|K|+|W|}\sum_{Q\text{ independent in }G[W]}(-1)^{|Q|}\\
&=(-1)^{|R|}I_{G[W]}(-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the exact interval formula.

**5. Forest bound.** For an induced subforest \(F\), the factorization \(I_{F_1\sqcup F_2}(t)=I_{F_1}(t)I_{F_2}(t)\) and the recurrence
\[
I_F(t)=I_{F-u}(t)+tI_{F-N[u]}(t)
\]
hold for every vertex \(u\), where \(N[u]\) is its closed neighborhood in \(F\). If \(F\) has an isolated vertex, \(I_F(-1)=0\). Otherwise choose a leaf \(v\) with neighbor \(u\). The graph \(F-u\) has isolated vertex \(v\), so \(I_F(-1)=-I_{F-N[u]}(-1)\). Iterating this identity on the strictly smaller forest, with \(I_{\varnothing}(-1)=1\), yields \(I_F(-1)\in\{-1,0,1\}\). The interval formula transfers this bound to every interval of \(\mathcal L(G)\).

**6. Cycles.** The vertex independence polynomial of \(C_n\), evaluated at \(-1\), equals the trace of the two-state transfer matrix
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed, the two states encode whether the previous vertex is excluded or included, and an included vertex forces exclusion of its successor. Its eigenvalues are \(e^{\pm i\pi/3}\), so \(I_{C_n}(-1)=\operatorname{tr}(T^n)=2\cos(n\pi/3)\). For the bottom-to-top interval of \(C_n\), we have \(U=\varnothing\), \(R=W=V(C_n)\); the claimed cycle formula follows. Disjoint unions multiply independence polynomials, proving the \(2^k\) family.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` independently enumerates the induced-three-vertex-path Horn hull, reconstructs the closure lattice and all interval Möbius values by its defining recurrence, and compares them with the proposed independence-polynomial identity. ## Relationship to prior work

The binary-premise closure is the induced-path \(l^2\)-convexity used in graph convexity, specialized to line graphs. Gutierrez, Protti, and Tondato (2022) characterize graphs for which induced-path convexity is a convex geometry; they do not derive the edge-induced closure and all-interval Möbius identity given here. Dourado, Penso, and Rautenbach (2022) study hull-number complexity in induced-three-vertex-path convexity; the theorem here instead computes incidence-algebra invariants of the entire Horn-model lattice. General Boolean-fiber Möbius inversion is classical; the step-2 identity is included as a proof tool, not presented as an independently new general theorem. Evaluating graph independence polynomials at \(-1\) is also classical; the nontrivial bridge is the explicit interval identification of the residual induced graph \(G[W]\). Existing results of Kim (2021) on independence complexes and forbidden cycles may imply further \(\{0,\pm1\}\) cases after applying this bridge; the forest bound alone is not asserted as novel. The novelty claim is restricted to the combined line-graph closure description and exact Möbius formula for **all** intervals, with its sharp structural consequences.

## Limitations

The asserted equality of closure with induced-edge completion is established for triangle-free simple graphs. It is false for a triangle as described above; no general formula is claimed for arbitrary graphs with triangles. The displayed Möbius formula can require an exponentially costly independence-polynomial evaluation for unrestricted triangle-free graphs. The forest and cycle formulas are efficient special cases. These statements have not undergone independent external mathematical validation. An equivalent result in graph-induced-subgraph lattices, antimatroid, closure-space or incidence-algebra literature cannot be ruled out merely by the absence of a search hit.
