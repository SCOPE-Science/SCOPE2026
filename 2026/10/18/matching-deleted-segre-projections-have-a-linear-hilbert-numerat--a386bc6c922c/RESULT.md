# Matching-deleted Segre projections have a linear Hilbert-numerator deficit

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field, let integers \(a,b\ge 3\) and \(0\le k\le\min(a,b)\), and consider the toric algebra
\[
R_{a,b,k}=K[u_i v_j: 1\le i\le a,\ 1\le j\le b,\ (i,j)\notin\{(s,s):1\le s\le k\}],
\]
with every displayed product assigned degree one. This is the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Zariski closure \(X_{a,b,k}\) of the coordinate projection of rank-one \(a\times b\) matrices away from \(k\) entries on a partial matching. All such rings are normal, Cohen--Macaulay, and projectively normal. For every integer \(t\ge0\),
\[
\dim_K (R_{a,b,k})_t=\binom{t+a-1}{a-1}\binom{t+b-1}{b-1}-k\binom{t+a+b-3}{a+b-2}.
\]
Their Hilbert series and projective degrees are
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_{a,b,k}}(z)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\min(a-1,b-1)}\binom{a-1}j\binom{b-1}jz^j-kz}{(1-z)^{a+b-1}},\qquad
\deg X_{a,b,k}=\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}-k.
\]
Their Krull dimension is \(a+b-1\), and the ring is Gorenstein if and only if \(a=b\) and either \(k=0\) or \(a=3\). The condition \(a=3\) includes all \(0\le k\le3\) in the square case.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings and varieties are considered over an arbitrary field \(K\), without characteristic restrictions. The integers satisfy \(a,b\ge3\), and omitted matrix coordinates form a partial matching: no two omitted entries share a row or column. After independent permutations of the rows and columns, such a pattern is the diagonal partial matching used in the formulas. Binomial coefficients have the convention \(\binom rs=0\) when \(r<s\) for nonnegative lower argument \(s\). A toric monomial \(u^r v^c\) in degree \(t\) has nonnegative row and column exponents with \(\sum r_i=\sum c_j=t\).

## Proof
**1. Semigroup description and saturation.** Let \(G\) be the bipartite graph on row vertices \(1,\ldots,a\) and column vertices \(1,\ldots,b\) whose edge \((i,j)\) is present exactly when the corresponding coordinate was retained. This graph is connected because \(a,b\ge3\) and the missing edges are pairwise disjoint. Its edge vectors \(e_i+f_j\) generate the exponent semigroup defining \(R_{a,b,k}\). A nonnegative integral pair of margin vectors \((r,c)\), with equal sum \(t\), belongs to this semigroup if and only if there is a nonnegative integral transportation table on the allowed edges with row sums \(r\) and column sums \(c\).

The bipartite integral max-flow/min-cut theorem characterizes existence by \(\sum_{i\in S}r_i\le\sum_{j\in N(S)}c_j\) for every subset \(S\) of rows. For \(|S|\ge2\), every column is in \(N(S)\), since a column misses at most one row; these inequalities are automatic. For a singleton row \(i>k\), its neighborhood is all columns. For \(i\le k\), its neighborhood omits only column \(i\), leaving the necessary and sufficient inequality
\[
r_i+c_i\le t.\tag{1}
\]
Thus *all* integral margins of total \(t\) satisfying (1) for the \(k\) deleted edges are attained by integral edge combinations. These constraints also describe the intersection of the rational cone with the integer lattice of equal-margin-sum pairs. Connectivity of \(G\) makes that equal-sum lattice the group generated by its edge vectors: differences along paths generate all zero-total row/column shifts. Therefore the semigroup is saturated and \(R_{a,b,k}\) is normal. Normal affine semigroup rings over any field are Cohen--Macaulay by Hochster's theorem. The grading is generated in degree one, so the corresponding projective embedding is projectively normal.

**2. Exact margin count.** Without the \(k\) restrictions, the number of nonnegative row margins is \(\binom{t+a-1}{a-1}\), independently of the \(\binom{t+b-1}{b-1}\) column margins. For a forbidden diagonal position \(i\), the bad event is \(r_i+c_i>t\). Two different forbidden positions cannot both be bad: indeed they would imply \((r_i+r_j)+(c_i+c_j)>2t\), contradicting the two margin sums. Hence inclusion--exclusion stops after the first term.

Fix one forbidden position. Write \(\alpha=t-r_i\) and \(\beta=t-c_i\), so a bad event is equivalent to \(\alpha+\beta\le t-1\). The other \(a-1\) row entries and \(b-1\) column entries can then be chosen in
\(\binom{\alpha+a-2}{a-2}\binom{\beta+b-2}{b-2}\) ways. The double hockey-stick/Vandermonde convolution gives
\[
\sum_{\alpha+\beta\le t-1}\binom{\alpha+a-2}{a-2}\binom{\beta+b-2}{b-2}
=\binom{t+a+b-3}{a+b-2}.
\]
For clarity, its generating series in \(t\) is \(z(1-z)^{-(a-1)}(1-z)^{-(b-1)}(1-z)^{-1}=z/(1-z)^{a+b-1}\), which proves the identity coefficientwise. Subtracting \(k\) disjoint bad-event counts proves the asserted exact all-degree Hilbert function, including \(t=0\).

**3. Hilbert numerator and degree.** The classical Segre generating-function identity is
\[
\sum_{t\ge0}\binom{t+a-1}{a-1}\binom{t+b-1}{b-1}z^t
=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\min(a-1,b-1)}\binom{a-1}j\binom{b-1}jz^j}{(1-z)^{a+b-1}}.
\]
Together with the bad-event generating series, this yields the Hilbert-series formula. Its numerator evaluated at \(z=1\) is \(\sum_j\binom{a-1}j\binom{b-1}j-k=\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}-k\) by Vandermonde. Because the connected graph has dimension \(a+b-1\) in the ring grading, this numerator value is the projective degree of \(X_{a,b,k}\).

**4. Sharp Gorenstein classification.** A normal standard graded affine semigroup ring associated with a lattice polytope is Gorenstein if and only if its Ehrhart/Hilbert \(h^*\)-polynomial is palindromic (the toric version of the canonical-module reciprocity criterion). Write the numerator as \(h_{a,b,k}(z)=\sum_j\binom{a-1}j\binom{b-1}jz^j-kz\). If \(a\ne b\), its constant coefficient is \(1\), whereas the leading coefficient is \(\binom{\max(a,b)-1}{\min(a,b)-1}>1\), so it cannot be palindromic. If \(a=b=n\ge4\), then for \(k>0\) its coefficient of \(z\) is \((n-1)^2-k\), while the symmetric partner at \(z^{n-2}\) is \((n-1)^2\), so it is not palindromic. If \(k=0\), the square Segre numerator is palindromic. Finally, if \(a=b=3\), the numerator is \(1+(4-k)z+z^2\) for every \(0\le k\le3\), hence palindromic. This exhausts all parameters.

## Verification
The supplied standalone Python checker independently enumerates degree-\(t\) sums of individual permitted edge vectors and compares their distinct exponent tuples with the closed Hilbert-function expression. It passed 139 separate \((a,b,k,t)\) cases for sizes up to \(6\times6\) and degree up to five. The finite tests corroborate only these cases; the all-parameter proof is the exact integral-flow and disjoint-exclusion argument above. To replay, execute `python3 verification/check_matching_segre.py` with a standard Python 3 interpreter; no external packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Kahle, Kubjas, Kummer, and Rosen analyze algebraic and real rank-one tensor completion using projections of Segre varieties, including a diagonal-*observed* example. The present observation pattern retains all coordinates *except* a partial diagonal matching; its all-degree Hilbert function is a different invariant from their completability criteria. Ohsugi and Hibi give general normality criteria for graph edge rings: normality here is background, not claimed as a new phenomenon. Matsui develops general Ehrhart-series tools and covers even-cycle examples, including the six-cycle boundary of this family, but the explicit all-parameter linear Hilbert-numerator deficit is not one of the formulas inspected there. Gitler and Valencia's graph-wide results concern multiplicity and a Gorenstein condition; the degree and Gorenstein classifications here may be specializations of general earlier criteria, whereas the main contribution is the precise entire graded Hilbert function. The later Higashitani--Deepthi paper computes a toric edge-ring \(h\)-polynomial of a different nonbipartite graph family, with a single-degree negative term; its introductory literature review lists the complete-bipartite Segre numerator as known but does not treat a deleted matching. Thus a subtraction term alone is not claimed as novel: the contribution here is the exact \(k\)-matching-deficit identity for the whole Segre family. A different later paper on "edge rings" of deficient complete bipartite graphs studies a Stanley--Reisner edge ideal rather than this toric edge subalgebra.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to deleted edges forming a matching with both matrix dimensions at least three. Overlapping missing positions have nontrivial intersections of bad-margin events and do not satisfy the displayed formula in general. The theorem does not establish defining ideals, syzygies, or numerical properties of arbitrary missing-edge patterns. General graph-theoretic algorithms in prior work may reproduce individual cases computationally; here the novelty claimed is the closed expression for the complete matching-deletion family, subject to the remaining literature-access risks discussed in the scientific review. No independent external audit has been performed.
