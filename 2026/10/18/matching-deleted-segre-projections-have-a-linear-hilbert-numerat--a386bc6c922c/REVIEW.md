# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The row/column exponent semigroup of the bipartite edge graph is characterized by integral max-flow conditions. The disjoint missing-edge pattern makes the entire Hall family reduce to the \(k\) inequalities \(r_i+c_i\le t\). Two failure events cannot coexist because their combined row/column exponent contributions would exceed \(2t\). Their individual sizes are exact double-binomial convolutions, giving an all-degree Hilbert function. The Hilbert-series numerator follows by the classical Segre generating identity. Normality and Cohen--Macaulayness follow from integral-flow saturation and normal semigroup theorems. The Gorenstein criterion is checked coefficientwise in all rectangular and square cases. The standalone enumerator independently confirmed 139 finite parameter/degree cases. These cases are supporting checks, not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS with disclosed residual risks
The motivating rank-one completion paper treats projections and semialgebraic completability, not the stated all-parameter graded Hilbert function for omitting a matching. Ohsugi--Hibi's normality theory directly covers the normality component, which is not separately claimed as new. Matsui's full-text treatment provides graph-Ehrhart methods and special even cycles, not the displayed closed all-parameter matching-deletion formula. Gitler--Valencia's abstract explicitly concerns multiplicities and graph-wide Gorenstein conditions: the degree and Gorenstein consequences may be covered as special cases, and are not the sole originality claim. The first-order linear perturbation of the entire Segre numerator is the non-implied identified contribution in the materials inspected. Unavailable full text of Gitler--Valencia is the main residual risk; this review cannot exclude all obscure prior equivalents.

## Value — PASS
These are explicit invariants of a natural class of projective coordinate projections of Segre varieties and toric models of partially observed rank-one matrices. The theorem supplies the dimension of every graded piece, not just isolated finite examples or a standard dimension count. It exposes an exact additivity phenomenon when sparse omissions are disjoint and identifies the exceptional square size where nontrivial deletion preserves Gorensteinness. The full family and sharp boundary are useful for testing algebraic-completion invariants and for contrasting intersecting missing patterns.

## Closest literature and limits
The six-cycle case, full Segre case, and general graph normality are prior material. The 2005 multiplicity paper was accessible only by abstract; although it does not advertise Hilbert-series formulas, undiscovered equivalent expressions remain a risk. A 2022 edge-ring paper explicitly quotes the full Segre formula and computes a different nonbipartite graph family with a negative first coefficient; neither generality nor a subtraction-term pattern alone is claimed as new. Later deficient-graph papers about edge *ideals* concern different rings. Scientific proof is self-reviewed; independent audit has not occurred.
