# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. All quantifiers and boundaries are explicit: at least three nonempty named parts, at least \(2(k-1)\) physical vertices, and a nonempty matching of projected part-pairs. A direct matching-to-fixed-tree count feeds a forest-restricted weighted Prüfer identity, whose falling-factorial transform gives the exact probability. The covariance and conditional formula are algebraic consequences. No finite test is mistaken for an infinite proof. A packaged checker verifies 6,312 finite quotient-tree cases by Prüfer enumeration and six cases by a separate physical-edge enumeration; both use exact arithmetic. Proof risks are concentrated in the forest contraction factorization and the factorial convolution, which have explicit derivations in the result.

## Originality

PASS under available literature. The closest primary source is Demange–Strozecki (arXiv:2605.18125, Theorem 11), which gives a fixed-quotient weight and the total number of physical interconnection trees; these were credited and excluded from the novelty claim. Its complete proof of Theorem 11 and relevant weighted-enumeration section were read, and neither states the all-orders disjoint projected-link joint law or the strict covariance. Searches of matching-constrained spanning trees, quotient edge inclusion, weighted Cayley trees and disjoint-edge correlations found no stronger covering theorem. Vondrák's notes give independence for disjoint edges of uniform *unconstrained* Cayley trees, which is not covering and helps distinguish the contribution. An unindexed equivalent treatment of degree-capacity weighted trees is an explicit remaining risk. Own accessible prior research claims and identified published mathematical records were screened for statement-level overlap; no equivalent or stronger claim was found. Search absence alone is not novelty proof.

## Value

PASS. The motivating paper studies interconnection trees for multipartite-network designs. Choosing a uniformly random feasible interconnection is a natural distribution, and knowing joint presence of requested part-to-part links is relevant to sampling and reliability. A full all-orders law and exact conditional probabilities expose a structural positive-dependence effect that is absent from ordinary uniform Cayley trees. This is neither a restatement of the previously known total count nor a small case-by-case number.

## Limits and review status

Only vertex-disjoint **projected** edges in uniformly sampled physical interconnection trees of complete multipartite graphs are included. The manuscript credits the preexisting counting formula, distinguishes index pairs from actual physical edges, and identifies residual bibliographic risk. All checks here are self-review, not independent auditing.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
