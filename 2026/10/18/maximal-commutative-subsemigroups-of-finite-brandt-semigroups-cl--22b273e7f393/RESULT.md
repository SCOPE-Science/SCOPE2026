# Maximal commutative subsemigroups of finite Brandt semigroups: classification and order series

## Finding
For a finite group \(G\) and an integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(B_n(G)\) denote the Brandt semigroup defined below. Put \(\mathcal A(G)\) for the set of inclusion-maximal abelian subgroups of \(G\), and
\[
H_G(z)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A(G)}z^{|A|}.
\]
Every inclusion-maximal commutative subsemigroup of \(B_n(G)\) has exactly one of the following forms:

1. The union of zero with a maximal abelian subgroup in *each* diagonal fiber:
   \[
   \{0\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^n\{(i,a,i):a\in A_i\},\qquad A_i\in\mathcal A(G).
   \]
2. Choose nonempty, disjoint, *ordered* source and target index sets \(S,T\subseteq[n]\), let \(U=[n]\setminus(S\cup T)\), and choose \(A_u\in\mathcal A(G)\) for each \(u\in U\). The subsemigroup is
   \[
   \{0\}\cup\{(i,g,j):i\in S,\ j\in T,\ g\in G\}
   \cup\bigcup_{u\in U}\{(u,a,u):a\in A_u\}.
   \]

These are all distinct. Consequently the *order distribution* of inclusion-maximal commutative subsemigroups is the polynomial
\[
\boxed{C_{n,G}(z)=z\left(H_G(z)^n+\sum_{\substack{s,t\ge1\\s+t\le n}}
\frac{n!}{s!\,t!\,(n-s-t)!}\,z^{|G|st}H_G(z)^{n-s-t}\right).}
\]
The coefficient of \(z^r\) is the number of such subsemigroups having exactly \(r\) elements. In particular, with \(a=|\mathcal A(G)|\), their total number is
\[
\boxed{C_{n,G}(1)=(a+2)^n-2(a+1)^n+2a^n.}
\]
For the nonabelian group \(G=S_3\), \(H_G(z)=3z^2+z^3\); the total numbers for \(n=2,3,4\) are \(18,94,558\), respectively. For the trivial group the total is \(3^n-2^{n+1}+2\).

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(G\) is any nonempty finite group (including the trivial group), \(n\) is an integer at least two, and \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). The semigroup
\[
B_n(G)=\{0\}\cup\{(i,g,j):i,j\in[n],\ g\in G\}
\]
has absorbing zero and multiplication
\[
(i,g,j)(k,h,\ell)=
\begin{cases}(i,gh,\ell),&j=k,\\0,&j\ne k.\end{cases}
\]
A commutative subsemigroup is closed under multiplication and has commuting elements; *maximal* means inclusion-maximal among commutative subsemigroups, **not** maximum cardinality. An abelian subgroup is *inclusion-maximal abelian* if no strictly larger abelian subgroup contains it; it need not be a maximal proper subgroup. If \(U=\varnothing\), the empty product in the series is one.

## Proof
**Step 1 (maximal commuting sets).** Every maximal commutative subsemigroup \(M\) contains \(0\), since zero can be adjoined to any subsemigroup without spoiling commutativity. Moreover, if an element \(x\notin M\) commutes with every element of \(M\), the subsemigroup generated by \(M\cup\{x\}\) is again commutative: all its generators commute. Maximality rules this out. Thus \(M\) is an inclusion-maximal pairwise commuting subset of \(B_n(G)\), and conversely any inclusion-maximal pairwise commuting subset is closed under products (each product commutes with every member), hence is a maximal commutative subsemigroup.

**Step 2 (off-diagonal sources and targets).** Write \(x=(i,g,j)\) with \(i\ne j\). If \(y=(k,h,\ell)\) is another off-diagonal element, the products \(xy\) and \(yx\) are both zero exactly when \(j\ne k\) and \(\ell\ne i\). If precisely one index equality holds, one product is nonzero and the other is zero. If both equalities hold, the products lie in different diagonal fibers indexed by \(i\) and \(j\), so cannot be equal. Therefore two off-diagonal elements commute exactly when both products are zero. Let \(S\) be all first indices and \(T\) all last indices occurring among the off-diagonal elements of \(M\). Then \(S\cap T=\varnothing\). Every element \((i,g,j)\) with \(i\in S\), \(j\in T\) and \(g\in G\) commutes with all of \(M\), except potentially diagonal elements in \(S\cup T\); Step 3 rules these out. By maximality all such elements belong to \(M\). This fully determines the off-diagonal part and its group labels.

**Step 3 (diagonal fibers).** For distinct indices \(i\ne j\), the diagonal elements \((u,a,u)\) and \((i,g,j)\) commute exactly when \(u\notin\{i,j\}\): otherwise one mixed product is nonzero and the other zero. Thus no diagonal fiber indexed by \(S\cup T\) occurs in \(M\). At any remaining index \(u\in U\), the elements \((u,a,u)\) commute with every off-diagonal member and with diagonal elements at other indices, while among themselves they commute precisely when their group labels commute. A maximal pairwise commuting subset of a finite group contains its identity, inverses and products (all commute with the subset); therefore it is an inclusion-maximal abelian subgroup \(A_u\). If there are no off-diagonal elements, the same reasoning forces such a subgroup at all \(n\) indices.

**Step 4 (sufficiency and uniqueness).** In each displayed family, every pair of off-diagonal elements has both products zero because \(S\cap T=\varnothing\). They have zero mixed products with diagonal fibers in \(U\); different diagonal fibers have zero products; each \(A_u\) is a commutative subgroup. Hence the displayed sets are closed commutative subsemigroups. No additional diagonal element can be adjoined: at a forbidden index it conflicts with an off-diagonal member, and at an allowed index maximality of \(A_u\) forbids it. Any off-diagonal element commuting with the whole displayed subsemigroup must avoid \(U\) at both endpoints, avoid \(T\) as a source, and avoid \(S\) as a target. Its source must therefore lie in \(S\) and its target in \(T\); all these elements were included. The all-diagonal family excludes every off-diagonal element by the diagonal group identities. Thus each displayed set is maximal. Nonempty \(S,T\) are recoverable uniquely from the off-diagonal elements, and all \(A_u\) are recoverable from the diagonal fibers, proving uniqueness.

**Step 5 (order series).** For the all-diagonal family, independently selecting the maximal abelian group fibers gives weight \(zH_G(z)^n\), including one factor \(z\) for the zero element. For ordered, nonempty disjoint \(S,T\) of sizes \(s,t\), the number of such assignments is \(n!/(s!t!(n-s-t)!)\), and there are \(|G|st\) off-diagonal elements. The \(n-s-t\) remaining fibers contribute \(H_G(z)^{n-s-t}\). Summation gives \(C_{n,G}(z)\). Setting \(z=1\), the nonempty-disjoint ordered-pair count weighted by \(a^{|U|}\) is \((a+2)^n-2(a+1)^n+a^n\) by inclusion-exclusion on three labels (unused, source, target). Adding the all-diagonal count \(a^n\) proves the final expression. \(\square\)

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_maximal_commuting.py` constructs actual Brandt multiplication tables, compares actual maximal commuting subsets (enumerated by a bitset Bron--Kerbosch routine) to the classification, checks multiplication closure, and compares all size multiplicities and the total-count expression. The checked finite groups are the trivial group, \(C_2\), \(C_3\), \(S_3\), and the order-eight dihedral group, with indices \(n\in\{2,3,4\}\) where computationally feasible. All tested cases pass. These exhaustive finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-group proof above. Run with `python3 verify_maximal_commuting.py` using Python 3 standard library only.

## Relationship to prior work
Margolis, Rhodes and Silva (2017) analyze subsemigroup lattices, subsemigroup complexes, and facets for the aperiodic Brandt semigroup. Kumar, Dalal and Pandey (2020), especially Lemma 3.10 and Theorem 3.14, establish source-target partition arguments and maximum clique size in the aperiodic case. These antecedents contain the central off-diagonal obstruction used here, and that obstruction itself is not proposed as new. The present result instead classifies **all inclusion-maximal** commuting subsemigroups for **every finite labeling group**, including nonabelian ones, and gives the full order-refined polynomial in the sizes of maximal abelian subgroups of that group. Paulista (2024) treats commuting subsemigroups of **completely simple** Rees matrix semigroups without zero; her maximum-order result concerns a different semigroup class and does not determine these Brandt (completely \(0\)-simple inverse) distributions. The related Dowling--Rhodes work of Margolis, Rhodes and Silva (2017) concerns lattices and matroids of group-labelled combinatorial structures, not maximal commuting subsemigroups.

## Limitations
The formula reduces the numerical distribution for a specified group to the finite-group invariant \(H_G(z)\); it does not furnish a closed universal formula for maximal abelian subgroups of arbitrary finite groups. The classification treats finite groups and \(n\ge2\), not infinite groups or arbitrary completely \(0\)-simple semigroups. Equivalents in unindexed semigroup literature may exist.
