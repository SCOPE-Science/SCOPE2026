# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Exact finite-state stationary weights follow from the known reversible product law. The sum over the number of negative sites yields the displayed partition coefficient; adjacent-summand ratios have a unique bulk mode, yielding the constant \(1/(\kappa\sqrt{r(1+r)})\) in the first-order logarithmic deficit. A global geometric bound, including the otherwise exceptional zero-debt endpoint under \(a_N=o(N)\), justifies the partition normalization, distributional limits, and mean convergence. The attached independent finite program completed 131 tests; finite tests are supportive rather than proofs of the limits.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest source is Sakagawa (arXiv:2610.00901v1), whose stationary product law and two-dimensional local limit theorem are prior mathematical contributions. The source's fixed-transfer-rate results and coarse total-debt saturation statement do not give the triangular \(N\)-dependent critical-bias bank-slack crossover. The fixed-balanced endpoint can be derived from existing local-limit ingredients with further arguments and is not treated as a standalone novelty claim. Lanchier–Reed, Cao–Motsch, and Cao–Reed address neighboring models and wealth marginals or mean-field dynamics. Search of published mathematical records found no matching three-regime claim, but this is not a uniqueness proof. A broader unlocated conditional-allocation theorem remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The total unused credit is a motivated microscopic observable in a capped collective-bank model. Its scale changes from the same order as typical individual wealth to a microscopic geometric count; the critical transfer-bias window and the intermediate law make the phase boundary quantitatively explicit. These distinctions are not determined by the macroscopic one-site wealth law.

## Scope and limitations

The assumptions require \(g_{-,N}\ge g_{+,N}>0\), constant positive/nonpositive transfer probabilities, symmetric connected interaction, \(a_N=o(N)\), and positive limiting scaled wealth and bank capacity. No general transfer function, nonlinear network effect, transient relaxation, or independent originality validation is claimed.
