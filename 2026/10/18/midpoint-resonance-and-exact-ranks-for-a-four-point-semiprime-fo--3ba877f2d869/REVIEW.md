# Scientific review: Midpoint resonance and exact ranks for a four-point semiprime Fourier aperture

## Correctness — PASS
For any odd prime \(p\), two Fourier rows belonging to the same projected residue but opposite binary parity isolate the two frequency-parity blocks. Three-versus-one parity partners are nonsingular by the explicit three-node Vandermonde determinant. Homogeneous partners have exactly rank three. For two-plus-two partners, elimination leaves a two-by-two matrix whose determinant is a product of three explicit cyclotomic differences. The first two factors never vanish; the third vanishes exactly when the two pair sums agree modulo \(p\). The residual rank is one, not zero, establishing exact rank three. Each sum has \((p-1)/2\) distinct unordered pairs, giving the claimed count. Every balanced frequency set has no proportional rows or columns: two columns with different parities are distinguished by the repeated-coordinate rows, while those of the same parity have distinct projected frequencies; analogous row-pair distinctions hold. Hence all \(p(p-1)^2/4\) balanced singular partners lack proportional rows or columns. Exact cyclotomic determinants were checked for every four-frequency subset at \(p=3,5,7,11,13\), with outputs 3, 30, 133, 935, 1898 singular partners and 3, 20, 63, 275, 468 nonproportional singular partners. The finite check does not substitute for the proof for arbitrary primes.

## Originality — PASS (residual literature risk)
Caragea–Lee (arXiv:2409.09793) prove nonsingularity of two- and three-point **principal** minors for squarefree moduli, not the arbitrary four-frequency classification here. Loukaki (DOI 10.1112/blms.70192) proves nonsingularity in the matched parity-count cases of order \(2p\), including the three-versus-one case here, but leaves the two-versus-two resonance undecided. Delvaux–Van Barel (TW477; DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2007.05.009) treat broader Kronecker rank-deficiency constructions and Hamming-number patterns, without this centered-aperture distribution. Published results on four-point indicators on cyclic semiprimes and phase-only four-point rectangles restrict the coefficients and do not characterize arbitrary Fourier-minor ranks. Full text of the closest primary articles and those two published related results was materially inspected; relevant semantic searches did not identify a matching midpoint test or exact count. Unrecognized equivalent formulations, notably sparse interpolation literature, cannot be ruled out by search alone.

## Value — PASS
The natural object is a four-sample aperture on a cyclic semiprime, and the natural invariant is the rank of all its square Fourier interpolation submatrices. It is a complete exact classification uniform across all odd primes, with a nontrivial resonant two-plus-two family and a sharp count \(p(p-1)^2/4\) of singular minors having no proportional rows or columns. The latter shows that rank loss can be caused by a global pair-midpoint relation rather than trivially duplicate rows/columns. This yields exact admissibility and obstruction counts for four-frequency interpolation on these supports. The result is restricted to a motivated symmetry class, not an arbitrary slice selected for its numerical answer.
