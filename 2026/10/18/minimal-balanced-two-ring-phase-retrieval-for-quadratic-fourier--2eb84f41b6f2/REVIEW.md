# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof derives the exact degree-two four-point angular discrete Fourier decomposition. For two radii, the linear factors have determinant \(rs(s^2-r^2)\); for the quadratic cross term, the real angular determinant has absolute value \(|\sin(2(\psi-\phi))|\). Under the stated conditions the Hermitian measurement kernel is precisely the full-rank line \(\mathbb R\operatorname{diag}(r^2s^2,-(r^2+s^2),1)\). Since the difference of two rank-one coefficient matrices has rank at most two, phase uniqueness follows. Explicit pairs disprove injectivity whenever either condition fails. The seven-sample lower bound follows from real Hermitian dimension nine, an odd-determinant argument on a two-plane in the kernel, and indefinite rank-two decomposition. The normalized two-row Gram matrix proves the exact conditioning claim. The standalone numerical/exact script passes, but infinite claims rest on analytic arguments.

## Originality — PASS, bounded assessment
The nearest explicit eight-point polynomial theorem is Pohl–Li–Boche (2017), Theorem 7: its degree-two design uses five points on one circle and three on another, not balanced four-plus-four squares; it supplies no necessary-and-sufficient relative-angle criterion. Bodmann–Hammen (2013) prove phase retrieval via two intersecting circles, not this coaxial design. General minimal-sample bounds and phase-lifting principles were already known; neither eight as an abstract number nor the Hermitian-lifting method is claimed to be new. The new claim is the exact, reproducibly dimensioned, balanced concentric sampling classification and its sharp normalized angular conditioning. Searches of mathematical research records under alternative descriptions (two rings, four quadrature points per ring, Hermitian rank-two obstruction, eight polynomial samples) gave no covering statement. The accessible source comparison is not a proof of historical priority: the full text of the 2026 STFT motivating paper was inaccessible, and an unindexed concentric-ring construction could exist.

## Value — PASS
Balanced four-point ring sampling admits especially simple discrete Fourier demixing: each ring uses the same four-angle acquisition protocol, and the exact forbidden angle set and optimal \(\pi/4\) stagger are operational design consequences. The proof identifies the only invisible Hermitian direction, explaining why a seemingly underdetermined eight-versus-nine lift can still yield universal phase retrieval. The full equivalence, explicit obstructions, minimum sample count, and exactly conditioned quadratic-product block together provide a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary finite enumeration.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem concerns all degree-at-most-two complex polynomials, exact pointwise intensity measurements, and two regularly spaced four-point rings. The singular values do not imply global nonlinear noise robustness. Relevant older geometric sampling theorems were compared by sample configurations, and residual priority risk from unavailable source text persists.
