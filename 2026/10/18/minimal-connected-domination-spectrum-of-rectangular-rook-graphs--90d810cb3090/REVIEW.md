# Scientific review: minimal connected domination on rook graphs

**Correctness — PASS.** The connected-dominating condition is exactly connectedness of the selected edge-incidence graph plus coverage of every row or every column. Cycles permit deletion; a bipartite tree meeting both full sides permits a leaf deletion. On a tree covering only one whole side, inclusion-minimality is precisely absence of a leaf on the opposite selected side. Weighted bipartite Prüfer enumeration then counts all these structures without overlap. All nine directly simulated boards agree coefficientwise with the formula over 1,413,959 nonempty subsets. This checks the formulation and boundary cases but does not substitute finite computation for the proof.

**Originality — PASS subject to residual bibliographic risk.** The 2016 Lokshtanov–Pilipczuk–Saurabh paper and 2022 ESA paper concern general enumeration of minimal connected dominating sets, but do not provide a closed rook-specific counting polynomial. Mertens 2024 enumerates all dominating rook placements, and OEIS A289196/A360876 enumerate connected (not inclusion-minimal) dominating configurations. Searches for equivalent connected edge-domination descriptions and the accessible mathematical-record comparisons identified no covering theorem. An unindexed exact count or terminology change could still cover the claim; absence of search hits is not proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The result is a full natural all-size classification rather than a new name for a minimum parameter. It provides a closed labeled enumeration for every board shape, yields both extremal multiplicities, and explains a sharp, arbitrarily long gap in feasible sizes when the rectangle is sufficiently elongated. These gap and exact multiplicity phenomena have direct structural content for domination enumeration.

**Scientific limits and closest literature.** The bipartite Prüfer count is pre-existing mathematics. The new claim lies in its sharp incidence-tree characterization and exact minimal connected-domination enumerator. General enumeration algorithms and broader all-dominating-set formulas do not by themselves imply this more selective enumerator. Equivalent literature outside the checked sources remains possible.
