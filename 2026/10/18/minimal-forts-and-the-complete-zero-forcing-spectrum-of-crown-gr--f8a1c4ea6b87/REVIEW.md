# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For \(n\ge3\), the external-neighbor counts are \(|Y|-\mathbf1_{i\in Y}\) and \(|X|-\mathbf1_{j\in X}\). These force each inclusion-minimal fort to be a pure triple or a matched pair of two deleted edges. The classical fort–forcing transversal equivalence follows directly by considering stalled white sets. Choosing omitted vertices in the two bipartition classes gives the five-term polynomial and exact multiplicities. Averaging all triple inequalities proves the fractional lower bound; uniform one-third weights attain it. Minimal forts have at least three vertices, and explicit disjoint triples plus a matched four-set attain the packing bound. Boundary cases \(n=3\), \(n=4\), the uniqueness threshold and all residue classes are covered. The included adjacency-based exhaustive tests are separate from the infinite proof.

Existing records on forts of complete multipartite graphs treat a different graph class. An unindexed equivalent theorem under tensor-product or bipartite-cover terminology remains possible; search noncoverage is not an independent priority certificate.

## Value — PASS

This determines the full fort obstruction hypergraph and the complete zero forcing set distribution, rather than a single extremal number, for a canonical distance-transitive bipartite graph family. It simultaneously settles a natural integer packing and its fractional transversal relaxation, proves uniqueness of the fractional optimum above the smallest case, and shows that the zero-forcing/fractional-zero-forcing integrality ratio approaches three. These connect exact forcing feasibility, polynomial enumeration and LP geometry and give natural reusable test cases for fort-based optimization.
