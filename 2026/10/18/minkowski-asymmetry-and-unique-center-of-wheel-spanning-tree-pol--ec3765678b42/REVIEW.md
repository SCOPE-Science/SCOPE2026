# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For each simple wheel with at least four rim vertices, the rim-cycle and hub-triangle support ranges force \(s\ge2n/(n-1)\). The proposed center is shown globally feasible by applying the full spanning-tree polytope inequalities to \((\lambda c-\chi_T)/s\) for each spanning tree, classifying induced rim subgraphs by runs. Hub-three-rim support equalities prove every minimizer equals the displayed center, without assuming group invariance. The exact-integer verifier passed 3,532 tree vertices and 1,390,868 vertex-set inequalities for sizes four through eight. The proof, not this finite sample, establishes the arbitrary-size theorem.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk

Backman--Liu (arXiv:2309.10229, Theorem 3.1, Definition 2.2 and Remark 3.9) proves regular unimodular triangulations of matroid base polytopes. Chaourar (arXiv:1903.07292, Theorems 2.1/2.3 and section 3) characterizes their spanning-tree facets. Neither examined work solves reflected-containment optimization or identifies the wheel-specific unique Minkowski center. Searches under the alternative terminology of graphic matroids, wheel matroids, asymmetry, symmetry, and reflection did not identify a stronger exact result. An earlier general theorem or planar-duality reformulation could cover the formula; the literature assessment is not a claim of exhaustive priority certification.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives a complete, exact affine-invariant classification of a natural infinite graph-polytope family and supplies rigidity of the minimizing center, which is not implied by general facet descriptions. Its proof isolates the role of hub triangles, the rim cycle and induced runs, producing a reusable structural containment argument. The claim does not purport to classify all graphic matroid polytopes.
