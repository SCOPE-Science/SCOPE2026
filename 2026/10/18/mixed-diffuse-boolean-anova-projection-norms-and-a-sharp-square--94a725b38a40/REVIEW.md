# Review of mixed diffuse–Boolean ANOVA projection norms

**Correctness: PASS.** Independent-coordinate ANOVA operators yield a generating kernel polynomial. Singular coordinate faces are mutually singular, so their total variations add, establishing the exact upper norm. Positive-measure rare-event embeddings attain the bound in the limit. The normal/Hermite crossover follows from Newton identities, the central limit theorem, and uniform second-moment bounds. A separate alternating-binomial leading-term argument proves the diffuse-dominated limit. Finite exact-rational checks exercise 440 parameter families; finite tests are supplemental, not proofs.

**Originality: PASS within examined literature.** The classical ANOVA expansion of Hegland–Pestov is a prerequisite but establishes mean-square smoothness-based approximation rather than sharp mixed-coordinate endpoint norms. Griebel–Kuo–Sloan's smoothing theorems concern differentiability gains, and Ginsbourger and collaborators' kernel decompositions concern random-field covariance structure. General Bernoulli rare-vertex projection results overlap with the finite-atom approximation method but do not state or imply the mixed atomless/Boolean Hermite-shift crossover with its three asymptotic regimes. Direct searches for aliases (Hoeffding decomposition, Sobol–ANOVA projection, low-degree Walsh truncation, endpoint Lebesgue constants) did not expose a dominating theorem. This is evidence of limited checked novelty, not a proof of uniqueness.

**Value: PASS.** Truncated ANOVA approximants are widely used for high-dimensional approximation and sensitivity analysis with heterogeneous predictor types. The exact sharp amplification determines how bounded rough inputs can be magnified, and the \(\sqrt n\) threshold gives a natural, explicit stability transition between continuous and binary features. The shifted Hermite profile is not a textbook one-line projection estimate; it links singular measure decomposition with joint probabilistic scaling.

**Risks and limitations.** Mixed-coordinate weights other than uniform intervals/fair signs, dependence, and growing interaction order are not covered. A related finite-dimensional rare-vertex theorem is prior machinery and should not be recast as new. An older treatment phrased using measure-valued tensor kernels may overlap, and historical priority remains unverified. Independent audit has not been performed.
