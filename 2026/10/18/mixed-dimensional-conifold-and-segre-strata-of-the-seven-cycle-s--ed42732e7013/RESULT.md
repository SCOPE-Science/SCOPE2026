# Mixed-dimensional conifold and Segre strata of the seven-cycle stable-set toric variety

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, let \(C_7\) be the cycle with vertices \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), and let \(P=\operatorname{STAB}(C_7)\subset\mathbb R^7\) in its standard lattice. Let \(X_P\) be the normal projective toric sevenfold corresponding to the normal fan of \(P\). Its reduced singular locus has exactly fourteen irreducible components and is **not pure-dimensional**. Seven are torus-invariant fourfolds, each the toric variety associated with \(\operatorname{STAB}(P_4)\); the other seven are torus-invariant threefolds isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^3_k\).

The generic transverse singularities along the fourfolds are ordinary threefold conifolds \(uv-wz=0\), of multiplicity \(2\). Along the threefolds they are the rank-at-most-one \(2\times3\) determinantal cones, of multiplicity \(3\). Exactly \(322\) torus orbits are singular, with orbit dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4\) occurring \(28,105,126,56,7\) times. All simplicial normal cones are unimodular, so the singular support equals the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus.

## Assumptions and scope
All indices are modulo \(7\). Denote the standard basis of the character lattice \(M=\mathbb Z^7\) by \(e_i\). A stable set contains no adjacent cycle vertices. The polytope is the convex hull of the \(29\) incidence vectors of stable sets, in the full lattice \(M\) (the empty and singleton stable sets show that no smaller lattice is intended).

An exact inequality representation is
\[
P=\{x\in\mathbb R^7:x_i\ge0,\ x_i+x_{i+1}\le1\ (i\in\mathbb Z/7),\ \sum_{i=0}^6x_i\le3\}.
\]
The \(15\) primitive outward normals in \(N=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb Z)\) are
\(u_i=-e_i\), \(v_i=e_i+e_{i+1}\), and \(w=\sum_i e_i\). These are normals in the dual lattice: the reuse of basis notation identifies the standard coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\), not their geometric roles.

Define \(A_i=\{x\in P:x_i=1\}\). Define \(B_i\) to be the face on which
\[
x_i=0,\qquad x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}=x_{i+3}+x_{i+4}=x_{i+5}+x_{i+6}=1.
\]
Each \(A_i\) is four-dimensional with \(8\) vertices. Each \(B_i\) is a three-dimensional lattice-unimodular simplex with \(4\) vertices. The stated components are precisely the orbit closures \(V(A_i)\) and \(V(B_i)\) in the fan-face correspondence.

## Proof
**Exact polytope and face poset.** Nonnegativity and edge inequalities bound the displayed rational polyhedron. The standalone checker visits every one of the \(\binom{15}{7}=6435\) possible choices of seven supporting hyperplanes, solves every nonsingular intersection over \(\mathbb Q\), and retains feasible intersections. There are exactly \(29\) and all are the stable-set incidence vectors. Each of the \(15\) hyperplanes contains points of affine span six, so none is redundant. Every nonempty face is an intersection of these facets; the bitset closure under their intersections gives exactly \(1151\) nonempty faces, including the whole polytope.

**Singular fan cones.** For every nonempty face \(F\), let \(I(F)\) be its containing facets, and let \(\sigma_F\subset N_{\mathbb R}\) be generated by their primitive normals. This cone has rank \(7-\dim F\). A toric orbit is smooth precisely when its cone is generated by part of a lattice basis; equivalently its active normals are linearly independent and the gcd of their maximal integral minors is \(1\). The exact finite certificate checks this at *every* nonempty face, including rank-deficient and non-unimodular cases. The nonsmooth-orbit counts by face dimension \(0\) through \(4\) are \(28,105,126,56,7\); every other face cone is smooth. All cones having an independent ray set are unimodular.

The inclusion-maximal singular faces are exactly the \(14\) faces \(A_i\) and \(B_i\). Indeed, the complete finite face list gives precisely \(7\) maximal singular faces of dimension \(4\) with \(4\) active facets and \(7\) of dimension \(3\) with \(5\) active facets; direct mask comparisons identify these with the displayed two cyclic families. Singular toric strata specialize precisely to orbits of larger cones, hence their reduced union has these \(14\) orbit closures as its irreducible components. Because every nonsimplicial cone is non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and all simplicial cones are smooth, the same union is the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus.

**The first family.** Fixing \(x_i=1\) forces \(x_{i-1}=x_{i+1}=0\); the remaining four vertices of \(C_7\) form a path \(P_4\). Hence the face polytope \(A_i\) is \(\operatorname{STAB}(P_4)\), with its induced full lattice, so \(V(A_i)\) is its normal-fan toric fourfold. Its rank-three normal cone has four rays
\[
u_{i-1},\quad u_{i+1},\quad v_{i-1},\quad v_i,
\]
with primitive circuit \(u_{i-1}+v_{i-1}=u_{i+1}+v_i\). Each three rays form a basis of the saturated rank-three cone lattice (gcd of their maximal minors is one). The dual semigroup is generated by four monomials with one quadratic relation, giving \(k[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\). This is the transverse conifold with multiplicity \(2\).

**The second family.** For fixed \(i\), the four stable sets choosing one endpoint from each of the three matched pairs while avoiding adjacency are affinely independent and have difference-lattice index one. Thus \(B_i\) is a unimodular tetrahedron and \(V(B_i)\cong\mathbb P^3_k\). Its rank-four normal cone has the five primitive rays
\[
u_i,\quad v_{i+1},\quad v_{i+3},\quad v_{i+5},\quad w,
\]
with circuit \(v_{i+1}+v_{i+3}+v_{i+5}=w+u_i\). Each four rays form a basis of the saturated rank-four cone lattice. The standard dual semigroup for this \((3,2)\)-circuit has six generators and the three \(2\times2\) minors of a \(2\times3\) matrix as relations. The transverse variety is the affine rank-one matrix cone; its projectivization is \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\) embedded by \(\mathcal O(1,1)\), of degree \(3\). Its vertex therefore has multiplicity \(3\).

All local descriptions hold over any algebraically closed field: they are integral, saturated toric-semigroup identifications, not characteristic-zero Jacobian extrapolations.

## Verification
The included `verify.py` requires Python \(3.10\) or later and SymPy \(1.10\) or later. Run `python verify.py` from the directory containing the extracted package. It performs exact rational intersection tests for the whole inequality system, computes all nonempty faces as facet intersections, tests cone ranks and all needed integral gcds of maximal minors, verifies both maximal-singular-face families, tests the unimodular threefold faces, and checks every primitive local circuit. A successful run ends with `VERIFY_OK`. The packaged checker has been executed from the actual embedded source and returned this output.

## Relationship to prior work
Euler, Jünger and Reinelt's \(1987\) paper discusses the odd-cycle facet inequalities of stable-set polytopes (following Padberg). Matsuda, Ohsugi and Shibata's preprint first available in \(2016\), later published in \(2019\), treats normality, generators and Gröbner bases of stable-set toric rings. Those results supply the natural polyhedral and toric context but do not state the fourteen-component normal-fan singular classification or either transverse model. The normal-fan toric variety must be distinguished from arbitrary embeddings or ideals of stable-set polytopes. Full-text examination of the Matsuda–Ohsugi–Shibata treatment and targeted searches did not reveal the same singular-locus theorem; absence from those sources is not proof of global uniqueness.

## Limitations
This is an exact structural result for the particular seven-cycle \(C_7\); it is not a uniform classification of all odd cycles. It describes the **reduced** singular support and generic transverse singularities, not the complete scheme structure of the singular locus or every local germ at intersection strata. A previously unindexed equivalent computation remains an originality risk. The arithmetic checker is a complete finite certificate for the stated seven-dimensional fan, not a symbolic theorem for arbitrary cycle length. Independent review has not been performed.
