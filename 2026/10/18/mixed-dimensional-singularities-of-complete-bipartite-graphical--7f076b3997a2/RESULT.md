# Mixed-dimensional singularities of complete-bipartite graphical-zonotope toric varieties

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge3\) and every algebraically closed field \(k\), let \(G=K_{2,m}\) have distinguished vertices \(a,b\) and other vertices \(1,\ldots,m\). Let \(Z_G\) be its graphical zonotope (the Minkowski sum of the segments \([0,e_u-e_v]\) for its edges) in the root lattice, and let \(X_m\) be the normal projective toric variety of its normal fan in the dual lattice. Then \(\dim X_m=m+1\), and its **complete reduced singular locus** has exactly
\[
2^{m-1}\binom m2+2^m
=2^{m-2}(m(m-1)+4)
\]
irreducible components of two different dimensions:

* Exactly \(2^{m-1}\binom m2\) components are torus orbit closures isomorphic to \((\mathbb P_k^1)^{m-2}\). Each has dimension \(m-2\), codimension three in \(X_m\), and generic transverse affine singularity \(\operatorname{Spec}k[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\).
* Exactly \(2^m\) further components are **isolated singular points**. At each of these points the full affine toric chart is the \((m+1)\)-dimensional affine cone over the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P_k^1\times\mathbb P_k^{m-1}\): equivalently, the rank-at-most-one locus of a \(2\times m\) matrix. Its local Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is \(m\). These points do not lie on the positive-dimensional singular components.

In particular the reduced singular locus is not pure dimensional for any \(m\ge3\), and its underlying closed set equals the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus. For \(m=3\), the components are twelve copies of \(\mathbb P^1\) and eight isolated points; for \(m=4\), they are forty-eight copies of \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) and sixteen isolated points.

## Assumptions and scope
The characteristic of \(k\) is arbitrary. The variety is the toric variety of the **normal fan of the graphical zonotope**, not the affine toric edge-ring variety of the graph. The cocharacter lattice is \(N=\mathbb Z^{m+2}/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\); its dual is \(M=\{(z_v)\in\mathbb Z^{m+2}:\sum_v z_v=0\}\). The argument uses the normal fan of \(Z_G\), whose cones are the sign regions of the graphical arrangement \(t_u=t_v\) for every edge. Generic transverse type refers to the toric germ at the dense orbit of an irreducible singular component. The excluded four-cycle case \(m=2\) has a different component count and is not subsumed by the formulas above.

## Proof
**1. Cones and smoothness.** A cone of the graphical-arrangement fan is specified by the zero/nonzero signs of the differences \(t_u-t_v\) over edges \(uv\). Contract the connected components of the graph formed by zero edges. Because the graph is connected, if there are \(q\) contracted blocks the cone has dimension \(q-1\). Between distinct blocks the strict signs induce an acyclic orientation; its transitive closure is a partial order. A facet inequality of the cone is precisely a cover relation of this order (parallel edge inequalities are counted only once). Its primitive facet normal is the difference of the two corresponding block characters.

The undirected cover graph is connected. If it is a tree, its \(q-1\) primitive difference vectors form an integral basis of the character lattice of the contracted-block quotient: this follows by successively deleting leaves of the tree. The cone is then unimodular and smooth. If the cover graph has a cycle, it has more than \(q-1\) facet inequalities, so the cone is nonsimplicial and singular. This proves, for every cone of this fan, that *smooth* and *simplicial* are equivalent.

**2. Exhaustion of singular cones.** A connected zero-edge block containing neither \(a\) nor \(b\) consists of one leaf vertex. If \(a\) and \(b\) lie in the same zero block, the quotient graph is a star, whose oriented cover graph is a tree; the cone is smooth. Otherwise the zero blocks are \(A\) containing \(a\), \(B\) containing \(b\), and \(t\) singleton leaves. Their quotient graph has edges from each singleton leaf to both \(A,B\), together with the edge \(AB\) if and only if at least one other leaf was contracted into \(A\) or \(B\). Multiple edges after contraction have no effect on the arrangement.

A Hasse diagram cannot contain an undirected triangle (one edge would be transitively redundant). Thus every cycle in the quotient cover graph is a four-cycle \(A-i-B-j-A\). If \(A\) and \(B\) are comparable, reverse all inequalities if necessary to write \(A<B\). Such a four-cycle exists exactly when at least two singleton leaves \(i,j\) satisfy \(A<i,j<B\). All other singleton leaves lie below \(A\), between \(A,B\), or above \(B\). By replacing the inequalities for all leaves other than \(i,j\) with suitable equalities to \(A\) or \(B\), one obtains a face that has exactly four blocks \(A',i,j,B'\), ordered \(A'<i,j<B'\). This is a singular three-dimensional cone. Conversely each such four-block diamond is singular, since its four cover edges form a cycle.

If \(A,B\) are incomparable, there can be no contracted extra leaf: such a leaf would create a direct \(AB\) edge and force comparability. Consequently all \(m\) leaves are singleton blocks, and each leaf is either strictly above both centers or strictly below both centers. There are exactly \(2^m\) such full-dimensional sign cones. Their cover graphs have \(2m>m+1\) edges, so they are singular. Every proper face merges a center with a leaf or ultimately merges the centers; this forces the two centers comparable (or identical), with no remaining leaf strictly between them. It is therefore smooth by the preceding criterion. These full-dimensional cones are minimal singular cones under face inclusion.

**3. Counting minimal cones.** A minimal singular cone of the comparable kind has four blocks \(A',i,j,B'\): choose the unordered pair of middle leaves in \(\binom m2\) ways; assign each of the other \(m-2\) labeled leaves to either center block in \(2^{m-2}\) ways; choose which center block is below the other in two ways. This gives exactly \(2^{m-1}\binom m2\) distinct minimal singular cones of dimension three. Together with the \(2^m\) incomparable full-dimensional cones from Step 2, they exhaust the minimal singular cones. For a normal toric variety, its reduced singular locus is the union of the orbit closures of the cones minimal among the singular cones, with different minimal cones furnishing different irreducible components. The component counts follow.

**4. Geometry of positive-dimensional components.** Fix a three-dimensional minimal cone. In coordinates with \(A'=0\), its inequalities are \(0\le u,v\le w\). Its primitive rays are \((0,0,1),(1,0,1),(0,1,1),(1,1,1)\), and the dual cone has primitive generators \(r_1=(1,0,0),\ r_2=(0,1,0),\ r_3=(-1,0,1),\ r_4=(0,-1,1)\). They form its full Hilbert basis: a dual lattice point \((x,y,z)\) satisfies \(z\ge0\), \(x+z\ge0\), \(y+z\ge0\), and \(x+y+z\ge0\); using \(\beta=\max(0,-x)\) and \(\delta=\max(0,-y)\), one has \(\beta+\delta\le z\) and can express the point as a nonnegative integer combination of the four generators by adding \(r_1+r_3\) for the remaining third coordinate. Their primitive relation is \(r_1+r_3=r_2+r_4\), so the dual semigroup ring has the single binomial relation \(uv=wz\), giving the ordinary conifold. For its toric orbit closure, the star of the cone is the arrangement fan cut out by edges whose endpoints were equated inside \(A'\) or \(B'\). Those contracted edges form two disjoint stars, containing altogether \(m-2\) edges and no cycle. Their differences form an integral coordinate basis, so this star fan is the product of \(m-2\) fans of \(\mathbb P^1\). Thus the orbit closure is \((\mathbb P^1)^{m-2}\). The dense-orbit transverse germ has the asserted conifold equation.

**5. Geometry of isolated components.** At a full-dimensional incomparable-center cone, choose \(t_a=0\) and \(s=t_b-t_a\). For a leaf above both centers set \(z_i=t_i\); for a leaf below both set \(z_i=s-t_i\). This is an integral unimodular coordinate change for every up/down assignment, and it puts the cone into the form \(z_i\ge0\) and \(z_i-s\ge0\) for all \(1\le i\le m\). Its dual affine semigroup is generated by \(e_i\) and \(e_i-e_s\) for all \(i\). These form a Hilbert basis: a dual-cone lattice point with coordinates \( (c_1,\ldots,c_m,d) \) satisfies \(c_i\ge0\) and \(-\sum_i c_i\le d\le0\). Choose integers \(0\le\beta_i\le c_i\) with \(\sum_i\beta_i=-d\); the point is then \(\sum_i(c_i-\beta_i)e_i+\sum_i\beta_i(e_i-e_s)\). The sole minimal relations are the \(2\times2\) minors of the matrix whose two rows correspond to the two types of generator. Hence the chart is the rank-one \(2\times m\) determinantal cone, i.e. the affine Segre cone. Its singular locus is its vertex alone for \(m\ge2\), and the multiplicity at that vertex is the Segre degree \(\binom m1=m\). This also independently shows that none of the isolated points lies on a positive-dimensional singular component.

**6. Non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus.** An affine toric chart is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial exactly for a simplicial cone. Step 1 shows that here every simplicial cone is actually unimodular; thus a torus orbit is smooth exactly when it is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. Passing to orbit closures gives the claimed equality of underlying closed loci.

## Verification
The included standalone `verify_k2m.py` enumerates all weak orderings of graph vertices for \(m=2,3,4,5\), deduplicates their edge-sign vectors, contracts equality blocks, computes the transitive reduction by graph reachability, and enumerates all nonsmooth cones minimal under face inclusion. It uses exact integers only. The counts of minimal singular cones by dimension are \((3:6)\) for \(m=2\), \((3:12,4:8)\) for \(m=3\), \((3:48,5:16)\) for \(m=4\), and \((3:160,6:32)\) for \(m=5\). The test is finite corroboration; the all-parameter classification and local algebra statements follow from Steps 1–6, not from extrapolation. Run the script with `python verify_k2m.py` using Python 3 and no third-party packages.

## Relationship to prior work
Postnikov, Reiner, and Williams describe graphical-zonotope fans using acyclic orientations and their posets and characterize which graphical zonotopes are globally simple (their Proposition 5.2). That result detects singularity somewhere for nontrivial bipartite examples but does not give this mixed-dimensional singular-locus decomposition or the local conifold and Segre charts. Grujić's graphical-zonotope face-enumeration theorem determines face counts from graph contractions; those counts do not identify which torus orbits are singular or their local analytic geometry. The present statement specializes these classical combinatorial tools to an all-parameter geometric classification for the normal-fan toric variety of \(K_{2,m}\). The graph edge-ring toric variety is a different construction and is not being treated here.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the reduced singular support, not the full scheme structure of the singular locus or all scheme-theoretic intersections between its components. The positive-dimensional components need not be pairwise disjoint. No independent audit has been performed. A differently formulated or unindexed earlier calculation may provide equivalent coverage; the literature comparison is evidence of no located direct covering result, not a uniqueness guarantee.
