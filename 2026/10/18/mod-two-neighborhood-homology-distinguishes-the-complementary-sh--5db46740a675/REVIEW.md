# Review of the mod-two neighborhood-homology claim

## Correctness — PASS
The hypotheses specify both sixteen-vertex adjacency relations, simple complements, open neighborhoods, augmented boundary conventions, and \(\mathbb F_2\) coefficients. Every simplex belongs to one of sixteen nine-element open neighborhoods; both programs exhaust this finite domain and reconstruct all face vectors and boundary matrices. Exact ranks in all degrees give the reduced homology by rank-nullity. An independent transpose/row computation in an isomorphic alternate Shrikhande presentation corroborates the column computation. Checks involve complete enumeration and exact integer bit operations, not floating point or sampling; no integral or homotopy claim follows.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit risk
Originality is asserted only for the paired *numerical homology classification* of \(\mathcal N(\overline S)\) and \(\mathcal N(\overline R)\). Matoušek–Ziegler and Kahle address neighborhood complexes generally; Matsushita–Wakatsuki's Alexander-dual theorem identifies the construction but does not compute these ranks. Cioabă–Guo–Ji–Mim discuss the same exceptional strongly regular pair in clique-complex cohomology and spectral terms, not these complementary neighborhood complexes. The original models have well-known different invariants; this is not claimed to be their first distinction. Searches using complement, Alexander-dual, non-dominating, and strongly regular aliases, including mathematical result indexes, found no implication covering the displayed ranks. Absence from indexed sources cannot establish absolute priority; unidentified prior computations remain a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The Shrikhande and four-by-four rook graphs form the natural exceptional pair of nonisomorphic strongly regular graphs with identical \((16,6,2,2)\) parameters and spectrum. Exact homological behavior of the same canonical complementary-neighborhood functor on both is an intrinsically motivated structural invariant. In particular, the degree-two discrepancy demonstrates a specific topology not determined by these parameters; the complete homology vectors are useful fixed exact test cases for the comparison of graph-topological constructions. This is not merely a recomputation of clique or independence homology or of the domination polynomial.

## Scope and residual limits
No integral torsion, full homotopy type, general family, or unconditional bibliographic priority is asserted. The earlier 1959 graph-classification paper is cited without inventing a first-public day; the verified exact public date belongs to the arXiv methodological reference.
