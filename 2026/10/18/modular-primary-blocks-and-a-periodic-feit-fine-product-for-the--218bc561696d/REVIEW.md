# Mathematical review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Nondegeneracy of the trace pairing identifies commutator-image membership with orthogonality to the full centralizer. In Jordan form the trace of \(Y^2\) times a commuting diagonal block is exactly the block length times the squared eigenvalue times the constant coefficient; individual block projectors give necessity, and vanishing for all centralizers gives sufficiency. The finite-field enumeration uses complete rational-canonical partition types, the standard centralizer-unit formula, the \(q\)-binomial identity and the monic-polynomial Euler product. The nilpotent and first nonnilpotent dimensions follow from orbit and affine-fiber dimensions. Exhaustive low-dimensional computations agree. Risk: a computer replay alone would not prove the full identities; the proof supplies the missing infinite step.

**Originality (O): PASS, with documented risk.** Iyudu's 2012 work proves the characteristic-zero nilpotence and component picture, so that part is not new. Benkart–Lopes–Ondrus classify positive-characteristic **simple** modules, including dimension-\(p\) ones, but do not thereby enumerate arbitrary possibly decomposable labeled modules. Feit–Fine/Fulman–Guralnick count ordinary commuting pairs, a related but different equation. A previously known characteristic-two two-dimensional special case is not claimed separately. Comparisons of block divisibility, all-size coefficients, and Euler-factor dominance revealed no theorem in inspected primary text that implies the entire result. Risk: Shirikov's 2007 full text was not accessible for inspection, and some specialized modular representation counting literature may have been missed.

**Value (V): PASS.** A uniform test determines exactly which matrix-conjugacy classes admit the Jordan-plane commutator relation, including all nonsemisimple primary types. The resulting coefficient formula determines a canonical representation-counting invariant in every dimension and reveals a sharp change from the nilpotent-second-matrix commuting enumeration to the full Feit–Fine Euler weights only at part sizes divisible by the characteristic. The first dimension jump is a meaningful geometric manifestation rather than a small arbitrary numerical instance.

**Scope.** The geometric claim concerns reduced supports. No description of nilpotent scheme structure or similarity orbit counts is asserted. Full historical uniqueness is not certified.
