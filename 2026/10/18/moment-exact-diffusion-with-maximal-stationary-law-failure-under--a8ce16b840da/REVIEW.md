# Scientific review of the common-reset moment-equivalence theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The two generators coincide exactly on the finite-dimensional vector space of quadratic polynomials, giving all-time moment identity without a small-reset expansion. The stationary Cauchy covariance follows from the same closed linear equations, and its determinant is strictly positive for distinct damping rates. Under full resets the jump stationary law lives on a one-dimensional exponential-age curve. The stationary diffusion's exponential-Brownian integral has a Malliavin matrix with trivial kernel, since its derivatives would otherwise produce a vanishing linear combination of distinct exponential functions. Its absolutely continuous stationary law is therefore mutually singular with the jump law. The proof gives the moment and Malliavin integrability conditions. Exact rational verification in `check.py` passed 21,140 assertions. The mathematical proof, not enumeration, establishes the unrestricted dimension and infinite-time statements.

**Originality — PASS with explicit priority risk.** Galla (arXiv:2602.21635v1, main text and supplement S2) gives a diffusion approximation for frequent weak resetting; its linear multi-particle correlations use zero immigration and individual additive noises, rather than heterogeneous affine drifts with all-rate moment agreement and maximum stationary total-variation discrepancy. Bressloff (arXiv:2401.03501, introduction and global-reset density analysis) treats common fixed-position resetting of interacting Brownian particles. Olsen (arXiv:2310.04840v1, sections II–IV) derives moments for reset-to-distribution processes. These results explain overlapping methods but do not imply the complete joint theorem, especially the absolutely continuous one-noise surrogate versus a singular full-reset process with identical full-rank covariance. The second-order generator comparison and Malliavin criterion are known tools, not claimed as discoveries. An older result combining these ingredients for common multiplicative catastrophes might exist; no absolute novelty or external independent priority confirmation is asserted.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A frequent modeling choice replaces common Poisson resetting by multiplicative diffusion noise. Exact agreement of all low-order joint moments at arbitrary reset strength could otherwise suggest distributional fidelity. The theorem proves a maximal stationary-law failure even when those matched covariances are strictly positive definite in every dimension: a structural distinction between low-moment closure and distributional approximation. The explicit sharp polynomial degree boundary and full-rank Cauchy geometry explain the mechanism rather than merely exhibiting one numerical mismatch. Distinct rates and nonzero forcing are natural nondegeneracy assumptions, not arbitrary parameter choices.

**Limits and risk.** The total-variation conclusion requires complete resets; the stationary density is shown to exist but is not explicitly calculated. Multi-time laws and rates of weak-reset approximation remain open. The general first-two-moment identity is a direct generator calculation, and its individual ingredients may appear separately in prior literature.
