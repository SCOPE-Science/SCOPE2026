# Mathematical assessment: dodecahedral perfect-matching homotopy

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is specified as \(GP(10,2)\), eliminating drawing or edge-identity ambiguity. Exhaustive perfect-matching generation gives 36 facets; independent recursion confirms the 30,504 total face count. A deterministic 15,238-pair Forman matching is checked against every one of the 163,650 Hasse incidences and leaves critical dimensions \((0:1,2:3,3:23)\). Signed gradient reduction gives an integral 3-by-23 cellular attaching matrix, whose Smith invariants are \((1,1,2)\); gcds are independently checked from every relevant minor. Since the resulting CW model is simply connected with cells only through dimension three, the attaching degrees determine the homotopy type and give exactly one Moore summand plus 20 three-spheres. A second method, elementary collapses followed by independent modular boundary reductions, matches all predicted field homology ranks. Principal limitations: the proof treats one graph, is computationally assisted, and has no external audit.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The complete 2022 foundational paper of Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega studies perfect-matching complexes of hexagonal honeycomb patches, not the twenty-vertex dodecahedral graph. Its concluding discussion explicitly motivates further families. A later accessible abstract treats Möbius and circular ladders, not \(GP(10,2)\). The nearest indexed dodecahedral result concerns the ordinary matching complex, a strictly different complex: knowing the ambient homotopy type does not imply the homotopy type of its perfect-matching subcomplex. A nearest perfect-matching result concerns the Fano incidence graph. Exact, alias, stronger-coverage and matching-table searches found no statement implying a degree-two Moore summand for the dodecahedral perfect-matching complex. Accessible historical claims were also checked against object, invariant, coefficients and graph parameters. Risk: absence from an index does not establish universal uniqueness; the later ladder paper's full text was not accessible through the tested source.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The dodecahedral graph is the canonical smallest fullerene and a natural symmetric test case for perfect-matching topology. Its 36 Kekulé/perfect matchings generate a 9-dimensional simplicial complex with unexpected integral two-torsion, and the finding resolves all nontrivial low-dimensional attaching maps into an explicit Moore-space wedge. In particular it distinguishes the perfect-matching complex from the ordinary matching complex by homotopy and torsion, addressing a structural question raised by the motivating literature. It is a natural complete finite invariant with a reproducible proof, rather than an arbitrary partial enumeration; no unproved general theorem is asserted.
