# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an identity in the Grothendieck ring, proved by a disjoint partition into exact-support tori. There is a complete treatment of all possible support cardinalities, including the empty support contribution for \(s=n-2\) and the exceptional \(s=1\) term in the quadric inclusion–exclusion formula. Characteristic zero makes each restricted quadratic form nonsingular; algebraic closure supplies the split-quadric classes. The inverse-torus quadratic locus is irreducible, and pure hypersurface dimension rules out a separate boundary component. An independent polynomial implementation agrees for eleven consecutive sizes and its finite-field point enumeration detects the expected nonsplit correction. These finite checks do not certify the infinite theorem; the symbolic proof does.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest inspected full-text source, Aluffi–Marcolli's 2008 banana-graph paper (arXiv:0807.1690, Section 3, Theorem 3.10), treats \(e_{n-1}\); its Cremona image is a hyperplane. The proposed claim instead treats \(e_{n-2}\), whose dense-torus Cremona image is a nondegenerate quadric and yields a distinct motivic class. Morales-Almazán (arXiv:1208.4129, Section 4.2) treats the banana/flower graph cases; Bilgin (arXiv:1112.2131) treats other special hypersurface families. The MathOverflow singular-support criterion is not a motivic formula. These are concrete nonimplication comparisons, not novelty inferred merely from absent word matches. Mathematical-record and literature searches located no dominating exact class calculation, though unindexed implicit coverage remains a material residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result computes a natural global invariant of the canonical uniform-matroid basis hypersurface one elementary-symmetric degree below the extensively studied banana graph family. It provides every compactly supported Hodge–Deligne coefficient and an unexpectedly alternating, exponentially growing Euler-characteristic formula, for infinitely many dimensions. Those data are not determined by the known singular-support classification or the banana-class calculation, and the quadratic Cremona transformation explains their mechanism. This is a meaningful exact classification rather than a selected numerical experiment.

## Sources and limitations

Primary full text was inspected for Aluffi–Marcolli arXiv:0807.1690 (theorem and preceding lemmas), Morales-Almazán arXiv:1208.4129 (Section 4.2), and Bilgin arXiv:1112.2131 (abstract and covered families). The proof depends on split quadric classes and is not claimed over arbitrary finite fields. Literature indexing might omit equivalent descriptions of the same hypersurface.
