# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact simplex criterion is that the union of all selected triple-subsets has at most \(n-3\) elements. For each permutation, fixed-face alternating traces are counted by the number of triple-orbits. Inversion over the Boolean lattice of permutation cycles produces the quartic signed trace, with all empty-face and orientation signs explicitly included. The existing concentration theorem identifies that Lefschetz number with the unique reduced homology character. A horizontal-strip Pieri identity and the induced-module character formula prove the three irreducible constituents for every \(n\ge7\). Independent finite calculations reproduce every conjugacy-class trace at \(n=7,8\) and the proposed Specht decomposition at \(n=7,8,9,10,11\). The checker does not substitute for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk.** Nilakantan–Singh, arXiv:1807.11732v1, Theorem 1.3, gives the ordinary triple-Kneser sphere-wedge **homotopy type and rank**, not its full symmetric-group homology action. Kozlov's earlier general wedge result provides concentration but does not, on the accessible evidence, settle this representation. Stable-Kneser equivariance is a dihedral action on different complexes; the existing two-subset Kneser result has distinct vertices and parameters and does not imply the three-subset formula. The explicitly queried research index yielded no covering triple-subset representation statement. The full Kozlov book text was not inspected, and a differently indexed equivalent treatment remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** A canonical infinite family of highly symmetric graph complexes receives a complete rational equivariant homology classification, not merely an integer rank. The three irreducible constituents are multiplicity-free; the trace depends only on fixed letters and transpositions; and the result determines the absence of both full-symmetric and alternating invariants. These features are not recoverable from the known homotopy type or Betti number and are applicable to rational symmetry quotients and comparative equivariant topology. No assertion about integral lattices or modular representations is made.
