# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Boolean term normal forms reduce all atomic equalities to the presence or absence of atom-word patterns under cyclic shifts. Explicit Boolean minterms separate different necklace supports. Every nonempty support of size at most the atom-cycle multiplicity is realizable. Commuting atom permutations yield the wreath-product orbit classification. The inverse theorem follows from a strict leading-coefficient monotonicity argument for all total atom counts at least three; the two-atom exceptional identity is verified symbolically. The executable finite checker corroborates the combinatorial formulas and does not substitute for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The relevant 2023 finite-algebra definability paper supplies a general subisomorphism criterion and algorithms, but not the cyclic-Boolean necklace formula, uniform-cycle inverse rigidity, or exact two-atom collision. The 2019 open-definability paper addresses a general complexity problem for relational structures. Related published orbit-support results use infinite atomless Boolean powers and count automorphism orbits rather than finite cyclic Boolean qf types; finite-Boolean quantifier-rank thresholds study a different invariant. Burnside necklace enumeration and Boolean atom representation are established and not claimed as new. Older unindexed equivalents or a hidden reduction in the literature remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The natural inverse problem asks whether counts of logically distinguishable tuples across all arities recover a finite Boolean dynamical system. The theorem supplies both a complete infinite-family reconstruction result with a sharp isolated collision and a logically meaningful threshold: for one or two atom cycles, all automorphism-invariant relations are quantifier-free definable, but for three cycles this already fails in unary arity. The exact gap between orbit multisets and quantifier-free supports clarifies information loss and cannot be inferred from automorphism counts alone.

Limitations: only nontrivial finite Boolean algebras with equally long atom cycles; no mixed-cycle classification or independently established publication priority. Primary references: Campercholi–Tellechea–Ventura (2023), arXiv:2303.17017; Areces–Campercholi–Penazzi–Ventura (2019), arXiv:1904.04637. Same-model arguments and finite checks do not replace an outside independent audit.
