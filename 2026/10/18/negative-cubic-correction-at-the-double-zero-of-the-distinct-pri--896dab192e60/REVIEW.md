# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** A self-contained Euler-product identity reduces the cubic Taylor term to the logarithmic derivative of a normally convergent prime product and the zeta Laurent coefficient. An exact rational bound controls the *entire* tail of the resulting prime logarithmic sum. The supplied standard-library checker confirms all rational inequalities and an independent finite-product derivative; the proof, not finite tests, handles infinitely many primes.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The closest primary paper, Álvarez Cruz–Álvarez Gutiérrez (arXiv:2610.13658v1, §6), derives the second derivative and quadratic term, but does not state the cubic coefficient or its strict sign. The residual Euler product is already present there, so further differentiation is straightforward in principle; the principal new content is the evaluated next coefficient and rigorous sign inequality. The complete source text and indexed mathematical searches were compared by implication. Older, unindexed formulations could cover the result.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The coefficient and its sign specify the first departure of a natural arithmetic generating series from the twin-prime-constant quadratic germ, including a precise one-sided approach to the leading normalization. This is a narrowly motivated local invariant, not a claim to an independent global prime theorem. The manuscript does not evaluate the divergent-product regularization or establish global monotonicity away from the double zero.
