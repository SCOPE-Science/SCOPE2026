# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For positive dependence the exact signed mixed derivative of the conditional log density proves monotone likelihood ratio, and expanded support makes the usual stochastic inequality strict. For nonpositive dependence an exact two-component decomposition of the conditional density yields a closed conditional CDF; the derivative numerator is a three-term sum whose sign is established for the full real parameter range, using explicit strict exponential inequalities. Exchangeability lifts strict univariate quantile ordering to a coupling with both coordinates strictly increasing. The rate is removed by positive rescaling. Rational and numerical checks provide nonessential cross-checks; the universal assertion depends on the written analytic argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Closest directly inspected texts were Saumard–Wellner (2018), Section 3.3.2, whose **uniform** FGM negative-dependence example does not transfer to exponential sums; Pellerey–Navarro (2021), whose stochastic-order sufficient hypotheses fail for the negative parameters here; and Ortega-Jiménez et al. (2022/2023), also reproduced in the 2022 thesis Chapter 6, treating the **nonnegative** FGM total-positivity range. Navarro (2022) treats ageing of an FGM exponential **sum**, not coordinate ordering conditional on the sum. No covering statement was identified in source/alias/parameter searches or earlier available mathematical records, but an independently unidentified equivalent result remains a priority risk. The claimed novelty is only the full negative-dependence stochastic-order conclusion and its sharp contrast with likelihood-ratio ordering; the positive-parameter likelihood-ratio fact belongs to previous literature.

## Value — PASS

Conditional-sum monotonicity is a nontrivial risk-allocation and stochastic-order question under dependence. This natural two-risk model admits a complete phase diagram in the full permissible FGM interval: strong vector usual stochastic order survives all negative dependence despite failed total-positivity and failed monotone likelihood ratios. The contrasting uniform-margin behavior in earlier work makes the exponential-margin boundary meaningful rather than a cosmetic model substitution. The theorem is not a general extension to arbitrary copulas or margins.
