# Scientific review: nilpotent-product probabilities of finite unital rings

## Correctness — PASS
The exact statement assumes a nonzero finite associative unital ring and independently uniform ordered factors. The proof reconstructs the classical count of individual nilpotent square matrices using the unique Fitting decomposition, derives an exact rank-conditioned probability for matrix products, and averages by counting kernels of uniform linear maps. Nilpotency lifts through a nilpotent ideal, so the formula holds across the finite Jacobson radical and each Wedderburn factor. The sharp maximizer statement follows from strict monotonicity of \((2Q-1)/Q^2\) for \(Q\ge2\). Eight independently enumerated small ring or matrix configurations matched, including a noncommutative local equality example. Finite checks supplement the proof rather than certify the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The October 6, 2026 paper by Sarma and Subedi introduces nilpotent-product probability and treats commutative and triangular families. The inspected Sections 1–2.3 and conclusion did not yield the rank-conditioned full-matrix formula or the complete semisimple-quotient formula for arbitrary finite unital rings. The Fine–Herstein individual-nilpotent enumeration, Gerstenhaber's alternate account, and standard Wedderburn structure are acknowledged ingredients but do not state these product-fiber results. Searches for random-matrix nilpotent products, semisimple-quotient probability formulas, equivalent field comparisons and exact powers did not identify prior stronger coverage. Nearby records concern nilpotent–involution factorizations and nilpotency-index distributions, which impose different conditions. These searches are not uniqueness proofs. Residual risks include unindexed probability literature and records for which only summaries were accessible.

## Value — PASS
The 2026 literature motivates computing nilpotent-product probabilities beyond commutative and triangular rings. The result computes the invariant exactly for *every* finite nonzero associative unital ring, showing it depends only on semisimple matrix factors, not nilpotent extensions. The equality of the invariant for \(M_n(\mathbb F_q)\) and \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\) is an unexpectedly simple structural bridge; the sharp upper-bound classification includes noncommutative local examples. The full natural family and precise, reusable probability law are mathematically motivated beyond a special-case computation.
