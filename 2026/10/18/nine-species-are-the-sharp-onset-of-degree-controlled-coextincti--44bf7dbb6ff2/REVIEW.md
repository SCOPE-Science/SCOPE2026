# Scientific review: degree-controlled coextinction variance

## Correctness
PASS. The exact covariance identity follows from the probability of each plant-neighborhood union disappearing. Matching plant degrees cancels all mean and individual-variance terms; matching pollinator degrees forces a double polynomial zero at complete pollinator extinction. The proof reduces all networks on at most eight total species to three exhaustive finite incidence-matrix cases. The standard-library program establishes exact matrix totals by independent inclusion-exclusion, checks all pairs of distinct union histograms within each degree class, and verifies every quotient's fixed sign. The connected nine-species witnesses are also checked by complete independent-outcome enumeration and rational arithmetic, not an approximation.

## Originality
PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty. The closest examined ecological references study secondary losses under pollinator deletion (Memmott–Waser–Price, 2004), nestedness and resilience in a dynamic Boolean community model (Campbell–Yang–Shea–Albert, 2012), and variance in an integrated robustness index under sequential knockouts (Bane and coauthors, 2018). None of the inspected mathematical statements establishes a first degree-matched crossing of an instantaneous plant-coextinction *variance* at nine total species. Existing exact work on regular incidence webs addresses variance extremizers within a regular family, not a reversal between irregular, degree-matched networks at different extinction probabilities. Alternate graph-reliability terminology is a residual literature risk; absence of an identified match is not proof of global novelty.

## Value
PASS. Degree distributions determine the expected number of remaining plants in this model, but do not determine its uncertainty. The result isolates the first possible nontrivial network size at which that uncertainty ranking changes when environmental loss pressure varies; the explicit factorization identifies the exact crossover without numerical fitting. This is a natural controlled-network risk comparison with direct interpretability in one-generation secondary extinction. It is not a general claim about dynamic ecological stability.
