For \(g(t)=t^{2}-t+1\), there are no integers \(a,b\ge2\) with both \(a\mid g(b)\) and \(b\mid g(a)\): a strict descent eliminates every directed two-cycle. Consequently, an integral reciprocal-gcd sum at a nontrivial squarefree integer requires at least three prime factors, and an explicit three-prime example shows this is sharp.
