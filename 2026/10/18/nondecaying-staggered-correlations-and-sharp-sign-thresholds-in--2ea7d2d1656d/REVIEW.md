# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The common-reset renewal coupling establishes uniqueness on the infinite chain and finite even rings. The single-spin stationary equation gives the alternating mean, while the two-spin generator yields a bounded second-order recurrence with a checked boundary at coinciding sites. Its unique bounded solution gives both covariance formulas. The odd-distance sign change reduces to a strictly increasing polynomial with exactly one root, and the Lambert scaling follows from explicit logarithmic bounds. A standalone full-generator computation agrees on 20 finite-ring parameter cases and performs 1,500 separate recurrence checks. Finite experiments are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS on examined evidence, subject to the bibliographic risk below. The 1995 voter-coarsening preprint does not introduce common global resets. The 2011 diffusion-resetting paper concerns a single diffusing particle rather than a coupled voter field. The 2020 global-reset Ising paper analyzes magnetization distributions and highlights two-point correlations as additional work; it does not derive the checkerboard covariance and sign thresholds. The 2022 voter-reset paper uses independently resetting sites and hence different two-point equations. The 2023 review distinguishes global and local interacting reset dynamics and does not supply this formula. Searches of related result records and alternate phrases found no stronger mathematical implication. A yet-unlocated source about deterministic periodic initial conditions remains a real residual risk; absence from an index is not proof of novelty.

## Value
PASS. A shared reset clock creates a nonzero staggered long-distance connected covariance in a coarsening interacting-particle system, a structural distinction from ordinary local-reset noise. The exact odd-distance sign thresholds and their logarithmic-over-distance-squared scale describe a nontrivial competition between diffusive alignment and common-age fluctuations. Exact finite-cycle formulas make this an analytical reference for finite-volume simulation.

## Closest work and limits
Magoni–Majumdar–Schehr (2020) is closest for global Ising resets, whereas Grange (2022/2023) is closest for voter two-point observables but uses independent resets. Neither examined full text implies the claim. The theorem is confined to unbiased one-dimensional voter copying and checkerboard global resets. Its threshold uniqueness applies only in infinite volume. No independent validation has been performed.
