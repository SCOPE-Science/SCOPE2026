# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Incomparable epistemic cells force a concrete two-valuation failure of binary-meet preservation. Under local nesting the bundle equals the ordinary S5 box for the intersection equivalence relation. Each incidence edge has a leaf precisely when the two corresponding cells are comparable, yielding the star-component normal form. On a component with \(j\) labelled worlds there are exactly \(2\mathsf{Bell}(j)-1\) ordered partition pairs, from which the exponential formula and recurrence follow. The standalone exhaustive checker independently confirms counts and semantic equivalences on the stated finite ranges.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority qualification. Ågotnes and Wáng (2021) establish generic failure of normality and develop a neighbourhood semantics for somebody-knows. Ding and Yang (2026) study the general bundled-operator logic and S5 axiomatization. Neither inspected work reports a necessary-and-sufficient local-nesting/star criterion or the precise labelled enumeration of the exceptional normal frames. Related work on distributed belief has a different modal operator. Searches for local refinement, graph-star incidence, equivalent modal representation and the resulting integer sequence identified no dominating theorem, but cannot establish complete historical uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. The exact frame criterion isolates every case in which a normally nonnormal epistemic bundle can be replaced by distributed knowledge. The component decomposition yields an explicit all-size census, and the four-world example shows that this collapse can occur without a globally most-informed agent. These are structural and enumerative facts about a natural modal-logic boundary, rather than a relabelling or arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limits and related literature

Two agents with finite labelled S5 worlds and universally quantified propositional valuations are assumed; no many-agent or restricted-valuation classification is claimed. Sources: Ågotnes and Wáng, *Somebody Knows* (2021), DOI:10.24963/kr.2021/1; Ding and Yang, *A General Theory of Propositional Modal Bundled Modalities* (2026), arXiv:2603.26268; Lindqvist, Velázquez-Quesada, and Ågotnes, *Variations on distributed belief* (2025), DOI:10.1007/s10849-025-09446-8. Prior results involving arbitrary interior operators or equivalent combinatorial enumeration under other vocabulary remain a residual priority risk.
