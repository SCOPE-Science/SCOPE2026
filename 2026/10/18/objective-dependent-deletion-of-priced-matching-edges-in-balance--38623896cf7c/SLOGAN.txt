For a budget-constrained \(K_{n,n}\) with its perfect-matching edges priced at \(t\), the unique resistance-minimizing conductance design removes the matching exactly at an explicit threshold \(t_{\rm R}(n)\); below it the optimal two-class weights are determined by a unique monotone rational root. The spanning-tree-maximizing design removes the same edges at the strictly lower threshold \(t_{\rm D}(n)\), producing a genuine interval where the two natural network objectives prescribe opposite edge support.
