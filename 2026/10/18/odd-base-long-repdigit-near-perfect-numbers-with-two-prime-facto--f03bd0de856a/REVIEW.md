# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof invokes the explicit three-family-plus-exception classification of two-prime near-perfect numbers (Ren–Chen 2013). For odd lengths at least five a uniform two-adic size bound contradicts the repunit growth. For even lengths at least six a coprime factorization forces two distinct odd primes. The length-three and length-four boundary cases are eliminated by square gaps, prime size, and residue arguments; the unique isolated exception is checked directly. The independent finite checker passed 3,975 classified-number/base pairs plus direct divisor-sum enumeration through 10,000. The theorem rests on these symbolic arguments, not the finite census.

**Originality — PASS against inspected literature.** Ren–Chen classify all two-prime near-perfect integers but do not classify odd-base long repdigit representations. Hasanalizade (arXiv:2206.10353, journal 2023) studies fixed bases through ten and its stated length bound is contradicted by \(40=(1111)_3\). The separately indexed even-base repdigit theorem has a disjoint base hypothesis. All-odd-base uniqueness and the complete length obstruction were not identified in the inspected sources. Unindexed deductions from the integer classification remain a risk.

**Value — PASS.** The natural two-prime near-perfect/repdigit intersection is settled uniformly for every odd base and every length at least three, while isolating a genuine exceptional four-digit representation missing from the published restricted-base statement. The result is more than an isolated corrected numeral because it proves no additional exceptions at arbitrarily large odd bases.

**Limits.** Does not handle even bases, arbitrary near-perfect prime support, or lengths one and two. Source novelty searches are not a proof of global uniqueness.
