Direct affine-image identities yield the exact depth/side transition kernel. For \(2/3<p<1\), the stationary generating function has positive coefficients, sums to one, and satisfies every stationary balance equation; irreducibility proves uniqueness within shell-constant densities. Its real poles at \(r(p)\) and \(2\) give the two regularity regimes and their double-pole boundary, while Laplace-transform convergence proves the exponential scaling. For \(0<p<2/3\), domination by a positive-drift random walk implies only finitely many depth-zero resets, and the strong law then gives exact escape speed. Rational-arithmetic checks are supportive rather than exhaustive.

The submitted all-parameter claim proves off-critical behaviors and the distinct \(p=6/7\) bounded-density transition. Semantic similarity queries returned no matching model-specific finding; the closest indexed results are on different expanding maps or generic endpoint laws. A prior broader theorem or inaccessible historical calculation might subsume part of this conclusion; external independent review remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** The two thresholds answer distinct mathematically motivated dynamical questions: whether the random system admits a normalizable stationary density, and whether that density remains bounded near its two geometrically attracting endpoints. The exact \(p=6/7\) logarithmic boundary, the \(p\downarrow2/3\) exponential scale, and the complementary escape speed provide a usable parameter-wide classification of the very family introduced to study critical infinite-measure statistics. Neither an arbitrary parameter slice nor a finite table is involved.
