# Off-cycle root distance controls the first magnetic-flux signature in weighted unicyclic quantum walks

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph with a unique cycle of length \(m\ge3\), rooted at a vertex \(v\) whose graph distance to the cycle is \(d\ge1\). Each edge has strictly positive hopping magnitude. The continuous-time quantum walk is generated by the Hermitian, zero-diagonal weighted adjacency operator \(H_\Phi\) having total oriented magnetic flux \(\Phi\) around the cycle.

Let \(C=\prod_{e\in E_\mathrm{cycle}}w_e\) and \(T=\prod_{e\in E_\mathrm{path}}w_e\), where the latter path is the unique route from \(v\) to its first cycle vertex. Both products are positive. For the return probability \(P_v(t;\Phi)=|\langle v|e^{-itH_\Phi}|v\rangle|^2\), the following exact first flux-dependent Taylor orders and coefficients hold for every real \(\Phi\):

\[P_v(t;\Phi)-P_v(t;0)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{4(-1)^{m/2+d}CT^2}{(m+2d)!}(\cos\Phi-1)t^{m+2d}+O(t^{m+2d+2}),&m\text{ even},\\[7pt]\displaystyle\frac{4(-1)^{m+d}C^2T^2}{(2m+2d)!}(\cos(2\Phi)-1)t^{2m+2d}+O(t^{2m+2d+2}),&m\text{ odd}.\end{cases}\]

For odd \(m\), the stronger exact symmetry \(P_v(t;\Phi+\pi)=P_v(t;\Phi)\) holds for all \(t\in\mathbb R\). Thus, for generic nonzero flux contrast, the first flux signature is delayed by the root distance and obeys a different parity law: order \(m+2d\) for even cycles, versus \(2(m+d)\) for odd cycles.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, connected, simple and unicyclic, with no restrictions on the sizes, shapes or edge weights of the trees attached to its unique cycle. Each magnitude \(w_e\) is strictly positive; zero onsite terms are assumed, and the unit of time fixes the Hamiltonian normalization. An edge oriented from \(u\) to \(z\) has matrix entry \(w_e e^{i\theta_e}\), with the reverse entry its complex conjugate. Conjugating by a diagonal unitary makes every off-cycle edge real and positive and localizes all phase dependence in the holonomy \(e^{i\Phi}\) on one cycle edge. The result is independent of this gauge choice and of the orientation convention because only cosine functions occur.

The root lies strictly outside the cycle: \(d\ge1\). The big-O statements are local in \(t\), with fixed finite graph, weights and flux; analyticity follows from the finite-dimensional matrix exponential. The coefficient may vanish at the identified blind fluxes, but is nonzero as a function of flux because \(CT>0\).

## Proof
**Step 1: winding expansion.** After gauge fixing, define rooted moments \(M_r(\Phi)=\langle v|H_\Phi^r|v\rangle\). Every term is a weighted closed walk of length \(r\) at \(v\), so
\[M_r(\Phi)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z} c_{r,q} e^{iq\Phi},\qquad c_{r,q}=c_{r,-q}\ge0.\]
Here \(q\) is the signed cycle winding number. Because the only cycle is the distinguished \(m\)-cycle, lifting the walk to the universal-cover tree shows that a walk with nonzero winding \(q\) must have length at least \(2d+m|q|\). Moreover, its length has parity \(r\equiv mq\pmod2\), because the lift is a tree and any excess length over the geodesic is even. The shortest \(q=\pm1\) walks are uniquely the path to the cycle, one oriented circuit and the path back, with amplitude \(T^2C\). The shortest \(q=\pm2\) walks are likewise two successive circuits, with amplitude \(T^2C^2\). In particular,
\[c_{m+2d,1}=c_{m+2d,-1}=T^2C,\qquad c_{2m+2d,2}=c_{2m+2d,-2}=T^2C^2.\]
Every zero-winding rooted closed walk has even length, since its lift is a closed walk in a tree.

**Step 2: real/imaginary separation.** Expand the rooted return amplitude
\[A_v(t;\Phi)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(-it)^r}{r!}M_r(\Phi)=R_v(t;\Phi)+iI_v(t;\Phi),\]
where \(R_v\) contains only even powers of \(t\), \(I_v\) only odd powers, and both are real. Thus \(P_v=R_v^2+I_v^2\) and the expansion of \(P_v\) contains only even powers.

**Step 3: even cycle.** When \(m\) is even, the least nonzero winding occurs in the even moment of order \(L=m+2d\). All lower moments entering \(R_v\) and \(I_v\) are flux independent. In the leading flux-dependent coefficient of \(R_v\), the first winding pair contributes \(2(-1)^{L/2}CT^2\cos\Phi/L!\). Since \(R_v(0;\Phi)=1\), squaring doubles the coefficient; cross terms with positive-order Taylor pieces occur strictly later. Subtracting the zero-flux value gives the asserted factor \(4(-1)^{m/2+d}CT^2(\cos\Phi-1)/(m+2d)!\). The next potentially nonzero Taylor order is \(L+2\).

**Step 4: odd cycle.** When \(m\) is odd, the first winding pair at \(L=m+2d\) enters only the imaginary odd-power amplitude \(I_v\). Hence it cannot interfere linearly with the even-power real amplitude. Its square can affect \(P_v\) no earlier than order \(2L=2m+4d\). But because \(d\ge1\), a twice-winding closed walk enters the real amplitude at the strictly earlier even order \(K=2m+2d<2L\). No earlier even moment can have nonzero winding because any even-order winding must be even, and its minimum order is \(K\). Odd moments can carry one-winding contributions starting at \(L\), but these enter \(I_v^2\) only at order \(2L>K\). Therefore the first flux-dependent contribution to \(P_v\) is solely twice the twice-winding coefficient of \(R_v\), namely
\[\frac{4(-1)^{K/2}C^2T^2}{K!}\cos(2\Phi),\]
followed by the subtraction of its zero-flux value. Since \(K/2=m+d\), this is the claimed odd-cycle coefficient. Again, the next possible term is order \(K+2\).

**Step 5: exact half-flux symmetry for odd cycles.** Flipping the sign of every hopping entry gives \(-H_\Phi\), whose cycle holonomy differs by \(\pi\) when \(m\) is odd. All off-cycle phase changes from the global sign flip are removable by a diagonal gauge because the attached subgraphs are trees. The return amplitude of \(-H_\Phi\) at time \(t\) is the complex conjugate of that for \(H_\Phi\) at time \(t\); its squared modulus is unchanged. Therefore shifting \(\Phi\) by \(\pi\) preserves \(P_v(t;\Phi)\) exactly.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify.py` enumerates exact Laurent-polynomial rooted moments in a single formal flux variable and combines them using rational Taylor coefficients. For cycle lengths \(3\le m\le8\), root distances \(0\le d\le3\), both choices of additional tree branches and two distinct integer-weight profiles, it checks the disappearance of flux dependence at every order before the asserted threshold and verifies the two signed leading Fourier coefficients at the threshold. It reports `VERIFY_OK rooted_unicyclic_profiles= 96 lengths= 3 .. 13 orders= 4 .. 20`. The \(d=0\) tests are separate boundary controls; the stated theorem concerns only \(d\ge1\). Such finite tests do not establish the unlimited-size theorem; the winding/geodesic argument above supplies that proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Coutinho et al. (2022) develop finite continuous-time quantum-walk methods and exact spectral analysis for integral Hamiltonians; their paper does not determine weighted magnetic short-time rooted contrast. Frigerio and Paris (2022) analyze phase-controlled swift quantum walks and return suppression, not the order of off-cycle flux onset. Chaves, Chagas and Coutinho (2022) study chiral Hermitian graph Hamiltonians and zero transfer on directed cycles. Luppi (2026) explicitly analyzes rooted moment interference and the parity-dependent contrast for walkers rooted **on** simple flux-threaded cycles, including the even/odd order distinction. The present statement treats roots **off** the cycle, arbitrary attached trees and arbitrary positive weights and determines the exact distance-dependent coefficient. This is a substantive extension in object and parameter dependence, but the uninspected primary full text of Luppi (2026) is a documented possible source of stronger coverage; priority beyond the inspected material is not asserted.

## Limitations
The graph must have exactly one cycle and no onsite potentials; no extension to multicycle graphs, roots on the cycle or vanishing edge weights is claimed. At fluxes where \(\cos\Phi=1\) (even case), or \(\cos(2\Phi)=1\) (odd case), the displayed contrast vanishes and the generic onset language does not apply. The analytic proof is unrestricted, while executable verification is finite. No independent validation or broad literature-priority guarantee is claimed.
