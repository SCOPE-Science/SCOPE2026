# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the exact domain \(a,b>0\), \(d>2a\), the proof derives the correct support halfwidth \(h_b(x)\) and the two-overlap / union-offset formulas under invariant plane measure. The threshold \(x_0\in(0,1)\), the three closed-form cases, and the prolate/oblate boundary are verified by direct elementary integration. The strict inversion proof uses an explicit monotone-likelihood-ratio order: normalized halfwidth weights shift toward small \(x\) as \(b\) increases, while the pair-hit fraction increases. The sharp limits follow from the displayed expansions. Standalone numerical quadrature, direct random-offset sampling, and inversion checks corroborate but do not establish the theorem.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
Voss and Cruz-Orive (2009) supply general flat-probe second-moment tools and invariant sampling, not the displayed two-coaxial-spheroid closed form or global inversion theorem. Rigaut (1985) deals explicitly with **spheres**, which appear only as the \(a=b\) specialization and are not claimed new. Related published studies of single ellipsoid central-section radii and ellipsoid axes inferred from central-section volume moments study different measurements; they do not imply this joint-hit statistic or its strict global monotonicity. Relevant published mathematical results were compared for equivalent formulations and stronger implications. Historical priority remains provisional because the full Rigaut text was unavailable and older stereological special-case tables may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Co-observation of two distinguishable particle profiles in random sections is a natural stereological measurement. For non-spherical particles the exact probability depends on the Euclidean invariant-plane geometry rather than a formal affine deformation of the sphere answer. The strict prolate-to-oblate monotonicity gives an entire natural shape family an identifiable parameter from a one-bit component count, while the needle and pancake asymptotics quantify severe shape dependence. This is a complete, motivated inverse classification, not merely a numerical evaluation or special-function substitution.

## Restrictions
Only coaxial congruent solids at fixed known axial radius and separation are treated. No finite-sample noise guarantees or definitive historical uniqueness is claimed.
