# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof separates nonsquare grid vertices by their distances to the marked corner and the uniquely distinguishable shorter-side corner. Square-grid stable cells are recovered by a layer-induction on predecessor-colour multisets. For square sides at most three, matching-cell interactions form a tree and all equitable realizations are isomorphic. From side four onward, an explicit matching switch preserves every stable-cell degree, while changing a colour-sensitive induced two-four-cycle configuration into an eight-cycle. The connectedness argument uses unaffected local detours. Independent finite checkers corroborated the structural assertions; no finite check is used as an infinite proof.

## Value — PASS

The question measures exactly when one natural geometric landmark makes a two-parameter graph family identifiable by an efficient logical invariant. The nonsquare/square dichotomy is all-size, and the precise square-size cutoff comes from the first cycle in the two-vertex cell-interaction graph. The result gives explicit competing connected graphs rather than only a nonidentification certificate. This is a motivated structural boundary, not merely a finite census.
