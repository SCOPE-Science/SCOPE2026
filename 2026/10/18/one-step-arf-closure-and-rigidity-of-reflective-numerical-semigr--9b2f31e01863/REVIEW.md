# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
Shor's exact gap characterization is a verified published premise. With \(g=qa+r\) and \(1\leq r<a\), the proof splits the ordinary and multiplicity-two cases from all others. The proposed oversemigroup is demonstrably Arf by examining whether the largest member of an ordered triple exceeds the original genus. Every new gap \(h=g+ka\) is independently shown to equal \(2(h-1)-(h-2)\), with both predecessors in the original semigroup because \(a\geq3\). Thus it is reached in a single Arf operation. The genus and conductor claims follow from exactly \(q\) filled high gaps. These are universal arguments, not extrapolations from the verifier.

## Originality (PASS within available evidence)
Compared the precise closure formula, one-step index, and Arf classification against Shor's classification of reflective numerical semigroups, García-Sánchez et al.'s Arf parametrizations, İlhan–Karakaş's general closure algorithm, and Çelik's prime-multiplicity two-generated symmetric families. The originating reflective classification gives the input gap set but does not derive this Arf closure. Generic algorithms can compute instances but do not imply a stated uniform result. Çelik covers some particular two-generated reflective examples and is credited; those examples do not cover arbitrary multiplicity or the broad one-step statement. Searches for equivalent terminology, Arf closure, and reflective/equidistributed gaps yielded no identified dominating theorem. Undiscovered equivalents remain possible.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The Arf closure and Arf index are canonical invariants of numerical semigroups and of their singularity-theoretic applications. This theorem gives an explicit closure and exact quantitative genus/conductor changes for the entire classified reflective family and a sharp zero-versus-one stabilization dichotomy. It answers a natural compatibility question between a number-theoretic gap symmetry and Arf regularity, instead of choosing arbitrary parameter slices. The result relies on a relatively short constructive argument, but it settles an infinite structural classification rather than a finite textbook calculation.
