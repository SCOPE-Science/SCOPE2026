# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The conditional clock factorization and first-order Poisson expansion give the exact covariance coefficient with controlled \(O(r^2t^2)\) error. The far-target convexity criterion \(d_2^2\ge6Dt\) makes its single-reset response strictly positive. The near-target convolution bound and explicit nonempty small-distance condition make the coefficient strictly negative. Equal distances give a strictly positive conditional variance for every positive reset rate. Numerical checking is supplementary, not a replacement for the infinite-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS, with qualified residual risk.** The 2026 motivating paper covers rank-dependent reset optimization and acknowledges shared-clock dependence, but neither the heterogeneous cross-survival sign reversal nor the explicit sufficient regime appears in the inspected main text and methods. The 2023 global-reset analysis addresses first arrival of identically situated walkers. A related previously recorded result concerns covariance of full hitting times at equal distances, which does not imply the negative cross-survival indicator theorem at unequal distances. Exact first-order comparisons and searched aliases showed no stronger direct coverage. A broad common-environment association theorem might implicitly include the observation; independent evaluation remains important.

**Value — PASS.** Global resetting is used as a noninteracting baseline when interpreting rank-dependent multiparticle arrival experiments. This theorem provides a substantive counterboundary to the tempting inference that a common clock always induces positive survival association: the sign can reverse solely because searchers start at different distances. This has a concrete effect on joint nonarrival variance and cannot be represented by assuming homogeneous positive correlations. The sufficient regime need not be optimal or readily experimental.
