# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The edgewise target normalization matches equations (3)–(6) of the temporally extended continuous-time 3CM source. Class equitability gives a two-dimensional invariant manifold; direct substitution yields the same strictly contracting scalar opinion gap in the two snapshot types. The mean drift changes sign under snapshot reversal and has strictly decreasing magnitude because its magnitude is an increasing function of the positive gap. The measure-constrained integral rearrangement therefore uniquely optimizes every measurable schedule (modulo null sets). Integrating the exact differential of the logarithmic-cosh function produces the endpoints. The balanced terminal union preserves the mean while contracting the gap. The small-window coefficient is the negative initial derivative of the drift weight. The standard-library independent full-vertex program passed all 24 parameter/topology comparisons and three small-window checks. No numerical extrapolation is used as proof.

## Originality

PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty. The motivating 2021 paper already claims and illustrates a *qualitative* nonlinear first-mover effect; that is fully credited. Its detailed Sections 3–5 do not state the incidence-independent optimal chronological ordering among arbitrary equal-budget schedules, the sharp consensus interval, or the exact small-window coefficient. The 2020 model paper and 2021 chapter explain group reinforcement and clustered topology but not this deterministic temporal extremal statement. Shang's 2023 article treats different stochastic discrete-time dynamics. A 2022 general three-body conservation paper was available in bibliographic/abstract form but its full manuscript was inaccessible in the attempted access route, leaving specific residual priority risk. Research-result searches and own prior claims did not yield an implying theorem. They do not prove mathematical uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. The temporal-hypergraph literature reports order effects primarily numerically. This result identifies a sharp schedule choice and exact attainable consensus range under a natural fair-exposure constraint, independent of the detailed wiring of an entire family of equitable community networks. The strict quadratic small-window law quantifies the onset of the effect when interactions switch rapidly, and the long-window formula shows how strong reinforcement allows nearly complete first-mover dominance despite balanced populations and discussion budgets. The prerequisites are idealized and are stated explicitly; no wider demographic or psychological prediction is claimed.
