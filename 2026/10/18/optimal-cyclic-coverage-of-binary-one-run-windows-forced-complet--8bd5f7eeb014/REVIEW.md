# Scientific review — optimal cyclic coverage of one-run binary windows

## Correctness
**PASS.** The compulsory-edge degrees are explicit and exhaustive. Each negative source \(N_i\) in the mandatory de Bruijn graph requires at least \(i\) supplementary edges to reach any positive sink \(P_j\); nonnegative integral-flow decomposition makes this a lower bound on **all** cyclic hosts, including those using arbitrary forbidden windows. Equality forces the destination permutation \(j=i\) and the unique intervening appended-one path. The resulting edges give a balanced, strongly connected graph, hence a matching Eulerian construction. The inward-arborescence argument forces every \(N_i\) to append zero and leaves independent choices at each \(P_i\); the BEST theorem counts \(2^{k-2}\) rooted tours, with one designated all-zero edge per rotation class. The base case \(k=2\) is included. A standard-library verifier independently checks constructions through \(k=18\), matching optimization through \(k=14\), exact tour counts through \(k=11\), and complete short-host exhaustion through \(k=5\). Those tests corroborate the proof; they do not replace it.

## Originality
**PASS against the inspected literature; residual risk remains.** Moreno and Matamala's de Bruijn sequence construction assumes exact traversal of admissible edges rather than finding a minimum repeat-permitting cover of this linearly one-run compulsory family. Leitner–Godbole and Blanca–Godbole treat different families of restricted words, including weight restrictions; fixed weight and contiguous position restrictions have different logical content. Fixed-weight binary de Bruijn results do not provide this optimality and forced-completion theorem for every weight simultaneously. Published source texts and records were checked under these distinctions, alongside direct title/alias searches for one-run words, circular intervals and shortest cyclic superstrings. No source inspected supplies both the exact minimum and the \(2^{k-2}\) optimum-class enumeration. An unindexed equivalent formulation and literature inaccessible beyond abstract level remain risks; absence from searches alone is not claimed to certify novelty.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete classification of a natural periodic window-coverage problem. Beyond computing a few optima, it identifies a forced extra-word family, proves a sharp all-length minimum even under unrestricted hosts, and counts every optimal rotation class. The linear versus circular interval distinction explains why a superficially simpler exact universal-cycle formulation is not the same minimization. The exact multiplicity gives a nontrivial complete invariant of the optimal solution space.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest methodological papers are Moreno–Matamala, arXiv:0711.1695; Leitner–Godbole, arXiv:0808.1309; Blanca–Godbole, arXiv:1008.2251; and Ruskey–Sawada–Williams, DOI:10.1137/100808782 (only abstract/excerpt checked for the last). The theorem is binary, requires at most one linear run of ones in the compulsory words, and identifies covers only up to rotation. General multi-run forbidden languages, linear superstrings and reversals are not classified. Independent validation remains outside this review.
