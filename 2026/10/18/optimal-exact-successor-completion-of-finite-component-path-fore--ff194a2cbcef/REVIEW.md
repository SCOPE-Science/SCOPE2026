# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Every component of a functional, inverse-functional acyclic graph is a directed path of one of four possible order types. Prescribed adjacent pairs force each component to be convex in any completion. The exact crossing-edge condition uses the left endpoint's greatest element and the right endpoint's least element. Each gap between successive finite components with no double ray produces at least one extra edge, independently of the number of one-sided rays, while an explicit ordering makes this lower bound tight. A dense index order supplies a zero-defect completion for infinitely many components. Exhaustive symbolic checks on 714 multisets up to nine blocks corroborate the finite transition optimization. Proof rather than enumeration justifies the all-cardinality statement.

## Originality
PASS with acknowledged residual priority risk. The directly motivating 2026 paper proves the infinite-component dense-completion lemma, not the finite-component sharp defect formula. The 2016 order-invariance paper explicitly works over finite domains, where the finite-only specialization is elementary. The closest published research-record hits address quantifier-rank equivalence on directed finite paths or order-expansive chain relations, not exact successor completion of finitely many potentially infinite components. No direct covering statement was identified in the specified searches or accessible preceding claims. This does not prove global novelty; older order-theoretic literature may contain an equivalent reformulation.

## Value
PASS. Exact successor realization is a necessary semantic condition when adjacency predicates are required to arise from linear orders. The motivation paper relies on infinitely many components to obtain this property; the finding determines the complementary finite-component obstruction and computes the optimal repair cost, not just a yes/no test. The formula isolates the distinctive bridging effect of bi-infinite paths, and gives sharp small examples that rule out replacing them by arbitrary one-sided rays. It is an exact structural classification; no downstream complexity theorem is asserted.

## Limitations and closest sources
The theorem applies to one directed relation with no additional order-type restriction. Dense completion for infinitely many countable components is prior work and is explicitly credited. The full generality of simultaneous multi-successor constraints is outside scope. The 2016 finite-only setting overlaps solely with the specialization \(z=0\) and all components finite. Historical priority under other terminology is unresolved.
