# Scientific assessment of the corrected finding

## Correctness

The universal pair-premise lower bound and sufficiency of geodeticity hold. The converse does not: a crown with five vertices per shore has nonunique shortest paths and a 75-literal basis, meeting the universal lower bound. Validity of a conclusion means membership in the iterated geodesic hull, not necessarily the original pair's geodesic interval. The corrected cycle argument also accounts for rules with premises larger than two: a semicircle can only be violated by a premise containing its endpoints, so a separate four-literal lower bound can be charged to each antipodal pair without double counting. The constructed bases and their equality counts meet these bounds. The standard-library checker corroborates the constructions and minimizes over all valid rules for the three smallest cycles; it does not prove arbitrary sizes.

## Originality and value

The distinct contribution is the all-optima classification and count on geodetic graphs and cycles under the grouped total-literal objective, especially the antipodal cost and census for every even cycle. Existing hydra results apply when all designated pairs have full hulls, and together with the known convex-set result explain tightness in larger crowns; the crown is a corrective example, not the originality claim. The two-by-two Cartesian box is an overlapping small example under rule-count optimization, not a theorem on the total-literal optimum of all cycles. The earlier optimum-basis result for anti-exchange convex geometries does not cover the whole cycle family. These are bounded prior-work comparisons, not proof of priority.
