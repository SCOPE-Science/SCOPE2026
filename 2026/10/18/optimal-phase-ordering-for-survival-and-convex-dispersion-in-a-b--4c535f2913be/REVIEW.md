# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The generating-function identity is reconstructed from the first-order branching backward equation by the change of variable \(y=1/(1-f)\). The exchange calculation is exact for all positive rates and all finite positive phase lengths. The factor \(h_i h_j a_{\rm pre}>0\) proves the swap sign for any phase suffix and prefix. Sorting therefore globally minimizes/maximizes the only order-dependent PGF parameter. Zero-inflated-geometric coefficients and integer stop-loss transforms establish the distributional convex order; convolution extends to all positive initial founder counts. Exact-arithmetic verification (1,024 adjacent pairs, 360 multi-stage permutations, 3,917 assertions) and an independent finite-state uniformization calculation corroborate the formulas, not the unrestricted proof. Risk: extensions to nonlinear, interacting or stochastic environmental schedules are unproved.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty

Kendall (1948, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177730285) solved the general time-dependent process; that exact law, and therefore its linear-fractional form, are not novel. Paradis (2011, DOI 10.1111/j.1558-5646.2010.01179.x) uses the general law for stepwise speciation and extinction models; its full accessible article was inspected through its substantive model, inference and discussion sections. Its results do not give phase-permutation extrema or convex order. Crawford and Suchard (2012, DOI 10.1007/s00285-011-0471-z), inspected in accessible complete primary text, derive transition probabilities for general birth–death chains with different assumptions and objectives. Bhattacharya and Perlman (2017, arXiv:1703.00337) study conditional survival asymptotics rather than finite stage ordering. Targeted searches of scholarly resources and mathematical-result records for phase order, rearrangements, ratios, extinction probability and convex order did not identify an equivalent theorem; these searches are not a proof of novelty. The assessed contribution is a global explicit sorting rule with strict equality and all-convex-loss optimality, rather than rederivation of the marginal law. Risk: full historical Kendall text and some older related probability literature remained inaccessible; hidden equivalent statements or reductions are possible. External originality validation is absent.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The ecological decision problem is concrete: allocate a fixed inventory of good and bad environments over a finite planning horizon while preserving their rates and durations. Final mean is *identical for all schedules* and therefore cannot distinguish risk; nonetheless the exact example changes finite survival from \(2/7\) to \(4/9\), halves the final variance, and simultaneously minimizes every convex abundance penalty. The global optimal permutation requires only sorting biological rate ratios rather than exploring factorially many schedules. The strict exchange formula gives a full classification of equally good schedules. No downstream general network theorem or field validation is claimed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
