# Assessment of the optimal threshold-three activation comparison

## Correctness: PASS
Both graphs are explicitly presented on sixteen coordinates; each has degree six and common-neighbour number two for every distinct vertex pair. The complete candidate space consists of 560 triples and 1,820 four-subsets per graph. The independent `verify_forward.py` and `verify_peeling.cpp` calculations certify that no triple percolates, and that precisely 864 and 720 four-subsets do, respectively. The first uses synchronous activation; the second deletes low-degree inactive vertices in the complementary residual four-core. They reproduce every jumping/sliding transition, every component, every eccentricity, and each exact orbit/round count reported in the theorem. The certificate has full state and edge lists and explicit diameter paths; its construction is replayable from the embedded paths. Scope is precisely the two finite graphs at threshold three.

## Originality: PASS under available evidence
The rook minimum cardinality four is a covered special case of Bidgoli–Mohammadian–Tayfeh-Rezaie (2019, Theorem 2.1), and is expressly credited. The original exact contribution concerns both labeled minimum-family enumerations, the automorphism orbit decompositions, round distributions, and complete exchange-graph metric comparison. Guggiola–Semerjian (2014) provides general regular-graph motivation and the core-duality framework, but its methods and results do not give these specific finite tables. Searches for percolating/dynamic-monopoly/target-set synonyms, distinguishing strong-regular parameters, configurations and graph-exchange metrics did not locate covering work. The nearest comparable published mathematical records concern conversion sets of complete multipartite graphs or unrelated topological invariants of the Shrikhande/rook pair, neither of which implies this result. An unindexed computation or unpublished table could still coincide, and novelty has not been independently certified.

## Value: PASS
These are the canonical nonisomorphic graphs with identical strongly regular parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). Their common optimum seed cardinality hides a genuine discrepancy in the *number*, symmetry-orbit structure, infection time and exchange geometry of optimal choices. In particular, unrestricted one-seed swaps are always globally possible, yet all rook configurations are central while 288 Shrikhande configurations are peripheral. This is a natural, complete finite classification of the optimal configurations for threshold-three activation, not an arbitrary unmotivated subcase or a restatement of the known rook minimum. It may inform tests of which infection observables are determined by intersection parameters, but no stronger general implication is claimed.
