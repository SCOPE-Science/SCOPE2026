# Scientific review: order-type violation polynomial

## Correctness — PASS

A strictly order-preserving map of bounded Heyting chains that preserves endpoints respects min, max, and the Gödel residual. Hence each term equation's truth is determined by the full boundary-marked weak order of its input values. Exactly \(\binom{n-2}{d}\) tuples realize each configuration with \(d\) interior blocks; this proves the exact formula. The maximal-degree term is counted by strict interior permutations, giving the rational limit. Monotonicity of \(\binom{n-2}{k}/n^k\) on \(n\ge k+2\), explicit treatment of smaller chains, and direct-product multiplicativity complete the gap proof. Reproducible enumeration of 201 equations agrees with every displayed exact count. The tests alone do not prove the arbitrary-equation result.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Bumpus–Kocsis (arXiv:2110.11515, Proposition 1.14 and Section 6.6) establish the degree framework and multiplicativity and propose studying restricted Gödel settings; their one-variable classification over *all* Heyting algebras is not claimed as new. Fermüller–Lang–Pavlova (DOI:10.1007/s11787-022-00300-0, Sections 1, 3–6) explain order-only Gödel semantics but do not enumerate all equation violations or classify finite satisfiability gaps. The examined primary texts do not assert the binomial-basis spectrum, universal rational limit, or multi-variable all-equation gap trichotomy on chain products. The central semantic lemma is known, so these consequences have a residual risk of prior equivalent presentation in enumerative fuzzy-logic literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact nonnegative coefficient vector simultaneously describes every chain size, distinguishes boundary/tie-only counterexamples from persistent generic ones, and decides finite-gap existence for arbitrary-arity equations from one bounded chain. This is an explicit all-equation classification on the natural chain-product subclass of Gödel logic, addressing a restricted form of a problem motivating published degree-of-satisfiability research. It is more than checking a single identity or a few small chains. No classification of all finite Gödel algebras or sharp optimal gaps is claimed.
