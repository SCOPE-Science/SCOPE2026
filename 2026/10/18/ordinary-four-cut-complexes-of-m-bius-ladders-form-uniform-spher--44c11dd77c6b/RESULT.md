# Ordinary four-cut complexes of Möbius ladders form uniform sphere wedges

## Finding
Let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\) and edges joining each \(i\) to \(i+1\) and to \(i+n\). For every integer \(n\geq5\), its **ordinary** four-cut complex satisfies
\[
\Delta_4(M_{2n})\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{2n-1}{3}-11n} S^{2n-5}.
\]
For example, the first three multiplicities, at \(n=5,6,7\), are \(29,99,209\). This statement does not concern the total four-cut complex.

## Assumptions and scope
Throughout, vertices and subscripts are interpreted modulo \(N=2n\), and \(n\ge5\). For a finite graph \(G\) with \(N\) vertices, the **ordinary** \(k\)-cut complex \(\Delta_k(G)\) is generated by the complements of \(k\)-element vertex sets inducing **disconnected** subgraphs. Its combinatorial Alexander dual \(L=\Delta_k(G)^\vee\) has as its faces precisely the vertex sets all of whose \(k\)-subsets induce connected subgraphs (the condition is vacuous below size \(k\)). No assumption of shellability is made.

## Proof
**1. All connected induced four-sets.** The cubic graph \(M_{2n}\) is triangle-free. Any connected induced subgraph on four vertices is a three-edge path, a claw, or a four-cycle. In this ladder, all such sets belong to the following six explicitly indexed families (with set braces disregarding order):
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_i&=\{i-1,i,i+1,i+n\},\\
R_i&=\{i,i+1,i+2,i+3\},\\
E_i^-&=\{i,i+1,i+2,i+n\},\\
E_i^+&=\{i,i+1,i+2,i+n+2\},\\
M_i&=\{i,i+1,i+n+1,i+n+2\},\\
Q_i&=\{i,i+1,i+n,i+n+1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(i\) ranges over \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\) in the first five families and over \(0,\dots,n-1\) in the last. To see exhaustiveness without relying on enumeration: a four-cycle necessarily alternates two adjacent rim positions and their opposite vertices, giving \(Q\); a claw uses all three neighbors of its center, giving \(S\); a four-vertex path must contain either three rim edges (\(R\)) or one or two spokes. Two spokes force the closing fourth edge of a square unless a vertex is repeated. With exactly one spoke, two consecutive rim edges on one side give \(E^-\) or \(E^+\), while one rim edge on each side gives \(M\). For \(n\ge5\), the listed sets have no additional edges, and no two entries coincide apart from \(Q_{i+n}=Q_i\). Their respective cardinalities are \(2n,2n,2n,2n,2n,n\), hence exactly \(11n\) connected four-sets.

**2. No five-vertex face in the dual.** If an induced five-vertex graph had every four-vertex vertex-deletion connected, it would be 2-vertex-connected. A triangle-free, 2-connected graph on five vertices contains a four- or five-cycle. Here a five-cycle cannot occur: a length-five closed walk with \(r\) spokes and \(5-r\) rim edges has net displacement \(rn+t\) modulo \(2n\), where \(t\) is the sum of \(5-r\) signs \(\pm1\). For even \(r\), the odd number \(5-r\) of rim steps gives odd nonzero \(t\) with \(|t|\le5<2n\); for odd \(r\), \(rn\equiv n\pmod{2n}\) but \(|t|\le4<n\). Neither case closes. Thus the induced graph contains a four-cycle. The fifth vertex must meet the four-cycle in at least two vertices; triangle-freeness forces these two vertices to be opposite on the square, forming an induced \(K_{2,3}\) subgraph. But in \(M_{2n}\) no pair of vertices has three common neighbors: each vertex's neighbors are \(\{i-1,i+1,i+n\}\), and these triples are distinct for \(n\ge5\). Contradiction. Therefore \(L\) has no faces of size five or larger.

Consequently \(L\) consists of the complete two-skeleton on \(N\) vertices plus the \(11n\) tetrahedra of Step 1.

**3. Explicit \(11n\) elementary simplicial collapses.** Delete tetrahedra in the order \(S\), \(R\), \(E^-\), \(E^+\), \(M\), \(Q\), pairing each with the following triangular face of the same index:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{tetrahedron}&\text{free triangle}\;\\ \hline
S_i&\{i-1,i+1,i+n\}\\
R_i&\{i,i+1,i+3\}\\
E_i^-&\{i,i+2,i+n\}\\
E_i^+&\{i,i+2,i+n+2\}\\
M_i&\{i,i+1,i+n+2\}\\
Q_i&\{i,i+1,i+n\}.
\end{array}
\]
Each face is contained in its displayed tetrahedron; checking adjacency among a possible fourth vertex shows that the first four triangles occur in no other remaining tetrahedron. A triangle from the \(M\) row can additionally occur in \(E_i^+\), which has already been removed. A triangle from \(Q\) can occur in earlier families but is contained in no other \(Q\)-tetrahedron. Therefore each indicated triangle has exactly one remaining maximal coface when removed, and the collapses are valid. Different indices within the same family can be processed in any order. These operations leave a two-dimensional complex \(L_0\) with \(N\) vertices, \(\binom N2\) edges, and \(\binom N3-11n\) triangles.

**4. Homotopy type of the dual.** The complete two-skeleton of a simplex is simply connected. Attaching three-simplices cannot change its fundamental group, and the collapses preserve homotopy, so \(L_0\) is simply connected. As a finite two-dimensional complex with \(H_1=0\) and torsion-free top homology, it is a wedge of two-spheres, of multiplicity \(\chi(L_0)-1\), namely
\[
\chi(L_0)-1=N-\binom N2+\binom N3-11n-1
=\binom{N-1}{3}-11n.
\]
The simple-connectivity conclusion and a choice of integral \(H_2\)-basis give the wedge equivalence by Hurewicz and Whitehead.

**5. Recover the ordinary four-cut complex.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_4(M_{2n});\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(L;\mathbb Z),
\]
so its sole nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{\binom{N-1}{3}-11n}\) in degree \(N-5\). Furthermore, because \(L\) has no five-vertex face, \(\Delta_4(M_{2n})\) contains the entire \((N-6)\)-skeleton of the simplex, and all its maximal faces have dimension \(N-5\). It is therefore \((N-7)\)-connected, and the vanishing of \(H_{N-6}\) upgrades this to \((N-6)\)-connectivity by Hurewicz. For \(N\ge10\), representing a free basis of top homology by sphere maps produces a homology isomorphism from a wedge of \((N-5)\)-spheres. The homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes that map a homotopy equivalence, proving the claim.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3, standard library only. It reconstructs all connected four-sets for \(5\le n\le12\), checks the claimed six-family census and every free-face removal by recomputing tetrahedron coface incidence, exhaustively verifies nonexistence of five-faces for \(5\le n\le10\), and checks the lower skeleton boundary rank over four finite fields for \(n=5,6,7\). The checker gives dual tetrahedra and collapse counts \(55,66,77,88,99,110,121,132\), and first three primal facet/top-rank pairs \((155,29),(429,99),(924,209)\). These finite tests support but do **not** replace the all-\(n\) combinatorial argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al. introduced ordinary cut complexes, their Alexander dual approach, and broad structural results [1]; their sequel treats squared paths and ordinary rectangular grids [2]. Agarwal and Basak studied independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius and circular ladders and posed computation of cut/total-cut complexes of Möbius ladders as a further direction [3]. Existing results for total three-cut complexes of Möbius ladders do not imply this ordinary four-cut theorem: both the connectivity condition and face dimensions differ. This proof gives a uniform topological classification, not merely a face-count or rational-homology formula.

## Limitations
The proof applies to **ordinary** four-cut complexes of \(M_{2n}\) for integers \(n\ge5\); it makes no claim for smaller ladders, total-cut complexes, different cut parameters, equivariant homology, shellability, or other graph families. The independent literary originality assessment remains incomplete for unindexed and inaccessible sources. Finite computational checks are not a proof beyond their enumerated ranges.
