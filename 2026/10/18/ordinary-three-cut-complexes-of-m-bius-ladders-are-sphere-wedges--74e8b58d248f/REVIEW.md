# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
A graph-theoretic classification shows that the Alexander dual consists of a complete one-skeleton, \(6n\) path triangles and exactly \(n\) square tetrahedra, with no higher faces. Explicit free-face pairs collapse it in \(6n\) steps to a connected graph on \(2n\) vertices with \(\binom{2n}{2}-5n\) edges. Its integral first homology has rank \(2n^2-8n+1\). Alexander duality plus the full lower skeleton and Hurewicz/Whitehead converts this to the precise wedge-of-spheres **homotopy type**. A standalone verifier independently replays all collapses for \(4\le n\le40\) and cut-complex boundary ranks for \(n=4,5,6\). The finite test is not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risk
The closest full primary article by Agarwal–Basak (arXiv:2608.30601v2, nineteen pages inspected) proves independence and perfect-matching results and explicitly lists the cut-complex problem as future work in Section 6. The closely related published ordinary three-cut result for circular ladders has exactly the same numerical formula but a different graph family. The edge-disjoint-square general theorem excludes Möbius ladders: consecutive squares share spokes. The high-girth theorem excludes their four-cycles. Published records for Möbius-ladder **total** three-cuts use a different complex. A broader unindexed theorem or a later unreviewed derivation remains a priority risk, not a proof of duplication.
