# Ordinary three-cut complexes of Möbius ladders are wedges of top-dimensional spheres

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder whose vertices are \(0,1,\ldots,2n-1\), with edges between consecutive vertices modulo \(2n\) and between each pair of opposite vertices. Its **ordinary three-cut complex** \(\Delta_3(M_{2n})\), generated by the complements of disconnected induced triples, has homotopy type

\[
\Delta_3(M_{2n})\simeq \bigvee^{2n(n-4)+1}S^{2n-4}.
\]

The sphere multiplicities for \(n=4,5,6,7,8\) are respectively \(1,11,25,43,65\). In particular, the reduced integral homology is free of rank \(2n(n-4)+1\) in degree \(2n-4\), and vanishes in all other degrees.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite and simple; all (co)homology groups in the proof have integral coefficients. The *ordinary* three-cut complex \(K=\Delta_3(G)\) has facets \(V(G)\setminus T\) as \(T\) ranges over three-vertex subsets for which \(G[T]\) is disconnected. This is not the total three-cut complex. A face \(F\) belongs to \(K\) exactly when \(V(G)\setminus F\) contains such a triple. The theorem covers every integer \(n\ge4\), including even and odd parameters, but makes no assertion for smaller degenerate ladders or larger cut parameters.

## Proof

Write \(N=2n\), \(V=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), and \(K=\Delta_3(M_{2n})\). The combinatorial Alexander dual is

\[
D=K^{\vee}=\{A\subseteq V: \text{every three-element subset of }A\text{ induces a connected graph}\}.
\]

We determine its homotopy type by elementary simplicial collapses.

**Step 1: Classify all faces of the dual.** The Möbius ladder is triangle-free when \(n\ge4\). Every connected induced triple is therefore an induced path on three vertices. There are three choices of two neighbors at each of the \(2n\) central vertices, for \(6n\) triangular faces of \(D\). Of these, exactly \(2n\) use the two outer-cycle edges at their central vertex (call them straight triangles). The other \(4n\) lie in the four-cycles

\[
Q_i=\{i,i+1,i+n,i+n+1\}\pmod{2n},\qquad 0\le i<n.
\]

These \(n\) four-cycles are all the four-cycles of the graph: a four-cycle cannot use no rungs, since the outer cycle has length at least eight; it cannot use four rungs, since rungs form a matching; and using two rungs forces neighboring endpoints along the outer cycle. Every four-subset whose triples all induce connected graphs must induce a four-cycle: in its induced graph each vertex is incident to at least two edges, while triangle-freeness forces degree at most two. Consequently the **only tetrahedral faces** of \(D\) are the \(n\) sets \(Q_i\). No five-subset is a face: if \(u,v\) are nonadjacent vertices of such a subset, each of its other three vertices would have to be a common neighbor of \(u,v\), whereas distinct nonadjacent vertices of \(M_{2n}\) have at most two common neighbors (directly from the neighborhoods \(\{i-1,i+1,i+n\}\)). Larger faces are therefore also impossible. The entire one-skeleton of \(D\) is complete, because the three-subset condition is vacuous for subsets of size at most two.

**Step 2: Collapse to a Möbius strip with extra chords.** Every straight triangle has a unique edge joining the endpoints at outer-cycle distance two; this diagonal occurs in no other triangle for \(n\ge4\). Collapse each straight triangle across its free diagonal, removing \(2n\) triangles and their \(2n\) diagonals. The remaining \(4n\) triangles are the four triangular facets of the \(n\) tetrahedra supported on the four-cycle vertex sets \(Q_i\). Their interiors and triangular faces do not overlap between distinct squares. For each \(Q_i\), collapse its tetrahedron across one triangle, then collapse a second triangle across the now-free diagonal joining a pair of opposite square vertices. The two other triangular faces form one triangulated square disk, with the other diagonal retained. These \(n\) square disks meet along the rung edges and together form the standard rectangular band with a half-twist: a Möbius strip \(B\), bounded by the outer \(2n\)-cycle. Thus \(B\simeq S^1\).

All remaining one-simplices outside \(B\) are isolated chords attached at their endpoints. The original complete one-skeleton had \(\binom{2n}{2}\) edges. The preceding collapses removed \(2n\) straight diagonals and \(n\) square diagonals; \(B\) has \(2n\) perimeter edges, \(n\) rung edges, and \(n\) surviving square diagonals. Hence the number of additional chords is

\[
\binom{2n}{2}-2n-n-(2n+n+n)=2n(n-4).
\]

Attaching an edge with both endpoints in a connected space adds one free circle up to homotopy. Therefore

\[
D\simeq B\vee\bigvee^{2n(n-4)}S^1
\simeq \bigvee^{2n(n-4)+1}S^1.
\]

**Step 3: Recover the homotopy type of the cut complex.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the \(N\)-vertex simplex gives

\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(D;\mathbb Z).
\]

Thus the only nonzero reduced integral homology of \(K\) lies in degree \(N-4\), with free rank \(2n(n-4)+1\). Since \(D\) has no faces with five vertices, every \(F\subseteq V\) with at most \(N-5\) vertices belongs to \(K\). Hence \(K\) contains the complete \((N-6)\)-skeleton, making it \((N-7)\)-connected; in particular it is simply connected, as \(N\ge8\). Its vanishing lower homology then implies, by successive applications of the Hurewicz theorem, that \(K\) is \((N-5)\)-connected. Finally, \(K\) has dimension \(N-4\). Choosing maps representing a basis of \(\pi_{N-4}(K)\cong H_{N-4}(K;\mathbb Z)\), their wedge produces a homology isomorphism from \(\bigvee^{2n(n-4)+1}S^{N-4}\) to \(K\). The simply connected Whitehead theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. This proves the assertion for all \(n\ge4\).

## Verification

The included `verify.py` is a standalone Python 3 (standard-library only) checker. It independently enumerates all three- and four-element faces of the Alexander dual, excludes five-element faces, and computes exact \(\mathbb F_2\) boundary ranks for every \(4\le n\le10\). It also enumerates all faces of the original cut complex for \(4\le n\le8\), constructs a lexicographic discrete-Morse matching, checks the directed Hasse graph is acyclic, and checks the unique integral lower-to-upper critical-cell attaching row has gcd one. The latter verification gives a separate finite homotopy-type check in all five cases. Execute `python3 verify.py`; a successful complete run prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the parameter-uniform proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram, and Xue introduced ordinary cut complexes and determined their homotopy types for several graph classes (`arXiv:2304.13675v2`). Their Theorem 6.1 gives conditional homology information when the full codimension-one skeleton occurs. That hypothesis fails here: the dual has four-vertex tetrahedral faces, so the cut complex omits their complements in codimension one. Their explicit prism theorem concerns a clique prism, rather than the cubic Möbius ladders. Neither result yields the claimed formula.

Agarwal and Basak studied independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius and circular ladder graphs (`arXiv:2608.30601v1,v2`) and identify other associated complexes, including cut complexes, as a future direction (version 2, Problem 26). Those distinct complexes do not determine \(\Delta_3(M_{2n})\). Recent work on cut complexes of powers of ordinary cycles (`arXiv:2603.23155`) concerns a different graph family; a Möbius ladder is not such a cycle power for the full range covered here. No mathematical priority claim beyond the stated inspected sources is implied.

## Limitations

The proof concerns the ordinary, not total, three-cut complex. It does not classify any \(k\)-cut complex with \(k>3\), the equivariant action on homology, or all cubic graphs. Originality remains conditional on the indexed and inspected literature; unindexed or unpublished equivalent work could exist. The full homotopy statement is proved by the general collapse and duality argument, not inferred from finite computational samples. Independent external validation has not been performed.
