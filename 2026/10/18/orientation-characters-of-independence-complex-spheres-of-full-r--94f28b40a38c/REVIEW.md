# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement applies to all integers \(b\ge2\), \(h\ge0\), with one ordinary reduced integral homology group when \(h\not\equiv0\pmod3\). The forest sphere-or-point dichotomy is provided by Ehrenborg and Hetyei [1, Corollary 6.1], while Marietti and Testa [2, Section 4.2] identify the spherical degree with domination number minus one. The recurrence for the signed independence polynomial is exact and has a complete finite-state cycle for each parity of \(b\). A dominating set and a packing of pairwise disjoint closed neighborhoods establish the claimed dimension without relying on an experimental guess. The reduced Hopf trace with the empty-face term yields the orientation quotient formula. Every tree automorphism is a product of local branch swaps, whose signed fixed-face sums and induced permutation signs agree by an exact state-pair calculation. These steps prove the whole infinite family and handle the contractible and one-vertex boundary cases. Two independently constructed finite checkers corroborated 2,700 recurrence/generator instances and 17 local swaps on explicitly built simplicial complexes; they are supporting checks, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, residual search risk

Direct comparison with full texts of [1] and [2] and the full 24-page preprint of [3] establishes substantial prior nonequivariant coverage. In particular, [3, Theorem 5.6], for the ordinary independence parameter, encompasses the sphere-or-point homotopy type for perfect \(m\)-ary trees; no such homotopy classification is claimed new. The reviewed sources do not present the action of their rooted automorphism groups on integral homology. Such an action cannot be deduced merely from the abstract equivalence to a sphere or knowledge of its dimension: the trivial and sign actions on rank-one homology have identical ordinary Betti numbers. The new formula computes the action for every group element. Focused searches for wreath-product orientation representations and signed fixed-face characters did not identify an equivalent result. An inaccessible full text [4] and potentially unindexed equivariant results remain genuine risks, not certified absence.

## Value — PASS

Perfect rooted trees have large canonical symmetry groups (iterated wreath products), whereas their ordinary independence complexes are either contractible or singly spherical. The exact integral homology character is a well-defined equivariant topological invariant that distinguishes orientation-reversing symmetries, has a closed formula for all tree heights and branching numbers, and is nontrivial at all spherical heights at least two. It refines an established topological classification by a whole-family symmetry invariant rather than altering a toy example, renaming a parameter, or recomputing the known sphere dimension. The result does not claim an equivariant homotopy classification.

## Limitations and relevant literature

Originality is conditional on the inspected primary literature and indexed searches; unindexed work remains possible. The full text of Kawamura's paper was unavailable, and the abstract does not determine any equivariant character. The provided computational scripts are finite checks, not an independent audit. The theorem concerns root-preserving automorphisms of perfect trees, not arbitrary forest symmetries.
