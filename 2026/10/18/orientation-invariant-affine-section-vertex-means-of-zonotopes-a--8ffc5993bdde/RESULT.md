# Orientation-invariant affine-section vertex means of zonotopes: an exact zone-balance criterion

## Finding
Let \(d\ge3\), and let \(Z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[0,v_i]\subset\mathbb R^d\) be full-dimensional, with all \(v_i\ne0\) and no two parallel. Let \(r_i\) be the number of edges of \(Z\) parallel to \(v_i\); equivalently, the number of chambers of the central arrangement on \(v_i^\perp\) given by \(w\cdot v_j=0\) for \(j\ne i\). For a prescribed unit vector \(n\), sample an affine hyperplane \(\{x:n\cdot x=t\}\), where \(t\) is uniform over the complete interval of offsets that intersect \(Z\). Write \(N(n,t)\) for its number of section vertices, ignoring a measure-zero set of exceptional offsets. Then
\[
\mathbb E_t N(n,t)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_i|n\cdot v_i|}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|n\cdot v_i|}.
\]
The mean is independent of the orientation \(n\) **if and only if** \(r_1=\cdots=r_m\). If every subset of \(d\) generators is linearly independent, then every \(r_i=2\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\binom{m-2}{j}\), so this common number is the orientation-independent mean. In three dimensions, \(r_i=2k_i\), where \(k_i\) counts distinct two-dimensional spans \(\operatorname{span}(v_i,v_j)\) over \(j\ne i\); equality of these counts precisely characterizes all orientation-independent cases, including non-generic ones.

## Assumptions and scope
All vectors are real Euclidean vectors, \(d\ge3\), the generators span \(\mathbb R^d\), and generator directions are distinct up to sign. Positive rescaling of an individual generator does not change its zone count. The random offset is **conditioned on intersection** for each fixed normal: it is uniform on \([\min_{x\in Z}n\cdot x,\max_{x\in Z}n\cdot x]\). This is not central-hyperplane sampling and is not an unconditional fixed offset law. The full interval has length \(W(n)=\sum_i|n\cdot v_i|>0\). For almost every offset, the slice avoids all vertices of \(Z\); genericity of the normal itself is not required. At exceptional offsets, the section vertex number may change, but this does not affect the integral.

## Proof
**1. Edge-zone correspondence.** Fix \(i\). A linear functional \(w\in v_i^\perp\) outside all hyperplanes \(w\cdot v_j=0\), \(j\ne i\), exposes the segment \([0,v_i]\) translated by the sum of those \(v_j\) having \(w\cdot v_j>0\). Since no other generator is parallel to \(v_i\), this is a one-dimensional exposed edge. Conversely, every edge parallel to \(v_i\) is obtained by such a functional, by choosing an interior normal within its normal cone. A chamber fixes every sign \(\operatorname{sgn}(w\cdot v_j)\); two distinct chambers cannot expose the same edge, because at an exposing normal changing an optimizing generator endpoint would strictly decrease the support value. Hence the number of edges parallel to \(v_i\) is exactly \(r_i\), the chamber count.

**2. Integral of section vertices.** Almost every intersecting hyperplane avoids the finitely many polytope vertices. Its section vertices are exactly its transverse intersections with edges; any edge parallel to the hyperplane is intersected only on an exceptional offset. The length of the interval of offsets meeting an edge that is a translate of \([0,v_i]\) is \(|n\cdot v_i|\). Integrating the number of crossed edges over the full offset range and exchanging a finite sum with an integral gives \(\int N(n,t)\,dt=\sum_i r_i|n\cdot v_i|\). Division by the offset interval length \(W(n)\) gives the claimed exact expectation.

**3. Necessity of zone balance.** If the expectation is a constant \(c\) for almost every orientation, continuity and homogeneity imply \(\sum_i(r_i-c)|x\cdot v_i|=0\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\). Fix \(i\). Since \(d\ge3\) and no other \(v_j\) is parallel to \(v_i\), one can choose \(x_0\in v_i^\perp\) with \(x_0\cdot v_j\ne0\) for every \(j\ne i\). Across \(x_0+s v_i\), all terms but the \(i\)th have identical left and right derivatives at \(s=0\); the \(i\)th term has a derivative jump \(2(r_i-c)\|v_i\|^2\). The identity forces \(r_i=c\). This holds for every \(i\). The converse follows directly from the expectation formula.

**4. Generic and three-dimensional counts.** Under general linear position, the restricted hyperplanes in \(v_i^\perp\) form a central arrangement of \(m-1\) hyperplanes in dimension \(d-1\) in general position. Its region count is \(2\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\binom{m-2}{j}\), by the standard add-one-hyperplane recurrence. In \(d=3\), the restricted arrangement is a set of \(k_i\) distinct lines through the origin in a two-plane; it has exactly \(2k_i\) sectors.

**5. Substantive degeneracy boundary.** Four generic generators in three dimensions yield constant mean six. A balanced but non-generic example has the six generators
\[
(1,1,0),\;(1,2,0),\;(2,3,0),\;(1,0,1),\;(1,0,2),\;(2,0,3).
\]
For every generator exactly one other generator direction shares its two-dimensional span and the remaining three give distinct mixed spans, hence every \(k_i=4\); its conditional mean is eight for **all** orientations. By contrast, the three planar generators \((1,1,0),(1,2,0),(2,3,0)\) together with \((0,0,1)\) give \((r_i)=(4,4,4,6)\), and their mean varies from four to six with the normal. Thus general position is sufficient but not necessary, while zonotopality alone is insufficient.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_zonotope.py` enumerates exact integer subset sums and polyhedral edges for the three displayed configurations, classifies the edges by generator zone, and compares the claimed mean against a separate sum of actual edge crossings on every open interval between projected vertex offsets. All twenty-one tested orientation cases use exact rational weighting for the final integral, and six general-position region-count computations are checked. Running `python3 verify_zonotope.py` with NumPy and SciPy produced `ZONOTOPE_OFFSET_MEAN_OK cases=3 normals=21 generic_region_checks=6`. The analytic argument, not finite testing, proves the all-dimensional statement.

## Relationship to prior work
The combinatorial relation between zonotope faces, generator zones and arrangements is classical: Shephard (1974) treats zonotope zones and face counts; Holtz and Ron (first public 2007) develop the associated hyperplane arrangement and zonotopal algebra. Their structure results are antecedents, **not claimed here as new**. Swan's published hypercube-slice mean theorem gives \(2^{d-1}\) for all orientations and notes extension to arbitrary parallelotopes; its proof exploits equal multiplicities of congruent coordinate faces. The present assertion is the exact if-and-only-if characterization for **all** zonotopes, including dependent generators and non-generic balanced examples; Swan's parallelotope result is a covered special case. No claim is made to newness of individual classical combinatorial lemmas or the line-intersection integration method.

## Limitations
Uniformity means uniform **conditional offset for each orientation**; a different sampling law can change the expectation. The criterion assumes pairwise nonparallel generators; parallel generators must first be combined. The formula counts vertices of generic affine hyperplane sections, not higher-codimension section faces. The finite script proves only its tested instances. Potential unindexed historical formulations of this characterization are a priority risk. Independent verification has not been performed.
