# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Exact formulas recover the labeled plant-neighborhood sizes and pairwise intersections from individual and joint extinction laws. For three or fewer plants, those quantities plus the pollinator-degree multiset determine the full incidence multiset. For all guild splits through eight total vertices, the included reproducible checker exhausts all nonzero binary incidence rows, checks graph connectivity, and compares the exact maximum-matching cardinality-sum vectors within every matching signature. The nine-species witness was separately checked across all 32 survival subsets. Its positive polynomial gap follows by explicit factorization; no extrapolation from finite enumeration is used.

## Originality

**PASS under best-knowledge comparison, with residual bibliographic risk.** The full Memmott–Waser–Price (2004) article models pollinator removals and counts secondary plant losses, not constrained maximum matching. The full available Bock–Rautenbach (2018) manuscript gives degree-sequence restrictions on static matching sizes, not pairwise-loss-identifiable survival rank profiles. Devoto et al. (2012) supplies functional-restoration motivation but the complete article was not inspected. Relevant indexed research comparisons did not establish this exact nine-species threshold. Existing work in transversal matroid rank reliability and unexamined ecological supplementary materials remains a possible source of an equivalent formulation. No absence-of-search-result is treated as proof of uniqueness or novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result sharpens the question of what low-order species-loss statistics can identify: every labeled plant has the same marginal extinction law, every labeled plant pair has the same joint law, every corresponding pollinator has the same degree, and both intact networks have the same maximal assignment capacity. Nevertheless seven identified pollinator survival outcomes make one network strictly better, so low-order extinction statistics do not identify expected one-to-one service capacity. The complete minimal size and exact positive gap form a reproducible finite boundary, rather than a cosmetic parameter change.

## Scope and risks

The nine-species lower bound assumes connected binary bipartite networks, independent equal-probability pollinator deletion, and full post-loss reassignment. An individual pollinator is artificially capacity-limited to one plant per allocation period. No ecological population survival or field prediction is implied. The equivalent-statements literature search cannot exclude every prior theorem. The results are not independently audited.
