# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Two disjoint rare-event corner wedges have independently calculated leading area, yielding the conditional density \(2v\). A short exact branch reduction sends both wedges to \((u,u-d)\), with \(u=1+O(d)\); the ceiling formula for the *first* failure of \(\kappa=2\) proves a uniform \(1/(2d)+O(1)\) exit time. A pathwise uniform approximation and a continuous-mapping argument give the claimed random trajectory. Exact-rational replay confirms the delicate strict branch boundary in sampled instances but is not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Athreya--Cheung (arXiv:1206.6597v1, Lemma 5.3) already prove that a large index forces many nearby index-two steps; the new statement calculates the exact first exit from that branch *conditioned on a large roof*, and its Pareto distribution. Classical fixed-dimensional Farey spacing laws do not handle observation indices proportional to the conditioning threshold. The 2026 BCZ strong-mixing theorem does not imply the moving-rare-event conditional limit. The 2001 original texts were not both fully accessible; an undiscovered equivalent conditional theorem remains a genuine bibliographic risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The first branch-exit clock is a natural renewal-scale statistic of the horocycle section. Its sharp Pareto law and the associated random macroscopic relaxation profile reveal quantitative structure invisible to fixed-block spacing laws, and explain why rare roof events impose a long deterministic refractory interval.

**Limits.** No finite-Farey-order uniformity, no postcritical orbit profile, no extremal point-process law and no independent priority validation are asserted.
