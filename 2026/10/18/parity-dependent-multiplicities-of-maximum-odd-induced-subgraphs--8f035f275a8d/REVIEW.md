# Scientific review: exact multiplicities of maximum odd induced rook subgraphs

## Correctness

PASS. With the maximum cardinality already public, the new part classifies every deletion pattern of minimum size. The untouched-row/untouched-column argument forces one omission per row on rectangles and one row- or column-covering family on squares. Row-count parity then forces all even column fibres when the shorter dimension is even, and a single full deleted column when it is odd. A permutation-matrix argument proves the two square families disjoint except on the one-cell board. The standard even-fibre generating function counts labelled deletions exactly. An independent exhaustive vertex-set checker matched all maximizing subsets (not just totals) on seventeen small graphs.

## Originality

PASS under accessible evidence, with residual full-text risk. The exact scalar maximum is **already known** from the June 24, 2026 Mathematics Stack Exchange discussion and is credited. That public discussion does not determine the equality cases or their number. The full accessible text of Ai et al. (arXiv:2511.15489) establishes a bounded-degree lower bound, not this equality classification. Recent papers on odd cuts and arbitrary parity prescriptions have broader objectives; only their abstracts were accessible, so their full-text contents are a residual bibliographic risk. Targeted mathematical-record searches on odd induced rook subgraphs, line-graph equivalents, counts and even-fibre formulas, plus available published records and local claims, found no covering multiplicity statement. Search failure by itself is not proof of novelty.

## Value

PASS. A previously settled extremal size does not determine its optimizer space. The new classification reveals a genuine parity bifurcation: for fixed odd shorter dimension, only the choice of a deleted column matters; for fixed even shorter dimension, arbitrarily many even-fibre assignments appear and grow at degree half the width. The exact count of all labelled optimal induced graphs is a natural structural completion of the known rook-graph extremal problem and has a direct combinatorial description.

## Closest literature, scope and risks

Dean and Earnest (2026) cover only the optimum **value**. Ai et al. (2025) treat degree-bounded lower bounds, while Zeng (2026) and Guo et al. (2026) study general existence and lower bounds. The two latter full texts could not be materially inspected and should be included in a subsequent independent bibliographic check. The theorem makes no claim about non-optimal sets or any graph beyond rook graphs.
