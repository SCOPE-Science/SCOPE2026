Greedy dimer adsorption across a single bridge has an exact parity-inverted jamming law: faster bridge deposition damages final coverage between two even cliques but improves it between two odd cliques. For proportionally growing dense communities, the competition has a critical bridge-rate scale proportional to clique order, an explicit crossover profile, and the fixed-rate coefficient \(\log\rho/(\rho-1)\) (continuously equal to one when \(\rho=1\)).
