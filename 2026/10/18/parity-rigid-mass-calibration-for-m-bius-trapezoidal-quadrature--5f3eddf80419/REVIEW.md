# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The complete domain includes every finite positive centered-Cauchy mixture, each integer node count, positive Möbius scale, and the explicit continuous endpoint extension. Direct conversion to a periodic Poisson kernel and absolutely convergent aliasing gives the mass formula. Monotonicity proves odd-node existence and uniqueness, and a factored positive second derivative proves strict logarithmic convexity for even nodes. Symmetry yields the sharp two-scale minimizer and a controlled root expansion yields the odd-node displacement. The included checker replayed successfully with 277 symbolic and numeric tests, but the universal claims rest on the analytical derivation.

**Originality — PASS with stated residual risk.** The nearest primary paper establishes the transformed quadrature for Schwartz weights and its convergence estimates; its required endpoint value vanishes, whereas Cauchy mixtures have a nonzero continuous endpoint. Classical Fourier aliasing and Poisson expansions cover the mechanism, not the presently stated full mixture parity/convexity/unique-calibration classification. Targeted literature searches and a comparison with earlier mathematical claims did not locate a statement covering the combined theorem. A specialist quadrature result could still imply some or all of these consequences without matching terminology; independent priority evaluation remains open.

**Value — PASS.** Cauchy mixtures are natural algebraically tailed probability weights and stress precisely the endpoint assumption in the motivating quadrature rule. The theorem resolves a finite-node mass-conservation design problem completely, exhibiting a parity obstruction to positive scale tuning, proving a unique best even-node scale and supplying an exact unavoidable bias for a two-width benchmark. It is not presented as a general-purpose accuracy improvement, and renormalizing weights remains possible.

**Closest literature and scope.** Suzuki–Hyvönen–Karvonen, arXiv:2407.13650, gives the transformation and quadrature in a Schwartz-weight context. Trefethen–Weideman, DOI:10.1137/130932132, gives classical Fourier aliasing. The new derivation does not assert general observable-error bounds, a Schwartz-theory extension, or independence of external review. Its Cauchy endpoint convention and unshifted-grid assumption are necessary.
