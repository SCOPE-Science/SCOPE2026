# Parity-sensitive square-zero image spectra for \(C_p\oplus C_{p^b}\)

## Finding

Fix a prime \(p\), an integer \(b\geq1\), and the abelian group
\[
G_{p,b}=C_p\oplus C_{p^b}.
\]
For each integer \(j\geq0\), define the exact image-order statistic
\[
c_{p,b}(j)=\#\{T\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(G_{p,b}):T^2=0,\ |\operatorname{im}T|=p^j\}.
\]
Every square-zero endomorphism of \(G_{p,b}\) has **cyclic image**, and all the statistics \(c_{p,b}(j)\) are as follows.

For \(b=1\), there is one zero map and \(p^2-1\) maps with image of order \(p\). For \(b\geq2\), put \(s=\lfloor b/2\rfloor\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{p,b}(0)&=1,\\
c_{p,b}(1)&=(p-1)(2p+1),\\
c_{p,b}(j)&=(p-1)(2p-1)p^{j-1}\quad(2\leq j\leq s).
\end{aligned}
\]
When \(b=2s+1\) is odd (necessarily \(s\geq1\)), there is one additional stratum
\[
c_{p,2s+1}(s+1)=(p-1)^2p^s.
\]
Every value not specified by these formulas is zero. In particular, the total number \(Z_{p,b}=\#\{T:T^2=0\}\) is
\[
\boxed{Z_{p,b}=\begin{cases}
p^2,&b=1,\\
(2p-1)p^{b/2},&b\geq2\text{ even},\\
p^{(b+3)/2},&b\geq3\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\]
The parity transition is substantive: in odd exponent \(b\), a new top-image-order layer arises from cancellation of two terms at the same \(p\)-adic valuation. The group \(G_{p,b}\) is the first non-homogeneous two-generator primary group, and square-zero endomorphisms are exactly differential structures on its underlying abelian group.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(C_{p^m}\) means the cyclic additive group of order \(p^m\). Endomorphisms are additive-group homomorphisms. The equation \(T^2=0\) means composition with itself is zero; \(j\) measures the order of the image, not the rank of an ambient vector space. The theorem holds for all primes, including two, and for all positive integers \(b\). No statement is made about a longer first cyclic summand or about maps of nilpotency index greater than two.

## Proof

**1. Unique endomorphism coordinates.** For \(b\geq2\), relative to canonical cyclic generators, each endomorphism is represented uniquely by
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\p^{b-1}w&z\end{pmatrix},
\quad u,v,w\in\mathbb F_p,\quad z\in\mathbb Z/p^b\mathbb Z.
\]
The top row is interpreted modulo \(p\) and the bottom row modulo \(p^b\). This is a coordinate description of additive-group homomorphisms; multiplying such matrices with those coordinate conventions agrees with composition. The four equations for \(T^2=0\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^2+p^{b-1}vw&=0\pmod p,\\
v(u+z)&=0\pmod p,\\
p^{b-1}w(u+z)&=0\pmod {p^b},\\
z^2+p^{b-1}wv&=0\pmod {p^b}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equation forces \(u=0\). The last equation modulo \(p\) then forces \(z=0\pmod p\). Consequently the two mixed equations vanish automatically, and the entire problem is reduced to the single congruence
\[
\boxed{z^2+p^{b-1}vw=0\pmod {p^b}.}\tag{1}
\]

**2. Valuation cutoff and cancellation.** Define \(\nu_p(z)\in\{0,\ldots,b\}\) to be the truncated valuation (with \(\nu_p(0)=b\)). If \(2\nu_p(z)<b-1\), the term \(z^2\) in (1) has strictly smaller valuation than the other term, so (1) is impossible.

If \(b=2s\), this forces \(\nu_p(z)\geq s\). Therefore \(z^2=0\pmod {p^b}\) and (1) is equivalent to \(vw=0\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). There are precisely \(2p-1\) ordered pairs \((v,w)\) with zero product, and precisely \(p^s\) eligible values of \(z\).

If \(b=2s+1\) with \(s\geq1\), either \(\nu_p(z)\geq s+1\), giving \(vw=0\), or \(\nu_p(z)=s\). In the latter case write \(z=p^s t\), where the residue \(t\pmod p\) is nonzero. Equation (1) becomes \(vw=-t^2\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). There are \(p-1\) choices of \((v,w)\) for each \(t\). Exactly \((p-1)p^s\) residues \(z\pmod {p^{2s+1}}\) have valuation \(s\). Thus the exceptional stratum has cardinality \((p-1)^2p^s\). The ordinary stratum has \((2p-1)p^s\) elements, and their sum is \(p^{s+2}\).

**3. Exact image orders.** The images of the two generators are
\[
T(1,0)=(0,p^{b-1}w),\qquad T(0,1)=(v,z).
\]
If \(z\) has additive order at least \(p^2\), the element \((0,p^{b-1}w)\) belongs to the cyclic group generated by \((v,z)\): an appropriate multiple of \(p^{j-1}(v,z)\), where \(|z|=p^j\), gives it. If \(z\) has order at most \(p\), (1) gives \(vw=0\), so the two images generate a cyclic group of order at most \(p\). This also handles \(z=0\). Thus every image is cyclic, and, when \(z\) has additive order \(p^j\) with \(j\geq2\), its image has exactly order \(p^j\).

Among residues of \(\mathbb Z/p^b\mathbb Z\), exactly \((p-1)p^{j-1}\) have additive order \(p^j\). At each ordinary image order \(2\leq j\leq s\), there are \(2p-1\) admissible choices of \((v,w)\), producing the stated middle layers. At image order \(p\), there are \((p-1)(2p-1)\) maps with \(z\) of order \(p\), plus \(2(p-1)\) maps with \(z=0\) and \((v,w)\ne(0,0)\). Hence \(c_{p,b}(1)=(p-1)(2p+1)\). The only zero map is \(u=v=w=z=0\). In the odd exceptional case \(\nu_p(z)=s\), the element \(z\) has additive order \(p^{s+1}\), giving the additional top layer already counted in step 2. This proves every image statistic.

**4. Homogeneous boundary.** If \(b=1\), then \(G_{p,1}\cong\mathbb F_p^2\). A nonzero square-zero linear operator has rank one and has image equal to kernel, a one-dimensional subspace. There are \(p+1\) possible subspaces and \(p-1\) nonzero isomorphisms from the one-dimensional quotient to that subspace. Together with the zero operator, this gives \(1+(p+1)(p-1)=p^2\) operators and the asserted image distribution. This also proves cyclicity of their images.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` enumerates endomorphisms directly via their images of the two cyclic generators, tests composition rather than relying on the valuation classification, explicitly builds each image subgroup, and compares its order-layer histogram with the closed formulas. Tested configurations include \((p,b)=(2,1),(3,1),(2,2),(3,2),(5,2),(2,3),(3,3),(5,3),(2,4),(3,4),(2,5),(3,5),(2,6),(2,7)\). Its successful last line is `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Hillar and Rhea, *Automorphisms of finite Abelian groups* (arXiv:math/0605185, first submitted 8 May 2006), give a general explicit matrix model for the endomorphism ring and then count its **invertible** elements. The unique coordinates used above specialize that model; the square-zero congruence, valuation cancellation, cyclic-image theorem and image-order census are not their automorphism count. Mader, *The automorphism group of finite abelian p-groups* (2012), likewise gives a structural description of endomorphisms and automorphisms but its inspected theorems concern automorphism orders, characteristic subgroups and endomorphism-ring structure, not the present square-zero image statistics. Edmunds, *Interchange Rings* (arXiv:1402.3699, first submitted 15 February 2014), analyzes **commuting idempotent** endomorphisms (equations \(E^2=E\)); that simultaneous-diagonalization classification has a different defining polynomial and does not directly enumerate the square-zero maps considered here. The distinction is mathematical rather than terminological: nonzero square-zero maps cannot be idempotent.

## Limitations

The proof covers the one-short-summand family \(C_p\oplus C_{p^b}\) and every prime, but does not compute the corresponding distributions for \(C_{p^a}\oplus C_{p^b}\) when \(a>1\), nor any higher-primary-rank modules. A search of inspected relevant sources did not locate an already stated equivalent rank-two image spectrum; this is not a guarantee that unlocated literature contains no such result. Finite verification is corroboration, not the reason the formulas hold for all primes and exponents.
