# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The Fourier eigenvector identity directly yields all energies. Folding angles to \([0,\pi]\) and pairing consecutive folded angles proves the two exact lower-envelope formulas, including their boundary degeneracies and the odd/even edge cases. The lower envelope has exactly one intersection of increasing and decreasing branches, proving global uniqueness of the optimum. Explicit substitution and small-angle expansions give sharp finite and asymptotic constants. The finite-time bound follows by integrating Fourier cross terms, without assuming that this bound is tight. The complete verification program passes 10,064 numerical checks; numerical tests are corroboration, not the infinite-size proof.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic uncertainty

The 2007 real-cycle mixing paper covers zero-flux degeneracy, not flux maximin optimization. The 2011 average-mixing paper and the 2026 chiral-circulant uniformity result cover simple-spectrum averaging (which is not claimed new), not the optimal worst-pair energy splitting. The strongest comparison is Frigerio et al. (2021), Eq. (26): it gives the exact same chiral-cycle spectrum and identifies the odd-phase \(\pi/(2n)\) as resonant for a different transport criterion. The odd phase itself is therefore acknowledged as previously recognized. The new result is the full pairwise maximin formula, unique optimal even-cycle flux, and two sharp parity-dependent large-size coefficients. Searches over aliases and flux/level-spacing terminology found no equivalent stated classification. Remaining risk: an equivalent optimization in older Aharonov–Bohm tight-binding-ring literature or an implicit result whose significance was not recognized in inspected primary texts.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The smallest splitting is a natural rigorous worst-resonance denominator for finite-time dephasing of a unitary walk. Optimizing it across all Fourier pairs is a meaningful spectral design problem. The exact parity transition between \(\pi/2\) and asymptotically \(\pi/3\), together with a sharp \(4/3\) asymptotic coefficient ratio, is a nontrivial all-size classification; the even-size optimum differs from prior transport-optimized phases. The result explicitly does not promise optimal physical mixing time.
