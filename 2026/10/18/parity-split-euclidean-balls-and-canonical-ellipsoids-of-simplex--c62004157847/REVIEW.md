# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The alternating-sign highest-Laplacian identity is reconstructed from the oriented boundary matrix; an exact rational checker independently reproduces it for fifteen dimensions. The even case is proved via an orthogonal free sum, an explicit exhaustive facet-normal family, and optimality/uniqueness certificates. In the odd case, barycentric-gradient lengths and their summation certify the unique inball; the regular majority-vertex subsystem and an exact positive clearance certify the unique minimum enclosing sphere. Affine singular values of the Laplacian map transfer the regular simplex John and Löwner ellipsoids. All results include their dimensions, normalizations, exceptional boundary case \(m=3\), and explicit scope.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The full primary preprint arXiv:2301.11602 establishes the top Laplacian matrix, parity-dependent combinatorial geometry, facets and triangulation/Ehrhart facts. Those facts alone do not determine the canonical Euclidean ball radii or semiaxes; no matching exact metric classification was found in the compared sources or published research index. The related graph-Laplacian-simplex theorem concerns a different geometry and does not imply the odd strict minority-screening phenomenon or the even free-sum contact theorem. The author thesis was inaccessible and remains a named bibliographic uncertainty; the claim is not an assertion of exhaustive global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

These are exact, dimension-uniform metrics of a natural simplex-boundary family whose combinatorial type changes with parity. The distinction between Euclidean inballs, minimum enclosing spheres and affine-canonical John–Löwner ellipsoids is substantive: even sizes have two spherical canonical ellipsoids, whereas odd sizes have factor-\(m\) anisotropy and a sharp strict contact-screening effect for roughly half the vertices. It distinguishes metric information from known combinatorial and lattice information and supplies explicit reusable certificates.

## Verification and limitations

The included standard-library program checks oriented boundary Gram matrices, even facets and odd circumradius clearances exactly for \(3\le m\le17\); odd radical incenter checks are floating-point. Its finite range cannot prove the theorem universally. A relevant thesis was not examined because the available repository access blocked full text. No claim of independent mathematical certification is made.
