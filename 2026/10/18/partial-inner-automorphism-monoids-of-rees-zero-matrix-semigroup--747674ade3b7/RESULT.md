# Partial inner automorphism monoids of Rees zero-matrix semigroups

## Finding

Let \(G\) be any group, let \(I\) and \(\Lambda\) be nonempty index sets, and let \(P=(p_{\lambda i})\) be an arbitrary \(\Lambda\)-by-\(I\) matrix over \(G\cup\{0\}\). Form the Rees zero-matrix semigroup \(S=\mathcal M^0(G;I,\Lambda;P)\), with its distinct zero and the usual collapsed-zero convention. Set
\[
E=\{(i,\lambda)\in I\times\Lambda:p_{\lambda i}\ne0\},\qquad H=G/Z(G).
\]
For any nonempty set \(E\), denote by \(B_E(H)\) the Brandt semigroup \((E\times H\times E)\cup\{0\}\), whose nonzero product \((e,a,f)(f',b,k)\) is \((e,ab,k)\) when \(f=f'\), and zero otherwise. A superscript \(1\) means an *external* identity adjoined, even if the semigroup already has local identities.

**Theorem.** The partial inner automorphism monoid defined using natural conjugacy satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(S)\cong
\begin{cases}
\{0,1\}, & E=\varnothing,\\
H^0, & |I|=|\Lambda|=1\text{ and }E\ne\varnothing,\\
B_E(H)^1, & E\ne\varnothing\text{ and }|I||\Lambda|>1.
\end{cases}
\]
The theorem also holds for infinite index sets or infinite groups. In the finite case, writing \(k=|E|\), the cardinality in the third case is \(k^2|G/Z(G)|+2\); the exceptional group-with-zero case has \(|G/Z(G)|+1\) elements.

Thus, except for the indicated boundary cases, the entire invariant depends only on the number of nonzero sandwich entries and the group \(G/Z(G)\), not on their locations or values. For nonzero finite patterns, these two data can also be recovered from the isomorphism type of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\): the number of nonidentity nonzero idempotents is \(k\), and each associated maximal subgroup is \(H\).

**Separating example.** With \(G\) trivial, \(I=\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\Lambda=\{0,1\}\), take
\[
P_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both matrices have four nonzero entries, every row and column nonzero, and
\(\operatorname{Inn}(S_A)\cong\operatorname{Inn}(S_B)\cong B_4(\{1\})^1\), a monoid of order \(18\). The semigroups are not isomorphic: the counts of nonzero idempotents in their \(\mathcal L\)-classes are respectively \(\{3,1\}\) and \(\{2,2\}\). More strongly, \(S_A\) has no complete mapping whereas \(S_B\) does. Therefore the partial inner invariant does not detect the complete-mapping property even in finite combinatorial completely zero-simple semigroups.

## Assumptions and scope

No finiteness, regularity, or cancellation assumption is imposed on \(G\), \(I\), \(\Lambda\), or \(P\). The usual multiplication is
\[
(i,x,\lambda)(j,y,\mu)=
\begin{cases}
(i,xp_{\lambda j}y,\mu),&p_{\lambda j}\ne0,\\
0,&p_{\lambda j}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
The symbol \(0\) on the right denotes the *single* absorbing zero, and it is distinct from each triple. Define \(S^1\) by adjoining an identity only when one is absent. For \(g,h\in S^1\), define
\[
D_{g,h}=\{a\in S:(gh)a=a=a(gh)\},\qquad
\phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad a\longmapsto hag.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) is the inverse monoid generated by all these partial maps under composition, as in Araújo et al. The empty partial bijection is not introduced: each map fixes \(0\), and \(\varepsilon_0:\{0\}\to\{0\}\) is the absorbing element of the generated monoid.

A complete mapping of a finite semigroup means a bijection \(\alpha:S\to S\) for which \(a\mapsto a\alpha(a)\) is also bijective. The example's complete-mapping comparison is separate from the construction of \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\).

## Proof

**1. Idempotents and local group coordinates.** A nonzero element \((i,x,\lambda)\) is idempotent if and only if \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\) and \(x=p_{\lambda i}^{-1}\). Each \(e=(i,\lambda)\in E\) therefore specifies
\[
e^*=(i,p_{\lambda i}^{-1},\lambda),\qquad
H_e=\{(i,x,\lambda):x\in G\}.
\]
The subset \(H_e\) is a maximal subgroup of \(S\) with identity \(e^*\). The map \((i,x,\lambda)\mapsto xp_{\lambda i}\) is a group isomorphism \(H_e\to G\), since the product in \(H_e\) is \((i,xp_{\lambda i}y,\lambda)\).

**2. Every non-global generating map has only one local group as domain.** Let \(g,h\in S^1\), and first suppose \(gh\in S\). If \(gh=0\), then \(D_{g,h}=\{0\}\). If \(gh=(i,v,\lambda)\ne0\) and \(a=(j,x,\mu)\ne0\) lies in \(D_{g,h}\), comparing first and last indices in \((gh)a=a=a(gh)\) forces \(j=i\) and \(\mu=\lambda\). Nonvanishing also forces \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\). The remaining group equations are
\[
vp_{\lambda i}x=x=xp_{\lambda i}v,
\]
so cancellation gives \(v=p_{\lambda i}^{-1}\). Conversely this equality makes \(gh=e^*\), and then every element of \(H_e\cup\{0\}\) satisfies the domain equations. Hence \(D_{g,h}\) is exactly \(\{0\}\), or is exactly \(H_e\cup\{0\}\) for one \(e\in E\). The only alternative \(gh=1\) is possible in the non-group-with-zero case only when \(g=h=1\), producing \(\operatorname{id}_S\). In the group-with-zero case \(|I|=|\Lambda|=1\) every nonzero local group is already all of \(S\setminus\{0\}\), so this is not a separate type.

**3. The local maps are exactly inner conjugations.** Consider nonzero \(g=(i,s,\mu)\), \(h=(j,t,\lambda)\) with \(gh=e^*\). Write \(p=p_{\lambda i}\), \(q=p_{\mu j}\), so both are nonzero. The equation \(gh=e^*\) reads \(sqtp=1\). It implies \(hg=(j,q^{-1},\mu)=f^*\), with \(f=(j,\mu)\in E\). For \(a=(i,x,\lambda)\in H_e\), its normalized group coordinate is \(a_G=xp\). The image \(hag=(j,tpxps,\mu)\) has normalized coordinate
\[
(tpxps)q=(tp)(xp)(sq)=(tp)a_G(tp)^{-1}.
\]
Writing \(u=(tp)^{-1}\), this is \(a_G\mapsto u^{-1}a_Gu\). Thus every generating map with nontrivial domain, apart from the global identity, is determined by \(e,f\in E\) and \(uZ(G)\in H\); denote it \(\psi_{e,f,uZ(G)}\). The group coordinate is well defined on cosets because the kernel of conjugation is exactly \(Z(G)\). If one of \(g,h\) is the adjoined identity, the resulting nontrivial map is the local identity, which is the case \(e=f\), \(uZ(G)=Z(G)\).

Conversely, for arbitrary \(e=(i,\lambda),f=(j,\mu)\in E\) and \(u\in G\), put \(p=p_{\lambda i}\), \(q=p_{\mu j}\), and choose
\[
g=(i,uq^{-1},\mu),\qquad h=(j,u^{-1}p^{-1},\lambda).
\]
Direct multiplication yields \(gh=e^*\), \(hg=f^*\), and \(\phi_{g,h}=\psi_{e,f,uZ(G)}\). Thus all such maps are present.

**4. Multiplication and exceptional cases.** Each \(\psi_{e,f,c}\) fixes zero and maps exactly \(H_e\cup\{0\}\) onto \(H_f\cup\{0\}\). Distinct local groups meet only in \(\{0\}\). Consequently, if partial maps are composed left-to-right,
\[
\psi_{e,f,c}\,\psi_{f',k,d}=
\begin{cases}
\psi_{e,k,cd},&f=f',\\
\varepsilon_0,&f\ne f'.
\end{cases}
\]
Every map composed with \(\varepsilon_0\) gives \(\varepsilon_0\), and the global identity acts as identity. These maps already form a monoid, so taking generated closure adds nothing. They have precisely the multiplication of \(B_E(H)^1\), except that the global identity coincides with the unique local identity when the Rees semigroup is \(G^0\). If \(E=\varnothing\), every nonidentity generator is \(\varepsilon_0\), giving \(\{0,1\}\). If \(S=G^0\), the sole nonzero local subgroup is all of \(S\setminus\{0\}\), so the global identity belongs to \(B_E(H)\), giving \(H^0\). This proves the classification and, for finite data, its cardinality formula.

**5. Distinct semigroups with identical invariants and different complete-mapping behavior.** For the two displayed \(2\)-by-\(3\) patterns, \(E\) has four members and \(H\) is trivial, hence both generated monoids have exactly \(4^2+2=18\) maps. Counts of idempotents in \(\mathcal L\)-classes are isomorphism invariants and are as displayed, so the original semigroups are not isomorphic.

For \(S_A\), any complete mapping \(\alpha\) would satisfy \(\alpha(0)=0\), since \(0\alpha(0)=0\) and the product map must be injective. The three elements \((i,1)\), for \(i=0,1,2\), could produce nonzero products only if their respective \(\alpha\)-images have first index \(0\), because the second row of \(P_A\) has a single nonzero entry. Only two possible images have that index, a contradiction to injectivity of \(\alpha\).

For \(S_B\), one explicit complete mapping sends \(0\) to \(0\) and is specified by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
a&(0,0)&(0,1)&(1,0)&(1,1)&(2,0)&(2,1)\\\hline
\alpha(a)&(0,0)&(1,1)&(0,1)&(2,0)&(1,0)&(2,1)
\end{array}.
\]
Its output list is a permutation of the six nonzero elements, while its product outputs, in the same order, are \((0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,0),(2,0),(2,1)\), also a permutation. This proves the asserted separation.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` constructs finite Rees zero-matrix multiplication tables from scratch, enumerates all defining maps \(\phi_{g,h}\), and compares their **graphs** against the maps predicted by the normal form. It also checks closure under composition in manageable examples. The tested groups include cyclic groups and \(S_3\); the matrices include Brandt patterns, nonzero group coefficients, rectangular patterns, zero rows/columns, and all-zero sandwich matrices. All \(14\) configurations passed. The script also exhaustively checks the two seven-element examples for complete mappings, finding counts \(0\) and \(24\) respectively, and verifies the two partial-inner monoids each have \(18\) elements. Run `python3 verify.py` without dependencies.

The checks confirm the finite instances stated; the universal assertion for arbitrary groups and index sets follows from the algebraic proof, not finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro, and Mercier defined \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) using the maps \(\phi_{g,h}\), classified related monoids for completely simple Rees matrix semigroups **without** zero, and specifically posed the completely zero-simple case as Problem 7.5 in their 2026 separately issued treatment (a portion of work publicly circulated in 2023). The present theorem gives an explicit answer for that case and, in fact, for every Rees zero-matrix semigroup over a group, including nonregular sandwich patterns. It does not claim originality for the general definition, the inverse-semigroup Brandt special case, or the earlier classification without zero.

Araújo, Bentz, Cameron, Hendrey, and Kinyon studied complete mappings of Rees zero-matrix semigroups in August 2026 and showed their existence is sensitive to the detailed sandwich support. The exhibited pair proves a direct distinction: this complete-mapping property is not detected by the much coarser partial-inner invariant. The independent finite verification supports the comparison but is not a substitute for bibliographic exclusion of every possible equivalent formulation.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only partial inner automorphisms generated by natural conjugators, not the full partial automorphism inverse monoid nor the full automorphism group. The distinction is essential. No claim is made for Rees matrices over arbitrary non-group coefficient semigroups. No algorithmic complexity bound for recognizing isomorphism of arbitrary input groups is asserted. The novelty comparison is based on inspected accessible texts and targeted searches; mathematically equivalent older results under alternative terminology remain possible. Independent audit has not been performed.
