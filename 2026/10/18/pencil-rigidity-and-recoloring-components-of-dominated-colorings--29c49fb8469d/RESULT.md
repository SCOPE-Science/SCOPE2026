# Pencil rigidity and recoloring components of dominated colorings in finite projective planes

## Finding
Let \(\Pi\) be any finite projective plane of order \(q\ge2\), not necessarily Desarguesian. Write \(G\) for its point–line incidence graph, \(v=q^2+q+1\), and \(m=q+1\). A *dominated coloring* is a proper vertex coloring in which every nonempty color class is contained in the **open** neighborhood of some vertex. Then
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{dom}}(G)=2m.
\]
Every optimal coloring, modulo permutation of color names, is specified uniquely by a point \(P\), a line \(\ell\), a line \(L_*\) through \(P\), and a point \(X_*\) on \(\ell\). The point-vertex color classes are
\[
\big\{(L\setminus\{P\})\cup(\{P\}\text{ if }L=L_*\text{ else }\varnothing):L\ni P\big\};
\]
the line-vertex color classes are the dual family
\[
\big\{(\{M:X\in M\}\setminus\{\ell\})\cup(\{\ell\}\text{ if }X=X_*\text{ else }\varnothing):X\in\ell\big\}.
\]
The two families are disjoint color palettes. There are exactly \(v^2m^2\) optimal partitions into unlabeled nonempty color classes, and exactly \((2m)!v^2m^2\) optimal colorings using \(2m\) specified, distinct color names.

Under *one-vertex recoloring*, two optimal colorings are adjacent if they differ in the color of exactly one vertex, with every intermediate coloring optimal. The resulting graph on **unlabeled color partitions** is
\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{unlabeled}}(G)\cong
\bigsqcup_{v^2}(K_m\mathbin{\square}K_m).
\]
For \(2m\) fixed, named colors it is instead the disjoint union of \(v^2(2m)!\) copies of \(K_m\square K_m\). Each component has \(m^2\) vertices, degree \(2(m-1)\), diameter \(2\), and exactly \(m^2(m-1)\) edges. In particular, each recoloring move preserves the two geometric pencil centers \((P,\ell)\): the only movable vertices are the distinguished point \(P\) and distinguished line \(\ell\).

## Assumptions and scope
A finite projective plane of order \(q\ge2\) has \(v=q^2+q+1\) points and as many lines, with \(q+1\) points on every line and \(q+1\) lines through every point; distinct lines intersect once and distinct points determine one line. The coloring convention uses nonempty color classes and domination by **open** neighborhoods, as in Merouane–Haddad–Chellali–Kheddouci. Unlabeled means that color-name permutations identify the same set partition; incidence vertices themselves remain distinguished. A recoloring changes one vertex's membership from one nonempty class to another, and never changes the number of classes. A named coloring uses each of the \(2m\) labels at least once.

## Proof
**1. Minimum covering rigidity.** Let \(r\) distinct lines cover all points. Their union has at most \((q+1)+(r-1)q=rq+1\) points: begin with one line, and each additional line meets that first line, so adds at most \(q\) points. Covering all \(q^2+q+1\) points requires \(r\ge q+1=m\). Equality \(r=m\) forces every later line to contribute its full \(q\) new points. Thus any two selected lines can only intersect on the first line: otherwise a later line meets two previously selected lines in distinct points and contributes at most \(q-1\). Since every two selected lines meet exactly once, their respective intersections with the first line are a common point \(P\). Hence exactly \(m\) lines cover the plane precisely when they are the entire pencil through \(P\). Duality proves: a point set meeting every line has size at least \(m\), and equality means it is all \(m\) points of a single line.

**2. Minimum coloring and classification.** Since \(G\) is bipartite and a vertex's open neighborhood lies entirely in the opposite part, any dominated color class lies wholly among point vertices or wholly among line vertices. The supporting line for each point-only class supplies a line cover of all points, so there are at least \(m\) point classes. Dually there are at least \(m\) line classes, giving \(\chi_{\mathrm{dom}}(G)\ge2m\). Choose a pencil of \(m\) lines through any point \(P\), assign the \(q\) off-center points on each pencil line to their own class, and place \(P\) in one of those classes. Dualize using the pencil of points on a line \(\ell\). The resulting \(2m\) classes are independent and openly dominated by their respective incident lines/points. Therefore equality holds.

Conversely, an optimal coloring has exactly \(m\) point classes. Choose a supporting line for each. Their \(m\) lines must be a pencil through a point \(P\) by step 1. Every selected line \(L\) has precisely \(q\) points outside \(P\) that occur in none of the other supporting lines; therefore all those \(q\) points must belong to the class supported by \(L\). The remaining point \(P\) belongs to one of the \(m\) classes. Since \(q\ge2\), each class has at least two off-center points, which determine its unique supporting line; thus the pencil center and the class containing \(P\) are recovered uniquely from the partition. The same argument on the line side recovers \(\ell\) and the class containing \(\ell\). The two centers may be incident or not; they are chosen independently. Counting gives \(v^2m^2\) unlabeled partitions, and every such partition has exactly \((2m)!\) distinct assignments of the specified labels.

**3. Rigidity of recoloring.** Within the point side, each optimal class contains the \(q\) off-center points of a unique pencil line \(L\). If a vertex \(Y\ne P\) is recolored into a different point class with line \(L'\), that target class already contains \(q\ge2\) distinct off-center points on \(L'\). Any line supporting the changed class must therefore equal \(L'\), but \(Y\notin L'\): distinct pencil lines intersect only at \(P\). So no such recoloring is valid. The point \(P\), by contrast, belongs to every pencil line and may be recolored into any one of the other \(m-1\) classes. Dually, the only possible recoloring on the line side transfers the distinguished line \(\ell\) among its \(m\) classes. Moving a vertex across the two color-class types is impossible because the resulting class would meet both bipartition classes and could not have an open-neighborhood dominator. Thus \((P,\ell)\) and the assignments of the other vertices to their class labels are invariant. For each fixed \((P,\ell)\), the two independent hub choices form exactly \(K_m\square K_m\); all \(v^2\) center pairs occur. With named colors, each component also retains the fixed bijection between colors and the \(2m\) supporting pencil lines/points, giving the factorial multiplier. Component metrics follow from the Cartesian product.

## Verification
The proof covers every existing finite projective plane and does not rely on enumeration. The included standalone programs verify the entire point-side minimum-partition spectrum and all one-vertex exchanges independently for \(\mathrm{PG}(2,2)\) and \(\mathrm{PG}(2,3)\): respectively \(21\) and \(52\) one-side optimal partitions; \(7K_3\) and \(13K_4\) one-side recoloring graphs; \(441\) and \(2704\) unlabeled two-side optimum colorings; \(49\) and \(169\) two-side components. Running `python3 exhaustive.py` and `python3 constructive_check.py` reproduces the enclosed JSON outputs. One program enumerates all partitions into openly dominated classes with no pencil assumption; the other constructs pencil partitions and independently compares each pair for a possible one-vertex recoloring. Only prime orders \(2,3\) are checked computationally; this is a check of the definitions and finite instances, not a proof of the infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Merouane, Haddad, Chellali and Kheddouci introduced dominated coloring and proved that its minimum color count equals total domination for triangle-free graphs. Their full available manuscript establishes the general equality but does not classify optimum dominated colorings on projective-plane incidence graphs or their recoloring moves. Héger and Nagy study *ordinary* dominating sets of projective-plane incidence graphs; their Proposition 2.1 states the standard sharp blocking-set bound and the collinearity equality case. Those bounds and the prior equality readily imply the **numerical** color minimum \(2(q+1)\); that numerical consequence is not presented as new. The added result is the all-optima geometric classification, exact labeled and unlabeled counts, and disconnected Cartesian-product recoloring geometry. Nearby published classifications of total domination in **subspace-intersection graphs** concern a different adjacency relation and cannot be used to infer an incidence-graph coloring partition or its recoloring structure.

## Limitations
Only minimum dominated colorings, not arbitrary nonoptimal colorings, are classified. Recoloring is strictly one-vertex and must stay optimal; allowing temporary extra colors could connect components. Equality is not a statement about ordinary domination, whose parameter differs for incidence graphs. The claim is conditional on a finite plane of order \(q\) existing, and applies equally to non-Desarguesian planes. The central-color class choices are not quotiented by geometric automorphisms. Searches did not establish publication priority: equivalent descriptions may appear in unindexed coloring papers or supplemental material. Independent external validation has not been performed.
