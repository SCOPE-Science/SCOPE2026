# A perfect discrete-Morse certificate at the first nontrivial Euclidean Rips scale of the 24-cell

## Finding

Let \(V=\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:1\le i<j\le4\}\) with independent signs in \(\mathbb R^4\), and let \(K\) consist of all nonempty subsets of \(V\) of diameter at most \(\sqrt2\) in the Euclidean metric. On this unsubdivided simplicial complex there is a certified acyclic discrete-Morse matching with one critical vertex, no critical edge and exactly 23 critical triangles. More concretely, deleting the 23 specified triangles but keeping all their proper faces leaves a spanning subcomplex that collapses to the specified vertex by 96 elementary free-face pairs. The deleted facets and the sequence of pairs are encoded in the squared-cutoff-2 entry of the supplied `24cell_certificate.json`.

## Certificate and proof

Order the vertices by increasing lexicographic coordinate pair \((i,j)\) and then by sign pair with \(-1\) before \(+1\). For a face \(F\), its mask is \(\sum_{a\in F}2^a\) in this zero-based ordering. Since every vertex has squared norm two, the adjacency test for distinct vertices is exactly \(\langle u,v\rangle\ge1\). Clique enumeration in the resulting graph gives face vector \((24,96,96)\), and there are no higher faces. The squared-cutoff-2 entry contains 120 operations: 96 pairs `[a,b]` and 24 singletons. Each pair removes the current face `a` and its unique immediate coface `b`; `b` is maximal at that step. Each singleton removes a maximal face. Replaying the sequence and exhausting all 216 faces verifies that the singletons are exactly 23 triangles and one vertex. An independent integer reconstruction by clique size, face-poset replay and directed cycle check recovered these counts without executing the supplied checker.

Orient a cover in the original Hasse diagram upward for a matched pair, downward otherwise. Removal time is constant along the reversed matched covers, and strictly increases along every downward cover: its lower face could not have been removed with the upper face still present, since the lower face would then be neither maximal nor free with its designated mate. A directed cycle would need at least one downward edge and is therefore impossible. This proves acyclicity directly from the certificate. The resulting one-vertex CW model has precisely 23 two-cells and no other positive-dimensional cells, so all its integral cellular differentials vanish and the matching is perfect.

For the spanning collapse, move the deletions of the critical triangles to the start. They are all maximal even in the original complex and are not members of any pair, so deleting them early leaves both faces in every later pair and cannot add cofaces to either. Keep the critical vertex instead of deleting it; an extra vertex is not a coface of any nonempty face and cannot obstruct any later pair. The same relative order of the 96 pairs therefore collapses the resulting spanning complex to that vertex. The spanning subcomplex has 24 vertices, 96 edges and 73 triangles. This construction specifies actual simplices and a reduction in the original triangulation; an abstract homotopy equivalence need not specify either.

## Context and reproducibility

Saleh's dissertation (*On Vietoris-Rips Complexes of the 2-Sphere*, §4.2) gives the 24-cell's homology table at its indexed scales. The same Euclidean filtration homotopy types, including the wedge of 23 two-spheres at the first scale via a two-skeleton argument, appear in *A marked cross-polytope collapse in the 24-cell Vietoris–Rips filtration* (Resultary 6421d0ac1ad1, Finding and Proof). Its explicit marked-collapse certificate is at the different radius \(2\); a collapse there onto a cross-polytope boundary also yields the existence of a perfect matching there. The contribution here is the explicit first-scale perfect matching and spanning collapse on the original faces, not the homology ranks, the pointwise filtration types, or perfectness at radius \(2\). No compatibility assertion is made about inclusion maps. To check the certificate, use the `"2"` entry under `"scales"` of the unchanged certificate and the vertex enumeration above; the supplied `verify_24cell.py` implements the original ordered checks.
