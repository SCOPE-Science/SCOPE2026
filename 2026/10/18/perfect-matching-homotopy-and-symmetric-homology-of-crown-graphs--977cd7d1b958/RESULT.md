# Perfect-matching homotopy and symmetric homology of crown graphs through seven pairs

## Finding

Write \(G_n\) for the crown graph on two labeled parts \(L=\{L_1,\ldots,L_n\}\) and \(R=\{R_1,\ldots,R_n\}\), with edges \(L_iR_j\) exactly when \(i\ne j\). Let \(\mathcal P_n\) be the simplicial perfect-matching complex on these graph edges. The four positive-dimensional initial cases satisfy

\[\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_4&\simeq\bigvee^4 S^1,\\
\mathcal P_5&\simeq\bigvee^{33}S^2,\\
\mathcal P_6&\simeq\bigvee^{256}S^3,\\
\mathcal P_7&\simeq\bigvee^{2175}S^4.
\end{aligned}\]

The natural symmetry group generated by simultaneous label permutations and exchanging the two bipartition classes is \(S_7\times C_2\). Let \(\tau\) exchange classes and let \(\chi\) be the character on \(\widetilde H_4(\mathcal P_7;\mathbb Q)\). Its complete table, constant on the conjugacy classes listed, is:

| Cycle type in \(S_7\) | Class size | \(\chi(\sigma,1)\) | \(\chi(\sigma,\tau)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(7\) | 720 | 5 | -1 |
| \(6,1\) | 840 | -1 | 1 |
| \(5,2\) | 504 | 0 | 0 |
| \(5,1,1\) | 504 | 0 | 0 |
| \(4,3\) | 420 | -2 | 0 |
| \(4,2,1\) | 630 | 3 | -1 |
| \(4,1,1,1\) | 210 | -5 | 3 |
| \(3,3,1\) | 280 | 9 | -1 |
| \(3,2,2\) | 210 | 0 | -2 |
| \(3,2,1,1\) | 420 | 0 | -2 |
| \(3,1,1,1,1\) | 70 | 12 | 2 |
| \(2,2,2,1\) | 105 | -37 | 19 |

| \(1,1,1,1,1,1,1\) | 1 | 2175 | -85 |

In particular \(\operatorname{tr}(\tau)=-85\), and the \((+1,-1)\)-eigenspace dimensions of \(\tau\) are \((1045,1130)\). Both \(\widetilde H_4(\mathcal P_7;\mathbb Q)^{S_7}\) and \(\widetilde H_4(\mathcal P_7;\mathbb Q)^{S_7\times C_2}\) have dimension one.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite and simple, and all complexes are abstract simplicial complexes with all faces of their perfect-matching facets. We distinguish the perfect-matching complex from the ordinary matching (chessboard) complex. The homotopy and representation conclusions apply only to the four indicated values of \(n\); they do not assert any formula for larger \(n\).

For every \(n\ge3\), the face criterion is uniform. Any nonattacking-rook placement on the off-diagonal \(n\times n\) board with at most \(n-2\) rooks is a face of \(\mathcal P_n\); with exactly \(n-1\) rooks it is a face precisely when the unmatched row and column have different labels, and with \(n\) rooks it is a derangement permutation matrix.

## Proof

**Extension criterion.** Remove the matched rows and columns from any partial matching \(M\) of cardinality \(k\le n-2\). The leftover bipartite board has \(m=n-k\ge2\) rows and columns, with forbidden squares only on the common diagonal labels. Each single remaining row has at least \(m-1\ge1\) allowed columns. Any collection of at least two rows has every remaining column as a neighbor: a given column forbids at most one of these rows. Hall's marriage condition therefore holds and the partial matching extends. If \(k=n-1\), only one row and column remain, so the criterion is exactly their inequality.

**Exact finite Morse reductions.** Number board vertices \((i,j)\) with \(0\le i,j<n\), \(i\ne j\), in row-major order. Exhaustively generate all derangements and every subface. At each listed vertex position \(v\), pair every still-unmatched nonempty face not containing \(v\) with the face obtained by adjoining \(v\), whenever both survive. The explicit vertex orders for \(4\le n\le7\) are in `verify_crown.py` under `ORDERS`. Each stage is an element matching; successive element matchings are acyclic by the standard acyclic-matching lemma (the precise hypothesis is also verified by the complete Hasse-graph check in the script).

For \(n=4,5,6,7\) the respective numbers of nonempty faces are \(99,734,6335,61984\). The pair counts are \(47,350,3039,29904\). Exactly one vertex and \(4,33,256,2175\) cells in the respective dimension \(n-3\) remain critical, with no other critical cells. Thus the associated CW complexes have one vertex and only cells of a single positive dimension. Their attaching maps necessarily land in the sole vertex for dimensions at least two; for dimension one each loop also attaches at that vertex. Hence they are wedges of the stated spheres, integrally, with no hidden torsion or unsolved attaching maps.

**Symmetric homology.** For \(n=7\), \(S_7\) acts on edge labels by \((i,j)\mapsto(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))\), while \(\tau(i,j)=(j,i)\). For every \(g=(\sigma,\tau^e)\), a face fixed setwise by \(g\) is a union of cycles of its action on the 42 board-edge vertices. If it contains \(c\) such cycles, its contribution to the reduced alternating trace on simplicial chains is \((-1)^{c-1}\), including the empty face as \(-1\). Since reduced rational homology is concentrated in degree four, its character is

\[\chi(g)=-\sum_{F\in\mathcal P_7,\;gF=F}(-1)^{c_g(F)}.\]

The empty face contributes in the sum and has \(c_g(\varnothing)=0\). Enumerating invariant faces gives the displayed fifteen-by-two character table. An independent signed-inversion enumeration of the same fixed simplices produces identical values. Weighted character averages over all fifteen \(S_7\) conjugacy classes give exactly one invariant dimension both before and after including \(\tau\). The eigenspace ranks follow from traces \(2175\) and \(-85\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_crown.py` using only the Python standard library. `crown_verification.json` is the corresponding captured output. It regenerates the 1,854 derangement facets at \(n=7\), all 61,985 faces (including the empty face), all 29,904 matched covering pairs, and checks acyclicity against each of the 298,788 nonempty Hasse incidences. The analogous exhaustive counts are checked at \(n=4,5,6\). An independent inclusion-exclusion face-count formula is used at each rank:

\[a_{n,k}=\sum_{t=0}^k(-1)^t\binom nt\binom{n-t}{k-t}^2(k-t)!\qquad(0\le k\le n-2),\]

with \(a_{n,n-1}=nD_n\) and \(a_{n,n}=D_n\), where \(D_n\) is the derangement count. All relevant equality assertions, cycle orientation checks and character averaging assertions are executable in the standalone code. The code verifies finite claims; no computation is offered as proof of an infinite-family homotopy statement.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2022 perfect-matching-complex paper defines this invariant and discusses the *complete* bipartite matching complex, which equals the standard chessboard complex; removing one perfect matching from the base graph changes the face set, so the chessboard conclusion does not imply this result. That paper's explicit homotopy classifications concern honeycomb graphs. The 2024 paper classifies two-row square grids and polygonal line tilings, not these highly symmetric off-diagonal square boards. Both provide relevant discrete-Morse techniques, but neither inspected theorem provides the crown-graph wedge ranks or symmetry character. Relevant indexed results for rook graphs and crown graph suspensions involve different simplicial functors and do not imply the perfect-matching complex calculation.

## Limitations

No homotopy assertion is made for \(n\ge8\); the uniform Hall lemma is not a substitute for such an assertion. The character is rational, although the homotopy result gives integral homology groups. No explicit decomposition into irreducible integral \(S_7\)-lattices is asserted. Originality has been checked against the identified publications and indexed records, not exhaustively against every inaccessible or unindexed work. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
