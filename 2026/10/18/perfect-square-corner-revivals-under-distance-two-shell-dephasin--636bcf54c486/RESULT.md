# Perfect-square corner revivals under distance-two-shell dephasing of a quantum hypercube

## Finding

On the binary hypercube of every dimension \(d\ge4\), consider the unweighted adjacency Hamiltonian \(H\) and independent, strictly positive site dephasing at **every** vertex of Hamming weight two, but nowhere else. Let \(B\) be the orthogonal projection onto the smallest \(H\)-invariant subspace containing those monitored vertices, let \(D=I-B\), and write \(b_d=\operatorname{rank}B\). Then
\[
b_d=(d+1)+(d-1)\bigl(d-1-\mathbf1_{2\mid d}\bigr)+\frac{d(d-3)^2}{2}-2\mathbf1_{d\text{ is a square}}.
\]
For **every** density matrix \(\rho_0\), the time-dependent asymptotic law is
\[
\rho_t=e^{-itH}D\rho_0D e^{itH}+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B\rho_0)}{b_d}B+o(1)
\]
in any matrix norm. In particular, this need not converge to a time-independent density matrix.

For a walker initially at the zero vertex \(o\), the asymptotic return is **constant** whenever \(d\) is not a perfect square:
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\langle o|\rho_t|o\rangle=\frac1{b_d}.
\]
When \(d=s^2\) is a square, put \(a_d=2^{-d}\binom{d}{(d-s)/2}\). Instead, the exact asymptotic return is
\[
\langle o|\rho_t|o\rangle=\frac{(1-2a_d)^2}{b_d}+4a_d^2\cos^2(st)+o(1).
\]
Hence a corner-localized walker retains genuine periodic return oscillations **if and only if the hypercube dimension is a perfect square**. The oscillations persist at all positive monitored-site dephasing rates. Along squared dimensions, their peak contribution is asymptotic to \(8/(\pi e d)\), whereas the non-square stationary return is asymptotic to \(2/d^3\). This is a distinction between origin-supported protected modes; dark states can exist elsewhere in non-square dimensions.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is \(\{0,1\}^d\). \(o=(0,\ldots,0)\), and \(H_{xy}=1\) precisely when \(x,y\) differ in one coordinate, zero otherwise. The monitored set is \(M=\{x:|x|=2\}\), where \(|x|\) is Hamming weight. At each \(x\in M\), the Lindblad operator is \(\sqrt{\gamma_x}|x\rangle\langle x|\), with **arbitrary** \(\gamma_x>0\). Therefore
\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]-\frac12\sum_{x\in M}\gamma_x[|x\rangle\langle x|,[|x\rangle\langle x|,\rho]].
\]
The system is finite dimensional at fixed \(d\). Time tends to infinity before any dimension limit; the error \(o(1)\) is not asserted to be uniform in \(d\) or in the rates. The adjacency hopping is one, so the two corner-supported dark eigenfrequencies are \(\pm s\) without further time rescaling.

## Proof

**1. Fourier decomposition and controllable subspace.** Index the normalized Walsh basis by subsets \(S\subseteq[d]\), writing \(\chi_S(x)=2^{-d/2}(-1)^{|S\cap\operatorname{supp}(x)|}\). Each \(\chi_S\) is an eigenvector of \(H\) with eigenvalue \(d-2|S|\). Let \(E_k\) project onto the span with \(|S|=k\). The invariant subspace generated by all monitored basis vectors is therefore
\[
\operatorname{ran} B=\bigoplus_{k=0}^d\operatorname{span}\{E_k|x\rangle:x\in M\}.
\]
Its spectral-level dimensions are the ranks of \(W_{2,k}\), the real matrix with rows indexed by two-element sets \(T\), columns by \(k\)-sets \(S\), and entries \((-1)^{|T\cap S|}\).

**2. Exact rank at each Fourier level.** Split the space of arrays \(u_{ij}\), \(i<j\), into the orthogonal constant, first-harmonic, and second-harmonic spaces \(V_0,V_1,V_2\) of dimensions \(1,d-1,d(d-3)/2\). Here \(V_1\) consists of arrays \(u_{ij}=a_i+a_j\), \(\sum_i a_i=0\), and \(V_2\) consists of arrays with \(\sum_{j\ne i}u_{ij}=0\) for each \(i\). The transform \(u\mapsto W_{2,k}^{\mathsf T}u\) is permutation-equivariant, and its Gram operator preserves these three orthogonal sectors.

For constant \(u\), its value at a \(k\)-set is \(((d-2k)^2-d)/2\), so the constant sector is killed exactly if \((d-2k)^2=d\). For \(u_{ij}=a_i+a_j\), direct summation gives \(-2(d-2k)\sum_{i\in S}a_i\). This vanishes identically exactly when \(k=0,d\) or \(2k=d\); for \(1\le k\le d-1\) away from the midpoint, it is injective on \(V_1\). For \(u\in V_2\), expansion of the parity factor yields \(4\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}u_{ij}\). For \(2\le k\le d-2\), the pair-incidence map is injective: if its every \(k\)-subset sum vanishes, comparing the sums for \(T\cup\{i\}\) and \(T\cup\{j\}\), for all \((k-1)\)-sets \(T\) avoiding \(i,j\), makes \(u_{ia}-u_{ja}\) independent of \(a\ne i,j\) and equal to zero; symmetry then makes all coefficients equal, and the zero sums force that common coefficient to vanish. For the endpoint levels \(k=0,1,d-1,d\), this quadratic sector is absent.

Thus, for all \(0\le k\le d\), the exact rank is
\[
r_{d,k}=\mathbf1_{(d-2k)^2\ne d}+(d-1)\mathbf1_{1\le k\le d-1,\,2k\ne d}+\frac{d(d-3)}2\mathbf1_{2\le k\le d-2}.
\]
Summing the ranks over all \(k\) gives the stated \(b_d\): there is exactly one missing first-harmonic level when \(d\) is even and exactly two missing constant levels if \(d\) is a perfect square. This step can alternatively be framed in the established Johnson-slice harmonic decomposition; no novelty is claimed for the underlying decomposition itself.

**3. Peripheral dynamics.** For every matrix \(X\), the Hilbert–Schmidt identity gives
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle X,\mathcal LX\rangle=-\frac12\sum_{x\in M}\gamma_x\|[|x\rangle\langle x|,X]\|_2^2.
\]
Consequently, every purely imaginary eigenoperator of \(\mathcal L\) commutes with every monitored rank-one projector. Such an operator has \(X|x\rangle=c_x|x\rangle\) and \(\langle x|X=c_x\langle x|\) for all \(x\in M\). If its eigenvalue is nonzero imaginary, the diagonal matrix element at \(x\) of \([H,X]\) forces \(c_x=0\); the commutator equation then forces \(XH^j|x\rangle=0\) and \(\langle x|H^jX=0\) for all \(j\ge0\). Hence all such eigenoperators act only on \(D\mathcal H\).

For zero eigenvalue, \([H,X]=0\). The two-step adjacency has \(\langle y|H^2|x\rangle=2\) whenever monitored two-sets \(x,y\) share exactly one coordinate. Their Johnson graph is connected, so \(c_x=c_y\) for all monitors. Commutation with \(H\) then forces \(X\) to be this same scalar on all of \(B\mathcal H\). On \(D\mathcal H\), dephasing vanishes and the dynamics is precisely unitary. The generated semigroup is Hilbert–Schmidt contractive and finite-dimensional, so it has no nontrivial Jordan blocks on the imaginary axis; every other mode decays. Trace conservation fixes the bright stationary coefficient as \(\operatorname{tr}(B\rho_0)/b_d\). This proves the full asymptotic law.

**4. Origin-supported modes and their normalization.** Since \(|o\rangle\) is invariant under all coordinate permutations, its projection to \(E_k\) is a multiple of the radial Walsh sum \(\psi_k=\binom dk^{-1/2}\sum_{|S|=k}\chi_S\). The coefficient is \(\langle\psi_k|o\rangle=\sqrt{2^{-d}\binom dk}\), and \(\psi_k\) vanishes on the weight-two shell precisely when \((d-2k)^2=d\). The only admissible solutions are \(d=s^2\), \(k_\pm=(d\pm s)/2\), with one radial dark vector at each of the two energies \(\mp s\). These are the **only** dark components of the origin state. If there are none, its entire mass becomes uniformly mixed on the bright subspace, and the return is \(1/b_d\).

For squared \(d\), each of the two orthogonal dark components has squared norm \(a_d\); their relative phase is \(2st\). Their coherent return amplitude is \(2a_d\cos(st)\). The remaining bright component has weight \(1-2a_d\), and its overlap with \(|o\rangle\) is also \(1-2a_d\), giving the bright contribution \((1-2a_d)^2/b_d\). This yields the stated formula. Finally, the central-binomial local limit at deviation \(s/2\) gives \(a_d\sim e^{-1/2}\sqrt{2/(\pi d)}\), while \(b_d\sim d^3/2\), proving the claimed contrasting return scales.

## Relationship to prior work

Moore and Russell (2001) solve the coherent hypercube walk and its mixing question, establishing the Fourier/hypercube Hamiltonian background, not the present spatially sparse Lindblad monitoring pattern. Baumgartner and Narnhofer (2008) establish general finite-dimensional quantum semigroup asymptotic structure; our dissipation and bright/dark argument applies that general theory to this specific graph and observable. Filmus (2014) provides the harmonic analysis of Boolean slices used to organize the Fourier restriction ranks.

The published mathematical note *Square-dimension spectral resonances for quadratic-band Walsh concentration on Hamming shells* studies **degree-at-most-two** Fourier concentration sampled on a fixed Hamming shell. Its full text gives a strong incidence-matrix antecedent from which parts of our exact-rank calculation may be reconstructed. It does not consider a Lindblad generator, exact decoherence-free motion, the corner return, or the square-only persistent revival criterion. Drezgich and collaborators (2008) analyze **tensor-product single-qubit projection noise**, not separate rank-one projectors at the vertices of one Hamming shell. Published hypercube parity-monitoring and two-trap results concern different monitored sets and cannot imply the present result merely by restricting their parameters; the peripheral algebras differ.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes unit unweighted adjacency hopping and independent dephasing at exactly all Hamming-weight-two vertices, all with strictly positive rates. It gives fixed-dimension asymptotics and no dimension-uniform relaxation rate. The bright-space rank follows established incidence and harmonic methods; only the combined exact spatial-monitoring dynamics and arithmetic corner-revival law are advanced as a distinct finding. An equivalent formulation in less visible quantum-walk or association-scheme literature remains a bibliographic risk; no independent validation is claimed.
