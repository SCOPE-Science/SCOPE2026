# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The symmetric two-velocity generator is reduced to a periodic traceless two-by-two first-order system. The determinant-one monodromy gives the exact Floquet trace identity, whose quadratic expansion in the tilted eigenvalue has an explicit two-point kernel. Fourier analysis of the periodic primitive evaluates that kernel and shows that the cumulant coefficients have the stated normalization. The sharp spectral variance bound follows from \(|q|\ge1\) for nonzero circle Fourier frequencies. Independent exact-rational transfer-matrix checks verify four coefficient equalities across 34 piecewise-constant profiles, but are not substituted for the general analytic proof. Essential restrictions are strict positivity and spatial periodicity of flip rates, constant speed, and unwrapped displacement.

## Originality — PASS within inspected primary literature

The most directly related studies concern run-and-tumble steady states (Solon–Cates–Tailleur, `arxiv:1504.07391`), spatially varying reversal rates in interval first-passage theory (Angelani–Di Leonardo–Paoluzzi, `arxiv:1506.08046`), and tilted-generator fluctuations in spatially homogeneous lattice active walks (Mallmin–Blythe–Evans, `arxiv:1905.10260`). The inspected material does not contain the all-profile fourth-cumulant identity, the spatial-primitive correction, or its sharp Fourier maximizer and sign threshold. Spatially varying first-passage dynamics does not imply these unwrapped periodic fourth cumulants; a homogeneous velocity large-deviation formula has no landscape parameter to recover the correction. Equivalent results embedded in general Floquet, inhomogeneous telegraph, or Markov-additive cumulant theory remain possible and require independent bibliographic review.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Long-time fourth cumulants capture a physically natural, finite-time non-Gaussian correction that diffusivity alone misses. The exact law proves that arbitrary microscopic heterogeneity can be invisible at second order while becoming visible with a rigid positive fourth-order increment. Its Fourier inequality gives a sharp extremizer class at fixed spatial flip-rate variance, and its smooth first-harmonic family gives an experimentally interpretable sign boundary, rather than an arbitrarily chosen finite parameter sample.

## Boundaries and integrity

Only the rate-normalized cumulants for orders two and four, not full finite-time distributions or higher cumulants, are proved. The first-public source date is supported by the dated initial preprint, not by a journal revision date. The accepted conclusions have received the review described here; no independent review or validation has been performed.
