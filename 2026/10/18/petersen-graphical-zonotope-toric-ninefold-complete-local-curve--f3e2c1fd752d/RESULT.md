# Petersen graphical-zonotope toric ninefold: complete local curve-degree spectrum and smooth-tree orbits

## Finding

Let \(G=KG(5,2)\) denote the Petersen graph, whose ten labeled vertices are the two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), with edges joining disjoint subsets. Its fifteen edges are used as the primitive roots \(e_i-e_j\) in the saturated rank-nine lattice
\[
M=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_{10})\in\mathbb Z^{10}:\sum_{i=1}^{10}x_i=0\}.
\]
With each unordered graph edge indexed so that \(i<j\), let \(Z_G=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G),\,i<j}[0,e_i-e_j]\) and let \(X_G\) be the normal projective toric variety defined by the normal fan of \(Z_G\). Its dimension is nine. For a torus-fixed point \(p\), define \(d(p)\) as the number of one-dimensional torus-orbit closures passing through \(p\).

**The complete local invariant-curve degree polynomial is**
\[
\sum_{p\in X_G^T}t^{d(p)}
=1080t^9+2280t^{10}+3600t^{11}+3120t^{12}
+3120t^{13}+2160t^{14}+1320t^{15}.
\]
In particular, there are exactly \(16680\) torus-fixed points, exactly \(1080\) of which are smooth; all other \(15600\) fixed points are nonsimplicial and hence not \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. The number of edges of \(Z_G\), equivalently invariant curves, is \(100080\).

The natural \(S_5\cong\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) action splits the fixed points into **168 orbits**. The exact numbers of orbits by local degree and orbit cardinality are:

| Local degree | Orbits of size 20 | Orbits of size 60 | Orbits of size 120 | Total points |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 9 | 0 | 2 | 8 | 1080 |
| 10 | 0 | 2 | 18 | 2280 |
| 11 | 0 | 4 | 28 | 3600 |
| 12 | 0 | 4 | 24 | 3120 |
| 13 | 0 | 12 | 20 | 3120 |
| 14 | 0 | 8 | 14 | 2160 |
| 15 | 6 | 16 | 2 | 1320 |

Among the \(2000\) spanning trees of \(G\), exactly \(540\) occur as the entire Hasse-cover graph of an acyclic orientation. These trees form **five** \(S_5\)-orbits of respective sizes \(60,120,120,120,120\). Each of the \(540\) trees supports exactly two opposite acyclic orientations. The latter are exactly the \(1080\) smooth fixed points; they form ten orbits under \(S_5\) alone.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field may be any algebraically closed field of any characteristic. Use the *normal-fan* toric variety, not the toric variety of a semigroup generated by vertices of the polytope. The lattice \(M\) is the **saturated** root lattice specified above, and the fan is taken in its dual \(N=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb Z)\). The \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and smoothness assertions concern torus-fixed points, not the entire singular locus. The final edge total is a check/recovery of a global invariant already covered by general face-counting results; the new subject is the full local-degree and symmetry spectrum and the coherent-tree orbit count.

## Proof

**Toric graph dictionary.** The hyperplanes \(\langle x,e_i-e_j\rangle=0\) form the graphical arrangement. Each chamber is uniquely a choice of directions on the fifteen edges with no directed cycle. This is because a strict potential assignment induces an acyclic orientation, and conversely a topological order of an acyclic orientation supplies such a potential. The chamber closures are the maximal cones of the normal fan. Two chambers share a facet precisely if they differ by reversing one *flippable* edge: an oriented edge \(u\to v\) is flippable exactly when no other directed path runs from \(u\) to \(v\). Thus the incident invariant curves at the fixed point labeled by an orientation are indexed by the edges of its transitive reduction (Hasse diagram).

**Smoothness and integrality.** In every acyclic orientation on a connected ten-vertex graph, the Hasse arcs span all ten vertices and have at least nine members. Hence a chamber has the minimum nine facets exactly if its Hasse graph is a spanning tree. For nine Hasse arcs, the nine incident primitive directions in \(M\) are the corresponding root vectors \(e_i-e_j\). A spanning tree's oriented incidence vectors form a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(M\): delete one row of the full incidence matrix and expand a leaf column recursively; each maximal determinant has absolute value one. The dual maximal cone is therefore smooth. Conversely, a chamber with more than nine walls is nonsimplicial, so it is non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and singular. This proves smoothness precisely for local degree nine without requiring any assumption about the characteristic.

**Exhaustive orientation census.** Order the ten graph vertices lexicographically as the two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), then order the fifteen undirected edges lexicographically. Each of the \(2^{15}=32768\) binary words orients each edge forward or backward. For each word, Kahn's algorithm rejects it precisely when it has a directed cycle. For each surviving word, process a topological ordering backward to compute all directed reachability bit sets. An oriented edge \(u\to v\) belongs to the Hasse reduction if and only if no *different* out-neighbor \(w\) of \(u\) reaches \(v\). The standalone file `CHECK.py` exhaustively performs this criterion and separately recomputes each Hasse count by reversing each edge and checking acyclicity with a DFS three-color test. The two methods agree in every case. The counters equal, in increasing degree order, \(1080,2280,3600,3120,3120,2160,1320\); their sum is \(16680\). Double counting the endpoints of each zonotope edge gives \(\tfrac12\sum_p d(p)=100080\).

**Symmetry refinement.** Every permutation of the underlying five-element set acts on its two-subsets and preserves the disjointness relation. These are all automorphisms: the five maximum independent vertex sets of cardinality four are exactly the five stars of two-subsets containing a given symbol, so an automorphism permutes the five stars faithfully. The same `CHECK.py` enumerates all \(120\) induced edge-bit transformations, computes the orbit of each acyclic orientation, and checks degree constancy within each orbit. The displayed orbit table is the exact counter; its total is \(168\) orbits and \(16680\) vertices.

**Independent coherent-spanning-tree census.** The fifteen edges have \(\binom{15}{9}\) nine-subsets, of which exactly \(2000\) connect all ten vertices. For each such tree and each of its six non-tree chords, calculate the unique undirected tree path joining the chord endpoints. A tree is the Hasse reduction of some acyclic orientation exactly when its nine edges admit directions under which every one of these six paths is consistently directed; the corresponding chord is then forced in the same direction and nonflippable. The direct certificate tries all \(2^9\) orientations for each candidate tree and counts precisely \(540\) admissible trees. The full \(S_5\) action partitions them into one orbit of size \(60\) and four of size \(120\). By the connected-biconnected tree criterion of Cordovil–Forge (Theorem 3), each admissible tree occurs for exactly two opposite acyclic orientations. Therefore \(2\cdot540=1080\), independently reproducing the smooth count from the full orientation census. In particular, the ten smooth fixed-point orbits under \(S_5\) are consistent with the five coherent-tree orbits.

Every algorithmic loop is bounded by the finite explicitly described sets. This is an exhaustive combinatorial proof for this one fixed graph; no infinite extrapolation or unverified symbolic computation is needed.

## Verification

Run `python3 CHECK.py` with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. It constructs \(KG(5,2)\) from disjoint pairs, exhausts all \(32768\) orientation words, independently checks the degree via edge reversals, checks every \(S_5\)-orbit, enumerates all potential nine-edge trees and their coherent orientations, and asserts every reported integer. The final line on successful completion is `VERIFY_OK`.

Checks independently cross-reference (i) Kahn reachability versus DFS edge-flip counts; (ii) direct degree-nine enumeration versus twice the number of coherent spanning trees; and (iii) point totals versus symmetry-orbit totals. The certificate relies on elementary finite exhaustiveness, while the geometric dictionary and the integral-tree basis argument are proved above.

## Relationship to prior work

Postnikov–Reiner–Williams, *Faces of Generalized Permutohedra* (arXiv:math/0609184, first posted 2006-09-06), Section 5, identify graphic zonotopes and characterize when an **entire** graphic zonotope is simple. Their Proposition 5.2 and subsequent proof supply a general tree-Hasse viewpoint, but not this Petersen local-degree distribution or its automorphism refinement. Cordovil–Forge, *Flipping in Acyclic and Strongly Connected Graphs*, Lemma 1 and Theorem 3, provide the general flippability and coherent-tree criteria, including the two opposite orientations per tree in biconnected graphs; this classification is used, not rediscovered. Grujić, *Counting Faces of Graphical Zonotopes* (arXiv:1604.06931, first posted 2016-04-23), Theorem 1.1 determines global face counts in general, so the recovered total of \(100080\) edges alone is **not** a new invariant. The full distribution of edges **among vertices**, the orbit splitting, and the count of coherent Petersen spanning-tree orbits are finer statements not determined by those global face numbers. Pilaud's work on acyclic reorientation lattices provides a broader flip-graph framework without the explicit Petersen census in the inspected text.

## Limitations

The statements describe torus-fixed points, their invariant-curve degrees, their smoothness, and the symmetry of these finite sets. They do not classify positive-dimensional singular strata or analytic local singularity types at nonsmooth points. The result is a graph-specific, computer-verifiable finite theorem, not a universal formula for all graphical zonotopes. The accessible literature comparisons do not rule out an unindexed equivalent census or a differently stated table. Independent peer audit has not been performed.
