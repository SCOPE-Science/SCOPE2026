# Self-review — ternary projective-plane transversal exchange geodesics

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is precisely the point-exchange geodesic theorem for line-hitting point subsets (including those containing a line) of the thirteen-point projective plane. Every possible subset is enumerated independently using two incidence models, and the resulting hitting-set families agree under an explicit projective/affine coordinate correspondence. The crucial constructive lemma is proved by finite exhaustive testing: for every ordered pair of distinct valid same-size sets at each of the eight cardinalities six through thirteen, an exchange inserting a target point and deleting a nontarget point is found. This checks all 3,995,862 pairs and inductively proves geodesics of length equal to Johnson distance. An independent all-source BFS at cardinality six verifies exact pair distances and the 468/234 eccentricity classes. The full source and output certificate are included. Mathematical limitation: the finite case analysis proves nothing about higher-order planes or the stricter nontrivial-blocking-set definition.

## Originality — PASS, with acknowledged residual access risk
The prior geometric literature and a 2022 complete classification of *minimal* blocking sets concern static enumeration and isomorphism, which do not by themselves give the directed exchange axiom, all-cardinality Johnson isometry, radius/diameter, or sharp reconfiguration threshold. The 2014 full-text independent-set reconfiguration paper concerns a different object (independent sets of claw-free graphs); its claims do not imply the hitting-set shortest-path identity. Searches of published mathematical findings for projective-plane point-exchange reconfiguration, target-directed exchanges, isometric Johnson embedding, order-three transversals and the numerical thresholds found no equivalent or stronger result; the closest hits concern other projective-plane orders, static blocking sets, topology, or an unrelated matroid exchange graph. The 2022 classification's publisher abstract/references and the associated static enumeration site were accessible, but the complete paper body was not obtained after lawful open-access and institutional attempts. Consequently, the possibility of an exchange corollary in that unavailable full text or other unindexed work is expressly retained as risk, not claimed to be disproved.

## Value — PASS
This result locates a natural and sharp reconfiguration phase transition at the first size just above minimal nontrivial blocking sets in the unique projective plane of order three: size-five feasible states form thirteen separate cliques, yet every pair of feasible size-six states admits a shortest Johnson path, and the same exact geodesic phenomenon persists at every larger size. Its all-pairs exchange assertion is stronger than connectivity, genuinely about constrained shortest paths, and makes the thirteen-point plane a finite benchmark for hitting-set reconfiguration. A verified violation of the full matroid basis-exchange axiom in the six-transversal family shows that the Johnson geodesicity is not a disguised immediate corollary of the standard matroid basis theorem. The extra exact degree and eccentricity structure at the threshold distinguishes geometric families of transversals. This is not offered as a new static small blocking-set classification.
