# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The deterministic identification with the upset lattice follows by monotone disjunctive normal form on realised valuations. On the distinct-vector matching event, each two-vertex chain contributes exactly a three-element chain of upsets and every isolated vertex contributes a two-element chain. The exact one-pair probability and a four-orientation bound for shared-vertex edges are independently verified; applying a dependency-neighbourhood Poisson bound and a union bound yields the displayed finite-parameter inequality. At the stated growth scale all exponential errors vanish. The standalone checker reconstructs the finite positive and Boolean closures from generators, rather than assuming the upset characterization. Its checks are finite corroboration, not proofs of asymptotics.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Dudley Stark (2004) established the Poisson comparable-pair law for the same random-set model, and Rényi established its earlier zero-count transition: those components are unambiguously **prior** and are credited. Finite distributive-lattice representation by upsets is also prior. The final novelty claim is specifically the high-probability isomorphism type of the positive truth-set lattice together with its quantitative Poisson *logarithmic expressivity deficit* and limiting retained fraction. The additional graph-matching step is necessary to pass from comparable-pair count to a lattice isomorphism type. Primary full-text comparisons of Stark, Marcelino--Rivieccio, Borlido--Suarez, Balogh--Krueger, and Alon--Gilboa--Gueron did not exhibit this combined statement. Its possible appearance in other probabilistic lattice literature remains a genuine residual risk; these searches cannot certify publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The number and lattice structure of definable subsets are canonical semantic invariants of a finite propositional model. This theorem gives the full nondegenerate onset distribution of the loss caused by omitting negation, not just a yes/no threshold. The three-chain factors identify the actual local semantic obstructions, and the limiting mean of \(K_{m,n}/2^n\) quantifies their net information cost. This is an exact natural mathematical invariant with a plausible role in quantitative finite-model semantics. No broader or independent theorem is claimed.
