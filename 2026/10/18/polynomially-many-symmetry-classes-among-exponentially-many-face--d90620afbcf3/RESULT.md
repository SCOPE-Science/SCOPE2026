# Polynomially many symmetry classes among exponentially many facets of mixed-theta transportation balls

## Finding

For each integer \(m\geq 2\), let \(G_m\) consist of two distinguished vertices \(a,b\), one internally disjoint \(a\)-to-\(b\) path of length two, and \(m\) additional mutually internally disjoint \(a\)-to-\(b\) paths of length three. Give every edge length one and equip the vertices with the shortest-path metric. Let \(P_m\) be the unit ball of the real Lipschitz-free (transportation-cost) space over this finite metric, regarded in the zero-coordinate-sum Euclidean vector space.

Let \(\Gamma_m\) be the group generated by graph automorphisms of \(G_m\) and central inversion of \(P_m\). Define
\[
H_m=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(j+1)(m-2j+1).
\]
**Theorem.** The facets of \(P_m\) form exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathcal O_m=\frac{1}{2}\left[\binom{m+5}{5}+H_m+3\binom{m+2}{2}+\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\right]}
\]
orbits under \(\Gamma_m\). Every orbit has the explicit multiplicity described below by a multinomial coefficient and one of the factors \(2\) or \(4\). In particular, \(\mathcal O_2=23\), \(\mathcal O_3=48\), \(\mathcal O_4=94\), and
\[
\mathcal O_m\sim m^5/240 \quad(m\to\infty).
\]
The much larger *total* facet count, previously encompassed by Braun--Bruegge's general path-bundle formula, is \(2\cdot 6^m+6\cdot3^m\). The new assertion is the orbit classification and its closed formula, not that total facet count.

The same classification shows that exactly \(2\cdot6^m+2\cdot3^m\) facets are simplices with \(2m+2\) vertices, whereas exactly \(4\cdot3^m\) facets have \(3m+1\) vertices and are nonsimplicial. This incidence split follows readily from the earlier path-bundle facet criterion and is recorded as context, not claimed as an independently novel theorem.

## Assumptions and scope

The number \(m\) is any integer at least two; the two paths' endpoints are different and all paths have disjoint interiors. There are \(2m+3\) graph vertices and \(3m+2\) graph edges, hence the transportation ball has dimension \(2m+2\). The action considered is **only** graph automorphisms and global sign reversal; the theorem does not assert that these exhaust abstract or linear polytope symmetries.

Write the short path as \(a,w,b\) and the long paths as \(a,u_i,v_i,b\) for \(1\leq i\leq m\). For a potential \(f\), normalize \(f(a)=0\), put \(\Delta=f(b)\), and encode each path by its consecutive increments from \(a\) to \(b\). A facet of \(P_m\) is supported by \(\sum_x f(x)z_x=1\) exactly when \(f\) is integer-valued, every edge increment is in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and the subgraph of edges with nonzero increment is connected and spanning. This is the established tight-edge facet criterion [1,2], recalled here to fix signs and normalization.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive local facet types.** Along a path whose internal vertices have degree two, two zero increments would disconnect an internal segment from the tight-edge subgraph. Thus every path has at most one zero increment. At least one whole path must have no zero increment, or the two poles would be disconnected. All paths have the same increment sum \(\Delta\). Since the short path has length two and every other path length three, parity forces exactly one of the following disjoint cases:

* \(\Delta=0\): the short path has increments \((1,-1)\) or \((-1,1)\); each long path has one zero and one each of \(+1,-1\), hence six possible words.
* \(\Delta=2\) or \(\Delta=-2\): the short path is \((1,1)\) or \((-1,-1)\); each long path has one zero and the other two increments equal to the sign of \(\Delta\), hence three words.
* \(\Delta=1\) or \(\Delta=-1\): the short path has exactly one zero, hence two possibilities; every long path has all edges tight and exactly one increment opposite in sign to \(\Delta\), hence three words.

In each case the nonzero-increment edges are connected and spanning. The numbers of facet potentials of these three types are respectively \(2\cdot6^m\), \(2\cdot3^m\), and \(4\cdot3^m\). The two even-\(\Delta\) types have \(2m+2\) tight edges, forming a spanning tree; the odd-\(\Delta\) type has \(3m+1\) tight edges. Each tight edge gives precisely one vertex on its supporting facet, so the former facets are simplices and the latter are not when \(m\ge2\).

**2. Exact symmetry group.** The poles are precisely the vertices of degree \(m+1>2\), and the short internal vertex is uniquely adjacent to both poles. Hence \(\operatorname{Aut}(G_m)\) permutes the \(m\) long arms arbitrarily and may simultaneously interchange the poles and reverse every arm. It is \(S_m\times C_2\); adjoining central inversion yields an action of \(S_m\times C_2\times C_2\). Let \(M(c)=m!/\prod_i c_i!\) for any vector of nonnegative multiplicities summing to \(m\).

**3. Zero endpoint difference.** Use sign reversal to select the short-path word \((1,-1)\). The six long-path words can be ordered as
\[
(1,0,-1),\ (-1,0,1),\ (0,1,-1),\ (1,-1,0),\ (0,-1,1),\ (-1,1,0).
\]
Pole interchange acts by reversing the order of every word and negating each entry. Thus its action on these six letters fixes the first two and interchanges the third/fourth and fifth/sixth. After quotienting by long-arm permutations, the data are six nonnegative multiplicities \(c\) summing to \(m\). Let \(c^*\) be obtained by the displayed two transpositions. One facet orbit is determined by the unordered pair \(\{c,c^*\}\); its size is \(2M(c)\) when \(c=c^*\) and \(4M(c)\) otherwise. There are \(\binom{m+5}{5}\) six-letter multisets, of which
\[
H_m=[t^m](1-t)^{-2}(1-t^2)^{-2}
=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(j+1)(m-2j+1)
\]
are fixed by the involution. Burnside's lemma gives \((\binom{m+5}{5}+H_m)/2\) orbits of this type.

**4. Endpoint difference of magnitude two.** Use sign reversal to select \(\Delta=2\), so the short word is \((1,1)\). The three long-path words have the single zero in position one, two, or three. The pole-interchange/sign composition now simply reverses these words, exchanging the outer two types. A count vector \(c=(c_1,c_2,c_3)\) is therefore identified with \((c_3,c_2,c_1)\). Its orbit size is \(2M(c)\) for a fixed vector and \(4M(c)\) otherwise. Exactly \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\) of the \(\binom{m+2}{2}\) three-letter multisets are fixed. This gives \((\binom{m+2}{2}+\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1)/2\) orbits.

**5. Endpoint difference of magnitude one.** Again normalize \(\Delta=1\). Each long arm has its unique negative increment in one of three positions, and the short path is \((0,1)\) or \((1,0)\). The pole-interchange/sign composition reverses all long words *and* exchanges the two short-path words. Therefore fixing the short word to \((0,1)\) chooses exactly one representative three-letter multiset \(c\) from each orbit. All \(\binom{m+2}{2}\) such multisets are inequivalent, and each corresponding orbit contains exactly \(4M(c)\) facets.

Adding the three Burnside counts proves the boxed orbit formula and all orbit-size claims. The first term has leading coefficient \(1/240\) in \(m\); the other terms grow at most cubically, proving the asymptotic. No finite enumeration is used in the all-\(m\) proof.

## Verification

The included standalone Python program `verify_theta.py` independently enumerates **every** normalized integral potential by assigning each spanning-tree increment one of \(-1,0,1\), testing the remaining edges, and checking tight-edge connectivity. The chosen spanning tree is only a coordinate system: it is not assumed tight. The program also generates actual potential orbits under adjacent arm exchanges, pole interchange, and central inversion, and compares the full *multiset of orbit sizes within each endpoint-difference class* with the above analytic classification.

Exact tests succeeded for \(m=2,3,4\): \((\#\mathrm{facets},\mathcal O_m)=(126,23),(594,48),(3078,94)\). Additional exhaustive potential/face-size tests succeeded for \(m=5\), with \(17010\) facets. All arithmetic is integer and uses the Python standard library. The finite tests are cross-checks, not a proof for arbitrarily large \(m\).

## Relationship to prior work

Higashitani--Jochemko--Michałek [1] proved the connected tight-edge facet criterion. Chen--Davis--Korchevskaia [2] developed the correspondence with maximal bipartite facet subgraphs and combinatorial facet generation. Braun--Bruegge [3, Proposition 4.6] gave a formula for **all** mixed-parity path bundles, which already implies the numerical count \(2\cdot6^m+6\cdot3^m\) for the present graph. D'Alì--Delucchi--Michałek [4] relate these graph polytopes to finite transportation-metric unit balls. None of these inspected treatments states the three multiset-orbit classifications, the exact orbit sizes, or the resulting closed degree-five symmetry-orbit formula for this family. No claim of novelty is made for the general facet criterion or the aggregate facet count.

## Limitations

The symmetry-orbit assertion is restricted to \(m\ge2\). The case \(m=1\) is a five-cycle with additional dihedral symmetries, and is deliberately excluded. The result concerns the real unit-edge graph metric, not arbitrary edge weights or the full group of every possible linear polytope automorphism. Broad combinatorial methods in the cited literature make the orbit result derivable with additional work; a previously unindexed explicit orbit census might exist. Independent external scientific audit has not been performed.
