# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is limited to fixed tensor dimension, strictly positive real resolvent parameter approaching zero, and nonzero nonnegative real inputs. Schur estimates yield \(\|T_d^n\|\le(n!)^{-d}\), ensuring a legitimate norm-convergent resolvent expansion. The integral kernel is the entire positive series \(E_d\), whose precise exponential type follows from an exponential-series upper bound and a single-term Stirling lower bound. The operator-norm lower bound uses the constant vector on a terminal output cube; the vector lower bound uses a fixed positive-measure support patch carrying a positive amount of input mass. All normalization and null-set issues are addressed in the proof. The marginals example is checked by direct support geometry. The standalone finite checks supplement the analytic proof only.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by priority risk

The full text of Hu–Ji–Liang, arXiv:2607.01614v1, gives an abstract result that \(1\) belongs to every quasinilpotent power set and a realization theorem for right-closed sets, but does not derive tensor Volterra local-type estimates. The full text of Ji–Zhang, arXiv:2305.09963v1, computes the one-variable Volterra power set and a family of one-variable witnesses, without the joint-support tensor formula. The published research-record searches found no theorem covering the present all-positive-vector multidimensional assertion. In particular, classification of the scalar one-variable power set does not imply the exact relative type of a general correlated tensor input. General Laplace principles and earlier multivariate integral-operator estimates remain possible priority risks; the absence of a direct match is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The power-set literature motivates quantitative local resolvent growth on quasinilpotent operators; computing only the range of values loses information about **which input vectors** have which growth rates. The theorem supplies a complete, exact, all-dimensional classification of the positive cone for a natural mixed-integration operator. The cross/diagonal pair proves that correlations between variables change this operator-theoretic invariant even when the marginal support data are identical. The fact is self-contained and defines a concrete support-geometric filtration, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a generic textbook renaming.

## Restrictions and residual risks

The ordinary complex-parameter power set, sign-changing inputs, and uniform estimates in growing dimension are not settled. Standard positive-kernel Laplace methods supply key techniques and could appear in broader literature not retrieved; no independent priority audit has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
