# Self-review

**Correctness: PASS.** Relative ranks make the claim distribution-free. The proof classifies maximum/minimum cell pairs by whether they share a row, a column, or neither, derives their joint events from independent uniform entries, and exactly evaluates all three beta integrals. Positivity is proved for the unrestricted range by a central-binomial lower bound. The separate checker compares 351 exact constraints, including exhaustive permutation arrays up to eight cells and independently expanded beta-binomial integrals. Enumeration is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS, with historical priority risk.** The closest direct predecessor, Roberts (2005), supplies the univariate pure-equilibrium count and compares payoffs among maxima; that does not determine the number of minima in the same array or their joint count. Bhamidi, Dey, and Nobel (2012 arXiv v1; published later) analyze Gaussian submatrix-sum optima, not this bivariate entrywise-maxima/minima law. Searches for dual max–min local-optimum count distributions, equivalences through payoff reversal, and published finding databases identified no stronger covering claim. The apparent difference is one of statements and implications, not wording alone. Some earlier combinatorial identities may implicitly contain the joint formula; independent priority review is warranted.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The two-row problem is the smallest nontrivial matrix size supporting multiple simultaneous row/column optima; the number of columns remains unbounded. The exact joint law resolves a natural dependence question left by the known marginal distribution. Strictly positive dependence paired with an \(n^{-2}\) correlation decay distinguishes finite and asymptotic coupling of the best and worst local optima. This is a complete natural two-row classification rather than an arbitrary fixed-size computation.

**Scientific limits.** Continuous i.i.d. entries and exactly two rows are essential to the statement. No larger-row formulas, nonidentical entry distributions, or independent literature certification are claimed.
