# Preference recovery and conditional spectra on Boolean formal contexts

## Finding

The following theorem gives an exact information comparison between typical concepts and defeasible attribute conditionals in a natural finite formal context. Let \(n\geq 2\), let \(G=M=[n]\), and use contranominal incidence \(gIm\) exactly when \(g\ne m\). Equip the objects with an arbitrary finite partial order \(P\), with \(x\prec_P y\) interpreted as \(x\) being strictly preferred to \(y\).

For a subset \(X\subseteq G\), let \(\operatorname{Min}_P(X)\) be its set of minimal elements. For the premise \(A=G\setminus X\), let \(D_P(X)\) be the singleton consequences of the preferential conditional \(A\rightsquigarrow\{x\}\) that do *not* follow from the ordinary formal-context implication \(A\to\{x\}\). Define the strict-consequence spectrum
\[
H_P(z)=\sum_{X\subseteq G}|D_P(X)|z^{|X|}.
\]
Then all of the following assertions hold, with no restriction on \(P\).

**(i) Two exact information levels.** The ordinary concept lattice is \(2^G\). Every preference order satisfies the validity condition used for typical concepts in the motivating preferential framework. The typical-concept extents are exactly the antichains of \(P\), ordered by inclusion. Consequently, the typical concepts determine precisely the *undirected comparability graph* of \(P\), not its orientations. They form a meet-closed family; two typical extents have a common upper bound in that family exactly when their union is an antichain. Therefore this family is a lattice (equivalently, a join-subsemilattice of the ambient Boolean lattice) exactly when \(P\) has no strict comparisons.

**(ii) Exact strict-conditional law and numerical reconstruction.** Write \(d_P(x)=|\{y:y\prec_P x\}|\). For every \(X\subseteq G\),
\[
D_P(X)=X\setminus\operatorname{Min}_P(X),
\qquad
H_P(z)=z\sum_{x\in G}\left[(1+z)^{n-1}-(1+z)^{n-1-d_P(x)}\right].
\]
In particular, \(H_P\) uniquely determines the multiset of strict-predecessor counts \(\{d_P(x):x\in G\}\), but need not determine the order, even when the entire labeled family of typical concepts is also known.

**(iii) Sharp recovery by conditionals.** For distinct \(x,y\in G\),
\[
x\prec_P y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(G\setminus\{x,y\})\rightsquigarrow\{y\}.
\]
The displayed conditional is genuinely nonclassical. Thus the singleton conditional table on premises of size exactly \(n-2\) reconstructs the entire labeled preference order. This bound is sharp in the worst case: for every \(n\ge3\) there are distinct preference orders which agree on *all* preferential conditionals with premises of size at most \(n-3\), even allowing arbitrary sets of attributes as conclusions.

The difference between these information levels appears on just three objects. Under \(a,b\prec c\) and its reversal \(c\prec a,b\), the typical-concept families coincide, but the respective strict-consequence polynomials are \(2z^2+z^3\) and \(2z^2+2z^3\). On five objects there are even nonisomorphic orders with the *same* labeled typical-concept family and the *same* strict-consequence polynomial; an explicit pair is given below.

## Assumptions and scope

All sets are finite. The partial order is antisymmetric, transitive and reflexive; \(\prec_P\) denotes its strict part. The attribute name \(x\) and object name \(x\) are deliberately identified but represent opposite sorts of the formal context. A premise \(A\) satisfies \(A'=G\setminus A\). The minimised derivation is the set of \(P\)-minimal objects in \(A'\); the positive preferential conditional holds precisely when every such minimal object has the conclusion attribute. The polynomial weights an antecedent by the cardinality of its matching-object set \(X=G\setminus A\), not by the cardinality of the premise itself. Counted consequents are singletons *not* already classically implied; arbitrary-conclusion conditionals are considered only for the sharp recovery lower bound. No claims are made for infinite contexts, arbitrary incidence matrices, or unrelated nonmonotonic semantics.

## Proof

**Incidence and typical concepts.** In the contranominal context every attribute set \(A\subseteq M\) has derivation \(A'=G\setminus A\), and every object set \(Y\subseteq G\) has derivation \(Y'=M\setminus Y\). Hence both double-derivation operators are identities. In particular every minimised object set is closed, so every preference order is admissible under the stipulated validity definition. The typical extent generated by \(A\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Min}_P(G\setminus A)\). This is always an antichain. Conversely, for an antichain \(Y\), choose \(A=G\setminus Y\); then \(\operatorname{Min}_P(Y)=Y\). Thus every antichain and no other subset occurs. A two-element subset is an antichain exactly when its points are incomparable, proving recovery of the undirected comparability graph. Antichains are closed under intersection. A common upper bound of two antichains, within the antichain family ordered by inclusion, must contain their union, so it exists exactly when that union is an antichain, in which case the union is their join. The family has all joins exactly when no comparable pair exists.

**Conditional formula.** Fix \(X\subseteq G\) and \(A=G\setminus X\). The attributes common to the preferred members of \(A'\) form \(M\setminus\operatorname{Min}_P(X)\). Ordinary derivation gives \(A''=A\). Subtracting the ordinary conclusions gives precisely \(D_P(X)=X\setminus\operatorname{Min}_P(X)\). Fix an element \(x\in G\). It contributes to \(D_P(X)\) exactly when \(x\in X\) and \(X\) contains at least one strict predecessor of \(x\). The generating polynomial for all sets \(X\) containing \(x\) is \(z(1+z)^{n-1}\); that for sets containing \(x\) but no predecessor is \(z(1+z)^{n-1-d_P(x)}\). Subtraction and summation prove the displayed identity. Moreover,
\[
n(1+z)^{n-1}-\frac{H_P(z)}z
=\sum_{x\in G}(1+z)^{n-1-d_P(x)}.
\]
The powers of \(1+z\) constitute a basis for polynomials of degree at most \(n-1\). Their coefficients therefore recover the multiplicity of each predecessor count: the coefficient of \((1+z)^{n-1-j}\) is exactly the number of elements with \(d_P(x)=j\).

**Exact reconstruction and sharp cutoff.** On \(X=\{x,y\}\), the element \(y\) is nonminimal exactly when \(x\prec_P y\). This proves the pairwise test and reconstruction. To prove that shorter premises cannot suffice, let \(G=\{x,y\}\sqcup Z\) with \(Z\ne\varnothing\). Define one order by declaring every element of \(Z\cup\{x\}\) strictly below \(y\) and no further comparisons; define another by declaring every element of \(Z\) strictly below \(y\), leaving \(x\) isolated. Their minimised derivations coincide on every \(X\) with \(|X|\ge3\): the only possibly different minimality status is that of \(y\), and whenever both \(x\) and \(y\) occur in such an \(X\), some member of \(Z\) also occurs and already defeats \(y\) in both orders. All preferential conditionals for premises \(A=G\setminus X\) of size at most \(n-3\) therefore agree. The premise omitting exactly \(x,y\) distinguishes the orders, giving sharpness.

**Two forms of information loss.** For the three-element fork \(a,b\prec c\) and its reversed fork \(c\prec a,b\), the incomparable pairs coincide, so the typical families are equal. Their predecessor-count multisets are \(\{0,0,2\}\) and \(\{0,1,1\}\), giving the two stated polynomials. For a stronger five-point collision, take the transitive closures of the following cover relations:
\[
P:\quad 2\prec3,\ 3\prec1,\ 3\prec4,\ 0\prec4;
\qquad
Q:\quad 4\prec2,\ 2\prec3,\ 1\prec2,\ 4\prec0.
\]
Both have the same six undirected comparable pairs
\[
\{\{0,4\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{2,4\},\{3,4\}\}
\]
and the same predecessor-count multiset \(\{0,0,1,2,3\}\). Hence they have identical labeled typical families and
\[
H_P(z)=H_Q(z)=6z^2+14z^3+11z^4+3z^5.
\]
Yet the unique vertex of degree three in their respective Hasse diagrams has one lower and two upper covers in \(P\), versus two lower and one upper cover in \(Q\). No order isomorphism can interchange these cases. This proves that even the pair of invariants (typical family, full strict-consequence polynomial) is incomplete; the individual pairwise conditional table is complete.

## Relationship to prior work

Carr, Leisegang, Meyer and Rudolph introduce object-preference minimisation, positive nonmonotonic attribute conditionals, and typical concepts; their 2024 preprint proves a meet-semilattice theorem under a validity assumption and asks about the relationship between typical concepts and the conditional relation. The present result concerns that relationship in the complete Boolean contranominal context, where their validity condition is automatic. Dürrschnabel, Koyda and Stumme describe contranominal contexts and their Boolean concept lattices; their 2021 work concerns attribute reduction rather than preference-order recovery or conditional spectra. The elementary fact that nondominated choices from two-element menus reveal a preference comparison is classical choice theory, and is not claimed as new. The contribution assessed here is the combined complete information hierarchy, the strict-consequence spectrum and its inverse, the sharp limitation of short premises, and the concrete distinction between the two information losses. No independence or publication-priority claim is made.

## Limitations

The conclusions are specific to finite contranominal incidence, not arbitrary formal contexts or arbitrary preferential logics. The polynomial records only singleton strict-consequence counts, not the full conditional truth table. The five-point example establishes nonuniqueness but not a complete classification of such collisions. The direct pairwise-recovery observation has a classical choice-theoretic analogue. Earlier equivalent FCA specialisations may exist under alternative terminology; external independent verification and exhaustive priority assessment have not been performed.
