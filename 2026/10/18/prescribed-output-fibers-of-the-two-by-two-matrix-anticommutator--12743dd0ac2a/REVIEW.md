# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof partitions the first matrix by trace and rank, computes the Sylvester-map rank and affine fiber over each stratum, and accounts for the centralizer/trace-pairing boundary in characteristic two. The rank-one line-counting argument identifies exactly which complements contribute. The general proof applies to every prime-power field. Exhaustive independent matrix-pair enumeration checks every prescribed target for fields of sizes two, three, four and five.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit residual risk.** The claim is the complete *nonzero-output* fiber distribution, not the existence of anticommutator values or the already known zero-fiber counts. Britnell–Wildon treats commuting similarity-class compatibility, Kanel-Belov–Malev–Rowen studies polynomial image sets, and Huang's Theorems 1.1–1.2 give the zero-output anti-commuting count. None of the inspected theorem statements furnishes the entire targetwise formula. Equivalent formulations under a difference-of-squares substitution, prior commutator-fiber counts in characteristic two, and unlocated papers on inhomogeneous quantum-plane representations remain plausible coverage risks; an independent literature audit is needed before asserting priority without qualification.

**Value — PASS.** Prescribed word-map fibers encode much more than image sets or the zero locus. The complete targetwise distribution is a natural first noncommutative matrix-word counting problem, distinguishes rational similarity types, and identifies a sharp characteristic-two collapse. It gives an exactly solvable benchmark for point counting on inhomogeneous matrix-relation varieties.
