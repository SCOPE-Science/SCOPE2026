# Primary Jacobian thickening and rational-normal-curve depth jump for full binary Perazzo hypersurfaces

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge3\) and every algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero, put
\[
F_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}X_j U^{m-j}V^j\in T=k[X_0,\ldots,X_m,U,V],\qquad J_m=(\partial F_m),\quad R_m=T/J_m.
\]
The hypersurface \(V(F_m)\subset\mathbb P^{m+2}\) is irreducible and has reduced singular support \(\Pi=V(U,V)\cong\mathbb P^m\). Its projective Jacobian singular scheme is **not reduced** but is **primary and saturated**:
\[
\sqrt{J_m}=(U,V),\quad \operatorname{Ass}_T(R_m)=\{(U,V)\},\quad J_m:(X_0,\ldots,X_m,U,V)^\infty=J_m.
\]
Let \(S=k[X_0,\ldots,X_m]\), and let \(\Phi_m\) be the \(m\)-by-\(2\) matrix whose row indexed \(0\le j<m\) is
\[
\big((m-j)X_j,\ (j+1)X_{j+1}\big).
\]
Writing \(E_m=\operatorname{coker}(S(-m)^2\xrightarrow{\Phi_m}S(-(m-1))^m)\), there is an exact graded \(S\)-module decomposition
\[
R_m\simeq\left(\bigoplus_{r=0}^{m-2}S(-r)^{r+1}\right)\oplus E_m.
\]
The matrix has rank strictly below two exactly on the rational normal curve
\[
C_m=\left\{[\binom{m}{0}a^m:\binom{m}{1}a^{m-1}b:\cdots:\binom{m}{m}b^m]:[a:b]\in\mathbb P^1\right\}\subset\Pi.
\]
Consequently the (nonreduced) projective Jacobian singular scheme is Cohen--Macaulay away from \(C_m\) and **not Cohen--Macaulay at any point of** \(C_m\). Its nilradical \(\mathcal N=(U,V)\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Sing}(V(F_m))}\) satisfies \(\mathcal N^m=0\) and has graded layers \(\mathcal N^r/\mathcal N^{r+1}\simeq\mathcal O_{\Pi}(-r)^{r+1}\) for \(1\le r\le m-2\), with final layer \(\mathcal N^{m-1}\simeq\widetilde E_m\). The generic multiplicity along \(\Pi\) is \(m(m+1)/2-2\).

The all-degree Hilbert series and arithmetic depth are
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Hilb}_{R_m}(t)=\frac{\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(r+1)t^r-2t^m}{(1-t)^{m+1}},\qquad \operatorname{depth}R_m=m<\dim R_m=m+1.}
\]
For \(m=3\), the final nilpotent layer is \(\widetilde E_3\simeq\mathcal I_{C_3}\), the ideal sheaf of a twisted cubic in \(\mathbb P^3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The integer \(m\) is any integer at least three, \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, \(T\) is standard graded with all variables of degree one, and \(J_m\) means the ideal generated by **all** first partial derivatives (not the apolar annihilator). The singular scheme is the projective scheme \(\operatorname{Proj}(T/J_m)\); by Euler's identity, \(F_m\in J_m\) in characteristic zero. The sheaf \(\widetilde E_m\) is the sheafification of the explicit graded \(S\)-module \(E_m\).

## Proof

**1. Jacobian presentation.** The \(m+1\) derivatives with respect to \(X_j\) are the degree-\(m\) monomials \(U^{m-j}V^j\), so their ideal is \((U,V)^m\). The other two derivatives are
\[
F_U=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(m-j)X_jU^{m-1-j}V^j,\quad
F_V=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(j+1)X_{j+1}U^{m-1-j}V^j.
\]
As an \(S\)-module, \(T/(U,V)^m\) has the direct-sum basis of the monomials \(U^{r-j}V^j\) of every \((U,V)\)-degree \(0\le r<m\). Since the remaining two Jacobian generators have \((U,V)\)-degree \(m-1\), multiplication by \(U\) or \(V\) annihilates them in this quotient. Thus all the relations occupy exactly the top direct summand of rank \(m\), and their coefficient map is \(\Phi_m:S(-m)^2\to S(-(m-1))^m\). Its minor from rows zero and one equals \(2mX_0X_2-(m-1)X_1^2\), a nonzero polynomial; hence \(\Phi_m\) is injective. This proves the claimed decomposition and the length-one graded free resolution of \(E_m\).

**2. Rank degeneracy and primaryness.** Define \(Y_j=X_j/\binom mj\). Row \(j\) of \(\Phi_m\) is the nonzero scalar \(m\binom{m-1}{j}\) times \((Y_j,Y_{j+1})\). Its rank-at-most-one minors therefore generate, after this coordinate rescaling, the standard Hankel-minor prime ideal of the rational normal curve \(C_m\). Its codimension in \(\operatorname{Spec}S\) is \(m-1\ge2\). Off this determinantal locus \(\Phi_m\) splits and \(E_m\) is free of rank \(m-2\). At a height-one prime \(\mathfrak p\), \(E_{m,\mathfrak p}\) is consequently free. At any prime of height at least two, the exact injective free presentation yields \(\operatorname{depth}_{S_{\mathfrak p}}E_{m,\mathfrak p}\ge\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)-1\ge1\) by Auslander--Buchsbaum. Hence \(E_m\) has no nonzero associated prime and is torsion-free over the domain \(S\). The decomposition shows \(R_m\) is also torsion-free as an \(S\)-module. Since \(U,V\) act nilpotently, its only associated \(T\)-prime is \((U,V)\), proving that \(J_m\) is \((U,V)\)-primary. This prime is strictly contained in the homogeneous irrelevant ideal, so \(J_m\) is saturated. Since \(\partial_{X_j}F_m\) vanish simultaneously exactly when \(U=V=0\), the reduced singular support is \(\Pi\).

**3. Cohen--Macaulay and nilpotent strata.** At a projective point \(p\in\Pi\setminus C_m\) the matrix splits, so \(R_m\) is free over the regular local ring \(S_p\); it is therefore Cohen--Macaulay. At \(p\in C_m\), the matrix has rank one and cannot be split injective; \(E_{m,p}\) has projective dimension one. Auslander--Buchsbaum gives \(\operatorname{depth}E_{m,p}=\dim S_p-1\), so \(R_{m,p}\), a direct sum with free modules, is not Cohen--Macaulay. The \((U,V)\)-degree splitting identifies the nilpotent filtration as stated; \((U,V)^m=0\) but \((U,V)^{m-1}\ne0\) generically. Over the function field \(k(\Pi)\), the top graded layer has dimension \(m-2\), while lower degrees contribute \(\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}(r+1)\). Their sum is \(m(m+1)/2-2\).

**4. Hilbert series and depth.** The free summands contribute \(\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}(r+1)t^r/(1-t)^{m+1}\). The injective two-column resolution of \(E_m\) contributes \((mt^{m-1}-2t^m)/(1-t)^{m+1}\). Their sum is the displayed formula. The \(S\)-projective dimension of \(R_m\) is one, and \(\dim S=m+1\); hence its graded depth is \(m\), equal to its depth as a finite \(S\)-algebra. For \(m=3\), the maximal minors of the three-by-two Hankel matrix are the quadrics of the twisted cubic, and the Hilbert--Burch resolution identifies \(E_3\) with \(I_{C_3}\). Finally, \(F_m\) is irreducible: as a polynomial linear in the \(X_j\), it is primitive over \(k[U,V]\) since the coefficients \(U^m,\ldots,V^m\) have gcd one.

## Verification

The standalone script `VERIFY_COMPUTATION.py` builds **all** Jacobian derivatives, enumerates homogeneous monomial multiples, and performs exact sparse modular rank computations in two primes. It checks the displayed all-degree Hilbert formula in degrees \(m\) through \(m+3\) for \(m\in\{3,4,5\}\), together with rank-one Veronese specializations of \(\Phi_m\); these 27 checks completed with `VERIFY_OK`. The infinite theorem follows from the symbolic free resolution and depth arguments above, not from extrapolation of finite tests. The source code is sufficient to reproduce the checks with a standard Python 3 installation.

## Relationship to prior work

The vanishing-Hessian family is part of the classical Perazzo / Gordan--Noether line of examples. Ciliberto--Russo--Simis, *Homaloidal hypersurfaces and hypersurfaces with vanishing Hessian*, Section 2.5, discuss Perazzo-type high-multiplicity linear cores and their polar geometry. Gondim--Russo, *On cubic hypersurfaces with vanishing hessian*, concern especially cubics, including the threefold \(m=2\) case. Mezzetti--Miró-Roig, *Perazzo n-folds and the weak Lefschetz property*, Section 2.3, describe full Perazzo forms and study the **Artinian apolar Gorenstein algebra** \(A_{F_m}=D/\operatorname{Ann}(F_m)\). That algebra must not be confused with the **ordinary Jacobian/Milnor algebra** \(R_m=T/(\partial F_m)\) studied here. The cited sources do not explicitly give the presented all-size primaryness, rational-normal-curve Cohen--Macaulay failure locus, or Hilbert series. The small cubic case \(m=2\), unlike the present range, has an embedded discriminant component in its Jacobian scheme. No claim about its novelty is made here.

## Limitations

This theorem is restricted to \(m\ge3\) and characteristic zero. It concerns the exact projective Jacobian singular scheme, not the apolar algebra, minimal free resolution over the full polynomial ring \(T\), or local analytic classification of the ambient hypersurface along its singular plane. The rank-degeneracy locus is classical; its role as the non-Cohen--Macaulay locus of this Jacobian thickening is the asserted new connection. Equivalent implicit coverage in earlier literature, especially work on gradient ideals of full binary-form coefficient hypersurfaces, remains a residual originality risk. Independent validation has not been performed.
