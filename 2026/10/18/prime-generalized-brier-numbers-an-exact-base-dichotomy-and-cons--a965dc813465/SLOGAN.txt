A base \(b\ge2\) admits infinitely many prime generalized Brier coefficients—and arbitrarily long strings of consecutive primes of that kind—if and only if \(b\) is even and \(3\nmid b-1\). In every qualifying base, a positive proportion of primes lie in one fixed progression whose sufficiently large elements make both \(kb^n-1\) and \(kb^n+1\) divisible by at least two distinct primes for every \(n\ge1\).
