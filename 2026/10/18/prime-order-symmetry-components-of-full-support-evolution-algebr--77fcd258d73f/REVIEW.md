# Scientific review: Prime-order symmetry components of full-support evolution algebras

## Correctness (PASS)

For \(n\ge4\) and arbitrary characteristic, monomiality of algebra isomorphisms between perfect evolution algebras follows from the independence of the natural-basis squares. Nonzero diagonal entries have a unique rescaling to one, so the residual ordinary automorphisms of a normalized full-support structure matrix are exactly its simultaneous row-column permutation stabilizers. The fixed locus of each permutation is the open determinant-nonvanishing part of the torus of its ordered-edge orbits, nonempty because the restricted determinant has constant term one. A prime-order permutation subgroup is recoverable from these ordered-edge orbitals: any permutation stabilizing each orbital acts by a common cyclic translation on all moved vertex cycles. This rules out containment among distinct prime-order fixed subtori, while every composite-order stabilizing permutation has a prime-order power. Thus all and only prime-subgroup fixed subtori are irreducible components.

Burnside's orbit count for a permutation of prime order \(p\) with \(r\) cycles yields codimension \(r(p-1)(2n-pr-1)\). Nontranspositions fix at most \((n-3)(n-4)\) off-diagonal ordered pairs, giving codimension at least \(3n-6\). Transpositions have codimension \(2n-3\), and finite-field determinant-zero estimates with inclusion-exclusion give the sharp coefficient \(\binom n2\) in the probability. The program `artifacts/check_symmetry_locus.py` exhaustively checks the orbital-rigidity lemma for \(n=4,5,6\), directly checks \(n=4\) full-support matrices over the binary and ternary fields, and checks selected fixed-locus counts. These computations are corroborations, not proof of the universal result. An ordinary finite quotient preserves the component dimensions; its component labels are the conjugacy types \((p,r)\).

## Originality (PASS within checked coverage)

Wei and Zou, arXiv:2310.12368v1 (18 October 2023), establish the monomial/permutation framework and Burnside class counts of perfect evolution algebras. The classification principle and their low-dimensional class census are *prior results*, and not claimed as novel here. The new statements concern the all-dimensional prime-order irreducible decomposition of the exceptional-stabilizer locus, its precise codimension stratification and the sharp leading asymptotic constant. The 2023 article of Costoya, Muñoz, Tocino and Viruel constructs Cayley evolution algebras realizing automorphism groups, and the related 2024/2025 Costoya, Mayorga and Viruel work treats permutation representations and symmetry restrictions, rather than these complete symmetry-locus components. A three-dimensional full-support automorphism-order census addresses a disjoint dimension and a different invariant.

Actual semantic searches of mathematical records covered full-support evolution-algebra generic rigidity, nontrivial stabilizers, prime subgroup fixed tori, transposition codimension and asymptotic symmetry probabilities. The closest published record identified a one-parameter evolution-algebra symmetry jump, not the all-dimensional geometric component formula. Full-text access to later sections of the Wei–Zou paper was intermittent; inspection was limited to its introduction, main class-counting statements and early orbit/Burnside derivations. Accordingly, an unindexed older colored-digraph stabilizer theorem or uninspected part of that preprint could imply part of this result. No exhaustive priority certification is claimed.

## Value (PASS)

The theorem identifies the entire reducible symmetry discriminant in a natural, high-dimensional moduli-chart family of perfect evolution algebras: its components, dimensions and multiplicities. The result explains quantitatively how generic automorphism rigidity emerges as the coefficient field grows and proves that transpositions, not more complicated automorphisms, determine the leading exceptional density. The quotient-component classification is also an intrinsic moduli invariant, not a renaming of an isolated finite count. Its all-dimensional structural and arithmetic consequences are mathematically motivated by the previously established permutation-action and isomorphism-class literature.

## Scope and residual risks

Only normalized full-support perfect evolution algebras and ordinary automorphisms are covered; the finite-field probability requires nonempty rational parameter sets. The classification does not address automorphism group schemes, exact finite-q subgroup distribution or all higher terms of the probability. The complete later sections of Wei–Zou (2023) could not be inspected in this execution; potential stronger general stabilizer-arrangement theorems were not ruled out by the checked material. Finite tests do not establish infinite-field statements. Independent mathematical review remains necessary for external certification.
