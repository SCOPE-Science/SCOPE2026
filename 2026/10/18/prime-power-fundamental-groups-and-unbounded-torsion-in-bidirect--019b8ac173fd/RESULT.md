# Prime-power fundamental groups and unbounded torsion in bidirected-polygon orbit spaces

## Finding
Let \(B_n\) be the bidirected polygon on the cyclic vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with one arrow in each direction between consecutive vertices. Let \(X_n\) be the simplicial multipath complex: an arrow set is a face exactly when its directed graph is a disjoint union of simple directed paths and isolated vertices. Write \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), acting through \(r(v)=v+1\) and \(s(v)=-v\).

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge6\) and every subgroup \(H\le D_n\), the orbit space \(|X_n|/H\) has trivial fundamental group **except** possibly when \(H=\langle r\rangle\) is the full rotation group. In that exceptional case,
\[
\pi_1(|X_n|/\langle r\rangle)\cong H_1(|X_n|/\langle r\rangle;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&n=p^e\text{ for a prime }p\text{ and }e\ge1,\\
0,&n\text{ has at least two distinct prime divisors}.
\end{cases}
\]
For every prime \(p\ge7\), the full cyclic action on \(|X_p|\) is free and the orbit has the following **additional integral homology**, in the complete range \(1\le j\le(p-5)/2\):
\[
H_j(|X_p|/\langle r\rangle;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,&j\text{ odd},\\
0,&j\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
In contrast, the covering complexes \(|X_p|\) have no homology in any of these positive degrees. In particular, the number of independent positive odd degrees exhibiting order-\(p\) torsion is \(\lfloor(p-3)/4\rfloor\), unbounded along primes.

## Assumptions and scope
The action is by permutations of **directed arcs**, not by reversal of individual arrows independent of graph symmetries. The quotients are topological orbit spaces of geometric realizations, not quotients of vertex sets or abstract orbit simplicial complexes. The fundamental-group statement covers every subgroup of the natural dihedral group for \(n\ge6\). The higher-degree integral statement is restricted to full cyclic quotients for prime polygon size \(p\ge7\) and to the displayed connectivity range; it does not determine higher homology or the full homotopy type of any quotient.

We use the established connectivity of \(X_n\) from Caputi–Collari–Smith (2026): their bidirected polygon with parameter \(n-1\) is \(\nu_n\)-connected, where \(\nu_n=\lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor-1\). Thus \(|X_n|\) is connected and simply connected for all \(n\ge6\). The same paper establishes torsion-free integral homology of each covering complex.

## Proof
**1. Exact fixed-point criterion for rotations.** Let \(r^a\) act on the arrows of \(B_n\). Put \(d=\gcd(n,a)\), with \(\gcd(n,0)=n\). A point of a simplicial realization is fixed by a simplicial permutation if and only if its uniquely supported minimal simplex is invariant and its barycentric coordinates are constant on the vertex-orbits in that simplex. In particular, an element has a fixed point if and only if some nonempty admissible arrow orbit is itself a multipath face: the forward implication follows by taking one orbit from the invariant support, and the reverse by taking its barycenter.

If \(d=1\), the \(r^a\)-orbit of each forward arrow is the entire consistently directed polygon (likewise for a backward arrow). That directed cycle is not a multipath, so no invariant nonempty simplex is possible. If \(d\ge2\), the orbit of the forward arrow \(0\to1\) consists of the arrows \(kd\to kd+1\) for \(0\le k<n/d\), in some order. Because \(d\ge2\), their underlying edges are pairwise vertex-disjoint (also across the cyclic wrap). They therefore span a multipath simplex, whose barycenter is fixed. Consequently
\[
|X_n|^{r^a}\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gcd(n,a)\ge2.
\]

**2. Every reflection fixes a point.** Every reflection is of the form \(r^as\). For odd \(n\), reflections are conjugate and we may take \(a=0\); for even \(n\), they form two conjugacy classes represented by \(a=0,1\). The forward arrow \(1\to2\) and its reflected image are vertex-disjoint in each of these cases when \(n\ge6\): the images are \((n-1)\to(n-2)\) for \(a=0\), and \(0\to(n-1)\) for \(a=1\). The two-arrow matching is reflection-invariant and hence supplies a fixed barycenter. Thus every reflection has nonempty geometric fixed-point set.

**3. All orbit-space fundamental groups.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem applies to the simply connected finite simplicial realization \(|X_n|\) under each finite subgroup \(H\le D_n\). It identifies the fundamental group of the orbit with \(H/N_H\), where \(N_H\) is the normal subgroup generated by the elements of \(H\) having fixed points.

Every rotation subgroup is \(\langle r^k\rangle\) for a divisor \(k\mid n\). If \(k\ge2\), its generator fixes a point by Step 1, so the quotient is simply connected (including the trivial subgroup \(k=n\)). For the full group \(\langle r\rangle\), Step 1 says that \(N_H\) is generated precisely by \(r^a\) for which \(\gcd(n,a)\ge2\). If \(n=p^e\) is a prime power, these exponents are exactly the multiples of \(p\), so \(N_H=\langle r^p\rangle\) and the quotient is \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\). If distinct primes \(p,q\) divide \(n\), both \(r^p\) and \(r^q\) have fixed points, and these two elements generate \(\langle r\rangle\), so the quotient is trivial.

Every subgroup containing a reflection is generated by \(\langle r^k,r^as\rangle\) with \(k\mid n\). If \(k\ge2\), both displayed generators have fixed points by Steps 1 and 2, hence \(N_H=H\). If \(k=1\), the subgroup is \(D_n\), generated by the two fixed-point reflections \(s\) and \(rs\). Again \(N_H=H\). This exhausts all subgroups. The first integral homology is the abelianization of the fundamental group, giving the claimed formula.

**4. Higher torsion for prime polygons.** Let \(p\ge7\) be prime. By Step 1, every nonidentity element of \(\langle r\rangle\) is fixed-point-free, so \(X_p\to Y_p=|X_p|/\langle r\rangle\) is a regular \(p\)-sheeted covering. By the cited connectivity theorem, \(\widetilde H_b(X_p;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(1\le b\le\nu_p=(p-5)/2\), while \(H_0(X_p;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\) with trivial group action. The Cartan–Leray homology spectral sequence
\[
E^2_{a,b}=H_a(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z;H_b(X_p;\mathbb Z))\Longrightarrow H_{a+b}(Y_p;\mathbb Z)
\]
therefore has only its \(b=0\) row in total degrees at most \(\nu_p\); no differential can enter or leave this row within those degrees. Thus \(H_j(Y_p;\mathbb Z)\cong H_j(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z;\mathbb Z)\) for \(1\le j\le\nu_p\). The standard periodic integral resolution of a cyclic group gives \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) in positive odd degrees and zero in positive even degrees. This proves the remaining statement.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_orbit_quotients.py` independently enumerates directed-arrow orbits for every rotation and reflection when \(6\le n\le60\), tests admissibility of the complete orbit as a directed path forest, and compares every predicted fixed-point condition. It enumerates every cyclic and dihedral subgroup of each \(D_n\) in this range, computes the subgroup generated by actual fixed-point elements, and checks the predicted quotient order. It also reconstructs the complete quotient cellular chain groups in dimensions zero through two for the free \(7\)-fold cyclic quotient; the orbit-cell ranks are \((2,10,22)\), and the first homology over \(\mathbb F_7\) has dimension one, while over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_{11}\) it is zero. Exact signed boundary compositions are zero. These finite checks corroborate the proof; neither an exhaustive finite range nor a field computation establishes the infinite-family integral assertions.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi–Collari–Smith (2026), Theorems 2.8 and 2.10, supply connectivity and integral homology of the original multipath complexes, but do not classify any orbit-space fundamental groups or the cyclic quotients' integral torsion. The published note *Dihedral rational homology of bidirectional-polygon multipath complexes* obtains the dihedral rational homology representation and identifies invariant rotation-arrow orbits in its proof; those fixed-orbit facts are prior work, not newly claimed here. Armstrong's 1965 theorem gives a general formula for fundamental groups of orbit spaces, without evaluating the stabilizer-generated subgroup in this graph family. The Cartan–Leray spectral sequence for free actions is also classical. The new conclusion is the complete subgroup-dependent fundamental-group classification and the infinite prime-index torsion range, information not supplied by the known rational characters or covering-space Betti numbers.

## Limitations
No new connectivity bound or original-graph homology formula is claimed. No higher quotient homology beyond the specified low-degree range, universal homotopy classification, or non-dihedral symmetry classification is asserted. The proof uses established general theorems and known fixed-orbit observations; an equivalent unpublished or unindexed specialization, or a general equivariant theorem directly subsuming this exact arithmetic classification, remains a possible originality risk. The finite checker does not replace the infinite proof or provide independent scientific validation.
