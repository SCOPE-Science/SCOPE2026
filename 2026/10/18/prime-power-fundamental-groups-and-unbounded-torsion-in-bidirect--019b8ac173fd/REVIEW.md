# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a rotation r^a, the arrow orbit has a fixed barycenter exactly when gcd(n,a)>=2: if gcd=1 the orbit is a full directed cycle and not a face; if gcd>=2, a translated set of forward arrows forms a vertex-disjoint matching. Each reflection has an invariant two-arrow matching for n>=6. The cited connectivity theorem makes X_n simply connected, so Armstrong’s orbit-space theorem gives pi1(|X_n|/H)=H/N_H where N_H is generated by elements with fixed points. For proper rotation subgroups the generator has a fixed point; for the full rotation group the fixed exponents generate <r^p> exactly when n=p^e and generate the whole group when n has distinct prime divisors; reflection-containing subgroups are generated by fixed-point reflections and, when needed, a fixed-point rotation. For prime p, the rotation action is free. The Cartan–Leray homology spectral sequence has only the b=0 row in total degrees <=(p-5)/2 because the covering complex has the cited connectivity, giving H_j(C_p;Z), which is Z/p in positive odd degrees and zero in positive even degrees. The finite verifier is only corroboration.

## originality

PASS

The closest published same-object finding was read in full and computes rational dihedral representations of the covering complexes; it explicitly does not compute orbit-space fundamental groups or integral quotient torsion. Caputi–Collari–Smith provide connectivity and homology of the cover, and Armstrong provides the general orbit-space formula, but neither supplies the object-specific fixed-point normal subgroup classification or the prime-family Cartan–Leray torsion range. The theorem is a synthesis of known machinery with nontrivial object-specific subgroup arithmetic. A short unindexed specialization remains a priority risk.

## value

PASS

The theorem classifies a natural family of orbit-space fundamental groups with a sharp prime-power boundary and gives unbounded low-degree integral torsion invisible in the highly connected cover. The exact all-subgroup statement and torsion range are structural topological invariants, not a small finite computation or a routine rational-character restatement.
