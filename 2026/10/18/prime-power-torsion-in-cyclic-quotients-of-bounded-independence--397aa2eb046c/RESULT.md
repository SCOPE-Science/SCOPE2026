# Prime-power torsion in cyclic quotients of bounded-independence spheres

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge3\), consider the cycle \(C_{2d}\) with vertex labels \(\mathbb Z/(2d)\). Its bounded-independence complex is
\[
K_d=B_d(C_{2d})=\{S\subseteq\mathbb Z/(2d):\alpha(C_{2d}[S])<d\}.
\]
Let \(r(i)=i+1\) and let \(Y_d=|K_d|/\langle r\rangle\) be the **geometric orbit space** of the full cyclic group of order \(2d\). Define
\[
c(d)=\begin{cases}
p,&\text{if }d=p^a\text{ for an odd prime }p\text{ and }a\ge1,\\
4,&\text{if }d=2^a\text{ with }a\ge2,\\
1,&\text{if }d\text{ has at least two distinct prime divisors}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the **integral fundamental group and integral first homology** have the exact common classification
\[
\boxed{\ \pi_1(Y_d)\cong H_1(Y_d;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/c(d)\mathbb Z.\ }
\]
In particular, cyclic quotients in this family have nonzero first-homology torsion precisely at prime-power parameters: of order \(p\) for odd prime powers, and of order four for powers of two. If \(d\) has at least two different prime factors, \(Y_d\) is simply connected. This is an integral orbit-space invariant not determined by the previously known rational homology character of the cyclic action.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(C_{2d}\) is a simple cycle; \(\alpha(G[S])\) is the maximum number of pairwise nonadjacent vertices in the induced subgraph on \(S\). Every face of \(K_d\) is a vertex subset containing neither full parity class \(E=\{0,2,\ldots,2d-2\}\) nor \(O=\{1,3,\ldots,2d-1\}\). The quotient is that of the **geometric realization**; it is not a naive vertex-orbit simplicial quotient. The dimension hypothesis \(d\ge3\) guarantees a simply connected covering space in the group-action theorem used below. No claim is made here for \(d=2\).

## Proof
**1. Identify the sphere and its simply connected dimension.** An independent set of size \(d\) in a cycle on \(2d\) vertices must occupy exactly alternate vertices: between each consecutive occupied pair there must be a gap of at least one vertex, and the sum of the \(d\) cyclic gaps is \(d\). Therefore the two and only two maximal independent sets are \(E\) and \(O\). Consequently
\[
K_d=\partial\Delta^E*\partial\Delta^O\cong S^{2d-3}.
\]
Because \(2d-3\ge3\), \(|K_d|\) is simply connected, connected, locally path-connected and locally simply connected.

**2. Characterize point-stabilizing rotations without a freeness assumption.** Let \(1\le m<2d\). The rotation \(r^m\) fixes a point of \(|K_d|\) if and only if it setwise stabilizes a nonempty face. Indeed, if it fixes a barycentric point, the set of vertices with positive barycentric coordinates is an invariant face; conversely, the barycenter of an invariant face is fixed. Because the complex is downward closed, an invariant nonempty face exists if and only if at least one orbit of \(r^m\) on the \(2d\) vertices is itself a face.

A nonempty vertex orbit is a face exactly when it contains neither all \(d\) even vertices nor all \(d\) odd vertices. If \(m=2k\) is even, the orbits are entirely of one parity and each has size \(d/\gcd(d,k)\). Hence
\[
r^{2k}\text{ fixes a point of }|K_d|\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gcd(d,k)>1.
\]
If \(m\) is odd, writing \(g=\gcd(d,m)=\gcd(2d,m)\), every orbit contains exactly \(d/g\) even and \(d/g\) odd vertices, so
\[
r^m\text{ fixes a point of }|K_d|\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gcd(d,m)>1
\qquad(m\text{ odd}).
\]
These criteria include all possible fixed points arising from barycenters of faces, not merely fixed vertices.

**3. Apply the orbit-space fundamental-group theorem.** Armstrong's fundamental-group theorem for a finite group acting on a simply connected suitable space identifies the fundamental group of its orbit space with the acting group modulo the normal subgroup generated by all elements having fixed points. Thus
\[
\pi_1(Y_d)\cong C_{2d}/N_d,
\quad N_d=\langle r^m:1\le m<2d,\ \operatorname{Fix}_{|K_d|}(r^m)\ne\varnothing\rangle.
\]
The acting group is cyclic, so normal closure and ordinary generated subgroup coincide.

**4. Compute the subgroup arithmetically.** Suppose first that \(d=p^a\) for an odd prime \(p\). For either parity of \(m\), the criterion in Step 2 is exactly \(p\mid m\) (for even \(m=2k\), note \(p\mid k\Longleftrightarrow p\mid m\)). Hence \(N_d=\langle r^p\rangle\), and the quotient has order \(p\).

If \(d=2^a\) with \(a\ge2\), no odd \(m\) fixes a point. For even \(m=2k\), the criterion reduces to \(2\mid k\), equivalently \(4\mid m\). Thus \(N_d=\langle r^4\rangle\), and the quotient has order four.

Finally, if \(d\) has at least two different prime divisors, there are two cases. For odd \(d\), choose different odd prime divisors \(p,q\); each of \(r^p,r^q\) has a fixed point, and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), so they generate all of \(C_{2d}\). If \(d\) is even, choose an odd prime divisor \(q\). The rotation \(r^4\) has a fixed point because \(\gcd(d,2)>1\), as does \(r^q\) because \(\gcd(d,q)>1\); since \(\gcd(4,q)=1\), they again generate the full rotation group. In either case \(N_d=C_{2d}\), giving the trivial quotient.

**5. Integral first homology.** For any path-connected space, the first integral homology group is the abelianization of its fundamental group. The groups calculated in Step 4 are already cyclic, so the abelianization leaves them unchanged. This yields the asserted integral \(H_1\) and completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone script `verification.py` independently enumerates all rotation vertex orbits for each \(3\le d\le100\) and every nonidentity exponent, verifying the point-stabilizer criterion in 9,996 cases and comparing the generated subgroup order with the prime-power formula. This finite corroboration is **not** the reason the theorem holds in all dimensions; Steps 1–5 are the proof.

For stronger independent finite evidence, `quotient_chaincheck.py` constructs the quotient of the barycentric two-skeleton and evaluates its cellular first-homology matrices modulo the primes \(2,3,5,7,101\) for \(d=3,4,5\). Quotient barycentric chains have unambiguous orientation because the simplex dimensions strictly increase along each chain. The resulting mod-prime dimensions agree with the predicted cyclic torsion. Run both programs using ordinary Python 3 and the standard library; `verification_output.txt` gives recorded results.

## Relationship to prior work
The bounded-independence complex and its cycle sphere type are studied in A. Carnero Bravo, *Total cut complexes and their duals* (arXiv:2602.21427v1, first posted February 24, 2026). The standard nonequivariant sphere formula and related total-cut dualities establish homotopy information but do not determine the fundamental groups of the **geometric cyclic orbit spaces**.

A 2026 note, *Dihedral orientation characters of bounded-independence spheres on cycles*, determines the exact action on top integral homology and the **rational** cyclic/dihedral quotient homology for these same spaces. In fact it explicitly leaves integral orbit-space homology outside its scope. Rational invariant homology cannot distinguish the prime-power torsion calculated here. Neither its character formula nor the original nonequivariant sphere classification implies the point-isotropy subgroup without further analysis.

Bayer and collaborators' earlier work on cut complexes of cycles includes equivariant homology for other, ordinary cut complexes, which must not be confused with the geometric fundamental group of the bounded-independence orbit considered here. Armstrong's 1965 orbit-space theorem is a credited, essential general result. The novelty asserted is the concrete all-parameter isotropy calculation and consequent integral classification for this naturally occurring family, not Armstrong's general theorem, the underlying sphere, or the rational orientation character.

## Limitations
No higher integral homology, homotopy type, or equivariant homotopy classification of \(Y_d\) is established. The proof is specialized to the critical even cycle length \(2d\), not to all cycle lengths or graph powers. Nontrivial torsion here does not imply that the quotient is a manifold or a lens space. The finite scripts do not independently prove the infinite theorem. An unindexed earlier application of the orbit-space theorem to precisely this join and rotation action remains a possible originality risk.
