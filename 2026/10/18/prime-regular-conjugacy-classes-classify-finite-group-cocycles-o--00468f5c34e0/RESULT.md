# Prime-regular conjugacy classes classify finite-group cocycles over prime-adic odometers

## Finding
Fix a prime \(p\) and a finite group \(G\). Continuous \(G\)-valued cocycles over the prime-adic adding machine \(T(x)=x+1\) on \(\mathbb Z_p\), modulo continuous group-valued transfer functions, are classified **exactly** by the conjugacy classes of elements of \(G\) whose orders are coprime to \(p\). Every cocycle is continuously cohomologous to a constant cocycle with a \(p\)-regular value, and two such constants are cohomologous precisely when they are conjugate in \(G\).

More explicitly, if the class is represented by a \(p\)-regular element \(h\) of order \(r\), then the skew product on \(\mathbb Z_p\times G\) has precisely \(|G|/r\) clopen minimal components; each is topologically conjugate to translation by \((1,1)\) on \(\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z\), equivalently the odometer with tower heights \(r p^j\). For each base point, the subgroup of finite cocycle values seen along arbitrarily close positive returns is conjugate to \(\langle h\rangle\), hence cyclic. By the classical Brauer counting theorem, the number of continuous cocycle cohomology classes equals the number of irreducible \(p\)-modular representations of \(G\) over an algebraically closed field.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(G\) is an arbitrary finite, potentially nonabelian, discrete group, and \(p\) is any prime. The base \(X=\mathbb Z_p=\varprojlim\mathbb Z/p^j\mathbb Z\) carries its profinite topology; \(T(x)=x+1\). A cocycle means a continuous map \(c:X\to G\). It defines \(F_c(x,g)=(Tx,g c(x))\). Two cocycles are cohomologous when there exists continuous \(b:X\to G\) with \(c'(x)=b(x)^{-1}c(x)b(Tx)\). This is equivalence under **principal-fibre translations**, not equivalence under arbitrary fibre automorphisms or all topological conjugacies.

Given \(c\), choose any \(s\ge0\) for which it depends only on \(x\bmod p^s\). Define its ordered loop product
\[
P_s=c(0)c(1)\cdots c(p^s-1)\in G.
\]
If \(\operatorname{ord}(P_s)=p^a r\) with \(\gcd(r,p)=1\), its \(p\)-regular part \(u_s\in\langle P_s\rangle\) is uniquely specified by \(u_s=P_s^t\), where \(t\equiv0\pmod{p^a}\) and \(t\equiv1\pmod r\). When \(r=1\), put \(u_s=e\). Let \(v_s\) be the inverse of \(p^s\) modulo \(r\) when \(r>1\), and define \(h_s=u_s^{v_s}\), taking \(h_s=e\) when \(r=1\). The resulting conjugacy class \([h_s]\) is independent of the permissible level \(s\) and is invariant under continuous cohomology.

The return-value subgroup at \(x\in X\) consists of all \(g\in G\) for which there is an increasing sequence of positive integers \(n_k\to\infty\) with \(T^{n_k}x\to x\) and \(c(x)c(Tx)\cdots c(T^{n_k-1}x)=g\) for every \(k\).

## Proof
**Finite cyclic-level gauge lemma.** Consider any cyclic base of length \(N\), with two \(G\)-label sequences \((a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1})\) and \((a'_0,\ldots,a'_{N-1})\). They are related by a gauge \(a'_i=b_i^{-1}a_i b_{i+1}\), with cyclic indices, exactly when their ordered products \(A\) and \(A'\) are conjugate. Necessity follows by multiplying, giving \(A'=b_0^{-1}A b_0\). Conversely, choose \(b_0\) with that equality, and recursively set \(b_{i+1}=a_i^{-1}b_i a'_i\); the loop-product identity gives \(b_N=b_0\).

**Passage to the odometer.** Any continuous map from compact \(X\) into finite \(G\) factors through some \(\mathbb Z/p^s\mathbb Z\). At a finer level \(j\ge s\), the same cocycle has ordered product \(P_s^{p^{j-s}}\). A continuous transfer function also factors through some finite level, so the finite cyclic-level gauge lemma gives the exact criterion: cocycles \(c,c'\) are cohomologous if and only if their ordered products at some common sufficiently fine level are conjugate.

**Construct a canonical constant representative.** With the above decomposition \(\operatorname{ord}(P_s)=p^a r\), choose \(j\ge s+a\). The \(p\)-primary component of \(P_s\) disappears under exponentiation by \(p^{j-s}\), hence
\[
P_s^{p^{j-s}}=u_s^{p^{j-s}}=h_s^{p^j}.
\]
The constant cocycle with value \(h_s\) has ordered product \(h_s^{p^j}\) at level \(j\). The finite-level gauge lemma therefore constructs an actual continuous transfer function between \(c\) and this constant. If two \(p\)-regular constants \(h,h'\) are cohomologous, at some finite level \(h^{p^j}\) and \((h')^{p^j}\) are conjugate. Exponentiation by \(p^j\) has an inverse on their (coprime-to-\(p\)) cyclic orders; raising the conjugacy identity to an integer inverse modulo the least common multiple of those orders shows \(h\) and \(h'\) are conjugate. Conversely, constant conjugate elements are cohomologous by a constant gauge. Every \(p\)-regular conjugacy class occurs through a constant cocycle. This proves the asserted bijection, the independence of \(s\), and its explicit formula. In particular, a class admits no two nonconjugate \(p\)-regular constant representatives.

**Minimal-component census.** For constant \(h\) of order \(r\) coprime to \(p\), the sets \(X\times g\langle h\rangle\), for the \(|G|/r\) left cosets, are pairwise disjoint, clopen and invariant. Identify each fibre coset with \(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z\) by powers of \(h\). The restricted dynamics become \((x,k)\mapsto(x+1,k+1)\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives a dense positive orbit in every cylinder of \(X\times\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z\), as \(\gcd(p,r)=1\), hence each component is minimal. Its finite cyclic levels have lengths \(r p^j\). A continuous gauge conjugacy transports this partition and its dynamics back to the initial \(c\).

**Local-return groups.** For the constant cocycle, the return times to the level-\(j\) base cylinder are positive multiples of \(p^j\), and the corresponding cocycle values range over \(\langle h^{p^j}\rangle=\langle h\rangle\). For every \(k\) modulo \(r\), the Chinese remainder theorem supplies an unbounded sequence \(n_j\equiv0\pmod{p^j}\), \(n_j\equiv k\pmod r\), proving the return group is exactly \(\langle h\rangle\). Under a gauge, the finite-valued endpoint transfer \(b(T^{n_j}x)\) eventually equals \(b(x)\), so local groups are conjugate. The final representation-count equality is the established Brauer theorem for \(p\)-regular conjugacy classes, and is not asserted to be new.

## Verification
Run the standalone standard-library Python program `verify.py` using `python verify.py`. It enumerates finite skew-product cycles for cyclic groups of orders \(6,7,8\), for \(S_3\), for primes \(2,3\), for constant and nonconstant cylinder cocycles, and for two larger finite levels; it checks the predicted component count, cycle length, stability of the normalized conjugacy representative under cylinder refinement, and a finite-level cohomology criterion. Its output in the present check was `VERIFY_OK exact finite-level assertions: 3250`. The proof above supplies the unrestricted statement; finite checks are not a proof for all finite groups or cocycles.

For a concrete distinction, on the binary adding machine with fibre \(C_7\), the seven constant cocycles give seven distinct continuous cohomology classes. The six nonidentity constants all have one minimal component, showing the component count alone does not classify the cocycles. In contrast, for \(S_3\) and \(p=2\), there are precisely two cohomology classes (identity and the class of three-cycles), yielding respectively six or two minimal components.

## Relationship to prior work
Bruin's 2026 paper develops finite nonabelian cocycle return-value groups and, in Remark 3.1, asks whether they are abelian over an equicontinuous base. The present result covers the prime-adic adding machine, identifies a cyclic return group, and gives a complete continuous cohomology classification with the exact component census. Matui (2002, Section 2) already observed that finite-group cocycles over odometers are cohomologous to cocycles taking only the identity and one further value, and that a minimal finite-group extension of an odometer must have cyclic fibre group. Those antecedents are **not** claimed as new. The additional assertion here is that **every** cocycle over a prime-adic adding machine is cohomologous to a uniquely determined \(p\)-regular constant *up to conjugacy*, with the explicit tower-product normalization and resulting complete class-to-component formula. Prior work on Borel cohomology is materially different because no continuous transfer functions are required.

## Limitations
The classification requires a prime-adic base, a **finite** discrete target group, and continuous cocycles and transfer functions. It is not a classification of arbitrary fibre-preserving topological conjugacies, measurable cocycles, generalized non-prime-power odometers, or infinite compact groups. Known cohomological normal forms make the proof elementary at finite level; historical priority for the full normalized classification, as opposed to its individual ingredients, has not been independently certified. The modular-representation equality invokes the classical Brauer theorem. The finite tests do not prove the infinite statement.
