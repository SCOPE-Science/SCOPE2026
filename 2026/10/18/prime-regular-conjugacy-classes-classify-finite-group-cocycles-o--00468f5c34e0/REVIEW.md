# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite cyclic-level lemma is proved by multiplying the gauge relation and by an explicit recursively constructed periodic transfer. Continuous finite-valued functions factor through a finite prime-power quotient. After a sufficiently high power, the prime-primary monodromy vanishes; the normalized prime-regular root is a genuine constant-cocycle representative, with uniqueness via inverses of prime powers modulo finite prime-regular orders. Chinese remaindering gives exactly the minimal component count and local return group. The standalone verifier returned 3250 exact finite-level checks; the universal claim rests on proof, not on enumeration.

## Originality — PASS under examined evidence
The inspected 2026 source discusses nonabelian return groups and conjectural abelianity in the equicontinuous case. Matui's 2002 work **already** gives finite-group odometer cocycle normalization to a spike and rules out minimal noncyclic finite-group extensions; those facts are acknowledged as prior. The newly assessed claim is the entire explicit classification of continuous cohomology by the prime-regular conjugacy classes (including uniqueness and the normalized product-root invariant), together with the precise minimal-component types/counts. Searches of published mathematical records and relevant primary texts did not identify an earlier statement of this complete classification. Given the elementary finite-level methods, an unlocated equivalent formulation in odometer cohomology or modular dynamics remains a concrete priority risk; absence from search is not evidence of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Classifying all continuous finite-group cocycles over the prime-adic adding machine is a natural complete problem, motivated by the return-group question and the classical odometer-extension obstruction. The classification is finer than dynamical component multiplicity: six distinct minimal \(C_7\)-cocycles over the dyadic odometer have identical one-component decompositions but remain inequivalent under continuous principal-fibre gauges. The exact equality of the class count with the established Brauer count of prime-regular conjugacy classes links the dynamical classification to a fundamental finite-group invariant. This is not a claim to originate the Brauer theorem, the finite-level gauge lemma or Matui's antecedent obstruction.
