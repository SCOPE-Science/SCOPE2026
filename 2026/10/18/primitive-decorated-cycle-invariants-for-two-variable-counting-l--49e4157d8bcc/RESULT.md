# Primitive decorated-cycle invariants for two-variable counting logic on finite endofunctions

## Finding
Let \(A=(V,R)\) and \(B=(W,R)\) be nonempty finite relational structures satisfying \(\forall x\,\exists^{=1}y\,R(x,y)\). Thus \(R\) is the graph of a total unary map; importantly, it is a **binary relation**, not a function symbol permitting arbitrarily nested terms. Write \(A\equiv_{C^2}B\) when the structures satisfy the same sentences of two-variable first-order logic with counting quantifiers.

Every weak component of the functional digraph has a unique directed cycle. At each cyclic vertex \(v\), attach the isomorphism type \(\tau(v)\) of the finite rooted in-tree consisting of \(v\) and all noncyclic vertices whose first cyclic iterate is \(v\). Read these rooted-tree types along the directed cycle, up to cyclic rotation, giving a decorated necklace \(w\) of length \(m\).

Define the **weighted primitive-necklace signature** \(\Phi(A)\), a finite nonnegative-integer-valued map. A component of cycle length \(1\) or \(2\) contributes one to the entry indexed by its *entire* decorated necklace, with its length retained as part of the key. For length \(m\ge3\), write \(w=p^r\), where \(p\) is the shortest cyclic period, of length \(d\) with \(rd=m\), and contribute \(r\) to the entry indexed by the rotation class of primitive \(p\), tagged as long. Sum all component contributions.

**Theorem.** For all nonempty finite total-function relational structures \(A,B\),
\[
 A\equiv_{C^2}B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Phi(A)=\Phi(B).
\]
In particular, nonisomorphic **weakly connected** functional digraphs are always distinguishable by \(C^2\). Among all functional digraphs on at most five vertices, \(C^2\) distinguishes every isomorphism type. At six vertices there are \(130\) isomorphism types but \(129\) \(C^2\)-classes: precisely one nonsingleton class, consisting of the directed six-cycle and two disjoint directed three-cycles. Their sizes and numbers of cycles differ, but their primitive decorated-necklace weights agree.

## Assumptions and scope
The language has equality and precisely one binary relation \(R\). Loops and reciprocal pairs are permitted. Every vertex has exactly one outgoing \(R\)-edge. No connectedness or extra colors are assumed for the main theorem. The equality \(\equiv_{C^2}\) allows every finite counting threshold and arbitrarily large quantifier rank, while restricting formulas to two variable symbols. In the signature, different rooted-tree isomorphism types are different letters. Distinct connected components with equal primitive labels are combined by adding their multiplicity weights. Long necklaces are read up to rotation only, not reversal. Short cycles receive separate tags because loops and reciprocal edges are detected by two-variable atomic formulas.

## Proof
**1. Cyclic points and attached rooted trees are definable with two variables.** Fix a pair of finite structures and let \(N\) exceed their total numbers of vertices. The one-variable formula asserting existence of a backward \(R\)-walk of length \(N\) is expressible by alternately binding \(x\) and \(y\). In a finite total functional digraph it selects exactly the cyclic points, since all noncyclic preimage chains have length less than \(N\). Call this predicate \(\operatorname{Cyc}(x)\). For each finite rooted in-tree \(T\), another one-variable \(C^2\) formula identifies those cyclic roots whose attached trees are isomorphic to \(T\): induct on the tree height, specifying for each child rooted-tree type \(U\) its exact number of noncyclic incoming neighbors and excluding all other incoming types. The quantifier \(\exists^{=k}y\,(R(y,x)\wedge\neg\operatorname{Cyc}(y)\wedge\theta_U(y))\) uses only \(x,y\), and substituting a nested one-variable formula requires only alpha-renaming. Tree induction terminates for the given finite pair. Therefore each \(\tau(v)\) is recoverable as a unary \(C^2\)-type. This finite-pair definability, not a single bounded-rank uniform formula for every tree, is all that is needed.

**2. Long-cycle weights are necessary.** From a cyclic vertex \(v\) one can follow \(R\) forward any prescribed finite number of steps in a two-variable formula, successively checking its recovered rooted-tree labels. Hence two cyclic vertices with different infinite forward label sequences can be separated by a one-variable \(C^2\) formula. Each infinite periodic label sequence has a unique least period \(p\) up to a choice of starting phase. For a long cycle decorated by \(p^r\), exactly \(r\) vertices have each phase of the primitive sequence. Across components, the total number of cyclic vertices with any fixed phase is consequently the summed \(r\)-weight. These phase counts are visible to counting sentences. A length-one cycle is visible by \(R(x,x)\); a length-two cycle is visible by \(\exists y\,(x\ne y\wedge R(x,y)\wedge R(y,x))\). Their tree-decorated necklaces are similarly determined using cyclic successor labels, and the number of each short component is obtained by dividing the counted cyclic phase occurrences by its known size. Thus \(A\equiv_{C^2}B\) forces \(\Phi(A)=\Phi(B)\).

**3. Equality of weights is sufficient.** Use the standard two-variable counting/one-dimensional color-refinement equivalence for finite relational structures, applied to the atomic directed relation with equality. For each short-cycle necklace, color its cyclic vertices by the corresponding tree-decorated phase; for each long primitive necklace, color cyclic vertices by the infinite forward periodic label sequence (one color per primitive phase). Color each off-cycle vertex by the primitive cyclic phase of its eventual cyclic root, the full isomorphism type of the rooted in-tree attached at that root, and the orbit of the vertex under root-preserving automorphisms of that in-tree. For short cycles, use the tagged short-cycle necklace and its phase instead. In a finite pair, only finitely many such colors occur. This description is intentionally finer than stable color refinement: it records a rooted tree with a distinguished internal vertex, so any two vertices with the same color have an isomorphism of their whole feeding trees carrying one vertex to the other. For a given color, its own-loop status, reciprocal-neighbor status, unique outgoing-neighbor color, and incoming-neighbor color multiplicities depend only on the described local rooted tree, phase, and short-cycle tag—not on the number of repeated periods forming a long component. Consequently this coloring is equitable for the directed adjacency relation, including its possible reversed and two-way atomic pair types. The count of every color in \(A\) equals its count in \(B\) when \(\Phi\) agrees: each primitive phase of a long necklace occurs as often as its total repetition weight, and each short necklace occurs as often as its component weight; all attached-tree vertices are then counted from the same decorations. Nonedge pair counts follow from total color-class sizes by subtraction. Thus the two structures admit a common balanced equitable coloring and cannot be separated by \(C^2\).

**4. Connectedness and the first collision.** A single component has one decorated necklace. For a short cycle, its entire necklace and multiplicity \(1\) are recovered. For a long cycle, its primitive necklace and repetition number are recovered; together they reconstruct the full cyclic word and every attached tree. Thus \(\Phi\) is injective on connected isomorphism types. A nonisomorphic collision between functional graphs requires redistributing some long-necklace repetition weight across components. Each long-cycle component has at least three cyclic vertices. With at most five total vertices there is no nontrivial redistribution. At six vertices the only possibility is a single primitive letter with six repetitions versus two components with three repetitions each: the roots must be the one-vertex trees because all six vertices lie on cycles. This gives exactly \(C_6\) and \(C_3\sqcup C_3\), with arcs following the cyclic orientation. The labeled function enumeration in the verification section independently confirms the isomorphism and logic counts.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_endofunctions.py` enumerates all \(n^n\) total maps on \(n\) labeled points for \(1\le n\le6\). It independently computes \(\Phi\) using cycle/tree decomposition and a global color-refinement partition using loop, mutual-edge, outgoing-color and incoming-color counts. At each \(n\), there are no partition disagreements. The observed \(C^2\)-class counts are \(1,3,7,19,47,129\), while the six-point isomorphism count is \(130\). The script also reconstructs the unique nonsingleton class. The complete output appears in `verification_output.txt`. Enumeration through six points checks the finite cutoff only; the unrestricted theorem is established by the mathematical argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Krebs and Verbitsky (arXiv:1407.3175; first publicly submitted July 11, 2014) establish connections among universal covers, color refinement, and \(C^2\) with depth bounds on general graphs. Kiefer, Schweitzer, and Selman (arXiv:1503.08792; March 30, 2015) classify which finite relational structures are individually identified by \(C^2\), using general equitable-partition and inversion methods. Neither inspected paper states the all-size equivalence criterion here in terms of rooted-tree-decorated primitive directed necklaces with special short cycles, nor its exact first six-point collision. The general counting-logic/color-refinement equivalence is used as a known input, not claimed as new. The underlying enumeration of unlabeled endofunctions is likewise classical; the six-point count \(130\) is only a verification baseline. The contribution is the restricted-class \(C^2\)-equivalence fingerprint and the sharp collision classification.

## Limitations
The theorem applies only to *total* functional binary relations, not partial functions, arbitrary digraphs, multiple functions, or a signature with a unary function *term operator*. It compares structures within this class rather than claiming connected endofunctions are identified among all binary relational structures. Its weighted signature need not reconstruct the number of connected components of long cycles. Although primary comparisons were made, an earlier unindexed specialization of equitable-partition theory or automata/pseudoforest literature could potentially entail the criterion; a specialized independent literature audit remains desirable. The six-point computation is exhaustive for the stated domain, but not a proof of the theorem for larger orders.
