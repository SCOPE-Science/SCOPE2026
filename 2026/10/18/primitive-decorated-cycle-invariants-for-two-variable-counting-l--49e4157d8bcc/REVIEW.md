# Research assessment

## Correctness
The all-size argument isolates cyclic vertices using finite backward-walk formulas, recovers attached rooted-tree labels with counting, and recovers each primitive phase from bounded forward observations chosen for the compared pair. Equality of the resulting weights produces a balanced equitable partition, hence counting-logic equivalence. Special treatment of loops and reciprocal two-cycles is essential. Exhaustive independent finite-state and canonical-map checks of all total functions of orders one through six reproduce the first collision and validate the small-order corollary. Remaining mathematical risk: a hidden distinction between full relational counting logic and the claimed equitable-coloring scheme should be independently scrutinized.

## Originality
Literature comparisons included Krebs–Verbitsky's universal-cover and two-variable-counting results (arXiv:1407.3175), Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman's classification of structures individually identified by counting logic (arXiv:1503.08792), and Charatonik–Witkowski's stronger logic on ranked-tree structures. Those establish broader tools and a distinct identification question, but the inspected material does not present the explicit weighted primitive-necklace classification on all finite total binary-successor relations. The known unlabeled-function counts are not claimed as new. Residual risk: unindexed automata/monounary-algebra or special-case equitable-partition results might already imply the exact statement.

## Value
The theorem supplies an all-size, efficiently describable complete invariant for indistinguishability in a natural class of unary dynamical systems, identifies exactly the information lost by counting logic (how repeated primitive directed cycles are grouped into components), and gives a sharp first obstruction at six points. It is a structural result, not an arbitrary finite tabulation.

## Limitations
Only nonempty finite total binary-successor relations; neither partial maps nor actual function-symbol terms. The six-point enumeration corroborates but does not extend the infinite theorem. The assessment is not independent certification.
