# Primitive square-pair cycles in three-robust ladder complexes

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\), let \(L_n=P_2\square P_n\), and let \(K_n\) be the three-robust clique complex: a vertex subset is a face exactly when the induced graph has no independent triple. Index the top and bottom vertices of the ladder by \(t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1}\) and \(b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}\). For each elementary square indexed by \(0\le i\le n-2\), orient its boundary as
\[
\omega_i=[t_i,t_{i+1}]+[t_{i+1},b_{i+1}]+[b_{i+1},b_i]+[b_i,t_i].
\]
For every \(0\le i<j\le n-2\), form the ordinary exterior product \(z_{ij}=\omega_i\wedge\omega_j\), interpreted as an integral simplicial three-chain by identifying an oriented four-vertex wedge with its oriented three-simplex. Then **the classes \([z_{ij}]\) form a primitive integral basis of \(\widetilde H_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\)**, with no rescaling or denominators.

Write \(R\) for interchanging the two rows and \(S\) for reversing all columns. Their action on this basis is exactly
\[
R_*[z_{ij}]=[z_{ij}],\qquad
S_*[z_{ij}]=[z_{n-2-j,n-2-i}].
\]
Consequently the complete integral \(\operatorname{Aut}(L_n)\cong C_2\times C_2\) module is the unsigned permutation lattice on unordered pairs of the \(n-1\) squares. With \(s=n-1\), \(N=\binom{s}{2}\), and \(t=\lfloor s/2\rfloor\), the restriction to column reversal decomposes integrally as
\[
\widetilde H_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{t}\oplus\mathbb Z[C_2]^{(N-t)/2},
\]
where the first summands are trivial and each rank-two regular summand exchanges its two basis elements. In particular, its column-reversal coinvariants are free of rank \((N+t)/2\), rather than having hidden \(2\)-torsion. The row interchange acts trivially on the entire lattice.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has exactly the top and bottom horizontal edges and the \(n\) vertical rungs. All homology uses ordinary simplicial chains with integer coefficients. The notation \(\wedge\) is the **integral exterior product of vertex-chain vectors**, not an operation on cohomology. Each summand in \(\omega_i\wedge\omega_j\) is zero if its two constituent oriented edges share a vertex.

Because \(L_n\) is bipartite, any five vertices contain an independent triple by pigeonhole, so \(K_n\) has dimension at most three. By König's theorem, a four-vertex set is a three-simplex of \(K_n\) exactly when its induced ladder graph has a matching of size two. In particular, every four-vertex term of the displayed exterior product is a genuine simplex of \(K_n\).

## Proof

**Step 1: Top homology rank.** Filakovský's three-robust grid theorem proves the integral homotopy equivalence \(K_n\simeq\bigvee^{N}S^3\) for this exact \(2\)-by-\(n\) graph, where \(N=\binom{n-1}{2}\). Therefore \(\widetilde H_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank \(N\). Since there are no four-simplices, it equals the subgroup \(\ker(\partial_3:C_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\to C_2(K_n;\mathbb Z))\).

**Step 2: Explicit cycles.** Let \(F=\mathbb Z^{V(L_n)}\) be freely generated by vertices, with \([u,v]=u\wedge v\in\bigwedge^2 F\). The augmented simplex boundary \(\partial:\bigwedge^q F\to\bigwedge^{q-1} F\) removes one vertex with alternating signs and satisfies \(\partial(a\wedge b)=(\partial a)\wedge b+(-1)^{\deg a}a\wedge(\partial b)\). Each \(\omega_i\) is a closed one-chain, so \(\partial(\omega_i\wedge\omega_j)=0\). The disjoint-edge description in the scope paragraph shows that this is a boundary-zero three-chain *inside* \(K_n\), not merely in the full simplex.

**Step 3: Primitive diagonal detection.** Fix \(0\le i<j\le n-2\) and consider the ordered-orientation-independent four-vertex simplex \(F_{ij}=\{t_i,t_{i+1},b_j,b_{j+1}\}\). Within the ladder, this vertex set has exactly one perfect matching: its top horizontal edge in column interval \(i\) and its bottom horizontal edge in interval \(j\). This remains true when \(j=i+1\), since the extra possible rung cannot be completed to a disjoint second edge. A top horizontal edge belongs to precisely one elementary square boundary, and likewise for a bottom horizontal edge. It follows that the coefficient of \(F_{ij}\), with any fixed oriented vertex ordering, is \(\pm1\) in \(z_{ij}\) and is zero in every \(z_{ab}\) with \((a,b)\ne(i,j)\), \(a<b\). Hence coefficient extraction on these \(N\) tetrahedra maps the \(N\) cycles onto a signed standard basis of \(\mathbb Z^N\).

**Step 4: Exhaustion over the integers.** Restrict the coefficient-extraction homomorphism to \(\widetilde H_3(K_n;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_3\). It is surjective by Step 3. Both domain and codomain are free abelian of the same finite rank \(N\) by Step 1, so it is an isomorphism. Thus the \([z_{ij}]\) are a complete *integral*, not just rational, basis. This argument excludes any finite-index defect.

**Step 5: Symmetry and integral decomposition.** Both row interchange and column reversal reverse the orientation of each elementary square; specifically, \(R(\omega_i)=-\omega_i\) and \(S(\omega_i)=-\omega_{n-2-i}\). Exterior products of two degree-two expressions commute, so their two minus signs cancel and the displayed square-pair permutation formulas follow integrally. Column reversal fixes exactly \(t=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\) unordered square pairs (the pairs exchanged by reversal) and partitions all others into two-element orbits. Each fixed pair yields an integral trivial summand; each two-element orbit yields a regular \(\mathbb Z[C_2]\)-summand. Coinvariants are therefore free of the claimed rank. For \(n\ge3\), the graph's full automorphism group is generated by these two involutions.

## Verification

The complete standalone script `verify_ladder.py` reconstructs the ladder, enumerates three- and four-vertex simplices, computes mod-two rank of the top boundary, constructs the integral exterior-product cycles from scratch, evaluates their integral boundaries, checks all unique coefficient pivots and confirms both group actions. Its recorded output in `verification_output.txt` covers every \(3\le n\le11\): 165 primitive square-pair cycles and matching top mod-two Betti ranks. The all-parameter claim is proved in Steps 1–5; these finite computations independently check the conventions and selected cases but are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The sphere-wedge homotopy type and abstract rank \(N\) are established by Filakovský and are **not** claimed new. A published rational result for total three-cut ladder complexes describes the square-pair *character* after Alexander duality, and another published result describes a rational square-pair representation for certain even grids. A broader published equivariant Euler-character calculation for three-robust bipartite-graph complexes also determines virtual rational representations. None of these stated rational conclusions determines the integral homology lattice: for example, the rank-two regular integral \(C_2\)-module and the sum of trivial and sign integral modules become isomorphic over \(\mathbb Q\), but their coinvariants differ by \(\mathbb Z/2\)-torsion. The contribution here is the explicit primitive chain-cycle basis and the resulting full integral module and coinvariants for *all* ladder lengths.

## Limitations

This theorem does not assert an equivariant homotopy splitting, determine the integral homology of the geometric quotient, or extend the primitive basis to arbitrary wider grids. The rank argument uses the published homotopy theorem; the original mathematical work is the integral chain construction, primitive detection and integral symmetry identification. The closest public-indexed rational analyses could be inspected only through their indexed scientific statements, not their complete full texts, so an unindexed or implicit equivalent integral lemma remains an originality risk. No independent audit has been performed.
