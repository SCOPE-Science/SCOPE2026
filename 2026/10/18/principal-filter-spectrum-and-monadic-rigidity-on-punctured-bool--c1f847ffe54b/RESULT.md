# Principal-filter spectrum and monadic rigidity on punctured Boolean nearlattices

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\) and let \(\mathcal N_n\) denote the nonempty subsets of \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). There are \(2^{n^2}\) quasi-modal filter-valued operations on this distributive nearlattice, but exactly
\[
U_n=2^{n^2}-(2^n-1)^n+n!
\]
are represented by an ordinary necessity operator. Their proportion satisfies \(U_n/2^{n^2}\sim n/2^n\). Each is explicitly classified by a finite coordinate condition. The monadic subclass consists of exactly \(B_n\) quasi-modal operators (the Bell number), of which exactly one admits a unary necessity representation.

## Assumptions and scope
Order \(\mathcal N_n=\mathcal P([n])\setminus\{\emptyset\}\) by inclusion. The top is \([n]\), binary join is union, and meet is ordinary intersection when it is nonempty. A filter is a nonempty upset closed under all existing meets of its members. The filter lattice \(\operatorname{Fi}(\mathcal N_n)\) is ordered by inclusion, with join equal to the generated filter. The principal filter of \(B\ne\emptyset\) is \([B)=\{A\in\mathcal N_n:B\subseteq A\}\). Here quasi-modal means \(\nabla([n])=\{[n]\}\) and \(\nabla(A\cap B)=\nabla(A)\vee\nabla(B)\) for each nonempty intersection; a unary necessity operation preserves top and these existing meets. The monadic axioms are those of Calomino (2023), whose Theorem 28 equates them with Boolean monadic quantifiers on the filter lattice.

## Proof
**Step 1: all filters.** If a filter \(F\) contains disjoint members \(A,B\), then for each \(i\in[n]\), the two members \(A\cup\{i\}\) and \(B\cup\{i\}\) intersect in \(\{i\}\). Hence every singleton belongs to \(F\), forcing \(F=\mathcal N_n\). Otherwise all members of \(F\) intersect pairwise and, as \(F\) is finite and closed under nonempty binary intersections, the intersection \(K\) of all its members is nonempty and belongs to \(F\). Then \(F=[K)\). Thus the complete list is \(\mathcal N_n\) and the principal filters \([K)\) for all \(\emptyset\ne K\subseteq[n]\). The map \(\theta([K))=[n]\setminus K\), \(\theta(\mathcal N_n)=[n]\), preserves and reflects inclusion and is therefore an isomorphism with the full Boolean lattice \(\mathcal P([n])\).

**Step 2: coordinate normal form.** Put \(f=\theta\circ\nabla\). The quasi-modal identities become \(f([n])=\emptyset\) and \(f(A\cap B)=f(A)\cup f(B)\) whenever \(A\cap B\ne\emptyset\). For each \(j\in[n]\), the zero locus \(F_j=\{A:j\notin f(A)\}\) contains top, is upward closed (by applying the meet law to nested pairs), and is closed under nonempty existing intersections. Step 1 supplies a unique \(K_j\subseteq[n]\) such that \(F_j=\{A:K_j\subseteq A\}\), where \(K_j=\emptyset\) denotes the full filter. Conversely, each tuple of subsets gives the displayed formula
\[
f(A)=\{j\in[n]:K_j\not\subseteq A\},\]
which directly satisfies the two quasi-modal axioms. Distinct tuples determine distinct operators. There are \(2^{n^2}\) such tuples.

**Step 3: principal-filter obstruction.** A quasi-modal operator equals \([\Box A)\) for some unary operation \(\Box\) precisely when every \(\nabla(A)\) is proper, since the only nonprincipal filter is \(\mathcal N_n\). Under \(\theta\) this says \(f(A)\ne[n]\), equivalently at least one \(K_j\subseteq A\) for every nonempty \(A\). If some \(K_j=\emptyset\), the condition holds. If no \(K_j\) is empty, applying it to every singleton \(A=\{i\}\) forces some \(K_j=\{i\}\) for each \(i\). There are exactly \(n\) coordinates, so all \(K_j\) are the singletons in a permutation. Conversely that condition covers every nonempty \(A\). Because \(\nabla\) preserves the quasi-modal laws, its proper principal generators automatically define a top- and existing-meet-preserving unary \(\Box\), with uniqueness following from uniqueness of principal generators. Counting tuples with some empty coordinate yields \(2^{n^2}-(2^n-1)^n\); the disjoint family of singleton permutations contributes \(n!\). Division by \(2^{n^2}\) and the estimate \(1-(1-2^{-n})^n\sim n2^{-n}\), together with \(n!/2^{n^2}=o(n2^{-n})\), gives the asymptotic.

**Step 4: monadic case.** By Calomino (2023), Theorem 28, the monadic quasi-modal operators correspond bijectively to monadic Boolean quantifiers \(\Delta\) on \(\operatorname{Fi}(\mathcal N_n)\cong\mathcal P([n])\). Such a quantifier is join-preserving, extensive, idempotent, fixes the empty set and satisfies \(\Delta(S\cap\Delta T)=\Delta S\cap\Delta T\). It is exactly saturation under an equivalence relation \(E\) on \([n]\). Indeed, joining singleton images defines a reflexive relation, and the monadic identity forces overlapping singleton images to coincide; this implies symmetry and transitivity. The converse is immediate for class saturation. Under the coordinate form of Step 2, \(K_j\) is the \(E\)-class of \(j\), which is never empty. Step 3 then says unary representability holds only if all \(E\)-classes are singletons, i.e. \(E\) is equality. Its operator is \(\nabla(A)=[A)\), hence \(\Box A=A\). The number of equivalences on an \(n\)-element set is \(B_n\).

## Verification
An independent finite Python implementation in the included `verify.py` enumerates all coordinate tuples for \(n=2,3\), checks the quasi-modal laws and the explicit principal-filter condition, and independently tests monadic identities both as Boolean quantifiers and in the original relational axiom formulation. It reports \(16\) quasi-modal and \(9\) unary-representable operations for \(n=2\), and \(512\) quasi-modal and \(175\) unary-representable operations for \(n=3\); the monadic counts are \(2\) and \(5\), with exactly one unary-representable monadic operation in each case. These finite checks do not substitute for the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Celani and Calomino (2019) introduce unary necessity operators on distributive nearlattices. Calomino, Celani, and González (2020) introduce filter-valued quasi-modal operators, and their Boolean powerset example concerns the **unpunctured** Boolean lattice, in which every filter is principal. Calomino (2023), particularly Theorem 28, establishes the general equivalence of monadic quasi-modal operators and Boolean quantifiers. These framework and correspondence results are used here, not claimed as new. The specific exact locus of principal-valued operations on the zero-punctured powerset, its asymptotic scarcity, and the resulting intersection with the monadic subclass are the findings asserted here. Related enumeration under alternative implication-algebra or relational terminology could exist; absence from the inspected references does not establish universal priority.

## Limitations
This theorem applies only to nonempty subsets of a finite nonempty set with the indicated partial meet and to the cited quasi-modal/monadic axioms. It does not classify quasi-modal operations on all finite distributive nearlattices or assert an independent historical priority determination. The computed examples verify only finite small cases; the general result rests on the displayed deductions. Independent scientific and originality audits have not been performed.
