# Quadratic hub depletion and vanishing coherence in postselected quantum-star walks

## Finding

Let \(K_{1,m}\), \(m\ge 2\), be the star graph, with a hub numbered zero. In the postselected quantum stochastic walk (QSW) with Laplacian Hamiltonian, QSW interpolation \(0<p\le 1\), and imperfect detection efficiency \(0\le\eta<1\), the normalized conditional dynamics has a unique globally attracting stationary density matrix \(\rho_m\). It has the permutation-invariant form
\[
\rho_m=\begin{pmatrix}
a_m&(u_m+iv_m)\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}\\
(u_m-iv_m)\mathbf 1_m&(b_m-d_m)I_m+d_m\mathbf 1_m\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}
\end{pmatrix},\qquad a_m+mb_m=1.
\]
For fixed \(p\in(0,1]\) and \(\eta\in(0,1)\), with \(m\to\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta m^2}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-3}),\\
\lambda_m&=-p(1+\eta)+\frac{p(1-\eta)^2}{\eta m}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-2}),\\
v_m&=\frac{2(1-p)}{p m^3}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-4}),\\
u_m&=\frac{4(1-p)^2}{p^2m^4}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-5}),\\
d_m&=-\frac{4(1-p)^2}{p^2(1-\eta)m^3}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-4}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\lambda_m\) is the exponential growth exponent of the unnormalized no-detected-jump ensemble, and \(\rho_m\) is normalized to trace one. The usual site-basis \(\ell_1\) coherence obeys, for fixed \(0<p<1\),
\[
C_{\ell_1}(\rho_m)=\sum_{j\ne k}|(\rho_m)_{jk}|=\frac{4(1-p)^2}{p^2(1-\eta)m}+O_{p,\eta}(m^{-2}).
\]
For \(p=1\), coherence is identically zero. Thus increasing star degree yields a *quadratically* depleted hub probability and *vanishing aggregate steady coherence*, despite the nonzero coherence seen in finite heterogeneous networks.

There is a sharp weak-monitoring crossover. For fixed \(p\in(0,1]\), \(\xi\ge0\), and \(\eta_m=\xi/m\) (for sufficiently large \(m\)),
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}m a_m=\frac{1}{1+\xi},\qquad
\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda_m=-\frac{p\xi}{1+\xi}.
\]
At exactly \(\eta=0\), \(\rho_m=I_{m+1}/(m+1)\) and \(\lambda_m=0\). These two regimes show that efficiency on the scale \(m^{-1}\) is sufficient to change the hub's leading weight.

## Assumptions and scope

Take the graph Laplacian \(H=\operatorname{diag}(m,1,\ldots,1)-A\), with \(A\) the star adjacency matrix. For every ordered pair of vertices define \(P_{kj}=H_{kj}|k\rangle\langle j|\), including the diagonal jump operators. The trace-decreasing linear generator, whose normalization gives the conditional evolution, is
\[
\mathcal K(X)=-i(1-p)[H,X]+p\sum_{k,j}\left((1-\eta)P_{kj}XP_{kj}^{\dagger}-\tfrac12\{P_{kj}^{\dagger}P_{kj},X\}\right).
\]
The normalized state is \(\rho(t)=e^{t\mathcal K}(\rho(0))/\operatorname{Tr}(e^{t\mathcal K}(\rho(0)))\). All convergence and asymptotic statements concern normalized finite-dimensional density matrices and the limits specified above; no uniform assertion for \(p\downarrow0\) or \(\eta\uparrow1\) is made.

## Proof

**1. Strict positivity and uniqueness.** The semigroup generated by \(\mathcal K\) is completely positive and trace-nonincreasing: the part without jump gains has a non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian, and all jump gains have positive coefficient \(p(1-\eta)>0\). For every nonzero positive matrix, at least one diagonal entry is positive. A prescribed sequence of on-site and nearest-neighbor jump Kraus operations, possible on the connected star, carries this positive mass to *each* vertex with positive Dyson weight for any \(t>0\). Hence \(e^{t\mathcal K}\) is positivity improving. Finite-dimensional Perron–Frobenius theory for positive semigroups supplies a simple real dominant eigenvalue \(\lambda_m\) and a unique positive-definite trace-one eigenmatrix; every initially positive trace-one state converges to it upon normalization. Star permutation symmetry forces the displayed five-real-variable form. When \(\eta=0\), the generator is trace preserving and unital; primitivity gives \(I_{m+1}/(m+1)\).

**2. Exact five-dimensional reduction.** Write \(w=1-p\), \(c=1-\eta\), \(A_m=m+\eta m^2\), \(B=1+\eta\), and \(\Gamma_m=(m^2+m+2)/2\). Directly evaluating the commutator and jump operators shows that the five real entries \((a,b,u,v,d)\) of an unnormalized permutation-invariant Hermitian matrix evolve with the *exact* matrix
\[
M_m=\begin{pmatrix}
-pA_m&pmc&0&2wm&0\\
pc&-pB&0&-2w&0\\
0&0&-p\Gamma_m&w(m-1)&0\\
-w&w&-w(m-1)&-p\Gamma_m&w(m-1)\\
0&0&0&-2w&-2p
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Perron eigenvector of this matrix, normalized by \(a+mb=1\), determines the full stationary matrix. The associated eigenvalue satisfies \(-2p<\lambda_m\le0\). For the lower bound take the antisymmetric leaf state \((|1\rangle-|2\rangle)/\sqrt2\): it is an eigenstate of \(H\) and of the total jump intensity \(G=\sum P^\dagger P\), with \(G\)-eigenvalue two. Its unjumped density component survives exactly as \(e^{-2pt}\); positive jump gains make the full semigroup dominate this component. Strict positivity implies a strictly larger Perron exponent.

**3. Scalar resolvent equations.** Since \(\lambda_m>-2p\) and \(\Gamma_m\ge4\), all denominators in
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_m(\lambda)&=\lambda+p\Gamma_m,\\
K_m(\lambda)&=T_m(\lambda)+\frac{w^2(m-1)^2}{T_m(\lambda)}+\frac{2w^2(m-1)}{\lambda+2p},\\
q_m(\lambda)&=\frac{2w^2}{K_m(\lambda)}
\end{aligned}
\]
are positive at \(\lambda=\lambda_m\). Solving the last three eigenvector equations gives
\[
u=\frac{w(m-1)v}{T_m(\lambda)},\qquad
v=-\frac{w(a-b)}{K_m(\lambda)},\qquad
d=-\frac{2wv}{\lambda+2p}.
\]
The remaining two equations yield, writing \(x=a/b\),
\[
x=\frac{m[pc+q_m(\lambda)]}{\lambda+pA_m+m q_m(\lambda)},\qquad
\lambda+pB=pcx+q_m(\lambda)(x-1).
\]
This supplies an exact one-scalar characterization of the physically admissible Perron root and all stationary entries for every finite \(m\).

**4. Fixed-efficiency asymptotics.** For fixed \(p>0\), \(0<\eta<1\), consider the preceding scalar equation for \(\lambda\) in a small fixed neighborhood of \(-pB\), which lies strictly between \(-2p\) and zero. Uniformly there,
\[
K_m(\lambda)=\tfrac p2m^2+O(m),\qquad
q_m(\lambda)=\frac{4w^2}{pm^2}+O(m^{-3}),\qquad
x=\frac{c}{\eta m}+O(m^{-2}).
\]
The scalar equation has a unique real root nearby by the implicit-function theorem (its derivative tends to one). The matrix recovered from this root is positive definite for all sufficiently large \(m\): \(a\asymp m^{-2}\), \(b\asymp m^{-1}\), and all offdiagonal entries are \(O(m^{-3})\). By positivity improvement this root is the dominant Perron root. Substituting the expansions gives \(\lambda_m=-pB+pc^2/(\eta m)+O(m^{-2})\), \(a=c/(\eta m^2)+O(m^{-3})\), and the displayed expansions for \(v,u,d\). Finally,
\[
C_{\ell_1}(\rho_m)=2m\sqrt{u_m^2+v_m^2}+m(m-1)|d_m|,
\]
which yields the claimed coefficient of \(m^{-1}\). When \(w=0\), the offdiagonal equations force \(u=v=d=0\) exactly.

**5. Critical weak-monitoring scaling.** With \(\eta_m=\xi/m\) and fixed \(\xi>0\), the same scalar argument near \(-p\xi/(1+\xi)\) yields \(q_m=O(m^{-2})\) and \(x\to1/(1+\xi)\). Since \(a=x/(m+x)\), \(ma\to1/(1+\xi)\). The second scalar equation gives \(\lambda_m\to-p\xi/(1+\xi)\). At \(\xi=0\), the exact unmonitored stationary state gives both limits directly.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone program `check_star.py` computes the exact five-dimensional reduction and independently constructs the entire \((m+1)^2\)-dimensional quantum Liouvillian for small stars. It compares leading eigenvalues, complete eigenmatrices, eigen-equations and positivity, and tests the displayed large-degree constants and weak-monitoring crossover. Running `python check_star.py` with NumPy and SciPy passes 216 assertions. These finite tests support, but do not replace, the analytical asymptotic and uniqueness proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Adithya L J, Hegde and Meena introduce the same postselected QSW, prove a general balance condition for uniform stationary states and a diagonal stationarity constraint, and numerically demonstrate peripheral localization/coherence; their separate XY-spin-network illustrations include one six-vertex star with different, edge-only jump operators. The present calculation uses their single-particle Laplacian-QSW generator but obtains a closed five-variable stationary reduction and rigorous arbitrary-degree asymptotics, including explicit loss of *aggregate* coherence and a monitoring-efficiency crossover not asserted in their results. Liu and Chen derive the general nonlinear postselection equation and study skin effects; Whitfield, Rodríguez-Rosario and Aspuru-Guzik introduce QSWs without the present conditioning; Bressanini, Benedetti and Paris analyze unconditional graph decoherence. Classical Perron–Frobenius and Schur-complement techniques are acknowledged, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns star graphs with the *Laplacian-amplitude* jump convention including diagonal jumps. It does not assert the same powers for different jump normalizations, fixed-degree graph families, perfect detection, or a simultaneous singular limit \(p\downarrow0\). The large-degree stationary coherence vanishes although fixed finite graphs can retain coherence. Convergence rates and experimentally required successful trajectory counts are not quantified.
