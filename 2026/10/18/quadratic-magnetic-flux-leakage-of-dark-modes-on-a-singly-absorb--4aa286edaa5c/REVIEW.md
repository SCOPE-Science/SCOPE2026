# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact secular polynomial follows from Fourier diagonalization of the cyclic hopping matrix and the cofactor/derivative identity for a rank-one loss. At the interior doubled free energies, the Chebyshev ODE proves the dark root is simple at every fixed positive loss strength. Expanding the secular equation and using evenness in the detuning yields an explicitly negative imaginary quadratic coefficient and a fourth-order remainder. The strict-decay conclusion at nonsymmetric flux follows from dissipativity plus free-ring simple spectrum. The independent standalone checker passed 33 direct determinant and 195 local linewidth evaluations; these are supporting finite checks, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected comparison
The strongest direct predecessor is Krapivsky–Luck–Mallick, arXiv:1311.6298, Sections 4–5, which already establishes zero-flux avoiding modes on trapped finite rings; that previously known fact is expressly excluded from the new contribution. The new statement instead concerns the **flux derivative** of each non-Hermitian eigenvalue, with an exact coefficient and vanishing quadratic real part. Longhi's arXiv:1312.4693 studies a continuum non-Hermitian ring; Gou et al.'s arXiv:2001.01859 is a three-site lossy flux-ring scattering element attached to a lead; Burke–Wiersig–Haque's arXiv:1910.07439 is an open tight-binding chain with a local imaginary scatterer. None of their inspected corresponding result statements supplies this coefficient for finite cycles of arbitrary size, and simple specializations of their models do not state the claimed flux curvature. Residual risk remains from less readily discoverable flux-impurity transfer-matrix calculations; originality here has not received independent priority review.

## Value — PASS
A zero-loss eigenmode at exactly integer/half-integer flux is destroyed by arbitrarily small flux errors, so the first nonzero imaginary perturbation controls the lifetime of an experimentally natural interference-protected mode. The sharp dependence \(\gamma^{-1}\), \(N^{-1}\), and \(\sin^2 q\) provides a quantitative sensitivity/lifetime calibration for **every** protected mode, and the exact absence of a quadratic real shift distinguishes phase sensitivity from dissipation. This is a nontrivial closed-form spectral structure, not a restatement of generic perturbation existence. The statement does not address a many-body gap, global slowest decay rate, or uniform limits.
