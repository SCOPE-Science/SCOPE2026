# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The coordinate equations are derived directly. In odd characteristic, a two-to-one sign/root parameterization includes all nonzero-square cases and the exceptional all-equal scalar solution; the discriminants yield the three exact branches. In characteristic two, the Artin–Schreier parameterization accounts for the two-to-one identification and whether the irreducible quadratic splits. Both arguments put every geometric solution inside the quadratic prime-field extension. The embedded checker exhaustively tests 38 finite configurations, including characteristics two, three, five, seven, eleven, and quadratic extensions. Limits: computational cases do not prove universal quantifiers; these follow from the written proof.

## Originality
PASS under self-review, with bibliographic uncertainty. Giacomelli's complete-graph example (arXiv:2605.12672, Example 10.2) concerns expansion and does not compute these idempotents. Narkuziyev's two-permutation model (arXiv:2005.05793, Section 4) is a distinct, sparse-structure class. Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel (arXiv:2610.22248, Sections 2.1.4 and 2.1.10) provides a general geometric upper bound and a finite-field average, neither of which gives the exact complete-graph counts or quadratic descent. Published mathematical-results searches for complete-graph idempotents, arithmetic character sums, and quadratic splitting yielded related idempotent-free evolution-algebra and graph results but no implication covering the present theorem. The risk of an unindexed equivalent computation remains; absence from searches is not absolute novelty evidence.

## Value
PASS. This exact natural-family classification determines all equilibrium/idempotent points of the canonical complete-graph evolution algebra, including a field-of-definition theorem, a parity phenomenon in extension degree, and explicit exceptional characteristic deficits. It supplies an arithmetic invariant for the highly connected example introduced in finite-field evolution-algebra research, rather than just verifying its obvious constant idempotents or listing small cases. It does not resolve the general expander-characterization problem.
