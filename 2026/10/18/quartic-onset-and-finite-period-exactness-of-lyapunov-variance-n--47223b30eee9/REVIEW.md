# Scientific review

## Correctness
The first-order perturbation equals \(q\circ T-2q\), where \(q(x)=\sin(2\pi x)\), so conjugating with \(H_\varepsilon(x)=x+\varepsilon q(x)\) cancels it. An explicit second-order derivative observable has frequencies 1, 2 and 3. Exactly at periods \(n\ge5\), the ±-frequency classes give disjoint Fourier characters modulo \(2^n-1\); their exact variance is \(36\pi^4/n\). The unweighted inverse-branch transfer operator contracts derivatives uniformly, so dominated convergence on its Green--Kubo correlations proves the infinite-period coefficient. All fixed-period error bounds are local in the parameter and allow constants depending on the period.

## Originality
The closest general theorem, Drach et al. (arXiv:2511.15662, Sections 1 and 4), proves asymptotic normality and identifies the variance, but supplies neither a quartic small-parameter coefficient nor the exact common finite-period leading coefficient for this family. Shub--Sullivan's multiplier rigidity does not quantify variance. The public integer-beta switching variance theorem and integer-beta pair-correlation variance theorem concern distinct objects or perturbations and do not imply the new calculation. Targeted mathematical-index searches for smooth-conjugacy tangencies, quartic Lyapunov variance, perturbative Fourier coboundaries and exact periodic multipliers found no equivalent statement. Remaining risk: unindexed classical Fourier perturbation calculations might already contain this special example.

## Mathematical value
The result isolates a genuinely weakly nonrigid regime: the usual first-order Lyapunov observable is a coboundary and produces no quadratic variance, but the second-order obstruction creates a positive quartic coefficient. The equality of the leading finite-period and asymptotic coefficients starting at the sharp all-period character threshold gives exact information about the small-parameter limit of periodic-orbit experiments. It quantitatively sharpens the broad positive-variance dichotomy and illustrates a structural perturbation direction rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limits
This is a local parameter theorem for a specific two-harmonic family. Uniform simultaneous period--parameter remainders and general perturbation classifications are not claimed. It has been checked by the same proof-and-code process only, not by an independent auditor.
