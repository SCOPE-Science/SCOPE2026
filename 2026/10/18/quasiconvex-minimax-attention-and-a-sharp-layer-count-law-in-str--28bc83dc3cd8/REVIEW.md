# Scientific review: minimax attention in strategic multiplex consensus

## Correctness — PASS

The assumptions match a primitive common-stationary-layer strategic reporting game with strictly positive quadratic report costs. The equilibrium is reconstructed directly from each agent's first-order conditions; the Hessian is positive definite, and the aggregation equation has one solution because \(1+K>0\). The worst-case opinion optimization is exact because the coefficient vector has zero sum and the opinion cube has binary extreme points. The squared-attention normalization is a bijection on the closed simplex, and the transformed denominator is concave since it is a positive linear form plus pairwise geometric means. The rotated cone inequalities provide an if-and-only-if feasibility certificate. Carathéodory's theorem yields the stated support bound. For the sharp family, elementary total-variation and Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities match at uniform attention on every allowed support. Exact rational verification reproduces all displayed identities on finite test cases. No unproved finite-to-infinite extrapolation is used.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated priority risks

The closest source is Ebrahimi and Franceschetti, arXiv:2609.22524 (September 18, 2026). Its Lemma 3.1 and Theorems 4.1--4.2 provide the equilibrium and fixed-opinion distortion; Theorem 7.1 minimizes fixed-opinion distortion under common centrality; Theorems 7.2--7.3 address opinion-specific gradients and general-layer polynomial optimization. Its variance-bounded worst-case distortion expression does not solve the present \([0,1]^n\)-robust platform optimization. The new statement concerns a **single** attention choice protecting against **all** bounded opinion profiles, gives an exact global second-order-cone feasibility formulation, and establishes sharp \(n\)-layer sufficiency/necessity and a full cardinality-constrained risk frontier. This is not a restatement of the paper's fixed-opinion result. Jalan and Papachristou (arXiv:2502.15931v2) address FJ adversaries, rather than this optimization, and Shiu and Naghizadeh (arXiv:2603.07633v3) study truthful multiplex coordination. Searches of published mathematical research records for common-stationary multiplex misreporting, all-profile neutrality, squared attention convexification and minimax cost distortions returned no implication-equivalent record. The underlying general quasiconvex and conic techniques are established prior mathematics and are not claimed as new. Historical-priority risk remains if a general robust-design theorem elsewhere already specializes to this result.

## Value — PASS

The source explicitly studies platforms choosing channel attention without necessarily knowing each private opinion. A policy optimized for one specified opinion vector can fail when the opinions change, motivating a worst-case protection rule. Exact conic feasibility replaces local nonconvex searches with a globally checkable computation; the convex-hull neutrality criterion distinguishes feasible from impossible all-opinion robustness before optimizing. The sharp layer-count construction establishes that up to \(n\) distinct channels can be **necessary**, not just sufficient, and quantifies the loss from every smaller channel budget. The result does not merely rename the fixed-opinion formula or compute an arbitrary constant: it solves the natural opinion-independent design and cardinality questions in a clearly delimited model.
