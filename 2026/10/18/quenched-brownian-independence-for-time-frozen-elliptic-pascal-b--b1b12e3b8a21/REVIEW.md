# Scientific review: quenched invariance for frozen Pascal bootstrap walks

**Correctness — PASS.** The result is about all deterministic probability sequences in a common \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) interval and almost every independently sampled *frozen* coefficient environment. The proof verifies a Lucas-support separation lemma, environment-character factorization, a uniform polynomial two-time covariance estimate, and the summable-subsequence interpolation needed for a genuinely almost-sure cross-variation limit. The bounded martingale jumps and diagonal quadratic variations complete the functional Brownian argument. The checker supplied in `verify_pascal.py` passed 65,904 finite exact assertions; none is presented as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Hamza--Tournier, arXiv:2610.01658v1, Section 4.3, explicitly treats this coefficient model at the **annealed** level only and lists quenched convergence as an unresolved question. Collevecchio--Hamza--Shi--Williams, arXiv:2010.16000, Theorem 6, provides a general deterministic covariance criterion but not a proof of its almost-sure hypotheses for this random environment. Previously known timewise-resampled coefficient conclusions and the homogeneous fair frozen case do not cover arbitrary frozen heterogeneous probabilities. Related research records and alternate Pascal-parity formulations were searched; no stronger covering source was identified. Some general nonstationary random-scenery theorem may still subsume part of this argument, and independent priority verification is required.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A natural, explicitly identified gap in a current functional limit theorem is resolved for the entire elliptic, nonstationary frozen-cardinality class. The significant point is the almost-every-environment limit, supported by an explicit covariance decay mechanism, not a fresh rephrasing of an already known annealed limit. No convergence of the coefficient parameters is assumed.

**Scope and limitations.** This theorem does not cover coefficient arrays with long-range dependence, parameters approaching zero or one, or non-Gaussian fluctuation limits for the unscaled cross-variation. Its polynomial decay exponent is not optimized. The original Pascal parity identities and martingale limit criterion remain attributed to prior work.
