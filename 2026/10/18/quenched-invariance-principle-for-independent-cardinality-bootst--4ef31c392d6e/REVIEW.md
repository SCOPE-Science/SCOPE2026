# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The construction agrees with the inclusion-induced one-factor formula. Lucas's theorem supplies exactly \(2^{b(s)}\) active independent Bernoulli signs. A dyadic digit-average identity, a geometric waiting-time strong law, and a conditional independent-variable strong law imply the normalized cross variation vanishes jointly almost surely. Fubini converts this to a quenched almost-sure statement; bounded increments and martingale quadratic variations then prove the full functional Brownian limit. The proof treats arbitrary heterogeneous coefficients within a fixed ellipticity interval. Finite checker cases were run from the delivered package; they are not the proof of the infinite assertion.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk.** In Hamza–Tournier arXiv:2610.01658v1, Corollary 9 proves only annealed normality for exactly the same time-independent coefficient rows, and the following remark calls the quenched extension unresolved. Their Theorem 2 assumes independent subset-indexed blocks and cannot cover repeated cardinality coefficients; Proposition 7(b) proves only a quenched mean condition. The earlier Collevecchio–Hamza–Shi–Williams criterion is an implication from cross-variation decay, not a proof that such decay holds for these random coefficients. Actual mathematical-record searches and comparisons of accessible prior claims revealed no equivalent theorem. An unindexed or subsequently released solution, or a general conditional-strong-law theorem specialized to this object, remains possible and must not be excluded by search absence alone.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Quenched versus annealed convergence is explicitly singled out as the unresolved substantive problem for this natural random bootstrap walk. A quenched functional limit is strictly stronger than distributional convergence after averaging the environment, and the almost-sure covariance argument pinpoints a reusable mechanism via binary-digit decay and geometric occupation times. This is not a parameter substitution of the earlier annealed statement.

The main source is Kais Hamza and Laurent Tournier, *Are random random walks normal?* (arXiv:2610.01658v1, 2026), Sections 2–4. The earlier general bootstrap-walk criterion (arXiv:2010.16000) is acknowledged. Limitations include temporal independence, uniform ellipticity and absence of a convergence-rate theorem.
