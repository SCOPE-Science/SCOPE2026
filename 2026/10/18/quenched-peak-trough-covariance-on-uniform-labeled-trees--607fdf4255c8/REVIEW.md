# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The local pair-covariance formula is derived by conditioning on the shared observations. On a tree, pairs at distance at least three have disjoint relevant mark sets. For distance one and two the stated beta integrals give exact joint probabilities; self-pairs have covariance minus the square of the marginal probability. The weighted forest-enumeration identity gives conditional endpoint-degree distributions after forcing one edge or a length-two path. Multiplying the \(2/n\) and \(3/n^2\) incidence probabilities proves the exact finite-size tree-average. Multinomial-to-Poisson convergence establishes the limit with bounded integrands. The strictly negative sign is backed by a rational tail certificate, not decimal numerics alone. Exact enumeration checks the finite identities through six vertices. Risks: numerical coefficient only applies to the tree-averaged conditional covariance, not the unconditional covariance; all hypotheses and quantifiers in `RESULT.md` are required.

## Originality — PASS, residual priority risk
The closest inspected article is Majumdar and Martin's arXiv:cond-mat/0609735, which studies one-statistic minima laws on fixed lattices and fixed-branching trees rather than paired extrema on a *uniform random labeled tree*. Clark's DOI:10.1155/IJMMS.2005.3867 analyzes extrema of internal rooted three-vertex paths; they are not all-neighbor extrema. Burdzy and Pal's arXiv:1606.08025 develops conditioning on one/two peaks, and Diaz-Lopez et al.'s arXiv:1708.08493 supplies a fixed-graph peak-set enumeration method, with a leaf-excluding convention; no inspected explicit theorem yields the mixed quenched covariance or the small negative asymptotic coefficient. General enumeration machinery may permit an indirect derivation and is acknowledged as a residual risk; no guarantee of historical priority or independent audit is asserted.

## Value — PASS
Both local minima and local maxima are natural critical-site counts in random energy landscapes. A priori, adjacent maximum/minimum events reinforce one another while distance-two events repel one another; their aggregate sign is therefore not a simple consequence of one-variable moments. The exact fixed-tree decomposition, conditioned-tree degree distributions, and certified negative linear coefficient explain the balance on a uniformly random labeled tree, in contrast to the positive covariance on long paths. This identifies a nontrivial quenched dependence parameter and separates mark-disorder fluctuations from tree-geometry fluctuations.
