Closed neighborhoods are disjoint at greater distances. In the forced-forest weighted Cayley sum, each forced edge contributes its two endpoint weights; contracting components gives one sum of weights per component and the remaining global multinomial power. Thus forcing one edge occurs with probability 2/n and gives an endpoint offset distributed by a fair two-way draw plus n−3 global draws; forcing a two-edge path occurs with probability 3/n² and gives a three-way offset (including the middle vertex) plus n−4 draws. The sums count ordered endpoints, and a length-two path has a unique middle, so the prefactors in the claimed finite average follow.
