# Rank-flat attractors and an exact update-order transition in polar spectral weight decay

## Finding

On the isotropic strongly convex matrix quadratic, the exact polar-factor version of post-step spectral weight decay has a completely solvable global singular-value dynamics. Generic trajectories do not approach the unique minimizer of the nuclear-norm-regularized objective: each nonzero singular value converges to the same **positive** value. The update-order choice changes the asymptotic attractor sharply when the smooth-gradient step crosses its one-step minimizer.

Let \(m,n\ge1\), \(a,\lambda,\eta>0\), \(0<\eta a<2\), and \(W_0\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) have rank \(r\ge1\). Write \(\alpha=1-\eta a\), \(q=|\alpha|\), and \(\tau=\eta\lambda\). For the **canonical partial polar factor** \(Q(U\operatorname{diag}(s_i)V^{\mathsf T})=U\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf1_{s_i>0})V^{\mathsf T}\), with \(Q(0)=0\), compare:

\[
W^+_{\mathrm{post}}=\alpha W-\tau Q(\alpha W),\qquad
W^+_{\mathrm{pre}}=\alpha W-\tau Q(W),\qquad
W^+_{\mathrm{prox}}=\operatorname{SVT}_{\tau}(\alpha W).
\]

For \(\eta a\ne1\), every initial positive singular mode that never hits zero satisfies the following. The post-step singular values converge geometrically, with error at most \(q^k\) times the initial singular-value error, to
\[
\boxed{\delta_{\mathrm{post}}=\frac{\eta\lambda}{1+|1-\eta a|}>0.}
\]
The exceptional set of initial positive singular values that eventually hit zero is countable; outside the union of those scalar exceptional sets the rank stays \(r\) and the entropy-based effective rank tends exactly to \(r\). Thus the asymptotic singular values become **flat**, not low rank.

If \(0<\eta a<1\), pre-step and post-step iterates agree exactly at every step; both approach an antipodal period-two pair with every nonzero singular value \(\delta_{\mathrm{post}}\). If \(1<\eta a<2\), post-step iterates instead converge to a **nonzero fixed matrix** of rank \(r\) with all positive singular values
\[
\boxed{\delta_{\mathrm{post}}=\lambda/a,}
\]
while pre-step iterates approach an antipodal period-two pair whose positive singular values equal
\[
\boxed{\delta_{\mathrm{pre}}=\frac{\eta\lambda}{2-\eta a}.}
\]
Consequently their limiting Frobenius magnitudes have ratio
\[
\boxed{\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{pre}}}{\delta_{\mathrm{post}}}=\frac{\eta a}{2-\eta a}\longrightarrow+\infty\qquad(\eta a\uparrow2).}
\]
At \(\eta a=1\) the post-step iteration reaches zero in one step, whereas the pre-step iteration started at nonzero \(W_0\) is an exact nonzero antipodal two-cycle from its first update.

In contrast, for every \(0<\eta a<2\), the genuine nuclear-norm proximal step reaches the unique minimizer \(W=0\) in finitely many steps. When \(1<\eta a<2\), the generic post-step fixed point has the strictly positive, learning-rate-independent regularized objective value
\[
\boxed{\frac{3r\lambda^2}{2a}},
\]
for \(F(W)=\tfrac a2\|W\|_F^2+\lambda\|W\|_*\). These are exact properties of the ideal partial-polar rule; no claim is made for inexact polynomial polar approximations or adaptive optimizers.

## Assumptions and scope

All matrices are real finite-dimensional; \(Q\) uses the compact SVD and assigns zero to vanishing singular directions. The step is constant, the smooth loss is exactly \(f(W)=\tfrac a2\|W\|_F^2\), and the initial rank is positive. The post-step rule is the exact-polar specialization of additive spectral weight decay. The pre-step rule is the corresponding decoupled correction evaluated at the old iterate; the proximal rule uses exact singular-value soft thresholding. No stochasticity, approximate polar computation, momentum, or nonisotropic Hessian is assumed.

## Proof

Fix an initial compact SVD \(W_0=U\operatorname{diag}(s_{1,0},\ldots,s_{r,0})V^{\mathsf T}\), with \(s_{i,0}>0\). Each of the three maps preserves the spaces generated by these singular vector pairs: a signed coefficient \(y_i\) evolves independently, and any coefficient that becomes zero stays zero. A change in coefficient sign changes the orientation of the corresponding singular-vector pair but not its singular value. Therefore it suffices to analyze one positive singular value \(s\).

For \(q\in(0,1)\), post-step partial-polar shrinkage obeys
\[
 s^+=\varphi(s):=|qs-\tau|\quad(s>0),\qquad \varphi(0)=0.
\]
The positive fixed point \(\delta=\tau/(1+q)\) satisfies \(\varphi(\delta)=\delta\). By the reverse triangle inequality,
\[
|\varphi(s)-\delta|=\big||qs-\tau|-|q\delta-\tau|\big|\le q|s-\delta|.
\]
As long as the orbit does not hit zero, induction gives \(|s_k-\delta|\le q^k|s_0-\delta|\). A positive orbit hits zero precisely if \(\varphi^j(s_0)=\tau/q\) for some \(j\ge0\). Each inverse tree has finitely many branches at fixed \(j\); their union \(E_{q,\tau}\) is countable (and measure zero). Outside \(E_{q,\tau}\) all initially positive modes stay positive and converge to \(\delta\). Since \(r\) singular values tend to the same positive number, \(p_i=s_i/\sum_js_j\to1/r\), giving effective rank \(\exp(-\sum_i p_i\log p_i)\to r\).

For \(0<\eta a<1\), \(\alpha=q>0\); hence \(Q(\alpha W)=Q(W)\) and pre-step equals post-step **exactly**, including after rank loss. For a nonexceptional mode, eventually \(qs_k<\tau\), so its signed coefficient then reverses sign each step. All \(r\) modes enter this region after finitely many steps, hence the even and odd matrix subsequences converge to opposite rank-\(r\) partial isometries scaled by \(\delta\).

For \(1<\eta a<2\), \(\alpha=-q\). The post-step signed coefficient is \(y^+=-qy+\tau\operatorname{sgn}(y)\), whose magnitude still follows \(\varphi\). Eventually \(q|y|<\tau\), so its sign thereafter stays unchanged. Therefore post-step matrices converge to a fixed partial isometry scaled by \(\delta=\tau/(1+q)\). Since \(1+q=\eta a\) in this regime, \(\delta=\lambda/a\). Its fixed-point equation follows directly: \(-q\delta+\tau=\delta\).

For the pre-step method with \(\alpha=-q\), the signed coefficient satisfies \(y^+=-qy-\tau\operatorname{sgn}(y)\). Its sign flips at **every** step and its positive magnitude evolves via \(s^+=qs+\tau\), so
\[
 s_k=\frac{\tau}{1-q}+q^k\left(s_0-\frac{\tau}{1-q}\right).
\]
It follows that all nonzero modes survive, their magnitudes tend to \(\tau/(1-q)=\eta\lambda/(2-\eta a)\), and even/odd subsequences approach opposite partial isometries. Their Frobenius magnitude divided by the post-step limiting magnitude is the ratio displayed above. This comparison remains valid despite a uniformly bounded **one-step** pre/post discrepancy: a bound on one-step updates is not a uniform long-horizon trajectory bound.

At \(\eta a=1\), \(\alpha=0\). Our convention gives \(Q(0)=0\), so post-step immediately reaches zero. Pre-step satisfies \(W^+=-\tau Q(W)\); for \(W\ne0\), the next update is \(+\tau Q(W)\), and thereafter the two matrices alternate.

For the true proximal map, each positive singular value satisfies \(s^+=\max(qs-\tau,0)\). As \(q<1\) and \(\tau>0\), the positive values decrease by at least \(\tau\) per step until zero, so all modes vanish after finitely many steps. Finally, \(F(W)\ge0\) has unique minimizer zero. The generic post-step fixed point for \(\eta a>1\) has \(r\) singular values \(\lambda/a\), whence \(F=r(a(\lambda/a)^2/2+\lambda(\lambda/a))=3r\lambda^2/(2a)\). This completes the claimed classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Andriianov, Veprikov, and Beznosikov introduce post-step spectral weight decay (their Equation (1)) and compare pre/post one-step outputs (Proposition 1 and Appendix A.1), while Appendix A.2 contrasts the polar step with the exact nuclear-norm proximal threshold. They do not establish the centered-quadratic infinite-horizon attractors, update-order transition at \(\eta a=1\), generic rank-flat limit, or the diverging two-order attractor ratio proved here.

González-Martínez and Liu introduce SLORR, whose nuclear-norm regularizer uses a polar-factor update; the adjacent pre-step variant is discussed in the first source. These methods establish related update definitions rather than the asymptotic fixed-step classification here. Existing constant-step subgradient instability studies concern broader nonconvergence phenomena; they do not by themselves provide the three-rule singular-value and matrix-attractor classification. A related analysis of exact-line-search matrix-polar descent instead uses a changing line-search step and yields a mean-centered spectrum, not this constant-step post/pre/prox comparison. A published scalar Polar-Express analysis uses a smoothed nonlinear factor applied to the gradient, not a weight polar factor applied after a smooth task step.

## Limitations

The countable resonance set can cause finite rank loss, and the stated rank-\(r\) attractors require avoiding it; the rank remaining after exact rank loss still follows the same scalar laws. Only the ideal canonical partial polar factor is considered, whereas implementations may approximate the full-rank polar factor using finite polynomial iterations. The model deliberately omits nonisotropic curvature, neural-network training, noise and adaptive optimization. Its persistent rank does not contradict empirically observed lower effective ranks in different optimization settings. This is a structured counterbenchmark, not a universal neural-network prediction. The relevant source and database searches cannot rule out all equivalent older nonsmooth-dynamics formulations.
