# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The equation \(CA+AC=C^2\), forced by two square-zero summands, determines all cross-eigenspace blocks of a summand for a split-semisimple target. Nonzero opposite-eigenvalue pairs reduce to anti-isometry torsors, with their exact sizes supplied by finite orthogonal groups. On the zero eigenspace, square-zero selfadjoint maps correspond bijectively to isotropic image subspaces together with nonsingular symmetric identifications of the dual and image. The isotropic-flag count yields the stated rank coefficients. Direct enumeration verifies ten specified finite-field/form/target configurations, and a separate exhaustive batch covers all 258 diagonal tests in dimensions one through three over the ternary field.

## Originality — PASS within inspected coverage
The strongest directly relevant source is de Seguins Pazzis, arXiv:2210.03955v1, Theorems 1.13 and 1.15, Sections 1.5, 1.6.3 and 4.2: it classifies whether selfadjoint square-zero decompositions exist, a stronger existence setting than assumed here. That existence theorem is acknowledged and excluded from the novelty claim. The rank-refined number of decompositions for each split-semisimple target does not follow numerically from its stated existence criterion. Separate literature on rank distributions of random symmetric matrices covers a different invariant. Prior-work searches under sum decompositions, orthogonal isometry torsors, and constrained Sylvester equations did not locate this precise rank-fiber formula. An equivalent old formula under different terminology remains an originality risk; absence from search results is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The rank-fiber polynomial answers a natural multiplicity question left by a general existence classification: how many selfadjoint square-zero summands realize a prescribed diagonalizable target, at which ranks? It covers all odd finite fields, all dimensions, all split semisimple target spectra, and all quadratic-form types. It exhibits a determinant obstruction and a Witt-index-controlled interval of possible summand ranks, and provides explicit multiplicities rather than an isolated small enumeration.

## Scope and limitations
The result is restricted to odd characteristic and split semisimple targets with a nondegenerate symmetric form. It does not address nonsplit or nonsemisimple targets. Finite exhaustive verification supports rather than replaces the symbolic proof. An independent mathematical audit has not been undertaken.
