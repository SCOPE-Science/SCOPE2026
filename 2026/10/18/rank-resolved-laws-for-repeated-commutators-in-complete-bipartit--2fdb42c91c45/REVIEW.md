# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The group law is associative as a two-step central extension, and direct group multiplication gives the rank-two commutator form. The free central-factor exponent is checked against the full group coordinates. Fourier orthogonality gives \(q^{2(a+b-s)}\) at dual rank \(s\), then subspace Möbius inversion produces the explicit \(K_s(r)\) formula; the sign in Fourier inversion is harmless because rank-stratified character sums are invariant under negation. The lower rank bound and rank-one decomposition give matching lower and upper commutator-length bounds. Nine exhaustive one-step cases and four repeated-convolution configurations independently test the finite-field formula, including nonprime fields. All derivations hold in characteristic two.

## Originality — PASS with residual risks

The source-aligned contribution is a closed, rank-resolved all-\(k\) prescribed-target commutator-fiber formula and the sharp elementwise width law for \(K_{a,b}\) graphical groups. Rossmann's inspected complete-bipartite example enumerates conjugacy classes by size, not prescribed values of products of group commutators. Naik studies other restricted commutator-word distributions; Akhavan-Malayeri–Rhemtulla addresses nilpotent commutator length without these fibers. Earlier general Frobenius formulas and matrix-rank Fourier/Krawtchouk transforms cover important constituent methods, but do not by themselves state this explicit specialization. The full text of Sinha's early article and of a potentially relevant rank-Fourier treatment were unavailable; an equivalent result there or in unindexed literature remains a named priority risk. Searches of published mathematical records uncovered no decisive matching statement. These bounded searches cannot prove global novelty.

## Value — PASS

Every rank stratum of every central target receives an exact k-fold word-map count, uniform in all prime powers and two independent graph-part sizes. This determines complete commutator-product distributions and sharp elementwise length, not simply whether a single commutator exists. The rank-three obstruction and its positive two-fold fibers for the smallest nontrivial rectangular examples are structural, and the formula supports exact comparisons of distribution and mixing across dimensions. The proof is self-contained and the checker makes small cases reproducible.

## Scientific limitations

The results are limited to complete-bipartite graphical groups. The finite computations are supplementary and cannot prove all parameter values; that scope is supplied by character orthogonality and Möbius inversion. Some closely related literature was accessible only at the abstract or bibliographic level. Earlier all-groups character formulas are acknowledged; independent priority and mathematical review remain outstanding.
