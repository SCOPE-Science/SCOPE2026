# Rank-two hollow symmetric determinantal sections: Segre components and product-node singularities

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field with \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\), let \(n\ge3\), and let \(A\) be the symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix whose diagonal entries are zero and whose off-diagonal entries \(a_{ij}=a_{ji}\) are independent variables. Put \(I=I_3(A)\), the ideal of **all** three-by-three minors, and \(X_n=\operatorname{Proj}(k[a_{ij}:i<j]/I)\subset\mathbb P^{\binom n2-1}\).

**Theorem.** The scheme \(X_n\) is reduced, equidimensional, locally a complete intersection and has pure dimension \(n-2\). Its irreducible components are precisely the \(2^{n-1}-1\) Segre varieties \(Z_{S,T}\simeq\mathbb P^{|S|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|T|-1}\), one for each unordered decomposition \([n]=S\sqcup T\) into nonempty subsets, embedded by the coordinates \(a_{ij}\) for edges crossing \(S,T\), with every other coordinate zero. In particular,
\[
\deg X_n=\frac12\binom{2n-2}{n-1}.
\]

For a point \([A]\in X_n\), let \(z(A)\) be its number of identically zero rows. Its completed local ring, after extension to the residue field if necessary, has a presentation
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X_n,[A]}\simeq
 k[[u_1,\ldots,u_{n-2-z},s_1,t_1,\ldots,s_z,t_z]]/(s_1t_1,\ldots,s_zt_z),\qquad z=z(A).
\]
Thus \([A]\) is singular exactly when \(z(A)\ge1\). The reduced singular locus has precisely
\[
 n(2^{n-2}-1)
\]
irreducible components of dimension \(n-3\): for each \(w\in[n]\) and unordered nonempty partition \([n]\setminus\{w\}=S\sqcup T\), the component \(Z_{w;S,T}\) is the Segre \(\mathbb P^{|S|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|T|-1}\) formed by setting row \(w\) to zero and retaining only rank-one cross-edges between \(S\) and \(T\). Its degree is
\[
\deg\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}=
\frac n2\binom{2n-4}{n-2}.
\]
At a generic point on each singular component the transverse completed local equation is \(st=0\) (two smooth branches crossing). The normalization of \(X_n\) is the disjoint union of its displayed Segre components.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient coordinates are all \(\binom n2\) independent off-diagonal entries; no equality between distinct entries is imposed. All minors (including non-principal minors) are included in \(I_3(A)\). The base field is algebraically closed with characteristic not equal to two; in particular, the asserted local equations do not apply unchanged in characteristic two. The degree refers to the natural projective embedding above and to the reduced projective scheme structure of the singular support, not the possibly nonreduced Jacobian scheme. A projective point cannot be represented by the zero matrix.

## Proof

**1. Rank and local equations.** Every nonzero hollow symmetric matrix of rank at most two has a nonzero off-diagonal entry \(a_{pq}\). Fix such a pair and work on the standard projective affine chart \(D_+(a_{pq})\), rescaling to \(a_{pq}=1\). The principal block at rows and columns \(p,q\) is the invertible hyperbolic matrix \(H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For every \(i\notin\{p,q\}\), set \(s_i=a_{pi}\) and \(t_i=a_{qi}\). The Schur complement of \(H\) in \(A\) has \((i,j)\) entry \(a_{ij}-s_it_j-t_is_j\). Since \(H\) is invertible, the localized ideal of all three-by-three minors is **exactly** the ideal generated by the entries of this Schur complement; this follows either from elementary invertible row-column operations or the determinantal Fitting ideal identity for a two-by-two invertible minor. The zero diagonal of \(A\) makes the diagonal equations \(-2s_it_i=0\). As two is a unit, eliminating all remaining \(a_{ij}\) gives an explicit *scheme* isomorphism
\[
 X_n\cap D_+(a_{pq})\simeq
 \operatorname{Spec}k[s_i,t_i:i\notin\{p,q\}]/(s_it_i:i\notin\{p,q\}).\tag{1}
\]
The charts cover \(X_n\), because the only hollow matrix with all off-diagonal entries zero is zero. Each chart is a reduced complete intersection of dimension \(n-2\). The presentation proves reducedness, equidimensionality and the local-complete-intersection property for the whole scheme.

**2. Global Segre components.** On chart (1) each irreducible branch is determined by selecting one of \(s_i=0\) or \(t_i=0\) for each index other than \(p,q\). Set \(p\in S\), \(q\in T\); the choice of vanished variables uniquely gives a bipartition \(S\sqcup T=[n]\). Then \(a_{ij}=0\) for pairs entirely in \(S\) or in \(T\), and every crossing coordinate is the product of one parameter on \(S\) and one on \(T\); the coordinate \(a_{pq}=1\) normalizes those products on this chart. Conversely every rank-one cross-matrix produces such a point; its projective closure is the Segre \(Z_{S,T}\). Thus all global components are these Segres. Their support patterns are distinct (a generic point has every crossing coordinate nonzero), and no such component contains another, since all have dimension \((|S|-1)+(|T|-1)=n-2\). Unordered nontrivial bipartitions number \((2^n-2)/2=2^{n-1}-1\). Every Segre is smooth and normal; hence their disjoint union is the normalization of the reduced scheme.

**3. Exact local singularity and singular components.** At a point of chart (1), \(s_i=t_i=0\) if and only if row \(i\) is identically zero (the forward implication follows from the eliminated formulas). For a nonzero pair \((s_i,t_i)\), exactly one member is nonzero because \(s_it_i=0\); consequently the hypersurface equation \(s_it_i=0\) becomes smooth near that pair and one variable can be eliminated. Each zero pair remains a transverse nodal factor. With \(z\) zero pairs, completion yields the asserted \(z\)-fold product of nodes times a smooth formal factor of dimension \(n-2-z\). The Jacobian criterion for this complete intersection (or the tangent-dimension comparison) gives regularity precisely when \(z=0\), and a generic point with \(z=1\) has the transverse double crossing \(st=0\). The zero-row condition is coordinate independent, so \(\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is the union over rows \(w\) of their zero loci inside \(X_n\). Such a zero-row locus is the image of the corresponding rank-two hollow symmetric determinantal scheme on the remaining \(n-1\) indices. Applying Step 2 on those indices gives exactly the Segre subvarieties \(Z_{w;S,T}\). They have dimension \(n-3\). Their generic matrices have exactly one zero row (all crossing parameters are nonzero), so each \(w\) is uniquely determined; their generic crossing support uniquely determines \(\{S,T\}\). Therefore all \(n(2^{n-2}-1)\) are distinct and maximal, and no other singular components exist.

**4. Projective degrees.** A Segre \(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\) has degree \(\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}\) because its hyperplane class is the sum of the hyperplane classes of the two factors. Since the components are distinct and have the same dimension, their degrees add. Summing over nonempty unordered bipartitions, the Segre degree of the component having \(|S|=a\) is \(\binom{n-2}{a-1}\), and Vandermonde's identity gives
\[
2\deg X_n=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\binom na\binom{n-2}{a-1}
=\binom{2n-2}{n-1}.
\]
Apply the same argument on each of the \(n\) choices of a zero row \(w\), with \(n\) replaced by \(n-1\), to get \(2\deg\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}=n\binom{2n-4}{n-2}\). This completes the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses exact symbolic arithmetic to test the hyperbolic-pivot Schur-complement polynomial identities. For \(3\le n\le8\) it exhaustively constructs bipartitions and checks component numbers, distinct supports, the two Vandermonde degree identities and how row-vanishing Segre supports lie on two ambient components. Independently, it enumerates all nonzero hollow symmetric matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\) for sizes \(n=3,4,5\) and checks that every rank-two example has a complete-bipartite rank-one support and satisfies the stated pivot identities. Run from a directory containing this file: `python verify.py`. The script uses Python 3 and SymPy. Finite-field enumeration and symbolic samples are **not** proofs for arbitrary \(n\); the all-size proof is Steps 1–4 above.

## Relationship to prior work

Segre defining equations and ideals are classical; Bernardi (2007/2008) supplies a broader Segre–Veronese treatment. Those presentations explain each component but do not specify the union, its singular scheme support or either degree formula in this hollow rank-two section. Conca–Welker (2018/2019) study graph-prescribed off-diagonal zeros in symmetric generic matrices with generic diagonal variables, a different coordinate section; their radicality/prime criteria do not directly identify the all-diagonal-zero rank-two scheme as a union of the stated Segres. Cai–Lee–Yu (2024/2025) study the tropicalization of the unrestricted symmetric rank-two variety, not this projective hollow section. Recent work on off-diagonal Gram maps studies arithmetic and singularity thresholds for a related coordinate projection, without directly asserting the present projective component/singularity/degree classification. The argument here is elementary and self-contained. An equivalent specialized statement could nevertheless be implicit in older determinantal or graph-matrix literature.

## Limitations

No claim is made about characteristic two, the nonreduced Jacobian ideal defining the singular scheme, or a minimal graded free resolution. The local equations describe the ordinary projective determinantal scheme formed with **all** three-by-three minors; they are not asserted for the ideal generated only by principal minors. The originality comparison is bounded and does not certify absolute novelty; the theorem can be checked independently using the supplied proof.
