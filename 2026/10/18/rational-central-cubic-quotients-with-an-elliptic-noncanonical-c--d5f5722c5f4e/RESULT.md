# Rational central cubic quotients with an elliptic noncanonical curve and four middle Betti classes

## Finding

Let \(F(u,v)\) be a separable homogeneous cubic and let \(G(x,y,z)\) be a homogeneous cubic whose zero locus is a smooth plane cubic over \(\mathbb C\). Set

\[
X=\{F(u,v)+G(x,y,z)=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4_{[u:v:x:y:z]},\qquad
Z=\langle\sigma\rangle\cong\mu_3,
\]

where \(\sigma[u:v:x:y:z]=[u:v:\zeta x:\zeta y:\zeta z]\) and \(\zeta\) is a primitive cube root of unity. Write \(Y=X/Z\). Then:

1. \(Y\) is rational, normal and \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial, with klt singularities and ample \(-K_Y\). Its anticanonical volume is \(\boxed{(-K_Y)^3=8}\).
2. The singular locus has exactly two kinds of connected components: the plane elliptic curve \(E=\{u=v=G(x,y,z)=0\}\) and three isolated points above the distinct roots of \(F\) on \(\mathbb P^1\). In analytic local coordinates, the singularity is \(\mathbb A^1\times(\mathbb A^2/\frac13(1,1))\) along \(E\) and \(\mathbb A^3/\frac13(1,1,1)\) at each isolated point.
3. The canonical divisor has local Cartier index \(3\) along \(E\) and index \(1\) at each isolated point. The curve singularities are noncanonical; the isolated singularities are Gorenstein canonical but not terminal.
4. The rational cohomology, including its pure weight-three Hodge structure, satisfies

\[
\boxed{P_Y(t)=1+t^2+4t^3+t^4+t^6,\qquad h^{2,1}(H^3(Y))=h^{1,2}(H^3(Y))=2.}
\]

This uniform statement includes the central order-three action on the Fermat cubic threefold singled out in [1, Theorem 5.1].

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). The binary discriminant \(\operatorname{disc}(F)\) is nonzero, and \(G=0\) is a smooth plane cubic. There are no restrictions on the \(j\)-invariant of that plane cubic. The conclusions concern the coarse projective quotient \(Y=X/Z\), not the full quotient by the larger nonabelian group appearing in [1]. All cohomology coefficients are rational unless explicitly stated otherwise; no claim is made about integral torsion or a crepant smooth resolution.

## Proof

**Smoothness and fixed locus.** The gradient of \(F+G\) splits into derivatives of \(F\) and derivatives of \(G\). A simultaneous projective zero of the derivatives would yield a singular point of the binary divisor or of the smooth plane cubic, contradicting the hypotheses. Hence \(X\) is smooth. The nontrivial elements of \(Z\) have two eigenspaces in the ambient vector space: \(\langle u,v\rangle\) and \(\langle x,y,z\rangle\). Their intersection with \(X\) consists exactly of three reduced points \(p_1,p_2,p_3\) cut out by \(F=0\) on the first projective line, together with the smooth cubic \(E\) cut out by \(G=0\) on the second projective plane. Both nonidentity group elements have this same fixed set. Away from it the action is free.

**Rationality.** On the dense open where both coordinate blocks are nonzero, projection to the blocks induces a rational map

\[
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1_{[u:v]}\times\mathbb P^2_{[x:y:z]}.
\]

Fix general representatives of a pair of projected points. The relative scale \(\lambda\) of the blocks obeys \(F(u,v)+\lambda^3G(x,y,z)=0\). Its three distinct solutions are precisely one \(Z\)-orbit. Thus the induced dominant map \(Y\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\) is birational. Equivalently, in coordinates \(a=u/v\), \(b=y/x\), \(c=z/x\), \(w=x/v\), the equation is \(F(a,1)+w^3G(1,b,c)=0\), and \(Z\) fixes \(a,b,c\) while multiplying \(w\) by \(\zeta\). Thus \(\mathbb C(Y)=\mathbb C(a,b,c)\).

**Analytic singularities.** At any point of \(E\), take an affine chart with \(x\ne0\). The two normal coordinates \(u/x,v/x\) have \(Z\)-character \(\zeta^{-1}\); a tangent parameter on \(E\) is invariant. By the equivariant holomorphic implicit-function theorem, the quotient germ is the product of a smooth one-dimensional germ with the cyclic surface quotient \(\frac13(1,1)\). At any \(p_i\), the condition that \(F\) have three distinct projective roots lets one solve a binary coordinate in terms of \(x,y,z\); these three tangent parameters all have character \(\zeta\). The quotient germ is therefore \(\frac13(1,1,1)\). Neither representation is generated by pseudoreflections, so all these quotient points are singular. Elsewhere the action is free and the quotient smooth.

**Canonical data.** Since the fixed locus has codimension at least two, \(\pi:X\to Y\) is finite of degree \(3\) and étale in codimension one; hence \(K_X=\pi^*K_Y\) as rational Cartier divisors. Adjunction gives \(-K_X=2H\), where \(H\) is the hyperplane class and \(H^3=3\). Finite descent of ampleness implies \(-K_Y\) is ample, and projection gives

\[
(-K_Y)^3=\frac{(-K_X)^3}{3}=\frac{8\cdot3}{3}=8.
\]

Finite quotient singularities of smooth complex varieties are klt and locally \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. The determinant characters on the three local tangent representations are \(\zeta^{-2}\) along \(E\) and \(\zeta^3=1\) at the isolated points; these give canonical indices \(3\) and \(1\), respectively. The Reid–Tai ages for nontrivial stabilizer elements are \(\frac23,\frac43\) on the surface normal slice and \(1,2\) at the isolated points. Thus the former is noncanonical, while the latter is canonical but not terminal.

**Rational Betti and Hodge numbers.** The smooth cubic threefold has Betti numbers \(b_0=b_2=b_4=b_6=1\), \(b_3=10\), with all others zero. For a finite group action, the rational cohomology of its coarse quotient is the invariant summand of the covering variety: \(H^i(Y,\mathbb Q)\cong H^i(X,\mathbb Q)^Z\). The hyperplane class generates the even middle cohomology and is invariant. For each nontrivial \(g\in Z\), the fixed-point Euler characteristic is \(\chi(X^g)=\chi(E)+3=3\). The topological Lefschetz formula therefore yields

\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H^3(X,\mathbb Q))=4-3=1,
\qquad b_3(Y)=\frac{10+1+1}{3}=4.
\]

This determines the displayed Betti polynomial. Rational cohomology of the finite projective quotient carries the invariant pure Hodge structures of the smooth covering, so the weight-three Hodge numbers follow from \(h^{3,0}(X)=0\) and Hodge symmetry: \(h^{2,1}(Y)=h^{1,2}(Y)=2\). Independently, the residue/Jacobian-ring model \(H^{2,1}(X)\cong R_1\) has invariant degree-one monomials exactly \(u,v\); the ambient determinant character is \(\zeta^3=1\), yielding the same dimension \(2\).

## Verification

The standalone `CHECK.py` verifies the weight decomposition of the Fermat specialization, the two local determinant characters, the fixed-locus Lefschetz trace, the invariant middle Betti dimension, and the anticanonical intersection arithmetic. It is a finite symbolic sanity check; the proof above applies to every pair \((F,G)\) satisfying the stated smoothness assumptions and does not rely on a finite sample.

## Relationship to prior work

Ballard, Duncan and Zhang [1, Theorem 5.1] explicitly identify the central order-three subgroup of an exceptional action on the Fermat cubic threefold, its fixed elliptic curve, its three isolated fixed points, and the failure of equivariant unirationality for the larger group. Those fixed components are initiating input rather than newly claimed observations. Their theorem does not state rationality, singularity types, canonical indices, anticanonical volume, or the rational Betti/Hodge profile of the central quotient.

Cheltsov, Tschinkel and Zhang [2] identify the exceptional group action in their broader equivariant-unirationality classification. The present statement instead concerns the coarse quotient by its central cyclic subgroup, and extends uniformly to arbitrary smooth split cubics \(F+G\). Classical cyclic-quotient and Lefschetz formulas are used as tools, not claimed as novel theorems.

## Limitations

The quotient is singular and its rational Betti numbers should not be conflated with the cohomology of a resolution. The formula for \(H^3(Y)\) does not determine integral cohomology, torsion, its intermediate Jacobian, or a minimal model. Noncanonicity along \(E\) rules out treating this quotient as a terminal Fano variety. An equivalent evaluation could be implicit in general equivariant or quotient-invariant literature; the mathematical claim is a specific uniform determination rather than a new general transfer theorem.
