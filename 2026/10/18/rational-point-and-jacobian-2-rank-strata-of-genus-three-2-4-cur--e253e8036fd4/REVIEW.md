# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain has exactly \(2^{15}-1\) nonzero coefficient triples. The two-chart Jacobian criterion is a necessary and sufficient geometric smoothness test, including all nonrational singularities. All triples were enumerated and the \(36\)-element coordinate group was explicitly exhausted. On representatives of **all** coordinate orbits, Gröbner-basis checks independently agreed with the Jacobian-polynomial test; finite-extension point counts through degree three independently verified the \(2\)-rank on every smooth representative through its zeta polynomial. The computation's output is the exact embedded joint census, not a probabilistic estimate. See `RESULT.md`, `verify.py`, `crosscheck.py`, and `data/joint_table.json`.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority risk
The full-text classification by Nart–Sadornil (2004) is of abstract genus-three hyperelliptic curves over characteristic-two fields, with Artin–Schreier ramification types and automorphism groups. Its known results, including the branch types, are not claimed as new. Booher–Pries (2020) explicitly discusses the known Deuring–Shafarevich \(p\)-rank relation, likewise used as prior theory. Neither source states the complete distribution of bidegree-\((2,4)\) **embedded** divisors, simultaneous rational-point and Jacobian \(2\)-rank strata, or their ruling-preserving orbit multiplicities. Nearby geometric comparison problems in genus four and genus six concern different bidegrees and do not imply this table. Targeted mathematical literature searches for bidegree, rank, point counts, numerical totals, and equivalent descriptions did not identify coverage. Priority remains uncertain because an earlier abstract classification could in principle be specialized and combined with separate degree-four-pencil enumeration, or an unindexed census might exist.

## Value — PASS
A smooth \((2,4)\) divisor is a genus-three hyperelliptic curve equipped with its hyperelliptic ruling and a second degree-four ruling map, a natural marked-embedding problem rather than an arbitrary polynomial slice. The \(17\)-cell joint distribution and \(294\) embedded orbits determine an arithmetic refinement not encoded in a bare rational-point bound or abstract curve-count formula. Particularly, the \(1{,}083\) six-point equations have precisely two distinct Jacobian \(2\)-rank possibilities, with \(282/801\) multiplicities and \(11/26\) orbit classes; this is a natural boundary of the rational fiber-splitting condition. The six-point upper bound and the Deuring–Shafarevich formula themselves have not been treated as contributions.
