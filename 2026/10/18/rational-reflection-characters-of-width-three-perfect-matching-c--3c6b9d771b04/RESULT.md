# Rational reflection characters of width-three perfect-matching complexes

## Finding
For \(n\in\{2,4,6,8\}\), let \(G_n=P_3\square P_n\) be the \(3\)-row grid and let \(K_n=\mathcal M_p(G_n)\) be the simplicial complex **generated by its perfect matchings**: its vertices are grid edges, and its faces are exactly the subsets of perfect matchings. Thus \(K_n\) is generally a proper subcomplex of the ordinary matching complex.

Let \(h(r,c)=(2-r,c)\) and \(v(r,c)=(r,n-1-c)\) be the row and column reflections. These act simplicially on \(K_n\) and generate the Klein four-group. The complete nonequivariant homotopy classifications are
\[
K_2\simeq\mathrm{pt},\qquad K_4\simeq S^1,\qquad K_6\simeq S^2,\qquad K_8\simeq S^3\vee S^3.
\]
For \(n=4,6,8\), the unique nonzero reduced rational homology occurs in degree \(d=n/2-1\). With the elements ordered \((1,h,v,hv)\), its rational character is
\[
\chi_4=(1,-1,1,-1),\qquad
\chi_6=(1,-1,-1,1),\qquad
\chi_8=(2,0,0,2).
\]
For \(n=2\), the reduced character is zero. In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(K_8;\mathbb Q)\) splits into a trivial representation and the one-dimensional representation on which both \(h\) and \(v\) act by \(-1\). Neither reflection alone acts trivially, while their product does.

## Assumptions and scope
The grid has vertices \((r,c)\) for \(0\le r<3\) and \(0\le c<n\). Edges join vertices at Manhattan distance one. Its \(5n-3\) edges are ordered first by vertical edges \(((r,c),(r+1,c))\) in increasing row and column, then by horizontal edges \(((r,c),(r,c+1))\), likewise. Face bitmasks refer to this ordering, counting bits from zero. A perfect matching covers all \(3n\) grid vertices exactly once. All results here are for the **four specified even widths only**; no recurrence or all-width homotopy classification is asserted. Rational characters are traces on reduced singular/simplicial homology and are not equivariant homotopy types.

## Proof
**(1) Complete facet enumeration.** Recursively choose the least uncovered vertex and pair it along every available incident edge. Branching visits every perfect matching exactly once because the partner of the least uncovered vertex is uniquely specified by each perfect matching. The numbers of facets for widths \(2,4,6,8\), respectively, are \(3,11,41,153\). An independent three-bit column-transfer count produces the same four numbers. Enumerating all submasks of these facet edge-bitmasks gives augmented face counts \(20,495,12375,309480\), including the empty face. Every face occurs by definition in a perfect matching, so enumeration is exhaustive.

**(2) Explicit discrete-Morse matching.** Start with all augmented faces unpaired. For each still-available edge-bit position \(e\), list all unpaired faces \(F\) not containing \(e\) for which \(F\cup\{e\}\) is unpaired. Choose an \(e\) with the largest list (smallest \(e\) breaks ties), pair **every** face in that list with its upper face, and remove both from consideration. Repeat until every such list is empty. Because the chosen coordinate is fixed during each stage, all pairs are disjoint and form codimension-one incidences. The resulting \((e,\text{number of pairs})\) stages are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 n&\text{stages}\\\hline
2&(4,7),(6,3)\\
4&(8,164),(14,83)\\
6&(12,4089),(22,2088),(16,7),(26,3)\\
8&(16,102249),(30,52233),(22,171),(36,86).
\end{array}
\]
The respective unpaired augmented face sets have \(0,1,1,2\) elements; the nonempty critical face bitmasks are \(20480\) for width \(4\), \(5505024\) for width \(6\), and \(1426063360,70078431232\) for width \(8\). Their dimensions are respectively \(1,2,3,3\).

**(3) Exhaustive acyclicity certificate.** For every codimension-one face relation orient the Hasse edge from the larger face to its facet, except reverse a matching edge. The checker independently computes the incoming edges, traverses outgoing edges, and performs Kahn's topological sort. It processes all \(20,495,12375,309480\) faces, respectively; the total Hasse incidence counts are \(34,1679,62994,2101259\). Therefore all four directed Hasse graphs are acyclic. In each augmented matching, the empty face is matched with a vertex. Removing the empty face leaves an acyclic ordinary matching with one critical vertex and, respectively, \(0,1,1,2\) critical cells in dimensions \(1,2,3\). By discrete Morse theory, these give the asserted contractible or sphere-wedge homotopy types. The attaching maps of higher-dimensional cells to the single vertex are constant; there are no cells in intermediate dimensions.

**(4) Signed fixed-simplex trace.** For \(g\in\{1,h,v,hv\}\), let \(p_g\) be its explicit edge-ID permutation. For every nonempty face \(F\) fixed setwise by \(g\), sort its edge IDs, apply \(p_g\), and compute the inversion parity of the resulting ordered list. If \(s_g(F)\in\{1,-1\}\) is this orientation sign, the reduced Hopf trace formula gives
\[
\widetilde\Lambda(g;K_n)=-1+\sum_{\substack{F\in K_n,\ F\ne\varnothing\\gF=F}}(-1)^{|F|-1}s_g(F).
\]
The \(-1\) subtracts the empty augmented face. Direct exhaustive enumeration gives, in group order \((1,h,v,hv)\), the counts of fixed nonempty faces and corresponding reduced Lefschetz numbers:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
n&\#\text{ fixed nonempty faces}&\widetilde\Lambda\\\hline
2&(19,3,11,3)&(0,0,0,0)\\
4&(494,16,42,20)&(-1,1,-1,1)\\
6&(12374,72,286,80)&(1,-1,-1,1)\\
8&(309479,313,1097,515)&(-2,0,0,-2).
\end{array}
\]
By part (3), for each \(n=4,6,8\), \(\widetilde\Lambda(g;K_n)=(-1)^d\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_d(K_n;\mathbb Q))\). Multiplying the displayed vectors by \((-1)^d\) yields the stated characters. Fourier inversion of these four values gives nonnegative integer multiplicities of all four rational one-dimensional characters. At width \(8\) the multiplicities, ordered by eigenvalue signs \((++,-+,+-,--)\), are \((1,0,0,1)\), proving the claimed decomposition.

## Verification
Two plain-Python programs in `verification/` exhaustively reconstruct the four complexes. `verify_three_row_perfect_matching.py` implements the independent tiling-transfer count, all subfaces, all matching stages, the complete directed-Hasse acyclicity proof, and direct reduced mod-two boundary ranks for widths at most \(6\). `verify_reflection_characters.py` builds the four actual grid symmetries, sums every signed invariant nonempty simplex and the reduced empty contribution, checks the characters, and computes Klein-four irreducible multiplicities. Both scripts use only the Python standard library and reside side by side; execute them as `python verification/verify_three_row_perfect_matching.py` and `python verification/verify_reflection_characters.py`. Expected terminal messages include `VERIFICATION_OK four exhaustive grid cases` and `VERIFICATION_OK 16 exact signed fixed-simplex sums`.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan–Sharma–Shukla (first publicly posted September 23, 2026, arXiv:2609.27366v1) studies the **ordinary** matching complexes of three-row rectangular grids, proving connectivity and reduced-homology statements for all widths. The character calculated here belongs to the different **perfect-matching-generated subcomplex** and is not a consequence of that homological concentration. Chandrakar–Singh (first posted July 8, 2024, arXiv:2407.05809) proves sphere-wedge classifications for polygonal line tilings, including two-row grids, and conjectures sphere-wedge topology for arbitrary rectangular-grid perfect-matching complexes. Its broader assertion is a conjecture, not a theorem that settles these examples, and it does not prescribe reflection characters. Other work on honeycomb perfect-matching complexes treats different graphs. Recent representation-theoretic work on matching-complex homology of complete hypergraphs likewise concerns different complexes. The present exact reflection characters are extra structure beyond nonequivariant homotopy types.

## Limitations
All assertions are **finite, exhaustive** calculations for exactly four widths, backed by an explicit inspectable certificate algorithm; the scripts do not establish any analogous assertion for wider boards. The decomposition is rational and does not determine an integral lattice representation, equivariant homotopy type, or quotient topology. A published-results comparison is not proof of priority: an unindexed, inaccessible, or unpublished equivalent computation may exist. The 2024 rectangular-grid conjecture is an earlier motivation for the nonequivariant aspect; the current contribution is distinguished by the explicitly computed reflection representations in the three-row setting.
