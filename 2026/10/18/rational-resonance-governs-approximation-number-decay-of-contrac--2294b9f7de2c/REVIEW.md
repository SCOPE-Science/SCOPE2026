# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every allowed coefficient Hilbert space admits the same exact diagonal singular-value representation. The rational-equivalence partition, compact-orbit Haar argument, componentwise analytic zero-set theorem, and logarithmic counting squeeze prove the infinite-index limit. Fourier inversion and a uniform phase-Lipschitz bound settle the realization and discontinuity claims. The zero case is explicitly excluded except in the two-symbol classification; no finite experiment is used as the asymptotic proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Li–Wang–Zhao (arXiv:2609.28111v1) characterize compactness of finite composition sums, but the source does not state an exponential approximation-number law. Bayart–Gilmore–Şahin (arXiv:2509.19797v1) give different approximation estimates for boundary-contact symbol differences. Li–Queffélec–Rodríguez-Piazza (arXiv:1407.2171v1) treat a single composition operator, which does not control the signed cancellation exhibited here. Searches against available mathematical records found no implication dominating the finite-mixture classification; priority remains subject to wider verification, especially exponential-polynomial density literature.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The rate is a natural quantitative invariant for compact operator mixtures and has a complete, nontrivial rational spectrum. The construction exhibits dense rate discontinuities along a norm-continuous family and is insensitive to analytic coefficient-weight choices. The arithmetic classification and inverse realization are mathematical information beyond the known compactness of the individual components.

**Limitations.** Equal-modulus contracting linear symbols, finite mixtures and Hilbert orthogonal monomial bases only. The general counting remainder is little-o; precise prefactors, nonlinear symbols and unequal moduli are not addressed. Numerical examples offer diagnostics, not proof. Earlier priority under alternative terminology has not been ruled out.
