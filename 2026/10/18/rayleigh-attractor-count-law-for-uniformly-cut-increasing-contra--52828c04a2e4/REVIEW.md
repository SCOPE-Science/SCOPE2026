# Scientific review: Rayleigh attractor-count law for uniformly cut increasing contractions

## Correctness — PASS

For the new map all jumps are positive and all branch slopes lie in (0,1); strict monotonicity rules out longer cycles. Away from finitely many threshold coincidences every orbit converges to an attracting branch fixed point. Exactly i-1 cuts below u_i characterizes the i-th fixed point. Exact multinomial block counts yield the factorial-moment coefficient formula; uniform Stirling bounds on all positive compositions and integrable Dirichlet limits give every normalized moment 2^(k/2)Gamma(1+k/2). Rayleigh moment determinacy, Stirling saturation asymptotics and exact n<=8 exhaustive occupancy verification complete the checks. Finite checks are supporting only.

A uniform cut is an order statistic of independent samples, not an independently randomized cut at each iteration. The exact finite checks exhaust all occupancy vectors through eight branches; the infinite Rayleigh theorem follows from the proved Dirichlet factorial-moment limit. All \(0<\lambda<1\) are covered without a uniform dynamical rate claim.

## Originality — PASS (priority remains provisional)

The inspected 2012, 2013 and 2014 primary papers yield deterministic upper bounds or generic convergence, not a random-cut, growing-n count distribution. A classical Brownian bridge local-time Rayleigh distribution is prior background. Actual published-finding corpus/own-ledger/text searches did not reveal the combined explicit finite all-factorial-moment law and dynamical Rayleigh limit. A general bridge local-time theorem could imply the asymptotic component under an unverified reduction, so absolute priority is not asserted.

Closest literature: Nogueira and Pires (2012), Theorem 1.1 and Appendix A; Nogueira–Pires–Rosales (2013), Theorem 1.1; Nogueira–Pires–Rosales (2014), Theorems 1.1 and 5.1; and Pitman's 1998 Brownian-bridge-local-time report (abstract inspected). A general stochastic local-time theorem may already explain the Rayleigh limiting factor, while the exact finite dynamical moment law and saturation event are the specific contribution.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This natural uniform-cut parameter ensemble tests the sharp n-orbit bound from established interval-contraction dynamics. The exact all-moment distribution proves typical order sqrt(n), a nondegenerate Rayleigh fluctuation law, and exponential rarity of saturation, furnishing quantitative information absent from the upper bound and qualitative generic periodicity alone.

The work does not claim new general Rayleigh probability theory, a law for arbitrary branch fixed points, or a law for basin sizes.
