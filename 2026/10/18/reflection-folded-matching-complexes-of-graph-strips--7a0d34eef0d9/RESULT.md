# Reflection-folded matching complexes of graph strips

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite simple graph with at least one edge, let \(k\geq2\), and let \(P_j\) denote the path on vertices \(0,\ldots,j-1\). Put \(H_k=G\square P_k\). For \(m\ge2\), let \(\rho_m(v,i)=(v,m-1-i)\), a graph automorphism of \(G\square P_m\). The induced involution on the ordinary matching complex \(\mathcal M(G\square P_m)\) has the following geometric fixed-point spaces:

\[
\boxed{\big|\mathcal M(G\square P_{2k+1})\big|^{\langle\rho_{2k+1}\rangle}\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ \big|\mathcal M(H_k)*\mathcal M(G)\big|,}
\]
\[
\boxed{\big|\mathcal M(G\square P_{2k})\big|^{\langle\rho_{2k}\rangle}\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ \big|\mathcal M(W_{\partial}H_k)\big|.}
\]
Here \(W_{\partial}H_k\) is the **boundary-whiskered half-strip** obtained from \(H_k\) by adjoining, for each \(v\in V\), one leaf adjacent solely to the terminal-layer vertex \((v,k-1)\). The homeomorphisms are natural and \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-equivariant (for the diagonal base-graph action on all layers and the two factors of the join).

A homotopy consequence is uniform in both the number of rows and the width of a rectangular grid: for every \(n\ge2\) and \(k\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{\big|\mathcal M(P_n\square P_{2k+1})\big|^{\langle\rho_{2k+1}\rangle}\simeq
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{pt},&n\equiv2\pmod3,\\
\Sigma^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}\big|\mathcal M(P_n\square P_k)\big|,&n\equiv0,1\pmod3.
\end{cases}}
\]
In particular the odd-height reflection-fixed space is contractible for **all heights** \(2k+1\ge5\) whenever \(n\equiv2\pmod3\), regardless of the possibly unknown nonequivariant homotopy type of the large-grid matching complex.

## Assumptions and scope
A matching is a set of distinct graph edges with pairwise disjoint endpoints. The ordinary matching complex \(\mathcal M(F)\) has vertices \(E(F)\) and faces all matchings (including the empty face). For a simplicial involution the notation \(|K|^{\langle\rho\rangle}\) means the **geometric fixed-point space**, not the simplicial subcomplex of fixed vertices. The Cartesian product here is the standard graph Cartesian product, not the categorical/tensor product. The graph \(G\) need not be connected, bipartite, regular, or without isolated vertices, but must contain an edge so that the displayed geometric realizations are nonempty. The restriction \(k\ge2\) marks the higher-layer domain of this finding: the boundary cases \(k=1\), corresponding to two and three layers, have already been analyzed in earlier related work and are used only as consistency checks.

All assertions are over ordinary finite simplicial complexes and hold integrally at the level of homotopy and PL topology; no choice of homology coefficients is required. The second formula is a reduction to a smaller graph matching complex, not a claim of a universal closed-form wedge-of-spheres type for that smaller complex.

## Proof
**1. General orbit-vertex model for geometric fixed points.** Let a simplicial involution \(\tau\) act on a finite complex \(K\). Any geometric point in a simplex has unique barycentric coordinates on its support. A fixed point must have \(\tau\)-stable support, and its coordinate is constant on each orbit of vertices in that support. Accordingly, form a new simplicial complex \(K_{\tau}\) whose vertices are vertex-orbits \(O\) such that \(O\) is itself a face of \(K\), with a collection of orbits a face precisely when their union is a face of \(K\). Map each orbit vertex to the barycenter \(|O|^{-1}\sum_{x\in O}x\). On every orbit simplex this extends affinely and bijectively onto the fixed part of the corresponding invariant simplex, and these maps agree on intersections. Thus \(|K_{\tau}|\) is canonically PL homeomorphic to \(|K|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\). For a matching complex, a vertex-orbit qualifies exactly when the edges in that orbit form a matching, and a set of orbits qualifies exactly when their union is a matching. Because pairwise disjointness characterizes matchings, the orbit complex is a flag complex on these qualifying orbits with adjacency given by disjoint endpoint sets.

**2. Odd-layer strip.** Take \(m=2k+1\). Every edge of \(G\square P_m\) either lies in a copy of \(G\) within a layer or joins two consecutive layers at the same base vertex. Edges lying entirely in the first \(k\) layers form the half-strip \(H_k\). Every such edge is paired by \(\rho_m\) with a disjoint edge in the last \(k\) layers. Edges within the central layer \(i=k\) are singleton orbits. The remaining edges cross between layer \(k-1\) and the center, or between the center and layer \(k+1\); each corresponding two-edge orbit has both edges incident to the same central-layer vertex, so it cannot occur in a matching. There are no other orbits. A set of permissible off-center orbits is a matching exactly when its first-half representatives are a matching of \(H_k\). A set of central singleton orbits is a matching exactly when it represents a matching of \(G\). Off-center and central orbits never share graph endpoints, so they are freely compatible. Consequently the orbit complex is **exactly** the simplicial join \(\mathcal M(H_k)*\mathcal M(G)\), establishing the first PL homeomorphism.

**3. Even-layer strip.** Take \(m=2k\). Every edge entirely in the first \(k\) layers belongs to a disjoint two-edge orbit paired with its reflected copy in the last \(k\) layers; these orbit vertices are naturally the edges of \(H_k\). The only other permissible orbits are the singleton edges crossing the central seam: for each \(v\in V\), the edge joining \((v,k-1)\) to \((v,k)\) is setwise fixed by \(\rho_m\). The seam edges are mutually disjoint. Such a seam edge conflicts with an off-center orbit precisely when the corresponding half-strip edge is incident to \((v,k-1)\). Hence replacing each seam edge by a new pendant edge at the terminal vertex \((v,k-1)\) gives **exactly** the same vertex and pairwise-conflict structure. The complete orbit complex is therefore \(\mathcal M(W_{\partial}H_k)\). No crossing edge is omitted and no higher compatibility condition is required beyond pairwise disjoint endpoints. This establishes the second PL homeomorphism.

**4. Naturality.** Every automorphism of \(G\) acting identically in each layer commutes with \(\rho_m\) and carries the lower-half, center-layer, and seam-edge orbit types to the same respective types. Both orbit identifications commute with this action, proving \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-equivariance.

**5. Grid corollary and the periodic contractibility boundary.** For \(G=P_n\), its matching complex is the independence complex of its line graph \(P_{n-1}\): \(\mathcal M(P_n)=\operatorname{Ind}(P_{n-1})\). The path independence complexes obey \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_j)\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(P_{j-3})\) for \(j\ge4\). Indeed, decomposing at vertex \(2\) gives a union of two contractible subcomplexes, namely the deletion of vertex \(2\) (a cone at vertex \(1\)) and the star of vertex \(2\), whose intersection is \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{j-3})\); the union has homotopy type its suspension. Initial cases \(j=1,2,3\) are respectively contractible, \(S^0\), \(S^0\). It follows that \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_{n-1})\) is contractible for \(n\equiv2\pmod3\), and otherwise is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor-1}\). Since the join with a nonempty contractible complex is contractible and \(X*S^d\simeq\Sigma^{d+1}X\), applying the first PL homeomorphism proves the displayed homotopy formula. All path and suspension boundary cases, including \(n=2\) and \(n=3\), are covered.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_folded_reflection.py` independently constructs the original strip graph, lists complete edge orbits under layer reversal, rejects every orbit whose members share endpoints, and tests the entire pairwise compatibility graph against the independently constructed half-strip/whisker or join model. It checks all \(71\) nonempty-edge labeled base graphs on \(2\), \(3\), or \(4\) vertices and depths \(k=1,2,3\) in both parities: **426 complete flag-complex isomorphism tests**, covering **5,550 permissible orbit vertices** in aggregate. It separately exhausts all orbit faces in **16** small even/odd examples and compares full face vectors.

The independent program `verify_path_period.py` builds the path independence chain complexes and computes every reduced mod-two Betti number at widths \(2\le n\le14\); the 13 results agree with the path mod-three classification. Both scripts require only the Python 3 standard library. Execute `python3 verify_folded_reflection.py` and `python3 verify_path_period.py`; the exact captured outputs are included as `verification_fold_output.txt` and `verification_path_output.txt`. Finite checks are **not** substitutes for the all-graph proof in steps 1–5, and mod-two Betti numbers alone do not establish integral homotopy types.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan, Sharma and Shukla study nonequivariant connectivity and homology of the three-row Cartesian grid matching complex in arXiv:2609.27366 (first public September 23, 2026). Their accessible abstract establishes homological results, not the higher-layer reflection-folding theorem stated here; their full manuscript was not accessible for complete comparison. Matsushita's 2019 work establishes wedge-of-spheres topology for small-width ordinary grid matching complexes, not arbitrary-graph reflection-fixed spaces. A published calculation titled *Orthogonal-reflection Lefschetz formulas for \(3\times n\) grid matching complexes* determines the reduced Lefschetz number of the three-row row reflection by splitting invariant matchings into two paths; that argument already implies the **three-row path special case** of the odd fold when coupled with the standard orbit-barycenter description. Neither that covered special case nor the earlier two-layer fully-whiskered prism reduction is claimed as new. The contribution is the **uniform exact geometric fixed-space reduction for every deeper reflection fold \(k\ge2\), in both parities and for arbitrary base graphs**, with its all-odd-height periodic contractibility consequence.

## Limitations
The work does not determine the nonequivariant homotopy type of \(\mathcal M(G\square P_m)\), nor the homotopy type of \(\mathcal M(W_{\partial}H_k)\) for every graph. Its geometric fixed spaces are not quotients, homotopy fixed points, or invariants of the perfect-matching subcomplex. The general orbit-barycenter lemma, the path independence suspension recurrence, and the two- and three-layer boundary cases are standard or previously recorded and are not originality claims. The complete 23-page arXiv manuscript of the closest 2026 grid paper was unavailable through the accessible routes; a hidden general lemma in that or another unindexed source remains a bibliographic risk. The full proof here is elementary and self-contained; no independent scientific audit has been performed.
