# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim uses the standard closed-neighborhood power-domination step followed by one-unobserved-neighbor forcing. For a nonempty selected set of edges of a complete multipartite graph, the initially unseen line-graph vertices are exactly the original edges whose endpoints are both unused. All unused original vertices have an initially observed cross-edge to the used endpoint set, because a selected edge meets at least two parts. Every cycle of unused edges is a permanent obstruction: a first observed cycle edge would need to be forced while its source has two as-yet-unobserved neighboring cycle edges. Conversely, observed cross-edges permit repeated leaf-edge forcing in every forest. These arguments are exhaustive and include empty residual forests, isolated unused vertices and singleton parts. The unused-vertex part profile is acyclic exactly when at most one part is occupied, or two are occupied with one count one. For a fixed unused set, inclusion–exclusion counts chosen edges with no isolated used endpoint, with no overcounting because the unused set is unique. Matching/wedge enumeration proves the two minimum-count refinements and handles their boundaries. A separate direct forcing simulator and an independent inclusion–exclusion implementation agreed on 27 complete multipartite graphs and 255318 candidate edge subsets; arithmetic checked rook and triangular extremes. This is a supporting finite test, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

Brimkov–Patel–Suriyanarayana–Teich (arXiv:1805.10984v1, Sections 3–4) give the power-domination polynomial as a general invariant, general zero-forcing and decomposition methods and formulas for other graph families, but not the all-edge-selection residual-forest criterion for complete multipartite line graphs. Soh–Koh (2014, Theorem 3.3 as indexed and cited by their accessible 2016 follow-up) give the scalar power domination number for rook graphs: the present formula explicitly does not claim that value as new. Koh–Soh's 2016 paper concerns Cartesian-product power-domination numbers and an inequality rather than exact distributions of every valid set. Girish–Asiri–Somasundaram (2026, full article, including its line-graph transformation theorem) study fault tolerance of selected configurations and provide a sufficient induced-set transformation, not the necessary-and-sufficient forest equivalence or complete enumerator. A nearby previously recorded statement enumerates the vertex-induced forests of multipartite graphs, and another enumerates star-isolating sets in rook graphs, but neither supplies the weight of edge sets whose exact uncovered vertex set is one such forest, and neither implies the power-domination propagation criterion. Searches of published mathematical research records under the alternate descriptions line-graph power domination, rook-graph power-domination polynomial, triangular-graph power domination, unused-endpoint induced forest and feedback-vertex-set transformation found no covering claim. Some equivalent or unindexed work may exist. The 2014 original PDF was inaccessible; its accessible scalar theorem and later citations were compared without claiming a full-document inspection.

## Value — PASS

The result resolves a natural precise question raised by the 2018 power-domination polynomial framework for the connected class of line graphs of arbitrary complete multipartite graphs. Its genuinely structural feature is that geodesically unrelated propagation in the line graph becomes the elementary cycle/forest criterion on unused original vertices. From that mechanism comes an exact all-cardinality polynomial for variable part-size profiles. The complete answer is mathematically distinct from, and strictly finer than, the known scalar optimum on rook graphs; the count of all optimal placements for each rectangular board and the triangular-graph parity refinement are meaningful reliability and enumerative data, not an arbitrary slice. The closed formula is exponential in the general number of parts and is not presented as a polynomial-time algorithm.
