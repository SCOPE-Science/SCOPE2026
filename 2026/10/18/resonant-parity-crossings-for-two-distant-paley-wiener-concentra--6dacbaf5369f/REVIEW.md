# Scientific review: two-interval Paley–Wiener parity crossings

## Correctness — PASS

The normalized compressed projection on two disjoint intervals is unitarily equivalent to the direct sum of the two self-adjoint parity branches. Positivity of the single-interval sinc kernel in the specified small-bandwidth regime proves leading-eigenvalue simplicity and the strict positivity of the coefficients used to locate crossings. Uniform kernel expansion to second order, explicit parity matrix elements, and a reduced-resolvent perturbation calculation yield the two leading eigenvalue expansions with uniform remainders. The differentiated expansion has a nonzero slope, so the intermediate-value root is an exact crossing and locally unique. The independent quadrature checker corroborates the asymptotic constants but is not used to infer an infinite statement. The fixed strict bandwidth condition and finite windows are essential hypotheses.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The July 2026 rearrangement theorem of Abreu and Speckbacher gives an absolute concentration upper bound, not two-eigenvalue asymptotics or exact parity-crossing locations. Zelent's October 2026 stability theorem concerns a vanishing detached piece near the interval and quantitative comparison to one interval. A directly inspected published related calculation resolves this latter local-detachment regime, not the present two fixed-size components at large separation. Fahs–Krasovsky study a sine-kernel Fredholm determinant as bandwidth grows, rather than its first two eigenvalues at fixed bandwidth with diverging separation. Repeated targeted research-database searches of the splitting, separated intervals, sinc resonances, prolate eigenvalues, and parity aliases did not identify a covering statement. Search failure does not certify novelty; older unindexed two-component spectral perturbation literature remains a documented priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Fixed-size separated observation windows are natural in concentration and bandlimited sensing. A first-order approximation by two independent single-interval operators misses both the exact degeneracies and their nonzero displacement from zero-crossings of the sinc interaction. The second-order reduced-resolvent constant, the explicit positive shift, and the existence of infinitely many exact two-fold top-spectrum degeneracies resolve a meaningful spectral-interference phenomenon. This is more than a renormalization or a numerical coefficient calculation.

## Limits of the review

No conclusion is made for component sizes that vary or for bandwidth products at or above the positivity threshold. The analytic proof is complete under the specified hypotheses. Unindexed historical precedent cannot be ruled out, and the numerical quadrature does not independently certify operator limits.
