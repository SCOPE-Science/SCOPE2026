# Rigidity and enumeration of minimum outer-independent three-rainbow functions on Möbius ladders

## Finding
For every integer \(m\geq 3\) with \(m\not\equiv1\pmod 6\), the Möbius ladder \(ML(m)\) has exactly
\[
N_m=\begin{cases}12,&m\equiv3\pmod6,\\12m,&m\equiv0,2,4,5\pmod6\end{cases}
\]
distinct minimum-weight *outer-independent* \(3\)-rainbow dominating functions, on its fixed, individually labeled vertices with fixed color names \(\{1,2,3\}\). Their entire structure is classified below; color permutations, graph automorphisms, and layer swaps are **not** identified in the count. For the included parameters their minimum weights are \(m\) in the first case and \(m+1\) otherwise. The minimum *ordinary* three-rainbow domination weights, without the independent-zero restriction, were already determined by Kuzman [1]; those numerical minimum-weight formulas are **not** claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
For \(m\ge3\), define \(ML(m)\) on labeled vertices \(u_i,v_i\), \(0\le i<m\), with rungs \(u_iv_i\), consecutive rail edges \(u_iu_{i+1},v_iv_{i+1}\) for \(0\le i<m-1\), and twisted end edges \(u_{m-1}v_0,v_{m-1}u_0\). Let \(C=\{1,2,3\}\). An outer-independent three-rainbow function is \(f:V\to2^C\) such that its zero set \(Z_f=\{x:f(x)=\varnothing\}\) is independent, and for every \(x\in Z_f\) the union of the colors on its three neighbors is \(C\). Its weight is \(w(f)=\sum_x|f(x)|\). Write \(N_m\) for the number attaining the smallest possible weight **among these functions**.

**Complete classification in the stated scope.** Each function can be obtained exactly once from these labeled choices (and all choices give valid functions):

1. For odd \(m\equiv3\pmod6\), choose one of two alternating zero-vertex layer patterns, with exactly one empty vertex in each rung. Put singletons on the opposite vertices in the periodic order \(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_0,c_1,c_2,\ldots\), where \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\) is an arbitrary permutation of \((1,2,3)\).
2. For even \(m\), choose the unique rung containing no empty vertex (\(m\) choices), and one of two alternating zero-layer patterns on the remaining \(m-1\) rungs. Rotate the twisted ladder so the exceptional rung is rung \(m-1\). Give the colored vertices of rungs \(0,\ldots,m-2\) singleton colors \(c_i=c_{i\bmod3}\), where \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\) is any permutation. On the exceptional rung, give each of its two vertices the unique color missing from the other two colored neighbors of the adjacent zero vertex on the corresponding end of the chain. Both assignments are forced and always compatible.
3. For odd \(m\equiv5\pmod6\), choose one doubled-color rung (\(m\) choices) and one of two alternating zero-layer patterns on all rungs. Rotate the doubled rung to \(m-1\). All other colored rung vertices receive the singletons \(c_i=c_{i\bmod3}\) for \(0\le i<m-1\), with \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\) any permutation. The colored vertex on rung \(m-1\) receives **exactly the two colors** \(C\setminus\{c_0\}\); its opposite rung vertex is empty.

In the second and third cases a 'rotate' instruction uses the order-\(2m\) graph automorphism \(T(u_i)=u_{i+1}\) and \(T(v_i)=v_{i+1}\) for \(i<m-1\), with \(T(u_{m-1})=v_0\) and \(T(v_{m-1})=u_0\). This preserves every rung, rail edge and twisted edge by direct inspection. All descriptions refer to the original labeled vertices after reversing that automorphism. Different exceptional rungs, zero-layer choices and color permutations give different functions.

## Proof
Each rung is an edge, hence contains at most one empty vertex. Write \(z=|Z_f|\), and let \(e=\sum_{x:f(x)\ne\varnothing}(|f(x)|-1)\) be the surplus beyond one color per nonempty vertex. Then
\[0\le z\le m,\qquad e\ge0,\qquad w(f)=2m-z+e.\]
Thus \(w(f)\ge m\). If equality holds, necessarily \(z=m,e=0\). There is one empty vertex per rung; independence across rail edges forces the empty layer to alternate on consecutive rungs. For *even* \(m\), this conflicts with the twisted end edges: the two endpoint empties become adjacent. For *odd* \(m\), these two twisted edges cause no conflict, and there are exactly two possible alternating zero patterns.

When \(z=m,e=0\), let \(c_i\) denote the singleton on the nonempty vertex of rung \(i\). Each empty vertex has its own rung's singleton and the neighboring rungs' singletons as its three neighbors, with twisted edge endpoints handled by the alternating-layer pattern. The rainbow condition is precisely that each cyclic triple \((c_{i-1},c_i,c_{i+1})\) contains all three different colors. Consequently \(c_{i+3}=c_i\) and consistency on the cyclic ring is possible if and only if \(3\mid m\). With \(m\) odd, this is exactly \(m\equiv3\pmod6\). This proves a sharp bound \(m\) in that class, with two zero patterns and six color permutations, establishing the first count \(12\). Otherwise weight is at least \(m+1\).

Suppose next that \(m\) is even and \(w(f)=m+1\). There cannot be \(m\) empty vertices by the parity/twist obstruction. The weight identity therefore forces \(z=m-1,e=0\). There is a unique rung without empties. Because the rail segment between its ends has exactly one empty vertex in every rung, their layers alternate, giving exactly two patterns once the exceptional rung is selected. Rotate the exceptional rung to \(m-1\). In each interior rung \(1\le i\le m-3\), the three colored neighbors of its empty vertex lie in rungs \(i-1,i,i+1\); all three are singletons and must be pairwise distinct. Hence the singleton sequence on rungs \(0,\ldots,m-2\) is uniquely determined by any permutation of the first three colors, repeating with period three. The empty vertex at rung \(0\) has two of these distinct singleton neighbors and one neighbor in the exceptional rung; its third color forces one exceptional-rung singleton. The empty vertex at rung \(m-2\) similarly forces the other exceptional-rung singleton. They are different vertices of the exceptional rung (since \(m\) is even), so no conflict occurs. Thus every one of \(m\cdot2\cdot6=12m\) choices works, and no other function of that weight exists. As weight \(m\) was excluded, these are exactly the minima.

Finally suppose \(m\equiv5\pmod6\) and \(w(f)=m+1\). The weight identity leaves only two possibilities: \((z,e)=(m,1)\) and \((m-1,0)\). In the second case, rotate the exceptional no-empty rung to \(m-1\). The other rungs' singleton colors again have period three, with \(c_0,c_1,c_2\) pairwise different. Now the empty vertices of rungs \(0\) and \(m-2\) have a **common** neighbor in the exceptional rung because \(m\) is odd. As \(m\equiv5\pmod6\), we have \(c_{m-2}=c_0\) and \(c_{m-3}=c_2\). To dominate the first endpoint empty vertex, this common neighbor must carry \(c_2\); to dominate the other, it must carry \(c_1\). They differ, so the case \((m-1,0)\) is impossible.

In the remaining case \((z,e)=(m,1)\), empties alternate over every rung and exactly one colored vertex has two colors, all others one. Rotate this vertex to rung \(m-1\). The untouched interior constraints force \(c_0,\ldots,c_{m-2}\) to be the period-three singleton word of an arbitrary first-three-color permutation. Again \(c_{m-2}=c_0\), so the empty vertex in rung \(m-1\), whose other two colored neighbors both have color \(c_0\), forces the double label \(C\setminus\{c_0\}\). The two endpoint empty vertices also see all three colors; the other empty vertices see three distinct singletons. This gives exactly \(m\cdot2\cdot6=12m\) functions of weight \(m+1\), completing the proof.

## Verification
The self-contained `checker.py` independently constructs the labeled Möbius-ladder adjacency lists and exhaustively visits every zero set, every allowed position for an additional color, and every singleton/double-color selection at the fixed optimal weight for sizes \(m=3,4,5,6,8,9,10\). It finds, respectively, \(12,48,60,72,96,12,120\) distinct labelings. A second cyclic-word enumeration fixes the double-color position for \(m=5,11\) and finds exactly six words for each. Run `python checker.py` with standard Python. These finite checks corroborate but **do not replace** the all-size combinatorial proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kuzman [1, Theorem 4.5, proof and Table 4] already determined the exact **ordinary** three-rainbow domination number of every Möbius ladder and exhibited admissible minimizing labelings. The numerical optimum displayed above follows from those prior results and is not a novelty claim. The additional result here is the exact enumeration and exhaustive rigidity classification, with an *independent-zero requirement*, of **every** minimum function in five of the six congruence classes. The ordinary minimum-value theorem and its displayed individual witnesses do not count or characterize all minimizing functions. The distinction between *a* witness and the entire labeled-minimum-function space is essential.

The more recent study [2] discusses structural properties of cubic graphs attaining the theoretical three-rainbow lower bound, including Möbius-ladder examples. It does not give the labeled outer-independent enumeration here. Its first arXiv posting is later than the source anchoring the present investigation; it is cited only as comparison literature.

## Limitations
The congruence class \(m\equiv1\pmod6\) is **not classified or enumerated** here. Its known optimum \(m+2\) allows more than one defect type, beyond the one-defect rigidity used in this result. No assertion is made about the number of ordinary three-rainbow minimizers without independent zeros, about all higher rainbow parameters, or about a classification up to graph automorphism. The claimed counts concern separately labeled functions, retaining color names.
