# Review of the orientation-rigidity theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The block perturbation calculation supplies both first-order quantities directly: the small spectral spread of the compressed diagonal operator and the orthogonal coupling to the original normal. Each coordinate probe forces an identical squared normal coordinate; this proves necessity including the zero-coordinate boundary. A balanced explicit orthonormal basis proves sufficiency algebraically. The geometric cube integral establishes the stated, deliberately restricted probability branch; the continuity argument justifies the random-event limit. The independently replayable symbolic/numerical verification checks the identities and asymptotic approximations, while finite trials are not used as proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit priority risk.** The source paper proves separate gap and residual bounds but not a necessary-and-sufficient classification for their deterministic *first-order proportionality*. A prior published exact joint-law example on a disconnected graph uses a different normal with a zero coordinate and studies nonmonotone dependence and covariance, not the all-orientation rigidity theorem; the two claims do not imply each other. The historical three-observation uniform variance law is not asserted to be new. Searches of public literature and mathematical research records found no exact rigidity formulation. The conclusion is a same-model best-knowledge comparison, not a uniqueness or priority certificate; equivalent specialized spectral results remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** The rigidity theorem characterizes exactly when random diagonal eigenvalue separation and nullspace contamination cannot be independently tuned, a natural pair of competing quantities for randomized nullspace computation. The unique symmetric orientation and sharp coefficient provide a structural boundary and a reproducible joint probability for evaluating perturbation strength. It is not a renaming of a known CDF or a single numerical counterexample: necessity quantifies over all orientations and all nonnegative diagonal choices. Its dimension-three rank-one and infinitesimal scope restricts generalization, and no finite-iteration convergence conclusion is drawn.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Kressner--Shao, arXiv:2407.04634v1/v2, is the defining numerical setting; Osan--Chu--Yu, arXiv:2609.25333v1, clarifies the long-known uniform three-point sample-variance marginal. Classical eigenspace perturbation is treated as a tool, not as a contribution. The classification does not cover arbitrary PSD matrices or finite perturbations. Independent literature priority review and independent theorem audit have not occurred.
