# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Rayleigh–Ritz supplies two budget-dependent global upper bounds for arbitrary independent edge weights. The symmetrical wheel Fourier spectrum attains their sharp maximum. Equality in both first-Fourier trial-vector inequalities forces the first-Fourier modes into the bottom nonconstant eigenspace; the imaginary part of their complex eigenvalue equation enforces equal rim conductances, after which all spokes must be equal. Boundary cases at the precise critical price and nonnegativity limits are covered. The reproducibility program returned 2141 assertions passed. Finite calculations are supporting checks, not the infinite-family proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Sun–Boyd–Xiao–Diaconis (2006) give the general budgeted continuous-time spectral-gap design framework and an explicit result for cycles, not the wheel two-price/all-optimizer classification. Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao (2009, §5.4.3) explicitly solve the same wheel topology under a **different discrete-time SLEM/stochastic** objective, and their Fourier block analysis is foundational but does not imply the claimed continuous-time budget threshold or full optimizer face. Ghosh–Boyd (2006) cover a discrete edge-addition SDP, and Shakeri et al. (2015) cover a different two-layer topology. Searches of mathematical research records and primary literature located no same-claim theorem. Related or unindexed papers remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete natural family classification, not a perturbative numerical improvement: the threshold shrinks quadratically with wheel size, the optimal support changes exactly there, the critical-price optimum is nonunique, and the proof establishes that no symmetry-breaking optimal designs have been overlooked. The full critical face matters to resource allocation under exact price ties. The elementary spectral ingredients are acknowledged; the precise all-allocation phase diagram and rigidity are the substantive output.

**Limits.** The theorem is for two uniform price classes, freely adjustable nonnegative conductances, and the combinatorial Laplacian's continuous-time spectral gap. It does not cover discrete-time SLEM or heterogeneous within-class prices. Searches are not proof of uniqueness in world literature.
