# Sampling design can reverse species-richness variance twice: a sharp six-individual threshold

## Finding

Fix a finite community with positive integer species abundances \(\boldsymbol a=(a_1,\ldots,a_s)\), and total census size \(N=\sum_i a_i\). For each integer \(1\le m\le N\), compare two random richness counts: \(K_m^{\rm finite}\), from a uniform size-\(m\) subset of the \(N\) labeled individuals, and \(K_m^{\rm iid}\), from \(m\) independent uniform draws of labeled individuals with replacement. Put
\[
\Delta_{\boldsymbol a}(m)=\operatorname{Var}(K_m^{\rm finite})-\operatorname{Var}(K_m^{\rm iid}).
\]
A *strict variance-order reversal* is a change between \(+\) and \(-\) in the successive signs of \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol a}(m)\) after zero entries, if any, are omitted.

**Sharp threshold and uniqueness.** Every abundance profile with \(N\le5\) has at most one strict variance-order reversal as \(m\) varies from \(2\) through \(N\). At \(N=6\), exactly one profile, up to permutation, has two reversals: \((4,1,1)\). For this profile,
\[
\Delta(2)=-\frac1{100},\qquad
\Delta(3)=\frac{137}{6480},\qquad
\Delta(4)=-\frac{15211}{233280}.
\]
The signs at depths \(2,3,4,5,6\) are \((- ,+,-,-,-)\), so both reversals are strictly between adjacent depths, not artifacts of zero entries.

**Unbounded family.** For every integer \(t\ge2\), the abundance profile consisting of one species of size \(2t\) and \(t\) singleton species (hence \(N=3t\)) satisfies \(\Delta(2)<0<\Delta(3)\) and \(\Delta(N)<0\). Therefore a two-reversal sign pattern, with intervening zeros ignored if they occur, exists for arbitrarily large census sizes. The second reversal in this unrestricted family is not asserted to occur at any predetermined adjacent pair of depths.

## Assumptions and scope

Species identities of all \(N\) distinct labeled individuals are known; \(a_i\) are strictly positive integers; both sampling designs use exactly \(m\) draws and the same relative species abundances \(a_i/N\). In the finite design all individual subsets of size \(m\) have equal probability. In the independent design each draw samples an individual uniformly from the entire original population, allowing repetition. Repeated selection of one individual does not create an additional species. Both variances are unconditional over the specified random sampling design, *conditional on the fixed known census profile*. At \(m=1\), both richness counts are identically one and their variances agree. At \(m=N\), the finite-sampling richness is deterministic; independent sampling need not be.

## Proof

**1. Exact indicator formula.** Write \(Q_i^d(m)\) for the probability of *missing* species \(i\) and \(Q_{ij}^d(m)\) for the probability of missing both species \(i,j\), with \(d\in\{\rm finite,\rm iid\}\). For the finite design,
\[
Q_i^{\rm finite}(m)=\frac{\binom{N-a_i}{m}}{\binom Nm},\qquad
Q_{ij}^{\rm finite}(m)=\frac{\binom{N-a_i-a_j}{m}}{\binom Nm};
\]
for the independent design,
\[
Q_i^{\rm iid}(m)=\left(1-\frac{a_i}{N}\right)^m,\qquad
Q_{ij}^{\rm iid}(m)=\left(1-\frac{a_i+a_j}{N}\right)^m.
\]
Binomial coefficients with an upper argument below \(m\) mean zero. Since richness is the sum of the indicators that each species has appeared, inclusion-exclusion gives the **exact** moment identity
\[
\operatorname{Var}(K_m^d)=\sum_i Q_i^d(m)(1-Q_i^d(m))+
 2\sum_{i<j}\left[Q_{ij}^d(m)-Q_i^d(m)Q_j^d(m)\right].\tag{1}
\]
All terms are rational for the stated census domain. No asymptotic approximation or independent-species assumption is used.

**2. Exhaustive sharp first layer.** Every positive integer abundance profile of census size \(N\), up to relabeling, is exactly one nonincreasing integer partition of \(N\). Enumerate such partitions for each \(1\le N\le6\), and apply (1) at every \(2\le m\le N\). The complete rational results are in `census_certificate.json`, generated by `verify.py`. The resulting count of profiles by number of strict sign reversals is:

| Census size | Profiles | Zero reversals | One reversal | Two reversals |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 5 | 4 | 1 | 0 |
| 5 | 7 | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| 6 | 11 | 8 | 2 | 1 |

The only two-reversal profile is \((4,1,1)\), whose exact initial three differences are displayed above; the full signs including depth six are certified in the table. Exhaustiveness follows because the recurrence used by the verifier chooses each next part between one and the preceding part, reaching every nonincreasing partition exactly once. All 29 partitions and every depth are included, not a selected computational sample.

**3. Infinite family.** Set \(\boldsymbol a_t=(2t,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{t\text{ times}})\) for integer \(t\ge2\), and \(N=3t\). In (1), only four different missing-event probabilities occur: those for the dominant species, a singleton, dominant-plus-singleton, and two different singletons. Under independent sampling these are
\[
\left(\frac13\right)^m,\quad \left(1-\frac1{3t}\right)^m,\quad
\left(\frac{t-1}{3t}\right)^m,\quad \left(1-\frac2{3t}\right)^m,
\]
respectively; under finite sampling replace each power by the corresponding ratio of binomial coefficients. Thus the variance in either design equals
\[
q_D(1-q_D)+tq_S(1-q_S)+2t(q_{DS}-q_Dq_S)
+t(t-1)(q_{SS}-q_S^2).\tag{2}
\]
Direct exact substitution at sample depth two simplifies to
\[
\Delta_{\boldsymbol a_t}(2)=
-\frac{(5t-1)(3t^2-2t+1)}{81t^2(3t-1)^2}<0.
\]
At sample depth three it simplifies to
\[
\Delta_{\boldsymbol a_t}(3)=
\frac{P(t)}{729t^4(3t-2)^2(3t-1)^2},\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
P(t)=8343t^7-24426t^6+21591t^5-5657t^4-1152t^3+757t^2-108t+4.
\]
To verify positivity for every \(t\ge2\), put \(u=t-2\ge0\) and expand:
\[
P(u+2)=8343u^7+92376u^6+429291u^5+1080733u^4
+1581152u^3+1329613u^2+583224u+98640>0.
\]
This proves \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol a_t}(3)>0\). Finally, at \(m=N\) the finite design takes the entire census and has variance zero, while the independent design has strictly positive variance: the sample has a positive chance of using only the dominant species and a positive chance of seeing multiple species. Thus \(\Delta_{\boldsymbol a_t}(N)<0\). The nonzero sign sequence therefore contains at least two reversals for every integer \(t\ge2\). These are algebraic statements for all \(t\), not extrapolations of the finite census.

## Verification

Run `python verify.py` alongside `census_certificate.json`. It checks formula (1) against *independent direct enumeration* of labeled \(m\)-element subsets and labeled ordered \(m\)-tuples for each of the 29 partitions of \(N\le6\), including endpoints \(m=1,N\). The two methods agree on 270 exact rational variance values. The program regenerates the complete finite certificate byte for byte and checks formulas (2)–(3) for \(2\le t\le9\) using exact arithmetic; the latter finite tests are supplementary to the unrestricted algebraic proof. The full certificate records signs and exact fractions for all profiles, not only the exceptional one.

## Relationship to prior work

Heck, van Belle and Simberloff (1975) established a finite-population rarefaction mean-and-variance framework. Gotelli and Colwell (2001) surveyed the importance of distinguishing sampling protocols, and Colwell and collaborators (2012) developed interpolation/extrapolation and variance estimators, contrasting multinomial sampling with finite-population models. These frameworks motivate the variance comparison, but the inspected full texts do not identify the sharp smallest two-reversal census, its unique profile, or the all-size family in (3). In particular, unconditional *estimators of a mean rarefaction curve* discussed in Colwell et al. (2012) are not the two fixed-census richness variances compared here. Robson and Rothschild (1967) derived a with-replacement distinct-individual counting law; distinct labeled individuals are not the same statistic as distinct species with unequal multiplicities. All formulas concerning basic missing-event probabilities are standard inputs, not claimed to be new.

## Limitations

The sharp classification concerns census sizes through six only; no global maximum on the number of reversals for larger arbitrary profiles is asserted. The infinite family guarantees two changes in the nonzero sign sequence, but does not locate its final adjacent transition. There is no claim about spatial aggregation, imperfect species detection, unequal sampling effort, random census profiles, or higher moments. Bibliographic equivalences under urn occupancy or sampling-design terminology remain a residual originality risk; independent literature audit has not occurred.
