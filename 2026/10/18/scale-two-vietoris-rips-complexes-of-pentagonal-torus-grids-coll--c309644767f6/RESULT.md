# Scale-two Vietoris–Rips complexes of pentagonal torus grids collapse to \(S^1\vee\bigvee^n S^2\)

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge7\), let \(G_n=C_5\square C_n\) be the Cartesian product of the five-cycle and the \(n\)-cycle, equipped with its graph shortest-path metric. Its inclusive radius-two Vietoris–Rips complex \(X_n=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(G_n))\) admits exactly \(40n\) elementary simplicial collapses onto a canonical complex \(Z_n\). The latter consists of a standard square-triangulated torus together with one full four-simplex on each five-vertex meridian. Consequently,
\[
X_n\simeq Z_n\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n}S^2.
\]
Its original face vector, in dimensions zero through four, is \((5n,30n,55n,35n,6n)\); the face vector of \(Z_n\) is \((5n,20n,20n,5n,n)\). In particular,
\[
\widetilde H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n,
\]
and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The result determines the complete homotopy type, not just ranks of homology groups.

## Assumptions and scope

Identify the vertices with \(V=\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Write \((i,j)\) for a vertex, where the first coordinate is called its row and the second its column, and let \(d_m\) be the shortest cyclic distance on \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). The metric is
\[
d((i,j),(k,\ell))=d_5(i,k)+d_n(j,\ell).
\]
A nonempty subset of \(V\) is a simplex of \(X_n\) exactly when all its distinct vertex pairs have distance at most two. All indices in the proof are cyclic. The restriction \(n\ge7\) is essential to the stated facet classification: the square of the column cycle has no triangular cliques other than three consecutive columns, and a pair of columns at distance two has a unique middle column.

For each column \(j\), define its five-vertex simplex
\[
A_j=\Delta\{(i,j):i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\}.
\]
For every row \(i\) and column \(j\), introduce vertices
\[
L=(i,j-1),\quad R=(i,j+1),\quad M_-=(i-1,j),\quad M=(i,j),\quad M_+=(i+1,j)
\]
and the full four-simplex \(B_{i,j}=\Delta\{L,R,M_-,M,M_+\}\).
For every unit square set
\[
a=(i,j),\quad b=(i+1,j),\quad c=(i+1,j+1),\quad d=(i,j+1),
\]
and let \(C_{i,j}=\Delta\{a,b,c,d\}\) denote its full tetrahedron. The notation \(\Delta W\) means the simplicial complex of all nonempty subsets of \(W\).

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive facet classification.** The projection of a radius-two clique to its column coordinates is a clique in the second graph power of \(C_n\). For \(n\ge7\), every such column set is contained in three consecutive columns. If it occupies two columns exactly two steps apart, each of the two endpoint columns contains only one selected vertex, and both vertices have the same row \(i\), because their column distance already consumes the entire radius-two allowance. Any vertex in the middle column must have row in \(\{i-1,i,i+1\}\). Thus every such face lies in the unique \(B_{i,j}\) with the given endpoint pair.

Consider now a face contained in two adjacent columns, with no distance-two column pair. Every cross-column row pair must differ by cyclic distance at most one. If one column contains only one row \(i\), all rows in the other column lie in \(\{i-1,i,i+1\}\), so the face extends to one of the \(B_{i,j}\). If both columns contain at least two rows, then each consists of two consecutive rows. Indeed, two rows at cyclic distance two have only one common radius-one row neighbor on the five-cycle, so cannot coexist with two rows in the opposite column. Two consecutive rows \(\{i,i+1\}\) have common radius-one neighbors exactly \(\{i,i+1\}\). Hence both columns use the same two rows, and the face lies in a square tetrahedron \(C_{i,j}\). Faces supported in a single column lie in \(A_j\). Conversely each of these listed vertex sets is a radius-two clique, and each displayed maximal simplex is genuinely maximal. Therefore \(X_n\) is generated by the \(n\) simplices \(A_j\), the \(5n\) simplices \(B_{i,j}\), and the \(5n\) tetrahedra \(C_{i,j}\).

**2. Six collapses per middle-column simplex.** Fix \(i,j\) and abbreviate \(M_-=M_0\), \(M=M_1\), \(M_+=M_2\) for this paragraph. In the following order remove each face displayed on the left with its unique containing codimension-one face displayed on the right:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{L,R,M_0,M_2\}&\prec\{L,R,M_0,M_1,M_2\},\\
\{L,M_0,M_2\}&\prec\{L,M_0,M_1,M_2\},\\
\{R,M_0,M_2\}&\prec\{R,M_0,M_1,M_2\},\\
\{L,R,M_0\}&\prec\{L,R,M_0,M_1\},\\
\{L,R,M_2\}&\prec\{L,R,M_1,M_2\},\\
\{L,R\}&\prec\{L,R,M_1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\prec\) indicates the lower free face and its paired coface, rather than a mere set inclusion. To verify freeness, first note that a face containing \(L,R\) has a unique distance-two column pair, and hence belongs to a unique \(B_{i,j}\). Similarly, any remaining face containing \(L,M_0,M_2\), or \(R,M_0,M_2\), is contained in this same \(B_{i,j}\): the extreme pair of middle-column rows determines their sole permissible neighbor row in the adjacent column. Faces belonging to the union of the \(A_j\) and \(C_{i,j}\) use neither of these characteristic patterns. Within each \(B_{i,j}\), the descending order in the display removes all competing cofaces before each move. Distinct \(B_{i,j}\) have disjoint families of the removed faces, so the procedure works in any order of \(i,j\). After \(6(5n)=30n\) elementary collapses, what remains is precisely the subcomplex generated by the \(A_j\) and \(C_{i,j}\).

**3. Two collapses per unit square.** For each square tetrahedron \(C_{i,j}=\Delta\{a,b,c,d\}\), delete
\[
\{a,b,d\}\prec\{a,b,c,d\},\qquad
\{b,d\}\prec\{b,c,d\}
\]
in this order. The triangle \(\{a,b,d\}\) lies in no other surviving tetrahedron, and, after its deletion, the diagonal edge \(\{b,d\}\) lies in only the triangle \(\{b,c,d\}\). The two off-diagonal triangles are thus deleted together with the square tetrahedron and the nonchosen diagonal, leaving the two triangles \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\). Periodic widths five and at least seven prevent any identification of different unit-square diagonals, so these are \(2(5n)=10n\) valid independent elementary collapses.

Thus \(X_n\searrow Z_n\) by exactly \(40n\) elementary pairs. The surviving unit-square triangles form the usual diagonal triangulation \(T\) of the torus \(C_5\times C_n\). For each \(j\), the full four-simplex \(A_j\) intersects \(T\) exactly in the embedded five-edge meridian \(C_5\times\{j\}\). The \(n\) added simplices are mutually disjoint and attach along \(n\) parallel meridians.

**4. Homotopy type.** Relative to its five-edge meridian, each attached four-simplex can be replaced up to homotopy by a cone on that meridian, or equivalently a two-disk along the same circle. This follows by the gluing lemma for cofibration pairs: both the four-simplex and the cone are contractible and contain that same boundary circle as a subcomplex. Hence \(Z_n\) has the homotopy type of a torus with \(n\) disks attached along parallel, primitive meridian loops. In the standard one-vertex CW presentation of the torus, denote the meridian and longitude generators by \(u\) and \(v\), and its two-cell by the commutator \([u,v]\). Attach one disk along \(u\) and cancel that disk with the \(u\)-one-cell. The torus commutator now attaches trivially, giving one two-sphere, and each of the other \(n-1\) meridian disks also attaches trivially, giving \(n-1\) additional two-spheres. The remaining one-cell is the longitude. Therefore \(Z_n\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n}S^2\). This argument establishes the homotopy type over the integers and does not rely on finite-field calculations.

**5. Face counts.** The torus triangulation has \(5n\) vertices, \(15n\) edges, and \(10n\) triangles. Adding the \(n\) four-simplices \(A_j\) contributes five new diagonals, ten triangles, five tetrahedra, and one four-simplex per column. Thus \(Z_n\) has face vector \((5n,20n,20n,5n,n)\). Reversing the thirty \(n\) pairs of step 2 and the ten \(n\) pairs of step 3 adds \((0,10n,35n,30n,5n)\) faces in respective dimensions, giving the stated initial vector \((5n,30n,55n,35n,6n)\).

## Verification

The included standard-library script `artifacts/verify.py` constructs every face directly from the shortest-path metric, without assuming the facet classification. For every integer \(7\le n\le14\), it compares that exhaustive face set with the proposed facets, replays every one of the \(40n\) free-face moves against all current codimension-one cofaces, checks the final triangulation plus filling simplices, and checks both face vectors. For \(7\le n\le10\), it additionally computes the exact mod-two boundary ranks of the resulting simplicial complexes and obtains Betti numbers \((1,1,n,0,0)\) in dimensions zero through four. Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py` from the directory containing this result. The script reports `VERIFY_OK` upon success. These finite corroborations are not the proof for unbounded \(n\); the preceding face classification, collapses, and CW argument provide that proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng, and Sterling [1] analyze radius-dependent homotopy types of **square** torus grids \(C_m\square C_m\) with the same Manhattan metric, including a torus at radius two for \(m\ge7\) and a ninefold wedge of two-spheres for \(C_5\square C_5\) at radius two (Theorem 5.12). Those are different parameter domains: their square \(5\)-by-\(5\) computation neither gives the all-\(n\) rectangular homotopy type nor its explicit collapse. Published work on \(C_4\square C_n\) at radius two [2] gives \(S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^3\); the five-cycle factor changes the spherical dimension to two. Results for clique-fiber products \(K_m\square C_n\) [3] cannot be substituted because a graph metric on \(C_5\) is not the metric on \(K_5\), even though their within-fiber radius-two Rips complexes are both simplices. The uniform pentagonal case is therefore distinct from these previously published statements and from their direct parameter specializations.

## Limitations

This theorem covers only the **graph-metric** inclusive radius-two complex of \(C_5\square C_n\) for \(n\ge7\). It does not assert a description for \(n\le6\), Euclidean torus distances, other scales, or wider rectangular factors. The homotopy classification is a mathematical deduction from an explicit uniform collapse, not an external certification. Unindexed or differently formulated prior literature remains a residual originality risk.
