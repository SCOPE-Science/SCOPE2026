# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The dual identity \(D^*=\{S:\alpha(G[S])\le2\}\) and integral combinatorial Alexander duality shift the required homology to degree-three cohomology. The proof gives explicit signed three-cycles formed as joins of edge cycles on *disjoint* induced squares, and identifies maximal tetrahedra whose indicator cochains are cocycles. Maximality is proved separately for disjoint horizontal edges and the consecutive four-vertex path case. Their integral cycle/cocycle evaluation is diagonal unimodular, establishing a **split** summand, not simply linear independence modulo a prime. Full finite checks corroborate seven cases, with complete characteristic-two homology calculations for six. The all-parameter statement relies on the mathematical proof, not on these finite experiments.

## Originality: PASS (with residual risk)

Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak, arXiv:2512.04486, Conjecture 4.3, states the *full exact rank* as a conjecture; it does not prove the present all-parameter integral split lower bound or non-shellability. Their 2025 complete primary text and computations were inspected. Filakovský's arXiv:2602.11365 has been inspected in full: its general three-robust clique theorem applies to rectangular \(P_m\square P_n\) grids and square-sequence graphs, rather than \(K_m\square P_n\) for \(m\ge3\), which contain triangles. Relevant published-record queries were compared at the level of objects and implications, not merely titles. The nearest records concern ordinary three-cut complexes of rook graphs, two-row ladder total cuts, and four-cut complexes of powered paths; none implies this statement. Earlier general literature and a 2026 related duality manuscript were inspected less extensively; undiscovered equivalent coverage remains the principal risk.

## Value: PASS

This produces a uniform infinite-family obstruction to shellability at a published conjectural homology threshold, with a transparent geometric construction and an explicit integer left-inverse. It establishes nonzero homology in a degree two below the pure complex's top dimension for every allowed parameter and certifies a growing split rank \((m-1)^2\binom{n-2}{2}\). It is neither a routine finite-table increment nor a claim to have solved the stronger exact-rank conjecture.

## Limitations

The proposed conjectured full rank \((m-1)^2\binom{n-1}{2}\) is **not** established; additional integral homology and torsion are not classified. Results are for \(m\ge3,n\ge4\) only. Prior-work incompleteness and independent verification remain risks. See RESULT.md and VERIFICATION.md.
