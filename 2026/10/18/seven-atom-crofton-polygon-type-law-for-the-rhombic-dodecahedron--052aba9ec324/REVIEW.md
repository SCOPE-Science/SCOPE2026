# Scientific review: seven-atom Crofton law

## Correctness — PASS

The object is explicitly the polytope \(K=\{|x_i|+|x_j|\le2:1\le i<j\le3\}\). Its six axial and eight diagonal vertices and its twenty-four length-\(\sqrt3\) edges follow from twelve quadrilateral facets. A generic offset plane crosses one edge per section vertex. On each of seven rational triangles covering a signed-permutation sector of the normal sphere, all ninety-one pairwise vertex-height differences have one fixed sign in the interior, verified with exact rational arithmetic. The successive offset intervals then give the three-region coefficient table in RESULT.md. The vector spherical-triangle moment identity integrates these linear coefficients; the stated elementary tangent identities simplify them to seven displayed exact masses. The denominator follows from the mean-width edge formula, and a separate edge-integral identity proves that the expectation is exactly \(6\). The supplied `check.py` reproduces the rational finite classification and independently checks the angular formulas numerically and simulates the correct width-weighted Crofton experiment. The finite checker is a supplement to the mathematical proof of the infinite-direction claim.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

The closest same-polytope paper is P. Socha, *Random Plane Section of a Polyhedron* (1988), whose complete six-page PDF was inspected. It evaluates the pooled distribution of individual interior angles of plane-section polygons, including rhombic-dodecahedral angle histograms, but does **not** give the probability of a plane having any specified number of sides. The latter is a distribution of a different random variable and does not follow from the pooled one-angle marginal. The 2014 Koca–Koca–Koc full-text sections containing the fourteen-vertex construction do not analyze random affine planes. Related published results on box and cuboctahedral section-vertex distributions use different solids; central-plane laws likewise use a different measure. Multiple semantic queries of the mathematical findings index, combined with targeted primary-literature searches for the object's alternative names and exact seven-class claim, did not find a covering statement. Search absence by itself is not a proof of historical novelty. An earlier 1986 Polish stereological conference contribution and unindexed geometric probability tables remain priority risks; no claim of independent priority certification is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete exact seven-atom law for a canonical space-filling Catalan polyhedron and face-centered-cubic Voronoi cell under the natural invariant affine-plane measure. The supporting three-cone chamber partition, simultaneous positivity of all seven classes, exact nontrivial quadrilateral atom and the exact six-vertex mean jointly give a reusable, object-specific stereological classification. The full evaluated probability law is not merely the universal crossed-edge counting formula or a routine numerical table.
