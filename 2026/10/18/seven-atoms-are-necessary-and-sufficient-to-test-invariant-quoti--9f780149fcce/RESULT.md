# Seven atoms are necessary and sufficient to test invariant quotient kernels of ordered triples

## Finding
Let \(A\) be an infinite set of pure-equality atoms, let \(k\ge2\), and let \(R\subseteq A^k\times A^k\) be invariant under every permutation of \(A\). A test restricted to any \(N_k=\max\{2k+1,3k-2}\) atoms detects equivalence exactly: \(R\) is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive on \(A^k\) if and only if it is so on the restricted tuple set. Every uniform test needs at least \(2k+1\) atoms. For ordered triples, the two bounds coincide: **the exact test threshold is seven atoms**. This is a finite-window criterion for the validity of equivariant quotient kernels, not a classification of their numbers.

## Assumptions and scope
An ordered \(k\)-tuple may have repeated atom values. For tuple \(u\), let \(\operatorname{supp}(u)\) denote the set of its distinct coordinates (at most \(k\) atoms). The restriction to \(B\) is the subset of \(R\) whose two tuples belong to \(B^k\). The atom set \(A\) is infinite, and all atom permutations are allowed (no parameters or additional order). Equivalence is reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity on all tuples, not merely the injective orbit.

## Proof
**1. Small configurations and symmetry.** Every tuple involves at most \(k\) atoms and every pair at most \(2k\). If the restriction to \(B\) is an equivalence and \(|B|\ge2k+1\), equivariance transports every individual reflexivity and symmetry instance into \(B\); both axioms hold globally. Moreover, a pair's membership in \(R\) depends only on its complete equality pattern and can be transferred from an arbitrary pair to \(B\).

**2. Disjointness saturation.** Fix \(n\ge2k+1\) atoms \(B\). A *tuple type* is the equality pattern within its \(k\) positions. A type with \(r\) distinct values identifies its orbit with the injective ordered \(r\)-tuples over \(B\), with each such tuple expanded by its fixed position pattern. For two types with \(r,s\le k\), form the bipartite incidence graph connecting representatives of the two types whenever their supports are disjoint (for a single type use the corresponding undirected disjointness graph). This graph is connected. Indeed, replacing one coordinate of an injective \(r\)-tuple by a fresh atom gives two representatives whose union has \(r+1\) atoms. Since \(n\ge r+s+1\), there is an injective \(s\)-tuple disjoint from both, producing a two-edge path. Single-coordinate replacements connect all injective ordered \(r\)-tuples (coordinate swaps can use one spare atom). The same argument applies to the \(s\)-side, and at least one disjoint edge exists since \(n\ge r+s\). This proves connectedness even for equal types. Hence, if any pair of disjoint-support tuples of these two types satisfies \(R\), equivariance supplies all edges of this graph; equivalence of \(R\) on \(B^k\) then forces *every pair of tuples of those types in \(B^k\)* to satisfy \(R\). By pair-pattern transport, every pair of these types in \(A^k\) satisfies \(R\). Call this disjointness saturation.

**3. General finite-window upper bound.** Suppose \(uRv\) and \(vRw\). If the union of the three supports contains at most \(N_k\) atoms, equivariance places all three tuples in \(B\), and transitivity there gives \(uRw\). Otherwise, because the sum of their three support sizes is at most \(3k\) and \(N_k\ge3k-2\), their union has either \(3k-1\) or \(3k\) distinct atoms. Consequently at most one repetition occurs across all three tuples taken together. In particular at least one of the two premise pairs \((u,v)\) or \((v,w)\) has disjoint supports. Assume \((u,v)\) does; the other case is symmetric. Disjointness saturation makes \(R\) total between the type of \(u\) and the type of \(v\). Independently, \(vRw\) transports to some pair of representatives \(v',w'\in B^k\). Since every representative of \(u\)'s type in \(B^k\) relates to \(v'\), equivalence on \(B^k\) implies that every such representative relates to \(w'\). Equivariance then extends this to every pair of the types of \(u\) and \(w\) (choose any representatives of these types and use the already total relation to the type of \(v\)). In particular \(uRw\). Thus \(R\) is transitive on all \(A^k\).

**4. General lower bound and exact triple cutoff.** On \(A^k\), define \(R_k\) to be equality on every noninjective tuple and to relate two injective tuples precisely if their unordered supports are equal or disjoint. This is permutation invariant. On any \(2k\)-element set, every \(k\)-subset has exactly one disjoint \(k\)-subset, its complement. Thus the injective-tuple classes form pairs of complementary support classes; all noninjective tuples are singleton classes. So the restriction is an equivalence. But on \(2k+1\) distinct atoms, take injective tuples \(u,v,w\) with supports \(\{k+1,\ldots,2k\},\{1,\ldots,k\},\{k+1,\ldots,2k-1,2k+1\}\) respectively. Then \(uR_kv\), \(vR_kw\), but not \(uR_kw\), because \(u\) and \(w\) overlap in \(k-1\) atoms without equal supports. This establishes the uniform lower bound \(2k+1\). Since \(N_3=\max\{7,7}=7\), seven is the precise ordered-triple threshold. (The construction also witnesses sharpness when \(k=2\).)

## Verification
The executable `verify.py` checks all five ordered-triple equality patterns on a seven-element set. For each of the 25 ordered pairs of types it constructs the disjointness incidence graph and independently verifies its connectedness. It exhaustively checks on six atoms that the proposed counterrelation agrees with an explicit partition into singleton noninjective tuples and paired complementary injective-support blocks. It then displays the required seven-atom nontransitive triple. Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the file; expected output ends `THREE_REGISTER_VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate the graph lemma at arity three and the sharpness example. The all-arity upper bound is established by the proof, not inferred from finite experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Ciobanu's *Finite-Context Semantics in Finitely Supported Structures* (2026), particularly Section 5, establishes that finitely supported equivalences induce supported quotient actions. Ciobanu's *Finite Dependence and Invariance Hierarchies for Finitely Supported Structures* (2026) addresses invariant relation cells and support calculus. Bojańczyk, Klin and Lasota's *Automata theory in nominal sets* (2014), notably its section on equivariant relations and orbit-finite representations, gives the underlying nominal symmetry framework. These works are not credited with the specific seven-atom equivalence test or the \(N_k\) bound. Related prior two-coordinate quotient enumeration establishes a five-atom cutoff in that distinct arity; that statement does not imply this three-coordinate or all-arity result. The elementary disjointness-graph argument is supplied here, rather than claimed as a previously unknown graph-theoretic fact.

## Limitations
Only the full permutation action on pure-equality atoms is covered; other data symmetries may fail the bound. The all-arity lower and upper bounds are not proved equal for \(k\ge4\). Historical originality remains vulnerable to equivalent formulations in older permutation-group or nominal-automata sources. This is a self-reviewed proof, not independent validation.
