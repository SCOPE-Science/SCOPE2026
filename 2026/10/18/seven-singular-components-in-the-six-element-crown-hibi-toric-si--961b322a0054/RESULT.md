# Seven singular components in the six-element crown Hibi toric sixfold

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any algebraically closed field, and let \(P\) be the six-element height-two crown poset, with minimal elements \(a_0,a_1,a_2\), maximal elements \(b_0,b_1,b_2\), and exactly the six strict comparisons \(a_i<b_j\) for \(i\ne j\), indices taken modulo \(3\). Let \(Q=\mathcal O(P)\subset\mathbb R^6\) be its order polytope, and let \(X_Q\) denote the normal projective toric variety with the normal fan of \(Q\). Then \(X_Q\) has dimension \(6\), and

\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_Q)_{\mathrm{red}}=Z_{A,0}\cup Z_{A,1}\cup Z_{A,2}\cup Z_{B,0}\cup Z_{B,1}\cup Z_{B,2}\cup L
\]

is its irredundant decomposition into **seven irreducible components**. Each \(Z_{A,j}\) and \(Z_{B,i}\) is a three-dimensional projective quadric cone in \(\mathbb P^4\); the seventh component \(L\cong\mathbb P^1\) is not contained in any of the six threefolds. Thus the reduced singular locus is non-pure, with components of codimensions \(3\) and \(5\).

At a generic point of each threefold component, the transverse normal toric singularity is the three-dimensional rank-one \(2\times2\) determinantal cone (ordinary conifold), with Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity \(2\). At a generic point of \(L\), the transverse singularity is the five-dimensional rank-one \(3\times3\) determinantal cone, of multiplicity \(6\). There are precisely \(96\) singular torus orbits, distributed by orbit dimension as \((17,43,30,6)\) in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\); there are no singular orbits of higher dimension.

Among the \(15\) unordered pairs of the six quadric-cone components, \(6\) intersect in a line, \(3\) in a point, and \(6\) are disjoint. The line component \(L\) meets three of the cones at one endpoint and the other three at its other endpoint. All assertions hold in every characteristic.

## Assumptions and scope
The coordinates on \(\mathbb R^6\) are \((a_0,a_1,a_2,b_0,b_1,b_2)\). The polytope is given by the following twelve **facet-defining** inequalities:

\[
a_i\ge0,\qquad b_j\le1,\qquad b_j-a_i\ge0\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Each \(a_i\) has a successor and each \(b_j\) a predecessor, so these inequalities also imply \(0\le a_i,b_j\le1\). This is the normal-fan (projective) toric variety, equivalently the projectivization of the associated normal Hibi semigroup cone, not a claim about the singularity of the affine Hibi cone's vertex. The assertion is about reduced singular support, not its Jacobian scheme structure. The names \(Z_{A,j}\) and \(Z_{B,i}\) are specified by the respective order-polytope faces:

\[
F_{A,j}=\{a_i=a_\ell=b_j=0\},\quad \{i,\ell\}=\{0,1,2\}\setminus\{j\};
\]
\[
F_{B,i}=\{a_i=b_j=b_\ell=1\},\quad \{j,\ell\}=\{0,1,2\}\setminus\{i\}.
\]

Here an equation defining a face is imposed together with every inequality of \(Q\). The orbit closure associated with \(F\) is written \(V(F)\), so \(Z_{A,j}=V(F_{A,j})\), \(Z_{B,i}=V(F_{B,i})\). The seventh face is \(F_L=\{a_0=a_1=a_2=b_0=b_1=b_2=t:0\le t\le1\}\); it defines \(L=V(F_L)\).

## Proof
**1. Integral normal rays and smoothness criterion.** Write \(e_{a_i},e_{b_j}\) for the standard basis of \(\mathbb Z^6\). The primitive inward normals of the twelve facets are \(u_i=e_{a_i}\), \(v_j=-e_{b_j}\), and \(w_{ij}=e_{b_j}-e_{a_i}\) for \(i\ne j\). This is a signed vertex-edge incidence matrix of a directed graph with one additional root vertex. Every square minor is \(0\), \(1\), or \(-1\) by the total-unimodularity theorem for directed incidence matrices. Consequently, any linearly independent subset of these normals extends to a lattice basis. For any face \(F\), its normal cone is generated by precisely the normals of facets containing \(F\). Such a cone is smooth if and only if these normals are linearly independent: independence implies a unimodular simplicial cone, while a minimal linear dependence gives a nonsimplicial cone. Thus the singular-orbit problem reduces **exactly** to cycles in the active normal-incidence graph.

**2. Only seven minimal dependent configurations.** The six comparison edges \(a_i b_j\), \(i\ne j\), form a single undirected six-cycle. Attach a root \(*\) to each \(a_i\) via the facet \(a_i=0\) and to each \(b_j\) via \(b_j=1\). In a face's active graph, a cycle avoiding \(*\) is necessarily the full comparison six-cycle, and its equality locus is \(F_L\). In a cycle containing \(*\), the two incident edges at \(*\) cannot consist of one \(a\)-edge and one \(b\)-edge: the intervening comparison path would force \(0=1\). If both are \(a\)-edges, every intermediate comparison vertex has coordinate \(0\), and the path contains adjacent \(a_i,b_j,a_\ell\); both additional root edges are automatically active. Hence this cycle contains the four-cycle \(*,a_i,b_j,a_\ell,*\), whose equality locus is \(F_{A,j}\). The analogous argument for two \(b\)-edges yields the three four-cycles defining \(F_{B,i}\). No other minimal dependent configuration can occur. Every face whose active normals are dependent is contained in at least one of these seven faces, and each of the seven is singular because its active normals have exactly the indicated circuit. Every strictly larger face loses a ray of that circuit, so these seven singular faces are inclusion-maximal. The orbit-face correspondence establishes precisely the seven irreducible singular components.

**3. Components and transverse models.** For \(F_{A,j}\), the three free coordinates can be written \((t,u,v)=(a_j,b_i,b_\ell)\), with \(0\le t\le u,v\le1\). This is the lattice square pyramid with base \(t=0\) and apex \((1,1,1)\). It has exactly five lattice points and one relation among the four square-base generators, giving the toric threefold \(\{x_0x_3-x_1x_2=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4\). For \(F_{B,i}\) one obtains the same square pyramid by complementing all coordinates. The face \(F_L\) is a primitive lattice segment, so \(L\cong\mathbb P^1\).

For each \(A\)- or \(B\)-face the four active primitive normals span a rank-three saturated lattice and satisfy an integral circuit with two positive and two negative unit coefficients, for example \(u_i+w_{ij}=u_\ell+w_{\ell j}\). In the lattice quotient presenting this cone, its dual semigroup is generated by the four pairwise positive-negative sums. Its algebra is the rank-one \(2\times2\) determinantal algebra \(k[z_{pq}]/(z_{11}z_{22}-z_{12}z_{21})\). At \(F_L\), the six active comparison normals span a rank-five saturated lattice. Their primitive relation equates the three edges of one perfect matching of the six-cycle to the three of the other. The dual semigroup is generated by all nine positive-negative pairs, giving \(k[z_{ij}:1\le i,j\le3]/I_2(z)\), the cone over \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2\). These are transverse normal models at the generic orbit points (with a smooth torus factor along each orbit). Their multiplicities are the degrees of the Segre varieties, \(2\) and \(\binom42=6\), respectively.

**4. Intersections and orbit census.** Intersections of torus orbit closures are the orbit closures of the intersections of their polytope faces, or empty if the faces are disjoint. Comparing the explicitly given faces shows the stated \(6/3/6\) pair-incidence among the six threefolds. The segment \(F_L\) meets every \(A\)-face at \((0,\ldots,0)\) and every \(B\)-face at \((1,\ldots,1)\), and is contained in none. For a complete independent finite census, the checker enumerates all \(2^{12}\) facet-subsets, removes empty/repeated intersections, and obtains the total face numbers \((18,73,129,116,54,12,1)\) by dimension \(0\) through \(6\). Exactly \((17,43,30,6,0,0,0)\) of these faces have dependent active normals. This gives \(96\) singular torus orbits, and establishes all enumerated counts without a genericity or characteristic restriction.

## Relationship to prior work
The normal Hibi toric variety and its distributive-lattice construction are classical. **Wagner (1996)** already treats exactly the *projective normal-fan Hibi variety for arbitrary distributive lattices*, proves a general orbit-smoothness criterion via tree-shaped fibers of poset contractions, and studies local and associated-graded rings. Thus the general cycle/tree smoothness criterion in Step 1 and the toric mechanism in Steps 2–3 are **not asserted novel**; they are an independently proved specialization. The article’s bibliographic record and abstract were readable, but its plausible original full text could not be accessed, so possible stronger preemption by its examples or local-ring results remains a material originality risk. Brown and Lakshmibai (first posted 2006-12-11) classify singular loci for Grassmann-Hibi varieties and give a general-Hibi counterexample to transferring their minuscule criterion. Lakshmibai and Mukherjee (posted 2007-06-18) derive an all-Hibi tangent-space criterion and a general expression for the affine cone singular support. Brown and Lakshmibai (posted 2007-07-16) prove pure codimension-three singular loci and quadric-cone generic slices for **grid-lattice/minuscule** cases. Their general criteria, especially Wagner’s all-poset projective criterion, are acknowledged and not claimed new here. The three-crown join-irreducible poset is not a grid lattice, and the inspected works do not give this project's concrete seven-component **projective** singular-support decomposition, non-pure component dimensions, distinct \(2\times2\) and \(3\times3\) Segre transverse types, or the complete component incidence and orbit counts. Those explicit combined assertions are the contribution; a general computable criterion alone does not state them. A differently indexed equivalent worked example remains possible.

## Limitations
This is a **single natural, fully classified example**, not a theorem covering larger crowns. The reduced support is determined; scheme-theoretic nilpotent or embedded structure of the Jacobian singular scheme is not determined. The multiplicity claims concern generic transverse germs, not arbitrary intersections of components. Independent verification by a separate researcher and a complete historical uniqueness proof are not claimed. Wagner’s general theorem covers the projective orbitwise smoothness test and discusses local rings; the uninspected full paper could contain more of these particular local classifications. The claimed contribution is only the explicit complete seven-component geometry and intersection data, which are not stated in any source material actually inspected; general algorithmic coverage and inaccessible potentially stronger examples are genuine originality risks.
