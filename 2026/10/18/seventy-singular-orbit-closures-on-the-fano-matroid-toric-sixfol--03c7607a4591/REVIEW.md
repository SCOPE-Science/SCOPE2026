# Review of the Fano-matroid toric sixfold singular locus

## Correctness — PASS
Edmonds' complete subset-rank inequality representation reduces the matroid polytope to \(126\) exact candidate inequalities; the included full checker obtains \(21\) facets and all \(772\) faces. Rational-rank, primitive-ray and integer-minor tests exhaustively identify \(308\) singular cones and \(70\) maximal ones, while a complete \(168\)-element automorphism enumeration validates orbit types \(21,21,28\). The standard toric orbit-cone and lattice regularity criteria connect this certified complete combinatorial census to the claimed reduced singular locus. The divisor sequence and gcd-minor checks give each generic class group \(\mathbb Z\).

## Originality — PASS, with risks
Kim (arXiv:0901.3534), García-Marco and Ramírez Alfonsín (arXiv:1408.0419), and Lasoń and Michałek (arXiv:2005.08359) address matroid polytope flags, toric ideals, and Gorenstein properties. They provide starting theory but the inspected material does not determine the Fano toric sixfold's maximal singular orbit closures or class groups. De Loera et al. (arXiv:2606.11014) concern Fano Gröbner bases and unimodular triangulations; only the abstract and overview could be inspected, so potential overlap in unreviewed text is a residual risk. Semantic research-database comparisons likewise found no equivalent or stronger result. Non-detection does not establish absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For the exceptional, symmetric nonrepresentable-over-characteristic-zero Fano matroid, the toric singular locus is a natural geometric invariant. Its unexpectedly large \(70\)-component structure, two different component dimensions, three automorphism orbit types, and uniformly free-rank-one generic class groups give a complete singular-locus classification and a sharp non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial boundary.

## Limits
This exact finite example does not classify arbitrary rank-three matroids. The reported validation is not independent certification or a scheme-theoretic study of singular subschemes. The 2026 Fano-specific source comparison is incomplete.
