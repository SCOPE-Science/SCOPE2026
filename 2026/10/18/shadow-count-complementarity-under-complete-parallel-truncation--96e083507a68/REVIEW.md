# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Barycentric inequalities give four disjoint triangular cuts, four hexagonal old faces, twelve directed-edge vertices and exactly eighteen edges. The paired old/new face normals permit an exact contour-edge count: original shadow count \(k(4-k)\), truncated count \(12-k(4-k)\) for all generic directions and all admissible unequal cuts. The probability consequence invokes the existing Feldman--Klain tetrahedral shadow theorem. Exact-rational and floating-hull reconstructions from the bundled checker support the incidence analysis but are not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS relative to the inspected primary results and searchable records. Feldman--Klain already provide the tetrahedral triangular-shadow probability and its sharp range; those components are explicitly not novel. The earlier Coxeter-plane paper exhibits a special uniform truncated-tetrahedron projection. The new part assessed here is the direction-by-direction \(12\)-complementarity for *all* nondegenerate tetrahedra and four independently chosen admissible parallel vertex cuts, together with its full two-point law. The closest indexed tetrahedral area-moment result does not treat fully truncated tetrahedra. Risk: a historical silhouette-geometry source not indexed in the searches could already imply the coupling.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The theorem completely classifies the generic shadow-complexity types across a natural four-parameter family over every tetrahedron, and reveals a non-obvious invariance under substantial, independent vertex cut deformations. It relates two different polyhedra *pointwise*, not merely by equality of expected values, and transfers a classical solid-angle invariant to all members of this family. This is a structural and exact classification rather than an isolated numerical calculation.

## Limits
This concerns Euclidean orthogonal shadow vertex counts only, with proper parallel cuts and generic directions; no polygon shape/area distribution is established. The probability identity leverages known tetrahedral work and is not independently original. Search incompleteness and lack of independent audit are outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
