The inspected arXiv full texts by Janson–Knuth–Łuczak–Pittel (1993) and Bhamidi–Budhiraja–Wang (2012) discuss respectively fixed-edge component enumerations and barely subcritical graph evolution, rather than directly presenting the finite-\(p\), all-shape covariance sign thresholds and the fine critical-window constants. General component generating functions could implicitly yield the moment identity. The detailed Pittel (1990) full text could not be accessed, and this leaves unresolved priority risk rather than evidence of novelty. **Value — PASS.** Pairwise component-type count dependence controls second-order fluctuations of a random graph's component census. The exact hierarchy makes it possible to predict, at every finite sample size, which pairs repel and which attract. It also resolves the nonzero order-one dependence left over at \(p=1/n\) when the classical order-\(n\) covariance disappears; the reversal offsets depend on the component sizes, not on their internal graph shapes. These features are stronger than the usual marginal component census and motivate the exact finite-size expression.
