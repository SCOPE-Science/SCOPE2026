# Review of the sharp parity-mode separation theorem

**Correctness.** The proof specifies both parity-conditioned laws, establishes strict interlacing of their generating-polynomial zeros directly from a strictly increasing argument function, derives a positive partial-fraction decomposition and the strict coefficient-ratio sandwich, and passes to boundary probabilities through polynomial inequalities. A separate strict log-concavity argument controls potential ties at boundary points. The two-point sharpness witness is expanded explicitly. The accompanying exact-integer program passed 60,986 positive-parameter and 52,686 boundary test cases; finite tests are corroboration rather than proofs.

**Originality.** Broadie and Petkova, arXiv:2603.09019v1, Theorem 1/Proposition 3, explicitly give separation at most \(5/2\), without the strict-interior adjacency or the sharp full-simplex \(3/2\) bound. The full article, including its proof and applications, was compared statement by statement. Gnedin, arXiv:2408.06477v1, concerns cross-modality and Darroch bounds for extended Bernoulli sums, not the claimed pairwise interlacing of integer/half-integer conditional modes. Targeted semantic record searches and literature searches uncovered no equivalent bound. Residual risk: a classical theorem on coefficient ratios of Hurwitz-stable polynomial parts could make the present bound an unreported corollary.

**Value.** Conditional score modes for independent win/tie/loss trials are natural prediction summaries. Replacing \(5/2\) with the sharp \(3/2\) boundary value and exact \(1/2\) interior adjacency reveals a structural connection not obtained by comparing each mode separately with the mean, and identifies an exact qualitative change at zero-probability outcomes. It is not merely a numerical example or parameter renaming. The result does not address modal-to-mean inequalities or matchup optimization directly.
