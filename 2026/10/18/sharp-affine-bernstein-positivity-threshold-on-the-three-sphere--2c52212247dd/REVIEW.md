# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** A zonal positive-definite function on the three-sphere is exactly a nonnegative summable mixture of the normalized Chebyshev polynomials \(U_m/(m+1)\). For Bernstein degrees one to four, every normalized basis image is expanded explicitly; the potentially negative degree-three and degree-four coefficients are controlled for all mode indices by \(|U_m(\cos\theta)|\le1/|\sin\theta|\) and finitely many exact exceptional modes. For degrees at least five, a centered finite-difference generating function gives the strictly negative top spherical coefficient of the admissible input \(U_{n+2}/(n+3)\). The limits of finite exact checks are clearly separated from this all-index proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The closest 2026 preprint (Otten–Nguyen–Watts, arXiv:2608.04836, full Section 4 inspected) treats the circle's angular Bernstein map and a degree-four counterexample on the two-sphere. The earlier general high-degree negative coefficient formula is antecedent, so it is not counted as new. The independently established affirmative borderline result for the three-sphere at degree four and the resulting exact threshold are not implied by those statements. Beatson–zu Castell (arXiv:1510.08658, Sections 1–2 inspected) construct different derivative/integral dimension-walk operators. No identified source establishes the present dimension-three affirmative degree-four theorem; unindexed priority remains possible.

## Value

**PASS.** An ordinary interval approximation operator need not preserve valid spherical covariance kernels. The result solves the smallest higher-dimensional case beyond the two-sphere, including its previously unresolved borderline degree, and provides an exact sharp decision rule for every positive approximation degree. The mode-level positivity and equality classification explain why that borderline is safe, while the next degree is not. This is a complete natural finite-dimensional classification rather than an arbitrary numerical example.

## Scope and limitations

The result concerns \(\mathbb S^3\), the dot-product affine Bernstein map, and positive semidefiniteness. It neither settles higher sphere dimensions nor proves strict positive-definiteness preservation. The computational checker provides independent arithmetic cross-checks, not a substitute for proof. Historical priority is not independently certified.
