# Sharp affine-rank failure and dominant hyperplanes of Bernoulli Boolean polytopes

## Finding

Let \(p\in(0,1)\) be fixed, \(q=1-p\), and form \(P_n=\operatorname{conv}(S_n)\subset\mathbb R^n\) by retaining every vertex of \(\{0,1\}^n\) independently with probability \(p\). Set \(N=2^n\), \(K=n(n+1)\), and assign dimension \(-1\) to the empty convex hull. For every \(n\ge3\),
\[
\left|\Pr(\dim P_n<n)-Kq^{N/2}\right|
\le {K\choose2}q^{3N/4}+{N\choose n}q^{5N/8}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(p\in(0,1)\), there is \(c_p>0\) such that
\[
\Pr(\dim P_n<n)=n(n+1)q^{2^{n-1}}
\bigl(1+O_p(e^{-c_p2^n})\bigr),\qquad n\to\infty.
\]

The conditional geometry is equally sharp: conditioned on \(\dim P_n<n\), with probability \(1-O_p(e^{-c_p2^n})\), its affine hull is **exactly one** of the following \(n(n+1)\) hyperplanes:
\[
 x_i=0,\quad x_i=1\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad
 x_i=x_j,\quad x_i+x_j=1\quad(1\le i<j\le n).
\]
Their affine-hull events are disjoint. Given that the affine hull belongs to this family, every one of the \(K\) possibilities has **exactly the same probability**. In particular, the conditional probability that the affine hull is of the two-coordinate equality/complement type, given membership in this family, is exactly \((n-1)/(n+1)\), not merely asymptotically. The unconditional rare-event conditional probability of this type is \((n-1)/(n+1)+O_p(e^{-c_p2^n})\).

## Assumptions and scope

The retention probability \(p\) is fixed independently of \(n\); the conclusions are not asserted uniformly for sequences tending to zero or one. Hyperplanes are affine hyperplanes over \(\mathbb R\), not over the field with two elements. The nonasymptotic inequality holds for every integer \(n\ge3\). The event includes zero-dimensional, empty, and all other rank-deficient outcomes.

## Proof

**1. Which hyperplanes contain many Boolean vertices?** Consider \(H=\{x:\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i=b\}\) with a nonzero real normal, and let \(k\) be the number of its nonzero coefficients. Independently complementing coordinates turns all these coefficients positive and only changes the level \(b\). Its trace on the cube is therefore a level set of a positive weighted subset sum. If two distinct subsets lie in the same level, neither strictly contains the other. Thus the level family is an antichain, and Sperner's theorem gives
\[
 |H\cap\{0,1\}^n|\le 2^{n-k}{k\choose\lfloor k/2\rfloor}.
\]
For \(k\ge3\), the central binomial fraction is at most \(3/8\), so the trace has at most \(3N/8\) vertices. For \(k=1\), a half-cube trace occurs only at \(x_i=0\) or \(x_i=1\). For \(k=2\), the four subset sums \(0,a_i,a_j,a_i+a_j\), after sign correction, have a duplicated value only when the two positive coefficients agree; the corresponding half-cube trace is \(x_i=x_j\) or \(x_i+x_j=1\). Other two-coordinate hyperplanes contain at most \(N/4\) vertices. Hence precisely the stated \(K=2n+2{n\choose2}=n(n+1)\) hyperplanes have \(N/2\) cube vertices, and every other hyperplane has at most \(3N/8\).

**2. Intersections of dominant hyperplanes.** View the equations defining those \(K\) hyperplanes as linear equations modulo two of Hamming weight one or two, with right side zero or one. Two distinct such equations either have the same left side and conflicting right sides, giving no common cube vertex, or have linearly independent left sides, giving exactly \(N/4\) common cube vertices. Thus all distinct dominant intersections have size at most \(N/4\).

**3. Exhaustion of deficient affine spans.** An affinely deficient selected subset \(S_n\) lies in some affine hyperplane generated by \(n\) affinely independent Boolean vertices. To see this, extend an affine-independent basis of the selected subset to \(n\) affinely independent vertices of the full cube, whose affine hull has dimension \(n-1\). Since each such hyperplane is determined by one \(n\)-element set, there are at most \({N\choose n}\) candidates. For a fixed hyperplane \(H\), the probability that all selected vertices lie in it is exactly \(q^{N-|H\cap\{0,1\}^n|}\). By step 1, the union of the events for the nondominant candidate hyperplanes has probability at most \({N\choose n}q^{5N/8}\).

**4. Two-sided probability bound.** Let \(E_H=\{S_n\subseteq H\}\) for each dominant hyperplane. Each event has probability \(q^{N/2}\), and by step 2 the intersection of two distinct such events has probability at most \(q^{3N/4}\). The first two Bonferroni bounds give
\[
 Kq^{N/2}-{K\choose2}q^{3N/4}
 \le\Pr(\bigcup_H E_H)\le Kq^{N/2}.
\]
Step 3 shows that every rank failure not captured here lies in the nondominant union, yielding the nonasymptotic estimate in the finding. Since \({N\choose n}\le N^n=\exp(n^2\log2)\), both errors divided by \(Kq^{N/2}\) are \(O_p(e^{-c_pN})\) for some positive \(c_p\), establishing the sharp asymptotic.

**5. Exact and asymptotic exceptional geometry.** Each dominant trace \(H\cap\{0,1\}^n\) is affinely equivalent to \(\{0,1\}^{n-1}\). Write \(\rho_m=\Pr(\dim P_m<m)\) for the identical sampling model in dimension \(m\). Conditional on \(E_H\), the vertices in the trace are independently retained with probability \(p\), so
\[
 \Pr(\operatorname{aff}(S_n)=H)=q^{N/2}(1-\rho_{n-1}).
\]
These events are pairwise disjoint, proving the exact uniformity and type ratio. Furthermore the hyperplane-union argument of step 3, now in dimension \(n-1\), gives
\[
 \rho_{n-1}\le {2^{n-1}\choose n-1}q^{2^{n-2}}
 =O_p(e^{-c'_pN})
\]
for a suitable \(c'_p>0\). Combining this with steps 3–4, the probability of rank failure without one of the stated affine hulls is \(O_p(e^{-c_pN})\) relative to the rank-failure probability. The conditional assertions follow. No independence of the dominant hyperplane events is assumed.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` uses exact rational row reduction to enumerate every hyperplane spanned by Boolean vertices in dimensions \(2,3,4\), verifies that precisely \(n(n+1)\) contain half the cube, and confirms that all other hyperplanes have at most \(3N/8\) vertices in the tested dimensions. It independently verifies all pairwise intersections of the distinguished hyperplanes in dimensions \(3,4,5\). Finally it exhausts all \(2^{2^n}\) retention outcomes for dimensions \(n=3,4\), checking the exact split of affine spans between coordinate and two-coordinate hyperplanes. The all-dimensional theorem is established by steps 1–5, not inferred from these finite checks.

Run `python3 checker.py` from the directory containing that file (Python standard library only). The checker prints a table of verified finite counts and terminates with assertions if a check fails.

## Relationship to prior work

Ferber–Krivelevich–Sales–Samotij, Guo–Tomon, and Christoph–Diskin–Lichev–Sudakov study the same Bernoulli cube-vertex polytope model, but their main conclusions control **edge expansion with high probability** rather than the rare-event probability or geometry of affine-rank failure. Older work by Kaibel and Remshagen analyzes graph density for a fixed-size vertex sample. Dyer–Füredi–McDiarmid study volume thresholds for random convex hulls in cubes. These results do not state the exact factor \(n(n+1)\), classify the dominant affine-rank failures, or provide the conditional two-coordinate/one-coordinate split. The combinatorial trace bound uses the classical Sperner antichain inequality and does not claim that inequality as new.

## Limitations

The theorem does not address variable retention probabilities \(p=p(n)\), the distribution of codimension two and greater, nor edge-expansion probabilities. No claim of exhaustive bibliographic novelty is made: an equivalent earlier statement in the classical random Boolean-polytope or Littlewood–Offord literature remains possible. The historical 2006–2007 dissertation literature was identified but was not fully inspected.
