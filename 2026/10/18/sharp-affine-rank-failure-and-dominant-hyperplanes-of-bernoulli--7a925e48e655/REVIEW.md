# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The single theorem is the sharp fixed-density probability and conditional hyperplane mechanism for affine-rank failure of independently retained cube vertices. The proof reconstructs the entire classification of halfcube intersections from positive subset-sum antichains; establishes the exact \(n(n+1)\) count and second-largest intersection density \(3/8\); bounds pair intersections by \(1/4\); and covers every deficient affine span by one of at most \({2^n\choose n}\) cube-spanned hyperplanes. Bonferroni plus the union bound yields the stated finite inequality, with the claimed exponentially small relative remainder at fixed \(p\). The exact conditional type ratio follows from uniform isomorphism of the \(n(n+1)\) halfcube traces. The standalone exact-rational checker verified all hyperplane traces for \(n=2,3,4\), all distinguished intersections for \(n=3,4,5\), and every retained subset for \(n=3,4\). These finite checks are not substitutes for the arbitrary-dimension proof.

## Originality — PASS (subject to stated priority risk)

Comparisons cover both alternative formulations (random rank of a Boolean vertex matrix and containment of a percolated cube in a real affine hyperplane) and potentially dominating geometry results. The inspected complete HTML texts of arXiv:2509.09831v1, arXiv:2604.09520v1, and arXiv:2604.20589v1 analyze edge expansion, not sharp affine-rank failure. Kaibel–Remshagen's accessible abstract treats graph density, Dyer–Füredi–McDiarmid's accessible abstract treats expected volume thresholds, and Gillmann's accessible dissertation description treats typical/extremal 0/1-polytopes without enough full text to exclude a priority overlap categorically. Independent searches for rank failure, hyperplane containment, and halfcube trace classifications did not surface the stated probability/conditional mechanism. Lack of a match alone is not a novelty certificate. An earlier result in dissertation or concentration literature is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For the fixed-density random-polytope model studied in recent work, usual high-probability results erase extremely rare degeneracy mechanisms. This theorem computes the exact leading double-exponential degeneration rate and its growing sharp coefficient, and isolates the unexpectedly dominant two-coordinate affine relations, not merely the obvious empty coordinate faces. It gives a transparent failure-mode classification and an exact finite-dimensional uniformity law conditional on the dominant geometry. These are motivated natural invariants, not arbitrary numerical samples.

## Scope and limitations

Only fixed \(p\in(0,1)\); the proof does not treat sparse/varying regimes, codimension at least two, nor graph expansion. Earlier formulations under rank/Boolean-halfspace terminology may remain undiscovered. Exact-arithmetic replay alone cannot resolve bibliographic priority.
