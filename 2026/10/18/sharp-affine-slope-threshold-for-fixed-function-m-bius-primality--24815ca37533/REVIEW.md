# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The original floor recurrence is transformed to an equivalent proper-divisor recurrence by exact subtraction. The convolution identity gives \(h_c\equiv\mu\pmod{c-1}\) for \(c\ge3\), so every nonsquarefree integer is excluded. Restriction to squarefree integers gives an exact exponentially generated Boolean-convolution sequence, whose coefficients have strictly increasing absolute values after the prime layer. The three counterexamples \(4,30,8\) for slopes \(0,1,2\) respectively prove sharpness. Recomputed original and divisor recurrences, numerical congruences and exhaustive finite cases passed; none is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Andrews–Kauffman–Sahoo, arXiv:2609.25628v1, contains the generalized recurrence and an asserted unrestricted fixed-function biconditional. Its actual composite-case reasoning distinguishes nonidentity as a polynomial in arbitrary function values rather than exclusion for a fixed function. The published constant-function obstruction confirms that an unrestricted assertion cannot supply the present fixed-function classification. A broad published-finding corpus semantic search for affine slopes, equivalent recurrence kernels, fixed-function primality criteria and ordered-set-partition formulas returned this obstruction as the closest result, not the complete sharp positive-and-negative affine threshold. Standard Möbius inversion and Dirichlet inverse identities provide tools but no identified covering affine classification. Uninspected equivalent consequences from general algebraic literature remain possible; failed search is not a proof of priority.

**Value — PASS.** The question asks which genuinely fixed members of a newly proposed arithmetic-function family are actual primality indicators. The theorem supplies an infinite positive class together with a sharp and complete integer affine boundary. Its arithmetic explanation separates repeated-factor divisibility from high-prime-support squarefree obstructions. The threshold is not merely a counterexample to an overbroad assertion, and the symbolic squarefree law is useful for analyzing further fixed-function subfamilies.

**Scope and limitations.** Only nonnegative integer slopes and integers \(n\ge2\) are covered. There is no complexity claim, and no claim of independent validation. Some source comparisons were limited to public summaries.
