## Mathematical value — PASS
Four complete mutually unbiased bases are a canonical minimal family for linear qutrit-state tomography, hence a natural and mathematically motivated set of sampling nodes. Exact-degree harmonic interpolation is a distinct, higher-order problem. The classification identifies the precise loss of four independent basis-average modes at degree one, the remaining global-average obstruction at degree two, and universal interpolation from degree three onward. The sharp uniform normalized lower/upper bounds of \(2/27\) and \(28/15\), and the exact worst Gram condition number of \(63/4\), quantify a genuinely non-obvious high-degree near-degeneracy at degree five. These exact constants are relevant to minimum-energy interpolation and the selection of stable harmonic degrees, rather than being arbitrary numerical recomputations.
