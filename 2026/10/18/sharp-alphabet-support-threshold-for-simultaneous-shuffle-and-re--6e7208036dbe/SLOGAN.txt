The largest number of distinct letters in a word of length \(2n\) that is simultaneously a shuffle square and a reverse shuffle square is exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\). Thus the shortest joint word containing every letter of a \(q\)-letter alphabet has length \(4q-4\) for \(q\ge2\), and the common language has fixed-length large-alphabet growth exponent \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\).
