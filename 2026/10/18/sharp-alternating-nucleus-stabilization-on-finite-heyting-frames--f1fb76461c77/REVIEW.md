# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The composition-to-alternating-path identity follows by direct quantifier expansion. An absent interval bridge yields an explicit separating upset, giving necessity as well as sufficiency. The adjacent-equality argument proves the universal exponent; a maximum strict chain with disjoint alternating colors proves optimality for each height. Idempotence of a product is exactly the one-step bridge case after applying the height bound. The independent finite verifier exhaustively confirms 57,332 ordered pairs on all posets of size at most four.

**Originality: PASS, with residual uncertainty.** The subset parametrization of nuclei and the existence of nuclear reflections are established prior results (Lindenhovius 2014, Ochs 2021, Escardó 2003), and are not claimed new. The contribution is the sharp height-indexed finite closure rate together with a necessary-and-sufficient alternating-path criterion and its pairwise-interval idempotence specialization. Searches of mathematical records, accessible prior results, and the relevant primary texts did not find this exact package. An equivalent general closure-operator theorem remains a priority risk; absence from search is not proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** This is an all-finite-posets structural classification of the cost of turning a pair of nucleus compositions into their nuclear join. It supplies a sharp bound based on order height instead of the often exponentially larger number of upsets, as well as an exact test for when the join needs no iteration. The sharp examples show the bound cannot be improved using height alone.

The general frame-join existence theory is not a new contribution, and the finite checks are not a substitute for the abstract proof.
