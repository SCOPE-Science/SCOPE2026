# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof is valid for every integer \(d\ge1\), all complex polynomial coefficients including vanishing endpoints, and every pair of positive distinct radii. Discrete Fourier orthogonality isolates exactly the two colliding Nyquist modes. The two angular alternation equations are invertible if and only if the stated sine is nonzero; rational circle reflection proves continuous two-radius phase uniqueness, and an explicit polynomial pair proves necessity. The Gram spectrum establishes the unique half-Nyquist best-conditioning offsets. The standalone script performed 990 consistency checks, which supplement rather than replace the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

The closest inspected finite construction is Pohl–Li–Boche's 2017 **asymmetric** \(2d+1\)/\(2d-1\) concentric two-circle design. Bodmann–Hammen's 2013 low-redundancy construction uses **intersecting** circles. Chalendar–Partington's 2024 theorem assumes **complete** modulus data on both circles. These results do not state or logically yield, without additional alias analysis, the present balanced \(2d\)/\(2d\) exact angular if-and-only-if, aligned-grid counterexamples, and sharp normalized Nyquist conditioning. Targeted literature and published-claim searches did not identify that combination. A hidden equivalent in pre-existing signal-processing literature remains possible, and independent priority has not been certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result resolves a whole, naturally defined low-measurement geometry, gives explicit necessary and sufficient design criteria, and determines the unique best-staggered angular offsets for the critical linear subsystem. It advances beyond the measurement count of pre-existing constructions. It does not prove stability for the complete nonlinear phase-retrieval inverse.

References: Bodmann–Hammen (arXiv:1302.5487); Pohl–Li–Boche (DOI:10.1109/SAMPTA.2017.8024411); Chalendar–Partington (arXiv:2403.16255). Those prior contributions are credited. Complete stability, arbitrary nonconcentric geometries, and independent publication-priority confirmation are outside scope.
