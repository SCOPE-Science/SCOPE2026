# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The deletion-output equality criterion is proved for all burst positions, yielding the standard shifted-mismatch count. The sign-expansion identity follows from exact independent fair-vertex character orthogonality. The two-lag graph embeds in modular addition by \(b\) on \(b+c\) vertices; each residue-class cycle has the claimed edge counts. The first completed cycle occurs at \(b+c-\gcd(b,c)+1\), and its positive contribution proves dependence even when the covariance is zero. All boundary quantifiers are explicit. Independent brute-force checks cover all \(165\) parameter triples with \(3\le n\le11\), but the general theorem is proved symbolically.

## Originality
PASS against inspected sources, with residual priority risk. Mercer's Proposition 1.1 gives single-lag edge-sign independence, not joint independence at two different shifts. Schoeny et al. and Xi et al. give the deterministic fixed-burst deletion-ball formula but do not assert a cross-width distribution. Inspected published results about two unrestricted deletions, single-burst correcting-code cardinality, and fixed-composition runs concern different statistics or input laws. The full implied statements, not just titles or matching words, were compared. An unindexed pre-existing formulation in the language of two-lag autocorrelations or high-temperature expansions remains possible.

## Value
PASS. Different burst widths create correlated observations of the same random binary source, yet their descendant-count sizes have zero covariance. The exact arithmetic boundary between independence and higher-order dependence, together with the first explicit joint-law correction, quantifies when independent-binomial models are valid. The result supplies a structural graph-cycle criterion that is useful in multi-width synchronization and burst-deletion analysis, not simply another computation of a known marginal or an arbitrary finite parameter slice.

## Scientific limits
The source is iid uniform binary; neither nonbinary nor biased-source extensions are proved. The compiled exhaustive checker covers finite short lengths and cannot substitute for the infinite argument. Independent external audit remains outstanding.
