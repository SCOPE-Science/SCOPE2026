## Correctness

**PASS.** The primitive exact overlap identity is proved from all \(B\) inverse branches, including the divisible-by-\(q\) boundary. Invariance of normalized Lebesgue measure is direct for integer multiplication maps. Multiplicative residue/reciprocal weighting yields the rational resolvent and strict nonnegativity. A prime-valuation argument proves *both* sides of the least cutoff. For the doubling/tripling mixture, every mixed word vanishes and the two surviving constant words yield a strictly positive parity formula. The unique minimizer follows from the crossing of the two exponential bases. Exact-rational computations corroborate, but do not stand in for, these proofs.

A distinct previous result about random integer beta maps excludes zero asymptotic variance for general BV observables but does not imply the exact two-point covariance profile or optimization of its exponential rate. Another result on coprime integer expanding maps studies random finite-grid Cramér discrepancy, not stationary cell observations. Aimino–Nicol–Vaienti’s 2013 full-text Example 7.3 concerns random integer beta maps and establishes a quenched limit theorem, not the present exact covariance. General interval-map correlation estimates could imply qualitative exponential decay, but not the sharp \(p=2/5\) observable-specific optimum by their stated conclusions. An unlocated equivalent Fourier/transfer-operator calculation remains possible. Literature retrieval is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Exact stationary two-time correlations are intrinsic observables, and a natural rational cell records arithmetic aliasing under integer beta maps. The prime-valuation theorem characterizes precisely when all its positive-lag correlations eventually become *identically zero*. The simplest coprime two-base example shows a nontrivial interior switching optimum with a cusp rate and a quantitative speedup over both deterministic endpoints. This is not a claim about universal optimal mixing or a general theorem for arbitrary observables.
