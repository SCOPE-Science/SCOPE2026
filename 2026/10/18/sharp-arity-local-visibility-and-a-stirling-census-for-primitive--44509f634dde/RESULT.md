# Sharp arity-local visibility and a Stirling census for primitive inclusion teams

## Finding
Fix integers \(k\geq2\) and \(1\leq r<k\), and let \(V=\{0,1\}^k\). A team is any subset \(T\subseteq V\). Consider the positive propositional team language generated by the truth constant, conjunction, **global** (truth-functional) disjunction, and nonempty primitive inclusion atoms of arity at most \(r\). In such an atom the left tuple consists of Boolean constants and the right tuple consists of distinct propositional variables. It asserts that **some** member of the team has the indicated partial assignment. No negation, general inclusion atom, or other connective is included.

For \(J\subseteq[k]\), let \(\pi_J(T)=\{t|_J:t\in T\}\). Define the \(r\)-projection closure
\[
C_r(T)=\{v\in V: v|_J\in\pi_J(T)\text{ for every }J\subseteq[k],\ |J|=r\}.
\]
Then the following statements hold.

1. **Exact observable invariant.** Two teams satisfy exactly the same formulas of the indicated language if and only if their families \((\pi_J(T))_{|J|=r}\) agree, equivalently if and only if their closures \(C_r(T)\) agree. The closure is the unique largest team in each theory class.
2. **First invisible tuple and rigidity.** Every team with at most \(r\) members is closed: \(C_r(T)=T\). A nonclosed team of size \(r+1\) exists. For a team \(T\) with exactly \(r+1\) members and \(v\notin T\), the tuple \(v\) lies in \(C_r(T)\) precisely when the \(r+1\) nonempty difference supports
\[
A_t(v)=\{i\in[k]:t_i\ne v_i\},\qquad t\in T,
\]
are pairwise disjoint. Hence the minimal invisible-point threshold is **exactly \(r+1\)**. For \(r\geq2\), a minimally sized nonclosed team has exactly one invisible tuple.
3. **Exact census.** Write \(\left\{\!\begin{smallmatrix}k\\j\end{smallmatrix}\!\right\}\) for a Stirling number of the second kind. The number of ordered pairs \((T,v)\) with \(|T|=r+1\), \(v\in C_r(T)\setminus T\), equals
\[
\boxed{2^k\left(\left\{\!\begin{smallmatrix}k\\r+1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right\}+(r+2)\left\{\!\begin{smallmatrix}k\\r+2\end{smallmatrix}\!\right\}\right).}
\]
For \(r\geq2\), the same expression counts the minimally sized nonclosed teams themselves. In particular, increasing the available primitive-atom arity from \(r\) to \(r+1\) strictly increases expressive power: each extremal pair \(T,T\cup\{v\}\) is distinguished by an atom of arity exactly \(r+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The Boolean variables are labeled and finite in number, every team is a **set** of valuations, and \(1\leq r<k\). The nonempty primitive inclusion atom corresponding to a coordinate set \(J\) and pattern \(a\in\{0,1\}^J\) is true of \(T\) exactly when \(a\in\pi_J(T)\). Arity zero, if used, only tests nonemptiness; it does not alter the asserted classification. The truth constant is true on all teams, including the empty team. Global disjunction means semantic **or**, not splitting a team. The theorem is uniform in the labeled variables; the census counts labeled teams and an additionally selected invisible valuation, not isomorphism classes.

## Proof
**Observable invariant.** An atom of arity at most \(r\) asks whether a coordinate pattern occurs. Any smaller coordinate set extends to an \(r\)-element one, so its truth follows from the \(r\)-projections. Conjunction and global disjunction preserve dependence on this information by structural induction. Conversely, if two \(r\)-projection profiles differ, an atom naming a pattern in their symmetric difference separates them. By definition, \(T\subseteq C_r(T)\), and \(\pi_J(C_r(T))=\pi_J(T)\) for every \(|J|=r\); applying the definition twice shows idempotence. Equal projection families have equal closures, and equality of closures gives equality of projection families. If \(T\) and \(S\) have equal profiles, then \(S\subseteq C_r(T)\), so the closure is their unique maximal representative.

**Lower bound.** Suppose \(|T|=m\leq r\) and \(v\notin T\). For each \(t\in T\), select a coordinate where \(t\) and \(v\) differ. The at most \(m\) selected coordinates can be extended to an \(r\)-element set \(J\subseteq[k]\). No member of \(T\) agrees with \(v\) on all of \(J\). Therefore \(v\notin C_r(T)\). This also handles \(T=\varnothing\).

**Exact extremal structure.** For \(v\notin T\), define the nonempty difference supports \(A_t(v)\). The condition \(v\in C_r(T)\) says that no \(r\)-element coordinate set meets every \(A_t(v)\); equivalently, the transversal number of the support family exceeds \(r\). When \(|T|=r+1\), choosing one element of each support gives a transversal of size at most \(r+1\). If two supports intersect, choose their common coordinate and one coordinate from each of the remaining \(r-1\) supports; this gives a transversal of size at most \(r\). Conversely, if the \(r+1\) supports are pairwise disjoint, any set of at most \(r\) coordinates misses at least one support, so the corresponding row agrees with \(v\) on those coordinates. Thus the supports are pairwise disjoint if and only if \(v\) is invisible. Existence follows by taking \(v=0^k\) and the \(r+1\) rows with a single \(1\) in each of \(r+1\) distinct coordinates.

**Uniqueness when \(r\geq2\).** Suppose distinct \(v,w\) are invisible to the same \((r+1)\)-row team. For a coordinate \(i\) at which \(v_i\ne w_i\), at most one row differs from \(v\) at \(i\), because the supports relative to \(v\) are disjoint. Thus at least \(r\geq2\) rows differ from \(w\) at \(i\). This contradicts the pairwise disjointness of supports relative to \(w\). The restriction is necessary: when \(r=1\), the team \(\{01,10\}\) has both \(00\) and \(11\) invisible.

**Enumeration.** Fix \(v\). In the Boolean setting, each row \(t\) is uniquely determined by its nonempty difference support. A minimally hiding team is therefore an *unordered* family of \(r+1\) disjoint nonempty blocks from \([k]\), with an optional unused block. If no coordinate is unused, there are \(\left\{\!\begin{smallmatrix}k\\r+1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right\}\) partitions. If there are unused coordinates, partition the \(k\) coordinates into \(r+2\) nonempty blocks and mark one of those blocks as unused, giving \((r+2)\left\{\!\begin{smallmatrix}k\\r+2\end{smallmatrix}\!\right\}\). Multiply by the \(2^k\) choices of \(v\). By uniqueness, for \(r\geq2\) this also counts teams. Finally, select one coordinate from each of the disjoint difference supports. The resulting \(r+1\) distinct coordinates define a pattern realized by \(v\) but by no row of \(T\), so the corresponding arity-\((r+1)\) atom separates \(T\) from \(T\cup\{v\}\). This proves strictness.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py`. The standalone program enumerates every team of size at most \(r+1\) for \(k=2,3,4\) and every valuation not in the team. It independently evaluates all \(r\)-coordinate projections, checks closure for teams of size at most \(r\), compares the exact support criterion on teams of size \(r+1\), checks invisible-point uniqueness for \(r\geq2\), and compares exhaustive counts with Stirling formulas. It completed **42,980 candidate tuple comparisons** without a mismatch; for \((k,r)=(4,2)\) it found exactly \(160\) hiding pairs, as predicted. The universal proof is the transversal and partition argument above, not extrapolation from finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Häggblom's 2026 work introduces the nonempty primitive inclusion atoms and establishes expressive completeness for unrestricted arity, including the normal form using all propositional variables (Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.12, Theorem 2.13). The present result concerns the **restricted-arity observation kernel** and quantifies its smallest loss of information, extremal rigidity, and labeled first-obstruction census, none of which is supplied by the inspected unrestricted-arity statements. Häggblom's earlier 2025 work addresses proof-rule arity (number of premises) and implication complexity for generalized inclusion dependencies; this differs from bounding the **length of primitive observation tuples**. Aho, Beeri, and Ullman's classical theory explains projection and natural-join information loss, but the inspected general lossless-join results do not give the specific support-disjointness characterization or Stirling census. The projection closure above is a natural join of all \(r\)-attribute projections; the result is consequently relevant to both team semantics and database reconstruction.

## Limitations
The language deliberately excludes general inclusion atoms, negation, implication, additional modalities, and other team connectives; the claimed invariant must not be transferred to those enrichments. The census is for binary labeled valuations and the first nonclosed layer only; it does not enumerate all closed teams or all theory classes. The uniqueness statement requires \(r\geq2\). Related results on join dependencies, coordinate shadows, or combinatorial reconstruction may give earlier equivalent formulations; no priority or independence is claimed. Independent validation has not been performed.
