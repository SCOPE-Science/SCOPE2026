# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The model and valuation hypotheses lead to the explicit basis. Three characteristic-two Cartier formulas are derived from the defining polynomial. The modular index is injective, its allowed values are disjoint from their negatives, and inverse doubling turns after \(n\) steps into negation modulo \(2^n+1\). This proves the universal upper bound without relying on finite enumeration. The displayed exact surviving chains prove sharpness and, for \(n\ge5\), rank at least two. A standard-library verifier independently checks the map and examples through \(n=15\). The full final rank sequence is not asserted.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Garcia–Tafazolian's Theorem 3.3 gives \(\mathcal C^n=0\) for all relevant maximal curves, but not equality of the nilpotency index. Shafighi proves the first Cartier map and the \(a\)-number of precisely these curves, but the inspected source does not state the nonvanishing of the penultimate iterate or the antipodal transversal. Almeida Filho–Tafazolian–Vieira's formulas concern odd-characteristic degree-\(p\) Artin–Schreier curves, not the characteristic-two degree-four covers here. Semantic searches of published mathematical findings and comparisons with recorded mathematical claims yielded no covering implication. The 2009 originating paper's full text was not accessible, and an unindexed equivalent result remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The nilpotency index is an invariant of the Cartier filtration of the Jacobian and is not determined by the previously known first Cartier rank or \(a\)-number. Proving sharpness in an infinite natural family resolves whether the general maximality bound is attained there; the antipodal structure gives a conceptual reason and two explicit longest chains distinguish the penultimate iterate from the already-known vanishing result. The proof does not claim a complete Ekedahl–Oort classification or exact final rank in the general case.
