# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** On every unweighted finite path of length \(n\ge3\), the fixed-radius sliding averages of a valley profile are valley-shaped. The proof treats both kinds of endpoint clipping and shows the centered maximal function itself has a single trough. Exact endpoint-prefix identities and the global mean give the sharp variation and mass-dependent deficit. Equality and boundary cases are reconstructed analytically. The supplementary exact-rational checker verifies \(13{,}652\) short profiles but is not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The March 2026 finite-graph paper states the all-path conjecture and proves only low-order cases; its Conjecture 5 and path-graph computations were materially inspected. Bilz–Weigt treats infinite-lattice indicator and attachment classes, and Ramos treats valley-shaped maximal functions on detachment intervals, not the full finite-path single-trough theorem. Prior monotone and single-peak cases do not imply the two-high-endpoint valley theorem, its sharp endpoint-mass gap, or equality characterization. Searches under valley, quasiconvex, centered averaging, sharp variation, and finite-path terminology found no equivalent statement; this is not a priority guarantee. Earlier unindexed work remains a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** The result settles a natural genuinely nonmonotone class of an explicitly posed all-length sharp-variation problem, proves a structural shape-preservation lemma despite boundary truncation, and gives a quantitatively sharp endpoint-mass gap. It rules out all one-trough profiles as extremizers except the monotone endpoint-spike boundary. The unrestricted problem remains open.
