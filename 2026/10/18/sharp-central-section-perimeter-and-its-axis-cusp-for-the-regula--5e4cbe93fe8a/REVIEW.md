PASS. The fourteen vertices and twenty-four edges are derived explicitly from the pairwise-coordinate inequalities. A tetrahedral four-segment Minkowski-sum identity turns planar projection perimeter into a tight-frame square-root sum, proving the sharp \(8\sqrt2\) bound and its exact equality orbit. The sum-of-fourth-powers identity and global strong concavity establish the stated deficit. Independent edge intersection enumeration in both normal chambers yields the complete six- and eight-sided perimeter expressions; differentiation in the eight-sided chamber proves the uniform cusp. The checker reconstructs 3,200 sections independently of the symbolic formulas and reproduces the special values. Numerical checks supplement, not replace, the continuum argument.

## Originality

PASS, relative to the comparisons actually made. The 1999 rhombic-dodecahedron universal-cover manuscript establishes different geometric functionals. The 2018 maximal-sectional-perimeter theorem applies to a cube, not this Voronoi cell, and its optimizer orbit differs. The 2019 FCC-volume manuscript concerns tetrahedral tessellations. Existing rhombic-dodecahedral section-area coordinates allow elementary construction of side lengths but do not themselves assert or prove the global sharp perimeter maximum, its all-direction quantitative deficit, or the axis cusp. No compared mathematical record was found to imply these combined perimeter claims. This is not a proof of absolute historical novelty; generic Cauchy and zonotope tools are prior knowledge.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Maximum possible central-section boundary length is an intrinsic and natural extremal measurement of a standard lattice Voronoi cell. Unlike maximal sectional area and the cubic section-perimeter problem, the maximizing directions are the fourfold vertex axes. The complete two-chamber perimeter law, rigidity of equality, explicit global anisotropic stability, and nonsmooth first variation supply independently usable benchmarks for convex-section analysis and lattice-cell geometry. The result is narrower than a general theorem for all zonotopes but is not merely a numerical special orientation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Hausel–Makai–Szűcs (1999) treats universal covers and inscribed cubes; König–Koldobsky (2018) treats central perimeters of cubes; Leppmeier (2019) concerns FCC tetrahedral volume comparisons. The central section-area construction for the same body is the closest geometric source of vertex-location data, but does not establish the new perimeter extremum and regularity classification. Residual risks: general inequalities may yield a shorter proof, and older precise section-perimeter results could exist outside the searched corpus. No noncentral or affinely distorted sections are addressed.
