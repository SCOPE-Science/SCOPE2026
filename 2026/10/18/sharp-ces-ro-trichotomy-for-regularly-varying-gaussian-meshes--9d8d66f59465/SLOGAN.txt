For admissible regularly varying Gaussian meshes in a firmly nonexpansive orbit, the Cesàro means have a sharp density-controlled trichotomy: when \(n d_n\to\infty\), their squared norms are asymptotic to \(\rho_\infty^2\sqrt\pi/((1+\alpha)n d_n)\); when \(n d_n\to a<\infty\), their squared norms tend to \(\rho_\infty^2\int_0^1e^{-a^2(\log r)^2}\,dr\). This quantitatively resolves the power-law convergence examples and the vanishing-density boundary, while the previously known pure-harmonic exact limit remains acknowledged prior work.
