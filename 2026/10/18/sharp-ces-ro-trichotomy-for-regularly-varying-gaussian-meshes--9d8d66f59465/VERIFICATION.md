# Verification of the analytic claim

The proof starts with the exact Gram formula from the constructed orbit, not a numerical fit. The new Gaussian row lemma has three separately controlled regions: indices below the base point, indices between the base point and twice it, and all larger indices. The last region is bounded by \(Ci\exp(-c(i d_i)^2)\), ensuring negligible mass relative to \(1/d_i\) when \(i d_i\to\infty\). Karamata's theorem yields \(\sum_{i\le n}1/d_i\sim n/((1+\alpha)d_n)\). The amplitude-weight error is negligible against this same sum, giving the high-density coefficient \(\rho_\infty^2\sqrt\pi/(1+\alpha)\).

For finite \(a=\lim n d_n\), each proportional window of mesh positions tends to \(a\log(s/t)\); a bounded-kernel Riemann sum yields the integral in the result. The \(a=0\) case and the large-\(a\) matching coefficient were checked directly from that integral. The finite-index power-mesh recurrence was checked algebraically, not merely at sampled values.
