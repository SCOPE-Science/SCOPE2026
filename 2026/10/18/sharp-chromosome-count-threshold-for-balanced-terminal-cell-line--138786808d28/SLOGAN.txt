For a telomerase-free synchronous binary cell genealogy with \(K\) independently segregating chromosomes, each carrying two initially equal budgets \(m\), the existence of any viable descendant at the maximal depth \(2m\) has a sharp first-order chromosome-count transition at \(K\sim4(\log2)m/\log m\). Below this scale by any fixed relative margin, the viable terminal-cell count concentrates around its mean; above it, no viable terminal cell remains with high probability.
