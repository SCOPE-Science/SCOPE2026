# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed-point lift equation is exact, and its two angular solutions exhaust each winding class. The Jacobian has determinant \(b\), trace \(1+b+K\cos\theta\), and the strict quadratic Schur test gives exactly the two-sided mode inequality, with no boundary inclusions. The interval overlap argument proves the maximum for every positive real width; an independent executable checker probes the strict integer-square cases without substituting finite enumeration for proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual bibliographic risk)
Zaslavsky (1978) derives the kicked map but studies attractors rather than a simultaneous sink census. A publicly available transport result for the same model proves bounds for invariant-measure mean drift; its extremal fixed points do not give the local spectral test or any maximum count. Wenzel, Biham and Jayaprakash (1991) report periodic-orbit diagrams and Arnold-tongue scaling; Celletti and Di Ruzza (2011) analyze periodic-orbit drift intervals. The accessible abstracts do not announce the zero-torque all-winding simultaneous capacity or its alternating strict thresholds. Their full texts were not accessible, so potential prior implication remains a concrete risk rather than a claim of exhaustive exclusion. A numerical 2015 kicked-rotor case with many coexisting attractors gives contextual motivation, not the exact census.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Coexistence capacity is a natural measure of multistability. The theorem sharpens a familiar individual-mode local calculation into an *exact global census over all integer winding modes and an optimal forcing strength*: the two thresholds distinguish even and odd multiplicity, identify open-boundary exclusions and give the weak-damping divergence rate. The count concerns only period-one sinks, which is stated explicitly; it does not infer facts about arbitrary attractors or basins.
