Symmetric transverse integration provides the uniform \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) line limit. Two exact polynomial identities establish the global orientation maximum and equality cases; the angle interval is obtained by a sign test. The bundled pure-Python verifier reproduces the identities exactly and illustrates, but does not prove, Gaussian convergence.

Cordero-Erausquin--Rotem (2020) provides the earlier nonconcavity mechanism. Putterman's arXiv:2608.08472 addresses stability of a different positive near-equality regime, but its full text was unavailable for comparison. Searches cannot establish uniqueness against unindexed literature.

**Value — PASS.** The exact maximum answers the natural quantitative strengthening of the Gaussian commutativity criterion. Its intrinsic matrix formula, square-deficit identity and two-axis rigidity are meaningful geometric information in a full continuous planar class, not simply a numerical instance.

The result optimizes only the degeneration family of orthogonal rectangles; it makes no unrestricted-convex-body claim. The proof uses ideas from the cited literature and no independent priority or certification is asserted.
