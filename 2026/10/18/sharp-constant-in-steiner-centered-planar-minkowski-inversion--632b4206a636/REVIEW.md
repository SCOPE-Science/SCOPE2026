# Scientific review: sharp planar Minkowski inverse coefficient

## Correctness — PASS
The published upper inequality follows from the Steiner-normalized Green kernel having geodesic Lipschitz seminorm exactly \(1/2\). The new matching lower bound is established by an explicit family of balanced, nondegenerate atomic measures; support function differences at angle zero and a feasible transport coupling yield the analytic lower bound \(2\pi E_\varepsilon/T_\varepsilon\to\pi\). Mass, first moments, nondegeneracy, angular distances, kernel normalization, and the two limiting quotients have been checked. Heat convolution preserves balance and gives strictly positive smooth curvature radii, extending sharpness to smooth strictly convex bodies. The executable checker supports but does not replace these exact arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk
C. An, arXiv:2610.11814v1, Proposition 6.1 proves the same *upper bound* and the paragraph after Proposition 6.3 explicitly states that the optimal *coefficient* is undetermined. Its smooth perturbation only excludes exponents exceeding one, producing coefficient \(\pi/3\) along that particular family. The new family closes the factor-three gap to \(\pi\). Full-text inspection of this initiating paper and targeted portions of quantitative Minkowski, random-normal reconstruction, and probabilistic Gauss--Minkowski sources found no implication of the exact best coefficient. Semantic searches of published records found no equivalent statement. These searches do not exclude all unindexed or historical formulations.

## Value — PASS
Determining a best global reconstruction constant explicitly left open by the initiating paper is a natural geometric inverse-problem question. The new construction identifies why nearly degenerate, but admissible, surface-area measures saturate the inverse Green kernel, and proves that imposing smooth strict convexity alone does not reduce the sharp constant. This has meaningful implications for the calibration of deterministic reconstruction error bounds without claiming stronger nonlinear or higher-dimensional results.

## Scope and remaining uncertainties
The result concerns one exact normalization: geodesic transport on the circle, Steiner centering, and perimeter \(2\pi\). The extremal constant is a supremum; no maximizing pair is claimed. Possible equivalent calculations in older transportation or Minkowski-inversion literature remain an originality risk. No independent audit has been performed.
