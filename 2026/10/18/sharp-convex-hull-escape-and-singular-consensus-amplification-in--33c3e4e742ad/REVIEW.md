# Review of sharp convex-hull escape and singular consensus amplification

**Correctness — PASS.** The update is reconstructed directly from Equation (1) of [1], with the sign of the pair difference retained even after exchanges of order. The pair difference is exactly multiplied by \(1-2\mu\), and the exponential utility gives the positive hyperbolic-tangent increment of the mean. The geometric upper bound proves convergence. Termwise strict monotonicity and endpoint limits give a unique phase boundary; two elementary hyperbolic-tangent bounds prove its small and large learning-rate asymptotics. The Riemann-sum argument yields the exact \((1-\mu)^{-1}\) amplification coefficient. Two rational integer comparisons certify the worked example strictly, without numerical extrapolation. The included script performs supplementary finite checks and prints the limiting-value approximations.

**Originality — PASS on accessible evidence, with residual risk.** The central source [1] provides the update and difference-contraction identity, and explores the *noisy* single-cluster diffusion, approximate Gibbs distribution and merging time; its accessible main text and full appendix do not state the noiseless exact consensus series or the two-parameter global phase boundary. The classical Deffuant variant [2] restricts attractive compromise to \(\mu_-\leq1/2\) and uses a separate repulsion rule for incompatible opinions; it does not cover over-relaxed *compatible* utility-weighted pairs. The review [3] treats the usual convex-combination consensus models and notes boundedness failures caused by additive disturbances; the present result identifies deterministic escape without additive disturbances. Relevant mathematical-index and literature queries found no covering statement. The exact identities in [1] are credited, and analogous exit phenomena in other opinion models are not presented as new. Full inspection of [3] was unavailable, and unindexed equivalent literature remains a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result fills a natural model-validity gap: contracting interpersonal distances alone do not ensure consensus remains in the initial opinion range. It gives a sharp universal learning-rate guarantee for arbitrary populations and utility functions, a unique utility-slope/learning-rate phase diagram in an exact two-agent specialization, two boundary scalings, and a singular high-learning consensus law. These are structural statements about the dynamics, not an arbitrary finite computation or merely the observation that a single update can overshoot. This result does not classify noisy trajectories, large-population outcomes above the half-threshold, or empirical attitudes.
