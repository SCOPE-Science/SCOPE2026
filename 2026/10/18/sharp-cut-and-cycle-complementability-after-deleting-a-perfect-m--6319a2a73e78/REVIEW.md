# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The unrestricted minimization reduces to the orthogonal cut and cycle projections because simultaneous paired-index permutations preserve every real edge \(\ell_p\) norm and any equivariant cross-map between the cut and cycle summands vanishes. The cross-map claim is proved without an unverified representation table: every equivariant linear map from a centered index vector into off-diagonal edge arrays must have entries \(a x_i+b x_j\), which already lie in the cut space. The graph Laplacian splits into three explicitly invertible nonconstant eigenspaces. Its column coefficients and signs give both exact endpoint constants for every integer \(n\ge3\). Exact rational checks at \(n=3,\ldots,9\) and independent pseudoinverse checks at \(n=3,\ldots,10\) passed. The checks are finite corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest primary literature establishes invariant-projection methods and analyzes tori, Hamming graphs, diamond graphs, and finite transportation quotients, not the general crown family. The known six-cycle and three-cube cases are explicitly excluded from the novelty assertion; the new content is the infinite range \(n\ge5\), the exact matching-deletion gaps, and the one-step shift in dominance of the complementary projections. Related exact cut/cycle results for complete multipartite, Johnson, and Kneser graphs and facet enumeration for matching-deleted bicliques do not numerically imply the present projection constants. Searches in mathematical research records and the inspected source literature did not reveal covering results. An unindexed equivalent formula remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The relative projection constants of graph cut and cycle spaces are natural quantitative complementability invariants for their corresponding finite transportation and Lipschitz presentations. Crowns are a canonical highly symmetric family obtained by deleting a perfect matching from a complete bipartite graph. The exact values show that one structural edge deletion lowers *both* best projection norms, by distinct explicit amounts, and shifts the cut-versus-cycle dominance threshold. These facts are not merely a restatement of the intact complete-bipartite formulas and supply an infinite, reproducible boundary comparison for projection methods. No downstream theorem or uniqueness among unrestricted minimizers is asserted.

## Closest literature and scientific limits

Relevant primary sources are arXiv:1807.03814 and arXiv:2305.12582. The latter's general equivariance theorem supplies a method, not the crown-specific optimal norm calculation. The former concerns general finite metric and recursively constructed graph spaces. The intermediate \(\ell_p\) optimal projection norms remain unevaluated; only the minimizing operators and endpoint values are claimed. Searches cannot rule out every equivalent historical result.
