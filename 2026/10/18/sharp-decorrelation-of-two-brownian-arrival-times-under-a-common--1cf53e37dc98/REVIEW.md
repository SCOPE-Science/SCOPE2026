# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The regenerative identity is a joint identity, not an unjustified product of independently reset paths. Conditioning on the same first clock gap gives the mixed moment, and the single-walker renewal transform gives the variance. Strict covariance positivity follows from conditional independence given the shared clock and nonconstancy of the conditional mean. The large-reset asymptotic follows from a unique saddle in the exact simultaneous-hitting integral, with exponential tail control and a separate bound on the cycle-time term. The definitions, all positive-parameter quantifiers and the irreversible-hit boundary are explicit in the proof. Exact-event simulation and independent quadrature provide checks but are not substitutes for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to stated literature risk.** The closest available full primary texts are Vatash et al. (2026, arXiv:2609.23542v1), which explicitly observes reset-induced correlation and studies ordered mean arrival times, and Biroli et al. (2023, arXiv:2303.18012v1), which computes the mean of the earliest arrival under global resets. Neither text presents the exact covariance of the two labeled irreversible hitting times or its sharp high-reset correlation asymptotic. published-finding corpus semantic searches for common-reset first-passage covariance and strong-reset exponential scaling retrieved closest records about different stationary-renewal correlation observables rather than this hitting-time statement. The abstract-accessible non-Poissonian common-reset correlation paper addresses particle positions, but inaccessible additional full text and unlocated general multivariate-renewal theory leave residual originality risk. Absence of a matching indexed title is not used as proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Shared Poisson resetting is a concrete experimental baseline for ranks and collective capture, and the dependence between the individual arrival times is a natural quantity that mean ordered passages do not identify. The theorem resolves both its exact positive sign and the unexpectedly fast rate at which this dependence becomes negligible when resetting is strong; its exponent \(\sqrt2-1\) is set by the rare event that two independent walkers complete a first passage in one clock interval. The normalization and prefactor make this a predictive quantitative observable, not a routine substitution into a one-particle MFPT.

**Limitations.** Two independent identical Brownian searchers only; perfect absorption; common Poisson resets without overhead. The asymptotic holds for high reset intensity, not uniformly at moderate intensity. No independent audit or claim of uniqueness over all theoretical literature is made.
