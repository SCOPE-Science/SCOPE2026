# Scientific review: sharp concentric Floquet-data degree

## Correctness — PASS
The construction has Cartesian degree exactly \(2m\) because the first component restricted to \(y=0\) has leading term \(Kx^{2m}\). Direct polar conversion gives zero radial velocity on every prescribed circle and strictly positive angular speed. The standard integral of the reciprocal affine cosine determines the least physical period. The transverse variational integral reduces to the claimed logarithmic multiplier, and the explicit parameter \(w_i\) solves that equation for any nonunit positive multiplier. This is an all-real-data analytical argument, not a finite search.

For the lower bound, the monic division argument establishes that every specified circle equation divides \(xP+yQ\). Pairwise coprimality, the degree bound and evaluation at the origin force this radial polynomial to vanish identically in any lower-degree field. A nonstationary circle then lies in a full annulus of periodic circles and cannot be hyperbolic. No genericity or symmetry assumption enters the lower bound. Numerical interpolation, independent Cartesian evaluation, quadrature and nonlinear Poincaré integration support the algebraic calculations, with their finite scope stated in `VERIFICATION.md`.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected record, subject to residual risks
Christopher (2001) proves the existence of fields whose degree matches a nonsingular algebraic curve's degree. Thus the bare \(2m\)-degree existence result for concentric circles is **prior work**, not claimed as novel. The present result instead allows the **independent exact numerical prescription of all periods and all nonunit transverse Floquet multipliers at the same sharp degree**. The published abstract of Christopher (2001) does not announce this parameter control, although its inaccessible full proof limits the comparison.

Margalef-Bentabol and Peralta-Salas (arXiv:1507.00698v1, 2015), Theorems 2.1 and 4.2, prescribe periods and stability properties, respectively, with different degree bounds and an exact-limit-cycle-set condition. Theorem 1.2 does not assert independent exact multiplier values with degree \(2m\). Nicklason (2016), Sections 5–6, provides related examples, not an all-size degree theorem. The 2026 radial-factorization approach is explicitly equivariant and cannot dominate a result allowing arbitrary multiplier sign patterns without that symmetry. Available indexed mathematical claims do not exhibit the exact current conjunction of geometric circles, independently fixed periods/multipliers and degree optimality. Novelty is not a claim of exhaustive historical priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Degree-optimal realization under quantitative dynamical constraints is a natural strengthening of classical algebraic limit-cycle synthesis. The theorem identifies a sharp geometric lower obstruction and constructs a field meeting it while controlling physical time and transverse stability independently. The possibility of two adjacent attracting or repelling designated circles distinguishes this non-equivariant construction from rotation-equivariant exact-cycle-set rigidity. It does not claim to remove additional orbits, a deliberately weaker containment problem than full configuration realization.
