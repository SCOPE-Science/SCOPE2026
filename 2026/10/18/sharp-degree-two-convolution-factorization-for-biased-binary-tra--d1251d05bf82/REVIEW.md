# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The two-state transition transfer matrix has trace one and determinant \(p(1-p)(1-z^2)\). The induced continuant expression makes any nonzero imaginary-axis root a simultaneous positive-real-parameter zero of two consecutive continuants, impossible by backwards recurrence to the constant initializer. Zero is not a root because constant sequences have positive probability. The polynomial's degree remains \(n-1\) throughout the connected bias interval; all roots start at \(-1\) in the fair case and cannot cross the imaginary axis. Real linear factors and conjugate quadratic factors have strictly positive coefficients, giving independent supported-on-\(\{0,1,2\}\) summands. Exact adjacent-transition covariance yields a strictly positive variance surplus over the sharp Poisson-binomial variance envelope for biased samples with at least three bits. The remaining boundary cases are explicitly Bernoulli/binomial. A separately coded exact-arithmetic checker reproduces the recurrence, transfer matrix, moments, and finite Hurwitz minors. Its finite checks do not replace the all-length proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
Shuldiner–Oldford [1] develop general dependent-indicator moment identities rather than a zero-location or independent-summand classification. Kim–Park [2, Theorem 2] give a real-rooted conditional success-run distribution; it does not imply the unconditional all-transition-count theorem. Kim [3] studies zeros of periodic one-dimensional spin-chain partition functions, which are not the open-boundary probability polynomial considered here. Searches for open-chain Fisher zeros, biased binary switch-count polynomials, Hurwitz stability, and independent two-point/three-point convolution decompositions, together with comparisons of the closest searchable published statements, did not reveal an identical or stronger claim. Classical one-dimensional transfer-matrix results remain a residual bibliographic risk; unsuccessful searches do not prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The number of adjacent changes is a foundational binary runs statistic. The all-sample, all-bias half-plane theorem yields a complete qualitative structural decomposition with a **sharp** and nontrivial support cutoff. The cutoff separates a fair-coin independent-transition law from every biased law for samples of length at least three. Its exact variance obstruction demonstrates why the more familiar independent-Bernoulli decomposition is impossible while independent quadratic summands remain available. This is a natural complete classification rather than a numerical exercise.

## Scope and limitations
The result concerns iid binary observations and the total count of both transition directions. No extension to non-iid or Markov samples, or an independent validation, is claimed. The finite checker is an additional diagnostic only.
