# Self-review

## Correctness: PASS
For \(k\ge2\), the odd-index inequalities \(kS_m+1\le S_{2m+1}\le kS_{m+1}-1\) and dyadic equality bound every normalized positive integer Galileo sequence between explicit digit and reflected-digit functions. Both are realized by entire positive integer sequences. Superadditivity, a sharp digit induction and a one-variable derivative establish the optimal normalized growth constants \(1/(k-1)\) and \(k-1/(k-1)\). All four extremal liminf/limsup claims have explicit achieving subsequences. The executable checker passed 32,000 extremal-prefix and 54,300 small exhaustive split comparisons; this finite evidence supplements, not replaces, the infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS, with identified residual risk
Cheah and Treeby (arXiv:2604.20889, 2026-04-19), materially read in its complete available HTML, provide the positive binary-tree representation and coarse growth estimates for monotone terms, but do not optimize pointwise partial sums or compute these sharp uniform coefficients. The digit representation itself is classical and is not claimed as an invention. A claim-level comparison against accessible earlier claims and semantic mathematical searches found no covering result; absence of a match is not proof of priority. The 1972 May and 1975 Zeitlin papers could not be fully examined, and could contain implicit earlier coverage.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The sharp lower and upper envelopes answer the natural extremal question left by a classification of all positive Galileo sequences. The result strengthens nonoptimal monotone-only power bounds to exact global constants for every positive integer sequence, classifies two simultaneous extremizers, and exposes substantial dyadic oscillation. For \(k=3\), this supplies informative growth laws despite the known absence of monotone solutions.
