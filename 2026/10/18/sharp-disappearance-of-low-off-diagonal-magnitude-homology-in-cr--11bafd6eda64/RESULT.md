# Sharp disappearance of low off-diagonal magnitude homology in crown graphs

## Finding
For an integer \(n\geq3\), let \(\mathrm{Cr}_n\) be the crown graph with vertices \(A_1,\ldots,A_n,B_1,\ldots,B_n\) and edges \(A_iB_j\) precisely when \(i\neq j\). Equivalently, \(\mathrm{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}\setminus M_n\), where \(M_n\) is a perfect matching. With the usual integral graph-magnitude homology, the complete two low off-diagonal groups are

\[
\operatorname{MH}_{2,3}(\mathrm{Cr}_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z^{6},&n=3,\\0,&n\geq4,\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{MH}_{3,4}(\mathrm{Cr}_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z^{12},&n=3,\\0,&n\geq4.\end{cases}
\]

The exceptional graph \(\mathrm{Cr}_3\) is the six-cycle, whose nonzero groups were previously calculated in the even-cycle magnitude-homology classification [2]. The next graph \(\mathrm{Cr}_4\) is the three-dimensional cube \(Q_3\), so its vanishing also follows from the Cartesian-product Künneth theorem in [1]. The family-wide calculation for \(n\geq5\) is the new portion of the classification. Every choice of three indices embeds an **isometric** copy of this six-cycle into each \(\mathrm{Cr}_n\) for \(n\geq4\); nevertheless, both displayed groups of the ambient crown vanish. Therefore each such inclusion induces the zero map in these two bidegrees.

## Assumptions and scope
For a finite connected graph \(G\), write \(d\) for shortest-path distance. The integral magnitude chain group \(\operatorname{MC}_{k,\ell}(G)\) is freely generated by tuples \((x_0,\ldots,x_k)\) of vertices with consecutive vertices distinct and \(\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(x_{i-1},x_i)=\ell\). The boundary removes an interior vertex \(x_i\) with sign \((-1)^i\) exactly when \(d(x_{i-1},x_{i+1})=d(x_{i-1},x_i)+d(x_i,x_{i+1})\); other terms are zero. Magnitude homology is the homology of this integer chain complex at fixed length \(\ell\). All quantifiers below range over integers \(n\geq3\) and admissible distinct consecutive vertices. The theorem makes no assertion about other bidegrees, diagonal homology, or all-length diagonality.

The relevant distance table is \(d(A_i,A_j)=d(B_i,B_j)=2\) for distinct indices, \(d(A_i,B_j)=1\) for distinct indices, and \(d(A_i,B_i)=3\). Reversing the two bipartition classes and simultaneously permuting indices preserves all chain groups. A tuple of odd total length has endpoints in opposite bipartition classes, while a tuple of even total length has endpoints in the same class. Each magnitude chain complex splits canonically by its ordered pair of endpoints.

## Proof
**Length three, degree two.** Fix the ordered endpoints \((A_a,B_b)\). If \(a=b\), the unique degree-one generator is \(T=(A_a,B_a)\). The degree-two generators are \(U_j=(A_a,B_j,B_a)\) and \(V_j=(A_a,A_j,B_a)\), with \(j\neq a\); both have boundary \(-T\). All degree-three generators are \(W_{j,k}=(A_a,B_j,A_k,B_a)\) for distinct \(j,k\neq a\), with \(\partial W_{j,k}=U_j-V_k\). Hence \(\operatorname{MH}_{2,3}(A_a,B_a)\) is the kernel of the augmentation on the free group on the \(U_j,V_j\), modulo the edge-difference relations \(U_j=V_k\) for \(j\neq k\). Those differences are the edges of a bipartite graph on \(2(n-1)\) labels. For \(n=3\), that graph has two connected components and the augmentation quotient has rank one. For \(n\geq4\), it is connected, so the quotient is zero. All relations have coefficients \(\pm1\), hence the quotient is free abelian.

If \(a\neq b\), the degree-one group at length three is zero. The degree-two generators are \(U_j=(A_a,B_j,B_b)\) and \(V_j=(A_a,A_j,B_b)\), for \(j\notin\{a,b\}\). They are boundaries individually: \(\partial(A_a,B_j,A_a,B_b)=U_j\) and \(\partial(A_a,B_b,A_j,B_b)=-V_j\). Thus all unmatched ordered endpoint classes vanish. By bipartition reversal there are exactly \(2n\) matched ordered endpoint classes. The first displayed classification follows.

**Length four, degree three.** Fix ordered endpoints \((A_a,A_b)\). Define admissible degree-three tuples

\[
P_{j,k}=(A_a,B_j,A_k,A_b),\quad
Q_{j,l}=(A_a,B_j,B_l,A_b),\quad
R_{k,l}=(A_a,A_k,B_l,A_b),
\]

where \(P\) requires \(j\neq a,\;k\neq j,b\), \(Q\) requires \(j\neq a,l\) and \(l\neq b\), and \(R\) requires \(k\neq a,l\) and \(l\neq b\). The degree-four tuples are precisely

\[
W_{j,k,l}=(A_a,B_j,A_k,B_l,A_b),
\qquad j\neq a,k,\quad l\neq k,b.
\]

Their signed boundaries, omitting a term whenever its stated condition fails, are

\[
\partial W_{j,k,l}=-[k\neq a]R_{k,l}+[j\neq l]Q_{j,l}-[k\neq b]P_{j,k}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here bracketed predicates equal one when true and zero when false. When \(a=b\), degree-two tuples are \(X_k=(A_a,A_k,A_a)\) for \(k\neq a\). When \(a\neq b\), degree-two tuples are \(X_k=(A_a,A_k,A_b)\) for \(k\notin\{a,b\}\), together with \(Y=(A_a,B_b,A_b)\) and \(Z=(A_a,B_a,A_b)\). For both cases the boundaries are

\[
\partial P_{j,k}=-[k\neq a]X_k+[j=b]Y,\quad
\partial Q_{j,l}=-[l=a]Z+[j=b]Y,\quad
\partial R_{k,l}=[k\neq b]X_k-[l=a]Z.
\tag{2}
\]

Undefined generators in (2) always occur with zero indicator. If \(a=b\), every \(X_k\) is a boundary of some \(-P_{j,k}\). If \(a\neq b\), the tuples \(P_{b,a}, Q_{j,a},P_{b,k}\), with \(j,k\notin\{a,b\}\), show that the image of \(\partial_3\) contains \(Y,Z\), and all \(X_k\). Consequently \(H_2(\operatorname{MC}_{*,4}(\mathrm{Cr}_n;\mathbb Z))=0\) in every endpoint class (there are no degree-one length-four generators because the graph has diameter three).

It remains to solve the degree-three cocycle equations over an arbitrary abelian group \(\Lambda\). Let a cochain assign coefficients \(p_{j,k},q_{j,l},r_{k,l}\) to the three types. By (1), the cocycle equation is

\[
-[k\neq a]r_{k,l}+[j\neq l]q_{j,l}-[k\neq b]p_{j,k}=0
\quad\text{for every admissible }(j,k,l).
\tag{3}
\]

If \(a=b\), the substitutions \(k=a\), \(j=l\), and then three pairwise distinct indices different from \(a\), into (3) give \(q_{j,l}=0\), \(r_{k,j}=-p_{j,k}\), and \(p_{j,k}=p_{l,k}\) whenever defined. Therefore \(p_{j,k}=u_k\) is independent of \(j\), \(r_{k,l}=-u_k\), and \(q=0\). These assignments are exactly the coboundary of the degree-two cochain assigning \(-u_k\) to \(X_k\). The same conclusion holds when only two indices remain: independence of \(j\) is then automatic.

If \(a\neq b\), set \(S=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{a,b\}\). Using a unique degree-two coboundary (2), normalize

\[
p_{b,a}=r_{b,a}=0,\qquad p_{b,k}=0\quad(k\in S).
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (3), first with \(k=a\) and \(j=l\), and then with \(k=a\) and \(j\neq l\), forces \(p_{j,a}=0\) for \(j\in S\) and \(q_{b,l}=q_{j,l}=0\) for \(l\in S\), \(j\neq l\). Using \(k=b\) and \(j=l\), then \(k=b\) and \(l=a\), forces \(r_{b,l}=0\) for \(l\in S\) and \(q_{j,a}=0\) for \(j\in S\). For each \(k\in S\), if there exists \(l\in S\setminus\{k\}\), applying (3) successively to \((j,k,l)=(b,k,l),(l,k,l),(l,k,a),(b,k,a)\) kills \(r_{k,l},p_{l,k},r_{k,a},q_{b,a}\), respectively. This works for all \(k\in S\) when \(|S|=n-2\geq2\), and kills every remaining coefficient. Hence all degree-three cocycles are degree-two coboundaries for \(n\geq4\).

When \(n=3\), the set \(S=\{k\}\) contains one element. The only coefficients left after (4) and the forced vanishings are \(r_{k,a}\) and \(q_{b,a}\), and the sole remaining relation is \(r_{k,a}=q_{b,a}\). The free value of \(q_{b,a}\) gives \(H^3(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{MC}_{*,4}(A_a,A_b),\Lambda))\cong\Lambda\), naturally for every abelian coefficient group \(\Lambda\). For \(n\geq4\) it is zero, and for \(a=b\) it is zero for all \(n\geq3\).

The chain groups are finite free, and the degree-two length-four homology vanishes. The universal coefficient theorem therefore identifies these degree-three cohomology functors with \(\operatorname{Hom}(H_3,\Lambda)\). Their calculations with all \(\Lambda\) show integrally that each distinct same-part ordered endpoint has \(H_3\cong\mathbb Z\) for \(n=3\) and zero for \(n\geq4\), whereas coincident endpoint classes vanish. There are exactly \(2n(n-1)=12\) such ordered endpoint pairs at \(n=3\), proving the second classification and ruling out torsion.

## Verification
The bundled `VERIFY.py` constructs the crown graph and its shortest-path distance matrix from its edge list; independently enumerates all length-three and length-four magnitude tuples with fixed endpoints; builds signed boundary matrices from the defining metric condition; and verifies \(\partial^2=0\) indirectly via consistent rank calculations. It tests \(n\in\{3,4,5,6\}\), every endpoint symmetry class, and the expected group ranks using characteristic \(2,3,5\). It also computes integer Smith normal forms for the tested \(n=3,4\) boundary matrices, confirming that every nonzero invariant factor is a unit. Those finite checks do not prove the all-parameter theorem, which rests on the explicit presentations and cocycle solution above.

## Relationship to prior work
Magnitude homology and its integral chain construction originate with Hepworth and Willerton [1]. Gu [2] computed magnitude homology of even cycles, including the exceptional six-cycle \(\mathrm{Cr}_3\). Gu's pawful-graph diagonal theorem assumes diameter at most two; the crown graphs here have diameter three, so that theorem does not imply the asserted vanishing. The Künneth theorem of [1] applies to Cartesian graph products, not directly to the categorical-product description \(K_2\times K_n\) of these crowns. There is an important exceptional isomorphism: \(\mathrm{Cr}_4\cong Q_3=K_2\square K_2\square K_2\), so the \(n=4\) vanishing is already implied by that Künneth theorem and is credited accordingly. For \(n\geq5\), this product reduction does not apply. The complete-bipartite calculations of [1] do not encompass deleting a perfect matching. The general girth-based statement of [3] and the generalized pawful criterion of [4] likewise do not establish this two-bidegree transition. The claim is limited to the precise two integral groups and their sharp threshold; these logical comparisons do not establish global uniqueness in the literature.

## Limitations
The calculation does not classify any higher magnitude group, does not establish full diagonality for \(n\geq4\), and does not give equivariant homology. An unindexed equivalent theorem or a stronger result in literature not identified by the searches remains possible. The finite verification is a consistency check, not the proof for arbitrary \(n\). No independent audit is claimed.
