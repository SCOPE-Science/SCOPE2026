The polytope radii are determined by elementary but exact extremal constraints: maximal Euclidean norm has \(k\) fully saturated coordinates and one fractional coordinate, while each defining halfspace contributes its precise normalized distance. More importantly, the all-ellipsoid lower bound is demonstrated rather than inferred from symmetric appearance: the hyperoctahedral orbit of each outer contact, together with the orbit of a polar contact, has scalar covariance. For every positive-definite quadratic metric, multiplying the resulting trace inequalities and applying the eigenvalue Cauchy inequality gives the exact sharp bound, with equality only for scalar metrics. A derivative calculation proves the unique cutoff at \(\sqrt d\), including all integer transition boundaries and exterior constant regimes. The finite exact checker confirms contact descriptions, isotropic averages and sample matrix inequalities, without being used as an infinite proof.

The closest exact-distance study, Grundbacher–Kobos (2026, arXiv:2603.18268, Theorem 1.1 and cone results), addresses direct sums and cones, rather than the simultaneous \(\ell_1\)-budget and coordinate-cap intersection studied here. The general Ader-characterization framework in Grundbacher–Kobos (2026, arXiv:2602.14847, Theorems 1.2 and 1.5) provides a conceptual method for certifying an optimal ellipsoid, but does not evaluate the full parameter-dependent distance of this normalized intersection family or optimize its cutoff. Their earlier extremal paper (arXiv:2407.08829) addresses the sharp \(\sqrt d\) endpoint distance and rigidity, not the interior rounding optimum. Prior exact computations concerning cube–cross-polytope intersections in other invariants (such as volumes or graph distances) are not Banach–Mazur calculations and do not imply the claim. The usual square-to-regular-octagon identity is known in dimension two; no priority is asserted for that benchmark by itself. A hidden equivalent computation in other normed-space terminology remains a real residual risk, so independent priority confirmation is not asserted.

## Value — PASS

This solves a concrete and naturally parameterized optimization over the complete dimension-independent family of intersections between two classical extremal Banach spaces. It proves that the optimal ellipsoid stays spherical under arbitrary affine adjustment, gives the entire cutoff profile rather than just endpoint estimates, pinpoints one unique dimension-dependent balance \(t=\sqrt d\), and quantifies the sharp asymptotic drop from \(\sqrt d\) to \(d^{1/4}\), including an arithmetic fractional-part correction and a cusp-versus-smooth-minimum transition between nonsquare and square dimensions. These facts provide a geometric benchmark for norm interpolation and affine rounding, not just a restatement of the classical \(\sqrt d\) upper bound.
