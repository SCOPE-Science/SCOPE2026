# Sharp exponent-two bilevel selection boundary for original dynamic FISTA on a rank-deficient quadratic

## Finding
Consider the original Fast Bi-level Proximal Gradient (FBi-PG) recurrence of Merchav, Sabach and Teboulle, with the FISTA parameter generated by its quadratic identity, applied to the rank-deficient two-dimensional quadratic below. Its iterates select the unique bilevel solution for **every** admissible offset and initial point when \(0<\gamma\le2\). At the critical exponent \(\gamma=2\), selection still holds despite the summability of the regularization coefficients. For **each** \(\gamma>2\), some admissible offset and initial point produce convergence to a *different*, strictly nonoptimal point of the inner solution line. Thus \(2\) is the exact upper boundary for a guarantee uniform over offsets and initializations on this concrete problem; it is not asserted as a universal boundary for every bilevel problem.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(x=(u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\). Define the convex smooth inner and outer objectives
\[\phi(u,v)=\frac32u^2,\qquad\omega(u,v)=\frac12v^2.\]
The inner solution set is \(X^*=\{(0,v):v\in\mathbb R\}\), and the unique outer minimizer over it is \(x^\dagger=(0,0)\). In source notation, take \(f=\phi\), \(\sigma=\omega\), and \(g=\psi=0\). The gradient Lipschitz constants are \(\beta_f=3\), \(\beta_\sigma=1\); hence the prescribed fixed majorizer is \(\beta=4\).

Fix \(\gamma>0\), integer \(a\ge2\), and arbitrary \(x_{-1}=x_0\in\mathbb R^2\). Set \(t_{-1}=0\), \(t_0=1\), and, for \(k\ge0\),
\[\alpha_k=(k+a)^{-\gamma},\quad t_{k+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}}2,\quad y_k=x_k+\frac{t_{k-1}-1}{t_k}(x_k-x_{k-1}),\quad x_{k+1}=y_k-\frac14\nabla(\phi+\alpha_k\omega)(y_k).\]
The extrapolation formula at \(k=0\) is unambiguous because \(x_{-1}=x_0\). Set \(\lambda_k=\alpha_k/4\), so \(0<\lambda_k<1\), and denote the two coordinates by \(u_k,v_k\).

**Exact statement.** For all \(0<\gamma\le2\), every \(a\ge2\) and every \(x_0\), we have \(x_k\to x^\dagger\). If \(0<\gamma<2\), then \(|v_k|=O(k^{\gamma/2-1})\), with a constant depending on the data. For every \(\gamma>2\), an integer \(a\ge2\) can be chosen so that for \(x_0=(0,1)\), we have \(x_k\to(0,v_\infty)\) with \(v_\infty\ge1-\frac14\sum_{k\ge0}t_k(k+a)^{-\gamma}>0\). The last series may be made strictly smaller than \(1\) by increasing \(a\).

## Proof
**1. Coordinate reduction and FISTA scale.** Write \(\widetilde u_k,\widetilde v_k\) for the extrapolated coordinates. The update is exactly
\[u_{k+1}=\frac14\widetilde u_k,\qquad v_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_k)\widetilde v_k.\]
The identity \(t_{k+1}^2-t_{k+1}=t_k^2\) implies \(1+k/2\le t_k\le k+1\) and \(t_k/k\to1/2\). For \(k\ge1\), the inertial coefficient \(b_k=(t_{k-1}-1)/t_k\) lies in \([0,1)\), so \(|u_{k+1}|\le\frac14(2|u_k|+|u_{k-1}|)\). If \(M_k=\max(|u_k|,|u_{k-1}|)\), then \(M_{k+2}\le\frac34M_k\) for \(k\ge1\), proving geometric convergence of \(u_k\) to \(0\). The first step is \(u_1=u_0/4\).

**2. Exact one-coordinate energy identity.** Define \(z_0=v_0\) and for \(k\ge1\)
\[z_k=t_{k-1}v_k-(t_{k-1}-1)v_{k-1}.\]
Then \(t_k\widetilde v_k=z_k+(t_k-1)v_k\) and \(z_{k+1}=z_k-\lambda_kt_k\widetilde v_k\). The following algebraic equality, valid for every \(k\ge0\), is the essential finite-step certificate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&z_k^2+\lambda_k(t_k^2-t_k)v_k^2-z_{k+1}^2-\lambda_kt_k^2v_{k+1}^2\\
&\quad=\lambda_kt_k(t_k-1)(v_k-\widetilde v_k)^2
+\lambda_kt_k\widetilde v_k^2
+\lambda_k^2t_k^2(1-\lambda_k)\widetilde v_k^2\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Set \(E_0=v_0^2\) and \(E_k=z_k^2+\lambda_{k-1}t_{k-1}^2v_k^2\) for \(k\ge1\). Since \(\lambda_k\le\lambda_{k-1}\) and \(t_k^2-t_k=t_{k-1}^2\), the equality yields
\[0\le E_{k+1}\le E_k-\lambda_kt_k\widetilde v_k^2\le E_k\le v_0^2.\]
In particular \(\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda_kt_k\widetilde v_k^2<\infty\), \(|z_k|\le|v_0|\), and \(\lambda_{k-1}t_{k-1}^2v_k^2\le v_0^2\).

**3. Subcritical exponents.** When \(0<\gamma<2\), the lower bound \(\lambda_{k-1}t_{k-1}^2\ge c k^{2-\gamma}\), for all sufficiently large \(k\), implies \(|v_k|\le |v_0|c^{-1/2}k^{\gamma/2-1}\to0\). This establishes both selection and the claimed rate.

**4. The critical exponent, including the endpoint.** For \(\gamma=2\), the same energy inequality and \(\inf_{k\ge1}\lambda_{k-1}t_{k-1}^2>0\) imply that \(v_k,z_k,\widetilde v_k\) are bounded. Since \(\widetilde v_k-v_k=(z_k-v_k)/t_k\) and \(v_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_k)\widetilde v_k\), we have
\[|v_{k+1}-v_k|=O(k^{-1}),\qquad |\widetilde v_{k+1}-\widetilde v_k|=O(k^{-1}).\]
Moreover \(\lambda_kt_k\asymp1/k\), so the energy inequality gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}\widetilde v_k^2/k<\infty\).

**Elementary logarithmic-mesh lemma.** If a real sequence \((w_k)\) satisfies \(|w_{k+1}-w_k|\le C/k\) eventually and \(\sum_{k\ge1}w_k^2/k<\infty\), then \(w_k\to0\). To prove it, interpolate linearly at the logarithmic mesh points \(s_k=\log k\). The interpolation is globally Lipschitz for large \(s\), since \((s_{k+1}-s_k)^{-1}|w_{k+1}-w_k|\) is bounded, and its squared integral is finite by comparison with \(\sum w_k^2/k\) (the interval endpoint values differ by \(O(1/k)\); the correction is integrable). A uniformly continuous, square-integrable function on a half-line tends to zero: otherwise its Lipschitz bound creates disjoint intervals of positive squared integral. Therefore \(\widetilde v_k\to0\); the update yields \(v_{k+1}\to0\), proving the previously excluded endpoint \(\gamma=2\).

**5. Supercritical obstruction.** Fix any \(\gamma>2\). Since \(t_k\le k+1\), dominated convergence implies
\[S(a):=\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda_kt_k=\frac14\sum_{k\ge0}t_k(k+a)^{-\gamma}\longrightarrow0\quad(a\to\infty).\]
Choose integer \(a\ge2\) with \(S(a)<1\), and start from \(v_{-1}=v_0=z_0=1\). Since \(\widetilde v_k=(z_k+(t_k-1)v_k)/t_k\) is a convex combination, induction gives \(0<z_k,v_k,\widetilde v_k\le1\) and \(z_{k+1}=z_k-\lambda_kt_k\widetilde v_k\ge1-S(a)>0\). Thus \(z_k\downarrow z_\infty\ge1-S(a)>0\). The exact recurrence
\[v_{k+1}=\left(1-\frac1{t_k}\right)v_k+\frac1{t_k}z_{k+1}\]
is an averaging scheme with \(\sum_k1/t_k=\infty\), so \(v_k\to z_\infty>0\) (subtract the limit and iterate, or use a standard weighted-average argument). Therefore the method converges to an inner minimizer that fails to minimize the outer objective. This proves the sharp universal-guarantee frontier.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses SymPy to expand the exact Lyapunov identity and tests its normalized recurrence against direct updates, including the initialization and both coordinates. Rational and floating-point checks verify representative trajectories on either side of \(\gamma=2\), but numerical iterates do **not** establish their infinite limits. The endpoint and counterexample are certified by the preceding analytic arguments. To reproduce: install Python 3 and SymPy, then run `python verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Merchav, Sabach and Teboulle introduced FBi-PG and the exact dynamic-FISTA recurrence (2.4)–(2.7), and their pointwise convergence theorem under an error-bound assumption explicitly treats \(1<\gamma<2\) with a different admissible momentum schedule. The subcritical part here is compatible with, and in this example partly implied by, their rate analysis. The specific contributions assessed here are the **endpoint \(\gamma=2\) selection argument for the original recurrence** and the **explicit all-supercritical-exponents obstruction** in a rank-deficient bilevel quadratic. The exponent \(2\) as a regularization/inertia scale is not claimed as an unprecedented phenomenon: Attouch–László and others study related continuous-time and differently regularized inertial algorithms. Their stated schemes do not instantiate the discrete recursion proved above.

## Limitations
This is an exact two-dimensional smooth quadratic benchmark, not a general bi-level convergence theorem or an impossibility result for every choice of offset when \(\gamma>2\). The supercritical counterexample requires sufficiently large allowed \(a\). No finite-precision, nonlinear, nonsmooth or arbitrary-initial-momentum extension is established. The numerical verifier checks identities and finite prefixes; it does not replace the proofs of infinite convergence. Scientific priority beyond the inspected literature is not independently established.
