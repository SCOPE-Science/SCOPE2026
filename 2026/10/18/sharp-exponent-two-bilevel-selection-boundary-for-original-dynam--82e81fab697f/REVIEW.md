# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Direct substitution into equations (2.4)–(2.7) gives the two independent coordinate recursions, with majorizer \(\beta=4\). The finite-step Lyapunov identity is algebraically exact. It yields the subcritical decay bound; at the critical exponent, boundedness, harmonic weighted square-summability, and a proved logarithmic-mesh lemma yield convergence. For any supercritical exponent, choosing a large admissible offset makes the total weighted regularization mass less than one, leaving a provably positive limiting nullspace coordinate. A standalone symbolic verifier tests the identity and direct recurrence, not the infinite quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS under the inspected sources; independent priority not assessed.** The 2024 FBi-PG paper defines the recurrence and proves general rate results that already imply much of the subcritical behavior. Its displayed pointwise theorem does not cover the standard recurrence at the critical endpoint, and its results do not give the supercritical constructive obstruction. The related inertial-Tikhonov papers address continuous-time flows or different discrete regularization placements. Closest published mathematical records concern original FISTA finite-iterate nonmonotonicity and Tikhonov summability in a different algorithm; neither logically implies this exact two-dimensional boundary. Search incompleteness and older inertial literature are residual risks.

**Value — PASS.** The result is a motivated, complete sharp frontier in a rank-deficient bilevel model. It separates the power of acceleration-weighted Tikhonov action from the summability of raw regularization, proves an endpoint unavailable from a naive power-counting estimate, and constructs failure immediately beyond the frontier. The benchmark is relevant when choosing decay exponents for bilevel selection, though its scope is deliberately limited to two smooth quadratics and the precise original momentum schedule.

**Closest literature and limits.** Merchav–Sabach–Teboulle (arXiv:2407.21221) is the motivating source and supplies the algorithm; Attouch–László (2024) has broader continuous-time context with a related inverse-square scale; László (arXiv:2302.02115) and Karapetyants–László (2024) use different discrete algorithms. An equivalent earlier specialized recurrence theorem remains possible. No nonlinear or general-dimensional claim is made.
