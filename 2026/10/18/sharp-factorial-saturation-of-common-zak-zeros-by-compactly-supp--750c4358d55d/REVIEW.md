# Review of sharp factorial Zak-zero saturation

## Correctness
The construction gives smooth compactly supported windows explicitly. The one-variable quotient has a strictly increasing logarithmic radial factor with exact limits \(0\) and \(+\infty\). Polynomial factorization rules out all common zeros with any zero denominator and identifies the rest as precisely the permutations of distinct assigned roots. A Vandermonde determinant and positive radial derivatives prove that each root is transverse with positive index and establish the exact common Jacobian formula. The universal lower bound follows from the predecessor's known top Chern class and the standard Euler zero-counting theorem. A standalone finite checker supports, but is not substituted for, the all-dimensional proof.

## Originality
The 2026 work of de Dios Pont, Liehr, and Taylor gives the crucial quasiperiodic line-bundle obstruction, computes \(\int c_d=d!\), and constructs \(d+1\) coefficient sections with no common zero. These ingredients and the implied signed lower bound are prior work. Its relevant full-text results and proof were inspected. The new content is an explicit \(d\)-section family with *exactly* \(d!\) nondegenerate common zeros, all positively oriented, admitting arbitrary distinct targets off a ray and a closed positive Jacobian. Searches against related Gabor, Zak, topological-degree and algebraic-intersection formulations found no statement with this explicit saturation and fiber classification. Algebraic intersection theory may nevertheless furnish abstract or alternative realizations; novelty is not claimed for Chern numbers, generic transversality or the elementary-symmetric identities in isolation.

## Value
The 2026 frame-existence result uses the inevitability of a common Zak zero as its topological obstruction. Determining the *least possible count* and *orientations* of isolated defects, and realizing the optimal count by concrete compactly supported windows, quantifies how that obstruction can be saturated. The complete root-parameter family and exact Jacobian discriminate transverse singularities from arbitrary degenerate zeros and make the phenomenon reproducible at every dimension.

## Scope and residual risks
This is a sharp statement for transverse zeros only, not for singular isolated zeros, and concerns the explicit polynomial-window family rather than all possible minimizers. The prior paper's coefficient basis and known Chern number are recognized as precursors. General topology and Segre embedding literature might contain closely related realizations; exhaustive historical priority is not claimed. Independent audit has not occurred.
