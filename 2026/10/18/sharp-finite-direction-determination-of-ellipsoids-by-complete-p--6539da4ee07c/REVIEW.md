# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The full sectional-volume formula is derived from the marginal distribution of a uniform Euclidean ball under an invertible affine map. Section-profile support endpoints determine center projections and quadratic widths; amplitudes determine the shared determinant. Spanning rank-one symmetric measurements imply exact reconstruction. The lower-bound argument explicitly produces an indefinite direction-null matrix for every deficient measurement family and then two positive-definite matrices with equal determinant on a common one-dimensional pencil; their full sectional profiles coincide. The explicit coordinate-plus-bisector inverse and its measurement-space Lipschitz estimate are algebraically exact. The companion checker passed 83 exact measurements and verified equal-volume ambiguities in dimensions two through seven.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical qualification.** Fish--Ryabogin--Sun--Yaskin study complete angular section-function information and general characterization phenomena, not the sharp number of finite calibrated directions within the ellipsoid family. Related results on full angular fixed-offset rigidity and unlabeled Haar section moments have different data and implications. Standard interpolation of quadratic forms is credited rather than presented as a new principle. The particularly relevant missing comparison is whether old geometric tomography or rank-one measurement literature already states the precise *global* minimality with known determinant and full profile data; searching did not identify such a statement, but unindexed equivalence remains possible.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete natural classification of finite sampling configurations for parallel-section tomography of arbitrary translated ellipsoids. It gives a sharp, dimension-uniform acquisition threshold, proves that the seemingly additional total-volume invariant never saves a view in the worst case, and supplies a concrete stable reconstruction design. The result does not solve the unrestricted convex-body inverse problem and does not require doing so to settle this finite-dimensional model completely.

## Closest literature and limitations
The motivating source is arXiv:2610.10229 (2026). Classical quadratic-form recovery and ellipsoid fitting literature are relevant ingredients; the claimed result is the equivalence between universal recovery of complete sectional profiles and full rank-one span, including an equal-volume obstruction for every deficient set. Global noisy-function recovery and uncalibrated directions are outside the theorem.
