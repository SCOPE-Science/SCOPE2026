# Scientific review: sharp finite-path variation for unimodal data

## Correctness
**PASS (self-review).** For a nonnegative unimodal vector on a finite path, the maximum centered ball average remains nondecreasing before any permitted peak and nonincreasing after it. If a maximizing ball average before the peak exceeds the next original value, shifting the center by one and decreasing the radius by one removes only left-end entries smaller than the old average, so the average cannot decrease. Reflection yields the decreasing side. Thus the output has the same peak value as the input, permitting a telescoping total-variation identity. Prefix and suffix balls containing the peak provide respective uplifts of \(A/(p+1)\) and \(B/(n-p)\) at the endpoints. This proves the factor \(1-1/n\) and the peak-location deficit for every \(n\ge3\). Equality forces an isolated endpoint spike above a constant baseline; such spikes attain the bound. The proof uses no inference from finite tests.

## Originality
**PASS (qualified self-review).** González-Riquelme, Kovač and Madrid, arXiv:2603.12462v1 (12 March 2026), formulate the unrestricted finite-path conjecture and compute small-graph cases, but its complete relevant introduction and path section do not establish the full all-orders unimodal inequality. *Monotone data attain the conjectured path-graph variational constant*, published identifier `2ae83e2401fb`, already proves the exact coefficient and extremal configurations for monotone inputs; its full RESULT.md was inspected, and those results are explicitly excluded from the new claim. Nonmonotone unimodal inputs include arbitrarily long rising and falling runs not covered by monotonicity or reversal. Bilz and Weigt (arXiv:2107.12404; J. Geom. Anal. 2025), in its complete relevant main results and discrete section, establish different one-dimensional variation estimates, including indicators and an attached-functions class, not this finite-path \(1-1/n\) bound on every positive unimodal input. Semantic finding searches for 'single-hump', 'quasiconcave' and 'unimodal' graph maximal variation found no stronger covering theorem. Absence of indexed matches is not proof of historical uniqueness.

## Value
**PASS (self-review).** The sharp unrestricted finite-path variation coefficient is the motivating open conjecture. The natural one-peak class is substantially larger than the previously settled monotone class, and the proof isolates a structural phenomenon, preservation of a peak under centered graph-ball maximization. The exact all-orders optimal constant, complete nonconstant equality rigidity and an explicit peak-position lower bound for the deficit give usable constraints on any future counterexample or general proof: it must have multiple peaks in absolute value. The result is a genuine structural subproblem rather than a numerical adjustment or arbitrary finite slice.

## Risks and limits
This theorem does not solve the conjecture for profiles with multiple peaks. The finite exact-rational test checks 13,652 profiles plus four rational examples, not infinitely many. Unindexed folklore or literature under different terminology could affect priority, although the closest primary full text and published monotone case were inspected.
