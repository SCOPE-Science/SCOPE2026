# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Every finite survivor condition is equivalent, outside a null dyadic set, to forbidding one of the pairs \(00\) and \(11\) at each consecutive binary position. The two exact transfer matrices produce the sorted-state recursion; induction proves both universal bounds and the unique extremizing schedules. The alternating product is triangular, and its two-step directed graph proves countably many eventually periodic infinite survivors. Fibonacci asymptotics and graph-directed cylinder dimension give the advertised entropy/dimension distinction. Independent enumeration of all digit words for all schedules through horizon eight agrees with the transfer recursion; all extremal schedules were tested through horizon fifteen. No untested numerical extrapolation is used in the proof.

## Originality
**PASS with priority qualification.** The explicit switching optimum over every length-\(n\) hole schedule, including uniqueness, is not stated or implied by the inspected 2014 moving-hole paper (sinusoidal/breathing motions and approximate escape rates) or the relevant sections of the 2021 general random-open-map paper (existence, pressure, entropy, escape formulas). Known Fibonacci counts for static forbidden binary pairs and the equivalent fixed-hole negative-doubling formulation cover components of the argument, not the all-schedule comparison. Database searches for the two hole positions, exact Fibonacci/linear bounds, arbitrary schedules, and equivalences returned related but different negative-doubling/survivor findings. Older symbolic optimization literature may contain a stronger equivalent result; this unresolved priority risk does not affect correctness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** For the smallest symmetric pair of disjoint dyadic Markov holes of equal measure, the theorem solves the global finite-time positioning-control problem exactly: only constant placement has Fibonacci survivor growth, and only alternating placement has linear growth. The limiting survivor set changes from an uncountable positive-dimensional Cantor set to a countably infinite zero-dimensional set. This is a complete natural two-hole classification, not a numerical increment or a claim that the underlying Fibonacci recurrence itself is new.
