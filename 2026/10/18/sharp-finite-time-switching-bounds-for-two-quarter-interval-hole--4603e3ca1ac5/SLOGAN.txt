For the doubling map, switching freely between its leftmost and rightmost quarter-interval holes gives surviving mass between \((n+2)/2^{n+1}\) and \(F_{n+3}/2^{n+1}\), where \(F_j\) is a Fibonacci number. Alternation uniquely minimizes finite-time survival and leaves a countably infinite zero-dimensional survivor set, while a constant hole uniquely maximizes finite-time survival and leaves a positive-dimensional Cantor set.
