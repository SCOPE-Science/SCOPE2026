# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The group law and the two forbidden-product permutations give a complete four-coset-cycle computation. Summing their independent-set counts yields the exact Lucas expression for every integer parameter. The bound isolates the central-order-two quotient contribution and bounds every other product cycle uniformly; its normalized finite inequality is positive at 41 and increases thereafter. An exact-integer certificate checks every missing parameter 2–40, independently reproduces the permutation method through 15, and exhausts all subsets for parameters 2–4. The proof excludes all zero cases. The result is restricted to first raw moments and does not infer subset-count dominance.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Amit et al., arXiv:2610.12912v1, Theorem 7.6 gives a general first-moment expansion but names the dicyclic parameter problem as unresolved in its future-work section; it does not supply the all-parameter sign or threshold. Mandal and Neetu, arXiv:2602.09073v1, supply fixed-cardinality counts and a restricted half-size lower bound, rather than the raw all-subset first moment. Targeted mathematical-results searches for dicyclic first moments, Lucas cycle formulas, sign reversal, and parameter 30 found other dicyclic subgroup invariants and unrelated moments, not an implication of this sign theorem. Unindexed earlier computations cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The raw first moment is the first required input to the moment-based approach to product–quotient dominance. The motivating article specifically identifies dicyclic groups as a delicate outstanding case. The result is a sharp natural complete sign classification, including the unexpected reversal at parameter 30 and a uniform proof for all higher orders, not an arbitrary small finite slice. It does not resolve the stronger dominance-count question or the second-moment criterion.
