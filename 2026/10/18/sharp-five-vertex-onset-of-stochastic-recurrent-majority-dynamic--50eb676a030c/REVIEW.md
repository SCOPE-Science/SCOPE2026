# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact definition of one-round transition support follows from strict neighborhood majorities and independent positive-probability tie outcomes. The balanced-hub lemma proves closure, irreducibility, temporal period two, and the uniform stationary law at fair ties. An exhaustive finite proof checks all 1, 4, 38, and 728 connected labelled graphs of sizes 2, 3, 4, and 5 respectively, examining every opinion state and all legal transitions. Independent support constructions and independent terminal-component algorithms agree for all cases, with the complete exceptional isomorphism census `59:60`, `221:60`, `495:15`. No simulation is promoted into an infinite theorem; the general structural lemma is analytical.

## Originality — PASS with residual literature risk

The 14 February 2023 arXiv primary source and its accessible May 2024 journal expansion were read in the model definitions and recurrent-class sections; the original paper's appendix construction yields large absorbing components and its cycle theorem yields only components of size at most two. Neither implies the sharp five-vertex cutoff or the three-class enumeration, which also do not appear explicitly in the inspected text. Related deterministic majority results address different tie handling. Targeted searches in public literature and indexed mathematical research records disclosed no matching first-layer census. Unindexed finite-model enumerations or alternative formulations remain a risk.

## Value — PASS

The first graph order at which stochastic tie-breaking qualitatively enlarges a recurrent communicating set is a natural structural invariant arising from an explicit gap between cycle-only and asymptotic large-graph results. The theorem supplies not just a witness but the full first occurrence, its exact incidence and three graph types, plus a general, interpretable sufficient mechanism. The four-state/period-two distinction prevents misidentifying this as an ordinary deterministic four-cycle. The result does not require a new asymptotic theorem to be a useful exact boundary.
