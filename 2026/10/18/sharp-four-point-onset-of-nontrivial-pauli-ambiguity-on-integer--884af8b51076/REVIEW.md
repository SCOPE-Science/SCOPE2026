# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Equality of all Fourier intensities is equivalent to equality of aperiodic autocorrelations. For one and two nonzero coefficients the largest difference fixes the relative phase. For three, the non-arithmetic-progression case is unique; the arithmetic-progression case reduces to an injective real-linear equation unless endpoint magnitudes agree, when the only second candidate is the allowed conjugate reflection. The four-point construction has nonzero coefficients, identical coordinate moduli and full-torus Fourier intensities, yet neither trivial equivalence. Its complete algebraic identities and the explicit rational witness are checked by `verify.py`.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The complete 2026 Novikov–Xu paper constructs finite-difference Pauli partners but does not solve this sharp one-dimensional least-support question. Friedman's 1989 Section 3 anticipates the finite-dimensional problem and calls the first three coordinates elementary; that historical partial coverage is acknowledged, not treated as new. Fourier-only sparse phase-retrieval results do not settle identical coordinate moduli, and continuous-real-line Pauli statements do not entail a four-point lattice cutoff. Searches for the direct four-point formulation and equivalently worded minimal-support results did not identify a stronger covering theorem. An older unindexed instance or equivalent construction is a genuine remaining risk.

## Value — PASS

The minimal support admitting nontrivial ambiguity is an intrinsic exact invariant of the paired position/Fourier measurement problem, and it is determined for every finite-support geometry at the rigidity side of the threshold. The construction gives a nonisolated family of explicit ambiguity witnesses and distinguishes aperiodic full-frequency data from finite-cyclic Fourier sampling. It is a natural sharp cutoff rather than an arbitrary fixed coefficient calculation. The theorem does not claim all four-point fiber geometries are classified.
