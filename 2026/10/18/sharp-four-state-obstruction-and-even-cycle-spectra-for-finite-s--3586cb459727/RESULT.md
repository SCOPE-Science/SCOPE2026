# Sharp four-state obstruction and even-cycle spectra for finite semigroup regional proximality

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge 2\), let \(X_m=\mathbb Z/(2m)\mathbb Z\), with its finite discrete topology, and let the transformation monoid \(S_m\) be generated by
\[
a(2j)=a(2j+1)=2j,\qquad b(i)=i+1\pmod{2m}.
\]
All indices in these formulas are interpreted modulo \(2m\). For an \(S_m\)-action write \(\mathrm{RP}\) for the forward regionally proximal relation of Blahodatna–Detmold–Glasscock–Le and \(\mathrm{RP}^{-}\) for their backward relation. Then:

1. The action \(S_m\curvearrowright X_m\) is minimal and is generated by two noncommuting transformations. Its monoid has exactly \(6m\) elements, and their image cardinalities are precisely \(m\) and \(2m\).
2. The complete forward relation and the complete backward relation are
\[
\mathrm{RP}=\{(i,i):i\in X_m\}\cup
\{(i,i+1),(i+1,i):i\in X_m\},
\qquad \mathrm{RP}^{-}=\{(i,i):i\in X_m\}.
\]
Thus the graph of distinct forward regionally proximal pairs is exactly the chordless cycle \(C_{2m}\). In particular \(0\mathrel{\mathrm{RP}}1\) and \(1\mathrel{\mathrm{RP}}2\) but \((0,2)\notin\mathrm{RP}\).
3. Among **all** minimal actions of transformation monoids on finite nonempty spaces, four points are the smallest number permitting nontransitivity of the forward regionally proximal relation. One generator never suffices. Both bounds are attained by the displayed system with \(m=2\).

Each such system lacks a common invariant probability measure. Consequently this is a sharp finite-state obstruction to extending transitivity and forward/backward equality beyond the commutative-action hypothesis; it does **not** answer the invariant-measure question for noncommutative semigroups.

## Assumptions and scope

A semigroup action here is by self-maps of a finite discrete compact Hausdorff space. An action is minimal if every forward semigroup orbit is the whole space. We use the definitions in the motivating source:
\[
\mathrm{RP}=\bigcap_{\alpha\supseteq\Delta}\overline{\bigcup_{s\in S}(s\times s)^{-1}\alpha},
\qquad
\mathrm{RP}^{-}=\bigcap_{\alpha\supseteq\Delta}\overline{\bigcup_{s\in S}(s\times s)\alpha},
\]
where \(\Delta\) is the diagonal and \(\alpha\) ranges over its open neighborhoods. Because the topology is finite discrete, choosing \(\alpha=\Delta\) shows that \((x,y)\in\mathrm{RP}\) if and only if **some** \(s\in S\) merges them, \(sx=sy\). In the same setting the backward relation consists of diagonal pairs reached from diagonal pairs. These simplifications should not be applied to infinite spaces.

Composition is written in functional order, \(fg=f\circ g\). No invariant-measure, amenability, invertibility, or surjectivity assumption is placed on the individual maps. The sharpness statement on the number of points quantifies over arbitrary finitely or infinitely generated transformation monoids acting minimally on finite sets, not just over the given family.

## Proof

**1. Exact monoid normal form and ranks.** For every \(i\in X_m\), direct evaluation gives
\[
a^2=a,\qquad ab^2=b^2a,\qquad aba=a,\qquad b^{2m}=\operatorname{id}.
\]
The first three identities reduce every word containing \(a\) to \(b^ka b^e\) with \(0\le k<2m\) and \(e\in\{0,1\}\). The words without \(a\) reduce to \(b^k\). Conversely all the displayed normal forms are realized by words in the two generators. For \(e=0\) and \(e=1\), each \(b^ka b^e\) sends \(0\) to \(k\). For \(e=0\) it also sends \(1\) to \(k\), whereas for \(e=1\) it sends \(1\) to \(k+2\). Since \(2m\ge4\), these values differ. This proves that the \(4m\) rank-\(m\) normal forms are pairwise distinct. The \(2m\) powers of \(b\) are pairwise distinct permutations, hence cannot coincide with any rank-\(m\) form. Therefore \(|S_m|=6m\), with rank spectrum exactly \(\{m,2m\}\). The \(2m\)-cycle \(b\) ensures that every orbit is all of \(X_m\), proving minimality. Also \(ab\ne ba\), as evaluation at \(0\) gives \(ab(0)=0\), \(ba(0)=1\).

**2. Exact forward relation.** For each rank-\(m\) normal form \(b^ka\), the only unordered distinct pairs it merges are contained in blocks \(\{2j,2j+1\}\). For each \(b^ka b\), they are contained in blocks \(\{2j-1,2j\}\). Each block is a two-element fiber. These two matchings together are exactly the edges of \(C_{2m}\). No permutation \(b^k\) merges a distinct pair. The finite-discrete description of \(\mathrm{RP}\) in the preceding section gives the claimed relation. It is nontransitive because \(0\) and \(2\) are not adjacent when \(2m\ge4\).

**3. Backward relation and invariant probabilities.** If \(\alpha=\Delta\), then \((s\times s)\alpha\subseteq\Delta\) for every \(s\); therefore \(\mathrm{RP}^{-}\subseteq\Delta\). Every \(x\in X_m\) lies in the image of \(b\), so its diagonal pair belongs to the backward relation: \(\mathrm{RP}^{-}=\Delta\). An \(S_m\)-invariant probability would be \(b\)-invariant and hence uniform because \(b\) is a single cycle. But \(a\) maps every state to an even state and does not preserve the uniform distribution. No common invariant probability exists.

**4. The universal four-point lower bound.** In an arbitrary finite discrete monoid action, \(\mathrm{RP}\) is the relation of mergeable pairs. It is symmetric and reflexive. Further, if \(x\) and \(y\) cannot be merged, neither can \(sx\) and \(sy\) for any \(s\): otherwise composing \(s\) with a merging map would merge \(x\) and \(y\). On a set of at most two points, every symmetric reflexive relation is transitive. On a three-point set, a nontransitive symmetric reflexive relation would have exactly two of the three distinct unordered pairs related, leaving a **unique** nonmergeable pair \(\{u,v\}\). By the forward-invariance property, every \(s\) must map \(\{u,v\}\) into that same pair; it cannot identify the points, or else they would be mergeable. Thus \(\{u,v\}\) is a nonempty proper forward-invariant subset, contradicting minimality. The example at \(m=2\) attains four points.

If a finite minimal action has only one generating transformation \(t\), all its orbits equal the whole state set, so \(t\) is a cyclic permutation. No two distinct states can then be merged, and \(\mathrm{RP}=\Delta\). Thus two generators are also necessary; \(a,b\) attain this bound.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` computes the full transformation monoid by exact tuple composition, independently computes the proposed \(6m\)-element normal-form set, compares all pairwise merging relations, checks the backward relation, verifies ranks and transitivity of state orbits, and exhaustively checks every two-letter minimal automaton on two or three states. It passed for \(2\le m\le16\), including \(6{,}055\) indexed normal-form and relation checks and the full finite two-letter tests. This finite test is **not** the reason the theorem holds for all \(m\): the normal form and three-point argument above provide the universal proof.

Run: `python3 verify.py`. Only Python's standard library is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Blahodatna, Detmold, Glasscock and Le prove equivalence of forward regional proximality with the equicontinuous structure relation, and equality with the backward relation, for minimal **commutative** semigroup actions (Theorems B and C); their Section 7.2 discusses the noncommutative frontier. They also cite previously known minimal nonabelian group actions with nontransitive regionally proximal relations. Accordingly the existence of nontransitivity outside the commutative hypothesis is **not** itself new. The present claim is the sharp finite-state/generator threshold and the explicit entire even-cycle family, including exact transformation-monoid normal forms and forward/backward spectra.

Schaefer's synchronization paper defines the **kernel graph** of a finite transformation semigroup using pairs which **cannot** be merged (Section 2). Our forward relation is exactly the complement of that graph off the diagonal, so no novelty is claimed for the finite-state equivalence between regional proximality and pair synchronization or for the general kernel-graph framework. The explicit computation here says that the kernel graph of \(S_m\) is the complement of \(C_{2m}\), with precise monoid order and minimum-state consequences. A generic graph-theoretic kernel-graph observation does not by itself state that this specific two-generated family has these properties. Equivalent prior examples in the automata literature remain a genuine bibliographic risk.

## Limitations

The action contains a nonsurjective generator and preserves no common probability measure. It therefore does not answer the paper's Question 7.2 concerning minimal semigroup actions that preserve a probability measure. The theorem gives a particular family and a sharp first cardinality, not a classification of all possible finite regional-proximal graphs. The four-point threshold is elementary once the finite-discrete pair-merging interpretation is recognized; its significance lies in the combined explicit cycle realization and exact semigroup structure, not in difficulty of the inequality alone. No priority or independent audit is claimed.
