# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The combinatorial law and beta-binomial representation are established in O'Neill; the proof checks exact normalization, mean and variance, beta-gamma standardized convergence, stable conditional-binomial convergence and the compact-subsequence argument. The finite-mean boundary follows from an explicit total-variation coupling and Le Cam's binomial–Poisson bound. The accompanying checker directly compares exact counting and moment expressions; finite checks do not prove asymptotics.

**Originality — PASS on inspected sources.** The 2022 preprint and 2025 full journal text of O'Neill have the same Theorems 6–7 with two narrower sufficient parameter regimes, not this if-and-only-if classification. Chatterjee's source concerns fixed interior continuous-population quantiles. Li and Ding concerns finite-population averages and rank tests. Existing beta-binomial and negative-hypergeometric identities are credited. The closest unresolved priority issue is Khan (1994), cited by O'Neill for one near-census regime; its full paper was not examined. A historically equivalent general result under other terminology remains possible and would invalidate originality if established.

**Value — PASS.** The criterion identifies the exact boundary at which Gaussian modeling remains valid when survey fraction, rank fraction and the census gap can vary jointly or oscillate. The Poisson alternative shows what takes its place at the natural failure boundary, and the finite-sample total-variation estimate gives a reproducible quantitative check. This is a complete classification within an explicitly motivated growing-rank regime, not a routine numerical approximation.

**Scope and limitations.** Both rank distances and the number of omitted population elements must diverge. No claim is made for fixed ranks, a joint quantile process, a uniform finite-sample normal bound or independent scientific certification. The closest historical references should be reviewed independently before publication.
