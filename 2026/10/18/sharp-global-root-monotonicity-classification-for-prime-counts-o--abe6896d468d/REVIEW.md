# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement quantifies over every integer \(n\ge2\) and \(k\ge1\). The crucial bound \(\Delta\le\lceil n/2\rceil\) counts only odd candidates in \((nk,n(k+1)]\) once \(nk\ge17\). Rosser--Schoenfeld gives \(\pi(x)>x/\log x\). Elementary differentiation establishes \((\log x)^5<x^2\) for \(x\ge17\) and \((\log x)^3<x\) for \(x\ge128\). These give strict positivity throughout both infinite regions. Exact integer powers, not floats, classify all \(71\) remaining pairs. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Sun's 2014 Conjecture 2.7(i) is restricted to \(1\le k\le n\), \(n>4\). A prior bounded-range proof covers that restriction; it does not assert or logically entail all \(k>n\). Full text of Sun--Zhao (2020) addresses representation among prime-count values rather than the root inequality. Direct searches by aliases, integer-power equivalents, and exceptional parameters, including a semantic mathematical-results search, did not identify an exact or dominating published result. Search absence is not uniqueness proof. An unindexed or stronger equivalent inequality remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The old conjectural parameter triangle is extended to an entire infinite half-plane, and the complete low-parameter behavior is now identified sharply. The improvement is not a fixed larger numerical cutoff: it covers infinitely many \(k>n\) for each fixed \(n\ge5\), identifies the new ray \(n=3\), and resolves whether the sequence is strictly decreasing, weakly decreasing, or initially increasing for every integer step \(n\ge2\). These are natural complete monotonicity properties of an existing arithmetic sequence rather than arbitrary slices. The proof is short but the mathematical classification is not a rewording of the bounded theorem.
