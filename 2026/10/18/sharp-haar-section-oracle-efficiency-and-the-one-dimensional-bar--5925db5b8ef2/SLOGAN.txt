The exact worst-case expected Frank–Wolfe gap fraction retained by a Haar-random \(d\)-dimensional section of a Euclidean ball is \((d-1)/(n-1)\) in dimension \(n\ge2\), sharpening earlier positive bounds and revealing a sharp one-versus-two dimensional threshold. For a random line, the chance of any progress at angular separation \(\phi\) is precisely \(\phi/\pi\), so the expected wait for the first improvement diverges like \(\pi/\phi\) near a boundary optimum.
