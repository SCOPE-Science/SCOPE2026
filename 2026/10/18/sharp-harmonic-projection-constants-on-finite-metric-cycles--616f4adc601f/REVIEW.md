# Scientific assessment: harmonic projections on metric cycles

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns all real unit-edge cycles of length \(n\ge3\) and every nonconstant real harmonic of order \(m\ge2\). The proof constructs an exact isometry between the Lipschitz space and a zero-sum \(\ell_\infty\) hyperplane. Cyclic averaging shows that the Fourier multiplier is optimal among *all* linear projections onto the prescribed spectral subspace. The dual quotient norm is a cosine median-deviation sum; its even and odd evaluations give the displayed exact constants, the complete contractivity criterion and the limiting constant. The small-dimension and one-dimensional boundary cases are treated explicitly. The companion program separately checks 1,998 finite trigonometric identities and 992 constrained optimization instances; it does not replace the uniform proof.

## Originality — PASS (with bibliographic risk)
The closest inspected primary group-action paper, *Projections in Lipschitz-free spaces induced by group actions* (Cúth–Doucha, arXiv:2104.11519v1), constructs orbit-related complemented spaces, not sharp nontrivial Fourier-eigenspace projections on cycles. The inspected public results on cycle numerical index, three-frequency \(\ell_\infty\) Fourier projection norms, and two-frequency complex-character concentration treat different operators and invariants: none gives or implies the cosine-median quotient norm or the full order-dependent complementability classification. Searches used metric-cycle, zero-sum-hyperplane, median, real harmonic, minimal projection, and Fourier aliases. Exact equivalence with an unindexed older cyclic approximation result cannot be excluded; no independent literature certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete parameter-free classification over every cycle size and frequency, not a single numerical table or an arbitrary finite slice. The exact Banach-space projection constant depends only on character order, has a sharp low-order contractivity threshold, and tends to \(4/\pi\) in all residue classes. It supplies structural quantitative information about the complementability of intrinsic Fourier subspaces of discrete Lipschitz spaces; the zero-sum derivative constraint makes the constants differ from ordinary \(\ell_\infty\) Fourier projection norms.

## Limitations
Only real scalars, unit-edge cycles and one harmonic pair are covered. The proof does not evaluate multi-harmonic projections or weighted graph metrics. Finite computations confirm examples but not the infinite quantifiers; older unindexed equivalent statements remain the central residual originality risk. No independent audit was performed.
