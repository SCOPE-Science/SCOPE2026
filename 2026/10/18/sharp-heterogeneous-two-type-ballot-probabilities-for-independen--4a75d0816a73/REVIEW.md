# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The winning event is unambiguously specified by the first completion; continuity excludes ties. The three combinatorial endpoint pairings have conditional success counts \(1,1,2\) for a given collector among four fair orientations. The nested-interval probability follows from conditional independence and a squared CDF increment, with both terms identified as twice a variance. The sharp bound follows from orthogonal conditional expectation projections of the Bernoulli ordering event. A separated-support mixture attains every point of the interval. Exponential substitution and the positive polynomial factorization prove the claimed narrower range. Exact rational race enumeration checked all 324 ordered integer rate pairs and yielded 352 checks in total.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Long (arXiv:2605.09641, especially introduction, Secs. 2–8 and concluding extension discussion) considers synchronous discrete uniform draws and a large-type asymptotic, not the independent continuous-clock model or a distribution-free two-type extremal theorem. Myers and Wilf (arXiv:math/0304229, Sec. 2 and 2.9) treat the synchronous discrete model, and note its ballot problem. Published record searches under two-sample rank permutations, exponential races, interval containment and heterogeneous coupon collection found no exact stronger covering statement. The equal-law Catalan value and orthogonal projection method are prior tools. An older general rank-event characterization remains a genuine priority uncertainty.

## Value

**PASS.** The theorem completely classifies the minimum continuous-clock model of two collectors and two types under arbitrary heterogeneous first-arrival laws, and exhibits a sharp 3/4 versus 2/3 contrast between general clocks and memoryless Poissonized collecting. This identifies which waiting-time structure matters for the probability of never trailing, beyond the discrete-round asymptotic. The explicit formula is a tractable benchmark for extensions to multiple types; no such extension is claimed.

## Limitations and nearest sources

The event is not the synchronous-round event of Long or Myers–Wilf. Clock laws are atomless, clocks independent and collectors identically distributed within each type. No claim is made for dependent clocks, discrete-time ties or three-plus types. The general exact identity may be a corollary of unlocated broader rank-order theory. Independent scientific and priority assessments are still outstanding.
