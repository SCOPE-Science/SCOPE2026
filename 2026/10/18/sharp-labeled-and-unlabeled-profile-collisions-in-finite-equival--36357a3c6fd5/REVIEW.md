# Scientific review: finite-equivalence profile collisions

## Correctness

PASS. The claim explicitly fixes the signature (equality plus one equivalence relation, no parameters). Sorting the block-size demands supplies an exact necessary and sufficient capacity test. Factorial counts enumerate labeled partitions without overcounting, and equality-block partitioning gives the Stirling transform. The triangular matrix recovers the labeled profile; the positive top coefficient yields the large-arity exponential base. The five-point profiles and all partitions through size five are given explicitly, so minimality does not depend on a numerical search. Independent direct enumeration of 44 small class-size structures agreed with the formula, including the complete collision through arity seven.

## Originality

PASS, with qualification. The source on relational open definability by Areces, Campercholi, Penazzi and Ventura (arXiv:1904.04637) treats preservation by partial isomorphisms and complexity rather than the exact all-arity collision threshold for finite equivalence relations. Campercholi, Tellechea and Ventura (arXiv:2303.17017) treat algorithms for function symbols. Pouzet and Thiéry (arXiv:0801.4404) define and analyze the unlabeled age-profile invariant, not the sharp labeled-versus-unlabeled equivalence-relation collision pair here. The Stirling transform and set-partition counting are standard methods; their rediscovery is not presented as original. Searches of relevant primary sources and mathematical research records did not identify the stated sharp pair and contrast. Unindexed prior examples remain a risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Counting definable relations in each arity is a natural compression of quantifier-free expressive power. The theorem determines its exact information content for all finite equivalence structures and supplies a sharp, fully classified boundary at which it fails to recover even a class-size multiset. The one-element difference between the first unlabeled and first labeled collision shows how the two natural inverse invariants differ. It is an exact structural counterexample and classification, not an arbitrary finite-table recomputation or a general claim of graph-profile uniqueness.

## Scope and limits

The five-point collision is only numerical; the actual sets of quantifier-free types differ, and a first-order formula distinguishes the structures. Degenerate equality/total relations are excluded from the nondegenerate cutoff. The classification is not extrapolated beyond five points. Published and indexed comparisons are not an independent proof of publication priority.
