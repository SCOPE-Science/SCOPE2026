# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The one-vacancy reduction gives an autonomous symmetric lane walk with a conditionally Poisson longitudinal count. The finite-chain covariance identity and cycle Poisson equation prove the general variance formula. Binary signs reduce sharp layout bounds to a parity lower bound and a strictly concave circular-gap minimization of a Green-kernel energy. Equality conditions are proved, including their uniqueness. The standalone checker independently reconstructs the full interacting-state generator and uses exact rational linear algebra for 18 test cases; 4,704 binary layouts supply additional finite consistency checks, not an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS within inspected material, subject to residual bibliographic risk.** The closest physical paper, Ezaki–Nishinari (2011; author-uploaded complete manuscript inspected), establishes stationary factorization and mean current for this multilane geometry. It does not state the long-time variance formula or optimize that variance over all permutations of two lane speeds. Asanjarani–Nazarathy (2022; complete author-uploaded text inspected) establishes general Markov-modulated arrival-process theory, including count overdispersion, but does not state this cylinder-specific sharp arrangement inequality. Ishiguro–Sato (2024; full open-access article inspected) extends exact multidimensional stationary laws and treats heterogeneous fast/slow lanes and mean current, but does not evaluate integrated longitudinal counting noise or optimal cyclic speed placements. Standard Markov-modulated Poisson variance methods and cycle Green functions remain acknowledged as antecedents. An older result combining those tools into an equivalent rearrangement theorem could still cover the claim; searches cannot certify uniqueness.

**Value: PASS.** At fixed particle count, fixed multiset of lane rates, and identical stationary measures and mean activity, the arrangement of lanes can change temporal counting noise by an exact unbounded factor in its excess over Poisson noise. Determining the sharp minimum and maximum over exponentially many configurations is a meaningful spatial-design and model-reduction question, rather than a selected small numerical instance. The result explicitly distinguishes equal-time statistics from dynamical fluctuations. It is limited to the high-density one-vacancy sector and does not purport to solve general-density transport.
