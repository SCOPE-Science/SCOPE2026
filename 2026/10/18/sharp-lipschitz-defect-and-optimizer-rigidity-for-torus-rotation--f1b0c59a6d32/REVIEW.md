# Review: sharp Lipschitz torsion defects for torus translations

## Correctness
**PASS.** The analytic proof first identifies every torsion on a continuous-function lattice as multiplication by a continuous unimodular phase. Torus homotopy supplies an integral winding vector; the metric normalization ensures its Euclidean norm does not exceed the phase's Lipschitz seminorm. The phase-error function has a Haar mean fixed by the winding and the translation. If the operator defect is below two, connectedness forces all phase errors into one open semicircle-sized branch, bounding the defect below by the character phase distance; characters attain this lower bound. Equality then forces an invariant real phase, constant for dense rotations. The proof treats the diameter-two endpoint separately and covers all dimensions and nonintegral budgets. Finite-grid perturbation checks are illustrative only.

## Originality
**PASS under the examined literature, with residual priority risk.** Dobrick's October 2026 cyclicity paper is the closest primary source: its Example 3.2 proves impossibility of exact anticommutation on an irrational circle, but does not determine a Lipschitz-constrained norm minimum in any dimension or classify minimizers. The explicit bound is not merely the statement that characters are Koopman eigenfunctions: it proves that every noncharacter phase, in its winding class, has defect at least the character's, and proves rigidity of equality. Searches under approximate eigenfunctions, circle cocycles, winding, and norm commutators did not identify a published covering result. Nearby work on regularity of Sturmian cohomological transfers and ergodic Daugavet renormings concerns different objects; it does not imply this norm optimization. The full primary texts of Exel’s *Soft Torus II* (1992, §§4–5) and Hua–Lin’s *Rotation algebras and Exel trace formula* (2014, Theorem 3.7) were checked for broader implications: both concern general pairs of unitaries, perturbation or trace/Bott obstructions, but neither fixes a Koopman translation while optimizing multiplication torsions with a Euclidean Lipschitz budget, nor establishes the optimizer rigidity. The trace formula might supply an alternate route to a lower bound, so the classical topological ingredients are not represented as novel. Unindexed topological cocycle literature is an acknowledged risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The exact operator-norm obstruction is the natural quantitative refinement of a concrete no-go example in the 2026 positive-operator cyclicity problem, and distinguishes fixed-translation/varying-torsion approximation from that paper's fixed-torsion/varying-minorant scheme. It gives the complete finite regularity-cost profile in arbitrary torus dimension, identifies every sharp optimizer below the diameter endpoint, and separates the full peripheral spectrum from achievable exact torsion intertwining. The Lipschitz budget is intrinsic to measuring phase complexity, not an arbitrary slice, and the sharp cost records meaningful Diophantine dependence on the rotation.

## Limits
The theorem does not address arbitrary positive Banach-lattice operators, does not claim a universal approximation rate, and does not assert rigidity at defect two. Its homotopy and character ingredients are classical; the asserted contribution is the sharp combined optimization and rigidity statement.
