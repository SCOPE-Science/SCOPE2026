# Scientific review: weighted best-line metric projections

## Correctness — PASS
The implicit scalar first-order equation follows from strict convexity of the real \(\ell_p\) objective. The derivative is the explicit nonnegative vector \(g_i=v_i|r_i|^{p-2}\) normalized by \(\sum_jv_jg_j\). For \(p>2\), normalizing the positive and negative moment masses and using the dual two-coordinate test proves the all-support upper bound; two-supported residuals attain its optimal infinitesimal value. For \(p<2\), a smooth monotone regularization proves the global bound without unjustified differentiability at vanishing residuals, and a nonzero-residual limiting family gives sharpness. Two-support metric projection is linear, and contractivity follows from equality in Hölder. The proof covers all quantified dimensions, generators, and exponents; finite checker results supplement rather than replace it.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The 2017 Borodin–Druzhinin–Chesnokova Theorem 2 was inspected in its complete primary text: it gives bounds and the finite-atomic Lipschitz criterion, not exact moduli for arbitrary weighted lines. The 2025 Németh Theorem 1 was inspected in its complete arXiv text: it characterizes Hilbert-space linearity of all metric projections qualitatively, not this exact quantitative fixed-line problem. Published calculations of the constant-vector line supply the equal-weight special case, which is acknowledged as covered. A published weighted cross-polytope calculation has the same two-dimensional rank-one norm factor, which is likewise acknowledged as a shared ingredient. The new asserted content is the exact nonuniform all-weight line formula with the two-coordinate reduction for \(p>2\), smallest-coordinate dominance for \(p<2\), and the sharp support/weight contractivity dichotomy. The full 1997 Berens–Finzel–Li–Xu paper was not accessible on the checked routes; a stronger earlier all-weight formulation remains a publication-priority risk. These searches do not establish bibliographic uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Earlier work motivated quantitative sharp bounds and supplied only coarse general estimates for finite-atomic lines. The new formula handles every positive weight pattern and every exponent in one unified classification. It exposes a structural optimization change at the Hilbert exponent: a worst two-coordinate pair above \(2\), against a single minimally weighted coordinate below \(2\), with nonexpansivity exactly classified for arbitrary supports. This is not merely reparametrization of the equal-weight case: unequal weights determine which pair dominates and produce arbitrarily large constants even with three active coordinates.

## Limitations and remaining checks
This is an internally reconstructed mathematical proof, not an independent audit. A historical result stated via a different modulus, a best-approximation error bound, or a dual rank-one projection could cover part of the formula. The present proof does not classify maximizing *separated* point pairs, does not address complex \(\ell_p\), and does not address nonatomic \(L_p\).

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
