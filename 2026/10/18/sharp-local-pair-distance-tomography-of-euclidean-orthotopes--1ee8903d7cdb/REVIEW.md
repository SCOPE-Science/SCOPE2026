# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For two independent volume-uniform points the difference density equals the normalized product of triangular one-coordinate densities. Integrating its finite expansion over a sufficiently small Euclidean ball gives an exact polynomial and the gamma coefficients. Since the volume normalization fixes the top elementary symmetric function of reciprocal normalized edge lengths, the first \(d-1\) nontrivial coefficients determine the root polynomial and hence the shape. Local polynomial coefficient perturbations show minimality. Rouché root matching proves compact uniform \(1/d\)-Hölder stability; a real-rooted constant-term perturbation normalized to fixed product proves its optimality even near the cube. The proof checks strict small-radius restrictions, duplicate roots, and nondegenerate positive lengths. The packaged checker verified finite numerical samples independently of coefficient expansion; the infinite-quantifier results are analytical.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)

The complete arXiv HTML of Wang's 2026 pair-distance paper was inspected, including its introduction and the indicated chapters on the covariogram/Minkowski problem and rigidity. It explicitly gives the normalized profile and the first perimeter correction, but not the all-dimensional orthotope short-distance jet or sharp inverse count. A complete published convex-polygon result covers the planar local quartic and angle stability but not dimensions \(d\ge3\). A complete published orthotope result uses *projection-volume moment statistics*, a different observation model; even its sharp \(1/d\) root stability does not imply the pair-distance coefficients here. The classical boxed free-path result concerns random chords and line trajectories. Exact formulas for rectangular distance distributions may exist in older unindexed tables, and the free-path source was checked only at abstract level: neither is treated as a proof of historical uniqueness. Closest-result and equivalence searches compared mathematical implications rather than solely titles.

## Value — PASS

Radial averaging loses the directional covariogram, and the motivating study emphasizes non-identifiability for general convex bodies. The theorem identifies a geometrically natural class where only \(d-1\) *local* scalar observations nevertheless determine shape, and proves both the information-theoretic minimal coefficient count and the optimal quantitative stability exponent. Those two sharp bounds distinguish the result from merely expanding an easy product. The proof yields a constructive polynomial-root inversion useful for assessing stability of radial pair-distance morphology. No claim is made about general convex bodies, nonorthogonal parallelotopes, or finite-sample statistical rates.

## Scientific limitations

Unpublished or poorly indexed rectangular-box distance literature may contain equivalent results. The sharp global result is limited to orthotopes in Euclidean dimensions at least three. The distance CDF is polynomial only before the shortest normalized side threshold; inverse stability holds on compact strictly positive ratio classes. No independent scientific review has been conducted.
