# Sharp logarithmic line-closure depth and direct Horn-basis inflation in finite projective geometries

## Finding

**Theorem (basis–depth profile).** Fix a prime power \(q\ge 2\) and an integer \(d\ge 2\). Let \(P=\operatorname{PG}(d-1,q)\) be the set of one-dimensional vector subspaces of \(\mathbb F_q^d\), equipped with projective span closure \(c\), where \(c(\varnothing)=\varnothing\). Write
\[
N=\frac{q^d-1}{q-1},\qquad N_j=\frac{q^j-1}{q-1}.
\]
Consider implications between subsets of \(P\), with the standard convention that a rule with one premise and multiple conclusions is one **grouped** implication.

1. **Canonical basis.** Every two-point set and no other set is a Duquenne–Guigues pseudo-intent. The canonical grouped basis is precisely
   \[
   \bigl\{\{a,b\}\longrightarrow c(\{a,b\})\setminus\{a,b\}:a,b\in P,\ a\ne b\bigr\}.
   \]
   It has \(\binom{N}{2}\) grouped implications, or \((q-1)\binom{N}{2}\) individual unit conclusions.

2. **Optimal one-pass basis.** Call an implication family *synchronously direct* if one simultaneous firing of every rule whose premise was already present recovers \(c(S)\), for every \(S\subseteq P\). The unique minimum-cardinality grouped direct basis after deleting surplus conclusions uses exactly one premise \(A\) for each independent subset \(A\subseteq P\) with \(2\le |A|\le d\). Its consequent consists of the points in \(c(A)\) having **all nonzero coordinates** relative to the projective basis \(A\). The exact minimum grouped size and unit size are
   \[
   D_{\mathrm{group}}(d,q)=\sum_{r=2}^{d}I_r(d,q),\qquad
   D_{\mathrm{unit}}(d,q)=\sum_{r=2}^{d}I_r(d,q)(q-1)^{r-1},
   \]
   where
   \[
   I_r(d,q)=\frac1{r!}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(N-N_j).
   \]
   The minimum-unit statement means the smallest number of single-conclusion rules in a synchronously direct basis. It is different from minimizing the total number of literals or the ordinary non-direct basis.

3. **Exact rounds of the canonical pair basis.** Start from \(R_0(S)=S\) and, for all integers \(t\ge0\), put
   \[
   R_{t+1}(S)=R_t(S)\cup\bigcup_{\{a,b\}\subseteq R_t(S)}c(\{a,b\}).
   \]
   For every \(S\subseteq P\) and \(t\ge0\),
   \[
   \boxed{R_t(S)=\bigcup_{\substack{A\subseteq S\\|A|\le 2^t}}c(A).}
   \]
   Consequently, among input sets of projective rank exactly \(r\), the worst possible number of synchronous iterations until full closure is **exactly** \(\lceil\log_2 r\rceil\), for \(1\le r\le d\). The empty set needs zero iterations. Every independent \(r\)-point set attains the bound.

For the projective plane \(d=3\), writing \(N=q^2+q+1\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{group}}(3,q)=\binom{N}{2}+\binom{N}{3}-N\binom{q+1}3.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{PG}(2,2)\) has \(21\) canonical versus \(49\) direct grouped rules, and \(\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\) has \(78\) versus \(312\), while the latter has \(1092\) direct unit rules. For fixed \(q\), as \(d\to\infty\), \(\log_q D_{\mathrm{group}}(d,q)=d^2-O(d\log d)\), but the canonical grouped count is of order \(q^{2d}\), giving a strong size–round tradeoff.

## Assumptions and scope

A projective point is a one-dimensional subspace, not an unnormalized nonzero vector. Consequently no two ground elements are parallel, and every projective line through two distinct points contains exactly \(q+1\) points. A set of projective points is independent if any selection of nonzero vector representatives is linearly independent. Its rank is the vector-space dimension of their span. The closure \(c(S)\) contains all projective points of the vector span, not just those generated by pairwise sums already in \(S\).

The pseudo-intent definition is recursive: a nonclosed set \(Q\) is a pseudo-intent when \(c(T)\subseteq Q\) for every proper pseudo-intent \(T\subset Q\). A grouped implication \(A\to B\) has nonempty consequent \(B\subseteq c(A)\setminus A\). A *synchronous one-pass application* tests every premise against the original input set, not against points generated earlier in the same pass. These conventions distinguish all size and depth assertions.

## Proof

**Canonical premises.** Singletons and the empty set are closed. Every two-point set \(A\) is nonclosed, because its projective line contains another \(q-1\ge1\) points. Hence each pair is a pseudo-intent. Suppose a nonclosed \(Q\) of size at least three were also a pseudo-intent. Its recursive condition forces \(c(\{a,b\})\subseteq Q\) for every two distinct \(a,b\in Q\). A nonempty projective point set containing the full line through every pair of its points is a projective subspace: the associated union of one-dimensional vector spaces, together with the zero vector, is closed under arbitrary field-linear combinations. Therefore \(Q=c(Q)\), a contradiction. Hence exactly the pairs are pseudo-intents. Each pair's line supplies \(q-1\) distinct new points, proving the canonical counts.

**All direct premises and counts.** Let \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_r\}\) be independent with \(r\ge2\), and choose representatives \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\). A point \(p\in c(A)\) lies outside \(c(A\setminus\{a_i\})\) for every \(i\) precisely when its expansion \(p=[\sum_i\alpha_i v_i]\) has \(\alpha_i\ne0\) for every \(i\). There are \((q-1)^r/(q-1)=(q-1)^{r-1}\) such projective points; none lies in \(A\). For each such \(p\), starting with input \(A\), no sound rule with a proper-subset premise can introduce \(p\) in a single simultaneous pass. Thus every direct unit basis must contain the implication \(A\to p\), and every direct grouped basis must contain a rule with premise exactly \(A\) and these roots among its conclusions if there is to be only one such rule.

Conversely, if \(p\in c(S)\setminus S\), choose a subset \(A\subseteq S\) inclusion-minimal with \(p\in c(A)\). Linear exchange makes \(A\) independent; minimality means all its coefficients for \(p\) are nonzero, and \(|A|\ge2\). Hence the described rules add \(p\) in one pass from \(S\). They are sufficient, and the previous compulsory-premise argument proves all claimed minima and the unique grouped minimum upon removing excess conclusions. To count independent \(r\)-sets, order their elements: after \(j\) independent points have been chosen, exactly \(N_j\) projective points are forbidden. Division by \(r!\) gives \(I_r\), and multiplication by the number of full-support roots gives the unit count.

**Exact iteration support.** We prove the displayed equality for \(R_t(S)\) by induction. At \(t=0\), the union of spans of singleton subsets is precisely \(S\), with the empty set contributing nothing. Suppose the formula holds at time \(t\). Two generated points each supported on at most \(2^t\) original points have a joining projective line contained in the span of the union of their supports. This proves the forward inclusion at time \(t+1\).

For the reverse inclusion take \(p\in c(A)\), where \(|A|\le2^{t+1}\). Partition \(A=A_1\sqcup A_2\) with each part having at most \(2^t\) points. Express a representative of \(p\) as the sum of two vectors \(w_1+w_2\), with \(w_i\) in the vector span of \(A_i\). If one summand vanishes, \(p\) belongs to \(R_t(S)\). Otherwise the projective points \([w_1]\) and \([w_2]\) lie in \(R_t(S)\), and \(p\) lies on their line. Thus \(p\in R_{t+1}(S)\) and the induction is complete.

If \(\operatorname{rank}(S)=r\), choose an independent spanning set \(B\subseteq S\) of size \(r\). Once \(2^t\ge r\), the support formula includes \(c(B)=c(S)\), so closure is reached. Conversely take \(S=B\) independent of size \(r\) and choose \(p=[v_1+\cdots+v_r]\) with all coefficients nonzero. Then \(p\) lies in no span of a proper subset of \(B\); it is not in \(R_t(B)\) when \(2^t<r\). This proves the sharp bound. The plane formula follows by counting all triples and removing the \(N\binom{q+1}3\) collinear triples, since every projective pair determines a unique line.

Finally, for fixed \(q\), the independent full-rank term equals \(I_d(d,q)=\frac1{d!}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{q^d-q^j}{q-1}\), which yields \(\log_q I_d(d,q)=d^2-O(d\log d)\); the bounds \(I_d\le D_{\mathrm{group}}\le dN^d\) prove a matching logarithmic upper bound. Meanwhile \(\binom{N}{2}=\Theta(q^{2d})\). The comparison follows.

## Verification

The self-contained standard-Python file `verify_projective.py` builds projective points and exact spans over the prime fields of orders \(2\) and \(3\). It recursively enumerates the pseudo-intents, identifies every direct minimal premise/root, checks the full finite-field counts, and compares synchronous closure with linear span. To reproduce, execute `python verify_projective.py` using Python 3.8 or later.

The observed outputs were: \(\operatorname{PG}(2,2)\): \(21\) pseudo-intents, \(49\) grouped and unit direct rules, all \(128\) subsets examined; \(\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\): \(78\) pseudo-intents, \(312\) grouped and \(1092\) direct unit rules, all \(8192\) subsets examined; \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\): \(105\) pseudo-intents and \(1365\) direct rules, with \(104\) selected closure inputs. In each, a basis of the ambient vector space attains the asserted maximum round count. Exhaustive finite checks do not substitute for the all-dimension, all-prime-power proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

The pair-to-line basis for vector spaces and the general theory of implication bases are classical; see Wild's survey, Section 4.1.4. Rooted circuits, stems, prime stems and their connections to pseudo-closed sets are studied by Kashiwabara and Nakamura. Bérczi–Boros–Makino study matroid Horn functions and minimization of circuit representations, while Adaricheva–Nourine–Vilmin study the complexity and computation of the \(D\)-base. None of those general definitions or the elementary field-vector counting techniques are claimed as new. The contribution assessed here is the **combined exact projective all-rank direct-rule census and sharp round-by-round support hierarchy**, together with its quantified size/depth contrast. The cited work contains substantial related general theory, so the risk of equivalent implicit formulations is real; the present work is not an independent publication-priority determination.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns Desarguesian finite projective geometries of arbitrary prime-power field order and every dimension at least two. It does not assert the same formulas for arbitrary simple matroids, non-Desarguesian planes, infinite geometries, different forward-chaining semantics, or bases optimized by total literal cost. The exact iteration theorem is an all-input statement, but only small **prime-field** instances were computationally checked; higher extensions and arbitrarily large dimensions are justified mathematically. All completeness and novelty assessments here remain subject to external scholarly validation.
