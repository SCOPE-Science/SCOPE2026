# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The universal ball-simplex inequality follows from an exact covariance determinant identity and eigenvalue AM–GM, including the regular-simplex equality condition. A specified ellipsoid contains the cap because its squared cross-section radius minus the ball's squared cross-section radius is an explicitly nonnegative product. An explicit apex-plus-regular-base simplex maps to a regular simplex in that ellipsoid, attaining its bound. Strict containment away from the two contact layers proves rigidity below the threshold; the tight-frame height identity proves it at the threshold. Above the threshold the full ball bound is attained by an already-feasible regular simplex. Exact-rational diagnostics and finite numerical examples corroborate the derivations; they do not establish the universal claim.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The 2015 MathOverflow cap-volume question is the same object and optimization problem. Its only displayed 2016 answer computes the apex-plus-regular-base lower bound and states that optimality and face-contact assumptions remain to be proved. The lower-bound expression is therefore acknowledged explicitly as known. Horváth–Lángi (arXiv:1402.6496, 2014) prove the regular-simplex optimizer of the *untruncated* ball; their Corollary 1 does not provide the cap-specific encompassing ellipsoid, the contact/equality argument or the saturation transition. Published-result searches for spherical caps, simplex determinants, halfspace-truncated balls and bounding ellipsoids did not locate a statement implying this entire theorem. A historically unlocated equivalent treatment, perhaps phrased in ellipsoid or determinant language, remains possible; a failed search is not proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS
The result settles the optimality question posed for a canonical family of convex bodies, rather than merely evaluating a preferred construction. It supplies an exact all-dimensional piecewise profile, the sharp aspect threshold where enlarging the cap ceases to help, and a complete unrestricted equality classification. These features are structurally relevant to extremal simplex placement and are not a routine numerical recomputation.

## Closest literature and limits
Roy Han, MathOverflow question 198405 (2015-02-25), and Moritz Firsching's lower-bound answer (2016-02-19), supply the original motivating problem and the known construction. Horváth and Lángi, “Maximum volume polytopes inscribed in the unit sphere,” arXiv:1402.6496 (2014), supply the full-ball regular-simplex comparison. The proof concerns only unit-ball caps and does not address more general intersection bodies. The verifier examines finite dimensions and samples; universality comes from the analytic proof. Historical priority still warrants independent assessment.
