# Review of the mesh–localization crossover theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The theorem is explicitly scoped to the second-order (quadratic weak-coupling) friction/noise coefficients. Cyclic Fourier diagonalization gives the aliased Bessel sums and an exact positive, unique modewise tumbling threshold. Gaussian Laplace estimates and a contour-shift exponential envelope justify passage through both infinite alias sums, including when the lattice size increases with localization. Dominated convergence and the cosecant-square identity give the coarse-mesh constant; a theta-series limit gives the jointly refined-mesh constant. Independent finite comparisons of site-space eigenvalues, Bessel identities, and the joint limit passed 492 assertions. No claim about uniform validity of the full physical weak-coupling expansion is made.

## Originality

PASS within accessible literature, with a documented possibility of implicit coverage by general Fourier-sampling asymptotic theory. The exact finite-parameter response and friction/noise formulas are established by Beyen, Maes and Santra (arXiv:2605.16209), Sections III and Appendix C; the continuum-string versions are established by Beyen, Maes and Pei (arXiv:2505.18665), Sections III–IV. The present theorem does not claim those formulas, a generic negative-friction criterion, or elementary Bessel asymptotics as new. Its assessed content is the sharp, *simultaneous* lattice-size/localization limit with a theta-profile transition between two different power laws, including exact mode dependence and the formal point-kernel singularity. Complete accessible relevant primary text was inspected. No equivalent finite-lattice double-scaling theorem was identified in the searched literature or indexed records. The existing all-parameter Fourier formula is the essential starting point, so the result is a derived asymptotic theorem, not independent evidence about the microscopic dynamics; more general aliasing results could imply this special profile.

## Value

PASS. A physically motivated smooth interaction explicitly tends to point coupling while the number of lattice oscillators sets a competing spatial resolution. The theorem identifies the sharp scaling at which that competition changes the induced-friction sign, and supplies an analytic crossover resolving why the fixed finite-chain and dense-chain approximations need not agree. The mode-independent leading negative friction for a fixed finite chain, contrasted with bounded induced noise, provides a precise warning that the perturbative response is not uniform in the point-particle limit. This is an analytic model-structure result, not a claim of observed full-model instability.

## Closest literature and limits

The 2026 chain and 2025 continuum-string papers provide general formulas and sufficient friction sign conditions, but no sharp joint theta-profile mesh–localization crossover in the inspected main text and appendices. General Poisson summation, Bessel local asymptotics, and cyclic sampling are standard mathematical ingredients; a broader theorem could imply the result. The weak-coupling remainder is not shown uniform in localization, lattice refinement, or joint scaling. The excluded boundary modes with vanishing fractional wavenumber require another asymptotic regime.
