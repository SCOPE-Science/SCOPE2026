# Review of the finite-band feedback minimax theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The feedback coordinate change is bijective for every positive shift even if the original dynamics have a nullspace. The transformed reduced Hessian has the spectral function \(f_\delta(a)=(1+\alpha a^2)/(a+\delta)^2\). A derivative test gives the exact worst-case condition-number curve, with maximum at spectral endpoints and minimum at the clipped critical point. Angular reparameterization makes the unique minimax configuration the interval centered on the sine maximum, giving the stated half-angle value. An explicit three-dimensional diagonal witness attains the bound. Symbolic and finite-matrix reproductions provide independent finite checks; the universal statements rely on the written proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Preuster et al. (arXiv:2607.11835v1) establish a feedback framework and optimize an *upper estimate* for a differently normed elliptic example, but do not provide this finite-band exact Euclidean minimax or its three-mode sharpness. Pearson–Stoll–Wathen (2012), DOI 10.1137/110847949, already establish a matching-square preconditioner and its universal factor-two spectral bound; Wang–Chen–Sun (2023), DOI 10.3390/math11122599, repeat the conventional parameter choice and analyze related block preconditioners. These known results are fully credited, rather than redescribed as discoveries. No inspected source or indexed similar claim supplied the unique finite-endpoint optimal tuning formula or exact endpoint-spectrum collapse; algebraically equivalent shifts might still appear elsewhere.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete natural parameter-optimization problem for a control-relevant shifted-square factor, not an isolated numerical threshold. The prior universal fixed shift can have factor \(2\) for the bounded test interval \([0,1]\) at unit regularization, whereas exact spectral-endpoint tuning lowers the sharp factor to \(4-2\sqrt2\). The theorem gives a dimension-independent guarantee and explains when a two-point spectrum is exactly preconditioned, as well as the three-mode obstruction. Knowledge of spectral bounds and expense of shifted inverses limit algorithmic conclusions.

**Scope and residual literature risk.** Finite-dimensional real symmetric positive-semidefinite dynamics, identity observation and control weights, Euclidean conditioning and exact arithmetic only. Related matching-square literature could contain an independently stated or equivalent finite-band optimization not indexed by the searches. No algorithmic wall-clock advantage or general PDE bound is asserted.
