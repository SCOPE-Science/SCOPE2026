# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Explicit inequalities for each nonright tangent cone show that a maximum-length leg vertex has a cone contained in the matching equal-leg cone, strictly when any legs differ. The right vertex is an orthant and cannot determine the minimum of the equal-leg simplex. A Gaussian-to-Dirichlet change of variables, including the simplex Jacobian and gamma integral, proves the exact expression; independent exponential coordinates, the strong law, dominated convergence and Stirling establish the full sharp equivalent. The proof is uniform over all \(d\ge2\) and every positive leg vector. Low-dimensional exact angle identities and Dirichlet moment algebra were checked by the standalone reproducible script. Risk: the large-dimension limit relies on the standard strong law and finite second moment of exponentials, not on sampled data.

**Originality — PASS on available evidence, with residual risk.** The nearest established primary articles concern a universal superexponential bound and regular-simplex angular extremality; their relevant full statements do not imply an equal-leg maximizer **within the orthogonal-edge class** or its \(e^{-1}\) sharp Dirichlet prefactor. Relevant published indexed records concern regular-simplex cone intrinsic volumes or orthoscheme surface-area inequalities, different functionals and equality hypotheses. A related equifacial tetrahedron angular result concerns a different geometric family and does not imply an all-dimensional orthogonal-edge formula. The motivating 2026 manuscript's full text could not be inspected; its abstract instead concerns relationships between fullness and minimum solid angle and so presents residual but not decisive priority uncertainty. No absence-of-search-result is treated as a proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Minimal vertex solid angle is a natural simplicial mesh-quality and geometric shape parameter. The theorem gives the **sharp achievable** minimum-angle threshold in a canonical family admitting arbitrary independent leg distortions, a rigid shape classifier, an exact dimension-dependent angular integral, and the leading asymptotic with its nontrivial constant. The pointwise containment alone would be elementary; the sharp high-dimensional limit makes the combined theorem substantially more informative than a normalization check. No unrestricted simplex extremal statement is inferred.

**Closest mathematical literature and limitations.** The full accessible proofs by Karasev et al. and Akopyan–Karasev concern arbitrary and regular simplices rather than the restricted family. Licht's 2026 preprint is relevant to the angular mesh-quality motivation, but its full manuscript remained inaccessible after arXiv and open-access attempts; institutional access was also unavailable. The possibility of unindexed or inaccessible stronger angular formulas remains a residual originality risk. Finite quadrature and Monte Carlo corroborate only the low-dimensional checks and convergence trend; the universal statements are analytic.
