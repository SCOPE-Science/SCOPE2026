# Sharp modal-depth collapse in the one-letter bilateral state logic

## Finding

Work in the bilateral state-based logic with exactly one propositional letter \(p\), the constants \(\bot\) and \(\mathsf{ne}\), bilateral negation \(\neg\), conjunction \(\wedge\), split disjunction \(\vee\), and the state modality \(\blacklozenge\). Two formulas are *bilaterally equivalent* when they have identical support and antisupport behavior on every team of Boolean valuations of \(p\).

The number of bilateral equivalence classes expressible at modal depths \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\) is, respectively,
\[
\boxed{39,\quad64,\quad66}.
\]
Every formula is bilaterally equivalent to one of modal depth at most \(2\). This depth bound is sharp. The only two classes missing at depth \(1\) have signature masks \((10,12)\) and \((12,10)\), represented respectively by
\[
\blacklozenge\neg\blacklozenge p
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\blacklozenge\neg\blacklozenge\neg p.
\]
Thus the full one-generator algebra of bilateral formula meanings is a \(66\)-element algebra, and the complete modal-depth spectrum is \(39\to64\to66\to66\to\cdots\). The class counts use *both* support and antisupport, not merely the positive denotation of a formula.

## Assumptions and scope

There are exactly two Boolean valuations, \(v_0(p)=0\) and \(v_1(p)=1\), and exactly four teams
\[
 t_0=\varnothing,\quad t_1=\{v_0\},\quad
 t_2=\{v_1\},\quad t_3=\{v_0,v_1\}.
\]
The semantics, including treatment of the empty team, are exactly the clauses in §3.1 of Aloni et al., *Teams, Truthmakers, Translations* (arXiv:2610.15680v1). In particular, subteams in split disjunction may be empty; for the modality the witnessing subteam must be nonempty; the modality preserves antisupport; and negation exchanges support and antisupport. No additional propositional connectives, atomic constants, or accessibility relation are admitted. Modal depth counts occurrences of \(\blacklozenge\) along the deepest syntactic branch; \(\neg,\wedge,\vee\) do not increase it.

For each formula \(\varphi\), write \(P_\varphi\) and \(N_\varphi\) for its subsets of the four teams supporting and antisupporting it. Encode a family of teams by
\[
  m(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{3} 2^i\,\mathbf1_{\{t_i\in X\}}.
\]
The signature of \(\varphi\) is \((m(P_\varphi),m(N_\varphi))\), an ordered pair of integers from \(0\) to \(15\). Signatures are compared on all four teams, so equality of signatures is precisely bilateral equivalence under this fixed one-letter valuation semantics.

## Proof

**Semantic algebra.** For sets \(X,Y\) of teams define
\[
 X*Y=\{s\cup u:s\in X,\ u\in Y\},\qquad
 \mathcal D(X)=\{t:\exists s\in X\,(s\neq\varnothing\land s\subseteq t)\}.
\]
Each family is a subset of the fixed four-element team universe. Directly applying the semantic clauses gives the following exact operations on ordered pairs of families:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\neg(P,N)&=(N,P),\\
(P,N)\wedge(Q,M)&=(P\cap Q,\ N*M),\\
(P,N)\vee(Q,M)&=(P*Q,\ N\cap M),\\
\blacklozenge(P,N)&=(\mathcal D(P),N).
\end{aligned}
\]
The three primitive signatures are
\[
 \bot=(1,15),\qquad\mathsf{ne}=(14,1),\qquad p=(5,3).
\]
These equations prove by structural induction that every formula is evaluated exactly by the finite algebra described above.

**Exhaustive finite closure.** Let \(L_0\) be the least set containing these three primitive signatures and closed under the three propositional operations (negation, conjunction, split disjunction). Recursively put
\[
 L_{d+1}=\operatorname{Cl}_{\neg,\wedge,\vee}
 \bigl(L_d\cup\{\blacklozenge x:x\in L_d\}\bigr).
\]
A second structural induction shows that \(L_d\) is exactly the set of signatures of formulas of modal depth at most \(d\): every stage is generated by actual formulas, and every formula of bounded depth is obtained through these operations.

The embedded deterministic certificate `signature_levels.json`, replayed by independent implementations `verify.py` and `crosscheck.py`, gives
\[
 |L_0|=39,\quad |L_1|=64,\quad |L_2|=66,\quad
 L_2\setminus L_1=\{(10,12),(12,10)\}.
\]
The certificate lists each of the \(66\) signatures, each depth-zero signature, and the two successive difference sets. A separate certificate `derivations.json` gives a \(66\)-node straight-line derivation from the three primitive formulas; each node is checked against the semantic operations, its earlier dependencies, and its modal depth. Thus the class counts are both upper and lower bounds, not estimates from sampling formulas.

Finally, exhaustive closure verification establishes \(\blacklozenge L_2\subseteq L_2\). By construction, \(L_2\) is already closed under the three propositional operations and contains the primitive signatures. A structural induction on *arbitrary* BSL formulas therefore places every signature in \(L_2\). This proves stabilization at depth \(2\) for unbounded formula length and modal nesting.

For sharpness, the masks of the two displayed witnesses are checked as \((10,12)\) and \((12,10)\). Neither belongs to \(L_1\), so neither is equivalent to any formula of modal depth \(0\) or \(1\), whereas both displayed witnesses have modal depth exactly \(2\). This completes both directions.

## Verification

With Python 3 and no additional packages, in the directory containing the four artifacts, run:

```text
python3 verify.py signature_levels.json derivations.json
python3 crosscheck.py signature_levels.json
```

The first checker implements the semantic operations directly on sets of teams, exhaustively computes the fixed closures, verifies each stored signature and straight-line formula derivation, and confirms the two distinguishing witnesses. The second checker independently implements eight-bit masks and a separate closure algorithm. Their expected output contains `BILATERAL_ONE_LETTER_DEPTH_COUNTS_OK 39 64 66 66` and `BILATERAL_BITMASK_CROSSCHECK_OK 39 64 66`. The computation exhausts the finite universe of semantic signatures; it does not treat a finite search over formula lengths as a proof about all formulas. The structural-induction argument above supplies the generality.

## Relationship to prior work

Anttila and Knudstorp, *Convex Team Logics* (arXiv:2503.21850), establish expressive completeness for convex and union-closed team properties; in particular, their results motivate the positive support properties of the present logic. Aloni, Baltag, van Benthem, Bezhanishvili, and Knudstorp, *Teams, Truthmakers, Translations* (arXiv:2610.15680v1), give the exact BSL semantics and a faithful modal-information translation. Anttila, *Bicompleteness Theorems for Team Logics with the Dual Negation* (arXiv:2410.07067), studies bilateral pairs in a related but different model-theoretic setting (BSML on relational frames).

These results supply the semantic and expressive-completeness background. They do not, in the materially inspected sections, state the one-letter, *bilateral* modal-depth counts or the sharp depth-two collapse. In particular, a theorem that characterizes support properties or unconstrained bilateral expressivity does not on its own quantify the least depth required to realize every pair. Only that quantitative depth profile and its complete one-letter algebra are asserted here as a finding; the known weak-ideal completeness and the semantic clauses are credited to prior authors.

## Limitations

The claim applies to the specified one-letter BSL over the four teams of Boolean valuations, **not** to general BSML with arbitrary Kripke frames, multiple proposition letters, additional connectives, or arbitrary infinite valuation domains. It gives no optimal formula-size bounds, no axiomatization of the whole logic, and no general multi-variable depth-collapse theorem. The binary encodings and detailed node certificates depend on the stated ordering of four teams; the numerical depth counts do not. A related earlier classification not encountered in the literature comparison could affect historical priority. No independent audit has been performed.
