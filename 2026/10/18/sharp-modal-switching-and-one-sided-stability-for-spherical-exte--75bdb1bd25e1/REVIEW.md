# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The result follows from spherical Fourier–Bessel diagonalization and the independently derived adjacent and two-step moment identities. The two-step identity is nonnegative for each angular degree and supplies the global comparison across infinitely many modes, not just sampled degrees. Neighboring Bessel functions cannot vanish at the same positive argument by ODE uniqueness, so all equality-space and strict-gap cases are exhaustive. Four gap formulas were reconstructed by comparing the first excluded harmonic in each parity chain; they are attained by explicit harmonics. Local Taylor expansions from the Bessel recurrences show the claimed one-sided orders at both transition types. Numerical checks are supplementary, not substitutes for analytic completeness.

## Originality

PASS, qualified historical assessment. The mathematical content compared was the *sharp norm for the finite-ball \(L^2\) extension*, the precise top-mode and double/triple-tie classification, and the exact distance-to-extremizers gap with one-sided quadratic onset. Existing inspected sources establish whole-space higher-exponent restriction inequalities, global Bessel-mode inequalities, and weighted approximate localization bounds, but do not imply this full finite-radius, sharp-gap package. Classical Bessel recurrences, harmonic diagonalization, and their immediate moment identities are credited as known. Semantic mathematical-record searches for aliases including truncated restriction, Herglotz ball concentration, Bessel-Lommel modal order, and one-sided spectral collisions found related but differently parametrized results. Residual risk: an older unindexed formula or an acoustic-scattering source might independently contain the finite-ball classification; no claim of exhaustive priority search or independent validation is made.

## Value

PASS. The problem asks which spherical input profiles maximize measured Fourier energy within a finite physical aperture, a natural localized restriction and inverse-wave stability question. Complete finite-aperture maximizers differ from the familiar untruncated constant-harmonic regime, with infinitely many explicit switching radii and triple-degree resonance. The sharp coercivity coefficient gives an optimal quantitative distance from the maximizing subspace and proves genuinely asymmetric quadratic-versus-linear conditioning at triple crossings. These all-radius/all-dimension structural and stability statements are not just a single Bessel substitution or an arbitrary numeric slice.
