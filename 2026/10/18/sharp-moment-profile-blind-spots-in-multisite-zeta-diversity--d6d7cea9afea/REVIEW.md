# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** For each species group, the number of sampling sets contained in its common range is exactly a binomial coefficient; interchanging finite sums yields a triangular transform and the binomial identity proves its inverse. Both numerical examples were directly enumerated and the boundary arguments cover all smaller site counts or species counts, including the three-site four-species edge case. The accompanying standard-library program performed 94,773 exact inversion comparisons, an exhaustive 2,800-matrix small-case survey and an independent check of the integer-triple collision classification. Infinite-scope conclusions rely on proof, not these tests.

**Originality — PASS, subject to outside review.** Hui–McGeoch (2014) already provide mean-zeta/occupancy inversion; we do not claim to invent binomial inversion. The identified novelty is the factorial-moment extension in this ecological sampling language together with distinct, sharp information-loss thresholds. Trejo-Barocio–Arita (2013) and Arita (2015) discuss co-occurrence and covariance but not those full-depth inverse/moment identification statements or extremal witnesses. Earlier equivalent work using derived tuple-species, hypergraph incidence, or finite design terminology remains a bibliographic risk.

**Value — PASS.** Ecological monitoring often retains zeta decline means or second-moment uncertainty summaries instead of the incidence matrix. This result identifies precisely the aggregate higher-order overlap information such summaries retain, and proves with smallest configurations—including a sharp seven-vertex combined minimum—when they can conceal radically different common-richness distributions (including opposite skewness). The inverse formulas can support exact-data diagnostics; uncertainty amplification and field inference are not addressed.

**Scope.** Fixed perfect-detection incidence, uniformly sampled distinct sites, known species count, complete exact moment curves. No causal interaction, numerical stability, or nonuniform sampling conclusions.
