For a word formed by the increasing permutation followed by another permutation on the same \(m\) symbols, every longest run subsequence has length \(m+1\), and the number of distinct optimum words is a weighted sum over the second permutation's left-to-right records. This multiplicity lies sharply between \(m\) and \(2^{m-1}\): the lower equality permutations are precisely adjacent-swap tilings (\(F_{m+1}\) choices), while upper equality occurs precisely for the two specified largest-symbol prefixes (\(m(m-2)!\) choices for \(m\ge2\)).
