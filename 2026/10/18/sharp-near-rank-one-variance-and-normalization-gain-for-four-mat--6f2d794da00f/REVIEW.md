# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The final theorem concerns the exact leave-one-out trace estimates specified in `RESULT.md`, not an arbitrary Gaussian trace estimator. Conditional unbiasedness follows because the omitted Gaussian test vector is independent of the sketch projector. The residual has a single exceptional eigenvalue \(\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)H_\varepsilon\) and \(n-2\) other eigenvalues \(\varepsilon\), where \(H_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2R^2/(X^2+\varepsilon^2R^2)\). These identities yield exact conditional variances, including the dimension-one complementary-space degeneracy. A change of variables at the small Gaussian coordinate, followed by dominated convergence, yields the explicit gamma-function constant. Bounding both leave-one-out cross moments by the product of expected residual norms gives \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) correlations, enough for the sharp coefficient. The rare-event probability follows from an exact Gaussian small-interval equivalence. The standalone verifier's beta integrals, direct matrix-vector formulas, and seeded Monte Carlo agree. Unproved finite-precision or growing-dimension extensions are not claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest direct source, Epperly--Tropp--Webber (arXiv:2301.07825, §§1.2.1, 2.3, 3.1, 5.1 and 5.6), *defines* both XTrace variants and proves general unbiasedness, error bounds and error-proxy covariance formulas. The inspected passages do not provide the exact near-rank-one variance coefficient, the dimension-dependent sharp normalization ratio, or the rare-sketch threshold law. In particular, their rank-sensitive variance bounds require larger sketch counts to obtain their refined spectral form, and do not imply the four-matvec asymptotics by substitution. Hallman's subsequent *Two Variations* (arXiv:2512.02316, §§1, 3--5) studies orthogonal symmetrization and Krylov-space expansion; its normalized baseline is credited but its conclusions do not imply these leading constants. Hallman's arXiv:2411.15454 treats the ordinary Gaussian quadratic-form estimator, which lacks the data-dependent leave-one-out residual. Other mathematically related rare-sketch and Gaussian/Nyström error asymptotics involve different random quantities and do not imply the two XTrace variances. No covering statement was identified by the recorded searches and implication comparisons. Residual risk: an equivalent Gaussian-projection singular-asymptotic calculation may occur in older trace-estimation or numerical probability literature. A failed keyword search alone is not novelty evidence.

## Value — PASS

XTrace is designed to exploit rapidly decaying spectra. The nearly rank-one family tests the most elementary such limit while keeping the operator positive definite and the sketch budget fixed. The theorem gives a sharp, nonanalytic \(O(\varepsilon)\) mean-squared error from rare near-orthogonal sketches, rather than the tempting \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) typical-sample prediction, and quantifies exactly how much the *already published* residual normalization alleviates that failure. The factor \((n-2)/(n+1)\) reveals a finite-dimensional effect that vanishes as dimension grows, with exact cancellation for a one-dimensional complement. Its scope and four-matvec cost are explicit. This is a spectral reliability characterization, not a claim to invent a new algorithm or to dominate all available estimators.

## Closest literature and limitations

The primary source is Epperly, Tropp and Webber (2023/2024); the strongest subsequent comparison is Hallman (2025). Earlier Gaussian quadratic-form estimates and low-rank approximation error laws provide tools but do not determine the leave-one-out normalization coefficient. Only exact arithmetic, fixed dimension, Gaussian probes, one large eigenvalue, and a flat positive small spectral tail are covered. Dimension two is an exact boundary case; in dimensions at most four, exact matrix reconstruction may be cheaper than a four-matvec estimator. The new theorem is relevant to below-dimension matvec budgets beginning at dimension five. Independent mathematical validation remains desirable.
