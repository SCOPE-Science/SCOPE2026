# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The fixed-length bound follows from coordinatewise convexity and an exact combinatorial covering of projective sign classes. The bound is attained because at most half of these classes are missing, permitting pairwise flips at a minimum-length input. Optimizing the remaining scalar lengths gives a two-dimensional Rayleigh quotient with a feasible top eigenvector. Exhaustive exact-rational diagnostics through four inputs and symbolic verification of the five-dimensional example corroborate the derivation, without serving as an infinite-dimensional or all-parameter certificate.

**Originality: PASS, with residual literature risk.** Kato–Takahashi–Hashimoto introduced the general invariant, and the full open-access Ciesielski–Płuciennik treatment gives the saturated regime once all projective sign patterns fit. The strict half-to-full finite-dimensional range and its sharp fixed-length row-covering formula are not implied by that saturation statement. The previously reported three-input, three-coordinate case is an included special case and is not itself a novelty claim. Comparisons included the altered unit-sphere definition, an unrelated rectangular Rademacher tensor bound, and the common type-two formulation. Searches did not identify an exact covering theorem; incomplete access to the foundational 1998 full text remains a genuine bibliographic risk, not evidence of uniqueness.

It identifies the combinatorial extremizers and yields the first nontrivial four-input, five-dimensional exact value. The extra fixed-input-norm theorem explains why the missing sign classes can all be handled at minimal loss.
