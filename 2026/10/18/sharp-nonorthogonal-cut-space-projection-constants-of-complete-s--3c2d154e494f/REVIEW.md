# Scientific review: sharp complete-split cut projections

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes real edge \(\ell_1\), all integers \(m,n\ge2\), and the full class of linear retractions onto the cut subspace. The signed-permutation action is isometric; averaging leaves the range, idempotence and identity-on-cuts intact. The edge and cut permutation-module decomposition leaves precisely one invariant freedom, exhibited by the explicit \(WU^*\) circulation-to-cut correction. Both edge-orbit column norms are computed from Laplacian solutions. Piecewise absolute-value inequalities exclude parameter values outside \([0,1]\), and the unique crossing inside that interval gives a global minimum. The exact-rational checker constructs actual edge matrices for sixteen parameter pairs, confirming identities independently of the analytic derivation. The full infinite family depends on the proof, not these tests.

## Originality — PASS with identified residual risk
The most relevant framework is Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582), whose accessible full text develops invariant minimal cycle projections mainly on discrete tori and Hamming graphs, but does not state this complete-split cut-space constant or the orthogonal-to-nonorthogonal transition. Their Laakso-graph paper (arXiv:2007.07949) studies another family. Related known results for balanced multipartite cut projections have equal-size independent classes and yield orthogonal optimality; they do not cover a clique joined to an independent class. Complete-split ellipsoid and graph-lattice norm statements concern different invariants, and the Cartesian graph-product Hodge norms describe orthogonal projectors rather than minimization over all cut projections. A triangular-book result covers **cycle-space** projections of the \(m=2\) boundary but does not compute these cut-space constants, still less the strict-gap family \(m\ge3\). Searches under electrical transfer-current, Lipschitz-free, cut/cycle, complete split and multipartite terminology did not identify a covering statement. Absence from searched records is not a proof of priority; equivalent alternative formulations remain a bibliographic risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A natural, two-parameter, highly symmetric family exhibits a sharp failure of orthogonal minimality, unlike balanced complete multipartite families. The explicit globally optimal nonorthogonal projection and strict-if-and-only-if threshold distinguish an operator-theoretic structure not captured by the Laplacian's canonical projector alone. The nonvanishing asymptotic gap \(3/14\) rules out interpreting the discrepancy as a low-dimensional numerical accident. The result does not classify all minimizers or address arbitrary edge weights, which are legitimate limits rather than missing prerequisites.
