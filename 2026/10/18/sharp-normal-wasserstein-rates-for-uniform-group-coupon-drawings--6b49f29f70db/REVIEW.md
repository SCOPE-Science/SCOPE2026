# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exchangeable missing sets can be symmetrized from independent Bernoulli configurations. The elementary-symmetric intersection polynomial is real-rooted by a Gauss–Lucas argument whose convex-hull exclusion is checked in the proof. The explicit first two factorial probabilities give variance \(\sigma_n^2\sim e^{-x}n^{1-\alpha}\). The standard independent-Bernoulli Stein bound yields Wasserstein error at most \(2/\sigma_n\), while a lattice-distance Lipschitz test yields at least \((1/4-o(1))/\sigma_n\). All proof stages cover the stated quantifiers. Exact rational tests and real-root isolation supplement, not substitute for, the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Betken–Thäle's 2022 primary source explicitly leaves the optimal lower-\(\alpha\) Wasserstein convergence rate open. Their published bounds, the classical geometric stability tools of Borcea–Brändén–Liggett, and the related group-allocation central limit theorems do not directly assert the exact Poisson–binomial count closure plus optimal all-\(\alpha\) Wasserstein rate. Search comparisons included the candidate, normal-limit aliases, group-drawing alternatives, real-rootedness, and more general stability. Available older related results overlap in ingredients rather than the precise theorem. Archival normal-approximation literature remains a residual historical-priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The sharp rate answers a documented problem in group coupon-collector normal approximation and removes the logarithmic and regime-dependent loss in the published estimate for \(\alpha\le1/3\). The accompanying exact finite-horizon structural theorem identifies why a dependent occupancy count admits independent-summand normal tools.

**Limits.** Fixed packet size and \(0<\alpha<1\), no claimed exact leading constant for the Wasserstein metric; historical novelty and full older-source coverage are not independently certified.
