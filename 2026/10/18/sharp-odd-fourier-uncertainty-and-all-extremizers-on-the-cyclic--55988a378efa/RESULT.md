# Sharp odd Fourier uncertainty and all extremizers on the cyclic group of order 25

## Finding

Let \(G=\mathbb Z/25\mathbb Z\), \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/25}\), and let
\[\widehat f(y)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\zeta^{xy}.\]
For every nonzero complex function \(f\colon G\to\mathbb C\) with \(f(-x)=-f(x)\),
\[\boxed{|\operatorname{supp}f|+|\operatorname{supp}\widehat f|\ge14.}\]
The bound is attained, and modulo multiplication by a nonzero complex scalar there are **exactly four equality cases**. They are the two functions
\[
f_t(5k)=\zeta_5^{tk}-\zeta_5^{-tk}\quad(k\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z),\qquad
f_t(x)=0\quad (5\nmid x),\qquad t\in\{1,2\},
\]
where \(\zeta_5=e^{2\pi i/5}\), together with their two Fourier transforms \(\widehat f_1,\widehat f_2\). Their respective full support sizes are \((4,10)\) and \((10,4)\). The four projective classes are distinct.

## Assumptions and scope

The Fourier transform has no normalizing prefactor, is taken over the additive **cyclic ring** \(\mathbb Z/25\mathbb Z\) (not the additive group of the field \(\mathbb F_{25}\)), and support counts nonzero complex-valued coordinates. Oddness requires \(f(0)=0\). In the twelve nonzero sign-pairs \(\{\pm j\}\), indexed by \(j\in I_0=\{1,2,\ldots,12\}\), the support sizes of any odd \(f\) and its odd transform are \(2r\) and \(2s\), respectively. Scalar equivalence does not identify Fourier-dual functions.

## Proof

**1. Sine reduction and Fourier duality.** Because the transform preserves oddness and \(\widehat{\widehat f}(x)=25f(-x)=-25f(x)\), both support sizes are even, and it suffices by Fourier duality to treat \(r\le s\). Writing \(u_x=f(x)\) for \(1\le x\le12\), its nonredundant Fourier coefficients satisfy
\[
\widehat f(y)=\sum_{x=1}^{12}(\zeta^{xy}-\zeta^{-xy})u_x
\qquad(1\le y\le12).
\]
Thus define the \(12\times12\) sine matrix \(S_{y,x}=\zeta^{xy}-\zeta^{-xy}\). A function supported on the sign-pairs \(A\subseteq I_0\) and with Fourier support contained in \(B\subseteq I_0\) exists precisely when the \((12-|B|)\times|A|\) matrix \(S_{I_0\setminus B,A}\) has a nonzero kernel.

**2. Single sign-pairs.** If \(r=1\), the sole column has zero entries exactly at \(y\in\{5,10\}\) when \(5\mid x\), and has no zero entries when \(5\nmid x\). Indeed \(\zeta^{xy}=\zeta^{-xy}\) is equivalent to \(25\mid 2xy\), hence to \(25\mid xy\). Consequently \(s\ge10\), so no \(r=1\) example can have \(r+s\le7\).

**3. Finite exact rank certificate.** Suppose \(r\ge2\) and \(r+s\le7\) with \(r\le s\). If a support pair existed with a strictly smaller \(s\), its Fourier support would be contained in some set of the same \(r\) and larger \(s\). It is therefore sufficient to exhaust the four boundary cases \((r,s)=(2,4),(3,3),(3,4),(2,5)\).

The accompanying standalone checker enumerates every pair \(A,B\) of the prescribed sizes, forms the exact matrix \(S_{I_0\setminus B,A}\) in each of two finite fields \(\mathbb F_{101}\) and \(\mathbb F_{151}\), and computes its rank by exact modular row elimination. The specializations send \(\zeta\) to elements of order precisely \(25\), respectively \(16\) and \(148\). A full-column-rank specialization proves full column rank over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), because a nonzero modular determinant comes from a nonzero cyclotomic-integer minor. Thus the certification of **nonexistence** is rigorous over \(\mathbb C\), rather than an inference from approximate floating-point ranks.

In each characteristic, the checker obtains exactly

| \((r,s)\) | Examined support pairs | Modular-rank failures |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \((2,4)\) | 32,670 | 0 |
| \((3,3)\) | 48,400 | 0 |
| \((3,4)\) | 108,900 | 0 |
| \((2,5)\) | 52,272 | 2 |

The only failures have \(A=\{5,10\}\), with respectively \(B=\{1,4,6,9,11\}\) and \(B=\{2,3,7,8,12\}\). Their zero-constraint matrices each have a one-dimensional complex kernel. To see this without relying on specialization, note that for \(x=5\) and \(x=10\) the columns are combinations of two distinct nonzero sine characters of the five-element quotient; on the complement of either displayed \(B\), the two columns have rank one, not zero. Their kernel lines are exactly those generated by \(f_1\) and \(f_2\).

**4. Constructions and completeness.** For \(t=1,2\), the displayed \(f_t\) is nonzero at the four points \(\pm5,\pm10\), and direct finite geometric sums give
\[
\widehat f_t(y)=5\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{y\equiv -t\pmod5\}}-\mathbf1_{\{y\equiv t\pmod5\}}\bigr).
\]
Its transform is nonzero at ten frequencies, so the support sum is fourteen. Steps 1–3 show that no smaller sum exists; they also force every equality case with \(r\le s\) to lie on one of the two lines above. Fourier duality gives precisely the two further lines with \(r>s\). This proves both the sharp bound and the complete projective equality classification.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_odd_sine_25.py`; the program requires only the Python standard library. The companion `verify_odd_sine_25_output.json` records the precise field roots, 484,484 tested matrix pairs across the two primes, and both identical exception lists. Exact modular tests establish nonvanishing in characteristic zero; the explicit geometric-sum formula establishes that both remaining modular failure pairs really are complex singular and produces all extremizers. The computational exhaustive step is a finite proof for this fixed group, not an infinite-order assertion.

## Relationship to prior work

Garcia, Karaali and Katz [1] established discrete uncertainty principles refined by odd symmetry, including discrete sine-matrix results over prime-order groups and certain finite-field generalizations. The cyclic prime-square group here is not a prime-order group or the additive group of the twenty-five-element field. The present result computes its sharp odd support-sum invariant and **all** projective equality cases. Ordinary unrestricted Fourier-minor rank classifications address different matrices: the odd-compressed matrix here has entries consisting of **differences** of paired Fourier characters. A general uncertainty lower bound or ordinary minor classification does not immediately imply the displayed sharp value or four-line census.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite result at cyclic order \(25\); it makes no statement for larger prime squares, even functions, different cyclic orders, or non-odd functions. The originality comparison is best-knowledge rather than a proof that no earlier equivalent or unindexed computation exists. The modular certificate proves the required exhaustive rank facts at this specific order; it is not an approximate experiment or a substitute for the stated algebraic reduction. The scientific result has not undergone independent external audit.
