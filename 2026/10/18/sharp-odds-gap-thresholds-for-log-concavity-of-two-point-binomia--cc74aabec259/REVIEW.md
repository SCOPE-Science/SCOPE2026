# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The displayed necessary-and-sufficient curvature tests follow by cancellation from the exact ratio of three neighboring binomial-mixture masses. The sharp uniform cutoffs follow from \(z/(1+z)^2\le1/4\), with equality achievable at any interior index by a strictly positive mixing weight. The maxima over indices are \(n-1\) and \(\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\), respectively. Strict logarithmic concavity of the two continuous index profiles proves that failure indices cannot form disjoint clusters. All hypotheses and equality cases are included. The extracted standard-library rational verifier checks one explicit witness plus 12,285 indices across 1,755 parameter settings; the universal theorem rests on the algebraic proof, not finite tests.

## Originality — PASS (priority risk remains)

Kella and Löpker (2022, arXiv:2202.10296, Section 6) introduce the same conditional mixing object but do not give these sharp LC or ULC cutoffs. Aravinda, Marsiglietti and Melbourne (2021, arXiv:2104.05054) start with an already-ULC law to prove concentration. Gross et al. (2014, arXiv:1407.6325) prove a sufficient synchronization criterion, which fails for the explicit pair \(n=10,p=1/4,q=1/2\) even though this theorem guarantees LC for every weight. Xia and Lv (2024) study compounded binomial sums rather than random success probabilities. Badiella et al. (2023) study discrete order statistics; that comparison was abstract-level, not full text. Searches revealed no dominating result, but terminology mismatch, incomplete access and unindexed earlier work remain priority risks. No independent priority audit has occurred.

## Value — PASS

A complete, sharp classification for a natural positively dependent exchangeable count model identifies nontrivial, distinct robust scales for LC and ULC and an exact consecutive-failure-band phenomenon at fixed weights. These thresholds make existing ULC concentration results applicable to explicit latent-probability models. They are not a general result for arbitrary binomial mixing measures or different trial numbers. The exact thresholds require the two-atom assumption.

## Scientific limitations

No claim is made for continuous mixing distributions, mixtures with unequal trial counts, high-order total positivity, or optimal concentration constants. The finite exact-rational computations serve as regression checks and do not certify the infinite parameter domain.
