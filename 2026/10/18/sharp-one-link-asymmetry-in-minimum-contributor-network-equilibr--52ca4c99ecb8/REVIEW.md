# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The best-response rule defines exactly the maximal independent sets. The lower bound follows by adding at most the single previously undominated endpoint to an optimal independent dominating set after the link. For the upper bound, fixing a minimum independent dominating set of the original graph and replacing either of the new edge endpoints by an independent dominating set of its private neighbors constructs a valid post-link independent dominating set. The private-neighbor sets are disjoint, yielding the floor bound. All vertices of the two-ended pendant-path extremizer are treated in an exhaustive three-case split, with attained witnesses in every case; the claimed values are exact. A separate exact labelled-graph census to six vertices and checks of both constructions through fourteen vertices agree. No infinite claim depends on enumeration.

## Originality

**PASS at the stated best-knowledge level, with a residual search risk.** An earlier construction already demonstrates the same tree-to-unicyclic independent-domination increase and first positive six-vertex example. Those facts are not claimed as new; the new claim is the universal sharp two-sided extremal bound for every connected graph. The original best-shot paper fixes the network and minimizes the contributors; it explicitly separates network modification from its mechanism. Goddard–Henning survey independent-domination inequalities but no single-added-edge sharp asymmetry was located in the materially inspected article text. Xu's independence-bondage section is about edge **removal** and whether the parameter rises, not the quantitative edge-**addition** difference. The 2019 tree/unicyclic stability article concerns removal of vertices. Thakkar–Kakrecha study domination by **edges**, a different invariant. Repeated claim-, alias-, family-, and parameter-specific searches of the mathematical records found no matching or logically stronger bound. Remaining risks: older or unindexed graph-sensitivity literature and implicit derivations of a portion of the bound from general order inequalities. The exact universal two-sided link-effect bound is distinguished from the earlier existential family, the previously known first-size threshold, and general unrelated order inequalities.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** An earlier example already shows that one added link can severely worsen independent domination. The genuinely new mathematical design question is how much it can worsen in the worst case at each order and how strongly it can improve: the universal theorem gives sharp opposite-side bounds, using a replacement argument applicable to arbitrary connected networks. The known six-agent example is a sharpness witness, not the novelty or value by itself.
