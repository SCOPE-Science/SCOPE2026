# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The ideal correspondence follows from independence of the squares and a dimension squeeze: an ideal whose union of supports is \(S\) must contain all \(e_i^2\) for \(i\in S\), and hence equals the coordinate span. Block-triangular invertible-matrix counting gives the exact cut probability. A uniform forced-zero estimate conditional on invertibility bounds intersections and all larger cuts. The resulting error is \(O_q(n^2q^{-2n+4})\) for fixed \(q\), smaller than the leading term. Finite enumeration checks seven matrix ensembles, including an extension field, but does not replace proof.

The May 21, 2025 paper arXiv:2505.15604v1 treats automorphism groups of fixed graph algebras, not random simple algebras. The older arXiv:1312.4685 provides graph background, not an inspected competing count. The closest published finite-matrix and evolution-algebra results deal with different invariants (Fourier frames, idempotents, diagonal automorphisms), and do not imply this conditional strong-connectivity asymptotic. Classical independent-edge random-digraph results do not include invertibility conditioning, which changes the leading coefficient. Residual priority risk: an unindexed equivalent theorem or general zero-pattern result might exist.

## Value — PASS
The number of simple objects in the natural full-rank matrix ensemble is a meaningful structural statistic, with a sharp, field-dependent leading probability rather than a mere almost-sure statement. The theorem also classifies all ideals as outgoing-closed coordinate sets, yields exact finite-dimensional cut probabilities, and identifies one-dimensional and codimension-one ideals as the two equally likely dominant defects. This distinguishes conditioned algebra sampling from the familiar independent-edge digraph ensemble and supplies a concrete quantitative direction for random finite-field evolution algebras.

## Limitations
Fixed field size, labelled full-rank matrices only. No claim of uniform error when the field order grows; finite tests are not an asymptotic proof, and the literature search does not establish global uniqueness. Independent validation has not been performed.
