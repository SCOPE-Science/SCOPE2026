# Review: sharp periodic-erasure bounds for hat-spline Gabor systems

## Correctness
PASS. The stated Zak transform is unitary, its integer translations and modulations diagonalize the frame operator as the multiplier \(F_J\), and the hat window produces exactly two contributing translates. The minimum over modulation phase is attained at \(\nu=1/2\) for each spatial point, reducing the operator's exact minimum to a circular squared-distance minimization. Every nearest representative for a fixed spatial point lies within a single length-\(m\) interval, hence corresponds to a cyclic lifting; completing squares and reversing the lift choice establishes the exact arithmetic formula. The integer-gap inequality proves the uniform bound and the exact equality cases. Exact-rational enumeration of 2,035 masks and 1,300 independent numerical symbol comparisons corroborate the proof, without replacing it.

## Originality
PASS on the available comparative evidence, with residual historical-priority uncertainty. Full-text inspection of the September 2026 spline Zak-zero source establishes that its frame-set theorem addresses complete regular lattices, not arbitrary period-class deletions. The second spline-frame source likewise concerns complete-lattice membership. Three closely related published mathematical findings were read in full: two concern exact bounds for *unthinned* spline lattices with different parameter ranges; the third concerns erasures of two-row finite cyclic harmonic frames and does not imply the present circular squared-distance formula. Hardin–Kendall–Saff analyze an unrestricted *best-placement* polarization problem, not the *worst subset* of a fixed circular grid. The proof of the integer-gap inequality is elementary and not itself claimed as a discovery. Unindexed equivalents in older shift-invariant or polynomial-spline sampling literature remain possible.

## Value
PASS. Periodic loss of translation-channel classes is a motivated robustness question for oversampled Gabor analysis. This result gives, for every redundancy and every surviving mask, an exact lower-frame spectral expression computable with finitely many integer variances; the sharp adversarial threshold, all equality masks, guaranteed survival with two cosets, and exact worst conditioning are global and structural, not a routine restatement of qualitative lattice frame-set membership.

## Scientific limits
Only the hat spline, integer frequency modulation, and full coset-wise periodic deletion are covered. Worst-case bounds do not imply optimal recovery in stochastic noise models; no claim for arbitrary erasures. Historical novelty remains open to further literature review, and no independent scientific validation has occurred.
