For uniform random points on a circle, a one-layer directional nearest-neighbor graph has a sharp candidate-pool transition at \(m\approx\log_2 n\): if \(n2^{-m}\to\lambda\), the number of missing directional edges converges to Poisson\((2\lambda)\). Universal exact greedy nearest-neighbor routing consequently succeeds with limiting probability \(e^{-2\lambda}\), yielding a sharp constant and critical window beyond logarithmic sufficiency bounds.
