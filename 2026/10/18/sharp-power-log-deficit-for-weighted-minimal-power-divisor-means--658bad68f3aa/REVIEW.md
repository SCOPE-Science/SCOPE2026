# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The prime-power formula is exact. Its positive divisor-convolution kernel has nonzero values precisely on exponents \(rj+1\) with \(j\ge1\). The squarefree weight at exponent \(1/2\) has a classical Selberg–Delange counting law. The residual Euler product has absolutely summable coefficients to the left of \(1/(r+1)\), allowing the convolution transfer. Regular variation and the fractional-part integral for the zeta function complete the sharp signed asymptotic. The finite exact-rational checker confirms the coefficients and the basic convolution identity but is not used as evidence for the asymptotic.

## Originality
**PASS (self-review), with residual risk.** The closest paper, Bouderbala (arXiv:2602.16027), introduces exactly this weighted minimal-power composition and proves a leading-order asymptotic with \(O_r(x/\log x)\) remainder at the critical weight \(\(k=2\)\); its higher-order inverse-log expansions do not determine the explicit smaller, noninteger-power deficit. The earlier digit-restricted divisor-quotient paper is for a different function. Searches using the function, its Euler-factor aliases, the zeta coefficient, the exact power and the squarefree convolution did not identify a published covering statement. Generic regular-variation transfer results might implicitly yield such a statement and were not exhaustively surveyed.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The natural division-by-unitary-divisor normalization is the parameter at which the leading singularity becomes a simple pole. Identifying a previously invisible, genuinely sublinear positive deficit, proving its optimal power-log order, and computing its sign and Euler-product coefficient for all fixed power orders answer a motivated precision question inaccessible from the published error bound.

## Limits and comparators
The argument uses a standard Selberg–Delange theorem, and gives no uniformity in the power parameter. The result is a precise secondary asymptotic, not a claim about all parameter weights or about effective zero-free-region errors. Full-text comparison was made with the motivating paper, alongside a 2022 paper on the distinct uncomposed quotient. Hidden implicit coverage in more general theorems remains possible.
