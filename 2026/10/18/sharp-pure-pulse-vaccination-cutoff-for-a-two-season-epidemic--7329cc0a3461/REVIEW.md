# Scientific review: sharp two-season pure-pulse cutoff

## Correctness — PASS
The statement quantifies over the entire positive periodic-measure class with fixed mass and correctly includes the equality \(B=B_*\). Formula (1) is the period-summed variation-of-constants identity and supplies a fixed positive denominator; differing measures yield strict convexity because their masses differ on a positive-measure set of time arcs. The baseline pulse trajectory follows from the scalar affine period map. Its integrated impulse response gives the first-variation function \(H\), and exact differentiation on the two constant-transmission subintervals reduces global optimality to two one-sided endpoint inequalities. At the boundary each interior marginal benefit is still strictly lower than the pulse's; above the boundary a strictly improving finite-pulse perturbation exists. The standalone checker independently evaluates affine periodic trajectories and 54 finite differences and tests 150 general-parameter families. These experiments support the algebra but are not used in the infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest paper is Gavish–Katriel, *Optimal Vaccination for Contagious Diseases with Seasonal Transmission*, DOI 10.1007/s11538-026-01741-0. Its Section 7 and Fig. 9 numerically locate a pure-pulse transition near \(B=0.4\), and its Theorem 10 does not give a necessary-and-sufficient pulse condition at an upward discontinuity. Their earlier general optimization paper, DOI 10.1007/s00245-025-10374-z, contains a convexity theorem and abstract optimality test; those ingredients are prior work. Its discontinuity results and examples do not identify the present exact two-season closed-form boundary. The new result is the explicit \(\alpha\)-dependent cutoff for every positive parameter choice, proved by reducing the infinite-dimensional first-order inequality to two endpoint tests, plus the sharp above-cutoff failure. Earlier Onyango–Müller and pulse-SIR research analyzes different vaccine eligibility/immune-duration assumptions or restricted pulse strategies. The remaining originality risk is an unlocated general theorem that supplies the identical endpoint reduction as an unstated corollary. Retrieval and publication searches cannot prove novelty, and no independent literature certification was performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The threshold gives a decisive and attainable policy-structure boundary for the disease-free seasonal SIRVS setting of a recent paper. The exact value \(B_*=0.390979957597\ldots\) sharpens the source's observed \(0.4\) transition and reveals which relative levels of high and low transmission and waning determine the pure-pulse phase. Because the comparison permits every nonnegative measure-valued schedule, not just a handful of fixed pulse patterns, the result addresses the central original optimization question rather than a routine prescribed-schedule calculation. It also gives the exact minimum invasion exposure for all budgets in that phase. It does not identify the post-cutoff optimum, predict full disease dynamics, or account for implementation constraints.

## Limitations and closest literature
The theorem is restricted to precisely two constant-transmission seasons, positive waning, fixed average vaccination intensity, and the disease-free linearization. For nearby but smoother seasonal profiles the same pulse conclusion need not hold. The primary vaccine paper and the general convexity paper were inspected in full accessible HTML at the key assumptions, theorems, examples, and proofs. Older relevant pulse vaccination sources were inspected at abstract/model-comparison level; incomplete inspection of those less comparable works is a residual literature risk.
