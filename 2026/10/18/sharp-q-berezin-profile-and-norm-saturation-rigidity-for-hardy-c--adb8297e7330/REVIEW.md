# Scientific review: sharp \(q\)-Berezin profile for constant-symbol Hardy composition

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem specifies the Hardy inner-product orientation, positive overlap \(0<q\le1\), all constant symbols \(c\in\mathbb D\), and the endpoints \(c=0\) and \(q=1\). Its proof starts from the exact normalized kernel overlap formula, obtains both admissible collinear branches, compares their numerators, and minimizes the angular denominator. The global scalar maximum has a perfect-square certificate valid at every radius. A rank-one operator-norm calculation and a unit-vector chord identity yield the sharp equality and quantitative deficit. Strict monotonicity settles the full constant-symbol range. ## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Bhattacharya–Patra [1], Theorem 2.2, supplies the kernel-pair parametrization; its Section 3.4 computes \(q\)-Berezin ranges for linear symbols \(\phi(z)=\xi z\), with \(\xi=0\) covering the \(c=0\) special case. These facts are excluded from the novelty assertion. The present two-variable exact radius for **every** nonzero constant symbol, its rigid norm-saturation parameter, unique extremizing pair, exact nonnegative defect, and sharp global range are not given by that full inspected source. The full 2025 general inequalities paper [2] does not give this particular formula; the full 2024 finite-rank Berezin study [3] treats the ordinary (one-kernel) invariant. The 2023 ordinary Berezin composition paper [4] studies other symbol families and does not resolve the \(q<1\) constrained two-kernel problem. Generic \(q\)-numerical-range bounds are not equivalent: they optimize over all Hilbert unit vectors instead of the normalized Szegő-kernel submanifold. Published semantic research-record searches found no covering claim. An earlier unnoticed exact computation or stronger general evaluation-kernel theorem remains a substantive priority risk, and independent scholarly review is still required.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The constant-symbol family is the canonical rank-one family of Hardy composition operators, and the 2026 source specifically motivates the quantitative study of \(q\)-Berezin geometry of composition operators. The theorem supplies its complete sharp two-parameter norm profile, with all equality cases and a rigid one-parameter norm-saturation curve. The squared-chord defect measures distance to saturation and the global half-open range displays bounded \(q\)-Berezin radii despite diverging operator norms. This is a natural complete extremal classification rather than a one-parameter numerical example, and it identifies a basic structural difference between kernel-constrained and unrestricted Hilbert-space radii.
