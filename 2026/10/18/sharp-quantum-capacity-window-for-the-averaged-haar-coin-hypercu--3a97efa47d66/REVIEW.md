# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The domain and normalization are explicit: the reduced averaged vertex channel on a \(d\)-hypercube after integer \(t\) steps; unassisted quantum capacity per independent use of that composed map. The degradability map is constructed directly from the preserved input diagonal; Pauli covariance plus established coherent-information concavity justifies the maximally mixed optimizer, giving the exact entropy identity. Pairing complementary characters on a fixed parity class yields the correct Fourier expansion. Orthogonality and the explicit exponential-series \(L^2\) tail bound justify the Hermite-lognormal likelihood limit and uniform integrability required for the relative-entropy constant. The first-crossing law follows from capacity monotonicity and the strict continuous limiting profile. Direct Kraus/Choi and exact integer enumeration tests are reported in `VERIFICATION.md`. They are checks, not substitutes for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Quillen (arXiv:2607.04584; Sec. IV B, Eqs. 58, 64) supplies the vertex channel, Fourier spectrum and persistent antipodal coherence; no newness is attributed to those facts. Devetak–Shor (arXiv:quant-ph/0311131, Appendix B) already handles generalized dephasing-channel capacity, and Pirandola et al. (doi:10.1038/ncomms15043, Eqs. 40–41) give related dephasing information bounds. The finite-time formula is not asserted to be a new general capacity principle. Diaconis–Graham–Morrison (doi:10.1002/rsa.3240010105) establish a closely related lazy-walk total-variation crossover; the present likelihood-ratio \(L^2\) control, periodic parity treatment, exact relative-entropy constant and quantum-capacity crossing time are not directly implied by total-variation alone. Primary full text for the 1990 work could not be examined, and it may contain stronger intermediate statements that imply the new result; priority is therefore not independently settled.

**Value — PASS.** The work converts a newly described nonergodic averaged quantum walk into a quantitatively sharp operational information-loss benchmark, including a dimension-independent protected qubit, a nontrivial \(O(d)\) capacity-relaxation window, and the time required to suppress any specified fixed excess quantum transmission rate. The constant and critical profile need nontrivial Fourier/entropy analysis; they are not a normalization check or arbitrary numerical slice. The construction is useful for distinguishing retained coherence from transmissible quantum information in a concrete decohering walk. The theorem does not describe individual Haar realizations, universal graph families or explicit optimal finite-block codes.

Closest literature: Quillen (2026) for the stochastic quantum model, Devetak–Shor (2005) and Pirandola et al. (2017) for general quantum capacity, Diaconis–Graham–Morrison (1990) and Voit (1996) for classical hypercube crossover behavior. The separate full-text access limitation and possible implicit derivation are retained rather than being treated as proofs of novelty.
