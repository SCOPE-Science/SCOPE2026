# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The argument transfers Minkowski decomposability to coordinate products using the identified deterministic theorem and proves a sharp Bonferroni lower bound. The uniform exponential suppression of every non-singleton coordinate split follows from an explicit single-column probability and two strict rate gaps. A hyperplane-count estimate verifies full-dimensional conditioning. The prime-factor statement follows after excluding multiple prism coordinates and the remaining product splits. The independent finite script exhausts the three- and four-dimensional sample sets and checks rational inequalities. It does not substitute for the asymptotic proof.

## Originality
PASS under the inspected literature, with residual historical priority risk. Higashitani–Padrol–Sanyal prove deterministic coordinate product structure but no random rare-event law. Ferber–Krivelevich–Sales–Samotij and Guo–Tomon use this or a closely related Bernoulli random Boolean polytope model but prove graph-expansion bounds rather than Minkowski-decomposition probabilities. Semantic database searches for Bernoulli Boolean polytopes, random Cartesian products, factorization in disjoint variables, and the exact exponent returned no stronger covering claim. Equivalent counting of AND-separable Boolean functions in older switching-theory literature remains a possible unindexed precursor.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The result determines the double-exponential rarity **with its sharp factor of \(n\)** and identifies a unique geometric mechanism for the exceptional polytopes. A mere high-probability indecomposability statement would not determine either the prefactor or the conditional structure. The exact prism-union formula further distinguishes the computable dominant event from all exceptional product types.
