# Sharp real-rootedness onset for dilated lattice tetrahedra

## Finding
Let \(S\subset\mathbb R^3\) be any full-dimensional lattice tetrahedron, with Ehrhart polynomial numerator \(h^*(S;t)=1+h_1t+h_2t^2+h_3t^3\). Let \(E\) be the sum of the six lattice edge lengths; an edge from \(u\) to \(v\) has length \(\gcd(|u_1-v_1|,|u_2-v_2|,|u_3-v_3|)\). Write \(\ell^*(mS;t)\) for the local Ehrhart (box) polynomial of the positive integral dilate \(mS\).

Define \(H=1+h_1+h_2+h_3\) and, for each integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
A_m=\sum_{j=0}^3h_j\binom{m+j-1}{3},\qquad(h_0=1),
\]
\[
G_m=\frac{H}{3}m^3+\left(E-\frac23(11+2h_1-h_2+2h_3)\right)m+1.
\]
Then the exact polynomial, including possible zero coefficients, is
\[
\boxed{\ell^*(mS;t)=A_mt+(2A_m+G_m)t^2+A_mt^3.}
\]
For every \(m\ge2\), \(G_m>0\). Thus \(\ell^*(mS;t)\) has a simple root at zero and **two distinct strictly negative roots** exactly when \(A_m>0\). The complete onset classification is:

- At \(m=1\): this happens precisely when \(h_3>0\) and \(E-6-h_1+h_2-h_3>0\).
- At \(m=2\): precisely when \(h_2+h_3>0\).
- At \(m=3\): precisely when \(h_1+h_2+h_3>0\), equivalently, \(S\) is non-unimodular.
- At every \(m\ge4\): for **every** lattice tetrahedron, without exceptions.

The universal dilation \(m=4\) is best possible: the unimodular tetrahedron has \(\ell^*(3S;t)=6t^2\), whereas \(\ell^*(4S;t)=t+19t^2+t^3\). The property, once attained, persists under every larger integral dilation.

## Assumptions and scope
A lattice tetrahedron is a three-dimensional simplex whose four vertices lie in \(\mathbb Z^3\). The Ehrhart numerator is characterized by \(\sum_{n\ge0}|nS\cap\mathbb Z^3|t^n=h^*(S;t)/(1-t)^4\). Its coefficients are nonnegative integers and \(H=6\operatorname{vol}(S)\ge1\). We adopt \(\binom{k}{3}=0\) for integers \(0\le k<3\). The box polynomial counts integer points in the open parallelepiped generated by the homogeneous lattice columns \((mv_i,1)\), \(0<\lambda_i<1\), graded by the integer sum \(\sum_i\lambda_i\in\{1,2,3\}\). A statement about three distinct zeros counts a zero at \(0\) of multiplicity exactly one.

## Proof
**1. Open-box symmetry and its first coefficient.** The substitution \(\lambda_i\mapsto1-\lambda_i\) preserves integrality of the homogeneous lattice point and sends height \(k\) to \(4-k\); consequently the coefficients of \(t\) and \(t^3\) coincide. A height-one open-box point has strictly positive barycentric coordinates summing to one, hence corresponds bijectively to an interior lattice point of \(mS\). Ehrhart–Macdonald reciprocity and the Ehrhart series give
\[
A_m=|\operatorname{int}(mS)\cap\mathbb Z^3|=-L_S(-m)=\sum_{j=0}^3h_j\binom{m+j-1}{3}.
\]

**2. Exact total open-box count.** Write the Ehrhart polynomial as \(L_S(n)=an^3+bn^2+cn+1\). Here \(a=H/6\) and \(b\) is one-quarter of the sum of the normalized two-dimensional lattice areas of the four facets. In the half-open fundamental parallelepiped there are \(6a\,m^3\) lattice points. Removing all coordinate faces \(\lambda_i=0\) by inclusion–exclusion subtracts the normalized facet areas times \(m^2\), adds the six edge lattice lengths times \(m\), subtracts four vertices, and adds the origin. Thus the number of open-box points is exactly
\[
T_m=6am^3-4bm^2+Em-3.
\]
Since symmetry makes \(T_m=2A_m+B_m\), where \(B_m\) is the height-two coefficient, the coefficient gap equals
\[
G_m:=B_m-2A_m=T_m-4A_m=2am^3+(E-4c)m+1.
\]
Direct expansion of the Ehrhart binomial basis yields
\[
a=\frac{1+h_1+h_2+h_3}{6},\quad
b=1+\frac{h_1-h_3}{2},\quad
c=\frac{11+2h_1-h_2+2h_3}{6},
\]
which proves the announced expression for \(G_m\) and the box polynomial.

**3. A positive quantitative gap for every dilation from two onward.** Every edge has lattice length at least one, hence \(E\ge6\); Ehrhart coefficients satisfy \(h_j\ge0\). At \(m=2\), the gap simplifies unexpectedly to
\[
G_2=2E+4h_2-11\ge1.
\]
For every integer \(m\ge2\), the exact cubic identity
\[
G_m=1+\frac m2(G_2-1)+\frac H3(m^3-4m)
\]
shows \(G_m>0\). If \(A_m>0\), the factorization \(\ell^*(mS;t)=t[A_m+(2A_m+G_m)t+A_mt^2]\) has simple zero at zero. The quadratic has discriminant \(G_m(4A_m+G_m)>0\), positive coefficients, and positive product of roots; its two roots are therefore distinct and negative. If \(A_m=0\), the box polynomial is \(G_mt^2\), so it cannot have the required simple zero and two additional negative roots.

**4. Sharp onset.** Ehrhart reciprocity gives, successively,
\[
A_1=h_3,\qquad A_2=h_2+4h_3,\qquad A_3=h_1+4h_2+10h_3,\qquad A_4=1+4h_1+10h_2+20h_3.
\]
All are nonnegative. The first value is positive precisely when \(h_3>0\); at \(m=1\), the gap is \(G_1=E-6-h_1+h_2-h_3\), which must also be positive for three distinct nonpositive roots. At \(m=2,3\), positivity of \(A_m\) gives the stated criteria. At \(m\ge4\), \(A_m\ge\binom{m-1}{3}>0\) by the \(h_0=1\) summand. A three-dimensional lattice simplex has normalized volume one exactly when \(h_1=h_2=h_3=0\), i.e. it is unimodular. Substitution gives \(\ell^*(3S;t)=6t^2\) and \(\ell^*(4S;t)=t+19t^2+t^3\) in that sharp example. This concludes the proof.

## Verification
`CHECKER.py` independently reconstructs the box polynomial from integer points in the four-dimensional open homogeneous fundamental parallelepiped for seven explicit lattice tetrahedra and dilations \(m=1,2,3,4\) (28 tests). It computes the Ehrhart coefficients by separate closed lattice-point enumeration and checks the polynomial identities symbolically in SymPy. All 28 enumerations and all symbolic checks passed. The universal proof is exact and does not infer infinitely many cases from numerical experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Borger, Kretschmer, and Nill (2023, Proposition 4.5) give a general expression for coefficients of the local Ehrhart polynomial of every three-dimensional lattice polytope in terms of interior lattice counts of the body, its double, and its facets. Our inclusion–exclusion step is consistent with that identity. Their theorem does **not** state the sharp universal integer-dilation threshold, the explicit edge-length/Ehrhart-coefficient gap, or the complete three-stage onset classification proved here. Nill (2026, Theorem 1.2) proves eventual distinct negative real roots for local box polynomials of simplices at sufficiently large lattice width, without a sharp finite bound. The present result provides an exact sharp dilation cutoff in dimension three, not a universal lattice-width cutoff for undilated tetrahedra. Prior theory is credited for definitions and general identities, not reclaimed as new.

## Limitations
Only three-dimensional lattice simplices and their positive integral dilates are covered. The optimality example shows that \(m=4\) cannot be reduced **uniformly over tetrahedra**; it does not assert optimality of an absolute lattice-width constant. The theorem is derived using standard Ehrhart reciprocity and boundary-coefficient theory; specialization of broader preexisting formulas may reduce its independent historical novelty. No independent peer review or priority certification is asserted.
