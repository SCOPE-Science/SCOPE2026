# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The object is the classical Jaccard index on detected-species incidence sets, not overlap between individuals. The first-principles inclusion–exclusion support counts recover the full exact finite-population distribution; the direct labeled-subset implementation independently agrees for all 135 profile/sample cells through census size six. Enumeration generates all positive integer partitions, and its 29-profile certificate verifies the sharp first decrease uniquely for abundances \((1,5)\). For arbitrary \(N\ge3\), the Bernoulli detection derivation proves the exact independence and disjoint-design formulas without extrapolating finite experiments. Both designs, sample-size-one endpoints, and feasible disjoint ranges are explicitly delimited.

## Originality

PASS on available evidence, with residual bibliographic risk. Wolda's full 1981 primary text covers sample-size dependence of a broad selection of similarity indices, mostly empirically and not the Jaccard exact finite classification. Chao et al.'s full 2005 primary article includes classic Jaccard comparison of two independently sampled replicates but uses within-replicate replacement and simulation; it does not give the present sharp six-person threshold or protocol gap. Cao et al.'s 2002 abstract directly motivates Jaccard autosimilarity and therefore remains the strongest inaccessible comparison; complete text was unavailable. Targeted research-index searches for exact aliases, hypergeometric supports, and sampling-effort monotonicity found no covering claim. These search outcomes are not proofs of novelty; comparable work outside the inspected records may exist.

## Value

PASS. Autosimilarity is used to assess ecological sampling representativeness, so its failure to be monotone in sample effort is a directly motivated boundary of that methodology. The result combines the natural smallest census, a unique minimal abundance profile, an analytic family with an order-one U-shaped defect, and an exact distinction between independent and non-overlapping survey designs, reaching a \(1/4\) expected-similarity discrepancy at half-census size. It does not merely recompute a table or change notation for a previously stated result.

## Limits

The calculation assumes exact species labels, uniform labeled-individual subsampling, fixed finite abundances, and specified replicate dependence. No claim is made for unseen-species estimation, unequal effort, environmental heterogeneity, or arbitrary large-population minimal classifications. A plausible older full text was inaccessible and an independent mathematical and bibliographic audit is still needed.
