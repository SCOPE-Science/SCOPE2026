# Sharp simultaneous two-point Lipschitz extensions of symmetric clustered metrics

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\) and \(n/(n-1)<t\le2\). A finite metric \(X=A\sqcup B\) has two classes of \(n\) points each, mutual distances \(t\) within either class and distance \(1\) between classes. Adjoin two distinct points \(u,v\). Their distances to \(A\), respectively \(B\), are \(a,b\) for \(u\), and \(b,a\) for \(v\), while \(d(u,v)=h\). Assume
\[
0<a\le b,\qquad t\le2a,\qquad b-a\le1,\qquad a+b\ge1,
\qquad 0<h\le a+b,\qquad b-a\le h.
\]
These and the preceding parameter inequalities make \(Y=X\cup\{u,v\}\) a metric space. Write
\[
w=\frac{n-1}{n}t>1,\qquad \gamma=w-1>0.
\]
For **all** real-linear simultaneous extension operators \(T:\operatorname{Lip}(X;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Lip}(Y;\mathbb R)\), preserving every value on \(X\), let \(\Lambda(n,t,a,b,h)\) be the infimum of the operator norm for Lipschitz seminorms. Then
\[
\boxed{\Lambda(n,t,a,b,h)=
\max\left\{1,\frac1a,\frac{1+w}{a+b},
\frac{1+w}{2a+\gamma h}\right\}.}
\]
The infimum is attained. The formula also equals the relative projection constant of the canonical isometric copy of the real Lipschitz-free space \(\mathcal F(X)\) inside \(\mathcal F(Y)\).

In particular, a norm-one real-linear simultaneous extension exists **if and only if**
\[
\boxed{a\ge1,\qquad a+b\ge1+w,\qquad 2a+\gamma h\ge1+w.}
\]
The last inequality is a genuinely joint constraint: norm-one extension to either single new point need not imply norm-one extension to both points simultaneously.

For example, take \(n=3\), \(t=2\), \(a=1\), \(b=3/2\). All \(1/2\le h\le5/2\) are admissible, and
\[
\boxed{\Lambda(3,2,1,3/2,h)=
\begin{cases}7/(6+h),&1/2\le h\le1,\\1,&1\le h\le5/2.\end{cases}}
\]
Each separate one-point extension already has norm \(1\), but the optimal **joint** norm is greater than \(1\) for every \(h<1\). The norm stops changing at \(h=1\) although the metric continues changing up to \(h=5/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All function spaces and operators are real. The Lipschitz seminorm is
\[
[f]_{\operatorname{Lip}(Z)}=\max_{x\ne y\in Z}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}.
\]
The operator norm is the best constant \(L\) with \([Tf]_{\operatorname{Lip}(Y)}\le L[f]_{\operatorname{Lip}(X)}\) for every \(f\), and \(Tf=f\) on \(X\). Such a bounded extension preserves constants. Equivalently one can choose a basepoint in \(X\) and work in the associated finite-dimensional normed \(\operatorname{Lip}_0\) spaces. The theorem assumes \(w>1\); it does not claim the displayed expression for \(t\le n/(n-1)\). The stated inequalities encompass all admissible metrics in the specified \(w>1\), \(a\le b\) family, including their equality boundaries.

## Proof
**1. The symmetric reduction is global.** The finite isometry group generated by independent permutations of \(A\) and \(B\) and the simultaneous interchange \((A,u)\leftrightarrow(B,v)\) acts on the domain and codomain. Average any extension over conjugation by this group. The average still extends every input function and has no larger operator norm. Its new values necessarily have the form
\[
(T_\alpha f)(u)=\alpha\bar f_A+(1-\alpha)\bar f_B,
\qquad
(T_\alpha f)(v)=(1-\alpha)\bar f_A+\alpha\bar f_B,
\]
with one real \(\alpha\), where \(\bar f_A=n^{-1}\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\) and similarly for \(B\). Coefficients sum to one because constants are fixed. This proves that optimization over \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) is sufficient even when unrestricted operators have negative coefficients and no symmetry.

**2. Exact norm for probabilistic coefficients.** For \(0\le\alpha\le1\), the coefficients at either new point form a probability distribution on \(X\). Fix \(x\in A\). The inequality \(|f(x)-\int f\,d\mu|\le\sum_y\mu(y)d(x,y)[f]_{\operatorname{Lip}(X)}\) is sharp: take \(f(y)=d(x,y)\). It gives the exact edge-norm contribution from \(u\) to \(x\) as \((1+\gamma\alpha)/a\). For \(x\in B\) it gives \((w-\gamma\alpha)/b\); the constraints from \(v\) give the same pair. Moreover,
\[
(T_\alpha f)(u)-(T_\alpha f)(v)
=(2\alpha-1)(\bar f_A-\bar f_B).
\]
Since every cross-distance is \(1\), \(\sup_{[f]_{\operatorname{Lip}(X)}\le1}|\bar f_A-\bar f_B|=1\), witnessed by values zero on one class and one on the other. The old edges force norm at least one. Therefore
\[
\|T_\alpha\|=
\max\left\{1,\frac{1+\gamma\alpha}{a},
\frac{w-\gamma\alpha}{b},\frac{|2\alpha-1|}{h}\right\},
\qquad 0\le\alpha\le1.
\]

**3. Signed invariant coefficients cannot improve the optimum.** For \(\alpha<0\), the test function equal to zero on \(A\) and one on \(B\) gives an \(u\)-to-\(A\) ratio \((1-\alpha)/a\ge1/a\). The distance-to-a-point test at \(B\) gives ratio \((w-\gamma\alpha)/b\ge w/b\). The \(u\)-to-\(v\) ratio is \(|2\alpha-1|/h\ge1/h\). Together with the original edges these bound \(\|T_\alpha\|\) below by \(\|T_0\|\). For \(\alpha>1\), the distance-to-a-point test at \(A\) gives \((1+\gamma\alpha)/a\ge w/a\), while the test equal to one on \(A\) and zero on \(B\) gives an \(u\)-to-\(B\) ratio \(\alpha/b\ge1/b\); the inter-apex ratio is again at least \(1/h\). Hence \(\|T_\alpha\|\ge\|T_1\|\). Thus the global infimum occurs in \([0,1]\).

**4. Solve the minimax exactly.** For \(L\ge1\), the inequalities \(\|T_\alpha\|\le L\) are equivalent to the existence of
\[
\alpha\in[0,1]\cap
\left[\frac{w-bL}{\gamma},\frac{aL-1}{\gamma}\right]
\cap\left[\frac{1-hL}{2},\frac{1+hL}{2}\right].
\]
Pairwise comparison of the three lower endpoints with the three upper endpoints shows that this intersection is nonempty exactly when
\[
L\ge\max\left\{1,\frac1a,\frac1b,
\frac{1+w}{a+b},\frac{1+w}{2a+\gamma h},
\frac{1+w}{2b+\gamma h}\right\}.
\]
Because \(a\le b\), both \(1/b\) and \((1+w)/(2b+\gamma h)\) are dominated, giving the claimed formula. Any \(\alpha\) in this displayed intersection at the optimal \(L\) defines an explicit optimal extension. The norm-one conditions follow by comparing each term with one.

**5. Projection interpretation and example.** The canonical inclusion \(\mathcal F(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal F(Y)\) is isometric because the restricted metric is unchanged. Finite-dimensional duality identifies adjoints of linear right inverses to restriction \(\operatorname{Lip}_0(Y)\to\operatorname{Lip}_0(X)\) with projections onto this copy of \(\mathcal F(X)\), preserving operator norms. This proves the projection-constant statement. In the indicated three-point-per-class example, \(w=4/3\) and \(\gamma=1/3\); substitution yields \(\max\{1,7/(6+h)\}\). For the one-point restriction to \(u\), assigning \(\bar f_B\) to \(u\) has norm one because \(d(u,A)=1\) and the mean distance within \(B\), divided by \(d(u,B)=3/2\), is \((4/3)/(3/2)=8/9\). The swapped assignment works for \(v\). Thus the joint obstruction is real and sharp.

## Verification
A standalone script `check_two_apex.py` constructs the anchored Lipschitz-unit-polytope vertices of several small domains and independently minimizes over **all** real-linear pairs of evaluation functionals by linear programming, without imposing symmetry or positivity. It passed 12 admissible test cases, including the critical values and both sides of the joint contractivity transition, with differences below the declared numerical tolerance. These are finite numerical checks only; the proof above establishes the infinite exact result.

## Relationship to prior work
Bruè, Di Marino and Stra provide general random-projection constructions and norm bounds for linear Lipschitz extensions, not this exact two-new-point optimum. Cúth and Doucha develop invariant projections of Lipschitz-free spaces, providing the broader finite-group averaging context but not the present coupling calculation. Known one-new-point formulas for equilateral cluster unions, including unequal apex-to-cluster radii, address only one new evaluation functional; their separate optima do not control the new pair constraint between \(u\) and \(v\). Published regular-polygon and hypercube extension formulas concern a single added center. The present result resolves the **joint** obstruction, including a range of metrics where each one-point problem has norm one but the two-point problem does not.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns finite equal-sized equilateral two-class metrics, two added points in the specified swapped-distance pattern, real scalars and real-linear simultaneous operators, and the supercritical range \(t>n/(n-1)\). It does not assert the same formula for unequal class sizes, arbitrary two-point attachments or nonlinear extension algorithms. The closed form is a finite-dimensional Banach-space constant; it is not an independent certification of bibliographic uniqueness. Some literature may contain an equivalent formulation under different vocabulary.
