# Scientific review — Walsh–Gabor six-atom threshold

**Correctness (PASS).** The universal upper bound follows from eighteen explicit integer skew-symmetry identities, three generically independent annihilators, and polynomial closure. A separate coefficient-wise expansion confirms all 28 maximal six-column minors vanish identically. The lower bound is a deterministic, exhaustive modular rank calculation on 595665 normalized five-column configurations at a fully specified positive integer window. The projective Gabor composition law justifies normalization, and nonzero modular determinants are genuine complex nonzero determinants. Together these prove sharp spark six and the generic two-versus-three synthesis transition. Residual concern is limited to implementation reproducibility; both supplied checkers were executed and inspected.

**Originality (PASS, with limits).** Malikiosis (arXiv:1602.09012, Theorem 1.2 and §3) proves failure of full spark for all noncyclic finite abelian groups but does not imply the specific six-column circuit or the universal independence of all five-column subsets here. Pfander (2012, §6) presents the general spark/linear-independence setup; Lawrence–Pfander–Walnut (2005) treats prime cyclic dimensions; Bui Dang et al. (2012) use a different, cyclic Weyl–Heisenberg representation with special windows. Searches of related mathematical findings, aliases, and group/parameter combinations returned other Walsh spectra and Gabor measurements, not this exact generic-spark theorem. An undiscovered equivalent in older Pauli/finite-frame literature remains possible; priority is not independently certified.

**Value (PASS).** Spark quantifies the smallest unavoidable sparse-synthesis collision. For the canonical eight-point noncyclic group, the previously available full-spark obstruction only bounds it by eight, whereas the exact answer six proves a sharp transition: every two-atom expansion is unique for generic windows, and some three-atom expansions collide. The finite computation certifies the natural complete incidence condition of every five-column set, not a selected arbitrary sample.
