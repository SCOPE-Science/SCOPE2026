# Scientific review of the two-mode sketched correction theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact sketched shift makes the residual orthogonal in the Gram inner product, so testing the projected correction equation against its one-dimensional complementary basis is legitimate. The complementary-vector matrix identities give an exact correction-solvability criterion and rational projective map without assuming invertibility of the shifted matrix. A norm-preserving embedding estimate gives a sharp geometric disk for the image of the input direction. Tangent-angle optimization gives the upper envelope, attained by an explicit symmetric Gram perturbation with extremal eigenvalues. The envelope crosses the identity precisely at the proposed radius, while an admissible diagonal Gram metric gives the singular boundary and divergence beyond it. The exact algebra and rational examples have been reproduced by a standalone program. Finite checks are corroboration, not a proof of universal claims.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual uncertainty
The September 2026 randomized Jacobi–Davidson paper explicitly establishes the correction equation, its link to inverse iteration, and local quadratic error for sketched shifts on normal matrices. This prior coverage rules out novelty claims about those facts, but does not by itself imply the exact uniform projective attraction radius, attained one-step envelope over all near-isometric sketches, or projected-equation failure at the optimal boundary. Earlier studies of general Rayleigh quotients and ordinary Rayleigh quotient dynamical basins are relevant antecedents, but accessible descriptions do not demonstrate the same uniform sketch-distortion threshold. The closest inspected current source was read through its correction equation, convergence section, remarks and references. Some earlier full texts could not be examined; an unrecognized equivalent specialized calculation remains possible and would require an independent historical comparison.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result is a natural complete sharp classification of robustness of the *exact correction equation* under the paper's near-isometric sketch assumption in the smallest invariant spectral plane. It quantifies both guaranteed convergence and exact loss of solvability, not just another local rate coefficient. The worst-case envelope and its explicit extremizer are useful for interpreting how sketch distortion changes convergence basins and for testing one-vector restart safeguards. The finding does not claim an improvement in the full unrestarted eigensolver or numerical throughput.
