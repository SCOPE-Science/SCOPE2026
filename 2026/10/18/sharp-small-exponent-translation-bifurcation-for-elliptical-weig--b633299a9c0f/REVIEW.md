# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact translated-boundary Hessian is reconstructed from a smooth ellipse parametrization. Reflections diagonalize it, and a two-parameter analytic expansion plus harmonicity of the logarithmic kernel give the sharp transition curve and its second coefficient; the numerical checker compares the expansions with direct quadrature. No inference from finitely many samples to the limiting assertion is used.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
The August 2026 primary paper provides the general translation first/second variations and weighted-barycenter condition but does not derive a near-disk elliptical transition or its sharp small-power threshold. The October 2026 paper treats inclusion-monotone weighted perimeters rather than translation stability. Broad and ellipse-specific published-result searches did not identify a matching claim; some database requests were temporarily unavailable, limiting the strength of exclusion. An older analytic-potential calculation may contain an equivalent result.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result resolves a natural local stability question for a canonical two-parameter class: the small-exponent and small-anisotropy limits interact on an exact critical curve. It shows that symmetry and vanishing first variation are insufficient to guarantee translational optimality at positive exponents, with a sharp second-order location of the instability.

## Scientific limits
Only smooth ellipses near disks and small positive powers are covered; the critical curve is Hessian-degenerate, and no higher-order extremum type is determined there. Global minimizing translations are not classified.
