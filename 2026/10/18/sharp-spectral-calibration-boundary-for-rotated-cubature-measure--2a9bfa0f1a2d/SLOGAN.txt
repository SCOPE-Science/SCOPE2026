For quadratic Gaussian observations, rotating the standard cubature Kalman rule's covariance square root makes its reported innovation variance sweep a sharp interval, and exact mean-square calibration is possible precisely when \(n\ge2+(\operatorname{tr}B)^2/\|B\|_F^2\). Above that spectral threshold both overconfidence and underconfidence occur for the identical isotropic prior, while a constant-diagonal frame can hide arbitrarily large actual estimation error.
