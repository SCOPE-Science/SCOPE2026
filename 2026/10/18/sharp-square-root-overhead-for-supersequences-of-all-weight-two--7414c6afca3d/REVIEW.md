# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The lower proof requires two distinct host ones in each zero-coordinate interval associated with every adjacent-one target; disjoint intervals force the sharp square-root tradeoff. The upper proof explicitly selects two host one positions and checks the zeros before, between and after them, with exact integrality control at the right boundary. The AM–GM minimization and rounding inequalities prove the claimed two finite inequalities for all integers \(n\ge3\). The independent exhaustive host enumeration for lengths \(3\)–\(11\) and construction checks through \(160\) agree. A finite check is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature
2010 restricted-supersequence literature optimizes containment under a prescribed host multiset, rather than minimizing a freely chosen host that covers every binary target of weight two. The 2015 all-permutations literature uses distinct symbols. Ma's 2025 balanced-supersequence theorem has equal growing symbol multiplicities; its exact \(4h-1\) optimum is not a parameter substitution for the two-one family. Complete published results on unrestricted binary subsequence universality and on insertion distance to unrestricted universality concern all binary length-\(k\) targets, not just the fixed sparse layer. Originality is a supported best-knowledge comparison, not a global uniqueness certificate. The residual risk is an obscure fixed-content SCS treatment or a stronger general implication not found in the inspected corpus.

## Value — PASS
The output resolves a natural structural change in shortest-common-supersequence length when the target family is a fixed sparse composition, giving the sharp leading constant \(2\sqrt2\) in the additive square-root overhead. The zero-window packing lemma and matching lattice construction explain the constant and are useful independently of the still-unknown exact finite formula. This is not merely a finite table, renaming, or a negligible numerical improvement.

## Scientific scope and limits
Exactly the binary fixed-weight-two family is covered, for every integer \(n\ge3\). Exact integer values outside the exhaustively checked short lengths, uniqueness of extremizers, and higher fixed weights are not claimed. Independently checking the literature could reveal an unindexed equivalent result.
