# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Independence gives an exact Poisson-binomial variance function with derivative an exponential polynomial on at most \(2n\) exponents. An elementary Rolle-induction bounds all derivative zeros by \(2n-1\), including multiplicity. The explicit rate vector \(\lambda_j=20^{j-1}\) gives \(2n\) chronologically ordered points of alternating derivative sign. The negative witnesses use \(f(\log4)=-1/8\) and the strictly bounded slower-rate geometric sum; positive witnesses use \(1-1/\sqrt2>1/4\) and a convergent faster-rate tail below \(1/20\). This forces exactly \(2n-1\) simple zeros and the stated maximum/minimum counts, for every integer \(n\). The delivered script programmatically checked 110 derivative signs, not the universal quantification. The theoretical proof supplies that quantification.

**Originality — PASS under the inspected sources, with a specified residual risk.** The general Chebyshev zero bound for exponential sums is classical and is excluded from any novelty claim. The finite-species sharp attainment of all \(n\) variance peaks and its open-neighborhood persistence are the assessed contribution. Full-text Sæther–Engen–Grøtan (2013) addresses community abundance variation and estimation, rather than the temporal extrema of the independent exponential survivor-count variance. Full-text Drake (2014) analyzes extinction-time tail laws, rather than moments of a cross-species survivor count. Fox–Kendall (2002) is concerned with individual demographic stochasticity; only its accessible abstract was inspected. Engen (2007) is materially relevant by title/abstract but the full text was inaccessible; this limits priority confidence. Indexed research comparisons involved temporal species discovery, records, abundance variance, exponential-system statistics, and ecological stochastic dynamics, but did not demonstrate an implication of this precise all-size sharpness theorem. No negative search is treated as proof of uniqueness. An independent literature assessment remains desirable.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The variance of surviving richness is the natural uncertainty measure of how many initially present species remain. Mean richness falls monotonically, so a single smooth temporal variance peak is a tempting simplifying assumption. This theorem gives a sharp *finite-complexity* bound, provides an explicit family attaining it for arbitrarily many species, and proves the behavior robust under small hazard perturbations. It characterizes a structural limit for independent exponential local extinctions without requiring a downstream policy optimization theorem.

**Limits and risks.** Correlated/nonequilibrium hazards or immigration invalidate the stated simple variance formula; the result does not characterize those cases. The rate separation is extreme and not empirically calibrated. Exhaustive publication priority is not certified; one plausible 2007 source could not be fully inspected.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
