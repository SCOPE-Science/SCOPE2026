# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

**PASS.** The sign criterion is derived from the explicit two-state Fourier resolvent for the telegraph process with independent Brownian diffusion. The denominator is positive away from a harmless Fourier origin, the numerator yields a necessary and sufficient quadratic discriminant, and Plancherel gives the weak-probe power. Construction by nonnegative Gaussian-modulated probes verifies attainment of positive leading power whenever the strict inequality holds. For the envelope, near-critical rescaling reduces a genuine two-parameter variational problem to maximizing \(y^2(1-z^2-y^2)\) on the unit disk, giving the exact coefficient. The independent numerical checks verify resolvent normalization and the predicted limiting coefficient, but are not substituted for proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to implicit-coverage risk.** Woo, Choi and Rieger (arXiv:2609.17047v1) already provide the general weak-probe response, the diffusion-free one-dimensional sign classification, and a positivity-preserving convolution for nonzero thermal diffusion. This report does not claim those results. The assessed addition is the precise necessary-and-sufficient all-probe thermal cutoff, its finite-speed spectral band, the nonnegative-probe construction, and the sharp critical quadratic optimum under a natural \(L^2\) interaction budget. No such combined result was located in the source's full accessible HTML, in the relevant primary texts of Kim–Choe–Baek or Pei–Maes, in claim-directed published research-record queries, or in targeted literature searches. Because the existing exact response determines the new quantities implicitly, an unlocated analytical exploitation of that response remains a meaningful residual risk.

## Value

**PASS.** A physically natural question left by the general sign and positivity-preservation arguments is how much independent thermal noise is enough to eliminate *all possible* positive-power channels, whether a localized repulsive probe can realize those channels below threshold, and how the best possible transfer disappears as noise reaches the critical value. The exact thermal cutoff and quadratic envelope answer these together without an arbitrary wave-number slice. The peak wave number and optimal speed scaling quantify physically interpretable tuning near the boundary. The result concerns the weak-potential coefficient, not a finite-coupling motor.

## Closest literature and scientific limits

The September 2026 source's general response and Brownian multiplicative ISF are stronger at the level of arbitrary model input; this work extracts a specifically quantified phase boundary and optimization. The 2023/2024 and 2025/2026 papers concern negative drag and active transmission, but do not identify the independent-Brownian thermal threshold or its quadratic envelope in the present class. The proof requires an ideal bath, two-state direction redrawing, homogeneous translational diffusion, and leading weak-probe response. Generality beyond those assumptions, and an independently audited novelty assessment, is not claimed.
