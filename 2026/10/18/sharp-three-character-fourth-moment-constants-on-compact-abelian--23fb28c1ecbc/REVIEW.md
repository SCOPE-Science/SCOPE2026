# Scientific review: sharp three-character fourth moments

## Correctness — PASS
The Fourier-autocorrelation calculation uses normalized Haar measure. Character orders \(m\ge5\), \(m=4\), and \(m=3\) have distinct collision patterns; the formulas are proved separately. Triangle and arithmetic-geometric inequalities reduce the large-order case to a sharp quadratic with equality at endpoint energy \(4/7\), and the order-four case to a sharp quadratic at endpoint energy \(2/3\). Every equality condition, including phase alignment and the order-four real-correlation condition, is checked in both directions. The order-three inequality and its equality cases follow from the three-point Fourier transform. The identity for the high-order deficit is derived by subtraction of the same exact correlations. The bundled verification code passed exact rational checks. There is no reliance on enumeration for the universal quantifier.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Gorbachev's recent survey motivates the finite-exponent Nikolskii sharp-constant problem but does not solve this small three-character configuration. Ling–Geng–Li–Wang treat averages for random polynomials, not coefficient supremum/equality. The closest located published three-frequency result determines endpoint Fourier **projection** norms by an \(L^1\) kernel, not the fourth moment of three-term **synthesis**; its full result was inspected. Related exact Fourier-support results concern zero sets, not the fourth-moment supremum. Searches by equivalent terminology, constants, and spectra did not identify a covering theorem. A classical uncatalogued low-degree identity may already compute the same constant and remains a genuine bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
The invariant is the smallest nontrivial constrained trigonometric \(L^2\)-to-\(L^4\) synthesis norm. The natural family of all cyclic quotients and their compact Abelian extensions exhibits an exact, complete three-regime phase transition at orders \(3,4,5\). Beyond sharp constants, all equality phases and masses are identified and the high-order deficit decomposes into three nonnegative rigidity defects. The conventional equal-coefficient Dirichlet sum fails to extremize from order five onward. The result is a limited but motivated finite-dimensional benchmark, not an assertion about the unresolved unrestricted Nikolskii problem.
