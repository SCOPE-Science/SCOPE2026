# Sharp three-point tangent filling by smooth binary plane quartics

## Finding

Let \(C\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) be a geometrically smooth plane quartic. Call \(C\) *tangent-filling* if the union of its tangent lines at \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational points contains every point of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_2)\). Then

\[
\min_{C\text{ smooth and tangent-filling}}\#C(\mathbf F_2)=3.
\]

There are exactly **364** defining binary quartic equations attaining this minimum, with equations considered up to nonzero scalar (trivial over \(\mathbf F_2\)). They form exactly **four** orbits under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\), with orbit sizes \(84,84,28,168\) and stabilizer orders \(2,2,6,1\), respectively. Representatives are

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_1&=x^3z+y^4+yz^3,\\
F_2&=x^3z+y^4+yz^3+z^4,\\
F_3&=x^3z+xy^2z+xyz^2+y^4+y^2z^2+z^4,\\
F_4&=x^3z+xy^3+y^4+y^3z+y^2z^2+yz^3+z^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

In each case the three rational points are noncollinear, and their three tangent lines form a pencil through one rational point. The pencil center lies on the curve for the first and fourth orbits (\(252\) equations altogether) and outside the curve for the second and third (\(112\) equations).

## Assumptions and scope

All coefficients and rational points are over \(\mathbf F_2\), while geometric smoothness means smoothness over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\). The term tangent denotes the unique projective tangent line at a smooth point. A geometrically smooth plane quartic is geometrically integral and has genus \(3\). The orbit action is by projective linear coordinate changes defined over \(\mathbf F_2\); no assertion is made about possible identifications after extending the ground field.

## Proof

The Fano projective plane has seven rational points, and each rational line contains three of them. One tangent line covers three points, while the union of two distinct rational lines covers five. Hence a tangent-filling curve must have at least three distinct rational tangent lines and thus at least three rational points. Three distinct lines cover all seven points if and only if they are concurrent: the union has seven points for a pencil and six for a triangle.

For the sharp equality case, write a general degree-four homogeneous polynomial as the binary coefficient vector of its fifteen monomials \(x^iy^jz^{4-i-j}\) for \(i,j\ge0\) and \(i+j\le4\). Enumerate all \(2^{15}-1=32767\) nonzero coefficient vectors. For each of the seven rational points, evaluate the polynomial and its three formal partial derivatives exactly in characteristic two. Retain a vector precisely if it has exactly three rational zeros, none singular, and their three rational tangent lines cover all seven points. This gives exactly **448** candidates.

Enumerate all invertible binary \(3\times3\) matrices. There are \((2^3-1)(2^3-2)(2^3-4)=168\), and since the multiplicative group of \(\mathbf F_2\) is trivial this is also the full projective group. Substitute each matrix's three row-linear forms into each candidate polynomial. The resulting 448 vectors form five disjoint orbits, with canonical integer bitmasks (in the monomial order above) and sizes

\[
(4114,84),\ (4115,84),\ (4306,84),\ (4309,28),\ (4383,168).
\]

For every orbit representative, dehomogenize successively in the charts \(x=1\), \(y=1\), \(z=1\) and compute the reduced Gröbner basis of the ideal generated by the affine polynomial and both affine partial derivatives, over \(\mathbf F_2\). The basis is \(\{1\}\) in **all three charts** for masks \(4114,4115,4309,4383\), proving there are no singular points over the algebraic closure. The remaining representative, mask \(4306\), has equation

\[
x^3z+xy^2z+xyz^2+y^4+yz^3=0,
\]

and its singularity ideal in the chart \(x=1\) has reduced Gröbner basis \(\{y^2+y+1,z+1\}\); therefore it has a geometric singular point \([1:\omega:1]\), where \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). Since singularity is preserved by the projective group, its entire 84-element orbit is excluded. The four surviving sizes total \(84+84+28+168=364\). Direct rational-point and tangent-line evaluation of the four representatives gives the claimed collinearity and center-incidence refinements; projective transformations preserve them.

## Verification

The standalone program `verify_fano_quartic.py`, invoked by `python3 verify_fano_quartic.py` with SymPy installed, reconstructs the complete 32767-form census, the 448-element candidate set, all 168 group transformations, their five orbits, the exact Gröbner smoothness tests, and the tangent-center incidence data. It also independently re-evaluates coefficients and all first derivatives without using its precomputed evaluation masks. `verification_output.json` is the captured successful execution output. The script contains hard assertions for all counts and for each orbit size, representative, and smoothness test.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca (first public preprint February 26, 2023) introduced tangent-filling curves and proved general degree obstructions and constructions, but did not determine this three-rational-point smooth-quartic extremum or its projective orbit census. Nart and Ritzenthaler (2003) gave a general classification of all geometrically smooth binary plane quartics; their normal forms provide background coverage of the ambient objects, but no tangent-filling classification or three-tangent-pencil counts. In particular, the present claim is a refined rational-tangent incidence classification, not a claim that these quartics were previously unknown as abstract curves. Homma and Kim's minimum-degree plane-filling examples concern quartics containing **all seven** rational points, not the three-point tangent-filling extremum proved here.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to smooth plane quartics over \(\mathbf F_2\) and to tangent coverage by *rational* contact points. It does not classify tangent-filling quartics over larger fields, characterize every binary quartic with the property, or assert that four orbit representatives remain pairwise nonisomorphic over the algebraic closure. Its originality assessment is based on the primary literature inspected and targeted searches; an unindexed explicit tabulation of the same tangent incidence remains possible. Independent validation is not included.
