# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The source's noncentral-Stirling mass formula and the published Stirling–Irwin–Hall identity imply an exact tilted continuous mixture of ordinary binomial laws. The endpoint Irwin–Hall density leads, by beta integrals and exponentially negligible tails, to a binomial success-parameter displacement of \(-k\phi/((k+\phi)R)+O(R^{-2})\) and a mixing variance of order \(R^{-2}\). A uniform order-\(R\) bound on the \(\ell^1\)-norm of the binomial mass second derivative shows that mixing changes the law by only \(O(R^{-1})\) in total variation after matching its mean. The binomial score identity plus its central limit theorem then supplies the exact \(R^{-1/2}\) constant. Exact-rational identities were independently checked for 150 parameter configurations; four large-\(R\) sequences approached the predicted limiting constant. Universal validity comes from the proof rather than these calculations.

## Originality (PASS on inspected evidence)
O'Neill's spillage articles provide the law, the asymptotic mean/variance, and a Stirling-number-based numerical approximation, but the inspected source's asymptotic and approximation sections do not establish the sharp total-variation distance to the fixed-parameter binomial surrogate. Gismatullin–Tardivel give the Irwin–Hall moment identity as prior mathematics; the present claim is not the identity alone, nor a restatement of O'Neill's moment asymptotics. Targeted searches for the error constant, equivalent tilted-binomial formulations, and broader coverage in published records did not reveal a dominating result. Residual risk: an older work under alternate occupancy or exchangeability terminology could derive the same total-variation constant.

## Value (PASS)
The approximation error's exact leading constant answers a natural accuracy question raised by use of asymptotic occupancy approximations. It establishes not just convergence but the sharp rate for a well-defined finite-support distribution family and explains it through a structured continuous mixture: the \(R^{-1}\) success-parameter shift contributes the leading \(R^{-1/2}\) distance, whereas random mixing contributes only \(O(R^{-1})\). This quantitative distinction is useful for deciding whether binomial approximations have reached the desired accuracy and does not require a downstream general theorem.

## Scope and literature risks
The claim requires fixed \(k\ge1\) and fixed finite positive \(\phi\). No uniform-parameter result, exhaustive literature uniqueness guarantee, or independent audit is asserted. The cited 2024 moment paper's formal contribution is separately credited; the same author's 2026 arXiv posting is a later version and not the first public date. The main 2022 source is the period-qualifying primary source.
