For a tetrahedron with four independently truncated corners, a plane through the original centroid cuts a triangle with positive probability exactly when the sum of the three smallest truncation odds \(t_i/(1-t_i)\) is below one; the entire triangular probability is an explicit sum of spherical solid angles. For equal cuts on a regular tetrahedron, triangular sections disappear sharply at one-quarter of every edge, with probability decaying quadratically toward that threshold.
