# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The reduction to an \(\mathcal F_a\)-measurable continuation event follows from two-date stopping and independent increments. Brownian reflection gives the exact half-normal drawdown and future-supremum laws. Strict rearrangement determines the extreme continuation events, direct differentiation verifies the error-function antiderivative, uniform-quantile sliding fills the interval, and inverse-quantile derivatives prove its symmetric strictly concave width. Numerical quadratures and derivative checks passed, but finite checks are not proofs.

## Originality
PASS, with material priority risk. Beiglböck–Eder–Elgert–Schmock (2018) already prove the broad maximum-optimal drawdown-barrier principle and discuss minimization; this result does not claim that geometry. Deng–Zhang (2026) addresses atomless stopping-time laws through a variational inequality; Bayraktar–Miller treats finite-atomic law optimization mainly with terminal payoffs. The independently evaluated two-date upper and lower values, entire attainable interval, explicit error functions, and strictly symmetric width were not located in the inspected primary materials or relevant research searches. Such a binary special evaluation may exist elsewhere, and independent priority verification is necessary.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Atomic stopping-time distributions are an intrinsic part of the constrained-stopping subject, not an arbitrary computational slice. The result supplies a complete baseline in a solvable natural model: **both** sharp expectations, constructive attainment of every intermediate value without external randomization, and an exact universal choice of mixing mass maximizing the feasible-value range. This complements, rather than replaces, general implicit optimization theories.

## Boundaries and risk
Results apply only to standard one-dimensional Brownian motion and two deterministic stopping dates. Published geometric optimality statements are prior knowledge. The priority of the closed-form frontier and width theorem has not been independently established.
