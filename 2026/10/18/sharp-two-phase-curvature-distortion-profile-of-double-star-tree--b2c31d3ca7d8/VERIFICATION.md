# Reproducibility and scientific checks

Run `python verify_double_star.py` with Python 3, NumPy and SciPy; dependency declarations appear in `requirements.txt`. The accompanying `verification_output.txt` was generated by executing that exact script.

The script checks 726 parameter-and-activity combinations against a separately implemented monotone scalar root finder and the three curvature inequalities. A distinct constrained nonlinear solver checks 180 symmetric optimization problems; these tests are stress checks, not global-optimality proofs. Exact rational arithmetic verifies all three endpoint edge curvatures for 300 integer leaf-class pairs. Finally, 180 direct Wasserstein-1 transport linear programs reproduce the weighted-tree curvature formula on small double stars, without calling the closed-form curvature implementation.

The mathematical proof separately handles all nonuniform leaf weights, both activity branches and the transition point, the entire range of positive integer leaf counts, the maximum-curvature feasibility boundary, and uniqueness of the normalized optimum. Numerical experiments do not independently prove an infinite theorem, bibliographic novelty, or independent validation.
