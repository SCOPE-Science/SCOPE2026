# Shortest cyclic covering of all even-parity binary five-words

## Finding
For a positive integer \(k\), let \(\mathcal E_k\) be the set of length-\(k\) binary strings having even Hamming weight. A *cyclic covering* is a nonempty cyclic binary string containing each member of \(\mathcal E_k\) among its cyclic windows of length \(k\), with wrapping allowed and repeated windows permitted. The host need not satisfy an even-parity constraint.

**Theorem.** For \(k=2,3,4,5\), the shortest cyclic covering lengths are respectively \(4,7,16,24\). At \(k=5\) there are exactly eight optimal cyclic strings modulo rotation, and exactly four modulo rotation and reversal. Each optimum has one occurrence of \(00000\), and the eight optimal rotations rooted at that occurrence are precisely:

```
000001001010001101111011
000001010010001101111011
000001010010111101100011
000001100010100101111011
000001100011011110100101
000001101111010010100011
000001101111011000100101
000001101111011000101001
```

Among these eight representatives, four have five odd-weight length-five windows and three repetitions beyond the sixteen required even-weight windows; four have seven odd-weight windows and only one repetition of an even-weight window. Consequently **every** length-24 optimum repeats a required even-parity window.

## Assumptions and scope
Words are binary, cyclic rotations are identified only when explicitly counting necklaces, and reversal is additionally identified only for the bracelet count. A length-\(n\) cycle has exactly \(n\) cyclic windows counted with multiplicity, also when \(n=k\). The enumerated optimal words are primitive because each has a unique occurrence of \(00000\).

## Proof
Represent length-\(k\) windows by edges of the binary de Bruijn graph on length-\(k-1\) vertices. For even-parity edges, each vertex has exactly one outgoing and one incoming required edge: the missing appended or removed bit is uniquely prescribed by parity. The required edges consequently decompose into directed cycles. At \(k=5\), these are the four necklace cycles generated by \(00000\), \(00011\), \(00101\), \(01111\), with lengths \(1,5,5,5\). The sixteen edges are therefore genuinely distinct requirements.

**Exhaustive lower bound and classification.** Any qualifying cycle includes the required factor \(00000\), so it admits a rotation whose first five positions are zeros. At a fixed candidate length \(n\ge5\), the remaining \(n-5\) bits are arbitrary; precisely \(2^{n-5}\) rooted candidates exist. The standalone program `verify.cpp` visits **every** one of them in increasing length, compares all cyclic length-five windows with the sixteen even-parity patterns, and thereby proves no covering exists at any \(n<24\). At \(n=24\) it finds exactly the eight displayed rooted candidates. It explicitly reduces these eight under all rotations and reversed rotations, yielding eight necklace classes and four bracelet classes. Each displayed word has a unique \(00000\) window, so an optimum cannot contribute more than one rooted representative to this search. This is an exact finite exhaustive proof, not extrapolation or randomized sampling.

To make the exhaustive bound directly reproducible, the program represents a candidate as a 64-bit integer with its top five bits zero, slides a masked five-bit register around the cycle, and builds a sixteen-bit incidence mask for the even-parity windows. The map from even-parity five-bit strings to mask indices is injective because the last bit is determined by the preceding four bits. The full required mask is all sixteen bits set. Bit ranges at length \(24\) are within 64-bit precision, and the search explores at most \(2^{19}\) anchored words at that length. The program also checks the shorter cases \(k=2,3,4\) identically, establishing lengths \(4,7,16\) by the same complete finite enumeration.

**Structural consequence: duplication cannot be avoided.** Suppose instead a cyclic covering traversed each required even-parity length-five edge *exactly once*. Its extra edges would all have odd parity. Odd-parity edges also form a permutation subgraph (one incoming and outgoing at each vertex). Flow conservation forces the multiset of extra edges to be a union of complete odd-edge cycles, with constant multiplicity around each such cycle. The odd-edge cycles at length five are generated by \(00001\), \(00111\), \(01011\), and \(11111\), of lengths \(5,5,5,1\). To connect the isolated even-edge component generated by \(00000\), the added odd edges must include the \(00001\) cycle. To connect the even-edge component generated by \(01111\), they must in addition include at least one of the two distinct five-edge cycles generated by \(00111\) or \(01011\); the odd singleton \(11111\) stays inside that component. Thus at least ten extra odd edges would be needed: such a covering has length at least \(26\). Yet a length-\(24\) covering exists, proving every optimum duplicates at least one even edge. The exact window-count split in the theorem is obtained exhaustively from the eight listed words.

## Verification
Compile the included `verify.cpp` with a C++17-compatible compiler and run the resulting binary. The program enumerates every anchored candidate until first success for \(k=2,3,4,5\). It emits all eight canonical strings at \(k=5\), and the exact grouping by odd-window and repeated-required-window multiplicities. The independent text-level witness check consists of listing all 24 cyclic five-windows of any displayed representative and confirming that their even-parity subset contains all sixteen required patterns. Source code and mathematical definitions are complete in this package.

## Relationship to prior work
King, Laubmeier, Orans, and Godbole, *Universal and Overlap Cycles for Posets, Words, and Juggling Patterns*, arXiv:1405.5938 (first posted 2014-05-23), studies universal and overlap cycles, including fixed-weight word classes. Its available full text was inspected for the results on fixed-weight words. A cycle containing each word of a fixed Hamming weight once, or an overlap cycle for that homogeneous family, does not by itself optimize a repeat-permitting cycle that covers the **union** of all even-weight five-bit words. In particular those results do not furnish the exact length-24 optimum, eight-way classification, or compulsory even-edge duplication identified here. Closely related parity-universal-cycle formulations and differently phrased shortest common cyclic superstring results remain possible sources of undiscovered coverage.

## Limitations
The classification is exact at five-bit windows; it makes no general formula claim for all \(k\). Computation is exhaustive only in the stated bounded length/parameter ranges. External independent review has not been performed. The prior-work comparison has residual risk from literature indexed under other terminology and from sources not available in full text.
