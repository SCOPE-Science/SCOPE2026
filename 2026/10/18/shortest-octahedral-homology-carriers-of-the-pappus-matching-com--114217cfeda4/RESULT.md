# Shortest octahedral homology carriers of the Pappus matching complex

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the eighteen-vertex Pappus graph in the labeled LCF realization \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\), and let \(K=\mathcal M(P)\) be its **ordinary matching complex**. The following statements hold for integral fifth homology.

1. If the induced subcomplex \(K[W]\), on a set \(W\) of graph edges, maps nontrivially to \(\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Z)\), then \(|W|\ge12\). Exactly **297** twelve-vertex induced subcomplexes meet this bound. Every one is an induced boundary of the six-dimensional cross-polytope, equivalently \((S^0)^{*6}\cong S^5\). All 297 oriented fundamental cycles represent **primitive, nonzero** elements of \(\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Z)\).
2. The full 216-element automorphism group of \(P\) acts on these 297 supports with five orbits of sizes \(108,36,108,36,9\). In the deterministic lexicographic enumeration specified below, the orbit representatives occur at zero-based positions `0,1,2,4,12`; the integral ranks of the lattices generated by their fundamental classes are respectively \(40,25,35,31,5\). Each orbit-generated lattice is primitive in ambient fifth homology.
3. **A single orbit of size 108 generates all fifth integral homology.** Indeed, 40 of its 108 sphere cycles form an integral basis of
\[
\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{40}.
\]
The 40 selected supports and an exact unimodular determinant certificate are reconstructed by `verify_pappus_carriers.py`. They belong to the orbit of the path partition with triples `(1,0,5)`, `(3,2,13)`, `(7,6,11)`, `(8,9,10)`, `(4,15,14)`, `(12,17,16)`; each triple is ordered as (endpoint, center, endpoint).

These are statements about *where existing fifth homology lives and which shortest spheres generate it*, not a new claim for the previously determined abstract homotopy type of \(K\).

## Assumptions and scope
Index graph vertices by \(0,\ldots,17\). Insert all cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\bmod18\}\), as well as the distinct chords \(\{i,i+s_i\bmod18\}\), where \((s_i)=[5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\). Sort the resulting 27 graph edges lexicographically to index the vertices of \(K\) by \(0,\ldots,26\). A face of \(K\) is a set of edge indices with pairwise disjoint endpoints. All simplices use the increasing-index orientation; integral homology is reduced where indicated.

A **six-block path partition** is a partition of all eighteen graph vertices into six disjoint three-vertex paths. Each block \((a,v,b)\) contributes the two incident edges \(\{a,v\}\) and \(\{v,b\}\); these form an induced zero-sphere in \(K\). Its sixfold simplicial join has twelve vertices and is an induced five-sphere. Reverse choices or orientations change only the sign of the fundamental homology class. The orbit computations use the full graph automorphism group, rather than only the evident cyclic subgroup.

The finite theorem is restricted to this labeled Pappus graph and the fifth homology of its ordinary matching complex. It does not assert that every inclusion-minimal nonzero support, of arbitrary size, has twelve vertices.

## Proof
**Sharp flag-support bound.** We first prove a general lemma. If a finite flag complex \(X\) has \(\widetilde H_d(X;\mathbb Z)\ne0\) for \(d\ge0\), then it has at least \(2d+2\) vertices. At equality it is exactly the join of \(d+1\) zero-spheres. For \(d=0\), nonzero reduced homology requires at least two vertices, and equality gives a pair of nonadjacent vertices. Induct on \(d\): choose any vertex \(v\). If \(v\) is adjacent to every other vertex, flagness makes \(X\) a cone, impossible. Otherwise, if \(|V(X)|\le2d+1\), the deletion \(X-v\) has vanishing degree-\(d\) homology by induction on the number of vertices (or by minimality of a support), while the link of \(v\), with at most \(2d-1\) vertices, has vanishing degree-\((d-1)\) homology by induction on \(d\). The Mayer–Vietoris sequence for \(X=(X-v)\cup(v*\mathrm{lk}_X(v))\) then contradicts nonzero degree-\(d\) homology. If \(|V(X)|=2d+2\), the same sequence implies that every vertex link has nonzero degree-\((d-1)\) homology and hence at least \(2d\) vertices. As no vertex is universal, every vertex has exactly one nonneighbor. The complement of the one-skeleton is therefore a perfect matching; flagness forces \(X\cong(S^0)^{*(d+1)}\). This induction is over integral homology and is not an inference from sampled computations.

Applying the lemma at \(d=5\) to any twelve-vertex induced subcomplex carrying ambient fifth homology shows that it must be an octahedral five-sphere. Nonedges of a matching complex are precisely pairs of graph edges meeting at a graph vertex. Therefore its six opposite-vertex pairs come from six pairwise vertex-disjoint paths of length two in \(P\), necessarily partitioning all eighteen graph vertices. Conversely, such a path partition determines an induced sixfold join of zero-spheres. Distinct partitions yield distinct induced twelve-vertex supports, since the six nonedge pairs are uniquely recovered from the induced one-skeleton.

**Exact support and symmetry enumeration.** Every graph vertex has three neighbors, hence there are exactly 54 centered two-edge paths. A finite exact-cover recursion partitions the eighteen vertices into six of these 54 paths. It returns 297 distinct partitions. Exhaustive automorphism enumeration gives \(|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|=216\) and orbit representatives at indices `0,1,2,4,12` with sizes \(108,36,108,36,9\). These counts come from disjoint finite recursions and are recomputed from the graph definition in the verifier.

**Integral Morse and lattice certificate.** The matching counts by cardinality zero through nine are
\[
(1,27,297,1719,5643,10557,10737,5319,1026,42).
\]
Include the empty matching as an augmented face, and toggle incident-free edge indices successively in the deterministic order

`[7,13,17,26,25,12,21,2,8,20,22,15,23,10,19,3,9,16,24,5,4,1,6,0,11,18,14]`.

At each stage pair every still-active face not containing that edge with its still-active one-edge extension, deleting both from later stages. There are 17,661 pairs and exactly 43 critical five-simplices and three critical six-simplices. Reversing matched face incidences in the oriented Hasse diagram gives an acyclic directed graph, verified by a complete topological sort on all 35,368 augmented faces. Thus integral algebraic discrete-Morse cancellation yields a three-column boundary matrix \(B\) on the 43 critical five-generators. Rows `2,3,15`, in sorted critical-face order, give a three-by-three determinant of absolute value one. Hence \(B\) is primitive of rank three, and the fifth homology lattice is a free rank-40 quotient.

For every degree-five simplex matched upward to degree six, recursively substitute the signed boundary of its paired six-simplex, dividing only by the unit incidence \(\pm1\). A degree-five simplex matched downward to dimension four projects to zero; a critical five-simplex projects to its coordinate basis vector. Acyclicity guarantees termination. This constructs the exact integral Morse projection. The package verifies that all 5,319 original six-dimensional simplicial boundaries reduce to the integer image of \(B\), not just that the three critical columns have correct rank.

For each path partition, expand the oriented join
\[
(e_1-e_1')*\cdots*(e_6-e_6')
\]
into its 64 signed five-simplices, reorienting each by increasing edge index. Its simplicial boundary vanishes exactly. Project this cycle through the integral Morse map, then eliminate the three primitive boundary columns by their unimodular three-row minor. The result is a 40-coordinate **integral** homology vector. Each of the 297 vectors has coordinate gcd one, proving that its represented class is nonzero and primitive.

A Smith normal form calculation on each orbit's 40-by-orbit-size integer matrix gives nonzero invariant factors all equal to one, with respective ranks \(40,25,35,31,5\). In particular, the size-108 orbit represented by packing index `0` contains the 40 packing indices listed in the standalone verifier as `BASIS_INDICES`. Their 40-by-40 coordinate matrix has **determinant \(-1\)**. They therefore form an integral basis of the entire ambient fifth-homology group. This is an exact unimodular integer certificate rather than a rational-rank or modular-spanning inference.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner, Lovász, Vrećica and Živaljević's 1994 matching-and-chessboard paper established foundational connectivity results for complete and complete multipartite matching settings, not these Pappus-specific induced supports. Marietti and Testa studied complexes arising from forests; the Pappus graph is cyclic. Bayer, Goeckner and Jelić Milutinović classify graphs whose matching complexes are homology manifolds; the Pappus matching complex is nonpure, with maximal matchings of sizes six through nine, so the manifold classification does not supply its carrier lattice.

A previously published explicit integral calculation for the **same ordinary Pappus matching complex** gives \(K\simeq\bigvee^{40}S^5\) and a different 43-by-three Morse certificate. That prior result is fully credited and is consistent with the rank here. Its mathematical statement, proof, and verification discussion do **not** determine the minimum induced-support size, the 297 primitive octahedral carriers, their five orbits, the orbit-specific integral ranks, or the 40-cycle unimodular basis entirely inside one orbit. A separate published mod-two calculation gives only the degree-five Betti number 40. Neither prior conclusion logically implies the induced-support or integral-spanning classification, and the numerical 297 is not the number of two-edge matchings (which is also 297, coincidentally); the two counts concern different finite sets.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the single labeled cubic Pappus graph, not all cubic symmetric graphs. The exact 297 is obtained from exhaustive finite path-factor enumeration, not a closed-form general counting theorem. The lattice calculation is for degree-five homology; other homological degrees are not newly classified. Matching-complex homotopy, torsion-freeness and rank 40 were already available from the prior integral computation and are not claimed here as original. An equivalent induced-carrier classification might remain in literature not indexed under Pappus or matching-complex terminology; absolute bibliographic priority is not asserted. No independent external mathematical audit of the new carrier statement has been performed.
