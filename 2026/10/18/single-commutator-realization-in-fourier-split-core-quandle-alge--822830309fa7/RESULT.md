# Single-commutator realization in Fourier-split core quandle algebras

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of **odd** order, let \(K\) be a field containing a primitive \(e\)-th root of unity, where \(e\) is the exponent of \(A\), and let \(\mathcal Q=\operatorname{Core}(A)\) be the quandle with operation \(a*b=2b-a\). In the (generally nonassociative) quandle algebra \(\mathcal R=K[\mathcal Q]\), let \(\mathcal R'\) denote the **nonunital subalgebra generated by all commutators** \([u,v]=uv-vu\).

Every element of \(\mathcal R'\) is **one commutator**. More precisely,
\[
\operatorname{cw}(\mathcal R)=
\begin{cases}
0,&3A=0,\\
1,&3A\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]
Here length zero is assigned to the zero element, and commutator width is the maximum minimum number of commutators needed to represent an element of \(\mathcal R'\). In addition, the already-known description of the commutator subalgebra specializes to
\[
\dim_K\mathcal R'=|A|-|A[3]|,
\qquad A[3]=\{a\in A:3a=0\}.
\]
In particular, every dihedral quandle algebra \(K[R_n]\) for odd \(n\ge5\) has commutator width one whenever \(K\) contains a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity; and \(K[R_1]\), \(K[R_3]\) have width zero. For finite fields \(K=\mathbb F_q\), the splitting hypothesis is exactly \(e\mid(q-1)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The group \(A\) is finite, abelian and of odd order; \(e\) denotes its exponent. The root-of-unity condition implies \(\operatorname{char}K\nmid |A|\), so \(|A|\) is invertible in \(K\), and all \(|A|\) characters of \(A\) are \(K\)-valued. No restriction on \(\operatorname{char}K\) other than the splitting hypothesis is used, including characteristic two. There is **no assertion** here for nonsplitting fields or fields whose characteristic divides \(|A|\). The theorem addresses commutator **width**, not just the already-known linear span of commutators.

Write \(e_a\) for the basis element of \(\mathcal R\) indexed by \(a\in A\). Thus \(e_a e_b=e_{2b-a}\). For each character \(\chi\in\widehat A=\operatorname{Hom}(A,K^\times)\), define
\[
F_\chi=\sum_{a\in A}\chi(a)e_a.
\]
The family \((F_\chi)_{\chi\in\widehat A}\) is a basis by character orthogonality.

## Proof

**1. Fourier multiplication.** A direct change of variables \(z=2b-a\), followed by character orthogonality, gives
\[
F_\chi F_\psi
=\sum_{a,b\in A}\chi(a)\psi(b)e_{2b-a}
=|A|\,\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\psi=\chi^{-2}\}}F_{\chi^{-1}}.
\tag{1}
\]
The indicator has value one precisely when the characters agree. Because \(|A|\) is odd, the map \(T:\widehat A\to\widehat A\), \(T(\chi)=\chi^{-2}\), is an automorphism. Its fixed points are exactly the characters satisfying \(\chi^3=1\). Furthermore, \(T^2(\chi)=\chi^4\), so a two-cycle would also satisfy \(\chi^3=1\), hence is actually a fixed point. Therefore every nonfixed \(T\)-orbit has length at least three.

**2. The commutator subalgebra.** Formula (1) implies that every commutator belongs to
\[
W=\operatorname{span}_K\{F_\chi: \chi^3\ne1\}.
\]
Indeed the output character \(\chi^{-1}\) is fixed if and only if the input character \(\chi\) is fixed, and in the fixed case the two input character indices must coincide, so their alternating coefficient vanishes. Conversely, if \(\chi^3\ne1\), then
\[
[F_\chi,F_{T\chi}]=|A|F_{\chi^{-1}},
\]
because \(T^2\chi\ne\chi\). Thus the **linear span** of commutators equals \(W\). Formula (1) also shows that \(W\) is a subalgebra: if a product of two of its Fourier generators is nonzero, its output is another such generator. Consequently \(\mathcal R'=W\). This gives \(\dim_K\mathcal R'=|A|-|\widehat A[3]|=|A|-|A[3]|\). The last equality follows by decomposing \(A\) into cyclic prime-power groups and counting their characters of order dividing three; this dimension is not the main width-one contribution.

**3. Cyclic determinant lemma, over an arbitrary field.** For every field \(K\), every integer \(m\ge3\) and every prescribed tuple \((c_0,\ldots,c_{m-1})\in K^m\), there exist \(v_i=(x_i,y_i)\in K^2\) such that
\[
\det(v_i,v_{i+1})=x_i y_{i+1}-y_i x_{i+1}=c_i,
\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(m=3\), if all prescribed values vanish take all vectors zero. Otherwise cyclically relabel so that \(c_0\ne0\), and take
\[
v_0=(1,0),\qquad v_1=(0,c_0),\qquad
v_2=(-c_1/c_0,-c_2).
\]
These yield exactly the required three determinants, including in characteristic two. For \(m>3\), replace the two last prescribed edges by a single edge with determinant \(1\), producing the shorter cyclic list \((c_0,\ldots,c_{m-3},1)\). By induction choose \(v_0,\ldots,v_{m-2}\) for that shorter list, so \(\det(v_{m-2},v_0)=1\). Insert
\[
v_{m-1}=c_{m-1}v_{m-2}+c_{m-2}v_0.
\]
Then \(\det(v_{m-2},v_{m-1})=c_{m-2}\) and \(\det(v_{m-1},v_0)=c_{m-1}\); all other edge determinants are unchanged. This proves the lemma constructively over every field.

**4. A single commutator realizes an arbitrary target.** Let \(w\in\mathcal R'=W\) be arbitrary, and write its unique Fourier expansion \(w=\sum_{\eta\in\widehat A} b_\eta F_\eta\); its coefficients vanish when \(\eta^3=1\). Partition the nonfixed characters into disjoint \(T\)-orbits. On an orbit \((\chi_0,\ldots,\chi_{m-1})\) indexed so that \(\chi_{i+1}=T\chi_i\), set
\[
c_i=|A|^{-1}b_{\chi_i^{-1}}.
\]
Every orbit length is at least three, so step 3 provides pairs \((x_i,y_i)\) with successive determinants \(c_i\). Assign \(x_{\chi_i}=x_i\), \(y_{\chi_i}=y_i\) on every orbit, and set both coefficients to zero at the fixed characters. Form
\[
u=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}x_\chi F_\chi,
\qquad
v=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}y_\chi F_\chi.
\]
Formula (1) gives the exact coefficient identity
\[
[u,v]
=|A|\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}
(x_\chi y_{T\chi}-y_\chi x_{T\chi})F_{\chi^{-1}}
=\sum_{\chi^3\ne1}b_{\chi^{-1}}F_{\chi^{-1}}=w.
\]
No cross-orbit terms occur, because (1) couples only a character to its successor under \(T\). Hence every element of \(\mathcal R'\) is one commutator.

Finally \(W=0\) exactly when every character has order at most three, equivalently \(3A=0\). Otherwise \(W\ne0\), and a nonzero element cannot have commutator length zero. Thus the asserted width is exactly zero or one. \(\square\)

## Verification

A standalone pure-Python verifier checks formula (1) implicitly by converting Fourier-constructed witnesses back into the original quandle basis and directly computing all products \(e_a e_b=e_{2b-a}\). It tests cyclic groups of orders \(1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15,21,25\) over splitting prime fields and product groups with invariant-factor lists \((3,5),(5,5),(3,9),(3,3,5),(7,7)\). Each test uses fresh target coefficients in the computed commutator subspace and constructs one commutator realizing that target. The verifier additionally checks every prescribed cyclic determinant list over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) in lengths three through six. Run \(\texttt{python3 checker.py}\); successful termination prints \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). These finite tests are consistency checks; the all-group, all-field result follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Sangare and Ta, *Idempotents, automorphism groups, and commutator widths of quandle algebras* (arXiv:2608.01550, first posted August 3, 2026), define the same nonunital commutator subalgebra and its width, ask for widths of dihedral quandle algebras in Problem 5.3, and in Theorem 5.4 give finite-field tables. In particular \(\operatorname{cw}(\mathbb F_5[R_5])=2\), which **does not contradict** the present theorem: characteristic five divides \(|R_5|\), so the required Fourier splitting fails. Their finite tables do not imply the uniform width-one theorem for all odd finite abelian groups over all splitting fields.

Zhang, *Group-ring methods for Alexander quandle rings* (arXiv:2610.08204v1, October 6, 2026), Theorem 2.3, identifies the **linear span/subalgebra** generated by all commutators with a subgroup ideal determined by the Alexander parameter. That result is credited and is compatible with step 2. It does **not** assert that each element of that subalgebra is itself one commutator; that stronger nonlinear decomposability assertion is the contribution proved here.

Bardakov and Sangare, *Constructions and automorphisms of quandles* (arXiv:2610.18676v1, first posted October 13, 2026), Corollary 5.8, identify the universal commutativization of a dihedral quandle. This concerns a quotient of quandles, not commutator lengths in the linearized algebra; it is comparison literature, **not** the motivating eligible source.

## Limitations

The splitting hypothesis is essential to this proof. Nothing here settles the commutator width for arbitrary nonsplitting coefficient fields or modular characteristic dividing \(|A|\). In particular, the known width-two case over \(\mathbb F_5[R_5]\) remains valid. The proof is not a classification of all quandles or all Alexander quandle algebras. The novelty assessment is based on the identified literature and is not an independent bibliographic certification; an unindexed equivalent theorem is possible.
