# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof computes the Fourier-basis product exactly using character orthogonality and the invertibility of the odd group order, identifies the derived subalgebra, and gives an explicit constructive cyclic determinant lemma valid in every characteristic. Every nonfixed orbit of character inversion-squaring has length at least three, so the lemma realizes arbitrary Fourier targets with one pair of algebra elements. The zero-width case is identified precisely. Critical assumptions are stated: finite abelian odd-order group and a splitting coefficient field. Independent direct-basis finite-field tests in the standalone verifier confirm the formulas, without being substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The precise advance is the uniform **one-commutator** representation theorem for all finite odd-order abelian core quandles over splitting fields, not the already-known description of their commutator subalgebra. Sangare–Ta (2026), Section 5, asks for commutator widths and reports bounded finite tables, including a modular width-two exception outside this theorem's hypotheses. Zhang (2026), Theorem 2.3, determines the commutator span but does not solve the nonlinear one-commutator realization question. Bardakov–Sangare (October 2026), Corollary 5.8, treats quandle commutativization, not algebra commutator width. Searches of the mathematical index and relevant primary literature did not identify a stronger or equivalent covering theorem; this is not proof of global uniqueness. Residual risk is unindexed prior work on Fourier cyclic bilinear maps or quandle commutator widths.

## Value — PASS

This gives an exact, constructive answer to the commutator-width problem in a natural infinite, unbounded-size family and extends the dihedral case to all finite abelian groups of odd order. The decisive lemma is uniform over all fields, the Fourier splitting is mathematically motivated, and the theorem explains why a modular example can have width two without contradicting width one in the split semisimple regime. It is a structural nonlinear image theorem, not merely another computation of a linear commutator span or a finite table.

## Scope and limitations

No result is claimed for nonsplitting fields or modular characteristic dividing the group order. Source equivalence under unindexed terminology is possible. No independent audit or independent validation has been performed.
