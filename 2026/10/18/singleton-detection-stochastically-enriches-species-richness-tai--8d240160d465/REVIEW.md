# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The conditional decomposition of detected richness, the nested coupling, and the exact sharpness witnesses were reconstructed. Uniformity within the non-tagged finite census follows from conditional hypergeometric selection; the independent scheme follows from conditional categorical draws. Pathwise richness increments are zero or one, so all feasibility boundaries and each threshold inequality follow. The no-conditioning-event case is handled only for covariance, not for an undefined conditional distribution. Exact subset and iid enumeration checked 12,127 finite dominance comparisons; this is a check on, not a replacement for, the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (self-review)

Gnedin–Hansen–Pitman (arXiv:math/0701718) analyze occupancy and frequency-count moments, not the general conditional stochastic order under both finite-census and independent sampling. The published finite-uniform-box covariance sign-switch result provides positive singleton covariance only in its narrower model, and explicitly excludes conditional order and heterogeneous laws. Coddington and colleagues examine ecological singletons empirically. The accessible Chao–Lin abstract treats rare-species richness estimators; its technical text was not inspected. The claimed contribution is the unrestricted per-tag upper-tail dominance together with the precise frequency-one versus higher-frequency sign boundary, not a newly invented occupancy representation. Closely related classical urn-model arguments might already imply it; independent literature verification remains necessary.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This provides a distribution-free direction for sample-richness tail association with singleton detection, simultaneously for all fixed communities, tags, sampling depths, and richness thresholds in two standard sampling designs. The singletons' special behavior is structurally sharp: every higher exact frequency admits both covariance signs at a common depth. This supports precise null-model diagnostics for relationships between rare species and observed biodiversity, beyond a specific finite-profile calculation. The result is not an estimator guarantee and is not asserted for clustered or species-detection-biased surveys.
