# Singular baskets of integer box-section toric varieties

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge4\), positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), and an integer \(k\) with \(0<k<A:=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Set
\[
P(\mathbf a,k)=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:0\le x_i\le a_i\ (1\le i\le n),\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i=k\}.
\]
Let \(X(\mathbf a,k)\) be the normal projective toric variety of the **normal fan** of this \((n-1)\)-dimensional lattice polytope in the affine lattice \(\sum_i x_i=k\).

**Theorem.** The reduced singular locus is a finite set, indexed bijectively by subsets
\[
\mathcal B(\mathbf a,k)=\bigl\{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:2\le |S|\le n-2,\ \sum_{i\in S}a_i=k\bigr\}.
\]
At the torus-fixed point indexed by \(S\), with \(s=|S|\) and \(t=n-s\), the **entire affine toric chart** is the affine rank-at-most-one determinantal variety of \(s\times t\) matrices,
\[
U_S\simeq\operatorname{Spec}K[z_{ij}:1\le i\le s,1\le j\le t]\big/I_2(z),
\]
where \(I_2(z)\) is generated by the \(2\times2\) minors. In particular, its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity at the torus-fixed point is \(\binom{n-2}{s-1}\); the local affine divisor class group is torsion-free infinite cyclic; and the singularity is Gorenstein (equivalently, \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein) exactly when \(2s=n\). All positive-dimensional torus orbits are smooth.

Write \(N_s(\mathbf a,k)\) for the number of singular points of matrix size \(s\times(n-s)\). The complete basket is extracted by a single finite subset-sum product:
\[
N_s(\mathbf a,k)=[u^s z^k]\prod_{i=1}^n(1+u z^{a_i}),\qquad 2\le s\le n-2.
\]
Thus \(\#\operatorname{Sing}(X(\mathbf a,k))=\sum_{s=2}^{n-2}N_s(\mathbf a,k)\). These conclusions hold for every algebraically closed base field \(K\), including positive characteristic.

For the natural nonuniform section \(\mathbf a=(1,1,1,2,2,3)\) and \(k=3\), the toric fivefold has \(24\) torus-fixed points. Exactly \(7\) are singular: \(6\) points of local type \(2\times4\) with multiplicity \(4\) and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein local rings, together with one point of local type \(3\times3\), multiplicity \(6\), and a Gorenstein local ring. The other \(17\) fixed points are smooth. Hence one natural toric variety supports both Gorenstein and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein isolated Segre-type singularities.

## Assumptions and scope

The lattice of tangent differences is \(M=\{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i m_i=0\}\). Its dual is \(N=\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\); all cones, primitive ray generators, class groups, and smoothness statements refer to this **saturated** lattice, not a nonprimitive sublattice. Normal-fan toric geometry, not the normal toric variety of a semigroup grading or the face fan, is intended. The section is full dimensional because \(0<k<A\) and each cap is positive.

A box vertex means a point with every coordinate at either its lower or upper cap. The affine chart of a box vertex is a torus-invariant open neighborhood of the associated fixed point. A matrix cone of size \(1\times t\) or \(s\times1\) is affine space and is not counted as singular. The theorem classifies reduced singular support and local rings; it does not claim a classification of global crepant resolutions, intersection cohomology, or embedded/nonreduced singular schemes.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustion of vertices.** A vertex of a hyperplane slice of an \(n\)-box must have at least \(n-1\) independent active coordinate bounds: with two free coordinates one can move one up and the other down, obtaining a line segment. Thus a vertex either (i) has exactly one coordinate strictly between its bounds or (ii) is a box vertex. Conversely, every feasible box corner, and every feasible point with one free coordinate and the other \(n-1\) coordinates at bounds, is a vertex. A box vertex is precisely
\[
v_S=(a_i\mathbf 1_{i\in S})_{i=1}^n\qquad\text{for a subset }S\text{ satisfying }\sum_{i\in S}a_i=k.
\]
At a one-free-coordinate vertex, the \(n-1\) active coordinate bounds give the signed classes of \(n-1\) distinct standard basis vectors in \(N\), so its normal cone is unimodular and its toric fixed point smooth.

**2. Integral tangent semigroup at a box vertex.** Fix \(v_S\). Write \(I=S\), \(J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus S\), with \(s=|I|\), \(t=|J|\). A tangent lattice vector \(\mathbf d\in M\) has \(d_i\le0\) for \(i\in I\) and \(d_j\ge0\) for \(j\in J\). Put \(p_i=-d_i\) for \(i\in I\) and \(q_j=d_j\) for \(j\in J\). The exact saturated tangent semigroup is
\[
\Gamma_{s,t}=\bigl\{(\mathbf p,\mathbf q)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{s+t}:\sum_i p_i=\sum_j q_j\bigr\}.
\]
Every such vector decomposes into unit pairs \((\mathbf e_i,\mathbf e_j)\): repeatedly select a positive coordinate on each side and subtract one from both, ending at zero. The semigroup homomorphism from the free monoid on \(st\) symbols \(z_{ij}\) to \(\Gamma_{s,t}\) is therefore surjective. Its kernel is exactly the ideal of the \(2\times2\) minors: monomial products can be interpreted as bipartite multigraphs with fixed degrees, and all such matchings are related by elementary swaps on four-cycles. Each swap corresponds to \(z_{ij}z_{i'j'}-z_{ij'}z_{i'j}\). Thus the normal-fan chart, whose coordinate semigroup is the dual cone lattice points, is \(K[\Gamma_{s,t}]\simeq K[z_{ij}]/I_2(z)\), with no additional saturation or normalization.

**3. All proper cones are regular.** Using the inward/minimizing normal-fan convention, the box-vertex normal cone has signed coordinate generators \(v_i=-[\mathbf e_i]\) for \(i\in I\) and \(v_j=[\mathbf e_j]\) for \(j\in J\), in \(N\). If \(s,t\ge2\), these \(n\) rays are all extremal. Their unique linear dependence is
\[
\sum_{i\in I}v_i=\sum_{j\in J}v_j.
\]
Any proper face has a supporting nonzero \(\mathbf m\in M_{\mathbb R}\) that pairs nonnegatively with each generator; hence its coordinates on \(I\) are nonpositive and those on \(J\) nonnegative. The equation \(\sum m_i=0\) forces \(\mathbf m\) to pair strictly positively with at least one ray in each group. A proper face therefore omits at least one ray of each sign. Its ray set is a subset of \(n-2\) signed standard-coordinate classes. Such a subset extends to \(n-1\) distinct signed standard-coordinate classes, an integral basis of \(N\). Thus **every proper face is unimodular**. Together with smoothness at all one-free-coordinate vertices, this shows that every non-fixed torus orbit lies in a regular cone. In contrast, when \(s,t\ge2\), the full-dimensional cone has \(n>n-1\) extremal rays, so its unique fixed point is singular. If \(s=1\) or \(t=1\), the semigroup above is free and the chart affine space.

**4. Numerical and canonical invariants.** For \(s,t\ge2\), the rank-one matrix variety is the affine cone over the Segre embedding \(\mathbb P^{s-1}\times\mathbb P^{t-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{st-1}\). Its homogeneous coordinate ring has degree-one Hilbert function \(H(d)=\binom{s+d-1}{s-1}\binom{t+d-1}{t-1}\). The top Hilbert coefficient gives degree, hence Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity, \(\binom{s+t-2}{s-1}=\binom{n-2}{s-1}\).

The primitive normals span \(N\) as an abelian group because any \(n-1\) distinct signed coordinate classes form a basis. The exact toric divisor sequence \(M\to\mathbb Z^n\to\mathrm{Cl}(U_S)\to0\) therefore yields a torsion-free rank-one class group. A toric canonical divisor is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier precisely if a rational character \(\mathbf m\in M_{\mathbb Q}\) pairs to \(1\) with **all** rays. These values force \(m_i=-1\) for \(i\in I\) and \(m_j=1\) for \(j\in J\), so the zero-sum requirement becomes \(s=t\). In the balanced case this character is integral and gives Cartier canonical divisor, proving both directions of the claimed Gorenstein/\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein classification.

**5. Enumeration.** A box vertex contributes a singular point exactly when \(2\le |S|\le n-2\) and the cap sum equals \(k\). Expanding \(\prod_i(1+u z^{a_i})\) independently selects each cap either zero or full; its \(u\)-exponent records cardinality and its \(z\)-exponent records the sum. Coefficient extraction gives the asserted complete basket, with no assumption of distinct caps. For the displayed example, elementary subset-sum counts give six size-two subsets and one size-three subset; direct box-edge enumeration gives \(24\) vertices and one additional smooth box corner.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library checker `verify_box_slices.py` reconstructs vertices as all feasible intersections of the slice with edges of the box, counts subsets independently, and examines all applicable wall vertices for six different nonuniform or uniform cap vectors. It checks lattice saturation indices for every proper normal-cone face at each nonsmooth box vertex by computing exact integer maximal minors. It separately enumerates small-degree semigroup monomials in each rank-one determinantal model. Both slight perturbations of the slice level are enumerated to check the predicted adjacent vertex counts. Run `python3 verify_box_slices.py`; successful output ends with `BALANCED_SEMIGROUP_DEGREES_OK` and `VERIFY_OK`.

These finite checks corroborate the proof but do not establish the theorem for unbounded numbers of coordinates or cap values. The infinite statements are proved in Steps 1--5 above.

## Relationship to prior work

Thaddeus (1994, arXiv:alg-geom/9405004) proves general geometric-invariant-theory wall-crossing and flip results. Hausel--Sturmfels (2002, arXiv:math/0203096) develop toric quotient methods. Lam--Postnikov (2005, arXiv:math/0501246) study hypersimplices, their triangulations, and related multi-hypersimplices. Craw--Maclagan (2005, arXiv:math/0510128) establish general normal-fan/fiber-fan relationships for toric quotients. These general theories support the setting but are not being claimed as new.

An earlier classification for **unit-cap hypersimplices** gives isolated rank-one local models and projective small resolutions. It is a genuine specialization: when every \(a_i=1\), all box-corner subsets have the same cardinality, so the singular basket has a single matrix size, rather than the simultaneous balanced and unbalanced types possible for nonuniform caps. The present claim is the exact arbitrary-integer-cap basket and its varying local Gorenstein behavior, not the already known unit-cap result or the general facts about rank-one matrix cones.

## Limitations

The theorem does not assert that these local models or their invariant computations were previously unknown individually. Its novelty claim is limited to the uniform classification and subset-sum count for arbitrary nonuniform integral box sections, including coexistence of inequivalent local canonical classes in one projective variety. The general GIT literature may imply portions abstractly, and an equivalent weighted-slice classification under other names may exist. The literature search is evidence of no identified direct coverage, not proof of absolute priority. Independent mathematical review remains necessary before treating the theorem as a certified novel result.
