# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof exhausts every vertex by its number of free box coordinates, identifies the exact saturated tangent semigroup at each box corner, presents it by the \(2\times2\)-minor ideal, and proves every proper normal cone unimodular by a signed-coordinate basis extension. The projective variety therefore has no positive-dimensional singular orbit. The Segre-degree and toric divisor-sequence computations establish the numerical and canonical invariants. The standard-library checker independently enumerates six weighted examples, tests exact minors and finite semigroup degrees, and reports `VERIFY_OK`. Finite enumeration is supplementary, not the infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS with residual risk. An earlier unit-cap hypersimplex result covers the equal-cap specialization, including rank-one local singularities, and is not reasserted as new. Thaddeus, Hausel--Sturmfels, Craw--Maclagan, and Lam--Postnikov give GIT, fan, and triangulation frameworks; the inspected portions do not state the exact arbitrary-cap subset-sum basket and the mixed Gorenstein/non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein singularity example. Searches for cap-sum normal-fan determinantal singularities and equivalent toric GIT weighted-slice statements did not identify a covering theorem. An equivalent unindexed classification remains possible; generic GIT methods might shorten the argument.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Integer box sections naturally represent level-varying toric quotients with nonuniform polarizations. A complete exact stratification by the arithmetic subset sums of cap weights determines the number, matrix sizes, multiplicities, class groups and local canonical status of every singular point. Simultaneous Gorenstein and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein isolated singularities in the explicit six-coordinate example cannot occur in a unit-cap hypersimplex of the same dimension and constitute useful geometric information, not an arbitrary numeric slice.

## Scope and risks

The classification concerns only the normal-fan projective toric variety and reduced singular support; it does not classify resolutions, cohomology or singular schemes. The closest earlier unit-cap theorem and general GIT frameworks are acknowledged, and absolute originality requires further specialist literature review.
