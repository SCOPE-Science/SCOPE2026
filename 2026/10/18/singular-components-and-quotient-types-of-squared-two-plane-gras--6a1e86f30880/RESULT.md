# Singular components and quotient types of squared two-plane Grassmannians

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb C\), and let \(n\ge5\). Let \(X_n\) be the projective closure of the squared-Plücker image of \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)\):
\[
\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)\longrightarrow \mathbb P^{\binom n2-1},\qquad (p_{ij})\longmapsto(q_{ij}=p_{ij}^{2}).
\]
For \(i\in[n]\), write \(D_i\subset X_n\) for the zero-row locus \(q_{ij}=0\) for every \(j\ne i\). For each unordered nontrivial partition \([n]=S\sqcup T\), let \(E_{S|T}\subset X_n\) consist of arrays whose within-block coordinates vanish and whose \(|S|\times|T|\) cross-block coordinate matrix has rank at most one. The latter is a Segre variety \(\mathbb P^{|S|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|T|-1}\).

Then the complete reduced singular locus is
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_n)_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}D_i\ \cup\! \bigcup_{\{S,T\}:S\sqcup T=[n],\ S,T\ne\varnothing} E_{S|T}.
\]
These are precisely \(n+2^{n-1}-1\) **distinct irreducible components**. The \(n\) zero-row components have dimension \(2n-6\) and codimension \(2\) in \(X_n\). The \(2^{n-1}-1\) Segre components have dimension \(n-2\) and codimension \(n-2\). At their respective generic points the formal transverse quotient singularities are \(\mathbb A^2/\{\pm1\}\) (the \(A_1\) surface cone) and \(\mathbb A^{n-2}/\{\pm1\}\) (the cone over the quadratic Veronese variety of \(\mathbb P^{n-3}\)). Moreover, \(X_n\) is normal and Cohen–Macaulay, has canonical but nonterminal singularities, and is Gorenstein **exactly when \(n\) is even**. In particular \(X_5\) has \(20\) irreducible singular components, five of dimension \(4\) and fifteen of dimension \(3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field is \(\mathbb C\) (the proof works over any algebraically closed characteristic-zero field). This is a classification of **reduced** singular supports and **generic** transverse completed local rings, not a scheme-theoretic primary decomposition of the Jacobian ideal or a classification of all component intersections. The first public mathematical model underlying the question is the rank-two projection determinantal probability law \(\det(K_I)=\det(B_I)^2\), described for general rank in Kulesza–Taskar's 2012 treatment of elementary projection determinantal processes. Its complex projective closure is the squared Grassmannian. Later algebraic-geometric presentations of \(X_n\) are cited explicitly rather than attributed to the earlier probability literature.

## Proof
**1. The finite sign quotient and generic fibers.** Let \(G=(\mu_2)^n/\mu_2\) act on \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)\) by changing signs of the \(n\) columns of a representing \(2\times n\) matrix; the diagonal sign acts trivially. Squaring Plücker coordinates is \(G\)-invariant and finite because ambient coordinatewise squaring on projective space is finite. On the open locus where every Plücker coordinate is nonzero, any two points in the same square fiber have Plücker coordinates related by \(p'_{ij}=\epsilon_{ij}p_{ij}\), after choosing projective representatives, with \(\epsilon_{ij}\in\{\pm1\}\). For every four indices, the two independent generic cross-products in the Plücker equation force
\[
\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{kl}=\epsilon_{ik}\epsilon_{jl}=\epsilon_{il}\epsilon_{jk}.
\]
Fixing index \(1\) and comparing all quadruples \((1,i,j,k)\) shows that \(\epsilon_{ij}=c\alpha_i\alpha_j\) for one global sign \(c\) and vertex signs \(\alpha_i\); the common factor \(c\) is projectively irrelevant. Thus generic fibers are exactly \(G\)-orbits (with \(|G|=2^{n-1}\)), and \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)/G\to X_n\) is finite and birational.

**2. Normality (and the quotient identification).** Devriendt–Friedman–Reinke–Sturmfels prove that the homogeneous prime ideal of \(X_n\) is generated by the \(4\times4\) minors of the symmetric hollow matrix \(Q=(q_{ij})\) with \(q_{ii}=0\). Let \(Y\) be a \(3\times n\) matrix with columns \(y_i\) and form the normal affine variety
\[
Z=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\{y_i\in\mathbb A^3: y_i\mathbin{\cdot}y_i=0\}.
\]
Each three-variable nondegenerate quadric cone is normal (singular only at its vertex, codimension two), and their product is normal. By the first and second orthogonal invariant theorems and exactness of invariants for reductive \(O_3\), the affine coordinate ring of the hollow symmetric rank-at-most-three matrices is \(\mathbb C[Z]^{O_3}\). The invariant subring of an integrally closed domain is integrally closed; hence the homogeneous cone and \(X_n\) are normal. Since the finite quotient of the smooth normal Grassmannian is also normal, the finite birational map from step 1 is an isomorphism:
\[
X_n\ \cong\ \operatorname{Gr}(2,n)/G.
\]
In characteristic zero, invariant rings of finite groups acting on regular local rings are Cohen–Macaulay; this applies to the completed local quotient charts.

**3. All fixed loci.** A nonidentity element of \(G\) corresponds to an unordered nontrivial decomposition \(\mathbb C^n=V_S\oplus V_T\) into its sign eigenspaces. Its fixed two-planes are precisely: a two-plane within \(V_S\), a two-plane within \(V_T\), or a direct sum of one line from each eigenspace. The first two types have all Plücker coordinates involving the complementary block zero, hence map into some \(D_i\). The mixed type is \(\mathbb P(V_S)\times\mathbb P(V_T)\), and after squaring its Plücker coordinates, its image is the Segre \(E_{S|T}\). Conversely, the generic point of \(D_i\) is fixed by the involution flipping column \(i\), and every point of \(E_{S|T}\) comes from a fixed mixed two-plane.

At a fixed two-plane \(W\), use \(T_W\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)=\operatorname{Hom}(W,\mathbb C^n/W)\). If \(W\subset V_S\), the nontrivial sign has a negative tangent eigenspace of dimension \(2|T|\); if \(W\subset V_T\), it has dimension \(2|S|\). In the mixed case \(W=L_S\oplus L_T\), the negative tangent eigenspace has dimension \((|S|-1)+(|T|-1)=n-2\). For \(n\ge5\) all these dimensions are at least two. Thus no nontrivial group element fixing a point acts as a pseudoreflection on its tangent space. Analytic/formal linearization of finite group actions at fixed points and the Chevalley–Shephard–Todd criterion imply that a quotient point is singular exactly when its stabilizer is nontrivial. Therefore the union of fixed-locus images from this step is the **whole** singular support, without reliance on finite sampling.

**4. Distinct components and generic local models.** A generic two-plane in the coordinate subspace \(\mathbb C^{n-1}\) avoiding column \(i\) has all other Plücker coordinates nonzero; it belongs only to \(D_i\), not another \(D_j\) or a mixed Segre. The generic mixed plane for partition \(S|T\) has all cross-block Plücker coordinates nonzero, distinguishing its bipartition from all others and excluding every zero-row locus. Each \(D_i\cong X_{n-1}\) is irreducible; each Segre \(E_{S|T}\) is irreducible. The fixed loci have dimensions \(2(n-3)\) and \((|S|-1)+(|T|-1)=n-2\). Since \(\dim X_n=2n-4\), the stated codimensions and component count follow.

The stabilizer at the generic point of each \(D_i\) and \(E_{S|T}\) is exactly the indicated order-two group; all other signs fail to fix that generic point. Formal linearization splits the tangent representation into fixed smooth directions and a negative transverse vector space of dimension \(2\) or \(n-2\), respectively. The completed local ring is therefore a power-series ring in the smooth parameters over \(\mathbb C[[z_1,\dots,z_c]]^{\pm1}\), giving the claimed local models. For \(c=2\), its invariant ring is \(\mathbb C[[u,v,w]]/(uw-v^2)\).

**5. Canonical singularities and Gorenstein parity.** For any point and any nonidentity element of its stabilizer, the number \(c\) of negative tangent eigenvalues is one of \(2|S|\), \(2|T|\), or \(n-2\), and in all cases \(c\ge2\). The age is \(c/2\ge1\). The Reid–Tai quotient criterion shows all quotient singularities are canonical. At a generic zero-row stratum age is exactly one, hence \(X_n\) is not terminal. With no pseudoreflections, Watanabe's determinant criterion says a local finite quotient is Gorenstein exactly when its stabilizer acts through \(\operatorname{SL}\) on the tangent space. The two unmixed dimensions are always even, while the mixed dimension \(n-2\) is even exactly for even \(n\). If \(n\) is even, every stabilizer element has determinant \(+1\); if \(n\) is odd, the generic point of any \(E_{S|T}\) has determinant \(-1\). This proves the global Gorenstein criterion.

## Relationship to prior work
Kulesza–Taskar (arXiv:1207.6083v1, 2012) introduce the elementary projection DPP whose rank-two sample probabilities are precisely squared maximal minors of an orthonormal two-row matrix; this provides the earliest inspected direct description of the probabilistic model. Bocci–Carlini–Kileel (arXiv:1504.04301v1, 2015) study coordinatewise Hadamard products of linear spaces, a related but **not identical** image construction. Al Ahmadieh–Vinzant (arXiv:2105.13444v1, 2021), Section 6.2, study polynomial relations among squared Plücker coordinates. Devriendt–Friedman–Reinke–Sturmfels (arXiv:2401.03684v1, 2024), Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.4, establish the hollow symmetric determinantal ideal and degree formula used here; these are prior results. Dey–Görlach–Kaihnsa discuss coordinatewise powers of varieties, especially lines and planes. The previously published explicit \(n=4\) result "Eleven singular surfaces on the smallest squared Grassmannian" classifies eight planes, three quadrics, and their transverse \(A_1\) types, including a sign-quotient identification. Our theorem explicitly **starts at \(n\ge5\)**; it supplies the distinct general-rank components, the two different transverse dimensions, and the Gorenstein parity not implied by the rank-four special case. The known general presentation alone does not supply an all-rank singular-component decomposition without the additional invariant-normality and fixed-locus arguments given here.

## Limitations
The asserted scheme is the reduced singular locus only; intersections among the listed components may be more singular, and the Jacobian scheme need not be reduced. The theorem is confined to rank-two Grassmannians, characteristic zero, and \(n\ge5\). Over positive characteristic dividing \(|G|\) the linearly reductive quotient argument fails. The direct geometric literature concentrates on the determinantal equations and degree, but an equivalent all-rank description using moduli of projection DPPs, principal-minor geometry, or finite-group quotients may remain uncatalogued.
