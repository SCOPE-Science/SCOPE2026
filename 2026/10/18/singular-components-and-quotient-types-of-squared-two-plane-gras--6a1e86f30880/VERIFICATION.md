# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Reconstructed the quotient without relying on a set-theoretic identification of determinantal schemes. On the chart p_ab=1 take pivot columns e_1,e_2 and remaining columns (x_i,y_i). Signs act as (s_a s_i x_i,s_b s_i y_i), equivalently independent simultaneous flips of each pair plus one common flip of all y coordinates. Invariants under the pair flips are generated by x_i²,y_i²,x_i y_i. The remaining invariants add (x_i y_i)(x_j y_j), which is one-half of x_i²y_j²+x_j²y_i²−(x_i y_j−x_j y_i)². Hence the projective square-image chart is exactly the invariant quotient chart, including at points with zero coordinates. Finite invariants give normality and the characteristic-zero finite quotient theorem gives Cohen–Macaulayness. Every stabilizing sign splits the fixed two-plane into two lines or has it entirely in one sign eigenspace. The images are the mixed Segre and coordinate zero-row loci, respectively, and the negative tangent dimensions from Hom(W,C^n/W) are n−2 for mixed and twice the opposite sign-block size otherwise. No stabilizer has a pseudoreflection, so the formal finite quotient is smooth exactly for trivial stabilizer. The generic mixed support has all cross edges and determines its unordered bipartition; a generic zero-row point has every other edge and cannot be mixed since the other indices contain a triangle. These generic points distinguish the components and have just the displayed order-two stabilizer. The transverse negative coordinates in a mixed chart can be taken as minor-to-major column-coordinate ratios with pivots on opposite sides; they all transform by the common relative sign, independently of the sign action on the major coordinates. At the generic zero-row locus take both pivots away from the missing column. Divide its y coordinate by a nonzero y_i/x_i from a remaining column to make both missing-column coordinates transform by the same sign, independent of the base action. Thus the transverse quotient types also split over the generic residue fields. All ages are at least one; the zero-row generic age equals one, so canonical but not terminal. Small-group Gorensteinness is the determinant-one criterion: only the mixed negative dimension can be odd, exactly when n is odd. The dimensions, n+2^(n−1)−1 count, and n=5 example follow.
