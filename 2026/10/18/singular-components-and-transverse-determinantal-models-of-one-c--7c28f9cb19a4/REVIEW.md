# Review of singular-component theorem for a one-circuit-hyperplane matroid

## Correctness — PASS
The exact statement covers integers \(r,s\ge3\) and the normal fan of the rank-\(r\) base polytope missing precisely one basis. The facet model is the hypersimplex with one cut, and its added facet is the product \(\Delta_{r-1}\times\Delta_{s-1}\). The proof classifies smoothness of all face normal cones and checks lattice saturation rather than using mere rational independence. Nonsmooth orbits within the new facet are precisely those indexed by faces having a singleton factor; off that facet, all positive-dimensional faces are smooth and every old vertex is nonsimplicial. Dual semigroup calculations prove the three asserted determinantal models. An exhaustive independent Smith-normal-form checker matches every singular face for five parameter pairs up to \(n=8\). The finite checks do not stand in for the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest prior descriptions include the same single-missing-basis polytope as an example in Ferroni--Schröter's face-enumeration paper, as well as Ardila--Benedetti--Doker's volume theory, Chatelain--Ramírez Alfonsín's decomposition, Bonin's sparse-paving circuits, and Lasoń--Michałek's global algebraic properties. Those treatments provide foundational polyhedral data or face counts, but the inspected portions do not assert the complete reduced singular support as an arrangement of projective components and isolated determinantal points. A previously recorded rank-two family has a related singularity classification; it concerns the rank boundary excluded here, and does not entail the higher-rank two-family intersection arrangement. Other checked matroid-toric examples concern different matroids. Publication under an unsearched alias remains possible, so this is not an assertion of proved historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The one-circuit-hyperplane matroid is a canonical minimal perturbation of a uniform matroid, so understanding exactly how its toric singularities depart from the hypersimplex is a natural question. The theorem gives a complete reduced singularity classification, component dimensions and numbers, a complete bipartite incidence pattern, and three explicit generic/isolated determinantal models for every rank and corank at least three. The distinction between the positive-dimensional Segre strata and isolated larger determinantal cones is structural information not encoded by the face vector. The nonreduced singular scheme and intersection-germ types remain outside scope.
