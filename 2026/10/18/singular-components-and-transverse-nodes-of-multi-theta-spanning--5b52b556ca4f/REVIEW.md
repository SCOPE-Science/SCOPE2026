# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof first identifies the exact spanning-tree polynomial, then establishes geometric irreducibility by a primitive degree-one variable argument. Its exhaustive case analysis by zero sums \(a_i+b_i\) proves the reduced set equality for all points; it is not a statistical assertion. The candidate components are separately shown to be irreducible, distinct, of the stated dimension and degree. Nondegenerate generic three-variable quadratic slices and Serre's criterion establish the claimed analytic type and normality. The standalone checker reproduces 3906, 3280, and 1023 exhaustive finite-field projective comparisons and verifies a representative Hessian computation. Positive-characteristic checks corroborate but do not prove the characteristic-zero result.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Patterson's rank-locus characterization (arXiv:1004.5166, Theorem 4.13) covers the general determinantal description but does not supply the present component enumeration. Denham–Schulze–Walther (DOI:10.1007/s11005-020-01352-3) give the same \(m=3\) polynomial and its four generic components in Example 5.1, as well as codimension-three general results. The accepted claim restricts novelty to \(m\ge4\), exact component degrees and their total, and the uniform generic transverse double-point description. Neither a title mismatch nor a failed search alone is treated as proof of uniqueness. Potential implicit or unindexed equivalent coverage remains a risk.

## Value — PASS
The complete component and degree formulas describe a natural infinite family of graph hypersurfaces, whose reducible singular support is finer than a rank-locus or codimension statement. The decomposition distinguishes two geometric component types and supplies a uniform local singularity profile, useful in understanding their normality and singular-cycle classes.

## Limitations
The work concerns reduced singular support and generic transverse singularities. It does not compute the full Jacobian scheme or nongeneric local completions. Independent validation is not claimed.
