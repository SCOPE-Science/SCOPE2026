# Singular strata and anticanonical degrees of complete type-A root toric Fano varieties

## Finding

Let \(n\geq 4\), and let \(X_n\) be the projective toric variety of the fan consisting of cones over the proper faces of the full type-A root polytope
\[
 P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i-e_j:1\leq i\ne j\leq n\}\subset N_{\mathbb R},\qquad N=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i z_i=0\}.
\]
Over any algebraically closed field, \(X_n\) is a terminal Gorenstein Fano variety of dimension \(n-1\). Its entire reduced singular support, equivalently its non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus, has exactly \(6\binom n4\) irreducible components, all of dimension \(n-4\). They are indexed by **ordered** pairs \((A,B)\) of disjoint two-element subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and each is abstractly isomorphic to \((\mathbb P^1)^{n-4}\). For \(n=4\), this product is understood to be a point.

Each component has an ordinary threefold node \(uv-st=0\) as the transverse completed singularity at its generic point. The anticanonical bundle restricts to \(\mathcal O(1,\ldots,1)\) on each component. Consequently, every component has anticanonical degree \((n-4)!\), and the reduced singular locus has total anticanonical degree \(n!/4\). The first examples comprise six points for \(n=4\), thirty anticanonical lines for \(n=5\), and ninety Segre quadrics of combined anticanonical degree \(180\) for \(n=6\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field \(k\) is algebraically closed of arbitrary characteristic. \(N\) is the root lattice, not \(\mathbb Z^n\), and \(X_n=X(\Sigma_n)\) uses the **face fan** \(\Sigma_n\) of \(P_n\), not the normal fan. The fan includes the zero cone. For nonempty disjoint subsets \(A,B\) of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), write
\[
 \sigma_{A,B}=\mathbb R_{\geq0}\langle e_a-e_b:a\in A,\ b\in B\rangle,
 \qquad Z_{A,B}=V(\sigma_{A,B}).
\]
The component indexing requires \(|A|=|B|=2\). The asserted singular scheme is explicitly the **reduced singular support**; no assertion is made about the nilpotent structure of the Jacobian singular subscheme or about singularities at intersections of these components. Anticanonical degree means top intersection with the specified ample Cartier anticanonical polarization.

## Proof

**Face and cone classification.** For any nonconstant linear form \(h\) on \(N_{\mathbb R}\), its maximum on the roots \(e_i-e_j\) is attained exactly when \(i\) belongs to the set \(A\) of maximal coordinates of \(h\) and \(j\) belongs to the disjoint set \(B\) of minimal coordinates. Conversely, any specified nonempty disjoint \(A,B\) is realized by choosing maximal, intermediate and minimal coordinate values. Therefore all proper nonempty faces are exactly
\[
 F_{A,B}=\operatorname{conv}\{e_a-e_b:a\in A,b\in B\}.
\]
The columns of the oriented incidence matrix of the complete bipartite graph on \(A\sqcup B\) span the \((|A|+|B|-1)\)-dimensional root sublattice supported there. Thus \(\dim\sigma_{A,B}=|A|+|B|-1\), while the cone has exactly \(|A||B|\) extremal rays. It is simplicial precisely when \(\min(|A|,|B|)=1\). In this case its primitive rays are the edge vectors of a star tree; completing that star by edges to the remaining indices extends them to a basis of \(N\). Hence every simplicial cone is unimodular, and no nonsimplicial cone is smooth. The local toric criterion likewise says that a cone is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial precisely when it is simplicial.

**Minimal singular cones and components.** A nonsimplicial cone has \(|A|,|B|\geq2\); consequently it contains \(\sigma_{A_0,B_0}\) for some two-element subsets \(A_0\subseteq A\), \(B_0\subseteq B\). Conversely such \(2\)-by-\(2\) cones have four rays in dimension three and are minimally nonsmooth. Since toric orbit closure reverses inclusion of cones, the entire singular locus is the union of their orbit closures. No two distinct such cones contain one another, so these are exactly the irreducible components. Choosing four distinct indices and then assigning two to the first set and two to the second gives \(\binom n4\binom42=6\binom n4\) ordered pairs. Each component has dimension \((n-1)-3=n-4\).

**The global geometry of a component.** Fix \(A=\{a,a'\}\), \(B=\{b,b'\}\), and let \(C\) be the remaining \(n-4\) indices. The saturated sublattice \(N\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(\sigma_{A,B})\) is the full rank-three root lattice on the four selected indices. In the quotient, the classes \(w_c=[e_c-e_b]\), \(c\in C\), give a lattice basis. Every face containing \(\sigma_{A,B}\) is obtained by independently placing some remaining indices in the enlarged top set, some in the enlarged bottom set, and leaving the others unused. Its image in the quotient is generated by \(w_c\) for indices placed on top and by \(-w_c\) for indices placed on bottom. Therefore the star fan is exactly the product of \(n-4\) complete one-dimensional fans, proving
\[
 Z_{A,B}\cong (\mathbb P^1)^{n-4}.
\]

**Transverse singularity.** Choose the primitive rays \(e_a-e_b\), \(e_{a'}-e_b\), \(e_a-e_{b'}\), and \(e_{a'}-e_{b'}\). Relative to the first three, which form a saturated rank-three basis, these rays are \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), and \((-1,1,1)\). The dual cone is cut out by \(x,y,z\geq0\) and \(-x+y+z\geq0\). Its lattice semigroup is generated by \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), \((1,1,0)\), and \((1,0,1)\), with the single primitive relation \((0,1,0)+(1,0,1)=(0,0,1)+(1,1,0)\). This gives the transverse affine toric ring \(k[u,v,s,t]/(uv-st)\). Over the function field \(K=k(O(\sigma_{A,B}))\) of the corresponding dense torus orbit, completion transverse to that orbit yields \(K[[u,v,s,t]]/(uv-st)\). The calculation is integral and characteristic-free.

**Fano properties and polarization.** The convex hull of the roots is precisely the section \(\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\sum z_i=0,\ \sum|z_i|\leq2\}\). Its only lattice points are the origin and the roots, giving terminality. Its polar lattice polytope, in \(M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\), is
\[
 P_n^\vee=\{[h]:\max_i h_i-\min_i h_i\leq1\}.
\]
This has integral vertices represented by zero-one vectors, so \(P_n\) is reflexive. The associated fan variety is therefore Gorenstein Fano, with \(-K\) ample Cartier. The face of the polar polytope corresponding to \(\sigma_{A,B}\) imposes \(h_a-h_b=-1\) for \(a\in A,b\in B\). After translation it is the unit cube on the coordinates in \(C\). Under the quotient lattice of the star fan this is exactly the polytope of \(\mathcal O(1,\ldots,1)\) on \((\mathbb P^1)^{n-4}\). Its normalized volume is \((n-4)!\), and adding this number over all \(6\binom n4\) irreducible reduced components gives \(n!/4\).

## Verification

The accompanying dependency-free `check.py` enumerates every pair \((A,B)\) for \(4\leq n\leq7\); verifies the rank and simplicial criteria; checks that all nonsimplicial cones contain a minimal square; counts these squares and their containing maximal cones; checks that their star fans have \(2^{n-4}\) maximal orthants; and verifies the four-ray integral relation. It returned `VERIFY_OK`, with square counts \(6,30,90,210\) and total anticanonical degrees \(6,30,180,1260\). These finite checks are corroboration, not a substitute for the all-\(n\) proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Higashitani's work [1] develops the directed-graph terminal Gorenstein Fano construction and establishes a smoothness criterion; the complete bidirected graph is an instance. Kara--Portakal--Tsuchiya [2] analyze rigidity, codimension-two smoothness, and codimension-three \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality of these varieties, including four-cycle obstructions. These results establish key background and nonsmoothness; the statement here refines that information by giving **every** irreducible singular component for every complete graph, its product geometry, its transverse local algebra, and its exact anticanonical degree. The existence of the six square facets in the four-vertex root polytope is also classical and is not claimed as new. Rietsch--Williams [3] discuss the broader root-polytope toric setting; [4] discusses face combinatorics in root lattices.

## Limitations

Originality remains subject to unidentified equivalent results under alternative terminology (type-A root toric Fano varieties, symmetric edge polytopes of complete graphs, directed graph Fano varieties). The statement is set-theoretic for the singular scheme and does not classify the completed singularities along component intersections. Generic node descriptions are transverse completions over orbit function fields, not assertions of globally constant analytic trivializations. Only the specified anticanonical polarization is used in degree computations.
