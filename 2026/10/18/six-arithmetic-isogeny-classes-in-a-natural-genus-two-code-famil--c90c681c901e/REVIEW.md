# Review of the six-class genus-two arithmetic partition

## Correctness

PASS. Explicit involutions and their independent invariant differential lines produce an isogeny from two copies of the same elliptic quotient to the genus-two Jacobian over the base field. The quotient quartic is smooth throughout the specified set. Exhaustive nineteen-term character sums give six elliptic traces; an independently implemented quadratic-extension curve-point enumeration agrees in all fifteen cases. The finite check certifies exact arithmetic of the specified parameters, while the argument for the full zeta numerator uses the genuine isogeny.

## Originality

PASS with specified database residual risk. The most relevant new primary source, Beshaj--Shaska Theorem 10.1, reports the fifteen-parameter hull-profile partition and shows that the parameters six and eight give nonisomorphic curves with the same hull profile. It does not give the complete parameter-to-Jacobian-isogeny classification. Cardona--Quer's earlier elliptic-subcover and dihedral-curve theory covers the underlying generic splitting idea and is expressly credited; it does not state the six particular finite-field trace classes. A public abelian-variety isogeny database lists isogeny classes over the base field but the inspected material does not give the mapping for these fifteen equations. Prior record searches revealed no direct duplicate. A broader implicit table or alternate models in the database remain a residual risk, not a claim of proven uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The cited code-theoretic study singles out a complete small and natural locus where the hull-profile map fails to determine geometric isomorphism. The exact arithmetic isogeny partition gives an independent full invariant comparison on that entire locus, showing a sharp further collapse from seven hull-profile values to six isogeny classes; indeed three opposite-coefficient pairs occupy a single arithmetic class. Knowing these six classes determines the zeta functions and point counts over all finite extensions for every admitted curve, not merely one sample's characteristic polynomial. The result is a complete natural finite classification and does not assert a new generic decomposition principle.
