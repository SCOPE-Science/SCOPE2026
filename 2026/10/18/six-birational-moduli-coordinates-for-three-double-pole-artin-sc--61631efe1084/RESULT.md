# Six birational moduli coordinates for three-double-pole Artin–Schreier curves

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\). Consider smooth projective curves represented by
\[
 y^3-y=f(x),\qquad
 f(x)=\sum_{p\in\{\infty,0,1\}}\bigl(A_pq_p(x)^2+B_pq_p(x)\bigr),
 \qquad A_\infty A_0 A_1\ne0,
\]
where equality of rational functions is understood modulo additive constants, and
\[
 q_\infty=x-\tfrac12,\qquad
 q_0=\tfrac12-\tfrac1x,\qquad
 q_1=-\tfrac12-\tfrac1{x-1}.
\]
These curves have genus \(7\), geometric \(3\)-rank \(4\), and exactly three poles of order two in the Artin–Schreier equation. Let \(\mathcal M\) be the coarse moduli locus of their underlying unmarked curves, with no chosen deck action or ordering of the three branch points.

Write the ordered coefficient triples as \(A=(A_\infty,A_0,A_1)\) and \(B=(B_\infty,B_0,B_1)\). Define
\[
 e_1=\sum_i A_i,\qquad e_2=\sum_{i<j}A_iA_j,\qquad e_3=\prod_i A_i,
\]
and, for \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), let \(T_j\) be the determinant of the matrix obtained by replacing column \(j\) of
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}1&A_\infty&A_\infty^2\\1&A_0&A_0^2\\1&A_1&A_1^2\end{pmatrix}
\]
with \((B_\infty,B_0,B_1)^\mathsf T\). Then the six **polynomial** functions
\[
 \boxed{e_2,\quad T_0,\quad T_2,\quad e_1^2,\quad e_1e_3,\quad e_1T_1}
\]
are invariant under every isomorphism of these unmarked curves. They generate the full **field of rational invariants**, are algebraically independent, and separate generic isomorphism classes. Consequently
\[
 \boxed{k(\mathcal M)\simeq k(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4,z_5,z_6),}
\]
so this natural six-dimensional component of the Artin–Schreier curve moduli locus is rational. This asserts a birational description of the moduli **field**, not a presentation of its full graded polynomial invariant ring or its boundary quotient.

## Assumptions and scope

All curves, isomorphisms, and moduli statements are geometric over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\). The equation denotes the unique smooth projective model of its separable function field. Each \(A_p\) is nonzero, and the denominators have exactly the three marked poles. The generic reconstruction is valid on the dense open set
\[
 U:\quad A_\infty A_0A_1\det(V)e_1\ne0.
\]
On \(U\), unordered branch points and the unchosen Artin–Schreier generator are both forgotten. No claim of generation of the entire polynomial invariant ring, global coarse-moduli isomorphism, or rationality of other ramification strata is made.

The pole-order genus formula gives
\[
 2g-2=-6+3(3-1)(2+1)=12,
\]
hence \(g=7\). The Deuring–Shafarevich formula gives geometric \(3\)-rank \((3-1)(3-1)=4\). The primary mathematical classification of this finding is MSC2020 \(14H10\), because its claim concerns the birational type and rational coordinates of a moduli family of algebraic curves.

## Proof

**1. Uniqueness of the degree-three quotient.** The Artin–Schreier projection \(C\to\mathbb P^1\) is separable of degree three. Suppose there were two inequivalent degree-three rational maps \(C\to\mathbb P^1\). Their degree-three rational function subfields are distinct and generate \(k(C)\): the intermediate degree over either subfield divides the prime number three. Thus the product map is birational to a curve of bidegree \((3,3)\) in \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), whose arithmetic genus is \((3-1)(3-1)=4\). This contradicts \(g(C)=7\). Therefore the rational subfield of degree three is unique, up to an automorphism of \(\mathbb P^1\). In particular, any isomorphism of two curves in the family preserves their Artin–Schreier quotient up to a Möbius transformation.

**2. Exact residual isomorphism group.** After the three poles are normalized to \(\{\infty,0,1\}\), the remaining Möbius transformations form \(\mathfrak S_3\), generated by \(x\mapsto1-x\) and \(x\mapsto1/x\). An isomorphism of separable cubic Artin–Schreier extensions has the form \(y\mapsto\lambda y+h(x)\), where \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_3^\times=\{\pm1\}\). If \(h(x)\) had a pole, then \(h^3-h\) would have a pole of order divisible by three, at least three, impossible for the difference of two normalized right-hand sides with poles of order at most two. Thus \(h\) is constant. Since \(k\) is algebraically closed, its Artin–Schreier constant can be absorbed into the equation. Hence the complete parameter equivalence is precisely the action of \(H=\mathfrak S_3\times C_2\), with the last factor simultaneously negating all six coefficients.

The functions \(q_p\) have a single simple pole at their indicated point. Direct substitution into both generating transpositions gives
\[
 q_p(gx)=-q_{g^{-1}p}(x)
\]
for every generating transposition \(g\); by composition the sign is \(\operatorname{sgn}(g)\) for every permutation. Consequently \(\mathfrak S_3\) simply permutes the three leading coefficients \(A_p\), whereas it acts on the three \(B_p\) by the sign-twisted permutation representation. The second factor \(C_2\) negates both triples.

**3. Exact invariant field under \(\mathfrak S_3\).** On \(\det V\ne0\), interpolate the three values \(B_i\) as
\[
 B_i=c_0+c_1A_i+c_2A_i^2\qquad(i\in\{\infty,0,1\}).
\]
Cramer's rule gives \(T_j=(\det V)c_j\). Permuting the three rows changes \(\det V\) by the permutation sign, and the sign twist of the \(B_i\) changes all \(c_j\) by the same sign. Therefore \(T_0,T_1,T_2\) are invariant. The fundamental theorem of symmetric rational functions, applied to the three distinct \(A_i\), yields
\[
 k(A_\infty,A_0,A_1,B_\infty,B_0,B_1)^{\mathfrak S_3}
 =k(e_1,e_2,e_3,T_0,T_1,T_2).
\]
Indeed, over the right-hand field the roots of the cubic with elementary coefficients \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) recover the unordered \(A_i\); after choosing their ordering, Cramer's rule reconstructs all \(B_i\). This shows equality of fields rather than merely inclusion.

**4. Invariants of the remaining involution.** Simultaneous sign change of both coefficient triples acts as
\[
 (e_1,e_2,e_3,T_0,T_1,T_2)
 \longmapsto(-e_1,e_2,-e_3,T_0,-T_1,T_2).
\]
On \(e_1\ne0\), its fixed field is
\[
 k\bigl(e_2,T_0,T_2,e_1^2,e_1e_3,e_1T_1\bigr).
\]
To see the reverse containment, adjoin a square root of \(e_1^2\), then recover \(e_3=(e_1e_3)/e_1\) and \(T_1=(e_1T_1)/e_1\). The two possible choices of square root are exactly the involution orbit. This proves the claimed invariant-field presentation.

**5. Algebraic independence and moduli interpretation.** The ordered six coefficient coordinates are algebraically independent. Their finite-group invariant field has transcendence degree six; since it equals the field generated by the six displayed invariants, these are algebraically independent. More concretely, the determinant of their six-by-six Jacobian with respect to the ordered \((A,B)\) coordinates is, up to the sign determined by order,
\[
 2\,(A_\infty-A_0)^3(A_\infty-A_1)^3(A_0-A_1)^3e_1^3,
\]
nonzero on \(U\) in characteristic three. The explicit cubic-root and interpolation reconstruction in Steps 3–4 proves generic orbit separation and the birational coordinate map. Step 1 ensures that this is the function field of the **unmarked curve** moduli locus, not merely of a parameter space of marked covers.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify.py` checks the exact identities for the two Möbius generators, all six invariant polynomials against all twelve elements of \(\mathfrak S_3\times C_2\), the three Cramer identities, and the nonvanishing of the symbolic Jacobian determinant modulo three. Running `python3 verify.py` from the package directory prints `VERIFY_OK`. All these are exact polynomial calculations; the genus, uniqueness, field equality and geometric moduli identification are established in the proof, not inferred from computation.

## Relationship to prior work

Duque-Rosero, Lorenzo García, Malmskog and Scheidler (2026, arXiv:2602.22435v1), Sections 2 and 6.3, provide the three-pole invariant-theory framework and explicitly describe the characteristic-three equal-double-pole case as a modular invariant-ring calculation that their Magma computation could not complete. The present theorem does **not** claim that ring calculation is complete: it instead supplies a constructive invariant **function-field** presentation and the rationality of the corresponding unmarked curve-moduli locus.

Duque-Rosero et al. (2024, arXiv:2401.08843) classify smaller genera and furnish Artin–Schreier standard forms. Pries–Zhu (2012, arXiv:math/0609657) give the relevant genus, \(p\)-rank, strata and dimension framework, not the displayed six-coordinate quotient. Reimers–Sezer (2025, arXiv:2505.20895) give generic separation methods for modular cyclic-group representations; this is relevant general machinery but does not specify these geometric coordinates or the unmarked curve quotient. Standard symmetric-function and interpolation identities explain part of the construction; novelty is asserted only for the explicit curve-moduli realization and its complete generic reconstruction.

## Limitations

The formulas describe a dense open subset of this one six-dimensional genus-seven stratum. They do not present the affine invariant ring, its relations, exceptional stabilizer strata, a compactification, or the boundary where \(e_1\det V=0\). Other Artin–Schreier ramification profiles are not classified here. While targeted literature and mathematical-record comparison found no statement of these particular six birational coordinates for unmarked curves, unindexed work or a stronger general rationality theorem may subsume the existence-of-rationality corollary. The explicit coordinates remain a distinct construction; historical priority has not been independently certified.
