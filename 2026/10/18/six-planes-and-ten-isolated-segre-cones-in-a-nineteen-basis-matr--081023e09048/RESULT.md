# Six planes and ten isolated Segre cones in a nineteen-basis matroid toric fivefold

## Finding

Let \(E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), \(H=\{0,1,2\}\), \(C=E\setminus H\), and let \(M\) have as bases the nineteen three-subsets of \(E\) other than \(H\). Let \(P\) be its lattice base polytope. For the normal projective toric variety \(X_P\), over any algebraically closed field, the reduced singular locus has exactly sixteen irreducible components: six copies of \(\mathbb P^2\) and ten isolated fixed points. The planes form two triples: planes in the same triple are disjoint; each cross-triple pair meets in a unique fixed point, with nine distinct cross-triple intersections. At the generic point of each plane the transverse toric germ is the three-dimensional node \(uv-wz=0\), of multiplicity two. At each of the ten isolated singularities, an entire affine toric chart is the rank-one \(3\times 3\) determinantal cone, of multiplicity six. There are exactly \(43\) singular orbits, with dimensions \(0,1,2\) occurring \(19,18,6\) times. The singular locus as a reduced set is exactly the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus.

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) is algebraically closed, of arbitrary characteristic. The toric variety is the normal toric variety defined by the normal fan of \(P\), not the affine semigroup cone over \(P\). Lattice calculations use \(N=\mathbb Z^6/\mathbb Z(1,1,1,1,1,1)\) and the dual character lattice \(\{m\in\mathbb Z^6:\sum_i m_i=0\}\). The face \(F\) of \(P\) corresponds to an orbit of dimension \(\dim F\); its affine transverse toric cone is the normal cone \(\sigma_F\). The phrase *singular locus* means the underlying reduced subvariety, not a claimed description of its Jacobian or Fitting scheme.

## Proof

**1. Polytope and facets.** The matroid rank function equals \(\min(|S|,3)\) except \(r(H)=2\). The matroid base-polytope inequalities simplify to
\[
P=\{x\in\mathbb R^6: 0\le x_i\le1,\;\sum_{i\in E}x_i=3,\;\sum_{i\in H}x_i\le2\}.
\]
The twelve coordinate inequalities and the last circuit inequality are all facets. To see that the displayed set has no other vertices, first consider the hypersimplex \(\Delta(3,6)\), the convex hull of its twenty integral zero-one vertices. The hyperplane \(\sum_Hx_i=2\) passes through precisely the nine vertices adjacent to the excluded vertex \(e_H\) and does not cut the relative interior of an edge of the hypersimplex. Thus intersection deletes only \(e_H\) and creates no new vertices. Conversely, the nineteen remaining vertices are all bases of \(M\). The new facet
\[
Q=P\cap\{\sum_Hx_i=2\}\cong\Delta(2,3)\times\Delta(1,3)\cong\Delta_2\times\Delta_2
\]
has nine vertices \(B_{ij}=(H\setminus\{i\})\cup\{j\}\), with \(i\in H\), \(j\in C\).

**2. Primitive normal cones.** In \(N\), the primitive outward facet normals are \(e_i\) at \(x_i=1\), \(-e_i\) at \(x_i=0\), and \(w=\sum_{i\in H}e_i\) at the circuit facet. For any face \(F\), \(\sigma_F\) is generated by the normals of exactly the thirteen inequalities which are tight on \(F\). Faces not contained in \(Q\) have only signed coordinate normals. Every positive-dimensional such face has at least two varying coordinates, so its fixed-coordinate normals form a proper subset of the standard rays of a hypersimplex normal cone; these form a saturated independent lattice set and hence a smooth cone. The vertices outside \(Q\) are exactly the ten bases with \(|B\cap H|\le1\). Their six active signed coordinate normals span a five-dimensional nonsimplicial cone; each is singular.

**3. All singular faces in the new facet.** Faces of \(Q\cong\Delta_2\times\Delta_2\) are products of faces of its two triangles. The full facet \(Q\), its six three-dimensional facets and its nine square two-faces have independent saturated primitive normals, and hence smooth normal cones. Its six triangular two-faces have exactly four distinct primitive rays in a rank-three normal cone. Three triangles are \(T_i=\operatorname{conv}\{e_{B_{ij}}:j\in C\}\) for \(i\in H\), and the other three are \(S_j=\operatorname{conv}\{e_{B_{ij}}:i\in H\}\) for \(j\in C\). At \(T_i\) the four rays are \(\{e_a,e_b,-e_i,w\}\), where \(\{a,b\}=H\setminus\{i\}\), and satisfy the primitive relation \(e_a+e_b=(-e_i)+w\). At \(S_j\) the rays are \(\{e_j,-e_c,-e_d,w\}\), where \(\{c,d\}=C\setminus\{j\}\), and satisfy \(e_j+w=(-e_c)+(-e_d)\) in \(N\). In both cases any three rays generate a saturated rank-three sublattice. These are ordinary square toric cones: their affine transverse coordinate rings are \(k[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\). Every edge or vertex of \(Q\) is contained in at least one of these triangles and is therefore singular by specialization. There can be no further singular faces by the preceding exhaustive product-of-faces cases.

**4. Components and intersections.** Orbit closures reverse face inclusion. The inclusion-maximal singular faces are exactly the six triangles \(T_i,S_j\) and the ten vertices outside \(Q\). All six triangles are unimodular in the face lattice, so their orbit closures are \(\mathbb P^2\). Two triangles of the same family are disjoint; \(T_i\cap S_j\) is exactly the single vertex indexed by \(B_{ij}\). The corresponding toric orbit closures therefore intersect exactly in that point, with nine distinct intersections. The ten other vertex closures are isolated components. The singular faces contained in the triangles comprise nine vertices, eighteen edges and six triangles; adding the ten outside vertices gives \(19+18+6=43\) singular torus orbits.

**5. Transverse invariants and factoriality.** A nondegenerate square is the polytope whose affine toric cone is the ordinary threefold conifold; its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is \(2\). For an outside vertex corresponding to a base \(B\), the vertex's chart is unchanged from the hypersimplex \(\Delta(3,6)\). Its dual affine semigroup is generated by the nine exchange differences \(e_j-e_i\) with \(i\in B\) and \(j\notin B\). The only toric relations are the two-by-two minors of the generic \(3\times3\) matrix, so the chart is the rank-one determinantal cone, of dimension \(5\), isolated singular point at the cone vertex and multiplicity \(\binom{4}{2}=6\) (the degree of \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2\) in the Segre embedding). Every nonsmooth normal cone in the classification has strictly more extremal rays than its dimension, and every simplicial normal cone is unimodular. The simplicial-cone criterion for toric \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality therefore gives equality of the reduced singular and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial loci.

## Verification

The included `verify_matroid19.py` needs Python 3 and the standard library only. Run `python verify_matroid19.py`. It enumerates all intersections of the thirteen facet-tight vertex sets, computes exact lattice ranks and maximal-minor gcds, and checks every normal cone. It returns `VERIFY_OK`, the full face vector \((19,81,111,60,13,1)\), a \((19,18,6)\) singular-orbit count and precisely the six triangular plus ten isolated maximal singular faces. It checks the four-ray primitive conifold relations and the six-ray isolated-vertex relations. These finite exhaustive computations supplement the structural proof; the geometric identifications of affine semigroup rings and multiplicities use standard toric and determinantal arguments, not an unchecked numerical inference.

## Relationship to prior work

Gel'fand--Goresky--MacPherson--Serganova relate matroid combinatorics, Grassmannian torus strata and base polytopes. Joswig--Schröter introduced split matroids by hypersimplex splits. Ferroni--Schröter explicitly study **this exact nineteen-basis matroid** and already give its face vector \((19,81,111,60,13,1)\), in Example 2.2; neither the matroid example nor the face vector is claimed new. Lasoń--Michałek study global algebraic properties of matroid polytopes. The present claim instead classifies the projective toric singular-orbit locus, its sixteen maximal component closures, their intersections, and their transverse models. The inspected relevant sections and full texts did not exhibit an equivalent classification; unindexed or differently framed coverage remains possible.

## Limitations

No scheme-theoretic structure of the full singular locus or independent external audit is asserted. The classification applies to the normal projective toric variety of this specific matroid base-polytope normal fan, not arbitrary six-element matroids or arbitrary toric embeddings. Determinantal singularity multiplicities are local at ten isolated points; the conifold type is transverse at the generic points of six planes. Absence of a matching database or literature search result does not prove novelty.
