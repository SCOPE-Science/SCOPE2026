# Review of the proper rational tritangent spectrum on a quadric cone

**Correctness (PASS).** The claim is restricted to canonical smooth sections of the rank-three quadric over \(\mathbf F_2\). The quotient of cubic sections modulo the cone equation has dimension 16, and geometric smoothness forces a nonzero \(W^3\) coefficient. Both affine charts, all 32,768 candidates, all 15 rational hyperplanes, and the full 48-element projective cone automorphism group are enumerated. The singularity criterion follows by eliminating \(w\) from the affine Jacobian ideal, not by finite-point sampling. An independently written finite-extension verifier reproduces every joint count, and an exact Gröbner computation agrees on all 752 coefficient-space orbit representatives. The maximum six is witnessed explicitly. Risks: software implementation or transcription mistakes remain possible despite independent calculations; these are mitigated by source code, data, exact proofs of the criteria, and a second algorithm.

**Originality (PASS, with residual priority uncertainty).** Xarles's 2020 genus-four census provides isomorphism and point-count classifications, not proper rational tritangent distributions. Kulkarni–Ren–Sayyary Namin–Sturmfels (2017) explains that singular-quadric ruling planes form a family of contact planes but does not classify the \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational proper-contact spectrum. Bouchet's invariant theory is characteristic zero, and Hanselman–Pieper–Schiavone's reconstruction applies to characteristic zero or sufficiently large positive characteristic. Dragutinović's 2023 supersingular-locus study treats Newton/Ekedahl–Oort invariants, not tritangent contact. The related smooth-quadric finite-field contact classifications concern a different unique canonical quadric and do not imply the rank-three result. Actual indexed-record and primary-source searches did not locate equivalent numerical/structural claims. This absence does not prove priority; unindexed prior computations and overlooked tables remain possible.

**Value (PASS).** The rank-three case has a geometrically distinguished ruling family of contact planes, unlike the two smooth-quadric forms. Its sharp bound six, the three exceptional classes (including a pointless class), and the complete seven-stratum distribution reveal contact geometry not determined by a curve census, by point counts, or by the classical existence of a tritangent ruling family. Distinguishing proper geometric contacts from odd-theta planes prevents an otherwise misleading comparison.

The checked primary literature includes the original open preprints arXiv:2007.07822, arXiv:1712.06274, arXiv:2310.01158, arXiv:2402.03160, and arXiv:2301.12897. The theorem is finite-field-specific and its originality remains subject to independent literature review.
