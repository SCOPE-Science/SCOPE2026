# Six shortest sphere carriers generate cube-matching homology and distinguish antipodal parity

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the graph on \(\{0,1\}^3\), with two vertices joined when their Hamming distance is one. Let \(K=\mathcal M(Q_3)\) denote the ordinary matching complex: its vertices are the twelve cube edges, and its simplices are sets of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges.

Among all induced subcomplexes of \(K\), **the minimum number of matching-complex vertices supporting a nonzero class in the ambient integral second homology is eight**. Exactly six eight-vertex subsets attain this minimum. Their complements are precisely the six perfect matchings of the cube that do not consist of four parallel edges. For each such subset \(W\),
\[
K[W]\searrow S_W\cong S^2,
\]
where \(S_W\) is a triangulated two-sphere with eight vertices, eighteen edges, and twelve triangles. All six sphere classes map to nonzero primitive classes in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\); they generate that group integrally. Indeed, four of them constitute an integral basis, and
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4.
\]
The six carriers form one orbit under the full order-\(48\) cube automorphism group.

Write \(\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)\cong S_4\times C_2\), where \(S_4\) preserves the parity of the sum of the three cube coordinates and \(C_2\) is generated by antipodal inversion. Let \(\varepsilon\) be the nontrivial character of \(C_2\). The complete rational second-homology representation is
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\cong
\bigl(S^{(2,1,1)}\boxtimes\mathbf1\bigr)
\oplus\bigl(\mathbf1\boxtimes\varepsilon\bigr).
\]
Here \(S^{(2,1,1)}\) is the three-dimensional Specht representation of \(S_4\). Consequently, the inversion-even eigenspace has dimension three, the inversion-odd eigenspace has dimension one, and the full group fixes no nonzero rational second-homology class.

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns ordinary simplicial matching complexes of a finite simple graph, **not** discrete-Morse matching complexes, higher matching complexes, or the matching complex of the cube's line graph. All integral homology statements use coefficients \(\mathbb Z\); only the group-representation decomposition uses \(\mathbb Q\).

Enumerate cube vertices as binary triples \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\), corresponding to integers \(0,\ldots,7\). The ordered cube edges, indexed \(0,\ldots,11\), are
\[
(0,1),(0,2),(0,4),(1,3),(1,5),(2,3),
(2,6),(3,7),(4,5),(4,6),(5,7),(6,7).
\]
All simplices have increasing edge indices, with standard alternating-boundary orientations.

## Proof
**1. Minimal carrier classification.** Enumerating subsets of the twelve matching-complex vertices and computing reduced simplicial homology over \(\mathbb F_2\), the counts of subsets with nonzero second homology, by subset size \(8,9,10,11,12\), are respectively \(6,24,42,12,1\); for every size at most seven the count is zero. In size eight the six subsets are exactly the complements of nonparallel perfect matchings of \(Q_3\). There are nine perfect matchings of the cube: three parallel and six nonparallel. The complete finite enumeration, including its rank algorithm and asserted counts, appears in `verify_cube_matching.py`.

A representative carrier in the stated edge-index convention is
\[
W=\{0,1,3,6,7,8,9,10\}.
\]
Its induced complex has face vector \((8,20,16,2)\). Perform elementary free-face collapses using the face/coface pairs
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\{0,6,7\},\{0,6,7,8\}),\\
&(\{1,3,9\},\{1,3,9,10\}),\\
&(\{3,9\},\{3,9,10\}),\\
&(\{6,7\},\{6,7,8\}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The remaining complex has face vector \((8,18,12)\). Every edge belongs to exactly two triangles, every vertex link is a connected cycle, its one-skeleton is connected, and its Euler characteristic is two. Therefore it is a triangulated two-sphere. All forty-eight cube automorphisms are explicitly enumerated, and they act transitively on the six supports. Hence every carrier simplicially collapses to a two-sphere.

**2. Integral spanning and minimality for ambient classes.** The full complex has face vector \((12,42,44,9)\). Its integer boundary matrices satisfy \(\partial_1\partial_2=\partial_2\partial_3=0\) and have ranks \(11,31,9\). All thirty-one nonzero Smith factors of \(\partial_2\) are units; consequently its first integral homology vanishes. For each carrier, construct an integral two-cycle \(z_i\) representing the locally nontrivial class by taking the first rational nullspace vector of its two-boundary whose image is not in the global three-boundary image, then clearing denominators and dividing by the coefficient gcd; the ordering and algorithm are fully specified in the verifier.

The nine columns of \(\partial_3\), together with \(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3\) for the first four carriers in lexicographic order, form a \(44\)-by-\(13\) integer matrix of rank thirteen. Its thirteen nonzero Smith factors are **all one**. This certifies that their image is a primitive rank-thirteen sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^{44}\). Since this image is contained in \(\ker\partial_2\), itself a primitive rank-thirteen sublattice, the two lattices coincide. It follows that the four carrier classes form an integral basis for
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z^4.
\]
Each of the remaining two carriers maps nontrivially; transitivity makes all six images primitive. A nonzero ambient integral class has infinite order, so its domain must have nonzero rational second homology, hence nonzero mod-two second homology. The enumeration in Step 1 therefore excludes every smaller support and all other supports of size eight.

**3. Full symmetry.** Cube automorphisms are coordinate permutations followed by independent binary-coordinate flips. The subgroup preserving parity of the sum of the three coordinates has order twenty-four and acts faithfully on the four even-parity vertices, giving \(S_4\); central antipodal inversion supplies the complementary \(C_2\). As \(H_1(K;\mathbb Q)=H_3(K;\mathbb Q)=0\), the reduced simplicial Lefschetz trace equals the trace on second homology.

For the \(S_4\) cycle types \((1,1,1,1),(2,1,1),(2,2),(3,1),(4)\), the traces on the parity-preserving component of the group are
\[
(4,0,0,1,2),
\]
and the traces after composing with antipodal inversion are
\[
(2,-2,-2,-1,0).
\]
Each trace is obtained by summing signed fixed oriented simplices, subtracting the contribution from the augmented empty simplex. Projecting onto the even/odd inversion eigenspaces gives the \(S_4\) characters \((3,-1,-1,0,1)\) and \((1,1,1,1,1)\), respectively. Their orthogonal decomposition against the five rational irreducible characters of \(S_4\) gives the representation in the Finding.

## Verification
Run `python verify_cube_matching.py` with SymPy installed as specified in `requirements.txt`. The verifier reconstructs the entire complex from the binary cube, enumerates every induced subcomplex, verifies the free-face collapses and sphere links, constructs signed integer boundary matrices, computes their ranks and Smith factors, verifies integral generation, and checks all forty-eight automorphism Lefschetz traces and the irreducible-character decomposition. It also verifies that the six canonical carrier cycles obey two independent integral homology relations. No manually supplied boundary matrix or external data file is required.

## Relationship to prior work
Braun and Hough, *Matching and Independence Complexes Related to Small Grids* (arXiv:1606.01204v1, first public 3 June 2016), develop the discrete-Morse study of planar two-row rectangular grid matching complexes and explicitly formulate the question of symmetry representations for their generalized grid family. Their graphs are not the three-dimensional cube, and their results do not give minimal cube-matching homology carriers or their full cube-symmetry module.

The published record *The cube matching complex is \(\bigvee^4 S^2\)* (identifier `79d70fe8b67e`) establishes the **abstract integral homotopy type of the exact same complex**. That prior theorem is credited: the abstract four-sphere wedge is not newly claimed. Its result and proof provide the face-vector and homotopy background but do not classify the six minimal induced supports, prove their primitive integral spanning, or identify the \(S_4\times C_2\) character. Its full public RESULT text was inspected for statement-level comparison.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the three-dimensional cube. It does not describe the corresponding complexes for higher-dimensional hypercubes, assert an integral direct-sum decomposition as a group-module, or compute higher homotopy groups. The six support classes generate the *ambient second homology*; the theorem does not assert that every induced subcomplex has torsion-free homology in every degree. The originality assessment is limited by discoverability of other published calculations under alternate terminology.
