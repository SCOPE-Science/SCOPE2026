# Six Sylow-five squares in a Desargues Hamilton-cycle flip graph

## Finding

Let \(D=Q_5(2,3)\) denote the middle-level graph of the five-dimensional cube: its vertices are the two-element and three-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), and two vertices are joined when one set is contained in the other. Thus \(D\) is the twenty-vertex cubic Desargues graph, also \(GP(10,3)\). Let \(\mathcal H\) be the set of **unoriented, unrooted** Hamilton cycles of \(D\). Define the graph \(\mathcal F_{10}(D)\) on \(\mathcal H\) by joining distinct cycles precisely when the symmetric difference of their edge sets is a single cycle of length ten. This is a single alternating ten-cycle flip, with Hamiltonicity required at both endpoints.

**Theorem (finite exact classification).** There are exactly \(24\) Hamilton cycles in \(D\), and \(\mathcal F_{10}(D)\) is connected, seven-regular, has \(84\) edges, and has both radius and diameter \(2\). Every vertex has distance-layer sizes \((1,7,16)\). The \(24\) flip edges that belong to no triangle form six disjoint four-cycles. Between every pair of these four-cycles, the other flip edges form a perfect matching. The six four-cycles can be identified equivariantly with the six subgroups of order five in \(S_5\): there is an \(S_5\)-equivariant bijection between \(\mathcal H\) and the \(24\) five-cycles of \(S_5\), with each four-cycle comprising the four nonidentity elements of one subgroup.

The length ten is the **minimum** length of a single alternating-cycle flip that takes one Hamilton cycle of \(D\) to a *different* Hamilton cycle. Exactly \(60\) unordered pairs of Hamilton cycles admit an alternating single-cycle symmetric difference of length \(18\). Those length-eighteen flips alone form two twelve-vertex connected components, each five-regular. This latter comparison uses the same Hamiltonicity requirement, not the unrestricted perfect-matching reconfiguration model.

## Assumptions and scope

Graphs are simple and undirected. A Hamilton cycle is a twenty-edge spanning cycle, counted only once modulo cyclic starting point and orientation. A perfect matching of \(D\) is a ten-edge set covering all twenty vertices exactly once. Since \(D\) is cubic, the edge complement of any perfect matching is a spanning two-regular graph; it is Hamiltonian precisely when this complement is connected. Conversely, each Hamilton cycle has a unique complementary perfect matching. For two such perfect matchings \(M\) and \(N\), the symmetric difference of their Hamilton complements equals \(M\mathbin{\triangle}N\). Thus a length-ten flip is exactly a single ten-edge alternating cycle in this matching symmetric difference, **restricted** to the perfect matchings with Hamiltonian complements.

All claims concern this single fixed finite graph; no assertion is made for higher-dimensional middle-level graphs. The word “edge” of \(\mathcal F_{10}(D)\) denotes an *unordered* configuration transition.

## Proof

This is a fully exhaustive finite proof with two different graph representations and standalone search procedures, rather than an extrapolation from sampled cases. The critical inference rules and their complete implementations are in the accompanying `verify_subsets.py` and `verify_cycles.py`.

**Step 1: Exhaustive state classification.** Label the twenty vertices by the ten lexicographically ordered two-subsets followed by the ten lexicographically ordered three-subsets. Add precisely the thirty containment edges. To enumerate all perfect matchings, repeatedly choose the smallest as-yet-unmatched vertex \(u\) and branch over **every** currently unmatched neighbour \(v\), then recurse after deleting \(u,v\). This yields every perfect matching exactly once: in any matching its partner at the first uncovered vertex occurs in exactly one branch. The search has no heuristic pruning and terminates upon covering all twenty vertices. It yields \(60\) matchings. Inspecting the components of each complementary two-factor gives exactly \(24\) connected twenty-cycles and \(36\) pairs of ten-cycles. Hence there are precisely \(24\) Hamilton cycles.

**Step 2: Exhaustive transition test.** For each of the \(\binom{24}{2}=276\) unordered pairs of the Hamilton-complement matchings, take the symmetric difference. It is an alternating single cycle if and only if the positive-degree vertices in the symmetric difference all have degree two and induce a connected graph. Both implications hold because each matching contributes at most one edge at any vertex, and the symmetric difference of two matchings is a disjoint union of alternating even cycles. The pairwise test produces \(84\) single cycles of length ten, \(60\) single cycles of length eighteen, and \(132\) disconnected differences. The latter consist of \(60\) pairs with twelve edges in the difference, \(60\) with sixteen, and \(12\) with twenty. No other possible single-cycle length occurs, proving the asserted minimum positive flip length and transition count. The independent Hamilton-cycle search in Step 5 computes the same partition directly without enumerating matchings.

**Step 3: Distance and intrinsic fibre structure.** Form the graph on the twenty-four states from exactly the eighty-four length-ten transitions. Exact breadth-first search from *every* state yields the same layers \((1,7,16)\), proving seven-regularity, connectedness, radius two and diameter two. Mark a flip edge \(uv\) exceptional if its endpoints have no common neighbour in the flip graph. There are exactly twenty-four such edges. Their induced edge subgraph has six connected components, each containing four vertices, each vertex having degree two within this exceptional-edge subgraph. Thus each component is a four-cycle; these are also induced four-cycles of the full graph because each has only two internal neighbours. For each of the \(\binom62=15\) pairs of components, the full flip graph has exactly four connecting edges, with pairwise distinct endpoints on each side: a perfect matching. These tests are exhaustive over all vertices and all component pairs.

**Step 4: Symmetric-group interpretation.** Every permutation of the five ground elements acts on the subset-labelled graph and hence on its Hamilton cycles. The exhaustive action test of all \(120\) permutations verifies a transitive action on the twenty-four cycles. The stabilizer of one chosen cycle has order five; its five permutations form a cyclic subgroup \(\langle r\rangle\). To each \(gH_0\) assign the five-cycle \(grg^{-1}\). This is well-defined because the stabilizer centralizes \(r\), and it is bijective because the orbit has twenty-four members. The six exceptional four-cycle components are exactly the subsets of four five-cycles generating each order-five subgroup. For a particular five-cycle \(r\), its two neighbours within its subgroup are \(r^2\) and \(r^3\); its other five neighbours form one orbit under conjugation by \(\langle r\rangle\). The verification checks this neighbour statement at the base cycle and transports it across **all** \(120\) relabelings. It therefore determines the full flip graph equivariantly and confirms the fibre claim independently of mere component-size counts.

**Step 5: Separate construction.** A second program constructs \(D\) as the generalized Petersen graph \(GP(10,3)\), with outer decagon edges, inner edges of step three and ten spokes. It fixes the start of a Hamilton tour and breaks reverse-orientation symmetry, recursively explores all possibilities without dropping branches, and obtains exactly twenty-four edge-distinct Hamilton cycles. Their complementary matchings generate the same single-alternating-cycle transition statistics. Full breadth-first searches and the intrinsic no-triangle edge decomposition independently verify the degree, diameter, and six-four-cycle cross-matchings. This provides a separate enumeration argument for the non-group-theoretic conclusions.

All observations in these steps are integer equality or graph-search assertions over an explicitly bounded finite space, and are reproduced by the complete source, not merely asserted by run logs.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_subsets.py` and `python3 verify_cycles.py` with Python 3 and its standard library only. Both halt after `VERIFY_OK` if every assertion succeeds; their actual outputs are recorded in `verify_subsets.txt` and `verify_cycles.txt`. The former counts perfect matchings, verifies the sixty complementary two-factor types, tests all 276 Hamiltonian pairs, all twenty-four breadth-first distance tables, the intrinsic exceptional-edge decomposition, and all \(120\) ground-set permutations. The latter separately enumerates cycles directly on \(GP(10,3)\), verifies every pairwise flip and all distance and fibre parameters. There are no random seeds, external packages, approximate comparisons, or partial searches.

## Relationship to prior work

Mütze and Weber's 2011 paper constructs parameterized two-factors in middle-level graphs, and its Section 6 tabulates resulting Hamilton cycles or paths from those constructions. It neither supplies a census of all Hamilton cycles of \(Q_5(2,3)\) nor classifies the graph of length-ten flips between them. Mütze's later proof of the middle-levels conjecture establishes Hamiltonicity in every odd-dimensional middle level and uses a different path-pair flipping mechanism within its existence proof; it does not determine this finite configuration graph. Ito and collaborators study reconfiguration of perfect matchings by arbitrary alternating cycles and shortest sequences, without requiring all intermediate complements to remain Hamiltonian or restricting every move to length ten. Thus existence and unrestricted matching reachability do not imply the asserted degree, distance, exceptional-square or Sylow-five structure. An unindexed equivalent result remains possible; absence from selected searches is not a proof of publication novelty.

## Limitations

The exhaustive result is for \(Q_5(2,3)\) alone. The proof is an exact finite enumeration by complete algorithms; it does not give a symbolic formula for larger middle-level graphs or prove that analogous six-fibre structure persists. The full literature cannot be exhaustively ruled out, including specialized studies or supplementary computer data on the twenty-vertex Desargues graph. The count of twenty-four Hamilton cycles and elementary symmetry properties may have been recorded before; the proposed distinct mathematical content is the shortest Hamiltonicity-preserving alternating-cycle flip graph and its precise intrinsic/Sylow-five decomposition.
