# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The absorbing-chain moment recurrences \((I-Q)a=\mathbf1\) and \((I-Q)b=2a-\mathbf1\) follow by first-step conditioning and include self-loops and both absorbing outcomes. The full-population variance subtracts the square of the uniform-vertex mixture mean. A complete enumeration of labeled graphs on \(n\le5\), with exact fraction elimination and a missing-edge check for each, gives zero discordant signs in \(0+3+84+3140\) comparisons. The two explicit connected six-vertex graphs give strict opposite signs in both possible directions. The signed rational values are printed in the result and regenerated by the checker. Risk: the lower bound is a computer-assisted finite exhaustion rather than a short invariant argument.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The closest primary work on the same birth-death Moran network model is Hindersin--Traulsen (arXiv:1406.4415), which analyzes mean **conditional fixation** times and sojourn contributions for the six order-four networks; it does not cover simultaneous *unconditional* absorption mean and variance sign changes. Díaz et al. (arXiv:1311.7631) focus on expected absorption times and bounds, while Hathcock--Strogatz (arXiv:1812.05652) analyze distributions for fixation conditioned on success on regular ring/complete topologies. Neither comparison implies the sharp minimum of six for moment discordance, and exact query-based literature checks did not identify an equivalent assertion. Risks: terminology overlap with voter/coalescence processes, and unindexed results or inaccessible source portions; lack of a match is not proof of worldwide novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Comparing first and second absorption-time moments matters when assessing evolutionary uncertainty, not merely average speed. The finding proves a sharp qualitative threshold and supplies each of two opposed mean--variance effects: a link may accelerate expected resolution while increasing timing risk, or delay resolution while reducing risk. The exhaustive size cutoff answers a natural first-possible-network question; the result is not just a numeric example, a renamed mean-time theorem, or an unsupported claim of practical magnitude.

**Scope and limitations.** Neutral reproductive fitness, discrete-time birth-death sampling, uniform single-mutant placement, unweighted connected simple graphs; unconditioned absorption rather than successful fixation. Computer-assisted finite coverage up to five vertices and two exact six-vertex witnesses only. Stronger spectral, selected-mutant and large-network claims are not made. No independent audit is claimed.
