# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact uniform-root susceptibility identity \(nM_G(p)=\sum_F(\sum_C|C|^2)p^{|F|}(1-p)^{m-|F|}\) is elementary and valid for every finite simple graph. The small-order classification explicitly enumerates every labeled graph with up to five vertices, canonicalizes using every vertex permutation, and evaluates all relevant edge states in integers. There are precisely two multiply realized connected degree profiles at order five; both polynomial differences have one sign throughout the open interval. The displayed six-vertex graphs are connected, triangle-free, degree-matched, and give a factored difference with exactly one interior zero. Their exact polynomial coefficients were independently reconstructed using path-pair reliability on unicyclic graphs. The scope excludes generic SIR dynamics, for which independent undirected edge percolation may fail.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

Moore–Newman (2000) establishes the outbreak–bond-component framework, not the sharp same-degree minimality. Kenah–Robins (2007) clarifies a different SIR/percolation equivalence question. Miller (2009) and Stegehuis–Peron (2021) study clustering and degree effects on outbreak outcomes; they do not derive the explicit smallest connected same-degree reversal pair and exact crossing. Published mathematical research record searches using the six-vertex graph descriptions, degree-matched terminology, reliability/susceptibility aliases, and transmissibility crossover returned no covering result. Earlier finite graph tables or non-epidemiological reliability-polynomial treatments remain possible.

## Value — PASS

The object is a natural epidemic-risk observable (mean size of the open component of a uniformly chosen seed). Degree profiles are common practical summaries of contact networks. The result gives the smallest finite obstruction to any universal ranking based on the degrees alone: even matching triangle counts and having no triangles do not prevent a strict change of the better graph as transmissibility changes. The exact threshold is interpretable and independently reproducible. It is a motivated sharp finite boundary, not an arbitrary graph lookup or a claimed universal SIR theorem.

## Scope and limitations

Only homogeneous independent bond transmission and connected finite simple contact networks are proved. The finite cutoff does not extend automatically to heterogeneous transmission, non-exponential infectious periods, directed networks, or more complicated epidemic dynamics. An independently conducted audit and comprehensive priority determination are absent.
