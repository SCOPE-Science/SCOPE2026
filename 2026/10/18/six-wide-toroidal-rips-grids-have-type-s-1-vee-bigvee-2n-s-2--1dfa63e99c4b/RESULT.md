# Six-wide toroidal Rips grids have type \(S^1\vee\bigvee^{2n}S^2\)

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge7\), the inclusive scale-two graph-metric Vietoris--Rips complex on the vertices of the Cartesian graph product \(C_6\square C_n\) satisfies

\[
\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(C_6\square C_n)\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{2n}S^2.
\]

In particular, its integral fundamental group is infinite cyclic, its first integral homology is \(\mathbb Z\), its second integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{2n}\), and all higher integral homology groups vanish. The homotopy equivalence is proved, not inferred from finite-field Betti numbers.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has vertex set \(V=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). For \((i,j),(k,l)\in V\), its distance is \(d_6(i,k)+d_n(j,l)\), where \(d_q(a,b)=\min(|a-b|,q-|a-b|)\) uses representatives in \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\). A simplex is exactly a nonempty finite set of pairwise graph distance at most two. The theorem requires \(n\ge7\); no assertion is made about shorter second cycles or other scales.

For each \((i,j)\), define the five-vertex **diamond**
\[
D_{i,j}=\{(i,j),(i-1,j),(i+1,j),(i,j-1),(i,j+1)\},
\]
and the four-vertex **square**
\[
Q_{i,j}=\{(i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1),(i+1,j+1)\}.
\]
All subscripts are taken modulo their respective cycle lengths. Let \(X_n\) be the simplicial subcomplex generated by all these diamonds and squares. For every column \(j\) and parity \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\), let
\[
E_{j,\varepsilon}=\{(\varepsilon,j),(\varepsilon+2,j),(\varepsilon+4,j)\},
\]
a filled two-simplex, and let \(L_n\) be the complex generated by these \(2n\) triangles.

## Proof

**1. All maximal faces are accounted for.** Because \(n\ge7\), the projection of a diameter-at-most-two clique to the second cyclic coordinate lies in one, two, or three consecutive columns. If it meets the first and third of three consecutive columns, vertices in those extremal columns share a single first coordinate, and the middle column is confined to its two neighbors and itself; the clique is contained in a diamond. If its projection is precisely a pair of columns separated by two steps, its vertices similarly lie in a diamond.

For a clique in two adjacent columns, the sets of first coordinates have cross-distance at most one. If one of these sets is a singleton, the other is confined to that row and its two neighboring rows, so the clique lies in a diamond. Otherwise both sets have at least two members; the cross-distance constraint forces both to use a common neighboring pair of rows, so the clique lies in a square. The last assertion uses that in a six-cycle two distinct vertices have at most one common neighbor unless they are adjacent, and checking the adjacent case forces the same adjacent row pair.

Finally, a clique contained in a single column is a clique in the square of \(C_6\). That graph is the octahedral graph (the complete graph on six vertices minus the three pairs of opposite vertices); its eight maximal triangles comprise the six triples of consecutive rows, already lying in diamonds, and the two parity triples \(E_{j,0}\) and \(E_{j,1}\). Conversely, each stated diamond, square, and parity triangle has diameter at most two. Therefore
\[
K_n=X_n\cup L_n,
\]
and the only additional maximal faces beyond those of \(X_n\) are the \(2n\) parity triangles.

**2. An explicit collapse of the base.** Fix a diamond centered at \(c=(i,j)\). Write \(N=(i-1,j)\), \(S=(i+1,j)\), \(W=(i,j-1)\), and \(E=(i,j+1)\). In each diamond first cancel the pairs
\[
\bigl(\{N,S\}\cup U,\ \{N,S,c\}\cup U\bigr)
\qquad(U\subseteq\{W,E\}),
\]
with \(U\) processed in descending cardinality. Next cancel
\[
\bigl(\{W,E\}\cup U,\ \{W,E,c\}\cup U\bigr)
\qquad(U\in\{\{N\},\{S\},\varnothing\}),
\]
again with larger subsets first. These are seven elementary collapse pairs per diamond. Opposite pairs \(\{N,S\}\) and \(\{W,E\}\) occur in no other diamond or square, because the two cycle lengths are respectively six and at least seven; after deleting higher-dimensional cofaces in the stated order, the first member of each pair is therefore free in \(X_n\). Diamonds may be processed in any order of centers.

For every square, with cyclically ordered corners \(a=(i,j)\), \(b=(i+1,j)\), \(c=(i,j+1)\), and \(d=(i+1,j+1)\), perform the two collapses
\[
(\{a,b,c\},\{a,b,c,d\}),\qquad
(\{b,c\},\{b,c,d\}).
\]
The first cancels its tetrahedron; the second removes its remaining antidiagonal edge. Every pair is a valid free-face pair after the preceding diamond cancellations, and no pair depends on a particular \(n\). The surviving triangles are exactly \(\{a,b,d\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\) for each original square, together with their faces: this is the ordinary periodic square triangulation \(T_n\) of a torus. The total number of elementary pairs is
\[
(7+2)\cdot 6n=54n,
\]
and \(X_n\searrow T_n\cong S^1\times S^1\). The collapse fixes all edges \((i,j)(i+1,j)\) of the first-coordinate meridians.

**3. The additional triangles fill one meridional generator.** For each \(E_{j,\varepsilon}\), every edge lies in \(X_n\): the edge between \((i,j)\) and \((i+2,j)\) belongs to the consecutive triple \(\{(i,j),(i+1,j),(i+2,j)\}\) of a diamond. But the entire parity triple does not lie in \(X_n\). Thus the intersection of each added two-simplex with \(X_n\) is precisely its boundary cycle, and distinct added two-simplices have no nontrivial overlap outside \(X_n\).

The boundary of \(E_{j,0}\) follows row indices \(0\to2\to4\to0\); that of \(E_{j,1}\) follows \(1\to3\to5\to1\). Within \(X_n\), each row-step-two edge is homotopic relative endpoints to the two-step path through its intermediate vertex via the consecutive-triple triangle. Hence both attaching cycles are freely homotopic to the primitive first-coordinate meridian \(i\mapsto(i,j)\) of the torus. The homotopy equivalence \(X_n\simeq T_n\) therefore carries each of the \(2n\) attaching loops to the same primitive meridional class (up to orientation and conjugacy).

**4. Compute the resulting homotopy type.** The preceding steps identify \(K_n\) up to homotopy with the torus with \(r=2n\) two-cells attached along a primitive meridian. Use the standard CW structure of the torus: one point, two one-cells \(a,b\), and a two-cell attached by the commutator \([a,b]\). The first new disk kills the meridian generator \(a\). The original commutator cell then has a null attaching map and becomes a two-sphere, while \(b\) survives as a circle. Each of the other \(r-1\) disks adds one further two-sphere. Thus the space has homotopy type \(S^1\vee\bigvee^{r}S^2\), proving the theorem for every \(n\ge7\). All homology statements are over the integers and follow from this explicit homotopy classification.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` independently enumerates all graph-metric cliques and validates their agreement with the claimed diamonds, squares and parity triangles. It executes every specified elementary free-face collapse, checking at every step that the lower face has exactly one remaining coface; it confirms that the survivor is precisely the standard torus triangulation and that all parity-triangle intersections equal their boundaries. It additionally computes full simplicial mod-two boundary ranks, without using the symbolic theorem. The cases \(n=7,8,9,10,11\) pass, giving mod-two Betti numbers \((\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)=(1,1,2n)\) with all higher ranks zero. See `verification_output.txt`. Finite tests are corroboration; the all-parameter conclusion follows from the local collapse, clique classification, and attaching-map proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng and Sterling [1] study Vietoris--Rips complexes of **square** torus grids \(C_q\square C_q\), including the exceptional \(q=6\), scale-two wedge of twenty-three two-spheres and the stable torus regime at larger square side lengths. Their statements do not give the homotopy type of the distinct, rectangular family \(C_6\square C_n\) for \(n\ge7\). Adamaszek's [2] analysis of clique complexes of powers of individual cycles includes the octahedral cross-section \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(C_6)\), but that individual-factor classification does not imply the product's circle-plus-sphere-wedge homotopy type.

A separate earlier exact calculation treats **five-wide** rectangular grids; it is not part of this theorem and is not claimed as new here. The six-wide case is structurally different: rather than filling each column with one four-simplex, it has two independent parity triangles per column, leading to a distinct integral sphere multiplicity. Other checked comparable results concern four-wide torus grids, cyclic products with complete-graph fibers, or neighborhood complexes; their hypotheses or constructions do not cover this six-wide result.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to graph-metric scale two, six rows and at least seven columns. It does not classify any other scale, assert a new classification for five rows, or address the case of six rows and six columns. It makes no claim about persistence maps or equivariant homology actions. The literature comparisons cannot exclude every unpublished or unindexed equivalent proof; independent scientific review remains to be done. Finite checker results alone do not certify the infinite proof.
