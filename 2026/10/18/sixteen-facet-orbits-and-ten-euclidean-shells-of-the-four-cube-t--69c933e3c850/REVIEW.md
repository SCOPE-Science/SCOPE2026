# Scientific review of the four-cube facet-orbit and distance-shell classification

## Correctness — PASS
The scope is the standard real Lipschitz-free unit ball of the 16-point Hamming cube \(Q_4\). Its polar consists exactly of the centered 1-Lipschitz potentials. A finite-dimensional polytope polar vertex is fixed by tight inequalities spanning the zero-sum coordinate space, equivalent to a connected spanning tight-edge graph. Bipartiteness forces each of the 32 graph edges to be tight: the tight connected spanning subgraph contains an odd-length path between endpoints of every cube edge, so the signed unit increments sum to an odd integer whose absolute value is at most one. Hence each facet is encoded by one of the \(2^{15}\) assignments of signed increments on a fixed spanning tree, with the remaining seventeen edges tested. The exhaustive parametrization is a mathematical proof of finiteness/completeness, not an experimental sample.

The embedded `verify_q4.py` was executed using standard Python 3. It generated exactly 990 admissible signed height assignments; the independent Hamming-layer recursion generated **the identical set**. The code enumerated the full 384-element automorphism group and all 768 signed actions, proved sixteen disjoint orbits whose sizes sum to 990, checked all sixteen representative height words and squared norms, and independently verified the Burnside fixed-point sum \(12288=16\cdot768\). The integer formula for centered Euclidean squared norm yields precisely the ten shell counts, totaling 990. Every assertion passed. An independent mathematical reviewer has not assessed the work.

## Originality — PASS under inspected-source evidence, with residual bibliographic risk
The known count of 990 normalized height functions is clearly published by Çelik et al. (arXiv:2003.06725, Proposition 10) and also appears in earlier rank-function literature (arXiv:1111.6055). General connected-tight-edge and bipartite all-tight-edge criteria are established in arXiv:1807.07678 and arXiv:1910.05193. A directly related published hypercube norm result proves the inradius and count of sixteen tangent facets for every dimension; neither fact is offered as new. Aliaga (arXiv:2102.01219) addresses primal extreme points and their finite supports, not the present facet classification.

The exact 16-orbit decomposition under \(B_4\times C_2\), complete representative words, and ten-valued rational squared-distance spectrum do **not** follow in evaluated form from the general criteria, known aggregate count, or known radius. Stronger-coverage searches for hypercube rank-function orbits, 990-height classification, Hamming Lipschitz polytopes, and full Euclidean facet spectra turned up no corresponding evaluated classification in inspected source texts or retrieved mathematical-result titles/summaries. Negative search alone is not uniqueness proof: obscure enumerations, data tables or alternative terminology remain a genuine residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The dimension-four Boolean cube is the first substantial 15-dimensional Hamming Lipschitz-free ball in which both nontrivial graph-height enumeration and a large hyperoctahedral action occur. Its previously known facet total alone conceals sixteen distinct orbit types, with markedly different orbit sizes, and a ten-level Euclidean facet-distance profile. Together with explicit representatives, these give a complete checkable benchmark for the geometry of finite transportation norms, orbit counting, and spectral contact questions. This is a natural complete finite classification; no general theorem for all dimensions or external downstream application is asserted. The table is not an arbitrary parameter slice: \(Q_4\) is the canonical four-bit Hamming metric and its full natural symmetry group is intrinsic.
