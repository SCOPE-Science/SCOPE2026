# Scientific review: cubic-section classification on the Schur quartic

## Correctness
**PASS.** The criterion \(E_S\sim3H\) is equivalent to the exact binary system \(Mx=3\mathbf1\) by the Hodge index theorem on a smooth quartic K3 surface. The packaged independent-input checker recomputes line containment over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), all 276 line determinants and 32 distinct concurrent triples, exact rank-16 integral lattice data, all 256 binary completions and the 64 valid sections. The no-four-clique certificate rules out linear factors of the cubic hypersurfaces. The restriction isomorphism in degree three proves uniqueness.

## Originality
**PASS, with disclosed bibliographic risk.** The originating configuration paper [1] establishes \(D\sim6H\), incidence and logarithmic slope but no explicitly identified count of cubic subdivisors in the inspected portions. Höhn [2] treats group structure and root-model symmetries of this same configuration; its full text could not be accessed, so possible implicit or explicit coverage there cannot be ruled out. Public indexed work on the *full* 64 Schur lines gives a distinct whole-graph spectrum, not a 24-line cubic-divisor classification. Actual semantic searches covered binary sign partitions, twelve-line cubic sections, lattices, and hyperplane-index synonyms. No found result makes the 64 count or its irreducibility an immediate published corollary. The originating article's Section 5 was not separately retrieved; this is a residual overlap risk rather than evidence of novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** The source's exceptional slope construction has divisor class \(6H\), so decompositions into cubic sections are an intrinsic, lowest nontrivial balanced-degree question, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The exact 64-to-32 classification produces geometrically irreducible cubic hypersurfaces, while the index-three hyperplane obstruction identifies a structural feature of the distinguished line lattice. Both refine the underlying geometry and supply concrete constraints for lattice and symmetry studies of the configuration.
