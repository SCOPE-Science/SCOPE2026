# Exact census of smooth ternary cubic pencils through a blocking cubic

## Finding
Let \(\mathscr P\) be the family of pencils of plane cubic curves over \(\mathbb F_3\) with no common \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational point, such that each of the four \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational pencil members is geometrically smooth. A member is *blocking* when its rational points meet every rational line of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\). Exactly **292032** pencils in \(\mathscr P\) contain a blocking member, which is necessarily unique.

For any fixed geometrically smooth blocking cubic, exactly **312** pencils in \(\mathscr P\) contain it. The three remaining cubic members have sorted rational-point counts in exactly the following profiles:

| Sorted counts of other three fibers | Number of pencils through the fixed blocking cubic |
| --- | ---: |
| \((1,1,5)\) | 24 |
| \((1,2,4)\) | 132 |
| \((1,3,3)\) | 42 |
| \((2,2,3)\) | 114 |

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are projective plane cubics defined by nonzero homogeneous degree-three forms over \(\mathbb F_3\). Two forms differing by a nonzero scalar are identified. A pencil is a two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_3\)-linear subspace of the ten-dimensional cubic-form space. No rational base point means that no \(P\in\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\) lies on all four rational members; it does not mean that the geometric base locus is empty. The curve defined by \(C=0\) is geometrically smooth when \(C\) and its three partial derivatives have no common projective zero over an algebraic closure. The equation \(C=0\) is essential in this test: in characteristic three, Euler's identity does not imply that a gradient zero lies on the cubic. The count concerns pencils as subspaces, not pencils modulo projective coordinate transformations.

## Proof
**1. Why the blocking member is unique.** The ternary projective plane has thirteen rational points and thirteen rational lines, with four points per line. Every smooth plane cubic over \(\mathbb F_3\) has at least one rational point: the genus-one Hasse bound yields \(\#C(\mathbb F_3)\ge4-2\sqrt3>0\). A geometrically smooth cubic has no line component, so Bézout's theorem prevents all four rational points on any rational line from lying on it. Every blocking subset without an entire line has at least six points, a special case of the blocking-set bound used in [2]. Here is a direct proof. If such a set has size \(b\le5\), then \(b\ge4\) since a point lies on only four lines. Write \(n_j\) for the number of lines meeting it in \(j\) points, \(j=1,2,3\). Counting lines, incidences, and point pairs gives \(n_2+2n_3=4b-13\) and \(n_2+3n_3=\binom b2\). At \(b=4\) this gives \(n_2=-3\), and at \(b=5\) it gives \(n_3=3\). Three collinear triples on three lines have union of size at least six because each pair of lines has at most one common point. Thus neither case is possible.

Distinct rational pencil members cannot share a rational point: a point on two distinct members lies on every linear combination. If two of the four members were blocking, they would use at least twelve disjoint rational points, leaving at most one point for the other two smooth members, each of which requires at least one.

**2. Single-cubic orbit used in the count.** The classification [4] gives a single orbit of 936 smooth blocking cubics modulo nonzero scalar. For reproducibility the verifier also derives this input directly. Normalize every nonzero ternary cubic coefficient vector by making its first nonzero coefficient one. There are \((3^{10}-1)/2=29524\) such vectors. Evaluate each cubic at all thirteen rational projective points. A form has a rational line component if and only if it vanishes at all four points of that line: a degree-three binary form with four projective roots is identically zero. The exact enumeration in `verify_ternary_pencil.py` finds 936 blocking vectors without such a line. For the explicit model
\[
 F=X^3+Y^3+Z^3-(X+Y+Z)(XY+XZ+YZ)+XYZ,
\]
the verifier computes Gröbner bases over \(\mathbb F_3\) for the ideals generated by \(F\), its partial derivatives, and in turn each chart equation \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), or \(Z=1\). All three ideals are the unit ideal, proving geometric smoothness. The verifier also computes exactly 936 distinct projective transforms of \(F\), each among the 936 blocking candidates. Equality of projective cubic forms can be detected by their projective point-evaluation vectors: the projective evaluation ideal on \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\) has no nonzero homogeneous element below degree four. Thus all candidates lie in one smooth orbit, and no other smooth blocking cubics are possible.

**3. Enumeration of rational-base-point-free smooth pencils through \(F\).** A pencil through \(F\) can be specified by a projective cubic \(G\) linearly independent from \(F\). Its other three projective members are \([G]\), \([G+F]\), and \([G-F]\). The pencil has no rational common base point exactly when \(G(P)\ne0\) at each of the six rational points of \(F\). Enumerate all 29524 normalized projective forms \(G\), discard those failing this test, and then discard candidates for which any of the three other members is geometrically singular. As in step 2, the geometric smoothness test is exact on all three affine charts and includes the cubic equation as well as its partial derivatives. The rational-singularity prefilter only speeds up the calculation. Exactly 936 normalized \(G\) pass. Their sorted rational-point-profile frequencies are
\[
 (1,1,5):72,\qquad(1,2,4):396,\qquad(1,3,3):126,\qquad(2,2,3):342.
\]
Each pencil is counted three times, once for each choice of \([G]\) among its non-\(F\) members. Division by three gives the four frequencies in the table, totaling 312. Transitivity from step 2 gives 312 pencils through any smooth blocking cubic. Step 1 makes the blocking member unique in each counted pencil, giving \(936\cdot312=292032\) pencils in all.

**4. Nonemptiness.** A concrete generator complementary to \(F\) is \(G=X^2Z+Y^3+YZ^2\). The exact smoothness tests for \(F\), \(G\), \(G+F\), and \(G-F\) and the rational base-point test verify this pencil, whose nonblocking members have sorted profile \((1,2,4)\).

## Verification
Run `python verify_ternary_pencil.py` using Python 3 and SymPy. The script exhausts all projective cubic coefficient vectors, enumerates the rational points and lines, checks the 936-cubic blocking orbit by exact projective transforms, tests geometric smoothness via modular Gröbner bases on three affine charts, and checks the four profile counts, the total 312 through \(F\), and the global total 292032. It also checks the explicit complementary generator. No random sampling or search cutoff on field extensions is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli, Ghioca, and Yip exhibit a smooth blocking cubic over \(\mathbb F_3\) [1] and study bounds for blocking and nonblocking members of pencils over finite fields [2]. The uniqueness bound here is a short consequence of their disjointness and nontrivial-blocking-set ingredients together with the Hasse bound. Their constructions of pencils of nonblocking hypersurfaces [3] have a different condition. The same single-cubic orbit is recorded separately in [4] and is not claimed as a new classification here. A census by rational base-point count [5] counts a broader class without selecting a blocking member. The contribution here is the pencil incidence count with a blocking member and the exact distribution of the three other members' point counts. Neither a single-cubic orbit count nor a total over all pencils with zero rational base points determines these incidence data.

## Limitations
This concerns pencils over \(\mathbb F_3\), with four geometrically smooth rational plane cubic members and no rational base point. It does not concern pencils over larger fields or the smoothness of nonrational members. The numerical census uses exhaustive exact computation; the upper bound is independent of that computation.
