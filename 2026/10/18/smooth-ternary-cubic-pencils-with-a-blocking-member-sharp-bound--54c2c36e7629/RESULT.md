# Smooth ternary cubic pencils with a blocking member: sharp bound and exact census

## Finding
Let \(\mathscr P\) be the family of pencils of plane cubic curves over \(\mathbb F_3\) with no common \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational point, such that each of the four \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational pencil members is geometrically smooth. A member is *blocking* when its rational points meet every rational line of \(\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\). Every pencil in \(\mathscr P\) contains **at most one** blocking member; the bound is attained. Exactly **292032** pencils in \(\mathscr P\) contain such a member.

There are exactly **936** smooth blocking cubic equations modulo nonzero scalar, forming a single orbit under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\). For any fixed blocking cubic, exactly **312** pencils in \(\mathscr P\) contain it. The three remaining cubic members have sorted rational-point counts in exactly the following profiles:

| Sorted counts of other three fibers | Number of pencils through the fixed blocking cubic |
| --- | ---: |
| \((1,1,5)\) | 24 |
| \((1,2,4)\) | 132 |
| \((1,3,3)\) | 42 |
| \((2,2,3)\) | 114 |

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are projective plane cubics, defined by nonzero homogeneous degree-three forms over \(\mathbb F_3\). Two forms differing by a nonzero scalar define the same curve. A pencil is a two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_3\)-linear subspace of the ten-dimensional cubic-form space, and has exactly four rational members. The no-rational-base-point condition means no \(P\in\mathbb P^2(\mathbb F_3)\) lies on all four members. **It does not require the geometric base locus over the algebraic closure to be empty** (indeed, distinct cubic plane curves intersect geometrically). Geometric smoothness means the three partial derivatives have no common projective zero over an algebraic closure. The count concerns rationally defined pencils as subspaces, not pencils modulo projective coordinate transformations.

## Proof
**1. The sharp upper bound.** The ternary projective plane has thirteen rational points and thirteen rational lines, with four points per line. Every smooth plane cubic over \(\mathbb F_3\) has at least one rational point: the genus-one Hasse bound yields \(\#C(\mathbb F_3)\ge4-2\sqrt3>0\). A geometrically smooth cubic has no line component, so Bézout's theorem prevents all four rational points on any rational line from lying on it. Every blocking subset of the ternary plane without an entire line has at least six points (alternatively, the exact thirteen-point incidence enumeration in the verifier establishes this elementary finite statement). Distinct rational pencil members cannot share a rational point, by the no-rational-base-point assumption: any point on two members lies on every linear combination. Hence, if two of the four members were blocking, they would use at least twelve disjoint rational points, leaving at most one point to distribute among the remaining two smooth members, each of which requires at least one rational point. Contradiction.

**2. Exactly 936 smooth blocking cubics.** For every nonzero ternary cubic coefficient vector, normalize its first nonzero coefficient to one; there are \((3^{10}-1)/2=29524\) such vectors. Evaluate at all thirteen rational projective points. A form has a rational line component exactly when it vanishes on all four points of that line: the restriction is a degree-three binary form and four projective roots force the zero restriction. The exact ten-coordinate enumeration finds 936 blocking vectors with no such line. For the explicit cubic
\[
F=X^3+Y^3+Z^3-(X+Y+Z)(XY+XZ+YZ)+XYZ,
\]
computing Gröbner bases for the ideals generated by \(F\), its partial derivatives, and each affine-chart equation \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), or \(Z=1\) gives the unit ideal, so \(F\) is geometrically smooth. Enumerating invertible projective \(3\times3\) coordinate changes yields exactly 936 distinct transforms of \(F\) among the 936 candidates. Equality of projective cubic forms is detected by their point-evaluation vectors because a ternary cubic of degree three that vanishes on every \(\mathbb F_3\)-point is the zero form (the first possible nonzero degree of the projective evaluation ideal is four). Thus the 936 candidates form one smooth orbit; no additional smooth blocking cubics exist, as a smooth cubic cannot have a rational line component.

**3. Enumeration of rational-base-point-free smooth pencils through \(F\).** A pencil through the fixed \(F\) may be specified by a projective cubic \(G\) linearly independent from \(F\). Its other three projective members are \([G]\), \([G+F]\), and \([G-F]\). The pencil has no rational common base point exactly when \(G(P)\ne0\) at each of the six rational points of \(F\). Enumerate all 29524 normalized projective forms \(G\), discard those failing this basepoint test, then discard candidates for which any of the three other members is geometrically singular. Geometric smoothness is tested by exact Gröbner calculations in all three standard affine charts, not merely by counting rational singular points. An optional exact rational-singularity prefilter speeds up the computation but does not affect its completeness. Exactly 936 normalized \(G\) pass every test. Their sorted rational-point-profile frequencies are, respectively,
\[
(1,1,5):72,\qquad(1,2,4):396,\qquad(1,3,3):126,\qquad(2,2,3):342.
\]
Each pencil through \(F\) is counted three times, once for each of its other members chosen as \(G\). Division by three gives the four reported profile frequencies, totaling 312. Every pencil in the global class contains exactly one blocking cubic by step 1, so the number of such pencils is exactly
\[
936\cdot312=292032.
\]

**4. Nonemptiness.** A concrete generator complementary to \(F\) is \(G=X^2Z+Y^3+YZ^2\). Exact chart smoothness tests certify that \(F\), \(G\), \(G+F\), and \(G-F\) are geometrically smooth, with empty rational common base locus. The three nonblocking members have sorted rational-point profile \((1,2,4)\).

## Verification
Run `python verify_ternary_pencil.py` using Python 3 and SymPy. The self-contained script exhausts all nonzero projective cubic coefficient vectors; enumerates the rational points and lines; proves the 936-cubic blocking orbit by exact projective transforms; checks algebraic smoothness via modular Gröbner bases on three affine charts; enumerates all complementary pencil generators; and asserts the four profile counts, the total 312, and the global total 292032. Every polynomial arithmetic operation is exact over \(\mathbb F_3\); no random sampling or finite extension search cutoff is used. The explicit pencil is independently checked by the same exact chart tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli, Ghioca, and Yip's work on blocking plane curves exhibits an exceptional smooth blocking cubic over \(\mathbb F_3\), but does not count smooth rational-point-basefree pencils through it [1]. Their study of the proportion of blocking curves in pencils provides general lower bounds for the number of nonblocking members [2]; the present result gives a sharp maximum and exact full pencil census for the constrained four-smooth-member ternary cubic case. Their constructions of pencils of nonblocking hypersurfaces [3] have a different condition and do not imply this smooth-pencil profile classification. The single-cubic projective-orbit enumeration, by itself, does not imply the additional 312-pencil incidence census.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to \(\mathbb F_3\), plane degree three, geometrically smooth rational pencil members, and the absence of *rational* base points. It makes no assertion about pencils whose members become singular over an algebraic extension, about pencils over larger fields, or about nonrational members of the parameter projective line. The new numerical census is computationally proved via an exhaustive, inspectable exact verifier; the upper bound is independent of that census. Literature coverage may be incomplete, especially for differently indexed classical finite-geometry tables.
