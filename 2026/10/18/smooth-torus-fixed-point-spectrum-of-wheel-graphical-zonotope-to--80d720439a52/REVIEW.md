# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies maximal cones with acyclic wheel orientations and uses the exact integral criterion that the Hasse graph is a spanning tree. In the mixed-spoke case it forces crossing edges, accounts for all cover relations, and counts each unique-extremum run with weight equal to its length. The cyclic pointing argument gives the factor \(m/r\), and the coefficient algebra produces the displayed closed form. Uniform-spoke cases require precisely one transitive rim shortcut, counted by \(2m\) in each direction. The symbolic argument covers all integers \(m\ge3\) and every algebraically closed base field; the packaged finite checker tests the independent enumeration for \(3\le m\le9\). Risk: the computer checks do not themselves certify the infinite statement; the run/cycle decomposition is the critical hand proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Postnikov–Reiner–Williams supplies the tree-poset criterion and classifies graphs whose **entire** zonotope is simple (Sections 3, 5); these are accepted as prior results. Grujić computes face numbers, not smoothness of individually indexed vertex cones. Iriarte's accessible 2014 preprint addresses orientation ideals and non-crossing trees and gives no wheel smooth-point count. A 2015 thesis covering broader graphical-arrangement ideas was identified, but its full PDF was inaccessible, leaving possible equivalent-language overlap. The searched indexed mathematical results found related wheel symmetric-edge and cycle graphical-zonotope invariants, but not a dominating theorem. This is a qualified comparison, not a claim of universal uniqueness. Risk: an equivalent enumerative formula may be hidden in unindexed work or the inaccessible thesis.

## Value — PASS
A nonsimple but natural infinite graphical-zonotope family needs quantitative information on where its associated toric variety is regular. The theorem specifies an exact intrinsic local smoothness invariant, its entire spoke-stratified spectrum, the smooth-to-singular fixed-point transition after the complete-graph case, and an exponential rarity law. These are not merely a restatement of the global simplicity criterion or of the ordinary face vector. The full singular locus and nonfixed orbits are explicitly outside scope.
