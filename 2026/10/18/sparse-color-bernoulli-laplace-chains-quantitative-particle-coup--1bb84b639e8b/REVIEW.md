# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The uniform finite-time total-variation estimate is derived by sequentially coupling the indicators of a fixed-size random subset to independent Bernoulli indicators. The proof explicitly records the one-step error for either urn, accumulates errors over time, couples the hypergeometric stationary law to a binomial, and uses contraction under projection. The exact independent-particle transition formula has eigenvalue \(\rho\) and handles negative values. The Gaussian profile follows from the triangular-array Bernoulli lattice local central limit theorem for the cutoff window, plus an endpoint Chebyshev argument and temporal contraction for the earlier and later ranges. Exact-rational finite-state computations (1,680 configurations) agree with the quantitative inequality. They do not certify infinite asymptotics.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual risk.** The closest same-model result, Griffin et al. (arXiv:2308.08676v1, Assumption 1.1, Theorem 1.4 and conclusion), requires a positive limiting red density and provides a cutoff window, not the sparse-minority exact Gaussian profile. Alameda et al. (arXiv:2203.08647, Theorem 1.2) use half-red balanced urns. Olesker-Taylor and Schmid (arXiv:2409.07900v1, Theorem A and proof sections) treat a one-pair-per-step urn with a different clock and spectrum. Other formulations based on tagged-particle asymptotic independence and classical sampling-without-replacement coupling could contain an equivalent result, and exhaustive historical priority is not proved. The present finite-time inequality and sparse simultaneous-swap limit do not follow directly from the inspected results.

**Value — PASS.** The sparse-color case is outside the positive-density hypotheses of a standard macroscopic-swap cutoff theorem. The finding identifies the correct mixing scale, gives the complete lattice-aware worst-start TV profile, and offers a nonasymptotic, checkable approximation. The dependence on red count rather than population size has an interpretable stochastic mechanism. This is not merely a reparametrization of the balanced result.

**Limitations.** The condition \(r^2\log r=o(N)\) is sufficient rather than claimed optimal. The zero-eigenvalue and dense-minority cases are excluded. The all-time error can be large. No independent assessment or historical-priority certification has been performed.
