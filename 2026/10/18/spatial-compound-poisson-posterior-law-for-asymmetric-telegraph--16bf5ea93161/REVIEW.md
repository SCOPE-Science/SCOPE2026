# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The resolvent of the two-state velocity chain yields an explicit signed two-sided exponential density for the time-discounted terminal location. Partial fractions independently identify both positive spatial decay rates and their amplitudes. Both amplitude ratios are Laplace transforms of strictly nonnegative probability measures through the two Bessel-Laplace identities; each spatial decay increment is the Laplace exponent of a finite-activity compound-Poisson process with an explicit positive measure. This establishes the stated posterior convolution identity for every positive choice of the rates and unequal positive speed magnitudes, not just tested parameters. The exact ballistic atom, stochastic ordering and affine moments follow by differentiation and stationary independent increments. A standalone script independently compares the resolvent with Bessel-density integration, including ballistic atoms.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The motivating Orsingher–Marchione paper (arXiv:2610.15094v1, especially Equation (48)) calculates the terminal joint position/time density for **symmetric** speeds and does not assert a compound-Poisson factorization of time posteriors. Ratanov (arXiv:2102.04905v1, Equations (1.4)–(1.7)) treats **asymmetric** fixed-time telegraph propagators and a distinct first-passage problem; these kernels can be used to derive a resolvent but do not state the new posterior spatial convolution theorem. Cinque (arXiv:2202.01904v1) gives velocity/switch-count conditional laws rather than posterior stopping-time ordering. The present contribution is not a new finite-time propagator or a new Bessel identity: it is the positive spatial Lévy-kernel decomposition and uniform convolution/stochastic-order statement, for unequal speeds as well as equal ones. The classical fixed-time kernels constitute serious background coverage, and a broad unpublished or overlooked Markov-additive factorization could affect priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem naturally arises when the endpoint of a randomly terminated finite-speed trajectory is observed but its stopping time is hidden. Direct joint-density formulas obscure comparisons of posterior elapsed times at different observations. The theorem supplies a constructive coupling and **complete** sign-specific stochastic ordering for all terminal distances, and exact affine formulas for both posterior mean and variance, using the same jump mechanism in both directions despite asymmetric speeds. This is a structural inference property with implications for conditional path-duration uncertainty, beyond calculating an isolated moment or rewriting a known parameter.

## Scope and limitations
Equal switching rates, independent exponential termination, equiprobable initial velocities and opposite-sign constant speeds are essential to the stated formulas. No claims are made for direction-dependent killing, general renewal times or more than two velocities. The numerical checks support, but do not prove, the analytic theorem. The relevant exact fixed-time literature is inspected; broader Markov-modulated fluid/resolvent factorization remains a scientific priority risk. Independent evaluation is outstanding.
