# Scientific review: Spatial Poisson errors and the sharp exact-ordering transition for exponential races

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns independent rate-parameterized exponential clocks, arbitrary positive rates whose neighboring ratios converge uniformly after multiplication by \(n\), and a continuous nonnegative limit profile. The proof establishes the exact pairwise comparison law and the \(nR_n^2/(1-R_n)\) bound for any nonadjacent inversion. That event bound is sufficient to identify the **whole** ranking permutation, not merely its inversion count. A direct exponential-ordering product bounds every overlapping run of adjacent errors by \(R_n\) to the power of its length; disjoint runs depend on disjoint clocks. The resulting dominated inclusion–exclusion calculation identifies the full limiting point-process Laplace functional. The geometric-rate exact-order probability follows from the Plackett–Luce sequential race rule, while the supercritical vanishing result follows from a disjoint subfamily of adjacent comparisons. Boundary cases of zero intensity, variable rates, and divergent scaled geometric ratios are included explicitly.

The checker performs 340 independent exact-rational finite assertions for the full finite permutation distribution, pairwise/overlapping comparison probabilities, disjoint-comparison independence, the identity-ranking products, and the long-inversion union bound. Its finite computations support the basic identities; the infinite point-process theorem relies on the supplied argument. No step treats a finite test as proof of convergence.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Turvey's *Permutations from ranking independent random variables* (arXiv:2610.11895v1; first posted 2026-10-08), inspected in full-text HTML, studies semialgebraic dimensions of attainable order laws and excludes exact nontrivial Mallows laws. It does not give Poisson error locations, the disjoint-transposition approximation, or an exact-order recovery cutoff for exponentially distributed variables. Babson et al., *Models of random spanning trees* (arXiv:2407.20226; author-hosted full PDF inspected), establish finite-dimensional product-measure ranking tools motivated by spanning trees; no matching growing-size rare-error theorem was found in its relevant sections. Guiver and Snelson's 2009 Plackett–Luce paper (full PDF, §§1–2 inspected) gives the finite ordering product, which is explicitly credited, not presented as new. Harry Joe's 2000 abstract describes general ordering inequalities rather than the asserted asymptotics; full text was not obtained and this remains a limited comparison.

Queries on adjacent ranking inversions, high-signal exponential races, spatial Poisson errors, geometric rate ordering, Mallows equivalence, and exact ranking recovery found no directly covering statement in the searched mathematical sources or earlier available claim records. Closest published records treat three-score ordering polytopes, heterogeneous order-marginal identifiability, proportional-hazards marginals, and a Poisson–Gumbel instability in a different Gaussian MIMO model. These do not imply the claim: the objects, dependencies, and parameters differ. Search absence alone is not proof of priority. Older unindexed rare-utility-ranking results, or a general approximation theorem specialized to this race, remain possible competing antecedents.

## Value — PASS

The primary question is how an entire random order fails under strong but imperfect separation of independent exponential rates. The result answers it with a spatial point-process law of inversion locations and a probabilistically complete *permutation-level* error description. The exact geometric-rate identity simultaneously produces a three-regime ordering-recovery cutoff and a Gumbel transition profile. The inhomogeneous intensity adds more information than a scalar Poisson approximation and allows unequal neighboring signal gaps. These are natural statistics of ranking reliability, not an arbitrary finite-parameter computation.

The theorem does not address nonexponential clocks, moderate separation, or optimal convergence rates. It is not claimed to supersede general Poisson-approximation principles or earlier work on finite Plackett–Luce likelihoods. The novelty judgment remains conditional on the available published evidence.
