# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The candidate-edge families have respectively \(n\) and \(2n\) members per fixed deficiency, with uniformly bounded overlap degree. Exact disjoint-pairing probabilities establish all marked factorial moments. Conditioning on any fixed collection of these rare edges deletes finitely many chords and can alter total crossings by only \(O(n)\), negligible compared with the established \(n^{3/2}\) crossing scale. This proves the required *joint* independence rather than inferring it from marginal laws. Exact enumeration verifies the finite covariance and moment relations for all matchings through seven chords. The nonstandard independence argument is proved explicitly, not delegated to computation.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** The nearest original source, Acan (arXiv:1501.01489, Theorem 4.2), treats fixed **small** chord lengths, not near-diametral moving lengths or joint Gaussian independence. Flajolet and Noy (INRIA RR-3914, Theorem 3) give the already-known crossing marginal. Paguyo (arXiv:2104.01134) studies individual crossing and simple-chord approximation. Sullivan (arXiv:1611.02771) studies a different linear minimum-length constraint. Relevant statements and their full primary sections were inspected, not merely titles. A broader unlocated matching theorem could still imply this joint result; lack of a matching database hit is not proof of historical priority.

**Value — PASS.** Maximum possible *circular* chord length is a natural geometric extremal statistic of a uniform chord diagram, and the precise deficiency law distinguishes antipodal chords (limiting intensity \(1/2\)) from every neighboring length (intensity \(1\)). Spatial marking resolves where extreme chords appear; joint independence specifies how conditioning on such rare geometry affects global crossing fluctuations. No collection of the previously known separate marginal laws entails that conditional statement. The finite covariance identity additionally shows an order-\(n\) residual finite-size dependence, clarifying why the asymptotic independence is scale-sensitive rather than literal.

**Limitations.** Only fixed finitely many near-diametral levels are covered; there is no growing-window theorem or finite-sample rate. The nontriviality is the *joint* marked/extreme/global conclusion, not the individual Gaussian or any simple Poisson marginal. Independent historical-priority assessment remains appropriate.
