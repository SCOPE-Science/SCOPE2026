# Sperner-optimal compression of positive graded modal stars and their exact two-variable spectrum

## Finding
For each integer \(m\ge1\) and grade bound \(k\ge1\), take the positive graded modal language over propositional letters \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\), constants \(\top,\bot\), connectives \(\wedge,\vee\), and graded diamonds \(\langle\ge r\rangle\) for \(1\le r\le k\). On the class of finite, nonreflexive, rooted Kripke stars with terminal successors, **every pointed model has an equivalent induced rooted substar with at most**
\[
 k\binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\]
terminal successors. This is the **least possible uniform upper bound**, including when arbitrary new stars rather than induced substars are allowed as representatives. Every formula is equivalent over this class to one of modal depth at most one.

For the first nontrivial label interaction, \(m=2\), let \(a,b,c,d\) count successors with valuation respectively \(00,10,01,11\). Two rooted stars satisfy exactly the same formulas of this language if and only if their root valuations agree and their following five truncated counts agree:
\[
\begin{split}
S_k(a,b,c,d)=(&\min(k,d),\min(k,b+d),\min(k,c+d),\\
&\min(k,b+c+d),\min(k,a+b+c+d)).
\end{split}
\]
The exact number of successor-profile equivalence classes is
\[
 N_k=\binom{k+3}{4}+\binom{k+2}{3}+\binom{k+3}{3},
\]
and the exact number of pointed semantic theories, including the four possible root valuations, is \(4N_k\). For \(k=1,2,3,4,5\), these numbers are respectively \(24,76,180,360,644\). A profile may require exactly \(2k\) successors, so the general width theorem is sharp already at two propositional letters.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite rooted star is a Kripke model with one distinguished root \(w\), a finite set \(L\) of distinct terminal worlds, and precisely the edges \(wR\ell\) for \(\ell\in L\). There are no loops or edges from any terminal world. Each world's atomic valuation is an arbitrary subset of the \(m\) propositional letters. The formula \(\langle\ge r\rangle\varphi\) holds at \(w\) exactly if at least \(r\) distinct successors satisfy \(\varphi\). Grade indices are fixed integers \(1\le r\le k\) throughout a formula, while nesting is unrestricted. An **induced rooted substar** retains the root, an arbitrary subset of terminal worlds, all retained valuations, and the incident root edges. Two models are logically equivalent when their roots agree on every formula in this language; equality of their root valuations is therefore necessary. Neither classical negation, graded boxes, universal modalities, nor cycles are included. The language is explicitly **constant-enriched**: \(\top\) is an essential test of total successor count, not an assumed abbreviation in an otherwise negation-free grammar.

## Proof
**1. Boolean reduction at terminal worlds.** Every modal subformula is false at a terminal world because grade indices are positive. Structural induction on formulas then reduces the truth of any subformula at a terminal world to a positive propositional expression (possibly a constant). Its satisfying valuations form an upset of the Boolean lattice \((2^{[m]},\subseteq)\). Consequently every modal formula at the root is determined by its atomic root valuation and, for each upset \(U\subseteq 2^{[m]}\), the count \(\min(k,c(U))\), where \(c(U)\) counts successors whose true-letter set lies in \(U\). Conversely, every upset is definable by a finite disjunction of conjunctions of atoms corresponding to its minimal elements (the empty upset by \(\bot\), the full upset by \(\top\)). For every \(1\le r\le k\), the graded formula testing that disjunction determines whether \(c(U)\ge r\). Thus these truncated upset counts and the root valuation exactly classify full theories; and formulas agree with explicit Boolean combinations of depth-zero root tests and depth-one graded upset tests. Depth one is sharp for \(k\ge1\): an empty star and a one-successor star with the same root atoms are distinguished by \(\langle\ge1\rangle\top\) but not by depth-zero formulas.

**2. A chain decomposition attaining the uniform bound.** A symmetric-chain decomposition partitions the Boolean lattice \(2^{[m]}\) into precisely \(b_m=\binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) inclusion chains (the classical Sperner width). For completeness, one can construct it inductively: from a chain \(S_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_t\) on \(m\) coordinates, make one chain \(S_0,\ldots,S_t,S_t\cup\{m+1\}\), and when \(t\ge1\), a second chain \(S_0\cup\{m+1\},\ldots,S_{t-1}\cup\{m+1\}\). These chains partition the expanded lattice, and the rank endpoints remain complementary around the middle rank; hence their count equals the size of the middle rank. For each chain, order its terminal successors by inclusion of their valuations, and retain the **last** \(\min(k,n_i)\) copies, where \(n_i\) is the total number of terminal successors on that chain. Any upset \(U\) intersects the chain in a suffix, hence the retained number inside \(U\) is exactly \(\min(k,c_i(U))\), where \(c_i(U)\) is the original number there. Summing over chains preserves the globally truncated count because
\[
\min\bigl(k,\sum_i \min(k,c_i(U))\bigr)=\min\bigl(k,\sum_i c_i(U)\bigr).
\]
Thus all formulas are preserved, and the retained number of terminal worlds is at most \(kb_m\).

**3. Sharpness for every number of letters.** Put \(r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and form a rooted star with **exactly** \(k\) successors at each valuation \(S\subseteq[m]\) satisfying \(|S|=r\), and no other successors. Let another star have the same pointed positive graded theory. For each such \(S\), the sentence-at-root \(\langle\ge k\rangle\bigwedge_{i\in S}p_i\) is true (interpreting the empty conjunction as \(\top\)). Every \((r+1)\)-element set \(T\subseteq[m]\) satisfies \(\neg\bigl(\langle\ge1\rangle\bigwedge_{i\in T}p_i\bigr)\) **as a semantic observation**, not as a formula in the positive language: in other words each indicated positive graded formula is false. Therefore the comparison star has no successor with more than \(r\) true atoms. To satisfy the first family of graded tests, it needs at least \(k\) successors of valuation **exactly** \(S\), separately for every middle-layer \(S\). Hence it has at least \(k\binom{m}{r}\) successors. In particular this lower bound holds for arbitrary equivalent replacement models within the star class, not just deletions.

**4. Two-letter five-test normal form.** The only upsets of the two-letter valuation lattice are \(\varnothing\), \(\{11\}\), \(\{10,11\}\), \(\{01,11\}\), \(\{10,01,11\}\), and the full set. Their definers are \(\bot,p\wedge q,p,q,p\vee q,\top\). The first has zero count; the remaining five yield exactly the displayed vector \(S_k\). The argument in paragraph 1 proves necessity and sufficiency of equality of the root valuation and this vector.

**5. Exact enumeration of two-letter signatures.** Split signatures according to the fifth component \(t=\min(k,a+b+c+d)\). When \(t<k\), all four raw counts are uniquely recovered and their total is below \(k\), contributing \(\binom{k+3}{4}\) possibilities. When \(t=k\), put \(s=\min(k,b+c+d)\). If \(s<k\), the three raw counts \(b,c,d\) are uniquely recovered with sum below \(k\), and the count \(a\) can be chosen to make \(t=k\); there are \(\binom{k+2}{3}\) possibilities. It remains to count \(s=k\). Fix the first component \(x=\min(k,d)\). If \(x=k\), all other displayed components equal \(k\), giving one profile. For \(0\le x<k\), let \(M=k-x\). The second and third components \(y,z\in\{x,\ldots,k\}\) can take arbitrary pairs with at least one equal to \(k\) (\(2M+1\) pairs), or both smaller than \(k\) with \((y-x)+(z-x)\ge M\) (\(M(M-1)/2\) pairs). This totals \(\binom{M+2}{2}\) choices for each \(M=1,\ldots,k\); including \(x=k\) adds the \(M=0\) term. The hockey-stick identity gives \(\sum_{M=0}^k\binom{M+2}{2}=\binom{k+3}{3}\). Adding all three disjoint cases proves the formula for \(N_k\). The four root valuations occur independently, giving \(4N_k\).

## Verification
An executable standard-library Python checker is included at `artifacts/verify.py`. Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py` from the extracted package root. It independently enumerates all capped two-letter count signatures for \(1\le k\le10\), verifies the formula in each case, implements and tests a symmetric-chain decomposition for \(0\le m\le4\), checks every upper set for random and boundary multiplicity configurations, checks the explicit two-letter deletion construction for \(1\le k\le6\), tests the sharpness witnesses through \(k=100\), and independently evaluates 240 randomly generated nested positive graded formulas on each of 625 count configurations and all four root valuations for \(k=3\). The core computations gave `SPERNER_WIDTH_SHARP_COMPRESSION_OK 27860`, `SHARP_CORE_UPPER_BOUND_OK 15235`, `SHARP_CORE_LOWER_BOUND_OK 100`, `NESTED_POSITIVE_GRADED_EQUIVALENCE_OK 600000`, and `POSITIVE_GRADED_STAR_CLASSIFICATION_OK`. These are finite checks; the all-size theorem follows from the preceding proofs and does not depend on finite extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Wałęga and Cuenca Grau's 2026 preservation theorem identifies existential-positive graded modal logic with preservation under injective homomorphisms for bounded-unravelling-invariant classes. Their Theorem 13 and Lemma 12 (arXiv:2602.01856) give qualitative characteristic-formula machinery over finite structures; no quantitative \(k\)-truncated star-spectrum formula or sharp Sperner-width successor budget is stated in the inspected text. Their June 2026 follow-up (arXiv:2606.17882, Theorem 22) develops related preservation and graph-neural-network expressiveness results, also without the present exact star calculation in the inspected accessible full text. The integer sequence \(1,6,19,45,90,161,\ldots\) occurs independently in OEIS A005712; **the sequence and its polynomial enumeration as numbers are not claimed new**. The proposed contribution is a modal-semantic interpretation and an optimal rooted-star compression theorem. Symmetric-chain decompositions, Sperner's width theorem and binomial counting identities are classical tools, not discoveries here. Prior general normal-form work may entail this specialisation after additional reasoning, and historical priority is not guaranteed.

## Limitations
The result concerns finite **nonreflexive one-step stars**, not arbitrary finite Kripke frames or deeper trees. It excludes negation, boxes, counting thresholds above \(k\), multiple accessibility relations, and modal fixed points. The exact polynomial semantic-type formula is established only for two propositional letters; the sharp compression bound holds for every finite positive number of propositional letters. There is no claim of an optimal algorithm for translating arbitrary formulas or of a new integer sequence. The primary 2026 sources study much broader qualitative preservation questions; no general theorem on GNNs follows solely from this result. Historical priority is not certified.
