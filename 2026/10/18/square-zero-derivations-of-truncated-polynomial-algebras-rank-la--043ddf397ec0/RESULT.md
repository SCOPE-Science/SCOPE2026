# Square-zero derivations of truncated polynomial algebras: rank laws and characteristic-two components

## Finding

Let \(K=\mathbb F_q\) be a finite field of characteristic \(p\), let \(n\ge1\), and let \(A=K[t]/(t^n)\). For ordinary \(K\)-linear Leibniz derivations \(D:A\to A\), define the rank enumerator
\[
P_{n,q}(z)=\sum_{D^2=0}z^{\operatorname{rank}_K D}.
\]
The following formulas determine every coefficient, including the homology distribution \(\dim_K\ker D/\operatorname{im}D=n-2\operatorname{rank}D\).

**Odd characteristic.** For \(p\ne2\), set
\[
a_0=\begin{cases}
 n/2,&2\mid n\ \text{and}\ p\mid n/2,\\
 \lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Then, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{P_{n,q}(z)=1+\sum_{a=a_0}^{n-1}(q-1)q^{n-a-1}z^{n-a-\lfloor(n-a)/p\rfloor},\qquad P_{n,q}(1)=q^{n-a_0}.}
\]
In particular, when \(n\) is even and \(p\mid n/2\), the square-zero locus has an extra valuation layer. It is the linear space \(t^{a_0}A\) under the identification of derivations with their values at \(t\).

**Characteristic two.** Write \(q=2^e\). For every \(m\ge1\), the **complete rank polynomials for two consecutive lengths coincide**:
\[
\boxed{P_{2m,q}(z)=P_{2m+1,q}(z).}
\]
Put \(c_m(j)= (q-1)q^{m-j-1}\) for \(0\le j<m\) and \(c_m(m)=1\). Their common value is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\substack{\lceil m/2\rceil\le b\le m\\0\le a\le m\\a+b\ge m}} c_m(a)c_m(b)z^{m-\min(a,b)}.}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{P_{2m,q}(1)=P_{2m+1,q}(1)=q^m+\lfloor m/2\rfloor(q-1)q^{m-1}.}
\]
Moreover the reduced square-zero locus in each of these two lengths has precisely \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\) irreducible components, **all linear and all of dimension \(m\)**. In even length exactly \((q-1)q^{m-1}\) derivations have zero homology; in odd length none do (because the vector-space dimension is odd). For \(n=1\), there is only the zero derivation.

For example, \(P_{4,2}(z)=P_{5,2}(z)=1+3z+2z^2\) and \(P_{8,2}(z)=P_{9,2}(z)=1+3z+12z^2+8z^3+8z^4\).

## Assumptions and scope

All fields are finite, but the geometric component statement also holds on scalar extension to any algebraically closed field of characteristic two. Derivations satisfy the **ordinary ungraded** Leibniz identity \(D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)\) and the condition \(D^2=0\) is composition as a linear operator, not the square in an associative derivation algebra. The enumerator weights by rank of the linear map underlying \(D\). No grading is imposed. In particular, this is not a classification of square-zero \(A\)-linear differential-module operators.

## Proof

**Step 1: parameterization.** Any derivation is uniquely \(D_f=f(t)\,d/dt\), subject to the single relation \(nt^{n-1}f(t)=0\) in \(A\); this follows by differentiating the defining relation \(t^n=0\). In particular, if \(p\nmid n\), the constant term of \(f\) vanishes. For every integer \(j\ge1\),
\[
D_f(t^j)=j t^{j-1}f,\qquad
D_f^2(t^j)=j(j-1)t^{j-2}f^2+jt^{j-1}ff'.
\]
These identities are also valid at the truncation boundary when interpreted in \(A\).

**Step 2: odd characteristic.** When \(p\ne2\), the conditions \(D_f^2(t)=D_f^2(t^2)=0\) imply \(ff'=0\) and \(f^2=0\) for \(n\ge3\), and the displayed identity makes them sufficient. For \(n=1,2\), direct use of \(t^n=0\) yields the same final enumeration. Let \(f\ne0\) have \(t\)-adic order \(a\). The equation \(f^2=0\) says \(2a\ge n\). When \(p\nmid a\), the leading coefficient of \(ff'\) is nonzero in degree \(2a-1\), so \(ff'=0\) additionally says \(2a-1\ge n\). When \(p\mid a\), differentiation raises the order enough and \(2a\ge n\) suffices. Thus the allowed orders are exactly \(a_0\le a<n\). There are \((q-1)q^{n-a-1}\) choices of \(f\) at order \(a\). Their images \(D_f(t^j)\), for \(1\le j\le n-a\) and \(p\nmid j\), have distinct leading exponents, while other images vanish. Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}D_f=n-a-\lfloor (n-a)/p\rfloor.
\]
Summing gives the odd-characteristic formula and shows that the zero locus is the specified vector subspace.

**Step 3: even length in characteristic two.** Let \(n=2m\), \(s=t^2\), \(B=K[s]/(s^m)\), so \(A=B\oplus tB\). Write \(f=u(s)+t v(s)\), uniquely with \(u,v\in B\); every \(f\) defines a derivation because \(n=0\) in \(K\). Here \(f'=v\), and \(D_f^2\) is itself a derivation in characteristic two. Therefore \(D_f^2=0\) if and only if \(ff'=0\), equivalently to the two equations
\[
uv=0,\qquad v^2=0\qquad \text{in }B.
\]
Let \(\nu(0)=m\) and \(\nu(w)\) be the \(s\)-adic order of \(w\ne0\) in \(B\). Put \(a=\nu(u)\), \(b=\nu(v)\). The two equations are precisely \(a+b\ge m\) and \(b\ge\lceil m/2\rceil\). Exactly \(c_m(a)c_m(b)\) pairs have these orders. The map \(D_f\) kills all even powers of \(t\), and its image is \(Bf\), generated by its values on odd powers. Its \(K\)-dimension is \(m-\min(a,b)\). This proves the even-length coefficient formula.

**Step 4: odd length and parity pairing.** Let \(n=2m+1\). The relation \(t^{2m+1}=0\) forces \(f(0)=0\), so every derivation has \(f=s u(s)+t v(s)\), with \(u,v\in B\). Its square vanishes precisely when \(suv=0\) modulo \(s^{m+1}\) and \(tv^2=0\) modulo \(t^{2m+1}\), exactly the same conditions \(uv=v^2=0\) in \(B\). Its image is spanned by \(s^jf\), \(0\le j<m\), and has dimension \(m-\min(\nu(u),\nu(v))\). Consequently the explicit linear bijection
\[
u(s)+t v(s)\ \longleftrightarrow\ s u(s)+t v(s)
\]
between even- and odd-length parameters restricts to square-zero derivations and preserves rank. This proves the asserted parity identity.

**Step 5: geometry and totals.** For each integer \(b\in[\lceil m/2\rceil,m]\), define the linear subspace
\[
L_b=s^{m-b}B\oplus t s^b B\subset B\oplus tB.
\]
The square-zero locus is exactly their union. Every \(L_b\) has dimension \(b+(m-b)=m\), and distinct \(L_b\)'s are incomparable: as \(b\) increases one summand enlarges and the other shrinks. They are therefore exactly the irreducible components of the reduced locus. The odd-length linear bijection from Step 4 transfers these components. To count points, \(v=0\) permits \(q^m\) choices of \(u\); for each nonzero \(v\) of exact order \(b\ge\lceil m/2\rceil\), the equation \(uv=0\) permits \(q^b\) choices of \(u\). For each such \(b<m\), this yields \((q-1)q^{m-b-1}q^b=(q-1)q^{m-1}\) pairs. There are \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) possible nonzero orders. Finally, rank \(m\) occurs exactly when \(u\) is a unit, which forces \(v=0\); this gives \((q-1)q^{m-1}\) zero-homology derivations in even length. For odd length a square-zero operator cannot be acyclic by parity.

## Relationship to prior work

Maya Banks, *Boij–Söderberg Conjectures for Differential Modules* (2022, arXiv:2212.03794v1), studies square-zero module differentials and their homological invariants, principally with linearity over the coefficient ring. Ordinary Leibniz derivations are not module-linear; the finite-field rank census and component structure proved here do not follow from the inspected differential-module results.

Alexander Premet, *Regular derivations of truncated polynomial rings* (2014, arXiv:1405.2426v3), studies invariant theory and regularity for restricted Witt derivation algebras in characteristic greater than two over algebraically closed fields. Its normality and regular-element theorems do not give the individual operator-square-zero rank polynomial, the characteristic-two component decomposition, or the parity pairing.

Cong Chen, *Nilpotent varieties of some finite dimensional restricted Lie algebras* (2019, arXiv:1910.00954v1), studies nilpotent varieties of restricted Lie algebras, including Cartan-type constructions and assumptions of odd characteristic. Ordinary square-zero Leibniz derivations form a specific smaller operator locus; the finite-field rank counts and characteristic-two geometry are not established by the portions examined.

## Limitations

The geometric assertion concerns the underlying reduced variety, not reducedness of the scheme defined by the quadratic equations. The result does not classify conjugacy orbits under algebra automorphisms, nor graded-commutative differentials. Exhaustive checks cover only the stated finite cases. Although literature and research-database comparisons found no statement implying the rank-refined formulas and parity law, equivalent descriptions in older modular-Witt, finite-group-scheme, or \(\alpha_2\)-action literature are a residual originality risk.
