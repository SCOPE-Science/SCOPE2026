# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A complete coefficient expansion establishes the exact system of four matrix equations. On the nonzero rank-one nilpotent reduction orbit, two kernel matrices anticommute to a scalar, which always lies in the image of the anticommutator map, leaving six free coefficients. The zero-reduction stratum is exactly the product of the four-dimensional unconstrained highest coefficient with the two-by-two anticommuting-pair locus. In odd characteristic the four classes of first coefficient matrices have kernel dimensions \(4,2,1,0\); in characteristic two, scalar versus nonscalar matrices have commutants of dimensions \(4,2\). Locally trivial constant-rank linear solution bundles justify the Grothendieck class calculation and dimensions. The standalone exact checker enumerates the full solution sets over the prime fields with two and three elements and anticommuting subproblems over two, three, five and seven. These finite tests are corroboration, not proof of arbitrary-field conclusions.

## Originality — PASS, with uncertainty

The closest located relevant result of Prasad–Singla–Spallone, arXiv:1212.6157, treats length-two principal local rings and nonprincipal rings with square-zero maximal ideal, whereas this ring has a nonzero second radical layer. Chen–Wang, arXiv:1805.00378, give the geometric theory of the **base** complex anticommuting-pair locus. This base theory is acknowledged as known. The new assertion concerns complete square-zero **matrix lifts** over a nonchain coefficient ring and their characteristic-dependent motivic/finite-field enumeration, not the isolated anti-commuting-pair problem. Ekhad–Zeilberger concern upper triangular finite-field matrices. Published research records and other relevant mathematical results were compared at the level of objects and implication, including earlier square-zero dual-number matrix varieties and short-ring derivation loci, which do not imply this result. Searches do not prove uniqueness, and a possible unlocated exact count remains a risk.

## Value — PASS

An exact count of square-zero endomorphisms of a free module over a finite local complete intersection is a natural arithmetic invariant. This is the smallest nonchain complete intersection having a nonzero second radical layer, where simultaneous first-order restrictions yield a nontrivial lift obstruction. The sharp change of geometric dimension across characteristic two and a unified computation of Grothendieck classes supply more than a numerical recomputation over a small field. The formula determines point counts for all finite extensions simultaneously.

## Scope and limitations

The original zero-scheme need not be reduced; the theorem deliberately studies the reduced support. No component classification, larger-size extension or absolute literature uniqueness is asserted. Similarity classes are not counted. The inspected sources include one relevant later paper as context but the earliest archive-eligible primary source is the 2012 initial submission.
