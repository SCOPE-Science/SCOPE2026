# Squarefree Pfaffian Lie pencils: centralizer strata and Frobenius zeta functions

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \(m\ge2\), and let \(f\in k[t]\) be any monic **squarefree** polynomial of degree \(m\). Construct the two-step nilpotent Lie algebra \(L_f\) of dimension \(2m+2\) described below. Write \(X_f\) for the determinantal subscheme of elements whose adjoint map has rank at most one.

**Theorem.** Over an algebraic closure, \(X_f\) is a reduced union of exactly \(m\) affine four-spaces, any two intersecting precisely in the common two-dimensional centre. Its singular locus is exactly that centre; it has Hilbert series
\[
H_{X_f}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-z)^2}\left(1+m\left(\frac{1}{(1-z)^2}-1\right)\right).
\]
These assertions hold also in characteristic two. If \(f=\prod_{i=1}^h f_i\) is its factorization into distinct monic irreducibles with degrees \(d_i\), then, for \(s_r=\sum_{d_i\mid r}d_i\),
\[
|X_f(\mathbb F_{q^r})|=q^{2r}\bigl(1+s_r(q^{2r}-1)\bigr),\qquad r\ge1,
\]
and its Hasse–Weil zeta function is
\[
\boxed{Z(X_f,T)=\frac{1}{1-q^2T}\prod_{i=1}^h\frac{1-q^{2d_i}T^{d_i}}{1-q^{4d_i}T^{d_i}}.}
\]
Consequently, the sequence of rational-point counts, equivalently this zeta function, uniquely recovers the multiset of irreducible-factor degrees of \(f\). For \(s=s_1\), the complete adjoint-rank enumerator is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{a\in L_f}u^{\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{ad}_a}=q^2\left(1+s(q^2-1)u+\bigl(q^{2m}-1-s(q^2-1)\bigr)u^2\right).}
\]
The element commuting probability is therefore
\[
\boxed{\Pr([a,b]=0)=\frac{q^{2m}+q^2-1+s(q^2-1)(q-1)}{q^{2m+2}}.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Take the companion matrix \(C\in M_m(k)\) of \(f\), with columns \(Ce_j=e_{j+1}\) for \(j<m\) and \(Ce_m=-\sum_{j=1}^m f_{j-1}e_j\), where \(f(t)=t^m+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}f_jt^j\). Put \(V=k^m\oplus k^m\), \(Z=kz_0\oplus kz_1\), and \(L_f=V\oplus Z\). Define the bracket for \((x,y),(x',y')\in V\) by
\[
[(x,y),(x',y')]=(x^{\mathsf T}y'-y^{\mathsf T}x')z_0+
(x^{\mathsf T}C^{\mathsf T}y'-y^{\mathsf T}Cx')z_1,
\]
and set \([Z,L_f]=0\). This is an alternating Lie bracket in every characteristic, and \(Z=[L_f,L_f]=\operatorname{Cent}(L_f)\). The defining ideal of \(X_f\) is generated by the \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors of the \(2\)-by-\((2m+2)\) matrix of \(\operatorname{ad}_a:L_f\to L_f\); equivalently, those of the \(2\)-by-\(2m\) coefficient matrix in the \(Z\) coordinates.

All component and singularity statements are scheme-theoretic for this determinantal scheme (not merely set-theoretic). Rational counts refer to extension fields \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\). No assertion is made for polynomials with repeated factors, for arbitrary non-regular pencils, or for group-theoretic analogues beyond their specified Lie algebra.

## Proof
**1. Lie structure and rank reduction.** On \(V\), let \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I_m\\-I_m&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(S=\operatorname{diag}(C,C^{\mathsf T})\). Then \(J\) is invertible, \(S^{\mathsf T}J=JS\), and the two coordinate forms in the bracket are \(v^{\mathsf T}Jw\) and \(v^{\mathsf T}JSw\). All Jacobi terms vanish since the bracket is central; alternatingness is immediate, including characteristic two. Since \(m\ge2\) and the minimal polynomial of \(C\) is \(f\), the two forms are independent. Thus the derived algebra equals \(Z\). A central vector in \(V\) is annihilated by the nondegenerate first form, hence is zero, so the centre is exactly \(Z\). The alternating-form pencil has matrix \(J(aI_{2m}+bS)\), whose determinant is \(\det(aI_m+bC)^2\). Its Pfaffian is \(\pm\det(aI_m+bC)\), a homogenization of the defining polynomial after reversing the affine parameter; this explains the use of the term Pfaffian pencil in the title, including in characteristic two. The adjoint rank is the rank of the two-row matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}v^{\mathsf T}J\\v^{\mathsf T}JS\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(v\ne0\), its first row is nonzero, and rank at most one means there exists \(\lambda\) with \(v^{\mathsf T}JS=\lambda v^{\mathsf T}J\). Since \(S^{\mathsf T}J=JS\), this is equivalent to \(Sv=\lambda v\). Thus the set of noncentral rank-one points is exactly the eigenvector locus of \(S\).

**2. Geometric eigenplanes.** The squarefree companion matrix \(C\) has exactly \(m\) distinct eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\) over \(\bar k\), each with a one-dimensional eigenspace. The same holds for \(C^{\mathsf T}\). Consequently \(S\) is semisimple and \(V_{\bar k}=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_m\), where every \(E_i=\ker(S-\lambda_i I)\) has dimension two. The rank-at-most-one locus consists of \(Z_{\bar k}\oplus E_i\) for \(1\le i\le m\), with common pairwise intersection \(Z_{\bar k}\).

**3. Scheme-theoretic reducedness.** Multiplying the two-row adjoint matrix on the right by \(J^{-1}\) and making invertible simultaneous coordinate changes does not change its ideal of maximal minors. Over \(\bar k\), a basis diagonalizing \(S\) turns that ideal into the ideal of minors of the matrix whose first row consists of the two coordinates \(v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\) of every \(E_i\), and whose second row has entries \(\lambda_i v_{i,1},\lambda_i v_{i,2}\). Internal minors of one block vanish. A cross-block minor equals \((\lambda_j-\lambda_i)v_{i,a}v_{j,b}\) for distinct \(i,j\). As the eigenvalues differ, the ideal is exactly
\[
I=\bigl(v_{i,a}v_{j,b}:i\ne j,\ a,b\in\{1,2\}\bigr)
 =\bigcap_{i=1}^m (v_{j,1},v_{j,2}:j\ne i).
\]
It is a radical squarefree monomial ideal, and adjoining the free central variables preserves reducedness. Faithfully flat descent then proves reducedness over \(k\). The components, each isomorphic to \(\mathbb A^4\), are all distinct and none contains another.

**4. Singular locus and Hilbert series.** At a point with \(v\in E_i\setminus\{0\}\), localizing at a nonzero \(v_{i,a}\) forces the other blocks to zero, leaving a smooth four-space. At \(v=0\), all minors vanish to order at least two, so the tangent space is the full \((2m+2)\)-dimensional ambient space, while the local dimension is four; since \(m\ge2\), every central point is singular. For each positive degree, the coordinate ring of \(I\) has the direct sum of the monomials supported in a single two-variable block; constants occur only once. This gives \(1+m((1-z)^{-2}-1)\), and the two central variables contribute \((1-z)^{-2}\).

**5. Arithmetic and Frobenius.** The Frobenius acts on the \(m\) geometric eigenplanes exactly as it acts on the roots of \(f\). Distinct eigenspaces intersect only at zero in \(V\). Therefore a nonzero \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\)-rational eigenvector must have an eigenvalue in \(\mathbb F_{q^r}\), and each such eigenvalue yields precisely \(q^{2r}-1\) nonzero vectors. The number of eigenvalues is \(s_r=\sum_{d_i\mid r}d_i\), giving the stated point count after multiplying by the \(q^{2r}\) choices of the central coordinates.

**6. Zeta function and recovery.** By definition, \(Z(X_f,T)=\exp(\sum_{r\ge1}|X_f(\mathbb F_{q^r})|T^r/r)\). For each factor degree \(d\), the identity
\[
\sum_{r\ge1}\mathbf 1_{d\mid r}\,d\,Q^r\frac{T^r}{r}=-\log(1-Q^dT^d)
\]
shows that its additional contribution beyond the central plane is \((1-q^{2d}T^d)/(1-q^{4d}T^d)\). This proves the product formula. Moreover, \(s_r=(|X_f(\mathbb F_{q^r})|/q^{2r}-1)/(q^{2r}-1)\), and Möbius inversion gives the number \(a_d\) of degree-\(d\) irreducible factors as
\[
a_d=\frac1d\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)s_e.
\]
Thus the entire factor-degree multiset is recovered, not the coefficients or individual roots of \(f\).

**7. Rank distribution and commuting probability.** At extension degree one, among the \(q^{2m}\) vectors in \(V\), one has rank zero, \(s(q^2-1)\) have rank one, and the others have rank two. Each has \(q^2\) central translates. For an element of rank \(j\), its Lie centralizer has \(q^{2m+2-j}\) elements. Dividing the resulting sum of commuting pairs by \(q^{4m+4}\) yields the stated probability.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify.py` constructs the actual companion matrix, bracket rows, and rank-one tests using finite-field arithmetic, without assuming the claimed ranks. It exhaustively checks all \(q^{2m}\) noncentral-coordinate possibilities over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_8,\mathbb F_9\) for split and irreducible squarefree polynomials of degrees two and three. It checks the expected root counts, rank enumerators and commuting-pair sums. Run `python3 verify.py`; successful output ends `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests are checks, not a substitute for the proof in all dimensions and extensions.

## Relationship to prior work
Lauret–Oscari (2012, especially Sections 1–3) study two-step nilpotent Lie algebras, nonsingularity, Pfaffian invariants and real classification of two-form pencils. Their paper motivates the natural centre-two Lie family but does not state a finite-field determinantal centralizer scheme, its reducedness and singular locus, or its zeta function. Mackey–Mackey–Mehl–Mehrmann (2012, Theorems 4.2 and 6.2) establish the paired elementary-divisor structure of skew-symmetric matrix polynomials over arbitrary fields. This provides relevant canonical background but not the rank-one scheme or its Frobenius point counts. The current theorem adds a separate determinantal and arithmetic analysis, with the explicit restriction to squarefree companion pencils. Prior structural normal-form results are not presented as new.

## Limitations
The squarefree hypothesis is essential to the monomial-ideal proof and to two-dimensional eigenspaces; nonsemisimple or repeated-factor pencils may have different scheme structure. This work is not a classification of all centre-two Lie algebras or their isomorphism classes, nor does the zeta function recover the exact polynomial. Full specialized literature coverage cannot be guaranteed, and independent verification has not been performed.
