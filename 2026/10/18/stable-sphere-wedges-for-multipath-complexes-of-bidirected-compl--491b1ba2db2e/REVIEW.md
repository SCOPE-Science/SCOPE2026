# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The key nonstandard argument is the exact classification of bent edges, proof that their stars intersect only along the four-row chessboard complex, and identification of every bent-edge link as a connected crown graph. For \(n\ge7\), Ziegler's published chessboard shellability theorem makes the core two-connected. As every attaching domain is a wedge of two-spheres, all attachments are nullhomotopic and the integral wedge count follows by Euler characteristic. The standalone verifier confirms face numbers and mod-two Betti numbers through \(n=9\), but finite computations are not used to prove the unrestricted claim.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest primary sources concern source-to-sink orientations of complete bipartite graphs, bidirectional paths and polygons, and ordinary chessboard complexes. None supplies the decomposition into bent-edge cones for symmetric complete-bipartite digraphs. Earlier calculations for a finite set of small widths do not cover the quantified range \(n\ge7\). Public indexing can omit overlapping theses, revised preprints and other nomenclature.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a complete homotopy classification for a natural infinite family of directed graph complexes, not an isolated Betti computation. The explicit cone decomposition explains why a family containing abundant directed two-cycles nevertheless becomes torsion-free and a pure three-sphere wedge once the chessboard core is two-connected.

**Scope and limitations.** No claim is made for widths below seven, more than two hubs, non-bidirectional orientations or canonical equivariant splittings. The trusted primary topological input is Ziegler's theorem, and the computational experiments are corroborative only.
