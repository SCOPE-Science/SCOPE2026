# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem fixes the rank-three matroid and affine hull and handles all nontrivial \(S(2,k,n)\) parameters. Pair counting proves \(n\ge k(k-1)+1\), ensuring support extrema zero and two for every block. The standard matroid rank-inequality description supplies a complete halfspace representation. The reflected-containment inequalities are necessary and sufficient; averaging over coordinates and over blocks gives four lower bounds attained by the uniform center. Elementary inequalities leave only the Fano exception. All optimal-center uniqueness follows from coordinate saturation, except for the Fano case where the invertible block incidence matrix forces uniformity. No finite computation is used as an infinite proof. The standalone exact-arithmetic verifier completed all five tested designs and 138927 reflected block inequalities.

## Originality — PASS, residual priority uncertainty

Hanely et al. (arXiv:2201.12442, Section 8 and Propositions 8.3–8.4) prove Ehrhart and volume formulas for the same matroid polytopes, not Minkowski asymmetry or minimizer uniqueness. Ferroni–Schröter (arXiv:2310.05487, Theorem 2.5 and Example 2.9) compute split matroid face numbers, not reflection dilation. The new statement is not a corollary of equality of Ehrhart polynomials or face vectors. Literature searches for Steiner/Fano polytope asymmetry, symmetry measures, flat-density formulas, and wider rank-three matroid results found no dominating treatment in the inspected sources. A stronger theorem under unfamiliar nomenclature may exist; no independent publication-priority certification is claimed.

## Value — PASS

The result gives a complete exact affine-invariant classification for natural rank-three matroids built from Steiner systems. It shows that design incidence alone forces a universal unique center, that asymmetry normally equals the hypersimplex benchmark regardless of nonisomorphic designs, and that the Fano plane is a sharp exceptional obstruction. This distinguishes metric symmetry from the already studied volume and face enumerators and yields useful explicit formulas rather than an isolated numerical case.

## Limits

The theorem applies only to actual nontrivial Steiner \(2\)-designs with block size at least three. General rank-three matroids and other convex-geometric invariants are not classified.
