The Galois closure has only four closed-set types: empty, singleton, line (block), and all attributes. From the recursive pseudo-intent condition, all pairs are pseudo-intents, forcing every larger pseudo-intent to be pair-completion-closed. Inclusion-minimal proper Steiner subsystems satisfy the remaining recursion, and nonminimal ones fail it. For the binary projective family, those minimal subsystems are precisely three-dimensional vector subspaces; ordered-basis division proves the Gaussian coefficient, giving the full claimed formula. The accompanying checker derives closures directly from block incidence and independently enumerates pseudo-intents and implication closures for three nontrivial test systems. The theorem is analytic for all finite Steiner systems, not merely a finite search conclusion.

This is a structural classification of all rules, not simply their count. In a classical infinite family the exceptional rules are parametrized by Fano subplanes, producing \(\Theta(v^3)\) canonical rules from only \(\Theta(v^2)\) input blocks. This demonstrates a nontrivial representational blow-up and gives an exact reproducible formula. It does not claim a lower bound for arbitrary noncanonical implication systems.
