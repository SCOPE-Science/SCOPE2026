# Scientific review: annular affine-projection endpoint norm

## Correctness — PASS

The rotational covariance is \((1+r^2)I/4\), fixing the kernel \(1+4x\cdot y/(1+r^2)\). Even radial convexity locates the Lebesgue-function maximum at the outer boundary; self-adjoint duality identifies the \(L^1\) norm. The negative cap is integrated exactly as the difference of two disk caps. The one-sign-change argument for the parameter derivative of the angular integral, combined with a strictly decreasing unweighted amplitude mean, proves strict monotonicity. A local boundary tangency integral proves the precise \(5/2\) coefficient and non-\(C^3\) regularity. Independent finite consistency checks reproduce sample norms, the disk/circle limits, and the cusp scaling. There is no appeal to finite experiments for the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS within examined materials

The full readable text of Saff–Stahl–Stylianopoulos–Totik (arXiv:1403.6456, sections 1, 2 and 5) investigates polynomial asymptotics and inverse recovery on regions with holes. Hangelbroek–Narcowich–Sun–Ward (arXiv:1005.2424, sections 1, 3 and 5) treats generic kernel least-squares stability. Neither inspected theorem implies the displayed radius-specific norm formula and \(5/2\) tangency regularity. Related affine-projection formulas on a disk and a regular polygon supply individual endpoint values but no annular monotonicity or boundary-contact transition. Searches of mathematical records for annular affine projectors, perforated-disk Lebesgue constants, and equivalent sign-cap transitions did not identify a covering result. Historical synonym risk remains; absence of a matching result is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS

The endpoint operator norm is a sharp stability factor for least-squares affine approximation. A centrally removed lake is a natural controlled geometric perturbation of area-orthogonal polynomial approximation, and the result quantifies its entire effect rather than a single artificial parameter value. The topological contact of the negative kernel cap with the hole produces a genuine loss of differentiability at an explicit threshold. The strictly monotone stability law, exact cusp exponent, and limiting disk/circle comparison provide information for perturbations of planar approximation domains.

## Scope and risk

The theorem concerns normalized area, centered annuli, and affine polynomials; it does not establish corresponding statements for shifted holes, anisotropic weights, or higher degree. Exact historical priority and independent proof checking remain open.
