# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The conclusion is conditional on one isolated transverse four-crossing cycle, not on a spurious algebraic four-tuple. Normal speeds at the two crossing heights prove the three section-slope coefficients are nonzero, with common sign. Exact quadratic conservation supplies two matching equations; degeneracies necessarily give a continuum of genuine transverse cycles, not an isolated one. Outer reflections have derivative negative one; central first-hit derivatives are ratios of Hamiltonian normal speeds. Their product and its difference from one give a strict signed factorization; all sign factors are checked. The accompanying program verifies six cited examples and independently integrates one sample, but the infinite-coefficient statement follows from the displayed proof, not numerical extrapolation.

## Originality

**PASS with documented residual priority risk.** Pessoa–Ribeiro (arXiv:2106.04638v1), Sections 3–4, prove a three-zone uniqueness/existence result and list six cycles; neither the multiplier nor a stability classification appears in the inspected arguments. Llibre–Valls (Symmetry 2021), Sections 1, 6 and conclusion, likewise focus on three-saddle cycle counts. The genuinely close Carmona–Fernández-Sánchez–Novaes hyperbolicity theorem (arXiv:2207.14634, Theorem A) requires a two-zone system with one interface and excludes the specified three-zone systems; applying it to individual interfaces cannot recover a four-flight global fixed point. The accessible abstracts of the mixed center/saddle and equilibrium-free three-zone existence papers do not report the claim, but their full texts were not available in this review. Searches by exact objects, parameters, normal-velocity ratios and equivalent Floquet formulations found no result with matching scope. Inaccessibility and absence from a search are not proofs of novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Earlier three-strip work determines how many cycles may exist but does not describe their dynamical type. A one-sided basin changes sharply at a multiplier of one, so excluding neutral isolated cycles and obtaining a sign-only attraction criterion adds qualitative dynamical information across all six natural center/saddle arrangements. The formula also permits exact comparison of their stability without solving flow times. Both stability signs occur by time reversal of a published example. This is a natural structural extension rather than a reparametrized existence bound.

## Residual limitations

No existence criterion, grazing/sliding classification, extra-switch itinerary, or global attraction is asserted. Full-text priority comparison was limited for two closest three-zone cycle-count articles because their accessible open copies could not be obtained; their abstract-level coverage cannot eliminate an equivalent unstated theorem. External independent validation remains outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
