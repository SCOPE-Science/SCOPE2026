# Scientific review: multipartite Lipschitz dual volumes

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every potential has a unique ordered minimum and maximum almost everywhere. When the spread exceeds one, the extrema must lie inside one part; relative-coordinate boxes have side lengths equal to the spread within that part and two minus the spread outside. Translation between anchor coordinates has determinant one in modulus. This gives the stated integral without a missing simplex/Jacobian factor. The binomial-tail identity, positive discrete second difference, strict integer transfer inequality, and two balance-sensitive asymptotic regimes were reconstructed and checked. Exact Ehrhart enumeration independently recovers ten rational volumes, while the script checks 492 endpoint identities and 2230 majorization pairs. The latter finite checks do not substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** Aliaga’s 2021 extreme-point paper is a primary-46B20 foundational reference but makes no bulk-volume claim. The 2019 finite-metric volume-product paper establishes the equilateral baseline, not the full interpolating multipartite volume. The 2018 symmetric-edge-polytope paper classifies endpoint facets, not the dual volumes or their Schur-convexity. The 2023 regular-matroid paper gives broader lattice-point methods, but its inspected contents do not imply the explicit all-profile majorization comparison. Published results on the identical interpolated multipartite metric give facet counts; other closely matched results give Euclidean inradii and extremal spread, not bulk volume. Earlier bipartite, split-graph and one-long-edge cases are excluded as independent novelty. Crucially, an inspected signed-chain-polytope theorem is exactly the \(K_{1,p,q}\) case at \(t=2\), and already establishes its volume and strict imbalance; that full subfamily is credited rather than reasserted as new. The claimed new contribution concerns arbitrary \(k\)-part compositions and \(1<t<2\), especially all parts nontrivial and higher-part comparison. The present contribution is the all-composition volume identity together with strict majorization and asymptotics. Remaining risk is an unindexed or equivalently stated formula, particularly in graph-potential or root-polytope terminology.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The invariant is the exact volume of the natural dual unit ball of a family of real finite-dimensional Banach spaces. Its extremal comparison over every complete multipartition of fixed size is a substantive global geometry theorem, not a finite table: the result identifies a unique balanced minimizer and concentrated maximizer for every nontrivial parameter, and gives contrasting linear, critical \(N^{3/2}\), and exponential growth regimes. It can inform volume normalization and geometric comparisons between finite transportation norms.

The definition uses anchored, not intrinsic, volume. No uniqueness or novelty is claimed for covered special cases; an independent audit remains outstanding.
