# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The cyclic conjugacy gives the exact third power of one comoving one-season matrix; its characteristic cubic was independently checked by exact arithmetic. The Perron root is simple for positive parameters and remains simple at zero migration. Evaluating the polynomial at the zero-migration root establishes the global lower root bound; implicit differentiation yields a strictly positive response for every real \(a>b\), positive \(T\) and finite \(m\ge0\). Direct substitution provides the boundary slope. The checker passed 648 finite assertions; proof, not finite tests, establishes the universal statement.

## Originality
**PASS, with publication-priority risk.** arXiv:2407.07553v2, Section 3.2.1, explicitly describes the strict increase in migration as a feature of Figure 8 and does not give a theorem or derivative formula for it. The earlier arXiv:2407.07553v1 Proposition 18 already claims a global upper bound; that *different* inequality is fully acknowledged and not claimed as new. The later arXiv:2411.07821 analyzes sufficient conditions for population growth and also cites the earlier upper bound, without the explicit strict migration-sensitivity identity. An equivalent unpublished or uninspected parameter-monotonicity result remains possible.

## Value
**PASS.** This resolves the actual direction of the response to migration intensity in a natural rotating-source/reducible-migration system, where classical migration-reduction intuitions need not apply. The exact derivative is useful for sensitivity analysis of population growth and gives a sharp weak-migration coefficient. The subject is a motivated model property discussed by the source, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits
The result does not establish monotonicity in the period or transfer to different dispersal topologies. Independent priority and scientific assessment remain outstanding.
