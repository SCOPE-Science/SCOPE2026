# Review: strict prime-exponent majorization

## Correctness

PASS. The claim is precisely the simultaneous strict inequality for each factorization length and each positive sum-system part count under a unit transfer from a prime exponent at least two larger than another, together with the fixed-support and variable-support extremizer classifications. The proof handles all positive exponent vectors, including degenerate profiles with only one admissible shape. The central nonstandard argument is a two-variable Schur-positive expansion of the **nonempty-slot-restricted** allocation series; the difference at adjacent exponent profiles extracts one nonnegative Schur coefficient. A chosen distribution of the other primes and a Kostka-dominance witness make this coefficient strictly positive at every factorization length. The variable-support comparison uses a separate injection and a second allocation outside its image. The published positive Stirling transform transfers these inequalities to every admissible number of sum-system parts. Exact finite tests independently checked the binomial formula against direct exponent-allocation matrices and exercised all balancing transfers for total prime-factor multiplicity at most twelve. Classical Pieri and Young/Kostka positivity are required external mathematical theorems, explicitly identified rather than inferred from the experiments.

## Originality

PASS on the inspected accessible literature, with the specific risks below. The 2026 source derives the divisor-function polynomials and the positive Stirling transform, not strict inequalities among **different prime-exponent vectors**. The 2026 c-polynomial factorization work enumerates factorization/coloring classes, and its prime-only constrained formula implies a straightforward *top-length* multinomial comparison, but it does **not** assert simultaneous strict inequalities at every shorter length or the joint extremal profiles. The 2025 multifactorization and 2020 sum-system papers supply the counting correspondences and inclusion--exclusion without the proposed exponent-balancing order. Fink's total ordered-factorization formula can suggest monotonicity of the total, not the coefficientwise strengthening proved here. Exponent-profile reconstructibility does not imply the stated monotone comparison. Searches for majorization, Schur concavity, balanced exponent vectors, path-coloring sums and ordered-factorization extremizers found no equivalent or stronger theorem. Residual risks: an implicit earlier symmetric-function argument may establish the same ordering under different terminology, and unidentified earlier literature may contain an equivalent result.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The recent literature supplies natural arithmetic functions for fixed-length factorizations and for complementary set systems but does not select the prime-exponent shapes that extremize all dimensions. The strict theorem solves this simultaneous extremal problem at *every* prescribed total prime-factor multiplicity and support size, with explicit equality classes and computable endpoint values. The finer fixed-length assertion does not follow from a statement about the total number of ordered factorizations, from the basic binomial formula by positivity of individual terms (which involve alternating inclusion--exclusion), or from knowing the endpoints of the top-length multinomial factorization. The result supplies a structural comparison principle useful whenever weighted nonnegative combinations of the factorization counts appear, including the sum-system counts of the motivating paper. No claim is made of an optimal rate or analytic asymptotic not established here.
