# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The full three-state transient generator and rational resolvent were reconstructed. At fixed total local extinction rate and common recolonization intensity, the negative derivative of the survival Laplace transform with respect to the product of the extinction rates factors into two Laplace-positive kernels. The first is a sum of the survival kernel and a nonnegative transient transition kernel; the second is a sum of positive normalized transient transition probabilities. Their convolution is positive at every strictly positive time, proving strict usual stochastic ordering. The zero-recolonization edge case is proved using a triple exponential convolution. Exact symbolic cofactor and derivative identities and 216 finite semigroup diagnostics passed. These checks support, but do not replace, the analytic all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specified risk
The closest ecological source, Etienne and Heesterbeek (2001, DOI `10.1086/321986`), reports comparative *mean* persistence and priority rules, not the exact fixed-total, all-horizon stochastic-order theorem in its accessible abstract; its full text was not accessible. Frank (2005, DOI `10.1086/428293`) analyzes stochasticity and heterogeneous landscapes, without establishing this order in its accessible abstract. More importantly, the accessible 2018 full-text paper by Corujo, Valdés and Laria (arXiv:`1804.03098`) does prove stochastic comparisons in repairable **standby** systems, but analyzes a different state graph and a different redistribution condition: their warm-standby model uses a principal and standby slot and two operational states, not two patch-specific extinction rates and three transient states. A classical no-recolonization parallel-system special case is not asserted as new. The exact positive-recolonization full-horizon theorem is not obtained as a logical corollary of any inspected result. The remaining risk is a stronger uninspected statement in historical conservation or repairable-system literature; semantic retrieval cannot prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result resolves a natural fixed-risk-allocation comparison at every conservation horizon. Earlier management rules focused principally on mean persistence; the theorem shows an exact and strict distribution-wide direction, despite recursive recolonization. The fixed-sum constraint is an interpretable vulnerability-redistribution experiment, and the strict survival conclusion is stronger than its elementary mean consequence. This excludes arbitrary higher-patch conclusions, hazard-rate ordering, and empirical policy prescriptions.
