# Strong Morse complexes of complete bipartite graphs have a canonical doubled-edge cycle lattice

## Finding

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be disjoint labeled sets with \(|A|=m\geq2\) and \(|B|=n\geq2\). Regard the complete bipartite graph \(K_{m,n}\) as a **one-dimensional simplicial complex**. Let \(\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})\) be the full strong discrete-Morse matching complex: its vertices are vertex-edge matching pairs, and its faces are subsets of matchings generated by strict vertex filtrations as in Knudson--Owens-White, Definition 4.2.

There is a homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})\simeq\bigvee^{\,2mn-m-n+1}S^1.
\]
In particular, all reduced integral homology outside degree one vanishes, and its fundamental group is free of rank \(2mn-m-n+1\).

There is more structure. Let \(\overleftrightarrow{K}_{m,n}\) denote the multigraph with vertex set \(A\sqcup B\) and **two distinct edges joining each pair** \(a\in A,b\in B\), one indexed by \(a\to b\), the other by \(b\to a\). Orient each indexed edge according to its displayed arrow. The natural automorphism action on the original graph induces an integral-module isomorphism
\[
H_1(\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n});\mathbb Z)
\cong_{\operatorname{Aut}(K_{m,n})}
\ker\!\left(\partial:\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Arc}(K_{m,n})]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[A\sqcup B]\right),
\qquad \partial(v\to w)=[w]-[v].
\]
For the partition-preserving subgroup \(S_m\times S_n\), this has the further integral splitting
\[
H_1\cong\mathbb Z[A\times B]\oplus
\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[A]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[B]\bigr),
\]
where \(\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[A]\) and \(\widetilde{\mathbb Z}[B]\) are augmentation kernels. The first summand represents oppositely oriented backtracking pairs, and the second represents ordinary bipartite graph cycles. For \((\sigma,\tau)\in S_m\times S_n\), if \(f_A\) and \(f_B\) are the fixed-point counts of \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\), the rational degree-one character is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\sigma,\tau\mid H_1)=2f_Af_B-f_A-f_B+1.
\]
When \(m=n\), each automorphism exchanging the two parts has degree-one trace \(1\). For all \(m,n\geq2\), the full automorphism group fixes an integral rank-one submodule generated by the sum of all backtracking pairs.

The previously studied thin case \(m=2\) is included for coherence; the new unrestricted two-parameter classification includes every \(m,n\geq3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is simple, undirected and complete bipartite, but is treated strictly as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. It is **not** a higher-dimensional clique complex, and \(\mathcal{SM}\) is **not** the ordinary acyclic-Morse-matching complex \(\mathfrak M\), nor the pure subcomplex \(\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}\).

An oriented symbol \(v\to w\) represents the pair consisting of the graph vertex \(v\) and the incident graph edge \(\{v,w\}\). A strict vertex order contributes \(v\to w\) exactly when \(w\) is the **unique** earlier neighbor of \(v\). This follows because a descending link in a bipartite graph is a discrete set: it is a simplicial cone precisely when it has one vertex. We take the downward closure over all strict orders and include the empty face in the abstract complex.

The restriction \(m,n\geq2\) is essential to the stated graph model. If one part has size one and the other at least two, the strong complex is contractible by the star classification. When both parts have size one, the strong complex is \(S^0\).

## Proof

**1. A complete contractible-star cover.** For each \(b\in B\), define a subcomplex \(U_b\) on the directed incidences of edges incident with \(b\). It is generated by the following \(m+1\) simplices:
\[
E_b=\{a\to b:a\in A\},\qquad
F_{b,a}=(E_b\setminus\{a\to b\})\cup\{b\to a\}
\quad(a\in A).
\]
Likewise, for \(a\in A\), define \(V_a\) with \(n+1\) generators
\[
E_a=\{b\to a:b\in B\},\qquad
F_{a,b}=(E_a\setminus\{b\to a\})\cup\{a\to b\}
\quad(b\in B).
\]
Every displayed generator is induced by an actual strict order on all vertices: for \(E_b\), order \(b\) before all vertices of \(A\), and put \(B\setminus\{b\}\) last. For \(F_{b,a}\), order \(a,b\), then \(A\setminus\{a\}\), then \(B\setminus\{b\}\). The analogous orders realize \(E_a\) and \(F_{a,b}\).

Conversely, fix any strict order and let \(a_0\) and \(b_0\) be its earliest vertices from the two sides. Every matched arrow originating in \(A\) points to \(b_0\), and every matched arrow originating in \(B\) points to \(a_0\). Suppose \(a_0\) comes before \(b_0\). If the second vertex of \(A\) comes before the second of \(B\), at most \(b_0\) among \(B\)-vertices is matched, so the matching lies in \(U_{b_0}\). If the second of \(B\) comes earlier, no matched arrow from \(A\) exists and the matching lies in \(V_{a_0}\). The symmetric argument applies when \(b_0\) is first. Therefore
\[
\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})=\bigcup_{b\in B}U_b\;\cup\!\bigcup_{a\in A}V_a.
\]

Each \(U_b\) is contractible: the incoming arcs \(E_b\) span a full simplex, and each outward vertex \(b\to a\) belongs to exactly one maximal simplex \(F_{b,a}\), where its link is the simplex on \(E_b\setminus\{a\to b\}\). Since \(m\geq2\), this link is a cone. Successively deleting those outward dominated vertices leaves the full simplex on \(E_b\). The same proof applies to every \(V_a\).

**2. Exact intersections.** Distinct \(U_b\)'s have disjoint vertex sets, as do distinct \(V_a\)'s. For each \((a,b)\in A\times B\),
\[
U_b\cap V_a=\bigl\{\varnothing,\{a\to b\},\{b\to a\}\bigr\}\cong S^0.
\]
Both vertices occur in both star complexes, but they cannot span an edge, because that would match the same graph edge twice. There are no nonempty triple intersections.

**3. Graph-of-spaces reduction.** Form the double mapping-cylinder replacement of the cover: take disjoint copies of the contractible spaces \(|U_b|\) and \(|V_a|\), and for each of the two points of \(|U_b\cap V_a|\), attach an interval between its two copies. Because all intersections are subcomplexes and no triple intersection exists, successive homotopy-pushout gluing shows this replacement is homotopy equivalent to \(|\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})|\). Contracting each contractible vertex space (in the homotopy colimit) gives the graph \(\overleftrightarrow{K}_{m,n}\): exactly \(m+n\) vertices and \(2mn\) edges. It is connected, so its first Betti number is \(2mn-(m+n)+1\), and it has no higher homotopy-dimensional cells. A connected finite graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of as many circles as its cycle rank. This proves the asserted homotopy type, not just a homology calculation.

**4. Integral symmetries.** The mapping-cylinder construction is natural under every graph automorphism: a directed incidence \(v\to w\) labels an interval running from the vertex space at \(v\) to the vertex space at \(w\). Graph automorphisms send it to the interval \(g(v)\to g(w)\). The homology equivalences therefore identify first integral homology, as an automorphism module, with the cycle kernel of the displayed directed incidence boundary.

To see the integral splitting for partition-preserving automorphisms, write each integral one-chain uniquely as
\[
z=\sum_{a,b}d_{ab}(a\to b)
  +\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}\bigl((a\to b)+(b\to a)\bigr).
\]
It is a cycle exactly when the matrix \((d_{ab})\) has every row sum and every column sum zero. The \(q_{ab}\)'s are unconstrained. This row-and-column-zero lattice is canonically the tensor product of the two augmentation lattices, giving the splitting. Finally, taking traces in the equivariant exact sequence of graph chains
\[
0\longrightarrow H_1\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Arc}(K_{m,n})]\xrightarrow{\partial}
\mathbb Z[A\sqcup B]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0
\]
gives the character formula. For a part-exchanging automorphism (only possible when \(m=n\)), no vertex or directed arc is fixed, so the trace is \(1\). The invariant backtracking sum is nonzero, and any invariant cycle must have equal coefficients within the two arc-orbits; the boundary equation then forces equal coefficients on the two orientations, proving that the fixed submodule has rank one.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify_biclique.py` generates strong gradients **directly from all vertex permutations**, independently generates the stated star-cover facets, and checks equality of their downward closures. It then computes the mod-two simplicial boundary ranks by sparse bitwise Gaussian elimination. For the pairs \((m,n)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,3),(3,4),(3,5),(4,4),(4,5)\), all \((m+n)!\) vertex orders were exhausted (up to \(362{,}880\) in the last case); every complex had reduced homology only in degree one with the asserted rank.

For \((2,2),(3,3),(3,4)\), the verifier additionally computes signed simplicial Lefschetz traces for every partition-preserving automorphism; it also checks every part-exchanging automorphism in the balanced cases. All computed traces equal the integral-cycle-module prediction. The file `verify_output.txt` records the exact enumerations and results. These finite checks test the proof's definitions and signs but do not replace the uniform arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (arXiv:2609.06844v1, first public 2026-09-06), introduce \(\mathcal{SM}\) in Definition 4.2 and compute stars in Theorem 7.1; they also treat paths, cycles, simplices and boundaries, and their Section 10 treats complete graphs and disjoint unions. Their star theorem supplies the local pieces but does not classify how they intersect when two bipartition sizes are simultaneously nontrivial. The new step is the global cover by both sides, the two-point intersections, the resulting doubled-edge graph, and the full integral automorphism module.

Fernández, *Strong discrete Morse theory* (arXiv:2504.15729), establishes the vertex-filtration framework, not the topology of the new complex \(\mathcal{SM}(K_{m,n})\). Results on the ordinary complex \(\mathfrak M(K_{m,n})\) address all acyclic Morse matchings, not this smaller strong-gradient-generated complex. A related earlier calculation treats only the thin family \(K_{2,n}\); its statement does not imply the classification when both sides have at least three vertices.

## Limitations

The result treats the **full** strong complex on a one-dimensional complete bipartite graph; it does not classify its pure subcomplex, arbitrary bipartite graphs, higher-dimensional clique complexes or the ordinary Morse-matching complex. The homology splitting is canonical for a **chosen ordered bipartition**; the undivided kernel description, not that splitting, is the natural formula for the full automorphism group when the parts have equal size. The displayed finite tests do not prove infinite quantifiers, which instead follow from the explicit cover and graph-of-spaces proof. Bibliographic completeness is not guaranteed; unindexed prior work might contain an equivalent classification.
