# Strong Morse complexes of \(K_{2,n}\): circle wedges and their symmetry modules

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(K_{2,n}\) have hubs \(a,b\) and leaves \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\). Let \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}(K_{2,n})\) be the full simplicial complex generated by all strong discrete Morse matchings obtained from strict vertex orderings, in the sense of Knudson--Owens-White. Then
\[
X_n\simeq\bigvee^{3n-1}S^1,\qquad
H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{3n-1},\qquad H_j(X_n;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(j\ge2).
\]
In particular, this complex of dimension \(n-1\) is homotopy equivalent to a graph, uniformly in \(n\).

The natural \(S_n\times C_2\) action (permuting leaves and exchanging hubs) on its integral first homology is the signed augmentation kernel
\[
L_n=\left\{(a_i,b_i,c_i)_{i=1}^n\in\mathbb Z^{3n}:\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i+b_i+c_i)=0\right\}.
\]
Leaf permutations permute subscripts; hub interchange acts by \((a_i,b_i,c_i)\mapsto(-b_i,-a_i,-c_i)\). Thus for a leaf permutation \(\sigma\) with \(f(\sigma)\) fixed labels,
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{H_1(\mathbb Q)}(\sigma,1)=3f(\sigma)-1,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{H_1(\mathbb Q)}(\sigma,s)=1-f(\sigma),
\]
where \(s\) exchanges hubs. If \(V_{\mathrm{std}}\) is the standard \((n-1)\)-dimensional rational \(S_n\)-module and \(\varepsilon\) is the nontrivial character of hub exchange, then
\[
H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbf1\oplus\varepsilon\oplus\bigl(V_{\mathrm{std}}\otimes(\mathbf1\oplus2\varepsilon)\bigr).
\]
The full automorphism-invariant subgroup of integral first homology has rank one, generated in the edge model below by \(\sum_i(e_{1i}-e_{2i})\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. For a one-dimensional graph considered as a simplicial complex, a strict total order on vertices gives a strong Morse gradient as follows: a vertex is paired to its incident edge precisely when its descending link is a cone; for a triangle-free graph this means that it has exactly one earlier neighbor. If the unique earlier neighbor is \(u\) and the current vertex is \(v\), write the gradient pair as the directed symbol \(v\to u\). The full complex contains every subset of every such gradient. No claim is made about the *pure* strong Morse complex, nor about ordinary Morse complexes. We state novelty for \(n\ge3\); the \(n=2\) case is the four-cycle already calculated in [1].

Write four kinds of directed pairs for each leaf:
\[
U_i=(z_i\to a),\quad V_i=(z_i\to b),\quad P_i=(a\to z_i),\quad Q_i=(b\to z_i).
\]
Here these symbols denote *vertices of the matching complex*, not edges of its geometric realization. Define two subcomplexes using ordinary simplex notation \(\Delta(T)\) for the full simplex spanned by a vertex set \(T\):
\[
D_a=\Delta(\{U_i:1\le i\le n\})\cup
\bigcup_{i=1}^n\Delta(\{P_i\}\cup\{U_j:j\ne i\}),
\]
\[
D_b=\Delta(\{V_i:1\le i\le n\})\cup
\bigcup_{i=1}^n\Delta(\{Q_i\}\cup\{V_j:j\ne i\}).
\]
Let \(e_{1i}=\{U_i,Q_i\}\), \(e_{2i}=\{P_i,V_i\}\), and \(e_{3i}=\{P_i,Q_i\}\) be one-simplices, and orient all of these from \(D_a\) to \(D_b\). Their interiors are pairwise disjoint.

## Proof
**1. Classification of all generating matchings.** Fix an arbitrary strict vertex order. Suppose hub \(a\) precedes hub \(b\). Split the leaves into \(L_0,L_1,L_2\), those preceding \(a\), strictly between the hubs, and after \(b\), respectively. Leaves in \(L_0\) or \(L_2\) have zero or two earlier hub-neighbors, respectively, and produce no gradient pair. Every leaf in \(L_1\) produces its \(U_i\) pair. Hub \(a\) produces \(P_i\) exactly when \(L_0=\{z_i\}\); hub \(b\) produces \(Q_i\) exactly when \(L_0\cup L_1=\{z_i\}\). Therefore each generator with \(a\) preceding \(b\) lies in \(D_a\), or in the isolated one-simplices \(e_{1i}\) or \(e_{3i}\). Reversing the hubs yields the symmetric statement for \(D_b\), together with \(e_{2i}\) and \(e_{3i}\). The equalities (rather than just containments) follow by choosing orders: putting \(a\) first and all leaves before \(b\) realizes the full simplex on the \(U_i\); putting \(z_i\) first, then \(a\), then every other leaf, then \(b\), realizes the flap \(\{P_i\}\cup\{U_j:j\ne i\}\). Reversing hubs realizes the \(D_b\) facets. The orders \(a<z_i<b\), \(b<z_i<a\), and \(z_i<a<b\), with every other leaf after both hubs, realize respectively the three transverse edges \(e_{1i},e_{2i},e_{3i}\). Hence as an equality of simplicial complexes,
\[
X_n=D_a\cup D_b\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^n(e_{1i}\cup e_{2i}\cup e_{3i}).
\]
There are **no** higher-dimensional simplices crossing the two pieces: the classification above lists every possible generating simplex.

**2. Contractible pieces and the homotopy type.** The vertex sets of \(D_a\) and \(D_b\) are disjoint. In \(D_a\), each flap apex \(P_i\) belongs only to the simplex \(\Delta(\{P_i\}\cup\{U_j:j\ne i\})\), whose attaching face \(\Delta(\{U_j:j\ne i\})\) is nonempty for \(n\ge2\). Thus each flap may be collapsed (within \(D_a\)) onto its attaching face; what remains is the full simplex on the \(U_i\). In particular \(D_a\) is contractible. The same argument makes \(D_b\) contractible. Since each is a finite CW subcomplex, collapsing each contractible piece to one vertex gives a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence onto a graph with two vertices and exactly \(3n\) parallel edges (the images of the \(e_{ki}\)). This graph is connected and has free fundamental group of rank \(3n-1\). It is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{3n-1}S^1\). This argument works for every \(n\ge3\), without finite-case extrapolation.

**3. Integral group action.** The two quotient vertices correspond to \(D_a,D_b\). All transverse edges are oriented from the first to the second. A leaf permutation \(\sigma\) sends each \(e_{ki}\) to \(e_{k,\sigma(i)}\). Exchanging hubs sends the oriented edges \(e_{1i}\mapsto-e_{2i}\), \(e_{2i}\mapsto-e_{1i}\), and \(e_{3i}\mapsto-e_{3i}\). These actions agree with the induced map of the quotient, which is an equivariant map and a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence. The graph boundary sends each oriented edge to \([D_b]-[D_a]\), so its first integral homology is precisely the stated signed augmentation kernel. The trace of the action on the \(3n\)-dimensional edge module equals \(3f(\sigma)\) without hub exchange and \(-f(\sigma)\) with hub exchange; subtract the one-dimensional quotient module, on which hub exchange acts by \(-1\), to obtain the stated character. Decomposing the leaf permutation module as \(\mathbf1\oplus V_{\mathrm{std}}\) and the three-type module as \(\mathbf1\oplus2\varepsilon\) proves the rational decomposition. An invariant integral vector must be constant in \(i\) and fixed by the signed swap, hence has coefficients \((t,-t,0)\), giving the stated primitive invariant generator.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_strong_k2n.py`, using only Python's standard library, constructs all vertex-ordering-induced gradients independently for \(2\le n\le7\), closes them downward, and compares the **entire exact face set** with the two-piece plus three-edge formula. It then constructs mod-two simplicial boundary operators and verifies the Betti ranks predicted by the homotopy theorem. For \(2\le n\le5\), it also checks the signed simplicial Lefschetz numbers of every leaf permutation, both with and without hub exchange, against the representation formula. The output in `verification_output.txt` records the completed checks. Finite computations corroborate the general proof; they are not used to justify the quantifier \(\forall n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens--White [1] define the full and pure strong Morse matching complexes and compute the full complexes of paths, stars, simplices and simplex boundaries, plus special information for cycles. Their Proposition 8.5 already determines the four-cycle \(K_{2,2}=C_4\), explaining our restriction to \(n\ge3\). The results stated in their Sections 7, 8 and 10 do not give the three-edge-per-leaf decomposition for the higher-rank complete bipartite graphs. Fernández [2] supplies the underlying strict-filtration strong Morse construction rather than this new family classification. Previously recorded *ordinary* Morse complexes of \(K_{2,n}\) have nonzero reduced homology in dimension \(n\), with ranks \(n^2-1\); the inclusion of the strong complex into the ordinary complex does not imply its homotopy type or integral symmetry module. In fact, for \(n\ge3\) its first-homology map to the ordinary complex vanishes. This is a distinct full strong-complex result, not a relabeling of a pure complex or an ordinary matching complex.

## Limitations
The theorem does not determine the equivariant homotopy type (as opposed to its integral homology representation); contracting the two pieces is an equivariant *map* and a nonequivariant homotopy equivalence, not necessarily an equivariant homotopy equivalence at all fixed-point sets. It does not classify the strong complexes of general \(K_{m,n}\) with \(m\ge3\). The finite verifier is not an infinite certificate. The literature comparison is comprehensive for the closest accessible primary statements and targeted indexed searches, but an unindexed equivalent result cannot be excluded.
