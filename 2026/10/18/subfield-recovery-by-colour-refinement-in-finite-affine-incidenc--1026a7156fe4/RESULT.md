# Subfield recovery by colour refinement in finite affine incidence planes

## Finding
Let \(q=p^e\), where \(p\) is prime and \(e\ge1\). In the simple undirected point-line incidence graph of \(\operatorname{AG}(2,\mathbb F_q)\), individually colour a set \(S\) of point vertices containing three noncollinear points, leaving every other vertex initially unmarked. Choose an ordered noncollinear triple \(A,B,C\in S\), and use its unique affine frame to write \(A=(0,0)\), \(B=(1,0)\), \(C=(0,1)\). Let \(K\le\mathbb F_q\) be the smallest subfield containing every coordinate of every point in \(S\).

**Subfield singleton law.** After ordinary one-dimensional colour refinement stabilizes, its singleton point classes are **exactly** the points of \(K^2\); its singleton line classes are **exactly** the affine lines with defining coefficients in \(K\). Consequently the numbers of singleton classes on the two sides are, respectively,
\[
|K|^2\qquad\text{and}\qquad |K|(|K|+1).
\]
In particular, the colouring is discrete if and only if \(K=\mathbb F_q\).

**Sharp point-landmark threshold.** If marks must be distinct point vertices with distinct individual colours, the least number that makes the incidence graph discrete under colour refinement is
\[
\begin{cases}
3,&e=1,\\
4,&e>1.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(e=1\), any three noncollinear marked points suffice. For \(e>1\), three never suffice; four points \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(t,0)\) suffice whenever \(\mathbb F_p(t)=\mathbb F_q\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has the \(q^2\) points of \(\mathbb F_q^2\) and its \(q(q+1)\) affine lines as vertices. A line is either \(x=c\) or \(y=mx+b\), with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_q\). A point and a line are adjacent precisely when they are incident. Marking is by a separate new colour for each selected point; all unmarked vertices may share an initial colour. Point degree is \(q+1\), whereas line degree is \(q\), so colour refinement itself distinguishes the two vertex sorts.

At each refinement round, a vertex retains its previous colour and records the multiset of neighbour colours. The assertion concerns *singleton stable colour classes*, not a claimed classification of every non-singleton class. For the subfield law, \(S\) is any finite subset of the point set containing a noncollinear triple; for the minimum-landmark assertion, sets with fewer than three marks are also considered. Only Desarguesian affine planes are claimed.

## Proof
**1. Geometric closure is visible to colour refinement.** In a stable equitable partition, any two differently individualized point vertices are singleton classes. If two points \(P,Q\) have singleton colours, their unique common incident line has a singleton colour: it is the only line with a neighbour in both singleton classes. Dually, if two distinct nonparallel lines have singleton colours, their intersection point has a singleton colour.

It remains to justify *parallel constructions*, which are the key step. Suppose the affine line \(L\) and point \(P\) have singleton stable colours. Because a point has a neighbour of the singleton colour of \(L\) exactly when it lies on \(L\), the entire point set on \(L\) is a union of stable point-colour classes. If \(P\notin L\), consider lines incident with \(P\). Exactly one of them has zero neighbours in that union: the line through \(P\) parallel to \(L\). Every other such line intersects \(L\) in exactly one point. Equitability therefore forces the parallel line to be a singleton class. If \(P\in L\), the required parallel is \(L\) itself. Thus joins of singleton points, intersections of singleton nonparallel lines, and parallels through singleton points all produce singleton vertices.

**2. The closure of the marked frame is the coordinate subplane over \(K\).** Normalize the three marked points to \(O=(0,0)\), \(U=(1,0)\), \(V=(0,1)\). The coordinate axes and the diagonal line \(UV\) become singleton lines. With joins, intersections and parallels, a point \(X(a)=(a,0)\) on the first axis gives the point \(Y(a)=(0,a)\): draw through \(X(a)\) a line parallel to \(UV\) and intersect it with the second axis. Having \(X(a)\) and \(X(b)\), use the horizontal line through \(Y(b)\) and the vertical line through \(X(a)\) to obtain \((a,b)\).

To construct \(X(a+b)\), intersect the first axis with the line through \((a,b)\) parallel to \(UV\). To construct \(X(ab)\), first form \((1,b)\), join it to \(O\), and intersect that line with the vertical through \(X(a)\); the intersection is \((a,ab)\). Its horizontal projection onto the second axis is \(Y(ab)\), which can be transferred back to \(X(ab)\) by a parallel to \(UV\).

Every additional marked point \((a,b)\in S\) yields \(X(a)\) and \(Y(b)\) by vertical/horizontal projection onto the axes. Hence the constructed first-axis coordinates contain the ring generated by \(\mathbb F_p\) and all marked coordinates. This ring equals \(K\), since any finite integral domain is a field. Thus every point of \(K^2\) is singleton, and every affine \(K\)-rational line is singleton by joining two distinct \(K\)-rational points.

**3. No further singleton classes exist.** Suppose \(K=\mathbb F_{p^d}\) is a proper subfield of \(\mathbb F_q\). The field automorphism \(\sigma:z\mapsto z^{p^d}\) is nontrivial, fixes precisely \(K\), and induces an automorphism of the incidence graph by applying \(\sigma\) to point coordinates and line coefficients. It fixes every marked point, hence preserves every stable colour class. Its fixed point vertices are exactly \(K^2\), and its fixed line vertices are exactly the lines \(x=c\) or \(y=mx+b\) with coefficients in \(K\). Every other vertex moves and cannot have a singleton stable colour. When \(K=\mathbb F_q\), Step 2 has already shown that all vertices are singleton. This proves the subfield law and the exact counts.

**4. The minimum numbers of marked points.** By an affine change of coordinates, two distinct marked points may be taken as \(O,U\). The nonidentity shear \((x,y)\mapsto(x+y,y)\) fixes them, so neither zero, one nor two marked points can make the graph discrete. The same shear fixes every point of the first coordinate axis, showing that three collinear marked points cannot suffice. For three noncollinear marked points, normalize to \(O,U,V\). If \(e=1\), Step 2 makes the entire graph discrete. If \(e>1\), the nonidentity Frobenius automorphism \(z\mapsto z^p\) fixes \(O,U,V\), so no triple can be sufficient. Finally, every finite field extension \(\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_p\) has a primitive field generator \(t\); marking \(O,U,V,(t,0)\) gives \(K=\mathbb F_q\), and Step 3 gives discreteness. The lower and upper bounds agree.

## Verification
The standalone `affine_refinement_check.py` implements exact polynomial-basis arithmetic and standard multiset-neighbour colour refinement. It covers prime orders \(2,3,5,7\) and prime-power orders \(4,8,9,16,25\). In each case it tests that the three affine-frame marks produce exactly \(p^2\) singleton points and \(p(p+1)\) singleton lines, with *exact coordinates and coefficients*, not just matching counts. In every nonprime case, adjoining a field-generating fourth mark gives a discrete partition. Two additional four-mark tests inside \(\mathbb F_{16}\) verify the proper intermediate field \(\mathbb F_4\): they yield exactly \(16\) singleton points and \(20\) singleton lines. The program's captured output ends with `ALL_CASES_PASS`.

Separately, `affine_generation_check.py` implements the geometric join, meet and parallel closure without calling the colour-refinement routine. It verifies the prescribed subplane closure and four-mark generation for the same nine finite fields, ending with `GEOMETRY_CLOSURE_PASS`. Both scripts use only the Python standard library and run as `python affine_refinement_check.py` and `python affine_generation_check.py` from a directory containing both files. Their text outputs are included as separate files. The infinite-family assertions follow from the proof, not from these finite runs.

## Relationship to prior work
Arvind, Fuhlbrück, Köbler, Kuhnert and Rattan (2016) formalize the **discrete individualization** problem and its complexity. Kiefer, Schweitzer and Selman (2015) explain two-variable counting identification and refinement, while Kryftis (2016), in Chapter 4, develops geometric reconstruction of field coordinates from an affine frame. These establish the surrounding algorithmic and geometric ingredients; the exact singleton-subfield correspondence and the three-versus-four point threshold are not stated in the inspected portions. Bartoli, Héger, Kiss and Takáts (2017) study the different *metric dimension* invariant, showing that an affine-plane incidence graph of order \(q\ge13\) has metric dimension \(3q-4\). The much smaller refinement-landmark number is possible because iterative neighbour counting extracts information beyond the original distance vector.

## Limitations
The proof requires a Desarguesian plane over a finite field. It does not classify the non-singleton stable colour classes or prove an optimal refinement-round bound. It does not assert an analogous formula for non-Desarguesian affine planes or for markings restricted to lines. Computational tests stop at order \(25\), and the finite computations are corroboration rather than a substitute for the all-prime-power proof. The literature comparison establishes no independent priority guarantee; an earlier specialized treatment may exist.
