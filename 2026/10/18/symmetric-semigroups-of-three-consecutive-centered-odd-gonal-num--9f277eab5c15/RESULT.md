# Symmetric semigroups of three consecutive centered odd-gonal numbers

## Finding

For every **odd integer** \(m\ge3\) and integer \(n\ge2\), put
\[
C_m(j)=1+\frac{m j(j-1)}2,\qquad
(a,b,c)=(C_m(n),C_m(n+1),C_m(n+2)),\qquad
S_{m,n}=\langle a,b,c\rangle.
\]
Here \(\langle a,b,c\rangle\) denotes the additive submonoid of nonnegative integers generated by its arguments; it has finite complement because the generators are jointly coprime. Define \(t=2n+1\) and \(K=3m+8\).

**Theorem.** The numerical semigroup \(S_{m,n}\) is symmetric, equivalently complete intersection or cyclotomic, **if and only if**
\[
\boxed{t\mid K\quad\text{or}\quad (n=2\text{ and }m\in\{3,5,7\}).}
\]
If \(t\mid K\), its Frobenius number and genus satisfy
\[
\boxed{F(S_{m,n})=\frac{ac}{t}-a-c+(t-1)b,\qquad
 g(S_{m,n})=\frac{F(S_{m,n})+1}{2}.}
\]
In the three additional symmetric cases, \(F(S_{m,2})=(3m^2+8m-1)/2\) and \(g(S_{m,2})=(F(S_{m,2})+1)/2\).
Since \(K\) is odd and not divisible by three, for each fixed odd \(m\ge3\) the **exact number** of symmetric indices \(n\ge2\) is
\[
\boxed{\tau(3m+8)-1+\mathbf 1_{m\in\{3,5,7\}},}
\]
where \(\tau\) counts positive divisors. For example, centered triangles \(m=3\) have symmetric indices \(n=2,8\), centered pentagons \(m=5\) have \(n=2,11\), and centered nonagons \(m=9\) have \(n=2,3,17\).

## Assumptions and scope

The parameters are exactly the positive odd polygonal side count \(m\ge3\) and the starting index \(n\ge2\). Symmetry means that, writing \(F=\max(\mathbb N_0\setminus S)\), every integer \(z\) has precisely one of \(z\in S\) and \(F-z\in S\); equivalently \(2g=F+1\), with \(g=|\mathbb N_0\setminus S|\). The semigroup polynomial \(P_S(z)=(1-z)\sum_{s\in S}z^s\) is cyclotomic when all its complex zeros are roots of unity. All three generators are minimal in the stated odd-\(m\) range; consequently the standard three-generator symmetry/gluing criterion and the equivalence of symmetry, complete intersection and cyclotomicity apply.

## Proof

**1. Gcd identities and minimal generators.** Direct differences give \(b-a=mn\), \(c-b=m(n+1)\), and \(c-a=m(2n+1)\). Since every \(C_m(j)\equiv1\pmod m\), the adjacent gcds are \(\gcd(a,n)\) and \(\gcd(b,n+1)\). Elementary parity reduction yields
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\begin{cases}2&n\equiv2\pmod4,\\1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad
\gcd(b,c)=\begin{cases}2&n\equiv1\pmod4,\\1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Using that \(t\) is odd and \(8a\equiv3m+8\pmod t\), one also obtains
\[
\boxed{\gcd(a,c)=\gcd(t,K).}
\]
We have \(a<b<c\) and \(2b>c\). The middle generator is not a multiple of \(a\): for \(n\ge3\), \(a<b<2a\); for \(n=2\), \(b=3a-2\) and \(a=m+1\ge4\). To express \(c\) using \(a,b\), at most one copy of \(b\) is possible. For \(n\ge6\), \(c<2a\), making such an expression impossible. For \(n=2,3,4,5\), the differences \(c-b=m(n+1)\) and the intervals for \(c/a\) exclude a representation: at \(n=2\), \(c=6a-5\), which could be divisible by \(a\) only if \(m+1\mid5\), impossible for odd \(m\ge3\); at \(n=3\), \(3a<c<4a\); at \(n=4,5\), \(2a<c<3a\); and \(c-b\) is never a nonnegative multiple of \(a\). Thus the embedding dimension is exactly three.

**2. Three-generator criterion.** For a minimally generated three-generator numerical semigroup, symmetry holds precisely if some two generators have gcd \(d>1\) and the remaining generator lies in the numerical semigroup generated by the two quotients by \(d\). This is the classical gluing/free-semigroup characterization (see the 2014 source, Section 3.1, Example 4, and the references therein). Conversely such a gluing is complete intersection and symmetric. We analyze all three possible pairs, rather than assuming pairwise coprimality.

**3. The nonadjacent pair.** Set \(d=\gcd(a,c)=\gcd(t,K)\). For \(d>1\), put \(A=a/d\), \(C=c/d\), and \(s=t/d\), all positive integers; \(d,s\) are odd. Since \(\gcd(m,d)=1\), the identities
\[
C=A+ms,\qquad b=dA+mn
\]
show that if \(b=xA+yC\) for nonnegative integers \(x,y\), then \(x+y=d+qm\) for some integer \(q\), and
\[
\boxed{n=qA+ys,\qquad0\le y\le d+qm.}
\]
If \(s\ge3\), then \(d\le t/3\). For \(m\ge3\) and \(n\ge2\), direct substitution gives \(3a>nt\) and \(3c>(n+1)t\), hence \(A>n\) and \(C>n+1\). The case \(q\ge1\) would imply \(n\ge A>n\). If \(q=-h\le-1\), then
\[
n=-hA+ys\le-hA+(d-hm)s=t-hC\le t-C<n,
\]
another contradiction. Therefore \(q=0\), so \(n=ys\). But \(2n+1=ds\) gives \(s\mid1\), contrary to \(s\ge3\). Thus the nonadjacent pair gives a gluing only when \(s=1\), i.e. \(t\mid K\). Conversely, if \(t\mid K\), then \(d=t\) and the explicit nonnegative representation
\[
\boxed{b=(n+1)(a/t)+n(c/t)}
\]
provides that gluing. If \(d=1\), this pair cannot provide a nontrivial gluing.

**4. The adjacent pairs.** The pair \((b,c)\) has nontrivial gcd only when \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), so \(n\ge5\). Then \(b/2<a\), \(a<b\), and \(a\) cannot be a sum of two or more elements from \(\{b/2,c/2\}\); a one-term representation would require \(a=b/2\) or \(a=c/2\). The former is ruled out by \(b<2a\). The latter is ruled out because
\[
c-2a=\frac{m(-n^2+5n+2)}2-1
\]
is positive for \(n=5\) and negative for \(n\ge6\). Hence this pair never gives symmetry.

The pair \((a,b)\) has gcd two only when \(n\equiv2\pmod4\). If \(n\ge6\), the inequality \(c<2a\) implies that any representation \(c=x(a/2)+y(b/2)\) has \(1\le x+y\le3\). Reducing twice this equation modulo odd \(m\) yields \(x+y\equiv2\pmod m\), so \(x+y=2\). But then \(c\le b\), impossible. For \(n=2\), write \(a=m+1\), \(b=3m+1\), \(c=6m+1\). If \(m\ge11\), then \(c/(a/2)<12\), so \(x+y\le11\); the same congruence again forces \(x+y=2\), impossible. The remaining odd parameters are \(m=3,5,7,9\), with actual representations
\[
\begin{aligned}
m=3:&\quad19=7\cdot2+1\cdot5,\\
m=5:&\quad31=5\cdot3+2\cdot8,\\
m=7:&\quad43=8\cdot4+1\cdot11,\\
m=9:&\quad55=11\cdot5+0\cdot14.
\end{aligned}
\]
The case \((m,n)=(9,2)\) is already included in \(t\mid K\) because \(5\mid35\). Exactly the three additional cases in the statement remain. The symmetry classification follows.

**5. Frobenius number and genus.** Whenever \(t\mid K\), let \(A=a/t,C=c/t\). The previous representation shows \(b\in\langle A,C\rangle\), while \(\gcd(t,b)=1\) and \(\gcd(A,C)=1\). Hence \(S=t\langle A,C\rangle+b\mathbb N_0\). Writing each integer uniquely as \(t k+r b\) with \(0\le r<t\), membership in \(S\) is equivalent to \(k\in\langle A,C\rangle\); the reverse direction follows by reducing the coefficient of \(b\) modulo \(t\), using \(b\in\langle A,C\rangle\). Sylvester's two-generator formula \(F(\langle A,C\rangle)=AC-A-C\) therefore gives
\[
F(S)=t(AC-A-C)+(t-1)b=ac/t-a-c+(t-1)b.
\]
For the exceptional triples, the analogous gluing is \(S=2\langle a/2,b/2\rangle+c\mathbb N_0\), and the same argument gives \(F(S)=ab/2-a-b+c=(3m^2+8m-1)/2\). All stated genera follow from symmetry. Finally, every proper divisor \(t>1\) of odd \(K=3m+8\) is at least five (since \(3\nmid K\)); the map \(t\mapsto n=(t-1)/2\) gives the divisor-count formula, with three separate low exceptions.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) evaluates exact gcd/gluing criteria for \(198602\) parameter pairs \(3\le m\le400,\ 2\le n\le500\), and computes Apéry sets by a separate shortest-path method for \(624\) pairs \(3\le m\le28,\ 2\le n\le25\). The Apéry calculation determines Frobenius numbers and genera without assuming the claimed symmetry criterion. It also checks all claimed Frobenius formulas within the test range. Execute \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) with Python 3.8 or newer; no external packages are needed. These tests are finite corroboration; Steps 1–5 prove the infinite statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Ciolan, García-Sánchez and Moree (2014) explain the equivalence of symmetry, complete intersection and cyclotomicity for embedding dimension at most three and the classical gluing characterization, but do not classify the odd-centered-polygonal generator triples. The centered pentagonal sequence is recorded independently as OEIS A005891. Yin, Mu and Komatsu (2024) study the related *even*-sided centered \(2a\)-gonal triples \(1+a n(n-1)\) for integer \(a\ge4\), including Frobenius and genus formulas; their parameter domain does not include odd \(m\). The present theorem specifically concerns the missing odd-sided family, where adjacent generators can acquire gcd two and three exceptional low-index symmetric semigroups appear. Existing centered-square specializations concern even \(m=4\) and are not claimed as new here.

## Limitations

The theorem makes no claim for even side counts or starting index \(n=1\). It classifies symmetry and gives Frobenius/genus values **in the symmetric cases**, not complete Frobenius/genus formulas for nonsymmetric cases. The 2024 generalized-star work covers substantial even-sided cases; the exact priority of the odd-sided classification remains subject to unlocated literature. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
