# Symmetry classification for three consecutive centered-square numbers

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge2\), let the centered-square number be \(q_n=n^2+(n-1)^2\), and let \(S_n=\langle q_n,q_{n+1},q_{n+2}\rangle\) denote the additive numerical semigroup generated by the indicated three numbers. Then
\[
S_n\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n=2.
\]
At \(n=2\), \(S_2=\langle5,13\rangle\), its Frobenius number is \(47\), its genus is \(24\), and its type is \(1\). For every \(n\ge3\), the three generators are minimal, \(S_n\) is nonsymmetric, and its type is exactly \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All indices \(n\) are integers at least \(2\). For a cofinite submonoid \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), write \(F(S)\) for the greatest integer outside \(S\), \(g(S)\) for the number of nonnegative integers outside \(S\), and \(t(S)\) for the number of pseudo-Frobenius numbers. Symmetry means \(m\in S\) if and only if \(F(S)-m\notin S\) for every integer \(m\); equivalently, \(2g(S)=F(S)+1\). The recognized three-generator criterion used here is: a minimally three-generated symmetric numerical semigroup must have a pair of generators of gcd \(d>1\), and the third generator belongs to the semigroup generated by the pair divided by \(d\) [2]. For a three-generated numerical semigroup, its type is \(1\) or \(2\), with type \(1\) exactly in the symmetric case [3]. These are general results, not new assertions.

## Proof
Write
\[
a=2n^2-2n+1,\qquad b=2n^2+2n+1,\qquad c=2n^2+6n+5.
\]
Then \(a<b<c\). Since \(b-a=4n\) and \(a\equiv1\pmod n\), and since \(c-b=4(n+1)\) and \(b\equiv1\pmod{n+1}\), all of \(a,b,c\) are odd and
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,c)=1.
\]
Moreover \(c-a=4(2n+1)\) and \(2a=(2n+1)(2n-3)+5\). Consequently
\[
\gcd(a,c)=\gcd(a,2n+1)=\gcd(5,2n+1)
=\begin{cases}5,&n\equiv2\pmod5,\\1,&n\not\equiv2\pmod5.\end{cases}
\]
In particular the three generators have total gcd \(1\), so \(S_n\) is a numerical semigroup.

For \(n=2\), the generators are \(5,13,25\), and \(25=5\cdot5\). Sylvester's two-generator formulas give \(F(S_2)=5\cdot13-5-13=47\) and \(g(S_2)=(5-1)(13-1)/2=24\). Every two-generator numerical semigroup is symmetric and of type \(1\).

For \(n\ge3\), all three generators are minimal. Indeed \(a\) is the smallest and \(b\) is not a multiple of \(a\), because \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(a>1\). For \(n\ge6\) we have \(c<2a\), \(c<2b\), and \(0<c-b=4(n+1)<a\). Thus the equation \(c=xa+yb\), with nonnegative integers \(x,y\), has no solution. For the remaining indices \(n=3,4,5\), the respective triples are \((13,25,41)\), \((25,41,61)\), and \((41,61,85)\). In each case \(c<2b\), neither \(c\) nor \(c-b\) is a multiple of \(a\); so again \(c\notin\langle a,b\rangle\).

If \(n\not\equiv2\pmod5\), all three pairs are coprime; the necessary three-generator symmetry criterion [2] therefore rules out symmetry.

It remains to consider \(n=5k+2\), where \(k\ge1\). The unique non-coprime pair is \((a,c)\), with gcd \(5\). Put
\[
A=a/5=10k^2+6k+1,\quad C=c/5=10k^2+14k+5,\quad B=b=50k^2+50k+13.
\]
Symmetry would require \(B=xA+yC\) for nonnegative integers \(x,y\) [2]. If \(k\ge2\), direct subtraction gives \(4C<B<6A\), and \(A<C\). Consequently \(x+y=5\); otherwise the proposed sum is at most \(4C\) or at least \(6A\). Substituting \(x=5-y\) yields
\[
y=\frac{B-5A}{C-A}=\frac{20k+8}{8k+4}=2+\frac{k}{2k+1},
\]
which is not an integer. If \(k=1\), the hypothetical equation is \(113=17x+29y\). It forces \(0\le y\le3\) but also \(12y\equiv11\pmod{17}\), so \(y\equiv8\pmod{17}\), again impossible. Thus \(B\notin\langle A,C\rangle\) for all \(k\ge1\), and the symmetry criterion excludes every \(n\equiv2\pmod5\) with \(n\ge3\).

We have shown \(S_n\) is nonsymmetric and minimally three-generated for every \(n\ge3\). The general type dichotomy for embedding dimension three [3] now gives \(t(S_n)=2\), completing the proof.

## Verification
A standalone Python checker, `verify_centered_square.py`, computes each Apéry set using shortest paths modulo the smallest generator, for every \(2\le n\le80\). It independently recovers \(F(S_n)\), \(g(S_n)\), and the claimed symmetry dichotomy; it also checks all three pairwise gcd formulas, generator minimality, and absence of the exceptional gluing relation in this finite range. It reports `VERIFY_OK`. These checks support, but do not replace, the infinite proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
The centered-square pair \(\langle q_n,q_{n+1}\rangle\), not its three-generator extension, appears in OEIS A069760 [1]. Fel gives the necessary and sufficient gluing criterion for a symmetric three-generated numerical semigroup and applies it to Fibonacci and Lucas triples [2]. Robles-Pérez and Rosales give formulas and a type discussion for general three-generator semigroups under specified hypotheses, without isolating the centered-square triple family [3]. Fel's study of ordinary consecutive perfect squares, cubes, and quartics [4] concerns different generators. The present calculation classifies precisely when the centered-square triple can satisfy the gluing criterion; general criteria alone do not state the result for this family.

## Limitations
The theorem classifies symmetry and type but does not compute a formula for \(F(S_n)\) or \(g(S_n)\) at \(n\ge3\). Its originality has been assessed against the cited accessible literature and mathematical search records, not by exhaustive examination of all possible publications. A stronger general classification or an unlocated specialization could cover the same statement. Independent mathematical and priority review has not been performed.
