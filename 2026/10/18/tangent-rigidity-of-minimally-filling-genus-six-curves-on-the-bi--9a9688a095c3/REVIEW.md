# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The filling space has dimension \(11\), proved from the two factorwise rational-point ideals. Geometric smoothness is tested with a Gröbner unit-ideal criterion on the entire affine-chart cover, not by finite-field sampling. Two independent eleven-parameter constructions and monomial orders reproduce \(66\) smooth forms, \(2145\) unordered pairs, and the exact common-tangent histogram \(\{3:642,5:981,7:468,9:54\}\). In characteristic two, shared nonzero gradients make the nonzero sum singular at that point, proving the no-pencil corollary. Independent external replication remains unperformed.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
The closest primary article by Homma and Kim establishes sharp minimum filling bidegrees and existence, not rational-tangent interactions of all smooth binary filling curves. Asgarli–Ghioca concern smooth hypersurface pencils over finite fields and do not determine this embedded filling-system obstruction; the later Ahammed–Campbell–Moon paper addresses existence for related bidegrees. Relevant published material, available prior claim summaries, and targeted semantic literature searches did not identify the exact paired tangent spectrum or a stronger result implying it. Individual-curve coefficient tables could nonetheless yield this statistic by additional computation; an unindexed prior pairwise enumeration remains a genuine priority risk. Absence of a search hit is not offered as a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
Homma–Kim's smallest binary filling bidegree is a motivated extremal setting, and whether compatible smooth members span a smooth pencil is a natural next question after their existence result and the finite-field smooth-pencil literature. The sharp universal lower bound of three shared rational tangents, all four permitted overlap multiplicities, and the global obstruction in the presence of \(512\) locally nonsingular pencils provide structural information inaccessible from merely counting individual smooth members. The result does not claim a general classification in other bidegrees.

## Limitations
Only bidegree \((4,3)\) on the split surface over \(\mathbf F_2\) is proved. The complete proof uses an exhaustive computer-assisted calculation, with openly provided standalone source and machine-checkable outputs. Bibliographic priority and implementation correctness would benefit from external audit.
