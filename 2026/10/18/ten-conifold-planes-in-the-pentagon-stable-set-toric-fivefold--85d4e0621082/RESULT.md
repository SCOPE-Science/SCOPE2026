# Ten conifold planes in the pentagon stable-set toric fivefold

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Let \(C_5\) be the cycle on vertices indexed by \(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\), and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^5\) be its stable-set polytope. Write \(X_P\) for the normal projective toric variety of the normal fan of \(P\). Then the reduced singular locus of the fivefold \(X_P\) is the union of **exactly ten irreducible surfaces**, each isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^2_k\). They split into two rotation orbits of five components, corresponding to the triangles
\[
A_i=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i,e_i+e_{i+2},e_i+e_{i+3}\},\qquad
B_i=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i+e_{i+2},e_i+e_{i+3},e_{i+1}+e_{i+3}\},
\]
where indices are read modulo five and \(e_i\) denotes a coordinate unit vector. In the correspondence between faces and torus orbits, each component is the orbit closure of one of these triangles.

At the generic point of every component, the transverse affine toric singularity is the threefold ordinary double point \(uv-wz=0\); its multiplicity is two and its local divisor class group is \(\mathbb Z\). The singular locus contains exactly forty torus orbits, grouped by orbit dimensions as ten zero-dimensional, twenty one-dimensional, and ten two-dimensional orbits. Every simplicial cone in the normal fan is unimodular, so the singular locus equals the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus set-theoretically.

The incidence geometry is also exact: of the 45 unordered pairs of irreducible components, ten pairs are disjoint, twenty pairs intersect at a point, and fifteen pairs intersect along a line. The singular locus has exactly five distinct torus-invariant lines contained in three components each, and fifteen other torus-invariant lines contained in only one component. Five torus-fixed points lie on five components each; five other singular torus-fixed points lie on one component each.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is algebraically closed; there is no restriction on characteristic. The polytope \(P\) is the convex hull of incidence vectors of independent sets of the five-cycle, with no loops or diagonals. The toric variety is defined via the **normal fan** of this full-dimensional lattice polytope, with lattice \(\mathbb Z^5\). The cone at a face \(F\) is generated by the primitive outward normals to facets containing \(F\), and the corresponding torus orbit has dimension \(\dim F\). All singular-locus equalities above refer to reduced underlying varieties or subsets, not to a claim about the Jacobian singular scheme.

## Proof
**1. The complete inequality and vertex descriptions.** The eleven vertices are \(0\), the five \(e_i\), and the five \(e_i+e_{i+2}\). The eleven facet inequalities are
\[
x_i\ge0,\qquad x_i+x_{i+1}\le1\quad(i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z),\qquad \sum_i x_i\le2.
\]
Each listed vertex satisfies these inequalities. They define a bounded polyhedron, because the first ten imply \(0\le x_i\le1\). The attached exact checker solves every five-equation subset of the eleven bounding hyperplanes (462 cases) and establishes that the only feasible vertices are the eleven listed above. The rank-four incidence of each inequality also verifies that all eleven inequalities are irredundant facets. Thus this is a complete \(H\)-description of \(P\), not merely a relaxation.

**2. Exhaustive face and smoothness test.** Every nonempty face of a polytope is an intersection of facets. The checker exhausts all 2,048 subsets of the eleven facets, represents each nonempty face by its exact set of vertices, and computes its dimension by rational row reduction. The complete face vector, in dimensions zero through five, is
\[
(11,35,50,35,11,1).
\]
For each face \(F\), it identifies the full set of tight facets and their primitive integral outward normals. The normal cone has dimension \(5-\dim F\). A toric orbit is smooth precisely when this cone has exactly that many rays and they form a basis of the saturated lattice in their span; in the simplicial case this is tested by the greatest common divisor of maximal minors. All simplicial cones pass the unimodularity test. The nonsmooth faces number ten vertices, twenty edges, and ten triangles, with no nonsmooth faces of greater dimension. The inclusion-maximal nonsmooth faces are exactly the ten triangles \(A_i,B_i\). The toric orbit-closure correspondence therefore yields precisely the ten claimed irreducible singular components, and establishes the asserted orbit counts and the equivalence of singular and non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial loci.

**3. Geometry of each component.** The two integral edge differences of each of the ten triangles generate the full affine-difference lattice of that face: the greatest common divisor of their \(2\)-by-\(2\) coordinate minors is one. Hence the intrinsic lattice triangle is unimodular, its normal-fan toric surface is \(\mathbb P^2\), and the face orbit closure inside \(X_P\) is \(\mathbb P^2\).

**4. Transverse singularities.** It is enough to consider \(A_0\) and \(B_0\) under cyclic rotation. At \(A_0\) the four primitive normal rays are \(-e_1,-e_4,e_0+e_1,e_4+e_0\), with primitive relation
\[
(-e_1)+(e_0+e_1)=(-e_4)+(e_4+e_0).
\]
At \(B_0\) the four rays are \(-e_4,e_0+e_1,e_2+e_3,\sum_j e_j\), with relation
\[
(-e_4)+\sum_j e_j=(e_0+e_1)+(e_2+e_3).
\]
In both cases these four rays have rank three, generate the full saturated lattice in their three-dimensional span, and any three form a lattice basis of that span (verified from integer maximal minors). Thus each transverse cone is integrally equivalent to the cone over a unit lattice parallelogram, whose affine toric coordinate ring is \(k[u,v,w,z]/(uv-wz)\). The orbit has dimension two; generically the ambient fivefold is a smooth two-torus factor times this threefold node. Its multiplicity is two. By the standard toric divisor exact sequence, the class group of this transverse threefold node is \(\mathbb Z\).

**5. Incidence of components.** For torus-invariant orbit closures, their intersection is the orbit closure of the common face when it exists, and is empty otherwise. The complete exact face intersections among \(A_i,B_i\) give ten empty intersections, twenty common vertices, and fifteen common edges when pairs are counted. Across all twenty singular edges, five are contained in three of the triangular components and fifteen in one. Exactly five nonzero-vertex singleton faces occur on one component each and five stable-pair vertices on five components each. This proves every stated intersection count.

## Verification
The standalone file `CHECK.py` uses only the Python standard library and exact integer/rational arithmetic. Execute `python3 CHECK.py` in the directory containing it. It asserts the full feasible-vertex enumeration; irredundancy of all eleven facets; the full face vector; all normal-cone dimensions, ranks, and lattice indices; the exact ten maximal singular faces; both five-element component orbits; the unimodularity of the component triangles; the balanced four-ray transverse relations and saturation; and all incidence counts. A successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK` and explicit summary lines. Finite exhaustive enumeration is sufficient here because the entire polytope and fan have finitely many faces. The separate standard toric correspondence between cones and local singularities is supplied in the proof, not inferred from output alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsuda, Ohsugi, and Shibata (first public preprint 2016) investigate stable-set toric rings, normality and Gröbner bases, including the ring-graph case. Their results establish useful global algebraic context but do not furnish the explicit pentagon normal-fan singular strata or their transverse conifold types. Engström and Norén's work concerns graph-homomorphism toric ideals and Gröbner bases; its available descriptions do not directly give this classification. The present result is a **normal-fan local singularity and incidence** calculation, not a claim that the stable-set polytope itself or its toric ideal was previously unknown.

## Limitations
This is a classification for the five-cycle only, not for every odd cycle or every stable-set toric variety. No resolution, intersection-theoretic Chow class, full Jacobian singular scheme, or all singular completed local rings at multiple-component intersections is asserted. The generic transverse conifold description does not assert that the total space is a product along the entire plane, especially at intersections of components. A published equivalent classification under different indexing or terminology is a residual literature risk; no independent external audit has been performed.
