# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Each plant loss indicator has marginal probability \(q^{d_i}\), and every pair has joint probability \(q^{|N_i\cup N_j|}\). The derived variance-difference polynomial follows immediately. The exhaustive verifier checks all connected incidence matrices with at most nine total vertices and all connected-output checkerboard swaps. Independent connected-graph recurrence counts check completeness. Exact integer coefficients after substituting \(q=x/(1+x)\) certify a constant sign for every probability in \((0,1)\), not merely a sampled grid. The ten-species witness is verified both from pair unions and independently from all \(32\) failure outcomes at two rational probabilities.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** A previous arbitrary degree-matched two-network coextinction-variance reversal occurs on nine species, but the pair is not related by one checkerboard switch and its theorem does not establish one-switch minimality. Prior one-switch NODF/modularity results use different invariants. Ramos-Jiliberto et al. (2012) study dynamical ecological rewiring and persistence, not exact static one-switch variance differences; the complete accessible paper was read. The 2010 Staniczenko food-web rewiring paper was accessible only by abstract, leaving an explicit bibliographic risk; nothing uninspected is asserted not to cover the claim. Research-index and further primary-literature queries found no equivalent theorem. Independent originality audit remains pending.

**Value — PASS.** Swapping two pollinator incidences preserves every species' degree and therefore the expected number of lost plants, but at ten species it can exchange the ordering of loss-count uncertainty as pollinator failure changes. The minimum-degree-two ten-species network ensures the effect does not depend on isolated vertices or degree-one specialists. The sharp cutoff is a concrete boundary for degree-controlled stochastic ecological network interventions, with an exact stand-alone test case.

The model omits adaptive rewiring during failures, stochastic abundance dynamics, and heterogeneously correlated pollinator failures. A finite small-network classification is not an unrestricted higher-order classification. Source-access limitations and absence of independent validation remain.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
