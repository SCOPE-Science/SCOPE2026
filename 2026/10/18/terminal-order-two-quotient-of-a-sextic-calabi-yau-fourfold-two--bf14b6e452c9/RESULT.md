# Terminal order-two quotient of a sextic Calabi–Yau fourfold: two genus-ten curves and exact Hodge diamonds

## Finding

Let \(f\in\mathbb C[u_0,u_1,u_2]\) be any homogeneous sextic whose plane curve \(C=V(f)\subset\mathbb P^2\) is smooth. Put
\[
X_f=\{f(u_0,u_1,u_2)+f(v_0,v_1,v_2)=0\}\subset\mathbb P^5,
\qquad \sigma([u:v])=[v:u],\quad Y_f=X_f/\langle\sigma\rangle.
\]
The singular locus of \(Y_f\) consists of exactly two disjoint curves isomorphic to \(C\), each of genus \(10\). Along both curves the completed transverse germ is \(\mathbb A^3/\{\pm1\}\), of cyclic type \(\frac12(1,1,1)\); the quotient is terminal, non-Gorenstein of canonical index \(2\), and has \(K_{Y_f}^{[2]}\cong\mathcal O_{Y_f}\). Its complex orbit space is simply connected, while its smooth locus has fundamental group \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

Blowing up the **reduced** two-curve singular locus gives a smooth projective fourfold \(Z_f\) whose two disjoint exceptional divisors are \(C\times\mathbb P^2\). Writing \(E\) for their sum, the discrepancy identity is
\[
K_{Z_f}=\pi^*K_{Y_f}+\tfrac12 E.
\]
The complete rational Betti-number vectors in degrees \(0\) through \(8\) are
\[
(b_i(Y_f))_{i=0}^8=(1,0,1,0,1283,0,1,0,1),\qquad
(b_i(Z_f))_{i=0}^8=(1,0,3,40,1287,40,3,0,1).
\]
Equivalently, the only nonzero rational Hodge numbers of \(Y_f\) are
\[
h^{0,0}=h^{1,1}=h^{3,3}=h^{4,4}=1,\qquad
h^{3,1}=h^{1,3}=208,\qquad h^{2,2}=867,
\]
and the only nonzero rational Hodge numbers of \(Z_f\) are
\[
h^{0,0}=h^{4,4}=1,\quad h^{1,1}=h^{3,3}=3,\quad
h^{2,1}=h^{1,2}=h^{3,2}=h^{2,3}=20,\quad
h^{3,1}=h^{1,3}=208,\quad h^{2,2}=871.
\]
In particular \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Y_f)=1287\) and \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Z_f)=1215\). These statements hold for every smooth plane sextic \(C\); no Waring-rank condition is imposed.

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is over \(\mathbb C\). Cohomology and Hodge numbers of the possibly singular quotient are ordinary singular cohomology with \(\mathbb Q\)-coefficients and its natural pure Hodge structure, **not** intersection cohomology. The expression \(K_{Y_f}^{[2]}\) denotes the reflexive square of the dualizing sheaf. The numerical list is independent of the smooth sextic chosen. The two fixed curves are the sections of \(X_f\) by the disjoint eigenspace planes \(u=v\) and \(u=-v\) in \(\mathbb P^5\).

## Proof

**Smoothness and fixed points.** Since \(C\) is smooth, the three partial derivatives of \(f\) have no simultaneous projective zero. The six partial derivatives of the defining polynomial of \(X_f\) therefore cannot vanish at a projective point, so \(X_f\) is smooth. Adjunction gives \(K_{X_f}\cong\mathcal O_{X_f}\), and Lefschetz gives \(\pi_1(X_f)=1\). If \([u:v]\) is projectively fixed by the block swap, then \(v=u\) or \(v=-u\) projectively, and restricting the sextic equation to either eigenspace gives \(2f(u)=0\). Hence the fixed locus is precisely \(C_+\sqcup C_-\), with \(C_\pm\cong C\), and \(g(C)=\frac{(6-1)(6-2)}2=10\).

**Local quotient.** At either fixed curve the ambient tangent bundle separates into the invariant tangent to the eigenspace and a rank-three anti-invariant normal space. The restriction of the sextic equation has nonzero derivative in the invariant normal direction to \(C\) inside its plane, whereas all anti-invariant first derivatives vanish by symmetry. The equivariant holomorphic implicit-function theorem gives local coordinates \((t,z_1,z_2,z_3)\) on \(X_f\) in which \(C_\pm=\{z_1=z_2=z_3=0\}\) and \(\sigma(t,z)=(t,-z)\). More precisely, the anti-invariant normal bundle \(N_{C_\pm/X_f}\) is the restriction of the normal bundle of an eigenspace \(\mathbb P^2\) in \(\mathbb P^5\), namely \(\mathcal O_C(1)^{\oplus3}\). The invariant ring in three \(z\)-variables is generated by the six quadratic products \(z_iz_j\), with the \(2\times2\) symmetric-minor relations; this is the affine cone over the Veronese surface. Its punctured transverse germ is smooth, and the vertex is a terminal cyclic quotient singularity \(\frac12(1,1,1)\), of index two (its nonidentity age is \(3/2>1\)). No other points have stabilizers. Thus the reduced singular locus is exactly the two genus-ten curves.

The block swap on six coordinates has determinant \(-1\), so it negates the nowhere-vanishing holomorphic four-form, represented by a Griffiths residue of the standard projective volume form. The canonical form does not descend as an invertible canonical section, although its square descends. Thus the quotient has global reflexive canonical order and index precisely two. Removing a complex codimension-three smooth submanifold leaves \(X_f\) simply connected. Since its complement of the fixed curves is a free connected double covering of \(Y_f^{\mathrm{reg}}\), one obtains \(\pi_1(Y_f^{\mathrm{reg}})=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Armstrong's orbit-space theorem, applied to the simply connected \(X_f\) with its fixed-point involution, yields \(\pi_1(Y_f)=1\).

**Blowup and discrepancy.** Let \(\widetilde X\) be the blowup of \(X_f\) along the two smooth fixed curves. Each exceptional divisor is \(\mathbb P(N_{C_\pm/X_f})\cong C\times\mathbb P^2\). The lifted involution fixes each exceptional divisor pointwise and changes sign on its normal line. Its quotient is therefore smooth. Locally, blowing up the invariant Veronese cone at the vertex yields the total space of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-2)\); this identifies \(\widetilde X/\langle\sigma\rangle\) with the blowup \(Z_f\) of \(Y_f\) along the reduced singular curves. If \(D\) is the exceptional divisor upstairs, the blowup formula and double-cover Hurwitz formula give
\[
K_{\widetilde X}=\beta^*K_{X_f}+2D,
\qquad K_{\widetilde X}=r^*K_{Z_f}+D,
\qquad r^*E=2D,
\]
while \(K_{X_f}=q^*K_{Y_f}\) in rational divisor classes because \(X_f\to Y_f\) is quasi-étale. Subtracting proves the coefficient \(\frac12\). The same orbit-space argument for the blowup shows \(\pi_1(Z_f)=1\).

**Invariant middle cohomology.** A smooth ternary sextic has Jacobian algebra \(A=\mathbb C[u_0,u_1,u_2]/(\partial f)\) with Hilbert series \(H_A(s)=(1+s+s^2+s^3+s^4)^3\): its partial derivatives are a regular sequence of three quintics. The Jacobian algebra of \(X_f\) is \(R=A\otimes A\), with series \(H_A(s)^2\). Griffiths's residue description identifies the primitive \(H^{4-q,q}(X_f)\) with \(R_{6q}\), for each \(0\le q\le4\). The residue action of \(\sigma\) is **minus** the exchange action on \(A\otimes A\), because the six-coordinate swap has determinant \(-1\). In total degree \(6q\), the exchange trace is \(\dim A_{3q}\) (only equal-degree pairs can contribute to trace). Therefore
\[
\dim H^{4-q,q}(X_f)^{\sigma}
=\tfrac12\!\left([s^{6q}]H_A(s)^2-[s^{3q}]H_A(s)\right).
\]
For \(q=0,1,2,3,4\), the pairs (total dimension, exchange trace) are \((1,1),(426,10),(1751,19),(426,10),(1,1)\), giving invariant primitive dimensions \((0,208,866,208,0)\). The ambient hyperplane classes are invariant and contribute one Tate class in even degrees. Transfer for a finite quotient identifies \(H^*(Y_f;\mathbb Q)\) with \(H^*(X_f;\mathbb Q)^{\sigma}\), including Hodge structures. This proves all asserted Hodge numbers and Betti numbers for \(Y_f\).

**Resolution cohomology.** The codimension-three blowup formula adds \(H^*(C)(-1)\oplus H^*(C)(-2)\) for **each** fixed curve. All these new classes are invariant, since \(\sigma\) acts trivially on the base curves and their projective normal fibers. Passing to the quotient therefore gives
\[
H^{\bullet}(Z_f)=H^{\bullet}(Y_f)\ \oplus\ 2H^{\bullet}(C)(-1)\ \oplus\ 2H^{\bullet}(C)(-2)
\]
as graded rational Hodge structures. The plane sextic has Hodge numbers \(1,10,10,1\) in bidegrees \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\). Adding their twists yields the displayed diamond and Betti vector. The Euler numbers follow. An independent check uses \(\chi(X_f)=2610\), \(\chi(C_+\sqcup C_-)=-36\), so \(\chi(Y_f)=(2610-36)/2=1287\). Each exceptional \(\mathbb P^2\)-fiber replaces one point, changing Euler characteristic by \(2\chi(C)\) per component; hence \(\chi(Z_f)=1287+2(-18)(2)=1215\).

## Relationship to prior work

Diamond introduced and studied precisely this Shioda-type sextic fourfold and its block-swap involution, concentrating on invariant Hodge coniveau and a Waring-rank-three case of the generalized Hodge conjecture. The present calculation instead determines the intrinsic quotient's complete singular strata, local canonical behavior, explicit blowup, and rational Hodge diamonds, uniformly for **all** smooth plane sextics. Related constructions by Camere, Garbagnati, and Mongardi examine quotients of hyperkähler fourfolds by involutions with fixed surfaces and different crepant-resolution behavior; their theorems do not determine this fourfold's two fixed curves or the terminal index-two quotient. Classical residue, blowup, finite-quotient, and orbit-fundamental-group theorems supply ingredients and are not claimed as new. No absolute bibliographic priority is asserted.

## Limitations

The calculation is for ordinary rational cohomology, not integral cohomology or intersection cohomology. In particular, integral torsion, the full cup-product ring, and detailed mixed structures on the smooth-locus complement are not determined. The explicit blowup is not crepant; no statement about a crepant resolution is asserted. A computation indirectly implied by a broader unindexed theorem remains a literature-priority risk. The result makes no assertion about the generalized Hodge conjecture for arbitrary Waring rank.
