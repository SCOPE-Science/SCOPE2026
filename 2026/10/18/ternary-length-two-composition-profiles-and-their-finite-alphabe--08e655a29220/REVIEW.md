# Scientific review of the ternary length-two profile theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The multigraph Euler-trail bijection classifies the compatible singleton-count vectors for every edge multiset. Partitioning ternary connected graphs by support size gives the rational function exactly; the unrestricted-alphabet asymptotic follows from parity-character equidistribution and a lower-dimensional exclusion of missing-edge graphs. Exact independent finite calculations verify ternary words through length 11, ternary multigraphs through length 28, and the graph theorem for alphabet four through length 7. These tests corroborate, rather than replace, the all-length proof.

**Originality — PASS against the material inspected.** The primary length-limited-composition paper defines the general observable but does not enumerate ternary length-two profiles; the exact previously published binary classification is confined to alphabet two. Ordered multispectra retain orientation, and directed cyclic Markov-type counts have different constraints and exponent. This comparison considers mathematical implication rather than titles alone. The strongest residual risk is an unindexed equivalent undirected-graph enumeration.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result gives the exact number of distinguishable ternary messages for every blocklength, plus the sharp asymptotic constant for all fixed alphabets, establishing substantive structure beyond a small finite census or routine parameter substitution. The enumeration identifies precisely how Euler-trail endpoints alter the profile count.

**Scientific limitations.** The exact generating function applies to ternary observations of factor lengths one and two, not longer factors, noisy observations or unlabeled alphabets. The general theorem provides a leading coefficient only. No independent audit has been performed.
