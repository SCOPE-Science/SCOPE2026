For the central trinomial coefficient \(T_n=[x^n](1+x+x^2)^n\), divisibility by exactly one factor of \(3\) is characterized by a unique digit \(2\) in the ternary expansion, occurring in the units place or immediately below a zero digit. Consequently exactly \((L+2)2^{L-2}\) indices below \(3^L\) have valuation one for every \(L\ge2\), giving a sharp first prime-power layer beyond the classical modulo-three digit rule.
