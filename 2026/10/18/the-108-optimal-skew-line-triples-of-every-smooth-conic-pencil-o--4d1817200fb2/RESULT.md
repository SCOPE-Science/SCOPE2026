# The 108 optimal skew-line triples of every smooth conic pencil over \(\mathbf F_4\)
## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbf F_4\). A *rationally smooth conic pencil* means a projective line of ternary quadratic forms over \(k\) for which all five \(k\)-rational members define geometrically smooth conics. A line is *skew to a conic* if the two have no common \(k\)-rational point; a set of lines *witnesses* a pencil if every rational member has at least one skew line in that set.

There are exactly \(20{,}160\) rationally smooth conic pencils among the \(93{,}093\) rational pencils in the projective space of conics. They form a single orbit under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), with stabilizer of order three. For **every** such pencil, exactly 15 of the 21 rational lines are skew to at least one member, and exactly \(108\) unordered triples of distinct rational lines witness the entire pencil. No one or two rational lines can witness all five members. In particular, the minimum witness cardinality is exactly three.

For an explicit representative, put \(k=\mathbf F_2[\omega]/(\omega^2+\omega+1)\) and
\[
Q=XZ+Y^2,\qquad R=XY+\omega Z^2,\qquad \mathcal P=\{sQ+tR:[s:t]\in\mathbf P^1(k)\}.
\]
An optimal triple is \(X+Z=0\), \(X+Y+Z=0\), and \(\omega X+\omega Y+Z=0\). The five conics in the order \(R,Q,Q+R,Q+\omega R,Q+\omega^2R\) are covered by the respective binary masks \(01100\), \(01001\), and \(10010\), whose union is \(11111\).

## Assumptions and scope
Pencils are two-dimensional \(k\)-linear subspaces of the six-dimensional vector space of homogeneous ternary quadratics, modulo the usual common nonzero scalar; hence each has exactly five rational members. Their geometric special members over extensions may be singular; the hypothesis requires smoothness only for rational members. Lines, coverage and witness counts are all over \(k\), not its algebraic closure. The 108 count treats triples as unordered sets of three distinct rational projective lines, not modulo projectivities.

## Proof
1. **Smooth representative and its base frame.** In characteristic two, the gradient of \(sQ+tR\) has its unique projective critical point at \([0:s:t]\) when \((s,t)\ne(0,0)\). Direct substitution, or rescaling, gives a nonzero scalar multiple of \(s^3+\omega t^3\), which never vanishes for a nonzero pair over \(k\), because every nonzero element of \(k\) has cube one and \(\omega\ne1\). Thus the five rational members are smooth. The base locus is \([1:0:0]\) together with \([\alpha^2:\alpha:1]\), \([\alpha^8:\alpha^4:1]\), \([\alpha^{32}:\alpha^{16}:1]\), where \(\alpha^3=\omega\). These are four points in general position: all lie on the smooth conic \(Q\), so no three are collinear. The cubic \(T^3+\omega\) is irreducible over \(k\).
2. **Stabilizer and orbit.** The four base points form a projective frame. Any rational projectivity preserving the pencil permutes these points and commutes with Frobenius, hence fixes the unique rational base point and cyclically permutes the three conjugates: its order divides three. Conversely each cyclic permutation of the conjugate points extends uniquely to a projectivity taking the projective frame to itself; Frobenius-equivariance makes this projectivity rational over \(k\). The stabilizer therefore has order three. Since \(|\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)|=60{,}480\), the orbit has \(20{,}160\) pencils.
3. **Exact complete pencil count.** Every ternary quadratic can be written \(aX^2+bY^2+cZ^2+dXY+eXZ+fYZ\). In characteristic two, if \((d,e,f)=(0,0,0)\), its gradient vanishes identically and the conic is geometrically singular. Otherwise the gradient vanishes precisely at \([f:e:d]\), and the conic is smooth if and only if its quadratic form does not vanish there. The accompanying standalone verifier enumerates all \((4^6-1)/(4-1)=1{,}365\) projective conic equations, finds exactly \(1{,}008\) smooth ones, and enumerates all \({6\brack2}_4=93{,}093\) rational pencils as distinct five-element sets. Exactly \(20{,}160\) have five smooth rational members. This equals the orbit size in Step 2, so every such pencil is projectively equivalent to the representative.
4. **Optimal witnesses.** The verifier exhaustively lists all 21 rational points and all 21 rational lines, checking line–conic disjointness by exact field arithmetic. For the representative, 15 lines are skew to some member, and exactly 108 of the \(\binom{21}{3}=1{,}330\) distinct line triples have full five-conic coverage. The three displayed lines are one such triple. These counts are invariant under rational projectivities, so the count applies uniformly; the verifier additionally repeats the exact 108-triple enumeration separately on all 20,160 smooth pencils. To see that two lines never suffice independently of computation, let two rational lines meet in a rational point \(P\). If \(P\) belongs to the base locus, both lines meet every member. Otherwise the evaluation functional \([s:t]\mapsto sQ(P)+tR(P)\) has a nonzero rational kernel, giving a rational member through \(P\). Both lines therefore meet that member, so neither is skew to it.

The script gives `VERIFY_OK`; it exhaustively verifies the 1,365 conics, 93,093 pencils, 20,160 rationally smooth pencils, 21 rational lines, 15 active skew lines per smooth pencil, all 108 witness triples per pencil, and impossibility of two witnesses. The finite enumeration is exhaustive over the full specified finite sets and the geometric smoothness test is an exact algebraic condition, not finite-point sampling. The projective-orbit argument is proved symbolically as above.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (arXiv:2203.07169), discuss the earlier existence construction of smooth rational conic pencils using one rational and three conjugate base points. Asgarli, Ghioca and Yip, *Existence of pencils with nonblocking hypersurfaces* (arXiv:2301.09215), prove existence of nonblocking pencils and introduce efficient three-line witnesses, giving a two-line obstruction and a three-witness construction under different, odd-characteristic hypotheses. Thus neither conic-pencil existence, the four-point base-locus construction, nor the abstract two-line obstruction is claimed as new. The present result is the exact all-pencil classification and **uniform 108-optimal-triple incidence theorem in characteristic two**. An unindexed equivalent finite-geometry enumeration is a residual bibliographic risk.

## Limitations
This is a theorem only for rationally smooth conic pencils over \(\mathbf F_4\). It does not classify singular rational pencils, general degree or field-size families, or rational isomorphism classes of ordered pencils. The group-orbit statement and the exact numerical incidence counts have different proof inputs: the former is a projective-frame argument combined with an exhaustive pencil count; the latter is an exhaustive exact finite-field computation. No independent review is asserted here.
