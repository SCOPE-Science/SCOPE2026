# The 58 singular components of the non-Fano matroid normal-fan toric sixfold

## Finding
Let \(M\) be the rank-three non-Fano matroid on \(E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}\), obtained by relaxing the Fano circuit-hyperplane \(H=\{0,1,2\}\). Write \(P(M)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_B:B\text{ is a basis of }M\}\) and let \(X\) be the normal projective toric variety of the normal fan of \(P(M)\), over any algebraically closed field. Then \(X\) has dimension six and its **reduced singular locus has exactly 58 irreducible components**, in four combinatorial classes:

| Number | Toric component | Codimension in \(X\) | Generic transverse affine germ | Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| 18 | \(\mathbb P^3\) | 3 | Rank-one \(2\times2\) determinantal cone (conifold) | 2 |
| 24 | \(\mathbb P^2\) | 4 | Rank-one \(2\times3\) determinantal cone | 3 |
| 15 | \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) | 4 | Rank-one \(2\times3\) determinantal cone | 3 |
| 1 | A single torus-fixed point | 6 | Rank-one \(3\times4\) determinantal cone | 10 |

The isolated component is precisely the fixed point corresponding to the newly relaxed basis \(H\). It lies in none of the other 57 components. Hence the reduced singular locus is disconnected and non-equidimensional. The 18 three-dimensional components have Gorenstein generic transverse singularities; the 39 two-dimensional components and the isolated point have non-\(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein transverse singularities. **All 29 torus-fixed points are singular**, and the singular support agrees with the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial support.

## Assumptions and scope
The seven lines of the Fano configuration are
\[
\{012,034,056,135,146,236,245\}.
\]
Here a three-digit string is an unordered triple of ground elements. The bases of \(M\) are all 35 three-subsets except \(034,056,135,146,236,245\); thus there are 29 bases, including \(012\). The toric variety uses the **normal fan** of \(P(M)\), not the embedded projective toric variety parametrized by basis monomials. Work with the full saturated difference lattice \(\{z\in\mathbb Z^7:\sum_i z_i=0\}\) and its dual. The component description concerns the support of the **reduced** singular locus and the generic transverse singularity to each component, not a claim about its full singular scheme or every nongeneric local germ.

## Proof

**1. Facets and lattice.** The basis-exchange axiom holds for the above 29 triples. Computing the rank of every subset gives seven rank-one singleton flats and nine rank-two proper flats: six unrelaxed three-element Fano lines and the three two-element subsets of \(H\). The matroid base-polytope inequalities therefore simplify to the 20 irredundant facets
\[
0\le x_i\le1\quad(i\in E),\qquad
\sum_{i\in L}x_i\le2\quad
(L\in\{034,056,135,146,236,245\}),
\]
inside \(\sum_i x_i=3\). The inequalities from the three two-element rank-two flats are consequences of singleton upper bounds. All 20 displayed inequalities cut five-dimensional faces of the six-dimensional polytope. Their primitive inward normals are read in the dual of the saturated difference lattice.

**2. Exhaustive face and normal-cone classification.** Each nonempty face is an intersection of its containing facets, so closure under intersections of the 20 facet-vertex masks gives an exhaustive certificate. In dimensions zero through six the face numbers are
\[
(29,138,263,242,108,20,1),
\]
for a total of 801 nonempty faces. For each face \(F\), its normal cone has precisely the rays corresponding to facets containing \(F\), and dimension \(6-\dim F\). The coefficient matrix of these primitive normals has Smith invariant factors **all equal to one** on its nonzero diagonal, for each of the 800 nonzero cones. Hence every simplicial cone is unimodular. Non-simplicial normal cones occur for 29 vertices, 120 edges, 111 two-faces and 18 three-faces, and none for faces of higher dimension.

The torus orbit associated to a nonsimplicial cone is singular; because every simplicial cone is smooth, no other singular orbit exists. A maximal irreducible singular component corresponds to an inclusion-maximal face \(F\) whose normal cone is nonsimplicial. Exhaustive comparison of the face masks leaves exactly 58 such faces: 18 tetrahedra, 24 triangles, 15 parallelograms, and one vertex \(H\). The tetrahedral, triangular, and parallelogram face difference lattices are saturated, and their normal fans give \(\mathbb P^3\), \(\mathbb P^2\) and \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), respectively. The singleton face \(H\) is not contained in any other maximal singular face, proving that its orbit closure is an isolated singular point disconnected from the remaining singular support.

**3. Transverse singularities.** For every one of these 58 faces, the primitive normal-ray matrix has a **one-dimensional integral kernel generated by a relation all of whose coefficients are \(\pm1\)**. The number of positive and negative terms, without regard to ordering, is respectively \((2,2)\) for the tetrahedra, \((2,3)\) for either kind of two-face, and \((3,4)\) for \(H\). Its Smith invariants are all one, so the ray lattice is the entire saturated lattice of the cone. For such a cone, its coordinate ring is the semigroup algebra of
\[
\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{a}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{b}:
\sum_i u_i=\sum_j v_j\},
\]
which is generated by the vectors indexed by pairs \((i,j)\). This is the coordinate ring of the affine variety of rank-at-most-one \(a\times b\) matrices. Hence the local product along the dense toric orbit of each component has the claimed transverse model. The degree of its projective Segre base \(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\) is \(\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}\), giving multiplicities 2, 3 and 10. The affine Segre cone is Gorenstein precisely when \(a=b\); for unequal \(a,b\), its canonical class is not even \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier. This yields the stated generic canonical-type distinction.

Since all 29 vertices have nonsimplicial normal cones, every torus-fixed point is singular. Because all simplicial normal cones are unimodular, the locus of non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality has the same support as the singular locus.

## Verification
`VERIFY.py` (included with this result) is a standalone, exact Python/SymPy checker. It independently checks the full basis-exchange axiom, all proper rank-one and rank-two flats, every facet's dimension, intersection closure of all 20 facet masks, and the complete dimension-counted face lattice. It computes rational ranks, Smith normal forms over \(\mathbb Z\), all 278 nonsimplicial cones, all 58 maximal faces, the primitive relation of each maximal cone and its full-rank lattice saturation. It separately confirms the extra 15 quadrilaterals are genuine parallelograms in the lattice and that the unique isolated vertex is \(H\). The program terminates with `VERIFY_OK` and the complete four-class component census. No finite sampling or probabilistic extrapolation is used: the input polytope is finite and the facet-face enumeration is exhaustive.

## Relationship to prior work
Edmonds's matroid base-polytope inequalities give the general facet framework. Ferroni, *On the Ehrhart Polynomial of Minimal Matroids* (arXiv:2003.02679), explicitly discusses the same 29-basis non-Fano matroid, its relaxation, and its lattice-point enumerator, but does not classify the singular locus of the **normal-fan** toric variety or its transverse determinantal germs. Ferroni and Schröter, *Face enumeration for split matroid polytopes* (arXiv:2310.05487), give a broader face-enumeration framework that may subsume the displayed face vector; **the face vector is not claimed as an independent novelty**. Ardila--Benedetti--Doker (arXiv:0810.3947), the dated historical source anchoring this study, treats matroid-polytope volume and related orbit geometry, not this 58-component singular classification. The proposed contribution is the complete reduced singular-stratum census and its three integral transverse determinantal types, especially the isolated relaxed-basis singularity. Equivalent or implicit coverage in unindexed literature remains possible.

## Limitations
Only the **reduced singular support** and the **generic** transverse singularity to each irreducible component are classified. The scheme structure of the Jacobian singular locus, individual intersections of the 57 positive-dimensional components, and nongeneric completed local rings are not classified. Originality is assessed against identified literature and accessible search records, not proved from absence of matches. The verification program requires Python 3 and SymPy; its exact enumeration is specific to the seven-element non-Fano matroid and is not claimed as a uniform all-matroids result. No independent validation has been performed.
