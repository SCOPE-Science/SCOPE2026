## correctness

PASS

Transversality gives 3a+2b=18 with a=0,2,4. For a=0, each line carries at most two all-line quadruple points and at most six lines can carry two, giving at most 15 of the required 16 incidences. For a=2, counting the other eight lines along a fixed line, with at least one other line at each of its two conic intersections, gives q_C+3q_L+2t_L=6. Each of the lines on exactly one off-conic quadruple uses one conic quadruple incidence. Thus the two off-conic quadruples must share one arrangement line, and the six singly incident lines use the full quota of six. The remaining two lines each carry three of the four off-conic triples, so would meet twice. For a=4 the nine lines have three nodes and eleven triples. The node graph has three edges and all degrees even, hence is a triangle. Each of the six nontriangle lines meets the triangle lines in three distinct mixed triples and therefore belongs to exactly one of the two all-nontriangle triple pencils. The nine mixed triples are exactly the cross-grid of the two pencils. Each triangle line induces an invertible affine bijection between their grid-coordinate sets. The three bijections have pairwise disjoint graphs on the grid; relative to one, they are the identity and the two powers of a 3-cycle on the three coordinates. The induced affine map has order three and a fixed point over C, so all three lines concur, impossible for the triangle. This reconstructs the proof without assuming freeness.
