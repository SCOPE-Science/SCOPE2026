# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. For every convex polygon with genuine corners, exposed endpoints on each sampled side give an exact perimeter decomposition into local corner chord-shortening terms. The joint survival probabilities of shrinking endpoint arcs establish independent exponential limits; exponential tails and exponentially rare missed sides yield convergence of all fixed positive integer moments. Independent Gamma/Uniform coordinates prove the stated product Laplace transform, closed mean, and variance. The included checker evaluated three direct hull identities, five angular quadratures, and three exponential Monte Carlo comparisons. Those finite checks are corroborative; the general theorem rests on the complete proof.

## Originality

PASS on accessible published evidence, with residual indexing risk. The closest same-object paper is Prochno–Schütt–Sonnleitner–Werner, arXiv:2404.02870 (2024), whose full accessible article proves the Hausdorff-distance profile. Its maximum-over-corners statistic does not imply the claimed additive perimeter-shortening Laplace law without another geometric reduction. Reitzner–Schütt–Werner, arXiv:1911.05917 (2019), develops different volume/f-vector asymptotics. The circle-perimeter mean calculation by Nikitin–Polevaya (2020) uses random points on a smooth circumference rather than a fixed polygon's arclength measure. Relevant mathematical-record searches returned only Hausdorff or other observables, not the stated limit. Unindexed equivalent literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Perimeter deficit is a natural intrinsic-volume approximation error not determined by the Hausdorff error distribution or volume deficit. The result identifies its *full* nondegenerate limiting probability distribution for every fixed convex polygon, exposes independence from side lengths after normalizing by perimeter, and gives explicit first and second asymptotic moments with strict angular ordering. This is a general geometric-probability invariant, not an isolated numerical computation.

## Limits

Only fixed polygons and arclength-uniform boundary points are treated. All-angle positive-integer moment convergence is proven, but no joint regime in which the polygon changes with the sample size is claimed. Related but unindexed work remains a bibliographic risk.
