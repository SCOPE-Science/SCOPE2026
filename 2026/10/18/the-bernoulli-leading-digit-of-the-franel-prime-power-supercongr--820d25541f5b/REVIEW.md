# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim reduces the difference of prime-power Franel sums to two residue blocks. Jacobsthal's binomial congruence with its explicit exponent bounds the block with indices divisible by the prime through one extra power; Lucas's theorem supplies the nondivisible block and its height-independent alternating sign. A Bernoulli polynomial power-sum identity evaluates the remaining reciprocal-cube residue. The proof treats endpoints, exponents beginning at one, oddness of the number of residue blocks, denominators, and the exceptional-prime boundary explicitly. The exact standalone computations provide diagnostics only.

## Originality
PASS, subject to a clearly identified residual literature risk. Straub (2014, Example 3.6 and Lemma 5.1) gives the stronger-in-parameters *baseline* supercongruence modulo \(p^{3r}\), but not the Bernoulli coefficient modulo \(p^{3r+1}\). Sun (2011) studies different Franel sums. Mao (2021/2023) studies weighted Franel sums involving Euler numbers, not this difference. The classical Bernoulli characterization of Wolstenholme primes is acknowledged. Targeted searches for Franel leading digits, binomial-cube sums and Wolstenholme primes, as well as full primary-text reading, did not find an equivalent result. This does not exclude an unindexed or implicit stronger theorem.

## Value
PASS. The exact leading coefficient sharpens an established infinite family of congruences simultaneously at *all* prime-power heights and gives an arithmetic sharpness criterion involving rare Wolstenholme primes. It explains both generic exactness and exceptional extra divisibility, rather than providing a routine isolated residue table or finite improvement.
