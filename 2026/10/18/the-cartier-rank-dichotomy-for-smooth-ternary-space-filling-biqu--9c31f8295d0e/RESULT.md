# The Cartier-rank dichotomy for smooth ternary space-filling biquartics

## Finding

Let \(Q=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_3}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_3}\). There are exactly \(264\) projective equations of geometrically smooth bidegree-\((4,4)\) curves \(C\subset Q\) satisfying \(C(\mathbf F_3)=Q(\mathbf F_3)\). Every such curve is geometrically integral of genus \(9\), and its Jacobian has **either** \(3\)-rank \(8\), with \(a\)-number \(1\), **or** \(3\)-rank \(9\), with \(a\)-number \(0\). In particular no such curve has \(3\)-rank below \(8\).

Of the \(264\) equations, precisely \(216\) have \((3\text{-rank},a)=(8,1)\), and precisely \(48\) are ordinary with \((3\text{-rank},a)=(9,0)\). Under the complete ambient rational automorphism group \(\bigl(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)^2\bigr)\rtimes C_2\) (order \(1152\)), these curves split into exactly four orbits:

| \(3\)-rank | \(a\)-number | Orbit size | Stabilizer order |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 8 | 1 | 144 | 8 |
| 8 | 1 | 36 | 32 |
| 8 | 1 | 36 | 32 |
| 9 | 0 | 48 | 24 |

Thus **all ordinary smooth filling biquartics form one ambient projective orbit**, whereas the nonordinary ones form three. These are classes of embedded curves/equations, not assertions about abstract geometric isomorphism classes.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is precisely \(\mathbf F_3\); geometric smoothness is over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_3\). The filling condition imposes all \(16\) rational points of \(Q\). A projective equation is a nonzero bihomogeneous section modulo multiplication by \(\mathbf F_3^*=\{1,2\}\), with no additional quotient; orbit counts refer only to the group stated above.

Set
\[
P_X=X_0^3X_1-X_0X_1^3,\qquad P_Y=Y_0^3Y_1-Y_0Y_1^3.
\]
All filling biquartics have the form
\[
F=P_X A(Y_0,Y_1)+P_Y B(X_0,X_1),
\]
with homogeneous binary quartics \(A,B\). The sole linear presentation ambiguity is \((A,B)\mapsto(A+tP_Y,B-tP_X)\) for \(t\in\mathbf F_3\). Write the quartic coefficient vector as \(v=(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4,B_0,B_2,B_3,B_4)\), choosing the unique gauge \(B_1=0\). The coefficients use ascending exponents in the second homogeneous coordinate: \(A=\sum_{i=0}^4A_iY_0^{4-i}Y_1^i\) and analogously for \(B\).

## Proof

**1. Complete finite parameter space.** The ideal of \(Q(\mathbf F_3)\) in bidegree \((4,4)\) has dimension \(5+5-1=9\). Its projective equations number \((3^9-1)/2=9841\). The gauge above is unique; multiplying the vector by \(2\) if necessary makes its first nonzero coordinate equal to \(1\).

Each rational root of \(P_X\) or \(P_Y\) is simple, so at a rational grid point the differential of \(F\) is the direct sum of nonzero scalar multiples of \(A(Y)\) and \(B(X)\). Hence \(C\) is nonsingular at all \(16\) rational points exactly when either \(A\) is nonzero at every rational point of the second factor or \(B\) is nonzero at every rational point of the first. Exhaustive coefficient enumeration leaves \(3504\) projective equations. The \(576\)-element factor-preserving group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)^2\) partitions these into \(56\) orbits.

**2. Geometric smoothness and orbits.** For one representative of each of these \(56\) orbits, form the four affine equations obtained from \(X_a=Y_b=1\), with \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\), and compute the ideals generated by each local equation and its two partial derivatives in \(\mathbf F_3[x,y]\). An affine chart is smooth over the algebraic closure exactly when its ideal is the unit ideal, checked by exact characteristic-three Gröbner reduction. Exactly seven factor-preserving orbits pass, of sizes \(18,18,24,24,36,72,72\), totaling \(264\) equations. Including the factor swap gives four full ambient orbits of sizes \(36,36,48,144\). Their stabilizer orders follow from orbit–stabilizer for a group of order \(1152\).

**3. Exact Cartier calculation.** On \(X_0=Y_0=1\) write \(f(x,y)=F(1,x;1,y)\). Adjunction identifies \(H^0(C,\Omega_C^1)\) with the nine differentials
\[
\omega_{ij}=x^{i-1}y^{j-1}\frac{dx}{f_y},\qquad 1\le i,j\le3.
\]
In this basis, the characteristic-three Cartier matrix has entries
\[
H_{(i,j),(k,\ell)}=[x^{3i-k}y^{3j-\ell}]\,f(x,y)^2,
\qquad 1\le i,j,k,\ell\le3.
\]
This is the usual Cartier coefficient formula applied to a bihomogeneous curve on a smooth surface. Because every coefficient lies in \(\mathbf F_3\), the Frobenius-semilinear iteration is ordinary matrix iteration. The \(a\)-number equals \(9-\operatorname{rank}(H)\), while the geometric \(3\)-rank is the stable rank \(\operatorname{rank}(H^9)\).

For complete reproducibility, encode a normalized vector by the integer \(r=\sum_{i=0}^8v_i3^i\). The following seven representatives, and their ranks, were computed exactly:

| \(r\) | Factor-preserving orbit size | \(\operatorname{rank} H\) | \(\operatorname{rank}H^9\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6898 | 72 | 8 | 8 |
| 6904 | 18 | 8 | 8 |
| 6919 | 36 | 8 | 8 |
| 7858 | 72 | 8 | 8 |
| 7876 | 24 | 9 | 9 |
| 8344 | 18 | 8 | 8 |
| 8356 | 24 | 9 | 9 |

The factor swap merges representative orbits in the pairs \((6898,7858)\), \((6904,8344)\), and \((7876,8356)\); the representative \(6919\) lies in a swap-invariant orbit. The two \(24\)-element ordinary orbits therefore combine into a unique \(48\)-element ambient orbit, and the other five orbits combine to give \(144+36+36=216\) nonordinary equations. This proves the precise \(3\)-rank/\(a\)-number classification.

**4. Genus and integrality.** Adjunction on \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) gives \(g=(4-1)(4-1)=9\). Any decomposition of a divisor with positive bidegrees \((4,4)\) into distinct effective components necessarily has intersecting components, except when all are parallel fibers (which is incompatible with this bidegree). Geometric smoothness rules out intersecting components, proving geometric integrality.

## Verification

The accompanying self-contained Python checker `verify.py` (Python 3.10 or later, SymPy 1.12 or later) reconstructs the complete \(9841\)-equation search, all \(56\) rationally nonsingular orbits, all four-chart Gröbner tests, both group actions, the seven exact Cartier matrices, and their iterated ranks. Run `python verify.py` in a directory containing the embedded file; no input data or network connection is required.

As a separate arithmetic consistency check, the program independently counts points of each representative over \(\mathbf F_{3^n}\) for \(1\le n\le4\), using irreducible-polynomial finite-field arithmetic; each of the \(28\) point counts obeys \(\#C(\mathbf F_{3^n})\equiv 1-\operatorname{tr}(H^n)\pmod3\). These checks corroborate the Cartier coefficient convention but are not used as a substitute for geometric smoothness or the exhaustive orbit proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Homma–Kim (first public preprint March 22, 2022) determined minimal filling bidegrees and the ideal-theoretic form of such curves. Ahammed–Campbell–Moon (first public preprint December 2, 2025), Section 4.1, already enumerated \(1584\) smooth **presentations by pairs of quartics** for \(q=3\). Accounting for three syzygy-gauge choices and two nonzero common scalar choices per projective defining equation, their count agrees with—and already covers—the \(264\)-equation total reported above. Their seven \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\)-orbits of quartics and pair-compatibility data also inform the ambient orbit comparison.

The present finding is **not** the raw smooth-curve count. It is the complete \(3\)-rank and \(a\)-number stratification of those smooth genus-nine curves, including the sharp bound on nonordinarity and the unique ordinary ambient orbit. Neither cited primary paper states these Jacobian invariant counts or their relationship to the complete ambient automorphism action. Stöhr–Voloch's Cartier formula is standard supporting theory, not asserted as new.

## Limitations

The classification concerns embedded minimal bidegree \((4,4)\) filling curves over precisely \(\mathbf F_3\); it is not a classification over larger fields, of abstract isomorphism classes, of endomorphism algebras, or of full Newton polygons. The finite group-orbit census and exact Gröbner computations require reproducible execution of the supplied code and SymPy; point counts through \(\mathbf F_{81}\) are independent checks only, not an alternative proof. An unindexed equivalent arithmetic stratification in other finite-geometric terminology remains possible. No independent external validation has been performed.
