# Review of the genus-nine filling-biquartic Cartier stratification

**Correctness: PASS.** The normal form has a nine-dimensional coefficient quotient; all \(9841\) scalar classes are included. Rational smoothness reduces to a proved point-grid condition. Every \(576\)-group orbit nonsingular on the rational grid has an exact four-chart Gröbner test over characteristic three. Seven smooth orbits are obtained, with \(264\) members. The explicit Cartier coefficient formula computes \(a=9-\operatorname{rank}H\) and stable \(3\)-rank; seven representatives give only \(H\)-ranks \(8,9\), already stable after iteration. The full group produces four ambient orbits, including the unique ordinary orbit. Twenty-eight extension-field point-count congruences independently check the Cartier convention. Residual correctness risk is implementation/library correctness rather than untested infinite-field extrapolation: the unit-ideal Gröbner criterion is valid over the algebraic closure.

**Originality: PASS, with bounded residual risk.** Homma–Kim proves the minimal bidegree and normal forms, while Ahammed–Campbell–Moon (2025), Section 4.1, actually obtains \(1584\) smooth pair presentations and a quartic-orbit compatibility table. Thus the smoothness **count alone is not new**, and the claimed contribution is specifically the computed Jacobian \(3\)-rank and \(a\)-number distribution and full ambient ordinary-orbit classification. Those invariants and their stratification do not appear in the inspected primary texts. Conceptual and parameter-equivalent searches against published research records returned no dominating statement. The 2025 complete presentation table could conceivably be used to rederive the new invariant census; an older/unindexed equivalent calculation remains the principal bibliographic risk.

**Value: PASS.** The arithmetic type of Jacobians is a natural geometric invariant independently of curve existence and the known coefficient census. All \(264\) smooth genus-nine minimal fillers lie in only the ordinary and one-step nonordinary strata, with a single ordinary ambient orbit. This rigid dichotomy and its exact distribution are not forced by genus, \(16\) rational points, or the published existence/count theorems; it distinguishes the geometry inside a previously enumerated natural family.

Closest literature: Homma–Kim, arXiv:2203.11595 (2022); Ahammed–Campbell–Moon, arXiv:2512.02796 (2025), Section 4.1; Stöhr–Voloch (1987) for the standard Cartier operator method. Scope excludes other characteristics, larger bidegrees, abstract-curve isomorphism counts, and full Newton-polygon classification.
