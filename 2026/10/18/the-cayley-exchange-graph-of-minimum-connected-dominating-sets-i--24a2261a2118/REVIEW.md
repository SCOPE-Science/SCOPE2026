# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Complete enumeration over all sixteen-vertex subsets through size six independently establishes the connected-domination minimum, the exact 192-state domain, and all token-jump edges. A second method grows connected subsets, enumerates all coordinate-word paths, and constructs an explicit regular action by the order-192 affine even-permutation group. Equality of every neighbour set with its Cayley generators proves the graph isomorphism. Exact breadth-first searches on both representations establish the distance layers and odd girth, and all possible adjacency-restricted replacements are exhaustively excluded. Every critical computation is supplied in executable source form and replayed against its packaged output. Finite exhaustive computation is a proof for this single finite graph; no assertion for higher cubes follows.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest checked papers establish ordinary domination reconfiguration by additions/deletions (Haas–Seyffarth; Suzuki–Mouawad–Nishimura), \(\gamma\)-graphs based on adjacent replacements of unconstrained minimum dominating sets (Fricke and collaborators), connected-dominating-set enumeration (Golovach–Heggernes–Kratsch), and hypercube dominating path properties (Dvořák–Havel–Mollard). These do not, from the material actually accessible, entail the full fixed-cardinality connected-domination **jump** graph, its regular affine Cayley action, distance profile or odd girth. The 2003 dominating-path full text and the 2015 one-movable full text were unavailable despite searches: both remain stated priority risks. The configuration count and induced path structure are used as foundations rather than claimed as novel on their own.

**Value — PASS.** Minimum connected dominating sets describe optimal connected coverage backbones. On a canonical symmetry-rich graph the theorem explains the entire communication network of optimal solutions: every one-for-one jump can connect the state space in at most seven steps, whereas physically local slides are impossible. Its nontrivial group-model identification and exact odd cycle and distance invariants provide a reusable structural benchmark beyond a table of minima or an arbitrary finite invariant.

**Scientific limitations.** Only the four-cube is covered. Priority is not externally certified; one especially relevant historic paper was not available in full text. The proof uses reproducible exhaustive finite checks and group multiplication, not a symbolic infinite-family theorem.
