# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every finite Zimin word has a valuation representation. A nonempty middle factor with maximum letter \(h\) has a unique offset within a \(Z_h\) block. If it is not the entire block, one neighboring letter is fixed, which forbids a missing two-sided extension. Complete-block extension pairs are exactly the cross pairs between letter \(j+1\) and any larger letter; the two missing families follow. Boundary-only cases at the outermost level and the empty middle are included. Exact contextual enumeration agrees through rank nine; finite tests are not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS against available evidence.** Cooper–Rorabaugh (arXiv:1409.3080) concerns pattern encounters rather than absent contiguous factors. Barton and coauthors (arXiv:1406.6341) supply a generic linear-time algorithm and extension criterion. Crochemore and coauthors (arXiv:1506.04917) analyze comparison algorithms and circular sequences. No inspected source contains the complete Zimin specialization or the cubic spectrum. Residual risk is an unindexed equivalent or a different terminology for the same valuation-language result.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact families, sparse length spectrum and cubic rank-growth are a complete classification of the antidictionary of a canonical recursively nested word, and not merely a numerical increment. They connect symbolic self-similarity with forbidden-factor compression. The statement is for finite linear words on their used alphabet, not arbitrary alphabets or circular factors.
