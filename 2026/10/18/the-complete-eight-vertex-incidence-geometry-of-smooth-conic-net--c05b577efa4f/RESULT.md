# The complete eight-vertex incidence geometry of smooth conic nets over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(V=H^0(\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2},\mathcal O(2))\). A subspace \(W\subseteq V\) is **all-smooth** if every nonzero member defines a geometrically smooth plane conic. The maximal all-smooth subspaces are exactly eight three-dimensional nets \(W_T\), indexed canonically by the eight noncollinear length-three Frobenius orbits \(T\subset\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_8)\). Each net contains seven conics and seven all-smooth pencils. There are precisely 56 all-smooth pencils, each contained in exactly one net, and precisely 28 geometrically smooth individual conics.

Each smooth conic occurs in exactly two nets, and each pair of different nets shares exactly one smooth conic. Thus **the 28 smooth conics are canonically the edges of a complete graph \(K_8\) with the eight nets as vertices**. The 56 pencils are canonically pairs \((T,P)\) with \(T\) one of the eight triangles and \(P\in\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_2)\). The eight nets form one orbit of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\), each stabilizer having order \(21\). There is no all-smooth subspace of dimension at least four.

The enumerative and incidence assertions concern distinct linear subspaces of the fixed coefficient space \(V\), not equations up to rescaling over larger fields or arbitrary conic bundles. The existence of one net and the four-dimensional obstruction are earlier results [1]; the claimed new contribution is the complete eight-vertex incidence and unique-extension classification.

## Assumptions and scope
All polynomial coefficients, linear subspaces, nets, pencils and projective transformations are over \(\mathbf F_2\); **smooth** always means geometrically smooth over an algebraic closure. A smooth conic has three rational points and nine \(\mathbf F_8\)-points. A Frobenius triangle is an orbit \(\{Q,Q^2,Q^4\}\subset\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_8)\) of size three. It is noncollinear if the three geometric points do not lie on a line. Projective nets have vector-space dimension three, pencils dimension two.

## Proof
**1. The 28 conics.** Any smooth conic over \(\mathbf F_2\) has three rational points, necessarily noncollinear. Conversely, a prescribed noncollinear rational triple determines a unique smooth conic: projectively send the points to the coordinate vertices. The conic has equation \(dXY+eXZ+fYZ\); its smoothness forces \(d=e=f=1\). There are seven rational points and seven rational lines, with three points on each line. Hence the number of smooth conics is \(\binom73-7=28\).

**2. The eight noncollinear Frobenius triangles.** Projective \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_8)\) has \(8^2+8+1=73\) points; seven are rational. Its 66 remaining points form 22 Frobenius orbits of size three. Every one of the seven rational lines contains six nonrational \(\mathbf F_8\)-points, constituting two collinear Frobenius triangles. Conversely, the line spanned by any collinear triangle is Frobenius-invariant and rational. Thus there are exactly \(22-7\cdot2=8\) noncollinear triangles.

**3. A net for each triangle.** Fix a noncollinear triangle \(T\). Evaluation of quadratic forms at its three geometric points is three independent linear constraints after scalar extension, descending to a codimension-three \(\mathbf F_2\)-subspace \(W_T\subset V\). Its base scheme consists exactly of \(T\), because over \(\mathbf F_8\) the three-point vanishing ideal in degree two is spanned by the products of pairs of coordinate variables. Every nonzero member of \(W_T\) is smooth. Indeed, a geometrically singular conic splits into two lines (possibly equal), defined individually over \(\mathbf F_4\). One of these lines contains at least two of the three \(\mathbf F_8\)-points of \(T\), so that line is defined both over \(\mathbf F_4\) and \(\mathbf F_8\), hence over \(\mathbf F_2\). Frobenius invariance would then put the entire triangle on one line, a contradiction. Thus the eight nets exist and each has seven smooth members.

**4. Their complete intersection graph.** A smooth conic \(C\) has nine \(\mathbf F_8\)-points, of which three are rational. Its six nonrational points split into exactly two Frobenius triangles. Neither is collinear because a line cannot meet a smooth conic in three distinct geometric points. Therefore \(C\) belongs to exactly two of the eight nets. Two distinct smooth conics cannot have the same two triangles: that would give six common geometric points, contradicting Bézout's four-point intersection degree. So the map from conics to unordered pairs of triangles is injective, and both finite sets have size \(28=\binom82\). It is a bijection. Every pair of nets therefore meets in one smooth conic, and the complete \(K_8\) assertion follows.

**5. Unique extension of each pencil.** Let \(\langle F,G\rangle\) be an all-smooth pencil. At any rational point, either all three equations \(F,G,F+G\) vanish, or exactly one does. Since each smooth conic has three rational points, the nine point-conic incidences give \(9=3b+(7-b)=7+2b\), where \(b\) is the number of common rational base points. Thus \(b=1\), say \(P\). The gradients of \(F\) and \(G\) at \(P\) are distinct nonzero vectors: otherwise their sum would have zero gradient at the base point, contradicting smoothness of \(F+G\). Their intersection at \(P\) is transverse of multiplicity one. Bézout gives total intersection degree four, leaving a degree-three base subscheme with no rational points. Such a residual length-three Galois-invariant subscheme is one reduced Frobenius orbit of degree three; it is noncollinear because it lies on smooth \(F=0\). Hence the pencil is contained in the uniquely determined net \(W_T\). Conversely, for each noncollinear triangle \(T\) and each rational point \(P\), evaluation at \(P\) on \(W_T\) is nonzero (since the net has base precisely \(T\)). Its kernel is a two-dimensional all-smooth pencil. This establishes the bijection of pencils with \((T,P)\) and the count \(8\cdot7=56\).

**6. Exhaustiveness and symmetry.** Let \(W\) be any three-dimensional all-smooth subspace. Every two distinct nonzero elements span an all-smooth pencil, so their associated edges of \(K_8\) intersect. A family of at least four pairwise-intersecting distinct edges in a complete graph is a star (the only non-star family has at most the three edges of a triangle). Since \(W\) contains seven distinct smooth conics, they are the seven edges of a star, and \(W=W_T\) for its unique center \(T\). A hypothetical four-dimensional all-smooth space contains a three-dimensional net, and every additional conic would have to be adjacent to all seven star edges in \(K_8\), which forces it to belong to that net—a contradiction. Finally, the exact coordinate-action check in `verify_coefficients.py` enumerates all 168 transformations of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\) and finds one orbit of size eight with stabilizers of order 21.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_coefficients.py` and `python3 verify_geometry.py` with standard Python 3; their exact expected outputs are provided in `coefficient_output.txt` and `geometry_output.txt`. The first independently enumerates all 63 nonzero quadratic forms using the exact characteristic-two discriminant \(af+be+cd+def\) for \(aX^2+bY^2+cZ^2+dXY+eXZ+fYZ\), constructs all pencils and nets, verifies the pair-incidence pattern and enumerates the 168 rational projective transformations. The second independently constructs \(\mathbf F_8\) as \(\mathbf F_2[t]/(t^3+t+1)\), lists all 73 projective points and Frobenius orbits, tests geometric singularities at every \(\mathbf F_8\)-point, and checks the triangle/net/pencil correspondences. The algebraic proof above does not depend on treating bounded extension-field point checking as a geometric smoothness certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli–Ghioca [1] already exhibit an all-smooth net of conics over \(\mathbf F_2\), discuss the 28 smooth conics, and rule out a higher-dimensional all-smooth conic linear system; those components are not claimed as new. Zanella [2] gives a general Frobenius-triple construction of nonsingular conic families, so the existence of the \(W_T\) construction is likewise prior work. The claimed refinement is the mutual incidence of *all* eight nets, the exact edge identification, and the bijection between all-smooth pencils and pairs \((T,P)\), with unique extension. The recent study [3] investigates webs and squabs, not this eight-vertex configuration; [4] studies nets primarily outside characteristic two. Full-text access to [2] was unavailable; its abstract was read and a possible implicit or explicit occurrence of part of the new incidence description remains a real priority risk.

## Limitations
This is a fixed \(\mathbf F_2\) classification; it makes no claim about larger finite fields, arbitrary conic bundles, or isomorphisms not induced by rational linear coordinate changes. The Frobenius construction, the existence of one net and the dimension bound are not original. The finite computation is independent supporting evidence, not an independent mathematical audit. The exact K8 incidence and unique-pencil extension appear absent from the accessible publications checked but may already occur in inaccessible older classification literature, especially [2].
