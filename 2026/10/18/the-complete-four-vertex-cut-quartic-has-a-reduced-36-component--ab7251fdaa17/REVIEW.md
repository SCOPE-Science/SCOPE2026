# Scientific review: Jacobian singular scheme of the complete-four-vertex cut quartic

## Correctness — PASS
The source equation is explicitly printed in Sturmfels–Sullivant Example 1.1. Direct differentiation yields the sum of two squarefree degree-three Veronese ideals on disjoint sets of variables. Its radical primary decomposition consists of the 36 codimension-four monomial primes; the Hilbert–Burch matrix and tensor product of exact resolutions independently yield the minimal Betti table, Cohen–Macaulay depth, degree and genus. The completed local equation at each generic component is a threefold ordinary node. Exact-arithmetic and finite-field tests corroborate the algebra; all-characteristic correctness follows from the symbolic argument.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Sturmfels–Sullivant describe the quartic itself and its smoothness obstruction, and classify singular planes for the different edge-deleted four-vertex graph. Engström concerns degrees of defining equations of cut ideals; Olteanu's monomial cut ideals are distinct ideals in edge variables. Their examined results do not directly give the 36-component Jacobian scheme, complete resolution, Cohen–Macaulay type and arithmetic genus of this precise quartic. Published mathematical-record searches for the exact graph, equation and invariants returned no dominating statement. The result may nonetheless be implicit in general treatments of squarefree-monomial Jacobian ideals; this is a residual originality risk, not a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
This gives a complete natural scheme-theoretic and homological classification for the smallest complete-graph cut hypersurface, at the first obstruction to quadratic generation. Beyond the immediate fact that the hypersurface is singular, it shows that its singular scheme is reduced, pure and arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay but of type nine, and measures its degree, arithmetic genus and all syzygies. These invariants distinguish this cut model from superficially similar small-graph cut varieties with embedded singular-scheme points. No claim is made for all graphs.
