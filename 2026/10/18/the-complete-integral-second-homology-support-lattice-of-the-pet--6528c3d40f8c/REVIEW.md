# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The chain-level proof identifies the relative degree-two triangle coordinates with the integer kernel of the ordinary graph triangle-boundary map, naturally for graph inclusions. The coordinate-intersection argument proves primitive injectivity, while the acyclic oriented simplex-chain sequence determines the full integral Petersen lattice, its relation, and its restriction ranks. The included checker independently enumerates all 1,024 restrictions over \(\mathbb F_2\) and all 120 signed symmetry traces. Mod-two computations are supporting checks, not substituted for the integral proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Linusson–Shareshian–Welker already establish the ambient nonmatching-complex homotopy type; Goyal–Shukla–Singh Lemma 3.2 already gives a general objectwise line-graph-clique-complex equivalence; and the five octahedral complements appear as Petersen graph substructures in Brouwer's reference. These facts are credited. Their inspected statements do not provide the complete primitive inclusion-image profile for every Petersen restriction, or the universal inclusion-level integral kernel model formulated here. A sufficiently explicit natural refinement of the published equivalence could make the universal lemma a straightforward corollary; an equivalent unindexed finite support classification remains possible. The originality assessment is comparative rather than a guarantee of priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The precise local-to-global integral question is natural because an ambient wedge-of-spheres result gives neither the minimum vertices supporting individual classes nor how the classes embed in the full homology lattice. The finding resolves all 1,024 restrictions simultaneously, supplies a sharp five-carrier orbit, classifies their integral relation and \(S_5\)-action, and separates degree-two persistence for line-graph filtrations from the usual clique-filtration behavior. It is not a claim of general higher-degree persistence.

**Closest primary sources:** Linusson–Shareshian–Welker, arXiv:math/0410345 (Theorem 1.1 and symmetry discussion); Goyal–Shukla–Singh, arXiv:2009.12130 (Lemma 3.2 and Section 3.1); Brouwer's Petersen graph structure reference. Specific residual risk concerns prior naturality refinements and differently indexed support computations.
