# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed complex, action, domain, and degree agree with the conventional definitions. The proof reconstructs fixed relative-open cells by equivariant color assignments, checks their phase counts and fixed-locus dimensions, and derives the exact signed Euler characteristic. The independently established Babson–Kozlov wedge theorem supplies the homology concentration needed to turn this Euler character into the **actual** top-degree character. The new diagonal-subgroup quotient corollary uses the three source cycle classes and rational finite-group transfer; the earlier color-only quotient is expressly excluded from novelty. The verifier tests signed cellular actions directly, including nontrivial permutations of both coordinate sets; finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-parameter argument. The diagonal quotient top Betti number is rational and is not confused with any integral or homotopy conclusion.

No directly identical statement was identified in the mathematical records compared, including checks for equivariant Hom representations, alternative symmetry descriptions, broader homology coverage, and quotient invariants. This is best-knowledge originality, not proof of global publication priority. A 2012 source with a plausible title was inaccessible in full text, and representations derived under a different combinatorial model may still cover the theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Complete graph Hom complexes are central examples in topological graph coloring, and their non-equivariant homotopy type has long been known. The theorem determines the action of **every** combined permutation of source vertices and colors from cycle data, including nontrivial interactions where isolated factor characters are insufficient. It determines each subgroup's rational quotient Betti numbers through character averaging; specifically, the new diagonal \(S_3\)-quotient formula detects nonvanishing top homology that the older all-color quotient theorem cannot identify. These are natural invariant computations rather than arbitrary parameter slices or restatements of the sphere count.
