# Review of the split-surface line scheme and divisor lattice

## Correctness

**PASS.** The argument classifies all linear embeddings into the branch surface by analyzing two coordinate-pair ranks. If both ranks are two, equality of the pulled-back binary forms would identify their root sets by a projective transformation; their cocircularity properties rule this out for \(d\ge4\). Consequently the full line family is the explicit product of two simple root divisors. Adjunction gives \(N_{L/S_d}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2-d)\), proving that the entire finite line scheme is reduced. Direct incidence and Hodge-index arguments justify the claimed divisor-class rank. The finite checker validates the relevant arithmetic for \(d=4,5,6\), not the infinite theorem itself.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual literature risk.** Cheltsov--Tschinkel arXiv:2610.07479v1 supplies the sextic equation and proves potential density, without giving its full line Hilbert scheme or line-class span. Poonen arXiv:0902.3961v2 concerns equality of one binary form with itself, which does not contain the inequivalent-form line calculation. Page--Ryan--Smith arXiv:2406.15868v2 gives general maximal-line bounds rather than exact invariants here. Literal polynomial, Fano-scheme, line-grid and binary-form searches did not locate the precise result. A specialized unindexed theorem about split hypersurfaces remains a real priority risk; nonappearance in searches is not novelty proof.

## Value

**PASS.** All lines on the exact arithmetic branch surface used for a new potential-density construction are natural and useful objects. The complete geometric and rational line classification, finite-étale scheme description, complete intersection pairing and rank-\(26\) line lattice answer intrinsic questions not implied by knowing that one rational line exists. Their uniform degree-\(d\) pattern has a natural lower boundary at \(d=4\), since all triples of points on \(\mathbb P^1\) are projectively equivalent.

## Scientific limitations

The theorem is limited to characteristic zero, and to lines on the branch surface. It does not prove that the Picard number is exactly \(26\) or analyze other rational curves. The source's arithmetic density theorem is not extended by this calculation. The mathematical result remains subject to independent verification.
