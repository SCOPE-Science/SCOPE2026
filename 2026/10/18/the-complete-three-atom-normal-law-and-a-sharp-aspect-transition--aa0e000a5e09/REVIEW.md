# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The boundary-normal definition is identical at smooth side interiors and nonsmooth vertices. In square coordinates the four foot tests are exact scalar-product inequalities; the two acute corners contribute unconditionally, while the two obtuse corners are determined by the stated conjunctions. The independent alternating-extrema argument proves that the total is twice the number of side-foot normals, and hence has only the three supported atoms. The eight-normal region is a rectangle after an invertible affine change of variables, whose inverse corners are all in the unit square when the threshold holds. The mean then determines the remaining probabilities. Derivative signs prove the claimed sharp aspect transition and equality cases. Exact rational chamber tests and independent Euclidean-coordinate point tests pass as described in `VERIFICATION.md`. Degenerate corners and transition sets have measure zero; both formula branches agree at the threshold.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources

The primary 2014 preprint of Domokos–Lángi states general bounds, equality conditions and broad history, but no complete angle/aspect probability law of normals to parallelograms in the inspected sections. Nasonov–Panina–Siersma's 2024 full HTML describes active regions and planar triangle counts, not the law of arbitrary parallelograms. Exact semantic checks found nearby published results concerning normal averages of regular odd polygons, ellipses and a chord invariant of parallelograms; neither states nor logically implies this full three-atom distribution. An earlier theorem gives a different exact normal law for obtuse triangles, not quadrilaterals. The prior literature does provide the general planar centrally symmetric upper bound of eight normals on average, which is expressly not claimed anew. Hann (1993) and Hug (1995) are potential older priority risks because their full texts could not be examined. Absence of a search hit is not a guarantee of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The main claim resolves the full probability distribution, not just the expectation, for an entire two-parameter natural family of nonorthogonal convex bodies. It reveals a sharp geometric threshold, a unique maximizing aspect ratio for the six-normal probability, a unique minimizing aspect ratio for the four-normal probability, and a sharp rhombus extremum for the mean. These features distinguish probability distributions for shapes that are affinely equivalent but not Euclidean-similar and quantify how Euclidean normal incidence responds to anisotropy. The result is more than a restatement of the classical universal eight-normal bound.
