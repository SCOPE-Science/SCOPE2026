The graph is specified both as the Petersen Kronecker double cover and as \(GP(10,3)\), with the asserted isomorphism verified edge by edge. Exact inductive face enumeration yields 6,212 faces. A deterministic vertex-toggle matching yields 3,103 pairwise-disjoint Hasse pairs and precisely six augmented critical faces. The full directed Hasse graph has 30,380 incidences and its complete topological sort proves acyclicity. Removing the empty-face pair leaves one critical 0-cell, one critical 4-cell, and five critical 5-cells. The signed integer Morse differential is zero, established by the explicit unit-pivot cancellation recurrence and confirmed by replaying the complete verifier. Since \(\pi_4(S^4)\cong\mathbb Z\) is classified by degree, zero attaching coefficients make each of the five 5-cell attachments null-homotopic. A separate mod-two chain rank calculation agrees with the asserted Betti numbers. The proof is finite and exhaustive, not a guess based on small instances.

Carnero Bravo's 2026 generalized-Mycielskian formula uses the independence complex of the Kronecker double cover as input without computing the Petersen input. Krnc–Pisanski identify the underlying double cover but do not compute the independence complex. Published Desargues *matching*-complex and dodecahedral \(GP(10,2)\) *independence*-complex results are mathematically different. Searches under Desargues, \(GP(10,3)\), Petersen canonical double cover, bipartite Kneser \(H(5,2)\), and homotopy/wedge/independence aliases found no statement implying this exact wedge decomposition. No negative literature search is a uniqueness proof; unindexed or unpublished equivalents remain a risk.

## Value — PASS
The Desargues graph is a natural, highly symmetric 20-vertex cubic bipartite graph with two independent reasons for study: it is the Petersen graph's canonical double cover and the smallest nontrivial bipartite Kneser graph \(H(5,2)\). Carnero Bravo's recent general reduction makes its exact independence-complex homotopy type a concrete missing input. The result exhibits a mixed-dimensional wedge with torsion-free integral homology and rigorously resolves a complete natural finite benchmark. The classification is not a rephrasing of known edge-ideal invariants or a routine corollary of the cited families, and it makes no unsupported claim about all generalized Petersen graphs.
