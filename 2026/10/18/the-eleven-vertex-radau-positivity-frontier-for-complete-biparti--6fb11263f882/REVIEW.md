# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim identifies exactly the class of unweighted complete bipartite graph Laplacians, the Radau IIA endpoint map and all nonnegative real steps. Its proof reconstructs the four Laplacian eigenspaces, gives five exact entry formulas, and shows all off-diagonal entries strictly positive at positive steps. The global scalar minimum \((5-3\sqrt3)/2\) certifies all diagonals for graph order at most eleven. For every larger graph, a symbolic diagonal identity at \(h=8/\max(p,q)\) and a strictly positive polynomial factor prove a negative entry without numerical interpolation. Continuity and the large-step rank-one limit prove the positive regions. The packaged symbolic and exact-rational tests passed; the proof does not rely on finite sampling.

**Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk.** Hairer–Wanner give the Radau stability function, not graph-dependent nonnegativity. Bolley–Crouzeix's no-universal-high-order result quantifies over arbitrary positive generators, not the complete-bipartite class. Horváth's full 2005 article develops general positivity-threshold and invariant-set theory; its graph-specific one-dimensional experiments do not give the all-bipartition twelve-vertex result. The earlier star-only interval theorem does not cover \(K_{p,q}\) with both parts nonsingleton. The earlier even-stage Gauss complete-multipartite theorem concerns a different method and is not a dominating implication. Actual semantic and bibliographic searches found no stronger matching result, but unknown or unindexed equivalent work remains a possibility.

**Value — PASS.** A natural third-order method has a sharp, topology-independent **vertex-count** cutoff across every complete bipartite graph, including stars and balanced dense graphs. The result identifies a common explicit failing step depending only on the larger part, and rigorously proves recovery of positivity at large steps. This resolves a family-wide qualitative question that is not settled by A-stability or generic small-step SSP bounds. The theorem does not claim individual maximal positivity intervals for every graph.

**Limitations.** Graphs are simple, unweighted and complete bipartite; only completed steps of linear diffusion are analyzed. Independent external validation is unavailable. Full text was obtained for three principal comparison sources; some older absolute-monotonicity references were checked bibliographically rather than in full.
