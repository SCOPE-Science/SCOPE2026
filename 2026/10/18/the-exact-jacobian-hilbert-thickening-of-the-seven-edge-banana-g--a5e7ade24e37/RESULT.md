# The exact Jacobian Hilbert thickening of the seven-edge banana graph hypersurface

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let \(S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_6]\) be standard graded, and let \(F=e_6(x_0,\ldots,x_6)=\sum_{i=0}^{6}\prod_{j\ne i}x_j\) be the seven-edge banana Kirchhoff polynomial. Write \(J=(\partial F/\partial x_i:0\le i\le6)\) and \(K=\sqrt J\). The radical \(K\) is generated by all squarefree coordinate monomials of degree five. Consequently the reduced projective singular support consists of exactly 35 coordinate projective three-spaces, one for each three-element zero-coordinate set.

The graded Jacobian excess has the **exact** Hilbert series
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(K/J;t)=\frac{14t^{5}+21t^{6}+21t^{7}+14t^{8}}{(1-t)^3}.
\]
In particular the Hilbert polynomials of the Jacobian singular scheme and its reduced support differ by
\[
P_{S/J}(d)-P_{S/K}(d)=35d^2-350d+903.
\]
The excess sheaf is supported on the 35 coordinate planes where at least four variables vanish, and at a generic point of each plane the transverse excess algebra is a square-zero length-two vector space.

## Assumptions and scope

All ideals and Hilbert functions are homogeneous over characteristic zero; the Jacobian singular scheme means the projective scheme cut out by \(J\), equivalently by \((F,J)\) since Euler's identity yields \(6F=\sum_i x_i\partial_i F\). The Hilbert series is the ordinary graded series of the **module** \(K/J\), not the Hilbert series of the underlying reduced variety or a localized Jacobian algebra. The integer formula for the Hilbert polynomial difference holds for sufficiently large integers \(d\), not necessarily every small degree. It is a fixed seven-variable theorem; no claim is made for arbitrary numbers of edges.

## Proof

**Reduced support.** Where all seven coordinates are nonzero write \(F=(\prod_i x_i)\sum_i x_i^{-1}\). At a zero of \(F\) on this torus, \(\partial_iF=-(\prod_jx_j)x_i^{-2}\ne0\). At a point with exactly one zero coordinate \(F\ne0\), while at a point with exactly two zero coordinates the derivative with respect to either vanished coordinate is the product of the remaining five nonzero coordinates and hence is nonzero. If three or more coordinates vanish, all degree-five partial derivatives vanish. Thus the singular support is precisely the union of the 35 triple-zero coordinate subspaces. Its radical is the squarefree degree-five ideal \(K\), which satisfies \(J\subset K\).

**Exact global Hilbert series.** Apply Buchberger's algorithm over \(\mathbb Q\) to the seven explicit partial derivatives, using graded reverse lexicographic order with \(x_0>\cdots>x_6\). The exact Gröbner basis has 51 members. Taking leading monomials and computing their Hilbert numerator with the monomial-ideal colon recursion \(0\to S/(I:x_i)(-1)\to S/I\to S/(I,x_i)\to0\), gives
\[
(1-t)^7\operatorname{Hilb}(S/J;t)=1-7t^5+6t^7+21t^{10}-35t^{11}+14t^{12}.
\]
The same exact recursion on the 21 squarefree degree-five generators of \(K\) gives
\[
(1-t)^7\operatorname{Hilb}(S/K;t)=1-21t^5+35t^6-15t^7.
\]
Subtracting these series, and using the exact sequence \(0\to K/J\to S/J\to S/K\to0\), yields the asserted positive Hilbert-series numerator after cancellation of \((1-t)^4\). Expanding the binomial-coefficient Hilbert polynomial of the result gives \(35d^2-350d+903\). The finite algorithm and all exact assertions above are reproduced from the original seven derivatives in `check.py`; neither an empirical finite-field count nor an interpolated Hilbert function is used as proof.

**Geometric location and generic length.** At a point with precisely three zero coordinates, the local leading transverse part is a nondegenerate three-variable quadratic elementary-symmetric polynomial, hence its gradient ideal equals the radical singular ideal in the local ring. Thus the excess module is supported where at least four coordinates vanish. At a generic point with exactly four zero coordinates \(y_1,\ldots,y_4\), the remaining three coordinates are invertible, so after division by a unit the local equation is \(e_3(y)+\lambda e_4(y)\). The coordinate substitution \(z_i=y_i/(1+(\lambda/4)y_i)\) transforms this equation into a unit times \(e_3(z)\). The four quadratic derivatives of \(e_3(z)\) are linearly independent in the six-dimensional span of the pairwise quadratic monomials. Every cubic monomial supported on at least two variables belongs to the derivative ideal (verified by exact division in `check.py`). Therefore the radical-to-Jacobian transverse quotient has length two and is annihilated by the maximal ideal. The 35 planes contribute the leading term \(35d^2\) in the global Hilbert-polynomial difference; the lower-degree terms encode global incidence and grading.

## Verification

Use Python 3 with SymPy 1.14.0 and run `python check.py` in the same directory. It reconstructs \(F\) and \(J\), computes the rational Gröbner basis, checks the ideal generators, performs an independently written monomial-ideal Hilbert-numerator recursion, evaluates five exact graded dimensions by direct standard-monomial enumeration, and checks the four-variable transverse derivative ideal. Expected terminal marker: `VERIFY_OK`. The full generator lists are regenerated, rather than imported from a prior calculation; `requirements.txt` pins the sole nonstandard dependency.

## Relationship to prior work

Aluffi and Marcolli (2008, arXiv:0807.1690) introduce and study precisely this family of banana graph hypersurfaces. Their Sections 1.4, 3, and 4 compute classes in the Grothendieck ring and Chern–Schwartz–MacPherson classes and study singular geometry through characteristic classes. Morales (2012, arXiv:1208.4129) also studies classes of banana and flower graphs. Neither of the inspected discussions supplies the specific seven-variable **graded Jacobian excess** series or its associated Hilbert-polynomial correction. These earlier classes are not being recomputed or claimed as new. A related previously recorded five-variable reciprocal quartic concerns five zero-dimensional embedded vertex defects; its statement does not imply the present positive-dimensional 35-plane excess or its complete graded numerator.

## Limitations

The Gröbner/Hilbert calculation is exact but depends on a reproducible computer-algebra calculation for this fixed polynomial; no human-independent verification has been conducted. The statement does not determine a primary decomposition of \(J\), all embedded associated primes, or a formula for all banana graph sizes. The characteristic-zero assumption matters to the nondegenerate transverse quadratic and coordinate substitution. Possible unnoticed equivalent coverage in specialized reciprocal-arrangement or graph-hypersurface literature remains an originality risk.
