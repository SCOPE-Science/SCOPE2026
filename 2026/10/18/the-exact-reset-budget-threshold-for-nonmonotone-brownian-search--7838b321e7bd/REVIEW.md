# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The probability of a reset preceding absorption in one attempt is \(q=1-e^{-a\sqrt{r/D}}\), which gives the exact mean \(r^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\mu+1}q^j\). Differentiation reduces its sign to \(H_n-2\). The truncated geometric mean shows \(H_n\) increases in the attempt allowance. The complete exact Bernstein certificate proves \(H_{21}<2\) globally; the rational inequality at \(q=6/7\) proves \(H_{22}>2\) somewhere. Both endpoint signs are negative, so the asserted threshold follows. Main risk: confusing the unconditional termination time with the absorption-conditioned time; the result deliberately excludes the latter.

**Originality — PASS in self-review.** Bressloff (arXiv:2310.20502v1), particularly section V.B, equation (5.37), has the same renewal model and exact mean, and Figure 4 depicts the qualitative change, but the mathematical literature examined here does not supply the first transition allowance of 21 or the proof of an if-and-only-if integer classification. The new statement is a global sign theorem, not a new derivation of the prior mean formula. Search cannot establish absolute priority: a general unimodality theorem for truncated geometric renewal means or an unindexed discussion of the exact 21/22 boundary could subsume it. The previously published formula makes the boundary computable but does not itself state or prove the sharp global inequality.

**Value — PASS.** A finite number of permitted resets is a natural operational resource constraint. The exact first integer at which additional resetting can **increase** expected time before search ends determines a qualitative design boundary that sample curves at well-separated allowances cannot locate. The threshold is independent of distance and diffusivity, and its nontrivial global proof requires all-positive Bernstein decompositions, not an arbitrary numerical scan. It does not claim a general optimal-reset-rate theorem or the uniqueness of local extrema.
