# The exact strict ternary synchronization boundary at length \(27\)

## Finding

For a word \(w\in\{0,1,2\}^{n}\) define its synchronization gap
\[
G(w)=\min_{0\le i<j<k\le n}\frac{k-i-2\operatorname{LCS}(w[i:j],w[j:k])}{k-i}.
\]
Intervals are half-open; \(\operatorname{LCS}\) is the length of a longest common subsequence. Because insertion--deletion distance is \(\operatorname{ED}(x,y)=|x|+|y|-2\operatorname{LCS}(x,y)\), a strict \(\varepsilon\)-synchronization string is precisely a word with \(G(w)>1-\varepsilon\).

**Theorem.** Exactly six ternary words of length \(27\) satisfy \(G(w)>1/8\): the six alphabet permutations of the word `012010212021012102010212012`. No ternary word of length \(28\), or of any greater length, does. Moreover
\[
\max_{w\in\{0,1,2\}^{27}}G(w)=\frac{3}{23},
\]
and its maximizers are exactly those same six words. The displayed representative has precisely two minimizing interval triples: \((0,12,23)\) and \((4,15,27)\).

## Assumptions and scope

There are three labeled symbols; blocks in adjacent-interval comparisons may have unequal lengths. The strict sign is essential: equality at a normalized edit distance of \(1/8\) fails. All claims are finite except the immediate monotonic consequence excluding longer words.

## Proof

**Canonical forms.** Any alphabet permutation preserves common subsequences. Relabeling symbols in their order of first appearance sends every nonempty word to a unique restricted-growth word: its first symbol is \(0\) and each subsequent symbol is at most one larger than the maximum previously used, with maximum label at most \(2\). Thus it suffices to enumerate restricted-growth words.

**Complete endpoint recursion.** Initialize \(\mathcal A_1=\{0\}\). To form \(\mathcal A_{m+1}\) from \(\mathcal A_m\), append each permitted digit \(c\in\{0,\ldots,\min(2,1+\max(u))\}\) to every \(u\in\mathcal A_m\), and retain \(v=uc\) if and only if
\[
16\operatorname{LCS}(v[i:j],v[j:m+1])<7(m+1-i)
\quad\text{for every}\quad0\le i<j<m+1.
\]
This comparison is exactly the strict normalized edit-distance test after clearing denominators. Every older triple with right endpoint at most \(m\) lies in the preceding prefix and has already been checked. Conversely every valid restricted-growth word has a valid restricted-growth prefix and is generated by exactly one extension. Consequently this recurrence is exhaustive, without heuristics or estimates.

The LCS routine uses the standard exact integer dynamic program: when processing a character, the new prefix-pair value is the maximum of the value from the previous row, that of the current previous column, and the previous-row previous-column value plus the indicator that the new symbols match. Induction on the two prefix lengths proves the routine calculates LCS exactly.

Executing the bounded recurrence through length \(28\) gives survivor counts, by increasing length from \(1\),

`1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 13, 14, 18, 17, 17, 16, 15, 12, 11, 10, 9, 9, 10, 11, 10, 9, 9, 10, 11, 1, 0`.

The unique length-\(27\) canonical survivor is `012010212021012102010212012`. The complete executable recursion and asserted outputs are in `verify.py`.

**Independent enumeration.** A word with a repeated adjacent square has normalized edit distance zero, so all valid words are square-free. Independently generate every square-free word beginning with \(0\) (no synchronization-gap pruning and no restricted-growth normalization), using the direct suffix-square test on each appended symbol. There are exactly \(5162\) at length \(27\), and \(6740\) at length \(28\). Using a separate full two-dimensional LCS implementation to test every adjacent interval pair, precisely two length-\(27\) words pass, related by exchanging symbols \(1\) and \(2\), and none of the length-\(28\) words pass. This independently confirms both the length barrier and the six labeled extremizers. The self-contained implementation `independent.py` checks these counts.

**Exact best gap.** The first algorithm also enumerates all \(\binom{28}{3}=3276\) interval triples for the sole canonical length-\(27\) survivor, using exact rational arithmetic rather than floating point. Its only minimizing triples are \((0,12,23)\) and \((4,15,27)\), each giving \(3/23\). In the first, the adjacent blocks `012010212021` and `01210201021` have a common subsequence of length \(10\), hence edit distance \(23-20=3\). Every other length-\(27\) word has gap at most \(1/8<3/23\), because it failed the exhaustive strict \(1/8\) classification. Thus this is the exact maximum.

Finally, any length greater than \(28\) has a length-\(28\) prefix, and all interval inequalities in that prefix are also among those in the longer word. Therefore the absence of length-\(28\) solutions excludes all longer ones. The proof is complete.

## Verification

With Python 3.9 or later, run `python verify.py` and `python independent.py`; the recorded outputs are in `verification_output.txt`. The first program asserts the complete canonical level-size vector, exact witness, its gap and both attaining triples, the six relabelings, and the absence of length-\(28\) words. The second enumerates all square-free candidates beginning with \(0\) and tests them by an independent LCS implementation. Finite testing by itself is not an infinite theorem: the prefix inheritance argument establishes the unbounded-length exclusion.

## Relationship to prior work

Xu, Fu, Qiao and Tao (2026, arXiv:2609.04122) prove ternary synchronization strings exist for every length with a fixed small positive gap and explicitly leave optimization of the ternary synchronization gap open. They supply the strict adjacent-interval definition but not the present finite sharp cutoff. Cheng and collaborators (2018) study alphabet-size bounds and establish four-symbol existence and a binary obstruction; they do not give the length-\(27\) ternary profile. Camungol and Rampersad (2016) study approximate repetitions measured by LCS for adjacent equal-length blocks and give numerical bounds for infinite words. Those equal-length conditions alone do not imply this complete all-adjacent finite classification.

## Limitations

The result is for the strict \(1/8\) gap, not an optimal gap valid uniformly for infinitely many lengths, nor a full parameter-dependent phase diagram. Reproducibility depends on executing the provided finite exhaustive code; no independent human review has been performed. Unindexed prior tables or a different but equivalent formulation could cover the finite result. Strict and non-strict versions must not be conflated.
