# The exact worst two-link spectral failure of every binary hypercube

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge3\), exactly two failed links degrade the unweighted hypercube consensus spectral gap most when they are the two **parallel opposite sides of a single square face**. No other distinct edge pair attains the same lowest gap. With \(\rho_d\) denoting this minimum, \[2^{3-d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\frac{\binom{d-2}{j}}{2(j+1)-\rho_d}=1,\qquad 0<\rho_d<2.\] There are exactly \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) minimizing edge pairs. In particular, \(\rho_3=2-\sqrt2\); the minimum increases strictly with dimension and \[2-\rho_d=2^{3-d}\bigl(1+O(d^{-1})\bigr).\]

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set of \(Q_d\) is \(\{0,1\}^d\), and an edge joins two vertices differing in exactly one coordinate. Every existing edge has unit conductance. The combinatorial Laplacian is \(L=D-A\); its second-smallest eigenvalue \(\lambda_2\), called algebraic connectivity, controls the slowest decay of continuous-time symmetric consensus. Delete **exactly two distinct edges**, with no retuning. An opposite parallel edge pair of a square face consists of the two edges in the same coordinate direction belonging to a single two-dimensional face. The claim is not about the adjacency spectral radius or a normalized Laplacian.

## Proof
**1. A two-edge resolvent criterion.** Write \(L_d\) for the Laplacian of \(Q_d\). Fix an orientation of each failed edge \(e\) and let \(b_e=\mathbf e_u-\mathbf e_v\). For two distinct edges \(e,f\), put \(B=[b_e\ b_f]\), so \(L_{d,F}=L_d-BB^{\mathsf T}\). All incidence vectors are orthogonal to constants. The Walsh basis gives the eigenvalues \(2k\) of \(L_d\), with multiplicity \(\binom dk\), for \(k=0,\ldots,d\). For \(0\le s<2\) define \(K_F(s)=B^{\mathsf T}(L_d|_{\mathbf1^\perp}-sI)^{-1}B\). The two deleted edges cannot disconnect \(Q_d\): each nontrivial cut of \(Q_d\) has at least \(d\ge3\) edges. Therefore \(K_F(0)\) has spectral radius less than one, by positive definiteness of the surviving Laplacian on \(\mathbf1^\perp\). The resolvent derivative \(K_F'(s)=B^{\mathsf T}(L_d|_{\mathbf1^\perp}-sI)^{-2}B\) is positive definite because incidence vectors of distinct edges are linearly independent. As \(s\uparrow2\), an edge's nonzero projection onto the first Walsh level forces the largest eigenvalue of \(K_F(s)\) to diverge. The determinant lemma/inertia continuity consequently identifies \(\lambda_2(L_{d,F})\) with the unique \(s\in(0,2)\) at which \(\lambda_{\max}(K_F(s))=1\).

**2. Exact heat correlations.** The one-bit heat kernel has diagonal \(a(t)=(1+e^{-2t})/2\) and off-diagonal \(b(t)=(1-e^{-2t})/2\), with \(a(t)>b(t)>0\) for finite \(t>0\). Tensorization of \(e^{-tL_d}\) and the Laplace-resolvent integral show that \(K_F(s)\) has the form \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A(s)&C_F(s)\\ C_F(s)&A(s)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), where \[A(s)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-(2-s)t}a(t)^{d-1}\,dt.\]
If the two edges are distinct and **parallel**, their bases differ in \(k\ge1\) of the other coordinates, and \[|C_F(s)|=2\int_0^\infty e^{-(2-s)t}a(t)^{d-1-k}b(t)^k\,dt\le P_d(s),\qquad P_d(s)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-(2-s)t}a(t)^{d-2}b(t)\,dt.\] Equality holds exactly for \(k=1\), i.e. the opposite parallel sides of a square. If the edges have **different coordinate directions**, their cross correlation, after choosing orientations, is a sign times \[\int_0^\infty e^{-(4-s)t}a(t)^{d-2-k}b(t)^k\,dt\] for some integer \(k\ge0\), so its absolute value is at most \[V_d(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-(4-s)t}a(t)^{d-2}\,dt.\] All comparisons are strict outside the stated equality cases except possibly a comparison between \(P_d\) and \(V_d\), addressed next.

**3. Strict dominance of neighboring parallel failures at their threshold.** Since \(a+b=1\), \[F_d(s):=A(s)+P_d(s)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-(2-s)t}a(t)^{d-2}\,dt=2^{3-d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\frac{\binom{d-2}{j}}{2(j+1)-s}.\] The function increases strictly from \(F_d(0)<1\) (for \(d\ge3\)) to infinity as \(s\uparrow2\); write its unique unit level as \(\rho_d\). To prove \(P_d(\rho_d)>V_d(\rho_d)\), substitute \(u=e^{-2t}\), \(k=d-2\) and \(\alpha=1-\rho_d/2\). Then \[1=F_d(\rho_d)=\int_0^1u^{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1+u}{2}\right)^kdu,\qquad P_d(\rho_d)-V_d(\rho_d)=\frac12\int_0^1u^{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1+u}{2}\right)^k(1-2u)du.\] For \(k\ge4\), integration by parts yields \[1=\alpha+\frac{k}{2}\int_0^1u^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1+u}{2}\right)^{k-1}du.\] Hence the mean of \(u\) under the probability density appearing in the first integral is strictly below \(2(1-\alpha)/k<1/2\), proving positivity of the second integral. In the three remaining dimensions \(d=3,4,5\), respectively choose \(s_0=1/2,1,7/5\). Direct finite rational-binomial integration gives \[\begin{array}{c|cc}d&F_d(s_0)&P_d(s_0)-V_d(s_0)\\\hline3&20/21&2/231\\4&14/15&1/35\\5&2980/3289&7230/141427\end{array}.\] Thus \(s_0<\rho_d\). The normalized density above shifts monotonically toward smaller \(u\) as \(s\) increases, so its mean decreases; therefore \(P_d(\rho_d)>V_d(\rho_d)\) in these dimensions too.

**4. Uniqueness of the worst edge geometry.** At \(s=\rho_d\) an opposite square pair has \(\lambda_{\max}(K_F)=A+P_d=1\). Every other pair has \(\lambda_{\max}(K_F)=A+|C_F|<A+P_d=1\), by steps 2–3, so its first crossing happens at a strictly larger \(s\). A square face is determined by two variable coordinates and fixed remaining bits, giving \(\binom d2 2^{d-2}\) faces; each has two choices of opposite parallel edges. This proves both global uniqueness up to symmetry and the number of minimizers.

**5. First dimension, monotonicity, and asymptotics.** In dimension three the equation reduces to \((2-s)^{-1}+(4-s)^{-1}=1\), whose unique root in \((0,2)\) is \(2-\sqrt2\). Since \(a(t)<1\) for \(t>0\), \(F_{d+1}(s)<F_d(s)\) for each \(s<2\), so \(\rho_{d+1}>\rho_d\). Put \(\varepsilon_d=2-\rho_d\) and isolate the \(j=0\) summand: \[1=\frac{2^{3-d}}{\varepsilon_d}+R_d,\qquad 0\le R_d=2^{3-d}\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}\frac{\binom{d-2}{j}}{2j+\varepsilon_d}=O(d^{-1}).\] The estimate follows from \(j^{-1}\le2/(j+1)\) and \(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom kj/(j+1)=(2^{k+1}-1)/(k+1)\). Therefore \(\varepsilon_d=2^{3-d}/(1-R_d)=2^{3-d}(1+O(d^{-1}))\), as claimed.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library script `verify.py` checks all rational finite-dimensional comparison certificates and exact Walsh resolvent correlations for every unordered pair of failed edges in cubes of dimensions three and four. Run `python3 verify.py` and expect a `VERIFY_OK` line with an exact count. These finite checks independently corroborate the algebra but are **not** the proof for arbitrary dimension. The infinite result depends on the explicit heat-kernel identities, strict integral comparisons, and rank-two resolvent argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Sun, Boyd, Xiao and Diaconis (2006) characterize the unweighted Laplacian spectral gap as the convergence rate of symmetric Markov processes and study spectral-gap optimization over edge rates. Their studied problem allocates rates to existing edges, rather than determining the worst pair of deleted unit-weight links in hypercubes. Li and Xu (2012) establish edge-fault tolerance and cut properties of hypercube-like networks, with different objectives and without this spectral extremal classification. Wu, Zhang, Chen and Wang (2017) examine **adjacency spectral radius** following link removals from hypercubes, a different spectral invariant for which the extremal geometry can behave differently. The exact minimizing edge orbit and the formula above were not identified in the inspected material. General rank-two resolvent identities and Boolean-cube Fourier diagonalization are standard techniques and are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
Only the unweighted combinatorial Laplacian and exactly two failed links are covered; no claim concerns optimal reweighting, other numbers of failures, normalized or directed processes, or the adjacency spectrum. The asymptotic error constant is not optimized. The originality comparison cannot rule out an equivalent theorem in material not located or incompletely indexed. Independent external mathematical validation has not been performed.
