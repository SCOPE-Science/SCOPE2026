# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The specified Heawood graph is defined by the cyclic Fano-plane incidence difference set. Every one of the \(16383\) nonempty vertex subsets is tested against the exact induced-degree condition. A deterministically reproducible greedy matching pairs precisely \(1121\) elementary face inclusions. Exhaustive enumeration of all \(10150\) face-poset cover relations and a complete directed topological sort prove acyclicity; this is stronger than merely observing numerical homology. The remaining one vertex and thirteen five-cells give the asserted integral wedge by the discrete-Morse CW theorem. Independent mod-two boundary ranks corroborate the cell count.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The most directly motivating recent theorem by Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150) is about higher independence complexes of trees; the Heawood graph has cycles. Ghosh–Selvaraja (arXiv:2505.06614) and Abdelmalek et al. (arXiv:2106.10863) treat other graph classes, principally block/chordal examples. Available indexed Heawood classifications concern ordinary independence, matching, perfect matching, or cut complexes, mathematically distinct objects. A result for the two-independence complex of the Petersen graph does not imply the Heawood calculation. published-finding corpus and external searches for the Fano incidence graph, Heawood, induced-component bound two, maximum induced degree one, and \(P_3\)-free induced subsets did not identify equivalent or stronger coverage. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work remains a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Heawood graph is the canonical Fano-plane Levi graph, a natural and highly symmetric nonchordal cubic test object. Its first nontrivial higher-independence complex admits an exact integral homotopy classification even though the original simplicial complex has six-dimensional faces. The explicit optimal critical-cell pattern is a reusable benchmark for methods on incidence graphs of finite projective planes; the theorem does not claim to settle their general case. This is a substantive homotopy-type result, rather than a relabeling of an existing tree or chordal-graph theorem.
