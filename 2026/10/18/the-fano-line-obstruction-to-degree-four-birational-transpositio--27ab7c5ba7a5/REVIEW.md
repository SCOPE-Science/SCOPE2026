# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The statement specifies maps and inverses defined at every rational point, and the low-degree homaloidal possibilities are exhaustively reduced by the two Noether equations. The explicit quadratic conjugate and its seven-line signature are checked against the full binary projective action. A quartic map factors into two degree-two birational permutations using its noncollinear multiplicity-two base orbit, which proves parity without depending on probabilistic tests. Three replayed finite programs corroborate the arithmetic and quartic-candidate cases; they do not claim to prove birationality for every candidate system.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Asgarli–Lai–Nakahara–Zimmermann prove the full symmetric image and quadratic parity, and Schneider establishes relevant Galois-orbit reductions. Neither inspected article states the degree-four lower obstruction for a single transposition or the quadratic zero-preserved-line signature. It requires a further incidence comparison and low-degree homaloidal argument. An implicit deduction or unindexed enumeration in prior work remains a risk; the known full symmetric image does not by itself determine minimum degrees.

**Value — PASS.** The least-degree problem for a simple transposition is naturally motivated by the known surjection onto the symmetric group. The result gives a precise, reproducible low-degree cutoff, together with a geometric line-incidence explanation that distinguishes the degree-two maps from elementary permutations. It does not claim a degree-five construction or downstream classification.

Closest prior literature: arXiv:1910.05302 (the symmetric-image theorem and quadratic parity), arXiv:2008.07991 (Galois-orbit and generator methods). The main limitation is that the asserted bound is only a lower bound.
