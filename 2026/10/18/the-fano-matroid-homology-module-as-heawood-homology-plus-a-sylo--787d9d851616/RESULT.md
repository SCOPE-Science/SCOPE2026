# The Fano-matroid homology module as Heawood homology plus a Sylow-seven augmentation module

## Finding
Let \(V=\mathbb F_2^3\setminus\{0\}\) be the seven-point Fano plane, and let \(G=\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) act naturally on its points and lines. Define \(\Delta\) as the independence complex of the rank-three Fano matroid on \(V\), and let \(Y=\operatorname{Ind}(H)\) be the ordinary simplicial independence complex of the point–line incidence graph \(H\) of the same Fano plane. Let \(\Omega\) denote the eight Sylow-seven subgroups of \(G\), under conjugation, and \(\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0\) the submodule of coefficients summing to zero.

There is an isomorphism of rational \(G\)-representations
\[
\widetilde H_2(\Delta;\mathbb Q)\ \cong_{\mathbb Q[G]}\ \widetilde H_3(Y;\mathbb Q)\oplus\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0.
\]
The top Fano-matroid character is \((13,-3,1,1,-1,-1)\), listed on conjugacy classes of element orders \((1,2,3,4,7A,7B)\). Over \(\mathbb C\), its three nonisomorphic irreducible constituents have dimensions \(3\), \(3\), and \(7\), with the three-dimensional pair Galois-conjugate. The seven-dimensional term is the augmentation representation on \(\Omega\). Neither the Fano module nor either summand has a nonzero \(G\)-fixed vector.

This is an abstract rational-module identification, not a claimed geometric map, integral splitting, or canonical choice of isomorphism.

## Assumptions and scope
A subset of \(V\) is independent when its vectors are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_2\). Thus \(\Delta\) contains the empty face, seven vertices, all 21 pairs, and the 28 triples that are not Fano lines. Its face vector in nonempty dimensions is \((7,21,28)\). The seven Fano lines have the form \(\{a,b,a+b\}\) for distinct nonzero \(a,b\).

The Heawood graph \(H\) has one vertex for each of the seven points and seven lines; an edge means incidence. Its independence complex \(Y\) consists of subsets containing no incident point–line pair. The symmetry group \(G\) has order 168, acts on both complexes, and acts by conjugation on its eight Sylow-seven subgroups. Homology has coefficients in \(\mathbb Q\), except where explicitly stated.

## Proof
**1. Homology concentration and ranks.** The full one-skeleton of \(\Delta\) is the complete graph on seven vertices. Every missing triangular face is a Fano line; the boundary of such a triangle is null-homotopic using the three filled triangles joining its three edges to any fixed point outside that line. Since the fundamental group of a complete graph with its two-skeleton is normally generated by its triangular edge loops, \(\Delta\) is simply connected. Its reduced Euler characteristic is \(-1+7-21+28=13\). Because \(\Delta\) is two-dimensional, its only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_2(\Delta;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{13}\).

For \(Y\), enumeration from the incidence rule gives face counts by cardinality \(0\) through \(7\): \((1,14,70,154,147,56,14,2)\). Exact mod-two ranks of its *augmented* boundary maps on faces of cardinalities \(1\) through \(7\) are \((1,13,57,97,44,12,2)\); thus its reduced mod-two Betti numbers vanish except \(\widetilde\beta_3(Y;\mathbb F_2)=6\). The universal coefficient theorem and reduction of integral chain matrices imply that rational Betti numbers cannot exceed the corresponding mod-two Betti numbers. The reduced Euler characteristic is \(-6\), forcing \(\widetilde H_3(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^6\) and vanishing rational homology in all other degrees. The exact enumerations and boundary-rank reductions are reproduced by the included executable verifier.

**2. Signed equivariant Lefschetz computation.** For any simplicial permutation \(g\) of a finite complex \(K\), its reduced Lefschetz number is
\[
L^{\sim}(g,K)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in K\\g\sigma=\sigma}}(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma),
\]
where vertices of a face are oriented in their numerical order. Because the only reduced rational homology of \(\Delta\) is in even degree two, its character equals this Lefschetz number. Because the only reduced rational homology of \(Y\) is in odd degree three, its character is the negative of the corresponding Lefschetz number.

The complete set of 168 invertible binary matrices has five distinct element-order profiles: identity (one element), order two (21), order three (56), order four (42), and order seven (48). The order-seven elements form two conjugacy classes of 24 each, on which the characters agree. Their permutations on the seven points have cycle profiles respectively \(1^7\), \(2^2 1^3\), \(3^2 1\), \(4\,2\,1\), and \(7\). Exact fixed-oriented-face counts yield the following degree-zero/degree-one/degree-two Fano chain traces:

| Class | Size | \(\operatorname{tr} C_0(\Delta)\) | \(\operatorname{tr} C_1(\Delta)\) | \(\operatorname{tr} C_2(\Delta)\) | \(\chi_\Delta\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1\) | 1 | 7 | 21 | 28 | 13 |
| \(2\) | 21 | 3 | 1 | -4 | -3 |
| \(3\) | 56 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| \(4\) | 42 | 1 | -1 | 0 | 1 |
| \(7A,7B\) | 24 each | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |

In each row, \(\chi_\Delta=\operatorname{tr} C_2-\operatorname{tr} C_1+\operatorname{tr} C_0-1\). Applying the identical signed-face procedure to \(Y\) gives its degree-three homology character \(\chi_Y=(6,-2,0,2,-1,-1)\) on the same classes.

**3. The eight-subgroup permutation module.** Every order-seven element generates one Sylow-seven subgroup. Enumerating all their generated cyclic subgroups gives precisely eight distinct subgroups. For \(g\in G\), its action on \(\Omega\) fixes exactly \((8,0,2,0,1,1)\) subgroups on the listed classes. The one-dimensional constant subrepresentation is removed by the sum-zero augmentation construction, giving
\[
\chi_{\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0}=(7,-1,1,-1,0,0).
\]
Term by term, \(\chi_\Delta=\chi_Y+\chi_{\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0}\) on all six conjugacy classes. Because rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and characterized by their characters, the asserted \(\mathbb Q[G]\)-module isomorphism follows. No restriction to an order-seven subgroup was used in the conclusion.

**4. Irreducibility and invariants.** For the 168-element group, the weighted character inner products give \(\langle\chi_Y,\chi_Y\rangle_G=2\), \(\langle\chi_{\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0},\chi_{\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0}\rangle_G=1\), and \(\langle\chi_Y,\chi_{\mathbb Q[\Omega]_0}\rangle_G=0\). Comparison with the standard complex character table identifies \(\chi_Y\) as the sum of the two conjugate irreducible characters of degree three, and the augmentation term as the irreducible character of degree seven. In particular, the trivial character has inner product zero with both; hence the Fano module contains no invariant line.

## Verification
The included `verify_fano_modules.py` uses only the Python 3 standard library. Execute `python3 verify_fano_modules.py` in the folder containing that file. It constructs each of the 168 binary invertible matrices, all faces of both complexes, seven Fano lines, all eight Sylow-seven subgroups, and exact mod-two boundary ranks. It checks signed Lefschetz traces for **every** group element, Sylow fixed-point traces, equality of all characters, and the stated character inner products. Expected output begins with `VERIFY_OK; |G|=168; rank(Fano H2)=13; rank(Heawood H3)=6; Sylow count=8`. Finite exhaustive enumeration is the proof mechanism here; it is not an extrapolation from small samples.

## Relationship to prior work
The Fano matroid and its rank-three geometry are classical; Björner's treatment of matroid independence complexes supplies the basic homological context. Moci and Pezzoli develop a general equivariant theory of matroid independence homology and connections to geometric lattices. Their general theorem is not asserted to be absent as a route to this result; the specific full-group Fano character and the cross-complex Sylow-seven decomposition are the contributions presented here. The previously determined six-dimensional Heawood independence homology representation is a direct input to the comparison, not a new homology calculation in this note. Results about the *matching* complex of the Heawood graph use a different complex and do not imply the present identification.

## Limitations
This is an abstract isomorphism over \(\mathbb Q\). Neither an integral module splitting nor a canonical equivariant map between the complexes is supplied. The calculation gives no decomposition under point–line duality outside \(G\), and its group-character evaluation is specific to the seven-point Fano plane. General equivariant matroid theorems and less-indexed literature may contain an equivalent formulation; bibliographic originality remains subject to independent specialist review.
