# Review of the finite-field Peirce-incidence obstruction

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof reduces membership in each rank-one idempotent's Jordan-multiplication image to a trace-hyperplane condition, then to a rational line/conic intersection. It handles anisotropic, split-isotropic, scalar and zero cases separately. Character sums count every incidence class without relying on extrapolation from experiments. A standalone finite-field enumerator checked all matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\), \(\mathbb F_7\), \(\mathbb F_{11}\), \(\mathbb F_{13}\), and \(\mathbb F_9\).

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The accessible abstract of the closest primary paper (Gogić and Tomašević, arXiv:2609.23581) states the relevant symmetric-matrix representation results under quadratic closure, not finite fields. Scientific searches for terminal Peirce incidences, quadratic-character obstruction, and finite-field idempotent Jordan words did not identify a matching count. The closest indexed factorization records use ordinary associative products, not the Jordan-multiplication images being classified. The primary full text was not accessible and may contain stronger hidden lemmas; absence from search results is not regarded as uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** The terminal-factor incidence is a natural invariant for the current idempotent-word question. Its exact four-level distribution displays a field-splitting effect and certifies a large, explicitly counted class of nonscalar matrices excluded from every idempotent word. It creates a sharp geometric obstruction for subsequent complete representability work. The present claim is deliberately limited to this obstruction rather than asserting a converse.

**Limitations.** Only odd fields and symmetric \(2\times2\) matrices are covered. Exact incidence-zero counts do not determine the total number of unrepresentable matrices absent a converse theorem. The complete full-text comparison with the closest paper and independent mathematical auditing remain outstanding.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
