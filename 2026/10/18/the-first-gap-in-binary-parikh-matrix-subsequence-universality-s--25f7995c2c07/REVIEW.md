# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof is exhaustive, not merely experimental. Any index at least two in a given fiber implies that every member of the fiber has index at least two (the length-two patterns are characterized by \(A,B\ge2\) and \(0<C<AB\)). Since every arch uses two positions, a gap cannot appear below length eight. At length eight, index four forces four two-letter arches and therefore \((A,B)=(4,4)\) and \(C\in\{6,7,8,9,10\}\). Explicit index-three witnesses eliminate four candidate fibers. The fifth has eight words by a complete bounded-partition listing; six have index four and two have index two. The independent direct-subsequence checker confirms all \(511\) length-at-most-eight words and all \(255\) realized fibers. The result neither treats finite experiments as infinite proofs nor assumes unavailable classifications.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence
The 2026 binary Parikh-matrix article provides existential and universal *endpoint* criteria and considers fixed-subsequence probabilities, but an endpoint pair \((2,4)\) does not decide whether index three is attained. Its relevant full-text sections on the universality indices were inspected; no intermediate-index spectrum or unique first-gap example was found. Richomme's full paper supplies the partition representation but not an arch-spectrum classification. The published exact generating function for the binary universality index aggregates over all binary words of fixed length, not over each fixed \((A,B,C)\) class. Recorded literature and research-database searches and comparison against earlier accessible distinct claims did not identify a covering assertion. The residual risk is an unindexed or differently named earlier example; search absence is not proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The fixed-Parikh-matrix spectrum is the natural refinement connecting existential and universal subsequence-universality extremal parameters studied in the motivating paper. A minimal and uniquely located hole disproves the plausible interval-filling property in the first possible length; the entire exceptional class and its two gap-producing words are determined structurally. The finite cutoff is mathematically forced by the two-letters-per-arch bound, not an arbitrary enumerated slice. The result motivates further work on holes at larger lengths without asserting such a classification.
