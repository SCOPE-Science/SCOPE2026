# The first mixed-dimensional fully three-stable Kneser pair box complex

## Finding
For \(m\in\{6,7,8\}\), define the fully three-stable Kneser pair graph \(G_m\) as in the next section, and let \(\mathcal N(G_m)\) denote its ordinary *open-neighborhood* complex. Then
\[
\mathcal N(G_6)\simeq\mathcal N(G_7)\simeq S^1,
\qquad
\mathcal N(G_8)\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3.
\]
The established nonequivariant equivalence \(B(G)\simeq\mathcal N(G)\) transfers these homotopy types to the standard box complex. Thus the third admissible size produces two distinct positive homotopy degrees rather than a single sphere dimension. For \(m=8\), reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^2\) in degree two and \(\mathbb Z\) in degree three, and zero otherwise.

## Assumptions and scope
Every graph is finite, simple, undirected, without loops. For \(m\ge6\), identify the ground set with \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) and define its eligible vertices
\[
V(G_m)=\left\{\{i,j\}:0\le i<j<m,\quad \min(j-i,m-j+i)\ge3\right\}.
\]
Two eligible pairs are adjacent in \(G_m\) if and only if they are disjoint. A nonempty collection \(\sigma\subseteq V(G_m)\) is a face of \(\mathcal N(G_m)\) if and only if some eligible pair is disjoint from *every* member of \(\sigma\). This is the complex generated by all open vertex neighborhoods of the graph. Its vertices are pair-objects, not the original elements of the cyclic ground set.

For \(m=8\), the eligible pair-objects are indexed, in the following exact lexicographic order, by
\[
(0,3),(0,4),(0,5),(1,4),(1,5),(1,6),(2,5),(2,6),(2,7),(3,6),(3,7),(4,7).
\]
The eight circular diagonals of cyclic length three together with four opposite diagonals form a copy of the Wagner graph after multiplying cyclic labels by three. Consequently, \(G_8\cong\overline{L(M_8)}\), where \(M_8\) is the eight-vertex Wagner/Möbius-ladder graph. This identifies the same object in line-graph language; it does not identify its neighborhood complex with the ordinary matching complex.

## Proof
The common-neighbor condition defines the complete face set by taking *all* nonempty subsets of every graph open neighborhood. There are twelve graph vertices and twelve generating neighborhoods, each with seven members. Their union has 910 nonempty simplices, with face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(12,66,204,304,228,84,12).
\]
The supplied `verify.py` independently constructs each pair-object and graph adjacency from the above definition; it then enumerates precisely those faces, without relying on a precomputed facet list.

Here is a finite, completely specified acyclic matching on the face poset of \(\mathcal N(G_8)\). Process the twelve vertex indices in the order
\[
7,0,1,5,3,9,2,8,4,11,10,6.
\]
At each index \(v\), run through the face masks in increasing numeric order; whenever both a presently unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) and \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) are currently unmatched, match them and remove both from consideration. This creates 453 matched pairs and leaves only four critical faces:
\[
\{7\},\quad \{0,1,5\},\quad\{0,4,10\},\quad\{1,3,6,8\}.
\]
To certify acyclicity, orient every codimension-one Hasse incidence downward unless it is matched, in which case orient it upward. The code constructs all 910 vertices of this directed Hasse graph and exhaustively removes every one in a topological ordering. Thus no directed cycle exists, so this is an acyclic Forman matching. Its Morse CW complex has exactly one zero-cell, two two-cells and one three-cell, with no other cells.

To determine the only possibly nonzero Morse attaching degrees, use oriented simplices in increasing vertex-index order and the exact integral boundary \(\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_d]=\sum_i(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat{v_i},\ldots,v_d]\). Define the projection of critical faces to themselves and of matched upper faces to zero. For a matched lower face \(\ell\) paired with upper face \(h\), if \(\epsilon=[\ell:\partial h]\in\{\pm1\}\), recursively set
\[
R(\ell)=-\epsilon\sum_{\ell'\ne\ell}[\ell':\partial h]R(\ell').
\]
Acyclicity guarantees recursion terminates. The resulting exact Morse boundary of critical \(c\) is \(\sum_{\ell}[\ell:\partial c]R(\ell)\). The executable calculation shows it is identically zero for every critical cell, including the unique critical three-cell. Hence the two-skeleton is \(S^2\vee S^2\). By the Hurewicz isomorphism \(\pi_2(S^2\vee S^2)\cong H_2(S^2\vee S^2;\mathbb Z)\), vanishing of the integral attaching degrees implies the three-cell attaching map is nullhomotopic; the whole Morse CW complex is \(S^2\vee S^2\vee S^3\). In particular, the argument proves the integral homotopy type and is stronger than a Betti-number computation.

For \(m=6\) and \(m=7\), apply exactly the same face-enumeration and greedy matching recipe with respective pivot orders \((2,1,0)\) and \((3,0,1,2,6,4,5)\). The resulting complexes contain respectively 6 and 56 nonempty simplices; the matchings contain respectively 2 and 27 pairs, and in each case leave exactly one zero-cell and one one-cell. Both directed Hasse graphs are exhausted by a topological sort. Thus their Morse CW complexes are circles.

The nonequivariant box-complex homotopy equivalence is the established theorem attributed to Csorba and collaborators in Daneshpajouh–Meunier, Theorem 3.2; it is used, not reproved or claimed as new.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` and `python3 check_mod2.py` from the extracted package directory using Python 3 and the standard library. The first program reconstructs all complexes, validates face counts and acyclic matching orientations, and computes signed integral Morse boundaries by exact integer arithmetic. The second creates a logically separate face enumerator and independently reduces all simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\). `verification_output.txt` records the actual expected output of both runs. The mod-two computation agrees: reduced Betti profiles are \((\widetilde\beta_1=1)\) for \(m=6,7\) and \((\widetilde\beta_2=2,\widetilde\beta_3=1)\) for \(m=8\). The integral conclusion relies on the acyclic matching and signed Morse-boundary calculation, not on finite-field agreement.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner–de Longueville classified ordinary **two-stable** Kneser neighborhood complexes as spheres, and Daneshpajouh–Osztényi (first publicly available 2019-04-17, arXiv:1904.08219) extended that classification to vector-stable gaps with the final gap in \(\{1,2\}\). Their theorem does **not** cover the fully three-stable final gap three. Osztényi (2021) studied \(\langle s,t\rangle\)-stable graphs, proved a special \(t<s\) sphere theorem, and tabulated several larger \(s\) parameters; its stated theorems and tables do not give the exact \(m=8,s=t=3,k=2\) result here. The Daneshpajouh–Meunier box-complex survey (arXiv:2307.00299, Section 9.2) explicitly highlights the poor understanding of box complexes of general \(s\)-stable Kneser graphs, and supplies the prior \(B(G)\simeq\mathcal N(G)\) theorem used here. Prior related matching- or cut-complex computations for the Wagner graph concern different simplicial constructions and do not imply this claim.

## Limitations
Only the three smallest admissible sizes \(m=6,7,8\) are classified. No result for larger \(m\), no assertion of uniqueness among all graphs, and no equivariant \(\mathbb Z_2\)-homotopy classification of the box complex is claimed. Computational checks concern finite complexes fully enumerated at these sizes; they are not an extrapolation to an infinite family. The prior-work comparison cannot exclude work absent from the inspected published and indexed sources.
