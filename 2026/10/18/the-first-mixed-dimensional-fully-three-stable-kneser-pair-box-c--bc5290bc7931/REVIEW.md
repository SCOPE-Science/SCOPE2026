## Correctness: PASS
The cyclic separation condition is defined literally and checked on every unordered pair. For the twelve eligible vertices at size eight, all twelve common neighborhoods and their downward closures are enumerated: 910 faces with the stated face vector. A fixed greedy face-poset matching has 453 pairs and four critical faces (one zero-, two two-, and one three-dimensional). A complete 910-vertex Hasse-direction topological sort proves its acyclicity. Exact signed integral Morse-boundary projection gives zero for all critical boundaries. The sole three-cell therefore attaches trivially by the Hurewicz isomorphism for a wedge of two two-spheres. The analogous complete matchings at sizes six and seven leave a vertex and an edge and prove two circles. A separate mod-two boundary calculation independently confirms every finite Betti number but is not relied on for integral homotopy.

## Originality: PASS with residual uncertainty
Full text of Daneshpajouh–Osztényi, arXiv:1904.08219, was compared at its Theorem 2 and proof: its last gap is restricted to one or two, whereas the present graph requires three. Osztényi's 2021 primary full text and tables concern other conditions, chiefly special \(t<s\) cases and documented larger parameters; the exact \(s=t=3,m=8,k=2\) type does not occur there. Daneshpajouh–Meunier (2023) explicitly lists general stable-Kneser box-complex topology as a motivating open direction and states the prior nonequivariant box/neighborhood equivalence, which is not new here. Semantic database searches of fully-three-stable, ordinary-neighborhood, box-complex and complement-of-Wagner-line-graph formulations produced related but noncovering findings on the Wagner matching/cut/Morse complexes and on two-stable *independence* complexes. A possible unindexed equivalent calculation is not ruled out by search.

## Mathematical value: PASS
This is the first small order at which the natural fully-three-stable two-subset family ceases to have a single sphere dimension. The explicit coexistence of two independent degree-two classes and one degree-three class provides a geometric obstruction to extending the standard two-stable sphere pattern, at a concrete natural boundary identified as interesting in the stable-Kneser literature. Its precise nonequivariant box-complex homotopy type is directly relevant to topology-based chromatic obstructions. It is a complete finite topological classification at this threshold, not a relabeled numerical exercise; it makes no unsupported claim about all sizes or equivariant topology.
