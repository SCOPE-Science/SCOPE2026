# The first modular rank-jump hypersurface for null-filiform Leibniz derivations

## Finding
Fix a prime \(p\), an integer \(f\ge1\), and \(q=p^f\). Write \(n=2p\). Let \(L=NF_n(\mathbb F_q)\) be the non-Lie null-filiform Leibniz algebra with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\), and product \([e_i,e_1]=e_{i+1}\) for \(1\le i<n\); all other basis products vanish. Derivations are linear maps \(D\) satisfying \(D[x,y]=[Dx,y]+[x,Dy]\).

**Theorem.** Every derivation is uniquely determined by \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb F_q\) as
\[
 D_a(e_i)=i a_1 e_i+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n} a_{j-i+1}e_j\quad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Let \(N_k=|\{D\in\operatorname{Der}(L):\operatorname{rank}D=k\}|\). Then
\[
N_0=1,\qquad N_k=(q-1)q^{k-1}\quad(1\le k\le2p-3),
\]
\[
N_{2p-2}=(q-1)(q+1)q^{2p-3},\qquad N_{2p-1}=(q-1)q^{2p-1}.
\]
No derivation has rank \(2p\). Within the open coefficient set \(a_1\ne0\), the rank-drop locus \(\operatorname{rank}D\le2p-2\) is a smooth, irreducible, rational hypersurface, isomorphic over \(\mathbb F_q\) to \(\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^{2p-2}\), with \((q-1)q^{2p-2}\) rational points. Dimension \(2p\) is the first dimension at which the open stratum \(a_1\ne0\) of null-filiform derivations has more than one possible rank: it has rank \(n-1\) in every dimension \(p\le n<2p\).

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field is exactly a finite field \(\mathbb F_{p^f}\), with arbitrary prime \(p\) including \(p=2\). The geometric assertions also hold after any field extension of characteristic \(p\). Ranks mean ranks of the derivations as linear endomorphisms of the underlying \(2p\)-dimensional vector space, not adjoint ranks and not dimensions of derivation-algebra orbits. The statement concerns the reduced rank locus restricted to \(a_1\ne0\).

## Proof
The algebra is generated by \(e_1\) under right-nested products, so \(D(e_1)=\sum_j a_je_j\) uniquely determines a derivation. Applying the derivation identity inductively to \(e_{i+1}=[e_i,e_1]\) gives the displayed formula: the coefficient of \(e_i\) grows by \(a_1\) at each step, while each higher coefficient shifts up one place. Conversely the displayed formula respects all products: for a zero product with right input \(e_j\) and \(j\ge2\), the right-hand side is zero because \(D(e_j)\) has no \(e_1\)-component; for right input \(e_1\), it holds by construction. Therefore \(a\mapsto D_a\) bijects \(\mathbb F_q^n\) with the derivation space.

Let \(J(e_i)=e_{i+1}\) for \(i<n\), and \(J(e_n)=0\). If \(a_1=0\), then \(D_a=\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}a_{r+1}J^r\). If the earliest nonzero coefficient occurs at index \(r+1\), then \(D_a=J^r\cdot U(J)\) for an invertible polynomial \(U(J)\), giving rank \(n-r\). The number of choices with that first nonzero coefficient is \((q-1)q^{n-r-1}\). The identically zero derivation is unique. These give \(N_k^{(a_1=0)}=(q-1)q^{k-1}\) for \(1\le k\le n-1\) and \(N_0^{(a_1=0)}=1\).

Now suppose \(a_1\ne0\) and \(n=2p\). The matrix is lower triangular, with diagonal \(i a_1\). Only positions \(i=p\) and \(i=2p\) have vanishing diagonal. In \(D_av=0\), rows \(1,\ldots,p-1\) force \(v_1=\cdots=v_{p-1}=0\). Row \(p\) imposes no further condition, and \(v_p=t\) is free. Successive rows \(p+j\) for \(1\le j<p\) yield \(v_{p+j}=u_jt\), where
\[
u_0=1,\qquad u_j=-\frac{1}{j a_1}\sum_{r=1}^{j}a_{r+1}u_{j-r}\quad(1\le j<p).
\]
All denominators are nonzero. The final row \(2p\) imposes \(\Delta_p(a)t=0\), where
\[
\Delta_p(a)=\sum_{r=1}^{p}a_{r+1}u_{p-r}.
\]
The final coordinate \(v_{2p}\) is always free. Hence the kernel has dimension two if \(\Delta_p=0\), and one otherwise. Crucially, \(u_1,\ldots,u_{p-1}\) depend only on \(a_1,\ldots,a_p\), and \(\Delta_p=a_{p+1}+\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}a_{r+1}u_{p-r}\). Thus \(\Delta_p=0\) uniquely determines \(a_{p+1}\) for any \(a_1\ne0\), any \(a_2,\ldots,a_p\), and any \(a_{p+2},\ldots,a_{2p}\). Its zero set is a graph of a regular function on \(\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^{2p-2}\), hence the claimed smooth rational irreducible hypersurface. The rank-\(n-2\) count in this open set is \((q-1)q^{n-2}\); the complementary rank-\(n-1\) count is \((q-1)^2q^{n-2}\). Combining with the \(a_1=0\) ranks gives the formulas.

In dimension \(p\le m<2p\), the same triangular diagonal has exactly one zero, at \(p\). Thus every derivation with \(a_1\ne0\) has nullity exactly one, since the earlier rows have nonzero diagonal and coordinate \(v_p\) is free. At dimension \(2p\) the second zero diagonal creates the first possible additional kernel direction, actually realized by choosing \(a_{p+1}\) on the hypersurface. For \(m<p\), all diagonal entries are nonzero and those derivations are invertible; the qualification \(p\le m\) is essential.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` constructs each derivation matrix directly, computes rank by finite-field Gaussian elimination, independently checks derivation identities in the smallest case, and compares ranks with the recurrence and census. Exhaustive checks: \((p,q,n)=(2,2,4),(2,4,4),(3,3,6)\). Observed rank-frequency vectors were \((1,1,6,8)\), \((1,3,60,192)\), and \((1,2,6,18,216,486)\), respectively. A deterministic sample of 3,000 matrices for \((p,q,n)=(5,5,10)\) matched the predicted rank criterion. The script prints `VERIFY_OK`. Finite tests supplement but do not replace the proof for all primes, dimensions, and field extensions.

## Relationship to prior work
Adashev, Camacho and Omirov (2015) define and study this exact null-filiform multiplication over characteristic zero, primarily treating central extensions; their setup supplies an archive-era source for the underlying algebra, but not the modular rank jump. Centrone and Zargeh (2021) study actions of Hopf algebras and include a characteristic-zero treatment of graded derivations of the same algebra. Jumayeva (2026) states a full derivation description for complex null-filiform Leibniz algebras and emphasizes local derivations, not finite-field rank loci. The present theorem counts all derivations by linear rank in positive characteristic at the first double-zero diagonal and identifies its rank-jump geometry; neither the preceding characteristic-zero derivation description nor a general nilpotent-shift formula explicitly settles the modular hypersurface statement.

## Limitations
No assertion is made about the complete derivation-conjugacy orbit classification, higher resonance dimensions \(n>2p\), or scheme structure beyond the stated open graph. The derivation-matrix form is elementary and known in characteristic-zero treatments; the specific positive-characteristic rank stratification is the assessed contribution. An unlocated positive-characteristic derivation-rank theorem could cover it; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
