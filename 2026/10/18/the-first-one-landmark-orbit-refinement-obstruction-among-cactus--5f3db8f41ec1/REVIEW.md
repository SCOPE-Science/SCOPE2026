# Review of the pointed-cactus cutoff

**Correctness — PASS.** The leaf-block deletion lemma proves the exhaustive recursive construction of all connected cacti through nine vertices; a separate removable-vertex augmentation independently generates the same graph counts. Each rooted instance is tested by exact counting refinement and automorphism existence. The two replayed checks agree on \((1,1,2,4,9,23,63,188,596)\) unrooted types and on exactly one bad rooting among \(5364\) order-nine rootings, with zero failures at smaller orders. The named nine-vertex graph has a direct three-cell equitable partition and a four-orbit automorphism partition.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Arvind et al. give the general refinability framework; Verbitsky and Zhukovskii address universal covers, unicyclic graphs and random cores, not the finite pointed-cactus census. The standard cactus graph-count sequence is not a table of refinement failures. Searches for pointed/individualized cactus refinability, aliases in two-variable counting logic, and a nine-vertex uniqueness theorem revealed no dominating result. Residual risk remains from unlocated specialized literature.

**Value — PASS.** This is the complete sharp failure threshold of a standard one-landmark individualization heuristic for a natural block-decomposable graph class, with a unique first obstruction and a transparent explanation of the cycle-length information lost by counting refinement. It distinguishes a genuine three-cycle-block obstruction from unicyclic behaviour and does not merely recompute graph counts. No claim is made for order ten or larger.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
