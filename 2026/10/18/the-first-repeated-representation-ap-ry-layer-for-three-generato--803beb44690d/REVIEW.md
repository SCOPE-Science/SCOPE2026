# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(b\ge2\), the proof constructs a bijection between representations at fixed residue and pairs \((t,j)\) satisfying the displayed exact height inequality. Row minima rise strictly by either \(2\) or \(b+2\), so the second-smallest admissible height is determined by the first two rows and the first nonzero within-row shift. The four residue strata are maximized explicitly; their comparison proves the phase change in \(g_1\), and a full residue sum proves the cubic \(n_1\) formula. Exact dynamic-programming and a separate congruence-enumeration routine corroborate the algebra without serving as an infinite proof. All endpoint cases, including \(b=2\), \(q=0\), and \(q=b\), are accounted for.

## Originality — PASS within checked sources
The originating generalized repunit paper (Branco–Colaço–Ojeda, arXiv:2112.01106) computes the ordinary first-representation Apéry set, not the first **two**-representation layer. Komatsu's repunit-length family and shifted-geometric family, the arithmetic-progression triple formulas of Komatsu–Ying, and the restricted three-variable formula of Subwattanachai were compared at the level of parameter domains and implication; none supplies this infinite family's displayed threshold. A direct generic exact-denumerant algorithm is not already an explicit closed threshold or the changeover at \(b=4\). Related results in the accessible mathematical finding index were compared by statement and do not cover the theorem. Residual risk remains from unexamined broad representation-count classifications or later updates.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a canonical minimal three-generator specialization of generalized repunit semigroups, the next representation-multiplicity layer is a natural unexplored invariant after the ordinary Frobenius problem. The exact classification gives every residue's transition, all gaps with at most one representation, and a sharp change in which residue controls the maximum; it is a uniform all-base result, not a table recomputation or a numerical gain. It creates a concrete baseline for higher multiplicity layers.
