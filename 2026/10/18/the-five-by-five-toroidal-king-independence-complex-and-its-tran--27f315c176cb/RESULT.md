# The five-by-five toroidal king independence complex and its translation homology

## Finding
Let \(G=C_5\boxtimes C_5\) be the strong product of two five-cycles, with vertex set \(\mathbf F_5^2\). Let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) be the independence complex. Its **integral homotopy type** is
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{141} S^3.
\]

The translation group \(T=\mathbf F_5^2\) acts on \(K\). Write \(\chi(a,b)\) for the character of its action on \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbf Q)\). The **complete translation character** is
\[
\chi(a,b)=\begin{cases}
141,&(a,b)=(0,0),\\
-4,&a\ne0\ \text{and}\ b\in\{2a,3a\},\\
1,&\text{all remaining nonidentity translations}.
\end{cases}
\]
There is no other reduced rational or integral homology. For each of the six order-five translation subgroups, the quotient has reduced rational homology only in degree three: rank \(25\) for the two subgroups generated by \((1,2)\) or \((1,3)\), and rank \(29\) for the other four. The full translation quotient has third reduced rational Betti number \(5\), and no other reduced rational homology.

## Assumptions and scope
All arithmetic in coordinates is modulo five. Vertices \((r,c)\) and \((s,d)\) are adjacent in \(G\) exactly when they are distinct and both \(r-s\) and \(c-d\) belong to \(\{0,1,-1\}\). A simplex of \(K\) is a nonempty independent vertex subset; the empty face is included in the augmented chain complex. Statements about geometric quotients refer to the orbit spaces \(|K|/H\) for subgroups \(H\le T\). Rational transfer refers to cellular chains on an equivariant barycentric subdivision, not to a presumption of a free action.

## Proof
**Integral homotopy via a complete finite Morse certificate.** Encode each of the 25 vertices by \(5r+c\) and each nonempty face by a 25-bit integer. The supplied `morse_certificate.json` gives 634 pairs \((\sigma,\tau)\), where \(\sigma\) is a codimension-one face of \(\tau\), and 142 unmatched faces. The supplied `verify_king25.py` regenerates all faces directly from the adjacency rule and checks that the pairs are disjoint, have the indicated dimensions and partition all 1,410 nonempty faces with the 142 unmatched faces. It orients **every** 4,550 Hasse-cover edge upward for a matched pair, downward otherwise, and verifies that the resulting finite directed graph is acyclic by a complete topological sort. Its unmatched cells consist of exactly one vertex and 141 three-simplices. Forman's discrete Morse theorem yields a CW homotopy model with one zero-cell, 141 three-cells and no other cells. Every three-cell attaches to the single zero-cell by the unique map from its two-sphere boundary, giving \(\bigvee^{141}S^3\). This proves the asserted integral homotopy type without inferring it merely from a Betti-number experiment.

**Maximal independent sets.** In any fixed row, an independent set contains at most two vertices. If a row contains two, their columns differ by two, and their combined closed horizontal neighborhoods cover all five columns; hence both neighboring rows are empty. The two remaining rows are adjacent in the cyclic five-row index and together accommodate at most two vertices, so such a set has cardinality at most four. Consequently, every independent set of cardinality five has exactly one point in each row. Each successive column difference along the row cycle must be \(+2\) or \(-2\), and the sum of the five signed differences is zero modulo five. Five such signs can sum to zero modulo five only when all are equal. Thus the ten five-element independent sets are precisely the affine lines
\[
S_{s,c}=\{(r,sr+c):r\in\mathbf F_5\},\qquad s\in\{2,3\},\ c\in\mathbf F_5.
\]
The verifier enumerates all faces and independently checks this classification. In fact the complete face vector (indexed by cardinality one through five) is \((25,200,600,575,10)\).

**Translation Lefschetz numbers.** Every nonzero element of \(T\) has order five, acts freely on the 25 vertices, and therefore can preserve a nonempty face setwise only if that face has cardinality five. Translation by \((a,b)\) fixes the affine line \(S_{s,c}\) as a set exactly when \(b=sa\). For nonzero translations in either of the two slope directions, there are five fixed four-dimensional simplices; each cyclically permutes its five vertices and therefore has orientation sign \(+1\). All other nonidentity translations preserve no nonempty simplex. With the empty face contributing \(-1\) to the reduced Lefschetz sum, these traces are \(4\) and \(-1\), respectively. Because the only reduced rational homology occurs in odd degree three, \(\chi=-\widetilde L\); this gives \(-4\) and \(+1\). The identity trace equals the proven homology dimension \(141\). All 25 cases are independently verified by signed simplex enumeration in `verify_king25.py`.

**Subgroup quotients.** Over \(\mathbf Q\), subdivision and averaging give \(\widetilde H_*(|K|/H;\mathbf Q)\cong \widetilde H_*(K;\mathbf Q)^H\). Thus the third Betti number is \(|H|^{-1}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)\). Every order-five subgroup is a line in \(\mathbf F_5^2\). For the two distinguished slope lines this average is \((141+4(-4))/5=25\); for each other line it is \((141+4)/5=29\). The average over all 25 translations is \((141+8(-4)+16)/25=5\). The other reduced rational homology vanishes by transfer.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_king25.py` with `morse_certificate.json` in the same directory. The verifier requires only the Python standard library and regenerates the graph and every simplex from definitions. It checks 634 explicit matching pairs, all 4,550 gradient-cover edges, every unmatched cell, the exact ten maximal independent five-faces, signed actions of all 25 translations, every order-five subgroup quotient and the full quotient. An independent \(\mathbf F_2\) boundary-rank computation returns \((24,176,424,10)\) in positive cardinality steps, consistent with the proved homology. `verification_output.txt` records a successful execution of this exact verifier and certificate. The finite certificate and exhaustive traversal, rather than that recorded output alone, supply the crucial finite argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Carnero Bravo, *On independence complexes of graph products* (arXiv:2505.06457, first submitted 9 May 2025), studies homotopy types for graph products involving paths, including several path strong products; its results do not specify this periodic strong product of two five-cycles. Hildebrant, *Transfer Operators and Independence Polynomials for Strong Powers of Circulant Graphs* (arXiv:2604.14416, first submitted 15 April 2026), computes independence-polynomial data for periodic strong products. OEIS A394697 explicitly gives the **same** five-by-five independence polynomial \(1+25z+200z^2+600z^3+575z^4+10z^5\); its face counts and resulting Euler characteristic are credited as known, not new. Neither the known face polynomial nor the cited path-product homotopy results imply the explicit integral wedge decomposition or this translation-homology character. Targeted comparisons with public related graph-complex results did not identify a statement implying either. Absence of a matching search result is not a proof of novelty.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns exactly the five-by-five periodic strong product, not all toroidal board sizes. The finite Morse certificate is independently reproducible, but independent mathematical review has not been performed. The subgroup-quotient conclusions are over \(\mathbf Q\); integral torsion and unstable homotopy types of those quotients are not determined. Unindexed equivalent equivariant classifications remain a possible literature-overlap risk. No claim is made about novel face counts or Euler characteristic.
