# The full arithmetic automorphism group of the one-point Hermitian-relative curve

## Finding
For every prime power \(s\ge 3\), write \(K=\mathbb F_{s^2}\). Fix \(\eta\in K\setminus\mathbb F_s\) and let \(C_\eta\) be the smooth plane curve
\[
X^{s+1}+\eta Y^{s+1}+Y^sZ+YZ^s=0.
\]
The *entire group of abstract \(K\)-automorphisms*, not merely a listed subgroup of the plane stabilizer, is represented uniquely by matrices
\[
T(u,v,h)=\begin{pmatrix}
u&v&0\\0&1&0\\-uv^s&h&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u^{s+1}=1,\quad v,h\in K,\quad h+h^s=-v^{s+1}.
\]
There is an exact semidirect-product description
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(C_\eta)\simeq U_s\rtimes\mu_{s+1},\qquad
|U_s|=s^3,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}_K(C_\eta)|=s^3(s+1).
\]
The description does not depend on the choice of \(\eta\) outside \(\mathbb F_s\).

## Assumptions and scope
The characteristic is arbitrary, including two, and \(s=p^r\ge3\) with \(p\) prime and \(r\ge1\). The symbol \(\mu_{s+1}\) denotes the cyclic group of norm-one elements of \(K^\times\), of order \(s+1\). The group \(U_s\) consists of the matrices \(T(1,v,h)\), where \(h+h^s=-v^{s+1}\); its multiplication is
\[
(v,h)(v',h')=(v+v',h+h'-v^s v').
\]
It is a two-step nilpotent Heisenberg group with center \(\{(0,h):h+h^s=0\}\) of order \(s\). This theorem is about automorphisms over the given finite field \(K\), not the geometric group after extending scalars. The case \(s=2\) is omitted because the curve is cubic and the geometric automorphism-extension argument changes.

## Proof
**Smoothness, unique point and tangent.** The partial derivatives of the defining polynomial are \(X^s\), \(\eta Y^s+Z^s\), and \(Y^s\). They cannot vanish simultaneously at a projective point, so \(C_\eta\) is smooth. For \((X,Y,Z)\in K^3\), the \(s\)-th Frobenius conjugate of the defining equation minus the original equation equals \((\eta^s-\eta)Y^{s+1}=0\). Since \(\eta^s\ne\eta\), any \(K\)-point has \(Y=0\), and the original equation then forces \(X=0\). Thus \(P=[0:0:1]\) is the only \(K\)-point. Its tangent is \(Y=0\).

**Why every abstract automorphism is projective.** The curve is a Hermitian-relative plane curve, so its base change to \(\overline K\) is projectively isomorphic to the classical Hermitian curve; see Homma--Kim and Pardini. For \(s\ge3\), the classical theorem identifying the full geometric Hermitian-curve automorphism group with \(\operatorname{PGU}(3,s)\) shows that every geometric automorphism acts projectively on this plane model. The projective representative of an automorphism defined over \(K\) is also defined over \(K\): its Galois conjugate has the same action on the nondegenerate curve, and the \(\operatorname{PGL}_3\)-action on that curve is faithful. Consequently every \(K\)-automorphism is represented by some \(T\in\operatorname{PGL}_3(K)\).

**Solving the complete stabilizer.** Each such \(T\) fixes the unique \(K\)-point and its tangent, hence has a representative
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0\\0&c&0\\d&e&f\end{pmatrix},\qquad acf\ne0.
\]
Define the \(s\)-conjugate transpose \(M^*=(m_{ji}^s)\) and the invertible coefficient matrix
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\eta&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The polynomial is \((X^s,Y^s,Z^s)A(X,Y,Z)^t\). Because its defining ideal is generated by this irreducible degree-\((s+1)\) polynomial, preserving the curve is equivalent to \(T^*AT=\lambda A\), with \(\lambda\in K^\times\). The necessary and sufficient entry relations are
\[
 a^{s+1}=\lambda,\qquad a^sb+d^sc=0,\qquad c^sf=f^sc=\lambda,
\]
\[
 b^{s+1}+\eta c^{s+1}+c^se+e^sc=\lambda\eta.
\]
The middle pair implies \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_s^\times\). Therefore \(b^{s+1}+c^se+e^sc=(\lambda-c^{s+1})\eta\), whose left side lies in \(\mathbb F_s\). Because \(\eta\notin\mathbb F_s\), this forces \(\lambda=c^{s+1}\) and \(b^{s+1}+c^se+e^sc=0\); the previous equality now implies \(f=c\). Divide the matrix by \(c\). Defining \(u=a/c\), \(v=b/c\), and \(h=e/c\), the relations become exactly
\[
 u^{s+1}=1,\qquad d/c=-uv^s,\qquad h+h^s=-v^{s+1}.
\]
Thus the normalized matrix is exactly \(T(u,v,h)\), and substitution shows conversely that every such matrix satisfies \(T^*AT=A\). The normalization by \(c=1\) is unique, making all matrices distinct projective transformations.

**Structure and count.** The norm map \(K^\times\to\mathbb F_s^\times\) has kernel of size \(s+1\). The trace map \(K\to\mathbb F_s\), \(h\mapsto h+h^s\), has kernel of size \(s\), including in characteristic two. Hence each of the \(s+1\) choices of \(u\) and \(s^2\) choices of \(v\) admits exactly \(s\) choices of \(h\). The subgroup \(u=1\) has the displayed Heisenberg multiplication law; the matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(u,1,1)\) split the quotient and act on \((v,h)\) by \((uv,h)\). Their product gives the asserted order and semidirect decomposition.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) with ordinary Python 3. The standalone program performs exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_9,\mathbb F_{25},\mathbb F_{49}\) (prime \(s=3,5,7\)); for each, it enumerates every matrix in the proposed family and checks \(T^*AT=A\), uniqueness, and the single rational point. For \(s=3\) it also exhausts all invertible projective matrices fixing the rational point and tests their polynomial stabilization, obtaining exactly \(108\) transformations. The respective exact family sizes for \(s=3,5,7\) are \(108,750,2744\). These are finite checks: the uniform result for *all prime powers* is proved by the matrix argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Homma and Kim identified and classified the one-rational-point type of Hermitian-relative curves in their publicly available 2026 conference and preprint treatments. Their 2024 work set up congruence of the matrices defining these curves. The projective-equivalence classification of May 2026 gives the natural arithmetic-geometric setting. The full *geometric* Hermitian automorphism group \(\operatorname{PGU}(3,s)\) is classical, whereas the current calculation explicitly identifies the entire subgroup descending to \(K\) for this non-Hermitian form. The resulting arithmetic group law and order are separate invariants, not consequences of the rational-point count alone.

## Limitations
No claim is made about \(s=2\), automorphisms over extension fields, or the integral automorphism group scheme. The classical projectivity theorem for geometric Hermitian automorphisms is used as a prior theorem rather than reproved. The related May 2026 conference chapter was available only as an abstract and publisher preview, so its full treatment of potential stabilizer computations could not be independently ruled out; this is a residual overlap risk.
