# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The plane quartic is smooth because the gradient has no common projective zero. There is exactly one point on the line \(Y=0\), and the affine patch \(Y=1\) is exhaustively enumerated over each of the three finite fields needed for genus three. The standalone verifier establishes irreducibility of all field moduli and independently computes the same affine totals by summing first over \(Z\) and then over \(X\). Newton's identities, the genus-three Weil functional equation, and a separate polynomial expansion reconstruct the exact Weil numerator. Tate-module semisimplicity and the characteristic polynomial modulo \(7\) prove that the rational group has exponent \(7\) and rank three. These are finite exact calculations and established theorems, not extrapolations from samples.

In particular, failed indexing is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The one-point class is canonical, not an arbitrary curve selected for ease of point counting. A single rational-point tally gives no extension-field counts or rational Jacobian structure. The complete Frobenius polynomial yields all extension-field counts and an arithmetic isogeny class, and its exact \(7\)-torsion yields the full rational Jacobian group, a natural structural invariant. The finite specialization is motivated by being the first nonelliptic genus-three case of a new classification; a family-wide theorem is not asserted or required.
