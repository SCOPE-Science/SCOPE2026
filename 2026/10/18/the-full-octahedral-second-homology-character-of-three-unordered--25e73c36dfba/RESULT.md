# The full octahedral second-homology character of three unordered cube particles

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the graph with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^3\), joining words at Hamming distance one, and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_3(|Q_3|)\) be the space of three distinct, unordered points on its geometric realization. The full graph automorphism group is \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)\cong S_4\times C_2\), of order \(48\). The subgroup \(S_4\) is the orientation-preserving cube rotation group, acting on the four body diagonals, and the central \(C_2\) is generated by antipodal inversion. Let \(\varepsilon\) denote its nontrivial rational character. For a partition \(\lambda\) of four, let \(S^\lambda\) denote the corresponding rational Specht representation of \(S_4\).

The integral homology is free and is given by
\[
H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6,\qquad
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{17},\qquad
H_k(X;\mathbb Z)=0\ (k\ge3).
\]
The complete rational second-homology representation is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong{}&
(S^{(3,1)}\oplus S^{(2,2)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1)})\boxtimes\mathbf1\\
&\oplus(S^{(2,2)}\oplus2S^{(2,1,1)}\oplus S^{(1,1,1,1)})\boxtimes\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular antipodal inversion has \(8\) positive and \(9\) negative second-homology eigenvalues. Full rotational invariants vanish, while \(\dim H_2(X;\mathbb Q)^{A_4}=1\), for \(A_4\) inside the rotational \(S_4\). Neither the full rotation group nor the full automorphism group fixes a nonzero rational second-homology class.

## Assumptions and scope
The configuration consists of **three unordered, distinct** particles moving continuously on the geometric graph, without collisions. Edges are genuine intervals, not just pairs of allowed occupancy vertices. Every original cube edge is subdivided once, producing \(20\) vertices and \(24\) edges; this subdivision is equivariant for the entire cube automorphism group. Every path between distinct essential vertices then has at least two edges and every noncontractible graph cycle has at least eight edges. The standard stable-discretization theorem for three particles therefore gives an ordinary homotopy equivalence from this cubical model to the full configuration space. Its inclusion is natural under all cube graph automorphisms, so the induced homology equivalence commutes with the group action.

Cells of degree \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) are unordered triples with precisely \(k\) mutually closure-disjoint open graph edges and \(3-k\) vertices disjoint from all their endpoints. Orient a cubical cell by increasing indices of its moving edges, each edge oriented by its declared endpoint order. Taking an endpoint \(t\in\{0,1\}\) of moving edge number \(j\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) contributes boundary sign \((-1)^{j+1+t}\). A graph automorphism acts on these oriented cubical chains with its ordinary product of edge-direction signs and edge-permutation sign.

## Proof
**Topological model and homology.** Direct exhaustive generation gives the integral cellular chain sizes
\[
(\operatorname{rank}C_0,\operatorname{rank}C_1,\operatorname{rank}C_2,\operatorname{rank}C_3)
=(1140,3672,3840,1296),
\]
so the Euler characteristic is \(12\). A reproducible sequence of \(1296\) free \(2\)-face/\(3\)-cube elementary collapses deletes *every* degree-three cell. At each step its chosen two-face has exactly one remaining three-dimensional coface; no homology inference is made from a partial enumeration. Thus \(H_3(X;\mathbb Z)=0\) and all higher homology vanishes. A spanning tree of the complete 1-skeleton contains \(1139\) edges, proving connectedness.

Ko–Park's integral first-homology classification applies because the cube is a simple, three-connected, planar graph. The graph cycle rank is \(12-8+1=5\); for three unordered particles the additional planar exchange class contributes one free summand, giving \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\). In particular this group has no torsion. The Euler characteristic then forces second Betti number \(17\). As the kernel of an integral homomorphism between free cellular chain groups, \(H_2\) is torsion-free once \(H_3=0\), hence it is \(\mathbb Z^{17}\). As an independent finite check, the boundary relations in the \(2533\) fundamental-cycle coordinates of the 1-skeleton have rank \(2527\) both modulo \(2\) and modulo \(101\), leaving first Betti number \(6\).

**Equivariant character.** The \(48\) graph automorphisms are exactly coordinate permutations and independent coordinate flips. They act on all four cubical chain modules. For each automorphism \(g\), direct signed enumeration of *all* fixed oriented cells determines
\[
L(g)=\sum_{k=0}^3(-1)^k\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_k(X;\mathbb Q)).
\]
The trace of \(g\) on \(H_1\) is obtained independently by integral fundamental loops of the cubical 1-skeleton, projected to six nonpivot classes modulo \(101\). As \(g\) has finite order and \(\dim H_1=6\), its rational trace is an integer in \([-6,6]\), uniquely determined by its modulo-\(101\) trace. For all \(48\) group elements, these six-dimensional calculations also agree with the identity
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1(X;\mathbb Q))
=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1(Q_3;\mathbb Q))+\det(g),
\]
where \(\det(g)\) is the geometric orientation sign of the cube symmetry. With \(H_0\) the trivial representation and \(H_{\ge3}=0\), the Lefschetz identity gives \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2)=L(g)-1+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)\), without assuming any basis for second homology.

Here is the *complete* second-homology character. Columns distinguish antipodal-even and antipodal-odd group elements; rows are cycle types of the orientation-preserving \(S_4\)-component:

| \(S_4\) type | Central even | Central odd |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((1,1,1,1)\) | 17 | -1 |
| \((2,1,1)\) | -3 | 3 |
| \((2,2)\) | 1 | -1 |
| \((3,1)\) | -1 | -1 |
| \((4)\) | 1 | -1 |

Exact inner products against the five ordinary \(S_4\) irreducible characters, each with the two central parities, give the displayed decomposition; their weighted dimensions sum to \(17\). The absent \(S^{(4)}\) constituents imply no invariant line under the full rotation group. The one sign constituent \(S^{(1,1,1,1)}\boxtimes\varepsilon\) is trivial on rotational \(A_4\), giving its single invariant line.

## Verification
Run the standalone file `verify.py` with Python \(\ge3.10\). It constructs the cubical model from scratch and uses only Python's standard library. Its checks include every integral relation \(\partial^2=0\) in degrees two and three, all \(1296\) actual free-top-cell collapses, connectedness, exact finite-field elimination at primes \(2\) and \(101\), construction and closure of six fundamental cycles, all \(48\) automorphism actions on first homology, full signed Lefschetz traces, all ten conjugacy-class values, and all ten irreducible multiplicities. The verifier prints `VERIFICATION SUCCESS` on completion. The independent *mathematical premise* concerning first integral homology is attributed to Ko–Park, not inferred from modular ranks alone.

## Relationship to prior work
Farley–Sabalka introduced a general discrete-Morse model and presentation methods for graph braid groups. Prue–Scrimshaw improved the subdivision threshold that legitimizes the 20-vertex cubical model used here. Ko–Park's general classification determines \(H_1\) for simple three-connected planar graphs, including this cube; that first-homology result is **not** claimed as new. An–Knudsen prove that second homology of planar graph configuration spaces is generated by toric and theta classes, while explicitly leaving combinatorial bases and relations as a general question. Their theorem neither supplies the numerical second Betti number in this particular unstable three-particle case nor its full 48-element homology character. The distinct result here is the exact natural 17-dimensional equivariant decomposition, supported by a complete cubical computation and a full-group character check.

The cubical graph is also called \(Q_3\), the square prism, or \(C_4\square K_2\). Searches for these aliases, the three-strand graph braid group, the rank \(17\), and full octahedral representation found no prior statement of this exact representation. Indexed results on *two* particles on the cube, three particles on the Heawood or Pappus graphs, or different simplicial complexes of the cube do not logically determine this representation. Nonindexed equivalent computations remain possible.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite-object computation with ordinary integral homology and a *rational* \(G\)-representation in degree two. It does **not** classify the integral \(\mathbb Z[G]\)-lattice on \(H_2\), provide geometric generators of all 17 second-homology classes, determine the cohomology ring, or establish a formula for more particles or other prism graphs. The construction of a free-face elimination is a complete finite proof only for the cubical model specified. The general results used from the literature and all finite calculations are individually identified; independent external audit has not been performed.
