# The full \(S_3\times S_4\)-module on three-particle \(K_{3,4}\) configuration homology

## Finding

Let \(G=K_{3,4}\), and let \(B_3(G)\) denote unordered configurations of three **distinct** points on its geometric realization. Its full graph automorphism group is \(W=S_3\times S_4\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), write \(\varepsilon_3\) for the sign and \(U_3\) for the two-dimensional standard representations of \(S_3\). Denote the rational \(S_4\)-representations \(S^{(3,1)}\), \(S^{(2,1,1)}\), \(S^{(2,2)}\), and \(S^{(1,1,1,1)}\) by \(V_4\), \(V'_4\), \(M_4\), and \(\varepsilon_4\), respectively. Then the complete degree-two rational homology representation is

\[
H_2(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\cong_{\mathbb Q[W]}
(\varepsilon_3\boxtimes (V_4\oplus V'_4\oplus 2M_4))
\oplus (U_3\boxtimes(\varepsilon_4\oplus V_4\oplus V'_4\oplus M_4)).
\]

It has dimension \(28\), contains seven nonisomorphic irreducible constituents (eight summands counting multiplicity), and has no nonzero \(W\)-fixed vectors. The complete rational Betti vector is \((1,6,28,0)\). The fifteen conjugacy-class traces of the degree-two action are

\[
(28,-2,4,-2,-2\ ;\ -10,0,-2,2,0\ ;\ 1,1,1,-2,1).
\]

The three five-entry blocks correspond to the \(S_3\) cycle types \((1^3)\), \((21)\), and \((3)\), and inside a block the \(S_4\) cycle types are ordered \((1^4)\), \((21^2)\), \((2^2)\), \((31)\), and \((4)\). These are the actual rational homology characters, not only an alternating chain character.

## Assumptions and scope

Use three left vertices \(0,1,2\) and four right vertices \(3,4,5,6\), joined by all twelve possible cross-part edges. The automorphism group acts on the underlying graph, not on labels of the indistinguishable particles. Every space is the usual collision-free topological configuration space. All module statements concern rational coefficients.

We use the functorial *reduced Świątkowski complex*, with one degree-zero weight-one variable for each graph edge and, at a vertex \(v\) with \(d_v\) incident halfedges, the degree-one weight-one local module of rank \(d_v-1\) spanned by differences \(h_{v,j}-h_{v,0}\). The differential is \(\partial(h_{v,j}-h_{v,0})=e_{v,j}-e_{v,0}\), extended with Koszul signs to tensor products at distinct vertices. Its particle-weight-three subcomplex calculates the homology of \(B_3(G)\) [2].

## Proof

**Betti numbers without equating modular and rational ranks.** An exact basis enumeration gives the weight-three chain-group dimensions \((364,1326,1476,491)\) in degrees zero through three. Integer boundary columns are generated directly from the local halfedge-difference differential. The rank triples computed by exact elimination are \((363,956,491)\) modulo \(2\), and \((363,957,491)\) modulo each of \(3\) and \(101\). Each finite-field rank is a lower bound for the corresponding rational rank.

Connectivity implies \(\beta_0=1\), so the first rational boundary rank equals \(363\). The degree-three boundary has full rank \(491\), so \(\beta_3=0\). The degree-two differential has rank at least \(957\); thus \(\beta_1\le1326-363-957=6\).

The total-motion homomorphism from the first homology of unordered configurations to \(H_1(G;\mathbb Q)\) is surjective. The graph cycle space has dimension \(12-7+1=6\) and is generated by six four-cycles. For each such square, choose an edge joining vertices outside that square (at least one left vertex and two right vertices remain); park two particles at distinct interior points of that edge and transport the third particle around the square. These loops map to the six cycle-space generators. Therefore \(\beta_1\ge6\). Consequently the rational boundary ranks are exactly \((363,957,491)\), giving \(\beta_1=6\) and \(\beta_2=1476-957-491=28\).

The total-motion map is natural under all graph automorphisms, so the equal dimensions imply \(H_1(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\cong_W H_1(G;\mathbb Q)\). Orient each edge from the three-vertex part to the four-vertex part. Every \(g\in W\) preserves this orientation, and the graph cycle character is

\[
\chi_1(g)=\#\operatorname{Fix}_E(g)-\#\operatorname{Fix}_V(g)+1.
\]

**Equivariant chain traces.** The action on a local difference sends it to the corresponding difference of halfedges at its image vertex; ordered degree-one factors acquire the usual Koszul sign. Equivariance of every boundary is verified on all chain basis vectors for a generating set of nine transpositions of \(S_3\times S_4\); those transpositions generate the group, so the result holds for its 144 elements.

For each \(g\in W\), let \(t_k(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_k)\). The Lefschetz trace identity and the preceding homology calculation give

\[
\chi_2(g)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^k t_k(g)-1+\chi_1(g).
\]

The standalone checker enumerates all 144 automorphisms and their four signed chain traces. As an independent combinatorial cross-check, it obtains the alternating chain trace as the coefficient of \(z^3\) in

\[
\frac{\prod_{\mathcal O\in V/\langle g\rangle}(1-z^{|\mathcal O|}\operatorname{tr}(g^{|\mathcal O|}\mid L_{v_\mathcal O}))}{\prod_{\mathcal P\in E/\langle g\rangle}(1-z^{|\mathcal P|})},
\]

where \(L_{v}\) is the degree-one local augmentation module, and the return-map trace is taken after cycling through its vertex orbit. The two calculations agree elementwise. The resulting character is constant on all fifteen conjugacy classes and has the values displayed above.

**Irreducible decomposition.** The rational irreducibles of \(S_3\times S_4\) are the fifteen external products of the three \(S_3\) Specht modules and the five \(S_4\) Specht modules. Calculating their character inner products over the 144 elements yields multiplicity one for \(\varepsilon_3\boxtimes V_4\), \(\varepsilon_3\boxtimes V'_4\), \(U_3\boxtimes\varepsilon_4\), \(U_3\boxtimes V_4\), \(U_3\boxtimes V'_4\), and \(U_3\boxtimes M_4\); multiplicity two for \(\varepsilon_3\boxtimes M_4\); and zero for the eight other types. Semisimplicity of rational representations of finite groups proves the asserted isomorphism and vanishing invariants.

## Relationship to prior work

Abrams [1] introduced foundational graph configuration-space methods. The reduced Świątkowski model [2] supplies the chain complex. Maciążek–Sawicki [3, §5.5] computed second homology for **the smaller** \(K_{3,3}\) graph, including its eight independent cycles for three particles; that graph and the group \(S_3\wr C_2\) do not determine the full \(S_3\times S_4\) character for \(K_{3,4}\). Edge stabilization [4] establishes general module properties, rather than this explicit representation. Ramos–He Yun [5] compute three-particle stable multiplicities for complete graphs but not the present unequal bipartite graph. Theorem 5.2 of [6] determines top-degree \(H_m(B_m(K_{a,b});\mathbb Q)\), which at three particles concerns degree three, not this degree-two module. No located exact-character table or stronger theorem supplied the fifteen traces above. Ordinary Morse-matching complexes with similar graph names are different objects.

## Limitations

Only rational, unlabeled-three-particle homology on this particular graph is classified. There is no assertion of integral torsion-freeness, integral module splitting, or an invariant geometric basis. Modular computations do not alone establish characteristic-zero ranks. Less-indexed literature might contain the same symmetry calculation; the statement is not independently audited.
