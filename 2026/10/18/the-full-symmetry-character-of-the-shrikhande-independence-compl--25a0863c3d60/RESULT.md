# The full symmetry character of the Shrikhande independence complex

## Finding
Let \(S\) be the Shrikhande graph and \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(S)\). Its full automorphism group is \(G=T\rtimes D\), where \(T=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\) acts by translations and \(D\cong D_{12}\) is the dihedral group of order twelve fixing the origin. The only nonzero reduced rational homology is \(V=\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\), of dimension \(23\).

**Full representation theorem.** Write \(\widehat T\cong(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\) by \(\lambda=(a,b)\mapsto\vartheta_\lambda(u,v)=i^{au+bv}\), and let \(D\) act on these labels by transpose matrices modulo four. There are four dual orbits, with representatives, orbit sizes and multiplicities in \(V_\mathbb C\):

| Representative \(\lambda\) | Orbit size | Multiplicity | Little-group representation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| \((0,0)\) | 1 | 2 | \(\alpha\oplus\beta\) of \(D\), defined below |
| \((0,1)\) | 6 | 1 | nontrivial sign of its order-two stabilizer |
| \((0,2)\) | 3 | 1 | character \(\tau\) of its order-four stabilizer, defined below |
| \((1,1)\) | 6 | 2 | twice the nontrivial sign of its order-two stabilizer |

Put
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\3&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(D=\langle R,F\mid R^6=F^2=1,\ FRF=R^{-1}\rangle\). Define the one-dimensional characters by \(\alpha(R)=1,\ \alpha(F)=-1\) and \(\beta(R)=\beta(F)=-1\). The stabilizer for \((0,1)\) is generated by \(K=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\), on which its character is \(-1\). The stabilizer for \((0,2)\) is \(\{I,\ A,\ B,\ K\}\), where \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\0&3\end{pmatrix}\), \(B=-I\), and \(\tau(I)=\tau(K)=1,\ \tau(A)=\tau(B)=-1\). The stabilizer for \((1,1)\) is \(\{I,F\}\); its multiplicity space is two copies of the sign character with \(F\) acting by \(-1\).

In particular the complete complex irreducible decomposition under all \(192\) automorphisms consists of **two distinct one-dimensional constituents, one three-dimensional constituent, one six-dimensional constituent of multiplicity one, and one other six-dimensional constituent of multiplicity two**. They have total dimension \(1+1+3+6+12=23\). Each induced constituent attached to a nonzero dual orbit is irreducible by the finite little-group theorem, and inequivalent dual orbits give inequivalent constituents.

The translation-fixed subspace has dimension \(\dim V^T=2\), but \(\dim V^G=0\). The full character takes values with the following exact multiplicities across the \(192\) elements:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
\text{trace}&-7&-3&-1&0&1&2&3&23\\
\text{count}&12&4&69&32&24&32&18&1
\end{array}.
\]
The explicit value at every affine transformation is additionally specified in `CHARACTER_TABLE.txt` (row \(M\in D\), column \(t\in T\), transformation \(x\mapsto Mx+t\)). The irreducible decomposition above determines the same full character without a table.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(S\) are \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\), listed in lexicographic order. Two distinct vertices are joined precisely when their difference lies in
\[
\{(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\}\pmod4.
\]
The independence complex consists of all independent vertex subsets. Every homology group and character in the principal theorem uses rational coefficients unless explicitly indicated otherwise; the complexification is \(V_\mathbb C=V\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb C\). The imaginary unit \(i\) satisfies \(i^2=-1\). The term “irreducible” in the decomposition refers to complex representations; no integral module decomposition is asserted.

## Proof
**Full automorphism group.** The twelve invertible matrices over \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) carrying the six Cayley differences onto themselves form \(D\). Together with the sixteen translations they give \(192\) distinct graph automorphisms. The induced graph on the six neighbors of the origin is a six-cycle. Any origin-fixing graph automorphism acts on this cycle, so has at most twelve possibilities if its action on these neighbors is faithful. Faithfulness holds because each of the other nine vertices has a distinct set of two neighbors within this six-cycle. Thus the constructed affine group is the *full* automorphism group.

**Homology concentration without a guessed rank.** An exhaustive enumeration of the \(2^{16}\) vertex masks gives the face numbers \(f_0=16,f_1=72,f_2=96,f_3=16\), and no higher-dimensional faces. The one-skeleton is connected, giving rank \(15\) for its rational first boundary. The full mod-two boundary matrices have ranks \(57\) in degree two and \(16\) in degree three. A matrix with rank \(r\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) has rank at least \(r\) over \(\mathbb Q\). On the other hand \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) forces rational rank \(\partial_2\le72-15=57\), while \(\partial_3\) has only sixteen columns. Hence the rational ranks are **exactly** \(57\) and \(16\). Consequently all reduced rational homology except degree two vanishes and
\[
\dim_\mathbb Q\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)=96-57-16=23.
\]
The result concerns rational homology; the modular calculation alone is not used to rule out odd-primary torsion in integral homology.

**Exact equivariant Lefschetz calculation.** For a graph automorphism \(g\), let \(\sigma\) run through all independent vertex subsets and set the signed fixed-face sum
\[
L(g)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in X\\g\sigma=\sigma}}(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma).
\]
The initial \(-1\) is the augmented empty face. Since the only reduced rational homology is in even degree two, \(L(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|V)\). A second, independent computation decomposes the vertex permutation of \(g\) into cycles, discards cycles that contain an internal graph edge, and forms the graph on remaining cycles with an edge whenever the two vertex orbits are not jointly independent. Then
\[
L(g)=-\sum_{\substack{A\text{ is a compatible}\\\text{collection of vertex cycles}}}(-1)^{|A|}.
\]
Both evaluations agree for **every one of the \(192\) automorphisms**, producing the full character table included here. The equality follows because an invariant independent simplex is a union of vertex cycles and its oriented fixed-face sign simplifies to \((-1)^{|A|+1}\).

**Fourier decomposition and irreducibility.** For each character \(\vartheta_\lambda\) of the normal abelian subgroup \(T\), Fourier projection yields its multiplicity \(m_\lambda\) in \(V_\mathbb C\):
\[
m_\lambda=\frac1{16}\sum_{t\in T}\overline{\vartheta_\lambda(t)}L(t).
\]
For \(M\in D\) stabilizing \(\lambda\), the same projector gives the character of the little-group multiplicity space:
\[
\psi_\lambda(M)=\frac1{16}\sum_{t\in T}\overline{\vartheta_\lambda(t)}L((M,t)).
\]
These are exact sums of Gaussian integers; all evaluated values have zero imaginary part and integer real part. Their four nonzero orbit types, multiplicities and stabilizer characters are precisely those displayed in the finding. For \(\lambda=(0,0)\), \(\psi_0(R^j)=1+(-1)^j\) and \(\psi_0(FR^j)=-1-(-1)^j\), the character of \(\alpha\oplus\beta\). The three other little groups give the one-dimensional sign, the specified \(\tau\), and twice sign, respectively. The normal-abelian-subgroup little-group theorem therefore gives the asserted complete irreducible decomposition. Summing the character over \(G\) gives zero; summing over \(T\) gives \(32\), proving the two invariant-dimension claims.

## Verification
`verify_shrikhande_equivariant.py` is self-contained standard-library Python 3. Executing `python3 verify_shrikhande_equivariant.py` generates the full face set, checks the graph and full affine group, computes the two finite boundary ranks, evaluates the signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz trace and independent vertex-orbit formula for all \(192\) elements, performs exact Fourier transforms, and asserts the four little-group characters. It prints `SHRIKHANDE_EQUIVARIANT_VERIFY_OK` only after all assertions succeed. The text table in `CHARACTER_TABLE.txt` is obtained from its `CHARACTER_ROWS` output. The proof uses exhaustive finite computations and exact algebra; it does not extrapolate from samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak's 2010 paper establishes foundational independence-complex topology but does not compute this graph's equivariant homology. A separately published calculation, *Independence complexes of the Shrikhande and \(4\times4\) rook graphs have different sphere-wedge homotopy types*, establishes \(\operatorname{Ind}(S)\simeq\bigvee^{23}S^2\) and the corresponding fifteen-sphere rook comparison. That **nonequivariant** result is taken as prior work rather than asserted new here. Its non-equivariant Morse reductions do not imply the specific action of any nonidentity automorphism on homology. Research on the *clique complex of the original Shrikhande graph* concerns \(\operatorname{Cl}(S)\), rather than the present \(\operatorname{Ind}(S)=\operatorname{Cl}(\overline S)\). The new claim is the evaluated full-group character and normal-abelian-subgroup irreducible decomposition.

## Limitations
The computation treats this canonical sixteen-vertex graph alone, not all strongly regular graphs or other Cayley graphs. It determines the rational representation after complexification, not an integral equivariant homotopy type or an integral irreducible lattice splitting. The literature search cannot exclude equivalent calculations in unindexed sources. Independent external validation is not claimed.
