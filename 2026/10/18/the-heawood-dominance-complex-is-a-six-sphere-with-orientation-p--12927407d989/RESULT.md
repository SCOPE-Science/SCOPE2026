# The Heawood dominance complex is a six-sphere with orientation-preserving full symmetry

## Finding
Let \(H\) denote the fourteen-vertex point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane, with points \(p_i\) and lines \(\ell_j\) indexed by \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\). Point \(p_i\) is adjacent to line \(\ell_j\) exactly when \(i-j\in\{0,1,3\}\). Define the dominance complex \(\mathcal D(H)\) to consist of all subsets of vertices whose complements dominate \(H\), where a set dominates a vertex if it contains that vertex or one of its neighbors.

**Theorem.** There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal D(H)\simeq S^6.
\]
The full graph automorphism group has order \(336\), and every automorphism acts as the identity on \(\widetilde H_6(\mathcal D(H);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Hence
\[
\widetilde H_k(|\mathcal D(H)|/\operatorname{Aut}(H);\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Q,&k=6,\\0,&k\ne6.\end{cases}
\]
The vertex-cover connectivity bound \(\operatorname{conn}_{\mathbb F_2}\mathcal D(H)+2\le\tau(H)\) is attained here: both sides equal \(7\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, undirected, simple, and have no loops. The graph \(H\) has fourteen vertices and twenty-one edges. We take **closed** neighborhoods \(N_H[v]=\{v\}\cup\{w:vw\in E(H)\}\). A subset \(S\) is a simplex of \(\mathcal D(H)\) exactly when it contains no entire closed neighborhood. Integral homotopy refers to underlying geometric realizations. All group actions are induced by simplicial automorphisms; the asserted quotient homology is only over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive finite face construction.** Represent a subset of the fourteen ordered vertices \((p_0,\ldots,p_6,\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_6)\) by its fourteen-bit incidence mask. For each mask \(s\), test all fourteen closed-neighborhood masks \(c_v\) and retain exactly those satisfying \(s\cap c_v\ne c_v\). This is equivalent, without approximation, to domination by the complement. Of the \(2^{14}=16{,}384\) masks, \(8{,}851\) are faces including the empty face. The nonempty face counts in dimensions \(0\) through \(9\) are
\[
(14,91,364,987,1862,2394,1962,931,224,21).
\]

**2. An explicit complete acyclic matching.** Initially regard every face, including the empty face, as unmatched. For each point-vertex pivot \(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_6\) in that order, pair every still-unmatched face not containing the pivot with its one-vertex extension, if both remain unmatched. Choose pairs in increasing bitmask order. The seven passes yield respectively \((3737,471,145,35,21,9,7)\) pairs. The pair \((\varnothing,\{p_0\})\) is discarded to obtain an ordinary matching of the nonempty face poset. It has \(4{,}424\) disjoint pairs, and precisely two unmatched nonempty faces: the vertex \(\{p_0\}\) and the six-simplex \(\{\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_6\}\).

For each cover relation \(\sigma\subset\tau\) of the nonempty face poset, orient the edge from the larger to the smaller face, except reverse the direction for the matched cover relations. The supplied checker constructs **every** such edge, obtaining \(52{,}304\) directed cover relations, and performs a complete Kahn topological sort over all \(8{,}850\) nonempty faces. The sort visits all \(8{,}850\) faces; hence there is no directed cycle. These arithmetic certificates are reproducible by `verify_morse.py`, with explicit construction and exhaustive checks rather than assumed logs. Forman's acyclic matching theorem yields a CW model with a single zero-cell and a single six-cell. Its attaching map is from \(S^5\) into a point and is null-homotopic. Thus \(\mathcal D(H)\simeq S^6\). In particular the integral sixth homology is a single free copy of \(\mathbb Z\), and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes.

**3. Exhaustive full-symmetry generation.** The Fano incidence structure admits exactly \(168\) point-class-preserving graph automorphisms: the checker exhausts all \(7!=5040\) point permutations, retaining precisely those preserving the seven line triples. These are generated by point translation \(i\mapsto i+1\), multiplication \(i\mapsto2i\), and the point involution \((0,1,4,3,2,6,5)\). Extending these point permutations to line permutations gives graph automorphisms. The polarity \(p_i\mapsto\ell_{-i}\), \(\ell_j\mapsto p_{-j}\) interchanges the bipartition classes; the checker confirms it preserves every adjacency. As the graph is connected and bipartite, its complete automorphism group has order \(2\cdot168=336\), generated by these four maps.

**4. Integral orientation character.** For any simplicial automorphism \(g\), the ordinary Lefschetz number is the alternating signed count of nonempty setwise invariant simplices. If an invariant simplex is a union of \(r\) vertex cycles of \(g\), its signed contribution is \((-1)^{r-1}\): the sign of the vertex permutation on it is \((-1)^{\sum(|C|-1)}\), while the dimension parity is \((-1)^{\sum|C|-1}\). Enumerating all unions of vertex cycles which are valid faces gives Lefschetz number **two** for each of the four stated group generators. The invariant-cycle face-count arrays and calculations appear in `verify_morse.py` and its output.

Only \(H_0\cong\mathbb Q\) and \(H_6\cong\mathbb Q\) survive. The Lefschetz identity therefore reads \(L(g)=1+\operatorname{tr}(g|H_6)\). Since \(L(g)=2\), each generator has sixth-homology trace \(+1\); on its rank-one integral homology this must be multiplication by \(+1\), not \(-1\). Every group element has the same action because the generators generate the entire group.

**5. Rational quotient and sharpness.** The finite simplicial action can be barycentrically subdivided to remove simplex inversions. Rational chains of the quotient compute the rational coinvariants of chains; averaging over a finite group is exact over \(\mathbb Q\). Therefore the rational homology of the orbit space is the invariant (equivalently coinvariant) part of the rational homology of \(\mathcal D(H)\), giving the asserted single reduced class in degree six. Finally, the seven point vertices form a vertex cover. The seven incidence edges of any perfect point–line matching are pairwise disjoint, so every vertex cover has size at least seven. The existence of a perfect matching follows from the regular bipartite graph and Hall's theorem. Thus \(\tau(H)=7\), while \(S^6\) has mod-two homological connectivity \(5\), making the previously published bound sharp in this nonchordal example.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_morse.py` and `python3 verify_independent.py` in a directory containing those supplied standalone Python files. They require no third-party packages. The first constructs every face and every matched Hasse arrow, verifies acyclicity, enumerates all point-class-preserving automorphisms and the four generating actions, and prints their Lefschetz values. The second independently relabels the Fano plane and checks every mod-two simplicial boundary rank: \((13,78,286,701,1161,1233,728,203,21)\). The resulting reduced mod-two Betti vector has a single \(1\) in degree six. Reproduction outputs are included as `verify_morse_output.txt` and `verify_independent_output.txt`; the code, not the log text alone, is the primary finite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita and Wakatsuki establish the general identity between the dominance complex of a graph and the combinatorial Alexander dual of the **open-neighborhood** complex of its **complement**. Their graph-family calculations and chordal-graph sphere theorem do not give this Fano-incidence example: the Heawood graph is nonchordal, and is not among their paths, cycle, ladder, or grid families. The Alexander duality identity itself is not claimed as new. Matsushita's earlier general vertex-cover inequality supplies the inequality shown sharp here; it does not identify the homotopy type of \(\mathcal D(H)\). Goldberg, Rajendraprasad, and Mathew study domination in projective designs and explicitly describe the Fano incidence graph, but do not compute its dominance-complex homotopy type. The ordinary Heawood **independence complex** and its symmetry action, as well as the ordinary matching and cut complexes, are different simplicial constructions and their stated classifications do not imply this one.

## Limitations
The result concerns this one finite projective plane of order two; no all-order classification is asserted. Although the orbit space has the stated **rational homology**, neither an integral homotopy type nor torsion classification for the orbit space is claimed. A literature search cannot rule out unindexed equivalent results. The proof and computations have not received an independent mathematical audit.
