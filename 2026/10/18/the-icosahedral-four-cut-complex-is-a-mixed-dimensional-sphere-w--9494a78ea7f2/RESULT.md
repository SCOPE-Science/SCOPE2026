# The icosahedral four-cut complex is a mixed-dimensional sphere wedge

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the graph of the regular icosahedron. For the ordinary four-cut simplicial complex of Bayer et al.,
\[
\Delta_4(I)\simeq\bigvee^{5}S^{6}\vee\bigvee^{20}S^{7}.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde H_6(\Delta_4(I);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5\), \(\widetilde H_7(\Delta_4(I);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{20}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. The pure seven-dimensional complex is not shellable because it has nonzero homology below the top dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
The icosahedral graph is represented on vertices \(\{0,1,\ldots,11\}\). For \(j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), put \(a_j=1+j\) and \(b_j=6+j\); subscripts are read modulo five. Its thirty undirected edges are \(\{0,a_j\}\), \(\{11,b_j\}\), \(\{a_j,a_{j+1}\}\), \(\{b_j,b_{j+1}\}\), \(\{a_j,b_j\}\), and \(\{a_j,b_{j-1}\}\) for all five values of \(j\). This is the usual two-ring description of the icosahedron, and every vertex has degree five.

The ordinary \(k\)-cut complex of a finite graph \(G\) is the simplicial complex generated by complements \(V(G)\setminus T\) of \(k\)-element subsets \(T\subseteq V(G)\) such that the induced subgraph \(G[T]\) is disconnected. Only \(k=4\) and the explicit graph \(I\) are considered; no general claim for all graphs or other thresholds is made.

## Proof
**Finite combinatorics.** Enumerate the \(\binom{12}{4}=495\) four-element vertex sets in lexicographic order and test connectivity by breadth-first search on the displayed thirty edges. Exactly 285 four-sets are disconnected and therefore give distinct eight-vertex facets. Enumerating all nonempty subfaces of the 285 facets yields face counts, in dimensions zero through seven,
\[
(12,66,220,495,792,912,720,285).
\]
This exhaustively specifies a finite complex of 3502 nonempty faces.

**Explicit discrete-Morse matching.** Encode a nonempty face as a twelve-bit integer and process vertices in the order
`[10,1,6,0,11,9,2,4,7,3,8,5]`. At each vertex \(v\), greedily pair every still-unmatched face \(F\) not containing \(v\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\), provided that both faces remain unmatched; process candidate faces by increasing integer mask. This defines disjoint elementary incidences. Orient matched incidences from the smaller to the larger simplex and all remaining cover incidences from the larger to the smaller simplex. The packaged verifier explicitly constructs the complete directed Hasse graph and performs a topological sort, proving it is acyclic. The critical cells are exactly one vertex, six six-simplices, and twenty-one seven-simplices, with no critical cells in intervening dimensions. Forman's theorem gives a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with those cells.

**Integral attaching matrix.** Orient each simplex by increasing vertex label and write its integral boundary with alternating signs. For every matched pair \(L\subset U\), with boundary coefficient \(\epsilon=[\partial U:L]\in\{\pm1\}\), algebraically eliminate \(L,U\) using the substitution \(L\mapsto-\epsilon\sum_{F\ne L}[\partial U:F]F\), and eliminate upper matched cells by zero projection. The recursive integral projection is encoded directly in `verify_icosa_4cut.py`. Applied to boundaries of all twenty-one critical seven-cells, it produces a six-by-twenty-one integer matrix whose only nonzero entries are four coefficients of absolute value one, all in the same column. Its integral Smith normal form is
\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0,0,0)
\]
(as a rectangular six-by-twenty-one matrix). Acyclicity ensures that these eliminations calculate the actual Morse cellular boundary, rather than merely numerical homology.

**Homotopy conclusion.** With no cells in dimensions one through five, the six-skeleton is a wedge of six six-spheres. Its sixth homotopy group is its sixth homology, \(\mathbb Z^6\), by Hurewicz. Thus each seven-cell attaching map is determined by its column of integral degrees. A primitive rank-one matrix can be reduced by integral changes of bases to a single unit entry. Geometrically these changes are induced by homotopy self-equivalences of the wedge of six-spheres and handle slides of the seven-cells. Cancel the unit-attached six/seven pair; the remaining cells attach trivially. Therefore the space is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of five six-spheres and twenty seven-spheres, as claimed. The six-dimensional reduced homology is nonzero below the complex's pure dimension seven, excluding shellability.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_icosa_4cut.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. It reconstructs the graph and all faces, verifies acyclicity of the *entire* gradient Hasse graph, computes the exact integer Morse boundary, and checks its Smith normal form. Run `python3 independent_face_count.py` using only the standard Python library: it builds the graph afresh, checks all disconnected four-subsets and all faces by explicit subset enumeration, and checks reduced Euler characteristic. Both scripts are included as complete standalone files; `verification_output.txt` records their actual stdout. The computations are finite and exhaustive for the specified twelve-vertex graph, not a claim about arbitrary graph families.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675, publicly submitted 2023-04-26), define the ordinary cut complex and classify several other graph families, but do not give this icosahedral four-cut homotopy type. Their sequel *Topology of Cut Complexes II* (arXiv:2407.08158) treats disjoint unions, squared paths, grids, and symmetry actions on other families. A separately catalogued result concerns the same icosahedral graph at threshold three, yielding a different complex; its homotopy type does not determine the threshold-four complex. Other reported four-cut results concern other graphs. The present explicit mixed-dimensional integral homotopy equivalence is not implied by those statements.

## Limitations
Only this fixed graph and threshold are treated. The proof uses exact finite enumeration, integral Morse reduction, and standard CW homotopy theory; it does not produce a uniform family theorem, a group action, or a natural map between cut thresholds. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed, and undiscovered equivalent work remains possible.
