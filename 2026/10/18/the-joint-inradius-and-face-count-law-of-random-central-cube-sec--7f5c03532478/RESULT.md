# The joint inradius and face-count law of random central cube sections

## Finding

Let \(Q=[-1,1]^3\) be the three-dimensional Euclidean cube, and let \(U\) be uniformly distributed on \(S^2\). Form the central polygonal section \(P_U=Q\cap U^\perp\), with number of edges \(N(U)\in\{4,6\}\) almost surely and Euclidean inradius \(R(U)\). Define the strictly increasing coordinate
\[
Z=\sqrt{1-R^{-2}},\quad z_0=\frac1{\sqrt6},\quad z_1=\frac1{\sqrt3},
\]
\[
\theta(z)=\arcsin\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-z^2}},\qquad
\phi(z)=\arcsin\frac{z}{\sqrt{2(1-z^2)}}.
\]
**Theorem (complete joint law and topology threshold).** The distribution of \((Z,N)\) is absolutely continuous in its first coordinate and has joint densities
\[
f_{Z,4}(z)=\begin{cases}
\frac{12}{\pi}\bigl(\frac\pi4-\theta(z)-\phi(z)\bigr),&0<z<z_0,\
0,&z_0<z<z_1,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
f_{Z,6}(z)=\begin{cases}
\frac{12}{\pi}\phi(z),&0<z<z_0,\
\frac{12}{\pi}\bigl(\frac\pi4-\theta(z)\bigr),&z_0<z<z_1.
\end{cases}
\]
Both densities vanish outside \([0,z_1]\); endpoints and the transition are atomless. Consequently
\[
\mathbb P(N=6\mid Z=z)=\begin{cases}
\frac{\phi(z)}{\pi/4-\theta(z)},&0<z<z_0,\
1,&z_0\le z<z_1.
\end{cases}
\]
This conditional probability strictly increases from \(0\) to \(1\) on \((0,z_0)\). **Every quadrilateral central section has inradius at most \(\sqrt{6/5}\)**; the constant is sharp, and all sections with strictly larger inradius are hexagons. Both four- and six-sided sections occur with positive conditional probability for each \(1<R<\sqrt{6/5}\). Furthermore, \(N=6\) is positively associated with every nonconstant increasing measurable function of \(R\) with finite expectation and nonconstant almost surely under the inradius law.

## Assumptions and scope

The plane distribution is Haar: the unoriented plane \(U^\perp\) is generated by a uniformly distributed unit normal \(U\), not by a random affine offset. A generic section is a centrally symmetric quadrilateral or hexagon. The inradius refers to the largest Euclidean disk anywhere within \(U^\perp\) contained in the section; it is not its circumradius or its area-equivalent radius. Because the section is centrally symmetric, an inball can always be chosen centered at the origin. The densities in the theorem are with respect to Lebesgue measure \(dz\); for \(1<r<\sqrt{3/2}\), the corresponding joint density in the inradius coordinate is
\[
f_{R,k}(r)=\frac{f_{Z,k}(\sqrt{1-r^{-2}})}{r^3\sqrt{1-r^{-2}}},\qquad k\in\{4,6\}.
\]
The value of this formula at boundary points is irrelevant. All angle functions use principal \(\arcsin\) values in \([0,\pi/2]\).

## Proof

**1. The section inradius is the smallest-normal-coordinate statistic.** Write \(U=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\). The section is the intersection in \(E=U^\perp\) of the six half-planes \(|x_i|\le1\). Each supporting line has distance \(\|P_E e_i\|^{-1}=(1-u_i^2)^{-1/2}\) from the origin whenever \(u_i^2<1\). A centered disk fits in the section if and only if it fits in all six half-planes, so its maximal radius is the smallest of these distances. If a disk centered at \(v\in E\) of radius \(r\) lies in a centrally symmetric convex polygon, so does the disk centered at \(-v\); convexity implies the disk centered at zero with the same radius also lies inside. Therefore the centered maximum is the true inradius, and
\[
R(U)=\frac1{\sqrt{1-c^2}},\qquad c=\min_i|u_i|.
\]
In particular \(Z=c\in[0,1/\sqrt3]\) and \(R\in[1,\sqrt{3/2}]\).

**2. The face-count criterion.** Up to spherical null sets, the section meets the relative interior of both faces \(x_i=\pm1\) exactly when \(|u_i|<\sum_{j\ne i}|u_j|\). For example, in the face \(x_1=1\), the equation \(u_1+u_2x_2+u_3x_3=0\) admits \(|x_2|,|x_3|<1\) precisely when \(|u_1|<|u_2|+|u_3|\). With the absolute normal coordinates ordered \(a\ge b\ge c\), the three strict inequalities are equivalent to \(a<b+c\). The central section has six edges exactly in this case, and four edges otherwise.

**3. Direct parametrization of all Haar directions.** Divide the sphere into its \(48\) sign-and-order sectors. On a representative sector we may set
\[
U=(\sqrt{1-z^2}\cos t,\sqrt{1-z^2}\sin t,z),
\]
\[
0\le z\le z_1,\qquad \theta(z)\le t\le\frac\pi4.
\]
Indeed \(b\ge c\) is \(\sin t\ge z/\sqrt{1-z^2}\), and \(a\ge b\) gives \(t\le\pi/4\). The unit-sphere area element in these coordinates is exactly \(dz\,dt\), and the \(48\) congruent sectors together imply a probability density factor \(48/(4\pi)=12/\pi\) per unit \(dz\,dt\).

The hexagon condition \(a<b+c\) is equivalent to
\[
\cos t-\sin t<\frac z{\sqrt{1-z^2}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t>\frac\pi4-\phi(z),
\]
using \(\cos t-\sin t=\sqrt2\sin(\pi/4-t)\), where \(0\le\pi/4-t\le\pi/4\). The two lower bounds on \(t\) meet when \(z=z_0=1/\sqrt6\). This follows directly from the boundary \(a=b+c\) together with \(b=c\): on the unit sphere \(a=2c\) implies \(6c^2=1\). For \(0<z<z_0\), the allowed \(t\)-interval splits into a quadrilateral portion of length \(\pi/4-\theta(z)-\phi(z)\) and a hexagon portion of length \(\phi(z)\). For \(z_0<z<z_1\), the entire interval of length \(\pi/4-\theta(z)\) gives hexagons. Multiplication by \(12/\pi\) proves both densities. All boundaries are finitely many curves of spherical measure zero; no atoms arise.

**4. Strict monotonicity and sharp cutoff.** On \((0,z_0)\), \(\phi(z)\) strictly increases from zero whereas \(\pi/4-\theta(z)\) strictly decreases, remaining positive. Hence their quotient strictly increases to one, and is exactly one afterward. Since \(R=(1-z^2)^{-1/2}\), the transition is \(R(z_0)=\sqrt{6/5}\). Normals immediately below \(z_0\) have positive-length angular subintervals of both polygon types, so no smaller universal quadrilateral cutoff works. Because the conditional hexagon probability is strictly increasing and nonconstant, its covariance with every nonconstant increasing measurable \(g(R)\) of finite expectation is strictly positive: for independent copies \(R,R'\), the covariance equals half the expectation of \((g(R)-g(R'))(p(R)-p(R'))\), which is strictly positive provided \(g\) is nonconstant under the atomless inradius law. This proves the stated positive association. \(\square\)

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 script `verify_joint_cube_section.py` requires only the standard library. It independently clips \(2,500\) central cube sections by enumerating all twelve cube edges, projects the intersection vertices into the cutting plane, computes the convex hull, and extracts the polygon type and Euclidean inradius from its edges. It then checks the exact predicted type and inradius. Simpson quadrature independently verifies normalization and reproduces the known unconditional hexagon probability \(1-(6/\pi)\arcsin(1/3)\) to better than \(10^{-8}\). It also checks strict conditional monotonicity at 99 sample points. Execution prints `VERIFY_OK`. Finite testing and numerical quadrature supplement but do not establish the continuum theorem; the four proof steps above are analytic.

## Relationship to prior work

Lonke [1], in particular Proposition 2.5 and its dimension-three remark, derives expected face counts of central cube sections, which determines the **unconditional** four-versus-six polygon-type probability. The cube's unconditional hexagon probability is also explicitly tabulated in OEIS A349605 [2]. A 2026 exact law for random central sections of rectangular boxes [3] extends that polygon-type marginal to arbitrary positive box aspect ratios; its full proof gives the triangle-inequality chamber but no joint distribution with the Euclidean inradius. The exact inradius distribution for random **projections of the polar octahedron** [4] is, by polarity, an inverse circumradius law for cube sections, not their inradius law; its full theorem and proof concern a different metric invariant and do not furnish this metric–combinatorial joint distribution. Abas [5] develops random spherical width distributions of orthotopes rather than inradii of random central cube sections. The new claim consists of the exact joint densities, sharp metric cutoff, and monotone conditional polygon-type law rather than either known marginal or the elementary inradius expression by itself. The box result's unconditional probability is recovered by integrating the joint hexagon density in the equal-edge case; this is a consistency check, not asserted novelty.

## Limitations

All statements use the centered three-dimensional cube, Euclidean inradius, and Haar-distributed plane normals. They do not extend automatically to noncubic rectangular boxes, translated planes, or different dimensions. Conditional probabilities given an exact radius use the explicitly displayed continuous density version, not point-event conditioning. Numerical testing does not establish novelty; unindexed earlier stereological formulas remain a priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
