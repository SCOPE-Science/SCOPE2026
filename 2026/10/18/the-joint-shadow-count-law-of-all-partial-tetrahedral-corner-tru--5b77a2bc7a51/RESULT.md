# The joint shadow-count law of all partial tetrahedral corner truncations

## Finding
Let \(T\) be any nondegenerate tetrahedron in Euclidean three-space, with vertices \(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\) and interior solid angles \(\Omega_i\) at these vertices. For each subset \(S\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}\), truncate precisely the vertices indexed by \(S\), using cutting planes parallel to their opposite faces. The cuts may have arbitrary, mutually unequal fractions strictly between \(0\) and \(1/2\) along each incident original edge. Call the resulting convex polytope \(T_S\).

Use the *same* uniformly random viewing direction \(U\in S^2\) for all sixteen polytopes, and let \(N_S\) be the number of vertices of the orthogonal planar shadow of \(T_S\). Put
\[
 p_i=\frac{\Omega_i}{2\pi},\qquad p=\sum_{i=0}^3p_i.
\]
Then the **complete joint law** of the vector \((N_S)_{S\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}}\) has precisely five atoms, described as follows. With probability \(1-p\), simultaneously for **every** \(S\),
\[
  N_S=4+|S|.\tag{1}
\]
For each index \(i\), with probability \(p_i>0\), simultaneously for **every** \(S\),
\[
  N_S=3+2\bigl(|S|-\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}\bigr).\tag{2}
\]
These five alternatives are disjoint, exhaustive, and each has positive probability. In particular, every individual subset \(S\) of size \(r\) has the following distribution (merge coinciding support points):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(N_S=4+r)&\mathrel{+}=1-p,\\
\mathbb P(N_S=3+2r)&\mathrel{+}=\sum_{i\notin S}p_i,\\
\mathbb P(N_S=1+2r)&\mathrel{+}=\sum_{i\in S}p_i.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]
The notation \(\mathrel{+}=\) means that if two right-hand support locations coincide, their displayed masses are added. For instance, for two cuts \(S=\{i,j\}\), the shadow has \(5,6,7\) vertices with respective probabilities \(p_i+p_j\), \(1-p\), \(p-p_i-p_j\).

Two consequences of the **joint** law are an almost-sure complementary relation for *every* partial cut pattern,
\[
  N_S+N_{S^c}=12,\tag{4}
\]
and a labeled reconstruction of the four solid angles from one-vertex-cut shadow experiments:
\[
  \mathbb P\bigl(N_{\{i\}}=3\bigr)=\frac{\Omega_i}{2\pi},\qquad i=0,1,2,3.\tag{5}
\]
The complementary relation for the special pair \(S=\varnothing\), \(S^c=\{0,1,2,3\}\) and its unlabeled \(8,9\) distribution appeared in preceding work; the findings here are the full sixteen-variable law, proper partial-cut distributions, and individual-angle recovery.

## Assumptions and scope
All four original vertices are affinely independent. For each \(i\in S\), the new cut points on the original edges \(v_iv_j\), \(j\ne i\), are \((1-t_i)v_i+t_iv_j\), where \(0<t_i<1/2\); the remaining original vertices are retained. Thus simultaneous cuts are disjoint, and all six original tetrahedral edges retain a nonzero central segment. The viewing direction is Haar-uniform on \(S^2\). The exceptional directions orthogonal to a facet normal, a polytope edge, or creating nongeneric overlaps comprise a finite union of great circles and have probability zero. The sixteen shadows are coupled using the identical direction, not independently sampled directions.

Each \(\Omega_i\) is the spherical area of the tangent cone generated by the three edge rays directed outward from \(v_i\) toward its three neighbors, not the exterior normal-cone solid angle. If \(a,b,c\) are unit vectors on these rays, the exact formula is
\[
\Omega_i=2\operatorname{atan2}\bigl(|\det(a,b,c)|,1+a\cdot b+b\cdot c+c\cdot a\bigr),\tag{6}
\]
with the positive branch of \(\operatorname{atan2}\). No shape regularity or equal cutting depths are required.

## Proof
**1. Signs of the four old facets.** Let \(F_i\) be the face opposite \(v_i\) with outward normal \(n_i\) and area \(A_i>0\). The equilibrium identity \(\sum_i A_i n_i=0\) implies that a generic direction has between one and three positive signs among \(s_i=\operatorname{sgn}(n_i\cdot U)\): never zero or four. A direction is therefore either of sign type \((2,2)\), or of type \((1,3)\) with unique outlier index \(i\) (after forgetting the global reversal of all signs).

**2. Every edge is accounted for.** The facets of \(T_S\) consist of the four truncated original facets \(F_i'\), still with outward normals \(n_i\), and, for each \(i\in S\), one new triangular facet with outward normal \(-n_i\). The edge set consists of six original-facet pairs \((F_i',F_j')\), one for each \(i<j\), and three cut-to-old pairs \((G_i,F_j')\) for each \(i\in S\), \(j\ne i\). There are no cut-to-cut edges because the depth condition keeps cuts disjoint. The original-edge intersection segments and all new edges are nondegenerate.

For a generic viewing direction, an edge belongs to the outline of the orthogonal shadow exactly when its adjacent outward facet normals have opposing scalar-product signs with the viewing direction. Each such edge projects to one edge of the projected polygon, and hence the number of such edges equals the number of projected polygon vertices. Consequently
\[
 N_S=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1_{\{s_i\ne s_j\}}
   +\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{j\ne i}\mathbf1_{\{s_i=s_j\}}.\tag{7}
\]
The second term has equality of old-facet signs because the new face normal is their negative.

**3. Evaluate the sign patterns.** When two signs are positive and two negative, the first sum of (7) equals \(4\). For each \(i\), exactly one of the other three old signs agrees with \(s_i\), so the second sum contributes exactly \(|S|\). This gives (1). When \(i\) is the unique outlier, the first sum equals \(3\). At the outlier there are zero other matching signs, while at each nonoutlier there are exactly two. The second sum is therefore \(2(|S|-\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}})\), giving (2).

**4. Determine the five probabilities exactly.** At \(v_i\), the tangent cone contains the three vectors \(v_j-v_i\), \(j\ne i\). A generic direction \(U\) lies inside this cone precisely when \(n_j\cdot U<0\) for every \(j\ne i\); the positive equilibrium relation then forces \(n_i\cdot U>0\). Thus the event that \(i\) is the unique sign outlier is exactly this tangent cone and its antipode, with total spherical area \(2\Omega_i\) and uniform probability \(p_i\). The four opposite-cone pairs are disjoint up to null boundaries. Their complement is the sign type \((2,2)\) with probability \(1-p\). This proves the complete joint law and (3).

The tangent cones have positive interiors, so every \(p_i\) is positive. There are also open \((2,2)\) sign chambers: take the viewing direction parallel to \(v_0+v_1-v_2-v_3\). The projected images of the two opposite-edge midpoints coincide, so the four projected vertices form a nondegenerate parallelogram; this remains a quadrilateral for nearby generic directions. Thus \(1-p>0\), giving exactly five positive-probability atoms.

**5. Complementary masks and inversion.** If signs are balanced, adding (1) for \(S\) and \(S^c\) gives \(12\). For the outlier \(i\), adding (2) for the complementary subsets gives \(3+2(r-\delta)+3+2(4-r-(1-\delta))=12\). For a singleton cut \(S=\{i\}\), (1) gives five vertices, (2) gives three only if the unique sign outlier is \(i\), and five otherwise. Therefore (5) follows and reconstructs each labeled solid angle separately. No dependence on depths enters these deductions.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to construct every partially truncated polytope and independently compute each projected convex hull. It tests four explicit affinely independent tetrahedra, 16 masks, unequal independently selected cut depths, and a collection of integral viewing directions. Each calculated number of projected vertices agrees with (1)--(2). For each direction, complementary-mask counts add to 12. An additional independent spherical Monte Carlo calculation checks the exact solid-angle probabilities (6), within sampling tolerance. Reproduce using Python 3, with NumPy installed: `python verify.py`.

The exact integer/rational finite comparisons validate the incidence counting in four families, but they are not a proof over every tetrahedron or continuum of directions. That generality follows from the facet-incidence argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Glisse and Lazard established the silhouette-edge framework and general average silhouette bounds for polyhedra (arXiv:cs/0702087v1, 14 February 2007). Feldman and Klain established that the probability of a triangular projection of an untruncated tetrahedron is the sum of its four interior vertex solid angles divided by \(2\pi\) (arXiv:0809.3459v1, 19 September 2008). Both provide ingredients used here; their classical untruncated results are not new claims.

A previously recorded result already establishes the complementary \(8\)-versus-\(9\) shadow counts for *fully truncated* tetrahedra. It neither specifies the separate proper-subset shadow distributions nor the **joint law for all sixteen labeled truncation patterns**. In particular, the identity (5) recovers the four individual solid angles, whereas the untruncated/full-truncation laws reveal only their sum.

Research on random polytope shadows by Black and Criado (arXiv:2406.06936v1, 11 June 2024) gives general expected-complexity bounds, not this joint distribution. The published tetrahedral area-weighted shadow formulas describe conditional projected areas of **untruncated** tetrahedra, not partial vertex cuts. The originality claim here is restricted to the complete simultaneous truncated-mask law and its inversion, never the underlying silhouette criterion or known special-mask probabilities.

## Limitations
The law assumes disjoint, parallel-to-opposite-face corner cuts with each edge fraction strictly below one half and an identical uniformly random orthogonal projection direction for every mask. It does not describe cuts that intersect one another, oblique cut planes, independent viewing directions, or face-metric distributions. Solid-angle recovery determines four vertex-cone measures, not a unique tetrahedron shape. General historical priority cannot be certified from the finite corpus inspected. Independent mathematical auditing remains unperformed.
