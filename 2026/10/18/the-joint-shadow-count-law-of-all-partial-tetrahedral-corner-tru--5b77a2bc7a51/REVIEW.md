# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies all four old tetrahedral face normal signs and all six old/old edge adjacencies, with three cut/old adjacencies for each selected corner. Balanced signs produce \(4+|S|\) vertices; one-outlier signs give \(3+2(|S|-\mathbf1_{i\in S})\). The five sign types partition the generic sphere. Each outlier cone pair has area \(2\Omega_i\), giving the exact distribution. Complement masks sum to twelve in either sign case, and singleton cuts recover labeled interior solid angles. All quantifiers, disjoint-cut assumptions, boundary exclusions and normalization are explicit. Rational projected-hull checks confirm the nonstandard edge-incidence step, not replacing its proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked sources
The 2007 Glisse–Lazard work covers silhouette edges and mean-complexity bounds; Feldman–Klain (2008) establishes the classical untruncated tetrahedral triangular-shadow probability; Black–Criado (2024) gives general bounds. An earlier result already determines full-corner-truncation shadow counts, hence the fully cut \(8/9\) case is expressly excluded from novelty. The full **joint** distribution for sixteen independently chosen masks, proper-subset shadow counts, and recovery of **four labeled** solid angles are not implied by these prior statements. A published tetrahedron result concerns conditional shadow *area* of untruncated bodies and does not cover these counts. Residual historical risk: an obscure source might formulate equivalent partial-truncation probabilities via signed facet arrangements.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Rather than a single ad hoc example, the theorem completely classifies the coupled combinatorial shadow behavior of the natural sixteen-member family of tetrahedral corner cuts, for every tetrahedron and every allowable independent cut depth. Its four labeled one-vertex experiments recover genuinely finer local vertex geometry than the formerly known aggregate solid-angle sum. The five-atom law, exact two-cut \(5/6/7\) distribution, and complementary-mask law give usable, sharp finite geometric information without any genericity assumption on tetrahedron shape.

## Limitations
General oblique or intersecting truncations, independent viewing directions and determination of a tetrahedron from four solid angles are not covered. The proof has not undergone independent mathematical validation or exhaustive historical-priority verification.
