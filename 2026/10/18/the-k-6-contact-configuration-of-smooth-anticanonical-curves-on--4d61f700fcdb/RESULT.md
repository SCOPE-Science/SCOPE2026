# The \(K_6\) contact configuration of smooth anticanonical curves on the split quintic del Pezzo surface over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(S\) be the split degree-five del Pezzo surface obtained by blowing up \(\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) at the four-point arc \(P_1=(1:0:0)\), \(P_2=(0:1:0)\), \(P_3=(0:0:1)\), \(P_4=(1:1:1)\). Among all \(63\) rational members of its complete anticanonical linear system \(|-K_S|\), exactly **six** are geometrically smooth. Each is a genus-one curve with precisely five rational points, hence a maximal elliptic curve over \(\mathbf F_2\).

The \(15\) rational points of \(S\) are canonically in bijection with the \(15\) unordered pairs of these six smooth members: each rational point belongs to exactly two smooth curves, and each pair of distinct smooth curves has a **single** geometric intersection point, rational over \(\mathbf F_2\), with local intersection multiplicity \(5=(-K_S)^2\). Equivalently, the entire rational incidence configuration is the edge-vertex incidence of the complete graph \(K_6\), with every edge carrying a maximally concentrated anticanonical intersection.

Under \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(S)\simeq S_5\), the six smooth members form one orbit. The action is sharply three-transitive on those six members, and the stabilizer of one member has order \(20\). All \(63\) anticanonical members form five automorphism orbits of respective sizes \(6,10,12,15,20\), only the first orbit being smooth. No pencil in \(|-K_S|\) has every \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational member geometrically smooth.

## Assumptions and scope
All geometric smoothness and geometric intersection assertions are over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbf F_2\); the words *rational* and *rational member* refer to \(\mathbf F_2\). The surface is specifically the **split** degree-five del Pezzo surface, not every arithmetic form. A rational pencil has three members, because a two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_2\)-vector space has exactly three nonzero vectors.

Let \(X,Y,Z\) be coordinates on the blown-down projective plane. The anticanonical vector space is the six-dimensional space of ternary cubic forms through the four specified points. Set
\[
(M_0,\ldots,M_6)=(X^2Y,X^2Z,XY^2,XYZ,XZ^2,Y^2Z,YZ^2).
\]
Every anticanonical section is represented uniquely by \(\sum_{i=0}^6c_iM_i\), where \(c_i\in\mathbf F_2\) and \(\sum_i c_i=0\). Thus there are \(2^6-1=63\) nonzero sections, each representing a distinct rational projective member.

## Proof
**(1) Complete smooth-section classification.** For each of the \(63\) forms, reduce to each of the three affine charts and form the ideal generated by the chart equation and its two first partial derivatives over \(\mathbf F_2\). The member is geometrically smooth if and only if the reduced Gröbner basis is \((1)\) on every chart. The exhaustive calculation yields exactly six smooth forms, specified by the following six integer coefficient masks \(m=\sum_{i=0}^{5}2^ic_i\), with \(c_6=\sum_{i=0}^{5}c_i\):
\[
\{7,22,38,49,51,53\}.
\]
The complete enumeration and its algebraic smoothness test are implemented in `verify_census.py`, with exact output in `census_expected.json`. This is a finite exhaustive argument, not a statistical test. For a smooth plane cubic passing through the base points, the strict transform is isomorphic to the cubic and is a smooth anticanonical genus-one divisor. If the plane cubic is singular at a blown-up point, the anticanonical divisor contains an exceptional component and is not smooth; singularities elsewhere likewise persist. Thus the criterion tests the actual anticanonical member.

Evaluation at the seven rational points of the plane shows that every one of the six smooth cubics contains the four base points and exactly one other rational point. After blowing up, that cubic still has exactly five rational points. The surface has \(7+4(3-1)=15\) rational points.

**(2) Rational incidence.** For a smooth plane cubic \(F\), its strict transform intersects the exceptional line above \(P_i\) in the tangent direction determined by \((F_X(P_i),F_Y(P_i),F_Z(P_i))\); it also contains any nonbase rational point of \(F\). Recording these five points for each of the six forms gives fifteen distinct point labels, each appearing on exactly two curves, and every unordered pair of curves appears exactly once. The complete point-to-curve incidence table is in `census_expected.json`, reproduced by `verify_census.py`. This proves the intrinsic \(K_6\)-incidence assertion without assuming that a rational intersection exhausts the geometric intersection number.

**(3) Concentration of the intersection number.** Take the smooth forms represented by masks \(7\) and \(22\):
\[
F=X^2Y+X^2Z+XY^2+YZ^2,\quad
G=X^2Z+XY^2+XZ^2+YZ^2.
\]
Their difference in characteristic two is \(F+G=X(XY+Z^2)\). Restriction of \(F\) to the line \(X=0\), in local coordinates \(Y=1,Z=t\), is \(t^2\), giving intersection order two at \(P_2\). Parametrize the smooth conic \(XY+Z^2=0\) by \((X:Y:Z)=(s^2:t^2:st)\); then \(F\) restricts to \(s^4t(t+s)\), giving order four at \(P_2\) and simple contacts at \(P_1,P_4\). The line gives one additional simple contact at \(P_3\). The plane intersection multiplicities are therefore \(6,1,1,1\), summing to nine as Bézout requires. Because the cubics are smooth at all four base points, blowing them up subtracts one from each local plane intersection multiplicity. Their strict transforms have exactly one intersection, supported over \(P_2\), of multiplicity \(6-1=5\); there is no intersection elsewhere. See `verify_contact.py` and `contact_expected.json`.

**(4) Full symmetry and no all-smooth pencil.** The \(24\) plane projectivities preserving the four-point arc and the standard quadratic Cremona involution \((X:Y:Z)\mapsto(YZ:XZ:XY)\) lift to automorphisms of \(S\). On the cubic coefficient space the Cremona map acts by \(F\mapsto F(YZ,XZ,XY)/(XYZ)\). Exact finite matrix closure gives a group of order \(120\), equal to the full automorphism group established independently in the literature. Its orbit sizes on all anticanonical members are \(6,10,12,15,20\). On the six smooth forms, the induced faithful permutation group has \(120\) elements and one orbit on all \(120\) ordered triples of distinct curves, hence acts sharply three-transitively. It follows that all distinct curve pairs are conjugate, so the contact calculation for \(F,G\) proves the multiplicity-five assertion for every pair. The computation is reproduced by `verify_symmetry.py` and `symmetry_expected.json`.

Finally, the sum of any two distinct masks from \(\{7,22,38,49,51,53\}\) is not in that set. Every rational pencil contains the sum of any two independent sections. Hence no all-smooth rational pencil exists.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_census.py`, `python3 verify_symmetry.py`, `python3 verify_contact.py`, and `python3 verify_extensions.py` in an environment with Python 3 and SymPy. Their standard output is canonical JSON and should match the four accompanying `*_expected.json` files byte for byte. The extension-field script independently tests every candidate singularity over degrees \(1\) through \(4\): it finds all \(57\) singular forms, including \(45\) with an \(\mathbf F_2\)-singular point and \(12\) whose first detected singular point is over \(\mathbf F_4\). This extension test is corroborative; the complete geometric proof uses the chartwise Gröbner basis, which rules out singular points over the entire algebraic closure.

## Relationship to prior work
Blache–Couvreur–Hallouin–Madore–Nardi–Rambaud–Randriam (2019) study anticanonical codes, especially Picard-rank-one forms, and use the split degree-five del Pezzo as a geometric model for automorphisms. Boitrel (first preprint 2023) and Zaitsev (2023) classify degree-five del Pezzo forms and automorphism groups. The automorphism identification \(S_5\), anticanonical dimension, and blow-up model are pre-existing; they are not claimed as discoveries. The finding here concerns the full finite Bertini orbit distribution, its six maximal smooth sections and the concentrated \(K_6\) intersection geometry over the smallest field. The inspected relevant sections of those works do not tabulate or imply that fine configuration without further computation and local intersection analysis.

## Limitations
This result concerns only the split surface over \(\mathbf F_2\), not nonsplit forms or larger fields. The algebraic Groebner criterion and the finite group closure are reproducible computational certificates, not independently peer-reviewed proofs. A classical or unindexed table of the exact same smooth-section contact configuration might exist. No assertion about the existence or nonexistence of pencils over finite extensions is made. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
