# Scientific review: maximal tetrahedra in the bicylinder

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof explicitly establishes the affine equivalence between the cylinder intersection and the hull of two perpendicular disks. Multi-affine replacement reduces unrestricted tetrahedra to generating-circle cases. The three-and-one case has a strictly smaller area-times-height bound; the two-and-two case has the exact chord determinant followed by an equality-sharp arithmetic–geometric mean inequality. The reverse-replacement argument, supported by unique exposed-circle points of the canonical tetrahedron, eliminates additional non-circle maximizers. The embedded checker was executed successfully; finite numerical tests are not treated as proof.

## Originality

**PASS under checked literature, with residual priority uncertainty.** Finch (arXiv:1211.4514) gives intrinsic volumes of the same orthogonal-disk hull, not the largest contained tetrahedron. Horváth (arXiv:1509.08859), relevant sections 3.1–3.2, concerns convex-hull volumes and related maximal simplices rather than the present containment problem. Three closely related published disk-hull results were inspected at the scientific-claim and relationship-to-prior-work level: a concurrent-disk surface/volume identity, an orthogonal-disk mean-width derivative law, and the intrinsic-volume decomposition of a three-disk hull. None implies the exact extremal tetrahedron-volume ratio or full equality classification. Exact historical uniqueness cannot be concluded from database search; unidentified treatments under the classical Steinmetz/Chinese-umbrella terminology are a residual risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The ratio between an extremal simplex volume and the ambient convex-body volume is a natural affine-invariant measure of approximation by a minimal-vertex polytope. The bicylinder is a classical smooth-faced body with a known volume but no formula for this invariant in the inspected primary source. The result solves its unrestricted four-point optimization exactly, determines the complete equality locus, and gives the sharp, dimensionless value \(1/(3\sqrt3)\). It does not rely on a hand-chosen parameter slice or rederive only an intrinsic volume.
