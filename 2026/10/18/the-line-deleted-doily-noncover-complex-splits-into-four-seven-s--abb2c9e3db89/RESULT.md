# The line-deleted doily noncover complex splits into four seven-spheres

## Finding

Let \(P=\binom{[6]}2\) be the fifteen duads and let \(\mathcal L\) consist of the fifteen partitions of \([6]\) into three pairs (synthemes). Write \(\mathrm{NC}(\mathcal L)\) for the simplicial complex of subsets of \(P\) disjoint from at least one member of \(\mathcal L\). Fix one line \(L_0\) and put \(K^- = \mathrm{NC}(\mathcal L\setminus\{L_0\})\), \(K=\mathrm{NC}(\mathcal L)\), \(F=\Delta_{P\setminus L_0}\), and \(J=K^-\cap F\). Then

\[
K^-\simeq\bigvee^4 S^7,\qquad J\simeq\bigvee^5 S^6,\qquad K\simeq K^-\vee\bigvee^5 S^7\simeq\bigvee^9S^7.
\]

In particular, the natural inclusion \(K^-\hookrightarrow K\) admits a homotopy retraction, and its map on integral seventh homology is a split injection with cokernel \(\mathbb Z^5\). All choices of \(L_0\) are equivalent under relabeling of the six-element ground set.

The subgroup \(H\leq S_6\) preserving \(L_0\) is \((C_2)^3\rtimes S_3\) of order \(48\). Let \(\epsilon\) be its character given by the parity of pairwise flips inside \(L_0\). Then, as rational \(H\)-representations,

\[
\widetilde H_7(K^-;\mathbb Q)\cong\epsilon\oplus U,
\]

where \(U\) is an irreducible three-dimensional representation. The four-dimensional module has no invariant vector. The five-dimensional quotient module \(\widetilde H_7(K,K^-;\mathbb Q)\) likewise has no invariant vector and is a sum of two inequivalent irreducibles.

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is defined over the fixed six-element set \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\); its 15 unordered pairs are vertices of the simplicial complexes. Each syntheme is a set of three disjoint such pairs, collectively covering all six elements. No geometric realization, field-dependent incidence model, or choice of coordinate system is required. The initial syntheme \(L_0=\{\{0,1\},\{2,3\},\{4,5\}\}\) fixes a convenient labeling; symmetric-group transitivity removes the choice. All homotopy statements concern ordinary topological realization and integral homotopy type, not merely field-coefficient homology.

## Proof

**Exact acyclic matchings.** Represent a face by a 15-bit mask. The facets of \(K\) are the 15 twelve-vertex complements of the synthemes. The facets of \(K^-\) omit the facet complementary to \(L_0\). The faces of \(J\) are precisely the faces of \(K^-\) whose masks lie inside \(P\setminus L_0\). Thus the three face sets are unambiguously generated by the executable verifier `code/verify.py`.

On each face poset, apply the fixed vertex-order toggle algorithm: successively, for the next vertex \(v\), match every as-yet-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) whenever both faces remain. For an ordinary (unreduced) acyclic matching, unmatch the pair containing the empty face, leaving one critical vertex. For every codimension-one face incidence, direct the Hasse edge upward if the two faces are paired, otherwise downward. A topological sort of this entire directed graph proves acyclicity, not just an absence of short cycles.

The complete calculation gives: \(24,823\) faces including the empty face, \(12,406\) ordinary matched pairs, and critical-cell dimensions \(0\) once and \(7\) nine times for \(K\); \(24,274\) faces, \(12,134\) matched pairs, and critical cells \(0\) once and \(7\) four times for \(K^-\); and \(3,547\) faces, \(1,770\) pairs, and critical cells \(0\) once and \(6\) five times for \(J\). The directed graphs contain, respectively, all \(170,550\), \(166,002\), and \(20,016\) nonempty codimension-one incidences. Every directed graph is acyclic. Discrete Morse theory therefore produces a CW model containing only a point and the specified equal-dimensional positive cells, giving all three sphere-wedge homotopy equivalences without computing an attaching boundary.

**The split inclusion.** Since the line-complement simplex \(F\) is contractible and \(K=K^-\cup F\) with intersection \(J\), simplicial inclusions give a homotopy pushout. Every map \(J\simeq\bigvee^5 S^6\to K^-\simeq\bigvee^4 S^7\) is nullhomotopic (\(\pi_6(K^-)=0\)). Thus attaching the contractible simplex has the homotopy effect of adjoining the suspension of \(J\), which is \(\bigvee^5S^7\). Under a chosen nullhomotopy, the inclusion of \(K^-\) identifies with the canonical wedge inclusion. This constructs a homotopy retraction and proves the split integral injection, not merely a rank comparison.

**Stabilizer representation.** A permutation of \([6]\) induces a permutation of the 15 duads. Exactly 48 permutations preserve \(L_0\); they form \((C_2)^3\rtimes S_3\). For each such permutation \(g\), compute the reduced Lefschetz number directly as a signed count of its invariant nonempty simplices, then use the fact that reduced rational homology of \(K^-\) lives only in degree seven to recover its trace. An independent method enumerates unions of cycles of the action of \(g\) on the duads; the two methods agree on all 48 elements. If \(\chi\) denotes the character of \(\widetilde H_7(K^-;\mathbb Q)\), the exact values satisfy

\[
\chi(1)=4,\quad\sum_{g\in H}\chi(g)=0,\quad\sum_{g\in H}\chi(g)^2=96=2|H|.
\]

The inner product of \(\chi\) with the internal-pair-flip sign \(\epsilon\) is exactly one. The inner-product equality \(\langle\chi,\chi\rangle_H=2\), combined with the known one-dimensional constituent, forces the remaining three-dimensional summand to be irreducible and different from \(\epsilon\). The character of the relative quotient has dimension five, zero invariant multiplicity, and character-square sum \(96\), so it likewise decomposes into two inequivalent irreducibles. As an independent consistency check, all 720 permutations of \([6]\) give a nine-dimensional full-complex character with square sum \(720\).

## Relationship to prior work

Kim and Kim (2021) formalized noncover complexes and analyzed general bounds and special hypergraph families; their definitions provide the natural topology here. A crucial earlier, stronger result for a related object is Linusson, Shareshian, and Welker's Corollary 1.2: the complex of all graphs on six labeled vertices *without* a perfect matching is a wedge of nine five-spheres. The intact doily noncover complex \(K\) is the combinatorial Alexander dual of that no-perfect-matching complex; thus the previously established result already implies \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\), and that homology rank is **not** presented as original here. Their theorem does not itself determine the line-deleted complex, the five-sphere intersection, the split homotopy inclusion or the stabilizer representation. Matsushita and Wakatsuki study other Alexander-dual, domination and neighborhood complexes, but not the line-deleted syntheme incidence hypergraph.

The substantive new content is the effect of **removing a single syntheme** from the fifteen-line incidence system, together with the inclusion's homotopy splitting and the residual symmetry module. The intact-complex wedge homotopy type is included to express that inclusion precisely and for an independent finite check, with its earlier Alexander-dual consequence clearly credited.

## Limitations

This is a finite classification for the unique order-two generalized quadrangle in its duad-syntheme model, and one line removed. It does not establish a corresponding formula for all generalized quadrangles, deletions of arbitrary multiple lines, or an equivariant homotopy splitting. The rational stabilizer representation is identified up to an irreducible three-dimensional complement but no preferred integral lattice model or homology basis is asserted. The related older no-perfect-matching theorem covers the intact homology rank, leaving only the puncture, split map and stabilizer calculation as originality candidates. The novelty comparison is limited by coverage of accessible literature and indexed research records.
