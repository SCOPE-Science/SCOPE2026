# The \(\mathfrak S_5\)-character of three-point configuration homology on \(K_5\)

## Finding
Let \(G=K_5\) be the complete graph on the labeled vertex set \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), and let \(B_3(G)=\operatorname{UConf}_3(|G|)\) be the unordered space of three distinct points on the geometric realization of \(G\). The graph-vertex permutation group \(\mathfrak S_5\) acts naturally on \(B_3(G)\). Its degree-two rational homology has the character
\[
\chi_{H_2(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)}=(30,0,-2,0,0,0,0),
\]
for conjugacy types \((1^5),(2,1^3),(2^2,1),(3,1^2),(3,2),(4,1),(5)\), respectively. Consequently, as a rational \(\mathfrak S_5\)-representation,
\[
H_2(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\cong S^{(4,1)}\oplus S^{(3,2)}\oplus 2S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,2,1)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)}.
\]
Here \(S^\lambda\) denotes the rational Specht module indexed by a partition \(\lambda\) of five. The rational Betti vector in degrees zero through three is \((1,6,30,0)\). In particular, degree-two rational homology contains neither an invariant line nor a sign-representation summand, and the irreducible six-dimensional constituent occurs with multiplicity two.

## Assumptions and scope
The five original vertices are all essential (degree four); introduce one midpoint on each of the ten original edges, producing a subdivided graph with fifteen vertices and twenty oriented half-edges. Every simple path between distinct essential vertices then uses at least two half-edges, and every non-nullhomotopic simple closed path uses at least six. For three particles, the subdivision hypothesis of the strengthened Abrams discretization theorem is satisfied: distinct-essential-vertex paths have length at least two, and essential nontrivial cycles have length at least four. Thus the natural inclusion of the finite unordered cubical discretized configuration space \(D_3(G')\) into \(B_3(G)\) is a homotopy equivalence. Crucially, the inclusion itself commutes with all five-vertex permutations, so it induces an isomorphism of \(\mathfrak S_5\)-representations; no equivariant retraction is assumed.

A cubical cell of dimension \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) is specified by \(k\) distinct half-edges with pairwise disjoint closed endpoints and by \(3-k\) further vertices disjoint from all those endpoints. The half-edge coordinates are ordered by their fixed index, and each half-edge is oriented from the original lower-numbered vertex toward its midpoint or from that midpoint toward the original higher-numbered vertex. Cubical incidence orientations are the corresponding ordered products.

## Proof
**1. Exact cubical chains and boundaries.** Enumerating all closed-edge-disjoint cell choices yields chain group dimensions
\[
(\dim C_0,\dim C_1,\dim C_2,\dim C_3)=(455,1560,1650,520).
\]
For an oriented cell with ordered edges \((e_1,\ldots,e_k)\), the signed boundary replaces each \(e_i\) in turn by its terminal vertex with coefficient \((-1)^{i-1}\) and by its initial vertex with coefficient \(-(-1)^{i-1}\). The complete matrices are generated by `verify.py`, which also checks their two consecutive products vanish integrally. Exact sparse elimination modulo three gives
\[
(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}\partial_1,\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}\partial_2,\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}\partial_3)=(454,1100,520).
\]
Consequently the rational boundary ranks are at least these numbers; the last is already the full column count, giving \(H_3(-;\mathbb Q)=0\).

**2. A topological upper bound makes the rank determination exact.** Sending a motion of three indistinguishable points to the sum of the resulting oriented one-particle traversals gives a well-defined homomorphism
\[
\tau:H_1(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H_1(G;\mathbb Q).
\]
At the cubical chain level, a one-cell maps to its oriented moving half-edge, independent of the locations of the stationary points; opposite sides of each two-cell cancel. For any triangle of \(K_5\), place the two stationary particles at the two complementary original vertices and circulate the third particle around the triangle. Therefore \(\tau\) hits every triangular cycle; these span the six-dimensional \(H_1(K_5;\mathbb Q)\), so \(\dim H_1(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\ge6\). The cubical degree-one chain dimension and the connectedness rank imply
\[
\dim H_1=1560-454-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_2\ge6,
\]
so \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_2\le1100\). The modular lower bound proves equality. Likewise \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_1=454\) because \(\partial_1\) cannot have rank above \(C_0-1\), and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_3=520\). Thus the rational Betti vector is exactly \((1,6,30,0)\). The surjective map \(\tau\) between six-dimensional representations is an \(\mathfrak S_5\)-equivariant isomorphism on \(H_1\).

**3. Signed Lefschetz traces.** For a permutation \(g\in\mathfrak S_5\), its action on an oriented cell transports each vertex and half-edge, multiplies by \(-1\) for every reversed half-edge orientation, and multiplies by the sign of the permutation needed to restore the sorted half-edge product order. Enumerating fixed oriented cells yields the following trace rows, indexed by the seven conjugacy types in the finding, with columns \(C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\mathrm{type}&C_0&C_1&C_2&C_3\\\hline
(1^5)&455&1560&1650&520\\
(2,1^3)&63&84&6&-16\\
(2^2,1)&19&0&-18&0\\
(3,1^2)&5&0&0&4\\
(3,2)&3&0&0&2\\
(4,1)&1&0&0&0\\
(5)&0&0&0&0
\end{array}
\]
The equivariant isomorphism \(H_1(B_3(G);\mathbb Q)\cong H_1(G;\mathbb Q)\) identifies its character with the signed graph-edge permutation character minus the vertex permutation character plus the trivial character, giving
\[
\chi_{H_1}=(6,0,-2,0,0,0,1).
\]
Since homology vanishes above degree two over \(\mathbb Q\), the alternating Lefschetz identity on each conjugacy class reads
\[
\chi_{H_2}=\sum_{k=0}^3(-1)^k\chi_{C_k}-1+\chi_{H_1},
\]
which gives precisely \((30,0,-2,0,0,0,0)\).

**4. Rational irreducible decomposition.** Let \(V=S^{(4,1)}\) be the four-dimensional standard representation. The seven irreducible rational characters are those of the trivial module, \(V\), the five-dimensional constituent of the permutation action on two-element subsets, \(\bigwedge^2V=S^{(3,1,1)}\), their respective sign twists, and the sign module. Taking character inner products with \((30,0,-2,0,0,0,0)\) using conjugacy-class sizes \((1,10,15,20,20,30,24)\) gives multiplicities \((0,1,1,2,1,1,0)\) in this order. Semisimplicity over \(\mathbb Q\) proves the stated module isomorphism.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with only the Python standard library. It reconstructs every cell, verifies \(\partial^2=0\) with integer coefficients, calculates all three mod-three boundary ranks (and mod-two cross-check ranks), computes the signed Lefschetz chain traces for all seven conjugacy-class representatives, checks the graph-cycle character, constructs the irreducible character vectors from standard permutation actions, and verifies each Specht multiplicity. The check returns `VERIFY_OK`. Exact finite field ranks are used only as certified lower bounds for rational ranks; the upper bound for the critical second boundary is proved separately by the motion-to-graph homology map.

## Relationship to prior work
The finite cubical graph-configuration model originates with Abrams, with the sharpened subdivision criterion proved by Prue and Scrimshaw. Ko and Park give a general formula for first homology of graph braid groups, consistent with the six-dimensional rational degree-one term here; their first-homology formula does not determine the degree-two \(\mathfrak S_5\)-character. Maciążek and Sawicki develop graph-configuration homology and explicitly calculate the complete-graph wheel case \(K_4\), as well as wheel graphs and selected bipartite graphs; a complete graph on five vertices is not a wheel graph, and their displayed statements do not imply the present vertex-permutation action on \(H_2(B_3(K_5);\mathbb Q)\). Published calculations for two-particle configuration spaces or other graph families do not specialize to this three-particle, five-vertex symmetry module. Absence of an indexed match is not evidence of a global uniqueness theorem.

## Limitations
This is a rational representation classification for precisely three unordered particles on the topological graph \(K_5\). It does not establish an integral module splitting, exclude integral torsion in second homology, describe an explicit geometric basis of second-homology cycles, or compute the configuration spaces of other particle numbers. The proof uses exact finite chain calculations reproducible by the embedded standalone program; no independent scientific audit is claimed. A published equivalent under different terminology could still exist.
