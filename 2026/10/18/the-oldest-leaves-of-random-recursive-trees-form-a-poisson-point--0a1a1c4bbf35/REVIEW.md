# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The model uses independent uniform attachment choices at each arrival; leafhood means zero outdegree at the final tree size. Conditioning sequentially on specified leafhood events produces the exact \(r\)-point product with the stated ranges and correct root exclusion. Its exact termwise bound by independent Bernoulli marginal products uniformly dominates the Laplace expansion. The fixed-order compact-window error is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) after summation, making the Poisson Laplace limit rigorous. Standard continuity of point-process counts and mapping of the cumulative intensity proves the Gamma/Rayleigh order-statistic consequences. The standalone checker passed 17,287 exact/computational checks, independently corroborating—not replacing—the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The materially read original work on one specified node's outdegree (Kuba–Panholzer, introduction and exact/limit theorem statements) does not yield the extreme-label joint process directly. Published Janson results concern global degree counts; Mahmoud–Smythe treat leaf populations within rooted descendant subtrees, and Dobrow–Smythe analyze comparison counts and distances. Fixed-number-of-labels joint-degree asymptotics do not directly establish a process built from a growing number of rare early-leaf events. Distinct equivalent descriptions were checked: oldest leaf, smallest labeled terminal vertex, early zero-outdegree vertices, and birth-index point processes. No matching stronger statement was found in the searched evidence; absence of a match is not proof of priority. Full inspection of some older sources remains incomplete, so hidden equivalence remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Global leaf abundance (about half the vertices) gives no law for the oldest remaining leaves. The theorem identifies a sharp square-root age scale and the full joint rare-event geometry of extremes, not just a first moment or isolated special case. The exact full joint inclusion identity explains why dependent early leaf events nevertheless converge to a simple inhomogeneous Poisson process; all fixed ranked extremes, not merely the minimum, follow from one structural limit.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Kuba–Panholzer, *On the degree distribution of the nodes in increasing trees* (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcta.2006.08.003) and Janson, *Asymptotic degree distribution in random recursive trees* (DOI: 10.1002/rsa.20046) are the most relevant degree-level results. Dobrow–Smythe's 1996 Poisson-approximation paper was compared at the theorem/section level and treats different statistics. Historical priority is unverified, no rate is proved, and full-text access is incomplete for some early papers. Finite enumeration does not substitute for the limit proof.
