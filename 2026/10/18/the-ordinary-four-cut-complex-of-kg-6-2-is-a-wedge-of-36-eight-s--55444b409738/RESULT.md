# The ordinary four-cut complex of \(KG(6,2)\) is a wedge of 36 eight-spheres

## Finding
Let \(G=KG(6,2)\) be the Kneser graph on the 15 two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\), with two vertices adjacent exactly when their subsets are disjoint. Its **ordinary** four-cut complex satisfies
\[
\boxed{\Delta_4(G)\simeq\bigvee^{36}S^8.}
\]
The complex has 615 facets, each of dimension 10. Its only nonzero reduced integral homology group is \(\widetilde H_8(\Delta_4(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{36}\); in particular, it is not shellable. The statement is nonequivariant and concerns this one specified Kneser graph.

## Assumptions and scope
All simplicial complexes are finite abstract complexes. For a graph on vertex set \(V\), \(\Delta_4(G)\) is generated by the complements \(V\setminus D\) of four-element vertex subsets \(D\) for which the induced subgraph \(G[D]\) is disconnected. This is distinct from the *total* four-cut complex, which uses independent four-element sets. The 15 vertices of \(G\) are indexed \(0,\ldots,14\) by the lexicographic order of the two-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). Changing the ground-set labels does not change the complex.

## Proof
**1. Correct Alexander dual.** Write \(K=\Delta_4(G)\) and \(L=K^\vee\), taking the Alexander dual in the full 15-vertex simplex. Since a set \(U\) belongs to \(K\) exactly when \(V\setminus U\) contains some disconnected four-set, the dual is
\[
L=\{S\subseteq V:\text{every four-element subset of }S\text{ induces a connected subgraph of }G\}.
\]
This formula includes larger faces: \(L\) is **not** merely the complex generated by the connected four-sets. The reproducible exhaustive enumeration in `verify_kg62_full.py` checks connectivity directly from the defining Kneser adjacency. The exact numbers of faces of cardinalities \(0,1,\ldots,6\) are
\[
(1,15,105,455,750,492,70),
\]
and there are no faces of cardinality seven; downward closure therefore precludes any larger faces. Exactly 615 of the \(\binom{15}{4}=1365\) four-sets are disconnected. Hence \(L\) has 1887 nonempty faces.

**2. Complete acyclic Morse matching.** On the nonempty face poset of \(L\), use the vertex order
`(8, 1, 5, 9, 11, 3, 14, 13, 12, 2, 6, 10, 0, 7, 4)`.
For each index in this order, inspect currently unmatched faces in ascending order by `(cardinality, integer bitmask)`. Pair any face not containing that index to its extension by the index if both remain unmatched, and remove the pair from the current pool. All sets and pairs are represented by exact integer bitmasks.

This deterministic construction yields 925 matched pairs, one critical vertex, and exactly 36 critical faces of dimension four, with no other critical faces. The verifier independently constructs **all 7455 covering relations** among the 1887 nonempty faces, directs each unmatched cover downward and each matched cover upward, and topologically sorts the resulting directed graph. All 1887 vertices are processed, so the matching is acyclic. This is an exhaustive finite certificate, not sampling or extrapolation. Forman's discrete Morse theorem supplies a CW complex with one zero-cell and 36 four-cells and no positive-dimensional cells below dimension four. Every attaching map of a four-cell to a point is null, whence
\[
L\simeq\bigvee^{36}S^4.
\]

**3. Integral Alexander duality.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the 15-vertex ground set gives, for every integer \(j\),
\[
\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\widetilde H^{12-j}(L;\mathbb Z).
\]
Therefore \(\widetilde H_8(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{36}\), and every other reduced integral homology group of \(K\) vanishes.

**4. Upgrade from homology to homotopy.** Direct enumeration, independently implemented in `check_original_kg62.py`, constructs all faces of \(K\) from its 615 complementary facets. Its face counts in cardinalities \(0,\ldots,11\) are
\[
(1,15,105,455,1365,3003,5005,6435,6435,4935,2511,615).
\]
In particular, \(K\) contains the complete seven-skeleton of the 14-simplex: all subsets of size at most eight occur. Hence \(K\) is six-connected. Because its seventh reduced homology vanishes, Hurewicz implies that its seventh homotopy group also vanishes; thus \(K\) is seven-connected. The next Hurewicz isomorphism gives \(\pi_8(K)\cong H_8(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{36}\). Choose representing maps of 36 eight-spheres for an integral basis. Their wedge map to \(K\) is an isomorphism on every integral homology group, hence a homotopy equivalence by the homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes. This proves the boxed claim.

Finally, the facets of \(K\) all have cardinality 11, so \(K\) is pure of dimension 10. A pure shellable complex can have reduced homology only in its top dimension; the nonzero eighth homology obstructs shellability.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram, and Xue introduce and study ordinary cut complexes in arXiv:2304.13675. Their Corollary 6.6 concerns **two-cut** complexes of Kneser graphs satisfying the triangle-free restriction; their Table 1 and Section 7 treat other graph families rather than the present ordinary four-cut complex. The same authors' arXiv:2209.13503 treats **total** cut complexes: its independent-set definition is strictly narrower than disconnectedness for four-sets in \(KG(6,2)\). Shen and coauthors' arXiv:2512.09674 concerns neighborhood complexes and total-cut correspondences for stable Kneser constructions, not this ordinary-cut complex. These results motivate the example and provide context but do not yield this homotopy classification. An equivalent unindexed computation remains a bibliographic risk.

## Limitations
The theorem is proved for \(KG(6,2)\) alone; no extension to \(KG(m,2)\) for other \(m\) is established. The homotopy equivalence is not claimed to respect the natural symmetric-group action. The finite enumeration is a complete proof for this fixed graph, not an infinite-family argument; the integral and homotopy consequences rely on standard theorems as specified above.
