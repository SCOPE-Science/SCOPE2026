# The Pappus graph's two-independence complex is a mixed-dimensional sphere wedge

## Finding
Let \(P\) denote the eighteen-vertex Pappus graph, with vertices \(0,1,\ldots,17\) and the Hamiltonian LCF representation described below. Define \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\) to be the simplicial complex of subsets \(S\subseteq V(P)\) for which every connected component of the induced graph \(P[S]\) has at most two vertices. Then
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\simeq\bigvee^{9}S^5\vee S^6.
\]
In particular, its reduced integral homology is free of rank nine in degree five, free of rank one in degree six, and zero in all other degrees. The result is about this exact finite graph and the component-size-two definition of higher independence, not graph-theoretic distance-two independent sets.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple and undirected. Label its vertices by integers from zero through seventeen; add the cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) modulo eighteen and, for each \(i\), the chord \(\{i,i+c_i\}\) modulo eighteen, with
\[
(c_0,\ldots,c_{17})=(5,7,-7,7,-7,-5,5,7,-7,7,-7,-5,5,7,-7,7,-7,-5).
\]
Repeated undirected edges are included once. This is the standard Pappus graph LCF representation, with eighteen vertices and twenty-seven edges. A vertex subset belongs to \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\) if and only if every vertex of its induced subgraph has induced degree at most one. Indeed, a finite connected graph on three or more vertices contains a length-two path, while components of size one or two have maximum degree at most one. The empty subset is included as the augmented simplex.

## Proof
Enumerate all \(2^{18}\) labeled vertex subsets and retain exactly those meeting the induced-degree criterion. The resulting numbers of nonempty simplices in dimensions zero through nine are
\[
(18,153,762,2394,4806,6027,4428,1728,308,27).
\]
Thus there are 20,651 nonempty simplices and 20,652 including the empty face. The reduced Euler characteristic is \(-8\).

Construct an acyclic partial matching by *successive element matching*: at each step, pair every currently unpaired eligible face \(F\) not containing the designated vertex \(v\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\), removing each pair before the next step. Use the vertex order
\[
(7,12,17,11,13,10,0,9,2,4,14,5,6,15,8,3,16,1).
\]
The staged element-matching theorem implies acyclicity; independently, the supplied verification checks the reversed matched/unmatched Hasse orientation on all 120,240 covering incidences by a complete topological sort. There are exactly 10,321 matched pairs, including a pair involving the empty face, and ten critical faces. Nine are dimension-five cells with bitmasks
`0x6621`, `0xcc03`, `0x12264`, `0x18846`, `0x20b18`, `0x22360`, `0x2c409`, `0x2c902`, `0x3404c`;
the unique dimension-six critical face is `0x3212a`. A bitmask denotes its selected vertex indices as set bits.

The resulting Morse CW model has a basepoint, nine five-cells, and one six-cell. It remains necessary to identify the degree vector of the six-cell attaching map: the list of critical cell dimensions alone does not determine the homotopy type. Orient every simplex by increasing vertex order. For each dimension-five face \(F\), define its integral projection \(Q(F)\) onto the free group generated by the nine critical five-faces as follows: \(Q(F)\) is the corresponding basis element if critical, zero if paired downward with a four-face, and if paired upward to the six-face \(U\), then
\[
Q(F)=-[U:F]^{-1}\sum_{F'\subset U,\;|F'|=|F|,\;F'\ne F}[U:F']Q(F').
\]
Here \([U:F]\in\{+1,-1\}\) is the signed simplicial incidence, so all operations occur in the integers. Acyclicity ensures that the recursion terminates. For the seven five-faces of the critical six-face `0x3212a`, only deletion of vertex five and deletion of vertex thirteen have nonzero signed projected contributions: respectively \(-e_5\) and \(+e_5\), where \(e_5\) denotes the fifth basis vector when the nine critical five-faces are indexed from one through nine in increasing bitmask order. The other five contributions vanish. Hence the exact integral Morse differential from dimension six to dimension five is the zero vector.

The five-skeleton of the Morse CW model is \(\bigvee^9S^5\). This space is four-connected, so the Hurewicz map from its fifth homotopy group onto its fifth integral homology group is an isomorphism. Therefore a zero attaching-degree vector makes the six-cell attaching map nullhomotopic. Attaching that cell gives the claimed \(\bigvee^9S^5\vee S^6\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_pappus.py` from the directory containing the accompanying standalone script. It uses only the Python standard library; it checks the exact graph, all subset memberships, face vector, complete staged matching, critical masks, every oriented Hasse cover, and the integral gradient-path differential. Separately from the Morse procedure, the script reduces the full augmented simplicial boundary matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\). Their ranks, by simplex size one through ten, are
\[
(1,17,136,626,1768,3038,2980,1447,281,27).
\]
This gives reduced mod-two Betti numbers nine in degree five and one in degree six, agreeing with (but not proving) the integral homotopy claim. The all-faces acyclicity test and exact signed gradient calculation provide the required non-modular proof data.

## Relationship to prior work
Deshpande and Singh introduced and classified higher-independence homotopy types for complete multipartite graphs, fully whiskered graphs, paths, cycles, and perfect rooted trees. Abdelmalek, Deshpande, Goyal, Roy, and Singh studied chordal-graph and hypergraph shellability criteria. The Pappus graph has induced six-cycles, so it is not chordal; it is not one of the graph families classified by these theorems. The underlying graph's ordinary independence complex, matching complex, and distance-two independence number involve different face conditions or different vertex sets; knowing their homology does not determine this second-stage higher-independence complex. The present classification is a mixed-dimensional example outside the listed equidimensional families.

## Limitations
This proof concerns only \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\) and its integral homotopy type. It does not classify \(\operatorname{Ind}_r(P)\) for other values of \(r\), determine any graph automorphism action, or prove that analogous results hold for all cubic bipartite graphs. Computation of a finite complex is exhaustive here, but it does not imply a general theorem about other graphs. Literature searches cannot exclude all unindexed or unpublished equivalent work. Independent mathematical audit is not included in this document.
