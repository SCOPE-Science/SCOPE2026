# The price-two rigidity transition and full optimizer faces for matched complete networks

## Finding

For each integer \(m\ge2\), partition the \(2m\) vertices of the complete graph into \(m\) prescribed pairs. Independently choose a nonnegative conductance on **every** edge. A within-pair edge costs \(t>0\) per conductance unit; each edge between different pairs costs one unit. Expenditure is exactly \(B>0\). The objective is to maximize the second eigenvalue \(\lambda_2(L)\) of the combinatorial weighted Laplacian.

The optimal value is
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_m(B,t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2B}{2m-2+t},&0<t\le2,\\
\dfrac{B}{m},&t\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
The two expressions agree at \(t=2\). For \(0<t<2\), the **unique unrestricted optimizer** assigns \(a=B/[m(2m-2+t)]\) to every edge, including the prescribed matching. For \(t>2\), **every** optimizer deletes all within-pair edges. At \(t=2\), positive within-pair edges are possible.

The full high-price and critical optimal faces admit the following exact characterization. Label the two vertices of pair \(i\) by \((i,+)\), \((i,-)\), where \(1\le i\le m\). For \(t\ge2\), every optimizer has a common conductance \(c_{ij}\ge0\) on all four edges between distinct pairs \(i,j\), and within-pair conductance \(d_i\ge0\). Define \(L_c\) to be the \(m\)-vertex Laplacian with edge weights \(c_{ij}\). Such an allocation is optimal **if and only if**
\[
\boxed{2d_i+2\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}=\frac Bm\quad(1\le i\le m),\qquad
2\lambda_2(L_c)\ge\frac Bm,\qquad
(t-2)\sum_i d_i=0.}
\]
(The preceding equations force the budget exactly: \(4\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}+t\sum_i d_i=B\).)

For \(t>2\), the optimal face is a singleton for \(m=2,3\), but for every \(m\ge4\) it has relative dimension \(m(m-3)/2\) and contains strictly nonuniform optimal edge allocations. At \(t=2\) the optimal face has relative dimension \(m(m-1)/2\) for every \(m\ge2\). Thus the price transition changes both the support and the dimension of the set of optimal designs.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are simple and undirected, \(m\ge2\), \(B,t>0\), and conductances are independently selectable in \([0,\infty)\). The weighted Laplacian is \(L=\sum_{\{u,v\}}w_{uv}(e_u-e_v)(e_u-e_v)^{\mathsf T}\). The budget is \(\sum_{u,v\text{ in different prescribed pairs}}w_{uv}+t\sum_i w_{(i,+),(i,-)}=B\). The spectral gap \(\lambda_2\) uses the usual Euclidean inner product on all \(2m\) vertices, without degree normalization; disconnected allocations have gap zero. All classification statements concern the entire unrestricted feasible set, not only symmetric trial weights.

## Proof

**1. The sharp optimum by a valid symmetry reduction.** The automorphism group generated by permuting the \(m\) pairs and swapping the two vertices in each pair preserves costs. The map \(L\mapsto\lambda_2(L)=\min_{x\perp\mathbf1,\|x\|=1}x^{\mathsf T}Lx\) is concave. Averaging any optimal Laplacian over these automorphisms therefore gives another optimum whose conductances equal \(a\) between distinct pairs and \(b\) inside each pair. This averaging asserts existence, **not** uniqueness.

The two invariant subspaces are the \(m-1\) dimensional pair-constant, zero-sum space and the \(m\)-dimensional pair-antisymmetric space. On them \(L\) has eigenvalues \(2ma\) and \((2m-2)a+2b\), respectively. Thus
\[
\lambda_2=\min\{2ma,(2m-2)a+2b\},\qquad m(2m-2)a+mtb=B.
\]
There is no advantage to choosing \(b>a\): lowering \(b\) to \(a\) and using the released cost to increase \(a\) strictly increases the minimum. For \(0\le b\le a\), substitution yields \(\lambda_2=B/m+(2-t)b\). Its maximizer is \(b=a\) if \(t<2\), \(b=0\) if \(t>2\), and every \(0\le b\le a\) if \(t=2\). This proves the stated optimum and symmetric witnesses.

**2. Global uniqueness for \(t<2\).** The symmetric optimum has every conductance equal to \(a>0\), hence \(L_{\mathrm{sym}}=2ma\,P\), where \(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/(2m)\). Let \(L\) be any other optimum with \(\lambda_2(L)=2ma\). Every conjugate \(L_g\) satisfies \(L_g-2ma P\succeq0\). Their group average equals \(L_{\mathrm{sym}}-2maP=0\). A finite average of positive-semidefinite matrices can vanish only if each is zero; therefore \(L=2maP\). Its off-diagonal entries force every conductance to equal \(a\). This proves uniqueness without assuming symmetry of \(L\).

**3. Universal high-price certificate and equality.** Let \(u_i=(e_{(i,+)}-e_{(i,-)})/\sqrt2\). These \(m\) vectors are orthonormal and perpendicular to \(\mathbf1\). For any allocation, writing \(d_i=w_{(i,+),(i,-)}\),
\[
 m\lambda_2(L)\le\sum_i u_i^{\mathsf T}Lu_i
 =\sum_{u,v\text{ in different pairs}}w_{uv}+2\sum_i d_i
 =B-(t-2)\sum_i d_i.
\]
For \(t\ge2\), this gives \(\lambda_2\le B/m\). Since step 1 attains the bound, every optimizer must make each \(u_i\) a \(B/m\)-eigenvector (each Rayleigh quotient is at least \(B/m\), and their sum is \(B\) at equality). If \(t>2\), equality also forces \(d_i=0\).

The equation \(Lu_i=(B/m)u_i\) at a vertex of another pair forces its conductances to \((i,+)\) and \((i,-)\) to coincide. Applying the analogous equation for \(u_j\) forces all four edges between pairs \(i,j\) to have common weight \(c_{ij}\). On \(\operatorname{span}\{u_i\}\), the eigenvalues are \(2d_i+2\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}\). On the orthogonal pair-constant space, the restriction is \(2L_c\); its constant vector gives the single zero eigenvalue. Therefore the displayed equations are both necessary and sufficient for optimality. Summing the first equations and using \((t-2)\sum d_i=0\) proves the exact budget identity.

**4. Dimensions and asymmetric witnesses.** At \(t>2\), the equations become \(d_i=0\) and \(\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}=B/(2m)\). For \(m\ge3\), the unsigned incidence matrix of the complete graph on \(m\) vertices has full row rank \(m\) (its left kernel would satisfy \(z_i+z_j=0\) for all distinct \(i,j\), hence \(z=0\) by a triangle). With \(m(m-1)/2\) edge variables, the affine solution dimension is \(m(m-3)/2\); for \(m=2\), its dimension is zero. Uniform weights \(c_{ij}=B/[2m(m-1)]\) satisfy the degree equations, are positive, and give \(2\lambda_2(L_c)=B/(m-1)>B/m\), so the spectral inequality is strict. Thus it does not reduce relative dimension locally. For \(m\ge4\), adding \(+\epsilon\) to \(c_{12},c_{34}\) and \(-\epsilon\) to \(c_{13},c_{24}\), with every other edge unchanged, preserves all degrees and the strict spectral condition for sufficiently small nonzero \(\epsilon\). Hence genuine asymmetric optimizers exist.

At \(t=2\), there are \(m(m-1)/2+m\) variables and \(m\) independent linear equations; the full-face affine dimension is \(m(m-1)/2\). Interior feasible points with strictly positive \(c_{ij}\) and \(d_i\) and strict spectral inequality exist: take \(c_{ij}=B(2m-1)/[4m^2(m-1)]\) and \(d_i=B/(4m^2)\). Then \(2\lambda_2(L_c)=B(2m-1)/[2m(m-1)]\), **which actually exceeds** \(B/m\); budget and eigenvalue equations hold exactly. This supplies an interior point, completing the dimension claim.

## Verification

The accompanying program `verify_matching.py` checks exact weighted-Laplacian identities and budget equations for dimensions \(4\) through \(12\), tests both sides of the price boundary, tests critical nonzero-matching allocations, verifies perturbed asymmetric optimizers for \(m\ge4\), and compares nonsymmetric random feasible allocations to the proved bound. It reports 203 passing checks under Python 3 with NumPy and SymPy. The proof above, not finite experiments, establishes all quantified claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Sun, Boyd, Xiao, and Diaconis (2006) formulate the general continuous-time fastest-mixing Markov process problem as a semidefinite optimization, establish duality, and explicitly analyze paths and cycles; their general theory contains the optimization framework used here, but its examples do not provide the price-two matched-complete-network formula or unrestricted optimizer-face classification. Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo, and Xiao (2007 preprint; 2009 journal) give symmetry and block-decomposition methods for **discrete-time** stochastic matrices, including complete multipartite graphs. Their stochastic nonnegativity/holding constraints and second-largest-eigenvalue-modulus objective differ from this continuous-time, linear-cost problem. The present proof uses a direct antisymmetric eigenspace certificate to characterize all (possibly asymmetric) optimal conductances.

The graph obtained for \(t>2\) by deleting the prescribed matching is the cocktail-party graph, equivalently the complete multipartite graph with \(m\) parts of size two. The general theory and block decomposition are acknowledged; neither is claimed to be new.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the **combinatorial continuous-time** Laplacian, not a discrete-time stochastic transition matrix or a degree-normalized Laplacian. It assumes equal prices for all matched edges and equal unit prices for all other edges, nonnegative weights, and a fixed perfect matching. The spectral condition \(2\lambda_2(L_c)\ge B/m\) is an exact characterization but is not replaced by an elementary finite list of facet inequalities for every \(m\). The novelty conclusion rests on inspected literature and database searches, not an exhaustive proof of absence from unindexed sources; independent external review has not been performed.
