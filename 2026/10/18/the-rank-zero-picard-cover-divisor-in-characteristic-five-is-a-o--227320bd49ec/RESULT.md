# The rank-zero Picard-cover divisor in characteristic five is a one-dimensional torus

## Finding

Let \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\). Consider smooth genus-three cyclic triple covers of \(\mathbf P^1_k\) with the branch point of local exponent two distinguished, represented in depressed monic form
\[
 C_{a,b,c}:\quad y^3=x^4+a x^2+b x+c,
 \qquad \Delta(a,b,c)\ne0.
\]
Two presentations are identified when \((a,b,c)\) and \((u^2a,u^3b,u^4c)\) differ by \(u\in k^\times\); this is the natural coarse parameter space of the indicated marked cyclic covers. Set
\[
 Q(a,b,c)=a^2c+ab^2+c^2.
\]
**The entire smooth \(5\)-rank-zero locus is \(Q=0\), and its coarse parameter curve is isomorphic over \(\mathbf F_5\) to \(\mathbf G_m\).** The other smooth covers have Jacobian \(5\)-rank two; every smooth member has \(a\)-number one. The rank-zero locus misses the bielliptic subfamily \(b=0\).

Concretely, put \(t=c/a^2\) for \(a\ne0\). Its rank-zero classes are parametrized by \(t\in k\setminus\{0,-1\}\), with a single additional class \(t=\infty\) represented by \(y^3=x^4+x\). Thus the parameter curve is \(\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,-1\}\), identified with \(\mathbf G_m\) by \(t\mapsto t/(t+1)\). The boundary in the weighted projective closure consists of precisely the two singular-cover classes \([1:0:0]\) and \([1:0:-1]\) (weights \((2,3,4)\)). Consequently its \(\mathbf F_{5^n}\)-rational **coarse parameter points** number \(5^n-1\) for every \(n\ge1\); this does not count arithmetic twists of covers.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(5\). The monic polynomial \(f(x)=x^4+a x^2+b x+c\) is squarefree, or equivalently its quartic discriminant \(\Delta\) is nonzero. Every smooth cyclic triple cover of the stated ramification type has a depressed monic equation after an affine change of \(x\) and a rescaling of \(y\). The distinguished branch point is the unique point of ramification exponent two; equivalence here means equivalence **as covers preserving the specified cubic deck action**, not unrestricted isomorphism of abstract genus-three curves. In the parameter quotient a scaling \(x\mapsto ux\), accompanied by a suitable scaling of \(y\), gives weights \((2,3,4)\). No assertion is made about the number of abstract isomorphism classes or about Newton slopes beyond the \(5\)-rank.

## Proof

**Cartier calculation.** A basis of regular differentials is
\[
 \omega_0=\frac{dx}{y^2},\qquad
 \omega_1=\frac{x\,dx}{y^2},\qquad
 \omega_2=\frac{dx}{y}.
\]
Because \(y^3=f(x)\) and \(p=5\), the Cartier identities give
\[
 \mathcal C(\omega_0)=\omega_2,\qquad
 \mathcal C(\omega_1)=0,\qquad
 \mathcal C(\omega_2)=(3Q)^{1/5}\omega_0+(3b)^{1/5}\omega_1.
\]
Indeed \(\omega_0=y^{-5}f(x)\,dx\), \(\omega_1=y^{-5}xf(x)\,dx\), and \(\omega_2=y^{-10}f(x)^3\,dx\). The relevant coefficients are \([x^4]f=1\), \([x^4]xf=0\), \([x^4]f^3=3Q\), and \([x^9]f^3=3b\). The image of \(\mathcal C\) has dimension two for every squarefree \(f\), since its first and third columns are independent unless \(Q=b=0\). The latter condition forces singularity by the discriminant identity below. Hence the \(a\)-number is \(3-2=1\).

When \(Q\ne0\), the stable image of the semilinear Cartier operator has dimension two: \(\omega_0\) and \(\omega_2\) cycle under repeated Cartier, modulo the one-dimensional killed direction generated by \(\omega_1\). When \(Q=0\), Cartier is nilpotent (its third iterate vanishes). The Cartier-stable rank therefore gives \(5\)-rank two or zero, respectively. No intermediate value occurs.

**Discriminant and smoothness.** Reduction of the monic quartic discriminant modulo \(5\) gives the exact polynomial identity
\[
 \Delta=(a^2+c)Q+3b^2(ac+b^2).
\]
Thus on \(Q=0\), \(\Delta=3b^2(ac+b^2)\). If \(b\ne0\) and \(ac+b^2=0\), then \(Q=a(ac+b^2)+c^2=c^2\) forces \(c=0\), hence \(b=0\), a contradiction. It follows that a point of \(Q=0\) is smooth exactly when \(b\ne0\). In particular the bielliptic divisor \(b=0\) is disjoint from the smooth rank-zero locus.

**Coarse parameter curve.** Over \(a\ne0\), rescale to \(a=1\); the equation \(Q=0\) becomes \(b^2=-t(t+1)\), with \(t=c\). Smoothness requires \(t\ne0,-1\). The residual scaling \(u=-1\) fixes \(a,c\) and interchanges the two choices of \(b\), so \(t\) is a complete coarse coordinate on that chart. When \(a=0\), the equation \(Q=0\) forces \(c=0\) and smoothness forces \(b\ne0\), leaving one scaling class, designated \(t=\infty\).

For the scheme statement, the quotient of the \(a\ne0\) chart by its residual \(\mu_2\)-action has coordinate ring \(k[t,1/(t(t+1))]\). Near the remaining class rescale \(b=1\), so the local equation is \(a+a^2c+c^2=0\). The implicit-function theorem gives \(a=-c^2+O(c^5)\); the residual \(\mu_3\)-action sends \(c\) to a primitive cube-root multiple, hence the coarse complete local ring is \(k[[c^3]]\). Moreover \(1/t=a^2/c=c^3+O(c^6)\), which is a local uniformizer. Therefore this point compactifies the \(t\)-line as its unique point at infinity, and the smooth locus is exactly \(\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,-1\}\). The two deleted points correspond to \(b=0,c=0\) and \(b=0,c=-a^2\), and both are singular quartic covers. The fractional linear map \(t\mapsto t/(t+1)\) supplies the stated torus isomorphism over \(\mathbf F_5\).

## Verification

The standalone \(\texttt{verify_picard5.py}\) checks the four generic coefficient identities over \(\mathbf F_5[a,b,c]\), including the discriminant factorization, using exact sparse-polynomial arithmetic. It independently enumerates all \(125\) normalized coefficient triples over \(\mathbf F_5\). There are \(100\) squarefree triples: \(84\) of \(5\)-rank two and \(16\) of \(5\)-rank zero. The \(16\) rank-zero triples form four weight-scaling orbits with coarse coordinates \(t\in\{1,2,3,\infty\}\), the four \(\mathbf F_5\)-points of the open parameter curve. Run \(\texttt{python3 verify_picard5.py}\). This finite census corroborates the universally quantified algebraic proof but is not itself used to deduce results over \(k\).

## Relationship to prior work

Laga and Shnidman's study of bielliptic Picard curves (arXiv:2308.15297v1, 2023) treats the distinguished \(b=0\) subfamily and its Pryms; it does not classify the rank-zero stratum in the full three-coefficient Picard family. Nourozi and Rahmati's Cartier-operator study (arXiv:2306.07823v1, 2023) gives the general characteristic-dependent \(a\)-number and Hasse–Witt-matrix method; the \(a\)-number-one statement is **not claimed as new here**. Asif, Fité, and Pentland (arXiv:2010.07247v1, 2020) develop generic Picard-curve Cartier–Manin computations for \(L\)-polynomials rather than the geometric rank-zero parameter curve. De Shalit and Goren (arXiv:1607.04170v1, 2016) study supersingular components on levelled Picard modular surfaces; their Fermat-curve component theorem does not identify the coarse cyclic-cover parameter stratum above. The finding here is the explicit **entire smooth rank-zero locus together with its irreducible torus quotient and boundary**, not a new general Cartier formula.

## Limitations

The classification concerns cyclic covers with their deck structure and distinguished branch point; forgetting the marking may further identify curves. A zero \(5\)-rank is not by itself proof of a supersingular Newton polygon, and no such claim is made. Statements about coarse \(\mathbf F_{5^n}\)-points do not enumerate arithmetic twists or fields of definition of all cover models. Prior generic Hasse–Witt formulas make the coefficient calculation accessible; an unindexed equivalent coarse-moduli stratification remains a priority risk, and independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
