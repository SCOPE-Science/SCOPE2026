The proof is self-contained. For the subgroup lemma, every permutation fixing an at-most-\(k\)-atom subset pointwise belongs to the stabilizer of its quotient point. The union of transposition graphs from two such pointwise stabilizers is connected outside the intersection because the two complement sets meet when the carrier has at least \(2k+1\) points; this yields intersection closure of supports. The least support is setwise preserved by the whole quotient stabilizer, giving exactly the \(\operatorname{Inj}_t/H\) orbit model. The quotient map on each equality-pattern tuple orbit is an explicit projection to selected distinct coordinates modulo \(H\). Pairwise membership needs at most \(2k\) atoms and transfers from the finite test set to arbitrary carriers. A complementary-support counterexample proves failure at size \(2k\) for each \(k\ge2\). No nontrivial numerical computation is needed, and no finite exhaustive experiment is used as a substitute for a universal argument.
