# The sharp unary-to-binary expressive boundary for inquisitive team logic

## Finding
For ordinary inquisitive team logic \(\mathsf{InqBT}\) on any fixed finite monadic relational vocabulary \(\sigma\) (equality, unary predicates, and optionally constants), the one-free-variable fragment is **exactly** the family of first-order-definable unary-team properties that are downward persistent and satisfy the empty-team property. In particular, all one-free-variable formulas have effective first-order translations, arbitrary nested team implications notwithstanding. Two free variables already exceed first-order expressivity in the empty background vocabulary, by the binary-team witness of Kontinen and Ciardelli [1, Theorem 3.6].

A uniform finite-model bound follows. With \(r\) unary background predicates, \(k\) named constants, and quantifier rank \(q\) of the explicitly constructed monadic second-order translation of a finite Boolean combination of team-support assertions, a witness exists, if any witness exists, on a domain of size at most \(k+2^{r+1+q}\). Thus unary-formula satisfiability, finite-premise entailment, and equivalence are decidable.

## Assumptions and scope
Structures have nonempty domains, are possibly infinite, and carry equality, \(r\) unary predicates and optionally \(k\) constant symbols. There are no positive-arity functions and no predicates of arity greater than one. A team \(X\) over the one variable \(x\) is identified with its support set \(R=X[x]\subseteq D\). All formulas are built from first-order atoms, \(\bot\), conjunction, inquisitive disjunction (support of at least one disjunct on the *whole* team), implication quantified over all subteams, and weak universal/existential quantifiers assigning one constant value across the team [1, Definition 2.1]. No range-generating quantifier \([y]\) is permitted. Alpha-conversion ensures that the weakly bound variables are distinct from the distinguished free variable \(x\).

A property \(P(\mathcal M,R)\) is *persistent* if \(P(\mathcal M,R)\) and \(S\subseteq R\) imply \(P(\mathcal M,S)\). It has the *empty-team property* if \(P(\mathcal M,\varnothing)\) holds for every \(\sigma\)-structure \(\mathcal M\). Both conditions are uniform across all structures, finite and infinite.

## Proof
**Step 1: Exact translation to monadic second-order logic.** For each team formula \(\varphi\), construct a monadic second-order formula \(T_\varphi(R,\bar c)\), where \(\bar c\) lists the fixed values of currently weakly-bound variables and \(R\) is a unary second-order parameter. With \(z\) fresh and \(\alpha\) atomic, define
\[
T_\alpha(R,\bar c):=\forall z\,(R(z)\to\alpha(z,\bar c)),\qquad T_\bot(R):=\neg\exists z\,R(z).
\]
Translate conjunction and inquisitive disjunction respectively by ordinary second-order conjunction and disjunction. For team implication define
\[
T_{\varphi\to\psi}(R,\bar c):=\forall S\,\bigl((\forall z\,(S(z)\to R(z)))\to(T_\varphi(S,\bar c)\to T_\psi(S,\bar c))\bigr),
\]
where \(S\) is a fresh *unary* quantified predicate. Translate weak quantifiers by \(T_{\forall^1 y\varphi}:=\forall y\,T_\varphi\) and \(T_{\exists^1 y\varphi}:=\exists y\,T_\varphi\). The variable \(y\) joins the fixed-value parameters, not the team coordinates. Structural induction against the actual support clauses [1] proves
\[
\mathcal M\models_X\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\mathcal M,R=X[x])\models T_\varphi(R)
\]
for every team, including the empty team. The implication step is exact because subteams of a one-coordinate team correspond bijectively to subsets \(S\subseteq R\).

**Step 2: Eliminate monadic second-order quantifiers on pure monadic structures.** We prove the required finite-color game lemma, including for infinite models. In a language of \(m\) unary predicates and equality with fixed named constants, two structures agree on every monadic second-order sentence of quantifier rank at most \(q\) if their named constants have the same equality and predicate patterns, and, after deleting the named elements, the cardinalities of each of the \(2^m\) unary Boolean colors agree after truncation at \(2^q\). Induct on \(q\). An individual-element move picks a color and reduces its count by one; if the pre-move count is at least \(2^q\), the remainder is still at least \(2^{q-1}\). A unary-set move divides each color into two colors. Whenever both old counts are at least \(2^q=2\cdot2^{q-1}\), any split can be matched so that all parts below \(2^{q-1}\) match exactly and all other parts remain at least \(2^{q-1}\). Otherwise, the split is copied exactly. This constructs a matching response to each first- or second-order quantifier; Boolean connectives are immediate. Constants are held fixed throughout. The argument works equally for infinite cardinalities because an infinite color may be split with prescribed finite parts or with two infinite parts.

Apply this to \(T_\varphi\), whose unary predicates are \(R\) and the \(r\) background predicates. Its truth depends solely on finitely many threshold-color-count vectors and the constant patterns. Each such vector is first-order definable by finite conjunctions of color-count conditions \(\exists^{\ge j}z\,\gamma(z)\) and their negations, with \(j\le2^q\), plus the constant requirements. A finite disjunction of accepted vectors is therefore an effectively constructible first-order sentence \(\theta_\varphi(R)\). Truncating each unnamed color to at most \(2^q\) elements preserves every rank-\(q\) test, and yields the claimed bound \(k+2^{r+1+q}\).

**Step 3: Converse expressive completeness.** Let \(\theta(R)\) be any first-order sentence satisfying persistence and the empty-team property uniformly on all structures. The classical Lyndon monotonicity/polarity preservation theorem, applied to the complement polarity of \(R\), gives an equivalent sentence in negation-normal form in which \(R\) appears only as \(\neg R(y)\) [3]. Alpha-rename its quantified first-order variables away from \(x\). Translate \(\neg R(y)\) into the **flat** team formula \(\neg(x=y)\); for a nonempty one-variable team this is supported exactly when \(y\notin R\). Translate other literals into their flat first-order versions, ordinary conjunction into conjunction, ordinary disjunction into inquisitive disjunction, and first-order quantifiers into corresponding weak quantifiers. By induction each translated subformula, with the weakly quantified values fixed, is supported by a **nonempty** team exactly when its ordinary first-order counterpart is true in \((\mathcal M,R)\). The empty-team case agrees automatically because both the resulting team formula and \(\theta(\varnothing)\) are true there. Conversely, every team formula is persistent and supports the empty team [1, Propositions 2.2–2.3]. This proves exact expressive completeness.

**Step 4: Sharpness and decision procedures.** In the empty background vocabulary, Kontinen and Ciardelli [1, Theorem 3.6] explicitly exhibit an \(\mathsf{InqBT}\) formula with the two free variables \(x,y\) for which no first-order sentence in the binary team-relation symbol gives the same support property. It follows that the lower bound of two free variables is attained. For finite collections of unary formulas, take the Boolean combination of their effective monadic second-order translations describing a witness to (non)entailment or (in)equivalence. The game lemma bounds the size of any possible witness, so exhaustive search of finite structures and unary team relations decides these problems.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_unary.py` reproduces the semantics directly on concrete teams of assignments, separately evaluates the recursive monadic-set translation, and compares them across generated formulas containing all five non-atomic primitives and nested weak quantifiers. It completed **13,440 comparisons** on 1–3-element domains and additional nonempty/empty checks of the translation of a negative team-membership atom on 1–4-element domains, returning `UNARY_TEAM_VERIFY_OK`. These are finite tests; the unrestricted statement rests on the displayed induction, the color-splitting lemma, and classical Lyndon preservation.

## Relationship to prior work
Kontinen and Ciardelli [1] prove that sentences of \(\mathsf{InqBT}\) have first-order expressive power but that some two-free-variable formulas escape first-order definability. Their proof analyzes a binary relation encoding functional injections and does not settle the exact one-free-variable fragment. Their later work [2] transfers additional global metatheoretic noncompactness and enumerability results; it studies a different, less restricted fragment. Classical monadic second-order elimination over unary relational structures and classical Lyndon preservation [3] are substantive ingredients, not claimed as new lemmas. Earlier work on weak intuitionistic dependence logic and dependence logics with stronger operators may overlap in terminology; these logics are not silently identified with the precise fragment studied here.

## Limitations
The classification and finite cutoff require **only unary relation symbols and constants** in the background vocabulary; it is not a statement for unrestricted relational structures, for signatures with functions, or for formulas with two free variables. The converse uses Lyndon's theorem over *all* structures, not an unjustified finite-only polarity preservation theorem. The stated finite bound is an effective conservative bound based on monadic second-order quantifier rank, not an optimal complexity or minimal-model bound. No corresponding claim is made for the range-generating quantifier \([x]\), the stronger dependence-logical connectives, or general \(\mathsf{InqBQ}\). Historical unindexed derivations of the exact boundary remain an originality risk.
