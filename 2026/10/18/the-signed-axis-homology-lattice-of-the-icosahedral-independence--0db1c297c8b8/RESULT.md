# The signed-axis homology lattice of the icosahedral independence complex

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the graph of the regular icosahedron on twelve vertices and put \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(I)\). Its graph automorphism group is \(G\cong A_5\times C_2\), where the central involution \(\alpha\) exchanges antipodal vertices and the rotation subgroup is \(R\cong A_5\). Define \(\varepsilon:G\to\{\pm1\}\) to be trivial on \(R\) and equal to \(-1\) at \(\alpha\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be the six antipodal vertex pairs and let \(\mathbb Z^{\pm\mathcal A}\) be the rank-six lattice generated by oriented antipodal pairs, subject to \([v,w]=-[w,v]\), with the natural action of \(G\).

There is a canonical \(G\)-invariant triangulated two-sphere \(S\subset X\), and \(X\) is obtained from \(S\) by attaching precisely the six antipodal edges without any additional two-simplices. Consequently,
\[
X\simeq S^2\vee\bigvee^{6}S^1,
\qquad
\widetilde H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{\pm\mathcal A},
\qquad
\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\varepsilon}.
\]
These are integral \(G\)-module identifications, not merely isomorphisms of abstract abelian groups. Every other reduced integral homology group vanishes.

For the order-ten stabilizer \(D_{10}\subset R\) of one antipodal pair, let \(\delta:D_{10}\to\{\pm1\}\) record whether the pair is exchanged. Then
\[
\widetilde H_1(X;\mathbb Z)|_R\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{D_{10}}^{A_5}\mathbb Z_\delta.
\]
The rational first-homology character on the five \(A_5\)-classes \((1,2A,3A,5A,5B)\) is \((6,-2,0,1,1)\), and on the five corresponding classes multiplied by \(\alpha\) it is \((-6,2,0,-1,-1)\). Thus
\[
\widetilde H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong W_6\boxtimes\varepsilon,
\qquad (W_6)_{\mathbb C}\cong V_3\oplus V'_3,
\]
where \(V_3\) and \(V'_3\) are the conjugate three-dimensional complex irreducible representations of \(A_5\), and \(W_6\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). In degree two, \(R\) acts trivially and the central antipodal involution acts by \(-1\). Hence no positive-degree rational homology class is fixed by all of \(G\), although the degree-two class is fixed by \(R\).

## Assumptions and scope
Use vertices \(u_0,\ldots,u_4,v_0,\ldots,v_4,N,S\), with indices modulo five. The edges of \(I\) are exactly \(Nu_i\), \(Sv_i\), \(u_iu_{i+1}\), \(v_iv_{i+1}\), \(u_iv_i\), and \(u_iv_{i-1}\), for each index. In the accompanying verifier their integer labels are \(u_i=i\), \(v_i=5+i\), \(N=10\), and \(S=11\).

An independent set is a subset without an edge of \(I\). The complex \(X\) consists of all independent sets, including the empty face. Let \(I_2\) be the graph on the same vertices joining exactly pairs at graph distance two in \(I\). The subcomplex \(S=\operatorname{Cl}(I_2)\) is the clique complex of this distance-two graph. The notation \(\mathbb Z^{\pm\mathcal A}\) does not choose orientations: replacing the generator \([v,w]\) by \([w,v]\) changes its sign. Only the non-equivariant wedge description depends on auxiliary paths.

## Proof
**1. A canonical spherical subcomplex.** From the thirty displayed edges the distance profile about every vertex is \((1,5,5,1)\). Every vertex \(x\) has a unique antipode \(\alpha(x)\) at distance three. Among distinct vertices, pairs missing from \(I\) are therefore exactly those at distance two and the six antipodal pairs at distance three. The graph \(I_2\) has thirty edges and is isomorphic to the icosahedral graph: in numerical order \((u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4,v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,N,S)\), an explicit isomorphism \(I\to I_2\) is
\[
(0,2,4,1,3,8,5,7,9,6,11,10).
\]
This notation lists the images of the twelve vertices, starting at zero. Comparison of the thirty listed edges verifies the bijection. An icosahedral graph has exactly twenty triangles, all facial; its clique complex is the usual triangulation of \(S^2\). Thus \(S=\operatorname{Cl}(I_2)\cong S^2\) as a simplicial complex. Since graph distances are preserved, \(S\) is invariant under every automorphism of \(I\).

**2. The six edges are isolated from higher-dimensional faces.** For all vertices \(x,y\), the antipodal distance identity is \(d_I(\alpha(x),y)=3-d_I(x,y)\). If \(y\) differs from \(x\) and \(\alpha(x)\), it is adjacent to at least one endpoint of the antipodal pair. Hence no antipodal pair lies in an independent triple. The maximal two-dimensional faces of \(X\) are exactly the twenty triangles of \(S\); its remaining edges, and only those, are the six antipodal edges. The face vector is \((12,36,20)\). Attaching a one-cell between two points in a connected simply connected CW complex adds a circle up to ordinary homotopy. Iterating this operation gives \(X\simeq S^2\vee\bigvee^6S^1\).

**3. Canonical integral homology modules.** Consider the pair \((X,S)\). All relative cellular chain groups vanish except \(C_1(X,S;\mathbb Z)\), freely generated by the six oriented antipodal edges. Its differential is zero because both endpoints belong to \(S\). Therefore \(H_1(X,S;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{\pm\mathcal A}\) canonically and equivariantly. Since \(S\) is a connected two-sphere, the long exact sequence of \((X,S)\) gives canonical \(G\)-isomorphisms \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(X,S;\mathbb Z)\) and \(H_2(S;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\). These are integral statements, requiring no field rank extrapolation or Smith-normal-form assumption.

**4. The full group action.** Standard icosahedral symmetry identifies \(G\cong A_5\times C_2\); the antipode is central. The index-two rotation subgroup \(R\) acts transitively on the six unordered antipodal axes, with order-ten stabilizer \(D_{10}\). The stabilizer fixes both endpoints under its order-five subgroup and exchanges them under its five involutions. The chain action on oriented axes is therefore exactly \(\operatorname{Ind}_{D_{10}}^{A_5}\mathbb Z_\delta\). An element of order five in \(R\) fixes one axis with positive orientation, contributing trace one; an element of order three fixes none, contributing zero. The fifteen order-two elements collectively have thirty stabilizer incidences with the six axes; each fixes two and reverses both, contributing trace \(-2\). The identity has trace six. Each central-inverted element has the opposite trace because \(\alpha\) reverses all six oriented antipodal edges.

The spherical fundamental class is fixed by every odd-order rotation. Those order-five rotations generate \(R\), so \(R\) acts on \(H_2(S;\mathbb Z)\) by \(+1\). The antipodal map acts freely on the triangulated sphere because no vertex, edge or triangle is setwise fixed; Lefschetz fixed-point theory on \(S^2\) implies that its degree is \(-1\). Thus the degree-two module is \(\mathbb Z_\varepsilon\).

**5. Rational irreducibility.** The known complex character table of \(A_5\) has the two conjugate three-dimensional characters, whose sum is \((6,-2,0,1,1)\) in the specified class order. The first rational homology therefore complexifies as \(V_3\oplus V'_3\). These two constituents are interchanged by the Galois automorphism of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), and consequently neither can constitute a proper rational submodule. Thus their direct sum is one irreducible rational six-dimensional module \(W_6\). Tensoring with the central sign character gives the asserted full \(G\)-module. The character averages over \(R\) and \(G\) are zero for \(H_1\), while the degree-two character averages to one over \(R\) and zero over \(G\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_ico_integral.py` using Python 3.9 or later. The standalone program uses only the Python standard library. It constructs exactly the displayed graph, independently computes all twelve distance profiles, the distance-two graph and its explicit icosahedral relabeling, the six antipodal edges and twenty triangular faces, a coherent signed fundamental two-cycle, and all 120 graph automorphisms by adjacency-preserving backtracking. It checks that the order-five elements generate a group of size sixty, that the central antipode commutes with all automorphisms, and that the signed-edge and spherical-orientation traces agree with the claimed formulas for every element. A passing run prints `ICOSAHEDRAL_SIGNED_HOMOLOGY_PASS`.

The structural proof of the integral homology is the long exact sequence above, not an inference from modular ranks. The finite checker verifies the graph-theoretic and representation-theoretic premises of that proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Barmak's 2010 study introduces the independence complex and develops star-cluster and suspension methods, with results for cycles, forests, Kneser graphs and other classes. It does not present this icosahedral integral homology action. MathWorld records the classical fact that the icosahedral graph is isomorphic to its distance-two graph, and identifies its 120-element automorphism group: neither classical graph fact is claimed as new. The additional point here is the exact decomposition of the independence complex into a canonical spherical subcomplex and six antipodal edges, together with the *integral signed-axis lattice* and its rational representation consequences.

Published calculations of the icosahedral open-neighborhood complex and all distance-neighborhood complexes use different simplicial definitions. In particular, the distance-neighborhood complexes consist of subsets of individual distance-shell unions, not all pairwise nonadjacent vertex sets. Their ordinary homotopy types and an earlier rational homology representation for the open-neighborhood complex do not determine the asserted natural integral first-homology module of \(\operatorname{Ind}(I)\). The appearance of an isomorphic six-dimensional rational \(A_5\)-representation in a *different* complex is not itself a new identification of these complexes or their integral lattices.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the one canonical twelve-vertex graph and its full graph-automorphism action; no family-wide classification or equivariant homotopy equivalence is asserted. The wedge equivalence is not claimed to be canonical or \(G\)-equivariant. The proof supplies a canonical integral homology-module statement but no new classification of integral lattices for other polyhedral complexes. While the compared indexed results do not imply this theorem, unindexed equivalent computations remain a bibliographic risk. The conclusions have not undergone independent scientific audit.
