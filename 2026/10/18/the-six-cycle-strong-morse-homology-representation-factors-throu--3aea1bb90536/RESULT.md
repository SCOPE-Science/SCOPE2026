# The six-cycle strong-Morse homology representation factors through triangle symmetries

## Finding

Let \(C_6\) be the cycle on vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_5\), with indices modulo six. Let \(X=\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(C_6)\) be the pure complex of strong discrete Morse matchings defined by Knudson and Owens–White. The dihedral group \(D_6=\langle r,s\mid r^6=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\) acts on \(X\) through the vertex permutations \(r(v_i)=v_{i+1}\) and \(s(v_i)=v_{-i}\).

The reduced rational homology of \(X\) is concentrated in degree two and has rank five. Its full \(D_6\)-character \(\chi\) is
\[
\chi(r^k)=\begin{cases}5&k\equiv0\pmod3,\\-1&k\not\equiv0\pmod3,\end{cases}
\qquad \chi(sr^k)=1\quad(0\le k<6).
\]
Consequently the central half-turn \(r^3\) acts trivially on \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\), so this representation factors through \(D_6/\langle r^3\rangle\cong S_3\). Its exact decomposition as a rational representation is
\[
\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbf1\oplus V_{\mathrm{std}}^{\oplus2},
\]
where \(V_{\mathrm{std}}\) denotes the two-dimensional standard representation of \(S_3\), inflated to \(D_6\). In particular, the homological action of the geometric rotation \(r\) has order exactly three, although \(r\) has order six on the graph.

## Assumptions and scope

A vertex of the strong-Morse complex represents an incidence pair consisting of a graph vertex and an incident graph edge. A strong matching is generated by a vertex ordering: for a graph, a vertex is paired with its unique earlier neighbor when exactly one such neighbor exists. The complex \(\mathcal{SM}(C_6)\) is the downward closure of these matchings. Its pure subcomplex is generated by maximum-dimensional facets. At this cycle length all maximal faces have this dimension, by the cited source's Theorem 8.4; the independent enumeration below also verifies that the two complexes coincide.

Label the incidence vertices \(0,1,\ldots,11\) so that \(2i\) is \((v_i,\{v_i,v_{i+1}\})\) and \(2i+1\) is \((v_{i+1},\{v_i,v_{i+1}\})\), for \(0\le i<6\). Each facet is determined by a gap vertex \(v_g\) and a root among the five vertices of the remaining path. Each nonroot path vertex is paired to the edge toward the root. Thus there are precisely \(6\cdot5=30\) facets of size four. The exact statement here is confined to this one finite graph, rational homology and the natural dihedral action.

## Proof

**Complete facet model.** For \(g\in\{0,\ldots,5\}\), take the path with ordered vertices
\[
(v_{g+1},v_{g+2},v_{g+3},v_{g+4},v_{g+5}),
\]
with indices modulo six. For each root position \(t\in\{0,\ldots,4\}\), assign to the edge from path positions \(j\) to \(j+1\) the incidence at the left endpoint when \(j<t\), and at the right endpoint when \(j\ge t\). This gives four distinct incidence labels. The resulting 30 facets are pairwise distinct. Independently enumerating all \(6!=720\) vertex filtrations yields exactly these 30 four-pair matchings, together with lower-dimensional matchings contained in them; this establishes the complete complex without extrapolating from samples.

**Rational homology concentration.** Form every face of these 30 facets, including the empty face. The numbers of nonempty faces in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\) are respectively \(12,48,72,30\). In the integral simplicial boundary matrices, reduce coefficients modulo two. Exact binary elimination gives ranks \(11,37,30\) for the boundary maps in degrees \(1,2,3\). Hence the reduced mod-two Betti vector is \((0,0,5,0)\). Over any field of characteristic zero, a boundary matrix has rank at least its rank modulo two. Consequently all rational Betti numbers except possibly the second vanish, and the second is at most five. The reduced Euler characteristic is
\[
12-48+72-30-1=5.
\]
It follows that \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^5\) and all other reduced rational groups vanish. This also independently recovers the rational consequence of the source's nonequivariant homotopy theorem.

**Action on incidence labels.** The rotation and reflection act by
\[
r(2i+\varepsilon)=2(i+1\bmod6)+\varepsilon,
\quad
s(2i+\varepsilon)=2(-i-1\bmod6)+(1-\varepsilon)
\]
for \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Both preserve the 30-facet set, and they satisfy the displayed dihedral relations. For a simplex \(F=(a_0<\cdots<a_d)\), its contribution to the trace on \(d\)-chains is zero unless its underlying vertex set is invariant; otherwise it is the sign of the permutation needed to sort \((g(a_0),\ldots,g(a_d))\). The augmented empty simplex contributes \(-1\) to the reduced Lefschetz number.

**Signed fixed-face count.** The following values are the alternating contributions \((-1)^d\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_d(X;\mathbb Q))\) for \(d=0,1,2,3\), followed by the reduced Lefschetz number (subtract one for the augmentation):

| Elements | Degree 0 | Degree 1 | Degree 2 | Degree 3 | Reduced Lefschetz |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Identity | 12 | -48 | 72 | -30 | 5 |
| \(r,r^2,r^4,r^5\) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -1 |
| \(r^3\) | 0 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| \(s,sr^2,sr^4\) | 0 | 4 | 0 | -2 | 1 |
| \(sr,sr^3,sr^5\) | 0 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

Every entry is produced by complete iteration over the 163 faces, not statistical sampling. Since reduced rational homology is concentrated in even degree two, the reduced Lefschetz number is exactly the character value \(\chi(g)\). This proves the character formula.

**Representation decomposition.** Since \(r^3\) is an involution and its trace is five on a five-dimensional characteristic-zero space, all its eigenvalues are \(+1\); thus it acts identically. Passing to the quotient \(D_6/\langle r^3\rangle\cong S_3\), the character takes the values \((5,-1,1)\) on the identity, three-cycles and transpositions. The three rational irreducible characters of \(S_3\) are the trivial \((1,1,1)\), the sign \((1,1,-1)\), and the standard \((2,-1,0)\), in that order. The identity
\[
(5,-1,1)=(1,1,1)+2(2,-1,0)
\]
gives the asserted decomposition. Nontrivial three-cycles act nontrivially because their trace is \(-1\), so \(r\) has exact homological order three.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens–White [1] introduce these strong-matching complexes and compute the nonequivariant homotopy type of the pure cycle complex. Their Theorem 8.6 implies \(X\simeq\bigvee^5 S^2\) at six vertices, but this does not determine the action of geometric automorphisms on homology. Their Proposition 8.3 gives the 30-facet count used as an independent cross-check. In contrast, the classical ordinary discrete-Morse matching complex [2] contains more matchings and is not interchangeable with the strong complex. General equivariant discrete-Morse methods [3] provide a framework but no character calculation for this specific graph complex. The additional result is the exact representation and its factorization through triangle symmetries, not the nonequivariant wedge count.

## Limitations

The calculation treats exactly \(C_6\); it is not a theorem for arbitrary cycle lengths. It determines a rational representation, not an integral \(\mathbb Z[D_6]\)-lattice or an equivariant homotopy decomposition. Source comparisons cannot rule out unpublished or unindexed equivalent calculations. The arithmetic verification is an exact finite certificate conditional only on the straightforward construction of the stated simplicial complex, not an appeal to numerical approximations.
