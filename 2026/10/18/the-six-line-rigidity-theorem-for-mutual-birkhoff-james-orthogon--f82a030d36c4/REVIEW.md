# Scientific review: six-line mutual Birkhoff--James orthograph theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses the exact \(\ell_1\) signed-support annihilation criterion and exhaustive **symbolic support cases**, not exhaustive enumeration of vectors. Singleton support reduces to a plane; pairs on a two-coordinate support are forced to its two diagonals; three full-support candidates cannot share the required sign-functional kernels. A full-support line permits at most five clique directions. The six stated diagonals all satisfy the bidirectional inequalities, and a six-line clique must be precisely this one. The independent grid checker agrees with a direct piecewise-linear norm minimization for 2466 ordered projective pairs; its finite checks do not prove the infinite-real-vector bound.

## Originality — PASS, subject to identified bibliography risks
Arambašić et al. (2021), DOI: 10.1016/j.jmaa.2021.125203, develop general symmetrized Birkhoff orthograph clique bounds, not the exact real \(\ell_1^3\) maximum and sole extremizer. Wang, Ji and Wei (2023), DOI: 10.3390/math11204320 (published 17 October 2023), distinguish mutual from one-sided sets and give an ordinary four-vector \(\ell_1^3\) example, not the target six-line theorem. The online note *Exponential mutual Birkhoff–James cliques in real \(\ell_1^n\)* explicitly proves lower growth estimates without exact clique numbers. The 2024 orthodigraph classification deals with dimension and norm properties, not this equality case. Dedicated terminology, parameter, equivalence and stronger-coverage searches found no implication yielding this theorem; this is evidence-based best-knowledge originality, not an unqualified assertion of uniqueness in the literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The clique number and equality-case classification are natural geometric invariants in a classical nonsmooth Banach space, specifically studied in connection with orthograph clique questions. The proof exposes a structural competition among one-, two- and three-coordinate norming-functional strata, resolving the exact first three-dimensional case in real \(\ell_1\). It is neither a random finite table entry nor a subsumed renaming of the previously studied two-dimensional case.

## Limits and close literature
Higher dimensions, complex scalars and nonmaximum clique classifications are not determined. An identified 2022 survey chapter, DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-02104-6_8, was not inspected in full, and equivalent unindexed material cannot be excluded. The theorem is based on an analytic argument supported by finite exact-rational checks; no independent audit or external certification has been performed.
