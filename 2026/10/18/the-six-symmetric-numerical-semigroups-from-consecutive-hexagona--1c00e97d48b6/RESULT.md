# The six symmetric numerical semigroups from consecutive hexagonal numbers

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge2\), put \(H_n=n(2n-1)\) and \(S_n=\langle H_n,H_{n+1},H_{n+2}\rangle\). **Theorem:**
\[
S_n\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in\{2,3,6,7,8,18\}.
\]
This is an exact, unbounded classification of a naturally tabulated family of Frobenius numerical semigroups [1].

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(\langle a,b,c\rangle=\{ua+vb+wc:u,v,w\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\}\). A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid of \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\); its Frobenius number \(F(S)\) is its greatest gap and its genus \(g(S)\) counts all nonnegative gaps. Symmetry means every integer \(t\) satisfies \(t\in S\iff F(S)-t\notin S\), equivalently \(F(S)=2g(S)-1\). All claims quantify over **every** integer \(n\ge2\), without restricting primes or generator sizes. The three hexagonal generators have overall greatest common divisor one.

## Proof
**1. Three-generator symmetry criterion.** For a minimally generated triple \(a<b<c\) with gcd one, the standard three-generator characterization [2, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1] says the semigroup is symmetric exactly when, after some permutation \((A,B,C)\), the number \(d=\gcd(A,B)>1\) satisfies \(\gcd(C,d)=1\) and \(C=u(A/d)+v(B/d)\) for nonnegative integers \(u,v\). The necessity uses minimality; sufficiency follows from the two-generator gluing criterion [2]. The exceptional \(n=3\) is treated by a direct gluing witness below. For \(n\ge4\) all three generators are minimal: indeed \(H_{n+1}<2H_n\), \(H_{n+2}<3H_n\), \(H_{n+2}<H_n+H_{n+1}\), and \(H_{n+2}<2H_{n+1}\), while \(H_{n+2}\ne2H_n\). Thus the largest generator is not generated by the smaller two (and neither smaller generator is generated by larger ones).

**2. Bounded gcds.** Elementary Euclidean reductions yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gcd(H_n,H_{n+1})&=\gcd(n+1,3),\\
\gcd(H_{n+1},H_{n+2})&=\gcd(n+2,3),\\
\gcd(H_n,H_{n+2})&=\gcd(n,6)\gcd(n+2,5).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the third identity, factor \(H_n=n(2n-1)\) and \(H_{n+2}=(n+2)(2n+3)\); a common prime can occur only by comparing factors at index difference two, yielding prime factors from \(2,3,5\), with the displayed coprime components. It follows that every candidate pair has \(2\le d\le30\) and the total gcd of the triple is one.

**3. No cases at large indices.** Write \(L=H_n\), \(U=H_{n+2}\). For every \(n\ge125\),
\[
30(U-L)=30(8n+6)<n(2n-1)=L.
\]
The inequality holds at \(n=125\) and its positive gap increases thereafter. If a gluing identity \(dC=uA+vB\) existed with \(A,B,C\in\{H_n,H_{n+1},H_{n+2}\}\), then \((u+v)L\le dC\le(u+v)U\). Since \(L\le C\le U\) and \(d(U-L)<L\), these inequalities force the *integer* \(u+v=d\). Therefore \(C\) is a convex combination of \(A,B\); \(A,B\) must be the two outer generators and \(C=H_{n+1}\). In that ordering,
\[
v=\frac{d(H_{n+1}-H_n)}{H_{n+2}-H_n}
  =\frac{d(4n+1)}{8n+6}
  =\frac d2-\frac{2d}{8n+6}.
\]
But \(0<2d/(8n+6)<1/2\) for \(n\ge125\) and \(d\le30\). If \(d\) is even this is strictly below an integer but above its predecessor; if \(d\) is odd it lies strictly between consecutive integers. Either way \(v\notin\mathbb Z\), a contradiction. Hence no \(n\ge125\) gives a symmetric semigroup.

**4. Exact finite remainder.** For each of the 123 integers \(n\in[2,124]\), enumerate the three unordered generator pairs. For each pair with \(d>1\), test \(\gcd(d,C)=1\), then for every integer \(u=0,\ldots,\lfloor C/(A/d)\rfloor\) perform an exact quotient-and-remainder test to determine whether \(v=(C-uA/d)/(B/d)\) is a nonnegative integer. This tests **every** solution of each nonnegative linear Diophantine equation, not a sample. The complete finite result is exactly \(n=2,3,6,7,8,18\). The following explicit certificates \((n; A,B,C; d;u,v)\) witness sufficiency:

- \(n=2\): pair \((H_2,H_3)=(6,15)\), \(d=3\), remaining \(H_4=28=4(6/3)+4(15/3)\).
- \(n=3\): pair \((H_3,H_5)=(15,45)\), \(d=15\), remaining \(H_4=28=1(15/15)+9(45/15)\). (The semigroup is two-generated.)
- \(n=6\): pair \((H_6,H_8)=(66,120)\), \(d=6\), remaining \(H_7=91=1(66/6)+4(120/6)\).
- \(n=7\): pair \((H_8,H_9)=(120,153)\), \(d=3\), remaining \(H_7=91=1(120/3)+1(153/3)\).
- \(n=8\): pair \((H_8,H_{10})=(120,190)\), \(d=10\), remaining \(H_9=153=8(120/10)+3(190/10)\).
- \(n=18\): pair \((H_{18},H_{20})=(630,780)\), \(d=30\), remaining \(H_{19}=703=5(630/30)+23(780/30)\).

The arithmetic bound and the exhaustive finite range together establish the theorem for every \(n\ge2\).

## Verification
Run `python3 checker.py` (standard library only). The executable directly enumerates every candidate gluing identity for \(2\le n\le124\), checks the three gcd formulas, minimality and the cutoff inequality. An independent residue-graph Dijkstra algorithm computes Apéry sets, Frobenius numbers and genera for \(2\le n\le60\), confirming the equivalence between the gluing classification and gap reciprocity there. The independent finite computations are corroboration; the infinite conclusion follows from Sections 1–3 and the exhaustive finite check.

## Relationship to prior work
Sequence OEIS A069758 [1] tabulates Frobenius numbers for three consecutive ordinary hexagonal numbers, but supplies no all-index symmetry classification. Fel [2] gives the three-generator gluing criterion and classifications for Fibonacci and Lucas triples; it is essential background but does not work out the hexagonal gcds, cutoff or six exceptions. Robles-Pérez and Rosales [3] treat **three consecutive triangular** and **four consecutive tetrahedral** numbers, not every-other-triangular-index hexagonal triples. The pairwise-coprime special family of [4] does not imply this exceptional-gcd classification. Related pentagonal triples require different gcd arithmetic; their known classification does not logically imply this hexagonal answer.

## Limitations
This is a finite-exception classification for a specified natural family, not a universal Frobenius-number formula for every \(n\), nor a claim about longer hexagonal tuples. The proof of necessity invokes the published three-generator gluing theorem [2]. Exhaustiveness below \(125\) depends on the exact, fully specified finite enumeration in `checker.py`. A differently worded or unindexed earlier classification of the same family cannot be completely excluded.
