# The six-vertex wheel strong-Morse complex collapses to sixteen circles, with signed dihedral homology

## Finding
Let \(W=K_1*C_5\) be the six-vertex wheel, considered as a **one-dimensional** simplicial complex: none of its graph triangles is filled. The full complex \(X=\mathcal{SM}(W)\) generated by vertex-ordering-induced strong discrete-Morse matchings admits an explicit sequence of twenty elementary simplicial collapses onto a connected graph with twenty vertices and thirty-five edges. Consequently,
\[
X\simeq\bigvee^{16} S^1,
\qquad \widetilde H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{16},
\qquad \widetilde H_k(X;\mathbb Z)=0\ (k\ne1).
\]
The stronger simple-homotopy statement is a collapse **onto a graph**, not a claim of a literal simplicial collapse directly onto a one-vertex wedge.

Let \(D_5=\operatorname{Aut}(W)\) act through rim rotations and reflections. For every nonidentity rotation, its trace on \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\) is \(1\); every reflection has trace \(-4\), while the identity has trace \(16\). With \(\varepsilon\) the one-dimensional reflection-sign module and \(V=\mathbb Q^5/\mathbb Q(1,1,1,1,1)\) the standard four-dimensional rational rim permutation quotient,
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong4\varepsilon\oplus3V.
\]
In particular, the \(D_5\)-invariant first rational homology is zero.

## Assumptions and scope
The rim vertices are \(0,1,2,3,4\), with edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) modulo five. The hub is \(5\), joined to all rim vertices. The graph has six vertices and ten edges. A vertex of \(X\) is an ordered pair \((u,v)\) encoding the incidence pairing between vertex \(u\) and graph edge \(\{u,v\}\); it is represented in the code as `u>v`.

For a permutation \(\pi\) of the six graph vertices, process its vertices in increasing order. In a one-dimensional simplicial graph the descending link of the current vertex is a discrete set of its preceding neighbors. It is a simplicial cone exactly when there is **one** preceding neighbor; in that case one strong matching pair \((u,v)\) is generated. If the set is empty or contains at least two vertices, no pair is generated at that step. By definition, the faces of the **full**, not pure, complex are all subsets of the matching sets generated by all vertex orders. This is the filtration-generated definition of the cited strong-Morse-matching complex.

## Proof
There are exactly \(6!=720\) orders. The deterministic enumeration in `verify_collapse.py` constructs all matching sets and takes their downward closure, using no homology assumptions. The resulting counts of nonempty simplices by dimension are
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(20,55,20).
\]
The maximal simplices comprise twenty triangles and twenty-five edges. No higher simplex occurs. Their exact face set and the twenty collapse pairs are generated without stochastic choices and can be checked using `collapse_certificate.json`.

For each pair \((e,T)\) in that certificate, \(e\) is a one-dimensional face of the triangle \(T\), and **among all simplices remaining at that step, \(T\) is the unique proper coface of \(e\)**. Therefore the deletion of \(e,T\) is an elementary simplicial collapse. After the twentieth deletion no triangles remain. The remaining graph has twenty vertices and thirty-five edges. Connectivity is checked by an explicit traversal of its edge set. Its first Betti number and integral first-homology rank are therefore
\[
35-20+1=16.
\]
A connected finite graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of as many circles as the rank of its cycle lattice; all other reduced integral homology vanishes. Elementary collapses preserve simple homotopy type, proving the first part.

For the equivariant claim, write \(r(i)=i+1\bmod5\) and \(s(i)=-i\bmod5\) on the rim, and let each fix the hub. The action on each oriented simplicial chain sends the incidence vertex \((u,v)\) to \((g(u),g(v))\). Directly summing orientation signs of setwise-invariant simplices gives the following traces on \((C_0,C_1,C_2)\): identity \((20,55,20)\); each nonidentity rotation \((0,0,0)\); and every reflection \((2,-3,0)\). Hence the simplicial Lefschetz numbers are \(-15,0,5\), respectively. Because \(X\) is connected and has only \(H_0,H_1\), the alternating trace identity yields the rational \(H_1\) character \((16,1,-4)\).

Finally, \(\varepsilon\) has character \((1,1,-1)\) and \(V\) has \((4,-1,0)\) on these three conjugacy-class types. Their combination \(4\varepsilon\oplus3V\) matches \((16,1,-4)\); finite-group rational representations are semisimple, and these are the complete rational irreducibles of \(D_5\), so the module formula follows. Neither constituent contains the trivial representation, proving the invariant-dimension claim.

## Verification
`verify_collapse.py` exhausts the 720 orders, verifies the entire face vector and every successive unique-coface condition using the supplied certificate, and traverses the final graph. `verify_character.py` independently constructs the face set from the graph edge relation, computes all simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), and evaluates the signed chain traces for all ten dihedral elements. The stored output text files contain the actual results. The two scripts use only the Python standard library and are invoked in a directory containing the certificate.

The certificate is exhaustive for this fixed six-vertex graph. No finite enumeration is used to assert a result for unexamined wheel sizes.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White (arXiv:2609.06844, first public September 6, 2026) define the full strong-Morse complex as the downward closure of filtration-generated matchings and classify several graph families, including cycles and stars. Their cycle results concern \(\mathcal{SM}(C_5)\), not the wheel obtained by adding its hub. The paper's complete-graph, simplex, and disjoint-union statements also do not give the present wheel complex. Fernández (arXiv:2504.15729) establishes the strong-Morse framework but does not calculate this particular complex.

Known ordinary discrete-Morse-complex results for wheels concern \(\mathfrak M(W)\), which uses **all** acyclic incidence matchings; \(\mathcal{SM}(W)\) is generally a strict subcomplex, and the homotopy and character of the former do not imply those of the latter. An accessible public result for a five-vertex wheel concerns an additional different graph, \(K_1*C_4\). Previously classified strong-matching complexes of complete multipartite graphs do not cover \(K_1*C_5\), which contains an induced five-cycle. The overlap evidence does not establish uniqueness against every unpublished or unindexed source.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the six-vertex wheel only. It gives no classification of strong-Morse complexes of larger wheels and does not claim an equivariant collapse: the explicit collapses need not respect the \(D_5\)-action. The module decomposition is over \(\mathbb Q\), not a classification of integral \(\mathbb Z[D_5]\)-lattices. Mathematical originality beyond the inspected indexed and primary sources is subject to the residual risk of unindexed overlap.
