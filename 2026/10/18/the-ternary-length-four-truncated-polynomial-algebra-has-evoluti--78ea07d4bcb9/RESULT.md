# The ternary length-four truncated-polynomial algebra has evolution dimension eight and 252 optimal quadratic supports

## Finding

Let \(A=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^4)\), with basis \((1,t,t^2,t^3)\). The smallest dimension of an evolution algebra into which \(A\) embeds as an ideal is exactly
\[
\operatorname{edim}(A)=8.
\]
Equivalently, the simultaneous Waring rank of the vector-valued squaring map \(H:A\to A\), \(H(x)=x^2\), is exactly \(8\).

There is also a complete count of its *optimal quadratic-support spaces*. Put
\[
V=\operatorname{Sym}^2((\mathbb F_3^4)^*),\qquad
S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_3}\{a^2,2ab,b^2+2ac,2ad+2bc\}\subset V.
\]
Exactly \(252\) eight-dimensional subspaces \(E\subset V\) satisfy both \(S\subseteq E\) and
\[
E=\operatorname{span}\{\ell^2:\ell\in (\mathbb F_3^4)^*,\ \ell^2\in E\}.
\]
Among these support spaces, \(243\) contain exactly \(10\) distinct projective squares of nonzero linear forms; the remaining \(9\) contain exactly \(16\). These are counts of subspaces in a fixed quadratic-form space, **not** counts of isomorphism classes of evolution envelopes.

## Assumptions and scope

All vector spaces and polynomial coefficients are over the three-element field \(\mathbb F_3\). Quadratic forms are *formal homogeneous polynomials* of degree two, rather than arbitrary functions on finite sets. Every such polynomial is nevertheless uniquely specified by its values on \(\mathbb F_3^4\).

An evolution algebra is an algebra possessing a basis \((e_i)\) with \(e_ie_j=0\) whenever \(i\ne j\). The evolution dimension \(\operatorname{edim}(A)\) is the least dimension of an evolution algebra containing \(A\) as an ideal. The equality of this dimension with the simultaneous Waring rank for a finite-dimensional *unital* commutative algebra over characteristic different from two is the established result of Costoya, Fernández Ouaridi and Viruel; it is used, not claimed as new here.

Write \(x=a+bt+ct^2+dt^3\). Since \(t^4=0\),
\[
H(x)=a^2+(2ab)t+(b^2+2ac)t^2+(2ad+2bc)t^3.
\]
The four coordinate forms are linearly independent, so \(\dim S=4\), \(\dim V=10\), and \(\dim(V/S)=6\).

## Proof

**Eight-square upper bound.** Set
\[
(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_8)=(a,b,c,d,a+b,a+c,a+d,b+c).
\]
The four coordinate polynomials of \(H\) have the following exact decompositions (all signs and coefficients are interpreted in \(\mathbb F_3\)):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a^2&=\ell_1^2,\\
 2ab&=\ell_5^2-\ell_1^2-\ell_2^2,\\
 b^2+2ac&=\ell_2^2+\ell_6^2-\ell_1^2-\ell_3^2,\\
 2ad+2bc&=\ell_7^2+\ell_8^2-\ell_1^2-\ell_2^2-\ell_3^2-\ell_4^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the simultaneous Waring rank is at most eight. The eight quadratic squares are linearly independent (their coefficient matrix has rank eight); in particular this is a genuine eight-dimensional square-generated support containing \(S\).

**Complete lower-bound reduction.** Let \(\pi:V\to Q=V/S\). Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the nonzero linear forms have \((3^4-1)/2=40\) projective classes, because multiplying a form by \(-1\) leaves its square unchanged. Let \(\mathcal P\) denote these \(40\) distinct squares.

For any subspace \(W\subset Q\), define
\[
M_W=\operatorname{span}\{p\in\mathcal P:\pi(p)\in W\}\subset V.
\]
Suppose \(S\) could be spanned by at most seven quadratic squares. Their span \(T\) would contain \(S\) and have \(\dim T\le7\), so \(\dim\pi(T)\le3\). Choose a three-dimensional subspace \(W\subset Q\) containing \(\pi(T)\). Necessarily \(S\subseteq T\subseteq M_W\), hence \(\dim(M_W\cap S)=4\).

That cannot happen. The finite enumeration described below checks **all**
\[
{6\brack3}_3=33880
\]
three-dimensional subspaces \(W\) of \(Q\), and returns the exact histogram

| \(\dim(M_W\cap S)\) | Number of three-dimensional \(W\) |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 31905 |
| 2 | 1830 |
| 3 | 145 |
| 4 | 0 |

Thus no seven squares, or fewer, can simultaneously represent all four coordinate quadrics. Combined with the explicit upper bound, the simultaneous Waring rank is exactly eight and the cited unital-algebra envelope theorem gives \(\operatorname{edim}(A)=8\).

**Optimal quadratic-support census.** An eight-dimensional subspace \(E\subset V\) containing \(S\) is the inverse image of a unique four-dimensional subspace \(W=E/S\subset Q\). It is spanned by squares if and only if \(\dim M_W=8\); indeed \(M_W\subseteq E\) always. Enumerate all
\[
{6\brack4}_3={6\brack2}_3=11011
\]
four-dimensional \(W\)'s as the kernels of all two-dimensional spaces of independent linear equations on \(Q\). Exactly \(252\) satisfy \(\dim M_W=8\). For \(243\) of them, exactly \(10\) projective square forms lie in \(E\), and for the remaining \(9\), exactly \(16\) do. Every counted support is generated by an eight-element subset of those squares, and no other support can occur.

**Why the finite certificates are exhaustive.** Every \(k\)-plane in \(\mathbb F_3^6\) has a unique reduced row-echelon basis. The verifier enumerates each possible increasing pivot tuple and independently assigns each admissible free entry the three field values. This generates each \(k\)-plane once. For the three-plane lower bound it computes ranks over \(\mathbb F_3\) of the lists of relevant ten-coordinate squares and of their six-coordinate quotient images. Their difference is exactly \(\dim(M_W\cap S)\). For the four-plane census it uses the corresponding row-echelon two-plane of defining equations, counts square forms in the kernel and tests the rank of their ten-coordinate coefficients. These are finite exact rational-integer modular calculations, not sampling or a timeout-based exclusion.

## Verification

Run `python3 VERIFY.py` from the directory containing this file. Python 3 standard library is the only requirement. The program recomputes the four quadratic coefficient vectors, verifies the explicit eight-square decomposition symbolically and on all \(81\) input vectors, exhausts the \(33880\) three-planes and the \(11011\) four-planes, checks the intersection histogram and produces the \(252\)-space count. It terminates with the line:

`VERIFY_OK: rank=8, 40 projective squares, 33880 three-planes, 11011 four-planes, 252 optimal support spaces (243 with 10 squares, 9 with 16)`

The finite enumeration itself is part of the proof of the lower bound and support census, not an extrapolation to unverified infinite cases.

## Relationship to prior work

Costoya, Fernández Ouaridi and Viruel (arXiv:2609.32784, first submitted September 26, 2026) establish the evolution-envelope/Waring-rank correspondence and determine the length-four truncated-polynomial case when the field has at least seven elements. Their Remark 5.4 specifically reports that the rank for \(\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^4)\) exceeds seven, without giving its exact value or classifying optimal support spaces. The present result supplies an eight-square decomposition, an independently reproducible exhaustive lower certificate, and the full \(252\)-space support census at this first small-field exception.

Classical work on multiplication algorithms over small fields, including Winograd (1977), bears on related bilinear rank bounds. Bilinear rank and symmetric simultaneous Waring rank are distinct minimization problems; existing bilinear algorithms cannot automatically be treated as symmetric-square decompositions. The previously available dimension formula for split square-zero local algebras does not cover this length-four algebra, whose maximal ideal has nonzero square.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns this single natural algebra over \(\mathbb F_3\); it does not classify optimal dimensions for every small field or higher truncation length. The \(252\) subspaces are not asserted to represent \(252\) nonisomorphic evolution algebras or nonisomorphic embeddings. The lower bound and support census depend on complete finite-field enumeration; the independent Python certificate is supplied so their exhaustive nature can be checked. Bibliographic searches cannot exclude unindexed equivalent calculations, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
