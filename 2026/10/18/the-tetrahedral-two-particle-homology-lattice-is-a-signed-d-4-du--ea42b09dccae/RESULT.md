# The tetrahedral two-particle homology lattice is a signed \(D_4\) dual lattice

## Finding
Let \(K_4\) be the graph on \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) having every possible edge, and put \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|K_4|)\). The natural \(S_4\)-module on its first integral homology is
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\ \cong\ \operatorname{sgn}\otimes L,
\qquad L=\mathbb Z^4+\mathbb Z\,u\subset\mathbb Q^4,
\quad u=\tfrac12(e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3),
\]
where \(S_4\) permutes the four coordinates. The lattice \(L\) equals the dual of the usual \(D_4\) root lattice \(\{z\in\mathbb Z^4:\sum z_i\equiv0\pmod2\}\).

More geometrically, for each \(i\) let \(F_i\) be the loop in which one point goes around the triangle avoiding vertex \(i\), while the other point remains at \(i\). Coherently orient the triangle using the increasing order on the other three labels, multiplied by \((-1)^i\). The four classes \([F_i]\) span a subgroup of index two in \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\). An exchange loop \(Y\) on the tripod centered at vertex zero satisfies
\[
2[Y]+[F_0]+[F_1]+[F_2]+[F_3]=0.
\]
The identification above sends \([F_i]\) to \(e_i\) and \([Y]\) to \(-u\), with the entire action twisted by \(\operatorname{sgn}\).

The induced integral \(S_4\)-equivariant sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{\operatorname{sgn}}
\longrightarrow H_1(X;\mathbb Z)
\stackrel{p_*}{\longrightarrow} H_1(K_4;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow0
\]
is **nonsplit**, where \(p_*\) sums the two particle motions. The first module is also **not** the four-point signed permutation lattice, despite possessing that same rational character.

## Assumptions and scope
Configurations are *unordered*, consist of exactly two distinct points on the geometric realization of the original six-edge tetrahedral graph, and permit movement within edges but no collisions. Integral coefficients are used throughout. All actions are induced by vertex permutations; no choice of a planar exterior face is invariant under the entire group. No claim is made about higher-particle configurations or about an integral equivariant homotopy decomposition.

For two particles, the Abrams discrete configuration cube model is a deformation retract: all essential-vertex paths have at least one edge, and every cycle in \(K_4\) has length at least three. Its vertices are unordered pairs of distinct graph vertices; edges consist of a graph edge together with a disjoint vertex; squares consist of two disjoint graph edges.

## Proof
Orient graph edges by increasing endpoint labels; orient a one-cell \((\{a,b\},v)\), \(a<b\), from \(\{a,v\}\) to \(\{b,v\}\). Orient the two-cell indexed by \((ab,cd)\) as the product of those two oriented edges, ordered lexicographically. The cube model has \(6\) vertices, \(12\) edges and \(3\) squares. Write its two signed cellular maps as \(\partial_1\) and \(\partial_2\). The exact chain construction appears in `verify.py`.

Choose the first five one-cells (in lexicographic order) as a spanning tree of the one-skeleton. The seven remaining one-cell coefficients give integral coordinates on \(\ker\partial_1\), since filling in tree coefficients is uniquely determined by the cycle condition. In these coordinates the seven columns comprising the three \(\partial_2\)-boundaries and four facial cycles \((F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3)\) have determinant \(2\). Replace the last facial column by the centered exchange loop \(Y\), which traverses
\[
\{1,2\}\to\{0,2\}\to\{3,2\}\to\{0,3\}
\to\{1,3\}\to\{0,1\}\to\{1,2\}.
\]
The resulting seven columns \((\partial_2S_0,\partial_2S_1,\partial_2S_2,F_0,F_1,F_2,Y)\) have determinant \(-1\). Thus these seven cycles are a **unimodular integral basis of the full cycle lattice**. In particular, the three square boundaries span a primitive subgroup, so \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z^4\), with integral basis \(([F_0],[F_1],[F_2],[Y])\).

The explicit signed boundary identity is
\[
2Y+F_0+F_1+F_2+F_3
=\partial_2(-S_0+S_1-S_2).
\]
It follows that the four facial classes span index two, and that \(H_1\) is exactly the lattice obtained from their free rank-four span by adjoining half their negative sum. At the chain level, each permutation \(g\in S_4\) carries \(F_i\) to \(\operatorname{sgn}(g)F_{g(i)}\), since the missing-vertex sign compensates the induced orientation of the other three vertices. The exchange relation and absence of torsion then force \(g[Y]=\operatorname{sgn}(g)[Y]\). Hence the displayed integral equivariant lattice isomorphism.

The one-particle motion map \(p_*\) sends each \(F_i\) to the correspondingly oriented facial cycle of \(K_4\), and sends \(Y\) to zero. Three facial graph cycles give an integral basis of \(H_1(K_4;\mathbb Z)\); their single relation is \(\sum_i p_*F_i=0\). The displayed unimodular basis proves that \(\ker p_*\) is generated by \([Y]\), so the exact sequence is established over \(\mathbb Z\).

Finally untwist the sign character. The \(S_4\)-fixed sublattice of \(L\) is exactly \(\mathbb Z u\). Every invariant integral linear functional \(L\to\mathbb Z\) is a multiple of coordinate sum, and so takes \(u\) to an even integer: \(\sum_i u_i=2\). No invariant retraction \(L\to\mathbb Z u\) exists, proving nonsplitting. The same evaluation invariant equals \(4\) for the ordinary four-point permutation lattice \(\mathbb Z^4\), where its primitive fixed generator is \(e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3\). These different intrinsic integers \(2\) and \(4\) prove that the two integral \(S_4\)-lattices are not isomorphic.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. It reconstructs all \(6+12+3\) cells and both integral boundary maps; tests \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\); checks boundary ranks \(5\) and \(3\), the face-index determinant \(2\), the full unimodular determinant \(-1\), and the explicit exchange/square boundary relation. It also checks the action on the four facial cycles for **all 24** vertex permutations and the corresponding exchange transformation modulo boundaries. These are complete finite checks for the indicated cell complex, not extrapolations from samples.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett–Farber study ordered two-point graph configuration spaces and their planar cycle geometry. Ko–Park determine first-homology groups for graph braid groups and imply rank four and torsion-freeness for two points on \(K_4\). Harrison–Keating–Robbins–Sawicki and Maciążek–Sawicki describe general Aharonov–Bohm and exchange cycles in planar graph braid groups; in particular the latter explicitly computes homology of \(K_4\) for larger particle numbers. An–Maciażek study geometric braid generators. Their known group ranks and cycle presentations do not state the present **integral \(S_4\)-module isomorphism, index-two facial embedding and nonsplit integral equivariant extension**. No originality is claimed for the abstract rank-four abelian group or for the existence of face and exchange loops.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns only two points on \(K_4\). It does not assert that the same lattice or extension appears for other complete graphs. It is a self-reviewed calculation, not an independently certified result. A previously unindexed equivariant lattice calculation remains a possible bibliographic overlap.
