# Assessment of the tetrahedral integral homology representation

**Correctness (PASS).** The result identifies the standard two-point Abrams deformation retract, gives every signed cellular boundary, and supplies an exact unimodular basis for its seven-dimensional one-cycle lattice. A determinant of \(-1\) for three square boundaries, three face cycles, and an exchange loop proves torsion-freeness and the index-two relation. The literal chain identity \(2Y+\sum_iF_i=\partial(-S_0+S_1-S_2)\) and all 24 face-permutation actions are checked. The nonsplitting argument computes the integral invariant evaluation of the primitive fixed vector, rather than relying on rational character theory.

**Originality (PASS, with residual risk).** Barnett–Farber analyze ordered configurations; Ko–Park compute braid-group abelianizations; Harrison et al. develop planar AB and exchange classes; Maciążek–Sawicki compute more-particle wheel homology and discuss the two-particle generators. These are closer than unrelated graph-complex character results. None of the inspected relevant statements provides the tetrahedral **integral full-symmetry lattice**, the index-two face embedding, or its nonsplit extension; the abstract group and generic face/Y generators are explicitly excluded from novelty. Later unindexed coverage remains possible.

**Value (PASS).** The first planar three-connected symmetric test case exposes an integral obstruction erased by rational isotypic decompositions. A geometric four-face lattice is not saturated; a single exchange loop adjoins the missing half-sum, yielding the root-dual lattice and an integral equivariant nonsplitting. This distinguishes integral symmetry modules with identical rational characters; it is not a computation of an arbitrary graph invariant.

**Scope and risks.** The result is finite but canonical and exact. It does not settle equivariant homotopy or higher-particle topology, and independent mathematical audit is absent.
